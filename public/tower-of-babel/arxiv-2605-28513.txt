Learning Theory of the SVRG: Generalization and Convergence Analysis
Yunwen Lei, Zimeng Wang, Xiaoming Yuan

Abstract
Variance reduction (VR) methods employ stochastic gradients with decreasing variance, and they have been widely applied to solve large-scale optimization problems in machine learning because of their efficiency. Existing theoretical studies of VR methods are mainly focused on the convergence analysis, leaving the generalization behavior largely unexplored. In this paper, we bridge this gap by developing the first non-vacuous generalization analysis of the representative VR method: Stochastic Variance Reduced Gradient (SVRG), through the lens of algorithmic stability. In particular, we establish sharp stability bounds of the SVRG in both convex and strongly convex settings by exploiting its algorithmic structure. The obtained bounds are data-dependent, because the training errors are incorporated along the trajectory. Our analysis clarifies the interplay between optimization and generalization, leading to optimal excess population risk bounds in both settings. Our approach differs substantially from existing analyses of stochastic algorithms in the sense that we decompose the SVRG update as an SGD-like step plus a zero-mean correction term and then introduce novel Lyapunov functions to absorb the additional gradient terms induced by the reference points. Our analytical framework can be generalized to other VR methods, and we demonstrate the generalization by the well-known Stochastic Average Gradient Accelerated (SAGA) method.



Keywords:
SVRG, SAGA, learning theory, algorithmic stability, generalization analysis, convergence analysis, excess population risk.

1
Introduction

The rapid growth of model complexity and data scale in modern machine learning has posed significant challenges for traditional optimization algorithms such as gradient descent (GD).
To address this bottleneck, stochastic methods have gained widespread adoption, which balance the computational cost and precision for scalability and efficiency. Among these, the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) method
[
37
]
and its variants
[
12
,
15
,
27
,
32
,
19
,
38
]
have emerged as the dominant optimization framework. Unlike GD, which relies on full gradient computations, SGD leverages random sampling to reduce per-iteration computational costs. This property enables its superior performance in large-scale model training across diverse applications. Unfortunately, SGD suffers from inherent limitations: its convergence rate is sublinear even for strongly convex objectives, as diminishing step sizes are required to ensure asymptotic convergence. This often results in slow practical performance, motivating the development of accelerated variants.
Among these, variance reduction (VR) methods such as the Stochastic Variance Reduced Gradient (SVRG)
[
22
,
48
]
, Stochastic Average Gradient (SAG)
[
39
]
, and Stochastic Average Gradient Accelerated (SAGA)
[
14
]
have been particularly successful in achieving faster convergence than SGD. Unlike SGD, which uses stochastic gradient estimates with non-vanishing variance, VR methods employ more sophisticated update rules that progressively reduce the variance of gradient approximations, and hence often exhibit faster convergence both in theory and practice. Specifically, these VR methods admit constant step sizes and can achieve linear convergence rates for strongly convex objectives.

Existing analyses of VR methods have primarily focused on optimization errors (i.e., the performance of models on the training dataset), while in machine learning, the generalization behavior of models on unseen testing samples is of greater interest. Unfortunately, the generalization analysis of VR methods is much lagging behind. There exist only very few investigations on the generalization behavior of SVRG
[
9
,
26
,
29
,
45
]
. Specifically, the studies
[
9
,
29
]
derive black-box generalization bounds for convergent learning algorithms, expressed in terms of optimization error and training sample size. While these bounds can be specialized to SVRG by invoking its known convergence rates, they fail to leverage the specific algorithmic structure of SVRG and imply loose guarantees. Furthermore, these results require additional assumptions such as the strong convexity and Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) conditions. More recently, some more refined analyses are taken in
[
26
,
45
]
, which investigate the uniform stability
[
7
]
and on-average stability
[
42
]
of SVRG. Although they consider the algorithmic structure, their analyses require a strong Lipschitzness assumption on the loss functions and very small step sizes for which the method converges at extremely slow rates.

Motivated by the above discussions, we aim to bridge the gap between the well-established optimization theory and the underdeveloped generalization analysis of VR methods. To this end, we develop the generalization analysis of SVRG through the lens of algorithmic stability
[
7
]
, which is a fundamental framework for quantifying the sensitivity of learning algorithms to perturbations in the training data. We then demonstrate the generality of our approach by extending the analysis to SAGA.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows.

1).

We develop generalization error bounds for SVRG in both convex and strongly convex settings by investigating its on-average model stability
[
24
]
, and extend the analysis to SAGA. Notably, we obtain these results without assuming Lipschitzness of the loss functions. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first non-vacuous generalization analysis of any VR method that can exploit the algorithmic structure (i.e., without interpreting it as a black-box method). Moreover, our stability bounds involve training errors, which show the benefit of optimization to generalization.

2).

We derive convergence guarantees for SVRG under convex loss functions with milder step size conditions compared to those in
Reddi et al. 2016
, and similarly establish optimization error bounds for SAGA with weaker step size requirements than those in the original work
[
14
]
. By combining these bounds with our generalization results, we further obtain excess population risk (EPR) bounds of SVRG and SAGA for both convex and strongly convex problems.

3).

The derived EPR bounds for SVRG and SAGA are optimal. Specifically, our bounds for both methods are of order
O(1/\sqrt{n})
for convex problems and of order
\widetilde{O}(1/(n\mu))
for
\mu
-strongly convex problems, where
n
denotes the sample size and
\widetilde{O}(\cdot)
hides logarithmic factors. These results match the existing minimax lower bounds for statistical guarantees
[
1
]
.

Our generalization analysis of SVRG differs significantly from existing ones of SGD and its variants
[
21
,
24
]
, owing to the more complicated gradient structure inherent in SVRG’s two-loop update mechanism. To overcome this difficulty, we split the iterative scheme into two parts: one mimics the SGD step and the other serves as a gradient correction. To handle the redundant terms arising from the gradient correction term, we introduce novel Lyapunov functions to help construct recursion inequalities. Additionally, to remove the Lipschitzness assumption, we leverage the self-bounding property
[
43
]
to bound the terms involving norms of gradients. These techniques extend naturally to SAGA, and we believe they can further inspire generalization analysis for other VR methods.

3

4

5

6

7

8

A
, and the strongly convex analyses of both SVRG and SAGA are deferred to Appendix
B
.

Related Work

In this section, we review related work on algorithmic stability and variance reduction methods.

Algorithmic stability.
Algorithmic stability is a fundamental concept in statistical learning theory to study the generalization behavior of learning algorithms
[
7
]
. Intuitively, a learning algorithm is said to be stable if the output of this algorithm changes slightly if we perturb the training dataset by a single example. Various stability concepts have been introduced in the literature, including the uniform stability
[
7
]
, on-average stability
[
42
]
, hypothesis stability
[
7
]
, argument stability
[
25
]
, and on-average model stability
[
24
]
. The seminal work
[
21
]
pioneered the stability analysis of SGD for smooth and Lipschitz problems. Recent progress relaxes the Lipschitzness assumptions and develops optimistic stability bounds by incorporating the training errors into the stability analysis
[
23
,
24
,
40
]
. A notable property of the stability analysis is that it can imply algorithm-dependent generalization bounds to understand the balance between optimization and generalization, which motivates the growing interest in studying the stability of various stochastic optimization algorithms, including federated learning
[
11
,
44
]
, delayed SGD
[
16
]
, stochastic proximal point methods
[
49
]
, zeroth-order SGD
[
33
]
, clipped-SGD
[
50
]
, Langevin dynamics
[
30
]
, minibatch SGD
[
34
]
, differential privacy
[
3
]
, multi-objective learning
[
10
]
and neural network training
[
17
,
36
,
46
]
.

Variance reduction methods.
The concept of variance reduction (VR) techniques was initially developed to mitigate the non-vanishing variance issue in SGD that prevents convergence
[
20
,
47
]
.
The contemporary era of VR methods began with the stochastic average gradient (SAG) method
[
39
]
, which was then followed by several influential alternatives, including the SAGA
[
14
]
, the stochastic variance-reduced gradient (SVRG)
[
22
]
, and the stochastic dual coordinate ascent (SDCA)
[
41
]
. Specifically, the SAG method is a stochastic variant of the earlier incremental aggregated gradient method
[
4
]
. For
\mu
-strongly convex and
\alpha
-smooth problems, SAG was the first stochastic method to enjoy a linear convergence rate with a total gradient complexity (number of component gradient evaluations) of
O((\alpha/\mu+n)\log(1/\epsilon))
to achieve a precision of
\epsilon>0
, using a step size of
O(1/\alpha)
. The SAGA method is an unbiased variant of SAG that employs reference points to construct unbiased stochastic gradients. Similar to SAG, it exhibits a linear convergence rate and a gradient complexity of
O((\alpha/\mu+n)\log(1/\epsilon))
, but offers a simpler theoretical analysis. Another prominent VR method, SVRG, also leverages reference points but reduces the storage cost of SAG while maintaining the same order of gradient complexity. The stability analysis of SVRG was also established in the literature, which, however, either interpreted SVRG as a black-box method
[
9
,
29
]
or implied vacuous risk bounds
[
26
,
45
]
. For a broader overview of VR methods, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
13
,
18
,
28
,
31
,
41
]
.

3
Problem Setup

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a probability measure defined on a sample space
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is an input space and
\mathcal{Y}
is an output space.
Let
S=\{\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{n}\}
be a sequence of training examples drawn independently from
\mathbb{P}
, from which we aim to learn a model
h:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{Y}
for prediction. We consider parametric models where each model is parameterized by a parameter
\mathbf{w}
in a parameter space
\mathcal{W}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
be the loss incurred by applying
\mathbf{w}
to do the prediction on
\mathbf{z}:=(\mathbf{x},y)
.
Popular examples of loss functions include the logistic loss
\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})=\log(1+\exp(-y\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle))
and the smoothed hinge loss for classification, as well as the least-squares loss
\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}-y)^{2}
and Huber loss for regression.
We are interested in minimizing the population risk

L(\mathbf{w}):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathbb{P}}[\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})],

where
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation w.r.t.
\mathbf{z}
.
Since the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
is unknown, we consider its empirical approximation

L_{S}(\mathbf{w}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z}_{i}),

(3.1)

which is referred to as the empirical risk (training error).

3.1
Stability and Generalization

We often apply a learning algorithm to approximately minimize the training error. We denote by
A(S)
the output model by applying an algorithm
A
to the dataset
S
. In statistical learning theory (SLT), we are especially interested in the relative behavior of the output model
A(S)
as compared to the best model
\mathbf{w}^{*}:=\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}L(\mathbf{w})
, which is measured by the excess population risk
L(A(S))-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})
. A standard approach to tackle the excess population risk is to consider the following error decomposition
[
6
]

\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[L(A(S))-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})]=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[L(A(S))-L_{S}(A(S))]+\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[L_{S}(A(S))-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})],

where we use the identity
\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})]=L(\mathbf{w}^{*})
since
\mathbf{w}^{*}
is independent of
S
. We refer to
L(A(S))-L_{S}(A(S))
as the generalization gap, which is the difference between testing and training on the output model. We refer to
L_{S}(A(S))-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
as the optimization error, which measures the suboptimality of the output model as compared to
\mathbf{w}^{*}
in training.
Generalization gap is a term of central interest in SLT, while optimization error has been extensively studied in optimization theory
[
5
]
.

In this paper, we leverage the algorithmic stability to study the generalization gap. Intuitively, we say an algorithm is stable if a change of a single example does not lead to a significant change in the behavior of output models. Various stability concepts have been introduced, including uniform stability
[
7
]
, on-average stability
[
42
]
, and on-average model stability
[
24
]
. In this paper, we focus on the on-average model stability since it can imply fast rates without a Lipschitzness assumption.

Definition 3.1

(On-average Model Stability
[
24
]
)
.

Let
S=\{\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{n}\}
,
S^{\prime}=\{\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{n}^{\prime}\}
be drawn independently from
\mathbb{P}
. For any
i\in[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, define

S^{(i)}:=\{\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{i-1},\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{z}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{n}\}

as the set formed from
S
by replacing its
i
-th element with
z_{i}^{\prime}
. Let
\epsilon>0
. We say a randomized algorithm
A
is on-average model
\epsilon
-stable if
\mathbb{E}_{S,S^{\prime},A}[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A(S)-A(S^{(i)})\|^{2}]\leq\epsilon^{2}
.

Before the analysis, we recall the definitions of convexity, Lipschitzness, and smoothness.

Definition 3.2

(Convexity, Lipschitzness, and smoothness)
.

Let
g:\mathcal{W}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be differentiable. Let
\sigma\geq 0,\alpha>0
, and
G>0
.

1).

We say
g
is
\sigma
-strongly convex if

g(\mathbf{w})\geq g(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})+\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\nabla g(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|^{2},\;\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

We say
g
is convex if the above inequality holds with
\sigma=0
.

2).

We say
g
is
G
-Lipschitz continuous if

|g(\mathbf{w})-g(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|\leq G\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|,\;\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

3).

We say
g
is
\alpha
-smooth if

\|\nabla g(\mathbf{w})-\nabla g(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\|\leq\alpha\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|,\;\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

We also recall the self-bounding property of smooth functions, which is a key tool to remove the Lipschitzness assumption in our analysis.

Lemma 3.3

(
Self-bounding
property
[
43
]
)
.

For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, if the function
\mathbf{w}\rightarrow\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative and
\alpha
-smooth, then
\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})\|^{2}\leq 2\alpha\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
.

Our stability analysis also requires the following lemma on the coercivity of smooth and convex functions.

Lemma 3.4

(
Coercivity

[
21
]
)
.

For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, if the function
\mathbf{w}\rightarrow\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is convex and
\alpha
-smooth, then

\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z})\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z})\|^{2},~\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

The following lemma gives a quantitative connection between the generalization gap and the on-average model stability for smooth problems.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
S,S^{\prime}
, and
S^{(i)}
be constructed as in Definition
3.1
, and
\gamma>0
. If for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the function
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative and
\alpha
-smooth, then

\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[L(A(S))-L_{S}(A(S))]\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[L_{S}(A(S))]+\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,S^{\prime},A}[\|A(S^{(i)})-A(S)\|^{2}].

3.2
The SVRG Method

We study the SVRG method
[
22
]
, which is arguably the most classic VR method for minimizing the empirical risk
L_{S}(\mathbf{w})
defined in Eq. (
3.1
). The detailed implementation of SVRG is summarized in Algorithm
1
, which features a two-loop structure: an outer loop indexed by
t
that updates the reference point, and an inner loop consisting of
m
stochastic gradient steps performed using the same reference point. Compared with some other VR methods, such as SAG and SAGA, SVRG reduces the storage cost from
O(nd)
to
O(d)
by maintaining only a single reference gradient.

Algorithm 1

SVRG for minimizing
L_{S}(\mathbf{w})

0:

initial point
\mathbf{w}_{1}=\mathbf{x}_{m}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; step size
\eta>0
.

for

t=1,2,\dots

do

Set
\mathbf{x}_{0}^{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}
(option I) or
\mathbf{x}_{0}^{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}
(option II).

for

k=0,\dots,m-1

do

Sample an index
i_{k}
uniformly at random from
[n]
.

Set
g_{k}^{t+1}=\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})+\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
.

Update
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}-\eta g_{k}^{t+1}
.

end

for

Update
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}\in\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}\}_{k=0}^{m-1}
uniformly at random.

end

for

In the next two sections (Sections
4
and
5
), we present a detailed analysis of SVRG, including stability, convergence, and excess population risk (EPR) guarantees. To better illustrate the main ideas, we focus on the behavior of SVRG under the assumption that
\ell(\cdot;\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative, convex, and
\alpha
-smooth for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, whereas the analysis for the strongly convex case is deferred to Appendix
B
. For simplicity, we use option I for the inner-loop initialization in the convex setting.

4
Stability Analysis of SVRG

In this section, we conduct stability analysis for SVRG, which together with Lemma
3.5
yields generalization error bounds. In particular, given neighboring datasets
S
and
S^{(i)}
as defined in Definition
3.1
, we aim at establishing on-average model stability bound
\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{m}^{t+1}\|^{2}
for
t\geq 1
, where
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
are the sequences generated by SVRG applied to
S
and
S^{(i)}
, respectively. This is much more challenging than the stability analysis of SGD
[
21
,
24
]
, and the difficulty mainly arises from: (i) the two-loop structure and (ii) the more complicated gradient constructions of SVRG.

Technical Strategies.

To overcome challenge (i), we adopt an inner-to-outer analysis: we first fix an outer-loop index
t\geq 1
and analyze the corresponding inner-loop updates, then telescope the resulting inequalities over the inner loop to derive a recursive relation for the outer-loop iterates. For notational convenience, we omit the superscript and denote
\mathbf{x}_{k}:=\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}
. Then the core step of SVRG reads as

\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\mathbf{x}_{k}-\eta\big(\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})+\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big),

(4.1)

which differs from SGD by involving additional gradient terms evaluated at the reference point
\mathbf{w}_{t}
. To mitigate the complexity brought by (ii), we define auxiliary operators
G_{k}
and
d_{k}
via

G_{k}:=\mathbf{x}_{k}-\eta\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}),\quad d_{k}:=\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t}).

(4.2)

Then Eq. (
4.1
) can be written as

\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=G_{k}+\eta d_{k}.

(4.3)

Hereafter, we denote by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
the expectation taken over all randomness and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]
the conditional expectation given all history up to time
(t,k)
.
From the uniform randomness of index
i_{k}\in[n]
, we know that
\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k}]=0
.
Therefore, the reformulation Eq. (
4.3
) can be viewed as an SGD-like update
G_{k}
plus a zero-mean correction term
\eta d_{k}
, which serves as the key starting point for analyzing the stability bound of SVRG within the inner loop.
After telescoping the inner loop, we then introduce a sequence of Lyapunov functions
\{U_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
defined by (we omit the dependency on
i
in
U_{t}
for simplicity):

U_{t}:=\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{m}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2},

(4.4)

where
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\}_{t\geq 1}
is the sequence of reference points generated by SVRG applied to
S^{(i)}=\big\{\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)}\big\}_{j=1}^{n}
. The introduction of
U_{t}
helps balance the additional terms arising from
d_{t}
, which in turn allows us to derive a recursive relation for
\{U_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and hence establish the stability bound for SVRG.
Note that the proposed analysis framework based on the reformulation Eq. (
4.3
) and the use of Lyapunov functions is fairly general and can be extended to other VR methods with similar gradient structures. As an illustration, we apply a similar approach to analyze SAGA in Section
6
.

The following theorem presents the stability bounds for SVRG in the convex setting. The upper bounds involve a summation of the empirical errors at the iterates, and improve for models with small training errors, which is consistent with existing analysis for SGD
[
24
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this gives the first non-vacuous stability analysis for SVRG and more generally, for any VR method in the literature.

Theorem 4.1

(Stability bounds)
.

Let
S,S^{(i)}
be defined in Definition
3.1
. Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
be the sequences produced by Algorithm
1
with option I applied to
S
and
S^{(i)}
, respectively.
Assume that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative, convex, and
\alpha
-smooth.
If
\eta\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}
, then for any

, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{m}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{16e\alpha m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8e\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big],

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+2\eta\langle G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+2\eta\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle

where
\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)}

Remark 4.2

(Novelty)
.

Note that our stability analysis for SVRG extends beyond existing analyses of SGD or its variants
[
21
,
24
]
.
To estimate the stability bound
\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}
within the inner loop, we utilize the reformulation Eq. (
4.3
) to derive

\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}=\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+2\eta\langle G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle.

In the above decomposition, the first term on the RHS corresponds to the stability bound of the standard SGD, which can be controlled using similar arguments as established in
[
21
,
24
]
. However, the other two terms are introduced due to the correction term
d_{k}
in the SVRG gradient construction, which require careful and refined estimation to prevent deteriorating the overall stability bound.
For this purpose, we first decompose
2\eta\langle G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle
into two components via

2\eta\langle G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle=2\eta\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle-2\eta^{2}\langle\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}^{(i)}),d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle,

where the first term vanishes after taking expectation since
\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k}]=0
, and the other term can be further controlled by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

-2\eta^{2}\langle\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}^{(i)}),d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\rangle\leq\eta^{2}\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}^{(i)})\|^{2}.

Using uniform randomness of index
i_{k}
, we further have

\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2},

which is fixed for all
k=0,\ldots,m-1
within the inner loop. After telescoping the stability bound for the inner loop, we introduce the Lyapunov function
U_{t}
in Eq. (
4.4
) to balance the redundant terms, and establish the following recursive relation for the outer loop:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{m}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}[U_{t+1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{1}{mt}\Big)^{m}\mathbb{E}[U_{t}]+\frac{8\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\Big(1+\frac{1}{mt}\Big)^{m}\eta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1})\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{1}{mt}\Big)^{mt}\mathbb{E}[U_{1}]+\frac{8\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\Big(1+\frac{1}{mt}\Big)^{m}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\Big(1+\frac{1}{mt}\Big)^{m(t-l)}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e\mathbb{E}[U_{1}]+\frac{8e\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big].

Different from the stability bound of SGD
[
24
]
, we have an additional term
e\mathbb{E}[U_{1}]
, which corresponds to
16e\alpha m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})/n
in our stability bound Eq. (
4.5
). Fortunately, as we show in Corollary
5.4
, this additional term does not deteriorate the excess population risk bound for properly chosen parameters.

4.1
Discussion with Existing Work

In this subsection, we present a comparison of our analyses to the existing stability analyses of SVRG
[
9
,
26
,
29
]
and SCAFFOLD
[
45
]
, which extends SVRG to the context of federated learning.

The work
[
9
,
29
]
studied SVRG by interpreting it as a black-box optimization algorithm, and related it to convergence rates under some additional assumptions, such as the strong convexity and the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition. Specifically, under a
\mu
-PL condition and
G
-Lipschitzness assumption, the work
[
9
]
showed that SVRG is
O\big(G(\epsilon_{c}/\mu)^{1/2}+G^{2}/(\mu n)\big)
-uniformly stable, where
\epsilon_{c}>0
denotes the optimization error of SVRG. Consequently, their approach fails to exploit the specific algorithmic structure of SVRG, leading to loose guarantees. In contrast, our analysis fully leverages the iterative schemes of SVRG to get tighter bounds.
For convex,
\alpha
-smooth and
G
-Lipschitz problems, the work
[
26
]
showed that zeroth-order SVRG with
\eta\leq C/(tm)
is
O(G^{2}t^{2C\alpha}/n)
-uniformly stable, where
t>0
is the outer-loop index and
m>0
denotes the inner-loop iteration number. The recent work
[
45
]
considered a VR method called SCAFFOLD for federated learning.
Under an additional assumption that the variance of
\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is bounded by
\sigma^{2}>0
, SCAFFOLD
[
45
]
is shown to be
O\big(G(G+\sigma)t^{C}\log t/n\big)
on-average stable with a step size
\eta\leq C/(2\alpha t)
. However, these step sizes are too small, for which the methods converge only at extremely slow rates. Therefore, the stability analyses
[
26
,
45
]
cannot balance between optimization and generalization.
Moreover, these results rely on a strong Lipschitzness assumption on the loss functions
[
9
,
26
,
29
,
45
]
, which is unnecessary for standard convergence analyses of SVRG. Notably, we remove this Lipschitzness assumption by leveraging the self-bounding property (Lemma
3.3
). Besides, utilizing the on-average model stability, the established bound incorporates a weighted sum of empirical risks rather than the Lipschitz constant, clarifying how optimization enhances stability and generalization by producing models with small empirical risks.

5
Excess Population Risk Analysis of SVRG

In this section, we study the excess population risk (EPR) bounds of SVRG. To this aim, we first analyze the optimization error of SVRG, and then combine it with the generalization gap obtained from our stability analysis to derive EPR bounds. We show that with properly chosen step size and total iterations, SVRG can achieve EPR bounds of order
O(1/\sqrt{n})
, which is minimax optimal for convex problems
[
1
]
.

5.1
Convergence Rates

We start by analyzing the decay of the optimization error for SVRG. The result established in the following theorem relaxes the step size requirement from
\eta<1/(4\alpha)
in the existing result of
[
35
, Theorem 6]
to
\eta<1/(2\alpha)
.

Theorem 5.1

(Optimization error)
.

Assume for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative, convex, and
\alpha
-smooth.
Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
be the sequence produced by Algorithm
1
with option I applied to
S
. Denote
\mathbf{w}_{S}:=\operatorname{\arg\min}_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}L_{S}(\mathbf{w})
and
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1}
. If
\eta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
, then for any
t\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big]\leq\frac{1}{2Mm\eta t}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}+4\alpha m\eta^{2}(L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{1})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}))\big],

where
M>0
is given by

M:=M(\eta)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\textit{if}~0<\eta\leq\frac{1}{4\alpha},\\
2(1-2\alpha\eta),\quad\textit{if}~\frac{1}{4\alpha}<\eta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}.\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Consider a fixed outer-loop index
t\geq 1
and denote
\mathbf{x}_{k}:=\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}
and
g_{k}:=g_{k}^{t+1}
, then the core step of SVRG can be written as
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\mathbf{x}_{k}-\eta g_{k}
. Expanding
\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}
gives

\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}-2\eta\big\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S},g_{k}\big\rangle.

(5.2)

Since
\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S},g_{k}\rangle]=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k})\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})+\frac{1}{2\alpha n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2},

(5.3)

where the inequality follows from coercivity of each
\ell(\cdot;\mathbf{z}_{j})
due to the convexity and
\alpha
-smoothness
[
22
]
:

\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z}_{j})\geq\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z}_{j})+\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z}_{j})\rangle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

(5.4)

To bound
\|g_{k}\|^{2}
, from
\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k})
, we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})+\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})+\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2},

(5.5)

where we apply Young’s inequality to derive the first inequality, and the second inequality holds by noting that
\mathbb{E}[\|\zeta-\mathbb{E}[\zeta]\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|\zeta\|^{2}]-\|\mathbb{E}[\zeta]\|^{2}
for any random vector
\zeta
.

Taking expectation on both sides of Eq. (
5.2

5.3

\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]\\
+\frac{2\eta^{2}-\eta/\alpha}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]-2\eta\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big].

Next, we analyze the outer loop. Summing Eq. (

\mathbf{x}_{0}^{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}
from option I, we have

\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]\\

(5.7)

Since
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
is chosen uniformly at random from
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}\}_{k=0}^{m-1}

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big].

Define the Lyapunov function

P_{t}:=\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}+\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}.

Applying Eq. (
5.7

5.8

\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[P_{t+1}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[P_{t}\big]+\frac{4\eta^{2}-\eta/\alpha}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]\\
-2\eta\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big].

Otherwise if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}2\alpha\big(\ell(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\langle\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{S};\mathbf{z}_{j}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\rangle\big)

\displaystyle 2\alpha(L_{S}(\mathbf{x})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})),~\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{W}.

Applying Eq. (

5.2
Excess Population Risk Bounds

Next, we apply Lemma
3.5
with the stability bounds established in Theorem
4.1
to derive generalization error bounds. Combining them with the optimization error bounds in Theorem
5.1
, we obtain the following EPR bounds. For ease of presentation, we denote
A\lesssim B
if there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that
A\leq cB
, and
A\asymp B
if both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A

Theorem 5.3

(Excess population risk)
.

Assume that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative, convex, and
\alpha
-smooth.
Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t}\}_{t,k}
be the sequence produced by Algorithm
1
with option I applied to
S
with
\eta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
and denote
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1}
.
Then for any
t\geq 1
and
\gamma>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{m\eta t}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{\eta}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{1}{m\eta t}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big).

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Note that
\frac{\eta}{\alpha}-\eta^{2}\geq 0
since
\eta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
. For any
s\in[m]
, applying Eq. (
4.10
) recursively and dropping the negative term yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{s}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{s}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{p}{n}\Big)^{s}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{m}^{t}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{m}^{t}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2+1/p}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\Big(1+\frac{p}{n}\Big)^{s-1-k}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\Big(1+\frac{p}{n}\Big)^{s-1-k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}\big],

which is an increasing function in
s
. Therefore, the RHS of Eq. (
4.13
) after dropping the negative term also upper bounds
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{s}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{s}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]
for all
s\in[m]
. Then it follows from Theorem
4.1
and convexity of
\|\cdot\|^{2}
that

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{16e\alpha m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8e\alpha(4+mt/n)\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big].

(5.13)

Applying Lemma
3.5
and plugging in Eq. (
5.13
) gives

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})]+\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\\
\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]+\frac{4(\alpha+\gamma)e\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big]+\frac{8(\alpha+\gamma)e\alpha m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}).

(5.14)

On the other hand, we know from the proof of Theorem
5.1
and
0\leq\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*})
that

\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{2Mm\eta t}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+4\alpha m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)

(5.15)

and

\sum_{l=1}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{l+1})\big]\leq mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{2M\eta}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+4\alpha m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big).

(5.16)

Plugging Eq. (
5.15
) and Eq. (
5.16
) into Eq. (
5.14

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{m\eta t}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{\eta}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

(5.17)

Combining Eq. (
5.15
) and Eq. (
5.17

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})=\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{m\eta t}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{\eta}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\big]+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\Big)

which completes the proof.
∎

We then specify the choices of
m,t,\gamma
, and
\eta
to derive the following corollary, which shows that SVRG achieves optimal EPR bounds of order
O\big(\sqrt{1/n}\big)
in the convex setting. Note the condition
mt\asymp n
ensures that the total number of inner-loop iterations scales proportionally with
n
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let the assumptions in Theorem
5.3
hold and assume that
\mathbb{E}_{S}[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]<\infty
. Then we can take
mt\asymp n
,
\eta\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}
, and
\gamma=\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}
to derive

\mathbb{E}[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})]\lesssim\sqrt{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})/n}.

Proof of Corollary
5.4
.

Firstly, it follows from Theorem
5.3
and boundedness of
\mathbb{E}_{S}[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]
that

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})\big]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{L(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma m\eta t}\big(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)\eta^{2}mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{n}\\
+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)\eta}{n}\big(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{1}{m\eta t}\big(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big).

(5.18)

From the condition on
\gamma
and
L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}_{1})
, we have that

\frac{L(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{\gamma}\leq\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}=\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}}.

(5.19)

Furthermore, it follows from
mt\asymp n
that

\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)\eta^{2}mtL(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{n}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot 1\cdot\frac{1}{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot n\cdot L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}=\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}}.

(5.20)

To proceed, note that
mt\asymp n
implies
m\lesssim n
, which further yields

1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\asymp 1+m\cdot\frac{1}{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\lesssim 1.

Then we can bound the remaining terms in (
5.18
) as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma m\eta t}\big(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma m\eta t}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot n/\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}=\frac{1}{n}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}},

(5.21)

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+mt/n)\eta}{n}\big(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\big)\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot 1/\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}},

(5.22)

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}\cdot n\cdot\frac{1}{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}{n}\cdot L(\mathbf{w}_{1})=\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}},

(5.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m\eta t}(1+m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}))\lesssim\frac{1}{m\eta t}\asymp\frac{1}{n/\sqrt{nL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}}=\sqrt{\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{n}}.

(5.24)

Plugging Eq. (
5.19
)-Eq. (
5.24
) into Eq. (
5.18
) gives

\mathbb{E}[L(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})]\lesssim\sqrt{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})/n},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Corollary
5.4
establishes the conditions for selecting the parameters
m,t,\eta
, and
\gamma
to obtain EPR bounds of order
O(\sqrt{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})/n})
in the convex case.
Notably, the derived bound of order
O(1/\sqrt{n})
remains minimax optimal in the general case. Therefore, our stability and optimization analysis implies optimal EPR bounds.
Furthermore, it shows that the generalization behavior depends on the initial point
\mathbf{w}_{1}
. If we initialize SVRG with a point of small population risk, then our analysis can capture this property to show that SVRG attains a good generalization behavior. This is in spirit similar to the existing data-dependent stability analysis of SGD
[
23
]
.

6
Extension to SAGA

In Section
4
, we established stability bounds for SVRG. The key technical ingredients are the reformulation Eq. (
4.3
), which decomposes the SVRG update into an SGD step plus a zero-mean correction term, and the Lyapunov function Eq. (
4.4
), which absorbs the additional gradient terms arising from the reference point. This analytical approach is not specific to SVRG; rather, it can be extended to a broad class of VR methods.

In this section, we take SAGA
[
14
]
as a concrete example and demonstrate how the same approach yields a stability analysis for SAGA. Moreover, by imitating the Lyapunov-based convergence analysis of Theorem

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we develop the learning theory of SVRG, a representative variance reduction (VR) method, by considering both its generalization and convergence behavior. We leverage algorithmic stability to study the generalization and apply techniques from optimization theory to analyze the convergence. By carefully balancing these two components, we further derive optimal excess population risk (EPR) bounds. To overcome the difficulties arising from the complicated algorithmic structure, we introduce several novel techniques, including the reformulation as an SGD update plus a correction term and the construction of tailored Lyapunov functions to derive recursive inequalities in both the stability and convergence analyses. The proposed approach is not specific to SVRG, and we demonstrate its generality by extending the framework to SAGA.

There remain several interesting problems worthy of further investigation. First, we consider convex and strongly convex problems in this paper. A natural extension would be to investigate SVRG and SAGA for nonconvex problems. For instance, applying our framework to analyze the training of neural networks with these methods, potentially by leveraging the weak convexity of the associated loss functions, appears to be a promising research direction. Second, while we focus on SVRG and SAGA here, it would be valuable to extend our analytical techniques to other VR methods. Finally, the EPR bounds developed in this work hold in expectation. Developing high-probability guarantees would provide a deeper understanding of the robustness of these VR methods.

Analysis under Strong Convexity

This section presents the strongly convex analysis for both SVRG and SAGA, including theorem statements and their proofs. In this setting, we assume the loss function
\ell(\cdot;\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative,
\mu
-strongly convex, and
\alpha
-smooth for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, which enables tighter stability and EPR bounds than the convex case.

B.1
Analysis of SVRG

In the strongly convex setting, we implement Algorithm
1
with option II, which initializes each inner loop with the reference point to facilitate linear convergence of SVRG
[
22
]
.

We first present the stability bound of SVRG in the following theorem, which improves upon the convex case (Theorem
4.1

(c-1)^{-(t-l)}
for the empirical error
\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l+1})]

\mu

\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};\mathbf{z})\rangle\geq\mu\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|^{2},~\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}.

This property helps establish a tighter estimate for
\mathbb{E}[\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}]

B.1.1

Theorem B.1

(Stability bounds)
.

Let
S,S^{(i)}
be defined in Definition
3.1
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}

\{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\}_{t\geq 1}

Assume that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}

\alpha
-smooth.

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{16\alpha m\eta^{2}}{(c-1)^{t}\,n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{(c-1)^{t-l}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l+1})\big].

Remark B.2
.

Theorem
B.1
shows that SVRG achieves optimal stability bounds of order

in the strongly convex case. To see this, consider
m=\lceil 3/(\eta\mu)\rceil
,

Plugging the above inequality into Theorem

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{m\eta^{2}}{2^{t}n}+\frac{m(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1+1/2^{l}}{2^{t-l}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{m\eta^{2}}{2^{t}n}+\frac{m\eta^{2}}{n}\Big(2+\frac{t}{2^{t}}\Big)\lesssim\frac{m\eta^{2}}{n}\asymp\frac{\eta}{\mu n}\leq\frac{1}{(\mu n)^{2}},

By the coercivity of
\ell
, we have

\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}.

(B.3)

Moreover, we know from
\mu
-strong convexity of
\ell
that

\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\rangle\geq\mu\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}.

(B.4)

Combining Eq. (
B.3
) and Eq. (
B.4
) gives

\displaystyle 2\eta\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{n-2}{n-1}+\frac{n}{n-1}\Big)\eta\langle\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)},\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}+\frac{n}{n-1}\eta\mu\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}.

Hence it follows from Eq. (
B.2
) that

\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}\leq\Big(1-\frac{n}{n-1}\eta\mu\Big)\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}-\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-\eta^{2}\Big)\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}.

(B.5)

Otherwise if
i_{k}=i
(with probability
1/n
), then
\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}=\mathbf{z}_{i}
and
\mathbf{z}_{i_{k}}^{(i)}=\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime}
. By Young’s inequality, for any
p>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}-\eta\big(\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+p)\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}+(1+1/p)\eta^{2}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\|^{2}.

(B.6)

Combining the two cases Eq. (
B.5
) and Eq. (
B.6
) leads to

\mathbb{E}\big[\|G_{k}-G_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq\Big(1+\frac{p}{n}-\eta\mu\Big)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\\
-\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]+\frac{1+1/p}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big].

(B.7)

Substituting Eq. (
B.7
) into Eq. (
B.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{p}{n}-\eta\mu\Big)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]-\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2+1/p}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big]+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}\big].

(B.8)

Next we analyze the outer loop. Recall notations
\mathbf{x}_{k}:=\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}
and
\mathbf{x}_{k}^{(i)}:=\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}
. Since
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
(resp.,
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}
) is uniformly chosen from
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}\}_{k=0}^{m-1}
(resp.,
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\}_{k=0}^{m-1}
), and
\mathbf{x}_{0}^{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}
(resp.,
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}
) from option II, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big].

(B.9)

Applying Eq. (
B.1.1
) once for each
k
in the summation term on the RHS of Eq. (
B.9
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]+\frac{1+p/n-\eta\mu}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{mn}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2+1/p}{mn}\eta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}\big].

(B.10)

For notational simplicity, we denote

\rho:=\frac{1}{m(\eta\mu-p/n)}.

Setting
p=\frac{n}{mt}
, we know from
c=m\eta\mu>2
and
t\geq 1
that

0<\frac{1}{c}<\rho=\frac{1}{c-1/t}\leq\frac{1}{c-1}<1.

(B.11)

For the second term on the RHS of Eq. (
B.10
), we have

\frac{1+p/n-\eta\mu}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1}\|^{2}\big]=\Big(1-\frac{1}{\rho m}\Big)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big].

(B.12)

Plugging Eq. (
B.12
) into Eq. (
B.10
) and rearranging gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho}{n}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{(2+mt/n)\rho}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{2\rho m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}\big].

(B.13)

To proceed, define the Lyapunov function
Q_{t}
via

Q_{t}:=\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}+\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}.

Applying Eq. (
B.13
) + Eq. (
4.12
)
\times 2m\eta^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t+1}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t}\big]-\left[\frac{\rho}{n}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)-\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\right]\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{j})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{(2+mt/n)\rho}{n}\eta^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big].

(B.14)

Since
\eta\leq\frac{n-2}{2\alpha(1+c)(n-1)}
, which together with Eq. (
B.11
) implies that

\frac{\rho}{n}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)>\frac{1}{cn}\Big(\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-2\eta^{2}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{cn}\big(2(1+c)\eta^{2}-2\eta^{2}\big)=\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}.

Then, dropping the nonnegative term in Eq. (
B.1.1
) leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t+1}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t}\big]+\left(\frac{(2+mt/n)\rho}{n}\eta^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{t+1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t}\big]+\frac{8\alpha(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1})\big]

\displaystyle=\rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t}\big]+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t+1})\big],

(B.15)

where we used
\rho<1
and Eq. (
4.16
) to derive the second inequality and the fact that
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
is uniformly chosen from
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}^{t+1}\}_{k=0}^{m-1}
for the equality.
Applying Eq. (
B.15
) recursively yields

\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t+1}\big]\leq\rho^{t}\,\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{1}\big]+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\rho^{t-l}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l+1})\big].

(B.16)

From
\mathbf{w}_{1}=\mathbf{w}_{1}^{(i)}
, we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{1}\big]=

\displaystyle\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{1};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{1};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2m\eta^{2}}{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{1};\mathbf{z}_{i})-\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{1};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{16\alpha m\eta^{2}}{n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}).

(B.17)

Plugging Eq. (
B.1.1
) into Eq. (
B.16
), together with Eq. (
B.11
), we conclude

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t+1}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16\alpha m\eta^{2}}{n}\Big(\frac{1}{c-1}\Big)^{t}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\Big(\frac{1}{c-1}\Big)^{t-l}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l+1})\big],

which completes the proof.
∎

B.1.2
Proof of EPR Bounds

We first recall the following convergence result from
[
22
, Theorem 1]
. It shows that SVRG converges linearly for strongly convex problems.

Theorem B.5

(Optimization error
[
22
]
)
.

Assume for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative,
\mu
-strongly convex, and
\alpha
-smooth.
Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
be produced by Algorithm
1
with option II applied to
S
. Denote
\mathbf{w}_{S}:=\operatorname{\arg\min}_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}L_{S}(\mathbf{w})
and
c:=m\eta\mu
. If
\eta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
and
c
is large enough such that

\rho:=\frac{1}{c(1-2\alpha\eta)}+\frac{2\alpha\eta}{1-2\alpha\eta}<1,

then for any
t\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}_{A}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big]\leq\rho^{t-1}(L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{1})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})).

Combining the stability bound from Theorem
B.1
and the convergence result from Theorem
B.5
, we are ready to prove the EPR bound for SVRG under strong convexity.

Proof of Theorem
B.3
.

Since
c\geq 3
, we have
\frac{1}{c-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then it follows from Theorem
B.1
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16\alpha m\eta^{2}}{2^{t-1}n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{2^{t-1-l}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l+1})\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16\alpha m\eta^{2}}{2^{t-1}n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+\frac{8\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})\big].

(B.18)

Applying Lemma
3.5
together with Eq. (
B.18
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big]\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big]+\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}\big]\\
\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big]+\frac{4(\alpha+\gamma)\alpha m(4+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})\big]+\frac{16(\alpha+\gamma)\alpha m\eta^{2}}{2^{t}n}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}).

(B.19)

We next bound the terms involving
\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})]
. Since
c\geq 3
and
\eta\leq\frac{1}{18\alpha}
, we know that

\frac{1}{c(1-2\alpha\eta)}+\frac{2\alpha\eta}{1-2\alpha\eta}\leq\frac{1}{3\cdot 8/9}+\frac{1/9}{8/9}=\frac{1}{2}.

Then it follows from Theorem
B.5
and
0\leq\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*})
that

\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})\big]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\leq\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big]\leq\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{l-1}},~\forall l\geq 1,

(B.20)

which together with
\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}=2(1-\frac{1}{2^{t}})\leq 2
further implies

\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{l})\big]\leq L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}+L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\sum_{l=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2^{t-l}}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{l-1}}\leq 2L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{tL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t-1}}.

(B.21)

Plugging Eq. (
B.20
) with
l=t
and Eq. (
B.21
) into Eq. (
B.19
) and omitting constant factors yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}}\Big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{tL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}}\Big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}n}.

(B.22)

Combining the generalization error Eq. (
B.22
) and the optimization error Eq. (
B.20
) with
l=t
gives

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\big]\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}}\Big)\\
+\frac{(1+\gamma)m(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{tL(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}}\Big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}n}+\frac{L(\mathbf{w}_{1})}{2^{t}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
B.4
.

We first verify the conditions required in Theorem
B.3
. It follows from
m\geq 3/(\eta\mu)
that
c=m\eta\mu\geq 3
. Additionally, from the choice of
\eta
and
\alpha\geq\mu
, we have

m\leq 3/(\eta\mu)+1=3(n+18\alpha/\mu)+1<3.5(n+18\alpha/\mu)=3.5/(\eta\mu),

which further gives
c=m\eta\mu<3.5
. This implies
\frac{n-2}{2\alpha(1+c)(n-1)}>\frac{1}{18\alpha}
considering
n\geq 3
, and hence we have

\eta=\frac{1}{\mu n+18\alpha}<\frac{1}{18\alpha}=\min\Big\{\frac{1}{18\alpha},\frac{n-2}{2\alpha(1+c)(n-1)}\Big\}.

Then we can apply Theorem
B.3
and use
L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\leq L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\lesssim 1
to obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\big]\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2^{t}}\Big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(1+\frac{t}{2^{t}}\Big)+\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}}{2^{t}n}+\frac{1}{2^{t}}.

(B.23)

To proceed, note from
\mu n>1
and the choices of
m,\eta
, and
t
that

\frac{mt}{n}\asymp\frac{t}{\eta\mu n}\asymp\frac{\mu n+18\alpha}{\mu n}\log_{2}(\mu n)<(1+18\alpha)\log_{2}(\mu n)\asymp\log_{2}(\mu n).

(B.24)

Applying Eq. (
B.24
) and the basic inequality
t\leq 2^{t}
, we derive

\frac{(1+\gamma)m(1+mt/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(1+\frac{t}{2^{t}}\Big)\lesssim\frac{\frac{\mu n}{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}\cdot\frac{1}{\eta\mu}\cdot\log_{2}(\mu n)}{n}\eta^{2}=\eta\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}{\mu n}.

(B.25)

Since
1\leq t\asymp\log_{2}(\mu n)
, we have
\frac{1}{\mu n}\asymp\frac{1}{2^{t}}\lesssim 1
. Therefore, we can bound the remaining terms on the RHS of Eq. (
B.23
) as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2^{t}}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}{\mu n},

(B.26)

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\gamma)m\eta^{2}}{2^{t}n}\lesssim\frac{\frac{\mu n}{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}\cdot\frac{1}{\eta\mu}\cdot\eta^{2}}{n}=\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}{\mu n},

(B.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{t}}\asymp\frac{1}{\mu n}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}{\mu n}.

(B.28)

Plugging Eqs. (
B.25
)–(
B.28
) into Eq. (
B.23
) gives

\mathbb{E}\big[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})\big]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\mu n)}}{\mu n},

which completes the proof.
∎

B.2

We proceed to study the behavior of SAGA for strongly convex problems. We first establish a refined stability bound for SAGA in the following theorem. Compared with the result in Theorem
6.1
, we have a potential contraction factor
(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t-k}
for the empirical error
\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{k})]
. The detailed proof is given in Appendix
B.2.1
.

Theorem B.6

(Stability bounds)
.

Let
S,S^{(i)}
be defined in Definition
3.1
. Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\}_{t\geq 1}
be the sequences produced by Algorithm
2
applied to
S
and
S^{(i)}
, respectively.
Assume that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative,
\mu
-strongly convex and
\alpha
-smooth.
If
\eta\leq\min\big\{\frac{1}{2\mu n},\frac{n-2}{6\alpha(n-1)}\big\}
, then for any
t\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{t}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t-k}\frac{8\alpha(6+t/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{k})]+32\alpha\eta^{2}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}).

Remark B.7
.

Similar to SVRG (see Remark
B.2
), SAGA also achieves optimal stability bounds of order
O(1/(\mu n))
in the strongly convex case, as indicated by Theorem
B.6
. To see this, we know from the analysis in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 1]
that if we set

\eta\leq\frac{1}{2\mu n+12\alpha}\leq\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2\mu n},\frac{n-2}{6\alpha(n-1)}\Big\},

then
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\lesssim(1-\eta\mu)^{k}
. Hence, from the
\alpha
-smoothness of
L_{S}
and optimality condition
\nabla L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})=0
, we further obtain

\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{k})]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\leq\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{k})-L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{k}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\lesssim(1-\eta\mu)^{k}.

Plugging the above inequality into Theorem
B.6
and choosing
t\asymp n
such that
1/t\leq\eta\mu/2
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{t}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t-k}\frac{1+t/n}{n}\eta^{2}\big[L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+(1-\eta\mu)^{k}\big]+\eta^{2}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t/n)\eta^{2}}{n}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\sum_{k=1}^{t}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t-k}+t(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}\Big)+\eta^{2}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t/n)\eta^{2}}{n}\Big(\frac{L(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{\eta\mu-1/t}+t\Big)+\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\leq\frac{(1+t/n)\eta^{2}}{n}\Big(\frac{2L(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{\eta\mu}+t\Big)+\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})\lesssim\frac{1}{(\mu n)^{2}}.

Combining the stability bound established in Theorem
B.6
and the existing convergence analysis of SAGA in the strongly convex case
[
14
]
, we obtain the following EPR bound. The proofs of Theorem
B.8
and Corollary
B.9
are given in Appendix
B.2.2
.

Theorem B.8

(Excess population risk)
.

Assume that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto\ell(\mathbf{w};\mathbf{z})
is nonnegative,
\mu
-strongly convex, and
\alpha
-smooth. Suppose
\mathbb{E}_{S}[\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]<\infty
and
n^{-1}\lesssim\mu
.
Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
be the sequence produced by Algorithm
2
applied to
S
with
\eta\leq\frac{1}{2\mu n+12\alpha}
.
Then for any
t>1/(\eta\mu)\asymp n
and
\gamma>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})]-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+(1-\eta\mu)^{t}/\mu\Big)\\
+\frac{(1+\gamma)(1+t/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\Big(\frac{L(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{\eta\mu-1/t}+t(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}/\mu\Big)+(1+\gamma)\eta^{2}L(\mathbf{w}_{1})+(1-\eta\mu)^{t}/\mu.

We then specify the parameters to derive EPR bounds of order
O\big(\log^{2/3}n/(\mu n)\big)
, which matches the minimax optimal rate
O\big(1/(\mu n)\big)
up to a logarithmic factor.

Corollary B.9
.

Let the assumptions in Theorem
B.8
hold and suppose
n^{-1}(\log n)^{2/3}\lesssim\mu
. Then we can take
t\asymp n\log n
,
\eta=\frac{1}{2\mu n+12\alpha}
, and
\gamma=\mu n/(\log n)^{2/3}
to derive

\mathbb{E}[L(\mathbf{w}_{t})-L(\mathbf{w}^{*})]\lesssim\frac{\log^{2/3}n}{\mu n}.

Proof of Stability Bounds

Proof of Theorem
B.6
.

We start by plugging Eq. (
A.2
) into Eq. (

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}-G_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]+\\

(B.29)

By similar arguments as in the derivation of Eq. (
B.7

\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}-G_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\leq(1+p/n-\eta\mu)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\\

(B.30)

Plugging Eq. (
B.30
) back to Eq. (
B.29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(1\!+\!\frac{p}{n}\!-\!\eta\mu\big)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}\!-\!\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]-\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{(n\!-\!2)\eta}{(n\!-\!1)\alpha}\!-\!2\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\!+\!\frac{2\!+\!1/p}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\|^{2}]\!+\!\frac{2\eta^{2}}{n}\!\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\!\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}].

(B.31)

To proceed, recall we have from the update rule of
\{\phi_{t+1,j}\}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t+1,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\phi_{t+1,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\!=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}\Big]\!+\!\frac{n\!-\!1}{n}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}\Big].

(B.32)

Multiplying both sides of Eq. (
B.32
) by
4\eta^{2}
and adding it to Eq. (
B.31
), we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}+4\eta^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t+1,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\phi_{t+1,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(1\!+\!\frac{p}{n}\!-\!\eta\mu\big)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}\!-\!\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\!-\!\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{(n\!-\!2)\eta}{(n\!-\!1)\alpha}\!-\!6\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\!+\!\frac{6\!+\!1/p}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\|^{2}]\!+\!\Big(4\!-\!\frac{2}{n}\Big)\eta^{2}\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\!\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}].

(B.33)

H_{t}

H_{t}:=\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\|^{2}+4\eta^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j};\mathbf{z}_{j})-\nabla\ell(\phi_{t,j}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(i)})\|^{2}.

From the condition

B.33

\mathbb{E}[H_{t+1}]\leq(1+p/n-\eta\mu)\mathbb{E}[H_{t}]\\
-\!\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{(n\!-\!2)\eta}{(n\!-\!1)\alpha}\!-\!6\eta^{2}\Big)\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{j})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}]\!+\!\frac{6\!+\!\frac{1}{p}}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t};\mathbf{z}_{i})\!-\!\nabla\ell(\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime})\|^{2}].

Since
\eta\leq\frac{n-2}{6\alpha(n-1)}

\frac{(n-2)\eta}{(n-1)\alpha}-6\eta^{2}\geq 0

Plugging Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^{(i)}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[H_{t+1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{t}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t-k}\frac{8\alpha(6+t/n)}{n}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[L_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{k})]+32\alpha\eta^{2}(1+1/t-\eta\mu)^{t}L(\mathbf{w}_{1}),

Proof of Corollary
B.9

With the given choices of parameters, we first show that

(1-\eta\mu)^{t}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}.

(B.45)

\eta=\frac{1}{2\mu n+12\alpha}

). Noting that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+(1-\eta\mu)^{t}/\mu\Big)\lesssim\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{\mu n}\Big(L(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{1}{\mu n}\Big)\lesssim\frac{(\log n)^{2/3}}{\mu n},

\displaystyle\lesssim

Plugging Eq. (
B.45
