Symptom burden, care pathways and treatment experiences of healthcare workers with post-COVID-19 syndrome (SCOPE-CARE): a mixed-methods study protocol
Authors: Mirja Baumgart, Laura Luedtke, Jonas Petter, Levente Kriston, Albert Nienhaus, Martin Härter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionHealthcare workers (HCWs) have been disproportionately affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, both as carers and as patients. Many individuals developed persistent symptoms following an acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, known as post-COVID-19 syndrome (PCS). Previous research indicates that a significant proportion of HCWs experience long-lasting and debilitating symptoms of PCS. The aim of this study is to investigate the longitudinal symptom burden as well as the care pathways and treatment experiences of PCS-affected HCWs insured by the German Social Accident Insurance Institution for Health and Welfare.Methods and analysisThe study uses a mixed-methods approach. The longitudinal survey will extend a cohort study of n=2436 HCWs (started in 2023) affected by PCS, adding two measurement points, set in 2025 (T3) and 2026 (T4). Additionally, interviews on care pathways and treatment experiences will be conducted with a subsample of n=60 PCS-affected HCWs. Another subsample of n=30 HCWs will be asked to participate in ecological momentary assessments of symptom burden, emotional well-being and coping strategies. Analyses of routinely collected data by the insurance company will complement the study data. The study is supported by an advisory board comprising PCS-affected HCWs who assist with the content of both qualitative and quantitative surveys.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Local Ethics Committee of the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf, Germany (LPEK-0909, 1 May 2025; amendment: LPEK-0954, 18 September 2025). Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic has imposed substantial physical and psychological burden on healthcare workers (HCWs), both as carers and as patients.1,3 Medical and nursing professionals experienced SARS-CoV-2 infections more frequently than the general population, particularly in the early phases of the pandemic.4 Until the end of June 2025, the German Statutory Accident Insurance (DGUV) had recognised over 370 000 cases of occupational diseases (ie, diseases that have been caused, worsened by or contracted in the work environment) caused by SARS-CoV-2 infections.5

A significant proportion of individuals infected with SARS-CoV-2 continue to experience or develop new symptoms beyond 12 weeks after the acute phase, which cannot be attributed to other infections.6 This condition is known as post-COVID-19 syndrome (PCS). Current literature estimates the prevalence of PCS ranging from 0.4% to 15%, depending on the study and vaccination status, with varying primary symptoms reported.7,9 The most common symptoms of PCS are fatigue, dyspnoea, neurocognitive impairments and sleep disturbances.10 11 Additionally, post-COVID-19 patients report heightened mental distress and psychological symptoms.12 As frontline workers in healthcare, PCS-affected HCWs combine clinical expertise and first-hand experiences of the healthcare system as both caregivers and service users. This provides them with a unique perspective on the challenges posed by these new syndromes.

In a preceding study, the course of PCS among HCWs, the sequelae of symptoms on activity, participation and quality of life as well as the need for care and rehabilitation in Germany have been investigated over a period of 12 months (“Long covid in health care workers” (LoCoVHCW)).13 For the baseline survey, a randomly selected sample of 20 000 HCWs insured by the German Social Accident Insurance Institution for Health and Welfare (Berufsgenossenschaft für Gesundheitsdienst und Wohlfahrtspflege (BGW)) with a confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection was contacted for the first time in May 2023, and contacted again for a second time in May 2024. Participants were asked about current post-COVID-19 symptoms, pre-existing conditions, the presence of typical post-COVID-19 symptoms before and during their acute infection, their use of healthcare and rehabilitation services, their mental health, quality of life, and levels of activities and participation and other patient-reported outcomes (PROMs) and patient-reported experience measures (PREMs). While the publications for LoCoVHCW are still in preparation, the final report of the study has been made available in German.14 A total of 2436 of the surveyed HCWs reported symptoms of PCS or having received a PCS diagnosis by a physician. Furthermore, the findings indicate a high level of self-reported psychological stress among participants, alongside a relatively low use of healthcare and rehabilitation services.15 However, the long-term progression of PCS, the care pathways followed by post-COVID-19 patients, and their experiences within the German healthcare and rehabilitation system remain largely unknown. Additionally, there is limited understanding of how post-COVID-19 patients perceive and evaluate their treatments and care processes.

Aims and objectives

The main objectives of the SCOPE-CARE study (“Symptom burden, care pathways and treatment experiences of healthcare workers with post-COVID-19 syndrome”) are to provide a deeper and comprehensive understanding of PCS symptom courses and their consequences, as well as care pathways and experiences of affected HCWs. In detail, the study aims at

Extending the existing dataset from the LoCoVHCW study by assessing the symptoms of PCS, as well as its course and characteristics, in HCWs in a 3-year longitudinal survey with four measurement points in total.

Gathering information on the consequences of PCS and psychosocial aspects (eg, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), activities and participation) and associations with sociodemographic and work-related variables (eg, age, gender and occupational field).

Methods and analysis

Study design

The SCOPE-CARE study (funding period: November 2024 to October 2027) is a mixed-methods expansion of the LoCoVHCW cohort study conducted between 2022 and 2024, including 3335 HCWs who reported a COVID-19 infection to the BGW. In the LoCoVHCW study, participating HCWs provided information about experienced COVID-19-related late sequelae in two surveys in 2023 (T1) and 2024 (T2), with 2436 of them reporting at least mild symptoms of PCS or a PCS diagnosis by a physician in 2023.

The study comprises four subprojects that follow a partially sequential design:

The 2436 HCWs from LoCoVHCW will be invited to two follow-up surveys in 2025 (T3) and 2026 (T4) (subproject 1).

Study setting

Subproject 1: survey

The 2436 HCWs from the LoCoVHCW study reporting at least mild symptoms of PCS or a PCS diagnosis were contacted again by the BGW in June 2025, informed about the study and invited to take part in the survey, interviews and/or EMA. Before the start of T3, the survey questionnaire was specified by a focus group interview. Survey participation was possible via an online questionnaire via REDCap (Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN, USA). A paper-pencil version was provided for participants preferring to fill out a paper version. Data from T3 were gathered during a 6-week period from June to August 2025. All T3 participants who consented to enter the draw had the chance to win one of 66 vouchers of €15. Survey participation will be anonymous to the research team. Drawing and shipping of the vouchers will be executed by the BGW.

During participation in T3, survey participants were informed about the possibility to take part in the EMA and/or interview subprojects. Interested participants were able to register proactively via the project website.

For T4, all 2436 HCWs will be contacted by the BGW again after 12 months and invited to participate during a 6-week period from June to August 2026.

Subproject 2: EMA

Between T3 and T4, a subsample of 30 patients with PCS was planned to report symptom experiences of PCS via EMA every 3 days over a period of 3 months. Based on estimated response rates, we anticipated inviting 150 individuals to achieve a complete dataset of 30 participants. A total of 102 participants registered until September 2025 for EMA participation. Of these, 46 participants provided informed consent for the linkage between the survey and EMA data. As the research team has no access to the list of study IDs kept by the administrative personnel, anonymity to the research team will be guaranteed throughout the study. Participants were screened for eligibility based on survey data. Three participants had to be excluded due to the lack of persistent symptoms of PCS (n=2) and the provision of two study IDs for the same participant (n=1), resulting in a final sample of 42 EMA participants taking part from the end of September to the end of December 2025. All participants received a remuneration in relation to the number of completed items, from a baseline of €40 to €360 for the complete EMA series.

Subproject 3: interviews

Following T3, semi-structured interviews will be conducted with 60 HCWs from September 2025 until summer 2026. As about 60 HCWs registered for interview participation, all registered HCWs who agree to be interviewed will be included. Participants will receive €50 remuneration for the 1-hour interview. Interviews will explore symptom progression, care pathways and patient experiences regarding care needs, facilitating factors and barriers in accessing care.

Subproject 4: routine data analysis

The secondary analysis of anonymised routine data is planned for Spring 2027. It will focus on costs concerning occupational disease reports from the BGW, specifically cases with documented virus detection (from 2020 onwards) and the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) codes U07.1 (COVID-19, virus detected) or U09.9 (post-COVID-19 condition, unspecified). It is planned to receive routine data from our cohort of 2436 HCWs comprising the years of the LoCoVHCW and SCOPE-CARE studies (2023–2026) to compare matching time spans.

The final expert workshop is planned to take place in autumn 2027. All quantitative and qualitative study results will be presented to and discussed with relevant stakeholders (members of the advisory board of affected HCWs and representatives of the BGW and healthcare insurers).

Patient and public involvement

The questionnaires for T3, T4, and the EMA, as well as the interview guidelines, have been or will be developed following a participatory research approach. To include the perspectives of PCS-affected HCWs, a patient advisory board had already been established through the regional representation of the BGW for the LoCoVHCW study. Of the five HCWs who have taken part in the initial advisory board, two have agreed to continue with SCOPE-CARE. Our goal was to establish an advisory board comprising five to eight PCS-affected HCWs. Six new members have been recruited through the regional representation of the BGW in Hamburg. We plan to hold eight advisory board meetings throughout the project duration. All members of the advisory board will receive a remuneration of €50 per hour each for their participation.

Eligibility criteria

Only participants who have already taken part in the LoCoVHCW study and reported at least mild symptoms of PCS or a PCS diagnosis will be included in the study. This results in a potential study population of 2436 HCWs (ie, nursing staff, physicians, occupational therapists, psychotherapists, physiotherapists, speech therapists and addiction counsellors) insured by the BGW with a SARS-CoV-2 infection registered until 14 February 2023 at the BGW.

Subproject 1: survey

Sample size

Our base sample is the 2436 HCWs reporting PCS from the LoCoVHCW study. Based on our experience with LoCoVHCW, we expect a 50% attrition rate at both survey points, resulting in a study sample of approximately n=1200 at T3 and n=600 at T4. A preliminary estimation of the necessary sample size, assuming a two-sided test, a correlation of 0.25 between repeated measures, a power of 95% and an alpha error probability of 5%, showed that complete data from 500 participants are sufficient to reveal a small standardised mean difference of Cohen’s d=0.2. As our study is exploratory and no specific hypothesis or predefined comparisons were available, the sample size calculation was intended as a general estimate. Therefore, a small effect size was chosen as a conservative assumption. For binary variables, this sample size is sufficient to reveal an OR of 1.4 in a McNemar test.

Focus group interviews

Before the start of T3, focus group interviews were conducted with members of the patient advisory board to further develop the survey and assess its validity. The aim of the focus group interview was to identify relevant topics and perspectives of the target population that had not yet been included and to check the comprehensibility and relevance of the planned survey items. Following a presentation of the planned survey, patient advisory board members were asked to provide feedback on each item of each instrument presented. The members subsequently gave feedback on the content and feasibility of the survey. The feedback was then analysed, discussed and integrated into the survey by the research team. The focus group interview was conducted via ZoomX and lasted 90 min. With consent from the advisory board, the focus group interview was audio-recorded; additionally, notes were taken during the interview to record non-verbal aspects. The interview data were analysed to identify missing concepts and opportunities for improvement regarding comprehensibility, comprehensiveness and practicability. The results were integrated into the survey by generating and adapting items.

Outcomes and measures

We will use several self-report scales following a list of consented PROMs and PREMs from the Federal Ministry of Health’s funding priority on long covid (https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/ministerium/ressortforschung/handlungsfelder/forschungsschwerpunkte/long-/post-covid.html). Our main outcome, post-COVID-19 symptom severity, will be measured with the post-COVID-19-score (PCS-score),16 complemented by the Fatigue Severity Scale.17 To assess psychological factors, items on depression and anxiety,18 psychosomatic symptoms19 20 and resilience,21 22 as well as psychosocial factors such as HRQoL,23,25 level of functioning26 27 and the ability to partake in social and occupational aspects of life28 are included. Based on the Questionnaire for health-related resource use in an elderly population (FIMA)29 and the consented list from the BMG funding initiative, items have been developed for the use of health and rehabilitation care and changes in the working conditions for LoCoVHCW. In coordination with other research projects from the Federal Ministry of Health’s funding priority, items have been further differentiated for SCOPE-CARE to assess the use and subjective evaluation of medical or therapeutic treatment, medication or instrument-based treatment. Data analysis

The T3 and T4 data will be integrated into the existing LoCoVHCW dataset using pseudonymised participant codes. Primary analyses will examine the prevalence of individual symptoms of PCS at each time point. Descriptive statistics will include means and SD for normally distributed continuous variables, medians and IQRs for non-normally distributed or ordinal variables, and frequencies for categorical variables.

To investigate the course of symptoms of PCS, we will estimate linear mixed models for continuous variables (PCS-score, depression and anxiety) and logistic mixed models for binary variables (eg, individual symptoms of PCS), accounting for the nested structure of repeated measurements within participants. To examine the course of psychosocial variables (HRQoL, level of functioning and work ability) and their association with symptoms of PCS, we will estimate additional linear mixed models. Models evaluating changes in psychosocial outcomes over time will include time and relevant covariates (eg, age, gender and profession) as predictors. To understand the relationship to symptoms of PCS, the symptoms assessed as part of the PCS-score will furthermore be included as predictors. Complex associations between various domains and variables will be investigated with multivariable and multivariate methods such as cross-lagged panel modelling and network analysis.

Missing data will be handled under the missing-at-random assumption inherent to mixed-model estimation. Patterns of missingness will be examined descriptively. For all models, standardised mean differences (Cohen’s d) will be used for effect size estimation in continuous outcomes, and ORs will be calculated for binary variables. Statistical significance will be set at p<0.05, and all estimates will be reported with 95% CIs. Analyses will be conducted in R (R Foundation for Statistical Computiong, Vienna, Austria) (V.4.5.2) using the package lme4. Results will be reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement regarding observational studies.30

Subproject 2: EMA

The primary goal of EMA is to capture symptom experiences in real time via REDCap, thereby reducing recall bias. During the 3-month period, 30 participants have been contacted every 3 days via e-mail, that is, 30 times in total. This sample size is in line with recommendations for multilevel analysis and network analysis.31 32 Time of contact was set at 08:00 for each of the 30 assessment points. Participants could then choose to complete the survey for each assessment point within a 3-day window until the start of the next assessment period. Real-time symptoms of PCS over a period of 3 months are the main outcome of the EMA. Additional measures of interest have been discussed with the advisory board and include emotional well-being, psychological distress, coping activities and personality. The advisory board considered the EMA as overall very comprehensive, and it was acknowledged that the cognitive demands might be challenging for some participants. However, the repeated assessments were considered important, and they recommended not to leave out any measurement points or items, as they were considered relevant and appropriate. The EMA data will be linked with the survey data. Primary analyses will use multilevel vector autoregressive (mlVAR) models to examine the interplay between symptoms over time.33 34 Specifically, all symptoms at time point t will be modelled as outcomes and predicted by all symptoms at time point t−1, following a standard mlVAR approach. This allows an estimation of three complementary network structures: (a) a temporal network capturing directed associations between symptoms from a time point to the next, (b) a contemporaneous network reflecting partial correlations between symptoms within the same measurement occasion after accounting for temporal effects, and (c) a between-person network representing associations between the mean symptom levels of participants across the observation period. The multilevel framework estimates both fixed effects reflecting average within-person and between-person associations and random effects as estimates of individual deviations from these averages. Network estimation will be conducted in R using the mlVAR package. Reported results will include network visualisations, centrality indices, global connectivity measures and 95% bootstrap CIs. In subsequent analyses, the role of additional variables (eg, personality or demographic characteristics) will be examined. Depending on the nature of the variables, this will be done either by including them as additional nodes in the network or by estimating moderated network models35 to assess whether symptom associations vary as a function of these variables.

Subproject 3: semi-structured interviews

In the semi-structured interviews, symptom burden, care needs, access to care, care experiences and barriers and resources at all levels of medical and therapeutic care for PCS-affected persons will be explored. Interviews with HCWs who report physical and/or psychological symptoms will be questioned about symptom burden, access to care, care pathways and treatment experiences in general and specifically regarding the treatment of psychological symptoms. To obtain a comprehensive understanding of the experiences of HCWs until thematic saturation is reached, we plan to conduct 60 interviews with HCWs reporting somatic and/or psychological symptoms.36 The interview guidelines will be developed with guidance from the advisory board and tested with PCS-affected HCWs beforehand. For interview transcription an artificial intelligence (AI) tool compliant with data protection regulations will be used. After the first transcription by the AI tool, all transcripts will be thoroughly checked by two team members.

The interviews will be analysed using qualitative content analysis with MAXQDA software (VERBI Software. Consult. Sozialforschung GmbH, Berlin, Germany).

Subproject 4: routine data analysis

The dataset from the BGW includes 89 documented types of insurance benefits and services, categorised into eight groups: outpatient treatment, inpatient medical treatment, injury benefits, medications and therapeutic aids, pensions, vocational rehabilitation and assistive devices, reports and assessment procedures (including verification tests and expert opinions). These categories will be analysed descriptively using absolute and relative frequencies or dispersion measures. Findings from routine data will be compared with self-reported patient survey data to enhance the understanding of healthcare use patterns by patients with PCS.

Mixed-methods integration of the subprojects’ findings

The SCOPE-CARE study uses a mixed-methods approach by combining both quantitative and qualitative methods to achieve the study aims. We aim to continuously integrate new findings from current subprojects into subsequent subprojects. Preliminary findings from T3 have been used to develop the EMA surveys. Insights from qualitative interviews will be used to modify the T4 survey in order to better address the study aims. Routine data will be complemented by self-report from the surveys and interviews.

Analyses for each subproject will be carried out separately. At the interpretation stage, quantitative and qualitative results will be integrated. The findings will be discussed in our final expert workshop with affected HCWs and other stakeholders to generate inferences for the improvement of PCS care.

Discussion

This mixed-methods study aims to collect longitudinal data on the symptom progression, care pathways and treatment experiences of HCWs with PCS in Germany. The findings of the LoCoVHCW study13 indicate a substantial psychosocial burden among affected individuals.14 In particular, severely affected individuals continue to experience persistent symptoms despite medical interventions. Consistent with previous results from LoCoVHCW, we expect an ongoing discrepancy between reported symptoms and impairments and the actual use of rehabilitative or other treatment measures.

The combination of qualitative and quantitative methods will provide a comprehensive understanding of the experiences and needs of PCS-affected HCWs. Our study follows the symptoms of PCS over a total period of 3 years, offering an opportunity to gather longitudinal data on PCS within this unique sample of HCWs. Additionally, EMA will provide insights into how symptoms evolve and affect HCWs in their daily lives. Through interviews with HCWs experiencing psychological or physical symptoms, we aim to gain a deeper understanding of the healthcare structures used by patients with PCS and their care needs. However, some limitations have to be taken into account regarding the representativeness of the study. Due to voluntary study participation, selection bias may occur, as either less or very strongly affected HCWs might be less likely to participate. We are addressing this potential bias by providing a short survey and a non-responder analysis. Nevertheless, the findings of the study can provide valuable insights by enhancing the understanding of PCS trajectories in the respective German healthcare system. They may serve as a foundation for developing targeted interventions to optimise healthcare services, reduce the burden on affected individuals, and contribute towards reducing long-term healthcare costs. By focussing on HCWs, the study can contribute to help promote the health of essential staff in times of staff shortage and thereby strengthening public health.

Ethics and dissemination

The study design was approved by the Local Ethics Committee of Center for Psychosocial Medicine at the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (No. LPEK-0909) in May 2025, prior to data collection. The study has been registered on 9 May 2025 with the German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS), ID DRKS00035957. The registration is updated regularly during the course of the project. Participation in all subprojects of the study is voluntary, and informed consent is a prerequisite for participation. In the anonymous survey, participants must actively provide consent for study participation and data processing by selecting a mandatory field in the online questionnaire. For the interviews and EMA, participants are required to register their wish to participate proactively and give written consent prior to participation. An amendment to permit the linkage of EMA and survey data was approved in September 2025 (No. LPEK-0954), prior to EMA data collection. We plan to present the findings from the SCOPE-CARE study at relevant conferences. The publishing of these findings in open-access journals is anticipated.
