Statistical Characterization of Wind-Induced Beam Refraction in Water-to-Air Optical Channels
M. Nennouche, I. C. Ijeh, M. A. Khalighi

Abstract
Direct water-to-air (W2A) optical communications experience strong beam refraction at the dynamic sea surface. This letter proposes a novel and tractable statistical channel model for a vertical W2A link between an underwater node and an unmanned aerial vehicle under varying wind speeds, modeling wind-induced pointing errors with a Beta mixture fitted via the Expectation–Maximization algorithm. By accounting for link interruptions due to total internal reflection (TIR) and receiver field-of-view limitations, we derive closed-form expressions for the channel distribution and link outage probability. Our analysis reveals a fundamental TIR-induced outage floor limiting link reliability and providing insight for robust W2A system design.



I
Introduction

Underwater communications are attracting growing interest, driven by the rapid expansion of the
internet of underwater things (IoUT)
across diverse applications, ranging from industrial pipeline inspection to environmental coral reef monitoring
[
6
,
8
]
. These use-cases require data rates and bandwidths beyond the capabilities of conventional acoustic systems. Consequently,
underwater wireless optical communications (UWOCs)
have emerged as a compelling alternative, enabling high-speed data transmission over distances of several tens of meters
[
6
]
.
In
UWOC
systems, retrieving data to the surface for remote processing remains a key challenge. The most common approach relies on surface platforms, such as buoys or autonomous surface vehicles
[
8
]
, introducing additional cost and environmental impact. As a result, direct
water-to-air (W2A)
communication has recently gained significant traction
[
8
]
. This approach, however, introduces increased channel complexity due to the dynamic nature of the sea surface. Wave-induced random refraction and reflection
[
8
,
12
]
make accurate modeling of the direct
W2A
channel highly challenging, yet such modeling is essential for ensuring reliable communication.

Several recent studies have focused on modeling the direct
W2A
channel, primarily through
Monte-Carlo (MC)
simulations, developing realistic simulators that incorporate various sea surface models and underwater impairments, including absorption and scattering by suspended particles, as well as the effects of air bubbles generated by wave breaking
[
12
,
7
,
1
]
. To mitigate the high computational cost of
MC
-based simulations, recent works have proposed statistical channel models for both
W2A

[
2
]
and
air-to-water (A2W)

[
14
]
links.

This work aligns with this ongoing research effort by providing a comprehensive modeling framework. We propose a model for optical beam refraction at the sea surface as a function of wind speed. In particular, we derive a novel closed-form and tractable expression to estimate the
pointing error (PE)
loss using a Beta mixture distribution, whose parameters are linked to wind speed. Furthermore, the link outage probability is evaluated, accounting for
total internal reflection (TIR)
. Our analytical results are validated through extensive
MC
simulations, enabling a detailed analysis of the outage performance.

II
Link Geometry and System Model

We consider a vertical
W2A
link between a base station equipped with a
laser diode (LD)
at depth
z_{w}
, and an
unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)
hovering at altitude
z_{a}
above the sea surface, equipped with a
photodetector (PD)
with
field-of-view (FoV)

\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}
. Perfect alignment between the
transmitter (Tx)
and
receiver (Rx)
is assumed, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. We consider a dynamic sea surface, with an incident angle
\theta_{\mathrm{I}}
defined between the surface normal
\vec{n}
and the
LD
beam, which is refracted to an angle
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}
with respect to the vertical axis, referred hereafter to as the
angle-of-arrival (AoA)
.

Fig. 1:
Illustration of the vertical water-to-air (W2A) optical wireless communication system model.

We employ
non-return-to-zero on-off keying (NRZ-OOK)
modulation, where a high transmit power
P_{\mathrm{Tx},1}
is assigned to ‘1’ bits and a low level
P_{\mathrm{Tx},0}=\xi P_{\mathrm{Tx},1}
to ‘0’ bits, with
\xi
denoting the extinction ratio. At the
Rx
, a high-sensitivity
silicon photo-multiplier (SiPM)
is employed to enhance the transmission range, followed by a
trans-impedance amplifier (TIA)
, and a
low-pass filter (LPF)
to limit the Rx noise level.

The overall channel loss, denoted by
h
, is formulated as:

h=h_{\mathrm{L}}h_{\mathrm{P}}h_{\mathrm{A}},

(1)

where
h_{\mathrm{L}}
represents the path loss,
h_{\mathrm{P}}
is the
PE
-induced loss, and
h_{\mathrm{A}}
accounts for the link interruption loss.
Oceanic turbulence-induced scintillation is neglected in this study, as the large
Rx
aperture (i.e., the
PD
lens) significantly reduces irradiance fluctuations through aperture averaging
[
4
]
. Furthermore, given the short in-air propagation distance and the broadened beam footprint over the vertical underwater segment, the effects of atmospheric turbulence and underwater beam wandering are considered negligible compared to the dominant surface-induced refractions.

III
Water-to-Air Refraction Effect Modeling

Assuming a static
Tx
and a
UAV
equipped with an attitude control system ensuring stable hovering
[
9
]
, the dominant source of
PEs
is the random motion of the sea surface. This results in fluctuations in the radial displacement
r_{d}
at the
Rx
plane. The resulting
PE
loss
h_{\mathrm{P}}
can be approximated as
[
4
]
:

h_{\mathrm{P}}\approx A_{0}\exp\left(-2r_{d}^{2}/\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}^{2}\right),

(2)

where
A_{0}=\left(\mathrm{erf}(v)\right)^{2}
represents the maximum fraction of received power (i.e., for
r_{d}=0
), with
\mathrm{erf}(.)
denoting the error function and
v=\left(D_{r}\sqrt{\pi}\right)/\left(2\sqrt{2}~\omega_{L}\right)
. Here,
D_{r}
is the
PD
lens diameter,
\omega_{L}
is the beam waist at the total propagation distance
L=z_{w}+z_{a}
, and
\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}
denotes the equivalent beam width at the
Rx
:

\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}=\omega_{L}\sqrt{\sqrt{\pi}~\mathrm{erf}(v)/\left[2v\exp\left(-v^{2}\right)\right]}.

(3)

The
probability density function (PDF)
of
h_{\mathrm{P}}
is then:

f_{h_{\mathrm{P}}}(h_{\mathrm{P}})=\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}^{2}}{4h_{\mathrm{P}}\sqrt{-\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}^{2}}{2}\ln\left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{P}}}{A_{0}}\right)}}f_{r_{d}}\left(\sqrt{-\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{Leq}}^{2}}{2}\ln\left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{P}}}{A_{0}}\right)}\right),

(4)

where
f_{r_{d}}(\cdot)
denotes the
PDF
of
r_{d}
, which depends on the
AoA

\theta_{\mathrm{A}}
. Given that the beam deviation occurs at the surface, and assuming
x_{\mathrm{Tx}}=y_{\mathrm{Tx}}=x_{\mathrm{Rx}}=y_{\mathrm{Rx}}=0
, the radial displacement can be expressed geometrically as
r_{d}=z_{a}\tan\left(\theta_{\mathrm{A}}\right)
.

III-A

AoA Statistical Modeling

Before modeling the
AoA
, it is necessary to establish an accurate statistical model for the incident angle at the sea surface,
\theta_{\mathrm{I}}
, a topic that has recently received considerable attention. Prior studies have modeled the instantaneous surface motion using distributions such as Rayleigh
[
2
]
and, more recently, Weibull
[
5
]
. Owing to its model consideration of the spatio-temporal correlations of wave dynamics, and agreement with experimental sea measurements, the Weibull distribution is adopted in this work, with the corresponding
PDF
given by:

f_{\theta_{\mathrm{I}}}(\theta_{\mathrm{I}})=\frac{k_{U}}{\lambda_{U}}\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{I}}}{\lambda_{U}}\right)^{k_{U}-1}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{I}}}{\lambda_{U}}\right)^{k_{U}}\right],

(5)

where
k_{U}=1.7454+0.0071\,U
and
\lambda_{U}=13.6485+0.2406\,U
are the
PDF
shape and scale parameters, respectively, valid for wind speeds
U\in[6,15]
m/s.

Let
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}
denote the transmission angle relative to the surface normal
\vec{n}
, see Fig.
1
. The
AoA
can then be expressed as
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}=\theta_{\mathrm{T}}-\theta_{\mathrm{I}}
. The angle
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}
is obtained via Snell’s law as
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}=\arcsin\left(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}\sin(\theta_{\mathrm{I}})\right)
, where
n_{1}\approx 1.33
and
n_{2}\approx 1
are the refractive indices of water and air, respectively
[
10
]
.

Due to the highly non-linear nature of this geometric transformation, deriving a tractable, closed-form
PDF
for
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}
is mathematically prohibitive. Therefore, we opt for a robust statistical approximation.
Using random numerical sampling for probability estimation, we evaluated several candidate distributions, including Rayleigh, Nakagami, and Weibull. The results, shown in Figs.
2a
,
2b
, and
2c
, for
U=6
,
10
, and
14
m/s, show that
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}
is best approximated by a Weibull distribution with shape and scale parameters denoted by
k_{\mathrm{A}}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}
, respectively.
Furthermore, regression analysis indicates that, over the considered wind-speed range,
the shape parameter remains approximately constant at
k_{\mathrm{A}}\approx 1.60
, while
\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}
scales linearly with
U
, such that
\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}\approx 4.7473+0.0957\,U
.

III-B

PE Modeling

The
PDF
of
r_{d}
can be obtained from (
5
) by applying a change of variable
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}=\arctan(r_{d}/z_{a})
:

f_{r_{d}}(r_{d})=\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}^{2}+r_{d}^{2}}\frac{k_{\mathrm{A}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\left(\frac{\arctan(r_{d}/z_{a})}{\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\right)^{k_{\mathrm{A}}-1}\\
\times\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\arctan(r_{d}/z_{a})}{\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\right)^{k_{\mathrm{A}}}\right).

(6)

By substituting (
6
) into (
4
), the exact closed-form expression for
f_{h_{\mathrm{P}}}(\cdot)
can be obtained. Nevertheless, due to the complexity of resulting composite non-linear functions (e.g., exponentials of arctangents), this formulation is analytically intractable. It prevents the derivation of essential closed-form performance metrics, such as outage probability, which is critical for robust
W2A
link design.
Here, we propose a novel statistical modeling approach based on a finite mixture model, optimized via the
expectation maximization (EM)
algorithm
[
11
]
. The EM algorithm is an iterative technique that alternates between estimating the unobserved component responsibilities (E-step) and updating the distribution parameters to maximize the overall log-likelihood (M-step).

To improve convergence of the EM algorithm and to obtain a generalized model independent of the
Rx
aperture size, we apply the
EM
algorithm to the normalized
PE
loss
h_{\mathrm{P,N}}=h_{\mathrm{P}}/A_{0}
.
Based on extensive evaluations, we propose the use of a
Beta mixture model (BMM)
to accurately characterize the
PDF
of
h_{\mathrm{P,N}}
, expressed as:

f_{h_{\mathrm{P,N}}}(h_{\mathrm{P,N}})\approx\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_{i}\frac{h_{\mathrm{P,N}}^{\alpha_{i}-1}(1-h_{\mathrm{P,N}})^{\beta_{i}-1}}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})},\quad 0\leq h_{\mathrm{P,N}}\leq 1,

(7)

where
w_{i}
denotes the mixing weight of the
i
-th component (with
w_{1}+w_{2}=1
), and
\mathrm{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the standard Beta function with
\alpha_{i}>0
and
\beta_{i}>0
as shape parameters.

) is the

is assessed for two
UAV

z_{a}=5

Although the
PDF
shapes vary significantly with altitude, our proposed
BMM
consistently adapts, providing a highly accurate fit in both scenarios. Furthermore, it is evident that at higher altitudes, the probability of severe fading (
h_{\mathrm{P}}\to 0
) increases substantially with wind speed.

Lastly, it is worth mentioning that, in (
7
), the summation is restricted to two components (
i=1,2
), as this provides sufficient accuracy. This can be justified by the bimodal nature of the distribution, with one component capturing the
PDF
peak near
0
and the other the peak near
1
(see Fig.
3
).

III-C

Link Interruption

The extreme effect of PEs is complete link interruption, which can result from two sequential physical mechanisms:
TIR
at the
W2A
interface and geometric misalignment when the refracted beam falls outside the
Rx

FoV
.
The link interruption coefficient
h_{\mathrm{A}}
takes values of
0
or
1
, and is modeled as
h_{\mathrm{A}}=h_{\mathrm{A,F}}h_{\mathrm{A,T}}
. Here,
h_{\mathrm{A,F}}=\Pi\left(\theta_{\mathrm{A}}/\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}\right)
accounts for
FoV
-induced interruption, where
\Pi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq 1
, and
0
otherwise. Also,
h_{\mathrm{A,T}}=\Pi(\theta_{\mathrm{I}}/\theta_{c})
models interruption due to
TIR
, which occurs when
\theta_{\mathrm{I}}
exceeds the critical angle
\theta_{c}=\arcsin\left(n_{2}/n_{1}\right)=48.75^{\circ}

[
10
]
.

Since
\theta_{\mathrm{I}}
follows a Weibull distribution defined by parameters
(k_{U},\lambda_{U})
, the probability of
TIR
is analytically given by the complementary
cumulative distribution function (CDF)
:

P_{\mathrm{TIR}}=\Pr(\theta_{\mathrm{I}}>\theta_{c})=\exp\Big(-\bigg(\frac{\theta_{c}}{\lambda_{U}}\bigg)^{k_{U}}\Big).

(8)

Conditioned on successful transmission across the interface, the absolute deviation angle
\theta_{\mathrm{A}}
follows the previously established Weibull distribution with parameters
(k_{\mathrm{A}},\lambda_{\mathrm{A}})
. Consequently, the probability that the beam is captured by the
PD
is given by the
CDF
evaluated at
\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}
:

P_{\mathrm{cap}}=\Pr(\theta_{\mathrm{A}}\leq\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}})=1-\exp\Big(-\bigg(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\bigg)^{k_{\mathrm{A}}}\Big).

(9)

Assuming these two events occur sequentially, the overall probability of maintaining an active link is given by the product of their respective probabilities. Accordingly, the total link interruption probability, denoted by
P_{\mathrm{int}}
, is formulated as:

P_{\mathrm{int}}=1-\left[\bigg(1-e^{-\left(\frac{\theta_{c}}{\lambda_{U}}\right)^{k_{U}}}\bigg)\left(1-e^{-\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{A}}}\right)^{k_{\mathrm{A}}}}\right)\right].

(10)

It can be observed from (
10
) that the first term is independent of the transceiver parameters, thereby imposing a fundamental lower bound on the system’s outage probability. This inherent physical limit motivates the adoption of mitigation strategies, such as spatial diversity, to further enhance the link reliability.

Based on (
10
), the
PDF
of
h_{\mathrm{A}}
can be expressed as:

f_{h_{\mathrm{A}}}(h_{\mathrm{A}})=P_{\mathrm{int}}\,\delta(h_{\mathrm{A}})+\left(1-P_{\mathrm{int}}\right)\delta\left(h_{\mathrm{A}}-1\right),

(11)

where
\delta(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta function.

IV
Link Outage Performance

As explained in Section
II
, the overall channel coefficient is expressed as
h=h_{\mathrm{L}}h_{\mathrm{P}}h_{\mathrm{A}}
. Since the link interruption coefficient
h_{\mathrm{A}}\in\{0,1\}
acts as a Bernoulli random variable, the
PDF
of
h
can be formulated as a mixed-type distribution:

f_{h}(h)=P_{\mathrm{int}}\,\delta(h)+\left(1-P_{\mathrm{int}}\right)f_{c}(h),

(12)

where
f_{c}(h)
denotes the
PDF
of
h_{c}\triangleq h_{\mathrm{L}}h_{\mathrm{P}}
.

To formulate
h_{\mathrm{L}}
, we assume that atmospheric attenuation over the relatively short in-air segment is negligible, such that the overall path loss is dominated by the underwater segment. This attenuation is due to absorption, scattering, and wave-generated air bubbles
[
3
]
. Accordingly:

h_{\mathrm{L}}=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{z_{w}}c_{w}(\lambda,z)dz\right),

(13)

where
\lambda
and
z
denote wavelength and depth, respectively, and
c_{w}(\lambda,z)=a(\lambda)+b(\lambda)+b_{\mathrm{bub}}(z,U)
denotes the attenuation coefficient. Here,
a(\lambda)
,
b(\lambda)
, and
b_{\mathrm{bub}}(z,U)
represent the absorption coefficient, the scattering coefficient, and the bubble-induced scattering coefficient, respectively
[
12
,
3
]
. To calculate
b_{\mathrm{bub}}
, we use the
Hall-Novarini (HN)
model
[
12
,
3
]
:

b_{\mathrm{bub}}=Q_{\mathrm{sca}}\Psi~N_{b}(z),

(14)

where
Q_{\mathrm{sca}}=2.0
and
\Psi\approx 3\pi r_{\mathrm{min}}^{2}
represent the mean scattering efficiency and the mean geometric cross-sectional area of the bubble population, respectively. Also,
N_{b}(z)
represents the bubble density at depth
z
, approximated as
[
12
]
:

N_{b}(z)\approx(1.6\times 10^{10})\frac{r_{\mathrm{ref}}^{4}}{3\,r_{\mathrm{min}}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{U}{13}\bigg)^{3}\exp\left[-\frac{z}{L(U)}\right].

(15)

Here,
r_{\mathrm{min}}=1\,\mu\mathrm{m}
and
r_{\mathrm{ref}}=54.4\,\mu\mathrm{m}+1.984\times 10^{-6}z
denote the minimum and reference air bubble radii, respectively. Lastly,
L(U)
represents the e-folding distance, defined as:

L(U)=\begin{cases}0.4,&U\leq 7.5\ \mathrm{m}/\mathrm{s},\\
0.4+0.115\left(U-7.5\right),&U>7.5\ \mathrm{m}/\mathrm{s}.\end{cases}

(16)

Returning to
h_{c}
, and exploiting the normalized PE model in (
7
) while incorporating
h_{\mathrm{L}}
and
A_{0}
, its PDF is given by:

f_{h_{c}}(x)=\frac{1}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_{i}\frac{\left(\frac{x}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}-1}\left(1-\frac{x}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\right)^{\beta_{i}-1}}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})},

(17)

which is valid for
0\leq x\leq h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}
.
Substituting (
17
) into (
12
), and applying the identities in
[
13
, Eq. (8.4.2.3)]
and
[
13
, Eq. (8.2.2.15)]
, allows simplification of the fractional binomial terms. The exact closed-form
PDF
of the overall channel
h
for
0\leq h\leq h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}
is thus obtained as:

f_{h}(h)=P_{\mathrm{int}}\delta(h)+\frac{1-P_{\mathrm{int}}}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_{i}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\\
\times G_{1,1}^{1,0}\left(\frac{h}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}-1\\
\alpha_{i}-1\end{array}\right.\right),

(18)

Here,
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function and
G_{p,q}^{m,n}(\cdot|\cdot)
the Meijer G-function
[
13
, Eq. (8.2.1.1)]
. Using this PDF, we can evaluate the link outage performance.
By definition, an outage occurs when the instantaneous
bit error rate (BER)
exceeds a target
BER
.
This condition is equivalent to considering a threshold on the channel gain
h_{\mathrm{th}}

[
4
]
, allowing the outage probability
P_{\mathrm{out}}
to be obtained via the
CDF
of
h
as:

P_{\mathrm{out}}(h_{\mathrm{th}})=\Pr(h\leq h_{\mathrm{th}})=\int_{0}^{h_{\mathrm{th}}}f_{h}(h)\,dh.

(19)

By substituting (
18
) into (
19
) and applying the integration property of the Meijer G-function
[
13
, Eq. (2.24.2.2)]
, the closed-form expression of
P_{\mathrm{out}}
can be obtained as:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}(h_{\mathrm{th}})

\displaystyle=P_{\mathrm{int}}+(1-P_{\mathrm{int}})\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_{i}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{th}}}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times G_{2,2}^{1,1}\left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{th}}}{h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}0,\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}-1\\
\alpha_{i}-1,-1\end{array}\right.\right),

which is strictly valid for
0\leq h_{\mathrm{th}}\leq h_{\mathrm{L}}A_{0}
.

Considering a transceiver configuration with an
LD
of half-divergence angle
\theta_{0}=0.05
rad at
\lambda=450
nm and
P_{\mathrm{Tx,1}}=50
mW, employing NRZ-OOK signaling with
\xi=0.2
, and a SensL B-series MicroSB 30020
SiPM
at the Rx
[
4
]
, along with coastal water conditions characterized by
a(\lambda)=0.0088~\text{m}^{-1}
and
b(\lambda)=0.216~\text{m}^{-1}

[
6
]
, the threshold
h_{\mathrm{th}}
is determined considering a target
BER
of
10^{-3}
using the relation provided in
[
4
, Eq. (A4)]
.

Figure
4
illustrates
P_{\mathrm{out}}
versus wind speed for various
Tx
depths,
Rx
altitudes, and
FoVs
, providing several key insights. First, the outage floor
P_{\mathrm{int}}
, Eq. (
10
), governed by
U
and
\theta_{\mathrm{FoV}}
, imposes an irreducible lower bound on
P_{\mathrm{out}}
in (
IV
). Furthermore, increasing the
UAV
altitude significantly degrades performance by exacerbating
PEs
, as previously observed in Fig.
3
. Conversely, increasing the
Tx
depth from
10
m to
30
m counter-intuitively enhances the outage performance. This behavior arises from a beam-spreading trade-off: while increasing depth leads to higher attenuation
h_{\mathrm{L}}
, it significantly enlarges the beam footprint at the
Rx
plane. This larger footprint mitigates wave-induced PEs. Owing to the high sensitivity of the
SiPM
, this geometric stabilization outweighs the attenuation penalty, resulting in a more resilient link.

V
Concluding Remarks

We presented a tractable statistical channel model for vertical
W2A
optical links under dynamic sea-surface refraction, characterizing
PEs
via an
EM
-fitted
BMM
and deriving exact closed-form
P_{\mathrm{out}}
accounting for
TIR
and Rx
FoV
limits. Our analysis reveals a fundamental
TIR
-induced outage floor that inherently restricts system reliability. It also shows that
UAV
altitude degrades performance significantly more than Tx depth. Ultimately, this framework serves as an efficient tool for designing robust underwater data harvesting systems. Ongoing work aims to relate physical link parameters with
BMM
parameters to derive a fully generalized analytical model.
