Essentially commuting projections onto shift-invariant subspaces
Rounak Biswas, Srijan Sarkar

Abstract
. In this article, using Halmos’ two projections theorem, we completely characterize the essential commutativity of the orthogonal projections onto the shift-invariant subspaces φ 1 ​ H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) \varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}) and φ 2 ​ H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) \varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}) of the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}) via local conditions on the inner functions φ 1 \varphi_{1} and φ 2 \varphi_{2} . Finite-rank commutators [ P φ 1 , P φ 2 ] [P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}] are also characterized. Using our methods, we connect the essential commutativity with the Fredholmness of the projections ( P φ 1 , P φ 2 ) (P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}) as introduced by Avron, Seiler and Simon. Applications include refining existing conditions for compactness of truncated Toeplitz operators corresponding to inner symbols and thereby, characterizing the compactness of certain contractions using the Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory. We conclude with several characterizations on the polydisc.

1.
Introduction

In this article, our primary goal is to study the essential commutativity of pairs of orthogonal projections onto shift-invariant closed subspaces of Hardy spaces. Recall that an orthogonal projection
P
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator satisfying the idempotent condition, that is
P^{2}=P
. A pair of orthogonal projections
(P,Q)
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be essentially commuting if the commutator
[P,Q]
defined by

[P,Q]:=PQ-QP,

is a compact operator. Essential commutativity of projections is an active topic of study
[
4
,
5
]
. We are interested in studying projections that are crucial for connecting operator theory and complex function theory. In particular, the projections are onto invariant subspaces of the Hardy space over unit disc
\mathbb{D}
denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Recall that

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}):=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}):\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(rz)|^{2}\,d\mu<\infty\},

where
\mu
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
and

\|f\|:=\big(\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(rz)|^{2}\,d\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad(f\in H^{2}).

The collection of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
are contained inside
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and is denoted
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. It is a well-known phenomenon that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is isometrically isomorphic to
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and this correspondence gives rise to a huge collection of bounded operators coming from functions in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. More precisely, corresponding to a
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the Toeplitz opearator
T_{\varphi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by
T_{\varphi}f=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}L_{\varphi}|_{H^{2}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
L_{\varphi}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. These operators behave like a multiplication operator, when the symbols
\varphi
come from
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, that is,
T_{f}g=fg
, when
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. There are several important facts that connect the operator-theoretic properties with function theory. One of the most simple yet profound property is the following: a Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
is an isometry if and only if
\varphi
and is an
inner
function. Recall that inner functions
\varphi
are functions in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, satisfying
|\varphi(e^{it})|=1
, almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. Another important aspect of Toeplitz operator comes via
T_{z}
also known as the shift-operator. It was shown by Beurling
[
10
]
, that any
T_{z}
-invariant closed subspace of
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is of the form
\mathcal{S}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\theta
is an inner function. Using Toeplitz operators we can explicitly write down the orthogonal projection to any shift-invariant subspace. In particular, let us denote
P_{\theta}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, then
P_{\theta}=T_{\theta}T_{\theta}^{*}
because
T_{\theta}
is an isometry. Recently, in
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, Debnath et al. characterized when a pair of these projections commute. In particular, they proved that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
if and only if
\varphi_{1}
divides
\varphi_{2}
or
\varphi_{2}
divides
\varphi_{1}
. It is clear that this is another instance, where operator-theoretic properies induces a factorization of the corresponding inner function, which is a function-theoretic property. This motivates us to study, the following general question.

Question
.

When does the orthogonal projections
P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{\varphi_{2}}
corresponding to inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}
, essentially commute?

Our approach to this question is based on the following:

(i)

finding representations of the orthogonal projections
P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}
using Halmos’ two projection theorem (see Theorem
2.1
),

(ii)

simplifying the Halmos decomposition by removing the common inner factors of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
as this keeps the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
unchanged (see Lemma
2.7
),

(ii)

use Coburn’s theorem for Toeplitz operators to simplify the decomposition further.

Following the above procedure, we are able to connect the question to essential commutativity of Toeplitz operators. Recall that a pair of bounded operators
(T_{1},T_{2})
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to
essentially commute
if
[T_{1},T_{2}]
is compact. In particular, in Theorem
3.1
, we prove that

P_{\varphi_{1}}\text{ essentially commutes with }P_{\varphi_{2}}\text{ if and only if }T_{\varphi_{1}}\text{ essentially commutes with }T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}.

Now, there exists deep results by Axler-Chang-Sarason and Volberg that decodes the essential commutativity of Toeplitz operators via properties of the symbols.

Theorem

(Axler-Chang-Sarason-Volberg Theorem)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Then
T_{fg}-T_{f}T_{g}
is compact if and only if
H^{\infty}[\bar{f}]\cap H^{\infty}[g]\subseteq H^{\infty}+C(\mathbb{T})
.

Recall that for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the Douglas algebra
H^{\infty}[f]
generated by
f
is defined to be the smallest closed sub-algebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
containing both
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and the function
f
. Notably, the general problem of characterizing the essential commutativity of a pair of Toeplitz operators was solved by Gorkin and Zheng in
[
21
]
. The above conditions on Douglas algebras can be converted into local behaviour of the functions. Following
[
21
]
, a subset of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is said to be a support set if it is the (closed) support of a representing measure for a linear functional in the maximal ideal space
M(H^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. The conditions on Douglas algebras in the Axler–Chang–Sarason–Volberg theorem become the following (see
[
21
, Theorem 0.8]
): for any support set
S
, either
\bar{f}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{g}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
. Using this correspondence into our result gives:

P_{\varphi_{1}}
essentially commutes with
P_{\varphi_{2}}
if and only if for each support set S, one of
\bar{\varphi}_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\varphi}_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
holds (see Theorem
3.3
). The main tool for proving this result is a general observation on a pair of projections that connects the compactness of
[P_{1},P_{2}]
with the compactness of the product
(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
(ref. Theorem
2.6
). While we were finishing writing this article, we found very recently that Zheng et al, have studied the same problem. Their method connects the essential commutativity of
P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{\varphi_{2}}
with the compactness of a certain product of Hankel and Toeplitz operators in
[
38
, Theorem 2]
, which is again equivalent to the condition that for each support set, either
\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
by using a result by Chu
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
. Clearly, our results give a stronger solution to the problem.

Motivated by the rich connection of essential commutativity of the projections with the functional behaviour of the symbols, we have completely characterized when the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
becomes finite-rank. In Theorem
3.7
, we prove that

[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]\text{ is finite-rank if and only if }\varphi_{1}\text{ or }\varphi_{2}\text{ is a finite Blaschke product}.

Furthermore, we prove that
\text{rank of }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\text{min}\{\text{deg}(\varphi_{1}),\text{deg}(\varphi_{2})\}
. Here
\text{deg}(\varphi)
denotes the degree of inner function
\varphi
and is equal to the number of zeroes of
\varphi
inside
\mathbb{D}
, counting multiplicities. Interestingly, finite-rank commutators of these projections have a connection with the Fredholmness of pairs of projections as introduced by Avron, Seiler and Simon in
[
7
]
. Following
[
7
]
, a pair of orthogonal projections
(P,Q)
on a separable Hilbert space is called a Fredholm pair if the operator
QP
, viewed as a map from ran
P
to ran
Q
is a Fredholm operator. In Theorem
4.2
, we prove that if
P_{\varphi_{1}}
essentially commutes with
P_{\varphi_{2}}
, then
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
can become a Fredholm pair only when the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
has finite rank and both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are finite Blaschke products. Moreover, in this case,

\mbox{index}(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})=\text{deg}(\varphi_{2})-\text{deg}(\varphi_{1}).

We have several interesting results in the polydisc case as well. The motivation comes from the recent result by Debnath et al.
[
17
]
, characterizing the commutativity of the orothogonal projections
P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{\varphi_{2}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(that is, the Hardy space over unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
). In particular, their result states that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
if and only if there exist inner functions
\psi,\tilde{\varphi_{1}},\tilde{\varphi_{2}}
, where
\tilde{\varphi_{1}}
and
\tilde{\varphi_{2}}
depends on disjoint subset of variables in
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
, such that

\varphi_{1}=\psi\,\tilde{\varphi_{1}}\text{ and }\varphi_{2}=\psi\,\tilde{\varphi_{2}}.

This led Debnath et al. to completely answer (
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
) the following question raised by R.G.Douglas: when does the product
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
become a finite-rank projection. In the recent article
[
38
]
, Zhang et al. observed that the compactness of the product is sufficient to reach to conclude that it must be a finite-rank projection. Their method depends on the behaviour of joint defect operator
\Delta_{T}
corresponding to pair of isometries
(T_{1},T_{2})
as observed by Guo and Wang in
[
23
]
. Although, we noticed that the joint defect operator
I-T_{1}T_{1}^{*}-T_{2}T_{2}^{*}-T_{1}T_{1}^{*}T_{2}T_{2}^{*}
considered by Zhang et al. in
[
38
]
, is different from the defect operator
\Delta_{T}=I-T_{1}T_{1}^{*}-T_{2}T_{2}^{*}+T_{1}T_{2}T_{1}^{*}T_{2}^{*}
considered in
[
23
]
. In Theorem
7.7
and Theorem
7.8
, we give new proofs of the observations by Zhang et al. using our methods in a completely different approach. The general problem of characterizing essential commutativity of these projections
P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is however a difficult problem. The first roadblock as apparent from the description of our approach is the Coburn’s theorem. Secondly, the lack of a fair understanding of when two inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
are
co-prime
. This is another place where we make an original contribution by identifying a new sufficient criterion for inner functions to be co-prime (see Definition
6.4
). Surprisingly, using this co-prime property of inner functions, we can bypass the lack of Coburn’s theorem on polydisc. In Theorem
7.2
, we prove that if
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are co-prime, then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact. This immediately resolves the finite-rank case. In Corollary
7.3
, we prove that if
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are co-prime, then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite–rank only when
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
. These results can be directly applied to inner functions in the bidisc algebra
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
, since we prove that any pair of inner functions without any common inner factor must be co-prime (see Theorem
6.9
).

Let us now outline the plan of this article. In section

, we give an overview of the Halmos’ two projection theorem for abstract projections followed by establishing the preliminary results involving the projections
P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, needed for the later sections. Section

consists of the characterizations on essential commutativity and finite-rank commutators. In section

, we characterize when essentially commuting projections
P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
become a Fredholm pair. Following this, in section
5
, we find more applications of our main results. In particular, we refine the criterion for compactness of truncated Toeplitz operator observed by Ma and Zheng
[

]
in the case of inner symbols (see Theorem
5.2
). Moreover, we completely characterize the compactness of certain contractions using the Sz.Nagy–Foias model theory (see Theorem
5.3
). Next, in section
6
, we find a sufficient criterion for inner functions to be co-prime over
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and establish explicit cases for co-primeness. Lastly, in section
7
, we prove the equivalence of the compactness
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
with the compactness of
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
in the case of co-prime inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Furthermore, we prove that there aren’t any non-trivial commutators with finite-rank in these cases. Moreover, we characterize when the product of projections
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
become a compact operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, our aim is to describe the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
corresponding to inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
using Halmos’s two projections theorem. First, let us briefly explain Halmos’s characterization for abstract Hilbert spaces. Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be two orthogonal projections onto closed subspaces
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, respectively. The first step is to find an orthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
using componenets of these subspaces. In particular, let us denote,

\mathcal{L}_{00}:=\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{N},\quad\mathcal{L}_{01}:=\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{N}^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{L}_{10}:=\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{N},\quad\mathcal{L}_{11}:=\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{N}^{\perp}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{L}_{0}:={\mathcal{M}}\ominus(\mathcal{L}_{00}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{01})
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}:={\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}\ominus(\mathcal{L}_{10}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{11})
, Halmos observed that

(2.1)

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}_{00}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{01}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{10}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{11}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{1}.

Using these decomposition, Halmos
[
24
, Theorem 2]
found representations of the projections
P_{1},P_{2}
in the following manner. We follow
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
below for this description.

Theorem 2.1

(Halmos
[
24
]
)
.

If one of the spaces
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is non-trivial, then
\mbox{dim }\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mbox{dim }\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Moreover, there exists a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{L}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle{P_{1}}

\displaystyle=(I_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus{W}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}I_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}{W},

(2.2)

\displaystyle{P_{2}}

\displaystyle=(I_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus{W}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}{T}&{\sqrt{T(I-T)}}\\
{\sqrt{T(I-T)}}&{I-T}\end{bmatrix}{W},

where
{W}=\text{diag}(I_{\mathcal{L}_{0}},{U})
. Here
{T}:\mathcal{L}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{0}
is the positive contraction defined by

T=P_{{\mathcal{L}_{0}}}P_{2}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}},

satisfying
\ker~T=\ker~(I-{T})=\{0\}.

This following few corollaries (probably well-known) are simple application of Halmos’ theorem.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(P_{1},P_{2})
be a pair of orthogonal projections on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the commutator
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is compact (or finite rank) if and only if
T(I-T)
is compact (or finite rank).

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.1
, we get

(2.3)

P_{1}P_{2}-P_{2}P_{1}=(0,0,0,0)\oplus W^{*}\begin{bmatrix}0&\sqrt{T(I-T)}\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&0\end{bmatrix}W.

Since
T(I-T)
is a positive operator,
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is compact (or finite rank) operator if and only if
T(I-T)
is a compact (or finite rank) operator.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(P_{1},P_{2})
be a pair of orthogonal projections on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the commutator
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is finite rank if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

By the above corollary, we already have that
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is finite rank if and only if
T(I-T)
has finite-rank. Moreover, since
T
and
I-T
both are injective operators. Thus,
T(I-T)
has finite-rank if and only if the domain of
T(I-T)
, that is,
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is finite dimensional.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(P_{1},P_{2})
be a pair of orthogonal projections on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the product
(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
is compact (or finite rank) if and only if
(I-P_{1})\mathcal{H}\cap(I-P_{2})\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional and
T
is compact (or finite rank).

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.1
, we get

(2.4)

\begin{split}(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})&=I-P_{1}-P_{2}+P_{1}P_{2}\\
&=(0_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&T\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

Thus, we can conclude that if
(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
is compact (or finite rank),
(I-P_{1})\mathcal{H}\cap(I-P_{2})\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional and
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&T\end{bmatrix}
is compact (or finite rank). Moreover,

\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&T\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&T\end{bmatrix}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&T\end{bmatrix},

shows that
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\sqrt{T(I-T)}&T\end{bmatrix}
is compact (or finite-rank) if and only if
T
is compact (or finite-rank). This completes the proof.

From the following expression

[P_{1},P_{2}]=[(I-P_{1}),(I-P_{2})],

it is clear that, the compactness of the product
(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
always imply the compactness of
[P_{1},P_{2}]
. We will now observe that the compactness of
T
can be waived off under an additional assumption. We will need John von Neumann’s famous result on alternating projections.

Theorem 2.5

(von Neumann
[
33
]
)
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be orthogonal projections on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(P_{1}P_{2})^{n}
converges strongly to the orthogonal projection onto
P_{1}\mathcal{H}\cap P_{2}\mathcal{H}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(P_{1},P_{2})
be a pair of orthogonal projections on
\mathcal{H}
such that
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is compact. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
is compact,

(ii)

(I-P_{1})\mathcal{H}\cap(I-P_{2})\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

We already know
(i)\implies(ii)
from Corollary
2.4
. For proving
(ii)\implies(i)
, let us first note that
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=(I-P_{1})\Big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\Big)(I-P_{2})\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=(I-P_{1})\Big([I-P_{1},I-P_{2}]+(I-P_{2})(I-P_{1})\Big)(I-P_{2})\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k+1}+(I-P_{1})[I-P_{1},I-P_{2}](I-P_{2})\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k+1}+(I-P_{1})[P_{1},P_{2}](I-P_{2})\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{k+1}+\text{ compact}.

The last equality follows from the assumption that
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is compact. Now, iterating the above identity for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, gives,

(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})=\big((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\big)^{n}+\text{ compact}.

Now, using von Neumann’s alternating orthogonal projection formula, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left((I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})\right)^{n}h=\tilde{P}h,\quad(h\in\mathcal{H})

where
\tilde{P}
is the orthogonal projection onto
P_{1}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\cap P_{2}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}
, and therefore, we get

(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})=\tilde{P}+\text{ compact}.

Therefore, if
P_{1}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\cap P_{2}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional or equivalently,
\tilde{P}
is finite-rank, then the product
(I-P_{1})(I-P_{2})
must be compact. This completes the proof.

We will now focus on understanding Halmos’s decomposition for shift-invariant subspaces of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular, let us consider pair of
T_{z}
-invariant closed subspaces
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. By Beurling’s theorem
[
10
]
there exists inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\varphi_{i}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for
i=1,2
. It is also well known that a Toeplitz operator is an isometry if and only if the corresponding symbol is an inner function
[
18
]
. Thus,
P_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}
and
P_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}.

For the sake of computation, let us denote these projections as

P_{\varphi_{1}}:=P_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\text{ and }P_{\varphi_{2}}:=P_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}.

It is evident from Halmos’s decomposition that there is a choice on whether we consider
P_{\varphi_{1}}
as the first projection and
P_{\varphi_{2}}
as the second, or vice versa. Since we are interested in the compactness of the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
, whatever be the choice it won’t affect the compactness of the commutator. But for the sake of simplifying the decomposition (
2.1
), as will be evident later, we choose a convention. In particular, it is well-known from Coburn’s theorem
[
13
]
that either

\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\{0\}\quad\text{or }\quad\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}=\{0\}.

(2.5)

\textsf{Convention: }P_{1}=P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{2}=P_{\varphi_{2}}\textsf{ when }\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\{0\}.

So, without loss of any generality let us assume
P_{1}=P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{2}=P_{\varphi_{2}}
, and proceed with Halmos’s decomposition. In particular,

\mathcal{M}=\text{ range }(P_{\varphi_{1}})=\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\text{ and }\mathcal{N}=\text{ range }(P_{\varphi_{2}})=\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Consequently, following
[
20
, Corollary 4.8]
, we obtain

(2.6)

\mathcal{L}_{00}=~\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\mbox{lcm}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})

and following
[
20
, Corollary 5.9]
, we get

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}_{11}=~\left(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\right)^{\perp}\cap\left(\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\right)^{\perp}=\left(\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})\right)^{\perp},

where the
greatest common divisor
of a pair of inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
or
\mbox{gcd }(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
is defined to be the inner function
\varphi
such that
\varphi|\varphi_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, and there does not exist a common inner factor for
\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi}
and
\frac{\varphi_{2}}{\varphi}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\right)\cap\left(\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\right)^{\perp}=

\displaystyle~\{\varphi_{1}f:f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})~\text{and}~\varphi_{1}f\perp\varphi_{2}h~\text{for all }h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\{\varphi_{1}f:f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})~\text{and}~T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}f\perp h~\text{for all }h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\{\varphi_{1}f:f\in\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}\},

and hence,

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}_{01}=\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap(\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))^{\perp}=\varphi_{1}\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}.

From our convention based on the Coburn’s theorem we get
\mathcal{L}_{01}=\{0\}
, and therefore,

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}_{0}=~\mathcal{M}\ominus\big(\mathcal{L}_{00}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{01}\big)=~\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\big(\mbox{lcm}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})\big).

Next, if we assume that
\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
, it is equivalent to
\mbox{lcm}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}
, and thus,

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}_{0}=~\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=~\varphi_{1}\big(H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\big)=\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}},

The following simple observation shows that we can take the assumption
\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
, without any loss of generality, as it won’t affect the compactness of the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be two inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
, and
\varphi=\mbox{gcd }(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
. Consider
\psi_{i}=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact (or finite rank) if and only if
[P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}}]
is compact (or finite-rank).

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{i}=\varphi\psi_{i}
for
i=1,2
, we get

T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}=T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}T_{\varphi}T_{\psi_{2}}=T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}T_{\psi_{2}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}T_{\psi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\psi_{2}}^{*}T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi}\left(T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}T_{\psi_{2}}T_{\psi_{2}}^{*}-T_{\psi_{2}}T_{\psi_{2}}^{*}T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}\right)T_{\varphi}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi}[P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}}]T_{\varphi}^{*}.

Since,
\varphi
is an inner function,
T_{\varphi}
is an isometry, and therefore,
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact (or finite-rank) if and only if
[P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}}]
is compact (or finite-rank).

In our setting of Beurling projections we can strengthen Corollary
2.2
using Theorem
2.6
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact.

(ii)

(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is compact.

(iii)

T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is compact.

Proof.

The
(i)\implies(ii)
step follows from Theorem
2.6
and
(iii)\implies(i)
follows from Corollary
2.2
. Now, the assumption
\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
and the the expression in (
2.7
) implies that

\mathcal{L}_{11}=P_{1}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\cap P_{2}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}=(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))^{\perp}\cap(\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))^{\perp}=\{0\}.

By using Corollary
2.4
, we get
(ii)\Longleftrightarrow(iii)
. This completes the proof.

3.
Compact and finite-rank commutator

In this section, we completely characterize the compactness of the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
corresponding to inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions with
\mbox{gcd }(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
. Then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if the semi-commutator
T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}
is compact.

Proof.

Now, using the convention in (
2.5
), we consider
P_{1}=P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{2}=P_{\varphi_{2}}
. According to Proposition
2.8
,
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
T
is compact, where
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
(from
2.10
).
Thus,
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mbox{range }T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
. Furthermore,

\big(T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\big)\big(T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\big)^{*}=T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}.

shows that
T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a partial isometry and hence,
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}
. Following Theorem
2.1
, we get

T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T^{*}_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}.

Now, we shall have a series of observations based on the fact that any bounded operator
A
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is compact if and only if
A^{*}A
is compact (or finite-rank). Thus, from the preceding expression of
T
, we get that
T
is compact if and only if
T^{*}_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}
is compact, which is again equivalent to
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}
being compact. Moreover,

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}=T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}=T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}.

Thus, we get
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if the semi-commutator
T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}
is compact. This completes the proof.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions and
\psi_{i}=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}
, for
i=1,2
, where
\varphi=\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
. Then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if the semi-commutator
T_{\bar{\psi}_{1}\psi_{2}}-T_{\psi_{2}}T_{\bar{\psi}_{1}}
is compact.

Proof.

This follows by using Lemma
2.7
and the above characterization.

Using the above characterization along with the Axler-Chang-Sarason-Volberg theorem gives the following complete description.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions and
\psi_{i}=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}
, for
i=1,2
, where
\varphi=\mbox{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

P_{\varphi_{1}}
essentially commutes with
P_{\varphi_{2}}
.

(ii)

T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}}-T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}\psi_{1}}
is a compact operator.

(iii)
For each support set
S
, either
\bar{\psi}_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\psi}_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
.

(iv)

H^{\infty}[\bar{\psi}_{1}]\cap H^{\infty}[\bar{\psi}_{2}]\subset H^{\infty}+C
.

Example 3.4
.

Let

\alpha_{n}=1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\text{ and }\beta_{n}=-1+\frac{1}{n^{2}},~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Define

\varphi_{1}(z)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{n}}{|\alpha_{n}|}\frac{\alpha_{n}-z}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n}z}\text{ and }\varphi_{2}(z)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\bar{\beta}_{n}}{|\beta_{n}|}\frac{\beta_{n}-z}{1-\bar{\beta}_{n}z}.

Then the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is a compact operator. If
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
and
\alpha\neq 1
, then
\varphi_{1}
admits a continuous extension at
\alpha
. Therefore, by
[
25
, p. 161]
, it follows that
\varphi_{1}
is constant on the fiber
M_{\alpha}
. Consequently, for any support set
S
contained in
M_{\alpha}
, we obtain
\bar{\varphi}_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
. Similarly, when
\alpha=1
, an analogous argument shows that for every support set
S
contained in
M_{1}
,
\bar{\varphi}_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
. Therefore, by the above theorem
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is a compact operator.

Remark 3.5
.

The inquisitive reader may question if the compactness of the semi-commutator
T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}-T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
is equivalent to the compactness of the semi-commutator
T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}}-T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}\psi_{1}}
. One side always holds, namely, compactness of
T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}-T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
will always imply compactness of
T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}}-T_{\bar{\psi}_{2}\psi_{1}}
. Since following
[
28
, Lemma 17]
, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(T)
and
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, if
f|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
, then
fg|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
. Thus, the condition
\bar{\varphi}_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\varphi}_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
will always imply that
\bar{\psi}_{1}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\psi}_{2}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
after multiplication by the common factor
\varphi
. The converse direction however does not hold as it can be seen with the following simple example:

\varphi_{1}(z)=\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\cdot\exp{\frac{1+z}{z-1}};\quad\varphi_{2}(z)=\frac{z-\beta}{1-\bar{\beta}z}\cdot\exp{\frac{1+z}{z-1}}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

for distinct
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{D}
. Clearly,
\bar{\varphi}_{1}|_{S}\notin H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\bar{\varphi}_{2}|_{S}\notin H^{\infty}|_{S}
since both the functions are bounded away at
1
, but
\frac{\bar{z}-\bar{\alpha}}{1-\alpha\bar{z}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\frac{\bar{z}-\bar{\beta}}{1-\beta\bar{z}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
.

We have a complete characterizatioin for finite-rank commutators, but before describing it let us have a simple observation.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
. If
\varphi_{2}
is a finite Blaschke product and
\mbox{deg }\varphi_{2}\leq\mbox{deg }\varphi_{1}
, then
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
h\in\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
, then
\varphi_{1}h\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
. Let
\mbox{deg }\varphi_{2}=n
and
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
be the set of all zeroes of
\varphi_{2}
. Then following
[
20
, Corollary 5.18]
, we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}}{(1-\bar{\alpha}_{1}z)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{2}z)\cdots(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n}z)}:a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

Thus, any function in
\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
must have at most
n-1
zeroes and therefore,
\varphi_{1}h
has at most
n-1
zeroes. On the other hand,
\varphi_{1}h
has at least
n
-many zeroes since
\mbox{deg }\varphi_{1}\geq\mbox{deg }\varphi_{2}=n
. This is a contradiction to
\varphi_{1}h\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
unless
h=0
, which implies that
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=0
. This completes the proof.

We are now ready to prove the main result on finite-rank commutator. For this purpose, let us recall that a Hankel operator
H_{\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by
H_{\varphi}:=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}}L_{\varphi}|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
corresponding to
\varphi\in L^{\infty}
, where
L_{\varphi}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)
the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank

(ii)

\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
is a finite Blaschke product.

(iii)

H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}
or
H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}
is finite-rank

Moreover, in this case,

\text{rank of }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\text{min}\{\text{deg}(\varphi_{1}),\text{deg}(\varphi_{2})\}.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.4
, it is clear that if
P_{1}=P_{\varphi_{1}}
and
P_{2}=P_{\varphi_{2}}
, then
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is finite-rank if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
is finite-dimensional, which is again equivlalent to
\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
being finite-dimensional and therefore,
\varphi_{2}
must be a finite Blaschke product. Similarly, if
P_{1}=P_{\varphi_{2}}
and
P_{2}=P_{\varphi_{1}}
, then
[P_{1},P_{2}]
is finite-rank if and only if
\varphi_{1}
is a finite Blaschke product. This proves the equivalence between
(i)
and
(ii)
. The equivalence between
(ii)
and
(iii)
follows from the observation that
\ker H_{\bar{\theta}}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for any inner function
\theta
on
\mathbb{D}
. For proving, the identity on
\mbox{rank }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
, let us assume without the loss of any generality that
\varphi_{2}
is a finite Blaschke product and
\text{deg}(\varphi_{2})=n\leq\text{deg}(\varphi_{1})
. Following, Lemma
3.6
, we get
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\{0\}
, and therefore, using identity (
2.8
), we get
\mathcal{L}_{01}=\{0\}
. Thus, using (
2.9
), we get

\mathcal{L}_{0}=T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

and since
\varphi_{2}
is a Blaschke product with
\mbox{deg }(\varphi_{2})=n
, therefore dim
\mathcal{L}_{0}=n
. Thus,
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is a finite-rank operator. Moreover by Theorem
2.1
,
\ker(T)=\ker(I-T)=\{0\}
, which implies that
\mbox{rank }T(I-T)=\dim\mathcal{L}_{0}=\dim\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=n
. Now, from the expression of the commutator (
2.3
) in Corollary
2.2
, we get

\text{rank of }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\mbox{ rank }\sqrt{T(I-T)}=2n.

Similarly, if
\varphi_{1}
is a finite Blaschke product and
\mbox{deg }\varphi_{1}=m\leq\mbox{deg }\varphi_{2}
, we shall get

\text{rank of }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2m.

Therefore,
\mbox{rank }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\min\{\mbox{deg }(\varphi_{1}),\mbox{deg }(\varphi_{2})\}
. This completes the proof.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
such that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank, then

\mbox{rank }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\mbox{ rank }H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}.

Proof.

Following
[
35
, Theorem 2]
, a formula for the rank of the product of Hankel operators
H_{\varphi}
and
H_{\psi}
corresponding to
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is given by

\mbox{rank }H_{\varphi}H_{\psi}=\min\{\mbox{rank }H_{\varphi},\mbox{rank }H_{\psi}\}.

Furthermore, when
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are inner functions, then
\mbox{deg }\varphi_{i}=\mbox{rank }H_{\bar{\varphi}_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
and thus,

\mbox{rank }H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}=\min\{\mbox{deg }\varphi_{1},\mbox{deg }\varphi_{2}\}.

By using the above theorem, we get

\mbox{rank }[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=2\mbox{ rank }H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}},

when
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank. This completes the proof.

4.
Fredholm pairs

In this section, we investigate when does a essentially commuting pair of projections
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
become a Fredholm pair. Following
[
7
]
, a pair of orthogonal projections
(P,Q)
on a separable Hilbert space is called a Fredholm pair if the operator
QP
, viewed as a map from ran
P
to ran
Q
is a Fredholm operator. The following result
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
gives an useful characterization.

Lemma 4.1
.

(P,Q)
is a Fredholm pair if and only if

(1)

1
and
-1
are isolated points of
\sigma(P-Q)
,

(2)

\ker(P-Q\pm 1)
are finite dimensional.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\text{index }(P,Q)=\text{dim}\ker(P-Q-I)-\text{dim}\ker(P-Q+I).

Proposition 4.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

A necessary and sufficient condition that
(P,Q)
be a Fredholm pair is that
P-Q=F+D
, where
F,D
are self-adjoint,
\|D\|<1
and
F
is finite-rank.

Let us now note that the Fredholmness is invariant under the division by factors.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, let
\varphi=\mbox{gcd }(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
and
\psi_{i}=\frac{\varphi_{i}}{\varphi}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a Fredholm pair if and only if
(P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}})
is a Fredholm pair.

Proof.

Let us first note that

P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=T_{\varphi}T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\psi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}-T_{\varphi}T_{\psi_{2}}T_{\psi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}=T_{\varphi}\big(P_{\psi_{1}}-P_{\psi_{2}}\big)T_{\varphi}^{*}.

If
(P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}})
is a Fredholm pair then by Lemma
4.2
, there exists self-adjoint operators
F,D
satisfying

P_{\psi_{1}}-P_{\psi_{2}}=F+D.

Thus,

P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}=T_{\varphi}FT_{\varphi}^{*}+T_{\varphi}DT_{\varphi}^{*}.

Now
F,D
being self-adjoint imply that
T_{\varphi}FT_{\varphi}^{*}
and
T_{\varphi}DT_{\varphi}^{*}
are self-adjoint operators. Moreover,
T_{\varphi}FT_{\varphi}^{*}
is finite-rank since
F
is finite-rank and
\|T_{\varphi}DT_{\varphi}^{*}\|<1
since
\|D\|<1
. Thus, again by Lemma
4.2
,
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
must be a Fredholm pair. Conversely, if
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a Fredholm pair, then there exists self-adjoint operators
\tilde{F},\tilde{D}
, where
\tilde{F}
is finite-rank,
\|\tilde{D}\|<1
and

P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}=\tilde{F}+\tilde{D},

and therefore,

P_{\psi_{1}}-P_{\psi_{2}}=T_{\varphi}^{*}\big(P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}\big)T_{\varphi}=T_{\varphi}^{*}\tilde{F}T_{\varphi}+T_{\varphi}^{*}\tilde{D}T_{\varphi}.

Thus, again by Lemma
4.1
,
(P_{\psi_{1}},P_{\psi_{2}})
becomes a Fredholm pair. This completes the proof.

We will now observe that if essentially commuting pairs of projections
(P_{\varphi_{2}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
are Fredholm then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
must be finite-rank. Before going into detail let us first collect an identity useful for the sequel. In particular, using the expressions in Theorem
2.1
, we get

(4.1)

P_{1}-P_{2}=(0_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},-I_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus{W}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}{I-T}&-{\sqrt{T(I-T)}}\\
-{\sqrt{T(I-T)}}&{T-I}\end{bmatrix}{W},

and hence,

(P_{1}-P_{2})^{2}=(0_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus{W}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}{I-T}&0\\
0&{I-T}\end{bmatrix}{W}.

In particular, if
P_{i}=P_{\varphi_{i}}
for
i=1,2
, then
\mathcal{L}_{01}=0
. Hence,

(4.2)

(P_{1}-P_{2})^{2}=(0_{\mathcal{L}_{00}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{01}},I_{\mathcal{L}_{10}},0_{\mathcal{L}_{11}})\oplus{W}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}{I-T}&0\\
0&{I-T}\end{bmatrix}{W}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be non-constant and distinct inner functions such that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact operator. If
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a Fredhlom pair then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]

Proof.

Suppose
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact but not finite-rank. By, Proposition
2.8
it follows that
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is compact and not finite-rank. Since
\ker(T)=\{0\},
the only way

of infinitely many distinct positive real numbers such that
\sigma(T)=\{\lambda_{n}\}\cup\{0\}
and

. From the identity (

), it is clear that
\sigma(I-T)\subset\sigma((P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})^{2})

In fact, Fredholm pairs

Lemma 4.5

Let

\displaystyle p(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\beta_{i})c(z),

Theorem 4.6

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant inner functions on

\displaystyle\text{index }(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})=\text{deg}(\varphi_{2})-\text{deg}(\varphi_{1}).

\displaystyle{P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}-I}

\displaystyle{P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+I}

\begin{bmatrix}x\\

5.
Applications

5.1.
Truncated Toeplitz operators

For any inner function
\theta
on
\mathbb{D}
, let
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}:=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
. Then for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
is defined by
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}f=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta}}\varphi f
for
f\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
. In particular, if
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, then
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}
is a bounded operator, although there are some symbols in
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\setminus L^{\infty}(T)
, for which
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}
is also a bounded operator. Ma and Zheng completely characterized when
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}
is a compact operator.

Theorem 5.1

(Ma & Zheng
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(T)
and
\theta
be an inner function. The truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}^{\theta}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
is compact if and only if for each
m\in M(H^{\infty}+C)
one of the following holds.

(i)

\theta|_{S_{m}}
is constant,

(ii)

\varphi|_{S_{m}}\in\theta|_{S_{m}}H^{2}(m)+\overline{\theta}|_{S_{m}}\overline{H^{2}(m)}
.

Here
S_{m}
is the support set of the representing measure corresponding to
m
. Using our methods, we can establish the following refined version for inner functions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)
the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}
or
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{\varphi_{1}}
is compact,

(ii)

P_{\varphi_{1}}
essentially commutes with
P_{\varphi_{2}}

(iii)
for each support set
S
, either
{\frac{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}{\bar{\varphi}}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
{\frac{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}{\bar{\varphi}}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
, where
\varphi=\text{gcd}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
.

Proof.

The equivalence between
(ii)
and
(iii)
comes from Theorem
3.3
. For
(i)\Longleftrightarrow(ii)
, let us first observe that

\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}=T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\varphi_{2}}}=T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}-T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{2}}.

Therefore it follows that

T_{\varphi_{1}}T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}=P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}.

Since the left-hand side is self-adjoint, we get

T_{\varphi_{1}}T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}=P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}.

Subtracting the above two identities gives,

[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{1}}\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}.

Thus,
T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}
compact implies
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact. Next, let us assume that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact. Then

[P_{\varphi_{1}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}]^{*}=[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=[P_{\varphi_{2}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}]

implies that both
[P_{\varphi_{1}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}]
and
[P_{\varphi_{2}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}]
are compact. From Coburn’s theorem, we know that
T_{\bar{\varphi_{1}}\varphi_{2}}=0
, or
T_{\bar{\varphi_{2}}\varphi_{1}}=0
. Let us assume that
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi_{2}}\varphi_{1}}=0
. It will imply that

\text{range}(P_{\varphi_{1}})\cap\text{range}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=\varphi_{1}H^{2}\cap(\varphi_{2}H^{2})^{\perp}=T_{\varphi_{1}}\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\{0\},

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})T_{\varphi_{1}}T^{*}_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})+(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}[P_{\varphi_{1}},(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})](I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})+\text{compact},

where, the last equality follows from the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}]
. Continuing in a similar manner we obtain for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\{P_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\}^{n}+\text{compact}.

Following von Neumann’s alternating orthogonal projection formula, we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\{P_{\varphi_{1}}(I-P_{\varphi_{2}})\}^{n}h\in\text{range}(P_{\varphi_{1}})\cap\text{range}(I-P_{\varphi_{2}})=\{0\}.

Thus, it follows that
\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)\left(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\right)^{*}
is a compact operator, and hence
T_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}
is a compact operator. Similarly, if
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi_{1}}\varphi_{2}}=0
, then using the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{2}},I_{H^{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}]
, we will get
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{\varphi_{1}}
is compact.

5.2.
Contractions on Hilbert spaces

A contraction
T
(i.e.
\|T\|\leq 1
) on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is defined to be pure if
\|T^{*n}h\|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. The defect space for a contraction
T
is defined to be
\mathcal{D}_{T}:=\overline{(I_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}}
. It is well-known from the Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory
[
32
]
, that corresponding to a pure contraction
T
with
\dim\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=1
, there always exists an inner function
\theta_{T}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
T
is unitarily equivalent the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{z}^{\theta_{T}}=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}}T_{z}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}}
. An important application of the Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory is the
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for these contractions. In particular, for any
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
,
\psi(T)
is unitarily equivalent to the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}^{\theta_{T}}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}
. Using these correspondences and the result in the previous section, we get the following characterization on compactness of
\psi(T)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
T
be a pure contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mbox{dim }\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=1
. Then, for any inner function
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
satisfying
\ker T_{\bar{\psi}{\theta}_{T}}\neq 0
, the following are equivalent.

(i)

\psi(T)
is a compact operator,

(ii)
for each support set
S
, either
\frac{\bar{\theta}_{T}}{\bar{\varphi}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
or
\frac{\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\varphi}}|_{S}\in H^{\infty}|_{S}
, where
\varphi=\mbox{gcd}(\theta_{T},\psi)
.

Proof.

From the Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory, it is well known that
T
is unitarily equivalent to
P_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}}T_{z}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}}
and since
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=1
, the characteristic function
\theta_{T}
is simply an inner function on
\mathbb{D}
. Using the
H^{\infty}
-calculus, we get that

\psi(T)\text{ is unitarily equivalent to }T_{\psi}^{\theta_{T}}\text{ on }\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}.

for any
\psi\in H^{\infty}
. So,
\psi(T)
is compact if and only if the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}^{\theta_{T}}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}
is compact. Since
\ker T_{\bar{\psi}\theta_{T}}\neq 0
, from Coburn’s theorem we get
\ker T_{\bar{\theta}_{T}\psi}=0
, and hence by the above proof, the truncated Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}^{\theta_{T}}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}
is compact if and only if
[P_{\psi},P_{\theta_{T}}]
is compact which is again equivalent to condition
(ii)
from Theorem
3.3
. This completes the proof.

Remark 5.4
.

From
[
5
, Lemma 5.3]
, it follows that
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}\psi}\neq 0
corresponding to inner functions
\varphi,\psi
, if and only if there exists an inner function
\theta
and a outer function
g
such that
\psi\theta g=\varphi\bar{g}
. So the above result will work for all inner functions
\psi
for which there exists an inner function
\theta
and an outer function
g
such that
\psi\theta g=\theta_{T}\bar{g}
. In particular, it will work for all inner functions
\psi
that divides
\theta_{T}
.

6.
Co-prime inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}

It is well-known that inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
do not admit a well-behaved gcd/lcm theory. Our main goal in this section is to study certain properties of co-primeness that may still be valid on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Let us first recall that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the space of all analytic functions
f(\bm{z})
on the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that

\|f\|:=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(r\bm{z})|^{2}d\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

where
\bm{z}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
\mu
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, that is the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The algebra of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is denoted by
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. A function
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is inner if
|\varphi(\bm{\mu})|=1
for almost everywhere
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. In the case of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, it is well-known that for inner functions,
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}
the following are equivalent
[
20
]
.

(i)

\mbox{gcd }(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=1
;

(ii)

\varphi_{1}H^{2}\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}
;

(iii)

\varphi_{1}H^{2}\bigvee\varphi_{2}H^{2}=H^{2}
;

(iv)

\varphi_{1}(H^{2}\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}=\{0\}
.

There are several examples (like the following) which show that these conditions do not remain equivalent on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
when
n>1
.

Example 6.1
.

Consider two inner functions
\varphi_{1}=z_{1},~\varphi_{2}=z_{2}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}).
Since every function
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}\bigvee\varphi_{2}H^{2}
vanishes at
(0,0)
, we have
f(0,0)=0
. Therefore,
\varphi_{1}H^{2}\bigvee\varphi_{2}H^{2}\neq H^{2}
. However,
z_{1}(H^{2}\ominus z_{2}H^{2})\cap z_{2}H^{2}=\{0\}
.

We will show that
(ii)
and
(iv)
are equivalent on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then

(6.1)

(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))\ominus\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}

Proof.

Let us begin by observing that

(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))\ominus\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}.

Furthermore, if
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
, then
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})f=f
.
But the following expressions,

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}+T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}.

and

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}+T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}.

imply that

T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}f=f=T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}f.

Thus,

\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\subseteq\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})\cap\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}).

Both
T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})
and
T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*})
are partial isometries and moreover,

\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})=\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{1}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}=\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
and
\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*})=\mbox{ran }T_{\varphi_{2}}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*})T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=\varphi_{2}\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}
. Conversely, if
f\in\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}
, then it is clear that
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}.
This completes the proof.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be distinct and non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,

(ii)

\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}=\{0\},

(iii)

\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\{0\}.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.2
, it is clear that
(i)\Longleftrightarrow(ii)
. For proving, the equivalence between
(i)
and
(iii)
, let us first assume
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and consider

f\in\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Then
f=\varphi_{1}h_{1}
for some
h_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, also the assumption further implies that
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).
Therefore
f=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g
, for some
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).
Hence, we have
\varphi_{1}h_{1}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g
, which implies
h_{1}=\varphi_{2}g
. But this is a contradiction to the fact that
h_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, unless
h_{1}=0
, which would further imply
f=0
. Thus,

\varphi_{1}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\{0\}.

For the other direction, we just need to show that
\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\{0\}
implies
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subseteq\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
since the other inclusion always holds. Suppose
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then

f=\varphi_{1}h_{1}\text{ for some }h_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Moreover,
h_{1}=\varphi_{2}g+k,
for some
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
k\in(\varphi_{2}H^{2})^{\perp}
using the orthogonal decomposition
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\oplus\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
. This further implies that

f=\varphi_{1}h_{1}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g+\varphi_{1}k.

Hence, we get

\varphi_{1}k=f-\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g,

which implies
\varphi_{1}k\in\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).
But
k\in(\varphi_{2}H^{2})^{\perp}
, implies that

\varphi_{1}k\in\varphi_{1}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\{0\}.

Thus,
\varphi_{1}k=0
, which further implies
k=0
and hence,
f=\varphi_{1}h_{1}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g
. This completes the proof.

Motivated by the above characterization, we frame the following definition.

Definition 6.4
.

For
n>1
, a pair of inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is said to be
co-prime
if
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Let us now identify few cases, where a pair of inner functions becomes co-prime.

Theorem 6.5
.

Any pair of inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
satisfying
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=\{0\}
is co-prime.

Proof.

The inclusion
\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subseteq\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is always satisfied. For the other direction, suppose
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Now,
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=\{0\}
implies that
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\bigvee\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(“
\bigvee
” denotes the span closure). Therefore, there exist
p_{k},q_{k}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

\varphi_{1}p_{k}+\varphi_{2}q_{k}\rightarrow 1,\text{in }H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Using Cauchy-Schwarz we get

f\varphi_{1}p_{k}+f\varphi_{2}q_{k}\rightarrow f\text{ in }H^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Since
f\in\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
f=\varphi_{1}h_{1}=\varphi_{2}h_{2}
, for some
h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Using this we get

f\varphi_{1}p_{k}+f\varphi_{2}q_{k}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}(h_{2}p_{k}+h_{1}q_{k})\rightarrow f.

Moreover,
\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a closed subspace in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, thus
f\in\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Let
f=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g
, for some
g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Since
|\varphi_{i}|=1
a.e.
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, then it implies
|f|=|g|
a.e.
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and since
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
therefore it follows that
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Hence we have
f=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}g
for some
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Thus
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subset\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. This completes the proof.

Let us now highlight a result that appeared in the article by Zhang et al. in
[
38
]
. For the sake of completeness, let us give an alternative proof for this fact.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
n>1
. If
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
has finite co-dimension, then
\varphi_{1}H^{2}\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}.

Proof.

The
n\geq 3
case, is a straightforward application of Ahern and Clark’s result
[
1
, Corollary]
, which states that the invariant subspace generated by a finite collection of functions
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
k<n
, is either the full space or has infinite co-dimension. Thus,
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
can have finite co-dimension only when
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, which is equivalent to saying that
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=\{0\}
Then, the conclusion follows by using Theorem
6.5
.

The
n=2
case follows verbatim as in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 3.5]
. The only thing to note is that the assumption
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
has finite co-dimension, implies that
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
is finite-dimensional. This further implies that

Z(\varphi_{1})\cap Z(\varphi_{2})\cap\mathbb{D}^{2},

is a finite set, where
Z(\varphi)
, corresponding to any analytic function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, denotes the set of all zeroes of
\varphi
inside
\mathbb{D}^{2}
. This completes the proof.

For the next result, we need to recall some facts about rational inner function from the influential book by Walter Rudin
[
36
]
. The following definition are from the excellent recent survey by Knese
[
29
]
.

Definition 6.7
.

Given polynomials
p,q\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
with no common factors, where
q(\bm{z})\neq 0
for
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the function
\varphi=p/q
is a rational inner function if
|\varphi(\bm{\mu})|=1
for almost every
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Rudin
[
36
, Theorem 5.2.5]
observed that any rational inner function
\varphi
can be re-written as the following (also see
[
29
, Theorem 1]
)

\varphi(\bm{z})=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\frac{\tilde{p}(\bm{z})}{p(\bm{z})},\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n},\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}),

where
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}
, and
\tilde{p}
is the reflection of the polynomial
p
defined by
\tilde{p}(\bm{z})=\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\overline{p(\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{z_{n}})}
. Here
\bm{m}=\mbox{deg}(p)=(\mbox{deg}_{z_{1}}(p),\ldots,\mbox{deg}_{z_{n}}(p))
is the multi-degree of
p
. Thus, any rational inner function
\varphi=\tilde{p}/p
by reflecting
p
at a degree higher than its multi-degree (as remarked by Knese
[
29
]
). Let us quickly observe a simple (probably well-known)fact useful for the sequel.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1}=\frac{\tilde{p}(\bm{z})}{p(\bm{z})}
and
\varphi_{2}=\frac{\tilde{q}(\bm{z})}{q(\bm{z})}
be rational inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. If
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
does not have any common inner factor, then
\tilde{p},\tilde{q}
does not have any common factor.

Proof.

Suppose
r
is a non-constant polynomial which is a common factor for
p
and
q
. Then
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
will have the common inner factor
\tilde{r}/r
. This will be a contradiction to the assumption
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
do not have any common inner factor. Now, it is folklore that
p,q
does not have a common inner factor if and only if the reflections
\tilde{p},\tilde{q}
does not have a common inner factor. It is a straightforward consequence of the following properties:
\widetilde{pq}=\tilde{p}\tilde{q}
and
\tilde{\tilde{p}}=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p
, for some
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. This completes the proof.

An interesting sub-collection of rational inner functions comes from the polydisc algebra. Recall that the polydisc algebra
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the collection of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
that are continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
. Rudin in
[
36
, Theorem 5.2.5]
, remarkably observed that any inner function
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is actually a rational inner function
\varphi(z)=\frac{\tilde{p}(\bm{z})}{p(\bm{z})}
such that
p(\bm{z})
has no zero on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
. Let us now present a few results essential for the sequel.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be inner functions inside
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. If
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
do not have any common inner factor. Then
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are co-prime

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1}=\frac{\tilde{p}}{p}
and
\varphi_{2}=\frac{\tilde{q}}{q}
. It is clear from Lemma
6.8
that if
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
do not have any common inner factor then
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{q}
do not have any common factor. Therefore, following
[
23
, Lemma 3.7, Step 2]
, we get that
\tilde{p}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\tilde{q}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
is a subspace of finite co-dimension and hence, it is also closed. Now,
p,q
have no zero on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{2}}
imply that
1/p,1/q\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Thus,

\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})=\frac{\tilde{p}}{p}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\frac{\tilde{q}}{q}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})\subseteq\tilde{p}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\tilde{q}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}),

which further implies that
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})=\tilde{p}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\tilde{q}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Thus,
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})+\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
has finite co-dimension. Therefore, by Theorem
6.6
, we get
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are co-prime. This completes the proof.

7.
On Hardy space over the unit polydisc

In this section, our goal is to establish several results on the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
and
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Let us now get back to the our main question of analysing the the compactness of the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
. Recall that using the Halmos decomposition (recall the expression in (
2.9
)) for the pair of projections
(P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}})
we got

\mathcal{L}_{0}=~\mathcal{M}\ominus\big(\mathcal{L}_{00}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{01}\big),

where

\mathcal{L}_{00}=\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\text{ and }\mathcal{L}_{01}=\left(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\right)\cap\left(\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\right)^{\perp}=\varphi_{1}\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}.

So, if we take a pair of
co-prime
inner functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
, then we get

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\big(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\oplus\varphi_{1}\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}\big)=\varphi_{1}\big(\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\ominus\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}\big)

Let us first collect an useful result.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then

\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}\ominus\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\overline{ran}[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}].

Proof.

Let us begin by noting that
\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}\subseteq\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
. This is true because, if
f\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}
, then
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}f=0
, therefore, multiplying on the left hand side by
T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}
gives
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}f=0
. Now, to prove the equality in the statement, we will first prove that
\mbox{ran }[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\subseteq\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}\ominus\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
. Let
f\in\mbox{ran }[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
, so there exists a
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

f=(T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}})g.

So,
T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}f=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}(T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}})g=(T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}})g=0
. Thus,
f\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
. Furthermore, let
h\in\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}\subseteq\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
. Then

\langle f,h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle(T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}})g,h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle(g,T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}})h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=0,

proves that
f\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\ominus\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
. Thus, we have proved
\mbox{ran }[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\subseteq\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}\ominus\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
and therefore,
\overline{\mbox{ran }}[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\subseteq\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}\ominus\ker~T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}
. For the opposite inclusion, we will prove that
\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\ominus\ker T_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}\varphi_{1}}=\overline{\mbox{ran}}\,T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mbox{ran}}[T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
. Let
x\in\overline{\mbox{ran}}\,T_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}\varphi_{2}}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
, so there must exist
\{y_{n}\}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that
x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}y_{n}
. Since
x\in\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
, we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}y_{n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}y_{n}=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}x=0.

This implies
x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]y_{n}
. Thus,
x\in\overline{\mbox{ran}}[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
. This completes the proof.

Using Lemma
7.1
, we get

(7.1)

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\varphi_{1}\overline{\mbox{ran}}[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}].

Moreover, we know from Lemma
2.2
that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
T(I-T)
is compact where
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
. Now,

T(I-T)=T-T^{2}=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\perp}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}},

implies that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\perp}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is compact. We are now ready to state our first main result of this section.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be a pair of co-prime inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
where
n>1
, then the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact.

Proof.

Let us first note that

\displaystyle[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}][T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]^{*}

\displaystyle=[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}][P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}+P_{\varphi_{1}}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}.

The third equality follows because
T_{\varphi_{2}}(\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))\subseteq\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Multiplying on both sides of the above identity by
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
further gives

P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}][T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]^{*}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\overline{\mbox{ran}}\,T_{\varphi_{1}}[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\subseteq\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we get

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}][T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]^{*}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}^{\perp}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}-P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore, we get
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]=0
, in other words,
\mbox{ran }[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\perp}
. Now, we know that the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is equivalent to the compactness of
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\perp}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
. The compactness of the later implies the compactness of
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]P_{\varphi_{2}}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
. This happens because

\mbox{ran }[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}\text{ and }\mbox{ran }[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\perp}

Now the compactness of
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]P_{\varphi_{2}}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
is again equiavalent to the compactness of
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
. Furthermore,

T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}=[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}][T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]^{*},

shows that the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is equivalent to the compactness of
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]
. Furthermore,

[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]T_{\varphi_{1}}=[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}],

shows that the compactness of
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
implies that
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact. The converse direction is straightforward. If
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact, then

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}+\text{ compact}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}+\text{ compact}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}+\text{ compact}

\displaystyle=P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{2}}+\text{ compact}.

This completes the proof.

The finite-rank case is more concrete. For proving that case, let us first recall an important identity on Toeplitz and Hankel operators that will be useful in the sequel.

(7.2)

T_{fg}=T_{f}T_{g}+H_{\bar{f}}^{*}H_{g}\quad(f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),

where
H_{\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the big Hankel operator defined by
H_{\varphi}:=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}L_{\varphi}|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
corresponding to
\varphi\in L^{\infty}
. Here
L_{\varphi}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be a pair of co-prime inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
where
n>1
, then the commutator
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank if and only if
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
.

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
7.2
, it is clear that
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank. However,

[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]=H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}^{*}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},

implies that
H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}^{*}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}
must be finite-rank, where
H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}},H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}
are the corresponding big Hankel operators. Now,
[
22
, Corollary 1]
, tells us that such products are finite-rank if and only if it is zero, so,
H_{\bar{\varphi}_{2}}^{*}H_{\bar{\varphi}_{1}}=0
. Thus,
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank implies
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
, which further implies that

P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{2}}=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}=T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}=P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}.

This completes the proof.

The above results have direct application to inner functions in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be inner functions in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
is compact.

Proof.

Following Lemma
2.7
, in a similar manner, we can consider
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
without any common inner factor. The proof then follows by using Theorem
6.9
and Theorem
7.2
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be inner functions in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Then
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]
is finite-rank if and only if
[P_{\varphi_{1}},P_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
.

Proof.

The proof then follows by using Theorem
6.9
and Corollary
7.3
.

We will now focus on when the product
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
becomes a compact operator.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is compact,

(ii)

[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact and
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.4
, we already know that the compactness of
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
implies that
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is finite dimensional and
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is compact. The finite-dimensionality of
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
implies
\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
from Theorem
6.6
. Thus, using identity (
7.1
), we get

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\overline{\mbox{ran}}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}].

We claim that
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact. Indeed,

P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}\text{ compact}\implies[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]P_{\varphi_{2}}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\text{ compact}\implies T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]\text{ compact}.

Now,

T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-P_{\varphi_{1}}=T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\big(P_{\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\big)T_{\varphi_{2}},

and

\big(P_{\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\big)^{2}=P_{\varphi_{1}}+T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}-P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}-T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}P_{\varphi_{1}}=P_{\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},

where the last equality follows by using the invariance
T_{\varphi_{2}}\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subseteq\varphi_{1}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Therefore, the compactness of
T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}[P_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}}]
implies that

T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\big(P_{\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}\big)=[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]\text{ is compact}.

This implies that
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},P_{\varphi_{1}}]T_{\varphi_{1}}=[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact.
Conversely, if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact, then

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}+\text{compact}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}+\text{compact}.

Since,
\mathcal{L}_{0}\subseteq\ker T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}}^{*}
, therefore
P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}=0
, and hence,
T=P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
is a compact operator. Thus, using Corollary
2.4
, we get
(ii)\implies(i)
. This completes the proof.

In the case of bidisc, the above characterization can be strengthened quite remarkably. The motivation comes from a recent characterization by Debnath et al. in
[
17
]
which proved that if
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a finite-rank projection then
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are separated and satisfies a finite Blaschke condition. As noted in the introduction, the following results were first observed by Zheng et al. in
[
38
]
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be non-constant and distinct inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is compact,

(ii)

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a finite-rank projection and thus, either one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
is a constant function.

(b)

\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are separated and finite Blaschke products.

Proof.

We already know from the above result that
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact and
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
is finite-dimensional. However, using
[
27
, Corollary 2]
, we can conclude that
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is compact if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]=0
, which further implies that
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a projection. Indeed,

\displaystyle(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+P_{\varphi_{1}}P_{\varphi_{2}}

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{2}}T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}-P_{\varphi_{2}}+P_{\varphi_{2}}P_{\varphi_{1}}

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}}).

Thus, we get
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is a compact projection, so it must be finite-rank. Moreover, by using
[
22
, Corollary 2]
, we can conclude that
[T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{2}}]=0
implies for each
i=1,2
, either
\bar{\varphi_{1}}(\bm{z})
or
\bar{\varphi_{2}}(\bm{z})
is analytic in
z_{i}
. In other words,
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are dependent on separate set of variables, and therefore,

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})\otimes(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

Hence, if the product is zero, then one of the symbols is a constant function. If it is non-zero then
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
both must be finite-Blaschke products depending on distinct variables. The converse part is straightforward. This completes the proof.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be a non-constant inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
n\geq 3
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is finite-rank,

(ii)

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=0
.

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
7.6
, in a similar manner, the product
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is finite-rank if and only if
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]
is finite-rank and
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}\cap\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
is finite-dimensional. However, using
[
22
, Corollary 1]
, we can again conclude that
[T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*},T_{\varphi_{1}}]=0
, which again by using
[
22
, Corollary 2]
, would imply that for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, either
\bar{\varphi_{1}}(\bm{z})
or
\bar{\varphi_{2}}(\bm{z})
is analytic in
z_{i}
. In other words,
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are dependent on disjoint set of variables, say
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\subseteq\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
, respectively such that
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\emptyset
, and therefore,

\begin{split}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=(I_{H_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})\otimes(I_{H_{\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})\otimes I_{H_{\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\setminus\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\\
\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\setminus\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})).\end{split}

where,
H_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
denote the functions in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
depending only on the variables in
\mathcal{I}
. Clearly,
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
cannot be finite-rank unless zero in the case when
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\neq\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{J}
. In the other case, both
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})
and
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
must be finite-rank projections. Hence,
\ker T_{\varphi_{1}}^{*}
and
\ker T_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}
must be finite-dimensional subspaces of
H_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
H_{\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
respectively. Since, at least one of
H_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
H_{\mathcal{J}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
are dependent on more than one variable, Ahern and Clark’s result will imply either
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})
or
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})
is zero, and so,
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{1}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P_{\varphi_{2}})=0.

This completes the proof.
