Effect of Chinese gallnuts tannic acids on the growth performance, intestinal morphometry and microbiome of broiler chickens
Authors: Jianguang Li, Zahid Shahbaz, Xiaoyue Feng, Xiangyue Han, Mengzhu Wang, Wangxin Du, Jialin Xu, Xiaoyu Li, Jiansong You, Dejie Liang, Xuemei Xiang, Lili Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rise in global restrictions on antibiotic growth promoters have intensified the search for sustainable and safe alternatives. Phytogenic feed additives (PFAs) have emerged as promising contestants because of their diverse biological properties. This research studied the potential effects of herbal extracts as the standardized PFA containing tannic acids derived from Chinese gallnuts on intestinal morphometry, growth performance and cecal microbiota. For this purpose, 224 one-day-old male Ross 308 broiler chicks were allocated to 28 cages, with eight birds per cage and seven replicate cages per treatment, in a 35-day trial. The four dietary treatments were: negative control (NC) without antibiotic growth promoters, NC + avilamycin at 100 g/t, NC + colistin sulfate at 100 g/t, and NC + Chinese gallnuts tannic acids (CGTA) at 200 g/t. The growth performance indices were observed during the whole production cycle. The intestinal morphometry was observed on the day 35. The dietary inclusion of CGTA significantly improved feed conversion ratio (FCR) during 0 to 10 and 0 to 21 days as compared to NC diet treatment. Moreover, the European Production Efficiency Factor (EPEF) of 441 was also observed numerically higher in the CGTA supplementation treatment. Additionally, the intestinal morphometric analysis revealed a substantial rise in both the duodenal and jejunum height of villus and a decrease in epithelial thickness in both duodenum and jejunum, indicating the enhanced absorptive capacity and gut integrity. Crypt depth and goblet cell numbers were not affected significantly. Notably, microbiota analysis showed no significant differences in alpha or beta diversity. In conclusion, dietary supplementation of CGTA at 200 g/t improved feed efficiency and intestinal morphometry in broilers, without altering the community of cecal microbiota.

Introduction

The modern global industry is facing unprecedented challenges in maintaining sustainable production practices while meeting the escalating demand for animal protein. In broiler production, the antibiotic growth promoters (AGPs) have played a fundamental role by effectively enhancing growth performance, improving feed efficiency, and controlling pathogenic bacteria through intestinal microbiota modulation (Broom, 2018). However, the occurrence and rapid dissemination of antimicrobial resistance have triggered a fundamental transformation in livestock production systems worldwide (Nathan et al., 2022; Ibeagha‐Awemu et al., 2025; Elbehiry and Marzouk, 2025). Many countries of the world have implemented strict regulations prohibiting the use of AGPs (Rahman et al., 2022). This paradigm shift creates an urgent need for the development of safe, efficient, and justifiable alternatives that can maintain gut well-being and efficiency in broilers while addressing public health concerns (Stoica and Cox, 2021). Recent studies also indicate that intestinal integrity, immunity, and performance are closely connected in broilers under disease and drug-resistance challenges (Hayajneh et al., 2024). In addition, pathogen pressure in poultry production systems remains an important challenge, further supporting the need for safe nutritional strategies that enhance gut health and production efficiency (Okpalaji et al., 2025).

The search for sustainable AGPs alternatives has started on a large scale, and this search has led the researchers to explore different natural compounds (Sapsuha et al., 2025). The PFAs have emerged as a promising competitor of AGPs among these natural compounds, and recent broiler studies using essential oils have also reported anticoccidial, growth-promoting, hematological and serological benefits (Almahallawi et al., 2024). The PFAs are plant-derived substances that consist of herbs, spices, and their extracted compounds, and recent work with Phyllanthus amarus leaf extract in broiler chicks further supports the use of plant-derived dietary interventions in poultry production (Ike et al., 2025). PFAs offer multidimensional benefits due to their unique and diverse bioactive properties (Attia et al., 2025), and other non-antibiotic feed interventions, such as probiotic-based strategies, have also been shown to improve broiler performance and meat quality (Susalam et al., 2024). The immunomodulatory, antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects are due to the unique chemical composition of PFAs (Wang et al., 2024; Ullah et al., 2025). Antibiotics usually target the specific microbial pathway, but phytogenic compounds show a pleiotropic mode of action; due to this property, they are less susceptible to resistance development (Biswas et al., 2024; Al-Arnoot et al., 2025).

Tannins represent a particularly fascinating class of polyphenolic compounds, such as tannic acids, within the broad category of phytogenic additives (Choi and Kim, 2020). Tannic acid, characterized by its ability to bind and precipitate proteins, represents a class of secondary metabolites (Hassanpour et al., 2011). The tannins, such as those derived from Chinese gallnuts, are hydrolysable and consist of a glucose core which is esterified with gallic acid (Macáková et al., 2014). These compounds possess broad-spectrum anti-microbial activity against both Gram-negative bacteria and Gram-positive bacteria, anti-inflammatory actions through modulation of inflammatory cytokine production, and antioxidant properties through free radical scavenging (Buzzini et al., 2008).

The source of tannic acids used in this study is Chinese gallnut, a traditional medicinal plant extensively utilized in East Asia pharmacology, which contains very high concentrations of hydrolysable tannins (Gao et al., 2018). The tannic acid extracted from Chinese gallnut contain a core of glucose and gallic acids molecules are attached to it in a specific symmetrical manner. These tannic acids are hyper galloylated as compared to other tannins extracted from other sources. Being more galloylated the tannic acids extracted from Chinese gallnut, showing stronger antioxidant property as compared to other tannins. Moreover, the tannic acids extracted from Chinese gallnut having purity > 90% as compared to other tannins extracted from different sources (Xu et al., 2008). The efficacy of tannin-based products is affected by different factors such as the botanical source, chemical composition, extraction methodology, dosage, and interaction of tannins with dietary components (Molino et al., 2023). Due to this variability, the evaluation of well-defined, standardized tannin products under controlled experimental conditions is essential to determine their effectiveness and ideal usage conditions.

Due to the unique physiological characteristics, the avian digestive system influences the efficacy of dietary interventions. The small intestine serves as the major source of nutrients’ absorption and represents a critical target for interventions aimed at improving production efficiency. Intestinal morphometry, such as the crypt depth, villus height, and epithelial integrity, directly correlates with the absorptive capacity and gut barrier function (Stalla et al., 2022; Gabler and Burrough, 2023). The microbiota of the gut plays a fundamental role in nutrient metabolism, pathogen exclusion, and immune system maturation (Stalla et al., 2022). The dynamic interplay between dietary components, microbial ecology, and intestinal morphometry creates a complex biological network that determines production outcomes (Zhang et al., 2022). The understanding of these interactions becomes crucial when evaluating novel feed additives.

Preliminary studies have suggested potential benefits of tannin-based additives in broiler nutrition, while comprehensive evaluations directly comparing CGTA with both negative controls and established AGPs across multiple parameters, such as intestinal morphometry, growth performance and microbial analysis, remain limited (Liu et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2024).

The aim of this research is to assess the effects of dietary supplementation of tannic acids extracted from Chinese gallnuts on growth performance, cecal microbiota, and intestinal morphometry of broiler chickens from 1 to 35 days of age, compared with antibiotic-free diets and those supplemented with conventional AGPs.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval

The Massey University Animal Ethics Committee authorized the experimental protocols for the animal trials, which followed the New Zealand Code of Practice for the Care and Use of Animals for Scientific Purposes (trial number P20004). Every animal experiment was carried out by Massey University's Monogastric Research Centre in Palmerston, North New Zealand.

Preparation of Chinese gallnuts tannic acids (CGTA)

The Chinese gallnuts were selected and grinded, and the fine powder obtained was subjected to solvent extraction system by using a hydro-organic solution (Liu and Li, 2007). The hydrolysable tannins were optimized under controlled conditions of agitation and temperature (Zhang et al., 2005). The resulting crude tannin solution was subsequently purified according to the methods of Cao (2013). The purified extract was then spray-dried to yield a stable powder form of CGTA. The purity of the tannic acid was confirmed to exceed 90% through HPLC analysis. The test products, colistin sulfate and avilamycin was provided at the research site.

Broiler chicks, housing, and dietary treatments

A total of 224 one-day-old male Ross 308 broiler chicks were obtained from a local hatchery. Upon arrival, all chicks were individually weighed and allocated to 28 cages, with eight birds per cage. The cages were randomly assigned to four dietary treatments, with seven replicate cages per treatment. The four dietary treatments were as follows: treatment A, negative control (NC) diet without AGPs; treatment B, NC diet supplemented with avilamycin at 100 g/t; treatment C, NC diet supplemented with colistin sulfate at 100 g/t; and treatment D, NC diet supplemented with CGTA at 200 g/t (A through D; Table 1). Diets and growth performance assessment

Feeding was done in three stages: starter, grower, and finisher. Diets for starters, growers, and finishers were available from days 0–10, 11–21, and 22–35, respectively. A NC diet composed of corn and soybean meal was formulated for every phase (Table 2). In each feeding program, six experimental meals were created from the NC diet utilizing various AGPs and test goods, as indicated in Table 1. Prior to being introduced to the batch mixer, the AGPs and test samples were combined with a part of the diet (about 10 kg) for the mixer. Each treatment diet was prepared, and the mixer was then cleaned. Every diet was steam-conditioned at 75 degrees Celsius. The mash was conditioned for thirty seconds, and the conditioner's outlet temperature was recorded. Following conditioning, the diets were pelleted in a pellet mill (Richard Size Limited Engineers, Orbit 15, Kingston-upon-Hull, UK) with a die ring (3-mm holes and 35-mm thickness) that could produce 180 kg of feed per hour. At weekly intervals, body weight and feed intake were noted. Every day, mortality was noted. The EPEF was calculated using the following formula: EPEF = (Average grams gained/day × % survival rate) / Feed per gain × 10.

16S-rRNA amplicon sequencing

On day 35, microbial community profiling was done using cecal sample. One bird per replicate cage was chosen and euthanized in a humane manner, by cervical dislocation and immediately dissected to extract the cecal tissue. A commercial Stool DNA extraction kit (Geneaid Presto™) was used to extract total genomic DNA from homogenized cecal contents by following instructions of the DNA isolation kit.

Gel electrophoresis was used to determine the quality and integrity of DNA before downstream analysis. The region (V3- V4) of 16S rRNA gene was amplified by using Barcoded forward (16SfV3: AATGATACGGCGACCACCGAGATCTACACxxxxxxxxTATGGTAATTGGCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG) and reverse (16SrV4: CAAGCAGAAGACGGCATACGAGATxxxxxxxxAGTCAGTCAGCCGGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT) amplicon primers and AccuPrime Pfx SuperMix (InvitrogenTM 12344040). “xxxxxxxx” indicating the unique sequence of barcode which was assigned to each sample.

High- fidelity DNA polymerase master mix was used to amplify the DNA using the following thermal cycling conditions: loading of initial denaturation at 95°C for 2 minutes, 30 cycles of (95°C, 55°C, 72°C) and final extension of 72°C for 10 minutes.

After amplifications, amplicons were normalized and purified with a commercial normalization plate kit (SequalPrepTM). The concentration of libraries was determined and fragment size was confirmed. The libraries were mixed in equal proportions so that the representation of each sample in the pool would be even. The library was diluted and then subjected to alkaline treatment to denature the pooled library to the right concentration. Similarly, a PhiX control was prepared and spiked into the final library mixture at 20 percent to serve as an internal sequencing control. The final library mixture was sequenced on Illunina MiSeq platform using paired- end chemistry.

The reads were handled by using the standard QIIME2 v.2020.6 pipeline. The samples are grouped by the categorical value “Treatment”. Alpha diversity measures were used to test the richness and evenness of microbes within a single sample, whereas beta analysis was used to test the differences in composition across treatment groups. The Naive Bayes classifier trained on the Silva_138 reference database at 99 percent sequence identity was used to classify the taxa.

Intestinal morphometry

On day 35, one bird per replicate cage was selected for intestinal morphometric analysis, resulting in seven birds per treatment and 28 birds in total. Immediately after euthanasia, approximately 5 cm segments were collected from the midpoint of the duodenum and jejunum of each selected bird. Thus, 56 intestinal samples were obtained in total, including 28 duodenal and 28 jejunal samples. Immediately after excision, these segments of intestine were washed carefully with ice-cold normal saline to remove the intestinal lumen contents after which these segments were immersed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin solution to fix them. After 72 hours, these segments were transported to 70% ethanol. An automated tissue processor was used for succeeding processing. During processing, at ambient temperature, the dehydration of the preserved segments was done by using progressive ethanol series. After this the isopropyl alcohol was used for clearing the segments. After clearing, the next steps such as infiltration and embedding in paraffin blocks were performed. Following embedding in paraffin blocks, all the tissue segments were sectioned at a thickness of 5 µm using a rotary microtome, consistent with routine histopathological processing approaches used for broiler tissues (Sharif et al., 2024). Tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for intestinal morphometric evaluation. The stained sections were examined using an Olympus BX43 light microscope (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), and images were captured under 10× and 40× objective lenses. For each intestinal segment, ten well-oriented villi and their associated crypts were selected per bird. Villus height was measured from the tip of the villus to the villus-crypt junction, whereas crypt depth was measured from the base of the crypt to the villus-crypt junction. The average of these ten measurements was calculated for each sample per bird per segment. Epithelial thickness was measured at representative points along the villus epithelium. Goblet cells were counted along the villus epithelium and expressed as the number of goblet cells per 100 µm of villus height.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the General Linear Model procedure of SAS. For growth performance variables, the cage was considered the experimental unit. For intestinal morphometric parameters and cecal microbiota analyses, the individual bird was considered the experimental unit. Treatment means were compared using the Least Significant Difference (LSD) test when the ANOVA indicated significance. Differences were considered significant at P ≤ 0.05.

Discussion

The findings of this study provide novel insights into the potential of tannin - based additive CGTA as an alternative to AGPs in broiler production. The results show that CGTA supplementation significantly improved key performance and intestinal health parameters while maintaining a stable cecal microbiome profile.

The most notable effect of tannic acid supplementation was observed on the broiler performance parameter, like feed per gain, during the critical starter period (0-10 days) and during (0-21 days) period. The F/G reflects the FCR. The significant improvements in FCR were observed during the starter period (1.051 for CGTA vs 1.085 for NC) and the continued superiority during the 0 to 21 days of trial (1.247 for CGTA vs 1.271 for NC) indicate enhanced nutrient utilization during these developmental stages. Moreover, the CGTA treatment exhibited a similar effect as colistin sulfate during the starter period (0–10 d). Specifically, at 21 days, the group supplemented with CGTA exhibited improved feed conversion ratio compared with the two antibiotic-supplemented treatments. Notably, the antibiotic treatments failed to demonstrate any significant enhancement in FCR, as evidenced by the absence of differences when compared to the control treatment. Since the values of FCR were low in tannic acid supplemented treatment, it indicates the high efficiency of these chicks. Similar improvements in broiler productivity have been linked with dietary strategies that support gut morphology, digesta characteristics and blood metabolites (Islam et al., 2024). The early improvement in FCR is particularly important in commercial broiler systems as it reduces the overall production cost (Pizolotto et al., 2024). Recent evidence also shows that plant-derived products can exert growth-promoting effects in broilers, particularly under intestinal disease challenge conditions (Abbas et al., 2025). Redondo et al. (2022) also observed the similar early phase improvements.

Liu et al. (2018) reported that the dietary supplementation of chestnut tannins led to improved FCR due to the reduced inflammation of intestine and improved barrier functions. In contrast, Zhang et al. (2022) reported that no significant difference was observed in FCR during 1 to 21 days when the broilers are fed with the diet containing tannic acids and aflatoxin (AF + 250 mg/kg TA) but there were significant improvements in the average daily gain and average daily feed intake.

In this study, other performance parameters, such as weight gain in the tannic acid - supplemented treatment, were numerically comparable to or even better than those in other treatments, including the antibiotic - supplemented treatment, during the early critical phase (0 - 10 days). However, feed intake did not significantly differ across all treatments throughout the entire broiler production trial period (0 - 35 days). This finding suggests that tannic acid supplementation does not enhance chick appetite compared with AGPs, which increase feed intake in broilers by modulating the gut microbiota (Gadde et al., 2017).

The EPEF served as an integrated performance indicator, and its results further underscore the practical significance of our findings. Although not statistically significant, higher numerical values of EPEF were observed in both the CGTA and avilamycin treatments compared to the NC treatment. These findings suggest potential economic benefits associated with higher EPEF in commercial production settings (Mavromati et al., 2018; Janjić et al., 2022; Kotarev and Ivanova, 2022). Mortality rates did not significantly differ throughout the entire broiler production cycle (0 - 35 days), with values ranging from 3.6% to 7.1% (Girsang et al., 2023). Specifically, the mortality rate in the CGTA - treated group was 3.6% at the end of the production trial. This rate was numerically lower than that in the NC treatment and comparable to the antibiotic - treated positive control 2 (colistin sulfate). Interestingly, the avilamycin added treatment exhibited the highest mortality rate of 7.1%. These results indicate that tannic acid derived from Chinese gallnuts is a safe alternative to antibiotics in feed additives for enhancing broiler production efficiency. This agrees with recent findings that phytogenic additives, including essential oils, may support growth performance and health-related indices in broiler birds (Almahallawi et al., 2024).

The morphological analysis revealed the substantial improvements in small intestine architecture following CGTA supplementation, with effects manifesting differently across intestinal segments. In the duodenum, the CGTA supplemented treatment exhibited villus height (1202 μm) significantly greater than the villus height (1073 μm) of the NC treatment. Similarly, in the jejunum, the villus height (697 μm) increased significantly in the tannic acid-supplemented treatment D as compared to the NC treatment. The increase in duodenal and jejunum villus height represents a physiologically meaningful enhancement in absorptive surface area (Donaldson et al., 2021; Chaiyasing et al., 2024), potentially contributing to improving feed utilization efficiency (Hassanpour et al., 2016; Madigan-Stretton et al., 2020). Similar improvements in intestinal morphometry have also been reported in broiler chickens supplemented with Moringa oleifera leaves powder and Saccharomyces cerevisiae (Nastoh et al., 2025). More importantly, supplementation of CGTA resulted in a significant decrease in epithelium thickness in both the duodenum and jejunum as compared to the NC treatment. A thinner epithelium is generally linked with the reduced inflammatory response and improved gut barrier function, suggesting that CGTA is contributing to a healthier intestinal mucosa (Seo et al., 2021). According to the study of Napier et al. (2022), the supplementation of tannic acid extracted from Galla Chinesis at 300 mg/kg resulted in improved intestinal morphometry and reduced expression of NF-κB in intestinal mucosa. And ultimately in our current study, the nutrient absorption enhances in the intestine, leading to the increased feed efficiency in broilers.

The analysis of crypt depth in our current study, revealed no statistically significant difference among dietary treatments in either the duodenum or jejunum. The maintenance of normal crypt depth measurement indicates that the observed benefits occurred without disrupting the fundamental regenerative processes of the intestinal epithelium (Williams et al., 2015). This finding supports the safety profile of CGTA supplementation and suggests that the morphological improvements reflect optimization rather than pathological changes. Goblet cells are critical for mucosal defense as these cells produce and secrete mucins, which form the protective mucous layer lining the intestinal epithelium (Fan et al., 2023). The stability in goblet cell numbers indicates that the fundamental defensive capacity of the mucosal barrier was not quantitatively altered by the inclusion of CGTA (Archer and Hodin, 2001).

Despite the significant improvements in intestinal morphometry and early-stage performance, deep sequencing analysis revealed no significant changes in the overall diversity or composition of cecal microbiota between treatments. The absence of significant changes in alpha and beta diversity suggests that CGTA supplementation did not cause broad restructuring of the cecal microbial community. Similar findings have been reported in poultry studies where non-antibiotic feed additives maintained or improved performance without producing major shifts in overall microbial diversity (Fonseca et al., 2024). Therefore, the beneficial effects of CGTA may be related more to improved intestinal mucosal structure, reduced epithelial stress, or modulation of microbial activity rather than large-scale changes in microbial composition detectable by 16S rRNA sequencing. In this context, 16S rRNA sequencing provides useful taxonomic information but does not directly measure microbial metabolites or functional activity, which may explain why performance and intestinal morphometry improved despite stable cecal microbial diversity. These results agree with many recent studies showing that not all the growth promoting additives induce major microbial shift (Demir et al., 2005; Polycarpo et al., 2022; Mazzero et al., 2023; Fonseca et al., 2024). The consistency of microbial composition across all the designated treatments in the current study suggests that the dramatic microbial shift may not be necessary for beneficial effects. The alpha diversity measured through Pielou’s evenness index showed that microbial composition was evenly distributed across all the designated treatments. Similarly, the beta diversity analysis revealed that there was no statistically significant difference in microbial composition between all the treatments. Three dominant bacterial genera (Bacteroides, Lactobacillus, and Romboutsia) remained consistent across all dietary treatments (Marimuthu et al., 2019). This finding indicates that the primary mode of action of CGTA may not involve large-scale restructuring of the cecal microbial ecosystem, but rather through modulation of microbial metabolism that is not detected by 16S RNA sequencing alone. The benefits may arise from improved gut barrier function, reduced inflammation, or subtle inhibition of specific pathogens without affecting overall microbial diversity.

Taken together, the improvements in FCR during the starter and grower phases appear to be more closely associated with changes in intestinal morphometry than with major alterations in cecal microbial diversity. Increased villus height in the duodenum and jejunum may enhance absorptive surface area, while reduced epithelial thickness may facilitate nutrient absorption and indicate improved mucosal condition (Chaiyasing et al., 2024; Nastoh et al., 2025). Because feed intake was not significantly changed among treatments, the improved feed efficiency in the CGTA group likely reflects better nutrient utilization rather than increased feed consumption. The stable cecal microbiota profile further suggests that CGTA did not disrupt the microbial ecosystem, which is desirable for maintaining intestinal homeostasis. Similar findings have been reported in poultry studies where non-antibiotic feed additives improved or maintained performance without necessarily producing major shifts in overall microbial diversity (Fonseca et al., 2024). Therefore, the performance response observed in this study may result from improved intestinal absorptive architecture together with preservation of cecal microbial stability.

Conclusion

Dietary supplementation with CGTA at 200 g/t improved feed efficiency during the early growth phase and enhanced intestinal morphometry in broiler chickens, particularly by increasing villus height and reducing epithelial thickness. The supplementation did not markedly alter cecal microbial diversity, indicating that the beneficial effects of CGTA may be associated mainly with improved intestinal absorptive structure rather than major shifts in the cecal microbiota. Therefore, CGTA may be considered a promising phytogenic feed additive for improving broiler production efficiency as an alternative to antibiotic growth promoters.
