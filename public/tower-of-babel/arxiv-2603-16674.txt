Profinite detection of free products and free factors
Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain, Henrique Souza, Pavel Zalesski

Abstract
. Let G G be the fundamental group of a graph of finitely generated virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. We show that G G is cohomologically good if G G is residually finite. If G G is LERF, we prove that G G splits non-trivially as a free product if and only if its profinite completion G ^ \widehat{G} splits non-trivially as a free profinite product. Moreover, we are able to detect one-ended free factors of G G from G ^ \widehat{G} . As an application, we deduce that any profinitely rigid word in a finitely generated free group is universally profinitely rigid.



1.
Introduction

A central theme in modern infinite group theory is the extend to which the finite quotients of a group determine its algebraic structure. Problems of this sort, colloquially known as
profinite rigidity
or
profinite detection
problems, asks which properties of a finitely generated residually finite group
G
can be recovered from its profinite completion
\widehat{G}
. In this setting, the strongest question one might ask is: given two finitely generated and residually finite groups
G
and
H
such that their profinite completions
\widehat{G}
and
\widehat{H}
are isomorphic, must
G
and
H
be themselves isomorphic?

Groups for which this holds are called profinitely rigid in the
absolute sense
. Going past the realm of abelian groups, very few groups are known to be profinitely rigid in the absolute sense, with some recent known examples coming from three-dimensional hyperbolic geometry (
[
5
,
6
]
), some lamplighter groups (
[
4
]
) and free meta-abelian groups (
[
40
]
). A major open problem in the field, due to Remeslennikov, asks whether free groups are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense (
[
20
, Problem 5.48]
). A weaker notion is that of
relative
profinite rigidity, where one asks whether the profinite completion
\widehat{G}
determines the isomorphism class of
G
among groups in a given class
\mathcal{C}
of finitely generated residually finite groups. A major achievement in this direction is due to Wilton (
[
34
]
), who showed that free groups are rigid in the class of hyperbolic fundamental groups of graphs of free groups with cyclic edge groups. Later, the hyperbolicity assumption was dropped by Morales in
[
26
]
.

Recently, two broader questions have come into consideration. We say that profinite completions
detect free products
in
\mathcal{C}
if every finitely generated residually finite group
G\in\mathcal{C}
whose profinite completion
\widehat{G}
decomposes as a non-trivial free profinite product
\widehat{G}=L\amalg K
also decomposes non-trivially as a free product
G=H*U
. We say that profinite completions
detect free factors
in
\mathcal{C}
if for every finitely generated residually finite group
G
with a finitely generated subgroup
H\leq G
such that
\widehat{G}\simeq\overline{H}\amalg K
there exists a subgroup
U\leq G
such that
G\simeq H*U
.

These two properties are related, but we have no reason to believe that one implies the other. It is known that, in the class of virtually free groups, profinite completions detect free products (
[
12
]
) and free factors (
[
15
]
). In this paper, we will consider this properties in the class of fundamental groups of graphs of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups, a class henceforth denoted by
\mathcal{C}
.

A group is called
LERF
(
locally extended residually finite
, or
subgroup separable
) if every finitely generated subgroup is closed in the profinite topology. Equivalently,
G
is LERF if for every
H\leq G
finitely generated and every
x\not\in H
there is a finite index subgroup
N\leq G
containing
H
but not
x
. If
G
is LERF, then the profinite topology of
G
induces the profinite topology on each of its finitely generated subgroups (
[
29
, Lem. 11.1.4]
). The main result of the paper is that, assuming the LERF condition, profinite completions detect free products in
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated LERF group that decomposes as the fundamental group of a graph of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. If
\widehat{G}
decomposes as a non-trivial free profinite product, then
G
decomposes as a non-trivial free product.

At this moment, we don’t know whether the LERF condition in Theorem
1.1
is necessary or if it can be replaced by the condition of residual finiteness. We also obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G=F_{1}*_{c_{1}=c_{2}}F_{2}
be the free product of two free groups amalgamated along non-trivial proper cyclic subgroups
\langle c_{i}\rangle<F_{i}
. If
\widehat{G}=L\amalg K
splits non-trivially as a free profinite product, then one of the
F_{i}
splits non-trivially as a free product
F_{i}=H_{i}*K_{i}
such that
c_{i}\in H_{i}
.

One of the main ingredients of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the proof of a Kurosh-style decomposition for virtually free groups.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually free group and suppose that
\widehat{G}\leq P=L\amalg K
. Then

G\simeq\left(\underset{g\in L\backslash P/G}{\ast}G\cap{{}^{g}L}\right)*\left(\underset{h\in K\backslash P/G}{\ast}G\cap{{}^{h}K}\right)*F

where
F
is a free subgroup of
G
.

With respect to the problem of detecting free factors, we can prove the following statement.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
be the fundamental group of a graph of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. Assume that
G
is finitely generated and LERF. Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
G
and moreover assume that either

(1)

H
has at most one end or

(2)

H\leq{{}^{g}\mathcal{G}(v)}
is contained in some
G
-conjugate of a vertex group.

If
\overline{H}
is a free profinite factor of
\widehat{G}
, then
H
is a free factor of
G
.

Another result we obtain, which is of independent interest, concerns the property of being
cohomologically good
(or
cohomologically separable
); that is, if
A
is a finite
G
-module, then the restriction map on cohomology

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{*}(\widehat{G},A)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(G,A)

is an isomorphism in every degree.
The profinite completion of a finitely generated LERF group
G
from
\mathcal{C}
has a very transparent structure: it is the profinite fundamental group of the induced graph of profinite groups given by the profinite completions of the vertex and edge groups of
G
. This implies that they are cohomologically good (see, for instance,
[
16
, Thm. 3.9]
). We extend this result to finitely generated residually finite groups in the class
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a graph of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. If
G
is finitely generated and residually finite, then
G
is cohomologically good. In particular, if
G
is not virtually free, then
\widehat{G}
has virtual cohomological dimension 2.

This result was previously known in the case when
G
is the fundamental group of a finite graph of cyclic groups by a result of Cohen and Wykowski (
[
10
]
).

We also present an application of Theorem
1.3
concerning profinite properties of words in free groups. Following Hanani, Meiri and Puder (
[
18
, Def. 1.3]
), we say that a word
w
in a finitely generated free group
F
is
profinitely rigid
if any other word
u\in F
that lies in the same
\Aut(\widehat{F})
-orbit of
w
is also in the same
\Aut(F)
-orbit (see Section
6
for an equivalent description). A profinitely rigid word
w\in F
is
universally profinitely rigid
if it is profinitely rigid in any free extension
F*H
of
F
, where
H
is another finitely generated free group.

It has been conjectured that primitive words, those that generate a free factor of
F
, are profinitely rigid, and this has been confirmed by Puder and Parzanchesvki in
[
27
]
who showed that they are also universally profinitely rigid. Another proof was given by Wilton in
[
34
]
. Later, it was shown in
[
18
]
that if
w\in F
is profinitely rigid then so is
w^{d}
for every
d\in\bbz
, as well as proving that a basic commutator
[x,y]
of two elements of a basis of
F
is profinitely rigid. In
[
35
]
, it was shown that the surface words
x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}\cdots x_{n}^{2}
and
[x_{1},x_{2}]\cdots[x_{2g-1},x_{2g}]
are profinitely rigid in
F_{n}
and
F_{2g}
respectively. We prove that the notions of profinite rigidity and universal profinite rigidity are equivalent:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F
be a finitely generated free group and
w\in F
. Then,
w
is profinitely rigid in
F
if and only if it is profinitely rigid in
F*H
for every finitely generated free group
H
.

Observe that any non-trivial element is clearly profinitely rigid in the free group of rank one, and hence Theorem
1.6
implies, in particular, the profinite rigidity of powers of primitive words.

Finally, we describe the structure of the paper. We collect in Section
2
essential results about the class
\mathcal{C}
, abstract and profinite Bass–Serre theories, small cancellation groups and completed group algebras that will be needed throughout the paper. In Section
3
we describe the profinite completion
\widehat{G}
of groups in
\mathcal{C}
splitting as graphs of groups with a single vertex, and prove
1.5
. We also pose a conjecture about the profinite completion of certain one-relator groups that is related to Remeslennikov’s question (see
1
).

The core of Section
4
is the proof of
1.3
, and the core of Section
5
is the proof of a slightly more precise version of
1.1
, from which both
1.4
and
1.2
also follows easily. The application to the profinite rigidity of words,
1.6
, is explained and proven in Section
6
. We finish the paper discussing possible generalizations of these results to the class
\mathcal{L}
of limit groups in Section
7
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

The disjoint union of two sets
A
and
B
is denoted by
A\sqcup B
. For two group elements

, conjugation is a left action:
{}^{g}h=ghg^{-1}
. If

(
[

, Sec. 2.3]
). A closed subgroup is open if and only if
[G\colon H]
is a natural number, that is,
a_{p}\in\bbz
for all
p
and almost all
a_{p}
are zero. The order of a profinite group

is the supernatural number
|G|=[G\colon\{1\}]
.

We denote the (abstract) free product of two groups
H
and
U
by
H*U
, and the free profinite product of two profinite groups

and
K
by
H\amalg K
. We refer the reader to
[
28
, Ch. 9]
for the definition and basic properties of free profinite products. If
G
is an abstract group, we denote by
\widehat{G}=\varprojlim_{N\unlhd_{\mathrm{f.i.}}G}G/N
its profinite completion, the inverse limit being taken over all normal subgroups
N\unlhd G
such that the index
[G\colon N]
is finite.

If
G
is an abstact (resp. profinite) group, we denote by
\mathrm{H}^{i}(G,A)
(resp.
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,A)
) the
i
-th cohomology group (resp.
i
-th continuous cohomology group) of a
G
-module (resp. discrete
G
-module)

. We say that
\cd_{p}(G)\leq n
if
\mathrm{H}^{i}(G,A)
(resp.
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,A)
) vanishes for every

and every
p
-primary
G
-module (resp.
p
-primary discrete
G
-module)

.

We denote by
\bbf_{p}
the field of
p
elements. If
X
is any set and

is a commutative unital ring,

denotes the free

-module with basis

. For any group
G
(resp.

-set

),

(resp.

) has naturally the structure of an

-algebra (resp.
R[X]
-module) called the group algebra of

over
R
. If

is a profinite group, the completed group algebra of

over
\bbf_{p}
is the profinite ring
\bbf_{p}[\![G]\!]=\varprojlim_{U\unlhd_{o}G}\bbf_{p}[G/U]
where

runs through the open subgroups of

. If

is a profinite group acting continuously on a profinite set

, where each

is a finite

\bbf_{p}[\![X]\!]
the
\bbf_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
\varprojlim\bbf_{p}[X_{i}]
. For any
G
-module

, we denote by

the submodule of

-fixed points.

If

is the augmentation map of an augmented

-algebra

, we denote by

its kernel

, called the augmentation ideal of

. It is well known that the augmentation ideal of
\bbf_{p}[G]
(resp.

where

is any generating (resp. topologically generating) set of

. When

If

2.2.
The fundamental group of a graph of groups

A
graph

Y=V(Y)\sqcup E(Y)
is the disjoint union of a set
V=V(Y)
of
vertices
and a set
E=E(Y)
of
edges
together with two maps
i,t\colon E\to V
that associate to each edge
e\in E
its
initial

i(e)
and terminal
t(e)
vertices. Loops
i(e)=t(e)
are allowed. In other words, we consider directed multigraphs. We say that
Y
is connected if it is connected as an undirected multigraph. A
tree
is a connected graph which has no undirected cycles. By Zorn’s lemma, every connected graph has a maximal subtree.

A
graph of groups

(Y,\mathcal{G})
consists of a graph
Y
, vertex groups
\{\mathcal{G}(v)\colon v\in V(Y)\}
and edge groups
\{\mathcal{G}(e)\colon e\in E(Y)\}
together with two homomorphisms
i_{e}\,,t_{e}\colon\mathcal{G}(e)\to\mathcal{G}(i(e))\,,\mathcal{G}(t(e))
for each edge
e\in E(Y)
. If all the homomorphisms
i_{e}\,,t_{e}
are injective, the graph of groups is called
faithful
. We will only consider faithful graphs of groups.

Given a connected graph of groups
(Y,\mathcal{G})
and a maximal subtree
T
of
Y
, the
fundamental group

G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G},T)
of
(Y,\mathcal{G})
with respect to
T
is the group with presentation:

•

generators
\{\mathcal{G}(v),q_{e}\colon v\in V(Y)\,,e\in E(Y)\}
;

•

relations of each
\mathcal{G}(v)
for each vertex
v\in V(Y)
;

•

relations
{{}^{q_{e}}i_{e}(g)}=q_{e}i_{e}(g)q_{e}^{-1}=t_{e}(g)
for each
e\in E(Y)
and
g\in\mathcal{G}(e)
;

•

relations
q_{e}=1
for each
e\in T
.

One of the first results of Bass–Serre theory (see
[
13
, Sec. I.7]
) is that any other maximal subtree yields an isomorphic fundamental group. Hence,
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
is well defined up to isomorphism. Another result (
[
13
, Cor. I.7.5]
) states that if
(Y,\mathcal{G})
is connected and faithful, then the canonical maps
\mathcal{G}(v)\to\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
are injective and we may identify each
\mathcal{G}(v)
with its image.

Let
G
act faithfully on a tree
X
and let
S\subset X
be a
connected transversal
for the
G
-action. This is a subset of
X
(in general, not a subgraph) that is in bijection with
G/X
, any two of whose vertices are connected by a path in
S
and such that
i(e)\in S
for each edge
e\in S
. For each edge
e\in S
, there is a unique vertex of
S
in the same
G
-orbit as
t(e)
, so we choose
q_{e}\in G
such that
q_{e}\cdot t(e)\in S
, taking
q_{e}=1
if
t(e)\in S
. The collection
(q_{e}\colon e\in E(Y)\cap S)
is a
connecting family
for
S
. For each
s\in S
, denote by
G_{s}
its stabilizer in
G
. Note that
G_{q_{e}\cdot t(e)}={{}^{q_{e}}G_{t_{e}}}
and that for every edge
e\in S
,
G_{e}=G_{i(e)}\cap G_{t(e)}=G_{i(e)}\cap{{}^{q_{e}^{-1}}G_{q_{e}\cdot t(e)}}
, so that
{{}^{q_{e}}G_{e}}\leq G_{q_{e}\cdot t(e)}
. These are exactly the conjugation relations that appear in the definition of the fundamental group of a graph of groups.

The structure theorem of Bass–Serre theory says that
G\simeq\pi(G/X,\mathcal{G})
, where
\mathcal{G}(s)=G_{s}
for
s\in S
and
i_{e}\,,t_{e}\colon\mathcal{G}(e)\to\mathcal{G}(i(e))\,,\mathcal{G}(q_{e}\cdot t(e))
are given by
g\mapsto g,{{}^{q_{e}}g}
respectively. Moreover, the isomorphism
\varphi\colon\pi(G/X,\mathcal{G})\to G
is defined by the inclusion maps
\mathcal{G}(v)\to G
and
q_{e}\mapsto q_{e}
for each vertex
v
and each edge
e
.

Let
X
be a
G
-tree with finite edge stabilizers. We say that an edge
e
of
X
is
compressible
if two vertices of
e
are not in the same
G
-orbit and, for at least one vertex
v
of
e
, we have
G_{v}=G_{e}
. Otherwise, we say that the edge
e
is
incompressible
. The
G
-tree
X
is
incompressible
if all its edges are incompressible (see
[
13
, Lem. III.7.2]
for an alternative definition).

2.3.
Derivations

Let
G
be a group and
A
a
G
-module. A
derivation
is a map
d\colon G\to A
such that

d(gh)=d(g)+g\cdot d(h)\quad\text{for all }g\,,h\in G\,.

A derivation
d
is called
inner
if there exists an element
a\in A
such that

d(g)=(g-1)\cdot a\quad\text{for all }g\in G\,.

The set of derivations from
G
to
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{C}^{1}(G,A)
, and the set of inner derivations by
\mathrm{B}^{1}(G,A)
. We can identify the quotient
\mathrm{C}^{1}(G,A)/\mathrm{B}^{1}(G,A)
with the first cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,A)
.

A direct application of the definition gives the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\colon G\to A
be a derivation from a group
G
to a
G
-module
A
, and let
h\in G
. If
d(h)=(h-1)a
for some
a\in A
, then

d({{}^{g}h})=({{}^{g}h}-1)\cdot(ga-d(g))\,.

As a consequence, if
d
is an inner derivation when restricted to a subgroup
H\leq G
, then
d
is an inner derivation when restricted to any
G
-conjugate of
H
. This applies in particular to
H=\ker d=\{g\in G\colon d(g)=0\}
. We will also make use of the following theorem of Dicks and Dunwoody (
[
13
, Cor. IV.2.8 and Rem. IV.2.9]
):

Theorem 2.2
.

If
d\colon G\to P
is a derivation to a projective
\bbf_{p}[G]
-module
P
, then there exists a
G
-tree
X
with finite edge stabilizers and in which a subgroup
H\leq G
stabilizes a vertex if and only if the restriction of
d
to
H
is inner. In particular, if
G
is torsion free, this characterizes
H
as a free factor.

2.4.
The profinite fundamental group of a graph of profinite groups

Given a finite graph of profinite groups
(Y,\mathcal{G})
, the
profinite fundamental group

\widehat{\pi}(Y,\mathcal{G})
is defined as the profinite group with the same generators and relations as its abstract analog
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
(see
[
29
, Sec. 6.2]
). There is one important difference with the abstact case: in the profinite setting, the canonical homomorphisms
\mathcal{G}(v)\to\widehat{\pi}(Y,\mathcal{G})
are not embeddings in general, but one can always replace them by their images in
\widehat{\pi}(Y,\mathcal{G})
and then the embedding will hold. From now on, we assume that the vertex subgroups
\mathcal{G}(v)
embed into
\widehat{\pi}(Y,\mathcal{G})
. If all the vertex subgroups are finitely generated, then
\widehat{\pi}(Y,\mathcal{G})
is isomorphic to the profinite completion of
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
.

Given the profinite fundamental group
G=\pi_{1}(Y,\mathcal{G})
of a finite graph of profinite groups, by
[
29
, Thm. 6.5.2]
we have the following exact sequence of left
\bbf_{p}[\![G]\!]
-modules:

(1)

0\to\bigoplus_{e\in E(Y)}\bbf_{p}[\![G/\mathcal{G}(e)]\!]\to\bigoplus_{v\in V(Y)}\bbf_{p}[\![G/\mathcal{G}(v)]\!]\to\bbf_{p}\to 0\,.

For a continuous
G
-module
M
, there exists a Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated to
(Y,\mathcal{G})
(see
[
32
, Thm. 8.4.7]
):

(2)

\cdots\to\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,M)\to\bigoplus_{v\in V(Y)}\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathcal{G}(v),M)\to\bigoplus_{e\in E(Y)}\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathcal{G}(e),M)\to\mathrm{H}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,M)\to\cdots

Let
(Y,\mathcal{G})
be a graph of abstract groups and assume that
G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
is residually finite. We denote by
(Y,\overline{\mathcal{G}})
the graph of groups with
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\overline{\mathcal{G}(x)}
the closure of
\mathcal{G}(x)
in
\widehat{G}
. If all the vertex and edge subgroups
\mathcal{G}(x)
are finitely generated, then we have

\widehat{\pi}(Y,\overline{\mathcal{G}})\simeq\widehat{\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})}\,.

We say that a graph of abstract groups
(Y,\mathcal{G})
is
efficient
if each vertex and edge group
\mathcal{G}(x)
is closed in the profinite topology of
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
and moreover this topology induces the full profinite topology on each
\mathcal{G}(x)
. For example, this occurs if
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
is LERF,
Y
is finite and all the vertex and edge subgroups
\mathcal{G}(x)
are finitely generated.

The simplest example of the profinite fundamental group of a finite graph of profinite groups is a free profinite product: if all edge groups are trivial, then the fundamental group is the free profinite product of the vertex groups and a free profinite subgroup. We will need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
28
, Prop. 9.1.12]
)
.

Let
G_{1}\,,\ldots\,,G_{n}
be profinite groups and
G=G_{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg G_{n}
be their free profinite product. Then
G_{i}\cap{{}^{g}G_{i}}=\{1\}
for every
x\in G\smallsetminus G_{i}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
P=K\amalg L
be a free profinite product.

(i)

I_{\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]}\simeq\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}\oplus\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}

(ii)

Let
U\leq P

2.5.
The class
\mathcal{C}

In this paper,
\mathcal{C}
denotes the class of fundamental groups of graphs of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. Observe that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under taking subgroups and free products. Moreover, torsion-free subgroups in
\mathcal{C}
are the fundamental groups of graphs of free groups with cyclic edge groups.

If
G
belongs to
\mathcal{C}
and is finitely generated, then it is also the fundamental group of a finite graph of finitely generated virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. Indeed, let

be a faithful

-tree with virtually free vertex stabilizers and virtually cyclic edge stabilizers. The convex hull
X^{\prime}
of the
G
-orbit of any vertex is a
G
-invariant subtree of
X
such that
Y=G/X^{\prime}
is finite. As the edge stabilizers of the
G
-action are virtually cyclic, their
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers vanish. If
S
is a connected transversal for the
G
-action on
X^{\prime}
, by
[
9
, Thm. 1.5]
, we have

\displaystyle b_{1}^{(2)}(G)+\sum_{v\in V(X^{\prime})\cap S}\frac{1}{G_{v}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(X^{\prime})\cap S}b_{1}^{(2)}(G_{v})+\sum_{e\in E(X^{\prime})\cap S}\frac{1}{|G_{e}|}+\frac{1}{|G|}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in V(X^{\prime})\cap S}b_{1}^{(2)}(G_{v})\,.

Thus, all the groups

are finitely generated and the graph of groups associated to the

-action on

is the desired decomposition.

2.6.
Residually finiteness and LERFness in the class
\mathcal{C}

We begin this section by showing that the residually finite groups in the class
\mathcal{C}
contain a torsion-free subgroup, which will be the fundamental group of a graph of groups with free vertex groups and cyclic edge groups.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
be the fundamental group of a finite graph of virtually finitely generated free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. If
G
is residually finite, then it is virtually torsion-free.

Proof.

Each vertex group
\mathcal{G}(v)
can be written as the fundamental group of a finite graph of finite groups. Let
x_{v,i}
denote the elements in all the finite vertex groups arising in this decomposition. Since
G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
is residually finite, there exists a finite-index normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
such that all the elements

remain distinct modulo
N
.

Consider the action of
N
on the Bass–-Serre tree associated to
(Y,\mathcal{G})
. This action defines a graph of groups decomposition for

, with virtually cyclic edge groups and vertex groups of the form
N\cap{{}^{g}\mathcal{G}(v)}
for some
g\in G
. Each such vertex group is a normal subgroup of the conjugate
{{}^{g}\mathcal{G}(v)}
and intersects trivially the finite vertex groups within that conjugate, since the corresponding elements were avoided by the choice of
N
.

It follows that
N\cap{{}^{g}\mathcal{G}(v)}
is torsion free and hence is a free group. Therefore,

is the fundamental group of a finite graph of finitely generated free groups with cyclic edge groups, which is torsion-free.
∎

A characterization of when the fundamental group of a graph of finitely generated free groups with cyclic edge groups is residually finite has been recently established in

(2)

As a particular case of the above lemma, we will also need the following description of how the augmentation map splits in the case of a semisimple completed group algebra of a procyclic group:

Lemma 2.14
.

Let

be a procyclic group and

2.9.
Flatness of the completed algebra of the profinite completion

One of the crucial tools in the proof of our main result is the following result, which slightly generalizes
[
15
, Thm. 5.6]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually free group. If

is a profinite group containing
\widehat{G}
, the left
\bbf_{p}[G]
-module
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
is isomorphic to a direct union of free left
\bbf_{p}[G]
-modules.

Proof.

Let
F\leq G
be a normal free subgroup of finite index. By
[
11
, Cor. 7.11.8]
, the algebra
\bbf_{p}[F]
is a free ideal ring, that is, any left or right ideal is a free as an
\bbf_{p}[F]
-module. Moreover, it was shown in
[
15
, Prop. 5.3]
that
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
is flat as an
\bbf_{p}[F]
-module. Observe that
\widehat{F}
is isomorphic to the closure
\overline{F}
of
F
as a subgroup of
G
since the index
[G\colon F]
is finite. We will first prove that
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
is flat as an abstract
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
-module.

Observe that
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
is semi-hereditary: if
I\leq\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
is a finitely generated left or right ideal, then it is closed and profinite. Since
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
has global dimension

in the category of profinite modules (
[
7
, Thm. 4.1]
),
I
is projective in said category. Therefore, any surjection
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]^{n}\to I
(which is automatically continuous) splits, showing that

is a direct summand of an abstract free module and thus projective as an abstract
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
-module.

By a theorem of Chase (
[
30
, Thm. 4.32]
), a ring is semi-hereditary if and only if it is coherent and submodules of flat modules are flat. Moreover, a ring is coherent if and only if any product of flat modules is flat (
[
30
, Thm. 7.9]
). Hence, in a semi-hereditary ring, any inverse limit of flat modules is flat. Since
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
is an inverse limit of finitely generated free
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]
-modules, it must be flat over
\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{F}]\!]

However, flatness is transitive, so
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
is flat over
\bbf_{p}[F]
. By
[
11
, Prop. 2.3.21]
, it must be the union of free
\bbf_{p}[F]
-modules. Choose now a normal open subgroup

of

such that

, and let
P_{0}=\widehat{G}U\leq_{o}P
. By the previous remarks,
\bbf_{p}[\![U]\!]
is a union of free
\bbf_{p}[F]
-modules, and since tensor products commute with direct sums,
\bbf_{p}[\![P_{0}]\!]\simeq\bbf_{p}[G]\otimes_{\bbf_{p}[F]}\bbf_{p}[\![U]\!]
is also a union of free
\bbf_{p}[G]
-modules. As an
\bbf_{p}[\![P_{0}]\!]
-module,
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
is isomorphic to
\bbf_{p}[\![P_{0}]\!]^{[P\colon P_{0}]}
. Since the property of being a direct union of free submodules is preserved under direct sums, we are done.
∎

M^{\overline{\langle a\rangle}}\simeq\Hom_{\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}}(\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}/\overline{\langle a\rangle}]\!],M)\simeq\Hom_{G}(\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}/\overline{\langle a\rangle}]\!],M)\overset{\Hom(\gamma)}{\simeq}\Hom_{G}(\bbf_{p}[\![G/\overline{\langle a\rangle}]\!],M)\,,

M^{d}\simeq\Hom_{\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}}(\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}]\!]^{d},M)\simeq\Hom_{G}(\bbf_{p}[\![\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}]\!]^{d},M)\simeq\Hom_{G}(\bbf_{p}[\![G]\!]^{d},M)\,.

Therefore, applying
\Hom_{\bbf_{p}[\![G]\!]}(-,M)
to the diagram above we get the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\overline{\langle a\rangle}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\overline{\langle a\rangle}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\tau_{G})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\tau_{\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

This gives us the isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\widehat{\Gamma_{k}},M)\simeq\mathrm{coker}(\Hom(\tau_{\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}}))\simeq\mathrm{coker}(\Hom(\tau_{G}))\simeq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,M)

for all finite
p
-primary
\widehat{\Gamma_{k}}
-modules

. Since
G
is projective,
\mathrm{cd}_{p}(G)\leq 1
and thus
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(G,M)=0
, showing that
\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\widehat{\Gamma_{k}})\leq 1
as well.
∎

We finish this section by proving
1.5
.

Proof of
1.5
.

We may assume that
G
is not virtually free. Moreover, upon passing to a subgroup of finite index, we may assume that
G
is torsion-free. Since
G
has cohomological dimension
2
, it suffices to show that for every finite-index subgroup
H\leq G
and any prime
p
the canonical map

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{2}(\widehat{H},\bbf_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\bbf_{p})

is an isomorphism. Observe that for finitely generated
H
this map is always injective, and so we only have to check that the dimensions of both spaces coincide.

By replacing
H
with
H*F_{k}
for some finitely generated free group
F_{k}
, we may assume that
H=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
where
Y
is a finite graph with a single vertex
u
,
F=\mathcal{G}(u)
is a finitely generated free group of rank
d
and each edge group
\mathcal{G}(e)
is cyclic.

From the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

0\to\bbf_{p}^{|E(Y)|}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\bbf_{p})\to\bbf_{p}^{d}\to\bbf_{p}^{|E(Y)|}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\bbf_{p})\to 0

for the fundamental group of a graph of groups we conclude that

\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\bbf_{p})=\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\bbf_{p})-d\,.

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the profinite fundamental group of a graph of groups gives us

(7)

\begin{split}\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\widehat{H},\bbf_{p})&=\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\widehat{H},\bbf_{p})-|E(Y)|+\sum_{e\in E}\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(e)},\bbf_{p})\\
&\hphantom{aaaa}-\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(u)},\bbf_{p})+\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(u)},\bbf_{p})\,.\end{split}

Let
\Gamma
be the graph associated with the standard presentation of
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
, and denote by
\Gamma_{p}
the union of the components
C
of the graph
\Gamma
for which
p\in P(C)
. Put
A_{p}=V(\Gamma_{p})
and
E_{p}=E(\Gamma_{p})
. By
3.1
, the Euler characteristic of such a component is
0
and so
|A_{p}|=|E_{p}|
. Observe now that
|E(Y)|=|E(\Gamma)|
and so

\sum_{e\in E(Y)}\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(e)},\bbf_{p})-|E(Y)|=-|E_{p}|\,.

From
3.3
we have

\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(u)},\bbf_{p})-\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\overline{\mathcal{G}(u)},\bbf_{p})=d-|A_{p}|\,,

and therefore from
Eq.

7
we conclude that

\displaystyle\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\widehat{H},\bbf_{p})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\widehat{H},\bbf_{p})-d

\displaystyle=\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\bbf_{p})-d

\displaystyle=\dim_{\bbf_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\bbf_{p})\,.

This finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Relative splitting theorem for virtually free groups

Our goal in this section is to prove a relative version of the splitting theorem for finitely generated virtually free groups. The following theorem implies
1.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually free group and let
C_{1}\,,\ldots\,,C_{n}
be subgroups of
G
. Suppose that
\widehat{G}\leq P=K\amalg L
, and that each
C_{i}
is conjugate in
P
to a subgroup of either
K
or
L
. Then
G
splits as a free product

G\simeq\left(\underset{i\in I}{\ast}G_{i}\right)*F\,,

where
F
is a free subgroup of
G
, each
G_{i}
is contained in some
P
-conjugate of
K
or
L
and each
C_{j}
is contained in a
G
-conjugate of some
G_{i}
.

Proof.

If
G
is contained in a
P
-conjugate of
K
or
L
, then there is nothing to prove, so we assume that this is not the case. Let
p
be a prime. By
2.4
(i), we have

I_{\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]}\simeq\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}\oplus\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}\,,

where this isomorphism is induced by the natural inclusions

I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}\,,I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}\hookrightarrow I_{\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]}\,.

In particular, we obtain a derivation
d_{L}\colon P\to\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
defined by
g\mapsto g-1
in
I_{\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]}
and then taking the quotient modulo
\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}
.

Let
U
be a subgroup of
G
contained in a
P
-conjugate of
K
or
L
. Then, by
2.1
, the restriction of
d_{L}
to
U
is inner. In particular, this applies to each subgroup
U=C_{i}
.

Claim 4.2
.

There is a finitely generated
\bbf_{p}[G]
-submodule
N
of
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
containing
d_{L}(G)
and such that the induced derivation
d_{L}\colon G\to N
is inner when restricted to the subgroups
C_{i}
.

Proof.

Since
G
is finitely generated, say by
x_{1}\,,\ldots\,,x_{k}
, then
d_{L}(G)
is contained in the
G
-submodule generated by
d(x_{1})\,,\ldots\,,d(x_{k})
. Moreover, for each
i
there is also an element
a_{i}
in
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
such that

d_{L}(g)=(g-1)a_{i}

for all
g\in C_{i}
. Hence, we may take
N
to be the
G
-submodule generated by all the
d(x_{i})
,
a_{i}
.
∎

By
2.15
, the
\bbf_{p}[G]
-module
N
is contained in a finitely generated free
\bbf_{p}[G]
-submodule
M
of
\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
. Thus we view
d_{L}
as a derivation
d\colon G\to M<\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
.

Claim 4.3
.

d
is not inner.

Proof.

Assume that
d
is inner. Let
a\in\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
be such that
d_{L}(g)=(g-1)a
for all
g\in G
. By the definition of
d_{L}
, we have:

(g-1)a\equiv 0\pmod{\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}}\,,

(g-1)a\equiv g-1\pmod{\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}}

for all
g\in\widehat{G}
, meaning that the image of
a
is a
\widehat{G}
-fixed point in
\bbf_{p}[\![P/L]\!]
and the image of
1-a
is a
\widehat{G}
-fixed point in
\bbf_{p}[\![P/K]\!]
. However,
\bbf_{p}[\![P/L]\!]^{\widehat{G}}=\bbf_{p}[\![P/K]\!]^{\widehat{G}}=0
, by
2.13
. Hence,
a\in\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}
and
1-a\in\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}
, from which we obtain

1=(1-a)+a\in\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}\oplus\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}=I_{\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]}\,,

a contradiction.
∎

Since
d
is a non-inner derivation to a projective
\bbf_{p}[G]
-module, by
2.2
there exists a
G
-tree
X^{\prime\prime}
with finite edge stabilizers such that a subgroup

stabilizes a vertex of
X^{\prime\prime}
if and only if the restriction of
d
to
H
is inner.
Let
v_{1}\,,\ldots\,,v_{n}
be vertices of

fixed by
C_{1}\,,\ldots\,,C_{n}
respectively. Since
G
is finitely generated, there exists a
G
-subtree
X^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime\prime}
containing
v_{1}\,,\ldots\,,v_{n}
such that

is finite. After collapsing all compressible edges, we may assume that
X^{\prime}
is incompressible. Observe that each
C_{i}
fixes at least one vertex in
X^{\prime}
.

Claim 4.4
.

For any
g\in P
and any vertex
v\in X^{\prime}
with
G_{v}
infinite, the indexes
[\overline{G_{v}}\colon\overline{G_{v}}\cap{{}^{g}K}]
and
[\overline{G_{v}}\colon\overline{G_{v}}\cap{{}^{g}L}]
are either
1
or
\prod_{p\text{ prime}}p^{\infty}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for
K
, as the same argument works for
{}^{g}K
or
{}^{g}L
. If
G_{v}\leq{K}
, then
\overline{G_{v}}\leq{K}
and hence the index is
1
. Assume now that
G_{v}
is not contained in
K
and consider a finite index torsion-free normal subgroup
H_{v}
of
G_{v}
. We cannot have
H_{v}\leq{K}
either, since in this case

by
2.3
and this normalizer contains
G_{v}
. If
x
is any element of
H_{v}\smallsetminus{K}
, then the infinite cyclic subgroup

generated by

intersects

trivially by
2.3
again.

Since
G
is cyclic subgroup separable, we have that
\overline{Z}=\widehat{\bbz}
is a subgroup of
\overline{G_{v}}
, and moreover, by
2.4
,
\overline{Z}\cap{K}=\{1\}
. This shows that the

-part of the index
[\overline{G_{v}}\colon\overline{G_{v}}\cap{K}]
must be infinite for every prime

.
∎

Claim 4.5
.

For each
G_{v}
there exists
g\in P
such that
G_{v}
is contained in
{}^{g}K
or
{}^{g}L
.

Proof.

If
G_{v}
is finite, then by
[
29
, Cor. 7.1.3(a)]
, some conjugate of
G_{v}
in
\widehat{G}
lies in
K
or
L
. Therefore, we may assume that
G_{v}
is infinite. Since
d
is inner in
G_{v}
, there exists
a\in\bbf_{p}[\![P]\!]
such that
d(h)=(h-1)a
for every
h\in G_{v}
. As in the proof of
4.3
, we have:

(h-1)a\equiv 0\pmod{\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![L]\!]}}}\,,

(h-1)a\equiv h-1\pmod{\widehat{{}^{P}I_{\bbf_{p}[\![K]\!]}}}

for all
h\in G_{v}
, which means that the image of
a
is a
G_{v}
-fixed point in
\bbf_{p}[\![P/L]\!]
and that the image of
1-a
is an
G_{v}
-fixed point in
\bbf_{p}[\![P/K]\!]
. If both were actually zero in those quotients, it would lead to the same contradiction as in
4.3
. Hence, at least one of
\bbf_{p}[\![P/L]\!]
or
\bbf_{p}[\![P/K]\!]
must contain a non-zero
G_{v}
-fixed point. By
2.13
, there exists
g\in P
such that either
[\overline{G_{v}}\colon\overline{G_{v}}\cap{{}^{g}L}|
or
[\overline{G_{v}}\colon\overline{G_{v}}\cap{{}^{g}K}]
is not divided by
p^{\infty}
. Therefore, by
4.4
,
G_{v}
is contained in
{}^{g}L
or
{}^{g}K
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of subgroups of
P
that are conjugates of
K
or
L
. By
2.3
, any two distinct subgroups in
\mathcal{S}
intersect trivially. Thus, for each vertex
v
of
X^{\prime}
, there exists a unique subgroup
U\in\mathcal{S}
such that
G_{v}\leq U
. Moreover, if
G_{e}
is a non-trivial edge stabilizer, then the stabilizers of its adjacent vertices are contained in the same subgroup of
\mathcal{S}
.

Let
X
be the tree obtained from

by collapsing all edges with non-trivial stabilizers. Assume, for contradiction, that the result tree consists of a single vertex. Then the closed subgroup of
\widehat{G}
generated by
\{G_{v}\colon v\in V(X^{\prime})\}
is normal and contained in a

-conjugate of
K
or
L
. Hence,
\widehat{G}
itself is contained in the same conjugate of
K
or
L
, which contradicts our assumption. Therefore,

is not a point.

Let
v
be a vertex of
X
and let
U\in\mathcal{S}
be such that
G_{v}\leq U
. If
g\in G
stabilizes
v
, then there are two vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}=gu_{1}
of
X^{\prime}
such that
G_{u_{1}}
and
G_{u_{2}}
are non-trivial and contained in

. Therefore,
G_{u_{1}}\leq{{}^{g^{-1}}U}\cap U\neq\{1\}
, and so

. Therefore, we have obtained an infinite tree on which
G
acts with trivial edge stabilizers, such that each
C_{i}
fixes at least one vertex, and any vertex stabilizer lies in some subgroup from
\mathcal{S}
. This implies that
G
splits as the desired free product.
∎

Proof of
1.3
.

Let
C_{1}\,,\ldots\,,C_{n}
be subgroups of the form
G\cap{{}^{g}L}
or
G\cap{{}^{h}K}
with
g,h\in P
. Then, by
4.1
,
G
splits as a free product

G\simeq\left(\underset{i\in I}{\ast}G_{i}\right)*F

where
F
is a free group, each
G_{i}
is contained in some
P
-conjugate of
K
or

and each

is contained in a

-conjugate of some

. It is clear that if

, then
C_{j}=G_{i}
. Since the number of possible non-trivial

is bounded, by choosing a sufficiently large number of subgroups

, we obtain that all

are of the form
G\cap{{}^{g}L}
or
G\cap{{}^{h}K}
.
∎

5.
Detecting free products and free factors

Our first goal in this section is to prove the following theorem, which in turn implies
1.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G=\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
be the fundamental group of a finite graph of finitely generated virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups. Assume that
G
is LERF and that
\widehat{G}=L\amalg K
decomposes non-trivially as a free profinite product. Then,
G
decomposes as a free product

(8)

G=\left(\underset{i\in I}{\ast}G_{i}\right)*F,

where
F
is a free group and each
G_{i}
is contained in some
\widehat{G}
-conjugate of
L
or
K
. Moreover, if
C_{1}\leq\mathcal{G}(v_{1})\,,\ldots\,,C_{n}\leq\mathcal{G}(v_{n})
are finitely generated subgroups of vertex groups lying in some
\widehat{G}
-conjugate of
L
or

, then we can also choose the

in
Eq.

such that each
C_{j}
lies in some

-conjugate of some

Proof.

Recall that by
2.6
,
G
contains a finite index subgroup

that is the fundamental group of a finite graph of finitely generated free groups with cyclic edge groups. By
1.5
,
N
is cohomologically good. In particular, the profinite completion of

is torsion-free, and so the closure
\overline{N}\simeq\widehat{N}
of

in
\widehat{G}
is torsion-free.

We call an edge

of

parabolic
with respect to the splitting

if
\mathcal{G}(e)
is infinite and is a subgroup of some
\widehat{G}
-conjugate of
L
or
K
. By

, if
\mathcal{G}(e)
has infinite interserction with a conjugate of
K
or

, then

is parabolic.

Let
\Delta
be the subgraph of

obtained by removing all the parabolic edges. Let
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
be the quotient graph of groups obtained by collapsing each connected component
X
of
\Delta
to a point

and setting
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\pi(X,\mathcal{G})
.

Claim 5.2
.

The vertex groups
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(x)
are virtually free.

Proof.

Since
G
is LERF, we have

\widehat{\pi(X,\widehat{\mathcal{G}})}\simeq\widehat{\pi(X,\mathcal{G})}\simeq\overline{\pi(X,\mathcal{G})}\subseteq\widehat{G}\,.

Let
U=\overline{N}\cap\overline{\pi(X,\mathcal{G})}
and for each

let

and

\pi(X,\widehat{\mathcal{G}})

From now on, we assume that all edge subgroups

\widehat{G}
-conjugate of
K
or

. Applying

, we can decompose each vertex group

\mathcal{G}(v)=\left(\underset{i\in I_{V}}{\ast}H_{i,v}\right)*F_{v}\,,

\mathcal{G}(v)\cap{{}^{g}L}

\mathcal{G}(v)\cap{{}^{g}L}

\mathcal{G}(v)

This allows us to construct another graph of groups

Let

Claim 5.3
.

For each vertex

in
\widetilde{Z}
, there exists

such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(v)

{}^{g}K
or
{}^{g}L
.

Let

be the set of subgroups of
\widehat{G}
that are conjugates of

or

∎

Now we prove both

.

Proof of
1.2
.

Observe that

[

, Thm. 1]
. By

, if

∎

Proof of
1.4
.

Assume in
5.1
that
L=\overline{H}
. If
H
is one-ended, then
H
belongs to some
{{}^{g}G_{i}}
. This subgroup
{{}^{g}G_{i}}
must lie in
\overline{H}
, and since
G
is LERF, we conclude that
{{}^{g}G_{i}}=H
. If
H
is contained in a conjugate of a vertex subgroup, we apply
5.1
with
C_{1}=H
. Then again
H
belongs to some
{{}^{g}G_{i}}
, and hence
{{}^{g}G_{i}}=H
.
∎

6.
Profinitely rigid words

If
F
is a free group of rank
d
and
w\in F
is a word, then for any finite group
G
one has a word map

w\colon\underbrace{G\times\cdots\times G}_{d}\to G

given by substitution. The pushfoward of the uniform measure on
G^{d}
to
G
defines the
w
-measure on
G
. Two words
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
F
are profinitely equivalent if the
w_{1}
- and
w_{2}
-measures coincide in every finite group
G
. Observe that this is automatically the case if
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
lie in the same
\Aut(F)
-orbit. The following result gives an alternative description of profinitely equivalent words, and also justifies the definition of profinite rigidity of a word:

Proposition 6.1

(
[
18
, Thm. 2.2]
)
.

Let
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
be two elements of
F
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The
w_{1}
-measure and the
w_{2}
-measure coincide in every finite group.

(2)

There is an automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(\widehat{F})
such that
\varphi(w_{1})=w_{2}
.

We recall that
w\in F
is profinitely rigid if any
u\in F
in the same
\Aut(\widehat{F})
-orbit of
w
also lies in the same
\Aut(F)
-orbit of
w
. It is universally profinitely rigid if it is profinitely rigid in
F*H
for any finitely generated free group
H
.

Following
[
23
, Sec. 3]
, we say that an element
w
of a free group
F
is
algebraic
if there is no proper free factor of
F
that contains
w
. Given an element
w\in F
, there exists a unique free factor
U
of
F
that contains
w
such that
w
is algebraic in
U
(
[
23
, Sec. 3.4]
). As in
[
23
]
, we call
U
the
algebraic closure
of
w
in
F
. The following proposition shows that the algebraic closure of a word in a free group is a profinite invariant.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
F
be a finitely generated free group and let
w\in F
. Let
L
be a free profinite factor of
\widehat{F}
that contains
w
. Then the algebraic closure of
w
in
F
is contained in
L
. In particular, if
U
is the algebraic closure of
w
, then
\overline{U}
is the smallest profinite free factor of
\widehat{F}
that contains
w
.

Proof.

Write
\widehat{F}=L\amalg K
. Then, by
4.1
,
F
splits as a free product
F=G_{1}*G_{2}
such that
w\in G_{1}
and
G_{1}
is contained in some
\widehat{F}
-conjugate of
K
or
L
. Since
w\in L
, the only possibility is
G_{1}\leq L
. Hence, the algebraic closure of
w
in
F
is contained in
L
.
∎

We are now ready to prove
1.6
.

Proof of
1.6
.

It suffices to prove the theorem for
w
algebraic in
F
. Assume that
w
is profinitely rigid in
F
, and let
u\in F*H
be such that there exists
\varphi\in\Aut(\widehat{F*H})
with
\varphi(w)=u
. We want to show that
u
and
w
are in the same
\Aut(F*H)
-orbit.

Let
U
be the algebraic closure of
u
in
F*H
. Then
F*H=U*H^{\prime}
, and by
6.2
we have
\varphi(\overline{F})=\overline{U}
, where
\overline{F}
and
\overline{U}
denote the closure of
F
and
U
respectively in
\widehat{F*H}
. Hence,
\operatorname{rk}(F)=\operatorname{rk}(U)
, and so
\operatorname{rk}(H)=\operatorname{rk}(H^{\prime})
. Therefore, there exists an automorphism of
F*H
sending
U
to
F
. Hence, we may assume that
U=F
. Since
w
is profinitely rigid in
F
and, as already noted,
\varphi(\overline{F})=\overline{F}
, it follows that
u
and
w
are in the same
\Aut(F)
-orbit.

Conversely, assume that
w
is profinitely rigid in
F*H
, and let
u\in F
be such that there exists
\varphi\in\Aut(\widehat{F})
with
\phi(w)=u
. Any automorphism of
\widehat{F}
extends to an automorphism of
\widehat{F*H}
, and thus we can find
\psi\in\Aut(F*H)
such that
\psi(w)=u
. Let
U
be the algebraic closure of
u
in
F*H
. Then, by
6.2
,
\psi(F)=U\leq F
. Since
\psi
is an automorphism, we have
\psi(F)=F
, showing that
w
is profinitely rigid in
F
as desired.
∎

7.
Further directions

Past the fundamental groups of graphs of virtually free groups with virtually cyclic edge groups, it is natural to try to extend
1.1
to the class
\mathcal{L}
of limit groups, described as follows.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a group and let
A\subseteq G^{\prime}
be a finite malnormal family. Let
Y
be a graph with vertex set
V(Y)=\{u\}\cup A
and edge set
E(Y)=\{e_{a}\colon a\in A\}
where each edge
e_{a}
satisfies
\iota(e_{a})=u
and
t(e_{a})=a
. Define the graph of groups
\mathcal{G}
on
Y
by means of
\mathcal{G}(u)=G^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{G}(a)=\langle a\rangle\times\bbz^{n_{a}}
for some
n_{a}>0
and
\mathcal{G}(e_{a})=\langle a\rangle
. The homomorphisms from the edge groups of
(Y,\mathcal{G})
into the vertex groups are taken to be natural inclusions. The group
\pi(Y,\mathcal{G})
is said to be obtained from
G^{\prime}
by an extension of centralizers.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{0}
consists of the class of free groups, and define
\mathcal{D}_{i+1}
to be the class of groups that can be obtained from groups in
\mathcal{D}_{i}
by an extension of centralizers. We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{i}
the class of finitely generated subgroups of groups in
\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Then, a
limit group
is any group in the class
\mathcal{L}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{L}_{i}
.

Conjecture 2
.

The profinite completion of a one-ended limit group does not split as a free profinite product.

Our methods allows one to obtain particular cases of this conjecture. By a result of Wilton, the limit groups are LERF (
[
33
]
). The same argument as in our proof of
5.1
provides:

Theorem 7.1
.

Profinite completions detect free products in
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

Proof.

Observe that limit groups are torsion-free, and any Baumslag–Solitar subgroup of a limit group must be isomorphic to
\bbz^{2}
, so that the last conclusion of
2.10
still applies. Moreover,
5.2
remains correct as any incompressible edge incident to an abelian vertex must be parabolic by
[
29
, Prop. 4.2.9]
– in fact, the abelian vertex group must itself be contained in the same
\widehat{G}
-conjugate of
K
or
L
. The rest of the proof follows without changes.
∎
