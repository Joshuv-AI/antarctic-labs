Mechanically Gated Generation of a 3H‑Anthra[2,1‑b]pyran Photoswitch Enabling Multicolor Switching
Authors: Yan Sun, Wendy Granados Razo, Maxwell J. Robb
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Mechanochromic mechanophores are a class of stress-sensitive molecules that undergo force-induced chemical transformations resulting in optical changes that enable direct visualization of stress and/or strain in polymeric materials. Unlike conventional mechanochromic mechanophores, the mechanically gated photoswitching strategy decouples the mechanochemical activation from the chromogenic response, enabling high force-specificity and modularity. However, previous designs are limited to diarylethene photoswitches, which typically provide only a simple binary (colorless-to-colored) response. Here, we report on a mechanically gated 3H-anthra­[2,1-b]­pyran photoswitch that enables a three-stage coloration process. Mechanochemical activation of the photoinert mechanophore generates a yellow-colored 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch via a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction. Subsequent UV photoirradiation induces a ring-opening reaction to produce an orange-red colored merocyanine dye. This study extends the repertoire of polymer mechanochromism to enable multicolor behavior in response to both force and light and provides a promising platform for applications such as force-sensing, multicolor patterning, and molecular logic gates.

In the nascent field of polymer mechanochemistry, mechanical force is employed as an external stimulus to activate covalent bond transformations within force-sensitive molecules known as mechanophores. − Among them, mechanochromic mechanophores produce visible color changes in response to mechanical force, providing a powerful platform for real-time visualization of stress and/or strain in polymeric materials. Typical mechanochromic mechanophores include spiropyran, − naphthopyran, − benzopyran, rhodamine, − spirothiopyran, oxazine, , π-extended anthracene adducts, , triarylmethanes, and diarylbibenzofuranone, , among many others. However, the reversible nature of most mechanochromic mechanophores upon stress release precludes their ability to permanently record the mechanical history of polymeric materials, limiting long-term detection. Additionally, their lack of stimulus specificity can lead to false-positive chromogenic responses by nonmechanical stimuli such as heat or light. ,,,

To address these limitations, our lab introduced the concept of mechanically gated photoswitching and developed diarylethene-based mechanophores in which a photoinduced color-changing reaction is gated by a mechanochemical reaction. , This strategy successfully decouples the mechanochemical activation from the ultimate chromogenic response, providing outstanding force-sensing capability and a high degree of modularity. However, previous designs have been largely limited to diarylethene-based systems (Figure a). To broaden the functional scope of mechanically gated photoswitching, we sought to extend this strategy to alternative classes of photoswitches capable of more complex responses beyond simple binary switching.

(a) Mechanochemical activation reveals a diarylethene photoswitch via a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction, gating the subsequent photoisomerization to form a colored species. (b) Mechanochemically triggered retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction generates a yellow 3H-anthra­[2,1-b]­pyran photoswitch, which undergoes a photoinduced ring-opening reaction to produce an orange-red merocyanine dye, enabling three-stage chromism.

Unlike conventional photoswitches that transform between colorless and colored states, para-methoxy 3H-anthrapyran reported by Gorelik et al. undergoes a photoinduced ring-opening reaction that converts the initial yellow form into an orange-red merocyanine dye. We envisioned that this anthrapyran could be leveraged in the design of a mechanically gated photoswitch, allowing for the development of a mechanophore with multistage color-changing behavior. Here we report a novel mechanophore based on an anthracene–maleimide adduct that undergoes a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction upon mechanochemical activation to unmask a 3H-anthra­[2,1-b]­pyran photoswitch, thereby enabling mechanically gated, multistage chromism (Figure b). We identified a para-methoxy-3H-anthrapyran photoswitch bearing an ortho-fluorophenyl substituent, which extends the lifetime of the photogenerated merocyanine to enhance the photochromic performance. The 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch is masked via a Diels–Alder reaction with maleimide, forming a colorless mechanophore containing a photochemically inert benzopyran unit. Upon mechanochemical activation, the anthracene–maleimide adduct undergoes a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction to reveal the yellow-colored (and fluorescent) anthrapyran photoswitch, which subsequently generates an orange-red merocyanine dye upon UV photoirradiation. Thus, this multistage chromogenic response is achieved only after mechanical activation. We take advantage of the distinct stimuli-responsive manifolds to design adaptive polymeric materials with complex multicolor switching properties, offering promising applications in multicolor patterning, data encryption, and molecular logic gates.

We initiated our study by evaluating the mechanochemical reactivity of the masked anthrapyran mechanophore in silico. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed on the truncated anthracene–maleimide model using the constrained geometries simulate external force (CoGEF) method (Figure ). , Starting from the equilibrium geometry, the displacement between the carbon atoms of two terminal acetoxy groups on the truncated model was increased in increments of 0.05 Å and the geometry of the molecule was optimized at each step. The computational results indicate that mechanical elongation induces a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction, generating the anticipated 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch and maleimide fragments, with a moderate rupture force (F max) of 4.1 nN. Although the absolute forces calculated by CoGEF are typically overestimated compared to experimental values, they provide a reliable indication of the relative reactivity of mechanophores. The F max value predicted for the masked anthrapyran mechanophore is comparable to F max values predicted for other anthracene–maleimide mechanophores, suggesting that the mechanochemical retro-Diels–Alder reaction should be achievable experimentally. −

Encouraged by the computational results, we next set out to synthesize the mechanophore and covalently incorporate it into a linear poly­(methyl acrylate) polymer to experimentally investigate its mechanochemical reactivity. When subjected to ultrasonication in solution, polymers experience high mechanical forces near the chain center due to cavitation-induced solvodynamic shear, while the chain ends experience negligible force. Thus, we targeted poly­(methyl acrylate) P1 incorporating the mechanophore near the chain center, along with control polymer P2 with the mechanophore located at the chain end for comparison (Scheme , see the SI for details). Starting from 2-methoxyanthracen-10­(9H)-one 1, DCC coupling with α-bromoisobutyric acid followed by demethylation using BBr3 afforded anthrol 3 in good yield. Next, reaction with propargyl alcohol 4 in the presence of pyridinium p-toluenesulfonate (PPTS) and trimethyl orthoformate (TMOF) following Carreira’s procedure produced 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch AP. The Diels–Alder reaction between AP and 6 generated a mixture of diastereomers; a single diastereomer of bis-initiator DA was isolated chromatographically and characterized by NMR spectroscopy (Figure S1). This bis-initiator was subsequently employed in the controlled radical polymerization of methyl acrylate using Cu(0) and Me6TREN following the procedure by Nguyen et al. to yield polymer P1 with the Diels–Alder adduct incorporated near the chain center. Control polymer P2 with the Diels–Alder adduct embedded at the chain end was prepared in a similar fashion.

The reactivity of the masked anthrapyran mechanophore was initially investigated by subjecting a dilute polymer solution (2 mg/mL in THF with 30 mM BHT) to various combinations of ultrasonication (0–4 °C, 1 s on/1 s off, 8.4 ± 0.4 W/cm2) and/or 365 nm UV photoirradiation. Aliquots were removed and analyzed by UV–vis absorption spectroscopy to characterize the mechanochemical reaction and photochromism (Figure and Figure S2 and S3). The untreated solution of P1 is colorless, and no changes were observed in the UV–vis absorption spectrum after UV photoirradiation (365 nm, 2 min), indicating that the benzopyran unit is effectively photoinert. Upon ultrasound-induced mechanical activation, new absorption peaks appeared between 350 and 550 nm, and the solution became light yellow, suggesting the putative generation of the anthrapyran photoswitch. Subsequent photoirradiation of the sonicated solution of P1 with UV light led to the emergence of a new visible absorption band with a maximum at 478 nm corresponding to a distinct orange-red coloration, indicating photochemical generation of the merocyanine dye. Critically, the sequence of mechanical activation followed by UV photoirradiation is essential for the multistage color-changing process, consistent with the mechanophore design where a retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction of the anthracene–maleimide adduct gates the formation of the anthrapyran photoswitch. In addition, the absorption spectrum and reversion profile of the sonicated, photoirradiated solution of P1 closely matches that of a small molecule model compound (Figure S4). These results indicate that mechanochemical activation successfully generates the anticipated anthrapyran photoswitch and subsequent photochemical activation causes the ring-opening reaction. The photochromic behavior of the sonicated solution of P1 was reversible over five cycles of alternating UV photoirradiation and thermal reversion in the dark (Figure S5). In direct contrast, the solution of control polymer P2 exhibited minimal changes under the same sequential treatment with ultrasonication and UV photoirradiation, indicating that mechanical force is responsible for the changes in the photochemical behavior of P1 and the observed multicolor switching is specifically triggered by mechanical activation (Figures S6 and S7). In addition to changes in absorption, ultrasound-induced mechanical activation of P1 also results in pronounced changes in fluorescence (Figure S8). Following mechanochemical activation, the solution displays fluorogenic behavior with a new emission peak at 451 nm, consistent with the generation of the anthrapyran. The fluorescence is diminished after subsequent UV photoirradiation, suggesting that the ring-opened merocyanine species is weakly emissive, which was confirmed by measurements of anthrapyran small molecule AP before and after photoirradiation.

Encouraged by the solution-phase reactivity, we next investigated the multistage coloration in solid-state polymeric materials (Figure ). A mechanochemically active poly­(methyl acrylate) network (PMA Active) was synthesized via free radical polymerization using a crosslinker derived from the masked anthrapyran mechanophore functionalized with terminal methacrylate groups (see the SI for details). For comparison, a control PMA network (PMA Control) was also prepared in which a monofunctional anthracene–maleimide adduct was introduced as a pendant group. The PMA Active film is colorless and exhibited no color change upon UV photoirradiation, consistent with the expected behavior of the benzopyran unit. However, striking the film repeatedly with a hammer resulted in the appearance of a distinct yellow color, which corresponds to the mechanochemical generation of the anthrapyran photoswitch. Subsequent UV photoirradiation of the mechanically activated film (365 nm, 2 min) further transformed the yellow colored material into an orange-red color, reflecting the photochemical formation of the ring-opened merocyanine dye. The appearance of the solid network is qualitatively consistent with the color change observed in more concentrated solutions of small molecule anthrapyran photoswitch AP after UV photoirradiation (Figure S9). Following mechanical activation, the photochromic response was reversible upon UV photoirradiation and subsequent equilibration under ambient conditions (Figure S10). Consistent with the solution-phase experiments, only sequential mechanical activation followed by UV irradiation enables the three-stage coloration. On the other hand, the PMA Control film showed no color change upon the same sequence of mechanical and photochemical treatment.

We have designed a mechanically gated 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch that enables multistaged multicolor switching in polymers in response to mechanical force and UV light. Mechanochemical activation of the anthracene–maleimide adduct promotes a formal retro-[4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction to unveil the yellow-colored anthrapyran photoswitch, which further transforms into an orange-red colored merocyanine dye via a photochemical ring-opening reaction. We demonstrate the three-stage color-changing process in solution via ultrasound-induced mechanochemical activation of a linear poly­(methyl acrylate) polymer, and in solid-state polymeric materials containing a mechanophore crosslinker. In contrast to typical mechanochromic mechanophores that only exhibit a simple binary response, this mechanically gated 3H-anthrapyran photoswitch responds to both force and light, enabling complex chromogenic behavior with high stimuli-specificity. We envision that this dual-responsive system may offer potential as a versatile platform for applications including stress sensing, molecular logic gates, and encryption.
