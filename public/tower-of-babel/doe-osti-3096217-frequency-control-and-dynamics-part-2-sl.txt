 Integration of Inverter-Based
 Resources (IBRs)

 IBRs Are Playing an Increasingly
 Large Role in Power Systems
 Around the World
 • Power systems historically have been
 dominated by synchronous generators and
 alternating current (AC) transmission.

 • Future power systems are expected to have
 large numbers of IBRs at various levels in
 power systems.

 • IBRs* are power electronics interfaced
 devices converting direct current (DC)
 power from an energy source to alternating
 current (AC) power for use in an AC power
 systems (solar photovoltaics, modern wind
 plants, battery energy storage).
 * Sometimes also referred to as converter-interfaced generation
 technologies (CIGs). Image from Lin et al., Research Roadmap on Grid-Forming Inverters (NLR, 2020),

What Responses Do You Want From BESS?

 What Responses Do You Want From BESS?
• IBRs (incl. BESS) can provide classical primary frequency response Three main types of FFR provided by IBRs:
 (a) grid frequency, f , (b) step response, Pffr1, (c) proportional response, Pffr2, (d) derivative response, Pffr3
 (PFR)

 ◦ Typically, a proportional response to speed.

• Most IBRs (incl. BESS) can provide equivalent inertial response as a
 fast frequency response (FFR)

 ◦ Based on step-response (frequency(F) or rate of chang of
 frequency (RoCoF) trigger)
 ◦ Based on the derivative of speed (RoCoF).

• The amount of BESS response and the response’s characteristics
 depend on your system and how it is expected to evolve:

 ◦ System inertia

 ◦ Size of credible contingency

 ◦ Speed of response

 ◦ Magnitude of response.
