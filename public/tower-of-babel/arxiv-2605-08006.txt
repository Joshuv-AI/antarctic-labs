Penalty-Based First-Order Methods for Bilevel Optimization with Minimax and Constrained Lower-Level Problems
Yiyang Shen, Yutian He, Weiran Wang, Qihang Lin

Abstract
We study a class of bilevel optimization problems in which both the upper- and lower-level problems have minimax structures. This setting captures a broad range of emerging applications. Despite the extensive literature on bilevel optimization and minimax optimization separately, existing methods mainly focus on bilevel optimization with lower-level minimization problems, often under strong convexity assumptions, and are not directly applicable to the minimax lower-level setting considered here. To address this gap, we develop penalty-based first-order methods for bilevel minimax optimization without requiring strong convexity of the lower-level problem. In the deterministic setting, we establish that the proposed method finds an ϵ \epsilon -KKT point with O ~ ​ ( ϵ − 4 ) \tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4}) oracle complexity. We further show that bilevel problems with convex constrained lower-level minimization can be reformulated as special cases of our framework via Lagrangian duality, leading to an O ~ ​ ( ϵ − 4 ) \tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4}) complexity bound that improves upon the existing O ~ ​ ( ϵ − 7 ) \tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-7}) result. Finally, we extend our approach to the stochastic setting, where only stochastic gradient oracles are available, and prove that the proposed stochastic method finds a nearly ϵ \epsilon -KKT point with O ~ ​ ( ϵ − 9 ) \tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-9}) oracle complexity.

Hyperparameter tuning for deep distributionally robust optimization (DRO)

Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) assumes identical training and testing distributions and assigns uniform weights to samples, resulting in poor worst-group performance on an unbalanced dataset
[
61
]
. Instead of creating balanced data which are expensive
[
60
]
, we propose a deep bilevel DRO framework by decomposing the model into a feature extractor
{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
and a linear classifier
{\mathbf{y}}_{1}
, where
\texttt{Enc}({\mathbf{a}};{\mathbf{x}}_{1})
produces representations on which
{\mathbf{y}}_{1}
predicts label
b
. This separation isolates non-convex representation learning from convex classification
[
31
,
35
]
.

Lower Level: Group DRO

Let
D_{\text{train}}=\cup_{g=1}^{G}D_{\text{train}}^{(g)}
.
We define

\min_{{\mathbf{y}}_{1}}\max_{{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}}\;\;\mathcal{L}_{\text{train}}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2};\eta):={\mathbf{y}}_{2}^{T}\mathcal{L}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}({\mathbf{y}}_{1})\;-\;\frac{\eta}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}_{2}-\tfrac{1}{G}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}\;+\;\lambda\|{\mathbf{y}}_{1}\|^{2},

(27)

where
\mathcal{L}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}({\mathbf{y}}_{1}):=(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{G}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}})
,
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}({\mathbf{y}}_{1}):=\frac{1}{|D_{\text{train}}^{(g)}|}\sum_{({\mathbf{a}},b)\in D_{\text{train}}^{(g)}}\ell^{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}({\mathbf{y}}_{1},({\mathbf{a}},b))

{\mathbf{y}}_{2}
emphasizes high-loss groups with

Limitations

This work has the following limitations: (1) Assumption
5.1
requires compactness of the feasible domains. (2) The
\epsilon
-KKT point in Definition
3.2
is defined in a lifted space with additional variables
{\mathbf{z}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{z}}_{3}

Conclusion

We studied bilevel optimization problems in which both the upper- and lower-level problems exhibit minimax structures. We proposed a penalty-based first-order method which finds an
\epsilon
-KKT point with
\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
complexity in the deterministic case and finds a nearly
\epsilon
-KKT point with
\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-9})
complexity in the stochastic case. When applied to deterministic bilevel problems with convex constrained lower-level minimization, the proposed framework improves the existing complexity bound from
\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-7})
in
[

]
and
\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-6})
in
[
53
]
to
\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
.

Model (

Distributionally robust learning with tuning hyperparameters

Let
{\mathbf{a}}
be a feature vector and
b
be a target variable. Suppose a model
s({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{a}})
parameterized by
{\mathbf{y}}_{1}
is used to predict
b
based on
{\mathbf{a}}
. Let
\ell(s({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{a}}),b)
be the loss of the prediction. Given a training dataset
D_{\text{train}}=\{({\mathbf{a}}_{t}^{i},b_{t}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{train}}}
and a validation dataset
D_{\text{val}}=\{({\mathbf{a}}_{v}^{i},b_{v}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{val}}}
, let
{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{train}}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{val}}}
be the weights on the training and validation instances, respectively. The weighted average losses on the training and validation sets are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{DRO}}(D_{\text{train}};{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{train}}}y_{2}^{i}\cdot\ell(s({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{a}}_{t}^{i}),b_{t}^{i})

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{DRO}}(D_{\text{val}};{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{val}}}x_{2}^{i}\cdot\ell(s({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{a}}_{v}^{i}),b_{v}^{i}).

(30)

The distributionally robust learning problem with tuning hyperparameters can be formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\eta\geq 0,{\mathbf{y}}_{1}}~

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in\Delta_{n_{\text{val}},p}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{DRO}}(D_{\text{val}};{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})

(31)

\displaystyle s.t.~

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}_{1}\in\argmin_{{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in\Delta_{n_{\text{train}},1}}\left\{\mathcal{L}_{\text{DRO}}(D_{\text{train}};{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2})-\frac{\eta}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}_{2}-\frac{1}{n_{\text{train}}}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}\right\}

where
\Delta_{n,p}=\{{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}|\mathbf{1}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}=1,y_{i}\in[0,p],i=1,\dots,n\}
is the truncated probability simplex. The objective function above is chosen to minimize the average loss on the
1/(pn)\times 100\%
worst cases. However, if one directly minimizes the average loss on the
1/(pn)\times 100\%
worst training instances, the model is not guaranteed to minimize the
1/(pn)\times 100\%
worst instances in the populations due to the generalization erro, especially, when
n_{\text{train}}
is small. Therefore, a regularization term
\frac{x_{1}}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}_{2}-1/n_{\text{train}}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}
with a changeable hyperparameter
x_{1}>0
is introduced to the lower level problem. The goal is to minimize the average loss on the
1/(pn)\times 100\%
worst cases on the validation set by the trained model through optimizing the parameter
x_{1}
.
