Minocycline hydrochloride ointment-assisted periodontal treatment of stage III/IV periodontitis associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus targeting Chinese people: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Hongqiao Zhao, Sichi Lin, Lu He, Min Zhen, Gang Yang, Xiane Wang, Jie Cao, Yi Li, Jianxia Hou, Li Xu, Huanxin Meng, Kaining Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionScaling and root planing (SRP) combined with adjunctive antibiotic therapy is widely adopted in the management of periodontitis in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), with the aims of ameliorating glycaemic control, alleviating local inflammation and facilitating periodontal tissue regeneration. As a topically administered adjunctive antibiotic for periodontal treatment, minocycline hydrochloride (MH) ointment has shown favourable clinical efficacy in systemically healthy patients with periodontitis. However, robust evidence supporting its clinical efficacy and potential glycaemic-improving effects in patients with periodontitis complicated by T2DM remains limited. The present study is designed to test the null hypothesis that no significant differences in clinical outcomes exist between SRP combined with MH and SRP alone in the management of periodontitis among patients with T2DM, with its primary objective to investigate whether MH as an SRP adjunct confers superior clinical benefits to SRP alone.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a randomised, single-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. 56 patients with T2DM-associated stage III/IV periodontitis will be recruited from the Department of Periodontology, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, Beijing, China. Eligible participants will be randomised into two groups: the experimental group will undergo SRP combined with topically administered MH ointment and the control group will undergo SRP with a matched placebo. The primary outcomes will include probing depth (PD) changes at periodontal pocket sites with a baseline PD ≥6 mm at 6 months post-baseline, with a specific focus on the percentage of such sites with PD reduced to ≤5 mm. The secondary outcomes will comprise PD changes at pocket sites with a baseline PD ≥5 mm at 6 months post-baseline, as well as clinical attachment loss, the plaque index, bleeding index, the levels of IL-1β, IL-17, calprotectin and insulin levels in gingival crevicular fluid and serum, complete blood count, blood biochemistry, including glycated haemoglobin levels, and the composition of subgingival plaques at baseline, and 2 and 6 months post-baseline.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology (PKUSSIRB-2024102139b). Results will be published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400092305.Protocol versionV.3.1 (date: 6 January 2026).Keywords: Antibiotics; Clinical Trial; Dentistry; Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2

Introduction

The bidirectional relationship between diabetes mellitus and periodontitis has been documented in detail.1,4 Periodontitis represents a highly prevalent chronic inflammatory disorder.1 Emerging evidence links periodontitis to insulin resistance, which contributes to impaired glycaemic control in patients with diabetes.3 The formation of subgingival biofilms elicits both local and systemic inflammatory responses, thereby precipitating diabetic complications and elevating the incidence of new-onset diabetes.2 Conversely, diabetes mellitus, one of the well-known risk factors for periodontitis, increases susceptibility to periodontal lesions, exacerbates oral inflammatory responses, disrupts the physiological bone coupling process, and ultimately leads to alveolar bone loss and tooth loss.4 Hyperglycaemia drives the accumulation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) and adipokines and disturbs host immune responses, which, in turn, upregulates the expression of proinflammatory cytokines in periodontal tissues.3 4

Scaling and root planing (SRP), the core procedure of initial periodontal therapy, modulates the production of inflammatory mediators and attenuates insulin resistance, resulting in significant improvements in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) levels among diabetic patients with periodontitis within 6 months post-treatment.5,7 Supportive periodontal care (SPC) implemented subsequent to SRP may effectively improve periodontal clinical outcomes including bleeding on probing (BOP), probing depth (PD), clinical attachment loss (CAL) and the full-mouth plaque score.8 However, in patients with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), SRP alone produces no significant improvement in HbA1c levels, exerts limited regulatory effects on the subgingival microbiome composition, and is associated with a higher risk of periodontal pocket recurrence, CAL recurrence and tooth loss than in those with well-controlled T2DM or non-diabetic individuals during long-term SPC.9 10

Given the limitations of SRP alone, previous studies demonstrated that adjunctive antibiotic treatment achieved better clinical therapeutic effects than SRP alone in T2DM patients with periodontitis.5 10 Systemic adjuvant antibiotics, such as the combination of amoxicillin and metronidazole, have been shown to improve glycaemic control and achieve better clinical efficacy.11 12 Doxycycline, a systemic tetracycline derivative, confers additional benefits for HbA1c levels, clinical and biochemical parameters and the suppression of pathogenic periodontal microbiota.13 14 As a topical adjunctive agent for periodontal therapy, chlorhexidine exhibits bacteriostatic properties that allow it to inhibit plaque biofilm formation and local inflammatory responses.15 Adjunctive photodynamic therapy has been shown to reduce AGE levels in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) and ameliorate periodontal health both clinically and immunologically.16 17

Minocycline is a derivative of tetracycline that is effective against a broad spectrum of Gram-positive and Gram-negative anaerobic bacteria.18 Minocycline hydrochloride (MH) has been widely used as an adjunctive antibiotic treatment together with SRP for systemically healthy patients with periodontitis.19 Topically applied MH exerts promising effects on periodontal parameters by reducing pathogens20 and bone loss.21 Previous studies reported better improvements in CAL, BOP22 23 and PD24 with the combination of MH and SRP than with SRP alone. Moreover, MH may be used in the surgical treatment of severe periodontitis, achieving significant reductions in PD and BOP and a marked improvement in CAL.25 26

The clinical benefits of locally administered MH as an adjunct to SRP for glycaemic control and periodontal disease management in patients with T2DM-associated periodontitis remain controversial.27 Although previous studies have reported that topical MH used as an adjunct to SRP may reduce HbA1c levels by lowering high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels,28 29 Matsumoto et al found no significant difference in glycaemic control in T2DM patients between MH-adjuvant and SRP alone groups, despite elevated adiponectin levels.30 Consistent findings were obtained in another study, which showed no statistically significant differences in interleukin-6 (IL-6), human soluble receptor for AGEs, hs-CRP or HbA1c levels between the MH-assisted SRP and SRP alone groups.31 In terms of periodontitis management, although improvements in PD and bleeding index (BI) were reported in diabetic patients with periodontitis following antimicrobial therapy with MH,28 a number of studies have suggested that MH cannot change the levels of receptors for AGEs from those with SRP alone and also exerts a very limited effect on periodontitis management without significant improvements in periodontal clinical parameters.30 31

Current evidence for the clinical outcomes of locally administered MH as an adjunct to SRP in patients with T2DM complicated by periodontitis remains markedly inconsistent.27,31 Existing studies lack a comprehensive, multidimensional analysis integrating periodontal parameters, the subgingival microbial composition and glycolipid metabolism.27,31 Against the backdrop of the aforementioned controversies and limitations in existing research, the present study aims to investigate whether MH-adjuvant SRP provides additional benefits over SRP alone for periodontal clinical parameters, local inflammation, subgingival microflora and systemic inflammation and metabolic levels among T2DM patients with severe (stage III/IV) periodontitis with a 6-month follow-up. Clinical parameters, including PD, BI, plaque index (PlI) and CAL, will be measured in a clinical examination. GCF samples will be taken from each subject and analysed for cytokines. Subgingival plaque samples will be collected for microbiological assessments. We will also follow-up with blood samples to assess the systemic inflammatory response and metabolic profile. This manuscript describes the trial in detail.

Methods and analysis

This is a randomised, single-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. T2DM patients with stage III/IV periodontitis32 will be recruited. The assessment, treatment, intervention and follow-up will be performed in the Department of Periodontology, Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, Beijing, China. The present study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology (PKUSSIRB-2024102139b). Recruitment

All participants involved will be recruited from the outpatient clinic of the Periodontology Department of Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology, Beijing, China. Patients will be informed about the need for periodontal treatment and the objectives and methods of this study by investigators. Participants will enter the screening period for the initial assessment only after signing the informed consent form (online supplemental file 1). After a reassessment 7–14 days after supragingival scaling, patients who still meet the inclusion criteria at the baseline will be included in the trial. The inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in table 1. Participants with T2DM will be enrolled in accordance with the diagnostic criteria established by the American Diabetes Association (ADA), meeting any one of the following four criteria33: fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L (≥126 mg/dL)33; 2 hours plasma glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dL) during a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test33; HbA1c ≥6.5%33; random plasma glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dL) in patients with classic hyperglycaemic symptoms.33 No restriction will be placed on the duration of diabetes as long as participants meet the ADA diagnostic criteria.

Randomisation and blinding

This is a single-blind randomised placebo-controlled clinical trial. Randomisation will be conducted by statistical professionals who will generate random results and random envelopes based on block randomisation. Subjects will be enrolled by the investigator according to protocol criteria. When subjects meet the inclusion criteria, the drug administrator will dispense a drug or placebo to the clinical investigator according to the visit order and randomisation results. The test group will undergo SRP combined with MH ointment. The control group will undergo SRP combined with a placebo. Patients will not be informed about their grouping during the trial. Due to the different colours of the MH and placebo ointments, it is impracticable to use blinding on therapists. To ensure the greatest scientific validity, a post-treatment review will be completed by an examiner who is unaware of the grouping. All the operations and data analysis will be conducted by skilled periodontists. The design, enrolment, randomisation, implementation, analysis and reporting of this study will be independent of the subjects and the public. Unblinding will only be performed if a participant experiences a serious adverse event or a life-threatening emergency that requires the clarification of the group assignment to formulate a targeted treatment plan, or after study completion and data locking for the purpose of conducting the final data analysis via comprehensive unblinding.

Patients who match the protocol criteria will undergo supragingival scaling during the screening phase and be reviewed at the baseline. Basic information and medical histories will be collected during screening. All participants will receive oral hygiene instructions, including the dangers of periodontal disease, the patient’s role in plaque control, tooth brushing instructions and the usage mode of inter-proximal cleaning tools. All participants will be provided with uniform toothpaste and toothbrushes.

Treatment and measurement protocols

GCF and subgingival plaque samples of the target teeth need to be collected at baseline and after 2 and 6 months. GCF samples from four selected interproximal pocket sites of the four assigned teeth are taken by absorbent paper points. A Mettler Toledo XPE206 analytical balance will be used to weigh 0.5 mL Eppendorf tubes with paper points measuring 10×2 mm prior to sampling. Periodontists gently insert the paper into the target sites, pausing for 30 s when resistance is encountered, and then immediately place it into an Eppendorf tube. The entire operation is conducted under clean, dry conditions on the dental surface. The Eppendorf tubes are weighed again and cryopreserved at –80°C. Four independent samples will be taken from each subject. The contents of IL-1β, IL-17, calprotectin and insulin in the elution will be assessed by ELISA. Subgingival plaque samples will be gathered into one 0.5 mL Eppendorf tube using a sterile small excavator from multiple sites at the four designated teeth. Eppendorf tubes are cryopreserved at –80°C. 16S rRNA from bacteria in plaque samples is sequenced with the third-generation Pacbio sequencing platform.

Fasting venous blood samples (approximately 15 mL per time) are collected at the screening visit, 2 months post-treatment and 6 months post-treatment. 10 mL blood samples are placed in a non-anticoagulant tube for 15–30 min. After centrifugation at 1700×g at 25°C, the upper serum layer is aspirated in 6–8 Eppendorf tubes and cryopreserved at –80°C for further IL-1β, IL-17, calprotectin and insulin measurements by ELISA. The remaining 5 mL blood samples are equally distributed for CBC, blood biochemistry and HbA1c measurements. A haematology analyser and full-automatic biochemical analyser are used to analyse blood samples. Biochemical indicators include the following: uric acid, triglyceride, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total protein, albumin, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, γ-glutamyl transferase, albumin/globulin ratio, blood glucose, urea, creatinine, total cholesterol, creatine kinase, lactate dehydrogenase, calcium, phosphorus, high-density lipoprotein (HDL) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL). SRP is performed at baseline. All operators are experienced periodontists. Periodontal examinations are conducted prior to SRP to identify treatment sites with PD≥4 mm. Prior to examinations, all examiners have completed a standardised calibration procedure for periodontal assessments and inter-examiner reliability has been formally verified. Local infiltration anaesthesia is administered at the corresponding sites using 1.7–3.4 mL per patient of Acteon articaine epinephrine injection. Following rinsing with 3% hydrogen peroxide, the operators complete full-mouth SRP (FM-SRP) in a single session within 3 hours using the Acteon ultrasonic handpiece with 1S tip and Hu-Friedy Gracey curettes. The operators confirm the presence of supragingival and subgingival calculus residues using Williams periodontal probes. The treatment is completed only when no calculus residues are detected at all treated sites, followed by the irrigation of periodontal pockets with 3% hydrogen peroxide solution to achieve haemostasis. The standardised SRP procedure is performed according to the guidelines described in Newman and Carranza’s Clinical Periodontology 13th ed, Chapter 50, Scaling and Root Planing.34

The application of 2% MH ointment (Periocline, Sunstar Inc., Japan), a commercially available drug registered with National Medical Products Administration (approval number: HJ20150106), as an adjunctive therapy to SRP in the test group is performed at baseline, and 1 week and 2 weeks post-treatment. Prior to drug administration, all treated teeth with PD≥5 mm will be isolated with sterile cotton rolls and dried gently to avoid dilution of the ointment by saliva. A sterile blunt-tipped syringe-type applicator provided commercially is used for drug delivery. In accordance with the package insert (online supplemental file 2), MH ointment will be injected into periodontal pockets slowly until mild overflow from the gingival margin is observed (approximately 0.05 mL per tooth, 500 mg per patient). The insertion depth depends on PD at the corresponding site. Dosage depends on pocket depth and number of PD≥5 mm sites. Following administration, neither occlusal adjustment nor periodontal dressing is required. Participants will be instructed to avoid mouth rinsing, drinking or eating for 30 min after MH administration. The dosage and administration of MH ointment in this study is based on the package insert (online supplemental file 2) including pharmacodynamic evidence from Sunstar Inc. (Japan) and prior study.35 According to the package insert, the recommended dosage and administration are as follows: after SRP, the ointment is injected to fill the periodontal pockets at the affected sites, once weekly, and optimal clinical efficacy is achieved with four consecutive applications. Since conventional SRP is performed a maximum of three times, we administer the medication once weekly for three consecutive weeks to avoid additional visits while maximising clinical efficacy.

Data collection

Statistical analyses will be synchronised directly to the computer and be accessible only to the researchers involved in the project and to the principal investigator. The storage and supervision of data will be performed simultaneously by two different research workers in consideration of the absence of a data monitoring committee in our hospital. All data collected will be tabulated by one researcher and be checked by another. The identification code will ensure the confidentiality of participants.

Sample size

We will investigate quantitative variables by setting equal sample sizes in the test and control groups and assessing differences between these groups. The sample size is calculated based on the null hypothesis that the difference in PD change between the two groups is zero.

In a previous study, PD change within each subject group was normally distributed with a standard deviation (σ) of 0.8 mm.14 The inspection level (α) associated with this test of the null hypothesis for equal population means in the experimental and control groups to be equal is 0.05. If the true difference in PD change between the experimental and control means (δ) is 0.7 mm,14 given a 20% participant loss rate, we will need to examine 28 experimental subjects and 28 control subjects in order to reject the null hypothesis with a power level (β) 0.2.

Evaluated outcomes and statistical analysis

Statistical analyses will be performed using SAS software V.9.4. P values<0.05 indicate a significant difference.

The intent-to-treat (ITT) study population will be conducted as a primary analysis set. The modified ITT study population will be conducted as a secondary analysis set, excluding subjects who violate essential eligibility criteria, do not receive any intervention or do not have any observational data after randomisation. The safety set (SS) will serve as the primary analysis set for security metrics.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome will be changes in pocket sites with PD≥6 mm from the baseline to 6 months post-treatment. The mixed model with repeated measures (MMRM) will be performed to analyse differences between the test and control groups. The dependent variable is the change in pocket sites with PD≥6 mm from the baseline to 6 months post-treatment. Treatment group, visit and group-visit interactions will be fixed effects. Subject will be the random effect and baseline PD values will be covariates. The variance-covariance structure in subjects will be an unstructured variance structure. Estimates of the least square mean of the change in PD values from the baseline, with 95% CIs, will be calculated for the test and placebo groups at 6 months. The change has important implications for the periodontal clinical efficacy of minocycline in severe periodontal destruction.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes will be changes in sites with PD≥5 mm from the baseline to 6 months, CAL, PlI, BI, HbA1c, CBC, blood biochemistry, IL-1β, IL-17, calprotectin and insulin levels in serum and GCF, and the microbiological composition of subgingival plaques before and 2 and 6 months after SRP. Changes in PD at PD≥5 mm sites, CAL, PlI, BI and cell factors in GCF indicate the recovery of periodontal inflammation. HbA1c, CBC, blood biochemistry and cell factors in serum direct systemic metabolic levels. The microbiological composition of subgingival plaques illustrates the role of MH in periodontal inflammation from a pathogenic viewpoint. MMRM will be applied to investigate differences between the intervention and control groups.

Withdrawal

Patients may quit the research whenever they want without affecting subsequent periodontal treatment in the department. During the study, all adverse events (AE) will be documented in detail and reported to the Ethics Committee and all investigators within 24 hours of occurrence. The continuation of the intervention will be determined by investigators according to AE severity.

Patient and public involvement

Participants and the public are not involved in the design, recruitment, assessment, implementation or reporting of the study.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval for the study was provided by the Ethics Committee of Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology (PKUSSIRB-2024102139b). The study protocol has been revised twice, involving changes to the eligibility criteria and outcome measures. The amendments to the study protocol included raising the upper age limit from 70 to 75 years in the eligibility criteria, excluding individuals who smoke≥10 cigarettes per day within 12 months prior to screening and adding outcome measures including HDL and LDL in blood samples and insulin level in serum and GCF. The modifications to outcome measures only involved laboratory test indicators for which testing had not yet been conducted. No participants violated the newly added exclusion criteria before the revision. Therefore, the revised protocol content will not have any impact on the final results of the trial. All amendments have been approved by the Ethics Committee. All researchers and participants have been informed of the revisions, and participants have signed informed consent forms accordingly. The relevant changes have also been updated on the clinical trial registration platform. All clinical statistics will be properly stored by two designated researchers. Our results will be published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal.

Discussion

This is a single-blind randomised controlled clinical trial mainly to account for differences between the colour of the placebo and MH ointment. We previously added various colours to the placebo that may be used as drug excipients; however, it was difficult to adjust the hue of the placebo to achieve full colour consistency with minocycline ointment, and the long-term toxicity and stability of the colour-added placebo have yet to be investigated. A mixture of petroleum jelly and paraffin oil was also considered as a placebo to achieve colour consistency with minocycline ointment. However, since hydrocarbon-based oils, such as petroleum jelly, lack hydrophilicity as a base, their workability is poor. Therefore, we will use a colouring-free placebo in this study and conduct a single-blind randomised placebo-controlled clinical trial. Although blinding cannot be implemented for researchers due to the colour difference between the two preparations, blinding will be applied to participants, outcome assessors and data analysts. The outcome assessors will not perform SRP or medication administration for any participants. This design minimises any potential bias, which avoids the effects of patients’ psychological expectations on self-reported outcomes and compliance, eliminates any subjective bias in clinical parameter measurements and ensures objective statistical processing and result interpretation, enhancing the reliability and validity of the study results.

The criteria for this study specified an upper limit of 9% HbA1c for patients with T2DM. Mean HbA1c values of 9.9 and 9.3% for subjects in the test and control groups, respectively, were used to examine the effects of SRP on clinical outcomes and inflammation levels in chronic periodontitis patients with poor glycaemic control.31 Therefore, the integral number of 9% was taken as an inclusion criterion for this study. In patients with decompensated diabetes mellitus, recent studies showed that there were no conclusive observational studies or systematic reviews suggesting that SRP increases the risk of infection or other systemic complications in diabetic patients with HbA1c>8%.36 The American Academy of Periodontology also suggested in 2000 that although diabetics are generally considered to be more susceptible to infections than non-diabetics, conclusive studies to support these clinical views do not yet exist.37 Glycaemic control may reverse the increased susceptibility to infection caused by defective polymorphonuclear function and insulin resistance; however, there is no clear threshold for HbA1c.37 Another clinical trial studying SRP in T2DM patients with periodontitis set the upper limit of HbA1c to 14% and did not report any periodontal treatment-related infections or complications.38 Therefore, we consider it to be scientific and safe to set the upper limit of HbA1c to 9% for the inclusion of T2DM patients in our study.

Based on the interference of the systemic use of antibiotics with the clinical results on the topical application of MH, the prophylactic use of antibiotics prior to periodontal treatment in diabetic patients will not be performed in this study. There are no conclusive studies indicating that the systemic use of antibiotics reduces the incidence of post-procedural complications after SRP in T2DM patients with periodontitis.36 39 Furthermore, a consent report in 2018 found no specific recommendation on the need for adjunctive systemic antibiotics during SRP in patients with diabetes.39 It also remains unclear whether systemic antibiotics provide limited clinical efficacy in diabetic patients with periodontitis. Qureshi et al reported that SRP combined with systemic antibiotics had no additive effect on glycaemic control or periodontal inflammation control.38 Botero et al reached a similar conclusion, indicating that systemic antibiotics had no additional value for glycaemic control, CAL or BOP. Despite a greater median reduction in PD after 9 months in the antibiotic group (0.71 mm) than in the placebo group (0.39 mm), there is no clinical value for this difference of less than 0.5 mm.40

The present study aims to evaluate changes in systemic and periodontal biomarkers. Periodontal dysbiosis and sustained local inflammation induce elevated levels of circulating pro-inflammatory biomarkers, including tumour necrosis factor-α, IL-6 and hs-CRP, which further impair insulin sensitivity and exacerbate glucose metabolism disorders.2 Under hyperglycaemic conditions, increased systemic inflammation also induces the up-regulated expression of local inflammatory factors in the periodontium, aggravating periodontal tissue destruction and forming a bidirectional pathological axis between periodontitis and diabetes mellitus.3 In the present study, fluctuations in inflammatory and glucose metabolism biomarkers in serum and GCF following SRP with or without adjuvant MH application may mutually validate and help illustrate the interactive relationship between periodontitis and diabetes mellitus.

To eliminate potential confounding factors associated with quadrant SRP (Q-SRP) and split drug administration, our study will conduct FM-SRP and simultaneously administer medication. It has already been confirmed that the clinical efficacy of FM-SRP is not inferior to that of Q-SRP.41 Debridement will be performed by experts with clinical experience to ensure therapeutic effects. Follow-up visits will be assigned 2 and 6 months after SRP. It is reasonable to evaluate improvements in clinical indicators after periodontal treatment on a 6-month cycle. Improvements are limited for clinical indicators and microbiological levels after 6 months. One clinical study observed an improvement in periodontal clinical indicators 3, 6 and 9 months after SRP and found no significant difference in periodontal clinical indicators from 6 to 9 months after basic periodontal treatment.42 Regarding subgingival microbial levels, a clinical study confirmed significant improvements in Prevotella intermedia, Porphyromonas gingivalis, Tannerella forsythia and Treponema denticola levels in diabetic patients with periodontitis 3 months after SRP.43 Therefore, the present study will conduct follow-ups 2 and 6 months after treatment.
