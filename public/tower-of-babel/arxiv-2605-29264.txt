The weak Galerkin method for a class of Gross-Pitaevskii type eigenvalue problems
Wei Lu, Qilong Zhai

Abstract
This paper aims to employ the weak Galerkin method to solve a class of nonlinear eigenvalue problems. We proved the weak Galerkin scheme produces lower bound for the energy. Moreover, by the post-processing technique, we obtain lower bound for the ground state eigenvalue. Finally, numerical experiments are provided to validate the theoretical analysis.

In this section, we state some notations and preliminaries.

The standard Sobolev space notations are used in this paper. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega

H^{m}(\Omega)
be the Sobolev space. The notations
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,D}

H^{m}(D)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{m,D}

(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,D}

Consider the following variational models of the form

\displaystyle\inf\{E(v):v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v^{2}d\Omega=1\},

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a convex bounded domain, and the energy function
E
is defined as

\displaystyle E(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)+\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(v^{2})d\Omega,

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle a(u,v)=(\mathcal{A}\nabla u,\nabla v)+(Vu,v),\quad\forall u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

with
\mathcal{A}(x)\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{2\times 2}
is symmetric, and

V(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is the non-negative potential.

Next, we introduce the following assumptions.

Assumption (A1).
There exists a constant
\alpha
such that
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}\xi^{T}\mathcal{A}(x)\xi\geq\alpha\xi^{T}\xi
,
\forall\xi\in R^{2}
,
\forall x\in\Omega
a.e..

Assumption (A2).
F(t)\in C^{1}[0,+\infty)\cap C^{2}(0,+\infty)
, and
\displaystyle\inf_{t>0}F^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
.

Assumption (A3).
There exists
q\in[0,2)
and a constant
C
such that
|F^{\prime}(t)|\leq C(1+t^{q})
,
\forall t\geq 0
.

Assumption (A4).
F^{\prime\prime}(t)t
locally bounded in
[0,+\infty)
.

Assumption (A5).
There exists
1<r\leq 2
and
0\leq s\leq 3-r
such that:
\forall R>0
, there holds
\forall t_{1}\in(0,R)
and
\forall t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
that

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}(t_{2}^{2})t_{2}-F^{\prime}(t_{1}^{2})t_{2}-2F^{\prime\prime}(t_{1}^{2})t^{2}(t_{2}-t_{1})|\leq C(1+|t_{2}|^{s})|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{r},

where C is a positive constant depend on R.

For simplicity, we denote by
f(t)=F^{\prime}(t)
. Then, under Assumption (A2),

is twice differentiable on
H^{1}(\Omega)
and we have

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(v)=A^{v}v,\quad\langle E^{\prime\prime}(u)v,w\rangle=\langle A^{u}v,w\rangle+2(f^{\prime}(u^{2})u^{2}v,w),\quad\forall u,v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where

\displaystyle A^{v}w=-\text{div}(\mathcal{A}\nabla\cdot w)+Vw+f(v^{2})w,\quad\forall v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega).

It is easy to check, for each
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)

A^{u}
defines a self-adjoint operator and satisfies

\displaystyle\langle A^{u}v,w\rangle=(\mathcal{A}\nabla v,\nabla w)+(Vv,w)+(f(u^{2})v,w),\quad\forall v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Theorem 3.1

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that the ground state

, then under Assumptions (A1)-(A3), we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}=o(h^{\varepsilon}),~as~h\rightarrow 0.

It follows from the definition (

\displaystyle 2(E_{h}(u_{h})-E(u))

\displaystyle=\langle A_{h}^{u}u_{h},u_{h}\rangle-\langle A^{u}u,u\rangle+\int_{\Omega}F(u_{h}^{2})-F(u^{2})-f(u^{2})(u_{h}^{2}-u^{2})d\Omega.

\displaystyle 2(E_{h}(u_{h})-E(u))

\displaystyle 2(E_{h}(u_{h})-E(u))\geq h^{\varepsilon}\|u_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{h}-\lambda,

\displaystyle+\|u-\widetilde{u}_{h}\|+\|u_{h}-\widetilde{u}_{h}\|^{2}.
