Boundary four-point connectivities of conformal loop ensembles
Gefei Cai

Abstract
We derive the boundary four-point Green’s functions for conformal loop ensembles (CLE) with κ ∈ ( 4 , 8 ) \kappa\in(4,8) . Specializing to κ = 6 \kappa=6 and κ = 16 / 3 \kappa=16/3 , we establish the exact formulas for the boundary four-point connectivities in critical Bernoulli percolation and the FK-Ising model conjectured by Gori-Viti (2017, 2018). In particular, we identify a logarithmic singularity in the critical FK-Ising model. Our approach also applies to the one-bulk and two-boundary connectivities of CLE, thereby extending the factorization formula of Beliaev-Izyurov (2012) to all κ ∈ ( 4 , 8 ) \kappa\in(4,8) .

Introduction

For over two decades, Schramm–Loewner evolution (SLE)
[
Sch00
]
and its loop counterpart, the conformal loop ensemble (CLE)
[
She09
,
SW12
]
, have played a central role in the study of scaling limits of two-dimensional critical models. The family
\SLE_{\kappa}
, indexed by
\kappa>0
, describes random fractal curves that arise as scaling limits of interfaces in critical lattice models, while
\CLE_{\kappa}
describes collections of loops that encode their full scaling limits, with each loop locally behaving like an
\SLE_{\kappa}
.

In this paper, we focus on the regime
\kappa\in(4,8)
, which is conjectured to describe the scaling limits of critical FK-
q
percolation models with
q\in(0,4)
, where
\sqrt{q}=-2\cos(4\pi/\kappa)
. For Bernoulli percolation (
q=1
), Smirnov’s seminal work
[
Smi01
]
established conformal invariance and convergence to
\SLE_{6}

[
Smi01
,
CN07
]
, and to
\CLE_{6}
for the full scaling limit
[
CN06
]
. For the FK-Ising model (
q=2
), the scaling limit has also been proved to be
\CLE_{16/3}

[
CDCH+14
,
KS19
,
KS16
]
.

Beyond scaling limits, SLE and CLE provide powerful tools for uncovering the rich integrable structures in critical planar lattice models, and many results that once appeared mysterious at the discrete level can now be established through SLE/CLE. Notable examples include percolation arm exponents
[
SW01
,
LSW02
]
, left-passage probabilities
[
Sch01
]
, Watts’ crossing formula
[
Dub06b
]
, and conformal radii of CLE
[
SSW09
]
. Such integrable structures also emerge in the study of multiple SLEs and systems of second-order Belavin–Polyakov–Zamolodchikov (BPZ) equations
[
BPZ84
]
, which yield connection probabilities for multiple interfaces in various critical models; see e.g.
[
PW19
,
LPW25
,
FPW24
,
FLPW24
]
. Exact solvabilities for SLE/CLE have also been obtained through their coupling with Liouville quantum gravity (LQG)
[
DMS21
,
AHS24
]
, including derivations of the backbone exponent
[
NQSZ24
]
and three-point connectivity
[
ACSW24
]
in critical percolation.

While two- and three-point observables are now well understood
[
ACSW24
]
, four-point connectivities exhibit nontrivial dependence on the conformal cross-ratio. In this paper, we derive boundary four-point connectivities for
\CLE_{\kappa}
with
\kappa\in(4,8)
. These quantities are the (conjectural) scaling limits of the probabilities that four marked boundary points are connected according to one of the three possible link patterns
\{(1234),(12)(34),(14)(23)\}
in critical FK-
q
percolation with free boundary conditions; see (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) for precise definitions. For special values of
\kappa
corresponding to integer
q
, explicit formulas were previously conjectured by Gori and Viti
[
GV17
,
GV18
]
from a conformal field theory (CFT) perspective.

In the four-point setting, conformal automorphisms cannot fix all marked points simultaneously, so the techniques from SLE/LQG coupling used in the three-point case
[
ACSW24
]
no longer apply. Moreover, since our problem concerns boundary connectivities in CLE rather than configurations involving multiple interfaces, the methods of multiple SLE and BPZ equations developed in
[
PW19
]
are not directly applicable. Our approach proceeds in four steps. First, we express the CLE boundary four-point connectivities in terms of boundary Green’s functions of the SLE bubble measure. Second, we prove that the boundary Green’s functions of chordal SLE satisfy a second-order BPZ equation, as expected in
[
FZ23
]
. Since the SLE bubble measure arises as a limit of chordal SLE, its boundary Green’s functions are limits of solutions to this equation. Third, we apply Dubédat’s fusion framework
[
Dub15a
]
to derive a third-order differential equation satisfied by these limiting objects, namely the four-point connectivities; see Theorem
1.3
. This framework, originating in CFT to produce higher-order BPZ equations, was rigorously formulated in
[
Dub15a
]
in the SLE setting using PDE and representation-theoretic methods. Finally, for each link pattern, we identify the corresponding solution to this third-order equation.

The final step constitutes the main technical novelty of the paper; see Theorem
1.4
. The third-order differential equation admits a three-dimensional space of solutions, corresponding to three distinct Frobenius series. For the link patterns
(12)(34)
(resp.
(14)(23)
), the leading-order asymptotics as the cross-ratio tends to
1
(resp.
0
) are governed by boundary three-arm exponents, and thus allow for direct identifications with the higher-order Frobenius series. For the remaining link pattern
(1234)
, however, the leading order corresponds to the lowest-order Frobenius series, and it is not a priori clear whether and how the other two Frobenius series may appear as subleading terms.
To resolve this ambiguity, we carry out a refined analysis of the subleading asymptotics, based on the observation that the subleading term of the CLE partition function defined in
[
MW18
]
with two wired boundary arcs decays rapidly. This enables us to determine the higher-order contributions and to uniquely identify each boundary connectivity with a specific solution to the third-order equation.

We first present our results in the setting of critical percolation, confirming the conjectural formula in
[
GV18
]
. We then extend the results to general
\CLE_{\kappa}
with
\kappa\in(4,8)
in Section
1.2
. In particular, for
\kappa=\frac{16}{3}
(corresponding to the FK-Ising model), we identify a logarithmic singularity in the boundary four-point connectivities, reflecting the underlying logarithmic CFT structure and confirming the conjectures of
[
GV17
]
. In Section
1.3
, we summarize the main ideas of the proof and discuss further applications. In particular, our approach also applies to one-bulk and two-boundary correlation functions; in this setting, we extend the factorization formula of
[
KSZ06
,
BI12
]
for
\kappa=6
to all
\kappa\in(4,8)
(see Theorem
1.7
).

1.1
Boundary four-point connectivities of Bernoulli percolation

Let
\delta\mathbbm{T}\cap\mathbbm{H}
be the triangular lattice on the upper half-plane with mesh size
\delta
. For
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}\in\mathbbm{R}
, we say they are in counterclockwise order if there exists
k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
such that
x_{k}<x_{k+1}<x_{k+2}<x_{k+3}
(we define
x_{i+4}:=x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 4

For
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
in counterclockwise order, let
x_{i}^{\delta}
be an approximation to

on
\delta\mathbbm{T}\cap\mathbbm{H}

\delta\mathbbm{T}\cap\mathbbm{H}

\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}

\displaystyle P^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}],

(1.1)

\displaystyle P^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\not\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}],

\displaystyle P^{(14)(23)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}\not\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}].

The existence of these limits is proved in
[
CF24
, Theorem 1.9]
, and the normalization factor
\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}
is from the percolation boundary one-arm exponent
\frac{1}{3}
. Note that when
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
is in counterclockwise order, one cannot have
\{x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\not\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}\}
.
We also let

P^{\rm total}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}):=P^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+P^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+P^{(14)(23)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}).

Our first main result, Theorem
1.1
, gives exact expressions for these limits, thereby proving the formulas conjectured in
[
GV18
]
. Let
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
be as above, and

\lambda:=\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{4}-x_{3})}{(x_{4}-x_{2})(x_{3}-x_{1})}\in(0,1)

be the cross-ratio of
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}
. Define two functions

\displaystyle F_{L}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\lambda(1-\lambda))^{\frac{4}{9}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(-\frac{2}{9},-\frac{1}{18},\frac{7}{9};\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{4}{27}\frac{(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+1)^{3}}{(1-\lambda)^{2}\lambda^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle F_{S}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)^{2}\lambda^{2}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\frac{4}{3},\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{3};\frac{8}{3},3;4\lambda(1-\lambda)\right).

Here

is the generalized hypergeometric function. For
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}
, let
V_{2}(\lambda):=F_{S}(\lambda)

V_{2}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}(1+\frac{1}{3}\lambda+O(\lambda^{2}))

. For

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V_{0},V_{2}
be defined as above. There exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle P^{\rm total}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{(x_{4}-x_{2})(x_{3}-x_{1})}{(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{4}-x_{3})(x_{3}-x_{2})(x_{4}-x_{1})}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}V_{0}(\lambda),

\displaystyle P^{(14)(23)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=AC\left(\frac{(x_{4}-x_{2})(x_{3}-x_{1})}{(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{4}-x_{3})(x_{3}-x_{2})(x_{4}-x_{1})}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}V_{2}(\lambda).

(with

\displaystyle P^{\rm total}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=C(x_{2}-x_{1})^{-\frac{2}{3}}(x_{4}-x_{3})^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left(1+\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{8}{45}|\log\lambda|+\frac{16}{25}\right)+O(\lambda^{3}|\log\lambda|)\right).

1.2
Boundary four-point connectivities of CLE

We now state our result for general
\CLE_{\kappa}
with
\kappa\in(4,8)
, from which Theorem
1.1
follows by taking
\kappa=6
.
\CLE_{\kappa}
is a random collection of loops satisfying the domain Markov property and conformal invariance, and each loop is an
\SLE_{\kappa}
curve
[
She09
,
SW12
]

\kappa\in(4,8)

\kappa\in(4,8),\hskip 10.00002pth:=\frac{8}{\kappa}-1\in(0,1).

Let
\Gamma
be a (non-nested)
\CLE_{\kappa}
on the upper half-plane
\mathbbm{H}
, and let
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
be the collection of loops in
\Gamma
that touch the boundary
\partial\mathbbm{H}=\mathbbm{R}
. For each loop
\ell\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
, denote
\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}
to be the
(1-h)
-dimensional Minkowski content measure of
\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}
, such that for any open interval
J\subset\mathbbm{R}
,

\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(J):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-h}{\rm Leb}_{\mathbbm{R}}(\{x\in J:{\rm\mathrm{dist}}(x,\ell\cap\mathbbm{R})<\varepsilon\}).

(1.2)

The existence of
\nu_{\eta\cap\mathbbm{R}}
is proved by
[
Law15
]
(see also
[
Zha22
]
) and the local absolute continuity between
\CLE_{\kappa}
and
\SLE_{\kappa}
.

For
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 4}\in\mathbbm{R}
in counterclockwise order, similar to (
1.1
), we define three types of boundary four-point Green’s functions of
\CLE_{\kappa}
as

\displaystyle G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}dx_{4}=\mathbbm{E}\left[\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx_{i})\right],

(1.3)

\displaystyle G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}dx_{4}=\mathbbm{E}\left[\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)}{\bf 1}_{\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx_{i})\prod_{j=3}^{4}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx_{j})\right],

\displaystyle G^{(14)(23)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=G^{(12)(34)}(x_{4},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Here the expectation
\mathbbm{E}
is with respect to the
\CLE_{\kappa}
configuration
\Gamma
on
\mathbbm{H}
.
We also let

G^{\rm total}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+G^{(14)(23)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}).

\CLE_{\kappa}

1.1
) in the critical FK-
q
percolation, whose scaling limit is conjectured to be
\CLE_{\kappa}
with

Let
\mathscr{P}
be the collection of link patterns, i.e.

\mathscr{P}:=\{(1234),(12)(34),(14)(23),{\rm total}\}.

Note that these boundary Green’s functions satisfy the conformal covariance

G^{p}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{4}|\phi^{\prime}(x_{i})|^{h}\right)\cdot G^{p}(\phi(x_{1}),\phi(x_{2}),\phi(x_{3}),\phi(x_{4})),\hskip 10.00002ptp\in\mathscr{P}

\mathbbm{H}
. Therefore, for each

(1.4)

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\kappa\in(4,8)
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\kappa^{3}\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}U^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+\kappa\left[3(\kappa-4)(\kappa-8)+\lambda(\lambda-1)(18\kappa^{2}-212\kappa+608)\right]U^{\prime}

See also

Remark 1.6
.

In

, the authors formulate a precise conjecture for the limiting connection probabilities of general critical loop

models, which also give the conjectural connection probabilities for multichordal

with

boundary arcs (see

). In the regime

, this conjecture was proved for

(FK-Ising)
[

]
and for

. In principle, by taking

and shrinking four of the eight boundary arcs, one could obtain the CLE boundary Green’s functions (

1.3
Discussions

We now briefly discuss our proof strategy. The first step is to identify a precise correspondence between
\CLE_{\kappa}
and the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure for
\kappa\in(4,8)
. To achieve this, we show that the loop sampled from the counting measure on boundary-touching loops in a
\CLE_{\kappa}
configuration is equal in law to the unrooted
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure, see Proposition
2.2
. This identification allows us to express the boundary Green’s functions of
\CLE_{\kappa}
(
1.3
) in terms of those of the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure. Furthermore,
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure is also the weak limit of the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
when its starting point approaches its end point. According to
[
FZ23
]
, the boundary Green’s functions of chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
are finite and satisfy a martingale property as the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
grows. Combined with their smoothness, the martingale property then yields a pair of second-order PDEs, known as BPZ equations. Establishing such smoothness is nontrivial and relies on Hörmander’s hypoellipticity; see Lemma
2.8
, as inspired by
[
Dub15b
,
FZ23
]
.
Then, we apply a fusion procedure to these PDEs to derive a third-order differential equation satisfied by boundary Green’s functions of the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble, which corresponds to collapsing two marked boundary points. As mentioned before, this step follows from the framework in
[
Dub15a
]
, which also appeared in recent works
[
KLPR25
,
LPR25
]
for specific values of
\kappa
. This proves Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem
1.4
requires a delicate asymptotic analysis for the boundary Green’s functions defined in (
1.3
). Its crucial ingredient is Proposition
4.1
, which establishes the
o(\lambda^{h})
subleading order in the sum of the Green’s functions
G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
as the cross-ratio
\lambda\to 0
. The proof of Proposition
4.1
is arguably the most subtle part of this paper. In particular, it involves re-expressing the Green’s functions via a careful decomposition of boundary-touching
\CLE_{\kappa}
loops (see Corollary
4.6
), as well as noting the rapid subleading decay in the partition function of
\CLE_{\kappa}
with two wired boundary arcs, based on its connection probability
[
MW18
]
(see (
4.11
)). According to
[
FLPW24
, Lemma 6.1]
, such rapid decay of subleading terms also holds for Coulomb gas integrals, which are conjectured to be the partition functions of general multichordal
\CLE_{\kappa}
. Theorems
1.1
and
1.5
then follow by taking
\kappa=6
and
\kappa=\frac{16}{3}
.

Our approach to the boundary four-point Green’s functions can be extended to the one-bulk and two-boundary connectivities of
\CLE_{\kappa}
.
Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbbm{R}
,
z\in\mathbbm{H}
, and consider the Green’s function

G(x_{1},x_{2},z)dx_{1}dx_{2}dz=\mathbbm{E}\left[\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)}\Upsilon_{\ell}(dz)\prod_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx_{i})\right].

(1.8)

Here,
\Upsilon_{\ell}
denotes the
Miller-Schoug measure

[
MS24
]
of the
\CLE_{\kappa}
gasket surrounded by
\ell
. The Miller-Schoug measure is a canonical measure supported on the
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
gasket, characterized by conformal covariance and the domain Markov property. It is conjectured to coincide with the
(2-\alpha)
-dimensional Minkowski content of the
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
gasket, where
\alpha:=\frac{(3\kappa-8)(8-\kappa)}{32\kappa}
. For
\CLE_{6}
gasket, its Miller-Schoug measure is shown to be the scaling limit of the normalized counting measure on the critical percolation cluster
[
GPS13
,
CL24
]
. Consequently,
G(x_{1},x_{2},z)
can be interpreted as the normalized probability that
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
, and
z
belong to the same cluster.

Theorem 1.7
.

For
\kappa\in(4,8)
, let
h=\frac{8}{\kappa}-1
and
\alpha
be as above. Then

G(x_{1},x_{2},z)=C|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{-h}{\rm Im}(z)^{h-\alpha}|z-x_{1}|^{-h}|z-x_{2}|^{-h}

(1.9)

for some constant
C\in(0,\infty)
.

For the case of critical Bernoulli percolation (i.e.
\kappa=6
), Theorem
1.7
recovers the factorization formula conjectured by
[
KSZ06
]
and later proved by
[
BI12
]
: the square of the one-bulk and two-boundary connectivity at
z\in\mathbbm{H},x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbbm{R}
factorizes as the product of two bulk-boundary connectivities at
(z,x_{1})
and
(z,x_{2})
and the boundary two-point connectivity at
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Theorem
1.7
extends this remarkably simple structure to all
\kappa\in(4,8)
, showing that the factorization is in fact a universal feature of
\CLE_{\kappa}
and hence of critical FK-
q
percolation.

However, our approach does not apply to the bulk two-point correlation functions, which were recently conjectured in physics
[
DJNS26
]
, as the tools of the Loewner equation are no longer available. The same limitation applies to the four-point functions on the sphere. For the latter, differential equations can be derived from the CFT perspective in some special cases
[
GC06
]
, which admit exact closed-form solutions. It would be interesting to explore whether such equations admit a probabilistic interpretation in the SLE context.

SLE bubble measures and CLE boundary Green’s functions

In the following, we fix
\kappa\in(4,8)
and
h:=\frac{8}{\kappa}-1\in(0,1)
.
For a finite measure
M
, we denote its total mass by
|M|
, and denote
M^{\#}:=\frac{1}{|M|}M
.
We usually use the superscript # to indicate that some measure is a probability measure. For a compact set
A\subset\overline{\mathbbm{H}}
, we denote
\mathcal{U}(A)
to be the unbounded connected component of
\mathbbm{H}\setminus A
. For
z\in\mathbbm{C}
and

, we let
B(z,\varepsilon):=\{w\in\mathbbm{C}:|w-z|<\varepsilon\}
. For
x\in\mathbbm{R}
, we also let

be the open interval
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
.

We first review the background on the SLE bubble measure in Section
2.1
, and then show the relation between boundary-touching CLE loops and the (unrooted) SLE bubble measure in Section
2.2
. In Section
2.3
, we relate the CLE boundary four-point connectivities (
1.3
) to the boundary Green’s functions of SLE bubble and of chordal SLE. In Section
2.4

.

2.1
SLE bubble measure

The
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure was first introduced in
[
SW12
]
, and systematically studied in
[
Zha25
]
. For
x,y\in\mathbbm{R}
, let
\mu_{\mathbbm{H},x,y}^{\#}
be the law of the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
from
x
to
y
. Then the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure
\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
rooted at
x
is defined by the weak limit

\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-h}\mu_{\mathbbm{H},x,x+\varepsilon}^{\#}.

(2.1)

For
\gamma
sampled from
\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
, the
(1-h)
-dimensional Minkowski content measure
\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}
of
\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}
exists
[
Zha22
, Theorem 6.17]
.
According to
[
Zha25
, Theorem 4.8]
, we can define the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure
\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
with two marked points
x,y
such that

\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dy=\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dy)\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma).

(2.2)

Here the total mass of
\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)
is
C|y-x|^{-2h}
, where
C\in(0,\infty)
is a fixed constant introduced in
[
Zha25
, Eq.(58)]
.
Such
\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
and
\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
corresponds to the one-point and two-point pinned loop measures in
[
SW12
]
, respectively. According to
[
Zha25
, Section 4.2]
, for
x<y
, the normalized probability measure
(\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}})^{\#}
can be decomposed as follows: first sample the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}(2)
curve
\eta
on
\mathbbm{H}
from
x
to
y
with force point at
x-
, and then sample the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
curve
\eta^{\prime}
on the remaining unbounded domain
\mathcal{U}(\eta)
from
y
to
x
. Then the concatenation of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
has the same law as
(\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}})^{\#}
. The marginal law of
\eta^{\prime}
is the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}(2)
from
y
to
x
, with force point at
y-
.

From (
2.1
), we also introduce the
unrooted

\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure
\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}
via

\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)=\frac{1}{|\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}|^{2}}\int_{\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}}\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dxdy.

(2.3)

By
[
Zha25
, Theorem 4.13]
,
\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}
then is invariant under Möbius transforms on
\mathbbm{H}
, with

\displaystyle\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dx,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dy)\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dxdy.

(2.5)

We refer readers to
[
Zha25
]
for further background on
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measures.

The following result from
[
SW12
]
links
\CLE_{\kappa}
to the
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure. The original statement in
[
SW12
]
is for simple
\CLE_{\kappa}
, i.e.
\kappa\in(\frac{8}{3},4]
. However, since the proof there only uses the conformal Markovian exploration process of

, which is based on the domain Markov property of

and conformal invariance, the result is still valid for

.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
SW12
]
)
.

Suppose
z\in\mathbbm{H}
, and
\Gamma
is a
\CLE_{\kappa}
on
\mathbbm{H}
. Let
T_{\varepsilon}(z)
(resp.
\widehat{T}_{\varepsilon}(z)
) be the event that the
\CLE_{\kappa}
loop surrounding
z
intersects
B(0,\varepsilon)
(resp.
I(0,\varepsilon)
). Then as
\varepsilon\to 0
, both
\mathbbm{P}[T_{\varepsilon}(z)]
and
\mathbbm{P}[\widehat{T}_{\varepsilon}(z)]
are
\varepsilon^{h+o(1)}
; conditioned on
T_{\varepsilon}(z)
(resp.

\ell
converges to

2.2
Boundary-touching CLE loops and unrooted SLE bubble measure

We now relate the boundary-touching
\CLE_{\kappa}
loops to the unrooted
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\CLE_{\kappa}
configuration on
\mathbbm{H}
, and let
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
be the collection of loops in
\Gamma
that touch
\mathbbm{R}
. Denote
\rho
to be the law of the outer boundary of the loop chosen from the counting measure, i.e.
\rho(\mathcal{A})=\mathbbm{E}\left[\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)}{\bf 1}_{\ell\in\mathcal{A}}\right]
for any measurable
\mathcal{A}
. Then there exists
{\mathfrak{C}}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\rho
is equal to the unrooted
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure
{\mathfrak{C}}\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}
defined in (
2.3
).

Proof.

Let
\ell_{z}\in\Gamma
be the outermost loop with its outer boundary surrounding
z\in\mathbbm{H}
. We claim that
\ell_{z}
, restricted on that
\ell_{z}\cap\mathbbm{R}\neq\emptyset
, has the same law as the unrooted
\SLE_{\kappa}
bubble measure
\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}
restricted on surrounding
z
. The result then follows by varying
z
.

Denote the law of
\ell_{z}
by
\Theta_{z}
. Let
u(\varepsilon)=\mathbbm{P}[\widehat{T}_{\varepsilon}(i)]=\varepsilon^{h+o(1)}
where
\widehat{T}_{\varepsilon}(i)
is defined in Lemma
2.1
. For
x\in\mathbbm{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
E_{z,x,\varepsilon}
be the event that
\ell_{z}
intersects
I(x,\varepsilon)
. Now, consider the measure
\mathbbm{M}_{\varepsilon}:=u(\varepsilon)^{-1}{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}\Theta_{z}(d\ell)dx
. Note that for fixed
x\in\mathbbm{R}
, Lemma
2.1
implies that the measure
u(\varepsilon)^{-1}{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}\Theta_{z}
converges weakly to
C{\bf 1}_{F_{z,x}}\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
F_{z,x}
is the event that the bubble rooted at
x
surrounds
z
and
C\in(0,\infty)
is some constant (throughout this proof,
C
stands for some constant whose value can vary from line to line). Furthermore, by conformal covariance, the convergence is uniform on any bounded interval
U\subset\mathbbm{R}
. Thus, we have the vague convergence

\mathbbm{M}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbbm{M}:=C{\bf 1}_{F_{z,x}}\mu_{x}^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dx=C{\bf 1}_{\gamma\text{ surrounds }z}\nu_{\gamma\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)

(2.6)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Here the last equality is due to (
2.4
). On the other hand, note that for a.s.
\ell
sampled from
\Theta_{z}(d\ell)
such that
\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}\neq\emptyset
, by
[
Zha22
, Theorem 6.17]
,
\varepsilon^{-h}{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}dx
weakly converges to the
(1-h)
-dimensional Minkowski content
\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}
of
\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}
.
Furthermore, for any bounded interval
J\subset\mathbbm{R}
, we have
\varepsilon^{-h}\int_{J}{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}dx\to\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(J)
for
\Theta_{z}
-a.s.
\ell
as well as in
L^{2}
. Therefore, for any compactly supported and continuous function
f
, we have

\varepsilon^{-h}\int{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}f(x,\ell)dx\Theta_{z}(d\ell)\to\int f(x,\ell)\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\Theta_{z}(d\ell).

Since
\int f(x,\ell)\mathbbm{M}_{\varepsilon}(dx,d\ell)=\frac{\varepsilon^{h}}{u(\varepsilon)}\varepsilon^{-h}\int{\bf 1}_{E_{z,x,\varepsilon}}f(x,\ell)dx\Theta_{z}(d\ell)
, which converges to
\int f(x,\ell)\mathbbm{M}(dx,d\ell)
by (
2.6
), this implies the existence of the limit
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon^{h}}{u(\varepsilon)}=C\in(0,\infty)
. Consequently,

\int f(x,\ell)\mathbbm{M}(dx,d\ell)=C\int f(x,\ell)\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\Theta_{z}(d\ell),

which gives the vague convergence

\mathbbm{M}_{\varepsilon}\to C{\bf 1}_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}\neq\emptyset}\nu_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}}(dx)\Theta_{z}(d\ell)

(2.7)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Comparing (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), by deweighting the total masses of the boundary Minkowski content measures, we obtain
{\bf 1}_{\gamma\text{ surrounds }z}\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)=C{\bf 1}_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}\neq\emptyset}\Theta_{z}(d\ell)
.

Now, since
\rho
is obtained from the counting measure on boundary-touching loops and every
z\in\mathbbm{H}
is surrounded by at most one CLE loop, we have
{\bf 1}_{\ell\text{ surrounds }z}\rho(d\ell)dz={\bf 1}_{\ell\cap\mathbbm{R}\neq\emptyset}\Theta_{z}(d\ell)dz
, where
dz
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbbm{H}
. Combined with the above result, we find
{\bf 1}_{\gamma\text{ surrounds }z}\mu^{\mathrm{Bub}}(d\gamma)dz=C{\bf 1}_{\ell\text{ surrounds }z}\rho(d\ell)dz
. The result then follows by deweighting the Lebesgue areas of the regions surrounded by the bubble (resp. loop) on both sides.
∎

According to Lemma

, the proof of Proposition

instead of

(and consider the event that

intersects

in the definition of

(or

\displaystyle G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

(2.10)

\displaystyle G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

Proof.

∎

2.4
Smoothness and second-order PDEs

Based on the local martingale property (
2.14
) in Lemma
2.6
, we are able to derive a second-order PDE satisfied by
F^{(j)}
for
j=1,2
. However, in order to apply Ito’s formula, we need to
a priori
know the smoothness of
F^{(j)}
. Here we use Hörmander’s hypoellipticity to obtain the smoothness, which is inspired by the proof of
[
Dub15b
, Theorem 6]
and
[
FZ23
, Remark 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
j=1,2
, the functions
F^{(j)}(y_{1},y_{2},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
in Definition
2.5
are smooth on
\{(y_{1},y_{2},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbbm{R}^{5}:x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}\text{ and }y_{1},y_{2}<x_{1}\}
.

Proof.

Let
F^{(j)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=F^{(j)}(0,\infty,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
for simplicity. By conformal covariance, it suffices to show the smoothness of
F^{(j)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
on
\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}^{3}:x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq 3
, let
I_{i}
be an open interval containing
x_{i}
, and
U=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}\subset\mathbbm{R}_{+}^{3}
. Define a second-order differential operator
\mathcal{L}
on
C^{\infty}(U)\cap C(\overline{U})
by

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\kappa}{2}{\bf X}^{2}+2{\bf Y}-2h\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt{\bf X}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{x_{i}},\hskip 10.00002pt{\bf Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{i}}.

(2.15)

Note that the Lie brackets
[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\partial_{x_{i}}
and
[{\bf X},[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]]=2\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{3}}\partial_{x_{i}}
. Since
x_{i}
’s are mutually different on
U
, the Lie algebra generated by
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
has rank
3
on
U
. Thus,
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the Hörmander’s condition
1
1

1

i.e. for two vector fields
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
,
{\bf X},{\bf Y}
and their iterated Lie brackets
[{\bf X},{\bf Y}],[{\bf X},[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]]
etc. span the whole tangent space at every point.
. By
[
Bon69
, Theorem 5.2]
, we can then define a Poisson operator
\mathcal{P}:C(\partial U)\to C^{\infty}(U)\cap C(\overline{U})
for
\mathcal{L}
such that for any
\omega\in C(\partial U)
,
(\mathcal{P}\omega)|_{\partial U}=\omega
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}\omega)=0
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be the Loewner map with driving function
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
. By (
2.14
), we know
M_{t}^{(j)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\prod_{i=1}^{3}|\varphi^{\prime}(x_{i})|^{h}F^{(j)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a continuous local martingale. For each
t\geq 0
, let
X_{t}:=(g_{t}(x_{1})-W_{t},g_{t}(x_{2})-W_{t},g_{t}(x_{3})-W_{t})
, and let
\tau
be the first hitting time of
\partial U
for
(X_{t})
.
By optional stopping theorem,
M_{0}^{(j)}(X_{0})=\mathbbm{E}[M_{\tau}^{(j)}(X_{0})]
.
On the other hand, note that
\mathcal{L}
defined in (
2.15
) is the infinitesimal generator of
(X_{t})
with the killing rate
\frac{2h}{X_{t}^{2}}
.
Let
N^{(j)}:=\mathcal{P}(M_{0}^{(j)}|_{\partial U})\in C^{\infty}(U)
(thus
\mathcal{L}N^{(j)}=0
). Applying Dynkin’s formula to
(X_{t})
yields

N^{(j)}(X_{0})=\mathbbm{E}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{2h}{X_{t}^{2}}dt}N^{(j)}(X_{\tau})\right]=\mathbbm{E}[|\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(X_{0})|^{h}N^{(j)}(X_{\tau})].

Since
X_{\tau}\in\partial U
, we also have

\mathbbm{E}[|\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(X_{0})|^{h}N^{(j)}(X_{\tau})]=\mathbbm{E}[|\varphi_{\tau}^{\prime}(X_{0})|^{h}M_{0}^{(j)}(X_{\tau})]=\mathbbm{E}[M_{\tau}^{(j)}(X_{0})]=M_{0}^{(j)}(X_{0})=F^{(j)}(X_{0}).

Therefore,
F^{(j)}=N_{0}^{(j)}\in C^{\infty}(U)
. The result then follows from varying
U
.
∎

Now we are able to derive the second-order PDEs satisfied by

.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
u=\frac{1}{2}(y_{1}+y_{2})
and

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{uv}-\frac{1-\frac{\kappa-6}{2}}{v}\partial_{u}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\frac{4v\partial_{x_{i}}}{(x_{i}-u)^{2}-v^{2}}+\frac{2h}{(x_{i}-u+v)^{2}}-\frac{2h}{(x_{i}-u-v)^{2}}\right)\right)F=0.

Let
\eta

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W_{t}},\hskip 10.00002ptdW_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}dB_{t}-\frac{\kappa-6}{g_{t}(y_{2})-W_{t}}dt.

\displaystyle\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2h}{(x_{i}-y_{1})^{2}}-\frac{\kappa-6}{y_{2}-y_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}}+\frac{2}{y_{2}-y_{1}}\partial_{y_{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2}{x_{i}-y_{1}}\partial_{x_{i}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}\right)F^{(j)}=0.

This is also known as the second-order BPZ equation at

.
Let

\displaystyle\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2h}{(x_{i}-u+v)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2v}\left((\partial_{u}+\partial_{v})-\frac{\kappa-6}{2}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{v})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2}{x_{i}-u+v}\partial_{x_{i}}+\frac{\kappa}{8}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{v})^{2}\right)F^{(j)}=0.

By reversibility, we can also view

\displaystyle\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2h}{(x_{i}-u-v)^{2}}-\frac{1}{2v}\left((\partial_{u}-\partial_{v})-\frac{\kappa-6}{2}(\partial_{u}+\partial_{v})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2}{x_{i}-u-v}\partial_{x_{i}}+\frac{\kappa}{8}(\partial_{u}+\partial_{v})^{2}\right)F^{(j)}=0.

(2.18)

Combining (

∎

3

For
j=1,2
, recall that Proposition
2.7
establishes the convergence of

Appendix B
Discrete convergence

In this section we complete the discrete parts in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.5
, as mentioned in Section
4.2
. We first focus on the Bernoulli percolation, and it suffices to show the following

Proposition B.1
.

Let
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
. There exist constants
C,C^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]=CG^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}),

(B.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\not\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]=C^{\prime}G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}).

(B.2)

Proof.

The existence of these two limits was shown in
[
CF24
]
.
For any disjoint
A,B\subset\mathbbm{H}
, we write
\{A\leftrightarrow B\}
for the event that there exists an open path connecting
A
and
B
.
Without loss of generality, we suppose
\min|x_{i}-x_{j}|>1
. For
s>0
, let
r_{i}<s
.
Define

V:=\{I(x_{1},r_{1})\leftrightarrow B(x_{2},r_{2})\leftrightarrow I(x_{3},r_{3})\leftrightarrow B(x_{4},r_{4})\},

and
W_{i}
(resp.
\widetilde{W}_{i}
) to be the event that there exists an open path connecting
I(x_{i},r_{i})
(resp.
B(x_{i},r_{i})
) and
B(x_{i},1)
. Then by
[
Con15
, Proposition 10]
, there exists increasing functions
\varepsilon(s),m(s)
of
s
with
\varepsilon(s),m(s)\to 0
as
s\to 0
, such that for all
\delta<m(s)
,

\left|\frac{\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]}{\prod_{i=1}^{4}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{i}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow B(x_{i},1)]}\Bigg/\frac{\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[V]}{\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[W_{1}]\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[\widetilde{W}_{2}]\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[W_{3}]\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[\widetilde{W}_{4}]}-1\right|<\varepsilon(s).

By
[
DGLZ24
, Theorem 1.1]
, for each
1\leq i\leq 4
,
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{i}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow B(x_{i},1)]=C_{1}\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}(1+o(1))
for some constant
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
. By
[
CF24
, Theorem 1.9]
,
\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]
converges to a limit
L\in(0,\infty)
as
\delta\to 0
.
On the other hand, when we first let
\delta\to 0
, due to the full scaling limit convergence of critical Bernoulli percolation to
\CLE_{6}

[
CN06
]
,
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[V]\to\mathbbm{P}[E]
as
\delta\to 0
, where the event
E
is defined in Proposition
A.9
. Meanwhile,
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[W_{i}]
and
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[\widetilde{W}_{i}]
converge to their
\CLE_{6}
analogs, denoted by
\mathbbm{P}[W_{i}]
and
\mathbbm{P}[\widetilde{W}_{i}]
, as
\delta\to 0
. Therefore, for all
r_{i}<s
, we have

L(1+\varepsilon(s))^{-1}\leq C_{1}^{4}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{4}r_{i}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathbbm{P}[W_{1}]\mathbbm{P}[\widetilde{W}_{2}]\mathbbm{P}[W_{3}]\mathbbm{P}[\widetilde{W}_{4}]\right)^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{4}r_{i}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathbbm{P}[E]\right)\leq L(1-\varepsilon(s))^{-1}.

(B.3)

Now we take the limit of (
B.3
) in the following order
\mathcal{O}
: first
r_{1}\to 0
, then
r_{2},r_{4}\to 0
, finally
r_{3}\to 0
. By Proposition
A.9
, under the limit order
\mathcal{O}
, we have
\prod_{i=1}^{4}r_{i}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathbbm{P}[E]\to C_{2}G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
for some
C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
. If we write
\overline{C}
and
\underline{C}
be the limsup and liminf of
\prod_{i=1}^{4}r_{i}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathbbm{P}[W_{1}]\mathbbm{P}[\widetilde{W}_{2}]\mathbbm{P}[W_{3}]\mathbbm{P}[\widetilde{W}_{4}]
under the limit order
\mathcal{O}
, then (
B.3
) yields

L(1+\varepsilon(s))^{-1}\leq\overline{C}^{-1}C_{1}^{4}C_{2}G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\leq\underline{C}^{-1}C_{1}^{4}C_{2}G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\leq L(1-\varepsilon(s))^{-1}.

Taking
s\to 0
yields
\underline{C}=\overline{C}
, and hence

for some
C\in(0,\infty)
. This gives (
B.1
).

Now we consider
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\not\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]
. Though it is not an increasing event (hence we may not use
[
Con15
, Proposition 10]
directly), we can instead consider
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta},x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]
, and define

V^{\prime}:=\{I(x_{1},r_{1})\leftrightarrow B(x_{2},r_{2}),\ I(x_{3},r_{3})\leftrightarrow B(x_{4},r_{4})\}.

Then one can similarly use Proposition
A.9
to show that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-\frac{4}{3}}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta},x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]=C^{\prime}G^{(12)(34)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+C^{\prime\prime}G^{(1234)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

for some constant
C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}\in(0,\infty)
.
Since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]}{\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[x_{1}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{2}^{\delta},x_{3}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow x_{4}^{\delta}]}
tends to
1
as
x_{2}\to x_{3}
, we find
C^{\prime\prime}=C
. Combined with (
B.1
), we obtain (
B.2
).
∎

Remark B.2
.

As a by-product, the above proof indeed shows that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[W_{1}]=Cr_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}(1+o(1))
and
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}[\widehat{W}_{1}]=\widehat{C}r_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}(1+o(1))
as

for some constants

.

Note that the proof of
[
Con15
, Proposition 10]
is based on RSW estimates and FKG inequality to derive a variant of the one-arm event coupling in
[
GPS13
]
. Both of these have counterparts on the critical FK-Ising model, see
[
BK89
,
DCHN11
]
. The full scaling limit
\CLE_{16/3}
of the critical FK-Ising model was established in
[
KS16
]
. The sharp asymptotics
\mathbbm{P}^{\delta}_{\rm FK}[x_{i}^{\delta}\leftrightarrow B(x_{i},1)]=C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+o(1))
for the FK-Ising model was derived in
[
CF25
, Eq.(1.7)]
. Thus, we have all the needed ingredients for the FK-Ising model, and the FK-Ising analog of Proposition
B.1
follows similarly.
