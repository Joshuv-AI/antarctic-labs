1.0 Introduction
 The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility
 operates a diverse suite of advanced atmospheric radar systems that provide continuous, high-quality
 observations of clouds, precipitation, and dynamic structures in the atmosphere across its fixed and
 mobile observatories. These measurements are central to ARM’s mission of advancing the understanding
 of atmospheric processes and improving their representation in Earth system models.

 To maximize scientific impact, ARM routinely evaluates its radar portfolio and balances operational
 needs, technology modernization, and available resources. This includes investing in new capabilities,
 upgrading existing systems, and retiring instruments when they are no longer sustainable or provide
 limited scientific value. The FY27 Radar Plan is intended not only to guide these internal priorities but
 also to transparently communicate ARM’s radar strategy and planned activities to the user community.

3.0 Radar Team

 site operations, and other experts who work collaboratively to ensure the successful deployment,
 operation, and data delivery of ARM’s radar systems. Together, these components form an integrated
 team that supports the full radar life cycle – from installation and operations to the delivery of
 science-ready data.

 teams are planned to have roughly between 4.75 and 5.75 full-time equivalents (FTEs) each to dedicate
 towards the priorities documented in this radar plan.

5.0    FY27 Plan
 The FY2027 radar priorities are guided by a combination of scientific demand, operational realities, and
 long-term strategic planning. These efforts aim to maximize the scientific return of ARM’s radar assets
 while ensuring sustainable operations. Key areas of focus include continued support of the KAZR as a
 vital cornerstone of ARM’s radar capabilities, support for mobile facility deployments, upgrades to
 ARM’s aging radars to make them more robust, and advancing ARM’s radar data products. A list of the
 priorities, timelines, effort allocations, and points of contact can be found in the following tables. The full
 FY27 Gantt chart is in Appendix 1.

 5

This plan allocates a total of 7.56 FTE for primary activities (priority 1) and 2.53 FTE for secondary ones
(priority 2). With an anticipated total FTE allocation of 9.5 between data and engineering teams, it is very
likely that some of the secondary activities will not be completed in FY27. The secondary activities taken
on will depend on the status of ongoing activities and DOE and ARM priorities at the time.

5.1 Radar Operations
Radar operations encompass a range of coordinated tasks across the engineering, data, site operations, and
ingest teams. For clarity, the following categories are used in the subsequent tables:
 • Radar Operations – Engineering and site operations tasks required to keep radars running, including
 maintenance, troubleshooting, and system repairs.
 • Data Quality Monitoring – Continuous monitoring of radar data by the data mentor team,
 addressing quality issues, and assessing how engineering work impacts the data.
 • b1-Analysis – Mentor team analysis to identify offsets and corrections. Deliverables include a
 technical report documenting methods, results, and recommendations, released alongside the data.
 • Ingest Dev and Proc – Configuration of data ingest systems and application of corrections.
 Processed data are then returned to the mentor team for review.

5.2 KAZR Operations
FTE: 1.98 Priority: 1

In FY27, ARM’s Ka-band ARM Zenith Radars (KAZRs) will continue their role as the primary vertically
pointing cloud radars across ARM observatories. Operations will emphasize maintaining high data
availability through routine monitoring, preventative maintenance, and calibration activities. Building on
the first releases of b1-level data, ARM will continue yearly releases for fixed and mobile observatories.
The following FTEs will not include effort for KAZR operations.

5.5.3 SACR Upgrade to Solid State

FTE: 0.20 Priority: 1

The Extended-Interaction Klystron Amplifier (EIKA) used in the SACRs are no longer in production with
current spares expected to last 2-4 years. ARM will have to upgrade the existing radars to solid state to
continue operations. ARM will start with two Ka solid-state transmitters for laboratory testing in FY27.

5.5.4 SACR2 Pedestal Upgrades

FTE: 0.20 Priority: 1

The SACR cooling system and rotary joints have been a significant source of failure and effort to repair.
ARM will upgrade two SACRs over the next 1.5-2 years to use the Peltier thermoelectric coolers and
change the positioner to use the Parker servo chain.

5.5.5 Handbook Update

FTE: 0.15 Priority: 1

Radar handbooks have not been updated in over 10 years. The mentor team started working to update the
KAZR, SACR, and XSAPR handbooks and will continue to update handbooks, as time permits, into
FY27.

5.5.6 Data Processing Publication

FTE: 0.10 Priority: 2

The data mentor team has spent a lot of time developing processes for creating b-level radar data products
that are calibrated and quality controlled. The specifics of this processing are detailed in the b-level
reports provided with each set of data. These reports require significant effort. An article documenting
ARM’s standard b-level processing is planned to streamline these reports and provide a citable reference
for ARM’s processing efforts. Due to shifting priorities, this may only be possible to proceed with
towards the end of FY27.

5.5.7 Define Baseline Scan Strategies

FTE: 0.05 Priority: 2

 10

ARM generally has relied on PIs to define scan strategies to maximize the impact of the data to their
deployments. However, it will benefit ARM to define baseline scan strategies for both fixed and mobile
observatories for which PIs could request modifications for ARM approval as part of a field campaign
request. This is to ensure that the mentor team is involved in any conversations around changes to scan
strategies to ensure that the appropriate data are collected to support the b-level processing. The scan
strategy for the BNF radars has been finalized but additional effort will be needed to define strategies for
other sites. This effort is lower priority and expected to continue into FY27.

5.5.8 Phased Array Radar Procurement

FTE: 0.15 Priority: 1

ARM will be putting out a request for information (RFI) in FY26 for commercial phased array radar
(PAR) systems. The results of the RFI will be discussed at a listening session and based on feedback from
the community, procurement will be started. This is expected to continue into FY27. Planning of possible
use modalities is required and will guide future software development.

5.5.9 Adaptive Scanning and Cell Tracking Development

FTE: 0.2 Priority: 1

ARM will continue to develop and refine adaptive scanning and cell tracking capabilities in FY27.
Operational problems with the CSAPR2 have resulted in delays on the testing and implementation of the
API. The changes to the BNF CSAPR2 present an opportunity to implement an initial system that will
work for both BNF and AMF1. A framework called Adapt was developed to support ARM’s adaptive
scanning capabilities through real-time cell identification and motion prediction of scan targets. Adapt
identifies scan targets along with their spatial extent, motion vectors, and likelihood of passing near an
ARM site. Currently, Adapt identifies convective cells and computes scan-wise cell properties. Ongoing
development involves the implementation of cell tracking to allow real-time estimates of the convective
cell life cycle and estimating cell volume properties. Adapt is released on DOE CODE (ID: 182016) as an
open-source software (https://doi.org/10.11578/dc.20260529.11).

Adapt will be tested on BNF CSAPR2 by the end of FY26. The control of ARM radar scan strategies
based on target cell recommendations derived from Adapt will initially remain with ARM staff (mentors,
translators, or site science team, etc.) but users will be able to request access through an ARM Field
Campaign (AFC) request. In the meantime, PIs can use and test the Adapt software for their systems and
eventual use during ARM deployments.

In parallel with the development of adaptive scanning capabilities, ARM will develop associated data
products including post-processed cell-tracking products, metadata standards, and processing workflows
needed to ensure that adaptive scanning datasets are readily usable by the broader scientific community.
These efforts will focus on providing standardized descriptions of dynamic scan strategies, preserving
sufficient metadata for reproducibility, and developing higher-level products that allow users to analyze
adaptive scanning observations without requiring detailed knowledge of the radar control system.

5.6 Future Priorities
As ARM continues to support cutting-edge atmospheric research, the radar program continues to evolve
its capabilities to address emerging scientific questions, and DOE priorities. Beyond sustaining routine
radar operations, several strategic activities are expected to shape the future of the radar portfolio over the
coming years. The noted fiscal years are tentative estimates of when ARM may be able to start these
activities.
 • Development of a new W-band vertically pointing radar (FY28-FY30) to restore and enhance
 capabilities previously provided by the retired MWACR.
 • Modernization of radar data quality monitoring and processing workflows (FY27-FY29)
 through increased automation and the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning
 (ML) techniques, while maintaining expert oversight from ARM’s mentor teams to ensure the
 production of trusted, science-quality data sets.
 • Expansion of adaptive scanning and AI-driven radar operations (FY27-FY28), including the use
 of the SGP I6 XSAPR as an operational testbed for intelligent scan optimization, automated target
 identification, and decision-support capabilities that enhance the scientific value of ARM’s scanning
 radar observations. Additional expansion of the Adapt capabilities including a cell life cycle-focused
 VAP will be explored.
 • Advancement of community software and data standards for phased array radar systems
 (FY27-FY28) including the development of open-source tools, interoperable data formats, and
 processing workflows that facilitate the adoption and scientific use of emerging radar technologies
 within ARM and the broader atmospheric research community.
 • Advancement of frozen precipitation and ice microphysical retrievals (FY27-FY28) by
 leveraging ARM’s Ka- and W-band scanning radars together with complementary instrumentation.
 Improving quantitative retrievals of snowfall and ice properties represents a significant scientific
 opportunity and will strengthen ARM’s capabilities in cold-season precipitation.

5.7.2.1 Corrected Moments in Antenna Coordinates

CMAC is ARM’s value-added product that enhances precipitation radar data in native range, azimuth,
and elevation coordinates. Where needed, it builds on the b-level products by dealiasing, attenuation
correcting, providing additional clutter ID, and retrieving rain rate or snowfall rate using the regionally
appropriate relations.

 13

 • Operationalize CMAC at Bankhead National Forest, dependent on CSAPR operations
 • Continue to monitor the performance of the retrievals at BNF
 • Implement FY25 work on sea ice detection at NSA
 • Use in situ measurements at E12 and C1 at NSA to determine snowfall and rainfall rate relations
 dependent on requirements
 • Perform initial CMAC (non-operational) on NSA XSAPR.

5.7.2.2 Surface Quantitative Precipitation Estimation (SQUIRE)

SQUIRE (Jackson et al. 2025), which takes CMAC as an input, maps radial coordinate precipitation rates
to a plane intended to represent the surface. Where the lowest elevation is compromised (blocked, clutter),
data is projected from a higher tilt to the surface. Data is aggregated into daily files (lat, lon, time) for
easy use by the community.

During FY27 we will:
 • Operationalize SQUIRE at BNF using ARMFlow
 • Gather feedback on usage at BNF and look for improvements
 • Produce initial SQUIRE products for NSA XSAPR (non-operational) dependent on requirements.

5.7.2.3 Extracted Radar Columns and In Situ Sensors (RadCLss)

RadCLss is a product that helps users with a very common operation needed for retrieval development:
simply extracting a column of radar data over an in situ or zenith-pointing site, effectively creating a
time-height series.

During FY27 we will:
 • Operationalize RadCLss at BNF, NSA, SGP, and ENA for all ARM radars and Next-Generation
 Weather Radar (NEXRAD)
 • Work with mentors on coordinates for matched columns.

5.7.2.4 LLM-Assisted Radar Scenes (LARS)

LARS is a framework for labeling scanning radar scenes using multimodal large language models
(LLMs). Development will first assess the feasibility of labeling such scenes through validation of LLM
outputs against domain expert labels.

During FY27 we will:
 • Develop a codebook for classifying CSAPR2 radar scenes from Bankhead National Forest
