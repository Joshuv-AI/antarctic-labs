Mengya Yue, Miaomiao Ren, Zidong Gao

Abstract
. We establish two sufficient conditions for an additively idempotent semiring to be nonfinitely based. As applications, we prove that two specific 4 4 -element additively idempotent semirings, S ( 4,545 ) S_{(4,545)} and S ( 4,634 ) S_{(4,634)} , whose additive reducts are chains, have no finite basis for their identities. Furthermore, we show that the interval [ 𝖵 ⁡ ( S ( 4,545 ) ) , 𝖵 ⁡ ( S ( 4,634 ) ) ] [\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)}),\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})] in the lattice of semiring varieties contains 2 ℵ 0 2^{\aleph_{0}} distinct varieties. Consequently, the join of two finitely based additively idempotent semiring varieties is not necessarily finitely based. Moreover, we obtain the smallest example of a finitely based additively idempotent semiring S S whose extension S 0 S^{0} (obtained by adjoining a new element) is nonfinitely based.

Introduction

An
additively idempotent semiring
(or
ai-semiring
for short)
is an algebra
(S,+,\cdot)
equipped with two binary operations
+
and
\cdot
satisfying the following axioms:

•

The additive reduct
(S,+)
forms a commutative idempotent semigroup;

•

The multiplicative reduct
(S,\cdot)
forms a semigroup;

•

The distributive laws hold:

x(y+z)\approx xy+xz,~\text{and}~(x+y)z\approx xz+yz.

An ai-semiring whose multiplicative reduct is commutative is called a
commutative ai-semiring
.

The class of ai-semirings includes
well-known examples such as the Kleene semiring of regular languages
[
5
]
,
the max-plus algebra
[
1
]
, the power semiring of a semigroup
[
8
]
,
the endomorphism semiring of a semilattice
[
9
]
,
the semiring of all binary relations on a set
[
7
]
, and distributive lattices
[
3
]
.
These and other similar algebras have significant applications in various fields,
including algebraic geometry
[
4
]
, tropical algebraic geometry
[
19
]
,
theoretical computer science
[
15
]
, information science
[
14
]
, and the theory of weighted automata
[
10
]
.

Given an ai-semiring
S
, the binary relation
\leq_{S}
defined by

a\leq_{S}b\Leftrightarrow a+b=b,

is a partial order on
S
.
One readily checks that
\leq_{S}
is compatible with both addition and multiplication.
For this reason an ai-semiring is often called a
semilattice-ordered semigroup
.
Whenever an order on an ai-semiring is mentioned, it always refer to the order defined above.

A class of ai-semirings is a
variety
if it is closed under taking subalgebras,
homomorphic images, and arbitrary direct products.
By Birkhoff’s theorem, a class of ai-semirings is variety if and only if
it is an equational class, that is, the class of all ai-semirings satisfying a certain set of identities.
Let
\mathcal{V}
be an ai-semiring variety.
If
\Sigma
is a set of identities that defines
\mathcal{V}
,
then
\Sigma
is an
equational basis
of
\mathcal{V}
.
The variety
\mathcal{V}
is
finitely based
if it admits a finite equational basis;
otherwise, it is
nonfinitely based
.

For an ai-semiring
S
, let
\mathsf{V}(S)
denote the variety generated by
S
,
that is, the smallest variety containing
S
.
Then
S
and
\mathsf{V}(S)
satisfy exactly the same identities.
If
\Sigma
is an equational basis of
\mathsf{V}(S)
, we also say that
\Sigma
is is an equational basis of
S
.
The ai-semiring
S
is called finitely based or nonfinitely based according to whether
\mathsf{V}(S)
is finitely based or not.

The
finite basis problem
for a class of ai-semirings,
one of the central questions in the theory of varieties,
concerns the classification of its members with respect to the finite basis property.
While we focus here on ai-semirings, the finite basis problem for arbitrary algebras
has deep and often surprising connections with
formal languages
[
2
]
, the complexity of cellular automata
[
16
]
and
classical number-theoretic conjectures
[
22
]
.

The present paper concentrates on two algebras from this type:
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
,
whose Cayley tables are presented in Tables

and

While both algebras have the same additive reduct (a
4

We shall prove that
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
is a subvariety of
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})
.
Moreover, we demonstrate that
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
contains both
S_{7}
and
S_{53}
.

Even though
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
are merely
4

which might suggest they are amenable to existing methods,
all known sufficient conditions in the literature for deciding the finite basis property of a finite ai-semiring fail for these two algebras.

We first develop two sufficient conditions for an ai-semiring to be nonfinitely based,
and then apply them to solve the finite basis problem for
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
.
In doing so we provide an explicit infinite equational basis for each of them,
employing syntactic approach together with the compactness theorem of equational logic (see Lemma
2.3
).
The main result of this paper is both
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
are nonfinitely based,
which constitutes a contribution to Problem
1.1
.

Although substantial progress has been made on the finite basis question for ai-semirings,
the following fundamental problem remains open.

Problem 1.2
.

Is the join of two finitely based ai-semiring varieties again finitely based?

We settle Problem
1.2
negatively by showing that
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})

is a nonfinitely based variety that can be expressed as
the join of two finitely based varieties.

Let
S
be an ai-semiring.
The algebra
S^{0}=S\cup\{0\}
obtained from
S
by adjoining a new element
0
is again an ai-semiring under the operations

(\forall a\in S\cup\{0\})\quad a+0=0+a=a,\quad a0=0a=0,

with the original operations of
S
unchanged.
Thus
S
is a subsemiring of
S^{0}
, and the element

0
is simultaneously the additive least element and the multiplicative zero of
S^{0}
.
One can easily check that
S_{(4,634)}
is isomorphic to
S^{0}_{53}
,
and so

\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})=\mathsf{V}(S^{0}_{53}).

When
S
is the trivial ai-semiring,
S^{0}
is the
2
-element distributive lattice
D_{2}
.
By
[
34
, Proposition 1.4]
,
D_{2}
is contained in every ai-semiring variety of the form
\mathsf{V}(S^{0})
.
We shall show that the variety
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
is exactly the join of
\mathsf{V}(S_{53})
and
\mathsf{V}(D_{2})
.
Hence
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
is a subvariety of
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})
.

The construction
S^{0}
has played a central role in earlier studies of the finite basis problem for ai-semirings,
see, for example,
[

.
For an ai-semiring
S
,
[\mathsf{V}(S),\mathsf{V}(S^{0})]
denotes the

\mathsf{V}(S^{0})
and contains
\mathsf{V}(S)
.

[

proposed the following problem:

Problem 1.3
.

(1)

Is
S_{7}^{0}
finitely based or not finitely based
?

(2)

What is the cardinality of the interval
[\mathsf{V}(S_{7}),\mathsf{V}(S_{7}^{0})]

?

(3)

Under what conditions is the

S

[

proved that
S_{7}^{0}
is nonfinitely based.
Zhao and Wu
[

Let

They proved that if

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic notation, definitions, and tools that will be used throughout the paper.
Although every ai-semiring in the subsequent sections is commutative,
the results quoted below are stated for general ai-semirings (whose multiplication need not be commutative).
This formulation keeps them applicable in future work that may not impose commutativity.

Let
X
be a countably infinite set of variables,
let
X^{+}
denote the free semigroup over
X
,

X^{*}
the free monoid over
X
(with
\varepsilon
denoting the empty word),
and
X_{c}^{+}
the free commutative semigroup over
X
.

An
ai-semiring term
(or simply a
term
) over
X
is a finite nonempty set of words in
X^{+}
.
(In what follows, terms are denoted by bold lowercase letters
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\dots
,
while ordinary lowercase letters
x,y,z,\dots
stand for variables.)
We write a term as a formal sum of its elements; that is,

\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{n}
means
\mathbf{w}=\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{n}\}
.
The order of the summands in the formal sum is irrelevant,
and repeated occurrences of the same word are identified with a single occurrence.
Two terms are equal if and only if their underlying sets coincide.

The collection of all terms over
X
, denoted by
P_{f}(X^{+})
,
forms an ai-semiring under the usual term addition and multiplication.
By
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
,
P_{f}(X^{+})
is free in the variety of all ai-semirings over
X
.
The multiplication of
P_{f}(X^{+})
is cancellative: for any terms
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{r}
, if
\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{p}\mathbf{r}
, then
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{r}
;
if
\mathbf{q}\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{r}\mathbf{p}
, then
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{r}
.
An
ai-semiring substitution
(or simply a
substitution
) is a semiring homomorphism from
P_{f}(X^{+})
to itself.

Let
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
be terms.
We say that
\mathbf{u}
is a subterm of
\mathbf{v}
, and write
\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{v}
,
if there exist terms
\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{3}
such that

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}_{1}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{p}_{2}+\mathbf{p}_{3}.

Here
\mathbf{p}_{1}
or
\mathbf{p}_{2}
may be the empty word (acting as the multiplicative identity),
and
\mathbf{p}_{3}
may be the empty set (acting as the additive identity).
In particular, if
\mathbf{p}_{1}
and
\mathbf{p}_{2}
are both empty, then
\mathbf{u}
is called an
additive subterm
of
\mathbf{v}
;
if
\mathbf{p}_{3}
is empty, then
\mathbf{u}
is called a
multiplicative subterm
of
\mathbf{v}
.
If
\mathbf{v}
is a word and
\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{v}
,
then
\mathbf{u}
must be a multiplicative subterm of
\mathbf{v}
and is itself a word;
in this case we say that
\mathbf{u}
is
subword
of
\mathbf{v}
.
Moreover, the relation
\leq
is a partial order on
P_{f}(X^{+})

and is compatible with multiplication.

The term
\mathbf{v}
is called
\mathbf{u}
-free
if for every substitution
\varphi
,
the term
\varphi(\mathbf{u})
is not a subterm of
\mathbf{v}
.
The following statement follows directly from the definitions of subterm and freeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
be terms.
If
\mathbf{v}
is
\mathbf{u}
-free and
\mathbf{u}
is a subterm of
\mathbf{w}
, then
\mathbf{v}
is also
\mathbf{w}
-free.

An
ai-semiring identity
(or simply an
identity
) is a formal expression of the form

\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
, where
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are terms.
Let
S
be an ai-semiring and
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
an identity.
We say that
S

satisfies

\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
, or that
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}

holds
in
S
,
if
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=\varphi(\mathbf{v})
for every semiring homomorphism
\varphi\colon P_{f}(X^{+})\rightarrow S
.

The following result concerns the equational logic of ai-semirings and is from
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a set of identities and let
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
be a nontrivial identity.
Then
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
is derivable from
\Sigma
if and only if there
exist terms
\mathbf{t}_{1},\mathbf{t}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}\in P_{f}(X^{+})
such that
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{t}_{1}
,
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{t}_{n}
and,
for each
1\leq i<n
, there are terms
\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in P_{f}(X^{+})
and a substitution

\varphi_{i}\colon P_{f}(X^{+})\to P_{f}(X^{+})
such that

\mathbf{t}_{i}=\mathbf{p}_{i}\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{s}_{i})\mathbf{q}_{i}+\mathbf{r}_{i},\quad\mathbf{t}_{i+1}=\mathbf{p}_{i}\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{i})\mathbf{q}_{i}+\mathbf{r}_{i},

where
\mathbf{s}_{i}\approx\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in\Sigma
or
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{i}\approx\mathbf{s}_{i}\in\Sigma
,

\mathbf{p}_{i}
or
\mathbf{q}_{i}
may be the empty word
(
acting as the multiplicative identity
)
,
and
\mathbf{r}_{i}
may be the empty set
(
acting as the additive identity
)
.

The following lemma, which is the compactness theorem of equational logic (see
[
3
, Exercise II.14.10]
),
will be our main tool in Sections
3
and
4
for proving that both
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
have no finite equational basis.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an ai-semiring variety.
If
\mathcal{V}
is finitely based,
then every equational basis of
\mathcal{V}
contains a finite subset that also defines
\mathcal{V}
.
Equivalently, if
\mathcal{V}
has an infinite equational basis none of whose finite subsets defines
\mathcal{V}
,
then
\mathcal{V}
is nonfinitely based.

Following Volkov
[
33
]
,
we write
\mathbf{u}\preceq\mathbf{v}
(or equivalently
\mathbf{v}\succeq\mathbf{u}
) to denote the identity
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}

and call it an
ai-semiring inequality
(or simply an
inequality
).
The term
\mathbf{u}
is called the
lower side
and
\mathbf{v}
the
upper side
of the inequality.
It is straightforward to verify that an ai-semiring
S
satisfies an inequality
\mathbf{u}\preceq\mathbf{v}

if and only if
\varphi(\mathbf{u})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{v})
for every semiring homomorphism
\varphi\colon P_{f}(X^{+})\rightarrow S
.
Consequently,
S
satisfies an identity
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}

precisely when it satisfies both inequalities
\mathbf{u}\preceq\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{v}\preceq\mathbf{u}
.
For this reason, every set of identities can be equivalently expressed as a set of inequalities.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a set of identities.
Let
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
be an identity such that

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{k},\quad\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}_{\ell},

where
\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\in X^{+}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq\ell
.
One can readily check that the ai-semiring variety defined by
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}

coincides with the ai-semiring variety defined by the inequalities

\mathbf{u}_{i}\preceq\mathbf{v},\quad\mathbf{v}_{j}\preceq\mathbf{u}\quad(1\leq i\leq k,~1\leq j\leq\ell).

Consequently, to prove that
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
is derivable from
\Sigma
,
it suffices to show that for every
i
and
j
,
the inequalities

\mathbf{u}_{i}\preceq\mathbf{v}
,
\mathbf{v}_{j}\preceq\mathbf{u}

can be derived from
\Sigma
.
For this reason, throughout this paper we restrict our attention to the inequalities of the form
\mathbf{q}\preceq\mathbf{u}
,
where
\mathbf{q}
is a word and
\mathbf{u}
is a term.
This technique will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

Next, we introduce some notation.
Let
\mathbf{w}
be a nonempty word, and let
x
be a variable. Then

•

c(\mathbf{w})
denotes the
content
of
\mathbf{w}

3.

In this section, we first establish a sufficient condition for an ai-semiring to be nonfinitely based.
As an application, we prove that
S_{(4,545)}
has no finite equational basis.
Throughout the remainder of the paper
every term is built from words in the free commutative semigroup
X^{+}_{c}
;
that is, words are considered up to permutation of variables.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, and let
0\leq k\leq n
. Define

\mathbf{u}_{n,k}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}x_{n+1}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}x_{n+1}y_{i}\right).

In particular, if

\mathbf{u}_{n,0}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{n+1}y_{i}\right);

For convenience, we sometimes factor

\mathbf{u}_{n,k}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}\right)x_{n+1}.

The following lemma collects several basic properties of the terms

which will be used repeatedly in the subsequent proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be an integer, and let

.

(a)

Every word in
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
is linear.

(b)

The content intersection of any two distinct words in
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
is a singleton.

(c)

No distinct words in
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
share a common subword of length
2
.

(d)

\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
has no subterm that is the square of a term.

(e)

If

and
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
are words in

with

\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{q}
.

(f)

x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
is the unique additive subterm of
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}

whose content is contained in
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.

(g)

If
n\geq 4
, then
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
is the unique word in
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
of length at least
4
.

(h)

If
n\geq 3
, then
the term
\mathbf{u}_{n,n}
contains exactly one word,
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
, whose length is at least
3
.

(i)

If

, then

\mathbf{u}_{n,n}
does not have a subterm of the form
\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}+\mathbf{t}_{2}\mathbf{t}_{3}+\mathbf{t}_{3}\mathbf{t}_{1}
.

(j)

\mathbf{u}_{n,0}
does not have a subterm of the form
\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}+\mathbf{t}_{2}\mathbf{t}_{3}+\mathbf{t}_{3}\mathbf{t}_{1}
.

Proof.

All statements follow directly from the explicit form of
\mathbf{u}_{n,k}
;
their verification is straightforward and is omitted.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{q}\preceq\mathbf{u}
be a nontrivial inequality such that

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{n}
and
\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{q}\in X^{+}_{c}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Then
\mathbf{q}\preceq\mathbf{u}
holds in
S_{53}

if and only if
L_{\geq 2}(\mathbf{u})\neq\emptyset
,
c(\mathbf{q})\subseteq c(\mathbf{u})
,
and for every
\mathbf{w}\in S_{2}(\mathbf{q})
there exists
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in S_{2}(\mathbf{u})
such that
c(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\subseteq c(\mathbf{w})
.

Proof.

The necessity follows from Yue et al.
[
35
, Lemma 3.7]
.
For sufficiency,
let
\varphi\colon P_{f}(X)\to S_{53}
be an arbitrary homomorphism.

If
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=\infty
, then
\varphi(\mathbf{q})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{u})
,
since
\infty
is the additive top element.

If
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=1
, then
\varphi(x)=1
for all
x\in c(\mathbf{u})
,
since
1
is the additive least element and its only multiplicative factor is itself.
As
c(\mathbf{q})\subseteq c(\mathbf{u})
, the same holds for all variables in
c(\mathbf{q})
;
hence
\varphi(\mathbf{q})=1
and so
\varphi(\mathbf{q})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{u})
.

If
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=a
, then
\varphi(x)\in\{1,a\}
for all
x\in c(\mathbf{u})
.
Indeed, if there is
x\in c(\mathbf{u})
such that
\varphi(x)=\infty
,
then because
\infty
is the multiplicative zero and the additive top element,
we would obtain
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=\infty
, contradicting the assumption
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=a
.
Since
c(\mathbf{q})\subseteq c(\mathbf{u})
, the same holds for all variables in
c(\mathbf{q})
.
Suppose for contradiction that
\varphi(\mathbf{q})=\infty
.
Then there exists
xy\in S_{2}(\mathbf{q})
such that
\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)=a
.
(Here the reasoning is that in
S_{53}
, the product of finitely many elements,
each equal to
a
or
1
, yields
\infty
precisely when at least two factors equal to
a
.)
By hypothesis, there exists
\mathbf{p}\in S_{2}(\mathbf{u})
such that
c(\mathbf{p})\subseteq\{x,y\}
,
and so
\varphi(\mathbf{p})=aa=\infty
.
This implies that
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=\infty
, a contradiction.
Thus
\varphi(\mathbf{q})\in\{1,a\}
and so
\varphi(\mathbf{q})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{u})
.

Therefore,
\mathbf{q}\preceq\mathbf{u}
holds in
S_{53}
.
∎

, and so

[\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)}),\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})]
contains
2^{\aleph_{0}}
distinct varieties.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

There are
2^{\aleph_{0}}
distinct nonfinitely based varieties in the interval
[\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)}),\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})]
.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
5.3
,
it is enough to show that for any infinite subset
M
of
\mathbb{N}
,
the subvariety
\mathcal{V}_{M}
of
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})
defined by the identities
\sigma_{m,0}
with
m\in M

is nonfinitely based.
Indeed, by Proposition
4.4
,

\Sigma=\{\eqref{54501},\eqref{54503},\eqref{63404},\eqref{63405},\eqref{63406},\eqref{63407}\}\cup\{\sigma_{m,0}\mid m\in M\}

is an equational basis for the commutative ai-semiring variety
\mathcal{V}_{M}
,
which contains an infinite subset of
\Omega_{0}
.
According to Theorem
3.3
, it suffices to prove that

\mathbf{u}_{m,0}
is
\mathbf{t}
-free for every inequality
\mathbf{s}\preceq\mathbf{t}

in
\Sigma\setminus\Omega_{0}
and for every
m\geq 4
.
Observe that

\Sigma\setminus\Omega_{0}=\{\eqref{54501},\eqref{54503},\eqref{63404},\eqref{63405},\eqref{63406},\eqref{63407}\}.

The required freeness follows from Lemma
3.1
(d) for
\mathbf{t}\in\Gamma
, where

\Gamma=\{\mathbf{w}\mid\mathbf{w}~\text{is an upper side of an inequality in}~\{\eqref{54501},\eqref{54503},\eqref{63404},\eqref{63406},\eqref{63407}\}\}

and from Proposition
5.2
for
\mathbf{t}\in\{\mathbf{v}\mid\mathbf{v}\in\Theta_{n},n\geq 2\}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

The interval
[\mathsf{V}(S_{53}),\mathsf{V}(S_{53}^{0})]
contains
2^{\aleph_{0}}
distinct varieties.

Proof.

Since
S_{53}^{0}
is isomorphic to
S_{(4,634)}
, and
S_{53}
belongs to
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
,
it follows that
the interval
[\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)}),\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})]

is contained in
[\mathsf{V}(S_{53}),\mathsf{V}(S_{53}^{0})]
.
Theorem
5.3
shows that the former already contains
2^{\aleph_{0}}
varieties,
hence the latter must also contain
2^{\aleph_{0}}
distinct varieties.
∎

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have provided two new sufficient conditions for an ai-semiring to be nonfinitely based,
and applied them to show that the
4
-element ai-semirings
S_{(4,545)}
and
S_{(4,634)}
have no finite equational basis.
As a consequence, the variety
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
is the first known example of a nonfinitely based ai-semiring variety
that can be written as the join of two finitely based varieties.

Both
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
and
\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,634)})
contain the
3
-element ai-semiring
S_{7}
;
hence the solution of their finite basis problem provides direct support
for the affirmative side of the question raised by Gao et al.
[
12
]
,
which asked whether every finite ai-semiring whose variety contains
S_{7}
is nonfinitely based.

Given an ai-semiring
S
whose additive top element
\infty
is also a multiplicative zero,
one can adjoin a new element
e
and define

e+a=a+e=e+e=ee=e,\quad ea=ae=\infty\quad(a\in S\setminus\{e\}).

The resulting algebra
(S\cup\{e\},+,\cdot)
, called the
idempotent extension
of
S
and denoted by
S^{e}
,
is isomorphic to a subdirect product of
S
and
D_{2}
;
hence
\mathsf{V}(S^{e})=\mathsf{V}(S,D_{2})
.
In particular,
\mathsf{V}(S_{53}^{e})=\mathsf{V}(S_{53},D_{2})
.
By Proposition
3.4
, this variety coincides with

\mathsf{V}(S_{(4,545)})
. Therefore,
S_{53}^{e}
is also nonfinitely based,
showing that the idempotent extension construction does not preserve the finite basis property.

On the other hand,
S_{(4,634)}
is isomorphic to
S_{53}^{0}
; thus
S_{53}^{0}
is nonfinitely based as well.
This yields the smallest example of a finitely based ai-semiring
S

whose extension
S^{0}
is nonfinitely based.

Combined with earlier results obtained by members of our group,
the present paper advances the finite basis problem for all
4
-element ai-semirings
whose additive reducts are chains within reach.
A complete solution of the finite basis problem for this subclass will be presented in a forthcoming work
[
26
]
.

Finally, we expect that the syntactic methods developed here, as well as the explicit sufficient conditions obtained,
will find further applications in the study of finite basis questions for other classes of ai-semirings.
