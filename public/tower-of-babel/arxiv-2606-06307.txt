Abstract
This paper develops a stochastic-geometry framework for high-altitude platform station (HAPs) networks in which platforms execute cyclic patrol trajectories anchored to designated service regions. We introduce two small-circle ring Cox process models on the spherical Earth. In the small-circle ring Poisson Cox process (SCR-PCP), platforms form one-dimensional Poisson point processes on localized patrol rings, whereas in the small-circle ring binomial Cox process (SCR-BCP), each ring contains a fixed number of uniformly distributed platforms. We establish the isotropy of both models and derive spatial statistics, including the distributions of the nearest-anchor, nearest-ring, and nearest-HAPs distances, together with the joint serving-distance and serving-ring-angle distribution required for SCR-BCP analysis. Building on these results, we derive coverage probability expressions under nearest-HAPs association by decomposing aggregate interference into same-ring and other-ring components and characterizing their conditional Laplace transforms. To account for the flight dynamics of patrol-based HAPs, we integrate a steady‑circular‑flight propulsion model with the communication analysis and introduce a coverage energy efficiency (CEE) metric. This yields an analytical condition for the energy-optimal patrol radius that balances coverage performance against the propulsion cost of circular flight. Numerical results reveal fundamental differences between intensity-driven (SCR-PCP) and finite-fleet (SCR-BCP) deployments and demonstrate that patrol geometry, platform density, and cruising velocity should be jointly optimized to achieve energy-efficient HAPs operation.

(anchors per km

IV-D

Discussion:
SCR-PCP
versus
SCR-BCP

Theorems
5
and
6
highlight the fundamental distinction between the two spatial models. Both
SCR-PCP
and
SCR-BCP
share the same parent anchor
PPP
and patrol-ring geometry; the difference arises solely through the intra-ring point process. In
SCR-PCP
, each patrol ring carries a one-dimensional
PPP
with intensity
\nu
, so the number of
HAP
on a ring is Poisson distributed with mean
2\pi a\nu
. Consequently, the coverage probability admits a ring-wise decomposition in terms of independent Laplace functionals. In
SCR-BCP
, each ring contains exactly
n_{\rm H}
platforms, which introduces finite-population dependencies after conditioning on the serving event. This dependence is reflected in the appearance of the joint density
f_{d_{0,{\rm B}},\phi_{\rm s}}(x,\phi)
and the finite-power terms in Theorem
6
. From a point-process perspective,
SCR-PCP
may be viewed as a Poisson mixture of ring configurations, whereas
SCR-BCP
fixes the per-ring population to a deterministic value. Thus,
SCR-BCP
is not a limiting case of
SCR-PCP
, but rather a distinct deployment regime in which the number of platforms per patrol ring is treated as a planning parameter.

VII
Conclusion

This paper develops an
SG
framework for patrol-based
HAP
networks on the spherical Earth. To capture the anchor-driven and cyclic nature of realistic
HAP
deployments, we introduced two small-circle ring Cox process models: the
SCR-PCP
, which represents intensity-driven deployments through a Poisson intra-ring structure, and the
SCR-BCP
, which captures dimensioned finite-fleet deployments through a fixed per-ring population. Building on these models, we developed spatial statistics, derived expressions for coverage probability under nearest-
HAP
association, and incorporated fixed-wing flight mechanics to formulate an energy-aware performance framework.

The analysis reveals that patrol geometry plays a fundamentally different role under the two deployment regimes. In
SCR-PCP
, the patrol radius directly controls both serving-link accessibility and interference generation, leading to non-trivial coverage and
CEE
optima. In contrast,
SCR-BCP
is primarily governed by the finite per-ring population, making network performance comparatively insensitive to the patrol radius once the fleet size is fixed. By coupling communication performance with steady-circular-flight propulsion costs, we further showed that the patrol radius should be jointly designed with platform density, fleet dimensioning, and cruising velocity to achieve energy-efficient operation.

A promising future direction is the integration of patrol-based
HAP
models into multi-layer terrestrial–aerial–satellite architectures, enabling joint optimization of mobility, coverage, and spectrum coexistence in future SAGIN deployments.

\Phi_{u}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Phi<x)=\mathbb{P}(V>\cos x)=\frac{1-\cos x}{2},\quad 0\leq x<\pi.

PPP
on

\displaystyle Q(\mathcal{C}(u,\gamma))

\displaystyle Q(\mathcal{L})=\big\{\mathcal{C}(Q(u),\gamma):u\in\Phi_{u}\big\},

\rho(\phi)=R_{\oplus}\sin\phi

\displaystyle d(\phi,\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle=d_{\rm plane}(\phi)^{2}+y(\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{\rm r}(\phi)^{2}+2a\rho(\phi)-2a\rho(\phi)\cos\alpha

\displaystyle=D_{\rm r}(\phi)^{2}+2aR_{\oplus}\sin\phi\,(1-\cos\alpha).

\displaystyle 1-\cos\alpha\leq\frac{d^{2}-D_{\rm r}(\phi)^{2}}{2aR_{\oplus}\sin\phi}=\Psi(\phi,d).

\alpha_{\max}(\phi,d)=\arccos\left(1-\Psi(\phi,d)\right),
so
L(\phi,d)=2a\,\alpha_{\max}(\phi,d)
. If
\Psi(\phi,d)\geq 2
, the ball fully engulfs the ring and
L(\phi,d)=2\pi a
.

Conditioned on the anchor at
u
, the
HAP
on
\mathcal{C}(u,\gamma)
form a one-dimensional
PPP
of intensity
\nu
along the ring. Thus, the conditional void probability for this ring is
p_{{\rm void},{\rm P}}(\phi,d)=\exp\left(-\nu L(\phi,d)\right).
Since
\Phi_{\rm H}^{\rm P}
is a Cox process driven by the anchor
PPP
, the event
\{d_{0,{\rm P}}>d\}
is the event that every ring has no
HAP
within distance
d
, which conditioned on
\Phi_{u}
has probability
\prod_{u_{i}\in\Phi_{u}}p_{{\rm void},{\rm P}}(\phi_{i},d)
. Deconditioning over
\Phi_{u}
with the PGFL of the homogeneous
PPP
on
\mathcal{S}_{R_{\rm H}}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm P}}>d)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Phi_{u}}\Big[\prod_{u_{i}\in\Phi_{u}}p_{{\rm void},{\rm P}}(\phi_{i},d)\Big]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\lambda_{\rm u}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{R_{\rm H}}}\big(1-p_{{\rm void},{\rm P}}(\phi,d)\big)\,{\rm d}S\right).

Using
{\rm d}S=R_{\rm H}^{2}\sin\phi\,{\rm d}\phi\,{\rm d}\theta
and integrating over
\theta
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm P}}>d)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-2\pi R_{\rm H}^{2}\lambda_{\rm u}\int_{0}^{\pi}

\displaystyle\left[1-\exp(-\nu L(\phi,d))\right]\sin\phi\,{\rm d}\phi\bigg).

Finally, by Lemma
3
,
L(\phi,d)>0
only for
\phi\in[\phi_{1}(d),\phi_{2}(d)]
, so the integral can be restricted to this belt. Taking
1-\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm P}}>d)
gives the stated
CDF
.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
4

Proof:

From the geometric analysis in Theorem
2
, the 3D visible arc length of a patrol ring anchored at a generic central angle
\varphi
inside a sphere of radius
x
is
L(\varphi,x)
. As established in Theorem
3
, the probability that
no

HAP
lies within distance
x
from this specific ring is

\displaystyle p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi,x)=\left(1-\frac{L(\varphi,x)}{2\pi a}\right)^{n_{\rm H}}.

(21)

The global void probability
\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm B}}>x)
is already fully characterized by evaluating (
8
) at
d=x
.

To obtain the joint CDF of
(d_{0,{\rm B}},\phi_{\rm s})
, we additionally track the angle of the serving anchor. By isotropy, the distribution of anchors is uniform over the sphere, and the serving anchor should be one of the anchors whose ring contributes at least one
HAP
within distance
x
. The event
\{\phi_{\rm s}\leq\phi\}
therefore restricts the serving anchor to the spherical cap defined by
[0,\phi]
in polar angle, while the void condition for distances exceeding
x
still applies globally.

A convenient way to express the joint CDF is through its complementary spatial events:

\displaystyle\{d_{0,{\rm B}}\leq x,\;\phi_{\rm s}\leq\phi\}=\{d_{0,{\rm B}}\leq x\}\setminus\{d_{0,{\rm B}}\leq x,\;\phi_{\rm s}>\phi\}.

(22)

The probability of the first term is simply
1-\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm B}}>x)
. The second term corresponds to the event that the nearest
HAP
lies within distance
x
, but originates from a ring whose anchor angle strictly exceeds
\phi
. Equivalently, there must be no
HAP
within distance
x
on rings with
\varphi\leq\phi
, and at least one
HAP
within distance
x
on rings with
\varphi>\phi
.

Conditioned on the anchor
PPP

\Phi_{u}
, the probability that no
HAP
within distance
x
originates from rings in the region
\varphi\leq\phi
is
\prod_{u_{i}\in\Phi_{u}:\,\varphi_{i}\leq\phi}p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi_{i},x)
. Conversely, the probability that at least one
HAP
within distance
x
originates from rings in the region
\varphi>\phi
is
1-\prod_{u_{i}\in\Phi_{u}:\,\varphi_{i}>\phi}p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi_{i},x).
Because a
PPP
is completely independent over disjoint spatial regions, the conditional probability of the joint event is the product of these two factors. Deconditioning with the PGFL over the disjoint spherical zones
[0,\phi]
and
(\phi,\pi]
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d_{0,{\rm B}}\leq x,\;\phi_{\rm s}>\phi)=

\displaystyle\exp\!\left(-2\pi\lambda_{\rm u}R_{\rm H}^{2}\int_{0}^{\phi}\big(1-p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi,x)\big)\sin\varphi\,{\rm d}\varphi\right)\times

\displaystyle\bigg[1-\exp\!\bigg(-2\pi\lambda_{\rm u}R_{\rm H}^{2}\int_{\phi}^{\pi}\big(1-p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi,x)\big)\sin\varphi\,{\rm d}\varphi\bigg)\bigg],

(23)

Finally, combining (
22
), (
8
), and (
23
) yields our result. Note that the sum of the integrals over
[0,\phi]
and
(\phi,\pi]
in (
23
) recovers the full integral in (
8
), so the joint CDF is fully consistent with the marginal nearest-distance distribution in Theorem
3
.
∎

Appendix E
Proof of Theorem
5

Proof:

We first condition on the serving distance
d_{0,{\rm P}}=x
and then decondition using its PDF. By the law of total probability,

\displaystyle P_{\rm c,P}(\tau)=\mathbb{E}_{d_{0,{\rm P}}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{G_{t}h_{0}x^{-\eta}}{I_{\rm s,P}}>\tau\,\Big|\,d_{0,{\rm P}}=x\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\int_{D_{\min}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(h_{0}>\frac{\tau x^{\eta}}{G_{t}}I_{\rm s,P}\,\Big|\,d_{0,{\rm P}}=x\right)f_{d_{0,{\rm P}}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

(24)

Since
h_{0}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
and is independent of
I_{\rm s,P}
, the inner probability can be expressed via the Laplace transform of
I_{\rm s,P}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(h_{0}>sI_{\rm s,P}\,\Big|\,d_{0,{\rm P}}=x\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\mathbb{E}_{I_{\rm s,P}}\left[\mathbb{P}(h_{0}>sI_{\rm s,P}\mid I_{\rm s,P})\,\Big|\,d_{0,{\rm P}}=x\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\mathbb{E}_{I_{\rm s,P}}\left[e^{-sI_{\rm s,P}}\,\Big|\,d_{0,{\rm P}}=x\right]=\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,P}}(s\mid x),

(25)

where
s=\tau x^{\eta}/G_{t}
. Substituting this identity into the expression for
P_{\rm c,P}(\tau)
yields

\displaystyle P_{\rm c,P}(\tau)=\int_{D_{\min}}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,P}}\!\left(\frac{\tau x^{\eta}}{G_{t}}\,\Big|\,x\right)f_{d_{0,{\rm P}}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

(26)

1) Decomposition of the interference.

By Slivnyak’s theorem for Cox processes, conditioning on the existence of a serving
HAP
at distance
x
does not alter the distribution of the remaining points. The aggregate interference can be decomposed into
I_{\rm s,P}=I_{\rm s,P}+I_{\rm o,P}
. Conditioned on the serving ring geometry, in particular the central angle
\phi_{\rm s}
, these two components are independent, so
\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,P}}(s\mid x)=\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,P}}(s\mid x,\phi_{\rm s})\,\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm o,P}}(s\mid x)
.

2) Exclusion region on a given ring.

Under nearest-neighbor association, no interfering
HAP
can be closer than the serving distance
x
. For a ring at central angle
\phi
, the distance from the user to an interferer at local angle
\alpha
is
d(\phi,\alpha)
given by (
9
). The exclusion condition
d(\phi,\alpha)<x
is equivalent to

\displaystyle 1-\cos\alpha<\Psi(\phi,x)=\frac{x^{2}-D_{\rm r}(\phi)^{2}}{2aR_{\oplus}\sin\phi},

so interferers must satisfy the complementary condition
1-\cos\alpha\geq\Psi(\phi,x)
. This leads to the boundary
\alpha_{0}(x,\phi)
as stated.

3) Same-ring interference.

On the serving ring at angle
\phi_{\rm s}
, the interfering
HAP
form a one-dimensional
PPP
of intensity
\nu
along the circle of radius
a
. Conditioned on
x
and
\phi_{\rm s}
, the interference is
I_{\rm s,P}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{u_{\rm s}}:\,\alpha\notin\text{exclusion}}G_{I}h_{\alpha}\,d(\phi_{\rm s},\alpha)^{-\eta}
. For a single interferer at distance
y
,
\mathbb{E}_{h}[e^{-sG_{I}hy^{-\eta}}]=1/(1+sG_{I}y^{-\eta})
. Applying the PGFL of a 1D
PPP
with intensity
\nu
per unit arc length and using bilateral symmetry yields (
11
).

4) Interference from other rings.

For a non-serving ring at angle
\phi
, the same construction gives the single-ring Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm r}}(s\mid x,\phi)
. The total interference from all non-serving rings is
I_{\rm o,P}=\sum_{u_{i}\in\Phi_{u}\setminus\{u_{0}\}}I_{\rm r}(u_{i})
, with independent ring-wise contributions conditioned on
\Phi_{u}
. De-conditioning over
\Phi_{u}
and using the PGFL of the homogeneous
PPP
on
\mathcal{S}_{R_{\rm H}}
, together with
{\rm d}S=R_{\rm H}^{2}\sin\phi\,{\rm d}\phi\,{\rm d}\theta
and isotropy, leads to (
12
).

Substituting
\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm o}}
into (
26
) with
s=\tau x^{\eta}/G_{t}
completes the proof.
∎

Appendix F
Proof of Theorem
6

Proof:

We first condition on the serving geometry
(d_{0,{\rm B}},\phi_{\rm s})=(x,\phi)
and then average over its joint PDF. Under this conditioning, the downlink
SIR
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{SIR}_{\rm B}=\frac{G_{t}h_{0}x^{-\eta}}{I_{\rm s,B}+I_{\rm o,B}},

where
h_{0}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
is the fading gain of the serving link,
I_{\rm s,B}
is the interference from the remaining
n_{\rm H}-1
platforms on the serving ring, and
I_{\rm o,B}
is the interference from all other rings. Using the memoryless property of exponential fading, the conditional success probability is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{SIR}_{\rm B}>\tau\mid d_{0,{\rm B}}=x,\phi_{\rm s}=\phi)=

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\!\left(-\frac{\tau x^{\eta}}{G_{t}}(I_{\rm s,B}+I_{\rm o,B})\right)\,\middle|\,x,\phi\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,B}}\!\left(\tfrac{\tau x^{\eta}}{G_{t}}\,\middle|\,x,\phi\right)\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm o,B}}\!\left(\tfrac{\tau x^{\eta}}{G_{t}}\,\middle|\,x\right),

(27)

where conditional independence of
I_{\rm s,B}
and
I_{\rm o,B}
given
(x,\phi)
has been used. Averaging (
27
) with respect to
f_{d_{0,{\rm B}},\phi_{\rm s}}(x,\phi)
yields (
14
). It remains to derive the Laplace transforms.

1) Same-ring Laplace transform.

Conditioned on the global serving distance
d_{0,{\rm B}}=x
, every non-serving ring at angle
\varphi
must have its nearest
HAP
strictly farther than
x
, i.e.,
R_{\min}(\varphi)>x
, where
R_{\min}(\varphi)=\min_{1\leq k\leq n_{\rm H}}l(k,\varphi),
and
l(k,\varphi)
is the distance from the user to the
k
-th
HAP
on that ring. Under
SCR-BCP
, the
n_{\rm H}
angular positions
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n_{\rm H}}
on that ring are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}[0,2\pi)
unconditionally. The per-ring void probability at threshold
x
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(R_{\min}(\phi)>x\mid\phi\big)=\left(1-\frac{L(\phi,x)}{2\pi a}\right)^{n_{\rm H}},

(28)

where
L(\phi,x)
is the visible arc length from Theorem
3
. Equivalently, by the exclusion boundary geometry in the
SCR-PCP
coverage analysis, and given
R_{\min}(\varphi)>x
, all points must lie in the safe arc
\{\alpha:\,d(\varphi,\alpha)>x\}=[\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi),\,2\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)],
so the conditional angular density is

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\mid D>x}(\alpha\mid\varphi,x)=\frac{{\bf 1}\{d(\varphi,\alpha)>x\}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\bf 1}\{d(\varphi,\beta)>x\}\,{\rm d}\beta}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt=\frac{1}{2\big(\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)\big)}\,{\bf 1}\{\alpha\in[\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi),\,2\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)]\},

(29)

and the
n_{\rm H}
angles remain i.i.d. under this conditioning.

For a non-serving ring at angle
\varphi
, define its aggregate interference as
I_{\rm ring}(\varphi)=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm H}}G_{I}h_{k}d(\varphi,\alpha_{k})^{-\alpha},
where the angles
\alpha_{k}
follow the truncated density (
29
). The conditional Laplace transform of the interference generated by a
single
platform on this ring is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1+sG_{I}d(\varphi,\alpha)^{-\eta}}\,\Big|\,d(\varphi,\alpha)>x\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\big(\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)\big)}\int_{\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}^{2\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}\frac{1}{1+sG_{I}d(\varphi,\alpha)^{-\eta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\frac{1}{\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}\int_{\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}^{\pi}\frac{1}{1+sG_{I}d(\varphi,\alpha)^{-\eta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha.

Step (a) follows directly from the symmetry relation in (
13
), which states that any integral of a function of
d(\varphi,\alpha)
over the safe arc
[\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi),\,2\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)]
can be written as twice the integral over
[\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi),\,\pi]
. We therefore define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(s\mid x,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi-\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}\int_{\alpha_{0}(x,\varphi)}^{\pi}\frac{1}{1+sG_{I}d(\varphi,\alpha)^{-\eta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha,

(30)

which is the conditional Laplace transform of the interference contribution of a single truncated platform on a ring at angle
\varphi
.

On the serving ring at angle
\phi
, one platform is pinned at distance
x
as the serving
HAP
, and the remaining
n_{\rm H}-1
platforms are distributed independently on the same safe arc with density (
29
). By Rayleigh fading and conditional independence, the Laplace transform of the same-ring interference is
\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm s,B}}(s\mid x,\phi)=\big(\mathcal{Q}(s\mid x,\phi)\big)^{n_{\rm H}-1},
which gives (
15
).

2) Aggregate other-ring Laplace transform.

For any non-serving ring at angle
\varphi
, conditioned on
R_{\min}(\varphi)>x
, all
n_{\rm H}
platforms follow the truncated law in (
29
), so the per-ring Laplace transform of its interference is
\big(\mathcal{Q}(s\mid x,\varphi)\big)^{n_{\rm H}}
. Such a ring appears in the post-conditioning configuration only if it satisfies the void event
R_{\min}(\varphi)>x
, which occurs with probability
p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi,x)=\big(1-L(\varphi,x)/(2\pi a)\big)^{n_{\rm H}}
as in (
28
). Under Palm conditioning with respect to the serving
HAP
, the non-serving anchors form a homogeneous
PPP
on
\mathcal{S}_{R_{\rm H}}
with intensity
\lambda_{\rm u}
.

The interference from all other rings is
I_{\rm o,B}=\sum_{u\in\Phi_{u}\setminus\{u_{\rm s}\}}I_{\rm ring}(\phi_{u}),
and its conditional Laplace transform given
d_{0,{\rm B}}=x
is obtained via the PGFL of a
PPP
of independent marked rings:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{I_{\rm o,B}}(s\mid x)=

\displaystyle\exp\!\left(-\lambda_{\rm u}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{R_{\rm H}}}\big(1-\big(\mathcal{Q}(s\mid x,\varphi)\big)^{n_{\rm H}}\big)p_{{\rm void},{\rm B}}(\varphi,x)\,{\rm d}S\right).

Substituting
{\rm d}S=R_{\rm H}^{2}\sin\varphi\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\theta
and integrating over
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
yields (
16
). Finally, substituting (
15
) and (
16
) into (
27
), and then averaging over
f_{d_{0,{\rm B}},\phi_{\rm s}}(x,\phi)
, completes the proof.
∎

Appendix G
Proof of Lemma
5

Proof:

By definition,
\eta_{{\rm CEE},k}(\gamma)=\frac{P_{{\rm c},k}(\tau;\gamma)}{P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma)}
. On the interior
(0,\phi_{\max})
, any stationary point
\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star}

\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\ln\eta_{{\rm CEE},k}(\gamma)\Big|_{\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star}}=0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{P_{{\rm c},k}(\tau;\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star})}\left.\frac{\partial P_{{\rm c},k}(\tau;\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}\right|_{\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star}}\!\!\!\!\!=\!\frac{1}{P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star})}\left.\frac{\partial P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}\right|_{\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{EE}}^{\star}}.

4
, we can write

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma)=P_{\mathrm{SHF}}\big(1+A\tan^{-2}\gamma\big),\quad A=\frac{V^{4}}{g^{2}R_{\rm H}^{2}}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma)}\frac{\partial P_{\mathrm{SCF}}(\gamma)}{\partial\gamma}

) yields (

), which completes the proof.
