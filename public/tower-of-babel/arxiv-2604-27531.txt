𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings and 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -quadratic forms on surfaces
Nariya Kawazumi

Abstract
. We introduce the notions of 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings, based 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings and relative 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings of a compact connected oriented surface Σ \Sigma for any commutative ring 𝕂 \mathbb{K} with unit, and a map which maps a based loop on Σ \Sigma to a homology class of its unit tangent bundle U ​ Σ U\Sigma , which recovers Johnson’s lifting in the case 𝕂 = ℤ / 2 \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}/2 . This generalizes the correspondence between a quadratic form and a spin structure established by Johnson to any commutative ring 𝕂 \mathbb{K} with unit. If the genus of Σ \Sigma is positive, we have a bijection between the set of 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings and the set of some twisted cocycles of the mapping class group of the surface Σ \Sigma . Through this bijection, in the case where the boundary ∂ Σ \partial\Sigma is non-empty and connected, we discuss some relation between 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -framings and the extended first Johnson homomorphism.

Introduction

A spin structure on an oriented surface
\Sigma
has played essential roles in various classical contexts, and is important even now, which is illustrated also by the fact that the underlying structure of a super Riemann surface is a spin Riemann surface. In some classical context, it is given as a theta characteristic, which Johnson
[
12
]
called a
quadratic form
on the surface
\Sigma
, while it is also a fiberwise double covering of the unit tangent bundle
U\Sigma
. It was Johnson
[
12
]
to establish a clear correspondence between these two notions by introducing a lifting
H_{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to H_{1}(U\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. But the lifting is defined by taking a simple closed curve as a representative of each homology class. Hence it is a little bit complicated.
In this paper, we construct a map
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to H_{1}(U\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which recovers Johnson’s lifting, by taking a generic immersed loop as a representative of each element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
. This should clarify a geometric meaning of Johnson’s lifting.

For a while, we confine ourselves to the surface
\Sigma_{g,1}
of genus
g
with one boundary component. Let
\Gamma_{g,1}
be the mapping class group of
\Sigma_{g,1}
fixing the boundary
pointwise
.
For the surface
\Sigma_{g,1}
, the Poincaré duality gives a
\Gamma_{g,1}
-equivariant isomorphism
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}})
. Morita
[
25
]
computed

H^{1}(\Gamma_{g,1};H^{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}}))={\mathbb{Z}}
and named its generator
k\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{g,1};H^{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}}))
, where he did not decide the sign of
k
. Prior to this computation, Earle
[
6
]
introduced a cocycle representing
k
in the context of the Abel-Jacobi map, so that
we call the class
k

the Earle class
.
Chillingworth
[
4
,
5
]
already constructed the class
k
on the Torelli subgroup of
\Gamma_{g,1}
using nowhere vanishing vector fields on
\Sigma_{g,1}
.
On the whole mapping class group
\Gamma_{g,1}
,
Furuta
[
27
, p.569]
remarked that the class
k
is the extension class of the extension of
\Gamma_{g,1}
-modules

0\to H^{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(U\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0\quad(\text{exact}).

In other words, the class
k
is the obstruction to take a
\Gamma_{g,1}
-invariant splitting of the extension. Trapp
[
32
]
also gave a similar observation. As was observed by Morita
[
26
]
,
the first Johnson homomorphism
[
13
]
on the Torelli subgroup is extended to a cocycle on the whole mapping class group with coefficients in
\Lambda^{3}H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})
, and its reduction by the map
\Lambda^{3}H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};{\mathbb{Q}})
using the algebraic intersection number equals the class
k
up to sign.
One can construct explicit cocycles representing the extended first Johnson homomorphism by using symplectic expansions
[
24
]

of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})
.
Thus we like to find a framing corresponding to a given symplectic expansion.
But the framing is not a usual framing of the bundle
U\Sigma_{g,1}
, but a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-framing in our sense. If
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{Z}}/2
, a
{\mathbb{K}}
-framing is exactly a spin structure. Thus we consider
{\mathbb{K}}
-framings for any commutative ring
{\mathbb{K}}
with unit.
In this paper we compute explicitly the
{\mathbb{K}}
-framing corresponding to any
{\mathbb{K}}
-expansion of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})
satisfying a weaker condition than symplectic expansions (Lemma
5.1
, Corollary
5.5
).

Any symplectic expansion provides the formality of the Goldman bracket
[
7
]
on the surface
\Sigma_{g,1}

[
17
]
.
In order to study the Turaev cobracket
[
34
]
more precisely,
our previous works
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
introduced its framed version, and proved the formality of the framed Turaev cobracket for any compact connected oriented surface
\Sigma
with non-empty boundary.
Furuta’s remark stated above made us aware of an essential role of framings in the formality.
Later Hain
[
8
]
gave an alternative proof of the formality, and clarified the algebro-geometric meaning of the relation between the Turaev cobracket and framings. The target of the reduced extended first Johnson homomorphism is not
H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Q}})
but
H_{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{Q}})
. They are different if the boundary
\partial\Sigma
is not connected.
Hence we introduce the notion of
relative

{\mathbb{K}}
-framings in §
6
, by modifying relative framings introduced by Randal-Williams
[
28
]
.
But the results in §
5
and §
6
require us to find an appropriate formulation of groupoid version of expansions for the surface
\Sigma
, which is our future problem.
Here it should be remarked that Taniguchi
[
30
]
studies a groupoid version of the framed based Turaev operation
\mu^{\xi}_{r}
using non-commutative modular vector fields.
Moreover, for any field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
0
, Taniguchi
[
31
]
established a natural relation between genus
g
Gozalez-Drinfel’d associators and
{\mathbb{K}}
-framings for any compact connected oriented surface
\Sigma
with non-empty boundary and any field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic

, which covers all possible
{\mathbb{K}}
-framings.
Our desired groupoid expansions should provide the formality of the groupoid version of
\mu^{\xi}_{r}
.

In §

The map

We call

-quadratic form

[

If

associated with the spin structure

.
For a closed connected oriented surface
\Sigma_{g,0}
of genus

,
by the inclusion isomorphism

1.
A refinement of Johnson’s lifting

Let
\Sigma
be a compact connected oriented
C^{\infty}
surface.
By the classification theorem of surfaces, it is classified by the genus
g\geq 0
and the number
n+1\geq 0

n\geq-1

\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,n+1}

If
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset

\partial_{j}\Sigma

0\leq j\leq n

\ast

0

\partial_{0}\Sigma

\pi:=\pi_{1}(\Sigma,*)

2g+n

The unit tangent bundle
\varpi:U\Sigma\to\Sigma

\Sigma

\Sigma

T^{\times}\Sigma/{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
of the complement
T^{\times}\Sigma

T\Sigma

{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

Any diffeomorphism
\varphi

\Sigma

U\Sigma
by its differential

We denote the unit interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
by
I=[0,1]

I^{\circ}:=\mathopen{]}0,1\mathclose{[}
, and its boundary by
\partial I:=\{0,1\}

Any
C^{\infty}
immersion
\gamma:I\to\Sigma

\overset{\cdot}{\gamma}:t\in I\mapsto(d\gamma)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)_{t}\bmod{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\in U\Sigma

2.
The lifting restricted to the fundamental group

In this section we confine ourselves to the case where

\partial_{0}\Sigma

For any compact connected oriented surface

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a simple closed curve. Then the value of

at the right-handed Dehn twist

along

k_{\xi}(T_{C})=(\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}C)(\cdot[C])\in H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}}),

where

means the algebraic intersection with the homology class

Proof.

It suffices to check the formula by evaluating at any immersed loop

. As before, we denote by
\overrightarrow{\alpha}\in H_{1}(U\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})
the homology class of the velocity vector
\overset{\cdot}{\alpha}:S^{1}\to U\Sigma

\displaystyle\langle k_{\xi}(T_{C}),[\alpha]\rangle=\langle T_{C}\cdot\xi-\xi,[\alpha]\rangle=\langle\xi\circ(d{T_{C}}^{-1})_{*},\overrightarrow{\alpha}\rangle-\langle\xi,\overrightarrow{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}({T_{C}}^{-1}\alpha)-\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}(\alpha)=([\alpha]\cdot[C])\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}(C).

This proves the lemma.
∎

Now we can determine the (non)triviality of the class

Theorem 4.2
.

is given by

(\delta u)(T_{C})=\langle u,[C]\rangle(\cdot[C]).

In fact, for any loop
\gamma
in
\Sigma
,
we have
\langle(\delta u)(T_{C}),[\gamma]\rangle=\langle u\circ({T_{C}}^{-1})-u,[\gamma]\rangle=\langle u,({T_{C}}^{-1})_{*}[\gamma]-[\gamma]\rangle=\langle u,([\gamma]\cdot[C])[C]\rangle=\langle u,[C]\rangle([\gamma]\cdot[C])

(I) The case
g=0
. If

, it is trivial since

. So we suppose

. Then the homology classes
\overrightarrow{\partial_{j}\Sigma}

(II) The case

for

to
that for
\Sigma_{1,n+1}

{\mathbb{K}}
-framing on

{\mathbb{K}}
-framing on

Hence it suffices to show the vanishing of

for
\Sigma_{1,0}

Under the identification

From (

\Sigma

Hence we compute the coboundary

of the element

In particular, we have

This implies

(III) The case

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
{\mathbb{K}}
-expansion
\theta

\theta_{2}(\zeta)=\sum^{g}_{i=1}A_{i}B_{i}-B_{i}A_{i}=\sum^{g}_{i=1}[A_{i},B_{i}]

(5.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum^{g}_{i=1}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})B_{i}+A_{i}\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})-\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})A_{i}-B_{i}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})-(A_{i}B_{i}-B_{i}A_{i})(A_{i}+B_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum^{g}_{i=1}[A_{i},\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})-B_{i}A_{i}]-[B_{i},\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})-A_{i}B_{i}].

Here
[X,Y]=XY-YX\in H^{\otimes 2}

and

.

Proof.

The maps
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
are group homomorphisms on the commutator subgroup

. Hence it suffices to compute
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
for a single commutator
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1}
of
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\pi
.
Only inside this proof, we write simply
\Gamma_{1}:=[\gamma_{1}]
and
\Gamma_{2}:=[\gamma_{2}]\in H
.
We look at the equation
\theta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\theta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})
, which we will compute explicitly as

\theta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\equiv 1+(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})+\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})+\theta_{3}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})

and so

\displaystyle\theta(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle\equiv 1+(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})+\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})+\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle+\theta_{3}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})+\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})+\theta_{3}(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}).

Hence we obtain

\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})=\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})-\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})=\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}-\Gamma_{2}\Gamma_{1}=[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}]

and

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})=\theta_{3}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})-\theta_{3}(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})-\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}{\gamma_{1}}^{-1}{\gamma_{2}}^{-1})(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1})\Gamma_{2}+\Gamma_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2})-\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2})\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1})-(\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}-\Gamma_{2}\Gamma_{1})(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=[\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1}),\Gamma_{2}]+[\Gamma_{1},\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2})]-[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Gamma_{1}]-[\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{2}]

\displaystyle=[\Gamma_{1},\theta_{2}(\gamma_{2})-\Gamma_{2}\Gamma_{1}]-[\Gamma_{2},\theta_{2}(\gamma_{1})-\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}].

This proves the lemma.
∎

.

If a

We devote the rest of this section to the proof of Theorem

.
Since both of
(1\otimes\flat)\tau^{\theta}_{1}
and
\jmath\circ\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau^{\theta}_{1}
are
1

.
As was proved by Johnson

The cocycle

Hence it suffices to prove that the right-handed Dehn twists

From the classification theorem of surfaces, we can have a symplectic generator system
\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\}^{g}_{i=1}\subset\pi
such that

is freely homotopic to
\alpha_{1}

In the following computation we suppose

unless otherwise specified. The Dehn twist
t_{C}
acts on the fundamental group
\pi
by

\displaystyle t_{C}(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{1},\quad t_{C}(\beta_{1})=\beta_{1}\alpha_{1},\quad t_{C}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i},\quad t_{C}(\beta_{i})=\beta_{i},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle t_{C}}^{-1}(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{1},\quad{t_{C}}^{-1}(\beta_{1})=\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1},\quad{t_{C}}^{-1}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i},\quad{t_{C}}^{-1}(\beta_{i})=\beta_{i}.

If we denote by
T=|t_{C}|
the action of
t_{C}
on the homology group
H
,
we have

on
H
.
The transpose
T^{*}
of

is given by

on

.
From what we recalled so far, we have

\displaystyle\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})A_{i}=(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}),\quad\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})B_{i}=(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}),\quad\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})A_{1}=(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}),

\displaystyle\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})B_{1}=\theta_{2}(\beta_{1})-T^{\otimes 2}\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})=\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{1})-T^{\otimes 2}\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})

\displaystyle=(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})+\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})+(B_{1}-A_{1})A_{1}.

Consequently we obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})

\displaystyle=\sum^{g}_{i=2}A^{*}_{i}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})+\sum^{g}_{i=2}B^{*}_{i}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})

\displaystyle+A^{*}_{1}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle+B^{*}_{1}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})+\sout{B^{*}_{1}\otimes\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})}+B^{*}_{1}\otimes(B_{1}-A_{1})A_{1}.

Since
T
preserves the intersection form
\flat
, we have
\flat(1-T^{\otimes 2})=0
, and so

(5.6)

(1\otimes\flat)(\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C}))=(\flat\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})-1)B^{*}_{1}.

On the other hand, since

1-T^{\otimes 2}=-1\otimes(T-1)-(T-1)\otimes 1-(T-1)\otimes(T-1),

we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(f\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})(X_{1}X_{2}))

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{c}(f\otimes(X_{1}(T-1)X_{2}+((T-1)X_{1})X_{2}+((T-1)X_{1})((T-1)X_{2})))

\displaystyle=-f(X_{1})(T-1)X_{2}-((T^{*}-1)f)(X_{1})X_{2}-((T^{*}-1)f)(X_{1})(T-1)X_{2}

\displaystyle=-f(X_{1})B^{*}_{1}(X_{2})A_{1}-f(A_{1})B^{*}_{1}(X_{1})X_{2}-f(A_{1})B^{*}_{1}(X_{1})B^{*}_{1}(X_{2})A_{1}

\displaystyle=-(f\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(X_{1}X_{2}A_{1}+A_{1}X_{1}X_{2})-(f\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(A_{1}X_{1}X_{2})A_{1}

for

and
X_{1},X_{2}\in H
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(A^{*}_{i}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}))

\displaystyle=-(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})A_{1}+A_{1}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}))-(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(A_{1}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}))

\displaystyle=-(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}))A_{1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(B^{*}_{i}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}))

\displaystyle=-(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})A_{1}+A_{1}\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}))-(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(A_{1}\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}))

\displaystyle=-(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}))A_{1},

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(A^{*}_{1}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))

\displaystyle=-(A^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})A_{1}+A_{1}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))-(A^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(A_{1}\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))

\displaystyle=-(A^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))A_{1}-\sout{(B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})}-\sout{(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))A_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(B^{*}_{1}\otimes(1-T^{\otimes 2})\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=-(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})A_{1}+A_{1}\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}))-(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(A_{1}\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=-(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}))A_{1}

\displaystyle=-(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}))A_{1}+\sout{(B^{*}_{1}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))A_{1}},

where the last equality follows from
\theta_{2}(\beta_{1})=\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{1})=\theta_{2}(\beta_{1}{\alpha_{1}}^{-1})+\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})+(B_{1}-A_{1})A_{1}
.
As was underlined, each of the terms
(B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1}))A_{1}
and
(B^{*}_{1}\otimes 1)(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})
appears in
\mathfrak{c}\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})

(5.7)

\mathfrak{c}\tau^{\theta}_{1}(t_{C})=\left(1-\sum^{g}_{i=1}(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}))+(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})(\theta_{2}(\beta_{i}))\right)A_{1}.

Now, from the first line of (

\displaystyle(\flat\otimes B^{*}_{1})\theta_{3}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\flat\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})-\flat(A_{1}B_{1}-B_{1}A_{1})+\sum^{g}_{i=1}(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})+(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i})

\displaystyle=\flat\theta_{2}(\alpha_{1})-2+\sum^{g}_{i=1}(B^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})\theta_{2}(\beta_{i})+(A^{*}_{i}\otimes B^{*}_{1})\theta_{2}(\alpha_{i}).

Here we used
\flat(A_{i}X)=B^{*}_{i}(X)
and

for any

.

6.
Relative
{\mathbb{K}}
-framings

In this section, we suppose
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, and prove that the Earle class
k_{\mathbb{K}}\in H^{1}(\Gamma;H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}}))
has a natural lift with respect to the inclusion homomorphism
j^{*}
in the cohomology exact sequence

0\to\widetilde{H}^{0}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\to H^{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{j^{*}}{\to}H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{i^{*}}{\to}H^{1}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{\delta^{*}}{\to}H^{2}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})

of the pair
(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
. If we denote

N_{\partial}(\Sigma)=N_{\partial}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}}):=\mathrm{Ker}(\delta^{*})=\mathrm{Im}(i^{*})\subset H^{1}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}}),

we have

(6.1)

0\to\widetilde{H}^{0}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\to H^{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{j^{*}}{\to}H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{i^{*}}{\to}N_{\partial}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\to 0\quad\text{(exact)}.

The lift should come from ‘relative
{\mathbb{K}}
-framings’ on
(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
.
The notion of a relative framing was introduced by Randal-Williams
[
28
]
.
It is an isomorphism
U\Sigma\cong\Sigma\times S^{1}
of oriented
S^{1}
-bundles, which restricts to a fixed isomorphism
\delta:U\Sigma|_{\partial\Sigma}\cong\partial\Sigma\times S^{1}
.
In this paper, we call its homotopy class
a relative framing
.
More precisely, for each
0\leq j\leq n
, choose a number
\rho_{j}\in{\mathbb{K}}
and a single point
\bullet_{j}\in\partial_{j}\Sigma
. The vector
(\rho_{j})^{n}_{j=0}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{n+1}

can be identified with a unique element
\rho\in H^{1}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
satisfying

\langle\rho,[\partial_{j}\Sigma]\rangle=\rho_{j}
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.
Then we have
\rho\in N_{\partial}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
if and only if
\sum^{n}_{j=0}\rho_{j}=0
.
Now we denote
P:=\{\bullet_{j}\}^{n}_{j=0}
and

F(\Sigma,P,\rho;{\mathbb{K}}):=\{\xi\in F(\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}});\,\,0\leq\forall j\leq n,\,\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}(\partial_{j}\Sigma)=\rho_{j}\},

whose element we call
a relative
{\mathbb{K}}
-framing
.
Here the inclusion
U\Sigma\hookrightarrow(U\Sigma,\tilde{P})
induces a map
F(\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}})\to F(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
, so that we may consider the rotation number
\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}\partial_{j}\Sigma\in{\mathbb{K}}
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.
The evaluation map
\mathrm{Map}(\partial_{j}\Sigma,S^{1})\to S^{1}
,
f\mapsto f(\bullet_{j})
, at
\bullet_{j}\in\partial_{j}\Sigma
is a fibration with fiber
\Omega S^{1}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Hence the tangent vector
v_{\bullet_{j}}
and the rotation number
\operatorname{rot}_{\xi}(\partial_{j}\Sigma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
describe the homotopy type of the space
\mathrm{Map}(\partial_{j}\Sigma,S^{1})
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.

Since
H^{*}(\partial\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}})\cong\prod^{n}_{j=0}\widetilde{H}^{*}(\partial_{j}\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
,
the cohomology exact sequence of the triple
(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma,P)
induces a short exact sequence

(6.2)

0\to H^{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{j^{*}_{P}}{\to}H^{1}(\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}})\overset{i^{*}_{P}}{\to}N_{\partial}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})\to 0\quad\text{(exact)}.

Lemma 6.1
.

(1) The set
F(\Sigma,P,\rho;{\mathbb{K}})
is non-empty if and only if
\sum^{n}_{j=0}\rho_{j}=\chi(\Sigma)\in{\mathbb{K}}
.

(2) If the set
F(\Sigma,P,\rho;{\mathbb{K}})
is non-empty, it is a
H^{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
-torsor.

Proof.

(1) The ‘only if’ part follows from Lemma
3.1
.
In order to prove the ‘if’ part, we take an element
\xi_{0}\in F(\Sigma;{\mathbb{K}})
,
since
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
.
From the exact sequence (
6.2
),
there is an element
u\in H^{1}(\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}})
such that
\langle i^{*}u,[\partial_{j}\Sigma]\rangle=\rho_{j}-\operatorname{rot}_{\xi_{0}}(\partial_{j}\Sigma)
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.
Then we have
\operatorname{rot}_{\xi_{0}+u}(\partial_{j}\Sigma)=\rho_{j}
for each
0\leq j\leq n
.
This proves (1).

(2) Recall the set
F(\Sigma,P;{\mathbb{K}})
is a
