Mixed-Categorical Black-Box Optimization via Information-Geometric Bilevel Decomposition
Marc Ong, Shinichi Shirakawa, Youhei Akimoto

Abstract
Mixed categorical-continuous optimization arises in many practical domains, yet remains challenging. In the black-box setting, evolution strategy-based approaches have shown promise in extending the efficiency and robustness of the CMA-ES to mixed-variable spaces. However, these methods exhibit worsened performance when strong categorical-continuous interactions are present, as their underlying search distributions assume independence between categorical and continuous variables. To address this limitation, we propose a bilevel optimization framework that explicitly captures such interactions by optimizing over categorical variables in an outer loop, and over continuous variables conditioned on each categorical configuration in an inner loop. We formulate each level of the bilevel problem as a stochastic relaxation under information-geometric optimization. To mitigate the high computational cost inherent to bilevel optimization, we introduce a warm-starting strategy that accelerates the lower-level search by selecting the best among multiple cached configurations and updating the cache after each iteration. Experimental results on binary-continuous domain demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in interaction-handling capability while also being more computationally efficient across benchmarks encompassing both previously reported and newly proposed types of interaction.

Proposed Method

2.1
Bilevel Stochastic Relaxation under IGO

The IGO framework
[
26
]
provides a recipe
for constructing rank-based evolutionary algorithms for black-box
optimization via stochastic relaxation. Given an objective
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, IGO minimizes its expectation

J(\boldsymbol{\theta})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}[f(\boldsymbol{x})]
under a
parametrized distribution
p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
by iteratively updating

\boldsymbol{\theta}
along a Monte-Carlo estimate of the natural gradient,
concentrating the distribution around the optimum
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}
.

We formulate each level of the bilevel problem
in
Equation

3
as a stochastic relaxation under IGO, using
a categorical distribution for the discrete upper level and a
multivariate Gaussian for the continuous lower level. We further
incorporate the adaptation mechanisms of the ASNG method at the upper
level and CMA-ES at the lower level.

Upper Level

Let
\boldsymbol{q}\triangleq[\boldsymbol{q}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{q}_{d_{c}}]^{\top}
, where
\boldsymbol{q}_{i}
is
the probability vector governing the distribution of
[\boldsymbol{c}]_{i}
. The
upper-level stochastic relaxation is

\min_{\boldsymbol{q}}J_{f^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{q}),\ \textnormal{where }J_{f^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{q})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{c}\sim\operatorname{Cat}(\boldsymbol{q})}[f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c})]=\sum_{\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{C}}f(\boldsymbol{c})p(\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{q}),

(4)

with upper-level objective
f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c})\triangleq f(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c}))
.
The natural gradient with respect to
\boldsymbol{q}
is obtained by
premultiplying the vanilla gradient by the inverse Fisher information matrix:

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{q}}J_{f^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{q})=\boldsymbol{F}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{q})\nabla J_{f^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{q})=\boldsymbol{F}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{q})\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{c}\sim\operatorname{Cat}(\boldsymbol{q})}[f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{q}}\log p(\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{q})],

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{q})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{c}\sim\operatorname{Cat}(\boldsymbol{q})}[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{q}}\log p(\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{q})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{q}}\log p(\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{q})^{\top}]
is the Fisher
information matrix. Its Monte Carlo estimate
\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}
using
\lambda_{c}
samples is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}}\boldsymbol{F}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{q})\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{c}}f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c}_{i})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{q}}\log p(\boldsymbol{c}_{i};\boldsymbol{q})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}}\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{c}}f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c}_{i})(\boldsymbol{c}_{i}-\boldsymbol{q}).

(6)

IGO employs fitness shaping, replacing
f^{\star}(\boldsymbol{c})
with rank-based
weights
w_{i}
(larger positive weights correspond to lower objective
values) to ensure invariance to monotonic transformations, giving the update

\boldsymbol{q}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{q}+\eta\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}},\ \mathrm{where}\ \boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}}\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{c}}w_{i}(\boldsymbol{c}_{i:\lambda_{c}}-\boldsymbol{q}).

(7)

We adopt ASNG for the upper-level optimization, which
casts
Equation

7
as a trust region problem under the
Fisher information metric, setting
\eta=\delta/\|\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\|_{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{q})}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{q})}
is the
Fisher norm and
\delta
is the trust radius. The trust radius is
adapted analogously to cumulative step-size adaptation
(CSA)
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{c}}(K_{i}-1)}};\ \boldsymbol{s}\leftarrow(1-\beta)\boldsymbol{s}+\sqrt{\beta(2-\beta)}\frac{\boldsymbol{F}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}{\|\boldsymbol{G}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\|_{\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{q})}};

(8)

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\leftarrow(1-\beta)^{2}\gamma+\beta(2-\beta);\ \delta\leftarrow\delta\exp\left(\beta\left(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{s}\|^{2}}{\alpha}-\gamma\right)\right),

where
\beta
is the cumulation rate,
\boldsymbol{s}
is a quantity analogous
to the evolution path in CSA,
\gamma
is the expected value of

\|\boldsymbol{s}\|^{2}
under noise, and
\alpha
controls the target signal-to-noise ratio.
To prevent the distribution from becoming degenerate,
\boldsymbol{q}
is then
clipped such that

\frac{1}{d_{c}(K_{i}-1)}\leq[\boldsymbol{q}_{i}]_{k}\leq 1-\frac{1}{d_{c}(K_{i}-1)},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,d_{c}\},\ \forall k\in\{1,\ldots,K_{i}-1\}.

(9)

We also adopt the ASNG rank-based weighting scheme:

w_{i}=\begin{cases}+1,&\mathrm{if}\ 1\leq i\leq\lceil\lambda/4\rceil,\\
-1,&\mathrm{if}\ \lambda-\lceil\lambda/4\rceil+1\leq i\leq\lambda,\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(10)

Lower Level

We minimize
J_{f_{\boldsymbol{c}}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
, the expectation of
f_{\boldsymbol{c}}(\boldsymbol{x})\triangleq f(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{x})
under a multivariate Gaussian parametrized by

\boldsymbol{\omega}\triangleq[\boldsymbol{m}^{\top},\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})^{\top}]^{\top}
:

\min_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}J_{f_{\boldsymbol{c}}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}),\ \textnormal{where
}J_{f_{\boldsymbol{c}}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})}[f_{\boldsymbol{c}}(\boldsymbol{x})]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}}f_{\boldsymbol{c}}(\boldsymbol{x})p(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

(11)

In the multivariate Gaussian case, the IGO parameter update analogous
to
Equation

7
based on Monte Carlo estimation of the
natural gradient of

using

samples

[

,
26
]

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\boldsymbol{m}+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{x}}w_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i:\lambda_{x}}-\boldsymbol{m});

(12)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\Sigma}+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{x}}w_{i}[(\boldsymbol{x}_{i:\lambda_{x}}-\boldsymbol{m})(\boldsymbol{x}_{i:\lambda_{x}}-\boldsymbol{m})^{\top}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}].

(13)

In practice, the CMA-ES algorithm commonly used today incorporates additional
heuristically motivated update rules beyond the rank-

update, most notably
the rank-one update of the covariance matrix and cumulative step-size
adaptation
[
17
]
. Within our proposed method, we
adopt a recent variant known as dd-CMA-ES
[
2
]

for the lower-level optimization, which
further incorporates the active update
[

]
and
adaptive diagonal decoding.

The details of the warm-starting procedure are shown in

with
optimum
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})=\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})
and isotropic Hessian
\boldsymbol{H}=2\boldsymbol{I}
.

For simplicity,
\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}
is defined as the affine map
\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})=a\boldsymbol{V^{\star}}\boldsymbol{c}+\boldsymbol{b^{\star}}
,
where
\boldsymbol{V^{\star}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times d_{c}}
,
\boldsymbol{b^{\star}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
, and
a\geq 0
. The parameter
a
controls the strength of the binary-continuous
interaction. As
a\to 0
, the problem approaches separability:
f_{\mathrm{II}}=\operatorname{OneMax}(\boldsymbol{c})+\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{b^{\star}}\|^{2}
, giving
\boldsymbol{c^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{1}
. For
non-negligibly large
a
, both terms become significant and
determining
\boldsymbol{c^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{x})
becomes a nontrivial binary quadratic
programming (BQP) problem. The global optimum is
(\boldsymbol{c^{\star}},\boldsymbol{x^{\star}})=(\boldsymbol{1},a\boldsymbol{V^{\star}}\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{b^{\star}})
.

3.3
Type-III Interaction

Type-III interaction combines the difficulties of types I and II:

\boldsymbol{c}
simultaneously masks the active components of
\boldsymbol{x}
and
determines the optimum
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})
. Assuming
d_{c}=d_{x}
, the benchmark problem is

f_{\mathrm{III}}(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{x})=\|\boldsymbol{x}\odot\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})\|^{2},

(17)

using the same affine map
\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})
as in
f_{\mathrm{II}}
. For a fixed

\boldsymbol{c}
, the optimal continuous vector
5
5

5

The solution is not
unique if
\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})
contains zeros.
is
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})=\boldsymbol{\phi^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})

and the Hessian is
\boldsymbol{H}=2\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{c})
. As with
f_{\mathrm{II}}
,
a
controls
the interaction strength: as
a\to 0
, the problem reduces to
f_{\mathrm{I}}
,
while for sufficiently large
a
determining
\boldsymbol{c^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{x})
becomes a BQP
problem. The global optimum is
(\boldsymbol{c^{\star}},\boldsymbol{x^{\star}})=(\boldsymbol{1},a\boldsymbol{V^{\star}}\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{b^{\star}})
.

3.4
Type-IV Interaction

For all three interaction types, a single isotropic covariance matrix
still suffices for CMA-ES to efficiently optimize
\boldsymbol{x}
regardless of

\boldsymbol{c}
: the Hessian is
2\boldsymbol{I}
for
f_{\mathrm{II}}
, and
2\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{c})
for
f_{\mathrm{I}}

and
f_{\mathrm{III}}
.
In many real-world applications, however, the optimal distribution of

\boldsymbol{x}
can vary significantly with
\boldsymbol{c}
, a dependency intractable
with product-distribution-based approaches.

To address this, we introduce type-IV variants of
f_{\mathrm{I}}
,
f_{\mathrm{II}}
, and

f_{\mathrm{III}}
, which we denote as
f_{\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{IV}}
,
f_{\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{IV}}
,
f_{\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{IV}}
,
respectively, by replacing the Euclidean norm
in
Equations

14
,
16
and
17
with a Mahalanobis norm

\|\boldsymbol{x}\|^{2}_{\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})}\triangleq\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})\boldsymbol{x}
dependent on

\boldsymbol{c}
, with the map
\boldsymbol{A}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_{x}}
. The resulting
Hessian then depends on
\boldsymbol{c}
as
\boldsymbol{H}=2\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})
for
f_{\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{IV}}
and

\boldsymbol{H}=2\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{c})\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{c})
for
f_{\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{IV}}
and
f_{\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{IV}}
.
Moreover, this modification leaves
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{c})
and the global optimum

(\boldsymbol{c^{\star}},\boldsymbol{x^{\star}})
unchanged, though the BQP parameters determining

\boldsymbol{c^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{x})
differ from the original
f_{\mathrm{II}}
and
f_{\mathrm{III}}
. The
combination of types II and III with type IV results in problems
where both
\boldsymbol{x^{\star}}
and the interactions within the continuous domain
depend on
\boldsymbol{c}
.

We set
\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})=\boldsymbol{Q}(\boldsymbol{c}){\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\boldsymbol{Q}(\boldsymbol{c})^{\top}
, where

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times d_{x}}
is a fixed diagonal matrix of
positive eigenvalues and
\boldsymbol{Q}(\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times d_{x}}
is
the orthogonal matrix

\boldsymbol{Q}(\boldsymbol{c})=\exp\left(\boldsymbol{M}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{c}}[\boldsymbol{c}]_{i}\boldsymbol{M}_{i}\right),

(18)

with fixed skew-symmetric matrices
\boldsymbol{M}_{0},\boldsymbol{M}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{M}_{d_{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times d_{x}}
and
\exp(\cdot)
the matrix
exponential. The condition number
\kappa(\boldsymbol{A})=\kappa({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})

controls the strength of the type-IV interaction: as

\kappa(\boldsymbol{A})\to 1
,
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\propto\boldsymbol{I}
and
\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{c})\propto\boldsymbol{I}
,
causing the type-IV interaction to vanish.

4.3
Termination and Restart Strategy

In addition to termination criteria based on the numerical properties of the
search distribution, we adopt budget-based termination.
Specifically, the algorithm is terminated if no improvement in the objective
function greater than
1\text{\times}{10}^{-6}
is observed after 50 generations of the
upper-level ASNG (
Algorithm

1
) for IGBD, or after 50 generations for
the CatCMA and ICatCMA baselines. For IGBD, the lower level
(
Algorithm

3
) is also terminated if no improvement greater than

1\text{\times}{10}^{-6}
is observed after 20 generations. Upon termination, a simple
restart strategy resets all distribution parameters; in the case of
IGBD, the parameters of both levels of the optimization are reset.
Restarts continue until any of
the following conditions is met: the objective function reaches within

1\text{\times}{10}^{-6}
of the true optimum or the total
number of FEs across restarts exceeds
$2\text{\times}{10}^{4}$\times d_{c}\times d_{x}
. The best
objective function value across all restarts is reported.

strategy ineffective, with both CatCMA

success under type-IV interaction.

5
Conclusion

We introduced IGBD, a bilevel information-geometric approach to
mixed-categorical black-box optimization with a
multi-configurational warm-starting mechanism. On the mixed
binary-continuous domain, we demonstrate that our method handles both
previously reported and novel interaction types significantly better
than current state-of-the-art methods, while also improving efficiency.

Several directions remain for future work. Increasing
K_{i}
beyond
binary problems is the natural next step, as is further
taxonomization and benchmarking of interaction types. Incorporating
low-effective-dimension handling into the lower-level optimizer would
likely accelerate cases where the categorical variable deactivates
certain continuous components, as in type-I interaction and many
real-world problems. The lower-level optimizer also extends readily
to variants such as CMA-ES with margin, enabling simultaneous
handling of integer, continuous, and categorical variables.
Finally, combining our method with lower-cost approaches such as
CatCMA or heuristics like early stopping or lower-level coevolution
may further alleviate the difficulty of bilevel optimization.
