Final analysis of the FeDeriCa phase III study: long-term efficacy, safety and pharmacokinetics of the fixed-dose combination of pertuzumab and trastuzumab for subcutaneous injection, plus chemotherapy, in HER2-positive early breast cancer
Authors: C Jackisch, S-A Im, A Mattar, R Colomer, D Stroyakovskiy, Z Nowecki, M De Laurentiis, J-Y Pierga, KH Jung, C Schem, S Heeson, I Yan, B Wang, E Restuccia, AR Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFeDeriCa (NCT03493854) showed that the fixed-dose combination of pertuzumab and trastuzumab for subcutaneous injection (PH FDC SC) was noninferior to intravenous (IV) P and H, with comparable total pathological response rates and safety profiles when given as neoadjuvant therapy for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive early breast cancer (BC). We report long-term efficacy and safety.Materials and methodsPatients received eight neoadjuvant chemotherapy + P IV + H IV cycles or PH FDC SC every 3 weeks (cycles 5-8; 1:1 randomisation). Patients continued adjuvant HER2-targeted treatment to complete 18 cycles.ResultsData cut-off was 2 June 2023, with a median follow-up of 51.4 (P + H IV arm) and 51.2 months (PH FDC SC arm). Four-year event-free rates (invasive disease-free survival, event-free survival, distant recurrence-free interval, and overall survival) were 89.6 [95% confidence interval (CI) 85.5-93.7], 88.5 (95% CI 84.4-92.5), 92.5 (95% CI 89.2-95.8) and 95.5 (95% CI 92.9-98.1) in the P + H IV arm and 88.5 (95% CI 84.3-92.7), 86.6 (95% CI 82.2-90.9), 91.9 (95% CI 88.4-95.4) and 94.1 (95% CI 91.1-97.1) in the PH FDC SC arm, respectively. No new safety signals (including cardiac safety) were observed.ConclusionsLong-term efficacy of PH FDC SC and P + H IV was comparable. Safety remained similar and consistent with P + H + chemotherapy. PH FDC SC is established as a faster, more convenient, less invasive patient- and health care professional-preferred alternative to P + H IV for HER2-positive BC.Key words: early breast cancer, HER2-positive, pertuzumab, subcutaneous, trastuzumab

Introduction

The overexpression or gene amplification of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) occurs in ∼15% to 20% of breast cancer (BC) cases and was historically associated with short survival and high disease recurrence.1,2 Various studies combining intravenous (IV) pertuzumab (P) with trastuzumab (H) and chemotherapy for patients with HER2-positive BC indicated that this combination significantly improved overall survival [OS, CLEOPATRA (NCT00567190)3; metastatic setting], pathological complete response rates [NeoSphere (NCT00545688)4; neoadjuvant setting] and invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) in a clinically meaningful manner in patients with a high risk of recurrence [APHINITY (NCT01358877)5; adjuvant curative setting]. P + H in combination with chemotherapy is therefore regarded as standard treatment for patients with HER2-positive early BC (EBC) and metastatic BC (MBC).

Due to the long administration time of P IV (30-60 minutes, with 30- to 60-minute observation times) and H IV (over 30-90 minutes, originally with 120- to 360-minute observation times) and the invasive nature of IV administration, which can be inconvenient and painful for patients, a different route of administration [subcutaneous injection (SC), taking 5 to 8 minutes, with 15- to 30-minute observation times] was investigated in the randomised, open-label, multicentre phase III (neo)adjuvant FeDeriCa study (NCT03493854).6 Previous studies indicated that the administration of H by SC injection resulted in noninferior serum trough (Ctrough) concentrations, consistent antitumor activity and comparable safety compared to IV administration in EBC7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 while maintaining similar efficacy and safety to P + H IV in MBC.14 P IV with H SC injected into the thigh offers a more convenient and faster option. Administration via SC injection reduces the burden and time that patients spend in infusion chairs and has the potential to optimise the use of medical resources.15,16 SC administration was preferred by most patients in the PrefHer (NCT01401166)10 and PHranceSCa (NCT03674112)17 studies, as it saves time and health care resource use,10,15,17 is also less invasive and more satisfactory for health care professionals10,17 and might potentially allow self-administration at home.18,19

Despite these advances, HER2-positive EBC remains a substantial global health burden, affecting hundreds of thousands of patients annually.20 The logistical and physical burdens of prolonged IV infusions—particularly in curative-intent settings where patients may receive up to 18 cycles of therapy—have highlighted the need for more convenient, less invasive treatment approaches that align with patient preferences and optimise health care resource use.

The fixed-dose combination of P and H for SC injection (PH FDC SC) has identical active ingredients to P IV and H IV and contains 1200 mg P + 600 mg H in a fixed, non-weight-based loading dose of 15 ml and a fixed, non-weight-based maintenance dose of 600 mg P + 600 mg H in 10 ml. PH FDC SC offers patients faster, more convenient, and less invasive treatment compared with IV infusions.6 PH FDC SC was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency for use in HER2-positive EBC and MBC based on the pivotal phase III FeDeriCa study.6 The primary analysis (clinical cut-off: 4 July 2019) showed that PH FDC SC cycle 7 P and H Ctrough concentrations were noninferior to P IV and H IV (the primary endpoint was met) in patients with operable, locally advanced or inflammatory stage II to IIIC BC (primary tumour >2 cm in diameter or node-positive disease).6 Comparable total pathological complete response (tpCR, i.e. in the breast and axilla) rates were obtained with P + H IV and PH FDC SC, and safety was similar between treatment arms and in line with other P, H and chemotherapy trials.6,21, 22, 23, 24 Similarly, in an analysis of safety from the adjuvant phase of the trial (clinical cut-off: 10 July 2020), overall safety and tolerability (including cardiac safety) of PH FDC SC was comparable with that of P + H IV, apart from adverse events (AEs) linked to different routes of administration.25 The results were in line with the expectation that most AEs associated with PH FDC SC or P + H IV are observed during concomitant chemotherapy.25 AE incidence rates were balanced across body weight quartiles, both within and between treatment arms and including the lowest quartile, which was consistent with the overall safety profile.25 Population pharmacokinetic and exploratory exposure–response analyses, in conjunction with clinical experience with P IV, H IV and H SC, indicated that the dose of PH FDC SC provides a positive benefit–risk profile with comparable efficacy with P + H IV that is achieved with a manageable safety profile.26 Here we report long-term efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetic data from the final analysis of FeDeriCa.

Materials and methods

Study design and procedures

The study design and patient population have been described previously.6 Patients received eight cycles of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (one of two standard regimens chosen by the investigator) + P IV (840 mg loading dose then 420 mg maintenance dose) + H IV (8 mg/kg then 6 mg/kg) or PH FDC SC (1200 mg P/600 mg H and then 600 mg each as FDC SC) every 3 weeks during cycles 5-8 in a 1 : 1 randomisation design.6 Patients continued adjuvant HER2-targeted treatment after surgery, per randomisation, to complete 18 cycles.6

Secondary endpoints in this final analysis include iDFS, event-free survival (EFS), distant recurrence-free interval (DRFI), OS and safety (including cardiac AEs). iDFS was defined as the time from the first date of no disease (i.e. the date of primary surgery after neoadjuvant treatment) to the first occurrence of one of the following events: (i) ipsilateral invasive breast tumour recurrence (i.e. an invasive BC involving the same breast parenchyma as the original primary lesion); (ii) ipsilateral locoregional invasive BC recurrence (i.e. an invasive BC in the axilla, regional lymph nodes, chest wall and/or skin of the ipsilateral breast); (iii) distant recurrence (i.e. evidence of BC in any anatomical site other than the two abovementioned sites that has either been histologically confirmed or clinically diagnosed as recurrent invasive BC); (iv) contralateral invasive BC; (v) death attributable to any cause, including BC, non-BC or unknown cause (but cause of death should be specified, if possible). Ipsilateral or contralateral in situ disease and second primary non-BCs (including in situ carcinomas and nonmelanoma skin cancers) were not counted as progressive disease (PD) or relapse. EFS was defined as the time from enrolment to the first occurrence of one of the following events: (i) PD; (ii) BC recurrence (as defined for iDFS) and (iii) death from any cause. Ipsilateral or contralateral in situ disease and second primary non-BCs (including in situ carcinomas and nonmelanoma skin cancers) were not counted as PD or relapse. DRFI was defined as the time between randomisation and the date of distant BC recurrence. OS was defined as the time from randomisation to death from any cause. Exploratory endpoints included P Ctrough and H Ctrough during cycle 12 (postsurgery cycle 13) to compare cycle 12 P Ctrough of P SC within PH FDC SC with P IV and H Ctrough of H SC within PH FDC SC to H IV.

The study was done in conformance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol (WO40324) was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at each study site. All amendments affecting trial recruitment and conduct were approved. All patients provided written informed consent.

Patient eligibility

The patient population has been described in full previously.6 Briefly, patients with HER2-positive operable, locally advanced or inflammatory stage II to IIIC BC, aged 18 years of age and older, with a primary tumour >2 cm in diameter or node-positive disease, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥55% (by echocardiography or multiple-gated acquisition scan), and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1 were enrolled. HER2 and hormone receptor status were confirmed centrally. Patients were ineligible if they had received any systemic therapy for treatment or prevention of BC, or radiotherapy for their disease, had a serious cardiac condition, impaired liver function or inadequate renal or bone marrow function.

Pharmacokinetic assessments

Blood samples for pharmacokinetic assessments were collected before IV infusion or SC injection of HER2-targeted therapy and after administration. Exact collection times were recorded for pharmacokinetic samples.

Statistical analysis

Efficacy analyses (except for iDFS) were carried out on the intent-to-treat population, defined as all randomised patients. For iDFS, patients who did not undergo surgery were excluded from the analysis, and patients without any postsurgery assessment were censored at time of surgery plus 1 day. For OS, patients not experiencing an event were censored at the date last known to be alive and event free. The safety analysis population included all patients who received at least one dose of study medication (i.e. chemotherapy, P IV, H IV, H SC or PH FDC SC) and patients were grouped according to actual treatment received. Patients in the P + H IV arm who switched to H SC after surgery were analysed as a separate subset. For cycle 12 (predose cycle 13), Ctrough in the adjuvant phase of the study, the arithmetic mean and geometric mean will be discussed. All patients with cycle 12 Ctrough (predose cycle 13) pharmacokinetic data were included in the cycle 12 Ctrough analysis regardless of whether the patients were in the per-protocol pharmacokinetic population at the time of primary analysis (neoadjuvant phase), i.e. patients with missing cycle 13 predose pharmacokinetic results were not included in the cycle 12 Ctrough analysis. Analysis of secondary time-to-event endpoints was carried out using Kaplan–Meier methodology. Cox proportional hazards methodology was used to obtain stratified and unstratified estimates of the hazard ratios and their corresponding confidence intervals (CIs). Patients who were without distant disease recurrence, alive or lost to follow-up for the respective endpoints were censored at the date they were last known to be event free. Patients randomised and without any postbaseline assessments were censored at time of randomisation plus 1 day. These analyses, being secondary and/or exploratory, were not powered to show statistically significant results. Safety was evaluated using descriptive analyses of incidence and severity of AEs and serious AEs, laboratory test abnormalities and incidence of a left ventricular systolic dysfunction (symptomatic/asymptomatic). All verbatim AEs terms were mapped to Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities thesaurus terms, and AE severity was graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for AEs, version 4.0. Congestive heart failure, in addition, was graded according to the New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification.

Results

Patients

Data cut-off was 2 June 2023, and median follow-up was 51.4 months in the P + H IV arm and 51.2 months in the PH FDC SC arm. In both arms, >80% of patients completed the adjuvant phase (Figure 1). The follow-up period was initiated by 247 of 252 patients (98.0%) in the P + H IV arm and 241 of 248 patients (97.2%) in the PH FDC SC arm; 222 of 252 patients (88.1%) in the P + H IV arm and 218 of 248 (87.9%) in the PH FDC SC arm completed the follow-up period. A mean of >13 out of 14 cycles planned in the adjuvant phase was completed in both treatment arms. There were proportionally higher discontinuations in the P + H IV arm (13.1%) compared with the PH FDC SC arm (6.9%), primarily due to nonsafety reasons. In the group of patients who switched to H SC in the adjuvant phase, completion of therapy as planned was slightly lower, with 50 patients (76.9%) completing a mean of 12.8 cycles. Results in this group must be considered with caution, as only 65 patients switched to H SC in the adjuvant phase.

Safety analysis

Delays in HER2-targeted therapy due to AEs occurred in 13.4% and 8.3% of patients receiving P + H IV and PH FDC SC, respectively; discontinuations of any study drug due to AEs, in 12.7% and 8.9%, respectively (of HER2-targeted therapy, in 6.0% and 4.8%, respectively; of any chemotherapy drug, in 9.1% and 5.6%, respectively). An overall summary of AEs is presented in Table 2. Overall, incidences of frequent AEs (in at least 5% of patients) were comparable between treatment arms, except for injection-site reactions which were, as expected, more common in the PH FDC SC arm [33 (13.3%)] than in the P + H IV arm [1 (0.4%)]. The most frequent AEs with incidences of ≥10% in the P + H IV and PH FDC SC arms were arthralgia [56/252 (22.2%) and 48/248 (19.4%), respectively], radiation skin injury [54/252 (21.4%) and 50/248 (20.2%), respectively] and diarrhoea [52/252 (20.6%) and 43/248 (17.3%), respectively]. Three patients (1.2%) in each arm had anaphylaxis/hypersensitivity; no grade ≥3 events occurred in the PH FDC SC arm, and one patient (0.4%) had a grade ≥3 event in the P + H IV arm. Summaries of primary and secondary cardiac events during the adjuvant and follow-up phases are presented in the Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328. During the adjuvant phase, three patients experienced NYHA class III/IV heart failure with a decrease in LVEF of at least 10% from baseline and to <50%: one patient (0.4%) and two patients (0.8%) in the P + H IV and PH FDC SC arms, respectively. The patient in the P + H IV arm subsequently died. One patient in the P + H IV arm had NYHA class III/IV heart failure, with a decrease in LVEF of at least 10% from baseline and to <50% during the follow-up period (Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328). There was no difference with respect to most of the other safety parameters analysed [serious treatment-emergent AEs, AEs leading to dose modifications, LVEF decrease (an AE of special interest), cardiac dysfunction or AEs leading to withdrawal of HER2-targeted therapy] between the two treatment arms across different weight quartiles (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328). Injection-site reactions were, however, more frequent in the PH FDC SC group than in the P + H IV group across all four body weight quartiles (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328).

Pharmacokinetics

The mean and geometric mean of cycle 12 (predose cycle 13) serum P Ctrough values were numerically higher in the PH FDC SC arm than in the P + H IV arm (geometric mean ratio 1.25, 90% CI 1.17-1.33) (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328). The mean and geometric mean of cycle 12 (predose cycle 13) serum H Ctrough values were numerically higher in the PH FDC SC arm than in the P + H IV arm (geometric mean ratio 1.39, 90% CI 1.29-1.50) (Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108328).

Discussion

The results obtained in this final analysis of the FeDeriCa study complement, in a long-term analysis, the results obtained in the primary analysis; relevance of tpCR comparability was supported by comparable long-term efficacy. There was a decrease in AEs in the adjuvant phase, except for administration-related reactions in the PH FDC SC arm, and no new safety signals, including for cardiac safety, were reported. Expectedly, local injection-site reactions were higher in the PH FDC SC arm compared with the P + H IV arm and increased in the adjuvant phase in light of the longer exposure (more cycles) versus the neoadjuvant phase.

Efficacy data were comparable with those of previous studies of P + H IV [BERENICE (NCT02132949),21 APHINITY,5 NeoSphere4 and TRYPHAENA (NCT00976989)22], although caution should be taken while comparing different trials. The incidence of cardiac AEs was low and similar between arms, with no difference in AEs across the two treatment arms in all weight quartiles6; these findings are similar to those in the HannaH study (NCT00950300) of H SC and H IV.9 The lack of any new safety signals with long-term follow-up adds to the body of evidence that SC injection of P and H is not impacted by patients’ body weight. Numerically higher Ctrough values at cycle 12 versus cycle 7 due to the reloading dose at cycle 9 in ∼90% of patients were reported.

Notably, although the study was not statistically powered to detect significant differences in long-term endpoints, the numerically higher Ctrough of both P and H in the PH FDC SC arm did not translate into increased toxicity or reduced efficacy. These findings support the pharmacological robustness and clinical viability of the SC formulation as an alternative to IV administration.

Final analysis data of the HannaH study support the long-term comparability of the efficacy and safety of H SC and H IV,9 consistent with previous reports from FeDeriCa. In addition, the FDChina (NCT04024462) study met its co-primary endpoints, indicating that PH FDC SC P and H cycle 7 Ctrough were noninferior to those for P + H IV.27 The tpCR rates in the FDChina study were comparable between arms and the global population of FeDeriCa, with no new safety signals identified, suggesting that PH FDC SC may be a viable treatment option for Chinese patients.27 The PHranceSCa study showed that most patients strongly preferred PH FDC SC over P + H IV. PH FDC SC was generally well tolerated, with no new safety signals (even when switching from P + H IV to PH FDC SC or vice versa) and offers a quicker alternative to IV infusion.17 Safety and efficacy results from the MetaPHER (NCT02402712) study in the MBC setting further support the conclusion of the HannaH study.14 The PrefHer study,15 in addition to a cost-minimisation analysis,28 adds to the evidence of cost savings when using PH FDC SC. An expanded access programme (NCT0439550829; including the HARRIET cardiac monitoring substudy30) evaluated the safety of PH FDC SC administered at home. Together, these results indicate that efficacy and safety of H and P do not change with SC route of administration. Additionally, they highlight increased flexibility in the treatment for patients with HER2-positive BC, shortening administration and patient clinic time and enabling the possibility of treatment outside the clinical setting (e.g. at home). While the increasing use of subcutaneously administered antitumoural treatments in oncology may raise concerns about potential physical discomfort and chronic soreness among nurses, SC administration of PH FDC SC remained the preferred option for health care professionals in the PHranceSCa study.17 Nonetheless, this aspect should be monitored over time to ensure appropriate measures are in place to support nurses and maintain ease of administration.

Personal experiences with PH FDC SC reported by an oncologist, a pharmacist, a health economist and (most importantly) a patient reported that patients appreciated the option to have flexible appointments; that pharmacies had released capacity for other treatments and had reduced costs and waste; that switching from P + H IV to PH FDC SC is likely to reduce costs and that PH FDC SC saved time and had positive benefits mentally.31 There is a strong rationale to support the exploration of more patient-centred and flexible formulations in oncology from the start of clinical development to optimise the treatment experience for patients and enhance overall quality of care. Limitations in the study include lack of statistical power for all of the long-term efficacy endpoints, as well as small patient numbers in some subgroups.

In conclusion, in this final analysis of the FeDeriCa study, the long-term efficacy of PH FDC SC was comparable with that of P + H IV. Apart from the expected injection-site reactions, the PH FDC SC safety profile remained similar to P + H IV. Observed serum P Ctrough mean and geometric mean values during cycle 12 (predose cycle 13) were numerically higher in the PH FDC SC arm compared with the P + H IV arm. Overall, these findings support the primary analysis results. The totality of the published evidence supports that PH FDC SC is established as a faster, more convenient, less invasive patient- and health care professional-preferred alternative to P + H IV for patients with HER2-positive EBC and MBC and reduces the cost and waste involved in treatment.
