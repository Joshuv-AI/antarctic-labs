A bibliometric analysis of post-translational modifications in esophageal cancer based on the Web of Science Core Collection database [2000–2026]
Authors: Fan Gao, Xiaokang Gong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEsophageal cancer (EC) is a highly lethal malignancy with limited therapeutic options. Post-translational modifications (PTMs) play critical regulatory roles in EC carcinogenesis, progression, and therapy resistance. However, current EC PTM research remains fragmented across different modification types and molecular targets, hindering a systematic understanding of research trajectories and knowledge gaps. To date, no comprehensive bibliometric analysis has quantitatively mapped the global landscape of EC PTM research. This study aims to systematically characterize the publication trends, geographic distribution, research hotspots, and unaddressed areas in EC PTM research over the past 26 years, and identify promising prognostic candidate genes for future preclinical investigation.MethodsWe retrieved EC PTM articles from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) [2000–2026] using a Boolean query. Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and full texts. Bibliometric analyses used R/Bibliometrix and VOSviewer. Abstract-level genes and PTMs were categorized and cross-referenced with the PTMD database; The Cancer Genome Atlas-Esophageal Carcinoma (TCGA-ESCA) data identified unstudied genes with differential expression and prognostic significance.ResultsOf 1,963 articles identified, output followed three phases: nascent growth [2000–2011], expansion [2012–2019], and maturation [2020–2026]. China contributed 69.7% of publications. Methylation (405 genes) and phosphorylation (326 genes) dominated, while glycosylation and sulfation were underrepresented. STAT3 was the most studied gene. We identified 1,776 unstudied PTM-related genes, of which 11 showed both differential expression and prognostic significance, enriched in nucleosome assembly and immune response pathways.ConclusionsThis study maps EC PTM research quantitatively. Methylation-based liquid biopsies and STAT3 inhibitors are translatable advances, yet knowledge siloes persist for multi-PTM crosstalk and underexplored modifications. The 11 candidate genes provide a concrete preclinical pipeline. Diversified research, CRISPR validation, and international consortia are needed to ensure clinical impact across diverse populations.

Key findings

• Esophageal cancer (EC) post-translational modification (PTM) research output followed three phases (nascent 2000–2011, expansion 2012–2019, maturation 2020–2026), with China contributing 69.7% of global publications. Methylation (405 genes) and phosphorylation (326 genes) dominated, while glycosylation and sulfation were severely underrepresented. Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) was the most studied gene. From 1,776 unstudied PTM-related genes, 11 showed both differential expression and prognostic significance in The Cancer Genome Atlas Esophageal Carcinoma data, enriched in nucleosome assembly and immune response pathways.

What is known and what is new?

• PTMs critically regulate esophageal carcinogenesis and therapy resistance; methylation-based liquid biopsies and STAT3 inhibitors have shown clinical promise; however, EC PTM research has remained fragmented across modification types and targets without systematic quantitative mapping.

• This first bibliometric analysis quantitatively maps the entire EC PTM field (2000–2026), revealing persistent knowledge siloes (multi-PTM crosstalk, glycosylation, sulfation) and a decoupling from gene level mechanistic inquiry. It also identifies 11 novel candidate PTM-related genes with differential expression and prognostic value, providing a concrete preclinical pipeline.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Diversified research efforts are urgently needed to explore underexplored modifications (glycosylation, sulfation) and multi-PTM crosstalk, reconnect PTM studies with gene level functional validation (e.g., CRISPR screening), and establish international consortia to validate biomarkers and therapies across genetically diverse populations. The 11 candidate genes should be prioritized for preclinical characterization, particularly their roles in chromatin remodeling and DNA damage response.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, accounting for approximately 511,000 new cases and 445,000 deaths annually (1). Histologically dominated by esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), EC is characterized by aggressive biological behavior, late clinical presentation, and limited therapeutic options, resulting in a five-year overall survival rate below 20% (2). These clinical challenges have intensified research efforts aimed at deciphering the molecular mechanisms driving esophageal carcinogenesis, among which post-translational modifications (PTMs) have emerged as critical regulatory events.

PTMs chemically alter proteins after ribosomal synthesis, thereby expanding functional diversity beyond the constraints of the genome (3). In cancer biology, aberrant PTMs—including phosphorylation, methylation, ubiquitination, acetylation, and glycosylation—fundamentally reshape protein stability, enzymatic activity, subcellular localization, and protein-protein interactions, contributing to malignant transformation, metastatic dissemination, and therapeutic resistance (3,4). In EC specifically, constitutive phosphorylation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) drives tumor proliferation and immune evasion (5,6); DNA methylation patterns serve as diagnostic biomarkers and prognostic indicators (7) and METTL3-mediated N6-methyladenosine modification promotes metastatic progression (8). Despite the biological and clinical significance of these findings, EC PTM research remains highly fragmented across diverse modification types, molecular targets, and experimental systems.

This fragmentation poses a significant barrier to identifying overarching research trends, collaborative networks, and knowledge gaps that could guide future investigations. While existing narrative and systematic reviews have provided in-depth mechanistic insights into individual PTMs (7,8), they are inherently limited in scope and cannot systematically synthesize the trajectory of an entire research domain spanning multiple decades, countries, and disciplines. Bibliometric analysis addresses this limitation by quantitatively mapping publication trends, citation impact, collaboration patterns, and thematic evolution across large scholarly corpora (9,10). In oncology, bibliometric approaches have been successfully applied to map research landscapes in gastric cancer immunotherapy, lung cancer epigenetics, and hepatocellular carcinoma metabolism, revealing hidden structural patterns and informing priority-setting for translational research (10).

To date, however, no study has conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of EC-related PTM research. This gap precludes a systematic understanding of how scientific attention has been distributed across different modification types, which institutions and countries have driven progress, and which molecular targets remain underexplored despite their clinical relevance. To address this, we performed a bibliometric analysis of publications from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database spanning 2000 to 2026. This study aims to: (I) characterize temporal publication trends and citation impact; (II) map collaborative networks among countries, institutions, and authors; (III) identify research hotspots and thematic evolution through keyword analysis; (IV) analyze the distribution of PTM types and their associated genes across the literature; and (V) integrate bibliometric data with functional genomics to identify unstudied PTM-related genes with differential expression and prognostic significance in EC. By providing a quantitative cartography of this field, we seek to inform future research priorities, foster international collaboration, and accelerate the identification of translational targets for EC. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-aw-2219/rc).

Methods

Data source and search strategy

A comprehensive bibliometric analysis was conducted using the WOSCC database, which was selected for its standardized citation metadata and authoritative coverage of peer-reviewed biomedical literature. The search strategy was developed to capture all publications investigating PTMs in EC. The following search query was applied:

(TI=(“esophageal neoplasm” OR “esophageal cancer” OR “esophagus neoplasm” OR “esophagus cancer” OR “esophageal tumor” OR “esophagus tumor” OR “esophageal carcinoma” OR “esophagus carcinoma” OR “ESCC”)) AND TS=(“Posttranslational Modifications” OR “Post-Translational Modifications” OR “Post Translational Modifications” OR “Protein Modification, Post-Translational” OR “Modification, Post-Translational Protein” OR “Modifications, Post-Translational Protein” OR “Post-Translational Protein Modifications” OR “Protein Modification, Post Translational” OR “Protein Modifications, Post-Translational” OR “Amino Acid Modification, Post-Translational” OR “Amino Acid Modification, Post Translational” OR “Post-Translational Amino Acid Modification” OR “Post Translational Amino Acid Modification” OR “Posttranslational Amino Acid Modification” OR “Amino Acid Modification Posttranslational” OR “Post-Translational Protein Modification” OR “Post Translational Protein Modification” OR “Post-Translational Modification” OR “Modification, Post-Translational” OR “Modifications, Post-Translational” OR “Post Translational Modification” OR “Posttranslational Modification” OR “Modification, Posttranslational” OR “Modifications, Posttranslational” OR “Phosphorylation” OR “Phosphorylations” OR “Acetylation” OR “Acetylations” OR “ubiquitylation” OR “Ubiquitylation” OR “methylation” OR “Methylation” OR “glycosylation” OR “Protein Glycosylation” OR “Glycosylation Protein” OR “sumoylation” OR “Sumoylations” OR “SUMO-Conjugation” OR “SUMO Conjugation” OR “SUMO-Conjugations” OR “myristoylation” OR “Lipoylation” OR “Palmitoylation” OR “Prenylation” OR “Isoprenylation” OR “Farnesylation” OR “Geranylgeranylation” OR “sulfation” OR “Lipidation” OR “Ubiquitination” OR “glycosylate” OR “sumoylate” OR “methylate”).

The publication date was restricted from January 1, 2000, to April 17, 2026. The year 2000 was selected as the starting point because it coincides with the post-genomic era, during which high-throughput proteomic technologies enabled systematic investigation of protein modifications in human cancers, including landmark studies establishing the oncogenic role of STAT3 (5) and the development of comprehensive PTM databases (3).

Data extraction and screening

Bibliographic data were downloaded in plain text format (“Full Record and Cited References”) from the WOSCC database. The extracted information included publication title, authors, affiliations, countries, institutions, journals, publication year, keywords, abstracts, citations, and reference lists. Because the search query was restricted to “Article” document type and English language within the WOSCC interface, all retrieved records met the basic inclusion criteria at the source level. Duplicate records arising from database overlap were identified and removed using Bibliometrix (n=498). Consequently, 1,963 unique original research articles were retained for bibliometric analysis.

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1) with the Bibliometrix package (version 4.1.3) (11) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) (12). The following analyses were conducted: (I) annual publication trends and growth patterns; (II) national, institutional, and author productivity and collaboration networks; (III) journal contribution and citation impact; (IV) keyword co-occurrence and thematic evolution; and (V) co-citation analysis. Bibliometric indicators including total publications, total citations, H-index, single country publications (SCP), multiple country publications (MCP), and centrality measures were calculated to evaluate research performance and network characteristics. In network visualizations, node size represents publication frequency or citation count, link thickness indicates collaboration or co-occurrence strength, and colors denote distinct clusters identified by modularity algorithms.

Gene and PTM classification

The abstracts of included articles were systematically reviewed to extract reported genes and PTMs. Based on prior literature and database classifications, PTMs were categorized into six main types: acetylation, glycosylation, methylation, phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and sulfation. PTMs not falling into these categories were classified as “Others”, and studies reporting multiple PTM types were categorized as “Various Modes”.

Functional enrichment and bioinformatics analysis

Functional enrichment analysis was performed using the ClusterProfiler package in R. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were conducted with a significance threshold of P<0.05. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks were constructed using the STRING database (confidence score ≥0.9) and visualized in Cytoscape, with the MCODE plugin applied to identify key subnetworks.

To identify candidate genes not yet studied in EC PTM research, PTM-related gene sets were retrieved from the PTMD database (https://ptmd.biocuckoo.cn/) and compared with genes reported in the literature. Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas-Esophageal Carcinoma (TCGA-ESCA) data was performed using the limma package (|log2 fold change| >1, adjusted P<0.05). Survival analysis was conducted using the survival and survminer packages (minprop =0.3, P<0.05). Genes exhibiting both differential expression and prognostic significance were identified as candidate targets.

Statistical analysis

WoSCC was used to analyze publication characteristics, including annual output, citations, H‑index, and productivity by country, institution, and author. Bibliometric network analyses (collaboration networks, keyword co‑occurrence, and thematic evolution) were performed using R/Bibliometrix (version 4.1.3) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.19). Differential expression and survival analyses of TCGA‑ESCA data were conducted using the limma and survival packages in R (version 4.3.1), respectively.

Results

Temporal evolution of publication output

Between January 2000 and April 17, 2026, a total of 1,963 original research articles investigating PTMs in EC were identified from the WOSCC. The annual publication volume demonstrated a distinct multiphase trajectory over the 25-year period (Figure 1). During the initial phase [2000–2011], publications grew gradually from 12 articles in 2000 to 50 in 2011, reflecting the early establishment of PTM research in EC. A marked inflection occurred in 2012, when annual output surged to 74 articles—a 48% increase from the previous year—heralding a sustained expansion phase. Output continued to climb steadily thereafter, reaching a peak of 156 articles in 2022. Notably, even during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic [2020–2021], productivity remained robust (143 and 138 articles, respectively), underscoring the resilience and continued prioritization of this research domain. The modest decline observed in 2023 (133 articles) and 2025 (85 articles) likely reflects publication lag effects rather than diminished scientific interest, as the 2024 rebound to 146 articles attests.

Global productivity, impact, and collaboration patterns

China emerged as the undisputed leader in EC PTM research, contributing approximately 69.7% of all publications and ranking first in both total citations and H-index, a composite metric reflecting both productivity and citation impact (Figure 2A,2B; Table S1). Japan and the United States followed as secondary contributors, though with substantially smaller outputs. This geographic distribution mirrors the epidemiological burden of ESCC, which disproportionately affects East Asian populations (13,14).

Annual publication number and distribution of publications. (A) Map of national publication volume. (B) Top 10 countries with the most publications. MCP, multiple country publications; SCP, single country publications.

Collaboration network analysis revealed a predominantly international research architecture (Figure 3A). Most publications from China, Japan, and India were single-country publications (SCP; studies authored exclusively by researchers from one country), whereas multiple-country publications (MCP; studies involving cross-border collaborative authorship) constituted a minority, with the United States exhibiting a relatively higher proportion of international collaborations (Figure 2B). At the institutional level, seven of the top 10 most productive institutions were based in China, led by Zhengzhou University, Hebei Medical University, and Shandong University (Figure 3B; Table S2). Author-level analysis similarly demonstrated Chinese dominance, with Kangdong Liu, En-Min Li, and Li-Yan Xu among the most prolific contributors (Figure 3C; Table S3). However, collaboration networks among authors were predominantly domestic or regional, with limited cross-border partnerships, suggesting that knowledge exchange in this field remains geographically constrained. Citation impact analysis further identified ten landmark studies that have each accumulated more than 200 citations, underscoring the field’s foundational advances (Table S4).

Research hotspots and thematic evolution

Keyword co-occurrence analysis revealed a densely interconnected thematic network centered on “methylation”, “prognosis”, “esophageal squamous cell carcinoma”, and “apoptosis” (Figure 4A). Word frequency analysis confirmed that “methylation” and “prognosis” were the most recurrent terms, followed by “DNA methylation”, “apoptosis”, “proliferation”, “metastasis”, and “invasion” (Figure 4B). This thematic profile indicates that the EC PTM literature has been heavily oriented toward epigenetic regulation of tumor behavior and patient outcomes.

“Methylation” and “Prognosis” are hot keywords for PTMs in esophageal cancer. (A) Network of keywords. (B) Keyword frequency (remove tumor characters). PTMs, post-translational modifications.

The temporal evolution of keywords suggests a gradual broadening of research focus. Early publications [2000–2010] concentrated primarily on DNA methylation and single-gene analyses. From 2011 onward, themes diversified to encompass RNA methylation (m6A), ubiquitination, and immune-related modifications, reflecting broader technological advances in epigenomics and proteomics. Nevertheless, certain high-potential areas—notably protein glycosylation, sulfation, and lipidation—remained markedly underrepresented throughout the entire study period, indicating persistent knowledge siloes.

Landscape of PTM types and gene associations

Systematic extraction of PTMs from article abstracts revealed that methylation (405 associated genes) and phosphorylation (326 associated genes) were the most extensively studied modification types, collectively accounting for the majority of PTM-related publications (Figure 5A,5B). Ubiquitination (167 genes), acetylation (158 genes), and glycosylation (104 genes) represented intermediate tiers of investigation, whereas sulfation (8 genes) and other rare modifications were minimally represented. This hierarchical distribution was consistent across the entire 25-year span, with methylation and phosphorylation maintaining dominance even as total publication volume expanded. Functional enrichment analysis of all PTM-related genes identified in the literature revealed significant involvement in gland development, Wnt signaling, epithelial cell proliferation, and chromosomal regulation (Figure S1), underscoring the biological relevance of the extracted gene set.

Keyword network analyses of the two dominant PTMs revealed distinct thematic profiles. Methylation-related research emphasized “promoter hypermethylation”, “DNA hypermethylation”, and “tumor suppressor gene silencing” (Figure 5C), whereas phosphorylation research focused on “expression”, “growth”, “activation”, and “kinase signaling” (Figure 5D). Intersection analysis of PTM-associated genes showed that overlap decreased as the number of PTM types increased, indicating that most studies investigate single-modification mechanisms rather than multi-modification crosstalk (Figure 5B).

Among the top 30 most frequently studied genes, STAT3 ranked first (72 mentions), followed by MAPK3 [49], DNMT1 [34], CDK4 [28], and ERBB2 [27] (Figure 6A). PPI network analysis revealed extensive connectivity among PTM-related genes, with methylation and phosphorylation modules exhibiting the most frequent inter-module interactions (Figure 6B). The highest-scoring MCODE subnetwork identified STAT3, AKT1, MAPK3, CDK1, and BRCA1 as critical bridge molecules integrating multiple PTM pathways (Figure 6C), underscoring the central role of STAT3 as a hub for post-translational regulatory networks in EC.

Strikingly, the proportion of PTM studies involving specific genes declined progressively from 2000 to 2026 (Figure 6D). In 2002, over 70% of publications identified specific target genes, whereas by 2023, more than 75% of studies did not report gene-level findings. This trend suggests a decoupling of PTM research from mechanistic gene-centric inquiry, potentially reflecting a shift toward broad epigenomic profiling or clinical biomarker validation without in-depth functional characterization.

Identification of candidate unstudied PTM-related genes

Comparison of literature-derived genes with the PTMD database revealed 212 genes common to both datasets, with 1,016 genes unique to the literature and 1,776 unique to the database (Figure 7A). KEGG pathway enrichment of these 1,776 unstudied genes highlighted significant enrichment in systemic lupus erythematosus, alcoholism, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, viral carcinogenesis, and ECM-receptor interaction pathways (Figure S2), suggesting that unexplored PTM targets in EC may extend beyond currently investigated oncogenic signaling networks. From the 1,776 unstudied PTM-related genes, differential expression analysis of TCGA-ESCA data identified 113 differentially expressed genes (Figure 7B), while survival analysis identified 202 prognosis-related genes (Figure 7C). The intersection yielded 11 candidate genes exhibiting both differential expression and prognostic significance (Figure 7D).

PTM type analysis of these 11 candidates revealed that 8 were phosphorylated, 5 were methylated, 4 were ubiquitinated, and 3 were SUMOylated, among other modifications (Figure 7E). Gene Ontology enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were primarily involved in nucleosome assembly, nucleosome organization, placenta development, and innate immune responses (Figure 7F). These findings suggest that unexplored PTM targets in EC may regulate chromatin remodeling and immune-related processes, offering novel avenues for mechanistic investigation.

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis provides the first quantitative cartography of PTM research in EC, spanning a transformative quarter-century from 2000 to 2026. The publication trajectory reveals three distinct phases: a nascent period of gradual growth [2000–2011], an expansion phase catalyzed by technological breakthroughs [2012–2019], and a maturation phase marked by sustained high-volume output [2020–2026]. The 2012 inflection point coincides with the widespread adoption of next-generation sequencing and mass spectrometry-based proteomics, which enabled high-resolution mapping of protein modifications at unprecedented scale (15). The continued growth during the COVID-19 pandemic further underscores the strategic prioritization of EC research in high-burden regions, particularly China.

The overwhelming research focus on methylation and phosphorylation is epidemiologically rational and clinically consequential. Aberrant DNA methylation—particularly promoter hypermethylation of tumor suppressor genes—represents one of the earliest and most stable epigenetic alterations in ESCC, and circulating methylated DNA has demonstrated diagnostic accuracy exceeding 90% in recent prospective and multicenter studies (16,17). Similarly, STAT3 phosphorylation drives constitutive oncogenic signaling in ESCC and has been extensively pursued as a therapeutic target, with multiple small-molecule inhibitors (e.g., TTI-101, OPB-31121) advancing to Phase I/II clinical trials (18,19). However, this dominance also exposes a critical structural gap: the vast majority of PTM types remain clinically untapped. Despite cataloging over 450 distinct modifications in the human proteome (20), EC research has concentrated on fewer than ten. Glycosylation, for instance, profoundly influences immune recognition and metastatic potential in multiple cancers yet attracted only minimal attention in our dataset. Sulfation—a modification regulating chemokine signaling and immune cell trafficking—was represented by merely eight genes. This concentration creates “knowledge siloes” wherein well-funded, mechanistically tractable PTMs attract disproportionate resources while equally biologically significant modifications languish in relative obscurity. From a translational perspective, this imbalance may delay the discovery of novel biomarkers and druggable targets that could diversify the currently limited therapeutic armamentarium for advanced EC.

STAT3’s emergence as the most frequently studied gene across all PTM categories aligns with its established role as a master regulator of oncogenic transcription, immune evasion, and metabolic reprogramming in ESCC (21,22). Our PPI network analysis positions STAT3 as a critical bridge connecting methylation, phosphorylation, and ubiquitination modules, reflecting its capacity to integrate diverse post-translational signals into coordinated cellular responses. This network centrality has direct clinical implications: STAT3 inhibitors are currently among the most advanced PTM-targeted therapeutics in oncology, with TTI-101 demonstrating objective responses in heavily pretreated solid tumor patients (19,23). Nevertheless, the bibliometric prominence of STAT3 also raises cautionary questions. The concentration of research effort on a single hub gene may divert attention from equally important but less well-characterized nodes. For example, METTL3—an m6A methyltransferase ranked among the top 30 genes—has only recently been recognized as a driver of ESCC metastasis (24), and its therapeutic potential remains largely unexplored. A more distributed research portfolio, encompassing both hub genes and peripheral network components, may accelerate the identification of synthetic lethal interactions and combination therapy strategies.

Our analysis further identifies several specific knowledge siloes that warrant targeted investigation. First, multi-PTM crosstalk remains poorly characterized; the decreasing overlap of gene sets across multiple modification types suggests that most studies adopt a reductionist, single-modification approach. Yet emerging evidence demonstrates that cooperative phosphorylation-acetylation-methylation switches govern the activity of transcription factors including SOX2 and p53, with direct relevance to ESCC stemness and therapy resistance (25,26). Second, gene-less PTM studies now constitute over 80% of recent publications, indicating a potential drift toward descriptive epigenomic profiling without mechanistic follow-up. Reconnecting PTM research with gene-level functional validation—using CRISPR-based screening and patient-derived models—would restore translational relevance to this expanding literature. Third, our screening of unstudied genes identified 11 candidates modified predominantly by phosphorylation and enriched in nucleosome assembly pathways. Chromatin-remodeling complexes are increasingly recognized as therapeutic vulnerabilities in solid tumors, and the nucleosome assembly function of these candidates suggests potential roles in regulating DNA damage response and replication stress—processes central to chemotherapy and radiotherapy resistance in EC (27).

The stark dominance of Chinese institutions and authors in EC PTM research reflects both the epidemiological reality—China bears approximately half of the global ESCC burden (28)—and substantial national investment in cancer research infrastructure. However, the preponderance of single-country publications and limited international collaboration networks raises concerns about geographic knowledge siloes. ESCC exhibits significant inter-ethnic molecular heterogeneity; for example, the genomic landscape of Chinese ESCC differs from that observed in Iranian or African high-incidence populations (29). Insufficient cross-border collaboration may limit the generalizability of findings and slow the integration of diverse genetic backgrounds into biomarker development and clinical trial design. Western countries, despite lower ESCC incidence, possess complementary strengths in drug development, clinical trial infrastructure, and regulatory frameworks. Enhanced bilateral partnerships—particularly between Chinese high-output institutions and Western translational research centers—could accelerate the progression of PTM-targeted agents from discovery to clinical application.

This study has several limitations. First, restricting the analysis to the WOSCC may have excluded relevant publications indexed exclusively in Scopus, PubMed, or regional databases. Second, our reliance on abstract-level gene and PTM extraction may have missed studies reporting modifications in full-text sections without abstract mention, potentially underestimating the true scope of certain PTMs. Third, the TCGA-ESCA dataset used for differential expression analysis encompasses both ESCC and EAC subtypes, and subtype-specific analyses were not performed due to sample size constraints. Fourth, bibliometric indicators such as citation counts and H-index are influenced by temporal factors—older publications have had more time to accumulate citations—and may not fully reflect current scientific impact. Moving forward, we propose several actionable directions for the EC PTM research community. Targeted funding initiatives should prioritize underexplored modifications—including glycosylation, sulfation, and lipidation—to diversify the research portfolio and identify novel therapeutic targets. Mechanistic studies must reconnect with gene-level functional validation, leveraging CRISPR screening and multi-omics integration to move beyond descriptive profiling. International consortia should be established to harmonize ESCC biobanking, molecular profiling, and clinical trial enrollment across high-incidence regions, ensuring that biomarkers and therapies are validated in genetically diverse populations. The 11 candidate genes identified in this study should be prioritized for preclinical characterization, with particular attention to their roles in chromatin remodeling and DNA damage response. Finally, as STAT3-targeted therapies advance through clinical trials, biomarker-driven patient stratification—incorporating phosphorylation status, co-occurring modifications, and tumor microenvironment context—will be essential to maximize therapeutic efficacy.

Conclusions

This comprehensive bibliometric analysis reveals that EC PTM research has undergone sustained expansion over the past 26 years, with China driving global productivity and methylation and phosphorylation dominating the thematic landscape. While these foci have yielded clinically translatable insights—including methylation-based liquid biopsies and STAT3-targeted therapeutics—significant knowledge siloes persist, particularly regarding multi-PTM crosstalk, underexplored modification types, and gene-less descriptive studies. The identification of 11 differentially expressed, prognostic, and previously unstudied PTM-related genes offers a concrete pipeline for future mechanistic and therapeutic investigation. To realize the full translational potential of EC PTM research, the field must embrace a more diversified, internationally collaborative, and mechanistically grounded approach that bridges bibliometric momentum with clinical impact.
