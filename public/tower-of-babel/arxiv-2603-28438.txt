Asymptotic behavior of small solutions to the Vlasov–Klein–Gordon system in high dimensions
Ho Lee

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behavior of small solutions to the Vlasov–Klein–Gordon system in high dimensions. The standard argument of Glassey and Strauss [ 14 ] for studying small solutions to the Vlasov–Maxwell system does not apply to the Vlasov–Klein–Gordon system due to the massiveness of the Klein–Gordon field. In this paper we use the vector field method and consider solutions in dimensions n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 with the hyperboloidal foliation of the Minkowski spacetime to obtain the asymptotic properties for the Vlasov–Klein–Gordon system.



1
Introduction

Let us consider an ensemble of relativistic particles interacting through a self-consistent field. In many situations this field is given by the Maxwell field, but in this paper we instead consider the Klein–Gordon field. Assuming that the particles are collisionless, we obtain the Vlasov–Klein–Gordon (VKG) system
[
18
,
19
]
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}f

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}^{2}\phi+\Delta_{x}\phi-\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f\,dv.

Here,
f=f(t,x,v)
and
\phi=\phi(t,x)
with
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are the distribution function and the Klein–Gordon (scalar) field, respectively, and
\hat{v}=v/v^{0}
denotes the relativistic velocity of a particle with momentum
v
. We consider particles of unit mass so that
v^{0}
is given by

\displaystyle v^{0}=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}.

In this paper we are interested in small solutions to the VKG system and study the asymptotic behavior of them by using the vector field method.

The VKG system was first studied in
[
18
,
19
]
to obtain mathematical insight into the global existence problem for the Vlasov–Maxwell (VM) system, which still remains unsolved for large initial data. By replacing the massless Maxwell field with a massive Klein–Gordon field, one obtains a faster temporal decay rate, suggesting a more favorable scenario for global existence. However, this enhanced decay rate does not necessarily simplify the problem. Indeed, for large initial data, the global existence for the VKG system was obtained in
[
19
]
, but it was under the same continuation criterion as in the VM case
[
13
]
.

For small initial data, the massiveness of the Klein–Gordon field even complicates the problem. While the Maxwell field decays at a rate of
t^{-1}
, its masslessness allows for an additional decay rate of
(t-r)^{-1}
inside the light cone. Then, by assuming compact support, this extra decay enables the application of the standard argument of Bardos and Degond
[
1
]
, which leads to the global existence of small solutions to the VM system
[
14
]
. In contrast, the Klein–Gordon field decays at a faster rate of
t^{-3/2}
, but this rate alone is insufficient to apply the argument of
[
1
]
. One needs to derive an additional decay rate, but it is not possible to apply the idea of
[
14
]
to the VKG case, due to the massiveness of the Klein–Gordon field. It violates the strong Huygens’ principle, and decay estimates based on the
(t-r)^{-1}
factor are irrelevant. To overcome this difficulty, Ha and Lee
[
15
]
studied the VKG system by incorporating an additional damping term, which enabled the necessary decay estimates. In the present paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of small solutions to the undamped VKG system and resolve the aforementioned issues by employing the vector field method. We refer to
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
for more details about the VM system and
[
23
,
28
,
29
]
for recent results on the VKG system.

The vector field method, introduced by Klainerman in the 1980s, has been a powerful technique for studying wave equations and has recently been extended to the study of kinetic equations. In
[
27
]
, the vector field method was successfully adapted to the study of the Vlasov–Poisson system. The main idea was to lift the commutation vector fields to the tangent bundle in order to apply them in the phase space. The method was then extended to the Vlasov–Nordström system
[
8
,
10
]
, to the VM system
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, to the Vlasov–Yukawa system
[
7
]
, to the Einstein–Vlasov system
[
6
,
9
]
, and now will be applied to the VKG system in this paper. The spatial dimensions will be restricted to
n\geq 4
. In three dimensions we will need to consider the method of modified vector fields as in
[
2
,
10
,
27
]
(see also
[
23
]
for a different approach), and this will be our future project.

Main result

In this paper we study the VKG system in dimensions
n\geq 4
. Let us write the VKG system as

\displaystyle T_{\phi}f

\displaystyle=0,

(1)

\displaystyle(\Box-1)\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f\,dv.

(2)

We are interested in small solutions to the VKG system (
1
)–(
2
) with initial data given at the unit hyperboloid
H_{1}
. In the following theorem, and throughout the paper, the terminologies
regular
functions,
regular
solutions or

[
8
]
. Details about the hyperboloidal foliation and the energies

,
\hat{E}_{N}(f)
and
\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)
in the following theorem will be provided in Section

.

then the corresponding solution satisfies the following estimates:

1.

The Klein–Gordon field satisfies for all
\tau\geq 1
,

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi)(\tau)\lesssim\varepsilon.

(3)

2.

The distribution function satisfies for all

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\tau)\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{\delta},

(4)

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
for
n=4
.

Moreover, for each
(t,x)
in the future of the unit hyperboloid, we have the following estimates:

4.

For any multi-index
A
satisfying
|A|\leq N-n
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{n-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}(t-|x|)^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}},

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{v^{0}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{n-\delta}},

(7)

\delta=0

, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}

.

For any multi-index
A

, and

for
n=4
.

Remark 1.1
.

Initial data are small with respect to the energies
E_{N}(\phi)
,
\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)
and
\hat{E}_{N+n}(f)
, but the corresponding solution is small with respect only to the energies
E_{N}(\phi)
and
\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)
. The smallness of the initial value of the higher order energy
\hat{E}_{N+n}(f)
will only be used to control the homogeneous part of the commuted Vlasov equation. Then, this will be used again to obtain the estimate (
5
). See Lemma
5.6
for more details.

Remark 1.2
.

If we assume compact support on initial data, then we obtain a lower bound of the quantity
t-|x|
, from which an additional decay in time is obtained for small solutions. This was a key idea in
[
14
]
for proving the global existence of small solutions to the VM system (see also Lemmas 6.3.1 and 6.3.2 of
[
11
]
). In this paper we do not assume compact support, but instead obtain an additional decay by using the hyperboloidal foliation and the vector field method.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Hyperboloidal foliation

In this paper we will consider the hyperboloidal foliation of the
(n+1)
-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. Let
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be the Cartesian coordinate system in the Minkowski spacetime. Then we define for each
\tau\geq 1
the hyperboloid
H_{\tau}
by

\displaystyle H_{\tau}=\left\{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:t\geq|x|,\,\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}\right\},

and obtain the hyperboloidal foliation of the Minkowski spacetime:

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\tau\geq 1}H_{\tau}=\left\{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:t\geq\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\right\}.

With this foliation we introduce the pseudo-Cartesian coordinate system
(\tau,y)\in\bigcup_{\tau\geq 1}H_{\tau}
defined by

\displaystyle\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}},\qquad y=x.

(11)

For each
\tau\geq 1
we have the induced volume form on
H_{\tau}
defined by

\displaystyle d\mu_{H_{\tau}}=\frac{\tau}{t}r^{n-1}dr\,d\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}},

(12)

where
(r,\sigma)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is the usual spherical coordinate system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with the standard volume form
d\sigma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. To each
H_{\tau}
we have the future unit normal
\nu_{\tau}
given by

\displaystyle\nu_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau}(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}).

(13)

We note that

\displaystyle\partial_{y^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}}+\frac{x^{i}}{t}\partial_{t}.

(14)

This will be used to prove the Klainermann–Sobolev inequalities in Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
. For more details about the hyperboloidal foliation we refer to
[
8
,
20
]
.

In this paper we study the VKG system in the Minkowski spacetime with the hyperboloidal foliation. The distribution function and the Klein–Gordon field will be understood as functions defined in

\displaystyle\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{v},\qquad\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau},

for some
T\in(1,\infty]
, respectively, and their initial data will be given on
H_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{v}
and
H_{1}
, respectively.

2.2
Killing fields

In order to apply the vector field method to the VKG system we consider the Killing fields in the Minkowski spacetime and their commutations with the differential operators for the Vlasov and the Klein–Gordon equations. For the Vlasov equation we define the transport operators by

\displaystyle T=v^{0}\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x},

\displaystyle T_{\phi}=T-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},

which we may call the free and the perturbed transport operators, respectively. Then the Vlasov equation can be written as

\displaystyle T_{\phi}f=0.

The Klein–Gordon operator will be denoted by
\Box-1
, so that the Klein–Gordon equation can be written as

\displaystyle(\Box-1)\phi=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f\,dv.

Now, we introduce the following vector fields:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}=\{\partial_{t},\,\partial_{x^{i}},\,\Omega_{0i},\,\Omega_{ij}:i,j=1,\dots,n\},

where
\Omega_{0i}
and
\Omega_{ij}
are defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Omega_{0i}=t\partial_{x^{i}}+x^{i}\partial_{t},&&1\leq i\leq n,\\
&\Omega_{ij}=x^{i}\partial_{x^{j}}-x^{j}\partial_{x^{i}},&&1\leq i<j\leq n,\end{aligned}

which are the Killing fields in the
(n+1)
-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. Then (
14
) can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{y^{i}}=\frac{1}{t}\Omega_{0i}.

(15)

We will denote an arbitrary member of
{\mathbb{P}}
by

\displaystyle Z_{a}\in{\mathbb{P}},

for
a=1,\dots,(n+1)(n+2)/2
. Any compositions of the members of
{\mathbb{P}}
will be written by using the multi-index notation. Let
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of integers such that

\displaystyle a_{i}\in\left\{1,\dots,\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}\right\},

for
i=1,\dots,q
. Then we define

\displaystyle Z_{A}:=Z_{a_{1}}\cdots Z_{a_{q}},

(16)

which is a differential operator of order
q
, and write
|A|=q
, which we call the length of
A
. Finally, we denote by
{\mathbb{P}}^{|A|}
the set of all the possible differential operators in the form of (
16
). Any
q
-tuple of integers will be referred to as a multi-index of order
q
, when it is used in the above manner.

We also need to consider the complete lifts of
{\mathbb{P}}
:

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}=\left\{\hat{\partial}_{t},\,\hat{\partial}_{x^{i}},\,\hat{\Omega}_{ij},\,\hat{\Omega}_{0i}:i,j=1,\dots,n\right\},

defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}_{t}=\partial_{t},

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}_{x^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}},

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{0i}=\Omega_{0i}+v^{0}\partial_{v^{i}},

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}+v^{i}\partial_{v^{j}}-v^{j}\partial_{v^{i}}.

We will denote an arbitrary member of
\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
by

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{a}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}},

for
a=1,\dots,(n+1)(n+2)/2
, and will use the multi-index notation for higher order derivatives. Let
A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
be a multi-index. Then we define

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{A}:=\hat{Z}_{a_{1}}\cdots\hat{Z}_{a_{q}},

and the set
\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}^{|A|}
is also defined in a similar way. For more details about the complete lifts to vector fields we refer to the Appendix of
[
8
]
.

2.3
Energy-momentum tensor

We also need to introduce the energy-momentum tensors for the distribution function and the Klein–Gordon field. We define the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\rm V}
for
f
as

\displaystyle T^{\rm V}_{\mu\nu}[f]:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{v_{\mu}v_{\nu}}{v^{0}}f\,dv,

and define the energy density as

\displaystyle\hat{e}(f):=T^{\rm V}[f](\partial_{t},\nu_{\tau})=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{v_{\mu}x^{\mu}}{\tau}f\,dv,

where
\nu_{\tau}
is the future unit normal (
13
) to
H_{\tau}
. For the Klein–Gordon field we define the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\rm KG}
as

\displaystyle T^{\rm KG}[\phi]:=d\phi\otimes d\phi-\frac{1}{2}(\eta(\partial\phi,\partial\phi)+\phi^{2})\eta,

where
\eta
is the Minkowski metric, and
\partial\phi
denotes
(\partial_{t}\phi,\nabla_{x}\phi)
. We define the energy density as

\displaystyle e(\phi):=T^{\rm KG}[\phi](\partial_{t},\nu_{\tau}),

where
\nu_{\tau}
is the future unit normal (
13
) to
H_{\tau}
.

2.4
Energies

With the Killing fields in Section
2.2
and the energy densities in Section
2.3
we define the energy for the distribution function as

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{|A|\leq N}\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}},

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{|A|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}+\sum_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1\leq|A|\leq N}\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}},

(18)

and the energy for the Klein–Gordon field as

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi):=\sum_{|A|\leq N}\int_{H_{\tau}}e(Z_{A}\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}.

(19)

Here,
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function, so that
\lfloor N/2\rfloor
is the greatest integer satisfying
\lfloor N/2\rfloor\leq N/2
. The summations are taken over all the possible multi-indices
A
satisfying
|A|\leq N
in (
17
) and (
19
), and
|A|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
and
\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1\leq|A|\leq N
in (
18
).

Basic estimates

In this section we introduce the basic estimates which are necessary for the application of the vector field method to the VKG system. In Section
3.1
we consider the estimates of the energy densities. The estimates in Section
3.1
will be extended to the Klainerman–Sobolev inequalities in Section

3.1
Estimates of the energy densities

3.1.1
Energy density for the distribution function

Let us first consider the energy density
\hat{e}
for the distribution function. Lemma
3.1
below shows that
\hat{e}
can be used to control the
L^{1}_{v}
-norm of
f
. In Lemma
3.2
we show that the integration over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{v}
commutes with the vector fields of
{\mathbb{P}}
or
\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
. Lemma
3.3
is an application of the divergence theorem to the transport equation with an inhomogeneous term.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
t\geq r
we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\hat{e}(|f|)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{v^{0}}|f|\,dv,

\displaystyle\hat{e}(|f|)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau}{2(t+r)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}v^{0}|f|\,dv,

\displaystyle\hat{e}(|f|)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|f|\,dv.

Proof.

We refer to Lemma 2.11 and Remark 2.12 of
[
8
]
for the proof of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
k=0,1
, we have the following identities:

1.

For
\mu=0,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{\mu}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}f\,dv=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}\hat{\partial}_{x^{\mu}}f\,dv.

2.

For
1\leq i<j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}f\,dv=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}\hat{\Omega}_{ij}f\,dv.

3.

For
i=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{0i}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}f\,dv=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}\hat{\Omega}_{0i}f\,dv+(1-k)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{v^{i}}{(v^{0})^{k+1}}f\,dv.

4.

All the above identities hold with
f
replaced by
|f|
.

Proof.

The first and the second identities are clear, and the third one is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{0i}\int\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}f\,dv

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}\hat{\Omega}_{0i}f\,dv-\int\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k-1}}\partial_{v^{i}}f\,dv

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}\hat{\Omega}_{0i}f\,dv+(1-k)\int\frac{v^{i}}{(v^{0})^{k+1}}f\,dv.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f
and
h
be regular functions satisfying
T_{\phi}f=h
defined in
\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{v}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\leq\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(|f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}+\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|h|+|\nabla_{x}\phi||f|\right)dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda.

Proof.

Note that the divergence of
T^{\rm V}
is given by

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}T^{\rm V}_{\mu 0}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{v_{\mu}v_{0}}{v^{0}}\partial^{\mu}f\,dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}Tf\,dv.

Integrating this over
\bigcup_{1\leq\lambda\leq\tau}H_{\lambda}
we obtain from the right hand side

\displaystyle-\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}Tf\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}h+v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}f\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}h-\left(\frac{v}{v^{0}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right)f\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda,

and from the left hand side

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\partial^{\mu}T^{\rm V}_{\mu 0}\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{\tau}}T^{\rm V}_{\mu 0}(\nu_{\tau})^{\mu}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}+\int_{H_{1}}T^{\rm V}_{\mu 0}(\nu_{1})^{\mu}d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(f)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}+\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(f)\,d\mu_{H_{1}},

where
\nu_{\tau}
and
\nu_{1}
are the future unit normals to
H_{\tau}
and
H_{1}
, respectively. Now, by the same argument as in Lemma 4.3 of
[
8
]
, we obtain the desired inequality for
|f|
.
∎

3.1.2
Energy density for the Klein–Gordon field

Applying the future unit normal
\nu_{\tau}
given by (
13
) to the definition of the energy density
e
for the Klein–Gordon field we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle e(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2\tau}\left((\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)+\frac{r}{\tau}(\partial_{t}\phi)(\partial_{r}\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2\tau}\phi^{2}+\frac{r}{2\tau}(\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{r}\phi)^{2}+\frac{t-r}{2\tau}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{t}{2\tau}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}-\frac{r}{2\tau}(\partial_{r}\phi)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{2\tau}\phi^{2}+\frac{t-r}{2\tau}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{t-r}{2\tau}|\nabla_{x}\phi|^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle e(\phi)\geq\frac{t}{2\tau}\phi^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2(t+r)}|\partial\phi|^{2},

(20)

by the definition (
11
) of
\tau
. This inequality shows that the energy (
19
) is basically an
L^{2}
-norm, while the energies (
17
) and (
18
) are
L^{1}
-norms (see Lemma
3.1
).

The following lemma is an application of the divergence theorem to the Klein–Gordon equation with an inhomogeneous term.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi
and
h
be regular functions satisfying
(\Box-1)\phi=h
defined in
\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}e(\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}=\int_{H_{1}}e(\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}-\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}h\partial_{t}\phi\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda.

Proof.

Divergence of the energy-momentum tensor for the Klein–Gordon field is given by

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}T^{\rm KG}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)+(\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\left(2(\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\kappa}\phi)(\partial_{\kappa}\phi)+2\phi(\partial^{\mu}\phi)\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=(\phi+h)\partial_{\nu}\phi-\phi(\partial_{\nu}\phi)

\displaystyle=h(\partial_{\nu}\phi).

Integrating the zeroth component over
\bigcup_{1\leq\lambda\leq\tau}H_{\lambda}
we obtain from the right hand side

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}h(\partial_{t}\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda,

and from the left hand side

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\partial^{\mu}T^{\rm KG}_{\mu 0}\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{\tau}}T^{\rm KG}_{\mu 0}(\nu_{\tau})^{\mu}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}+\int_{H_{1}}T^{\rm KG}_{\mu 0}(\nu_{1})^{\mu}d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{\tau}}e(\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}+\int_{H_{1}}e(\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{1}},

where
\nu_{\tau}
and
\nu_{1}
are the future unit normals to
H_{\tau}
and
H_{1}
, respectively. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Klainerman–Sobolev inequalities

In order to apply the vector field method to the VKG system we need to derive the Klainerman–Sobolev inequalities for the distribution function and the Klein–Gordon field. We introduce the Klainerman–Sobolev inequalities for the distribution function in Proposition
3.1
and the ones for the Klein–Gordon field in Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f
be a regular distribution function defined in
\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
for some
T\in(1,\infty]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(t,x,v)\,dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n-1+k}\tau^{1-k}}\hat{E}_{n}(f)(\tau),\qquad k=0,1,

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|f|(t,x,v)\,dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\sum_{|A|\leq n}\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}},

(22)

for
t
,
x
and
\tau
satisfying (
11
).

Proof.

We basically follow the proof of Theorem 8 of
[
8
]
. Let
(t,x)\in\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}
be fixed with
\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
and
r=|x|
, and consider the function defined by

\displaystyle\psi(y):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(\tau,x+ty,v)\,dv\qquad\mbox{for}\quad|y|\leq\frac{1}{8},

where the integrand is written in the pseudo-Cartesian coordinate system. By the one dimensional Sobolev inequality we obtain

\displaystyle|\psi(0)|\lesssim\int_{|y^{1}|\leq\frac{1}{8\sqrt{n}}}\left(|\psi|(y^{1},0,\dots,0)+|\partial_{y^{1}}\psi|(y^{1},0,\dots,0)\right)dy^{1}.

Note that the point
(\tau,x+ty)
in the pseudo-Cartesian coordinates can be written as
(s,x+ty)
in the usual Cartesian coordinate system, where

\displaystyle s=\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x+ty|^{2}},

so that we obtain by (
15
)

\displaystyle\partial_{y^{i}}\psi(y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{y^{i}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(\tau,x+ty,v)\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{s}\Omega_{0i}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(s,x+ty,v)\,dv.

Since we have for

\displaystyle s=\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x+ty|^{2}}\geq\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t,

(23)

and for

\displaystyle s=\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x+ty|^{2}}\geq|x+ty|\geq r-t|y|\geq\frac{3}{8}t,

(24)

we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{y^{1}}\psi(y)|\lesssim\left|\Omega_{01}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(s,x+ty,v)\,dv\right|.

Consequently we obtain by Lemma
3.2

\displaystyle|\psi(0)|\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq 1}\int_{|y^{1}|\leq\frac{1}{8\sqrt{n}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\left(s,x+t(y^{1},0,\dots,0),v\right)dv\,dy^{1}.

Inductively we have

\displaystyle|\psi(0)|\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq n}\int_{|y|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|(s,x+ty,v)\,dv\,dy.

By the change of variables we obtain

\displaystyle|\psi(0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\sum_{|A|\leq n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\left(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|z|^{2}},z,v\right)dv\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\tau}\sum_{|A|\leq n}\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|(\tau,z,v)\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n-1+k}\tau^{1-k}}\sum_{|A|\leq n}\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}},

where we used (
12
) and Lemma
3.1
. Note that the point
(\tau,x)
in the pseudo-Cartesian coordinates is written as
(t,x)
in the usual Cartesian coordinates. Hence we have

\displaystyle\psi(0)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(v^{0})^{k}}|f|(t,x,v)\,dv,

and this completes the proof of the first inequality. The second inequality is derived from the first inequality with
f
replaced by
v^{0}f
with
k=1
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be a regular function defined in
\bigcup_{1\leq\tau<T}H_{\tau}
for some
T\in(1,\infty]
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\phi|(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)(\tau),

(25)

\displaystyle|\partial\phi|(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)(\tau),

(26)

for

,
x
and
\tau
satisfying (
11
).

Proof.

We first note that by (

)

\displaystyle E_{m}(\phi)(\tau)\geq\frac{t}{2\tau}\sum_{|A|\leq m}\int_{H_{\tau}}|Z_{A}\phi|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}.

(27)

On the other hand, let
(t,x)
be fixed, and consider

\displaystyle\psi(y):=\phi(\tau,x+ty),

where we used the pseudo-Cartesian coordinate system for
\phi
. For simplicity, we write

\displaystyle s=\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x+ty|^{2}},

as in Proposition
3.1
, and obtain by (

) and the Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle|\psi(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\int_{|y|\leq\delta}|\partial_{y}^{k}\psi(y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\int_{|y|\leq\delta}\frac{t^{2k}}{s^{2k}}\left|Z_{A}\phi(s,x+ty)\right|^{2}dy.

In a similar way to (

)–(

\displaystyle|\phi(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\int_{|y|\leq\delta}\left|Z_{A}\phi(s,x+ty)\right|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\sum_{|A|\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|Z_{A}\phi\left(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|z|^{2}},z\right)\right|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\tau}\sum_{|A|\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\int_{H_{\tau}}|Z_{A}\phi|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

where we used (

) and (

∎

4
Commuted equations

We need to estimate higher order derivatives of the distribution function and the Klein–Gordon field in order to control the energies (
17
), (
18
) and (
19
). Recall that the Vlasov equation can be written as

\displaystyle T_{\phi}f=0.

Then, the equation for
\hat{Z}_{A}f
with
|A|\geq 1
will be given in the form of

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f=g,

where we need commutation formulas for
T_{\phi}
with
\hat{Z}_{A}
to find the right hand side. The Klein–Gordon equation can be written as

\displaystyle(\Box-1)\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f\,dv.

It is well-known that the operator
\Box-1
commutes with
Z_{A}
(for instance see
[
16
,
17
]
), but we already have an inhomogeneous term on the right hand side, so that we will obtain

\displaystyle(\Box-1)Z_{A}\phi=h,

where we need to use Lemma
3.2
to find the right hand side.

In this section we will derive the equations for
\hat{Z}_{A}f
and
Z_{A}\phi
. In Section
4.1
we will consider the commutation formulas for the transport operator
T_{\phi}
with the vector fields
\hat{Z}_{A}
for
|A|\geq 1
to derive the equation for
\hat{Z}_{A}f
. In Section
4.2
we apply Lemma
3.2
to obtain the equation for
Z_{A}\phi
.

4.1
Vlasov equation

We need to consider the commutation formulas for the transport operator
T_{\phi}
with
\hat{Z}_{A}
in order to derive the equation for
\hat{Z}_{A}f
. Let us first consider the commutation formulas for the free transport operator
T
with single vector fields
\hat{Z}_{a}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
. One can easily obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following commutation rules hold:

1.

For any
\hat{Z}_{a}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
, the free transport operator
T
satisfies

\displaystyle\left[T,\hat{Z}_{a}\right]=0.

2.

For any
\hat{Z}_{a},\hat{Z}_{b}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
, there exist constant coefficients
C_{ab}^{c}
such that

\displaystyle\left[\hat{Z}_{a},\hat{Z}_{b}\right]=\sum_{\hat{Z}_{c}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}}C_{ab}^{c}\hat{Z}_{c}.

3.

Concerning the derivatives
\partial_{v^{i}}
we have

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\partial}_{t},\partial_{v^{i}}\right]=0,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\partial}_{x^{i}},\partial_{v^{j}}\right]=0,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Omega}_{0i},\partial_{v^{j}}\right]=-\frac{v^{j}}{v^{0}}\partial_{v^{i}},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Omega}_{ij},\partial_{v^{k}}\right]=-\delta^{i}_{k}\partial_{v^{j}}+\delta^{j}_{k}\partial_{v^{i}},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Omega}_{0i},v^{j}\partial_{v^{j}}\right]=\frac{1}{v^{0}}\partial_{v^{i}},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Omega}_{ij},v^{k}\partial_{v^{k}}\right]=0,

for any
i,j,k=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

The lemma is proved by direct computations. We refer to
[
8
,
27
]
for more details.
∎

It is more involved to derive commutation formulas for the perturbed transport operator
T_{\phi}
with
\hat{Z}_{A}
of order
|A|\geq 1
. For the simplicity of notations let us denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
the sets of polynomials defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{the set of polynomials of $v^{1}/v^{0}$, $v^{2}/v^{0}$ and $v^{3}/v^{0}$,}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{the set of polynomials in the form of $P_{0}+tP_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{k}P_{k+1}$ with $P_{0},\dots,P_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{P}}$.}

In other words,
{\mathcal{P}}
is the set of polynomials of
v^{j}/v^{0}
with constant coefficients, while
{\mathcal{Q}}
is the set of first order polynomials of
t
and
x^{j}
with coefficients in
{\mathcal{P}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\hat{Z}_{a}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{a}\right]=v^{0}\sum_{|B|\leq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\mu\leq n\\
1\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}P^{\mu Bi}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\partial_{v^{i}},

(28)

for some
P^{\mu Bi}_{a}\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

Recall that
T_{\phi}=T-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}
. By Lemma
4.1
we only need to consider

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{a}\right]=\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\hat{Z}_{a}\right].

Since
\hat{\partial}_{t}=\partial_{t}
and
\hat{\partial}_{x^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}}
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\hat{\partial}_{t}\right]=v^{0}\nabla_{x}(\partial_{t}\phi)\cdot\nabla_{v},

(29)

\displaystyle\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\hat{\partial}_{x^{i}}\right]=v^{0}\nabla_{x}(\partial_{x^{i}}\phi)\cdot\nabla_{v}.

(30)

For
\hat{Z}_{a}=\hat{\Omega}_{ij}
, we compute as follows:

\displaystyle\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\hat{\Omega}_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\Omega_{ij}\right]+\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},v^{i}\partial_{v^{j}}-v^{j}\partial_{v^{i}}\right]

\displaystyle=v^{0}\Omega_{ij}(\nabla_{x}\phi)\cdot\nabla_{v}-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\left((\nabla_{v}v^{i})\partial_{v^{j}}-(\nabla_{v}v^{j})\partial_{v^{i}}\right)+\left(v^{i}\frac{v^{j}}{v^{0}}-v^{j}\frac{v^{i}}{v^{0}}\right)\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}

\displaystyle=v^{0}\nabla_{x}(\Omega_{ij}\phi)\cdot\nabla_{v}.

(31)

Similarly, for
\hat{Z}_{a}=\hat{\Omega}_{0i}
, we have

\displaystyle\left[-v^{0}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v},\hat{\Omega}_{0i}\right]

\displaystyle=v^{0}\nabla_{x}(\Omega_{0i}\phi)\cdot\nabla_{v}-v^{0}(\partial_{t}\phi)\partial_{v^{i}}-(v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi)\partial_{v^{i}}+v^{i}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}.

(32)

All the quantities (
29
)–(
32
) can be written as in (
28
) for some polynomials
P^{\mu Bi}_{a}\in{\mathcal{P}}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\hat{Z}_{a}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{a}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|\leq 1\\
|C|=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C},

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{a}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Proof.

Concerning the derivatives
\partial_{v^{i}}
we can use the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{i}}=\frac{1}{v^{0}}\hat{\Omega}_{0i}-\frac{1}{v^{0}}(t\partial_{x^{i}}+x^{i}\partial_{t}).

(33)

Note that
\hat{\Omega}_{0i}
,
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{x^{i}}
belong to
\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}
. Now, we apply (
33
) to Lemma
4.2
to obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\hat{Z}_{A}\in\hat{{\mathbb{P}}}^{|A|}
with
|A|\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{A}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C},

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Proof.

The lemma holds in the case
|A|=1
by Lemma
4.3
. Suppose that the lemma holds for some
|A|=k
, and compute

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{a}\hat{Z}_{A}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{a}\right]\hat{Z}_{A}+\hat{Z}_{a}\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{A}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|\leq 1\\
|C|=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}\right)\hat{Z}_{A}+\hat{Z}_{a}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|\leq 1\\
|C|=|A|+1\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\hat{Z}_{a}\left(Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}\right),

where
Q^{\mu BC}_{a}
and
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}
are polynomials in
{\mathcal{Q}}
, which may change from line to line. Let us compute the
\hat{Z}_{a}
derivative in the second term. Note that the polynomial
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}
can be written as

\displaystyle Q^{\mu BC}_{A}=P_{0}+tP_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{k}P_{k+1},

for some
P_{0},\dots,P_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{P}}
. It is easy to see that
\hat{Z}_{a}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{P}}
for
\hat{Z}_{a}=\partial_{t}
or
\partial_{x^{j}}
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{i}}\left(\frac{v^{j}}{v^{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{v^{0}}\left(\delta_{i}^{j}-\frac{v^{i}v^{j}}{(v^{0})^{2}}\right),

which shows that
v^{i}\partial_{v^{j}}P,v^{0}\partial_{v^{i}}P\in{\mathcal{P}}
for any
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Then, we can conclude that
\hat{Z}_{a}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
for
\hat{Z}_{a}=\hat{\Omega}_{ij}
or
\hat{\Omega}_{0i}
. Next, concerning the quantity
\hat{Z}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)
, we note that

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x^{\mu}}\hat{Z}_{a}(Z_{B}\phi)+\left[\hat{Z}_{a},\partial_{x^{\mu}}\right](Z_{B}\phi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{a}Z_{B}\phi)+c^{\nu}_{a\mu}\partial_{x^{\nu}}(Z_{B}\phi),

for some constants
c^{\nu}_{a\mu}
. Hence, we may write

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{a}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)=\sum_{|B|\leq|C|\leq|B|+1}c^{\nu C}_{a\mu}\partial_{x^{\nu}}(Z_{C}\phi),

for some constants
c^{\nu C}_{a\mu}
. Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\hat{Z}_{a}\left(Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+2\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|+1\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{aA}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C},

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{aA}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now, Lemma
4.4
shows that the evolution equation for
\hat{Z}_{A}f
with
|A|\geq 1
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}f,

(34)

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

4.2
Klein–Gordon equation

It is well-known that the Klein–Gordon operator
\Box-1
commutes with all of the members in
{\mathbb{P}}
. The equation for

can easily be derived by applying the vector fields to the both sides of the Klein–Gordon equation (

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
Z_{A}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{|A|}
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{A}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f\,dv=\sum_{|B|\leq|A|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}P^{B}\hat{Z}_{B}f\,dv,

.

for some

.

-estimate given in Section

Bootstrap assumption

Suppose that initial data is given as in (

\displaystyle<2\varepsilon,

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\tau)

\displaystyle<2\varepsilon\tau^{\delta},

(38)

36
), and
\delta\geq 0
is another small number which will be determined later. Let
[1,T)
be the largest interval on which the estimates (
37
)–(
38
) hold.

Below, in Sections
5.2
and
5.3
, we will show that the estimates (
37
)–(
38
) can be improved. Then, we can conclude that the estimates (
37
)–(
38
) hold for all
1\leq\tau<\infty
, and this completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5.2
Improvement for the Klein–Gordon field

In this section we improve the estimate (
37
). In Lemma
5.1
, we assume further that higher order derivatives of the distribution function satisfy the estimate (
39
) and obtain the improvement (
40
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that for
N-n+1\leq|A|\leq N
the distribution function
f
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{2\delta-n},

(39)

where
\delta\geq 0
is the small number given in the assumption (
38
). Then we have

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi)(\tau)\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon,

(40)

for
1\leq\tau<T
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.4
to the equation (
35
) we obtain

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi)(\tau)-E_{N}(\phi)(1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq N}\sum_{|B|\leq|A|}\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)\left|\partial_{t}Z_{A}\phi\right|\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|A|\leq N}\sum_{|B|\leq|A|}\int_{1}^{\tau}\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{\lambda}{t}|\partial_{t}Z_{A}\phi|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda.

Since
(\lambda/t)|\partial_{t}Z_{A}\phi|^{2}\lesssim e(Z_{A}\phi)
by (
20
), we have

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi)(\tau)-E_{N}(\phi)(1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|B|\leq N}\int_{1}^{\tau}\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)(\lambda)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|B|\leq N}\int_{1}^{\tau}\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda,

by the assumption (
37
). For
|B|\leq N-n
, we apply Proposition
3.1
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\lambda}\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\lambda)\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\lambda^{2\delta}}{t^{2n-2}\lambda^{2}}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\lambda^{2\delta-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+t+r)^{2n-2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\lambda^{2\delta-2}}{t^{n-2}},

where we used (
12
). Note that the last integral converges for
n\geq 3
. Since
t\geq\lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{B}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\lambda^{2\delta-n}.

For
N-n+1\leq|B|\leq N
, we use the assumption (
39
) of the lemma to obtain the same result. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{N}(\phi)(\tau)-E_{N}(\phi)(1)\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{1}^{\tau}\lambda^{\delta-\frac{n}{2}}\,d\lambda,

which is integrable for small
\delta\geq 0
and
n\geq 3
. This proves the lemma for small
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

5.3
Improvement for the distribution function

In this section we improve the estimate (
38
). Recall that the energy
\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)
defined in (
18
) has lower order derivatives with
v^{0}
-weight and higher order derivatives without the weight. We first consider the higher order derivatives in Lemma
5.2
and then the lower order derivatives in Lemma
5.3
. In Lemma
5.4
we combine the Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
to obtain the desired improvement (
44
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
|A|\geq\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\leq\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}+C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}&(n\geq 5),\\
&\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{-1}\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\end{aligned}\right.

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.3
to the equation (
34
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}-\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C}f|+|\nabla_{x}\phi||\hat{Z}_{A}f|\right)dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda.

Note that the second term in the integrand can be absorbed into the first term. Since the polynomials
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}
can be estimated as
|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}|\lesssim t
, we only need to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}t|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda.

For
|B|\leq N-\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1
, we apply Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.1
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}t|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\lambda}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(Z_{B}\phi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}E_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)\int_{H_{\lambda}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{C}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}E_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)\hat{E}_{N,1}(f).

(41)

For
|B|\geq N-\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, we notice that
|B|+|C|\leq N+1
and
N\geq 3n+2
to conclude

\displaystyle|C|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n.

Hence, we obtain by the estimate (
22
) of Proposition
3.1

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\sum_{|D|\leq n}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{D}\hat{Z}_{C}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\hat{E}_{N,1}(f),

which shows that

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}t|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)\int_{H_{\lambda}}|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|^{2}\frac{\lambda}{t}\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{1}{t^{2n-2}}\frac{t}{\lambda}\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)\left(\int_{H_{\lambda}}e(Z_{B}\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{t^{2n-2}}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)E_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)\left(\frac{1}{t^{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(42)

where we used (
20
) and (
12
), and the last inequality holds for
n\geq 3
. We combine (
41
) and (
42
) to conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}-\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{1}^{\tau}\lambda^{\delta+1-\frac{n}{2}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}(\tau^{\delta+2-\frac{n}{2}}-1)}{\delta+2-\frac{n}{2}},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
|A|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\leq\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}+C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}&(n\geq 5),\\
&\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{-1}\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\\
\end{aligned}\right.

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

Let us consider the evolution equation of
v^{0}\hat{Z}_{A}f
:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\left[v^{0}\hat{Z}_{A}f\right]=(T_{\phi}v^{0})\hat{Z}_{A}f+v^{0}T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f.

(43)

For the first term on the right hand side we notice that
T_{\phi}v^{0}=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi
. We use Proposition
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle|T_{\phi}v^{0}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau}v^{0}.

For the second term we note that the quantity
T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f
can be written as in (
34
) for
|A|\geq 1
. We now apply Lemma
3.3
to the equation (
43
) to obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}-\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|(T_{\phi}v^{0})\hat{Z}_{A}f+v^{0}T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f\right|+|\nabla_{x}\phi||v^{0}\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\lambda}v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|A|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}v^{0}|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}||\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)||\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda,

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. The first quantity is estimated by using Lemma
3.1
as

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\lambda}v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}}\hat{E}_{N,1}(f),

since
|A|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
. For the second quantity we notice that
|B|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
and

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N+n+2}{2}\right\rfloor\leq N,

since
N\geq 3n+2
. Now, we apply Proposition
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}v^{0}|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}||\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)||\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{t}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\lambda}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(Z_{B}\phi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}E_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi)\int_{H_{\lambda}}\hat{e}(v^{0}|\hat{Z}_{C}f|)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda^{\delta+1-\frac{n}{2}}.

Integrating the above quantities for
1\leq\lambda\leq\tau
as in the previous lemma we obtain the desired result for
|A|\geq 1
. For
|A|=0
, we can easily obtain the same result, since the second term on the right hand side of (
43
) vanishes, and this completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For
1\leq\tau<T
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\tau)\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\tau^{\delta},

(44)

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
for
n=4
.

Proof.

We combine Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
to obtain for
n\geq 5
,

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\tau)\leq\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(1)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Since
\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(1)<\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon>0
is small, we obtain the desired result for
n\geq 5
. For
n=4
, we choose
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
to obtain

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(\tau)\leq\hat{E}_{N,1}(f)(1)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{4}}\tau^{\delta}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is small, we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.4
L^{2}
-estimate for the distribution function

In order to complete the bootstrap arguments we need to show that the
L^{2}
-estimate (
39
) for the higher order derivatives
\hat{Z}_{A}f
of the distribution function can be derived from the bootstrap assumptions (
37
) and (
38
). Recall that the evolution equation of
\hat{Z}_{A}f
with
|A|\geq 1
is given by (
34
), which can be written as

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{A}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq|C|\leq|A|}R^{C}_{A}\hat{Z}_{C}f,

(45)

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle R^{C}_{A}=\sum_{|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi).

(46)

We can estimate the coefficients as follows.

Lemma 5.5
.

The coefficient functions
R^{C}_{A}
given by (
46
) satisfy the following estimates:

(i)

For
\lfloor N/2\rfloor-n+1\leq|A|\leq N
and
\lfloor N/2\rfloor-n+1\leq|C|\leq|A|
, we have

\displaystyle|R^{C}_{A}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}.

(ii)

For
\lfloor N/2\rfloor-n+1\leq|A|\leq N
and
1\leq|C|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor-n
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\tau}{t^{3}}|R^{C}_{A}|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

(iii)

For
1\leq|A|\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor-n
and
1\leq|C|\leq|A|
, we have

\displaystyle|R^{C}_{A}|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}.

Proof.

For
(i)
we use Proposition
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(Z_{B}\phi),

(47)

and notice that
|B|
is small in the sense that

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+|B|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+|A|+1-|C|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+N-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+n

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3n+2}{2}\right\rfloor+N-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq N,

since
N\geq 3n+2
. Hence, we can apply the bootstrap assumption (
37
) to (
47
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau}.

Since
|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}|\lesssim t
, we obtain the desired result for
(i)
. For
(ii)
we can only use (
20
) to have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|^{2}\lesssim\frac{t}{\tau}e(Z_{B}\phi).

Applying this to (
46
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\tau}{t^{3}}|R^{C}_{A}|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\tau}{t^{3}}|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}|^{2}|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)|^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|B|\leq N}\int_{H_{\tau}}e(Z_{B}\phi)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon,

since
|Q^{\mu BC}_{A}|\lesssim t
. For
(iii)
we have

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+|B|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+|A|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n\leq N,

since
N\geq 3n+2
. Hence, we obtain the same result as in the case of
(i)
.
∎

Now, we use Lemma
5.5
to rewrite the equation (
45
) as in
[
8
]
. Let
F
and
F_{0}
denote the vector-valued functions given by

\displaystyle F:=\left(\hat{Z}_{A_{1}}f,\dots,\hat{Z}_{A_{q}}f\right),\qquad F_{0}:=\left(\hat{Z}_{B_{1}}f,\dots,\hat{Z}_{B_{r}}f\right),

where
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
are all the possible multi-indices satisfying

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n+1\leq|A_{i}|\leq N,\qquad 1\leq|B_{j}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n,

with suitable orderings. Then, the equation (
45
) can be decomposed into the equations for
F
and
F_{0}
given by

\displaystyle T_{\phi}F+R_{1}F

\displaystyle=R_{2}F_{0},

(48)

\displaystyle T_{\phi}F_{0}

\displaystyle=R_{3}F_{0},

(49)

where
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
are matrix-valued functions satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{v}\|R_{1}\|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau},\qquad\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\tau}{t^{3}}\sup_{v}\|R_{2}\|^{2}\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\lesssim\varepsilon,\qquad\sup_{v}\|R_{3}\|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}.

(50)

Here,
\|\cdot\|
denotes a suitable matrix norm, for instance
\|R_{1}\|=\left(\sum_{a,b}((R_{1})^{a}_{b})^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. Below, we will show that
F
satisfies the
L^{2}
-estimate (
39
).

5.4.1
Decomposition of solution

We note that
F
satisfies the equation (
48
), where the coefficients
R_{1}
in the homogeneous part and
R_{2}
in the inhomogeneous part have different behavior as in (
50
). We decompose
F
into the homogeneous and inhomogeneous parts as follows:

\displaystyle F=F_{1}+F_{2},

where
F_{1}
is the solution of the following homogeneous equation:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}F_{1}+R_{1}F_{1}=0,

(51)

with initial data
F_{1}(1)=F(1)
, and
F_{2}
is the solution of the following inhomogeneous equation:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}F_{2}+R_{1}F_{2}=R_{2}F_{0},

(52)

with vanishing data
F_{2}(1)=0
.

5.4.2
Homogeneous part

We recall that
F
has the higher order derivatives of
f
of order up to
N
. If we want to apply Proposition
3.1
to
F
, then we need to estimate the derivatives of
F
of order up to
n
, which requires the derivatives of
f
of order up to
N+n
. The following lemma shows that if we only consider the homogeneous part
F_{1}
, then we can simply assume that the derivatives of
f
of order up to
N+n
are small at
\tau=1
to estimate the derivatives of
F_{1}
of order up to
n
.

Lemma 5.6
.

The homogeneous part
F_{1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(\tau)\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{\delta},

(53)

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
for
n=4
.

Proof.

Applying
\hat{Z}_{D}
to the equation (
51
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\hat{Z}_{D}F_{1}=-\hat{Z}_{D}\left(R_{1}F_{1}\right)+\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{D}\right]F_{1}.

(54)

Concerning the first term on the right hand side we notice that
R_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{1}=\sum_{|B|+|C|\leq|A|+1}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi),

with multi-indices
A
and
C
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n+1\leq|A|\leq N,\qquad\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-n+1\leq|C|\leq|A|.

Then, we obtain for any
|D|\leq n
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{D}Z_{B}\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau}E_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(Z_{D}Z_{B}\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\tau},

by Proposition
3.2
and the bootstrap assumption (
37
), since
N\geq 5n+2
and

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+|D|+|B|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1+n+N+1-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+n-1

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{5n+2}{2}\right\rfloor+N-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq N.

We recall that
\hat{Z}_{D}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, so that
|\hat{Z}_{D}Q^{\mu BC}_{A}|\lesssim t
, and note that
Z_{D}
commutes with
\partial_{x^{\mu}}
as in Lemma
4.1
. Now, we can conclude that for any
|D|\leq n
,

\displaystyle|\hat{Z}_{D}(R_{1}F_{1})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}\sum_{|D_{1}|\leq n}|\hat{Z}_{D_{1}}F_{1}|.

For the second term on the right hand side of (
54
) we use Lemma
4.4
to have

\displaystyle\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{D}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|B|+|C|\leq|D|+1\\
1\leq|C|\leq|D|\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq n}Q^{\mu BC}_{D}\partial_{x^{\mu}}(Z_{B}\phi)\hat{Z}_{C},

for some
Q^{\mu BC}_{D}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Since
|D|\leq n
, we obtain by the same arguments as above

\displaystyle\left|\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{D}\right]F_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}\sum_{|D_{1}|\leq n}|\hat{Z}_{D_{1}}F_{1}|.

Now, we apply Lemma
3.3
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{D}F_{1}|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}-\int_{H_{1}}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{D}F_{1}|)\,d\mu_{H_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{D}(R_{1}F_{1})|+\left|\left[T_{\phi},\hat{Z}_{D}\right]F_{1}\right|+|\nabla_{x}\phi||\hat{Z}_{D}F_{1}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{\tau}\int_{H_{\lambda}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\lambda}\sum_{|D_{1}|\leq n}\hat{e}(|\hat{Z}_{D_{1}}F_{1}|)\,d\mu_{H_{\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})\,d\lambda,

which implies that

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(\tau)\leq\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(1)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(55)

Since
F_{1}(1)=F(1)
and
\hat{E}_{n}(F)(1)\lesssim\hat{E}_{N+n}(f)(1)<\varepsilon
, we can find
C_{1}>0
and
\tau_{1}>1
such that

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(1)\leq C_{1}\varepsilon,\qquad\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(\tau)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &2C_{1}\varepsilon&(n\geq 5),\\
&2C_{1}\varepsilon\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\end{aligned}\right.

(56)

for
1\leq\tau<\tau_{1}
. Then, the inequality (
55
) implies that

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{n}(F_{1})(\tau)\leq C_{1}\varepsilon+C\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}&(n\geq 5),\\
&C_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{-1}\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\end{aligned}\right.

for
1\leq\tau<\tau_{1}
. We take
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
to conclude that (
56
) holds for all
\tau\geq 1
.
∎

5.4.3
Inhomogeneous part

To estimate the inhomogeneous part
F_{2}
we introduce the matrix-valued function
K
satisfying

\displaystyle T_{\phi}K+R_{1}K+KR_{3}=R_{2},

(57)

with vanishing data
K(1)=0
. We notice that
K
is a
q\times r
matrix and
F_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle F_{2}=KF_{0}.

Below, we will need to estimate the following quantity:

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{K}:=\int_{H_{\tau}}\hat{e}(\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}},

which is the same as
\hat{E}_{0}(\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|)
by the definition of (
17
), but we will use the notation
\hat{E}_{K}
for simplicity. In the following lemma, we will estimate the quantity
\hat{E}_{K}
by using the properties of
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
of (
50
) in a suitable way.

Lemma 5.7
.

The quantity
\hat{E}_{K}
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{K}(\tau)\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{\delta},

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
for
n=4
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is basically the same as the proof of Lemma
5.6
. For each
a
and
b
, we consider the evolution equation of
(K^{a}_{b})^{2}F_{0}
:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}((K^{a}_{b})^{2}F_{0})=-2(R_{1}K)^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}-2(KR_{3})^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}+2(R_{2})^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}+(K^{a}_{b})^{2}R_{3}F_{0},

by the equations (
57
) and (
49
). Here, the repeated indices are not summed over. In order to apply Lemma
3.3
, we need to estimate the right hand side. For the terms having
R_{1}
or
R_{3}
, we apply (
50
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}2|(R_{1}K)^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}|+2|(KR_{3})^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}|+(K^{a}_{b})^{2}|R_{3}F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\tau}\hat{e}(\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|)\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{K}.

For the terms having
R_{2}
, we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}2|(R_{2})^{a}_{b}K^{a}_{b}F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}((R_{2})^{a}_{b})^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(K^{a}_{b})^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The first integration on the right hand side is estimated by using (
50
),

\displaystyle\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}((R_{2})^{a}_{b})^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\sup_{v}\|R_{2}\|^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\sup_{v}\|R_{2}\|^{2}\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\tau}\hat{E}_{n}(F_{0})\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\tau}\sup_{v}\|R_{2}\|^{2}\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{E}_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(F_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(f),

and the second integration is simply estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{H_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(K^{a}_{b})^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{K}.

Now, we apply Lemma
3.3
and consider all the possible indices
a
and
b
to obtain

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{K}(\tau)\lesssim\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{K}(\lambda)+\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\hat{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(f)(\lambda)\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{K}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,

(58)

since
K(1)=0
. We can find
C_{2}>0
and
\tau_{2}>1
satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{K}(\tau)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{2}\varepsilon&(n\geq 5),\\
&C_{2}\varepsilon\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\end{aligned}\right.

(59)

for
1\leq\tau<\tau_{2}
. Then, the inequality (
58
) together with Lemma
5.4
implies that

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{K}(\tau)\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}\,d\lambda\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}&(n\geq 5),\\
&\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1-\delta}}\,d\lambda\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{-1}\tau^{\delta}&(n=4),\end{aligned}\right.

for
1\leq\tau\leq\tau_{2}
. We now take
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
to conclude that (
59
) holds for all
\tau\geq 1
.
∎

5.4.4
Proof of the
L^{2}
-estimate

We combine Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
to prove the
L^{2}
-estimate (
39
) for the distribution function. We note that Lemma
5.6
together with Proposition
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F_{1}|\,dv\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{n-1}\tau^{1-\delta}},

(60)

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
for
n=4
. Now, we obtain the
L^{2}
-estimate in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
N-n+1\leq|A|\leq N
, the distribution function satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{Z}_{A}f|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{2\delta-n},

where
\delta=0
for
n\geq 5
, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/4}
for
n=4
.

Proof.

We notice that it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{2\delta-n},

and we consider
F=F_{1}+F_{2}
separately. The homogeneous part
F_{1}
is estimated by using (
60
) with (
12
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F_{1}|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{n-1}\tau^{1-\delta}}\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\tau^{2-2\delta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{t^{2n-2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\tau^{2-2\delta}t^{n-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\tau^{n-2\delta}}.

Similarly, the inhomogeneous part
F_{2}=KF_{0}
is estimated by using Lemma
5.7
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F_{2}|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|K\||F_{0}|\,dv\right)^{2}d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|\,dv\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|F_{0}|\,dv\right)d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{H_{\tau}}\frac{t}{\tau}\hat{e}(\|K\|^{2}|F_{0}|)\frac{1}{t^{n-1}\tau}\hat{E}_{n}(F_{0})\,d\mu_{H_{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}\hat{E}_{K}\hat{E}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\tau^{n-2\delta}},

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎
