A note about exponential tractability of linear weighted tensor product problems in the worst-case setting
Zirong Liu, Heping Wang, Kai Wang

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to discussing the weighted linear tensor product problems in the worst case setting. We consider algorithms that use finitely many evaluations of arbitrary continuous linear functionals. We investigate exponential ( s , t ) (s,t) -weak tractability (EXP- ( s , t ) (s,t) -WT) with max ⁡ ( s , t ) < 1 \max(s,t)<1 and exponential uniform weak tractability (EXP-UWT) under the absolute or normalized error criterion. We solve the problem by filling the remaining gaps left open on EXP-tractability. That is, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for EXP- ( s , t ) (s,t) -WT with max ⁡ ( s , t ) < 1 \max(s,t)<1 and for EXP-UWT.



1.
Introduction and main results

We study
d
-variate linear problem
S=\{S_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
, where
S_{d}
is a linear operator from a Banach function space
H_{d}
into another Banach function space
G_{d}
,
d
may be large even huge. We consider
algorithms that use finitely many continuous linear functionals on

H_{d}
. The information complexity
n(\varepsilon,S_{d})
is defined to be
the minimal number of linear functionals needed to find an
approximation of
S_{d}
to within an error threshold
\varepsilon
.
Tractability is the study of how information complexity
n(\varepsilon,S_{d})
depends on
\varepsilon
and
d
. There are two kinds of
tractability based on polynomial convergence and exponential
convergence. The algebraic tractability (ALG-tractability)
describes how
n(\varepsilon,d)
behaves as a function of
d
and

\varepsilon^{-1}
, while the EXP-tractability does as one of
d
and

(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})
. Recently the study of ALG-tractability and
EXP-tractability has attracted much interest, and a great number
of interesting results
have been obtained (see

[
1
,
3
,

,
5
,
7
,

,
10
,
11
,

,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and the references therein).

This paper is devoted to discussing EXP-tractability of linear weighted
tensor product problems in the worst case setting. For unweighted
linear tensor product problems, the results about the
EXP-tractability were obtained in
[
6
,
13
,
14
]
.
However,
there are many negative results for exponential tractability. Hence the authors in

[
9
]
introduced weighted

weights and univariate singular values. Note that in
[
9
]

the cases of EXP-
(s,t)
-weak tractability (EXP-
(s,t)
-WT) with

In this paper we investigate EXP-
(s,t)

1.1.
Weighted linear tensor product problems.

Let
H_{1}
be a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with
inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{1}}
. We consider a
compact linear operator
S_{1}:H_{1}\to G_{1}
, where
G_{1}
is also a
Hilbert space. Then
W_{1}:=S_{1}^{*}S_{1}:H_{1}\to H_{1}
is a compact
self-adjoint non-negative operator, where
T^{*}
is the adjoint
operator of a linear operator
T
. Let
\{(\lambda_{j},e_{j})\}_{j\in{N}}
denote the eigenpairs of
W_{1}
, that is,

W_{1}e_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},\ e_{j}\in H_{1},\ \ \lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0,

and
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in{N}}
is an orthonormal basis in
H_{1}
.
Without loss of generality we assume that
\lambda_{1}=1
and

\lambda_{2}>0
in the sequel. Due to compactness of
S_{1}
we have

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=0
. Denote

H_{1,0}={\rm span}\,\{e_{1}\},\ \ H_{1,1}=H_{1,0}^{\bot}.

Then
H_{1}=H_{1,0}\oplus H_{1,1}.
Hence for each
f\in H_{1}
, we have the
unique orthogonal decomposition
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
, where
f_{0}\in H_{1,0},\ f_{1}\in H_{1,1}
.

For
\gamma>0
, we define the weighted Hilbert space
H_{1,\gamma}
to be the Hilbert space
H_{1}
with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{H_{1,\gamma}}:=\langle f_{0},g_{0}\rangle_{H_{1}}+\gamma^{-1}\langle f_{1},g_{1}\rangle_{H_{1}}.

And the weighted operator
S_{1,\gamma}
is defined to be the
operator
S_{1}
from the weighted Hilbert space
H_{1,\gamma}
to

G_{1}
. It follows that the operator

W_{1,\gamma}=S_{1,\gamma}^{*}S_{1,\gamma}:H_{1,\gamma}\to H_{1,\gamma}
is a compact linear operator and the eigenpairs of
the operator
W_{1,\gamma}
are
\{(\lambda_{\gamma,j},e_{j})\}_{j\in{N}}
, where

(1.1)

\lambda_{\gamma,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{ if }j=1,\\
\gamma\lambda_{j},&\mbox{ if }j\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

Let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of
non-increasing positive reals, which we refer to as weights in the
following. For simplicity we assume that
\gamma_{j}\in(0,1]
. For

d\in{N}
, the tensor product spaces
H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
and
G_{d}
are denoted by

H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=H_{1,\gamma_{1}}\otimes H_{1,\gamma_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes H_{1,\gamma_{d}},\ \ G_{d}=G_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes G_{1},

respectively, the tensor product operator
S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\to G_{d}
is represented by

S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=S_{1,\gamma_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes S_{1,\gamma_{d}}.

Clearly,

W_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:=S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{*}S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\to H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
is a compact linear operator and the eigenpairs
of
W_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
are

\{(\lambda_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bf j}},e_{{\bf j}})\}_{{\bf j}\in{N}^{d}}
, where

\lambda_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bf j}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k,j_{k}},\ \ \ \ e_{{\bf j}}=e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{d}}.

\lambda_{k,j_{k}}:=\lambda_{\gamma_{k},j_{k}}
is defined as in (
1.1
).

In the following, we write
[d]:=\{1,2,\cdots,d\}.
For
{\bf j}=(j_{1},\cdots,j_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, define

\mathfrak{u}({\bf j}):=\{k\in[d]:j_{k}\geq 2\}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \lambda_{d,{\bf j}}:=\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{d}}.

The eigenvalues of
W_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
can also be written as

\lambda_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bf j}}:=\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}({\bf j})}\lambda_{d,{\bf j}}=\left(\prod_{k=1\atop j_{k}\geq 2}^{d}\gamma_{k}\right)\lambda_{j_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{d}}=\left(\prod_{k\in\mathfrak{u}({\bf j})}\gamma_{k}\right)\lambda_{d,{\bf j}}.

See
[
10
, Section 5.3]
or
[
9
]
.

If
\gamma_{j}\equiv 1
, the weighted tensor product space

H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
recedes to the tensor product space

H_{d}=H_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{1}
, and the weighted linear
tensor product operator
S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
reduces to the linear
tensor product operator
S_{d}=S_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes S_{1}
.

Now consider the linear weighted tensor product problem

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\{S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\}_{d\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. We want to
approximate
S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
by algorithms
A_{d,n}(f)
of the
form

(1.2)

A_{d,n}(f)=\phi_{d,n}(L_{1}(f),L_{2}(f),\cdots,L_{n}(f)),

where
L_{1},\cdots,L_{n}
belong to
\Lambda_{d}^{\rm all}=H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{*}
, the space consisting of all continuous
linear functionals on
H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and
\phi_{d,n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to G_{d}
is an arbitrary mapping. The choice of
L_{j}
can be
adaptive, i.e., it may depend on the previously computed
information
L_{1}(f),L_{2}(f),\cdots,L_{j-1}(f)
. The worst case
error of an algorithm
A_{d,n}
is defined as

e(A_{d,n})=\sup_{f\in H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\atop\|f\|_{H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}\leq 1}\|S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(f)-A_{d,n}(f)\|_{G_{d}}.

The
n
-th minimal worst-case error is defined by

e(n,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=\inf_{A_{d,n}}e(A_{d,n}),

where the infimum is taken over all algorithms of the form (
1.2
). When
n=0
, the initial error
e_{0}
is denoted by

e_{0}=\sup_{f\in H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\atop\|f\|_{H_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}\leq 1}\|S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(f)\|_{G_{d}}.

Clearly, we have

e_{0}=\|S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\|=\|W_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\|^{1/2}=\max_{{\bf j}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}(\lambda_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bf j}})^{1/2}=1.

Hence, the
problem is well normalized for all
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all product
weights
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. The information complexity for the normalized
or absolute error criterion is defined by

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=\min\{n\,:\,e(n,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq\varepsilon\}.

It is known that for the class
\Lambda_{d}^{\rm all}
the
information complexity is fully characterized in terms of the
singular values of
S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, or equivalently, in terms of
the eigenvalues of
W_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{*}S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}

(see
[
10
]
).

Denote

A_{\varepsilon,d}=\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}:\lambda_{1,n_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d,n_{d}}>\varepsilon^{2}\}.

Then the
information complexity can be rewritten as

(1.3)

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=\bigg|\Big\{{\bf j}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}:\lambda_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bf j}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k,j_{k}}=\lambda_{1,j_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d,j_{d}}>\varepsilon^{2}\Big\}\bigg|=\big|A_{\varepsilon,d}\big|,

where
|A|
denotes the number of the elements in a set
A
.
Clearly,

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq n(\varepsilon_{1},S_{d_{1},\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\ \ \ \mbox{for all $\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon$ and $d_{1}\geq d$}.

Hence, for decreasing
\varepsilon
and increasing
d
, the information complexity
is non-increasing.

1.2.
Various notions of EXP-tractability

In this subsection we briefly recall the basic
EXP-tractability notions. We are interested in the behavior of

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})
when
d+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}
goes to
infinity in an arbitrary way. We define various notions of EXP
tractability.

The problem
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\{S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\}
is said to be:

•

Exponentially strongly polynomially
tractable (EXP-SPT)
if there exist
C,p\geq 0
such that

(1.4)

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq C(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{p},\ \ \ \ \forall d\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \forall\varepsilon\in(0,1].

The infimum of all exponents
p\geq 0
such that
(
1.4
) holds for some
C\geq 0
is called the exponent
of EXP-SPT and is denoted by
p^{*}
.

•

Exponentially polynomially tractable (EXP-PT)
if
there exist
C,p,q\geq 0
such that

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq Cd^{\,q}(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{p},\ \ \ \ \forall d\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \forall\varepsilon\in(0,1].

•

Exponentially quasi-polynomially tractable
(EXP-QPT)
if there exist
C,t\geq 0
such that

(1.5)

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln d)(1+\ln(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}))),\ \ \ \ \forall d\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \forall\varepsilon\in(0,1].

The infimum of all exponents
t\geq 0
such that
(
1.5
) holds for some
C\geq 0
is called the exponent
of EXP-QPT and is denoted by
t^{*}
.

•

Exponentially
(s,t)
-weakly tractable (EXP-
(s,t)
-WT)

for fixed positive
s
and
t
if

\lim_{d+\varepsilon^{-1}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})}{d^{t}+(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}}=0.

If
s=t=1
, we speak of

exponential weak tractability (EXP-WT)
.

•

Exponentially uniformly weakly tractable (EXP-UWT)
if
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds for all positive
s
and
t
.

1.3.
Main results

In the worst case setting, for the (unweighted) linear tensor
product problem, necessary and sufficient conditions on
univariate singular values under which we can or cannot achieve
different notions of exponential tractability were obtained in

[
6
,
13
,
14
]
. However, we have many negative results
for EXP-tractability. For example, for the linear tensor product
problem, we cannot achieve EXP-WT.

For the weighted linear tensor product problems, the authors in

[
9
]
obtained necessary and sufficient conditions for
various notions of EXP-tractability on weights
\{\gamma_{j}\}
and
univariate singular values
\{(\lambda_{j})^{1/2}\}
, where in the
sequel we always assume that
\{\gamma_{j}\}
is a non-increasing
positive sequence, and
\{\lambda_{j}\}
is a non-increasing sequence
satisfying
1=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}>0
and
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=0
. See the following
EXP-tractability results of the problem
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
.

\bullet
EXP-SPT holds if and only if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}=0\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ B_{{\rm EXP-SPT}}:=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{d(\varepsilon)\ln j(\varepsilon)}{\ln\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}<\infty,

where

(1.6)

j(\varepsilon)=\max\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}:\lambda_{j}>\varepsilon^{2}\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ d(\varepsilon)=\max\{d\in{\mathbb{N}}:\gamma_{d}\,>\,\varepsilon^{2}\}.

If this holds then the exponent of EXP-SPT is
p^{*}=B_{{\rm EXP-SPT}}
.

\bullet
EXP-SPT and EXP-PT are equivalent.

\bullet
EXP-QPT holds
if and only if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}=0\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ B_{{\rm EXP-QPT}}:=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{d(\varepsilon)\ln j(\varepsilon)}{[\ln d(\varepsilon)]\ln\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}<\infty.

If this holds then the exponent of EXP-QPT is
t^{*}=B_{{\rm EXP-QPT}}
.

\bullet
Let
s=t=1
. EXP-WT holds if and only if

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{j}=0\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s=1
and
t<1
.
EXP-
(1,t)
-WT holds if and only if

(1.7)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}}{\ln j}=\infty\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s=1
and
t>1
.
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds if and only if

\mbox{$\gamma_{j}$'s are arbitrary}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s>1
,
t\leq 1
and
\lambda_{2}<1
.
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds if and only if

\mbox{$\gamma_{j}$'s are arbitrary}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}})^{s}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s>1
,
t\leq 1
and
\lambda_{2}=1
.
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds if and only if

\exists\,p\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \gamma_{p}<1\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}})^{s}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s>1
and
t>1
.
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds if and only if

\mbox{$\gamma_{j}$'s are arbitrary}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}})^{s}}{\ln j}=\infty.

\bullet
Let
s<1
and
t>1
.
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds for arbitrary
\gamma_{j}
’s if and only if

(1.8)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\left(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\eta}}{\ln j}=\infty\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \eta=\frac{s(t-1)}{t-s}.

\bullet
Let
s<1
and
t=1
.
EXP-
(s,1)
-WT holds if and only if for arbitrary integers

d,k,j
with
j\geq 2
and
k\leq d
it is true that

(1.9)

\lim_{d+\gamma_{k}^{-d}\lambda_{j}^{-d}\to\infty}\frac{\left(\ln\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\right)^{s}+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}_{j}\right)^{s}}{d^{1-s}\ln j}=\infty.

However, the authors in
[
9
]
did not find out sufficient and
necessary conditions on
\{\gamma_{j}\}
and
\{\lambda_{j}\}
for
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT with
\max(s,t)<1
and EXP-UWT. In this paper, we
solve the problem by filling the remaining gaps on
EXP-tractability. That is, we obtain necessary and sufficient
conditions for EXP-
(s,t)
-WT with
\max(s,t)<1
(see Theorem

1.1
) and for EXP-UWT (see Theorem
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<s<1
and
0<t\leq 1
. EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds if and only if

(1.10)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\ln\ln j(\varepsilon)}{\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}=0

and

(1.11)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{d(\varepsilon)^{1-s}\ln j(\varepsilon)}{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}}=0.

From Theorem
1.1
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
0<s<1
and
0<t\leq 1
.

(1) If EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds, then we have

(1.12)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}})}{\ln(\ln j)}=\infty

and

(1.13)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}}{j^{\frac{1-s}{s}}}=\infty;

(2) if (
1.12
) holds and

(1.14)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}}{j^{\frac{1-s}{s}+\delta}}=\infty

holds for some
\delta>0
, then EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds.

We remark that if
\lambda_{j}=0
for some
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then (
1.12
) holds.

Theorem 1.3
.

EXP-UWT holds if and only if (
1.12
) holds and

(1.15)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln(\ln\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}})}{\ln j}=\infty.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we give some
necessary preliminaries in the worst case setting. In Section 3,
we give the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

2.
Premilinaries

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=0
, define

(2.1)

j(\varepsilon)=\max\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}:\lambda_{j}>\varepsilon^{2}\}.

Then
j(\varepsilon)
is well defined and always finite. We put
j(\varepsilon)=0

for
\varepsilon\geq 1
. Since
\lambda_{1}=1
, we have
j(\varepsilon)\geq 1
for

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Furthermore,
j(\varepsilon)
goes
to infinity if and only if all
\lambda_{j}
’s are positive.

We also assume that the weights satisfy

(2.2)

1\geq\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma_{3}\geq\dots>0.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define

d(\varepsilon)=\sup\{d\in{\mathbb{N}}:\gamma_{d}\,>\,\varepsilon^{2}\}.

Then
d(\varepsilon)
is well defined. We put
d(\varepsilon)=0
for

\gamma_{1}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
, and note that
d(\varepsilon)\geq 1
for

\gamma_{1}>\varepsilon^{2}
. Both
j(\varepsilon)
and
d(\varepsilon)
are non-decreasing,
and
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}d(\varepsilon)=\infty
. If

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}=0
, then
d(\varepsilon)<\infty
for any

\varepsilon>0
.

The following lemma shows how the information complexity can be
bounded in terms of
j(\varepsilon)
and
d(\varepsilon)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(See
[
9
, Lemma 1]
)

If

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}=0
,
then for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq j(\varepsilon)^{\min(d,d(\varepsilon))}\leq n(\varepsilon^{2d(\varepsilon)},S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}),

and for
d\geq d(\varepsilon)
,

n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})=n(\varepsilon,S_{d(\varepsilon),\boldsymbol{\gamma}}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a non-increasing sequence and
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{j}=0
. Then (
1.10
) holds if and only if (
1.12
) holds if and only if

(2.3)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\ln j(\varepsilon)}{(\ln{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{\delta}}=0

holds for any
\delta>0
.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{j}=0
for
some
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
\lambda_{j}=0
for
j\geq j_{0}
and hence

j(\varepsilon)\leq j_{0}
. It follows that (
1.10
), (
1.12
) and (
2.3
) hold in this case. Without loss of generality, we
assume that
\{\lambda_{j}\}
is a positive sequence.

We first show that (
1.10
) implies (
1.12
). Assume that
(
1.10
) holds. For every
\eta\in(0,1)
, there exists an

\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{\ln\ln j(\varepsilon)}{\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}<\eta\ \ \ {\rm for}\ \varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}).

This implies

j(\varepsilon)<e^{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{\eta}}\leq\lceil e^{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{\eta}}\rceil:=v(\varepsilon),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the ceiling of a real number
x
. From the definition of
j(\varepsilon)
, we
have
j(\varepsilon)+1\leq v(\varepsilon)
and

\lambda_{v(\varepsilon)}\leq\lambda_{j(\varepsilon)+1}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

Let
v(\varepsilon)=k
. If we vary
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, then
k
can take the
values

k=\lceil e^{(\ln\varepsilon_{0}^{-1})^{\eta}}\rceil,\lceil e^{(\ln\varepsilon_{0}^{-1})^{\eta}}\rceil+1,\cdots.

Since
k\leq e^{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{\eta}}+1
, we get

\varepsilon^{2}\leq e^{-2\big(\ln(k-1)\big)^{1/\eta}}.

Hence,

\lambda_{k}\leq\varepsilon^{2}\leq e^{-2\big(\ln(k-1)\big)^{1/\eta}}\ \ \ {\rm for}\ k\geq\lceil e^{(\ln\varepsilon_{0}^{-1})^{\eta}}\rceil.

Taking the logarithm yields

\frac{\ln\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}}{\ln\ln k}\geq\frac{\ln 2+\frac{1}{\eta}\ln\ln(k-1)}{\ln\ln k}\to\infty\ \ \ {\rm as\ }k\to\infty,\ \eta\to 0.

Therefore, we obtain (
1.12
).

Next, if
(
1.12
)
holds, then for every
\delta\in(0,s)
there exists a

J>0
such that

Lemma 2.3

Lemma 2.8

\displaystyle\leq\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)\ln\frac{e\,|\rho(i)|}{\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)}\lesssim\frac{\ln\varepsilon^{-2}}{2^{(i-1)\frac{1-s}{s}}h(2^{i-1})}\ln\frac{e\,|\rho(i)|}{\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ln\varepsilon^{-2}}{2^{(i-1)\frac{1-s}{s}}h(2^{l_{0}})}\Big(i-l_{0}+\ln\frac{e\,i}{l_{0}}\Big).

Going back to (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\ \ln n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\ln C_{|\rho(i)|}^{\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)}+\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)\ln j(\varepsilon)\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{l_{0}}\Big(\ln C_{|\rho(i)|}^{\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)}+\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)\ln j(\varepsilon)\Big)+\sum_{i=l_{0}+1}^{\infty}\Big(\ln C_{|\rho(i)|}^{\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)}+\mathaccent 869{a}_{i}(\varepsilon)\ln j(\varepsilon)\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{l_{0}}2^{i}\ln j(\varepsilon)+\sum_{i=l_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}{2^{(i-1)\frac{1-s}{s}}h(2^{l_{0}})}\Big(i-l_{0}+\ln\frac{e\,i}{l_{0}}+\ln j(\varepsilon)\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{l_{0}}\ln j(\varepsilon)+\frac{\ln\varepsilon^{-2}}{2^{(l_{0}-1)\frac{1-s}{s}}h(2^{l_{0}})}\big(1+\ln j(\varepsilon)\big)\lesssim 2^{l_{0}}\ln j(\varepsilon).

Now we want to show that

2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}}\lesssim\mathaccent 869{d}(\varepsilon)\leq d(\varepsilon),
where

\mathaccent 869{d}(\varepsilon):=\max\{j:\mathaccent 869{\gamma}_{j}>\varepsilon^{2}\}\leq\max\{j:{\gamma}_{j}>\varepsilon^{2}\}=:d(\varepsilon).

Note that

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{d}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\max\{j:\ln\frac{1}{\mathaccent 869{\gamma}_{j}\lambda_{2}}<\ln\varepsilon^{-2}-\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\}

\displaystyle=\max\{j:j^{\frac{1-s}{s}}h(j)<\ln\varepsilon^{-2}-\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\}.

Choose a sufficiently large positive number
c_{0}>1
, such that for
sufficient small
\varepsilon>0
,

\ln\Big(\mathaccent 869{\gamma}_{2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}-c_{0}}}\lambda_{2}\Big)^{-1}=\big(2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}-c_{0}}\big)^{\frac{1-s}{s}}h\big(2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}-c_{0}}\big).

From (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\ln\Big(\mathaccent 869{\gamma}_{2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}-c_{0}}}\lambda_{2}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{l_{0}}{s}}\cdot 2^{-c_{0}\cdot\frac{1-s}{s}}\cdot h(2^{l_{0}})\cdot\frac{\ln\big(1+2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}}-c_{0}\big)}{\ln(2^{l_{0}}+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{l_{0}}{s}}h(2^{l_{0}})\cdot 2^{-c_{0}\cdot\frac{1-s}{s}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln\varepsilon^{-2}<\ln\varepsilon^{-2}-\ln\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}.

It implies that

2^{\frac{l_{0}}{1-s}-c_{0}}\leq\mathaccent 869{d}(\varepsilon)\leq d(\varepsilon),\ \ {\rm or,\ equivalently}\ \ \ 2^{l_{0}}\lesssim d(\varepsilon)^{1-s}.

Then by (
1.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}\frac{\ln n(\varepsilon,S_{d,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})}{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}\lesssim\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}\to\infty}\frac{2^{l_{0}}\ln j(\varepsilon)}{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}\to\infty}\frac{d(\varepsilon)^{1-s}\ln j(\varepsilon)}{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}}=0.

This means that in the case of
\ln\varepsilon^{-1}\geq d^{t}
,
EXP-
(s,t)
-WT holds.

Theorem
1.1
is proved.
\hfill\Box
