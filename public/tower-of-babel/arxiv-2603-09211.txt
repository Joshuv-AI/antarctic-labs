Asymptotics for a nonstandard risk model with multivariate subexponential claims and constant interest force
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis, Hui Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, the asymptotic behavior of the entrance probability of discounted aggregate claims of a certain family of rare sets is studied, considering the finite and infinite time horizons. This multivariate risk model, driven by a common counting process, has a constant interest rate and allows the interdependence of claim vectors. For the finite time horizon, the multivariate subexponential distribution of the common claim vector and the weak dependence structure of regression dependence are used. For the infinite time horizon, the claim vector is taken from a smaller distribution class, and the weak dependence structure is more general. Both results are derived under some additional assumptions on the moments of the counting process, which is fulfilled by all inhomogeneous renewal processes and many quasi-renewal processes, respectively. Moreover, the results are specialized to the multivariate regularly varying case, where more explicit results on the asymptotic behavior of the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims are derived. At the end of the paper, the results obtained are used to study the finite and infinite time horizon ruin problems of a risk model with eventual Brownian perturbations.



Keywords: multivariate nonstandard risk model; discounted aggregate claims; ruin probability;
interdependence; finite and infinite time horizon; Brownian perturbations

Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 62P05 ; Secondary 60G70.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Model description

In this paper, we consider an insurer operating
d
-lines of business, with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, each sharing a common counting process for claims. Let the sequence of random vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represent claim vectors, where each
{\bf X}^{(i)}=\left(X_{1}^{(i)},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}^{(i)}\right)^{\top}
and
{\bf X}^{(i)}
follows a distribution defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}:=[0,\,\infty)^{d}
, the components of each vector may include zeros, but not all components can be zero. Claims arrive at times
\{\tau_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, with the convention
\tau_{0}=0
, defining a counting process

\displaystyle N(t):=\sup\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{i}\leq t\}\,,

(1.1)

for any
t\geq 0
, with
\sup\emptyset=0
by convention. The counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is assumed to be a
general counting process, with a finite mean function

\displaystyle m(t)=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i}\leq t)<\infty\,,

for any fixed
t\geq 0
. We restrict our analysis to the time interval
\Lambda:=\{t>0\;:\;m(t)>0\}
, since the probability
P({\bf D}(T)\in x\,A)
for
T\notin\Lambda
are zero.
The insurer invests their surplus in risk free investments with a constant interest rate
r\geq 0
. Therefore, the insurer’s discounted aggregated claims up to time
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
are given by

\displaystyle{\bf D}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}},\cdots,\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\right)^{\top}\,,

(1.2)

For the infinite time horizon,
T=\infty
, the discounted aggregate claims are defined similarly, with
N(\infty)=\infty
.

Our goal is to derive asymptotic estimations for the entrance probabilities of
{\bf D}(T)
and
{\bf D}(\infty)
into some rare set
x\,A
, where
A
belongs to
a broad family of sets
\mathscr{R}
as
x\to\infty
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(T)\in x\,A]\,,\qquad\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A]\,,\quad x\to\infty.

(1.3)

These probabilities are related to ruin probabilities (for corresponding ruin sets), but they can also be used in broader applications of risk management and insurance solvency. For example, in the one-dimensional subcase with
A=(1,\,\infty)
, these probabilities provide tails for discounted aggregate claims in insurance portfolios, over both finite and infinite time horizons. We review the relevant literature related to these results in the next subsection. Notably, even in the one-dimensional case where
A=(1,\,\infty)
, the main results of this paper remain novel.

Let us present the following assumption, which will hold hereafter. We remind that the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
from (
1.1
) is a general counting process with mean function
m(t)
.

Assumption 1.1
.

The claim vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are identically distributed copies of the general random vector
{\bf X}
, following a distribution
F
with support on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
. We also assume that the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
\{\tau_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent sequences.

1.2.
Overview of existing results

Our results aim to address two key objectives, which are of particular importance in actuarial practice:

(1)

Non-standard driven multivariate risk models: This refers to moving beyond standard renewal processes, or quasi-renewal processes, in risk modeling.

(2)

Interdependent claim vectors: We consider the possibility of both dependence within claim vectors and dependence across different claim vectors over time.

In terms of non-standard counting processes, we look at models that don’t necessarily follow the conventional renewal or quasi-renewal patterns. While many studies have tried to push the boundaries of traditional models in the context of one-dimensional risk models, such as those discussed in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
39
]
, these works primarily explore asymptotics for ruin probabilities in models without considering investments. Our work differs in that we build on
[
15
]
, which considered a one-dimensional, inhomogeneous Poisson-driven risk model with risk free investments
(r>0)
and derived asymptotic estimations for ruin probabilities over finite time.

In the more recent literature, attention has shifted to multivariate risk models, which are more practical for real-world applications, as seen in
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
26
]
,

[
28
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
36
]
, and others. These models often assume a renewal counting process or quasi-renewal counting processes where the inter-arrival times are identically distributed, and weak dependence is incorporated. However, few studies have dealt with more complex risk models where the counting process does not follow these standard structures, allowing for more sophisticated dependence relationships between the claim vectors.

For multivariate risk models driven by non-renewal, or non-quasi-renewal processes, the literature offers only a few contributions. For instance, in many of these papers, additional assumptions have been considered, such as the finite moment conditions for the counting process associated with the Matuszewska indices of the claim vectors. As a consequence, many subexponential claim amount distributions are not considered. Other supplementary conditions consist of specific tail conditions for the counting process and the claim amount distribution; see
[
10
]
,
[
47
]
and
[
40
]
for more details.

Motivated by these limitations, in this paper we propose two general conditions (Assumption
3.1
for the finite-time horizon and Assumption
3.2
for the infinite-time horizon) that generalize the renewal framework in several directions. In particular, our conditions encompass quasi-renewal processes as well as inhomogeneous renewal processes (that is, processes with independent but not identically distributed inter-arrival times). In addition, our conditions cover many situations in which the inter-arrival times in the counting process are neither identically distributed nor independent. Such a general framework is particularly relevant in actuarial practice. For example, inhomogeneous renewal processes (such as inhomogeneous Poisson processes) can be used to model processes with seasonal patterns. For instance, in fire insurance, the number of claims tends to increase during the summer months.

Another situation in which the renewal process seems to defect in the risk model of (
1.2
) can be described as follows. Because the
d
-lines of business share a common counting process, the occurrence of a claim in one particular insurance portfolio at time point
i
, say
X_{1}^{(i)}
, may increase the probability of future claim arrival in the other portfolios, namely the appearance of
X_{k}^{(j)}
, for
j>i
and
k=2,\,\ldots,\,d
. In order to better understand this scenario, let us assume that an insurance company has two insurance portfolios: one for car insurance and another for health insurance. Suppose that we are involved in a serious car accident and we have to pay for damage caused to the car, which is represented by
X_{1}^{(1)}
. After that, we could be asked to pay for some injuries sustained during the accident in the portfolio of health insurance and generate a sequence of claims such as
X_{2}^{(2)}\,,\;X_{2}^{(3)}\,,\;\ldots
. This scenario shows that claim arrival times could become smaller, and consequently there is a ’negative dependence’ among the inter-arrival times. Such examples highlight the need for a more general framework allowing non-independent and non-identically distributed inter-arrival times.

We now proceed to address point (2). By interdependent claim vectors, we mean that there can be both component-wise as well as vector-wise dependencies.
In Section 2, the former can be characterized via multivariate heavy-tailed distribution classes, which include the range from independence to asymptotic dependence among the components of the claim vectors. For the latter, we use weak and sufficiently general dependencies, which include independence as a special case.

From one hand side, there is a need for component-wise dependencies so as to capture the interactions between the different portfolios of the insurer, while, on the other hand, dependencies among claim vectors play an important role for the evaluation of the total amount of the losses occurring at each claim arrival time
\tau_{i}.
The example of the car insurance and health insurance policies discussed above is again relevant, but now attention is focused on the sizes of the claims rather than their arrival times.

As far as we know, only a few papers that consider continuous time risk models with interdependent claims, namely
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
29
]
, and
[
32
]
. However, these works focus exclusively on renewal risk models.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide the preliminary concepts regarding the multivariate heavy-tailed distribution classes and the dependence structures, which will be employed. In Section 3, we give the two main results, for finite and the infinite-time horizons, together with their proofs and some auxiliary lemmas. Finally in Section 4, we present an application to ruin probabilities for finite and infinite-time horizons in a risk model with a general premium process and Brownian perturbations.

x\to\infty
. All vectors are denoted in bold, and their dimension is
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For two vectors

,

,

\displaystyle{\bf x}\pm{\bf z}=(x_{1}\pm z_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{d}\pm z_{d})^{\top}\,,\;{\bf x}\odot{\bf z}=(x_{1}\,z_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{d}\,z_{d})^{\top}\,,\;y\,{\bf x}=(y\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,y\,x_{d})^{\top}\,.

The vector
{\bf 0}=(0,\,\ldots,\,0)^{\top}
denotes the origin of the axes. For two real numbers
a,\,b
, we write
a\vee b:=\max\{a,\,b\}
and

. For any set
{\mathbb{B}}\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}:=[-\infty,\,\infty]^{d}
, we denote by
{\mathbb{B}}^{c}
its complement set, by
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}}
its closed hull, by
\partial{\mathbb{B}}
its border, and by
{\bf 1}_{{\mathbb{B}}}
its indicator function.

Recall that a set
{\mathbb{B}}
is called increasing if for any
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{B}}
and
{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, it holds that
{\bf x}+{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{B}}
. For two positive uni-variate functions
f,\,g
, we write
f(x)\sim c\,g(x)
, for some
c\in(0,\,\infty)
, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=c\,,

we write

, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0\,,

write

, if

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty\,,

and
f(x)\asymp g(x)
holds, if both

and

, are true.

Correspondingly, if
\widetilde{f},\,\widetilde{g}
are
d
-variate functions, and

,

, if

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\widetilde{f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{\widetilde{g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}<\infty\,.

Finally for a random variable (or vector)
\Theta
, we write
\Theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}V
to indicate that
\Theta
has distribution
V
. The tail distribution of
V
is denoted by
\overline{V}(x)=1-V(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.1.
Heavy tailed random variables

Let us recall a one-dimensional distribution
V
is said to be heavy-tailed, if
\overline{V}(x)>0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\varepsilon\,x}\,V(dx)=\infty\,,

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Next, we introduce the following family of sets,

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}=\left\{A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;\text{ open, increasing}\,,\;A^{c}\;\text{ convex}\,,\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\right\}\,,

(2.1)

in order to define the rest subclasses of multivariate, heavy tailed distributions. Two important sets, from the family
\mathscr{R}
, which play a crucial role in actuarial practice, are the following:

\displaystyle A_{1}=\left\{{\bf z}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\,z_{i}>c\right\}\,,

(2.2)

with
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}\geq 0
,
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{d}=1
, and some
c>0
, and

\displaystyle A_{2}=\left\{{\bf z}\;:\;z_{i}>c_{i}\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\right\}\,,

(2.3)

with
c_{1},\,\ldots,\,c_{d}>0
. In the special one-dimensional case, the set
A_{2}
reduces to the interval
(c,\,\infty)
. See Remark
3.3
for the role of the sets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
in actuarial practice.

Let
{\bf X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}F
be a random vector, with distribution
F
, defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
. As shown in
[
43
]
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, the random variable

\displaystyle X_{A}:=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf X}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

(2.4)

follows a proper distribution
F_{A}
, whose tail satisfies

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\,{\bf X}>x\right)=\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A)\,,

(2.5)

for any
x>0
, where
I_{A}
is a suitable set of vectors whose existence for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
was shown in
[
43
, Lemma 4.3(c)]
. Thus, applying (
2.1
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), the class of multivariate subexponential distributions was defined in
[
43
]
as follows.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. We say that
F
belongs to the class of multivariate subexponential distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F_{A}^{n*}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=n\,,

where
F_{A}^{n*}
represents the
n
-th order convolution power of
F_{A}
. Further, we
write

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{S}_{A}\,,

for the class of multivariate subexponential distributions.

Following the same line, the following two classes were introduced in
[
30
]
. Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. We say that
F
belongs to the class of multivariate long tailed distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{L}
, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)
y>0
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(x-y)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

We say that
F
belongs to the class of multivariate consistently varying distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{C}
,
namely it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{b\uparrow 1}\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

Further, another general
multivariate distribution class was introduced in
[
31
]
, which also contains light-tailed distributions.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. We say that
F
belongs to class of multivariate positively decreasing distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in(\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)

it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}<1\,.

We also denote
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, if it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
. For all previous classes we keep the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{B}_{A}\,,

for any
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}\,,\;\mathcal{C}\,,\;\mathcal{P_{D}}\,,\;\mathcal{L}\}

Here, we deal mostly with the classes
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and

, for the description of the distribution of claim vectors. We should mention that the class

is general enough, and hence the class

.

These classes have found applications in several multivariate risk models, see in

[

]
,
[
9
]
,

[
28
]
. For examples of distributions, that belong to classes
(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
and

, we refer to
[
43
, Sec. 4]
and
[

.

A famous class of heavy-tailed distributions in multidimensional set up is the (standard) multivariate regularly varying class, symbolically
MRV
. We remind that an one-dimensional distribution
V
belongs to the class of regularly varying distributions, with index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, if for any
t>0
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}\,.

We say that distribution
F
belongs to
MRV
, if there exists some
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, and some non-degenerate to zero Radon measure
\mu
, such that

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{1}{\overline{V}(x)}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,{\mathbb{B}}\right)=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

(2.6)

for any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, which is such that
\mu(\partial{\mathbb{B}})=0
. Then we denote
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
. The Radon measure
\mu
, possess the property of positive homogeneity, namely for any real
y>0
,
and Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mu(y\,{\mathbb{B}})=y^{-\alpha}\,\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,.

(2.7)

For further reading about
MRV
distributions, we refer the reader to
[
41
]
.

If
MRV
represents the union of all
MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, for any
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
and any Radon measure
\mu
, then by combination of
[
30
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
31
, Proposition 3.1]
we find the following inclusions:

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,.

(2.8)

These inclusions in (

) remain valid if the classes
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
are replaced by
\mathcal{B}_{A}
, for any

.
Consequently, the ordering of these classes in the one-dimensional case also extends to the multidimensional case. For further discussion on the ordering of heavy-tailed distributions in one dimension, we refer to
[
34
, Chapter 2]
.

Finally we recall the Matuszewska indexes, which are useful for characterizing the heavy tailed and related distributions classes. For an one-dimensional distribution
G
, with
\overline{G}(x)>0
, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the upper and lower Matuszewska indexes are correspondingly defined as

\displaystyle J_{G}^{+}:=-\lim_{v\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{G}_{*}(v)}{\log v}\,,\qquad J_{G}^{-}:=-\lim_{v\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{G}^{*}(v)}{\log v}\,,

where

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{*}(v)=\liminf\dfrac{\overline{G}(v\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\,,\qquad\overline{G}^{*}(v)=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{G}(v\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\,.

Note that
G\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
if and only if
J_{G}^{-}>0
.
If
G\in\mathcal{C}
, then
J_{G}^{+}<\infty
, and if
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
,
then
J_{G}^{-}=J_{G}^{+}=\alpha
. For further discussions about the Matuszewska indexes we
refer to
[
4
, Subsection 2.1.2]
and to
[
34
, subsection 2.4]
.

2.2.
Discussion on the dependence

Now we introduce two main dependence structures that will be used to model the dependence among claim vectors. We first define their one-dimensional forms and then their multidimensional forms by reducing the problem to the one-dimensional form via the set
A\in\mathscr{R}
. The first dependence structure was introduced in
[
33
]
, and since then it has been used in several topics related with subexponential distributions, see e.g.
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
among others. In order to avoid unnecessary details, we only define these structures for the nonnegative case.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the sequence of non-negative, random variables
\{Z_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
with corresponding distributions
\{G_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are regression dependent, symbolically
RD
, if there exists some
x_{0}>0
, and some
K>0
, such that the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{i}>x_{i}\;\big|\;Z_{j}=x_{j}\,,\;j\in J_{i})\leq K\,\overline{G}_{i}(x_{i})\,,

holds for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j\in J_{i}\subset{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{i\}
, with
J_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}>x_{0}
.

The
RD
structure is a dependence structure, which is commonly employed when
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
,
for the extension of results that were initially proved for independent random variables, see Remark
2.1
below. It is obvious that the
RD
structure contains
the independence structure as a special case. However, in the case, where
G_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, we can generalize statements on independent random variables via a more general dependence structure, namely the quasi asymptotic independence, denoted by
QAI
, which was introduced in
[
7
]
. This dependence structure has been extensively employed in the literature, see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
35
]
, among others.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the sequence of non-negative, random variables
\{Z_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
with distributions
\{G_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
QAI
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}(Z_{i}>x\,,\;Z_{j}>x)}{\overline{G}_{i}(x)+\overline{G}_{j}(x)}=0\,,

for any
i,\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with
i\neq j
.

The following two definitions represent the multivariate versions of Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, appearing in
[
30
]
, and further used in
[
9
]
, for the description of interdependent claim vectors in a renewal driven risk model. For the sequence of non-negative, random vectors
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, we define

\displaystyle Z_{A}^{(i)}=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We say that the sequence of random vectors
\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is regression dependent on
A
, symbolically
RD_{A}
, if the sequence
\left\{Z_{A}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is
RD
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We say that the sequence of random vectors
\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is quasi asymptotically independent on
A
, symbolically
QAI_{A}
, if the sequence
\left\{Z_{A}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is
QAI
.

It is easy to see that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, it holds
RD_{A}\subsetneq QAI_{A}
. For examples of dependencies in Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
see in
[
9
, Subsection 2.2]
.

The following remarks are mainly meant to provide information regarding the dependence structures that preserve the
important properties of subexponential distributions, such as the single big jump principle, in one-dimensional as well as multidimensional cases.

Remark 2.1
.

A classical question of significant interest in applied probability concerns the single big jump principle. Specifically, suppose that we have random variables
Z_{A}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,Z_{A}^{(n)}
with distributions
G_{A}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,G_{A}^{(n)}
, respectively, under what assumptions for the distribution class of
\left\{G_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,n\right\}
and the dependence structures for the
\left\{Z_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,n\right\}
we obtain the asymptotic relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)\,.

(2.9)

This problem either in the form (
2.9
), or in more general weighted form, has been investigated in many papers, see in
[
13
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
44
]
,
[
49
]
, among many others.

In general, the following intuitive interpretation is true:

(A)

The smaller is the the distribution class of
\left\{G_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,n\right\}
, the larger (weak) dependence structures can be used, in order to keep (
2.9
) true.

Although in the cases where
G_{A}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{S}
, with
J_{G_{A}^{(i)}}^{+}<\infty
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
the dependence structures are ’well’ ordered with respect to distribution classes (in order to keep (
2.9
) true),
in the cases where
G_{A}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{S}
, with
J_{G_{A}^{(i)}}^{+}=\infty
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
this is no more true. The reason for this, has to do with the two different approaches on this issue.

The first approach, which we shall follow, is based on the structure
RD
from Definition
2.1
and the structure introduced in
[
24
]
. These dependence structures allow comparison with the others in question (A) (as for example
QAI
structure with
\mathcal{C}
class), while in the same time these two assumptions can operate in complementary mode, in relation to question (
2.9
), see in
[
22
]
for more discussions on these two structures.

The second approach, given in
[
18
]
, considered that the
\{Z_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,n\}
are conditionally independent, given some
\sigma
-algebra. This idea undoubtedly has great theoretical value and appears general enough. However, in that paper, additional conditions are needed to establish (
2.9
), which seems rather restrictive. Most of their examples satisfying these conditions can be reduced to subcases of the first approach. Namely, in
[
18
, Example 1]
, we can easily see that
G_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, while the structure of dependence for the
Z_{A}^{(i)}
is the
QAI
. In
[
18
, Example 4]
, the
Z_{A}^{(i)}=\xi_{i}\,\eta_{1},\,\eta_{2},\,\cdots,\,\eta_{i}
, with
\{\xi_{i}\}
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with distribution
F_{\xi}\in\mathcal{C}
, and with
\{\eta_{i}\}
i.i.d. with distribution
F_{\eta}
, which belongs to the class of rapidly varying distributions, where
\{\xi_{i}\}
independent of
\{\eta_{i}\}
. We can easily find out, through
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
, that
G_{A}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{C}
while the structure of dependence for the
Z_{A}^{(i)}A^{(i)}
is the
QAI
. Even more, we can further generalize, with
\{(\xi_{i}\,,\;\eta_{i})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
sequence of i.i.d. pairs,
\xi_{i},\,\eta_{i}
arbitrarily dependent
F_{\xi}\in\mathcal{S}
, and with
J_{F_{\xi}}^{+}<\infty
, and
\mathbf{E}[\eta^{p}]<\infty
, for
p>J_{F_{\xi}}^{+}
, and again from
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, the relation (
2.9
) remains true. Finally,
[
18
, Example 5]
focuses in case, where
G_{A}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{S}
with
J_{G_{A}^{(i)}}^{+}=\infty
, but the dependence structure, that follows from multivariate normal distribution, represents a special case of
RD
, as we find in
[
20
, p. 876]
.

Therefore, we consider that in case, where
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
, it is plausible to use
RD
, rather than the conditions in
[
18
]
(see also in
[
15
]
), although they are not overlapping each other.

Remark 2.2
.

The corresponding multivariate analogue of relation (
2.9
), can take two forms: 1) the multivariate linear single big jump principle and 2) the multivariate non-linear single big jump principle, see further for 2) in
[
29
]
. Here we put emphasis in linear form, which is satisfied by the classes in relation (
2.8
), except
class
\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}
, for independent random vectors. Namely, the (A) is reduced to

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(2.10)

for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. The name ’linear’ stems from the fact that relation (
2.10
) is insensitive with respect to the dimension of the random vectors. Hence, due to this insensitivity with respect to dimensionality, the question of (A) for (
2.10
), can be easily transferred to the question of (A) for (
2.9
), by employing the corresponding multivariate distribution classes and dependence structures on set
A\in\mathscr{R}
.

3.
Main results

Now we can give the two main results of the paper.

3.1.
Finite-time horizon

In this section we assume that the discounted aggregate claims have claim vectors that permit the
RD_{A}
structure, with distribution from class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

We also consider the following general assumption for the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, that is related with its second factorial moment measure, symbolically
\alpha^{(2)}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha^{(2)}(ds,dt):=E\left[N(ds)\,N(dt)\right]-E[N(ds)\delta_{s}(dt)],

where
\delta_{s}(dt)
is the Dirac mass on the diagonal
t=s
. This means that the second factorial moment measure is the mean measure of
N(ds)N(dt)
with the diagonal
t=s
removed.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let consider a fixed
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
. We assume that there exists
C<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\alpha^{(2)}(ds,dt)\leq C\,m(ds)m(dt)\,,

on
[0,\,T]^{2}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that for any inhomogeneous renewal process, because counts on disjoint sets are independent, it holds

\displaystyle\alpha^{(2)}(ds,dt)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[N(ds)\,N(dt)\right]-E[N(ds)\delta_{s}(dt)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[N(ds)\right]E\left[N(dt)\right]+E[N(ds)\delta_{s}(dt)]-E[N(ds)\delta_{s}(dt)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[N(ds)\right]E\left[N(dt)\right]=m(ds)m(dt)\,.

So Assumption
3.1
holds with
C=1
for all
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is a fact that Assumption
3.1
, does not include only inhomogeneous renewal processes but it can cover also cases where the
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, does not have necessarily neither independent, nor identically distributed increments. For example, it contains all the processes, such that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[N(T)\,N(T)]<\infty\,,

(3.1)

for fixed
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
. Indeed, let
\underline{t}:=\inf\{t\;:\;t\in\Lambda\}
. Let
\mathbf{P}(\tau_{1}=\underline{t})>0
. We examine two subcases. Firstly, if either
s\in[0,\,\underline{t})
, or
t\in[0,\,\underline{t})
, then it holds

\displaystyle\alpha^{(2)}(ds,dt)\leq\mathbf{E}[N(ds)\,N(dt)]=0\,,

and therefore Assumption
3.1
holds trivially, as
0=0
.

For the second case, if
s,\,t\in[\underline{t},\,T]
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha^{(2)}(ds,dt)}{m(ds)\,m(dt)}\leq\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[N(ds)\,N(dt)]}{m(ds)\,m(dt)}\leq\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[N(T)\,N(T)]}{[m(\underline{t})]^{2}}\,,

and from relation (
3.1
), the last ratio is bounded from above. In the case where
\mathbf{P}(\tau_{1}=\underline{t})=0
, we examine the two subcases, for
s\in[0,\,\underline{t}]
or
t\in[0,\,\underline{t}]
, and for
s,\,t\in(\underline{t},\,\infty]
, and further the methodology is similar. We can see that as smaller the
T\in\Lambda
, so easier relation (
3.1
) is satisfied. This shows the generality - flexibility provided by Assumption
3.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and the discounted aggregate claims of (
1.2
). We suppose that Assumptions
1.1
and
3.1
with fixed
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
hold, and
\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
are
RD_{A}
, with
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in x\,A\right)\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,.

(3.2)

Remark 3.3
.

If in conditions of Theorem
3.1
we require
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
, and the structures of
\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
are
RD_{A}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, then we can imply, from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, that relation (
3.2
) holds for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Although relation (
3.2
) eventually seems complicated in calculation, it can take several simpler expressions, when we determine the set
A
, the dependence structures of the marginals, and sometimes also the distribution class of marginals, see in
[
28
, Section 4]
, for related discussions.

Further, the sets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),
respectively, represent two important sets for the insurance industry, since provide two different kind of control for the insurer’s solvency. Concretely, the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims into set
A_{1}
, indicates the probability of the event, the sum of the discounted aggregate claims of
d
-lines to exceed some ’high’ threshold, while the corresponding entrance into
A_{2}
, shows the probability in one of the
d
-lines the discounted aggregate claims to exceed some ’high’ threshold. This ’high’ threshold usually has relation with the insurer’s initial capital.

Let us note that in one-dimensional sub-case, where
A=(1,\,\infty)
, this result continuous to be new, and in some sense is similar to
[
15
, Theorem 1]
, where was considered the ruin probability over finite-time horizon, but there was employed conditional independent claims instead of
RD
, and a inhomogeneous Poisson driven risk model.

The following corollary presents a more explicit asymptotic expression, in comparison with (
3.2
), when the distribution
F
is restricted in
MRV
. We obserbe that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we obtain
\mu(A)\in(0,\,\infty)
, see in
[
43
, proof of Proposition 4.14]
, while if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, then there exists
V\in R_{-\alpha}
, recall (
2.6
).

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.1
, with restriction that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in x\,A\right)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{T}\,e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,m(ds)\,.

3.2.
Infinite-time horizon

For the infinite-time horizon we need some alternative conditions, which guarantee the finiteness of the sums, like that in (
1.2
) with
T=\infty
, are non-defective, namely they have not mass on
\infty
.

Some sufficient conditions, are provided by the requirement of positive and finite Matuszewska indexes on the claim vectors, in combination with the fact that
r>0
, and the following moment condition.

Assumption 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set,
r>0
, and
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
. We assume that there exist some
q_{1},\,q_{2}
, with
0<q_{1}<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<q_{2}<\infty
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{1}\,r\,\tau_{i}}\right]\bigvee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{2}\,r\,\tau_{i}}\right]\right)^{1/q}<\infty\,,

(3.3)

where
q={\bf 1}_{\left\{0<J_{F_{A}}^{+}<1\right\}}+q_{2}\,{\bf 1}_{\left\{J_{F_{A}}^{+}\geq 1\right\}}
.

Although Assumption
3.2
is more restrictive than Assumption
3.1
, used for the finite time horizon, it continuous to remain quite general. The following examples demonstrate this generality.

Example 3.1
.

We consider that the inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}:=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are widely lower orthant dependent, symbolically
WLOD
, namely there exists a sequence of finite positive numbers
\{g_{L}(n)\,,\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, such that for any
x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}
to hold

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\{\theta_{i}\leq x_{i}\}\right)\leq g_{L}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}(\theta_{i}\leq x_{i})\,,

(3.4)

see
[
46
]
for the introduction of
WLOD
.

Further, we suppose that the
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are identically distributed. Hence, we obtain that the
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a quasi-renewal process. If additionally there exists some
\delta\in(0,\,-\log\mathbf{E}[e^{-r\,J_{F_{A}}^{-}\,\theta_{1}}])
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}g_{L}(n)\,e^{-\delta\,n}<\infty\,,

(3.5)

then Assumption
3.2
holds. This can be verified following the lines of the proof of
[
48
, Th. 2]
. We note that relation (
3.4
) represents a general enough dependence structure and the additional condition (
3.5
) is quite mild. We refer to
[
46
]
and
[
45
]
for more details about (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

It is obvious that if the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
represents a renewal process, then relation (
3.5
) is true for all
\delta>0
and hence also Assumption
3.2
via previous example.

Example 3.2
.

Let consider the inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, that are arbitrarily dependent with distributions, which are bounded from below, namely it holds
\theta_{i}\geq a_{i}
, almost surely, with
a_{i}>0
. Such cases are very realistic, especially in life insurance. Let
\hat{a}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{1}\,r\,\tau_{i}}\right]\bigvee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{2}\,r\,\tau_{i}}\right]\right)^{1/q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{1}\,r\,\sum_{j=1}^{i}\theta_{j}}\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-q_{2}\,r\,\sum_{j=1}^{i}\theta_{j}}\right]\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left[e^{-q_{1}\,r\,\hat{a}}\right]^{i}+\left[e^{-q_{2}\,r\,\check{a}}\right]^{i}\right)^{1/q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[e^{-q_{1}\,r\,\check{a}}\right]^{i/q}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[e^{-q_{2}\,r\,\check{a}}\right]^{i/q}<\infty\,,

where at the pre-last step we used the elementary inequality
(x+y)^{z}\leq x^{z}+y^{z}
, for any
x,\,y\geq 0
, and any
z\in(0,\,1]
.

In spite of the restrictions of Assumption
3.2
, in comparison with the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, namely (
3.3
) and
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, the following result permits dependence structure
QAI_{A}\supsetneq RD_{A}
, for the claim vectors.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and consider the discounted aggregate claims of relation (
1.2
). We suppose that Assumptions
1.1
and
3.2
are valid, and the dependence structure of
\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is
QAI_{A}
. Then we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,.

(3.6)

Similarly with what was noticed after Theorem
3.1
, here also, if we consider that
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{\mathscr{R}}
,
\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
are
QAI_{A}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, and there exist
q_{1}(A),\,q_{2}(A)
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, such that relation (
3.3
) holds, then (
3.6
) is true for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
.

The following corollary gives a more explicit form of (
3.6
) under the
restriction that
F\in MRV
. The proof of this corollary, follows the same argument as in proof of Corollary
3.1
, and therefore is omitted. We should mention that if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, then
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, and hence
J_{F_{A}}^{-}=J_{F_{A}}^{+}=\alpha
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, under the additional restriction that

F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, we have the relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\,e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,\,m(ds)\,.

We note that the results of Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.2
are new even for the one-dimensional subcase, with
A=(1,\,\infty)
.

Remark 3.4
.

We should mention that the proof of Theorem
3.2
could not be derived by the current result from stochastic recurrence equations, as it happens several times for the ruin probability over infinite time horizon, see for example in
[
27
]
. Indeed, our proof is based on the multivariate linear single big jump principle of some infinite randomly weighted sums. Although the framework of stochastic recurrence equations in multidimensional set up provides a more general model, due to random matrices as random weights, the current results can be applied to this model, for
T=\infty
, only in the case, where the
\left\{\left({\bf X}^{(i)},\,e^{-r\,\theta_{i}}\right)\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
are i.i.d. copies of
\left({\bf X},\,e^{-r\,\theta_{1}}\right)
and
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
. Hence, it holds for renewal risk model with independent claim vectors, which we aim to generalize in the nonstandard model, see in
(1)
and
(2)
in Subsection
1.2
. For more details about the stochastic recurrence equations see in
[
5
]
.

3.3.
Argumentation

Before the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we need two preliminary lemmas, that are related with the multivariate linear single big jump principle for (randomly) weighted sums.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and choose some fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We
consider that the
{\bf Z}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n)}
are nonnegative
RD_{A}
random vectors with distributions
G_{1},\,\ldots,\,G_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, respectively, and
G_{i}*G_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. Then, for some constants
0<a\leq b<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=0\,,

(3.7)

where
{\bf c}_{n}:=(c_{1},\,\ldots,\,c_{n})
.

Proof.

At first, we notice that since
G_{i},\,G_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
G_{i}*G_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, then by
[
30
, Th. 3.4]
we obtain
G_{A}^{(i)}*G_{A}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{S}
. Hence, for the upper bound of (
3.7
) it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, where at the first step we used
[
30
, Prop. 2.4]
, and at the second we applied
[
44
, Lem. 8]
.

For the lower bound of (
3.7
), since the summands are nonnegative and the
x\,A\in\mathscr{R}
is an increasing set (recall that
\mathscr{R}
represents a cone with respect to positive scalar multiplication), we find via Bonferroni’s inequality that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left\{c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right\}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)-\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;c_{j}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right).

for all
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
. For each term of the last sum in (
3.9
), since the
{\bf Z}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n)}
are
RD_{A}
, and
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, we can find some sufficiently large
x_{0}^{*}>0
, such that for any
x\geq x_{0}^{*}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;c_{j}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\,,\;c_{j}\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq K\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{j}\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x\right)\leq K\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{j}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right).

So, from relations (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\gtrsim[1-o(1)]\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(3.11)

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
. From (
3.8
), (
3.11
) in combination with their uniformity with respect to
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, we conclude (
3.7
).
∎

The next lemma clarifies the concept of multivariate linear single big jump principle for the weighted sum of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and choose some fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
, for some constants
0<a\leq b<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\left\{c_{k}\,{\bf Z}^{(k)}\notin x\,A\right\}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}=0\,.

(3.12)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\left\{c_{k}\,{\bf Z}^{(k)}\notin x\,A\right\}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\left\{c_{k}\,{\bf Z}^{(k)}\in x\,A\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\left\{c_{k}\,{\bf Z}^{(k)}\in x\,A\right\}\right)\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)

4.
Ruin probabilities

Now we provide an application of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, on ruin probabilities, over finite and infinite-time horizon, respectively, in a multivariate risk model, under weak enough assumptions on the premiums, and eventual Brownian perturbations. As expected, the asymptotic behavior of the ruin probability coincides with the asymptotic behavior of the discounted aggregate claims, while we can realize the asymptotic insensitivity of the the ruin probability with respect to the Brownian perturbations, which is expected when the claims have heavy-tailed distributions, see for one and multidimensional risk model
[
11
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
, among others.

We assume that the insurer’s surplus process at the moment
t\geq 0
, is given through the relation

\displaystyle{\bf U}(t):=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
U_{d}(t)\end{array}\right)=x\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l_{1}\\
\vdots\\
l_{d}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,c_{1}(s)\,ds\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,c_{d}(s)\,ds\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\delta_{d}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,B_{1}(ds)\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,B_{d}(ds)\end{array}\right)-{\bf D}(t)

\displaystyle=:x\,{\bf l}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf c}(s)\,ds+\vec{\delta}\odot\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf B}(ds)-{\bf D}(t)\,,

(4.17)

where
x>0
, represents the insurer’s initial capital, which is allocated into
d
-lines of business through vector
{\bf l}=(l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d})
, with
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
and
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{d}=1
. The vector
\{{\bf c}(t)=(c_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,c_{d}(t)\,)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
represents the densities of the premiums of the
d
-lines of business. Hereafter, we assume that the densities of the premiums are non-negative and bounded from above, namely for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, there exists some
M_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
, such that for any
t\geq 0
it holds

\displaystyle 0\leq c_{i}(t)\leq M_{i}\,.

(4.18)

The assumption of insurer’s bounded densities, provides significant flexibility in premium modeling for the risk model (
4
). Indeed, relation (
4.18
) is a sufficient condition, in order to consider that the premiums are arbitrarily dependent with the discounted aggregate claims
{\bf D}(t)
, which is realistic in actuarial practice. For example some large claim usually affects to future premiums. Further, the condition (
4.18
) is not strict, since allows for any
T\in(0,\,\infty)
and any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-r\,s}\,c_{i}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r\,s}\,c_{i}(s)\,ds\leq\dfrac{M_{i}}{r}\,,

(4.19)

which means that the total discounted premiums are bounded from above
by the total discounted premiums of constant premium density
M_{i}
, for arbitrarily large, fixed
M_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
.

The
\vec{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\delta_{d})\geq{\bf 0}
, is called diffusion coefficient, while the random field
\{{\bf B}(t)=(B_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,B_{d}(t))\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a multivariate standard Brownian motion, that is independent of all the other sources of randomness, and contains arbitrarily correlated components. Thus,
\{{\bf B}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
represents one more source of randomness, that can stem either from the premiums or form the claims, or from any small perturbations in the company by the market. Note that
{\bf B}(0)={\bf 0}
.

The ruin probability in multivariate risk models, can be defined by several ways,
since the entrance sets of the surplus that indicate some insurer’s economic rigidity have not unique definition. We follow Assumption
4.1
in order to describe the properties of such kind of ’ruin’ sets. This assumption was introduced in
[
43
]
, and is directly related with set family
\mathscr{R}
. Let us remind that if a set
-{\mathbb{B}}
is increasing, the set
{\mathbb{B}}
is called decreasing.

Assumption 4.1
.

Let
L
be a set that is open, decreasing,
{\bf 0}\in\partial L
,
L^{c}
is convex, and
for any
x>0
, it holds
x\,L=L
.

Remark 4.1
.

It is not difficult to verify that if the set
L
satisfies Assumption
4.1
, then
A:={\bf l}-L\in\mathscr{R}
. Through this property follows the direct connection of the distribution class of claim vectors with the ruin sets of interest. Two typical ruin sets
L
, that satisfy Assumption
4.1
are the following:

\displaystyle L_{1}=\left\{{\bf z}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}<0\right\}\,,\qquad L_{2}=\left\{{\bf z}\;:\;z_{i}<0\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\right\}\,,

related with the sets
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), respectively. So, the entrance probability of surplus process into
L_{1}
, indicates the probability that at some moment the sum of surpluses of
d
-lines of business becomes negative, while the entrance probability of surplus process into
L_{2}
, indicates the probability that at some moment the surplus for someone of the
d
-lines becomes negative. We can notice, that the typical one-dimensional ruin set
L_{3}=(-\infty,\,0)
, satisfies Assumption
4.1
(because is a special case of
L_{2}
), and is related to
1-L_{3}=(1,\,\infty)=A\in\mathscr{R}
of the one-dimensional tail.

Now, we can define the ruin probabilities with respect to the ruin sets
L
, of Assumption
4.1
. For a fixed
T\in(0,\,\infty)
, the finite-time ruin probability in risk model (
4
) is expressed as

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T):=\mathbf{P}({\bf U}(t)\in L\,,\;\exists\;t\in[0,\,T])

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf c}(s)\,ds-\vec{\delta}\odot\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf B}(ds)\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;t\in[0,\,T]\right)\,,

and the corresponding infinite-time ruin probability as

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty):=\mathbf{P}({\bf U}(t)\in L\,,\;\exists\;t\geq 0)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf c}(s)\,ds-\vec{\delta}\odot\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf B}(ds)\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;t\geq 0\right)\,,

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
L
satisfy Assumption
4.1
, and
A={\bf l}-L
. We suppose in risk model (
4
)
that
\{{\bf B}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is independent from all the other sources of randomness. For any fixed
T\in\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\}
we have the following:

(i)

under conditions of Theorem
3.1
it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A)\,m(ds)\,,

(4.21)

(ii)

under conditions of Corollary
3.1
it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,m(ds)\,.

(4.22)

Proof.

(i)

For any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, we take

\displaystyle M_{i}(T):=\delta_{i}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-r\,s}\,B_{i}(ds)\,,

(4.23)

and

\displaystyle\underline{M}_{i}(T):=\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}M_{i}(t)\leq 0\,,\qquad\overline{M}_{i}(T):=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}M_{i}(t)\geq 0\,,

(4.24)

(having in mind that
B_{i}(0)=0
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
). The random variables from
(
4.24
) are non-defective, namely the have no mass at
-\infty
, or at
\infty
, respectively, and hence the following quantities

\displaystyle\widehat{\underline{M}}(T):=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}\underline{M}_{i}(T)\leq 0\,,\qquad\check{\overline{M}}(T):=\bigvee_{i=1}^{d}\overline{M}_{i}(T)\geq 0\,,

(4.25)

are also non-defective random variables, independent of
{\bf D}(T)
and of the premiums. Therefore, by (
4.20
), and the fact that the set
x\,A
is increasing and that
{\bf D}(t)
is non-decreasing function with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\leq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)-\widehat{\underline{M}}(T)\in x\,A\right)

(4.26)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)+y\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(-\widehat{\underline{M}}(T)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in(x-k_{y})\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(-\widehat{\underline{M}}(T)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in(x-k_{y})\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,\mathbf{P}\left(-\widehat{\underline{M}}(T)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,,

where at the third step we used the fact that from
[
43
, Lemma 4.3(d)]
, for any
y\in[0,\,\infty)
, there exists some
0<k_{y}
, such that for all
x>k_{y}
it holds

\displaystyle(x+k_{y})\,A\subsetneq x\,A+y\subsetneq(x-k_{y})\,A\,,

(4.27)

and at the fourth step we applied Theorem
3.1
. In order to see this, can be used the relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in(x-k_{y})\,A\right)\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in(x-k_{y})\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,,

for any
k_{y}
, that corresponds to some
y\in[0,\,\infty)
. Finally at the last step we took into consideration that
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

Now we deal with the lower bound. Following similar path and using for this case the first inclusion of (
4.27
) we find

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)-\int_{0}^{T}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf c}(s)\,ds-\vec{\delta}\odot\int_{0}^{T}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf B}(ds)\in x\,A\right)

(4.28)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)-\check{\overline{M}}(T)\in(x+k)\,A\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in(x+k)\,A+y\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(\check{\overline{M}}(T)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(T)\in(x+k+k_{y})\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(\check{\overline{M}}(T)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,,

where here at the second step we also used the condition (
4.18
) of bounded premiums, to find the
k>0
, (recall also (
4.19
)). Hence, from (
4.26
) and (
4.28
) we get relation (
4.21
).

(ii)

For the proof we follow the line of part (i), with only exception, instead of Theorem
3.1
we use Corollary
3.1
. ∎

Now, we proceed to the infinite time ruin probability.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
L
satisfy Assumption
4.1
, and
A={\bf l}-L
. We suppose in risk model (
4
) that
\{{\bf B}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is independent from all the other sources of randomness. Then we obtain the following:

(i)

under conditions of Theorem
3.2
it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A)\,m(ds)\,,

(4.29)

(ii)

under conditions of Corollary
3.2
it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,m(ds)\,.

(4.30)

Proof.

(i)

We follow similar argumentation with that in proof of Corollary
4.1
(i).
Hence, we present only the points that are different from there.

We define relations (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) for
T=\infty
(where
0\leq t\leq\infty
, is understood as
t\geq 0
), and following relation (
4.25
) we find that

\displaystyle\widehat{\underline{M}}(\infty):=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}\underline{M}_{i}(\infty)\leq 0\,,\qquad\check{\overline{M}}(\infty):=\bigvee_{i=1}^{d}\overline{M}_{i}(\infty)\geq 0\,,

are non-defective random variables, independent of
{\bf D}(\infty)
and of the premiums. For the upper bound of (
4.29
), we repeat the steps of (
4.26
) applying now Theorem
3.2
, to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\leq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)-\widehat{\underline{M}}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)+y\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(-\widehat{\underline{M}}(\infty)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in(x-k_{y})\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(-\widehat{\underline{M}}(\infty)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,.

Now we proceed to the lower bound of (
4.29
), which follows as in case of (
4.28
) but now applying Theorem
3.2
, to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf c}(s)\,ds-\vec{\delta}\odot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r\,s}\,{\bf B}(ds)\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)-\check{\overline{M}}(\infty)\in(x+k)\,A\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in(x+k)\,A+y\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(\check{\overline{M}}(\infty)\in dy\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in(x+k+k_{y})\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(\check{\overline{M}}(\infty)\in dy\right)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right)\,m(ds)\,,

which implies the desired lower bound.

(ii)

For the proof we follow the line of part (i), with only exception, instead of Theorem
3.2
we apply Corollary
3.2
. ∎
