A note on the well-posedness of the quartic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation on ℝ × 𝕋 {\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}
Jakob Nowicki-Koth

Abstract
. By using a bilinear smoothing estimate recently developed in [ 12 ] , together with several linear Strichartz-type estimates established therein, we improve the threshold for local well-posedness of the quartic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation and prove that it is locally well-posed in H s ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}) for all s > 1 2 s>\frac{1}{2} .



1.
Introduction

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})
, we consider the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}u=\pm\partial_{x}\left(u^{4}\right),\qquad u(t=0)=u_{0},

(CP)

associated with the quartic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (
3
-gZK). The equation itself constitutes a generalization of other well-known equations in two distinct ways. On the one hand, it can be viewed as a two-dimensional analogue of the quartic Korteweg-de Vries equation, for which the well-posedness theory has been extensively studied in the past; see, for instance,
[
8
,
5
,
7
,
15
,
1
,
14
,
2
]
, as well as the more recent global well-posedness result at low regularity in
[
3
]
.
On the other hand,
3
-gZK is a higher order generalization of the Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (with quadratic nonlinearity), which has been derived on both
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as a model for the propagation of nonlinear waves in plasmas
[
16
,
9
]
.

We briefly review the development of the well-posedness theory for
3
-gZK, both in the non-periodic and semiperiodic settings: On
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Linares and Pastor
[
10
]
initiated the study by proving local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>\frac{3}{4}
, global well-posedness for
s\geq 1
under an additional smallness assumption on the
H^{1}
-norm of the initial data, and ill-posedness for initial data in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, in the sense that the data-to-solution map fails to be uniformly continuous at this scaling-critical regularity.
By further optimizing the interplay between the well-known Kato smoothing effect and two maximal function estimates, Ribaud and Vento
[
13
]
improved the local result to
s>\frac{5}{12}
, and Grünrock
[
6
]
subsequently closed the remaining gap of
\frac{1}{12}
of a derivative to the scaling threshold by proving local and small data global well-posedness in homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{2,q}
, using a newly developed maximal function estimate within the framework of the function spaces
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
.

Regarding the semiperiodic case, Farah and Molinet
[
4
]
established local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})
for all
s>1-
by developing a linear
L^{4}
-estimate and using it in conjunction with a bilinear smoothing estimate due to Molinet and Pilod
[
11
]
. This result, again under a smallness assumption on the
H^{1}
-norm of the initial data, implied global well-posedness for all
s\geq 1
, and just recently, the local result was improved to
s>\frac{8}{15}

[
12
]
by developing novel linear
L^{4}
- and
L^{6}
-Strichartz-type estimates and using them in the context of a fixed-point argument in Bourgain’s
X_{s,b}
spaces.

In this short note, we build on the results from
[
12
]
and further refine the local theory by additionally making use of a bilinear smoothing estimate developed therein. This allows us to lower the regularity threshold to
s>\frac{1}{2}
and the precise statement of our result is given in

Theorem 1.1
.

The Cauchy problem
(
CP
)
for the quartic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation is locally well-posed for every
s>\frac{1}{2}
. That is, for each
s>\frac{1}{2}
and every
u_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})
, there exist a lifespan
\delta=\delta(\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}})>0
and a unique solution

u\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{\delta}(\varphi)

to
(
CP
)
. Moreover, for every
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta)
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})
of
u_{0}
such that the data-to-solution map

S:H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})\supseteq\mathcal{U}\rightarrow X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{\tilde{\delta}}(\varphi),\qquad v_{0}\mapsto S(v_{0})\coloneqq v

is smooth.

2.
Notation, function spaces, and Strichartz-type estimates

In this preliminary section, we fix notational conventions, introduce the function spaces we will work with, and recall the known linear and bilinear Strichartz estimates that will be needed in the proof of Theorem

In the following, we denote by
\lvert x\rvert_{2}^{2}\coloneqq x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{n}^{2}
the Euclidean norm and by
\langle x\rangle\coloneqq(1+\lvert x\rvert_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

(\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}

\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\coloneqq 3\xi^{2}+q^{2}
to simplify notation, and for
a\in\mathbb{R}
and particularly small
\varepsilon>0
, we write
a+\coloneqq a+\varepsilon
and
a-\coloneqq a-\varepsilon
for brevity
1
1

We also often write
\infty-
to denote a very large positive real number.
.
If
a,b
are positive real numbers, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a constant

such that
a\leq cb

a\sim b

a\ll b

can be chosen to be very close to zero. For an admissible function
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we denote its space-time Fourier transform by
\widehat{f}=\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,q)
, and partial Fourier transforms are denoted using subscripts, as in
\mathcal{F}_{x}f
or
\mathcal{F}_{xy}f
. We also use the Fourier transform and its inverse to define the Riesz and Bessel potential operators of order

I^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert_{2}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{xy}\quad\text{and}\quad J^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{s}\mathcal{F}_{xy}.

As with the Fourier transform, we use subscripts whenever the operators are meant to act with respect to a single variable only.

We equip the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}})
with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{s}}\coloneqq\lVert J^{s}\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}}

L_{tx}^{p}L_{y}^{q}

with the usual nested
L^{p}
- and
L^{q}
-norms (see the notation in
[
12
]
). Here, we use the subscript

when the integration in the time variable is taken over all of
\mathbb{R}
, and

when the time variable

is restricted to the interval
[-T,T]
. If, moreover,

denotes the phase function associated with the linear part of

X_{s,b}(\varphi)

, are given by

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b}(\varphi)}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi,q)\rangle^{2b}\lvert\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi).

Lastly, for
\delta>0
, we also introduce the time-restricted Bourgain spaces

X_{s,b}^{\delta}(\varphi)\coloneqq\{f|_{[-\delta,\delta]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\ :\ f\in X_{s,b}(\varphi)\},

endowed with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b}^{\delta}(\varphi)}\coloneqq\inf\{\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{X_{s,b}(\varphi)}\ :\ \tilde{f}\in X_{s,b}(\varphi),\quad\tilde{f}|_{[-\delta,\delta]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}=f\},

as these will be the spaces in which we seek local solutions to (

For notational convenience, we suppress the dependence of the phase function, and we now collect the Strichartz-type estimates that will be needed later.

We begin by recalling the bilinear smoothing estimate

\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0,b}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,\quad b>\frac{1}{2}

(1)

with

\displaystyle\widehat{MP(u,v)}(\tau,\xi,q)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\widehat{u}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},q_{1})\widehat{v}(\tau-\tau_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1}),

which allows for a gain of up to
\frac{1}{2}-
derivatives for widely separated wave numbers and originally goes back to Molinet and Pilod
[
11
]
(see Proposition 3.1 in
[
12
]
for a proof of this particular version). As explained in Remark 3.2 of

]
, this estimate can be interpolated with a trivial bilinear bound to obtain

\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{2}+,\frac{1}{2}-}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0+,\frac{1}{2}-}},

(2)

which will be useful for estimates by duality.
In addition to that, we have a collection of linear estimates at our disposal, all of which were established in
[
12
]
. These include the almost optimal linear
L^{4}
-estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\varepsilon,b}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,\quad b>\frac{1}{2},

(3)

the Airy

-estimate

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6}}\lesssim_{b}\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}},\qquad b>\frac{1}{2},

(4)

and the optimized

-estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon,b}},\qquad T,\varepsilon>0,\quad b>\frac{1}{2}.

(5)

For later estimates involving duality, we interpolate the above bounds with the trivial bounds

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}=\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0,0}}

(6)

and

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{1+\varepsilon,b}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,\quad b>\frac{1}{2},

(7)

respectively, thereby obtaining the estimates

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4+}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0+,\frac{1}{2}+}},

(8)

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4-}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0+,\frac{1}{2}-}},

(9)

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6+}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}+,\frac{1}{2}+}},

(10)

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6-}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}+,\frac{1}{2}-}},

(11)

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6+}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}+}},

(12)

and

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6-}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}-}}.

(13)

All of these estimates were already employed in
[
12
]
to establish local well-posedness for

. In the present work, we additionally incorporate the bilinear refinement

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P^{1}(uv)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\varepsilon,b}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{\varepsilon,b}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,\quad b>\frac{1}{2}

(14)

of the linear estimate (
3

P^{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\chi_{\{\lvert 3\xi^{2}-q^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi\rvert,\ \lvert\xi\rvert\gtrsim 1\}}\mathcal{F}_{xy},

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We now turn to the proof of a quadrilinear
X_{s,b}
-estimate. The well-posedness statement in Theorem
