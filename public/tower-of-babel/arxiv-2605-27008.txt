Rigidity and equidistribution of random walks by diffeomorphisms near the conservative regime
Timothée Bénard, Zhiyuan Zhang

Abstract
. We consider a random walk on a closed manifold M M driven by a probability measure μ \mu on the set of C 2 C^{2} -diffeomorphisms. Provided μ \mu has compact support, statisfies certain gap and pinching conditions, and is weak- ∗ * close to volume-preserving, we prove M M carries exactly one atom-free stationary probability measure Υ μ \Upsilon_{\mu} . It has full Frostman dimension and coincides with volume in the volume-preserving setting. Moreover, for every x ∈ M x\in M , the n n -step distribution μ ∗ n ∗ δ x \mu^{*n}*\delta_{x} converges to Υ μ \Upsilon_{\mu} unless x x is trapped in a finite μ \mu -invariant set. Our result applies to a variety of situations: bi-expanding random walks on surfaces, non-linear perturbations of Zariski-dense random walks on the torus 𝕋 d {\mathbb{T}}^{d} , on cocompact lattice quotients of SO ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) , SO ⁡ ( 3 , 1 ) {\rm SO}(2,1),{\rm SO}(3,1) , or on the sphere 𝕊 d {\mathbb{S}}^{d} .



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
{\rm Diff}^{2}(M)
the space of
C^{2}
-diffeomorphisms of
M
. Given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in M
, set
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}
to be the law of
g_{n}\dots g_{1}x
where the
g_{i}
are i.i.d. variables of law
\mu
. The probability measure
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}
may be interpreted as the
n
-step distribution of a random walk on
M
with origin
x
and driven by random instructions given by
\mu
. We refer to this process as the
\mu
-walk on
M
.

Our aim is to study the
\mu
-walk on
M
, by classifying stationary measures and establishing equidistribution properties under general
conditions on
\mu
. Recall that a probability measure
\nu
on
M
is
\mu
-stationary if it satisfies
\nu=\int g_{\star}\nu d\mu(g)
. We equip
M
with a volume form and write
{\rm vol}
the associated normalized volume measure on
M
. We denote by
\Gamma_{\mu}:=\langle{\rm supp}(\mu)\rangle_{\text{grp}}
the group generated by the support of
\mu
. Assuming
\Gamma_{\mu}
preserves
{\rm vol}
,
and
\mu
satisfies certain expansion and pinching conditions, we show the following:

(a)

Rigidity
: The volume measure
{\rm vol}
is the only atom-free
\mu
-stationary probability measure on
M
.

(b)

Equidistribution
: For every
x\in M
with infinite
\Gamma_{\mu}
-orbit, we have the weak-
*
convergence:

\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}\rightarrow{\rm vol}.

Our result applies in a
variety of situations
, including perturbations of Zariski-dense linear random walks on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, on cocompact lattice quotients of
\mathrm{SO}(2,1)
and
\mathrm{SO}(3,1)
, and on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. We also treat the case
d=2
for general surfaces, without requiring perturbations of an underlying linear dynamics.

Our methods also apply in
dissipative
settings, when
\Gamma_{\mu}
is not assumed to preserve volume. In this context, the rigidity statement must be modified as follows: there exists a unique atom-free
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\Upsilon_{\mu}
on
M
. Moreover,
\Upsilon_{\mu}
is full-dimensional in the sense that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
x\in M
and
r>0
,

\Upsilon_{\mu}(B_{r}(x))\leq C_{\varepsilon}r^{d-\varepsilon}.

The corresponding equidistribution statement continues to hold: for every
x
not contained in a finite
\mu
-invariant set, we have

\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}\rightarrow\Upsilon_{\mu}.

In the dissipative setting, we still require
\mu
to be weak-
*
close to a volume-preserving probability measure (conservative case). Note this allows
{\rm supp}(\mu)
to contain highly volume-disrupting elements, although with small mass.

Related works
. Rigidity phenomena for stationary measures were first studied in the linear setting. In their breakthrough work
[
BFLM
]
, Bourgain-Furman-Lindenstrauss-Mozes consider a random walk on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
driven by a finitely supported strongly irreducible, proximal, probability measure on
{\rm SL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
. By means of Fourier analysis and techniques from additive combinatorics, they are able to show rigidity (a) and equidistribution (b) as stated above. Soon after, Benoist-Quint
[
BQ2
]
provide another proof of (a) for linear walks on the torus, making use of completely different tools inspired by the proof of Ratner’s rigidity theorems for unipotent flows. Benoist-Quint’s method has the advantage to extend to arbitrary lattice quotients of Lie groups provided the measure driving the walk satisfies a certain semisimplicity assumption
[
BQ2
,
BQ3
]
. In
[
BQ4
]
, classification of stationary measures allow Benoist-Quint to derive a weak version of (b), in the sense that convergence only holds in Cesaro average:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}\rightarrow{\rm vol}.

Regarding non-homogeneous settings, Benoist-Quint’s approach has been influential in the groundbreaking work of Eskin-Mirzakhani
[
EM
]
, on rigidity properties of the
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-action on moduli space of translation surfaces, and in the landmark work of Brown-Rodriguez Hertz
[
BRH
]
dealing with random walks by diffeomorphisms on closed surfaces. The latter shows that any ergodic stationary measure for a random walk on a surface falls in one of three categories: either it is uniform on a finite invariant set, or the stable direction of the walk is non-random,
or the measure is SRB meaning it is leafwise absolutely continuous for a notion of unstable foliation. Building on techniques from Tsujii
[
Tsu
]
, Brown-Lee-Obata-Ruan
[
BLOR
,
BLOR2
]
are able to promote this SRB conclusion to absolute continuity with respect to volume under certain expansion conditions.
Note the works
[
BRH
,
BLOR
,
BLOR2
]
are constrained to dimension
2
, and rely on techniques in the spirit of Benoist-Quint/Eskin-Mirzakhani. For that reason, they can derive equidistribution in Cesaro average only. The question of removing the Cesaro average is explicitly asked in
[
BLOR2
, Question 1.2]
.

Our result deals with arbitrary dimension
d\geq 2
and shows equidistribution without extra Cesaro-averaging
.

In their recent
tour de force

[
BEFRH
]
, Brown-Eskin-Filip-Rodriguez Hertz establish a measure rigidity theorem in a general framework, applicable in all dimensions. In the deterministic setting, namely for iterates of a single diffeomorphism, they classify all generalized u-Gibbs states under a Quantitative Non-Integrability condition. This condition is relatively mild; see
[
EPZ
,
ES
]
.
In the random walk setting, they prove that every ergodic
\mu
-stationary measure is
\Gamma_{\mu}
-invariant under suitable expansion assumptions, and further show absolute continuity whenever the measure has no zero Lyapunov exponents.
One advantage of our approach in the random walk setting is that
we allow for the presence of zero Lyapunov exponents
.
While such a phenomenon cannot occur on surfaces, where it is excluded by expansion, it arises naturally in dimensions
3
and higher. For instance, if
\mu
is symmetric (i.e. invariant under
g\mapsto g^{-1}
) and
M
is odd-dimensional, then every ergodic
\mu
-stationary measure has a zero Lyapunov exponent.

The approach we follow is in the spirit of that of
[
BFLM
]
for the torus case mentioned above, and more precisely its recent extension to the setting of lattice quotients of simple Lie groups
[
BH
,
BH2
]
. Noting that rigidity (a) follows from equidistribution (b), the proof is divided in three steps: (Phase I) for large enough
n
, we show the distribution
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}
has positive dimension above scale
\rho_{\mu}(n)>0
where
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{\mu}(n)=0
. (Phase II) We bootstrap positive dimension to almost full dimension using a local variant of the multislicing method from
[
BH
,
BH2
]
. (Phase III) We promote high dimension to equidistribution by invoking the spectral gap of the convolution operator
f\mapsto f*\mu
on an appropriate Sobolev space. This spectral result follows from recent work of
[
DD
,
DDZ
]
.

We point out that the method of proof is quite close to provide a rate for the convergence
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}\rightarrow\Upsilon_{\mu}
. Indeed, Phases II and III are quantitative, the only obstruction lies in Phase I where the function
\rho_{\mu}(n)
is not explicit. In the homogeneous setting, making Phase I quantitative is possible but usually requires arithmetic assumptions (as in
[
BFLM
,
BH
,
BH2
,
BG
,
LMW
,
Yan
]
etc.) which are not naturally available in the smooth setting. Obtaining positive-dimensional estimates without arithmetic input is a well-known open question.

1.1.
Examples of application

Let
M
be a closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
. Equip
M
with a volume form and write
{\rm vol}
the associated volume measure on
M
, normalized so that
{\rm vol}(M)=1
. We say that a measure
\Upsilon
on
M
is full dimensional if the following holds:
\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists C_{\varepsilon}>0,\forall x\in M,\forall r>0
,
\Upsilon(B_{r}(x))\leq C_{\varepsilon}r^{d-\varepsilon}.
Here
B_{r}(x)
refers to the open ball of radius
r
and center
x
in
M
. Note that the property of being full dimensional does not depend on the metric.

We denote by
{\rm Diff}^{k}(M)

the set of
C^{k}
-diffeomorphisms of
M
endowed with the
C^{k}
-topology, and
{\rm Diff}^{k}(M,{\rm vol})\subseteq{\rm Diff}^{k}(M)
the closed subset of those preserving the volume. Given a Borel set
A\subset{\rm Diff}^{k}(M)
, we write
{\mathcal{P}}(A)
the set of Borel probability measures on
A
, endowed with the weak-
*
topology, and call
{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(A)
the subset of measures having compact support. The subsets of
{\rm Diff}^{k}(M)
we will consider will always be Borel subsets, though sometimes we may omit to mention it. Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Diff}^{k}(M))
we write
\Gamma_{\mu}
the semigroup generated by its support.

Our main results apply to show rigidity of stationary measures and equidistribution in various contexts, as recorded in the next theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu_{0}
be a compactly supported probability measure on
{\rm Diff}^{2}(M,{\rm vol})
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\rm Diff}^{2}(M)
be a bounded set containing
{\rm supp}(\mu_{0})
.

Assume
\mu_{0}
pertains to either the surface case (I), or the torus case (II), or the lattice quotient case (III) described below.

Then there is a neighborhood
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mu_{0}
in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{K}})
with respect to the weak-
\star
topology, such that every
\mu\in\mathfrak{U}
has the following property:

(a)

There exists a unique atom-free
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\Upsilon_{\mu}
on
M
. Moreover
\Upsilon_{\mu}
is full dimensional, and absolutely continuous with density in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(M)
for some
s=s(\mu_{0})>0
.

(b)

For every
x\in M
, either
x
has finite
\Gamma_{\mu}
-orbit, or
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}=\Upsilon_{\mu}
.

Remark 1.2
.

If the measure

in Theorem

is supported on

so we recover items (a) and (b) from the introductory paragraph.

We first need to introduce a gap condition. Informally, a measure

Definition 1.6

(Gap)
.

Consider integers

\displaystyle\int_{G}\Big(\log\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V)^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|-\log\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{S}}(V)}\|Dg(x,v)\|\Big)d\mu^{*n_{0}}(g)<-n_{0}\kappa.

Theorem 1.9
.

3.1.
Gaps and pinching of singular values

We investigate how gap/pinching assumptions on the measure
\mu
(as defined in §
1.2
) affect the behavior of the singular values of a typical element
g\in G
sampled by
\mu^{*n}
.

Given
x\in M
and
g\in G
, we fix a
Cartan decomposition
of
Dg(x)
. More precisely, we write

\displaystyle Dg(x)=R^{\prime}(x,g)\mathop{\rm diag}(e^{\lambda^{(1)}(x,g)},\cdots,e^{\lambda^{(d)}(x,g)})R(x,g)

for some reals
\lambda^{(1)}(x,g)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda^{(d)}(x,g)
, and some linear isometries
R^{\prime}(x,g):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to T_{g(x)}M
and
R(x,g):T_{x}M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We observe that the vector of exponents
(\lambda^{(1)}(x,g),\cdots,\lambda^{(d)}(x,g))
is unique: it lists the logarithm of the singular values of
Dg(x)
. The maps
R^{\prime}(x,g),R(x,g)
are not necessarily unique.

The next lemma provides a variational characterization of singular values of
Dg(x)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
b\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, and all
g\in G
,
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)}

\displaystyle\,=\,\inf_{V\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b)}\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V)^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|

\displaystyle\,=\,\sup_{V^{\prime}\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b+1)}\inf_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\prime})}\|Dg(x,v^{\prime})\|.

Proof.

We show the first equality, the proof of the second one is similar. As isometries do not affect the norm and commute with taking the orthogonal, it is enough to show the result for the diagonal part
A\in\GL({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
given by
A=\mathop{\rm diag}(e^{\lambda^{(1)}(x,g)},\cdots,e^{\lambda^{(d)}(x,g)})
instead of
Dg(x)
, and subspaces varying in
\Gr({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
instead of
T_{x}M
.
Set
W=\{0\}^{b-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-b+1}
, and note that the action
A\curvearrowright W
satisfies
\|Aw\|\geq e^{\lambda_{b}(x,g)}\|w\|
for every
w\in W
. Let
V\in\Gr(d,d-b)
. Note that for dimensional reasons, there exists
w\in W\cap(AV)^{\perp}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. Then setting
u=P_{V^{\perp}}A^{-1}w
, and noting
P_{(AV)^{\perp}}AV=0
, we find

\|P_{(AV)^{\perp}}Au\|=\|P_{(AV)^{\perp}}AA^{-1}w\|=\|w\|.

Normalizing so that
\|u\|=1
, we have
\|w\|\geq e^{\lambda_{b}(x,g)}
and the desired inequality follows. Equality is achieved by taking
V=W
.
∎

We now use Lemma
3.2
to relate the gap
\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)-\lambda^{(b^{\prime})}(x,g)
between the
b
-th and
b^{\prime}
-th smallest logarithmic singular values of
Dg(x)
, to the expansion/contraction data that appear in the gap or pinching condition from §
1.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

For all integers
b,b^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots d\}
, all
g\in G
,
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)-\lambda^{(b^{\prime})}(x,g)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\Gr(T_{x}M,\,d-b)\\
V^{\prime}\in\Gr(T_{x}M,\,d-b^{\prime}+1)\end{subarray}}\left(\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V)^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|/\inf_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\prime})}\|Dg(x,v^{\prime})\|\right).

Proof.

It is a direct application of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

In order to make use of Corollary
3.3
, we now present how a gap/pinching condition can be converted into a moment bound for quotient of derivatives.

We are now in a position to conclude the paragraph, by showing that a
b
-gap condition on
\mu
yields a linear separation between
\lambda_{b}(x,g)
and
\lambda_{b+1}(x,g)
as
x\in M
and
g\sim\mu^{*n}
, while on the other hand, a
(b_{0},b_{1})
-pinching condition is reflected by the proximity of
\lambda_{b_{0}+1}(x,g)
and
\lambda_{b_{1}}(x,g)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The following is true.

(1)

Let
b\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
,
\kappa>\kappa^{\prime}>c>0
,
n,n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in M
. If
\mu
has a
(n_{0},\kappa,b)
-gap,
and
c\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}1
, then

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}\Big(g\in G\mid\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)+n\kappa^{\prime}<\lambda^{(b+1)}(x,g)\Big)>1-2e^{-cn}.

(2)

Let
b_{0}<b_{1}
be integers in
\{0,\dots,d\}
, let
\eta^{\prime}>\eta>c>0
,
n,n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in M
.
If
\mu
is
(n_{0},\eta,b_{0},b_{1})
-pinched, and
c\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime}}1
, then

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}\Big(g\in G\mid\lambda^{(b_{0}+1)}(x,g)+n\eta^{\prime}>\lambda^{b_{1}}(x,g)\Big)>1-2e^{-cn}.

Proof.

We establish (1). The proof of (2) is similar.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}\in(0,1/2)
.

Let
V\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b)
. By Corollary
3.3
, we have for every
g\in G
,

\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)-\lambda^{(b+1)}(x,g)\leq\log\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V)^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|-\log\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{S}}(V)}\|Dg(x,v)\|.

Set
\psi(g,V)
the right-hand side of the above equation. Note that
\psi:G\times\Gr(TM,d-b)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
W\in\Gr(TM,d-b)
,

\psi(g_{2}g_{1},W)\leq\psi(g_{2},g_{1}W)+\psi(g_{1},W).

Moreover
\psi
is bounded on
{\rm supp}(\mu^{*n_{0}})\times\Gr(TM,d-b)
, and the
(n_{0},\kappa,b)
-gap assumption means that

\sup_{V\in\Gr(TM,d-b)}{\mathbb{E}}_{g\sim\mu^{*n_{0}}}(\psi(g,V))<-n_{0}\kappa.

We may then apply the general large deviation estimate formulated in Proposition
A.1
to
E:=G\times\Gr(TM,d-b)
,
f:=\psi+n_{0}\kappa
and the Markov chain on
E
with the transition probabilities

P_{(h,W)}=\mu^{*n_{0}}\otimes\delta_{hW}
. We obtain for all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mu^{*kn_{0}}\Big(g\in G\mid\lambda^{(b)}(x,g)-\lambda^{(b+1)}(x,g)\geq-kn_{0}(\kappa+\kappa^{\prime})/2\Big)<e^{-cn}.

where
c=c(M,{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})>0
. Noting that for any
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
g,h\in G
,
x\in M
, we have

|\lambda^{(j)}(x,hg)-\lambda^{(j)}(x,g)|\leq\log(\max\|Dh(gx)\|,\|Dh^{-1}(gx)\|),

Item (1) in the lemma follows for all
n
, up to reducing
c
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

In the proof of Lemma
3.4
above, we only used the second part of Proposition
A.1
. By applying the first part, we obtain the following complementary information:
\exists\gamma=\gamma(M,{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa)>0
,
\forall n\geq 0
,
\forall x\in M
,
\forall V\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b)
,

\displaystyle\int\Big(\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V)^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|/\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{S}}(V)}\|Dg(x,v)\|\Big)^{\gamma}d\mu^{*n}(g)\,\leq\,2e^{-n\gamma\kappa}.

This estimate will play a role in the proof of Proposition
3.6
below.

3.2.
Non-concentration of the contracting filtration

Given
g\in G
,
x\in M
, we introduce a flag of
T_{x}M
given by

\displaystyle\{0\}\subsetneq W_{1}(x,g)\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq W_{d}(x,g)=T_{x}M

where

(2)

\displaystyle W_{i}(x,g)=R(x,g)^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{i}\times\{0\}^{d-i}),\quad i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}.

Roughly speaking,
W_{i}(x,g)
is a “most contracted”
i
-dimensional subspace of
T_{x}M
under
Dg(x)
. If
\lambda^{(i)}(x,g)<\lambda^{(i+1)}(x,g)
, then
W_{i}(x,g)
is uniquely defined (indepedently of the choices for
R^{\prime}(x,g)
,
R(x,g)
).

In the next proposition, we assume that
\mu
has a
b
-gap and examine the statistics of
W_{b}(x,g)\in T_{x}M

as
g
varies randomly according to
\mu^{*n}
. Note that by Lemma
3.4
, the random subspace
W_{b}(x,g)
is uniquely defined for most
g\sim\mu^{*n}
. We show it satisfies a certain non-concentration property at scale
\rho\geq e^{-n}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
b\in\{1,\cdots,d-1\}
,
\kappa,c,\rho>0
,
n,n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in M
and
V\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b)
. Assume that
\mu
has a
(n_{0},\kappa,b)
-gap, and the bounds
c,\rho\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa}1
and
n\geq|\log\rho|
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}(g\in G\mid\measuredangle(W_{b}(x,g),V)<\rho)<\rho^{c}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that with probability
1-\rho^{c}
, every vector
v^{\prime}\in T_{x}M
of the form

v^{\prime}=v+u
where
v\in{\mathbb{S}}(V)
,
u\in V^{\perp}
,
\|u\|\leq\rho
, satisfies

\|Dg(x,v^{\prime})\|>2e^{\lambda_{b}(x,g)}.

We consider a sequence of integers
0=k_{0}\leq k_{1}\leq\dots\leq k_{i}\leq\dots
which will be specified later depending on
M,{\mathcal{K}},\kappa
.

Given a sequence
{\underline{g}}=(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in G^{{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
, we define
(h_{i})_{i\geq 1}
by
h_{i}=g_{k_{i}}\dots g_{k_{i-1}+1}
, and set
x_{i}=h_{i}\dots h_{1}x
,
V_{i}=D(h_{i}\dots h_{1})(x)V
.
Under the splittings
T_{x_{i-1}}M=V_{i-1}\oplus V_{i-1}^{\perp}
and
T_{x_{i}}M=V_{i}\oplus V_{i}^{\perp}
, we may write

\displaystyle Dh_{i}(x_{i-1})=\begin{bmatrix}A_{i}&C_{i}\\
0&B_{i}\end{bmatrix}

where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{i-1},V_{i})
,
B_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(V^{\perp}_{i-1},V^{\perp}_{i})
,
C_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(V^{\perp}_{i-1},V_{i})
.
Consider
l_{i}:=k_{i}-k_{i-1}
. By applying Remark
3.5
(conditioned on
g_{1},\dots,g_{k_{i-1}}
), we have for some
\gamma>0
depending on
M,{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\underline{g}}\sim\mu^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}}[(\|A_{i}^{-1}\|\|B_{i}\|)^{\gamma}]\leq 2e^{-l_{i}\gamma\kappa}.

The Markov inequality then implies

\mu^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}({\underline{g}}\,:\,\|A_{i}^{-1}\|\|B_{i}\|>e^{-l_{i}\kappa/4})\leq 2e^{-\frac{3}{4}l_{i}\gamma\kappa.}

Let
s\in(0,1)
be a parameter to be specified below depending on
M,{\mathcal{K}},\kappa
.
Choosing
(k_{i})_{i\geq 1}
so that
l_{i}\geq l_{1}+s\,i
, the upper bound is summable over
i
with total sum
O_{\gamma,\kappa,s}(e^{-\frac{3}{4}l_{1}\gamma\kappa})
. By subadditivity of measures, it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle\mu^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}(E)\geq 1-O_{\gamma,\kappa,s}(e^{-\frac{3}{4}l_{1}\gamma\kappa})

for

E:=\{{\underline{g}}\,:\,\forall i\geq 1,\,\,\|A_{i}^{-1}\|\|B_{i}\|\leq e^{-l_{i}\kappa/4}\}.

Let
{\underline{g}}\in E
. We now check that any vector
u
as described above has the desired growth under
g_{n}\dots g_{1}
. To do so, we will rely on an iterated application of the next lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
i\geq 1
. Let
v^{\prime}_{i-1}\in T_{x_{i-1}}M
of the form
v^{\prime}_{i-1}=v_{i-1}+u_{i-1}
where
(v_{i-1},u_{i-1})\in V_{i-1}\times V^{\perp}_{i-1}
and
\|v_{i-1}\|=1
,
\|u_{i-1}\|\leq 2^{-1}D_{1}^{-2l_{i}}
.

Then
D(h_{i})(x_{i-1},v^{\prime}_{i-1})=R_{i}(v^{\prime}_{i}+u_{i})
where
R_{i}\geq 2^{-1}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{-1}
is a constant and
(v_{i},u_{i})\in V_{i}\times V^{\perp}_{i}
satisfy
\|v_{i}\|=1
,
\|u_{i}\|\leq 2e^{-l_{i}\kappa/4}\|u_{i-1}\|
.

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

We can write
h_{i}v^{\prime}_{i-1}=(A_{i}v_{i-1}+C_{i}u_{i-1})+B_{i}u_{i-1}
where the first term (in brackets) belongs to
V_{i}
and the second term belongs to
V^{\perp}_{i}
. We have

(4)

\displaystyle\|A_{i}v_{i-1}+C_{i}u_{i-1}\|\geq\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{-1}-\|C_{i}\|\|u_{i-1}\|\geq 2^{-1}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{-1}

where the first inequality uses
\|v_{i-1}\|=1
and the second inequality uses
\|A^{-1}_{i}\|,\|C_{i}\|\leq D_{1}^{l_{i}}
and
\|u_{i-1}\|\leq 2^{-1}D_{1}^{-2l_{i}}.

On the other hand, recalling
{\underline{g}}\in E
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{\|B_{i}u_{i-1}\|}{2^{-1}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{-1}}\leq 2\|A_{i}^{-1}\|\|B_{i}\|\|u_{i-1}\|\leq 2e^{-l_{i}\kappa/4}\|u_{i-1}\|.

Equations (
4
), (
5
) together justify Lemma
3.7
. ∎

We may now conclude the proof of Proposition
3.6
. Assume
\rho\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},l_{1}}1
so that
\rho<2^{-1}D_{1}^{-2l_{1}}
.
Choose
(k_{i})_{i\geq 0}
depending on
{\mathcal{K}},\kappa,s
so that
l_{1}
satisfies
2e^{-l_{1}\kappa/4}<e^{-l_{1}\kappa/8}
, and
l_{i}=l_{1}+\lceil s\,i\rceil
. Note
4
4

4

Even though
l_{i}
depends
s
, this claim can be checked by direct computation.
that by assuming
s\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},\kappa}1
, we have
e^{-s^{-1}l_{i}\kappa/8}<2^{-1}D_{1}^{-2(l_{i}+1)}
. The condition on
\rho
allows to apply Lemma
3.7
to
v^{\prime}_{0}:=v^{\prime}
, thus yielding a vector
v^{\prime}_{1}
, and the choice of
l_{i}
and
s
allow to iterate, thus yielding
v^{\prime}_{2},v^{\prime}_{3}
etc.
By construction, we know that

\|D(h_{j}\dots h_{1})(x,v^{\prime})\|=\|v^{\prime}_{j}\|\geq 2^{-j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{-1}\geq 2^{-j}e^{(\sum_{i=1}^{j}l_{i})\kappa/4+\lambda_{b}(x,h_{j}\dots h_{1})}

where the last inequality uses

\|A_{i}^{-1}\|\|B_{i}\|\leq e^{-l_{i}\kappa/4}\quad\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\quad\prod_{i=1}^{j}\|B_{i}\|\geq e^{\lambda^{(b)}(x,h_{j}\dots h_{1})}

(from Lemma
3.2
). Coosing
j_{0}=\min\{j\,:\ l_{1}+\dots+l_{j}\geq n\}
, we get

\|D(g_{n}\dots g_{1})(x,v^{\prime})\|\geq 2^{-j_{0}}D_{1}^{-2l_{j_{0}}}e^{(\sum_{i=1}^{j_{0}}l_{i})\kappa/4+\lambda_{b}(x,g_{n}\dots h_{1})}.

Choosing
\rho\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},\kappa,(k_{i})}1
and
n\geq|\log\rho|
, we have
(\sum_{i=1}^{j_{0}}l_{i})\kappa/8\geq 2l_{j_{0}}\log D_{1}+j_{0}\log 2
, thus yielding the desired growth:

\|D(g_{n}\dots g_{1})(x,v^{\prime})\|\geq e^{(\sum_{i=0}^{j_{0}}l_{i})\kappa/8+\lambda_{b}(x,g_{n}\dots h_{1})}.

Note that all the conditions imposed along the proof allow to choose
(k_{i})
and
s
in terms of
M,{\mathcal{K}},\kappa
only, and
\rho\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},\kappa}1
.
Recalling (
3
), this concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition
3.6
will play a crucial role in the rest of the paper.
As a first application, we use it to show that gaps at two consecutive entries implies pinching.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
b_{0}\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
,
\kappa>0
.
Assume that
\mu
has a
(\kappa,\{b_{0},b_{0}+1,b_{0}+2\})
-gap.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, the measure
\mu
is
(\varepsilon,\{b_{0},b_{0}+1\})
-pinched.

Note however that in the above statement, the time
n
needed for
\mu
to be
(n,\varepsilon,b_{0},b_{0+1})
-pinched a priori goes to infinity when
\varepsilon
tends to
0
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

We deal with the case
b_{0}\in\{1,\dots,d-2\}
, the proof can easily be adapted to the limit case
b_{0}\in\{0,d-1\}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\varepsilon<\min(\kappa,1)
.
Let
n\geq 1
, set
\rho=e^{-n\varepsilon/10}
.

We fix some
x\in M
,
V_{0}\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b_{0})
and
V_{1}\in\Gr(T_{x}M,d-b_{0}-1)
with
V_{1}\subset V_{0}
.
We set

\displaystyle A_{0}=\{g\in G\mid\measuredangle(W_{b_{0}}(x,g),V_{0})<\rho\},

\displaystyle A_{1}=\{g\in G\mid\measuredangle(W_{b_{0}+1}(x,g),V_{1})<\rho\}.

By Proposition
3.6
, provided that
n\ggg_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa}1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}(A_{0})<\rho^{c},\ \mu^{*n}(A_{1})<\rho^{c}.

where
c=c(M,{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\kappa)>0
.
For every
g\in{\rm supp}(\mu^{*n})\setminus(A_{0}\cup A_{1})
, it is direct to verify that

\displaystyle\log\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}(V_{1}^{\perp})}\|P_{Dg(x,V_{1})^{\perp}}(Dg(x,u))\|\leq(\lambda^{(b_{0}+1)}+\varepsilon/3)n,

\displaystyle\log\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{S}}(V_{0})}\|Dg(x,v)\|\geq(\lambda^{(b_{0}+1)}-\varepsilon/3)n.

This implies that
\mu
is
(n,\varepsilon,b_{0},b_{0}+1)
-pinched.
∎

3.3.
Almost volume-preserving estimate

We continue to denote by
\mu
a compactly supported Borel probability measure on
G={\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
, as in Setting
3.1
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\eta>0
. We say
\mu
is
(n_{0},\eta)
-almost volume-preserving if for every
x\in M
,

\int_{G}\log\det Dg(x)d\mu^{*n_{0}}(g)\in(-n_{0}\eta,n_{0}\eta).

The next lemma is a large deviation estimate for the determinant of an element
g
selected by an almost volume-preserving measure.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume
\mu
is
(n_{0},\eta)
-almost volume-preserving. Let
\eta^{\prime}>\eta
. Then for
c\lll_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime}}1
, every
n\geq 1
,
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}\Big(g\in G\mid\det Dg(x)\in(e^{-n\eta^{\prime}},e^{n\eta^{\prime}})\Big)>1-2e^{-nc}.

Proof.

Set
E=G\times M
, consider
\psi:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
the function given by
\psi(g,x)=\log\det Dg(x)
. Note that
\psi
is a cocycle:
\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
x\in M
,

\psi(g_{2}g_{1},x)=\psi(g_{2},g_{1}x)+\psi(g_{1},x).

Note also that
\psi
is bounded on
{\rm supp}(\mu^{*n_{0}})\times M
by
O_{{\mathcal{K}},n_{0}}(1)
, and by assumption, we have for every
x\in M
,

\int_{G}\psi(g,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{*n_{0}}(g)\in(-n_{0}\eta,n_{0}\eta).

We are thus in a position to apply the large deviation estimate formulated in Proposition
A.1
, for the functions
f_{1},f_{2}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by
f_{1}(g,x)=\psi(g,x)-n_{0}\eta
,
f_{2}(g,x)=-\psi(g,x)-n_{0}\eta
, and the Markov chain on
E
with transition probabilities
P_{(h,W)}=\mu^{*n_{0}}\otimes\delta_{hW}
. We obtain for all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mu^{*kn_{0}}\Big(g\in G\mid\det Dg(x)\notin(e^{-kn_{0}(\eta+\eta^{\prime})/2},e^{n(\eta+\eta^{\prime})/2})\Big)<e^{-kn_{0}c}.

where
c=c(M,{\mathcal{K}},n_{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime})>0
. Using that for any
g,h\in G
,
x\in M
,

|\log\det D(hg)(x)-\log\det Dg(x)|\leq d\log\max(\|Dh(gx)\|,\|Dh^{-1}(gx)\|),

we obtain the announced result (for all
n
) up to taking
c
smaller. ∎

3.4.
Expansion and coexpansion

Keep the notations of Setting
3.1
. We study the action of the
\mu
-random walk on the tangent and cotangent bundles of
M
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We say that
\mu
is
(n_{0},\kappa)
-expanding if

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in M,v\in\mathbb{S}(T_{x}M)}\,\int_{G}\log\|Dg(x,v)\|\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{*n_{0}}(g)>n_{0}\kappa.

We say that
\mu
is
(n_{0},\kappa)
-coexpanding if

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in M,\xi\in\mathbb{S}(T^{*}_{x}M)}\,\int_{G}\log\|\xi\circ(Dg(x))^{-1}\|\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{*n_{0}}(g)>n_{0}\kappa.

Note that by definition
\xi\circ(Dg(x))^{-1}\in T^{*}_{gx}M
is a linear form on
T_{gx}M
.
Note also we may allow
\kappa
in Definition
3.11
to be negative.

We see that under the conditions of Theorem
1.9
,
\mu
is always expanding.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
b\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
,
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime},\eta>0
,
n_{0},n_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that the measure
\mu
has a
(n_{0},\kappa,b)
-gap, is
(n_{0},\eta,b,d)
-pinched, and is
(n_{0},\eta)
-almost volume-preserving.
If

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
which captures the geometry of the box is chosen randomly by a measure satisfying suitable projection theorems. Compared to
[
BH2
]
, we allow the measure
\nu
to be supported on a microscopic ball and have microscopic mass, as well as the side lengths of the boxes to vary.

Since
M
is irrelevant to this section, all implicit constants are stated explicitly in the statements in this section.

5.1.
Boxes

Let
d\geq 2
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}
the full flag variety of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, in other terms it is the collection of all the tuples of subspaces
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\{0\}\subsetneq W_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq W_{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad\forall i,\,\dim W_{i}=i.

We set

\boxslash:=\{(t_{i})_{i=1}^{d}:\,0\leq t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{d}\leq 1\}.

For
\mathcal{W}=(W_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathcal{F}
,
\mathbf{t}=(t_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\boxslash
, and
\rho\in(0,1)
, we introduce the box

B_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}}}^{\mathcal{W}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}B^{W_{i}}_{\rho^{t_{i}}}.

We call
\mathcal{W}
the flag (or the filtration) carrying the box
B_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}}}^{\mathcal{W}}
.

We will consider boxes
B_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}}}^{\mathcal{W}}
whose exponent
\mathbf{t}
satisfies certain gap and pinching conditions. For that, given
m\in\{1,\cdots,d-1\}
, we set
P_{m}(d)=\{\mathbf{d}=(d_{i})_{i=1}^{m+1}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m+1}\,:\,0<d_{1}<\dots<d_{m+1}=d\}
. For
\mathbf{d}\in P_{m}(d)
,
k_{0}\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
,
\kappa,\eta>0
, we introduce the notation

\boxslash(\mathbf{d},k_{0},\kappa,\eta)
to be the collection of
\mathbf{t}\in\boxslash
such that

•

t_{d_{2}}\geq\kappa
,

•

t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}-t_{d_{k_{0}}}\geq\kappa
,

•

for all
k\in\{0,\cdots,m\}
,
i,j\in\{d_{k}+1,\cdots,d_{k+1}\}
, one has
|t_{i}-t_{j}|\leq\eta
(where
d_{0}=0
by convention).

5.2.
Projection theorems

Fix an integer
d^{\prime}\in\{1,\cdots,d-1\}
. The following definition depicts abstractly what it means for a measure on the Grassmannian of
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
to satisfy a
subcritical projection theorem
. Given
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\rho>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)
the
\rho
-covering number of
A
, i.e. the least number of open
\rho
-balls needed to cover
A
. .

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a probability measure on
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
. Let
\rho,\varepsilon,\tau>0
. We say
\sigma
has the
subcritical property

\SubP
with parameters
(\rho,\varepsilon,\tau)
if for every set
A\subseteq B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
, the exceptional set

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}:=\Bigl\{\,W\in\Gr(d,d^{\prime})\mid\exists A^{\prime}\subseteq A\,\,&\text{ with }\,\,\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A^{\prime})\geq\rho^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)\\
&\text{ and }\,\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(P_{W^{\perp}}A^{\prime})<\rho^{\tau}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)^{\frac{d-d^{\prime}}{d}}\Bigr\}\end{split}

has measure
\sigma(\mathcal{E})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon}
.

The quantity
d-d^{\prime}
appearing in
\mathcal{E}
should be interpreted as
\dim W^{\perp}
, i.e. the rank of the projector
P_{W^{\perp}}
. Roughly speaking,
\SubP
requires that for any fixed
A\subseteq B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
, for most subspaces
W
chosen by
\sigma
, for every rather large subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
, the orthogonal projection of
A^{\prime}
to
W^{\perp}
has covering number at least
\rho^{\tau}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)^{\frac{\dim W^{\perp}}{d}}
. Here the parameter
\tau
expresses a dimensional loss compared to the heuristic that
\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(P_{W^{\perp}}A)\geq\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)^{\frac{\dim W^{\perp}}{d}}
(for which equality is achieved when
A
is a ball).

The following is a useful criterion for verifying the subcritical property.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a probability measure on
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
. Let
r,c>0
.
We say that
\sigma
has the
non-concentration property
\NCSub
with parameters
(r,c)
if the following holds.

For every non-zero linear subspace
V\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, setting

\displaystyle\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V)=\{W^{\prime}\in\Gr(d,d^{\prime})\mid\dim(V\cap W^{\prime})/\dim(V)>d^{\prime}/d\},

we have

\displaystyle\sigma(W\in\Gr(d,d^{\prime})\mid{\rm dist}(W,\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V))<r)<r^{c}.

The collection
\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V)
is called the
d^{\prime}
-dimensional
constraining Schubert variety
at
V
. Avoiding constraining Schubert varieties is a necessary condition for a measure to satisfy a subcritical projection theorem with small dimension loss. The following result, extracted from
[
BH2
, Theorem 4.1]
, claims it is also sufficient.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
c,\varepsilon,\rho\in(0,1/10)
such that
\varepsilon\lll_{d,c}1
and
\rho\lll_{d,c,\varepsilon}1
.
If
\sigma
satisfies
\NCSub
with parameters
(\rho^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},c)
, then
\sigma
has the subcritical property
\SubP
with parameters
(\rho,\varepsilon,Dc^{-1}\sqrt{\varepsilon})
for some
D=O_{d}(1)
.

We now turn to supercritical estimates. Given
\alpha,\tau,\rho>0
, and
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we set

(12)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{(\alpha,\tau)}_{\rho}(A):=\Big\{\,W\in\Gr(d,d^{\prime})\mid\exists A^{\prime}\subseteq A\,\,&\text{ with }\,\,\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A^{\prime})\geq\rho^{\tau}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A)\\
&\text{ and }\,\,\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(P_{W^{\perp}}A^{\prime})<\rho^{-\alpha(d-d^{\prime})-\tau}\Big\}.\end{split}

Again the quantity
d-d^{\prime}
appearing here should be interpreted as the rank of the projector
P_{W^{\perp}}
.

The following definition depicts abstractly what it means for a measure on
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
to satisfy a
supercritical projection theorem
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\sigma
be a probability measures on
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
. Let
\rho,\varkappa,\tau>0
. We say
\sigma
has the
supercritical property

\SupP
with parameters
(\rho,\varkappa,\tau)
if the following holds.

Let
A\subseteq B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
be any non-empty subset satisfying for some

\alpha\in[\varkappa,1-\varkappa]
, for every
r\geq\rho
,

(13)

\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A\cap(B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{r}+v))\leq\rho^{-\tau}r^{d\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(A).

Then we have

\sigma(\mathcal{E}^{(\alpha,\tau)}_{\rho}(A))\leq\rho^{\tau}.

We record a useful criterion for verifying the supercritical property.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\sigma
be a probability measure on
\Gr(d,d^{\prime})
. Let
\rho,\varepsilon,c>0
.
We say that
\sigma
has the
non-concentration property

\NCSup
with parameters
(\rho,\varepsilon,c)
if for every
V\in\Gr(d,d-d^{\prime})
we have

\displaystyle\sigma(W\in\Gr(d,d^{\prime})\mid\measuredangle(V,W)<r)<\rho^{-\varepsilon}r^{c},\quad r\geq\rho.

The following is due to Bourgain
[
Bou
, Theorem 5]
for projectors of rank
1
(i.e.
d^{\prime}=d-1
) and to He
[
He
, Theorem 1]
in higher rank (i.e.
d^{\prime}\leq d-2
).

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\varkappa,c,\varepsilon,\rho>0
with
\rho,\varepsilon\lll_{d,\varkappa,c}1
. If
\sigma
satisfies
\NCSup
with parameters
(\rho,\varepsilon,c)
, then

\sigma
has the supercritical property
\SupP
with parameters
(\rho,\varkappa,\varepsilon)
.

We end this subsection by observing that the non-concentration condition
\NCSup
in the supercritical theorem is stronger than its subcritical counterpart
\NCSub
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
c,\varepsilon,\rho>0
with
\rho\lll_{d}1
and
4\varepsilon<c
. If
\sigma
satisfies
\NCSup
with parameters
(\rho,\varepsilon,c)
,
then
\sigma
satisfies
\NCSub
with parameters

(\rho,c/4)
.

Proof.

Let
V\in\Gr({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\smallsetminus\{0\}
. We will give a lower bound for the distance between
\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V)
and most
W\sim\sigma
.

Fix an arbitrary subspace
U\subseteq V
with

\dim U=\min(\dim V,\,d-d^{\prime}).

Given any
W^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V)
, observe that
\dim W^{\prime}\cap V+\dim U>\dim V
, thus

W^{\prime}\cap U\neq\{0\}
. In particular, if
{\rm dist}(W,W^{\prime})<r
, then every line of
W^{\prime}
is
O_{d}(r)
-close to a line in
W
, which leads to

\measuredangle(W,U)=O_{d}(r).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sigma(W\mid{\rm dist}(W,\Sigma_{d^{\prime}}(V))<r)\leq\sigma(W\mid\measuredangle(W,U)=O_{d}(r))\leq\rho^{-\varepsilon}(O_{d}(r))^{c}

where the last upper bound relies on the
\NCSup
assumption. By taking
r=\rho
, and assuming
\rho\lll_{d}1
;
4\varepsilon<c
, this finishes the proof. ∎

5.3.
Multislicing theorem

We present the main result of the section, Theorem
5.8
. Before giving the precise statement, we describe the heuristic.

First, we consider a random box in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, of the form
(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
where
\theta
is a random variable with distribution
\sigma
,
(\mathcal{W}_{\theta})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
is a random flag,
\rho\in(0,1)
a scale, and
(\mathbf{t}_{\theta})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
prescribes random side length exponents. We assume the set of exponents
\mathbf{t}_{\theta}
all belong to a subset
\square^{\prime}\subseteq\boxslash
, in which every
\mathbf{t}=(t_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
is pinched along a prescribed subsequence
t_{d_{1}}<\dots<t_{d_{m+1}}
, with
\kappa
-gap between
t_{d_{k_{0}}}
and
t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}
for some
k_{0}\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, and second minimum
t_{d_{2}}
bounded from below by
\kappa
. For all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we assume the random subspace
(\mathcal{W}_{\theta,d_{k}})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
satisfies a subcritical projection theorem, and also a supercritical projection theorem for
k=k_{0}
.

Second, we consider a measure
\nu
on
B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
which has normalized dimension
\alpha\in(0,1)
at scales that appear as side length of the random box, i.e. the
\rho^{t_{i}}
where
\mathbf{t}\in\square^{\prime}
, and also on the interval
[\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}},\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}}}}]
as this is needed to exploit the supercritical assumption on
(\mathcal{W}_{\theta,d_{k_{0}}})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
. We do not assume that
\nu
is a probability measure. In fact, in the context of our application to random walks, the theorem below will be applied to a measure
\nu
supported on a ball of radius
\rho^{1/3}
and of mass smaller than
\rho^{c_{\mu}/3}
where
c_{\mu}>0
depends on the measure
\mu
driving the walk. We let
\zeta
be a parameter so that
\nu
is supported on a ball of radius
\zeta
in which all the random boxes
B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}
fit as well.

The conclusion is that for most parameters
\theta
with respect to
\sigma
, up to throwing away a small part of the measure
\nu
(relatively to
\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})+\zeta^{d}
), the measure
\nu
has improved dimension with respect to
B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}
and all its additive translates in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Theorem 5.8

(Non-homogeneous & local supercritical multislicing)
.

Let
d>m\geq 1
be integers,
\mathbf{d}\in P_{m}(d)
,
k_{0}\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
.
Consider parameters
\rho,\eta,\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},\varkappa,\kappa,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in(0,1/10)
. Let
\square^{\prime}\subseteq\boxslash(\mathbf{d},k_{0},\kappa,\eta)
.

Let
(\Theta,\sigma)
be a measurable space
\Theta
endowed with a probability measure
\sigma
. Consider a measurable familiy of flags
(\mathcal{W}_{\theta}=(W_{\theta,i})_{i=1}^{d})_{\theta\in\Theta}\in\mathcal{F}^{\Theta}
and of exponents
(\mathbf{t}_{\theta}=(t_{\theta,i})_{i=1}^{d})_{\theta\in\Theta}\in\square^{\prime\Theta}
.
We assume the following is true:

(1)

for every
\mathbf{t}\in\square^{\prime}
, every
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, the distribution of
(W_{\theta,d_{k}})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
satisfies
\SubP
with parameters
(\rho^{t_{d_{k+1}}},\varepsilon,\tau)
,

(2)

for every
\mathbf{t}\in\square^{\prime}
, the distribution of
(W_{\theta,d_{k_{0}}})_{\theta\sim\sigma}
satisfies
\SupP
with parameters
(\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}-t_{d_{k_{0}}}},\varkappa,\tau^{\prime})
.

Let
\nu
be a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of mass at most
1
, and such that for some
\alpha\in[\varkappa,1-\varkappa,]
, for all
\mathbf{t}\in\square^{\prime}
, all
r\in\{\rho^{t_{d_{k}}}\}_{k=1}^{m+1}\cup[\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}},\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}}}}]
, all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\nu(B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{r}+v)\leq\rho^{-\varepsilon}r^{d\alpha}.

Let
\zeta\in(0,1]
such that
\zeta\geq\diam({\rm supp}\nu)
and
\zeta\geq\max_{t_{1}\in\square^{\prime}}\rho^{t_{1}}
.

If
\varepsilon^{\prime}\lll\varepsilon
, and
\eta,\varepsilon,\tau\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime}}1
, and
\rho\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime},\varepsilon}1
, then there is an event
\mathcal{E}\subset\Theta
such that
\sigma(\mathcal{E})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, and for each
\theta\in\Theta\setminus\mathcal{E}
, there is a set
F_{\theta}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfying
\nu(F_{\theta})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})+\zeta^{d})
, such that for every
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(\nu|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\smallsetminus F_{\theta}})\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}+v\right)\leq\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(200d)}\leb\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

The case where the pinching parameter
\eta=0
, the support parameter
\zeta=1
, and
\square^{\prime}
is a singleton (i.e. one considers a single exponent) is due to
[
BH2
, Theorem 3.4]
, with slightly better dimensional gain (
\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(100d)}
instead of
\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(200d)}
). Our first step is to derive Theorem
5.8
for arbitrary
\eta,\zeta
, while still assuming that
\square^{\prime}
is a singleton. This is done by tessalating
B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
with copies of
\nu
. Afterward, we derive Theorem
5.8
in full generality by discretizing
\square^{\prime}
to reduce to the singleton case.

Note that throughout the proof we may assume that
\rho
is small enough in terms of
\varepsilon^{\prime}
as well (not just
d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime},\varepsilon
). This is because if the conclusion of theorem holds for a given value of
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, then it holds for all smaller values.

Step 1
:
If
\square^{\prime}
is a singleton
\square^{\prime}=\{\mathbf{t}\}
then Theorem
5.8
holds, with better dimensional gain
\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(150d)}
instead of
\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(200d)}
.

One may assume
\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\geq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\zeta^{d}
otherwise the result is trivial (taking
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
,
F_{\theta}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
). Let
Q
be a ball of radius
\zeta
such that
{\rm supp}(\nu)\subseteq Q
. Consider a finite family
(w_{j})_{j\in J}\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{J}
such that the translates
Q+w_{j}
are
2\zeta
-separated, included in
B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{1}
, set
\nu_{j}
the measure on
Q+w_{j}
obtained by pushforward of
\nu
under
v\mapsto v+w_{j}
. Provided
\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\lll_{d}1
, the bounds
1\geq\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\geq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\zeta^{d}
allow to choose the family
(w_{j})_{j\in J}
so that
\tilde{\nu}:=\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}
satisfies

1\geq\tilde{\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\geq\rho^{2\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

Observe that the measure
\tilde{\nu}
still satisfies the required non-concentration assumption: By the
2\zeta
-sepratation assumption on the
(Q+w_{j})_{j}
and the condition
\rho^{t_{1}}\leq\zeta
, we have for all
r\in\{\rho^{t_{d_{k}}}\}_{k=1}^{m+1}\cup[\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}+1}}},\rho^{t_{d_{k_{0}}}}]
, all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\tilde{\nu}(B^{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}_{r}+v)\leq\rho^{-\varepsilon}r^{d\alpha}.

We write

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{t}}:=(\underbrace{t_{d_{1}},\dots,t_{d_{1}}}_{d_{1}},\underbrace{t_{d_{2}},\dots,t_{d_{2}}}_{d_{2}-d_{1}},\dots,\underbrace{t_{d_{m+1}},\dots,t_{d_{m+1}}}_{d_{m+1}-d_{m}}).

Namely,
\overline{\mathbf{t}}
is the set of exponents obtained from
\mathbf{t}
by collapsing each
t_{i}
to smallest
t_{d_{k}}
above it. We apply Theorem
5.8
in the regime
\eta=0
,
\zeta=1
,
\square^{\prime}=\{\,\overline{\mathbf{t}}\,\}
(known by
[
BH2
, Theorem 3.4]
and with better dimensional gain, as mentioned above). Provided
\varepsilon^{\prime}\lll\varepsilon
, and
\varepsilon,\tau\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime}}1
, and
\rho\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime},\varepsilon}1
, we obtain the existence of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq\Theta
and
\tilde{F}_{\theta}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(for
\theta\in\Theta\smallsetminus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
) such that
\sigma(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}),\tilde{\nu}(\tilde{F}_{\theta})\leq\rho^{3\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, and for all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\smallsetminus\tilde{F}_{\theta}})\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\overline{\mathbf{t}}}}+v\right)\leq\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(100d)}\leb\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\overline{\mathbf{t}}}}\right)^{\alpha}.

Assuming
\eta\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime}}1
, this inequality implies in the case of exponents given by
\mathbf{t}
:

(14)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nu}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\smallsetminus\tilde{F}_{\theta}})\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}}}+v\right)\leq\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(150d)}\leb\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}}}\right)^{\alpha}.

By the pigeonhole principle and the bounds
\tilde{\nu}(\tilde{F}_{\theta})\leq\rho^{3\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\tilde{\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, there exists
j\in J
such that
\nu_{j}(\tilde{F}_{\theta})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\nu_{j}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Setting
\mathcal{E}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
,
F_{\theta}=\tilde{F}_{\theta}-w_{j}
, we find
\sigma(\mathcal{E})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
,
\nu(F_{\theta})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and that Equation (
14
) is valid with
(\nu,F_{\theta})
in the place of
(\tilde{\nu},\tilde{F}_{\theta})
. This concludes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2
:
Theorem
5.8
holds (even if
\square^{\prime}
is not a singleton).

Let
\{\mathbf{t}^{(l)}=(t^{(l)}_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\}_{l=1}^{L}
be a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated subset of
\square^{\prime}
. In particular,
L\ll_{d}\varepsilon^{-d}
. Fix
l\in\{1,\dots,L\}
and, invoking Step 1, apply Theorem
5.8
with the same random flag
(\mathcal{W}_{\theta})_{\theta\sim\sigma}

but with exponents given by the singleton
\{\mathbf{t}^{(l)}\}
. Call
\mathcal{E}_{l}\subseteq\Theta
and
F_{l,\theta}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(for
\theta\in\Theta\smallsetminus\mathcal{E}_{l}
) the associated exceptional subsets given by Step 1. Set
\mathcal{E}=\cup_{l=1}^{L}\mathcal{E}_{l}
, and for
\theta\notin\mathcal{E}
, set
F_{\theta}=\cup_{l=1}^{L}F_{l,\theta}
. Provided
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
and
\rho\lll_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}1
, we then have
\sigma(\mathcal{E})\leq L\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}/2}
and similarly
\nu(F_{\theta})\leq\rho^{\varepsilon^{\prime}/2}(\nu({\mathbb{R}}^{d})+\zeta^{d})
. Moreover, for
\theta\notin\mathcal{E}
, choosing
l=l(\theta)
such that
t^{(l)}_{i}-\varepsilon\leq t_{\theta,i}\leq t^{(l)}_{i}+\varepsilon
for all

, we find for every
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\displaystyle(\nu|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\smallsetminus F_{\theta}})\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}+v\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{-2d\varepsilon}\sup_{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\nu|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\smallsetminus F_{\theta}})\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}^{(l)}}}+w\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{-2d\varepsilon+\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(150d)}\leb\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}^{(l)}}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{\kappa\tau^{\prime}/(200d)}\leb\left(B^{\mathcal{W}_{\theta}}_{\rho^{\mathbf{t}_{\theta}}}\right)^{\alpha}.

where the last inequality uses
\varepsilon\lll_{d,\kappa,\tau^{\prime}}1
. This concludes the proof.

6.
Dimensional increment : Proof of Proposition
2.10

In this section, we complete Phase II, by proving Proposition
2.10
. To highlight which assumptions on
\mu
will play a role, we place ourselves in the following more general setting.

Setting 6.1
.

Let
\kappa,\eta>0
,
n_{0},n_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
J=\{0=d_{0}<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{m+1}=d\}
a subset of
\{0,\cdots,d\}
.
Let
\mu
be a compactly supported probability measure on
{\rm Diff}^{2}(M)
. Assume that

\mu
has a
(n_{0},\kappa,J)
-gap, is
(n_{1},\eta,J)
-pinched, and is
(n_{1},\eta)
-almost volume-preserving.
Let
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\rm Diff}^{2}(M)
be a bounded set containing the support of
\mu
.

We show the following dimensional increment result.

Proposition 6.2

(Dimensional increment)
.

Let
\varkappa,\rho,\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
,
\tau\geq 0
,
\alpha\in[\varkappa,1-\varkappa]
. Let
\nu
be a Borel measure on

7.2.
Equidistribution

In order to exploit the previous spectral gap estimates, we will approximate the measure
\nu
by a density on
M
. Given
\rho\in(0,1)
, we define the
mollification of
\nu
at scale
\rho
to be the probability measure on
M
satisfying that
for any measurable
f\colon M\rightarrow{[0,+\infty]}
,

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{M}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}=\int_{M}\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{\rho}(y))}\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}f\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y).

Note that
\nu_{\rho}
is absolutely continuous with repsect to
{\rm vol}
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative given by:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}(x)=\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{\rho}(y))}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y).

In particular, if
\nu
is
(1-\varepsilon,\mathcal{B}_{\rho},0)
-robust, then we have

(27)

\displaystyle\Bigl\lVert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\rho^{-d\varepsilon}.

We will also use the following classical result.

Lemma 7.6
.

There is a constant
C=C(M)>0
such that for every
s\in[0,1]
, for every Lipschitz function
f:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\|f\|_{H^{s}(M)}\leq C\|f\|_{\Lip}
.

Proof.

By Rademacher’s theorem, a Lipschitz function
f
is differentiable almost-everywhere, with differential clearly bounded by the Lipschitz constant of
f
. Given
s\in[0,1]
, it follows that
\|f\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(M)\|f\|_{\Lip}
.
∎

We are now ready to establish the equidistribution result Proposition
7.2
.

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

Let
\Upsilon_{\mu}
be the
\mu
-stationary measure given by Theorem
7.3
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\tau=0
.

We let
c,\varepsilon,\rho\in(0,1/10)
,

be parameters to be specified later. We let
\nu
be a
(1-\varepsilon,\mathcal{B}_{\rho},0)
-robust measure on
M
.

By Theorem
7.3
, we may also consider
s,\kappa^{\prime}>0
depending on
M,\mu_{0}
such that

has spectral radius at most

on

. We let

We compute

\displaystyle|\mu^{*n}*\nu(f)|=\left\lvert\int_{M}T_{\mu}^{n}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\int_{M}T_{\mu}^{n}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{M}T_{\mu}^{n}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}-\int_{M}T_{\mu}^{n}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rvert.

To bound the first term, we observe

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{M}T_{\mu}^{n}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\mu}^{n}f\|_{L^{1}}\Bigl\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\mu}^{n}f\|_{H^{s}}\Bigl\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rho}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\rm vol}}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mu,\kappa^{\prime}}(1-\kappa^{\prime})^{n}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\rho^{-d\varepsilon}

Appendix A
Large deviation estimate

In this appendix we record a general large deviation estimate for Markov chains. The argument is a standard adaptation of the large deviation principle for sums of i.i.d. variables and appears in the literature in several more specific settings. For convenience, we present the result here in an abstract form. In the present paper we apply it in two different contexts, and we hope that this formulation provides a convenient reference for future works.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
E
be a measurable space. Let
f:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded function. Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a Markov chain on
E
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
the probability distribution for the Markov chain conditionally to
X_{0}=x
, and write
{\mathbb{E}}_{x}
the corresponding expectation.

Assume that for every
x\in E
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(f(X_{1})\right)\leq 0.

Then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
, for
\gamma:=\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\sup|f|)^{2}}
, for all
x\in E
, we have

(39)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(\exp(\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(X_{i}))\right)\leq\exp(\gamma\varepsilon n).

In particular,

(40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(X_{i})\geq 2\varepsilon n\right)\leq\exp(-\gamma\varepsilon n).

Proof.
