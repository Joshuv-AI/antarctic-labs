On deformations of foliations and characteristic classes Thanks: This work is partly supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP21H00980.
Taro Asuke

Abstract
. We study characteristic classes for deformations of foliations. Those classes include known classes such as the Godbillon–Vey class and the Fuks–Lodder–Kotschick class. We introduce a certain differential graded algebra (DGA for short) which recovers the Bott vanishing and some formulae by Heitsch. Some basic properties and structures of the cohomology of those DGA’s are discussed. In particular, it is shown that at the level of the cohomology of DGA, there are some classes which cannot be described by the Godbillon–Vey class and the Fuks–Lodder–Kotschick class. It is also shown that if a certain type of characteristic classes admit non-trivial deformations in examples, then they yield another kind of classes which admit also non-trivial deformations.

Introduction

Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}
be a real codimension-one foliation which is transversely orientable.
Then, there is a
\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle T{\mathcal{F}}=\ker\omega

\displaystyle 1

The
\displaystyle 3
-form
\displaystyle\eta\wedge d\eta

This class is called the

Let
\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\}

Then, we have a smooth families of
\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\{\omega_{s}\}

\displaystyle\{\eta_{s}\},\{\mu_{s}\}

\displaystyle d\eta_{s}=\omega_{s}\wedge\mu_{s}

If ‘

\displaystyle(d\eta)\dot{\,}=d\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle d\dot{\eta}=\dot{\omega}\wedge\mu+\omega\wedge\dot{\mu}

\displaystyle\omega=\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=\eta
and
\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{0}

It follows that
\displaystyle d\eta\wedge d\dot{\eta}=0
and that
\displaystyle\eta\wedge\dot{\eta}\wedge d\eta

Indeed, the latter
\displaystyle 4
-form represents a characteristic class of families of foliations
[
Fuks
]
,
[

]
,
[

and called the

It is not difficult to show that the Godbillon–Vey class, its derivative and the Fuks–Lodder–Kotschick class are the only characteristic classes obtained from
\displaystyle\eta
,
\displaystyle\dot{\eta}
,
\displaystyle d\eta
and
\displaystyle d\dot{\eta}
.
We can consider differentials of higher order.
For example, we have
\displaystyle d\ddot{\eta}=\ddot{\omega}\wedge\mu+2\dot{\omega}\wedge\dot{\mu}+\omega\wedge\ddot{\mu}
.
Hence
\displaystyle d\ddot{\eta}\wedge d\eta+(d\dot{\eta})^{2}=0
, and
\displaystyle\eta\wedge\ddot{\eta}\wedge d\eta+\eta\wedge\dot{\eta}\wedge d\dot{\eta}

The latter form represents also a characteristic class for families of foliations.
Actually, we have
\displaystyle\eta\wedge\ddot{\eta}\wedge d\eta+\eta\wedge\dot{\eta}\wedge d\dot{\eta}=(\eta\wedge\dot{\eta}\wedge d\eta)\dot{\,}
.
This implies that the class is just a derivative of the Godbillon–Vey class and is not really a new one.
In this paper, we first introduce spaces of deformations of higher order and characteristic classes for such deformations.
These are generalization of
[

]
and

asuke:UT
]

In the study of characteristic classes, the Bott vanishing
[

]

The former is the very basic in defining characteristic classes.
On the other hand, the latter describes derivatives of characteristic classes.
In particular, it largely divides the classes into those which vary according to deformations and rigid ones.
Characteristic classes introduced in this article well reflect such phenomena.
Finally, we will present a candidate of a characteristic class which is not a priori described by a combination of the Godbillon–Vey class and the Fuks–Lodder–Kotschick class.

\displaystyle(\{\overline{\omega}^{(k)}\}_{k=0,1},\{\overline{\theta}^{(k)}\}_{k=0,1})\in\widehat{D}(\mathcal{F},\theta)
, then
\displaystyle\overline{\omega}^{(1)}
is a infinitesimal deformation of
\displaystyle\omega_{0}
with respect to
\displaystyle\theta=\theta^{(0)}
(up to signatures) and
\displaystyle\theta^{(1)}
is the infinitesimal deformation of
\displaystyle\theta
with respect to
\displaystyle\omega_{0}
.

To be precise, we consider the restrictions of
\displaystyle\overline{\omega}^{(1)}
to
\displaystyle\overline{\theta}^{(1)}
to the tangent bundle of foliations in Theorem
3.21
.

II. Characteristic classes

In what follows, deformations and realizations are assumed to be framed if the normal bundle of the foliation under consideration is trivialized.

4.
Basic definitions

We discuss assuming that foliations are real and that normal bundles of them are trivialized.
We can argue in a parallel way even if we study foliations of which the normal bundles are non-trivial, or ones which admits transversal structures such as metric structure (Riemannian foliation) or holomorphic structure (transversely holomorphic foliation).
In order to study such foliations, we need to replace
\displaystyle\mathrm{W}_{q}
by other ones such as
\displaystyle\mathrm{WO}_{q}
,
\displaystyle\mathrm{W}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{WU}_{q}
, etc.
Note that if the normal bundle of the foliation under consideration is trivial, then we need to fix the homotopy type of the trivialization.

Definition 4.1
.

We set

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}=\{A_{0}+A_{1}t+\cdots+A_{r}t^{r}\mid A_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}})\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}=\{A_{0}+A_{1}t+\cdots+A_{r}t^{r}\mid A_{0}\in\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}),\ A_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}})\text{ if $\displaystyle i>0$}\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[[t]])=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[[t]])=\varprojlim_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}.

The multiplication in
\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}
and the inverse in
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}
are taken modulo
\displaystyle t^{r+1}
.

The following is an easy

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{0}\\
X_{1}&X_{0}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\\
X_{r}&\cdots&X_{1}&X_{0}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{s}^{r}
, we set
\displaystyle\varphi(X)=X_{0}+X_{1}t+\cdots+X_{r}t^{r}
.
Then,
\displaystyle\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{r}
to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}
.
Moreover, the restriction of
\displaystyle\varphi
to
\displaystyle S^{r}
is isomorphism to
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}
.

We set
\displaystyle X(t)=\varphi(X)
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{0}\\
X_{1}&X_{0}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\\
X_{r}&\cdots&X_{1}&X_{0}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{s}^{r}
.
We represent
\displaystyle X^{k}
as
\displaystyle X^{k}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{0}(k)\\
Y_{1}(k)&Y_{0}(k)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\\
Y_{r}(k)&\cdots&Y_{1}(k)&Y_{0}(k)\end{pmatrix}
and set
\displaystyle C_{k,l}(X)=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{-1}{2\pi}\right)^{k}\tr Y_{l}(k)
.

Let
\displaystyle c_{i}
be the
\displaystyle i
-th Chern polynomial, that is, we define
\displaystyle c_{i}
to satisfy

\det\left(\lambda I_{q}-\frac{1}{2\pi}X\right)=\lambda^{q}+\lambda^{q-1}c_{1}(X)+\cdots+\lambda c_{q-1}(X)+c_{q}(X),

where
\displaystyle X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}})
.
We set
\displaystyle C_{k}(X)=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{-1}{2\pi}\right)^{k}\tr X
, which corresponds to the
\displaystyle k
-th Chern character.
It is well-known that there uniquely exist polynomials
\displaystyle\varphi_{k}
in free variables
\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
such that

c_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varphi_{k}(C_{1},2C_{2}\ldots,k!C_{k})

for
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q
.
The polynomials
\displaystyle\varphi_{k}
are often called the
Newton polynomial
.
See
[
Husemoller
]
for details.

Let
\displaystyle x_{i},\dot{x}_{i},\ddot{x}_{i},\ldots,x^{(m)}_{i},\ldots
, where
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq q
, be free variables.
Let
\displaystyle\delta
be the derivation on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}[x^{(m)}_{i}]
such that
\displaystyle\delta x^{(m)}_{i}=x^{(m+1)}_{i}
.
Then, each
\displaystyle\delta^{l}\varphi_{k}
is a polynomial in
\displaystyle x^{(m)}_{i}
, where
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq k
and
\displaystyle 0\leq m\leq l
.
The polynomial
\displaystyle\delta^{l}\varphi_{k}
evaluated by
\displaystyle x^{(m)}_{i}
is denoted by
\displaystyle\delta^{l}\varphi_{k}(x_{i},\dot{x}_{i},\ldots,x^{(l)}_{i})
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{0}\\
X_{1}&X_{0}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\\
X_{r}&\cdots&X_{1}&X_{0}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{s}^{r}
.
We set
\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{k,l}(X)=k!C_{k,l}(X)
and

c_{k,l}(X)=\delta^{l}\varphi_{k}(C^{\prime}_{i,0}(X),C^{\prime}_{i,1}(X),\ldots,C^{\prime}_{i,l}(X)).

We can describe the invariant polynomials on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{r}
as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\displaystyle I(S^{r})
be the algebra of invariant polynomials on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{r}
.
Then, we have
\displaystyle I(S^{r})={\mathbb{R}}[c_{k,l}]
, where
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq q
and
\displaystyle 0\leq l\leq r
.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq q
, we set
\displaystyle\tau_{i}(X)=\tr X^{q}
.
If we extend
\displaystyle\tau_{i}
naturally to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r}
, then we have
\displaystyle I(\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}[t])_{r})\cong{\mathbb{R}}[\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{q}]
.
If
\displaystyle X\in\mathfrak{s}^{r}
, then we have
\displaystyle\tau_{k}(X(t))=\sum_{l=0}^{r}\tr Y_{l}(k)t^{l}
.
Since
\displaystyle t
is an independent, we are done.
∎

5.
Relevant differential graded algebras

We introduce some differential graded algebras (DGA’s for short).

Definition 5.1
.

We set

\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}=\bigwedge[h_{i,(a)}]_{1\leq i\leq q,\ 0\leq a\leq r}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}[c_{i,(b)}]_{1\leq i\leq q,\ 0\leq b\leq r}.

We allow
\displaystyle r
to be
\displaystyle\infty
.

1)

We define an exterior derivative
\displaystyle d
on
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}
by the conditions
\displaystyle dh_{i,(j)}=c_{i,(j)}
and
\displaystyle dc_{i,(j)}=0
.
We formally set
\displaystyle h_{i,(-1)}=c_{i,(-1)}=0
for all
\displaystyle i
, and define a (unsigned) derivation
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}
on
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}
by the conditions that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(c_{i,(a)})=c_{i,(a+1)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(h_{i,(b)})=h_{i,(b+1)},

where
\displaystyle a<r
.

2)

We set
\displaystyle\ord(c_{i,(a)})=a
,
\displaystyle\ord(h_{i,(b)})=b
and naturally extend
\displaystyle\ord
to the whole
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}
.

3)

We set
\displaystyle\deg(c_{i,(a)})=2
,
\displaystyle\deg(h_{i,(b)})=1
and naturally extend
\displaystyle\deg
to the whole
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}
.

We set
\displaystyle h_{i}=h_{i,(0)}
and
\displaystyle c_{i}=c_{i,(0)}
.
Note that
\displaystyle d
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}
commute each other.
In what follows, we do not explicitly indicate exterior products, namely, we omit the symbol ‘
\displaystyle\wedge
’.

Definition 5.2
.

We set

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{i,(l)})=\frac{1}{2}l(l-1)c_{i,(l-1)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}(h_{i,(l)})=\frac{1}{2}l(l-1)h_{i,(l-1)}.

We extend
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}
to the whole
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}}_{q}
as a (unsigned) derivation.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\displaystyle c\in\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
is a monomial and if
\displaystyle\ord(c)<r
, then we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(c)-\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(c)=\ord(c)c
.

Proof.

The claim holds if
\displaystyle c=c_{i,(l)}
or if
\displaystyle c=h_{i,(l)}
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(c_{i,(l)})-\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{i,(l)})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{i,(l+1)})-\frac{1}{2}l(l-1)\widetilde{\delta}(c_{i,(l-1)})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(l+1)lc_{i,(l)}-\frac{1}{2}l(l-1)c_{i,(l)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=lc_{i,(l)}.

Similar calculations are valid for
\displaystyle h_{i,(l)}
.
Suppose that the claim holds for monomials of degree less than or equal to
\displaystyle k
.
Let
\displaystyle c\in\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
be a monomial of degree
\displaystyle k+1
.
If
\displaystyle c
admits a decomposition that
\displaystyle c=c_{1}c_{2}
with
\displaystyle\ord(c_{1})\leq k
and
\displaystyle\ord(c_{2})\leq k
, then we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(c)-\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(c)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{\delta}(c_{1})c_{2}+c_{1}\widetilde{\delta}(c_{2}))-\widetilde{\delta}(\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{1})c_{2}+c_{1}\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{\delta}(c_{1}))c_{2}+\widetilde{\delta}(c_{1})\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{2})+\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{1})\widetilde{\delta}(c_{2})+c_{1}\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{\delta}(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}-\widetilde{\delta}(\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{1}))c_{2}-\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{1})\widetilde{\delta}(c_{2})-\widetilde{\delta}(c_{1})\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{2})-c_{1}\widetilde{\delta}(\widetilde{\sigma}(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\ord(c_{1})c_{1}c_{2}+\ord(c_{2})c_{1}c_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\ord(c)c.

Otherwise, we have
\displaystyle c=c_{1}c_{2}
with
\displaystyle c_{1}=c_{i,(k+1)}
or
\displaystyle c_{1}=h_{i,(k+1)}
, and that
\displaystyle\ord(c_{2})=0
.
Then, by the similar arguments as above, we can show that the claim also holds for
\displaystyle c
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We set
\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{i}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{l!}t^{l}h_{i,(l)}
.
If we termwise apply
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}
, we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}\widehat{h}_{i}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{l!}t^{l}h_{i,(l+1)}
.
On the other hand, we have
\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\widehat{h}_{i}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{l!}t^{l}h_{i,(l+1)}
.
In this sense, we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}
.
Similarly, we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}
.
As this correspondence is contravariant,
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}-\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}
corresponds to
\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\circ\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\circ\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}
.
Indeed, let
\displaystyle f
be a function in
\displaystyle t
.
Then, we have
\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\circ\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)(f)-\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\circ\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}(f)=t\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}
.
This operator just corresponds to
\displaystyle h_{i,(l)}\mapsto lh_{i,(l)}
.

Let
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l}
be the subspace of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
which is generated by monomials of order
\displaystyle l
.
If
\displaystyle l<0
, then we set
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l}=\{0\}
.
We have the following

Lemma 5.6
.

1)

If
\displaystyle l<r
, then we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l})\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l+1}
.

2)

We have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l})\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{l-1}
.

We will make use of the following variants of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}
.

Definition 5.7
.

We set

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}(c_{i,(l)})=lc_{i,(l-1)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}(h_{i,(l)})=lh_{i,(l-1)}.

We extend
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}
to the whole
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
as a (unsigned) derivation.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\displaystyle c=h_{i_{1},(l_{1})}\cdots h_{i_{a},(l_{a})}c_{j_{1},(k_{1})}\cdots c_{j_{b},(k_{b})}
, where
\displaystyle(i_{a},(l_{a}))\neq(i_{a^{\prime}},(l_{a^{\prime}}))
if
\displaystyle a\neq a^{\prime}
.
We set
\displaystyle l(c)=a+b
and call
\displaystyle l(c)
the
length
of
\displaystyle c
.
The subspace of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
generated by elements of length
\displaystyle l
is denoted by
\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{l}
.

We have the following lemma which corresponds to Lemma
5.3
.

Lemma 5.9
.

If
\displaystyle c\in\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{r}\mathrm{W}_{q}}
is a monomial and if
\displaystyle l(c)<r
, then we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}(c)-\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(c)=l(c)c
.

6.
Bott vanishing theorem and Heitsch formula

The most basic characteristic classes for foliations are Pontrjagin classes of the normal bundles.
Usually we only consider the Pontrjagin classes of degree
\displaystyle 4k
, however, in order to introduce secondary classes, we also consider the classes of degree
\displaystyle 4k+2
.
Let
\displaystyle c_{k}
denote the
\displaystyle k
-th Chern polynomial, which will corresponds to the Pontrjagin form of degree
\displaystyle 2k
.

Definition 6.1
.

If
\displaystyle c=c_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots c_{q}^{j_{q}}\in{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{q}]
, then we set
\displaystyle\lvert c\rvert=j_{1}+2j_{1}+\cdots+qj_{q}
.

We will extend
\displaystyle\lvert\;\cdot\;\rvert
later in Definition
7.4
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{F}}=\{\omega\in\Omega^{*}(M)\mid\text{$\displaystyle\omega$ is locally of the form $\displaystyle\textstyle{\sum}\,\omega_{i}\wedge dy^{i}$}\}
, where
\displaystyle(y^{1},\ldots,y^{q})
are local coordinates in the transversal direction.
Elements of
\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{F}}^{k}
are said to be of
transveresal degree greater than or equal to
\displaystyle k
, or of transversal degree greater than
\displaystyle k-1
.

The following is a fundamental result by Bott.

Theorem 6.3

(Bott
[
Bott:LNM
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\nabla
be a Bott connection for
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}
.
For
\displaystyle c\in{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{q}]
,
\displaystyle c(\nabla)
denotes the Pontrjagin form which is calculated by we using
\displaystyle\nabla
.
If
\displaystyle\lvert c\rvert=k
, then
\displaystyle c(\nabla)\in I_{\mathcal{F}}^{k}
.
In particular,
\displaystyle c(\nabla)
is trivial as a differential form if
\displaystyle\lvert c\rvert>q
.

Remark 6.4
.

1)

The differential form
\displaystyle c_{i}(\nabla)
is always exact if
\displaystyle i
is odd.
If the normal bundle is trivial, then
\displaystyle c_{i}(\nabla)
are also exact for
\displaystyle i
even.

2)

If foliations under considerations are transversely holomorphic, then we make use of the Chern classes and
\displaystyle c_{i}
denotes the
\displaystyle i
-th Chern class.
In this case, only the imaginary parts of
\displaystyle c_{i}(\nabla)
are exact so that we have to modify arguments.

Next, we recall the Heitsch formula for deformations
[
Heitsch:Topology
]
.
Let
\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}
be a one-parameter
\displaystyle C^{1}
-family of foliations with
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{0}={\mathcal{F}}
.
We fix identifications of
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}}_{t})
with
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}})
, and let
\displaystyle\{\theta_{t}\}
be a family of Bott connections with
\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\theta
.
If
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}})
is trivial, then we fix a trivialization, say
\displaystyle e
, and let
\displaystyle\theta^{f}
be the flat connection with respect to
\displaystyle e
.
If we do not assume the triviality, then let
\displaystyle\theta^{m}
a metric connection with respect to a Riemannian metric on
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}})
.
We represent by
\displaystyle\theta^{\natural}
either
\displaystyle\theta^{f}
or
\displaystyle\theta^{m}
according to the assumption.
Usually we assume that
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}})
is trivialized so that
\displaystyle\theta^{\natural}
denotes
\displaystyle\theta^{f}
.

Definition 6.5
.

If
\displaystyle G
is a Lie group, then
\displaystyle I^{k}(G)
denotes the algebra of
\displaystyle\Ad_{G}
-invariant symmetric multilinear mappings on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
of degree
\displaystyle k
.
If
\displaystyle c
is an
\displaystyle\Ad_{G}
-invariant polynomial of degree
\displaystyle k
on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
, then the polarization of
\displaystyle c
is also denoted by
\displaystyle c
by abuse of notations.

Remark 6.6
.

If
\displaystyle c\in I^{k}(G)
and if
\displaystyle f_{c}
denotes the polarization of
\displaystyle c
, then we have

f_{c}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=\left.\frac{1}{k!}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\cdots\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}c(t_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}X_{k})\right|_{t_{1}=\cdots=t_{k}=0}

for
\displaystyle X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\displaystyle t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
are free variables.
We have
\displaystyle f_{c}(X,\ldots,X)=c(X)
.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in I^{k}(\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}))
.
We set
\displaystyle\theta_{t,s}=s\theta_{t}+(1-s)\theta^{\natural}
,
\displaystyle\Omega_{t,s}=d\theta_{t,s}+\theta_{t,s}\wedge\theta_{t,s}
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\psi_{t}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta_{t},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=k\int_{0}^{1}f(\theta_{t}-\theta^{\natural},\Omega_{t,s},\ldots,\Omega_{t,s})ds,

\displaystyle\displaystyle V_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=\int_{0}^{1}sf(\psi_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\natural},\Omega_{t,s},\ldots,\Omega_{t,s})ds.

Note that
\displaystyle\theta_{t,0}=\theta^{\natural}
and
\displaystyle\theta_{t,1}=\theta_{t}
.
Hence we have
\displaystyle\Omega_{t,0}=d\theta^{\natural}+\theta^{\natural}\wedge\theta^{\natural}
and
\displaystyle\Omega_{t,1}=d\theta_{t}+\theta_{t}\wedge\theta_{t}
.

Remark 6.8
.

The differential form
\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{2\pi}\right)^{i}\Delta_{c_{i}}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})
will be the image of
\displaystyle h_{i}
by the characteristic mapping.
See Definition
7.2

The following is shown by Heitsch.

Theorem 6.9

(
[
Heitsch:Topology
]
)
.

The following formulae hold
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=k(k-1)dV_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})+kf(\psi_{t},\Omega_{t,1},\ldots,\Omega_{t,1}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}d(\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural}))=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}f(\Omega_{t,1},\ldots,\Omega_{t,1})=kdf(\psi_{t},\Omega_{t,1},\ldots,\Omega_{t,1}).

Definition 6.10
.

If
\displaystyle f\in I^{k}(\mathrm{GL}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}))
and if
\displaystyle r\geq 1
, then we set

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha^{(r)}_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=\dfrac{\partial^{r-1}}{\partial t^{r-1}}f(\psi_{t},\Omega_{t,1},\ldots,\Omega_{t,1}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle V^{(r)}_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=\dfrac{\partial^{r-1}}{\partial t^{r-1}}V_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural}).

We set
\displaystyle\alpha^{(0)}_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=\Delta_{c_{i}}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})
.

The following is immediate.

Lemma 6.11
.

We have
\displaystyle\alpha^{(r)}_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})\in I_{\mathcal{F}}^{k-r}
.

Corollary 6.12
.

If
\displaystyle r\geq 1
, then we have

\dfrac{\partial^{r}}{\partial t^{r}}\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})=k(k-1)dV^{(r)}_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})+k\alpha_{f}^{(r)}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural}).

An infinitesimal version of above formulae is given as follows.

Corollary 6.13
.

The following formulae hold
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\partial^{r}}{\partial t^{r}}\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural})\right|_{t=0}=k(k-1)dV_{f}^{(r)}(\theta,\theta^{\natural})+k\alpha_{f}^{(r)}(\dot{\theta},\Omega,\ldots,\Omega),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\partial^{r}}{\partial t^{r}}d(\Delta_{f}(\theta_{t},\theta^{\natural}))\right|_{t=0}=\left.\dfrac{\partial^{r}}{\partial t^{r}}f(\Omega_{t,1},\ldots,\Omega_{t,1})\right|_{t=0}=kd\alpha_{f}^{(r)}(\dot{\theta},\Omega,\ldots,\Omega),

where
\displaystyle\dot{\theta}=\left.\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta_{t}\right|_{t=0}
,
\displaystyle\Omega=d\theta+\theta\wedge\theta
and
\displaystyle r\geq 1
.

7.
Characteristic mapping

Characteristic classes for deformations of foliations are realized as follows.
If
\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}\in\widehat{D}^{r}({\mathcal{F}})
, then
\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}
induces a Bott connection, say
\displaystyle\nabla^{(r)}
, on
\displaystyle N^{r}
.
If
\displaystyle Q({\mathcal{F}})
is trivialized, then we consider the induced trivialization of
\displaystyle N^{r}
and let
\displaystyle\nabla_{f}^{(r)}
be the flat connection.
Otherwise, we fix a Riemannian metric on
\displaystyle N^{r}
and let
\displaystyle\nabla_{m}^{(r)}
be a metric connection.
We set
\displaystyle\nabla_{\natural}^{(r)}
to be
\displaystyle\nabla_{f}^{(r)}
or
\displaystyle\nabla_{m}^{(r)}
.

We briefly recall the Chern–Simons forms.
Let
\displaystyle f
be a polynomial in
\displaystyle c_{i,(l)}
, and let
\displaystyle\theta
and
\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}
be connection forms.
We set
\displaystyle\theta_{t}=(1-t)\theta+t\theta^{\prime}
and let
\displaystyle R_{t}
be the curvature form of
\displaystyle\theta_{t}

9.

If
\displaystyle T

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle T
of eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda
as the
\displaystyle\lambda
-eigenspace of
\displaystyle T
.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda}
be the
\displaystyle\lambda
-eigenspace of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}
.
The subspace of
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda}
which consists of order
\displaystyle k
is denoted by
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
.
Similarly,
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
denotes the
\displaystyle\lambda
-eigenspace of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\prime}_{\lambda,k}
denotes the subspace which consists of elements of order
\displaystyle k
.

We have
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widetilde{F}^{\prime}_{\lambda,k}
.

Lemma 9.2
.

1)

We have
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}=\widetilde{F}^{\prime}_{\lambda+k,k}
.

2)

Let
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{\lambda,k}
be the restriction of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}
to
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
.
Then, the image of
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{\lambda,k}
is contained in
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda-k+1,k-1}
.
If
\displaystyle\lambda\neq 0
, then
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{\lambda,k}
is an isomorphism and the inverse is given by
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\widetilde{\delta}
.

Proof.

First we show 1).
Suppose that
\displaystyle\omega
is of order
\displaystyle k
.
We have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(\omega)=k\omega+\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega)
by Lemma
5.3
.
Hence
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(\omega)=(\lambda+k)\omega
if and only if
\displaystyle\omega\in\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
.

Next, we show 2).
If
\displaystyle\omega\in\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
, then we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega))=\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega))-\mbox{$\displaystyle(k-1)$}\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega)=(\lambda-k+1)\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega)
so that we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega)\in\widetilde{F}_{\lambda-k+1,k-1}
.
If conversely
\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}\in\widetilde{F}_{\lambda-k+1,k-1}
, then we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\widetilde{\delta}(\omega^{\prime})=\lambda\omega^{\prime}
by 1).
As we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}(\omega)=\lambda\omega
on
\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\lambda,k}
, the claim holds.
∎

\displaystyle k=0

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}\omega\in\widetilde{\mathrm{D}^{\infty}\mathrm{W}_{q}}{}^{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\mathrm{id}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{2^{i}(2k-2i-1)!}{i!(2k-i-1)!}\widetilde{\delta}^{i}\circ p_{1,k-i}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{i}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle p_{1,k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\mathrm{id}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k-i-1}\frac{2^{i}(2k-2i-1)!}{i!(2k-i-1)!}(-1)^{l}\frac{2^{l}(2k-2i-l-2)!}{(2k-2i-2)!l!}\widetilde{\delta}^{i+l}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{i+1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\mathrm{id}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{m=i}^{k-1}(-1)^{m-i}\frac{2^{m}(2k-2i-1)!(2k-m-i-2)!}{i!(m-i)!(2k-i-1)!(2k-2i-2)!}\widetilde{\delta}^{m}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{m}

\displaystyle\displaystyle={\mathrm{id}}-\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-i}\frac{2^{m}(2k-2i-1)!(2k-m-i-2)!}{i!(m-i)!(2k-i-1)!(2k-2i-2)!}\widetilde{\delta}^{m}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{m},

where
\displaystyle m=i+l
.

Now let
\displaystyle P(i)=\frac{1}{(2k-i-1)\cdots(2k-2i-2)(2k-2i)\cdots(2k-m-i-1)}
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq m
.
Then, we have

p_{1,k}={\mathrm{id}}-\frac{2^{m}}{m!}\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-i}\dbinom{m}{i}P(i)\widetilde{\delta}^{m}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{m},

Hence, it suffices to show that

\frac{2^{m}}{m!}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i-1}\dbinom{m}{i}P(i)=\frac{2^{m}(2k-m-2)!}{m!(2k-2)!}

holds for
\displaystyle 1\leq m\leq k-1
.
Actually, we have

(9.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i-1}\dbinom{m}{i}P(i)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}(-1)^{i-1}\dbinom{m}{i}P(i)+(-1)^{a-1}\dbinom{m-1}{a-1}\frac{1}{(2k-a-1)\cdots(2k-m-a)}.

Indeed, the equality (
9.8
) holds if
\displaystyle a=m
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{2k-2a+1}\dbinom{m-1}{a-1}-\frac{1}{2k-a}\dbinom{m}{a-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{(m-1)!}{(a-1)!(m-a+1)!}\frac{(m-a+1)(2k-a)-m(2k-2a+1)}{(2k-2a+1)(2k-a)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{(m-1)!}{(a-2)!(m-a+1)!}\frac{2k-m-a}{(2k-2a+1)(2k-a)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\dbinom{m-1}{a-2}\frac{2k-m-a}{(2k-2a+1)(2k-a)}.

It follows that the equality (
9.8
) also holds if
\displaystyle a=m-1
.
Hence, by induction, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i-1}\dbinom{m}{i}P(i)=\dbinom{m-1}{a-1}\frac{1}{(2k-2)\cdots(2k-m-1)}
.
Thus the equality (
9
) holds so that we are done.
∎

Proof.
