Activin signaling inhibitor Sotatercept in pulmonary veno-occlusive disease: building a bridge to transplantation?
Authors: Marcel Mietzner, Matthias Held, Elena Pfeuffer-Jovic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulmonary veno-occlusive disease (PVOD) is a rare form of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). Its prognosis is considerably worse and no medical treatment has a proven efficacy. Vasodilating drugs for PAH may contribute to a pulmonary edema in PVOD. Sotatercept is the latest approved medical treatment for PAH with a unique mode of action and promising treatment effects. However, none of the clinical trials of sotatercept have included patients with PVOD, so the effect of sotatercept is unknown.Case descriptionWe report two cases of hereditary PVOD that were treated with sotatercept in addition to established pulmonary arterial hypertension therapy. Under treatment with sotatercept, both patients showed stabilization of clinical condition and gas exchange, accompanied by slight improvements in DLCOc and KCOc.ConclusionsThese two cases suggest that sotatercept may have a beneficial effect on disease stabilization in hereditary PVOD, as reflected by improvements in DLCOc and KCOc as well as stabilization of clinical status. Therefore, it may be a therapeutic option to stabilize patients with PVOD and potentially prolong the bridging time to lung transplantation.Keywords: PAH, PVOD, Sotatercept, Activin-signaling

Introduction

The term pulmonary veno-occlusive disease (PVOD) was suggested almost sixty years ago as a separate entity causing pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) that involves primarily pulmonary veins showing intimal fibrosis both in small venules and larger veins. As a result, those changes lead to an occlusion of pulmonary venous vessels [1]. In contrast to that, PAH and other forms of pulmonary hypertension (PH) show mainly intimal and medial thickening of pulmonary arterial vessels [2]. There are several subclassifications of PVOD of which hereditary PVOD is associated with biallelic mutations of the EIF2AK4 gene. Although it has been known for sixty years, no medical therapy has a proven efficacy and prognosis is considerably worse than other forms of PAH with 1-year mortality of 72% [3,4].

Sotatercept is a newly approved medical treatment for PAH with a unique mode of action as it acts as a ligand trap for GDF8/11 and activin A/B to restore a balance of TGFβ and BMP signaling. Its treatment effects are promising in various PAH forms, but there is no experience in use of this substance in PVOD [5,6]. We herein present two cases of hereditary PVOD under treatment with sotatercept.

Case 1

A 35-year old Caucasian man was sent as outpatient to our center for pulmonary hypertension in September 2024 due to progressive dyspnea on exertion (WHO functional class III). The onset of symptoms occurred three years ago. He was a nonsmoker, worked for a delivery service and had two siblings. His older brother was diagnosed in 2017 with PAH and died in 2019; the younger brother was diagnosed in January 2024 with a severe PAH, in July 2024 a homozygous EIF2AK4 mutation was found.

Upon initial presentation, NT-proBNP level was unremarkable, as was the echocardiography. Hyperventilation at rest was present, which became more evident during the 6-min walk test. This is consistent with the findings of cardiopulmonary exercise testing, where clear signs of a gas exchange disorder and a pulmonary perfusion abnormality were observed (VE/VCO2 slope of 106,65, AaDO2 of 30,68 mmHg before and 59,6 mmHg afterwards, PaETCO2 7,25 mmHg). Furthermore, peak VO2 was severely reduced with 11,7 ml/min/kg. Pulmonary function testing was performed with VyntusTM BODY Plethysmograph. Both DLCOc and KCOc were adjusted for hemoglobin. DLCOc was severely reduced to 28% predicted raising high suspicion for PVOD. Initial examination results as well as follow-up examinations are documented in Table 1.

Two weeks later the patient was administered for right heart catheterization (RHC), which demonstrated a mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) of 31 mmHg, pulmonary arterial wedge pressure (PAWP) of 9 mm Hg, pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) of 3.9 Wood units (WU), pulmonal-arterial compliance (PAC) of 4.03 ml/mmHg and cardiac index (CI) of 2.71 L/min/m2. Moreover, vasoreactivity testing revealed a decrease in mPAP to 22 mmHg and PVR to 2.4 WU.

The high-resolution chest CT scan images showed bilateral hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. However, the V/Q scan showed bilateral subsegmental mismatch-findings. Since PVOD can mimic thromboembolic mismatch findings on scintigraphy, the diagnosis of PVOD was made in view of the family history and the typical findings of a severely reduced DLCO. Genetic testing ultimately confirmed the diagnosis by demonstrating the underlying genetic defect of a homozygous EIF2AK4-mutation (c.2458C > T, p.(Arg820∗)) in December 2024.

Treatment with riociguat was initiated in October 2024 and cautiously uptitrated. WHO functional class remained unchanged.

Re-evaluation in March 2025 demonstrated a hemodynamic response with a reduction in PVR and mPAP while CI remained stable; hyperventilation also improved under therapy. Nevertheless, DLCOc and KCOc decreased. Given the known progressive nature of the disease, treatment with sotatercept was initiated in May 2025. The patient was introduced to a lung transplant center.

Although his WHO functional class did not change, both DLCOc and KCOc showed a slightly improvement in contrast to riociguat monotherapy. Arterial blood gases tests also indicated an increase of PaO2 after exertion.

After four months on sotatercept another RHC was performed, that showed similiar findings of mPAP, PVR and PAC as without sotatercept, but a decrease in CI. Furthermore, another chest CT scan now showed new signs of PVOD as ground glass opacity and interlobular septal thickening.

Up until now the treatment with sotatercept was well tolerated except for occasional epistaxis and an increasing in hemoglobin with the first discontinuation needed in February 2026. DLCOc and KCOc have increased under therapy to date. The patient has remained stable up until now. Another chest CT in March 2026 showed stable radiological findings without any significant signs of progression. Case 2

In September 2024, a 30-year-old woman of Middle Eastern origin was referred to our pulmonary hypertension center because of progressive dyspnea on minimal exertion (WHO functional class III) and recurrent chest pain, which had been present for several years. Her family history was unremarkable for hereditary diseases. She was a non-smoker with no history of other medical conditions. The initial NT-proBNP level was highly increased with 1730 pg/ml and echocardiography showed signs of a severe pulmonary hypertension an estimated systolic pulmonary arterial pressure (sPAP) of 83 mmHg, a dilated right ventricle with positive D-shaping of the left ventricle, and impaired right ventricular function as indicated by a TAPSE of 15.3 mm. Exercise capacity was severely reduced, with a 6-min walking distance (6MWD) of 90 m. Pulmonary function testing showed a severely reduced DLCOc of 23% (performed with VyntusTM BODY Plethysmograph).

She was diagnosed pulmonary arterial hypertension according to the initial RHC in September 2024 with mPAP of 45 mmHg, PAWP of 3 mmHg, PVR of 11.6 WU. Subcarinal and unilateral hilar lymphadenopathy were present on chest CT, together with subtle ground-glass opacities and discrete interlobular septal thickening. Furthermore, ventilation/perfusion scintigraphy showed no perfusion mismatches.

A dual therapy with sildenafil and macitentan was initiated along with long-term oxygen therapy. Due to an insufficient therapeutic response and a persistent intermediate-high risk profile, selexipag was added in November 2024. Selexipag was poorly tolerated because of jaw pain and myalgia and therefore could not be up-titrated beyond 400 μg/day. Right heart catheterization performed under triple therapy demonstrated a significant improvement in hemodynamics and a slight improvement in DLCOc and KCOc. However, the patient remained markedly limited regarding exercise capacity and gas exchange.

In June 2025, genetic testing revealed a homozygous EIF2AK4-mutation (c.3344C > T, p.(Pro1115Leu)) leading to the diagnosis of hereditary PVOD. Chest CT performed in July 2025 demonstrated unchanged lymphadenopathy, with a slight increase in ground-glass opacities and mild progression of interlobular septal thickening. Given inadequate tolerability of selexipag and radiological signs of disease progression with increasing ground-glass opacities despite vasoactive therapy, we decided to replace selexipag with sotatercept in September 2025. In parallel, the patient was referred to a lung transplantation center for further evaluation. CT imaging in November 2025 showed stable disease without progression of ground-glass opacities under the new treatment with sotatercept.

During treatment with sotatercept, DLCOc increased from initially 25% to 35%. Right heart catheterization in March 2026 showed further hemodynamic improvement, with a reduction in mean pulmonary arterial pressure to 25 mmHg and pulmonary vascular resistance to 4.6 WU. Clinically, the patient demonstrated marked improvement in hyperventilation and functional status, enabling discontinuation of resting oxygen therapy. Follow-up at seven months confirmed a sustained response with continued improvement in DLCOc. Except for epistaxis, sotatercept was well tolerated. Discussion

We present two cases of hereditary PVOD under treatment with sotatercept. Although there is a short recent report of five patients with presumed PVOD treated with sotatercept showing a clinical improvement, the diagnosis of those cases was based solely on chest CT findings, decreased DLCO and clinical response to vasodilatory therapy, without histological or genetic confirmation [7]. Given the overlap between severe PAH and PVOD-like phenotypes, the diagnosis of PVOD in such cases cannot be established with certainty and may partly reflect severe PAH with PVOD-like features rather than true PVOD. To our knowledge, our two presented cases are the first reports of sotatercept therapy in genetically confirmed PVOD.

Despite hemodynamic improvement under vasodilator therapy, the high mortality rate and rapid disease progression characterized by a median time from diagnosis to death or lung transplantation of 11.8 months prompted the decision to escalate treatment with sotatercept in both cases [8]. None of the clinical trials PULSAR or STELLAR had any PVOD patients included [6,9], thus there is no data about the efficacy of sotatercept in PVOD patients.

In PVOD, vasodilator therapy appears to be of limited efficacy and tolerability. Due to the complex pathogenesis involving fibrotic obliteration of small pulmonary veins and venules, a therapeutic approach with antiproliferative effects is considered promising. Whether sotatercept can exert clinically relevant antiproliferative effects in a cohort of genetically confirmed PVOD patients remains unclear. There is experimental evidence that transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)/Smad plays a role in PVOD pathobiology. In a mitomycin C–induced rat model of PVOD, activation of the TGF-β/Smad3 pathway promoted pulmonary vascular endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, resulting in venular remodeling and development of a PVOD-like phenotype. Importantly, pharmacological targeting of Smad3 attenuated vascular remodeling and led to a milder disease course [10]. These findings suggest that dysregulated TGF-β/Smad signaling may contribute not only to arterial remodeling but also to venous and capillary remodeling in PVOD. In this context, sotatercept may be mechanistically relevant through modulation of downstream TGF-β superfamily signaling pathways. Although direct experimental data for sotatercept in PVOD are lacking, restoration of the balance between pro-proliferative SMAD2/3 signaling and protective BMP/SMAD1/5/8 pathways may represent a plausible mechanism contributing to disease stabilization.

Our patient of the first case has been treated for 10 months now with sotatercept and shows a clinical stable condition. Despite radiological changes on CT in September 2025, including new ground-glass opacities and interlobular septal thickening compared with the previous scan, pulmonary function tests showed a slight increase in DLCOc. Although DLCO measurements can fluctuate within individuals, in this patient DLCO has shown a consistent increase under therapy, supporting its reliability to monitor treatment response. The observation of a stable to temporarily slightly improved DLCOc suggests that sotatercept may have a positive effect on disease progression in this hereditary form of PVOD and that some of the expected antiproliferative effects may also be present in PVOD. Moreover, the interpretation of the radiological course is limited by the absence of a chest CT prior to initiation of sotatercept in May 2025. The last available CT before sotatercept treatment (09/2024) demonstrated only bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy, after which riociguat therapy was started. As vasoactive therapies may promote and unmask radiographic features of PVOD, the subtle groundglass opacities and interlobular septal thickening observed on CT in September 2025 could also be linked to disease progression occurring under riociguat treatment before starting sotatercept therapy. Notably, DLCOc decline was already documented in March 2025, prior to sotatercept initiation, suggesting that PVOD-associated changes seen in chest CT in September 2025 may already have been evolving before sotatercept exposure. Importantly, follow-up imaging in March 2026 demonstrated radiological stability without further progression of ground-glass opacities or septal thickening, paralleled by stable DLCOc values. Taken together, the overall radiological and functional course may therefore be more consistent with stabilization of disease activity under sotatercept rather than continued progression.

This interpretation is supported by a second case, in which ground-glass opacities were already present on CT performed shortly before initiation of sotatercept and had slightly increased during preceding vasodilator therapy. Under sotatercept, these radiological abnormalities remained stable during short-term follow-up, while DLCOc continued to improve over the longer term.

The presumed therapeutic effect is further supported by the finding of relatively stable post-exertional PaO2 and PaCO2 over an extended period in case 1. During the latest follow-up examination, a worsening of both PaO2 and PaCO2 after exertion may indicate a progression of PVOD without limiting our patient's condition nor his daily life. This observation does not contradict our hypothesis that sotatercept may slow disease progression. Without therapy, the progression of PVOD would be expected to occur more rapidly, as suggested by existing literature. An even more pronounced improvement in gas exchange was observed in case 2 during treatment with sotatercept, allowing discontinuation of oxygen therapy at rest and accompanied by a clear reduction in hyperventilation. Differences in treatment response between the two cases may be related, at least in part, to the underlying EIF2AK4 variants, with a nonsense mutation in case 1 and a missense mutation in case 2 potentially contributing to differences in residual gene function and phenotypic expression.

Extended longitudinal follow-up of the course of disease would help to define the efficacy and safety of sotatercept in hereditary PVOD. In this case, clinical, functional and radiological monitoring is ongoing. In addition, the obseravtions from another patient with hereditary PVOD, who has shown a similar response to treatment with sotatercept may promote to clarify the potential role of sotatercept in stabilizing disease progression in this rarecondition. Prospective data collection and future clinical trials in genetically defined PVOD patients are needed. Given the extreme rarity of PVOD, this attempt will be challenging and is unlikely to be feasible within single-center studies. Therefore, multicenter collaboration and registry-based approaches will be essential to systematically collect longitudinal data and should be actively pursued to better characterize the safety and efficacy of sotatercept in this patient population.

Conclusion

Sotatercept may be a therapeutic option to stabilize the condition of patients with PVOD while improving DLCO. Furthermore, it may potentially prolong the bridging period to transplantation, the only curative therapy known.

As hereditary PVOD is a rare form of PAH, further investigations about the effect of sotatercept on disease progress will be needed.
