Executive Summary
Expanding Access to Charging at Homes (“EACH” or “Program” or “Project”) is a nationwide
multifamily housing charging program consisting of education, outreach, and technical
assistance with the purpose of enabling a large-scale increase in installations of electric vehicle
(“EV”) charging infrastructure at multifamily housing (“MFH”) properties. The program was
branded as “Charge at Home” and will be referenced as “Charge at Home", the “Program” or the
“Project” throughout the report depending on the subject matter of the section. Charge at Home
provides educational tools and recommendations for multifamily housing industry stakeholders,
incentive providers, including utilities, state agencies, cities, and other governmental agencies,
to eliminate the barriers to accessing funds for installing EV charging infrastructure at multifamily
properties.

Through award DE-EE0010612 from the US Department of Energy’s Office of Energy Efficiency
and Renewable Energy (EERE), on behalf of the Vehicle Technologies Office (VTO), Forth led
and executed a two-year long project, referred to externally as “Charge at Home”, with support
Project Partners CALSTART, Electrification Coalition, Energetics, GRID Alternatives, Clean
Cities and Communities of Central New York, Virginia Clean Cities, Michigan Clean Cities and
Drive Clean Indiana. The Project sought to address barriers to EV charging at multifamily
properties by centering the multifamily decision maker as the target audience. With this in mind,
the Project Team developed a comprehensive suite of resources with special attention to
segmenting the multifamily market, led a sweeping outreach and marketing campaign, engaged
the target audience through a variety of mediums, provided opportunity for community input into
resource development, and created a direct channel for multifamily decision makers to receive
specialized, in-depth support with their EV charging projects.

a. Target Audiences

The primary audience the Program supports are property managers, property owners, property
developers; National, regional, and local Housing Industry Associations (and similar
organizations); Homeowners Associations (HOAs), Public Housing Authorities;
Community-based Organizations (CBOs); Student Housing Stakeholders; State and Local
Housing Financing Agencies (HFAs); Electric utilities; Cities and local governmental entities; EV
charging providers; EV charging installers and infrastructure distributors. This Program
considers the following secondary audiences: Architects, Parking Management Organizations,
Multifamily Residents and Transportation agencies.

Project Background

Problem Statement
Electric vehicle (EV) charging is most convenient and cost-effective for EV owners when
available at home, paid through residential utility bills. However, residents of multifamily housing
(MFH) properties face a number of barriers to achieving cost-effective access compared to
single-family homes due to only a fraction of properties providing EV charging on-site.
Over 40 million apartment renters live in ~ 23 million apartment homes, making up 31.4% of all
housing in the United States. According to the National Multifamily Housing Council, one in
three apartment renters do not have access to EV charging where they live. Survey data shows
that multifamily resident interest in electric vehicles grew from 27% in 2022 to 34% in 2024, and
that 47% of respondents are either considering or definitely purchasing an EV as their next
vehicle.1 However, residents of MFH properties face a number of barriers to achieving
cost-effective access compared to single-family homes due to only a fraction of properties
providing EV charging on-site.
While financial barriers can exist at MFH properties, the lack of EV charging knowledge by
property decision-makers poses a considerable risk to MFH EV charging adoption. Access to
research-based guidance on MFH charging is a significant barrier for MFH decision-makers.
Knowledge gaps include, but are not limited to, MFH property managers’, HOAs’ and
developers’ lack of knowledge about EVs, EV charging and charging options, available financial

 7

incentive programs, innovative charging technologies designed for MFH settings and current
and future demand for EV charging.
The Department of Energy’s Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy (EERE), on
behalf of the Vehicle Technologies Office (VTO), issued Funding Opportunity Announcement
(FOA) DE-EE0010612 (FOA) entitled, “ Fiscal Year 2022 Vehicle Technologies Office Program
Wide Funding Opportunity Announcement ”. This FOA sought research projects to address
priorities in the following area, which resulted in the EACH Project: the cost - effective
deployment of EV charging for those without easy home charging; innovative solutions to
improve mobility options for underserved communities; community engagement to accelerate
clean transportation options.

Prime Forth and its partners, Energetics, Electrification Coalition, CALSTART, GRID
Alternatives, National Multifamily Housing Council, Virginia Clean Cities, Clean Communities of
Central New York, Drive Clean Indiana, Michigan Clean Cities and the Community Climate
Collaborative, developed and executed the Expanding Access to Charging at Home (“EACH”)
DE-EE0010612 project, which provides a suite of free tools, best practices, resources and
site-specific consultations for the multifamily industry to reduce barriers to EV charging at
multifamily housing (“MFH”) properties.

Project Approach
The goal of the Expanding Access to Charging at Homes (“EACH” or “Program” or “Project”)
was to develop a nationwide multifamily housing charging program consisting of education,
outreach, and technical assistance that enables a large-scale increase in installations of electric
vehicle (“EV”) charging infrastructure at multifamily housing (“MFH”) properties. Decisions made
throughout the Project Period centered on best practices and recommendations for multifamily
housing industry stakeholders. Information gathered through months of research, meetings with
EV charging professionals and MFH property representatives and iterations of website and
program content feedback from EV and MFH industry leaders resulted in Forth’s project delivery
and workflow goals. The direction of the Program was a direct result of the project team’s
findings on the greatest need of the MFH industry to effectively overcome the education and
resource barriers to bringing EV charging to multifamily residents.

Project Delivery and Workflow
The Expanding Access to Charging at Home program commenced in October 2023 and ended
in September 2025. The program accomplished and surpassed its goals, and has a firmly
established sustainability plan, ensuring investments made in the program make an impact
beyond the period of performance of the grant. By focusing on information gathering,
partnership building and framing the website and educational content throughout 2024, the
Project Team experienced a successful website launch in January 2025. The website launch
was soon followed by a series of interactive webinar sessions, phased educational material
launch, continued in-person engagement at conferences and community events across the
country, and engagement with various EV charging incentive program providers.

Task 1 focused on planning, developing, and refining the website containing technical resources
for the target audience and gathering stakeholders to create market-segmented best practices
reports. This phase also focused on outreach in Core Cities and at national and regional
conferences.

Task 2 focused on national and regional outreach, evaluating outreach experience in Core
Cities, online marketing of the website, and finalizing and publishing market-segmented best
practices reports. This phase also focused on evaluating the national outreach efforts, revising
best practices reports, identifying a sustainability plan for the website, educational offerings and
continued technical assistance for MFH and EV charging stakeholders, and completing all
necessary reporting.

Task 1 (Core Cities, Website Development, and Targeted
Outreach) Project Approach:

Outreach and communications infrastructure was advanced through development of a unified
project identity and supporting digital platform. The project was branded as Charge at Home to
align with Forth’s involvement in the Charge@Work program, which is a complementary
program focused on advancing EV charging access at workplaces across the country. Website
development progressed through content creation, partner review, and iterative design and user
experience refinement. The website soft launch timeline was ultimately delayed as the transfer
was dependent on the completion of the VCI-MUD.org platform transfer. Outreach planning also
included development and review of project partner outreach plans, a national outreach and
marketing plan, finalization of conference participation, and developing strategies to create the
most impactful public presence.

Year 1 also established the foundation for development of a key technical assistance resource:
an interactive Multifamily Housing (MFH) Project Builder tool. Project staff evaluated vendors,
finalized a scope of work, reviewed budget estimates, and selected Atlas Public Policy as the

 11

development partner. Development of the Project Builder tool was initiated, positioning the
project for implementation and stakeholder-facing deployment in subsequent project years.

Task 1.1 Outreach and Engagement Planning

Task 1.1 Core Cities, Website Development and Targeted Outreach​
Task 1.1 focused on preparing for and beginning national outreach to major target audience
stakeholders and collaborating with the program’s five core cities to define useful strategies for
engaging multi-family stakeholders. Task 1 entailed the creation of outreach and engagement
plans for core cities, and the creation of a national outreach plan and the development of a
website. This was accomplished by establishing program infrastructure, formalizing partner
coordination, advancing outreach planning, and initiating development of core technical
assistance resources for multifamily housing (MFH) stakeholders. Significant effort was devoted
to building efficient internal management systems and strengthening external partnerships.
Subrecipient agreements were executed, and a standardized framework for project meetings
and reporting was implemented, including regular all-partner coordination and ongoing project
check-ins. These systems were designed to ensure effective communication, performance
tracking, and fiscal oversight throughout the project lifecycle.

Foundational project management activities included development and refinement of the Project
Management Plan (PMP) and initiation of subcontracting with key partners. Scopes of work
were finalized, deliverables confirmed, and reporting and invoicing procedures established to
support consistent coordination and accountability. Engagement with industry stakeholders
through targeted conferences informed early project direction, improving understanding of MFH
market dynamics, including nuances of condominium, student housing and affordable housing
market segments. These engagements highlighted sector-specific operational differences and
the complexities of integrating EV charging within affordable housing developments governed by
layered subsidy programs.

The Project Team developed adaptable MFH stakeholder outreach and engagement plans for
each of the Core Cities by scoping local and regional MFH events, creating location-specific
educational materials, and working with local and regional partners, particularly organizations
representing historically underserved communities.

Task 1.2 Website Development and Refinement
Over the course of the project period, the Project Team developed and launched a
comprehensive suite of resources and interactive tools on www.chargeathome.org to support
multifamily housing (MFH) stakeholders in planning and implementing electric vehicle (EV)
charging. Each major element of the website represents a distinct content category designed to
provide actionable guidance, technical resources, and decision-making support for property

 12

owners, managers, and developers. Task 1.2 outlined the elements of the website, which were
further developed and refined in Task 2.

1. MFH EV Charging Toolkits​
The website hosts seven (7) tailored toolkits for various MFH property types, including new and
existing market-rate, affordable, student housing, and condominium properties. These toolkits
provide step-by-step guidance on planning and deploying EV charging infrastructure and were
reviewed by subject matter experts to ensure accuracy and usability. Utilities best practices
documentation is also included as a resource for site-specific planning considerations.

2. Resident and Stakeholder Resources​
Resident-facing materials, technical one-pagers, and case studies are available to inform
stakeholders about EV charging benefits, project logistics, and operational considerations.
These resources enhance understanding among residents, property managers, and
community-based organizations, facilitating broader engagement in MFH EV adoption.

 13

3. Project Builder Tool​
The interactive Multifamily Housing Project Builder Tool guides MFH stakeholders through the
process of evaluating and planning EV charging projects. Developed in collaboration with Atlas
Public Policy, the tool incorporates state-specific cost data, scenario planning, and user
experience refinements. The tool takes simple inputs from users, such as location, property size
(units and parking stalls), parking configuration, location, class, average rent, electrical capacity
and electricity rates that a developer or property manager should have on hand. The tool runs
analyses against collected data and models to provide information on average installation costs,
available incentives based on location, expected payback period for the property owner, and
expected refueling savings available to residents (as compared with both internal combustion
engine vehicles and public EV charging). The results of these analyses are presented to the
user in an easy-to-understand summary, along with actionable next steps such as where to
apply for a specific incentive. In addition to helping MFH stakeholders assess deploying
charging infrastructure at specific sites, this tool is useful for educating users and storytelling for
overall electrification goals.

Multiple rounds of feedback from the Project Team, partners, and subject matter experts
ensured the tool is practical, accessible, and directly applicable to property-specific decisions.

 14

4. Case Studies​
The website features 18 MFH EV charging project case studies, nine of which were developed
during the project period, highlighting real-world installations in collaboration with different EV
charging companies, installers and incentive providers. These case studies illustrate best

 15

practices, challenges, and lessons learned, serving as practical examples for property
decision-makers.

5. Free Consultation Program​
The Charge at Home Free Consultation Program offers personalized guidance for MFH
properties. Through an online intake form, users submit property-level details such as building
type, ownership structure, parking configuration, and readiness considerations. The Charge at
Home team then analyzes these inputs and delivers customized guidance through the user’s
preferred communication channel. The program includes immediate feedback and six-month
follow-up surveys to evaluate outcomes, such as EV charging installations. 52 consultation
requests were completed during the project period, primarily from condominium representatives.

6. Outreach and Event Resources​
The website lists information from webinars, roundtables, and other stakeholder engagement
events, providing summaries, key takeaways, and relevant materials to extend learning and
accessibility beyond live sessions.

7. Continuous Improvement and User Feedback​
All website content categories were developed iteratively, incorporating internal and external
feedback from partners, subject matter experts and community stakeholders. This approach
ensures that www.chargeathome.org is a comprehensive, practical and user-friendly platform
for MFH EV charging planning and adoption. The website was continually refined as it garnered
feedback from the MFH industry and Project Team members before and after launch.

Task 1.3 Direct Outreach and Engagement in Core Cities and Nationally
Under Task 1.3, outreach and engagement were carried out in regions in and surrounding South
Bend, Indiana, Syracuse, New York, Portland, Oregon, Charlottesville, Virginia, as well as
nationally. The Project Team connected with MFH property owners, managers, developers,
utilities, state agencies, EV charging companies, installers and community-based organizations
through direct outreach, meetings, and local events. Speaking opportunities at housing and
sustainability events enabled the team to share information on EV charging technologies,
incentives, and available resources. The Project Team also responded to regional inquiries by
providing virtual site assessments, guidance on site eligibility, and access to educational tools.

The Project Team conducted coordinated outreach and engagement activities with a focus on
advancing electric vehicle (EV) charging readiness across all multifamily housing market
segments. Activities emphasized relationship-building with local partners, property stakeholders,
and community-based organizations, while piloting localized engagement strategies that
complemented national program efforts. Monthly coordination meetings were held to share
updates, align regional outreach strategies, and ensure consistency with Charge at Home
program goals. These efforts included staff participation from regional offices to maintain
alignment across local initiatives.

In Michigan, joint outreach targeted property management and development companies,
housing advocacy organizations, and public agencies, introducing program resources and
technical assistance opportunities. Engagements with stakeholders such as municipal entities
and sustainability districts explored pathways to expand MFH EV charging through events,
webinars, and regular communication and follow-up. Michigan Project Partners also supported
the development of promotional materials, including event flyers and webinar graphics, and led
a regional webinar highlighting MFH EV charging efforts in Lansing, Michigan.

In Portland, Oregon, outreach activities emphasized promoting the free resources and technical
assistance available through the Charge at Home program to support EV charging planning at
multifamily properties. Engagement included participation in six structured events: two regional
multifamily conferences, one reverse trade show, and three county-level workshops. The team
attended additional local housing, transportation, and community events. These efforts

 18

facilitated meaningful, solution-oriented conversations about EV charging readiness, with
particular interest driven by the availability of no-cost, third-party EV charging consultation.
Through these engagements, the team connected with a broad range of key stakeholders,
including property management companies, real estate owners and operators, construction and
electrical contractors, utilities, financial institutions, transportation agencies, local governments
and nonprofit organizations. Property management firms and multifamily owners represented a
significant share of engaged stakeholders, reflecting strong interest in practical guidance for
planning and implementing EV charging infrastructure. County workshops and community
events further expanded engagement with public agencies, utilities, and sustainability-focused
organizations, strengthening cross-sector awareness of multifamily EV charging needs.

Outreach in Syracuse and Upstate New York focused on engaging stakeholders that influence
multifamily housing planning, development, and operations, including homeowners
associations, property management companies, real estate developers, commercial real estate
firms, universities and community groups. These efforts emphasized the value of integrating EV
charging into both existing and new multifamily properties, while providing guidance on
planning, design, and available incentives. Outreach materials leveraged centralized website
resources and customized presentations to ensure consistent and accessible messaging across
audiences. As a result of outreach in Syracuse, EV charging was successfully installed in a
newly constructed multifamily parking garage near a university district, and EV infrastructure is
now routinely considered during the planning phase for new developments. More broadly, the
project established the coalition as a trusted regional resource, with developers, builders, and
engineering firms regularly seeking guidance on federal, state, and utility incentives to support
EV charging infrastructure deployment in multifamily housing.

In Indiana, the Project Team participated in and presented at numerous regional events,
generating engagement across multiple cities, including South Bend. Outreach efforts involved
collaboration with local governments and housing authorities to identify suitable MFH sites for
EV charging deployment. Stakeholder discussions frequently centered on installation costs,
hardware and software procurement, and funding needs. To help address deployment barriers,
the Project Team contributed in-kind equipment support to advance local readiness.

In Virginia, the Project Team conducted extensive community-facing outreach through resident
events, youth forums, landlord networking opportunities, and regional sustainability and
EV-focused gatherings. EV and charging demonstrations were used to increase awareness, and
a tailored Charge at Home presentation was delivered to multiple local jurisdictions. The Project
Team developed and distributed outreach materials across multiple platforms, managed direct
outreach to targeted MFH sites, and convened planning and brainstorming sessions with
partners to strengthen engagement pipelines.

National outreach and engagement was carried out throughout the entirety of the project period
by a team of project partners, including the National Multifamily Housing Council, Forth,

Technical Key Findings
Existing Multifamily Properties​
Note: For the complete Best Practices Report, please see the MFH EV Charging Existing
Properties Best Practices Report submitted alongside this report.
​
For existing buildings, successful EV charging projects begin with a clear understanding of
current site conditions. Early resident demand surveys, electrical evaluations, and load studies
are essential to determine feasible charging strategies and to set realistic expectations around
cost, phasing, and timelines. Unlike new construction, existing properties must balance charging
goals with limited panel capacity, aging infrastructure, and the need to minimize disruption to
residents.

Best practices emphasize phased deployment, starting with solutions that maximize available
electrical capacity, such as EV energy management systems (EMS) paired with Level 2 (L2)
chargers, communal chargers, and Level 1 or 2 outlets, while planning for future expansion.
Installing EV-capable and EV-ready infrastructure during retrofits helps reduce future costs even
when immediate charger installation is limited.

Property context significantly influences implementation strategies. While all property managers
want to utilize incentives, market-rate properties often focus on revenue recovery and amenity
value with short-term payback in mind. Affordable housing, deed restricted specifically, providers
usually require incentives to consider charger installation and typically prioritize access to
charging for residents over a quick payback. Condominiums face additional complexity related
to deeded parking and owner installed and maintained equipment, association governance
bylaws, billing process and responsibility, and board approvals, making early policy and bylaw
review critical, incentives important, and technical assistance invaluable.

New Development Multifamily Properties​
Note: For the complete Best Practices Report, please see the MFH EV Charging New
Properties Best Practices Report submitted alongside this report.

 27

Across new multifamily developments, the most effective strategy is early coordination among
development, design, and construction teams to incorporate EV-capable and EV-ready
infrastructure into initial electrical plans. Installing conduit, panel capacity, and raceways during
construction significantly lowers future charger installation costs compared to retrofits, and
allows properties to scale charging deployment as resident demand grows.

Best practices emphasize selecting appropriate charging configurations, dedicated or
communal, based on parking layout, targeted tenant expectations, and anticipated use patterns;
aligning EV infrastructure with applicable state and local codes; and integrating accessibility
requirements from the outset. Energy management systems and thoughtful panel design allow
properties to support higher charger counts without oversized electrical service.

While foundational principles are consistent, recommended best practices vary by property type.
Market-rate developments often position EV charging as a premium amenity and
revenue-generating feature. However, EV charging’s primary selling point at market-rate
properties is that this feature tends to attract higher income tenants, while reducing unit
turnover, often justifying a lower cost to the tenant and more revenue-neutral business model.
Off-campus (car centric) student housing, similar to market-rate, invests in EV charging as a
premium amenity to attract higher-end renters most commonly through communal charging
configurations. This lowers infrastructure investment ensuring resident satisfaction through
access controls.

Affordable housing developments prioritize cost containment and incentive use, allowing use by
non-residents, building staff, contractors, and guests, even if public access is denied. Affordable
housing providers have noted more resident EV ownership, particularly with tenants driving for
rideshare apps like Uber and Lyft, which is a result of more EVs entering the used market.

Condominiums introduce considerable complexity due to developers' diligence to keep costs
low, ensuring returns to investors when units sell or are presold. Condo developers should
familiarize themselves with EV charging energy management systems and optimized
EV-capable conduit and panel installation as a way to enable scalability while avoiding a full
EV-ready parking garage buildout. ​

Conclusion
The Expanding Access to Charging at Home (EACH) project successfully met and exceeded its
objectives to reduce barriers to electric vehicle (EV) charging deployment in multifamily housing
(MFH) by delivering a comprehensive, nationally scalable suite of education, outreach, and
technical assistance resources. Through the Charge at Home platform, the project established a
trusted, centralized source of practical guidance tailored to the diverse needs of MFH
stakeholders, including property owners, managers, developers, affordable housing providers,
and condominium associations. By combining market-segmented toolkits, an interactive Project

 28

Builder tool, case studies, webinars, and direct engagement, the project addressed critical
knowledge gaps related to costs, incentives, technology options, and implementation pathways.
Strong partnerships with national and local organizations enabled broad reach and credibility,
resulting in significant engagement metrics, measurable commitments to EV charging
installations, and increased readiness across the MFH sector.

A defining outcome of the EACH project is the establishment of a durable framework for
long-term impact beyond the DOE funding period. The Charge at Home Free Consultation
Program emerged as the central sustainability mechanism, providing no-cost, third-party,
property-specific guidance to MFH decision-makers while generating actionable insights for
utilities, incentive administrators, and public agencies. Early demand for consultations,
particularly from condominium stakeholders, underscores both the ongoing need for tailored
technical assistance and the program’s value as an implementation catalyst. The integration of
structured feedback and follow-up evaluation further positions the program to document
outcomes, refine resources, and demonstrate continued market transformation as EV adoption
accelerates.

Replicability
Collectively, the EACH project demonstrates a replicable and scalable model for supporting
cost-effective EV charging deployment in multifamily housing nationwide. By centering MFH
decision-makers, leveraging trusted partnerships, and aligning technical assistance with
financial and utility engagement, the project advances DOE and VTO priorities related to
equitable access to charging, community engagement, and clean transportation adoption. With
sustained operation of the Charge at Home platform and consultation program, Forth and its
partners are well-positioned to continue supporting MFH stakeholders, utilities, and
policymakers in expanding EV charging infrastructure and ensuring that multifamily residents,
including those in underserved and low-income communities, are not left behind in the transition
to electric mobility. For more information of the continuation of the resulting product of this
project, please see the Technology Transfer Plan and Market Transformation Report submitted
alongside this report.
