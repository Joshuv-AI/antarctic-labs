Quantum Dynamical Entropy and non-Markovianity: a collisional model perspective
Giovanni Nichele, Fabio Benatti

Abstract
Accessing the physical mechanisms behind non-Markovian phenomena in open quantum dynamics requires the study of the statistical properties of the joint system-environment dynamics. This is impossible at the level of the reduced dynamics of the open system alone as the latter is obtained by suitably eliminating the environment. The task is instead made possible by considering multi-time correlation functions involving observables of the open system, only: the open system-environment interactions turn them into global ones thus building up correlations between the two systems. Multi-time correlations form the basis of both the theory of quantum stochastic processes and of the Alicki-Lindblad-Fannes dynamical entropy (ALF entropy for short). This latter quantity provides for quantum systems a measure of the dynamical entropy production as the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy does for classical systems. In the case of a collisional model whereby the dissipative dynamics of a finite-level system is obtained by its coupling to an infinite classical spin chain, the ALF entropy can be explicitly computed. It turns out to depend on the parameters characterizing the statistical properties of the environment and can be related to the activation and super-activation of memory effects in the open quantum system.



I
Introduction

Due to their importance in quantum information technologies, the non-Markovian features of open quantum dynamics have recently become the focus of much research and have been addressed by several, non-equivalent, techniques resulting in an intricate hierarchy
Li et al. 2018
.
As an instance, typically the time-evolution of an open quantum system is provided by a family
\{\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP) linear maps
\rho\longmapsto\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{t}[\rho]
on the space of states

;

In such a scenario, the lack of positivity-preservation and thus of contractivity of the propagators gives rise to the increase, instead of the decrease, of several information distances, a phenomenon generally interpreted as a physical back-flow of information from the environment into the open system
Breuer et al. 2009

Considering instead a classical discrete time scenario as a benchmark for possible quantum extensions of the notion of Markovianity, there one deals with multi-time probabilities

The proper quantum extension of non-Markovian stochastic processes, including the multi-time statistics, is rooted in a long history dating back to pioneering works
Accardi et al. 1982
;
Accardi 1981
;
Lindblad 1979
;
Lewis 1981
;
Frigerio 1981

Alicki and Fannes 2001
;
Breuer et al. 2016
;
Milz et al. 2020

;
Pollock et al. 2018a
;
Pollock et al. 2018b

Chiribella et al. 2008
;
Milz et al. 2020

;
Budini 2022
and temporal entanglement (see the recent work
Vilkoviskiy et al. 2025

Furthermore, in such a non-commutative scenario, G. Lindblad
Lindblad 1979
proposed to look at Markovianity using
a quantum version of the Kolmogorov-Sinai (KS) entropy, this latter concept measuring the amount of entropy, that is of ignorance before measuring or, equivalently, of information gathered afterwards, per unit time-step provided by the dynamics.
Such a notion was later fully developed by Alicki and Fannes in
Alicki and Fannes 1994
, with applications mainly to the reversible dynamics of quantum many-body systems
Alicki and Fannes 2001
. Differently from other proposals of quantum dynamical entropy,
the Alicki-Lindblad-Fannes (ALF) entropy takes into consideration the fact that information about quantum systems is obtained by measuring schemes that, unlike classical phase-space localization with respect to a suitable coarse graining, interfere with the given dynamics. Notice, that such an approach contrasts with the idea of information back-flow which instead focuses on the dynamics of the system
S
only,
without the need of any measurement and so, effectively, on marginal probabilities.

In the following, we adapt the ALF entropy to an open quantum system scenario, whereby a one-qubit Pauli dynamics is obtained by means of a so-called collisional model
Ciccarello et al. 2022
;
Campbell and Vacchini 2021
with the environment being a correlated classical spin chain and the open quantum system interacting locally with a single site algebra at each tick of time
Benatti and Nichele 2025
. In such a context, we are able to explicitly compute the ALF entropy of the irreversible qubit dynamics and relate its behaviour to the activation and super-activation of memory effects, the latter being a pure quantum phenomenon whereby back-flow of information shows up putting together a system with no back-flow of information with a copy of itself.

The work is structured as follows. In Section
II
, we review the main algebraic and conceptual tools necessary in the remaining sections, such as the QR formula and the ALF entropy for reversible systems. In Section
III
, we introduce the concept of open-system ALF entropy and compare and contrast its typical behavior for closed finite systems with Markovian open ones. In Section
IV
, we discuss the main results of the work. Their proper operational interpretation is further discussed in the subsequent Section
V
, before ending with the Conclusions.

Results

The main results of the paper are as follows.
First, in Section
III
, we give an independent new proof of the results of
Lindblad 1979
; namely, we show that, by looking at specific measurement-dependent entropy rates, one can ascertain the property of QR-Markovianity.
Then, we propose the decrease of the open-system ALF entropy with respect to the QR-Markovian setting as a measure of how much information has flown back from environment to system.

Furthermore, by framing collisional models within the algebraic approach, we provide a general upper bound to the ALF entropy of a finite level system coupled to a quantum spin chain.

In Section
IV
, we focus on the concrete model of a qubit collisionally interacting with a classical spin chain, for which we explicitly compute the open-system ALF entropy; in particular, we show that a dissipative, highly non-Markovian dynamics can achieve vanishing entropy production,
as a reversible dynamics would.

Finally, in Section
V
, the operational interpretation of the previous results is discussed in the state purification scheme known as GNS construction. There, we show that the algebraic structure underlying the computation of the ALF entropy of
the open symbolic dynamics is helpful in assessing memory effects that are not detectable by the reduced dynamics, only; in particular, the so-called super-activation of memory effects that may characterize dissipative dilations of the the reduced dynamics.

II.2
Quantum Regression

Non-Markovianity is intuitively associated with the presence of memory effects; as already anticipated in the Introduction, given the reduced dynamics
\{\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of an open quantum system, a standard approach is via the notion of
divisibility
. Namely, one looks at propagators

\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n}=\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n,m}\circ\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{m}\,,\quad n\geq m

(12)

and call the reduced evolution (C)-P divisible if
\Lambda_{n,m}^{\ddagger}
are (completely) positive and trace preserving for all
n\geq m
. In such a setting,
CP-divisibility is often deemed a good notion of Markovianity
Chruściński 2022
. An associated remarkable feature of non-Markovianity is the so-called back-flow of information
Breuer et al. 2009
;
Wißmann et al. 2015
, that manifests itself when the evolution is not even P-divisible.
For algebraically invertible dynamics, in fact, lack of P-divisibility equals lack of trace-norm contractivity with the consequence that
the distinguishability of two density matrices
\rho,\sigma
might increase in time. Such time-revivals of trace norms,
\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}
, as well as of other distances or entropic divergences,
is accordingly interpreted as back-flow of information from the environment into the system, a clear signature of memory effects.
Back-flow of information can also occur in a commutative setting when the transition matrices from time-step
m
to time-step
n
are not positive
Benatti et al. 2024
;
Amato et al. 2025
; instead, a purely quantum phenomenon, impossible in a commutative scenario, is the super-activation of back-flow of information
Benatti et al. 2017
;
Benatti and Nichele 2023

Indeed, going from a single-system reduced dynamics
\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n}
to a bipartite one of the form
\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n}\otimes\Lambda^{\ddagger}_{n}
, lack of CP-divisibility of the former might generally cause super-activation of back-flow of information in the latter, while the subsystems do not display back-flow in terms of distinguishability revivals.

Evidently, the physical mechanisms behind a possible back-flow of information cannot be appreciated sticking to the open quantum system only, as it asks to
take into account the full joint system-environment dynamics
\Theta

For the moment, we shall not assume that the family

II.3
ALF Entropy

The degree of instability of a classical dynamics, for instance the existence of a positive Lyapounov exponent, can be associated to dynamical entropy production
by means of the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy
Walters 1982
. The basic idea is to reduce the dynamics to the shift along sequences of symbols labeling
the cells of a finite partition of the phase-space that are visited by the time-evolving system at each time-step. Any time-invariant classical state
assigns probabilities to the sequences with Shannon entropies tending to a well-defined asymptotic rate.
The Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy is the largest among such rates.
The ALF-entropy provides a quantum counterpart to it in that
the coarse-graining of the phase-space is replaced by a POVM-measurements at successive time-steps of the state dynamics.
Let a quantum dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},\omega,\Theta)
be endowed with a reversible automorphic dynamics
\Theta
and with a
\Theta
-invariant state
\omega
on
\mathcal{A}
,
\omega\circ\Theta_{n}=\omega
.
Then, any finite POVM
\mathcal{X}
consisting of
|\mathcal{X}|
operators
\mathcal{X}:=\{X_{a}\}_{a=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|}\,\subseteq\,\mathcal{A}

such that
\sum_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{X}}}X_{a}^{\dagger}X_{a}^{\phantom{*}}=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
gives rise to a family of time-dependent POVM
\mathcal{X}^{(n)}=\left\{X_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}\right\}_{\boldsymbol{a}}
, where
\boldsymbol{a}=a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}
and

X_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}:=\Theta_{n-1}[X_{a_{n-1}}]\,\dots\,\Theta_{1}[X_{a_{1}}]\,X_{a_{0}}\,.

(21)

Notice that this is not true if
\Theta_{n}
is dissipative (see Remark
3
).
For such a reversible dynamical system, it follows that

\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\ =\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}\omega\left(X_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(n)\dagger}\,X_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}\right)\outerproduct{\bs{a}_{[0,n-1]}}{\bs{b}_{[0,n-1]}}

(22)

is a density matrix, that is self-adjoint, positive and normalized with multi-time correlation functions as matrix elements:

\displaystyle\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle=\omega\left(X_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(n)\dagger}X_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt=\omega\bigg(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\Theta_{1}\big[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\big]\Theta_{2}\big[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}\big]\cdots\Theta_{n-1}\big[X_{b_{n-1}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n-1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big]\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\dots\Theta_{2}\big[X_{a_{2}}\big]\Theta_{1}\big[X_{a_{1}}\big]X_{a_{0}}\bigg)\,.

(23)

Using (
13
) and the fact that
\Theta_{n,m}[XY]=\Theta_{n,m}[X]\,\Theta_{n,m}[Y]
, along with (
13
), one can recast (
23
) in a “nested” fashion as in Remark
1
:

\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}=\omega\bigg(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\Theta_{1}\bigg[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\Theta_{2,1}\Big[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}\cdots\\
\dots\Theta_{n-1,n-2}\big[X_{b_{n-1}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n-1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big]\cdots X_{a_{2}}\big]X_{a_{1}}\bigg]X_{a_{0}}\bigg).

(24)

The state
\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}]
provides a description of the statistics of the evolving state as reconstructed by measuring a fixed POVM.

Remark 2
.

By passing from the Heisenberg to the Schrödinger picture via the following dual maps on the space of linear functionals
\omega
over the algebra
\mathcal{A}
of the system:

\omega\mapsto\mathbb{E}^{\ddagger}_{ab}[\omega]
and
\omega\mapsto\Theta_{k,k-1}^{\ddagger}[\omega]
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\ddagger}_{ab}[\omega](X)

\displaystyle=\omega(X_{b}^{\dagger}\,X\,X_{a})\,,

\displaystyle\Theta_{k,k-1}^{\ddagger}[\omega](X)

\displaystyle=\omega(\Theta_{k,k-1}[X])\,,

one rewrites

\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}=\mathbb{E}^{\ddagger}_{a_{n-1}b_{n-1}}\circ\Theta_{n-1,n-2}^{\ddagger}\circ\cdots\\
\cdots\circ\mathbb{E}^{\ddagger}_{a_{1}b_{1}}\circ\Theta_{1}^{\ddagger}\circ\mathbb{E}^{\ddagger}_{a_{0}b_{0}}[\omega](\mathds{1})\,.

(25)

The diagonal entries of
\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]
are then the probabilities of sequences of selective measurements of the various POVM terms followed by the one-step reversible dynamics.

The information gathered in the long run can then be characterized by the so-called ALF-entropy of the given POVM
Alicki and Fannes 1994
, by means of the following entropy rate

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{\omega}(\Theta,\mathcal{X}):=\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}S\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)\,,

(26)

where
S(\rho)
is the von Neumann entropy. One has to do with the
\limsup
since the limit of the ratio could not exist. Indeed, even if the family of automorphisms
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n}
form a group, the family of density matrices
\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]
is not generally stationary since measuring the POVM changes in general the state on which the POVM measurement is performed.
The entropy rate is finally made independent from the chosen POVM by maximizing over all of them chosen from a suitable subalgebra of physically admissible partitions
\mathcal{B}
, typically assumed to be globally
\Theta
-invariant and dense in
\mathcal{A}
:

\mathfrak{h}_{\omega}^{\mathcal{B}}(\Theta)\ :=\ \sup_{\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{B}}\ \mathfrak{h}_{\omega}(\Theta,\mathcal{X})\;.

(27)

A useful control of the ALF entropy is in some cases provided by the following bound whose proof can be found in
Alicki and Fannes 2001
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
\omega(A)=\Tr(\rho A)
for some density matrix
\rho

and let
\mathcal{X}=\{{X}_{a}\}_{a=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
be a POVM.
Then,

S\left(\rho\left[\mathcal{X}\right]\right)\ \leq\ S(\rho)\ +\ S\left(\sum_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}\,X_{a}\,\rho X_{a}^{\dagger}\right)\,.

(28)

Remark 3
.

If the quantum dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},\omega,\Theta)
is dissipative, the dynamics
\Theta
stands for a family of
CPTP channels
\Lambda_{n}
. Then, given a POVM

\mathcal{X}=\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{X}}}
, it is no longer true that
\mathcal{X}^{(n)}
as in (
21
) is a POVM as required by the
ALF construction. Indeed, for general CPU maps the Schwarz inequality yields

\sum_{a_{n-1}}\Lambda_{n-1}[X^{\dagger}_{a_{n-1}}]\Lambda_{n-1}[X_{a_{n-1}}]\\
\leq\sum_{a_{n-1}}\Lambda_{n-1}[X^{\dagger}_{a_{n-1}}X_{a_{n-1}}]=\mathds{1}\,,

so that the quantity

\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}^{(n)}}X^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{a}^{(n)}}X_{\boldsymbol{a}^{(n)}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}^{(n)}}X_{a_{0}}\Lambda_{1}(X^{\dagger}_{a_{1}})\cdots\\

does not generally sum to the identity.
Nevertheless, consider the nested form (

\Theta
with CPU intertwiners
\Lambda_{n,m}

\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}=\omega\Big(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{1}\big[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{2,1}\big[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}\cdots\\
\cdots\Lambda_{n-1,n-2}\big[X_{b_{n-1}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n-1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big]X_{a_{2}}\big]X_{a_{1}}\big]X_{a_{0}}\Big)\ .

(29)

One thus obtains a compatible family of legitimate density matrices,
\Tr_{n-1}\left(\rho[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}]\right)=\rho[\mathcal{X}^{(n-1)}]

As seen in the Quantum Regression formula (

Indeed, as we shall stress in Section
V
, the decrease of the remaining ignorance about the system dynamics signals information coming back from the environment. Moreover, such depletion of gathered information per unit time-step is a stronger witness of back-flow of information than lack of contractivity; indeed, if distinguishability od evolving states increase, than there cannot be P-divisibility of propagators, therefore no QR is possible which, in turn, implies a dynamical entropy rate strictly smaller than the QR-rate.

In the following section, we exploit the collisional approach to open systems to get more insight regarding the behaviour of
\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)
beyond the Markovian regime, i.e. beyond Quantum Regression.
We now begin with a general upper bound to the ALF entropy
\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)
of the collisional model discussed in Section
II
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{E}
be a
\Theta
-invariant state. Then,

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)\ \leq\ \mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}\,+\,\log(D)\,,

(43)

where
\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}

is the mean von Neumann entropy of the chain (
8
).

Proof.

Consider a POVM involving operators of the system
S
only, as in (
30
):

\mathcal{X}=\left\{{X}_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}\right\}_{a},\qquad X_{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{S},\qquad\sum_{a=1}^{d}X_{a}^{\dagger}X_{a}=\mathds{1}_{d}\,.

After
n
steps of the dynamics the algebra of the system is mapped into the local algebras
\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{E}^{[1,n]}
, due to the action of the shift (see (
11
)):
\Theta^{n}(\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E})\ \subseteq\ \mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{E}^{[1,n]}
.
With respect to a
\Theta
-invariant state, the correlation matrix reads:

\displaystyle\left(\rho\left[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\right]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{SE}\left({X}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(n)\dagger}\,{X}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\Omega_{S[1,n]}\,
{X}_{\bs{b}}^{(n)\dagger}\,{X}_{\bs{a}}^{(n)})\,,

with
\Omega_{S[1,n]}=\rho_{S}\otimes\rho_{E}^{[1,n]}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{D}^{[1,n]}(\mathbb{C})
. Hence,
from Proposition
1
, together with the fact that
S(\Omega_{S[1,n]})=S(\rho_{S})+S(\rho_{E}^{[1,n]})
and

S\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}}{X}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)}\,\Omega_{S[1,n]}\,{X}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n)\dagger}\right)\leq\log(dD^n)\ ,

the following upper bound ensues:

\displaystyle S(\rho[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}])\leq S(\rho_{S})+S\big(\rho_{E}^{[1,n]}\big)+\log(d)+n\log(D)\ .

Dividing both sides by
n
and taking the
\limsup
, one gets inequality (
43
).
∎

Remark 7
.

The so-called quantum mutual information,

\displaystyle I_{n}(\omega_{E}):=S(\rho_{E}^{[1,n]})+S(\rho_{E}^{\{1\}})-S(\rho_{E}^{[1,n+1]})

quantifies the correlations between the first
n
sites of the spin chain and the
n+1
-th. From (
8
), the large
n
limit yields

\mathfrak{I}_{\omega_{E}}:=\lim_{n}I_{n}(\omega_{E})=S(\rho_{E}^{\{1\}})-\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}\geq 0\,,

(44)

the inequality saturating for a Bernoulli source. Thus, the stronger the correlations in the environment, the lower results its mean entropy and thus the tighter becomes the bound in (
43
) on the maximal entropy production of the open system
S
. Notice that the r.h.s. of bound (
43
) amounts to the ALF entropy of the shift on a quantum spin chain as proved in
Alicki and Fannes 1994
. However that quantum bound is obtained using all possible POVMs of the chain, whereas here we restricted to those of the open system appended to the chain. It is an open question whether for special system POVMs bound (
43
) could be saturated.

IV
Dynamical entropy and collisional models

In this section, we compute the ALF entropy
\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)
for the collisional model studied in
Benatti and Nichele 2025
which in its simplicity exhibits different types of memory effects directly related to the “microscopic” parameters of the environment
E
. The latter is taken to be a classical stationary source; namely, each site of the chain carries a same commutative, that is diagonal, matrix algebra
\mathcal{A}=D_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, spanned by
1
-dimensional orthogonal projections
\{\Pi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{D-1}
,
\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\Pi_{k}=\mathds{1}_{D}
.
Furthermore, the chain is endowed with a shift-invariant state
\omega_{E}
whose restrictions to any local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{E}^{[1,2]}
are diagonal density matrices:

\rho_{E}^{[a,b]}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}\,\Pi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}^{[a,b]}\,,\quad\Pi^{[a,b]}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}=\bigotimes_{k=a}^{b}\Pi_{i_{k}}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{A}_{E}^{[a,b]},

(45)

with the family of probabilities
\pi_{[1,n]}=\{p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}
satisfying the consistency and stationarity conditions

\sum_{i_{b}}p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}=p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b-1]}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sum_{i_{a}}p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a,b]}}=p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[a-1,b]}}\,.

A finite
d
-level open quantum system is coupled to such a classical environment by means of an interaction with the chain
0
-th site via a
controlled-unitary type,

\displaystyle\Phi[X_{S}\otimes A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\phi_{k}[X_{S}]\otimes\Pi_{k}^{(0)}A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}\Pi_{k}^{(0)}

(46)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\phi_{k}[X_{S}]\otimes A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}\Pi_{k}^{(0)},\quad\phi_{k}[X_{S}]:=U_{k}^{\dagger}\,X_{S}\,U_{k}

where
U_{k}U_{k}^{\dagger}=U_{k}^{\dagger}U_{k}=\mathds{1}_{d}
so that the dual maps on the states of
S
,
\phi^{\ddagger}_{k}[\rho_{S}]=U_{k}\,\rho_{S}\,U^{\dagger}_{k}=\phi^{-1}_{k}[\rho_{S}]
, coincide with the inverse maps as well as the the dual map of
\Phi^{{\ddagger}}
extended to
\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{E}
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{{\ddagger}}[X_{S}\otimes A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\phi^{\ddagger}_{k}[X_{S}]\otimes\Pi_{k}^{(0)}A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}\Pi_{k}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}[X_{S}\otimes A_{i_{0}}^{(0)}]\,.

Finally, the compound
SE
-automorphic dynamics is given by powers of
\Theta=({\rm id}_{S}\otimes\sigma_{E})\circ\Phi
obtained by acting with the chain right-shift after the interaction of the system
S
with the
0
-th site of the classical chain (see (
11
)).

We then take as state of the system
S
the maximally mixed state
\omega_{S}(X_{S})={\rm Tr}(\rho_{S}X_{S})
, where
\rho_{S}=\mathds{1}_{d}/d
, thus enforcing the time-invariance of
\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{E}
. Indeed, leveraging the shift-invariance of
\omega_{E}
and the commutativity of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{E}
, one gets, for all
X_{S}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}
and
X_{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{E}
,

\displaystyle\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{E}(\Theta(X_{S}\otimes X_{E}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\Tr(\phi_k[X_S])\,\omega_{E}\left(\sigma_{E}\left(\Pi_{k}^{(0)}\,X_{E}\,\Pi_{k}^{(0)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Tr(X_S)}{d}\omega_{E}\!\left(\sum_{k}\Pi_{k}^{(0)}X_{E}\Pi_{k}^{(0)}\right)=\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{E}(X_{S}\otimes X_{E}).

IV.1
Structure of the coarse grained density matrix

The
n+1
-th step coarse-grained density matrix based upon a system
S
POVM
{\mathcal{X}}=\left\{X_{a}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}\right\}_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{X}}}
,
X_{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}=M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, and the
\Theta
-invariant state
\omega_{SE}=\omega\otimes\omega_{E}
takes the form

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\left[{\mathcal{X}}^{(n+1)}\right]\ =\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\,p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\ \rho_{S}\left[\mathcal{X}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\right]\,,

(47)

where
\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}=i_{1}\ldots i_{n}
; namely, of a convex combination of sub-coarse-grained matrices
\rho_{S}\left[\mathcal{X}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\right]
with entries

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{S}\left[\mathcal{X}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\right]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{S}\bigg(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{\{1\}}}[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}]\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,2]}}[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}]\ldots

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\ldots\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}[X_{b_{n}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}]\ldots\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,2]}}[X_{a_{1}}]\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{\{1\}}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\omega_{S}\Bigg(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{i_{1}}\bigg[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\phi_{i_{2}}\Big[X_{b_{2}^{\dagger}}\ldots

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\ldots\phi_{i_{n}}\left[X_{b_{n}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\right]\ldots X_{a_{2}}\Big]X_{a_{1}}\bigg]X_{a_{0}}\Bigg)

(48)

specified by the automorphisms
\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,k]}}=\phi_{i_{1}}\circ\,\cdots\,\circ\,\phi_{i_{k}}
and by the
n
-step POVM
\mathcal{X}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}

from
\mathcal{A}_{S}
with components

X_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}:=\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}[X_{a_{n}}]\cdots\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}
.

Proof.

Consider
X_{a_{1}}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}
; then,

\displaystyle\Theta[X_{a_{1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]\otimes\Pi_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\ ,

\displaystyle\Theta^{2}[X_{a_{2}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\phi_{k_{1}}\circ\phi_{k_{2}}[X_{a_{2}}]\otimes\Pi_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\otimes\Pi_{k_{2}}^{(2)}\,,

so that the
2
-step POVM consists of operators

\Theta^{2}[X_{a_{2}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]\Theta[X_{a_{1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]X_{a_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}\\
=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\phi_{k_{1}}\circ\phi_{k_{2}}[X_{a_{2}}]\phi_{k_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\otimes\Pi_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\otimes\Pi_{k_{2}}^{(2)}\,.

Thus, one has

\displaystyle{X}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\Theta^{n}[X_{a_{n}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]\ldots\Theta[X_{a_{1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}]X_{a_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X_{a_{n}}]\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n-1]}}[X_{a_{n-1}}]\ldots\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\{1\}}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\otimes

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\Pi_{k_{j}}^{(j)}\,.

The elements of the coarse-grained density matrix then become

\displaystyle\omega_{SE}\left({X}_{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(n+1)\dagger}{X}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(n+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}p_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}\,\omega_{S}\bigg(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{k_{1}}[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}]\ldots

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\ldots\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X_{b_{n}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{n}}^{\phantom{\dagger}}]\ldots\phi_{{k}_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\bigg)\,.

Given a fixed multi-index
\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}
, the corresponding operators

{X_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}:=\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X_{a_{n}}]\cdots\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,2]}}[X_{a_{2}}]\phi_{{k}_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}}
form a POVM. Indeed, the maps
\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n-1]}}
are automorphisms; namely, they are invertible and such that
\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[XY]=\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X]\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[Y]
and
\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X^{\dagger}]=(\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X])^{\dagger}
. In the case
k=2
, for example,

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}}\left(X_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,2]}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}\right)^{\dagger}X_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,2]}}^{\boldsymbol{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{0},a_{1}}X_{a_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{k_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}^{\dagger}]\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,2]}}\left[\sum_{a_{2}}X_{a_{2}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{2}}\right]\phi_{{k}_{1}}[X_{a_{1}}]X_{a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{0}}X_{a_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{{k}_{1}}\left[\sum_{a_{1}}X_{a_{1}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{1}}\right]X_{a_{0}}=\sum_{a_{0}}X_{a_{0}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{0}}=\mathds{1}_{S}\,.\qed

Remark 8
.

Similarly, the reduced Heisenberg dynamics

\Lambda_{n}[X_{S}]=\mathrm{id}_{S}\otimes\omega_{E}(\Theta^{n}[X_{S}\otimes\mathrm{1}_{E}])
is implemented by the CPU map

M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\,\ni X\ \longmapsto\ \Lambda_{n}[X]\,=\,\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}\,p_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}\ \phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}[X]\,.

(49)

If the classical chain represents a Bernoulli process, then

p_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,p_{k_{j}}
.
The collisional environment is fully uncorrelated and the reduced dynamics is a semigroup,
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda_{1}^{n}
, that is the reduced dynamics at time-step
n
is the
n
-th power of the single time-step reduced dynamics
\Lambda_{1}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\,\phi_{k}
. Further, QR holds. For example, after two iterations of the dynamics,

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{S}\big[\mathcal{X}^{(2)}\big]\right)_{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{2},k_{1}}p_{k_{1}}p_{k_{2}}\ \omega_{S}\left(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\phi_{k_{1}}\left[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\phi_{k_{2}}[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{2}}]X_{a_{1}}\right]X_{a_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega_{S}\left(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\sum_{k_{1}}p_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{1}}\Bigg[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\sum_{k_{2}}p_{k_{2}}\phi_{k_{2}}\left[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{2}}\right]X_{a_{1}}\Bigg]\right)

\displaystyle=\omega_{S}\left(X_{b_{0}}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{1}\left[X_{b_{1}}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{1}\left[X_{b_{2}}^{\dagger}X_{a_{2}}\right]X_{a_{1}}\right]X_{a_{0}}\right)\,,

so that the knowledge of
\Lambda_{1}
suffices to reconstruct the full coarse-grained density matrix.

IV.2
Upper and lower bounds to the ALF entropy

Given the convex combination (
47
), its von Neumann entropy can then be bounded from above as:

\displaystyle S\Big(\rho_{S}\left[{\mathcal{X}}^{(n+1)}\right]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\ H\left(\pi_{[1,n]}\right)+\sum_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}S\left(\rho_{S}\left[\mathcal{X}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ H\left(\pi_{[1,n]}\right)\,+2\,\log(d)\,,

(50)

where we used the well-known inequality for the entropy of a convex mixture
(
Nielsen and Chuang 2002
, Theorem 11.10)
, along with Proposition
1
and the fact that, for all states of
d
-level systems,
S(\rho)\leq\log d
.
Dividing both sides of (
50
) by
n
and taking the
\limsup
, one gets that the dynamical entropy of the open system is upper-bounded by the
entropy rate of the chain,

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)\ \leq\ \lim_{n}\,\frac{1}{n}H\left(\pi_{[1,n]}\right)=\ \mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}\,,

(51)

We now show that inequality (
51
) can be saturated.

Proposition 6
.

Consider a
d
-level system collisionally interacting with a classical stationary spin chain through (
46
). With respect to the invariant state
\mathds{1}_{d}/d\otimes\omega_{E}
and the special POVM
\mathcal{F}
in (
33
), the coarse-grained density matrix takes the form

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\left[\mathcal{F}^{(n+1)}\right]

(52)

\displaystyle\hskip-39.83368pt=\frac{\mathds{1}_{d}}{d}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{d}}{d}\otimes\left(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{\ddagger}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{d}^{\otimes n}\left[\outerproduct{\sqrt{\frac{\mathds{1}_d}{d}^{\otimes n}}}{\sqrt{\frac{\mathds{1}_d}{d}^{\otimes n}}}\right]\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle\hskip-38.12659pt\mathbb{T}_{n}\ =\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\,p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\,\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}^{\otimes[1,n]}\,,\quad\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}^{\otimes[1,n]}:=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{i_{k}}\,,

(53)

Moreover, if the unitaries in (
46
)
are such that
\Tr({U_{j}^\dagger}{U_{k}})=d
,

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)=\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta,\mathcal{F})=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}H\big(\pi_{[1,n]}\big)=\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}\,,

(54)

that is, the ALF entropy of the system is equal to the chain entropy rate.

The proof is reported in Appendix
E
.

Remark 9
.

Suppose that
\Tr({U_{j}^\dagger}{U_{k}})=d
. As shown in Remark
7
,

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)=\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}=S(\rho_{E}^{(1)})-\mathfrak{I}_{\omega_{E}}\,.

If the environment is a Bernoulli source, one has simply

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)=S(\rho_{E}^{(1)})=H(\pi_{\{1\}})\,.

Note also that

\ket{\sqrt{\rho_S}}=\ket{\sqrt{\mathds{1}_d/d}}=d^{-1/2}\sum_{i}\ket{i\otimes i}=:\ket{\psiplus{d}}
, namely the fully symmetric maximally entangled state. Then,

\Lambda_{1}^{\ddagger}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{d}\left[\outerproduct{\psiplus{d}}{\psiplus{d}}\right]=\sum_{i}p_{i}\,U_{i}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}\outerproduct{\psiplus{d}}{\psiplus{d}}U_{i}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}.

Since
U_{i}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}\ket{\psiplus{d}}
,
i=1,\dots,d^{2}
form a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
, the von Neumann entropy of the latter state amounts to
H(\pi_{\{1\}})=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}
and thus, consistently, equals to
\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)
since QR holds (compare Corollary
1
).

IV.3
Markov chain environment

We now let the classical environment to be a stationary Markov chain; namely, we choose the probabilities
\pi_{[1,n]}=\big\{p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\big\}_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}
to form a stationary Markov process,

p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}=\prod_{k=2}^{n}\,T_{i_{k}\,i_{k-1}}\ p_{i_{1}}\,,

(55)

defined by the stochastic matrix

T=[T_{ij}]
,
T_{ij}\geq 0
, with

\sum_{i}T_{ij}=1
,
\sum_{j}T_{ij}\,p_{j}=p_{i}
. The mean entropy rate of the Markov source is equal to the two-site conditional entropy of the
chain
Walters 1982
,

\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}=-\sum_{ij}p_{j}\,T_{ij}\log(T_{ij})=H(\pi_{1})-I(\pi_{1};\pi_{2})\ ,

(56)

where

I(\pi_{1};\pi_{2})=H({\pi_{1}})+H(\pi_{2})\,-\,H(\pi_{[1,2]})\,,

(57)

denotes the mutual information between the first two subsequent sites of the classical chain. Because of stationarity, the latter quantity does not depend on where the chosen pair is located along the chain and measures the correlations between any two subsequent sites. Then, one sees that the entropy rate decreases with increasing correlations between subsequent sites.
In the following, we construct a process with
U_{k}
as in Remark
9
and for which
T_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
, so that

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)=\mathfrak{S}_{\omega_{E}}=0
as for reversible time-evolutions.

Qubit Pauli dynamics.

We now let the open quantum system
S
to be a qubit (
d=2
), we couple it to a Markov chain with
4\times 4
diagonal matrices at each site and choose the
local probability distributions to be generated by the following
4\times 4
stochastic matrix

\displaystyle T=\begin{pmatrix}p_{0}&p_{0}&p_{0}&p_{0}\\
p&p+\Delta&p-\Delta&p\\
p&p-\Delta&p+\Delta&p\\
r&r&r&r\\
\end{pmatrix},

(58)

\displaystyle 0\leq\Delta\leq p\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,\quad p_{0}+2p+r=1\,,

with invariant probability vector
\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{0},p,p,r)
,
guaranteeing stationarity
T\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{p}
. Let the coupling be as in (
46
) with

\phi_{k}[X]=\sigma_{k}\,X\,\sigma_{k}\,,\qquad k=0,\dots,3\,,

(59)

\sigma_{0}=\mathds{1}_{2}
and
\sigma_{j},j=1,2,3
being the Pauli matrices so that the reduced dynamics corresponds to a Pauli map

\Lambda_{n}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}\,\phi_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}=\Lambda_{n}^{\ddagger}\,,\qquad\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}=\phi_{k_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{k_{n}}\,.

(60)

The latter can be explicitly computed for the Markov chain generated by (
58
). Indeed, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (
60
) is diagonal in the Pauli basis,

\Lambda_{n}[\sigma_{\alpha}]=\lambda_{n}^{(\alpha)}\sigma_{\alpha}\,,\qquad\alpha=0,\dots,3\,,

(61)

where
\lambda_{n}^{(0)}=1
follows from the preservation of unity and the other eigenvalues evolving according to recursive equations,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{0}+p(\mu_{1}^{(\alpha)}+\mu_{2}^{(\alpha)})+r\mu_{3}^{(\alpha)}\right)\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{n-1}

(62)

\displaystyle+\Delta\,p\left(\mu_{1}^{(\alpha)}-\mu_{2}^{(\alpha)}\right)^{2}\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{n-2}\,,\quad\alpha=0,\dots,3\,,

where
\phi_{\beta}[\sigma_{\alpha}]=\mu_{\beta}^{(\alpha)}\sigma_{\alpha}
,
\mu_{0}^{(\alpha)}=\mu_{\beta}^{(0)}=1
and
\mu_{\beta}^{(\alpha)}=-1
for
\beta\neq\alpha\in\{1,2,3\}
. Accordingly, (
62
) are rewritten as

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(1,2)}

\displaystyle=\left(1-2(p+r)\right)\ \lambda_{n-1}^{(1,2)}+4\,p\,\Delta\ \lambda_{n-2}^{(1,2)}\,,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\,(1-4p)\ \lambda_{n-1}^{(3)}\,,

which are easily solvable, yielding

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{n}^{(1,2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{B_{p,r,\Delta}+A_{p,r}}{2\,B_{p,r,\Delta}}\right)\left(\frac{A_{p,r}+B_{p,r,\Delta}}{2}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{B_{p,r,\Delta}-A_{p,r}}{2\,B_{p,r,\Delta}}\right)\left(\frac{A_{p,r}-B_{p,r,\Delta}}{2}\right)^{n},

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=(1-4p)^{n}\,,

(63)

where we set
A_{p,r}=1-2(p+r)
and
B_{p,r,\Delta}=\sqrt{A_{p,r}^{2}+16\,p\,\Delta}
.
In
Benatti and Nichele 2025
, it was proved that the divisibility features of the one-qubit dynamical map
\Lambda_{n}
are
controlled by the parameter
\Delta
as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}\;\textrm{is CP-divisible iff}

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{A_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2p}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}\otimes\Lambda_{n}\;\textrm{is P-divisible iff}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\frac{\Delta}{A_{p,r}}\leq\frac{r}{2p}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4p(1-2p)}}{4p}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}\;\textrm{is P-divisible iff}

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{A_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2p}+\frac{1}{2}\,.

(64)

Hence, by tuning
\Delta
and keeping fixed the other parameters, conditions (
64
) establish that one can change the divisibility degree of the dynamics by increasing the correlations of the environment.
Indeed, by increasing
\Delta

\Lambda_{n}\otimes\Lambda_{n}

and finally the P-divisibility of the one qubit dynamics

and thus the possibility that the trace-distances of suitable states increase from time-step

to time-step

Furthermore, the map

so that
\mathbb{T}_{n}=\mathbb{T}_{n}^{\ddagger}

The purification of

corresponds to the maximally entangled state

\ket{\psi_+^{{[1,n]}}}=2^{-n/2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{[1,n]}}\ket{\bs{k}_{[1,n]}\otimes\bs{k}_{[1,n]}}\,.

Set then

\,p_{\boldsymbol{i}_{[1,n]}}
and associated eigenvectors

With

parametrized as in (
58

\mathfrak{h}_{S}(\Theta)=\eta(p_{0})+\eta(r)\\
+2\left(2p\,\frac{\eta(p+\Delta)+\eta(p-\Delta)}{2}+(1-2p)\eta(p)\right),

(66)

where

,
\eta(0):=0
.
The associated two-site mutual information reads

\displaystyle I(\pi_{1};\pi_{2})

\displaystyle=2p\Big(2\eta(p)-\eta(p+\Delta)-\eta(p-\Delta)\Big)

\displaystyle=4p^{2}\left(\log 2-h\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2p}\right)\right),

(67)

where

,
0\leq x\leq 1
is the Shannon binary entropy, which is a decreasing function of
x
for

. Therefore, the two-site correlations increase with

\Delta
.
Consider

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\,&n\ \mathrm{odd}\,,\end{cases}\Lambda_{1}=\frac{\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}}{2}\,.

V
ALF entropy: operational interpretation

We now discuss a natural physical interpretation of the ALF entropy provided by the so-called GNS representation and
apply it to the collisional model of Section
IV
.

Given an operator algebra
\mathcal{A}
equipped with a state
\omega
, the familiar Hilbert space setting of quantum mechanics
emerges through the approach developed by Gelfand, Neimark and Segal (GNS construction). It consists in associating to the pair

(\mathcal{A},\omega)
a triple
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\ket{\Omega_\omega})
consisting of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
, a representation
\pi_{\omega}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})
of observables as bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
and a state vector
\ket{\Omega_\omega}
enjoying the cyclic property, i.e. such that the subspace

\pi_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\ket{\Omega_\omega}:=\{\pi_{\omega}(X)\ket{\Omega_\omega},\ X\in\mathcal{A}\}\,.

is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. The expectation value of any observable
X\in\mathcal{A}
can be then expressed as

\omega(X)=\innerproduct{\Omega_\omega}{\pi_\omega(X)|\Omega_\omega},\quad X\in\mathcal{A}\,.

(74)

The GNS triple
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\ket{\Omega_\omega})
is unique up to a unitary equivalence. From this fact, one deduces that
every automorphism
\Theta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
that leaves
\omega
invariant, namely such that
\omega\circ\Theta=\omega
, can be implemented by a unitary operator

\mathbb{U}_{\omega}[\pi_{\omega}(X)]:=U^{\dagger}_{\omega}\,\pi_{\omega}(X)\,U_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}(\Theta(X))\,,\quad X\in\mathcal{A}\,.

(75)

where
U_{\omega}^{\dagger}
is defined on the dense subspace
\pi_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\ket{\Omega_\omega}
as

U_{\omega}^{\dagger}\,\pi_{\omega}(X)\ket{\Omega_\omega}:=\pi_{\omega}\circ\Theta(X)\ket{\Omega_\omega}\,,\quad U_{\omega}^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega_\omega}=\ket{\Omega_\omega}\,.

(76)

Example 1
.

Finite
d
-level systems are described by the
C^{*}
-algebra of
d\times d
matrices
X\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
(endowed with the matrix norm) and by states given by density matrices

\omega(X)=\Tr(\rho\,X)\,,\qquad M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\,\ni\,\rho\geq 0\,,\ \Tr(\rho)=1\,.

(77)

For faithful density matrices
\rho=\sum_{\alpha}r_{\alpha}\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}
,
0<r_{\alpha}<1
, the GNS construction is achieved by state purification. Indeed, through the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product
\Tr(X^\dagger Y)=\innerproduct{X}{Y}
operators
X\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
are readily identified as vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then, one introduces the following representations of
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
:

\pi(A)\ket{B}=\ket{AB}\,,\qquad\pi^{\prime}(A)\ket{B}=\ket{BA}\ .

(78)

Then, by vectorizing matrices
A=\sum_{\alpha\beta}A_{\alpha\beta}\outerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
as
\ket{A}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}A_{\alpha\beta}\ket{\alpha\otimes\beta}
the above ones become factor representations:

\pi(A)=A\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}\,,\qquad\pi^{\prime}(A)=\mathds{1}_{d}\otimes\overline{A}\,,\qquad A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\,,

(79)

where
\innerproduct*{\alpha}{\overline{A}|\beta}\equiv\overline{\innerproduct{\alpha}{A|\beta}}
.
Then, for faithful density matrices
\rho>0
,

\Tr(\rho\, A)=\Tr(\sqrt{\rho}\,A\,\sqrt{\rho})=\bra{\sqrt{\rho}}{\pi(A)}\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}\ ,

(80)

where the purification of
\rho
,

\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{r_{\alpha}}\ket{\alpha\otimes\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\,,

(81)

is a cyclic vector for
\pi(\mathcal{A})
, which is also separating, namely,
\Tr(\rho\,X^\dag X)=0
if and only if
X=0
, thus, equivalently, cyclic for the commutant
\pi^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})
.
Let the automorphism of
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
that leaves
\rho
invariant be
\Theta(A)=V^{\dagger}AV
with dual map
\Theta^{\ddagger}
such that

\Theta^{\ddagger}[\rho]=V\rho\,V^{\dagger}=\rho
.
Then its GNS implementation reads

U_{\rho}=V\otimes\overline{V}
.
Indeed, as
V
commutes with
\rho
,

\displaystyle U_{\rho}^{\dagger}\pi_{\rho}(A)\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{r_{\alpha}}\,V^{\dagger}\,A\,\ket{\alpha}\otimes\,\overline{V}^{\dagger}\ket{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{r_{\alpha}}\,V^{\dagger}A\ket{\alpha}\bra{\alpha}V\ket{\beta}\otimes\ket{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta}V^{\dagger}A\sqrt{\rho}\,V\ket{\beta}\otimes\ket{\beta}=V^{\dagger}\,A\,V\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\rho}(\Theta(A))\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}\,.

Setting
A=\mathds{1}_{d}
yields
U_{\rho}^{\dagger}\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}=U_{\rho}\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}=\ket{\sqrt{\rho}}
. As a concrete instance, let
V=e^{iH}
with

H=\sum h_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}\bra{\alpha}
, that is
H
and
V
are diagonal in the eigenbasis
\{\ket{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
of any fixed faithful
\rho
.
Then,
\overline{V}=V^{\dagger}
.

If a time-invariant state
\omega\circ\Theta=\omega
is considered, the physical interpretation of the ALF entropy
(
27
)
is neatly obtained in the GNS representation as an alternating sequence of measurement processes and reversible one-step dynamics. This possibility already emerged in Remark
2
; however, there the interpretation is only valid for the diagonal entries of
\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]
.
One indeed proves that the von Neumann entropy of the coarse-grained density matrix can equivalently be computed, in the GNS representation, as
Alicki 2002
;
Alicki and Fannes 2001
,

S\left(\rho\big[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\big]\right)=S\left(\left(\mathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\ddagger}\circ\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\ddagger}\right)^{n}[\outerproduct{\Omega_\omega}{\Omega_\omega}]\right),

(82)

where
\mathbb{U}_{\omega}[\pi_{\omega}(X)]=U_{\omega}^{\dagger}\pi_{\omega}(X)U_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}(\Theta[X])
is the unitary implementation of the dynamics in the GNS representation and, for any given POVM
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}[A]:=\sum_{a}\pi_{\omega}(X_{a})^{\dagger}\,A\,\pi_{\omega}(X_{a})\,,\qquad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})\ ,

(83)

while
\mathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\ddagger}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\ddagger}
are the dual maps of
\mathbb{U}_{\omega}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}
in the Schrödinger picture.
The derivation of (
82
) is reported in Appendix
F
and its structure supports an interpretation of the ALF entropy similar to that of the classical KS entropy that is as the maximal information per time-step extracted from repeated measurements on the system intertwined with the time evolution. Only, measurements and dynamics do not commute in a quantum context.

Remark 13
.

The previous discussion allows to better interpret the results of Section
IV.3
, where the open-system dynamical entropy was found to be a monotonically decreasing function of the bath correlations
\Delta

Remarkably, in Section

In the GNS approach to the ALF entropy, the measurements affect only
\pi_{S}
and not the commutant
\pi_{S}^{\prime}
,
while the dynamics affects non-trivially also the commutant, as emerges from (

\displaystyle\bra{\sqrt{\rho_S}\otimes\Omega_E}\pi_{S}\otimes\pi_{E}(X^{\dagger}\otimes\mathds{1}_{E})\left(\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E}\right)\circ\mathbb{U}_{\Theta}\right)^{n}\left[\pi_{S}\otimes\pi_{E}(A\otimes\mathds{1}_{E})\pi_{S}^{\prime}\otimes\pi_{E}(B\otimes\mathds{1}_{E})\right]\,\pi_{S}\otimes\pi_{E}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{E})\ket{\sqrt{\rho_S}\otimes\Omega_E}

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt=\Tr(\Gamma_n^{\mathbb{X}}

where we introduced a CPTP map

on

\pi_{S}
and
\pi_{S}^{\prime}

\pi_{S}
and
\pi_{S}^{\prime}

(see Example

The factorization of the
S+E

\left(\mathbb{U}_{\Theta}^{\ddagger}\circ\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}^{\ddagger}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E}\right)\right)^{n}

\Gamma_{n}^{\mathbb{X}}
.
We expect that such dissipative bipartite dynamics could help in assessing the lack of Markovianity of the stochastic process, for example by revealing memory effects that cannot be simply ascertained through the reduced dynamics
\Lambda_{n}^{\ddagger}

\rho_{S}=\mathds{1}/{d}

The tensor shift on the chain is implemented by the unitary operator defined by

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}^{\dagger}\,\pi_{E}\left(\Pi_{\boldsymbol{i}^{[a,b]}}^{[a,b]}\right)\ket{\Omega_E}:

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega_E}

\displaystyle U_{\Theta}^{\dagger}\,\pi_{S}(A)\pi_{S}^{\prime}(B)\otimes\pi_{E}\left(\Pi_{\boldsymbol{i}^{[a,b]}}^{[a,b]}\right)\ket{\psiplus{d}\otimes\Omega_E}

Pauli GNS reduced dynamics

We now consider the environment to be a classical Markov chain, as in Section

Corollary 3
.

Let
\Delta
be such that

is P-divisible but not CP-divisible. Then, the evolution of the system does not display back-flow of information in terms of revivals. On the other hand, as the two-qubit dilation

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have extended the ALF-entropy which has mainly been applied in reversible, quantum many-body settings, to dissipative finite dimensional
quantum systems and named
open-system ALF entropy
.
The analytical properties that characterize it, in particular its decreasing in the most general non-Markovian settings with respect to the Markovian ones, makes it a
suitable witness of information flowing from the environment back into the open quantum system.

In particular, we have explicitly computed the open-system ALF entropy for a finite-level quantum system coupled to a classical stationary chain: the entropy rate equals the mean entropy of the environment. This result has a simple but clear information-theoretic interpretation: the ALF entropy measures the average amount of information that can be extracted by probing the open system alone. Such an information rate decreases as the environment becomes more correlated.
In the qubit case, this physical effect has been compared to the memory effects present in the qubit reduced dynamics, which are likewise governed by the strength of the environmental correlations.

The ALF entropy, obtained from the coarse-grained matrix
\rho_{S}[\mathcal{X}^{(n)}]
built from the system multi-time correlation functions, is neatly interpreted when going to the GNS representation which involves a purifying ancillary system. Accordingly, we studied the GNS reduced dynamics obtained by tracing away the environment for the model of Section
IV
. Interestingly, the GNS dilation is affected by memory effects that are seen by the reduced dynamics. In particular, it can show super-activation of back-flow of information in terms of revivals, as it typically occurs when a tensor product dilation of the reduced dynamics is considered
Benatti et al. 2017
;
Benatti and Nichele 2023
;
Benatti and Nichele 2025
.

In a more general scenario of a system coupled to a quantum spin chain, the above interpretation is supported by an upper bound to the ALF entropy of the open system by the mean entropy of the environment (plus a quantum correction). For a stationary chain, the more correlated it is, the tighter is such bound. This is only a partial result, a thorough study of the ALF entropy in the case of a genuinely quantum environment still awaiting to be developed.

Appendix F
ALF entropy in the GNS representation.

F.1
Measurement model and entropy exchange.

Consider a POVM on a
d
-level quantum system,

\mathcal{X}\,=\,\left\{X_{a}\right\}_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}\;\subseteq\;M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\,,

(F1)

and the associated CPTP map

\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho]=\sum_{a}X_{a}\rho X_{a}^{\dagger}
.
To a POVM
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{a}\}_{a}
made of orthogonal projections,
P_{a}P_{b}=\delta_{a,b}P_{a}
, there corresponds a so-called projective (non-selective) measurement,

\mathbb{P}[Y]=\sum_{a}P_{a}YP_{a},\qquad\mathbb{P}\circ\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}\,,

(F2)

which is itself a projector on
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
. Measuring orthogonal projectors
P_{a}
can be reduced to distinguishing the positions of a pointer on a grade. Less straightforward is sorting out the different labels
a
in the case of measurements described by a generic POVM
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{a}\}_{a}
, whereby they must be read by measuring generically non-orthogonal components
X_{a}
. For this one needs first dilating to a larger Hilbert space over which performing again an orthogonal measurement. This can be seen by means of the following simple model
Nielsen and Chuang 2002
.
Let
\ket{0}
a fixed vector in
\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
and
define the isometry
V:\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\ket{0}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}
,

V\ket{\psi}\otimes\ket{0}=\sum_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}X_{a}\ket{\psi}\otimes\ket{a}\,.

\rho^{\prime}:=V\rho\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}V^{\dagger}=\sum_{a,b}X_{a}\rho X_{b}^{\dagger}\otimes\outerproduct{a}{b}\,,\ \Tr_{2}\rho^{\prime}=\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho]\,.

(F3)

The isometry
V
provides a Stinespring-like dilation of the map
\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}
and can be extended to a unitary on the whole
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
, so that (
F3
) can be seen as a joint evolution of system and measurement apparatus
Nielsen and Chuang 2002
.
Performing then a projective measurement
\mathbb{P}
, with
P_{a}=\ket{a}\bra{a}
, on the apparatus (which does not influence the state of the system) yields,

\mathrm{id}_{d}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho^{\prime}]=\sum_{a}X_{a}\rho X_{a}^{\dagger}\otimes\outerproduct{a}{a}.

(F4)

Thus, measuring the projectors
\mathds{1}\otimes\outerproduct{a}{a}
, the post-measurement state of the system reads

\rho_{a}^{\prime}=\frac{X_{a}\rho X_{a}^{\dagger}}{\Tr(\rho X_a^\dagger X_a)}\,,

with probability
p_{a}:=\Tr(\rho X_a^\dagger X_a)
. The non-selective version of the measurement corresponds indeed to the action of the CPTP map
\rho\mapsto\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho]=\Tr_{2}(\rho^{\prime})
.
Instead, tracing over the degrees of freedom of the
apparatus yields:

\rho_{2}^{\prime}=\Tr_{1}(\rho^{\prime})=\sum_{a,b=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}\Tr(\rho X_b^\dagger X_a )\outerproduct{a}{b}=:\rho[\mathcal{X}]\,.

(F5)

Its diagonal entries correspond to the outcome probabilities relative to the projective measurement performed on the apparatus

p_{a}
,
while the off-diagonal ones
are correlation terms contain further information about the apparatus. In fact, the von Neumann entropy of a density matrix is always less or equal to the Shannon entropy of its diagonal, so that

S\left(\rho[\mathcal{X}]\right)\ \leq\ H\left(\big\{p_{a}\big\}_{a=1}^{\absolutevalue{\mc{X}}}\right)\ ,

(F6)

the inequality being saturated when the POVM
\mathcal{X}
is made of orthogonal projections. The quantity
S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])
is sometimes called
entropy exchange

Schumacher 1996
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2002
. It represents the entropy increase in the apparatus, initially in the
pure state
\outerproduct{0}{0}
and thus at zero entropy, due to the measurement process that sends it into the mixed state
\rho[\mathcal{X}]
.
When the state
\rho
of the system is pure, so is the compound state
\rho^{\prime}
in (
F3
) emerging from the measuring unitary interaction; then, its two marginals
{\rm Tr}_{2}\rho^{\prime}=\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho]
(see (
F3
)) and
{\rm Tr}_{1}\rho^{\prime}=\rho[\mathcal{X}]

(see (
F5
)) have the same von Neumann entropy, whence the energy growth in the apparatus,
S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])
, equals the entropy growth in the system,
S(\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho])
, due to the non-selective measurement process
\rho\mapsto\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho]
.
We now show, following
Lindblad 1973
, that the entropy gained by the measurement apparatus is at least as large as the information gained during the measurement. Recalling the definition of quantum relative entropy
S(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr(\rho\,(\log\rho-\log\sigma))
, consider

S(\rho^{\prime}\|\rho_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\prime})=S(\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho])+S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])-S(\rho)\,,

(F7)

where we used the fact that, due to (
F3
),
S(\rho^{\prime})=S(\rho)
.
Further, consider the action of the full decoherence map
\mathbb{P}
on the second party,

\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho^{\prime}]=\sum_{a=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|}\,p_{a}\,\rho_{a}^{\prime}\otimes\outerproduct{a}{a},\qquad\rho_{a}^{\prime}=\frac{X_{a}\rho X_{a}^{\dagger}}{p_{a}}\,,

(F8)

so that

S(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho^{\prime}]\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{2}])=S(\mathbb{X}^{\ddagger}[\rho])-\sum_{a}p_{a}S(\rho_{a}^{\prime})\,.

(F9)

Hence, due to the monotonicity of the relative entropy under CPTP maps, the difference

S\left(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho^{\prime}]\middle\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{P}[\rho_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\prime}]\right)-S\left(\rho^{\prime}\middle\|\rho_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\prime}\right)\\
=S(\rho)-\sum_{a}p_{a}S(\rho_{a}^{\prime})-S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])\leq 0\,,

or, equivalently,

S(\rho)-\sum_{a}p_{a}S(\rho_{a}^{\prime})\,\leq\,S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])\,.

(F10)

On the left-hand side, one has the average information gain about the state
\rho
when the measurement associated with
\mathcal{X}
is performed. Hence, the entropy exchange
S(\rho[\mathcal{X}])
provides a universal upper bound for the average information gain.

Appendix H
Two-qubit Pauli maps.

A one qubit Pauli map has a spectral decomposition

\Lambda[X]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}\lambda^{(\alpha)}\Tr(\sigma_\alpha X)\sigma_{\alpha}\,,

(H1)

and can be equivalently rewritten as

\Lambda[X]=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}q^{(\alpha)}\sigma_{\alpha}X\sigma_{\alpha}\,,\qquad\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}{q}^{(\alpha)}=1\,.

(H2)

The spectrum
\lambda^{(\alpha)}
and the coefficients
q^{(\alpha)}
are related by a linear transformation

{q}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta=0}^{3}H_{\alpha\beta}\lambda^{(\beta)}\,,

(H3)

given by an Hadamard matrix
H
,
H_{\alpha\beta}=\Tr(\sigma_\alpha\sigma_\beta\sigma_\alpha\sigma_\beta)
. Complete positivity of
\Lambda
is equivalent to
q^{(\alpha)}\geq 0
. Similarly, a two-qubit Pauli map is defined by its spectral decomposition

\Gamma[X]=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta=0}^{3}\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}\Tr(\sigma_\alpha\otimes\sigma_\beta X)\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\beta}\,,

(H4)

and can be recast in the form

\Gamma[X]=\sum_{\alpha\beta=0}^{3}q^{(\alpha,\beta)}\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\beta}X\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\beta}\,,\quad\sum_{\alpha\beta=0}^{3}q^{(\alpha,\beta)}=1\,,

(H5)

where the matrices describing the spectrum
G:=[\gamma^{(\alpha,\beta)}]
, respectively the coefficients
Q:=[q^{(\alpha,\beta)}]
are similar and related through the Hadamard transformation,

Q=\frac{H\,G\,H}{16}\,.

(H6)

Suppose now that the spectrum has the special form

G=\begin{pmatrix}[c]1&\lambda&\lambda&\lambda^{(3)}\\
\lambda&1&\lambda^{(3)}&\lambda\\
\lambda&\lambda^{(3)}&1&\lambda\\
\lambda^{(3)}&\lambda&\lambda&1\\
\end{pmatrix},

(H7)

Then, using (
H6
), the matrix coefficients is diagonal,

Q=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}[c]1+\lambda^{(3)}+2\lambda&0&0&0\\
0&1-\lambda^{(3)}&0&0\\
0&0&1-\lambda^{(3)}&0\\
0&0&0&1+\lambda^{(3)}-2\lambda\\
\end{pmatrix},

(H8)

whence (
H5
) has the form

\Gamma[X]=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}q^{(\alpha,\alpha)}\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha}X\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha}\,.
