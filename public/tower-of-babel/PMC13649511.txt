The changing etiology of liver cancer: A comparative study of global and Chinese burden trends from hepatitis dominance to metabolic risks (1990–2021) and forecasts to 2035
Authors: Xue Zhou, Mingle Huang, Xiaofen Li, Minjie Zhao, Moushen Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and aimsLiver cancer remains a major global health burden with a complex and shifting epidemiology. While viral hepatitis continues to drive significant morbidity, metabolic risk factors are increasingly contributing to the incidence of liver cancer. Utilizing data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2021, this analysis examines China’s incidence, prevalence, and age-standardized disability-adjusted life year (DALY) rates for liver cancer from 1990 to 2021, comparing these trajectories against global and regional patterns. This study further analyzed the correlations between liver cancer burden and socioeconomic development, aiming to provide evidence for targeted intervention strategies and health policy formulation.MethodsThis study utilized data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 to analyze the burden of liver cancer from 1990 to 2021 across 204 countries and territories. Standardized methodologies were used to estimate prevalence, incidence and DALYs. The Socio-demographic index (SDI) was applied to evaluate Socioeconomic disparities. Joinpoint regression was employed to analyze temporal trends, and age-period-cohort modeling was applied to estimate drivers of disease burden. Age-standardized rates were projected to 2035 using statistical modeling techniques.ResultsGlobally, the absolute number of liver cancer cases increased continuously, whereas age-standardized rates declined from 1990 to 2021. Prevalent cases increased by 113.7% (from 345,912.7 to 739,299.5), incident cases increased by 116.2% (from 244,689.4 to 529,202.5), and total DALYs grew by 70.6% (from 7,553,666.8 to 12,887,652.4); the age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) declined with an estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) of −0.6 (95% CI –0.72 to −0.48). Hepatitis B-related DALYs decreased significantly, while non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) and alcohol-related cases increased. China achieved marked reductions in age-standardized rates: the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) decreased from 10.6 to 9.5 per 100,000 (EAPC –0.28, 95% CI –0.42 to −0.13), and the ASDR fell from 334.5 to 239.9 per 100,000 (EAPC –1.16, 95% CI –1.34 to −0.98), especially in hepatitis B, though metabolic-related burdens grew. Prevalent cases in China doubled (from 132,779.2 to 265,539.4) and incident cases rose by 104.0% (from 96,434.3 to 196,636.6). Gender and age disparities were evident, with males and older adults bearing higher burdens. High-income regions saw rising metabolic-related rates, whereas low- and middle-income areas faced persistent viral hepatitis burdens. Projections suggest a continued rise in metabolic-related liver cancer globally, with NASH-related rates accelerating fastest in China, underscoring the need for dual preventive strategies targeting both infection and lifestyle factors.ConclusionsThe global burden of liver cancer is undergoing an epidemiological transition, marked by declining viral hepatitis-related rates but rising metabolic- and alcohol-related cases. Significant reductions in age-standardized rates were observed in China, though absolute case number increased. Socioeconomic and geographic disparities persist, with high-income regions facing growing metabolic burdens. Future trends emphasize the need for integrated strategies addressing both infectious and lifestyle-related risk factors.

Introduction

Liver cancer constitutes a significant global public health burden, being the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide.1 Based on the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 results, the primary etiological factors for liver cancer include hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, alcohol consumption, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), and other causes.2,3 The prevalence and incidence of liver cancer are markedly elevated in developing regions of Asia and Africa. Eastern Asia exhibited the highest ASIR, followed by Northern Africa and South-Eastern Asia. Countries such as Mongolia, Egypt, and Laos faced the most severe burdens.3,4 China alone accounted for nearly 45% of global liver cancer cases and deaths.5

The GBD 2021 data reveal that the etiological landscape of liver cancer is undergoing a significant transition, especially in middle-income nations such as China.6 While HBV remains the dominant global driver of liver cancer disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), its age-standardized rates (ASRs) are declining globally.7,8 Conversely, liver cancer attributable to alcohol use and NASH shows alarming increases worldwide.9,10 China exemplifies this complex shift, where historically high HBV prevalence coexists with rapidly rising metabolic- and alcohol-related liver cancer risks.7,11 Socio-demographic index (SDI) stratification highlights this transition: Middle SDI regions, including China, exhibit declining HBV-related ASRs of liver cancer DALYs, alongside sharp rises in alcohol-related disease and NASH. High-middle SDI regions similarly show substantial NASH-related ASRs of liver cancer DALYs.12,13 This underscores an emerging “dual burden”, where persistent infectious causes intersect with escalating non-communicable drivers.

To address this evolving challenge, robust epidemiological intelligence is essential. The GBD study provides the most comprehensive framework for quantifying disease burden, employing DisMod-MR 2.1, a Bayesian meta-regression tool, within a systematic cascade approach.14 This methodology models incidence, prevalence, remission, and mortality with internal consistency, utilizing Meta-Regression-Bayesian, Regularized, Trimmed (MR-BRT) techniques to ensure cross-study comparability.15 Crucially, it explicitly contrasts with China’s epidemiological transition against global patterns, providing evidence critical for developing targeted national strategies for liver cancer prevention, screening, and treatment in an era of shifting etiological dominance.

Methods

Ethical approval

The GBD dataset is publicly available and fully anonymized. This study conducted a secondary analysis of the global burden of diseases, injuries, and risk factors based on GBD data. This study was reviewed by the Research Ethics Committee of the Women and Children’s Medical Center of Guangzhou Medical University (Approval No. 2026336A01, approval date: June 9, 2026), which determined that ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Data sources and definitions

The GBD 2021 dataset provides estimates of incidence, prevalence, mortality, years of life lost (YLLs), years lived with disability (YLDs), and DALYs for 371 diseases and injuries in 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2021.15,16 Data on liver cancer burden in China and globally from 1990 to 2021 were extracted via the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx). Detailed methodological descriptions are provided in the GBD 2021 capstone publications.14, 15, 16

The socio-demographic index (SDI)

The SDI, developed within the Global Burden of Disease framework, is a composite indicator of population development status that is associated with health outcomes. It is calculated as the geometric mean of three normalized components (each scaled 0–1): the total fertility rate among women under 25 (TFU25), average years of education among individuals aged 15 and older (EDU15+), and lag-distributed income (LDI) per capita.14,17 Theoretically, an SDI value of 0 indicates the lowest level of development relevant to health, whereas a value of 1 reflects the highest. In GBD 2021, each of the 204 countries and territories was categorized based on its respective SDI value.15

Statistical analysis

In this study, the Estimated Annual Percentage Change (EAPC) was employed to quantify temporal trends in ASRs of liver cancer prevalence, incidence, and DALYs.18 The 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) were demonstrated for each estimated quantity. Segmented regression analysis was performed using Joinpoint software (version 5.1.0.0) to quantify temporal trends in incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates for liver cancer in China and globally between 1990 and 2021.19 This method employs Lerman’s grid search algorithm to detect significant joinpoints where trend slopes change significantly. Statistical significance was evaluated using permutation tests with Monte Carlo simulation and Bonferroni correction.20 The annual percent change (APC) for each segment was calculated as the geometric mean.21 Both indices were calculated using the World Health Organization’s Health Equity Assessment Toolkit (HEAT), implemented in R software (version 4.5.1).

Projections of liver cancer prevalence through 2035 were generated using a multilevel logit-transformed model based on GBD 2021 data:E[logit(Yl,a,s,y)]=β1SDIl,y+αl,a,s

In the model, Y denotes the location-(l), age-(a), and sex-(s) specific prevalence in year (y), β1 represents the fixed coefficient for the SDI trajectory, and αl,a,s constitutes the random intercepts. Projections of SDI incorporated recency-weighted historical trends.14 The model was calibrated by benchmarking 2021 forecasts against empirical GBD estimates, followed by extrapolation to 2035.

Results

Prevalence, incidence and DALYs of the burden of liver cancer in China and worldwide

Between 1990 and 2021, the global absolute burden of liver cancer demonstrated a substantial increase. Prevalent cases rose by 113.7%, from 345,912.7 (95% UI 299,826.7–376,632.3) to 739,299.5 (673,114.2–821,948.2), while incident cases increased by 116.2%, from 244,689.4 (224,795.5–268,549.0) to 529,202.5 (480,339.5–593,849.1). Total DALYs grew by 70.6%, reaching 12,887,652.4 (11,673,532.6–14,472,228.0) in 2021 compared to 7,553,666.8 (6,897,511.3–8,296,181.5) in 1990. Despite these absolute surges, global age-standardized rates remained relatively stable: the age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR) moved from 7.8 (6.9–8.4) to 8.7 (7.9–9.7) per 100,000 (EAPC 0.30 [95% CI 0.15 to 0.44]), the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) remained largely unchanged (EAPC 0.03 [–0.08 to 0.15]), and the age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) exhibited a modest decline (EAPC –0.60 [–0.72 to −0.48]) (Table 1, Fig. 1). China exhibited a distinct epidemiological divergence. Although absolute burden increased—with prevalent cases rising by 100.0% from 132,779.2 (108,924.1–155,563.9) to 265,539.4 (212,435.0–331,148.9) and incident cases rising by 104.0%–196,636.6 (158,273.1–243,557.7)—significant declines or stability were observed in age-standardized metrics. China’s ASIR declined from 10.6 (8.9–12.4) to 9.5 (7.7–11.8) per 100,000 (EAPC –0.28 [–0.42 to −0.13]), while the ASDR fell significantly from 334.5 (281.1–393.1) to 239.9 (192.0–299.4) per 100,000 Discussion

The global epidemiological landscape of liver cancer reflects a complex transition shaped by demographic changes and public health interventions.22 Our analysis of GBD 2021 data also indicates that while absolute cases of liver cancer and its prevalence, incidence, and DALYs have increased substantially over the past two decades—largely attributable to population growth and aging—age-standardized rates have shown encouraging declines.6,23 Geographical disparities in liver cancer burden reflect distinct stages of epidemiological transition. Regions with historically high viral hepatitis prevalence, including East Asia and sub-Saharan Africa, have demonstrated declining trends, suggesting successful implementation of vaccination programs and antiviral therapies.24, 25, 26 Conversely, high-income regions such as North America and Western Europe exhibit rising burdens despite advanced healthcare systems, indicating the growing influence of metabolic and lifestyle-related risk factors.27,28

Distinct etiological patterns were observed across socioeconomic strata. High-income regions demonstrated a characteristic predominance of metabolic and alcohol-related etiologies, while low- and middle-income regions showed persistent dominance of viral hepatitis-related burden. Our analysis revealed strong positive correlations between regional development levels and proportional contributions of NASH and alcohol-related liver cancer. Conversely, HBV-related burden exhibited an inverse relationship with socioeconomic development. While viral hepatitis infections (HBV and HCV) remain the predominant etiology globally, accounting for approximately 65% of cases, their proportional contribution is decreasing.29,30 Meanwhile, NASH- and alcohol-related liver cancer have shown the most rapid growth since 2000, particularly in high- and middle-SDI countries.9,31 This transition necessitates a re-evaluation of prevention strategies, as the traditional focus on infectious causes must now be complemented with interventions addressing metabolic risk factors and alcohol consumption.32

Age-specific patterns reveal particularly concerning trends in older populations, especially in Western countries, with metabolic factors becoming increasingly predominant in this age group.33 The global male-to-female disparity shows significant regional variations, with notably lower ratios in low-SDI regions and divergent gender-specific trends across countries.34,35 A distinct age-dependent etiological transition was observed, where NASH-related cases demonstrate a significantly increased proportional representation in advanced age groups, particularly those over 80 years. While prevalence, incidence, and DALYs exhibit highly consistent global distribution patterns, DALYs reveal a disproportionately higher burden in younger cohorts compared to prevalence, reflecting the metric’s sensitivity to premature mortality and years of life lost. These findings identify middle-aged male populations as the primary priority for viral hepatitis screening and treatment. The intersection of these patterns suggests that global control strategies must address a complex nexus of biological susceptibility, regional healthcare access, and sociocultural determinants through age-tailored interventions—prioritizing viral eradication in the workforce and metabolic management in aging populations.

This pattern appears driven by two primary factors: the potential resurgence of viral hepatitis-related cases in certain populations and the continuing rise of metabolic-related liver cancer against a backdrop of slowing population growth.36,37 The convergence of these trends suggests that without intensified intervention efforts, the gains achieved in viral hepatitis control may be offset by the growing burden of metabolic-related liver cancer.

Longitudinal projections through 2035 reveal a concerning U-shaped trajectory: an initial period of decline followed by a projected resurgence in rates across multiple etiological subgroups and regions. The significant declining trajectories of hepatitis B and C-related ASDR reflect the success of expanded vaccination coverage and potent antiviral therapies. In stark contrast, the rising burden of NASH-related liver cancer underscores the intensifying impact of the global obesity and metabolic syndrome epidemic. Meanwhile, the alcohol-related burden remains persistently stable, suggesting that current public health measures have yet to effectively curb mortality from alcohol consumption. China’s marked reduction in hepatitis-related liver cancer burden is largely attributable to its rigorous implementation of infant HBV vaccination since 1992, achieving over 90% coverage, coupled with progressive expansion of antiviral therapy reimbursement under national health insurance.38,39 Concurrently, rapid economic growth and urbanization have driven shifts toward obesogenic lifestyles, characterized by increased consumption of processed foods and reduced physical activity. This nutritional transition, alongside an aging population, has accelerated metabolic syndrome prevalence, thereby elevating NASH-related liver cancer risk.40,41 The coexistence of declining viral hepatitis and rising metabolic etiologies underscores an evolving epidemiological transition, necessitating dual-strategy interventions that sustain vaccination and antiviral programs while integrating public health measures targeting weight management, diabetes prevention, and alcohol consumption. Future policies must address these dual burdens through integrated primary care strategies.42 The protective effects of dietary modifications such as Mediterranean-style diets and physical activity promotion offer promising avenues for reducing metabolic-related liver cancer burden.43,44 Future research should focus on understanding the complex interactions between multiple risk factors and developing targeted interventions for high-risk populations.

This study has several limitations inherent to its reliance on the GBD 2021 dataset. The findings are contingent on the accuracy and completeness of the original data sources, which may vary across regions due to differences in diagnostic practices, reporting systems, and healthcare infrastructure.45 Methodological constraints include the aggregation of diverse liver cancer subtypes under broader categories, which may obscure important etiological distinctions. Additionally, the analysis could not fully account for contextual factors such as regional variations in healthcare access, screening protocols, or specific policy interventions.46 Despite these limitations, the consistent patterns observed across multiple metrics support the robustness of the primary findings. Future refinements to the GBD framework—including more granular subphenotype classification and integration of local health system data—would enhance the precision of burden estimates and better capture national and regional epidemiological nuances.

Conclusions

The global burden of liver cancer demonstrates a complex epidemiological transition, characterized by declining age-standardized rates amid rising absolute cases—driven by population growth and aging—while revealing significant geographical and etiological heterogeneity. Viral hepatitis (HBV/HCV) remains dominant but is decreasing, while NASH and alcohol-related etiologies surge, especially in high-income regions. Future projections indicate a concerning U-shaped trajectory post-2035, driven by metabolic trends and potential viral resurgence. Addressing this dual burden demands integrated strategies: sustaining viral hepatitis control through vaccination and antiviral therapies, while implementing population-level interventions targeting obesity, alcohol use, and promoting healthy diets and active lifestyles to mitigate emerging metabolic risks.
