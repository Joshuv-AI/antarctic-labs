Fredholm Criteria for G G -pseudodifferential Operators Thanks: Key words: Fredholm operator, C ∗ C^{*} -algebra, pseudodifferential operator, group actions. AMS Subject classification: 47A53, 58J40, 47L80, 46N20.
Alexandre Baldare, Anton Yu. Savin, Elmar Schrohe

Abstract
. Let G G be a compact Lie group that acts smoothly on a closed manifold M M . Using a general Simonenko principle, we derive a novel criterion for the Fredholm property of G G -pseudodifferential operators acting on Sobolev spaces of sections of vector bundles over M M . In case the group is finite, we obtain a further characterization of the Fredholm property of G G -pseudodifferential operators in terms of the invertibility of suitable symbols.



Introduction

This paper is devoted to the investigation of Fredholm criteria for a certain class of operators on closed manifolds. More precisely, let
G
be a compact Lie group which acts smoothly on a compact manifold
M
without boundary. There is no loss of generality in assuming that
M
is Riemannian and that
G
acts on
M
by isometries. Let
E^{\pm}\rightarrow M
be
G
-equivariant vector bundles equipped with
G
-invariant Hermitian metrics. We are interested in so-called
G
-pseudodifferential operators, i.e., operators of the form

(1)

P+\mathcal{D}:H^{s}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}),\quad(P+\mathcal{D})u=Pu+\int_{G}D_{g}(T_{g}u)\ dg,

where
P
is a pseudodifferential operator of order
m
,
D_{g}
,
g\in G
, is a smooth family of pseudodifferential operators of order
m
,
T_{g}
,
g\in G
, is a family of so-called shift operator defined by

T_{g}u(x)=g(u(g^{-1}x))
, and
dg
is the normalized Haar measure of
G
;
H^{s}(M,E^{+})
and
H^{s-m}(M,E^{-})
are the standard Sobolev spaces of sections of
E^{\pm}
.

When the operator
P
is elliptic, it is in particular Fredholm. Therefore, if
P
is elliptic, using a parametrix for the operator
P
, we see that we can assume that
E^{+}=E^{-}=E
,
P=\operatorname{Id}
, and that
D_{g}
is a smooth family of pseudodifferential operators of order
0
acting on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M,E)
of square integrable sections of
E
, see Section
1
.
For a general reference on the class of
G
-pseudodifferential operators we refer to

[
2
,
3
,
20
,
25
]
and the references therein.

In Section
2
, we use the main theorem of
[
6
]
in order to derive a Fredholm criterion for
G
-pseudodifferential operators. More precisely, we use a local principle à la Simonenko
[
24
]
, see also
[
1
,
12
]
for more general local principles. We would like to point out that local principles were intensively
used to obtain Fredholm conditions for many classes of singular operators, see for examples

[
3
,
10
,
14
,
18
,
15
,
19
,
23
]
and the references therein. In particular in
[
6
]
, the first author of this paper
showed a general Simonenko principle using
C^{*}
-algebra techniques and then derived from this result a Fredholm criterion for the restriction of
G
-invariant pseudodifferential operators to isotypical components. In this paper, we apply the same method to characterize Fredholm
G
-pseudodifferential operators, and we obtain the following result; see Section
2
for more details.

Theorem

(Simonenko’s principle for
G
-operators)
.

Assume that
M
does not contain any clopen orbit.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
G
-pseudodifferential operator as in Equation (
1
). Then

(a)

\operatorname{Id}+\mathcal{D}
commutes modulo compact operators with
\mathcal{C}(M)^{G}=\mathcal{C}(M/G)
.

(b)

\operatorname{Id}+\mathcal{D}:L^{2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
is Fredholm if, and only if, it is locally invertible on
M/G
.

See Section
2
for the precise definition of locally invertible
operators.

In Section
3
we consider the case, where the group is finite. To avoid any confusion,
we shall then denote our group by
\Gamma
. So
\Gamma
is a finite group acting by isometries on
M
and
E
, and we consider
\Gamma
-pseudodifferential operators, i.e., operators of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}:L^{2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E),

where the
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}
are pseudodifferential operators of order zero acting on sections of
E
, and the
T_{\gamma}
are shift operators as before.
As
\Gamma
is finite, the identity operator is already included in this class; this explains the simpler form compared to (
1
).

We start by recalling the pseudodifferential uniformization principle shown in
[
25
]
, see also
[
21
]
or
[
20
]
in our simpler case of finite groups.
In particular, this allows to view a
\Gamma
-pseudodifferential operator

\mathcal{D}:L^{2}(M,E)\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E)
as the restriction to
\Gamma
-invariant sections of a
\Gamma
-invariant pseudodifferential operator
\hat{\mathcal{D}}:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])
with coefficients in the Hermitian vector bundle
E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
. We then use the main result of
[
8
]
to characterize the Fredholm property of the
\Gamma
-invariant pseudodifferential operator
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
restricted to the subspaces of invariant sections in terms of its principal symbol.

In order to simplify the statement of our result, we first assume that
M/\Gamma
is connected
and let
\Gamma_{0}
be a minimal isotropy subgroup of
\Gamma
, see
[
11
,
26
]
.
We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\Gamma
-operator as in (
2
).
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The operator
\mathcal{D}:L^{2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
is Fredholm.

(ii)

The restriction to
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{\Gamma}

of the pseudodifferential
\Gamma
-invariant operator
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
introduced in Theorem
3.2
is Fredholm.

(iii)

The restriction of the principal symbol

\sigma_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})(\xi):\big(E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\big)^{\Gamma_{0}}\rightarrow\big(E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\big)^{\Gamma_{0}}

is invertible for all
\xi\in S^{*}M^{\Gamma_{0}}
.

We finally explain how to relax the hypothesis that
M/\Gamma
is connected, see Proposition
3.5
, and discuss some standard cases: trivial actions, topologically free actions and some non-topologically free actions.

In
[
4
, Theorem 4]
Antonevich has shown that the invertibility of the principal symbol

\sigma_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})(\xi):E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\rightarrow E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]

is sufficient for the Fredholm property of
\mathcal{D}
and that it is also necessary, if the action of
\Gamma
is efficient. If the action is efficient, a minimal isotropy group is trivial, and we recover Antonevich’s result. On the other hand, if a minimal isotropy group is not trivial, we obtain a new necessary and sufficient condition for the Fredholm property.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a compact Lie group which acts smoothly on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
M
is endowed with a
G
-invariant metric and that
G
acts by isometries.
Let
E^{\pm}\rightarrow M
be
G
-equivariant vector bundles equipped with
G
-invariant Hermitian metrics.
We shall denote by
\psi^{m}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
the space of
classical pseudodifferential operators of order
m
, acting between
sections of the
G
-equivariant vector bundles
E^{\pm}\rightarrow M
.
As pointed out above, we are interested in operators of the form

P+\mathcal{D}:C^{\infty}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M,E^{-}),\qquad(P+\mathcal{D})s(x)=Ps(x)+\int_{G}D_{g}(T_{g}s)(x)dg,

where
P\in\psi^{m}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
is elliptic,
D_{g}\in\psi^{m}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
,
g\in G
, is a smooth family of pseudodifferential operators,
T_{g}
,
g\in G
, are given by

T_{g}s(x)=g(s(g^{-1}x))
and
dg
is the normalized Haar measure of
G
.

Let us introduce the class
\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
of
G
-pseudodifferential operators,
that is operators of the form
\mathcal{D}=\int_{G}D_{g}T_{g}\ dg
as before.
The class
\psi^{m}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
coincides with
the class of operators of the form

\int_{G}T_{g}D_{g}\ dg
because
\int_{G}T_{g}D_{g}\ dg=\int_{G}T_{g}D_{g}T_{g^{-1}}T_{g}\ dg
and

T_{g}D_{g}T_{g^{-1}}\in\psi^{m}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
is a smooth family
(with respect to
g\in G
) of pseudodifferential operators,
see
[
5
,
25
]
.

Let
\nabla^{\pm}
be metric preserving,
G
-invariant connections on
E^{\pm}
.
In the sequel,
\Delta^{\pm}:=\nabla^{\pm*}\nabla^{\pm}
will
denote the (positive) Laplacian on
M
with coefficients
in
E^{\pm}
. Since
M
is compact, the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(M,E^{\pm})
can be defined for any

s\in[0,\infty)
as the domain of
(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta^{\pm})^{\frac{s}{2}}
in
L^{2}(M,E^{\pm})
, so that

(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta^{\pm})^{\frac{s}{2}}:H^{s}(M,E^{\pm})\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E^{\pm})
is
an isomorphism. For
s<0
, we define
H^{s}(M,E^{\pm})
as the dual of
H^{-s}(M,E^{\pm})
with
respect to the pairing defined by the
L^{2}
-inner product.
In order to simplify the notation, we shall denote
\Delta^{\pm}

by
\Delta
in the sequel
since the notation should be clear from the context.

Recall that a classical pseudodifferential operator

P\in\psi^{m}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
extends to a bounded linear map

P:H^{s}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}).

Furthermore, if
P\in\psi^{m}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
and
\mathcal{D}\in\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-})

then
P+\mathcal{D}
extends to a bounded operator

(3)

P+\mathcal{D}:H^{s}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}),

with norm controlled by

\|P+\mathcal{D}\|\leq\|P\|+\int_{G}\|D_{g}T_{g}\|dg\leq\|P\|+\int_{G}\|D_{g}\|dg.

We shall denote by
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
the set of bounded linear maps
from
H^{s}(M,E^{+})
to
H^{s-m}(M,E^{-})
. The closures of

\psi^{0}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
and
\psi^{0}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
, respectively, in the norm topology of

\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E^{+}),L^{2}(M,E^{-}))

will be denoted in the sequel by

\Psi(M;E^{+},E^{-})
and
\Psi_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
, respectively.

When
E^{+}=E^{-}=E
, we shall use the standard conventions
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E),L^{2}(M,E))=\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E))
,

\psi^{m}(M;E,E)=\psi^{m}(M,E)
,
\psi^{m}_{G}(M;E,E)=\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E)
,

\Psi(M;E,E)=\Psi(M,E)
and
\Psi_{G}(M;E,E)=\Psi_{G}(M,E)
.
The sets
\Psi(M,E)
and
\Psi_{G}(M,E)

are sub-
C^{*}
-algebras of
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E))
.
Notice that in general
\Psi_{G}(M,E)
is non-unital, i.e., in general
\operatorname{Id}\not\in\Psi_{G}(M,E)
.

We rely on Atkinson’s theorem to characterize Fredholm operators:
A bounded linear operator is Fredholm if, and only if, it is invertible modulo compact operators.
We recall the following standard lemma concerning order reduction for
G
-operators, see e.g.
[
7
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

(a)

A bounded operator
A:H^{s}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow H^{s-m}(M,E^{-})
is Fredholm if, and only if,

\tilde{A}:=(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{\frac{s-m}{2}}A(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}:L^{2}(M,E^{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E^{-})

is Fredholm.

(b)

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
be a Fredholm operator,
S
an inverse modulo compact operators for
A
, and
B\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
.
Then

A+B
is Fredholm if, and only if,
\operatorname{Id}+SB
is Fredholm.

(c)

Let
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
. Then

P\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E^{+}),L^{2}(M,E^{-}))
is Fredholm if, and only if,

\begin{pmatrix}0&P^{*}\\
P&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M,E))
is Fredholm.

(d)

If
P
is the limit in the norm
topology of
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
of a sequence of operators
P_{n}\in\psi^{m}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
,
then
\tilde{P}
, defined as in (a), belongs to
\Psi(M,E^{+},E^{-}):=\overline{\psi^{0}(M;E^{+},E^{-})}
.

(e)

If
\mathcal{D}
is the limit in the norm
topology of
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
of a sequence of operators
\mathcal{D}_{n}\in\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
,
then
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
, defined as in (a) belongs to
\Psi_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-}):=\overline{\psi^{0}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})}
.

(f)

If
R
is the limit in the norm
topology of
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E^{-}))
of a sequence of operators
P_{n}+\mathcal{D}_{n}\in\psi^{m}(M;E^{+},E^{-})+\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
, an
\tilde{R}
is as in (a),
then
\tilde{R}\in\Psi_{G}^{\mathrm{full}}(M,E^{+},E^{-}):=\overline{\psi^{0}(M;E^{+},E-)+\psi^{0}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})}
.

Proof.

(a) The operator
P
is Fredholm if, and only if,
\tilde{P}
is Fredholm, because

(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{s}:H^{m}(M,E^{\pm})\rightarrow H^{m-2s}(M,E^{\pm})
is an isomorphism for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b) This is a trivial consequence of Atkinson’s theorem.

(c) This is clear.

(d) According to
[
22
]
, the complex powers of the Laplace operator are classical pseudodifferential operators. Hence the result is a consequence of the
continuity of the multiplication in the operator norm.

(e) Notice that if
\mathcal{D}\in\psi^{m}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
then
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\in\psi^{0}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
.
Indeed, the
G
-invariance of the Laplace operator implies that

(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{\frac{s-m}{2}}D_{g}T_{g}(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}=(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{\frac{s-m}{2}}D_{g}(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}T_{g}

and it follows again from Seeley’s result
[
22
]
that

(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{\frac{s-m}{2}}D_{g}(\operatorname{Id}+\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}

is an order
0
classical pseudodifferential operator.
Now assume that
\mathcal{D}
is the limit in the norm
topology of
\mathcal{L}(H^{s}(M,E^{+}),H^{s-m}(M,E))
of a sequence of operators
\mathcal{D}_{n}\in\psi^{m}_{G}(M,E^{+},E^{-})
.
Then we get from the previous discussion that
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{n}\in\psi^{0}_{G}(M;E^{+},E^{-})
.
The result follows then as above from the continuity of the multiplication
in the operator norm.

(f) The last item is completely similar.
∎

2.
A Simonenko local principle for
G
-pseudodifferential operators

We start by recalling the definition of locally invertible operators and the general Simonenko principle shown in
[
6
]
, where more references can be found.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
T
a compact Hausdorff space and
\mathcal{C}(T)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
a non-degenerate faithful
representation (i.e.
\mathcal{C}(T)
identifies with its image in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and the image of the
constant function
1
is
\operatorname{Id}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
).
Assume that the image of
\mathcal{C}(T)
does not intersect

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{0\}
. In other words, we are assuming that
\mathcal{C}(T)
is a unital sub-
C^{*}
-algebra
of the Calkin algebra
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{H}):=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. We shall denote by
M_{f}
the image of a
function
f\in\mathcal{C}(T)
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and call it the multiplication operator by
f
.

Define
\Psi_{T}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
as the
C^{*}
-algebra consisting of all

P\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
M_{\phi}PM_{\psi}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is a compact operator
for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}(T)
with disjoint support.
By Kasparov’s lemma (see
[
13
, Lemma 5.4.7]
)
\Psi_{T}(\mathcal{H})
consists of all operators
P
such that
PM_{f}-M_{f}P\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
for all
f\in\mathcal{C}(T)
(pseudolocal operators).

Definition 2.1

(see
[
6
,
8
]
)
.

An operator
P\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is
locally invertible at
x\in T
if
there exist:

(i)

a neighbourhood
V_{x}
of
x
and

(ii)

operators
Q^{x}_{1}
and
Q^{x}_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})

such that, for all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(V_{x})

Q^{x}_{1}PM_{f}=M_{f}=M_{f}PQ^{x}_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}).

The operator
P
is
locally invertible
if it is locally invertible at any
x\in T
.

We shall say that
\mathcal{C}(T)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
has the property of
strong convergence to
0
if for all

x\in T
, all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a neighborhood
V
of
x

such
\|M_{\chi}h\|<\varepsilon

for every
\chi\in\mathcal{C}(T,[0,1])
that is equal to
1
on a neighborhood of
x
and supported in
V
,
see
[
6
, Definition 2.9]
.

The following was shown in
[
6
, Section 2]
.

Proposition 2.2

(General Simonenko localization principle)
.

Let
P\in\Psi_{T}(\mathcal{H})
.
Assume that
\mathcal{C}(T)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
has the property of strong convergence to
0
.
Then

P
is locally invertible on
T
if, and only if,
P
is Fredholm.

Before stating the Simonenko principle for
G
-pseudodifferential operators,
let us recall more material. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and
recall that any
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
generates a vector field
Y_{M}(x):=\frac{d}{dt}_{|_{t=0}}e^{tY}\cdot x
.
Let
T^{*}_{G}M
be the closed conical subset of
T^{*}M
with fiber in
x\in M
given by

(4)

(T^{*}_{G}M)_{x}:=\{\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M,\ \xi(Y_{M}(x))=0,\ \forall Y\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

We also denote
S^{*}_{G}M=T^{*}_{G}M\cap S^{*}M
.

The following lemma was shown in
[
6
]
. We recall its easy proof for the benefit of the reader.

Lemma 2.3
.

(a)

Let
x\in M
be such that
(T^{*}_{G}M)_{x}=\{0\}
.
Then the orbit of
x
is a clopen of
M
. (Here,
M
needs not to be compact.)

(b)

The set of points
\mathcal{P}:=\{x\in M,\ (T^{*}_{G}M)_{x}=\{0\}\}
is a clopen in
M
.

Proof.

(a) Since
(T^{*}_{G}M)_{x}=\{0\}
, we obtain that
S_{x}=\{x\}
is the only slice at
x
. From the slice theorem,
we deduce that the orbit
Gx\cong G\times_{G_{x}}\{x\}=G\times_{G_{x}}S_{x}
is open but it is also
compact. Therefore,
Gx
is a union of connected components of
M

in bijection with the connected components of
G/G_{x}\cong Gx
.

(b) It follows from the first part that
\mathcal{P}
is a union of connected components of
M
.
Since
M
is compact, there is only a finite number of connected components.
This implies that
\mathcal{P}
is a finite union of clopen and therefore is a clopen.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The set
\mathcal{P}
will be empty for example in the following cases:

(a)

if
M
is connected and not reduced to a single orbit,

(b)

if
\dim M>\dim G
.

Notice that
M
is the disjoint union of the closed
G
-submanifolds
M\setminus\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
\chi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}}
be the characteristic function of
M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}
.
Then the multiplication operator
M_{\chi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}}}

by
\chi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}}
is a
G
-invariant orthogonal projection,
denoted
p
in the sequel,
with image
L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})
.
The corresponding orthogonal decomposition is given by

L^{2}(M,E)=L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})\oplus L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})
.
Recall that
\mathcal{C}(M/G)=\mathcal{C}(M)^{G}
.

We can now state the main result of this section. Below we consider
L^{2}(M,E)
as a
\mathcal{C}(M/G)
-module.

Theorem 2.5

(Simonenko’s principle for
G
-operators)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{G}(M,E)
be a
G
-operator as in Section
1
.
Then
\operatorname{Id}+\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if,
p\mathcal{D}p+\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})}
is locally invertible.

Proof.

Recall that
p=M_{\chi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}}}
,
\operatorname{Id}-p=M_{1-\chi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}}}
and
let us write

\mathcal{D}=p\mathcal{D}p+(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)+p\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)+(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}p.

The operators
(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
,
p\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
and
(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}p
are compact.
Indeed, let

\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}(M)^{G}
be
G
-invariant functions with disjoint supports.
Then
T_{g}M_{\psi}=M_{\psi}T_{g}
for all
g\in G
and therefore

M_{\phi}\mathcal{D}M_{\psi}=\int_{G}M_{\phi}D_{g}M_{\psi}T_{g}\ dg\in\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(M,E)),

because
M_{f_{1}}PM_{f_{2}}
is compact whenever
P\in\Psi(M,E)
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
have disjoint supports.
This implies that
p\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
, and
(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}p
are compact operators.

To show that
(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
is a compact operator, it is enough to show that

(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)\in\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}}))
.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}=\sqcup C_{i}
is a finite union of clopen orbits
C_{i}\cong G/G_{x_{i}}
with suitable
x_{i}\in M

and that
E|_{C_{i}}\cong G\times_{G_{x_{i}}}E_{i}
,
where
E_{i}
is a unitary representation of
G_{x_{i}}
.
Let
\phi_{i}
be the characteristic function of
C_{i}
. Then we can write

M_{1-\chi}\mathcal{D}M_{1-\chi}=\sum_{i}M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{i}}+\sum_{i\neq j}M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{j}}.

From the previous discussion we know that
M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{j}}
is compact if
i\neq j

and therefore
\sum_{i\neq j}M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{j}}
is compact.
Let us show that also
M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{i}}
is compact.
Notice that
M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{i}}=\int_{G}M_{\phi_{i}}D_{g}M_{\phi_{i}}T_{g}\ dg
is a
G
-pseudodifferential operator
on
C_{i}\cong G/G_{x_{i}}
. By definition,
M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{i}}
is the limit of sums of operators of the form

B\circ T_{\varphi}
, where
B
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(C_{i},E|_{C_{i}})
,

T_{\varphi}:=\int_{G}\varphi(g)T_{g}\ dg
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G)
.
Since
E|_{C_{i}}\cong G\times_{G_{x_{i}}}E_{i}
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G)
the operator

\hat{T}_{\varphi}:=\int_{G}\varphi(g)T_{g}\ dg:L^{2}(G,E_{i})\rightarrow L^{2}(G,E_{i})
is compact,
because its Schwartz kernel is given by the smooth function
k_{\varphi}(g,h):=\varphi(gh^{-1})
.
It follows that the restriction
\hat{T}_{\varphi}^{G_{i}}
of
\hat{T}_{\varphi}
to

L^{2}(G,E_{i})^{G_{x_{i}}}\cong L^{2}(G/G_{x_{i}},G\times_{G_{i}}E_{i})\cong L^{2}(C_{i},E|_{C_{i}})
is compact.
But modulo the isomorphism
L^{2}(G,E_{i})^{G_{x_{i}}}\cong L^{2}(C_{i},E|_{C_{i}})
the operator
\hat{T}_{\varphi}^{G_{i}}
is exactly
T_{\varphi}
,
and this shows that
B\circ T_{\varphi}
is compact.
Since the ideal of compact operators is closed, we see that
M_{\phi_{i}}\mathcal{D}M_{\phi_{i}}
is compact.

Therefore, it follows that
\operatorname{Id}+\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if,

\operatorname{Id}+p\mathcal{D}p
is Fredholm.
The operator
\operatorname{Id}+p\mathcal{D}p
is diagonal with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(M,E)=L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})\oplus L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})
.
Thus,
\operatorname{Id}+\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if,

\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})}+p\mathcal{D}p
is Fredholm.

Since for any
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P})^{G}
with disjoint support,

M_{\phi}\big(\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})}+p\mathcal{D}p\big)M_{\psi}=M_{\phi}\mathcal{D}M_{\psi}

is a compact operator, we get that

\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})}+p\mathcal{D}p\in\Psi_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}/G}\big(L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})\big).

Consequently, the result follows from Proposition
2.2

because on
M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}
the property of strong convergence to
0
is satisfied.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

In the previous proof we could have shown that
\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})}+M_{1-\chi}\mathcal{D}M_{1-\chi}
is Fredholm using
the pseudodifferential uniformization principle from
[
25
]

and the fact that
S^{*}_{G}M|_{\mathcal{P}}=S^{*}_{G}\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
.
Indeed, in this case
\operatorname{Id}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})}+M_{1-\chi}\mathcal{D}M_{1-\chi}

is elliptic in the sense of
[
25
, Definition 5.1]
and therefore Fredholm.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
P\in\Psi(M,E)
and
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{G}(M,E)
.
Then
P+\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if,
P
is elliptic on
\mathcal{P}
and
the restriction of
P+\mathcal{D}
to
M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}
is locally invertible.

Proof.

For
P\in\Psi(M,E)
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
with disjoint supports, the operator
M_{f_{1}}PM_{f_{2}}
is compact.
Therefore, writing

P+\mathcal{D}=p(P+\mathcal{D})p+(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)+(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})p+p(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p),

we see that
(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})p
and
p(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)
are compact.
Moreover,
(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)=(\operatorname{Id}-p)P(\operatorname{Id}-p)+(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
and
in the last proof we showed that
(\operatorname{Id}-p)\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Id}-p)
is compact.
Thus,
(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}}))
is Fredholm if, and only if,

(\operatorname{Id}-p)P(\operatorname{Id}-p)\in\Psi(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})
is Fredholm.
Since a classical pseudodifferential operator is Fredholm if, and only if, it is elliptic,
it follows that
(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}}))
is Fredholm if, and only if,

(\operatorname{Id}-p)P(\operatorname{Id}-p)\in\Psi(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})
is elliptic.

Now,
p(P+\mathcal{D})p\in\Psi_{(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P})/G}(L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E))
and
on
M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}
the property of strong convergence to
0
is satisfied.
Thus, we can apply Proposition
2.2

to the operator
p(P+\mathcal{D})p
and we obtain that

p(P+\mathcal{D})p:L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E)\rightarrow L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E)

is Fredholm if, and only if, it is locally invertible on
(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P})/G
.
Using again that
p(P+\mathcal{D})p+(\operatorname{Id}-p)(P+\mathcal{D})(\operatorname{Id}-p)

is diagonal with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(M,E)=L^{2}(M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P},E|_{M\smallsetminus\mathcal{P}})\oplus L^{2}(\mathcal{P},E|_{\mathcal{P}})
,
we obtain the result.
∎

3.
The Fredholm property for finite groups

In this section, we shall apply the main result of
[
8
]
, see also
[
7
,
9
]
,
to characterize Fredholm
G
-operators when
G
is a finite group.
To avoid any confusion, we shall denote our group by
\Gamma
in this section.

Pseudodifferential uniformization for finite groups

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group and let
M
be a compact manifold without boundary.
Let
E\rightarrow M
be a Hermitian
\Gamma
-vector bundle as before.
Assume that
\Gamma
acts on
M
and
E
by isometries.

Denote by
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
the finite dimensional complex vector space of functions over
\Gamma
,
that is the set of finite sums of the form
\sum_{\Gamma}a_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}
,
where
a_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\delta_{\gamma}(\gamma^{\prime})=1
if
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
and
0
otherwise.
Notice that
L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)=L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])
.

We consider two representations of
\Gamma
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}1\otimes R_{\gamma}:L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)\rightarrow L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E),&(1\otimes R_{\gamma})(s)(x,\gamma^{\prime})=s(x,\gamma^{\prime}\gamma),\\
T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma}:L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)\rightarrow L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E),&(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma})(s)(x,\gamma^{\prime})=\gamma s(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}).\end{array}

Clearly, these representations commute:
(1\otimes R_{\gamma})(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})(1\otimes R_{\gamma})
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
.

We shall need the unitary isomorphism

(5)

Q:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]),\qquad Qs(x,\gamma)=\gamma s(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1}).

Notice that
Q^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
and therefore
Q^{*}=Q^{-1}=Q
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The isomorphism
Q
intertwines the representations
1\otimes R
and
T\otimes L
:
Q(1\otimes R_{\gamma})=(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma})Q
forall
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Moreover, we have the following unitary isomorphism

(6)

L^{2}(M,E)\cong L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)^{1\otimes R}\cong L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)^{T\otimes L}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle Q(1\otimes R_{\gamma})s(x,\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(1\otimes R_{\gamma})s(\gamma^{\prime-1}x,\gamma^{\prime-1})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}s(\gamma^{\prime-1}x,\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma),

and

\displaystyle(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma})Qs(x,\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\gamma Qs(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\gamma\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}s(\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma),

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}s(\gamma^{\prime-1}x,\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma).

The first isomorphism is given by
s\mapsto s\otimes 1_{\Gamma}
, i.e.
(s\otimes 1_{\Gamma})(x,\gamma)=s(x)
.
Indeed,

(1\otimes R_{\gamma})(s\otimes 1_{\Gamma})(x,\gamma^{\prime})=(s\otimes 1_{\Gamma})(x,\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)=s(x)=(s\otimes 1_{\Gamma})(x,\gamma^{\prime}).

Now, if
s\in L^{2}(M\times\Gamma,E)^{1\otimes R}
then
s(x,\gamma)=(1\otimes R_{\gamma}s)(x,e)=s(x,e)
.
These two morphisms are clearly isometric (with respect to the normalized Haar measure) and inverse to each other.

The second isomorphism is given by the unitary
Q
since
Q(1\otimes R_{\gamma})=(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma})Q
.
∎

The following theorem was shown in
[
25
]
for general compact Lie groups. Since here we are only interested in finite groups (see also
[
4
]
), we give the proof in this simpler case for the benefit of the reader.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
and

(7)

\hat{\mathcal{D}}:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}Q\big(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]}\big)Q(1\otimes R_{\gamma}):L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])\longrightarrow L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]).

(a)

Then
\hat{\mathcal{D}}\in\Psi(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])
is a

\Gamma
-invariant classical pseudodifferential operator on
M
with respect to the
(T\otimes L)
-action with
coefficients in
E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\rightarrow M
, i.e.
\hat{\mathcal{D}}\in\Psi(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}
.

(b)

Moreover, the restriction of
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
to
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

(a) First,
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is clearly
\Gamma
-invariant with respect to the
(T\otimes L)
-action.
Indeed, we have

(1\otimes R_{\gamma})(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})(1\otimes R_{\gamma})
. That

Q(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(1\otimes R_{\gamma^{\prime}})Q
follows from Lemma
3.1
and
Q^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
. Finally,

(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]})(1\otimes R_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(1\otimes R_{\gamma^{\prime}})(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]})
.
This implies that
\hat{\mathcal{D}}(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\otimes L_{\gamma^{\prime}})\hat{\mathcal{D}}
.

Moreover, the fact that

\displaystyle Q(s\otimes\delta_{\gamma^{\prime}})(x,\gamma)

\displaystyle=T_{\gamma}(s)(x)\delta_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\gamma^{-1})=T_{\gamma^{\prime-1}}(s)(x)\delta_{\gamma^{\prime-1}}(\gamma)

implies that

(8)

Q\big(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]}\big)Q(s\otimes\delta_{\gamma^{\prime}})=(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{-1}s)\otimes\delta_{\gamma^{\prime}}.

Therefore,
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is a
(T\otimes L)
-invariant element of
\Psi(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]))
.

(b) Since the unitary
Q
intertwines the representations
T\otimes L
and
1\otimes R
, we have that

Q\hat{\mathcal{D}}Q
is the
(1\otimes R)
-invariant operator

Q\hat{\mathcal{D}}Q=\sum_{\Gamma}(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]})(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma}).

Recall that the first isomorphism of Lemma
3.1
is given for

s\in L^{2}(M,E)
and
f\in L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}
by

Vs=s\otimes 1_{\Gamma}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V^{-1}(f)(x)=f(x,e).

Since
(T_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma})V(s)=V(T_{\gamma}s)
,
it follows that
V
intertwines the restrictions of
Q\hat{\mathcal{D}}Q
and

\mathcal{D}=\sum_{\Gamma}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Characterization of Fredholm
\Gamma
-operators

Let as before
\Gamma
be a finite group acting by isometries on a compact manifold without boundary
M
and on a Hermitian
\Gamma
-vector bundle
F\rightarrow M
.
Let
\Gamma_{x}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma,\gamma x=x\}
be the stabiliser of
x
in
\Gamma
.

The case when
M/\Gamma
is connected

We assume that
M/\Gamma
is connected.
Then the principal orbit theorem, see
[
11
,
26
]
, implies that there is a subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
such that

•

the set
M_{(\Gamma_{0})}:=\{x\in M,\ \Gamma_{x}\ \mbox{is conjugated with }\Gamma_{0}\}
is an open dense submanifold of
M
;

•

\forall x\in M
,
\Gamma_{x}
contains a subgroup conjugated with
\Gamma_{0}
.

Such a subgroup
\Gamma_{0}
is called a
minimal isotropy subgroup

and
M_{(\Gamma_{0})}
is called the
principal orbit bundle
.

The following result was obtained in
[
8
, Theorem 1.5 and Proposition 5.9]
, see also
[
7
,
9
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
P\in\psi^{m}(M;F)^{\Gamma}
, for some
m\in\mathbb{R}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

The principal symbol
\sigma_{m}(P)
defines by restriction to
\Gamma_{0}
-invariant vectors an
isomorphism

\sigma_{m}(P)(\xi):F_{\xi}^{\Gamma_{0}}\longrightarrow F_{\xi}^{\Gamma_{0}}\qquad\text{for all }\xi\in(T^{*}M^{\Gamma_{0}}\smallsetminus\{0\})

where
\Gamma_{0}
is the minimal isotropy subgroup introduced above.

(ii)

The restriction
P^{\Gamma}:H^{s}(M,F)^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow H^{s-m}(M,F)^{\Gamma}

of
P
to the subspaces of
\Gamma
-invariant sections is Fredholm.

Notice that
\Psi(M,E)\subset\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
since
\Gamma
is discrete.
Therefore, in particular,
\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
is unital, i.e.
\operatorname{Id}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
.
We can now give the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
be a
\Gamma
-pseudodifferential operator.
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The operator
\mathcal{D}:L^{2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
is Fredholm.

(ii)

The restriction to
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}

of the operator
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
introduced in Theorem
3.2
is Fredholm.

(iii)

The restriction

(9)

\sigma_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})(\xi):\big(E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\big)^{\Gamma_{0}}\rightarrow\big(E_{\xi}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\big)^{\Gamma_{0}}

is invertible
\forall\xi\in S^{*}M^{\Gamma_{0}}
.

Proof.

The equivalence
(\rm{i})\Leftrightarrow(ii)
is a consequence of Theorem
3.2
,
while the equivalence
(\rm{ii})\Leftrightarrow(iii)

follows from Theorem
3.3
applied with
P=\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and
F=E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
.
∎

The case when
M/\Gamma
is not connected

We now explain how to reduce the general case to the case, where
M/\Gamma
is connected.

Let
\pi:M\to M/\Gamma
be the quotient map and consider the decomposition

M/\Gamma=\sqcup_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}
as the
disjoint
union
of its connected components.
We then introduce the preimages
M_{i}:=\pi^{-1}(C_{i})

of these connected components. In
general, the submanifolds
M_{i}
are not connected, but, for each
i
,

M_{i}/\Gamma=C_{i}

is connected
and
M_{i}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

We shall denote by
\chi_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M)^{\Gamma}
the characteristic function of
M_{i}
and
by
E_{i}:=E|_{M_{i}}
the restriction of a
\Gamma
-equivariant vector bundle
E
over
M
to
M_{i}
.
Notice that
p_{i}:=M_{\chi_{i}}:L^{2}(M,E)\rightarrow L^{2}(M,E)
is a
\Gamma
-invariant projection
such that
p_{i}p_{j}=0
for all
i\neq j
and
\operatorname{Id}=\sum p_{i}
.
Therefore, we have

(10)

L^{2}(M;E)\,\simeq\,\oplus_{i=1}^{N}L^{2}(M_{i};E_{i})\quad\mbox{ and
}\quad\oplus_{i=1}^{N}\Psi_{\Gamma}(M_{i};E_{i})\,\subset\,\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)\,.

Recall that
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the algebra of compact operators on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The following proposition provides the desired
reduction to the connected case.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p_{i}:=M_{\chi_{i}}:L^{2}(M;E)\to L^{2}(M_{i};E_{i})
be the canonical orthogonal
projection as before. For
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
, we have

\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}+K,\quad\text{where $\mathcal{D}_{i}=\sum p_{i}D_{\gamma}p_{i}T_{\gamma}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M_{i},E_{i})$ }

and
K\in\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(M,E))
is compact.
Moreover,
\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if, all
\mathcal{D}_{i}
are Fredholm.
In particular,
\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if, for every
i
, the operator
\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.4
(iii).

Proof.

We write

\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i,j}p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{j}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{i}+K,

where
K=\sum_{i\neq j}p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{j}
.
By
\Gamma
-invariance of
\chi_{i}
,
we obtain
T_{\gamma}p_{i}=T_{\gamma}M_{\chi_{i}}=M_{\chi_{i}}T_{\gamma}=p_{i}T_{\gamma}
.
It follows that

p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{j}=\sum_{\Gamma}p_{i}D_{\gamma}p_{j}T_{\gamma}.

Since
p_{i}D_{\gamma}p_{i}
is a classical
pseudodifferential operator on
M_{i}
with coefficients in
E_{i}
,
we get that
\mathcal{D}_{i}=p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{i}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M_{i},E_{i})
.
Moreover, if
i\neq j
then
p_{i}D_{\gamma}p_{j}
has zero principal symbol and hence is compact.
It follows that
K=\sum_{i\neq j}p_{i}\mathcal{D}p_{j}
is a finite sum of compact operators
and therefore is compact.

The rest follows from Equation (
10
)
since
\sum_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}
is a diagonal operator with respect to the direct sum

L^{2}(M,E)\cong\bigoplus_{i}L^{2}(M_{i},E_{i})
.
∎

Application

We now give the following result regarding the index of a Fredholm
\Gamma
-operator,
see also
[
8
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
17
]
for more details.
We denote by
\Sigma
the
C^{*}
-algebra of symbols
{\sigma}_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})
with
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
of order zero
and
{\sigma}_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})
as in Theorem
3.4
. In other words,

\Sigma
is the range of the restriction homomorphism

\mathcal{C}(S^{*}M,\operatorname{End}(E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]))^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}(S^{*}M^{\Gamma_{0}},\operatorname{End}((E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{\Gamma_{0}})).

We shall denote by
\hat{\Psi}(M,E)
the
C^{*}
-algebra
of restrictions to
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}
of elements of
\Psi(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}
, see Theorem
3.2
. Notice that
\hat{\Psi}(M,E)\cong\Psi(M,E)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)
be Fredholm. Then the Fredholm index of
\mathcal{D}
is given by

\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{D})=\partial[\widehat{\sigma}_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})],

where

\partial:K_{1}(\Sigma)\rightarrow K_{0}(\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(M,E)))\cong\mathbb{Z}

is the boundary map associated with the exact sequence

(11)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(M,E))\longrightarrow\hat{\Psi}_{\Gamma}(M,E)\longrightarrow\Sigma\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

The fact that the sequence in (
11
) is exact follows from the isomorphism

L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])^{T\otimes L}\cong L^{2}(M,E)
and Proposition
3.5
.
The proof is completed using that the index morphism is the boundary map in
K
-theory for the Calkin exact sequence, e.g., see
[
8
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
17
]
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

We refer to
[
4
,
16
,
20
]
for index theorems under the assumption that
\sigma_{0}(\hat{\mathcal{D}})\in\mathcal{C}(S^{*}M,\operatorname{End}(E\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]))^{\Gamma}
is invertible.

Particular cases

In this section, we discuss particular cases when
E=M\times\mathbb{C}
with the trivial action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{C}
. We shall denote simply
\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,E)=\Psi_{\Gamma}(M,\mathbb{C})
by
\Psi_{\Gamma}(M)
and similarly
\Psi(M,E)
by
\Psi(M)
. Finally, we assume that
M/\Gamma
is connected.

\bullet
If
\Gamma
acts trivially on
M
then
\Psi_{\Gamma}(M)=\Psi(M)

and
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M)
is Fredholm if, and only if,
\mathcal{D}
is elliptic.
This is consistent with Theorem
3.4
because by Equation (
8

Egorov’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\Big(\sum Q(D_{\gamma}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]})Q(1\otimes R_{\gamma})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma}\underset{\gamma^{\prime}}{\operatorname{diag}}\big(T_{\gamma^{\prime}}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma^{\prime-1}}\big)(1\otimes R_{\gamma})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\underset{\gamma^{\prime}}{\operatorname{diag}}\big(\gamma^{\prime*-1}(\sigma_{0}(D_{\gamma}))\big)R_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\underset{\gamma^{\prime}}{\operatorname{diag}}\big(\sigma_{0}(D_{\gamma})\big)R_{\gamma},

where
\underset{\gamma^{\prime}}{\operatorname{diag}}(A_{\gamma^{\prime}})(v\otimes\delta_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}})=(A_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}v)\otimes\delta_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}

By triviality of the action on

\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma

is invertible if, and only if,
\sum\sigma_{0}(D_{\gamma})
is invertible.
In other words,
\mathcal{D}
is Fredholm if, and only if,
\mathcal{D}

\bullet
Recall that the action of a discrete group is
topologically free

if for all
N
and
\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{N}\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
the union
\cup_{j=1}^{N}M^{\gamma_{j}}
of fixed point sets does not contain an open set in
M
. If
\Gamma
is a finite group, then this condition is equivalent to the condition the, for any open set
U\subset M
, there is
x\in U
such that all
\gamma x
are distinct. Note that this condition is equivalent to the condition that
\dim M^{\gamma}<\dim M
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
. It is also equivalent to the condition that
\Gamma_{0}=\{e\}
, where
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
is a minimal isotropy subgroup of the action.
Assume that the action of
\Gamma
is topologically free on
M
.
In this case, it was shown in
[
2
,
3
]

that
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M)
is Fredholm if, and only if, its trajectory symbol is invertible.
In other words,
\mathcal{D}\in\Psi_{\Gamma}(M)
is Fredholm if, and only if,
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is elliptic.
Again this is consistent with Theorem
3.4
.

\bullet
If a minimal isotropy subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
of the action is a nontrivial normal subgroup, then the restriction of the action to
\Gamma_{0}
is trivial. Hence, we obtain a topologically free action of the quotient group
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
and we can write a
\Gamma
-operator as a
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
-operator:

\mathcal{D}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}D_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}=\sum_{\langle\gamma\rangle\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}}\left(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\langle\gamma\rangle}D_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)T_{\gamma}.

Then one can show that the symbol (
9
) for
\mathcal{D}
as a
\Gamma
-operator is isomorphic to the symbol of this operator as a
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
-operator for the topologically free action of
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
.

\bullet
Let
H\subset\Gamma
be a subgroup and consider
M=X\times(\Gamma/H)
, where
X
is a connected closed smooth manifold. Let
\Gamma
act by left multiplications:
\gamma\in\Gamma
takes
(x,\gamma^{\prime}H)

(x,\gamma\gamma^{\prime}H)

\Gamma_{0}=H
and we have an isomorphism

\Gamma

\Gamma

JDJ^{-1}=\sum_{\gamma}D_{\gamma}L^{-1}_{\gamma},

(L_{\gamma}u)(\gamma^{\prime})=u(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})

\Gamma
