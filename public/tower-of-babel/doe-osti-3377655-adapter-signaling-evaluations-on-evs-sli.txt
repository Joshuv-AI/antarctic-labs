 Background

• J3400 and J3400/1 identify a basic signaling (analog) approach for DC
  charging adapters to alert the EV and EVSE to an over-temperature event.
• J3400 requires the EV to handle mitigation and corrective action for EVSEs
  that do not respond to adapter thermal events.
• Functional safety for use of adapters relies on proper implementation EV
  adapter detection.

                                                             National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   3

 Objectives

• Develop test cases to assess scenarios in which an EV interacts with EVSEs
  that do not monitor or respond to Proximity signals.
 ◦ These test cases were derived from prior work conducted during ChargeX adapter
 testing (1).
• Analyze EV OEM approach to the adapter-related thermal events.
• Evaluate responsiveness of production vehicles to confirm field readiness and
  ensure performance consistency.
• Recommend adapter thermal event test cases to standards organizations
  (J3400) for consideration.

Experimental Setup
Overview

 Test Configuration

• NLR developed “Breakout Fixture” to simulate adapter thermal events by adjusting the
  proximity voltage.
• The Breakout Fixture acts as an inline adapter between the EVSE and EV, allowing
  uninterrupted charging current while interrupting signal lines to enable manual control of
  the control pilot (CP)* and proximity pilot (PP) states as specified in SAE J1772, J3400,
  and J3400/1.
• The adapter breakout fixture is split into two:
 ◦ High Voltage Breakout Fixture: This section manages high voltage and high current.
 ◦ Control Breakout Fixture: This section supports control and monitoring functions.

 Experimental Setup

• During testing, the high voltage breakout fixture is
  installed between the EV and the EVSE. This is done Control
  by connecting the charging cable from the EVSE to Breakout Fixture
  the HV breakout fixture and then linking the HV
  breakout fixture connector to the EV’s charging inlet.
• CP and PP communication signals are routed and
  intercepted in control breakout fixture, enabling users
  to manually adjust the voltage states as needed.
• The control breakout fixture is equipped with knobs to
  lock/unlock the charging connector from the EVSE to High Voltage
  the HV fixture, ensuring a stable connection during Breakout Fixture
  operation. Additionally, a data logger is connected to
  the fixture port to capture and record test data

 Summary
• The tables below summarizes how production EVs respond to various proximity signal events.
• Some EV OEMs appear to follow the J3400 standard, but due to its evolving nature, they are not aligned with the latest version.
 An agreement should be reached within the standards bodies to encourage compliance and minimize confusion.
• Next steps: Publish test cases and procedures aligned with J3400, J3400/1 for various adapter conditions
