STRAP promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression through activation of an STRAP/Wnt-β-catenin/STRAP positive feedback loop
Authors: Chunxun Liu, Qiyue Xuan, Zuoming Guo, Xiaoxuan Zhang, Yubao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummarySTRAP is upregulated in multiple cancers; however, its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains insufficiently defined. Using publicly available databases, cell lines, and clinical specimens, we confirmed that STRAP is overexpressed in HCC and is associated with poor prognosis. Functional assays demonstrated that STRAP accelerates HCC progression through activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Mechanistically, STRAP interacts with DKK1, thereby preventing β-catenin degradation and promoting its nuclear accumulation and transcriptional complex formation with TCF4. The β-catenin/TCF4 complex subsequently binds to the STRAP promoter, thereby establishing an STRAP/Wnt/β-catenin positive feedback loop that sustains oncogenic signaling. Combined targeting of STRAP and the Wnt/β-catenin pathway markedly suppressed HCC progression, underscoring its therapeutic potential. In summary, we identified an STRAP-dependent mechanism of Wnt/β-catenin activation and a reinforcing STRAP/Wnt/β-catenin feedback loop. These findings provide mechanistic insight and a rationale for therapeutic strategies targeting STRAP in HCC.

Introduction

Worldwide, hepatic malignancies rank sixth in incidence and represent the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality.1 Due to their high incidence and mortality rates, they have emerged as a major global public health challenge.2 Notably, China accounts for nearly half of the global burden of both newly diagnosed cases and deaths attributable to liver cancer.3 Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) constitutes the majority of primary hepatic malignancies. A substantial proportion of patients with HCC present with advanced-stage disease, for which limited therapeutic options and poor survival outcomes remain major challenges.4,5 Accordingly, elucidating the molecular mechanisms that drive HCC initiation and progression is essential for early clinical detection and the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

STRAP, a serine/threonine kinase receptor-associated protein, participates in multiple cellular processes, including signal transduction, transcriptional regulation, RNA processing, and vesicle-mediated transport.6,7 Accumulating evidence indicates that STRAP promotes tumorigenesis by enhancing cell proliferation, inhibiting apoptosis, and increasing stemness and metastatic potential through key oncogenic pathways, such as Wnt/β-catenin, MEK/ERK, and NOTCH signaling.8,9,10 Elevated STRAP expression has been correlated with poorer prognosis across several tumor types, highlighting its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. Notably, in HCC, aberrant activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway occurs in the vast majority of cases, with reported frequencies approaching 95%.11 This dysregulation is closely associated with tumor progression and therapeutic resistance.12,13 Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying Wnt pathway hyperactivation is imperative for the development of novel therapeutic strategies and the improvement of clinical outcomes.

In this study, we demonstrated that high STRAP expression was significantly associated with advanced tumor stage and poor prognosis in patients with HCC. Mechanistically, STRAP promoted HCC progression through activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Importantly, we further demonstrated that STRAP interacted with DKK1 and inhibited its binding to LRP6. Consequently, the half-life of β-catenin was prolonged, its nuclear translocation was enhanced, and the interaction between β-catenin and TCF4 was strengthened. In addition, STRAP was identified as a downstream target gene of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and activation of this pathway increased STRAP expression, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop that sustained oncogenic signaling and promoted HCC progression.

Results

STRAP was highly expressed in HCC and negatively correlated with prognosis

Transcriptomic profiling using the GEPIA database demonstrated that STRAP mRNA was consistently elevated across multiple malignancies, including HCC (Figure S1A). Analysis of the TCGA-LIHC dataset further confirmed this trend (Figures 1A and 1B). In addition, compared with patients with G1/2 tumors, those with G3/4 tumors exhibited higher STRAP expression levels, and patients with advanced-stage disease also showed increased STRAP expression (Figure S1B). Cox regression analysis identified STRAP expression as an independent prognostic risk factor for HCC (Figure S1C). In HCC, elevated STRAP expression was associated with worse clinical outcomes (Figure 1C). Normal liver cells exhibited lower STRAP expression levels compared with HCC cell lines (Figure S1D). Western blot and immunohistochemistry (IHC) analyses of tumor and adjacent normal tissues obtained from patients with HCC at our hospital demonstrated increased STRAP expression in tumor tissues (Figures 1D and 1E). Patients with advanced-stage disease exhibited higher STRAP expression (Figure 1F). STRAP promoted HCC progression in vitro and in vivo

HCC cell lines with STRAP overexpression or knockdown were established to assess its functional contribution. Alterations in STRAP expression were confirmed by western blot and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analyses (Figures S1E and S1F). Wound-healing and transwell migration assays indicated that STRAP overexpression enhanced the migratory capacity of HCC cells (Figures 2A and 2B). Following STRAP knockdown, the colony-forming ability of HCC cells was significantly reduced (Figure 2C). In addition, the 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) incorporation assay demonstrated that STRAP overexpression increased the proliferative capacity of HCC cells (Figure 3A). To determine whether STRAP knockdown inhibited tumor growth and progression in vivo, a subcutaneous xenograft mouse model was established. Significant reductions in tumor growth and tumor weight were observed in the STRAP knockdown group (Figure 3B). Compared with the control group, mice in the STRAP knockdown group also exhibited reduced tumor cell proliferation, as indicated by a lower proportion of Ki-67-positive cells (Figure 3C). STRAP positively regulated the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway

Previous studies have shown that STRAP regulates multiple signaling pathways, including TGF-β, NOTCH, MEK/ERK, and Wnt/β-catenin.8,14,15,16 Analysis of the TCGA-LIHC dataset revealed that STRAP expression was positively correlated with multiple Wnt/β-catenin pathway-related genes, such as MYC, BIRC5, and TCF3 (Figures S2A and S2B). To investigate the impact of STRAP on Wnt/β-catenin signaling, we performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis comparing STRAP knockdown HCC cells with control cells (Figure 4A). STRAP knockdown reduced the expression of Wnt/β-catenin pathway-related genes (Figure 4B). To further assess how STRAP influences the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, western blot analysis demonstrated that STRAP overexpression increased the protein levels of c-Myc, cyclin D1, and survivin—key downstream targets of this signaling cascade—whereas STRAP silencing produced the opposite effect (Figure 4C). Consistently, qRT-PCR analysis revealed that STRAP knockdown decreased the mRNA expression of multiple Wnt/β-catenin target genes in HCC cells, confirming its regulatory role. In contrast, STRAP overexpression increased the transcript levels of these target genes, further supporting its positive modulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling (Figure 4D). The TOP/FOP luciferase reporter assay demonstrated that STRAP regulates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway and that STRAP overexpression activates this signaling cascade (Figure 4E). Gene set enrichment analysis indicated that the Wnt/β-catenin pathway was significantly enriched in the control group (Figure S2C), suggesting that this pathway was suppressed following STRAP knockdown. The IHC staining results showed a positive correlation between the expression of STRAP and the Wnt/β-catenin pathway gene NOTUM (Figure 4F). In addition, survivin expression was reduced in tumors derived from mice in the STRAP knockdown group (Figure S2D). Collectively, these results indicate that STRAP knockdown attenuates Wnt/β-catenin signaling.

Wnt/β-catenin pathway was essential for the effect of STRAP on HCC

To further determine whether the effects of STRAP on HCC were mediated through the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, we conducted in vitro experiments. Wound-healing and transwell migration assays showed that β-catenin knockdown or treatment with the Wnt/β-catenin inhibitor MSAB suppressed HCC cell migration. Moreover, β-catenin knockdown or MSAB treatment attenuated the promotive effect of STRAP overexpression on HCC cell migration (Figures 5A–5D). In addition, STRAP overexpression enhanced the proliferative capacity of HCC cells, whereas β-catenin knockdown or MSAB treatment suppressed this effect (Figures 5E and 5F). Similarly, β-catenin knockdown or MSAB treatment reduced the enhancing effect of STRAP overexpression on HCC colony-forming ability (Figures 5G and 5H). STRAP inhibited the interaction between DKK1 and LRP6

To identify key mediators involved in STRAP-dependent regulation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, we performed co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) followed by mass spectrometry to screen for STRAP-associated proteins in HCC cells. DKK1, a negative regulator of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, was enriched in STRAP immunoprecipitates (Figures 6A and 6B). As a canonical Wnt pathway inhibitor, DKK1 suppresses Wnt signaling by binding to LRP6. Our data indicated that STRAP overexpression inhibited the interaction between DKK1 and LRP6, whereas STRAP knockdown enhanced this interaction (Figure 6C). In STRAP knockdown cells, increased binding of DKK1 to LRP6 promoted β-catenin degradation. In contrast, STRAP overexpression increased intracellular β-catenin protein levels (Figure 6D). Further experiments demonstrated that STRAP overexpression prolonged the half-life of β-catenin (Figure 6E). Activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway requires translocation of β-catenin into the nucleus, where it interacts with TCF/LEF family transcription factors to form a transcriptional activation complex. This complex activates a series of downstream target genes, driving cellular processes such as proliferation and differentiation and thereby promoting tissue growth, repair, and regeneration. To further assess the impact of STRAP on β-catenin subcellular distribution, nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions were analyzed by western blot. STRAP knockdown markedly reduced nuclear β-catenin levels (Figure 7A). In addition, immunofluorescence assays were performed in STRAP knockdown and overexpression cells. Nuclear localization of β-catenin was significantly increased in STRAP-overexpressing cells (Figure 7B). Immunoprecipitation followed by western blot analysis further demonstrated that STRAP knockdown reduced the binding of β-catenin to TCF4, whereas STRAP -overexpression enhanced this interaction (Figure 7C). Blocking the STRAP-Wnt positive feedback loop inhibited HCC progression

Interestingly, we found that the Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibitor MSAB significantly downregulated STRAP protein levels in HCC cells in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 8A). Another small-molecule inhibitor, iCRT3, produced a similar effect (Figure 8B). β-catenin knockdown also reduced STRAP protein levels (Figure 8C). In contrast, stimulation with the ligand Wnt3A increased STRAP expression in a time-dependent manner (Figure 8D). To determine whether STRAP is directly regulated by Wnt/β-catenin signaling, chromatin immunoprecipitation assays confirmed that TCF/LEF transcription factors bind to the STRAP promoter region (Figure 8E). In vivo studies further demonstrated that pharmacologic inhibition of Wnt signaling combined with STRAP knockdown significantly suppressed HCC tumor growth (Figure 8F). Collectively, these findings indicate that STRAP is a downstream target gene of the β-catenin pathway and that the STRAP/Wnt/β-catenin positive feedback loop drives HCC progression. Discussion

STRAP is a WD repeat domain-containing protein that plays a critical role in carcinogenesis and cancer progression. Early studies have shown that STRAP participates in the regulation of several oncogenic biological processes, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, tumorigenesis, and metastasis. In colorectal cancer, STRAP maintains cancer stem cell homeostasis through the NOTCH1-HES1 axis, thereby promoting tumor progression and metastasis.8 The proliferative phenotype associated with STRAP and its potential as a prognostic biomarker have also been reported in gliomas.17 Under hypoxic conditions, STRAP mediates FTO degradation in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby promoting colorectal cancer metastasis.9 Our findings indicate that STRAP is highly expressed in HCC and is associated with poor prognosis, with activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway representing a key mechanism underlying its promotive effect on HCC progression.

The Wnt signaling pathway regulates critical cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis.18 Dysregulation of Wnt signaling at multiple levels is closely associated with tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance.19 STYK1 and EIF4E activate the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, thereby promoting pancreatic cancer initiation and progression.20,21 Similarly, STK3 enhances DNA repair and stemness in gastric cancer by activating the Wnt pathway through YAP1.22 Moreover, aberrant Wnt signaling in other components of the tumor microenvironment also influences tumor behavior. Cancer-associated fibroblasts within the tumor microenvironment activate the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby promoting lymph node metastasis in colorectal cancer.23 Hypoxia-induced inflammatory fibroblasts in the microenvironment suppress angiogenesis via Wnt5a, thereby maintaining hypoxia and promoting colorectal cancer progression.24 Targeting the Wnt pathway has been shown to be an effective and safe strategy to inhibit tumor growth and enhance the efficacy of existing cancer therapies.25,26,27 In the present study, we demonstrated that STRAP activates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway in HCC. Mechanistically, STRAP interacts with DKK1 and inhibits its binding to LRP6, thereby prolonging the half-life of β-catenin. Furthermore, activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway enhances STRAP expression, establishing an STRAP/Wnt/β-catenin positive feedback loop that accelerates HCC progression. Previous studies have reported that STRAP activates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway in various tumors by increasing LRP6 phosphorylation and inhibiting β-catenin ubiquitin-mediated degradation.10,14,28 Consistent with these reports, our results showed that multiple Wnt pathway-related genes, including MYC, CCND1, and BIRC5, were downregulated in STRAP knockout cells.28

DKK1, a secreted glycoprotein, inhibits the Wnt signaling pathway by binding to LRP6 and blocking the interaction between Wnt ligands and LRP6, thereby regulating tumorigenesis and immune responses. Elevated DKK1 levels are associated with colorectal cancer recurrence and reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration, resulting in poor responses to PD-1 blockade.29 Furthermore, DKK1 upregulates SLC7A11 expression, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis and promoting tumor metastasis.30 High DKK1 expression suppresses CD8+ T cells,31 macrophages,32 natural killer cells,33 and neutrophils,34 thereby weakening antitumor immunity. The anti-DKK1 antibody DKN-01 has demonstrated promising efficacy when combined with standard therapies, such as 5-fluorouracil/oxaliplatin and tislelizumab, in the treatment of gastric cancer.35

In conclusion, high STRAP expression is strongly associated with poor prognosis in HCC, and STRAP accelerates disease progression by enhancing Wnt/β-catenin pathway activity. STRAP interacts with DKK1 to prevent its inhibitory effect on Wnt signaling, thereby forming a positive feedback loop that further drives tumor progression. Thus, the role of STRAP in HCC provides a theoretical basis for its potential as a therapeutic target.

Limitations of the study

Although our study provides novel insights into the mechanism by which STRAP influences HCC progression, the specific interaction sites between STRAP and DKK1 remain unclear. Future studies should focus on elucidating STRAP-related regulatory networks and developing targeted small-molecule inhibitors, which may provide a theoretical basis for HCC treatment.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human HCC samples

The data and tissues of 47 HCC patients who visited Harbin Medical University Affiliated Cancer Hospital were collected. This study follows the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital (KY2023-32), and all patients signed a written informed consent form. In addition, HCC tissue chips were purchased from Outdo Biotech (Shanghai, China). The information of the HCC patient included is found in Tables S1–S4.

Ethics statement

This study follows the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration. Ethics approval for human subjects was provided by the Ethics Committee of Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital (KY2023-32). Besides, all the animal studies were approved by the the Animal Ethics Committee of Harbin Medical University Affiliated Second Hospital (YJSDW2023-256).

Animal experiments

The 5-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were obtained from Jiangsu Huachuang sino Pharma Tech Co.,Ltd. The animal experimental protocol has been approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Harbin Medical University Affiliated Second Hospital (YJSDW2023-256). In the subcutaneous model, female BALB/c nude mice aged 4–6 weeks were subcutaneously injected with 1 ∗ 107 MHCC-97L cells. Measure tumor volume every 2–3 days. The formula for calculating tumor volume is V = L × W2 × 0.52, where L refers to the major diameter, while W indicates the minor diameter.

Method details

Cell lines and culture

MIHA cells were purchased from Otwo Biotech (Shenzhen, China). Hep3B, Huh-7, MHCC-97L, HCCLM3, and PLC/PRF/5 cells were obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China). Hep3B and PLC/PRF/5 cells were cultured in MEM (Gibco, USA), MIHA, Huh-7, and HCCLM3 cells were cultured in DMEM (Gibco, USA), and MHCC-97L cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA). All cells were cultured at 37°C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2. All cell lines tested by commercial laboratory and confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination.

Western blot

For cellular protein extraction, RIPA lysis buffer containing PMSF was lysed and collected under low temperature conditions, followed by protein quantification using BCA assay. In the half-life determination experiment, cells were treated with 20 μM cycloheximide and collected at different times to detect the half-life of the protein. The tissue stored in liquid nitrogen is also cleaved by RIPA after mechanical fragmentation.

According to the needs of the experiment, add an appropriate amount of protein to the sample on the SDS-PAGE gel for electrophoretic separation. After electrophoresis, transfer the separated proteins onto PVDF. After sealing the membrane with skim milk powder, the primary antibody (4°C overnight) and secondary antibody (room temperature) were incubated separately. The ECL system is used for development, and the image is obtained by the Tanon 5200 imaging system.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) and hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining

Tissue samples were immobilized using a 4% paraformaldehyde solution and later embedded in paraffin wax. After slicing, dewaxing, hydration, and antigen repair were sequentially completed. Subsequently, the slices were processed in H2O2 and serum containing blocking solution, followed by incubation with primary antibody (4°C) and secondary antibody (room temperature), and protein expression regions were visualized by color.

During the H&E staining process, after dewaxing and hydration of the slices, they were sequentially stained with hematoxylin and eosin to label the nuclear and cytoplasmic structures, respectively.

Establishment of stable cell lines

The overexpression and knockdown of STRAP were achieved through a lentiviral system, and virus preparation was carried out by Genechem (Shanghai, China). β-catenin knockdown was achieved using shRNA expressed from the GV112 vector under the control of the U6 promoter. The shRNA target sequences used are shown in Table S5.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using Total RNA Kit I, reverse transcribed using TOYOBO assay kit to obtain cDNA, and then amplified using SYBR system in StepOnePlus instrument. Using β-actin as an internal reference, calculate the relative expression level through 2 - Δ Δ Ct. The relevant primers were synthesized by Seven Biotech (Beijing, China), and the detailed sequences are shown in Table S6.

Wound healing assay

Inoculate the cells into a six well plate, wait for the cells to reach logarithmic growth phase, and use a sterile pipette tip to draw uniform scratches on the cell monolayer. After removing suspended cells, continue to culture in serum-free medium and record wound closure at 0 and 24 h to calculate migration ability.

Transwell

Collect the target cells and inoculate them into the upper chamber of the Transwell chamber containing serum-free culture medium. Add culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) to the lower chamber. Cultivate at 37°C for 48 h. Specimens were subjected to a 15-min fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde and thereafter stained with crystal violet solution.

Colony formation assay

Inoculate 2000 cells into a 6-well plate, culture for 14 days, wait for the cells to grow and form visible clones. Specimens were subjected to a 15-min fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde and thereafter stained with crystal violet solution.

EdU assay

Cell proliferation was quantified using the BeyoClick™ EdU-488 detection kit. After EdU incorporation during culture, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and then treated with 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS for 15 min to achieve permeabilization. Add 500 μL of click reaction solution to each well and incubate at room temperature in the dark for 30 min. Hoechst 33342 incubated cells in the dark for 10 min. Take photos with a fluorescence microscope.

RNA-seq

RNA was extracted with TRIzol, and mRNA species bearing poly(A) tails were isolated via Oligo(dT)-magnetic bead enrichment. The mRNA was fragmented under controlled conditions and converted into double-stranded cDNA through reverse transcription. The resulting cDNA molecules then underwent end polishing, A-tailing, adaptor ligation, size selection, amplification, and purification to generate sequencing-ready libraries. Libraries quantified at appropriate concentrations were pooled according to the desired sequencing depth and subjected to high-throughput sequencing on an Illumina platform.The raw sequencing reads were processed using fastp to remove low-quality sequences and adaptor contaminants. Reference genome sequences and annotation files were retrieved from public databases. After alignment, gene-level read counts were obtained with featureCounts (v2.0.6), and FPKM values were calculated based on gene length and mapped read numbers. Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 (v1.42.0). Multiple comparisons were controlled via the Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Genes with adjusted p-values ≤0.05 and absolute log2 fold changes of at least 1 were deemed significantly regulated.

Luciferase assays

Cells were plated in 12-well plates and allowed to adhere overnight. TOP/FOP Flash reporter plasmids were introduced into the cells using Lipofectamine 3000 following the manufacturer’s protocol. pRL-TK was included as an internal control to monitor transfection efficiency. After 48 h, cells were collected and analyzed for luciferase activity using a Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Abbkine, Wuhan, China). The firefly luciferase signals were normalized to the corresponding Renilla luciferase activity.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and mass spectrometry (MS)

Smart-CoIP™ CoIP Kit was used for Co IP experiments. After cell lysis, non-specific binding was first removed using Protein A/G, and then the target antibody and IgG control were added separately and incubated at 4°C. Add Protein A/G and incubate at room temperature for 2 h. The combined complex was washed and eluted before enzymatic hydrolysis, followed by separation on a reverse phase chromatography column and gradient elution on the EASY nLC 1200 system. The peptide segments were detected using an Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer, with a CV of −45 V in FAIMS mode and an MS/MS resolution of 30000 m/z.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Inoculate the cells into a confocal dish. After a 15-min fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde, cells were incubated for 1 h at room temperature in PBS containing 5% FBS and 0.3% Triton X-100. Primary antibody staining was performed overnight at 4 °C. On the following day, secondary antibody labeling was conducted for 2 h at room temperature in the dark. Finally, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 10 min at room temperature under light-protected conditions. Use FV3000 laser scanning confocal microscope (Olympus) to capture images.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (CHIP)

PureBinding® Chromatin immunoprecipitation Kit was used to investigate whether transcription factors can bind to the promoter of the target gene. Cross link the collected cells in 37% formaldehyde for 5 min. Neutralization of glycine stops cross-linking. Add a lysis buffer and fully lyse under ultrasound assistance. Add magnetic beads pre incubated with antibodies to the sample and incubate at room temperature for 1 h. After hot washing and purification, the target DNA fragment was obtained and subjected to qPCR detection.

Quantification and statistical analysis

According to the situation, use bilateral unpaired or paired Student’s t-tests to evaluate the statistical differences between the two groups. Multiple sets of data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA. The correlation was analyzed using Spearman method, and survival analysis was based on Kaplan Meier curve. The data was processed in GraphPad Prism 10.0, and p < 0.05 was considered a significant difference standard.
