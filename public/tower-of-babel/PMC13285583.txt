Response to Letter to the Editor
Authors: Joshua W. Pate, Rebecca Fechner, Scott D. Tagliaferri, Elan Schneider, Bruno Saragiotto
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank Daungsupawong and Wiwanitkit for their interest in our AI chatbot benchmarking paper and for their thoughtful comments.

We agree with the broad thrust of their letter. Our study was intended as a focused longitudinal benchmark using a single clinically meaningful prompt [1], building upon an earlier study with the same prompt, rather than a comprehensive test of all pediatric pain scenarios or real‐world user outcomes. This narrow design allowed us to examine change over time in responses to a stable prompt, but we recognize that broader evaluation is needed. As noted in our original discussion, important next steps include broader prompt sets, multi‐turn interactions, refined evaluation criteria, and end‐user testing with children and families.

We also agree that increasingly rigorous evaluation methods will be needed as generative AI systems continue to evolve. Although inter‐assessor reliability was reported in the original paper using prevalence‐adjusted and bias‐adjusted kappa, we agree with the broader point that this field will benefit from stronger and more clinically meaningful benchmarking approaches.

The letter also raises an important point about the interpretation of chatbot performance when explicit scoring criteria are provided. We agree this finding should be interpreted carefully. In our view, however, it is a useful result precisely because it suggests that structured guidance can improve response quality. It also highlights a methodological challenge for future research: once existing criteria produce near‐ceiling performance, benchmarks must be revised to test more complex, nuanced, and clinically consequential aspects of response quality.

Our subsequent studies are already extending this work through broader prompts, different scenarios, higher‐ceiling criteria, and voice‐based methods. We hope this next phase will help address several of the important issues raised in the letter.

We welcome the discussion and agree that continuous, open, and clinically grounded benchmarking will be essential as these technologies continue to rapidly develop.
