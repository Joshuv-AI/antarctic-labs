Quasisimple groups with a proper subgroup having the same vector orbits in characteristic 2 2
Chris Parker, B. G. Rodrigues

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime, G G be a finite group, H H a proper subgroup of G G and V V a finite dimensional GF ⁡ ( p ) ​ G \mathrm{GF}(p)G -module. The triple ( G , H , V ) (G,H,V) is immutable if and only if G G and H H have the same orbits on the vectors of V V . 1.
Introduction

Group theorists often say, “you know a group by its actions.”
In this paper, we examine this principle in the setting of quasisimple groups acting on vector spaces over
\mathrm{GF}(2)
where typically exceptions occur.
Specifically, we ask whether the orbit structure of a quasisimple group
G
on a
\mathrm{GF}(2)G
-module
V
provides a certificate of largeness, distinguishing
G
from its proper subgroups.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G
be a group,
H<G
, and
V
be a
\mathrm{GF}(p)G
-module.
We say that the triple
(G,H,V)
is
immutable
if and only if
G
and
H
have exactly the same orbits on
V
.

Equivalently (viewing
V
as a right
\mathrm{GF}(p)G
-module),
(G,H,V)
is immutable if and only if
vG=vH
for all
v\in V
. There are two extreme cases of immutability. The first possibility is the transitive case: if
H
acts transitively on the non-zero vectors
V^{\#}
of
V
, then
(G,H,V)
is immutable. The second possibility is the trivial case in which
V
is centralized by
G
in which case
G
cannot be distinguished from any of its subgroups.

The subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
which act transitively on
X^{\#}
have been completely determined by Huppert and Hering
[
18
,
17
]
(see also
[
20
, Section 7]
and the enumeration in
[
29
, Appendix 1]
). Hence, whenever we find that
H
acts transitively on
V^{\#}
, we may regard
(G,H,V)
as determined by this classification.

Our main results show that, apart from the transitive and trivial cases, immutable triples are rare when
G
is quasisimple and
p=2
. In particular, the orbit structure of a
\mathrm{GF}(2)G
-module almost always distinguishes
G
from its proper subgroups. In
[
30
]
, Liebeck explores a property related to immutability when
G
is a classical group acting on its natural module and
H
is a subgroup of
G
. These examples appear itemised as parts (i) , (ii) and (iii) of
A
. Our work extends
[
30
, Theorem]
as it does not specify the module
V
involved other than requiring that it is irreducible.
B
removes the irreducibility requirement and determines all immutable triples with
H
maximal and
V
a faithful
\mathrm{GF}(2)G
-module.

In the statement of
A
, the
natural module
for a classical group defined in characteristic
2
refers to that group’s usual module, regarded as a
\mathrm{GF}(2)G
-module.
Similarly, we use the standard terminology for

and

modules for orthogonal and symplectic groups in characteristic
2

For

\mathrm{GF}(2)G
-modules of dimension

2.
Immutability

In this section we catalogue various consequence of
(G,H,V)
being immutable. Let
p
be the characteristic of the field over which
V
is defined. Initially, we present results for an arbitrary prime
p
. We start with an elementary result which says that immutability leads to factorisations of
G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
V
is a
\mathrm{GF}(p)G
-module and
H<G
. Then, for
v\in V
,
vG=vH
if and only if
G=C_{G}(v)H
.

Proof.

This is just the Frattini Argument: Let
g\in G
, then
vg\in vG=vH
and so there exists
h\in H
such that
vh=vg
. Hence
gh^{-1}\in C_{G}(v)
and this yields
g=gh^{-1}h\in C_{G}(v)H
. Conversely, if
G=C_{G}(v)H
, then surely
vH=v{C_{G}(v)H}=vG
.
∎

For a
\mathrm{GF}(p)G
-module
V
and
v\in V
, we say that an orbit
vG
is
regular
provided
C_{G}(v)=1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(G,H,V)
is immutable. Then the following hold:

(i)

G
does not have a regular orbit on
V
.

(ii)

There exists
v\in V^{\#}
with
|G:C_{G}(v)|
prime to
p
and
G=C_{G}(v)H
.

(iii)

For
G>L>H
,
(G,H,V)
is immutable if and only if
(L,H,V|_{L})
and
(G,L,V)
are immutable.

Proof.

(i) Suppose that
v\in V
is a representative of a regular orbit. Then, by
Lemma

2.1
,
G=C_{G}(v)H=H
, which is a contradiction.

(ii) As
V
is a
\mathrm{GF}(p)G
-module, there exists
v\in V^{\#}
which is centralized by a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Hence
|G:C_{G}(v)|
is prime to
p
.

(iii) Let
v\in V
. Then
vH\subseteq vL\subseteq vG
. If

\displaystyle\frac{2.7!.n}{2n(2n-1)(2n-2)(2n-3)(2n-4)(2n-5)(2n-6)-8.7!.n^{3}}

Remark 2.23
