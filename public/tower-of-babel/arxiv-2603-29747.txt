Semilattice sums of algebras and Mal’tsev products of varieties Research of the first author was partially supported by the National Science Foundation under grant no. 1500235. The second author’s research was supported by the Warsaw University of Technology under grant number 504/04259/1120.
T. Penza, A.B. Romanowska

Abstract
. The Mal’tsev product of two varieties of similar algebras is always a quasivariety. We consider the question of when this quasivariety is a variety. The main result asserts that if 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is a strongly irregular variety with no nullary operations and at least one non-unary operation, and 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is the variety, of the same type as 𝒱 \mathcal{V} , equivalent to the variety of semilattices, then the Mal’tsev product 𝒱 ∘ 𝒮 \mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S} is a variety. It consists precisely of semilattice sums of algebras in 𝒱 \mathcal{V} . We derive an equational base for the product from an equational base for 𝒱 \mathcal{V} . However, if 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is a regular variety, then the Mal’tsev product may not be a variety. We discuss various applications of the main result, and examine some detailed representations of algebras in 𝒱 ∘ 𝒮 \mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S} .

1.
Background

Let
\tau\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a similarity type of
\Omega
-algebras with
no nullary operation symbols and containing at least one non-unary operation symbol. Such
a similarity type is called
plural

[
24
]
. For a positive integer
n

T_{n}
be the set of all
n
-ary
\Omega
-terms containing precisely
n
different
variables. They may be denoted
x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n}
or by some other letters like

or

z

Definition 1.1
.

An identity is called
regular
if precisely the same variables appear on both
sides. At the opposite extreme, an identity of the form
t(x,y)=x
, for some
t\in T_{2}

is called
strongly irregular.
A variety is regular if it satisfies only regular
identities. It is strongly irregular if it satisfies at least one strongly irregular
identity.

Let
t\in T_{2}
. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{t}
the variety defined by the
single identity
t(x,y)=x
. We use the notation
\mathcal{V}_{t}
to indicate a typical subvariety
of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
, that is, an arbitrary variety satisfying the strongly irregular identity

t(x,y)=x
. We often find it convenient to write
x\cdot y
(or
x\cdot_{t}y
) in place of

t(x,y)
.

Let us note that the variety of semilattices satifies all the regular identities
of the type with one binary operation.

Definition 1.2
.

The variety
\mathcal{S}
of
\Omega
-semilattices
is defined to be the variety of

\Omega
-algebras satisfying all regular identities.

It is fundamental to the subject that in a plural similarity type,
\mathcal{S}
is the unique
variety that is equivalent to semilattices. Fix any
t\in T_{2}
and write
x\cdot y
in
place of
t(x,y)
. Then for any
S\in\mathcal{S}
, the algebra
\langle S,\cdot\rangle
is a
semilattice. Conversely, for any basic
n
-ary operation symbol
\omega\in\Omega
we
have that

x_{1}\dots x_{n}\omega=x_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot x_{n}

(1.1)

holds in
S
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety of
\Omega
-algebras.
An
\Omega
-algebra

is a

semilattice sum

-algebras if

-algebra.

is a disjoint sum of these

, where the

\varrho

S
is (isomorphic to)

. The class of

\mathcal{V}
-algebras forms the Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
of the

and

, and is known to be a quasivariety. (See Mal’tsev
[
15
,
16
]

]

Whenever

-algebras, it suffices to take

[

]

\mathcal{V}
-subalgebras (see
[
24

In this paper we are especially interested in semilattice sums of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
-algebras. Since

is a strongly irregular variety, each algebra

\mathcal{T}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}

of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
-algebras

The variety

contains other irregular varieties. In particular, if

is a
subvariety of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
, then it is known that

is defined by a set

consisting of a set

. (See e.g.
[
21
]
and
[
24

.) The Mal’tsev product

-subalgebras. Moreover it contains

and

of

[

2.
Some varieties related to the quasivariety
\mathcal{T}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}

Recall that, for
x\cdot y=t(x,y)\in T_{2}
, the subvariety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
is defined by a set
\Sigma_{t}
consisting of the identity
x\cdot y=x
and a set of regular identities
\Gamma_{t}
. The best known subvariety of
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
, different from
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{S}
, is the regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
of the variety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
.
We will briefly recall the basic facts concerning the regularization.

The variety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
can be viewed as a category in which the morphisms are simply the
homomorphisms between
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras. Any ordered set,
S
, can be viewed as a category
whose objects are the elements of the set. If
r,s\in S
then there is a unique morphism
from
r
to
s
if
r\geq s
. Otherwise there are no morphisms from
r
to
s
. Of course,
any semilattice can be ordered by considering the basic operation to be the infimum.

Suppose that
S
is a semilattice and there is a covariant functor

F\colon S\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{t};\ (r\geq s)\mapsto(\varphi_{r,s}\colon A_{r}\rightarrow A_{s}).

Then the disjoint sum
\bigsqcup_{s\in S}A_{s}
may be viewed as the semilattice-ordered system

\bigl(\{A_{s}\mid s\in S\};\,\{\varphi_{r,s}\mid r,s\in S,r\geq s\}\bigr),

(2.1)

where the
\Omega
-operations on the sum are defined by

\omega\colon A_{s_{1}}\times\dots\times A_{s_{n}}\rightarrow A_{s};(a_{s_{1}},\dots,a_{s_{n}})\mapsto a_{s_{1}}\varphi_{s_{1},s}\dotsm a_{s_{n}}\varphi_{s_{n},s}\,\omega

for each (
n
-ary)
\omega\in\Omega
, where each
s_{i}\in S
and

s=s_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot s_{n}
. The resulting semilattice sum is called the
Płonka sum
of the algebras
A_{s}
, and is denoted by
\sum_{s\in S}A_{s}
. (See
[
19
]
,

[
21
]
,
[
24
, Ch. 4]
.)

It is a remarkable fact that the class of Płonka sums of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras forms the

regularization

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
of the variety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
. The class
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
is the join (in
the lattice of varieties) of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}
. Thus it satisfies precisely the regular
identities true in
\mathcal{V}_{t}
. Its axiomatization follows from Płonka’s theorem (see

[
21
]
,
[
24
, Ch. 4]
). The variety
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
is defined by the regular identities

\Gamma_{t}
of
\Sigma_{t}
and the following identities (P1) – (P5):

(P1)

x\cdot x=x
,

(P2)

x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(x\cdot y)\cdot z
,

(P3)

x\cdot(y\cdot z)=x\cdot(z\cdot y)
,

(P4)

y\cdot x_{1}\dots x_{n}\omega=y\cdot x_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot x_{n}
,

(P5)

x_{1}\dots x_{n}\omega\cdot y=(x_{1}\cdot y)\dots(x_{n}\cdot y)\,\omega,

where
\omega
ranges through all symbols in
\Omega
. Let us note that if
\Gamma_{t}
is
empty, then the regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
becomes the regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{t}}
, and is defined by
the identities (P1) – (P5).

By results of
[
4
]
it is known that the quasi-regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{q}_{t}
of
\mathcal{V}_{t}

consists of Płonka sums of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras as in (
2.1
) in which the morphisms

\varphi_{r,s}
are all injective. It coincides with the class

\mathsf{I}\mathsf{S}\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{V}_{t}\cup\mathcal{S})
of (isomorphic copies of) subalgebras
of products of algebras in
\mathcal{V}_{t}\cup\mathcal{S}
, and is defined, relative to
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
, by the quasi-identity

(x\cdot y=x\mathbin{\&}y\cdot x=y\mathbin{\&}x\cdot z=z\cdot x=z\mathbin{\&}y\cdot z=z\cdot y=z)\rightarrow(x=y).

The congruence
\varrho
of a
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
-algebra
A
providing its decomposition into the Płonka sum of its
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-summands can be obtained explicitly as

\varrho=\{(a,b)\mid a\cdot b=a\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,b\cdot a=b\}.

(2.2)

Now, let
\overline{\mathcal{T}_{t}}
denote the variety defined by the identities (P1) – (P4)
above. It follows immediately that
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{T}_{t}}
. For a subvariety

\mathcal{V}_{t}
of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
, let

be defined by

together with (P1)
– (P4). As observed by C. Bergman and D. Failing

. That is to say, each algebra

is a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-subalgebras. Moreover, the

) as is the case for the

\overline{\mathcal{V}_{t}}
-algebras are not necessarily Płonka

\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras, though the reduct

of each

-algebra

of the left-zero band reducts

the

of the variety
\mathcal{V}_{t}

If the operation

of an

The third variety we are interested in will play an essential role in our main results.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma
be an identity of type
\tau
of the form

where not all the variables

necessarily appear in

and

.

For each

, define

\displaystyle\sigma^{p}_{m}=\bigl\{\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}r_{1}\ldots\mathbf{x}r_{n}u=\mathbf{x}r_{1}\ldots\mathbf{x}r_{n}v\mid

\displaystyle r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\in T_{m},\mathbf{x}r_{i}=x_{1}\ldots x_{m}r_{i}\,\bigr\}.

Let
\sigma^{p}
be the union of all

for

. For a set

identities, define its

to be

\Sigma^{p}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma^{p}.

The intent of Definition

is that, for every positive integer

, each

in

is replaced by a term of the form

. However, since

, each term

must contain precisely all of the

. Thus even if the identity

is irregular, every member

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety of
\Omega
-algebras, and let
\operatorname{Id}(\mathcal{V})
be the
set of all equations that hold in
\mathcal{V}
. We define

,

denoted

, to be the variety of all algebras satisfying

. Put succinctly,

{\mathcal{V}}^{p}=\operatorname{Mod}(\operatorname{Id}({\mathcal{V})}^{p})
.

In fact, in this definition, it suffices to replace
\operatorname{Id}(\mathcal{V})
by any
equational base for

. This will follow from Corollary

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\sigma
be an identity that holds in a variety
\mathcal{V}
. Then the quasivariety
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
satisfies the identities of
\sigma^{p}
.

Proof.

Assume
\sigma
is as in Definition
2.1
and let
A
be in

\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
with semilattice replica congruence
\varrho
. Let

be a positive
integer and

. Choose

and write

\mathbf{a}r_{i}
as a shorthand for

. We must show that

\mathbf{a}r_{1}\ldots\mathbf{a}r_{n}u=\mathbf{a}r_{1}\ldots\mathbf{a}r_{n}v

.

For any

, the identity

is

is a semilattice, it must satisfy this identity. Hence

Thus all of the elements

, lie in the same congruence

, so it satisfies the

. From this we obtain

as desired.

Proposition 2.4

For any variety

3.
Free algebras in varieties
{\mathcal{V}}^{p}

Basic properties of free algebras
in sub(quasi)varieties of
{\mathcal{V}}^{p}
follow from basic properties of free algebras in
prevarieties of
\Omega
-algebras (i.e. classes of similar algebras closed under subalgebras
and direct products) as given for example in

If
\mathcal{Q}
and

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
are two subquasivarieties with

-replica of the algebra

. (See also

(3.1)

Recall also that the free semilattice

over

is isomorphic to the semilattice

of all finite non-empty subsets of

under union.

For an
\Omega
-term

will denote the set of variables that actually
appear in

. Suppose that
\mathcal{V}
is a regular variety and let

be an element of

. There is a term

so that

. If

the identity

holds in

. By regularity we must have

, which we can take
as a definition of

. As is customary, we often blur the distinction between
an element of the free algebra and a representing term.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a regular variety and let
\varrho
be the
semilattice replica congruence of the free algebra
X\mathcal{V}
over

. Then for

(w,v)\in\varrho\iff\mathsf{var}(w)=\mathsf{var}(v).

Proof.

Let

defined by

(w,v)\in\theta\iff\mathsf{var}(w)=\mathsf{var}(v).

It is easy to see that the relation

is a congruence relation, and is the kernel of
the homomorphism

h\colon X\mathcal{V}\rightarrow P_{f}(X);\ x_{1}\dots x_{n}u\mapsto\mathsf{var}(u)

onto the semilattice of all finite subsets of

under union. Now by universality of
replication (see
[

, Lemma 3.3.1]
), it follows that there is a unique homomorphism

such that the composition

equals

. In particular, under this composition

u\mapsto u/\varrho\mapsto\mathsf{var}(u)
. By (

),

is an

\theta=\varrho
.
∎

In particular, for any variety
\mathcal{V}
, the variety
{\mathcal{V}}^{p}
is regular. Thus

X{\mathcal{V}}^{p}/\varrho\cong X\mathcal{S}.

(3.2)

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety with equational base

. Let

be the variety defined
by the prolongation
{\Sigma}^{p}
(see Definition

of

is

.

In particular, by taking

Proof.

By (

is isomorphic to
S=X\mathcal{S}
. We

satisfies the identities of

\Sigma

of

\displaystyle(x_{1}\dots x_{m}r_{1})\,\ldots\,(x_{1}\dots x_{m}r_{n})u=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\dots x_{m}r_{1})\,\ldots\,(x_{1}\dots x_{m}r_{n})v=a_{1}\dots a_{n}v,

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety with equational base

Let

Corollary 3.4

The variety

Lemma 4.1

Let

be a congruence of the free

-algebra

. Assume that there is a pair

of elements of

, where

and any

(b_{s},b_{s}a_{r})\in\theta.

Proof.

From our assumptions,
b_{s},b^{\prime}_{s}\in A_{s}
,
a_{r},a^{\prime}_{r}\in A_{r}
and
(b_{s}a^{\prime}_{r}),(a_{r}b^{\prime}_{s})\in A_{rs}
. Each of
A_{r}
,

, and

lie in

. Consequently

b_{s}=b_{s}b^{\prime}_{s},\quad a_{r}=a_{r}a^{\prime}_{r},\quad(b_{s}a^{\prime}_{r})(a_{r}b^{\prime}_{s})=b_{s}a^{\prime}_{r}.

Combining these equalities with the assumption that
b^{\prime}_{s}\mathrel{\theta}a^{\prime}_{r}
we obtain

b_{s}a_{r}=(b_{s}b^{\prime}_{s})(a_{r}a^{\prime}_{r})\mathrel{\theta}(b_{s}a^{\prime}_{r})(a_{r}b^{\prime}_{s})=(b_{s}a^{\prime}_{r})\mathrel{\theta}(b_{s}b^{\prime}_{s})=b_{s}.

∎

Suppose

in

and each

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

-algebra and let

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\theta
be a congruence of the free
{\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra

, and

be the semilattice replica congruence of

\theta\vee\varrho=\theta\circ\varrho\circ\theta.

(4.1)

Proof.

Suppose that

a_{r}\ \varrho\ a^{\prime}_{r}\ \theta\ b^{\prime}_{s}\ \varrho\ b_{s}.

Since

and

lie in

, and

is commutative

Therefore

\varrho\circ\theta\circ\varrho\,\subseteq\,\theta\circ\varrho\circ\theta.

By Lemma
4.3
, it follows that

\theta\vee\varrho=\theta\circ\varrho\circ\theta.

∎

Let
\theta
be a congruence of a
{\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra

, and
\varrho
the

. Let

be the join of the congruences

and

. It is well known that

, and since

is a member of the variety

of

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\theta
be a congruence of a
{\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra

, and let
\psi=\theta\vee\varrho
. Then a congruence class

of

if and only if, for any

(a_{r},b_{s})\in\psi\implies(a_{r}b_{s},a_{r})\in\theta.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let

and

be members of

. Then

(a_{r}/\theta,b_{s}/\theta)\in\psi/\theta\,\,\iff\,\,(a_{r},b_{s})\in\psi.

On the other hand

Hence the assertion that

is equivalent to (

).
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\theta
be a congruence of the free
{\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra
A=X{\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}

over
X
, and set

. Then the quotient

of

is
a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
-subalgebras. In particular,
\psi/\theta
is the

, and

-classes

as

ranges over

.

Proof.

Since

, it follows that

. Thus we only need to show

, the congruence class

So our aim now is to verify the implication (

). For

and

, let

. By Lemma

Thus there are elements

Let us note that

is isomorphic to the semilattice sum of the

of

, where each

Each element of

Problem 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a proper regular variety of groupoids. Is it true that
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}

fails to be a variety?

With regard to this latter problem, let us note that, with
\mathcal{G}
denoting the variety
of all groupoids,
\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{G}
, which is obviously a variety. Thus the requirement that
\mathcal{V}
be a proper subvariety is necessary.

5.
The Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}

Now let
\mathcal{V}_{t}
be a (strongly irregular) subvariety of the variety
\mathcal{T}_{t}
of

\Omega
-algebras defined by the identity
t(x,y)=x
for some

and a set

\Gamma_{t}
of regular identities. Recall from Section
2
that the

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
of

is defined by
\Gamma_{t}
and the identities (P1) –
(P5), with

, and consists of Płonka sums of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras. Moreover

\mathcal{T}_{t}\wedge\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}=\mathcal{V}_{t}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\mathcal{T}_{t}\vee\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{t}}.

Since every Płonka sum is a semilattice sum,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
. On the
other hand,
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{T}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
. And by

2.4
,
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
. Finally, by
Theorem
4.7
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}={\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}
. Summarizing

\mathcal{V}_{t}\,\subseteq\,\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}\,\subseteq\,\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}\,\subseteq{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}\,\subseteq\,\mathcal{T}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}={\mathcal{T}_{t}}^{p}.

And by Corollary
3.4
, the variety
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
is the smallest variety
containing the quasivariety
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
.

Recall also that, by (
3.2
),

X{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}/\varrho\cong X\mathcal{S},

and for any two
\Omega
-terms

(w,v)\in\varrho\iff\mathsf{var}(w)=\mathsf{var}(v).

As a direct consequence of Proposition
3.2
one obtains the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1
.

For any subvariety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
, the free
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra

B=X{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
is a semilattice of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras. In particular,

B=\bigsqcup_{s\in S}B_{s}
, where

is the free semilattice over
X

\mathcal{V}_{t}

B=X{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}\in\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}.

We intend to show that the classes
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
and
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
coincide.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\delta
be a congruence of the free
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
-algebra
B=X{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}

over
X
. Then the quotient
C=B/\delta
is a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras.

Proof.

We first use the fact that
C
is the quotient
B/\delta
of
B
to describe the
decomposition of
C
into a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{T}_{t}
-algebras in terms of
the algebra
B
.
Let
\varrho_{B}
be the semilattice replica congruence of the algebra
B
, and let

\psi=\delta\vee\varrho_{B}
. Since
\psi\geq\varrho_{B}
it follows that
B/\psi
is a
semilattice. On the other hand, the minimality of
\varrho_{B}
guarantees that
\psi
is
the smallest congruence above
\delta
such that
B/\psi\in\mathcal{S}
. It follows that

\psi/\delta
is the semilattice replica congruence of
B/\delta
.
Since each element of
C
is of the form
b/\delta
for some
b\in B

Theorem
4.7
implies that

C=\bigsqcup_{b/\delta\in B/\delta}(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)

in which each summand
(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)\in\mathcal{T}_{t}
.

On the other hand, by Corollary
5.1
,
B=\bigsqcup_{s\in S}B_{s}
, in which

S
is a semilattice and all

are members of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
.

It remains to show that the
(\psi/\delta)
-delta classes,
(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)
,
satisfy the identities of
\Gamma_{t}
. Let

x_{1}\ldots x_{k}u=x_{1}\ldots x_{k}v

(5.1)

be a typical member of
\Gamma_{t}
.
We want to show that (

) holds in each
\psi/\delta
-class. This means that
for any
c^{1}=b^{1}/\delta,\ldots,c^{k}=b^{k}/\delta\in(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)
, with

b^{i}\in B_{s_{i}}
and
b\in B
, we have

c^{1}\ldots c^{k}u=c^{1}\ldots c^{k}v,

(5.2)

which is equivalent to

(b^{1}\ldots b^{k}u,b^{1}\ldots b^{k}v)\,\in\,\delta.

(5.3)

Note that
b^{i}/\delta,b^{j}/\delta\in(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)
if and only if

(b^{i},b^{j})\in\psi
. Since each
(b^{i}/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)
satisfies the identity

x\cdot y=x
, we may use Lemma
4.5
repeatedly to obtain the following:

b^{i}\ \delta\ b^{i}\cdot b^{1}\ \delta\ (b^{i}\cdot b^{1})\cdot b^{2}\ \delta\ ((b^{i}\cdot b^{1})\cdot b^{2})\cdot b^{3}\ \delta\dots{\\
}\delta\ (\ldots((b^{i}\cdot b^{1})\cdot b^{2})\cdot\ldots)\cdot b^{k}\ =:d^{i}.

Thus
b^{i}\ \delta\ d^{i}
for each

of

that

d^{1}\ldots d^{k}u=d^{1}\ldots d^{k}v.

Finally note that

(b^{1}\ldots b^{k}u,d^{1}\ldots d^{k}u)\in\delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad(b^{1}\ldots b^{k}v,d^{1}\ldots d^{k}v)\in\delta,

which implies

(b^{1}\ldots b^{k}u,b^{1}\ldots b^{k}v)\in\delta.

This verifies (

∎

Let us note that in particular,
\psi/\delta
is the semilattice replica congruence of

and the quotient

is the semilattice sum of
\psi/\delta
-classes
(b/\delta)/(\psi/\delta)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{t}
be a variety satisfying the identity
t(x,y)=x
and with equational base

\Sigma
. Then the Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
is a variety, having

\Sigma_{t}^{p}
as an equational base.

Proof.

We have already observed that
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
. Conversely, if
an algebra

is a member of
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
, then there is a set

so that

is a
homomorphic image of
X{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
. Then by Proposition

, the algebra

\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
. Thus the quasivariety
\mathcal{V}_{t}\circ\mathcal{S}
and the

{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
coincide.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be any variety of
\Omega

of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}

Problem 5.5
.

Does Theorem
5.3
hold for an irregular variety which is not strongly irregular?

6.
Examples and counterexamples

First consider the type
\tau
with one binary multiplication
\cdot
, as in
Sections
4.1
and
4.2
. We could observe that the Mal’tsev products

\mathcal{LZ}\circ\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{S}
considered in these examples do not satisfy the
associative law. This is however an instance of a more general phenomenon. Consider a
variety of plural type
\tau
, and assume that the equational base
\Sigma
of a
variety
\mathcal{V}
contains a (non-trivial) linear identity
\sigma
. (An identity is

linear
if the multiplicities of each variable on each side are at most
1
. This is
not to be confused with the notion of linear identity as it appears in
[
10
]
.) Then
the identities of
\sigma^{p}
obtained from
\sigma
are never linear, since each variable
of
\sigma
was replaced in
\sigma^{p}
by a term
r_{i}\in T_{m}
with
m\geq 2
, and all of
these terms contain the same sets of variables. Moreover, consequences of the identities

\Sigma^{p}
are never linear.

This can be summarised as follows.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any non-trivial variety
\mathcal{V}
, the Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
does not
satisfy any (non-trivial) linear identity.

In particular, for no variety
\mathcal{V}
of semigroups is the Mal’tsev product

\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
a class of semigroups. A similar observation can be made for many
familiar varieties, for example groups, rings, lattices, semilattices etc.

Frequently more interesting are proper subvarieties of
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
.

Example 6.2

(
Bands
)
.

As already mentioned in Section
4.1
, each band is
a semilattice sum of rectangular bands. (See
[
18
]
and
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.)
Let
\mathcal{SG}
be the variety of semigroups,
\mathcal{B}
the variety of bands, and

\mathcal{RB}
the variety of rectangular bands. Then, by Corollary
5.4
,

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{RB}\circ_{\mathcal{SG}}\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{SG}\cap(\mathcal{RB}\circ\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{SG}\cap{\mathcal{RB}}^{p}.

Note that
{\mathcal{RB}}^{p}
is a variety of groupoids, but is not a variety of semigroups.

In the following examples, the (pseudo-)partition operation
x\cdot y
will be denoted by

t(x,y)
to avoid conflict with the basic operation of multiplication in the algebras
considered.

Example 6.3

(
Birkhoff systems
)
.

Lattices may be defined as algebras
(A,+,\cdot)
with two semilattice reducts, the
(join-)semilattice
(A,+)
and the (meet-)semilattice
(A,\cdot)
, satisfying the

Birkhoff identity

x+xy=x(x+y)
and the
absorption law

t(x,y)=x+xy=x
. They form the variety
\mathcal{L}
of lattices. By dropping the absorption law one
obtains the definition of the more general variety
\mathcal{BS}
of
Birkhoff
systems
. (See
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.) An essential role in this variety is
played by two
3
-element bi-chains
\mathbf{3}_{m}
and
\mathbf{3}_{j}
built on the set
\{0,1,2\}
by defining the meet-semilattice order as
0<_{\cdot}1<_{\cdot}2
and the
join-semilattice order as
0<_{+}2<_{+}1
in
\mathbf{3}_{m}
and as
1<_{+}0<_{+}2
in

\mathbf{3}_{j}
. Let
\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})
denote the class of Birkhoff systems which do not
contain either
\mathbf{3}_{m}
or
\mathbf{3}_{j}
as subalgebras.
It was shown in
[
9
]
that the class
\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})
is
a variety, and is characterized as precisely the class of Birkhoff systems which are
semilattice sums of lattices. It follows that

\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})=\mathcal{L}\circ_{\mathcal{BS}}\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{BS}\cap(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{BS}\cap{\mathcal{L}}^{p}.

Note that none of the basic operations of
{\mathcal{L}}^{p}
-algebras are associative or commutative.

As already mentioned, for a strongly irregular variety
\mathcal{V}_{t}
, the best known
and understood subvariety of
{\mathcal{V}_{t}}^{p}
(different from
\mathcal{V}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}
) is the
regularization,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
, of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
. Much less is known about the pseudo-regularization. It
is an easy exercise to show that the regularization and the pseudo-regularization of the
variety
\mathcal{LZ}
of left-zero bands coincide. However it is possible for the
regularization of a variety to be distinct from its pseudo-regularization. The first
example was discovered in
[
3
]
, and investigated in connection with the constraint
satisfaction problem.

Example 6.4

(
Steiner quasigroups
)
.

A
Steiner quasigroup
or
squag
is a commutative idempotent groupoid
satisfying the identity
t(x,y)=xy\cdot y=x
. The variety formed by these groupoids
is denoted by
\mathcal{SQ}
. As shown in
[
3
]
, the regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{SQ}}
of

\mathcal{SQ}
is the variety of commutative and idempotent groupoids
satisfying the identity
x(x\cdot yz)=(x\cdot xy)z
. The pseudo-regularization

\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}
of
\mathcal{SQ}
is defined to be the variety of commutative idempotent groupoids
satisfying the identities (P1) – (P4) from Section
2
, where
\cdot
is replaced by

t(x,y)=xy\cdot y
. Groupoids in the regularization are Płonka sums of
squags. Groupoids in the pseudo-regularizations are semilattice sums of squags. As was
shown in
[
3
]
, the variety of commutative idempotent groupoids
satisfying the identity
x(y\cdot yz)=(xy\cdot y)z
(called
\mathcal{T}_{2}
in
that paper) is contained in the variety

\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}
, and is different from
\widetilde{\mathcal{SQ}}
. It follows
that the three varieties
\mathcal{SQ}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{SQ}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}
are
distinct:

\mathcal{SQ}\,\subset\,\widetilde{\mathcal{SQ}}\,\subset\,\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}.

However we do not know if the varieties
\mathcal{T}_{2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}
coincide,
though we think it is not likely. The Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{SQ}\circ\mathcal{S}
coincides with
the variety
{\mathcal{SQ}}^{p}
. Since commutativity is a linear identity, members of the
variety
{\mathcal{SQ}}^{p}
are not commutative. The pseudo-regularization

\overline{\mathcal{SQ}}
is obviously contained in but not equal to
{\mathcal{SQ}}^{p}
.

In fact, Examples
6.3
and
6.4
were the inspiration for
undertaking investigations of the most general class of algebras representable as
semilattice sums of their subalgebras.

Examples of other varieties of algebras with distinct regularization and
pseudo-regularization are provided by bisemilattices, algebras with two semilattice
operations (one interpreted as a join and one as a meet, similarly as in the case of
Birkhoff systems.)

Example 6.5

(
Semilattice sums of lattices
)
.

First recall that members of the class
\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})
of Birkhoff
systems considered above are all semilattice sums of lattices. However, it is
not difficult to check that this class is not the pseudo-regularization of the variety

\mathcal{L}
of lattices. The partition operation
t(x,y)=x+xy
of the regularization

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is not a pseudo-partition operation for

\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})
. Indeed, an easy exercise shows that the requirements for
a pseudo-partition operation are not satisfied. A witness is a certain
3
-element
member of
\mathcal{BS}^{-}(\mathbf{3}_{m},\mathbf{3}_{j})
. This is the bichain
\mathbf{3}_{n}
built on the
set
\{0,1,2\}
with the meet-semilattice order defined as for
\mathbf{3}_{m}
and

\mathbf{3}_{j}
and the join-semilattice order defined by
2<_{+}0<_{+}1
. Note that

\mathbf{3}_{n}
is the semilattice sum of the
2
-element lattice
\{0,1\}
and the

1
-element lattice
\{2\}
, but it is not a Płonka sum of these lattices. (See

[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.)

Now let
A
be a subdirectly irreducible lattice and set
A_{1}=A
. Let
A_{0}
be a
1
-element
lattice
\{\infty\}
disjoint from
A_{1}
. Finally let
A^{\infty}
be the Płonka
sum of
A_{1}
and
A_{0}
over the
2
-element meet semilattice
\{0,1\}
with
0<1
. Then
the bisemilattice
A^{\infty}
generates the regularization of the variety
\mathsf{V}(A)

generated by
A
. (See e.g.
[
24
, Ch. 4]
.)

There is another interesting family of semilattice sums obtained from the lattice
A
in
the case when
A
is a bounded lattice. Once again set
A_{1}=A
and denote the bounds of

A
by
0_{A}
and
1_{A}
. We define the bisemilattice
A_{\infty}=A\cup\{\infty\}
as
follows. For an
a\in A
,
a\cdot\infty=0_{A}
and
a+\infty=1_{A}
. It is easy to see
that this yields a bisemilattice, and is a semilattice sum of the lattices
A_{1}
and
A_{0}

over the meet semilattice
\{0,1\}
with
1<0
. A somewhat more complicated exercise
shows that the operation
t(x,y)=x+xy
is a pseudo-partition operation on the algebra

A_{\infty}
, but it is not a partition operation. It follows that each variety

\mathsf{V}(A_{\infty})
is contained in the pseudo-regularization of
\mathcal{L}
but is
different from the regularization of the variety
\mathsf{V}(A)
. We do not know if the
varieties
\mathsf{V}(A_{\infty})
and
\overline{\mathsf{V}(A)}
coincide.

Example 6.6

(
Groups
)
.

Groups may be defined as algebras
(G,\cdot,^{-1})
satisfying the identities

xy\cdot z=x\cdot yz
,
y^{-1}y\cdot x=x\cdot yy^{-1}
, and

x\cdot yy^{-1}=x
. They form a strongly irregular variety with

t(x,y)=x\cdot yy^{-1}
, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{GP}
. The regularization

\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}
of
\mathcal{GP}
may be defined by the first two identities defining
\mathcal{GP}
and
the identities
(xy)^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1},\,(x^{-1})^{-1}=x
and
xxx^{-1}=x
. (See e.g.
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
.) The variety
\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}
consists of Płonka sums of groups. In semigroup theory, Płonka sums of groups are called

strong semilattices of groups
. By results of Clifford
[
5
]
, it is known
that the class of Płonka sums of groups coincides with the class of semigroups which
are semilattices of groups and with the class of so-called
Clifford semigroups
,
semigroups with a unary operation
-1
satisfying the identities

(x^{-1})^{-1}=x,xx^{-1}x=x,xx^{-1}=x^{-1}x
and

(xx^{-1})(yy^{-1})=(yy^{-1})(xx^{-1})
. (See also
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
and

[
12
]
.) The class
\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}
is a subvariety of the variety
\mathcal{IS}
of
inverse semigroups, semigroups with one unary operation
-1
satisfying the last
three identities defining
\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}
. Members of
\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}
are precisely inverse
semigroups which are Płonka sums of groups (or as semigroup theorists would say,
strong semilattices of groups). (See e.g.
[
21
]
and references there.) In fact,

\widetilde{\mathcal{GP}}=\mathcal{IS}\cap(\mathcal{GP}\circ\mathcal{S}).

Groups defined as algebras with two basic operations
\cdot
and
-1
as above do not
contain any nullary operations in the similarity type. Hence Theorem
5.3
applies, and the
Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{GP}\circ\mathcal{S}
is the variety
{\mathcal{GP}}^{p}
. Note that

{\mathcal{GP}}^{p}
is a variety of groupoids with one unary operation, but it is not a variety
of semigroups. The pseudo-regularization of
\mathcal{GP}
has not yet been investigated.

The assumption that the type of a variety
\mathcal{V}
, in a Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
,
has no nullary operation symbols is due to the fact that semilattices have no nullary
operations. However, if
\mathcal{V}
is a variety with constant (nullary) operations, then the
latter assumption may easily be omitted, as it was done in the example above. Instead of a
constant operation one may take a unary operation defining this constant, and consider an
equivalent variety
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
with such a unary operation replacing each constant
operation. Then if
\mathcal{V}
is strongly irregular, Theorem
5.3
applies, and the
Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{S}
is a variety.

If
\mathcal{V}
is an irregular, but not strongly irregular variety,
then the class of Płonka sums of
\mathcal{V}
-algebras does not necessarily coincide with the
regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of
\mathcal{V}
. (See
[
24
, Ch. 4]
and
[
21
]
for more
information and some references.) The following example concerns the case of semigroups.

Example 6.7

(
Semigroups
)
.

It was shown by V. N. Saliǐ
[
25
,
26
]
that each semigroup in the
regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
of an irregular variety
\mathcal{V}
of semigroups embeds into a
Płonka sum of
\mathcal{V}
-algebras. (This however is not true for general algebras, see

[
7
]
and
[
22
]
.) Hence in the case of semigroups the
regularization
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
is contained in the Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{V}\circ\mathcal{S}
. However not all of its members are Płonka sums of
\mathcal{V}
-algebras.

Example 6.8
.

This example comes from the geometry of affine spaces and convex sets. (See
[
24
]

and references there.) Affine spaces over a subfield
F
of the field
\mathbb{R}
of reals
(affine
F
-spaces) may be defined as algebras
(A,\underline{F})
, where

\underline{F}
is the set of binary operations

xy\underline{p}=\underline{p}(x,y)=x(1-p)+yp
, for all
p\in F
. Affine
F
-spaces
defined in this way form a variety. Convex subsets of affine
F
-spaces (convex

F
-sets) may be defined as algebras
(C,\underline{I}^{o})
, where

\underline{I}^{o}=\bigl\{\underline{p}\mid p\in I^{o}=]0,1[\bigr\}
and
]0,1[

is the open unit interval of
F
. Fix a subfield
F
of
\mathbb{R}
. Then the convex

F
-subsets of affine
F
-spaces generate the variety
\mathcal{BA}
of
barycentric
algebras
. Convex
F
-sets form a subquasivariety
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{BA}
. The variety

\mathcal{BA}
is defined by the following identities

\displaystyle xx\underline{p}=x,

\displaystyle xy\,\underline{p}=yx\,\underline{1-p},

\displaystyle xy\,\underline{p}\ z\,\underline{q}=x\,\,yz\,\underline{q/(p\circ q)}\,\,\underline{p\circ q}

for all
p,q
in
I^{o}
. Here
p\,\circ\,q=p+q-pq
.
(See
[
24
, §5.8]
.)
Its subquasivariety
\mathcal{C}
is defined by the cancellation laws

(xy\underline{p}=xz\underline{p})\rightarrow(y=z),

(6.1)

which hold for all
p\in I^{o}
. It is known
[
24
, §7.5]
that each barycentric
algebra is a semilattice sum of (open) convex sets. This shows that
\mathcal{BA}
is contained
in the Mal’tsev product
\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{S}
:

\mathcal{BA}\subset\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{S}\subset{\mathcal{BA}}^{p}.

Since the quasivariety
\mathcal{C}
satisfies linear identities, the varieties
\mathcal{BA}
and

{\mathcal{BA}}^{p}
are distinct.

In fact, a stronger result was proved, which we provide here in a somewhat reformulated way.

Theorem 6.9
.

[
24
, §7.5]

Each barycentric algebra is a semilattice sum of open convex sets over its semilattice
replica, and is a subalgebra of a Płonka sum of convex sets over its semilattice
replica.

The summands of the Płonka sum are certain canonical extensions of the
summands of the semilattice sum.

7.
Concluding remarks and the problem of representation of algebras in Mal’tsev products

The fact that an algebra
A
is a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-subalgebras says very little
about its detailed structure. An exception is of course given by algebras represented as
Płonka sums. (The second best representation is given by subalgebras of Płonka sums.)
So it is natural to ask how the summands of a semilattice sum are put together or how to
reconstruct the algebraic structure of a semilattice sum
A=\bigsqcup_{s\in S}A_{s}

from its
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-summands
A_{s}
and the quotient semilattice
S
. Such a construction
exists and was introduced for general algebras in
[
23
, §6.2]
, under the name of a
Lallement sum, as a generalization of a Płonka sum. (See also
[
14
]
for a basic
construction introduced in the case of semigroups, and
[
22
]
,
[
21
]
and

[
24
, Ch. 4]
for similar constructions in the case of general algebras.) The primary
idea was to relax the
requirement
of functoriality in the definition of Płonka
sums. There are several types of such constructions and the definition may be formulated
for algebras of any plural type. However, to avoid excessive notation we will consider
here only plural type with operations of arity two, and will limit ourself to the
following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a (meet) semilattice. (Here all operations of
\Omega
are equal to

\cdot
). For each
s\in S
, let a
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebra
A_{s}
of a plural binary type

\tau\colon\Omega\rightarrow\{2\}
be given, and for each operation
\star\in\Omega
an
extension
(E_{s}^{\,\star},\star)
of
(A_{s},\star)
. For each pair
t\leq s
of
S
,
let

\varphi_{s,t}^{\,\star}\colon(A_{s},\star)\rightarrow(E_{t}^{\,\star},\star)

be a
\star
-homomorphism
such that the following three conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\varphi_{s,s}^{\star}
is the embedding of
A_{s}
into
E_{s}^{\star}
;

(2)

\varphi_{s,s\cdot t}^{\star}(A_{s})\star\varphi_{t,s\cdot t}^{\star}(A_{t})\subseteq A_{s\cdot t}
;

(3)

for each
u\leq s\cdot t
in
S
and
a_{s}\in A_{s},\,b_{t}\in A_{t}

\varphi_{s\cdot t,u}^{\star}(\varphi_{s,s\cdot t}^{\star}(a_{s})\star\varphi_{t,s\cdot t}^{\star}(b_{t}))=\varphi_{s,u}^{\star}(a_{s})\star\varphi_{t,u}^{\star}(b_{t}).

An
\Omega
-algebra structure on the disjoint sum
A
of all
A_{s}
is given by defining the operations
\star
in
\Omega
as follows:

a_{s}\star b_{t}=\varphi_{s,s\cdot t}^{\,\star}(a_{s})\star\varphi_{t,s\cdot t}^{\,\star}(b_{t}).

Then all
A_{s}
are subalgebras of
A
, and
S
is a quotient of
A
. The semilattice sum

A
of
A_{s}
is said to be the
semilattice sum of
A_{s}
by the mappings

\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}
. If additionally, for each
t\in S
, one has

E_{t}^{\star}=\{\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}(a_{s})\mid s\geq t,\,a_{s}\in A_{s}\}
, and all

E_{s}^{\star}
are certain canonical extensions of
A_{s}
(see
[
23
, § 6.1]
), then
this semilattice sum is called a
Lallement sum
.

By
[
23
, Th. 624]
each semilattice sum of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras can be reconstructed as
a Lallement sum of these algebras. The usefulness of Lallement sums depends on properties
of the available extensions
E_{s}^{\star}
. In particular, a nice situation appears if for
each
s\in S
, all extensions
E_{s}^{\star}
coincide with the summand
A_{s}
. Such
Lallement sums are called
strict
.

We next consider a special case of strict Lallement sums of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras, which
extends a construction described for Birkhoff systems in
[
9
]
. This construction
works nicely for
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras where each operation
\star\in\Omega
has a one-sided
unit. In what follows we assume that all one-sided units are right-sided and call them
simply units. We will call such algebras briefly
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras with units
.

Let
A
be a semilattice sum
\bigsqcup_{s\in S}A_{s}
of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras
A_{s}
with
units, and let
e_{s}^{\star}
be the (right-)unit element of
\star
in

A_{s}
. Additionally assume that for all
s,t,u\in S
with
u\leq s,t

(a_{s}\star b_{t})\star e_{u}^{\star}=(a_{s}\star e_{u}^{\star})\star(b_{t}\star e_{u}^{\star}),

(7.1)

and for
t\leq s
in
S
define the maps

\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}\colon A_{s}\rightarrow A_{t};\ a_{s}\mapsto a_{s}\star e_{t}^{\star}.

Note that
t\leq s
in
S
means
s\cdot t=t
, so these maps are well
defined. It is easily seen that each
\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}
is a
\star
-homomorphism
from
A_{s}
into
A_{t}
. Moreover, by (
7.1
),

a_{s}\star b_{t}=(a_{s}\star b_{t})\star e_{st}^{\star}=(a_{s}\star e_{t}^{\star})\star(b_{s}\star e_{t}^{\star})=\varphi_{s,s\cdot t}^{\star}(a_{s})\star\varphi_{t,s\cdot t}^{\star}(b_{t}).

Then for each
u\leq s\cdot t
in
S

\displaystyle\varphi_{s\cdot t,u}^{\star}(\varphi_{s,s\cdot t}^{\star}(a_{s})\,\star\,\varphi_{t,s\cdot t}^{\star}(b_{t}))=\varphi_{s\cdot t,u}^{\star}(a_{s}\,\star\,b_{t})=

\displaystyle(a_{s}\star b_{t})\star e_{u}^{\star}=(a_{s}\star e_{u}^{\star})\star(b_{t}\star e_{u}^{\star})=\varphi_{s,u}^{\star}(a_{s})\star\varphi_{t,u}^{\star}(b_{t}).

It follows that the maps
\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}
satisfy the requirements of a strict
Lallement sum. One obtains the following theorem, a corollary of Theorem 624 of

[
23
]
, and an extension of Theorem
4.15
of
[
9
]
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
A
be a semilattice sum of
\mathcal{V}_{t}
-algebras
A_{s}
with units of a plural binary
type. Then
A=\bigsqcup_{s\in S}A_{s}
satisfies the condition (
7.1
) if and
only if it is a strict Lallement sum of the subalgebras
A_{s}
over the semilattice
S

given by the homomorphisms
\varphi_{s,t}^{\star}
described above.

Examples are provided by Birkhoff systems which are semilattices of bounded lattices, and
more general bisemillatices which are semilattice sums of bounded lattices. In particular,
members of the pseudo-regularizations of the variety
\mathcal{L}
of lattices provided above
are of this type.

Note also that the condition (
7.1
) holds for all algebras
A
of plural binary
type with self-entropic operations, i.e., satisfying
(x\star y)\star(z\star t)=(x\star z)\star(y\star t)
, for each
\star\in\Omega
.

One more final remark concerns the assumption about strong irregularity of the summands

A_{s}
. In this paper, we were interested in semilattices sums of algebras in a strongly
irregular variety. However, both Definition
7.1
and Theorem
7.2
may be
extended easily to semilattice sums of algebras in any variety.
