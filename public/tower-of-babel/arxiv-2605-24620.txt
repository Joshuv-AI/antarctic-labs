Optimized multilevel Monte Carlo methods in Banach spaces
Kristin Kirchner a,b , Fabio Nobile c , Christoph Schwab d and Tommaso Vanzan e

Abstract
. We present a comprehensive theoretical and numerical analysis of Monte Carlo methods for the estimation of statistical moments of random variables X : Ω → E X\colon\Omega\rightarrow E taking values in a Banach space E E . For practical computation, we consider finite-dimensional approximation subspaces ( E ℓ ) ℓ ∈ ℕ ⊂ E {(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subset E} of increasing dimension. We develop a refined error analysis that explicitly accounts for a dependence of the Rademacher type constants on the dimension of E ℓ E_{\ell} , leading to novel complexity results for single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods to estimate the mean and injective moments of arbitrary order, which are, in certain cases, sharper than those derived in [ 25 ] . Moreover, we show that, in favorable cases, the resulting error-vs.-work bounds are independent of the Rademacher type of E E . We then focus on L p ​ ( S ) L^{p}(S) -valued random variables for a σ \sigma -finite measure space ( S , 𝒮 , μ ) (S,\mathcal{S},\mu) satisfying certain approximation properties, and prove that for a random variable X ∈ L q ​ ( Ω , L p ​ ( S ) ) ∩ L p ​ ( S , L q ​ ( Ω ) ) X\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))\cap L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega)) , with q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) q\in(1,\infty) and p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) , the L q L^{q} -convergence rate of a multilevel Monte Carlo estimator is determined exclusively by the integrability parameter min ⁡ { q , 2 } \min\{q,2\} , with no dependence on the Rademacher type min ⁡ { p , 2 } \min\{p,2\} of L p ​ ( S ) L^{p}(S) . This analysis reveals an interplay between the sampling error and the strong approximation error, and leads to optimized error–vs.-work bounds for both single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods. Numerical experiments confirm the sharpness of the analyses presented in estimating both first and second moments.

1.
Introduction

Estimating statistical moments of random variables taking values
in infinite-dimensional vector spaces is a fundamental challenge in the
field of uncertainty quantification.
This setting naturally arises in mathematical models involving
random or stochastic differential equations,
where the solution itself is not a finite-dimensional vector,
but instead an element of an appropriate function space.
While the first moment, that is the mean, is often
itself the quantity of interest,
higher-order moments provide additional information
about the probability distribution of the random variable,
and can be relevant in statistical tests
[
31
]
.

To estimate moments, a widely used approach is the
Monte Carlo method.
It is well-known that for a random variable
X\in L^{2}(\Omega;H)
, where
H
is a Hilbert space and

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
denotes a complete probability space,
the Monte Carlo method to approximate the mean converges
with the rate
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
in the number of samples.
This result extends to the estimation
of statistical moments of any order
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
viewed as elements of the Hilbert tensor product space
H^{(k)}\!
,
provided that
{X\in L^{2k}(\Omega;H)}
.
However, this picture drastically changes
when the random variable has i) reduced integrability
or ii) takes values in a Banach space
E
.
The convergence rate indeed depends on both the integrability
of the random variable and on
geometric properties of the Banach space
E
.
In rigorous terms, if
E
is of
Rademacher type

p\in[1,2]
(see
[
21
, Chapter 7]
)
and
X\in L^{q}(\Omega;E)
, with
q\in[1,\infty)
,
then the Monte Carlo method to approximate
the mean converges in
L^{q}
at the rate
1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}
,
see e.g.
[
28
, Proposition 9.11]

and
[
25
, Corollary 3.15]
.
The extension of this result to arbitrary
k
th moments
is not straightforward since,
in contrast to the Hilbertian setting,
there is no canonical choice for the norm
on the algebraic tensor product space
\otimes^{k}E
.
Provided that
X\in L^{kq}(\Omega;E)
, Kirchner and Schwab
[
25
]

have recently proved the Monte Carlo convergence rate

1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}

in the
injective tensor norm
,
which is the weakest crossnorm,
and showed that the Monte Carlo method
does in general not converge
in the
projective tensor norm
,
which is the strongest crossnorm,
see
[
33
, Chapter 6]
.
In practice, the convergence rate

1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}
can be discouraging,
since to achieve an error of order
\epsilon

it suggests to use asymptotically

\epsilon^{-\max\{p^{\prime}\!,\,q^{\prime}\}}
samples,
where
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
are the Hölder conjugates of
p
and
q
.
This could be a much larger sample size
than the asymptotic
\epsilon^{-2}
many samples
which are needed for a random variable
X\in L^{2}(\Omega;H)
,
and it could become rapidly unaffordable
as
p
or
q
decrease.
We note that while it is quite immediate to verify
the sharpness of the rate
1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}

with respect to
q
(take, e.g., the real-valued random variable

u(\omega):=\omega^{-\frac{1}{q+\delta}}
,
\delta>0
,
which lies in
L^{q}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
, and estimate

\int_{0}^{1}u(\omega)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega
using the Monte Carlo method),
to the best of the authors’ knowledge,
there are no numerical experiments yet
confirming the sharpness of
the rate with respect to the Rademacher type
p
;
an aspect that will be addressed in this work.

When solving differential equations,
numerical methods yield finite-dimensional approximations
of the random variable
X
.
As the corresponding additional discretization error
has to be taken into account for the overall accuracy,
this gives rise to more sophisticated sampling methods,
which leverage the availability of
several
levels
of approximations,
each of different accuracy but also of different computational cost.
A vast literature has been devoted in the last two decades to
MultiLevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) methods,
starting from the seminal works of Heinrich
in the context of numerical integration
[
18
]

and of Giles for
stochastic differential equations
[
13
]
.
More recently, a multi-index variant that permits
to consider multiple discretization parameters
simultaneously has been proposed in
[
17
]
.
Single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
have been extensively analyzed in the Hilbertian case
to approximate the mean of the solution for
partial differential equations with random coefficients,
random initial data or random boundary
conditions
[
2
,
8
,
15
,
30
,
32
,
35
]

and for stochastic differential
equations
[
9
,
12
,
14
,
19
,
36
]
.
Concerning higher-order moments,
[
3
,
4
]

rely on multilevel strategies to
approximate diagonals of central statistical moments,
while
[
2
,
7
,
32
]

explore sparse tensor methods to approximate full moments.
With regard to Banach space valued random variables,
the MLMC method applied to a
random degenerate convection diffusion equation
is analyzed in
[
26
]
,
while an abstract analysis of single- and multilevel Monte Carlo
methods for both the mean and arbitrary injective
k
th moments
is discussed in
[
25
]
.
In particular, it is shown in
[
25
]

that the Rademacher type of
E
generally determines
the Monte Carlo convergence rate of injective moments
of arbitrary order;
consequently,
the number of samples to use on each level
for MLMC estimation is chosen accordingly.

1.1.
Contributions

The contributions of this manuscript are threefold.

Our first contribution relies on the observation that
numerical methods yield approximations that belong
to finite-dimensional subspaces

(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq E
.
It is known that a finite-dimensional normed space is of
any Rademacher type
{r\in[1,2]}
,
but with Rademacher
type constants

that may depend on the dimension of the subspace.
We therefore revisit the analysis of
[
25
]

by taking into account the dependence
of these type constants on the dimension
of the approximation space, and we derive
refined single- and multilevel Monte Carlo error estimates
for the first moment in the
L^{q}(\Omega;E)
-norm
using the Rademacher type property
for any
r\in[1,\min\{q,2\}]
.
By further optimizing the error bound over
r
,
we obtain new complexity results, which we further extend to
arbitrary injective
k
th moments provided that
X\in L^{kq}(\Omega;E)
.
In the most favorable case,
the new analysis reveals that
the complexity of single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
is
independent
of the Rademacher type
p
of
E
,
and depends exclusively on the integrability
q

of the random variable,
and on additional parameters describing
the growth of the type constants with respect to the dimension.

Our second contribution is devoted to

L^{p}(S)
-valued random variables which are central
in many applications.
Here,
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
is
a
\sigma
-finite measure space
which allows to approximate functions
in
L^{p}(S)
by simple functions based on
integral averages on disjoints subsets in
\mathcal{S}
.
This property is satisfied, e.g., by
any (not necessarily bounded) Borel
subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We show that for a random variable

{X\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))}
,

q\in(1,\infty)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
,
satisfying the additional integrability assumption

X\in L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
, i.e.,
the pointwise
q
th moments
are
L^{\nicefrac{{p}}{{q}}}(S)
-integrable,
the convergence rate of the Monte Carlo first moment estimator,

even at the continuous level
,
is exclusively determined by the integrability parameter
\min\{q,2\}
,
and thus independent of the Rademacher type
\min\{p,2\}
.
In particular, this guarantees convergence of the
Monte Carlo method for a class of

L^{1}(S)
-valued random variables
which is not covered by the
standard analysis based on the Rademacher type.
Furthermore, motivated by numerical experiments,
we examine the setting in which the Monte Carlo error
is measured in the
L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))
-norm,
while
X
possesses
additional regularity characterized
by a parameter
\widetilde{q}
,
that is,

\penalty\ X\in L^{\widetilde{q}}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))\cap L^{p}(S;L^{\widetilde{q}}(\Omega))

with
\widetilde{q}\in[q,\infty)
.
This analysis reveals a trade-off in the error estimates
between the decay rate of the sampling error
and of the strong approximation error and ultimately
leads to the derivation of
new optimized multilevel complexity estimates.

Finally, our third contribution consists in extensive numerical
experiments that i) verify the sharpness of
the convergence rate
1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}

with respect to the Rademacher type
p
at the continuous level,
ii) verify that for a random variable

X\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))\cap L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))

the convergence rate at the continuous level is

1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}
irrespectively of
p
,
iii) confirm the sharpness of the
single- and multilevel complexity analyses
presented in this manuscript
in approximating both the mean and the second moment.
Although the numerical experiments rely on rather simple
mathematical models,
their implementation is very delicate:
the need to handle random variables
with low integrability and with values
in a Banach space leads to pathwise solutions
with singularities, which in turn demand robust
and accurate numerical methods.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Given parameter sets

\mathscr{P},\,\mathscr{Q}
,
and mappings

\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\colon\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we use the notation

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)

to indicate that for each
q\in\mathscr{Q}

there exists a constant
{C_{q}\in(0,\infty)}

such that

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\leq C_{q}\,\mathscr{G}(p,q)

holds
for all
{p\in\mathscr{P}}
.
Whenever both relations,

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)

and
\mathscr{G}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{F}(p,q)
,
hold simultaneously,
we write
\mathscr{F}(p,q)\eqsim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)
.

We denote by
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
a Banach space
over
\mathbb{R}
,

B_{E}:=\{x\in E:\|x\|_{E}\leq 1\}
its closed unit ball,
and

\mathcal{B}(E)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
on

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
.
The dual space
of all continuous linear functionals

g\colon E\to\mathbb{R}

is denoted by
E^{\prime}
.
We write
g(x)
or
\langle g,x\rangle

for the duality pairing between
g\in E^{\prime}

and
x\in E
, and

\|g\|_{E^{\prime}}:=\sup_{x\in B_{E}}|g(x)|

for the norm on
E^{\prime}
.

For a measure space
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)

and
p\in[1,\infty)
,

L^{p}(S;E):=L^{p}(S,\mathcal{S},\mu;E)

denotes
the space of (equivalence classes of)

E
-valued, strongly measurable,

p
-integrable functions on
S
,
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(S;E)}:=\left(\int_{S}\|f(s)\|_{E}^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}.

Whenever the functions are real-valued,
we abbreviate

L^{p}(S):=L^{p}(S;\mathbb{R})
. The indicator function of a measurable subset
A
of
S
is denoted by
\mathbf{1}_{A}
.
For a second measure space

(\widetilde{S},\widetilde{\mathcal{S}},\widetilde{\mu})
,

(S\times\widetilde{S},\mathcal{S}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{S}},\mu\otimes\widetilde{\mu})

denotes
the product measure space, i.e.,

S\times\widetilde{S}
is the
set of all tuples
(s,\widetilde{s})
with

s\in S
,
\widetilde{s}\in\widetilde{S}
,

\mathcal{S}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is the product

\sigma
-algebra generated by
all sets of the form
A\times\widetilde{A}
with
A\in\mathcal{S}
,

\widetilde{A}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
,
and
\mu\otimes\widetilde{\mu}
is the uniquely defined
product measure satisfying

(\mu\otimes\widetilde{\mu})(A\times\widetilde{A})=\mu(A)\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{A})

for all
A\in\mathcal{S}
,

\widetilde{A}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and for an open subset

D
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

W^{k,p}(D)
is the Sobolev space
containing all elements
in
L^{p}(D)=L^{p}(D,\mathcal{B}(D),\lambda_{d};\mathbb{R})
,

\lambda_{d}
being the Lebesgue measure,
whose weak derivatives up to order
k

exist and are in
L^{p}(D)
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\ell^{p}
is the Banach space of

p
-summable real-valued sequences
equipped with the norm

\|x\|_{\ell^{p}}:=\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{p}\bigr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}

and, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}:=\left\{1,2,\dots\right\}
,
we denote by
\ell_{N}^{p}
the space
\mathbb{R}^{N}

equipped with the
\ell^{p}
-norm.
For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote
by
p^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
p
,
i.e.,
\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
,
using the convention that
\tfrac{1}{\infty}=0
.
In addition,
\ell^{\infty}
is the space of all bounded sequences,
L^{\infty}(D)

consists of essentially bounded
strongly measurable functions,
and
W^{k,\infty}(D)
consists of functions
whose weak derivatives up to order
k

are in
L^{\infty}(D)
.

Throughout this article, we assume that

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
is
a complete probability space
with expectation operator
\mathbb{E}
,
and we mark statements which hold

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely with
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
We only consider strongly measurable mappings
X\colon\Omega\rightarrow E
,
that is, mappings which are
i) measurable with respect to the
Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(E)
on
E
,
and ii) almost surely separably valued, i.e.,

X\in E_{0}
holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for some separable subspace

E_{0}\subseteq E
.
For any strongly measurable map

X\colon\Omega\rightarrow E

further satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\|X\|_{E}]<\infty
,
the Bochner integral

\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=:\mathbb{E}[X]\in E,

is well-defined

[
20
, Proposition 1.2.2]
.
For any
q\in[1,\infty)
and real Banach space

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

we denote by

L^{q}(\Omega;E)
the
Bochner space consisting of all (equivalence classes of)

E
-valued strongly measurable random variables

{\eta\colon\Omega\rightarrow E}

such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\eta\|_{E}^{q}\right]<\infty
,
equipped with the norm

\|\eta\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}:=\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{q}\bigr]\bigr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}
.

2.2.
Rademacher types

In this subsection, we recall the necessary definitions and tools
to develop a Monte Carlo error analysis in Banach spaces.
For a comprehensive overview,
we refer to the monographs

[
21
,
28
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Rademacher family)
.

Let
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

be a complete probability space and

r_{j}\colon\widetilde{\Omega}\to\{-1,1\}
,
j\in J\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
be a family of independent random variables such that

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(r_{j}=-1)=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(r_{j}=1)=\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
j\in J
.
Then, the collection of random variables

(r_{j})_{j\in J}
is called a
Rademacher family
.

Rademacher families frequently appear
when estimating
sums of independent zero-mean
random variables taking values in
a Banach space
E

in the norm of
L^{q}(\Omega;E)

due to the following symmetrization result,
see e.g.
[
28
, Lemma 6.3]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Symmetrization)
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
(r_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
be
a Rademacher family on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
and let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}\in L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)
be
centered random variables,
i.e.,
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[\eta_{j}]=0
for
1\leq j\leq M
,
taking values
in a real Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

such that
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M},r_{1},\ldots,r_{M}
are independent.
Then,

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\eta_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\eta_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}.

There exists an extensive literature
on Rademacher sums
(and on more general random sums,
see e.g.
[
21
, Chapter 6]

for the corresponding analysis in Banach spaces).
In this context, the following
Khintchine and Kahane–Khintchine
inequalities (see
[
28
, Lemma 4.1]

and
[
23
]
)
play a central role.

Definition 2.3

(Khintchine constants)
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
.
The Khintchine constants
A_{q},B_{q}\in(0,\infty)

are the
largest, respectively,
smallest positive constants such that,
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

(with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
)
and any finite sequence
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}

of real numbers, it holds that

A_{q}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}a_{j}\biggr|^{q}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}a_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\leq B_{q}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

Since the norm on
L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})

is increasing in
q
and

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}a_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}=\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

it holds that
A_{q}=1
for every
q\in[2,\infty)

while
B_{q}=1
for every
q\in[1,2]
.
Sharp values of
A_{q}
and
B_{q}

in the remaining nontrivial cases were
obtained in
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Kahane–Khintchine constants)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty)
.
The
(q,p)
Kahane–Khintchine constant
K_{q,p}

is the smallest positive constant
such that
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
for any real Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
,
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
,

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}\leq K_{q,p}\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}.

Remark 2.5
.

For
q\leq p
,
K_{q,p}=1
holds
due to Hölder’s inequality.
For
q>p
, finiteness of the constants
K_{q,p}

was proved by Kahane
[
23
]
,
and it implies that all
L^{q}
-norms are equivalent
for Rademacher sums.

Definition 2.6

(Rademacher type)
.

A real Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is said to be of
(Rademacher) type

p\in[1,2]

if there exists a constant
\tau\in(0,\infty)

such that
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

(with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
),
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and all vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
,

(2.1)

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}=\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{E}^{p}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\leq\tau\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{E}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!.

In this case, the smallest constant
\tau\in(0,\infty)

in (
2.1
)
is called the
type
p
constant of
E

and will be denoted by
\tau_{p}(E)
.

Remark 2.7
.

Due to the triangle inequality,
every Banach space is at least of type
1
.
Conversely, the type cannot be larger than
2

(due to finiteness of the Khintchine constant
A_{q}

for any
q\in[1,\infty)
)
and every Hilbert space is of Rademacher type 2,
see
[
27
]
.
In addition,
since the Bochner norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}

is increasing in
q
while the sequence norm

\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}}
is decreasing in
q
,
if
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
has type
p\in[1,2]

with type constant
\tau_{p}(E)
,
then it is also of type
r\in[1,p]
, with
\tau_{r}(E)\leq\tau_{p}(E)
.

Example 2.8
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
The spaces
\ell^{s}
,
L^{s}(S)
and
W^{k,s}(D)

all have Rademacher type
p=\bar{s}:=\min\left\{s,2\right\}
.
For this
p
,
the type
p
constants of
\ell^{s}
and
L^{s}(S)
satisfy

\tau_{p}(\ell^{s})=\tau_{p}(L^{s}(S))=\tau_{\bar{s}}(\ell^{s})=\tau_{\bar{s}}(L^{s}(S))=B_{s}
,
where
B_{s}\in(0,\infty)
is
the Khintchine constant of Definition
2.3

and we recall that
B_{s}=1
for
s\in[1,2]
.

Remark 2.9
.

Any real Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})

of finite dimension is of Rademacher type
2
.
To see this, let
N
be the dimension of
E
,

\left\{\widetilde{x}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{x}_{N}\right\}

a basis of
E
,
and consider the linear isomorphism

T\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}

such that
T(\widetilde{x}_{j})=\bm{e}_{j}
,
where
\bm{e}_{j}
is
the
j
th canonical unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\!
,
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
.
The space
E
is then isomorphic to
\ell^{2}_{N}
,
that is,
\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with the Euclidean norm,
but in general not isometrically.
However, as all norms are equivalent
in a finite-dimensional space,
there exist two constants

\underline{C}_{N}
and
\overline{C}_{N}
,
which may depend on the dimension
N
,
such that

\underline{C}_{N}\|Tx\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}\leq\|x\|_{E}\leq\overline{C}_{N}\|Tx\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}\qquad\forall x\in E.

Hence,
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}_{N}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}Tx_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega};\ell^{2}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}_{N}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|Tx_{j}\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq\frac{\overline{C}_{N}}{\underline{C}_{N}}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{E}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\!,

where we used that
\ell^{2}_{N}
is of
Rademacher type
2

and has type
2
constant equal to
1
.
We conclude that
E
is of type
2

with
\tau_{2}(E)\leq\frac{\overline{C}_{N}}{\underline{C}_{N}}
.

2.3.
k
-fold
tensor product spaces and

k
th moments

In this subsection,
we introduce
k
-fold tensor products of a
real Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

and interpret the
k
th moment
of a random variable
X\colon\Omega\rightarrow E

as an element of a specific
complete normed tensor product space.

For an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
the
algebraic tensor product space

\otimes^{k}E

is defined as the vector space consisting of all finite sums
of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k},\qquad x_{j,i}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M,\;1\leq i\leq k,\;M\in\mathbb{N},

equipped with the algebraic operations rendering
the tensor product linear in each of its
k
components,
see e.g.
[
11
, Section 1.1]

and
[
33
, Chapter 1]
.
The space
\otimes^{k}E
can be equipped
with different norms.
Common choices are the

injective
and
projective

tensor norms, see

[
33
, Chapters 2 and 3]
,
which are respectively the weakest
and strongest crossnorm,
see
[
33
, Proposition 6.1]
.
As shown in
[
25
, Example 3.21]

Monte Carlo methods will, in general,
not convergence in the projective tensor norm,
and we therefore
introduce only the former.
For
U\in\otimes^{k}E
with representation

U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}
,
the
injective tensor norm
is defined by

\|U\|_{\varepsilon}:=\sup\left\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{j,i})\biggl|\;\Bigg|\;f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in B_{E^{\prime}}\right\},

and it can be shown that the value of this supremum
is independent of the choice for the representation of

U\in\otimes^{k}E
.
The crossnorm property implies that,
for every elementary tensor

x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}
,

(2.2)

\|x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}\|_{\varepsilon}=\sup\left\{\biggl|\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{i})\biggl|\;\Bigg|\;f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in B_{E^{\prime}}\right\}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|x_{i}\|_{E}.

The
k
-fold injective tensor product space
,
denoted by
\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{k}E
,
is then defined as the closure of
\otimes^{k}E

with respect to the injective norm, that is,

\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{k}E:=\overline{\otimes^{k}E}^{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}}\!
.

The analysis of Subsection
3.3

builds on the convergence results for
multilevel Monte Carlo methods presented in
[
25
]
,
which are formulated in a
symmetric
injective tensor norm.
We therefore introduce
subspaces of
\otimes^{k}E
and of
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
,
containing only symmetric elements.
To this end, for any
x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}\in\otimes^{k}E
,
we introduce its symmetrization,

s(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}):=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)},

where
S_{k}
is the group of permutations of
the set
\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}
.
The
k
-fold symmetric algebraic tensor product of
E
,
denoted by
\otimes^{k,s}E
,
is then defined by

\displaystyle\otimes^{k,s}E

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}s(x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k})\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\;x_{j,i}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M,\;1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}:}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\;x_{j}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M\right\},

see
[
11
, Section 1.5]
,
where we used the notation

{\otimes^{k}}x:=\underbrace{x\otimes\cdots\otimes x}_{\text{$k$ times}}\qquad\forall x\in E.

For

U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}\in\otimes^{k,s}E
,
the
symmetric injective tensor norm
is given by

\|U\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}:=\sup\left\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}f(x_{j})^{k}\biggr|\;\Bigg|\;f\in B_{E^{\prime}}\right\},

which does not depend on the choice
of the representation of
U
either.
The
k
-fold symmetric injective tensor product of
E

is defined at the closure of
\otimes^{k,s}E

with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}
.
We finally recall that the symmetrization
s

can be extended to a linear and continuous projection
from
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E

to
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,
and further that
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

and
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}

are equivalent as norms on
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,
with equivalence constants depending on
k
,
see
[
25
, Section 2.2]
.

For a strongly measurable
E
-valued random variable
X
,
its
injective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]

is defined as
the expectation
(see, e.g.,
[
22
, Section 3.1]
)

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}X\bigr]=\int_{\Omega}{\otimes^{k}}X(\omega)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\underbrace{X(\omega)\otimes\cdots\otimes X(\omega)}_{\text{$k$ times}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega),

whenever this integral exists (in Bochner sense)
in the
k
-fold injective
tensor product space
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
.
A sufficient condition for the existence
of the injective
k
th moment

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]
is that

X\in L^{k}(\Omega;E)
.
Indeed, the strong measurability of
X

implies that

{\otimes^{k}}X\colon\Omega\to\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E

is strongly measurable since the nonlinear mapping

E\ni x\mapsto{\otimes^{k}}x\in\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E

is continuous and, secondly, due to
(
2.2
),

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|{\otimes^{k}}X\|_{\varepsilon}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X\|^{k}_{E}\bigr]<\infty.

Thus,
{\otimes^{k}}X\in L^{1}(\Omega;\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E)

and the existence of
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]

follows from the definition of the Bochner integral

[
20
, Proposition 1.2.2]
.
Finally, notice that the injective
k
th moment is
an element of the
k
-fold
symmetric

injective tensor product space since,
due to the continuity and linearity of the symmetrization,

s\bigl(\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]\bigr)=s\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}X}\bigr]\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[s({\otimes^{k}}X)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}X\bigr]=\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X].

We have so far introduced the
k
-fold
(symmetric) injective tensor product of
a generic Banach space
E
.
In Section
4
,
we will perform an analysis
tailored for
L^{p}(S)
-valued
random variables, where
p\in[1,\infty)
.
The results presented there
for first moments readily extend to
higher-order moments
when considered as elements
of a tensor product space equipped
with a crossnorm which is stronger
than the injective tensor norm and
which arises naturally
on the algebraic tensor product space
{\otimes^{k}}L^{p}(S)
,
see e.g.
[
10
, Chapter I, Section 7]
.
To introduce this tensor norm,
let
(S^{k}\!,\mathcal{S}^{k}\!,\mu^{k})

be the product measure space
of
k
copies of
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
and consider the injective linear map

\phi\colon{\otimes^{k}}L^{p}(S)\rightarrow L^{p}(S^{k})
such that

\phi:\;\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}.

Here,
\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}
is interpreted as
(the equivalence class of)
the product function

\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}:\;(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})\mapsto f_{j,1}(s_{1})\cdots f_{j,k}(s_{k}),

and the injectivity of
\phi
follows from

[
33
, Proposition 1.2]
,
the identification of
(L^{p}(S))^{\prime}
with
L^{p^{\prime}\!}(S)
,
and the Fubini theorem.
Using the mapping
\phi
, any element of
{\otimes^{k}}L^{p}(S)

can be identified with a function in
L^{p}(S^{k})
.
We thus can define a crossnorm

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\,\cdot\,\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert
on
{\otimes^{k}}L^{p}(S)
as

\Biggl\lvert\!\Biggl\lvert\!\Biggl\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}\Biggr\rvert\!\Biggr\rvert\!\Biggr\rvert:=\Biggl\|\phi\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}\Biggr)\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S^{k})}=\Biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j,i}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S^{k})}\!,

and consider the tensor product space
obtained by taking the closure of
{\otimes^{k}}L^{p}(S)

with respect to this norm,

\otimes^{k}_{p}L^{p}(S):=\overline{\otimes^{k}L^{p}(S)}^{\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert}
.

Assume now that

X\colon\Omega\rightarrow L^{p}(S)

is a strongly measurable random variable .
Its
L^{p}

k
th moment
is defined as the expectation

(2.3)

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{p}[X]:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}X\bigr]=\int_{\Omega}{\otimes^{k}}X(\omega)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega),

whenever this integral exists (in Bochner sense)
as an element of

\otimes^{k}_{p}L^{p}(S)\cong L^{p}(S^{k})
.
A sufficient condition for the
k
th moment to exist is

X\in L^{k}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))
, since then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert{\otimes^{k}}X\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X\|_{L^{p}(S)}^{k}\bigr]<\infty,

and the existence of
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{p}[X]
follows again
from the theory of the Bochner integral.

3.
Optimized Monte Carlo error analysis
based on dimension-dependent type constants

In this section, we present a Monte Carlo error analysis
that accounts for the dependence of the Rademacher
type constants on the dimension of the approximation space.
Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
study,
respectively, single-level and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
to approximate the first moment,
that is the mean, of a random variable with values
in a generic real Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
.
Subsection
3.3

extends this analysis to the approximation of arbitrary
k
th moments.

3.1.
Single-level Monte Carlo methods

Suppose that
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
is a real Banach space
of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,
with type
p
constant
\tau_{p}(E)\in(0,\infty)
.
From Definition
2.6
,
we thus have for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

(with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
),
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and all vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
,

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}=\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{E}^{p}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\leq\tau_{p}(E)\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{E}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!.

Assume that
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of
subspaces of
E
,
which have finite dimension

N_{\ell}:=\dim(E_{\ell})<\infty
.
Then, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

E_{\ell}
has any Rademacher type
r\in[1,2]

(due to
\dim(E_{\ell})<\infty
,
see Remarks
2.7
and
2.9
)
and we denote the corresponding type constants
by
\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})
,
r\in[1,2]
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consequently, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
r\in[1,2]
,
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

(with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
),
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and all vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E_{\ell}
,

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{r}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}=\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}x_{j}\biggr\|_{E}^{r}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}}}\leq\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{E}^{r}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}}}\!.

Note that the type constants satisfy the following:

(3.1)

\forall r\in[1,p]:\quad\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})\leq\tau_{p}(E)<\infty,

and that, for
r>p
, the type
r
constant

\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})
will, in general,
not be bounded uniformly in
\ell
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and consider the Banach space
\ell^{s}

which is of type
p=\bar{s}:=\min\{s,2\}
.
Furthermore, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
E_{N}:=\ell^{s}_{N}\subset\ell^{s}
.
Then,

\tau_{r}(E_{N})=\tau_{r}(\ell^{s}_{N})=\begin{cases}B_{s}&\text{ if }r\in[1,p],\\
N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}&\text{ if }r\in(p,2],\end{cases}

where
B_{s}\in(0,\infty)
is the
Khintchine constant from Definition
2.3
.
For
r\in[1,p]
, the claim follows from
(
3.1
) and Remark
2.7
,
see also Example
2.8
,
while for
p=s<2
and

r\in(p,2]
we may use
the equivalence relations

\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{r}}\leq\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{p}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\|u\|_{\ell^{r}_{N}},\quad\forall u\in\ell^{p}_{N}=\ell^{r}_{N},\quad 1\leq p\leq r<\infty.

Since the finite-dimensional subspaces
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

of
E
have any Rademacher type
{r\in[1,2]}
,
we readily obtain from
[
25
, Corollary 3.15]

the following single-level Monte Carlo
error estimate for the first moment.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\eta\in L^{1}(\Omega;E)
,

q\in[1,\infty)
,
and set
\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}
.
For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
M_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}\in L^{q}(\Omega;E_{\ell})
are
independent and identically distributed
random variables taking values in a finite-dimensional
subspace
E_{\ell}\subseteq E
.
Then, we have, for
every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
all
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{E}

\displaystyle\quad+2K_{q,r}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\bigl\|\xi_{\ell,1}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}.

In the case that, in addition to the assumptions made in
Proposition
3.2
,
there exist constants
\alpha,C_{\alpha},C_{\sf stab}\in(0,\infty)

such that

(3.2)

\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}N_{\ell}^{\alpha}\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{E}\leq C_{\alpha},\qquad\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\,\bigl\|\xi_{\ell,1}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq C_{\sf stab},

then
we obtain, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and all
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
,
the estimate

(3.3)

\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq C_{\alpha}N_{\ell}^{-\alpha}+2K_{q,1}C_{\sf stab}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\!},

where we interpret
\tfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=0
for
r=1
.
Here, we also bounded the Kahane–Khintchine
constant
K_{q,r}
from above by
K_{q,1}
,
since it does not depend on
\ell
.
Note that by
[
21
, Theorem 6.2.4 and p. 49]

we have

(3.4)

\sup_{r\in[1,2]}K_{q,r}\leq K_{q,1}\leq\begin{cases}K_{2,1}\quad\;\;\;=\sqrt{2}&\text{ if }q\in[1,2],\\
K_{q,2}K_{2,1}\leq\sqrt{2(q-1)}&\text{ if }q\in(2,\infty).\end{cases}

Provided that
q>1
and
r\in(1,\bar{q}]
,
we thus choose the number of Monte Carlo samples as

M_{\ell}=\Bigl\lceil[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{r^{\prime}}\Bigr\rceil=\Bigl\lceil[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})]^{r^{\prime}\!}N_{\ell}^{\alpha r^{\prime}}\Bigr\rceil,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N},

which balances the two contributions
in (
3.3
)
and yields the estimate

(3.5)

\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq\bigl(C_{\alpha}+2K_{q,1}C_{\sf stab}\bigr)N_{\ell}^{-\alpha}\!.

Since this estimate holds for all
r\in(1,\bar{q}]
,
we can optimize the number of Monte Carlo samples,
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, by setting

M_{\ell}^{\rm opt}:=\begin{cases}\bigl\lceil\inf_{r\in(1,\bar{q}]}[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{r^{\prime}}\bigr\rceil&\quad\text{ if }p=1,\quad\;\;\;q\in(1,\infty),\\
\bigl\lceil\inf_{r\in[p,\bar{q}]}[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{r^{\prime}}\bigr\rceil&\quad\text{ if }p\in(1,2],\ q\in[p,\infty),\end{cases}

whenever these infima exist.
To restrict the interval of
r

from
(1,\bar{q}]
to
[p,\bar{q}]

in the second case,
we used (
3.1
)
as well as the fact that the map

r\mapsto N_{\ell}^{\alpha r^{\prime}\!}

is monotonically decreasing in
r
.
To summarize the previous observations in a corollary we define,
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the function

(3.6)

f_{\alpha;\ell}\colon(1,2]\to[0,\infty),\qquad f_{\alpha;\ell}(r):=[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{r^{\prime}\!}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,
let
{\eta\in L^{1}(\Omega;E)}
,

q\in(1,\infty)\cap[p,\infty)
,
and set

\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}
,

R(p,q):=[p,q]\cap(1,2]
.
In addition, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
let

{(\xi_{\ell,j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{q}(\Omega;E_{\ell})}
be
independent and identically distributed
random variables taking values in a subspace

E_{\ell}\subseteq E
of dimension

N_{\ell}:=\dim(E_{\ell})<\infty

such that (
3.2
) holds
for some constants
\alpha,C_{\alpha},C_{\sf stab}\in(0,\infty)
.

(i)

In the case that
the function

r\mapsto f_{\alpha;\ell}(r)
defined
in (
3.6
)
is
monotonically
decreasing on
R(p,q)\subseteq(1,2]

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
then for the choice

M_{\ell}:=M_{\ell}^{\rm opt}=\Bigl\lceil[\tau_{\bar{q}}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\Bigr\rceil,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N},

the (single-level)
Monte Carlo error estimate (
3.5
) holds.

(ii)

Whenever
p\in(1,2]

and the function

r\mapsto f_{\alpha;\ell}(r)

defined in (
3.6
) is monotonically increasing on
R(p,q)

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
then for the choice

M_{\ell}:=M_{\ell}^{\rm opt}=\Bigl\lceil[\tau_{p}(E_{\ell})N_{\ell}^{\alpha}]^{p^{\prime}}\Bigr\rceil,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N},

the (single-level)
Monte Carlo error estimate (

) holds.

In order to formulate a complexity result
for the single-level Monte Carlo method, we make the following assumption about the
growth of the type constants with respect
to the dimension of the subspaces
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Assumption 3.4

(Growth of type constants)
.

Let
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

be of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,

(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of subspaces
of
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
with dimensions

N_{\ell}:=\dim(E_{\ell})<\infty
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exist constants
a_{0},a_{1}\in[0,\infty)

and
C_{\tau}\in[1,\infty)
such that

(3.7)

\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{r\in[p,2]\cap(1,2]}\Bigl(N_{\ell}^{-(a_{0}-a_{1}r^{\prime})}[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})]^{r^{\prime}\!}\Bigr)\leq C_{\tau}.

Under Assumption
3.4
,
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
the function
f_{\alpha;\ell}\colon(1,2]\to[0,\infty)

in (

)
admits the upper bound

\forall r\in[p,2]\cap(1,2]:\quad f_{\alpha;\ell}(r)\leq C_{\tau}N_{\ell}^{a_{0}+(\alpha-a_{1})r^{\prime}\!},

which is monotonically decreasing in
r

if

and
strictly monotonically increasing
in

if
\alpha<a_{1}
.
This motivates to choose the number
of Monte Carlo samples for a

in the setting of Corollary

as

(3.8)

M_{\ell}^{\rm opt}:=\begin{cases}\Bigl\lceil N_{\ell}^{a_{0}+\bar{q}^{\prime}\!(\alpha-a_{1})}\Bigr\rceil&\text{ if }\alpha\geq a_{1},\\
\bigl\lceil N_{\ell}^{a_{0}+p^{\prime}\!(\alpha-a_{1})}\bigr\rceil&\text{ if }\alpha<a_{1}\text{ and $p\in(1,2]$ is the type of $E$}.\end{cases}

Example 3.5
.

Consider again the Banach space

which is of Rademacher type

if

.
Let

,

let

and

) holds.

(a)

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,
let
{X\in L^{1}(\Omega;E)}
,

q\in(1,\infty)\cap[p,\infty)
,
and set

\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}
.
Let
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
be a
family of subspaces of
E

of
dimension

N_{\ell}:=\dim(E_{\ell})<\infty

satisfying Assumption
3.4
.
For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
(X_{\ell,j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

be a sequence of independent
copies of
an
E_{\ell}
-valued random variable

X_{\ell}\in L^{q}(\Omega;E_{\ell})
such that

\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}N_{\ell}^{\alpha}\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[X]-\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}]\bigr\|_{E}\leq C_{\alpha},\qquad\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\,\bigl\|X_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}]\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq C_{\sf stab},

for some constants
\alpha,C_{\alpha},C_{\sf stab}\in(0,\infty)
.
In addition, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
denote the cost
(number of floating point operations)
to generate one sample of
X_{\ell}

and assume that there exist
constants
\gamma,C_{\gamma}\in(0,\infty)

and
A\in(1,\infty)
such that

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\leq C_{\gamma}N_{\ell}^{\gamma}

and
N_{\ell}\eqsim A^{\ell}
.
Then, for every
\epsilon\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}]
,
there exist integers
L\in\mathbb{N}
and

M_{L}\in\mathbb{N}
such
that the
L^{q}
-accuracy
\epsilon
of
the single-level Monte Carlo
estimator for
\mathbb{E}[X]
,

\mathrm{err}^{\sf SL}_{q}(X):=\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[X]-\frac{1}{M_{L}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{L}}X_{L,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}<\epsilon,

can be achieved at a computational cost of order

(3.9)

\mathcal{C}^{\sf SL}_{q}(X)\lesssim\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-\bar{q}^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma+a_{0}-a_{1}\bar{q}^{\prime}}{\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha\geq a_{1},\\
\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-p^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma+a_{0}-a_{1}p^{\prime}}{\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha<a_{1}\text{ and $p\in(1,2]$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By assumption we may choose

L\in\mathbb{N}

as the smallest integer
such that

N_{L}^{-\alpha}\bigl(C_{\alpha}+2K_{q,1}C_{\sf stab}C_{\tau}^{1/2}\bigr)<\epsilon
.
Defining
M_{L}
as
in (
3.8
)
with
\ell:=L

then yields

\mathrm{err}^{\sf SL}_{q}(X)<\epsilon,

cf. (
3.3
).
The cost of this estimator can be bounded as

(3.10)

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{\sf SL}_{q}(X)\eqsim\mathcal{C}_{L}M_{L}&\leq\begin{cases}C_{\gamma}N_{L}^{\gamma}\Bigl\lceil N_{L}^{a_{0}+\bar{q}^{\prime}(\alpha-a_{1})}\Bigr\rceil&\text{ if }\alpha\geq a_{1},\\[6.0pt]
C_{\gamma}N_{L}^{\gamma}\Bigl\lceil N_{L}^{a_{0}+p^{\prime}(\alpha-a_{1})}\Bigr\rceil&\text{ if }\alpha<a_{1}\text{ and $p\in(1,2]$},\end{cases}\\
&\lesssim_{(\alpha,\gamma,q,A)}\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-\bar{q}^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma+a_{0}-a_{1}\bar{q}^{\prime}}{\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha\geq a_{1},\\
\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-p^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma+a_{0}-a_{1}p^{\prime}}{\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha<a_{1}\text{ and $p\in(1,2]$},\end{cases}\end{split}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The complexity bound
of Theorem
3.6

identifies two different cases:
If the rate
\alpha
is sufficiently large, i.e.,

\alpha\geq a_{1}
,
then the single-level Monte Carlo error achieves
an error of size
\epsilon
with an asymptotic cost
that is
independent
of the type
p
of
E
,
and only determined by the integrability parameter
\bar{q}
,
and constants
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
.
Conversely, if
\alpha<a_{1}
,
achieving the desired accuracy
requires a higher-dimensional subspace
E_{L}\subseteq E
,
and then the asymptotic complexity
does depend on the type
p
.

Furthermore, note that if, in addition to
Assumption
3.4
,
one assumes that the constants
a_{0},a_{1}

satisfy
a_{0}-a_{1}p^{\prime}=0

(which, e.g., is satisfied by the spaces

\ell^{s}
for all
s\in(1,\infty)
),
then the second case of
(
3.9
)
reduces to

\mathcal{C}^{\sf SL}_{q}(X)\lesssim\epsilon^{-p^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}

which is the same asymptotic complexity obtained
in a Monte Carlo error analysis that does not account for
dimension-dependent type constants
[
25
]
.

3.2.
Multilevel Monte Carlo methods

In this subsection we focus on the analysis of
multilevel Monte Carlo methods.
To this end, we make an additional
assumption on the
finite-dimensional subspaces
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Assumption 3.8

(Nestedness)
.

The family
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
of
subspaces
of
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is nested,

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq E_{\ell}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq E.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

are finite-dimensional subspaces
of
E
satisfying
Assumption
3.8
.
Let
{X\in L^{1}(\Omega;E)}
,

q\in[1,\infty)
,
and define
{\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}}
.
Assume further that
L\in\mathbb{N}
and,
for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,

{X_{\ell}\in L^{q}(\Omega;E_{\ell})}
,

M_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}

are independent
copies of the
E_{\ell}
-valued
random variable

(3.11)

\xi_{\ell}:=X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\in L^{q}(\Omega;E_{\ell}),\qquad X_{0}:=0\in E_{1}.

Then, we have, for all
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[X]-\mathbb{E}[X_{L}]\bigr\|_{E}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2K_{q,r}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Bigl[\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\bigl\|X_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}]-(X_{\ell-1}-\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell-1}])\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\Bigr].

Proof.

First note that, for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}

the random variables

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}

are identically distributed
and we have that

\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\Biggr]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell-1}]\bigr)=\mathbb{E}[X_{L}].

Thus, we find by the triangle inequality
on
L^{q}(\Omega;E)
that

\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[X]-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[X-X_{L}]\bigr\|_{E}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\!.

By Proposition
3.2
above we obtain,
for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
and all
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
, that

\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq 2K_{q,r}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\bigl\|\xi_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;E)},

which leads to the claimed estimate.
∎

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\sf ML}_{q;r}(X)\lesssim_{(\beta,\gamma,a_{0},a_{1},A,r,q)}\begin{cases}N_{L}^{\gamma}+N_{L}^{\alpha r^{\prime}\!}&\text{ if }(\beta+a_{1})r^{\prime}>\gamma+a_{0},\\
N_{L}^{\gamma}+N_{L}^{\alpha r^{\prime}\!}L^{r^{\prime}+1}&\text{ if }(\beta+a_{1})r^{\prime}=\gamma+a_{0},\\
N_{L}^{\gamma}+N_{L}^{\alpha r^{\prime}\!+\gamma+a_{0}-(\beta+a_{1})r^{\prime}\!}&\text{ if }(\beta+a_{1})r^{\prime}<\gamma+a_{0},\end{cases}

and behaves in terms of the accuracy

\epsilon
as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\sf ML}_{q;r}(X)\lesssim_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,a_{0},a_{1},A,r,q)}\begin{cases}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-r^{\prime}}&\text{ if }r^{\prime}>t,\\
\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-r^{\prime}}|\log_{A}\epsilon|^{r^{\prime}+1}&\text{ if }r^{\prime}=t,\\
\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{-r^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma+a_{0}-(\beta+a_{1})r^{\prime}\!}{\alpha}}&\text{ if }r^{\prime}<t,\end{cases}

where
t:=\frac{\gamma+a_{0}}{\beta+a_{1}}
.
Finally, since the preceding error and cost bounds
hold for all
r\in R(p,q)=[p,q]\cap(1,2]
, we
can minimize the costs
by choosing

r^{\prime}\!\in[q^{\prime}\!,p^{\prime}]\cap[2,\infty)

and, thus,

r\in R(p,q)

as follows:

\displaystyle r^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\bar{q}^{\prime}&\text{ if }t\leq\bar{q}^{\prime}\!,\\
\bar{q}^{\prime}&\text{ if }t\in(\bar{q}^{\prime}\!,p^{\prime}]\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha\geq\beta+a_{1},\\
t&\text{ if }t\in(\bar{q}^{\prime}\!,p^{\prime}]\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha<\beta+a_{1},\\
\bar{q}^{\prime}&\text{ if }t>p^{\prime}\!\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha\geq\beta+a_{1},\\
p^{\prime}&\text{ if }t>p^{\prime}\!\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha<\beta+a_{1}.\end{cases}

This choice yields the cost bound
(
3.13
)
which completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Extension to
k
th moments

The analysis presented in
Subsections
3.1
and
3.2

can straightforwardly be generalized
to the estimation of
k
th injective moments.
The technical tools are slight generalizations
of the error estimates
derived in
[
25
]
,
which are explicit in the dependence on the type constant.

The following error estimate
plays for
k
th moments the same role as
Proposition
3.2
for the first moment.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
,

k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and set
\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}
.
For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
M_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}\in L^{kq}(\Omega;E_{\ell})
are

random variables taking values in a finite-dimensional

.
Then,
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
for all
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
,
and for any

\displaystyle\biggl\|U-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\otimes^{k}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,s}E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}_{\varepsilon}^{k}[\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{q,k}^{\sf SL}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|\xi_{\ell,1}\|^{k}_{L^{kq}(\Omega;E)},

where
C_{q,k}^{\sf SL}:=2(2kK_{q,1}+B_{q})
,

being the
Khintchine
and Kahane–Khintchine
constants
from Definitions
2.3

and
2.4
, respectively.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\biggl\|U-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\otimes^{k}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,s}E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}_{\varepsilon}^{k}[\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}\left[\xi_{\ell,1}\right]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\otimes^{k}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,s}E)}.

Since
E_{\ell}
is of type
r
for any
r\in[1,\bar{q}]
,
the second term on the right-hand side
can be bounded
by an application of
[
25
, Theorem 3.16]
,
which yields the estimate

\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}\left[\xi_{\ell,1}\right]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\otimes^{k}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,s}E)}\leq 2(2kK_{q,r}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})+B_{q})M_{\ell}^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\|\xi_{\ell,1}\|^{k}_{L^{kq}(\Omega;E)}.

The claim follows then using that

K_{q,r}\leq K_{q,1}

and
\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})\geq 1
for any

,
see (
3.4
) and, e.g.,

[
21
, Section 7.1.a]
.
∎

In the case that, in addition to the assumptions made in
Proposition
3.11
,
there exist constants

such that

\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}N_{\ell}^{\alpha}\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}_{\varepsilon}^{k}[\xi_{\ell,1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq C_{\alpha},\qquad\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\,\|\xi_{\ell,1}\|^{k}_{L^{kq}(\Omega;E)}\leq C_{\sf stab},

we obtain, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

and for all

, the estimate

(3.16)

\biggl\|U-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\otimes^{k}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,s}E)}\leq C_{\alpha}N_{\ell}^{-\alpha}+C_{q,k}^{\sf SL}C_{\sf stab}\tau_{r}(E_{\ell})M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)},

which has the same structure as
(
3.3
),
modulo the constants.
We thus derive the following complexity result
for single-level Monte Carlo methods
for
k
th moments.

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume that

We conclude this section by deriving a complexity result
for the multilevel Monte Carlo method for the estimation of

The next proposition adapts to our needs

Proposition 3.13
.

Suppose that

4.
Optimized Monte Carlo error analysis in
L^{p}
spaces based on Minkowski’s integral inequality

In this section, we restrict our study to
L^{p}
spaces,
and we present an analysis that,
for a class of
L^{p}(S)
-valued random variables,
under an additional integrability assumption
on the random variables
and an approximation property
of the measure space
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
,
permits to obtain sharper Monte Carlo error estimates
than those provided by
the analysis based on Rademacher types.

We start with recalling the following classical result,
see
[
34
, Appendix A.1]

or
[
20
, Propositions 1.2.22 and 1.2.25]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Minkowski’s integral inequality)
.

Assume that
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
and
(T,\mathcal{T},\nu)
are two

\sigma
-finite measure spaces
and let
f\colon(S\times T,\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{T})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))

be measurable.
Then we have, for every
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,

(4.1)

\biggl[\int_{T}\biggl(\int_{S}|f(s,t)|^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(t)\biggr]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\biggl[\int_{S}\biggl(\int_{T}|f(s,t)|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(t)\biggr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\biggr]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In particular, the Bochner spaces

L^{q}(T;L^{p}(S))=L^{q}(T,\mathcal{T},\nu;L^{p}(S,\mathcal{S},\mu))

and

L^{p}(S;L^{q}(T))=L^{p}(S,\mathcal{S},\mu;L^{q}(T,\mathcal{T},\nu))

satisfy the following relation:

\forall\;1\leq p\leq q<\infty:\qquad\|f\|_{L^{q}(T;L^{p}(S))}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(T))},

holding for all

f\colon S\times T\to\mathbb{R}

measurable,
and
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(T))

may be identified with
a subspace of
L^{q}(T;L^{p}(S))

which is continuously embedded
in
L^{q}(T;L^{p}(S))
.

Remark 4.2
.

For Minkowki’s integral inequality
(Theorem
4.1
),
it is assumed that

f
is measurable on the
product measurable space
(S\times T,\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{T})
.
In what follows,
we will consider
a probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
,
a
\sigma
-finite measure space

(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
, and

L^{p}(S)
-valued random variables
in
L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))
for some

p,q\in[1,\infty)
.
We note that
Bochner integrability
of
\eta\colon\Omega\to L^{p}(S)

implies that there exists
a measurable
function on the product space

\widetilde{\eta}\colon(S\times\Omega,\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{A})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
,
which is

(\mu\otimes\mathbb{P})
-almost everywhere
uniquely defined,
such that

\eta(\omega)=\widetilde{\eta}(\,\cdot\,,\omega)

in
L^{p}(S)

for
\mathbb{P}
-almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,
see e.g.
[
20
, Proposition 1.2.25]
.
The additional integrability condition

\eta\in L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
means that the
mapping
s\mapsto\widetilde{\eta}(s,\,\cdot\,)
is
in
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
.
Finally, we sometimes write

\eta(s)

or

\eta(s,\omega)

for
s\in S
,
\omega\in\Omega
,
and it will be clear from the context
that we are referring to

\widetilde{\eta}(s,\,\cdot\,)

and
\widetilde{\eta}(s,\omega)
, respectively.

Definition 4.3

(Approximation by averaging)
.

We say that a
\sigma
-finite
measure space

(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
satisfies the

approximation-by-averaging property

if, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a family

\mathfrak{B}_{n}=\{B_{\kappa}^{n}:\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\}
,
which contains finitely or countably infinitely many
pairwise disjoint measurable subsets of
S

of positive, finite
\mu
-measure,

(4.2)

\forall\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}:\quad B_{\kappa}^{n}\in\mathcal{S},\quad\mu(B_{\kappa}^{n})\in(0,\infty),\quad B_{\kappa}^{n}\cap B_{j}^{n}=\emptyset\;\;\text{if}\;\;j\neq\kappa,

such that,
for any
Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
,
all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
and every function
f\in L^{p}(S;E)
,
the sequence of functions

(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

defined by averaging

with respect to
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
,

(4.3)

f_{n}:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{\kappa}^{n})}\int_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}f(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right),

converges to

in the norm of
L^{p}(S;E)
, i.e.,

.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that by Definition

,
for every

the family
\mathfrak{B}_{n}

and thus

and the subsets

are independent
of the choice
of the Banach space
E
,
the integrability
p\in[1,\infty)
,
and the function

.

Remark 4.5
.

The fact that,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the sets
\{B_{\kappa}^{n}:\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\}

of
\mathfrak{B}_{n}

are pairwise disjoint implies,

in (

)

)

.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that

(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
is a

\sigma
-finite measure space
which satisfies the approximation-by-averaging
property of Definition
4.3
.
Let

p,q\in[1,\infty)
,

{M\in\mathbb{N}}
,
and

\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}

be
independent and identically
distributed
L^{p}(S)
-valued random variables
in
L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))\cap L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))

with vanishing mean,

\mathbb{E}[\eta_{1}]=0
.

There exist
sequences

(\eta_{1}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\ldots,(\eta_{M}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of simple functions in

with the property that,
for every

, there are finitely many pairwise disjoint
sets

with positive, finite
\mu
-measure
and zero-mean

Y_{11}^{n},\ldots,Y_{1K_{n}}^{n},\ldots,Y_{M1}^{n},\ldots,Y_{MK_{n}\!}^{n}\in L^{q}(\Omega)

such that, for all

,

(4.5)

\eta_{j}^{n}=\sum\limits_{\kappa=1}^{K_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}^{n}}Y_{j\kappa}^{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j}^{n}\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}=0,

and, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
,
we have that

(4.6)

Y_{1\kappa}^{n},\ldots,Y_{M\kappa}^{n}\text{ are independent and
identically distributed}.

Proof.

Since
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
has the
approximation-by averaging property,
there exists a sequence of families

\mathfrak{B}_{n}=\{B_{\kappa}^{n}:\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
each of finite or countably infinite
cardinality
|\mathscr{K}_{n}|
and
containing subsets
B_{\kappa}^{n}\subseteq S
satisfying (
4.2
),
such that for any
f\in L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))

the sequence
f_{n}
defined via (
4.3
)
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
.

Case 1:

|\mathscr{K}_{n}|<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If the cardinality
K_{n}:=|\mathscr{K}_{n}|

of the family
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we may choose the sets

\{A_{1}^{n},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}^{n}\}=\{B_{\kappa}^{n}:\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\}

(in an arbitrary but fixed ordering)
and define,
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
,
the random variables

Y_{j\kappa}^{n}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

(4.7)

Y_{j\kappa}^{n}:=\frac{1}{\mu(A_{\kappa}^{n})}\int_{A_{\kappa}^{n}}\eta_{j}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\langle g_{\kappa}^{n},\eta_{j}\rangle,\qquad g_{\kappa}^{n}:=\frac{1}{\mu(A_{\kappa}^{n})}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}^{n}},

where
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle

denotes the duality pairing between

(L^{p}(S))^{\prime}\cong L^{p^{\prime}}\!(S)
and
L^{p}(S)
.
Note that

g_{\kappa}^{n}\in L^{p^{\prime}}\!(S)
holds
with
\|g_{\kappa}^{n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\!(S)}=\mu(A_{\kappa}^{n})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!
,
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
.
As
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}
are assumed to be zero-mean and
independent, identically distributed as

L^{p}(S)
-valued random variables in
L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))
,
we thus conclude that,
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
all
n\in\mathbb{N}

and every
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
,

(4.8)

Y_{j\kappa}^{n}\in L^{q}(\Omega)\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_{j\kappa}^{n}\bigr]=\langle g_{\kappa}^{n},\mathbb{E}[\eta_{j}]\rangle=0,

and, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every

\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
,

(4.9)

\bigl(Y_{j\kappa}^{n}\bigr)_{j=1}^{M}=\bigl(\langle g_{\kappa}^{n},\eta_{j}\rangle\bigr)_{j=1}^{M}\quad\text{are independent and identically distributed}.

Finally, setting

\eta_{j}^{n}:=\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}^{n}}Y_{j\kappa}^{n}
,

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the convergence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{j}^{n}=\eta_{j}

in
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
,
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
is immediate from the
construction using the
approximation-by-averaging property
of
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
.

Case 2:

|\mathscr{K}_{n}|

is countably infinite for at least one
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
by unconditional convergence of the series
in (
4.3
),
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

and all
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
there exists a finite subset

\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{n}
, i.e.,

|\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}|<\infty
,
such that

\Biggl\|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}<\frac{1}{Mn},\qquad\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}:=\frac{1}{\mu(B_{\kappa}^{n})}\int_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\eta_{j}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

We then consider, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathscr{J}_{n}:=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{M}\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j},\qquad K_{n}:=|\mathscr{J}_{n}|<\infty,\qquad\{A_{1}^{n},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}^{n}\}=\{B_{\kappa}^{n}:\kappa\in\mathscr{J}_{n}\},

where again the ordering
of the sets

A_{1}^{n},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}^{n}

is arbitrary but fixed.
Furthermore, we
define, for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K_{n}\}
,
the random variables

Y_{j\kappa}^{n}
as in (
4.7
).
Then,
(
4.8
)
and (
4.9
) still hold
and, for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
the sequence

\eta_{j}^{n}:=\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}^{n}}Y_{j\kappa}^{n}
,

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
converges to
\eta_{j}

in
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))
.
The latter follows, since
the sequence

\widetilde{\eta}_{j}^{n}:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{\mu(B_{\kappa}^{n})}\int_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\eta_{j}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\biggr)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

has the limit
\eta_{j}
in
L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))

by the approximation-by-averaging
property of
(S,\mathcal{S},\mu)
,
and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\eta}_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle-\eta_{j}^{n}\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}=\Biggl\|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}-\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{J}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl\|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{M}\Biggl\|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr{J}_{n}^{i}\setminus\mathscr{J}_{n}^{j}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{\kappa}^{n}}\,\overline{\eta}_{j\kappa}^{n}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))}<\frac{1}{n},

by the choice of

and

Proof of Proposition
4.9
.

Step 1:

We first prove the claim
in the case that,
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,

\eta_{j}\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{p}(S))\cap L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega))

\eta_{j}=\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}}Y_{j\kappa}
,
where
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
the sets

A_{1},\ldots,A_{K}\in\mathcal{S}
are pairwise disjoint,

\mu(A_{\kappa})\in(0,\infty)

for every
\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
,
and

\forall\kappa\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:\quad Y_{1\kappa},\ldots,Y_{M\kappa}\in L^{q}(\Omega)\ \text{are i.i.d.\ and have mean zero}.

To this end,
let
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
be a second probability space
with expectation operator
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
, and

(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

denote
the product probability space.
Let
(r_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
be a Rademacher family on
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

and, for
j\in\left\{1,\dots,M\right\}
,
let
\bm{r}_{j}\colon\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\rightarrow\left\{-1,1\right\}

and

\bm{\eta}_{j}\colon\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\rightarrow L^{p}(S)

denote the mappings that satisfy

\forall(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})\in\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}:\quad\bm{r}_{j}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})=r_{j}(\widetilde{\omega}),\quad\;\;\bm{\eta}_{j}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})=\eta_{j}(\omega)=\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{A_{\kappa}}Y_{j\kappa}(\omega).

Note that on
(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

the random variables
(\bm{r})_{j=1}^{M}
are a Rademacher family,
and
(\bm{\eta}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
and
(\bm{r}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
are independent.
Then, by symmetrization
and Minkowski’s integral inequality
(see Lemma
2.2
,
Theorem
4.1

and Remark
4.2

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bm{\eta}_{j}

\displaystyle\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};L^{p}(S))}\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bm{r}_{j}\bm{\eta}_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};L^{p}(S))}\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bm{r}_{j}\bm{\eta}_{j}\biggr\|_{L^{p}(S;L^{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}))}

\displaystyle=2\Biggl[\int_{S}\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\eta_{j}(s,\omega)\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\Biggr]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore,
by the Khintchine inequalities
(see Definition
2.3
)
and the fact that
(\mathbb{R},|\,\cdot\,|)
has
Rademacher type

\bar{q}:=\min\{q,2\}
,
with
\tau_{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R})=1
,
we find that

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bm{\eta}_{j}

\displaystyle\biggr\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};L^{p}(S))}\leq 2B_{q}\Biggl[\int_{S}\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\eta_{j}(s,\omega)\biggr\|_{L^{\bar{q}}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}^{q}\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\Biggr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

Remark 4.19

In this section, we have so far discussed
single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
to approximate the mean of an

5.1.
Linear elliptic boundary value problem with random forcing

Let
I:=(0,1)
,
\eta\in(1,2)
and, for every
y\in I
, let

f_{y}\colon I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a forcing term defined as
f_{y}(x):=C_{\eta}|x-y|^{-\eta}
,

C_{\eta}
being a suitable constant to be specified later, and
g_{y}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For a given
y\in I
,
we consider the boundary value problem

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -u_{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)&=f_{y}(x),\quad\forall x\in I,\\
u_{y}(0)&=0,\\
u_{y}^{\prime}(1)&=g_{y}.\end{aligned}\right.

Problem (
5.1
) requires a nonstandard variational formulation since,
for any
y\in I
and
\eta\in(1,2)
,
f_{y}\notin L^{1}(I)
,
and thus the forcing term has to be interpreted as an element of a suitable dual space.
Since
I
has dimension one, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
, elements in
W^{1,p}(I)
coincide
(upon modification on a subset of
\overline{I}
of zero Lebesgue measure)
with a unique function
\tilde{v}\in C^{0}(\overline{I})
.
Hence, we may define the subspace

W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I):=\{v\in W^{1,p}(I):\;\tilde{v}(0)=0\},

and we denote by
W^{-1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)
the dual space of
W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I)
.
Due to the Poincaré inequality,

W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)
can be equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}:=\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(I)}
.

By multiplying formally the first equation in (
5.1
)
with a test function
v

and integrating by parts both sides,
we derive the following variational formulation for any
p\in(1,\infty)
:

(5.2)

\text{Find }u_{y}\in W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)\text{ such that}\quad B(u_{y},v)=L_{y}(v)\quad\forall v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I),

where

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\colon W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)\times W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle B(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{I}u^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle L_{y}

\displaystyle\colon W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle L_{y}(v)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{I}v^{\prime}(x)F_{y}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+v(1)(F_{y}(1)+g_{y}),

and
F_{y}(x):=C_{\eta}\operatorname{sign}(x-y)|x-y|^{1-\eta}/(1-\eta)

is a primitive of
f_{y}
.
For any
p\in(1,\infty)
,
the bilinear form
B
is continuous and satisfies the inf-sup condition

\inf_{0\neq w\in W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}\sup_{0\neq v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I)}\frac{B(w,v)}{\|w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}\|v\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\{0\}}(I)}}\geq 1,

together with its symmetric counterpart,
see
[
25
, Section 4.2.1]

and
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
for a constructive proof.
In addition, provided that
p\in\bigl(1,\frac{1}{\eta-1}\bigr)
,
for any
y\in I
, we have that
F_{y}\in L^{p}(I)
and

\|L_{y}\|_{W^{-1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}=\|F_{y}(1)+g_{y}-F_{y}\|_{L^{p}(I)}<\infty.

Therefore, for any
y\in I
,
\eta\in(1,2)
and
p\in(1,\frac{1}{\eta-1})
,
(
5.2
) admits a unique solution
u_{y}\in W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)
,
satisfying the a-priori bound

\|u_{y}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}\leq\|L_{y}\|_{W^{-1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}
.
The range
\eta\in\bigl[\frac{3}{2},2\bigr)
is of particular interest since then
p\in(1,2)
.

Next, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space

The previous discussion permits to construct pathwise,

and

, and the nodal interpolant

\mathcal{I}^{2}_{h_{\ell}}\colon C^{0}(\overline{I\times I})\rightarrow S_{0}^{1,1}(I\times I,\mathcal{T}^{2}_{h_{\ell}})
,
satisfying
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{h_{\ell}}=\mathcal{I}^{1}_{h_{\ell}}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{1}_{h_{\ell}}
.
Since a closed form expression for
\mathbb{E}[u\otimes u]
is not available,
we used in our experiments a numerically computed “reference second moment”,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{I}^{2}_{h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}}\!(u\otimes u)\bigr]
.
To this end, we used a sufficiently small mesh size

h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}=2^{-\ell_{\text{ref}}}
,
\ell_{\text{ref}}\in\mathbb{N}
,
which is stored as matrix of dimension
(N_{h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}}\!+1)\times(N_{h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}}\!+1)
.
Its
(i,j)
th element corresponds to

(5.6)

(\mathbb{E}[u\otimes u])(x_{i},x_{j})=\int_{0}^{1}u_{y}(x_{i})u_{y}(x_{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

where
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}}\!

for any
i,j\in\left\{0,\dots,N_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}\right\}
.
Since the mapping
y\rightarrow u_{y}(x_{i})u_{y}(x_{j})

is not differentiable at
y=x_{i}
and
y=x_{j}
,
the integral in (
5.6
) is split
over the subintervals
[0,\min\{x_{i},x_{j}\}]
,
[\min\{x_{i},x_{j}\},\max\{x_{i},x_{j}\}]
and
[\max\{x_{i},x_{j}\},1]
.
Then, each of the three integrals is approximated using an adaptive composite
hp
quadrature formula.
Similarly to the first moment, given
p\in(1,2]
,
we set
\eta=1+\frac{1}{p}-10^{-2}
, and estimate through
least squares a pair
(C_{\alpha_{p}},\alpha_{p})\in(0,\infty)^{2}

such that

(5.7)

\bigl\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{I}^{2}_{h_{\ell_{\text{ref}}}}\!(u\otimes u)\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathcal{I}^{2}_{h_{\ell}}(u\otimes u)\bigr]\bigr\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}\leq C_{\alpha_{p}}h_{\ell}^{\alpha_{p}},\quad\ell<\ell_{\text{ref}}.

Since
(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto(\mathbb{E}[u\otimes u])(x,x^{\prime})
has a kink along the diagonal
x^{\prime}=x
,
we observe rates
\alpha_{p}<1
, which deteriorate as
p
decreases.
Note further that (
5.7
) involves the

numerical evaluation of the injective tensor norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)}
.
The numerical algorithm we used for this purpose
is detailed in the Appendix.

shows the decay of

Rademacher type of the image space of the random variable,
is not merely of theoretical interest,
but also of practical relevance.

6.
Conclusion

We investigated the convergence of single- and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
for random variables with finite
k
th moments
in Banach spaces
E
of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
.
We showed, theoretically and numerically,
that Monte Carlo sample sizes to reach a prescribed target tolerance

\epsilon\in(0,1]
must in general account for the Rademacher type of
E
.
In favorable cases, we have also shown that
the theoretical convergence rate
1-\frac{1}{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}

can be overly pessimistic for actual numerical computations
due to the finite dimension of the approximation subspaces.
The present findings have implications for multilevel strategies
in numerical statistical estimation
for solutions of nonlinear PDEs
with random data which are well-posed
in non-Hilbertian Banach spaces
of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2)
as, e.g.,
degenerate convection-diffusion equations
[
26
]

or compressible fluid flows
[
29
]
.

Future efforts are aimed at extending the present framework to anisotropic
k
-fold correlations
of the form
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{k}]
,
where
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
may belong to different Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}

of different Rademacher types
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in[1,2]
, and to the analysis of sparse estimators.

Appendix A
Computation of the injective norm

We here detail an algorithm to numerically approximate
the injective tensor norm
\|\cdot\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}
, for
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Recall that for a
U\in\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)
with representation
U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}u_{j,1}\otimes u_{j,2}
,
the injective tensor norm is defined as

\|U\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}:=\sup\Biggl\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}f_{1}(u_{j,1})f_{2}(u_{j,2})\biggr|:f_{1},f_{2}\in B_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}\Biggr\},

B_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}
being the unit ball in
W^{-1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)
.
Due to
[
6
, Proposition 8.14]
,
for any
f\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)
there exists an element
l\in L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)
such that

f(u)=\int_{I}u^{\prime}(x)l(x)\;\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=:l(u),\;\;\forall u\in W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I),\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}\!}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}=\|l\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}.

Hence, the injective tensor norm can be equivalently reformulated as

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}=\sup\Biggl\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}l_{1}(u_{j,1})l_{2}(u_{j,2})\biggr|:l_{1},l_{2}\in B_{{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Biggl\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}l_{1}(u_{j,1})l_{2}(u_{j,2})\biggr|:l_{1},l_{2}\in{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}\text{ s.t.}\ \|l_{1}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}\leq 1,\;\|l_{2}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}\leq 1\Biggr\}.

Our procedure consists in a finite dimensional approximation of
l_{1}
and
l_{2}

in order to relate the computation of the supremum in (
A.1
)
to a finite dimensional optimization problem.
The latter is then solved using an alternating procedure.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a partition of
I
(see Section
5.1
),
and denote the space of continuous, piecewise polynomials of order
k\geq 0
by

S^{k}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\bigl\{v\in C^{0}(\overline{I}):\;v|_{I_{h}^{j}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k},\;j\in\left\{0,\dots,N_{h}-1\right\}\bigr\}.

Although it is possible to consider any polynomial order,
in our numerical experiments we set
k=1
.
Furthermore, let
\widetilde{N}_{h}
denote the dimension of
S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})

and
\mathcal{B}:=\left\{\varphi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}

be the nodal basis for
S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
For any
l\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
,
{\bm{l}=(l_{i})_{i=1}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}

is the vector expressing the coefficients of
l

with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
.

Next, we approximate the supremum in (
A.1
) by restricting
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
to
S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
, leading to the maximization problem,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\max_{l_{1},l_{2}\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}l_{1}(u_{j,1})l_{2}(u_{j,2})\biggr|

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\|l_{1}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}\leq 1,\quad\|l_{2}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}\leq 1.

Problem (
A.2
) is amenable to numerical solution,
using numerical routines to approximate the norm on
L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)
,
and observing that for any
l\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\{0\}}(I)
,

l(u)=\bm{l}^{\top}\bm{c}(u)
where
\bm{c}(u)_{i}=\int_{I}u^{\prime}\varphi_{i}
,

i\in\bigl\{1,\dots,\widetilde{N}_{h}\bigr\}
.
The computational cost could however be prohibitive.
A favorable setting arises when
U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}u^{h}_{j,1}\otimes u^{h}_{j,2}
,
with
u^{h}_{j,i}\in S^{1}_{0}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})\subset S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

i=1,2
,
j\in\{1,\dots,M\}
, as it is the case in Subsection
5.1
.
Indeed, for any
u\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
l\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
,
l(u)=\bm{l}^{\top}H\bm{u}
,
where
H\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}\times\widetilde{N}_{h}}
with
(H)_{i,j}:=\int_{I}\varphi_{i}\varphi^{\prime}_{j}
.
Denoting with
U_{M}
the matrix representation of
U
into the tensorized basis
\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}
,
we then have

\sum_{j=1}^{M}l_{1}(u_{j,1})l_{2}(u_{j,2})=\bm{l}_{1}^{\top}HU_{M}H^{\top}\bm{l}_{2}.

Next, for any
l\in S^{1}(I,\mathcal{T}_{h})
we approximate
\|l\|_{L^{p^{\prime}\!}(I)}
using a trapezoidal rule,

\|l\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(I)}\approx\sum_{i=0}^{N_{h}-1}\frac{|l(x_{i})|^{p^{\prime}}+|l(x_{i+1})|^{p^{\prime}\!}}{2}\,h=\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}|l_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}d_{i}^{p^{\prime}}=\|D\bm{l}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}^{p^{\prime}}=:\|\bm{l}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h}\!,D}}\!,

where
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{\widetilde{N}_{h}})
,
with
d_{1}=d_{\widetilde{N}_{h}}=\bigl(\tfrac{h}{2}\bigr)^{1/p^{\prime}}

and
d_{i}=h^{1/p^{\prime}}\!
, for any
i\in\{2,\dots,\widetilde{N}_{h}-1\}
.
Any quadrature formula can be used in what follows,
provided that it leads to a positive diagonal matrix
D
.

We are thus led to consider the optimization problem

(A.3)

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{l}_{1},\bm{l}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}|\bm{l}_{1}^{\top}HU_{M}H^{\top}\bm{l}_{2}|

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\|\bm{l}_{1}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}\!\leq 1,\quad\|\bm{l}_{2}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}\!\leq 1,

To solve (
A.3
) we consider an iterative alternating procedure.
Starting from two initial guesses
\bm{l}_{1}^{0},\bm{l}_{2}^{0}
satisfying the constraints,
at each iteration
k\geq 1
we solve separately,

(A.4)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}&\max\limits_{\bm{l}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}|\bm{l}_{1}^{\top}\bm{h}_{2}^{k-1}|&&\max\limits_{\bm{l}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}|\bm{l}_{2}^{\top}\bm{h}_{1}^{k}|\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad\|\bm{l}_{1}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}\!\leq 1,&&\text{s.t.}\quad\|\bm{l}_{2}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}\!\leq 1,\end{array}

with
\bm{h}_{i}^{k}:=HU_{M}H^{\top}\bm{l}_{i}^{k}
,
i=1,2
,
and
(\bm{l}_{1}^{k},\bm{l}_{2}^{k})
denote the approximated maximizers computed at iteration
k
.
Each subproblem in (
A.4
) consists in the maximization of the absolute value of a linear function over the unit ball of a weighted
\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}
space. A close formula is available.
Indeed, for a general problem

\displaystyle\max\limits_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}}|\bm{l}^{\top}\bm{c}|

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\|\bm{l}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}=1,

we remark that

|\bm{l}^{\top}\bm{c}|=\biggl|\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}d_{i}l_{i}\tfrac{1}{d_{i}}c_{i}\biggr|\leq\|\bm{l}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}\|\bm{c}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D^{-1}}}=\|\bm{c}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D^{-1}}},

hence the objective functional is bounded from above by
\|\bm{c}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D^{-1}}}
.
By direct construction, the vector
\bm{l}^{\star}
of components

(A.5)

l^{\star}_{i}=\frac{\operatorname{sign}(c_{i})|c_{i}|^{p-1}}{d_{i}^{p}}\frac{1}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{N}_{h}}d_{i}^{p^{\prime}(1-p)}|c_{i}|^{p^{\prime}(p-1)}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}},

i\in\{1,\dots,\widetilde{N}_{h}\}
, satisfies
\|\bm{l}^{\star}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D}}=1

and
(\bm{l}^{\star})^{\top}\bm{c}=\|\bm{c}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\widetilde{N}_{h},D^{-1}}}
,
and thus is the sought maximizer.
The overall procedure to approximate the injective norm is summarized in Algorithm 1.

Algorithm 1

Approximation of
\|U\|_{\otimes_{\varepsilon}^{2}W^{1,p}_{\left\{0\right\}}(I)}

1:

Input
:
U_{M}
,
H
,
\bm{l}_{1}^{0}
,
\bm{l}_{2}^{0}
, max_it, Tol.

2:

\bm{l}_{j}^{0}=\bm{l}_{j}^{0}/\|\bm{l}^{0}_{j}\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime},D}}
,
j=1,2
. (Normalize)

3:

Set
k=1
,
\text{Err}=1
,
f^{0}_{\text{opt}}=(\bm{l}_{1}^{0})^{\top}HU_{M}H^{\top}\bm{l}_{2}^{0}
.

4:

while

k\leq\text{max\_it}
and
\text{Err}>Tol

do

5:

Compute
\bm{l}_{1}^{k}
solving the first subproblem in (
A.4
) using (
A.5
).

6:

Compute
\bm{l}_{2}^{k}
solving the second subproblem in (
A.4
) using (
A.5
).

7:

Compute
f^{k}_{\text{opt}}=|(\bm{l}^{k}_{1})^{\top}HU_{M}H^{\top}\bm{l}^{k}_{2}|
.

8:

Set
\text{Err}=|f^{k}_{\text{opt}}-f^{k-1}_{\text{opt}}|
,
k=k+1
.

9:

end

while

10:

Output
:
f^{k-1}_{\text{opt}}
.
