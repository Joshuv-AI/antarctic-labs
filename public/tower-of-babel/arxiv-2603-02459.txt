A survey of sampling discretization of integral and uniform norms
F. Dai, E. Kosov and V. Temlyakov

Abstract
This paper surveys recent developments in the sampling discretization of integral and uniform norms for functions in general finite-dimensional spaces. These results generalize the classical Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for trigonometric and algebraic polynomials, which play a crucial role in Fourier analysis, interpolation, and approximation theory. We focus on the problem in the broad context of finite-dimensional subspaces, where norms defined by general probability measures are approximated by their discrete counterparts. The primary emphasis is on results closely related to the authors’ recent research. A key objective is to highlight the main ideas and techniques that form the foundation of the proofs in this area. This survey serves as a complement to three recently published survey papers on sampling discretization [ 15 , 31 , 44 ] . MSC: 41-02, 41A17, 41A10, 47-02

Introduction

The classical Marcinkiewicz inequality has the following original form (see
[
67
, Vol. II, p. 30]
): For any
1<p<\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{p}\geq 1
depending only on
p
such that for every

f\in\mathcal{T}_{n}:=\operatorname{span}\bigl\{e^{\mathbf{i}kx}\colon k\in\mathbb{Z},\,-n\leq k\leq n\bigr\}
,

C_{p}^{-1}\|f\|_{L_{p}[0,2\pi]}\leq\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Lambda)}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}[0,2\pi]},

(1.1)

where
\Lambda=\Lambda_{2n+1}:=\bigl\{\frac{2j\pi}{2n+1}\colon j=0,1,\ldots,2n\bigr\}
,
and
\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Lambda)}
denotes the
L_{p}
-norm of
f
defined with respect to the uniform probability measure on
\Lambda
; that is,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Lambda)}:

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum\limits_{\omega\,\in\Lambda}|f(\omega)|^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p},&\ \ \text{if $1\leq p<\infty$,}\\
\max\limits_{\omega\,\in\Lambda}|f(\omega)|,&\ \ \text{if $p=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Throughout this paper,
|E|
denotes the cardinality of a finite set
E
. It is important to note that the constant
C_{p}
on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) can be chosen to be independent of
p
. Indeed, the right-hand inequality in (
1.1
) remains true
at the endpoints
p=1
,
\infty
. However, the left-hand inequality requires stronger conditions at the endpoints. It holds for the full range of
1\leq p\leq\infty
with
\Lambda_{4n+1}
in place of

\Lambda_{2n+1}
.

Historically, the inequality (
1.1
) for the case
p=\infty
(discretization of the uniform norm)
was first established by Bernstein in 1931-1932
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.
The result for
1<p<\infty
was proved by J. Marcinkiewicz, while A. Zygmund extended the study to the endpoints
p=1,\infty

in 1937 (see
[
31
, pp.4]
and
[
67
, Vol. II, p. 30]
). Therefore, inequalities of the form (
1.1
) are often referred to as
Bernstein-type theorems when
p=\infty
, and Marcinkiewicz-type theorems when
1\leq p<\infty
(see
[
15
,
31
,
61
,
63
]
).
In broader literature, these results are known as Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities
(see, for instance,
[
8
,
15
,
31
]
).
The Marcinkiewcz type inequality (
1.1
) is a basic tool in classical analysis, playing a crucial role in the study of Fourier series convergence, Lagrange interpolation, sampling discretization, and weighted approximation.
For various extensions of these results, we refer the reader to
[
8
,
15
,
31
,
34
,
46
,
47
,
50
]
and the references therein.

This survey focuses primarily on recent work by the authors and their collaborators concerning Marcinkiewicz-type and Bernstein-type theorems for general finite-dimensional subspaces. We do not impose structural assumptions on these spaces, except for conditions on the embedding into the space of bounded functions. Our objective is to highlight the key techniques and methods used to establish inequalities of the form (
1.1
) in this general setting. The primary technical tools rely on concentration inequalities rooted in the work of J. Bourgain, J. Lindenstrauss, V.D. Milman, G. Schechtman and others (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
29
,
51
]
and references thererin), as well as chaining arguments (see
[
58
]
). We aim to demonstrate how these techniques apply specifically to the problem of sampling discretization.

It should be noted that this survey is largely confined to results closely related to the authors’ recent research on sampling discretization.
Given the extensive literature in this area, the references provided here are by no means complete. Our intention is to focus on the results we are most familiar with, rather than covering topics beyond the immediate scope of our work. In particular, we do not discuss in detail the important and related applications in sampling recovery. For these topics, we refer the reader to the surveys
[
22
,
31
]
and the significant recent contributions by D. Krieg, M. Ullrich, T. Ullrich and their collaborators (see
[

Let us introduce a general framework for the sampling discretization problem we are going to study.
Let
(\Omega,\mu,{\mathcal{F}})
be a probability space, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
\Omega
and
\mu
is a probability measure on
{\mathcal{F}}
. Given
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
denote the
L_{p}
-space defined with respect to the measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}
given by

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}=\Bigl(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\Bigr)^{1/p}.

Let
X_{N}
denote a linear space of
{\mathcal{F}}
-measurable functions on
\Omega
of dimension
N
(where
N
is typically large).
We assume that every function in
X_{N}
is defined everywhere on
\Omega
, and satisfies

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|.

Finally, we denote the unit ball of
X_{N}
in
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
by

X_{N}^{p}:=\bigl\{f\in X_{N}\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq 1\bigr\}.

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem with weights.

Let
C_{2}\geq C_{1}>0
be two given positive constants.
The weighted Marcinkiewicz discretization of the
L_{p}
norm,
1\leq p<\infty
, in the space
X_{N}
approximates the continuous
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
norm using
a discrete signed measure supported on a finite subset
\Omega_{m}=\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
. Specifically, we require that the integral norm of every
f\in X_{N}
is bounded by a weighted discrete sum as follows:

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu,

(1.2)

where the nodes
\xi^{j}\in\Omega
and the weights
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are independent of
f\in X_{N}
.

If the estimate (
1.2
) holds for every function
f
in a subspace
X_{N}
, we say that
X_{N}
admits a

weighted Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem
with parameters
p
and

m\in\mathbb{N}
, and constants
C_{1},C_{2}
.

Of particular interest are the following special cases of (
1.2
) where the weights are uniform, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{m}=\frac{1}{m}
.

(i)

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem.

We say that
X_{N}
admits a
Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem

with parameters
p
and

m\in\mathbb{N}
, and constants
C_{1},C_{2}

if

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(1.3)

(ii)

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem with
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

We say that
X_{N}
admits a
Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem with
\epsilon
and
parameters
p
and

m\in\mathbb{N}

if

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(1.4)

The Bernstein discretization problem.
The above definition can also be modified to include the case
p=\infty
, where we ask for

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|f\left(\xi^{j}\right)\right|\leq\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Universal Marcinkiewicz-type discretization problem.

We can also consider a universal Marcinkiewicz-type discretization problem for a finite collection
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{L}
of finite-dimensional spaces of functions on
\Omega
. Given
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a finite set
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides
universal discretization
of the
L_{p}
norm for the collection
{\mathcal{X}}
with positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
if for any
f\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{L}X_{k}
,

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}.

Universal weighted discretization can be defined likewise.

In the framework described above,
X_{N}
can be either a real or complex space. For Marcinkiewicz discretization with general constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, results for complex spaces can be derived from real case by separating the real and imaginary parts. However, this reduction fails for the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem with
\epsilon>0
, as the separation argument distorts the isometric constants
(1\pm\epsilon)
. For simplicity, we generally assume
X_{N}
is a real space unless stated otherwise. Nevertheless, most results extend to complex spaces using similar techniques.

This survey presents tools and techniques for the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem on finite-dimensional spaces subject to certain additional conditions.
Typically, two main types of conditions are used: (i) bounds on entropy numbers; and (ii) Nikol’skii-type inequalities.

We postpone the definition and discussion of entropy numbers to Section
4
. In this section, we focus on Nikol’skii-type inequalities. These inequalities constitute the main assumption imposed on the subspaces in our setting and appear throughout the paper in the formulation of various results.

Nikol’skii-type inequality.

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, an
N
-dimensional subspace
X_{N}\subset L_{\infty}(\Omega)
is said to satisfy the
(p,\infty)
-Nikol’skii inequality with constant
M>0
if

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N},

(1.5)

in which case we write
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(M)
. Of particular interest is the case of
p=2
, where the exact constant
M
in (
1.5
) can be written explicitly as follows. Let
\{\varphi_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{N}
be an orthonormal basis of
X_{N}\subset L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then we have

\sup_{f\in X_{N},\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq 1}|f(x)|=\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}(x)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}=:\sqrt{\Phi(x)},\ \ \forall x\in\Omega,

which implies

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(M)\iff\|\Phi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M^{2}.

(1.6)

The function

\lambda(X_{N},\mu;x):=\frac{1}{\Phi(x)},

known as the Christoffel function of the space
X_{N}
, is independent of the selection of orthonormal basis of
X_{N}
.

Finally, since

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\,\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2-p}

for
1\leq p\leq 2
, the condition

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{M}\,\|f\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}}

implies

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq M^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}.

(1.7)

In terms of the Nikol’skii inequalities, this establishes the implication

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{M})\implies X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(M^{1/p})\ \ \text{for any $1\leq p\leq 2$}.

(1.8)

Notation.
To conclude this section, we introduce some notation that will be used throughout this paper.
Given a finite set of points
\Omega_{m}=\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}
, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
L_{p}(\Omega_{m})
the space of all functions on
\Omega_{m}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{m})}:=\begin{cases}\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p},&\ \ \text{if $1\leq p<\infty$},\\[14.22636pt]
\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq m}|f(\xi^{j})|,&\ \ \text{if $p=\infty$}.\end{cases}

We identify vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with functions defined on the set

\Omega_{m}:=\{1,\ldots,m\}.

In this special case, we adopt the following conventions for vectors
f\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We denote the normalized norm
\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{m})}
by
\|f\|_{L_{p}^{m}}
and the unnormalized norm by

\|f\|_{\ell_{p}^{m}}:=m^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}^{m}}=\begin{cases}\bigl(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|f(j)|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p},&\ \ \text{if $1\leq p<\infty$},\\[14.22636pt]
\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq m}|f(j)|,&\ \ \text{if $p=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Accordingly, we define the unit ball

B_{\ell_{p}^{m}}:=\bigl\{f\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\|f\|_{\ell_{p}^{m}}\leq 1\bigr\}.

More generally, for a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
, we define

B_{X}:=\bigl\{f\in X\colon\|f\|_{X}\leq 1\bigr\}.

We also denote the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
|x|
.
Unless otherwise stated, the symbols
C,c,C_{1},c_{1},\ldots
denote positive universal constants.

Organization of the paper.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we discuss the close connections between the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem and several related areas, including embeddings of finite-dimensional subspaces, frames, spectral properties, and operator norms of submatrices of a given matrix.

In Section 3, we survey recent results on Marcinkiewicz sampling discretization for
L_{2}
-norms, which is the most extensively studied case.

In Section 4, we present one of the general technical approaches to the Marcinkiewicz sampling discretization of
L_{p}
-norms for all
1\leq p<\infty
.
It is stated in the form of the conditional sampling discretization theorem, which highlights the connection between the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem and entropy numbers of compact subsets of bounded functions under the uniform metric. We also outline the key ideas used in the proofs.

In Section 5, we discuss universal discretization, and its connection with the Restricted Isometry Property, a fundamental concept in compressed sensing.

Section 6 covers recent improvements in bounds for sampling discretization of integral norms, along with the main ideas behind their proofs.

Finally, in Section 7, we briefly discuss some recent progress on sampling discretization in the uniform norm.

2
Connections with other areas

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem is closely connected to several other areas. In this section, we provide a brief overview of these interactions and reformulate the problem in different contexts. We refer to the survey paper
[
31
]
for a detailed discussion of these connections.

First,
there are deep results on embeddings of finite-dimensional subspaces of
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
into
\ell_{p}^{N}
, which are closely related to the sampling discretization of the
L_{p}
norms (see, for instance,
[
7
,
29
]
). Techniques developed for these embedding problems have proven highly effective for sampling discretization (see
[
29
]
). A detailed discussion of this connection is available in Section 4.2 of
[
31
]
.

Second, there is a close connection between the Marcinkiewicz discretization of
L_{p}
-norms and the concept of

(\Lambda,p)
-frames (see Section 5 of
[
12
]
). We recall the following definition from
[
12
, Definition 5.2]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda:=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. A sequence
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of functions in
X_{N}
is called a
(\Lambda,p)
-frame
of
X_{N}
with positive constants
A
and
B
if for any
f\in X_{N}
we have

A\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|\langle f,\psi_{j}\rangle|^{p}\leq B\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product of
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. When
\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{m}=1
, a
(\Lambda,p)
-frame is called a
p
-frame (see
[
1
]
).

Let
\mathcal{D}(X_{N},\cdot,\cdot)
denote the reproducing kernel (Dirichlet kernel) of the space
X_{N}\subset L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
; namely,

\mathcal{D}(X_{N},x,y):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}(x){u_{j}(y)},\ \ x,y\in\Omega

where
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is an orthonormal basis (o.n.b.) of
(X_{N},\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)})
. Note that
\mathcal{D}(X_{N},\cdot,\cdot)
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis.
The Marcinkiewicz inequality (
1.2
) is equivalent to

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|\langle f,\mathcal{D}(X_{N},\xi^{j},\cdot)\rangle|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

In other words, in terms of
(\Lambda,p)
-frames,
X_{N}
admits a weighted Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem of the form (
1.2
)
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0

if and only if
there exist points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
and weights
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
the sequence
\{\mathcal{D}(X_{N},\xi^{j},\cdot)\}_{j=1}^{m}
forms a
(\Lambda,p)
-frame of
X_{N}
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

Third, there is a probabilistic approach to the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem with
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. It aims to establish that for IID random points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
drawn from
\mu
on
\Omega
, and
1\leq p<\infty
, the inequality

\sup_{f\in X_{N}^{p}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\Bigr|<\epsilon

(2.1)

holds with high probability, which, in particular, implies that

X_{N}
admits a Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem of the form (
1.4
).
This approach connects to moment estimates of random vectors in probability. Let
\xi
be a random point drawn from
\mu
. Given a basis
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of
X_{N}
, consider the vector-valued function
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
on
\Omega
, and the random vector
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mathbf{u}(\xi)
. Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}=\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
be independent copies of
\boldsymbol{\eta}
. Since each function
f\in X_{N}
has a unique representation

f=\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}\rangle
with
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the inequality (
2.1
) above is equivalent to

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in K}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}\rangle|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|\langle\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\eta}\rangle|^{p}\Bigr|<\epsilon,

(2.2)

where
K:=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ \mathbb{E}|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}\rangle|^{p}\leq 1\}
is a symmetric convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle
denotes the dot product
\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}y_{j}
of
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Thus, the probabilistic approach to the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem asks: How many independent copies of a random vector
\boldsymbol{\eta}
are needed to ensure that (
2.2
) holds with high probability?

Fourth, the Marcinkiewicz discretization in finite-dimensional spaces relates to the spectral properties and operator norms of submatrices of a given matrix. It can be reduced to a corresponding matrix problem. As above, we fix a basis
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of
X_{N}
, and define the vector-valued function
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
on
\Omega
.
Given a set
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\{\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}
of distinct points in
\Omega
, consider the
m\times N
matrix

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{1})\\
\mathbf{u}(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{u}(\xi^{m})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}(\xi^{1})&u_{2}(\xi^{1})&\cdots&u_{N}(\xi^{1})\\
u_{1}(\xi^{2})&u_{2}(\xi^{2})&\cdots&u_{N}(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}(\xi^{m})&u_{2}(\xi^{m})&\cdots&u_{N}(\xi^{m})\end{bmatrix}.

Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, define the following norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\|\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}}=\|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}\rangle\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The Marcinkiewicz inequality (
1.3
) can then be reformulated as

C_{1}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}}\leq\bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbf{y}\bigr\|_{L_{p}^{m}}\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}},\ \ \forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

If
p=2
and
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
(X_{N},\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)})
, this is equivalent to

C_{1}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}\leq\bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbf{y}\bigr\|_{L_{2}^{m}}\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}},\ \ \forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

which can be reformulated in terms of the spectrum of the sampling matrix
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
. Specifically, this translates into the following estimates for the maximum and minimum singular values:

\sigma_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}))\leq C_{2}\sqrt{m}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sigma_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}))\geq C_{1}\sqrt{m}.

If
\Omega=\Omega_{M}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\}
is a finite set, then the vector-valued function
\mathbf{u}
can be represented as a matrix with
M
rows and
N
columns:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}(x_{1})\\
\mathbf{u}(x_{2})\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{u}(x_{M})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}(x_{1})&u_{2}(x_{1})&\cdots&u_{N}(x_{1})\\
u_{1}(x_{2})&u_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{N}(x_{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}(x_{M})&u_{2}(x_{M})&\cdots&u_{N}(x_{M})\end{bmatrix}.

In this case, we are looking for an
m\times N
submatrix
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
of the full matrix
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
such that for each
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

C_{1}\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{M})}^{p}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}^{m}}^{p}\leq C_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{y}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{M})}^{p}.

Finally, the universal Marcinkiewicz-type discretization problem can alternatively be reformulated in terms of certain properties of matrices. In particular, the universal discretization of the
L_{2}
norm is closely linked with the concept of the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP), which plays significant roles in compressed sensing. A detailed discussion of this connection will be presented in Section
5
of this paper.

3
Marcinkiewicz discretization of
L_{2}
norms

In this section, we survey some known results on the Marcinkiewicz sampling discretization of the
L_{2}
-norms, which is the most studied case.
Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. For simplicity, we assume all functions in
X_{N}
are real valued.
Our goal is to discretize the
L_{2}
-norm in
X_{N}
via a finite discrete sum:

(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\,d\mu\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\,d\mu,\ \ \forall f\in X_{N},

(3.1)

where
\xi^{j}\in\Omega
,
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j=1,2,\ldots,m
, and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

3.1
Characterization of (
3.1
) via spectral norms

The discretization (
3.1
) of the
L_{2}
-norms can be equivalently formulated using spectral norms of certain symmetric matrices. To this end, we first recall some useful results from matrix analysis.

Let
A
be an
N\times N
real symmetric matrix.
By the spectral theorem, one can diagonalize
A
using a sequence of
N
real eigenvalues,

\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{N}(A),

and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
u_{1}(A),\ldots,u_{N}(A)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

A\big(u_{i}(A)\big)=\lambda_{i}(A)u_{i}(A),\ \ \ i=1,2,\ldots,N.

Let
\lambda_{\max}(A):=\lambda_{1}(A)
and
\lambda_{\min}(A):=\lambda_{N}(A)
.
Then we have

\lambda_{\max}(A)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \lambda_{\min}(A)=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle,

(3.2)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the dot product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
In general, the Courant-Fischer min-max theorem asserts that
for each
1\leq k\leq N
,

\lambda_{k}(A)=\max_{\dim V=k}\ \ \min_{x\in V,|x|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle

and

\lambda_{k}(A)=\min_{\dim V=N-k+1}\ \ \max_{x\in V,|x|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle,

where
V
ranges over all subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the indicated dimension. If
A
is positive definite,
\lambda_{\max}(A)
is the spectral norm of
A
, which coincides with the operator norm
\|A\|
of
A
:

\|A\|:=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|Ax|.

For a general symmetric matrix, we have

\|A\|=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\langle Ax,x\rangle|=\max\{|\lambda_{\max}(A)|,|\lambda_{\min}(A)|\}.

Now we give an alternative formulation of (
3.1
) using spectral norms.
Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
X_{N}\subset L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, and consider the vector-valued function

\mathbf{u}(\xi):=(u_{1}(\xi),\ldots,u_{N}(\xi)),\ \ \xi\in\Omega.

Define

\mathbf{G}(\xi):=\mathbf{u}(\xi)\otimes\mathbf{u}(\xi)=\Bigl[u_{i}(\xi)u_{j}(\xi)\Bigr]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},\ \ \xi\in\Omega.

Then for each
f=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}u_{j}=\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}\rangle\in X_{N}
with
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}-\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\mathbf{a}^{T}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{G}(\xi^{j})-I\Bigr)\mathbf{a}.

Here we treat
\mathbf{a}
as a column-vector and
\mathbf{u}(x)
as a row-vector.
This together with (
3.2
) yields the following alternative formulation of (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Then

X_{N}
admits a Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem of the form
(
3.1
)
if and only if

\Bigl\|I-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{G}(\xi^{j})\Bigr\|\leq\epsilon,

where
I
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the spectral norm.

Lemma
3.1
establishes a connection between the
L_{2}
-discretization (
3.1
) and the following nonlinear
m
-term approximation problem:

\sigma_{m}(I,{\mathcal{D}})=\inf_{B\in\Sigma_{m}({\mathcal{D}})}\|I-B\|,

where
{\mathcal{D}}:=\{\mathbf{G}(\xi)\colon\xi\in\Omega\}
and
\Sigma_{m}({\mathcal{D}})
denotes the set of all
N\times N
matrices of the form

B=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{G}(\xi^{j}),\ \ \lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{R},\ \ \xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega.

Using this connection, one can apply a relaxed greedy algorithm to construct a set of points

\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, with
m
of order
N^{2}
, for which (
3.1
)
holds with uniform weights
\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{m}=\frac{1}{m}

(see
[
63
, Proposition 5.1]
).

3.2
Discretization using random samples

We start with the following result, which was derived in
[
63
]
using Lemma
3.4
below.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
63
]
)
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
such that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})

for some constant
K\geq 1
.
Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be a sequence of IID random points drawn from the probability measure
\mu
on
\Omega
. If
m\geq 3AKN\epsilon^{-2}\log N
for some constants
A>1
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then
the inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}

(3.3)

holds for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability at least
1-2N^{-A+1}
.

Remark 3.3
.

By setting
A=\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{3KN\log N}
, we may reformulate the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
as follows. If
m\geq 3KN\epsilon^{-2}\log N
, then the estimate (
3.3
)
holds for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability
at least

1-2N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\varepsilon^{2}}{3KN}\Bigr).

We note that results of this type can be traced back to Rudelson

[
55
]
, whose work implies a similar discretization bound for random points but yields a slightly weaker probability estimate.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
relies on the following result of Tropp
[
65
]
.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
65
]
)
.

Let
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be a sequence of independent
N\times N
positive semi-definite random matrices, and define
T=\sum_{k=1}^{m}T_{k}
. Assume that

\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\|T_{k}\|\leq M<\infty\quad\text{almost surely}

for some constant
M>0
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the spectral norm.
Let

\lambda_{\min}:=\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{E}T)
and

\lambda_{\max}:=\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb{E}T)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\min}(T)\leq(1-\epsilon)\lambda_{\min}\Bigr]\leq N\left(\frac{1}{e^{\epsilon}(1-\epsilon)^{1-\epsilon}}\right)^{\lambda_{\min}/M},\ \ \ \forall\epsilon\in[0,1),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\max}(T)\geq(1+\epsilon)\lambda_{\max}\Bigr]\leq N\left(\frac{e^{\epsilon}}{(1+\epsilon)^{1+\epsilon}}\right)^{\lambda_{\max}/M},\ \ \forall\epsilon>0.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\varphi(t):=(1-t)\log(1-t)
for
t\in[0,1)
. Since

\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}>0,\ \ \forall t\in[0,1),

it follows by Taylor’s theorem that

\varphi(t)\geq\varphi(0)+\varphi^{\prime}(0)t+\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}=-t+\frac{t^{2}}{2},\ \ \forall t\in[0,1),

which implies

(1-t)^{1-t}\geq\exp\Bigl(-t+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Bigr),\ \ \forall t\in[0,1].

Similarly, using Taylor’s theorem, one has

(1+t)\log(1+t)\geq t+\frac{1}{2}\frac{t^{2}}{1+t},\ \ \forall t\geq 0,

implying

(1+t)^{1+t}\geq\exp\Bigl(t+\frac{1}{2}\frac{t^{2}}{1+t}\Bigr),\ \ \forall t\geq 0.

Substituting these estimates into Lemma
3.4
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\min}(T)\leq(1-\epsilon)\lambda_{\min}\Bigr]\leq N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{M}\Bigr),\ \ \ \forall\epsilon\in[0,1),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\max}(T)\geq(1+\epsilon)\lambda_{\max}\Bigr]\leq N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2(1+\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{M}\Bigr),\ \ \forall\epsilon>0.

(3.5)

For completeness, we provide a proof of Theorem
3.2
as follows.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
X_{N}
, and let

\mathbf{u}(x):=(u_{1}(x),\ldots,u_{N}(x))\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

The condition
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})

implies (see (
1.6
))

|\mathbf{u}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}(x)|^{2}\leq KN,\ \ \forall x\in\Omega.

Now consider the
m\times N
random matrix

\boldsymbol{\Phi}{u}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{1})\\
\mathbf{u}(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{u}(\xi^{m})\end{bmatrix}.

Let
T:=\frac{1}{m}\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}
. Then

T=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})^{T}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}T_{j}\ \ \ \text{with}\ \ T_{j}=\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})^{T}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j}).

Clearly, the
T_{j}
are independent, positive semi-definite random matrices satisfying that

\|T_{j}\|=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}x^{T}T_{j}x=\frac{1}{m}|\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq\frac{KN}{m}.

Moreover, since
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
is an orthonormal basis of
X_{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}T=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})^{T}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{u}(\xi^{1})^{T}\mathbf{u}(\xi^{1})\Bigr]=I_{N},

where
I_{N}
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix. Now applying Lemma
3.4
, (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) with
M=\frac{KN}{m}
and
\lambda_{\max}=\lambda_{\min}=1
yields that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[1-\epsilon\leq\lambda_{\min}(T)\leq\lambda_{\max}(T)\leq 1+\epsilon\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\min}(T)\leq 1-\epsilon\Bigr]-\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\max}(T)\geq 1+\epsilon\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 1-N\Bigg[\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{m}{KN}\Bigr)+\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2(1+\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{m}{KN}\Bigr)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq 1-2N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{3KN}\Bigr)\geq 1-2N^{-A+1}

provided that
m\geq 3KAN\epsilon^{-2}\log N
.
Theorem
3.2
then follows by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Clearly, the above proof also yields the following estimate: for any
\epsilon\geq 1
and any
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\max}(T)\leq 1+\epsilon\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 1-N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2(1+\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{m}{KN}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq 1-N\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\epsilon}{4}\frac{m}{KN}\Bigr).

In particular, if
1\leq m\leq KN\log N
, then taking

\epsilon=\frac{4AKN\log N}{m}
for some constant
A>1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda_{\max}(T)\leq 1+\frac{4AKN\log N}{m}\Bigr]\geq 1-N^{-A+1}.

Since for any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\langle T\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}(\xi^{j})\rangle|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}(T)\cdot|y|^{2},

this implies the following one-sided
L_{2}
-Marcinkiewicz inequality.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
, and assume that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})

for some constant
K\geq 1
.
Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be a sequence of IID random points drawn from the probability measure
\mu
on
\Omega
. If
1\leq m\leq KN\log N
, then for any given constant
A\geq 1
,
the one-sided inequality

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{4AKN\log N}{m}\Bigr)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}

holds for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability at least
1-N^{-A+1}
.

The Nikol’skii condition
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
plays an important role in the above proof of Theorem
3.2
.
However, the constant
K
in this assumption can be very large, in which case one cannot expect a Marcinkiewicz type theorem with uniform weights and at most
CN\log N
points with
C>0
being a universal constant. For instance, consider the space
\mathcal{P}_{N}
of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most
N-1
on
[-1,1]
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{P}_{N}
satisfies the
(2,\infty)
-Nikol’skii condition
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
with a least constant
K\geq CN
, and any Marcinkiewicz type inequality with uniform weights for the space
\mathcal{P}_{N}
requires at least
CN^{2}
points (see
[
23
, Remark 2.1]
).

Nonetheless, Theorem
3.2
with
K=1
combined with a change of density argument could be used to yield a weighted discretization result for spaces
X_{N}
that may not satisfy
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
assumption or satisfy it only with a very large constant
K
. To be more precise, let
\lambda(X_{N},\mu;\cdot)
denote the Christoffel function on
\Omega
associated with the space
X_{N}
and the measure
\mu
, defined as

\lambda(X_{N},\mu;x):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in X_{N}\\
f(x)=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{2}\,d\mu(\xi),\ \ x\in\Omega,

(3.6)

where it is agreed that
\lambda(X_{N},\mu;x)=\infty
if
f(x)=0
for all
f\in X_{N}
.
It can be easily seen that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\lambda(X_{N},\mu;x)}d\mu(x)=N.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
with the associated Christoffel function
\lambda_{N}(\cdot)=\lambda(X_{N},\mu;\cdot)
as defined in (
3.6
).
Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be a sequence of IID random points drawn from the distribution
\frac{1}{N\lambda_{N}(x)}\,\mu(dx)
. If
m\geq 3AN\epsilon^{-2}\log N
for some constants
A>1
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then
the inequalities

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{N}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{N}(\xi^{j})\left|f\left(\xi^{j}\right)\right|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\quad\forall f\in X_{N},

hold simultaneously with probability at least
1-2N^{-A+1}
.

For completeness, we provide a proof of this corollary as follows.

Proof.

Let
\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\right\}
be an orthonormal basis of
X_{N}
. Then

\frac{1}{\lambda_{N}(x)}=NF(x)^{2}\ \ \text{with}\ \ F(x):=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|u_{k}(x)\right|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Consider the probability measure
d\nu:=F^{2}d\mu
on
\Omega
. Define a mapping
U:L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{2}(\Omega,\nu)
by
Uf(x)=f(x)/F(x)
if
F(x)>0
, and
Uf(x)=0
otherwise.
Since
F(x)=0
implies
f(x)=0
for all
f\in X_{N}
, it follows that

\|Uf\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\nu)}=\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Moreover, it is easily seen that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}|g(x)|\leq\sqrt{N}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\nu)},\ \ \forall g\in UX_{N}.

Corollary
3.8
then follows by applying Theorem
3.2
to the space
UX_{N}\subset L_{2}(\Omega,\nu)
with
K=1
.

∎

3.3
The minimum number of points required for
L_{2}
-discretization

Given two constants
C_{2}\geq 1\geq C_{1}>0
, we denote by
m(X_{N};2;C_{1},C_{2})
the smallest positive integer
m
for which there exist
m
points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
such that

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Clearly,
m(X_{N};2;C_{1},C_{2})\geq N
.
Regarding the upper bound,
Theorem
3.2
(originally due to Rudelson
[
54
]
) establishes that if
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
, then

m(X_{N};2;1/2,3/2)\leq CKN\log N.

The following remarkable theorem asserts that
the extra factor
\log N
in this bound can be removed.
The proof relies on the breakthrough theorem of A. Marcus, D. Spielman, and N. Srivastava
[
49
]

combined with an iterative procedure initially proposed in
[
7
,
48
]
.

Theorem 3.9

(
[
37
,
45
]
)
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
such that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
for some constant
K\geq 1
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exist
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
with
m\leq C\epsilon^{-2}KN
and
C
being an absolute constant such that

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)K\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(3.7)

We point out that Theorem
3.9
was established in
[
45
]
with general positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}K
instead of the constants
1-\epsilon
and
(1+\epsilon)K
in
(
3.7
). The current version of Theorem
3.9
is due to
[
37
]
, where it was observed that
inequality (
3.7
) can be obtained by refining the iteration technique introduced in
[
45
]
.

Theorem
3.9
combined with the change of density argument as given in the proof of Corollary
3.8
yields immediately the following result:

Corollary 3.10

(
[
37
]
)
.

Given any
N
-dimensional space
X_{N}
of bounded functions on
\Omega
, and any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exist
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
with
m\leq C\epsilon^{-2}N
and
C
being an absolute constant and positive weights
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}>0
such that

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Example 3.11
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite set of frequencies, and consider the space

\mathcal{T}(Q):=\operatorname{span}\bigl\{e^{\mathbf{i}\langle k,\cdot\rangle}:k\in Q\bigr\}

of all trigonometric polynomials with frequencies from
Q
on the cube

\Omega=[0,2\pi)^{d}
, equipped with the normalized Lebesgue measure
\mu
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{T}(Q)\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{N})
with

N:=\dim\mathcal{T}(Q)=|Q|
.
Therefore, by Theorem
3.9
, we can find a set of
m\leq C\epsilon^{-2}N

points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega

such that

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{T}(Q).

We conclude this section with the following tight estimate on weighted Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality of
L_{2}
-norms, which follows directly from a result of G. Schechtman
[
56
]
.

Theorem 3.12

(
[
56
]
)
.

If
X_{N}
is an
N
-dimensional subspace of
L_{2}(\Omega)
, then for any
b\in(1,2]
, there exist a set of
m\leq bN
points
x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}\in\Omega
and a set of nonnegative weights
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
such that

\|f\|_{2}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\left|f\left(x^{j}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C}{b-1}\|f\|_{2},\quad\forall f\in X_{N}

where
C>1
is an absolute constant.

Note that in Theorem
3.12
, the parameter
b>1
can be chosen to be arbitrarily close to
1
.

4
Marcinkiewicz discretization theorems

This section presents general results and methods concerning the discretization of
L_{p}
norms for
p\neq 2
.

4.1
A conditional theorem

Let

\mathcal{W}
be a compact subset of the space of bounded functions on
\Omega
.
Let
\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\ldots
be a sequence of independent random points in
\Omega
. Consider the following random sampling discretization problem: for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{W}
,

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}.

(4.1)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\mathcal{W}\subset B_{L_{p}}:=\bigl\{f\in L_{p}(\Omega,\mu):\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq 1\bigr\}

since otherwise we may replace
\mathcal{W}
with the set
\bigl\{f/\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\colon f\in\mathcal{W}\bigr\}.

Our goal is to estimate the probability that (
4.1
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{W}
in terms of the number
m
of required random points.
Such an estimate can be established in terms of an integral of the
\epsilon
-entropy of the set
\mathcal{W}
in the uniform metric. We begin by recalling standard definitions and known estimates concerning entropy (see
[
9
,
25
,
58
]
and
[
62
, Chapter 7]
).

Entropy.

Consider a normed linear space

X:=(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
, and let

B_{X}:=\bigl\{x\in X\colon\|x\|_{X}\leq 1\bigr\}

denote the closed unit ball in
X
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, the covering number
N_{\epsilon}(A,X)
of a compact
subset
A\subset X
is defined as the smallest positive integer
n
for which
there exist elements
x^{1},\ldots,x^{n}\in A
such that

A\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(x^{j}+\epsilon B_{X}\bigr).

The
\epsilon
-entropy
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A;X)
of a compact set
A
in
X

is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A,X):=\log_{2}N_{\epsilon}(A,X),

while the entropy numbers
e_{k}(A,X)
of the set
A
in
X
are defined by

e_{k}(A,X):=\inf\{\epsilon>0\colon\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A,X)\leq k\},\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

Note that in our definition we require
x^{j}\in A
, which differs from the standard
definition of
N_{\epsilon}(A,X)
and
e_{k}(A,X)
, where this restriction is not imposed.
However, it is well known (see
[
62
]
, p. 208) that these characteristics may
differ by at most a factor of
2
.
To emphasize the dependence on the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, we often write

e_{k}(A,\|\cdot\|_{X})=e_{k}(A,X)
and

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A,\|\cdot\|_{X})=\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A,X)
.

There is a simple and useful bound for the entropy of the unit ball in the finite-dimensional case.
If
X=\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then for any
0<\epsilon\leq 1
,

m\log_{2}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\leq\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(B_{X},\|\cdot\|_{X})\leq m\log_{2}\Bigl(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\Bigr).

(4.2)

This also implies that

2^{-1}\cdot 2^{-k/m}\leq e_{k}(B_{X},\|\cdot\|_{X})\leq 6\cdot 2^{-k/m},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Sharp entropy estimates are generally difficult to establish in a general high-dimensional setting. Here we only formulate a useful bound for entropy numbers, which will be used later.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
36
, Lemma 4.10, Corollary 4.2]
)
.

Let

\Omega_{m}=\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
be a finite subset of points in
\Omega
.
Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
satisfying

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{M}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N},

(4.3)

where
M\geq 2
is a constant.
Then for any
1\leq p\leq 2
,

e_{k}(X_{N}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m})})\leq C_{p}\sqrt{\log m}(\log M)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{M}{k}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ k\in\mathbb{N},

(4.4)

where
X_{N}^{p}:=\{f\in X_{N}\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq 1\}
.

Remark 4.2
.

For
p\in(1,2)
, Theorem
4.1
was established in
[
36
, Lemma 4.10]
via a slight modification of the proof of Proposition 16.8.6 in
[
58
]
. The case
p=2
is an immediate consequence of
[
36
, Corollary 4.2]
. Finally, for
p=1
, the conclusion follows from
[
13
, (3.8) and (3.10)]
and the following estimate on entropy numbers:

e_{2k}(X_{N}^{1},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m})})\leq Ce_{k}(X_{N}^{1},\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)})e_{k}(X_{N}^{2},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m})}).

Remark 4.3
.

While the dimension
N
does not appear explicitly in the estimate (
4.4
), the constant
M
in the Nikolski inequality
4.3
typically depends on
N
, particularly when
m\gg N
.
Furthermore, the use of the
L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m})
norm embeds the original space
X_{N}
into a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Remark 4.4
.

By definition, it is easily seen that the estimates of entropy numbers

e_{k}(A,X)\leq Rk^{-\alpha},\ \ k=1,2,\cdots

imply

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(A,X)\leq c(R/\epsilon)^{1/\alpha},\ \ \epsilon>0,

where
R>0
is a constant independent of
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
c>0
is an absolute constant.
As a result, Theorem
4.1

implies that under the condition
4.3
, for any
1\leq p\leq 2
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(X_{N}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m})})\leq C_{p}(\log m)^{\frac{p}{2}}(\log M)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}M\epsilon^{-p},\ \ \epsilon>0.

We are now ready to formulate the conditional discretization theorem for the
Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality of the form (
4.1
).
The following theorem
extends results in
[
14
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
63
]
.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu,{\mathcal{F}})
be a probability space. Let
\mathcal{W}
be a nonempty set of bounded functions on
\Omega
satisfying the following conditions for some
1\leq p<\infty
:

(i)

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{W}}\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\leq R^{1/p}
for some constant
R\geq 1
;

(ii)

\{\lambda f\colon\lambda>0,f\in\mathcal{W},\ \|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq 1\}=\mathcal{W}
.

Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be a sequence of independent random points on
\Omega
satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}[\xi^{k}\in E]=m\cdot\mu(E),\ \ \forall E\in{\mathcal{F}}.

(4.5)

Then there exist positive constants
C_{p}\geq 1
and
c_{p}\in(0,1)

depending only on
p
such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
and any
integer

m\geq C_{p}\epsilon^{-5}\Bigl(\log\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\int_{c_{p}\epsilon}^{R}{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon t^{1/p}}(\mathcal{W};L_{\infty})dt,

(4.6)

the inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{W}
with probability at least

1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}m\epsilon^{4}}{R(\log\frac{R}{\epsilon})^{2}}\Bigr).

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem
4.5
was proved in
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
, where (
4.6
) is replaced by the following slightly different
condition:

m\geq C_{p}\epsilon^{-5}\left(\int_{c_{p}\epsilon^{1/p}}^{R^{1/p}}u^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Bigl(\int_{u}^{R^{1/p}}\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon t}(\mathcal{W},L_{\infty})}{t}\,dt\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}du\right)^{2}.

(4.7)

Note that, performing the change of variables
v=u^{p}
and
s=t^{p}

yields

\text{RHS of \eqref{6-4}}=C^{\prime}_{p}\epsilon^{-5}\left(\int_{c^{\prime}_{p}\epsilon}^{R}v^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{v}^{R}\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon s^{1/p}}(\mathcal{W},L_{\infty})}{s}\,ds\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}dv\right)^{2},

where
c_{p}^{\prime}\in(0,1)
.
Using the Cauchy–Bunyakovsky–Schwarz inequality, and the Fubini theorem, we then obtain

RHS of (
4.7
)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{-5}\Bigl(\log\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\int_{c_{p}^{\prime}\epsilon}^{R}{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon t^{1/p}}(\mathcal{W};L_{\infty})dt.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that either of the following two conditions implies the condition (
4.5
) in Theorem
4.5
:

(i)

\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
are identically distributed according to
\mu
;

(ii)

there exists a partition
\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}\}
of
\Omega
such that
\mu(\Lambda_{j})=\frac{1}{m}
and
\xi^{j}\in\Lambda_{j}
is distributed according to
m\cdot\mu\Bigl|_{\Lambda_{j}}
for each
1\leq j\leq m
.

Theorem
4.5
combined with Theorem
4.1
implies Corollary
4.8
below.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L}
be a sequence of linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfies the following condition for some constant
R\geq 1
:

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}}\leq\sqrt{R}\|f\|_{L_{2}^{n}},\ \ \ \forall f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}.

(4.8)

Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be a sequence of independent random variables taking values in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and satisfying

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}[\xi^{j}\in E]=\frac{|E|}{n},\ \ \forall E\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then there exist constants
C_{p}(\epsilon)=C_{p}\epsilon^{-5-p}\log^{2}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\geq 1
and
c_{p}>0
such that for any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)R\Bigl[(\log R)^{3-\frac{p}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{p}{2}}+(\log L)(\log R)\Bigr],

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}^{n}}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}^{n}}^{p}

(4.9)

holds for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
with probability

1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}m\epsilon^{4}}{R(\log\frac{R}{\epsilon})^{2}}\Bigr).

(ii)

If
2<p<\infty
, then there exist constants
C_{p}(\epsilon)=C_{p}\epsilon^{-7}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}>1
and
c_{p}>0
such that for any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)R^{p/2}\log R\Bigl[\log n+\log L\Bigr],

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality (
4.9
)
holds for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
with probability

1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}m\epsilon^{4}}{R^{p/2}(\log\frac{R}{\epsilon})^{2}}\Bigr).

Proof.

Let

\Omega_{n}:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and let
\mu_{n}
denote the uniform distribution on
\Omega_{n}
. Then the sequence of random variables
\xi^{j}
satisfies the condition (
4.5
) with
\Omega=\Omega_{n}
and
\mu=\mu_{n}
. Let

\mathcal{W}_{p}:=\Bigl\{f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}:\ \|f\|_{L_{p}^{n}}\leq 1\Bigr\}.

(i) Let
1\leq p\leq 2
. The assumption (
4.8
) implies that
\|f\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}}\leq R^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}^{n}}
for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
(see (
1.7
)). Therefore, using Theorem
4.1

(see Remark
4.4
), we obtain

{\mathcal{H}}_{t}(\mathcal{W}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}})\leq C_{p}t^{-p}R(\log R)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{p}{2}}+2\log L,\ \ \forall t>0,

implying

\displaystyle\int_{c_{p}\epsilon}^{R}{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon t^{1/p}}(\mathcal{W}_{p};\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}})dt\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{-p}\Bigl(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigr)R(\log R)^{2-\frac{p}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{p}{2}}+2R\log L.

Thus, applying Theorem
4.5
to

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{p}
, we prove (i).

(ii) Now assume that
p>2
. Then for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
, we have

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}}\leq\sqrt{R}\|f\|_{L_{2}^{n}}\leq R_{1}^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}^{n}}\ \ \text{with}\ \ R_{1}=R^{p/2}.

Since
\mathcal{W}_{p}\subset\mathcal{W}_{2}
, we obtain from (
4.8
) that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{t}(\mathcal{W}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{t}(\mathcal{W}_{2},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}})\leq Ct^{-2}R(\log n)+2\log L,

implying that

\displaystyle\int_{c_{p}\epsilon}^{R_{1}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\epsilon t^{1/p}}(\mathcal{W}_{p};\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}^{n}})dt\leq CR^{p/2}\Bigl[\epsilon^{-2}\log n+\log L\Bigr].

(ii) then follows by applying Theorem
4.5
to

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{p}
.
∎

4.2
Preliminary discretizations

Theorem
4.5
is often applied together with the entropy estimates stated in
Theorem
4.1
, as was done in the proof of Corollary
4.8
.
This normally requires a preliminary step of discretization involving more random points.
The following result is useful.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
be a fixed number. Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L}
be a sequence of linear subspaces of
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\dim V_{j}\leq s
for all
1\leq j\leq L
, and

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq R^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \forall f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j},

(4.10)

for some constant
R\geq 1
.
Assume that
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{8}
,
m
is an integer satisfying

m\geq 16R\epsilon^{-2}\Bigl[\log L+s\log\frac{3}{\epsilon}\Bigr],

(4.11)

and
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is a sequence of IID random points drawn from the distribution
\mu
on
\Omega
. Then
the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality

\displaystyle(1-5\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+5\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}

(4.12)

holds for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
with probability
\geq 1-2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{16R}\Bigr)
.

For the proof of Proposition
4.9
, we need the following two lemmas. The first lemma appears in
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
, but also follows directly from the classical Bernstein inequality
(see
[
60
, Theorem 4.22]
).

Lemma 4.10

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be an independent sequence of random variables such that
\mathbb{E}\eta_{j}=0
,
\mathbb{E}|\eta_{j}|\leq M_{1}
and
|\eta_{j}|\leq M_{\infty}
almost surely for all
1\leq j\leq m
and some constants
M_{1},M_{\infty}>0
. Then for any
0<\lambda<M_{1}
,

\mathbb{P}\Bigl[|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}|\geq m\lambda\Bigr]\leq 2e^{-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{4M_{1}M_{\infty}}}.

A slight variant of the second lemma also appears in
[
7
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 4.11

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Let
T:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator between two normed linear spaces
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Assume that there exists an
\epsilon
-net
{\mathcal{A}}\subset B_{X}
of
B_{X}
such that for some constants
\alpha,\beta>0
,

\alpha\|x\|_{X}\leq\|Tx\|_{Y}\leq\beta\|x\|_{X},\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathcal{A}.

Then for any
z\in X
, we have

\alpha(1-\gamma\epsilon)\|z\|_{X}\leq\|Tz\|_{Y}\leq\beta(1+2\epsilon)\|z\|_{X}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \gamma=\frac{3\beta}{\alpha}.

For completeness, we provide a proof of Lemma
4.11
below.

Proof.

For
z\in B_{X}
, we denote by
\varphi(z)
an element in
\mathcal{A}
such that
\|z-\varphi(z)\|_{X}\leq\epsilon
. Then for any
z\in B_{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\|Tz\|_{Y}\leq\|T\|\epsilon+\|T\varphi(z)\|_{Y}\leq\|T\|\epsilon+\beta\|\varphi(z)\|_{X}\leq\|T\|\epsilon+\beta,

which, taking supremum over all
z\in B_{X}
, implies

\|T\|\leq\frac{\beta}{1-\epsilon}\leq\beta(1+2\epsilon).

To prove the left-hand-side estimate, we note that for any
z\in X
with
\|z\|_{X}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|Tz\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\geq\|T\varphi(z)\|_{Y}-\|T\|\epsilon\geq\alpha\|\varphi(z)\|_{X}-\|T\|\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\alpha(1-\epsilon)-2\beta\epsilon\geq\alpha-3\beta\epsilon.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.9
.

By inequality (
4.2
), for each
1\leq j\leq L
,
there exists an
\epsilon
-net
{\mathcal{A}}_{j}\subset V_{j}^{p}
of
V_{j}^{p}:=V_{j}\cap B_{L_{p}}
in the space
L_{p}
such that

|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}|\leq\bigl(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\bigr)^{v}.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}{\mathcal{A}}_{j}.

Then

\displaystyle\log|{\mathcal{A}}|

\displaystyle\leq\log\Bigl[L\Bigl(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\Bigr)^{v}\Bigr]\leq\log L+v\log\frac{3}{\epsilon}.

(4.13)

Furthermore, (
4.10
) implies that

\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}}=\Bigg\|\frac{|f|^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigg\|_{\infty}\leq R\ \ \text{ for all $f\in{\mathcal{A}}$,}

where
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|
and
\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}
.
Thus, using (
4.13
), and Lemma
4.10
with
M_{\infty}=R
,
M_{1}=2
and

\eta_{j}=\frac{|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}}-1,\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,m,

we conclude that the inequalities

\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\Bigr|\leq\epsilon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in{\mathcal{A}}

(4.14)

hold with probability

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-2|{\mathcal{A}}|\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{8R}\Bigr)=1-2\exp\Bigl(\log|{\mathcal{A}}|-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{8R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-2\exp\Bigl(\log L+v\log\frac{3}{\epsilon}-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{8R}\Bigr).

However, the condition (
4.11
) implies

\log L+v\log\frac{3}{\epsilon}\leq\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{16R}.

It follows that
(
4.14
)
holds for all
f\in{\mathcal{A}}

with probability

\geq 1-2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m\epsilon^{2}}{16R}\Bigr).

To complete the proof, we just need to note that by Lemma
4.11
, (
4.14
) implies (
4.12
).

∎

4.3
Sampling discretization of
L_{p}
norms

In this subsection, we show how to use the preliminary discretization from the last subsection,
together with Corollary
4.8
, to deduce a Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
, and assume that

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
for some constant
K\geq 2
.
Let
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of IID
random points drawn from the distribution
\mu
.
Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
.

(i)
If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then there exists a constant
C_{p}(\epsilon)\geq 1
such that for any given parameter
A\geq 1
, and any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)A\cdot KN\cdot\log^{3}(2KN),

(4.15)

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}

holds for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability
\geq 1-(KN)^{-A}
.

(ii)
If
2<p<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C_{p}(\epsilon)\geq 1
such that for any given parameter
A\geq 1
, and any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)A\cdot(KN)^{p/2}\cdot\log^{2}(2KN),

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}

holds for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability
\geq 1-e^{-A}
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of (i), when
1\leq p\leq 2
. The assumption
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
implies (see (
1.8
)) that

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq(KN)^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ f\in X_{N}.

Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that

m\leq CKN^{2}\epsilon^{-2}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},

where
C>0
is a sufficiently large fixed constant, since otherwise
Theorem
4.12
follows directly from Proposition
4.9
with

L=1
,
s=N
, and
R=KN
.
We will use Corollary
4.8
(i).
To this end, we need a step of preliminary discretization involving more points.

Let
\ell_{N}
denote the smallest integer
\geq CKN^{2}\epsilon^{-2}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}
. Let
m_{1}:=m\ell_{N}
, and let
\Omega_{m_{1}}:=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m_{1}}\}\subset\Omega
be a set of IID random points drawn from the distribution
\mu
. Note that

C_{1}(C,\epsilon)\log(KN)\leq\log m_{1}\leq C_{2}(C,\varepsilon)\log(KN)

for some constants
C_{2}(C,\epsilon)\geq C_{1}(C,\epsilon)>0
. Furthermore, by Proposition
4.9
, the following inequalities
hold simultaneously with probability
\geq 1-4\exp\Bigl(-\frac{cm_{1}\epsilon^{2}}{KN}\Bigr)\geq 1-4e^{-mN}
, for a sufficiently large constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}-\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{m_{1}})}^{p}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{10}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N},

(4.16)

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{m_{1}})}^{2}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{10}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(4.17)

Let
\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}\}
be a partition of the set
\{1,\ldots,m_{1}\}
such that
|\Lambda_{j}|=\ell_{N}
for
1\leq j\leq m
.
Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
be an independent sequence of random variables that is independent of
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m_{1}}\}
such that each
n_{j}
is uniformly distributed in
\Lambda_{j}
. Define

\xi^{j}=x_{n_{j}}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,m
. Then
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
are IID random points with common distribution
\mu
.

Next, we fix a set
\Omega_{m_{1}}
of random points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m_{1}}
for which both (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) hold. Instead of dealing with functions on the entire set
\Omega
, we consider their restrictions on the set
\Omega_{m_{1}}
equipped with the uniform distribution.
From the assumption
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})

and by (
4.17
), we have

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{m_{1}})}\leq\sqrt{2KN}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{m_{1}})},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Thus, applying Corollary
4.8
(i) with
L=1
,
n=m_{1}
and
R=2KN
, we conclude that
the inequalities

\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f({x_{n_{j}}})|^{p}-\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{m_{1}})}^{p}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{10}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{m_{1}})}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}

(4.18)

hold with probability

\geq 1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}(\epsilon)m}{KN\log^{2}(KN)}\Bigr)

provided that

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)KN\log^{3}(KN).

Now, we recall that
\xi^{j}=x_{n_{j}}
.
Clearly,
(
4.18
) combined with (
4.16
) yields that

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(4.19)

To conclude the proof, we estimate the probability of the event
E
that (
4.19
) holds.
Let
E_{1}
denote the event that both (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) hold. Note that
\chi_{E_{1}}
is a function of
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m_{1}})
, and
\mathbb{P}(E_{1})\geq 1-4e^{-mN}
.
Moreover, from the above proof,
we have

\displaystyle\chi_{E_{1}}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\chi_{E}|x_{1},\ldots,x_{m_{1}}]

\displaystyle\geq\chi_{E_{1}}\cdot\left(1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}(\epsilon)m}{KN\log^{2}(KN)}\Bigr)\right),

implying that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\chi_{E_{1}}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\chi_{E}|x_{1},\ldots,x_{n_{1}}]\Bigr]\geq\mathbb{P}(E_{1})\cdot\left(1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}(\epsilon)m}{KN\log^{2}(KN)}\Bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-5\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}(\epsilon)m}{KN\log^{2}(KN)}\Bigr).

Thus, if
m
is an integer satisfying (
4.15
) for some
A\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(E)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\chi_{E\cap E_{1}}]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\chi_{E_{1}}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\chi_{E}|x_{1},\ldots,x_{n_{1}}]\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 1-5\exp\Bigl(-C_{p}(\epsilon)c_{p}(\epsilon)A\log(KN)\Bigr)\geq 1-(KN)^{-A}

provided that the constant
C_{p}(\varepsilon)>0
is sufficiently large.
This completes the proof of (i).

The proof of (ii) is almost identical, using Corollary
4.8
(ii) and the fact that

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}

for
p>2
.
∎

4.4
Sampling discretization with weights

The constant
K
in the
(2,\infty)
-Nikol’skii condition
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
can grow substantially as the dimension of
X_{N}
increases. For instance, if
X_{N}
is the space of all algebraic polynomials of degree less than
N
on the interval
[-1,1]
and
\mu
is the probability measure on
[-1,1]
given by

d\mu(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{\pi}}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx

for some constant
\lambda>0
, then
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
with the least constant
K\sim N^{2\lambda}
(see, e.g.,
[
50
, (7.13), (7.14)]
). In such instances, Theorem
4.12
requires a considerably larger number of random points for effectively discretizing
L_{p}
norms.

Similar to the case of
L_{2}
-discretization, a change of density argument allows us
to apply Theorem
4.12
to establish a weighted discretization theorem that does not
require the
(2,\infty)
–Nikol’skii condition.
The proof is similar to that of Corollary
3.8
for
L_{2}
-discretization. It relies on the following Lewis’ change of density lemma:

Lemma 4.13

(
[
7
, Lemma 7.1]
,
[
43
]
)
.

Given any
1\leq p<\infty
and any
N
-dimensional subspace
X_{N}
of
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
, there exists a basis
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
X_{N}
such that the function
F:=\bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}|^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}
has the following properties:
\|F\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}=1
, and
for all scalars
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\Omega}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}u_{j}(x)\Bigr|^{2}F(x)^{p-2}\,d\mu(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{2}.

(4.20)

To illustrate the idea, let
F
be the function as given in Lemma
4.13
.
Consider the probability measure
\nu(dx)=|F(x)|^{p}\mu(dx)
on
\Omega
, and the linear mapping
U:L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)\to L_{p}(\Omega,\nu)
given by

Uf(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(x)}{F(x)},\ \ &\text{if $F(x)\neq 0$};\\
1,\ \ &\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since each function in
X_{N}
is identically zero on the set
\{x\colon F(x)=0\}
, we have

\|Uf\|_{L_{p}(\nu)}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

In addition, (
4.20
) implies that the sequence

\psi_{j}:=Uu_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
forms an orthonormal basis of the space
UX_{N}
with respect to the inner product of
L_{2}(\Omega,d\nu)
. Moreover, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}=N,\ \ \forall x\in\Omega,

implying

\|Uf\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Uf\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\nu)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Thus, applying Theorem
4.12
to the space
UX_{N}
and the measure
\nu
, we deduce
the following result.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional space of bounded functions on
\Omega
associated with a function
F
given in Lemma
4.13
.

Consider a new measure
\nu
given by

d\nu=|F|^{p}\,d\mu.

Let
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of IID
random points drawn from the distribution
\nu
.
Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
. For
j=1,2,\ldots,m
, let
\lambda_{j}:=\frac{1}{m}\frac{1}{|F(\xi^{j})|^{p}}
if
F(\xi^{j})\neq 0
, and
\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{m}
if
F(\xi^{j})=0
.

(i)
If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then there exists a constant
C_{p}(\epsilon)\geq 1
such that for any given parameter
A\geq 1
, and any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)A\cdot N\cdot\log^{3}(2N),

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequalities

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\left|f\left(\xi^{j}\right)\right|^{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}

(4.21)

hold for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability
\geq 1-N^{-A}
.

(ii)
If
2<p<\infty
,
then there exists a constant
C_{p}(\epsilon)\geq 1
such that for any given parameter
A\geq 1
, and any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)A\cdot N^{p/2}\cdot\log^{2}(2N),

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization

inequalities (
4.21
) hold for all
f\in X_{N}
with probability
\geq 1-e^{-A}
.

5
Universal discretization

We start with the following general result on universal discretization.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{L}
be a sequence of linear subspaces of
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
which satisfies the following condition for some constant
R\geq 1
:

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{R}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \ \forall f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}.

Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be an independent sequence of random variables taking values in
\Omega
and satisfying

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}[\xi^{j}\in E]=\mu(E),\ \ \forall E\in{\mathcal{F}}.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then there exist constants
C_{p}(\epsilon)\geq 1
and
c_{p}>0
such that for any parameter
A\geq 1
and any integer

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)\cdot A\cdot R\Bigl[(\log R)^{3}+(\log R)^{3-\frac{p}{2}}(\log\log L)^{\frac{p}{2}}+(\log L)(\log R)\Bigr],

the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization inequality

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},

holds for all
f\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{L}V_{j}
with probability
\geq 1-R^{-A}
.

Theorem
5.1
can be deduced directly from Corollary
4.8

and Proposition
4.9
, following a similar approach as the proof of Theorem
4.12
.

In this section, we explore universal discretization
for special collections of subspaces generated by a given finite set of bounded functions on
\Omega
.
In this context, we can improve the estimates provided in Theorem
5.1
.

We introduce the general setting as follows.
Let

{\mathcal{D}}_{N}=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a fixed system of linearly independent bounded functions on
\Omega
satisfying

\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\varphi_{j}(x)|\leq 1,\ \ j=1,2,\ldots,N.

Given an integer
1\leq s\leq N
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
the collection of all linear spaces spanned by
\{\varphi_{j}\colon j\in J\}
with
J\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
and
|J|=s
. A function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
s
-sparse with respect to
\mathcal{D}_{N}
if it belongs to a linear space from the collection
\mathcal{X}_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
.
We
denote by
\Sigma_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
the set of all
s
-sparse functions with respect to the dictionary
\mathcal{D}_{N}
; namely,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}):

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{V\in{\mathcal{X}}_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}V.

Recall that a finite sequence
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of points in
\Omega
is said to provide
universal discretization of the
L_{p}
norm for
\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
,

1\leq p<\infty
, with positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
if

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in\Sigma_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}).

(5.1)

Consider a sequence
\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of IID random points with common distribution
\mu
.
Our goal is to establish an estimate for the probability that (
5.1
) holds in terms of the number
m
of random points.
In the above context, we have the following result, which improves the estimates in Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{N}=\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be a set of
N
bounded functions on
\Omega

satisfying

\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1.

Let
1\leq s\leq N
be a given integer. Assume that there exists a constant
K\geq 1
such that

\sum_{j\in J}|a_{j}|^{2}\leq K\Bigl\|\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \ \forall a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}

(5.2)

whenever
J\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
|J|=s
.
Let
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be IID
random points with common distribution
\mu
on
\Omega
.
Then given
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
there exist constants
C_{p}(\varepsilon)>1
and
c_{p}(\varepsilon)>0
,
depending only on
p
and
\varepsilon
, such that the inequalities

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \ \forall f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})

hold with probability
\geq 1-2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c_{p}(\epsilon)m}{Ks\log^{2}(2Ks)}\Bigr)
provided that

m\geq C_{p}(\epsilon)Ks\log N\cdot(\log(2Ks))^{2}\cdot(\log(2Ks)+\log\log N).

Theorem
5.2
was proved in
[
21
]
. Note that the problem of universal discretization for some subspaces of the
trigonometric polynomials was studied in
[
15
,
64
]
.

The condition (
5.2
) is satisfied for all
1\leq s\leq N
if
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
are linearly independent in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, in which case
one can choose
K^{-1}
to be the smallest eigenvalue of the
N\times N

matrix
\Bigl[\langle\varphi_{j},\varphi_{k}\rangle_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\Bigr]_{1\leq j,k\leq N}
.
In applications, we often assume that
\mathcal{D}_{N}
is a Riesz basis; that is, there exist constants
0<R_{1}\leq R_{2}<\infty
such that for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
,

R_{1}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq R_{2}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Note however that estimates in Theorem
5.2
are independent of the constant
R_{2}
.

The universal discretization of
L_{2}
norm is closely related to the concept of Restricted Isometry Property (RIP), which plays a central role in compressed sensing (see, e.g.,
[
27
]
and the references therein).
Given an integer
1\leq s\leq N
, we denote by
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the set of all
s
-sparse vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
; i.e., vectors
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with at most
s
nonzero coordinates
x_{i}
.
An
m\times N
matrix
\mathbf{A}
is said to satisfy the RIP of order
s
if there exists a positive constant
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

(1-\delta)\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}^{2}\leq(1+\delta)\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2},\ \ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(5.3)

The smallest positive constant
\delta
that satisfies (
5.3
), denoted by
\delta_{s}(\mathbf{A})
, is called the restricted isometry constant.

Results on the RIP properties of the random matrix
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
associated with uniformly bounded orthonormal systems
\mathcal{D}_{N}
can be found in
[
27
]
.
Generalizations of the RIP, where one or both of the

\ell_{2}
-norms are replaced by other norms, typically
\ell_{p}^{N}
-norms, have also proven
useful in this context (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
10
]
,
[
27
, Exercise 9.6]
).
To express universal sampling discretization within the RIP framework,
the following extension was introduced in
[
59
, Section 5]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
1\leq s,m\leq N
be integers. An
m\times N
real matrix
\mathbf{A}
is said to have the
s
-th order
RIP\left(\ell_{p}^{m},\|\cdot\|\right)
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
if for any
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

C_{1}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{p}\leq\|\mathbf{Ax}\|^{p}_{\ell_{p}^{m}}\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{p}.

Next, let
\Phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a vector-valued function on
\Omega
given by

\Phi(\mathbf{x})=(\varphi_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\varphi_{N}(\mathbf{x})),\ \ \mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

Here and elsewhere in this section, we always treat
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
as a row vector.
Given a finite sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
of points, consider the
m\times N
random matrix

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi(\xi^{1})\\
\Phi(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots\\
\Phi(\xi^{m})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{1}(\xi^{1})&\varphi_{2}(\xi^{1})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{1})\\
\varphi_{1}(\xi^{2})&\varphi_{2}(\xi^{2})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}(\xi^{m})&\varphi_{2}(\xi^{m})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{m})\end{bmatrix}.

(5.4)

Additionally, given
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,\Phi}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

\|\mathbf{a}\|_{p,\Phi}=\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

With the above notation, we can then write the inequality (
5.1
) in the form

C_{1}\|\mathbf{a}\|_{p,\Phi}\leq\bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbf{a}\bigr\|_{L_{p}^{m}}\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{a}\|_{p,\Phi},\ \ \forall\mathbf{a}\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

which is equivalent to asserting that matrix
m^{-1/p}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
has the
s
-th order
RIP\left(\ell^{m}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{p,\Phi}\right)
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.
In particular, if
p=2
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
is an orthonormal system in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, then the universal discretization (
5.1
) with
C_{1}=1-\epsilon
and
C_{2}=1+\epsilon

can be formulated equivalently in terms of the RIP property of order
s
of the random matrix
m^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
:

(1-\epsilon)\|z\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2}\leq m^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})z\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|z\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2},\ \ \forall z\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In summary, we have

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that

{\mathcal{D}}_{N}=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}
is a finite system of real valued functions on
\Omega
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq s,m\leq N
are integers. Then a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
m
points in
\Omega
provides the universal discretization (
5.1
) of the
L_{p}
norm for
\Sigma_{s}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
if and only if the
m\times N
matrix
m^{-1/p}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
, with
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
being defined in (
5.4
), has the
s
-th order
RIP\left(\ell^{m}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{p,\Phi}\right)
with constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. In particular, if
p=2
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
is an orthonormal system in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, then the universal discretization (
5.1
) with constants
C_{1}=1-\epsilon
and
C_{2}=1+\epsilon

holds if and only if the matrix
m^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
has
the RIP property of order
s
with constants
1\pm\epsilon
.

Some applications of universal discretization and Theorem
5.4
can be found in
[
18
,
19
]
.

6
Some improved bounds in sampling discretization of integral norms

Analogous to the
L_{2}
-discretization discussed in Section
3.3
, for any given constants
C_{2}\geq 1\geq C_{1}>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
m(X_{N};p;C_{1},C_{2})
to be the smallest positive integer
m
for which there exist
m
points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
such that

C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

For simplicity, we denote this quantity by
m(X_{N};p)
when the specific values of the absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2}
are understood or not important from context.

According to Theorem
4.12
, for every space
X_{N}
satisfying the
(2,\infty)
-Nikol’skii inequality
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
,
we have

m(X_{N};p)\leq C_{p}\begin{cases}KN\log^{3}(KN),&\ \ \text{if $1\leq p\leq 2$};\\
(KN)^{p/2}\log^{2}(KN),&\ \ \text{if $2<p<\infty$}.\end{cases}

(6.1)

The upper bounds in (
6.1
) turn out to be nearly optimal as
N\to\infty
for a general space
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
.
Indeed, this is clear for
1\leq p\leq 2
as the lower bound
m(X_{N};p)\geq N

holds trivially. For
2<p<\infty
, it is known (see, for instance,
[
31
,
D.20]
) that for
X_{N}=\operatorname{span}\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}\}
with
r_{j}(t):=\text{sgn}(\sin(2^{j+1}\pi t))
,
t\in[0,1]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
being the sequence of Rademacher functions on
[0,1]
and for the ususal Lebesgue measure on
\Omega=[0,1]
, the
(2,\infty)
-Nikol’skii inequality
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
is satisfied with
K=1
, and
m(X_{N};p)\geq cN^{p/2}
for any
2<p<\infty
, where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

It is natural to ask whether the upper bounds in (
6.1
) remain valid without the extra logarithmic factors under the condition
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
. For the discretization of
L_{2}
-norm, the answer is positive, due to the breakthrough work of A. Marcus, D. Spielman, and N. Srivastava
[
49
]
. In fact, according to
Theorem
3.9
, we have

m(X_{N};2;1/2,3/2)\leq CKN.

For
1\leq p<2
, this remains a challenging open problem.

There has been recent progress on this problem for
1\leq p<2
. It was established in
[
12
]
that
for
1\leq p<2
and every
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
, the following refined upper bound holds:
m(X_{N};p;1/2,3/2)\leq C_{p}\Phi_{p}(KN)
,
where

\Phi_{p}(t):=\begin{cases}t\log t,&\ \ \text{if $p=1$, $2$},\\
t(\log t)(\log\log t)^{2},&\ \ \text{if $1<p<2$},\end{cases}\ \ t\geq 2.

(6.2)

More precisely, one has

Theorem 6.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Given
1\leq p\leq 2
,
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
K\geq 2
and any
N
-dimensional subspace

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
,
there exist

\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
with
m\leq C_{p}(\epsilon)\Phi_{p}(KN)

such that

(1-\epsilon)\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N},

(6.3)

where
\Phi_{p}
is given in (
6.2
),

C_{p}(\epsilon)=C\epsilon^{-2}
for
p=1,2
, and
C_{p}(\epsilon)=C_{p}\epsilon^{-2}\log^{3}\frac{1}{\epsilon}
for
1<p<2
.

Historical discussions on sampling discretization for the cases
p=2
and

1\leq p<2
can be found in Subsections
D.15
and
D.16
of
[
31
]
,
respectively. Here we only mention that the best previously known results are as follows.
It was proved in
[
13
]
that, under the
(2,\infty)
–Nikol’skii inequality assumption

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
, the discretization estimate (
6.3
)
holds for
1\leq p<2
provided that

m\geq C(p,K,\varepsilon)\,N(\log N)^{3}
.
This estimate on
m
was further improved to

m\geq C(p,K,\varepsilon)\,N(\log N)^{2}
for
1<p<2
in
[
36
]
.
We also point out that sampling discretization for
p>2
under the

(p,\infty)
-Nikol’skii inequality
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}((KN)^{1/p})
was studied in
[
36
]
as well,
where the results were further improved for
p>3
in
[
17
]
.

Combining Theorem
6.1
with Lewis’ change of density lemma
(see Lemma
4.13
), and following the proof of Corollary
4.14
,
we obtain the following weighted discretization result.

Corollary 6.2
.

Given
1\leq p\leq 2
,
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
and any
N
-dimensional subspace
X_{N}\subset L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
there are a finite set of points
\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
with
m\leq C_{p}(\epsilon)\Phi_{p}(KN)

and a set of nonnegative weights
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}

such that for any

,

{(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p},}

where
\Phi_{p}
is given in (
6.2
),

C_{p}(\epsilon)=C\epsilon^{-2}
for
p=1,2
, and
C_{p}(\epsilon)=C_{p}\epsilon^{-2}\log^{3}\frac{1}{\epsilon}
for
1<p<2
.

We give a brief description of the scheme of the proof of Theorem
6.1
, which can be divided into three steps.

Step 1.
Preliminary discretization.

In this step, we establish results on simultaneous discretization of the
L_{2}
and
L_{p}
norms, using
M\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{-r_{1}}N(\log N)^{r_{2}}
points. This step allows us to reduce the original problem of discretization to a problem in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
.
The main ingredient in this step is the following lemma, which follows directly from Proposition
4.9
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and
0<\epsilon_{0}<1/4
. Let
X_{N}
be a subspace of
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
of dimension
N
such that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{KN})
for some
K\geq 2
. Then there exists a finite set of points

x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\Omega
with

m\leq C_{p}\epsilon_{0}^{-8}KN(\log(KN))^{3}

such that for any
f\in X_{N}
, we have

(1-\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(x_{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}}^{p},

and

(1-\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(x_{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}}^{2}.

Step 2.
Estimate the expectation
\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in X_{N}\cap B_{p}^{M}}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}{\varepsilon_{j}}|f(j)|^{p}\Bigr|\Bigr)
, where

\{\varepsilon_{j}\colon j=1,2,\ldots,M\}
denotes
a sequence of independent Bernoulli random variables taking
values
\pm 1
with probability
1/2
, and
B_{p}^{M}:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\colon\|f\|_{\ell_{p}^{M}}\!\leq\!1\}
.

For
p=1,2
, we have the following lemma, which was proved by Rudelson
[
55
, Lemma 1]
for
p=2
, and
by Talagrand
[
57
]
for
p=1

(see also
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
29
, Theorem 13]
and
[
42
, Proposition 15.16]
).

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
X_{N}
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
of dimension at most
N
satisfying

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{KN}\|f\|_{L_{2}^{M}},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{N}

for a constant
K\geq 1
.
Then for
p=1
and
p=2
we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in X_{N}\cap B_{p}^{M}}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}{\varepsilon_{j}}|f(j)|^{p}\Bigr|\Bigr)\leq C\sqrt{\frac{KN\log N}{M}},

where
C
is a positive absolute constant.

For
1<p<2
, following Talagrand’s proof of
[
58
, Theorem 16.8.2]
, we have

Lemma 6.5

(
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
X_{N}
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
of dimension at most
N
satisfying

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{KN}\|f\|_{L_{2}^{M}},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{N}

for some constant
K\geq 1
.
Then for any
p\in(1,2)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in X_{N}\cap B_{p}^{M}}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}|f(j)|^{p}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigr|\Bigr)\leq C(p)\sqrt{\frac{KN\log M}{M}}\log\Bigl(\frac{M}{KN}+2\Bigr).

For
1<p<2
, Talagrand
[
58
, Theorem 16.8.2]
proved a result similar to Lemma
6.5

for a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{M}
satisfying
\mu\{j\}\leq\frac{2}{M}
for
1\leq j\leq M
, under the following stronger assumption:
for each orthonormal basis
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
of
(X_{N},\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mu)})
,

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varphi_{k}(j)^{2}=1,\ \ j=1,2,\ldots,M.

Lemmas

and

play crucial roles in the proof of Theorem

. Roughly speaking, they allow us to reduce the number of points required for good discretization by
approximately a factor of two.

Step 3.
Iteration.

To illustrate the idea, we sketch the proof of
p=1
below.

First, by Lemma
6.3
in the preliminary step, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Omega=\Omega_{M}=\{1,2,\ldots,M\}

and
\mu
is the uniform probability measure on
\Omega_{M}
.
For each
I\subset\Omega_{M}
, we denote by
R_{I}
the orthogonal projection onto the space spanned by
\{e_{i},i\in I\}
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
,
\ldots
,
e_{M}=(0,\ldots,0,1)
is a canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Thus, for each
f\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
(R_{I}f)(j)=f(j)
for

, and
(R_{I}f)(j)=0
for
j\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I
.
Second, using Lemma
6.4
, one can obtain

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
X_{N}
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{M}

of dimension at most
N
satisfying

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{KN}\|f\|_{L_{2}^{M}},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

for some constant
K\geq 1
. Let
J\subset\Omega_{M}:=\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
.
Assume that there exist positive constants
\alpha_{J}
,
\beta_{J}
such that for any

we have for both

and

Then there exists a subset

with

\frac{|J|}{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\Bigr)\leq|I|\leq\frac{|J|}{2}

such that for any

we have for both

and

\alpha_{I}\|f\|^{p}_{\ell_{p}^{M}}\leq\|R_{I}f\|^{p}_{\ell_{p}^{M}}\leq\beta_{I}\|f\|^{p}_{\ell_{p}^{M}}

where

\alpha_{I}:=\frac{(1-\sigma)\alpha_{J}}{2},\ \ \beta_{I}:=\frac{(1+\sigma)\beta_{J}}{2},\ \ \sigma:=C\sqrt{\frac{KN\log N}{\alpha_{J}M}},

and

is an absolute constant.

We can iterate Lemma

with an appropriate stopping time and finish the proof.

Example 6.7

Let

\Omega

In the classical setting of univariate algebraic and trigonometric polynomials,
this problem was extensively studied (see, e.g.,

We start with the following result, which is well known for

(see
[

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional real subspace of
\mathcal{C}(\Omega)
. Given
a positive integer
\ell
, let

X_{N}(\ell):=\operatorname{span}\Bigl\{f_{1}f_{2}\ldots f_{\ell}:\ \ f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in X_{N}\Bigr\},

and

N_{\ell}:=\dim X_{N}(\ell)
.
Then there exists a set
\left\{\xi^{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N_{\ell}}
of points in
\Omega
such that

\max_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\leq(N_{\ell})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\max_{1\leq j\leq N_{\ell}}\left|f\left(\xi^{j}\right)\right|,\ \ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

Proof.

Given a basis
\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N_{\ell}}\}\subset X_{N}(\ell)
of
X_{N}(\ell)
, we define a function
\Delta:\Omega^{N_{\ell}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Delta(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N_{\ell}}):=\det\left[\begin{matrix}\varphi_{1}(x_{1})&\varphi_{2}(x_{1})&\cdots&\varphi_{N_{\ell}}(x_{1})\\
\varphi_{1}(x_{2})&\varphi_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&\varphi_{N_{\ell}}(x_{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}(x_{N_{\ell}})&\varphi_{2}(x_{N_{\ell}})&\cdots&\varphi_{N_{\ell}}(x_{N_{\ell}})\end{matrix}\right]

for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{\ell}}\in\Omega
.
Since
\Delta
is a continuous function on the compact set
\Omega^{N_{\ell}}
, there must exist
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{N_{\ell}}\in\Omega
such that

\Delta(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{N_{\ell}})\equiv\Delta_{N,\ell}:=\max_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{\ell}}\in\Omega}\Delta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{\ell}}).

It is easily seen that
\Delta_{N,\ell}>0
.
For
1\leq j\leq N_{\ell}
, define
L_{j}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

L_{j}(x)=\frac{\Delta(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{j-1},x,\xi^{j+1},\ldots,\xi^{N_{\ell}})}{\Delta_{N,\ell}},\ \ x\in\Omega.

Clearly,
L_{j}\in X_{N}(\ell)
,
L_{j}(\xi^{k})=\delta_{k,j}
and
\|L_{j}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
for
1\leq j,k\leq N_{\ell}
. Moreover, for each
f\in X_{N}
, we have

\bigl(f(x)\bigr)^{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\ell}}f(\xi^{j})^{\ell}L_{j}(x),\ \ x\in\Omega.

This implies that for each
f\in X_{N}
,

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\ell}\leq N_{\ell}\max_{1\leq j\leq N_{\ell}}|f(\xi^{j})|^{\ell}\implies\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq(N_{\ell})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\max_{1\leq j\leq N_{\ell}}|f(\xi^{j})|.

This completes the proof.
∎

We give a few remarks on Theorem

.

Remark 7.4
.

(i) Of particular interest is the case when
\ell=1
, where Theorem
7.3
gives the discretization inequality with the minimum number of points:

\max_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\leq N\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\left|f\left(\xi^{j}\right)\right|,\ \ \ \forall f\in X_{N}.

(7.3)

Using the notation introduced in the beginning of this section, this implies
m(X_{N};\infty;N)=N
.

This is a well known result, for which the above proof can be found in the book of E. Novak
[
53
, Proposition 1.2.3]
.

(ii) For any positive integer
\ell
, we have

N_{\ell}\leq\binom{N-1+\ell}{\ell}\leq\Bigl(\frac{e(N-1+\ell)}{\ell}\Bigr)^{\ell}.

However, in many cases, this last estimate is far from being optimal. For example, if
X_{N}=\Pi_{n}^{d}
is the space of all algebraic polynomials of total degree at most
n
in
d
variables, and
\Omega
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with nonempty interior, then

N=\dim\Pi_{n}^{d}=\binom{n+d}{d}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ N_{\ell}\leq\dim\Pi_{n\ell}^{d}.

As a result, we have

\frac{N_{\ell}}{N}\leq\frac{\binom{n\ell+d}{d}}{\binom{n+d}{d}}=\frac{(n\ell+d)(n\ell+d-1)\cdots(n\ell+1)}{(n+d)(n+d-1)\cdots(n+1)}=\ell^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{n+\frac{j}{\ell}}{n+j}\leq\ell^{d}.

Assume in addition that
n\geq d^{2}
and
d\geq 2
. Then

N=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Bigl(1+\frac{n}{j}\Bigr)\geq\Bigl(1+\frac{n}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\geq d^{d}.

Now we specify the integer
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\log N-1\leq\ell<\log N.

Then
\ell\geq d\log d-1
,

N_{\ell}\leq\ell^{d}N\leq N(\log N)^{d},

and

N_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\leq(\ell^{d}N)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\exp\Bigl(\frac{d}{\ell}\log\ell+\frac{1}{\ell}\log N\Bigr)\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>0
is an absolute constant (independent of
d
).
Thus, Theorem
7.3

\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of

\Omega
such that

\max_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\leq C_{1}\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|f(\xi^{j})|,\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}=\Pi_{n}^{d}.

This last estimate was previously established in
[
