1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of normalized analytic functions
f(z)
defined in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, with
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
. Then each
f\in\mathcal{A}
has the representation

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of univalent (
i.e.
, one-to-one) functions. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the class of functions with positive real part, having the series expansion

p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}.

Let
f
and
g
be two analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is said to be subordinate to
g
, written as
f\prec g

or
f(z)\prec g(z)
, if there exists a function
w(z)
, analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
w(0)=0
,
|w(z)|<1
such that
f(z)=g(w(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and
f(0)=g(0)
,
then
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq g(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{S}
consisting of starlike functions that map
\mathbb{D}
onto a starlike domain. Geometrically,
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
if, and only if,

\operatorname{Re}\,\!\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0\quad\mbox{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

We define the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
such that

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},

where
\varphi
is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, normalized by
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
, and symmetric with respect to the real axis. For our investigation, we consider the specific choice
\varphi(z)=\left(1+z/2\right)^{2}
.

In 1992, Ma and Minda
[
9
]
introduced a unified approach to the study of starlike and convex functions via subordination.

A function
\psi
is called a Ma–Minda function if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\psi
is analytic and univalent in the
\mathbb{D}
,

(2)

\operatorname{Re}\,\psi(z)>0

for all

z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(3)

\psi(0)=1
and
\psi^{\prime}(0)>0
,

(4)

\psi(\mathbb{D})
is symmetric with respect to the real axis,

(5)

\psi(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to
1
.

Given such a
\psi
, the associated Ma–Minda subclasses are given by

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\ \frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\psi(z)\right\},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\psi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\ 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\psi(z)\right\}.

By choosing specific forms of
\psi
, several well-known subclasses of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
can be obtained, and many researchers have examined their geometric properties, radii of starlikeness, and coefficient bounds. In 1971, Janowski
[
5
]
introduced the subclass

\mathcal{S}^{*}[A,B]:=\mathcal{S}^{*}\!\left(\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\right),\quad\text{where }-1\leq B<A\leq 1,

which provides a two-parameter family unifying many earlier examples. Building on this perspective, further notable contributions identified concrete Ma–Minda choices that yield certain well-known subclasses: the class
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(\sqrt{1+z})
studied by Sokół and Stankiewicz
[
16
]
, and the cardioid-type class
\mathcal{S}_{C}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(1+{4}/{3}z+{2}/{3}z^{2})
introduced by Sharma
et al
.
[
15
]
. In the same vein, several other choices of
\psi
have been investigated, the class
\mathcal{S}_{e}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(e^{z})
introduced by Mendiratta
et al
.
[
10
]
, the class
\Delta^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(z+\sqrt{1+z^{2}})
was introduced by Raina
et al
.
[
13
]
, the class
\mathcal{S}_{SG}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}\!\left(2/(1+e^{-z})\right)
considered by Goel and Kumar
[
4
]
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\wp}^{*}:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(1+ze^{z})
investigated by Kumar and Kamaljeet
[
6
]
. These examples illustrate how specific analytic targets
\psi
naturally organize families of starlike functions under subordination.

2.
Certain properties of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)

In this section we prove that
\varphi(z)
satifies all the properties of Ma-Minda function.

It is immediate that
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
, and that
\varphi
is symmetric with respect to the real axis.
To obtain the bounds, note that

\left|\varphi(z)\right|=\left|1+\frac{z}{2}\right|^{2}.

Since
|z|<1
, we have
1-{|z|}/{2}<\left|1+{z}/{2}\right|<1+{|z|}/{2}
, and hence

\frac{1}{4}\,<\left|\left(1+\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2}\right|\,<\frac{9}{4},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2.1)

Next we verify that
\varphi
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Assume that
\varphi(z_{1})=\varphi(z_{2})
. Then

\left(1+\frac{z_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}=\left(1+\frac{z_{2}}{2}\right)^{2},

so

1+\frac{z_{1}}{2}=\pm\left(1+\frac{z_{2}}{2}\right).

If
1+{z_{1}}/{2}=1+{z_{2}}/{2}
, then
z_{1}=z_{2}
. If

1+\frac{z_{1}}{2}=-\left(1+\frac{z_{2}}{2}\right),

then

1+\frac{z_{1}}{2}=-1-\frac{z_{2}}{2}

Therefore,

z_{1}+z_{2}=-4,

which is impossible for
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
because
|z_{1}+z_{2}|\leq|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|<2
.
Therefore
\varphi
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
.

We now verify that
\varphi
is starlike with respect to the point
\varphi(0)=1
. Since

\varphi^{\prime}(z)=1+\frac{z}{2},\qquad\varphi(z)-1=\left(1+\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2}-1=z+\frac{z^{2}}{4}=z\left(1+\frac{z}{4}\right),

(2.2)

we have

\frac{z\,\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)-1}=\frac{z(1+z/2)}{z(1+z/4)}=\frac{1+z/2}{1+z/4}=:M(z).

A direct computation gives

\frac{M(z)-1}{M(z)+1}=\frac{\frac{1+z/2}{1+z/4}-1}{\frac{1+z/2}{1+z/4}+1}=\frac{z}{8+3z}.

For
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we obtain

\left|\frac{z}{8+3z}\right|\leq\frac{|z|}{8-3|z|}<\frac{1}{5}<1.

Hence
\left|\frac{M(z)-1}{M(z)+1}\right|<1
, which is equivalent to
\operatorname{Re}M(z)>0
. Therefore,

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{z\,\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)-1}\right)=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{1+z/2}{1+z/4}\right)>0,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

and consequently
\varphi
is starlike with respect to
\varphi(0)=1
.

Finally, we prove that
\operatorname{Re}\varphi(z)>0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Indeed, set

w=1+\frac{z}{2}=u+iv,\qquad u=\operatorname{Re}w,\quad v=\operatorname{Im}w.

Since
|z|<1
, we have

|w-1|=\left|\frac{z}{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2}.

Consequently,

u=\operatorname{Re}w=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+\frac{z}{2}\right)=1+\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\geq 1-\left|\frac{z}{2}\right|>1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2},

and also

|v|=\left|\operatorname{Im}w\right|=\left|\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right|\leq\left|\frac{z}{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2}.

Now

\varphi(z)=w^{2}=(u+iv)^{2}=(u^{2}-v^{2})+i(2uv),

so

\operatorname{Re}\varphi(z)=u^{2}-v^{2}.

Since
|v|<1/2
, we have
-v^{2}>-\left(1/2\right)^{2}
, and hence

\operatorname{Re}\varphi(z)=u^{2}-v^{2}>u^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}.

Since
u>1/2
, it follows that

u^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}>0.

Therefore,

\operatorname{Re}\varphi(z)>0\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore,
\varphi
satisfies the standard hypotheses of a Ma–Minda function. We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)

the class of starlike functions subordinate to
\varphi
, namely

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi):=\left\{\,f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\,\right\}.

3.
Integral representation of the class

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
, then we have
{zf^{\prime}(z)}/{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)
. Which implies that there exists a Schwarz function
w(z)
such that
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is analytic,
w(0)=0
, and

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\varphi\big(w(z)\big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Integrating the logarithmic derivative, we obtain

\log\!\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\varphi(w(\xi))-1}{\xi}\,d\xi,

and therefore the integral representation

f(z)=z\exp\!\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\varphi(w(t))-1}{t}\,dt\right).

(3.1)

Assuming
w(t)=t,
and take
\widetilde{f}=f
for this case, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(z)

\displaystyle=z\exp\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\varphi(t)-1}{t}\,dt

(3.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(z)

\displaystyle=z+z^{2}+\frac{5}{8}z^{3}+\frac{7}{24}z^{4}+\cdots.

(3.3)

The next theorem gives conditions under which the Möbius function

p(z)=\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}

is subordinate to
\varphi(z)
, when satisfying the condition in the following theorem
ensuring that
p(\mathbb{D})\subset\varphi(\mathbb{D})
.
Geometrically, this means that the image circle of
p(z)
lies inside the image
of
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
, which guarantees subordination.
Using the theorem in the
[
4
]
, we prove similar result for our class.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
-1<B<A\leq 1
, and define
p(z)=\dfrac{1+Az}{1+Bz}
. Then
p(z)\prec\varphi(z)
provided that
\dfrac{1}{4}\leq\dfrac{1-A}{1-B}
and
\dfrac{1+A}{1+B}\leq\dfrac{9}{4}
.

Proof.
Assume that
-1<B<A\leq 1
and that

\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{1-A}{1-B}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1+A}{1+B}\leq\frac{9}{4}.

(3.4)

We prove that
p(z)\prec\varphi(z)
, where
\varphi(z)=(1+z/2)^{2}
.

Since

p(z)=\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz},

is a Möbius transformation with real coefficients, the image of
\partial\mathbb{D}

is a Euclidean circle. A standard computation shows that
p(\partial\mathbb{D})

is the circle with center

a=\frac{1-AB}{1-B^{2}}

(3.5)

and radius

r=\frac{A-B}{1-B^{2}}.

(3.6)

Since
p(0)=1
lies inside this circle, the map
p
sends the unit disk conformally onto
the disk
\mathbb{B}(a,r),

p(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{B}(a,r).

Using (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

a-r=\frac{1-A}{1-B},\qquad a+r=\frac{1+A}{1+B}.

(3.7)

Substituting (
3.7
) into the inequalities in (
3.4
) shows that
(
3.4
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{4}\leq a-r\qquad\text{and}\qquad a+r\leq\frac{9}{4}.

(3.8)

The pair of inequalities in (
3.8
) is equivalent to the condition

|a-5/4|+r\leq 1,

hence

\mathbb{B}(a,r)\subset\mathbb{B}\!\left(\frac{5}{4},\,1\right).

(3.9)

Next we show that

\mathbb{B}\!\left(\frac{5}{4},\,1\right)\subset\varphi(\mathbb{D}).

(3.10)

For
z=e^{it}
, a direct computation shows that

\left|\varphi(e^{it})-\frac{5}{4}\right|^{2}=\frac{9}{8}-\frac{1}{8}\cos(2t).

and this quantity is at least
1
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore the entire
curve
\varphi(\partial\mathbb{D})
lies outside the circle of radius
1

centered at
5/4
, except at two tangency points at
t=0
and
t=\pi
. This proves (
3.10
).

Combining (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) gives

p(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{B}(a,r)\subset\mathbb{B}\!\left(\frac{5}{4},\,1\right)\subset\varphi(\mathbb{D}).

Since
\varphi
is univalent on
\mathbb{D}
, the map

w(z):=\varphi^{-1}(p(z))

is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
, satisfies
w(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
,
and follows
w(0)=0
because
p(0)=\varphi(0)=1
.
Thus

p(z)=\varphi(w(z))\quad\mbox{for all }z\in\mathbb{D},

which shows that
p\prec\varphi
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We denote the class
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
as below,

\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\operatorname{Re}\,\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>\gamma,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\right\},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\quad 0\leq\gamma<1.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
in
|z|<r_{\gamma}
, where
r_{\gamma}
is the least positive root of the equation
g(r)=\gamma
, and

g(r)=\left(1-r-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right)-\frac{r\left(1+\frac{r}{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2}(1-r^{2})},\qquad\gamma\in[0,1).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
. Then

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)=\left(1+\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2},

so there exists an analytic Schwarz function
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
w(0)=0
such that

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\varphi(w(z))\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Equivalently,

zf^{\prime}(z)=f(z)\,\varphi(w(z)).

Differentiating both sides gives

zf^{\prime\prime}(z)+f^{\prime}(z)=f^{\prime}(z)\varphi(w(z))+f(z)\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))\,w^{\prime}(z),

and hence

1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\varphi(w(z))+\frac{f(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\,\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))\,w^{\prime}(z).

Taking real parts and using
\operatorname{Re}(A+B)\geq\operatorname{Re}(A)-|B|
, we get

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(w(z)))-\left|\frac{f(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\,\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))\,w^{\prime}(z)\right|.

(3.11)

From
zf^{\prime}(z)=f(z)\varphi(w(z)),
we have

\frac{f(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{z}{\varphi(w(z))}.

(3.12)

Substituting (
3.12
) into (
3.11
) gives

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(w(z)))-\frac{|z|}{|\varphi(w(z))|}\,|\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))|\,|w^{\prime}(z)|.

Now apply the Schwarz–Pick lemma to
w,

|w^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1-|w(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}},

therefore, we have

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(w(z)))-\frac{|z|}{|\varphi(w(z))|}\,|\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))|\,\frac{1-|w(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

(3.13)

Since
0\leq 1-|w(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, we further obtain

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(w(z)))-\frac{|z|}{|\varphi(w(z))|}\,|\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))|\,\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Next, for
\varphi(z)=\left(1+z/2\right)^{2}
we have
\varphi^{\prime}(z)=1+z/2
, and therefore using Schwarz lemma, we obtain

\left.\begin{aligned} |\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))|&=\left|1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right|\leq 1+\frac{|w(z)|}{2}\leq 1+\frac{|z|}{2},\\[11.38109pt]
|\varphi(w(z))|&=\left|1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right|^{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{|w(z)|}{2}\right)^{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{2},\end{aligned}\right\}

(3.14)

substituting (
3.14
) into (
3.13
), and using

\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(w(z)))=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right)^{2},

we obtain

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{|z|\left(1+\frac{|z|}{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})}.

Now estimating the real part,

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right)^{2}=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+w(z)+\frac{w(z)^{2}}{4}\right)\geq 1-|w(z)|-\frac{|w(z)|^{2}}{4}\geq 1-|z|-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4}.

Therefore, for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\left(1-|z|-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4}\right)-\frac{|z|\left(1+\frac{|z|}{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})}=g(|z|),

where

g(r)=\left(1-r-\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right)-\frac{r\left(1+\frac{r}{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2}(1-r^{2})},\qquad 0\leq r<1.

It remains to prove that
g
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
. Write

g(r)=1-r-\frac{r^{2}}{4}-h(r),\qquad h(r):=\frac{r\left(1+\frac{r}{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2}(1-r^{2})}.

Then

g^{\prime}(r)=-1-\frac{r}{2}-h^{\prime}(r).

A simple computation gives

h^{\prime}(r)=\frac{4\bigl(-r^{4}-3r^{3}+2r^{2}+3r+2\bigr)}{(2-r)^{3}(1-r)^{2}(1+r)^{2}}.

It is easy to see that

-r^{4}-3r^{3}+2r^{2}+3r+2=(1-r^{2})(r^{2}+3r+2)+3r^{2}>0\qquad\text{for }0<r<1,

furthermore,

(2-r)^{3}(1-r)^{2}(1+r)^{2}>0\qquad\text{for }0<r<1.

Thus, we have

h^{\prime}(r)>0\qquad\text{for }0<r<1.

Therefore

g^{\prime}(r)=-1-\frac{r}{2}-h^{\prime}(r)<0\qquad\text{for }0<r<1,

so
g
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
.
Thus, for each
\gamma\in[0,1)
, the equation
g(r)=\gamma
has a unique solution
r_{\gamma}\in(0,1)
.

Finally, if
|z|<r_{\gamma}
, then since
g
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
,

g(|z|)>g(r_{\gamma})=\gamma.

Consequently,

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq g(|z|)>\gamma.

Hence

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>\gamma\qquad\text{for }|z|<r_{\gamma},

that is,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
in
|z|<r_{\gamma}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
8
]

Let
P(z)
be a function in the Carathéodory class
\mathcal{P},
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, then
|p_{n}-p_{n-s}\,p_{s}|\leq 2,

n\geq s.
for
n=1,2,3,\ldots

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
.
Then the following coefficient bounds hold

|a_{2}|\leq 1,\quad|a_{3}|\leq\frac{5}{8},\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{4}|\leq 0.338667.

All these bounds are sharp.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
. Then there exists a Schwarz function
w(z)
satisfying

w(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}z^{k}

such that

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\left(1+\frac{w(z)}{2}\right)^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Assume that

w(z)=\frac{p(z)-1}{p(z)+1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{P}.

After substituting the expressions for
w(z)
,
p(z)
, and
f(z)
, and comparing coefficients, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{2}=\frac{p_{1}}{2},\qquad a_{3}=\frac{p_{1}^{2}+8p_{2}}{32},\qquad a_{4}=\frac{32p_{3}-p_{1}^{3}}{192},

Using the coefficient estimate for the Carathéodory class
\mathcal{P}
, we have
|p_{n}|\leq 2
for
n\geq 1
.

We now compute the bounds for
a_{2},a_{3}
, and
a_{4}
respectively,

|a_{2}|\leq 1,

which is attained by the Schwarz function
w(z)=z
, giving

f(z)=z\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}+z\right).

Similarly, using Lemma
3.1
we can easily obtain

|a_{3}|\leq\frac{5}{8}.

Which is achieved by
w(z)=z
, which also gives

f(z)=z\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}+z\right),

For the coefficient
a_{4},
we use the relation between the coefficients of Schwarz and Carathéodory functions,

w(z)=w_{1}z+w_{2}z^{2}+w_{3}z^{3}+w_{4}z^{4}+\cdots,\qquad w(0)=0,\qquad|w(z)|<1.

Define the associated Carathéodory function by

p(z)=\frac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)}=1+p_{1}z+p_{2}z^{2}+p_{3}z^{3}+p_{4}z^{4}+\cdots.

\dfrac{288}{5}&\dfrac{5968}{15}&\dfrac{2252}{3}&\dfrac{12772}{15}&\dfrac{5384}{15}\\[14.22636pt]
196&\dfrac{2496}{5}&\dfrac{12158}{15}&910&\dfrac{2504}{5}\\[14.22636pt]
\dfrac{1904}{5}&\dfrac{3103}{5}&\dfrac{7606}{9}&\dfrac{13583}{15}&\dfrac{9284}{15}\\[14.22636pt]
\dfrac{1328}{3}&607&\dfrac{2170}{3}&\dfrac{2159}{3}&524\\[14.22636pt]
61&61&61&61&61\end{pmatrix}.

Now we use the basic Bernstein-basis enclosure property: if

F(p,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}\sum_{j=0}^{4}b_{ij}\,B_{i}^{6}(p)\,B_{j}^{4}(x)\qquad\text{on }\quad[0,1]\times[0,1],

then

\min_{i,j}b_{ij}\leq F(p,x)\leq\max_{i,j}b_{ij}\qquad\text{for all }(p,x)\in[0,1]\times[0,1].

From the coefficient matrix above, the largest Bernstein coefficient is

\max_{0\leq i\leq 6,\,0\leq j\leq 4}b_{ij}=910.

Therefore,

F(p,x)\leq 910\qquad\text{for all }(p,x)\in[0,1]\times[0,1].

Equivalently,

\displaystyle G_{2}(p_{1},x)\leq 910\qquad\text{for all }0\leq p_{1}\leq 1,\,0\leq x\leq 1.

Thus we obtain the desired upper bound

\max_{0\leq p_{1}\leq 1,\,0\leq x\leq 1}G_{2}(p_{1},x)\leq 910.

Combining both cases we obtain that

H_{1}(p_{1},x,y)\leq 1024\quad\text{over}\quad\mathcal{D}.

Therefore

|H_{3}(1)|\leq\frac{1024}{9216}=\frac{1}{9}.

Finally, the estimate is sharp. Taking
w(z)=z^{3}
in the defining subordination,

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\left(1+\frac{z^{3}}{2}\right)^{2},

and integrating gives

f(z)=z\exp\!\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+t^{3}/2)^{2}-1}{t}\,dt\right)=z\exp\!\left(\frac{z^{3}}{3}+\frac{z^{6}}{24}\right).

For this function,
a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{5}=0
and
a_{4}=1/3
, hence

H_{3}(1)=-a_{4}^{2}=-\frac{1}{9},

Therefore, we have
|H_{3}(1)|=1/9
. This completes the proof.
