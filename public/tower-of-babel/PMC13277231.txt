Predictors of functional outcome of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage in elderly patients from a comprehensive stroke care unit, prospective cohort hospital based study
Authors: Islam El-Malky, Hosam Alieldeen Abdelmageed, Mahmoud Abdelhafiz, Hussein Bahey El-Deen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFew studies have been reported on intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH) in elderly patients at Middle East and North Africa region (MENA). It is thought that epidemiological, radiological characteristics, and neurological outcomes differ in elderly patients from younger age group. Our study’s aim is detection of the difference between them as regard clinical and radiological characters, functional outcome, and its predictors.MethodsAll patients with spontaneous ICH were divided into two groups, elderly group (≥ 70 years old) and younger age group. All clinical and-radiological data were collected. The patients were classified into four categories (probable Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy, Hypertensive, Vascular and Undetermined). One-month mortality and three months’ functional outcome, using modified Rankin Scale (mRS). were determined. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to determine the predictors of favorable functional outcome.ResultsProspective cohort study was conducted on 71 old age patients from 238 patients (30.3%). Hypertension, higher NIHSS (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale) lower GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale) and previous medications of antiplatelet or anticoagulant were more common in elderly group with statistically significant difference (P value: < 0.017, 0.002, 0.002, and 0.003, respectively). Probability of CAA (Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy) was common in elderly patients. Younger age group had better outcome as regard three months’ functional outcome and one-month mortality (44.3% versus 19.7% with p value: < 0.001, 21.6% versus 39.4% with p value: 0.006, respectively). GCS and NIHSS are predictors of favorable functional outcome.ConclusionSpontaneous ICH in elderly has different demographic, clinical and radiological findings with worse One-month mortality and functional outcome. Higher GCS and lower NIHSS are predictors of favorable functional outcome.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-026-05023-z.Keywords: Intracerebral hemorrhage, Elderly patients, Functional outcome

Introduction

Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) represents nearly 25–30% of all strokes worldwide and has one-month mortality rate of up to 40% [1]. Furthermore, only 10–25% of survivors achieve functional independence and recurrence of ICH is common between them [2]. Middle East and North Africa region (MENA) and worldwide population continue to grow older, there will be changes to the landscape of healthcare and unmet need for a better understanding of stroke in the elderly patients. A systematic review and meta-analysis of hospital-based studies in sub-Saharan Africa (countries with poverty) reported that the overall 30-day survival of overall stroke was much lower compared with western countries due to weak healthcare systems [3].

In previous ICH studies, age was an independent predictor of poor functional outcomes using the Functional Independence Measurement score [4]. As the population age increases, the incidence of ICH in the elderly will likely increases [5]. Elderly patients characterized by more vascular risk factors and easily failed organs such as kidneys due to, for example, acute kidney injury by contrast which is used for angiographic imaging [6]. Also, elderly population usually uses more medications (antiplatelet and anticoagulants. ICH patients, in general need more duration of hospital admission and more ICU (Intensive Care Unit) resources and this is expected more in this category of patients. 2019, MENA region death rate of stroke was highest in the oldest age group, which reached its highest in the 75–79 and 80–84 age groups for males and females, respectively [7]. A 2021 report by the World Health Organization (WHO) identified urgent demand to increase stroke units and services globally, particularly among low- and middle-income regions [8]. So, understanding the risk factors of ICH morbidity and mortality will help in improving the disease outcome in the future in this region.

There is a paucity in research regarding ICH in elderly patients and its functional outcome, especially in MENA region. In our study, we aim to investigate the clinical, radiological and predictors of favorable functional outcome in elderly patients with spontaneous ICH in Qena Governorate, south of Egypt which has more than 3.6 million inhabitants as a country from MENA region.

Methods

We conducted a prospective cohort study from July 2024 to July 2025 in our comprehensive Stroke Care Unit at a tertiary Centre. Our university ethical committee approved the study (SVU/MED/NAP020/24/8/913). Helsinki declaration was followed and written consent from the patients or their relatives were obtained. This study follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) Statement for cross sectional studies (S1 File).We collected all intracerebral hemorrhage (parenchymal type). We included all patients over 18 years old. We excluded cases with primary subdural, epidural or subarachnoid hemorrhage. We excluded traumatic ICH, hemorrhage due to cancer, transformation of cerebral infarction (with or without thrombolysis), coagulopathy due to hepatic, renal or hematological diseases. We divided patients into two groups, younger age group (< 70 years old) and elderly one ≥ 70 years old). Fig. 1 reported the flowchart of patient selection.

Clinical assessment

Demographic data such as age, gender, waist to hip ratio, family history of ICH, medications (antiplatelet and or anticoagulants) and smoking were reported. Comorbidities such as hypertension (HTN), diabetes mellitus (DM), Hyperlipidemia, and previous stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic) were revealed. modified Rankin Scale (mRS) before the recent attack was reported in every patient with past history of stroke. The patient was counted hypertensive if BP > 140/90 mmHg twice before the stroke or patient was under antihypertensive treatment currently [9]. The patients were first diagnosed as hypertensive if his blood pressure continued > 140/90 two weeks after occurrence of ICH and discharged on antihypertensive. The patient was counted diabetic if glycaemia ≥ 126 mg/dl pre-prandial on at least 2 examinations, ≥ 200 mg/dl post-prandial, HbA1c ≥ 6.5% or already under hypoglycemic treatment [10]. Medication with antiplatelet and or anticoagulants was reported from all patients. National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) [11] and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores [12] were reported at admission. Complications such as seizures, re-bleeding, infection (aspiration pneumonia or skin infection), deterioration of conscious level and electrolyte disturbances were collected.

Radiological assessment

CT brain scan (within one hour) was obtained at admission using a 64-slice CT scanner. All CT scans were performed in supine position. CT slice thickness was 5 mm. Assessment of the hematoma included its site (basal or lobar), presence of leukoariosis (White matter lesions WMLs), regularity, size, presence of multiple hemorrhages, hydrocephalus, mass effect, and presence of intraventricular or subarachnoid extension of hemorrhage. The hematoma volume (HV) on CT was measured according to ABC/2 method. A (Largest Diameter) was represented in cm on the CT slice showing the largest hemorrhage area in axial view. B (Perpendicular Diameter) was measured in cm on the same slice, perpendicular to A.C (Approximate Height) was measured by number of slices multiplied by the slice thickness in cm [13]. Two seniors’ neuro-radiologists performed these measurements blindly, in addition to third opinion if there is a difference between them. Hematoma expansion (HE) was confirmed by an absolute HV increase ≥ 12.5 mL or a proportional increase ≥ 33% at follow up CT Scan 3–72 h or at deterioration of conscious level [14].

MRI brain (1.5 Tesla) with T1, T2, T2* and MRA was conducted to detect micro bleeds and its site, in addition to vascular causes of hemorrhage. Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) was conducted in cases with suspicion of vascular malformation and noninvasive studies didn’t report an evident cause, especially in young normotensive patients. DSA was repeated one month later, if nothing revealed in the first DSA.

We classified the patients according to the clinical and radiological data to four categories, hypertensive, probable CAA, vascular, undetermined. Hypertensive category was determined in hypertensive patients with basal hematoma or basal micro bleeds. We used Simplified Edinburgh Criteria and Boston Criteria version 1 for determination of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) probability [15, 16]. Undetermined category included hemorrhagic cases without cause or cases with mixed presentation (such as hypertensive patients with lobar hematoma and low probability of CAA). ICH score to predict 30 days’ mortality was obtained in each patient [17]. ICH score has five predictive factors (ICH volume, low score on GCS, infratentorial location of ICH, intraventricular extension of the hemorrhage, and older age [more than 80 years]). The ICH score (0–6) was counted as reported by Hemphill et al. [17]. One point was given for age > 80 years, one point for infratentorial origin, one point for ICH volume > 30 ml, one point for a GCS of 5–12, one point for intraventricular extension of ICH, and two points for a GCS of 3–4.

Treatment and outcome measurement

All patients were admitted to a stroke care unit and antihypertensive (Amlodipine, Labetalol) was taken according to patient condition [16]. Patients had a surgery at admission If HV > 10 or 30 cc in the infratentorial or supratentorial region, respectively, or GCS score less than 8. Craniotomy or craniectomy was approved for hematoma evacuation when at least one pupil was dilated or loss of reflex. ICH catheter introduction was performed when the pupil was not dilated and the pupil reflex was normal. Conservative treatment was approved in patients with hematoma volume < 10 or 30 cc in the infratentorial or supratentorial regions, respectively, or with brainstem hemorrhage. In our stroke care unit, we usually use mannitol in the majority of cases, except in cases with renal impairment, we use hypertonic saline. CSF drainage was applied in case of progressive hydrocephalus due to intraventricular expansion and associated disturbance of consciousness. Craniectomy was performed if intractable brain swelling or bulging after evacuation of the hematoma was observed [18]. One-month mortality and Three months mRS (modified Rankin Scale) for outcome assessment were reported [19]. Favorable functional outcome was counted if mRS was 0 to 2. In cases when the patient could not follow up in the stroke clinic, a telephonic communication was conducted to report the mRS.

Statistical analysis

Analysis of the collected data was performed by a software (IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27.0). Median and IQR (interquartile range) was performed for not normally distributed continuous data. Mean and SD (stander deviation) was obtained for normally distributed continuous variables. Normality was setteled by operating Shapiro–Wilk or Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. We operated frequencies or proportions for categorical variables. Multiple demographic, clinical and radiological data were analyzed between elderly and younger age groups to show the differences between them, using Chi square test or Fischer exact test when expected low cell counts. Student t-test test or Mann–Whitney U were used for normally and not normally distributed continuous variables, respectively. Multiple demographic, clinical and radiological data were analyzed between favorable and bad functional outcome groups to show the differences between them. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Binary multivariate logistic regression tests were conducted for the demographic, clinical and radiological data which might have an effect on the functional outcome. Entered factors included those with at least marginal significance (P < 0.1) on univariate analysis, using enter method. Nagelkerke R square was 0.615 and p value of Hosmer–Lemeshow test was 0.960, indicating a well-fitting model. Multi-collinearity of the final model was tested. If the variance inflation factor (VIF) of a variable was greater or equal to 10 or the tolerance limit was less than 0.1, The variable would be removed from the model. Bonferroni correction was performed to adjust for type I error inflation, so P-value less than 0.004 would be significant for the regression analysis model.

Discussion

Our prospective cohort study reported the rate of spontaneous ICH (sICH) in elderly patients, epidemiological characters, mortality and functional outcome in comparison to younger age population in south of Egypt, one of the largest population country in MENA region. We reported that higher GCS and lower NIHSS were predictors of favorable functional outcome.

Our study revealed 29.8% of all cases, in concordance with El-Tallawy H et al. who reported 32.5% aged more than 60 years old in south Egypt [20]. Ala Rashi M. et al. reported 34% of his population aged more than 60 years old from Syria [21]. These reported percentages had confirmed the results of a meta-analysis, done by Wolsink A. and his collages, which showed increasing sICH with increasing age, with the lowest incidence in age less than 45 years and the highest incidence in age more than 85 years [22]. The Dijon Stroke Registry reported in France that Ageing and growth of the population led to an increase in the absolute number of strokes, especially in the elderly [23].

Our study reported the reduction of the gap between female and male sICH rate in elderly patients in comparison to younger patients which was similar to the global trend [24]. As the global population age increases and female life expectancy exceeds that of males with improvement of health care and risk factors changes, the ICH burden in females is approaching that of males, reducing gender differences [25].

HTN and medications use were prominent in elderly group in our study. Forman et al. reported that HTN is the first cause of sICH in elderly patients (52%), in concordance with our study 49.3% [26]. Regarding WHO reports, hypertension was present in more than 45% of African population (older than 25 years old) and more than 50% of hypertensive patients were not diagnosed, or did not take treatment [27, 28]. Hypertension was common in both young and elderly group in our study, but lower occurrence in young age versus elderly one (74.3% versus 87.3%). This revealed that MENA region needs more strategies for health care awareness, control HTN, and consecutively ICH. A meta-analysis was performed by Maeda T et al. reported the importance of HTN control to less than 130 systolic blood pressure for prevention of ICH recurrence [29]. Lone AF (Atrial fibrillation) is increasing with age so usage of anticoagulant is common in elderly patients [30]

The second most common cause of sICH is probable CAA which represent 18.3% in our cohort, in concordance with Inoue Y. et al. who noticed that lobar hematoma was common in elderly people 31.3% of elderly Asian population (more than 80 years old) [31]. Our study reported 69.2% of cases with lobar hematoma in elderly group while an Italian study showed that the percentage of lobar ICH was almost similar between elderly patients aged older than 85 years (52.7%) and younger patients aged 65–74 years (52.3%) [32]. These differences were usually due to different ethnicity and different risk factors exposure.

Our study reported more surgical intervention in elderly group (11.3% versus 3.6%). This could be explained by more indications of surgery in this group such as midline shift and hydrocephalus which were more observed in this group,as a result of more hematoma size and more probability of CAA. Also, elderly group had less GCS at presentation which was a predictor of unfavorable outcome in our study. Ching-Jen Chen and his collages reported more surgical intervention in young age group, opposite to our study [33].

Our study reported 39.4% mortality in concordance with other studies [22, 26]. Wolsink A. and his collages reported in meta-analysis better functional outcome than our study (35%) and this might be due to inclusion of studies which most of them were from western countries [22, 31]. Wang H. and his colleges reported in a study where they analyzed Data from the 2021 Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) in Africa that ICH has a larger disease burden in Africa, followed by the Americas, while Asia and Europe have a less disease burden, but still less than 1990, indicating a good progress as regard prevention and treatment [34]. This was because of the low socioeconomic state and less health care in Africa.

Less NIHSS and high GCS were the predictors of favorable functional outcome in our study. Batista A and his collages reported that higher GCS was a predictor of good functional outcome and age was not a predictor [35]. Zeng L. and his collages reported multiple predictors of poor outcome such as older age, higher NIHSS, and IVH. [36]. Some studies didn’t report hematoma size as a predictors of functional outcome like our study [37]. This might be due to the impact of initial hematoma volume on functional outcome is often expressed by other variables, such as NIHSS score at admission, which quantifies initial neurological deficits caused by the hematoma. Also, Different methods for size estimation, such as the ABC/2 formula, could have overestimation errors in comparison to volumetric analysis.

In spite of prospective nature of our study, but it has some limitations. Single center and hospital based study prevent generalizability of the results. The sample size of elderly patients was relatively small and lack of data measures such as blood pressure on hospital arrival. DSA was not performed to all cases, also, there was no genetic test or biopsy confirmation to determine definite CAA. So, many cases fall in the undetermined classification. Also, the lack of laboratory investigations was one of the limitations. The incomplete investigations were due to uncovering most of populations in MENA region by health insurance.

Conclusion

Spontaneous ICH in elderly has different demographic, clinical and radiological findings with worse one-month mortality and three months’ functional outcome than younger age. Still, hypertension is the first cause in both age groups in MENA region for performance of more effort to control it and, consecutively ICH. Higher GCS and lower NIHSS are predictors of favorable functional outcome.
