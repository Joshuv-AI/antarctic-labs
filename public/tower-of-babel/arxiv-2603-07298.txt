Time Dependent String Compactification: Towards Bouncing Cosmology
Mir Mehedi Faruk a,b

Abstract
We study the Null Energy Condition (NEC) arising from the Virasoro constraint on the string worldsheet. We then analyze how the NEC in the external spacetime directions emerges under general time-dependent string compactifications. Finally, we exhibit compactifications in which the averaged Einstein-frame condition allows the lower-dimensional description of the external spacetime to violate the NEC, thereby realizing a bouncing cosmology, while the higher-dimensional NEC remains satisfied, as dictated by worldsheet symmetry. We comment on scale-separated solutions obtained through the averaged Einstein-frame condition.

Summary and Discussion

In this article, we first focused on how worldsheet symmetry leads to the NEC. We considered bosonic string theory, where the string couples to the dilaton, metric, and antisymmetric

In Section

4

33
). In that section, both the warp factor and the internal coordinates depend on time. We additionally imposed periodicity in the warp factor so that its contribution averages to zero over each cycle. In this way, we obtained a comprehensive understanding of how the NEC in the external spacetime directions emerges under general time-dependent string compactifications. By contrast, if one implements the unaveraged condition and imposes the higher-dimensional NEC pointwise, then the lower-dimensional NEC follows trivially upon compactification. In the usual time-independent compactifications

;
60
, this unaveraged condition is typically imposed, which guarantees the lower-dimensional NEC from the higher-dimensional one; this observation is often used to argue against bouncing cosmologies.

Bouncing cosmologies are often considered as possible alternatives to the standard Big Bang singularity. To realize a bounce, the universe must transition from contraction to expansion: during contraction the Hubble rate
H
is negative, while during expansion it is positive. Thus, at the bounce

must increase, passing through

Most interestingly, the averaged Einstein-frame condition also allows scale-separated solutions.

Scale separation is a key phenomenological requirement: the compact internal space must be much smaller than the non-compact spacetime directions, so that the physics can be reliably captured by a lower-dimensional effective description.
Such a hierarchy is notoriously difficult to realize in known string-theory vacua

Appendix A: Non-negativity of
\text{tr}\left[(h^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h))^{2}\right]

In this section, we prove that

\text{tr}\left[(h^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h))^{2}\right]\geq 0,

(75)

is non-negative, where we denote
\bar{\nabla}_{l}\equiv l\cdot\bar{\nabla}
. Firstly, since
h_{pq}
is a symmetric matrix, we can diagonalize it using matrix notation as:

h=RDR^{-1}\,,

(76)

where
D
is a diagonal matrix with non-negative elements (as
h_{pq}
is a positive semi-definite matrix), and
R
is an orthogonal matrix (i.e.,
R^{-1}=R^{t}
). Notice that the covariant derivative
\bar{\nabla}_{l}
of

can affect both
D
and
R

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{l}h=

\displaystyle R(\bar{\nabla}_{l}D+R^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}R)D+D(\bar{\nabla}_{l}R^{-1})R)R^{-1}=R(\bar{\nabla}_{l}D+[M,D])R^{-1}\,,

(77)

where we defined

This allows us to write (

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left[D^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{l}DD^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{l}D\right]+2~\text{tr}\left[D^{-1}\bar{\nabla}_{l}DD^{-1}[M,D]\right]

\displaystyle+\text{tr}\left[D^{-1}[M,D]D^{-1}[M,D]\right]\,.

which is a sum of non-negative terms, as each element

The factor
\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega/\Omega
.
Since
\Omega
depends only on
t
(and
y
),

\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega=l^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Omega=l^{t}\dot{\Omega},\hskip 23.49976pt\Rightarrow\hskip 23.49976pt\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega}{\Omega}=l^{t}\frac{\dot{\Omega}}{\Omega}=l^{t}\dot{f}.

(89)

Hence

-(d-2)\left(\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega}{\Omega}\right)^{2}=-(l^{t})^{2}(d-2)\dot{f}^{\,2}.

(90)

Next we evaluate the trace term.
Because
h_{mn}=h_{mn}(t,y)
,

(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h)_{mn}=l^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}h_{mn}=l^{t}\dot{h}_{mn},

(91)

so

h^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h)=l^{t}(h^{-1}\dot{h})=l^{t}\mathcal{B}.

(92)

Therefore

-\frac{1}{4}\,\tr\!\left(h^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h)\,h^{-1}(\bar{\nabla}_{l}h)\right)=-(l^{t})^{2}\frac{1}{4}\,\tr(\mathcal{B}^2).

(93)

Furthermore,
Using
X_{\mu}=\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\ln(\Omega^{d-2}\sqrt{\det h})
and (
46
),

X_{\mu}=\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\big((d-2)f+k\big).

(94)

Since
f
and
k
depend only on
t
(and
y
), we have

X_{t}=(d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k},\hskip 23.49976ptX_{i}=0,

(95)

and thus

X_{l}=l^{\mu}X_{\mu}=l^{t}\big((d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k}\big).

(96)

Combining this with
\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega/\Omega=l^{t}\dot{f}
, we obtain

2\,\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{l}\Omega}{\Omega}\,X_{l}=2(l^{t}\dot{f})\Big(l^{t}\big((d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k}\big)\Big)=(l^{t})^{2}\Big(2(d-2)\dot{f}^{\,2}+2\dot{f}\,\dot{k}\Big).

(97)

We compute

\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{\alpha}=\partial_{\beta}X_{\alpha}-\bar{\Gamma}^{\sigma}_{\beta\alpha}X_{\sigma}.

(98)

Since
X_{i}=0
, we split

l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{\alpha}=l^{t}l^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{t}+l^{i}l^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{i}.

For the first piece,
\partial_{i}X_{t}=0

(100)

For the second piece,
X_{i}=0
implies

\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{i}=-\bar{\Gamma}^{t}_{\beta i}\,X_{t}.

(101)

In FRW, the only nonzero Christoffel needed is

\bar{\Gamma}^{t}_{ji}=a\dot{a}\,\delta_{ji},\hskip 23.49976pt\bar{\Gamma}^{t}_{ti}=0,

(102)

l^{i}l^{\beta}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}X_{i}=-l^{i}l^{j}\,\bar{\Gamma}^{t}_{ji}\,X_{t}=-(a\dot{a})\,\delta_{ij}l^{i}l^{j}\,X_{t}.

(103)

Using
X_{t}=(d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k}

\bar{\nabla}_{l}X_{l}=(l^{t})^{2}\big((d-2)\ddot{f}+\ddot{k}\big)-(a\dot{a})\,\delta_{ij}l^{i}l^{j}\,\big((d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k}\big),

(104)

and therefore

-\bar{\nabla}_{l}X_{l}=-(l^{t})^{2}\big((d-2)\ddot{f}+\ddot{k}\big)+(a\dot{a})\,\delta_{ij}l^{i}l^{j}\,\big((d-2)\dot{f}+\dot{k}\big).

(105)
