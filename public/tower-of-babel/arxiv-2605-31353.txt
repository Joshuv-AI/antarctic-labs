Sensing with Random Signals: The Role of Time Sharing
Yi Geng, Wenyi Zhang

Abstract
In monostatic, decision-aided, or known-waveform integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) formulations, the sensing receiver is often modeled as knowing the transmitted waveform. This assumption is not suitable for passive, bistatic, or distributed settings where the sensing receiver knows the signaling rule but not the transmitted symbols. We study such a symbol-unaware ISAC model, where sensing is measured by the unconditioned mutual information I ⁡ ( S , V ) I(S;V) rather than the symbol-aware quantity I ⁡ ( S ; V | X ) I(S;V|X) . For discrete-input memoryless channels, we characterize the capacity–sensing region through an auxiliary time-sharing variable, showing that the optimal upper boundary is the upper concave envelope of the single-mode frontier. Thus, explicit time sharing is unnecessary when the single-mode frontier is already concave, but strictly beneficial when its upper concave envelope strictly dominates the frontier. For Rayleigh-fading BPSK, we further show that the curvature of the single-mode boundary is determined by the stochastic ordering of the communication- and sensing-side effective SNR distributions. Communication-side dominance yields a concave single-mode frontier and no time-sharing gain, sensing-side dominance yields a convex single-mode frontier and a strict time-sharing gain, and equality yields a linear boundary. The result extends to SIMO-BPSK through the ordering of post-combining SNR distributions. These findings explain when symbol-unaware ISAC optimally moves from data-symbol transmission to pilot-like sensing modes.

II
System Model

Consider a symbol-unaware sensing ISAC system over a state-dependent memoryless channel as shown in Fig.1. A message
W\in[1:2^{nR}]
is transmitted over
n
channel uses. In addition to reliable communication, the transmitted signal probes an i.i.d. state sequence
S^{n}=(S_{1},\dots,S_{n})
through sensing observations
V^{n}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
.

Fig. 1
:
symbol-unaware sensing ISAC system

Unlike distortion-based formulations, we do not introduce an explicit estimator. Instead, sensing performance is quantified by the mutual information between the state and sensing observation without conditioning on the transmitted symbols, capturing the symbol-unaware sensing nature.

The physical channel is a state-dependent memoryless channel
specified by
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{V},P_{S},W),
where
S_{i}\sim P_{S}
is i.i.d. and independent of the message, and
W(y,v|x,s)=P_{Y,V|X,S}(y,v|x,s)
is an arbitrary joint transition law from
(X,S)
to
(Y,V)
.
Thus, for a length-
n
input sequence,

P(s^{n},y^{n},v^{n}|x^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{S}(s_{i})W(y_{i},v_{i}|x_{i},s_{i}).

(1)

The induced communication and sensing marginal laws are

\displaystyle W_{c}(y|x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{s,v}P_{S}(s)W(y,v|x,s),

(2)

\displaystyle W_{s}(v|x,s)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{y}W(y,v|x,s),

(3)

or equivalently
P_{S,V|X}(s,v|x)=P_{S}(s)W_{s}(v|x,s).

The product model
P_{S}(s)W_{c}(y|x)W_{s}(v|x,s)
is only a special coupling of these two marginals and is not assumed in the general model.

Definition 1
.

A
(2^{nR},n)
code consists of:
(i) a message set
\mathcal{W}=[1:2^{nR}]
,
(ii) an encoder
f_{n}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{X}^{n}
,
and (iii) a decoder
\phi_{n}:\mathcal{Y}^{n}\to\mathcal{W}
.

For an input distribution
P_{X}
, define the communication and sensing metrics as
R(P_{X})=I(X;Y)
and
D(P_{X})=I(S;V)
.

For a given code, the induced marginal at time
i
is

P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}(x)=\Pr[X_{i}=x]
, and the sensing performance is defined as

D_{n}(f_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D\!\big(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\big).

(4)

Definition 2
.

P_{e}^{(n)}
denotes the average probability of error. A pair
(R,D)
is achievable if there exists a sequence of
(2^{nR},n)
codes such that
P_{e}^{(n)}\to 0
and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(f_{n})\geq D
.

Remark 1
.

The use of
D_{n}(f_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(S_{i};V_{i})
follows directly from the memoryless sensing model. Since each state
S_{i}
is observed through its corresponding sensing output
V_{i}
, a symbol-by-symbol sensing metric is natural, particularly for practical receivers that process sensing observations per channel use or per snapshot. This averaged single-letter metric preserves the symbol-unaware sensing effect of the unknown transmitted symbol and does not require starting from the block mutual information
I(S^{n};V^{n})
.

III
Single-Letter Communication–Sensing Region under Averaged Symbol-unaware SMI

We now characterize the communication–sensing region of the symbol-unaware ISAC model in single-letter form.
The auxiliary random variable
Q
represents a time-sharing schedule across channel uses.
Since the sensing metric is defined symbol by symbol,
Q
should be understood as a per-coordinate scheduling variable rather than as a block-level sensing operation.

(Marginal invariance)

Fix

\sum_{y}W(y,v|x,s)=\sum_{y}\widetilde{W}(y,v|x,s).

Then the achievable communication–sensing region under the averaged

Theorem 1
.

For the memoryless symbol-unaware sensing ISAC model with arbitrary joint transition
law
W(y,v|x,s)
, the achievable communication–sensing region, and
conversely the closure of all achievable pairs, is

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{Q}P_{X|Q}:\\
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq 2\end{subarray}}\left\{(R,D):\begin{aligned} &0\leq R\leq I(X;Y|Q),\\
&0\leq D\leq I(S;V|Q)\end{aligned}\right\},

(7)

where
I(X;Y|Q)
is computed with the communication marginal
W_{c}
, and

I(S;V|Q)
is computed with the sensing marginal
P_{S}W_{s}
. Consequently,

\mathcal{C}
depends on the physical joint channel
W(y,v|x,s)
only
through the two marginals
W_{c}(y|x)
and
W_{s}(v|x,s)
.
Equivalently, the dominant tradeoff boundary of
\mathcal{C}
is the upper
concave envelope of the single-mode frontier generated by input distributions

P_{X}
, and the full region is the downward closure of this boundary. Hence,
when the endpoint values are nonzero, the complete outer boundary also includes
the horizontal and vertical endpoint extensions induced by this downward closure.

Proof.

For any
P_{Q}P_{X|Q}
, the communication quantity
I(X;Y|Q)
depends only on
the marginal channel
P_{Y|X}=W_{c}
. Similarly, the symbol-unaware sensing quantity

I(S;V|Q)
depends only on the marginal law
P_{S,V|X}=P_{S}W_{v}
. Hence the
set of single-mode operating points

\mathcal{A}=\left\{(I(X;Y),I(S;V)):P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

(8)

is the same under
W
and
\widetilde{W}
. Since the achievable region is the
downward closure of the convex hull of
\mathcal{A}
, the two regions are
identical.
∎

Geometrically, the upper boundary is given by the upper concave envelope, while the full region also contains the horizontal and vertical boundary segments induced by the downward closure.

Remark 2
.

The cardinality bound
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq 2
follows from the Fenchel–Eggleston strengthening of Carathéodory’s theorem. Indeed, the set
\mathcal{A}
is compact and connected, since
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
is compact and connected and mutual information is continuous in
P_{X}
. Hence every point in
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
can be represented as a convex combination of at most two points in
\mathcal{A}
, which corresponds to a binary time-sharing variable
Q
.

Remark 3

(Relation to
[
14
]
and the role of
Q
)
.

The proof follows the constrained channel-coding argument of
[
14
]
, but the role of the sensing constraint is different. In
[
14
]
, after optimizing the symbolwise estimator, the sensing constraint becomes an expected per-input cost, which is affine in
P_{X}
. Hence any time sharing can be absorbed into the averaged input distribution without decreasing the communication rate, since
I(X;Y)
is concave in
P_{X}
. An explicit auxiliary variable
Q
is therefore unnecessary there.

In the present blind-sensing model, the sensing metric is instead
D(P_{X})=I(S;V),
which is generally convex, not affine, in
P_{X}
. Thus, for

\bar{P}_{X}=\sum_{q}P_{Q}(q)P_{X|Q=q},

one only has

D(\bar{P}_{X})\leq\sum_{q}P_{Q}(q)D(P_{X|Q=q})=I(S;V|Q).

(9)

Absorbing
Q
into a single input distribution may therefore reduce the blind-sensing value. This is why
Q
is generally needed in (
7
), and why the optimal upper boundary is the upper concave envelope of the single-mode frontier.

Proof.

We give the main steps, since the reliability part follows the standard constant-composition coding argument for constrained channel coding.

Achievability

Fix
P_{Q}P_{X|Q}
with finite
\mathcal{Q}
. Choose a deterministic time-sharing sequence
q^{n}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
whose empirical distribution tends to
P_{Q}
, and define
\mathcal{I}_{q}=\{i:q_{i}=q\}
. Choose a conditional type
P_{X|Q,n}
compatible with
q^{n}
such that

P_{X|Q,n}(\cdot|q)\to P_{X|Q}(\cdot|q),\qquad\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}.

(10)

Generate each length-
n
codeword uniformly from the conditional type class

T(P_{X|Q,n}|q^{n})
, so that for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
q\in\mathcal{Q}
,
every codeword
x^{n}(m)
satisfies

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\mathbf{1}\{x_{i}(m)=x\}=P_{X|Q,n}(x|q).

(11)

It remains to verify the sensing metric. For a fixed input distribution
P_{X}
, define
D(P_{X})\triangleq I(S;V)
under the law induced by
P_{X}(x)P_{S}(s)W_{s}(v|x,s)
. For fixed
P_{S}
and
W_{s}
,
P_{V|S}
is affine in
P_{X}
; since
I(S;V)
is convex in
P_{V|S}
for fixed
P_{S}
,
D(P_{X})
is convex in
P_{X}
.

Let
P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}
denote the input marginal at coordinate
i
induced by the uniform message over the generated codebook. The conditional-type constraint gives, for every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}(x)=P_{X|Q,n}(x|q).

(12)

Therefore, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle D_{n}(f_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)})

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}{n}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}D(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}{n}D\!\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{q}|}{n}D\!\left(P_{X|Q,n}(\cdot|q)\right).

Letting
n\to\infty
and using the continuity of mutual information in the input distribution gives

\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(f_{n})\geq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}P_{Q}(q)D(P_{X|Q=q})=I(S;V|Q).

(14)

Thus every pair satisfying

R<I(X;Y|Q)
and
D<I(S;V|Q)
is achievable; the non-strict inequalities in (
7
) follow by closure.

Converse

Consider any achievable sequence of codes. Let
M
be uniformly distributed over the message set and let
X^{n}=f_{n}(M)
. By Fano’s inequality, the data-processing inequality, and the memoryless
property of the channel,

nR\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y_{i})+n\epsilon_{n},\qquad\epsilon_{n}\to 0.

(15)

where
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
. Hence

R\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y_{i})+\epsilon_{n}.

(16)

Let
T
be uniformly distributed over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and independent of all other variables, and define
(X,S,Y,V)=(X_{T},S_{T},Y_{T},V_{T}).
Then

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y_{i})=I(X;Y|T),

(17)

and, by the definition of the symbol-by-symbol symbol-unaware sensing metric,

D_{n}(f_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(S_{i};V_{i})=I(S;V|T).

(18)

The index
T
only single-letterizes the
n
coordinates of an arbitrary code; the final auxiliary variable is obtained by replacing the resulting averaged point by an equivalent cardinality-reduced time-sharing representation. Indeed, the pair
\bigl(I(X;Y|T),I(S;V|T)\bigr)
belongs to
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
, where

\mathcal{A}=\left\{\bigl(I(X;Y),I(S;V)\bigr):P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\right\}.

(19)

Since
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
is compact and connected, and mutual information is continuous in
P_{X}
, the set
\mathcal{A}
is compact and connected. Hence any subsequential limit of the averaged pairs remains in
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
. By the Fenchel–Eggleston theorem, this limiting point can be represented by a time-sharing variable
Q
with
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq 2
. Consequently,

R\leq I(X;Y|Q),\quad D\leq I(S;V|Q).

This proves the converse and completes the proof.
∎

IV
Geometry of the Single-Mode Boundary under BPSK and SIMO-BPSK

In this section, we specialize the single-letter time-sharing structure from Section III to Rayleigh-fading BPSK and SIMO-BPSK models. Our goal is to determine whether the single-mode frontier
C_{0}(D)
, generated without explicit time sharing, is already concave or whether its upper concave envelope strictly improves the achievable boundary.

IV-A

Scalar BPSK Frontier and Curvature Criterion

Consider the scalar Rayleigh-fading BPSK model with

X=\sqrt{P}B
,
B\in\{+1,-1\}
, and
\Pr(B=+1)=p
.
By the sign symmetry of BPSK, it suffices to take
p\in[1/2,1]
.
The communication and sensing observations are

Y=HX+N_{1},

(20)

and

V=SX+N_{2},

(21)

where
H\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Omega_{H})
,

S\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Omega_{S})
,

N_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{1}^{2})
, and

N_{2}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{2}^{2})
. The communication receiver knows

H
, whereas the sensing receiver does not know the transmitted symbol
realization.

Define the scalar BPSK-AWGN mutual-information kernel

J(p,\gamma)\triangleq I(B;\sqrt{\gamma}B+Z),

(22)

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. For each fixed
\gamma
,
J(p,\gamma)
is
symmetric around
p=1/2
and nonincreasing in
p
on
[1/2,1]
.

Conditioned on
H=h
, the communication channel is a scalar BPSK-AWGN
channel with effective SNR
\frac{\gamma=2P|h|^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}
. Hence

R(p)\triangleq I(X;Y|H)=\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(23)

where
w_{c}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{c}}
, and
\bar{\gamma}_{c}=\frac{2P\Omega_{H}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

For sensing, the chain rule gives

I(S;V)=I(S;V|X)+I(X;V)-I(X;V|S).

(24)

Since
S
is zero-mean circularly symmetric complex Gaussian,

V|X=+\sqrt{P}
and
V|X=-\sqrt{P}
have the same distribution, and thus

I(X;V)=0
. Therefore

G(p)\triangleq I(S;V)=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(25)

where
w_{s}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{s}},
and
\bar{\gamma}_{s}=\frac{2P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}.
.

Since
J(p,\gamma)
is nonincreasing in
p
,
R(p)
decreases and
G(p)

increases with
p
. The nontrivial single-mode frontier is therefore

C_{0}(D)=R(G^{-1}(D)),\qquad D\in[D_{\min},D_{\max}],

(26)

where
D_{\min}=G(1/2)
and
D_{\max}=G(1)
. With time sharing, the
corresponding boundary is the upper concave envelope of
C_{0}(D)
, and the
achievable region is its downward closure. Since (
26
)
only covers
D\in[D_{\min},D_{\max}]
, the complete boundary also includes
the endpoint extensions

R=R(1/2)
for
0\leq D\leq D_{\min}
and

D=D_{\max}
for
0\leq R\leq R(1)
.

The endpoint
p=1/2
corresponds to equiprobable data symbols, whereas

p=1
corresponds to a deterministic pilot-like mode. Thus the curvature of

C_{0}(D)
determines whether continuous input biasing is sufficient or
explicit time sharing is needed.

Define the marginal communication cost

\eta(p)\triangleq-\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}.

(27)

Since

C_{0}^{\prime}(D)=\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}=-\eta(p),

(28)

we have

C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)=-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}.

(29)

Thus, on the interior interval where
G^{\prime}(p)>0
, the sign of the frontier
curvature is opposite to the sign of
\eta^{\prime}(p)
.

Here and in the sequel, subscripts of
J
denote partial derivatives with
respect to the BPSK input-bias parameter
p
; that is,

J_{p}(p,\gamma)=\frac{\partial J(p,\gamma)}{\partial p}
and

J_{pp}(p,\gamma)=\frac{\partial^{2}J(p,\gamma)}{\partial p^{2}}
.

Let

\kappa_{p}(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{J_{pp}(p,\gamma)}{J_{p}(p,\gamma)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\log[-J_{p}(p,\gamma)].

(30)

For
a\in\{c,s\}
, define the tilted SNR density

\mu_{a,p}(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{-J_{p}(p,\gamma)w_{a}(\gamma)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}-J_{p}(p,t)w_{a}(t)dt}.

(31)

Then

\eta^{\prime}(p)=\eta(p)\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]\right).

(32)

Hence the curvature problem reduces to comparing two tilted averages of the
same function
\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
. Equivalently, if

\Delta_{\kappa}(p)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)],

(33)

then

C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(G(p))=-\frac{\eta(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}\Delta_{\kappa}(p).

(34)

Thus the sign of
\Delta_{\kappa}(p)
determines the local concavity or
convexity of the single-mode frontier.

The following two lemmas turn this comparison into an ordering condition.

Lemma 1
.

For every fixed
p\in(1/2,1)
, the function

\gamma\mapsto\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof is standard and is omitted; see the extended arXiv version for details.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
be probability densities on an interval

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. If
q_{1}/q_{2}
is nondecreasing, then for
every measurable increasing function
\phi
with finite expectations,

\mathbb{E}_{q_{1}}[\phi(X)]\geq\mathbb{E}_{q_{2}}[\phi(X)].

(35)

If both
q_{1}/q_{2}
and
\phi
are strictly increasing, the inequality is
strict.

Proof.

The proof is deferred to the extended arXiv version due to space limitations.
∎

By Lemmas
1
and
2
, the sign of

\eta^{\prime}(p)
is determined by the monotone likelihood-ratio ordering between

\mu_{c,p}
and
\mu_{s,p}
. In the scalar Rayleigh case, this ordering
reduces to comparing
\bar{\gamma}_{c}
and
\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, as shown next.

IV-B

Scalar Rayleigh-BPSK Classification

We now specialize the curvature criterion to the scalar Rayleigh-fading BPSK model. Recall that
w_{a}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{a}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{a}}
for
a\in\{c,s\}
, where
\bar{\gamma}_{c}=\frac{2P\Omega_{H}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}
and
\bar{\gamma}_{s}=\frac{2P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}
.
For the tilted SNR densities defined in (
31
), their likelihood
ratio is

\frac{\mu_{c,p}(\gamma)}{\mu_{s,p}(\gamma)}=C_{p}\frac{w_{c}(\gamma)}{w_{s}(\gamma)}=C_{p}\frac{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}\exp\!\left[\gamma\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}-\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}\right)\right],

(36)

where
C_{p}>0
is independent of
\gamma
. Hence the MLR ordering between

\mu_{c,p}
and
\mu_{s,p}
is determined solely by the average effective
SNRs
\bar{\gamma}_{c}
and
\bar{\gamma}_{s}
.

Theorem 2
.

For the scalar BPSK-CSCG/Rayleigh model, the single-mode boundary

C_{0}(D)=R(G^{-1}(D))
satisfies:

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}>\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is strictly concave on
the interior interval, and time sharing is unnecessary;

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}<\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is strictly convex on
the interior interval, and time sharing gives a strict gain;

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}=\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is linear.

Proof.

When
\bar{\gamma}_{c}>\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, (
36
) is
strictly increasing in
\gamma
, so
\mu_{c,p}
dominates
\mu_{s,p}
in the
MLR order. Since Lemma
1
shows that

\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
is strictly increasing, Lemma
2

implies

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]>\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)].

By (
32
) and (
29
), this gives

C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)<0
. The case
\bar{\gamma}_{c}<\bar{\gamma}_{s}
is identical with the MLR
ordering reversed, giving
C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)>0
. If

\bar{\gamma}_{c}=\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
w_{c}=w_{s}
, and hence

R(p)+G(p)=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right),\qquad p\in[1/2,1],

so the frontier is linear.
∎

The theorem gives a data-to-pilot interpretation of the symbol-unaware ISAC
frontier. If the communication-side effective SNR dominates, gradually
biasing the BPSK input from the equiprobable data mode toward the
deterministic pilot-like mode is already optimal. If the sensing-side
effective SNR dominates, the biased-BPSK operating points are dominated by
time sharing between the two endpoints; in the strictly convex case, the
true boundary is therefore the chord connecting the data endpoint and the
pilot-like endpoint. Fig.
2
illustrates the three regimes.

Although the SNR ordering determines the sign of
C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)
, the kernel curvature ratio
\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
is bounded for every fixed
p\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Hence, the time-sharing gain grows only modestly with the SNR separation.

80
dB effective-SNR separation.

IV-C

Closed-form criterion for i.i.d. SIMO

Before specializing to the i.i.d. case, we recall how the scalar curvature
criterion extends to SIMO-BPSK. Conditioned on
\bm{H}=\bm{h}
, maximal-ratio
combining reduces the communication channel to a scalar BPSK-AWGN channel
with effective SNR
\Gamma_{c}=2P\|\bm{h}\|^{2}/\sigma^{2}
. Similarly, conditioned on

\bm{S}=\bm{s}
, the sensing-side penalty term
I(X;\bm{V}|\bm{S}=\bm{s})
is the
same scalar kernel evaluated at
\Gamma_{s}=2P\|\bm{s}\|^{2}/\sigma^{2}
. Hence

R(p)=\mathbb{E}[J(p,\Gamma_{c})]

(37)

and

G(p)=C_{s}-\mathbb{E}[J(p,\Gamma_{s})].

(38)

Consequently, all curvature arguments above remain valid after replacing
the exponential densities
w_{c},w_{s}
by the post-combining SNR densities

f_{c},f_{s}
. In particular, if
f_{c}/f_{s}
is nondecreasing, the single-mode frontier
C_{0}(D)
is
concave; if it is nonincreasing, the the single-mode frontier
C_{0}(D)
is convex.

Consider the i.i.d. case. The communication covariance is
R_{c}=\Omega_{c}I_{N_{c}}
. The sensing covariance is
R_{s}=\Omega_{s}I_{N_{s}}
.

Define
\theta_{c}=\frac{2P\Omega_{c}}{\sigma^{2}}
and
\theta_{s}=\frac{2P\Omega_{s}}{\sigma^{2}}
. Then
\Gamma_{c}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(N_{c},\theta_{c})
, and
\Gamma_{s}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(N_{s},\theta_{s})
.

The density ratio can be written as

\frac{f_{c}(\gamma)}{f_{s}(\gamma)}=C\,\gamma^{N_{c}-N_{s}}\exp\!\left[-\gamma\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{c}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{s}}\right)\right],

(39)

where
C>0
is independent of
\gamma
. Taking the derivative of the logarithm yields

\frac{d}{d\gamma}\log\frac{f_{c}(\gamma)}{f_{s}(\gamma)}=\frac{N_{c}-N_{s}}{\gamma}-\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{c}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{s}}\right).

(40)

Since
\frac{f_{c}(\gamma)}{f_{s}(\gamma)}>0
on
(0,\infty)
, the monotonicity of

\frac{f_{c}(\gamma)}{f_{s}(\gamma)}
is equivalent to the sign of

\frac{d}{d\gamma}\log\frac{f_{c}(\gamma)}{f_{s}(\gamma)}
on
(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 3
.

Consider the i.i.d. SIMO-BPSK setting with

R_{c}=\Omega_{c}I_{N_{c}}
and
R_{s}=\Omega_{s}I_{N_{s}}
.
Let
\theta_{c}=\frac{2P\Omega_{c}}{\sigma^{2}}
and

\theta_{s}=\frac{2P\Omega_{s}}{\sigma^{2}}
.

•

If
N_{c}\geq N_{s}
and
\theta_{c}\geq\theta_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is concave; if at least one inequality is strict, it is strictly concave, hence time-sharing is unnecessary.

•

If
N_{c}\leq N_{s}
and
\theta_{c}\leq\theta_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is convex; if at least one inequality is strict, it is strictly convex, hence time-sharing yields a strict gain.

•

If
N_{c}=N_{s}
and
\theta_{c}=\theta_{s}
, then
C_{0}(D)
is linear.

•

Otherwise (i.e.,
N_{c}>N_{s}
and
\theta_{c}<\theta_{s}
, or
N_{c}<N_{s}
and
\theta_{c}>\theta_{s}
), the ratio
f_{c}(\gamma)/f_{s}(\gamma)
is not monotone on
(0,\infty)
;

In the SIMO case, the antenna numbers and per-antenna channel powers jointly determine the ordering of the post-combining SNR distributions.
A larger number of communication antennas or a larger communication-side scale parameter shifts the communication SNR distribution toward larger values, favoring a concave frontier.
Conversely, if the sensing side has both a larger diversity order and a larger scale parameter, the single-mode frontier becomes convex, so the corresponding single-mode region is nonconvex and time sharing with the pilot-like endpoint becomes beneficial.
When these two effects are mixed, the likelihood-ratio ordering may fail to be monotone, and the global curvature can no longer be determined by a simple dominance rule. We next validate the corollary through three i.i.d. SIMO-BPSK examples shown in Fig.
3
.

In Fig.
2
and Fig.
3
, the horizontal span of the sensing coordinate between the deterministic endpoint and the uniform-BPSK endpoint equals the nuisance-symbol information
I(X;V\mid X)
. In the high sensing-SNR regime, this term approaches
H(X)=1
bit for uniform BPSK, reflecting the binary sign ambiguity induced by the unknown transmitted symbol. At moderate sensing SNR, the same loss term is smaller than one bit because the symbol itself is not fully resolvable from the noisy sensing observation.

:

80
dB effective-SNR separation.

V
Conclusion

This paper studies ISAC when the sensing receiver knows the signaling law but does not have access to the realized transmitted symbols. Under this symbol-unaware sensing model, the sensing metric retains the uncertainty induced by the transmit signal, leading to a communication–sensing tradeoff fundamentally different from formulations that condition on the channel input.
For Rayleigh-fading BPSK, we derive an exact global criterion under which the single-mode frontier is strictly concave, strictly convex, or linear, depending on the ordering between the communication-side and sensing-side average effective SNRs.
These results demonstrate that, under symbol-unaware sensing, time sharing is not merely an implementation option but a structural component of the optimal capacity–sensing boundary whenever the single-mode frontier is nonconcave.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition 1

For any code, the decoding error probability depends on the physical
channel only through the marginal law
P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}
. By memorylessness,
this marginal is

P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}(y^{n}|x^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{c}(y_{i}|x_{i}),

(41)

which is the same for
W
and
\widetilde{W}
.

Similarly, the sensing metric is symbol-by-symbol:

D_{n}(f_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(S_{i};V_{i}).

(42)

For each coordinate
i
, the joint law of
(S_{i},V_{i})
induced by the
input marginal
P_{X_{i}}
is

P_{S_{i},V_{i}}(s,v)=P_{S}(s)\sum_{x}P_{X_{i}}(x)W_{s}(v|x,s),

(43)

which depends only on the sensing marginal
W_{s}
. Hence the sensing
value is also the same for
W
and
\widetilde{W}
.

Therefore every code has the same pair of reliability and sensing
performance under the two joint channels. The achievable regions are

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem 1

A. Achievability

Fix an arbitrary pmf
P_{Q}P_{X|Q}
with
\mathcal{Q}=\{1,\ldots,K\}
. We show that every pair
(R,D)
satisfying

R<I(X;Y|Q)
and
D<I(S;V|Q)
is achievable.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
. Choose positive integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{K}
such that
\sum_{q=1}^{K}n_{q}=n
and
\frac{n_{q}}{n}\to P_{Q}(q)
for every
q
. For each
q
, choose an
n_{q}
-type
P_{q,n}
such that
P_{q,n}\to P_{X|Q=q}
.

For each
q
, the constant-composition coding theorem for discrete memoryless channels guarantees a code of blocklength
n_{q}
whose codewords all belong to the type class
T(P_{q,n})
, whose average error probability vanishes as
n_{q}\to\infty
, and whose rate approaches
I_{P_{q,n}}(X;Y)
. Here

T(P_{q,n})=\{x^{n_{q}}\in\mathcal{X}^{n_{q}}:\hat{P}_{x^{n_{q}}}=P_{q,n}\}.

(44)

Hence, for all sufficiently large
n_{q}
, there exists a subcode with message size
M_{q,n}
satisfying

\bar{P}_{e,q}^{(n_{q})}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{K}

(45)

and

\frac{1}{n_{q}}\log M_{q,n}\geq I_{P_{q,n}}(X;Y)-\delta.

(46)

We concatenate these
K
subcodes to form an overall length-
n
code. The message set is
\mathcal{W}_{n}=\prod_{q=1}^{K}[1:M_{q,n}]
, and for
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{K})\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
, the encoder outputs

f_{n}(w)=\bigl(f_{1,n}(w_{1}),\ldots,f_{K,n}(w_{K})\bigr).

(47)

The decoder operates blockwise, applying the
q
-th subdecoder to the
q
-th received subblock.

Let
E_{q}=\{\hat{w}_{q}\neq w_{q}\}
. Since
\{\hat{w}\neq w\}\subseteq\cup_{q=1}^{K}E_{q}
,

P_{e}^{(n)}\leq\sum_{q=1}^{K}\bar{P}_{e,q}^{(n_{q})}\leq\epsilon.

(48)

Therefore, the overall average error probability tends to zero.

We next verify the communication rate. Since
M_{n}=\prod_{q=1}^{K}M_{q,n}
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}=\sum_{q=1}^{K}\frac{n_{q}}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{n_{q}}\log M_{q,n}.

(49)

Combining (
46
) and (
49
),

\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}\geq\sum_{q=1}^{K}\frac{n_{q}}{n}\bigl(I_{P_{q,n}}(X;Y)-\delta\bigr).

(50)

Letting
n\to\infty
and using the continuity of mutual information in the input distribution,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{q=1}^{K}P_{Q}(q)\,I(X;Y|Q=q)-\delta

(51)

\displaystyle=I(X;Y|Q)-\delta.

Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary, every
R<I(X;Y|Q)
is achievable.

It remains to verify the sensing requirement. Let
\mathcal{I}_{q}
denote the coordinate set of the
q
-th subblock. For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}(x)=\frac{1}{M_{n}}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\mathbf{1}\{f_{n}(w)_{i}=x\}.

(52)

Since every
q
-th subblock codeword lies in
T(P_{q,n})
,

\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\mathbf{1}\{f_{n}(w)_{i}=x\}=P_{q,n}(x)

(53)

for every
w
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}=P_{q,n}.

(54)

Lemma 3
.

For a fixed state distribution
P_{S}
and sensing channel
W_{s}(v|x,s)
, define

D(P_{X})\triangleq I(S;V),

(55)

where the joint law of
(S,V)
induced by the input distribution
P_{X}
is

P_{S,V}(s,v)=P_{S}(s)\sum_{x}P_{X}(x)W_{s}(v|x,s).

(56)

Then
D(P_{X})
is convex in
P_{X}
. Namely, for any two input distributions
P_{X}^{(1)}
and
P_{X}^{(2)}
, and any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

D\!\left(\lambda P_{X}^{(1)}+(1-\lambda)P_{X}^{(2)}\right)\leq\lambda D\!\left(P_{X}^{(1)}\right)+(1-\lambda)D\!\left(P_{X}^{(2)}\right).

(57)

Proof.

For each fixed
s
, the conditional distribution of
V
given
S=s
is

P_{V|S}(v|s)=\sum_{x}P_{X}(x)W_{s}(v|x,s),

(58)

which is affine in
P_{X}
. It follows that both
P_{S,V}
and the induced marginal
P_{V}
depend affinely on
P_{X}
. On the other hand,

I(S;V)=D\!\left(P_{S,V}\,\middle\|\,P_{S}P_{V}\right),

(59)

and the relative entropy is jointly convex in its two arguments. Therefore,
I(S;V)
is convex as a function of
P_{X}
, which proves the claim.
∎

We now apply the above convexity result to the empirical input distributions within each index set
\mathcal{I}_{q}
. Since
D(P_{X})
is convex in
P_{X}
, Jensen’s inequality yields

\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}D\bigl(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\bigr)\geq D\!\left(\frac{1}{n_{q}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\right)=D(P_{q,n}).

(60)

That is, the average sensing value over the positions in
\mathcal{I}_{q}
is lower bounded by the sensing value induced by the averaged input distribution
P_{q,n}
.

Summing (
60
) over all
q\in[1:K]
, and using the decomposition of the blockwise sensing metric, we obtain

D_{n}(f_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D\bigl(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\bigr)\geq\sum_{q=1}^{K}\frac{n_{q}}{n}\,D(P_{q,n}).

(61)

Finally, by the convergence
n_{q}/n\to P_{Q}(q)
and
P_{q,n}\to P_{X|Q=q}
as
n\to\infty
, together with the continuity of mutual information in the input distribution for finite alphabets, we obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(f_{n})\geq\sum_{q=1}^{K}P_{Q}(q)\,D(P_{X|Q=q})=I(S;V|Q).

(62)

Therefore, any sensing level strictly below
I(S;V|Q)
is achievable. In particular, every
D<I(S;V|Q)
can be attained.

Since
P_{Q}P_{X|Q}
was arbitrary, the achievability of (
7
) follows.

B. Converse

Suppose
(R,D)
is achievable. Then there exists a sequence of
(2^{nR_{n}},n)
codes such that
R_{n}\to R
,
P_{e}^{(n)}\to 0
, and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(f_{n})\geq D
. Let
W
be uniform on
[1:2^{nR_{n}}]
, and let
X^{n}=f_{n}(W)
.

By Fano’s inequality, there exists
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
such that

R_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}I(W;Y^{n})+\epsilon_{n}.

(63)

Since
W\to X^{n}\to Y^{n}
,

I(W;Y^{n})\leq I(X^{n};Y^{n}).

(64)

Hence,

R_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}I(X^{n};Y^{n})+\epsilon_{n}.

(65)

By the chain rule,

I(X^{n};Y^{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X^{n};Y_{i}|Y^{i-1}).

(66)

Because the communication channel is memoryless,

I(X^{n};Y_{i}|Y^{i-1})\leq I(X_{i};Y_{i}).

(67)

Therefore,

R_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y_{i})+\epsilon_{n}.

(68)

Introduce a time-sharing index
T
, uniform on
[1:n]
and independent of everything else. Define
Q_{n}=T
and
(X_{n},S_{n},Y_{n},V_{n})=(X_{T},S_{T},Y_{T},V_{T})
. Then

I(X_{n};Y_{n}|Q_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y_{i}).

(69)

Substituting (
69
) into (
68
) gives

R_{n}\leq I(X_{n};Y_{n}|Q_{n})+\epsilon_{n}.

(70)

For the sensing term, by definition of
D(\cdot)
,

I(S_{i};V_{i})=D\bigl(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\bigr)

(71)

for each
i
. Hence,

\displaystyle I(S_{n};V_{n}|Q_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(S_{i};V_{i})

(72)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D\bigl(P_{X_{i}}^{(n)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=D_{n}(f_{n}).

Define
\mathcal{A}=\left\{\bigl(R(P_{X}),D(P_{X})\bigr):P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.
Since
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
is compact and connected, and both
R(P_{X})
and
D(P_{X})
are continuous in
P_{X}
, the set
\mathcal{A}
is compact and connected. Moreover, for every
n
, the pair
\bigl(I(X_{n};Y_{n}|Q_{n}),I(S_{n};V_{n}|Q_{n})\bigr)
belongs to
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
.

Because
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
is compact, there exists a subsequence along which

\bigl(I(X_{n};Y_{n}|Q_{n}),I(S_{n};V_{n}|Q_{n})\bigr)\to(\bar{R},\bar{D})\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A}).

(73)

Using (
70
), together with
R_{n}\to R
and
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
, yields

R\leq\bar{R}.

(74)

Likewise, (
72
) and the assumption
\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(f_{n})\geq D
imply

D\leq\bar{D}.

(75)

Finally, by the Fenchel–Eggleston theorem, every point in
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{A})
can be represented as a convex combination of at most two points in
\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, there exists an auxiliary random variable
Q
with
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq 2
such that

\displaystyle I(X;Y|Q)=\bar{R},

(76)

\displaystyle I(S;V|Q)=\bar{D}.

(77)

Combining this representation with (
74
) and (
75
), we obtain

\displaystyle R\leq I(X;Y|Q),

(78)

\displaystyle D\leq I(S;V|Q).

(79)

This proves the converse and completes the proof of (
7
).

Appendix C
Derivation of the Scalar BPSK Frontier

This appendix gives the details behind the scalar BPSK expressions used in Section IV-A.

C-A

Communication Mutual Information

For the scalar communication channel
Y=HX+N_{1}
with
X=\sqrt{P}B
,
B\in\{+1,-1\}
,
H\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Omega_{H})
, and
N_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{1}^{2})
, conditioning on
H=h
yields an equivalent scalar BPSK-AWGN channel.

The effective instantaneous SNR is
\gamma=\frac{2P|h|^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}
. The factor
2
appears because the complex Gaussian noise is represented through an equivalent real scalar BPSK-AWGN kernel with unit real noise variance.

Let
J(p,\gamma)\triangleq I(B;\sqrt{\gamma}B+Z)
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. Then

I(X;Y|H=h)=J\left(p,\frac{2P|h|^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right).

(80)

Averaging over
H
gives

R(p)=I(X;Y|H)=\mathbb{E}_{H}\left[J\left(p,\frac{2P|H|^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\right].

(81)

Since
H\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Omega_{H})
, the random variable
|H|^{2}
is exponentially distributed with density

f_{|H|^{2}}(t)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{H}}e^{-t/\Omega_{H}},\qquad t\geq 0.

(82)

Using the change of variables
\gamma=\frac{2Pt}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}
,
t=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2P}\gamma
, and
dt=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2P}d\gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle R(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}J\left(p,\frac{2Pt}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{\Omega_{H}}e^{-t/\Omega_{H}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2P\Omega_{H}}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2P\Omega_{H}}\gamma}d\gamma.

(83)

Define
\bar{\gamma}_{c}\triangleq\frac{2P\Omega_{H}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}
, and
w_{c}(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{c}}
. Then

R(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma.

(84)

C-B

Sensing Mutual Information

For the sensing channel

V=SX+N_{2},

(85)

where
S\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Omega_{S})
and
N_{2}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{2}^{2})
, the chain rule gives

I(S;V)=I(S;V|X)+I(X;V)-I(X;V|S).

(86)

We first show that
I(X;V)=0
. Since
S
is zero-mean circularly symmetric complex Gaussian,

S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}-S.

(87)

Therefore,

V|X=+\sqrt{P}=\sqrt{P}S+N_{2}

(88)

and

V|X=-\sqrt{P}=-\sqrt{P}S+N_{2}

(89)

have the same distribution. Hence the marginal law of
V
is independent of
X
, and

I(X;V)=0.

(90)

Next, conditioned on
X=\pm\sqrt{P}
, the sensing observation is a scalar Gaussian channel for
S
:

V=\pm\sqrt{P}S+N_{2}.

(91)

Thus

I(S;V|X)=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right).

(92)

Finally, conditioned on
S=s
, the channel from
X
to
V
is again an equivalent BPSK-AWGN channel with instantaneous effective SNR
\gamma=\frac{2P|s|^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}
. Therefore,

I(X;V|S=s)=J\left(p,\frac{2P|s|^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right).

(93)

Averaging over
S
yields

I(X;V|S)=\mathbb{E}_{S}\left[J\left(p,\frac{2P|S|^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)\right].

(94)

Since
|S|^{2}
is exponentially distributed with mean
\Omega_{S}
, the same change of variables gives

I(X;V|S)=\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(95)

where
\bar{\gamma}_{s}\triangleq\frac{2P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}
,
w_{s}(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{s}}
.
Substituting these terms into (
86
), we obtain

G(p)=I(S;V)=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P\Omega_{S}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}J(p,\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma.

(96)

C-C

Curvature of the Single-mode Boundary

Because
J(p,\gamma)
is nonincreasing in
p
over
p\in[1/2,1]
, the communication mutual information decreases with
p
, while the symbol-unaware sensing mutual information increases with
p
:

R^{\prime}(p)\leq 0,\qquad G^{\prime}(p)\geq 0.

(97)

Therefore the single-mode boundary can be written as

C_{0}(D)=R(G^{-1}(D)),\qquad D=G(p).

(98)

Differentiating with respect to
D
gives

C_{0}^{\prime}(D)=\frac{dR(p)}{dG(p)}=\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}.

(99)

Define
\eta(p)\triangleq-\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}
. Then
C_{0}^{\prime}(D)=-\eta(p)
. Differentiating once more with respect to
D
gives

C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)=-\frac{d\eta(p)}{dD}=-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}.

(100)

Hence the sign of
C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)
is opposite to the sign of
\eta^{\prime}(p)
.

We now compute
\eta^{\prime}(p)
. By definition,

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dp}\left(\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(p)G^{\prime}(p)-R^{\prime}(p)G^{\prime\prime}(p)}{[G^{\prime}(p)]^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{R^{\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}\left[\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(p)}{R^{\prime}(p)}-\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta(p)\left[\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(p)}{R^{\prime}(p)}-\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}\right].

(101)

Next, observe that

R^{\prime}(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{p}(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(102)

and

R^{\prime\prime}(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{pp}(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma.

(103)

Similarly,

G^{\prime}(p)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{p}(p,\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(104)

and

G^{\prime\prime}(p)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{pp}(p,\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma.

(105)

For
p\in(1/2,1)
, define
a_{p}(\gamma)\triangleq-J_{p}(p,\gamma)>0
and
\kappa_{p}(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{J_{pp}(p,\gamma)}{J_{p}(p,\gamma)}.
Also define

A_{c}(p)\triangleq\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{p}(\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma,\quad A_{s}(p)\triangleq\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{p}(\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)d\gamma.

Then

\mu_{c,p}(\gamma)=\frac{a_{p}(\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)}{A_{c}(p)},

(106)

and

\mu_{s,p}(\gamma)=\frac{a_{p}(\gamma)w_{s}(\gamma)}{A_{s}(p)}

(107)

are probability densities on
(0,\infty)
.

Using these definitions,

\displaystyle\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(p)}{R^{\prime}(p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{pp}(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma}{\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{p}(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)d\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{pp}(p,\gamma)}{J_{p}(p,\gamma)}\frac{-J_{p}(p,\gamma)w_{c}(\gamma)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}-J_{p}(p,t)w_{c}(t)dt}d\gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{p}(\gamma)\mu_{c,p}(\gamma)d\gamma

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)].

(108)

Likewise,

\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(p)}{G^{\prime}(p)}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)].

(109)

Substituting into (
101
) yields

\eta^{\prime}(p)=\eta(p)\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,p}}[\kappa_{p}(\Gamma)]\right).

(110)

C-D

Likelihood-Ratio Reduction in the Rayleigh Case

The tilted densities satisfy

\frac{\mu_{c,p}(\gamma)}{\mu_{s,p}(\gamma)}=\frac{A_{s}(p)}{A_{c}(p)}\frac{w_{c}(\gamma)}{w_{s}(\gamma)}.

(111)

Since
A_{s}(p)/A_{c}(p)>0
is independent of
\gamma
, the monotonicity of
\mu_{c,p}/\mu_{s,p}
is the same as that of
w_{c}/w_{s}
.

For the Rayleigh fading case,

\displaystyle\frac{w_{c}(\gamma)}{w_{s}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{c}}}{\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}\exp\left[\gamma\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{s}}-\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}_{c}}\right)\right].

(112)

Therefore,

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}>\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
w_{c}(\gamma)/w_{s}(\gamma)
is strictly increasing in
\gamma
;

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}<\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
w_{c}(\gamma)/w_{s}(\gamma)
is strictly decreasing in
\gamma
;

•

if
\bar{\gamma}_{c}=\bar{\gamma}_{s}
, then
w_{c}=w_{s}
.

Combining this likelihood-ratio ordering with the monotonicity of
\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
proves the scalar BPSK classification theorem in Section IV-B.

Appendix D
Proof of Lemma 2

For convenience, all logarithms in this appendix are natural logarithms.

Set
m=2p-1\in(0,1)
, so that
p=(1+m)/2
. Writing the same kernel as
J(m,\gamma)
, the chain rule gives
J_{p}=2J_{m}
and
J_{pp}=4J_{mm}
. Hence

\kappa_{p}(\gamma)=2\,\frac{-J_{mm}(m,\gamma)}{-J_{m}(m,\gamma)}.

(113)

Thus it suffices to prove that

\gamma\mapsto\frac{-J_{mm}(m,\gamma)}{-J_{m}(m,\gamma)}

(114)

is strictly increasing.

The proof has three steps. First, we rewrite the derivatives of
J
as expectations of two scalar functions of a random variable
U_{\gamma}\in(0,1)
. Second, we show that the family
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
is strictly increasing in the monotone likelihood-ratio (MLR) order. Third, we verify that the relevant likelihood-ratio functional is strictly increasing in
U_{\gamma}
, which implies the desired monotonicity.

D-A

Derivative representation

Consider (
20
), let

c_{\gamma}(y)\triangleq\phi(y)e^{-\gamma/2}\cosh(\sqrt{\gamma}\,y),

(115)

t_{\gamma}(y)\triangleq\tanh(\sqrt{\gamma}\,y),

(116)

where
\phi(y)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-y^{2}/2}
. A direct calculation shows that the output density can be factorized as

q_{m,\gamma}(y)=c_{\gamma}(y)\bigl(1+mt_{\gamma}(y)\bigr).

(117)

Moreover,
c_{\gamma}
is even,
t_{\gamma}
is odd, and
c_{\gamma}=q_{0,\gamma}
is a probability density.

Since
J(m,\gamma)=h(Y)-h(Y|B)
and
h(Y|B)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e)
, we have

J(m,\gamma)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{m,\gamma}(y)\log q_{m,\gamma}(y)\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e).

(118)

Substituting (
117
) into (
118
) and using the parity of
c_{\gamma}
,
t_{\gamma}
, and
\log c_{\gamma}
, we obtain

J(m,\gamma)=C_{\gamma}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}c_{\gamma}(y)\bigl(1+mt_{\gamma}(y)\bigr)\log\bigl(1+mt_{\gamma}(y)\bigr)\,dy,

(119)

where
C_{\gamma}
depends only on
\gamma
.

Now fix an arbitrary compact interval
K=[m_{0},m_{1}]\subset(0,1)
. Since
t_{\gamma}(y)\in(-1,1)
for all
y
, one has
1+mt_{\gamma}(y)\in[1-m_{1},1+m_{1}]
for every
m\in K
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\Big[(1+mt_{\gamma}(y))\log(1+mt_{\gamma}(y))\Big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,K},

(120)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial m^{2}}\Big[(1+mt_{\gamma}(y))\log(1+mt_{\gamma}(y))\Big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2,K},

(121)

for finite constants
C_{1,K},C_{2,K}
independent of
y
. Because
c_{\gamma}
is a probability density, the bounds in (
120
)–(
121
) are integrable against
c_{\gamma}(y)\,dy
. Hence the dominated convergence theorem allows differentiation under the integral sign in (
119
), yielding

\displaystyle J_{m}(m,\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}c_{\gamma}(y)t_{\gamma}(y)\log\bigl(1+mt_{\gamma}(y)\bigr)\,dy,

(122)

\displaystyle J_{mm}(m,\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}c_{\gamma}(y)\frac{t_{\gamma}(y)^{2}}{1+mt_{\gamma}(y)}\,dy.

(123)

Let
Y_{0}\sim c_{\gamma}
, and define
T_{\gamma}\triangleq t_{\gamma}(Y_{0})
and
U_{\gamma}\triangleq|T_{\gamma}|
. Because
c_{\gamma}
is even and
t_{\gamma}
is odd,
T_{\gamma}
is symmetric about zero. Symmetrizing (
122
) and (
123
) gives

\displaystyle-J_{m}(m,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[U_{\gamma}\,\operatorname{artanh}(mU_{\gamma})\right],

(124)

\displaystyle-J_{mm}(m,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{U_{\gamma}^{2}}{1-m^{2}U_{\gamma}^{2}}\right].

(125)

Define

\displaystyle A_{m}(u)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{u^{2}}{1-m^{2}u^{2}},

(126)

\displaystyle B_{m}(u)

\displaystyle\triangleq u\,\operatorname{artanh}(mu),\qquad u\in(0,1).

(127)

Then (
113
), (
124
), and (
125
) imply

\kappa_{p}(\gamma)=2\,\frac{\mathbb{E}[A_{m}(U_{\gamma})]}{\mathbb{E}[B_{m}(U_{\gamma})]}.

(128)

D-B

MLR monotonicity of the transformed variable

Since
c_{\gamma}=q_{0,\gamma}
, the random variable
Y_{0}
has the same law as the channel output under equiprobable input. Hence

Y_{0}\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{\gamma}\,B+Z,\qquad\Pr(B=\pm 1)=\frac{1}{2}.

(129)

Multiplying by
\sqrt{\gamma}
and using the symmetry of
B
and
Z
yields

U_{\gamma}=\bigl|\tanh(\sqrt{\gamma}\,Y_{0})\bigr|\overset{d}{=}\tanh\bigl(|\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma}Z|\bigr).

(130)

Define
V_{\gamma}\triangleq|\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma}Z|
, so that
U_{\gamma}=\tanh(V_{\gamma})
.

For
v\geq 0
, the density of
V_{\gamma}
is

\displaystyle f_{V_{\gamma}}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\gamma}}\left(e^{-(v-\gamma)^{2}/(2\gamma)}+e^{-(v+\gamma)^{2}/(2\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cosh v}{\sqrt{2\pi\gamma}}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{v^{2}}{2\gamma}}.

(131)

Therefore, for any
\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}>0
,

\frac{f_{V_{\gamma_{2}}}(v)}{f_{V_{\gamma_{1}}}(v)}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}}e^{\left(-\frac{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}{2}\right)}e^{\left[\frac{v^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}}\right)\right]}.

(132)

Because
\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}}>0
, the right-hand side of (
132
) is strictly increasing in
v
. Thus the family
\{V_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
is strictly increasing in the MLR order.

Next, note that
u=\tanh v
is a strictly increasing
C^{1}
bijection from
(0,\infty)
onto
(0,1)
, with inverse
v=\operatorname{artanh}u
. By change of variables,

f_{U_{\gamma}}(u)=\frac{f_{V_{\gamma}}(\operatorname{artanh}u)}{1-u^{2}},\qquad 0<u<1.

(133)

Hence, for
\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}
,

\frac{f_{U_{\gamma_{2}}}(u)}{f_{U_{\gamma_{1}}}(u)}=\frac{f_{V_{\gamma_{2}}}(\operatorname{artanh}u)}{f_{V_{\gamma_{1}}}(\operatorname{artanh}u)},

(134)

which is strictly increasing in
u
because both the likelihood ratio in (
132
) and the map
u\mapsto\operatorname{artanh}u
are strictly increasing. Therefore,
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
is also strictly increasing in the MLR order.

D-C

Monotonicity of the ratio functional

For
u\in(0,1)
, consider the ratio

\psi(u)\triangleq\frac{A_{m}(u)}{B_{m}(u)}=\frac{u}{(1-m^{2}u^{2})\operatorname{artanh}(mu)}.

(135)

Let
x=mu\in(0,1)
and define

h(x)\triangleq\frac{x}{(1-x^{2})\operatorname{artanh}x}.

(136)

Then
\psi(u)=m^{-1}h(mu)
. Hence it is enough to prove that
h
is strictly increasing on
(0,1)
. Differentiating
\log h(x)
gives

\frac{h^{\prime}(x)}{h(x)}=\frac{(1+x^{2})\operatorname{artanh}x-x}{x(1-x^{2})\operatorname{artanh}x}.

(137)

Let

g(x)\triangleq(1+x^{2})\operatorname{artanh}x-x.

(138)

Since
g(0)=0
and

g^{\prime}(x)=2x\operatorname{artanh}x+\frac{2x^{2}}{1-x^{2}}>0,\qquad x\in(0,1),

(139)

we have
g(x)>0
for all
x\in(0,1)
. By (
137
), this implies
h^{\prime}(x)>0
on
(0,1)
, and therefore
\psi(u)
is strictly increasing on
(0,1)
.

Now define the
B_{m}
-tilted density

\tilde{f}_{\gamma}(u)\triangleq\frac{B_{m}(u)f_{U_{\gamma}}(u)}{\int_{0}^{1}B_{m}(t)f_{U_{\gamma}}(t)\,dt},\qquad 0<u<1.

(140)

Because
B_{m}(u)>0
on
(0,1)
, for any
\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}
we have

\frac{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)}{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)}=C_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\frac{f_{U_{\gamma_{2}}}(u)}{f_{U_{\gamma_{1}}}(u)},

(141)

where
C_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}>0
does not depend on
u
. Thus the family
\{\tilde{f}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
is also strictly increasing in the MLR order.

Fix
\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}
and write

r(u)\triangleq\frac{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)}{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)}.

(142)

Since

is strictly increasing and
\int_{0}^{1}r(u)\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)\,du=1
, there exists a unique

such that

\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)-\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)\begin{cases}<0,&0<u<u_{\star},\\
=0,&u=u_{\star},\\

\psi

\bigl(\psi(u)-\psi(u_{\star})\bigr)\bigl(\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)-\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)\bigr)

(144)

and strictly positive except at

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\psi}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}}[\psi(U)]-\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}}[\psi(U)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\psi(u)\bigl(\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)-\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)\bigr)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\psi(u)-\psi(u_{\star})\bigr)\bigl(\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{2}}(u)-\tilde{f}_{\gamma_{1}}(u)\bigr)\,du

(145)

Hence
\gamma\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}[\psi(U)]

(0,\infty)
.

Finally, by the definition of the tilted density in (

\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}\!\left[\frac{A_{m}(U)}{B_{m}(U)}\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[A_{m}(U_{\gamma})]}{\mathbb{E}[B_{m}(U_{\gamma})]}.

(146)

is strictly increasing. By (

is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma 3

Define

\Delta\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{q_{1}}[\phi(X)]-\mathbb{E}_{q_{2}}[\phi(X)].

(148)

Since
q_{1}=rq_{2}
, we have

\Delta=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)\bigl(r(x)-1\bigr)q_{2}(x)\,dx.

(149)

Moreover, because both
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are probability densities,

\int_{\mathcal{X}}r(x)q_{2}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathcal{X}}q_{1}(x)\,dx=1.

(150)

Hence,

\Delta=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)r(x)q_{2}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)q_{2}(x)\,dx\int_{\mathcal{X}}r(x)q_{2}(x)\,dx.

(151)

If
X\sim q_{2}
, then

\Delta=\operatorname{Cov}_{q_{2}}\bigl(\phi(X),r(X)\bigr).

(152)

Now let
X
and
Y
be i.i.d. random variables with density
q_{2}
. Using the symmetric representation of covariance, we obtain

2\,\operatorname{Cov}_{q_{2}}\bigl(\phi(X),r(X)\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\phi(X)-\phi(Y))(r(X)-r(Y))\right].

(153)

Therefore,

\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\phi(X)-\phi(Y))(r(X)-r(Y))\right].

(154)

Equivalently,

\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(r(x)-r(y))\,q_{2}(x)q_{2}(y)\,dx\,dy.

(155)

Since both
\phi
and
r
are nondecreasing, for every
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,

(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(r(x)-r(y))\geq 0.

(156)

Thus the integrand is everywhere nonnegative, which implies
\Delta\geq 0
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}_{q_{1}}[\phi(X)]\geq\mathbb{E}_{q_{2}}[\phi(X)].

(157)

If, in addition, both
\phi
and
r
are strictly increasing, then for every
x\neq y
,

(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(r(x)-r(y))>0.

(158)

Since
X
and
Y
both admit the density
q_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(X=Y)=0.

(159)

Therefore,

(\phi(X)-\phi(Y))(r(X)-r(Y))>0\qquad\text{almost surely},

(160)

and hence

\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\phi(X)-\phi(Y))(r(X)-r(Y))\right]>0.

(161)

It follows that

\mathbb{E}_{q_{1}}[\phi(X)]>\mathbb{E}_{q_{2}}[\phi(X)].

(162)

This appendix describes how the curvature values reported in the numerical examples are computed.
The discussion also clarifies why a large SNR separation does not necessarily produce a proportionally large boundary deformation.

Recall that the single-mode frontier is parameterized as (
26
)-(
29
).
Thus, the sign of
C_{0}^{\prime\prime}(D)
determines whether the raw frontier is locally concave or convex.

To compare curvature values across different SNR settings, we use normalized coordinates

x(p)=\frac{G(p)-G(1/2)}{G(1)-G(1/2)}=1-\frac{A_{s}(p)}{A_{s}(1/2)},

(163)

and

y(p)=\frac{R(p)}{R(1/2)}=\frac{A_{c}(p)}{A_{c}(1/2)}.

(164)

The normalized signed geometric curvature is then computed as

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(p)=\frac{x^{\prime}(p)y^{\prime\prime}(p)-y^{\prime}(p)x^{\prime\prime}(p)}{\left([x^{\prime}(p)]^{2}+[y^{\prime}(p)]^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

(165)

Here

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{A_{s}^{\prime}(p)}{A_{s}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)},

(166)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{A_{s}^{\prime\prime}(p)}{A_{s}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)},

(167)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{c}^{\prime}(p)}{A_{c}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)},

(168)

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{c}^{\prime\prime}(p)}{A_{c}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

(169)

The derivatives are obtained by differentiating under the expectation:

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[J_{p}(p,\Gamma_{i})],

(170)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\prime\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[J_{pp}(p,\Gamma_{i})].

(171)

For numerical integration, the Gaussian expectation inside
J(p,\gamma)
,
J_{p}(p,\gamma)
, and
J_{pp}(p,\gamma)
is evaluated using Gauss-Hermite quadrature. The outer expectation over
\Gamma_{i}
is evaluated using Gauss-Laguerre quadrature in the scalar Rayleigh case and generalized Gauss-Laguerre quadrature in the i.i.d. SIMO case. Specifically, for the scalar exponential case,

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(r)}(p)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial p^{r}}J(p,\Gamma_{i})\right]

(172)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial p^{r}}J(p,\bar{\gamma}_{i}t_{\ell}),\qquad r=0,1,2,

where
\{t_{\ell},w_{\ell}\}
are the standard Gauss-Laguerre nodes and weights. For the i.i.d. SIMO case,

A_{i}^{(r)}(p)\approx\frac{1}{\Gamma(N_{i})}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}^{(N_{i}-1)}\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial p^{r}}J(p,\theta_{i}t_{\ell}),\qquad r=0,1,2,

(173)

where
\{t_{\ell},w_{\ell}^{(N_{i}-1)}\}
are the generalized Gauss-Laguerre nodes and weights associated with the weight
t^{N_{i}-1}e^{-t}
.

In addition to local curvature, we also report the normalized endpoint chord deviation. In the normalized coordinates
(x,y)

y_{\rm TS}(x)=1-x.

(174)

Therefore, the normalized endpoint time-sharing gain is

\Delta_{\rm TS,n}=\max_{p\in[1/2,1]}\left[1-x(p)-y(p)\right]_{+},

(175)

where
[a]_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
. We also define the normalized absolute chord deviation

\Delta_{\rm ch,n}=\max_{p\in[1/2,1]}\left|1-x(p)-y(p)\right|.

(176)

The quantity
\Delta_{\rm TS,n}
measures the actual gain from endpoint time sharing, whereas
\Delta_{\rm ch,n}
measures the global deviation from the endpoint chord irrespective of whether the frontier is concave or convex.

gives representative numerical values. The curvature maximum is computed over

The table shows that the sign of the reported signed curvature follows the analytical concavity criterion, but the normalized curvature and the normalized chord deviation remain moderate. This explains why, even when time sharing is theoretically necessary in the convex cases, the practical gain over a properly selected single-mode input can be limited.

This behavior can be partially understood from the boundedness of the kernel curvature ratio (
30
). For every fixed
p\in(1/2,1)
,
\kappa_{p}(\gamma)
is strictly increasing in
\gamma
, but it is bounded between its low-SNR and high-SNR limits:

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow 0}\kappa_{p}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{2p-1},

(177)

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\kappa_{p}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(1-p)\ln\frac{p}{1-p}}.

(178)

Hence, for
\gamma>0
,

\frac{2}{2p-1}\leq\kappa_{p}(\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{p(1-p)\ln\frac{p}{1-p}}.

(179)
