 In non-degenerate integrable Hamiltonian systems, invariant tori can be parameterized equiv-
 alently by action variables or by their fundamental frequencies. We introduce an invariant-flow
 formulation, consistent with the geometric structure underlying the Liouville-Arnold theorem, for
 extracting fundamental frequencies associated with invariant tori of integrable Hamiltonian systems.
 By treating invariants as generators of commuting Hamiltonian flows, the frequencies are obtained
 from time-of-flight parameters along these flows, providing a direct alternative to action-angle con-
 structions and spectral methods based on long time series. The approach yields an explicit numerical
 procedure that extends naturally to systems with multiple degrees of freedom. Its effectiveness is
 demonstrated using the McMillan map, where high accuracy is achieved. More broadly, the proposed
 framework provides an invariant-centered viewpoint for studying near-integrable nonlinear dynam-
 ics, where high-quality approximate invariants serve as the primary objects from which dynamical
 information, including frequencies, can be recovered.

 rate and robust. Near the boundary of regular motion,
 the quality of the recovered frequencies depends on the
 quality of the approximate invariants and may therefore
 provide a quantitative diagnostic of non-integrability. In
 contrast, for strongly chaotic trajectories or unstable pe-
 riodic orbits, where invariant-torus structures no longer
 exist, the invariant-flow reconstruction is generally un-
 able to reproduce the one-turn map with a small mis-
 match. The resulting lack of convergence may therefore
 serve as an indicator of chaotic behavior. Similar ideas
 may also be extended to periodically driven systems by
 considering the corresponding stroboscopic map or the
 associated autonomous formulation in an extended phase
 space. A systematic study of these extensions is beyond
 the scope of the present work and will be the subject of
 future investigation.
 An additional advantage of the invariant-flow formu-
 lation is that it does not require analytic expressions for
 the invariants themselves. The method relies only on
 the evaluation of the Hamiltonian vector fields generated
 by the invariants, [z, Ki ], which depend on derivatives of
 the invariants with respect to phase-space coordinates.
 This makes the approach naturally compatible with data-
 driven or neural-network-based representations of invari-
 ants, where gradients are readily available through auto-
 matic differentiation.
