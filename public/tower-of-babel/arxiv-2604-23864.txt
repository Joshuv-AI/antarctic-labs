Bourgain’s method for K K -closedness in the semicommmutative setting
Hugues Moyart

Abstract
. In the early 1990s, J.Bourgain proved a general result K K -closedness result in the context of classical harmonic analysis. In this paper, we extend Bourgain’s method to the semicommutative setting, making use of the recent semicommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition introduced by L.Cadilhac, JM.Conde-Alonso and J.Parcet. As an application, we recover Pisier’s result about K K -closedness of noncommutative Hardy spaces on the torus, and we also establish new interpolation results for noncommutative Sobolev spaces on the torus.

Introduction

The paper is motivated by Bourgain’s approach to Jones’ theorem in the context of interpolation of Hardy spaces on the torus. Let us review the results obtained in this context. Let
{\mathbb{T}}
denote the unit circle. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
H_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
denote the associated analytic Hardy space. Then, Jones established in
[
14
]
that there is a universal constant
C
such that for every
f\in H_{1}({\mathbb{T}})+H_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, and
t>0
, we have

(0.1)

K_{t}(f,H_{1}({\mathbb{T}}),H_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))\leq CK_{t}(f,L_{1}({\mathbb{T}}),L_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))

where
K
refers to Peetre’s
K
-functional that is used to define real interpolation spaces. According to the terminology introduced by G.Pisier in
[
21
]
, one can reformulate Jones’ theorem by saying that the subcouple
(H_{1}({\mathbb{T}}),H_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))
is
K
-closed
(see Part 1) in the compatible couple
(L_{1}({\mathbb{T}}),L_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))
, with a universal constant. This
K
-closedness result completely characterizes the properties of analytic Hardy spaces under the real interpolation method. In particular, it implies that, if
0<\theta<1
then

(0.2)

(H_{1}({\mathbb{T}}),H_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))_{\theta,p_{\theta},K}=H_{p_{\theta}}({\mathbb{T}})

with equivalent norms, where
1/p_{\theta}=1-\theta
, and where the notation on the right-hand side refers to the real interpolation method. In
[
3
]
, using the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition—the fundamental tool of harmonic analysis—and starting from a projection defined by a singular kernel satisfying the standard conditions, Bourgain proved a
K
-closedness result for the compatible couple
(H_{1},H_{2})
. By applying this result to the Riesz projection and its complement projection, he was then able to recover (
0.2
). As highlighted by SV.Kislyakov and Q.Xu in
[
17
]
, from Bourgain’s approach one can actually deduce (
0.1
). Bourgain’s approach in this commutative setting later received further development in a number of works on interpolation of abstract Hardy-type spaces, for instance in the recent survey
[
22
]
.

This paper is devoted to the noncommutative extension of Bourgain’s method. It essentially relies on the recent noncommutative generalization of the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition introduced by J. Parcet, J.M. Conde Alonso and L. Cadilhac in
[
5
,
4
]
in the context of Euclidean spaces. In view of applying this method in other contexts (such as the torus), our results are stated in the framework of spaces of homogeneous type (and more precisely spaces of homogeneous type that satisfy an Ahlfors-type regularity condition). The details about the construction of the noncommutative Calderón–Zygmund decomposition are given in Part 4. The mathematical formalism of this article is that of abstract Hardy spaces, introduced in
[
19
]
. This framework is briefly described below. See Part 1 for details on the mathematical background.

If
M
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra,
P
is any projection on
L_{2}(M)
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define
H_{p}(P)
to be the closure of
L_{p}(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
in
L_{p}(M)
(for the norm topology if
p<\infty
, and the w*-topology if
p=\infty
). They are the
abstract Hardy spaces
associated with
P
. A natural question is whether abstract Hardy spaces form an interpolation scale for the real method. The main tool for addressing this problem is the notion of
K
-closedness
, introduced by Pisier in
[
21
]
, also referred to as
quasi-complementation
in the terminology adopted in this thesis (and likewise called
K
-closedness in
[
17
]
). The problem of proving a noncommutative analogue of Jones’ result for the compatible couple
(H_{1},H_{\infty})
—that is, showing that the subcouple
(H_{1},H_{\infty})
is
K
-closed (or quasi-complemented, according to our terminology) in the compatible couple
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
—has triggered substantial developments in abstract interpolation theory. Relevant references include
[
3
,
13
,
17
]
.

The main result of the paper is stated as follows. See Part 2 for the unexplained notions.

Theorem

(Bourgain’s method).

Let
{\mathbb{X}}
be a Ahlfors regular locally compact space, let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra, and let
N:=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{X}})\bar{\otimes}M
be the tensor product von Neumann algebra equipped with the tensor product trace. Let
P
be a projection on
L_{2}(N)
which satisfies the technical assumptions, and which is given by a singular kernel on
{\mathbb{X}}
. Then the subcouple
(H_{1}(P),H_{\infty}(P))
is quasi-complemented in
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
.

In Part 3, we build on the results of the previous section to develop noncommutative harmonic analysis on the torus. Our approach differs from classical methods, which typically rely on transference principles from Euclidean spaces. Instead, we work directly on the torus, making essential use of Bourgain’s method.

Let
d\geq 1
be a positive integer, let
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
denote the
d
-torus, let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra, and let
N:=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\bar{\otimes}M
denote the tensor product von Neumann algebra equipped with the tensor product trace.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the
analytic Hardy space

H_{p}(N)
as the space of
f\in L_{p}(N)
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}f(z)z^{n}dz=0,\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{d}.

Then, in the case
d=1
, by applying our noncommutative version of Bourgain’s method, we provide a new proof of the following well-known theorem, independent of any complex-variable techniques.

Theorem

(Pisier, Xu
[
20
]
).

Assume
d=1
. The subcouple
(H_{1}(N),H_{\infty}(N))
is quasi
-
complemented in the compatible couple
(L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N))
with a universal constant, and for every
1<p<\infty
we have

(H_{1}(N),H_{\infty}(N))_{\theta,p,K}=H_{p}(N)

with equivalent norms, with constants depending on
p
only, where
\theta:=1-1/p
.

If
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the
(homogeneous) Sobolev space

W_{1,p}(N)
as the closure in
L_{p}(N)
(for the norm topology if
p<\infty
and the w*-topology if
p=\infty)
of trigonometric polynomials
g\in L_{p}(N)
under the (semi)norm

\|g\|_{W_{1,p}(N)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{j}g\|_{L_{p}(N)}^{p}\Big]^{1/p}&\text{if}\ p<\infty\\
\sup_{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}\|\partial_{j}g\|_{L_{\infty}(N)}&\text{if}\ p=\infty\end{array}\right..

As already noticed in
[
3
]
, Bourgain’s method can be applied in this context. With our noncommutative version of Bourgain’s method, we obtain the following result, which provides an operator-valued extension of a classical result originally due to DeVore and Scherer
[
9
]
in the context of inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces on Euclidean spaces.

Theorem.

Let
f\in W_{1,1}(N)+W_{1,\infty}(N)
. Then, for every
t>0
, up to a constant depending only on
d
, we have

K_{t}(f,W_{1,1}(N),W_{1,\infty}(N))\ \simeq\ \sum_{j=1}^{d}K_{t}(\partial_{j}f,L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N)).

Corollary.

If
1\leq p\leq\infty
then

W_{1,p}(N)=(W_{1,1}(N),W_{1,\infty}(N))_{\theta,p,K}

with equivalent norms, with constants depending on
p,d
only, where
\theta:=1-1/p
.

For the complex method, we get the following result.

Theorem.

For every
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
, we have

[W_{1,p_{0}}(N),W_{1,p_{1}}(N)]_{\theta}=W_{1,p_{\theta}}(N)

with equivalent norms, with constants depending only on
p_{0},p_{1},\theta,d
, where

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

The determination of complex interpolation spaces for the compatible couple
(W_{1,1}(N),W_{1,\infty}(N))
remains open, even in the commutative case.

Part I
Preliminaries

1.
Abstract interpolation theory

The material in this section is taken from
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
.

1.1.
Compatible couples

A
compatible couple
is a couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
of subspaces of a common Hausdorff topological vector space
E
, such that
E_{j}
is equipped with a complete norm that makes the inclusion
E_{j}\to E
continuous, for
j\in\{0,1\}
. Then the intersection space
E_{0}\cap E_{1}
and the sum space
E_{0}+E_{1}
are canonically equipped with the complete norms
\|\cdot\|_{E_{0}\cap E_{1}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{E_{0}+E_{1}}
given by the expressions

\|u\|_{E_{0}\cap E_{1}}:=\max\big\{\|u\|_{E_{0}},\ \|u\|_{E_{1}}\big\},

\|u\|_{E_{0}+E_{1}}:=\inf\big\{\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}}+\|u_{1}\|_{E_{1}}\ :\ u=u_{0}+u_{1},\ u_{0}\in E_{0},u_{1}\in E_{1}\big\}.

An
intermediate space
for a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is a subspace
E_{\theta}
of
E_{0}+E_{1}
that contains
E_{0}\cap E_{1}
, and that is equipped with a complete norm that makes the inclusions
E_{0}\cap E_{1}\to E_{\theta}
and
E_{\theta}\to E_{0}+E_{1}
both continuous. If
E_{\theta_{0}}
,
E_{\theta_{1}}
are intermediate spaces for a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, then their sum
E_{\theta_{0}}+E_{\theta_{1}}
and their intersection
E_{\theta_{0}}\cap E_{\theta_{1}}
are also intermediate spaces for
(E_{0},E_{1})
when equipped with the corresponding sum norm
\|\cdot\|_{E_{\theta_{0}}+E_{\theta_{1}}}
and intersection norm
\|\cdot\|_{E_{\theta_{0}}\cap E_{\theta_{1}}}
as defined above.

1.2.
Compatible bounded operators

Let
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
be two compatible couples. A
compatible bounded operator

(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})
is an operator
T:E_{0}+E_{1}\to F_{0}+F_{1}
such that, if
j\in\{0,1\}
, then
T
maps
E_{j}
into
F_{j}
, and
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is bounded. In this situation, we set

\|T\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})}:=\max\big\{\|T\|_{E_{0}\to F_{0}},\|T\|_{E_{1}\to F_{1}}\big\}.

Let
T:(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})
be a compatible bounded operator. Note that
T
is injective (resp. surjective, bijective) if and only if
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is, for
j\in\{0,1\}
.

We say that
T
is an
embedding/quotient
of compatible couples if
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is an embedding/quotient of normed spaces for
j\in\{0,1\}
(recall that a bounded operator
T:E\to F
between normed spaces is an embedding/quotient if it is injective/surjective and the induced bounded operator
E/\ker T\to\ima T
is an isomorphism of normed spaces). We say that
T
is an
isomorphism
of compatible couples if
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is an isomorphism of normed spaces, for
j\in\{0,1\}
.

We say that
T
is
contractive
if
\|T\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})\to(E_{0},E_{1})}\leq 1
. We say that
T
is an
isometric embedding/coisometric quotient
of compatible couples if
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is an isometric embedding/coisometric quotient of normed spaces for
j\in\{0,1\}
(recall that a quotient of normed spaces
T:E\to F
is coisometric if the induced isomorphism of normed spaces
E/\ker T\to F
is isometric). We say that
T
is an
isometric isomorphism
of compatible couples if
T:E_{j}\to F_{j}
is an isometric isomorphism of normed spaces, for
j\in\{0,1\}
.

Remark
.

There is an obvious way to define the category of compatible couples and compatible (contractive) bounded operators. The isomorphisms in this category correspond to the (isometric) isomorphisms of compatible couples.

An
interpolation space
with constant
C
for a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is an intermediate space
E_{\theta}
for
(E_{0},E_{1})
, such that, if
T:(E_{0},E_{1})\to(E_{0},E_{1})
is a compatible bounded operator, then
T
maps
E_{\theta}
into
E_{\theta}
and
\|T\|_{E_{\theta}\to E_{\theta}}\leq C\|T\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})\to(E_{0},E_{1})}
. An
exact interpolation space
is an interpolation space with constant
C\leq 1
. The sum/intersection of (exact) interpolation spaces is again an (exact) interpolation space. More generally, an
interpolation pair
with constant
C
for a pair of compatible couples
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
is a pair of intermediate spaces
E_{\theta}
and
F_{\theta}
for
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
respectively, such that, if
T:(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})
is a compatible bounded operator, then
T
maps
E_{\theta}
into
F_{\theta}
and
\|T\|_{E_{\theta}\to F_{\theta}}\leq C\|T\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})}
.

An
exact interpolation pair
is an interpolation pair with constant
C\leq 1
.

1.3.
Interpolation functors

An
interpolation functor
with constant
C
is a map
\mathcal{F}
that assigns to each compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
an intermediate space
\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
, such that, if
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
are a pair of compatible couples, then
\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
and
\mathcal{F}(F_{0},F_{1})
is an interpolation pair with constant
C
for
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
(in this situation, if
(E_{0},E_{1})
is a compatible couple, then
\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
is necessarily an interpolation space with constant
C
for
(E_{0},E_{1})
).

An
exact interpolation functor
is an interpolation functor with constant
C\leq 1
.

Remark
.

For instance, the map
\Sigma
(resp.
\Delta
) that assigns to each compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
the sum space
E_{0}+E_{1}
(resp. the intersection space
E_{0}\cap E_{1}
) is an exact interpolation functor.

If
\mathcal{F}
is an (exact) interpolation functor, then
\mathcal{F}
defines in an obvious way a functor from the category of compatible couples and compatible (contractive) bounded operators to the category of complete normed spaces and (contractive) bounded operators.

The following result is the so-called Aronszajn-Gagliardo theorem.

Theorem I.1

(
[
1
]
(Theorem 2.5.1))
.

Let
E_{\theta}
be an exact interpolation space for a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
. Then there is an exact interpolation functor
\mathcal{F}
such that
E_{\theta}=\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
.

1.4.
Subcouples

A
subcouple
of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is a couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
where
A_{j}
is a closed subspace of
E_{j}
for
j\in\{0,1\}
. In this situation, the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
inherits a unique structure of compatible couple making the inclusion
A_{0}+A_{1}\to E_{0}+E_{1}
an isometric embedding of compatible couples
(A_{0},A_{1})\to(E_{0},E_{1})
. Thus, if
\mathcal{F}
is an (exact) interpolation functor, then the inclusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})&\rightarrow&\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})\\
u&\mapsto&u\end{array}\right.

is a well-defined bounded (contractive) injective operator, but it may fail to be an embedding of normed spaces (this is in particular the case for
\mathcal{F}=\Sigma
, but note that, however, in the case
\mathcal{F}=\Delta
, the inclusion
A_{0}\cap A_{1}\to E_{0}\cap E_{1}
is an isometric embedding of normed spaces).

1.5.
Complementation

A subcouple
(A_{0},A_{1})
of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is
complemented
with constant
C
if there is a bounded (contractive) compatible operator
P:(E_{0},E_{1})\to(E_{0},E_{1})
such that
P:E_{j}\to E_{j}
is idempotent with range
A_{j}
, and
\|P\|_{E_{j}\to E_{j}}\leq C
, for
j\in\{0,1\}
.

A subcouple
(A_{0},A_{1})
of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is
contractively complemented
if it is complemented with constant
C\leq 1
.

If
(A_{0},A_{1})
is a (contractively) complemented subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, then for every (exact) interpolation functor
\mathcal{F}
, the inclusion
\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
is an (isometric) embedding of normed spaces, with range
(A_{0}+A_{1})\cap\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
, and the projection

\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(E_{0}/A_{0},E_{1}/A_{1})
is a (coisometric) quotient of normed spaces, with kernel
\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})
.

1.6.
K
-functionals

Let
(E_{0},E_{1})
be a compatible couple. The
K
-functional
of
u\in E_{0}+E_{1}
is defined for
t>0
as

K_{t}(u)=K_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1}):=\inf\big\{\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}}+t\|u_{1}\|_{E_{1}}\ :\ u_{0}\in E_{0},\ u_{1}\in E_{1},\ u=u_{0}+u_{1}\big\}.

For fixed
t>0
,
K_{t}
is an equivalent norm on
E_{0}+E_{1}
. If
(E_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},F_{1})
are two compatible couples and
T:(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})
a compatible bounded operator, then

K_{t}(Tu,F_{0},F_{1})\leq\|T\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})\to(F_{0},F_{1})}K_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1})

for every
u\in E_{0}+E_{1}
and
t>0
.

A
K
-
method parameter
is a complete normed space
\Phi
of (class of) Lebesgue measurable functions with variable
t>0
such that,

\triangleright

if
f,g\in\Phi
with
|g|\leq|f|
then
\|g\|_{\Phi}\leq\|f\|_{\Phi}
,

\triangleright

the function
t\mapsto 1\wedge t
belongs to
\Phi
.

If
\Phi
is a
K
-
method parameter and
(E_{0},E_{1})
is a compatible couple, then the
K
-method interpolation space

K_{\Phi}(E_{0},E_{1}):=\big\{u\in E_{0}+E_{1}\ :\ t\mapsto K_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1})\in\Phi\big\}

is a subspace of
E_{0}+E_{1}
and is equipped with the complete norm
\|\cdot\|_{K_{\Phi}(E_{0},E_{1})}
given by the expression

\|u\|_{K_{\Phi}(E_{0},E_{1})}:=\|t\mapsto K_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1})\|_{\Phi}.

This construction defines an exact interpolation functor
K_{\Phi}
called the
K
-
method
with parameter
\Phi
.

If
(A_{0},A_{1})
is a subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, then we have

K_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1})\leq K_{t}(u,A_{0},A_{1}),\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ u\in A_{0}+A_{1},\ t>0

A subcouple
(A_{0},A_{1})
of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is
K
-
closed
with constant
C
if

K_{t}(u,A_{0},A_{1})\leq CK_{t}(u,E_{0},E_{1})

for every
u\in A_{0}+A_{1}
and
t>0
.

Remark
.

If
(A_{0},A_{1})
is a
K
-closed subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, then
A_{0}+A_{1}
is closed in
E_{0}+E_{1}
(in other words, the inclusion
A_{0}+A_{1}\to E_{0}+E_{1}
is an embedding of normed spaces).

The following proposition follows directly from the definitions.

Proposition I.2
.

If
(A_{0},A_{1})
is a
K
-closed subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, then for every
K
-
method parameter
\Phi
, the inclusion
K_{\Phi}(A_{0},A_{1})\to K_{\Phi}(E_{0},E_{1})
is an embedding of normed spaces, with range
(A_{0}+A_{1})\cap K_{\Phi}(E_{0},E_{1})
.

The notion of
K
-closedness used in
[
17
]
is slightly different from the one we use here. In order to avoid any confusion we introduce a new terminology.

Definition I.3
.

A subcouple
(A_{0},A_{1})
of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
is
quasi
-
complemented
with constant
C
if, for every
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
, if
a=u_{0}+u_{1}
with
u_{j}\in E_{j}
for
j\in\{0,1\}
, then
a=a_{0}+a_{1}
with
a_{j}\in A_{j}
and
\|a_{j}\|_{E_{j}}\leq C\|u_{j}\|_{E_{j}}
for
j\in\{0,1\}
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
. If
(A_{0},A_{1})
is complemented in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
C
, then
(A_{0},A_{1})
is clearly quasi-complemented in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
C
. Moreover, it is clear that if
(A_{0},A_{1})
is quasi-complemented in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
C
, then it is
K
-closed in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
C
, and in addition we have
A_{j}=E_{j}\cap(E_{0}+E_{1})
for
j\in\{0,1\}
, so that it is also normal (and thus
J
-closed) in
(E_{0},E_{1})
.

Proposition I.4
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a subcouple of a compatible couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
such that
A_{j}=E_{j}\cap(E_{0}+E_{1})
for
j\in\{0,1\}
and which is
K
-closed in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
C
. Then
(A_{0},A_{1})
is quasi-complemented in
(E_{0},E_{1})
with constant
4C
.

Proof.

Let
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
, and write
a=u_{0}+u_{1}
with
u_{j}\in E_{j}
for
j\in\{0,1\}
. If
u_{j}=0
, then
a\in(A_{0}+A_{1})\cap E_{j}=A_{j}
and there is nothing to prove. Thus, we can assume
u_{0},u_{1}\neq 0
. Set
t:=\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}}/\|u_{1}\|_{E_{1}}
. Then, we can write
a=a_{0}+a_{1}
with
a_{j}\in A_{j}
and

\|a_{0}\|_{E_{0}}+t\|a_{1}\|_{E_{1}}\leq 2K_{t}(a,A_{0},A_{1})\leq 2CK_{t}(a,E_{0},E_{1})\leq 2C(\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}}+t\|u_{1}\|_{E_{1}})=4C\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}}.

This gives the estimates

\|a_{0}\|_{E_{0}}\leq 4C\|u_{0}\|_{E_{0}},\ \ \ \ \|a_{1}\|_{E_{1}}\leq 4C\|u_{1}\|_{E_{1}}.

∎

2.
Real and Complex interpolation spaces

2.1.
The real method

Let
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
\Phi_{\theta,p}
denote the space of Lebesgue
-
measurable functions
f
with variable
t>0
such that

\|f\|_{\Phi_{\theta,p}}:=\|t\mapsto t^{-\theta}f(t)\|_{L_{p}(dt/t)}<\infty

Then
\Phi_{\theta,p}
is a
J
-
method parameter (and thus also a
K
-
method parameter). If
(E_{0},E_{1})
is a compatible couple, let us denote

(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta,p,K}:=K_{\Phi_{\theta,p}}.

By convention, we set
(E_{0},E_{1})_{0,p,K}:=E_{0}
and
(E_{0},E_{1})_{1,p,K}:=E_{1}
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. They are the so called
real interpolation spaces
.

2.2.
The complex method

Let
{\mathbb{S}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\ :\ 0<\re z<1\}
denote the open unit strip in the complex plane, with boundary
\partial{\mathbb{S}}=\{it\ :\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}\cup\{1+it\ :\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
and closure
\overline{{\mathbb{S}}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\ :\ 0\leq\re z\leq 1\}
.

Let
(E_{0},E_{1})
be a compatible couple. Let
\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
denote the space of functions
f:\overline{{\mathbb{S}}}\to E_{0}+E_{1}
continuous on
\overline{{\mathbb{S}}}
, holomorphic on
{\mathbb{S}}
, such that
f(j+it)\in E_{j}
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
j\in\{0,1\}
, and such that the functions
{\mathbb{R}}\to E_{j}
,
t\mapsto f(j+it)
are continuous and vanishes at infinity, for
j\in\{0,1\}
. If
f\in\mathcal{F}(E_{0},F_{1})
, we set

\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})}:=\max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(j+it)\|_{E_{j}}.

If
0<\theta<1
, the
complex interpolation space

[E_{0},E_{1}]_{\theta}
is the subspace of
E_{0}+E_{1}
of elements of the form
f(\theta)
with
f\in\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
. It is equipped with the complete norm
\|\cdot\|_{[E_{0},E_{1}]_{\theta}}
given by the expression

\|u\|_{[E_{0},E_{1}]_{\theta}}:=\inf\big\{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})}\ :\ f\in\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1}),\ u=f(\theta)\big\}.

This construction yields, for fixed
0<\theta<1
, an exact interpolation functor. By convention, we set
(E_{0},E_{1})_{0}:=E_{0}
and
(E_{0},E_{1})_{1}:=E_{1}
.

. An other relevant reference is

3.1.
Semifinite von Neumann algebras

Let
M
be a
(semi)finite von Neumann algebra
, i.e. a von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal (semi)finite faithful (n.s.f.) trace
\tau
. Let
H
denote the Hilbert space on which
M
acts. A closed and densely defined operator
x
on
H
with polar decomposition
x=u|x|
and spectral decomposition
|x|=\int_{0}^{\infty}sde_{s}
is
affiliated
with
M
if
u\in M
and
e_{s}\in M
for all
s>0
. The
distribution function
of
x
is the right-continuous nonnegative decreasing function of the variable
s>0
denoted
\lambda_{x}
, such that

\lambda_{x}(s)=\tau(1-e_{s}),\ \ \ \text{for}\ s>0.

The
singular function
of
x
is the right-continuous nonnegative decreasing function of the variable
s>0
denoted
\mu_{x}
such that

\mu_{x}(s)=\inf\big\{t>0\ :\ \lambda_{x}(t)\leq s\big\},\ \ \text{for}\ s>0.

A closed and densely defined operator
x
on
H
is
\tau
-
measurable
if it is affiliated with
M
and if its distribution function (or its singular function) takes at least one finite value. Any element of
M
is
\tau
-
measurable. The set
L_{0}(M)
of
\tau
-
measurable operators then admits a canonical structure of
\ast
-
algebra, so that the inclusion
M\to L_{0}(M)
is a
\ast
-morphism and
\tau
is canonically extended to the positive part
L_{0}(M)_{+}
of
L_{0}(M)
so that

\tau(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{x}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda_{x}(s)ds,\ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in L_{0}(M)_{+}.

For every
x\in L_{0}(M)
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we set

\|x\|_{L_{p}(M)}=\|x\|_{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{x}(s)^{p}ds\Big]^{1/p}=\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda_{x}(s)ps^{p-1}ds\Big]^{1/p}&\text{if}\ p<\infty\\
\inf\{s>0\ \mid\ \tau(1_{(s,\infty)}(|x|))=0\}&\text{if}\ p=\infty\end{array}\right..

Then, for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the associated
L_{p}
-space
,

L_{p}(M):=\big\{x\in L_{0}(M)\ :\ \|x\|_{L_{p}(M)}<\infty\big\}

is a subspace of
L_{0}(M)
and becomes a complete normed space under
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(M)}
. Note that we have
L_{\infty}(M)=M
with equal norms.

We will need the following version of Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.

Theorem I.5

(Marcinkiewicz)
.

Let
T:L_{2}(M)\to L_{2}(M)
be a self-adjoint bounded operator which is of weak-type
(1,1)
with constant
C
, i.e. for every
x\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(M)
, we have

\sup_{s>0}s\lambda_{T(x)}(s)\leq C\|x\|_{L_{1}(M)}.

Then, for every
1<p<\infty
, the operator
T
is
L_{p}(M)
-bounded with a constant depending only on
p,\|T\|_{L_{2}(M)\to L_{2}(M)}
and
C
.

3.2.
The compatible couple
(L_{1},L_{\infty})

The
\ast
-algebra
L_{0}(M)
admits a canonical Hausdorff topology so that the inclusions
L_{1}(M)\to L_{0}(M)
and
L_{\infty}(M)\to L_{0}(M)
become continuous, making the couple
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
compatible.

Proposition I.6
.

Let
E(M)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
.

(1)

If
x\in E(M)
, then
x^{*}\in E(M)
, with
\|x^{*}\|_{E(M)}=\|x\|_{E(M)}
.

(2)

If
x\in E(M)
,
a,b\in M
, then
axb\in E(M)
with
\|axb\|_{E(M)}\leq\|a\|_{M}\|x\|_{E(M)}\|b\|_{M}
.

The following results are classical.

Theorem I.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then

L_{p}(M)=[L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M)]_{1/q}

with equal norms, where
1<q<\infty
is such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

Theorem I.8

(
[
10
]
(Theorem 3.9.16))
.

Let
x\in L_{0}(M)
. Then
x\in L_{1}(M)+L_{\infty}(M)
if and only if for every
t>0
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{x}(s)ds<\infty

and in that case, we have

K_{t}(x,L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))=\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{x}(s)ds,\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ t>0.

Corollary I.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then

L_{p}(M)=(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))_{1/q,p,K}

with equivalent norms, with constants depending on
p
only where
1<q<\infty
is such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

The following deep result says that the compatible couple
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
is a Calderón couple. We refer the reader to
[
15
]
for more background on Calderón couples.

Theorem I.10

(
[
10
]
(Corollary 7.2.3))
.

Let
E(M)
be an intermediate space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
. Then
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
if and only if it is fully symmetric, i.e. for every
x,y\in L_{0}(M)
such that
K_{t}(x)\leq K_{t}(y)
for all
t>0
, if we have
y\in E(M)
, then
x\in E(M)
with
\|x\|_{E(M)}\leq\|y\|_{E(M)}
.

Remark
.

As a consequence, if
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
, and if
x\in E(M)
, then
|x|\in E(M)
with
\||x|\|_{E(M)}=\|x\|_{E(M)}
.

We consider the commutative semifinite von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
of bounded Lebesgue
-
measurable functions on the half-line
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
whose trace is given by the Lebesgue measure. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we will denote
L_{p}:=L_{p}(L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}))
. Note that, if
x\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
, then its singular function
\mu_{x}
lives in
L_{1}+L_{\infty}
, as an immediate consequence of
I.8
. If
E
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
, then

E(M):=\big\{x\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)\ :\ \mu_{x}\in E\big\}

is a subspace of
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
and is equipped with the complete norm
\|\cdot\|_{E(M)}
given by the expression

\|x\|_{E(M)}:=\|\mu_{x}\|_{E},\ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in E(M).

Theorem I.11

(
[
10
]
(Corollary 7.2.4))
.

If
E
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
, then
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
.

Theorem I.12

(
[
10
]
(Theorem 7.3.4))
.

Let
E_{0},E_{1}
be exact interpolation spaces for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
, let
\mathcal{F}
be an exact interpolation functor, and set
E_{\theta}:=\mathcal{F}(E_{0},E_{1})
. Then

E_{\theta}(M)=\mathcal{F}(E_{0}(M),E_{1}(M))

with equal norms.

Remark
.

As a consequence of the above result together with the Aronszajn–Gagliardo theorem, if
E_{0},E_{1}
are exact interpolation spaces for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
, then the exact interpolation spaces for
(E_{0}(M),E_{1}(M))
are those of the form
E_{\theta}(M)
where
E_{\theta}
is an exact interpalation space for
(E_{0},E_{1})
.

3.3.
Trace duality

Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra, with trace denoted
\tau
. Then the trace
\tau
extends to a positive and contractive linear form on
L_{1}(M)
, again denoted
\tau
.

If
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
, its
Köthe dual

E^{\times}(M):=\big\{y\in L_{0}(M)\ :\ \forall x\in E(M),\ xy\in L_{1}(M)\big\}

=\big\{y\in L_{0}(M)\ :\ \forall x\in E(M),\ yx\in L_{1}(M)\big\}

is a subspace of
L_{0}(M)
and is equipped with the complete norm
\|\cdot\|_{E^{\times}(M)}
given by the expression

\|y\|_{E^{\times}(M)}=\sup_{x\in E(M),\ \|x\|_{E(M)}\leq 1}|\tau(xy)|=\sup_{x\in E(M),\ \|x\|_{E(M)}\leq 1}|\tau(yx)|,\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ y\in E^{\times}(M).

By definition, if
x\in E(M)
and
y\in E^{\times}(M)
, then
xy,yx\in L_{1}(M)
and

\max\{\|xy\|_{L_{1}(M)},\|yx\|_{L_{1}(M)}\}\leq\|x\|_{E(M)}\|y\|_{E^{\times}(M)}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \textit{(generalized H\"{o}lder's inequality)}

If
E
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
, and if
F:=E^{\times}
denote its Köthe dual, then by
[
10
]
[Theorem 6.1.6], the Köthe dual of
E(M)
is
F(M)
(with equal norms). Hence, the notation
E^{\times}(M)
is consistent with the notations introduced in the previous paragraph. The following results are consequence of
[
10
]
(Proposition 3.4.8, Theorem 3.4.24, Corollary 4.3.9, Corollary 6.2.1).

Theorem I.13
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Then, we have
L_{p}(M)^{\times}=L_{q}(M)
with equal norms. Moreover, we have
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)^{\times}=(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
and
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)^{\times}=(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
with equal norms.

Theorem I.14
.

Let
E(M)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
. Then the Köthe dual
E^{\times}(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
. Moreover, the sesquilinear form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}E(M)\times E^{\times}(M)&\rightarrow&{\mathbb{C}}\\
(x,y)&\mapsto&\tau(xy^{*})\end{array}\right.

is non-degenerate, and thus, defines a canonical duality between
E(M)
and
E^{\times}(M)
, called
trace duality
. Finally,
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
separates the points of both
E(M)
and
E^{\times}(M)
w.r.t. this duality.

Let
E(M)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
, and let
E^{*}(M)
denote the dual space of
E(M)
as a normed space. Then trace duality yields a canonical isometric (antilinear) operator
E^{\times}(M)\to E^{*}(M)
, but in general it may not be surjective. By
[
10
]
[Theorem 5.2.9, Proposition 5.3.2], it is surjective if and only if the norm of
E(M)
is
order
-
continuous
, i.e. if for every decreasing net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
E(M)_{+}
such that
\inf_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=0
then
\inf_{\alpha}\|x_{\alpha}\|_{E(M)}=0
. Thus, if
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm, then
E(M)
is a predual of
E^{\times}(M)
so that
E^{\times}(M)
inherits a canonical w*-topology. For instance, if
1\leq p<\infty
, then
L_{p}(M)
has an order
-
continuous norm.

The following result directly follows.

Theorem I.15
.

Let
E(M)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order-continuous norm. Then the subspace
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
is norm-dense in
E(M)
and
w*
-dense in
E^{\times}(M)
.

4.
Interpolation of abstract Hardy-type spaces

Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra.

Notations.
If
P
is any (self-adjoint) projection on
L_{2}(M)
and if
E(M)
is any exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
, we define
H_{E}(P)
to be the
\sigma(E(M),E^{\times}(M))
-
weak closure of the subspace

E(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))

in
E(M)
. Let us denote
H_{p}(P):=H_{L_{p}}(P)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. By definition,
H_{2}(P)=P(L_{2}(M))
. They are the
abstract Hardy spaces
associated with
P
.

Remark
.

If
P
is a projection on
L_{2}(M)
with complement projection
P^{\bot}
, and if
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
, then from the definitions it is clear that
H_{E}(P)
and
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
are respectively included in the orthogonal of
H_{E^{\times}}(P^{\bot})
and
H_{E}(P^{\bot})
w.r.t. trace duality between
E(M)
and
E^{\times}(M)
.

A projection
P
on
L_{2}(M)
is said to satisfy the
technical assumptions
if

(1)

If
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm, then the orthogonals of
H_{E}(P)
,
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
w.r.t. trace duality coincide respectively with
H_{E^{\times}}(P^{\bot})
,
H_{E}(P^{\bot})
.

(2)

There is a subspace
H(P)
of
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
, which is weakly-closed in
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
(w.r.t. trace duality between
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
and
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
), and such that, if
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm, then we have

H_{E}(P)=H(P)\cap E(M),\ \ \ \ H_{E^{\times}}(P)=H(P)\cap E^{\times}(M).

(3)

If
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm, then the subspace

(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))

is norm-dense in
H_{E}(P)
and w*-dense in
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
.

Proposition I.16
.

Let
P
be a projection on
L_{2}(M)
which is
L_{1}(M)
-bounded. Then
P
satisfies the technical assumptions.

Proof.

As
P
is self-adjoint on
L_{2}(M)
, we see that
P
is also
L_{\infty}(M)
-bounded, so that it extends to a bounded compatible operator
P:(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))\to(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
. Then, it is easy to show that, if
E(M)
is an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm, we have

H_{E}(M)=\big\{x\in E(M)\ :\ Px=x\big\},\ \ H_{E^{\times}}(M)=\big\{x\in E(M)\ :\ Px=x\big\}

By using the fact that
P
is self-adjoint on
L_{2}(M)
, it is then easy to check that
P
satisfies the technical assumptions.
∎

Proposition I.17
.

Let
P
be a projection on
L_{2}(M)
which satisfies the technical assumptions. Then the complement projection
P^{\bot}
on
L_{2}(M)
also satisfies the technical assumptions.

Proof.

As
P
satisfies (1), by the bipolar theorem it is clear that
P^{\bot}
satisfies (1).

For (2), let
H(P^{\bot})
denote the orthogonal of
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
w.r.t. trace duality between
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
and
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
. Then
H(P^{\bot})
is indeed weakly-closed in
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
. Let
E(M)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
with order
-
continuous norm. As
H_{E}(P^{\bot})
is orthogonal to
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
, and because
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
contains
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
, we deduce that
H_{E}(P^{\bot})
is included in
H(P^{\bot})
, and thus in
E(M)\cap H(P^{\bot})
. For the converse inclusion, let
x\in E(M)\cap H(P^{\bot})
. As
P
satisfies (3), we deduce that
x
is orthogonal to
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
. As
P
satisfies (1), we deduce that
x\in H_{E}(P^{\bot})
. Thus
H_{E}(P^{\bot})=E(M)\cap H(P^{\bot})
as desired. In a similar way, we prove that
H_{E^{\times}}(P^{\bot})=E^{\times}(M)\cap H(P^{\bot})
.

It remains to justify (3). Note that
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P^{\bot}(L_{2}(M))
is weakly closed in
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
, as a consequence of the fact that
P
is weakly-continuous for the weak topology of
L_{2}(M)
. Thus, as
P
satisfies (1), by the bipolar theorem it suffices to show that, if
x\in E^{\times}(M)
resp.
x\in E(M)
is orthogonal to
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P^{\bot}(L_{2}(M))
, then
x\in H_{E^{\times}}(P)
(resp.
x\in H_{E}(P)
). Let
x\in E^{\times}(M)
(resp.
x\in E(M)
) orthogonal to
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\cap P^{\bot}(L_{2}(M))
. Then
x
is in the biorthogonal of
H(P)
w.r.t. trace duality between
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
and
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
. By the bipolar theorem, and because
H(P)
is, by assumption, weakly closed in
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
, we deduce that
x\in H(P)
. Thus
x\in E^{\times}(M)\cap H(P)
(resp.
x\in E^{\times}(M)\cap H(P)
). As
P
satisfies (2), we finally deduce that
x\in H_{E^{\times}}(P)
(resp.
x\in H_{E}(P)
), as desired.
∎

Proposition I.18
.

Let
P
be a projection on
L_{2}(M)
which satisfies the technical assumptions, let
E_{0}(M)
,
E_{1}(M)
be two exact interpolation spaces for
(L_{1}(M),L_{\infty}(M))
such that the subcouple
(H_{E_{0}}(P),H_{E_{1}}(P))
is quasi-complemented in
(E_{0}(M),E_{1}(M))
with constant
C
, and let
\Phi
be a
K
-parameter space such that the exact interpolation space

E_{\theta}(M):=K_{\Phi}(E_{0}(M),E_{1}(M))

has order-continuous norm. Then

H_{E_{\theta}}(P)=K_{\Phi}(E_{0}(M),E_{1}(M))

with equivalent norms, with constants depending on
C
only.

Proof.

By Proposition
I.2
, it suffices to check that

(H_{E_{0}}(P)+H_{E_{1}}(P))\cap E_{\theta}(M)

is a dense subspace of
H_{E_{\theta}}(P)
. It is a subspace because of point (2) of the technical assumptions, and it is dense because of point (3) of the technical assumptions.
∎

A projection
P
on
L_{2}(M)
is said to be of
Jones-type
if

(1)

P
satisfies the technical assumptions.

(2)

If
1<p<\infty
, then
P
is
L_{p}(M)
-bounded with a constant depending on
p
only.

(3)

The subcouples
(H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))
and
(H_{1}(P^{\bot}),H_{2}(P^{\bot}))
are both quasi
-
complemented in the compatible couple
(L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))
with a universal constant.

Remark
.

If
P
is a projection
L_{2}(M)
which is of Jones-type, then the complement projection
P^{\bot}
is also of Jones-type.

A detailed proof of the following result is given in
[
19
]
[Theorem 2.6]. It essentially relies on the Wolff-type theorem for
K
-functionals established by Kislyakov and Xu in
[
16
]
[Theorem 2].

Theorem I.19
.

Let
P
be a Jones-type projection on
L_{2}(M)
. If
1\leq p_{0},p_{0}\leq\infty
, then the subcouple
(H_{p_{0}}(P),H_{p_{1}}(P))
is quasi
-
complemented in the compatible couple
(L_{p_{0}}(M),L_{p_{1}}(M))
with a constant depending only on
p_{0},p_{1}
.

By Proposition
I.18
, we deduce the following result.

Theorem I.20
.

Let
P

5.
Operators of Calderón-Zygmund type

Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with trace denoted
\tau
.

Definition II.1
.

A bounded operator
T
on
L_{2}(M)
is of
Calderón-Zygmund type
with constant
C
if for every
s>0
and
y\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(M)
there is a decomposition

y=a+b

with
a,b\in L_{2}(M)
and a projection
p\in M
such that

•

\|a\|_{2}^{2}\leq C^{2}s\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\tau(1-p)\leq C^{2}s^{-1}\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\|pT(b)p\|_{1}\leq C\|y\|_{1}
.

Remark
.

Every bounded operator
T
on
L_{2}(M)
which is also
L_{1}
-bounded is of Calderón-Zygmund type with constant
\|T\|_{L_{1}\to L_{1}}
(in the above definition, take
a=0
,
p=1
, and
b=y
).

Theorem II.2
.

Let
T:L_{2}(M)\to L_{2}(M)
be a bounded operator which is of Calderón-Zygmund type with constant
C
. Then
T
is of weak type
(1,1)
with a constant depending on
C,\|T\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}
only.

Proof.

Let
y\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(M)
and
s>0
. Then there is a decomposition

y=a+b

with
a,b\in L_{2}(N)
and a projection
p\in N
such that

•

\|a\|_{2}^{2}\leq C^{2}s\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\tau(1-p)\leq C^{2}s^{-1}\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\|pT(b)p\|_{1}\leq C\|y\|_{1}
.

where
C
is a universal constant. As

T(y)=T(a)+T(b)=T(a)+(1-p)T(b)+pT(b)(1-p)+pT(b)p,

we have

\lambda_{T(y)}(4s)\leq\lambda_{T(a)}(s)+\lambda_{(1-p)T(b)}(s)+\lambda_{pT(b)(1-p)}(s)+\lambda_{pT(b)p}(s).

We clearly have

\lambda_{(1-p)T(b)}(s)\leq\sigma(1-p)\leq Cs^{-1}\|y\|_{1},\ \ \ \lambda_{pT(b)(1-p)}(s)\leq\sigma(1-p)\leq Cs^{-1}\|y\|_{1}.

By the Markov inequality, we have

\lambda_{T(a)}(s)\leq s^{-2}\|T(a)\|_{2}^{2}\leq s^{-2}\|T\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}^{2}\|a\|_{2}^{2}\leq s^{-1}\|T\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}^{2}C^{2}\|y\|_{1}

and

\lambda_{pT(b)p}(s)\leq s^{-1}\|pT(b)p\|_{1}\leq Cs^{-1}\|y\|_{1}.

By combining the previous estimates, we get

\lambda_{T(y)}(4s)\leq C^{\prime}s^{-1}\|y\|_{1}

where
C^{\prime}:=3C+\|T\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}^{2}C^{2}
. Thus

\|Ty\|_{1,\infty}=\sup_{s>0}s\lambda_{T(y)}(s)\leq 4C^{\prime}\|y\|_{1}

as desired.
∎

Theorem II.3
.

Let
P:L_{2}(M)\to L_{2}(M)
be a projection which satisfies the technical assumptions and which is of Calderón-Zygmund type with constant
C
. Then, the subcouple
(H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))
is quasi-complemented in
(L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))
with a constant depending on
C
only.

Proof.

By the technical assumptions, we already have

H_{1}(P)=L_{1}(M)\cap(H_{1}(P)+H_{2}(P)),\ \ \ \ H_{2}(P)=L_{2}(M)\cap(H_{1}(P)+H_{2}(P)).

Thus, it suffices to show that
(H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))
is
K
-closed in
(L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))
with a constant depending on
C
only. Let
t>0
, and let
x\in L_{1}(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
such that

K_{t}(x,L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))\leq 1

There is
y\in L_{1}(M)
,
z\in L_{2}(M)
such that
x=y+z
and
\|y\|_{1}+t\|z\|_{2}\leq 2
. Then
y\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(M)
and by applying the above definition with
y
and the parameter
s=t^{-2}
, there is a decomposition

y=a+b

with
a,b\in L_{2}(M)
and a projection
p\in M
such that

•

\|a\|_{2}^{2}\leq C^{2}t^{-2}\|y\|_{1}\leq 2C^{2}t^{-2}
,

•

\tau(1-p)\leq Ct^{2}\|y\|_{1}\leq 2Ct^{2}
,

•

\|pP(b)p\|_{1}\leq C\|y\|_{1}\leq 2C
,

Then, we set

y^{\prime}:=P(b),\ \ \ \ \ z^{\prime}:=P(a+z).

As
x=P(x)=P(y)+P(z)
, it is clear that
x=y^{\prime}+z^{\prime}
. On the one hand, as
P
is
L_{2}
-contractive, we have

\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq\|P(a)\|_{2}+\|P(z)\|_{2}\leq\|a\|_{2}+\|z\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}Ct^{-1}+2t^{-1}\leq 2(C+1)t^{-1}

On the other hand, we can write
y^{\prime}=py^{\prime}p+(1-p)y^{\prime}+py^{\prime}(1-p)=pP(b)p+(1-p)y^{\prime}+py^{\prime}(1-p)
, so that

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|pP(b)p\|_{1}+\|(1-p)y^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|py^{\prime}(1-p)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2C+\|(1-p)y^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|y^{\prime}(1-p)\|_{1}.

As
y^{\prime}=x-z^{\prime}=y+(z-z^{\prime})
we have

\displaystyle\|(1-p)y^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-p)y\|_{1}+\|(1-p)(z-z^{\prime})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2+\|1-p\|_{2}\|z-z^{\prime}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2+\tau(1-p)^{1/2}(\|z\|_{2}+\|z^{\prime}\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2+\tau(1-p)^{1/2}(2t^{-1}+2(C+1)t^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq 2+\sqrt{2}C^{1/2}t(2t^{-1}+(C+1)t^{-1})

\displaystyle=2+2\sqrt{2}C^{1/2}(C+3)=:C^{\prime},

and similarly, we have

\|y^{\prime}(1-p)\|_{1}\leq C^{\prime}.

Thus

\|y^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq 2C+2C^{\prime}.

As we have
y^{\prime}\in L_{1}(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
and
z^{\prime}\in P(L_{2}(M))
, this shows that

K_{t}(x,H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))\leq\|y^{\prime}\|_{1}+t\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq(2C+2C^{\prime})+2(C+1)=:C^{\prime\prime}.

Hence, we have shown that for every
x\in L_{1}(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
, we have

K_{t}(x,H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))\leq C^{\prime\prime}K_{t}(x,L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M)).

By the technical assumptions, we know that
L_{1}(M)\cap P(L_{2}(M))
is dense in both
H_{1}(P)
and
H_{2}(P)
. Thus, it is dense in
H_{1}(P)+H_{2}(P)
. As the
K
-functional
K_{t}(-,H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))
and
K_{t}(-,L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))
are both continuous on
H_{1}(P)+H_{2}(P)
, the above estimate extends to every
x\in H_{1}(P)+H_{2}(P)
. Hence, we obtain that
(H_{1}(P),H_{2}(P))
is
K
-closed in
(L_{1}(M),L_{2}(M))
with constant
C^{\prime\prime}
, as desired.
∎

6.
Singular kernel operators

Let
{\mathbb{X}}
be a
Ahlfors
d
-regular locally compact space
, i.e. a locally compact space equipped with a Radon measure and a metric that induces its topology, for which the open balls of finite radius are relatively compact, and we have the Ahlfors
d
-regularity condition

|B_{r}(x)|\simeq r^{d},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \ \ x\in{\mathbb{X}},\ 0<r<\diam({\mathbb{X}}).

Here
|\cdot|
refers to the volume w.r.t. the measure and
B
refers to the open balls w.r.t. the metric, and the notation
\simeq
means that the estimate holds, up to some constants depending only on
{\mathbb{X}}
. We refer the reader to the appendix for more details.

Remark
.

Any
d
-Riemanian manifold equipped with its volume measure and its geodesic distance is an Ahlfors
d
-regular locally compact space
[
6
]
.

In this situation, we have the following generalised doubling condition

|B_{\lambda r}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{d}|B_{r}(x)|,\ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{X}},\ r>0,\ \lambda\geq 1

and the following reverse doubling condition for small balls

|B_{\lambda r}(x)|\gtrsim\lambda^{d}|B_{r}(x)|,\ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{X}},\ r>0,\ \lambda\leq 1.

In particular
{\mathbb{X}}
is a space of homogeneous type in the usual sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
8
]
. For
x,y\in{\mathbb{X}}
, we set

V(x,y):=|B_{d(x,y)}(x)|.

If
x,y\in{\mathbb{X}}
, we have (cf. appendix)

V(x,y)\lesssim V(y,x).

A
kernel
on
{\mathbb{X}}
is a scalar-valued continuous function defined on

\big\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{X}}\times{\mathbb{X}}\ :\ x\neq y\big\}.

Let
k
be a kernel on
{\mathbb{X}}
. Then
k
is said to be
singular
if it satisfies the
size condition

|k(x,y)|\lesssim V(x,y)^{-1}

for
x,y\in{\mathbb{X}}
with
x\neq y
, and the
L_{2}
-Hörmander regularity condition

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})|^{1/2}\sup_{y\in B_{r/3}(y^{\prime})}\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})}\big|k(x,y)-k(x,y^{\prime})|^{2}dx\Big]^{1/2}\lesssim 1

for every
y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}
and
r>0
. This last condition is taken from
[
4
]
. It is stronger than the usual Hörmander regularity condition, but weaker than the classical Lipschitz regularity condition, as shown in the next proposition.

Lemma II.4
.

If
y\in{\mathbb{X}}
,
m\geq 0
and
r,\alpha>0
then

(6.1)

\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y)\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y)}\frac{d(x,y)^{-2\alpha}}{V(x,y)^{2}}dx\Big]^{1/2}\lesssim[2^{m}r]^{-\alpha}|B_{2^{m}r}(y)|^{-1/2}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y)\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y)}\frac{d(x,y)^{-2\alpha}}{V(x,y)^{2}}dx\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim[2^{m}r]^{-\alpha}\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y)\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y)}\frac{dx}{V(y,x)^{2}}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq[2^{m}r]^{-\alpha}\frac{1}{|B_{2^{m}r}(y)|}\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y)\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y)}dx\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq[2^{m}r]^{-\alpha}\frac{|B_{2^{m+1}r}(y)|^{1/2}}{|B_{2^{m}r}(y)|}

\displaystyle\lesssim[2^{m}r]^{-\alpha}|B_{2^{m}r}(y)|^{-1/2}.

∎

Proposition II.5
.

Let
k
be a kernel on
{\mathbb{X}}
. If
k
satisfies the Lipschitz regularity condition

|k(x,y)-k(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim V(x,y^{\prime})^{-1}\frac{d(y,y^{\prime})^{\alpha}}{d(x,y^{\prime})^{\alpha}}

for
x,y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}
with
2d(y,y^{\prime})<d(x,y)
, where
\alpha\in(0,1]
is a constant, then
k
satisfies the
L_{2}
-Hörmander regularity condition.

Proof.

Let
y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}
,
r>0
. If
y\in B_{r/3}(y^{\prime})
and
x\in{\mathbb{X}}\setminus B_{r}(y^{\prime})
, then

d(x,y)\geq d(x,y^{\prime})-d(y,y^{\prime})>r-r/3=2r/3\geq 2d(y,y^{\prime})

thus, by the previous lemma we get

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})}\big|k(x,y)-k(x,y^{\prime})|^{2}dx\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})}V(x,y^{\prime})^{-2}\frac{d(y,y^{\prime})^{2\alpha}}{d(x,y^{\prime})^{2\alpha}}dx\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}\Big[\int_{B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})}\frac{d(x,y^{\prime})^{-2\alpha}}{V(x,y^{\prime})^{2}}dx\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\alpha m}|B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})|^{-1/2}.

As we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|B_{2^{m+1}r}(y^{\prime})\setminus B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})|^{1/2}\times 2^{-\alpha m}|B_{2^{m}r}(y^{\prime})|^{-1/2}\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-\alpha m}\lesssim 1

we get the desired conclusion.
∎

Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra and let
N:=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{X}})\bar{\otimes}M
be the tensor product von Neumann algebra equipped with the tensor product trace denoted
\sigma
. A bounded operator

on

is
given by a kernel

on
{\mathbb{X}}
if

, where

is a bounded operator on

that admits the integral representation

(Sf)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{X}}}k(x,y)f(y)dy

for every

with compact support and

outside the support of

.

Remark
.

Note that if
T
is a bounded operator on
L_{2}(N)
given by a kernel on
{\mathbb{X}}
, then for every scalar

, the bounded operator

is also given by the same kernel on

.

The following theorem relies on the semicommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition introduced by J. Parcet, J.M. Conde Alonse and L. Cadilhac in
[
4
]
. This construction is detailed in Appendix A.

Theorem II.6

(Calderón-Zygmund decomposition)
.

If
s>0
and
f\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(N)
there is a decomposition

f=a+b

with
a,b\in L_{2}(N)
, and a projection
p\in N
, such that

•

\|a\|_{2}^{2}\leq C^{2}s\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\sigma(1-p)\leq C^{2}s^{-1}\|y\|_{1}
,

•

\|pT(b)p\|_{1}\leq C_{T}\|y\|_{1}
for every bounded operator
T
on
L_{2}(N)
given by a singular kernel on
{\mathbb{X}}
, where
C_{T}
is positive constant depending on
T
only,

where
C
is a constant depending on
{\mathbb{X}}
only.

Proof.

Let
f\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})(N)
and
s>0
. It is classicaly known that we have a decomposition

f=f_{1}-f_{2}+i(f_{3}-f_{4})

with

for

. For

and

The the decomposition

clearly satisfies the required conditions with the projection

.
∎

Corollary II.8
.

Let

, and for

be a bounded operator on

Let

and

. Then we can write

8.
Preliminaries

In this chapter,
d\geq 1
is a fixed positive integer. Let
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
denote the
d

{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, and let
p:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
denote the canonical projection, defined by
p(x)=(e^{ix_{1}},\ldots,e^{ix_{d}})
for

. The
d
-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}

d(p(x),p(y))=\inf_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|x-y+2\pi n|,\ \ \ x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Finally, the
d

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}f(z)dz=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{[0,2\pi)^{d}}f(p(x))dx,\ \ \ \ f\in C({\mathbb{T}}^{d}).

Then, the
d
-torus

is a Ahlfors

-regular (locally) compact space. In this preliminary section, we provide criteria that allow us to justify that the examples of bounded Fourier multipliers on

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Proposition III.1
.

Let
K\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
be a
2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-periodic
C^{1}
-function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that

|K(y)|\underset{|y|\to 0}{=}O(|y|^{-d}),\ \ \ |\nabla K(y)|\underset{|y|\to 0}{=}O(|y|^{-(d+1)}).

Let
k
be the kernel on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
such that

k(p(x),p(y))=K(x-y),\ \ \ \ \ x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ x-y\notin 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Then
k
satisfies the size condition and the Lipschitz regularity condition (with
\alpha=1
). As a consequence,
k
is a singular kernel on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Proof.

The fact that
k
satisfies the size condition is obvious. Let
z,w,w^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
such that
2d(w,w^{\prime})<d(z,w^{\prime})

such that

,
w=p(y)
,
w^{\prime}=p(y^{\prime})
, with
d(w,w^{\prime})=|y-y^{\prime}|
. By the mean value theorem, we have

|K(x-y)-K(x-y^{\prime})|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla K(\gamma(t))|\cdot|y-y^{\prime}|

where
\gamma
is the smooth path such that

\gamma(t)=(1-t)(x-y)+t(x-y^{\prime})=(x-y)+t(y-y^{\prime}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ t\in[0,1].

Note that

\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\inf_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\gamma(t)-2\pi n|\geq\min\big\{d(z,w),d(z,w^{\prime})\big\}\geq\frac{1}{2}d(z,w^{\prime}).

Thus, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla K(\gamma(t))|\lesssim d(z,w^{\prime})^{-(d+1)}

and

|K(x-y)-K(x-y^{\prime})|\lesssim d(z,w^{\prime})^{-d}\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{d(z,w^{\prime})}.

Hence, we get

|k(z,w)-k(z,w^{\prime})|\lesssim d(z,w^{\prime})^{-d}\frac{d(w,w^{\prime})}{d(z,w^{\prime})}.

Finally, from the estimate

V(z,w)=|B_{d(z,w)}(z)|\lesssim d(z,w)^{d}

we obtain

|k(z,w)-k(z,w^{\prime})|\lesssim V(z,w)^{-1}\frac{d(w,w^{\prime})}{d(z,w^{\prime})}

as desired.
∎

Theorem III.2
.

Let
m\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a
0
-homogeneous bounded symbol on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, whose inverse Fourier transform, as a distribution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, is a smooth function
K\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\infty}^{d-1}}K(x)dx=0

where

{\mathbb{S}}_{\infty}^{d-1}:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ :\ |x|_{\infty}:=\sup_{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}|x_{i}|=1\big\}

denotes the
(d-1)
-sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
w.r.t the norm
|\cdot|_{\infty}
. Let
\tilde{m}\in C_{b}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
be a bounded symbol on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
\tilde{m}(n)=m(n)
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
T
be the bounded Fourier multiplier on
L_{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
with symbol
\tilde{m}
. Then
T
is given by a kernel on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
that satisfies the size condition and the Lipschitz regularity condition (with
\alpha=1
). In particular
T
is given by a singular kernel on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Proof.

First, as
m
is
0
-homogeneous, we know that
K
is
(-d)
-homogeneous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, so that we have the following estimates

|K(y)|\lesssim|y|^{-d},\ \ |\nabla K(y)|\lesssim|y|^{-(d+1)},\ \ \text{for}\ y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

We assert that the expression

\tilde{K}(x):=K(x)+\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\\
|m|_{\infty}=R\end{subarray}}K(x+2\pi m),\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

defines a
2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-periodic continuous function
\tilde{K}\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. In order to justify this, we can use the mean value theorem to get the estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(x+2\pi m)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}|K(x+2\pi m)-K(2\pi m)|+\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(2\pi m)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}\frac{|x|}{|m|^{d+1}}+\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(2\pi m)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x|}{R^{d+1}}\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}1+\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(2\pi m)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x|}{R^{d+1}}R^{d-1}+\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(2\pi m)\Big|.

where we used the obvious asymptotic

\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}1\underset{R\to\infty}{=}O(R^{d-1}).

As
K
is
(-d)
-homogeneous, a standard quadrature formula (see for instance
[
2
]
) gives the asymptotic

R^{-(d-1)}\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K\big(\frac{m}{R}\big)\underset{R\to\infty}{=}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\infty}^{d-1}}K(y)dy+O(R^{-1})\underset{R\to\infty}{=}O(R^{-1}).

(For the example to which we want to apply the result, the above sum is actually zero). Thus, again by
(-d)
-homogeneity, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(2\pi m)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-d}\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K\big(\frac{m}{R}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=R^{-1}\Big|R^{-(d-1)}\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K\big(\frac{m}{R}\big)\Big|\underset{R\to\infty}{=}O(R^{-2}).

Finally, we obtain the estimate

\Big|\sum_{|m|_{\infty}=R}K(x+2\pi m)\Big|\lesssim\frac{|x|+1}{R^{2}}.

Hence
\tilde{K}
is a well-defined
2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-periodic continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, and we have

|\tilde{K}(x)|\underset{|x|\to 0}{=}O(|x|^{-d}).

In addition,
\tilde{K}
is clearly
C^{1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, with

\nabla\tilde{K}(x)=\nabla K(x)+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\nabla K(x+2\pi n),\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

(where the sum is uniformly convergent on compacts subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
), so that we also have

|\nabla\tilde{K}(x)|\underset{|x|\to 0}{=}O(|x|^{-(d+1)}).

Now, the bounded Fourier multiplier on
L_{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
with symbol
\tilde{m}
is given by the kernel
k
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
such that

k(p(x),p(y))=\tilde{K}(x-y),\ \ \ \ \ x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ x-y\notin 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

By the previous proposition, the kernel
k
is singular. The proof is complete.
∎

9.
Spectral projections

Let
(\chi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
denote the canonical Hilbert basis of
L_{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, defined by

\chi_{n}(z)=z^{n}:=z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\ldots z_{d}^{n_{d}},\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.

Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with trace denoted
\tau
, and let
N:=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\bar{\otimes}M
denote the tensor product von Neumann algebra equipped with the tensor product trace denoted
\sigma
. The
Fourier transform
of any
f\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)
is the function
\widehat{f}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
which takes values in
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
defined by the equations

\sigma(f(x\otimes\chi_{n}))=\tau(\widehat{f}(n)x),\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ x\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M).

Let
I
be a fixed subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, and let
P
be the
spectral projection
associated with the data
({\mathbb{T}}^{d},M,I)
, i.e. the projection on
L_{2}(N)
such that, if
f\in L_{2}(N)
then

P(f)=\sum_{n\in I}\chi_{n}\otimes\widehat{f}(n),\ \ \ \text{in}\ L_{2}(N).

In other words,
P
is the bounded Fourier multiplier on
L_{2}(N)
whose symbol is the characteristic function of
I
. Note that the complement projection
P^{\bot}
coincides with the spectral projection associated with the data
({\mathbb{T}}^{d},N,I^{\bot})
where
I^{\bot}
is the complement subset of
I
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. In the sequel, we denote

H(P):=\big\{f\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)\ :\ \forall n\notin I,\ \widehat{f}(n)=0\big\}

so that we clearly have

P(L_{2}(N))=L_{2}(N)\cap H(P).

Lemma III.3
.

Let
(K_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
denote the Féjer kernel on
N
, i.e. the family of compatible bounded operators
(L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N))\to(L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N))
such that

K_{\lambda}(f)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ \|n\|_{1}\leq\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{|n_{1}|}{\lambda}\Big)\ldots\Big(1-\frac{|n_{d}|}{\lambda}\Big)\chi_{n}\otimes\widehat{f}(n),\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \lambda>0,\ f\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N).

Let
E(N)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N))
with order
-
continuous norm. then for every
f\in E(N)
(resp.
f\in E^{\times}(N)
), the net
(K_{\lambda}(f))_{\lambda>0}
converges (in the limit
\lambda\to\infty
) to
f
in
E(N)
for the norm topology (resp. in
E^{\times}(N)
for the
w*
-topology), and thus, for every
f\in E(N)
and
g\in E^{\times}(N)
, we have

(9.1)

\sigma(fg)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ \|n\|_{1}\leq\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{|n_{1}|}{\lambda}\Big)^{2}\ldots\Big(1-\frac{|n_{d}|}{\lambda}\Big)^{2}\tau(\widehat{f}(n)\widehat{g}(n)).

Proof.

It is a well-known fact (see
[
12
]
[Remark 3.19]) that
\sup_{\lambda>0}\|K_{\lambda}\|_{L_{1}(N)\to L_{1}(N)}\leq 1
. By duality, we deduce that
\sup_{\lambda>0}\|K_{\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}(N)\to L_{\infty}(N)}\leq 1
. By interpolation, we deduce that

\sup_{\lambda>0}\|K_{\lambda}\|_{E(N)\to E(N)}\leq 1,\ \ \ \ \ \ \sup_{\lambda>0}\|K_{\lambda}\|_{E^{\times}(N)\to E^{\times}(N)}\leq 1.

As the linear span
\{\chi_{n}\otimes a\ :\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ a\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)\}
is dense in
E(N)
for the norm topology and dense in
E^{\times}(N)
for the w*-topology, the desired conclusion easily follows.
∎

Theorem III.4
.

Let
E(N)
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1}(N),L_{\infty}(N))
with order
-
continuous norm. Then

H_{E}(P)=E(N)\cap H(P),

H_{E^{\times}}(P)=E^{\times}(N)\cap H(P).

Moreover, the subspace

(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)\cap H(P)

is norm-dense in
H_{E}(P)
and
w*
-dense in
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
.

Proof.

It is clear that

E(N)\cap H(P)=\big\{f\in E(N)\ :\ \forall n\notin I,\ \widehat{f}(n)=0\big\}

is a norm-closed subspace of
E(N)
that contains
E(N)\cap P(L_{2}(N))
, and moreover
E(N)\cap P(L_{2}(N))
clearly contains
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)\cap H(P)
. Thus, it suffices to show that
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)\cap H(P)
is norm-dense in
E(N)\cap H(P)
. Let
f\in E(N)\cap H(P)
. As the net
(K_{\lambda}(f))_{\lambda>0}
converges to
f
in
E(N)
for the norm topology and also belongs to
E(N)\cap H(P)
, we can assume that there is
g\in E(N)\cap H(P)
and
\lambda>0
such that
f=K_{\lambda}(g)
. Then, we have

f=\sum_{n\in I,\ \|n\|_{1}\leq\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{|n_{1}|}{\lambda}\Big)\ldots\Big(1-\frac{|n_{d}|}{\lambda}\Big)\chi_{n}\otimes\widehat{g}(n).

As
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
is norm-dense in
E(N)
, there is a net
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
that converges to
g
in
E(N)
for the norm topology. We set

f_{\alpha}:=\sum_{n\in I,\ \|n\|_{1}\leq\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{|n_{1}|}{\lambda}\Big)\ldots\Big(1-\frac{|n_{d}|}{\lambda}\Big)\chi_{n}\otimes\widehat{g_{\alpha}}(n).

Then
f_{\alpha}\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)\cap H(P)
. As the net
(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
clearly converges to
f
in
E(N)
for the norm topology, the proof is complete. The dual statement is proved similarly.
∎

Corollary III.5
.

The spectral projection

satisfies the technical assumptions.

Proof.

Let

,

. By Theorem
III.4
, it suffices to check that the subspaces

and

In the sequel, we assume

and

is the

]

The Riesz projection

10.
The Leray projection

Let
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
denote the space of smooth functions on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
equipped with its canonical locally convex topology. Let
M
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with trace denoted
\tau
, and let
N:=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\bar{\otimes}M
denote the tensor product von Neumann algebra equipped with the tensor product trace denoted
\sigma
. Let

D(N):=C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\odot(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)

denote the algebraic tensor product, identified as a subspace of
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
, and equipped with the projective topology (in the sense of tensor products of locally convex spaces), when
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M)
is equipped with its weak topology coming from trace duality with
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(M)
. By the definition of the projective tensor product, the inclusion
D(N)\to(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
is continuous, where
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
is equipped with its weak topology coming from trace duality with
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)
. As a consequence, every
f\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)
can be considered as an element of the dual space
D^{\prime}(N)
by the equation

\langle f,g\otimes a\rangle=\sigma(f(g\otimes a)),\ \ \ \ g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}),\ a\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(M).

and the associated injective operator
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)\to D^{\prime}(N)
is continuous, when
(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N)
is of course equipped with its weak topology coming from trace duality with
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N)
. For
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, let
\partial_{j}
denote the usual partial derivative operator on
L_{2}(N)
with respect to the
j
-th coordinate on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
(it is the Fourier multiplier on
L_{2}(N)
with symbol
\eta_{j}\in C({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
such that
\eta_{j}(n)=in_{j}
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
). Then
\partial_{j}
clearly defines a continuous operator on
D(N)
, and thus by duality we can also consider
\partial_{j}
as an operator on the dual space
D^{\prime}(N)
.

For
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we consider the bounded function
\rho_{ij}\in C_{b}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
such that

\rho_{ij}(0)=\delta_{ij},\ \ \ \rho_{ij}(n)=\delta_{ij}-\frac{n_{i}n_{j}}{|n|^{2}},\ \ \ \text{for}\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

and let
R_{ij}
denote the associated bounded Fourier multiplier on
L_{2}(N)
. From the obvious relations

\overline{\rho_{ij}}=\rho_{ji},\ \ \ \ \ \rho_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\rho_{ik}\rho_{kj},\ \ \text{for}\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}

we deduce that the bounded operator
P
on
L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})=L_{2}(N)^{\oplus d}
given by the matrix
(R_{ij})_{i,j}
is a projection. It is the
Leray projection
associated with the data
({\mathbb{T}}^{d},M)
.
In the sequel, we denote

H(P):=\Big\{(f_{j})_{j}\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\ :\ \sum_{j}\partial_{j}f_{j}=0\Big\}

and

H(P^{\bot}):=\Big\{(f_{j})_{j}\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\ :\ \forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ \partial_{i}f_{j}=\partial_{j}f_{i},\ \widehat{f}_{j}(0)=0\Big\}

Then, an easy computation shows that

P(L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d}))=L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)

and

P^{\bot}(L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d}))=L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P^{\bot}).

If
\lambda>0
and
f\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
, the element
g\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
such that
g_{j}=K_{\lambda}(f_{j})
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, will be denoted
K_{\lambda}(f)
. As Fourier multipliers commute with each other, if
f\in H(P)
, then clearly
K_{\lambda}(f)\in H(P)
for every
\lambda>0
.

Theorem III.9
.

Let
E
be an exact interpolation space for
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
with order
-
continuous norm. Then

H_{E}(P)=E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P),

H_{E^{\times}}(P)=E^{\times}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P).

Moreover, the subspace

(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)

is norm-dense in
H_{E}(P)
and
w*
-dense in
H_{E^{\times}}(P)
.

Proof.

As the identity operator
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\to E(N)^{\oplus d}
is an isomorphism of normed spaces, it is clear that

E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)=\Big\{(f_{j})_{j}\in E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\ :\ \sum_{j}\partial_{j}f_{j}=0\Big\}

is a weakly-closed and thus norm-closed subspace of
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
that contains
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap P(L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d}))
, and it is clear that
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap P(L_{2}(N^{\bar{\oplus}d}))
contains
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
. As a result, it suffices to show that
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
is norm-dense in
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
. Let
f\in E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
. As the net
(K_{\lambda}(f))_{\lambda>0}
converges to
f
in
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
for the norm topology and also belongs to
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
, we can assume that there is
g\in E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
and
\lambda>0
such that
f=K_{\lambda}(g)
. As
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
is norm-dense in
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
, there is a net
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
that converges to
g
in
E(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})
for the norm topology. We set

f_{\alpha}:=K_{\lambda}(P(g_{\alpha}))=P(K_{\lambda}(g_{\alpha}))

Then clearly
f_{\alpha}\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(N^{\bar{\oplus}d})\cap H(P)
. Moreover, as the Fourier transform of each coordinate of
K_{\lambda}(g_{\alpha})
is supported on
\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\ :\ |n|_{1}\leq\lambda\}
, since
P
is a Fourier multiplier it is clear that the net
(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
converges to
f
in
E(N)
for the norm topology, which concludes the proof. The dual statement is proved similarly.
∎

The following result is proved in a similar way.

.

Corollary III.11

The Leray projection
P
satisfies the technical assumptions.

Proof.

Lemma III.12
.

If
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, the bounded operator
R_{ij}
is given by a singular kernel on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
m_{ij}\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the bounded symbol on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

m_{ij}(\xi)=\delta_{ij}-\frac{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{2}},\ \ \ \text{for}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

By a classical computation (see
[
18
]
[Proposition 4.70]), the inverse Fourier transform
K_{ij}
of
m_{ij}
, as a distribution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, is a smooth function
K_{ij}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
such that

K_{ij}(x)=-\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big[\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d}}\Big]=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\omega_{d-1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}-\frac{d}{\omega_{d-1}}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{d+1}},\ \ \ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

where
\omega_{d-1}
denotes the surface measure of the
(d-1)
-sphere. We have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{\infty}}K_{ij}(y)dy=0.

Indeed, if
i\neq j

Lemma IV.2
