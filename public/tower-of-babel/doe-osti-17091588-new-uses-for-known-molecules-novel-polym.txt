New Uses for Known Molecules: Novel Polymeric Energetics Enabling High Explosives
 with Enhanced Energetic Output and Mechanical Performance
 James P. Lewicki
                                          24-ERD-020
Project Overview
Current PBX (Polymer Bonded eXplosive) systems face challenges in meeting (in particular,
mechanical) performance targets for next-generation applications & in long-term supply chain
security, driving a broad need for replacement. This project aimed to meet that need via
developing IHE (Insensitive High Explosive) systems based on chemically modifying energetics in
use today to form co-polymeric crosslinked systems, capable of chemically coupling with the HE
(High Explosive) crystalline phase in a composite PBX. Such ‘poly-HE’ can be utilized as a
reactive ‘binder-phase’ for IHE’s enabling enhanced output and improved mechanical
performance vs. current systems.
Our approach was to develop a holistic loop of computational modeling/simulation, small scale
synthesis and small-scale testing of candidate systems; to efficiently identify promising
candidate systems without resorting to exhaustive exploratory synthetic studies. We were
successful in this approach and rapidly identified three candidate chemical systems with the
potential to serve as a reactive, crosslinkable and energetic ‘binder’ in an IHE system.
Results showed that significant improvements in mechanical properties of the composite were
achievable though the use of ‘thermoset’ binders, that the energetic content of these binders
was such that they could contribute mechanical energy to the overall detonation of the IHE
composite. Advanced mechanical simulations developed under the ER (Exploratory Research)
further showed that with efficient chemical coupling to the HE crystals by our reactive
thermoset binder, we can approach the fundamental limit in strength of a dispersed crystalline
phase/binder composite HE: That it is the crystal layers themselves that fail under a tensile load
in this scenario. With our approach we can for the first time leverage the full strength of the
crystalline HE phases, and failure no longer occurs within the binder or at the binder/crystal
interface.

Mission Impact
The outputs of this research represent highly impactful new directions for SD and NNSA mission
space in the form of transformative, next generation IHE systems. Indeed, the outputs of this
research are already being used as a basis for scale up and maturation efforts with the goal of
developing a productionizalbe IHE system with enhanced energetic and mechanical
performance. If successfully productionized, such a family of robust, energy dense IHE’s offer a
home-grown low supply chain security risk option for next-generation, increased performance
IHE&CHE (Conventional High Explosive) systems. The modeling frameworks and small-scale
synthesis/analysis loops pioneered and developed under this LDRD also offer new and efficient
tools for future accelerated efforts in the design, and optimization of new energetic materials
with significant reductions in time and effort.
