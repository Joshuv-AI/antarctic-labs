Compatible Hamilton cycles in graphs with large minimum degree
Natalie Behague, Francesco Di Braccio, Bertille Granet, Allan Lo

Abstract
The renowned theorem of Dirac states that if G G is a graph with minimum degree at least n / 2 n/2 then G G has a Hamilton cycle. A natural generalisation asks what properties of an edge-colouring of G G guarantee the existence of a properly edge-coloured Hamilton cycle in G G . This concept can be further generalised as follows: an incompatibility system for G G is a set ℱ \mathcal{F} of ‘forbidden’ pairs of adjacent edges, that is, ℱ ⊆ { { u v , v w } ∈ ( E ⁡ ( G ) 2 ) } \mathcal{F}\subseteq\{\{uv,vw\}\in\binom{E(G)}{2}\} . A cycle in G G is then compatible if no two of its edges form a pair in ℱ \mathcal{F} . The system ℱ \mathcal{F} is called μ ​ n \mu n -bounded if for all v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) and u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) uv\in E(G) , there are at most μ ​ n \mu n pairs { u ​ v , v ​ w } ∈ ℱ \{uv,vw\}\in\mathcal{F} . How small must μ \mu be to guarantee the existence of a compatible Hamilton cycle in G G ? Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov showed that μ = 10 − 16 \mu=10^{-16} suffices (for n n large), while an example of Bollobás and Erdős shows that μ ≤ 1 / 4 \mu\leq 1/4 is necessary. We significantly reduce this gap for large graphs of minimum degree at least ( 1 / 2 + ε ) ​ n (1/2+\varepsilon)n , by showing that μ = 1 / 8 \mu=1/8 suffices but μ ≤ 1 / 6 \mu\leq 1/6 is necessary for such graphs. In fact, we give more precise bounds which are functions of δ ⁡ ( G ) / n \delta(G)/n .

Introduction

1.1
Background and results

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 3
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
.
In 1952, Dirac famously proved that
G
contains a Hamilton cycle. Since then, a well-studied strand of research consists in generalising this seminal result, in particular by finding Hamilton cycles satisfying specific properties.

A typical example of this is to colour the edges of
G
and seek Hamilton cycles with specific colour patterns, such as
rainbow
Hamilton cycles, where all edges have distinct colours.
For example, Albert, Frieze and Reed
[
1
]
showed that every edge-coloured
K_{n}
with no colour appearing more than
n/32
times contains a rainbow Hamilton cycle.
This was then strengthened by Coulson and Perarnau
[
8
]
, who showed that if an edge-coloured graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
has no colour appearing more than
\mu n
times, for some sufficiently small
\mu>0
, then it contains a rainbow Hamilton cycle.

In this paper, we focus on local restrictions on Hamilton cycles.
Typically one seeks
properly
edge-coloured Hamilton cycles, where any two incident edges receive distinct colours, in edge-coloured (complete) graphs. Let
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)
be the maximum number of times a colour appears at a vertex.
Equivalently,
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)\coloneqq\max\{\Delta(H):H\subseteq G\text{ is monochromatic}\}
.
The Bollabás–Erdős conjecture
[
3
]
states that
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(K_{n})<\lfloor n/2\rfloor
suffices for the existence of a properly edge-coloured Hamilton cycle.
Following various results
[
3
,
23
,
2
,
5
]
toward this conjecture, it was then solved asymptotically by the last author
[
17
]
.

Here, we will generalise proper edge-colourings further using incompatibility systems.
Let
G
be a graph.
An
incompatibility system

\mathcal{F}
for
G
is a family of pairs of intersecting edges of
G
, that is,
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in E(G)\colon e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}
.
Here,
\mathcal{F}
is to be understood as the list of ‘forbidden’ pairs of edges.
We say that a subgraph
H
in
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-compatible
if there do not exist
e,e^{\prime}\in E(H)
such that
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
, that is,
\binom{E(H)}{2}\cap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.
For example, consider an edge-coloured graph
G
.
If
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
consists of all pairs of intersecting edges of the same colour, then
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
-compatible is equivalent to properly edge-coloured.

Recall that for all
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
Thus
\mathcal{F}
can be written as
\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\mathcal{F}_{v}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{v}
is the list of forbidden pairs of edges containing the vertex
v
, that is,
\mathcal{F}_{v}\subseteq\{\{e,e^{\prime}\}:e\neq e^{\prime}\in E(G),e\cap e^{\prime}=\{v\}\}
.
We say an incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}
is
\Delta
-bounded
if for each
v\in V(G)
and edge
e
containing
v
, there are at most
\Delta
other edges
e^{\prime}
such that
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
is
\Delta_{\textrm{mono}}(G)
-bounded.

An additional motivation for the study of compatible Hamilton cycles in Dirac graphs is that they also generalise
transition systems
, which are equivalent to 1-bounded incompatibility systems. Motivated by a problem of Nash-Williams on cycle coverings of Eulerian graphs, Kotzig
[
15
]
first introduced this concept in 1968.
Häggkvist (see
[
4
, Conjecture 8.40]
) then
conjectured that if
G
is a Dirac graph and
\mathcal{F}
is a 1-bounded incompatibility system (a.k.a. transition system) for
G
,
then
G
contains an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible Hamilton cycle.
The first major result on this topic is due to Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov
[
16
]
, who proved Häggkvist’s conjecture in a strong sense.

Theorem 1.1

(Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov
[
16
]
)
.

For

with
\delta(G)\geq n/2

-bounded incompatibility system

\mathcal{F}
-compatible Hamilton cycle.

They also mentioned that the constant

This gives rise to the following natural question, also included in a survey of Sudakov
[

(Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov

1.2
Corollaries for properly coloured Hamilton cycles

As previously discussed, compatible Hamilton cycles generalise properly coloured Hamilton cycles, through the incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
consisting of all pairs of intersecting edges of the same colour.
Recall that
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)\coloneqq\max\{\Delta(H):H\subseteq G\text{ is monochromatic}\}
.
Although they are straightforward corollaries, we explicitly state the following two results on properly coloured Hamilton cycles as they are likely to be of independent interest.

Corollary 1.8
.

For any
\rho>0
and
\delta>1/2
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds.
If
G
is an edge-coloured
n
-vertex graph with
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\delta(G)\geq\delta n\text{ and }\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)\leq(1-\rho)\frac{\delta+\sqrt{2\delta-1}}{4}n,

then
G
contains a properly coloured Hamilton cycle.

Corollary 1.9
.

For any
\rho,{\varepsilon}>0
, there exist
\gamma>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph where
n\geq n_{0}
and
(d-\gamma)n\leq\delta(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq(d+\gamma)n
for some
d\geq 1/2+{\varepsilon}
. If
G
is edge-coloured such that
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)\leq(1-\rho)dn/2
,
then
G
contains a properly coloured Hamilton cycle.

The two corollaries are immediate from
Theorem

1.6
and
Theorem

1.7
respectively, using the incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
defined above.
Corollary
1.9
is asymptotically sharp by Proposition
1.4
.
Note that by taking
d=1
, Corollary
1.8
implies that if
\delta(G)\geq(1-\gamma)n
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{mono}}(G)\leq(1-\rho)n/2
, then there is a properly coloured Hamilton cycle.
Thus, we recover an asymptotic version of the Bollabás–Erdős conjecture, which is also generalised to almost complete graphs.
(The original proof in
[
17
]
relies heavily on the fact that
G
is complete, so it does not generalise to almost complete graphs easily.)

1.3
Paper outline and proof overview

We start with the two constructions proving
Propositions

1.4
and
1.5
in
Section

2
and some preliminaries in
Section

3
. We end with some open problems in
Section

8
. The rest of the paper is devoted to the proofs of
Theorems

1.7
and
1.6
, which follow a common strategy which we now briefly present.

One of the most famous techniques for finding long paths is Pósa’s rotation-extension.
Roughly speaking, given a path
P=v_{1}\dots v_{\ell}
of maximum length and an edge
v_{1}v_{i}
with
i\geq 3
, we have that either
P\cup v_{1}v_{i}
is a cycle or
(P\cup v_{1}v_{i})-v_{i-1}v_{i}
is another path of the same length.
The latter is often referred to as a
rotation of
P
.
However, as observed in
[
16
]
, a compatible path may not have a single rotation.
One idea that was used to circumvent this issue in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
in
[
16
]
is to restrict to compatible paths with stronger properties that enable rotations.
Unfortunately, this results in a very small
\mu
.

In this paper, we instead adapt another celebrated technique for constructing Hamilton cycles: absorption, which is often credited to Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
22
]
.
The proof is divided into three key lemmas: connecting, absorbing and covering.

The connecting lemma enables us join any two compatible paths together using constantly many vertices.
The absorbing lemma states that one can reserve a small set of vertices
A
such that given a compatible linear forest
H
in
G\setminus A
with at most
\beta n
component (that is,
H
is union of at most
\beta n
paths), there exists a compatible cycle covering
V(H)\cup A
.
Finally, the covering lemma states that we can indeed find such a compatible linear forest.

Adapting this methodology to the compatible setting is non-trivial.
The main difficulty lies in the covering lemma, that is, finding an almost spanning compatible linear forest.
Fix an equipartition of
V(G)
into
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
, each of size
\beta n
and with
m
even.
A linear forest can be constructed by picking a perfect matching
M_{i}
between
V_{i}
and
V_{i-1}
for each
i<m
.
But clearly, an arbitrary choice of such a linear forest may not be compatible.
A key property is that the incompatible system
\mathcal{F}
only imposes local restrictions, that is,
e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for all
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
.
Consider the union of the odd matchings,
M_{1}\cup M_{3}\cup\dots\cup M_{m-1}
, which is itself a matching and so is compatible.
The choices of
M_{2},M_{4},\dots,M_{m-2}
are now independent of each other.
Therefore if we know how to pick
M_{2}
, then we should know how to pick the rest and so obtain a compatible linear forest. We discuss this strategy in more detail in
Section

4
.

is
\lceil d/2\rceil
-bounded and that an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible cycle in
G
is a directed cycle in
H
.
However
H
does not have a directed Hamilton cycle as
x
is a source, as desired.

For the ‘moreover part’, define an edge-colouring on
G
with colour set
V(G)
such that the edge
uv\in E(G)
receives colour
v
if and only if
uv\in E(H)
.
Note that a properly coloured cycle corresponds to a directed cycle in
H
.
Thus it is the desired edge-colouring of
G
.
∎

Next we exhibit a different construction to prove
Proposition

1.5
, which significantly improves this upper bound.

Proof of
Proposition

1.5
.

Let
a
be the unique even integer such that
2n/3<a\leq 2n/3+2
.
Let
A
and
B
be disjoint vertex sets of sizes
a
and
n-a
respectively. Let
G
be a graph on
A\cup B
where each vertex in
B
joined to all other vertices and
G[A]
is
(\delta n-(n-a))
-regular.
Note that since
a
is even and
a>(\delta n-(n-a))>0
it is always possible to find a
(\delta n-(n-a))
-regular graph
G[A]
– for example, take an appropriate circulant graph.
Let

\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\left\{\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\binom{E(G[A]))}{2}\colon e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Note that this is the incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}
for the edge colouring of
G
where all edges in
A
are coloured red and all other edges receive unique colours.
The resulting incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}
is
(\delta n-n+a-1)
-bounded, where we have
\delta n-n+a-1\leq\delta n-n/3+1
since
a\leq 2n/3+2
.

Since
a>2n/3
, we have that
|A|>2|B|
. Therefore, any Hamilton cycle in
G
contains two consecutive edges in
A
. However, any two such edges are incompatible, as desired.

For the ‘moreover part’, we now edge-colour
G
such that all edges in
G[A]
use red and each remaining edge of
G
receives a unique colour.
A properly coloured cycle is
\mathcal{F}
-compatible, so
G
has no properly coloured Hamilton cycle.
∎

Preliminaries

3.1
Probabilistic estimates

Let
n,m,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
m,k<n
.
Recall that the hypergeometric distribution with parameters
n,m
and
k
is the distribution of the random variable
X
defined as follows.
Let
M
be a random subset of
[n]
of size
m
and let
X\coloneqq|M\cap[k]|
.

We will need the following standard Chernoff bound, as well as McDiarmid’s and Markov’s inequalities.

Lemma 3.1

(Chernoff’s inequality (see e.g.
[
12
, Corollary 2.3 and Theorem 2.10]
))
.

Let
X
denote the sum of independent identically distributed binomial random variables, or a hypergeometric random variable. Let
\mu\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[X]
. Then for any
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(|X-\mu|\geq\delta\mu)\leq 2\exp(-\delta^{2}\mu/3).

For simplicity of use, we state the following corollary of
Lemma

3.1
, which follows by applying it to the hypergeometric distribution with parameters
n,m,(\alpha+\lambda)n
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\alpha,\lambda>0
with
\alpha+\lambda<1
.
Suppose that
Y\subseteq[n]
and
|Y|\geq(\alpha+\lambda)n
.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\left\{M\in\binom{[n]}{m}:|M\cap Y|\leq\alpha m\right\}\right|\leq 2\binom{n}{m}e^{-\lambda^{2}m/3}.

Theorem 3.3

(McDiarmid’s inequality
[
19
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\geq 0
. For each
i\in[n]
, let
X_{i}
be an independent random variable taking values in
\Omega_{i}
, and let
X\coloneqq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. Let
f:\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Omega_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function such that, for each
i\in[n]
, changing
X
in the
i
th coordinate changes the value of
f(X)
by at most
c_{i}
. Then, for all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|f(X)-\mathbb{E}[f(X)]\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i\in[n]}c_{i}^{2}}\right).

Lemma 3.4

(Markov’s inequality (see
[
12
, Equation 1.3]
))
.

For any non-neqative random variable
X
and
a>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}(X>a\mathbb{E}[X])\leq 1/a.

3.2
Regularising

When proving
Theorem

1.6
,
it will be useful to first find a spanning regular subgraph and then to find a compatible linear forest in that regular subgraph. The following result allows us to pass to a regular subgraph.

Theorem 3.5

(Christofides, Kühn and Osthus
[
7
, Theorem 7]
)
.

Let
1/n\ll{\varepsilon}
, let
\delta\geq(1/2+{\varepsilon})
and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\leq(\delta-{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{2\delta-1})n/4
.
Then every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree
\delta n
contains a spanning connected
2\ell
-regular subgraph.

Note that
Theorem

3.5
is essentially tight up to the
{\varepsilon}
factor: for all positive integers
n
with and all
1/2<\delta<1
such that
\delta n
is an integer, Christofides, Kühn and Osthus
[
7
]
exhibit an
n
-vertex graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)=\delta n
such that
G
contains no
r
-regular graph for any

r>(\delta+\sqrt{(2\delta-1)})n/2+1
.

There are better bounds when the graph is almost regular.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
7
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
0<1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll 1
and
\delta\geq 1/2+{\varepsilon}
.
Then every
n
-vertex graph with
\delta n\leq d(v)\leq(\delta+\varepsilon^{2}/{5})n
for all vertices

contains a spanning regular subgraph with degree at least
(\delta-\varepsilon)n
.

Theorem

3.5
also enables us to ‘regularise’ our incompatibility system.
More precisely, we say an incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}
is

-regular
if for every vertex
v\in V(G)
and every edge

containing

, there are exactly
d
edges

such that
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
.
The next proposition allows us to pass from a bounded incompatibility system to a regular one.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
0<1/n\ll\rho,d\leq 1
and
\mu\coloneqq(1-\rho)d/2
.
Define
\mu^{\prime}
such that
\mu^{\prime}n=dn-2\lfloor dn/8\rfloor-1
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
dn
-regular graph and let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\mu n
-bounded incompatibility system for
G
.
Then there is a
\mu^{\prime}n
-regular incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
for
G
containing
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\rho/4
and
\delta\coloneqq 1/2+\rho/4
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta-{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{2\delta-1}}{4}\geq\frac{1/2+\sqrt{{\rho}/{2}}}{4}>\frac{1}{8}.

Let
v\in V(G)
. Let
H_{v}
be the graph on vertex set
N_{G}(v)
such that
uw\in E(H_{v})
if and only if
\{vu,vw\}\notin\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
Since
G
is
dn
-regular and
\mathcal{F}
is
\mu n
-bounded,
H_{v}
is a
dn
-vertex graph with minimum degree

\delta(H_{v})\geq dn-1-\mu n=(1+\rho)dn/2-1>\left(1/2+\rho/4\right)dn

and so, by
Theorem

3.5
, it has a spanning
2\lfloor dn/8\rfloor
-regular subgraph

.

Now define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{v}
to be the set of all
\{vu,vw\}
such that

and let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\prime}
. Given
uv\in E(G)
, the number of edges

is compatible with is

and so the number of edges it is incompatible with is
dn-1-d_{H_{v}^{\prime}}(v)=dn-2\lfloor dn/8\rfloor-1=\mu^{\prime}n
. In particular,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a
\mu^{\prime}n
-regular incompatibility system for

containing
\mathcal{F}
, as required.
∎

3.3
Matchings

We will need two easy corollaries of König’s theorem.

Lemma 3.8

(König’s theorem
[
13
]
(see also
[
14
]
for a German translation))
.

Any bipartite graph
G
can be decomposed into
\Delta(G)
matchings.

Corollary 3.9

(see e.g.
[
26
, Exercise 7.1.33]
)
.

For all
D\in\mathbb{N}
, any bipartite graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq D
can be decomposed into
D
matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{D}
such that
||M_{i}|-|M_{j}||\leq 1
for all
i,j\in[D]
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Any
2n
-vertex balanced bipartite graph
G
such that
d_{G}(v)=d\pm r
for all vertices
v\in V(G)
, with
d+r\leq n
,
contains a matching with at least
n-2rn/(d+r)
edges.

Proof.

We have
e(G)\geq(d-r)n
.
By
Lemma

,

can be partitioned into

Hence there exists a matching of size at least

∎

4
Finding a compatible linear forest

In this section we prove that the graph contains a compatible linear forest on many edges, that is, consisting of few paths.
We first give an overview of our approach.

A folklore method to construct a large linear forest with few paths in an
n
-vertex
dn
-regular graph
G
proceeds as follows. (Here we have
0<d<1
.)
First randomly partition
V(G)
into equal-sized
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
such that
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
is (almost)
d|S_{i}|
-regular for each
i\in[m-1]
.
Hence for each
i\in[k-1]
,
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
contains an (almost) perfect matching (by Corollary
3.10
).
The union
\bigcup_{i\in[k-1]}M_{i}
is then an (almost) spanning linear forest which consists of (roughly)
|S_{1}|
paths.

We now attempt to apply this to incompatible systems.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(1/2+{\varepsilon})n
and a
\mu
-bounded incompatible system
\mathcal{F}
.
By Theorem
3.5
we may now assume that
G
is
dn
-regular for some
0<d<1
.

Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
be as above.
A naive approach would be to choose the matchings one by one: first choose a matching
M_{1}
of
G[S_{1},S_{2}]
freely, and then for each
i\geq 2
in turn, choose a matching
M_{i}
in the subgraph
G_{i}^{*}
of
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
induced by the edges which are compatible with
M_{i}
.
There is however no guarantee that
G_{i}^{*}
is anywhere close to being regular, and so
M_{i}
could be very small.
We now highlight two main issues of this approach that prevent
G^{*}_{i}
from being almost regular and explain how we address them.

Firstly, the subgraph
G_{i}^{*}
is obtained from
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
by deleting all edges that are incompatible with
M_{i-1}
.
This edge deletion is not symmetrical as it is based on how edges are intersecting at
S_{i}
.
We overcome this by choosing an almost perfect matching
M_{i}
in
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
for each
odd

i\in[k-1]
.
For each
even

i\in[k-1]
,
G^{*}_{i}
is the induced subgraph of
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
given by the edges which are compatible with
M_{i-1}
and
M_{i+1}
.

Consider even
i\in[k-1]
.
To ensure that
G^{*}_{i}
is almost regular, it is natural to choose
M_{i-1}
and
M_{i+1}
randomly.
Recall that
G[S_{i-1},S_{i}]
is almost
d|S_{1}|
-regular, so an edge
e^{\prime}
in
G[S_{i-1},S_{i}]
appears in
M_{i-1}
with probability roughly
d
(and a similar statement for
e^{\prime}\in G[S_{i+1},S_{i+2}]
).
However the probability that an edge

lies in
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
may still vary dramatically depending on
\mathcal{F}_{u}
and
\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
Our second idea is to ensure that
\mathcal{F}
is regular, which can be achieved by
Proposition

3.7
.

For technical reasons, the random matchings
M_{i}
for odd
i\in[k-1]
will not be sampled from the set of all large matchings of
G[S_{i},S_{i+1}]
.
We also randomly partition each
S_{j}
further into
S_{j}^{(1)},\dots,S_{j}^{(\sqrt{k})}
.
The random matching
M_{i}
is now the union of an independently chosen random matching of

for each
j\in[\sqrt{k}]
.
This enables us to apply McDiarmid’s inequality (
Theorem

3.3
) to show that for even
i\in[k-1]
,

is almost regular with high probability.

Therefore, our main goal of this section is to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<1/n\ll\mu\leq 4d/5\leq 4/5
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
dn
-regular graph and let

be a
\mu n
-regular incompatibility system for

contains an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible linear forest on at least

edges.

Before we prove this lemma, note that by

3.7

in

-boundedness.

.

Let

6
Absorbing lemma

We will now prove an absorbing lemma. The idea is that we can set aside a small set of vertices such that given a compatible linear forest without too many components, we can glue together the paths of the linear forest into a compatible Hamilton cycle using the reserved vertices.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll\alpha\ll\rho,{\varepsilon}
.
Let
\delta\geq 1/2+{\varepsilon}
and
\mu\coloneqq(1-\rho)\delta/2
.
Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq\delta n
and
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mu n
-bounded incompatibility system for
G
.
Then there exists
A\subseteq V(G)
with
|A|\leq\alpha n
such that, given any compatible linear forest
H
in
G\setminus A
with at most
\beta n
components, there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible cycle with vertex set
A\cup V(H)
.

Note that the statement of
Lemma

6.1
differs somewhat from the typical absorbing lemma for Hamilton cycles, as seen in e.g.
[
22
]
: Usually, one reserves an absorber
A
, as well as a separate small set of vertices
R
called a reservoir. Then,
R
and the connecting lemma (
Lemma

5.1
) are first used to glue all the paths in the linear forest
H
into a long cycle (the connecting step). Finally,
A
is used to transform this cycle into a Hamilton cycle (the absorbing step). For cosmetic reasons, we merge these two steps into a single statement where the absorber
A
directly turns any linear forest into a Hamilton cycle.

In this section, we use the following conventions. We assume that all paths are an ordered sequence of vertices, so that
v_{1}\dots v_{\ell}
and
v_{\ell}\dots v_{1}
are distinct (when
\ell\geq 2
).
If
P=v_{1}\dots v_{\ell}
we write
P^{-1}
for
v_{\ell}\dots v_{1}
.

Let
P=v_{1}\dots v_{\ell}
be a path.
We say that
v_{1}v_{2}
and
v_{\ell}v_{\ell-1}
are the
end-edges
of
P
.
Note that implicitly there is an ordering on the vertices of
v_{1}v_{2}
and
v_{\ell}v_{\ell-1}
and we will always write these edges in this way with the endpoints of
P
first.

The proof of the absorbing lemma follows a fairly standard approach. Given a path
Q
in the linear forest, an absorbing structure will be itself a compatible path
P
that can be altered to form a new compatible path with the same end-edges as
P
that covers all of
V(P)\cup V(Q)
.
The vertex set
A
that we set aside will be the vertices of a compatible cycle made by connecting together absorbing paths. Suppose that for each compatible linear forest on
G\setminus A
we can pair each component
Q
of the linear forest with a suitable absorbing path
P
in this cycle. Then by definition we can alter
P
to a path with the same end-edges that also covers all the vertices of the corresponding path
Q
. Doing this absorption for each component of the linear forest in turn gives a compatible Hamilton cycle on
G
.

We first prove that given any pair of edges (the end-edges for a non-trivial path in the linear forest) or any vertex (for trivial components of the linear forest) there exists an absorbing path. By a supersaturation result we further show that there are many such absorbing paths for every pair of edges and every vertex. Then taking absorbing paths at random and gluing them into a not-too-large cycle gives the result.

6.2
Supersaturation

By examining the proofs of Lemmas
6.3
and
5.1
, one could deduce that there are many absorbing paths, see Corollary
6.5
for the statement.
However, counting the number of ways we have for constructing (absorbing) paths in those lemmas is cumbersome, so we use the following result instead, whose proof is based on a result of Mubayi and Zhao
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
0<1/n\ll 1/m\ll{\varepsilon}\ll\mu,1/s
, and let
\delta\geq\mu/2
.
Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq\delta n
and
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mu n
-bounded incompatible system for
G
.
Let
S\subseteq V(G)
be such that
|S|=s
.
Then, there are at least
\binom{n-s}{m-s}/2
sets
M\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
of size
m-s
such that
\delta(G[M\cup S])\geq(\delta-{\varepsilon})m
and
\mathcal{F}[M\cup S]
is a
(\mu+{\varepsilon})m
-bounded incompatibility system for
G[M\cup S]
.

Proof.

We say that
M
is
bad for
a vertex
x
if
x\in M\cup S
and
d_{G}(x,M\cup S)\leq(\delta-{\varepsilon})m
.
Let
x\in V(G)
. Note that
d_{G}(x,V(G)\setminus S)\geq\delta n-s\geq(\delta-{\varepsilon}/4)(n-s)
.
By Corollary
3.2
(applied with
n-s,N_{G}(x),\delta-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/4,m-|\{x\}\cup S|
playing the roles of
n,Y,\alpha,\lambda,m
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus S}{m-s}\colon\text{$M$ is bad for $x$}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\left\{M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus(\{x\}\cup S)}{m-|\{x\}\cup S|}\colon d_{G}(x,M)\leq(\delta-{\varepsilon})m\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\left\{M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus(\{x\}\cup S)}{m-|\{x\}\cup S|}\colon d_{G}(x,M)\leq(\delta-{\varepsilon}/2)(m-|\{x\}\cup S|)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\begin{cases}2\binom{n-s}{m-s}e^{-({\varepsilon}/4)^{2}(m-s)/3}&\text{if $x\in S$}\\
2\binom{n-s-1}{m-s-1}e^{-({\varepsilon}/4)^{2}(m-s-1)/3}&\text{if $x\in V(G)\setminus S$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\dbinom{n-s}{m-s}\frac{1}{12}\cdot\begin{cases}\frac{1}{s}&\text{if $x\in S$}\\
\frac{1}{n}&\text{if $x\in V(G)\setminus S$}.\end{cases}

Consider an (ordered) edge
uv
in
G
.
Let
Y(uv)
be the set of vertices
w\in V(G)\setminus(S\cup\{u,v\})
such that
\{uv,uw\}\notin\mathcal{F}
,
so
|Y(uv)|\geq(1-\mu)n-s-2\geq(1-\mu-{\varepsilon}/4)(n-s)
.
We say that
M
is
bad for

uv
if
u,v\in M\cup S
and
|(M\cup S)\cap Y(uv)|\leq(1-\mu-{\varepsilon})m
.
If
M
is not bad for
uv
, then there are at most
(\mu+{\varepsilon})m
vertices
w\in M\cup S
such that
\{uv,uw\}\in\mathcal{F}
.
Similarly, by Corollary
3.2
(applied with
n-s,Y(uv),1-\mu-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/4,m-|\{u,v\}\cup S|
playing the roles of
n,X,\alpha,\lambda,m
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus S}{m-s}:\text{$M$ is bad for $uv$}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\left\{M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus(\{u,v\}\cup S)}{m-|\{u,v\}\cup S|}:|M\cap Y(uv)|\leq(1-\mu-{\varepsilon}/2)(m-|\{u,v\}\cup S|)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\begin{cases}2\binom{n-s}{m-s}e^{-({\varepsilon}/4)^{2}(m-s)/3}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=2$}\\
2\binom{n-s-1}{m-s-1}e^{-({\varepsilon}/4)^{2}(m-s-1)/3}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=1$}\\
2\binom{n-s-2}{m-s-2}e^{-({\varepsilon}/4)^{2}(m-s-2)/3}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=0$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\binom{n-s}{m-s}\frac{1}{12}\cdot\begin{cases}\frac{1}{s^{2}}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=2$}\\
\frac{1}{sn}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=1$}\\
\frac{1}{n^{2}}&\text{if $|\{u,v\}\cap S|=0$}.\end{cases}

Therefore, the number of
M\in\binom{V(G)\setminus S}{m-s}
which are not bad for any vertex or edge is at least

\displaystyle\left(1-s\cdot\frac{1}{12s}-n\cdot\frac{1}{12n}-s^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{12s^{2}}-2sn\cdot\frac{1}{12sn}-n^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{12n^{2}}\right)\binom{n-s}{m-s}\geq\frac{1}{2}\binom{n-s}{m-s}.

For each such
M
, we have
\delta(G[M\cup S])\geq(\delta-{\varepsilon})m
and
\mathcal{F}[M\cup S]
is
(\mu+{\varepsilon})m
-bounded.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll 1/\ell\ll\rho,{\varepsilon}
and suppose
3
divides
\ell
.
Let
\delta\geq 1/2+{\varepsilon}
and
\mu\coloneqq(1-\rho)\delta/2
.
Suppose that
G
is an
n

\mathcal{F}
is a
\mu n
-bounded incompatibility system for

.
Then, for any

Fix an additional constant

and

6.3
(applied with

, and

n^{\prime}\geq(1-\alpha)n
vertices. Since
G^{\prime}
has minimum degree
\delta(G^{\prime})\geq(d-\gamma-\alpha)n
, setting
\delta^{\prime}\coloneqq\delta(G^{\prime})/n^{\prime}
, we see that
\delta^{\prime}\geq d-\gamma-\alpha\geq 1/2+{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}
induces a
\mu^{\prime}n^{\prime}
-bounded incompatibility system for
G^{\prime}
, where
\mu^{\prime}\coloneqq\mu/(1-\alpha)
. Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be a largest regular spanning subgraph of
G^{\prime}
and let
d^{\prime}
be such that
G^{\prime\prime}
is
d^{\prime}n^{\prime}
-regular. We claim that
G^{\prime\prime}
contains a large compatible linear forest.

Observe that
G^{\prime}
has
maximum degree
\Delta(G)\leq(d+\gamma)n\leq(1+2\alpha)(\delta^{\prime}+2\gamma+\alpha)n^{\prime}\leq(\delta^{\prime}+{\varepsilon}^{\prime 2}/5)n^{\prime}
. Thus
Theorem

3.6
implies that
d^{\prime}\geq\delta^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\geq d-\gamma-\alpha-{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\geq(1-3{\varepsilon}^{\prime})d
.
In particular,

\mu^{\prime}=\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{2}\leq\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-\alpha)(1-3{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}

and so we can apply

4.2
to obtain a spanning compatible linear forest in

containing at most

components. By

6.1
,
G
has an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible Hamilton cycle.

The most pertinent open questions remain
1.2
and the following generalisation to
\mu
as a function of
\delta
:

Question 8.1
.

What is the value of the function
\mu(\delta)
given by Definition
1.3
?

We anticipate that the answer to
8.1
will be either the upper bounds given by
Propositions

1.4
and
1.5
or the lower bound given by
Theorem

1.6
, though we do not make a guess as to which is correct.

Theorems

1.6
and
1.7
only give asymptotic bounds on
\mu
, and only apply to graphs with minimum degree at least
\left(1/2+{\varepsilon}\right)n
. One open problem is whether the
{\varepsilon}
and
\rho
in the statements of these theorems can be removed. This is likely to prove difficult: indeed,
Theorem

1.7
with
\rho=0
and
d=1
implies the full Bollobás-Erdős Conjecture.

As mentioned in the introduction, a different generalisation of Dirac’s Theorem to edge-coloured graphs has been to seek rainbow Hamilton cycles, where all edges have distinct colours.
This inspires a generalisation of the local concept of incompatibility systems to a global one. A
global incompatibility system

\mathcal{F}
for
G
is a family of ‘forbidden’ pairs of edges of
G
(where the pairs are not necessarily intersecting). As before, we say that a subgraph
H
in
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-compatible
if there does not exist
e,e^{\prime}\in E(H)
such that
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
.
We say a global incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}
is
\Delta
-bounded
if for each edge
e
there are at most
\Delta
other edges
e^{\prime}
such that
\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{F}
.

For example, consider an edge-coloured graph
G
.
If
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}^{\prime}
consists of
all
pairs of edges of the same colour, then
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}^{\prime}
-compatible is equivalent to rainbow.
Coulson and Perarnau
[
8
]
showed that there exists some sufficiently small
\mu>0
such that if an edge-coloured graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
has no colour appearing more than
\mu n
times, then it contains a rainbow Hamilton cycle. This is equivalent to the statement that if
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}^{\prime}
is
\mu n
-bounded then
G
has an
\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{mono}}^{\prime}
-compatible Hamilton cycle.

Question 8.2
.

Does there exist a constant
\mu
such that every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
and a
\mu n
-bounded global incompatibility system
\mathcal{F}
has an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible Hamilton cycle?

A positive answer to this question would generalise both Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov’s result on (local) incompatibility systems as well as Coulson and Perarnau’s result on rainbow Hamilton cycles.
