A novel time-series modeling framework for predicting tuberculosis incidence in Sichuan, China
Authors: Daren Zhao, Shiyuan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTuberculosis (TB) remains a major infectious disease and public health issue in Sichuan Province, China. This study aimed to explore and validate a novel adaptive hybrid model integrating Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD), Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA), and Exponential Smoothing (ES), and apply it to forecasting TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China, for the first time.MethodsWe collected monthly TB incidence data from 2006 to 2020 in Sichuan Province, China, and divided the time series into training and test sets. The dataset comprised 180 monthly data points, which were split in an 8:2 ratio, with the training set covering 2006–2017 (144 months) and test set covering 2018–2020 (36 months). Subsequently, SARIMA, ES, and EEMD-SARIMA-ES hybrid models were developed based on the training set. The predictive performance was evaluated using a set of metrics, including the Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE).ResultsThe TB incidence from 2006 to 2020 exhibited distinct seasonal and cyclical patterns. The optimal ES model was the Holt-Winters additive (BIC=-0.845; residuals: Ljung Box Q = 22.721, p > 0.05). The optimal SARIMA model was SARIMA(1,0,0)(0,1,1)12 (AIC = 282.18, BIC = 293.71; residuals: Ljung Box Q = 0.079, p > 0.05). The constructed EEMD-SARIMA-ES hybrid model demonstrated superior predictive performance, achieving the lowest error metrics on both the training set (MSE = 3.5050, MAPE = 6.69%, RMSE = 4.9568) and test set (MSE = 0.6897, MAPE = 3.92%, RMSE = 0.9754), thereby outperforming either the ES or SARIMA model alone.ConclusionsThe EEMD-SARIMA-ES hybrid model effectively captured the TB incidence trend in Sichuan Province, China. This model serves as an effective tool for TB surveillance and early warning in Sichuan Province.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13783-9.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease that poses a major public health challenge worldwide. According to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2022 report, although the global TB incidence rate has shown an annual decline of approximately 2% over the past two decades, the incidence in 2021 has increased by 3.6% compared with that in 2020 [1]. All ten countries with the highest TB burden, including China, failed to achieve the control targets set at the 2018 United Nations High-Level Meeting on Tuberculosis [2]. Concurrently, 450,000 new cases of rifampicin-resistant TB were reported globally in 2021, and the overall TB burden has increased by 3% compared with that in 2020 [1]. Consequently, TB prevention and control remain priorities in global infectious disease efforts.

In China, TB is classified as a Category B notifiable infectious disease [3]. Although the TB incidence in China has shown a consistent downward trend, it is among the top notifiable infectious diseases in the country. Sichuan Province in southwestern China has 21 divisions, including 18 prefecture-level cities and three autonomous prefectures, and is characterized by both a large resident population and substantial population mobility. TB incidence in the province has consistently exceeded the national average, posing continued challenges to local TB prevention and control efforts [4]. Among the various notifiable infectious diseases in the province, TB consistently ranks among the top three in terms of annual incidence, making it a priority for predictive modeling and targeted intervention [5].

Surveillance and early warnings are critical for effective TB control. Advancements in information science and technology have driven the application of diverse methods and techniques in TB monitoring and forecasting. Recent literature indicates that commonly used TB predictive models can be classified into three main categories: compartmental, traditional time-series, and machine-learning models [6–14]. Compartmental models, such as the SIR, SEIR, and BSEIR models, were used by Yasin et al. [6] to forecast TB cases in Turkey. Traditional time-series models include the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) [7], Exponential Smoothing (ES) [8], and grey self-memory system models [9]. Machine learning models include Random Forest (RF) [10], Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) [11], Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN) [12], Support Vector Regression (SVR) [13], and deep learning models such as the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network [14]. Research has confirmed that these models are highly effective and deliver robust performance in predicting the incidence of infectious diseases [6–14].

Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD) is an adaptive signal decomposition method based on noise-assisted analysis [15]. Its core objective is to suppress mode mixing through ensemble averaging, thereby achieving robust and effective time-frequency analysis of nonlinear and non-stationary signals [15]. The EEMD method has been increasingly adopted in a wide range of disciplines such as seismic signal processing [16], financial data analysis [17], mechanical fault diagnosis [18], and environmental monitoring [19]. Recently, there has been an emerging application of EEMD in the domains of public health and infectious disease forecasting. Evidence from previous research has shown the superior predictive performance of EEMD in forecasting the incidence of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) [20], COVID-19 [21], and TB [22]. For instance, Li et al. [22] employed an EEMD-ARIMA-NARNN model to forecast TB incidence trends in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. Their findings demonstrated that the EEMD-ARIMA-NARNN model outperformed other comparative models.

A research gap currently exists regarding the application of a hybrid approach integrating the EEMD, SARIMA, and ES models to forecast the incidence trend of TB in Sichuan Province, China. This study extends our prior work on TB incidence in Western China [23], with a specific focus on forecasting the disease trend in Sichuan Province, China. Our prior work showed that TB incidence in Sichuan Province exhibits distinct seasonal, cyclical, and long-term trends, with non-stationarity driven primarily by trends and seasonal fluctuations rather than high-frequency nonlinear abrupt changes [23]. Therefore, the SARIMA or ES model is particularly well suited for forecasting in this context, given its well-validated parsimony, interpretability, and stability with small samples [23]. However, existing EEMD-based hybrid models typically apply a single forecasting method (e.g., SARIMA or ES alone) to all decomposed intrinsic mode functions (IMFs), ignoring that different IMFs exhibit distinct temporal characteristics of different IMFs [24].

In this study, EEMD is employed not merely as a nonlinear processing tool, but as an adaptive approach to disentangle multiscale components, thereby overcoming the limitations of SARIMA when applied to raw non-stationary series. To further advance the methodology, we introduce an adaptive model selection mechanism: for each IMF, we construct both SARIMA and ES as competing candidates and independently select the optimal model based on AIC, BIC, or RMSE, which to our knowledge has not been previously integrated into an EEMD forecasting framework.

To address this limitation, we collected 2006–2020 TB incidence data from Sichuan Province and forecasted using the proposed adaptive hybrid model with competitive candidate selection. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to incorporate both SARIMA and ES as competing candidates within an EEMD framework, implement a per-IMF adaptive model selection, and apply such a hybrid model to forecast TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China. This study aimed to apply this novel adaptive hybrid model for the first time to forecast TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China, to provide methodological support for disease surveillance and early warning, and to inform the optimization of prevention and control strategies.

Methods

Study area

Sichuan Province, located in southwestern China, covers an area of 486,000 square kilometers and comprises 21 cities and 183 counties (cities and districts) under its jurisdiction. It borders seven provinces (municipalities), including Chongqing, Guizhou, Yunnan, Tibet, Qinghai, Gansu, and Shaanxi, and is home to the largest concentration of the Yi ethnic group, the second-largest concentration of the Tibetan ethnic group, and the only concentration of the Qiang ethnic group in China. By the end of 2020, Sichuan Province had a household population of 77.093 million and a resident population of 83.675 million, of which 5.688 million were ethnic minorities [25]. Socioeconomic disparities exist across regions, with some mountainous and rural areas facing limited healthcare access, which may contribute to higher TB transmission risk [5]. To control tuberculosis, the Sichuan provincial government has implemented a comprehensive prevention and control system. This system includes directly observed therapy, short-course (DOTS) strategy, routine screening in high-risk populations, and a network of designated TB hospitals [4]. It also incorporates supporting measures, such as comprehensive case management, drug resistance screening, medical insurance coverage, health education, and targeted efforts in ethnic minority areas [4]. Together, these components form a closed-loop system that integrates prevention, treatment, management and insurance.

Data source

Data on the incidence of TB from January 2006 to December 2020 in Sichuan Province, China, were obtained from the China Public Health Science Data Center website (https://www.phsciencedata.cn/Share/index.jsp). TB is one of the 27 Category B notifiable infectious diseases in China and is subject to strict monitoring and reporting mandates. Consequently, all confirmed cases of TB, defined by both laboratory criteria and clinical diagnosis, are mandatorily notifiable within 24 h of diagnosis through the national web-based surveillance system. Monthly TB incidence data were uninvolved with patients’ personal information; therefore, ethical approval was not required.

This study used 15 years (180 months) of TB incidence data from January 2006 to December 2020 in Sichuan Province, China (Additional File 1). The 180 monthly TB incidence data points were split into training and test sets in an 8:2 ratio. Accordingly, the training set consisted of 144 months of data (2006–2017) for model fitting, whereas the hold-out test set consisted of the remaining 36 months (2018–2020) to validate the model’s generalization ability. The 8:2 split was selected to ensure that the training set contained exactly 12 complete years (144 months), which facilitated stable identification of seasonal patterns and long-term trends. The test set corresponds to the final three years (36 months), representing a complete forecasting window for evaluating predictive performance.

Prediction methods

EEMD model

Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), proposed by Huang et al. [26] in 1998, is an adaptive time-frequency analysis technique designed for nonlinear and non-stationary signal processing. This method decomposes complex signals into a collection of Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs). An IMF is defined by two requirements: the number of local extrema and zero crossings must be identical or differ by one, and the mean of its upper and lower envelopes, defined by the local maxima and minima, respectively, must be zero [26]. This definition ensures that each IMF represents a simple oscillatory mode,. The Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD) algorithm proposed by Wu and Huang in 2009 [15] was developed to overcome the mode mixing limitation inherent in the original EMD framework. The foundation of the EEMD algorithm is the introduction of white noise with a uniformly distributed spectrum into the time series, which enables the signal to be automatically distributed at appropriate scales [15].

EEMD is generally applicable to nonlinear, non-stationary time series with moderate-to-high noise levels [15]. This method adaptively decomposes a signal into a set of intrinsic mode functions without requiring a predefined basis function [15]. This makes it particularly effective for data characterized by multi-scale oscillations. Although EEMD possesses these advantages, it may be unsuitable under certain conditions. First, when the series length is insufficient, the decomposition tends to become unstable owing to a lack of adequate data points [15]. Second, if the series is stationary and exhibits extremely low noise, the inherent requirement of EEMD to add white noise during the procedure may introduce spurious artifacts into the decomposition [15]. Third, a high proportion of missing values in the time series can compromise both the stability and accuracy of the EEMD.

The EEMD modelling steps are based on [15, 27–30], see Appendix 1).

ES model

The exponential smoothing approach, introduced by Brown in 1956, operates based on the fundamental principle of capturing the level, trend, and seasonality in a time series by applying exponentially decreasing weights to past data points [31–33]. Based on the presence of trends and seasonal components in the time series, the exponential smoothing model produced a series of variants. Simple Exponential Smoothing, Holt’s linear exponential smoothing, and Holt-winter exponential smoothing are three versions that incorporate additional elements to account for trends and seasonality in the data [33]. The ES modelling steps are based on [31–34], see Appendix 1).

SARIMA model

Box and Jenkins proposed the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model in 1970 [35]. The ARIMA model has since become a cornerstone methodology in time-series analysis, leading to its extensive application in infectious disease forecasting. Owing to its capability to capture and predict incidence trends, seasonal fluctuations, and short-term trajectories of diseases such as influenza [13], dengue fever [36], and COVID-19 [37], this model provides valuable insights into public health planning and intervention strategies.

The Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) model is an important extension of the standard ARIMA framework, specifically designed to capture seasonal patterns in time-series data. This model demonstrates superior performance in forecasting seasonal time series, owing to its structural capacity to systematically capture and modeling periodic behavior. The general form of the model is denoted as SARIMA(p, d, q)(P, D,Q)s; the modeling procedure for the SARIMA model comprises four main steps [13, 38–41], see Appendix 1).

In this study, we preprocessed the TB incidence data from Sichuan, China, followed by decomposition using the EEMD model. The decomposed data were then forecasted using both the ES and SARIMA models to identify the optimal modeling approach. Data analysis

MATLAB software (Version R2020b, MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) was used to construct the EEMD model, and SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.1.1 were used to construct the ES and SARIMA models. The level of significance was set at p < 0.05.

This study analyzed monthly TB incidence data from Sichuan Province, China (2006–2020). As shown in Table 1, the mean monthly incidence was 6.2612 per 100,000 population, with a standard deviation of 1.6583. The skewness and kurtosis values indicated a distribution with a slight right skew and a flatter shape. A sustained and stable long-term declining trend was observed throughout the study period with no persistent rebound or resurgence. The seasonal pattern, illustrated in Fig. 2, showed regular annual fluctuations, with the incidence consistently peaking in January and reaching its lowest levels between November and December.

SARIMA model predictions

Following the standard SARIMA modeling procedure, monthly TB incidence data from Sichuan Province, China (2006–2017) were used for model development. The Augmented Dickey Fuller test indicates that the original time series is non stationary. After applying a seasonal difference (D = 1), the series became stationary (ADF statistic = -7.1716, lag order = 5, p = 0.01). The auto.arima function in R selected the optimal model as SARIMA(1,0,0)(0,1,1)12, with AIC = 282.18 and BIC = 293.71. The residuals passed the white noise test (Ljung Box Q = 0.079, p = 0.785).

With the standard deviation of the added Gaussian white noise (Nstd) set to 0.1, and the ensemble number (Ne) set to 100, the decomposition yielded six Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) and one residual. Figure 3 shows the time-series characteristics of the six extracted IMF components. IMF1 and IMF2 did not exhibit regularity, and were identified as random components. IMF3, IMF4, and IMF5 represent the periodic components of the series, whereas IMF6 and the residual (Res) constitute the trend components. Discussion

Our findings demonstrate that the TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China, from 2006 to 2020, exhibited distinct seasonal and long-term periodic patterns. This pattern could be attributed to several factors. First, in terms of geographical factors, Sichuan Province is situated in southwestern China, featuring terrain that is higher in the west and lower in the east, with the western region comprising plateaus and mountains and the eastern area dominated by basins and hills. The geographical characteristics of mountains and basins in Sichuan Province hinder the dispersion of pollutants [42], thereby prolonging the survival of pathogens in air. Second, regarding climatic factors, Sichuan Province encompasses subtropical humid and plateau-frigid zones. Precipitation is scarce in the basin areas during winter, while humidity remains high. Notably, temperatures in the basin reach their lowest annual levels, particularly from January onwards. Activities such as mahjong and card games are popular pastimes among the local population [43]. Consequently, during the winter months, people tend to congregate indoors for recreational activities. Frequently, inadequate ventilation in these indoor spaces may facilitate the transmission of TB during winter; the resulting cases would then be diagnosed and reported in the following weeks to months, contributing to the spring peak in incidence [23]. Furthermore, studies have indicated that individuals with vitamin D deficiency are more susceptible to developing TB [44–47]. The Sichuan Basin experiences a shorter duration and weaker intensity of sunlight during winter, leading to reduced vitamin D synthesis in the human body, thereby diminishing resistance to tuberculosis [48]. Furthermore, holiday-related factors were found to have a significant contribution. The Spring Festival, which occurs in January and February, coincides with the annual TB incidence peak in January and sees customs such as returning to hometowns and traveling [49]. These practices lead to substantial population mobility across and within provinces, intensifying disease transmission [50]. Following tradition, people from both urban and rural areas return to their hometowns to celebrate the New Year with their families, relatives, and friends. As one of China’s most populous provinces, Sichuan has a substantial migrant workforce employed in other regions. The mass return migration of this population during the holiday period, often utilizing overcrowded and poorly ventilated public transportation, substantially increases the risk of TB transmission [51, 52].

Tuberculosis incidence in Sichuan Province from 2006 to 2020 exhibited distinct seasonal and cyclical patterns. Guided by these analytical findings, we prioritized forecasting models specifically for their demonstrated capacity to capture the distinct seasonal and periodic characteristics of TB incidence. Previous studies have confirmed that both SARIMA and ES models deliver reliable performance in predicting infectious disease incidence with seasonal and periodic characteristics [7, 8, 53]. For instance, SARIMA models specialize in handling seasonal and periodic time series [7], with the specific ability to capture intricate seasonal variations and autocorrelation relationships. ES models demonstrate effective predictive capability for seasonal and periodic time series, with advantages such as a straightforward structure, low modeling complexity, and minimal parameter requirements [33]. However, both SARIMA and ES models have inherent limitations: SARIMA requires strict time-series stationarity [41, 54], and differencing to achieve stationarity may lead to the loss of information from the original series; ES models are sensitive to missing values and outliers while showing limited flexibility in long-term trend forecasting [55]. Therefore, addressing the complementary limitations of the SARIMA and ES models constitutes a crucial methodological challenge for our research approach.

The findings of our study demonstrate that TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China during the 2006–2020 period exhibits distinct seasonal and cyclical characteristics. These patterns may also contain non-stationary and nonlinear features, owing to stochastic fluctuations arising from complex factors, including policy interventions and climatic conditions. To address the limitations of the existing forecasting methods, we employed the EEMD method. EEMD is an adaptive data-driven decomposition method [15, 26]. Its key strength lies in its powerful, self-adaptive, and multi-scale signal decomposition capability [15]. This approach can break down complex time series into multiple intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) with frequencies ranging from high to low and a residual term [15]. In our study, the EEMD method successfully captured the multi-scale characteristics and inherent fluctuation patterns in the tuberculosis incidence time series in Sichuan Province, China, from 2006 to 2020, including its stochastic, seasonal, cyclical, and trend components. Therefore, we applied EEMD to obtain six IMFs and modeled them using the optimal SARIMA and ES models. This hybrid approach achieved enhanced accuracy in predicting TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China during 2006–2020, thereby providing deeper insights into the inherently complex dynamics of TB transmission.

However, the applicability of the EEMD depends on several key conditions. The method is best suited for non-stationary or noisy time-series data and requires a sample size that includes at least two full cycles to ensure reliable decomposition results [15]. If these requirements are not met, the decomposition results may be unreliable and pseudocomponents may be generated [56].

Furthermore, the training errors were consistently lower than the test errors. This phenomenon can be primarily attributed to two factors. First, the tuberculosis incidence data were characterized by a clear downward trend accompanied by periodic fluctuations. This consistent pattern enabled the models to effectively capture the underlying data structure during the training phase, thereby enhancing forecasting accuracy during testing. Second, owing to the relatively limited sample size and the absence of extreme values or outliers in the testing set (covering the period 2018–2020), the calculated error metrics are likely to be further reduced. Building on this observation, a comparative analysis of the three forecasting models reveals that the EEMD-ES-SARIMA hybrid model achieves superior predictive performance over both the single SARIMA and single ES models, thereby confirming the effectiveness of EEMD in improving forecasting outcomes.

Based on the seasonal and cyclical characteristics of the TB incidence in Sichuan Province and the predictive results obtained in this study, targeted preventive measures can be implemented to support TB control efforts. First, during the winter and spring seasons, provincial and municipal health departments should optimize the allocation of respiratory healthcare resources in advance to ensure adequate medical capacity for TB patients. Second, relevant government agencies should issue early warnings to the public regarding the risk of TB transmission, enabling individuals to take personal protective measures. The public should be advised to wear masks in enclosed public venues to reduce the risk of transmission of TB. Third, during the New Year and Spring Festival holidays, health departments at the provincial and municipal levels should coordinate with transportation and meteorological authorities to address jointly the potential spread of TB associated with large-scale population movements during holiday periods.

Although our study demonstrated the effectiveness of the EEMD-ES-SARIMA model in forecasting TB incidence in Sichuan Province, China, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the data we collected suffered from a timeliness lag, as the TB incidence data for Sichuan Province, China, are only available up to 2020, which consequently constrains the predictive models we developed. In the future, we will continuously supplement and update the dataset to provide reliable data support for building more accurate forecasting models. Second, each model has inherent limitations and our study is no exception. Because real-world infectious disease incidence involves highly complex factors, our EEMD-ES-SARIMA model only provides predictions from a data-driven perspective, without incorporating other complex elements such as socio-economic, [57, 58] meteorological [59], and environmental [60] dimensions. Consequently, our predictions are subject to certain constraints. In future studies, we plan to enhance our forecasting approach by appropriately integrating these additional factors to achieve more accurate predictions, thereby providing better support for public health policy formulation. Third, we did not systematically assess the stability of the selected hyperparameters across different training data configurations, which limits the generalizability of our findings. In future work, we plan to investigate this issue using cross-validation techniques to validate the robustness of the selected models further.

Conclusions

This study identified pronounced seasonal and cyclical patterns in TB incidence in Sichuan Province from 2006 to 2020, with peak incidence observed during winter and spring. The proposed novel adaptive EEMD-ES-SARIMA hybrid model, which introduced both ES and SARIMA as competing candidates within an EEMD framework for the first time, achieved satisfactory forecasting performance. The model decomposes the original time series into multiple intrinsic mode functions (IMFs), independently selects the optimal ES or SARIMA model for each IMF based on information criteria such as AIC, BIC, or RMSE, and then linearly sums the predicted values of all the components. In the test set, compared with the optimal single SARIMA and ES models, the hybrid model reduced the RMSE by 37.5% and 43.6% and reduced the MAPE by 35.3% and 38.2%, respectively, indicating a substantial predictive advantage over individual models.

The adaptive hybrid framework proposed in this study is the first to integrate EEMD decomposition with a competitive per-IMF selection between ES and SARIMA, effectively handling non-stationary, multi-scale oscillatory infectious disease time series, and provides empirical evidence for enhancing the forecasting accuracy of TB incidence. From a practical application perspective, attention should first be paid to the applicability conditions of EEMD, and the decision to adopt this framework should be guided by the actual characteristics of the time series under investigation. As the first application of this adaptive EEMD-ES-SARIMA model to TB incidence forecasting in Sichuan Province, China, supports public health authorities in predicting future incidence peaks in advance and optimizing resource allocation.
