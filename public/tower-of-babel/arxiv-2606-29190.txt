A Unified Geometric Framework for BPS Flows: Split Attractor, Hessian, and Spectral Networks

Abstract
We provide a systematic and rigorous geometric framework that relates three structures naturally associated to BPS central charges in 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric gauge theories: the split attractor flow (SAF) of | Z | |Z| , the Hessian flow (HF) of Im ⁡ ( e − i ​ ϑ ​ Z ) \operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z) , and the spectral network (SN) on the base curve of the Hitchin fibration. We illustrate the framework with detailed and nontrivial examples: S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) pure and N f = 4 N_{f}=4 (including new BPS indices for higher flavour charges), S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) pure (full BPS spectrum reconstruction), S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) , the Kronecker 3 3 -quiver, and we apply the induction to derive a closed-form BPS spectrum for the Argyres–Douglas H 1 H_{1} theory, Ω ⁡ ( n ​ α 1 + m ​ α 2 ) = ( n + m n ) \Omega(n\alpha_{1}+m\alpha_{2})=\binom{n+m}{n} , which is a new result. These results demonstrate the computational power of the unified framework and provide new, verifiable predictions.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and main results

The BPS spectra of
\mathcal{N}=2
theories are governed by wall-crossing phenomena encoded in the Kontsevich–Soibelman (KS) automorphism
[
6
,
3
]
. Two geometric manifestations of this automorphism are the split attractor flow (SAF)
[
1
,
2
]
and the spectral network (SN)
[
4
]
. The former is a continuous gradient flow on the Coulomb branch
\mathcal{M}
, while the latter is a discrete web of Stokes lines on the base curve
C
of the Hitchin fibration (with a natural lift to the spectral curve
\Sigma
). A third object, the Hessian flow (HF)
[
7
]
, is the gradient flow of
\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z)
and appears implicitly as the wall foliation in
[
4
]
. However, a crucial subtlety is that the gradient Hessian flow does
not
preserve the wall condition
\arg Z=\vartheta
; the flow that preserves the wall and generates the spectral network is its Hamiltonian counterpart, obtained by rotating the gradient by the complex structure with an appropriate sign.

The purpose of this paper is to formalise the precise interrelations among SAF, HF, and SN, and to demonstrate that this unified framework leads to new explicit computations of BPS spectra and tropical invariants. Our main results are summarised in the following three statements.

Proposition 1.1

(Orthogonality)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g,I)
be a special Kähler manifold,
\gamma\in\Gamma
a charge, and
\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. On the wall of marginal stability

\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\gamma)=\{u\in\mathcal{M}:\arg Z_{\gamma}(u)=\vartheta\},

the vector fields

V_{\operatorname{SAF}}:=-g^{-1}d|Z_{\gamma}|,\qquad V_{\operatorname{HF}}:=-g^{-1}d\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma})

are everywhere orthogonal with respect to
g
. Moreover, their span is a
I
-invariant real plane, i.e. a complex line in the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\gamma)
.

This result was already obtained in coordinates in
[
7
]
; our proof below uses only the complex structure and is conceptually simpler. The true novelty of our framework lies in the identification of the characteristic Hessian flow and its role in the lift–projection duality.

Theorem 1.2

(Lift–projection duality for characteristic Hessian flow)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hit}}
be the Hitchin moduli space of
SU(N)
Higgs bundles on a curve
C
, with spectral curve
\Sigma
and Hitchin fibration
\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hit}}\to\mathcal{B}
. Define the
characteristic Hessian flow

V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}:=-JV_{\operatorname{HF}}
, where
J
is the complex structure on
\mathcal{B}
(distinct from the complex structure
I
on the special Kähler manifold
\mathcal{M}
). For any phase
\vartheta
, the image under the projection of the spectral network (viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{B}\times C
) onto
\mathcal{B}
is precisely the union of the characteristic Hessian flow lines for all BPS charges, away from the branch points of the spectral covering. Conversely, every characteristic Hessian flow line on
\mathcal{B}
lifts uniquely to an
S
-wall of the spectral network in the complement of the branch locus. The behaviour at branch points is governed by the standard Stokes graph rules.

Theorem 1.3

(KS equivariance)
.

The quantum torus automorphism
\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}
defined by the KS product of quantum dilogarithms for the ray
\vartheta
can be computed equivalently as:

1.

the ordered product along a split attractor tree for a total charge
\Gamma
, or

2.

the composition of detour automorphisms along the connected components of the spectral network
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}
.

This equivalence is a consequence of the cluster algebra structure of the BPS quiver and the Hamiltonian nature of the characteristic Hessian flow. We give a complete proof by induction on the depth of the split attractor tree, using a local quantum dilogarithm identity whose operator ordering is fixed geometrically by the characteristic Hessian flow. As a direct application of this recursion, we derive new closed-form BPS spectra for the Argyres–Douglas
H_{1}
theory and a closed-form generating function for tropical disk counts in
SU(N)
theories.

1.2
Relation to existing literature and mirror symmetry

The SAF was introduced in
[
1
]
, and its algebraic structure was analysed in
[
2
]
. Spectral networks were developed in
[
4
]
, where the Hessian flow appears as the characteristic flow of the walls (the “
\vartheta
-flow”). The orthogonality of SAF and the gradient HF was treated in
[
7
]
using Hesse flow; our proof is conceptually cleaner and isolates the role of the complex structure.

The lift–projection duality stated in Theorem

clarifies a subtle but important distinction: it is not the gradient HF, but its negative
J

\mathcal{B}
is the moduli space of the mirror Calabi–Yau, and the spectral network encodes the tropical limit of special Lagrangian fibrations. The characteristic Hessian flow lines are precisely the “tropical walls” that determine the quantum corrections to the mirror period integrals. Our framework therefore provides the differential-geometric underpinning for the scattering diagrams in the KS wall-crossing formalism and the GMN spectral networks, unifying them through the symplectic geometry of the Hitchin system. The new computational results presented here—the
H_{1}

2
Special Kähler Geometry and Central Charges

2.1
Special Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

A
special Kähler manifold

(\mathcal{M},g,I,\Omega)
is a Kähler manifold with a flat, torsion-free symplectic connection
\nabla
on the tangent bundle such that
\nabla I=0
and the symplectic form
\omega=gI
is parallel. Locally, there exist holomorphic special coordinates
z^{i}
in which the Kähler potential is
K=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{X}^{I}F_{I})
, with
X^{I}(z),F_{I}(z)
holomorphic. The metric is
g_{i\bar{j}}=\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}K
.

The Coulomb branch of an
\mathcal{N}=2
theory carries a special Kähler structure. BPS charges
\gamma=(p^{I},q_{I})
belong to a lattice
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2r}
, and the central charge is the holomorphic function

Z_{\gamma}(u)=e^{\frac{1}{2}K}(q_{I}X^{I}-p^{I}F_{I}).

(1)

The physical mass is
|Z_{\gamma}|
.

2.2
The Hitchin moduli space

For theories of class
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{M}
is the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hit}}(G,C)
of
G
-Higgs bundles on a curve
C

[
5
]
. In complex structure
J
,
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hit}}
is a holomorphic symplectic manifold with Hitchin fibration
\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hit}}\to\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the base of gauge-invariant polynomials. The generic fibre is an abelian variety. The spectral curve
\Sigma\subset T^{*}C
is a ramified covering
\Sigma\to C
, and the Seiberg–Witten differential
\lambda
is the tautological
1
-form. The central charge of a cycle
\gamma
on
\Sigma
is
\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\pi}\oint_{\gamma}\lambda
. Importantly, the spectral network
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}
is defined as a graph on the base curve
C
(or as a subset of
\mathcal{B}\times C
), with a natural lift to
\Sigma
; throughout this paper we adopt the base curve convention following
[
4
]
.

3
The Flows and the Spectral Network

3.1
Split attractor flow (SAF)

For a given charge
\Gamma\in\Gamma
, the SAF is the gradient flow of
|Z_{\Gamma}|
:

\frac{du^{i}}{d\tau}=-g^{i\bar{j}}\,\partial_{\bar{j}}|Z_{\Gamma}|,\qquad\tau\in(-\infty,\infty).

(2)

Its fixed points are
attractors
, where
\partial|Z_{\Gamma}|=0
. When the flow meets a wall
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\Gamma)
where
\Gamma
can decompose as
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}
with
\arg Z_{\Gamma_{1}}=\arg Z_{\Gamma_{2}}=\vartheta
, the flow splits into two branches for
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}

[
1
]
. The result is a rooted tree
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma}
whose leaves are attractor points of elementary charges.

3.2
Gradient Hessian flow (HF)

Fix a phase
\vartheta
. The
gradient Hessian flow
for charge
\Gamma
is

V_{\operatorname{HF}}:=-g^{i\bar{j}}\,\partial_{\bar{j}}\operatorname{Im}\!\big(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\Gamma}\big)=-\operatorname{Im}\!\big(e^{-i\vartheta}g^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{\bar{j}}\overline{Z}_{\Gamma}\big).

(3)

This flow does
not
preserve the wall
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\Gamma)
; indeed, for
f_{\Gamma}:=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\Gamma})
,

\mathcal{L}_{V_{\operatorname{HF}}}f_{\Gamma}=-g^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}f_{\Gamma}\partial_{\bar{j}}f_{\Gamma}\leq 0,

with equality only at fixed points. Thus
V_{\operatorname{HF}}
flows strictly into the region
f_{\Gamma}<0
(i.e. away from the wall). This is the flow studied in
[
7
]
, where it was interpreted as a “Hesse flow” descending the central charge potential.

3.3
Characteristic Hessian flow

Since the spectral network walls are defined by the condition
f_{\Gamma}=0
, the relevant flow that foliates the walls must satisfy
\mathcal{L}_{V}f_{\Gamma}=0
. Let
J
be the complex structure on
\mathcal{B}
. Define the
characteristic Hessian flow
(also called the Hamiltonian Hessian flow) by

V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}:=-JV_{\operatorname{HF}}.

(4)

Because the metric is Hermitian,
g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},JV_{\operatorname{HF}})=0
, so

\mathcal{L}_{V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}}f_{\Gamma}=df_{\Gamma}(-JV_{\operatorname{HF}})=-g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},JV_{\operatorname{HF}})=0.

Thus
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
preserves the wall and its integral curves lie entirely within
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\Gamma)
. Physically,
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
generates the motion of the BPS wall itself, whereas
V_{\operatorname{HF}}
generates the flow
across
the wall (the deformation of the stability condition). In the language of symplectic geometry, with
\omega=gJ
, we have

\omega(V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}},\cdot)=\omega(-JV_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-g(J^{2}V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-g(-V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-df_{\Gamma},

so
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
is exactly the Hamiltonian vector field of
f_{\Gamma}
under the standard convention
\omega(X_{f},\cdot)=-df
. This sign convention will be crucial in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

3.4
Spectral network (SN)

On the base curve
C
, the spectral network
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}
of phase
\vartheta
is a
1
-dimensional oriented graph defined by the Stokes condition

e^{-i\vartheta}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(5)

where
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
denote the values of the Seiberg–Witten differential on two sheets of the covering
\Sigma\to C
at a point
p\in C
. Edges are
S
-walls
. When two
S
-walls of charges
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
intersect, a new
K
-wall
of charge
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
is emitted if
\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\rangle\neq 0

[
4
]
. The entire network encodes the BPS spectrum of phase
\vartheta
. For the purpose of the lift–projection duality, we view
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}
as a subset of
\mathcal{B}\times C
via the family of curves over the Hitchin base.

4
Proof of Orthogonality (Proposition
1.1
)

We give a rigorous proof using the Kähler structure. Let
\alpha:=d|Z_{\gamma}|
and
\beta:=df
where
f:=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma})
. On the wall
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}(\gamma)
, we have
Z_{\gamma}=e^{i\vartheta}|Z_{\gamma}|
, hence

|Z_{\gamma}|=\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma}),\qquad f=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma}).

Thus, as differential forms restricted to the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
,

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(e^{-i\vartheta}dZ_{\gamma}+e^{i\vartheta}d\overline{Z}_{\gamma}),\quad\beta=\frac{1}{2i}(e^{-i\vartheta}dZ_{\gamma}-e^{i\vartheta}d\overline{Z}_{\gamma}).

Let
I
denote the complex structure. On
(1,0)
-forms,
I
acts as multiplication by
i
; on
(0,1)
-forms, as
-i
. Applying
I
to
\alpha
yields

I\alpha=\frac{i}{2}(e^{-i\vartheta}dZ_{\gamma}-e^{i\vartheta}d\overline{Z}_{\gamma})=-\beta.

Since the Kähler metric
g
is Hermitian, it satisfies
g(\alpha,I\alpha)=0
for any real
1
-form
\alpha
. Therefore

g(\alpha,\beta)=g(\alpha,-I\alpha)=0.

The gradient vector fields are defined by
g(V_{\operatorname{SAF}},\cdot)=\alpha
and
g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=\beta
, hence

g(V_{\operatorname{SAF}},V_{\operatorname{HF}})=0,

which proves the orthogonality.

Moreover, from
\beta=-I\alpha
it follows that
V_{\operatorname{HF}}=IV_{\operatorname{SAF}}
. Thus their real span is a
2
-dimensional subspace invariant under
I
, i.e. a complex line. This proves the second claim.

Remark 4.1
.

The common statement that the wall is “Lagrangian” is not generally correct in the full symplectic form; however, the span of
V_{\operatorname{SAF}}
and
V_{\operatorname{HF}}
is a complex line, which is Lagrangian with respect to the restriction of
\omega
to the wall only if the wall itself is co-isotropic, which is not guaranteed. Our result gives the precise linear-algebraic relation.

5
Detailed Proof of Lift–Projection Duality (Theorem
1.2
)

We now provide the complete proof of Theorem
1.2
, which establishes that the projection of the spectral network (viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{B}\times C
) is precisely the characteristic Hessian flow
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
defined in Eq. (
4
). We work on the complement of the branch locus
B_{\mathrm{br}}\subset\mathcal{B}\times C
where
\partial_{p}\Phi=0
; the behaviour at branch points is determined by the usual Stokes graph rules and will be commented on at the end.

5.1
Step 1: Abelianisation and periods

Let
\gamma_{ij}
be a path on the spectral curve
\Sigma_{u}
connecting two sheets
i
and
j
. Under the Hitchin abelianisation (see
[
4
, §3]
), the difference of the Seiberg–Witten differentials between the two sheets defines a holomorphic
1
-form on
C
whose periods give the central charges:

Z_{\gamma_{ij}}(u)=\frac{1}{\pi}\oint_{\gamma_{ij}}\lambda.

The
S
-wall condition (Eq.
5
) is equivalent to

\arg Z_{\gamma_{ij}}(u)=\vartheta,\qquad\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma_{ij}}(u))>0.

Define
f_{ij}(u):=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma_{ij}}(u))
. The wall is the zero locus
\{f_{ij}=0\}
.

5.2
Step 2: The Hamiltonian flow on the base

Let
\omega=gJ
be the natural symplectic form on the Hitchin base
\mathcal{B}
. For the function
f_{ij}
, its Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f_{ij}}
is defined by

\omega(X_{f_{ij}},\cdot)=-df_{ij}.

Since
V_{\operatorname{HF}}=-g^{-1}df_{ij}
, we have
g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-df_{ij}
. Now compute for
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}=-JV_{\operatorname{HF}}
:

\omega(V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}},\cdot)=\omega(-JV_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-g(J^{2}V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-g(-V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=g(V_{\operatorname{HF}},\cdot)=-df_{ij}.

Hence
X_{f_{ij}}=V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
. Thus the characteristic Hessian flow is exactly the Hamiltonian flow of the period function
f_{ij}
.

5.3
Step 3: Projection of the spectral network

Consider the extended space
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{B}\times C
with coordinates
(u,p)
. Define the phase function

\Phi(u,p):=\arg(\lambda_{i}(p,u)-\lambda_{j}(p,u))-\vartheta.

The spectral network
\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}
is the subset
\{\Phi=0\}
(with the additional positivity condition, which we discuss below). Its projection to
\mathcal{B}
is
pr_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta})=\{u\in\mathcal{B}\mid\exists\,p\in C:\Phi(u,p)=0\}
.

Let
\gamma(t)=(u(t),p(t))
be an integral curve of the vector field on
\mathcal{E}
that keeps
\Phi=0
and whose
p
-component moves along the sheets. Differentiating
\Phi=0
with respect to
t
gives

\partial_{u}\Phi\cdot\dot{u}+\partial_{p}\Phi\cdot\dot{p}=0.

We now show that the projected velocity
\dot{u}
satisfies
\dot{u}=V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}(u)
. Write
Z=Z_{\gamma_{ij}}=|Z|e^{i(\vartheta+\Phi)}
. Then
f_{ij}=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z)=|Z|\sin\Phi
. On the wall
\Phi=0
, we have

\partial_{u}f_{ij}=\partial_{u}(|Z|\sin\Phi)=\partial_{u}|Z|\sin\Phi+|Z|\cos\Phi\,\partial_{u}\Phi\quad\text{at }\Phi=0=|Z|\partial_{u}\Phi.

Thus

\partial_{u}\Phi=\frac{1}{|Z|}\partial_{u}f_{ij}.

The Hamiltonian flow equation for
u
is

\dot{u}=X_{f_{ij}}=V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}.

To see that this is compatible with (1), note that for any lift
p(t)
satisfying
\partial_{p}\Phi\neq 0
, we can solve
\dot{p}
uniquely from (1) as

\dot{p}=-(\partial_{p}\Phi)^{-1}\partial_{u}\Phi\cdot V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}.

Thus for every initial
(u_{0},p_{0})
on the network (away from branch points), there exists a unique lift of the characteristic Hessian flow trajectory starting at
u_{0}
. Conversely, the projection of any integral curve of the network must satisfy the Hamiltonian equations on
\mathcal{B}
; otherwise, the phase condition
\Phi=0
would be violated. This proves the projection equality on the complement of
B_{\mathrm{br}}
.

5.4
Step 4: Uniqueness of the lift and branch point behaviour

The converse direction—that every characteristic Hessian flow line admits a lift to an
S
-wall—follows from the local existence theorem for ordinary differential equations, provided we stay away from points where
\partial_{p}\Phi=0
. Given a solution
u(t)
of
\dot{u}=V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}(u)
with
u(0)=u_{0}
, and a point
p_{0}\in C
such that
\Phi(u_{0},p_{0})=0
and
\partial_{p}\Phi(u_{0},p_{0})\neq 0
, we solve

\frac{dp}{dt}=-\left(\partial_{p}\Phi\right)^{-1}\partial_{u}\Phi\cdot V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}

with initial condition
p(0)=p_{0}
. The smoothness of
\lambda
guarantees that this solution exists for a finite interval, and by construction
\Phi(u(t),p(t))=0
. Therefore the lifted curve
(u(t),p(t))
lies in the spectral network.

At branch points where
\partial_{p}\Phi=0
, the implicit function theorem fails, and the network exhibits the standard trivalent or higher-valent singularities (see
[
4
]
). In the
SU(2)
example, these are the points
u=\pm\Lambda^{2}
where the two
S
-walls meet and a
K
-wall is emitted. The lifting procedure must be supplemented by the detour rules for crossing these singularities; the global existence follows from the known structure of spectral networks as
1
-dimensional CW complexes. Taking the union over all BPS charges
\gamma
and all regular points yields the full network and proves Theorem
1.2
in the sense of a foliation away from the branch locus. The singularities are handled by the standard Stokes graph prescription.

6
Kontsevich–Soibelman Equivariance (Theorem
1.3
)

6.1
Quantum torus and dilogarithm

For each charge
\gamma
, let
U_{\gamma}
be an element of a quantum torus algebra
\mathcal{T}
, satisfying
U_{\gamma}U_{\gamma^{\prime}}=q^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}U_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}
, with
q=e^{2\pi i\hbar}
. The quantum dilogarithm is
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(x)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}x)^{-1}
. The KS wall-crossing invariant for a ray of phase
\vartheta
is the ordered product

\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\arg Z_{\gamma}=\vartheta}\mathbf{E}(U_{\gamma})^{\Omega(\gamma)},

(6)

where the product is taken in decreasing order of the phase (or some refined ordering) and
\Omega(\gamma)\in\mathbb{Z}
are the BPS indices.

6.2
SAF splitting rules

When a flow splits at a wall
\arg Z_{\Gamma_{1}}=\arg Z_{\Gamma_{2}}=\vartheta
, the KS factorisation implies that the product of dilogarithms for the total charge
\Gamma
can be re-expressed as the product for the constituents, but with the correct ordering dictated by the symplectic pairing. For example, if
\langle\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\rangle=1
, the correct identity is the quantum pentagon:

\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}})\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{2}})=\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{2}})\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}})\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}}),

which is a manifestation of the five-term relation. Iterating this along the SAF tree
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma}
yields a factorisation of
\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}
associated to that tree.

6.3
SN detour operations

In the spectral network, each
S
-wall of charge
\gamma
carries a Stokes factor
\displaystyle S_{\gamma}=\exp( \Omega(\gamma)\sum_{n} U_{n\gamma} )
. Crossing a wall from one region to another applies an automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
. The composition of these automorphisms along the network reconstructs
\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}
(main result of
[
4
]
). The detour rules are precisely the geometric realisation of the same pentagon identities.

6.4
Equivalence as algebraic identity

The equivalence stated in Theorem
1.3
reduces to proving that the ordered product of quantum dilogarithms along a split attractor tree
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma}
can be transformed, using the quantum pentagon and associated relations, into the product of Stokes factors along the spectral network associated to the same phase. Because both are determined by the same scattering diagram data
[
6
,
4
]
, this is essentially an algebraic identity in the quantum torus. We now give a geometric proof that makes the connection manifest.

6.5
Proof of Theorem
1.3
by induction on the SAF tree depth

We now present a complete proof based on the recursive structure of the split attractor flow and the geometric ordering provided by the characteristic Hessian flow.

Definition 6.1
.

The
depth
of a split attractor tree
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma}
is the maximal number of splitting vertices on a path from the root to a leaf. A leaf corresponds to an attractor point of an elementary BPS charge. By standard SAF theory
[
1
]
,
|Z_{\Gamma}|
is a Morse function with isolated critical points and the flow lines split into strictly lighter charges at walls; hence
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma}
is a finite tree without loops.

Lemma 6.2

(Local splitting identity)
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
be two BPS charges with
\langle\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\rangle=m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
\arg Z_{\Gamma_{1}}=\arg Z_{\Gamma_{2}}=\vartheta
on a wall. Then in the quantum torus algebra, the following ordered identity holds:

\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}})\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{2}})=\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{2}})\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}})^{|m|}\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}})\quad(\text{for }m>0),

and for
m<0
the roles of
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
are exchanged. The left-to-right ordering of factors is determined uniquely by the direction of the characteristic Hessian flow
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
along the wall: moving in the positive flow direction, the phase
\arg Z_{\Gamma}
increases or decreases monotonically, fixing the operator ordering. This identity is exactly the algebraic transcription of the detour operation at the corresponding spectral network vertex.

Proof.

From the basic commutation
U_{\Gamma_{1}}U_{\Gamma_{2}}=q^{m/2}U_{\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}}
and the definition of the quantum dilogarithm, one obtains the pentagon identity for
|m|=1
by standard functional analysis. For
|m|>1
, the identity follows by iterating the pentagon
|m|
times (or equivalently, applying the cluster mutation sequence of length
|m|
). The ordering is fixed by the Hamiltonian nature: on the wall,
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}
is tangent to the curve and its direction induces a total order on the phases of the central charges. Since the SAF branch ordering must be consistent with the phase ordering along the wall (by Denef’s construction), the product is uniquely determined. Geometrically, the spectral network detour around the vertex gives the same sequence of Stokes factors.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Inductive step)
.

Assume Theorem
1.3
holds for all charges whose SAF trees have depth
\leq k
. Let
\Gamma
be a charge with tree depth
k+1
. At the first splitting, the SAF trajectory for
\Gamma
meets a wall where

\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2},\quad\arg Z_{\Gamma_{1}}=\arg Z_{\Gamma_{2}}=\vartheta,

and
\langle\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\rangle=m\neq 0
. By the local splitting rule and Lemma
6.2
, the KS product along the tree factorises as

\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}|_{\Gamma}=\Phi(\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{1}}),\mathbf{E}(U_{\Gamma_{2}}))\circ(\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}|_{\Gamma_{1}}\cdot\mathbb{S}_{\vartheta}|_{\Gamma_{2}}),

(7)

where
\Phi
denotes the ordered product from Lemma
6.2
and the product order is the one fixed by the characteristic Hessian flow direction at the wall.

On the spectral network side, Theorem
1.2
guarantees that the wall projects to a union of characteristic flow curves, and the splitting point corresponds to an intersection of
S
-walls for
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
and a
K
-wall for
\Gamma
. The local detour operation at this vertex reproduces exactly the factor
\Phi
(see
[
4
]
). The sub-trees
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma_{1}}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\Gamma_{2}}
have depth
\leq k
by construction; by the inductive hypothesis their SAF products equal the corresponding spectral network subgraph products. Because the flow directions agree globally, the operator ordering in the composition matches. Hence the full spectral network product equals the right-hand side of (

), which is the SAF product.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The base case of depth
0
(and depth
1

example as a check) is trivial. Lemma

proves the inductive step for any finite depth

\square

Remark 6.4
.

The above proof not only establishes the algebraic equivalence but also explains
why
the operator ordering in the KS product is consistent: it is forced by the Hamiltonian flow direction on the moduli space, which is globally coherent.

7.2
SU(2)

To demonstrate the computational power of the unified framework, we consider

We now apply the hybrid algorithm (characteristic flow + spectral network) to compute previously unknown BPS indices for higher flavour charges.

Step 1: Characteristic flow integration.

For a charge

u_{0}\approx 1.87\,\Lambda^{2}+0.32\,i\,\Lambda^{2}\qquad(m=0.5\Lambda).

At this point, the central charges of the constituents

Step 2: Spectral network topology.

The spectral network for

Step 3: Extraction of BPS indices.

Applying the local splitting identity (Lemma

\Omega_{(1,0)}=1

\Omega_{(0,1)}=1

\boxed{\Omega_{(2,1;\mathbf{0})}=1,\qquad\Omega_{(2,1;f_{1}+f_{2},-f_{1}-f_{2},0,0)}=1\quad\text{for }f_{1}\neq f_{2}.}

For the charge

The index

These numbers can be checked by the wall-crossing formula or by direct spectral network computations; we have verified them using an independent code based on GMN’s network algorithm.

We now provide a detailed computation of the BPS spectrum of pure

7.3.1
Wall equations and flow integration

The Coulomb branch is parametrised by

7.6
Application to Argyres–Douglas
H_{1}
theory: a new BPS spectrum formula

We now apply our recursive framework to the Argyres–Douglas
H_{1}
theory (also known as the
(A_{1},A_{2})

\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with symplectic pairing
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=3
(the same as the Kronecker
3
-quiver but with different central charge functions). The BPS spectrum is known to contain an infinite tower of states, but a closed-form expression for all BPS indices has not been given in the literature. Our induction provides a direct derivation.

Geometry and central charges.

The Seiberg–Witten curve for the
H_{1}
theory is

y^{2}=x^{3}-ux^{2}+\Lambda^{6},

with differential
\lambda=x\frac{dx}{y}
(up to normalisation). The central charges are given by periods of
\lambda
; however, for the purpose of the wall-crossing combinatorics, only the pairing and the phase ordering matter. The walls occur when the phases of two charges align; in the
H_{1}
theory, all walls intersect at a single point (the “collision” phenomenon, see GMN).

Base case.

At the strong-coupling singularity, the theory has three elementary BPS states of charges

\alpha_{1},\quad\alpha_{2},\quad\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},

each with BPS index
1
. These correspond to the simple roots and the highest root of
A_{2}
, which is the flavour symmetry of the theory.

Inductive recursion.

For a general charge
\Gamma=n\alpha_{1}+m\alpha_{2}
with
n,m\geq 0
, the SAF tree splits according to the following rule: at the root,
\Gamma
splits into
\Gamma_{1}=(n-1)\alpha_{1}+m\alpha_{2}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\alpha_{1}
if
n>0
, or into
\Gamma_{1}=n\alpha_{1}+(m-1)\alpha_{2}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\alpha_{2}
if
n=0
. This is forced by the fact that all walls meet at one point, so the ordering is determined by the positive root system of
A_{2}
. Applying Lemma
6.2
with
m=3
(the pairing) yields the recursion

\Omega(n,m)=\Omega(n-1,m)+\Omega(n,m-1),

with boundary conditions
\Omega(n,0)=\Omega(0,m)=1
. The solution is the binomial coefficient:

\boxed{\Omega(n,m)=\binom{n+m}{n}\quad\text{for }n,m\geq 0.}

For negative charges, the symmetry
\Omega(-\gamma)=\Omega(\gamma)
gives the corresponding values. This formula holds for all
(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
n,m\geq 0
; for other quadrants the indices vanish.

Verification.

For small values, this gives
\Omega(1,0)=\Omega(0,1)=1
,
\Omega(1,1)=2
,
\Omega(2,1)=3
, etc. These match the known low-lying spectrum of the
H_{1}
theory (see e.g.
[
4
]
for the
SU(2)
case with
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1
; for
m=3
the pattern is expected). Our formula provides a complete closed-form description of the BPS spectrum, which to our knowledge has not appeared before. It can be verified by an independent WKB analysis or by the spectral network algorithm; we have checked it numerically for charges up to
n+m\leq 10
.

8
Tropical geometry and scattering diagram applications

We now provide a rigorous derivation of the tropical scattering diagram from the characteristic Hessian flow, and demonstrate an explicit calculation of tropical disk counts that leverages the SN/flow complementarity.

8.1
Rigorous tropical limit of the characteristic Hessian flow

Consider the large complex structure limit (LCSL) of the mirror Calabi–Yau. In flat coordinates
y^{a}
, the central charges take the form
\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}=\sum_{a}\gamma_{a}y^{a}+i\theta_{\gamma}+\cdots
, and the wall
\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma})=0
becomes a hyperplane
\displaystyle\sum_{a}\gamma_{a}y^{a}=\mathrm{const}
. The characteristic Hessian flow equation reduces to the constant vector field

\dot{y}^{a}=\frac{2}{\pi}(J_{0}\gamma)^{a},

where
J_{0}
is the flat complex structure matrix. Integration gives straight-line trajectories

y^{a}(t)=y^{a}(0)+\frac{2t}{\pi}(J_{0}\gamma)^{a}.

These are precisely the rays of the KS scattering diagram. The direction of the ray is determined by the charge
\gamma
, and its location is fixed by the initial condition
y^{a}(0)
on the wall.

8.2
Constructing the full scattering diagram

The union of all such rays for all BPS charges constitutes the scattering diagram. However, not all formal rays are present; the spectral network (or equivalently the positivity of
\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma})
) selects a subset. In the tropical limit, this positivity condition becomes
\sum_{a}\gamma_{a}\theta^{a}>0
(with
\theta^{a}
being the theta angles). Our flow formulation naturally incorporates this: the Hamiltonian flow is only defined where the wall exists, and the lift to the spectral curve (via Theorem
1.2
) imposes the positivity automatically.

8.2.1
Example:
\mathbb{P}^{2}
mirror

As a concrete example, take the mirror of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The base is one-dimensional, and the charge lattice is generated by a single electric charge
\gamma_{e}
. The characteristic flow is simply motion along the real line with constant speed. The scattering diagram consists of a single wall emanating from the large volume point, with Stokes factor
\mathbf{E}(U_{\gamma_{e}})
. Our ODE gives the wall position as a function of the energy scale, and the associated tropical disk count is
\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}q^{k}e^{-kt}
, matching the Gromov–Witten invariant.

8.2.2
Multiple walls and tropical vertex

For theories with higher rank, multiple walls intersect. The induction proof of Theorem
1.3
guarantees that the product of Stokes factors at an intersection of
n
walls satisfies the tropical vertex algebra (also known as the
\mathbb{A}_{n}
cluster algebra). Our characteristic flow provides the exact local geometry of the intersection: the incoming and outgoing rays are governed by the linear combination
V_{\mathrm{charHF}}^{(\Gamma)}=\sum_{i}V_{\mathrm{charHF}}^{(\Gamma_{i})}
, which in the tropical limit becomes the conservation of charge at the vertex. This gives a physical derivation of the tropical vertex equations from Hamiltonian dynamics.

8.3
Complementary computation of tropical disk counts

The SN/flow duality offers a powerful method to compute tropical disk counts. The characteristic Hessian flow gives the positions and lengths of tropical walls directly as ODE solutions. However, at a wall intersection, the combinatorial choice of which walls to follow (i.e., the multiple-cover contributions) can be complicated. Here the spectral network provides a clean answer: the lift to the spectral curve organizes the walls into a graph, and the tropical disks correspond to certain paths in this graph. By applying the flow to trace the walls and the spectral network to resolve the vertices, we obtain a complete algorithm for disk counting.

Explicit computation for
SU(3)
tropical disk.

Consider the pure
SU(3)
theory in the tropical limit. The charge lattice is rank 2. The characteristic flow lines for charges
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are straight rays in the
y
-plane. Their intersection point is found by solving

y^{a}_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})=y^{a}_{\alpha_{2}}(t_{2}).

The spectral network tells us that at this intersection, a
K
-wall of charge
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
is emitted, and the Stokes factor product is the pentagon identity. The tropical disk of charge
k\alpha_{1}+l\alpha_{2}
corresponds to a path in the network consisting of
k
segments of the
\alpha_{1}
wall and
l
segments of the
\alpha_{2}
wall. The total number of such disks is given by the number of ways to order these segments, which is the binomial coefficient
\binom{k+l}{k}
. Our flow method directly computes the affine lengths of these segments, giving the disk areas
kA_{1}+lA_{2}
, and the product of Stokes factors yields the generating function

Z_{\mathrm{disk}}^{SU(3)}=\prod_{k,l\geq 0}(1-q^{k-1/2}e^{-(kA_{1}+lA_{2})})^{-\Omega(k,l)}

with
\Omega(k,l)
being the BPS indices from the previous section. For pure
SU(3)
, these indices are
\Omega(k\alpha_{1})=1
,
\Omega(k\alpha_{2})=1
, and
\Omega(k(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}))=k+1
. Substituting gives a closed form.

8.4
New result: generating function for
SU(N)
tropical disks

As a direct consequence of the SAF recursion established in the previous section, we obtain the correct exponential form of the tropical disk generating function for pure
SU(N)
gauge theories.

Corollary 8.1

(Tropical disk generating function for pure
SU(N)
)
.

In the tropical limit, the full scattering diagram for pure
SU(N)
Yang–Mills theory is the ordered product (over all positive roots
\Phi_{+}
of
A_{N-1}
) of the factors

\Theta_{\alpha}=\exp\,\!\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\binom{k+\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle}\Bigr),

(8)

where
\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)
denotes the height of the root
\alpha
and
y
are the flat coordinates on the Coulomb branch. The ordering of the factors is dictated by the characteristic Hessian flow; since the classical automorphisms commute, the full generating function can be written as the single exponential

Z_{\mathrm{disk}}^{SU(N)}(y)=\exp\!\,\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\binom{k+\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle}\Bigr).

(9)

Proof.

From the SAF induction in Section 6 (see also Theorem
1.3
and the examples that follow), the multiple‑cover BPS multiplicities of a positive root
\alpha
are

\Omega_{k\alpha}=\binom{k+\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}.

The tropical KS factor attached to the ray
\alpha
is, by definition,

\Theta_{\alpha}=\exp\!\,\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega_{k\alpha}}{k}e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle}\Bigr).

Substituting the binomial coefficients immediately yields (
8
). The full scattering diagram is the ordered product of the
\Theta_{\alpha}
over all positive roots; because the classical factors commute, the product is equivalent to the sum of the exponents, giving (
9
).
∎

For
N=2
this result reduces to
\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{disk}}^{SU(2)}(y)=\exp\!\,\bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle}\bigr)
, which is the well‑known exponential of a single wall. For
N=3
, it reproduces the expression derived from the explicit
SU(3)
analysis above. The formula (
9
) is the correct tropical limit of the quantum dilogarithm product; earlier statements in the literature that write the generating function as a product
\prod(1-e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle})^{-\binom{k+\mathrm{ht}-1}{\mathrm{ht}-1}}
correspond to a different choice of variables and must be used with care.

9
Connections with Wang (2024) and Mirror Symmetry

9.1
Relation to Wang’s Hesse flow

In
[
7
]
, the Hesse flow is defined precisely as our gradient Hessian flow
V_{\operatorname{HF}}=-g^{-1}d\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z)
. Wang proved that this flow, together with the attractor flow
V_{\operatorname{SAF}}
, satisfies an orthogonality relation on the wall and that they are dual under a
\mathbb{Z}
-affine structure. Our Proposition
1.1
provides a simpler proof of this orthogonality. However,
[
7
]
does not discuss spectral networks. The missing link is the observation that the spectral network’s walls are foliated not by
V_{\operatorname{HF}}
, but by
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}=-JV_{\operatorname{HF}}
.

Thus we can summarise the relation as follows:
- Wang’s Hesse flow
V_{\operatorname{HF}}
is the gradient flow that **crosses** the wall (normal direction).
- The spectral network
S
-walls are generated by the **Hamiltonian flow**
V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}=-JV_{\operatorname{HF}}
, which **foliates** the wall (tangent direction).
- The two flows are related pointwise by the complex structure
J
of the special Kähler manifold (up to sign). This is exactly the duality between “Hesse flow” and “dual Hesse flow” observed by Wang: the dual flow is obtained by rotating the gradient by the complex structure (with the appropriate sign to match the Hamiltonian convention).

Therefore, our framework extends Wang’s work by identifying the dual Hesse flow with the characteristic foliation of the spectral network, thereby bridging the gap between the wall-crossing structures studied by Kontsevich–Soibelman and the explicit Stokes geometry of GMN.

9.2

V_{\operatorname{HF}^{\mathrm{char}}}

X
be a Calabi–Yau threefold with a Hitchin system description (e.g., the mirror of a class

\mathcal{B}
is the moduli space of complex structures on the mirror

\mathcal{W}_{\vartheta}

In this context, the condition

is the gradient of the argument of the period, which in mirror symmetry determines the “quantum corrected” periods:

The flow lines of

10
Conclusions and Outlook

We have provided a systematic and rigorous geometric framework that unifies the split attractor flow, gradient Hessian flow, characteristic Hessian flow, and spectral network for
\mathcal{N}=2
theories. Our main contributions include a new concise proof of the orthogonality (Proposition 1), a precise formulation and proof of the lift–projection duality (Theorem 2) that resolves the subtle distinction between gradient and Hamiltonian Hessian flows, and a complete inductive proof of the KS equivariance (Theorem 3) whose algebraic steps are geometrically ordered by the characteristic flow. We have illustrated the framework through
SU(2)
(pure and with flavours, the latter providing new BPS indices for higher flavour charges),
SU(3)
(full BPS spectrum reconstruction),
SU(4)
, and the Kronecker quiver. As a novel application, we used the induction to derive a closed-form BPS spectrum for the Argyres–Douglas
H_{1}
theory,
\Omega(n\alpha_{1}+m\alpha_{2})=\binom{n+m}{n}
, which is a new result. Furthermore, in the tropical limit we obtained a closed-form generating function for disk counts in
SU(N)
theories,
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\prod_{k\geq 1}(1-e^{-k\langle\alpha,y\rangle})^{-\binom{k+\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)-1}}
, also new to our knowledge. These results demonstrate that the unified framework is not merely a formal structure but a powerful tool for deriving explicit, verifiable physical predictions.

Future work will extend the recursion to all class
\mathcal{S}
theories via cluster algebras and explore the resurgence structure of BPS states, where the characteristic Hessian flow controls the Stokes rays in the Borel plane, while the SAF describes the saddle-point transitions, and the SN yields the alien calculus of the trans-series.

Appendix A
Coordinate Verification of Orthogonality

In special coordinates
z^{i}
, the metric is
g_{i\bar{j}}=\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}K
. Let
Z=Z_{\gamma}
. On the wall,

\partial_{i}|Z|=\frac{1}{2}e^{-i\vartheta}\partial_{i}Z,\quad\partial_{\bar{i}}|Z|=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\vartheta}\partial_{\bar{i}}\overline{Z},

and for
f=\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\vartheta}Z)
,

\partial_{i}f=\frac{1}{2i}e^{-i\vartheta}\partial_{i}Z,\quad\partial_{\bar{i}}f=-\frac{1}{2i}e^{i\vartheta}\partial_{\bar{i}}\overline{Z}.

Thus
\partial_{i}f=-i\,\partial_{i}|Z|
and
\partial_{\bar{i}}f=i\,\partial_{\bar{i}}|Z|
. The inner product

g(V_{\operatorname{SAF}},V_{\operatorname{HF}})=i\,g^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}|Z|\partial_{\bar{j}}|Z|

is purely imaginary but must be real, hence it vanishes. This matches the
I
-operator proof in Section 4.

Appendix B
Non‑trivial Worked Example:
SU(3)
with
N_{f}=2
Fundamental Flavours

We now apply our unified flow framework to a genuinely challenging theory:
SU(3)

\mathcal{N}=2
super‑Yang–Mills with
N_{f}=2
massless fundamental hypermultiplets. This theory has a two‑dimensional Coulomb branch and a rich wall‑crossing structure that is difficult to handle with spectral networks alone. The characteristic Hessian flow, combined with the lift–projection duality, provides a practical computational tool.

B.1
Seiberg–Witten curve and central charges

The Seiberg–Witten curve for
SU(3)
with
N_{f}=2
massless flavours is a family of cubic curves in
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
given by
[
4
]

y^{2}=z^{3}-3uz-2v+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{z},

(10)

where
(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
are the gauge‑invariant Casimirs and
\Lambda
is the dynamical scale. The Seiberg–Witten differential is

\lambda=\frac{y}{z}\,dz.

(11)

A symplectic basis of cycles
\{A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}\}
on the compactified curve yields the electric periods
\displaystyle a_{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{A_{i}}\lambda
and magnetic duals
\displaystyle a_{D,i}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{B_{i}}\lambda
, with the constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}=0
(we work in a basis with
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=0
). The BPS charges are labelled by a pair of electric and magnetic vectors
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
with
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, giving central charge

Z_{(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(p_{i}a_{D,i}-q_{i}a_{i}).

(12)

The presence of massless flavours introduces a flavour symmetry
U(2)
, but for the purpose of BPS walls on the Coulomb branch the flavour charges do not appear explicitly; they affect the asymptotic periods.

B.2
Asymptotic periods for numerical integration

To initialise our flow, we need accurate asymptotic expansions of
a_{i}(u,v)
and
a_{D,i}(u,v)
in the weak‑coupling region
|u|,|v|\gg\Lambda^{3}
. A convenient parametrisation is

u=-\frac{1}{2}R^{2}e^{2i\theta},\qquad v=-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}R^{3}e^{3i\theta},

(13)

with
R\gg\Lambda
. The electric periods have the large‑
R
expansions

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}Re^{i\theta}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{3\sqrt{2}Re^{i\theta}}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-3}),

(14)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}Re^{i(\theta+2\pi/3)}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{3\sqrt{2}Re^{i(\theta+2\pi/3)}}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-3}),

(15)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}Re^{i(\theta+4\pi/3)}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{3\sqrt{2}Re^{i(\theta+4\pi/3)}}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-3}).

(16)

The magnetic periods are obtained from the prepotential
\mathcal{F}
, which in this massless case reads

\mathcal{F}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}^{2}\log\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\text{non‑perturbative terms}\Big].

(17)

The non‑perturbative contributions start at order
\Lambda^{6}/R^{3}
and are negligible for initialising the flow far from the origin. We keep the one‑loop logarithmic terms.

B.3
The characteristic Hessian flow on the
(u,v)
plane

The Coulomb branch metric
g_{I\bar{J}}
is obtained from
\tau_{IJ}=\partial_{a_{I}}\partial_{a_{J}}\mathcal{F}
by the chain rule. For our numerical work we use the explicit expressions from the Picard–Fuchs equations, but for brevity we only note that they are smooth functions on the complement of the discriminant locus.

Given a phase
\vartheta
(we take
\vartheta=0
for definiteness), the wall condition for a charge
\gamma
is

f_{\gamma}(u,v)=\operatorname{Im}\big(e^{-i\vartheta}Z_{\gamma}(u,v)\big)=0.

(18)

The characteristic Hessian flow that foliates this wall is

\dot{u}=-\big(g^{u\bar{u}}\partial_{\bar{u}}f_{\gamma}+g^{u\bar{v}}\partial_{\bar{v}}f_{\gamma}\big)_{\text{Hamiltonian}}=-J\,V_{\text{grad}},

(19)

with the complex structure
J
acting as multiplication by
i
in the holomorphic coordinate
(u,v)
. In real components, if we write
u=x_{1}+ix_{2}
,
v=x_{3}+ix_{4}
, the Hamiltonian equations are

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{2}}f_{\gamma}^{\text{grad}},\quad\dot{x}_{2}=\partial_{x_{1}}f_{\gamma}^{\text{grad}},

(20)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{3}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x_{4}}f_{\gamma}^{\text{grad}},\quad\dot{x}_{4}=\partial_{x_{3}}f_{\gamma}^{\text{grad}},

(21)

where
f_{\gamma}^{\text{grad}}=g^{I\bar{J}}\partial_{\bar{J}}f_{\gamma}
. These equations can be integrated with a standard ODE solver.

B.4
Algorithm to map the full wall network

We illustrate the computational procedure that takes full advantage of the characteristic flow:

1.

Select a ray of initial points.
Choose a large circle
R=R_{0}
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
in the weak‑coupling region. On this circle, solve
f_{\gamma}(u,v)=0
for each charge
\gamma
of interest (simple roots, sums of roots, dyons). This gives a set of starting points on the various walls.

2.

Integrate the characteristic flow.
For each starting point, evolve the ODE system in both forward and backward directions until a singularity (branch point or strong‑coupling attractor) is reached. The projection onto the wall is maintained automatically (up to numerical drift, corrected by one Newton step).

3.

Detect intersections.
While flowing a wall for charge
\gamma
, monitor the wall functions of other charges. A sign change of
f_{\gamma^{\prime}}
indicates an intersection. Record the coordinates
(u,v)
and the tangent vectors
V_{\text{charHF}}^{\gamma},V_{\text{charHF}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}
.

4.

Construct the local scattering diagram.
At each intersection, use the linear relation of tangent vectors (Theorem
1.2
) to identify the emitted
K
‑wall, and then start new flows for the composite charge from that point.

5.

Assemble the BPS indices.
Encircle each intersection with a small loop. The product of quantum dilogarithms around the loop yields the local KS automorphism. Expand in formal variables to read off the BPS indices
\Omega(\gamma)
.

B.6
Discussion: why the flow method wins

In the traditional spectral network approach, one must solve a PDE (the Stokes condition) on a three‑real‑dimensional space
(u,v,z)
and then project. Here we only solve ODEs on the four‑real‑dimensional Coulomb branch. The characteristic flow respects the wall condition by construction, so the dimensional reduction is exact. Moreover, the linear tangent relation at vertices provides an automatic consistency check: if numerical errors accumulate, the vertex relation
V_{1}+V_{2}=V_{3}
can be used to correct the trajectory. This makes the algorithm robust and suitable for automated scanning of moduli spaces of higher‑rank theories.
