Gaussian closure and dynamical mean-field theory for self-avoiding heteropolymers
Andriy Goychuk

Abstract
Analytical treatments of polymer dynamics have mostly been restricted to linear response theory around some steady state obtained via perturbative field theory. Here, I derive an analytical framework that yields unified access to the evolution of conformations, contact probabilities, and fluctuations within a dynamical mean-field theory. Starting with the Langevin equation of a hydrodynamically coupled and self-avoiding heteropolymer, the key idea is to focus on the two-point correlator as the lowest-order relevant observable. Truncating higher-order correlations via a Gaussian closure leads to a self-consistent diffusion equation for the chain correlations. The theory is validated by contrasting coiled, globular, and self-avoiding polymers within a single dynamical framework, and predicts hyper-compacted fractal states in hydrodynamically coupled active polymers such as chromatin.

Model—
The conformation of a polymer at some time
t
is represented by the relative arrangement of
N
monomers with positions
\bm{r}_{i}(t)
where the indices
i
define the sequence coordinate.
The chain conformation is associated with an energy
E(\{\bm{r}_{i}\})
that contains linear and higher-order contributions such as self-avoidance.
The couplings due to the friction of the monomers with the surrounding fluid and with each other are embodied by the mobility tensor
\bm{M}_{ij}(\{\bm{r}\})
, which maps forces to velocities depending on the polymer conformation, as will be detailed later.
Neglecting memory and nonreciprocal forces in the present work, local force balance corresponds to the following Langevin dynamics
34
,

\partial_{t}\bm{r}_{i}(t)=\bm{M}_{ij}(\{\bm{r}\}){\cdot}\left[-\frac{\delta E}{\delta\bm{r}_{j}(t)}+\bm{\eta}_{j}(t)\right]+\bm{\xi}(\bm{r}_{i}(t),t)\,,

(1)

where repeated indices indicate summation.
Here,
\bm{\eta}_{i}(t)
are monomer-specific excitations, with zero mean and
\langle\bm{\eta}_{i}(t){\otimes}\bm{\eta}_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=(C_{ij}\bm{I}/3)\,\delta(t-t^{\prime})
covariance
17
.
Moreover,
\bm{\xi}_{j}(\bm{r},t)
is additional advection, or spatial kicks in the laboratory frame, with zero mean and
\langle\bm{\xi}(\bm{r},t){\otimes}\bm{\xi}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=(A\,\bm{I}/3)\,C_{\xi}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})\,\delta(t-t^{\prime})
covariance
26
;
55
;
53
;
23
.
Both types of excitations induce spatially correlated chromatin motion.
In this manuscript, I consider polymers in
d=3
dimensions and will systematically study Eq. (
1
) by gradually increasing complexity.

Gaussian closure—
A polymer such as chromatin lacking a well-defined crystal structure, up to translation and rotation, has a vanishing first moment
\langle\bm{r}_{i}(t)\rangle=0
.
Such polymers only have structure in the statistical sense, represented by correlators of second order and higher even orders, while odd correlators vanish due to symmetry.
According to Marcinkiewicz’ theorem
27
, the hierarchy of correlators either truncates at the Gaussian level, namely the two-point correlator
\langle\bm{r}_{i}(t)\cdot\bm{r}_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle
, or is infinite.
Current experimental measurements on chromatin yield pairwise contact probability maps, but lack the information required to constrain higher-order correlators.
Given this lack of data, I consider a Gaussian approximation for the joint distribution of the chain configurations.
The Gaussian distribution can be interpreted as the least-biased distribution that maximizes the information-theoretic entropy given available data, and has been used successfully to reproduce chromatin conformations in linear network models
45
;
24
;
48
;
49
;
50
;
47
.

The Gaussian closure is analogous to Hartree-Fock theory expanding the dynamics around a self-consistent “ground state”
51
, and is a well-known approximation to deal with the infinite Bogoliubov–Born–Green–Kirkwood–Yvon hierarchy.
Truncating the series of correlators enables the use of powerful tools such as correlation splitting techniques, which I exploit to derive a dynamical mean-field theory for the dynamics of the pairwise contact or looping probability in active heteropolymers such as chromatin.
Dynamical mean-field theories have previously been described at the level of concentration fields
11
, yet an analogous theory of pairwise contact probabilities in the context of genome organization has so far been lacking.
In other words, the goal is to derive the time evolution of the same-time position correlator
X_{in}(t)\coloneqq\langle\bm{r}_{i}(t)\cdot\bm{r}_{n}(t)\rangle
.

Dynamical mean-field theory of polymer folding—

On time scales where the excitations decorrelate and can be effectively considered Markovian, using the Novikov-Furutsu theorem
31
;
14
and respecting causality of the excitations leads to (details in End Matter)

\partial_{t}X_{in}(t)=-\hat{J}_{im}\,X_{mn}(t)-X_{im}(t)\,\hat{J}^{T}_{mn}\\
+\hat{M}_{ij}C_{jk}\hat{M}_{kn}+A\,\langle C_{\xi}(\bm{r}_{n}(t)-\bm{r}_{i}(t))\rangle\,.

(2a)

The mean squared separation between monomers is
\Delta R^{2}_{ij}\coloneqq\langle|\bm{r}_{i}(t)-\bm{r}_{j}(t)|^{2}\rangle=X_{ii}+X_{jj}-2X_{ij}
.
The fluctuations
X_{in}(t,t^{\prime})\coloneqq\langle\bm{r}_{i}(t)\cdot\bm{r}_{n}(t^{\prime})\rangle
obey

\partial_{t}X_{in}(t,t^{\prime})=-\hat{J}_{im}X_{mn}(t,t^{\prime})\,,\quad\text{for}\quad t>t^{\prime}\,,

(2b)

with the initial condition
X_{in}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})=X_{in}(t^{\prime})
.
This dynamical mean-field theory models evolving contact maps in polymers such as chromatin, with the effective hydrodynamic kernel
\hat{M}_{ij}
and the response matrix
\hat{J}_{ij}
detailed below, and is the central contribution of this paper.

Pairwise hydrodynamic interactions neglecting many-body effects are described by the Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa tensor
40
;
58
.
In the last step of deriving Eq. (
2a
), the covariance of the mobility tensor was simplified via a pre-averaging approximation
\bm{M}_{ij}\to\bm{I}\,\hat{M}_{ij}
.
This choice is not required to complete the Gaussian closure and does not change the asymptotic scaling (e.g., for
a\to 0
) of the effective interactions.
Using fluid incompressibility, this led to the Zimm kernel
60
;
29
,

\hat{M}_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{1}{3}\left\langle\tr(\bm{M}_{ij})\right\rangle=D_{0}\,h\biggl(\frac{\Delta R_{ij}^{2}}{6a^{2}}\biggr)\,,

(3)

with monomer radius
a
and diffusion coefficient
D_{0}
.
The conformation-averaged hydrodynamic coupling,
h(r)\coloneqq\erf(r^{-1/2})-\sqrt{r/\pi}\,(1-e^{-1/r})
, scales as
h(r)\sim r^{-1/2}
at large distances.
Hydrodynamic coupling causes the displacements of the chain (kicks) to be effectively correlated in sequence space even when the monomer excitations (forces) are not.
Importantly, the fluctuation-dissipation theorem holds if and only if
A=0
and
\hat{M}_{ij}C_{jn}=T\delta_{in}
with constant
T
.
Otherwise, the conformational ensemble is not just set by the energetics, and active excitations will fold the polymer
17
.

Under pre-averaging of the Zimm kernel, a choice not required for the Gaussian closure and not affecting the scaling, the effective response matrix in Eq. (
2a
) is given by

\hat{J}_{im}(t)\coloneqq\hat{M}_{ij}\,\frac{1}{3}\tr\left\langle\frac{\delta^{2}E}{\delta\bm{r}_{m}(t)\delta\bm{r}_{j}(t)}\right\rangle\,.

(4)

This reveals that the Gaussian ensemble dynamics resembles how different segments of the polymer move in response to the mean-field generated by other segments.
Consequently, the Gaussian closure is equivalent to assuming that the dynamics of each segment is fast so that it can explore the full phase space admitted by the constraints from the neighboring segments.

Effective models based on linear response—
In steady state,
t=t^{\prime}\to\infty
, the conformation-averaged response becomes time-invariant.
Integration of Eq. (
2b
) then yields the linear response
X_{in}(t,t^{\prime})=[e^{-\hat{J}(t-t^{\prime})}]_{im}X_{mn}(t^{\prime})
.
For example, for a fractal globule in thermal equilibrium,
\hat{J}_{im}=(T/2)\hat{M}_{ij}{X^{-1}}_{jm}
, which implies that the effective response mimics long-ranged spring couplings.
Conversely, such power-law couplings have been posited by phenomenological linear models to mimic the structure and dynamical fluctuations of fractal chains
1
;
42
;
36
;
37
.
The Gaussian closure reveals that such phenomenological models are reliable in steady state, which can be determined by fixed-point iteration of Eq. (
2a
), where the linear response reproduces all dynamical scaling exponents.

The framework also enables analyzing the fluctuations in general polymers beyond steady state, and complements recent scaling arguments
21
;
38
;
44
;
43
.
It is agnostic to the present choice to focus on potential forces derived from an energy
E
, and can be generalized by including non-potential forces at the level of the Langevin equation [Eq. (
1
)].
Linear extensions such as non-reciprocal Hookean interactions
K_{ij}\neq K_{ji}
or soft sequence-dependent confinement
\sum_{i}K_{ij}\neq 0
, for example, will simply propagate through the theory without modification.
Such non-reciprocal interactions can arise when different regulatory elements are attracted towards specific genomic regions, for example mediated by condensates
16
, but not vice versa.

Polymer energetics—
To model the energetics of the polymer, consider the following two main contributions.
First, reciprocal harmonic springs connect different monomers according to the Kirchhoff matrix
K_{ij}
with
\sum_{i}K_{ij}=0

45
;
24
;
36
;
48
.
Second, purely local interactions are incorporated via a normalized Gaussian,
\bar{\delta}(r,a)=(\sqrt{\pi}a)^{-3}\,e^{-r^{2}/a^{2}}
, which in the limit
a\to 0
converges to the 3D
\delta
-distribution and models contacts.
Together, the conformational energy is given by
E=\sum_{i<j}[\frac{1}{2}K_{ij}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j})^{2}+\gamma\,\bar{\delta}(|\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}|,a)]
up to the second virial coefficient
\gamma

9
.
Here,
\gamma>0
corresponds to repulsive interactions indicative of self-avoidance, whereas
\gamma<0
models pairwise attraction upon contact.
After evaluating the Gaussian integrals, one has

\hat{J}_{im}\approx\hat{M}_{ij}\biggl\{-K_{jm}+2\pi\gamma\biggl[\left\langle\bar{\delta}\right\rangle_{c}^{5/3}(\Delta R_{jm},a)\\
-\sum_{i}\left\langle\bar{\delta}\right\rangle^{5/3}(\Delta R_{im},a)\,\delta_{jm}\biggr]\biggr\}\,,

(5)

with the effective contact kernel

\left\langle\bar{\delta}\right\rangle(r,a)=\pi^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(a^{2}+\frac{2}{3}r^{2}\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,.

(6)

indicating in the limit
a\to 0
the probability density of pairwise contacts between monomers.

The second line of Eq. (
5
) is an effective local pressure that makes self-avoidance reciprocal and pushes monomers away from the centroid of the polymer for
\gamma>0
.
Compared to the contact probability density, Eq. (
6
), the response matrix shows an additional scaling
\propto\langle\bar{\delta}\rangle^{2/3}
.
This is because the Hessian involves real-space gradients averaged over the chain conformation, and these distances scale as
\langle\bar{\delta}\rangle^{-1/3}
.
This interpretation is consistent with the idea that each polymer segment responds to the mean-field generated by other segments.
It also allows extrapolating that tertiary interactions would enter as
\langle\bar{\delta}\rangle^{2/3}\langle\bar{\delta}\rangle\langle\bar{\delta}\rangle
with an additional sum over particle combinations.

Effective diffusion dynamics on a triangular domain—
Having set up the general framework, the natural question is how it connects to existing theory.
To answer this question, I will begin by reframing the dynamics of polymer conformations as a 2D diffusion problem that represents the propagation of tension along the chain (Fig.
1
).
To that end, consider a simplified scenario lacking spatial excitations,
A\to 0
, self-avoidance,
\gamma\to 0
, and hydrodynamic interactions,
\hat{M}_{ij}\to\delta_{ij}
.
Moreover, take an interaction matrix
K_{ij}=k\,[\delta_{i+1,j}+\delta_{i-1,j}-2\delta_{ij}]
corresponding to Hookean springs between neighboring monomers.

After working with a discrete chain so far, the key step to extract further insights is to now take the continuum limit by mapping discrete monomer indices into a continuous sequence space,
i\to s/ds
, where
ds
is the discretization.
This turns tensor contraction over monomer indices into convolution in sequence space, which enables a much more intuitive discussion.
In the continuum limit, the springs among neighbors correspond to a Laplace operator, and Eq. (
2a
) can be interpreted as a diffusion equation
\partial_{t}X(s,s^{\prime})=k\,ds^{2}\,[\partial_{s}^{2}+\partial_{s^{\prime}}^{2}]X(s,s^{\prime})+C(s,s^{\prime})
.

Due to symmetry, the relevant domain for this diffusion process is the triangular half-space defined by
s\geq s^{\prime}
.
Considering spatially independent but nonuniform excitations,
C(s,s^{\prime})=C(s)\delta(s-s^{\prime})
leads to a Neumann boundary condition,
\bm{\nabla}X(s,s^{\prime})|_{s=s^{\prime}}=-C(s)/(\sqrt{2}k\,ds^{2})\,\hat{\bm{e}}_{s-s^{\prime}}
, for the slope of the two-point correlation function.
This reveals that correlations are injected at the diagonal
s=s^{\prime}
and propagated away from the diagonal by tension, which explains how patterns imposed by nonuniform activity emerge
17
.
For uniform
C(s)
, integration leads to
X(s,s^{\prime})\propto-|s-s^{\prime}|
in steady state and the mean squared distance
\Delta R^{2}(s,s^{\prime})=X(s,s)+X(s^{\prime},s^{\prime})-2X(s,s^{\prime})\propto|s-s^{\prime}|
expected from a Rouse chain.

Flory scaling, chain collapse, and globule—
To validate the theory, consider how it recovers classical results such as the Flory scaling of self-avoiding polymers
10
, originally developed based on arguments for the primitive probability density, in thermalized polymers with contact energy
\gamma\neq 0
and reciprocal couplings.
Asymptotic scale invariance implies that the correlator
X(s,s^{\prime})\sim-\Delta s^{2\nu}
with
\Delta s\coloneqq|s-s^{\prime}|
.
The sign of
\gamma
and the competition between the different terms in Eq. (
2a
) control which asymptotic scaling exponent
\nu
is realized (Fig.
2
).
The tension (Laplace) term scales as
-\Delta s^{2\nu-2}
and competes with the excitations
\sim\Delta s^{-1}
on short length scales to give Rouse behavior,
\nu=1/2
.
For sufficiently large separations (conversely,
a\to 0
) the pairwise contact term scales as
-\gamma\int ds^{\prime\prime}\,\Delta R^{2}(s,s^{\prime\prime})^{-5/2}X(s^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime})\sim\gamma|s-s^{\prime}|^{-5\nu+2\nu+1}
where the convolution contributed the increment
+1
.

Asymptotic behavior of a polymer in thermal equilibrium
.
Classical scalings emerge from a competition between different asymptotic terms in the dynamical mean-field theory, Eq. (
2a
).
Hydrodynamic interactions were neglected because they are irrelevant for equilibrium structure.

For self-avoidance
\gamma>0
, the pairwise contacts compete with tension on large separations
\Delta s
where excitations are irrelevant.
This yields the classical Flory scaling
\nu=3/5
in 3D and can be easily generalized to other dimensions
9
.
For self-attraction
\gamma<0
, at short distances
\Delta s
, the competition with excitations will lead to
\nu=2/3
, but this is unstable (UV divergence) with respect to finite tension.
In general, self-attracting chains require repulsive tertiary interactions to stabilize them.
Compared to pairwise couplings, each convolution with another contact kernel adds
-3\nu+1
, thus modifying the scaling to
\gamma_{3}|s-s^{\prime}|^{(-3\nu+1)(n-1)}
for
n
-body contacts.
At intermediate distances
\Delta s
where excitations are irrelevant, pairwise and triple contacts will balance when they are scale-free, leading to a Lifshitz globule
\nu=1/3

25
.
At distances
\Delta s
where local interactions are irrelevant, one again recovers Rouse behavior,
\nu=1/2
, from the Laplacian structure representing tension propagation.

These results indicate that the dynamical theory derived from a Gaussian closure recapitulates classical mean-field homopolymer results within a description that truncates the correlator hierarchy at pairwise interactions.
Looking forward, it provides a non-perturbative method for studying the dynamics of self-avoiding heteropolymers in the presence of hydrodynamic interactions, which has so far been widely considered analytically intractable.

Spatial noise compacts Rouse polymers—
Finally, motivated by the literature on coherent chromatin flows
59
;
2
;
46
, consider the effects of spatially correlated excitations on timescales where they are approximately Markovian.
Coherent flows in the cell nucleus occur on length scales
\lambda\sim$1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$

59
, far exceeding the range of molecular contacts,
\lambda\gg a
.
Below the distance
\lambda
, coherent flows do not agitate the separation of monomers via shear, thus recovering the polymer behaviors discussed above.
On larger distances, the contact energy can be neglected, and, for strong non-equilibrium driving, the active flows are expected to dominate over thermal noise
\hat{M}_{in}C_{nj}\sim\delta_{ij}
.

Finite-range correlated flows
C_{\xi}(\bm{r})=\bar{\delta}(|\bm{r}|,\lambda)
scale as
\sim\Delta s^{-3\nu}
, whereas power-law correlated flows
C_{\xi}(\bm{r})\sim 1/|\bm{r}|^{n}
scale as
\Delta s^{-n\nu}
, and both compete with the tension term
\sim-\Delta s^{2\nu-2}
.
Matching the exponents leads to an asymptotic scaling
\nu=2/5
for finite-range correlated flows, which is more compact than a Rouse chain but less compact than a globule
25
;
20
.
For power-law correlated flows, one instead has
\nu=2/(2+n)
, which for
n=2
recovers Rouse statistics, for
n=3
is finite-range-like, and for
n\geq 3
requires regularization to prevent a UV divergence.

If chromatin undergoes spatially correlated nonequilibrium excitations as recently inferred from pairwise locus tracking experiments and simulated with Rouse phantom chains lacking hydrodynamic coupling
23
, then this structural signature should be visible in contact frequency data.
The underlying reason for the compaction is that excitations correlated in real space map, via the mean squared separation, to effective excitations correlated along the sequence of the chain.
As shown with linear response theory and simulations in our previous work, correlated kicks lead to an effective attraction among monomers independent of distance in sequence space
17
.

Spatial noise induces hyper-compaction in Zimm chains—
The analysis so far is limited by having neglected hydrodynamic interactions.
Introducing hydrodynamic interactions [
\hat{M}_{ij}\neq\delta_{ij}
, Eq. (
3
)] modifies the scaling of the tension term
\sim-\Delta s^{2\nu-2-\nu+1}
where the Zimm coupling contributes
-\nu
and the convolution contributes
+1
.
If the fluctuation-dissipation theorem holds,
\hat{M}_{ij}C_{jn}\propto\delta_{in}
, monomer excitations will scale as
\Delta s^{-\nu}
, and matching the asymptotic behavior in absence of active spatial excitations,
A=0
, recovers the Rouse scaling
\nu=1/2
as an important validation.

In contrast, if finite-range correlated flows
C_{\xi}(\bm{r})=\bar{\delta}(|\bm{r}|,\lambda)
dominate over monomer excitations, then the noise scales as
\Delta s^{-3\nu}
, as discussed before, and the asymptotic analysis reveals a modified scaling
\nu=1/4
of the mean squared separation with distance in sequence space.
Strikingly, this hyper-compact scaling with fractal dimension
1/\nu=4
has been observed in chromatin
54
and prompted theoretical interest
49
;
5
;
47
.
The active Zimm theory recovers this anomalous scaling without invoking loop extrusion
5
.

Discussion—
In summary, the main contribution of this paper is a simple analytical framework [Eq. (
2
)], based on a Gaussian closure, for the conformational dynamics of active, hydrodynamically coupled, and self-avoiding or self-attracting heteropolymers such as chromatin.
The unified theory geometrically interprets polymer folding as a non-linear and non-local diffusion of the pairwise contact probability in sequence space (Fig.
1
).
It was validated by reproducing the known scaling behavior of fractal globules and self-avoiding chains, and predicts hyper-compaction with fractal dimension
1/\nu=4
in actively driven Zimm chains.
The trade-off is a slight deviation of the scaling in self-avoiding chains from the exact
\nu=0.588
, a surprising accuracy enabled by the same cancellation of errors as Flory theory.
In agreement with our previous work
17
, these results underscore that correlated hydrodynamic fluctuations can induce attraction between different genomic loci.
It will be interesting to explore how this couples to other mechanisms that can bring polymer loci together, such as correlated hydrodynamic fluctuations inducing capillary forces between biomolecular condensates
19
and chemical reactions mediating non-reciprocal interactions
16
.

In future work, this framework can be further tested by studying the propagation of tension along chains that are inextensible
22
, incorporating hard confinement, and considering tertiary interactions.
For example, I expect that self-attraction overcoming tension and competing with bending will lead to Allen-Cahn or Cahn-Hilliard like dynamics
4
and thus phase separation in sequence space.
More generally, mapping the problem of polymer folding to non-local diffusion and reaction among pairwise contact probabilities opens a path towards analytically studying the joint dynamics of chromatin-bound proteins and chromatin folding with the rich machinery of reaction-diffusion systems
13
.
In this context, the coupling between concentration fields and geometry
57
, both along the chain and in the nucleoplasm, could be particularly interesting.
