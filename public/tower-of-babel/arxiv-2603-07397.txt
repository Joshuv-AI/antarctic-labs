1.
Introduction

Roots of polynomials with coefficients in a finite set often produce intricate loci in the complex plane. In the classical
\{-1,0,1\}
case the closure coincides with the
Mandelbrot set
for a pair of linear maps
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. For the root set, membership is finite by construction; the difficult part is the closure.

The principal contribution of this paper is the formulation of a finite mechanism that accounts for that transition. For the specific family considered here, the closure of the set of roots is exactly a connectedness locus for a collinear affine iterated function system, and membership in that locus is controlled by a single marked point in the corresponding difference attractor. On a natural two-disk lens in parameter space we construct a canonical trap and enclosure and use them to define finite-capture sets for that marked point.

The main new ingredient is a bounded-delay closure theorem. Exact membership in the root set is equivalent to a finite backward iterate of the marked point landing at
0
. For the closure, exact landing is too rigid. We replace it by finite entry into the trap and prove that every limit of depth-
k
capture parameters is captured after at most two additional inverse steps. This yields an exact description of the non-real part of the closure inside the lens and, after a sharp threshold theorem, globally for every
n\geq 20
.

Now fix an integer
n\geq 2
. Let

D_{n}:=\{-n+1,-n+2,\dots,n-1\},

and let
\mathcal{R}_{n}
be the set of roots of monic polynomials whose non-leading coefficients lie in
D_{n}
:

\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\Bigl\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}:\ c^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}c^{j}=0,\ d_{j}\in D_{n},\ m\geq 1\,\Bigr\}.

We study the closure of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
outside the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. By
Sections

2.3
and
2.3
, this is equivalently the zero locus of reciprocal power series

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}\qquad(d_{k}\in D_{n}).

Thus, the problem can be phrased either algebraically, in terms of restricted roots, or analytically, in terms of zeros of restricted reciprocal series.

The dynamical dictionary begins with the digit set

A_{n}:=\{-n+1,-n+3,\dots,n-1\},

and the affine maps

f_{t}(z)=t+\frac{z}{c}\qquad(t\in A_{n},\ |c|>1).

Let
E(c,n)
be the attractor of the corresponding iterated function system and define the connectedness locus

\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:E(c,n)\text{ is connected}\,\}.

A basic fact proved in
Section

2.3
is that

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

Thus the closure of a countable algebraic root set is literally the connectedness locus of a collinear affine IFS.

The key reduction comes from the difference attractor. Writing
N:=2n-1
and
A_{N}:=A_{n}-A_{n}
, let
E(c,N)
denote the attractor of the associated difference system. Then connectedness is equivalent to a one-point condition:

(1.2)

c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2c\in E(c,N),

see
Section

2.2
. In particular, the geometry of
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is determined by the inverse dynamics of the single marked point
2c
. By
Section

2.4
, a parameter
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{n}
if and only if some finite backward iterate of
2c
under the inverse branches
g_{t}(z):=c(z-t)=f_{t}^{-1}(z)
lands at
0
. Exact landing therefore describes
\mathcal{R}_{n}
itself, but it is too rigid to control the boundary. Our replacement is finite capture into an explicit interior region.

That region is available on the two-disk lens

\mathcal{X}_{n}=\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:\ |c\pm 1|<\sqrt{2n}\,\}.

For every non-real
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
we construct a canonical trap
\mathcal{C}(c,N)
and a canonical enclosure
\mathcal{E}(c,N)
such that

\mathcal{C}(c,N)\subset\operatorname{int}E(c,N)\subset E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N).

Finite backward entry of
2c
into the trap defines the finite-capture sets

\Theta_{k}(n):=\Bigl\{\,c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}:\ \exists\,u\in A_{N}^{\leq k}\text{ such that }g_{u}(2c)\in\mathcal{C}(c,N)\,\Bigr\},

and the finite-capture locus

\Theta_{n}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\Theta_{k}(n).

Thus
\Theta_{n}
is a dynamical thickening of the exact finite-landing locus; see
Figure

1
. Since
0\in\mathcal{C}(c,N)
throughout
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, every restricted root in the lens belongs to
\Theta_{n}
.

Main results.

The first theorem is the key closure statement.

Theorem A

(Two-step closure theorem)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For every
k\geq 0
, the finite-capture sets satisfy

\overline{\Theta_{k}(n)}\cap(\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R})\subset\Theta_{k+2}(n).

The inclusion says that limits of depth-
k
capture parameters are still captured after at most two additional inverse steps. The delay-two bound is uniform in both
k
and
n
.

The second theorem identifies the non-real part of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
in the lens with the closure of the finite-capture locus.

Theorem B

(Non-real closure in the lens)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then

(\mathcal{M}_{n}\cap\mathcal{X}_{n})\setminus\mathbb{R}=\overline{\Theta_{n}}\cap(\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}).

Thus, within
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, the non-real connectedness locus is recovered exactly from finite capture.

Corollary C

(Completion by the real trace in the lens)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{n}\cap\mathcal{X}_{n}=\bigl(\overline{\Theta_{n}}\cup(\partial\mathcal{M}_{n}\cap\mathbb{R})\bigr)\cap\mathcal{X}_{n}.

The next theorem gives the exact threshold for the lens regime.

Theorem D

(Sharp threshold for the non-real locus)
.

For every
n\geq 20
,

\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}\subset\mathcal{X}_{n}.

Moreover, for each
2\leq n\leq 19
there exists a non-real parameter in
\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

Corollary E

(Global description for
n\geq 20
)
.

If
n\geq 20
, then

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\Theta_{n}}\cup(\partial\mathcal{M}_{n}\cap\mathbb{R}).

In view of
Equation

1.1
, the last statement gives a complete description of
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
for
n\geq 20
, away from the real boundary trace.

Relative to
[
7
]
, the advances are threefold:

(i)

Canonical trap and enclosure.
The earlier self-covering rectangle is replaced by a canonical trap–enclosure pair defined throughout the lens.

(ii)

Finite capture and closure.
Exact algebraic landing is replaced by finite capture, and finite capture satisfies a uniform delay-two closure theorem that yields the exact non-real description of
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
inside the lens.

(iii)

Sharp threshold.
The global lens regime improves from
n\geq 21
to the optimal threshold
n\geq 20
; the sharpness direction for
2\leq n\leq 19
is certified in the appendix.

Certified computation enters the theorems themselves, not only the illustrations: the inverse-certification algorithm produces finite witnesses, and the sharp-threshold theorem combines certified computation with analysis.

Strategy of the proof.

Two features of the argument are worth emphasizing. First, the set
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is defined
a priori
by infinitely many reciprocal-series constraints. Second, the proof replaces these constraints by explicit geometric bounds and a finite-capture filtration. Parameters are certified by finite backward itineraries, and the two-step closure theorem shows that these certificates persist under limits.

The argument has three layers:

(1)

Algebraic–dynamical layer.

Section

2
identifies
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, records the reciprocal-series description, and reduces connectedness to the marked-point criterion
Equation

1.2
.

(2)

Geometric layer.

Section

3
constructs the canonical trap–enclosure pair.

(3)

Structural layer.

Sections

4
,
5
and
6
turns that geometry into a finite-capture filtration, proves the two-step closure theorem, and recovers the closure of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
first in the lens and then globally once the sharp threshold is available.

A central observation is that
0\in\mathcal{C}(c,N)
throughout
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Therefore every restricted root in the lens is automatically a finite-capture parameter. In this sense, finite capture extends exact landing from the countable set
\mathcal{R}_{n}
to its closure. The same pattern may be useful for other affine families.

2.
Roots, closures, and the marked-point criterion

We now set up the algebraic–dynamical correspondence that governs the paper. The closure problem for restricted roots is recast as a connectedness problem, and connectedness is then reduced to the backward dynamics of the marked point
2c
in the difference attractor.

2.1.
The collinear fractals family

Fix
n\geq 2
and
A_{n}=\{-n+1,-n+3,\dots,n-1\}
. For
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
|c|>1
, consider the IFS
\{f_{t}\}_{t\in A_{n}}
given by
f_{t}(z)=t+z/c
. The Hutchinson operator
\mathcal{H}_{c}
acts on nonempty compact subsets
K
of
\mathbb{C}
by

\mathcal{H}_{c}(K)=\bigcup_{t\in A_{n}}f_{t}(K).

By classical IFS theory
[
36
]
,
\mathcal{H}_{c}
has a unique fixed point
E(c,n)
, the attractor, which admits the series representation

(2.1)

E(c,n)=\left\{\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}c^{-k}:a_{k}\in A_{n}\ \right\}.

2.2.
Connectedness as a marked-point condition

Set
N=2n-1
and denote by
E(c,N)
the attractor for the difference alphabet

A_{N}=A_{n}-A_{n}=2D_{n}
, where
D_{n}=\{-n+1,-n+2,\dots,n-1\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
n\geq 2
,

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:2c\in E(c,N)\,\}.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Prop. 2.2]
, the attractor
E(c,n)
is connected if and only if two consecutive first-level pieces of the original IFS overlap, that is,

f_{t}\bigl(E(c,n)\bigr)\cap f_{t+2}\bigl(E(c,n)\bigr)\neq\varnothing.

Equivalently, there exist digits
a_{k}\in A_{N}
such that

(2.2)

0=2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}c^{-k}.

Multiplying by
-c
yields

2c=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}-a_{k}c^{-(k-1)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}-a_{j+1}c^{-j}.

Since
A_{N}
is symmetric,
-a_{j+1}\in A_{N}
. The series representation (
2.1
) (with
A_{N}
in place of
A_{n}
) therefore shows that this is equivalent to
2c\in E(c,N)
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Since
A_{N}=2D_{n}
, write
a_{k}=2d_{k}
with
d_{k}\in D_{n}
and divide
(
2.2
) by
2
. Then, for
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
(equivalently
|c|>1
),

c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
if and only if there exists
(d_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset D_{n}
such that

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0
.

Remark 2.3

(Basic symmetries)
.

The characterization in
Section

2.2
shows that
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is invariant under complex conjugation and under the map
c\mapsto-c
. Indeed, if
1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0
with
d_{k}\in D_{n}
, then conjugation gives
1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}\,\overline{c}^{-k}=0
, and replacing
d_{k}
by
(-1)^{k}d_{k}\in D_{n}
gives
1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}d_{k}\,(-c)^{-k}=0
.

2.3.
Restricted roots and closure outside the unit disk

The one-point criterion also admits an algebraic reformulation. Setting
\lambda:=1/c
transfers the regime
|c|>1
to
|\lambda|<1
, so that restricted-coefficient polynomials in
c
become reciprocal power series in
\lambda
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The connectedness locus
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

The map
c\mapsto E(c,N)
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
in the Hausdorff metric
(a standard fact for contractive IFS attractors; cf.
[
36
]
).
Hence
\phi(c):=\dist(2c,E(c,N))
is continuous.
By
Section

2.2
,
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\phi^{-1}(0)\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
, so
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}
be the set of roots of monic polynomials with coefficients in
D_{n}=\{-n+1,-n+2,\dots,n-1\}
:

\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\Bigl\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}:c^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}c^{j}=0,\ d_{j}\in D_{n},\ m\geq 1\,\Bigr\}.

For
n\geq 2
,

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

Proof.

Step 1:
\mathcal{R}_{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Let
c\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
with
|c|>1
. Then

c^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}c^{j}=0

for some
d_{j}\in D_{n}
. Dividing by
c^{m}
and setting
e_{k}:=d_{m-k}\in D_{n}
gives

1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}e_{k}c^{-k}=0.

Extending by
e_{k}=0
for
k>m
,
Section

2.2
yields
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Step 2:
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}
.

Let
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. By
Section

2.2
there exists
(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset D_{n}
with

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0.

Set
\lambda:=1/c
and

F(z):=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}z^{k},\qquad F_{m}(z):=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{k}z^{k}.

Then
F(\lambda)=0
with
|\lambda|<1
, and
F
is holomorphic on
\{|z|<1\}
. Choose a sequence
r_{j}\downarrow 0
such that
\overline{B(\lambda,r_{j})}\subset\{|z|<1\}
and
F
has no zeros on
\partial B(\lambda,r_{j})
for every
j
. Since
F_{m}\to F
uniformly on each
\overline{B(\lambda,r_{j})}
, Rouché’s theorem (equivalently, the local form of Hurwitz’s theorem) implies that for each
j
there exists
m_{j}
such that
F_{m_{j}}
has a zero
\lambda_{j}\in B(\lambda,r_{j})
. Hence
\lambda_{j}\to\lambda
.

Define

Q_{j}(z):=z^{m_{j}}F_{m_{j}}(1/z).

Then
Q_{j}
is monic with coefficients in
D_{n}
, and
Q_{j}(1/\lambda_{j})=0
. Thus
c_{j}:=1/\lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
and
c_{j}\to c
, so
c\in\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}
.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
by Step 1 and
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
by
Section

2.3
, it suffices to observe that
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}
. If
c\in\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, choose
c_{m}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
with
c_{m}\to c
. Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is an open neighborhood of
c
, we have
c_{m}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
for all sufficiently large

, so
c\in\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Combining the two steps gives
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

Corollary 2.6

(Restricted reciprocal-series description)
.

For every
n\geq 2
,

\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\Bigl\{\,c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:\ \exists(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset D_{n}\text{ such that }1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0\,\Bigr\}.

Proof.

Combine
Section

2.3
with
Section

2.2
.
∎

The closure identity describes
\mathcal{M}_{n}
as a limit of finite algebraic data. Through
Section

2.3
, it also identifies
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with a restricted reciprocal-series zero locus. To extract finite witnesses from this description, we now rewrite the same condition in terms of backward iterates of the marked point

2.4.
Backward dynamics and polynomial evaluation

Definition 2.7

(Words and composed branches)
.

Fix
c
with
|c|>1
and an alphabet
A_{N}
.
For each
t\in A_{N}
define

f_{t}(z):=t+\frac{z}{c},\qquad g_{t}(z):=c(z-t)=f_{t}^{-1}(z).

For a word
u=t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{k}\in A_{N}^{k}
of length
|u|:=k
, define

f_{u}:=f_{t_{1}}\circ f_{t_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{t_{k}},\qquad g_{u}:=g_{t_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{t_{2}}\circ g_{t_{1}}=f_{u}^{-1}.

For words

we have
f_{uv}=f_{u}\circ f_{v}
and
g_{uv}=g_{v}\circ g_{u}
. Let
A_{N}^{\leq k}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}A_{N}^{j}
, and set

f_{\epsilon}=g_{\epsilon}=\mathrm{id}
for the empty word
\epsilon
.

The backward dynamics of
2c
under the difference IFS encodes evaluations of restricted polynomials.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
u=t_{1}\cdots t_{k}\in A_{N}^{k}
and
t_{j}=2d_{j}
with
d_{j}\in D_{n}
. Then,

g_{u}(2c)=2c\,P_{u}(c),

where
P_{u}(z)=z^{k}-d_{1}z^{k-1}-\dots-d_{k}
is a monic polynomial whose non-leading coefficients lie in
-D_{n}=D_{n}
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the word length
k
. For
k=1
,

g_{t_{1}}(2c)=c(2c-t_{1})=c(2c-2d_{1})=2c(c-d_{1}),

so the claim holds with
P_{t_{1}}(z)=z-d_{1}
.

For the inductive step, write
u=u^{\prime}t_{k}
, where
u^{\prime}
has length
k-1
. Then

g_{u}(2c)=g_{t_{k}}\bigl(g_{u^{\prime}}(2c)\bigr)=c\bigl(g_{u^{\prime}}(2c)-t_{k}\bigr).

By the induction hypothesis,
g_{u^{\prime}}(2c)=2c\,P_{u^{\prime}}(c)
, hence

g_{u}(2c)=c\bigl(2c\,P_{u^{\prime}}(c)-2d_{k}\bigr)=2c\bigl(c\,P_{u^{\prime}}(c)-d_{k}\bigr).

Thus the recursion
P_{u}(z)=zP_{u^{\prime}}(z)-d_{k}
gives the required monic polynomial of degree
k
with non-leading coefficients in
-D_{n}=D_{n}
.
∎

In view of
Section

2.4
,
g_{u}(2c)=0
if and only if
c
is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in
D_{n}
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}_{n}
consists precisely of parameters
c
for which a finite backward iterate of
2c
hits
0
.

Remark 2.9
.

For every
|c|>1
, one has
0\in E(c,N)
because the constant address
a_{k}\equiv 0
(and
0\in A_{N}
)
gives

.
Consequently, if for some word

we have

, then
2c=f_{u}(0)\in E(c,N)
and hence

by

2.2
.

3.
Canonical parallelograms in the lens

For non-real parameters in the lens we introduce coordinates adapted to the inverse dynamics, build an explicit self-covering trap, and then complement it by a canonical enclosure. Together these parallelograms provide the interior and exterior geometry from which finite capture emerges.

Lemma 3.2

(Dynamics in canonical coordinates)
.

Let
c=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
with
\rho:=|c|
, and let
z=u+iv\in\mathbb{C}
.
Set
(s,v)=(\ell_{s}(z;c),\ell_{v}(z))
.
For
t\in A_{N}
, the backward iterate
z^{\prime}=g_{t}(z)=c(z-t)
has canonical coordinates
(s^{\prime},v^{\prime})
given by

v^{\prime}=\rho s-yt,\qquad s^{\prime}=\frac{2x}{\rho}\,v^{\prime}-\rho v.

Proof.

Since
z^{\prime}=cz-ct
and
\ell_{s},\ell_{v}
are linear, it suffices to compute

and
\ell_{v}(cz)

A direct calculation gives
\ell_{s}(cz;c)=2xs-\rho v
and
\ell_{v}(cz)=\operatorname{Im}(cz)=\rho s
.
Also
\ell_{s}(ct;c)=\frac{2xy}{\rho}t
and
\ell_{v}(ct)=yt
because

Subtracting yields
v^{\prime}=\rho s-yt
and

s^{\prime}=(2xs-\rho v)-\frac{2xy}{\rho}t=\frac{2x}{\rho}(\rho s-yt)-\rho v

The key point is that the digit

3.2.
Self-covering parallelograms and interior inclusion

Definition 3.3

(Self-covering set)
.

Fix
|c|>1
and consider the difference IFS
\{f_{t}(z)=t+z/c\}_{t\in A_{N}}
with inverse branches

g_{t}(z)=c(z-t)
.
A nonempty set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
is called
self-covering
(for this IFS) if

(3.3)

U\subset\bigcup_{t\in A_{N}}f_{t}(U)\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad(\forall z\in U)\,(\exists t\in A_{N}):\ g_{t}(z)\in U.

The significance of this notion is that a bounded open self-covering set automatically lies in the interior of the attractor.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
|c|>1
, and let
U\subset\mathbb{C}
be a nonempty bounded open set such that

U\subset\mathcal{H}_{c}(U)
for the difference IFS. Then
U\subset\operatorname{int}E(c,N)
.

Proof.

Fix
z_{0}\in U
. Since
U\subset\mathcal{H}_{c}(U)
, there exist sequences
(t_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset A_{N}
and
(z_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset U
such that
z_{k}=f_{t_{k}}(z_{k+1})
for all
k\geq 0
. Induction gives

z_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}t_{j}c^{-j}+z_{m}c^{-m}\qquad(m\geq 1).

Because
U
is bounded, the remainder term
z_{m}c^{-m}
tends to
0
as
m\to\infty
. Hence
z_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t_{j}c^{-j}\in E(c,N)
by the series representation (
2.1
) with digit set
A_{N}
. Since
z_{0}
was arbitrary and
U
is open, we conclude that
U\subset\operatorname{int}E(c,N)
.
∎

We now show how the nearest admissible digit rule produces such self-covering parallelograms. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
with canonical coordinates
(s,v)
, set

\alpha:=\frac{\rho}{y}\,s.

Then
Section

3.1
gives
v^{\prime}=\rho s-yt=y(\alpha-t)
. Thus any digit
t\in A_{N}
satisfying
|\alpha-t|\leq 1
automatically yields
|v^{\prime}|\leq|y|
.

3.3.
Nearest digits and one-step return

Definition 3.5

(Nearest admissible digit rule)
.

For any real number
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let
t^{*}(r)\in A_{N}
denote an element of
A_{N}
minimizing the distance to
r
, with a fixed tie-breaking convention toward
0
. For a point
z
with canonical slanted coordinate
s=\ell_{s}(z;c)
, define
\alpha:=\frac{\rho}{y}\,s
and choose the digit
t=t^{*}(\alpha)
. We call this assignment the
nearest admissible digit rule
.

Lemma 3.6

(Nearest admissible digit)
.

The difference alphabet is
A_{N}=\{-N+1,-N+3,\dots,N-1\}
. If a real number
r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
|r|\leq N
, then there exists an admissible digit
t\in A_{N}
such that
|r-t|\leq 1
. In particular, the parity-constrained choice
t^{*}(r)
from
Section

3.3
satisfies
|r-t^{*}(r)|\leq 1
.

Proof.

The set
A_{N}
forms an arithmetic progression with step
2
. Hence, every point in the interval

[-N,N]
lies within distance
1
of some element of
A_{N}
; at the boundary points, one may take

t=\pm(N-1)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Vertical control)
.

Let
\alpha=\rho s/y
, and let
t\in A_{N}
satisfy
|\alpha-t|\leq 1
.
Then

|v^{\prime}|=|\rho s-yt|\leq|y|.

If in addition
|y|<\mathcal{V}
, then
|v^{\prime}|<\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Since
v^{\prime}=\rho s-yt=y(\alpha-t)
, we have
|v^{\prime}|=|y|\,|\alpha-t|\leq|y|
. The second statement is immediate.
∎

Suppose now that
z\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
, so
|s|<\mathcal{S}
and
|v|<\mathcal{V}
. If a digit
t\in A_{N}
satisfies
|\alpha-t|\leq 1
, then
Section

3.3
gives
|v^{\prime}|\leq|y|
, while
Section

3.1
yields

|s^{\prime}|\leq\rho|v|+\frac{2|x|}{\rho}|v^{\prime}|<\rho\mathcal{V}+\frac{2|x||y|}{\rho}.

The next proposition unifies these two estimates into a one-step self-covering criterion.

Proposition 3.8

(A nearest-digit self-covering criterion)
.

Assume the standing assumptions and the following inequalities:

(i)

|y|<\mathcal{V}
;

(ii)

\rho\mathcal{S}\leq N|y|
;

(iii)

\rho\mathcal{V}+\dfrac{2|x||y|}{\rho}\leq\mathcal{S}
.

Then the parallelogram
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
is self-covering in the
sense of
Section

3.2
. More precisely, the nearest admissible digit rule defines a strategy

\tau:\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})\to A_{N}
such that

g_{\tau(z)}(z)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V}).

Proof.

Define

\tau(z):=t^{*}\!\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\,\ell_{s}(z;c)\right)\qquad\bigl(z\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})\bigr).

Let
z\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
with
(s,v)=(\ell_{s}(z;c),\ell_{v}(z))
, and set
\alpha:=\rho s/y
. Then
\tau(z)=t^{*}(\alpha)
. By the digit-range condition,

|\alpha|=\frac{\rho|s|}{|y|}<\frac{\rho\mathcal{S}}{|y|}\leq N.

Hence
Section

3.3
gives
|\alpha-\tau(z)|\leq 1
, and
Section

3.3
yields
|v^{\prime}|\leq|y|<\mathcal{V}
.

For the slanted coordinate,
Section

3.1
gives

|s^{\prime}|=\left|\frac{2x}{\rho}v^{\prime}-\rho v\right|\leq\frac{2|x|}{\rho}|v^{\prime}|+\rho|v|<\frac{2|x||y|}{\rho}+\rho\mathcal{V}\leq\mathcal{S}.

Thus
g_{\tau(z)}(z)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
for every
z\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
, so
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
is self-covering by (
3.3
).
∎

Proposition 3.10

(Feasibility of the nearest-digit criterion)

Let

3.5.
The exact canonical enclosure

For exterior certification we use an explicit canonical parallelogram containing

. The vertical half-width is the basic quantity; the slanted half-width is recovered exactly from it.

Definition 3.14

(Canonical enclosure)
.

Let
c=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\overline{\mathbb{D}}\cup\mathbb{R})
, and write
c=\rho e^{i\theta}
. Define

(3.6)

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N):=(N-1)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl|\ell_{v}(c^{-k})\bigr|=(N-1)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\,|\sin(k\theta)|.

Also set

(3.7)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N):=(N-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl|\ell_{s}(c^{-k};c)\bigr|=(N-1)\frac{|y|}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N).

The
canonical enclosure
is

\mathcal{E}(c,N):=\overline{\mathcal{P}_{c}\!\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N),\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)\bigr)}.

Proposition 3.15

(Exact canonical enclosure)
.

Let
c=x+iy
with
|c|>1
and
y\neq 0
. Then

\sup_{z\in E(c,N)}|\ell_{v}(z)|=\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N),\qquad\sup_{z\in E(c,N)}|\ell_{s}(z;c)|=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N).

Consequently,

E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N),

and
\mathcal{E}(c,N)
is the unique smallest closed canonical parallelogram containing
E(c,N)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-linear functional. If
z=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}c^{-k}\in E(c,N)
with
a_{k}\in A_{N}
, then

|\varphi(z)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\,|\varphi(c^{-k})|\leq(N-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\varphi(c^{-k})|.

This bound is sharp. Because
0,\pm(N-1)\in A_{N}
, choosing

a_{k}:=(N-1)\sgn\!\bigl(\varphi(c^{-k})\bigr)\in A_{N}\qquad(\text{with }\sgn(0)=0)

defines a point
z_{\varphi}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}c^{-k}\in E(c,N)
for which equality holds. Hence

(3.8)

\sup_{z\in E(c,N)}|\varphi(z)|=(N-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\varphi(c^{-k})|.

Applying (
3.8
) first to
\varphi=\ell_{v}
and using
\ell_{v}(1)=0
gives

\sup_{z\in E(c,N)}|\ell_{v}(z)|=(N-1)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\ell_{v}(c^{-k})|=\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N).

For
\varphi=\ell_{s}(\,\cdot\,;c)
, we use
\ell_{s}(1;c)=\frac{y}{\rho}
and
\ell_{s}(c^{-k};c)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}(c^{1-k})}{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho}\,\ell_{v}(c^{-(k-1)})
for
k\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in E(c,N)}|\ell_{s}(z;c)|

\displaystyle=(N-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\ell_{s}(c^{-k};c)|

\displaystyle=(N-1)\frac{|y|}{\rho}+\frac{N-1}{\rho}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|\ell_{v}(c^{-m})|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N).

Thus every
z\in E(c,N)
satisfies
|\ell_{v}(z)|\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
and
|\ell_{s}(z;c)|\leq\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
, so
E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N)
. Finally, if
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})}
is any closed canonical parallelogram containing
E(c,N)
, then necessarily
\mathcal{V}\geq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
and
\mathcal{S}\geq\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
. Therefore
\mathcal{E}(c,N)
is the unique smallest closed canonical parallelogram containing
E(c,N)
.
∎

4.
Finite capture and inverse certification

The canonical trap and enclosure turn membership in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
into a finite-depth certification problem. Trap entry supplies interior certificates, enclosure extinction supplies exterior certificates, and together they define the capture sets
\Theta_{k}(n)
, the survival sets
\Xi_{k}(n)
, and the finite-capture locus
\Theta_{n}
.

4.1.
Interior and exterior certification

The backward search compares
2c
with two canonical parallelograms. Since
E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N)
, if no enclosure-admissible branch survives to some finite depth, then
2c\notin E(c,N)
, hence
c\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
. On the lens,
\mathcal{C}(c,N)\subset\operatorname{int}E(c,N)
, so if some backward branch satisfies
g_{u}(2c)\in\mathcal{C}(c,N)
, then
2c\in f_{u}(\mathcal{C}(c,N))\subset E(c,N)
, and therefore
c\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
.

Definition 4.1

(Enclosure-admissible words)
.

Let
m\geq 0
. A word

is
\mathcal{E}
-admissible
if every prefix iterate remains inside the parallelogram:

For
m=0
, this means
u=\epsilon
and
2c\in\mathcal{E}(c,N)
.

If
2c\in E(c,N)
, then
2c
has an infinite address
(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
with every prefix point in
E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N)
. Hence at every depth there exists at least one
\mathcal{E}
-admissible word. In particular, if at some depth
k
there are no
\mathcal{E}
-admissible words, then necessarily
2c\notin E(c,N)
and therefore
c\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
by
Section

2.2
. This is the mechanism behind the
Exterior
verdict in
Algorithm

4.1
.

Trap entry (interior certificate).

If
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
and
g_{u}(2c)\in\mathcal{C}(c,N)
for some word
u
, then
g_{u}(2c)\in\operatorname{int}E(c,N)
by
Section

3.4
. Applying
f_{u}
gives
2c\in E(c,N)
, and hence
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
by
Section

2.2
.

Given upper bounds
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}}\geq\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}}\geq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}(c,N):=\overline{\mathcal{P}_{c}(\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}},\widehat{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}})}.

Then

3.5
yields
E(c,N)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(c,N)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
.

, determining whether

Remark 4.2
.

and

Definition 4.3

(Depth-

(Finite-depth extinction gives an exterior certificate)
.

4.3.

On

Proposition 4.9

(Geometry of
\Theta_{0}(n)
)
.

For
n\geq 3
, one has

(4.1)

\Theta_{0}(n)=\{c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}:\ \rho^{2}+|x|<N/2\},\qquad c=x+iy,\ \rho=|c|,\ N=2n-1.

Equivalently,
\Theta_{0}(n)
is the intersection of the two open disks

\left\{|c-\tfrac{1}{2}|<\sqrt{n-\tfrac{1}{4}}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\{|c+\tfrac{1}{2}|<\sqrt{n-\tfrac{1}{4}}\right\},

with the closed unit disk and the real axis removed.

Proof.

Let
c=x+iy
with
y\neq 0
and
\rho=|c|
. By definition,

if and only if
|\ell_{v}(2c)|<\mathcal{V}(c,N)
and
|\ell_{s}(2c;c)|<\mathcal{S}(c,N)

The vertical inequality is

|2y|<\frac{(N-2|x|)|y|}{\rho^{2}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho^{2}+|x|<\frac{N}{2}.

The slanted inequality is

\frac{|\,\operatorname{Im}(2c^{2})\,|}{\rho}<\frac{N|y|}{\rho}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 4|x|<N.

For

N\geq 5

. If
|x|\geq N/4

a contradiction. Thus
|x|<N/4
, i.e.
4|x|<N
.

Finally,
\rho^{2}+|x|<N/2
is equivalent to the pair of inequalities
\rho^{2}\pm x<N/2
. Completing the square gives

x^{2}\pm x+y^{2}<\frac{2n-1}{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left(x\pm\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+y^{2}<n-\frac{1}{4},

which is exactly the stated intersection of the two disks.
Conversely, if

|c\pm 1|\leq|c\pm\tfrac{1}{2}|+\tfrac{1}{2}<\sqrt{n-\tfrac{1}{4}}+\tfrac{1}{2}<\sqrt{2n},

so

; removing
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\cup\mathbb{R}
then gives the stated description.
∎

4.5.

The enclosure geometry also controls the finite combinatorics of the exact backward tree. The next proposition isolates the vertical digit window at each node, and the following theorem gives a uniform outer-radial branching cap.

Proposition 4.13

(Vertical branch truncation)
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and set
N=2n-1
. Let
c=x+iy
with
\rho=|c|>1
and
y\neq 0
, and let
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
denote the vertical half-width of the canonical exterior enclosure. For a node
(s,v)
in canonical coordinates, define the set of vertically admissible digits:

\mathcal{A}_{v}(s):=\{\,t\in A_{N}:\ |\rho s-yt|\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)\,\}.

Any preimage node
(s^{\prime},v^{\prime})
that satisfies the vertical bounding test
|v^{\prime}|\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)
must be generated by a digit
t\in\mathcal{A}_{v}(s)
. Moreover, the cardinality of this admissible set is strictly bounded:

\#\mathcal{A}_{v}(s)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{E}}(c,N)}{|y|}\right\rfloor+1.

Proof.

By

5.3.
The two-step buffer

The next result gives the uniform two-step buffer that drives the closure argument.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, then

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}(c)
.
Consequently, for every
k\geq 0
,

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k}(c)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{k+2}(c).

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, and let
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{V})
be the canonical trap half-widths. By
Section

3.4
, one has
|y|<\mathcal{V}
.

Take
z_{0}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)}
, and write its canonical coordinates as
(s_{0},v_{0})
. Then
|s_{0}|\leq\mathcal{S}
and
|v_{0}|\leq\mathcal{V}
. Apply the nearest admissible digit rule to
z_{0}
, obtaining
t_{1}\in A_{N}
. By
Section

3.3
, the point
z_{1}:=g_{t_{1}}(z_{0})
satisfies
|v_{1}|\leq|y|<\mathcal{V}
. The shear bound from
Section

3.3
then gives

|s_{1}|\leq\frac{2|x|}{\rho}|v_{1}|+\rho|v_{0}|\leq\frac{2|x||y|}{\rho}+\rho\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{S}.

If
|s_{1}|<\mathcal{S}
, then
z_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)
, so
z_{0}\in f_{t_{1}}(\mathcal{C}_{0}(c))\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}(c)\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}(c)
.

If instead

, apply the nearest admissible digit rule once more to
z_{1}
, obtaining

and
z_{2}:=g_{t_{2}}(z_{1})
. Again
|v_{2}|\leq|y|<\mathcal{V}
, and now

|s_{2}|\leq\frac{2|x|}{\rho}|v_{2}|+\rho|v_{1}|<\frac{2|x||y|}{\rho}+\rho\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{S}.

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}(c)

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k}(c)}=\bigcup_{u\in A_{N}^{k}}f_{u}\bigl(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)}\bigr)\subset\bigcup_{u\in A_{N}^{k}}f_{u}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{2}(c)\bigr)=\mathcal{C}_{k+2}(c).

4.2

7.
Discussion and outlook

The main point of the paper is that the fractal closure of a restricted root set can be accessed by finite dynamical certificates. Outside the unit disk the closure of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
admits an exact dynamical description. On the lens
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
we construct a canonical trap and a canonical enclosure, define finite capture, and prove a two-step closure theorem. This gives an exact description of the non-real locus in the lens and, for
n\geq 20
, a global description up to the real trace.

Two features are central. The first is the uniform delay-two phenomenon

\overline{\Theta_{k}(n)}\cap(\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R})\subset\Theta_{k+2}(n),

which shows that finite-depth boundaries are still governed by finite witnesses. The second is the sharp threshold at
n=20
: from that point onward the lens already contains the entire non-real connectedness locus, so the finite-capture description becomes global up to the real trace. In terms of roots, this yields a geometric description of
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
through explicit dynamical traps and an intrinsic stratification by capture depth.

Several natural questions remain. One is to understand the real trace
\partial\mathcal{M}_{n}\cap\mathbb{R}
more precisely from the algebraic viewpoint. Another is to describe the residual interior set
\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\setminus\Theta_{n}
, namely the part of the closure not detected by finite capture itself. A third is to extend the present lens-based mechanism intrinsically to the off-lens region for
2\leq n\leq 19
, where the sharp threshold fails but the root problem remains.
