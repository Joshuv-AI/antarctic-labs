Counting points on braid varieties and the Deligne–Simpson problem
Masoud Kamgarpour, Bailey Whitbread

Abstract
. We solve the isoclinic Deligne–Simpson problem for exceptional groups, completing a program initiated by Sage et al. and Jakob–Yun. As a by-product, we obtain new examples of physically rigid irregular G G -connections on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . Our approach uses the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence to reduce the problem to determining the non-emptiness of certain braid varieties associated to periodic braids. We show that this can be achieved by counting points over finite fields. Our approach is inspired by Lusztig’s construction of a map from conjugacy classes in the Weyl group to unipotent classes.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a complex reductive group. The Deligne–Simpson problem concerns the existence of
G
-connections on curves with prescribed singularities at finitely many points. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, substantial progress has been made using non-abelian Hodge theory
[
Simpson
]
,
\ell
-adic cohomology
[
Katz
]
, and quiver varieties
[
CB
,
Hiroe
]
. In contrast, much less is known for groups of general type.

In this paper, we solve the Deligne–Simpson problem for a class of
G
-connections on
\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,\infty\}
known as
isoclinic
.

1

[
JY
]
but different from
[
SageReview
]
. In particular, what we call isoclinic is called toral in
[
SageReview
]
. Following
[
HJ
]
, we reserve the term toral for a more general notion; see §
2
.
Such connections have an irregular singularity at
0
with a regular semisimple Levelt–Turrittin leading term, and a regular singularity at
\infty
.
Important examples include generalised Frenkel–Gross connections (also known as generalised Kloosterman connections)
[
FrenkelGross
,
HeinlothNgoYun
]
, Yun’s
\theta
-connections
[
YunEpipelagic
,
ChenRigid
]
, and Coxeter connections
[
KS-rigid
,
KS-galois
]
.

Suppose
G
is a simple group and let
W
denote its Weyl group. Then it is known that the slope of an isoclinic connection is of the form
\nu=d/m
, where
m
is a regular number for
W
(in the sense of Springer
[
Springer
]
) and
d
is a positive integer coprime to
m
, cf.

[
JY
, Lemma 2.2]
. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

There exists a (necessarily unique) unipotent conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}_{\nu}\subset G
such that there is an isoclinic
G
-connection of slope
\nu
and unipotent monodromy
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\nu}
.

2

2

Here
\geq
denotes the usual partial order on unipotent conjugacy classes:
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{C}}\supseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
When
\nu>1
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{\nu}=\{1\}
. When
\nu<1
and
G
is of exceptional type, the classes
\mathcal{C}_{\nu}
are listed in §
A.1
.

When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
m=n
, this result was established by Sage and collaborators
[
SageEtAl
,
LivesaySageNguyen
]
using intricate linear algebra. Jakob and Yun
[
JY
]
proved the theorem for classical groups via non-abelian Hodge theory. They also treated some exceptional cases; however, groups of type
E
remained unresolved. Our approach for exceptional groups is different and uses instead the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.

The version of the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence we need was established recently by Hohl and Jakob
[
HJ
]
, building on prior work of Boalch
[
BoalchTopology
]
. The key input is that irregular connections give rise to positive braids
[
BBAMY
]
. The moduli space of irregular connections is then identified with the corresponding braid variety.
These varieties first appeared in the work of Deligne on the
K(\pi,1)
Conjecture
[
Deligne
]
, and have recently attracted considerable interest due to their central role in symplectic topology, knot invariants, and cluster algebras
[
STZ
,
Trinh
,
GorskyKivinenSimental
,
IanEtAl
,
Mellit
,
GLSB
]
. To take into account the monodromies of connections, we need to work with variants of braid varieties introduced by Trinh
[
Trinh
]
.

It turns out that the class of braids arising from isoclinic connections coincide with
periodic
braids, i.e., those with a non-trivial central power.
More precisely, we show that the braid associated to an isoclinic connection of slope
\nu=d/m
is
(\widetilde{w_{m}})^{d}
, where
w_{m}
is an
m
-Springer element
(in the sense of
[
BM
]
), and
\widetilde{w_{m}}
denotes its lift to the positive braid monoid. Deep results of Broué–Michel and Bessis
[
BM
,
BessisKPi1
]
imply that, up to conjugacy in the braid group,
\widetilde{w_{m}}
is the unique
m^{\text{th}}
-root of the full twist.

The above discussion implies that the existence of an isoclinic connection of slope
\nu
and monodromy
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the non-emptiness of the braid variety associated to
(\widetilde{w_{m}})^{d}
and
\mathcal{C}
. We shall see that this in turn is equivalent to the condition

(Bw_{m}B)^{d}\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset.

The problem of determining when the intersection of a Bruhat double coset with a conjugacy class is non-empty has been studied extensively and is known to be difficult even for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, cf.
[
EG04
,
EG07
,
CLT10
,
Lusztig
,
LusztigCSmall
,
Malten
]
. When
w
is an element of minimal length in its conjugacy class, Lusztig proved that there exists a (necessarily unique) unipotent conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}_{w}
such that a unipotent class
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
BwB\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathcal{C}_{w}
. This yields Lusztig’s map

\displaystyle\{\textrm{conjugacy classes in }W\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\{\textrm{unipotent conjugacy classes in }G\}

\displaystyle[w]

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathcal{C}_{w}.

For groups of exceptional type, Lusztig’s proof relied on counting points over finite fields
[
Lusztig
]
. As he observed, the count reduces to character values of the finite reductive group
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the finite Hecke algebra
H_{q}
.

Our proof of Theorem
1
for exceptional groups follows the same strategy; that is, we count points on the relevant braid varieties (more precisely, braid stacks) over finite fields. As in Lusztig’s case, the count reduces to character theory of
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
H_{q}
. The necessary character computations are carried out with the CHEVIE package for Julia developed by Michel
[
Michel
]
. Note that
m
-Springer elements and their powers do not usually have minimal length in their conjugacy classes. Moreover, in general, we need to take powers of Bruhat double cosets. Thus, our main theorem is not a formal consequence of Lusztig’s result.

As a byproduct of Theorem
1
, we obtain new examples of cohomologically rigid isoclinic connections; see §
A.2
. We show that these connections are physically rigid by proving that the corresponding braid varieties are singletons. Constructing the
\ell
-adic analogues of these connections and their Hecke eigensheaves is an interesting open problem.

Finally, it is natural to ask for explicit realisations of the isoclinic connections whose existence is guaranteed by our main theorem. We make partial progress in this direction by establishing Conjecture 5.7.(2) of
[
SageReview
]
, concerning Coxeter
G
-connections with minimal monodromy.

1.1.
Structure of the text

In §
2
, we introduce toral connections (which are a generalisation of isoclinic connections), and discuss the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence and the Deligne–Simpson problem for them. We also recall Lusztig’s theorem and outline his proof.

In §
3
, we focus on toral connections that are isoclinic. We first give an explicit description of their associated braids. Then, we prove Theorem

by counting points on the corresponding braid stacks over finite fields. We conclude by discussing rigid isoclinic connections and explicit realisations of Coxeter connections.

In the appendix, we give tables listing minimal unipotent classes for isoclinic connections and rigid connections. We also give sample computations with the code used to establish Theorem

.

Toral connections

Let
G
be an almost simple complex algebraic group,
B\subset G
a Borel subgroup,
T\subset B
a maximal torus,
W
the Weyl group,
\Phi
the root system, and
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{t}
denote the Lie algebras of
G
and
T
, respectively.

2.1.
Basic definitions

For each positive integer
m
, let
\mathcal{K}_{m}=\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{1/m})\!)
, and write
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\times}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{K})
denote the formal punctured disk. Let
\nabla
be a formal
G
-connection, i.e., a connection on a
G
-bundle on
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
. By a classical theorem of Turrittin and Levelt (cf.
[
BabbittVaradarajan
]
), there exists a positive integer
m
such that
\nabla
is
G(\mathcal{K}_{m})
-gauge equivalent to a connection of the form

(1)

d+(A(t^{1/m})+\cdots)\frac{dt}{t}.

Here

(2)

A(t^{1/m})=A_{d}\,t^{-d/m}+A_{d-1}\,t^{-(d-1)/m}+\cdots+A_{1}t^{-1/m}\in t^{-1/m}\mathfrak{t}[t^{-1/m}],

A_{d}
is non-nilpotent, and the symbol
\cdots
in (
1
) denotes an element of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{C}([\![t]\!]))
.

The polynomial
A=A(t^{1/m})
is called an
irregular class
associated to
\nabla
, the element
A_{d}\in\mathfrak{t}
is a
Levelt–Turrittin leading term
, and the rational number
d/m
is the
slope
of
\nabla
.

An element
A(t^{1/m})\in t^{-1/m}\mathfrak{t}[t^{-1/m}]
is called an
irregular class
if it arises as the irregular class of a formal connection on
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
. Following
[
HJ
]
, we call
A(t^{1/m})

toral
if the only Weyl group element fixing
A_{d},A_{d-1},\cdots,A_{1}
is the trivial element. Likewise, a formal
G
-connection is called
toral
if its irregular class is toral. This condition is independent of the choice of
m
and the gauge transformation.

2.2.
Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

Let
A=A(t^{1/m})\in\mathfrak{t}[t^{-1/m}]
be a toral irregular class and
\mathcal{C}\subset G
a conjugacy class.
Let
\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
denote the stack of
G
-connections on
\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,\infty\}
with an irregular singularity at
0
of irregular class
A
, and a regular singularity at
\infty
with monodromy
\mathcal{C}
. Our aim is to describe the Betti analogue of this stack.

Let
B_{W}^{+}
denote the positive braid monoid. As explained in
[
BBAMY
, §4.3.3]
, the element
A(t^{1/m})
determines a positive braid
\beta_{A}\in B_{W}^{+}
, unique up to a cyclic shift. We now define the corresponding braid stack.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
denote the flag variety of
G
.
Given
w\in W
, we write
\widetilde{w}\in B_{W}^{+}
for its positive lift.
For a positive braid
\beta=\widetilde{w_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{w_{d}}\in B_{W}^{+}
, define the corresponding braid stack by

\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C}):=\bigg\{(g,F_{1},\dots,F_{d+1})\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathfrak{B}^{d+1}\,\bigg|\,gF_{1}=F_{d+1},\,\,w(F_{i},F_{i+1})=w_{i},\;i=1,\ldots,d\bigg\}\bigg/G,

where
w(F,F^{\prime})\in W
denotes the relative position of the flags
F
and
F^{\prime}
. (The associated braid variety is defined by taking the GIT quotient instead.) One readily checks that

\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{w},\mathcal{C})=(BwB\cap\mathcal{C})/B.

Theorem 2
.

There is an equivalence of groupoids

\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathcal{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})(\mathbb{C})
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, this follows from results of Boalch
[
BoalchTopology
]
. For general
G
, see
[
HJ
]
, Theorem 3.5.3 and Proposition 4.2.2.
It is expected that the above equivalence can be upgraded to an isomorphism of complex analytic stacks, cf.
[
BBAMY
, Conjecture 4.4.4]
.

2.3.
The Deligne–Simpson problem

Let
A\in t^{-1/m}\mathfrak{t}[t^{-1/m}]
be an irregular class and
\mathcal{C}\subset G
a conjugacy class.
The corresponding Deligne–Simpson problem asks when
\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
is non-empty.
By the above discussion, when
A
is toral, this problem is equivalent to determining when
\mathcal{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})
is non-empty.

We now give a group-theoretic criterion for the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C})
for a general positive braid
\beta=\widetilde{w_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{w_{n}}\in B_{W}^{+}
. Let

B(\beta):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(Bw_{i}B)\subseteq G.

Proposition 3
.

We have
\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
B(\beta)\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
B(\beta)\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
and choose an element

g=b_{1}w_{1}b_{2}w_{2}\cdots b_{n}w_{n}b_{n+1}

in this intersection.
Then

(g,\,B,\,b_{1}w_{1}B,\,b_{1}w_{1}b_{2}w_{2}B,\,\dots,\,gB)\in\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C}).

Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
and choose
(g,F_{1},\dots,F_{n+1})
in this stack.
Using the
G
-action, we may assume
F_{1}=B
.
The condition
w(F_{1},F_{2})=w_{1}
implies
F_{2}=b_{1}w_{1}B
for some
b_{1}\in B
.
Similarly, the condition
w(F_{2},F_{3})=w_{2}
implies
F_{3}=b_{1}w_{1}b_{2}w_{2}B
for some
b_{2}\in B
.
Continuing in this way, we obtain
F_{n+1}=gB
, where

g=b_{1}w_{1}\cdots b_{n}w_{n}b_{n+1},\qquad b_{i}\in B.

By assumption
g\in\mathcal{C}
; thus, we have

g\in\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}Bw_{i}B\Big)\cap\mathcal{C}
,
as required.
∎

For future reference, we record the following simple result:

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
\beta\in B_{W}^{+}
contains the full twist
\Pi_{W}
. Then
B(\beta)=G
; in particular,
B(\beta)\cap\mathcal{C}
is non-empty for every conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}\subset G
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that

B(\Pi_{W}):=(Bw_{0}B)(Bw_{0}B)=G.

This follows from the fact that in a connected algebraic group
G
, the product
UV
of two dense open subsets equals the whole group. Indeed, for every
g\in G
,
gV^{-1}
and
U
are also dense open subsets; thus, their intersection is non-empty. This implies that
g=uv
for some
u\in U
and
v\in V
.
∎

2.4.
Nice braids

Let us call a braid
\beta\in B_{W}^{+}

nice
if there exists a (necessarily unique) unipotent class
\mathcal{C}_{\beta}\subset G
such that for any unipotent class
\mathcal{C}\subset G
, we have
\mathcal{M}(\beta,\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
. It seems reasonable to conjecture that braids arising from toral irregular classes are nice. Our main theorem amounts to the statement that braids arising from isoclinic irregular classes are nice. This will be discussed further below. In this subsection, we recall a related result of Lusztig.

Theorem 5

(
[
Lusztig
]
)
.

Suppose
w\in W
has minimal length in its conjugacy class. Then
\widetilde{w}
is nice.

Let us outline Lusztig’s proof for groups of exceptional type. Let
k
be an algebraic closure of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and consider an almost simple algebraic group over
k
with the same root system as
G
. By abuse of notation, we denote this group by
G
as well. Assume that the characteristic of
k
is a good prime for
G
. Then, the unipotent classes of
G(k)
are in bijection with the unipotent classes of
G(\mathbb{C})
, cf.
[
Premet
]
.

Now, choose an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-split rational structure on
G
, and let
F:G\to G
denote the Frobenius map. Let
H_{q}
be the finite Hecke algebra. The space of functions
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{B}^{F}]
then carries the structure of a
(\mathbb{C}[G^{F}],H_{q})
-bimodule. We write
(g,T_{w})
for the operator
f\mapsto g\cdot f\cdot T_{w}
, where
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Recall that there is a canonical bijection between irreducible representations of
W
and irreducible unipotent principal series representations of the finite group
G^{F}
. For
E\in\mathrm{Irr}(W)
, we let
\rho_{E}
denote the corresponding unipotent representation of
G^{F}
. Likewise, there is a canonical bijection between irreducible representations of
H_{q}
and irreducible representations of
W
; we denote by
E_{q}
the representation of
H_{q}
corresponding to
E
.

In
[
Lusztig
, §1.2]
, Lusztig observes that

(3)

\big|\big((BwB\cap\mathcal{C})/B\big)^{F}\big|=\frac{1}{|G^{F}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{F}}\mathrm{tr}((g,T_{w}),\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{|G^{F}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{F}}\sum_{E\in\mathrm{Irr}(W)}\mathrm{tr}(T_{w},E_{q})\,\mathrm{tr}(g,\rho_{E}).

For groups of exceptional types, Lusztig explicitly computes the right-hand side using a computer and determines when it is non-zero.

Using (
3
), one can also find elements
w\in W
such that
\widetilde{w}
is not nice. One example is when
G
is of type
F_{4}
and
w
is
s_{2}s_{3}s_{2}s_{4}s_{3}s_{2}s_{3}
; see §
A.3
. Note that this is a reduced expression for
w
; however,

does not have minimal length in its conjugacy class, since it is conjugate to
s_{4}s_{3}s_{2}
.

3.
Isoclinic connections

An irregular class
A=A_{d}\,t^{-d/m}+\cdots+A_{1}t^{-1/m}\in t^{-1/m}\mathfrak{t}[t^{-1/m}]
is called
isoclinic
if its leading coefficient
A_{d}
is regular semisimple, i.e.,
\mathrm{Stab}_{W}(A_{d})=\{1\}
. Likewise, a formal
G
-connection is called
isoclinic
if its irregular class is isoclinic.

Our first goal is to determine the braid
\beta_{A}
associated to an isoclinic irregular class. To this end, we need to recall some facts about regular elements in
W
.

3.1.
Springer elements

Let
m
be a regular number for
W
. By
[
Springer
, Proposition 4.10]
, there exist
w\in W
and
x\in\mathfrak{t}
such that

w\cdot x=e^{2\pi i/m}x\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Re}(\alpha(x))>0\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\Phi^{+}.

Following Broué and Michel
[
BM
]
, we call
w
an
m
-Springer element
. For example, when
W=S_{4}
with the standard Coxeter structure,
s_{1}s_{3}s_{2}
is a Springer element while
s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}
is not. We refer the reader to Appendix 1 of
op. cit.
for a list of Springer elements in all types.

Let
B_{W}
denote the Artin braid group of
W
.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
w\in W
is an
m
-Springer element.

(1)

If
m
is elliptic, then
w
has minimal length in its conjugacy class.

(2)

For all positive integers

, we have
\ell(w^{d})=d\,\ell(w)=d\,|\Phi|/m
.

(3)

We have
(\widetilde{w})^{m}=\Pi_{W}
.

(4)

Every
m^{\text{th}}
root of
\Pi_{W}
is
B_{W}
-conjugate to
\widetilde{w}
.

(5)

Suppose

is an element of
W
such that
\big(\widetilde{w^{\prime}}\big)^{m}=\Pi_{W}
. Then

and

are related by a cyclic shift.

Proof.

Part (1) is proved in Proposition 4.10 of
[

]
. Part (2) is proved in
[

. Part (3) is Proposition 3.12 of
[

]
. Part (4) is Theorem 12.4 of
[

]
. Part (5) follows from Theorem 3.17 of
[

.

Let
W(m)\subseteq W
denote the conjugacy class corresponding to a regular number

. We have seen that there exist elements of

which are not

-Springer with respect to our choice of positive roots. However, by

The braid associated to an isoclinic class

Let
A
be an isoclinic irregular class of slope

\gcd(d,m)=1
. Then

is a regular number for

Let

be an

Proposition 7

Up to cyclic shift, we have

If

Outline of the proof.

Let

be a toral irregular class. We first recall the definition of the conjugacy class in the braid group

associated to

. Observe that we can think of

\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{\mathrm{reg}}/W

. Thus,

. One can show that this class is independent of the circle (provided

Now suppose

. It is easy to see that the braid associated to the irregular class

. Thus,

must be an

root of

. By Theorem

\beta_{A}=\widetilde{w_{m}}
. The proof for general

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1

We now give the proof of our main theorem. The case
\nu>1
follows from Lemma
4
. So, suppose
\nu<1
. Let
w
be an
m
-Springer element.
We follow Lusztig’s strategy (discussed in §

) and count points on
\mathcal{M}((\widetilde{w})^{d},\mathcal{C})
over finite fields.
We have the following generalisation of (
3
):

(4)

|\mathcal{M}((\widetilde{w})^{d},\mathcal{C})^{F}|=\frac{1}{|G^{F}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{F}}\mathrm{tr}((g,(T_{w})^{d}),\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{|G^{F}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{F}}\sum_{E\in\mathrm{Irr}(W)}\,\mathrm{tr}((T_{w})^{d},E_{q})\,\mathrm{tr}(g,\rho_{E}).

See, for instance,
[
Trinh
, Lemma 8.5.7]
where a closely related formula appears. (Note, however, Trinh considers a slightly different action of
H_{q}
on
\mathcal{F}
.)

We prove the theorem by explicitly computing the right hand side of (
4
) for every Springer element
w
and every unipotent class
\mathcal{C}
. The naive approach is to expand
(T_{w})^{d}
in terms of the standard basis of
H_{q}
and compute the above value using the character table of
H_{q}
and
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Indeed, one can verify the theorem in many cases by following this approach. However, for certain slopes in types
E_{6}
,
E_{7}
and
E_{8}
, this approach becomes computationally unfeasible.

We can dramatically simplify the computation using the following fact:

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathbf{c}(E_{q}):=|\Phi|-\mathbf{a}(E_{q})-\mathbf{A}(E_{q})
be the content of
E_{q}
, where
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{A}
are Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
- and
\mathbf{A}
-function, respectively. Let
w
be a Springer element. Then, we have:

\mathrm{tr}((T_{w})^{d},E_{q})=q^{\nu\,\mathbf{c}(E_{q})}\,\mathrm{tr}(w^{d},E).

Proof.

See, for example,
[
BM
, Corollary 4.20]
and
[
Trinh
, Corollary 9.2.2]
.
∎

The above discussion implies

(5)

|\mathcal{M}((\widetilde{w})^{d},\mathcal{C})^{F}|=\frac{1}{|G^{F}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}^{F}}\sum_{E\in\mathrm{Irr}(W)}\,q^{\nu\,\mathbf{c}(E_{q})}\,\mathrm{tr}(w^{d},E)\,\mathrm{tr}(g,\rho_{E}).

Observe that the summands involve only character values of
W
and
G^{F}
. These quantities can be quickly computed using CHEVIE
[
Michel
]
, allowing us to prove the theorem.
We refer the reader to
[
Whitbread
]
for the code computing the above sum and §
A.3
for examples. ∎

3.4.
Rigid isoclinic connections

In
[
JY
, §7.9]
, a table listing local data of
potentially
rigid elliptic isoclinic connections is presented. However, the table was left incomplete, since the authors were only able to determine the existence of such connections in a limited number of cases. Using our main theorem, we can complete this table; see §
A.2
. Moreover, we can prove:

Proposition 9
.

Let
G
be an almost simple group of exceptional type. Then the cohomologically rigid elliptic isoclinic
G
-connections (whose local data is given in §
A.2
) are physically rigid.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a unipotent class such that the stack
\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
contains an elliptic cohomologically rigid connection. Let
\mathtt{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
be its coarse moduli space. We would like to show that this variety has a unique
\mathbb{C}
-point. Equivalently, we want to prove that the coarse moduli space
\mathtt{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})
on the Betti side has a unique
\mathbb{C}
-point.
By
[
KatzAppendix
]
, it suffices to show that
\mathtt{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})
has a unique
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point.
Now, it is known that (under the assumption that
w
is elliptic) the action of
G
on

\bigg\{(g,F_{1},\dots,F_{d+1})\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathfrak{B}^{d+1}\,\bigg|\,\begin{subarray}{c}\displaystyle gF_{1}=F_{d+1}\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle w(F_{i},F_{i+1})=w,\,i=1,\ldots,d\end{subarray}\bigg\}

has finite stabilisers. Thus,
\mathcal{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})
is a Deligne--Mumford stack.
4
4

4

Here is an argument in the de Rham setting. It is known that an elliptic isoclinic connection is irreducible, cf.
[
Yi
, Lemma 17]
. It follows that every connection in
\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
is irreducible. In particular, these connections have finite stabilisers. Therefore,
\mathrm{Conn}(A,\mathcal{C})
is a Deligne–Mumford stack.
As noted in
[
stacks
, §2]
, the number of points of a Deligne–Mumford stack over a finite field is the same as the number of points of its coarse moduli space. Finally, our computer calculations show that in all cohomologically rigid elliptic cases, we have

|\mathcal{M}(\beta_{A},\mathcal{C})^{F}|=1
.
∎

We expect this result to hold for classical groups as well; see
[
JY
, Table 11]
for the local data of the corresponding connections.

3.5.
Explicit realisation

Let
G
be an almost simple algebraic group with Coxeter number
h
. Let
d<h
be a positive integer coprime to
h
and let
\nu=d/h
. Choose a generator
x_{\alpha}
for each root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Let

N_{d}:=\sum_{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)=-d}x_{\alpha},\qquad E_{d}:=\sum_{\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)=h-d}x_{\alpha},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{\nu}:=d+(N_{d}+t^{-1}E_{d})\frac{dt}{t}.

Following
[
KS-rigid
]
, we call
\nabla_{\nu}
a Coxeter
G
-connection. Note that
\nabla_{\nu}
is regular singular at
\infty
with monodromy
\exp(N_{d})
. As shown in
op. cit
, it is irregular (and isoclinic) at
0
with slope
d/h
.

Proposition 10
.

The connection
\nabla_{\nu}
has minimal (local) monodromy among all Coxeter
G
-connections of slope
d/h
and unipotent monodromy, i.e.
\exp(N_{d})=\mathcal{C}_{d/h}
.

Proof.

When
G
is of classical type, the partition corresponding to
N_{d}
was computed in
[
KS-rigid
, §4.3]
. One readily verifies that the result agrees with the explicit description of
\mathcal{C}_{d/h}
given in Table 1 of
[
JY
]
.
When
G
is of exceptional type, we have checked that
\exp(N_{d})
agrees with the class
\mathcal{C}_{d/h}
given in §
A.1
, using a GAP package developed by de Graaf
[
dG
]
.
∎

We refer the reader to
[
KS-galois
]
for a description of the differential Galois group of
\nabla_{\nu}
.
