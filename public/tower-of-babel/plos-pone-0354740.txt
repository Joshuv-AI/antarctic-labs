Robust disturbance-aware actor–critic reinforcement learning for multi-DOF robotic manipulators

Viet Ngu Nguyen, Thi Minh Tam Le, Duc Hung Pham, Huu Cuong Nguyen

ABSTRACT
Building on recent insights that augmenting reinforcement‐learning policies with disturbance estimates improves robustness and sim-to-real transfer, this paper proposes a disturbance-aware actor–critic RL framework for high‐precision robotic manipulators. We derive the dynamics of manipulators ranging from two to six degrees of freedom and design a nonlinear disturbance observer that provides real-time estimates of lumped uncertainties. Unlike conventional feedforward-only designs, the proposed DACRL framework deeply integrates DOB information into the actor-critic’s learning state, cost function, and update laws to explicitly compensate for unknown dynamics and disturbances. A Lyapunov-based analysis proves that tracking errors, observer errors and neural-network weight errors remain uniformly ultimately bounded. Extensive simulations show that the proposed controller achieves sub-degree tracking errors across multiple DOF cases, with the 2-DOF example achieving steady-state errors below ±0.02 rad (Joint 1) and ±0.05 rad (Joint 2) and delivering faster convergence and smoother torques than conventional RL and DOB baselines. The disturbance-aware controller generalizes across trajectories and payloads, offering improved robustness while retaining learning flexibility. While the current study focuses on performance and robustness, future work could explore the integration of disturbance-observer-based control barrier functions to formally address safety constraints during the learning process. Simulation results suggest that the disturbance-aware controller can improve robustness while retaining learning flexibility. Real-platform validation remains an important direction for future work.

1. Introduction Reinforcement learning (RL) has been increasingly adopted in robotics as a data-driven paradigm for sequential decision-making and control in complex nonlinear systems [ 1 ]. Broad overviews of RL algorithms and methodological families further consolidate this trend and clarify common design choices [ 2 ]. Recent monographs also provide rigorous foundations for sequential decision-making and optimal control perspectives that are frequently used in continuous-time robotic control formulations [ 3 ]. Within this context, model-based RL has attracted growing attention due to its potential for improved sample efficiency and stronger links to control-theoretic structure [ 4 ]. In addition, unified viewpoints connecting RL with dynamic programming and model predictive control help motivate control-oriented RL formulations and performance indices for real-time control [ 5 ]. Meanwhile, continuing progress in algorithmic design and discovery has expanded the practical deployment space of RL in complex systems [ 6 ], and complementary advances in scalable learning architectures and training heuristics further broaden applicability in robotics settings [ 7 ]. Despite these developments, achieving high-precision trajectory tracking for robotic manipulators using RL remains challenging in the presence of strong nonlinear coupling, parameter variations, friction, payload changes, and time-varying disturbances. Safety and constraint satisfaction motivate safe-RL mechanisms such as control barrier functions (CBFs) for enforcing state/input constraints [ 8 ]. Learning-based CBF formulations and DOB-assisted CBF designs further provide practical routes for integrating learning with safety guarantees under uncertainty [ 9 ]. In parallel, disturbance observers (DOBs) provide a computationally efficient means to estimate lumped uncertainties and reject matched disturbances in nonlinear systems [ 10 ]. DOB-based robust tracking control has been widely studied for manipulators under modeling errors and external disturbances [ 11 ]. Additional DOB/observer-enhanced robust control designs demonstrate improved tracking performance and robustness under practical disturbance conditions [ 12 ]. These considerations have motivated observer-integrated adaptive optimal control and reinforcement learning frameworks for uncertain nonlinear systems [ 13 ]. DOB-based actor–critic learning is one representative direction that combines online policy improvement with disturbance compensation [ 14 ]. Other DOB-enhanced ADP/RL schemes emphasize robustness and practical stability properties while learning in continuous time [ 15 ]. Disturbance-aware objective shaping has also been explored to better reflect uncertainty in the performance index used for learning and optimization [ 16 ]. Further, stability-oriented analyses are commonly developed to establish boundedness/UUB-type guarantees under approximation and observer assumptions [ 17 ]. Observer-integrated adaptive optimal control for robotic manipulators further extends these ideas toward real robotic platforms under disturbances and uncertainties [ 18 ]. However, in several DOB–RL implementations, the DOB is primarily designed and tuned independently, and its estimate is used mainly for feedforward compensation, while the learning objective and policy updates are only indirectly influenced by observer mismatch. Under time-varying disturbances, this separation may require the learning component to accommodate disturbance-induced transients and fast components, which can reduce learning efficiency and adversely affect control smoothness. These observations motivate a co-designed disturbance-aware actor–critic framework in which DOB information is embedded into the learning loop at both the state and objective levels, while preserving a control-theoretic structure suitable for real-time implementation. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. 1 Disturbance-aware objective shaping: A disturbance-aware performance index is formulated by explicitly incorporating the measurable DOB filter residual as a penalty term into the RL cost function. Unlike prior works [ 13 , 16 – 18 ] that construct the learning objective solely from tracking error and control effort — leaving the learning loop without a direct signal when the DOB estimate is inaccurate the proposed cost explicitly penalizes estimation mismatch, incentivizing the policy to operate in regions where the observer performs well. 2 Co-designed DOB–Actor–Critic learning rule: The actor update law is augmented with a disturbance-aware gradient term derived from the measurable residual, and the learning state includes both the disturbance estimate and the filter residual. This addresses two gaps absent from [ 13 , 14 , 17 , 18 ]: (i) cascade designs provide no gradient signal preventing the policy from selecting torques that amplify the DOB residual; and (ii) augmenting the state with the disturbance estimate alone cannot distinguish a well-converged observer from a poorly converged one, whereas including the filter residual allows the critic to condition value estimation on current observer quality. 3 Composite control architecture for manipulators: A composite torque law integrates a nominal/robust baseline, DOB compensation, and an actor corrective term, consistent with [ 10 – 12 , 19 , 20 ]. Unlike cascade architectures where the DOB and AC blocks operate independently, the proposed structure couples the DOB output into the learning loop at both the state level and the objective level, ensuring the actor corrective term is learned with explicit awareness of current observer performance rather than treating the DOB-compensated plant as a fixed, uncertainty-free system. 4 Stability-oriented analysis: A Lyapunov-based analysis is provided to establish boundedness (UUB-type) of closed-loop signals under standard observer and function-approximation assumptions, aligning online learning with stability requirements in continuous-time control [ 13 – 18 ]. 5 Progressive evaluation and scalability: The method is evaluated on a 2-DOF manipulator and further assessed on a 6-DOF industrial manipulator under composite disturbances, with comparisons to DOB-only, standard actor–critic, and DOB-based ADP/RL baselines [ 13 – 18 ]. Fault-tolerant RL control of manipulators under actuator faults further motivates evaluation under non-ideal actuator conditions [ 21 ]. Modern dynamics and simulation/control references are also included to support modeling and implementation practices [ 22 – 24 ]. 2. Related work This section provides a brief review of the foundational research informing the present study. While RL for robotic control has been extensively studied for optimal control [ 1 ]– [ 6 ], achieving robust, real-time deployment under modeling errors and disturbances remains a persistent challenge [ 7 ]. To ensure reliability, safety and constraint enforcement through Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) has emerged as a key area of interest, particularly when integrated with RL and disturbance-aware filtering [ 8 , 9 ]. Complementary to this, DOB for robust tracking offers an efficient means of estimating lumped uncertainties in nonlinear manipulators, though its efficacy is often limited by bandwidth-noise trade-offs [ 10 ]– [ 12 ]. The key technical distinction of the proposed framework is threefold. First, prior DOB-RL designs ([ 13 , 16 – 18 ]) formulate the RL objective independently of DOB estimation quality: the cost function penalizes tracking error and control effort but not disturbance mismatch. In DACRL, the measurable filter residual 𝐫 ~ = 𝐫 − 𝐝 ^ appears directly in the cost via a λ | 𝐫 ~ | 2 penalty, creating an explicit learning incentive to reduce estimation mismatch. Second, the actor update in DACRL includes a gradient term Δ W a | 𝐫 ~ | 2 , which discourages policies that amplify the DOB residual a mechanism absent from all cascade designs reviewed. Third, both 𝐝 ^ and 𝐫 ~ are included in the learning state, allowing the critic to condition value estimation on current observer quality rather than just the estimated disturbance magnitude. The engineering motivation for the disturbance-aware coupling term in the actor update is to prevent the policy from selecting torque commands that, while reducing tracking error in the short term, amplify uncompensated disturbance components and degrade observer convergence. A standard actor update driven solely by the TD error carries no such signal. By explicitly penalizing the DOB filter residual in the policy gradient, the actor is guided to operate the plant in regimes where the disturbance observer remains effective, thereby preserving the disturbance rejection capability that the composite control architecture relies upon. 5. Simulation scenarios and results 5.1. Simulation setup All simulations were implemented in Python 3.10 using PyTorch 2.2 and SciPy 1.13 for numerical integration. The DACRL controller is implemented online with a fixed sampling time of Ts = 1 ms. The plant dynamics are integrated using a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method, while the actor, critic, and DOB are updated at every sampling instant. The actor adopts a linear-in-weights policy using normalized and clipped handcrafted polynomial/RBF features of the disturbance-aware state. The critic uses an RBF-based linear-in-weights value function. RBF centers are selected by k-means clustering from a short warm-up rollout, and RBF widths are chosen from the mean distance to neighboring centers. The actor and critic weights are updated by projected gradient descent, with projection bounds used to avoid parameter drift. Convergence is evaluated empirically through bounded actor/critic losses, bounded weights, reduced TD error, bounded DOB estimation error, post-transient tracking RMSE, peak tracking error, and torque-energy behavior. To maintain consistency with the Lyapunov/UUB-oriented analysis, both learning components follow the linear-in-weights approximation used in (27)–(32). This design prioritizes analytical tractability, bounded online adaptation, and real-time feasibility. Nevertheless, it may sacrifice approximation capacity compared with deep neural networks, and the impact of higher-capacity approximators will be investigated in future work. Specifically, the actor is parameterized as 𝐮 a ( x ) = 𝐖 a ⊤ ϕ ( x ) where ϕ ( x ) is a handcrafted basis vector. The critic is parameterized as 𝐕 ^ ( x ) = 𝐖 c ⊤ ψ ( x ) where ψ ( x ) is an RBF feature vector. Online updates use per-step gradient descent with projection (31)–(32) to keep weights bounded and avoid parameter drift, rather than deep-network backpropagation. RBF centers were obtained by k-means clustering over state samples collected from a short warm-up rollout; widths were set to the mean distance to the k-nearest centers. The nominal parameters in Tables 3 and 4 were selected to obtain stable online learning and smooth torque responses; however, the influence of these parameters on tracking accuracy, torque energy, and disturbance-estimation performance should be further quantified through a dedicated sensitivity study. The current implementation uses a first-order dob, linear-in-weights actor–critic approximators, and projection-based gradient updates to reduce computational complexity and support real-time control. nevertheless, the computational cost increases with the number of dofs, the size of the disturbance-aware state, and the number of actor/critic basis functions. Limitations and experimental validation. Although the proposed DACRL framework shows robust tracking performance in both 2-DOF and 6-DOF simulations, the present study is limited to numerical validation. Therefore, the results should be interpreted as a simulation-based proof-of-concept rather than full evidence of real-world deployment readiness. Practical factors such as sensor noise, communication delay, actuator saturation, friction uncertainty, payload variation, and real-time computational constraints may affect closed-loop performance. Future work will validate the proposed controller on a physical manipulator, including payload-change tests, external disturbance rejection tests, trajectory generalization, and comparisons with DOB-only and conventional actor–critic controllers under identical hardware conditions. Safety supervision, including torque limits and possibly control-barrier-function-based constraints, will also be considered before online learning is deployed on hardware. The present stability analysis assumes bounded and piecewise differentiable matched disturbances. This assumption is standard in many DOB-based robust control designs, but it does not fully cover abrupt impacts, discontinuous disturbances, severe actuator saturation, or actuator faults such as bias, partial loss of effectiveness, or stuck actuators. Although the simulations include sinusoidal disturbances, periodic step changes, and amplitude variations, more aggressive non-smooth disturbances and fault scenarios require additional fault-tolerant and safety mechanisms. Future work will extend the proposed DACRL framework by incorporating fault-detection and isolation, actuator-saturation handling, adaptive fault-tolerant compensation, and safety constraints such as control barrier functions. 6. Conclusion and future work This paper presents DACRL, a co-designed reinforcement learning framework for high-precision robotic trajectory tracking under nonlinear dynamics and disturbances. By integrating a Nonlinear Disturbance Observer (NDOB) directly into the actor–critic learning loop via state augmentation and objective shaping, the system enables real-time disturbance rejection and explicit reaction to uncertainties. Supported by a Lyapunov-based stability analysis ensuring UUB convergence, the method was validated on 2-DOF and 6-DOF (Mitsubishi RV-2FBD) manipulators. Simulation results demonstrated improved tracking accuracy, faster convergence, and smoother torque profiles compared with standard RL and cascade designs under the tested disturbance profiles. However, experimental validation on a physical manipulator is still required to assess real-time implementation issues, actuator constraints, sensor noise, and sim-to-real transfer performance. The current DACRL framework does not explicitly enforce actuator limits or formal safety constraints. Although the simulated torques remain bounded and relatively smooth, practical deployment requires constraints on joint positions, velocities, torques, and collision avoidance to be incorporated into the controller. Future work will integrate actuator saturation handling, safety filters, and control-barrier-function-based constraints to ensure safe online learning and hardware implementation.
