Critical subgraphs and the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs
Nguyen Thu Hang, Thanh Vu

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple graph. We demonstrate a method for using t t -admissible subgraphs of G G to determine the regularity of the t t -th symbolic power of the cover ideal of G G . As an application, we compute the regularity of powers of cover ideals of bipartite unicyclic graphs.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
13D02, 05E40

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph. In
[
7
]
, Dung, Hang, and Vu introduced the notion of
admissible subgraphs
and used it to compute the depth of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs. This approach is made possible by Hochster’s depth formula
[
16
]
together with the observation that the associated radicals of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs correspond to the cover ideals of certain subgraphs of
G
, which they called admissible subgraphs. In this work, we demonstrate how to use admissible subgraphs to study the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs. We now introduce some notation.

Let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
, and let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of
S
. Denote by
\Min(I)
the set of minimal associated primes of
I
. Then the
t
-th symbolic power of
I
, denoted by
I^{(t)}
, is defined by

I^{(t)}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Min(I)}I^{t}S_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap S.

By a result of Cutkosky, Herzog, Trung
[
5
]
and independently of Kodiyalam
[
18
]
, the regularity function of the ordinary powers of
I
is eventually linear. On the other hand, the regularity of the symbolic powers of
I
can exhibit much more complicated behavior
[
4
]
. When
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, Herzog, Hibi, and Trung
[
13
]
showed that the regularity function of
I^{(t)}
is eventually quasi-linear.

We now introduce the edge ideal and the cover ideal of a graph, which are the main objects of study in this work. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
and the
cover ideal
of
G
, denoted by
I(G)
and
J(G)
, respectively,
are defined by

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G)),\qquad J(G)=\bigcap_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(x_{i},x_{j}).

The study of the regularity of ordinary and symbolic powers of
I(G)
and
J(G)
is an active area of research. Despite this, the precise behavior of these functions for general graphs remains unknown. For edge ideals, most recent work focuses on Minh’s conjecture, which states that
\reg(I(G)^{t})=\reg(I(G)^{(t)})
. In particular, it is not yet known whether
\reg(I(G)^{(t)})
is eventually linear. Explicit calculations have shown that Minh’s conjecture holds for certain classes of graphs; for a survey on this topic, see
[
21
]
. On the other hand, Dung, Hien, Nguyen, and Trung
[
6
]
recently demonstrated that the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs
might not be eventually linear. The study of the regularity of powers of cover ideals has largely centered on the Herzog–Hibi–Ohsugi conjecture
[
12
]
, which asserts that all powers of the cover ideal of a chordal graph are componentwise linear. This conjecture has led to explicit formulas for the regularity of ordinary and symbolic powers of cover ideals for several classes of graphs. We refer to the survey article
[
9
]
and the references therein for the current status of the conjecture.

Recently, Hien and Trung
[
14
]
established a sharp bound for the regularity of symbolic powers of squarefree monomial ideals. Building on this result, Fakhari
[
8
]
determined the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of Cameron–Walker graphs and of claw-free graphs with no cycles of lengths other than
3
and
5
. However, the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of cycles remains unknown.

Minh, Nam, Phong, Thuy, and Vu
[
24
]
showed how to compute the regularity of a monomial ideal via its associated radicals and proved that Minh’s conjecture holds for the second and third powers. These methods have since been used to verify the conjecture for other classes of graphs and to study the regularity of powers of edge ideals
[
22
]
. In this work, we further demonstrate the usefulness of this approach in studying the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of unicyclic graphs. We note that this problem is closely related to the study of the homological dimension of the simplicial complex associated with the cover ideal. The following result on the regularity of the cover ideal of a unicyclic graph is new. For a graph
G
, we denote by
\tau_{\max}(G)
the maximum cardinality of a minimal vertex cover of
G
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph whose unique cycle is
C_{n}
. Then

\reg\bigl(J(G)\bigr)=\begin{cases}\tau_{\max}(G)+1&\text{if }n\equiv 1\pmod{3}\text{ and }G\text{ is reducible to }C_{n},\\
\tau_{\max}(G)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For powers, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph whose unique cycle is
C_{n}
with
n
even. Then, for all
t\geq 2
,

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{t}\bigr)=\begin{cases}t\,\tau_{\max}(G)+1&\text{if }n=4\text{ and }G\text{ is reducible to }C_{4},\\
t\,\tau_{\max}(G)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The notion of a graph being reducible to
C_{n}
is adapted from the work of Pham and Vu
[
25
]
; we refer to Section
3
for further details. For symbolic powers, we actually prove a stronger result, which includes the corresponding statement for symbolic powers of cover ideals of other non-bipartite unicyclic graphs, including all odd cycles. It is closely related to the concept of asymptotic cover degree, for which we also refer to Section
3
for further details.

Section
2
is devoted to developing the machinery needed to use admissible subgraphs to compute the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs. In Section
3
, we apply this machinery to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Critical subhypergraphs

In this section, we recall basic notation and results used throughout the paper. We then introduce the notion of
t
-critical subhypergraphs and explain how to use them to compute the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of hypergraphs. Throughout the paper, let
\mathrm{k}
be a field,
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a polynomial ring, and
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
the
maximal homogeneous ideal
of
S
.

2.1.
Simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
; that is, a collection of subsets of
[n]
that is closed under taking subsets. It is clear that
\Delta
is uniquely determined by its maximal elements under inclusion, called
facets
. The set of facets of
\Delta
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
. For a facet
F
of a simplicial complex
\Delta
, we denote by
\Delta\setminus F
the simplicial complex whose set of facets is

\mathcal{F}(\Delta\setminus F)=\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\setminus\{F\}.

For a face
F\in\Delta
, the link of
F
in
\Delta
is defined by

\lk_{\Delta}F=\{G\in\Delta\mid F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\emptyset\}.

Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be simplicial complexes. The
join
of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, denoted by
\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}
, is the simplicial complex on the vertex set
V(\Delta_{1})\sqcup V(\Delta_{2})
whose facets are precisely the sets
F\cup G
, where
F
is a facet of
\Delta_{1}
and
G
is a facet of
\Delta_{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex.

(1)

The
q
-th reduced homology group of
\Delta
with coefficients in
\mathrm{k}
, denoted by
\widetilde{H}_{q}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})
, is the
q
-th homology group of the augmented oriented chain complex of
\Delta
over
\mathrm{k}
.

(2)

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
acyclic
if
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})=0
for all
i
.

(3)

If
\Delta
is not acyclic, then the
homological dimension
of
\Delta
over
\mathrm{k}
, denoted by
\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta)
, is defined by

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta)=\max\{\,i\mid\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\,\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. Then

(1)

\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
if and only if
\Delta
is the empty complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\{\emptyset\}
).

(2)

If
\Delta
is a cone over some
t\in[n]
or
\Delta
is the void complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\emptyset
), then it is acyclic.

For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
be a squarefree monomial of
S
. We now recall the Stanley-Reisner correspondence.

Definition 2.3
.

(1)

For a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
, the Stanley-Reinser complex of
I
is defined by
\Delta(I)=\{F\subseteq[n]\mid x_{F}\notin I\}
.

(2)

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by
I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta)
.

2.2.
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity

Let
L
be a nonzero finitely generated graded
S
-module. Let
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)
denote the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with support in
\mathfrak{m}
. Then the regularity of
L
is defined by

\reg(L)=\max\{j+i\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)_{j}\neq 0,\ \text{for }i=0,\ldots,\dim(L),\ j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We now recall the following result of Minh, Nam, Phong, Thuy, and Vu
[
24
]
, which provides a method for computing the regularity of a monomial ideal via its associated radicals. To this end, we introduce some notation. For an exponent
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we denote by
x^{\mathbf{a}}
the monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
in
S
, and we set
|\mathbf{a}|=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}.
The support of
\mathbf{a}
is defined by

\supp(\mathbf{a})=\{i\in[n]\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}.

For a positive integer
t
, we define the truncation of
\mathbf{a}
at level
t
by
\mathbf{a}^{<t}=(a_{1}^{<t},\ldots,a_{n}^{<t})
, where

a_{i}^{<t}=\begin{cases}a_{i},&\text{if }a_{i}<t,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.4
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
, and let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. The
degree complex
of
I
in degree
\mathbf{a}
is defined by

\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\Delta\bigl(\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\bigr).

By
[
24
, Lemmas 2.12 and 2.19]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. Then

\reg(S/I)=\max\bigl\{|\mathbf{a}|+i\;\big|\;\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\ i\geq 0,\ \widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\\
\text{for some }F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)\text{ such that }F\cap\supp(\mathbf{a})=\emptyset\bigr\}.

2.3.
Hypergraphs and their cover ideals

We recall some basic notions from hypergraph theory; for further details, see
[
3
]
. Let
G
be a simple hypergraph with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. We assume throughout that
G
has no isolated vertices. Each edge
e
of
G
is a subset of
V(G)
, and no edge is properly contained in another; that is, for any two distinct edges
e,f\in E(G)
, neither
e\subsetneq f
nor
f\subsetneq e
holds.

A hypergraph
H
is a
subhypergraph
of
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. It is an
induced subhypergraph
of
G
if a subset
e\subseteq V(H)
is an edge of
H
if and only if
e
is an edge of
G
.

A subset
W\subseteq V(G)
is called a
cover
of
G
if
W\cap e\neq\emptyset
for every edge
e\in E(G)
. It is called a
minimal cover
if no proper subset of
W
is a cover of
G
. The maximum cardinality of a minimal cover of
G
is denoted by
\tau_{\max}(G)
.

When
|e|=2
for every edge
e
of
G
, we simply call
G
a
graph
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
and
cover ideal
of
G
, denoted by
I(G)
and
J(G)
, are defined by

I(G)=(x_{e}\mid e\in E(G))\quad\text{and}\quad J(G)=\bigcap_{e\in E(G)}(x_{i}\mid i\in e).

In particular,
J(G)
is the Alexander dual of
I(G)
. It is well known that

J(G)=(x_{W}\mid W\text{ is a minimal cover of }G).

2.4.
Symbolic powers of cover ideals and critical subhypergraphs

Definition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph on
n
vertices. The
t
-th symbolic power of
J(G)
is defined by

J(G)^{(t)}=\bigcap_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\in E(G)}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})^{t}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph, and let
x^{\mathbf{a}}
be a monomial in
S
. Then

\sqrt{J(G)^{(t)}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=J(H),

where
H
is the subhypergraph of
G
with edge set

E(H)=\bigl\{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\in E(G)\;\big|\;a_{i_{1}}+\cdots+a_{i_{s}}<t\bigr\}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sqrt{J(G)^{(t)}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(\bigcap_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\in E(G)}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})^{t}\right):x^{\mathbf{a}}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\Bigl\{(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})\;\Big|\;\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\in E(G)\text{ and }a_{i_{1}}+\cdots+a_{i_{s}}<t\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=J(H).

The conclusion follows.
∎

We also need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. Let
F
be a face of
\Delta(J(G))
. Let
H
be the hypergraph with vertex set
V(H)=V(G)\setminus F
and edge set
E(H)=\{e\in E(G)\mid e\subseteq V(G)\setminus F\}.
Then

\lk_{\Delta(J(G))}(F)=\Delta(J(H)).

Proof.

By definition, a subset
W\subseteq V(G)
is a face of
\Delta(J(G))
if and only if there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
such that
W\cap e=\emptyset
.

Now let
W\subseteq V(G)\setminus F
. Then
W
is a face of
\lk_{\Delta(J(G))}(F)
if and only if
F\cup W
is a face of
\Delta(J(G))
. Equivalently, there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
such that

(F\cup W)\cap e=\emptyset.

This is equivalent to the conditions
e\cap F=\emptyset
and
e\cap W=\emptyset
. The first condition implies that
e\in E(H)
, while the second condition means precisely that
W
is a face of
\Delta(J(H))
. Therefore,

\lk_{\Delta(J(G))}(F)=\Delta(J(H)),

as desired.
∎

Note that in Lemma
2.9
,
J(H)
is viewed as an ideal in the polynomial ring whose variables are indexed by the vertex set
V(G)\setminus F
, and the Stanley–Reisner correspondence is taken with respect to this ground set.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]
, and let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. The
t
-
admissible subhypergraph
of
G
with respect to
\mathbf{a}
is the hypergraph
H
with vertex set

V(H)=\{i\mid a_{i}<t\}

and edge set

E(H)=\bigl\{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\in E(G)\;\big|\;a_{i_{1}}+\cdots+a_{i_{s}}<t\bigr\}.

The pair
(H,\mathbf{a})
is called a
t
-admissible pair
of
G
.

Definition 2.11
.

A
t
-admissible pair
(H,\mathbf{a})
of
G
is called a
t
-critical pair
if
\Delta(J(H))
is not acyclic. In this case, the hypergraph
H
is called a
t
-critical subhypergraph
.

The following lemma is the main technical result of this section.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
G
be a nonempty hypergraph. Then

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=2+\max\Bigl\{\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)+|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|\;\Big|\;(H,\mathbf{a})\text{ is a $t$-critical pair of }G\Bigr\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
and
[
23
, Remark 2.13]
, there exist an exponent
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and a face
F
of

\Delta=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)

such that
a_{j}<t
for all
j
,
F\cap\supp(\mathbf{a})=\emptyset
,

\widetilde{H}_{i-1}\bigl(\lk_{\Delta}(F);\mathrm{k}\bigr)\neq 0
, and

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=i+1+|\mathbf{a}|.

By Lemma
2.8
, we have
\Delta=\Delta(J(H))
, where
H
is the subhypergraph of
G
consisting of all edges

e=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}
such that
a_{i_{1}}+\cdots+a_{i_{s}}<t.
Let
\mathbf{b}
be the exponent defined by

x^{\mathbf{b}}=x^{\mathbf{a}}\cdot\prod_{j\in F}x_{j}^{t}.

Since
F\cap\supp(\mathbf{a})=\emptyset
,
\mathbf{b}^{<t}=\mathbf{a}
. By Lemmas
2.8
and
2.9
, we obtain

\lk_{\Delta}F=\Delta(J(K)),

where
K
is the induced subhypergraph of
H
on the vertex set
V(K)=V(G)\setminus F.
In particular,
(K,\mathbf{b})
is a
t
-critical pair of
G
. Furthermore, since

\widetilde{H}_{i-1}\bigl(\Delta(J(K));\mathrm{k}\bigr)\neq 0,

we have

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=i+1+|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(K))\bigr)+|\mathbf{b}^{<t}|.

Conversely, let
(H,\mathbf{a})
be a
t
-critical pair of
G
. Set
U=\{i\mid a_{i}<t\}.
By definition,
V(H)=U
, and
\Delta(J(H))
is not acyclic. Let

F=\{i\mid a_{i}\geq t\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}^{<t}.

Let
K
be the subhypergraph of
G
such that

J(K)=\sqrt{J(G)^{(t)}:x^{\mathbf{b}}}.

By definition,
F\cap e=\emptyset
for every edge
e\in E(H)\subseteq E(K)
. In other words,
F
is a face of
\Delta(J(K))
. By Lemma
2.9
,

\lk_{\Delta(J(K))}F=\Delta(J(H)).

Applying Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)\geq 2+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)+|\mathbf{b}|.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

(1)

The idea of eliminating the link in Lemma
2.5
was initiated by Hoang and Vu
[
15
, Lemma 2.9]
. In our setting, the resulting formula is somewhat cleaner, with the effect of removing the link being absorbed by the truncation operation. In general, however, this simplification is largely cosmetic. For cover ideals, the formulation is particularly natural, since it involves subhypergraphs of the original hypergraph.

(2)

If we adopt the convention that
\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta)=-\infty
whenever
\Delta
is acyclic, then in Lemma
2.12
the maximum may be taken over all
t
-admissible pairs. We state the result in terms of critical pairs in order to emphasize the role of
t
-critical subhypergraphs in the study of the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals.

(3)

Note that every squarefree monomial ideal is the cover ideal of some hypergraph. Hence, the results in this section apply to all symbolic powers of squarefree monomial ideals. Similar approaches have also been used by Hang and Trung
[
10
]
and by Hien and Trung
[
14
]
to study the regularity of symbolic powers of squarefree monomial ideals.

2.5.
Symbolic Polyhedra

Let
G
be a hypergraph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
, and assume that
G
has no isolated vertices. The
symbolic polyhedron
of
J(G)
is defined by

\SP(G)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\middle|\;x_{i}\geq 0\text{ and }\sum_{i\in e}x_{i}\geq 1\text{ for all }e\in E(G)\right\}.

Definition 2.14
.

The
asymptotic cover degree
of
G
is defined by

\delta(G)=\max\{|\mathbf{v}|\mid\mathbf{v}\text{ is a vertex of }\SP(G)\}.

For a homogeneous ideal
I
of
S
, let
\omega(I)
denote the maximal degree of a minimal generator of
I
. Dung, Hien, Nguyen, and Trung
[
6
]
proved the following fundamental theorem.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph without isolated vertices. Then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\omega\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)}{t}=\delta(G).

The following result, essentially due to Hien and Trung
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
, provides a sharp upper bound for
|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|
when
(H,\mathbf{a})
is a
t
-critical pair of
G
. It was proved in the language of critical exponents by Minh and Vu
[
23
, Theorem 2.18]
. We give an alternative proof using our terminology.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
G
be a nonempty hypergraph, and let
(H,\mathbf{a})
be a
t
-critical pair of
G
. Then

|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|\leq\delta(G)(t-1).

Proof.

When
t=1
, the claim is immediate from the definition. Assume now that
t\geq 2
. We claim that
(H,s\mathbf{a})
is an
(s(t-1)+1)
-critical pair of
G
for every positive integer
s
. Indeed, for every edge
e\in E(H)
, we have

\sum_{i\in e}sa_{i}=s\sum_{i\in e}a_{i}\leq s(t-1)<s(t-1)+1.

For every edge
e\in E(G)\setminus E(H)
, we have

\sum_{i\in e}sa_{i}=s\sum_{i\in e}a_{i}\geq st\geq s(t-1)+1.

Hence, by definition,
(H,s\mathbf{a})
is an
(s(t-1)+1)
-critical pair of
G
. By Lemma
2.12
, we obtain

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(s(t-1)+1)}\bigr)>s|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|.

Dividing both sides by
s(t-1)+1
and letting
s\to\infty
, we deduce that

\frac{|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|}{t-1}\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(s(t-1)+1)}\bigr)}{s(t-1)+1}=\delta(G),

where the equality follows from Theorem
2.15
. The desired inequality follows.
∎

The following lemma allows us, in many situations, to reduce to the case of connected hypergraphs.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be hypergraphs with vertex sets
V(G_{1})
and
V(G_{2})
, respectively, such that

V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\emptyset.
Assume that
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
. Then

\tau_{\max}(G)=\tau_{\max}(G_{1})+\tau_{\max}(G_{2})

and

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G_{1}))\bigr)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G_{2}))\bigr)+2.

Proof.

The statement concerning
\tau_{\max}
follows immediately from the definition.

For simplicity, let

\Delta=\Delta(J(G)),\qquad\Delta_{1}=\Delta(J(G_{1})),\qquad\Delta_{2}=\Delta(J(G_{2})).

Then

\Delta=\Delta_{1}*V(G_{2})\;\cup\;V(G_{1})*\Delta_{2}.
Both
\Delta_{1}*V(G_{2})
and
V(G_{1})*\Delta_{2}
are cones, and hence are acyclic. Moreover,

\Delta_{1}*V(G_{2})\;\cap\;V(G_{1})*\Delta_{2}=\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}.

It follows from the Mayer–Vietoris sequence that

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2})+1.

By the Künneth formula for joins,

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2})=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta_{1})+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta_{2})+1.

The conclusion follows.
∎

3.
Applications

In this section, we apply the results of the previous section to determine the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of bipartite unicyclic graphs. We begin by recalling some basic definitions.

A cycle
C_{n}
on
n
vertices is the graph with vertex set

V(C_{n})=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set

E(C_{n})=\{\{1,2\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\},\{1,n\}\}.

The homology of the independence complexes of cycles was determined by Kozlov
[
19
]
. In particular, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C_{n}
denote a cycle on
n\geq 3
vertices. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{\max}(C_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(C_{n}))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{2n-4}{3}\right\rfloor.

In particular,

\tau_{\max}(C_{n})-\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(C_{n}))\bigr)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n\equiv 1\pmod{3},\\
2&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A graph
G
is called
unicyclic
if it contains exactly one cycle. In particular, every connected unicyclic graph can be obtained by attaching a tree to each vertex of a cycle.

It turns out that the graph simplification introduced in
[
25
]
, which consists of truncating branches at branching points, is particularly useful for analyzing critical subgraphs of unicyclic graphs. The reduction lemmas in this section are motivated by these simplification moves. We now introduce some terminology. Let
u
be a vertex of a graph
G
. The
neighborhood
of
u
in
G
, denoted by
N_{G}(u)
, is the set of all vertices
v
such that
\{u,v\}
is an edge of
G
. The
degree
of
u
, denoted by
\deg_{G}(u)
, is the cardinality of
N_{G}(u)
. A vertex
u
is called a
branching point
if
\deg_{G}(u)>2
, and a
leaf
if
\deg_{G}(u)=1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph, and let
u
be a branching point of
G
. A path that starts at
u
and ends at a leaf is called a
pure branch
if every vertex on the path, except for
u
, has degree at most
2
in
G
.

We define the following reduction moves:

(1)

If a vertex
u
has
s\geq 2
leaves attached to it, remove all but one of these leaves.

(2)

If a vertex
u
has
s\geq 2
pure branches of length
2
attached to it, remove all but one of these branches.

(3)

If a vertex
u
has a pure branch of length at least
3
attached to it, shorten the branch by
3
by removing the three vertices closest to the leaf, including the leaf itself.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. If a graph
H
can be obtained from
G
by a sequence of the reduction moves above, then
H
is called a
reduction
of
G
. We also say that
G
is
reducible
to
H
.

Example 3.3
.

The graph in Figure
1
is reducible to
C_{4}
by first removing a branch of length
2
and then deleting the three vertices closest to the leaf in a pure branch of length
3
.

Figure 1.
A graph reducible to
C_{4}
.

Example 3.4
.

The graph in Figure
2
is not reducible to
C_{4}
. At one of its branch vertices, it has both a branch of length
1
and a branch of length
2
. Hence, the simplicial complex associated with its cover ideal is acyclic, so the graph cannot appear as a critical candidate.

Figure 2.
A graph not reducible to

.

We now outline the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
3.15
.

(1)

First, we consider the case where
G=C_{n}
separately.

(2)

Suppose that
G
has a leaf. Then either one of the reduction steps described above can be applied. In each case, we reduce to an induced subgraph
G_{1}
of
G
such that every critical subgraph of
G
gives rise to a critical subgraph of
G_{1}
, with the homological dimension decreasing by the appropriate amount. This is established in the reduction lemmas that follow.

(3)

If none of the reduction steps can be applied, then
\Delta(J(G))
is acyclic. In other words, any critical subgraph of
G
must arise from a proper subgraph of
G
, and the conclusion follows by induction. Thus, either
G
is reducible to a path of length at most
2
, which is straightforward to verify, or to a cycle, which is handled in the first step, or
\Delta(J(G))
is acyclic and
G
contains a proper subgraph to which one of the reduction steps can be applied.

(4)

When considering powers, one only needs to keep track of the degree
|\mathbf{a}|
of the exponent vector
\mathbf{a}
in a critical pair
(H,\mathbf{a})
. The decrease in
\tau_{\max}
between
G
and its reductions accounts for the corresponding change in this degree.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a simple graph, and let
u
be a vertex of
G
. Assume that
u
has at least two leaf neighbors
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. Let

. Then:

(1)

\tau_{\max}(H)\leq\tau_{\max}(G)-1;

(2)

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)-1.

Proof.

(1)
Let
C
be a minimal cover of
H
with
|C|=\tau_{\max}(H)
. If

, then

is a minimal cover of

\tau_{\max}(G)\geq|C|+1=\tau_{\max}(H)+1,

which proves the claim. Now assume that

L=\{v\in N_{H}(u)\mid v\text{ is a leaf}\}\text{ and }A=\{v\in N_{H}(u)\mid v\notin C\text{ and }v\text{ is not a leaf}\}.

is a minimal cover of

, and
|C^{\prime}|\geq|C|.
Since

\tau_{\max}(H)\leq\tau_{\max}(G)-1.

(2)
Let

\Delta=\Delta(J(G)),\qquad F=V(G)\setminus\{u,v_{1}\},\qquad\Gamma=\Delta\setminus F.

Then
\Gamma
is a cone with apex
v_{1}
, and hence is acyclic. By the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, we obtain

(1)

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Delta)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(F\cap\Gamma)+1.

Now,

is the union of the facet

F_{1}=V(G)\setminus\{u,v_{1},v_{2}\},

and a simplicial complex
\Gamma_{1}
whose facets are of the form

V(G)\setminus\bigl(\{u,v_{1}\}\cup e\bigr),

where
e
is an edge of
H
other than
\{u,v_{2}\}
. Note that
\Gamma_{1}
is a cone with apex
v_{2}
. Hence,

(2)

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(F\cap\Gamma)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(F_{1}\cup\Gamma_{1})=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(F_{1}\cap\Gamma_{1})+1.

Now,

is the simplicial complex
\Sigma
consisting of the facets

V(G)\setminus\bigl(\{u,v_{1},v_{2}\}\cup e\bigr),

where
e
is an edge of
H
other than
\{u,v_{2}\}
.

On the other hand, decompose

as
\Delta(J(H))=F_{2}\cup\Lambda,
where

F_{2}=V(H)\setminus\{u,v_{2}\}=V(G)\setminus\{u,v_{1},v_{2}\},

and

is the subcomplex generated by all facets other than
F_{2}
. Then

Since
\Lambda
is also a cone with apex

, it follows that

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(F_{2}\cap\Lambda)+1=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}(\Sigma)+1.

Combining this with Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)=\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G
be a simple graph, and let

be a vertex of

. Assume that

has a leaf

Remark 3.13

Lemma 3.14
.

Let

be a cycle on

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=\begin{cases}2t+1&\text{if }n=4,\\

Proof.

Note that

\tau_{\max}(G)=\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor.
If
n=4
, then

J(G)=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4}),
which is a complete intersection. Hence,

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=2t+1
for all
t\geq 1
.

Now assume that
n\neq 4
. By degree considerations and Lemma
2.12
, it suffices to show that if
(H,\mathbf{a})
is a critical pair for
G
, then

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)+|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|\leq t\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor-2.

First, assume that
H
is a proper subgraph of

. We may assume that

. Let
G_{1}
be the path obtained from

by deleting the edge

. Then, by definition,

is a critical pair for

. By Lemma
2.12
and the case of paths (which follows from
[
2
]
, or can be proved by induction as in the proof of Theorem
3.15
), we obtain

|\mathbf{a}|+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)\leq t\tau_{\max}(G_{1})-2=t\tau_{\max}(G)-2.

Now assume that

. In other words,

a_{i}+a_{i+1}\leq t-1\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,n.

Hence,

|\mathbf{a}|\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil(t-1).

Therefore,

|\mathbf{a}|+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil(t-1)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr).

When
n=3
or
5
, we have

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil=\tau_{\max}(G)\quad\text{and}\quad\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)=\tau_{\max}(G)-2,

and therefore

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil(t-1)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)=t\tau_{\max}(G)-2.

If
n\geq 6
, then

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil<\tau_{\max}(G)\quad\text{ and }\quad\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)\leq\tau_{\max}(G)-1.

Consequently,

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil(t-1)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(G))\bigr)\leq t\tau_{\max}(G)-2,

which yields the desired inequality.
∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph whose unique cycle is
C_{n}
. Assume that
\delta(G)=\tau_{\max}(G)
. Then for all
t\geq 2
,

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)=\begin{cases}t\tau_{\max}(G)+1&\text{if }n=4\text{ and }G\text{ is reducible to }C_{4},\\
t\tau_{\max}(G)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the case
n\neq 4
, and leave the case
n=4
to the interested reader. The proof in this case follows a similar line of argument, with additional attention to the homological dimension.

By degree reasons, it suffices to show that

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{(t)}\bigr)\leq t\tau_{\max}(G).

We proceed by induction on the number of vertices of
G
. The case
n\leq 2
is clear.

By Lemma
2.12
, it suffices to show that if
(H,\mathbf{a})
is a
t
-critical pair of
G
, then

\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)+|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|\leq t\tau_{\max}(G)-2.

By Lemma
3.14
, we now assume that
G
has a leaf. We perform a sequence of reduction moves. We present one case in detail; the remaining cases are handled similarly.

Suppose that
G
has a vertex
u
with at least two leaves, say
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. First, assume that one of these leaves does not belong to
H
, say
v_{1}\notin V(H)
. Let
G_{1}=G\setminus v_{1}
. Then
(H,\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
, where
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
is the restriction of
\mathbf{a}
to
V(G_{1})
, is a
t
-critical pair of
G_{1}
. Hence, by the induction hypothesis,

|\mathbf{a}^{\prime<t}|+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)\leq t\tau_{\max}(G_{1})-2.

By Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{a}^{\prime<t}|+(t-1)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq t\tau_{\max}(G_{1})-2+(t-1)

\displaystyle\leq t\tau_{\max}(G)-2.

Now assume that all leaves attached to
u
remain in
H
. Let
G_{1}=G\setminus v_{1}
as above, and set
H_{1}=H\setminus v_{1}
. Then
(H_{1},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
is a
t
-critical pair of
G_{1}
. By the induction hypothesis and Lemma
3.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{a}^{<t}|+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H))\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{a}^{\prime<t}|+(t-1)+\hdim_{\mathrm{k}}\bigl(\Delta(J(H_{1}))\bigr)+1

\displaystyle\leq t\tau_{\max}(G_{1})-2+(t-1)+1

\displaystyle\leq t\tau_{\max}(G)-2.

Thus, we may assume that every vertex of
G
has at most one leaf attached to it.

We then apply the reduction steps described in Lemmas
3.6
,
3.7
, and
3.8
to reduce to the case where
G
is either a path of length at most
2
or a unicyclic graph in which each vertex on the cycle has at most one pure branch of length
1
or
2
attached to it. In the latter case, we apply Lemmas
3.11
and
3.12
to reduce to the case of a tree, and hence to a path of length at most
2
, for which the result is clear.

We note that a given reduction step may be applied more than once. For example, after shortening a branch, several branches of the same length may arise; after removing these branches, the branching vertex itself may become part of a pure branch. However, at each step, the number of vertices decreases by at least one.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

When
G
is a bipartite graph, it follows from a result of Herzog, Hibi, and Trung
[
13
]
that
J(G)^{t}=J(G)^{(t)}
for all
t
. Hence, we have
\tau_{\max}(G)=\delta(G)
; see also
[
6
, Theorem 4.9]
. The conclusion then follows from Theorem
3.15
.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

(1)

In general, if
H
is a subgraph of
G
, one does not necessarily have

\tau_{\max}(H)\leq\tau_{\max}(G);
see
[
8
]
.

(2)

By
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
,
\reg\bigl(J(C_{n})^{t}\bigr)=\reg\bigl(J(C_{n})^{(t)}\bigr)
where
C_{n}
is an odd cycle. Furthermore,

\delta(C_{n})=\tau_{\max}(C_{n}).
Hence, Theorem
3.15
yields a formula for the regularity of powers of cover ideals of cycles.

(3)

Bijender, Kumar, and Kumar
[
2
]
proved that all powers of the cover ideal of a simplicial forest are componentwise linear. In particular,

\reg\bigl(J(G)^{t}\bigr)=t\tau_{\max}(G)

for all
t\geq 1
. Consequently, the symbolic powers of cover ideals of path ideals of paths are also componentwise linear. It would be interesting to determine the regularity of symbolic powers of cover ideals of path ideals of cycles, as the corresponding problem for path ideals of cycles is itself subtle; see
[
1
]
.
