Generalized transverse momentum distributions at small- x x Preprint: ZTF-EP-26-04
Sanjin Benić, Yoshikazu Hagiwara, Boris Šarić, Eric Andreas Vivoda

Abstract
We compute the complete set of the leading-twist gluon and sea-quark generalized transverse momentum distributions (GTMDs) in the small- x x , or eikonal, approximation at vanishing skewness ξ = 0 \xi=0 . All the gluon GTMDs become expressed in terms of the basic gluon dipole operator featuring also proton helicity-flips. Consequently, we establish universal relations between otherwise distinct GTMDs that hold in the small- x x limit. The obtained results are systematically projected onto the transverse momentum dependent distributions (TMD) and the generalized parton distribution (GPD) cases, recovering known results where available. In case of sea-quarks all the GTMDs are given in terms of the gluon dipole convoluted with a hard kernel. We generalize the unpolarized sea-quark GTMD to non-zero momentum transfers and find new results for the proton helicity-flip distributions. We pay special attention to their perturbative, high transverse momentum, tails to confirm that it becomes governed by the small- x x gluon GPDs. The obtained results provide guidance to the phenomenological modeling of the GTMDs but also allow for their explicit computations.

We now turn to the computation of

For

and

In order to compute

\Gamma=\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}

(57)

The remaining steps of the computation proceed analogously to those for
U^{q}
. Thus, we can obtain the result simply by replacing
\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}_{2}\to-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\times\boldsymbol{q}_{2}
to find

(58)

with the kernel

A_{L}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1},\boldsymbol{q}_{2})=\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\times\boldsymbol{q}_{2}}{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}^{2}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{q}_{2}^{2}}\right)\,.

(59)

The results (
55

and in

(

and

According to (
44
), we need to expand
U^{q}
and
L^{q}
to linear order in
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
. Since
A_{U}
is symmetric with respect to
\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{q}_{2}
exchange, we already know the hard kernel in

must be even in

and so

A_{U}(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g},\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}/2)+A_{U}(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}/2,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g})=A(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})+O(|\boldsymbol{\Delta}|^{2})\,,

(60)

where we retain only the constant term

A(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})=2\left[1-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}-\boldsymbol{q}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{q}^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(61)

Here
\boldsymbol{q}
is a shorthand
\boldsymbol{q}\equiv\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g}
. The
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
dependence will be obtained from the dipole GTMD
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{g},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
. On the other hand,
A_{L}
is antisymmetric under
\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{q}_{2}
making the hard kernel in (
58

\boldsymbol{\Delta}\to-\boldsymbol{\Delta}
and so the expansion starts with a linear term

A_{L}(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g},\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}/2)+A_{L}(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}/2,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g})=\Delta^{i}B^{i}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})+O(|\boldsymbol{\Delta}|^{3})\,,

(62)

where

\begin{split}&B^{i}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})=2\delta^{ij}k^{j}\frac{\boldsymbol{q}\times\boldsymbol{k}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}B_{1}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})-\epsilon^{ij}q^{j}B_{2}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})\,,\\
&B_{1}=\frac{1-\boldsymbol{k}^{2}B_{2}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}-\boldsymbol{q}^{2}}\,,\\
&B_{2}=\frac{\log\left(\boldsymbol{k}^{2}/\boldsymbol{q}^{2}\right)}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}-\boldsymbol{q}^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(63)

We start by calculating the case without proton helicity flip. Since the hard kernels
A(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})
and
B^{i}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{k})
are helicity independent there will only be contributions from helicity independent Pomeron and Odderon. Up to linear order in
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
we have

\begin{split}&xU^{q}_{+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}A\left(\mathcal{P}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{g}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}}{M^{2}}\mathcal{O}\right)\,,\\
&xL^{q}_{+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}(\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{k})B_{2}\mathcal{P}\,.\end{split}

(64)

For the second term in (
62
) we have used the
\epsilon
-identity in the following form

\boldsymbol{k}^{2}(\boldsymbol{q}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta})=(\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{k})(\boldsymbol{k}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta})+(\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta})(\boldsymbol{q}\times\boldsymbol{k})\,,

(65)

and also that
\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}(\boldsymbol{q}\times\boldsymbol{k})B_{1,2}\mathcal{P}=0
.

Employing (
64
) in (
44
) we immediately deduce that the GTMDs associated with helicity distributions and orbital angular momentum are vanishing in the strict eikonal limit:
S^{0,-;q}_{1,1a}=0
and
S^{0,+;q}_{1,1b}=0

are parametrized as

for which we find

\begin{split}&xX^{S0,+;q}_{1,1a}=\frac{N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}A\mathcal{P}_{0}+\frac{N_{c}}{\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{2(\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta})^{2}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{2}}-1\right]\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}\left[\frac{2(\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{g})^{2}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}^{2}}-1\right]A\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}\,,\\
&xY^{S0,+;q}_{1,1a}=\frac{N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{g}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}}A\mathcal{O}_{0}\,.\end{split}

(67)

We have decomposed the Pomeron into its isotropic and elliptic parts and we have kept only the isotropic part of the Odderon.
The first term in the first line in (
67
) is an off-forward generalization of a known result from

;
90
. The second term in the first line takes into account the elliptic Pomeron. The second line is new, see also
64
for a very recent considerations covering also the case of non-zero skewness. The second line in (

is controlled by the spin independent Odderon. As a technical aside we note that the decomposition of GTMD such as (

\boldsymbol{\Delta}\to-\boldsymbol{\Delta}
(same holds for gluons - see the discussion below Eq. (

and the hard kernel

is purely real.

As for

, its real and imaginary parts are found as

\begin{split}&xX^{S0,-;q}_{1,1b}=2N_{c}M^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{g}}(\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{k})B_{2}\mathcal{P}\,,\\
&xY^{S0,-;q}_{1,1b}=0\,.\end{split}

(68)

Noticing now the relation

(69)

where at

&+\frac{\Delta_{L}}{M}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}A\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{g}^{2}}{M^{2}}\left[1-\frac{(\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{g})^{2}}{\boldsymbol{k}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{g}^{2}}\right]\mathcal{P}_{1T,0}^{\perp}\,.\end{split}

(72)

Computation steps leading to (

IV
Conclusions

We have performed a systematic computation of all gluon and sea-quark GTMDs at small-
x
and vanishing skewness
\xi
in terms of the (helicity-dependent) gluon dipole distribution
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
given by (
9
). We find that the small-
x
limit connects gluon GTMDs with different orbital angular momentum transfer or helicity transfer between gluons. This is akin to the connection between the unpolarized and the linearly polarized gluon TMD
82
. Out of the total of 16 gluon GTMDs, the four helicity-type GTMDs vanish in the small-
x
limit, and the remaining 12 are fully described in terms of the three Pomerons and Odderons - the real and the imaginary parts of
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
. In other words, within the remaining set of non-vanishing 12 gluon GTMDs only three are independent at small-
x
. As a corollary, we have clarified the role of Pomerons and Odderons in the gluon GPDs, leading to new results for transversity-type gluon GTMDs.

For the sea-quark GTMDs, we find that all helicity-type and transversity-type GTMDs vanish, so in the full set of 16 sea-quark GTMDs the non-zero entries concern only the total of 6 GTMDs; the unpolarized GTMD
19
, the spin-orbit GTMD
16
and the GTMDs for transversely polarized proton. The closed-form analytic expressions are determined by the gluon dipole
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
convoluted with a hard kernel that is real (and independent of parton helicity). For this reason, the real (imaginary) parts of all the sea-quark GTMDs are controlled by the real (imaginary) part of
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
- the Pomerons (Odderons). In particular, the imaginary part of the unpolarized sea-quark GTMD is determined by the spin-independent Odderon and the imaginary parts of the proton helicity-flip
P
-wave GTMDs are given in terms of the spin-dependent Odderon. Each GTMD is controlled by its own hard kernel and in the general kinematics we find no new connections between the sea-quark GTMD apart from the known unpolarized and the spin-orbit GTMD
16
connection. In the perturbative, high-
\boldsymbol{k}
, limit we were able to factorize all the sea-quark GTMDs in terms of the small-
x
gluon GPDs. The real parts of the GTMDs are all sourced by different gluon GPDs and therefore unrelated even at high-
\boldsymbol{k}

Appendix A
Parametrization and Inner Products in Two Dimensions

The general structure of the parametrization of
H_{\Lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda\lambda}^{g,q}
, which accounts for the change in the orbital angular momentum, is determined by terms of the form

\displaystyle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m}\,,

(91)

characterized by the “weight”
W=N-n+M-m
. Without loss of generality, in this analysis we take
W>0
. Since
k_{L}
and
\Delta_{L}
can be expressed in terms of the
k_{R}
and
\Delta_{R}
through the relations
k_{L}=\boldsymbol{k}^{2}/k_{R}
and
\Delta_{L}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{2}/\Delta_{R}
, we choose
k_{R}^{W}
and
\Delta_{R}^{W}
as independent structures
6
6

6

In the case
W<0
independent structures would instead be
k_{L}^{|W|}
and
\Delta_{L}^{|W|}
.
. to which (
91
) can be decomposed as

\displaystyle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m}

\displaystyle=ak_{R}^{W}+b\Delta_{R}^{W}\,.

(92)

To determine the coefficients
a
and
b
systematically, we define an inner product for two vectors of the same “weight”
p
as

\displaystyle\langle v^{p}_{R},w^{p}_{R}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(v^{p}_{R}w^{p}_{L}+v^{p}_{L}w^{p}_{R})\,.

(93)

This inner product is symmetric and linear in each argument

\displaystyle\langle v^{p}_{R},aw^{p}_{R}+bz_{R}^{p}\rangle=a\langle v^{p}_{R},w^{p}_{R}\rangle+b\langle v^{p}_{R},z_{R}^{p}\rangle\ \ \ (a,b\in\mathbb{R}).

(94)

Furthermore, it satisfies

\langle k_{R}^{p}\Delta_{R}^{q},k_{R}^{p+q}\rangle=(k_{R}k_{L})^{p}\langle\Delta_{R}^{q},k_{R}^{q}\rangle=\langle k_{R},k_{R}\rangle^{p}\langle\Delta_{R}^{q},k_{R}^{q}\rangle\,,

(95)

as well as analogous relations for other combinations, such as

\langle k_{R}^{p}\Delta_{L}^{q},v_{R}^{p-q}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(k_{R}^{p}\Delta_{L}^{q}v_{L}^{p-q}+k_{L}^{p}\Delta_{R}^{q}v_{R}^{p-q})=\langle k_{R}^{p},\Delta_{R}^{q}v_{R}^{p-q}\rangle\,.

(96)

Writing the vectors in (
93

\cos(p\phi_{k\Delta})=\frac{\langle\Delta_{R}^{p},k_{R}^{p}\rangle}{(\sqrt{\langle\Delta_{R},\Delta_{R}\rangle\langle k_{R},k_{R}\rangle})^{p}}=T_{p}\Bigg(\frac{\langle\Delta_{R},k_{R}\rangle}{(\sqrt{\langle\Delta_{R},\Delta_{R}\rangle\langle k_{R},k_{R}\rangle})}\Bigg)\,,

(97)

\langle k_{R}^{p},k_{R}^{p}\rangle=(k_{R}k_{L})^{p}=\langle k_{R},k_{R}\rangle^{p}

In the second line we have used the definition of the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind. This expression allows products of higher powers to be evaluated in a relatively simple way.

\displaystyle\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},k_{R}^{W}\rangle

\displaystyle=a\langle k_{R}^{W},k_{R}^{W}\rangle+b\langle k_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle\,,

(98)

\displaystyle\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle

\displaystyle=a\langle\Delta_{R}^{W},k_{R}^{W}\rangle+b\langle\Delta_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},k_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle\Delta_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle-\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle k_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle}{\langle k_{R}^{W},k_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle\Delta_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle-\langle k_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle^{2}}\,,

(100)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{-\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},k_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle k_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle+\langle k_{R}^{N}k_{L}^{n}\Delta_{R}^{M}\Delta_{L}^{m},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle k_{R}^{W},k_{R}^{W}\rangle}{\langle k_{R}^{W},k_{R}^{W}\rangle\langle\Delta_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle-\langle k_{R}^{W},\Delta_{R}^{W}\rangle^{2}}\,.
