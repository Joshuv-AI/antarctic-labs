Deformation maps on quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebras
Taoufik Chtioui, Sami Mabrouk, Abdenacer Makhlouf

Abstract
In this paper, we develop a unified approach for various operators on Lie conformal algebras. Given a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra ( ℰ , 𝔞 , 𝔟 ) (\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}) , we introduce two dual families of operators: right deformation maps D : 𝔞 → 𝔟 D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b} and left deformation maps B : 𝔟 → 𝔞 B:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{a} . Each family simultaneously subsumes several classical structures: modified r r -matrices, crossed homomorphisms, derivations, and Lie conformal algebra homomorphisms in the right case, relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators, and deformation maps of matched pairs in the left case. Using Voronov’s derived bracket method, we construct the controlling homotopy algebras: a curved L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra governing right deformation maps and an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra governing left deformation maps, with Maurer-Cartan elements precisely characterizing each type. We further develop the associated deformation theories via twisted L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras and define cohomology complexes for both types of deformation maps, recovering and extending the cohomologies of all classical and conformal operators already developed in the literature.



Keywords and phrases:
Lie conformal algebra, twilled Lie conformal algebra, representation, matched pair, left deformation map, right deformation map, Rota-Baxter operator, cohomology,
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Introduction

The theory of Lie conformal algebras, introduced by Kac
[
25
]
and
further developed by Bakalov-D’Andrea-Kac
[
3
]
and De Sole-Kac
[
14
,
15
]
,
provides the algebraic language underlying the operator product expansion in
two-dimensional conformal field theory. These algebras encode the singular
part of the OPE of chiral fields, and their cohomological and
representation-theoretic properties have been the subject of active
investigation; see
[
4
,
15
,
17
]
for foundational results.

In parallel, the study of operator-theoretic structures on Lie
algebras: Rota-Baxter operators, averaging operators, Reynolds
operators, modified
r
-matrices, derivations and their relatives has undergone
a renaissance, driven in large part by their connections to integrable
systems, the Yang-Baxter equation, dendriform algebras, and post-Lie
algebras. A central theme in this modern treatment, pioneered by
Tang-Bai-Guo-Sheng
[
38
,
39
]
and developed further
in
[
9
,
10
,
24
,
31
,
40
]
, is that each class of operators should
be governed by a
controlling homotopy algebra
(a (curved)

L_{\infty}
-algebra or differential graded Lie algebra) whose
Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely the operators in question, and
whose cohomology captures the infinitesimal deformation theory of those
operators. Extending this philosophy to the conformal setting is the
principal aim of this paper.

The notion of a
twilled
(or bicrossed product) Lie algebra, in
which a Lie algebra splits as
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
with both summands being
subalgebras, and its generalization to
quasi-twilled
Lie algebras
(where only
\mathfrak{b}
is required to be a subalgebra), provide a natural
unifying framework: by varying the quasi-twilled structure, one obtains
the semi-direct product, the
\phi
-deformed semi-direct product, and
many other standard constructions. In the conformal setting, the
structure
2
-cochain
\Omega
of a quasi-twilled conformal Lie algebra
admits a bihomogeneous decomposition

\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}

with respect to the Nijenhuis-Richardson bigrading, and the
Maurer-Cartan equation
[\Omega,\Omega]_{NR}=0
decomposes into a
hierarchical system of compatibility conditions. The
Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket, which underlies this framework, was
studied in the conformal context in
[
17
]
.

The concept of a deformation map for twilled Lie algebras was introduced by
Agore and Militaru
[
1
,
2
]
in their study of the
classifying complements problem, whose aim is to classify, up to
isomorphism, all complements of a given Lie subalgebra.
One of the remarkable features of deformation maps is that they provide a
unified framework encompassing several important classes of operators on Lie
algebras, including Lie algebra homomorphisms, derivations, crossed
homomorphisms
[
35
]
, and relative Rota-Baxter operators (or

\mathcal{O}
-operators) of weights
0
and
1

[
28
,
42
]
.
The theory of Rota-Baxter operators, originating from the work of
Baxter
[
5
]
and subsequently developed by Rota
[
37
]
, has
received renewed attention due to its deep connections with
Connes-Kreimer renormalization
[
7
,
8
]
, the classical Yang-Baxter
equation, and dendriform algebras
[
20
]
. Nevertheless, several operators
closely related to Rota-Baxter operators, such as twisted Rota-Baxter
operators
[
11
]
, Reynolds operators
[
36
]
, and modified
Rota–Baxter operators
[
42
]
, cannot be realized as deformation maps in
the setting of twilled Lie algebras.
To overcome this limitation, Jiang, Sheng, and Tang
[
23
]
introduced the
notion of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra together with two types of
deformation maps, namely type-I and type-II deformation maps.
They also developed a corresponding cohomology theory that unifies the
cohomological frameworks associated with all the operators mentioned above.
Subsequently, deformation maps for quasi-twilled associative algebras were
investigated in
[
32
]
. The notions of deformation maps in
proto-twilled Leibniz algebras and proto-twilled Poisson algebras were studied
in
[
12
,
13
]
. More recently, deformation maps of quasi-twilled

3
-Lie algebras have been explored in
[
21
]
.

The aim of this paper is to introduce and study two families of
operators on quasi-twilled conformal Lie algebras.
A
right deformation map
on quasi-twilled conformal Lie algebra is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism

D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
whose graph
\mathrm{Gr}(D)\subset\mathcal{E}
is a conformal Lie
subalgebra, or equivalently, whose defining
equation (
3.1
) holds. Special cases include conformal
modified
r
-matrices (Example
3.6
), conformal crossed
homomorphisms (Example
3.7
), conformal derivations
(Example
3.8
), and conformal Lie algebra
homomorphisms (Example
3.9
). A
left deformation
map
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
B:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{a}

satisfying (
4.1
), and its special cases include conformal
relative Rota-Baxter operators (Example
4.4
), conformal
twisted Rota-Baxter operators (Example
4.5
), conformal
Reynolds operators (Example
4.6
), and deformation
maps of matched pairs (Example
4.7
).
For each family, we establish the following results.

1.

Induced structures and Cohomology.

Each deformation map
D
(resp.
B
) induces a Lie conformal
algebra structure on
\mathfrak{a}
(resp.
\mathfrak{b}
) and a module structure
on the opposite summand, yielding a Chevalley-Eilenberg-type
cochain complex whose cohomology is called the

cohomology of the deformation map

(Definitions
3.13
and
4.10
).
We show that the twisted differential
l_{1}^{D}
(resp.
l_{1}^{B}
)
coincides, up to sign, with this Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary.
Moreover, we show that the second cohomology group classifies infinitesimal
deformations.

2.

Controlling homotopy algebras.

Using Voronov’s curved
V
-data construction
[
43
]
, we show
that right deformation maps are Maurer-Cartan elements of a curved

L_{\infty}
-algebra on

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

(Theorem
3.20
), and left deformation maps are
Maurer-Cartan elements of an (uncurved)

-algebra on

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})

(Theorem
4.17
). In each case, the structure maps
are given explicitly by iterated Nijenhuis-Richardson brackets
with the components of
\Omega
.

3.

Deformation theory.

Given a deformation map
D
(resp.
B
), the twisted

L_{\infty}
-algebra governs perturbations
D+D^{\prime}
(resp.
B+B^{\prime}
),
and the classical Maurer-Cartan formalism yields the precise
obstruction-theoretic description of such deformations
(Theorems
3.25
and
4.23
).

As corollaries of the general theory, we recover and extend the
controlling algebras and deformation cohomologies for conformal

\mathcal{O}
-operators
[
44
]
,
conformal modified
r
-matrices
[
22
]
, conformal
derivations
[
40
]
, and deformation maps of matched pairs, several
of which appear to be new even in the conformal setting.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a review of Lie conformal algebras, their representations
and cohomology, the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and its bigrading, and

-algebra of Theorem

In Section

-algebra, develop the deformation

The last Section deals with a parallel procedure for left

2.1
Lie conformal algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A conformal algebra is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{E}
endowed with a

\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\mu:\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{E}[\lambda],\qquad a\otimes b\;\mapsto\;a_{\lambda}b,

satisfying
conformal sesquilinearity
:

(\partial a)_{\lambda}b=-\lambda\,a_{\lambda}b,\qquad a_{\lambda}(\partial b)=(\partial+\lambda)\,a_{\lambda}b,\qquad\forall\;a,b\in\mathcal{E}.

(2.1)

From (
2.1
) one derives, by variable substitution, the
secondary identities (
a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}
):

\displaystyle(a_{-\lambda-\partial}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle=(a_{\mu}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c,

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(b_{-\lambda-\partial}c)

\displaystyle=a_{\mu}(b_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}c),

(2.2)

\displaystyle(a_{-\mu-\partial}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}c

\displaystyle=(a_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}c,

\displaystyle a_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(b_{-\lambda-\partial}c)

\displaystyle=a_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(b_{\mu}c).

(2.3)

These identities will be used repeatedly in later computations.

Definition 2.2
.

A Lie conformal algebra is a conformal algebra
\mathcal{E}
with product

\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}[\lambda]
,
a\otimes b\mapsto\{a_{\lambda}b\}
,
called the
\lambda
-bracket
, subject to the following identities for
all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}
:

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}

\displaystyle=-\{b_{-\lambda-\partial}a\},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;\;\;\text{ (Skew-symmetry)}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}\{b_{\mu}c\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\lambda+\mu}c\}+\{b_{\mu}\{a_{\lambda}c\}\},\text{ (Jacobi identity)}.

(2.5)

Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules. A
conformal linear
map
from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

f_{\lambda}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[\lambda]
satisfying

f_{\lambda}(\partial u)=(\partial+\lambda)f_{\lambda}u,\qquad\forall\;u\in\mathcal{M}.

The
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of all such maps is denoted
\mathrm{Chom}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime})

and is itself a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module via
(\partial f)_{\lambda}:=-\lambda f_{\lambda}
.
The
conformal dual
of
\mathcal{M}
is the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module

\mathcal{M}^{*c}:=\mathrm{Chom}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}),

where
\mathbb{C}
is considered as the trivial
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module (
\partial\cdot 1=0
).
Concretely,

\mathcal{M}^{*c}=\{\xi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}[\lambda]\mid\xi_{\lambda}(\partial u)=\lambda\,\xi_{\lambda}u,\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{M}\}.

For a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{M}
, the space

\mathrm{Cend}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathrm{Chom}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})

is an associative conformal algebra under the composition product

(f_{\lambda}g)_{\mu}v:=f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}v)
.
The associated conformal Lie algebra, denoted
\mathrm{gc}(\mathcal{M})

and called the
general conformal Lie algebra
on
\mathcal{M}
, has
brackets

[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}v:=f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}v)-g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}v),\qquad f,g\in\mathrm{Cend}(\mathcal{M}),\;v\in\mathcal{M}.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{M}
is a module over a Lie conformal algebra

(\mathcal{E},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
if there exists a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\varrho:\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Cend}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\varrho(a)_{\lambda}\varrho(b)_{\mu}-\varrho(b)_{\mu}\varrho(a)_{\lambda}=\varrho(\{a_{\lambda}b\})_{\lambda+\mu},\qquad\varrho(\partial a)_{\lambda}=-\lambda\varrho(a)_{\lambda},\quad\forall\;a,b\in\mathcal{E}.

(2.6)

Equivalently,
\varrho
is a homomorphism of conformal Lie algebras

\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{gc}(\mathcal{M})
. We write
(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
for this module.

One verifies from Definition
2.3
the supplementary
identities

\displaystyle\varrho(\{a_{\lambda}b\})_{-\partial-\mu}

\displaystyle=\varrho(a)_{\lambda}\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\mu}-\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu}\varrho(a)_{\lambda},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\varrho(\{a_{\mu}b\})_{-\partial-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\varrho(a)_{\mu}\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\lambda}-\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\lambda-\mu}\varrho(a)_{-\partial-\lambda},

(2.8)

which appear in the explicit computation of coboundary operators.

The
dual module
structure on
\mathcal{M}^{*c}
is given by

\varrho^{*}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{gc}(\mathcal{M}^{*c})
,

(\varrho^{*}(a)_{\lambda}\xi)_{\mu}v:=-\xi_{\mu-\lambda}(\varrho(a)_{\lambda}v),\qquad a\in\mathcal{E},\;\xi\in\mathcal{M}^{*c},\;v\in\mathcal{M}.

Setting
\mathrm{ad}(a)_{\lambda}b:=\{a_{\lambda}b\}
for
a,b\in\mathcal{E}
yields the

adjoint module

(\mathcal{E};\mathrm{ad})
, and hence the

coadjoint module

(\mathcal{E}^{*c};\mathrm{ad}^{*})
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a conformal Lie algebra and
(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
be a module. The

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{M}
carries a conformal Lie algebra structure

[(a,m)_{\lambda}(b,n)]:=\bigl(\{a_{\lambda}b\},\;\varrho(a)_{\lambda}n-\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\lambda}m\bigr),\quad a,b\in\mathcal{E},\;m,n\in\mathcal{M},

(2.9)

called the semi-direct product and denoted
\mathcal{E}\ltimes_{\varrho}\mathcal{M}
.

More generally, given a
2
-cocycle
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
, the formula

[(a,m)_{\lambda}(b,n)]^{\phi}:=\bigl(\{a_{\lambda}b\},\;\varrho(a)_{\lambda}n-\varrho(b)_{-\partial-\lambda}m+\phi_{\lambda}(a,b)\bigr)

(2.10)

defines a Lie conformal algebra structure on
\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{M}
, called the

\phi
-deformed semi-direct product
and denoted

\mathcal{E}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathcal{M}
.

Let us recall the cohomology complex for a Lie conformal algebra with coefficients in a given
module introduced in
[
16
]
. Fix a Lie conformal algebra
(\mathcal{E},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
and a module

(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
. Set
\mathscr{C}^{0}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{M}/\partial\mathcal{M}
. For

k\geq 1
, let
\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
be the space of
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps

\varphi:\mathcal{E}^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1}]\otimes\mathcal{M}

satisfying the following two conditions, where

\lambda_{k}^{\ddagger}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}-\partial^{\mathcal{M}}
:

(C1) Sesquilinearity.

For
1\leq i\leq k-1
,

\varphi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\ldots,\partial a_{i},\ldots)=-\lambda_{i}\,\varphi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\ldots,a_{i},\ldots),\text{ and }\varphi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\ldots,\partial a_{k})=-\lambda_{k}^{\ddagger}\,\varphi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\ldots,a_{k}).

(C2) Graded antisymmetry.

For every permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
,

\varphi_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=(-1)^{\sigma}\,\varphi_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(k-1)}}(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(k)})\big|_{\lambda_{k}\mapsto\lambda_{k}^{\ddagger}}.

(2.11)

The
coboundary operator

\mathbf{d}:\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})\to\mathscr{C}^{k+1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})

is defined for
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
,
k\geq 1
, by

\begin{split}(\mathbf{d}\varphi)_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}}&(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k+1})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\varrho(a_{i})_{\lambda_{i}}\varphi_{\widehat{\lambda_{i}}}(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{k+1})\big|_{\lambda_{k+1}\mapsto\lambda_{k+1}^{\ddagger}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\varphi_{\widehat{\lambda_{i}}}(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{k},\{a_{i\,\lambda_{i}}a_{k+1}\})\big|_{\lambda_{k+1}\mapsto\lambda_{k+1}^{\ddagger}}\\
&\quad+\!\!\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}\!\!(-1)^{k+i+j+1}\varphi_{\widehat{\lambda_{i}},\widehat{\lambda_{j}},\lambda_{k+1}^{\ddagger}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\{a_{i\,\lambda_{i}}a_{j}\})\big|_{\lambda_{k+1}\mapsto\lambda_{k+1}^{\ddagger}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{k}\varrho(a_{k+1})_{\lambda_{k+1}^{\ddagger}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}),\end{split}

(2.12)

where hats denote the omission of the corresponding index or argument.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
16
]
)
.

For every
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
, one has
\mathbf{d}\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{k+1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})

and
\mathbf{d}^{2}\varphi=0
. Hence
(\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}),\mathbf{d})
is a cochain complex,
whose cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
is the
conformal Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathcal{E}
with values in
(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
.

2.2
The Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and bigrading

We now enrich the cochain complex
\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
with an additional
algebraic structure, a graded Lie bracket that underlies all the
operator-theoretic constructions in this paper.

A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is an
(i,n-i)
-unshuffle

if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
; the
set of all such permutations is denoted
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
.

Set
\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
. For

f\in\mathscr{C}^{m}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
and
g\in\mathscr{C}^{n}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
, define the

Nijenhuis-Richardson
(NR) bracket

[f,g]_{NR}:=f\star g-(-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)}\,g\star f,

(2.13)

where the
pre-bracket

(f\star g)\in\mathscr{C}^{m+n-1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
is

\displaystyle(f\star g)_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m+n-2}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n,m-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}f_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{\sigma(n)},\,\lambda_{\sigma(n+1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(m+n-2)}}

\displaystyle\bigl(g_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(n-1)}}(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(n)}),a_{\sigma(n+1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(m+n-1)}\bigr)\big|_{\lambda_{m+n-1}\mapsto\lambda_{m+n-1}^{\ddagger}},

with
\lambda_{m+n-1}^{\ddagger}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{m+n-2}\lambda_{i}-\partial
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
17
]
)
.

The pair
(\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}),[-,-]_{NR})
defines a graded Lie algebra. A
2
-cochain

\omega\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
defines a Lie conformal algebra structure
on
\mathcal{E}
via
\{a_{\lambda}b\}:=\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)
if and only if

[\omega,\omega]_{NR}=0
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules (not yet assumed to carry any
algebraic structure). Elements of
\mathfrak{a}
will be written
a,b,a_{i},\ldots

and elements of
\mathfrak{b}
as
u,v,u_{i},\ldots
.

Definition 2.7
.

A cochain
f\in\mathscr{C}^{k+l+1}(\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b})
has
bidegree
k\mid l
(written
\|f\|=k\mid l
) if the following
three conditions hold:

(i)

If
X\in\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes k+1}\otimes\mathfrak{b}^{\otimes l}
,
then
f(X)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k+l}]\otimes\mathfrak{a}
.

(ii)

If
X\in\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathfrak{b}^{\otimes l+1}
,
then
f(X)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k+l}]\otimes\mathfrak{b}
.

(iii)

f(X)=0
in all remaining input-type combinations.

We call
f
bihomogeneous when it has a bidegree.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
\|f\|=k_{f}\mid l_{f}
and
\|g\|=k_{g}\mid l_{g}
, then

[f,g]_{NR}
has bidegree
k_{f}+k_{g}\mid l_{f}+l_{g}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\|f\|=-1\mid l
(resp.
l\mid-1
) and
\|g\|=-1\mid k

(resp.
k\mid-1
), then
[f,g]_{NR}=0
.

Given
\alpha:\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}[\lambda]
and

\beta:\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{b}[\lambda]
, their
lifts
to

\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b})
are defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda}\bigl((a_{1},u_{1}),(a_{2},u_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle:=\bigl(\alpha_{\lambda}(a_{1},a_{2}),\,0\bigr),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}\bigl((a_{1},u_{1}),(a_{2},u_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle:=\bigl(0,\,\beta_{\lambda}(a_{1},u_{2})-\beta_{-\partial-\lambda}(a_{2},u_{1})\bigr),

(2.15)

and both have bidegree
1\mid 0
. Setting
\widehat{\nu}:=\widehat{\alpha}+\widehat{\beta}
,
we obtain the semi-direct product type operation

\widehat{\nu}_{\lambda}\bigl((a_{1},u_{1}),(a_{2},u_{2})\bigr)=\bigl(\alpha_{\lambda}(a_{1},a_{2}),\;\beta_{\lambda}(a_{1},u_{2})-\beta_{-\lambda-\partial}(a_{2},u_{1})\bigr).

(2.16)

The following result recasts the module axiom in terms of the
NR bracket
and supplies the cohomological interpretation of
\mathbf{d}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\omega)
be a conformal Lie algebra and

\varrho:\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Cend}(\mathcal{M})
a map with

\varrho(\partial a)_{\lambda}=-\lambda\varrho(a)_{\lambda}
. Then

(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
is a module if and only if

[\widehat{\omega}+\widehat{\varrho},\;\widehat{\omega}+\widehat{\varrho}]_{NR}=0
.
Moreover, the coboundary operator (

) satisfies

\mathbf{d}_{\omega+\varrho}\,\varphi=(-1)^{k-1}[\widehat{\omega}+\widehat{\varrho},\;\widehat{\varphi}]_{NR},\quad\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}).

(2.17)

2.3
Twilled and quasi-twilled conformal Lie algebras

Let
(\mathcal{E},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
be a Lie conformal algebra with a
fixed direct-sum decomposition
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules.
Denote by
\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{a}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{b}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathfrak{b}

the natural projections, and define six structural maps by

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\{a_{\lambda}b\}),

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}a

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\{u_{\lambda}a\}),

\displaystyle\psi_{2,\lambda}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\{u_{\lambda}v\}),

\displaystyle\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\{u_{\lambda}v\}),

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}v

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\{a_{\lambda}v\}),

\displaystyle\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\{a_{\lambda}b\}),

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
. The full
\lambda
-bracket on
\mathcal{E}
reads

[(a,u)_{\lambda}(b,v)]=\bigl(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\psi_{2,\lambda}(u,v),\;\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}v-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}u+\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b)\bigr).

(2.18)

Denoting the Lie conformal algebra structure on
\mathcal{E}
by
\Omega\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
,
Lemma
2.8
yields the unique bihomogeneous decomposition

\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}+\widehat{\psi}_{2},

(2.19)

where
\|\widehat{\psi}\|=2\mid{-1}
,
\|\widehat{\nu}_{1}\|=1\mid 0
,
\|\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|=0\mid 1
,

\|\widehat{\psi}_{2}\|=-1\mid 2
, with explicit formulas

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}((a,u),(b,v))

\displaystyle=(0,\,\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b)),

(2.20)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{1,\lambda}((a,u),(b,v))

\displaystyle=(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}},\;\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}v-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}u),

(2.21)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{2,\lambda}((a,u),(b,v))

\displaystyle=(\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)_{-\lambda-\partial}a,\;\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}),

(2.22)

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{2,\lambda}((a,u),(b,v))

\displaystyle=(\psi_{2,\lambda}(u,v),\,0).

(2.23)

The Maurer-Cartan equation
[\Omega,\Omega]_{NR}=0
decomposes by bidegree into

\begin{cases}[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\psi}]_{NR}=0,\\[3.0pt]
\tfrac{1}{2}[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\nu}_{1}]_{NR}+[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\widehat{\psi}]_{NR}=0,\\[3.0pt]
[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\nu}_{2}]_{NR}+[\widehat{\psi},\widehat{\psi}_{2}]_{NR}=0,\\[3.0pt]
\tfrac{1}{2}[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\widehat{\nu}_{2}]_{NR}+[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\psi}_{2}]_{NR}=0,\\[3.0pt]
[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\widehat{\psi}_{2}]_{NR}=0.\end{cases}

(2.24)

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
be a Lie conformal algebra with structure
(
2.19
).

(i)

The triple
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is called a
quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra if
\psi_{2}=0
,
i.e.
\mathfrak{b}
is a sub-algebra of
\mathcal{E}
. The structure then reduces to

\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}
.

(ii)

The triple
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is called an
twilled Lie conformal algebra (or bicrossed
product) if
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}=0
, i.e. both
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are
sub-algebras of
\mathcal{E}
. We then write
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\bowtie\mathfrak{b}
.

When

)
reduces to the four conditions

[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\psi}]_{NR}=0,\quad\tfrac{1}{2}[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\nu}_{1}]_{NR}+[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\widehat{\psi}]_{NR}=0,\quad[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{\nu}_{2}]_{NR}=0,\quad\tfrac{1}{2}[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\widehat{\nu}_{2}]_{NR}=0.

(2.25)

In particular,
(\mathfrak{b},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{b}})

and

2.4
L_{\infty}
-algebras and Maurer–Cartan elements

Definition 2.12

(
[
30
,
29
]
)
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a graded vector space

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{g}^{i}
equipped with
multilinear maps
\ell_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
of
degree
2-k
(
k\geq 1
) satisfying, for all homogeneous

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
:

(i)

Graded antisymmetry:

\ell_{k}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(k)})=\chi(\sigma)\,\ell_{k}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})

for every
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
, where
\chi(\sigma)
is the Koszul sign.

(ii)

Higher Jacobi identity:
for each
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{i+j=n+1}(-1)^{i}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}}\!\!\chi(\sigma)\;\ell_{j}\!\bigl(\ell_{i}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(i)}),v_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}\bigr)=0.

An element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
is a Maurer-Cartan element if

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\,\ell_{k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)=0.

The following theorem together with
[
41
, Corollary 3.5]

supplies the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs all subsequent deformation
problems.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\Omega)
be a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra
with
\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}
. Define maps on
\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})
by

\displaystyle\delta(f)

\displaystyle:=[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\,\widehat{f}]_{NR},

(2.26)

\displaystyle\{f_{1},f_{2}\}

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{m-1}[[\widehat{\nu}_{1},\widehat{f}_{1}]_{NR},\,\widehat{f}_{2}]_{NR},

(2.27)

\displaystyle\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{n-1}[[[\widehat{\psi},\widehat{f}_{1}]_{NR},\,\widehat{f}_{2}]_{NR},\,\widehat{f}_{3}]_{NR},

(2.28)

for all
f\in\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})
,
f_{1}\in\mathscr{C}^{m}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})
,

f_{2}\in\mathscr{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})
,
f_{3}\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})
. Then

(\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a}),\,\delta,\,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

is an

-algebra.

Corollary 2.14
.

For a twilled conformal Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}\bowtie\mathfrak{b}

in which
\widehat{\psi}=0
and (

(\mathscr{C}^{*}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a}),\,\delta,\,\{\cdot,\cdot\})

graded Lie algebra.

3
Controlling algebras and cohomologies of right deformation maps

In this section, we introduce the notion of right deformation map, which unify
modified
r
-matrices, crossed homomorphisms,
derivations, and Lie conformal algebra homomorphisms. The triple

(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is always a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra with structure
\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}
, where the six components are

\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}},\quad\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}v,\quad\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}},\quad\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}a,\quad\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b),

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
(and
\psi_{2}=0
).

3.1
Right deformation maps

Let
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism. Its
graph
is the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-submodule

\mathrm{Gr}(D):=\{(a,D(a))\mid a\in\mathfrak{a}\}\subset\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}=\mathcal{E}.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
be a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra.
A
right deformation map
of
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{b}

(i.e. a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map satisfying
D(\partial a)=\partial D(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
)
such that
\mathrm{Gr}(D)
is a Lie conformal sub-algebra of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
is a right deformation map if and only if, for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, we have

\displaystyle D\!\bigl(\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(a))_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(b))_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a)+\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b).

(3.1)

Proof.

For
(a,D(a)),(b,D(b))\in\mathrm{Gr}(D)
, the
\lambda
-bracket of
\mathcal{E}
gives

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda}\!\bigl((a,D(a)),(b,D(b))\bigr)=\Bigl(

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(a))_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(b))_{-\lambda-\partial}a,

\displaystyle\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a)+\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b)\Bigr).

Then
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
is a right deformation map if and only if the
\mathfrak{b}
-component equals

D
applied to the
\mathfrak{a}
-component, which is precisely Eq. (
3.1
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We require
D
to be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-
module
homomorphism, that is, a

\mathbb{C}
-linear map satisfying
D(\partial a)=\partial D(a)
. This condition is
strictly weaker than conformal linearity, which would demand a

\lambda
-parametric family
D_{\lambda}
with
D_{\lambda}(\partial a)=(\partial+\lambda)D_{\lambda}a
.
The module-homomorphism condition is the natural one for deformation
maps: it ensures that
D
is compatible with the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module structure
of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
without introducing a spectral-parameter shift, and
it is precisely the condition needed for the graph
\mathrm{Gr}(D)
to
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-submodule of
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Consider the
\phi
-deformed semi-direct product

\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathcal{M}
(with
\mathfrak{b}=\mathcal{M}
,
\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
,

\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
,
\psi_{1}=\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathfrak{a},\mathcal{M})
a
2
-cocycle).
A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathcal{M}
is a right deformation map if and only if

\phi_{\lambda}(a,b)=-\bigl(\varrho(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a)-D(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}})\bigr)=\delta(-D)_{\lambda}(a,b)

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
, where
\delta
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary of
(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{a}})

with coefficients in
(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
.
Thus
\mathcal{E}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathcal{M}
admits a right deformation map if and only if
\phi
is an
exact
2
-cocycle.

Remark 3.5
.

Proposition
3.2
shows that finding a complement of

\mathfrak{b}
in
\mathcal{E}
that forms a matched pair with
\mathfrak{b}
is equivalent to
finding a right deformation map of
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
.

Example 3.6

(Modified
r
-matrix)
.

Modified
r
-matrices play an important role in the study of
conformal analogues of Lax equations and factorization problems in Lie conformal algebras, extending the classical picture of
Semenov-Tian-Shansky
[
33
,
34
]
.
Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
with
\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}
,

\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}=\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathrm{ad}
, and

\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b)=-\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}
.
A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
is a right deformation map if and only if

\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{a}}-D\!\bigl(\{D(a)_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\{a_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{a}}\bigr)=-\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}},\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a}.

(3.2)

This is the modified Yang-Baxter equation on

(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{a}})
; its solutions are called
modified
r
-matrices.

Example 3.7

(Crossed homomorphism)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\ltimes_{\varrho}\mathfrak{b}
be the
\lambda
-weighted semi-direct product and

\psi_{1}=0
.
A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
is a right deformation map if and only if

D(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\varrho(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a)+\lambda\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{b}},\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},

(3.3)

i.e.
D
is a crossed homomorphism of weight
\lambda

from
(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{a}})
to
(\mathfrak{b},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{b}})
.

Example 3.8

(Derivation)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\ltimes_{\varrho}\mathcal{M}
with
\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
,
\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
,
\psi_{1}=0
.
A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathcal{M}
is a right deformation map if and only if

D(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\varrho(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a),\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},

(3.4)

i.e.
D
is a derivation from

(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{a}})
to
(\mathcal{M};\varrho)
. When
\varrho=\mathrm{ad}
,
one recovers the usual notion of a derivation on a Lie conformal algebra.

Example 3.9

(Lie conformal algebra homomorphism)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
be the direct product with

\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}=\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
,
\psi_{1}=0
.
A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
is a right deformation map if and only if

D(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{b}},\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathfrak{a},

(3.5)

i.e.
D
is a Lie algebra conformal homomorphism from

(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{a}})
to
(\mathfrak{b},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{b}})
.

3.2
Cohomology of right deformation maps

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
be a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra and

D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
be a right deformation map. Define the map
\bar{D}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},\ (a,u)\mapsto(0,D(a))
. It is clear that
\bar{D}^{2}=0
.
Consider the map
I+\bar{D}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},\ (a,u)\mapsto(a,u+D(a))
. It is an invertible
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-homomorphism with inverse
I-\bar{D}
. Then it induces a new Lie conformal algebraic structure on
\mathcal{E}
, given by

\displaystyle\Omega^{D}_{\lambda}((a,u),(b,v))=(I-\bar{D})\Omega_{\lambda}((I+\bar{D})(a,u),(I+\bar{D}(b,v)).

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
D:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{b}
be a right deformation map on a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}^{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}=

defines a Lie conformal algebra structure on
\mathfrak{a}
. We denote it by
\mathfrak{a}^{D}
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathfrak{a}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{D}_{\lambda}((a,0),(b,0))=

\displaystyle(I-\bar{D})\Omega_{\lambda}((I+\bar{D})(a,0),(I+\bar{D}(b,0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\bar{D})\Omega_{\lambda}((a,D(a)),(b,D(b)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\bar{D})(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(a))_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(b))_{-\lambda-\partial}a,

\displaystyle\{D(a)_{\lambda}D(b)\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}D(b)-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}D(a)+\psi_{1,\lambda}(a,b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(a))_{\lambda}b-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(D(b))_{-\lambda-\partial}a,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\{a_{\lambda}b\}^{D}_{\mathfrak{a}},0).

Since
\Omega_{\lambda}
is a Lie conformal bracket then
\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}
so is. Hence
(\mathfrak{a},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie conformal algebra.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

The previous Theorem shows that

\mathfrak{a}
is a subalgebra.

\displaystyle\{D(a)_{\lambda}u\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)_{\lambda}u+D(\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{-\lambda-\partial}a).

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Controlling algebras and cohomologies of left deformation maps

In this section, we introduce the notion of left deformation map on a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra, unifying relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators, and deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie conformal algebras. We also describe the controlling algebra of left deformation maps on a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra, showing that it carries the structure of a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Throughout this section,
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
denotes a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra with structure
\Omega=\widehat{\psi}+\widehat{\nu}_{1}+\widehat{\nu}_{2}

4.1
Left deformation maps

Let
B:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{a}
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism. Its

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-submodule

\mathrm{Gr}(B):=\{(B(u),u)\mid u\in\mathfrak{b}\}\subset\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}=\mathcal{E}.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
be a quasi-twilled Lie conformal algebra.
A

of

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism

Proposition 4.2
.

A
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism

is a left deformation map if and only if, for all

\displaystyle\{B(u)_{\lambda}B(v)\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}B(v)-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)_{-\lambda-\partial}B(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(B(u))_{\lambda}v-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(B(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}u+\psi_{1,\lambda}(B(u),B(v))),

(4.1)

Proof.

For any
(B(u),u),(B(v),v)\in\mathrm{Gr}(B)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda}((B(u),u),(B(v),v))=

\displaystyle(\{B(u)_{\lambda}B(v)\}_{\mathfrak{a}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)_{\lambda}B(v)-\varrho_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)_{-\lambda-\partial}B(u),

\displaystyle\{u_{\lambda}v\}_{\mathfrak{b}}+\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(B(u))_{\lambda}v-\varrho_{\mathfrak{a}}(B(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}u+\psi_{1,\lambda}(B(u),B(v))).

Then

is a left deformation map if and only if the
\mathfrak{a}
-component equals

B

\mathfrak{b}
-component, which is precisely Eq. (
4.1
).
∎

\displaystyle=[\widehat{\nu}_{2},\,\widehat{f}\,]_{NR},

\displaystyle l_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})
