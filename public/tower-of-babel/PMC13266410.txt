Mutational dynamics in patients with del(5q) MDS treated with lenalidomide prior to transfusion dependency—Molecular results from the Sintrarev clinical trial
Authors: Sofía Toribio Castelló, Felix López‐Cadenas, Claude Preudhomme, Teresa González, Angela Villaverde Ramiro, Laurène Fenwarth, Blanca Xicoy, Aline Renneville, Joaquín Sánchez‐Garcia, Rosa Coll, Bohrane Slama, José Ángel Hernández‐Rivas, Sylvain Thepot, Teresa Bernal, Agnès Guerci‐Bresler, Raúl Azibeiro, Monica Del Rey, Katharina S Götze, Pierre Fenaux, Jesús María Hernández‐Rivas, María Díez‐Campelo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLenalidomide (Len) is the standard of care for red blood cell transfusion‐dependent (TD) patients with myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) and del(5q). A Phase III clinical trial (SintraRev) demonstrated better efficacy of early treatment with Len in anemic del(5q) MDS patients prior to TD, but clonal evolution with this approach remained unexplored. We evaluated changes in mutational profiles of non‐TD del(5q) MDS patients treated with Len to assess whether early administration of Len could reduce or worsen the mutational burden in those patients. The molecular profile of patients included in the SintraRev trial was analyzed by targeted sequencing. Median follow up of patients was 60.6 months (interquartile range [IQR] 32.3–73.9). Next‐generation sequencing (NGS) was available in a total of 51 patients. SF3B1 (25.5%) and TP53 (21.3%) were the most frequently mutated genes. Clonal evolution during treatment among patients receiving Len or placebo (Pcb) was markedly different, with a 41.2% reduction in predominating clones in the Len arm, while remaining stable/increase in 100% of Pcb patients after 2 years. Mutations in SF3B1 and TP53 decreased/remained stable in the Len cohort, while only DNMT3A mutations increased under cytogenetic response. Regarding disease progression, only 20% of Len patients carrying TP53 mutations developed acute myeloid leukemia (AML), similar to the Pcb group (20%). Treatment with low‐dose Len in transfusion‐independent del(5q) MDS reduced the mutational burden of most genes and did not promote the expansion of preexisting clones or AML progression, especially TP53‐mutated clones. Early administration of Len in del(5q) MDS patients without TD may be an effective therapeutic approach with a manageable safety profile regarding clonal evolution.

INTRODUCTION

Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) are clonal bone marrow disorders characterized by ineffective hematopoiesis leading to blood cytopenia and variable risk of progression to acute myeloid leukemia (AML) through genetic instability. 1 Cytogenetic alterations are found in approximately 50% of patients with MDS. 2 Deletion of the long arm of chromosome 5 (del[5q]), when isolated and in the absence of excess marrow blasts, defines a distinct clinical and pathological MDS entity in the 2022 World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Consensus Classification (ICC), 3 , 4 and is associated with about 20% risk of AML progression after 4 years. 5

Somatic point mutations, found in 80%–90% of MDS, generally have a poor prognostic impact. 6 TP53 mutation is particularly associated with poorer outcome, especially in MDS with isolated del(5q). 7 These mutations occur in 20%–25% of the cases, often during evolution, and are a main driver of progression to AML through genetic instability, leading to a complex karyotype. 8 , 9 SF3B1, generally associated with a favorable prognosis in MDS, is paradoxically related to higher risk of AML progression and shorter overall survival (OS) in del(5q) MDS. 10

Patients with del(5q) MDS generally become red blood cell (RBC)‐transfusion‐dependent (TD) shortly after diagnosis. 11 Erythropoietic stimulating agents (ESA) are less effective in del(5q) MDS than in other low risk MDS, while lenalidomide (Len), that selectively targets the 5q deletion clone, is the standard of care in del(5q) MDS when they become RBC‐TD, leading to RBC transfusion independence (TI) and complete cytogenetic response (CyR) in about 50% of patients. 12 , 13 However, it is unclear if Len has a positive effect on disease progression and OS. 14 , 15 In addition, the effect of Len on the mutational profile in MDS with isolated del(5q) is not known. In particular, response to Len is lower and shorter in patients with TP53 mutations. 16 , 17 In those patients, it is hypothesized that Len, by positively selecting the TP53‐mutated subclones, could potentially accelerate disease progression, although it is not demonstrated.

Our cooperative group completed SintraRev, a Phase III clinical trial comparing low‐dose Len treatment and placebo (Pcb) in anemic, non‐TD patients with del(5q) MDS, which showed a high rate of RBC‐TI, CyR, and longer time to RBC‐TD in treated patients, potentially suggesting a role for early Len treatment in delaying disease progression. 18

We took advantage of this study to evaluate the molecular changes of the early administration of Len and assess if this early therapeutic approach could drive molecular responses or might, on the contrary, be associated with the expansion and acquisition of somatic mutations.

METHODOLOGY

Patients included in the SintraRev trial and study design

SintraRev is a Phase III multicenter, randomized, double‐blind, Pcb‐controlled trial. MDS‐del(5q) patients with low/intermediate‐1 risk were enrolled between 2010 and 2018 in 22 different locations from three European countries (Spain, France, and Germany). Full eligibility/exclusion criteria and study design are detailed in Díez‐Campelo et al. 18 Briefly, patients were randomly assigned 2:1 to receive Len (5 mg/day in a 28‐day cycle) or Pcb for 104 weeks (treatment phase of 2 years), until disease RBC‐TD or unacceptable toxicity (last day of Len, end of treatment day, EOT). Responses were assessed following the criteria of the International Working Group. 19 Patients were monitored for two additional years of follow‐up (FU) after the EOT period (end of study, EOE). Data on RBC‐TD occurrence, AML transformation, and death were collected. Additionally, an extended long‐term follow‐up (LTFU) after the end of the study was reported to update information regarding TD and the patient's outcome (LTFU). 18 This extended FU (LTFU) finished in March 2022 and the median follow‐up time (excluding the treatment period) was 39 months (interquartile range [IQR] 24–55).

Sample procedure and genetic analyses

Bone marrow (BM) samples were obtained from patients at inclusion prior to treatment, at Week 12 (w12), and then every 24 weeks until last follow‐up. Additional samples were obtained to confirm clinical indications of disease progression.

Conventional G‐banding analysis was performed in all patients, and karyotypes were described in accordance with the International System for Human Cytogenetic Nomenclature. 20 Deletion of the long arm of chromosome 5 was confirmed by interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH).

Next‐generation sequencing (NGS) was performed in available samples using two in‐house custom capture‐enrichment panel (Twist Bioscience) targeting 65 and 92 myeloid genes. Libraries were prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions and sequenced on Illumina platforms. 21 , 22

For true oncogenic somatic variant calling, synonymous, noncoding variants and polymorphisms, present at a population frequency, minor allele frecuency ≥ 1% in single nucleotid polymorphism (SNP) under databases, were excluded. In addition, variants recurrently observed, suspected of being sequencing errors, were removed. The remaining variants were considered candidate somatic mutations when (i) the sequencing depth at the genomic position of the mutation was higher than 2000 reads; (ii) variant allele frequency (VAF) was higher than 5% in at least one of the sequenced samples; (iii) VAF was higher than 2%; and (iv) described in COSMIC and/or ClinVar as being cancer‐associated and known hotspot mutations with clinical pathogenic significance. 22 Variants with VAF < 2% were considered negative for analysis, although low‐level TP53 signals were still closely monitored due to their clinical relevance.

Statistical analysis

Dichotomous variables were compared between different groups using the χ 2 test and continuous variables by the Kolmogorov, Student's t‐test, Mann–Whitney, and Kruskal–Wallis tests. Results were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Paired‐sample tests (Wilcoxon and Friedman tests) were applied to compare each arm of the study within the different points of time under analysis.

Time to RBC‐TD, AML progression, OS, or time of responses to treatment were estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method, and groups were compared by two‐sided log‐rank test. Time to AML progression was considered as the time from the inclusion in the study to the AML diagnosis and censored in patients without AML transformation at the last follow‐up or date of death.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 25 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.0.2 with biostatistical packages (https://cran.r-project.org/).

Molecular analysis at baseline (BSL) revealed the presence of 41 somatic mutations (24 in the Len arm and 17 in the Pcb group). Median number of mutations per patient was 1 (range 0–3) in both Len and Pcb groups.

The most frequently mutated genes were SF3B1 (25.5%) and TP53 (21.3%), followed by DNMT3A (19.1%). Distribution of SF3B1, TP53, and DNMT3A gene mutations was well‐balanced in the treatment and the Pcb arms (Table 1, Figure 1). Median VAF of mutations at BSL was 14.7% (range 0%–87%) in the Len arm and 18% (range 2%–40%) in Pcb. All these data confirmed that patients included in both arms were comparable at BSL regarding clinical and molecular features.

Cytogenetic response in patients treated with Len was accompanied by a reduction in VAF of mutations at w12, remaining stable during treatment

To evaluate the effect of Len in early treated patients with del(5q) MDS, we analyzed clonal architecture in sequential samples of 21 patients treated with Len and 13 with Pcb in the short‐term (BSL vs. w12) and during the entire treatment phase (BSL vs. EOT).

In the Len group, the mean percentage of cells with the 5q deletion detected by FISH decreased from 37.8% to 3.7% in treated patients (P < 0.0001), with 80.9% (17 of 21) of patients achieving CyR. A total of 17 somatic mutations were found in 13 patients: seven mutations (41.2%) showed a mean VAF reduction of 26.3% (Figure 2A), and seven mutations (41.2%) remained stable after 12 weeks of treatment. Only one patient showed an increase in the VAF of a DNMT3A mutation at w12, when CyR was achieved (Figures 2A and S1, P04‐001, P < 0.0001). In addition, eight patients did not show somatic mutations at BSL and all of them remained without detectable mutation at w12 of treatment. Regarding the acquisition of mutations, one patient showed two TP53 mutations at w12 for the first time, both with VAF below 2%. These mutations were not detected in the BSL sample, but no disease progression was observed 25.8 months after Len treatment discontinuation. Altogether, median VAF of mutations harbored in patients treated with Len showed a significant reduction at w12 [from 14.7% (range 0%–87%) to 7% (range 0%–36%), P = 0.013; Figure 2B,D).

Interestingly, at the EOT, most mutations found at BSL remained stable or had decreased VAF/disappeared under treatment (82.3%). Only three DNMT3A mutations showed a VAF increase, all of them in patients achieving CyR to Len. Overall, the mean VAF of mutations in patients treated with Len was 9% (range 0%–41%) at the EOT, remaining lower than BSL VAF (Figure 2B,D).

By contrast, patients included in the Pcb group did not achieve CyR, showing a slightly increased percentage of 5q deletion clones by FISH (50.8% at BSL and 56.2% at w12, P = 0.063). Contrary to the Len arm, all mutations found in patients from the Pcb group (n = 9) remained stable in the short‐term (18% median VAF at BSL and w12, P = 0.594, Figure 2C,D). Furthermore, the long‐term monitoring of somatic mutations at EOT in Pcb patients revealed clonal expansion in four mutations, while the remaining alterations were stable (74.9%). A significant increase in median VAF was observed in Pcb patients in the EOT compared to BSL (29.5% vs. 18%, respectively; P = 0.007; Figure 2C,D).

Mutations in SF3B1, TP53, and DNMT3A exhibited a different behavior under Len administration

Clonal dynamics of SF3B1, TP53, and DNMT3A mutations under Len administration were independently analyzed in treated patients. Incidence of these mutations in both the Len and the Pcb groups is shown in Table 1.

Dynamics of SF3B1 mutations under Len treatment was heterogeneous. In fact, 37.5% of treated patients showed a significant reduction in SF3B1 mutant allele burden, while it remained stable in 62.8% of cases (as occurred in the Pcb group). All patients achieved CyR and erythroid response (ER) with Len, but most of them (seven out of eight) lost responses at the EOT. Median duration of CyR was shorter in patients with SF3B1 mutations than in those with wild‐type SF3B1 (12 vs. 24.6 months, P = 0.047). Regarding disease progression, a lower percentage of patients with SF3B1 mutations became TD in the Len (37.5%) versus the Pcb group (100%, n = 4) (P = 0.08). Median time to TD in patients with SF3B1 mutations was longer in the Len arm than in the Pcb arm (21.8 vs. 6.3 months; P = 0.006). In addition, the incidence of AML progression was lower in SF3B1‐mutated patients in the Len (25%, two out of eight), as compared to the Pcb group (50%, two out of four) (Table 2). In the subset of patients harboring SF3B1 mutations, the median time to AML was 24.1 and 16 months in Len and Pcb groups, respectively.

Clonal evolution of TP53‐mutated patients treated with Len revealed a substantial reduction in VAF of all mutations (median VAF reduction of 10%) during the treatment. Conversely, VAF of TP53 alterations observed in Pcb patients remained stable, and one even increased. Among five patients with TP53 mutations, three (60%) and two (40%) achieved CyR and ER with Len, respectively, while a higher percentage of patients without these alterations achieved responses to treatment (CyR: 91.7%; ER: 83.3%) with similar duration of responses. In the LTFU, a total of fifteen patients with TP53 mutations were considered, regardless of the TP53 status at BSL (see Table 3 for more details; 10 patients in the Len group: 5 with BSL mutations of TP53, 3 without TP53 alterations at the beginning, and 2 without BSL NGS data available). A similar percentage of TP53‐mutated patients became TD (80% Len and 60% Pcb, P = 0.56) and underwent AML transformation (3 out of 10 and 1 out of 5 within the Len and Pcb arms, respectively). Of note, transformation to AML occurred in a total of eleven patients in this study, and 72.7% of them did not harbor mutations in TP53 during all the follow‐up. Two TP53 mutations appeared after loss of response to Len, EOT, without evidence that these mutations were present at BSL. VAFs were lower than 5%, and neither of these two patients progressed to TD nor developed AML in 64.8 and 96.6 months of follow‐up (40 and 71 months after Len discontinuation, respectively). Moreover, one patient with <2% VAF mutation in TP53 first observed at w12, did not exhibit AML transformation after 35.1 months of follow‐up (exitus due to infection).

Regarding clonal evolution of DNMT3A mutations, four out of seven patients (57.1%) harbored mutations only in DNMT3A, while three cases showed mutational concurrence with SF3B1 (3) and TP53 (1) (Figure S1). Most DNMT3A mutations (85.7%) were not affected by Len, with VAF either remaining stable (as in the Pcb group) or even increasing under treatment. Interestingly, in all cases with commutations, Len revealed either a substantial reduction or stability in the additional coexisting mutations. All patients with DNMT3A mutations achieved both CyR and ER to treatment and maintained it during the entire trial, suggesting that these mutations might have a role independent from the 5q‐ clone, and its persistence did not seem to influence responses to Len.

Long‐term follow‐up revealed clonal stability in del(5q) patients treated with Len

A total of 23 BM samples from patients with del(5q) (17 in the Len arm and 6 in the Pcb group) were sequenced after the end of the SintraRev trial monitoring. Disease progression and patient follow‐up were available up to 104 months after the end of the trial, with a median of 43.6 months of extended follow‐up. Expansion of preexisting mutations (defined as a >50% increase in VAF relative to the previous measurement), as well as acquisition of additional mutations, were found in 4 (17.4%) patients after the end of the SintraRev trial monitoring, which was less frequent in the Len arm (11.8%) than in the Pcb arm (33.3%) (P = 0.27).

In the Len group, 15 patients (88.2%) did not show clonal expansion or acquisition of new mutations after Len administration with a median follow‐up of 43.7 months. Patients without BSL mutations remained stable and did not acquire somatic alterations after the end of the trial. By contrast, only one DNMT3A‐mutated clone that decreased under Len treatment showed an expansion in the long‐term, consistent with a relapse of the malignant clone after Len discontinuation (patient 04‐001, R882H mutation; Figure S1).

Regarding TP53 mutations in patients with available samples after completing the trial, two of three patients remained stable after the end of the study. Furthermore, two patients with SF3B1 mutations at BSL showed acquired TP53 mutations after losing response to treatment (>2 years) and with VAF below 5% (Figure S2). Neither of these two patients experienced AML transformation after 96.6 and 64.8 months of follow‐up, respectively. Otherwise, only in one TP53‐mutated patient, treatment failure was observed, leading to premature study exit. Interestingly, during Len treatment, the VAF of this TP53 mutation dropped from 23% to 4%. Seventeen months after leaving the trial, the TP53 mutant clone re‐expanded (40% VAF) and the disease transformed into AML, ultimately causing the death of the patient. Of note, no clinical data were available from the end of the study regarding additional treatments received and clinical parameters that might have influenced the expansion of this TP53 mutant clone.

In the Pcb group, four patients (66.7%) analyzed in the long‐term remained stable, and two cases showed clonal expansion. In one patient, an expansion of a TP53 mutant clone was observed (with a VAF increase from 29% at BSL to 42% in the last follow‐up). In the other patient, the acquisition of a JAK2 mutation, as well as the expansion of a preexisting mutation in MPL, was observed in a Pcb patient after the end of the study. Overall, long‐term follow‐up confirmed marked clonal stability in Len‐treated patients, with fewer expansions or new mutations compared with Pcb. This sustained molecular stability was accompanied by a non‐significant trend toward improved OS, as shown in Figure S3.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we show that low doses of Len during only 2 years of treatment in non‐TD del(5q) MDS patients reduced the mutational burden of TP53 and SF3B1, and did not increase the risk of AML transformation.

In our cohort, SF3B1 and TP53 were the most frequently mutated genes, with a similar incidence for TP53 (21.3%) and slightly higher in SF3B1 (25.5%), as compared to previous cohorts of del(5q) MDS. 8 , 9 , 10 Both mutations have been reported as poor prognostic factors in del(5q) MDS, but scarce data are available on their effect on response to Len and their evolution in patients treated with Len. We found that similar molecular responses were achieved in the Len group, regardless of the presence of mutations in TP53 or SF3B1. Specifically, a significant reduction of mutation VAF was observed in the Len group, but not in the Pcb. This finding is in accordance with the decrease of major mutations after Len treatment reported in both del(5q) and non‐del(5q) MDS patients. 23 , 24 In non‐del(5q) MDS patients, Chesnais et al. found that some additional mutations remained stable or displayed a relative increase under treatment in responders, 23 similar to what we observed in DNMT3A mutations in patients who achieved CyR (Figure S2). This observation could potentially be explained by a selective effect on Len on del(5q) clones, versus its effect on non‐clonal hematopoietic cells 25 or CHIP‐associated alterations (like DNMT3A mutations). 26 , 27

The long‐term follow‐up data confirmed that Len administration was safe in our cohort, as near 90% of the treated patients remained stable or even decreased the mutated allele burden, confirming molecular responses induced by Len treatment, conversely to what occurred in Pcb patients. Remarkably, no mutations were found with the 65‐gene panel used in eight patients in the Len group at BSL, and all of them remained unmutated until the last follow‐up, several years after the end of the trial. Furthermore, only 10% of patients showed an increased VAF in DNMT3A mutations, which, in most cases, could be related to a CHIP process. In fact, only one DNMT3A‐mutated patient showed a VAF reduction in this mutation after Len treatment. By contrast, approximately 30% of patients in the Pcb arm showed clonal expansion, with acquisition of a JAK2 mutation and expansion of MPL and TP53 mutations, all these alterations related to poor prognosis within MDS and AML patients. 28 , 29

All patients harboring SF3B1 mutations achieved responses to Len. It should be noted that the duration of CyR was shorter in SF3B1‐mutated compared to SF3B1‐wild type cases. However, the early administration of Len in patients with SF3B1 alterations resulted in a lower incidence of TD and AML progression, as well as a prolonged time until its occurrence (more than twice as long). These observations suggest that the early treatment with a low dose of Len could be an effective therapeutic approach improving outcome in SF3B1‐mutated del(5q) MDS patients, regardless of the duration of CyR.

The presence of a TP53 mutation was associated with a lower response rate to Len, as only 40% of TP53‐mutated patients achieved ER and 60% CyR, in agreement with previously published data. 16 The administration of Len in patients with del(5q) MDS carrying TP53 mutations is still controversial. An expansion of TP53 mutations under Len treatment has been described. Indeed, there is a concern about Len promoting AML progression in patients with these mutations 16 , 17 and, for this reason, TP53 must be carefully studied in patients with del(5q) MDS before being treated with Len. 30 To date, scarce data regarding sequential samples before and after Len treatment have been evaluated (only in two patients). 16 Here, we evaluated the mutational dynamics of TP53 in several time points under Len treatment. In our cohort, the mutational burden of TP53 decreased with Len, and only a small proportion of patients showed disease progression years after treatment. In fact, 20% of patients treated with Len and showing TP53 mutations developed AML, an incidence similar to that of the Pcb group, and similar to the 23.2% observed in the MDS‐004 clinical trial 13 (Table S1). These results suggest that the early treatment of del(5q) MDS patients with lower doses of Len may prevent AML progression, compared to standard treatment with higher Len doses in more advanced phases of the del(5q) MDS. Moreover, not only patients with TP53 mutations at BSL were evaluated regarding disease progression, but also patients showing these alterations at any time in the trial. Interestingly, two patients showed newly‐acquired TP53 mutations (with 5% VAF) several months after Len failure, both of them maintaining RBC‐TI and with leukemia‐free survival times beyond 40 months after treatment discontinuation (Figure S1). According to this finding, small TP53 clones are known to frequently appear transiently in del(5q) MDS. 16 The restoration of normal hematopoiesis in these patients could be the reason for the appearance of new stable clones, small and not visible by these techniques, which do not increase the risk of progression to AML.

Few data regarding long‐term follow‐up of del(5q) MDS patients treated with Len and carrying TP53 mutations are available, and the expansion of a clone with an acquired TP53 mutation after losing response to Len is infrequent. 24 It should be noted that the poor prognosis traditionally associated with TP53 mutations in MDS has recently being linked to “multiple hits” or “biallelic alterations” in TP53, which may occur by either the presence of two mutations on distinct alleles or loss of heterozygosity, mainly due to del(17p). 3 , 4 , 7 In that sense, none of the patients in our cohort showed neither biallelic TP53 affection nor VAF > 20%, which may explain the relatively favorable prognosis observed in our TP53‐mutated cases, according to previous published data. 9 Therefore, this study opens a new possibility of making Len the standard treatment in all del(5q) MDS patients without RBC‐TD. Additional studies are needed to evaluate the safety of Len administration in larger cohorts of del(5q) MDS patients with TP53 mutations after being treated with Len.

In conclusion, the clonal dynamics of mutations generally decreased or remained stable after treatment with Len in non‐TD MDS with del(5q). Moreover, treatment with Len did not appear to promote expansion of preexisting clones or accelerate AML progression, especially in TP53‐mutated cases. Our study provides evidence that the early administration of low doses of Len in del(5q) MDS patients without RBC‐TD is an effective and safe therapeutic approach that could improve disease symptoms by prolonging the time until RBC transfusion requirements without accelerating disease progression. It remains to be demonstrated whether early reduction of the del(5q) clone, with or without other mutations, can delay disease progression.
