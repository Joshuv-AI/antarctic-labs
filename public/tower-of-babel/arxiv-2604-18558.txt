Uniform analyticity of local observables in FK-percolation and analyticity of the Ising spontaneous magnetisation
Lucas D’Alimonte, Loïc Gassmann

Abstract
. We prove that, in the FK-percolation model, the probabilities of local events are uniformly analytic in the percolation parameter p p under suitable mixing assumptions on the measure, and satisfy a uniform exponential growth bound. This result allows us to prove that the magnetisation of the Potts model is analytic in a suitable range of parameters, including the Ising case in all dimensions d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 in the whole supercritical regime. We also provide a proof of the analyticity of the susceptibility of the Potts model with q q colours, for any q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 in the whole subcritical interval. Finally, we prove the analyticity of various quantities in the FK-percolation measure, including the multi-point and truncated multi-point connectivity probabilities.



1.
Introduction

The question of the regularity of thermodynamic quantities in lattice spin models is considered to be fundamental, and has attracted a lot of interest since the systematic study of these models.
Indeed, in the physics literature, these regularity properties are used to characterize the nature of the phase transition exhibited by these models, if any.
A parameter at which the analyticity of a thermodynamic quantity is broken is considered by the physicists to be a phase transition point, and further properties of the phase transition can be derived by the way in which the analyticity is broken (to the best knowledge of the authors, these ideas were systematically introduced by Paul Ehrenfest
[
14
]
).
It is thus of deep interest to understand those regularity properties, both away and at the transition points.

Another more specific reason for considering the question of the analyticity properties of thermodynamic quantities away from transition points is the discovery by Griffiths
[
17
]
of an intriguing phenomenon, now known as
Griffiths singularities
.
In this seminal work, a phenomenon leading to a breaking of analyticity of the magnetisation and the free energy in models
away from the critical point
was identified, in the setting of Ising models with random Hamiltonians, or
diluted magnets
models.
In
pure
models, however, one generally expects the absence of Griffiths singularities, and a strict equivalence between analyticity breaking and point of phase transition.

This article is concerned with establishing analyticity properties of some thermodynamic observables of the
Potts model with
q
colours
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
It is one the most studied lattice spin models, and includes as a special case when
q=2
the celebrated
Ising model
.
As mentioned previously, the question of the off-critical analyticity has attracted a lot of attention during the past century.
We shall however try to review the most significant contributions to this problem, some of them being of very different nature.

Let us first discuss results concerned with analyticity of the
free energy
(sometimes referred to as the
pressure
, up to a
\beta
multiplicative factor).
The first range of results concerns
perturbative arguments
.
Convergent cluster expansions have been used to establish analyticity properties in perturbative regimes (that is, very high or very low temperature Ising models) in
[
28
,
29
]
.
These type of arguments have been refined to general Gibbsian fields (including the Potts model) at very high temperatures in
[
35
]
.
General arguments regarding
complete analyticity
of lattice spin models were provided in
[
7
]
to argue that, under a set of strong assumptions including a strong mixing property, the free energy is analytic in the temperature.
In two dimensions, perturbative arguments have been used in
[
38
]
to establish
complete analyticity
of the Potts model with large
q
in the whole subcritical regime.
Moreover, the Ising model in two dimensions is exactly solvable, and its free energy has been computed by Onsager
[
30
]
. In particular, it is indeed an analytic function except at the critical point.
On the side of non-perturbative arguments, general Lee-Yang techniques
[
24
,
25
]
have been successful in proving the analyticity of the free energy in the magnetic parameter
h
in the region
\Re(h)>0
.
The picture has finally been completed in the case of the Ising model by Ott
[
32
,
31
]
who showed that the free energy of the Ising model can be analytically continued around each non-critical inverse temperature
\beta
.
Finally, we also mention the recent
[
13
]
which establishes the analyticity of the free energy of the XY model in its disordered phase, as well as the analyticity of the free energy of the 2D Coulomb gas in the complement of the BKT phase of the Villain model in 2D.

One may also consider different thermodynamic quantities, such as the
spontaneous magnetisation
or the
mass
of the model.
In that case, the picture is much less complete and almost no non-perturbative argument is known, except for the two-dimensional Ising model, for which the mass was computed in
[
39
]
and the magnetisation in
[
40
]
, again defining analytic functions away from criticality.
Actually, the article
[
2
]
shows that all the averages of local functions under the extremal Ising measures in two dimensions are analytic away from the critical point, using algebraic methods related to the
C^{*}
-algebras theory.

However, it has been increasingly realised that
graphical representations
of spin models
[
8
]
are of great interest to pinpoint finer properties of these models, and this is the approach that we shall use in this article.
In that direction, the first results were obtained in the influential
[
23
]
, in which Kesten proved the analyticity of the susceptibility
\chi
and of the number of open clusters per vertex
\kappa
in the full subcritical regime of Bernoulli percolation.
Later on, analyticity of the
mass
(or
inverse correlation length
) of subcritical Bernoulli percolation was established in
[
4
]
.
We also mention the proof of the analyticity of the mass of the self-avoiding walk in the subcritical regime of
[
22
]
.

In the Edwards–Sokal coupling of the Potts model, the magnetisation is related to the probability in the FK-percolation that the origin is contained in an infinite cluster.
This probability is often called
\theta(p)
, when
p
is the percolation parameter, and is non-zero in the
supercritical
regime of percolation.
The question of the analyticity of
\theta
is thus of deep interest, and we note that it was actually raised by Kesten in
[
23
]
.
This question was answered in the case of Bernoulli percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in the work
[
16
]
, in which the analyticity of the
\theta
function of Bernoulli percolation in the full supercritical regime is proved.
For Bernoulli percolation beyond the graph
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we may cite the very recent
[
27
]
, that extends the precedent result to Bernoulli percolation on a much more general class of graphs of polynomial growth.
Finally we may mention the closely related work
[
33
]
, which proves analyticity of the
\theta
function of yet a different model called the
Gaussian free field percolation
.

In this article, we investigate these questions for a classical graphical representation of the Potts model called
FK-percolation
, that generalizes Bernoulli percolation and the edges of which are no longer independent.
The analyticity of some quantities of FK-percolation naturally implies similar results in the case of the Potts model by the so-called
Edwards–Sokal coupling
.
In particular, we rigorously prove that:

(1)

The susceptibility of the Potts model is analytic in the whole subcritical regime, in any dimension, for any
q\geq 2
.

(2)

The spontaneous magnetisation of the Ising model is analytic in dimension
d\geq 3
in the whole supercritical regime, answering Kesten’s question
[
23
]
for the FK-percolation with
q=2
(also known as the
FK-Ising
model),

(3)

The multi-point connectivity probabilities of the FK-Ising measure are analytic in dimension
d\geq 3
when
p\neq p_{c}
.

(4)

Local observables of FK-percolation are uniformly analytic and have
exponentially bounded growth
in a suitable range of parameters, that includes the subcritical regime for any
q\geq 1
, and the whole off-critical regime for
q=2
.

The method consists in refining the cluster expansion method developed in
[
32
,
31
]
to prove item (4).
The proof of items (1), (2) and (3) is then done using a straightforward adaptation of
[
16
]
.

1.1.
Definition of the models

In this work, we shall work on the graph
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, with
d\geq 2
, endowed with its nearest-neighbour graph structure.
The nearest-neighbour connectivity will be denoted by the symbol
\sim
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we define the box
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq[-n,n]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

1.1.1.
The Potts model

The Potts model is a celebrated example of lattice spin model (we refer to
[
12
]
for an extensive review).
In
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, with
d\geq 1
, a formal definition is given below. First set an integer
q\geq 2
(the number of colours).
The spin space will be the set
\{1,\dots,q\}
. Given an inverse temperature
\beta>0
, a finite set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and a
boundary condition

\tau\in\{0,1,\dots,q\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, define the probability measure:

\mu^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\beta}[\sigma]=\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\exp\big(\beta\sum_{x\sim y\atop x,y\in\Lambda}\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{y}\}}+\beta\sum_{x\sim y\atop x\in\Lambda,y\notin\Lambda}\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma_{x}=\tau_{y}\}}\big).

The constant
\mathbf{Z}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\beta}
is the unique number normalizing the measure to 1, and is called the
partition function
.
We will mostly be interested in
monochromatic
measures.
For
j\in\{0,1,\dots,q\}
, abbreviate
\mathbf{j}
for the boundary condition constant equal to
j
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
The measure
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}_{\Lambda,\beta}
will be referred to as
free
in the volume
\Lambda
, while
\mu^{\mathbf{j}}_{\Lambda,\beta}
with
j\neq 0
will be referred to as
monochromatic
in volume
\Lambda
.
It is classical (see
[
12
]
) that all the measures
\mu^{\mathbf{j}}_{\Lambda,\beta}
admit limits when
\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, provided a suitable growth condition on the volumes
\Lambda
(the sequence of boxes
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies this growth condition).
Those limits will be referred to as
\mu^{\mathbf{j}}_{\beta}
.

When
d\geq 2
, the Potts model in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
notoriously exhibits a
phase transition
phenomenon.
Define the
spontaneous magnetisation
1
1

1

The choice of
\mathbf{1}
for the boundary condition matching the event
\{\sigma_{0}=1\}
is conventional: by spin symmetry, this quantity is the same if one replaces the boundary condition by
\mathbf{j}
and the considered event by
\{\sigma_{0}=j\}
.
as

m^{*}(\beta)\coloneqq\mu^{\mathbf{1}}_{\beta}[\sigma_{0}=1]-\frac{1}{q}.

The
critical parameter
is defined as
\beta_{c}\coloneqq\sup\{\beta>0,m^{*}(\beta)=0\}
.
Then, when
d\geq 2
, a celebrated and classical argument due to Peierls implies that for any
q\geq 2
, one has
\beta_{c}\in(0,+\infty)
.

We will also be interested in the so called
susceptibility function
, defined for any
\beta<\beta_{c}
as follows:

\chi_{\mathsf{Potts}}(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\{\mu^{\mathbf{1}}_{\beta}[\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{x}]-\tfrac{1}{q}\}.

When
\beta<\beta_{c}
, the susceptibility is finite in any dimension

; this is a consequence of the deep results of
[
11
]
.

1.1.2.
The random-cluster model

The Potts model is classically related to a percolation model called
FK-percolation
(sometimes referred to as
random-cluster model
), that we now define.
Slightly abuse notation by writing
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\coloneqq(V(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),E(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
, where
V(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
denotes the vertex set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the set of nearest-neighbour unoriented edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
A
percolation configuration
is a configuration of
\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
.
As previously, we start by defining the measure in finite volumes. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
For any two percolation configurations
\omega,\xi\in\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
, define
k^{\xi}_{G}(\omega)
to be the number of vertex connected components (hereafter referred to as
clusters
) of
\omega_{|E(G)}\cup\xi_{|E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\setminus E(G)}
intersecting
V(G)
.
For any two parameters
p\in(0,1),q\geq 1
, define the random cluster measure in
G
with boundary condition
\xi
as follows:

(1)

\phi^{\xi}_{G,p,q}[\omega]=\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}^{\xi}_{G,p,q}}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{|\omega|}q^{k^{\xi}_{G}(\omega)},

where
\mathbf{Z}^{\xi}_{G,p,q}
is a renormalisation function. Note that this function is a polynomial in
\frac{p}{1-p}
. As previously, when the boundary conditions are either
free
(
i.e.

\xi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) or
wired
(
i.e.

\xi\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
), the measures can be extended to the full lattice by taking appropriate limits
G\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Those measures will respectively be referred to as
\phi^{0}_{p,q}
and
\phi^{1}_{p,q}
.

Another type of boundary conditions will also be considered in this article.
Define
\mathbb{T}_{N}
to be the torus of radius
N
.
For a percolation configuration
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{T}_{N})}
, define
k_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}(\omega)
to be the number of open clusters of
\omega
, where the connectivity notion is inherited from the torus connectivity.
Then, we define the FK-percolation measure with
periodic
boundary conditions
\phi_{\mathbb{T}_{N},p,q}
by (
1
), with
k_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
in place of
k^{\xi}_{G}
.

This model also undergoes a phase transition for the existence of an infinite cluster: indeed, first define

\theta(p)\coloneqq\phi_{p,q}^{1}[0\leftrightarrow\infty],

where
\{0\leftrightarrow\infty\}
denotes the event that the cluster of 0 has infinite cardinality.
Further define
p_{c}\coloneqq\sup\{p\in[0,1],\theta(p)=0\}
. Then, when
d\geq 2
, the above-mentioned
Peierls’ argument
allows to prove that
p_{c}\in(0,1)
.

In this setting, the susceptibility is defined for
p<p_{c}
as

\chi_{\mathsf{FK}}(p)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi_{p,q}^{1}[0\leftrightarrow x]=\phi^{1}_{p,q}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}|],

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}
denotes the connected component of 0.
Again, when
p<p_{c}
, the susceptibility is finite in any dimension
d\geq 2
due to
[
11
]
.

Moreover, the
multi-point connectivity functions
are defined as follows, for
p\in(0,1)
,
q\geq 1,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

•

\tau_{p,q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\phi^{1}_{p,q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\text{ lie in the same cluster}]
,

•

\tau_{p,q}^{\mathsf{f}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\phi^{1}_{p,q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\text{ lie in the same \emph{finite} cluster}]
.

1.1.3.
Edwards–Sokal coupling

It is well-known that the Potts model with
q
colours at inverse temperature
\beta>0
and the random-cluster model with parameters
p(\beta)\coloneqq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}
and
q
can be coupled through the so-called
Edwards–Sokal coupling
(again we refer to
[
12
]
for a more extensive background).
In particular, it follows from the properties of the coupling that for any
G\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
finite,

\mu^{\mathbf{1}}_{G,\beta,q}[\sigma_{0}=1]=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{q-1}{q}\phi^{1}_{G,p(\beta),q}[0\leftrightarrow\partial G].

Letting
G\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we obtain the following identity that relates the magnetisation of the Potts model and the function
\theta
in the random-cluster model:

(2)

m^{*}(\beta)=\frac{q-1}{q}\theta(p(\beta)).

The key result concerns analyticity of local events in the FK measure.
In the next statement, the notation
\phi_{\mathbb{T}_{N},p+z,q}

A priori

3

3

This is a key difference with the case of Bernoulli percolation, treated in

1.3.1.
Comments on the statements

We start with some comments concerning our main results.

•

The main innovation of this result is the control of the exponential growth of the probability of a local event under the complex measure given by (

Indeed let us mention that point (1) of Theorem

The bound (

The reader might be worried about the specific choice of toroidal boundary conditions.

•

While the Ising model is integrable in dimension 2 and the magnetisation explicitly computable in that setting

In this sense, the analyticity property proved in this article is the best “rigidity” result one could expect in absence of an explicit computation.

1.3.2.
Overview of the proof and organisation

As mentioned in the introduction, the proofs of Theorems
1.4
,
1.6
, and
1.7
are fairly standard, once Theorem

is established.
Indeed, the analyticity of the FK susceptibility follows from the fact that the cluster volume enjoys the

The proof of the analyticity of

However it was observed in

that a much more refined notion of
separating component
do enjoy this property, and that thanks to a combinatorially involved inclusion-exclusion principle, one may prove the analyticity of
\theta
, provided a good exponential control on the growth rate of the analytic extensions of the probabilities of local events.
This strategy was then successfully implemented by these authors in the case of Bernoulli percolation (corresponding to
q=1
in the random-cluster model).
The conclusion of this discussion is that to extend these methods to FK-percolation, the crux is Theorem
1.2
and especially the bound given by (

⋆

‣
2
).
The results concerning the Potts model trivially follow from the Edwards–Sokal coupling.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is done in Section
2
, while the standard proofs of Theorems
1.4
,
1.6
, and
1.7
are sketched in Section

.

For proving Theorem
1.2
, we rely on two main ingredients.
The first one is the observation by Ott
[
31
]
, based on ideas coming from
[
26
]
and
[
21
]
that a construction based on the coupling from the past of the Glauber dynamics for FK-percolation allows to construct a

.
Following the strategy of
[
31
]
, we use this dependency percolation as a blackbox to apply cluster expansion methods.

However, it turns out that one faces severe difficulties in implementing “direct” cluster expansion methods for local observables of the FK-percolation, and that any such attempt will produce a radius of analyticity that depends on the size of the support of the observable that one tries to expand (and that this radius will shrink to 0 when the size of the support increases). In particular, expanding observables as one single polymer partition function will lead to such a phenomenon, as illustrated in the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
31
]
, for instance.

Our main contribution is to overcome this difficulty. The method starts by expanding complex perturbations of local observables as
weighted sums
of polymer partition functions instead of a single one.
The main effort is then dedicated to control the complex modulus of the weight function to produce a global upper bound on the modulus of those complex perturbations.
The paper is organised as follows.

•

Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2

–

In Section

–

In Section

–

In Section

–

In Section

•

Section

•

Technical tools about the cluster expansion are deferred to the Appendix.

\sum_{\{\bar{\gamma}\}\in\mathfrak{I}\cap I(\Delta)\atop|\mathsf{Tr}(\{\bar{\gamma}\})|>K|\Delta|}\exp\Big(\big[c_{\varepsilon}-c_{L}+\log(1+C_{\varepsilon}^{L}\mathrm{e}^{a_{L}})\big]|\mathsf{Tr}(\{\bar{\gamma}\})|\Big)\exp((a_{L}+c_{\mathsf{FE}})|\Delta|).

We then resum over the possible values of

Up to potentially increasing the value of

Now, the fact that both

Thus, for this choice of

Consider the choice of

and

Combining (

As the values of

3.2.
Analyticity of
\theta

The previous example stresses the importance of the exponential upper bound given by Theorem
1.2
in order to prove the analyticity of percolation quantities that can be expressed as a sum of probabilities of local events. Unfortunately, a naive adaptation of the proof of Proposition

is doomed to fail for proving the analyticity of

in the supercritical regime, as the quantity

is expected to decay as

[
5
]
for the “historical” proof in the case of Bernoulli percolation). Indeed, this bound is not good enough for the above-mentioned strategy to work, because one would require an exponential bound.

In
[

that separate 0 from infinity, and by showing that the probability that the percolation configuration exhibits some

of size

as a sum of probabilities of local events with the required exponential bound.

The argument of
[

]
is mostly based on combinatorial arguments, which do not rely on the independence of the edges in Bernoulli percolation.
In the proof below, we give the details on how to extend their argument to the general FK-percolation case by using Theorem
1.2
.
As most of the argument is the same as
[
16
]
, the proof is not self-contained, and we point out to the relevant statements of
[
16
]
that we use.

Sketch of proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let us take
(d,p_{0},q)\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{FK}}
. If
p_{0}<p_{c}
then

is obviously true in this case. So, let us suppose that

.
Before starting with the argument, let us recall that in the regime under consideration, the model possesses the

In the proof, this property will be used in a classical way to perform a coarse-graining argument.
We fix a positive integer

and we call

the coarse-grained lattice with mesh

This is the lattice whose vertex set is given by

For a given configuration

Roughly, a separating component corresponds to a connected component of bad boxes, together with their boundary which consists of good boxes,

The precise definition of a separating component is not needed here, but we refer the reader to section 8.2 of
[
16
]
.
We will just use three claims about

These claims are proven in
[
16
]
for Bernoulli percolation and we explain how to prove them in the general FK-percolation case.

The first claim is a simple geometric observation that follows from the definition of a separating component.
In what follows, when

.

Claim A.
The event that

In order to state the next claim, we define

Claim B.
There exists

The careful reader may check that Claim B can be obtained by a simple adaptation of the proof of

Claim C.
Let

and

the event that the cluster of

has a diameter smaller than

. Then it holds that

\displaystyle 1-\theta(p)=\phi_{p,q}[D_{N}]-\phi_{p,q}[\text{Some separating component occurs},D_{N}^{c}],

The claim above is stated in
[

Thus, choose

We use the same resummation as in the Bernoulli case, but Theorem

Since two intersecting separating components cannot occur at the same time, it follows from Claim B and the inclusion-exclusion principle that

(10)

\displaystyle\phi_{p,q}[\mbox{Some separating component}

\displaystyle\mbox{ occurs},D_{N}^{c}]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\sum\limits_{S\in MS_{n}^{N}}(-1)^{c(S)+1}\phi_{p,q}[S\mbox{ occurs},D_{N}^{c}],

The result for the magnetisation

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\atop A\cap(\gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{n})\neq\emptyset}\big|\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\big|\prod_{k=1}^{n}\big|\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma_{k})\big|\\
\leq\frac{|A|}{|\mathbb{T}_{N}^{L}|}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}}|\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+|\gamma_{k}|)\cdot|\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma_{k})|.

Note that
\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}}\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\prod_{k\geq 1}(1+|\gamma_{k}|)\cdot\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma_{k})
is the cluster expansion of a polymer partition function with modified activities
\tilde{\mathsf{w}}_{z}(\gamma)\coloneqq(1+|\gamma|)\cdot\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma)
.
Observe that these modified activities satisfy the requirement of Theorem
A.1
. So, by the second point of this theorem, we obtain that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\in\mathsf{P}_{N}^{L}}|\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+|\gamma_{k}|)\cdot|\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma_{k})|\leq c_{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{T}_{N}^{L}|,

with
c_{\varepsilon}
that goes to
0
as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
. Substituting this in (
16
) gives

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\atop A\cap(\gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{n})\neq\emptyset}\big|\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\big|\prod_{k=1}^{n}\big|\mathsf{w}_{z}(\gamma_{k})\big|\leq c_{\varepsilon}|A|,

which concludes the proof.

∎
