Scattering for anisotropic potentials
Evgeny Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider the scattering for the operator H = H o + V H=H_{o}+V , where the unperturbed operator H o H_{o} is not assumed to be elliptic and the potential V V is anisotropic. Under some conditions on H o H_{o} and V V we show that the wave operators for H o , H H_{o},H exist and are complete, H H has no singular continuous spectrum and the eigenvalues of H H can accumulate only to zero. For stronger conditions on V V the operator H H has finite number of eigenvalues only. Moreover, these results are applied to the invariance principle and for time-dependent potentials.

1.2.
Main results

Consider the self-adjoint operators
H_{o}=P(-i\mathop{\mathrm{\nabla}}\nolimits),H=H_{o}+V
acting
on the Hilbert space

\varrho^{\varepsilon}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{\nu}\varrho_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}}(x),\quad\varrho_{j}=\langle x_{j}\rangle:=(1+x_{j}^{2})^{-{1\over 2}},\qquad x=(x_{j})_{1}^{\nu}\in\oplus_{1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

We introduce the space

Definition.

i) Let

denote the space of real

\varrho^{-\varepsilon}f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\qquad\sup_{|x|=r}|\varrho^{-\varepsilon}(x)f(x)|\to 0\qquad{\mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits}\quad r\to\infty,

{\mathbb{E}}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\pm}=\bigcap_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}_{\pm}}{\mathbb{E}}_{j},\qquad\ {\mathbb{E}}_{o}=\{\varepsilon\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{\nu}:\mathfrak{r}>1,\mathfrak{r}_{j}>{1\over 2}\ {\rm for\ some}\ j\in{\mathbb{J}}\},\quad{\mathbb{J}}={\mathbb{J}}_{\nu}=\{1,..,\nu\},

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathbb{E}}_{j}=\Big\{\varepsilon\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{\nu}:\varepsilon_{j}+\mathfrak{r}-\mathfrak{r}_{j}>1\Big\}\setminus\Big\{\varepsilon\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{\nu}:\varepsilon_{j}\leqslant{1\over 2},\ \ \mathfrak{r}_{m}\leqslant{1\over 2},\ {\rm for\ some}\ m\in{\mathbb{J}}\setminus\{j\}\Big\},

j\in{\mathbb{J}},\quad\textstyle\mathfrak{r}=\sum_{1}^{\nu}\mathfrak{r}_{j},\ \ \mathfrak{r}_{j}={1\over 2a_{j}}\min\{2\varepsilon_{j},d_{j}\},\quad{\mathbb{J}}_{+}=\{1,2,..,j_{+}\},\quad{\mathbb{J}}_{-}=\{j_{-}-1,j_{-},..,\nu\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider
H=H_{o}+V

and

be some interval and the mapping

1.3.
Time-dependent potentials

Now we consider the scattering for the time-dependent Schrödinger
equation with the Hamiltonian
H(t)=H_{o}+V_{t}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
given by

\textstyle i{\partial u(t)\over\partial t}=H(t)u(t),\qquad u(0)=u_{o}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
H_{o}=P(-i\mathop{\mathrm{\nabla}}\nolimits)
is the unperturbed operator as before and the
potential
V_{t}(x)
is time-dependent, anisotropic. We assume that
the potential
V_{t}(x),t\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

Condition VT.

The function
V_{t}(x)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}})
is real, the mapping
t\to V_{t}
is the continuously
differentiable
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
–valued function.

In order to discuss scattering we need to introduce the propagator
for our Hamiltonian. A
propagator
is a two-parameter family
of unitary operators
U(t,s)
,
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, acting on
{\mathscr{H}}
and
satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet

U(t,s)U(s,r)=U(t,r)
for all
t,s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\bullet

U(t,t)=1\!\!1
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
1\!\!1
is the identity
operator in
{\mathscr{H}}
,

\bullet

U(t,s)
is strongly continuous in
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Our Hamiltonian
H(t)=H_{o}+V_{t},t\in{\mathbb{T}}
is a family of
time-dependent operators on
{\mathscr{H}}
and
V_{t}
is a bounded operator.
Suppose a propagator
U(t,s)
leaves
{\mathscr{D}}={\mathscr{D}}(H_{o})
invariant and
that

\textstyle\frac{d}{dt}U(t,s)f=-iH(t)U(t,s)f,\qquad f\in{\mathscr{D}},

(1.4)

in the strong sense. Then the family
U(t,s)
is called the
propagator for
H(t)
. In this situation we have

\textstyle\frac{d}{ds}U(t,s)f=iU(t,s)H(s)f,\qquad f\in{\mathscr{D}},

(1.5)

where the derivative has the strong sense. Due to well known results
about time-dependent Hamiltonians (see e.g. Theorems X.70, X.71 in

[
32
]
) for our Hamiltonian
H(t)
under the Condition VT
there exists the propagator
U(t,s)
. The propagator
U_{o}(t,s)
for

H_{o}
has the form
U_{o}(t,s)=e^{-i(t-s)H_{o}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
H(t)=H_{o}+V(t)
, where
H_{o}=P(-i\mathop{\mathrm{\nabla}}\nolimits)
and a potential
V_{t}

satisfy Conditions VT and the following

|V_{t}(\cdot)|\leqslant g(t)\varrho^{\varepsilon},\quad{\rm where}\quad\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{E}}_{o},\qquad\langle t\rangle^{-\gamma}g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

(1.6)

for some
\gamma>0
such that
\gamma+\mathfrak{r}>{1\over 2}
. Then there exist the unitary wave
operators given by

{\mathcal{W}}_{\pm}=s-\lim U(0,t)e^{-itH_{o}}\qquad as\qquad t\to\pm\infty.

(1.7)

Now we discuss the scattering for time periodic Hamiltonian

H(t)=H_{o}+V_{t},t\in{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
H_{o}=P(-i\mathop{\mathrm{\nabla}}\nolimits)
is the
operator as before. We assume that the potential

V_{t}(x),t\in{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
is 1-periodic in time, i.e.,
V_{t+1}=V_{t}
for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and satisfies Condition VT. Here we
introduce the additional periodicity condition for the propagators:

\bullet

U(t,s)
is
1
–periodic, i.e.
U(t+1,s+1)=U(t,s)
for
all
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

It is well known that for our Hamiltonian
H(t)
there exists
the propagator
U(t,s)
, which is 1-periodic, i.e. it obeys

U(t+1,s+1)=U(t,s)
for all
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, see
[
10
]
,

[
36
]
.
Introduce the monodromy operators
M=U(1,0)
and
M_{o}=e^{-iH_{o}}

for
H(t),H_{o}
respectively. We present our main results about time
periodic scattering.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
H(t)=H_{o}+V(t)
, where
V_{t}(\cdot)
is 1-periodic in time and
obeys Condition VT and the following

|V_{t}(\cdot)|\leqslant F,\quad{\rm where}\quad F\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,q},

and
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{E}}_{-}\cap{\mathbb{E}}_{+},q>1
. Then the wave operators

{\mathcal{W}}_{\pm}=s-\lim U(0,t)e^{-itH_{o}}\qquad as\qquad t\to\pm\infty,

(1.8)

exist and are complete, i.e.,
{\mathcal{W}}_{\pm}{\mathscr{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{ac}(M)
,

\sigma_{sc}(M)=\emptyset
and eigenvalues of
M

have finite multiplicity and can accumulate only to 1.
If in addition,
F\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{E}}_{o}
, then
M
has a
finite number of eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity.
