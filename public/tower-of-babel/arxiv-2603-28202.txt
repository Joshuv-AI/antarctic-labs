Towards Pósa’s Conjecture for 3 3 -graphs
Debmalya Bandyopadhyay, Allan Lo, Richard Mycroft

Abstract
We prove that every 3 3 -graph H H on n n vertices with minimum codegree δ 2 ​ ( H ) ≥ 7 ​ n / 9 + o ⁡ ( n ) \delta_{2}(H)\geq 7n/9+o(n) contains the square of a tight Hamilton cycle. This strengthens a theorem of Bedenknecht and Reiher that δ 2 ​ ( H ) ≥ 4 ​ n / 5 + o ⁡ ( n ) \delta_{2}(H)\geq 4n/5+o(n) is sufficient. The central novelty of our arguments is an improved understanding of the connectivity structure of 3 3 -graphs with large minimum codegree.



1
Introduction

A fundamental question in graph theory is to determine optimal conditions on a host graph
G
— often expressed in terms of the minimum degree
\delta(G)
— which guarantee the existence of a given structure in
G
. Dirac’s theorem that every graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
admits a Hamilton cycle is an archetypal result of this kind. Since then, a vast body of theory has been developed describing sufficient conditions for embeddings in graphs, hypergraphs, directed graphs and other combinatorial settings. One of the most influential problems in developing this theory was the Pósa–Seymour conjecture stating that every graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{r}{r+1}n
contains the
r
-th power
\mathcal{C}^{r}
of a Hamilton cycle, that is, the graph obtained from a Hamilton cycle by adding the edges of a copy of
K_{r+1}
on each set of
r+1
consecutive vertices of the cycle
(Pósa’s conjecture was the case
r=2
concerning squares of Hamilton cycles, and Seymour
[
26
]
later proposed the more general statement).

The difficulty of this conjecture arises principally due to the high level of connectivity of powers of cycles. Indeed, a perfect
K_{r+1}
-tiling (a spanning collection of vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{r+1}
) can be viewed as a less-connected analogue of
\mathcal{C}^{r}
, since locally each clique has the same structure as
r+1
consecutive vertices of
\mathcal{C}^{r}
. The Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem
[
9
]
, which can be proved by relatively simple techniques, states that the same minimum degree condition as in the Pósa–Seymour conjecture forces
G
to contain a perfect
K_{r+1}
-tiling. By contrast, after a great deal of attention and partial results by many researchers
[
6
,
7
,
15
,
16
]
, it was only by using extensive novel machinery including Szemerédi’s Regularity lemma and the Blow-up lemma that the Pósa–Seymour conjecture was finally proven for sufficiently large
n
by Komlós, Sarközy and Szemerédi
[
17
]
. For small values of
n
the conjecture remains open to this day.

The question we consider here is the generalisation of the Pósa–Seymour conjecture to
3
-graphs (i.e.
3
-uniform hypergraphs — see Section
2
for this definition and for other standard terms pertaining to hypergraphs). Specifically, we consider minimum codegree conditions which ensure the existence of the square of a tight Hamilton cycle in a
3
-graph. The
minimum codegree
of a
3
-graph
H
, denoted by
\delta_{2}(H)
, is the largest
m
for which every pair of vertices of
H
is contained in at least
m
edges. A
3
-graph contains the
square of a tight Hamilton cycle
if its vertices can be cyclically ordered so that every set of four consecutive vertices forms a tetrahedron (that is, a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
, the complete
3
-graph on four vertices).
Our main result is that a minimum codegree of
7n/9+o(n)
suffices for this purpose.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
\alpha>0
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every
3
-graph
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
contains the square of a tight Hamilton cycle.

This strengthens a previous result of Bedenknecht and Reiher
[
4
]
, who showed that a minimum codegree of
4n/5+o(n)
is sufficient. Pavez-Signé, Sanhueza-Matamala and Stein
[
21
]
subsequently generalised their argument to give a bound for the
r
-th power of a tight Hamilton cycle in a
k
-graph for each
r
and
k
, with the same
4n/5+o(n)
bound for the square of a tight Hamilton cycle in a
3
-graph.

As in the graph case, the central difficulty in proving Theorem
1.1
arises due to the high level of connectedness of the structure we are aiming to embed. Indeed, the best-possible minimum codegree condition for a perfect tetrahedron tiling in a
3
-graph was identified as essentially
3n/4
by Lo and Markström
[
19
]
and by Keevash and Mycroft
[
14
]
(the latter authors in fact gave the precise optimal bound for sufficiently large
n
). Every four consecutive vertices in the square of a tight Hamilton cycle induce a tetrahedron, so the perfect tetrahedron tiling can be seen as a less-connected analogue of the structure we seek to embed. More widely, our understanding of embeddings of spanning structures in hypergraphs is poor in comparison to the well-developed theory of spanning structures in graphs, and a key reason for this is our poor understanding of how to handle connectivity in hypergraphs. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
makes a significant contribution on this specific point: the key ideas which enable us to improve on the work of Bedenknecht and Reiher
[
4
]
is a better understanding of the connectivity structure of
3
-graphs satisfying the given minimum codegree condition, as outlined in Section
1.1
.
We also recommend the surveys by Rödl and Ruciński
[
23
]
and by Zhao
[
27
]
for further discussion of Hamilton cycles and tilings in uniform hypergraphs.

We do not believe that the bound of Theorem
1.1
is optimal. Indeed, we conjecture that a condition of
3n/4+o(n)
is likely to be sufficient for the existence of the square of a tight Hamilton cycle in a graph on
n
vertices.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For all
\alpha>0
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every
3
-graph
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(3/4+\alpha)n
contains the square of a tight Hamilton cycle.

A slight modification of a construction by Pikhurko
[
22
]
shows Conjecture
1.2
, if true, would be best-possible up to the
\alpha n
error term. This is the following proposition, which we prove in Section
2.1
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For each
n>4
there exists a
3
-graph
H
on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)=\lfloor 3n/4\rfloor-2
that does not contain the square of a tight Hamilton cycle.

The minimum degree condition of Theorem
1.1
is crucial to our proof arguments, and significant new ideas would be needed to reduce the gap in minimum codegree between Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.3
. We consider this to be a highly significant open problem in extremal graph theory, since its resolution appears to require a much better understanding of how to handle connectivity in uniform hypergraphs under minimum codegree conditions, which is likely to prove useful for many other problems in this area.

1.1
Key ideas and definitions

The central new ideas in our work relate to connectivity properties in
3
-graphs. Specifically, we study the tight connectivity of the
tetrahedral graph

\mathcal{T}(H)
of a
3
-graph
H
of large minimum codegree. This is defined to be the
4
-graph with vertex set
V(H)
whose edges are all sets of four vertices which induce a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
.

Let
H
be a
k
-graph. For edges
e,f\in E(H)
, a
tight walk
from
e
to
f
in
H
is a sequence of (not necessarily disjoint) vertices
W=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell})
with
e=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
and
f=\{v_{\ell-k+1},\dots,v_{\ell}\}
such that
\{v_{i},\dots,v_{i+k-1}\}\in E(H)
for each
i\in[\ell-k+1]
. In other words,
W
begins with the vertices of
e
in some order, concludes with the vertices of
f
in some order, and each set of
k
consecutive vertices in
W
forms an edge of
H
. A set
\mathscr{E}\subseteq E(H)
of edges of
H
is
tightly connected
if for all
e,f\in\mathscr{E}
there exists a tight walk from
e
to
f
. Observe that the relation on
E(H)
in which
e\sim f
if there exists a tight walk from
e
to
f
is an equivalence relation; the equivalence classes of this relation are the
tight components
of
H
. We say that
H
is tightly connected if
E(H)
is
tightly connected
, meaning that
H
has a single tight component.

It is straightforward to show that if
H
is a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>4n/5
, then
\mathcal{T}(H)
is tightly connected.
Indeed, note that any two copies of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
sharing three vertices are tightly connected in
\mathcal{T}(H)
.
Since
H
is tightly connected (c.f.
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
), it suffices to show that given any two edges
\{x,y,z\}
and
\{w,y,z\}
in
H
, there exists a vertex
u
such that both
\{x,y,z,u\}
and
\{w,y,z,u\}
form a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
. Since
\delta_{2}(H)>4n/5
, we may do this by choosing
u
to be a common neighbour of the five pairs
xy
,
xz
,
yz
,
wy
and
wz
.
A variant of this argument played a key role in Bedenknecht and Reiher’s proof
[
4
]
.

A key step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is to show that the weaker minimum codegree condition
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
is also sufficient to ensure that
\mathcal{T}(H)
is tightly connected. This is the following lemma, whose proof is the subject of Section
3
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
. Then
\mathcal{T}(H)
is tightly connected.

A
squared-tight-walk
in a
3
-graph
H
is an ordered sequence
W=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell})
of vertices of
H
in which every set of four consecutive vertices forms a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
. So squared-tight-walks in
H
are precisely tight walks in the tetrahedral graph
\mathcal{T}(H)
. We refer to the ordered triples
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
and
(v_{\ell-2},v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell})
as the
initial triple
and the
final triple
of
W
respectively, and we refer to both triples as
ends
of
W
. For convenience of notation we often write ordered triples as, for example,
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
. We say that
W
is
from
\mathbf{u}
to
\mathbf{v}
if
W
has initial triple
\mathbf{u}
and final triple
\mathbf{v}
. Of particular importance are
squared-tight-paths
in
H
, which are squared-tight-walks in
H
in which all vertices in the sequence are distinct.

Since the minimum degree condition of Lemma
1.4
ensures that every edge of
H
extends to a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
, the conclusion of Lemma
1.4
can be equivalently reformulated by saying that for all
e,f\in E(H)
there is a squared-tight-walk from
e
to
f
. However, this squared-tight-walk is not necessarily a squared-tight-path in
H
. By applying Lemma
1.4
to the reduced
3
-graph of
H
obtained from an application of the Regular Slice Lemma of Allen, Böttcher, Cooley and Mycroft
[
1
]
, we can show that indeed there must be a squared-tight-path from
e
to
f
in
H
for all
e,f\in E(H)
. In fact we give a stronger result – the following connecting lemma – which allows us to find squared-tight-paths linking each of several pairs of edges. Moreover, we may insist that these squared-tight-paths are pairwise vertex-disjoint and avoid a given small set
X
of ‘forbidden’ vertices. For a set
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell}\}
of squared-tight-paths, we write
V(\mathscr{P})
for
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq\ell}V(P_{i})
. The asymptotic notation
a\ll b
used here — which is standard in this field — is defined formally in Section
2
.

Lemma 1.5

(Connecting Lemma)
.

Let
1/n\ll\psi\ll\alpha
. Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
. Let
X\subseteq V(H)
be such that
|X|\leq\psi n
. Let
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{s},\mathbf{y}_{s}
be vertex-disjoint edges in
H\setminus X
with
s\leq\psi n
. Then there exists a set
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{s}\}
of vertex-disjoint squared-tight-paths in
H\setminus X
such that for each
i\leq s
,
P_{i}
is from
\mathbf{x}_{i}
to
\mathbf{y}_{i}
,
|V(\mathscr{P})|\leq\psi^{1/2}n
and
|V(\mathscr{P})\cap X|=\emptyset
.

The derivation of Lemma
1.5
from Lemma
1.4
is presented in Section
5
, and the definitions and results concerning hypergraph regularity used in this argument are presented in Section
4
.

The remaining elements of the proof of Theorem
1.1
follow an absorbing strategy, an approach which is now fairly commonplace for extremal problems in combinatorics. This combines the aforementioned connecting lemma with the following two lemmas; we note that Lemma
1.5
plays a part in the proof of each of these results, and that the proof of Lemma
1.7
also makes use of hypergraph regularity.

Lemma 1.6

(Absorption Lemma)
.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll\alpha
. Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
. Then there exists a squared-tight-path
P
in
H
on at most
7\sqrt{\beta}n
vertices such that for all sets
L\subseteq V(H)\setminus V(P)
with
|L|\leq\beta^{2}n
, there is a squared-tight-path
P^{\prime}
with
V(P^{\prime})=V(P)\cup L
such that
P
and
P^{\prime}
have the same initial and final triples.

Lemma 1.7

(Path Cover Lemma)
.

Let
1/n\ll\gamma\ll\alpha
. Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
. Then for all disjoint pairs of ordered triples of vertices
\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
that are edges of
H
, there is a squared-tight-path from
\mathbf{e}_{1}
to
\mathbf{e}_{2}
in
H
which covers all but at most
\gamma n
vertices of
H
.

We give the proof of Lemma
1.6
in Section
6
and the proof of Lemma
1.7
in Section
7
.
Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, then follows by combining Lemmas
1.5
,
1.6
and
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
1/n\ll\gamma\ll\beta\ll\alpha
.
By Lemma
1.6
, there is a squared-tight-path
P
in
H
on at most
7\sqrt{\beta}n
vertices with the absorbing property stated in Lemma
1.6
. Let
\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
be the initial and final triples of
P
respectively, so
\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
are disjoint edges of
H
. Let
\mathrm{int}(P)=V(P)\setminus\left(V(\mathbf{e}_{1})\cup V(\mathbf{e}_{2})\right)
. By Lemma
1.7
with
H\setminus\mathrm{int}(P)
playing the role of
H
, there is a squared-tight-path
\hat{P}
from
\mathbf{e}_{2}
to
\mathbf{e}_{1}
in
H
which covers all vertices of
H\setminus\mathrm{int}(P)
except for a set
L
of at most
\gamma n
vertices. As
\gamma<\beta^{2}
, by Lemma
1.6
there is a squared-tight-path
P^{\prime}
from
\mathbf{e}_{1}
to
\mathbf{e}_{2}
in
H
such that
V(P^{\prime})=V(P)\cup L
. Then
P^{\prime}\cup\hat{P}
is the square of a tight Hamilton cycle in
H
.∎

2
Preliminary results and notation

We will use constant hierarchies in our statements as follows: the phrase “
a\ll b
” means “for every
b>0
there exists
a_{0}>0
such that for all
0<a\leq a_{0}
the following statements hold”. Hierarchies with more constants are defined analogously, and we implicitly assume that if a term of the form
1/m
appears in such a hierarchy then
m
is a positive integer. We omit floors and ceilings when they do not affect the calculations. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we write
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
, and for a set
V
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\binom{V}{k}
to denote the set of all subsets of
V
of size
k
. For notational simplicity, we write
v_{1}\dots v_{k}
for the set
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
or the ordered
k
-tuple
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
; it will be clear from the context which interpretation is intended.

A
k
-uniform hypergraph

H
(or a
k
-graph
for brevity) consists of a vertex set
V(H)
and an edge set
E(H)\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{k}
. We make the following definitions for
k
-graphs
H
. For a vertex subset
S\subseteq V(H)
, the
neighbourhood
of
S
is
N_{H}(S):=\{T\subseteq V(H)\setminus S\colon S\cup T\in E(H)\}
and the
degree
of
S
is
\deg_{H}(S):=|N_{H}(S)|
. The
minimum
\ell
-degree
of
H
is
\delta_{\ell}(H)=\min\{\deg_{H}(S)\colon S\in\binom{V(H)}{\ell}\}
. We also refer to the minimum
(k-1)
-degree
\delta_{k-1}(H)
as the
minimum codegree
of
H
. Sometimes we wish to consider the minimum degree over just those
(k-1)
-sets which are contained in at least one edge of
H
, for which we define the
minimum positive codegree
of
H
to be

\delta_{k-1}^{+}(H):=\min\left\{\deg_{H}(S)\colon S\in\binom{V}{k-1},\deg_{H}(S)>0\right\}.

For a set of vertices
U\subseteq V(H)
we write
H[U]
to denote the subgraph induced by
U
, which has vertex set
U
and edge set
\{e\in E(H):e\subseteq U\}
. For
k
-graphs
G
and
H
we write
H\setminus G
to denote the subgraph obtained by deleting
V(G)
from
H
and
H-G
to denote the subgraph obtained by deleting
E(G)
from
E(H)
.
For a vertex set
U\subseteq V(H)
we define
H\setminus U:=H[V(H)\setminus U]
.
For vertex sets
S
and
W
, we write
N_{H}(S,W)=N_{H}(S)\cap\binom{W}{k-|S|}
and
\deg_{H}(S,W)=|N_{H}(S,W)|
.
For a vertex
v\in V(H)
, let
L(v)
denote the
link graph of
v
in
H
, which is a
(k-1)
-graph with vertex set
V(H)\setminus\{v\}
and
E(L(v))=N_{H}(v)
. For an edge
e\in E(H)
we write
\partial e:=\binom{e}{k-1}
, and we write
\partial H
to denote the
(k-1)
-graph on
V(H)
with edge set
\bigcup_{e\in E(H)}\partial e
.

Now let
H
be a
3
-graph on vertex set
V
, and let
W_{1}=x_{1}\dots x_{\ell}
and
W_{2}=y_{1}\dots y_{m}
be tight walks in
H
. If
x_{\ell-1}=y_{1}
and
x_{\ell}=y_{2}
, then we define
W_{1}W_{2}:=x_{1}\dots x_{\ell}y_{3}y_{4}\dots y_{m}
. Observe that
W_{1}W_{2}
is a tight walk in
H
. Furthermore, if
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are tight paths in
H
and
V(W_{1})\cap V(W_{2})\setminus\{y_{1},y_{2}\}=\emptyset
then
W_{1}W_{2}
is also a tight path in
H
. Similarly, if
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are tight paths in
H
and we also have
y_{m-1}=x_{1}
and
y_{m}=x_{2}
and
V(W_{1})\cap V(W_{2})\setminus\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\}=\emptyset
, then
W_{1}W_{2}
is a tight cycle in
H
.

For
k,t\in\mathbb{N}
we write
K_{t}^{k}
for the complete
k
-graph on
t
vertices, which has vertex set
[t]
and whose edges are all sets
e\in\binom{[t]}{k}
.

2.1
Extremal construction

The following slight modification of a construction given by Pikhurko
[
22
]
demonstrates that, if true, Conjecture
1.2
would be best-possible up to the
\alpha n
error term.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Let
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
and
V_{4}
be pairwise-disjoint sets of vertices with
\sum_{i\in[4]}|V_{i}|=n
and
\lfloor n/4\rfloor\leq|V_{i}|\leq\lceil n/4\rceil
for all
i\in[4]
. Let
H
be the
3
-graph with vertex set
V:=\bigcup_{i\in[4]}V_{i}
whose edges are all triples
e\in\binom{V}{3}
which satisfy one of the following mutually exclusive properties.

(i)

|e\cap V_{1}|=2
,

(ii)

|e\cap V_{1}|=|e\cap V_{i}|=|e\cap V_{j}|=1
for distinct
i,j\in[4]\setminus\{1\}
,

(iii)

|e\cap V_{i}|=3
for some
i\in[4]\setminus\{1\}
, or

(iv)

|e\cap V_{i}|=1
and
|e\cap V_{j}|=2
for distinct
i,j\in[4]\setminus\{1\}
.

For
xy\in\binom{V}{2}
with
x\in V_{i}
and
y\in V_{j}
, we then have

\displaystyle N(xy)=\begin{cases}V\setminus(V_{1}\cup\{x,y\})&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
V\setminus(V_{j}\cup\{x\})&\text{if $i=1\neq j$,}\\
V\setminus(V_{k}\cup\{x,y\})&\text{if $\{i,j,k\}=[4]\setminus\{1\}$}.\end{cases}

Hence
\delta_{2}(H)=\lfloor 3n/4\rfloor-2
.

Every copy
K
of
K_{4}^{3}
in
H
intersects
V_{1}
in exactly
0
or
2
vertices. Indeed, there are no edges of
H
in
V_{1}
, so
|K\cap V_{1}|\leq 2
. If
|K\cap V_{1}|=1
, then
e:=V(K)\setminus V_{1}
is an edge of
H
, so our choice of
E(H)
implies that we cannot have
|e\cap V_{i}|=1
for each
i\in[4]\setminus\{1\}
. We must therefore have
|e\cap V_{i}|\geq 2
for some
i\in[4]\setminus\{1\}
, and so
K
contains an edge of
H
with two vertices in
V_{i}
and one in
V_{1}
, but again this is not possible by our choice of edges.

Let
\mathcal{C}=v_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{\ell}
be the square of a tight cycle in
H
. Note that each set of four consecutive vertices of
\mathcal{C}
forms a copy of
K_{4}^{3}
, and so has either
0
or
2
vertices in
V_{1}
. It follows that either every four consecutive vertices of
\mathcal{C}
have no vertices in
V_{1}
, or every four consecutive vertices of
\mathcal{C}
have precisely two vertices in
V_{1}
. In the former case we have
\ell\leq|V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4}|\leq\lceil 3n/4\rceil
, and in the latter case we have
\ell\leq 2|V_{1}|\leq 2\lceil n/4\rceil
. This proves the proposition.∎

2.2
Turán density

Given a
k
-graph
F
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
Turán number

\ex(n,F)
is the maximum number of edges that an
n
-vertex
k
-graph can have without having
F
as a subgraph. The asymptotics of this quantity are more interesting, hence the
Turán density

\pi(F)
of
F
is defined as

\pi(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ex(n,F)}{\binom{n}{k}}.

It is not hard to see that this limit exists for every
k
-graph
F
.

Several variants of Turán density have been considered. Mubayi and Zhao
[
20
]
introduced the
codegree Turán number

\ex_{\mathrm{co}}(n,F)
as the maximum
d
such that there exists an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with minimum codegree
d
which does not have
F
as a subgraph. They defined the
codegree Turán density
of
F
as

\gamma(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ex_{\mathrm{co}}(n,F)}{n}

and proved that this limit exists for every
k
-graph
F
.

Given a
k
-graph
F
and a positive integer
t
, we write
F(t)
to denote the
t
-blowup of
F
, which is the
k
-graph formed as follows. For each vertex
v\in V(F)
let
U_{v}
be a set of
t
vertices, with the sets
U_{v}
being pairwise vertex-disjoint. The vertex set of
F(t)
is
V:=\bigcup_{v\in V(F)}U_{v}
, and a set
e\in\binom{V}{k}
is an edge of
F(t)
if and only if there exists an edge
f\in F
with
|e\cap U_{v}|=1
for each
v\in f
.

We use the following result on supersaturation and the Turán density and codegree Turán density of blowups. Supersaturation for Turán density was discovered by Erdős and Simonovits
[
5
]
, while for codegree Turán density it was shown by Mubayi and Zhao
[
20
]
. The following statement can be deduced by combining results from Keevash’s survey
[
13
]
with those of Mubayi and Zhao
[
20
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(cf.
[
13
, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2]
,
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
)
.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll\alpha,1/k,1/m,1/t
. Let
F
be a
k
-graph on
m
vertices and
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
|E(G)|\geq(\pi(F)+\alpha)\binom{n}{k}
or
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq(\gamma(F)+\alpha)n
. Then
G
contains at least
\beta\binom{n}{tm}
copies of
F(t)
.

We also use the following result by Balogh, Clemen and Lidický
[
3
]
which provides an upper bound on the codegree Turán density of
K_{5}^{3}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.2, Table 1]
)
.

\gamma(K_{5}^{3})\leq 0.74
.

2.3
Probabilistic tools

Our arguments make use of the following standard probabilistic inequalities. For a positive integer
n
and
p\in[0,1]
, the
binomial distribution

\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
is the number of successes from
n
independent trials, where the probability of success in each trial is
p
. Now let
N,n,m
be positive integers with
n,m\leq N
, and fix a set
T
of size
N
and a subset
S\subseteq T
of size
|S|=m
. The
hypergeometric distribution

{\mathrm{Hyp}}(N,n,m)
is the distribution of the random variable
X=|S\cap R|
, where
R\subseteq T
of size
|R|=n
is selected uniformly at random among all subsets of
T
with
n
elements.

Lemma 2.3

(Markov’s inequality cf.
[
12
, Equation 1.3]
)
.

If
t>0
and
X
is a non-negative random variable, then
\mathbb{P}[X\geq t]\leq{\mathbb{E}X}/{t}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Chernoff’s inequality cf.
[
12
, Remark 2.5]
)
.

If
0<\varepsilon\leq 3/2
and
X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
, then
\mathbb{P}(|X-\mathbb{E}X|\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}X)\leq 2e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}\mathbb{E}X}.

Lemma 2.5

(Hoeffding’s inequality cf.
[
12
, Theorem 2.10]
)
.

If
0<\varepsilon\leq 3/2
and
X\sim{\mathrm{Hyp}}(N,n,m)
, then
\mathbb{P}[|X-\mathbb{E}X|\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}X]\leq 2e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}\mathbb{E}X}
.

3
Tight Connectivity

In this section we prove Lemma
1.4
, which states that if
H
is a 3-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
then the tetrahedral 4-graph
\mathcal{T}(H)
of
H
is tightly connected.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be the set of distinct tight components of
\mathcal{T}(H)
. Recall that these components
T\in\mathscr{T}
partition the set of copies of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
.
Throughout this section the 3-graphs
H
we work with will have the property that every edge of
H
is contained in at least one copy of
K_{4}^{3}
.
(Observe that having minimum positive codegree
\delta_{2}^{+}(H)>2n/3
is a sufficient condition for this.)
Moreover, recalling that the tight components
T\in\mathscr{T}
partition the set of copies of
K^{3}_{4}
in
H
, we observe that all of the copies of
K^{3}_{4}
which contain a given edge
e\in E(H)
must be contained in the same tight component
T\in\mathscr{T}
. So for each edge
e\in E(H)
there is precisely one tight component
T\in\mathscr{T}
which contains the copies of
K^{3}_{4}
containing
e
(of which there is at least one); we denote this tight component by
\phi(e)
, giving an edge-colouring
\phi:E(H)\to\mathscr{T}
of
H
(where the colours are the tight components in
\mathscr{T}
).
We identify each tight component
T\in\mathscr{T}
with the
3
-graph on vertex set
V(H)
and whose edges are all edges
e\in E(H)
with
\phi(e)=T
; in this way we may discuss
N_{T}(S)
,
N_{\partial T}(S)
,
\deg_{T}(S)
and
\deg_{\partial T}(S)
, as defined earlier.

For each
v\in V(H)
, we write
\phi_{v}(uw):=\phi(uvw)
.
So
\phi_{v}
is an edge-colouring of
L(v)
(the link graph of
v
).
Also, for each
S\subseteq V(H)
with
|S|\leq 2
, let
\phi(S)=\{\phi(e)\colon e\in E(H)\text{ and }S\subseteq e\}
, so
\phi(S)
is the set of colours of edges which contain
S
as a subset (this set may contain multiple elements). If
T\in\phi(S)
, then we say that
S

is in tight component

T
.

Since two copies of
K^{3}_{4}
which share three vertices must lie in the same tight component, we have the following fact.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph.
Suppose that
e,e^{\prime}\in E(H)
have
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=2
and
\bigcap_{P\in\partial e\cup\partial e^{\prime}}N_{H}(P)\neq\emptyset
.
Then
e
and
e^{\prime}
are in the same tight component of
\mathcal{T}(H)
.

Our definition of
\phi(S)
ensures that
\phi(uv)\subseteq\phi(u)\cap\phi(v)
and
\phi(uvw)\in\phi(uv)\cap\phi(uw)\cap\phi(vw)
. The following statement follows immediately, since if
\delta_{2}(H)>0
then for each pair
u,v
of distinct vertices of
H
there is an edge of
H
containing both
u
and
v
.

Fact 3.2
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>0
in which each edge is contained in a copy of
K^{3}_{4}
.
Then for all
u,v\in V(H)
we have
\phi(u)\cap\phi(v)\neq\emptyset
.
In particular, if there exists
u\in V(H)
satisfying
|\phi(u)|=1
, then
\phi(u)\subseteq\phi(v)
for all
v\in V(H)
.

The next fact gives a lower bound on the number of vertices covered by each tight component, and holds because for each pair
a,b
of distinct vertices of an edge
e
there are at most
n-\delta^{+}_{2}(H)
vertices
u\in V(H)
for which
abu
is not an edge of
H
, and so at least
n-3(n-\delta^{+}_{2}(H))
vertices of
H
form an edge with all three pairs of vertices of
e
.
Recall that
\delta_{2}^{+}(H)
is the minimum positive codegree of
H
, and (for the second part of the statement) that all the copies of
K^{3}_{4}
extending a given edge lie in the same
T\in\mathscr{T}
.

Fact 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}^{+}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
. Then every edge
e\in E(H)
has
\deg_{\mathcal{T}(H)}(e)\geq(1/3+3\alpha)n
.
If additionally
\delta_{2}(H)>0
, then for all
xy\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
and
T\in\phi(xy)
we have
|V(T)|\geq\deg_{T}(xy)\geq(1/3+3\alpha)n
.

Our next proposition asserts that if
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at least two tight components, then some pair of vertices of
H
is in at least two of these tight components.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
for which
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at least two tight components.
Then there exists
xy\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
such that
|\phi(xy)|\geq 2
.
Moreover, for all
xy\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
, all
T_{1},T_{2}\in\phi(xy)
and all
z\in N_{T_{1}}(xy)
, there exists
w_{z}\in V(H)\setminus\{x,y,z\}
with
w_{z}xy,w_{z}yz\in E(H)
and
\phi(w_{z}xy)=T_{2}
.

Proof.

By Fact
3.2
there must exist
x\in V(H)
with
|\phi(x)|\geq 2
, as otherwise by choosing vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
with
\phi(u_{1})\neq\phi(u_{2})
we would have
\phi(u_{1})\cap\phi(u_{2})=\emptyset
. Fix such an
x
and distinct tight components
T_{1},T_{2}\in\phi(x)
; we may then choose
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(H)\setminus\{x\}
with
T_{1}\in\phi(xv_{1})
and
T_{2}\in\phi(xv_{2})
.
If
|\phi(xv_{i})|\geq 2
for some
i\in[2]
, then we may take
y=v_{i}
; otherwise, we may choose
y\in N(xv_{1})\cap N(xv_{2})
.

For the second part of the statement, let
xy\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
have
T_{1},T_{2}\in\phi(xy)
and choose
z\in N_{T_{1}}(xy)
.
By Fact
3.3
we have
\deg_{T_{2}}(xy)>n/3
, so we may choose
w_{z}\in N_{H}(yz)\cap N_{T_{2}}(xy)
.
∎

Our next proposition shows that in any collection of nine pairs of vertices of
H
there are some eight pairs which share a common neighbour. Similarly, given a nominated pair of vertices of
H
and a further seven pairs, we can find seven pairs including the nominated pair which share a common neighbour. For notational simplicity, for a set
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{2}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, we write
\mathcal{P}\setminus P
to mean
\mathcal{P}\setminus\{P\}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
.

(i)

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{2}
have
|\mathcal{P}|\leq 9
. Then some
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
has
\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P^{\prime}}N_{H}(P)\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{2}
have
|\mathcal{P}|\leq 8
and fix
P\in\mathcal{P}
. Then some
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P
has
\bigcap_{P^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P^{\prime}}N_{H}(P^{\prime\prime})\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

For
(i)
, note that
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\deg_{H}(P)>|\mathcal{P}|(7n/9)\geq(|\mathcal{P}|-2)n
. Since
H
has
n
vertices, by averaging some
x\in V(H)
must be contained in
N_{H}(P)
for at least
|\mathcal{P}|-1
of the pairs
P\in\mathcal{P}
.

For
(ii)
, fix
W\subseteq N_{H}(P)
with
|W|=7n/9
, and observe that

\sum_{P^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P}|N_{H}(P^{\prime\prime})\cap W|>(|\mathcal{P}|-1)(5n/9)\geq(|\mathcal{P}|-3)7n/9=(|\mathcal{P}|-3)|W|.

By averaging we deduce that some vertex
x\in W
is contained in
N_{H}(P^{\prime\prime})\cap W
for at least
|\mathcal{P}|-2
of the pairs
P^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P
.
∎

Our proof of Lemma
1.4
proceeds through three key steps. First we show that every vertex of
H
is in at most two tight components of
\mathcal{T}(H)
in Section
3.1
. We then show that
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at most two tight components in Section
3.2
. Finally, we show that in fact
\mathcal{T}(H)
has only one tight component in Section
3.3
.

3.1
Every vertex is in at most two tight components

The aim of this section is to prove Lemma
3.7
, which states that every vertex of
H
is in at most two tight components of
\mathcal{T}(H)
.
We proceed by studying the properties of the edge-coloured link graph of a vertex in the following lemma, leading to the conclusion that each pair of distinct vertices is in at most two tight components.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
. Then for all
v\in V(H)
the following statements hold for the edge colouring
\phi_{v}
of
L(v)
.

(i)

There is no properly coloured copy of
C_{4}
in
L(v)
.

(ii)

There is no copy of
C_{4}
in
L(v)
with
3
colours.

(iii)

There is no copy of
P_{4}
in
L(v)
with
3
colours.

(iv)

For each
u\in V(H)\setminus\{v\}
we have
|\phi(uv)|\leq 2
.

Proof.

For
(i)
and
(ii)
, observe that if a copy of
C_{4}
in
L(v)
is properly coloured or has three colours, then we may write its vertices as
u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}
in such a way that
\phi_{v}(u_{i-1}u_{i})\neq\phi_{v}(u_{i}u_{i+1})
for each
i\in[3]
.
Let
\mathcal{P}=\{vu_{i}\colon i\in[4]\}\cup\{u_{i}u_{i+1}\colon i\in[4]\}
, with addition in the index performed modulo 4, so
|\mathcal{P}|=8
. By Proposition
3.5
(ii)
with
vu_{2}
playing the role of
P
, we deduce that there exists a pair
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{vu_{2}\}
and a vertex
x
such that
x\in\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P^{\prime}}N_{H}(P)
. It follows that for some
i\in[3]
we have

x\in\bigcap_{P\in\partial vu_{i-1}u_{i}\cup\partial vu_{i}u_{i+1}}N_{H}(P),

so
\phi_{v}(u_{i-1}u_{i})=\phi_{v}(u_{i}u_{i+1})
by Fact
3.1
, giving a contradiction.

For
(iii)
, suppose for a contradiction that
wxyz
is a path on
4
vertices in
L(v)
with
3
colours.
By Fact
3.3
we have
\deg_{\phi(vyz)}(vy)>n/3
. So we may choose a vertex
u\in N_{\phi(vyz)}(vy)\cap N_{H}(vw)
, and then
uwxy
forms a copy of
C_{4}
in
L(v)
with at least
3
colours, contradicting
(ii)
.

For
(iv)
, suppose for a contradiction that some
v\in V(H)\setminus\{v\}
has
|\phi(uv)|\geq 3
. Choose distinct tight components
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\in\phi(uv)
and vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
and
v_{3}
with
uvv_{i}\in T_{i}
for each
i\in[3]
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{uv,uv_{i},vv_{i}\colon i\in[3]\}
, so
|\mathcal{P}|=7
. By Proposition
3.5
(ii)
with
uv
playing the role of
P
, we obtain
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{uv\}
for which
\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P^{\prime}}N_{H}(P)\neq\emptyset
. It follows that there exist distinct
i,j\in[3]
such that
\bigcap_{P\in\partial uvv_{i}\cup\partial uvv_{j}}N_{H}(P)\neq\emptyset
. By Fact
3.1
we then have
T_{i}=\phi(uvv_{i})=\phi(uvv_{j})=T_{j}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
. Then every vertex is in at most two tight components of
\mathcal{T}(H)
, that is,
|\phi(v)|\leq 2
for all
v\in V(H)
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that a vertex
v\in V(H)
is in three tight components
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
of
\mathcal{T}(H)
. For each
i\in[3]
we have
\deg_{\partial T_{i}}(v)>n/3
by Fact
3.3
, so we must have

N_{\partial T_{i}}(v)\cap N_{\partial T_{j}}(v)\neq\emptyset
for some distinct
i,j\in[3]
. Assume without loss of generality that
N_{\partial T_{1}}(v)\cap N_{\partial T_{2}}(v)\neq\emptyset
and fix
u\in N_{\partial T_{1}}(v)\cap N_{\partial T_{2}}(v)
and
w\in N_{H}(uv)\cap N_{\partial T_{3}}(v)
.

By Lemma
3.6
(iv)
we have
\phi_{v}(uw)=\phi(uvw)\in\phi(uv)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
; assume without loss of generality that
\phi_{v}(uw)=T_{1}
.
Let
u^{\prime}\in N_{T_{2}}(uv)
and
w^{\prime}\in N_{T_{3}}(vw)\setminus\{u^{\prime}\}
. Then
u^{\prime}uww^{\prime}
is a copy of
P_{4}
in
L(v)
with
\phi_{v}(u^{\prime}u)=T_{2}
,
\phi_{v}(uw)=T_{1}
and
\phi_{v}(ww^{\prime})=T_{3}
, a contradiction to Lemma
3.6
(iii)
.
∎

3.2
There are at most two tight components

The aim of this section is to prove Lemma
3.9
, which states that
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at most two tight components. For this we will use the following proposition asserting that
H
cannot contain 3 vertices which each have a different set of two out of three colours.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
and
\mathscr{T}^{*}=\{T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\}
be a set of three distinct tight components of
\mathcal{T}(H)
. Then there do not exist three vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
of
H
such that
\phi(v_{i})=\mathscr{T}^{*}\setminus\{T_{i}\}
for each
i\in[3]
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that there exist
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V(H)
such that
\phi(v_{i})=\mathscr{T}^{*}\setminus\{T_{i}\}
for each
i\in[3]
. Choose
u\in N_{H}(v_{1}v_{2})\cap N_{H}(v_{2}v_{3})\cap N_{H}(v_{1}v_{3})
, which exists since

|N_{H}(v_{1}v_{2})\cap N_{H}(v_{2}v_{3})\cap N_{H}(v_{1}v_{3})|\geq 3\delta_{2}(H)-2n>0.

For all distinct
i,j\in[3]
we have
\phi(uv_{i}v_{j})\in\phi(v_{i}v_{j})\subseteq\phi(v_{i})\cap\phi(v_{j})=\mathscr{T}^{*}\setminus\{T_{i},T_{j}\}
, so
\mathscr{T}^{*}\subseteq\phi(u)
, contradicting Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)>7n/9
. Then
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at most two tight components.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{T}(H)
has at least
3
distinct tight components.
By Proposition
3.4
and Lemma
3.6
(iv)
, there exists
xy\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
such that
|\phi(xy)|=2
. Let
\phi(xy)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
, so
\phi(x)=\phi(y)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
by Lemma
3.7
. Let
T_{3}
be a tight component in
\mathcal{T}(H)
distinct from
T_{1}
or
T_{2}
. By Fact
3.3
we have
|V(T_{3})|>n/3
, so we may choose
z\in N_{H}(xy)\cap V(T_{3})
. Since
\phi(xyz)\in\phi(xy)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
, we assume without loss of generality that
\phi(xyz)=T_{1}
, so
\phi(z)=\{T_{1},T_{3}\}
by Lemma
3.7
. Also by Fact
3.3
we have
\deg_{\partial T_{3}}(z)>n/3
, so we may choose
w\in N_{\partial T_{3}}(z)\cap N_{H}(yz)
, and then we have
\phi(wyz)\in\phi(yz)=\phi(y)\cap\phi(z)=\{T_{1}\}
. Since
T_{3}\in\phi(wz)
and
T_{2}\in\phi(xy)
, we may choose
u,v\in V(H)\setminus\{x,y,z,w\}
for which
\phi(uwz)=T_{3}
and
\phi(vxy)=T_{2}
. This gives the structure shown in Figure
1
.

Edges and components in Lemma
3.9

Let
\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{e\in\{vxy,xyz,wyz,uwz\}}\partial e
, so
|\mathcal{P}|=9
. Since
\phi(xyz)\neq\phi(vxy)
and
\phi(uwz)\neq\phi(wyz)
, by Fact
3.1
we have

\bigcap_{P\in\partial xyz\cup\partial vxy}N_{H}(P)=\emptyset\mbox{ and }\bigcap_{P\in\partial wyz\cup\partial uwz}N_{H}(P)=\emptyset.

On the other hand, by Proposition
3.5
(i)
there exists
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
with
\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal{P}\setminus P^{\prime}}N_{H}(P)\neq\emptyset
. So we must have

, and therefore may choose
p\in\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{yz\}}N_{H}(P)
. Since
puwz
and
pvxy
are edges of
\mathcal{T}(H)
we have
\phi(puw)=\phi(uwz)=T_{3}
and
\phi(pvx)=\phi(vxy)=T_{2}
, so
\phi(p)=\{T_{2},T_{3}\}
by Lemma
3.7
. So
\phi(x)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\},\ \phi(p)=\{T_{2},T_{3}\}
and
\phi(z)=\{T_{1},T_{3}\}
, contradicting Proposition
3.8
.

3.3
There is only one tight component

In this section we complete the proof of Lemma
1.4
. By Lemma
3.9
we know that
H
has only two tight components, which we call red and blue (denoted
r
and
b
), so each edge of
H
is coloured either red or blue. We now give several results describing certain colour patterns that cannot occur in

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

be a
3
-graph on

, and let

3

Suppose for a contradiction that

\partial G

there exists

.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

is in three red edges, in which case we may assume without loss of generality we are in Case 1 below, or

Case

\phi(xy_{1}y_{2})=\phi(xy_{1}y_{3})=\phi(xy_{2}y_{3})=r

.

Case

:

\phi(xy_{1}y_{2})=\phi(xy_{2}y_{3})=\phi(zy_{1}y_{3})=r

(i)

:

Observe that no pair

4
Hypergraph Regularity

In this section, we formulate the notion of hypergraph regularity that we use, closely following the formulation from Allen, Böttcher, Cooley and Mycroft
[
1
]
.
Recall that a
hypergraph

\mathcal{H}
is an ordered pair
(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H}))
, where
E(\mathcal{H})\subseteq 2^{V(\mathcal{H})}
.
We identify the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with its edge set
E(\mathcal{H})
.
A subgraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph with
V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
and
E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})
.
It is
spanning
if
V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})=V(\mathcal{H})
.
For
U\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, we define
\mathcal{H}[U]
to be the subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
with
V(\mathcal{H}[U])=U
and
E(\mathcal{H}[U])=\{e\in E(\mathcal{H})\colon e\subseteq U\}
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is called a
complex
if
\mathcal{H}
is down-closed, that is if for an edge
e\in\mathcal{H}
and
f\subseteq e
, then
f\in\mathcal{H}
.
A
k
-complex
is a complex having only edges of size at most
k
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}
the spanning subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
containing only the edges of size
i
. Given a
k
-complex
\mathcal{H}
and a set of vertices
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, the subcomplex induced on
S
is obtained by taking all edges of
\mathcal{H}
induced on
S
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of
V(\mathcal{H})
into vertex classes
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}
. Then we say that a set
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is
\mathcal{P}
-partite
if
\left\lvert S\cap V_{i}\right\rvert\leq 1
for all
i\in[s]
.
For
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{V_{i_{1}},\dots,V_{i_{r}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, we define the subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
induced by
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{P^{\prime}}]
or
\mathcal{H}[V_{i_{1}},\dots,V_{i_{r}}]
, to be the subgraph of
\mathcal{H}[\bigcup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}]
containing only the edges that are
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-partite.
The hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
\mathcal{P}
-partite if all of its edges are
\mathcal{P}
-partite. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is
s
-partite
if it is
\mathcal{P}
-partite for some partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V(\mathcal{H})
into
s
parts.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
\mathcal{P}
-partite hypergraph.
If
X
is a
k
-set of vertex classes of
\mathcal{H}
, then we write
\mathcal{H}_{X}
for the
k
-partite subgraph of
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
induced by
\bigcup X
, whose vertex classes are the elements of
X
.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{X^{<}}
the
k
-partite hypergraph with

.
In particular, if

is a complex, then

-complex because

is a set of size

.

Let

be a partition of a vertex set

and

-partite

-partite

-graph

. We denote by

the

-partite

whose edges are all

-partite

which are supported on

. Now we define the

with respect to

to be

if

and

is the proportion of

.
We say that

(d_{i},\varepsilon,r)
-regular with respect to

, if we have

-set
\mathbf{Q}
of subgraphs of

with

.
We say that

is
(\varepsilon,r)
-regular with respect to
H_{i-1}
if there exists some

for which

is
(d_{i},\varepsilon,r)
-regular with respect to
H_{i-1}
.
Finally, given an
i
-graph

whose vertex set contains that of

, we say that

(d_{i},\varepsilon,r)
-regular with respect to
H_{i-1}
if the

-partite subgraph of

is
(d_{i},\varepsilon,r)
-regular with respect to

.
We refer to the density of this

-partite subgraph of

with respect to

as the

with respect to

.

Now let

and let

be an
s
-partite
k
-complex on vertex classes

. For any set

, we write

for

. Note that, if

in

, which are indeed edges. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is
(d_{k},\dots,d_{2},\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon,r)
-regular
if

(a)

for any

and any

-regular with respect to

-regular with respect to

For
\mathbf{d}=(d_{k},\dots,d_{2})
, we write

-regular to mean
(d_{k},\dots,d_{2},\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon,r)
-regular.

A key property of regular complexes is that the restriction of such a complex to a large subset of its vertex set is also a regular complex, with the same relative densities at each level of the complex, although with somewhat weakened regularity properties. The next lemma states this property formally.

Lemma 4.1

(Regular Restriction Lemma
[
1
, Lemma 28]
)
.

Suppose integers
k,m
and reals
\alpha,\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{k}
, and

are such that

1/m\ll\varepsilon\ll\varepsilon_{k},d_{2},\dots,d_{k-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon_{k}\ll\alpha,1/k\,.

For any
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
and

, set
\mathbf{d}=(d_{k},\dots,d_{2})
and let
\mathcal{G}
be an
s

-complex whose vertex classes

-regular.

We say that a

-complex

(a)

(b)

There exists a

such that, for each

, we have
d_{i}\geq 1/t_{1}
and
1/d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{J}
is
(\mathbf{d},\varepsilon,\varepsilon,1)
-regular.

For any
k
-set

of clusters of
\mathcal{J}
, we denote by
\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{X}
the

-partite
(k-1)
-graph

and call

a

. Given a
(t_{0},t_{1},\varepsilon)
-equitable
(k-1)
-complex
\mathcal{J}
and a

-graph

on

, we say that

is
(\varepsilon_{k},r)
-regular with respect to a

if there exists some

is

-regular with respect to the polyad

.
Moreover, we write

for the relative density of

with respect to

; we may drop either subscript if it is clear from context.

We can now give the crucial definition of a regular slice.

Definition 4.2

(Regular slice)
.

Given
\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{k}>0,r,t_{0},t_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, a
k
-graph
G
, a
(k-1)
-complex
\mathcal{J}
on

for

is

-equitable and

is

-regular with respect to all but at most

of the

-sets of clusters of
\mathcal{J}
, where

is the number of clusters of
\mathcal{J}
.

If we specify the density vector

and the number of clusters

of an equitable complex or a regular slice, then it is not necessary to specify

and

(since the only role of these is to bound

and

\mathcal{J}
is

-equitable, or is a

.

Given a regular slice

for a
k
-graph

, it will be important to know the

for
k
-sets

of clusters of

Definition 4.3

(Weighted reduced
k
-graph)
.

Given a
k
-graph

and a
(t_{0},t_{1},\varepsilon)
-equitable

(k-1)
-complex
\mathcal{J}
on

, we let

be the complete weighted

-graph whose

and where each edge

(in particular, the weight is in

). When

is clear from the context we often simply write

instead of

.

Given a set

of size

\overline{\deg}(S;G)
of

with respect to

Similarly, if

is a weighted
k
-graph with weight function

, then we define

\overline{\deg}(S;G)=\frac{\sum_{e\in E(G)\colon S\subseteq e}d^{*}(e)}{\binom{|V(G)\setminus S|}{k-j}}.

Given a

-graph

and distinct ‘root’ vertices

of

-graph

in

to

in

to

for each

. Then the

This density has a natural probabilistic interpretation: choose uniformly at random an injective map
\psi:V(H)\to V(G)
such that
\psi(x_{j})=v_{j}
for each

. Then

is the probability that
\psi
embeds
H
into
G
.
We now define
\mathcal{H}^{\text{skel}}
to be the
(k-1)
-complex on

generated by the down-closure of

(and all edges containing them) and deleting all the edges of size

. Given a

-equitable

-complex
\mathcal{J}
on

, the

supported by
\mathcal{J}
, written

, is defined as the number of labelled rooted copies of

in

such that every vertex of

lies in a distinct cluster of
\mathcal{J}
and the image of

is in
\mathcal{J}
(note that we do
not
require the edges involving

to be contained in or supported by

and
typically they will not be so). We also define

to be the number of labelled copies of

in
\mathcal{J}
with each vertex of
\mathcal{H}^{\text{skel}}

\mathcal{J}
. Then the

of rooted copies of

Again there is a natural probabilistic interpretation: let
\psi:V(\mathcal{H}^{\text{skel}})\to V(G)

to a map

for each

. Then

is the conditional probability that
\psi^{\prime}
embeds

in

, given that
\psi
embeds
\mathcal{H}^{\text{skel}}
in
\mathcal{J}
with every vertex of
\mathcal{H}^{\text{skel}}

\mathcal{J}
.

for a

5
Connecting Lemma

The main aim of this section is to prove the Connecting Lemma (Lemma
1.5
). A rough sketch of the proof of the Connecting Lemma is as follows. In the proof of Lemma
1.5
, we use the strong Hypergraph Regularity, particularly, the Regular Slice Lemma (Lemma
4.4
) proved by Allen, Böttcher, Cooley and Mycroft
[
1
]
. As a consequence of the Regular Slice Lemma, an appropriate reduced
3
-graph of
H
inherits the minimum codegree condition approximately. In order to convert this to a minimum codegree condition, we use a result (Lemma
5.1
) by Ferber and Kwan
[
8
]
. Once we have a minimum codegree in the reduced
3
-graph, we use Lemma
1.4
to get a squared-tight-walk between any two triples of vertices of the reduced
3
-graph. Note that a tight walk lets us fix the order of the initial triple but not the final one. In order to have a squared-tight-walk that respects the order of both the initial and final triples of vertices, we preserve a
K_{5}^{3}
in the reduced graph. We then use the Extension Lemma (Lemma
4.7
) due to Allen, Böttcher, Cooley and Mycroft to get a squared-tight-path between almost all pairs of ordered vertex-triples supported on the regular slice (see Section
4
for the definition of a regular slice) on a constant number of vertices. Each pair of vertex-triples
(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})
in
H
locally extends to one such pair of ordered vertex-triples supported on the regular slice, avoiding the small forbidden set
X
.

The next result by Ferber and Kwan
[
8
]

provides a way of translating an ‘almost minimum codegree’ condition to an exact minimum codegree condition on a suitable subgraph.

We first find a short squared-tight-walk between any two ordered triples of vertices that are edges of an appropriate reduced graph, respecting the order of both triples.

We first formally define an absorber for our problem. Let
H
be a
3
-graph on vertex set
V
and let
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})\in V^{4}
. A labelled set of
36
vertices
A=\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i},u_{i,j}\colon i\in[4],\text{ }j\in[6]\}
is an
absorber for

\mathbf{v}:=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
if the ordered sequences

(i)

T_{1}(A):=x_{1}\dots x_{4}y_{1}\dots y_{4}z_{1}\dots z_{4}
,

(ii)

T_{2}(A):=x_{1}\dots x_{4}z_{1}\dots z_{4}
,

(iii)

U_{i}(A,\mathbf{v}):=u_{i,1}u_{i,2}u_{i,3}v_{i}u_{i,4}u_{i,5}u_{i,6}
for each
i\in[4]
and

(iv)

U_{i}(A):=u_{i,1}u_{i,2}u_{i,3}y_{i}u_{i,4}u_{i,5}u_{i,6}
for each
i\in[4]

are each squared-tight-paths in
H
. We say that
A
is an
absorber
if it is an absorber for some
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})\in V^{4}
. We also write
f_{A,\mathbf{v}}
for the function which maps
T_{2}(A)
to
T_{1}(A)
and, for each
i\in[4]
, maps
U_{i}(A)
to
U_{i}(A,\mathbf{v})
.

The purpose of this definition is the following. Suppose
A
is an absorber for
\mathbf{v}:=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
, where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
and
v_{4}
are distinct vertices of
H
. Then

\mathcal{P}_{A}:=\{T_{2}(A),U_{1}(A),U_{2}(A),U_{3}(A),U_{4}(A)\}

is a collection of five vertex-disjoint squared-tight-paths with vertex set
V(\mathcal{P}_{A})=A
. Moreover,

\mathcal{Q}_{A,\mathbf{v}}:=\{f_{A,\mathbf{v}}(P)\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{A}\}=\{T_{1}(A),U_{1}(A,\mathbf{v}),U_{2}(A,\mathbf{v}),U_{3}(A,\mathbf{v}),U_{4}(A,\mathbf{v})\}

is a collection of five vertex-disjoint squared-tight-paths with vertex set
V(\mathcal{Q}_{A,\mathbf{v}})=A\cup\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
, and each path in
\mathcal{Q}_{A,\mathbf{v}}
has the same ends as the corresponding path in
\mathcal{P}_{A}
. So the effect of replacing each path
P\in\mathcal{P}_{A}
by
f_{A,\mathbf{v}}(P)
is to ‘absorb’ the vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
and
v_{4}
into the squared-tight-paths of the absorber.

We first show that, for each
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})\in V^{4}
, a constant proportion of all labelled sets of 36 vertices of
H
are absorbers for
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
.
Recall that, for a
3
-graph
F
,
\gamma(F)
is the codegree Turán density of
F
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
1/n\ll c\ll\alpha
, let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
, and let
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
,
v_{3}
and
v_{4}
be vertices of
H
. Then there are at least
cn^{36}
absorbers for
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
in
H
.

Proof.

Let
\beta=(2c)^{1/5}
.
By a greedy argument analogous to Fact
3.3
, we have
\gamma(K_{4}^{3})\leq 2/3
. Since
\delta_{2}(H\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\})>7(n-4)/9
, by Theorem
2.1
, there are at least
\beta n^{12}
copies of
K_{4}^{3}(3)
in
H
avoiding
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
.

Fix a copy of a
K_{4}^{3}(3)
whose vertex classes are
V_{i}=\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}
for each
i\in[4]
. Note that
x_{1}\dots x_{4}y_{1}\dots y_{4}z_{1}\dots z_{4}
and
x_{1}\dots x_{4}z_{1}\dots z_{4}
are both squared-tight-paths in
H
.

Fix
i\in[4]
and consider
L_{i}=L(v_{i})\cap L(y_{i})\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
. Note that
L_{i}
is a
2
-graph on
n-5
vertices with

\delta(L_{i})\geq 2(7/9+\alpha)n-n-3\geq(5/9+\alpha)n.

(6.1)

This implies that for any edge

, we have

|N_{L_{i}}(a)\cap N_{L_{i}}(b)|\geq 2(5/9+\alpha)n-n\geq(1/9+2\alpha)n.

The number of triangles in
L_{i}
is therefore at least

\frac{1}{3}|E(L_{i})|\left(\frac{1}{9}+2\alpha\right)n\geq\frac{5}{81}\binom{n}{3}.

(6.2)

We say an unordered triple of vertices
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
in
L_{i}
is a

if
w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}
is a triangle in
L_{i}
as well as
w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}\in E(H)
. We now show that there is a set of nice triples corresponding to every triangle in
L_{i}
. Let
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
be a triangle in
L_{i}
. Observe that

n\geq\left|\bigcup_{j\in[3]}N_{L_{i}}(a_{j})\right|\geq\sum_{j\in[3]}|N_{L_{i}}(a_{j})|-\sum_{jk\in\binom{[3]}{2}}|N_{L_{i}}(a_{j})\cap N_{L_{i}}(a_{k})|,

implying that

\sum_{jk\in\binom{[3]}{2}}|N_{L_{i}}(a_{j})\cap N_{L_{i}}(a_{k})|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eqn: mindegree of L}}}{{\geq}}3(5/9+\alpha)n-n\geq(2/3+3\alpha)n.

By averaging, we may assume without loss of generality, that
|N_{L_{i}}(a_{1})\cap N_{L_{i}}(a_{2})|\geq(2/9+\alpha)n
. Using the codegree of

we deduce that

|N_{H}(a_{1}a_{2})\cap N_{L_{i}}(a_{1})\cap N_{L_{i}}(a_{2})|\geq(2/9+\alpha)n-(2/9-\alpha)n=2\alpha n.

Therefore, there is a set
B\subseteq V(L_{i})
of at least
2\alpha n
vertices such that for all

, we have
a_{1}a_{2}b
is a triangle in
L_{i}
and
a_{1}a_{2}b\in E(H)
. For each triangle in
L_{i}
, we thus obtain at least
2\alpha n
nice triples. Observe that each nice triple can be obtained from at most
3n
triangles in
L_{i}
. So by (
6.2
) there are at least

\frac{1}{3n}\left(\frac{5}{81}\binom{n}{3}\right)2\alpha n\geq\frac{\alpha n^{3}}{200}

nice triples of vertices in
L_{i}
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be an auxiliary
3
-graph on vertex set
V(H)
whose edges are nice triples of
L_{i}
. Since there is a positive density of edges in
H^{\prime}
and the Turán density of an edge is
0
, by Theorem
2.1
, there are at least
\beta n^{6}

2
-blowups of edges of
H^{\prime}
. Observe that a
2
-blowup of an edge in

is a set of
6

such that
u_{i,1}\dots u_{i,6}
is a squared path in

and furthermore
u_{i,j}u_{i,j+1}u_{i,j+2}\in E(H)
for all

.

By repeating this argument for each

, we obtain at least
\beta^{5}n^{36}
choices for an absorber

for
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
. The only way this could fail to be an absorber is if the

36^{2}n^{35}
many such sets where the vertices are not distinct, we obtain at least
\beta^{5}n^{36}/2=cn^{36}
absorbers for

We say two absorbers
A_{1}
and

are

if the underlying (unlabelled) sets are disjoint, and otherwise that

and
A_{2}

. In the next lemma we use a random selection to obtain a linear-size family

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll\alpha
and let
H
be a
3
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{2}(H)\geq(7/9+\alpha)n
. Then there exists a family
\mathcal{F}
of at most
\beta n
pairwise-disjoint absorbers such that for each
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})\in V(H)^{4}

\beta^{2}n
absorbers for
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
in
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
1/n\ll\beta\ll c\ll\alpha
.
For each
4
-tuple of vertices
\textbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots.v_{4})\in V(H)^{4}
, let
\mathcal{A}(\textbf{v})
denote the set of absorbers for

. Choose a family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of labelled sets of size

by selecting each of the

labelled sets with probability

, independently of all other choices. Then

is a binomial random variable with expectation

\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=p\binom{n}{36}36!\leq\frac{\beta n}{2},

so by Chernoff’s inequality (Lemma

) we have
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq\beta n
with probability at least

. Similarly, for each
\textbf{v}\in V(H)^{4}

is a binomial random variable with

\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{A}(\textbf{v})\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\geq cn^{36}p=\frac{c\beta n}{2}.

So by applying Chernoff’s inequality (Lemma
2.4
) and taking a union bound over all
\textbf{v}\in V(H)^{4}
, with probability at least 3/4 it holds that for every
\textbf{v}\in V(H)^{4}
we have
|\mathcal{A}(\textbf{v})\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\geq c\beta n/4
.

So by Markov’s inequality (Lemma
2.3
), with probability at least

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\text{ contains at most }36^{2}\beta^{2}n\text{ pairs of intersecting absorbers.}

Fix a choice of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
for which each of these three events occurs, and let
\mathcal{F}
be the subfamily of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
formed by deleting from
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
both members of each intersecting pair of absorbers and also each
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
which is not an absorber.
Then
\mathcal{F}
is a family of at most
\beta n
pairwise-disjoint absorbers with the property that
|\mathcal{A}(\textbf{v})\cap\mathcal{F}|\geq c\beta n/4-2(36\beta)^{2}n\geq\beta^{2}n\text{ for each }\textbf{v}\in V(H)^{4}.

∎

By choosing a family of absorbers as in Lemma

Lemma 6.3
.

7
Path Cover Lemma

In this section, we prove the Path Cover Lemma (Lemma
1.7
). For this we make use of the following weakened version of a theorem by Keevash and Mycroft
[
14
]
. For constants
1/n\ll 1/\ell\ll\gamma\ll\beta\ll 1/k
, the full statement of the result assumes instead the weaker edge-containment condition that for each
i\in[k-1]
, each
e\in\mathcal{J}^{(i)}
is contained in at least
(\frac{k-i}{k}-\gamma)n
edges of
\mathcal{J}^{(i+1)}
. The conclusion is then that either
\mathcal{J}^{(k)}
has a matching covering all but at most
\ell
vertices, or that there is a subset
S
of
V(\mathcal{J})
with
|S|=\lfloor jn/k\rfloor
for some
j\in[k-1]
for which at most
\beta n^{k}
edges of
\mathcal{J}^{(k)}
contain more than
j
vertices of
S
. The latter outcome is referred to as a ‘space barrier’, but a straightforward counting argument shows this cannot occur under the stronger edge-containment assumption of the version of the theorem stated below.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

Let
1/n\ll 1/\ell\ll\alpha\ll 1/k
and let
\mathcal{J}
be a
k
-complex on
n
vertices with
\mathcal{J}^{(1)}=\{\{v\}\colon v\in V(\mathcal{J})\}
. If for each
i\in[k-1]
each edge
e\in\mathcal{J}^{(i)}
is contained in at least
(\frac{k-i}{k}+\alpha)n
edges of
\mathcal{J}^{(i+1)}
, then
\mathcal{J}^{(k)}
contains a matching that covers all but at most
\ell
vertices of
\mathcal{J}
.

We will apply Theorem
7.1
with
k=4
in the proof of the following lemma, showing that every
3
-graph
H
on
n
vertices with minimum codegree at least

admits an almost-spanning collection of pairwise vertex-disjoint squared-tight-paths.
