The Andersen-Hoffman Theorem for Equitable Rectangles
Amin Bahmanian, Anna Johnsen-Yu

Abstract
. More than forty years ago, Andersen and Hoffman independently proved that every symmetric Latin rectangle can be extended to a symmetric Latin square with prescribed diagonal entries. We generalize this theorem as follows. Let k ≤ n 2 k\leq n^{2} , and let M M be an n × n n\times n array whose top-left r × r r\times r subarray is filled with symbols from { 1 , 2 , … , k } \{1,2,\ldots,k\} . Suppose that, for each i ∈ { 1 , … , r } i\in\{1,\ldots,r\} and each symbol, the number of occurrences of that symbol in row i i equals its number of occurrences in column i i , and that each remaining diagonal entry is either empty or already contains a symbol from { 1 , … , k } \{1,\ldots,k\} . We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for completing M M so that the resulting array is symmetric off the prescribed r × r r\times r subarray, each symbol occurs a specified total number of times in M M , and, for every symbol, its numbers of occurrences in any two rows (respectively, columns) differ by at most one. Restricted to symmetric arrays, our theorem generalizes results of Cruse (1974), Goldwasser et al. (2015), and Bahmanian and Hilton (2025). It also extends Baranyai’s theorem for complete graphs (1973) by characterizing when a partial coloring of K r K_{r} with a loop on every vertex can be extended to an almost regular coloring of K n K_{n} with a loop on every vertex, where n ≥ r n\geq r .

We introduce one more theorem that is used in the proof of our main result. Let

and

be integer functions on the vertex set of a graph

for

(g,f)
-factor
is defined to be a spanning subgraph

of

such that

for each

to be the neighborhood of

-factor Theorem
[

]

Theorem 2.2

[

A given bipartite graph

has a
(g,f)
-factor if and only if the following holds.

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}f(a)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{a\notin A}\max\left\{g(a)-\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G-A}(a),0\right\}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for }A\subseteq X\cup Y.

Theorem

was later simplified as follows.

Theorem 2.3

[

A given bipartite graph

-factor if and only if the following holds.

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}g(a)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in N_{G}(A)}\min\left\{f(a),\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(aA)\right\}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for }A\subseteq X,A\subseteq Y.

\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)\geq r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}+|M_{\ell}|-d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor&\text{ for }\ell\in[k].\end{cases}

Note that just as (
10
) results in one lower bound and two upper bounds on
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(\ell)
, we have that (
12
) results in one upper bound and two lower bounds on
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)
.

To prove this claim, first suppose that
\Theta\subseteq\Gamma
satisfying (
10

\tilde{\Theta}
be the graph with vertex set

and edge set

for

when

, and
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(x_{i}\ell)\leq 1

\tilde{\Gamma}
. Moreover, for

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Gamma}(x_{i})-\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(x_{i})=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-r-\left(n-r\right)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-n,

and we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Gamma}(\ell)-\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(\ell)=r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|-\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(\ell).

Hence, by (

i\in[r]
and

that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)

\displaystyle\leq r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|-\left(\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-(n-r)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\right)=n\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor-\rho_{\ell}+r\left(\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\right),

that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)

\displaystyle\geq r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|-\left(\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-(n-r)\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil\right)=n\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\rho_{\ell},

and that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)

\displaystyle\geq r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor=r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}+|M_{\ell}|-d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor,

\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\ell)

satisfies (

).

Conversely, suppose that

) exists.

it follows that
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(x_{i}\ell)=0
if

and
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(x_{i}\ell)\leq 1
if

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{\tilde{\Theta}}(x_{i}\ell)\leq\left\lceil\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rfloor\quad\text{ for }i\in[r],\ell\in[k],

and so as

Our main result implies the following result for constructing equitable

Let

\displaystyle\Big|\{\ell\in[k]\ :\ \rho_{\ell}\nequiv d_{\ell}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\}\Big|\leq n-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}d_{\ell},

\displaystyle n-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}d_{\ell}\equiv\Big|\{\ell\in[k]\ :\ \rho_{\ell}\nequiv d_{\ell}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\}\Big|\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2).

When

.

\displaystyle 2r-n\leq|M_{\ell}|-d_{\ell}.
