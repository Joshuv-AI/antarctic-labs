Regularized vacuum stress tensor of a scalar field as the inflaton or dark energy
Xuan Ye

Abstract
We study the regularized vacuum stress tensor of scalar fields in maximally symmetric spacetime and assess the feasibility of driving primordial inflation or current cosmic acceleration by analyzing the existence of solutions to the Friedmann equation. We find that a conformally coupled scalar field with mass of order 10 10 M pl M_{\text{pl}} can be a candidate for both the inflaton and dark energy, suggesting that these two components may have the same quantum origin. In contrast, a minimally coupled scalar field cannot serve as either the inflaton or dark energy regardless of its mass.

2
Point-splitting Regularization for Vacuum Stress Tensor

In this section, we review the point-splitting method for deriving the UV divergent vacuum stress tensor, present the subtraction terms constructed from the adiabatic mode functions, and show how these terms remove the UV divergences
[
43
]
.

2.1
Unregularized vacuum stress tensor

Consider a free scalar field
\phi
propagating in a curved spacetime and obeying the curvature coupled Klein–Gordon equation,

(\Box+m^{2}+\xi R)\phi=0,

(1)

where
\Box\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}
is the d’Alembert operator,
R
is the Ricci scalar,
m
is the mass of the field, and
\xi
is the coupling constant. The field is conformally coupled for
\xi=1/6
and minimally coupled for
\xi=0
.

In a spatially flat Robertson–Walker spacetime with metric

ds^{2}=a(\tau)^{2}(d\tau^{2}-\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}),

the field operator can be expanded in Fourier modes as

\phi(\mathbf{x},\tau)=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left[a_{\mathbf{k}}\phi_{k}(\tau)e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}+a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\phi_{k}^{*}(\tau)e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\right],

(2)

where
\tau
is conformal time, and
a_{\mathbf{k}}
and
a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}
are annihilation and creation operators satisfying the canonical commutation relation

[a_{\mathbf{k}},a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}]=\delta(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
.

In de Sitter space the scale factor is

a(\tau)=-\frac{1}{H\tau},

(3)

where
H
is a constant Hubble rate. Although the inflationary and current accelerating regimes undergo quasi-exponential expansion, they can be roughly described by the scale factor (
3
). Various inflation models have different expansion rates. For specificity, we take
[
1
]

H_{\mathrm{Inf}}\approx 10^{14}\,\mathrm{GeV}\approx 10^{-5}M_{\mathrm{pl}}.

(4)

For the present accelerating expansion, we use
[
2
]

H_{0}\approx 10^{-42}\,\mathrm{GeV}\approx 10^{-61}M_{\mathrm{pl}},

(5)

where
M_{\mathrm{pl}}=G^{-1/2}
is the Planck mass.

Defining
v_{k}\equiv a\phi_{k}
, the mode equation is

v_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left[k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)a^{2}R\right]v_{k}=0,

(6)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to conformal time. The Ricci scalar is given by
R=6a^{\prime\prime}/a^{3}
, which reduces to
R=12H^{2}
in de Sitter space. The positive frequency solution of Eq. (
6
) in de Sitter space is

v_{k}(\tau)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{x}{2k}}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(\nu+\frac{1}{2})}H^{(1)}_{\nu}(x),

(7)

where
H^{(1)}_{\nu}
is the Hankel function of the first kind,

x\equiv-k\tau
, and
\nu\equiv\sqrt{9/4-m^{2}/H^{2}-12\xi}
.
In the high-
k
limit, the solution (
7
) approaches the plane wave form

(2k)^{-1/2}e^{-ik\tau}
, which corresponds to the Bunch–Davies vacuum state

|0\rangle
defined by
a_{\mathbf{k}}|0\rangle=0
.

The unregularized Green’s function in the Bunch–Davies vacuum is
[
43
]

\displaystyle G(\sigma)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle 0|\phi(x^{\mu})\phi(x^{\prime\mu})|0\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-\nu\right)\Gamma\!\left(\nu+\frac{3}{2}\right)\,{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left[\frac{3}{2}+\nu,\frac{3}{2}-\nu,2,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}\right],

where the mode expansion (
2
) has been used.
Here
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the hypergeometric function and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma
function. The variable
\sigma
is defined as
\sigma\equiv(2\tau\tau^{\prime})^{-1}\big[(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}-|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|^{2}\big].

As
\sigma\to 0
, the Green’s function exhibits UV divergences and has the expansion

\displaystyle G(\sigma)\approx\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}+W\ln\epsilon^{2}+X+Y\epsilon^{2}\ln\epsilon^{2}+Z\epsilon^{2}\right),

(9)

where
\epsilon^{2}\equiv\sigma/H^{2}
, and
W,X,Y,Z
are functions of
H
and
m

[
43
]
. The Green’s function at the coincidence limit therefore contains quadratic and logarithmic divergences, as well as finite terms. The counterterms required to subtract these divergences can be constructed from the Fourier transform of the adiabatic power spectrum
[
44
]
.
Subtracting these counterterms from Eq.(
8
) yields a UV and Infrared convergent regularized Green’s function.
Since this paper focuses on the regularized stress tensor, we do not explicitly regularize the Green’s function here.

The unregularized stress tensor of a scalar field in the point-splitting scheme is defined as

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}G(\sigma),

(10)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\Big[\frac{1}{2}(1-2\xi)(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu^{\prime}}+\nabla_{\mu^{\prime}}\nabla_{\nu})-\xi(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}+\nabla_{\mu^{\prime}}\nabla_{\nu^{\prime}})+(2\xi-\tfrac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994ptg_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla^{\sigma^{\prime}}+\xi g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla^{\sigma}+\nabla_{\sigma^{\prime}}\nabla^{\sigma^{\prime}})-\xi G_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}m^{2}g_{\mu\nu}\Big]G(\sigma),

where
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}
is a differential operator and

G_{\mu\nu}
denotes the Einstein tensor.
Here
\nabla_{\mu}
and
\nabla_{\mu^{\prime}}
are covariant derivatives with respect to

x
and
x^{\prime}
, respectively. Substituting (
8
) into (
10
) and taking the coincidence limit

x^{\prime}\to x
, one finds

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{4}},\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}},\ln\epsilon^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}Y\ln\epsilon

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{R}{12\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{W}{\epsilon^{2}}+(Y+\tfrac{1}{2}Z)g_{\mu\nu}.

(11)

The unregularized stress tensor therefore contains quartic, quadratic, and logarithmic divergences, together with finite terms.

2.2
Regularized vacuum stress tensors

The vacuum stress tensor (
11
) exhibits UV divergences in the coincidence limit. To remove these divergences while preserving the covariant conservation of the stress tensor, one introduces counterterms for the Green’s function, denoted by
G_{\text{sub}}(\sigma)
. This function shares the same divergent structure as the unregularized Green’s function (
9
), but differs in its convergent terms. To this end, we employ the adiabatic mode function

\displaystyle v_{k}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Omega_{k}^{(i)}}}\exp\!\left[-i\int^{\tau}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Omega_{k}^{(i)}(\tau^{\prime})\,d\tau^{\prime}\right],

(12)

where
\Omega_{k}^{(i)}
are the effective frequencies of adiabatic order
i
, indicating the number of time derivatives involved. By substituting (
12
) into (
6
) and solving iteratively order by order, one obtains
[
44
]

\displaystyle\Omega_{k}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\equiv\omega=\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}},

(13)

\displaystyle\Omega_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=3\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\frac{1}{\omega}\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}-\frac{m^{2}a^{2}}{4\omega^{3}}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}+\frac{a^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}\Big)+\frac{5m^{4}a^{4}}{8\omega^{5}}\frac{a^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}.

(14)

The adiabatic mode function (
12
) satisfies Eq. (
6
) at each adiabatic order, thereby ensuring the conservation of the adiabatic stress tensor at each order. As the adiabatic order increases, the mode function approaches the exact solution (
7
) and becomes more accurate in the ultraviolet regime. By replacing the exact mode functions with the adiabatic ones in the definition of the Green’s function (
8
), one obtains the 0th and 2nd order subtraction Green’s functions

G(\sigma)^{(0)}_{\text{sub}}=\frac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{m}{H}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2\sigma}}K_{1}\!\left(\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}\right),

(15)

and

\displaystyle G(\sigma)^{(2)}_{\text{sub}}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{m}{H}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2\sigma}}K_{1}\!\left(\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}\right)+(1-6\xi)K_{0}\!\left(\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{4}\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}K_{1}\!\left(\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}\right)+\frac{\sigma}{12}\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}K_{2}\!\left(\frac{m}{H}\sqrt{-2\sigma}\right)\Bigg],

(16)

where
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
, and
K_{2}
are modified Bessel functions of the second kind.

The 0th order regularized stress tensor for a conformally coupled scalar field is
[
43
]

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\text{reg}}

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}G(\sigma)-\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}G^{(0)}_{\text{sub}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=g_{\mu\nu}\frac{m^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\Big[\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}-\nu_{c}\Big)+\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\nu_{c}\Big)+\ln\frac{R}{12m^{2}}\Big],

(17)

where
\nu_{c}\equiv\sqrt{1/4-m^{2}/H^{2}}
. The 2nd order regularized stress tensor for a minimally coupled scalar field is
[
43
]

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\text{reg}}\equiv

\displaystyle\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}G(\sigma)-\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}G^{(2)}_{\text{sub}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{64\pi^{2}}\Bigg[m^{2}\Big(m^{2}-\frac{R}{6}\Big)\Big(\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}-\nu_{m}\Big)+\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\nu_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle+\ln\frac{R}{12m^{2}}\Big)+\frac{m^{2}R}{9}+\frac{R^{2}}{24}\Bigg],

(18)

where
\nu_{m}\equiv\sqrt{9/4-m^{2}/H^{2}}
and
\psi(x)
is the digamma function.

3
Vacuum Stress Tensor for the Conformally Coupled Scalar Field

The regularized stress tensor (
17
) of a conformally coupled scalar field is maximally symmetric in de Sitter space,

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\text{reg}}=g_{\mu\nu}\Lambda_{c},

(19)

where the constant
\Lambda_{c}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{c}\equiv\frac{m^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\Big[\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}-\nu_{c}\Big)+\psi\!\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\nu_{c}\Big)+\ln\frac{H^{2}}{m^{2}}\Big],

(20)

and we have used the scale factor (
3
). As shown in Fig.
1
, the black curve shows that the regularized energy density

\rho_{c}\equiv\Lambda_{c}
is positive definite. Eq. (
20
) therefore represents an effective cosmological constant.

In the small-mass limit
(m/H)^{2}\to 0
, Eq. (
20
) reduces to

\displaystyle\lim_{m^{2}/H^{2}\to 0}\Lambda_{c}\approx\frac{H^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\Big)^{2}\ln\frac{H^{2}}{m^{2}},

(21)

which depends explicitly on both the mass and the Hubble rate. The cosmological constant (
21
) vanishes in the limit
(m/H)\to 0
.

Taking the stress tensor (
19
) as the only source of the Einstein equations, the Friedmann equation reads

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}=\frac{8\pi Ga^{2}}{3}\rho_{c}=\frac{GH^{4}a^{2}}{24\pi}\Big(\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\Big)^{2}\ln\frac{H^{2}}{m^{2}}.

(22)

Substituting the scale factor (
3
) into Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{GH^{2}}=\frac{1}{24\pi}\Big(\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\Big)^{2}\ln\frac{H^{2}}{m^{2}}.

(23)

The positivity of both sides of (
23

m^{2}/H^{2}<1

The right hand side of Eq. (

2.9\times 10^{-4}
at
(m/H)^{2}\approx 0.6
.
However,
this value is far smaller than the left hand side during inflation,
where
1/(GH_{\mathrm{Inf}}^{2})\approx 10^{10}
,
and during the present accelerating expansion,
where
1/(GH_{0}^{2})\approx 10^{122}
.
Eq. (
23
) has no solution in either epoch, as shown in Fig.1 at the low mass regime. Therefore,
a conformally coupled scalar field in the small mass regime

(m/H)^{2}\ll 1

cannot act as the source of either inflation or dark energy.

In the large mass limit
(m/H)^{2}\to\infty
,
Eq. (
20

\displaystyle\lim_{m^{2}/H^{2}\to\infty}\Lambda_{c}\approx\frac{H^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\frac{2}{3}\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}},

Using (

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}=\frac{8\pi Ga^{2}}{3}\rho_{c}=\frac{GH^{4}a^{2}}{36\pi^{2}}\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}},

This means that (

These results indicate that a supermassive conformally coupled scalar field with

Because the field is extremely massive, it cannot be excited into particle states,

5
Summary

In this paper, we examine whether the Friedmann equation in de Sitter space allows consistent solutions during both the inflationary epoch and the present accelerating expansion when the source is a conformally or minimally coupled scalar field. We then assess whether such fields can act as the inflaton or as the present dark energy.

We find that the regularized energy density of a conformally coupled scalar field cannot act as the inflaton or as dark energy in the small mass regime
(m/H)\ll 1
, since such a ratio cannot be realized during either inflation or the current accelerating expansion. In contrast, in the large mass regime with

m\approx 10\,M_{\mathrm{pl}}\approx 10^{20}\,\mathrm{GeV}
, the conclusion changes. In this case, the field mass determined from the Friedmann equation is proportional to
G^{-1/2}
and independent of the Hubble rate. The field thus can act as a candidate for both the inflaton and present dark energy, suggesting that they may share a common quantum origin. Because the field is extremely massive, particle excitations are effectively absent, and it can be probed only through its vacuum effects.

For a minimally coupled scalar field, the regularized energy density is insensitive to the ratio
(m/H)
and leads to a Hubble rate

H\approx M_{\mathrm{pl}}\approx 10^{19}\,\mathrm{GeV}
. This value is very different from both the inflationary Hubble scale

H_{\mathrm{Inf}}\approx 10^{14}\,\mathrm{GeV}
and the present value

H_{0}\approx 10^{-42}\,\mathrm{GeV}
. Consequently, a minimally coupled scalar field cannot serve as either the inflaton or dark energy, regardless of its mass.

6
Discussion and Outlook

The description of inflation adopted here differs from the standard picture.
In conventional approaches, a scalar field is decomposed into a homogeneous
background component and perturbations. The background evolves under a suitable
potential and drives a quasi-exponential expansion, while the perturbations are
quantized and provide the seeds of the cosmic microwave background anisotropies
and large-scale structure. In the present work, we instead treat the scalar as a
single quantum field and ask whether its regularized vacuum stress tensor can
itself support an inflationary de Sitter phase. Within the fixed-background
approximation, the Friedmann equation indicates that a conformally coupled scalar
field can provide such a source. The regularized vacuum stress tensors (
19
) and (
29
) have
been obtained in exact de Sitter space, where the Hubble parameter is constant.
This setting allows the contribution of the vacuum stress tensor to be isolated
in a controlled way. A complete cosmological scenario, however, requires going
beyond this approximation and including the dynamical response of the geometry.
In particular, backreaction effects may lead to a departure from the exact
de Sitter phase and provide a possible mechanism for ending inflation.

Several directions remain open. First, it would be useful to clarify under what
conditions a highly symmetric de Sitter initial state can be dynamically selected
or approximated at very high energy scales. Second, the exit from inflation and
the subsequent reheating stage should be studied, possibly through backreaction
or through couplings between the scalar field and other degrees of freedom that
convert vacuum energy into particle excitations. Third, a phenomenological
analysis is needed to determine whether this framework can reproduce current
observational constraints, including the scalar spectral index and the
tensor-to-scalar ratio
[
6
]
.

For the dark-energy application, the isolated de Sitter analysis should also be
extended. Two issues are particularly relevant. The first is whether a
quasi-de Sitter phase can emerge naturally after the dust-dominated era. The
second is whether the backreaction of the regularized stress tensor of a
conformally coupled scalar field can generate an effective equation of state
that evolves from the phantom regime to the quintessence regime, as suggested by
recent DESI data
[
48
]
.
