Executive Summary
This document is a Record of Decision regarding changes or modifications to the LAMP Conceptual
Design. Specifically, the following change has been reviewed and is approved:
  Switching to a LEBT design requiring a single H- ion source with macropulse-to-macropulse
 current control.
This design change impacts:
  The LAMP LEBT design;
  LAMP MEBT chopper performance requirements; and
  Technology maturation.

This change is assessed as being beneficial to the project for the following reasons: performance,
maintainability, flexibility, and cost.

1 Introduction
The LAMP Conceptual Design (LCD) is described in the LAMP Conceptual Design Report [1]. The
proposed new configuration of the LAMP H- LEBT encompasses multiple changes to the LCD. The new
configuration makes use of a single H- source to supply beam to all LANSCE user stations except IPF; this
eliminates one of the H- sources in the LCD, and the associated pulsed merger magnet the use of two such
sources required. The source current from the sole H- source is gated on a macropulse-by-macropulse basis
using techniques demonstrated at SNS (Spallation Neutron Source), specifically, changes to the RF plasma
drive power. A pulsed electrode system is used to match the beam into the LEBT transport line, variable
on a macropulse-by-macropulse basis to match the source beam current.
Potential disadvantages require running the H- source at higher beam currents for generation of the WNR
beam current, and the requirement of pulsed electrodes to match the variable beam currents into the H-
LEBT line. This latter requirement introduces a new Critical Technology Element. The new CTE has been
assessed at TRL-5, is seen as being relatively straightforward to address, and can be readily incorporated
into CTE maturation planning, e.g. early testing at the SNS test stands.

2 Potential Impacts
This section describes the changes under consideration, and brief assessments of the potential impacts of
those proposed changes.

2.1 Summary of proposed change

The LAMP LEBT would make use of a single H- source, resulting in changes to source operating parameters
and additional requirements on beam focusing. The approach to WNR bunch formation would be changed
from a chopper plus low-frequency buncher, both upstream of the LEBT merge magnet, to a two-chopper
approach with one chopper in the H- LEBT line and one immediately upstream of the RFQ.

2.2 Impact on Key Performance Parameters

No direct impact on KPPs is expected. Operating hour and uptime requirements may prove easier to meet
due to the removal of one of the ion sources requiring regular maintenance.

2.3 Impact on Accelerator Performance

The proposed changes are not expected to have a significant impact on overall performance of the LAMP
accelerator as a whole.

2.4 Impact on Technology Maturation

The proposed change will likely require adding a CTE: the use of pulsed electrodes to provide variable
focusing, on a macropulse-by-macropulse basis, to match the H- source beam at different currents into the
LAMP LEBT.

2.5 Impact on Project Risks

Risk #65 – Kicker Requirement: this risk is eliminated, as a pulsed kicker magnet is no longer required to
merge the beams from two separate H- sources.

Risk #78 – Rack space: the elimination of a second H- source and portions of the LEBT will reduce required
rack space.

Risk #81 – LEBT and Chopper Reuse: the simplified beamline layout and component count reduction from
the LAMP conceptual design is expected to make transport and reuse of the LAT beamline components
more straightforward.

2.6 Impact on Commissioning, Operations and Maintenance

The proposed change adds several commissioning tasks related to the macropulse-by-macropulse beam
current variation, but eliminates others related to the concurrent operation of two separate H- sources and a
large pulsed magnet. Operationally the relative impact should be minimal. In terms of maintenance, the
elimination of one of the H- sources can be expected to overall simplify maintenance requirements and
reduce spares requirements.

2.7 Impact on Major Accelerator Subsystems

The expected impact of the proposed changes on the major subsystems of LAMP on major accelerator
subsystems is discussed below.

2.7.1 Ion Sources

One of the H- ion sources is eliminated. The remaining H- source must run at higher beam currents for
WNR bunch generation than in the conceptual design and be capable of altering output current on a
macropulse-by-macropulse basis.

2.7.2 Low-Energy Beam Transport

The H- LEBT is significantly redesigned and simplified compared to the LAMP conceptual design. The
pulsed merger magnet is removed from the design.

2.7.3 Radiofrequency Quadrupole

No impact.

2.7.4 Medium-Energy Beam Transport

No general impact;

2.7.5 Drift Tube Linac

No impact.

2.7.6 Controls and Diagnostics

Additional controls will be required for the pulse-by-pulse current control and two-chopper WNR beam
formation approach. Controls for a second H- ion source and the pulsed merger magnet are eliminated.
The redesigned LEBT beamline allows for considerably easier diagnostic placement versus the conceptual
design.

2.7.7 DC and Pulsed Power

No significant changes are expected for the LEBT DC power requirements. Two pulsed high-voltage
systems are added, for the two focusing electrodes. Removal of the pulsed merger magnet removes all
associated pulsed power requirements.

2.7.8 RF Systems

Removal of the subharmonic (low-frequency) buncher eliminates the need for its low-level RF control and
RF power source.

2.7.9 Vacuum Systems

The vacuum system in the LEBT is generally simplified by the removal of the 2nd source’s LEBT line; no
changes to specifications are expected. Pumping port and gate valve placement is easier than in the
conceptual design, due to the layout of the redesigned beamline.

2.7.10 Cooling Systems

Generally, no impact.

2.7.11 Protective Systems

A large high-voltage system (an H- ion source) is removed; two smaller high-voltage systems (pulsed
focusing electrodes) are added. A large pulse magnet is removed, eliminating a potential inductive load
hazard.

2.7.12 Infrastructure

There will be increased room in Sector J around the beamline due to the removal of one of the ion sources.

3 Assessment of the Proposed Changes
The proposed changes have been evaluated by a panel consisting of Greg Dale, John Lewellen, Michael
Holloway, Kip Bishofberger, Anna Alexander, Tyler Thornton, and Henry Gaus, with further discussion
and advice from the contributors listed in the Section 7 Acknowledgements.

The evaluation was conducted by assessing the potential impact of the proposed changes across a variety
of factors, using the LAMP Conceptual Design as a baseline for comparison. Comparisons are made
between the conceptual design and the proposed change for each factor, and quantified using a 5-level scale
ranging from -2 (notably worse than the LAMP Conceptual Design) to +2 (notably improved from the
LAMP Conceptual Design). Each factor is assigned a relative weight, or importance; the product of the
scores and weights for each factor are then summed to provide an aggregate score assessing the proposed
change. A positive aggregate score indicates the proposed change is favored over the conceptual design; a
negative score indicates the conceptual design is preferred. A list of the factors, along with description and
weights used in this evaluation, is given below in Table 1.

Scores are averaged over all evaluators to provide a final score for the proposed change.

4 Comments, Concerns, Suggestions
This section presents additional comments, concerns and suggestions from the evaluation panel members
regarding the criteria in the evaluation table.

Mission and Beam Performance

  Emittance is decreased by removing the LFB. The LFB was somewhat of a holdover from the
 current CW system in my opinion. What was working great at 750 kV was problematic even at
 100 kV. Didn’t seem possible at 65 kV, but the current modulated single H- source obviates the
 need for pre-bunching before emitting into the RFQ, hence the improvement in the emittance.

  Removing the LFB improves emittance.

Technical Maturity

  Adds a CTE for the current modulated H- ion source. Based on recent tests at SNS, this is
 believed to be at TRL-5. Test stand at SNS makes this low risk for maturing to TRL-7. But, does
 add a CTE.

  Has no change on the CTEs or range in which to operate equipment.

Integration and Interface Risk

  Interface with the accelerator timing system should be easier.

  Removes the LFB so improves the simplicity. Does add a modulated current source, but at much
 lower frequency (~120 Hz macropulse frequency at the max) so doesn't need to be so tightly
 phase locked with the RF as an LFB would need to be.

  Single source reduces footprint, which reduces likelihood of having to build mezzanines in Sector
 J or managing vibration impacts.

Safety and Authorization Basis Impact

  Having a single H- source should simplify system interlocks.

  These are dependent on energy.

Capital Cost

  Elimination of one of the H- sources may result in not having to move or remove a non-load-
 bearing wall.

  Single H- ion source reduces capital costs compared with 2 in the conceptual design

Lifecycle Cost: Operations and Maintenance

  Single H- ion source reduces the need for critical spares, reduces maintenance, reduces
 installation and commissioning time compared to two H- ion sources.

  Single H- source decreases cost, a third chopper increases cost, assuming the H- source cost >>
 chopper cost.

Schedule Risk

  Single H- ion source reduces the number of long-lead procurements compared with two sources
 and allows the commissioning team to focus on bringing a single H- ion source online. This latter
 point is somewhat offset by the added complexity of the modulated output current, but not by
 enough to outweigh all the other benefits.

  I don't see how removing the second H- source will drive commissioning duration.

  Alignment during installation is noticeably simpler with a single source. Steering two beams into
 one beamline can be challenging. Solenoid-based focusing is simpler to commission than
 quadrupole-based focusing.

Upgrade and Future Flexibility

  Having two H- sources could allow greater flexibility, especially if one of them could be pulsed
 as proposed in this change.

  The adoption of a modulated H- ion source could be much more flexible to meeting future
 mission needs than the conceptual design system with H- ion sources dedicated to WNR and
 Lujan/Prad/UCN.

Operability and Commissioning Complexity

  Operating a single H- ion source will be less work than operating two H- ion sources. Tuning the
 new design may be more complicated with the modulated output current, but this is still less
 complex than phase-locking into a LFB and having to tune a beam with the resulting energy skew
 through a bend (merge) magnet. Also gets rid of the pulsed deflector magnet for the Lujan H-
 source. Small win here for the new design in my opinion.

 pulse RF power and electrodes voltages. I believe we would have solved the space problem with

  A single, straight beamline with axisymmetric focusing will be easy to commission than two

  For the full LANSCE system, reliability will be improved by consolidating down to a single H- ion

5 Conclusions
The proposed change is assessed as being generally beneficial to the Project in all areas considered, with
the exception of technical maturity. The averaged score for technical maturity, -0.14 (unweighted),
represents a modest disadvantage versus the conceptual design. This arises from the adoption of pulsed-
electrode focusing, requiring additional technical maturation. The development path appears
straightforward and tractable with existing technology, and thus does not appear to represent a significant
risk to project success.
The proposed change is approved.
