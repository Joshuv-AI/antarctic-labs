Auditing Frontier Vision-Language Models for Trustworthy Medical VQA: Grounding Failures, Format Collapse, and Domain Adaptation
Xupeng Chen, Binbin Shi, Chenqian Le, Qifu Yin, Lang Lin, Haowei Ni, Ran Gong, Panfeng Li

Abstract
Deploying vision-language models (VLMs) in clinical settings demands auditable behavior under realistic failure conditions, yet the failure landscape of frontier VLMs on specialized medical inputs is poorly characterized. We audit five recent frontier and grounding-aware VLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, o3, GLM-4.5V, Qwen 2.5 VL) on Medical VQA along two trust-relevant axes. Perception : all models localize anatomical and pathological targets poorly—the best model reaches only 0.23 mean IoU and 19.1% Acc@0.5—and exhibit clinically dangerous laterality confusion. Pipeline integration : a self-grounding pipeline, where the same model localizes then answers, degrades VQA accuracy for every model—driven by both inaccurate localization and format-compliance failures under the two-step prompt (parse failure rises to 70%–99% for Gemini and GPT-5 on VQA-RAD). Replacing predicted boxes with ground-truth annotations recovers and improves VQA accuracy, consistent with the failure residing in the perception module rather than in the decomposition itself. These observational findings identify grounding quality as a primary trustworthiness bottleneck in our SLAKE bounding-box setting. As a complementary fine-tuning follow-up, supervised fine-tuning of Qwen 2.5 VL on combined Med-VQA training data attains the highest reported SLAKE open-ended recall (85.5%) among comparable methods, suggesting that the VQA-level gap is tractable with domain adaptation; whether this also closes the perception/trustworthiness bottleneck is left to future work.



1
Introduction

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly proposed as clinical decision-support tools for radiology and pathology, yet deploying them safely requires concrete evidence about
where
and
how
they fail—not only aggregate scores on benchmarks. Medical Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a clean test bed for this auditing question: it demands two equally critical skills—high-level reasoning, at which modern LLMs excel, and low-level perception, namely the precise localization of anatomical structures or pathologies in a complex medical image. A failure of perception can render even the most sophisticated reasoning useless, and produces clinically dangerous errors such as laterality confusion in chest radiographs.

We audit the failure landscape of five frontier VLMs along two trust-relevant axes: (i) the
perception
axis—can a model find the relevant region in a medical image at all?—and (ii) the
pipeline-integration
axis—when grounding and answering are coupled, does the system degrade gracefully or collapse? We further test whether the medical-VQA gap revealed by this audit can be partially closed through targeted supervised fine-tuning of a strong open-source VLM. Our key contributions are:

•

Perception audit.
We construct a dedicated visual grounding benchmark from SLAKE and evaluate five leading VLMs, revealing that all models exhibit remarkably poor medical image localization (best IoU: 0.23) and systematic laterality confusion—a clinically dangerous failure mode.

•

Pipeline-integration audit.
We demonstrate that self-grounding—where the same model localizes then answers—degrades VQA accuracy for all models, with severity tracking grounding quality and, for Gemini and GPT-5, dominated by format-compliance collapse (70%–99% parse failure under the two-step prompt).

•

Oracle-grounding diagnostic.
We show through an oracle GT-grounding experiment that accurate grounding
recovers and improves
VQA performance—supporting the interpretation that the failure resides in the perception module rather than the decomposition—and identifying grounding quality as a primary trustworthiness bottleneck in our SLAKE bounding-box setting.

•

Forward-looking remedy.
We supervised-fine-tune Qwen 2.5 VL on the combined VQA-RAD / SLAKE / PathVQA training sets, reaching the highest reported SLAKE open-ended recall (85.5%) among published baselines we compare against in Table
1
, and matching or exceeding LLaVA-Med-reported numbers on four of six dataset–metric combinations (within
\sim
1 pp on the other two).

3
Experiments

To audit the failure landscape of current leading Vision-Language Models (VLMs) along the two trust-relevant axes (perception and pipeline integration), we evaluate them on two coupled tasks: a standard Medical VQA assessment (the integration axis) and a precise visual grounding assessment (the perception axis). This section details the models, datasets, evaluation metrics, and audit setup used in our study.

3.1
Models

We evaluate five recent VLMs spanning proprietary and open-source frontiers: Gemini 2.5 Pro
(
4
)
, GPT-5
(
18
)
, o3 (OpenAI, accessed 2026), GLM-4.5V
(
6
)
, and Qwen 2.5 VL
(
1
)
. Among the five, GLM-4.5V and Qwen 2.5 VL both explicitly advertise built-in grounding/localization capabilities; we therefore highlight them as the grounding-aware frontier models in our audit.

3.2
Datasets

We use three standard Med-VQA benchmarks, covering both radiology and pathology. Across all three we follow the common convention of categorizing questions as either
closed-ended
(yes/no or single-word, e.g., “are regions of the brain infarcted?”
\to
“yes”) or
open-ended
(descriptive, e.g., “what abnormality is seen?”
\to
“blind-ending loop of bowel arising from the cecum”).

VQA-RAD

(
9
)
contains 315 radiology images and 3,515 clinician-curated QA pairs. We evaluate on the 451-QA test split following the convention adopted by LLaVA-Med
(
10
)
. VQA-RAD does
not
provide bounding-box annotations, so it is excluded from the SLAKE-derived grounding benchmark (Table
2
) and from the GT-Grounding VQA condition; it is, however, included in Direct VQA and Self-Grounding VQA, since those do not require bounding-box ground truth.

SLAKE

(
12
)
is a semantically labeled, knowledge-enhanced radiology dataset with 642 images, over 14,000 QA pairs, and rich spatial annotations (segmentation masks and bounding boxes for organs and pathologies). We use SLAKE
twice
: (i) its 1,061-QA test split for VQA evaluation, and (ii) a grounding split that we programmatically derive from SLAKE’s bounding-box annotations by generating an explicit grounding query (e.g., “Locate the liver.” or “Locate the lung cancer.”) for each annotated target, yielding 418 image–query–bbox triples that span both anatomical structures (e.g., liver, kidneys, spleen) and pathological findings (e.g., lung cancer, cardiomegaly), used for the grounding benchmark and the GT-Grounding VQA condition.

PathVQA

(
7
)
is a pathology Med-VQA dataset derived from medical textbooks, containing 4,998 images and 32,799 QA pairs. We evaluate on its 6,761-QA test split. PathVQA does not provide bounding-box annotations and is used only for the VQA task.

3.3
Evaluation Metrics

VQA.
We report closed-ended accuracy and open-ended recall. Ground-truth answers are routed into the two types by a keyword vocabulary, then matched with a rule-based pipeline tailored to medical language: closed-ended answers are first screened for direct contradictions (e.g., “left” vs. “right”) against a curated dictionary, then matched through a synonym map; open-ended answers use synonym-expanded token overlap with a dynamic threshold (50% for answers
\leq
3 words, 30% otherwise) and a laterality contradiction penalty. Because this matching is heuristic rather than human-validated, we discuss its limitations in Section
5
.

Grounding.
We report the mean Intersection over Union (IoU), the Dice coefficient, and Accuracy@0.5 (the fraction of predictions with IoU
\geq
0.5) between the predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes.

3.4
Experimental Setup

Our evaluation methodology is centered around a two-step pipeline (Figure
1
), applied consistently across all models. Proprietary models are accessed via their official APIs; open-source models are run in a controlled local environment.

1.

Step 1 – Grounding.
The model is prompted as an “expert radiologist AI” to return the bounding box of the object referenced in the question, in a strict
[x1, y1, x2, y2]
format, with the original image width and height included in the prompt. A regular expression extracts the first tuple from the response; unparseable outputs are assigned a sentinel box
[0,0,0,0]
and kept in the final score so that reported numbers reflect real-deployment robustness.

2.

Step 2 – Question Answering.
The model, re-prompted as a “clinical Q&A assistant,” answers the original question using the visual input from Step 1: either the cropped region (if grounding was used) or the full image (otherwise). The prompt asks for brief answers to closed questions and descriptive answers to open ones.

Prompt, parsing, and scoring are identical across all models, where applicable to each condition.

3.5
Supervised Fine-Tuning Setup

To test whether VQA performance can be substantially raised by domain-specific supervised fine-tuning, we fully fine-tune
Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct
on the combined train splits of VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and PathVQA, with no external instruction data. We train all parameters (vision encoder and language model) for
3 epochs
with
effective batch size 32
(per-device batch 4, gradient accumulation 8), AdamW with learning rate
1e-5
, cosine decay, and warmup ratio 0.03. Training runs on a single node with
8
\times
NVIDIA A100 80GB
, taking approximately 8 hours. We evaluate the fine-tuned model on the same test splits used for zero-shot evaluation and report closed-ended accuracy and open-ended recall.

All evaluated VLMs exhibit a stark perceptual gap. Even the best model, Qwen 2.5 VL, reaches only 0.23 mean IoU and 19.1% Acc@0.5—fewer than one in five predicted boxes overlap the ground truth by at least half. GPT-5 and o3 attain near-identical IoU 0.16 (Acc@0.5 7.9–9.1%); Gemini 2.5 Pro trails at IoU 0.09. Notably, GLM-4.5V—one of the two models in our audit that explicitly advertise built-in grounding—underperforms GPT-5 and o3 (IoU 0.13), indicating that general-purpose grounding features do not transfer to the fine-grained spatial demands of medical imaging. Figure
2

We observe two systematic failure modes across models.
(i) Laterality confusion
: models frequently confuse left and right in chest radiographs (which are conventionally mirrored), producing bounding boxes on the anatomically incorrect side—a clinically dangerous error.
(ii) Scale mismatch
: for small targets such as lesions or nodules, models default to large bounding boxes encompassing the entire organ or image quadrant, suggesting an inability to perform fine-grained localization at the pathology level. As Figure
2
(top row) shows, all models fail completely on the small “Lung Cancer” lesion. These patterns indicate that the grounding gap reflects fundamental limitations in spatial reasoning when models must produce precise numeric coordinates rather than natural language.

Format compliance is not the grounding-task bottleneck.
One might worry that low IoU merely reflects failure to emit a parseable bounding box. On the dedicated grounding task only Gemini 2.5 Pro shows substantial parse failure (61/418 samples, 14.6%); GLM-4.5V, GPT-5, and Qwen 2.5 VL achieve 0% and o3 only 0.5%. Restricting Gemini to parseable outputs barely shifts its IoU (0.09
\to
0.11), so the reported scores reflect genuine localization errors rather than formatting artifacts.

Self-grounding degrades VQA.

Counter to our initial hypothesis, self-grounding—where the same model performs both localization and answering—
degrades
VQA performance for all models. The most extreme case is Gemini 2.5 Pro, whose closed-ended accuracy collapses from 39.9% to 0.0% and open-ended recall drops from 25.8% to 0.6%. This catastrophic failure has two compounding causes: not only is Gemini’s grounding accuracy low (IoU 0.09), but its bbox parse failure rate escalates from 14.6% on the dedicated grounding task to 98.9% on VQA-RAD, where the combined grounding-then-answering prompt proves much harder to constrain. GPT-5 shows a similarly dramatic decline (67.6%
\to
19.3% closed, with 70.3% parse failures on VQA-RAD grounding), while GLM-4.5V and Qwen 2.5 VL suffer moderate degradation (e.g., Qwen: 70.6%
\to
65.9% closed on VQA-RAD, with only 0.2% parse failures). This pattern suggests that self-grounding degradation is driven by
both
inaccurate localization and format compliance difficulties under the more complex two-step prompt.

With no model exceeding 0.23 mean IoU, current VLMs nevertheless appear to lack the spatial precision needed for grounding to consistently help.

5
Limitations

We note five limitations.
Evaluation methodology.
Our VQA scoring relies on heuristic rules (synonym matching, contradiction detection, dynamic thresholds) that have not been validated against human judgment or reference-based metrics such as BERTScore or RadGraph F1, and may be sensitive to prompt style and answer verbosity; this caveat applies to both our zero-shot and SFT numbers.
Grounding task design.
We use SLAKE bounding boxes (organs and discrete pathological findings) with template queries, which does not capture diffuse pathologies, irregular lesion shapes, or ambiguous boundaries where masks or point grounding would be more appropriate.
Dataset scope.
Zero-shot and grounding evaluation cover VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and PathVQA (with grounding restricted to SLAKE); generalization to other modalities (dermatology, ophthalmology) and larger-scale benchmarks remains unverified.
Fine-tuning scope.
Our SFT study is confined to a single base model (Qwen 2.5 VL 7B) and a single configuration (full fine-tuning, three datasets’ train splits, 3 epochs); we do not ablate PEFT methods (LoRA/QLoRA), data-mix compositions, or training duration, which limits attribution of the observed gains to specific design choices. We also evaluate the SFT model only on VQA metrics, so we cannot disentangle whether the gains reflect improved perception, better answer priors, or both.
Causal interpretation.
The IoU-vs-VQA pattern is observational across five VLMs and partly confounded by format-compliance failure (notably 70.3% parse failure for GPT-5 and 98.9% for Gemini on VQA-RAD self-grounding); we therefore frame “grounding quality is the bottleneck” as a hypothesis well supported by our data rather than a tested causal claim.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we presented a diagnostic benchmark that separately evaluates the visual question answering (reasoning) and visual grounding (perception) capabilities of five vision-language models on medical imaging data. By constructing a dedicated grounding evaluation split from the SLAKE dataset and comparing direct VQA, self-grounding, and GT-grounding pipelines, we provide empirical evidence on the relationship between perception and reasoning in medical VQA.

Our results reveal four key findings. First, all evaluated VLMs—including those with explicitly advertised grounding features—exhibit remarkably poor localization in the medical domain, with the best model (Qwen 2.5 VL) achieving only 0.23 mean IoU and 19.1% Acc@0.5 on our SLAKE-derived grounding split. Second, self-grounding degrades VQA accuracy for all models, with severity tracking grounding quality (and, for some models, format-compliance failure under the two-step prompt). Third, replacing predicted bounding boxes with ground-truth annotations
recovers and improves
VQA accuracy beyond direct VQA baselines, indicating that grounding is genuinely beneficial when accurate. Fourth, as a secondary follow-up, supervised fine-tuning of Qwen 2.5 VL 7B on the combined train splits attains the highest SLAKE open-ended recall in Table

(85.5%) and matches or exceeds LLaVA-Med-reported numbers on four of six dataset–metric combinations while remaining within

1 pp on the other two, showing that the VQA-level gap is tractable via domain adaptation; whether this closes the trust-relevant perception bottleneck is left to future work, as we evaluate only VQA metrics on the SFT model.

These results point to visual grounding quality as a primary bottleneck in the SLAKE bounding-box setting we evaluate. The grounding-enhanced pipeline is not inherently flawed; rather, current VLMs lack the perceptual precision needed for grounding to serve as a reliable intermediate step in this setting.
