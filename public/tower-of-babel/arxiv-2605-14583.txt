Weierstrass semigroups at totally ramified places of degree one on Kummer extensions Journal: Finite Fields and Their Applications
Huachao Zhang, Chang-An Zhao

Abstract
We explicitly describe the set of gaps and the Weierstrass semigroup at a totally ramified place of degree one on a Kummer extension defined by the affine equation y m = f ⁡ ( x ) y^{m}=f(x) over K K , an algebraic extension of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , where f ⁡ ( x ) ∈ K ⁡ ( x ) f(x)\in K(x) . Our description takes a unified form for distinct totally ramified places of degree one. We then provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the Weierstrass semigroup at a totally ramified place of degree one to be symmetric. Furthermore, we investigate the minimal generating set of the Weierstrass semigroups at many totally ramified places of degree one. We not only explicitly describe the minimal generating set, but also construct functions whose pole divisors have coefficients lying in the set. Finally, we apply our results to specific Kummer extensions, including function fields of GGS curves and subcovers of the BM curve.

For

the greatest common divisor of
a
and
b
. Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
.
For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\lfloor c\rfloor
the largest integer not greater than
c
and by
\lceil c\rceil
the smallest integer not less than

.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
13

Let
a
and

be elements in

. The following statements hold:

(i)
\left\lfloor-a\right\rfloor=-\left\lceil a\right\rceil
.

(ii)

(iii) If

2.1
Function fields

Let
F/K
be a function field with constant field
K
. Let
g
be the genus of
F
. We denote by
\mathbf{P}_{F}
the set of places of
F
, by
\Omega_{F}
the module of Weil differentials of
F
, by
v_{P}
the discrete valuation of
F
with respect to the place
P\in\mathbf{P}_{F}
, and by
{\rm Div}(F)
the free abelian group generated by the places in
F
. A place of degree one is called a rational place of
F
. An element
D\in{\rm Div}(F)
is called a divisor of
F
and its degree is given by
{\rm deg}\,D:=\sum_{P\in{\operatorname{supp}D}}v_{P}(D)\cdot{\rm deg}\,P
, where
\operatorname{supp}D
is the support of
D
.
For a non-zero element
z\in F
, we denote by
(z)_{F}
,
(z)_{\infty}
, and
(z)_{0}
the principal divisor, the pole divisor and the zero divisor of
z
, respectively.
For a non-zero element
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
, we denote by
(\omega)_{F}
the canonical divisor corresponding to
\omega
.

Given a divisor
D\in{\rm Div}(F)
, we have the following two vector spaces over
K
: the Riemann-Roch space

\mathcal{L}(D):=\{z\in F\mid(z)_{F}\geq-D\}\cup\{0\}

and the space of Weil differentials

\Omega_{F}(D):=\{\omega\in\Omega_{F}\mid(\omega)_{F}\geq D\}\cup\{0\}.

We denote by
\ell(D)
the dimension of
\mathcal{L}(D)
over
K
. A non-zero Weil differential
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
is said to be regular if
\omega\in\Omega_{F}(0)
.
Since
\Omega_{F}
is a one-dimensional vector space over
F
, it follows that
(z\omega)_{F}=(z)_{F}+(\omega)_{F}
is also a canonical divisor for any non-zero elements
z\in F
and
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
.
Let
W
be a canonical divisor of
F/K
. Then for each divisor
D\in{\rm Div}(F)
, the Riemann-Roch Theorem says that

\ell(D)={\rm deg}\,D+1-g+\ell(W-D).

Next we introduce the notion of Weierstrass semigroups in
F
.
Let
P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}
be
s
rational places of
F
. The Weierstrass semigroup at
P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}
is defined by

H(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}):=\left\{(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}~|~\exists z\in F\text{~with~}(z)_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}P_{i}\right\}.

The complementary set
G(P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s}):=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\setminus H(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})
is called the set of gaps at
P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s}
.
An element in
G(P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s})
is called a gap at
P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s}
.

Let
P
be a rational place of
F
. The Weierstrass semigroup
H(P)
at a single rational place has additional properties. If
g\geq 1
, then
G(P)=\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus H(P)
contains exactly
g
gaps
1=a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{g}\leq 2g-1
at
P
.
The smallest non-zero element of
H(P)
is called the multiplicity of
H(P)
and is denoted by
m_{H(P)}
,
while the largest element of
G(P)
is called the Frobenius number of
H(P)
and is denoted by
F_{H(P)}
. We say that
H(P)
is symmetric if
F_{H(P)}=2g-1
.
There are two useful lemmas concerning
H(P)
and
G(P)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
25
, Remark 4.4]

The Weierstrass semigroup
H(P)
is symmetric if and only if
W=(2g-2)P
is a canonical divisor.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
43
, Corollary 14.2.5]

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
a\in G(P)
if and only if there exists a regular Weil differential
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
such that
v_{P}(\omega)=a-1
.

2.2
The minimal generating sets of Weierstrass semigroups

To describe the minimal generating sets of Weierstrass semigroups at several rational places, we first introduce additional notation. For two elements
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s}),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, we define a partial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
by
\mathbf{a}\preceq\mathbf{b}
if and only if
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.
Furthermore, if
a_{i}<b_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq s
, we write
\mathbf{a}\prec\mathbf{b}
. Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
and
\mathbf{a}\in S
. We say that
\mathbf{a}
is minimal in
S
with respect to
\preceq
if
\mathbf{b}\not\preceq\mathbf{a}
for all
\mathbf{b}\in S\setminus\{\mathbf{a}\}
. We denote by
|K|
the cardinality of
K
. If
K
is an infinite field, we adopt the convention that
a<|K|
for every integer
a
.

For a function field
F/K
with genus
g>0
, let
P_{1},\cdots,P_{s}
be
s
distinct rational places of
F
, where
1\leq s\leq|K|
. From the work of Carvalho and Torres
[
10
]
, the dimensions of Riemann-Roch spaces can be used to characterize
H(P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s})
and
G(P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{s})
. Given an
s
-tuple
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, we have that
\mathbf{n}\in H(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})
if and only if

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}P_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}P_{i}-P_{j}\right)+1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s.

Moreover, we have that
\mathbf{n}\in G(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})
if and only if

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}P_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}P_{i}-P_{j}\right)\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq s.

Next, we introduce the definition of
\Gamma(P_{1},\cdots,P_{t})
for
1\leq t\leq s
, which is proposed by Matthews
[
31
]
. Let
\Gamma(P_{1}):=H(P_{1})
. For
s\geq 2
, define
\Gamma(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})
by

\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\mid\mathbf{a}\text{ is minimal in }\{\mathbf{n}\in H(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})~|~n_{i}=a_{i}\}\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq s\}.

.

[
31

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
. Then
\mathbf{a}
is minimal in

with respect to
\preceq

\mathbf{a}
is minimal in

with respect to
\preceq
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
11
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})\in H(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})
and
A=n_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}P_{s}
. Then
\mathbf{n}\in\Gamma(P_{1},\cdots,P_{s})

\ell(A)=\ell(A-P)+1=\ell(A-P-Q)+1\text{ and }\ell(A)=\ell(A-Q)+1=\ell(A-P-Q)+1

.

[

, Lemma 4]

Suppose that
s\geq 2
. Then

For
s=2
, suppose that

and
G(P_{2})=\{b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{g}\}
.
For each gap
a_{i}
at
P_{1}
, let
n_{a_{i}}=\min\{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid(a_{i},b)\in H(P_{1},P_{2})\}
.
From

, we have the equality
\{n_{a}\mid a\in G(P_{1})\}=G(P_{2})
, and therefore there exists a permutation

such that

.
The graph of the bijection between

and

\Gamma(P_{1},P_{2})=\{(a_{i},b_{\tau(i)})\mid i=1,\cdots,g\}
.
The following lemma characterizes it.

Lemma 2.7
.

[

, Lemma 2]

Let
\Gamma
be a subset of

If there exists a permutation

Let

and
I=\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{t}\}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,s\}
. Define the natural inclusion

where

The minimal generating set of

Given

, where

The following theorem shows that

is determined by

\gcd(m,q)=1
, and let
r\geq 2
be an integer. Let
f(x)\in K(x)
such that for every
d\mid m
with
d\geq 2
,
f(x)
is not a
d
-th power of any element in
K(x)
.
Consider the Kummer extension
F=K(x,y)/K(x)
defined by the equation (
1
):

y^{m}=f(x)=\alpha\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}(x)^{\lambda_{i}},

where
\alpha\in K\setminus\{0\}
, each
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, and
p_{1}(x),\cdots,p_{r}(x)\in K[x]
are pairwise distinct monic irreducible polynomials. Note that
\varphi(T)=T^{m}-f(x)\in K(x)[T]
, which is the minimal polynomial of
y
over
K(x)
, is also irreducible in
\bar{K}(x)[T]
, where
\bar{K}
is the algebraic closure of
K
. It follows that
K
is the full constant field of
F
.

Let
d_{i}:={\rm deg}\,\,p_{i}(x)
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Let
\lambda_{0}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}d_{i}
and
d_{0}:=1
.
By
[
40
, Proposition 3.7.3]
, the genus of
F
is given by

g=\frac{2-2m+\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left(m-\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})\right)\cdot d_{i}}{2}.

For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
P_{i}
and
P_{\infty}
be the places in
\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}
corresponding to the zero of
p_{i}(x)
and the pole of
x
, respectively.
If
\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})=1
, we denote by
Q_{i}
the only place in
F
lying over
P_{i}
and say that
Q_{i}
is totally ramified.
If
\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})=1
, we denote by
Q_{\infty}
the only place lying over
P_{\infty}
and say that
Q_{\infty}
is totally ramified.

For convenience, if
\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})=1
, we also denote by
P_{0}
the pole of
x
in
\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}
, and by
Q_{0}
the only place lying over
P_{0}
.
If
d_{i}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})=1
for some
0\leq i\leq r
, then
Q_{i}
is a totally ramified place of degree one, which is our main object of study.

We set
z_{0}:=1
and
z_{i}:=p_{i}(x)
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Then we have the following principal divisors:

(z_{i})_{F}=\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{i}}Q-\frac{md_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{\infty}}Q,\text{ for }0\leq i\leq r,

(2)

(y)_{F}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\sum_{{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{i}}}Q.

(3)

The different of
F/K(x)
is

{\rm Diff}(F/K(x))=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}-1\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{i}}Q.

Thus, there exists a canonical divisor
W
given by

\begin{split}W&=-2(x)_{\infty}+{\rm Diff}(F/K(x))\\
&=-\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}+1\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{0}}Q+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}-1\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{i}}Q.\end{split}

(4)

For any divisor
D
of
F
, write

D=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P}\,n_{Q}\,Q
.
We define the restriction of
D
to
K(x)
as

D\Big|_{K(x)}:=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n_{Q}}{e(Q|P)}\right\rfloor\colon{Q|P}\right\}P,

where
e(Q|P)
is the ramification index of
Q
over
P
.
If
K
contains a primitive

-th root of unity, then the extension
F/K(x)
is Galois. The following result was given by Maharaj in
[

]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
27
, Theorem 2.2]

Suppose that
K
contains a primitive
m
-th root of unity. Then for any divisor
D
of
F
that is invariant under the action of the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F/K(x))
,

\mathcal{L}(D)=\bigoplus\limits_{t=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{L}\left(\left[D+(y^{t})_{F}\right]\Big|_{K(x)}\right)\,y^{t}.

Remark 2.10
.

In the classical framework, the extension
F/K(x)
is said to be a Kummer extension under the assumption that

contains a primitive

-th root of unity. In this paper, we remove this assumption and still call

3
The Weierstrass semigroup at a totally ramified place of degree one on Kummer extensions

In this section, we consider the Kummer extension
F=K(x,y)/K(x)
defined by the equation (
1
).
First, we present some required lemmas.
Then we give a unified description of the set of gaps
G(Q)
at any totally ramified place
Q
of degree one.
Furthermore, we explicitly provide a system of generators, the multiplicity, and the Frobenius number of
H(Q)
.
Finally, we determine a necessary and sufficient condition for
H(Q)
to be symmetric.

3.1
Some required lemmas

In this subsection, we first state several lemmas that will be used in the later proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
(a_{0},\cdots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
,
b,c\in\mathbb{Z}
and
b\equiv c\pmod{m}
. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+b\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+c\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}\text{~~and~~}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{a_{i}+b\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rceil d_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{a_{i}+c\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rceil d_{i}.

Proof.

Suppose that
b=c+km
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows from
\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}d_{i}=0
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+b\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+(c+km)\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+c\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+k\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+c\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}.

Similarly, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{a_{i}+b\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rceil d_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{a_{i}+c\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rceil d_{i}
.
∎

Next we present a lemma similar to part (iii) of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rceil=\frac{m(\lambda+1)-\lambda-\gcd(m,\lambda)}{2}.

Proof.

If
\lambda=0
, one verifies that

\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rceil=0=\frac{m(\lambda+1)-\lambda-\gcd(m,\lambda)}{2}.

If
\lambda>0
, since
\#\{1\leq i\leq m-1\mid m\text{ divides }i\lambda\}=\gcd(m,\lambda)-1
, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=m-\gcd(m,\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=m-\gcd(m,\lambda)+\frac{(m-1)(\lambda-1)+\gcd(m,\lambda)-1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(\lambda+1)-\lambda-\gcd(m,\lambda)}{2}.

If
\lambda<0
, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rceil=-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{-i\lambda}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=-\frac{(m-1)(-\lambda-1)+\gcd(m,\lambda)-1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(\lambda+1)-\lambda-\gcd(m,\lambda)}{2}.

The discussion of the above three cases completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is derived from the theory of constant field extensions of function fields.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
K^{\prime}
is an algebraic extension of
K
such that
K^{\prime}
contains a primitive
m
-th root of unity. Let
F^{\prime}=FK^{\prime}
be the constant field extension of
F
, and let
\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\cdot)
be the conorm with respect to
F^{\prime}/F
.
Let
D
be a divisor of
F
such that
\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D)
is invariant under the action of the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\prime}/K^{\prime}(x))
. Then

\ell(D)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\ell\left(\left[D+(y^{t})_{F}\right]\Big|_{K(x)}\right).

Proof.

Let
A
be a divisor of
F
. Let
\operatorname{Con}_{K^{\prime}(x)/K(x)}(\cdot)
be the conorm with respect to
K^{\prime}(x)/K(x)
. According to
[
40
, Theorem 3.6.3]
, we have
\ell(\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(A))=\ell(A)
. Moreover, we can write

A=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P}n_{Q}\,Q\text{~~and~~}\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(A)=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P}\,n_{Q}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}_{F^{\prime}}\,Q^{\prime}|Q}Q^{\prime}.

We obtain that

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(A)\right]\Big|_{K^{\prime}(x)}=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\sum_{P^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}_{K^{\prime}(x)},\,P^{\prime}|P}\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n_{Q}}{e(Q^{\prime}|P^{\prime})}\right\rfloor\colon{Q^{\prime}|P^{\prime}}\right\}P^{\prime},

where
Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F}
with
Q^{\prime}|Q
. For each
Q^{\prime}|P^{\prime}|P
, we have
e(Q^{\prime}|P^{\prime})=e(Q|P)
since
e(Q^{\prime}|Q)=1=e(P^{\prime}|P)
.
Thus

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(A)\right]\Big|_{K^{\prime}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\sum_{P^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}_{K^{\prime}(x)},\,P^{\prime}|P}\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n_{Q}}{e(Q|P)}\right\rfloor\colon{Q^{\prime}|P^{\prime}}\right\}P^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathbf{P}_{K(x)}}\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n_{Q}}{e(Q|P)}\right\rfloor\colon{Q|P}\right\}\sum_{P^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}_{K^{\prime}(x)},\,P^{\prime}|P}P^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Con}_{K^{\prime}(x)/K(x)}\left(A|_{K(x)}\right).

This implies that
\ell(\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D))=\ell(D)
and

\displaystyle\ell\left(\left[\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D)+(y^{t})_{F^{\prime}}\right]\Big|_{K^{\prime}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\operatorname{Con}_{K^{\prime}(x)/K(x)}\left(\left[D+(y^{t})_{F}\right]\Big|_{K(x)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\left[D+(y^{t})_{F}\right]\Big|_{K(x)}\right)

for each
0\leq t\leq m-1
.
By Theorem
2.9
, we have

\ell(\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D))=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\ell\left(\left[\operatorname{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D)+(y^{t})_{F^{\prime}}\right]\Big|_{K^{\prime}(x)}\right).

Then the result of the lemma follows.
∎

From the second assertion of the above lemma, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\{\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r}\}
be a permutation of
\{0,1,\cdots,r\}
and
0\leq s\leq r
. Suppose that
\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{i}})=1
for all
0\leq i\leq s
, and let
(a_{0},\dots,a_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s+1}
. Then

\ell\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}Q_{\ell_{i}}\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\max\left\{0,\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{\ell_{i}}+1\right\}.

Proof.

Note that
Q_{\ell_{0}},Q_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}}
are totally ramified places in the Kummer extension
F/K(x)
since
\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{i}})=1
for all
0\leq i\leq s
. The principal divisor of
y
in
F
is

(y)_{F}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\lambda_{\ell_{i}}Q_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{\ell_{i}}}\frac{\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{i}})}Q.

Then for each
t\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
,

\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}Q_{\ell_{i}}+(y^{t})_{F}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(a_{i}+t\lambda_{\ell_{i}})Q_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{\ell_{i}}}\frac{t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{i}})}Q.

It follows that

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}Q_{\ell_{i}}+(y^{t})_{F}\right)\Big|_{K(x)}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor P_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor P_{\ell_{i}}.

By Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\ell\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}Q_{\ell_{i}}\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\ell\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor P_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor P_{\ell_{i}}\right).

According to Riemann-Roch Theorem, we have that

\ell\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}Q_{\ell_{i}}\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\max\left\{0,\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{\ell_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{\ell_{i}}+1\right\}.

∎

3.2
A unified description of
G(Q_{s})

In this subsection, we give a unified description of
G(Q_{s})
for a totally ramified place of degree one in the Kummer extension
F/K(x)
. First, we characterize
G(Q_{s})
as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo m.
Then

G(Q_{s})=\left\{a\in\mathbb{N}~\Big|~\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+\left\lceil\frac{a}{m}\right\rceil\leq-1\right\}.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
We have
a\in G(Q_{s})
if and only if
\ell(aQ_{s})=\ell((a-1)Q_{s})
. By Corollary
3.4
, the equality
\ell(aQ_{s})=\ell((a-1)Q_{s})
is equivalent to the condition that for each
t\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
, either

\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0,i\neq s}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}\leq-1

or

\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0,i\neq s}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}\geq 0\text{ and }\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}-1}{m}\right\rfloor.

Note that
\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor\neq\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}-1}{m}\right\rfloor
if and only if
t\equiv-a\lambda\pmod{m}
. Thus
a\in G(Q_{s})
if and only if

\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0,i\neq s}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}\leq-1

for the unique
t\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
satisfying
t\equiv-a\lambda\pmod{m}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{a+t\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0,i\neq s}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}=\left\lfloor\frac{a-a\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0,i\neq s}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}

Let
\lambda\lambda_{s}=1+km
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\left\lfloor\frac{a-a\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor=-ka
and
\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor=-ka-\left\lceil\frac{a}{m}\right\rceil
.
Thus, we get that
\left\lfloor\frac{a-a\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{a}{m}\right\rceil
.
This yields
a\in G(Q_{s})
if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+\left\lceil\frac{a}{m}\right\rceil\leq-1.

∎

Now we explicitly describe the set of gaps at a totally ramified place of degree one in a unified way.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
. Then for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(m,\lambda)=1
, the set
G(Q_{s})
is given by

\left\{mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s}\,\Big|\,1\leq i\leq m-1,\,\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil-1\right\}.

In particular, let
\lambda
be the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo
m
. Then

G(Q_{s})=\left\{mj+i~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,\,0\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-2\right\}.

(5)

Proof.

Define the set

G=\left\{mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s}~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,\,\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil-1\right\}.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo
m
. For
mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s}\in G
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-(mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s})\lambda^{\prime}\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{s}\lambda^{\prime}\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{k}=-\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}.

It follows that

\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-(mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s})\lambda^{\prime}\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{k}+\left\lceil\frac{mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil=j-\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}+\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil\leq-1.

By Proposition
3.5
, we conclude that
mj+i\lambda\lambda_{s}\in G(Q_{s})
. Hence
G\subseteq G(Q_{s})
.

Now let
mj_{1}+i_{1}\lambda\lambda_{s},mj_{2}+i_{2}\lambda\lambda_{s}\in G
. If
mj_{1}+i_{1}\lambda\lambda_{s}=mj_{2}+i_{2}\lambda\lambda_{s}
, then
(i_{1}-i_{2})\lambda\lambda_{s}\equiv 0\pmod{m}
.
Since
1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq m-1
, we must have
i_{1}=i_{2}
and hence
j_{1}=j_{2}
. By Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\#G

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rfloor\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-(m-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{m(\lambda\lambda_{k}+1)-\lambda\lambda_{k}-\gcd(m,\lambda\lambda_{k})}{2}\cdot d_{k}-(m-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{2-2m+\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(m-\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})\right)\cdot d_{k}}{2}=g.

This proves that
G(Q_{s})=G
.

If
\lambda
is the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo
m
, then
\lambda\lambda_{s}=1+bm
for some
b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We have
\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil=-ib
and
\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{s}}{m}\right\rceil=ib+1
.
Then

G(Q_{s})=\left\{mj+ibm+i\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1,\,-ib\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-ib-2\right\}.

Letting
j^{\prime}=j+ib
yields the desired expression.
∎

.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
\lambda_{i}\equiv\lambda_{j}\pmod{m}
,
d_{i}=d_{j}=1
, and
\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})=\gcd(m,\lambda_{j})=1
for some
0\leq i<j\leq r
, then
G(Q_{i})=G(Q_{j})
and
H(Q_{i})=H(Q_{j})
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be the inverse of
\lambda_{i}
modulo
m
. Since
\lambda_{i}\equiv\lambda_{j}\pmod{m}
, then
\lambda
is also the inverse of
\lambda_{j}
modulo
m
. By Proposition
3.6
, we have
G(Q_{i})=G(Q_{j})
, and hence
H(Q_{i})=H(Q_{j})
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo
m
. Then

H(Q_{s})=\left\langle m,m\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1\right)+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\right\rangle.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle H(Q_{s})=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus G(Q_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\{mj+i~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,\,j\geq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1\right\}\cup\{jm\mid j\geq 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\langle m,m\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1\right)+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\right\rangle.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda_{s}
modulo
m
. Let
S=\left\{1\leq i\leq m-1\mid\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1=0\right\}\cup\{m\}
.
Define

i_{\min}=\min S,~~i_{\max}=\arg\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-2,

and

j_{\max}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-2.

Then
m_{H(Q_{s})}=i_{\min}
and
F_{H(Q_{s})}=mj_{\max}+i_{\max}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.8
, we have

m_{H(Q_{s})}=\min\left\{m,m\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil-1\right)d_{k}+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\right\}.

Thus
m_{H(Q_{s})}=\min S
.
The second assertion follows directly from the definition of
F_{H(Q_{s})}
and Proposition
3.6
.
∎

3.3
The symmetry of
H(Q_{s})

In this subsection, given a totally ramified place
Q_{s}
of degree one, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for
H(Q_{s})
to be symmetric.

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
. Then
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric if and only if there exists
1\leq u\leq m-1
such that

\frac{u\lambda_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\equiv\frac{u\lambda_{j}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{j})}\pmod{m}

for all
i,j\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda\lambda_{s}=am+1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
. From Proposition
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle G(Q_{s})

\displaystyle=\left\{mj+i~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,\,0\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil-2\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{mj+i~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,\,0\leq j\leq\sum_{k=0,k\neq s}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil+ia-1\right\}

(6)

By the divisor (
4
), there exists a Weil differential
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
such that

(\omega)_{F}=-\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}+1\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{0}}Q+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{k}}Q.

For each
1\leq i\leq m-1
, using divisors (
3
) and (
2
), we obtain

(y^{i\lambda})_{F}=\sum_{k=1,k\neq s}^{r}\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{k}}Q+(iam+i)Q_{s},

and

\displaystyle\left(\left(z_{s}^{d_{k}}/z_{k}\right)^{\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil}\right)_{F}=m\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}Q_{s}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{k}}Q

for each
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.
Thus we get

\displaystyle\left(y^{i\lambda}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{r}\left(z_{s}^{d_{k}}/z_{k}\right)^{\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil}\right)z_{k}^{-2}\omega\right)_{F}=\left(m\left(\sum_{k=0,k\neq s}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}+ia-1\right)+i-1\right)Q_{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0,k\neq s}^{r}\left(\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q\mid P_{k}}Q.

For each
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
and
1\leq i\leq m-1
, write
i\lambda\lambda_{k}=m\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor+t_{ik}
with
0\leq t_{ik}\leq m-1
.
If
m\mid i\lambda\lambda_{k}
, then

\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil=\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1\geq 0.

(7)

If
m\nmid i\lambda\lambda_{k}
, then
1\leq t_{ik}\leq m-1
and

\displaystyle\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rfloor-1

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left({\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}}-\frac{t_{ik}}{m}\right)-1=\frac{t_{ik}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1\geq 0.

(8)

Hence

\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil\geq 0

for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
and
1\leq i\leq m-1
.
For each
1\leq i\leq m-1
satisfying
\sum_{k=0,k\neq s}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}+ia-1\geq 0
, by Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

m\left(\sum_{k=1,k\neq s}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}+ia-1\right)+i\in G(Q_{s}).

Moreover, all gaps at
Q_{s}
are obtained in this way according to (
6
).
Note that every non-zero canonical divisor of
F
has degree
2g-2
. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric if and only if there exists
1\leq u\leq m-1
such that

\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-1+\frac{u\lambda\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}-\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\left\lceil\frac{u\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil=0

(9)

for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.

If
m\mid u\lambda\lambda_{k}
for some
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
, then
t_{uk}=0
. It follows from (
7
) that (
9
) holds if and only if
\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})=m
.
Thus (
9
) holds if and only if
t_{uk}\equiv 0\pmod{m}
.

If
m\nmid u\lambda\lambda_{k}
for some
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
, it follows from (
8
) that (
9
) holds if and only if
t_{uk}\equiv\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})
.
Since
t_{uk}\equiv i\lambda\lambda_{k}
for all
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
, we have

\displaystyle~\eqref{sym_condition}\text{ holds for all }k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~t_{uk}\equiv\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})\pmod{m}\text{ for all }k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~u\lambda\lambda_{k}\equiv\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})\pmod{m}\text{ for all }k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~\frac{u\lambda_{k}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{k})}\equiv\lambda_{s}\pmod{m}\text{ for all }k\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}.

Therefore
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric if and only if
\frac{u\lambda_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\equiv\frac{u\lambda_{j}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{j})}\pmod{m}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.
∎

In
[
35
, Theorems A and B]
, for the infinite place
Q_{\infty}
, Mendoza provided some conditions for the Weierstrass semigroup
H(Q_{\infty})
to be symmetric. We generalize these results to arbitrary totally ramified places of degree one in the following two corollaries.

Corollary 3.11
.

For each
0\leq i\leq r
, write
\lambda_{i}=mb_{i}+\bar{\lambda}_{i}
, where
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq\bar{\lambda}_{i}\leq m
. Suppose that
d_{s}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{s})=1
for some
0\leq s\leq r
.
Then

F_{H(Q_{s})}=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)-\sum_{i\neq s,\,i=0}^{r}\bar{\lambda}_{i}d_{i}\text{ and }H(Q_{s})\text{ is symmetric},

if and only if
\bar{\lambda}_{i}\mid m
for all
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.

Proof.

If
\bar{\lambda}_{i}\mid m
for all
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
, then
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\equiv 1\pmod{m}
for all
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
. It follows from Theorem
3.10
that
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric and

\displaystyle F_{H(Q_{s})}=2g-1

\displaystyle=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)+2-\sum_{i=0}^{r}\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})\cdot d_{i}-1

\displaystyle=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)-\sum_{i\neq s,\,i=0}^{r}\bar{\lambda}_{i}d_{i}.

Conversely, assume that
F_{H(Q_{s})}=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)-\sum_{i\neq s,\,i=0}^{r}\bar{\lambda}_{i}d_{i}
and
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric. Then

\displaystyle F_{H(Q_{s})}

\displaystyle=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)-\sum_{i\neq s,\,i=0}^{r}\bar{\lambda}_{i}d_{i}

\displaystyle=2g-1=m\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}-2\right)-\sum_{i\neq s,\,i=0}^{r}\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})\cdot d_{i}.

This implies that
\bar{\lambda}_{i}\mid m
for all
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
0\leq s\leq r
. Suppose that
\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})=1
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
d_{s}=1
.
Then
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric if and only if
\lambda_{i}\equiv\lambda_{j}\pmod{m}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.

Proof.

Since
\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})=1
for all
0\leq i\leq r
, we have
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}\equiv\lambda_{i}\pmod{m}
for all
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.
It follows from Theorem
3.10
that
H(Q_{s})
is symmetric if and only if
\lambda_{i}\equiv\lambda_{j}\pmod{m}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1,\cdots,r\}\setminus\{s\}
.
∎

Besides the conditions in the above corollaries, there may exist other situations in which the Weierstrass semigroup is symmetric, as shown in the following example.

Example 3.13
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{11}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{11}(x)
be a Kummer extension defined by
y^{8}=x^{3}(x-1)^{6}
. Take
m=8
,
\lambda_{1}=3
,
\lambda_{2}=6
and
\lambda_{0}=-9
. Then
\gcd(m,\lambda_{1})=1
and
\gcd(m,\lambda_{2})=2
, so
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{1})}=3=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{2})}
. Hence
H(Q_{\infty})
is symmetric.

4
The minimal generating sets of Weierstrass semigroups on Kummer extensions

In this section, we investigate the minimal generating sets of the Weierstrass semigroups at several totally ramified places of degree one on the Kummer extension
F=K(x,y)/K(x)
defined by the equation (
1
).
Suppose that
\bar{r}:=\min\{r+1,|K|\}-1
and that
\{\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r}\}
is a permutation of
\{0,1,\cdots,r\}
. We present our main results below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1\leq s\leq\bar{r}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(m,\lambda)=1
. Suppose that
d_{\ell_{k}}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{k}})=1
for all
0\leq k\leq s
.
Then

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})=\Bigg\{(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1}~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle~j_{k}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\text{ for }0\leq k\leq s,~\sum_{k=0}^{s}j_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\Bigg\}.

Moreover, for each
(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})
,

\left(\frac{\prod_{k=s+1}^{r}z_{\ell_{k}}^{\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil}}{y^{i\lambda}\prod_{k=0}^{s}z_{\ell_{k}}^{j_{k}}}\right)_{\infty}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}(mj_{k}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}})Q_{\ell_{k}}.

In addition, the set
\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})=\varnothing
if and only if
\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}\leq s\leq\bar{r}
.

A result similar to Theorem
4.1
also appears in the recent work
[
14
, Corollary 3.5]
.
In contrast to
[
14
, Corollary 3.5]
, we allow the exponents
\lambda_{i}

(1\leq i\leq r)
to be arbitrary integers, rather than restricting them to the range
1\leq\lambda_{i}\leq m-1
. In addition, we do not require
f(x)
in the equation (
1
) to be completely split over
K
.
Moreover, we provide explicit functions whose pole divisors have coefficients lying in
\Gamma(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
, along with a necessary and sufficient condition for
\Gamma(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
to be nonempty.
By selecting a suitable
\lambda
, we are able to obtain a simplified expression for
\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})
when some of the
\lambda_{\ell_{k}}
are congruent modulo
m
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
1\leq s\leq\bar{r}
and
0\leq t\leq s
. Suppose that
d_{\ell_{k}}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{k}})=1
for all
0\leq k\leq s
and
\lambda_{\ell_{0}}\equiv\cdots\equiv\lambda_{\ell_{t}}\pmod{m}
.
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda_{\ell_{0}}
modulo
m
.
Then for
1\leq s\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
,

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})=\Bigg\{(mj_{0}+i,\cdots,mj_{t}+i,mj_{t+1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{t+1}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1}~\Big|

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq m-1,\,j_{k}\geq 0\text{ for }0\leq k\leq t,\,j_{k}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\text{ for }t+1\leq k\leq s,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{s}j_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\sum_{k=t+1}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil-t-1\Bigg\}.

For each
(mj_{0}+i,\cdots,mj_{t}+i,mj_{t+1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{t+1}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})
,

\left(\frac{\prod_{k=s+1}^{r}z_{\ell_{k}}^{\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil}}{y^{i\lambda}\prod_{k=0}^{s}z_{\ell_{k}}^{j_{k}}}\right)_{\infty}=\sum_{k=0}^{t}(mj_{k}+i)Q_{\ell_{k}}+\sum_{k=t+1}^{s}(mj_{k}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}})Q_{\ell_{k}}.

Proof.

For
1\leq s\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
, it follows from Theorem
4.1
that

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})=\Bigg\{(mj^{\prime}_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,mj^{\prime}_{s}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\mathbb{N}^{s+1}~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle~j^{\prime}_{k}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\text{ for }0\leq k\leq s,~\sum_{k=0}^{s}j^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\Bigg\}.

For each
0\leq k\leq t
, let
b_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}=mb_{k}+1
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq m-1
, we have
\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=-ib_{k}
and
\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=ib_{k}+1
.
Let
j_{k}=j^{\prime}_{k}+ib_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq t
and
j_{k}=j_{k}^{\prime}
for
t+1\leq k\leq s
. Then we obtain the desired expression.
∎

We will prove Theorem
4.1
by induction. First, we show that Theorem
4.1
holds for
s=1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
d_{\ell_{0}}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{0}})=1
and
d_{\ell_{1}}=\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{1}})=1
.
Then for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(m,\lambda)=1
,

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{\ell_{0}},Q_{\ell_{1}})=\Bigg\{

\displaystyle(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},mj_{1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1,\,j_{0}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}}}{m}\right\rceil,

\displaystyle~j_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}}}{m}\right\rceil,\quad j_{0}+j_{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}}}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}}}{m}\right\rceil\Bigg\}.

Proof.

Define the set

\displaystyle\Gamma=\Bigg\{

\displaystyle(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},mj_{1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1,\,j_{0}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}}}{m}\right\rceil,

\displaystyle~j_{1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}}}{m}\right\rceil,\quad j_{0}+j_{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}}}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}}}{m}\right\rceil\Bigg\}.

Given a tuple
(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}},mj_{1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}})\in\Gamma
, by the divisors (
2
) and (
3
), we obtain the following principal divisor:

\displaystyle\left(y^{-i\lambda}z_{\ell_{0}}^{-j_{0}}z_{\ell_{1}}^{-j_{1}}\prod_{k=2}^{r}z_{\ell_{k}}^{\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil}\right)_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}}Q_{\ell_{0}}-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}}Q_{\ell_{1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{r}\frac{-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{k}})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{\ell_{k}}}Q

\displaystyle~-mj_{0}Q_{\ell_{0}}+\frac{mj_{0}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{0}}Q-mj_{1}Q_{\ell_{1}}+\frac{mj_{1}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{0}}Q

\displaystyle~+\sum_{k=2}^{r}\frac{m\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{k}})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{\ell_{k}}}Q-\sum_{k=2}^{r}\frac{m\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil d_{\ell_{k}}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{0}}Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{k=2}^{r}\frac{m\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{\gcd(m,\lambda_{\ell_{k}})}\sum_{Q\in\mathbf{P}_{F},\,Q|P_{\ell_{k}}}Q-(mj_{0}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{0}})Q_{\ell_{0}}-(mj_{1}+i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{1}})Q_{\ell_{1}}.

Since
m\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil-i\lambda\lambda_{\ell_{k}}\geq 0
for all
2\leq k\leq r
, we conclude that

.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{j},i}=(mj_{0}+i\lambda_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\Gamma_{1}(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})
.
For each
0\leq k\leq s
, let
b_{i,k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq c_{i,k}\leq m-1
such that
i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}=mb_{i,k}+c_{i,k}
.
Then we have
\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=-b_{i,k}
and
\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=b_{i,k}+1
. Thus

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left(j_{k}-\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left(-\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac{i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-s-1.

This implies that
s\leq\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
for some
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Thus
1\leq s\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
.

If
1\leq s\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
, then
s\leq\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
for some
1\leq i\leq m-1
. For each
0\leq k\leq s
, let
b_{i,k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq c_{i,k}\leq m-1
such that
i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}=mb_{i,k}+c_{i,k}
. Then we have
\left\lceil\frac{-i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=-b_{i,k}
and
\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{\ell_{k}}}{m}\right\rceil=b_{i,k}+1
. Let
j_{0}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil-s-1-b_{i,0}
and
j_{k}=-b_{i,k}
for
1\leq k\leq s
. It is straightforward to verify that
(mj_{0}+i\lambda_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,mj_{s}+i\lambda_{\ell_{s}})\in\Gamma_{1}(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\Gamma_{1}(Q_{\ell_{0}},\cdots,Q_{\ell_{s}})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
1\leq s\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda_{k}}{m}\right\rceil d_{k}-1
.
∎

5
Some examples

In this section, we apply our results to present explicit examples of certain Kummer extensions, including Kummer extensions with the same multiplicities, function fields of GGS curves, and function fields of subcovers of the BM curve.

5.1
Kummer extensions with the same multiplicities

Let
q
be a prime power and let
m\geq 2
be an integer.
In this subsection, we consider the Kummer extension
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
defined by the equation:

y^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\alpha_{i})^{\lambda},

(14)

where
\gcd(r\lambda,m)=1
,
\gcd(m,q)=1
, and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\in K
are pairwise distinct.
The genus of
F
is given by
g=(m-1)(r-1)/2
.
Let
Q_{\infty}
denote the place in
\mathbf{P}_{F}
corresponding to the pole of
x
. For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
Q_{i}
denote the place in
\mathbf{P}_{F}
corresponding to the zero of
x-\alpha_{i}
.
By
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
, the Weierstrass semigroup is given by
H(Q_{\infty})=\langle m,r\rangle
. Next, we establish further properties of
H(Q_{\infty})
and
H(Q_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proposition 5.1
.

(i)
H(Q_{\infty})
is symmetric,
m_{H(Q_{\infty})}=\min\{m,r\}
and
F_{H(Q_{\infty})}=mr-m-r
.

(ii) For each
1\leq i\leq r
,

H(Q_{i})=\left\langle m,m\left(r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor\right)+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\right\rangle,

m_{H(Q_{i})}=m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
and
F_{H(Q_{i})}=m\left(r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-2\right)+1
.
Moreover, we have that
H(Q_{i})
is symmetric if and only if
r\equiv-1\pmod{m}
.

Proof.

(i) It follows from Corollary
3.12
that
H(Q_{\infty})
is symmetric, and hence
F_{H(Q_{\infty})}=2g-1=mr-m-r
. Since
H(Q_{\infty})=\langle m,r\rangle
, we have
m_{H(Q_{\infty})}=\min\{m,r\}
.

(ii) Without loss of generality, assume that
i=1
. Let
\lambda^{\prime}
be the inverse of
\lambda
modulo
m
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{-ir\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{-ir}{m}\right\rceil=r-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor

for each
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Then by Corollary
3.8
,

H(Q_{i})=\left\langle m,m\left(r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor\right)+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\right\rangle.

Now let
r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor=0
. Then
(r-1)m\leq ir\leq(r-1)m+(m-1)
, which implies that

m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil\frac{(r-1)m}{r}\right\rceil\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{rm-1}{r}\right\rfloor=m-1.

If
m<r
, then
\{1\leq i\leq m-1\mid r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor=0\}=\varnothing
. It follows from Corollary
3.9
that
m_{H(Q_{i})}=m=m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
.
If
m>r
, then
\{1\leq i\leq m-1\mid r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor=0\}\neq\varnothing
. It follows from Corollary
3.9
that
m_{H(Q_{i})}=m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
.
Thus
m_{H(Q_{i})}=m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
.

Note that
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor=\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}r-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor
, that is,
r-\left\lfloor\frac{ir}{m}\right\rfloor
is maximum when
i=1
. Thus by Corollary
3.9
, we obtain
F_{H(Q_{1})}=m\left(r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-2\right)+1
.

It follows from Theorem
3.10
that
H(Q_{1})
is symmetric if and only if
-r\lambda\equiv\lambda\pmod{m}
. Therefore
H(Q_{1})
is symmetric if and only if
r\equiv-1\pmod{m}
.
∎

We observe that the description of
[
44
, Theorem 9]
is imprecise. Moreover, the argument presented in the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 9]
(page 272, line 15), where the authors claim that “Without loss of generality, we may assume that
j_{l}=\max\{j_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq l-1\}
”, is not fully rigorous. In what follows, we present a revised statement of this result together with a more rigorous proof.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
r+1\leq q
. For
1\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
, the set
\Gamma(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
is given by

\displaystyle\Big\{(mj_{0}-ir,m

\displaystyle j_{1}+i,\cdots,mj_{s}+i)\in\mathbb{N}^{s}~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle j_{0}\geq\left\lceil\frac{ir}{m}\right\rceil,\,j_{k}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq k\leq s\text{~and~}\sum_{k=1}^{s}j_{k}=r-s\Big\},

and
\Gamma(Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
for
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1<s\leq r
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\left\lceil\frac{-ir\lambda}{m}\right\rceil+r\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda}{m}\right\rceil\right\}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\left\lceil\frac{-ir}{m}\right\rceil+r\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil\right\}=\left\lceil\frac{-r}{m}\right\rceil+r=r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda
modulo
m
.
Then there exists
b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lambda\lambda^{\prime}=1+bm
. By Corollary
4.2
, for
1\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
, the set
\Gamma(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\Big\{(mj^{\prime}_{0}-ir\lambda\lambda^{\prime},mj_{1}+i,\cdots,mj_{s}+i)\in\mathbb{N}^{s}~\Big|~1\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle j_{0}^{\prime}\geq\left\lceil\frac{ir\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil,\,j_{k}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq k\leq s,\text{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{s}j_{k}=r\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil-s\Big\},

and
\Gamma(Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
for
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1<s\leq r
. Note that

r\left\lceil\frac{i\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil=r\left\lceil\frac{i(1+bm)}{m}\right\rceil=r+irb\text{ and }\left\lceil\frac{ir\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{ir(1+bm)^{\prime}}{m}\right\rceil=irb+\left\lceil\frac{ir}{m}\right\rceil.

Let
j_{0}=j^{\prime}_{0}-irb
. Then we must have
\left\lceil\frac{ir}{m}\right\rceil\leq\sum_{k=1}^{s}j_{k}=r-s
. We get that
\frac{ir}{m}<r-s
, and then
i<m-\frac{ms}{r}\leq m-\frac{m}{r}
.
Thus

i\leq\left\lfloor m-\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor=m-\left\lceil\frac{m}{r}\right\rceil=m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor.

Therefore, we obtain the desired expression.
∎

5.2
Function fields of GGS curves

Let
q
be a prime power and let
n\geq 3
be an odd integer. In this subsection, we consider the GGS curve
GGS(q,n)
, which is defined by the equations

\begin{cases}Y^{q+1}=X^{q}+X,\\
Z^{\frac{q^{n}+1}{q+1}}=Y^{q^{2}}-Y.\end{cases}

(15)

The genus of
GGS(q,n)
is
\frac{1}{2}(q-1)(q^{n+1}+q^{n}-q^{2})
.
The GGS curve is the first generalization of the GK curve
[
18
]
. When
n=3
, the curve
GGS(q,3)
is a GK curve; see
[
19
]
.
The curve is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
-maximal. A plane model for the curve
GGS(q,n)
can be given by

y^{q^{n}+1}=(x^{q}+x)((x^{q}+x)^{q-1}-1)^{q+1}.

(16)

Note that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
is a Kummer extension.
We write

(x^{q}+x)((x^{q}+x)^{q-1}-1)^{q+1}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q}(x-\alpha_{i})\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q^{2}-q}(x-\beta_{i})^{q+1}\right),

where
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{q},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{q^{2}-q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.
Let
P_{\infty}
be the pole of
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
. For each
1\leq i\leq q
, let
P_{i}
be the zero of
x-\alpha_{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
.
It is obvious that
P_{\infty}
and
P_{i}(1\leq i\leq q)
are totally ramified in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
.
Denote by
Q_{\infty}
the only place lying over
P_{\infty}
, and by
Q_{i}
the only place lying over
P_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq q
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
1\leq k\leq q
and
m=(q^{n}+1)/(q+1)
. Then

G(Q_{k})=\left\{(q^{n}+1)j+i\mid 1\leq i\leq q^{n},\,0\leq j\leq q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor-2\right\},

and

H(Q_{k})=\left\langle q^{n}+1,(q^{n}+1)\left(q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor-1\right)+i:1\leq i\leq q^{n}\right\rangle.

Moreover,
H(Q_{k})
is symmetric if and only if
n=3
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle~q\left\lceil\frac{i}{q^{n}+1}\right\rceil+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{q^{n}+1}\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac{-iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor.

Thus, by Proposition
3.6
and Corollary
3.8
, we have

G(Q_{k})=\left\{(q^{n}+1)j+i\mid 1\leq i\leq q^{n},\,0\leq j\leq q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor-2\right\},

and

H(Q_{k})=\left\langle q^{n}+1,(q^{n}+1)\left(q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor-1\right)+i:1\leq i\leq q^{n}\right\rangle.

By Theorem
3.10
, we get that
H(Q_{k})
is symmetric if and only if
-q^{3}\equiv 1\pmod{q^{n}+1}
. Thus
H(Q_{k})
is symmetric if and only if
n=3
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose that
n=3
. Then for each
1\leq s\leq q
,

\displaystyle G(Q_{\infty})=G(Q_{s})=\{\,

\displaystyle j(q^{3}+1)+k(q^{2}-q+1)+t\mid

\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq q,\,1\leq t\leq q^{2}-q+1,\,0\leq j\leq q^{2}-1-t-k\}.

Proof.

Since
-q^{3}\equiv 1\pmod{q^{3}+1}
, it follows from Corollary
3.7
that
H(Q_{\infty})=H(Q_{s})
for
1\leq s\leq q
.
Note that

\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{3}+1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}+i-i}{q^{3}+1}\right\rfloor=i+\left\lfloor\frac{-i}{q^{3}+1}\right\rfloor=i-1

for each
1\leq i\leq q^{3}
.
By Proposition
5.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle G(Q_{s})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left\{(q^{3}+1)j+i\mid 1\leq i\leq q^{3},\,0\leq j\leq q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{q^{2}-q+1}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{3}+1}\right\rfloor-2\right\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left\{(q^{3}+1)j+i\mid 1\leq i\leq q^{3},\,0\leq j\leq-i+q-1+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{q^{2}-q+1}\right\rceil\right\}.

Let
i=k(q^{2}-q+1)+t
, where
0\leq k\leq q
and
1\leq t\leq q^{2}-q+1
. Then

-i+q-1+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{q^{2}-q+1}\right\rceil=q-1+q^{2}-q-t-k=q^{2}-1-t-k.

The desired result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
m=(q^{n}+1)/(q+1)
and

2\leq s\leq\min\left\{q,\max_{1\leq i\leq q^{n}}\left\{q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor\right\}\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\Big\{(

\displaystyle(q^{n}+1)j_{1}+i,\cdots,(q^{n}+1)j_{s}+i)\mid 1\leq i\leq q^{n},

\displaystyle j_{k}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq k\leq s,\,\sum_{k=1}^{s}j_{k}=q-s+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor\Big\}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle~q\left\lceil\frac{i}{q^{n}+1}\right\rceil+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{q^{n}+1}\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac{-iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle q+(q^{2}-q)\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{iq^{3}}{q^{n}+1}\right\rfloor.

Setting
\lambda=1
in Corollary
4.2
, the desired result follows.
∎

5.3
Function fields of subcovers of the BM curve

Let
q
be a prime power and let
n\geq 3
be an odd integer. Let
m
be a divisor of
q^{n}+1
and
d
be a divisor of
q+1
such that
\gcd(m,d(q-1))=1
.
In this subsection, we study the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
-maximal curve
\mathcal{Y}_{d,m}
defined by the affine equation

\mathcal{Y}_{d,m}:~~y^{m}=x^{d}(x^{d}-1)\left(\frac{1-x^{d(q-1)}}{x^{d}-1}\right)^{q+1}.

(17)

This curve was introduced in
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
, and it is a subcover of the BM curve given by Beelen and Montanucci in
[
9
]
. Note that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
is a Kummer extension.
We write

x^{d}(x^{d}-1)\left(\frac{1-x^{d(q-1)}}{x^{d}-1}\right)^{q+1}=x^{d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{i})\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d(q-2)}(x-\beta_{i})^{q+1}\right),

where
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{d(q-2)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.
Let
P_{\infty}
be the pole of
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
. For each
1\leq i\leq d
, let
P_{i}
be the zero of
x-\alpha_{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
.
It is obvious that
P_{i}

(1\leq i\leq d)
are totally ramified in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}(x)
. Denote by
Q_{i}
the only place lying over
P_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proposition 5.8
.

(i) Let
1\leq k\leq d
. Then
G(Q_{k})
is given by

\displaystyle\Big\{mj+i\mid

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq m-1,0\leq j\leq d+\left\lceil\frac{di}{m}\right\rceil+d(q-2)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{idq(q-1)}{m}\right\rfloor-2\Big\},

and
H(Q_{k})
is given by

\displaystyle\Big\langle m,m\left(d+\left\lceil\frac{di}{m}\right\rceil+d(q-2)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{idq(q-1)}{m}\right\rfloor-1\right)+i:1\leq i\leq m-1\Big\rangle.

(ii) Let

2\leq s\leq\min\left\{d,\max_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\left\{d+\left\lceil\frac{di}{m}\right\rceil+d(q-2)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{idq(q-1)}{m}\right\rfloor\right\}\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\Big\{(

\displaystyle mj_{1}+i,\cdots,mj_{s}+i)\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1,\,j_{k}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq k\leq s,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{s}j_{k}=d-s+\left\lceil\frac{di}{m}\right\rceil+d(q-2)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{idq(q-1)}{m}\right\rfloor\Big\}.

Proof.

Note that
d+d+d(q-2)(q+1)=dq(q-1)
. We obtain

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle d+\left\lceil\frac{di}{m}\right\rceil+d(q-2)\left\lceil\frac{i(q+1)}{m}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\frac{idq(q-1)}{m}\right\rfloor.

Then the first statement follows from Proposition
3.6
and Corollary
3.8
.
The second statement follows by setting

in Corollary

.
