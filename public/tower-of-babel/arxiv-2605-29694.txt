Tripartite Interactions Induced Strongly Correlated Quantum Emissions
Qian Bin, Ying Wu, Franco Nori, Xin-You Lü

Abstract
Efficient generation of multiquanta emission is crucial for quantum information processing but remains challenging due to its typical reliance on higher-order quantum processes. Here, we theoretically demonstrate strongly correlated photon–phonon emission enabled by direct tripartite interaction. This interaction facilitates the formation of high-order multiquanta states without more intermediate state transitions, thereby avoiding the suppressed transition rates associated with multiple sequential processes and substantially improving resonant transitions. As a result, high-efficiency strongly correlated even-quanta emission (e.g., two photons and two phonons) can be achieved in the presences of dissipation. Beyond that, we show that introducing two-photon dissipation enables strongly correlated odd-quanta emission (e.g., two photons and one phonon) in the tripartite interaction system by parity-protected suppression of single-photon loss and reconstruction of higher-order multiquanta processes. Our work extends multiquanta emission into the tripartite coupling regime and holds promising potential for applications in hybrid quantum networks.

Conclusion
.—We have shown that direct photon–phonon–atom tripartite interaction provides an efficient mechanism for generating enhanced multiquanta emission in a cavity–atom–mechanical system. By directly coupling photon and phonon excitations at the single-quantum level, the tripartite interaction enables resonant multiquanta transitions through reduced intermediate-state pathways, leading to enhanced transition efficiency and strongly correlated even-quanta emission. Furthermore, engineered two-photon dissipation reshapes the emission dynamics by suppressing single-photon loss channels and promoting higher-order correlated emission processes. This proposal provides a route for odd-quanta emission, tailoring emission properties in hybrid quantum platforms, paving a promising way for developing controllable quantum sources for on-chip quantum communication and hybrid quantum network applications

\omega_{L}
, Eq. (
S3
) becomes

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=(\omega_{a}-{\omega_{L}})a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{\sigma}-{\omega_{L}})\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\lambda_{ab\sigma}(a^{\dagger}\sigma+a\sigma^{\dagger})(b^{\dagger}+b)+\Omega(\sigma+\sigma^{\dagger})+\Omega_{p}(a^{{\dagger}2}+a^{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{aL}a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\sigma L}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\lambda_{ab\sigma}(a^{\dagger}\sigma+a\sigma^{\dagger})(b^{\dagger}+b)+\Omega(\sigma+\sigma^{\dagger})+\Omega_{p}(a^{{\dagger}2}+a^{2}),

(S4)

where
\Delta_{aL}=\omega_{a}-{\omega_{L}}
and
\Delta_{\sigma L}=\omega_{\sigma}-{\omega_{L}}
. We next apply a unitary squeezing transformation

\displaystyle U=\exp[r(a^{{\dagger}2}-a^{2})],

(S5)

with the squeezing parameter determined by

\displaystyle\tanh(4r)=2\Omega/\Delta_{aL}.

(S6)

Neglecting constant energy shifts, the transformed Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\sigma L}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{aL}\sinh(4r_{s})(a^{{\dagger}2}+a^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{aL}\cosh(4r_{s})(2a^{{\dagger}}a+1)-\frac{1}{2}-\Omega\sinh(4r_{s})(2a^{{\dagger}}a+1)

\displaystyle~~~~-\lambda_{ab\sigma}\sinh(2r_{s})(a^{\dagger}\sigma^{\dagger}+a\sigma)(b^{\dagger}+b)+\lambda_{ab\sigma}\cosh(2r_{s})(a^{\dagger}\sigma+a\sigma^{\dagger})(b^{\dagger}+b)+\Omega\cosh(4r_{s})(a^{{\dagger}2}+a^{2})\,

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Delta_{aL}^{2}-4\Omega_{p}^{2}}a^{{\dagger}}a+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\sigma L}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b-\lambda_{ab\sigma}\sinh(2r)(a^{\dagger}\sigma^{\dagger}+a\sigma)(b^{\dagger}+b)\,

\displaystyle~~~~+\lambda_{ab\sigma}\cosh(2r)(a^{\dagger}\sigma+a\sigma^{\dagger})(b^{\dagger}+b)+\Omega(\sigma+\sigma^{\dagger})\,

(S7)

where
\Delta_{a}=\sqrt{\Delta_{aL}^{2}-4\Omega_{p}^{2}}
,
\Delta_{\sigma}=\Delta_{\sigma L}
,
\lambda=\lambda_{ab\sigma}\cosh(2r)
, and
\lambda^{\prime}=-\lambda_{ab\sigma}\sinh(2r)
. The counter-rotating terms
a^{\dagger}\sigma^{\dagger}
and
a\sigma
describe the non-conserving of excitation number and can be neglected under the condition
|\lambda^{\prime}|/(\Delta_{a}+\Delta_{\sigma})\ll 1
, corresponding to the rotating-wave approximation. The effective Hamiltonian is then given by

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{a}a^{{\dagger}}a+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\sigma}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\lambda(a^{\dagger}\sigma+a\sigma^{\dagger})(b^{\dagger}+b)+\Omega(\sigma+\sigma^{\dagger}),

(S8)

where
a
denotes the annihilation operator of the squeezed cavity mode. In the strong-squeezing regime
2r\gg 1
, the effective atom-cavity coupling strength can be significantly enhanced as

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda_{ab\sigma}\cosh(2r)\approx\lambda_{ab\sigma}e^{2r}/2.

(S9)

II
Derivation of multiquanta resonance conditions within the Mollow regime

In the strong driving regime, the laser field dresses the two-level atom, and the driven-atom subsystem forms two new eigenstates that are quantum superposition of the bare states
\{|e\rangle,|g\rangle\}
. In the frame rotating at the driving frequency
\omega_{L}
, the Hamiltonian of the strongly driven two-level atom is given by

\displaystyle H_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\sigma}\sigma_{z}+\Omega(\sigma+\sigma^{\dagger}),

(S10)

with the corresponding dressed eigenstates

\displaystyle|+\rangle=c_{+}|g\rangle+c_{-}|e\rangle,~~~~~~|-\rangle=c_{-}|g\rangle-c_{+}|e\rangle,

(S11)

where the coefficients are

\displaystyle c_{\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{2\Omega^{2}}{(\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}+4\Omega^{2})\pm\Delta_{\sigma}\sqrt{\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}+4\Omega^{2}}}},

(S12)

satisfying
|c_{+}|^{2}+|c_{-}|^{2}=1
. The corresponding eigenenergies are

\displaystyle E_{|\pm\rangle}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}+4\Omega^{2}}.

(S13)

For resonant driving,
\Delta_{\sigma}=\omega_{\sigma}-\omega_{L}=0
, the dressed eigenstates reduce to

\displaystyle|\pm\rangle=(|g\rangle\pm|e\rangle)/\sqrt{2},

(S14)

with corresponding eigenenergies
E_{\pm}=\pm\Omega
. Transforming the effective Hamiltonian in Eq.(
S8
) into the dressed-state basis
\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\to\tilde{\sigma}_{z},~~\sigma_{z}\to\tilde{\sigma}_{x},~~\sigma_{y}\to-\tilde{\sigma}_{y}.

(S15)

As a result, Eq.(
S8
) becomes

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{a}a^{{\dagger}}a+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\Omega\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda[(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger}+ab)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b-ab^{\dagger}-ab)(\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\sigma})],

(S16)

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{z}=|+\rangle\langle+|-|-\rangle\langle-|
and
\tilde{\sigma}=|-\rangle\langle+|
. Together with the photon and phonon modes, and neglecting the influence of the tripartite interaction on the energy structure, the eigenenergies of the system Hamiltonian can be given by

\displaystyle E_{|n_{a}n_{b}\pm\rangle}=n_{a}\Delta_{a}+n_{b}\omega_{b}\pm\Omega,

(S17)

where
n_{a},n_{b}=0,1,2,\dots
. To realize multiquanta resonant transitions, the total energy of
n_{a}
photons and
n_{b}
phonons matches the energy difference between the dressed states
|+\rangle
and
|-\rangle
, i.e.,
E_{|00+\rangle}=E_{|n_{a}n_{b}-\rangle}
, then

\displaystyle n_{a}\Delta_{a}+n_{b}\omega_{b}-2\Omega\approx 0,

(S18)

thus the driving amplitude
\Omega\approx(n_{a}\Delta_{a}+n_{b}\omega_{b})/2
. Under this resonance condition, the initial state
|00+\rangle
can be resonantly excited to the state
|n_{a}n_{b}-\rangle
. However, not all such transitions are allowed even when the resonance condition is satisfied. Owing to the parity symmetry of the two-boson-mode interaction Hamiltonian, only transitions to states with an even total number of excitations from the initial state
|00+\rangle
are permitted. Consequently, the state
|00+\rangle
can be resonantly coupled only to even-excitation states
|n_{a}n_{b}-\rangle
satisfying
n_{a}+n_{b}\geq 2
and
n_{a}+n_{b}
even. Representative examples of such multiquanta resonant transitions, namely
|00+\rangle\rightarrow|11-\rangle
and
|00+\rangle\rightarrow|22-\rangle
, are shown in Figs. 2(d) and 2(e) of the main text.

III
Derivation of effective multiquanta transition rates

In the above section, we have derived the effective Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{a}a^{{\dagger}}a+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\Omega\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda[(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger}+ab)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b-ab^{\dagger}-ab)(\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\sigma})],

(S19)

where the first three terms constitute the free Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, while the remaining terms form the interaction Hamiltonian

\displaystyle V=\lambda[(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger}+ab)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b-ab^{\dagger}-ab)(\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\sigma})]/2,

(S20)

which is responsible for inducing state transitions. To derive the multiquanta effective transition rates, we treat the interaction Hamiltonian
V
as a perturbation under the condition
\lambda\ll\Delta_{a}+\omega_{b},2\Omega
. According to the perturbation theory, the transition rate between an initial sate
|i\rangle
and a finial state
|f\rangle
can be given by

\displaystyle W_{|i\rangle\to|f\rangle}^{\rm eff}=2\pi|V^{\rm eff}|^{2}\delta(E_{|f\rangle}-E_{|i\rangle}),

(S21)

where
E_{|i\rangle}
and
E_{|f\rangle}
denote the energies of the initial state
|i\rangle
and final state
|f\rangle
, respectively, and
V^{\rm eff}
is the effective coupling strength connecting the initial state
|i\rangle
and final state
|f\rangle
. For resonant transition from
|i\rangle
to
|f\rangle
, the transition rate
W_{|i\rangle\to|f\rangle}^{\rm eff}
is determined predominantly by the effective coupling strength
V^{\rm eff}
: a larger effective coupling leads to a higher transition rate.

If the initial and final states are directly coupled through a first-order perturbation process, the effective coupling strength is simply given by

\displaystyle V^{\rm eff}=V_{fi}=\langle f|V|i\rangle.

(S22)

If the coupling occurs only via second- or third-order perturbation processes, the corresponding effective coupling strengths are determined by high-order perturbation theory, involving one or more intermediate states. For the second- or third-order processes, the corresponding effective coupling strengths are given, respectively, by
Ma2015Law
;
Garziano2016MS

\displaystyle V^{\rm eff}=\sum_{n}\frac{\langle f|V|n\rangle\langle n|V|i\rangle}{E_{|i\rangle}-E_{|n\rangle}},

(S23)

and

\displaystyle V^{\rm eff}=\sum_{m,n}\frac{\langle f|V|n\rangle\langle n|V|m\rangle\langle m|V|i\rangle}{(E_{|i\rangle}-E_{|n\rangle})(E_{|i\rangle}-E_{|m\rangle})},

(S24)

where the states
|n\rangle
and
|m\rangle
are intermediate states involved in the transition pathways. More generally, if the initial and final states are connected only through higher-order perturbative processes, the effective coupling strength
V^{\rm eff}
can be obtained in an analogous manner. The resulting effective interaction between the two resonant states can therefore be described by the reduced two-level Hamiltonian of the form

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}^{\prime\prime}=V^{\rm eff}|f\rangle\langle i|+{\rm h.c.}.

(S25)

For the resonant transition from
|00+\rangle
to
|11-\rangle
, the process can be induced directly by the tripartite interaction and does not involve any intermediate states, i.e.,
a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\tilde{\sigma}|00+\rangle\to|11-\rangle
, then the resulting effective coupling strength can be obtained from first-order perturbation theory and is given by

\displaystyle V_{11}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\langle 11-|V|00+\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 11-|\frac{1}{2}\lambda[(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger}+ab)\tilde{\sigma}_{z}+(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b-ab^{\dagger}-ab)(\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\sigma})]|00+\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda,

(S26)

where
V_{11}^{\rm eff}\propto\lambda
, since the transition occurs directly without involving any intermediate states. Thus, the effective multiquanta transition rate from
|00+\rangle
to
|11-\rangle
is

\displaystyle W_{11}^{\rm eff}=2\pi|V_{11}^{\rm eff}|=\frac{1}{2}\pi\lambda^{2}.

(S27)

In contrast, the resonant transition from
|00+\rangle
to
|22-\rangle
occurs predominantly through two second-order transition pathways, i.e.,
|00+\rangle\to|11+\rangle\to|22-\rangle
and
|00+\rangle\to|11-\rangle\to|22-\rangle
, each involving an intermediate state. Consequently, the effective coupling strength for this process needs to be evaluated using second-order perturbation theory. The resulting effective coupling is given by the sum of the contributions from both transition pathways, with

\displaystyle V_{22}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{\langle f|V|n\rangle\langle n|V|i\rangle}{E_{|i\rangle}-E_{|n\rangle}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle 22-|V|11+\rangle\langle 11+|V|00+\rangle}{E_{|00+\rangle}-E_{|11+\rangle}}+\frac{\langle 22-|V|11-\rangle\langle 11-|V|00+\rangle}{E_{|00+\rangle}-E_{|11-\rangle}}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(\Delta_{a}+\omega_{b})}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2[2\Omega-(\Delta_{a}+\omega_{b})]}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\Delta_{a}+\omega_{b}}+\frac{1}{2\Omega-(\Delta_{a}+\omega_{b})}\right],

(S28)

where
V_{22}^{\rm eff}\propto\lambda^{2}
, since each dominant transition pathway involves one intermediate state. The effective multiquanta transition rate from
|00+\rangle
to
|22-\rangle
can thus be given by

\displaystyle W_{22}^{\rm eff}=2\pi|V_{22}^{\rm eff}|=\frac{1}{2}\pi\lambda^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2\Omega-\Delta_{ap}-\omega_{b}}+\frac{1}{\Delta_{ap}+\omega_{b}}\right)^{2}.

(S29)

Figures 3(c) and 3(d) of the main text compare the effective coupling strength
W_{11}^{\rm eff}
(and
W_{22}^{\rm eff}
) between the states
|00+\rangle
and
|11-\rangle
(and
|22-\rangle
) obtained from the analytical expression Eqs. (
S27
) [and Eqs. (
S29
)] via the perturbation theory with results from the full-numerical simulations. The excellent agreement between the two confirms the validity of our approximation.
