Synthesis and Characterization
of Naringin Phenolate
Salts for Cultivated Meat

Matilda Dirawy, Muhammad Abdel-Haq, Sarah E Hasenson, Sara Mahajna, Mohamed Mahameed, M N V Ravi Kumar, Meenakshi Arora, Abraham J Domb

Abstract

The high cost of
growth factors has limited the commercial-scale
production of cultivated meat products. In this study, we report on
the synthesis and characterization of a series of naringin phenolate
salts. Phenolate salts with sodium (Na+), potassium (K+), calcium (Ca2+), and tetrabutylammonium (TBA+) counterions were prepared to introduce water solubility
to the natural flavonoid naringin. The synthesized salts were comprehensively
characterized to confirm their chemical structures. Solvated cations
were examined for their effect on the salts’ physicochemical
and biological properties. Solubility studies show that alkali metal
salts are highly soluble in water, while the Ca2+ and TBA+ salts are less soluble. Acid regeneration studies confirm
the reversibility of the salt formation. Preliminary studies indicate
that selected naringin salts may support cell proliferation in cultivated
meat systems. Overall, this study presents the first comprehensive
report on naringin phenolate salts and their potential use in cultivated
meat production.

Introduction

Naringin is a naturally
occurring polyphenolic secondary metabolite
predominantly found in citrus fruits, particularly grapefruits and
oranges. Chemically, it is a glycosylated
derivative of the flavonoid naringenin, formed by the attachment of
the disaccharide neohesperidose (rhamnose–glucose) to the C-7
hydroxyl group of naringenin
−

 (). Due to the unique position of this glycosidic
linkage, naringin has attracted considerable scientific interest in
recent years, leading to reports that naringin exhibits anti-inflammatory,
antioxidant, hypolipidemic, and anticancer effects, among others.
−

 These biological effects, combined with its natural abundance and
favorable safety profile, have made naringin a promising candidate
for the development of novel therapeutic and nutraceutical formulations.
Cultivated meat is part of an emerging technology for producing meat
in a more sustainable way. One of the aspects of cultivated meat production
is the scale-up of cell growth by using growth-promoting factors (GPFs). Despite biological advantages, the use of growth
factors such as Fibroblast Growth Factor 2 (FGF2) remains impractical
for large-scale cultivated meat applications due to its high cost,
dosage requirement, and short shelf life.
−

 Studies show that polyphenols
may interact with pathways mediated by Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptors
(FGFRs), partially recapitulating the effect of FGF2 and representing
a more stable, cost-effective means of supporting cell growth in a
serum-free culture.
,
 In our recent report, we show
that polyphenol salts, including naringenin and quercetin derivatives,
do mimic the activity of growth factors. Specifically, potassium naringenin (K-Ng) and sodium quercetin (Na-Q)
were shown to promote aggregate growth in serum-free culture in the
absence of FGF2. Inspired by these findings
and considering that naringin is a glycosylated derivative of naringenin,
we hypothesize that naringin and its salt derivatives may promote
cell proliferation for cultivated meat.

Despite the numerous
biological activities attributed
to naringin,
poor solubility of around 0.5 mg/mL in water, together with its low
bioavailability, limit its further utilization in clinical applications.
,
 Various strategies, including prodrug design, chemical modification,
particle size reduction, and advanced drug delivery systems, have
been explored in the literature to improve the solubility and bioavailability
of drugs. While these approaches have
shown promise, their widespread application is often limited by challenges
related to scalability, high manufacturing costs, formulation complexity,
and potential toxicity concerns. In recent years, our laboratory has
developed a facile and economical approach for enhancing the aqueous
solubility of polyphenolic compounds through their conversion into
phenolate salts.
−

 The current study represents a significant iteration and advancement
of that established methodology. Phenolic groups are weakly acidic
in nature. Upon deprotonation, their conjugate bases (phenolate ions)
are stabilized through resonance delocalization of the negative charge. This structural feature enhances their polarity
and aqueous solubility, which may, in turn, improve their bioavailability
and biological activity. Since naringin is a naturally occurring dietary
flavonoid widely consumed via citrus fruits, its derived alkali metal
salts present a highly viable pathway toward achieving Generally Recognized
as Safe (GRAS) status.
,
 This regulatory feasibility has
a crucial advantage for the seamless integration of these cost-effective
media components into commercial-scale food production systems.

In the present study, naringin phenolate salts (Na+,
K+ , Ca2+ and TBA+) were synthesized
by the reactions of naringin with their corresponding hydroxides (NaOH,
KOH, Ca­(OH)2, or TBAOH) in either aqueous or alcoholic
medium. While phenolate salt formation has been reported for simple
phenolic compounds, studies involving structurally complex polyphenols,
such as naringin, remain limited. Because of its multiple hydroxyl
groups and glycosidic moieties, naringin is an interesting candidate
for salt formation and functional modification.

This study synthesizes
and comprehensively characterizes naringin
salts and evaluates their physicochemical properties, with particular
emphasis on aqueous solubility and stability. Structural and thermal
analyses were performed on the synthesized salts using proton nuclear
magnetic resonance (1H NMR), Fourier-transform infrared
spectroscopy (FT-IR), elemental analysis, and thermogravimetric analysis
(TGA). Acid regeneration studies were also performed to confirm the
reversibility of salt formation. In addition, the solubilities of
the salts were assessed in various aqueous media.

Following
comprehensive chemical characterization, the salts were
evaluated for cytocompatibility using a 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium
bromide (MTT) assay. Finally, the synthesized salts were evaluated
for their ability to stimulate the proliferation of embryonic chicken
stem cells through cell counting, cell viability analysis, and population
doubling level (PDL) calculations. These preliminary experiments serve
as a proof of concept to assess the potential of the naringin salts
to support cell proliferation.

Acid-Back-Generation
Experiments

Naringin content in the synthesized salts was
assessed through acid-back-generation
experiments. Treatment of the salts with 1 N HCl or acetic acid promoted
reprotonation and precipitation of the parent naringin, while the
metal ion remained in solution. The regenerated naringin was extracted
with ethanol and quantified by UV–visible spectroscopy, using
calibration curves in ethanol solution (Figure S1, Supporting Information).

MTT Assay

Cell viability was evaluated
using the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide
(MTT) assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific), which measures cellular metabolic
activity as an indicator of viable cells. Human hepatocellular carcinoma
cells (HepG2) were seeded onto the flat-bottomed, 96-well plates at
a density of 1 × 104 cells per well in 100 μL
of complete culture medium and allowed to adhere overnight. Cells
were subsequently treated with naringin (200 μM) for 24 and
48 h. Following treatment, the culture medium was replaced with 100
μL of fresh medium, and 40 μL of MTT solution (1.25 mg/mL
in phosphate-buffered saline. PBS was added to each well. Plates were
incubated for 5 h at 37 °C to allow the formation of formazan
crystals. After incubation, 115 μL of the medium was carefully
removed from each well, and 100 μL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)
was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. The plates were then
incubated at 37 °C for an additional 10 min with gentle shaking.
Metabolic activity was determined by measuring the absorbance at 540
nm, using a BioTek Synergy H1 microplate reader (Agilent Technologies).
Metabolic activity was expressed relative to untreated control cells
and calculated according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
Statistical analysis for the MTT assay was performed using GraphPad
Prism software with data from four biologically independent replicates
and using a two-tailed, unpaired Student’s t test. The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Results and Discussion

Synthesis of Naringin Salts

Na and
K naringin salts were successfully synthesized by reacting with stoichiometric
ratios of 1:4 and 1:3 (NaOH and KOH, respectively) in water/ethanol
cosolvent systems (1:29.4 v/v for Na; 1:4 v/v for K) at room temperature
for 24 h ().
TBA-naringin salt was prepared by reacting with a 1:2 stoichiometric
ratio of naringin and tetrabutylammonium hydroxide in pure ethanol
under identical conditions. The calcium (Ca2+) salt of
naringin was obtained using a 1:1.5 stoichiometric ratio of naringin
to calcium hydroxide in water.

Characterization

Proton Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (1H NMR) Spectroscopy

The structural integrity and
coordination environment of the naringin salts were evaluated using 1H NMR spectroscopy in dimethyl sulfoxide-d
6 (DMSO-d
6) (). Additionally, spectra for
the alkali metal (Na and K) salts were recorded in deuterium oxide
(D2O) to confirm stability (Supporting Information, Figure S2).

Base-Induced FlavanoneChalcone Tautomerization:
The carbonyl
α-carbon protons of naringin have weakly acidic protons, which
can be deprotonated under strong basic conditions to generate a resonance-stabilized
enolate intermediate that tautomerizes to yield the final product.
Treatment of naringin under strong basic conditions (pH > 11) induces
a flavonoid-chalcone tautomerization, or the opening of the flavanone
ring of naringin, to yield its corresponding chalcone structure. This
phenomenon has been well documented in the literature. The formation of a chalcone structure was confirmed
by Heteronuclear Single Quantum Coherence (HSQC) of the product (), with both spectra
displaying NMR features characteristic of extended conjugated systems.
However, for the metal naringin salts that were acidified, the open-chain
chalcone structure was found to go intramolecularly back to the flavanone
(naringin), showing the reversibility of the interconversion of the
two forms at a particular pH. Ring opening introduces a second phenolic
hydroxyl group that is also deprotonated by very strong bases, further
stabilizing the conjugated system. This is consistent with the spectral
observations in the study. The proposed mechanism for the base-induced
ring opening and subsequent tautomerization is shown in .

Fourier-Transform
Infrared Spectroscopy
(FT-IR)

The FT-IR spectra of naringin salts were recorded
and compared to those of naringin (Figure S3). In most salts, a reduction in the intensity of the hydroxyl stretching
band was observed, which indicates deprotonation. In all of the salts,
however, new stretching bands appeared in the hydroxyl region (3200–3600
cm–1), suggesting the presence of bound water. This
was further confirmed by elemental analysis results. For naringin
metal salts, characteristic bands corresponding to the C–H
bonds were stretched at 2917–2930 cm–1. In
the case of TBA naringin, the aliphatic C–H bond stretching
was observed at 2961–2874 cm–1. In the Na,
K, and Ca naringin salts, the disappearance of the CO stretching
band may result from electron delocalization that reduces the double-bond
character of the carbonyl group.

Acid
Back-Generation and Reversibility Studies

Since phenolate
salts are characterized by labile ionic bonds,
they undergo protonation in aqueous acidic media to revert to their
neutral phenolic forms. Evaluating this capacity for reprotonation
serves as a robust validation of the ionic nature of the synthesized
products. Since these phenolate salts contain a precise stoichiometric
fraction of the parent polyphenol, isolating and quantifying the recovered
phenolic fraction upon acidification offer definitive evidence of
successful salt synthesis while validating the proposed molecular
formulas and calculated molecular weights.

The recovery efficiencies
for the inorganic naringin salts (Na, K, and Ca) were in close agreement
with the theoretical values predicted by their stoichiometric formulas
(). Complete
quantitative conversion was marginally limited, which is attributed
to competing tautomerization pathways and the structural influence
of native glycosidic bonds. The structural identity and purity of
the regenerated free naringin were confirmed via 1H NMR
and FT-IR spectroscopic analyses (Figure S4 and S5)

Conversely, the organic tetrabutylammonium
(TBA) salt
could not
be successfully converted back to its parent acidic form. This distinct
behavior is attributed to the pronounced steric hindrance imposed
by the bulky, hydrophobic TBA cation, which effectively obstructs
hydronium ion access to the phenolate target sites and prevents reprotonation.

closure is depicted in .

Elemental
Analysis

Elemental composition
analysis (C, H, N, and O) was performed to elucidate the empirical
formulas of the synthesized naringin salts and to quantify the number
of coordinated water molecules within their respective crystal lattices.
Minor deviations between the experimental and theoretical carbon and
hydrogen weight fractions were observed across the metal phenolate
series (). These
variations are inherently characteristic of polyphenolic architectures
like naringin, which feature multiple chemically distinct phenolic
hydroxyl groups possessing unique pK
a values.
Under basic conditions, competing deprotonation pathways can result
in microheterogeneity regarding the exact coordination states or localized
hydration levels across the bulk material. Consequently, the elemental
data reflect a statistically dominant, average stoichiometric composition
for the isolated salts. This closely aligns with the complex coordination
equilibria frequently documented for flavonoid-metal complexes, confirming
successful bulk modification.

Thermal Analysis

The melting points
of the naringin Na and K salts were >350 °C, which is significantly
higher than the melting temperature of unmodified naringin (164–173
°C) and naringin-TBA salt (105–110 °C) (Table S1, Figure S6). Thermogravimetric analysis
(TGA) of the naringin Na and K salts indicates a 12.5% weight loss
between 70 and 150 °C, which can be attributed to the evaporation
of lattice-bound water molecules.

Solubility
Studies

The solubility
profiles of the various naringin salts in Double Distilled Water (DDW),
phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.2), acidic buffer (pH 4), and
Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) media (containing 10%
FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S)) are summarized in and Table S2. Naringin salts bearing alkali metal
counterions (Na+/K+) exhibite good solubility
in DDW, whereas Ca2+ salts show poor aqueous solubility.
This reduced solubility of calcium salts can be attributed to the
formation of oligomeric complexes in water and physiological media.
In contrast, TBA naringin salts display increased lipophilicity due
to the presence of bulky organic cations with long hydrocarbon chains,
resulting in decreased solubility in PBS and DDW. Overall, naringin
salts demonstrate moderate solubility in PBS and DMEM culture media.
Notably, all alkali metal salts show substantially improved aqueous
solubility compared to unmodified naringin.

MTT Assay

HepG2 cells were used as
a preliminary and reproducible mammalian cell model to screen the
cellular metabolic response to the newly synthesized naringin salts
and identify potential cytotoxic effects. The MTT assay reveals time-dependent
changes in metabolic activity following treatment with free naringin
and its corresponding salts (). Free naringin reduces metabolic activity relative
to the untreated control, whereas several of its corresponding salts
produce higher MTT signals, particularly after 48 h of exposure (A). Qualitative microscopic
examination also indicates an apparent increase in cell density in
some salt-treated groups. Collectively, these findings suggest that
selected naringin salts enhance the cellular metabolic activity and
may support a growth-promoting cellular response. This is consistent
with previous findings, demonstrating the growth-promoting potential
of selected polyphenol salts in primary cell cultures. These observations provided a rationale for
further evaluation of the synthesized salts in a more application-relevant
cellular model.

Effect of Naringin Salts on Viability and
Proliferation

After identifying the Naringin salts that showed
increased metabolic activity, as determined by the MTT assay, we next
evaluated their potential to support cell proliferation relative to
commercially available FGF2 in the cultivated meat application. In
a representative experiment (n = 2), we evaluated the viability of
primary EChSCs under conditions using either standard FGF2 or Na/K-Naringin.
The EChSCs cultured with Na-Naringin in the absence of FGF2 were almost
100% viable in cell culture, and cells treated with K-Naringin showed
100.15% viability relative to control (A).

As a proof-of-concept, the cumulative
population doubling levels (PDLs) of cells treated with Na-naringin
or commercial FGF2 were monitored over 6 days. In this representative
experiment (n = 2), Na-Naringin maintained cell viability and supported
proliferative capacity, as reflected by the PDL values, at levels
comparable to those observed with commercially available FGF2 (B).

Similar to Na-naringin, K-naringin maintained high
cell viability
in the absence of commercial FGF2. In addition, relative cell proliferation
was assessed following treatment with either commercial FGF2 or K-naringin.
In this preliminary experiment, a higher relative proliferation value
was observed in K-naringin-treated cells compared with the commercial
FGF2-treated reference condition (C).

In a representative cell count
experiment (n = 1), Na-Naringin-treated
cells showed higher cell numbers than the commercial FGF2-treated
reference condition at most time points until near the end of the
experimental period. In contrast, K-Naringin supported initial increases
in cell number but exhibited a reduction in growth rate after approximately
one and a half weeks, resulting in lower cell numbers at later time
points (A). These preliminary observations suggest
different growth profiles for Na-Naringin and K-Naringin over the
experimental period. Na-Naringin-treated cultures showed relatively
sustained cell numbers, whereas K-Naringin-treated cultures showed
a reduction in cell numbers at later time points. In K-Naringin-treated
cells, proliferation was initially observed. After about one and a
half weeks, however, the rate of cell division decreased, leading
to a reduced number of cells in the end of the experiment. In accordance
with these findings, cells treated with Na-Naringin had considerably
greater PDL values across all passages, indicating that Na-Naringin-treated
cells showed higher PDL values across the monitored passages, whereas
K-Naringin-treated cells showed lower PDL values at later stages of
culture (B).

Studies of cell growth
kinetics suggested that
Na-naringin may
have the potential to support cell proliferation with values falling
within the range observed for the commercial FGF2-treated reference
condition.

Additionally, Na-naringin-treated cells showed higher
values than
the FGF2-treated reference condition across the tested conditions,
suggesting that this phenolate salt may support the expansion phase
(Figure S7). In addition, higher accumulated
PDL values were observed in Na-naringin-treated cultures than in the
FGF2-treated reference condition.

Taken together, these preliminary
observations suggest that Na-naringin
may have the potential to support growth performance and replicative
longevity at levels comparable to those observed with FGF2 (Figure S8).

Cell proliferation kinetics
(n = 1) upon treatment with both the
commercial FGF2 preparation and the polyphenol Na-naringin were analyzed
by flow cytometry of cells in control and treatment conditions at
the time of seeding and at each time point before and after treatment.

In this single representative experiment (n = 1), Na-naringin-treated
cells showed a proliferation profile that appeared similar to that
observed under the commercial FGF2-treated reference conditions ().

On the basis of this proof of concept, in vitro experiments
using EChSCs suggest preliminary observations
that alkali
naringin salts, particularly the sodium and potassium salts, may have
the potential to support cell proliferation. These findings warrant
further investigation of their possible applications in cultivated
meat production. Future studies will focus on elucidating the molecular
mechanisms underlying these observations and determining whether these
salts could reduce the requirement for FGF2 or other growth factors,
potentially contributing to the development of more cost-effective
culture media.
