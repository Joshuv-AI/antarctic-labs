 ACCOMPLISHMENTS

1. What are the major goals of the project?

The scientific objectives of the NCCU research group are categorized as:
  § Low-Energy Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The first focuses on measurements that help guide
 the development of theoretical connections between QCD and the low-energy structure of
 nucleons; e.g., chiral effective field theories and lattice QCD calculations. NCCU led experiments
 at the TUNL are providing high-precision data for Compton scattering from unpolarized
 cryogenic liquid targets that contribute significantly to the world database for determining the
 electric and magnetic polarizabilities of the proton and neutron. The goal now is to extend this
 work to perform the first Compton-scattering measurements on 3He. The aim of few-nucleon
 experiments at TUNL is to provide data that are important for evaluating the interactions used in
 ab-initio few-nucleon calculations, and for developing low-energy QCD theories of nuclear
 reactions and structure.
  § Neutrino Physics: The second scientific objective of the NCCU research group focuses on searches for
 physics beyond the Standard Model and nuclear physics interactions important to astrophysics,
 using neutrinos as a probe. Low-energy neutrino-nucleus interaction cross sections play a major
 role in supernova processes and contribute to nuclear physics inputs of models that affect our
 understanding of nuclear composition, neutron stars and neutrino property
 measurements. The initial goal is a suite of first or improved measurements of the low-
 energy (<60 MeV) neutrino charged-current cross sections on I-127, Fe-56, and Pb-208 that will
 constrain theoretical models used to calculate nuclear excitation by neutrinos. Neutrino
 interaction rates on materials of iron and lead will enable dark matter and other sensitive
 experiments to calculate neutrino background interactions and will improve the sensitivity of
 a lead based supernova neutrino detector (HALO). Measurements on iodine will contribute to
 nuclear physics calculations critical for extracting the effective Majorana neutrino mass from
 neutrino-less double beta decay measurements.

2. What is the impact on other disciplines?

The impact on other disciplines include the following development:
1. Low-temperature physics: Cryogenic target
2. High energy-resolution gamma ray measurements: Large Crystals
3. Development of electronics and materials development in fundamental symmetries

3. What is the impact on the development of human resources?

 In addition to the generation of important knowledge for the scientific community, the proposed
collaborative environment
at the Triangle Universities Nuclear Laboratory (TUNL) will enable recruitment and retention of African
American students to graduate physics education in TUNL consortium universities. We graduated three
African--American students who were supported by this award during this reporting period. These students
are now pursing higher education in STEM fields.The skills acquired during the proposed training will
prepare these students to serve at national laboratories to mitigate the scarcity of underrepresented
minorities in STEM
professions. Support for undergraduate students will address the academic retention shortfalls by
providing paid opportunities in mentored research experiences. The proposed research program arranges
for NCCU undergraduate and graduate students to train in-house and at TUNL, and collaborate with the
broader nuclear physics community by attending conferences and workshops. This program will
contribute positively to the production of a diverse and competitive scientific workforce by educating
and training underrepresented minorities in STEM.
4. What is the impact on physical, institutional, and information resources that form infrastructure?

As part of this project, we have now developed a testing and development laboratory for nuclear
detectors, electronics, and computation on the campus of North Carolina Central University. No such
infrastructure existed on campus and our students had to travel to Triangle University Nuclear Laboratory
(TUNL) for testing and development of various detectors, electronics, and mechanical design.
A new machine shop is fully established and staffed.

6. What is the impact on society beyond science and technology?

During the this year, we have provided training to under-represented minority students (URM), in skill
sets relevant to job placement in areas of national needs. These trainings are tuned towards placing
graduating students in the workforce related to sciences at national laboratories, national security,
information systems, and data sciences. The goal of skills acquired during the training mitigate the
scarcity of underrepresented minorities in workforce. The support for undergraduate students will
address the academic retention shortfalls by providing opportunities in mentored research experiences.
This project contributes positively to the production of a diverse and competitive scientific workforce by
educating and training of underrepresented minorities.
