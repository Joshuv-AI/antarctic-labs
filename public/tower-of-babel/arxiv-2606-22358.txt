Gabriel-Type Estimates for Harmonic Quasiregular Mappings and Stoilow Classes
Elver Bajrami

Abstract
Gabriel’s classical theorem bounds the integral of | f | p |f|^{p} over every convex curve in the unit disk by the boundary H p H^{p} norm of an analytic function, with sharp constant 2 2 . Das recently proved a harmonic Hardy-space analogue for p > 1 p>1 , with constant 4 4 when p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . This paper records several quasiregular variants of Gabriel’s inequality and separates the genuinely quantitative estimates from the general maximal-function mechanism behind them. The main estimate concerns sense-preserving harmonic K K -quasiregular mappings. If f = h + g ¯ ∈ h 2 f=h+\overline{g}\in h^{2} and | g ′ | ≤ k ​ | h ′ | |g^{\prime}|\leq k|h^{\prime}| , where K = ( 1 + k ) / ( 1 − k ) K=(1+k)/(1-k) , then every convex curve Γ ⊂ 𝔻 \Gamma\subset\mathbb{D} satisfies ∫ Γ | f ⁡ ( z ) | 2 ​ 𝑑 s ​ ( z ) ≤ 2 ​ ( 1 + k ) 2 1 + k 2 ​ ∫ 𝕋 | f ∗ ​ ( ζ ) | 2 | 𝑑 ζ | = 4 ​ K 2 K 2 + 1 ​ ∫ 𝕋 | f ∗ ​ ( ζ ) | 2 ​ | 𝑑 ζ | . \int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{2}\,ds(z)\leq 2\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|=\frac{4K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|. 1
Introduction

Let

\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\},\qquad\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D},

and let
|d\zeta|
denote arclength measure on
\mathbb{T}
. For
0<p<\infty
, the analytic Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|<\infty.

Gabriel proved that if
f\in H^{p}
and
\Gamma
is a convex curve in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|.

(1)

Here
f^{*}
is the radial boundary function. The constant
2
is sharp; see
[
6
,
5
]
.

A complex-valued harmonic analogue was recently obtained by Das
[
4
]
. If
f\in h^{p}
,
p>1
, and
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
is convex, then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq A_{h}(p)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|,

(2)

where

A_{h}(p)=\begin{cases}4,&p\geq 2,\\[3.0pt]
2\sec^{p}\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2p}\right),&1<p<2.\end{cases}

(3)

Das also showed that the convex-curve inequality does not extend in this general form to the full range
0<p\leq 1
.

The present paper concerns Gabriel-type estimates for planar quasiregular mappings. A mapping
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is
K
-quasiregular if
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{D})
,
J_{f}\geq 0
a.e., and

|Df(z)|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}(z)\qquad\text{for a.e. }z\in\mathbb{D}.

(4)

Equivalently, in complex notation,
|f_{\bar{z}}|\leq k|f_{z}|
a.e., where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)
. Background on quasiregular and quasiconformal mappings may be found in
[
11
,
3
,
1
,
2
]
.

The most direct radial analogue of
H^{p}
is the class

\mathcal{H}^{p}_{qr,K}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{f\in\operatorname{QR}_{K}(\mathbb{D}):\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|<\infty\right\}.

(5)

This radial condition alone is not the correct endpoint for a boundary-value version of Gabriel’s inequality: unlike analytic functions, general quasiregular mappings satisfying (
5
) need not have almost everywhere boundary values. Thus a literal inequality with the right-hand side

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|

requires additional boundary assumptions. This point is essential in the quasiregular setting and motivates the formulation used below.

The guiding point is that there is no single automatic quasiregular analogue of Gabriel’s boundary inequality on the full radial Hardy class. Additional boundary control is needed. The paper therefore has three purposes.

(i)

We give a general maximal-function principle. If a quasiregular class has non-tangential boundary values and an
L^{p}
non-tangential maximal estimate, then Gabriel-type inequalities follow immediately from the Carleson-measure geometry of convex curves.

(ii)

We identify two concrete subclasses where better information is available: mappings with log-subharmonic modulus, where Gabriel’s sharp constant
2
is retained, and harmonic
K
-quasiregular mappings, where the case
p=2
admits a
K
-dependent improvement of Das’s general harmonic constant.

(iii)

We record Stoilow-factorization criteria under explicit boundary distortion hypotheses. These results are conditional but useful: they show precisely what kind of boundary control is sufficient to transfer analytic Gabriel estimates through a quasiconformal change of variables.

The main new quantitative estimate is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in h^{2}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|\qquad(0\leq k<1).

Normalize the decomposition by taking
g(0)=0
, and put
K=(1+k)/(1-k)
. Then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{2}\,ds(z)\leq 2\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|=\frac{4K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|.

For
K=1
this gives the analytic constant
2
. As
K\to\infty
the bound tends to the general harmonic constant
4
in (
3
). We do not claim sharpness for finite
K>1
; this remains open.

Let mention that Gabriel’s problem can also be viewed in connection with Riesz-type inequalities for quasiregular functions. In particular, when the convex curve is specialized to a concentric circle, the resulting estimate is reminiscent of the classical circle-mean estimates appearing in the theory of quasiregular Hardy spaces. A detailed treatment of such Riesz-type inequalities may be found in
[
7
]
.

2
Carleson geometry of convex curves

For an arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
, let
|I|
be its arclength and define the Carleson box

S(I)=\left\{re^{it}:e^{it}\in I,\ 1-\frac{|I|}{2\pi}\leq r<1\right\}.

A finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson measure if

\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}:=\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}<\infty.

Throughout the paper, a convex curve means a simple rectifiable arc, or a closed rectifiable curve, contained in the boundary of a convex subset of
\mathbb{D}
; line segments are allowed. This convention excludes repeated parametrizations, since the relevant measure is arclength on the trace.

Lemma 2.1

(Arclength on convex curves is Carleson)
.

There is an absolute constant
C_{0}
such that, for every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
, the measure

d\mu_{\Gamma}=\,ds|_{\Gamma}

is Carleson and

\|\mu_{\Gamma}\|_{\operatorname{Car}}\leq C_{0}.

Proof.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{T}
. If
|I|\geq 1
, then

\mu_{\Gamma}(S(I))\leq\ell(\Gamma)\leq 2\pi,

since the perimeter of a convex set contained in the unit disk is at most the perimeter of the unit disk. Hence
\mu_{\Gamma}(S(I))\leq 2\pi|I|
in this case.

Suppose now that
0<|I|<1
. The box
S(I)
is contained in a Euclidean disk
B(\xi_{I},c|I|)
, where
\xi_{I}
is the midpoint of
I
and
c
is absolute. We use the elementary fact that the length of a convex curve contained in a disk of radius
R
is bounded by
2\pi R
. More generally, the part of a convex curve lying in such a disk has length at most
CR
with an absolute constant
C
; this follows by applying Cauchy’s projection formula to the convex hull of that part. Consequently

\mu_{\Gamma}(S(I))\leq Cc|I|.

The two estimates give the desired uniform Carleson bound.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

No attempt is made to optimize
C_{0}
. The sharp constant in Gabriel’s analytic theorem does not follow from this Carleson-measure argument; the lemma is used only to obtain robust finite bounds in non-sharp quasiregular settings.

Fix
\alpha>1
and define the Stolz cone

\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\xi|<\alpha(1-|z|)\},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

For a function
u
on
\mathbb{D}
, set

N_{\alpha}u(\xi)=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}|u(z)|.

Lemma 2.3

(Carleson embedding through non-tangential maximal functions)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>1
. There exists
C_{\alpha}<\infty
such that, for every Carleson measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
and every measurable function
u
on
\mathbb{D}
,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|u(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}u(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|.

(6)

Proof.

Let
E_{t}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}:N_{\alpha}u(\xi)>t\}
. If
|u(z)|>t
, then
z
belongs to the tent over
E_{t}
associated with the aperture
\alpha
. The Carleson condition gives

\mu(\{z:|u(z)|>t\})\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}|E_{t}|.

Using the layer-cake formula,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|u|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|u|>t\})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}|E_{t}|\,dt

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}u(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|.

∎

Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
immediately imply the following curve estimate.

Corollary 2.4
.

For every
0<p<\infty
and every
\alpha>1
there is a constant
B_{\alpha}<\infty
such that, for every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
and every measurable function
u
on
\mathbb{D}
,

\int_{\Gamma}|u(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq B_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}u(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|.

(7)

3
A maximal-function formulation for quasiregular classes

The preceding section gives a simple general principle: to prove a Gabriel inequality, it is enough to control a non-tangential maximal function by the boundary function.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
K\geq 1
,
\alpha>1
, and
M<\infty
. We say that a
K
-quasiregular mapping
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to

\mathcal{G}^{p}_{K,\alpha}(M)

if the following hold:

(i)

f
has a non-tangential boundary limit

f^{*}(\xi)=\lim_{z\to\xi,\,z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}f(z)

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
;

(ii)

f^{*}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
;

(iii)

the maximal estimate

\|N_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leq M\|f^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

(8)

holds.

Theorem 3.2

(Maximal-function Gabriel principle)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
K\geq 1
,
\alpha>1
, and
M<\infty
. If
f\in\mathcal{G}^{p}_{K,\alpha}(M)
, then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq B_{\alpha}M^{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|,

(9)

where
B_{\alpha}
is the constant in Corollary
2.4
. More generally, if
\mu
is a Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq C_{\alpha}\|\mu\|_{\operatorname{Car}}M^{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|.

(10)

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.3
to
u=f
and then use (
8
). This gives (
10
). Taking
\mu=\,ds|_{\Gamma}
and using Lemma
2.1
gives (
9
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Theorem
3.2
is a framework theorem rather than a new boundary theory for quasiregular Hardy spaces. Its usefulness depends on independent hypotheses or previously known results that give the maximal estimate (
8
). This distinction is essential: the radial condition (
5
) alone does not generally provide a.e. boundary values or a non-tangential maximal theorem.

4
The log-subharmonic modulus case

A non-negative function
\Phi
is called log-subharmonic if
\log\Phi
is subharmonic, with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
, and let
f
be a mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\Phi=|f|
is log-subharmonic and

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(r\zeta)^{p}\,|d\zeta|<\infty.

(11)

Then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi^{*}(\zeta)^{p}\,|d\zeta|,

(12)

where
\Phi^{*}
is the radial boundary function supplied by the Loziński majorant theorem. If
f
itself has radial boundary values a.e., then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|.

(13)

The constant
2
is best possible for any subclass containing the analytic Hardy space
H^{p}
.

Proof.

By the Loziński majorant theorem
[
8
]
, there exists an analytic function
F\in H^{p}
such that

\Phi(z)\leq|F(z)|\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D})

and

\Phi^{*}(\zeta)=|F^{*}(\zeta)|\qquad\text{for a.e. }\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Gabriel’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(z)^{p}\,ds(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma}|F(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}|F^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi^{*}(\zeta)^{p}\,|d\zeta|.

If
f^{*}
exists a.e., then
\Phi^{*}=|f^{*}|
a.e. Sharpness follows from the sharpness of Gabriel’s analytic theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
K\geq 1
. Suppose that
f
is
K
-quasiregular in
\mathbb{D}
, satisfies (
11
) with
\Phi=|f|
, has radial boundary values a.e., and
\log|f|
is subharmonic. Then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|.

The constant
2
is sharp in any such class that contains all analytic
H^{p}
functions.

Remark 4.3
.

No quasiregularity is used in Theorem
4.1
. The point of Corollary
4.2
is that the quasiregular Gabriel problem has a sharp answer on the special subclass for which the modulus admits an analytic majorant with the same boundary modulus. Related uses of Loziński-type majorants in isoperimetric inequalities for subharmonic or harmonic functions appear in
[
9
,
10
]
.

5
Harmonic quasiregular mappings

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a complex-valued harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic. If
f
is sense-preserving and

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|,\qquad 0\leq k<1,

then
f
is
K
-quasiregular with

K=\frac{1+k}{1-k}.

The next result is an immediate consequence of Das’s harmonic theorem.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
p>1
and let
f\in h^{p}
be a harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}
. Then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq A_{h}(p)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|,

where
A_{h}(p)
is defined by (
3
).

Proof.

A harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping in
h^{p}
is a harmonic Hardy-space function. The assertion is exactly Das’s theorem
[
4
]
restricted to this subclass.
∎

We now prove the dilatation-dependent improvement for
p=2
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in h^{2}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, and suppose that

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|\qquad(0\leq k<1).

Normalize the decomposition by taking
g(0)=0
. Then every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{2}\,ds(z)\leq 2\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|.

(14)

Equivalently, if
K=(1+k)/(1-k)
, then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{2}\,ds(z)\leq\frac{4K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|.

(15)

Proof.

The normalization
g(0)=0
is obtained by adding a constant to
g
and subtracting its conjugate from
h
; it does not change
f
or the derivatives.

Set

H=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|h^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|,\qquad G=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|.

Since
g(0)=0
, the Hardy–Stein identity for analytic functions gives

G=4\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|z|}\,dA(z),

where
dA
denotes planar area measure. Similarly,

H-2\pi|h(0)|^{2}=4\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|z|}\,dA(z),

with the same normalization of boundary arclength as above. Using
|g^{\prime}|\leq k|h^{\prime}|
, we obtain

G\leq k^{2}\bigl(H-2\pi|h(0)|^{2}\bigr)\leq k^{2}H.

Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{2}\,|d\zeta|=H+G.

Indeed, the cross term is

2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{T}}h^{*}(\zeta)g^{*}(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|=2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(2\pi h(0)g(0)\bigr)=0.

Gabriel’s theorem applied separately to
h
and
g
gives

\int_{\Gamma}|h|^{2}\,ds\leq 2H,\qquad\int_{\Gamma}|g|^{2}\,ds\leq 2G.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|f|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma}(|h|+|g|)^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2H+2G+2\left(\int_{\Gamma}|h|^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Gamma}|g|^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\sqrt{H}+\sqrt{G})^{2}.

If
H=0
, then
h\equiv 0
, and the derivative inequality forces
g
to be constant; since
g(0)=0
, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise let
t=\sqrt{G/H}
. Since
0\leq t\leq k
and the function
t\mapsto(1+t)^{2}/(1+t^{2})
is increasing on
[0,1]
,

\frac{\int_{\Gamma}|f|^{2}\,ds}{\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}|^{2}\,|d\zeta|}\leq 2\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\leq 2\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{1+k^{2}}.

Finally, because
K=(1+k)/(1-k)
,

2\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{1+k^{2}}=\frac{4K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}.

This proves (
14
) and (
15
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The estimate in Proposition
5.2
is an elementary refinement of Das’s
p=2
harmonic inequality on the subclass with bounded second complex dilatation. It improves the constant
4
for every finite
K
, gives
2
when
K=1
, and tends to
4
as
K\to\infty
. We do not know whether the constant
4K^{2}/(K^{2}+1)
is sharp for
1<K<\infty
.

For completeness, we also record the circle consequence of Das’s theorem.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
f\in h^{p}
be a harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping, and let
\Gamma
be a circle contained in
\mathbb{D}
. If
p\geq 2
, then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|.

If
1\leq p<2
, then

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)<(1+r)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\zeta)|^{p}\,|d\zeta|,

where
r
is the distance from the center of
\Gamma
to the origin.

Proof.

This is Das’s circle theorem
[
4
, Theorem 4]
, restricted to the harmonic quasiregular subclass.
∎

6
Stoilow factorizations with boundary control

Every non-constant planar quasiregular mapping admits a Stoilow factorization

f=g\circ\phi,

(16)

where
g
is analytic and
\phi
is quasiconformal. In the disk setting, after normalization, one may take
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
. To obtain a boundary-norm Gabriel inequality from (
16
), one needs quantitative control of the boundary map

\psi=\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}.

The following result states this control explicitly.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. Suppose that the push-forward measure
\psi_{*}(|d\xi|)
is absolutely continuous with respect to arclength. Its density
w_{\psi}
is defined by

\int_{\mathbb{T}}H(\eta)w_{\psi}(\eta)\,|d\eta|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}H(\psi(\xi))\,|d\xi|

for all non-negative Borel functions
H
.
For
1<p<\infty
, we say that
w_{\psi}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{T})
if

[w_{\psi}]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}w_{\psi}\,|d\xi|\right)\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}w_{\psi}^{-1/(p-1)}\,|d\xi|\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

Theorem 6.2

(Weighted Stoilow criterion)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha>1
. Suppose

f=g\circ\phi,

where
g\in H^{p}
,
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is quasiconformal, and
\psi=\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}
has boundary weight
w_{\psi}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{T})
. Assume also that
g^{*}\in L^{p}(w_{\psi}|d\zeta|)
and that, for some
\beta>1
,

\phi(\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi))\subset\Gamma_{\beta}(\psi(\xi))\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{T}).

(17)

Then
f
has non-tangential boundary values

f^{*}(\xi)=g^{*}(\psi(\xi))

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
, and every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq B_{\alpha}C_{p,\beta}([w_{\psi}]_{A_{p}})\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|.

(18)

Proof.

By (
17
),

N_{\alpha}f(\xi)\leq N_{\beta}g(\psi(\xi)).

Therefore, by the definition of
w_{\psi}
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}f(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\beta}g(\eta)^{p}w_{\psi}(\eta)\,|d\eta|.

For analytic
H^{p}
functions,
N_{\beta}g
is pointwise controlled, up to a constant depending on
\beta
, by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function of
|g^{*}|
. Since
w_{\psi}\in A_{p}
and
1<p<\infty
, the weighted Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\beta}g(\eta)^{p}w_{\psi}(\eta)\,|d\eta|\leq C_{p,\beta}([w_{\psi}]_{A_{p}})\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\eta)|^{p}w_{\psi}(\eta)\,|d\eta|.

The last integral equals

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\psi(\xi))|^{p}\,|d\xi|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|.

The boundary-value assertion follows because
g
has non-tangential limits a.e. and
\psi_{*}(|d\xi|)=w_{\psi}|d\xi|
is absolutely continuous. Thus
f
satisfies the admissible maximal estimate of Definition
3.1
. The conclusion follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Theorem
6.2
is deliberately conditional. Stoilow factorization alone does not imply the right-hand side of (
18
). The boundary weight and cone-distortion assumptions are the mechanisms that allow one to compare the analytic boundary norm of
g
with the boundary norm of
f
.

A simple concrete version is obtained under a bi-Lipschitz boundary hypothesis.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
K\geq 1
,
L\geq 1
, and let
\alpha,\beta>1
. We say that
f
belongs to the controlled bi-Lipschitz Stoilow class
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{K,L}(\alpha,\beta)
if

f=g\circ\phi,

where
g\in H^{p}
,
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is
K
-quasiconformal, the boundary extension
\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}
is
L
-bi-Lipschitz with respect to arclength on
\mathbb{T}
, and

\phi(\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi))\subset\Gamma_{\beta}(\phi(\xi))\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{T}).

(19)

Theorem 6.5

(Controlled bi-Lipschitz Stoilow class)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
K\geq 1
,
L\geq 1
, and
\alpha,\beta>1
. There exists

A=A(p,L,\alpha,\beta)<\infty

such that every
f\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{K,L}(\alpha,\beta)
and every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfy

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq A\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|.

(20)

Proof.

Write
f=g\circ\phi
as in Definition
6.4
. The cone condition (
19
) gives

N_{\alpha}f(\xi)\leq N_{\beta}g(\phi(\xi)).

Since
\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}
is
L
-bi-Lipschitz, the push-forward of arclength by
\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}
has density bounded above and below by constants depending only on
L
. Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}f(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|\leq C(L)\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\beta}g(\eta)^{p}\,|d\eta|.

The analytic non-tangential maximal theorem gives, for every
0<p<\infty
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\beta}g(\eta)^{p}\,|d\eta|\leq C(p,\beta)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\eta)|^{p}\,|d\eta|.

Using the lower measure bound from the bi-Lipschitz hypothesis,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\eta)|^{p}\,|d\eta|\leq C(L)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\phi(\xi))|^{p}\,|d\xi|=C(L)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{*}(\xi)|^{p}\,|d\xi|.

Thus
f
satisfies the admissible maximal estimate with a constant depending only on
p,L,\alpha,\beta
. Theorem
3.2
proves (
20
).
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The cone condition is written as a hypothesis in Definition
6.4
. This avoids hiding a boundary-regularity issue inside the phrase “Stoilow factorization”. In applications one may verify (
19
) from additional regularity of the quasiconformal factor, but it is not the factorization itself that provides the boundary-norm comparison.

Corollary 6.7

(One-sided Stoilow estimate)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and let
f=g\circ\phi
, where
g\in H^{p}
and
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is quasiconformal. Assume that, for some
\alpha,\beta>1
,

\phi(\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi))\subset\Gamma_{\beta}(\phi(\xi))\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{T}),

and suppose that
\phi^{-1}|_{\mathbb{T}}
is
L
-Lipschitz. Then for every convex curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
,

\int_{\Gamma}|f(z)|^{p}\,ds(z)\leq C(p,L,\alpha,\beta)\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{*}(\eta)|^{p}\,|d\eta|.

Proof.

The cone condition gives
N_{\alpha}f(\xi)\leq N_{\beta}g(\phi(\xi))
. The Lipschitz condition on
\phi^{-1}|_{\mathbb{T}}
gives the one-sided change-of-variables estimate

\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\alpha}f(\xi)^{p}\,|d\xi|\leq C(L)\int_{\mathbb{T}}N_{\beta}g(\eta)^{p}\,|d\eta|.

The analytic non-tangential maximal theorem and Corollary
2.4
complete the proof.
∎

7
Open problems

The results above leave several natural sharpness and boundary-regularity questions.

Problem 7.1

(Sharp harmonic
K
-quasiregular constant)
.

For harmonic
K
-quasiregular mappings in
h^{2}
, Proposition
5.2
gives

A^{\mathrm{harm}}_{2,K}\leq\frac{4K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}.

Is this bound sharp for
1<K<\infty
? If it is not sharp, determine the correct dependence on
K
.

Problem 7.2

(Quasiregular Hardy classes with boundary control)
.

Find natural subclasses of the radial quasiregular Hardy class (
5
) for which almost everywhere non-tangential boundary values and an estimate of the form

\|N_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\|f^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

hold. For such classes, determine the best Gabriel constant and its dependence on
p
and
K
.

Problem 7.3

(Boundary distortion in Stoilow factorization)
.

Can the
A_{p}
boundary-weight hypothesis in Theorem
6.2
be weakened? In particular, is an
A_{\infty}
condition, a reverse-Hölder condition, or a condition depending only on the quasisymmetry data of
\psi=\phi|_{\mathbb{T}}
sufficient for a boundary-norm Gabriel inequality, possibly together with an appropriate non-tangential approach-region condition?

Problem 7.4

(Endpoint exponents)
.

Das’s theorem shows that the full harmonic convex-curve inequality fails in general for
0<p\leq 1
. Which additional quasiregular or harmonic quasiregular assumptions restore a Gabriel-type inequality at
p=1
or below?
