Couch-Torrence inversion of D3-brane backgrounds
Antonio Cristofaro

Abstract
An asymptotically flat spacetime in D = 4 D=4 can be mapped via Couch-Torrence conformal inversion to the geometry around an extremal non-expanding and non-rotating horizon. At the linearized level, an infinite tower of conserved Newman-Penrose charges can be found at null-infinity, while infinitely many Aretakis charges are conserved in the near-horizon. Couch-Torrence inversion allows one to establish a matching between the two sets of asymptotic charges. In this work we construct the Newman-Penrose and Aretakis scalar charges in higher-dimensional geometries of D3-branes in D = 10 D=10 and D3-brane bound states in D = 4 D=4 and D = 5 D=5 and establish a precise matching between them through the inversion. By exploiting the residual unbroken supersymmetry of Type IIB supergravity, we demonstrate that it is possible to relate scalar (complex dilaton) charges to higher spin charges. In particular, we determine infinite towers of conserved asymptotic spinorial charges associated with the dilatino fluctuations, and determine the map through inversion.

2
General setting

The aim of this section is to generalize the Couch-Torrence (CT) conformal inversion to self-dual, higher-dimensional backgrounds, such as D3-brane solutions and bound states thereof. We begin with a pedagogical introduction to CT inversion using as a prototype the extremal Reissner-Nordström solution, where we derive the conserved charges in both the near-infinity and near-horizon limits. We then demonstrate how the CT inversion leads to a direct matching between these two sets of asymptotic charges. Later, we extend this formalism to higher-dimensional self-dual geometries, including those with compact dimensions. Finally, we establish the multipole expansions for a scalar field at large and small radius in these backgrounds, providing the necessary framework to define NP charges and Aretakis charges in these geometries.

2.1
Extremal Reissner-Nordström: a pedagogical case study

Consider the metric of an extremal Reissner-Nordstrom (RN) black hole (BH)

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2}\ ,

(2.1)

where
f(r)=\left(1-\frac{M}{r}\right)^{2}
is the ‘red-shift’ factor (for
M=|Q|
) and
d\Omega^{2}_{2}=d\vartheta^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta d\phi^{2}
is the metric on
S^{2}
. The horizon corresponds to
r_{H}=M
while the photon sphere is at
r_{c}=2M
.

Shifting the radial coordinate as

\rho=r-M\ ,

(2.2)

the horizon and the photon sphere are shifted to
\rho_{H}=0
and
\rho_{c}=M
.
One can then define the retarded and advanced time
u=t-\rho^{*}
and
v=t+\rho^{*}
, where
\rho^{*}
is the tortoise coordinate, defined such that

d\rho^{*}=\frac{(M+\rho)^{2}}{\rho^{2}}d\rho\ .

(2.3)

In particular, choosing
\rho^{*}
such that
\rho^{*}(\rho_{c})=0
, yields

\rho^{*}=\rho-\frac{M^{2}}{\rho}+2M\ln\frac{\rho}{M}\ .

(2.4)

In outgoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
\{u,\rho,\vartheta,\phi\}
the metric reads

ds^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}=-\frac{\rho^{2}}{(M+\rho)^{2}}du^{2}-2dud\rho+(M+\rho)^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2}\ ,

(2.5)

while in ingoing coordinates
\{v,\rho,\vartheta,\phi\}
it takes the form

ds^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{\rho^{2}}{(M+\rho)^{2}}dv^{2}+2dvd\rho+(M+\rho)^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2}\ .

(2.6)

The Couch–Torrence (CT) inversion symmetry is a radial inversion, effectively exchanging the near-horizon region with null infinity, and it is realized in RN BH as the conformal map

u\rightarrow v\qquad\rho\rightarrow\frac{M^{2}}{\rho}\ ,

(2.7)

that relates the retarded and advanced metrics in eqs. (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) as

2

2

Note that this form of the metric, although suited for the description of past and future null infinity, is not the metric on the conformal boundary. Therefore the emergence of Carroll geometry is not yet apparent, while it is of crucial significance for exploiting CT inversion in the Carrollian perspective on flat space holography. ds^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow\frac{M^{2}}{\rho^{2}}ds^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\ .

(2.8)

Under this conformal inversion
\rho^{*}\to-\rho^{*}

\rho^{*}=0
is a fixed point of this symmetry and the effective potential for null geodesics or waves is an even function of
\rho^{*}
.

Now consider the dynamics of an outgoing massless test scalar field
\Phi
in the background geometry of the metric (
2.5
), by solving the Klein-Gordon equation

\Box_{\mathcal{I}}\Phi=0

(2.9)

through the near-infinity asymptotic expansion

\Phi(u,\rho,\vartheta,\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{+\ell}\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell,m}(u)Y_{\ell,m}(\vartheta,\phi)\ ,

(2.10)

where
Y_{\ell,m}
are the spherical harmonics on
S^{2}
.
Denoting the derivative with respect to
u
by a dot and dropping an overall factor
(M+\rho)^{-2}
, the Klein-Gordon equation reads

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell,m}\Big\{\sum_{n}\Big[-\ell(\ell+1)+n(n+1)\Big]\rho^{-n-1}\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell,m}+2M^{2}\sum_{n}(n+1)\rho^{-n-2}\dot{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}

\displaystyle+2M\sum_{n}(2n+1)\rho^{-n-1}\dot{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}+2\sum_{n}n\rho^{-n}\dot{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}\Big\}Y_{\ell,m}=0\ .

Orthogonality of the spherical harmonics implies that each term in the bracket has to separately vanish, resulting in the equation

[-\ell(\ell+1)+n(n+1)]\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell,m}+2M^{2}n\dot{\varphi}^{(n-1)}_{\ell,m}+2M(2n+1)\dot{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}+2(n+1)\dot{\varphi}^{(n+1)}_{\ell,m}=0

(2.11)

for each coefficient in the
1/\rho
expansion. Noting that the first term in the equation above vanishes for
n=\ell
, one can extract the infinite tower of conserved charges
[
3
,
4
,
17
,
16
,
1
]

N_{\ell,m}=2M^{2}\ell\varphi^{(\ell-1)}_{\ell,m}+2M(2\ell+1)\varphi^{(\ell)}_{\ell,m}+2(\ell+1)\varphi^{(\ell+1)}_{\ell,m}\ .

(2.12)

These charges are conserved in the sense that

\partial_{u}N_{\ell,m}=0\ ,

(2.13)

barring non-linear terms arising from self-interactions of the scalar field or back-reaction on the metric.

Let us now consider the near-horizon expansion of the incoming massless scalar field
\widetilde{\Phi}
on the metric (
2.6
). In this case the field expansion is

\widetilde{\Phi}(v,\rho,\vartheta,\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{+\ell}\widetilde{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}(v)Y_{\ell,m}(\vartheta,\phi)\ .

(2.14)

Repeating the same computation, at the end one obtains the conserved Aretakis charges

A_{\ell,m}=2\ell\widetilde{\varphi}^{(\ell-1)}_{\ell,m}+2M(2\ell+1)\widetilde{\varphi}^{(\ell)}_{\ell,m}+2M^{2}(\ell+1)\widetilde{\varphi}^{(\ell+1)}_{\ell,m}\qquad\partial_{v}A_{l,m}=0.

(2.15)

Since ERN background has vanishing scalar curvature
R=0
, the equation of motion for the scalar field is conformally invariant. This means that under the map (
2.7
) the scalar field transforms as

\widetilde{\Phi}(v,\rho,\vartheta,\phi)=\frac{M}{\rho}\Phi(u\to v,\rho\to\frac{M^{2}}{\rho},\vartheta,\phi)

(2.16)

and hence

\widetilde{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,m}=\frac{1}{M^{2n+1}}\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell,m}\ .

(2.17)

Notice that the scaling dimension is, in general, related to
n
rather than to
\ell
.
Using this relations one can obtain the matching between Aretakis and Newman-Penrose charges:

A_{\ell,m}=\frac{1}{M^{2\ell+1}}N_{\ell,m}\ .

(2.18)

Up to a (rather trivial) mass factor they actually coincide. Since the near-infinity charges are mapped to the near-horizon charges with the same
(\ell,m)
, the conformal inversion preserves the multipolar structure of charges.

2.3
Multipolar expansion of a scalar field

The (radiative) multi-polar expansion of a scalar field
\Phi
for large
r
reads

\Phi(u,r,\vartheta^{a},\vec{x},\mathbf{z})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\vec{m}\in M_{\ell}}\frac{1}{r^{n+\delta}}\varphi^{(n)}_{\ell,\vec{m}}(u,\vec{x},\mathbf{z})Y_{\ell,\vec{m}}(\vartheta^{a})\ ,

(2.24)

where
Y_{\ell,\vec{m}}
are scalar spherical harmonics, satisfying

\nabla^{2}Y_{\ell,\vec{m}}=-\ell\left(\ell+{d-1}\right)Y_{\ell,\vec{m}}\ ,

(2.25)

with
{\vec{m}\in M_{\ell}}
representing the independent projections (Dynkin labels) of the totally symmetric traceless tensors labelled by
\ell

r
behaviour is controlled by the parameter

\delta={d-1}\ .

(2.26)

The near-horizon expansion is instead

\widetilde{\Phi}(v,r,\vartheta^{a},\vec{x},\mathbf{z})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\vec{m}\in M_{\ell}}r^{n}\widetilde{\varphi}^{(n)}_{\ell,\vec{m}}(v,\vec{x},\mathbf{z})Y_{\ell,\vec{m}}(\vartheta^{a})\ .

(2.27)

In the following we suppose that fields do not depend on additional coordinates
\vec{x}
and
\mathbf{z}
. Under the conformal inversion between null infinity and null horizon (
2.23
), the scalar field transforms according to

\widetilde{\Phi}(v,r,\vartheta^{a})=\alpha^{\delta}\Phi(u\to v,r\to r^{2}_{c}/r,\vartheta^{a})\ .

(2.28)

This mapping ensures that the multipolar structure is preserved, providing a natural way to relate Aretakis charges to NP charges.

The metric for a single stack of

4
Higher spin asymptotic charges in Type IIB supergravity

In this Section we first introduce the fundamental features of Type IIB supergravity, namely the BPS projection, which halves the number of independent Killing spinors, and the supersymmetric transformations relating the bosonic and fermionic fields of the theory.
We then analyse the NP and Aretakis charges in this supersymmetric setting. Starting form scalar charges associated with the dilaton fluctuations, it is possible, by means of supersymmetry transformations, to construct spinorial charges associated with the dilatino fluctuations. A similar procedure can be employed to construct higher-spin charges, up to the one associated to the graviton.
This analysis is motivated by an interesting relation between the structure of the on-shell superfield in
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)
supersymmetry and the form of what would correspond to NP and Aretakis conserved charges.

To make this proposal more concrete, we explicitly compute the near-infinity and near-horizon charges associated with the dilatino and verify the matching between them via Couch-Torrence inversion.

5
Conclusions and outlook

This work exploits Couch-Torrence conformal inversion for (intersecting) D3-brane configurations in Type IIB supergravity in order to identify the map between Newman-Penrose charges at null infinity and Aretakis charges in the near-horizon. Both sets of charges are exactly conserved at the linear level. It is tempting to speculate a holographic interpretation in terms of ‘protected’ KK modes of the supergravity fields residing in 1/2 BPS multiplets in the dual
{\cal N}=4
SYM theory. This may provide hints towards a possible ’flat Carrollian holographic’ interpretation of NP charges at null infinity. Supersymmetry is used to generate higher spin asymptotic charges for Type IIB. In the case of the dilatino, the asymptotic charges are constructed explicitly and shown to be consistent with the supersymmetrisation of the scalar charges.

A strong motivation for our work is to find a connection between the flat space holography program and the standard AdS/CFT picture. In this light, the investigation presented here establishes a map between the flat space and near horizon,
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
region of D3-branes, as well as their bound states. It would be very interesting to combine this bulk perspective with some of the recent developments employing a similar philosophy on the dual field theory. For instance, in the context of ABJM theory
[
2
]
a recent paper
[
5
]
has discussed different ways of defining Carrollian fermions showing that the flat space limit of the bulk corresponds to the limit whereby the speed of light
c
goes to zero in the boundary. The role of BMSvB has also been emphasized in their analysis.

Let us collect some interesting open questions for the future. A natural extension is to repeat the analysis of the dilatino equations, presented here for single D3-brane stacks to the intersecting D3-brane configurations discussed in sections
3.2
and
3.3
.
It would also be interesting to go beyond the linearised level. Non-linear effects may spoil the analysis but the robustness of multiplet shortening may come to rescue. We plan to study this and related issues in the near future.
More qualitatively, one might ask what are the conditions under which brane solutions in supergravity theories can possess a CT inversion symmetry. For instance, D
p
-brane solutions for
p\neq 3
, do not seem to enjoy this property. Note however, that T-dual solutions to the two-stack D3-branes discussed in this work, like the M2-M5 intersection in M-theory are known
[
30
]
, and may be a natural setting to ask such questions. The near extremal limit
[
19
,
20
,
18
]
and relation between Aretakis charges and conformal invariance is another natural direction of investigation.
Finally, it would be very interesting to consider strings, or other extended objects, propagating in a background enjoying CT inversions, and extend the notion of asymptotic charges for extended probes.

We decompose the dilatino as

\Lambda(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{4},\eta}\Lambda_{\ell_{4},\eta}(x){\cal Y}_{\ell_{4},\eta}(\vartheta)

(B.1)

with
{\cal Y}_{\ell_{4}}^{\pm}
spinor spherical harmonics, such that

i\not{D}{\cal Y}_{\ell_{4},\eta}=\eta\left(\ell_{4}+{5\over 2}\right){\cal Y}_{\ell_{4},\eta}\ ,

(B.2)

and
\eta=\pm 1
. The relation between spinor spherical harmonics and scalar ones is the following:

{\cal Y}_{\ell,\eta}=(i\not{D}+\ell+4)Y_{\ell}\kappa_{\eta}\ ,

(B.3)

or alternatively

{\cal Y}_{\ell,-}=(i\not{D}+\ell+1)Y_{\ell+1}\kappa_{+}\ ,

(B.4)

where
\kappa_{\eta}
is the Killing spinor on
S^{5}
satisfying

D_{a}\kappa_{\eta}+\eta\frac{i}{2}\widetilde{\gamma}_{a}\kappa_{\eta}=0\ .

(B.5)

There are two (complex) branches of modes with

M_{\eta}L=\eta\left(\ell_{4}+{5\over 2}+\eta\right)

(B.6)

in conjugate representations of
SU(4)

i.e.

[1,\ell_{4},0]
and
[0,\ell_{4},1]
and scaling dimension

\Delta_{\eta}=|M_{\eta}L+2\eta|\ .

(B.7)

B.1.2
Gravitino

The transverse (to the sphere),
\gamma
-traceless components of gravitino can be decomposed as

\Psi_{\tilde{\mu}}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{4},\eta}\psi_{\tilde{\mu},\ell_{4}.\eta}(x){\cal Y}_{\ell_{4},\eta}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.8)

Also for the gravitino there are two branches, with

M_{\eta}L=\eta\left(\ell_{4}+{5\over 2}-\eta\right)\ ,

(B.9)

in conjugate representations of
SU(4)

i.e.

[0,\ell_{4},1]
and
[1,\ell_{4},0]
and scaling dimension

\Delta_{\eta}=|M_{\eta}L|+2\ ,

(B.10)

The longitudinal (to the sphere),
\gamma
-traceless components of the gravitino are

\Psi_{a}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{t},\eta}\psi_{\ell_{t}.\eta}(x){\cal Y}_{a,\ell_{t},\eta}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{d},\eta}\psi_{\ell_{d},\eta}(x)D_{a}{\cal Y}_{\ell_{d},\eta}(\vartheta)\ ,

(B.11)

where
{\cal Y}_{a,\ell_{t},\eta}^{\pm}
are spinor-vector spherical harmonics. Mass branches are

M_{t,\eta}L=\eta\left(\ell_{t}+{5\over 2}-2\right)\ ,

(B.12)

in conjugate representations of
SU(4)

i.e.

36,140,...
and
36^{*},140^{*},..
and

M_{d,\eta}L=\eta\left(\ell_{t}+{5\over 2}-2\eta\right)\ ,

(B.13)

in conjugate representations of
SU(4)

i.e.

[1,\ell_{4},0]
and
[0,\ell_{4},1]
; and scaling dimension

\Delta_{\eta}=|M_{d,\eta}L|+2\ .

(B.14)

B.2
Bosons

B.2.1
Dilaton

The dilaton can be expanded as

\Phi(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}\varphi_{\ell_{1}}(x){Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)\ ,

(B.15)

with
{Y}_{\ell_{1}}
scalar spherical harmonics, such that

\nabla^{2}{Y}_{\ell_{1}}=-\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}+4){Y}_{\ell_{1}}\ .

(B.16)

There is only one (complex) branch of modes with

M^{2}L^{2}=\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}+4)\ ,

(B.17)

i.e.\ \Delta=\ell_{1}
, where we use
\ell_{1}
to label singlet of
SO(5)
.

B.2.2
Complex 2-form / Kalb-Ramond

The transverse components of the complex 2-form are

B_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}b_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}^{\ell_{1}}(x){Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)

(B.18)

with
{Y}_{\ell_{1}}
scalar spherical harmonics.
There are two (complex) branches of modes:

M^{2}L^{2}=\ell_{1}^{2}\qquad\ell_{1}\geq 1\ ,

(B.19)

in the
[0,\ell_{1},0]
of
SU(4)
and

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{1}+4)^{2}\qquad\ell_{1}\geq 0\ ,

(B.20)

in the
[0,\ell_{1},0]
.

The ’vector’ components are

B_{\tilde{\mu}a}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{5}}b_{\tilde{\mu}}^{\ell_{5}}(x){\cal Y}_{a,\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{1}}\tilde{b}_{\tilde{\mu}}^{\ell_{1}}(x)D_{a}{Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.21)

The latter term can be gauged away, the former yield two (complex) branches of vector modes with

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{5}+2)(\ell_{5}+4)

(B.22)

in the
[1,\ell_{5},1]
of
SU(4)
.

The longitudinal components can be written as

B_{ab}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{10}}b^{\ell_{10}}(x){\cal Y}_{[ab]}^{\ell_{10}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{5}}\tilde{b}^{\ell_{5}}(x)D_{[a}{\cal Y}_{b]}^{\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.23)

Also in this case, the latter term can be gauged away, the former yield two (complex) branches of scalar modes with

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{10}+3)(\ell_{10}-1)\ ,

(B.24)

in the
[2,\ell_{10},0]
of
SU(4)
and

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{10}+7)(\ell_{10}+3)\ ,

(B.25)

in the
[2,\ell_{10},0]
of
SU(4)
and their complex conjugate.

B.2.3
Graviton / RR 4-form and their mixing

The transverse traceless graviton can be expanded as

H_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}h_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}^{\ell_{1}}(x){Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.26)

There is one branch of symmetric traceless tensor modes with

M^{2}L^{2}=\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}+4)

(B.27)

in the
[0,\ell_{1},0]
of
SU(4)
.

The ’vector’ components are

H_{\tilde{\mu}a}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}h_{\tilde{\mu}}^{\ell_{5}}(x){\cal Y}_{a,\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{1}}\tilde{h}_{\tilde{\mu}}^{\ell_{1}}(x)D_{a}{Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.28)

The latter term can be gauged away, the former mix with the following components of the RR 4-form:

A_{\tilde{\mu}abc}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{5}}a_{\tilde{\mu}}^{\ell_{5}}(x)\varepsilon_{abcde}D^{d}{\cal Y}^{e}_{\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)\ ,

(B.29)

and yield two branches of real vector modes with

M^{2}L^{2}=\ell_{5}(\ell_{5}+2)

(B.30)

in the
[1,\ell_{5},1]
of
SU(4)
, and

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{5}+4)(\ell_{5}+6)

(B.31)

in the
[1,\ell_{5},1]
of
SU(4)
.

The longitudinal (to the sphere) graviton components are:

H_{(ab)}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{14}}h^{\ell_{14}}(x){\cal Y}_{(ab)\ell_{14}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{5}}\tilde{h}^{\ell_{5}}(x)D_{(a}{\cal Y}_{b)\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{1}}\tilde{\tilde{h}}^{\ell_{1}}(x)D_{(a}D_{b)}{Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.32)

The last two terms are gauge trivial, the symmetric traceless part yields scalars in the
[2,\ell_{14},2]
of
SU(4)
with mass

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{14}+2)(\ell_{14}+6)\ .

(B.33)

Now we consider the mixing between
H^{a}{}_{a}
and
A_{abcd}
. The former can be expanded as

H^{a}{}_{a}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}h^{\ell_{1}}(x){Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)

(B.34)

while the latter is

A_{abcd}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{1}}a^{\ell_{1}}(x)\varepsilon_{abcde}D^{e}{Y}_{\ell_{1}}(\vartheta)

(B.35)

Eliminating
H^{\tilde{\mu}}{}_{\tilde{\mu}}
produces two sets of scalar modes in the
[0,\ell_{1},0]
of
SU(4)
and masses

M^{2}L^{2}=\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}-4)\ ,

(B.36)

with
\ell_{1}\geq 2
or

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{1}+4)(\ell_{1}+8)\ ,

(B.37)

valid for any
\ell_{1}
.

Finally the fields
A_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\sigma}}
(scalar
{Y}_{\ell_{1}}
) and
A_{a\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}\tilde{\rho}}
(vector
{\cal Y}^{\ell_{1}}_{a}
) can be eliminated algebraically thanks to the self-duality constraint, while

A_{ab\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(x,\vartheta)=\sum_{\ell_{10}}a^{\ell_{10}}_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(x){\cal Y}_{[ab]}^{\ell_{10}}(\vartheta)+\sum_{\ell_{5}}\tilde{a}^{\ell_{5}}_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}}(x)D_{[a}{\cal Y}_{b]}^{\ell_{5}}(\vartheta)\ .

(B.38)

Gauging away the last term produces antisymmetric tensors in the
[2,\ell_{10},0]
and
[0,\ell_{10},2]
with mass

M^{2}L^{2}=(\ell_{10}+3)^{2}

(B.39)
