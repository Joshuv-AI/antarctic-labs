Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair for Tricuspid Regurgitation

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTricuspid regurgitation (TR) is common, and severe TR independently predicts mortality and heart failure hospitalization, leading to functional impairment and poor quality of life. Optimal medical therapy (OMT), mainly using diuretic agents, provides symptomatic relief but does not correct valvular dysfunction and is often limited by the resistance of diuretic agents. Isolated tricuspid valve surgery remains high risk. Transcatheter options have therefore emerged, with transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) widely adopted.ObjectivesThe Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair for Tricuspid Regurgitation in China (TEER-CHINA) study is designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T System compared with OMT in patients with severe TR.MethodsThe TEER-CHINA trial is a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled trial (NCT05556460) in which a total of 236 participants will be enrolled across 35 centers in China and randomized 1:1 to the device group or OMT alone. Key inclusion criteria include patients with severe or greater TR (≥3+) who remain clinically symptomatic despite OMT for at least 30 days, are at intermediate or higher surgical risk for tricuspid valve surgery by the local Heart Team, and are anatomically suitable for use of the investigational device. Participants will be followed up at 30 days, 6 months, 12 months, and annually thereafter for up to 5 years.ResultsThe primary endpoint is a hierarchical composite assessed at 12 months, including all-cause mortality or tricuspid valve intervention, annualized heart failure hospitalization rates, and improvement in health status measured by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire.ConclusionsThe TEER-CHINA trial is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of the DragonFly-T System and to inform future treatment strategies for severe TR.Key words: DragonFly-T, randomized controlled trial, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair, tricuspid regurgitation

Tricuspid regurgitation (TR), once considered a neglected valvular disorder, is increasingly recognized a clinically relevant therapeutic target. The prevalence of TR increases with advancing age, and significant TR affects approximately 4% of individuals aged 75 and older.1 Epidemiologic studies in Chinese people further indicate that the prevalence of TR ranked second only to mitral regurgitation (MR) as a common valvular disorder in the elderly.2,3 The clinical burden of TR is substantially amplified in patients with heart failure (HF), with a prevalence exceeding 50% among those with advanced symptoms.4 TR is disproportionately observed in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), a phenotype that currently accounts for up to half of the overall patients with HF. Prior studies have demonstrated that moderate-to-severe TR is approximately twice as prevalent in patients with HFpEF compared with those with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).5,6 Importantly, severe TR is a well-established, independent predictor of all-cause mortality and HF hospitalization, irrespective of comorbidities, ventricular function, or pulmonary pressures.7

With respect to medical therapy, current evidence-based guidelines provide limited support for pharmacological treatment of TR. According to the 2020 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines, there is no Class I indication for drug therapy targeting TR, and diuretic agents are recommended (Class IIa) primarily for symptomatic management of systemic congestion in patients with severe TR.8 However, medical therapy does not correct the underlying valvular pathology and is frequently limited by diuretic resistance as the disease progresses.8,9 Although surgical repair or replacement is routinely performed at the time of left-sided valve surgery, isolated tricuspid valve surgery remains infrequently undertaken and is associated with substantial perioperative risk (with an in-hospital mortality rate of 10%-12%), largely attributable to delayed referral and advanced right ventricular (RV) dysfunction.8

Transcatheter tricuspid valve interventions (TTVIs), such as transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) and replacement, have emerged as lower-risk alternatives. Recent pivotal trials, such as TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial (The Trial to Evaluate Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients Treated with the Tricuspid Valve Repair System Pivotal) and TRISCEND II pivotal trial (EVOQUE Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Pivotal Clinical Investigation of Safety and Clinical Efficacy Using a Novel Device), have demonstrated acceptable procedural safety profiles and clinically meaningful improvements in TR severity as well as patient-reported quality of life.10,11 According to growing evidence, the 2025 European Society of Cardiology guidelines assign a Class IIa recommendation for TTVIs, including TEER, to improve quality of life and promote RV remodeling in high-risk patients with symptomatic severe TR despite optimal medical therapy (OMT), in the absence of severe RV dysfunction or precapillary pulmonary hypertension (PH).12 In parallel, randomized evidence in this field continues to evolve. The CLASP II TR trial (Edwards PASCAL Transcatheter Valve Repair System Pivotal Clinical Trial, NCT04097145) is currently enrolling patients to further evaluate the safety and efficacy of the PASCAL Transcatheter Valve Repair System. The DragonFly-T System (Valgen Medtech) is a novel, China-developed, edge-to-edge repair device specifically designed for the treatment of TR. A prospective, multicenter, parallel, randomized controlled study to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the DragonFly-T transcatheter tricuspid valve repair system in the treatment of TR (TEER-CHINA study) has been initiated to assess the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T System in patients with severe TR who remain symptomatic despite OMT. In this report, we present the study design, methods, and statistical analysis plan of the TEER-CHINA study.

Methods

The TEER-CHINA study is a prospective, multicenter, parallel, randomized controlled study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T System with OMT compared with OMT in patients with at least severe TR (NCT05556460). All participants will undergo clinical follow-up before discharge (not applicable to the control group), at 30 days, 6 months, 12 months, and annually from 2 to 5 years post treatment. The study results may be used to support a regulatory submission to the National Medical Products Administration of China for indication approval.

Study group

In this study, all enrolled patients were required to have received OMT, including therapy with diuretic agents for at least 30 days. For patients with HFpEF, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) was recommended when clinically appropriate. For patients with HFrEF, guideline-directed medical therapy consisted of quadruple therapy for HFrEF, as recommended by HF guidelines. All medical therapies were fully documented; if medical therapy was not prescribed, the medical reasons for nonuse were recorded. In addition, all patients were required to undergo invasive hemodynamic assessment to evaluate pulmonary artery pressures and right- and left-sided hemodynamic parameters, enabling differentiation among precapillary, postcapillary, or mixed PH phenotypes and supporting the identification and characterization of HFpEF when applicable.

A detailed list of eligibility criteria is provided in Table 1. Key inclusion criteria include patients with severe or greater TR (≥3+) who remain clinically symptomatic despite OMT for at least 30 days, are at intermediate or higher surgical risk for tricuspid valve surgery as determined by the local heart team, and are anatomically suitable for using the investigational device. Study device description

The DragonFly-T System (Figure 1) is designed for the treatment of TR. The system consists of a valve clip system and a guiding sheath, with a dedicated delivery system used in conjunction with a steerable guiding sheath. Similar to the TriClip system (Abbott), the DragonFly-T System is based on a tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) mechanism, achieving leaflet approximation by grasping either the anterior and septal leaflets or the posterior and septal leaflets, thereby promoting tricuspid annular reduction and reducing regurgitation. The DragonFly-T System incorporates several distinctive design features, including a central compliant filler that effectively blocks the regurgitant orifice while maintaining a low tricuspid valve gradient, a stable and reliable mechanical transmission system that enhances procedural safety, independent leaflet grasping capability to accommodate complex tricuspid valve anatomies, and an optimized, ergonomically designed delivery system to facilitate smooth device navigation and precise deployment. Notably, the guiding sheath is interchangeable with that used for the DragonFly-M System for the treatment of MR, enabling the potential use of a single sheath platform for the transcatheter treatment of both MR and TR.13,14

Committee and trial oversight

Rigorous trial oversight is provided by multiple independent committees. A steering committee is responsible for the overall scientific conduct and strategic oversight of the study. An eligibility committee, comprising cardiologists, HF specialists, and cardiac imaging specialists, determines patient eligibility. An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board regularly reviews accumulating safety data, monitors overall trial safety, and provides recommendations regarding study continuation or modification, as appropriate. The Data and Safety Monitoring Board review was limited to descriptive safety assessment; no formal interim efficacy or futility analyses, prespecified early stopping rules, or alpha-spending procedures were planned. A clinical events committee independently reviews and adjudicates all prespecified adverse events according to standardized definitions, primarily based on the Tricuspid Valve Academic Research Consortium criteria.15 In addition, an independent echocardiographic core laboratory (Echo Core Lab) performs centralized analysis of all echocardiographic data, including the assessment of TR severity and etiology. Imaging data from computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging are independently analyzed by a dedicated computed tomography/magnetic resonance imaging core laboratory. A list of participating study centers is provided in Supplemental Table 1. The sponsor (Valgen Medtech) is responsible for trial conduct, including site selection and management, data management, monitoring, and analysis.

Randomization

The randomization schedule is generated by an independent statistician using SAS software according to the block randomization method. Blinding is applied to outcome assessors responsible for evaluating NYHA functional classification, the 6-minute walk test, and the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ).

Treatment

Patients assigned to the device group underwent T-TEER using the DragonFly-T System with OMT. The procedure was performed via a transfemoral venous approach under digital subtraction angiography and transesophageal echocardiography guidance, according to the standardized procedural protocol. Clip implantation strategy, including target leaflet, the number of clips, and final clip positioning, was determined by the local heart teams based on tricuspid valve anatomy and intraprocedural imaging. Following the procedure, patients continued with medication adjustments permitted based on physicians’ evaluation. Patients assigned to the control group received OMT alone. Treatment optimization and adjustments were performed at the discretion of the treating physicians based on the clinical status of the patient while avoiding substantial deviations from stable medication during the screening/baseline period (eg, dosing <50% or >100%, or changes in medication classes). Interventional or surgical tricuspid valve treatment was not permitted in the first year unless clinically mandated.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint is a hierarchical composite endpoint assessed at 12 months after treatment and will be analyzed using a prespecified hierarchical testing strategy (Central Illustration). The hierarchical structure was designed to reflect the relative clinical importance of outcomes and was informed by experience from prior win ratio–based trials in structural valve disease.11 The hierarchical endpoint consists of 1) time to all-cause death or tricuspid valve intervention; 2) annualized rate of heart failure hospitalization (HFH); and 3) clinically meaningful improvement in health status, defined as a ≥15-point increase in the KCCQ score from baseline.

Follow-up

Study participants will undergo assessments at the following time points after treatment: discharge (not applicable to the control group) and at 30 days, 6 months, 12 months, and annually from 2 to 5 years follow-up. Subjects in the control group will be permitted to cross over to the investigational treatment after completion of the 12-month follow-up, with a repeat eligibility assessment. Primary and secondary endpoint analyses will be conducted through the 12-month follow-up period prior to any crossover. For descriptive endpoints beyond 12 months, outcomes in the control group will be summarized descriptively by separating participants into cross over and pure control (non–cross over) subgroups. Participants undergoing cross over will be required to complete an additional postprocedure follow-up visit at 30 days after the procedure. The schedule of assessments is summarized in Supplemental Table 2.

Sample size calculation

Sample size estimation was based on assumptions derived from available data from prior studies evaluating TriClip therapy in patients with severe TR.11,17, 18, 19 Based on a meta-analysis of available data, assumptions for the investigational vs OMT groups were specified for each component of the hierarchical primary endpoint, including all-cause death or tricuspid valve surgery, HF rehospitalization, and clinically meaningful improvement in health status as assessed by the KCCQ score. A shared frailty exponent of 1.5 was assumed, consistent with the TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial.16 Sample size and power were evaluated using simulations conducted in R based on 10,000 simulated data sets with 5 iterations under these assumptions, indicating that a total of 212 randomized subjects were required to achieve adequate statistical power (see Supplemental Table 3). Allowing for an anticipated 10% attrition rate, the final estimated sample size was 236 patients, which is expected to provide approximately 80% power to detect superiority of the investigational device for the primary endpoint at a two-sided 5% significance level.

Planned statistical analyses

All statistical analyses will be performed based on predefined analysis populations. The primary endpoint is a hierarchical composite endpoint assessed at 12 months after treatment and will be analyzed in the intention-to-treat population. Sensitivity analyses will be performed in the as-treated population, based on actual treatment received, and in the per-protocol population, excluding data collected after major HF interventions, including tricuspid valve intervention, LV assist device implantation, or heart transplantation. The primary comparison will be conducted using the Finkelstein–Schoenfeld method and the treatment effect summarized by the win ratio.20 Continuous variables will be summarized using appropriate descriptive statistics, including means, SDs, medians, and quartiles, and categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables will be performed using analysis of variance or the Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate based on data distribution, whereas categorical variables will be compared using Pearson's chi-square test. For pairwise comparisons, Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test will be applied, as appropriate. Time-to-event outcomes will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves, with between-group comparisons performed using the log-rank test. All statistical analyses will be performed using SAS, version 9.4 or later (SAS Institute Inc).

Data collection

Investigators and their study teams will be responsible for subject recruitment and the ethical conduct of the trial. All study data will be collected using a secure electronic case report form system, with each participant identified by a unique study identification number only. Data used for analysis and reporting will be fully anonymized and will not contain any patient-identifiable information. Reasons for subject withdrawal or dropout will be documented and summarized in the final analysis.

Discussion

As summarized in the Central Illustration, the TEER-CHINA trial is a prospective, multicenter, randomized trial enrolling patients with symptomatic severe TR despite OMT. Patients are randomized 1:1 to DragonFly-T plus OMT or OMT alone. The hierarchical primary endpoint is evaluated using a win ratio approach incorporating mortality or TV reintervention, HFH, and improvement in KCCQ at 12 months, with secondary endpoints including quality of life, functional capacity, procedural performance and safety, and echocardiographic outcomes. Notably, the study implements a standardized procedural protocol across all participating sites and requires optimization of medication prior to randomization, with SGLT2i therapy recommended for patients with HFpEF when clinically appropriate. In addition, comprehensive assessment of tricuspid valve anatomy and hemodynamic status is performed using transesophageal echocardiography and right heart catheterization, which is recommended in patients with severe TR to accurately characterize hemodynamic parameters. In the context of increasing recognition of severe TR and its complex interaction with HF, comprehensive hemodynamic assessment may increasingly become part of routine clinical evaluation. Therefore, the study patients defined by these criteria are expected to remain clinically relevant while enabling clearer interpretation of treatment effects.

Severe TR is relatively common, yet available treatment options, including medical therapy and isolated surgical intervention, are often limited in effectiveness and associated with substantial morbidity and mortality.21 Beginning with initial off-label applications of transcatheter mitral devices for tricuspid repair, development of tricuspid-specific transcatheter devices has expanded rapidly, with numerous systems now being evaluated worldwide.21, 22, 23 T-TEER is currently the most widely adopted transcatheter approach for tricuspid valve repair. Two devices have received Conformité Européenne mark approval, including TriClip and PASCAL Precision (Edwards Lifesciences), with TriClip additionally approved by the Food and Drug Administration in April 2024. The DragonFly-T System, a domestically developed device optimized for tricuspid valve anatomy in China, completed its first-in-human implantation in 2020. Early feasibility studies have demonstrated favorable outcomes, highlighting the need for a multicenter, prospective, RCT to rigorously evaluate its safety and efficacy.

In previous research, multiple clinical studies have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of T-TEER. In the early feasibility TRILUMINATE study, sustained reduction of TR was observed at 1 year after TriClip implantation, with 71% of 85 patients maintaining TR at moderate or less, accompanied by improvements in right atrial and RV function.18 In the TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial, symptomatic patients with severe TR were randomized to medical therapy alone or medical therapy plus T-TEER. The hierarchical primary endpoint—comprising all-cause mortality/surgical intervention, HFH, and quality-of-life improvement—favored the T-TEER group (win ratio, 1.48 [95% CI: 1.06-2.13]; P = 0.02). Although 1-year mortality and HFH rates were not significantly different between groups, T-TEER-treated patients experienced marked improvement in the KCCQ score (mean increase: 12 vs 0.6 points; P < 0.001), with greater quality-of-life benefits observed when residual TR was reduced to moderate or less.11 Two-year follow-up indicated lower annualized HFH and fewer tricuspid interventions in the T-TEER group, suggesting potential durability of clinical benefit with long-term follow-up compared with conservative therapy.24 Similarly, the TRI-FR trial (Evaluation of Tricuspid Valve Percutaneous Repair System in the Treatment of Severe Secondary Tricuspid Disorders) enrolled 300 patients assigned to T-TEER plus OMT or OMT alone. The primary 1-year composite endpoint—including NYHA functional class, patient global assessment, and major cardiovascular events (cardiovascular hospitalization and/or death), showed improvement in 74.1% of patients who were treated with T-TEER vs 40.6% in the OMT group, P < 0.001. KCCQ scores improved by 14.5 points in the T-TEER group, surpassing the OMT group, although 1-year mortality and HFH remained comparable, consistent with TRILUMINATE Pivotal results.25 Furthermore, the CLASP TR (Edwards PASCAL TrAnScatheter Valve RePair System in Tricuspid Regurgitation [CLASP TR] Early Feasibility Study) using the PASCAL system reported a 30-day mortality of 3.1%, with 86% of patients achieving moderate or less TR at 1 year.26 Beyond Western cohorts, the development of T-TEER in Asia has been more recent but is rapidly evolving. An early multicenter experience from four Asian centers reported outcomes in 106 patients undergoing T-TEER between 2017 and 2024. At 30 days, device success was achieved in 74.0% of patients, whereas 95.2% experienced at least a one-grade reduction in TR severity. Clinical status improved substantially, with 96.2% of patients in NYHA functional class I or II, and the incidence of major adverse events was low (1.9%).27

As the understanding of TR mechanisms and its relationship with HF has deepened, the relationship between TR and HFpEF is increasingly recognized as bidirectional.28 HFpEF, characterized by LV diastolic dysfunction, atrial fibrillation, and atrial myopathy, leads to elevated left-sided filling pressures, increased PCWP, and subsequent RV afterload. Chronic pressure overload and atrial remodeling promote functional tricuspid annular dilatation and progression of TR. In turn, significant TR exacerbates right-sided volume overload, resulting in RV dilatation, impaired RV function, abnormal RV-LV interaction, and reduced forward stroke volume, thereby worsening systemic congestion and HF symptoms and creating a vicious pathophysiological feedback loop. In the EuroTR registry (The large European Registry of Transcatheter Repair for Tricuspid Regurgitation), an international cohort of patients undergoing percutaneous treatment for severe TR, right heart catheterization data showed that approximately 68% of patients had a PCWP ≥15 mm Hg, suggesting a high prevalence of underlying HFpEF in this study group.29 However, despite this high prevalence suggested by baseline hemodynamic profiles, HFpEF was neither systematically identified nor specifically treated in prior research, including the TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial, because these studies were conducted before SGLT2i was incorporated into HF guidelines as a Class I recommended therapy for HFpEF.30 In the TEER-CHINA study, consistent with updated guideline recommendations, SGLT2i therapy is recommended when clinically appropriate, particularly in patients with HFpEF.30,31 Prior studies and case reports have suggested that SGLT2i, when used in combination with other therapies, may be associated with improvements in TR, potentially through favorable effects on pulmonary hemodynamic parameters and RV function.28,32, 33, 34

Altogether, the DragonFly-T System represents the first domestically developed T-TEER system specifically optimized for tricuspid valve anatomy, marking a significant milestone in advancing precision valve therapy in China. This RCT aims to rigorously evaluate the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T System in patients with severe TR who remain symptomatic despite OMT, also providing critical evidence for the use of T-TEER in an Asian patient group. Looking forward, more ongoing randomized controlled studies of TTVI are expected to inform future trial design, establish new standards, and expand treatment options, thereby supporting consideration of T-TEER as the standard of care for patients with symptomatic severe TR.

Study limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, a sham-control procedure was not included, primarily because of ethical concerns related to exposing control patients to general anesthesia and femoral venous access without an anticipated therapeutic benefit. Therefore, blinding was applied to independent outcome assessors to minimize potential bias in the evaluation. Second, cross over from the control group to the investigational treatment is permitted after completion of the 12-month follow-up. Prior studies of TTVIs have suggested that longer-term follow-up, extending to 2 years or beyond, may be required to fully capture the clinical benefits of device therapy in patients with HF. Consequently, the early cross over design may attenuate the ability to detect longer-term differences in clinical outcomes between treatment groups.

Conclusions

The TEER-CHINA study is designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T System in patients with severe TR despite receiving OMT. Through a standardized procedural approach, comprehensive imaging and hemodynamic evaluation, and OMT before randomization, this trial aims to generate robust evidence specific to the Chinese patients on the clinical value of TEER using this novel device. The findings are expected to support clinical practice and inform treatment decisions for patients with tricuspid valve disease in the era of modern guideline-directed therapy.

Trial Status

This trial initiated patient enrollment on September 3, 2024. As of January 14, 2026, the study had been initiated at 35 centers, with a total of 118 patients enrolled.
