Robust Multi-Stream Massive MIMO Satellite Systems Based on Statistical CSI
Hangsong Yan, Member, IEEE, Alexei Ashikhmin, Fellow, IEEE, Hong Yang, Senior Member, IEEE, Bin Song, Senior Member, IEEE, Shu Sun, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract
This paper investigates multi-stream downlink precoding for massive multiple-input multiple-output low-Earth-orbit satellite (SAT) communication systems. We adopt a delay and Doppler precompensation approach to achieve coherent transmission. Under this setting, we formulate a signal transmission model that incorporates the near-independent properties of inter-SAT interference and compensation errors. We then demonstrate that moving beyond single-stream transmission requires both multi-SAT cooperation and multi-antenna UTs. Based on this configuration and the established signal transmission model, we derive the first- and second-order statistical channel characteristics and utilize them to design locally optimal precoding algorithms for both total power constraint (TPC) and per-antenna power constraint (PAPC) conditions, which rely only on statistical channel state information (sCSI). In particular, the designed PAPC algorithm achieves linear complexity with respect to the number of antennas on the cooperative SATs. To reduce the computational complexity of the locally optimal precoder under TPC, we propose a low-complexity and robust precoding scheme optimized for both minimum mean squared error and sum-rate maximization objectives. Using majorization theory, we also provide a rigorous theoretical analysis of the optimal precoding structure under TPC. Moreover, the Lanczos algorithm is adopted to further reduce the complexity of the proposed robust designs. Simulation results show that when each SAT is equipped with a sufficiently large number of antennas, the proposed sCSI-based designs achieve performance comparable to that of instantaneous CSI-based designs.

Satellite (SAT) communication possesses the distinctive ability to cover wide geographical areas especially in areas underserved by terrestrial mobile networks

However, LEO SAT communication continues to face multiple challenges. The long distance between LEO SATs and ground user terminals (UTs) causes higher path loss compared to terrestrial communication. Furthermore, the high-speed motion of LEO SATs not only causes large Doppler shifts but also leads to severe channel aging, making it difficult to acquire accurate instantaneous channel state information (iCSI) at the SATs. Building on this analysis, they further proposed a robust sCSI-based precoding scheme and a strategy for SAT-UT association

Exploiting the properties of the beam matrix, the authors proposed low-complexity decoupled designs for the DL precoder and UL receiver.

Each SAT is equipped with a large number of antennas and each UT has multiple antennas, enabling multi-stream transmission. Multiple UTs are served by more than one SAT using the same time and frequency resources. To the best of our knowledge, such a scenario utilizing sCSI-based precoding has not been treated before. We stress that this multi-antenna UT configuration is prevalent, since a large portion of handheld devices (e.g., smartphones) are now equipped with multiple antennas. However, the considered scenario faces the following challenges: a) The long propagation delay of SAT communication leads to rapid aging of iCSI, requiring a robust transmission framework based on sCSI, which is required to be adaptable to long-term channel characteristics. b) In addition to enhancing the power of desired signals, the precoder design needs to suppress interference between UTs and among multiple data streams within each UT, leading to high algorithmic complexity.
c) The PAPC is more practical but imposes more stringent limitations compared to the TPC, making it difficult to obtain a solution that balances performance and algorithmic complexity.
To tackle these challenges, the main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

1) We adopt a delay and Doppler precompensation approach to ensure coherent transmission and formulate a multi-stream transmission model that incorporates the near-independent properties of inter-SAT interference and compensation errors.

2) We perform a detailed analysis of SAT communication channel characteristics, demonstrating that multi-stream transmission to a single UT requires both multi-SAT cooperation and multi-antenna UTs. By exploiting the Rician-distributed channel characteristics, we derive the first- and second-order channel statistics.

3) Based on the WMMSE framework, we provide locally optimal iCSI-based precoding algorithms for both TPC and PAPC conditions. In particular, by sequentially updating the precoding vector of each antenna, the designed PAPC algorithm achieves linear computational complexity with respect to the number of antennas on the cooperative SATs.

4) By utilizing the obtained first- and second-order channel statistics, we derive statistical expressions for the achievable rate, mean squared error (MSE) matrix, and MMSE matrix. Subsequently, we propose locally optimal sCSI-based precoding designs under the WMMSE framework for both TPC and PAPC.

5) To tackle the high computational complexity (i.e., cubic with respect to the number of antennas on the cooperative SATs) in the TPC design, we propose a robust and low-complexity precoding scheme leveraging sCSI that is optimized for both the MMSE and sum-rate maximization criteria. Using majorization theory, we provide a rigorous theoretical analysis of the optimal precoding structure under TPC in Theorem 1. To the best of our knowledge, such a rigorous treatment based on majorization theory has not been presented before.

6) We employ the Lanczos algorithm to further reduce the computational complexity of the eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) in the proposed low-complexity precoding scheme, with only a slight performance degradation. Comprehensive simulations are conducted, and the results show that the performance of the proposed sCSI-based precoding schemes is comparable to that of the locally optimal iCSI-based precoding scheme when each SAT is equipped with a sufficiently large number of antennas.

, where
\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{s,k}=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{s,k}\gamma_{s,k}}{2(\kappa_{s,k}+1)}}(1+j)\mathbf{d}_{s,k,1}
represents the deterministic LoS component at the receiver, and
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{s,k}
corresponds to the random scattering component (i.e., non-line-of-sight (NLoS)), whose elements follow an i.i.d.
\mathcal{CN}(0,\frac{\gamma_{s,k}}{\kappa_{s,k}+1})
distribution. Accordingly, we have
\mathbf{H}_{s,k}^{\text{LoS}}=(1+j)\mathbf{d}_{s,k,1}\mathbf{g}_{s,k}^{H}
and
\mathbf{H}_{s,k}^{\text{NLoS}}=\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{s,k}\mathbf{g}_{s,k}^{H}
.

We define
\beta_{s,k}^{\text{LoS}}\triangleq\frac{\kappa_{s,k}\gamma_{s,k}}{2(\kappa_{s,k}+1)}
.
Based on the channel model in (
18
), the statistical mean of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
, denoted as
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
, is given by:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{k}=

\displaystyle\Big[\sqrt{\beta_{1,k}^{\text{LoS}}}\mathbf{H}_{1,k}^{\text{LoS}}\bar{\varphi}_{1,k}\;\sqrt{\beta_{2,k}^{\text{LoS}}}\mathbf{H}_{2,k}^{\text{LoS}}\bar{\varphi}_{2,k}\;\cdots\;\sqrt{\beta_{S,k}^{\text{LoS}}}\mathbf{H}_{S,k}^{\text{LoS}}\bar{\varphi}_{S,k}\Big],

(19)

where
\bar{\varphi}_{s,k}=\mathbb{E}\{\varphi_{s,k}\}
denotes the expected phase error. Furthermore, the second-order statistic of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,k}
,
\mathbb{E}\{\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s_{1},k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s_{2},k}\}
, is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s_{1},k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s_{2},k}\}

\displaystyle\;=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\gamma_{s,k}\mathbf{g}_{s,k}\mathbf{g}_{s,k}^{H},&s_{1}=s_{2}=s\\
\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{s_{1},k}^{H}\bar{\mathbf{d}}_{s_{2},k}\bar{\varphi}_{s_{1},k}^{*}\bar{\varphi}_{s_{2},k}\mathbf{g}_{s_{1},k}\mathbf{g}_{s_{2},k}^{H},&s_{1}\neq s_{2}.\end{array}\right.

Given (
19
) and (
II-C
),
\mathbb{E}\{\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\}=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}=[\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\;\text{diagblk}\{[\sqrt{\rho_{1,k}}\mathbf{g}_{1,k},\dots,\sqrt{\rho_{S,k}}\mathbf{g}_{S,k}]\}],

(22)

where
\rho_{s,k}=\frac{\gamma_{s,k}(1+\kappa_{s,k}(1-|\bar{\varphi}_{s,k}|^{2}))}{(\kappa_{s,k}+1)}
. Note that both
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
will be used to compute the precoding matrix under the sCSI condition.

The computation of
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}
relies on the statistical information of
\{\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\}
,
\{\mathbf{g}_{s,k}\}
,
\{\gamma_{s,k}\}
,
\{\kappa_{s,k}\}
, and
\{\bar{\varphi}_{s,k}\}
. This statistical CSI can be obtained through UT feedback or UL channel estimation. Since statistical reciprocity exists between the UL and DL channels, each SAT can first estimate its sCSI from UL pilot signals, and subsequently use it for DL transmission
[
14
]
. In particular, accurate directional cosines can be acquired from satellite ephemeris and global navigation satellite system (GNSS) positioning
[
23
]
.

Furthermore, the sCSI can be updated whenever the SATs have received UL pilot signals. Taking
\gamma_{s,k}
as an example, let
\hat{\gamma}_{s,k}^{t}
denote its estimate at time instant
t
, which can be computed as:

\hat{\gamma}_{s,k}^{t}=\alpha\hat{\gamma}_{s,k}+(1-\alpha)\hat{\gamma}_{s,k}^{t-1},

(23)

where
\hat{\gamma}_{s,k}
represents the estimate of
\gamma_{s,k}
based on the most recently received pilot signals, and
\alpha\in[0,1]
is a tunable weight factor balancing the current and previous estimates.
It is worth noting that the update period of the sCSI can be aligned with the UL pilot signal transmission interval, which is typically
20
ms in terrestrial communications
[
24
]
.
On the other hand, the sCSI is assumed to be quasi-static as long as the SATs and UTs are within a certain range. Therefore, the update period for the precoding matrices can exceed
20
ms. As reported in
[
14
]
and shown in the simulation results, an update period of
100
ms results in negligible performance degradation.

III
Locally Optimal iCSI Precoder Design

In this section, we present the iCSI precoder design subject to both TPC and PAPC, which achieves a locally optimal solution. In highly dynamic LEO SAT communications, accurate iCSI acquisition is practically challenging. Consequently, the iCSI-based design, evaluated under a favorable assumption of known instantaneous channel and impairment information, serves as a performance upper bound for the sCSI-based algorithms rather than a practically matched benchmark. Furthermore, as shown in Section
IV
, the algorithmic framework provided in this section applies to the sCSI-based algorithm designs.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}\times 1}
denote the received multi-stream signal at the
k
-th UT. Based on the transmission model given in (
16
),
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}
is given by:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbf{H}_{s,k}\Big(\mathbf{W}_{s,k}\mathbf{s}_{k}\varphi_{s,k}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\mathbf{W}_{s,k^{\prime}}\breve{\mathbf{s}}_{s,k^{\prime}}\varphi_{s,k^{\prime},k}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{n}_{k},

(24)

where
\mathbf{F}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times d_{k}}
denotes the receiving matrix for
\mathbf{s}_{k}
. We assume that the information of different UTs is independent,
\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{s}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{k}^{H}\}=\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}
, and
\mathbb{E}\{\breve{\mathbf{s}}_{s,k^{\prime}}\breve{\mathbf{s}}_{s,k^{\prime}}^{H}\}=\mathbf{I}_{d_{k^{\prime}}}
. Due to the independence of inter-SAT interference as described in Remark
1
, we approximate
\mathbb{E}\{\breve{\mathbf{s}}_{s_{1},k^{\prime}}\breve{\mathbf{s}}_{s_{2},k^{\prime}}^{H}\}\approx\mathbf{0}
.
Based on the above analysis, the MSE matrix for the
k
-th UT is given by:

\displaystyle\text{MSE}_{k}=\;

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}-\mathbf{s}_{k})(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k}-\mathbf{s}_{k})^{H}\}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}(\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbf{F}_{k}-\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}

\displaystyle-\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}+\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}}=\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\bigg(\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}^{K}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\bigg)\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{k}=[\mathbf{W}_{1,k}^{T}\;\mathbf{W}_{2,k}^{T}\;\cdots\;\mathbf{W}_{S,k}^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{SM\times d_{k}},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}=\text{diagblk}\{[\mathbf{W}_{1,k^{\prime}},\mathbf{W}_{2,k^{\prime}},\dots,\mathbf{W}_{S,k^{\prime}}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{SM\times Sd_{k^{\prime}}}.

(26)

The linear receiver that minimizes the trace of the MSE matrix is the well-known Wiener filter
[
25
]
, given by:

\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\text{opt}}=(\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}.

(27)

Substituting
\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\text{opt}}
into (
) yields the MMSE matrix for the
k
-th UT:

\displaystyle\text{MMSE}_{k}=\;

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}+\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k})^{-1}.

(28)

Furthermore, the achievable rate for the
k
-th UT,
r_{k}
, derived from (
24
), is given by:

r_{k}=\log\det(\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}+\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}).

(29)

III-A

Total Power Constraint

The sum-rate maximization problem subject to the TPC is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\quad\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\big(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P,

(30)

where
P
denotes the maximum available transmit power for the cooperative SATs.
To solve problem (
30
), we first notice that
r_{k}=\log\det(\text{MMSE}_{k}^{-1})
. We then formulate a convex version of (
30
), which is given by:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k},\mathbf{F}_{k},\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\text{MSE}_{k})-\log\det(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H})\leq P,

(31)

where
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}\times d_{k}}
can be interpreted as the auxiliary weight matrix for the MSE matrix of the
k
-th UT. Note that when
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}=\text{MMSE}_{k}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{k}=\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\text{opt}}
, problem (
31
) is equivalent to problem (
30
).
Such a formulation is called the WMMSE approach
[
26
]
.

Problem (
31
) can be solved using the block coordinate descent (BCD) method. As proven in
[
26
]
, the BCD method converges to a nontrivial stationary point of (
30
). Specifically, the objective function of (
31
) is minimized by sequentially fixing two of the three variables,
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
,
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\}
, and
\{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}
, and updating the remaining one. Since the objective function of (
31
) is convex with respect to
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}
, we have
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}^{\text{opt}}=\text{MSE}_{k}^{-1}
. Furthermore, the optimal receiving matrix
\mathbf{F}_{k}
is given by
\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\text{opt}}
in (
27
). Finally, the optimization of
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
can be formulated as the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\text{MSE}_{k})\quad\text{s.t.}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H})\leq P.

(32)

When
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\}
and
\{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}
are fixed, (
32
) becomes a convex quadratic problem with respect to
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
, which can be solved using standard convex optimization algorithms. However, the Lagrange multiplier method provides a more efficient approach. Specifically, the Lagrangian of (
32
) is expressed as:

\displaystyle L(\{\mathbf{W}_{k},\mu\})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\Big(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\Big(\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}(\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbf{F}_{k}

\displaystyle-\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}-\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}+\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mu\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H})-P\Big),

(33)

where
\mu
denotes the Lagrange multiplier for the TPC.
By taking the gradient of (
33
) with respect to
\mathbf{W}_{k}
and setting it to
\mathbf{0}
, we obtain the structure of
\mathbf{W}_{k}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{k}(\mu)=

\displaystyle\Big(\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{k^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{k^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}+\mu\mathbf{I}_{SM}\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k},

(34)

where
\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{k^{\prime}}=\text{diagblk}\{[\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{1,k^{\prime}}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k^{\prime}},\dots,\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{S,k^{\prime}}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k^{\prime}}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{SM\times Sd_{k^{\prime}}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{k^{\prime}}=\text{diagblk}\{[\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k^{\prime}},\dots,\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k^{\prime}}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{Sd_{k^{\prime}}\times Sd_{k^{\prime}}}
. The Lagrange multiplier
\mu\geq 0
must be chosen such that the complementary slackness condition of the TPC in (
32
) is satisfied. This implies that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k}(\mu)\mathbf{W}_{k}(\mu)^{H})=P
, which is equivalent to the following condition:

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}((\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}+\mu\mathbf{I}_{SM})^{-2}\mathbf{\Theta}_{k})=P,

(35)

where

\mathbf{\Theta}_{k}=\mathbf{U}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k},

(36)

and

\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{H}
represents the eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{k^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{k^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}
. Since
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}
is a diagonal matrix, the left-hand side of (
35
) is a monotonically decreasing function of
\mu
for
\mu\geq 0
. Therefore, the optimal value of
\mu
, denoted as
\mu^{*}
, can be obtained by using a simple bisection method. The overall algorithm for solving problem (
30
) is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

.

.

III-B

Per-antenna Power Constraint

Instead of the ideal TPC, a PAPC might be more practical for cooperative systems. Under the PAPC condition, the sum-rate maximization problem can also be solved using the WMMSE framework. The problem can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k},\mathbf{F}_{k},\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\text{MSE}_{k})-\log\det(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{K}[\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}]_{m,m}\leq P_{m},\forall m,

(37)

where
P_{m}=P/SM
, for
m=1,\dots,SM
, denotes the maximum transmit power of the
m
-th antenna. Note that
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\}
and
\{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}
can be updated similarly to the TPC case, and the problem for updating
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
is given by:

\min_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\text{MSE}_{k})\quad\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}[\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}]_{m,m}\leq P_{m},\forall m.

(38)

Since there are generally
SM
power constraints in (
38
), a simple bisection search relying on the Lagrange multiplier method is no longer applicable.

To improve the computational efficiency of solving (
37
), we consider employing the BCD method to sequentially optimize
\{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\}
,
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\}
, and
\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}\}
, where
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}\times 1}
denotes the
m
-th column of
\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}
. In other words, instead of directly optimizing
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
, we optimize each column of
\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}
sequentially. Problem (
38
) is decoupled into
SM
subproblems, and the
m
-th subproblem is formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}\}_{k=1}^{K}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\Big(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{SM}(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}^{H})\sum_{m=1}^{SM}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}^{H})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\sum_{m=1}^{SM}(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime},m}^{H})\sum_{m=1}^{SM}(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime},m}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}^{H})\Big)\mathbf{F}_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle-2\text{tr}\Big(\mathcal{R}\Big(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}\sum_{m=1}^{SM}(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m})\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}^{H})\leq P_{m},

(39)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime},m}\in\mathbb{C}^{Sd_{k^{\prime}}\times 1}
is the
m
-th column of
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}
, and
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the
m
-th column of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
.
Next, we show that a closed-form expression for the optimal
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}
exists when applying the Lagrange multiplier method.

Let
\mu_{m}\geq 0
denote the Lagrange multiplier associated with the power constraint of the
m
-th antenna. By formulating the Lagrangian
L({\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m},\mu_{m}})
, taking its gradient with respect to
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}
, and setting it to
\mathbf{0}
, we obtain the structure of the stacked vector
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{m}=[\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{1,m}^{H}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{2,m}^{H}\cdots\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{K,m}^{H}]^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times 1}
as:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{m}(\mu_{m})=\frac{\mathbf{b}_{m}}{\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}^{H}\mathbf{\Xi}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}+\mu_{m}},

(40)

where
d=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}
denotes the total number of signal streams transmitted to all served UTs,
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}=[\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{1,m}^{H},\dots,\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{K,m}^{H}]^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{KN\times 1}
,
\mathbf{\Xi}=\text{diagblk}\{[\mathbf{\Xi}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{\Xi}_{K}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{KN\times KN}
with
\mathbf{\Xi}_{k}=\mathbf{F}_{k}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{b}_{m}
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{m}=\mathbf{\Sigma}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}-\Big(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{s},m^{\prime}\neq m}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{m^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m^{\prime}}^{H}+\sum_{m^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}}\mathbf{\Omega}_{m^{\prime}}\Big)\mathbf{\Xi}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m},

(41)

where
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\text{diagblk}\{[\mathbf{\Gamma}_{1}\mathbf{F}_{1}^{H},\dots,\mathbf{\Gamma}_{K}\mathbf{F}_{K}^{H}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times KN}
and
\mathbf{\Omega}_{m^{\prime}}=\text{diagblk}\{[\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{1,m^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{1,m^{\prime}}^{H},\dots,\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{K,m^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{K,m^{\prime}}^{H}]\}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times KN}
.
Here, we assume that the considered
m
-th antenna belongs to the
s
-th SAT, where
\mathcal{M}_{s}
denotes the antenna index set for this SAT (i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{s}=\{(s-1)M+1,\dots,sM\}
).

Similar to the TPC case,
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}(\mu_{m})
must satisfy the complementary slackness condition for the PAPC, which is given by
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}(\mu_{m})\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k,m}^{H}(\mu_{m}))=P_{m}
. This yields:

\frac{\|\mathbf{b}_{m}\|_{2}^{2}}{(\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}^{H}\mathbf{\Xi}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}+\mu_{m})^{2}}=P_{m}.

(42)

Therefore, the optimal value of
\mu_{m}
is given by:

\mu_{m}^{*}=\max\Bigg(0,\sqrt{\frac{\|\mathbf{b}_{m}\|_{2}^{2}}{P_{m}}}-\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}^{H}\mathbf{\Xi}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}\Bigg).

(43)

Furthermore,
\mathbf{b}_{m}
can be written as
\mathbf{b}_{m}=\mathbf{\Sigma}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}-(\mathbf{C}_{s}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{m}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}^{H})\mathbf{\Xi}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
, where
\mathbf{C}_{s}=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{m^{\prime}}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m^{\prime}}^{H}+\sum_{m^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}}\mathbf{\Omega}_{m^{\prime}}
. Observe that
\mathbf{C}_{s}
only needs to be updated when the considered SAT changes. The overall algorithm to solve problem (
37
) is summarized in Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm 2

Locally Optimal Precoder Design Under PAPC

1:

, set the iteration index
n=0
, and define the maximum number of iterations,
N_{\text{iter}}
.

Repeat

.

5:

.

6:

For

s=1,\dots,S

do

7:

.

8:

For

m\in\mathcal{M}_{s}

do

9:

. Because

IV
Locally Optimal sCSI Precoder Design

We now consider the locally optimal precoding design based on sCSI under the WMMSE framework.
Note that a key step in applying the WMMSE framework to the sCSI scenario is formulating closed-form expressions for
\mathbb{E}\{r_{k}\}
and
\mathbb{E}\{\text{MSE}_{k}\}
.

Given the sCSI models in (
19
) and (
22
), we propose that
\mathbb{E}\{r_{k}\}
under SAT communication can be approximated by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{r_{k}\}

\displaystyle\approx\log\det\Big(\mathbf{I}_{N(S+1)}+\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}

\displaystyle\cdot\Big(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\big)\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N(S+1)}\Big)^{-1}\Big).

(44)

The corresponding statistical MSE,
\mathbb{E}\{\text{MSE}_{k}\}
, is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{\text{MSE}_{k}\}=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{H}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}

\displaystyle-\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}+\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}^{K}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H})\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N(S+1)},

(45)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}
denotes the receiving matrix for the
k
-th UT. Its optimal structure is given by:

\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{\text{opt}}=(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}+\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}.

(46)

The proof of these results is provided in Appendix
A
.

Comparing the expressions for
\mathbb{E}\{\text{MSE}_{k}\}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_{k}^{\text{opt}}
with those for
\text{MSE}_{k}
and
\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\text{opt}}
, we observe that the only difference is that the channel matrix
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
is replaced by
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
.
Therefore, the locally optimal sCSI-based precoder under the TPC condition can be computed using Algorithm
1
by simply replacing
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
with
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
. Similarly, the locally optimal solution for the sCSI-based sum-rate maximization problem under the PAPC can be obtained using Algorithm
2
by replacing
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
and
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
with
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
, respectively.

Complexity analysis: The overall computational complexity of the locally optimal sCSI precoder design under the PAPC is also dominated by line 7 of Algorithm
2
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
is replaced by
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
, the complexity becomes
\mathcal{O}(SMdK(S+N)N_{i})
. For the TPC case, the computational complexity is still dominated by the matrix inversion operation in line 7 of Algorithm
1
, yielding a total complexity of
\mathcal{O}(S^{3}M^{3}KN_{i})
.

V
Robust Precoding Design with TPC

As detailed in the complexity analysis of Algorithm
1
, its implementation relies on an iterative process, where the complexity per iteration scales cubically with the total number of antennas (i.e.,
SM
). In this section, we explore the optimal precoder structure under the TPC to propose a low-complexity algorithm. The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed approach is also robust to variations in the Rician factor and the variance of the compensated phase error.

V-A

Inter-user Interference Suppression

To avoid the high computational complexity of iterative procedures and facilitate the receiver design, we first consider canceling the inter-UT interference at the SAT side. To achieve this, the precoder
\mathbf{W}_{s,k}
of the
s
-th SAT for the
k
-th UT should lie in the null space of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{|s,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K-1)N\times M}
, which is defined as:

\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{|s,k}\triangleq[\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,1}^{H}\quad\cdots\quad\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,k-1}^{H}\quad\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,k+1}^{H}\quad\cdots\quad\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,K}^{H}]^{H}.

(47)

Note that
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{s,k}=\varphi_{s,k}\mathbf{d}_{s,k}\mathbf{g}_{s,k}^{H}
is a rank-
1
matrix. Therefore, computing the null space of
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{|s,k}
is equivalent to computing the null space of
\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{K-1\times M}
, which is given by:

\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k}=[\mathbf{g}_{s,1}\;\cdots\mathbf{g}_{s,k-1}\;\mathbf{g}_{s,k+1}\;\cdots\mathbf{g}_{s,K}]^{T}.

(48)

An orthogonal basis for the null space of
\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k}
can be obtained by performing a singular value decomposition (SVD) on
\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k}
. The right singular vectors corresponding to the zero singular values span this null space. We denote the matrix containing these basis vectors as
\mathbf{V}_{s,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times I_{s,k}}
, where
I_{s,k}=M-\text{rank}(\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k})
. The precoder for the
k
-th UT can then be written as:

\mathbf{W}_{k}=\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k},\quad\mathbf{V}_{k}=\text{diagblk}\{[\mathbf{V}_{1,k},\mathbf{V}_{2,k},\dots,\mathbf{V}_{S,k}]\},

(49)

where
\mathbf{A}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{k}\times d_{k}}
is a linear combination matrix with
I_{k}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}I_{s,k}
.
When the precoding matrix
\mathbf{W}_{k}
adopts the structure in (
49
), the interference terms become:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}^{K}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}=\mathbf{0}_{N}\text{ and }\mathbf{R}_{z}^{{}^{\prime}}=\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}.

(50)

V-B

Precoding based on Statistical CSI

In this section, we first consider the problem of minimizing the sum of the traces of
\mathbb{E}\{\text{MMSE}_{k}\}
across all UTs, which is formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{W}_{k}}}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbb{E}\{\text{MMSE}_{k}\})\quad\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\big(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P.

(51)

Because inter-UT interference was suppressed in the previous subsection,
\mathbb{E}\{\text{MMSE}_{k}\}
can be approximated based on (
66
) as:

\mathbb{E}\{\text{MMSE}_{k}\}\approx\mathbf{E}_{k}=(\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}+\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}/\sigma_{k}^{2})^{-1}.

(52)

By substituting (
52
) and the precoder structure
\mathbf{W}_{k}=\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}
into (
51
), we can rewrite the optimization problem as:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{A}_{k}\}}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}(\mathbf{E}_{k})\quad\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\big(\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P.

(53)

To solve (
53
), we first examine the feasible set of
\{\mathbf{A}_{k}\}
satisfying its power constraint. Let
\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}=\mathbf{V}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}/\sigma_{k}^{2}
. According to Lemma
1
on majorization theory outlined in Appendix A, for a given Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{R}
(which, in our context, is
(\mathbf{I}_{d_{k}}+\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k})^{-1}
, or equivalently,
\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}
), the vector containing its diagonal elements,
\text{diag}\{\mathbf{R}\}
, is majorized by the vector
\bm{\lambda}
, which is formed by its eigenvalues. Building upon this, we establish the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be positive semi-definite with eigenvalue decomposition (EVD)
\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{U}_{2}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2}\mathbf{U}_{2}^{H}
, and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times s}
with
n\geq s
and
\text{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{H})\leq P
. Further, let the EVD lead to
\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}^{H}
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{U}_{2}\mathbf{P}^{1/2}
, where the real-valued
n\times s
matrix
\mathbf{P}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\text{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}})\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-s)\times s}\end{array}\right]
with
[\tilde{\mathbf{p}}]_{i}=[\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}]_{i,i}/[\mathbf{\Lambda}_{2}]_{i,i}
,
i=1,\dots,s
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{H})\leq\text{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{H}).

The proof of Theorem
1
is provided in Appendix
C
. Theorem
1
establishes that for any feasible matrix
\mathbf{A}
, we can always find another feasible matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
achieving the same objective value (i.e.,
\text{tr}(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\mathbf{A})=\text{tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
). Moreover, the total power consumed by
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
is less than or equal to that of
\mathbf{A}
. Consequently, we can restrict the feasible set from
\{\mathbf{A}_{k}\}
to
\{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}\}
without incurring any performance loss. Since each matrix within
\{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}\}
diagonalizes
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}
, the optimization problem is effectively transformed into optimizing the diagonal variables
\mathbf{P}_{k}=\text{diag}\{[p_{k,1},\dots,p_{k,d_{k}}]\}
.

We apply Theorem
1
to problem (
53
) by
setting
\mathbf{A}_{k}=\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{P}_{k}^{1/2}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{k}
consists of the eigenvectors corresponding to the
d_{k}
largest eigenvalues of
\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}
. Consequently, problem (
53
) simplifies to:

\displaystyle\min_{\{p_{k,i}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\big(1+p_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}p_{k,i}\leq P,\;p_{k,i}\geq 0,\;k=1,\dots,K,\;i=1,\dots,d_{k},

(54)

where
\lambda_{k,i}
denotes the
i
-th eigenvalue of
\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}
. Since both the objective function and the constraints of problem (
54
) are convex, this problem can be efficiently solved using standard convex optimization tools. Notably, the transformation from
\mathbf{A}_{k}
to
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}
not only recasts (
51
) as the convex optimization problem in (
54
) but also significantly reduces the problem’s dimensionality (i.e., from
I_{k}\times d_{k}
complex variables to
d_{k}
real variables), which effectively decreases the computational complexity. In fact, by applying the Lagrange multiplier method, the optimal power allocation
p_{k,i}^{*}
for (
54
) is given by
p_{k,i}^{*}=\max\Big(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu^{*}\lambda_{k,i}}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,i}}\Big)
. The optimal Lagrange multiplier,
\mu^{*}
, can be found via the bisection method such that the TPC
\sum_{k,i}p^{*}_{k,i}=P
is satisfied.

V-C

Sum Rate Maximization with TPC

The sum-rate maximization problem subject to the TPC under the sCSI condition is formulated as:

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbb{E}\{\log_{2}(1+\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}})\}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\big(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P,

(55)

where
\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}}
is given by:

\displaystyle\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}}=\frac{\xi_{k,i}}{1-\xi_{k,i}},\quad\xi_{k,i}=\mathbf{w}_{k,i}^{H}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{\Phi}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{w}_{k,i},

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}_{k}=\mathbf{H}_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{d_{k}}(\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime},i^{\prime}}\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime},i^{\prime}}^{H})\mathbf{H}_{k}^{H}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}.

(56)

Let
[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}
denote the
i
-th diagonal element of
\text{MMSE}_{k}
. By applying the Woodbury matrix identity, it can be readily shown that this diagonal element satisfies
[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}=(1+\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}})^{-1}
.
Consequently, we show that problem (
55
) can be equivalently recast into the following form:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{A}_{k}\}}-\prod_{k,i}\frac{1}{[\mathbf{E}_{k}]_{i,i}}\quad\text{s.t. }\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{tr}\big(\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P.

(57)

The detailed derivation is provided in Appendix
D
.

Next, we demonstrate that the optimal structure of
\mathbf{A}_{k}
derived in Theorem
1
is also applicable to the sum-rate maximization problem in (
57
).
First, observe that the objective function in (
57
) takes the form of
f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=-\prod_{i}x_{i}^{-1}
, which is a Schur-concave function (the proof is provided in Appendix
E
).
Because
f_{0}(\mathbf{x})
is Schur-concave, the properties of majorization dictate that the vector of diagonal elements
\text{diag}\{\mathbf{E}_{k}\}
is majorized by the vector of its eigenvalues
\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{E}_{k})
. This implies that:

-\prod_{k,i}\frac{1}{[\mathbf{E}_{k}]_{i,i}}\geq-\prod_{k,i}\frac{1}{[\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{E}_{k})]_{i}}.

(58)

In Theorem
1
, we proved that for any feasible matrix
\mathbf{A}_{k}
that constructs
\mathbf{E}_{k}
and satisfies the power constraint in (
57
), we can always find another matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}=\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{P}^{1/2}
. This matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{k}
makes
\mathbf{E}_{k}=\text{diag}(\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{E}_{k}))
and also satisfies the power constraint in (
57
).
Thus, problem (
57
) can be equivalently recast as:

\displaystyle\max_{\{p_{k,i}\}}\sum_{k,i}\log(1+p_{k,i}\lambda_{k,i})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{k,i}p_{k,i}\leq P,\;\;p_{k,i}\geq 0,k=1,\dots,K,i=1,\dots,d_{k}.

(59)

Since
\lambda_{k,i}\geq 0
and
p_{k,i}\geq 0,\forall k,\forall i
, the Hessian matrix of the objective function in (
59
) is negative semi-definite. Thus, the objective function is concave
[
27
]
, and (
59
) can be efficiently solved using standard convex optimization tools.
Similar to the MMSE criterion, by applying the Lagrange multiplier method,
the optimal power allocation
p_{k,i}^{*}
for (
59
) is given by
p_{k,i}^{*}=\max\Big(0,\frac{1}{\mu^{*}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,i}}\Big)
.
The overall algorithm for solving the sCSI-based sum-rate maximization subject to the TPC is summarized in Algorithm
3
.

Algorithm 3

Robust Precoder Design under TPC

1:

Calculate the null space matrix
\mathbf{V}_{k}
for each UT by performing SVD on
\{\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{|s,k}\},\forall s,\forall k
.

2:

Perform an EVD on
\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}=\mathbf{V}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}/\sigma_{k}^{2}
to obtain the largest
d_{k}
eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{d_{k}},\forall k
and their corresponding eigenvectors
\mathbf{U}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{k}\times d_{k}}
.

3:

Use the bisection method to calculate
\mu^{*}
such that
\sum_{k,i}\max\Big(0,\frac{1}{\mu^{*}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,i}}\Big)=P
.

4:

Calculate the power allocation
p_{k,i}^{*}
as
p_{k,i}^{*}=\max\Big(0,\frac{1}{\mu^{*}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k,i}}\Big)
.

5:

Calculate the precoder as
\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\text{*}}=\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}\text{diag}\{[\sqrt{p_{k,1}^{*}},\dots,\sqrt{p_{k,d_{k}}^{*}}]\}
.

Complexity analysis: The overall computational complexity is dominated by line 2 of Algorithm
3
.
Specifically, the complexity of performing the EVD on
\{\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\},\forall k
scales as
\mathcal{O}(I_{k}^{3}K)
.

V-D

\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{k}\times I_{k}}

\mathcal{O}(I_{k}^{3})

d_{k}

d_{k}\leq\min(S,N)\ll I_{k}
. Thus, we only require the
d_{k}

\mathbf{\Psi}_{k}
and their corresponding eigenvectors, which can be efficiently calculated using the Lanczos algorithm
[
28

If

i<\max(S,N)

\beta_{i}=0

9:

\mathbf{C}
to its first
i
columns:
\mathbf{C}=[\mathbf{C}]_{1:i}

10:

Construct the
i\times i
tridiagonal matrix
\mathbf{T}

\displaystyle[\mathbf{T}]_{j,j}=\alpha_{j},j=1,\dots,i,

\displaystyle[\mathbf{T}]_{j,j+1}=[\mathbf{T}]_{j+1,j}=\beta_{j},j=1,\dots,i-1.

(60)

11:

Terminate the iteration.

12:

Else If

\beta_{i}\neq 0
:

\mathbf{C}

\mathbf{T}

),

\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{k}

.

\max(S,N)

.

\{\lambda_{k,i}\}
obtained from Algorithm

) to further calculate the power control coefficients.
Note that the overall computational complexity of Algorithm

is dominated by the
\max(S,N)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{i=1}^{\tilde{d}_{k}}p_{k,i}\leq\frac{P}{K},\quad p_{k,i}\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,\tilde{d}_{k}.

(61)

The above problem maximizes the achievable rate per UT under an equal power allocation assumption (i.e.,
\frac{P}{K}
per UT). We iterate
\tilde{d}_{k}
from
1
to
d_{k}^{\max}
to find the optimal number of streams,
d_{k}^{\text{opt}}
, for rate maximization, where
d_{k}^{\max}=\min(S,N)
. Additionally, the distribution of the phase error
\varphi_{s,k}
depends on the specific estimation method and is beyond the scope of this paper. Without loss of generality, we adopt the modeling approach in
[
29
,
20
]
. Specifically,
\varphi_{s,k}=e^{j\psi_{s,k}}
, with
\psi_{s,k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\varrho_{s,k}^{2})

VII
Conclusion

We have demonstrated the feasibility and performance benefits of SAT cooperation in DL multi-stream, multi-SAT mMIMO systems.
Iterative WMMSE precoding designs using iCSI and sCSI under TPC and PAPC conditions are provided. Furthermore, a robust and low-complexity precoder design under the TPC condition is also developed. By utilizing the Lanczos algorithm, the computational complexity of this design is significantly reduced, making it highly suitable for practical applications. Notably, when the ratio between the number of antennas per SAT and the number of served UTs is large—as is typical in the mMIMO regime—the proposed robust and low-complexity method achieves performance close to the local optimum. Finally, the precoding matrices generated by the proposed low-complexity method can also serve as an effective starting point for the iterative WMMSE algorithms to facilitate convergence. Importantly, while the proposed designs excel in average performance and robustness, they are not uniformly superior across all user-centric fairness metrics. Weighted approaches
[
26
]
can be incorporated when considering different fairness requirements.

[\lambda_{1}^{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}^{n}]
. This vector is composed of the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{Q}
.

From Lemma
1
,
\mathbf{z}\prec\mathbf{y}
, which indicates that
[\mathbf{y}]_{1}\geq[\mathbf{z}]_{1}
. To satisfy the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\mathbf{y}]_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\mathbf{z}]_{i}
, this requires an index subset
\{i\}
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{i}\leq[\mathbf{z}]_{i}
to compensate for the gap
[\mathbf{y}]_{1}-[\mathbf{z}]_{1}
(the compensation may not be precise). We then probably have another index
j
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{j}\geq[\mathbf{z}]_{j}
, this necessitates another index subset
\{i\}
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{i}\leq[\mathbf{z}]_{i}
to compensate for the gap
[\mathbf{y}]_{j}-[\mathbf{z}]_{j}
. In general, due to the constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\mathbf{z}]_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}[\mathbf{y}]_{i},1\leq k\leq n-1
, the index
i
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{i}\leq[\mathbf{z}]_{i}
to compensate for the gap
[\mathbf{y}]_{j}-[\mathbf{z}]_{j}
is larger than
j
. On the other hand, we can certainly have an index subset
\{i\}
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{i}\geq[\mathbf{z}]_{i}
, and the gap of
\sum_{i\in\{i\}}([\mathbf{y}]_{i}-[\mathbf{z}]_{i})
can be compensated by a single index
j
with
[\mathbf{y}]_{j}\leq[\mathbf{z}]_{j}
. Again, due to the constraints in Definition
1
, the index
j
must be larger than the indices in
\{i\}
.

We assume
\lambda_{2}^{i}p_{i}\geq\lambda_{2}^{i}q_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}^{i}p_{i}<\lambda_{2}^{i}q_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{2}^{i}q_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{2}^{i}p_{i}
,

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\lambda_{2}^{i}(p_{i}-q_{i})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\lambda_{2}^{i}(q_{i}-p_{i}).

(76)

According to the explanation in the previous paragraph, the indices in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
are, on average, smaller than the indices in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Since
\{\lambda_{2}^{i}\}
are in decreasing order with respect to
i
, the values
\{\lambda_{2}^{i}\}
for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
are, on average, larger than the values
\{\lambda_{2}^{i}\}
for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Thus, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\|^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\|^{2}=\text{tr}(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A}),\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}=\text{tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}).

(77)

Thus, we have
\text{tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{H})\leq\text{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{H})
. The proof is complete.

Due to the concavity of

\log_{2}(\mathbb{E}\{1+\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}}\})
.
Because
\log_{2}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\underset{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}}{\text{max}}\prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbb{E}\{1+\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}}\}\;\;\text{s.t. }\text{tr}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big(\mathbf{W}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{H}\big)\leq P.

\mathbb{E}\{1+\text{SINR}_{k,i}^{\text{MMSE}}\}=\mathbb{E}\{[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}^{-1}\}.

[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}>0,\forall i,k

[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}^{-1}

\mathbb{E}\big\{[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}^{-1}\big\}\geq(\mathbb{E}\{[\text{MMSE}_{k}]_{i,i}\})^{-1}

Substituting this lower bound into the objective, problem (
