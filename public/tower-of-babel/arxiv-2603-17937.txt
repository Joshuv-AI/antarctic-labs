Non-Fermi-liquid behaviour of electrons coupled to gauge phonons Preprint: APS/123-QED
Rutvij Gholap, Alexey Ermakov, Alexander Kazantsev, Mohammad Saeed Bahramy, Marco Polini, Alessandro Principi

Abstract
We identify overdamped gauge phonons as a new microscopic route to non-Fermi-liquid behaviour in Dirac materials. These phonons couple to electronic currents rather than densities, thereby realising a lattice analogue of transverse gauge-field mechanisms without requiring proximity to a quantum critical point. By computing the electronic self-energy with a phonon propagator dressed by electron-phonon interactions, we show that the low-energy behaviour is controlled by the orbital susceptibility χ \chi and a dimensionless damping parameter α ¯ \bar{\alpha} . In the overdamped regime, α ¯ ≫ 1 \bar{\alpha}\gg 1 , quasiparticles display strong deviations from Fermi-liquid theory. For χ > 0 \chi>0 , Fermi-liquid behaviour persists only in a parametrically narrow infrared window before crossing over to non-Fermi-liquid scaling. For χ < 0 \chi<0 , the Fermi-liquid regime is replaced by marginal-Fermi-liquid behaviour at the lowest energies, followed by a crossover to non-Fermi-liquid scaling. These results establish strain-induced gauge phonons as a promising source of anomalous metallic behaviour in systems such as twisted bilayer graphene.



Introduction—
Landau’s Fermi-liquid (FL) theory provides the canonical low-energy description of interacting metals in terms of long-lived fermionic quasiparticles
2
;
16
;
15
. In this picture, the phase space available for scattering is strongly restricted near the Fermi surface, so that at zero temperature the quasiparticle decay rate scales as
\Gamma\propto\varepsilon^{2}
, vanishing faster than the excitation energy
\varepsilon
. As a result, interactions renormalize quasiparticle properties, such as the effective mass and response functions, but preserve the basic quasiparticle description and the overall low-energy phenomenology of the metal.
However, many correlated metals exhibit robust non-Fermi-liquid (NFL) behaviour. In particular, an electrical resistivity that is approximately linear in temperature over broad parameter ranges is widely regarded as a hallmark of strange-metal phenomenology
6
;
19
. These observations motivate the search for generic microscopic mechanisms that can produce deviations from Fermi-liquid behaviour down to the lowest energies.

One standard route to the breakdown of Landau’s Fermi-liquid picture is the coupling of electrons to gapless bosonic collective fluctuations. A weak departure from Fermi-liquid behaviour occurs in the marginal-Fermi-liquid (MFL) scenario, in which the quasiparticle decay rate scales linearly with energy,
\Gamma\propto\varepsilon
, and is therefore not parametrically smaller than the quasiparticle energy
31
.

A more pronounced breakdown of the quasiparticle picture occurs in quantum-critical metals, which provide a canonical setting for NFL behaviour. For example, within the Hertz–Millis framework
13
;
21
, coupling to overdamped order-parameter fluctuations can strongly renormalize the low-energy fermionic self-energy. In two dimensions, this coupling can, in certain cases, produce a quasiparticle decay rate
\Gamma(\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{2/3}
, which becomes parametrically larger than the excitation energy as
\varepsilon\to 0
, signaling the breakdown of the quasiparticle description. Other power laws may arise depending on the universality class and on the structure of the critical fluctuations
32
;
1
;
20
;
23
.

Recent work has also highlighted spatial randomness as an additional ingredient in strange-metal phenomenology. In particular, in two-dimensional quantum-critical metals, random Yukawa couplings were shown to generate robust
T
-linear resistivity together with other universal strange-metal signatures
24
.

A further broad class of NFL states arises when fermions couple to overdamped transverse gauge fluctuations. Unlike quantum-critical scenarios, this mechanism does not require proximity to a continuous phase transition. In two dimensions, such an interaction can result in a fermion decay rate
\Gamma(\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{2/3}
, signaling the breakdown of the quasiparticle description
25
;
3
;
18
;
17
. Microscopically, the gauge field couples to the electronic current. The resulting low-energy interaction is therefore renormalized by the dynamical transverse current-current response, whose long-wavelength form includes both a regular
q^{2}
contribution (proportional to the orbital magnetic susceptibility
\chi
) and a Landau-damping term, controlled by a dimensionless parameter
\bar{\alpha}
. When
\bar{\alpha}\gg 1
, gauge fluctuations are overdamped and NFL behaviour emerges. A realization of this mechanism has recently been proposed in strongly coupled cavity QED near the onset of a superradiant transition
27
;
28
.
This suggests that any bosonic mode coupling predominantly to electronic currents, rather than densities, may provide an alternative route to NFL behaviour.

In this work, we show that
gauge phonons

29
, which couple to electronic currents rather than densities, can induce pronounced NFL behaviour. Such couplings arise naturally in crystals with non-orthogonal lattice vectors. In two dimensions, relevant examples include oblique and triangular lattices, as well as strained square or rectangular structures
30
. Motivated by the broad relevance of this mechanism, we focus here on experimentally accessible Dirac materials, in particular magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG), where lattice deformations act as effective valley-dependent gauge fields
33
;
12
.

We show this by computing the electronic self-energy at leading nontrivial order, with the transverse phonon propagator dressed by the electron-phonon interaction. When gauge phonons become strongly damped, they provide a new route to NFL behaviour, distinct from conventional order-parameter criticality and closely related to mechanisms based on transverse gauge fluctuations.

The resulting low-energy physics is controlled by the orbital response
\chi
and the damping parameter
\bar{\alpha}
. NFL behaviour develops for
\bar{\alpha}\gg 1
. We distinguish two cases. For
\chi>0
, the electronic self-energy exhibits a quantum-critical-like crossover structure. Although the system remains Fermi-liquid-like within an extremely narrow low-energy window, much narrower than in conventional Fermi liquids, it rapidly crosses over to an NFL regime. By contrast, for
\chi<0
, the softening of the gauge-phonon dispersion replaces the Fermi-liquid infrared behaviour by a marginal-Fermi-liquid one, which in turn crosses over to stronger NFL scaling at higher energies.

Model—

\hbar=1

where

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm e\text{-}ph}=\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{q}},\alpha,\beta}c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}-{\bm{q}},\alpha}\Big({\bm{A}}_{\bm{q}}\!\cdot\!{\bm{j}}_{\bm{q}}+V_{\bm{q}}\openone\Big)_{\alpha\beta}c_{{\bm{k}},\beta}~,

We consider the one-loop electron–phonon self-energy

\Sigma_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{n})=-\beta^{-1}\sum_{\omega_{m}}\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}D^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},i\omega_{m})

(2)

Here
\varepsilon_{n}=(2n+1)\pi/\beta
and
\omega_{m}=2m\pi/\beta

are fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies, respectively,

D^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},i\omega_{m})
is the dressed phonon propagator,
and

G^{(0)}_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{n})=1/(i\varepsilon_{n}-\xi_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})

is the bare electron Green’s function.

After analytic continuation
i\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon+i0^{+}
,
the imaginary part of the retarded self-energy can be written as

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma_{\lambda}^{(R)}({\bm{k}},\varepsilon)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\big[n_{\rm B}(\omega)+n_{\rm F}(\omega+\varepsilon)\big]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Im mD^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},\omega)\Im mG^{(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega)

(3)

We now focus on the low-energy self-energy at the Fermi surface.
For
{\bm{k}}\simeq{\bm{k}}_{\rm F}
and
T\to 0
, the chemical potential
coincides with the Fermi energy of the non-interacting system,

\mu=\varepsilon_{\rm F}=v_{\rm F}k_{F}
. In this limit the factor

n_{\rm B}(\omega)+n_{\rm F}(\omega+\varepsilon)
restricts the

|\varepsilon|
, while the

The current matrix elements therefore simplify to

\displaystyle j^{\rm L}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=v_{\rm F}\frac{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{2}e^{i(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varphi_{\bm{k}})/2},

(4)

\displaystyle j^{\rm T}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=v_{\rm F}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda}{2}e^{i(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varphi_{\bm{k}})/2}.

(5)

Since at the Fermi surface
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
, the longitudinal matrix
element vanishes and only the transverse channel contributes. This is

Dressed phonon propagator.—

We focus on the transverse–transverse (TT) propagator that enters the
low-energy self-energy at the Fermi surface. Since the transverse gauge
field is a linear combination of phonon coordinates (L and T), the bare TT propagator reads,

\displaystyle D^{(0),TT}({\bm{q}},\omega)=\sum_{\nu={\rm L,T}}g_{\nu}^{2}(q)\,

\displaystyle\times\big[\tilde{M}_{{\rm T},\nu}(\hat{\bm{q}})\big]^{2}\frac{2\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}{(\omega+i0^{+})^{2}-\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}^{2}}.

(6)

where
g_{\nu}(q)=(g_{2}/v_{\rm F})\times\sqrt{q^{2}/2\rho_{m}{\bar{\omega}}_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}
. For the purpose of scaling analysis, we approximate the longitudinal and
transverse acoustic branches by a single effective mode,

\omega_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}\simeq\omega_{{\bm{q}},{\rm T}}\equiv\bar{\omega}_{\bm{q}}
.
This simplification only affects overall prefactors and does not modify the
scaling exponents discussed below.

We now dress the phonon propagator by electron–hole excitations using an
RPA-like resummation. Since the electron–phonon interaction in
Eq. (
1
) couples the gauge field to the electronic current,
the polarization entering the Dyson equation is the transverse
current–current response. The dressed propagator therefore satisfies

\big[D^{TT}({\bm{q}},\omega)\big]^{-1}=\big[D^{(0),TT}({\bm{q}},\omega)\big]^{-1}-\chi^{(T)}({\bm{q}},\omega).

(7)

For a non-interacting electron liquid and in the regime

\omega\ll v_{\rm F}q\ll\varepsilon_{\rm F}
, the transverse
current–current response admits the low-energy expansion

\displaystyle\chi^{(T)}(q,\omega)\simeq\chi\,q^{2}-i\,\alpha\frac{\omega}{v_{\rm F}q}.

(8)

The coefficient of the imaginary term,
\alpha
, describes Landau damping due to particle–hole
excitations, while the real coefficient
\chi
determines the
stiffness of the transverse response. On dimensional grounds, one has

\alpha\sim\nu_{0}v_{\rm F}^{2}
and

\chi\sim\nu_{0}v_{\rm F}^{2}/k_{\rm F}^{2}
, where
\nu_{0}
is the
density of states at the Fermi energy. We also note that the orbital
magnetic susceptibility is proportional to
-\chi
.

Using Eq. (
8
), the dressed TT phonon propagator takes the
form

\displaystyle D^{TT}({\bm{q}},\omega)=g^{2}(q)\,\frac{2\bar{\omega}_{\bm{q}}}{(\omega+i0^{+})^{2}-\bar{\omega}_{\bm{q}}^{\,2}-\bar{\chi}q^{4}+i\bar{\alpha}q\omega}.

(9)

Here we introduced the effective parameters

\bar{\chi}=(g_{2}^{2}\chi)/(v_{\rm F}^{2}\rho_{m})
and

\bar{\alpha}=(g_{2}^{2}\alpha)/(v_{\rm F}^{3}\rho_{m})
.
The sign of
\bar{\chi}
determines whether the real part of the phonon
dispersion stiffens (
\bar{\chi}>0
, corresponding to a diamagnetic response) or softens (
\bar{\chi}<0
, corresponding to a paramagnetic response), while
\bar{\alpha}>0

ensures that the poles of the propagator lie in the lower half of the
complex-frequency plane.

To analyse the scaling behaviour, it is convenient to express the
self-energy in dimensionless form using the Fermi energy

\varepsilon_{F}=v_{\rm F}k_{\rm F}
. To this end, we define

\bar{q}=q/k_{\rm F},\bar{\omega}=\omega/(v_{\rm F}k_{\rm F}),\bar{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/(v_{\rm F}k_{\rm F}),
and rescale the susceptibility parameters to a dimensionless form such that,

\bar{\chi}\to\bar{\chi}k_{\rm F}^{2}/v_{\rm F}^{2}
and

\bar{\alpha}\to\bar{\alpha}/v_{\rm F}
. The self-energy is thus rewritten as,

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma_{+}^{(R)}(k_{\rm F},\bar{\varepsilon})=\int_{0}^{\bar{\varepsilon}}\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{\pi}\int_{\bar{\varepsilon}+\bar{\omega}}^{2+(\bar{\varepsilon}+\bar{\omega})}\frac{d\bar{q}}{\pi}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{q}|\bar{\varepsilon}+\bar{\omega}+1|}{\sqrt{(2\bar{q})^{2}-\big[(\bar{\varepsilon}+\bar{\omega}+1)^{2}-(1+\bar{q}^{2})\big]^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{\alpha}\bar{q}^{3}\bar{\omega}}{\big[\bar{\omega}^{2}-(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}\bar{q}^{2}-\bar{\chi}\bar{q}^{4}\big]^{2}+(\bar{\alpha}\bar{q}\bar{\omega})^{2}}.

(10)

The intermediate steps leading to Eq. (
10
)
are presented in Appendix
C
.

Results.—

Figure
2
shows the numerical evaluation of
Eq. (
10
) in the regime of strong
Landau damping,
\bar{\alpha}\gg 1
. In this regime, the self-energy deviates
from the standard Fermi-liquid form, above the characteristic crossover scale

\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}\sim\frac{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{F})^{3}}{\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{\bar{\chi}}}.

(11)

For
\bar{\chi}>0
, the low-energy regime remains Fermi-liquid like, while at
higher energies the self-energy crosses over to a regime controlled by the
Landau-damped phonon, whose characteristic momentum scales as

q\sim(\bar{\alpha}\omega/\bar{\chi})^{1/3}
. Figures
2
(a) and

2
(b) show this crossover for different values of
\bar{\chi}
and

\bar{\alpha}
, which shift the location of
\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}
on a log-log
scale. The corresponding local logarithmic slope is shown in
Figs.
2
(d) and
2
(e), where we define

\zeta(\bar{\varepsilon})=d\ln\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{+}(k_{F},\bar{\varepsilon})/d\ln\bar{\varepsilon}
. At the lowest energies,

\zeta(\bar{\varepsilon})\to 2
is consistent with Fermi-liquid behaviour,
whereas above the crossover, it approaches the non-Fermi-liquid value

\zeta(\bar{\varepsilon})\simeq 2/3
. This is in agreement with the scaling found more
generally in theories of fermions coupled to overdamped transverse bosonic
modes
20
;
23
. A closely related self-energy study was
recently reported by Rao and Piazza
27
for electrons coupled
to a critical transverse electromagnetic mode. In both problems, the
essential ingredient is a transverse boson that becomes Landau damped by the
electronic current response, leading to the same
\varepsilon^{2/3}

self-energy scaling. In the present case, however, the relevant mode is a
phonon-generated gauge field, and for
\bar{\chi}>0
the
q^{4}
term stiffens the
dressed phonon dispersion. Therefore, Figure
2
uniquely shows a
crossover from low-energy Fermi-liquid behaviour to the

\bar{\varepsilon}^{2/3}
regime above the scale
\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}
. An
analytical estimate for
\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}
, together with the asymptotic
behaviour of the self-energy in the two regimes, is derived in
appendix
D
.

Figures
2
(c) and
2
(f) show the behaviour for
\bar{\chi}<0
,
corresponding to a paramagnetic current response. In this regime, the

q^{4}
term softens the dressed phonon dispersion, signalling the proximity
to an instability of the transverse mode. As a consequence, the self-energy
acquires a qualitatively different scaling structure. In contrast to the

\bar{\chi}>0
case, where the infrared regime remains Fermi-liquid like, here the
infrared behaviour is no longer described by a Fermi liquid. Instead,
the local logarithmic slope approaches
\zeta(\bar{\varepsilon})\simeq 1
,
corresponding to
\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{+}(\bar{\varepsilon})\propto\bar{\varepsilon}
,
which is the characteristic signature of a marginal Fermi liquid. As
proposed in previous works
11
;
27
, such an
instability may be associated with a superradiant phase transition. The same
crossover scale
\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}
continues to govern the transition
between regimes. While the higher-energy behaviour retains the non-Fermi-liquid

\bar{\varepsilon}^{2/3}
scaling associated with the Landau-damped mode,
the infrared regime is dominated by a shell of softened phonon modes at the
characteristic finite momentum

\bar{q}_{\rm shell}\sim c_{\rm ph}/(v_{F}\sqrt{|\bar{\chi}|})
.
This is reminiscent of Brazovskii-type soft-mode physics
5
,
in which the bosonic spectrum softens not at
q=0
but on a finite-momentum
shell, thereby enhancing the phase space of soft fluctuations. In this
respect, the
\bar{\chi}<0
regime is also analogous to a finite-
q
instability,
as in a charge-density-wave problem, but with the important caveat that
the softened modes lie on a shell of momenta rather than at a single
ordering wavevector.

The physical content of this regime is distinct from the conventional
phenomenological marginal-Fermi-liquid picture. In the original proposal of
Varma
et al.

31
, the linear-in-energy
scattering rate was introduced phenomenologically in terms of a broad
critical bosonic spectrum. Here, by contrast, the marginal-Fermi-liquid
scaling emerges microscopically for
\bar{\chi}<0
. Figures
2
(c) and

2
(f), together with the analytically derived asymptotic behaviour

\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{+}(\bar{\varepsilon})\propto\bar{\varepsilon}
in
appendix
D
, show that the present model does not merely
reproduce a marginal-Fermi-liquid exponent, but provides a microscopic route
to marginal-Fermi-liquid behaviour together with its crossover into the
higher-energy non-Fermi-liquid regime, both controlled by the same scale

\bar{\varepsilon}_{*}
.

We now turn to material estimates for monolayer graphene and magic-angle
twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG). To connect the effective theory to
experiment, we estimate the parameters entering the dressed transverse phonon
propagator in Eq. (
9
) from the transverse current
response. For the static term, we use the relation
9

\bar{\chi}=\Re(\chi_{\rm orb})/\mu_{0}e^{2}
, where
\chi_{\rm orb}
is the orbital
magnetic susceptibility. Using the value reported in
Ref.
9
, we take for monolayer graphene, in the near-charge-neutrality regime, where the orbital
susceptibility is approximately on a plateau,
\chi=-5.22\times 10^{29}\ {\rm m^{2}\,J^{-1}\,s^{-2}}
. For the
dissipative term, we use the leading-order current-response result for doped
graphene
26
,

\Im m\chi_{\rm orb}(q,\omega)=\varepsilon_{\rm F}/(2\pi\hbar^{2})\,\omega/(v_{\rm F}q)
, from which we identify

\alpha=\varepsilon_{\rm F}/(2\pi\hbar^{2})
. Taking

\varepsilon_{\rm F}=0.1\ {\rm eV}
,
v_{\rm F}=10^{6}\ {\rm m\,s^{-1}}
, and

c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F}=10^{-2}
for monolayer graphene, we obtain

\alpha=2.29\times 10^{47}\ {\rm J^{-1}\,s^{-2}}
. Using the definitions of
the rescaled parameters, together with the mass density of a single graphene
layer, this gives the effective dimensionless values

\bar{\chi}=5.3\times 10^{-9}
and
\bar{\alpha}=9.9\times 10^{-8}
. The small magnitude
of these parameters implies that a non-Fermi-liquid regime does not develop
at accessible values of
\bar{\varepsilon}
in monolayer graphene, as seen in
Fig.
3
.

In contrast, for MATBG the Fermi velocity is strongly reduced. Taking

v_{\rm F}^{\ast}\sim 10^{4}\ {\rm m\,s^{-1}}
,

\varepsilon_{\rm F}\sim 1\ {\rm meV}

22
, and

c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F}^{\ast}\sim 10^{-1}

35
, the
same procedure yields substantially enhanced effective parameters,

\bar{\chi}=5.3\times 10^{-3}
and
\bar{\alpha}=0.1
. This enhancement originates from
the strong suppression of
v_{\rm F}^{\ast}
together with the larger
low-energy density of states in the moiré bands. Since both the static
coefficient
\bar{\chi}
and the damping coefficient
\bar{\alpha}
scale with the
electronic response, increasing the density of states enhances both
parameters and pushes the crossover scale closer to the Fermi surface. The
same mechanism would also operate in monolayer graphene, where increasing
the density of states would move the crossover to more accessible fractions
of
\varepsilon_{\rm F}
.

MATBG is particularly interesting because its van Hove singularities lie
much closer to charge neutrality than in monolayer graphene
4
. As a result,
proximity to the van Hove singularity can make entry into a paramagnetic
orbital-response regime plausible, rather than the strongly diamagnetic
response more typical of monolayer graphene. In this connection, previous
studies have discussed marginal-Fermi-liquid behaviour in MATBG and its
competition with other interaction effects
10
;
7
. Within the present framework, this makes MATBG a
more natural platform for the
\bar{\chi}<0
regime discussed in Fig.
2
,
where the infrared self-energy becomes marginal-Fermi-liquid like.
Fig.
3
therefore, highlights a plausible route toward marginal
and non-Fermi-liquid behaviour in real materials.

Conclusions—
In this work, we studied the electronic self-energy generated by the coupling of electronic currents to gauge phonons. The transverse phonon propagator entering this calculation is itself dressed by the electron-phonon interaction. Since the phonon self-energy is proportional to the transverse current-current response, the resulting low-energy behaviour is controlled by two parameters: the orbital susceptibility
\chi
and the dimensionless damping parameter
\bar{\alpha}
. We showed that when
\bar{\alpha}\gg 1
,
i.e.
when phonons are overdamped, the system exhibits robust non-Fermi-liquid behaviour reminiscent of both quantum-critical metals and systems coupled to transverse gauge fluctuations.

We also found that the sign of
\chi
controls the low-energy structure of the quasiparticle decay rate. For
\chi>0
, the decay rate remains Fermi-liquid-like only asymptotically close to the Fermi surface and rapidly crosses over to non-Fermi-liquid scaling at higher energies. The corresponding Fermi-liquid window is much narrower than in conventional metals and shrinks with increasing
\bar{\alpha}
. This behaviour resembles that of a system close to, but not exactly at, quantum criticality, where the onset of non-Fermi-liquid behaviour is controlled by the bosonic gap.

More intriguingly, for
\chi<0
the Fermi-liquid asymptotic regime is entirely absent. The softened dispersion of the dressed transverse mode instead produces a marginal-Fermi-liquid-like regime at the lowest energies, followed by a crossover to non-Fermi-liquid behaviour at higher energies. The corresponding crossover scale is set by the competition between the bare transverse-phonon dispersion and the orbital-susceptibility correction.

These results identify strain-induced gauge phonons as a novel route to non-Fermi-liquid physics in Dirac materials, with magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene providing a particularly promising platform.

More broadly, the same mechanism may be relevant in systems with different lattice geometries, since the existence of gauge phonons that couple to electronic currents does not rely on hexagonal symmetry alone. Similar gauge-like phonon couplings can arise in other Dirac lattices and may also occur in distorted square-lattice systems where pseudo-gauge fields emerge once lattice symmetry is reduced
30
. In this sense, the present mechanism may offer a complementary microscopic route toward strange-metal phenomenology in a wider class of materials. Assessing its possible relevance to systems such as the cuprates, where linear-in-temperature transport remains a central open problem
6
;
19
, requires a more detailed investigation that lies beyond the scope of the present work.

Appendix A
Derivation of the strain-induced electron–phonon coupling in graphene

In this appendix we derive the low-energy scalar and vector electron–phonon
couplings used in Eq. (
1
), starting from the modulation of
nearest-neighbour hoppings by a smooth in-plane displacement field

{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})
. In particular, we recover the explicit scalar
deformation-potential coupling
V_{\bm{q}}
and the strain-induced gauge field

A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}
entering the low-energy interaction Hamiltonian.
The full low-energy interaction Hamiltonian is

{\cal H}_{\rm e\text{-}ph}=\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{q}},\alpha,\beta}c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}-{\bm{q}},\alpha}\Big({\bm{A}}_{\bm{q}}\!\cdot\!{\bm{j}}_{\bm{q}}+V_{\bm{q}}\openone\Big)_{\alpha\beta}c_{{\bm{k}},\beta},

(12)

where the gauge field couples to the longitudinal and transverse current
components

j_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}=v_{\rm F}\,\hat{\bm{q}}\!\cdot\!{\bm{\sigma}}
and

j_{{\bm{q}},{\rm T}}=v_{\rm F}\,(\hat{\bm{z}}\times\hat{\bm{q}})\!\cdot\!{\bm{\sigma}}
,
with
{\bm{\sigma}}=(\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y})
. The scalar coupling is

V_{\bm{q}}=g_{1}\,q\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}}}\,\Big(a_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}\Big),

(13)

while the gauge-field components take the form

A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}=\sum_{\nu^{\prime}={\rm L,T}}g_{\nu^{\prime}}(q)\,\tilde{M}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(\hat{\bm{q}})\,\Big(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}\Big),

(14)

with

g_{\nu}(q)=\frac{g_{2}}{v_{\rm F}}\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}},\qquad\tilde{M}(\hat{\bm{q}})=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}&-\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\\
\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}&\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

We now derive these expressions from the strain dependence of the
nearest-neighbour hoppings.

Let
{\bm{\delta}}_{n}
(
n=1,2,3
) denote the undeformed nearest-neighbour
vectors, with
|{\bm{\delta}}_{n}|=a
and

\hat{\bm{\delta}}_{n}={\bm{\delta}}_{n}/a
. To linear order in gradients, the
deformed bond vectors are

{\bm{\delta}}^{\prime}_{n}({\bm{r}})={\bm{\delta}}_{n}+{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}}+{\bm{\delta}}_{n})-{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})\simeq(\mathbb{I}+\nabla{\bm{u}})\,{\bm{\delta}}_{n}.

(16)

The corresponding fractional bond-length change is

\frac{|{\bm{\delta}}^{\prime}_{n}|-a}{a}\simeq\hat{\bm{\delta}}_{n}^{\,T}\,u({\bm{r}})\,\hat{\bm{\delta}}_{n},\qquad u_{ij}({\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{i}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{i}\big),

(17)

where
u_{ij}
is the strain tensor.

We model the strain-induced hopping modulation as

33
;
8

t_{n}({\bm{r}})=t\exp\!\left[-\beta\left(\frac{|{\bm{\delta}}^{\prime}_{n}({\bm{r}})|}{a}-1\right)\right]\simeq t+\delta t_{n}({\bm{r}}),

(18)

which gives, to linear order,

\delta t_{n}({\bm{r}})\simeq-\beta t\,\hat{\bm{\delta}}_{n}^{\,T}\,u({\bm{r}})\,\hat{\bm{\delta}}_{n}.

(19)

For graphene one has
\beta\simeq 3

34
.

Near valley
{\bm{K}}_{\tau}
with
\tau=\pm 1
, we define

\delta f_{\tau}({\bm{r}})\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{3}\delta t_{n}({\bm{r}})\,e^{i{\bm{K}}_{\tau}\cdot{\bm{\delta}}_{n}}.

(20)

For the standard nearest-neighbour vectors one finds

34
;
8

\delta f_{\tau}({\bm{r}})=\tau\frac{3\beta t}{4}\Big[(u_{xx}-u_{yy})-i\,2u_{xy}\Big].

(21)

This correction enters the Dirac Hamiltonian as a valley-odd pseudo-gauge
field. Writing
\Psi_{\tau}({\bm{k}})
for the two-component pseudospin spinor in
valley
\tau
, the strain contribution takes the form

{\cal H}_{\rm strain}=v_{\rm F}\sum_{\tau=\pm 1}\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{q}}}\Psi^{\dagger}_{\tau}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{q}})\,\big[\tau\,{\bm{A}}({\bm{q}})\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}\big]\,\Psi_{\tau}({\bm{k}}),

(22)

so that the corresponding current operator is

{\bm{j}}=\delta H/\delta{\bm{A}}=v_{\rm F}{\bm{\sigma}}
. The real-space
pseudo-gauge field is therefore

{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})=g_{2}\begin{pmatrix}u_{xx}({\bm{r}})-u_{yy}({\bm{r}})\\
-2u_{xy}({\bm{r}})\end{pmatrix},\qquad g_{2}=\frac{3\beta t}{4}.

(23)

We now express
{\bm{A}}({\bm{q}})
in terms of phonon modes by quantizing the
displacement field as

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}({\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\bm{q}},\nu={\rm L,T}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}}\,{\bm{e}}_{\nu}(\hat{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle\times\Big(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu}e^{i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\Big),

(24)

where
{\bm{e}}_{\rm L}=\hat{\bm{q}}
and

{\bm{e}}_{\rm T}=\hat{\bm{z}}\times\hat{\bm{q}}
. Since

u_{ij}({\bm{q}})=\frac{i}{2}(q_{i}u_{j}+q_{j}u_{i})
, the combinations entering
Eq. (
23
) are

\displaystyle u_{xx}-u_{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(q_{x}u_{x}-q_{y}u_{y}),

(25)

\displaystyle-2u_{xy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i(q_{x}u_{y}+q_{y}u_{x}).

(26)

Writing the displacement in the polarization basis

{\bm{u}}=u_{\rm L}\hat{\bm{q}}+u_{\rm T}(\hat{\bm{z}}\times\hat{\bm{q}})
,
one has

u_{x}=\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm L}-\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm T}
and

u_{y}=\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm L}+\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm T}
.
Projecting the gauge field onto longitudinal and transverse components,

A_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}=\hat{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{A}}({\bm{q}})
and

A_{{\bm{q}},{\rm T}}=(\hat{\bm{z}}\times\hat{\bm{q}})\cdot{\bm{A}}({\bm{q}})
,
one obtains

\displaystyle A_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{2}q\Big(\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm L}-\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm T}\Big),

(27)

\displaystyle A_{{\bm{q}},{\rm T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{2}q\Big(\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm L}+\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,u_{\rm T}\Big).

(28)

Here the overall factor of
i
originating from gradients has been absorbed
into the phase convention for the phonon operators, so that

A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}
is chosen real. Using

u_{\nu}({\bm{q}})=\sqrt{1/(2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu})}\,(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu})
,
Eq. (
28
) reduces to
Eq. (
14
) with

g_{\nu}(q)
and
\tilde{M}(\hat{\bm{q}})
given in
Eq. (
15
).

Finally, the scalar deformation-potential coupling follows from

V({\bm{r}})=g_{1}\big(u_{xx}+u_{yy}\big)
. Using Eq. (
24
),
one finds

u_{xx}({\bm{q}},\nu)+u_{yy}({\bm{q}},\nu)=iq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}}\,\big(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu}\big)({\bm{e}}_{\nu}\cdot\hat{\bm{q}}),

(29)

so that only longitudinal phonons contribute. This yields
Eq. (
13
),

V_{\bm{q}}=g_{1}\,q\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}}}\,\Big(a_{{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},{\rm L}}\Big).

(30)

For completeness, the explicit gauge-field coupling entering the main text is
therefore

A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}=\sum_{\nu^{\prime}={\rm L,T}}g_{\nu^{\prime}}(q)\,\tilde{M}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(\hat{\bm{q}})\,\Big(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}\Big),

(31)

with

g_{\nu}(q)=\frac{g_{2}}{v_{\rm F}}\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho_{m}\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}},~\tilde{M}(\hat{\bm{q}})=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}&-\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}\\
\sin\varphi_{\bm{q}}&\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\end{pmatrix}.

(32)

Appendix B
Self-energy derivation

In this appendix we provide the intermediate steps leading from the
Matsubara Fock self-energy, Eq. (
2
), to the real-frequency
expression Eq. (
3
) and to the transverse-only form
used in the low-energy analysis.

We begin by recalling the dressed phonon propagator as the Matsubara
correlator of the vector-potential components,

D^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},i\omega_{m})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,e^{i\omega_{m}\tau}\,\big\langle{\cal T}_{\tau}A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}(\tau)A_{-{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}(0)\big\rangle.

(33)

The bare electron Green’s function is

G^{(0)}_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{n})=\frac{1}{i\varepsilon_{n}-\xi_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}}.

(34)

For later use, the Cartesian current matrix elements between incoming and
outgoing Dirac states are

\displaystyle j^{x}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=v_{\rm F}\frac{\lambda e^{-i\varphi_{\bm{k}}}+\lambda^{\prime}e^{i\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}}}{2},

(35)

\displaystyle j^{y}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=v_{\rm F}\frac{i\lambda e^{-i\varphi_{\bm{k}}}-i\lambda^{\prime}e^{i\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}}}{2}.

(36)

Since we need the retarded self-energy, we analytically continue

i\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon+i0^{+}
. Starting from Eq. (
2
),
we rewrite the Matsubara sum as a contour integral,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},i\varepsilon_{n})=-\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,n_{\rm B}(z)\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times j^{\nu}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime}}j^{\nu^{\prime}}_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda}D^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},z)G^{(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},i\varepsilon_{n}+z).

(37)

Here
D
and
G^{(0)}
are understood as the bosonic and fermionic
propagators analytically continued away from the Matsubara axis. The
integrand has branch cuts at
\Im m(z)=0
and
\Im m(z)=-i\varepsilon_{n}
.
Integrating along these branch cuts and continuing

i\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon+i0^{+}
yields

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\lambda}^{(R)}({\bm{k}},\varepsilon)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}j^{\nu}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime}}j^{\nu^{\prime}}_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda}

\displaystyle\times\Big[n_{\rm B}(\omega)\,\Im mD^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},\omega+i\eta)\,G^{(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega+i\eta)

\displaystyle-n_{\rm F}(\omega+\varepsilon)\,D^{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},\omega-i\eta)\,\Im mG^{(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega+i\eta)\Big],

(38)

with
\eta\to 0^{+}
. Taking the imaginary part gives

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{\lambda}({\bm{k}},\varepsilon)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\,\big[n_{\rm B}(\omega)+n_{\rm F}(\omega+\varepsilon)\big]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\Im mD^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},\omega)\,\Im mG^{(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega)

\displaystyle\times j^{\nu}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime}}j^{\nu^{\prime}}_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime};{\bm{k}},\lambda},

(39)

which is Eq. (
3
) of the main text.

We now take the low-energy limit at the Fermi surface. Evaluating the bare
retarded Green’s function,

G^{R,(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{p}},\epsilon)=1/(\epsilon-\xi_{{\bm{p}},\lambda^{\prime}}+i0^{+})
, gives

\Im mG^{R,(0)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega)=-\pi\,\delta\big(\varepsilon+\omega-\xi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime}}\big),

(40)

which pins the intermediate fermionic state to the mass shell

\xi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\lambda^{\prime}}=\varepsilon+\omega
.

In the
T\to 0
limit, the thermal factor in
Eq. (
39
) simplifies to

n_{\rm B}(\omega)+n_{\rm F}(\omega+\varepsilon)=-\Theta(-\omega)+\Theta(-\omega-\varepsilon).

(41)

For
\varepsilon>0
, this equals
-1
for
\omega\in(-\varepsilon,0)

and vanishes otherwise, while for
\varepsilon<0
it equals
+1
for

\omega\in(0,-\varepsilon)
. This yields the compact frequency window

\omega\in[\min(0,-\varepsilon),\max(0,-\varepsilon)]
.

Finally, for
{\bm{k}}\simeq{\bm{k}}_{\rm F}
, the on-shell condition in
Eq. (
40
) implies that

{\bm{k}}^{\prime}={\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}
also lies close to the Fermi surface. In this
limit, the longitudinal and transverse current matrix elements can be
constructed from Eq. (
36
) as

\displaystyle j^{\rm L}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{j}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=v_{\rm F}\frac{i(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)}{2}\,e^{i(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varphi_{\bm{k}})/2},

(42)

\displaystyle j^{\rm T}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(\hat{\bm{z}}\times\hat{\bm{q}})\cdot{\bm{j}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda;{\bm{k}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}=v_{\rm F}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda}{2}\,e^{i(\varphi_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varphi_{\bm{k}})/2}.

(43)

At the Fermi surface one has
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
, so the longitudinal
matrix element vanishes and only the transverse channel survives. Equation
(
39
) therefore reduces to

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma^{(R)}_{+}({\bm{k}},\varepsilon)=-v_{\rm F}^{2}\int_{\min(0,-\varepsilon)}^{\max(0,-\varepsilon)}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Im mD^{TT}({\bm{q}},\omega)\,\Im mG^{(0)}_{+}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega),

(44)

which is the transverse-only form used in the main text.

Appendix C
Bare and RPA-dressed transverse phonon propagator

In this appendix we summarize the intermediate steps entering the dressed
transverse phonon propagator and the dimensionless self-energy used in the
main text. We first derive the bare transverse–transverse (TT) propagator
from the gauge-field coupling, then discuss its RPA-like dressing in the
transverse current channel, and finally show the angular integration used
to obtain the reduced self-energy.

The gauge-field components are linear combinations of phonon coordinates, as seen in Eq.
32
,
The bosonic propagator is canonically defined as

\displaystyle D^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}({\bm{q}},i\omega_{m})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,e^{i\omega_{m}\tau}\left\langle{\cal T}_{\tau}\,A_{{\bm{q}},\nu}(\tau)\,A_{-{\bm{q}},\nu^{\prime}}(0)\right\rangle.

(45)

Using the free-phonon correlator

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,e^{i\omega_{m}\tau}\Big\langle{\cal T}_{\tau}\,\big(a_{{\bm{q}},\nu}(\tau)+a^{\dagger}_{-{\bm{q}},\nu}(\tau)\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(a_{-{\bm{q}},\nu}(0)+a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{q}},\nu}(0)\big)\Big\rangle=\frac{2\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}{(i\omega_{m})^{2}-\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}^{2}},

(46)

and one finds for the bare TT component

\displaystyle D^{(0),TT}({\bm{q}},i\omega_{m})=\sum_{\nu={\rm L,T}}g_{\nu}^{2}(q)\

\displaystyle\times\big[\tilde{M}_{{\rm T},\nu}(\hat{\bm{q}})\big]^{2}\frac{2\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}}{(i\omega_{m})^{2}-\omega_{{\bm{q}},\nu}^{2}}.

(47)

We now show the angular integration used after inserting the dressed
propagator into the low-energy self-energy. Defining

\displaystyle{\cal I}(k,q,\varepsilon+\omega+\mu)\equiv\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi_{\bm{q}}}{2\pi}\,\Im m\,G^{(0)}_{+}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}},\varepsilon+\omega+i0^{+})

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\pi}d\varphi_{\bm{q}}\,\delta\!\left(\varepsilon+\omega+\mu-v_{\rm F}|{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}|\right),

(48)

the delta function implies
\varepsilon+\omega+\mu>0
and, choosing

\varphi_{\bm{k}}=0
,

\displaystyle\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}=\frac{(\varepsilon+\omega+\mu)^{2}-v_{\rm F}^{2}(k^{2}+q^{2})}{2v_{\rm F}^{2}kq}.

(49)

The condition
-1\leq\cos\varphi_{\bm{q}}\leq 1
restricts the momentum
integration to
q_{\rm min}<q<q_{\rm max}
, with

\displaystyle v_{\rm F}q_{\rm min}

\displaystyle=\max\!\Big(0,\;v_{\rm F}k-\mu-\varepsilon-\omega,\;\varepsilon+\omega+\mu-v_{\rm F}k\Big),

\displaystyle v_{\rm F}q_{\rm max}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon+\omega+\mu+v_{\rm F}k.

(50)

Under these conditions,

\displaystyle{\cal I}(k,q,\varepsilon+\omega+\mu)=-\,2\,|\varepsilon+\omega+\mu|

\displaystyle\times\Big[(2v_{\rm F}^{2}kq)^{2}-\big((\varepsilon+\omega+\mu)^{2}-v_{\rm F}^{2}(k^{2}+q^{2})\big)^{2}\Big]^{-1/2}.

(51)

Substituting this result into the low-energy self-energy and then
rescaling to dimensionless variables leads to
Eq. (
10
) in the main text.

Appendix D
Power laws of the self-energy

In this appendix we extract the low-energy power laws of the imaginary part
of the retarded self-energy at the Fermi surface from the low-energy form of
the dressed TT phonon propagator. Throughout we set

{\bm{k}}\simeq{\bm{k}}_{\rm F}
and consider

0<\varepsilon\ll\mu
, so that at
T=0
the frequency transfer is
restricted to
\omega\in[-\varepsilon,0]
. It is convenient to write the
self-energy in the schematic form

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{+}(k_{\rm F},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\simeq-C\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\int_{q_{\rm min}}^{q_{\rm max}}\frac{dq}{\pi}\,{\cal K}(q,\omega,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{\alpha}\,q^{3}|\omega|}{\big[\omega^{2}-(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}q^{2}-\bar{\chi}q^{4}\big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}q^{2}\omega^{2}},

(52)

where
C=g_{2}^{2}/\rho_{m}
absorbs smooth prefactors, and
{\cal K}
denotes the kinematic
factor in Eq .
51
. For
{\bm{k}}\simeq{\bm{k}}_{\rm F}
this
factor is smooth and finite away from the integration endpoints. At the endpoints,
{\cal K}
has a singularity, which is integrable using the Duffy coordinate transformation. Thus, the
scaling is governed by the bosonic kernel.

It is useful to keep in mind that the frequency integral is dominated by

|\omega|\sim\varepsilon
. The scaling of the self-energy is therefore
determined by the characteristic momenta selected by the peak of the bosonic
factor. In the large
\bar{\alpha}
regime the dressed phonon is overdamped,
and this is precisely the regime in which anomalous scaling emerges.
However, overdamping alone does not guarantee non-Fermi-liquid behaviour,
depending on which momentum region dominates the integral, the self-energy
may still remain Fermi-liquid-like.

D.1
Overdamped reduction for large
\bar{\alpha}

When
\bar{\alpha}
is large, the characteristic momenta selected by the
bosonic kernel satisfy
q\gg|\omega|
in the regimes of interest. One may
then neglect the
\omega^{2}
term inside the square brackets of
Eq. (
52
), so that

\displaystyle\big[\omega^{2}-(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}q^{2}-\bar{\chi}q^{4}\big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}q^{2}\omega^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\simeq q^{2}\Big[(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}+\bar{\chi}q^{2}\Big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}\omega^{2}.

(53)

The bosonic factor then reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\alpha}\,q^{3}|\omega|}{\big[\omega^{2}-(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}q^{2}-\bar{\chi}q^{4}\big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}q^{2}\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\bar{\alpha}\,q|\omega|}{q^{2}\big[(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}+\bar{\chi}q^{2}\big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}\omega^{2}}.

(54)

The scaling of the self-energy is therefore controlled by the momenta at
which the denominator of Eq. (
54
) is minimal.

D.2
Fermi-liquid-like regime for
\bar{\chi}>0

We first consider the regime in which the quartic term is negligible,

i.e.
when
\bar{\chi}q^{2}\ll(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}
. In this case
the denominator of Eq. (
54
) reduces to

(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{4}q^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}\omega^{2},

(55)

so the integrand is peaked at

\displaystyle q_{\rm FL}\sim\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}}\,|\omega|.

(56)

Since the frequency integral is restricted to
|\omega|\lesssim\varepsilon
,
the dominant contribution comes from
|\omega|\sim\varepsilon
, giving

q_{\rm FL}\sim\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}}\,\varepsilon.

(57)

Thus
q
scales linearly with
\varepsilon
. We may then set

\omega=\bar{\omega}\,\varepsilon
and
q=\bar{q}\,\varepsilon
in
Eq. (
52
). Since the measure contributes

d\omega\,dq\sim\varepsilon^{2}
, while both
{\cal K}
and the overdamped
kernel remain finite in this scaling limit, one obtains

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma_{+}^{(R)}(k_{\rm F},\varepsilon)\simeq-\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{m}}\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{\pi}\,\varepsilon\int_{1+\bar{\omega}}^{2v_{\rm F}k_{\rm F}/\varepsilon+(1+\bar{\omega})}\frac{d\bar{q}}{\pi}\,\varepsilon

\displaystyle\times\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\bar{q}\,|1+\bar{\omega}+\varepsilon_{\rm F}/\varepsilon|}{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{4\varepsilon_{\rm F}^{2}\bar{q}^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}-\Big[(1+\bar{\omega}+\varepsilon_{\rm F}/\varepsilon)^{2}-\varepsilon_{\rm F}^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}-\bar{q}^{2}\Big]^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\alpha\,\bar{q}\bar{\omega}}{\varepsilon^{2}\Big((c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{4}\bar{q}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\bar{\omega}^{2}\Big)}

\displaystyle\to-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{m}}\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{\pi}\int_{1+\bar{\omega}}^{\infty}\frac{d\bar{q}}{\pi}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{q}}{2\sqrt{\bar{q}^{2}-(1+\bar{\omega})^{2}}}\frac{\bar{\alpha}\,\bar{q}\bar{\omega}}{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{4}\bar{q}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\bar{\omega}^{2}}.

(58)

Hence the self-energy remains Fermi-liquid like, even though the phonon mode
is overdamped.

D.3
Non-Fermi-liquid regime for
\bar{\chi}>0

We now consider the regime in which the quartic term dominates over the
acoustic piece,

(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}\ll\bar{\chi}q^{2}.

(59)

Then the
q
-dependent part of the denominator in
Eq. (
54
) becomes

\sim\bar{\chi}^{2}q^{6}
, and the bosonic kernel is peaked at

\displaystyle q_{\rm NFL}\sim\left(\frac{\bar{\alpha}|\omega|}{\bar{\chi}}\right)^{1/3}.

(60)

Again taking
|\omega|\sim\varepsilon
, this gives

q_{\rm NFL}\sim\left(\frac{\bar{\alpha}\varepsilon}{\bar{\chi}}\right)^{1/3}.

(61)

In this regime
q_{\rm NFL}\gg|\omega|
parametrically as

\varepsilon\to 0
, so the overdamped approximation is self-consistent.
We now scale

\omega=\bar{\omega}\,\varepsilon,\qquad q=\bar{q}\left(\frac{\bar{\alpha}\varepsilon}{\bar{\chi}}\right)^{1/3}.

(62)

Then Eq. (
52
)

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma_{+}^{(R)}(k_{\rm F},\varepsilon)\to-\varepsilon^{2/3}\frac{g_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{m}}\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\bar{q}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{\alpha}\,\bar{q}\bar{\omega}}{\bar{\chi}^{2}\bar{q}^{6}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\bar{\omega}^{2}}.

(63)

The remaining integral is finite, so that

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma^{R}_{+}(k_{\rm F},\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{2/3}.

(64)

D.4
Crossover scale for
\bar{\chi}>0

The crossover between the Fermi-liquid like and non-Fermi-liquid regimes
occurs when the characteristic momenta
Eqs. (
56
) and (
60
) become comparable:

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}}\,\varepsilon\sim\left(\frac{\bar{\alpha}\varepsilon}{\bar{\chi}}\right)^{1/3}.

(65)

Solving for
\varepsilon
gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\ast}\sim\frac{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{3}}{\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{\bar{\chi}}}.

(66)

For
\varepsilon\ll\varepsilon_{\ast}
the self-energy is Fermi-liquid like,
Eq. (
58
), whereas for
\varepsilon\gg\varepsilon_{\ast}
it crosses
over to the non-Fermi-liquid form Eq. (
64
).

D.5
Soft-shell scaling for
\bar{\chi}<0

We now consider the case
\bar{\chi}<0
. Writing
\bar{\chi}=-|\bar{\chi}|
,
the real part of the bosonic denominator softens at the finite momentum

\displaystyle q_{\rm shell}=\frac{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})}{\sqrt{|\bar{\chi}|}}.

(67)

This is the soft shell discussed in the main text.

In the overdamped limit, the bosonic denominator becomes

q^{2}\Big[(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|q^{2}\Big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}\omega^{2},

(68)

which is minimized near
q=q_{\rm{shell}}
. We therefore write

q=q_{\rm shell}+\delta q,\qquad|\delta q|\ll q_{\rm{shell}}.

(69)

Expanding the real part of the denominator around
q_{\rm{shell}}
gives

\displaystyle(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|q^{2}

\displaystyle=(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|(q_{\rm{shell}}+\delta q)^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq-2|\bar{\chi}|q_{\rm{shell}}\,\delta q,

(70)

since
(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|q_{\rm{shell}}^{2}=0
by definition of

q_{\rm{shell}}
. Substituting this into the denominator yields

\displaystyle q^{2}\Big[(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|q^{2}\Big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\omega^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\simeq 4|\chi|^{2}q_{\rm{shell}}^{4}(\delta q)^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\omega^{2}.

(71)

Thus, close to the shell, the bosonic factor takes a Lorentzian form in

\delta q
,

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\alpha}q|\omega|}{q^{2}\big[(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}-|\bar{\chi}|q^{2}\big]^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{\,2}\omega^{2}}\simeq\frac{\bar{\alpha}q_{\rm{shell}}|\omega|}{4|\bar{\chi}|^{2}q_{\rm{shell}}^{4}(\delta q)^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\omega^{2}}.

(72)

The apparent singularity at
q=q_{\rm{shell}}
is therefore regulated by the damping
term and does not produce a divergence.

Balancing the two terms in Eq. (
71
) gives the shell width

\displaystyle\delta q_{\rm shell}\sim\frac{\alpha}{2|\bar{\chi}|q_{\rm{shell}}^{2}}\,|\omega|=\frac{\alpha}{2(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{2}}\,|\omega|.

(73)

Since the remaining kinematic factors are smooth at finite momentum

q\simeq q_{\rm shell}
, the singular part of the
q
integral reduces to

\displaystyle I_{\rm shell}(\omega)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{q_{\rm min}-q_{\rm shell}}^{q_{\rm max}-q_{\rm shell}}d(\delta q)\,\frac{\bar{\alpha}q_{\rm shell}|\omega|}{4|\bar{\chi}|^{2}q_{\rm shell}^{4}(\delta q)^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\omega^{2}},

(74)

where

q_{\rm min}=\frac{\varepsilon+\omega}{v_{\rm F}},\qquad q_{\rm max}=2k_{\rm F}+\frac{\varepsilon+\omega}{v_{\rm F}}.

(75)

We now rescale

\delta q=|\omega|x,\qquad d(\delta q)=|\omega|dx.

(76)

Then Eq. (
74
) becomes

\displaystyle I_{\rm shell}(\omega)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{x_{\rm min}}^{x_{\rm max}}|\omega|\,dx\,\frac{\bar{\alpha}q_{\rm shell}|\omega|}{4|\bar{\chi}|^{2}q_{\rm shell}^{4}\omega^{2}x^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{x_{\rm min}}^{x_{\rm max}}dx\,\frac{\bar{\alpha}q_{\rm shell}}{4|\bar{\chi}|^{2}q_{\rm shell}^{4}x^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}},

(77)

with

x_{\rm min}=\frac{q_{\rm min}-q_{\rm shell}}{|\omega|},\qquad x_{\rm max}=\frac{q_{\rm max}-q_{\rm shell}}{|\omega|}.

(78)

If the shell lies well inside the allowed integration region,
i.e.
,
q_{\rm min}\ll q_{\rm shell}\ll q_{\rm max}
,
then in the low-energy limit
|\omega|\to 0
one has

x_{\rm min}\to-\infty,\qquad x_{\rm max}\to+\infty.

(79)

Therefore the shell contribution reduces to the full-line Lorentzian integral

\displaystyle I_{\rm shell}(\omega)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\,\frac{\bar{\alpha}q_{\rm shell}}{4|\bar{\chi}|^{2}q_{\rm shell}^{4}x^{2}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}},

(80)

which is independent of
\omega
up to a finite dimensionless constant.
Hence

I_{\rm shell}(\omega)\sim\omega^{0}.

(81)

Since the
\omega
integral extends over a window of width
\varepsilon
, the
soft-shell contribution to the self-energy scales as

\displaystyle\Im m\Sigma_{+}^{(R)}(k_{\rm F},\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon.

(82)

Hence the
\bar{\chi}<0
regime yields a marginal-Fermi-liquid like contribution
dominated by the soft shell.
At higher energies the large-
q
part of the overdamped kernel can again
take over, leading to the same crossover scale as in the
\bar{\chi}>0

case. Equating the soft-shell momentum
q_{\rm{shell}}
with the non-Fermi-liquid
momentum scale gives
\frac{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})}{\sqrt{|\chi|}}\sim\left((\alpha\varepsilon)/{|\bar{\chi}|}\right)^{1/3},

and therefore
\varepsilon_{\ast}\sim{(c_{\rm ph}/v_{\rm F})^{3}}/({\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{|\bar{\chi}|}}).

Thus the
\bar{\chi}<0
regime exhibits a marginal-Fermi-liquid like infrared
behaviour, followed by a crossover into the same non-Fermi-liquid regime
encountered for
\bar{\chi}>0
.
