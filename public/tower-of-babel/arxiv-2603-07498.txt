Properties of best approximations with respect to Ky Fan p p - k k norm, and strict spectral approximants of a matrix
Priyanka Grover 1 , Krishna Kumar Gupta 2

Abstract
. Some questions raised in [K. Ziętak, From the strict Chebyshev approximant of a vector to the strict spectral approximant of a matrix , Warsaw : Banach Center Publ., 112 Polish Acad. Sci. Inst. Math. (2017)] are discussed. To do so, the subdifferential set of Ky Fan p p - k k norm is computed. A characterization for the best approximations with respect to the Ky Fan p p - k k norms is given. Further, necessary and sufficient conditions for ε \varepsilon -orthogonality with respect to the Ky Fan p p - k k norm are also derived.

We first observe that the Ky Fan

\displaystyle\|A\|_{(p,k)}

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n\times k}({\mathbb{C}})\\

We now recall a result on the Fréchet differentiability of matrix-valued functions defined over a subset of Hermitian matrices.
Before stating the result, we introduce the following notations.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be the open interval. Let
C^{1}(\mathcal{I})
denote the space of continuously differentiable real-valued functions on
I.
For a function
f\in C^{1}(\mathcal{I}),
define the map
f^{(1)}:\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f^{(1)}(\lambda,\alpha)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{f(\lambda)-f(\alpha)}{\lambda-\alpha},&\lambda\neq\alpha,\\
f^{\prime}(\lambda),&\lambda=\alpha.\end{cases}

Let
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
denote the set of all
n\times n
Hermitian matrices. Let
{\mathcal{G}}(I)
be the set of Hermitian matrices whose eigenvalues lie in
I.
Let
B\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with spectral decomposition
B=UDU^{*},
where
D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}).
Define the induced map
f:{\mathcal{G}}(I)\to{\mathbb{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
by
f(B)=Uf(D)U^{*},
where
f(D)=\operatorname{diag}(f(\lambda_{1}),\dots,f(\lambda_{n})).
Let
\mathcal{D}f(B)
denote the Fréchet derivative of the function
f
at
A
.

Proposition 2.1
.

4
;
5

Let
B\in{\mathcal{G}}(I).
Then, for any
H\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),

\mathcal{D}f(B)(H)=U\left[f^{(1)}(D)\circ(UHU^{*})\right]U^{*},

where
f^{(1)}(D)
is the matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is
f^{(1)}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}),
and
\circ
denotes the Schur-product of two matrices.

The proof of the above proposition is presented in
(
4

for the interval

With minor modifications, the same proof can be extended to any open interval

Alternatively, another proof can be found in
(
5

For
p>2,
let
\hat{f}:(-1,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be defined by
\hat{f}(x)=|x|^{\frac{p}{2}}.
Then
\hat{f}\in C^{1}((-1,\infty))
and

Using the above proposition, we derive the derivative of the function
X\mapsto{\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}(VV^{*}(X^{*}X)^{\frac{p}{2}}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
2<p<\infty
. Let
V
be a
n\times k
matrix whose columns are
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
satisfying
A^{*}Av_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{2}(A)v_{i},
for
1\leq i\leq k.
Let
f_{V}:{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a function defined as

f_{V}(X)={\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}(VV^{*}(X^{*}X)^{\frac{p}{2}})={\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}(X^{*}X)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)\text{ for }X\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}}).

Then, the derivative of
f_{V}
at
A
is given by

(10)

{\mathcal{D}}f_{V}(A)(X)=p\,{\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}\left(\big(AVV^{*}(A^{*}A)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big)^{*}X\right)\text{ for }X\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}}).

Proof.

For each
V\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n\times k}({\mathbb{C}})
, let
h_{V}:{\mathbb{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the differentiable function defined as
h_{V}(H)={\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}(VV^{*}H)
for
H\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}).
Let
g_{1}:{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathcal{G}}
be the differentiable function defined as
g_{1}(Y)=Y^{*}Y
for
Y\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}}).

Thus, for
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}}),

f_{V}(A)=(h_{V}\circ\hat{f}\circ g_{1})(A)
. By the chain rule, we get

(11)

{\mathcal{D}}f_{V}(A)(X)={\mathcal{D}}h_{V}\big(\hat{f}\circ g_{1}(A)\big)\Big({\mathcal{D}}\hat{f}(g_{1}(A))\big({\mathcal{D}}g_{1}(A)(X)\big)\Big).

Since
A^{*}A\in{\mathcal{G}}((-1,\infty)),
by Proposition (
2.1
) we get

{\mathcal{D}}\hat{f}(g_{1}(A))\big({\mathcal{D}}g_{1}(A)(X)\big)={\mathcal{D}}\hat{f}(A^{*}A)(A^{*}X+X^{*}A)=V[\hat{f}^{(1)}(\Sigma^{*}\Sigma)o(V^{*}(A^{*}X+X^{*}A)V)]V^{*},

where
\Sigma^{*}\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}[\sigma_{1}^{2}(A),\ldots,\sigma_{n}^{2}(A)].
Observe that the
(i,j)
th entries of
(XV)^{*}AV
and
(AV)^{*}XV
are
\langle Xv_{i},Av_{j}\rangle
and
\langle Av_{i},Xv_{j}\rangle,
respectively. Additionally,
V^{*}\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}V=\begin{bmatrix}I_{k\times k}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.
Thus,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}f_{V}(A)(X)=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}\left({\mathcal{D}}\hat{f}(A^{*}A)(A^{*}X+X^{*}A)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{p-2}(A){\operatorname{Re}}\langle Av_{i},Xv_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=p\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\operatorname{Re}}\langle X^{*}Av_{i},(A^{*}A)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=p\,{\operatorname{Re}}\,{\operatorname{tr}}\left(\big(AVV^{*}(A^{*}A)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big)^{*}X\right).

∎

Proposition 2.3

(
28
)
.

Let J be a compact set in some metric space. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a collection of continuous convex functions from
{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}})
to
{\mathbb{R}}
such that for

the maps

are upper semi-continuous. Let

Let
J(A)=\{i\in J:g(A)=f_{i}(A)\}.
Then

\partial g(A)=\operatorname{conv}(\cup\{\partial f_{i}(A):i\in J(A)\}).

The proof of this proposition is provided in Theorem 4.4.2 of

A similar argument can be extended to real-valued continuous convex functions on a normed space (see

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

\begin{split}\partial\|A\|_{(p,k)}=\operatorname{conv}&\Bigg\{\frac{1}{\|A\|_{(p,k)}^{p-1}}A(A^{*}A)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}:v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\text{ o.n. vectors with }\\

Let

Case 2

The right hand derivative is directly related to the concept of approximate Birkhoff orthogonality, see

The concept of
Birkhoff-James orthogonality

7
;
16
plays a fundamental role in the study of the geometry of Banach spaces.
For
A,B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}})
,
A
is said to be Birkhoff-James orthogonal to
B
if
\|A+\lambda B\|\geq\|A\|

\text{ for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}.
A matrix
A
is said to be Birkhoff-James orthogonal to a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{C}})
if
\|A+B\|\geq\|A\|

\text{ for all }B\in{\mathcal{M}}.

Subsequently, certain approximation concepts extending notion of this orthogonality were introduced in
9
;
10
. In
9
