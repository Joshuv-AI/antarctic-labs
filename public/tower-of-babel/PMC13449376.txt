Superselective intra-arterial chemoradiotherapy for maxillary sinus squamous-cell carcinoma: results from the T4bN0M0 cohort of JCOG1212☆
Authors: H Tachibana, K Matsuura, R Onimaru, D Yoshida, T Kobayashi, T Ueda, M Nakahira, A Ohkoshi, G Omura, T Mukaigawa, K Katagiri, T Asakage, H Shinomiya, F Ito, Y Asada, T Beppu, T Fujii, Y Hirayama, K Tsukahara, Y Saito, K Ichikawa, H Uemura, M Tahara, N Hanai, N Kiyota, S Iwamoto, K Sasaki, J Mizusawa, H Fukuda, A Homma, JCOG Head and Neck Cancer Study Group of the Japan Clinical Oncology Group (JCOG-HNCSG)
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundJCOG1212 is a multi-institutional prospective single-arm trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of superselective intra-arterial cisplatin infusion with concomitant radiotherapy (RADPLAT) for locally advanced maxillary sinus squamous-cell carcinoma (MS-SCC). We previously reported the results for the T4aN0M0 cohort (n = 64), in which RADPLAT was shown to be feasible and to provide favorable survival outcomes. This report presents the results for the T4bN0M0 cohort.Patients and methodsPatients with previously untreated T4bN0M0 MS-SCC received weekly intra-arterial cisplatin (100 mg/m2) for seven cycles in combination with radiotherapy (70 Gy in 35 fractions). The primary endpoint was 3-year overall survival (OS), with a predefined efficacy threshold of 35% and a one-sided alpha of 5%. Secondary endpoints included event-free survival (EFS), local EFS (LEFS), clinical response, and toxicity.ResultsSixty-four patients were enrolled from 20 institutions. The 3-year OS was 68.8% [90% confidence interval (CI) 58.1% to 77.2%], exceeding the efficacy threshold. The clinical complete response rate was 54.7%. The 3-year EFS and LEFS was 51.6% (95% CI 38.8% to 63.0%) and 57.8% (95% CI 44.8% to 68.8%), respectively. With regard to acute adverse events, neutropenia ≥ grade 3 (18.0%), mucositis ≥ grade 3 (23.0%), hearing impairment ≥ grade 2 (3.3%), and stroke ≥ grade 2 (1.6%) were observed. No grade 2 ≥ creatinine increase was observed. There were no treatment-related deaths.ConclusionsRADPLAT achieved favorable survival outcomes with acceptable toxicity in patients with T4bN0M0 MS-SCC. These results establish RADPLAT as a feasible and effective organ-preserving treatment and demonstrate the potential of intra-arterial chemoradiotherapy for unresectable MS-SCC.Key words: chemotherapy, cisplatin, intra-arterial, maxillary sinus cancer, radiotherapy

Introduction

Maxillary sinus squamous-cell carcinoma (MS-SCC) is a rare malignancy, accounting for ∼0.1% of all cancers and 3% of head and neck cancers in Japan,1, 2, 3 yet it is the most common malignancy among sinonasal cancers. As the disease may remain asymptomatic until it becomes advanced, some patients are diagnosed at a stage where surgery is no longer feasible.

T4b maxillary sinus cancer is defined as a very advanced tumor characterized by invasion of vital structures such as the orbital apex, dura, brain, middle cranial fossa, cranial nerves other than the maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve, the nasopharynx, or the clivus. T4b comprises ∼20% of maxillary sinus cancers.1,4 Patients with T4b tumors are generally not candidates for surgical resection, and therefore, intravenous chemoradiotherapy (IV-CRT) or induction chemotherapy followed by radiotherapy (RT) is applied, but the outcomes remain poor.5, 6, 7 Even in cases where surgery is technically feasible, it often requires extensive resection—which may include one or more of the following structures: orbital exenteration, palate, overlying skin, dura, or brain—resulting in profound functional and cosmetic consequences that substantially affect patients’ quality of life. Thus, more effective therapies are urgently needed for T4b MS-SCC.

Intra-arterial (IA) chemotherapy has been studied in relation to head and neck cancers since the 1950s, based on the rationale that this region is well suited for regional drug delivery because its blood supply arises predominantly from branches of the external carotid artery, which are readily accessible.8,9 In Japan, IA chemotherapy has been used mainly for maxillary sinus cancer since the 1960s.10,11 With the development of interventional radiology in the 1980s, superselective catheterization of the external carotid branches became feasible. In the 1990s, Robbins et al.12 developed a protocol combining IA cisplatin with sodium thiosulfate to reduce systemic toxicity. RT was subsequently incorporated, leading to the regimen known as RADPLAT.13 This approach was applied mainly to locally advanced head and neck cancers in western countries. Although the initial results reported by Robbins were encouraging,13,14 a randomized trial in the Netherlands comparing RADPLAT with IV-CRT showed no difference in locoregional control or overall survival (OS) after a median follow-up of 33 months.15 Consequently, RADPLAT did not attract further attention worldwide.

Although RADPLAT did not demonstrate superiority to IV-CRT in a randomized trial conducted in the Netherlands, that study did not include sinonasal cancers, and therefore its results cannot be directly extrapolated to MS-SCC. In contrast, several observational studies, mainly from Japan, have consistently reported favorable results for RADPLAT specifically in locally advanced MS-SCC, and this regimen has been widely adopted across multiple institutions.16, 17, 18, 19 These findings suggested that RADPLAT is a promising treatment option for this disease, warranting evaluation in a prospective multi-institutional setting. Therefore, we conducted a dose-finding and efficacy confirmation trial of RADPLAT for locally advanced MS-SCC (JCOG1212) (Figure 1). We have already reported the results of the dose-finding phase and the efficacy confirmation phase, demonstrating that weekly cisplatin at 100 mg/m2 for seven cycles combined with concurrent RT was feasible.20,21 In T4aN0M0 cohort (n = 64), the 3-year and 5-year OS were 82.8% and 71.9%, respectively, confirming the utility of RADPLAT.22,23 In this study, we report the results from the T4bN0M0 cohort.

Patients and methods

Eligibility

Eligibility criteria in the T4b cohort were as follows: primary lesion located at the maxillary sinus, histologically proven SCC, clinical stage T4bN0M0, no severe carotid stenosis as evaluated by ultrasonography, aged between 20 and 75 years, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0 or 1, no prior therapy for maxillary sinus cancer, no prior RT to the head and neck or brain, no prior chemotherapy for any other malignancies, sufficient organ function as shown by a peripheral blood white blood cell count of between 4000 and 12 000 cells/μl, hemoglobin concentration of at least 9.5 g/dl, platelet count of at least 100 000 cells/μl, serum total bilirubin concentration of ≤2.0 mg/dl, aspartate aminotransferase of ≤100 IU/l, alanine aminotransferase of ≤100 IU/l, serum creatinine concentration of ≤1.2 mg/dl, normal electrocardiogram, the ability to reach the external carotid arteries from the femoral artery with a catheter, and satisfying the normal tissue radiation dose constraints for the ipsilateral eyeball and optic nerve, spinal cord, brain stem, and chiasma. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before registration. The eligible criteria were changed as follows: from May 2017, white blood cell count of at least 4000 cells/μl and no upper limit; and from May 2019, aged ≥20 years, hemoglobin concentration of at least 9.0 g/dl, and serum creatinine concentration of ≤1.3 mg/dl.

For staging evaluation, contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head and neck, contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) of the head and neck, and contrast-enhanced CT from the chest to the pelvis were mandatory. 2-Fluoro-2-deoxy-d-glucose–positron emission tomography (FDG–PET) was permitted as an alternative to whole-body CT.

Patients were excluded if they had any of the following conditions: simultaneous or metachronous (within 5 years) double cancers other than in situ carcinoma or intramucosal tumors; active infection requiring systemic therapy; body temperature ≥38°C; women during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or within 28 days after delivery; severe psychosis; need for systemic steroid medication or immunosuppressant medication; poorly controlled diabetes mellitus; poorly controlled hypertension; an angina pectoris attack within 3 weeks or myocardial infarction within 6 months; or positive test for serum hepatitis B antigen.

Study design

JCOG1212 is a multi-institutional, single-arm, prospective interventional study of superselective IA infusion of high-dose RADPLAT for patients with T4aN0M0 and T4bN0M0 locally advanced MS-SCCs conducted across 25 institutions in Japan. The trial consisted of a dose-finding phase and an efficacy confirmation phase. It was registered with the UMIN Clinical Trials Registry (Number: UMIN000013706) and the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (Number: jRCTs031180004) and approved by the institutional review board of all participating sites.20

The dose-finding phase was designed to determine the recommended number of cycles through the study of patients with either T4aN0M0 or T4bN0M0 tumors. In this phase, 100 mg/m2 of cisplatin was administered IA weekly for 7 weeks with concomitant RT (70 Gy/35 fractions).21 As a result, all 18 patients received a full dose of RT. The number of cycles of cisplatin was 7 in 13 patients and 6 in 5 patients. Dose-limiting toxicities were observed in five patients. These results indicated that this therapy is safe and well tolerated at seven cycles of cisplatin, which was determined to be the recommended number of cycles for locally advanced MS-SCC.

In the efficacy confirmation phase, the objective is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of RADPLAT for patients with locally advanced MS-SCC. The efficacy confirmation phase is being conducted separately for patients with T4aN0M0 and T4bN0M0 MS-SCC. The results of the T4a cohort have already been reported previously.22,23

Per-protocol disease assessment (physical examination, MRI of head and neck, and thoracoabdominal CT) and adverse event data were required every 3 months for the first year, every 4 months for years 2 and 3, and then every 6 months for years 4 and 5. All patients who were enrolled in the study were to be followed for at least 5 years, while analysis of the primary endpoint of the efficacy confirmation phase was planned to be conducted 3 years after accrual completion.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint of the efficacy confirmation phase was the 3-year OS, and the secondary endpoints were OS, event-free survival (EFS), local EFS (LEFS), clinical complete response (CR) rate, incidence of adverse events, and serious adverse events. OS was computed from the date of registration to the time of death from any cause. EFS was computed from the date of registration to the time of death from any cause, any progression (including recurrence), and salvage surgery. LEFS was computed from the date of registration to the time of death from any cause, primary disease progression (including recurrence), and salvage surgery for the primary lesion. The clinical CR rate was determined from the proportion of CRs and good partial responses (PRs) among all eligible patients. Good PR was characterized as a secondary change unique to after conformal radiotherapy that was regarded as a remaining scar but not as a residual tumor. Good PR in this study was defined as lesions ≤10 mm in size or those not enhanced on contrast-enhanced MRI. This criterion was used in JCOG0706 by the same head and neck cancer group and was also applied in this study.24 Toxicities were evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 4.0.

Treatments

The protocol treatment consisted of weekly superselective IA infusion of RADPLAT and salvage surgery where necessary.

Chemotherapy

Cisplatin (100 mg/m2) was administered IA weekly for 7 weeks. At the same time, sodium thiosulfate was administered at a dose of 20 g/m2 i.v. to neutralize the cisplatin. Digital subtraction angiography and/or an interventional radiology CT system, which combines angiography and CT imaging while the patient remains on the same table, was carried out to accurately and carefully identify the arteries feeding the tumor and their perfusion. Tumors of the maxillary sinuses are usually fed by the internal maxillary artery, but for cases in which arteries such as the facial artery or transverse facial artery fed the tumor, part of the dose was administered through these alternative arteries. The dose of cisplatin infused from each artery was determined as described in previous reports.18,25 Seven cycles, which was determined as the recommended number of cycles in the dose-finding phase, was applied in the efficacy confirmation phase. To control the quality of the interventional technique, central review of photographs and movies in arbitrarily selected patients was carried out at a semiannual investigators’ meeting. All interventional procedures were carried out or directly supervised by interventional radiologists certified by the study chair.

Radiotherapy

Three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy (3D-CRT) or intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) was chosen at institutional discretion (this study began in April 2014, and IMRT became available after March 2016). For quality control and assurance of RT, compliance with protocol-specified RT planning was examined for all enrolled patients at completion of RT.

RT was administered with high-energy photons of 4-10 MV X-rays to a total dose of 70 Gy in 2 Gy fractions five times weekly. A fixation method, such as a thermoplastic mask, was mandatory.

The gross tumor volume (GTV) was determined based on multiple imaging findings, including not only lesions showing enhancement on CT but also areas of abnormal signal on MRI and sites of abnormal uptake on FDG–PET.

The clinical target volume (CTV) was defined as the GTV plus a 5-mm margin, including the affected maxillary sinus. Furthermore, depending on the tumor’s location, degree of invasion, and the clarity of its borders on imaging, it was permitted to extend the CTV margin into adjacent regions such as the orbit, nasal cavity, ethmoid sinus, sphenoid sinus, infratemporal fossa, pterygopalatine fossa, and buccal soft tissues. In cases without bone destruction, an extraosseous margin of ≤5 mm was also permitted. Intermediate- and low-risk volumes around the GTV were not created because of the short distance between the GTV and organs at risk, such as the optic nerve, retina, and chiasm, in contrast to the approach reported by Grégoire et al.26 As reports suggest that the incidence of regional lymph node metastasis in maxillary sinus cancer is low,1,2 we adopted a policy of not administering prophylactic irradiation to the regional lymph nodes. Therefore, in this clinical trial, no moderate-risk or low-risk prophylactic regional lymph node volumes were designated. Depending on the extent of disease progression, incidental irradiation of the VII lymph node26 was not prohibited.

The planning target volume (PTV) was defined as the CTV plus a margin of ∼5 mm. If the risk of serious complications—such as bilateral blindness, myelitis, or extensive cerebral necrosis—was deemed to exist, it was permitted to reduce the margin toward the unaffected eye, optic nerve, optic chiasm, spinal cord, and brain to as little as 1 mm. However, the protocol specified that any reduction in the PTV margin solely for the reason of lowering the dose to the affected eye was unacceptable, based on the idea that the risk of visual impairment in this patient group was considered acceptable. Furthermore, the PTV was required to include the GTV.

Statistical analysis

This trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of RADPLAT for patients with T4bN0M0 in the efficacy confirmation phase.

For T4bN0M0, the primary endpoint of the 3-year OS was used to evaluate whether RADPLAT therapy is superior to IV-CRT, considered the de facto standard treatment of patients with T4bN0M0.

The historical control was based on data collected from 28 high-volume centers in Japan.27 Among the cases of previously untreated T4 MS-SCC that were treated at each center from 2006 to 2008, the 3-year OS of seven patients who received IV-CRT with T4bN0M0 was 14.3%. Based on this information, the historical control for the 3-year OS was set at 20% IV-CRT. Therefore, we test the null hypothesis that the RADPLAT would result in a true 3-year OS of ≤20% against the alternative hypothesis of a true 3-year OS of 35% or greater. A targeted sample size of 59 was required to achieve 80% power with a one-sided 5% significance level. Patients who received less than or equal to the recommended number of doses in the dose-finding phase were considered to have a low risk of bias for overestimation of efficacy and were therefore included in the efficacy confirmation phase. In view of some ineligibility and loss to follow-up, the planned number of T4bN0M0 patients enrolled was 62.

For all eligible patients, the two-sided 90% confidence interval (CI) for the 3-year OS estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method was calculated by the Greenwood formula to determine whether the null hypothesis could be rejected. When the lower bound of the 90% CI exceeded 35%, the threshold 3-year OS, the null hypothesis was rejected and judged to show superiority to IV-CRT.

OS, EFS, and LEFS were calculated by the Kaplan–Meier method. Hazard ratios were computed using a univariable Cox proportional hazards model to compare the efficacy between subgroups, and two-sided P values were reported. The CI for the CR rate was calculated by exact methods. All analyses were carried out using SAS version 9.4.

Results

Patient characteristics

From April 2014 to November 2021, 64 patients were enrolled in the T4b cohort from 20 institutions, consisting of 51 males and 13 females with a median age of 64.5 years (range 37-79) and an ECOG PS of 0 or 1 (44/20) (Table 1). The sites of tumor extension in all enrolled patients are presented in Supplementary Table S1 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336). All 64 patients were included in the primary analysis of efficacy and safety (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336). Eleven patients received 3D-CRT, and 50 received IMRT. The RT LINACs and treatment planning systems used in this study are presented in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336). The remaining three patients did not receive the protocol treatment: two refused the protocol therapy and one was treated with IMRT at an institution where its use was not permitted.

Efficacy

The clinical CR rate among all patients was 54.7% (35/64, 95% CI 41.8% to 67.2%), with 9 showing a CR and 26 showing a good PR. A representative case that achieved CR is presented in Supplementary Figure S2 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336).

The median follow-up was 4.6 years in all patients, and the primary endpoint for 3-year OS was 68.8% (90% CI 58.1% to 77.2%), which met the prespecified hypothesis (Figure 2A). The 3-year EFS and LEFS were 51.6% (95% CI 38.8% to 63.0%) and 57.8% (95% CI 44.8% to 66.8%), respectively (Figure 2B and C). In the univariable analysis, patients with the pterygoid plate extension had significantly worse OS and EFS than did those without extension (Supplementary Tables S4 and S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336). In the analysis of LEFS, age ≥65 years and tumor extension to the medial wall of the orbit were associated with poorer prognosis (Supplementary Table S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108336). Regarding the cause of death, 20 patients died of the disease, with no treatment-related deaths. Seven patients died of other causes: three from pneumonia, one from intestinal obstruction, and three from unknown causes, all of whom were free of disease at the time of death.

Safety

Among the 61 patients who received RADPLAT, 60 completed the planned RT to a total dose of 70 Gy, and 46 patients completed all seven cycles of cisplatin. Median total dose and duration of RT was 70 Gy (IQR, 70-70 Gy) and 51 days (IQR, 51-52 days), respectively, with the median number of cycles of cisplatin being 7 (IQR, 7-7 cycles). The results of quality assurance for RT showed no unacceptable deviations.

Acute adverse events included mucositis ≥ grade 3 in 14 patients (23.0%), neutropenia ≥ grade 3 in 11 (18.0%), hearing impairment ≥ grade 2 in 2 (3.3%), and stroke ≥ grade 2 in 1 (1.6%). No patients experienced creatinine increase ≥ grade 2 (Table 2). There were no treatment-related deaths in the T4b cohort. In terms of late adverse events (≥grade 3), osteonecrosis of the mandible occurred in two patients (3.4%), and fatigue and trismus occurred in one patient each (1.7%). Affected-side eye disorders were also observed, such as cataract in eight (13.6%), retinopathy in eight (13.6%), glaucoma in three patients (5.1%), and corneal ulceration in two (3.4%).

Twenty-nine patients had residual or recurrent diseases as follows: the primary site was involved in 22, regional lymph nodes involved in 16, and distant metastasis in 7 (some overlapping) (Figure 3A). In terms of the primary site, 2 of the 12 patients with residual disease received salvage surgery, with 1 of them alive and 1 dead to date (Figure 3B). Discussion

This prospective multi-institutional single-arm trial demonstrated that RADPLAT achieved favorable survival outcomes in patients with T4bN0M0 MS-SCC, a population historically associated with poor prognosis. The 3-year OS exceeded the prespecified threshold of 35% and compared favorably with previous reports.4,6,7

Hoppe et al.6 reported outcomes for 39 patients with unresectable paranasal sinus carcinoma (stage IVB) who were treated with definitive RT with or without chemotherapy between 1990 and 2006. Of these patients, 15 had SCC by histology, while 19 had primary tumors originating from the maxillary sinus. With a median follow-up of 90 months, the 5-year OS was only 15%. In our study, the historical control for IV-CRT was based on a small cohort of seven patients, in whom the 3-year OS was 14.3%; therefore, the threshold for the primary endpoint was set at 20%.27 This threshold was considered reasonable in light of the limited evidence available at the time of trial design, including the report by Hoppe et al., and the historical control was thus deemed appropriate for the purposes of this study. van de Velde et al.4 reported a population-based cohort of 568 cases of sinonasal SCC diagnosed in the Netherlands between 2008 and 2021, in which the 5-year OS was 43.0%. Despite advances in treatment over time, the authors observed no statistically significant improvement in survival during the study period. They also found that maxillary sinus primaries tended to have a worse prognosis than other subsites. Li et al.,7 using the SEER database, examined 220 patients with T4bN0M0 sinonasal SCC diagnosed between 2004 and 2015. Patients who underwent surgery plus post-operative RT had a 5-year OS of 42.5%, compared with 21.7% for those treated with RT alone.7 Taken together, no previous studies have shown a 5-year survival exceeding 50% for T4b disease. The results of the present trial therefore appear highly favorable and support RADPLAT as a promising treatment option for unresectable maxillary sinus cancer.

The management of the clinically N0 neck in patients with MS-SCC remains controversial. Although elective neck irradiation (ENI) has been shown to reduce lymph node recurrence, previous reports have not shown a survival benefit.28, 29, 30, 31 After discussion within the Japan Clinical Oncology Group (JCOG) Head and Neck Cancer Study Group, ENI was not carried out because of concerns regarding mucositis during treatment and worsening dry mouth after treatment.

Regional lymph node recurrence was observed in 16 of 64 patients in this trial. All four patients with isolated neck recurrence were successfully salvaged by surgery, whereas patients with nodal recurrence accompanied by primary and/or distant failure were rarely salvaged. Tumors that recur in the lymph nodes, often in combination with local and/or distant failure, may be biologically more aggressive, and it remains unclear whether ENI would have altered these outcomes.

This study was initiated using 3D-CRT because IMRT was not yet widely adopted in Japan at that time; however, as IMRT became standard practice, the protocol was amended to allow its use. Given the frequency of nodal recurrence observed in this study and the potential of IMRT to reduce salivary gland toxicity compared with 3D-CRT, the role of ENI warrants further evaluation in future trials.

Several strengths of this study should be highlighted. Firstly, this trial, although limited to T4b disease, includes a relatively large number of cases for this rare entity of T4b MS-SCC, ensuring the robustness and generalizability of the findings. Secondly, the survival outcomes observed in this study are remarkable compared with historical controls, underscoring the efficacy of RADPLAT in this otherwise difficult-to-treat population. Thirdly, the treatment duration was limited to only 7 weeks, identical to the course of definitive RT, which provides a practical advantage and avoids prolongation of therapy. In contrast, recent reports have described strategies incorporating induction chemotherapy followed by surgery or RT, which inevitably extend the overall treatment period.32, 33, 34, 35, 36 Fourthly, surgery was not incorporated into the initial treatment strategy, sparing patients from procedures associated with significant functional and cosmetic morbidity.

Another important feature of this trial is the use of interventional radiology (IR) techniques for superselective IA infusion. Although IA chemotherapy has sometimes been regarded as technically demanding and operator dependent within the oncology community, the present study shows that the procedure can be delivered in a standardized and reliable manner across multiple centers. This was supported by central reviews of procedure photographs and videos at investigators’ meetings and by requiring that all cases be carried out or directly supervised by study chair-certified interventional radiologists, which helped maintain consistent technical quality and contributed to continued improvement among participating institutions. The use of digital subtraction angiography and interventional CT enabled precise catheterization and drug delivery, maximizing tumor exposure while minimizing systemic toxicity. Rather than being considered a limitation, the involvement of IR should be recognized as a strength, highlighting how advances in imaging systems, catheter technology, and IA infusion techniques can expand therapeutic opportunities for solid tumors.37

Regarding adverse events, cerebrovascular ischemia caused by catheter manipulation represents a serious concern in RADPLAT since the catheter is inserted into the feeding artery of the tumor via the common carotid artery for IA infusion. In the most frequently cited single-center study with the largest sample size, neurologic sequelae of ≥grade 3 were reported in 9 of 213 patients (4.2%).38 In multicenter studies, they were observed in 5 of 61 patients (8.2%, ≥grade 3)39 and in 8 of 118 patients (6.8%, ≥grade 2),15 respectively. In JCOG1212, the acute incidence of stroke (≥grade 2) was 1.6% (T4a: 1/64, T4b: 1/61). Notably, the aforementioned reports were all published between 2000 and 2010, and it is presumed that the incidence has subsequently decreased owing to continued advances in imaging guidance, catheter design, and IA delivery techniques.

As for adverse events other than stroke, they were manageable, with no treatment-related deaths observed. Acute adverse events such as neutropenia and mucositis occurred at expected frequencies, while late toxicities were limited and acceptable given the aggressive nature of the disease. Salvage surgery was feasible in selected patients with residual/recurrent disease, further contributing to long-term disease control.

This study has some limitations. A key limitation is that the threshold for the 3-year OS in the historical control was derived from a limited dataset comprising only seven patients. And the study was conducted in Japan, where IA chemotherapy has a long history, and its reproducibility in countries without established IR expertise may require careful consideration. Additionally, this study employed a single-arm design, since a randomized controlled trial would have been ideal but was not practically feasible, and evaluation was made through comparison with historical treatment outcomes. Nevertheless, the magnitude of the benefits demonstrated in this multi-institutional prospective trial provides compelling evidence for the clinical value of RADPLAT in this setting.

Another limitation of this study is the exclusion of patients with lymph node metastases. Patients with maxillary sinus cancer with lymph node metastasis have a poorer prognosis than those without lymph node metastasis.27,40 According to the Japanese Head and Neck Cancer Registry, lymph node metastases were observed in 27.3% to 37.1% of T4b maxillary sinus cancer cases, including ∼20% non-SCC cases.1,2 However, this trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy of RADPLAT, which was expected to be highly effective for control of the primary tumor. As the inclusion of patients with lymph node metastasis would have complicated the analysis, these patients were excluded from the present study.

The inclusion of cases treated with 3D-CRT is also a limitation of this study. IMRT was covered by health insurance in Japan in 2008; therefore, given the limited adoption of IMRT in routine clinical practice at the time the trial was initiated, treatment was administered using 3D-CRT. Since this trial’s RT regimen did not include prophylactic irradiation of the regional lymph nodes, the dose to the contralateral salivary glands was deemed to be within acceptable limits even with 3D-CRT. However, reducing the dose to the affected optic nerve and eyeball to achieve curative doses was considered difficult in some cases, so no dose constraints were set for the affected optic nerve or eyeball. As a result, as described in the paper, cataracts and retinopathy occurred in 13.6% of cases. Given the current widespread adoption of IMRT, which has the ability to reduce complications in organs at risk such as the salivary glands, the affected optic nerve and eyeball, and the brain, we believe that IMRT should be used for the treatment of maxillary sinus cancer.

Conclusion

This trial was designed as a confirmatory phase III trial and demonstrates that RADPLAT provides meaningful survival benefits for patients with T4bN0M0 MS-SCC. The results highlight not only the therapeutic potential of superselective IA chemotherapy but also the value of integrating IR into the multidisciplinary management of head and neck cancer. Future efforts should aim to further refine catheter-based techniques and explore biomarkers predictive of response. Although our findings provide important evidence supporting the potential role of RADPLAT as one of the standard treatment options, additional prospective studies with larger cohorts are warranted to further validate these results and establish its clinical position more definitively.
