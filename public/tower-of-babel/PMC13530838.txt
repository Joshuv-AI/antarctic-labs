Poster 190. Clinical Outcomes Following Ulnar Collateral Ligament Reconstruction with Palmaris Longus versus Gracilis Autograft in Baseball Players: A Cohort Analysis
Authors: Joshua Scheiderer, Nathaniel Tchangou, Harrison Fellheimer, Daniel Kwak, Brooks Martino, Frank Cautela, Christopher C. Dodson, Michael C. Ciccotti, Steven B. Cohen, Michael G. Ciccotti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objectives:Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) reconstruction is a well-established procedure for overhead athletes, particularly baseball pitchers who sustain valgus injuries of the elbow1,2,3,4. Incidence of UCL injuries has continued to rise in recent years, with reconstruction performed as a routine intervention for those seeking to return to competitive play.1,5,6 A variety of technical modifications have been made to UCL surgery including exposure, ulnar nerve management, tunnel size/location/configuration, fixation, and graft source. The graft source remains a critical consideration, with the palmaris longus (PL) and gracilis (GR) autografts representing the two most commonly utilized autograft options.1,7 Biomechanical studies have demonstrated that both provide similar tensile strength.8,9 However, despite widespread use, limited literature directly compares their clinical outcomes in baseball players. Previous retrospective studies suggest similar return-to-play rates between graft types, but variations in recovery timelines, subsequent injury profile and long-term performance metrics remain incompletely understood.7,10,11 This study aims to compare patient reported outcomes following UCL reconstruction (UCLR) with palmaris longus versus gracilis autografts in baseball players.Methods:Patients who underwent an isolated UCLR with either palmaris longus or gracilis autograft and minimum 2 years follow-up were identified from the institution's database. All patients underwent UCLR via the Modified Jobe or Docking techniques12 and underwent a standardized UCLR rehabilitation, as detailed by Hadley et al.13The electronic medical record was reviewed for sport participation and baseline demographic characteristics including age at time of surgery, and body mass index (BMI). Preoperative elbow function and operative details were also recorded. Included patients completed patient reported outcome (PRO) surveys including the Kerlan Jobe Orthopaedic Clinic Shoulder & Elbow score (KJOC), the subjective portion of Timmerman and Andrews elbow score, the Conway-Jobe scale, and custom patient satisfaction and return to play questionnaires.2,14,15Results:Seventy-one total patients (50 Gracilis, 21 Palmaris) underwent primary UCL reconstruction and completed all surveys. The two groups did not differ significantly in any demographic or anthropometric data including age (p=.099), BMI (p=.462), level of play (p=.451), or position played (pitcher vs. non-pitcher) (p=1.0). They also did not differ in any preoperative elbow range of motion, valgus/varus stress testing or preoperative radiographic measurements (tear thickness seen on MRI (p=.252) or joint space gapping seen on ultrasound (p=.446)). There was no difference in pre-operative PROs using the KJOC score between the two groups (p=.814). Surgical techniques, use of internal brace, nerve management, and intra-operative complications were not different between the two groups (p=1.0). Post-operative complications were also not different between the groups (p=.421). Postoperatively, all PRO measures were similar (Conway Jobe classification (p=.867), KJOC (GR: 77.6 ± 22.4 vs PL: 75.0 ± 22.6, p=.584) and Timmerman-Andrews (GR: 89.9 ± 13.2 vs PL: 87.2 ± 8.16, p=.093). The only significant difference post-operatively was an increase in elbow flexion in the PL group (148o) compared to the gracilis group(144o) (p=<.001). Other measurements, including extension (p=.133), supination (p=.304), and pronation (p=.304), were all similar between the two groups.Conclusions:This study demonstrates that at 2-year follow-up, baseball players undergoing UCL reconstruction with either a palmaris longis or gracilis autograft achieve comparable clinical outcomes. These findings suggest that both grafts provide effective and functional solutions for restoring elbow stability in overhead athletes, allowing for individualized graft selection based on patient anatomy and surgeon preference.
