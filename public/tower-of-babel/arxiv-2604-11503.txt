Arbitrary-Velocity Volkov Wavepackets
D. Ramsey, J. McKeown, J. P. Palastro

Abstract
The evolution of a charged lepton in the field of an electromagnetic plane wave can be described as a superposition of Volkov states. Here we demonstrate that imposing specific momentum correlations among Volkov states produces a spatiotemporally structured wavepacket whose probability-density peak travels at an arbitrary, tailored velocity. This velocity can be chosen independently of both the field amplitude and the velocity expectation value. The imposed momentum correlations modify the expectation-value trajectory, providing a measurable signature of the arbitrary velocity within a physical observable.

Here, we show that the probability-density peak of a spatiotemporally structured lepton wavepacket can travel through an electromagnetic plane wave at any velocity, independent of its velocity expectation value. These wavepackets are constructed by prescribing specific correlations among the momenta of the constituent Volkov states. The correlations can be built into the initial wavepacket before it enters the electromagnetic field to produce a desired peak velocity within the field. The imposed correlations also modify the expectation-value trajectory, providing a measurable signature of the peak’s velocity. As an extension, we show that conventional wavepackets, with no prescribed momentum correlations, acquire correlations between their dressed momenta upon interaction with an electromagnetic plane wave.

The total wavepacket
\psi(x)
of a charged lepton can be constructed from a superposition of “partial” wavepackets
\Phi(x,\eta)
parameterized by
\eta
:

\psi(x)=\int\mathcal{N}(\eta)\Phi(x,\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta,

(1)

where
\mathcal{N}(\eta)
is a scalar weighting function normalized such that
\int|\mathcal{N}(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta=1
and
\int\bar{\psi}(x)\,\gamma_{-}\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{\mathrm{x}}=1
. In the absence of electromagnetic fields,

\Phi(x,\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int\frac{u(p)}{\sqrt{2p_{-}}}\delta(p_{0}-E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}})f(p,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{-ip\cdot x}\mathrm{d}^{4}p,

(2)

where
p_{-}\equiv p_{0}-p_{3}
,
E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}\equiv(m^{2}+|\bm{\mathrm{p}}|^{2})^{1/2}
, and the bispinor
u
is normalized according to
\bar{u}\gamma_{-}u=2p_{-}
. The complex, scalar distribution
f(p,\eta)
determines the
\eta
-dependent amplitude and phase of each momentum state composing the wavepacket.

The presence of an electromagnetic plane wave modifies the bispinor and phase of the partial wavepackets:

\Phi(x,\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int\frac{\mathcal{V}}{\sqrt{2p_{-}}}\delta(p_{0}-E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}})f(p,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{iS}\mathrm{d}^{4}p,

(3)

where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{V}(p,x_{-})\equiv\left[\mathbb{I}-\frac{\gamma_{-}\gamma_{1}\,eA_{1}(x_{-})}{2p_{-}}\right]u(p)\end{split}

(4)

is the dressed bispinor,
\mathbb{I}
denotes the identity matrix, and

S(p,x)=-p\cdot x+\frac{1}{p_{-}}\int_{0}^{x_{-}}\Big[ep_{1}A_{1}(x_{-}^{\prime})-\tfrac{e^{2}}{2}A_{1}^{2}(x_{-}^{\prime})\Big]\mathrm{d}x_{-}^{\prime}

(5)

is the classical action. The resulting total wavepacket is a superposition of Volkov states. While
A_{1}
may be any function of
x_{-}
, the examples presented here adopt the specific form

A_{1}(x_{-})=\xi(x_{-})\cos(\omega x_{-}+\phi),

(6)

where the profile
\xi(x_{-})
varies slowly compared to the period
2\pi/\omega
and
\phi
is an arbitrary phase. The following subsections develop the framework for arbitrary-velocity Volkov wavepackets by first reviewing the construction of conventional and field-free arbitrary-velocity wavepackets.

II.1
Field-Free Conventional Wavepackets

Outside of an electromagnetic field, conventional wavepackets are synthesized as superpositions of mono-energetic partial wavepackets with
\eta=p_{0}
. That is,
\Phi(x,\eta)
is chosen to be time-independent up to a global phase advance, such that

\Phi(x,\eta)-\Phi(x_{0}+T,\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{\perp},x_{3},\eta)\mathrm{e}^{i\eta T}=0,

(7)

where
T
is an arbitrary time interval. Taking the Fourier transform of Eq. (
7
) and multiplying by
\bar{u}\gamma_{-}
yields the constraint

f(p,\eta)\left[1-\mathrm{e}^{-i(p_{0}-\eta)T}\right]=0,

(8)

which can only be satisfied if
\eta=p_{0}
or equivalently
f(p,\eta)\propto\delta(\eta-p_{0})
. After imposing the on-shell condition to replace
p_{0}
with
E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}
in Eq. (
2
), the longitudinal momentum
p_{3}
is expressed in terms of
\eta
and
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
to enforce
\eta=E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}
:

p_{3}(\eta,\mathrm{p}_{\perp})=\,\pm\,\sqrt{\eta^{2}-(m^{2}+\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2})},

(9)

where
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}\equiv|\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}|
. Here, the negative sign is selected so that all plane waves composing the wavepacket travel in the negative
x_{3}
direction.

Choosing partial wavepackets that have the time-translation property described by Eq. (
7
) does not imply that the total wavepacket
\psi(x)
will have the same property. However, under the right conditions,
\psi(x)
will approximately hold this property for a finite duration. The first condition is that
\mathcal{N}(\eta)
is narrowly peaked about some characteristic value of
\eta
(e.g., the average energy in the case of
\eta=p_{0}
). The second condition is that there are no correlations between
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
and
\eta
:

\langle\eta\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle-\langle\eta\rangle\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle=\bm{\mathrm{0}},

(10)

where the angled brackets denote an expectation value. In order to satisfy the second condition,
f(p,\eta)
must take the form

f(p,\eta)=\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta}{\mathrm{d}p_{3}}\Big|^{1/2}\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})\delta(\eta-p_{0}).

(11)

The factor
|\mathrm{d}\eta/\mathrm{d}p_{3}|^{1/2}
ensures the probability density is separable in
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
and
\eta
. The transverse momentum distribution
\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
is normalized so that
\int|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}=1
, and is sufficiently localized in
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
to exclude evanescent waves (
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2}\ll\eta^{2}-m^{2}
), making it independent of
\eta
. For computational efficiency, all numerical examples employ a reduced transverse dimensionality, with
|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})|^{2}=(w/\sqrt{2\pi})\mathrm{exp}(-w^{2}p_{1}^{2}/2)\delta(p_{2})
, where
w
characterizes the transverse momentum spread.

\bar{\Phi}\gamma_{-}\Phi
. Due to the transverse momentum spread (
\propto w^{-1}
), the partial wavepacket “focuses” along
x_{3}
to a minimum transverse width
w
and then diffracts. Figure

11
)], with only the negative branch of Eq. (
9
) selected (green curve). The momentum domain lies at the intersection of the mass shell
E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}
(blue) and the horizontal plane
\eta=p_{0}
(translucent).

II.2
Field-Free Arbitrary-Velocity Wavepackets

Arbitrary-velocity lepton wavepackets are superpositions of propagation-invariant partial wavepackets with
\eta=p_{0}-v_{a}p_{3}
. The partial wavepackets satisfy

\Phi(x,\eta)-\Phi(x_{0}+T,\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{\perp},x_{3}+v_{a}T,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{i\eta T}=0

(12)

for an arbitrary velocity
v_{a}
and time interval
T

28
;
2
. This property implies that the amplitude of the partial wavepackets remains invariant in time along characteristics
x_{3}-v_{a}x_{0}=\mathrm{const}
. Note that setting
v_{a}=0
recovers the same translational property expressed by Eq. (
7
). Thus a conventional partial wavepacket can be recovered by setting
v_{a}=0
.

Taking the Fourier transform of Eq. (
12
) and multiplying by
\bar{u}\gamma_{-}
yields the constraint

f(p,\eta)\left[1-\mathrm{e}^{-i(p_{0}-v_{a}p_{3}-\eta)T}\right]=0,

(13)

which can only be satisfied if
\eta=p_{0}-v_{a}p_{3}
or
f(p,\eta)\propto\delta(\eta-p_{0}+v_{a}p_{3})
. After imposing the on-shell condition to replace
p_{0}
with
E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}
,
p_{3}
is expressed in terms of
\eta
and
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}
to enforce
\eta=E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}-v_{a}p_{3}
:

p_{3}(\eta,\mathrm{p}_{\perp})=\eta v_{a}\upgamma_{a}^{2}\,\pm\,\sqrt{(\eta\upgamma_{a}^{2})^{2}-\upgamma_{a}^{2}(m^{2}+\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2})},

(14)

where
\upgamma_{a}\equiv(1-v_{a}^{2})^{-1/2}
. Equation (
14
) shows that even though there are no correlations between
\eta
and
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}
[Eq. (
10
)], the on-shell condition and constraint
\eta=E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}-v_{a}p_{3}
impose a correlation between
p_{3}
and
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}
. The sign is chosen based on the value of
v_{a}
. For
|v_{a}|\leq 1
, the negative branch ensures that the longitudinal momenta are negative in a Lorentz frame moving with velocity
v_{a}
. For
|v_{a}|>1
, the positive branch ensures that the longitudinal momenta are negative in a Lorentz frame moving with velocity
1/v_{a}
. These two cases correspond to wavepackets with time-independent and longitudinal-coordinate-independent “foci” in the Lorentz frame, respectively (see Ref.
28
for details).

As before, the total wavepacket
\psi(x)
will approximately hold the property described by Eq. (
12
) if
\mathcal{N}(\eta)
is narrowly peaked about a characteristic value of
{\eta}
and
\langle\eta\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle-\langle\eta\rangle\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
. This latter condition requires that
f(p,\eta)
take the form

f(p,\eta)=\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta}{\mathrm{d}p_{3}}\Big|^{1/2}\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})\delta(\eta-p_{0}+v_{a}p_{3}),

(15)

which builds the correlation into the wavepacket. The transverse momentum distribution
\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
is again normalized so that
\int|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}=1
and excludes evanescent waves (
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2}\ll\upgamma_{a}^{2}\eta^{2}-m^{2}
).

Figure 1(c) illustrates the time-dependent amplitude of an arbitrary-velocity partial wavepacket
\bar{\Phi}\gamma_{-}\Phi
with a peak amplitude that moves at
v_{a}=-0.3
. Like the conventional case, the space–time wavepacket has a minimum width
w
determined by its transverse momentum spread. However, now the “focusing” and diffraction occur with respect to the coordinate
x_{3}-v_{a}x_{0}
. Figure
1
(d) provides the geometric interpretation of the momentum distribution. The domain lies along the negative branch of Eq. (
14
) (green curve), corresponding to the intersection of the mass shell
E_{\mathbf{p}}
(blue) and the plane
\eta=p_{0}-v_{a}p_{3}
(translucent). Along the intersection

\left(\frac{\partial{E}_{\mathbf{p}}}{\partial{{p}}_{3}}\right)_{\eta}=v_{a},

(16)

where
()_{\eta}
denotes that the derivative is taken with
\eta
held fixed. This relation indicates that
f(p,\eta)
features a unique correlation among the momenta, i.e.,
\partial{E}_{\mathbf{p}}/\partial{{p}}_{3}|_{\eta}\neq 0
. Nevertheless, when superposing partial wavepackets,
\eta
varies on the mass shell, such that
\partial E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}/\partial p_{3}=p_{3}/E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}
. Thus, while
v_{a}
can take any value, the expectation value of the longitudinal velocity,
\langle p_{3}/E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}\rangle
, is always subluminal.

II.3
Arbitrary-Velocity Volkov Wavepackets

In the presence of an electromagnetic plane wave, field-dependent terms in the phase
S
[Eq.(
5
)] modify the constraint required for propagation invariance. To determine this constraint, consider a charged lepton interacting with a four-potential that has a constant strength
\xi=\xi_{*}
, such that
A_{1}(x_{-})=\xi_{*}\cos(\omega x_{-}+\phi)
. The partial wavepackets are chosen to be propagation-invariant up to a global phase advance when averaged over a cycle of the potential:

\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{\omega}}\left[\Phi(x,\eta)-\Phi(x_{0}+T,\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{\perp},x_{3}+v_{f}T,\eta)e^{i\eta T}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{-}=0,

(17)

where
T
is an arbitrary time interval and
v_{f}
is the arbitrary velocity in the field. Taking the Fourier transform of Eq. (
17
), multiplying by
\bar{u}\gamma_{-}
, and applying the Jacobi–Anger expansion to simplify the cycle average yields the constraint

f(p,\eta)\left[1-\mathrm{e}^{-i(q_{0}-v_{f}q_{3}-\eta)T}\right]=0.

(18)

Here, the field-dressed four-momenta
q
are defined as

q\equiv p+\frac{e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}}{4p_{-}}n,

(19)

subject to the dressed-shell condition
q_{0}=U_{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}\equiv(m^{2}+e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}/2+|\bm{\mathrm{q}}|^{2})^{1/2}

3
. The constraint expressed by
Eq. (
18
) can only be satisfied if
f(p,\eta)\propto\delta(\eta-q_{0}+v_{f}q_{3})
, which shows that propagation invariance in an electromagnetic plane wave requires correlations among the dressed momenta.

At this stage, the wavepacket construction could proceed along the same path as in the previous subsections, with the momenta replaced by the dressed momenta. However, this approach has two practical limitations. First, a physically realizable wavepacket would typically be prepared outside the field, where imposing correlations among dressed momenta—whose dependence on the momenta is nonlinear—would be challenging. Second, the strength of a physical four-potential is never constant, i.e.,
\xi
depends on
x_{-}
, such that
A_{1}(x_{-})=\xi(x_{-})\cos(\omega x_{-}+\phi)
. As a result, the propagation invariant property described by Eq. (
17
) can only occur at a target potential strength
\xi=\xi_{*}
.

Notably, these limitations do not apply in the special case of
v_{f}=1
, where
\eta=p_{-}
and the probability-density peak travels at the constant velocity
v_{a}={v_{f}}=1
regardless of the local value of
\xi(x_{-})

8
. This occurs because
p_{-}=q_{-}
is a conserved quantity, ensuring that Eq. (
17
) is satisfied across all potential strengths.

Here, the goal is to prepare a wavepacket with a linear momentum correlation outside the field, so its probability-density peak travels at a desired velocity
v_{f*}
at a target strength
\xi_{*}
inside the field. This is achieved by appropriately setting the out-of-field velocity
v_{a}
. To determine this velocity, the out-of-field plane
\eta=p_{0}-v_{a}p_{3}
is expressed in terms of the dressed momenta at
\xi=\xi_{*}
, yielding the surface

\lambda(\eta,q_{-})-q_{0}+v_{f*}q_{3}=0,

(20)

where

\lambda\equiv\left(\frac{1-v_{f_{*}}}{1-v_{a}}\right)\eta-\left(\frac{v_{a}-v_{f_{*}}}{1-v_{a}}\right)q_{-}+(1-v_{f*})\frac{e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}}{4q_{-}}.

(21)

The surface is locally planar with slope
v_{f*}
relative to
q_{3}
if

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial q_{3}}=\left(\frac{v_{a}-v_{f_{*}}}{1-v_{a}}\right)+(1-v_{f*})\frac{e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}}{4q_{-}^{2}}=0.

(22)

When evaluated on-shell,
q_{-}=p_{-}=E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}-p_{3}(\eta,\mathrm{p}_{\perp})
[see Eq. (
14
)], this condition depends on
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}
and
\eta
. To first approximation, this dependence can be removed by evaluating
q_{-}
at the expectation values
\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle
and
\langle\eta\rangle
, providing the out-of-field velocity

v_{a}\approx\frac{{v}_{f*}-(1-{v}_{f*})\tfrac{e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}}{1-(1-{v}_{f*})\tfrac{e^{2}\xi_{*}^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}},

(23)

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{-}\equiv p_{-}(\eta=\langle\eta\rangle,\,\mathrm{p}_{\perp}=\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle)
. With
v_{a}
determined, the out-of-field distribution
f(p,\eta)
is given by Eq. (
15
). The resulting in-field distribution produces the desired linear correlation between the dressed momenta at
\xi=\xi_{*}
:
f(p,\eta)\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\cr\propto\cr\kern 2.0pt\cr\sim\cr\kern-2.0pt\cr}}}\delta[\Lambda-q_{0}+v_{f*}q_{3}]
in the vicinity of
q_{-}=\mathcal{P}_{-}
and
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}=\langle{\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle}
, where
\Lambda\equiv\lambda(\langle\eta\rangle,\mathcal{P}_{-})
. Together with the on-shell condition,
q_{0}=U_{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}
, the constraint imposed by the delta function reveals a correlation between
q_{3}
and
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}
:

\begin{split}q_{3}(\Lambda,\mathrm{p}_{\perp})\approx&\Lambda v_{f*}\upgamma_{f*}^{2}\\
&\pm\sqrt{(\Lambda\upgamma_{f*}^{2})^{2}-\upgamma_{f*}^{2}(m^{2}+\tfrac{e^{2}}{2}\xi_{*}^{2}+\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2})},\end{split}

(24)

where
\upgamma_{f*}\equiv(1-v_{f*}^{2})^{-1/2}
, which is a dressed version of Eq. (
14
).

III
Trajectories

This section examines the distinct dynamics of the expectation and peak-probability trajectories by presenting their equations of motion, comparing their evolution, and analyzing the influence of momentum correlations and
v_{a}
on the expectation trajectory.

III.1
Peak-Probability Trajectory

When the weighting function
\mathcal{N}(\eta)
is narrowly peaked about the expectation value
\langle{\eta}\rangle
, the phase
S
[Eq. (
5
)] can be expanded about the intersection of
\langle{\eta}\rangle
and the mass shell. When the paraxial approximation is valid,
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2}\ll(\langle\eta\rangle^{2}\upgamma_{a}^{2}-m^{2})
, the phase can also be expanded to second order about
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}=\langle\mathrm{p}_{\perp}\rangle=0
. Together, these approximations yield

\begin{split}S\approx&\,{\mathcal{P}}_{3}x_{3}-{\mathcal{E}}x_{0}-\frac{1}{2\mathcal{P}_{-}}\mathcal{I}_{2}(x_{-})\\
+&p_{1}x_{1}+\frac{p_{1}}{{\mathcal{P}}_{-}}\mathcal{I}_{1}(x_{-})+p_{2}x_{2}\\
+&\frac{\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{2(\mathcal{E}v_{a}-{\mathcal{P}}_{3})}\left(x_{3}-v_{a}x_{0}-\frac{1-v_{a}}{2{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{2}(x_{-})\right),\end{split}

(26)

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{3}\equiv p_{3}(\eta=\langle\eta\rangle,\mathrm{p}_{\perp}=0)
with
p_{3}
given by Eq. (
14
),
{\mathcal{E}}\equiv(m^{2}+\mathcal{P}_{3}^{2})^{1/2}
, and

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{1}(x_{-})&=\int_{0}^{x_{-}}eA_{1}(x_{-}^{\prime})\;\mathrm{d}x_{-}^{\prime},\\
\mathcal{I}_{2}(x_{-})&=\int_{0}^{x_{-}}e^{2}A_{1}^{2}(x_{-}^{\prime})\;\mathrm{d}x_{-}^{\prime}.\end{split}

Although the final term has an apparent singularity at
v_{a}={\mathcal{P}}_{3}/{\mathcal{E}}
, this value of
v_{a}
lies outside the regime of validity of the approximations made to derive Eq. (
26
) (see Appendix A).

Each term in Eq. (
26
) can be understood by conceptualizing the wavepacket as an envelope modulated by a carrier wave. The carrier describes the motion of the phase fronts, which is governed by the terms of
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{0})
[first line of Eq. (
26
)]. The envelope describes the trajectory of the probability-density peak
\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{f}
, which is governed by the remaining terms [second and third lines of Eq. (
26
)]. The transverse and longitudinal locations of the peak are determined by the vanishing of the
\mathcal{O}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2})
terms, respectively, for all
p
. For
v_{a}\neq 1
, this results in the equations of motion:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{f1}}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{eA_{1}}}{{\mathcal{P}}_{-}},

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{f2}}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=0,

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{f3}}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{v_{a}}}{1-{v_{a}}}+\frac{e^{2}A_{1}^{2}}{2{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}.

(29)

For
v_{a}=v_{f}=1
,
\mathrm{d}x_{f3}/\mathrm{d}x_{0}=1

and the longitudinal trajectory is non-oscillatory at any field amplitude
8
. With
A_{1}=\xi(x_{-})\cos(\omega x_{-}+\phi)
, the cycle average (denoted by a tilde) of Eq. (
29
) becomes

\frac{\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{f3}}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}=\frac{{v_{f}}(x_{-})}{1-{v_{f}}(x_{-})},

(30)

where

v_{f}(x_{-})=\frac{v_{a}+(1-v_{a})\tfrac{e^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\xi^{2}(x_{-})}{1+(1-v_{a})\tfrac{e^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\xi^{2}(x_{-})}.

(31)

Equation (
31
) shows that the longitudinal velocity of the probability-density peak depends on the local value of
\xi
and confirms that a constant velocity
v_{f*}
can only be achieved at a target value
\xi=\xi_{*}
. Note that even when
v_{a}=0
, the probability-density peak follows a trajectory determined by the local potential strength.

III.2
Expectation-Value Trajectory

The equations of motion for the position expectation values follow from

\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle\bm{\mathrm{x}}\rangle}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}\int\bm{\mathrm{x}}\;\bar{\psi}(x)\,\gamma_{-}\,\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{\mathrm{x}}.

Componentwise, this gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle x_{1}\rangle}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=-\int\left(\frac{eA_{1}}{{p}_{-}}\right)|\mathcal{N}(\eta)|^{2}|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle x_{2}\rangle}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=0,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle x_{3}\rangle}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\frac{p_{3}}{p_{-}}+\frac{e^{2}A_{1}^{2}}{2p_{-}^{2}}\right)|\mathcal{N}(\eta)|^{2}|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

(34)

where
\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
is assumed to be an even function of
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
and the momenta are on-shell:
p_{-}(\eta,\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})=E_{\bm{\mathrm{p}}}-p_{3}(\eta,\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
with
p_{3}(\eta,\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp})
given by Eq. (
14
).

An approximate expression for the cycle-averaged longitudinal velocity is obtained by expanding the terms in parentheses about
\eta=\langle\eta\rangle
and
\mathrm{p}_{\perp}=0
to second order, leading to

\begin{split}{\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle\tilde{x}_{3}\rangle}{\mathrm{d}x_{-}}}\approx&\frac{v_{1\mathrm{D}}}{1-v_{1\mathrm{D}}}\\
&+\frac{1-v_{f}v_{1\mathrm{D}}}{2{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}(v_{f}-v_{1\mathrm{D}})}\int\mathrm{p}_{\perp}^{2}|\mathcal{T}(\bm{\mathrm{{p}}}_{\perp})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp},\end{split}

(35)

where

v_{\mathrm{1D}}=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{3}/\mathcal{E}+(1-\mathcal{P}_{3}/\mathcal{E})\tfrac{e^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\xi^{2}(x_{-})}{1+(1-\mathcal{P}_{3}/\mathcal{E})\tfrac{e^{2}}{4{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\xi^{2}(x_{-})}

(36)

is the one-dimensional drift velocity of a planar wavepacket. The second term in Eq. (
35
) accounts for the effect of transverse momentum and depends explicitly on
v_{f}
(and thus on
v_{a}
). This dependence is a consequence of enforcing separability between
\eta
and
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
, which necessarily introduces correlations among other momentum combinations. For example, a conventional wavepacket (
v_{a}=0
) exhibits no correlation between
p_{0}
and
\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}
, i.e.,
\langle p_{0}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle-\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
, whereas an arbitrary-velocity wavepacket (
v_{a}\neq 0
) does:
\langle p_{0}\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle-\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle\bm{\mathrm{p}}_{\perp}\rangle\neq\bm{\mathrm{0}}
. Note that the apparent singularity at
v_{1\mathrm{D}}=v_{f}
is equivalent to the singularity observed in Eq. (
26
) [cf. Eqs. (
35
) and (
31
) and see Appendix A].

IV
Discussion and Conclusion

The probability-density peak of a charged-lepton wavepacket can move through the field of an electromagnetic plane wave at an arbitrary, prescribed velocity. This is achieved by constructing the wavepacket with a specific momentum correlation prior to its interaction with the field. Upon entering the field, the initial structure gives rise to correlations between the dressed momenta of the constituent Volkov states. These correlations leave an imprint on the expectation trajectory, providing a potentially measurable signature of the prescribed velocity.

In configuration space and outside of the field, arbitrary-velocity Volkov wavepackets have constant spherical or hyperbolic wavefronts with respect to the comoving coordinate
x_{3}-v_{a}x_{0}
. A potential experimental realization of such a wavepacket could involve introducing a time-dependent curvature phase at a fixed value of
x_{3}
. This can be accomplished using the Kapitza–Dirac effect. More specifically, two counter-propagating light waves with time-varying amplitudes form a standing wave that acts as a “lens,” focusing different temporal slices of the wavepacket to different locations. This mechanism is directly analogous to optical “flying-focus” techniques
41
;
32
;
28
;
2
. In this context,
v_{a}
can be interpreted as the “focal” velocity.

The theory of arbitrary-velocity Volkov wavepackets was presented for linearly polarized electromagnetic plane waves, and the examples performed for wavepackets with longitudinal momentum anti-parallel to the plane-wave propagation direction. However, both the theory and examples generalize to arbitrary polarizations, relative orientations, and peak velocity directions. Any in-field velocity vector
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{f*}
can be realized for a general plane-wave four-potential
A(x_{-})
. Outside the field, this generalization is achieved by setting
\eta=p_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{p}
. Because the transverse dressed momenta equals the transverse momenta, the in-field and out-of-field transverse velocities are identical. As a result, a specific
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{f*}
can be obtained by following the procedure in Sec.
II
C with
\xi^{2}_{*}/2
replaced by the cycle average of
-A\cdot A
at the target strength. As before, the component of
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{a}
along the propagation direction of the plane wave is chosen to ensure that the target velocity is reached at
\xi_{*}
.

More broadly, this work highlights that electromagnetic fields induce spatiotemporal structure in charged-lepton wavepackets. Even a conventional wavepacket, separable in energy and transverse momentum, acquires a correlation among its dressed momenta during its interaction with an electromagnetic wave. These correlations cause the probability-density peak to follow a trajectory that deviates from the expectation trajectory. Thus, the dynamics of structured wavepackets are more than a product of wavefunction engineering; they are fundamental to the interaction of charged particles with electromagnetic fields. In fact, the structure of a conventional wavepacket is only preserved during interactions with stationary potentials. These results point to space–time correlations as a general feature of wavepacket dynamics, with implications beyond the leptonic systems studied here. For instance, analogous correlations would arise in conventional electromagnetic waves propagating through dynamic and structured media, where the refractive index acts as the potential.

v_{a}=\mathcal{P}_{3}/\mathcal{E}

26

\eta=p_{\mp}

(37)

p_{\mp}

\mathrm{p}_{\perp}

p_{3}(\langle{\eta}\rangle,\mathrm{p}_{\perp})=\mathcal{P}_{3}\pm i\frac{\mathrm{p}_{\perp}\mathcal{E}}{m}.

Appendix B
Lifetime of the Peak-Probability Trajectory

The trajectory of the probability-density peak is only apparent while it remains within the wavepacket envelope, which follows the expectation-value trajectory. Consequently, the length of the envelope,
\Delta x_{3}
, determines the lifetime of the peak. The length is inversely proportional to the spectral width
\Delta\eta
:

\Delta x_{3}\propto\bigg|v_{a}-\frac{{\mathcal{P}}_{3}}{{\mathcal{E}}}\bigg|\frac{1}{\Delta\eta}.

(39)

To leading order, the rising and falling edges of the envelope follow the trajectories

\begin{split}{\langle\tilde{x}_{3}\rangle}_{\mathrm{edge}}\approx\frac{{\mathcal{P}}_{3}}{\mathcal{P}_{-}}x_{-}+\frac{e^{2}}{2{\mathcal{P}}_{-}^{2}}\int_{0}^{x_{-}}\tilde{A^{2}_{1}}(x_{-}^{\prime})\;\mathrm{d}x_{-}^{\prime}\pm\frac{\Delta x_{3}}{1-{\mathcal{P}}_{3}/{\mathcal{E}}},\end{split}

(40)

while the probability-density peak follows [Eq. (
30
)]

\tilde{x}_{f3}=\int_{0}^{x_{-}}\frac{v_{f}}{1-v_{f}}\;\mathrm{d}x_{-}^{\prime}.

(41)

The intersections of these trajectories,

\tilde{x}_{f3}-{\langle\tilde{x}_{3}\rangle}_{\mathrm{edge}}=0,

(42)

determine the duration
\Delta x_{-}
over which the peak persists. For a varying potential strength, these intercepts must be computed numerically. For a constant potential strength
\xi=\xi_{*}
, the duration can be computed analytically:

\begin{split}\Delta x_{0}&=\frac{2\Delta x_{3}}{|1-{\mathcal{P}}_{3}/\mathcal{E}|}\left|\frac{1-v_{\mathrm{1D}}}{v_{f*}-v_{\mathrm{1D}}}\right|,\\
\end{split}

(43)

where
v_{\mathrm{1D}}
is evaluated at
\xi=\xi_{*}
[Eq. (
36
)]. In the absence of an electromagnetic field,
\xi_{*}=0
,
v_{\mathrm{1D}}={\mathcal{P}}_{3}/{\mathcal{E}}
,
v_{f*}=v_{a}
, and Eq. (
43
) reduces to

\Delta x_{0}=\frac{2\Delta x_{3}}{|v_{a}-v_{\mathrm{1D}}|},

(44)

consistent with the out-of-field result in Ref.
28
.
