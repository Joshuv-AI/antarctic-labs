Objective

The Jaguar (Panthera onca) is a large cat species endemic from America. Although this felid is a successful predator in the ecosystem, it has recently suffered population declines coupled to habitat loss [1], being assessed as “Nearly-Threatened” in 2017 by the IUCN [2]. A high quality annotated reference genome, to which additional transcriptomic or genomic sequences can be mapped, is an important resource not only to help monitoring or conserving relevant species, but also to understand their biology and evolution [3]. The first draft genome sequence for Jaguar was published back in 2017 [4], which revealed complex patterns of past introgressions with other cat lineages. This reference has served as the basis for multiple analyses, including population genomics by remapping exome or whole genome shotgun sequencing data from diverse individuals [5], or to trace specific evolutionary adaptations [6]. However, the limited contiguity of this reference assembly may limit the type of insights that it can lead to and underscores the need for improvement to reach the higher standards fulfilled by other related species [7]. Here, we set out to provide an improved assembly for the Jaguar, using two Argentinian captive samples sequenced with long and short read technologies. For this, we sequenced, assembled, and annotated the genome of P. onca using material from two individuals. A hybrid assembly approach, including short illumina and long ONT reads was then undertaken and combined with Hi-C scaffolding using public data from the same species. Consistent with the known karyotype (2n = 38) [8], the resulting chromosome-level genome assembly has 19 super-scaffolds corresponding to the 18 autosomes and the X chromosome. The results from this work represent a significant advancement in the genomic research on this endangered iconic South American species and can help to design more appropriate strategies for conservation and reintroduction.

Data description

Samples

We used fibroblast cells derived from primary cell cultures obtained from muscle and ear biopsies of two captive individuals housed at the ex Buenos Aires Zoo (Eco-Park, CABA, Argentina) [17]. These two specimens were a male named Tango (mPanOnc1) and a female named Gorda (mPanOnc3).

DNA extraction and sequencing

As fibroblast cultures from the mPanOnc3 biopsy worked very well this sample was chosen for ONT sequencing. In both cases, DNA was extracted from fibroblast cultures using the salting-out method [18]. We used the kit SQK-LSK109 with the mPanOnc3 High-Molecular Weight DNA to prepare the ONT libraries and sequencing was performed using the ONT MinION Mk1B device producing 93.05 Gb of data. The mPanOnc1 DNA sample was used to prepare PE libraries sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq2000 machine producing 66 Gb of Illumina PE 2 × 126 reads.

Public sequence data

Hi-C data previously generated for another female jaguar (biosample: SAMN14122073) totalling 290,369,408 read pairs and 82.18 Gb was downloaded from the Sequence Read Archive at NCBI (Table 1) [13–16].

Overview of data files/data sets

Genome assembly

First, we preprocessed Illumina reads using Cutadapt v3.2 [19] and the ONT reads with FiltLong v0.2.0 (https://github.com/rrwick/Filtlong). Second, the ONT reads were assembled with Flye v2.8.3 [20] and options -i 2 and --min-ovlp 5000. Third, the Flye assembly contigs were polished twice with ONT and twice with illumina reads using NextPolish v1.4.1 [21]. Fourth, the contigs assembly was collapsed with purge_dups v1.2.5 [22]. Fifth, the purged assembly was cleaned from contaminants and a mitochondrial insertion. Sixth, these contigs were scaffolded using the Hi-C data with YaHS v1.2 [23] without allowing contig breaks. Seventh, the scaffolded assembly was manually curated using PretextViewAI v1.0.5 and chromosomal assignments were done by aligning our curated assembly against the domestic cat FelCat9 assembly (GCF_000181335.3) with minimap2 [24] v2.24 and option -x asm5. Finally, our assembly mPanOnc3.5 has 18 autosomes and the X chromosome.

Genome annotation

The gene annotation was obtained by combining transcript alignments, protein alignments and ab initio gene predictions. First, the Illumina RNA-seq reads from several tissues of Panthera leo (SRR13380817, SRR13380818, SRR5485090, SRR5485091, SRR5485092, SRR5485093, SRR5485094) were aligned to the genome using STAR v-2.7.2a [25] and transcript models were subsequently generated using Stringtie v2.2.1 [26]. All the models produced were combined using TACO v0.7.3 [27]. High-quality junctions were obtained by running ESPRESSO v1.3.0 [28] after mapping with STAR. Finally, PASA assemblies were produced with PASA v2.5.2 [29] by adding also the Panthera onca transcripts annotated on a GCF_028533385.1. The TransDecoder program, which is part of the PASA package, was run on the PASA assemblies to detect coding regions in the transcripts. Third, the complete proteomes of Panthera leo, P. tigris altaica, Felis catus, Acinox jubatus, Lynx pardinus and L. canadiensis were downloaded from Uniprot in October 2022 and aligned to the genome using Miniprot [30]. Ab initio gene predictions were performed on the repeat-masked assembly with three different programs: GeneID v1.4 [31], Augustus v3.5.0 [32] and GeneMark-ET v4.71 [33] with and without incorporating evidence from the RNAseq data. Finally, all the data were combined into consensus CDS models using EvidenceModeler-1.1.1 (EVM) [29]. Additionally, UTRs and alternative splicing forms were annotated via two rounds of PASA annotation updates. Functional annotation was performed on the annotated proteins with Pannzer’s [34] online server.

Limitations

First, as expected the genome assembly does not contain the Y chromosome, but it contains a complete X chromosome which includes the PAR region. Second, this is a collapsed reference genome assembly as the ONT data used did not allow resolving the entire genome in two haplotypes. Third, as the Illumina data used for polishing comes from a different individual the reference contains some “donor-specific” alleles from another population, but this will not bias mapping-based variant calling to detect SNPs segregating in the populations or studying structural variants (SVs) in this species by comparison with the other chromosome-level assemblies.
