Quantum geometry from commutators: a Heisenberg-picture framework and a toy application to early structure

Abstract
We develop a Heisenberg-picture kinematical framework in which (i) time is treated as a quantum observable, admitting both a relational POVM construction for semibounded spectra and a fully self-adjoint realization on an enlarged (conjugate-energy) Hilbert space enabled by a gravitational conjugation symmetry 𝒞 g \mathcal{C}_{g} , and (ii) the generators of spacetime translations need not commute in curved backgrounds. I
Introduction

Reconciling quantum mechanics with dynamical spacetime motivates elevating geometric data to quantum operators. Here we develop a compact, explicitly
kinematical
framework where the spacetime metric arises
from commutators
. The guiding observation is twofold:
(i) in relativity the time interval depends on the observer, motivating time as an observable at the quantum level; (ii) in curved spacetime the naive notion that translations commute can fail—the covariant derivative algebra contains curvature and, more generally, torsion
Wald:1984
;
MTW:1973
;
BirrellDavies:1982
.

Our starting point is the unified canonical relation

[\,\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}(\hat{x}),

(1)

which defines the Hermitian
metric operator
by its symmetrized form,

\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\hbar}\!\left(\,[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}]+[\hat{x}_{\nu},\hat{P}_{\mu}]\,\right),\qquad\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-,+,+,+),

(2)

and reduces to Minkowski space in the flat limit. Demanding algebraic closure fixes the “structure functions” appearing in the commutator of translation generators and yields an operator form of metric compatibility. In a worked
FRW

[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]=2\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)\,H(t)\,\hat{P}_{i}
(Sec.
V

in cosmic-time gauge

,
making explicit that expansion can drive non-commuting translation generators.

Gravitational conjugation (overview).

A central structural result of this paper is a
gravitational conjugation
symmetry
\mathcal{C}_{g}
:
let
\Theta
be antiunitary (
\Theta\,\mathrm{i}\,\Theta^{-1}=-\mathrm{i}
); define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{g}:\;\hat{x}^{\mu}\mapsto\Theta\hat{x}^{\mu}\Theta^{-1},\quad\hat{P}_{\mu}\mapsto-\,\Theta\hat{P}_{\mu}\Theta^{-1},\quad

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\mapsto\Theta\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{-1},\quad\hat{J}_{ab}\mapsto\Theta\hat{J}_{ab}\Theta^{-1}.

This map preserves the canonical commutators and the full
[P,P]

Physically,
\mathcal{C}_{g}
organizes negative-energy solutions into a conjugate sector while preserving the operator algebra, in analogy with the Dirac reinterpretation of negative-energy solutions in relativistic quantum mechanics
Dirac:1928
.
We stress that this paper is kinematical: we do not propose a full interacting field theory of sector dynamics.

Time as an observable (overview).

Treating time as an operator raises the standard Pauli-type obstruction for semibounded Hamiltonians
Pauli:1933
;
AharonovBohm:1961
;
Muga:2008

;
Rovelli:1991
;
BuschGrabowskiLahti:1995

Here we present both: a conservative POVM realization (semibounded spectra) and a more symmetric self-adjoint realization on an enlarged Hilbert space once
\mathcal{C}_{g}
is included.

Contributions.

This paper: (1) states a closed operator algebra in which the metric is a commutator and Jacobi identities imply operator metric-compatibility; (2) provides two consistent realizations of time as an observable (POVM and self-adjoint on an enlarged spectrum); (3) constructs explicit representations, including a simple

Kinematical postulates and time as an observable

II.1
Postulates

We adopt the following operator algebra on a dense domain
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle[\,\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}\,]

\displaystyle=0,

(4a)

\displaystyle[\,\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}(\hat{x}),

(4b)

\displaystyle[\,\hat{P}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{C}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}(\hat{x})\,\hat{P}_{\rho}\quad\text{(minimal scalar sector)},

(4c)

where
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric and of Lorentzian signature in expectation values on physical states. The
structure functions

\hat{C}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}
will be fixed by the Jacobi identities below. Equation (
4c

II.2
Time as an observable and Heisenberg evolution

Promoting
\hat{x}^{0}
to an operator with

[\,\hat{x}^{0},\hat{P}_{0}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{g}_{00}

raises the familiar tension with Pauli-type arguments when
\hat{P}_{0}
is semibounded and
\hat{x}^{0}
is required to be self-adjoint
Pauli:1933
;
AharonovBohm:1961
;
Muga:2008
. In the present framework this issue can be addressed in two complementary ways that correspond to different physical realizations.

(i) Relational / POVM implementation (semibounded spectra).

For systems whose Hamiltonian spectrum remains semibounded,
\hat{x}^{0}
may be treated as a maximally symmetric operator associated with a positive-operator-valued measure (POVM) of time. In this form the commutator (
1
) holds on a dense domain without demanding self-adjointness. Physical evolution is described in the Heisenberg picture with respect to an external parameter
\tau
(for example, an affine parameter), and “time” predictions are
relational
, conditioned on the outcomes of the clock POVM
PageWootters:1983
;
Rovelli:1991
;
BuschGrabowskiLahti:1995
;
Muga:2008
.

(ii) Conjugate-energy / self-adjoint realization (enlarged spectrum).

Once
\mathcal{C}_{g}
is included, it is natural to organize the kinematics on an enlarged Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys}\;=\;\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-},

(5)

where the two sectors are exchanged by
\mathcal{C}_{g}
(Sec.
VII
) and the total energy spectrum need not be semibounded. In such a setting the spectral premise of Pauli-type no-go arguments is absent
Pauli:1933
;
Muga:2008
, and one may realize
\hat{x}^{0}
as a genuinely self-adjoint operator conjugate (in the sense of (
1
)) to the appropriate energy generator on a common dense domain. In this realization one may work with generalized eigenstates

\hat{x}^{0}|\tau\rangle=\tau|\tau\rangle,

so that Heisenberg evolution proceeds directly in the physical time variable.

Scope and failure modes.

This paper does
not
claim that every interacting theory admits such a self-adjoint realization. In particular, strong sector-mixing interactions (operators odd under
\mathcal{C}_{g}
) could spoil superselection and reintroduce stability issues. We therefore view (ii) as a kinematical possibility that becomes available once the spectrum is enlarged by
\mathcal{C}_{g}
, and leave a full dynamical implementation to future work.

V
Worked example: spatially flat FRW

FRW with lapse and the meaning of
\hat{x}^{0}
.

To make the time-choice explicit, consider spatially flat
FRW
with a general lapse
N(t)
,

ds^{2}=-N^{2}(t)\,dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\,d\bm{x}^{2},\qquad\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-,+,+,+).

(18)

In the scalar coordinate representation (
15
), we take the coordinate
t
to be the time coordinate on the background manifold and represent the time operator as multiplication by that coordinate,

(\hat{x}^{0}\psi)(t,\bm{x})=t\,\psi(t,\bm{x})
, with
c=1
so that
t
has dimensions of length.
For the worked example below we will frequently specialize to cosmic-time gauge
N(t)=1
, in which
t
coincides with proper time along comoving worldlines; the lapse-dependent formulas make the gauge choice transparent.

Choose the scalar representation (
15
) with

\hat{P}_{0}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\,g_{00}\,\partial_{t}=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)\,\partial_{t},\qquad\hat{P}_{i}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\,g_{ij}\,\partial_{j}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\,a^{2}(t)\,\partial_{i}.

(19)

Then

\displaystyle[\,\hat{x}^{0},\hat{P}_{0}\,]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,g_{00}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t),\qquad[\,\hat{x}^{i},\hat{P}_{j}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,a^{2}(t)\,\delta^{i}{}_{j},

(20)

\displaystyle[\,\hat{P}_{i},\hat{P}_{j}\,]

\displaystyle=0,\qquad[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]=2\,\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)\,H(t)\,\hat{P}_{i},

(21)

with

H(t)\equiv\dot{a}(t)/a(t)

defined with respect to the coordinate time
t
. In cosmic-time gauge
N=1
, Eq. (
21
) reduces to the simpler form
[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]=2\mathrm{i}\hbar H\hat{P}_{i}
quoted in the main text.
Equation (
21
) explicitly realizes the Second Principle: the generator of time translations fails to commute with spatial translation generators by an amount set by the expansion rate.

Special epochs:
H=0
and
H\to\infty
.

Because the noncommutativity is proportional to
H(t)
, two limiting regimes are worth making explicit.

(i) Turning point / bounce:
if
H(t_{\ast})=0
at some instant
t_{\ast}
(e.g. a turnaround between expansion and contraction), then
[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]_{t_{\ast}}=0
and the algebra reduces smoothly (at that instant) to commuting translation generators in this scalar realization. If
H
changes sign across
t_{\ast}
, the sign of the commutator flips continuously, with no algebraic inconsistency.

(ii) Singular regime:
in classical FRW solutions with a big-bang-type singularity one has
|H(t)|\to\infty
as
a(t)\to 0
. In the representation (
15
), the commutator acts on a dense domain (e.g. smooth compact-support functions in
\bm{x}
with suitable time regularity) as

[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]\psi=2\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)H(t)\,\hat{P}_{i}\psi
,
which is well-defined for any finite
H(t)
. The limit
H\to\infty
corresponds to an unbounded blow-up of the commutator coefficient and signals breakdown of the background representation (and, physically, breakdown of the classical FRW description) rather than an algebraic contradiction. We therefore interpret Eq. (
21
) as applying on epochs where
N^{2}H
remains finite, or after suitable regularization (e.g. bounce/cutoff) of the background.

Role of symmetry and global topology.

Homogeneity and isotropy are encoded here by the restriction
g_{ij}=a^{2}(t)\delta_{ij}
and
N=N(t)
, which in particular implies
[\,\hat{P}_{i},\hat{P}_{j}\,]=0
for the scalar representation above. More general cosmologies (anisotropic Bianchi models or curved-

Global topology affects the Hilbert space and spectra but not the local commutator. For instance, replacing
\mathbb{R}^{3}

enforces periodic boundary conditions so that the spectrum of

becomes discrete; however the local relation (
21

For scalars with the Levi–Civita covariant derivative one has

HayashiShirafuji:1979

For simplicity, in this conventions note we specialize to cosmic-time gauge
N=1

\hat{P}_{i}^{(\mathrm{fr})}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar a^{-1}\partial_{i}

VI
Second check beyond FRW: weak-field (linearized) background

To demonstrate that the commutator-geometry construction is not special to
FRW
, we briefly consider a weak-field perturbation around Minkowski space in the scalar coordinate representation. Let

g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}(x),\qquad|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll 1,

(22)

with indices raised/lowered by
\eta
at leading order. Expanding Eq. (
9
) to first order in
h
yields

C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}=\partial_{\nu}h^{\rho}{}_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}h^{\rho}{}_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

(23)

and hence, in the minimal scalar sector,

[\,\hat{P}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,(\partial_{\nu}h^{\rho}{}_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}h^{\rho}{}_{\nu})\,\hat{P}_{\rho}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

(24)

Newtonian limit (static weak field).

As a concrete specialization, take a static weak-field metric

ds^{2}=-(1+2\Phi(\bm{x}))\,dt^{2}+(1-2\Psi(\bm{x}))\,d\bm{x}^{2},

(25)

so that
h_{00}=-2\Phi
,
h_{0i}=0
, and
h_{ij}=-2\Psi\,\delta_{ij}
. If
\Phi
and
\Psi
are time-independent, Eq. (
23
) gives, in particular,

C_{0i}{}^{0}=\partial_{i}h^{0}{}_{0}=2\,\partial_{i}\Phi,\qquad C_{0i}{}^{j}=-\partial_{0}h^{j}{}_{i}=0,

(26)

and therefore

[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]=2\,\mathrm{i}\hbar\,(\partial_{i}\Phi)\,\hat{P}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

(27)

Thus, in the weak-field (inhomogeneous) regime, the noncommutativity of translation generators is controlled by gradients of the Newtonian potential, and reduces smoothly to the commuting Minkowski algebra when
\partial_{i}\Phi=0
(or
h_{\mu\nu}=0
).

Behavior under
\mathcal{C}_{g}
at first order.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{g}
acts by
\hat{P}_{\mu}\mapsto-\Theta\hat{P}_{\mu}\Theta^{-1}
while transforming
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
covariantly, the linearized structure functions in Eq. (
23
) transform covariantly as well. Consequently the form of the
[P,P]
algebra in Eq. (
24
) is preserved under
\mathcal{C}_{g}
order-by-order in
h
(for real classical
h_{\mu\nu}
this is immediate, since
\Theta
reduces to complex conjugation on
c
-number coefficients).

VII
Conjugate-energy sector and gravitational conjugation

Dirac resolved the negative-energy problem of relativistic quantum mechanics by reinterpreting negative-energy solutions as physical antiparticles
Dirac:1928
. Here we propose an analogous organizational principle
within our operator framework
: negative-energy gravitational solutions are grouped into a conjugate sector related to the positive-energy sector by a symmetry that preserves the algebra

[\,\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}

and

[\,\hat{P}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}\hat{P}_{\rho}+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\hbar}{2}\,\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}\hat{J}_{ab}.

VII.1
Gravitational conjugation: definition and algebraic invariance

Let
\Theta
be an antiunitary operator (
\Theta\,\mathrm{i}\,\Theta^{-1}=-\mathrm{i}
). Define the
gravitational conjugation
map
\mathcal{C}_{g}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{g}:\;\hat{x}^{\mu}\mapsto\hat{x}^{\prime\mu}=\Theta\hat{x}^{\mu}\Theta^{-1},\quad\hat{P}_{\mu}\mapsto\hat{P}^{\prime}_{\mu}=-\,\Theta\hat{P}_{\mu}\Theta^{-1},\quad

(28)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\mapsto\hat{g}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\Theta\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{-1},\quad\hat{J}_{\mu\nu}\mapsto\hat{J}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\Theta\hat{J}_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{-1}.

Proposition (CCR invariance).

The canonical relations are preserved:

[\,\hat{x}^{\prime\mu},\hat{P}^{\prime}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{g}^{\prime\mu}{}_{\nu}.

(29)

Sketch.

[\,\Theta\hat{x}^{\mu}\Theta^{-1},-\,\Theta\hat{P}_{\nu}\Theta^{-1}\,]=-\,\Theta[\hat{x}^{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}]\Theta^{-1}=-\,\Theta(\mathrm{i}\hbar\hat{g}^{\mu}{}_{\nu})\Theta^{-1}=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\Theta\hat{g}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\Theta^{-1}.

Proposition ([P,P] invariance).

With

\hat{T}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}\;\equiv\;-\,\Theta\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}\Theta^{-1},\qquad\hat{R}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}\;\equiv\;\Theta\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}\Theta^{-1},

(30)

one has

[\,\hat{P}^{\prime}_{\mu},\hat{P}^{\prime}_{\nu}\,]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\hat{T}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}\,\hat{P}^{\prime}_{\rho}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\hbar}{2}\,\hat{R}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}\,\hat{J}^{\prime}_{\rho\sigma}.

(31)

Sketch.

[-\Theta\hat{P}_{\mu}\Theta^{-1},-\Theta\hat{P}_{\nu}\Theta^{-1}]=\Theta[\hat{P}_{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}]\Theta^{-1}
and rewrite the RHS in terms of primed tensors using (
30
) and (
VII.1
).

VII.2
Hilbert-space organization and superselection

A convenient way to encode the conjugate sector is the direct-sum structure (
5
).
Sector transitions are absent unless mediated by operators that are odd under
\mathcal{C}_{g}
; this provides a kinematical stability condition in a single-particle (non-field) setting. A full interacting quantum field theory implementation is beyond the scope of this paper.

VII.3
Relation to standard discrete symmetries

The map
\mathcal{C}_{g}
is not identified with charge conjugation, parity, or time reversal, nor with CPT. Operationally it is defined by its action on translation generators and geometric operators: it flips all
\hat{P}_{\mu}
while covariantly transforming
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
and the Lorentz sector in a way that preserves the commutator algebra. This is the only property needed in what follows.

VII.4
Concrete realization in FRW

For spatially flat
FRW
(Sec.
V
) with

[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]=2\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)\,H(t)\,\hat{P}_{i}
,
apply
\mathcal{C}_{g}
:

\hat{P}^{\prime}_{0}=-\Theta\hat{P}_{0}\Theta^{-1},\ \hat{P}^{\prime}_{i}=-\Theta\hat{P}_{i}\Theta^{-1}\Rightarrow[\,\hat{P}^{\prime}_{0},\hat{P}^{\prime}_{i}\,]=2\mathrm{i}\hbar\,N^{2}(t)\,H(t)\,\hat{P}^{\prime}_{i}
.
Thus the same Hubble-controlled non-commutativity appears in the conjugate sector; the sector exchange acts on energy-momentum expectation values, not on the algebra itself.

VIII
Uncertainty relations and a toy cosmological remark

For any pair of operators the Schrödinger–Robertson bound gives

\Delta x^{\mu}\,\Delta P_{\nu}\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,|\left\langle[\,\hat{x}^{\mu},\hat{P}_{\nu}\,]\right\rangle|\;=\;\tfrac{\hbar}{2}\,|\left\langle\hat{g}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\right\rangle|.

(32)

Two observations follow.

(i) Classical background.

If
\hat{g}\to g(t,\bm{x})
is classical, Eq. (
32
) becomes a
coordinate-dependent
statement. In
FRW
, e.g.
\Delta x^{i}\,\Delta P_{j}\geq(\hbar/2)\delta^{i}{}_{j}
. Thus, classical backgrounds alone do not alter physics.

(ii) Metric–operator fluctuations.

Novel effects arise when
\hat{g}
has quantum fluctuations and does not commute with
\hat{P}
. Writing
\hat{g}=\bar{g}+\delta\hat{g}
and expanding the Schrödinger–Robertson inequality yields

\Delta x^{\mu}\,\Delta P_{\nu}\;\gtrsim\;\tfrac{\hbar}{2}\Big(|\bar{g}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}|+\mathcal{O}\big(\left\langle(\delta\hat{g})^{2}\right\rangle^{1/2}\big)\Big),

(33)

so that metric fluctuations act as
multiplicative quantum noise
in the canonical normalization.

Toy cosmological proxy (deferred).

A minimal proxy is a small rescaling of primordial two-point amplitudes,

P(k)\to(1+\epsilon)\,P(k)
, which can be propagated to high-
z
halo abundances using standard Press–Schechter/Sheth–Tormen machinery. Since this step is
model dependent
and not derived here from a full field-theoretic embedding, we quarantine it in Appendix
D
and label it explicitly as a toy illustration.

IX
Discussion and outlook

We have presented a compact algebra where the metric is a commutator, time is an observable in a consistent POVM sense or (on an enlarged spectrum) as a self-adjoint operator, and the non-commutativity of translation generators in curved backgrounds follows from Jacobi identities. The
FRW
example shows explicitly that
[\,\hat{P}_{0},\hat{P}_{i}\,]\propto N^{2}H\,\hat{P}_{i}
, with a natural anholonomy/teleparallel interpretation for the scalar realization, and Sec.
VI
shows how the same algebra reduces smoothly in a weak-field (linearized) background.

Several natural directions follow:
(i) develop the field-theory generalization so that
(\phi,\pi)
inherit modified commutators from (
4b
); (ii) analyze semiclassical states where
\left\langle\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle
satisfies Einstein equations (or their deformations) while
\delta\hat{g}
seeds additional fluctuations; (iii) classify unitary representations of the enlarged algebra (including the local Lorentz sector) and connect to current algebra approaches
Johnson:2016smy
;
Johnson:2022lcj
; (iv) confront the framework with laboratory constraints (atomic clocks and interferometers) and cosmology (power spectra and high-
z
structure), with the toy Appendix
D
serving only as an illustrative propagation of a proxy parameter.

Appendix D

This appendix sketches a
toy

The key assumption is that metric-operator fluctuations act as an (approximately)

multiplicative rescaling of the primordial two-point amplitude:

P(k)\;\longrightarrow\;(1+\epsilon)\,P(k),\qquad\epsilon\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{g}^{2},

(35)

where
\sigma_{g}^{2}\equiv\langle(\delta\hat{g}/\bar{g})^{2}\rangle
(evaluated in the relevant era) parametrizes the variance of fractional metric-operator fluctuations.
See Sec.
VIII
for motivation; a fully consistent field-theory treatment is left for future work.

We use the standard linear form

P_{\rm lin}(k,z;\epsilon)\;=\;(1+\epsilon)\,D^{2}(z)\,P_{0}(k),\qquad P_{0}(k)=A_{s}\,k^{n_{s}}\,T^{2}(k),

(36)

with
D(z)
the growth factor in flat
\Lambda
CDM (e.g. the Carroll–Press–Turner approximation
CarrollPressTurner:1992
),
and a transfer function
T(k)
(e.g. Eisenstein–Hu
EisensteinHu:1998
).
The overall amplitude
A_{s}
is fixed by
\sigma_{8}
at
z=0
. Cosmological parameter values used for the numerical illustration may be taken from Planck 2018
Planck2018:VI
.

As usual, the comoving mass is
M=(4\pi/3)\,\bar{\rho}_{m0}\,R^{3}
with
\bar{\rho}_{m0}=\Omega_{m}\,\rho_{c0}
and
\rho_{c0}=3H_{0}^{2}/(8\pi G)

\sigma(M,z;\epsilon)\;=\;D(z)\,\sigma_{0}(M)\,\sqrt{1+\epsilon},

(37)

where
\sigma_{0}(M)\equiv\sigma(M,z{=}0;\epsilon{=}0)

ShethTormen:2002

\displaystyle\nu(M,z;\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{c}}{\sigma(M,z;\epsilon)},

M_{\min}

n(>M_{\min},z;\epsilon)=\int_{\ln M_{\min}}^{\ln M_{\max}}\frac{dn}{d\ln M}(M,z;\epsilon)\,d\ln M.
