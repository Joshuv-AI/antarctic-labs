Abstract
. This paper considers the combinatorics of continuous and Borel rectangular partitions of free actions of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} on 0 0 -dimensional Polish spaces, specifically the free part F ⁡ ( 2 ℤ n ) F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}) of the shift action of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} on the space 2 ℤ n 2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}} . This is done through the study of a corresponding notion of regulated partitions of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . The main concepts studied are the continuous and Borel regulation numbers of the partition. This is defined as the maximum number of rectangles in the corresponding regulated partition that can intersect in a point. The continuous and Borel regulation numbers γ c \gamma_{c} , γ B \gamma_{B} are the minimum possible values of these numbers as we range over continuous (respectively Borel) rectangular partitions of F ⁡ ( 2 ℤ n ) F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}) . It is shown that for n = 2 n=2 that γ c = γ B = 3 \gamma_{c}=\gamma_{B}=3 , and for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 that n + 2 ≤ γ B ≤ γ c ≤ 3 ⋅ 2 n − 2 n+2\leq\gamma_{B}\leq\gamma_{c}\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-2} . For n = 3 n=3 we improve this to γ c = γ B = 5 \gamma_{c}=\gamma_{B}=5 . This shows a striking difference between the Borel combinatorics of dimension n = 2 n=2 and dimensions n > 2 n>2 .



1.
Introduction

This paper can be regarded as a contribution to the study of Borel combinatorics of countable abelian group actions. The study of Borel combinatorics in general began with the seminal paper of Kechris, Solecki and Todorcevic
[
6
]
. Since its inception, the study has repeatedly encountered the phenomenon that there is often a difference between the answers to questions in Borel combinatorics and those in classical combinatorics with a countable domain. For instance, in the context of a marked group
G
(a group with a specific finite set of generators), combinatorial questions about the Cayley graph of
G
often have different answers than Borel combinatorial questions about the Schreier graph of a free Borel action of
G
on a Polish space
X
. In this paper we give one more instance of this kind of difference.

The specific combinatorial question we consider in this paper comes from another thread of research, namely the study of Borel marker constructions for countable Borel equivalence relations. This is a technique originally designed for hyperfiniteness proofs (see, e.g.
[
5
]
and
[
2
]
). More recently, it has played a crucial role in answering some Borel combinatorial questions about abelian group actions (see
[
4
]
).

More specifically, we consider the so-called
Borel rectangular partitions
of a Polish space
X
where the additive group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
freely acts on
X
in a Borel manner. These are Borel partitions
\mathcal{P}
of
X
where each set
P
in the partition
\mathcal{P}
is of the form
R\cdot x
for some
x\in X
and some
R
which is a rectangle in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Since the action is free, the Schreier graph on
X
, when restricted to each equivalence class of the action, is essentially a copy of the Cayley graph on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and a Borel rectangular partition
\mathcal{P}
on
X
, when restricted to each equivalence class, gives rise to a rectangular partition
\mathcal{Q}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

To study a rectangular partition
\mathcal{Q}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we replace each element of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by an
n
-dimensional cube of side length
1
centered about the element, and thus obtain a division of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
n
-dimensional rectangles with disjoint interiors, which we call
regulated partitions
. Technically, we require each region in a regulated partition to be finite (i.e., of finite side lengths). In dimension
2
, these have been called
rectangulations
in the literature and have been an object of research for combinatorial geometry (see, e.g.
[
8
]
and
[
7
]
). For regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we are particularly interested in counting how many different regions in the division can come together to a point (the
regulation number
of a point with respect to a regulated partition). We will show in Lemma
4.2
that for any regulated partition
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists some point whose regulation number with respect to
\mathcal{P}
is at least
n+1
. Thus, when every point has a regulation number at most
n+1
, we will call the regulated partition
\mathcal{P}

minimal
.

Minimal regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have a remarkable property (Theorem
3.10
) that around any point
x
there is a sufficiently small neighborhood
U
such that any subcollection of the induced regulated partition within
U
unions up to a set which is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional rectangle. This is a property that would simplify some Borel marker constructions (as in
[
4
]
) and yield marker sets with stronger topological regularity properties. In dimension
2
, minimal regulated partitions have been called
generic rectangulations
(see
[
8
]
), while the term of
minimal rectangulation
has been used (e.g. in
[
7
]
) to refer to a rectangulation of a finite polygonal region with a minimal number of rectangles.
The above topological regularity property for generic rectangulations in dimension
2

has been noted in
[
9
]
. The interest in minimal rectangulations was primarily motivated by the observation that finding minimal rectangulations can be performed in polynomial time (see e.g.
[
7
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
), which explains why the literature about them is mostly in computer science.

We thus call a rectangular partition on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

minimal
if its induced regulated partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is minimal. Moreover, we also refer to a rectangular partition on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a
regulated partition
. This terminology is similarly transferred to the context of rectangular partitions of a free Borel action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Our main result of this paper, which is somewhat surprising, is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any free Borel action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
on a Polish space
X
, there exists a Borel minimal regulated partition of
X
. In contrast, for any
n\geq 3
, and for any free Borel action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on a Polish space
X
, there does not exist a Borel, nondegenerate, minimal regulated partition of
X
.

Here a regulated partition is
nondegenerate
if it does not consist of rectangular regions of lower dimensions all of which are perpendicular to a single standard unit vector.

In view of Theorem
1.1
, it is natural to introduce the Borel and continuous

regulation numbers

\gamma_{B}(n)
,
\gamma_{c}(n)
for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For a given Borel or continuous
rectangular partition
\mathcal{P}
of the free part of the shift action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on
X=2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
, for each
x\in X

the partiton
\mathcal{P}
induces a partition
\mathcal{P}_{x}
of the equivalence class
[x]

which can be viewed as a partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. This also gives a
corresponding regulated partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{x}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as mentioned above. Let
\gamma(\mathcal{P})
be the least

k
so that for all
x\in X
, every point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
lies in at most
k
of the rectangles of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{x}
.
Then
\gamma_{B}(n)
is the minimum value of
\gamma(\mathcal{P})
amongst Borel nondegenerate
rectangular partitions of
X
. Likewise,
\gamma_{c}(n)
is defined in the same way but using
continuous partitions
\mathcal{P}
of the shift space. We clearly have
\gamma_{B}(n)\leq\gamma_{c}(n)

for all
n
. For
n=2
we actually show that
\gamma_{B}(2)=\gamma_{c}(2)=3
, and for
n>2
Theorem
1.1

says
\gamma_{c}(n)\geq\gamma_{B}(n)>n+1
. We will show an upper-bound for
\gamma_{c}(n)
which with
Theorem
1.1
gives the following results about the continuous and Borel regulation numbers.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n=2
we have
\gamma_{B}(2)=\gamma_{c}(2)=3
. For
n>2
we have

n+2\leq\gamma_{B}(n)\leq\gamma_{c}(n)\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-2}
.
For
n=3
we have the improvement
\gamma_{B}(3)=\gamma_{c}(3)=5
.

We do not know if there are any values of
n
for which
\gamma_{B}(n)\neq\gamma_{c}(n)
. We also do not know
the asymptotic growth rates of
\gamma_{B}(n)
and
\gamma_{c}(n)
, in particular whether they
grow linearly or exponentially.

Our proof of the main theorem uses forcing. Forcing is known to be a useful tool for proving negative results about Borel combinatorics (see
[
3
]
). In fact we give two forcing proofs of the main theorem. In the first, we prove the theorem in dimension
3
and then lift up the proof to arbitrary dimension
n\geq 3
. In the second, we give a different forcing construction by working directly in dimension
n\geq 3
. However, both these proofs are based on some observations of minimal regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, we prove the following finitary version of the main theorem for minimal locally regulated partition of a rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any rectangle
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a finite collection
\mathcal{S}
of pairwise disjoint subrectangles of
R
, there is a finite minimal locally regulated partition
\mathcal{P}
of
R
with
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
. In contrast, for any
n\geq 3
and for any
n
-dimensional rectangle
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a finite collection
\mathcal{S}
of pairwise disjoint
n
-dimensional subrectangles of
R
such that there does not exist any finite minimal locally regulated partition
\mathcal{P}
of
R
with
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we define the basic notions and prove a general property of all regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which allows us to define minimality for regulated partitions and locally regulated partitions. In Section
3
we give a canonical description of minimal locally regulated partitions (Theorem
3.9
) and use it to prove the topological regularity property of minimal locally regulated partitions (Theorem
3.10
). In Section
4
we define and characterize minimal regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and show their existence. In Section
5
we analyze minimal (locally) regulated partitions and prove the second part of Theorem
1.3
. In Section
6
we show that for any
n\geq 2
, considering the continuous action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on the free part of the Bernoulli shift space
2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
, there is a clopen regulated partition where the regulation number of all points are bounded by
3\cdot 2^{n-2}
(Theorem
6.9
). In Section
7
we improve this bound to
5
for dimension
3
. Section
8
is the main section in which we prove the second part of Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section
9
we make some more observations about minimal regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in general, and sketch an alternative forcing proof of the main result.

2.
Regulated Partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Throught the paper we fix
n\geq 1
, let
\vec{0}
be the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
denote the standard unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

e_{i}=(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0),

where the only
1
appears in the
i
-th coordinate. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\pi_{i}(x)=\pi_{e_{i}}(x)=\langle e_{i},x\rangle
be the
i
-th coordinate of
x
. For any subset
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
V_{A}=\{e_{i}\colon i\in A\}
and let

\pi_{A}=\pi_{V_{A}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{A}\cong\mathbb{R}^{|A|}

be the projection map given by

\pi_{A}(x)=\pi_{V_{A}}(x)=(\pi_{i}(x))_{i\in A}=(\pi_{v}(x))_{v\in V_{A}}.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, a
k
-dimensional rectangle
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of the form

R=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n},

where

(i)

each
I_{i}
is of the form
[a_{i},b_{i}]
for real numbers
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
;

(ii)

for exactly
k
many
i
,
I_{i}
is not a singleton.

Thus in the degenerate case, a
0
-dimensional rectangle is just a point.

If
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote its interior as
\Int(R)
. Thus for

R=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{n},b_{n}],

where
a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
i
,

\Int(R)=(a_{1},b_{1})\times\cdots\times(a_{n},b_{n}).

We also let
\partial R
denote the boundary of
R
, i.e.,
\partial R=R\setminus\Int(R)
.
\partial R
can be written in a unique way as a union of
2n
many distinct
(n-1)
-dimensional rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which we call
faces
of
R
. For each face
F
of
R
, the
normal vector
of
F
is the unique unit vector
e_{i}
perpendicular to all vectors in
F
; the
direction
of
F
is the unique vector of the form
+e_{i}
or
-e_{i}
, whichever is pointing to the interior of
R
from
F
, where
e_{i}
is the normal vector of
F
. If two distinct faces of
R
have the same normal vector, then they are parallel and they have the opposite directions; otherwise they are perpendicular. Any set of pairwise perpendicular faces of
R
has a nonempty intersection. If
\mathcal{F}
is a nonempty set of pairwise perpendicular faces of
R
and
|\mathcal{F}|=k
, then
1\leq k\leq n
and the intersection
\bigcap\mathcal{F}
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
x\in R
, we define the
boundary rank
of
x
with respect to
R
, denoted
\beta_{R}(x)
, to be the largest
0\leq k\leq n
such that there is a set
\mathcal{F}
of pairwise perpendicular faces of
R
with
|\mathcal{F}|=k
and
x\in\bigcap\mathcal{F}
. Thus
\beta_{R}(x)=0
iff
x\in\Int(R)
, and if
x\in\partial R
, then
1\leq\beta_{R}(x)\leq n
. For
0\leq m\leq n-1
, the
m
-boundary
of
R
is the set of all
x\in R
with
\beta_{R}(x)=n-m
. These geometric concepts can be easily described algebraically as follows. If

R=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{n},b_{n}]

where
a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A face of
R
is of the form

\{x\in R\colon\pi_{i}(x)=a_{i}\}\mbox{ or }\{x\in R\colon\pi_{i}(x)=b_{i}\}

for some
1\leq i\leq n
; the direction of the former face is
+e_{i}
and that of the latter face is
-e_{i}
. For each
x\in R
, define the
boundary incidence sequence
of
x
with respect to
R
to be

B_{R}(x)=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})

where for each
1\leq i\leq n
,

\beta_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+1,&\mbox{ if $\pi_{i}(x)=a_{i}$,}\\
-1,&\mbox{ if $\pi_{i}(x)=b_{i}$,}\\
0,&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
\beta_{R}(x)
is the number of nonzero entries of
B_{R}(x)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. A
regulated cover
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set
\mathcal{P}
of
n
-dimensional rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

there is a discrete subset
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a constant
d>0
(called a
discreteness constant
for
\mathcal{P}
) such that

•

for any distinct
r_{1},r_{2}\in D
,
|r_{1}-r_{2}|>d
, and

•

each
R\in\mathcal{P}
is of the form

[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{n},b_{n}]

where
a_{i},b_{i}\in D
and
a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is
R\in\mathcal{P}
such that
x\in R
.

A regulated cover
\mathcal{P}
is a
regulated partition
if it satisfies additionally

(3)

if
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
are distinct then
\Int(R_{1})\cap\Int(R_{2})=\varnothing
.

A collection which satisfies (2) and (3) is called a
rectangulation
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Clause (3) in the definition of regulated partition is equivalent to

(3’)

if
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
then either
R_{1}\cap R_{2}=\varnothing
or
R_{1}\cap R_{2}
is a
k
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
k<n
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
locally regulated cover
of
R
is a finite set
\mathcal{P}
of
n
-dimensional rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{P}=R
. A locally regulated cover
\mathcal{P}
of
R
is a
locally regulated partition
of
R
if for any distinct
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
,
\Int(R_{1})\cap\Int(R_{2})=\varnothing
.

The following lemma is immediate from the definitions.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is a regulated cover (partition) of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle, then

\mathcal{Q}=\{P\cap R\colon P\in\mathcal{P}\mbox{ and $P\cap R$ is an $n$-dimensional rectangle}\}

is a locally regulated cover (partition) of
R
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
x\in\Int(R)
.
A locally regulated cover (partition)
\mathcal{P}
of
R
is said to be
about

if for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
and for all faces

of
P
, either
x\in F
or

.

It is easy to see that if
\mathcal{P}
is a locally regulated cover of
R
about
x
then
x\in\bigcap\mathcal{P}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}
is a locally regulated partition of
R
, then it is about

x\in\bigcap\mathcal{P}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, and
\mathcal{P}
is a locally regulated cover (partition) of

, then there is an
n
-dimensional rectangle

such that

.

Proof.

Note that

is also a neighborhood of

; otherwise we can let

.
Then we can let

be an
n
-dimensional rectangle such that

x\in\Int(R_{x})\subseteq R_{x}\subseteq U\cap V.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x\in\Int(R)
, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. For any nonempty set
V
of boundary vectors of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{V}
be the set of all
P\in\mathcal{P}
such that some
v\in V
is a boundary vector of
x
with respect to
P
. Define
\mathcal{P}_{\varnothing}=\mathcal{P}
. Then
|\mathcal{P}_{V}|\geq|V|+1
.

Proof.

By induction on
|V|
. If
|V|=1
then the theorem holds by Lemma
2.13
(ii). Suppose
|V|>1
. Without loss of generality assume
e_{1}\in V
. Then
(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})_{e_{1}}
is nonempty, and disjoint from
\mathcal{P}_{1,+}
. Let
A=\{2,\dots,n\}
. Then by Lemma
2.12
(i),
\mathcal{Q}=\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})
are both locally regulated partitions of
\pi_{A}(R)
about
\pi_{A}(x)
. Let
V^{\prime}=V\setminus\{e_{1}\}
. Then
V^{\prime}
is nonempty. Let
U\subseteq V^{\prime}
be the set of all boundary vectors
v\in V^{\prime}
of
\pi_{A}(x)
with respect to
\mathcal{Q}
. Then by the inductive hypothesis,
|\mathcal{Q}_{U}|\geq|U|+1
. It follows that
|(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})_{U}|=|\mathcal{Q}_{U}|\geq|U|+1
. Let
W=V^{\prime}\setminus U
. If
W=\varnothing
, then

|\mathcal{P}_{V}|\geq|(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})_{U}|+|(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})_{e_{1}}|\geq|\mathcal{Q}_{U}|+1\geq|V|+1.

Assume
W\neq\varnothing
. Then every
v\in W
is a boundary vector of
\pi_{A}(x)
with respect to
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
. Hence by the inductive hypothesis,
|\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{W}|\geq|W|+1
. This implies that
|\mathcal{P}_{W}|\geq|\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{W}|\geq|W|+1
. Since every
v\in W
is not a boundary vector of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{1,+}
, we have that
\mathcal{P}_{W}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{1,+}
. Thus

|\mathcal{P}_{V}|\geq|\mathcal{P}_{W}|+|(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})_{U}|\geq|W|+1+|U|+1=|V|+1,

as desired.
∎

3.
Minimal Locally Regulated Partitions

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, and
\mathcal{P}
be a locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
minimal
if
\nu_{\mathcal{P}}(x)=\beta_{\mathcal{P}}(x)+1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, and
\mathcal{P}
be a locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. Let
1\leq j\leq n
and
A=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j\}
. Assume
\mathcal{P}
is minimal. Then both
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{j,+})
and
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{j,-})
are minimal locally regulated partitions of
\pi_{A}(R)
about
\pi_{A}(x)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume
j=1
. Then
A=\{2,\dots,n\}
. We show that both
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})
and
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})
are minimal locally regulated partitions of
\pi_{A}(R)
about
\pi_{A}(x)
. Let
M=(a_{i,j})_{m\times n}
be a principal matrix of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
. The lemma is trivial if the first column of
M
is all-zero, or equivalently,
e_{1}
is not a boundary vector of
x
. So we assume the first column of
M
is nonzero. Let
\{A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\}
be a partition of
A
defined as follows.

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{0}=\{1<j\leq n\colon\mbox{ the $j$-th column of $M$ is all-zero}\},\\
A_{1}=\{1<j\leq n\colon j\notin A_{0}\mbox{ and the $j$-th column of $M_{1,+}$ is all-zero}\},\\
A_{2}=\{1<j\leq n\colon j\notin A_{0}\mbox{ and the $j$-th column of $M_{1,-}$ is all-zero}\},\\
A_{3}=A\setminus(A_{0}\cup A_{1}\cup A_{2}).\end{array}

Note that
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\varnothing
.

Let

\begin{array}[]{l}C_{+}=\{1\leq i\leq m\colon a_{i,j}=+1\},\\
C_{-}=\{1\leq i\leq m\colon a_{i,j}=-1\},\\
C_{0}=\{1\leq i\leq m\colon a_{i,j}=0\}.\end{array}

Then
\{C_{+},C_{-},C_{0}\}
is a partition of
\{1,\dots,m\}
. The submatrix of
M
corresponding to rows indexed by
C_{-}
and columns indexed by
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
is the matrix
N
considered in the proof of Theorem
2.18
, and by that proof, we have

|C_{-}|\geq|A_{1}|+1.

The submatrix of
M
corresponding to rows indexed by
C_{+}\cup C_{0}
and columns indexed by
A_{2}\cup A_{3}
is the matrix
M_{1,+}^{*}
considered in the proof of Theorem
2.18
, and by that proof, we have

|C_{+}|+|C_{0}|=|C_{+}\cup C_{0}|\geq|A_{2}\cup A_{3}|+1=|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|+1.

By symmetry, we also have

|C_{+}|\geq|A_{2}|+1.

It follows that

\nu_{\mathcal{P}}(x)=|C_{+}\cup C_{0}\cup C_{-}|\geq|C_{+}\cup C_{0}|+|C_{-}|\geq|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|+1+|A_{1}|+1=\beta_{\mathcal{P}}(x)+1.

By minimality, we have that the equality holds, and it follows that
|C_{-}|=|A_{1}|+1
and
|C_{+}|+|C_{0}|=|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|+1
. By symmetry, we also have
|C_{+}|=|A_{2}|+1
. It follows that
|C_{0}|=|A_{3}|
.

Now it is easy to see that
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})
is a locally regulated partition of
\pi_{A}(R)
about
\pi_{A}(x)
. Thus

\nu_{\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})}(\pi_{A}(x))=|\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})|=|C_{+}\cup C_{0}|=|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|+1=\beta_{\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})}(x)+1,

and hence
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})
is minimal. Similarly,
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})
is also a minimal locally regulated partition of
\pi_{A}(R)
about
\pi_{A}(x)
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, and
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
be locally regulated partitions of

about

. We say that
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are

if the set of all boundary vectors of

with respect to
\mathcal{P}
is disjoint from the set of all boundary vectors of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{Q}
.

The following is a corollary of the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, and
\mathcal{P}
be a minimal locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. Let
1\leq j\leq n
and
A=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j\}
. Suppose for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
\pi_{j}(B_{P}(x))\neq 0
. Then
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{j,+})
and
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{j,-})
are orthogonal.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume
j=1
. In the notation of the proof of Theorem
3.2
, our assumption is
C_{0}=\varnothing
. Thus
|A_{3}|=|C_{0}|=0
. It follows that
M_{1,+}^{*}
is contained exactly in columns of
M
indexed by
A_{2}
, and
M_{1,-}^{*}
(consist of nonzero columns of
M_{1,-}
) is contained exactly in columns of

indexed by

\{e_{i}\colon i\in A_{2}\}
is the set of all boundary vectors of
\pi_{A}(x)
with respect to
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,+})
, and
\{e_{i}\colon i\in A_{1}\}
is the set of all boundary vectors of
\pi_{A}(x)
with respect to
\pi_{A}(\mathcal{P}_{1,-})

In the rest of this section we give a complete description of all minimal locally regulated partitions about a point.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathcal{P}
be a locally regulated partition of

about

be a boundary vector of

with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, and let

and

an

-pair

\pi_{j^{\prime}}(B_{P}(x))=\pi_{j^{\prime}}(B_{Q}(x))
for all

and
\pi_{j}(B_{P}(x))\pi_{j}(B_{Q}(x))=-1
.

Equivalently, if

is a principal matrix of

with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, then
(P_{i},P_{i^{\prime}})
is an

-pair iff the

-th and
i^{\prime}
-th rows of

are identical except

and

take opposite nonzero values. In this case we also say that
(i,i^{\prime})
is an

-pair in

.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, let
\mathcal{P}
be a locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
, let
e_{j}
be a boundary vector of

with respect to

, and let

be an

-pair. Then

\{P\cup Q\}\cup\mathcal{P}\setminus\{P,Q\}

is still a locally regulated partition of

about

.

Only note that if

form an

-pair, then

is a rectangle.

Example 3.7
.

The following example of a locally regulated partition
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
does not contain an
e_{j}
-pair for any
1\leq j\leq 3
. First partition
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into eight octants, and denote them as octants I through VIII in the usual order. Then

\mathcal{P}=\{\mbox{I}+\mbox{IV},\mbox{II}+\mbox{VI},\mbox{III},\mbox{V},\mbox{VII}+\mbox{VIII}\}

is such a partition. Its principal matrix is

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}+1&0&+1\\
-1&+1&0\\
-1&-1&+1\\
+1&+1&-1\\
0&-1&-1\end{array}\right].

In this matrix every pair of distinct rows differ in at least two entries.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
x\in\Int(R)
, let
\mathcal{P}
be a minimal locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. Then there exist a
1\leq j\leq n
and an
e_{j}
-pair
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
|\mathcal{P}|
. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
enumerate
\mathcal{P}
. Let
M=(a_{i,j})_{m\times n}
be a principal matrix of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
. Consider
j=1
and
A=\{2,\dots,n\}
. Using the notation in the proof of Theorem
3.2
,
A=A_{0}\cup A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}
. Consider the rows of
M
correspondent to
\mathcal{P}_{1,+}
. These are the rows indexed by
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
where
a_{i,1}=0
or
+1
. In the notation of the proof of Theorem
3.2
, the indices of these rows are from
C_{+}\cup C_{0}
.

In the rest of the proof we use induction on
|C_{0}|=|\{P\in\mathcal{P}\colon\pi_{1}(B_{P}(x))=0\}|
.

First consider the case
C_{0}=\varnothing
. Then by Theorem
3.2
,

In the following we describe a canonical way to construct minimal locally regulated partitions about a point. We first define a notion of a labelled simple tree as follows. Let

0

k
, and at each level

, at most one node on level
\ell
has two children. A simple tree is
full
if at each level above the terminal level, exactly one node on that level has two children. Note that in a full simple tree
T
of more than one level the root always has two children.

We assign labels
+1,-1
and
0
to the nodes of a simple tree as follows. If a node does not have a sibling, then it is labelled
0

+1
for one and
-1
for the other. If
T
is a labelled simple tree and

, then define a labelled simple tree

to be the substree of
T
with
t
as the root and containing all descendants of
t
in
T
as nodes; the label of
t
in
T_{t}
is (re)assigned as
0
(if necessary), and the labels of all other nodes are the same as their labels in
T
. Given a simple tree
T
of
k+1
levels, let
T^{\prime}
be the full simple tree obtained from
T
by removing all levels
\ell>0
of

on which every node has no siblings; we call

the

of

. Let

in the construction of

from

∎

Corollary 5.10

For each

By induction on

\mathcal{P}_{n}

Lemma 6.5

Proof.

Next we consider continuous and Borel constructions in the space

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on

of

Suppose

for some

is an
n
-dimensional rectangle
if

is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Note that this does not depend on the choice of

.

Definition 6.6

A

of

Theorem 6.7

(
[

)
.

For any integers

of

such that each

is an
n

or

.

Definition 6.8
.

Let

The main result of this section is the following theorem on clopen regulated partitions.

Theorem 6.9
.

For each
n\geq 2
there is a clopen
3\cdot 2^{n-2}
-regulated partition of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
.

We note that when
n=1
there is obviously a clopen
2
-regulated partition of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}})
; such a regulated partition is minimal. Among all the dimensions considered in this theorem, only in the case
n=2
is the regulated partition obtained minimal. In the rest of this paper we will show that there do not exist Borel minimal regulated partitions of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
for any

. The rest of this section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
6.9
.

Throughout the rest of this section fix
n\geq 2
. Let
\rho
be the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any real number

\rho
-closed ball of radius

centered at

, is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with side length

. The distance function
\rho
can be extended to
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
: for
u,v\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
, define

\rho(u,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(g,\vec{0}),&\mbox{ if $g\cdot u=v$,}\\
\infty,&\mbox{ if $(u,v)\not\in E_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}$.}\end{array}\right.

We will use the following lemmas proved in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 6.10

(Basic clopen marker lemma,
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
r>0
be any real number. Then there is a relatively clopen set
S\subseteq F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
such that

(i)

if
u,v\in S
are distinct, then
\rho(u,v)>r
;

(ii)

for any
u\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
, there is
v\in S
such that
\rho(u,v)\leq r
.

Lemma 6.11

(
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
r_{2}>2r_{1}>0
be real numbers, and let
S_{1}
be a relatively clopen subset of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
as in Lemma
6.10
for
r_{1}
. Then there is a clopen set
S_{2}\subseteq S_{1}
such that

(i)

if
u,v\in S_{2}
are distinct, then
\rho(u,v)>r_{2}-2r_{1}
;

(ii)

for any
u\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
, there is
v\in S_{2}
such that
\rho(u,v)\leq r_{1}+r_{2}
.

Toward proving Theorem
6.9
, let
r_{2}\gg r_{1}\gg\ell\gg 0
and let
S_{2}\subseteq S_{1}
be clopen subsets of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
given by Lemmas
6.10
and
6.11
for
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
enumerate all nonidentity elements of the set

D=\{g\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon\rho(g,\vec{0})\leq r_{1}+r_{2}\}.

Inductively define a sequence
T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
of subsets of
S_{1}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}T_{0}=S_{2},\\
T_{i}=S_{1}\cap(g_{i}\cdot S_{2})\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}T_{j},\mbox{ for $1\leq i\leq k$.}\end{array}

Then
T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
form a partition of
S_{1}
by clopen sets. By Lemma
6.11
(i), for any
0\leq i\leq k
and any distinct
u,v\in T_{i}
,
\rho(u,v)>r_{2}-2r_{1}
.

The rest of our construction will be invariant. This means that, one can view the construction as if we have chosen a base point
z
in each
E_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
-class, but the result of the construction will not depend on the choice of
z
. Moreover, we will describe a construction of a regulated partition
\mathcal{P}_{z}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for each orbit
[z]
. The desired regulated partition of

consists of
(P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\cdot z

.

To each
z\in T_{i}
we associate an
n
-dimensional rectangle
R_{z}
by induction on
0\leq i\leq k
. Without loss of generality assume
r_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
and
\ell>0
is an integer. If
i=0
we let
R_{z}
be the
n
-dimensional rectangle
B_{\rho}(z,r_{1})
. Note that if
u,v\in T_{0}
are distinct then
R_{u}\cap R_{v}=\varnothing
since
\rho(u,v)>r_{2}-2r_{1}\gg 2r_{1}
if we ensure
r_{2}\gg r_{1}
appropriately. Suppose
0<i\leq k
and assume
R_{u}
has been defined for all
u\in\bigcup_{j<i}T_{j}
. Let
z\in T_{i}
. We first let
R^{*}_{z}
be the
n
-dimensional rectangle
B_{\rho}(z,r_{1})
. Then for each face
F^{*}
of
R^{*}_{z}
, if there is a
u\in\bigcup_{j<i}T_{j}
such that
\rho(u,z)\leq 3r_{1}
and there is a face
F
of
R_{u}
such that the perpendicular distance of
F^{*}
and

is less than
\ell
, then adjust
F^{*}
by moving it outward by no more than
\frac{1}{10}r_{1}
so that the resulting face has level in
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
and has perpendicular distance at least
\ell
from all parallel faces of
R_{u}
for
u\in\bigcup_{j<i}T_{j}
with
\rho(u,z)\leq 6r_{1}
. Note that there is a constant upper bound (depending on
n
but nothing else) on the number of

with
\rho(u,z)\leq 6r_{1}
. Hence if

have been appropriately chosen, such an adjustment of

can be found. The
n
-dimensional rectangle

is the result after such adjustments for all faces of

. Note that if
u,v\in T_{i}
are distinct then

, and thus the constructions of

and

and

.

By Lemma
6.10
(ii), on each orbit
\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq k}\{R_{u}\colon u\in T_{i}\}
is a regulated cover of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Next, for each
z\in T_{i}
we define a locally regulated partition of

H_{z}=R_{z}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}\{R_{u}\colon u\in T_{j}\}

using the following recursive division algorithm. Note that
H_{z}
is an
n
-dimensional rectangular polyhedron
, i.e., it is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional rectangle and is the union of finitely many
n
-dimensional rectangles. Moreover, any two distinct parallel faces of
H_{z}
have perpendicular distance at least
\ell
. The algorithm is as follows. Take all faces of
H_{z}
with normal vector
e_{n}
, extend them into hyperplanes, and use these hyperplanes to divide
H_{z}
into finitely many sets of the form
L\times[a,b]
, where
L
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional rectangular polyhedron and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
. Note here that
L
satisfies the similar property that any two distinct parallel faces of
L
have perpendicular distance at least
\ell
. Now apply this algorithm recursively to each

to obtain a regulated partition
\mathcal{P}_{L}
of
L
. Then sets of the form
P\times[a,b]
for

give a locally regulated partition
\mathcal{P}_{z}
of
H_{z}
.

When we have completely defined the locally regulated partitions
\mathcal{P}_{z}
of
H_{z}
for all

and

, we have obtained a regulated partition
\mathcal{P}_{[z]}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for each orbit. Since this construction is finitary, the resulting regulated partition on

is clopen.

It remains to verify that on each orbit we have obtained a
3\cdot 2^{n-2}
-regulated partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For this we consider a regulated partition
\mathcal{P}_{[z]}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for an arbitrary orbit
[z]
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\gamma_{n}=3\cdot 2^{n-2}
. Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

such that
\mathcal{P}=\{P\cap R\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{[z]}\}
is a locally regulated partition of
R
about
x
. We note that if
x
has no boundary vectors with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, then
|\mathcal{P}|=\nu_{\mathcal{P}}(x)=1\leq\gamma_{n}
and there is nothing more to prove. Thus we may assume without loss of generality that
x
has at least one boundary vector with respect to
\mathcal{P}
. Also note that if there is
u\in S_{1}
such that

, then by our construction,
e_{1}
is not a boundary vector of

; in this case
|\mathcal{P}|\leq 2^{n-1}\leq\gamma_{n}

x\not\in\Int(H_{u})
for any
u\in S_{1}
. Let
i_{x}
be the largest

with
0\leq i\leq k
and
x\in\partial H_{z}
for some
z\in T_{i}
. The

witnessing

is unique; we fix it in the rest of the proof.

If
e_{j}
is a boundary vector of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, we call
e_{j}

active
for
x
if
e_{j}
is the normal vector of a face
F
of
\partial H_{z}
with

, and call
e_{j}

passive
for
x
otherwise. In particular, if
x
is on a hyperplane extending a face of
H_{z}
, then the normal vector of the face is active for
x
iff
x
is on that face of
H_{z}
. We claim that this statement still holds for all
u\in S_{1}
with
x\in\partial H_{u}
. To see this, suppose
u\in S_{1}
is such that
x\in\partial H_{u}
. Then
u\in T_{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq i_{x}
. Now suppose
x
is on a hyperplane extending a face
F
of
H_{u}
. Suppose first
x\in F
. Then there is a face
F^{\prime}
of
H_{z}
such that
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
and
x\in F^{\prime}
. Hence the normal vector of
F
is active for
x
. Suppose next
x\not\in F
. Then by the orthogonality, there is no face
F^{\prime}
of
H_{z}
with
x\in F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
lying on the same hyperplane as the one extending

. Hence the normal vector of

is passive for
x
.

We note that, by our construction,
e_{1}
is not passive for
x
, since no faces of
R_{u}
with normal vector
e_{1}
are ever extended in the construction. Since
x\in\partial H_{z}
, there exists at least one active boundary vector for
x
. Let
\alpha(x)
denote the number of active boundary vectors of
x
and
\pi(x)
denote the number of passive boundary vectors of
x
. We have
\alpha(x)\geq 1
and
\alpha(x)+\pi(x)\leq n
. Note that the number of regions in
\mathcal{P}
that are defined by the active boundary vectors (namely the hyperplanes with the active boundary vectors as normal vectors) is at most
\alpha(x)+1
. This is because, if
e_{j}
and
e_{l}
are both active vectors and
1\leq j<l\leq n
, the hyperplane with
e_{j}
as the normal vector does not cross the hyperplane with
e_{l}
as the normal vector, according to our construction. Thus the number of elements in
\mathcal{P}
is at most
(\alpha(x)+1)\cdot 2^{\pi(x)}
. If
\alpha(x)\geq 2
or
\alpha(x)+\pi(x)<n
, then we have that

|\mathcal{P}|\leq(\alpha(x)+1)\cdot 2^{\pi(x)}\leq(\alpha(x)+1)\cdot 2^{n-\alpha(x)}\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-2}=\gamma_{n}.

It remains to consider the case
\alpha(x)=1
and
\pi(x)=n-1
. Since
e_{1}
is not passive for
x
, in this case the only active boundary vector of

is
e_{1}
and every other
e_{j}
,
1<j\leq n
, is a passive boundary vector of
x
. Under our assumption, for each
1\leq j\leq n
, there exists a face
F_{j}
of some
P_{j}\in\mathcal{P}
such that
x\in F_{j}
and
e_{j}
is a normal vector of
F_{j}
. Let
N_{j}
be the hyperplane containing
F_{j}
.

For distinct
1\leq j,l\leq n
, we say that
e_{j}

crosses

e_{l}
if on each of the two sides of
N_{l}
there are faces
F_{j}^{*}
of some
P_{j}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}
with normal vector
e_{j}
. Note that, under our assumption,

crosses

for all

.

Lemma 6.12
.

Under our assumption that
\alpha(x)=1
and
\pi(x)=n-1

does not cross

.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}=z,u_{2},\dots,u_{n}
be elements of
\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq i_{x}}T_{i}
such that

(*)

for each

extends a face

of
R_{u_{j}}
,
x\in N_{j}
and
x\not\in R_{u_{j}}
.

Intuitively,

lies in the interior of a face of
R_{u_{1}}
with normal vector
e_{1}
, and for each

,

is also on the extension of a face of

since

is a passive boundary vector of
x
. By the orthogonality of the construction,
u_{j}
is unique for each

.

Assume toward a contradiction that each
e_{j}
crosses
e_{l}
for all distinct

. Then we claim that for distinct

,
u_{j}\neq u_{l}
. First it is clear that

Before continuing the proof we adopt a coordinate system to address the elements of

Since for each

Suppose

8.
Borel Regulated Partitions of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})

Recall from Definition
4.1
the notion of a
\gamma
-regulated
partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and its extension in Definition
6.3
to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and in Definition
6.6
to
the equivalence relation
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. Recall also Definitions
6.1
and
6.2

which allow us to identify rectangular partitions of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with regulated partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The notion of a
\gamma
-regulated partition of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
in Definition
6.6
further
generalizes in the obvious way to free actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on
0
-dimensional Polish spaces.
In the following we introduce a notion of nondegeneration for regulated partitions of such actions.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
E
be the orbit equivalence relation of a free action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on a
0
-dimensional Polish space
X
.

(i)

A
regulated partition
of
E
is a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
X
such that each element of
\mathcal{P}
is of the form
R\cdot x
, where
R
is an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

(ii)

A regulated partition
\mathcal{P}
is
nondegenerate
if for every
x\in X
and every
1\leq i\leq n
, there is
y\in[x]
such that
e_{i}\cdot y
and
y
belong to the same element of
\mathcal{P}
.

In practice, we always work with regulated partitions where the rectangles in the partition have positive side lengths. Such regulated partitions are all nondegenerate.

For nondegenerate regulated partitions we can define the continuous and the Borel regulation numbers
of the action.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
E
be the orbit equivalence relation of a continuous, free action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on a
0
-dimensional
Polish space. The
continuous regulation number

\gamma_{c}(E)
of
E
is the least
\gamma

such that there is a clopen nondegenerate
\gamma
-regulated partition of
E
. Similarly, the
Borel
regulation number

\gamma_{B}(E)
of
E
is the least
\gamma
such that there is a Borel
nondegenerate
\gamma
-regulated partition of
E
.

Our main interest is in the spaces
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
themselves, but the arguments apply
to more general case of
0
-dimensional Polish spaces on which there is a free continuous action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The main question
concerning regulated partitions is the following.

Question 8.3
.

What are the values of
\gamma_{c}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
and
\gamma_{B}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))
?

For notational convenience we let
\gamma_{c}(n)
abbreviate
\gamma_{c}(F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}))

and likewise for
\gamma_{B}(n)
.
In view of our results proved in the earlier part of the paper, in particular Lemma
4.2
and Theorems
6.9
and
7.1
,
we have

\gamma_{c}(1)=\gamma_{B}(1)=2,

\gamma_{c}(2)=\gamma_{B}(2)=3,

4\leq\gamma_{B}(3)\leq\gamma_{c}(3)\leq 5,

and for all
n>3
,

n+1\leq\gamma_{B}(n)\leq\gamma_{c}(n)\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-2}.

Because of this last result, the
n+1
-regulated partitions of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-actions are called
minimal
regulated partitions.

Our goal in this section is to improve these estimates about
\gamma_{c}(n)
and
\gamma_{B}(n)
for
n\geq 3
.
The next theorem is our main theorem of this section.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer. Let
E
be the orbit equivalence relation induced by a free Borel action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

on a
0
-dimensional Polish space. Then there does not exist a Borel, nondegenerate,
minimal regulated partition of
E
.

This theorem shows a stark contrast between the two-dimensional case and the cases of higher dimensions.
Recall that for dimensions
n\leq 2
, there exist clopen nondegenerate minimal regulated
partitions of
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. However, for dimensions
n\geq 3
, not only are there no
clopen nondengerate minimal regulated partitions for
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
, but there are not even any Borel ones.
That is, for
n>2
we have
\gamma_{c}(n)\geq\gamma_{B}(n)>n+1
.
Combining this with Theorem
6.9
, we have the following result.

Theorem 8.5
.

For any
n\geq 3
we have

n+2\leq\gamma_{B}(n)\leq\gamma_{c}(n)\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-2}.

For
n=3
, we obtain the exact values of
\gamma_{c}(3)
and
\gamma_{B}(3)
as follows.

Corollary 8.6
.

\gamma_{B}(3)=\gamma_{c}(3)=5
.

In the rest of this section we prove Theorem
8.4
.
In the argument to follow, we will consider the case
X=F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
and
E
the equivalence relation
generated by the shift action of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on
F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
. The more general case stated in Theorem
8.4

does not differ in any significant way. So, our goal is to show
\gamma_{B}(n)\geq n+2
.

We will give the proof of Theorem
8.4
for the case

in Subsection
8.2

and then for the general case in Subsection

Before giving these proofs, we need to clarify the notation we use.

is minimal.

Now we focus on the class

for dimensions

Definition

As in Definition

as there using now

. We then define

as there. The notion of a rectangle

We first extend Lemma

concerning maximal surfaces.
We use the following lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let

. Let

is homeomorphic to a
k
-dimensional rectangle and it is the union of finitely many

Proof.

The assumption implies that for every point

\partial M

centered about

about

This easily imples that

Now we show by induction on

for every hyperplane

perpendicular to some

(k-1)
-dimensional rectangle. Consider a hyperplane

perpendicular to

which intersects

is a
(k-1)
-dimensional rectangle.
If

is not a

-dimensional rectangle, then there must be two points

such that the line

through

and the line

through

have different intersections with

be a sequence of points in

and

differ at exactly one coordinate. There must be some

have different intersections with

But then the 2-dimensional plane

Theorem 9.2
.

Let

Let

, say at level

-dimensional rectangle.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
b>d>0
. Let
R
be an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

with side lengths at least
b
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a minimal locally regulated partition of
R

with discreteness constant
d
. Suppose
\Int(R)\cap\partial\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
.
Then there is a line segment
s\subseteq R
parallel to some
e_{j}

such that
s
is of length
\sqrt{bd}/(n-1)
and
s\subseteq\partial\mathcal{P}
.
In fact, for some
e_{i}\neq e_{j}
we have that
s\subseteq\partial_{e_{i}}\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
c=\sqrt{bd}/(n-1)
. Without loss of generality
we may assume that all
P\in\mathcal{P}
have side lengths less than
c
, since otherwise the lemma holds trivially.
Let
M_{1}
be a maximal surface at level
d_{1}
, where
\min\pi_{n}(R)<d_{1}<\min\pi_{n}(R)+c
.
Then
\mathcal{P}
respects
M_{1}
from
d_{1}
to some
d_{2}>d_{1}
, where
d_{2}
is the largest such. If
d_{2}-d_{1}\geq c
or if
M_{1}
has a side length at least
c

then we are done, so we assume otherwise. By Theorem
9.3

there is a virtual maximal surface
M_{2}
, which is an
(n-1)
-dimensional rectangle properly containing
M_{1}
, such that
\mathcal{P}
respects
M_{2}\times[d_{2},d_{3}]
for some
d_{3}>d_{2}
. Again, if
d_{3}-d_{2}\geq c
then we are done.
Note that at least one of the
n-1
side lengths in the
e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}
directions
has gotten strictly larger in going from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
. By the definition of the discreteness constant of
\mathcal{P}
, the increase of the side length is at least
d
. If any of the side lengths has increased to be at least

c
then we are done, so we assume not. We repeat this argument until we find a virtual maximal surface

at level

where

y\in F(2^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})

\mathcal{P}
be the

y
, which we identify with an
minimal regulated partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be the corresponding
minimal regulated partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Applying Lemma
9.4
to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{dom}(q)
(which
we can view as an
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) we get a line segment
s^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{dom}(q)
parallel to one of the
coordinate axes, say
e_{j}
, and an
e_{i}\neq e_{j}
with
s^{\prime}\subseteq\partial_{e_{i}}\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

and with the length of
s^{\prime}
at least

\sqrt{b}/(n-1)
.

Let
s\subseteq\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\partial P
be a segment in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

parallel to
s^{\prime}
and of length at least
|s^{\prime}|-1
and such that
|\pi_{i}(s)-\pi_{i}(s^{\prime})|\leq 1/2
.
So, for any point
u
on
s
, the points
u
and
u+e_{i}
lie in different
elements of
\mathcal{P}
.
The segment
s
intersects at least

\left(\frac{\sqrt{b}}{n-1}-1\right)\frac{1}{2Cd_{0}}>5nCd_{0}

H_{m}
.
Thus there is an
m
with

\frac{m}{2Cd_{0}}\equiv j-1\!\!\mod n\mbox{ and }m\notin[0,n]\cup[b-n,n]

such that

s\cap H_{m}\neq\emptyset\mbox{ and }[\pi_{j}(z)-Cd_{0},\pi_{j}(z)+Cd_{0}]\subseteq\pi_{j}(s)

where
z
is the point of intersection of
s
with
H_{m}
. There is an
a<d_{0}^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}Cd_{0}

such that
z+ae_{j}
, which lies on
s

p_{0}

(note that

Cd_{0}

z+(a+c)e_{j}

which contradicts
