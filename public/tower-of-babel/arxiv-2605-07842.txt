Abstract
. Gabor phase retrieval for signals has attracted considerable attention in recent years. For the more general short-time linear canonical transform (STLCT), which arises naturally in optical systems and canonical time–frequency analysis, existing work has so far focused mainly on uniqueness and sampling conditions. Explicit reconstruction formulas, quantitative stability estimates, and robust reconstruction algorithms, however, are still missing. In this paper, we study uniqueness, stability, and robust reconstruction for phase retrieval from phaseless STLCT measurements in the complex Gaussian shift-invariant space V β ∞ ​ ( φ ) V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi) . We first prove that every signal in V β ∞ ​ ( φ ) V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi) is uniquely determined, up to a global unimodular constant, by its phaseless STLCT measurements on the semi-discrete set β 2 ​ ℤ × ℝ \frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R} , and we derive an explicit reconstruction formula. We then establish stability on intervals under an anchor-point condition, showing that the stability constant is governed by the maximal spacing between adjacent anchor points rather than by the radius of the whole interval. This prevents exponential deterioration with respect to the interval size. Motivated by the practical setting in which only finitely many discrete noisy magnitude samples are available, we further develop an explicit reconstruction algorithm with quantitative robustness guarantees, where the reconstruction error is controlled by the discretization parameters, the noise level, and the conditioning induced by the anchor points. In the Fourier case, our results recover the corresponding Gabor phase retrieval results of Grohs and Liehr and provide improved stability constants.



1.
Introduction

Phase retrieval concerns the reconstruction of a function
f
from magnitude-only measurements
\{\,|\psi(f)|:\psi\in\Psi\,\}
, where
\Psi
is a family of linear functionals acting on a function space. Such problems are inherently nonlinear and arise naturally in many scientific and engineering applications in which detectors record only intensities rather than the underlying signal, including X-ray crystallography
[
49
]
, coherent diffractive imaging
[
14
]
, and optics
[
56
]
. From a mathematical point of view, phase retrieval is a nonlinear inverse problem in which one seeks to recover a function from quadratic measurements. Its central questions concern injectivity of the associated nonlinear measurement map, stability of the inverse problem, and the design of constructive reconstruction procedures, see
[
27
,
31
]
for surveys, as well as
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
15
,
18
,
21
,
29
,
35
,
37
,
54
]
and references therein.

A classical example is Fourier phase retrieval, corresponding to the choice of
\Psi
as the Fourier transform. In this setting, the modulus of the Fourier transform does not determine the signal uniquely. Besides the trivial ambiguities of translation, conjugate reflection, and multiplication by unimodular constants, there exist infinitely many essentially different functions sharing the same Fourier magnitude
[
2
,
13
,
40
,
56
]
. This shows that taking the modulus alone is, in general, insufficient for recovering the phase information. It therefore motivates the search for alternative measurement schemes with enough redundancy to guarantee uniqueness and stable recovery.

A prominent alternative is provided by the short-time Fourier transform (STFT)

\mathcal{V}_{\omega}f(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\overline{\omega(t-x)}\,e^{-2\pi i\xi t}\,dt,

which yields a highly redundant time–frequency representation and has attracted considerable attention in phase retrieval
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
16
,
31
,
32
,
34
,
36
,
37
,
57
,
58
,
59
]
. When the window function
\omega
is chosen to be the Gaussian

\varphi:=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},

with variance
\sigma^{2}
, the transform
\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}f
enjoys strong analytic properties and is commonly referred to as the Gabor transform
[
28
]
. The corresponding phase retrieval problem is known as
Gabor phase retrieval
. Under mild nondegeneracy assumptions on the window, the spectrogram
|\mathcal{V}_{\omega}f|^{2}
determines the signal uniquely up to a unimodular constant
[
6
,
12
,
31
,
37
]
. However, in infinite-dimensional spaces, uniqueness does not in general imply uniform stability: the nonlinear map
f\mapsto|\mathcal{V}_{\omega}f|
typically fails to admit a uniform Lipschitz inverse
[
4
,
15
]
. This has led to the study of conditional stability under additional structural assumptions
[
5
,
37
,
38
]
.

The shift-invariant space

V_{\beta}^{p}(h)=\Bigl\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}\,h(\cdot-\beta k):\ \{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\Bigr\},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,

is a fundamental class of function spaces in harmonic analysis and sampling theory
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
25
,
30
]
. They interpolate between finite-dimensional approximation spaces and classical spaces such as Paley–Wiener and spline spaces, and provide a natural framework for sampling and reconstruction problems. For suitable generators, and in particular for the Gaussian, elements of these spaces admit strong analytic structure, which makes them especially amenable to complex-analytic methods. Phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces has been extensively studied for real-valued signals via phaseless pointwise sampling
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
29
,
44
,
51
,
53
,
54
]
, and for complex-valued signals via structured measurements designed
[
17
,
23
,
43
,
48
]
.

Some particularly relevant recent results are due to Grohs and Liehr
[
33
,
35
]
, who studied Gabor phase retrieval for signals in the complex Gaussian shift-invariant space
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
. In
[
33
]
, they proved that every signal
f\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
is uniquely determined, up to a global unimodular constant, from the phaseless Gabor lattice measurements
|\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}f(\frac{\beta}{2+\epsilon}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})|
for any
\epsilon>0
.
They later showed in
[
35
]
that phaseless Gabor measurements on parallel lines in the time–frequency plane determine the signal uniquely, up to a global unimodular constant, established conditional stability estimates on intervals, and developed an explicit reconstruction procedure from finitely many noisy samples. These results demonstrate that the combination of Gaussian shift-invariant structure and redundant time–frequency measurements leads not only to uniqueness, but also to stable and constructive recovery.

These results naturally raise the question whether the same program can be carried out beyond the Fourier framework. This motivates the study of the short-time linear canonical transform (STLCT), which extends the STFT by replacing the Fourier kernel with the kernel of the linear canonical transform (LCT). The LCT is parametrized by

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},\ a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},ad-bc=1,

and includes the Fourier transform, the fractional Fourier transform, and the Fresnel transform as special cases
[
39
,
60
]
. It plays an important role in optics
[
39
,
52
]
and quantum mechanics
[
50
]
. For a window function
\omega
, the STLCT is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\omega(t-x)}\,K_{\mathbf{A}}(\xi,t)\,dt,

where

K_{\mathbf{A}}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ib}}\exp\!\left(\frac{i\pi}{b}(at^{2}-2\xi t+d\xi^{2})\right)

is the LCT kernel
[
42
,
60
]
. Existing results on STLCT phase retrieval mainly address uniqueness and sampling conditions
[
26
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
. By contrast, explicit reconstruction formulas, quantitative stability estimates, and robust algorithms for infinite-dimensional signal classes remain largely missing. This gap is particularly significant from the practical point of view, since in applications one only has access to finitely many discrete phaseless samples, typically corrupted by noise, and therefore any useful phase retrieval theory must ultimately lead to a robust reconstruction algorithm.

The purpose of this paper is to develop such a theory for Gaussian shift-invariant signals from phaseless STLCT measurements. Using a chirp-modulated Gaussian window, we show that the analytic framework underlying the Gabor case extends to the STLCT setting. First, we prove that every signal in
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
is uniquely determined, up to a global unimodular constant, by its phaseless STLCT measurements on the semi-discrete set
\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, and we derive an explicit reconstruction formula, see Theorem
3.3
. Next, we establish conditional stability on bounded intervals. By introducing anchor points at which the signal magnitude is bounded away from zero, we show that the stability constant depends on the maximal gap between adjacent anchor points rather than on the radius of the whole interval, thereby avoiding exponential deterioration with respect to the interval size, see Theorem
4.2
. Finally, motivated by the practical setting in which only finitely many discrete noisy phaseless STLCT samples are available, we develop an explicit reconstruction algorithm and establish quantitative robustness estimates, see Theorem
5.4
. The algorithm first detects anchor points from the sampled magnitude data, then performs local reconstruction on neighboring anchor intervals, and finally propagates the phase information across adjacent anchors to recover the signal globally. Our robustness analysis makes precise how the reconstruction error depends on the discretization parameters, the measurement noise, and the conditioning induced by the anchor point condition. Numerical experiments illustrate the effectiveness of the algorithm. In the Fourier case, our results recover the corresponding Gabor phase retrieval theory of Grohs and Liehr
[
35
]
, while also yielding sharper stability bounds.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect the necessary preliminaries on the STLCT and Gaussian shift-invariant spaces. In Section
3
, we establish the uniqueness result of STLCT phase retrieval for complex-valued signals in Gaussian shift-invariant spaces. Section
4
is devoted to the corresponding stability analysis of STLCT phase retrieval. Finally, in Section
5
, we develop the reconstruction algorithm from finitely many discrete phaseless STLCT samples, derive quantitative approximation guarantees, and present numerical experiments illustrating the effectiveness of the proposed method.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect the notation, definitions, and preliminary results that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1.
Time-frequency transforms

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, its
Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}f
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)e^{-2\pi i\xi t}dt.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, its
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT)
\mathcal{V}_{\omega}
with a window function
\omega\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\omega}f(x,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\omega(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}dt.

Given a parameter matrix
\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

such that
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
ad-bc=1
. We define the
linear canonical transform
(LCT)
[
39
,
60
]

\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{A}}
of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with parameter matrix
A
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{A}}f(\xi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)K_{\textbf{A}}(\xi,t)dt,&b\neq 0;\\
\sqrt{d}e^{i\pi cd\xi^{2}}f(d\xi),&b=0,\end{array}\right.

where the transform kernel
K_{\textbf{A}}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle K_{\textbf{A}}(\xi,t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ib}}e^{\frac{i\pi}{b}(at^{2}-2\xi t+d\xi^{2})},\ b\neq 0.

When
b=0
, the LCT degenerates into a scaling and chirp multiplication operator. Excluding this degenerate case, we assume
b>0
throughout this paper, since the case
b<0
can be handled analogously.

The
short-time linear canonical transform
(STLCT) of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with parameter matrix
\mathbf{A}
and window function
\omega\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\omega(t-x)}K_{\mathbf{A}}(\xi,t)\,dt,

where
K_{\mathbf{A}}
is as in (
2.1
)
[
42
,
60
]
. When
\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
, the STLCT reduces to the classical STFT.

To establish the relationship between STLCT and STFT, we introduce the following auxiliary functions.
Given the parameter matrix
\mathbf{A}
and two complex-valued functions
f,\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
along with two constants
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the following functions

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\check{f}(t)=e^{i\pi\frac{a}{b}t^{2}}f(t),

(2.3b)

\displaystyle f^{\omega,x}(t)=f(t)\overline{\omega(t-x)}e^{i\pi\frac{a}{b}t^{2}},

(2.3c)

\displaystyle f_{\xi}(t)=f(t-\xi)\overline{f(t)},

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We refer to
\check{f}
as the
chirp modulation
of
f
, and
f_{\xi}
as the
tensor product
of
f
.
These auxiliary functions allow us to express the STLCT in terms of the STFT:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ib}}e^{i\pi\frac{d}{b}\xi^{2}}\mathcal{V}_{\omega}\check{f}(x,\frac{\xi}{b})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ib}}e^{i\pi\frac{d}{b}\xi^{2}}\mathcal{F}f^{\omega,x}(\frac{\xi}{b}).

The tensor product
f_{\xi}
in (
2.3c
) plays a crucial role in our analysis, as it captures the phase information of
f
through the phaseless STLCT measurements, see Section
3
.

2.2.
Gaussian shift-invariant spaces

We now introduce the shift-invariant spaces that will be the signal class of interest. Let
\varphi:=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}
be a Gaussian function with variance
\sigma^{2}
. The
Gaussian shift-invariant space
is defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle V_{\beta}^{p}(\varphi):=\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}\varphi(\cdot-\beta k):\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\Big\},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

where
\beta>0
is the step-size.
It is well known that
V_{\beta}^{p}(\varphi)\subset V_{\beta}^{q}(\varphi)\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty

[
8
]
.
Define the
\frac{1}{\beta}
-periodic map
\Psi_{\beta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
via

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\beta}(t){}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{F}\varphi(t+\frac{n}{\beta})|^{2}.

One may easily verify that
\{\varphi(\cdot-\beta n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
forms a Riesz basis for
V_{\beta}^{2}(\varphi)
, i.e., there exist constants
0<A_{2}\leq B_{2}<\infty
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle A_{2}\|c\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\varphi(\cdot-\beta n)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq B_{2}\|c\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})},

cf. Proposition
2.2
, where we only need verify that
\beta A_{2}\leq\Psi_{\beta}(t)\leq\beta B_{2}
holds for almost every
t\in[0,\frac{1}{\beta}]
.

As (
2.7
) holds,
there exists a unique dual generator
\widetilde{\varphi}\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\varphi)
such that
\{\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot-\beta n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a Riesz basis for
V_{\beta}^{2}(\varphi)
, and
\varphi
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
satisfy the biorthogonality relation

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot-\beta n),\varphi(\cdot-\beta k)\rangle=\delta_{n,k},

where
\delta_{n,k}=1
for
n=k
and
\delta_{n,k}=0
for
n\neq k
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
T_{x}
the translation operator

T_{x}f(t):=f(t-x),\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover,
f\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\varphi)
admits the following biorthogonal expansion

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,T_{\beta n}\varphi\rangle T_{\beta n}\widetilde{\varphi},

which holds in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The following proposition says that
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta}(\varphi)\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
can still be represented in a biorthogonal expansion and the series converges uniformly on compact intervals of the real line.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
35
, Proposition 2.5]
Let
\varphi=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}
be the Gaussian function and
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
be the shift-invariant space with step-size
\beta>0
in (
2.5
).
If
f\in V^{\infty}_{\beta}(\varphi)
, then

(2.8)

\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,T_{\beta n}\varphi\rangle T_{\beta n}\widetilde{\varphi}(t),\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},

where the series on the right converges uniformly on compact intervals of the real line.

2.3.
Riesz basis and dual generator

A key fact for our analysis is that the tensor product
\varphi_{\xi}
in (

Specifically,

Recall that the Jacobi theta function of third kind

(2.9)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}(z,c):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{n^{2}}e^{2niz}.

For every fixed

,
\vartheta_{3}
is periodic in

. Fix

, it is well known that
\vartheta_{3}(z,c)
is minimal at point

and maximal at point

(see
[

, p. 178]

To give a characterization of Riesz basis and the dual generator, we first recall the definition of

in (

]

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\varphi=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}

be the shift-invariant space with step-size

in (

(\varphi(\cdot-\beta n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

Moreover, for every

3.
Uniqueness of STLCT phase retrieval in Gaussian shift-invariant spaces

Let
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
be the Gaussian shift-invariant space defined in (
2.5
). For complex-valued signals in
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, phaseless pointwise sampling does not in general yield phase retrieval because of the conjugate ambiguity
[
17
,
18
,
29
]
. In contrast, phaseless Gabor measurements provide a natural alternative. Grohs and Liehr
[
33
]
proved that any
f\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
is uniquely determined, up to a global phase, by its phaseless Gabor measurements
|\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}f(\frac{\beta}{2+\epsilon}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})|
for any
\epsilon>0
. Subsequently, they considered the semi-discrete grid
\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
and further derived an explicit reconstruction formula and quantitative local stability estimates
[
35
]
. Since the short-time linear canonical transform (STLCT) in (
2.2
) generalizes the Gabor transform and the parameter matrix
\mathbf{A}
provides additional flexibility, it is natural to ask whether the corresponding uniqueness and stability results for phase retrieval continue to hold for phaseless STLCT measurements.

More precisely, given
f\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, we investigate whether
f
can be recovered, up to a global unimodular constant, from its phaseless STLCT measurements
\bigl|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})\bigr|
, where
\check{\varphi}
is the chirp-modulated Gaussian introduced in (
2.3a
). Start from showing that the auxiliary family
f_{\xi}:=(T_{\xi}f)\overline{f}
lives in the shift-invariant space
V_{\beta/2}^{\infty}(\varphi_{\xi})
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
in Lemma
3.1
. Hence
f_{\xi}
admits a biorthogonal expansion as in (
2.8
) with respect to the translates of the dual generator
\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}
, and the coefficients in the biorthogonal expansion are determined by the phaseless STLCT measurements, see Lemma
3.2
. This allows us to recover the family
\{f_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}
and consequently reconstruct
f
up to a global unimodular constant.

The main result of this section is Theorem
3.3

f
in the Gaussian shift-invariant space
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
is uniquely determined, up to a global unimodular constant, by its phaseless STLCT measurements
\bigl|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})\bigr|
. Moreover, Theorem
3.3
provides an explicit reconstruction formula for
f
.

We begin with the fact that the auxiliary family
f_{\xi}
remains in a Gaussian shift-invariant space.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
35

Let
\varphi=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}

\langle f_{\xi},T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\varphi_{\xi}\rangle=e^{\frac{\pi ia\beta n\xi}{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta n}{2},t)|^{2}e^{-\frac{2\pi i\xi t}{b}}dt,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Substituting (
3.5
) into (
3.4
), evaluating at
t=p+\xi
, and combining with (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle f(p+\xi)=\tau|f(p)|^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{\pi ia\beta n\xi}{b}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta n}{2},t)|^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi i\xi t}{b}}dt\right)T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(p+\xi),\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}.

It remains to determine
|f(p)|
from the measurements. Setting
\xi=0
in (
3.4
) gives

f_{0}(p)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{0},T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\varphi_{\xi=0}\rangle\,T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\xi=0}(p).

Since
f_{0}=|f|^{2}
, we have
f_{0}(p)=|f(p)|^{2}
. Moreover, by (
3.5
) with
\xi=0
,

\langle f_{0},T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\varphi_{\xi=0}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}n,t)|^{2}\,dt.

Therefore,

|f(p)|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta n}{2},t)|^{2}dt\right)T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\xi=0}(p),

and hence

|f(p)|=\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta n}{2},t)|^{2}dt\right)T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\xi=0}(p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This proves the reconstruction formula and shows that
f
is uniquely determined, up to a unimodular constant, by
|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})|
.
∎

We note that
|f(p)|
does not need to be prescribed in advance, since it is determined by the phaseless STLCT measurements through (
3.2
).

For

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

the STLCT becomes the Gabor transform
\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}
, and Theorem
3.3
yields
[

, Theorem 3.6]
.

Corollary 3.4

(
[
35
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

Let
\varphi=e^{-\frac{\cdot^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}
be the Gaussian function and let
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
be the shift-invariant space with step-size
\beta>0
in (

). Then
f\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
is uniquely determined, up to a unimodular constant, by
|\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}f(\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})|
. Moreover, there exists a unimodular constant
\tau\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\displaystyle f(p+\xi)=\tau|f(p)|^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{V}_{\varphi}f(\tfrac{\beta n}{2},t)|^{2}e^{2\pi i\xi t}dt\right)T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(p+\xi),\ \xi\in\mathbb{R},

where

4.
Stability of STLCT phase retrieval in Gaussian shift-invariant spaces

Having established the uniqueness of STLCT phase retrieval in Gaussian shift-invariant space, we now turn to the question of stability. It is well known that phase retrieval cannot be uniformly stable in infinite-dimensional spaces
[
4
,
15
]
. Accordingly, we work with a conditional notion of stability. Given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we ask whether there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
, depending on
f,g\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, such that

(4.1)

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\widetilde{C}\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{D}}

holds for all
g\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}}
is a suitable norm on the measurement space. The main issue is then to understand how the stability constant depends on the geometric size of the interval and on lower bounds for the signal at suitable reference points.

In this section, we first establish such a stability estimate on a bounded interval, see Theorem
4.1
. More precisely, we show that the reconstruction of signals in the Gaussian shift-invariant space
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
from phaseless STLCT measurements is stable on an interval
I=[p-r,p+r]
. However, this local estimate also reveals an essential difficulty, that is the corresponding stability constant contains the exponential factor
e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}
depending on the radius
r
of the interval. Consequently, if one applies the local estimate directly on a large interval, then the stability constant deteriorates exponentially with the interval length. This shows that a purely local argument is not sufficient for obtaining a meaningful stability result on large intervals.

To overcome this challenge, we adopt the following anchor point condition, which was introduced in the Gabor phase retrieval setting in
[
35
]
.

Condition
(\mathbf{P})
.
There exist
J\geq 2
points

p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{J}

and a constant
\gamma>0
such that

|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma,\ 1\leq j\leq J.

Condition
(\mathbf{P})
provides a family of anchor points at which the signal is bounded away from zero. This makes it possible to replace a single stability estimate on a large interval by a phase propagation argument along adjacent intervals centered at the anchor points.
The local stability estimate in Theorem
4.1
is then applied only at the scale of the maximal distance between consecutive anchors. In Theorem
4.2
, we show that the exponential factor in the stability bound is of the form
e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}
, where
r:=\max_{1\leq j\leq J-1}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})
, rather than depending on the total length of the interval. The passage from local to global stability therefore introduces only a linear accumulation with respect to the number of propagation steps, and in particular avoids exponential growth with the interval length.

The stability bound in (
4.1
) also depends on the constant

(4.2)

C(\sigma,\beta):=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Lambda(t)\right|,

where
\Lambda
is defined in (
2.11
). Under suitable assumptions on

\sigma
and
\beta
, there exist constants
K=K(\sigma,\beta)
and

\nu=\nu(\sigma,\beta)
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Lambda(t)|\leq Ke^{-\nu|t|},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

For example, if
\frac{\beta}{4}\leq\sigma\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\leq 1
, then
(
4.3
) holds with
K\leq\frac{205}{\sigma}
and
\nu\geq\frac{1}{4}
,
cf.
[
35
, Lemma 3.11]
. In this case, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Lambda(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\nu|t-\frac{\beta}{2}n|}\leq K\Bigl(2+\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\nu\beta}{2}|x|}\,dx\Bigr)=2K\Bigl(1+\frac{2}{\nu\beta}\Bigr).

Hence
C(\sigma,\beta)
is well defined.

To measure the discrepancy of phaseless STLCT measurements, we introduce the mixed norm

\|F\|_{\alpha,\infty}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|F(\alpha n,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\ \alpha>0,

for measurable functions
F
on
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. If
F
is
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then we write

\|F\|_{\alpha,\infty}:=\|F|_{\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}}\|_{\alpha,\infty},

where
F|_{\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}}
denotes the restriction of
F
to

\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

We begin with a local stability estimate on a bounded interval.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f,g
be the Gaussian shift-invariant functions in
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, and let
p\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

f(p)\neq 0
and
g(p)\neq 0
. For
r>0
, set
I:=[p-r,p+r].

Then

(4.4)

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\sqrt{2}\zeta\,C(\sigma,\beta)\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty},

where
\zeta:=\frac{1}{|g(p)|}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{|f(p)|+|g(p)|}+e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}\right)
and
C(\sigma,\beta)
is given in (
4.2
).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have
f_{\xi},g_{\xi}\in V_{\beta/2}^{\infty}(\varphi_{\xi})
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
Applying the biorthogonal expansion together with Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f_{\xi}(t)-g_{\xi}(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{\xi},T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\varphi_{\xi}\rangle T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(t)-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle g_{\xi},T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\varphi_{\xi}\rangle T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\frac{\pi ia\beta n\xi}{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}n,s)|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g(\tfrac{\beta}{2}n,s)|^{2}\Bigr)e^{-\frac{2\pi i\xi s}{b}}\,ds\,T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(\tfrac{\beta}{2}n,\cdot)|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g(\tfrac{\beta}{2}n,\cdot)|^{2}\Bigr\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,|T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(t)|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,e^{\frac{\xi^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}\,C(\sigma,\beta)\,\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty},\ \ \xi,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Here the second identity follows from Lemma
3.2
, while the last inequality
uses Proposition
2.2
and the definition of
C(\sigma,\beta)
.

Setting
\xi=0
in (
4
), we obtain

(4.6)

\bigl||f(t)|^{2}-|g(t)|^{2}\bigr|\leq\sqrt{2}C(\sigma,\beta)\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Now define
\tau_{1}:=\frac{\overline{g(p)}\,|f(p)|}{|g(p)|\,\overline{f(p)}}\in\mathbb{T}.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\xi|\leq r
, we have

\displaystyle|f(p+\xi)-\tau_{1}g(p+\xi)|=\left|\frac{1}{|f(p)|}f_{-\xi}(p)-\frac{1}{|g(p)|}g_{-\xi}(p)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{|g(p)|-|f(p)|}{|f(p)g(p)|}\right||f_{-\xi}(p)|+\frac{1}{|g(p)|}|f_{-\xi}(p)-g_{-\xi}(p)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|g(p)|}\left(\frac{\bigl||g(p)|^{2}-|f(p)|^{2}\bigr|}{|f(p)|+|g(p)|}\,|f(p+\xi)|+|f_{-\xi}(p)-g_{-\xi}(p)|\right).

Using (
4
) and (
4.6
), together with
|\xi|\leq r
, we conclude that

|f(p+\xi)-\tau_{1}g(p+\xi)|\leq\sqrt{2}\zeta C(\sigma,\beta)\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty},

where

\zeta=\frac{1}{|g(p)|}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{|f(p)|+|g(p)|}+e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}\right)
.

Taking the supremum over
|\xi|\leq r
yields

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\|f-\tau_{1}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\sqrt{2}\zeta C(\sigma,\beta)\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty}.

Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem
4.1
shows that STLCT phase retrieval is stable on a single interval
I=[p-r,p+r]
. Meanwhile, the estimate (
4.4
) makes clear the two sources governing the local stability constant, one is the the exponential factor
e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}
which grows with the radius
r
of the interval, and the other is the reciprocal factors involving the anchor values
|f(p)|
and
|g(p)|
.

For
\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
,
the STLCT reduces to the Gabor transform. In this case, Theorem
4.1
agrees with
[
35
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Under Condition
(\mathbf{P})
, the local stability estimate from Theorem
4.1
can be applied at a family of anchor points where the signal is bounded away from zero. Let
r:=\max_{1\leq j\leq J-1}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})
and define
I_{j}:=[p_{j}-r,p_{j}+r],1\leq j\leq J.
Since the distance between two consecutive anchor points is at most
r
, these intervals overlap successively and cover the whole interval

I=[p_{1}-r,p_{J}+r].

Theorem
4.1
provides a local stability estimate on each
I_{j}
. The main issue is then to pass from these local estimates to a single stability bound on the whole interval
I
. To this end, we compare the phases at neighboring anchor points and propagate the phase information step by step from one anchor interval to the next. In this way, the global stability estimate is obtained by iterating the local estimate only across adjacent anchor pairs, rather than by applying it once on the entire interval. As a consequence, the exponential factor appearing in the final stability constant is determined by the maximal spacing between consecutive anchor points

r:=\max_{1\leq j\leq J-1}(p_{j+1}-p_{j}),

instead of by the total length of
I
. This is precisely the mechanism that prevents exponential deterioration of the stability constant with respect to the interval length. The resulting global stability bound is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f
be a Gaussian shift-invariant function in
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, and

p_{1}<\cdots<p_{J}\in\mathbb{R}

be such that Condition
(\mathbf{P})
holds with
\gamma>0
. Set
r:=\max_{1\leq j\leq J-1}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})
and
I:=[p_{1}-r,p_{J}+r].

Then, for every
g\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, one has

(4.7)

\displaystyle\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f\hskip-2.168pt-\hskip-2.168pt\tau g\|_{L\hskip-1.4457pt^{\infty}(\hskip-0.72229ptI\hskip-0.72229pt)}\hskip-2.168pt\leq\hskip-2.8903pt\frac{16\sqrt{2}}{3}\hskip-2.168pt\left\lceil\hskip-2.168pt\frac{J}{2}\hskip-2.168pt\right\rceil\hskip-2.168pte^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}\hskip-2.168ptC(\hskip-2.168pt\sigma,\hskip-2.168pt\beta)\frac{\max\{1,\|f\|_{L\hskip-1.4457pt^{\infty}(I)}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\|g\|_{L\hskip-1.4457pt^{\infty}(I)}\}}{\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}}\bigl\|\hskip-1.4457pt|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|\hskip-0.72229pt^{2}\hskip-2.8903pt-\hskip-2.168pt|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|\hskip-0.72229pt^{2}\hskip-1.4457pt\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty},

where
C(\sigma,\beta)
is the constant defined in (
4.2
).

Remark 4.3

(Comparison with
[
35
]
)
.

When
\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
, the STLCT reduces to the Gabor transform, and Theorem
4.2
recovers
[
35
, Theorem 3.10]
.
Note that our stability constant in (
4.7
) depends linearly on
{\rm max}\{1,\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}
and the reciprocal of
\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}
, whereas the stability constant in
[

depends linearly on
{\rm max}\{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{2},\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}
and the reciprocal of
\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{3}\}
. Especially when
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
is large or
\gamma
is small, our result substantially improves the one in
[
35
]
.

For the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we require the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z_{1}|\neq 0
and
|z_{2}|\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z_{1}}{|z_{1}|}-\frac{z_{2}}{|z_{2}|}\right|\leq 2\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|z_{1}|}.

The proof of the above lemma is given in Appendix
A.3
.

We now proceed to prove Theorem
4.2
, which gives the phase retrieval stability on the interval
I=\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}I_{j}
under Condition
(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Set

\Delta:=\bigl\||S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f|^{2}-|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}g|^{2}\bigr\|_{\frac{\beta}{2},\infty}

and

B:=\sqrt{2}C(\sigma,\beta)\Delta.

By (
4.6
), we have

(4.8)

\bigl\||f|^{2}-|g|^{2}\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq B.

We will do the proof by cases.

Case 1.
Assume that
\Delta\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}C(\sigma,\beta)}
, or equivalently,

B\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
.

Then, for every
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
,

\bigl||f(p_{j})|-|g(p_{j})|\bigr|\leq\frac{\bigl||f(p_{j})|^{2}-|g(p_{j})|^{2}\bigr|}{|f(p_{j})|}\leq\frac{B}{|f(p_{j})|}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2},

where we used (
4.8
) and Condition
(\mathbf{P})
. Since

|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma
, it follows that

|g(p_{j})|\geq\frac{\gamma}{2},\ 1\leq j\leq J.

For
1\leq j\leq J
, define
I_{j}:=[p_{j}-r,p_{j}+r].
Since
|p_{j+1}-p_{j}|\leq r
for all
j
, these intervals cover
I=[p_{1}-r,p_{J}+r]=\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}I_{j}.

Let

s:=\left\lceil\frac{J}{2}\right\rceil\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tau_{s}:=\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}\,|f(p_{s})|}{|g(p_{s})|\,\overline{f(p_{s})}}\in\mathbb{T}.

Then

\|f-\tau_{s}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}=\max_{1\leq j\leq J}\|f-\tau_{s}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}.

We now estimate
\|f-\tau_{s}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}
for each
j
.

Step 1: The middle interval
I_{j}
with
j=s
.

By Theorem
4.1
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|f-\tau_{s}g\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}\leq\frac{B}{|g(p_{j})|}\left(e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}+\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}}{|f(p_{j})|+|g(p_{j})|}\right)\leq\frac{2B}{\gamma}\left(e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}+\frac{2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}}{3\gamma}\right),

since
|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma
and
|g(p_{j})|\geq\gamma/2
.

Step 2: The intervals
I_{j}
with
j<s
.

Fix
j<s
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\xi|\leq r
. Then

\displaystyle|f(p_{j}+\xi)-\tau_{s}g(p_{j}+\xi)|=\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}}{|f(p_{s})|}f(p_{j}+\xi)\hskip-2.168pt-\hskip-2.168pt\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}}{|g(p_{s})|}g(p_{j}+\xi)\right|\hskip-3.61371pt

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}}{|f(p_{s})|}-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\overline{g(p_{j})}}\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}}{|g(p_{s})|}\right||f(p_{j}+\xi)|+\left|\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{j})}{|g(p_{s})||g(p_{j})|^{2}}\right||f_{-\xi}(p_{j})-g_{-\xi}(p_{j})|

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}}{|f(p_{s})|}-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\overline{g(p_{j})}}\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}}{|g(p_{s})|}\right|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j})}+\frac{2}{\gamma}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}B,

where the last inequality follows from (
4
) and

|g(p_{j})|\geq\gamma/2
.

It remains to estimate the phase mismatch

\delta_{j,s}:=\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}}{|f(p_{s})|}-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\overline{g(p_{j})}}\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}}{|g(p_{s})|}\right|.

We propagate the phase from
p_{s}
to
p_{j}
along adjacent anchor points. Inserting

p_{s-1}
and applying the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{j,s}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}f(p_{s-1})}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}\frac{\overline{f(p_{s-1})}}{|f(p_{s-1})|}-\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})}{|g(p_{s})g(p_{s-1})|}\frac{\overline{f(p_{s-1})}}{|f(p_{s-1})|}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})}{|g(p_{s})g(p_{s-1})|}\frac{\overline{f(p_{s-1})}}{|f(p_{s-1})|}-\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})}{|g(p_{s})g(p_{s-1})|}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\overline{g(p_{j})}}\frac{\overline{g(p_{s-1})}}{|g(p_{s-1})|}\right|

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}f(p_{s-1})}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}-\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})}{|g(p_{s})g(p_{s-1})|}\right|+\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s-1})}}{|f(p_{s-1})|}-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\overline{g(p_{j})}}\frac{\overline{g(p_{s-1})}}{|g(p_{s-1})|}\right|.

By Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{s})}f(p_{s-1})}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}-\frac{\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})}{|g(p_{s})g(p_{s-1})|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}\left|\overline{f(p_{s})}f(p_{s-1})-\overline{g(p_{s})}g(p_{s-1})\right|

(4.12)

\displaystyle\hskip-108.405pt=\frac{2}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}|f_{p_{s}-p_{s-1}}(p_{s})-g_{p_{s}-p_{s-1}}(p_{s})|\leq\frac{2Be^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|},

where we used (

) and the fact that
|p_{s}-p_{s-1}|\leq r
.

Combine (

) and (
4
), we have

\delta_{j,s}\leq\frac{2Be^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}}{|f(p_{s})f(p_{s-1})|}+\delta_{j,s-1}.

Iterating along the chain
p_{s},p_{s-1},\dots,p_{j}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{j,s}\leq\sum_{k=j+1}^{s}\frac{2Be^{\frac{r^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k-1})|}+\delta_{j,j}

5.
Robust reconstruction from noisy discrete phaseless STLCT samples for Gaussian shift-invariant signals

Having established uniqueness and stability for STLCT phase retrieval in
V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
, we now address the problem of practical reconstruction from finite noisy data. In this section, we develop an explicit reconstruction algorithm for recovering
f\in V_{\beta}^{\infty}(\varphi)
from noisy phaseless STLCT samples on a finite lattice, and we derive quantitative bounds for the reconstruction.

In Theorem
3.3
, we know that the auxiliary function
f_{\xi}
admits the representation

(5.1)

f_{\xi}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\frac{\pi ia\beta n\xi}{b}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}M_{f}\!\left(\frac{\beta n}{2},u\right)e^{-\frac{2\pi i\xi u}{b}}\,du\right)T_{\frac{\beta}{2}n}\widetilde{\varphi_{\xi}}(t),\ \xi\in\mathbb{R},

where

(5.2)

M_{f}(x,t):=\bigl|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,t)\bigr|^{2},\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

are the STLCT magnitudes. Based on a discretization of (
5.1
) and a phase propagation procedure across adjacent anchor points, we formulate an explicit reconstruction algorithm, see Algorithm
1
.

To establish robustness of the reconstruction algorithm, we first analyze the local reconstruction error. In Theorem
5.1
, we derive an explicit upper bound in which the total error is decomposed into truncation of the spatial sum, frequency discretization by the trapezoidal rule, and measurement noise. A key ingredient is that the STLCT magnitude
|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,\cdot)|^{2}
extends to an entire function, see Lemma
5.2
. This analytic structure permits the use of the trapezoidal rule with exponential convergence in the frequency variable, see Lemma
5.3
.

Finally, combining the local error estimate with the phase propagation argument, we prove a quantitative robustness result for Algorithm
1
, see Theorem
5.4
. More precisely, for any prescribed tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, we derive explicit conditions on the sampling parameters and the noise level under which the global reconstruction error is bounded by
\varepsilon
times the conditioning factor

\kappa_{f,\gamma}:=\frac{\max\{1,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}}{\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}},

which reflects the signal amplitude on the reconstruction interval together with the lower bound at the anchor points.

5.1.
The Reconstruction Algorithm

In this section, we develop an explicit reconstruction procedure. The main idea is to discretize the integral term in (
5.1
), use the resulting approximation to reconstruct the signal locally near suitable anchor points, and then propagate the phase information across adjacent anchor intervals to obtain a global reconstruction.

Let

5.3.
Stability of the Reconstruction

We now turn to the stability of Algorithm
1
. The first step is to quantify the local error produced by the discrete reconstruction procedure. We introduce a tolerance parameter
\varepsilon>0
and require the sampling lattice and the noise level to be chosen so that the local reconstruction errors satisfy

cf. (

measures the total local numerical error coming from the truncation of the spatial sum, the discretization of the Fourier-type integral by the trapezoidal rule, and the measurement noise.

To pass from this local control to a global reconstruction bound, we introduce the conditioning factor

(5.16)

\kappa_{f,\gamma}:=\frac{\max\{1,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}}{\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}}.

The quantity
\kappa_{f,\gamma}
reflects the stability of the phase propagation step in the algorithm. More precisely, the factor
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
accounts for the size of the signal on the reconstruction interval, while the denominator records the lower bound
|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma
at the anchor points. In particular, small anchor values lead to a less favorable conditioning of the reconstruction problem.

The main result below shows that, under explicit assumptions on the sampling lattice and the noise level, the anchor points detected by Algorithm

(\mathbf{P})

. By Step 2 of Algorithm

, the selected anchor points satisfy

p_{j+2}-p_{j}\geq r,\ 1\leq j\leq J-2.

Thus the odd-indexed anchor points and the even-indexed anchor points are each
r
-separated. Since both subsequences are contained in the interval
[-s,s]
, whose length is
2s
, we obtain

J-2\leq\frac{4s}{r}.

Moreover, since
r\leq 2s
, we also have
2\leq 4s/r
. Hence

(5.23)

\max\{J-2,2\}\leq\frac{4s}{r}.

Set
m:=N-\left\lceil\frac{2}{\beta}\left(s+\frac{r}{2}\right)\right\rceil
and
q:=H-\left\lceil\frac{|a|\beta N}{2h}\right\rceil.

Let
\Xi_{1}:=D_{1}\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}e^{-\frac{\nu\beta}{2}m}
,
\Xi_{2}:=D_{2}\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{b}{\sigma h}}-1}
,
\Xi_{3}:=D_{3}\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}h^{2}q^{2}}{b^{2}}}
and
\Xi_{4}:=D_{4}h\|\eta\|_{\infty}
.
By the assumptions (
5.18
) - (
5.21
), we have

\Xi_{k}\leq\frac{r\varepsilon}{16s},\ k=1,2,3,4.

Hence, (
5.14
) yields

(5.24)

\max_{1\leq j\leq J-1}\|E_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,p_{j+1}-p_{j}])}\leq\Xi_{1}+\Xi_{2}+\Xi_{3}+\Xi_{4}\leq\frac{r\varepsilon}{4s}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Consequently, for
1\leq j\leq J-1
,

E_{j}(\xi)\leq\frac{r\varepsilon}{4s}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\ 0\leq\xi\leq p_{j+1}-p_{j}.

Step 1: Verification of Condition
(\mathbf{P})
.

For each anchor point
p_{j}
, we have
A_{j}=\mathcal{A}(p_{j})
by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
).
Moreover, we have

E_{j}(0)=\bigl||f(p_{j})|^{2}-A_{j}\bigr|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},\ 1\leq j\leq J

by (
5.11
), (
5.24
) and (
5.17
).
Since Step 2 of the algorithm ensures

A_{j}\geq\widetilde{\gamma}=\frac{3}{2}\gamma^{2},

it follows that

|f(p_{j})|^{2}\geq A_{j}-\bigl||f(p_{j})|^{2}-A_{j}\bigr|\geq\gamma^{2},\ 1\leq j\leq J.

Therefore,

|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma,\ 1\leq j\leq J,

and thus
f
satisfies Condition
(\mathbf{P})
with anchor points

-s=p_{1}<\cdots<p_{J}=s.

Step 2: Prove the well-definedness of phase transition factor
\rho_{j}
in (
5.7
).

Let
1\leq j\leq J-1
and
0\leq\xi\leq p_{j+1}-p_{j}
. By the definition of
R_{j}
,
A_{j}
, and
E_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|f(p_{j}+\xi)-\frac{f(p_{j})}{|f(p_{j})|}R_{j}(\xi)\right|=\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{|f(p_{j})|}f(p_{j}+\xi)-R_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{|f(p_{j})|}f(p_{j}+\xi)-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}f(p_{j}+\xi)\right|+\left|\frac{f_{-\xi}(p_{j})}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}-R_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{|f(p_{j})|^{2}-A_{j}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}(|f(p_{j})|+\sqrt{A_{j}})}\right|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\left|\overline{f_{\xi}(p_{j}+\xi)}-\sqrt{A_{j}}R_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{j}(0)}{\sqrt{A_{j}}(|f(p_{j})|+\sqrt{A_{j}})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}E_{j}(\xi).

As
|f(p_{j})|\geq\gamma
and
A_{j}\geq\frac{3}{2}\gamma^{2}>\gamma^{2}
,
we have

\sqrt{A_{j}}\geq\gamma\ \ {\rm and}\ \ |f(p_{j})|+\sqrt{A_{j}}\geq 2\gamma.

Combine with (
5.17
) and (
5.24
),
we get

(5.25)

\left|f(p_{j}+\xi)-\frac{f(p_{j})}{|f(p_{j})|}R_{j}(\xi)\right|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{2\gamma^{2}}E_{j}(0)+\frac{1}{\gamma}E_{j}(\xi)\leq\frac{\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{4\gamma^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}\leq\frac{3\gamma}{4}.

Taking
\xi=p_{j+1}-p_{j}
yields

\left|f(p_{j+1})-\frac{f(p_{j})}{|f(p_{j})|}R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})\right|\leq\frac{3\gamma}{4}.

As
|f(p_{j+1})|\geq\gamma
, we have

|R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})|\geq|f(p_{j+1})|-\left|f(p_{j+1})-\frac{f(p_{j})}{|f(p_{j})|}R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})\right|\geq\frac{\gamma}{4}.

Hence
R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})\neq 0
, and the phase factor

\rho_{j}=\frac{R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})}{|R_{j}(p_{j+1}-p_{j})|}

is well defined for
1\leq j\leq J-1
.

Step 3: Global reconstruction error.

Set

\mu:=\left\lceil\frac{J}{2}\right\rceil\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \tau_{\mu}:=\frac{f(p_{\mu})}{|f(p_{\mu})|}\in\mathbb{T}.

We estimate
f-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}
separately on the right, middle, and left parts of the interval
I=[p_{1},p_{J}]
.

The right interval
(p_{\mu+1},p_{J}]
.

Let

t=p_{j}+\xi\in(p_{\mu+1},p_{J}],

where
j\in\{\mu+1,\dots,J-1\}
and
0<\xi\leq p_{j+1}-p_{j}
. By the definition of the reconstruction
\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle|f(t){}-\frac{f(p_{\mu})}{|f(p_{\mu})|}\mathcal{R}(t)|=\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}f(p_{j}+\xi)-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}R_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}f(p_{j}+\xi)-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\overline{f(p_{j})}f(p_{j}+\xi)\right|+\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\overline{f(p_{j})}f(p_{j}+\xi)-R_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}E_{j}(\xi)

(5.26)

\displaystyle<\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma},

where the last inequality follows from (
5.24
) and
A_{j}>\gamma^{2}
.

It remains to estimate

\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|.

As

\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu+1})}}{|f(p_{\mu+1})|}\right|+\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu+1})}}{|f(p_{\mu+1})|}-\rho_{\mu+1}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|,

we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\mu}^{j-1}\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{k})}f(p_{k+1})}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|}-\rho_{k}\right|+\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{|f(p_{j})|}-\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|

(5.27)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\mu}^{j-1}\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{k})}f(p_{k+1})}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|}-\rho_{k}\right|+\frac{E_{j}(0)}{\sqrt{A_{j}}\bigl(|f(p_{j})|+\sqrt{A_{j}}\bigr)}.

For each
k\in\{\mu,\dots,j-1\}
, Lemma
4.4
yields

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{k})}f(p_{k+1})}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|}-\rho_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{k})}f(p_{k+1})}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|}-\frac{\overline{\mathcal{G}_{p_{k}}(p_{k+1}-p_{k})}}{|\mathcal{G}_{p_{k}}(p_{k+1}-p_{k})|}\right|

(5.28)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\bigl|\overline{f(p_{k})}f(p_{k+1})-\overline{\mathcal{G}_{p_{k}}(p_{k+1}-p_{k})}\bigr|}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|}=\frac{2E_{k}(p_{k+1}-p_{k})}{|f(p_{k})f(p_{k+1})|},

where the first equality follows by (
5.5
), (
5.6
), (
5.7
) and (
5.10
).
Substituting (
5.3
) into (
5.3
), and combine with
|f(p_{k})|\geq\gamma
,
A_{j}\geq\gamma^{2}
, (
5.23
) and (
5.24
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\overline{f(p_{\mu})}}{|f(p_{\mu})|}-\rho_{\mu}\cdots\rho_{j-1}\frac{\overline{f(p_{j})}}{\sqrt{A_{j}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\mu}^{j-1}\frac{2E_{k}(p_{k+1}-p_{k})}{\gamma^{2}}+\frac{E_{j}(0)}{2\gamma^{2}}

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J-1}{2}\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}\frac{\varepsilon}{J-2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\gamma^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}=\frac{9\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}.

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.3
), we obtain

(5.30)

\|f-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}((p_{\mu+1},p_{J}])}\leq\frac{9\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{4\gamma^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}.

The middle interval
[p_{\mu},p_{\mu+1}]
.

Let
t=p_{\mu}+\xi\in[p_{\mu},p_{\mu+1}],\ 0\leq\xi\leq p_{\mu+1}-p_{\mu}
.
No phase propagation is needed. Therefore, by the same estimate as in Step 2,

|f(t)-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}(t)|\leq\frac{E_{\mu}(0)}{2\gamma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{E_{\mu}(\xi)}{\gamma}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}.

Therefore

(5.31)

\|f-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}([p_{\mu},p_{\mu+1}])}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}.

The left interval
[p_{1},p_{\mu})
.

The same argument as for the right interval, with the phase propagating from
p_{\mu}
to the left, gives

(5.32)

\|f-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}([p_{1},p_{\mu}))}\leq\frac{9\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}{4\gamma^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}.

Finally, combining (
5.30
), (
5.31
), and (
5.32
), we arrive at

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\|f-\tau_{\mu}\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\frac{9\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}\leq\frac{11\varepsilon}{4}\frac{\max\{1,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}}{\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}}=\frac{11}{4}\varepsilon\kappa_{f,\gamma},

where

\kappa_{f,\gamma}:=\frac{\max\{1,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\}}{\min\{\gamma,\gamma^{2}\}}.

If
J=2
, then
p_{1}=-s
,
p_{2}=s
,
\mu=1
, and the phase propagation step is not needed. In this case,

\mathcal{R}(t)=R_{1}(t-p_{1}),\ t\in I=[p_{1},p_{2}].

By the same argument used in Steps 1 and 2, we know that

|f(p_{1})|\geq\gamma,\ \ A_{1}\geq\gamma^{2},

and
\rho_{1}
is well defined.
Set

\tau_{1}:=\frac{f(p_{1})}{|f(p_{1})|}\in\mathbb{T}.

For
t=p_{1}+\xi\in I,0\leq\xi\leq p_{2}-p_{1}
, the middle-interval estimate gives

|f(t)-\tau_{1}\mathcal{R}(t)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\gamma^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2\gamma}.

Hence

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\frac{11}{4}\varepsilon\kappa_{f,\gamma}

also holds for
J=2
. The proof is complete.

∎

Remark 5.5

(Noiseless reconstruction)
.

Under the same setting as Theorem
5.4
with
\eta=0
, the noise condition is vacuously satisfied. Hence, if the sampling lattice satisfies conditions (
5.18
) - (
5.20
), then

\displaystyle\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}\|f-\tau\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\frac{33}{16}\,\varepsilon\,\kappa_{f,\gamma}.

In particular, by choosing
N
,
1/h
, and
H
sufficiently large, the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily small, showing that Algorithm
1
achieves arbitrarily accurate reconstruction from exact phaseless STLCT samples on a sufficiently fine lattice.

Remark 5.6

(Comparison with
[

]
)
.

For the special case

Lemma A.1
.

[

\Psi_{\beta/2}(t)

Proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\check{\omega},x}(t)|^{2}dt{}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t)\overline{\omega(t-x)}|^{2}dt\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\omega(t-x)|^{2}dt<\infty,

then

, and hence

by Plancherel’s theorem. It follows from (

. Consequently,

and therefore the Fourier transform of the function

.

By (

) and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\overline{f}(\xi)=\overline{\mathcal{F}f(\xi)}
, we have

\displaystyle|S_{\check{\omega}}^{\textbf{A}}f(x,b\xi)|^{2}{}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}(\mathcal{F}f^{\check{\omega},x})(\xi)\overline{(\mathcal{F}f^{\check{\omega},x})(\xi)}=\frac{1}{b}(\mathcal{F}f^{\check{\omega},x})(\xi)(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\overline{f^{\check{\omega},x}})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}^{2}f^{\check{\omega},x}\ast\overline{f^{\check{\omega},x}})(\xi),

where the last equality follows from the convolution theorem.
The convolution

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}f^{\check{\omega},x}\ast\overline{f^{\check{\omega},x}}(\xi){}

\displaystyle\overset{t=\xi-t}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\check{\omega},x}(t-\xi)\overline{f^{\check{\omega},x}(t)}dt=e^{\frac{-2\pi iax\xi}{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\xi}(t)\overline{T_{x}\omega_{\xi}(t)}dt

Combine (

Evaluating the Gaussian Fourier integral yields

\displaystyle S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,t)=\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{ib}}e^{i\pi\frac{d}{b}t^{2}}e^{-i\pi\frac{a}{b}x^{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}}{b^{2}}(t-ax)^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\pi i\frac{(x+\beta k)(t-ax)}{b}}.

\displaystyle|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\sigma^{2}}{b}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}}{b^{2}}(t-ax)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\overline{c_{k}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta n)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}e^{\pi i\frac{\beta(k-n)(t-ax)}{b}}.

\displaystyle|S_{\check{\varphi}}^{\mathbf{A}}f(x,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\sigma^{2}}{b}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}}{b^{2}}(t-ax)^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\overline{c_{n+\ell}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta n)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta(n+\ell))^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}\right)e^{-\pi i\frac{\beta ax}{b}\ell}e^{\pi i\frac{\beta t}{b}\ell}.

where

(A.3)

V_{x}(t):=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}e^{\pi i\frac{\beta\ell}{b}t}

with coefficients
\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}
defined in (
5.13
).

It remains to estimate the coefficients
\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}
. By the definition of
\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}
in (

), we have

\displaystyle|\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta n)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{(x-\beta(n+\ell))^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}.

(x-\beta n)^{2}+(x-\beta(n+\ell))^{2}=2\Bigl(x-\beta n-\frac{\beta\ell}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}\ell^{2}}{2}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle|\breve{r}_{\ell}^{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}e^{-\frac{\beta^{2}\ell^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\left(n+\frac{\ell}{2}-\frac{x}{\beta}\right)^{2}}.

Since the Gaussian is bounded by
1
and integrable, we have

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}(n+\alpha)^{2}}\leq 1+\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}u^{2}}du=1+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}{\beta}.
