Characterizing exact dynamics of a trapped active Brownian particle under torque in two and three dimensions
Anweshika Pattanayak, Amir Shee, Abhishek Chaudhuri, Debasish Chaudhuri

Abstract
The interplay of chirality, self-propulsion, and spatial confinement generates striking non-equilibrium fluctuations whose higher-order statistics carry information about the dynamics and shape of the position distribution. Here, we present an exact analytical framework, based on a Laplace-transform solution of the Fokker-Planck equation, for the transient dynamics of a chiral active Brownian particle in a harmonic trap, in both two and three dimensions. We obtain closed-form expressions for all time-dependent moments up to fourth order, enabling a complete characterization of the excess kurtosis throughout the transient and steady-state regimes. In two dimensions, the excess kurtosis exhibits a damped oscillatory response with multiple re-entrant crossovers, evolving from negative values that reflect active off-centered ring-like position distributions to positive values characteristic of heavy-tailed fluctuations. This damped oscillatory excess kurtosis appears both for free and harmonic confinement, although increasing the trapping stiffness progressively suppresses it, and the positive excess kurtosis eventually vanishes at sufficiently high stiffness. In contrast, in three dimensions, the excess kurtosis remains negative, indicating a robustly active non-Gaussian state characterized by half-ring-like to band-like position distributions in the two-dimensional plane spanned by the torque axis and its normal radial direction. Our results demonstrate how chirality, propulsion, and confinement, together with dimensionality, shape transient dynamics, while providing experimentally accessible signatures of confined chiral active dynamics.

In this work, we present an exact analytic theory of the dynamics of a trapped chiral active Brownian particle in both two and three dimensions.
We employ a method based on the Fokker–Planck equation, derived from the governing equations of motion, originally proposed for calculating configurational moments in polymer chains

Shee et al. 2020
;
Chaudhuri and Dhar 2021
;
Patel and Chaudhuri 2023

Pattanayak et al. 2024
and steady states under harmonic confinement
Pattanayak et al. 2025

By directly solving the full Fokker–Planck equation using Laplace transform based method
Pattanayak et al. 2025
, we obtain closed-form expressions for the time-dependent moments such as the mean-squared displacement and the fourth order moment of displacement, which together capture the complete temporal evolution of the excess kurtosis.

Our analysis reveals striking dimensional differences: in two dimensions, the excess kurtosis exhibits damped oscillations between negative and positive values, signaling crossovers between off-centered and heavy-tailed distributions, while in three dimensions, the kurtosis remains strictly negative, reflecting robustly active, non-Gaussian fluctuations. These predictions are in excellent agreement with numerical simulations and suggest experimentally accessible routes to probe chirality-induced non-Gaussian dynamics.
By developing a detailed understanding of how self-propulsion, chirality/torque, and confinement collectively shape non-equilibrium dynamics, our work establishes a comprehensive and exact theoretical framework for describing non-equilibrium fluctuations in chiral active matter. Moreover, our theory in both two and three dimensions highlights the crucial role of dimensionality in determining universal signatures of confined chiral active dynamics, offering direct avenues for experimental verification and characterization of chiral active motion across scales.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we derive an exact analytical framework for the transient dynamics of a chiral active Brownian particle confined in a harmonic trap, in both two and three dimensions.
Starting from the Fokker–Planck equation and using a Laplace transform approach, we obtain closed-form expressions for all time-dependent displacement moments up to fourth order.
These expressions allow a complete and exact characterization of the excess kurtosis throughout the transient dynamics and its steady-state regimes.

In two dimensions, the excess kurtosis begins near zero at short times, where translational diffusion dominates and the statistics are nearly Gaussian.
As time progresses, activity takes over and the excess kurtosis becomes negative, reflecting the emergence of off-centered, ring-like position distributions driven by persistent circular motion.
At intermediate times, and for sufficiently strong chirality, the excess kurtosis develops damped oscillations, alternating between negative values associated with off-centered bimodal distributions and small positive values associated with heavy-tailed fluctuations.
These oscillations are in phase with the orientation autocorrelation, demonstrating that the time-dependent non-Gaussian character of particle positions is directly controlled by how the particle orientation rotates and loses memory under the combined action of torque and noise.
The number and amplitude of these sign reversals increase monotonically with activity strength, decrease with increasing trap stiffness, and vary non-monotonically with chirality.
Specifically, increasing chirality first amplifies the oscillatory behavior at intermediate values, then progressively suppresses the oscillations at large values as rapid precession averages out the circular motion; however, the excess kurtosis remains negative throughout, indicating that the position distribution never fully recovers Gaussian character; only its oscillatory transient structure is eliminated.
At long times, the excess kurtosis relaxes to a negative steady-state value, reflecting a persistent active non-Gaussian state.

In three dimensions, the behavior is qualitatively different.
The excess kurtosis again starts near zero at short times, becomes negative as activity builds up, and relaxes toward a negative steady-state value.
Crucially, however, the excess kurtosis remains negative throughout the entire dynamics, with no oscillatory crossovers to positive values.
This dimensional difference arises because the geometry of chiral trajectories in three dimensions; helical rather than circular; and the higher-dimensional orientational relaxation together prevent the kind of transient heavy-tailed fluctuations seen in two dimensions.
At intermediate times, the non-Gaussian minimum is governed primarily by activity and is largely insensitive to chirality and trap stiffness, because confinement and torque enter the kurtosis dynamics at a higher order than activity does.
At longer times, stronger chirality and stronger confinement each increase the magnitude of the negative steady-state kurtosis.
The spatial structure associated with the active non-Gaussian regime evolves from half-ring-like position distributions at weak torque to increasingly narrow band-like structures at strong torque, reflecting how fast rotational precession suppresses radial spreading and enhances anisotropic motion.

Across both dimensions, the exact analytical predictions are in excellent quantitative agreement with numerical simulations over the full range of parameters.
The framework identifies several experimentally accessible signatures.
The oscillatory excess kurtosis in two dimensions, its phase relationship with the orientation autocorrelation, the shift of the non-Gaussian minimum to earlier times with increasing activity, and the dimensional contrast between oscillatory and monotone kurtosis evolution are all directly measurable in existing experimental platforms such as synthetic circle microswimmers, chiral granular rotors, and air-driven spinners
Kümmel et al. 2013
;
López-Castaño et al. 2022
.
In summary, our results establish a rigorous theoretical foundation for characterizing higher-order fluctuations in torque-driven active systems under confinement, and open new directions for probing chirality-induced dynamics in many-body and complex environments.

To determine the higher-order statistics of the particle’s motion, we next compute the fourth order moment of the displacement. Substituting

Appendix B
Derivation of exact analytic moments in three-dimensions

Moments generator equation:

The Fokker-Planck equation satisfied by the single particle probability distribution function,
P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},t)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P

\displaystyle=D\nabla^{2}P+D_{r}{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}P+\nabla\cdot[(\mu{\textbf{r}}-v_{0}\hat{\textbf{u}})P]-\bm{\omega}\cdot{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}P\,.

(59)

Here
{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}\equiv\hat{\bm{u}}\times\nabla_{\hat{\textbf{u}}}
is equivalent to the gradient operator in the orientation vector space.
The detailed derivation of the Fokker–Planck equation (
59
) corresponding to Eqs. (
9
)–(
11
) in the absence of harmonic confinement (
\mu=0
) is presented in Pattanayak
et al.

Pattanayak et al. 2024
.
In the following sections, we have derived various moments associated with the motion of the particle.
By applying the Laplace transform to the Fokker-Planck equation,

\displaystyle-P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},0)+s\tilde{P}(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},s)=D\nabla^{2}\tilde{P}+D_{r}{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}\tilde{P}+\mu\nabla\cdot({\textbf{r}}\tilde{P})-v_{0}{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\cdot\nabla\tilde{P}-\bm{\omega}\cdot{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}\tilde{P}\,,

(60)

where
\tilde{P}(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dte^{-st}P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},t)
is the Laplace transformation of
P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},t)
and
P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},0)=\delta^{d}(\textbf{r})\delta(\hat{\textbf{u}}-\hat{\textbf{u}}_{0})
is the initial probability distribution function.
Let us consider an arbitrary function
\psi=\psi(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}})
. If we multiply Eq. (
60
) with
\psi
and integrate w.r.t
r
and
\hat{\textbf{u}}
over the whole space,

\displaystyle-\psi_{0}+s\langle\psi\rangle_{s}=D\langle\nabla^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+D_{r}\langle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\langle\hat{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}-\mu\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}+\langle\bm{\Omega}\cdot{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}\psi\rangle_{s}\,,

(61)

where
\psi_{0}=\int d\textbf{r}\int d\hat{\textbf{u}}P(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},0)\psi(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}})
and
\langle\psi\rangle_{s}=\int d\textbf{r}\int d\hat{\textbf{u}}\tilde{P}(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}},s)\psi(\textbf{r},\hat{\textbf{u}})

Let us consider the direction of the constant torque along the

\displaystyle-\psi_{0}+s\langle\psi\rangle_{s}=D\langle\nabla^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+D_{r}\langle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\langle\hat{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}-\mu\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}\psi\rangle_{s}\,.

(62)

\displaystyle\langle x(t)\rangle=\frac{-v_{0}e^{-\mu t}\left[\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right]}{\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)^{2}+\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{v_{0}e^{-2D_{r}t}\left[\sin(\omega t)\left(\omega\text{u}_{x0}-\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{y0}\right)+\cos(\omega t)\left(\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)\right]}{\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right){}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\,,

(73)

\displaystyle\langle y(t)\rangle=\frac{v_{0}e^{-\mu t}\left(\omega\text{u}_{x0}-\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{y0}\right)}{\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right){}^{2}+\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{v_{0}e^{-2D_{r}t}\left[\sin(\omega t)\left(\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)+\cos(\omega t)\left(\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right)\text{u}_{y0}-\omega\text{u}_{x0}\right)\right]}{\left(\mu-2D_{r}\right){}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\langle z(t)\rangle=-\frac{v_{0}\text{u}_{z0}\left(e^{-2D_{r}t}-e^{-\mu t}\right)}{2D_{r}-\mu}\,.

(75)

These expressions reveal two competing dissipative scales: the trap relaxation rate
\mu
and the rotational decorrelation rate
2D_{r}
. The
x
and
y
components display oscillatory relaxation reflecting chiral precession with frequency
\omega
, while the
z
–component decays monotonically since the torque does not act along that direction.

In the absence of rotational noise
D_{r}=0
,

\displaystyle\langle x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}}{\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}\left[\cos(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)+\sin(\omega t)\left(\omega\text{u}_{x0}-\mu\text{u}_{y0}\right)-e^{-\mu t}\left(\mu\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\langle y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}}{\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}\left[\cos(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{y0}-\omega\text{u}_{x0}\right)+\sin(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)-e^{-\mu t}\left(\omega\text{u}_{x0}-\mu\text{u}_{y0}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\langle z(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}\text{u}_{z0}\left(1-e^{-\mu t}\right)}{\mu}.

In this deterministic limit, the particle traces a circular or helical trajectory, depending on the initial vertical component
u_{z0}
. The trap suppresses motion exponentially in time, but a steady oscillatory component remains in the transverse plane due to the torque.

At long times, by setting
t\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\langle x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}}{\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}\left[\cos(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)+\sin(\omega t)\left(\omega\text{u}_{x0}-\mu\text{u}_{y0}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\langle y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}}{\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}\left[\cos(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{y0}-\omega\text{u}_{x0}\right)+\sin(\omega t)\left(\mu\text{u}_{x0}+\omega\text{u}_{y0}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\langle z(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}\text{u}_{z0}}{\mu}.

At long times, the motion in the
x
–
y
plane approaches a periodic orbit determined by
\omega
and the trap strength
\mu
, while the mean
z
–position saturates to a finite value. This reflects a balance between self–propulsion along the torque axis and confinement, giving a stationary helical orbit in the absence of noise.

Position-orientation cross-correlation:

To calculate position-orientation cross-correlation, we consider
\psi=\textbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}
in Eq. (
62

\nabla\psi={\hat{\textbf{u}}}
and
{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})=-2\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{s}=\frac{1}{(s+2D_{r}+\mu)}\left[\frac{v_{0}}{s}+\omega\langle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})\rangle_{s}\right]\,.

(76)

Next we consider
\psi={\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})=-(x\text{u}_{y}-y\text{u}_{x})
.

\displaystyle\nabla{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})

\displaystyle={\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}\nabla(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})={\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\hat{\textbf{u}})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})

\displaystyle=\hat{\textbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}\nabla(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})=\hat{\textbf{u}}\cdot{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}{\hat{\textbf{u}}}=0\,,

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})

\displaystyle=-2{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})\,,

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})

\displaystyle={\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(-(x\text{u}_{y}-y\text{u}_{x}))=-(x\text{u}_{x}+y\text{u}_{y})=-\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}+z\text{u}_{z}\,.

Hence from Eq. (
62
), we get

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{z}(\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}})\rangle_{s}=\frac{1}{(s+2D_{r}+\mu)}\left[-\omega\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle z\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}\right]\,.

(77)

Putting this back into Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{s}=\frac{v_{0}(s+2D_{r}+\mu)+s\,\omega^{2}\langle z\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}}{s\left[(s+2D_{r}+\mu)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right]}\,.

(78)

Following the same procedure and using the relation,
{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}\text{u}_{i}\text{u}_{j}=-6\text{u}_{i}\text{u}_{j}+2\delta_{ij}

\langle z\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}
reads

\displaystyle\langle z\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=\frac{v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}}{(s+2D_{r}+\mu)}\,,

(79)

and
\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}=\frac{2D_{r}/s+\text{u}_{z0}^{2}}{s+6D_{r}}\,.

(80)

Finally, we obtain the position–orientation cross-correlation as a function of time by performing the inverse Laplace transform of Eq. (
78

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle(t)=\frac{v_{0}}{3}\Bigg(\frac{3\left(2D_{r}+\mu\right){}^{2}+\omega^{2}}{\omega^{2}\left(2D_{r}+\mu\right)+\left(2D_{r}+\mu\right){}^{3}}+\frac{\omega^{2}\left(1-3\text{u}_{z0}^{2}\right)e^{-6tD_{r}}}{\left(4D_{r}-\mu\right)\left(\left(\mu-4D_{r}\right){}^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}\\

(81)

The quantity
\langle\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle
measures how strongly the particle’s displacement remains aligned with its propulsion direction.
In three dimensions, this correlation decays under the combined action of trap relaxation and rotational diffusion, while chirality introduces damped oscillations whose instantaneous frequency is set by the torque magnitude but whose amplitude and decay envelope are governed by the combined rate of orientational relaxation and trap confinement, as reflected in the full pole structure of Eq. (
81
).
The presence of the additional term
\langle zu_{z}\rangle
reflects the special role of the torque axis: motion parallel to the axis relaxes monotonically, whereas the transverse components precess, giving rise to oscillatory modulation.

In the steady state, Eq. (
81

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{\rm st}=\frac{v_{0}}{3}\left[\frac{2(\mu+2D_{r})}{(\mu+2D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mu+2D_{r}}\right]\,.

(82)

At long times, the cross-correlation saturates to a finite, non-zero value,
indicating persistent alignment between position and orientation even in the presence of rotational noise.
In the limit
\omega\to 0
, the oscillatory contributions vanish, recovering the standard active Brownian limit.
Conversely, increasing chirality suppresses longitudinal alignment while generating a finite transverse component, consistent with helical trajectories in steady state.

The dimensionless form of position-orientation cross correlation,

\displaystyle\langle{\tilde{\textbf{r}}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle({\tilde{t}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Pe}}{3}\left[\frac{2(\beta+2)}{(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta+2}+\frac{e^{-6{\tilde{t}}}\left(3\text{u}_{z0}^{2}-1\right)\Omega^{2}}{(\beta-4)\left((\beta-4)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right)}+\frac{e^{(\beta+2)(-{\tilde{t}})}\left(6-3(\beta+2)\text{u}_{z0}^{2}\right)}{(\beta-4)(\beta+2)}\right]

(83)

In the steady state, Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle{\tilde{\textbf{r}}}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{\rm st}=\frac{\text{Pe}}{3}\left[\frac{2(\beta+2)}{(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta+2}\right]\,.

Perpendicular position–orientation correlation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}^{\perp}\rangle=\cos{\omega t}\frac{v_{0}\omega e^{-t\left(2D_{r}+\mu\right)}\left(-4D_{r}\left(\mu-3D_{r}\right)-\text{u}_{z0}^{2}\left(4D_{r}\left(D_{r}+\mu\right)+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}{\left((\mu-4D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}\\

&-\left(\text{u}_{z0}^{2}\left(\mu-4D_{r}\right)\left(4D_{r}\left(D_{r}+\mu\right)+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\right)+\mu\left(\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\bigg)\\
&+\frac{v_{0}\omega}{3}\left(\frac{\left(3\text{u}_{z0}^{2}-1\right)e^{-6tD_{r}}}{-8\mu D_{r}+16D_{r}^{2}+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}-\frac{2}{4D_{r}\left(D_{r}+\mu\right)+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(85)

The quantity
\langle\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}^{\perp}\rangle
characterizes the coupling between the particle’s position and the component of its self-propulsion direction perpendicular to both the torque axis and the instantaneous orientation,

{\hat{\textbf{u}}}^{\perp}=-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\,u_{y}+\hat{\mathbf{y}}\,u_{x}
. This correlation captures the transverse displacement generated by chiral rotation. The full expression contains oscillatory
\cos(\omega t)
and
\sin(\omega t)
contributions, modulated by exponential damping
e^{-t(2D_{r}+\mu)}
, reflecting the competition between deterministic torque–driven precession and orientational decorrelation. The prefactors depend on the initial orientation through
u_{z0}
, demonstrating that the strength of transverse motion is maximal when the swimmer initially lies in the plane perpendicular to the torque axis, and vanishes when initially aligned with it.
The final term, which includes
e^{-6D_{r}t}
, represents the decay of higher–order orientational correlations. Altogether,
\langle\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}^{\perp}\rangle
reveals a damped helical drift orthogonal to the driving torque, which disappears in the limits
\omega\to 0
or
D_{r}\to\infty
, and persists only when chirality breaks rotational symmetry.

In the long-time limit, the perpendicular position–orientation correlation converges to

\displaystyle\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}^{\perp}\rangle_{\rm st}=\frac{-2v_{0}\omega}{3\left(4D_{r}\left(D_{r}+\mu\right)+\mu^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}

(86)

This finite value reflects a persistent chiral drift transverse to the propulsion direction.
The correlation is antisymmetric in
\omega
, indicating that the sign of the transverse alignment directly encodes the handedness of the torque.
The magnitude decreases with increasing rotational noise
D_{r}
or trap stiffness
\mu
, both of which suppress coherent precessional motion.
In the limit
\omega\to 0
, the correlation vanishes, consistent with the disappearance of chirality-induced circulation.
Thus, at steady state, confinement and rotational diffusion attenuate the helical bias, yet a residual transverse displacement persists whenever chirality breaks mirror symmetry.

(88)

This steady value highlights the persistent transverse bias induced by chirality.
The correlation is antisymmetric in
\Omega

Its magnitude decreases with increasing confinement strength

In the achiral limit

Pattanayak et al. 2024
.
Thus, a finite transverse displacement persists at long times whenever a torque acts on an active particle in a trap, constituting a steady-state signature of chiral active dynamics.

(96)

The expression for

contains both passive and active contributions.
The passive part represents thermal diffusion within the harmonic trap and corresponds to the equilibrium Brownian variance scaled by

.
The active part, proportional to
{\rm Pe}^{2}

The first term,

, represents the passive thermal contribution corresponding to the equilibrium variance of a Brownian particle confined in a harmonic trap.
The second term, proportional to
{\rm Pe}^{2}

This active contribution decreases with increasing
\beta

In the limit
{\rm Pe}\to 0

\beta\to 0

Hence,
\langle\tilde{z}^{2}\rangle_{\rm st}
quantifies how activity, rotational relaxation, and confinement jointly determine the steady-state amplitude of fluctuations along the torque axis.

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+2D_{r}/s-2\omega\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}\,,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}+2\omega\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+2D_{r}/s\,.

(104)

\displaystyle\textrm{Considering $\psi=\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}$},

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}+\omega\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle\text{u}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}+2\omega\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}-\omega/s+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}\,,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}-2\omega\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}-\omega/s+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}\,.

(105)

The expressions of
\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}
and
\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}
,

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\Big]\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}=\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\Big(\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+2D_{r}/s\Big)-2\omega\Big(\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}-\omega/s+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}\Big)\,,

(106)

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\Big]\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\Big(\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}-\omega/s+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}\Big)+2\omega\Big(\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+2D_{r}/s\Big)\,.

(107)

Here, we used the normalization condition
\text{u}_{x}^{2}+\text{u}_{y}^{2}+\text{u}_{z}^{2}=1
, and in Laplace space, it follows that
\langle\text{u}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{s}=1/s-\langle\text{u}_{x}^{2}\rangle_{s}-\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{s}
. The final expression of
\langle x^{2}\rangle_{s}
,

\displaystyle\langle x^{2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{2v_{0}^{2}\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}\left(2D_{r}+s\text{u}_{x0}^{2}\right)-2s\omega\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}(6D_{r}+s)+2\omega^{2}\left(4D_{r}-s\text{u}_{z0}^{2}+s\right)\right)\right)}{(s(6D_{r}+s))\left(\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\right)}

(108)

\displaystyle+\frac{2v_{0}^{2}\left(-\omega\left(\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}(6D_{r}+s)+\omega\left(2\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+\text{u}_{z0}^{2}-1\right)\right)\right)}{\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}+\frac{2D}{s(2\mu+s)}\,.

(109)

Averaging over initial orientations and performing inverse Laplace transform,

\displaystyle\langle x^{2}\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{D\left(1-e^{-2\mu t}\right)}{\mu}+\frac{v_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{2D_{r}+\mu}{(2D_{r}+\mu)^{2}+\omega^{2}}+\frac{(\mu-2D_{r})e^{-2\mu t}}{(\mu-2D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}}\right)}{3\mu}

\displaystyle+\frac{2v_{0}^{2}e^{-t(2D_{r}+\mu)}\left(\left(4D_{r}^{2}-\mu^{2}-\omega^{2}\right)\cos(\omega t)-4D_{r}\omega\sin(\omega t)\right)}{3\left((\mu-2D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\left((2D_{r}+\mu)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}\,.

(110)

In dimensionless form:

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{x}^{2}\rangle(\tilde{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1-e^{-2\beta\tilde{t}}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{Pe}^{2}}{3\beta}\left[\frac{\beta+2}{(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}+\frac{(\beta-2)e^{-2\beta\tilde{t}}}{(\beta-2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\,\mathrm{Pe}^{2}}{3}\,\frac{e^{-(\beta+2)\tilde{t}}\left[(4-\beta^{2}-\Omega^{2})\cos(\Omega\tilde{t})-4\Omega\sin(\Omega\tilde{t})\right]}{\left[(\beta-2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right]\left[(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right]}\,.

(111)

At long times (
\tilde{t}\to\infty
), the exponentials vanish and the steady state is

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{x}^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}=\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{\mathrm{Pe}^{2}(\beta+2)}{3\beta[(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}]}\,,

(112)

shows that confinement(
\beta
) limits both thermal and active fluctuations, while torque(
\Omega
) suppresses the active contribution by averaging out directed motion. For small torque or weak trapping, activity enhances the steady-state spread, whereas strong torque or confinement localizes the particle near the trap center.

Calculation of
\left\langle y^{2}\right\rangle_{s}

Using
r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}
, we can write
\left\langle y^{2}\right\rangle_{s}=\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle_{s}-\left\langle x^{2}\right\rangle_{s}-\left\langle z^{2}\right\rangle_{s}
, gives

\displaystyle\langle y^{2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\omega^{2}v_{0}^{2}\left(16D_{r}^{2}+8\mu D_{r}+2D_{r}s\left(6\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+\text{u}_{z0}^{2}+3\right)+s\left(2\mu+2s\text{u}_{x0}^{2}-s\text{u}_{z0}^{2}+s-2\mu\text{u}_{z0}^{2}\right)\right)}{s(6D_{r}+s)(2\mu+s)\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\omega\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}v_{0}^{2}(2\beta+10D_{r}+3s)}{(2\beta+s)\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2v_{0}^{2}(6D_{r}+s)(\mu+2D_{r}+s)\left(2D_{r}+s\text{u}_{y0}^{2}\right)}{s(2\mu+s)\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}+\frac{2D}{s(2\mu+s)}\,.

(113)

Taking inverse Laplace transform and averaging over initial orientations,

\displaystyle\langle y^{2}\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{2D_{r}+\mu}{(2D_{r}+\mu)^{2}+\omega^{2}}+\frac{(\mu-2D_{r})e^{-2\mu t}}{(\mu-2D_{r})^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\right)}{3\mu}+\frac{D\left(3-3e^{-2\mu t}\right)}{3\mu}

\displaystyle+\frac{2v_{0}^{2}e^{-t(2D_{r}+\mu)}\left(\left(4D_{r}^{2}-\mu^{2}-\omega^{2}\right)\cos(\omega t)-4D_{r}\omega\sin(\omega t)\right)}{3\left((\mu-2D_{r})^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\left((2D_{r}+\mu)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}\,.

(114)

In dimensionless form:

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{y}^{2}\rangle(\tilde{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(1-e^{-2\beta\tilde{t}}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{Pe}^{2}}{3\beta}\left[\frac{\beta+2}{(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}+\frac{(\beta-2)\,e^{-2\beta\tilde{t}}}{(\beta-2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\,\mathrm{Pe}^{2}}{3}\,\frac{e^{-(\beta+2)\tilde{t}}\left[\left(4-\beta^{2}-\Omega^{2}\right)\cos(\Omega\tilde{t})-4\Omega\sin(\Omega\tilde{t})\right]}{\left[(\beta-2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right]\left[(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right]}\,.

(115)

As torque acts along
z
-axis, the dynamics in the
x-y
plane remain radially symmetric, hence
\langle\tilde{y}^{2}\rangle(\tilde{t})=\langle\tilde{x}^{2}\rangle(\tilde{t})
. At long times (
\tilde{t}\to\infty
), the exponential terms vanish and the steady state is

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{y}^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}=\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{\mathrm{Pe}^{2}(\beta+2)}{3\beta\big[(\beta+2)^{2}+\Omega^{2}\big]}\,.

(116)

Calculation of
\left\langle xy\right\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle(s+2\mu)\langle xy\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\Big[\langle x\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}+\langle y\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}\Big]\,.

(117)

The expression of
\langle x\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}
is already found in the previous section.
y\text{u}_{x}
satisfies the following pair of equations,

\displaystyle\Big(s+\mu+2D_{r}\Big)\langle y\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle y\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}\,,

(118)

\displaystyle\Big(s+\mu+2D_{r}\Big)\langle y\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle y\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{s}\,,

(119)

\langle y\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}
satisfies,

\displaystyle\langle y\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)^{2}+\omega^{2}\Big]=v_{0}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle\text{u}_{y}^{2}\rangle_{s}\Big]\,.

(120)

The final expression of
\langle xy\rangle_{s}
,

\displaystyle\langle xy\rangle_{s}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}\left(\tau\left(2\text{u}_{x0}^{2}+\text{u}_{z0}^{2}-1\right)(2\mu+10D_{r}+3s)+2\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}(6D_{r}+s)(\mu+2D_{r}+s)-4\omega^{2}\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{y0}\right)}{(2\mu+s)\left((6D_{r}+s)^{2}+4\omega^{2}\right)\left((\mu+2D_{r}+s)^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)}\,.

(121)

Averaging over all possible initial conditions, we get
\langle xy\rangle_{s}=0
.
Since the applied torque acts along the
z
-axis, the dynamics of the particle remain
rotationally symmetric in the
x
–
y
plane. As a result, cross correlations between
orthogonal directions, such as
\langle xy\rangle
, vanish after averaging over all possible
initial orientations. Although transient correlations
\langle xy\rangle(t)
may arise for specific
initial conditions—reflecting the instantaneous phase difference between
x
and
y
oscillations
induced by torque—the isotropy of the
x
–
y
plane ensures that these correlations cancel out
in the ensemble average. Therefore,
\langle xy\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}=0
signifies that the steady state
preserves rotational symmetry about the torque axis, with no preferred azimuthal direction.

Calculation of
\left\langle xz\right\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle(s+2\mu)\langle xz\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\Big(\langle x\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}+\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}\,,

\displaystyle\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{y}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}\,.

Solving the above equations for
\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}
and
\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}
,

\displaystyle\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)^{2}+\omega^{2}\Big]=v_{0}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)^{2}+\omega^{2}\Big]=v_{0}\Big[\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle\text{u}_{y}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}+\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{z0}\,,

\displaystyle\Big(s+6D_{r}\Big)\langle\text{u}_{y}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}-\omega\langle\text{u}_{z}\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}=\text{u}_{y0}\text{u}_{z0}\,.

If we average over all possible initial orientations,
\langle\text{u}_{x0}\text{u}_{z0}\rangle=0=\langle\text{u}_{y0}\text{u}_{z0}\rangle
, which gives
\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=0=\langle\text{u}_{y}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}
resulting in
\langle z\text{u}_{x}\rangle_{s}=0=\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}
.

\langle x\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}
satisfies,

\displaystyle\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle x\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{x}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=0\,.

(122)

Hence,
\langle xz\rangle_{s}=0
if averaged over initial orientations.
Since the torque acts along the
z
-axis, the particle’s motion in the
x
–
y
plane
is decoupled from that along
z
. Consequently, the cross correlations between in-plane
(
x
or
y
) and out-of-plane (
z
) components vanish after averaging over all possible
initial orientations. While individual trajectories may show finite
\langle xz\rangle(t)

due to specific initial alignment of the propulsion direction, ensemble averaging restores
symmetry about the torque axis, leading to
\langle xz\rangle_{\mathrm{st}}=0
.
This reflects the absence of any preferred tilt or coupling between transverse and axial
fluctuations in the steady state.

Calculation of
\left\langle yz\right\rangle_{s}

The cross-correlation between the
y
and
z
components satisfies

\displaystyle(s+2\mu)\langle yz\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\Big(\langle y\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}+\langle z\text{u}_{y}\rangle_{s}\Big)\,.

(123)

Here,
\langle yu_{z}\rangle_{s}
obeys

\displaystyle\Big(s+2D_{r}+\mu\Big)\langle y\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\langle\text{u}_{y}\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}=0\,.

(124)

The corresponding expression for
\langle zu_{y}\rangle_{s}
has been obtained in the previous section.
Averaging over all possible initial orientations gives
\langle yz\rangle_{s}=0
.
The vanishing of
\langle yz\rangle_{s}
has the same physical interpretation as that of
\langle xz\rangle_{s}
.

The mean-square displacement (MSD) of a three-dimensional chiral active Brownian particle (ABP) naturally decomposes into two independent contributions: one parallel to the torque axis,
\langle z^{2}(t)\rangle
, and the other in the plane perpendicular to the torque,
\langle\mathbf{r}_{\perp}^{2}(t)\rangle=\langle x^{2}(t)\rangle+\langle y^{2}(t)\rangle
.
These components are obtained by performing the inverse Laplace transform of the corresponding expressions given in Eq. (
94
), Eq. (
109
), and Eq. (
113
).
The resulting exact expressions for the parallel and perpendicular MSD components, expressed in dimensionless form, are presented in the main text (see Eq. (
14
) and Eq. (
15
)).

B.2
Fourth order moment of displacement

To compute the fourth-order displacement moment, we substitute
\psi=\mathbf{r}^{4}
into the
moment-generating Eq. (
62
). This leads to the following relation:

\displaystyle(s+4\mu)\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{4}\right\rangle_{s}=20D\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+4v_{0}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\right\rangle_{s}\,,

(125)

where
\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}
is already calculated in previous section. The second term can be evaluated following the steps outlined below. During this calculation, we encounter the operator
R_{z}\psi
which can be determined using the same procedure employed in the previous section for obtaining
\langle\textbf{r}\cdot\hat{\textbf{u}}\rangle_{s}
.

Calculation of

\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\right\rangle_{s}

To determine the mixed moment
\langle\mathbf{r}^{2}\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle_{s}
,
we substitute
\psi=\mathbf{r}^{2}\,\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}
into the moment-generating
Eq. (
62
). The resulting equation reads:

\displaystyle\left[s+2D_{r}+3\mu+\frac{\omega^{2}}{s+2D_{r}+3\mu}\right]\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle_{s}=10D\left[\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle_{s}+\frac{\omega\langle R_{z}({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})\rangle_{s}}{s+2D_{r}+3\mu}\right]

\displaystyle+v_{0}\left[2\left\langle({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+\frac{\omega\langle R_{z}({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}\rangle_{s}}{s+2D_{r}+3\mu}+\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\rangle_{s}\right]+\frac{\omega^{2}\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}z\text{u}_{z}\rangle_{s}}{s+2D_{r}+3\mu}\,.

(126)

For the mixed moment
\left\langle\mathbf{r}^{2}zu_{z}\right\rangle_{s}
,
we substitute
\psi=\mathbf{r}^{2}zu_{z}
into the moment-generating Eq. (
62
). The resulting relation reads:

\displaystyle\left[s+2D_{r}+3\mu\right]\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}=10D\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}+2v_{0}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}\,.

(127)

For the correlation term
\left\langle\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}zu_{z}\right\rangle_{s}
,
we set
\psi=\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}zu_{z}
in the moment-generating Eq. (
62
). The resulting equation is

\displaystyle\left[s+6D_{r}+2\mu+\frac{\omega^{2}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}\right]\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}=2D\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+2D_{r}\left\langle z^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{\omega^{2}\left\langle z^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}+v_{0}\left[\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+\frac{\omega\left\langle R_{z}\left({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2})\right\rangle_{s}\right.}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}\right]\,,

(128)

and

\displaystyle\left(s+6D_{r}+2\mu\right)\left\langle R_{z}\left({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}z\text{u}_{z}\right)\right\rangle_{s}=v_{0}\left\langle R_{z}\left({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right)\right\rangle_{s}-\omega\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}+\omega\left\langle z^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}\,.

(129)

For the correlation term
\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}
,
we set
\psi={\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}
in the moment-generating Eq. (
62
). The resulting equation is

\displaystyle\left[s+12D_{r}+\mu+\frac{\omega^{2}}{s+12D_{r}+\mu}\right]\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}=2D_{r}\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle_{s}+4D_{r}\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{\omega^{2}\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}^{3}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+12D_{r}+\mu}+\frac{2\omega D_{r}\left\langle R_{z}({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})\right\rangle_{s}}{s+12D_{r}+\mu}\,,

(130)

and

\displaystyle{\left[s+12D_{r}+\mu\right]\left\langle R_{z}\left({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right)\right\rangle_{s}=2D_{r}\left\langle R_{z}({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})\right\rangle_{s}}

\displaystyle-\omega\left\langle({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{S}+\Omega\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}^{3}\right\rangle_{s}\,.

(131)

Now,

\displaystyle\left[s+12D_{r}+\mu\right]\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}^{3}\right\rangle_{s}=6D_{r}\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}+v_{0}\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{4}\right\rangle_{s}\,,

(132)

with

\left[s+20D_{r}\right]\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{4}\right\rangle_{s}=12D_{r}\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+\text{u}_{z0}^{4}\,,

\displaystyle\left(s+6D_{r}+2\beta\right)\left\langle z^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}=2D\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+2D_{r}\left\langle z^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+2v_{0}\left\langle z\text{u}_{z}^{3}\right\rangle_{s}\,,

(133)

\displaystyle\left(s+6D_{r}+2\beta\right)\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}=2D_{r}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+6D\left\langle\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+2v_{0}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}\,.

(134)

Calculation of

\left\langle({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}\right\rangle_{s}

To evaluate the orientationally coupled displacement moment

\langle(\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}\rangle_{s}
,
we substitute
\psi=(\mathbf{r}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}
into the moment-generating Eq. (
62
).
The corresponding relation reads:

\displaystyle\left[s+6D_{r}+2\mu+\frac{4\omega^{2}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}\right]\left\langle({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})^{2}\right\rangle_{s}=\frac{2D}{s}+2D_{r}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}+2v_{0}\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\omega v_{0}\left\langle R_{z}({\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}})\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}+6\omega^{2}\frac{\left\langle{\textbf{r}}\cdot{\hat{\textbf{u}}}z\text{u}_{z}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}+2\omega^{2}\frac{\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}-\frac{2\omega^{2}\left\langle z^{2}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\omega^{2}\left\langle{\textbf{r}}^{2}\text{u}_{z}^{2}\right\rangle_{s}}{s+6D_{r}+2\mu}\,,
