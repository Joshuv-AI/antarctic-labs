Abstract

The growth is governed by the structural condition 0 < i a ≤ t ​ g ′ ​ ( t ) / g ⁡ ( t ) ≤ s a < 1 . 0<i_{a}\leq tg^{\prime}(t)/g(t)\leq s_{a}<1. We obtain two types of regularity results: pointwise Wolff potential estimates for the gradient of solutions in the singular regime i a ∈ ( n − 1 2 ​ n − 1 , 1 ) i_{a}\in\big(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1\big) , and Lipschitz regularity of the solutions in the regime i a ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) i_{a}\in(0,1) . 1.
Introduction

We investigate gradient estimates for solutions to the following quasilinear elliptic equation with measure data under Orlicz-type growth:

(1.1)

-\DIV{\mathcal{A}}(x,Du)=\mu,

in a bounded open subset
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
n\geq 2
and
\mu
is an arbitrary bounded measure on
\Omega
. The vector field
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is assumed to be
continuously differentiable with respect to the gradient variable and to satisfy the
following structural conditions: for all
x,x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.2)

\begin{split}|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)|+|D_{\xi}\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)|\,|\xi|&\leq\Lambda\,g(|\xi|),\\
\langle D_{\xi}\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle&\geq\lambda\,g^{\prime}(|\xi|)\,|\eta|^{2},\\
|\mathcal{A}(x_{1},\xi)-\mathcal{A}(x_{2},\xi)|&\leq w(|x_{1}-x_{2}|)\,g(|\xi|),\end{split}

with
0<\lambda\leq 1\leq\Lambda
, and

w:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a nondecreasing, subadditive modulus of continuity with

(1.3)

w(0)=0,\qquad\lim_{\rho\to 0}w(\rho)=0,

and satisfies the Dini-type integrability condition

(1.4)

\int_{0}^{R}\frac{w(\rho)^{\frac{2i_{a}}{(1+i_{a})^{2}}}}{\rho}\,d\rho<\infty\qquad\text{for every }R<\infty.

Throughout this paper,
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-function satisfying

(1.5)

0<i_{a}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}=:s_{a}<1,\qquad G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau,

where
G
is an
N
-function (see Section
2
).
In addition, we work under the assumption that there exists constant
c>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

(1.6)

g(t)\geq c\,t^{i_{a}}.

We see that typical examples of
g
include the power-type growth
g(t)=t^{p-1}
with
1<p<2
, the borderline logarithmic growth
g(t)=t^{p-1}\log(e+t)
with
1<p<2
, and the double-phase growth
g(t)=t^{p-1}+t^{q-1}
with
1<p\leq q<2
.

The study of potential theory can be traced back to
[
32
,
33
]
. A fundamental step in nonlinear potential theory was made by Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
24
]
, who derived pointwise Wolff potential estimates for solutions to
p
-Laplace equations with nonnegative measures. Subsequently, alternative proofs and extensions of these estimates have been developed by Trudinger and Wang
[
40
,
41
]
, allowing one to treat more general subelliptic operators. Further proofs of pointwise potential estimates can be found in
[
22
,
27
]
, where an approach covering the case of general signed measures was developed. For more results concerning pointwise estimates of solutions, we refer to
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
25
,
35
,
36
]
.

The first extension of pointwise potential estimates of solutions to the corresponding gradient estimates was obtained in
[
34
]
, where Riesz potential estimates for gradients of solutions to the
p
-Laplace equation were established in the linear case
p=2
. For
p>2
, Wolff potential estimates for gradients of solutions were established in
[
22
]
. These results were subsequently refined in
[
28
]
, where gradient estimates were further expressed in terms of Riesz potentials. Moreover, Duzaar and Mingione
[
21
]
obtained Riesz potential estimates for gradients in the subquadratic range
p\in(2-\frac{1}{n},\,2)
. Later, Baroni
[
2
]
extended these results to elliptic equations with Orlicz growth, establishing Riesz potential estimates for gradients of solutions of the form

g(\left|\nabla u(x_{0})\right|)\leq Cg\!\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2R)}|\nabla u|\,dx\right)+C\mathcal{I}_{1}^{2R}(|\mu|)(x),

under the structural assumption

1\leq i_{a}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}=:s_{a},

where

\mathcal{I}_{1}^{2R}(|\mu|)(x):=\int^{2R}_{0}\frac{|\mu|(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n-1}}\,\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

denotes the Riesz potential of the measure
|\mu|
. In addition, Wolff potential estimates for the Orlicz growth equations under the assumptions that
1-\frac{1}{n}<i_{a}\leq s_{a}<1
were studied by Yao and Zhou in
[
44
]
. We refer the readers to the comprehensive survey
[
29
]
for further background on potential estimates and their applications.

In a more recent work, Nguyen and Phuc
[
37
]
investigated the singular range

\frac{3n-2}{2n-1}<p\leq 2-\frac{1}{n},

for the
p
-Laplace equation with Dini-continuous coefficients, and established Wolff potential estimates for gradients of solutions.
Dong and Zhu
[
20
]
subsequently strengthened the gradient estimates by replacing Wolff potentials with Riesz potentials, and further obtained Lipschitz regularity of solutions for
p\in(1,2)
. For the lower range
p\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
, Riesz potential estimates for gradients were obtained in
[
39
]
. We also mention that related gradient estimates for
p
-Laplace type equations with more general Dini-continuous coefficients can be found in
[
43
]
. As potential estimates plays an important role in regularity theory, the study of gradient potential estimates has attracted significant interest and has been extended to a variety of equations
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
30
,
42
]
.

The papers mentioned above motivate the study of analogous questions for elliptic equations with Orlicz growth in the regime
i_{a}<1-\frac{1}{n}
. However, as shown in
[
21
]
, when
0<i_{a}<1-\frac{1}{n}
, a distributional solution to (
1.1
) with bounded measure data may fail to belong to
W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. To address this, a weaker notion of derivative based on truncations is considered, following the approach introduced in
[
37
]
. For
k>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the standard truncation is defined by

T_{k}(s):=\max\{-k,\min\{s,k\}\}.

If
u
satisfies
T_{k}(u)\in W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
for every
k>0
, then there exists a measurable vector field
Z_{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\nabla T_{k}(u)=\chi_{\{|u|<k\}}\,Z_{u}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

see
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
. The vector field
Z_{u}
is referred to as the
generalized gradient
of
u
and denoted by
\nabla u
. We point out here that if
u
is a distributional solution to the Dirichlet problem

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\DIV\left({\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\right)=\mu&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

such that for every
k>0
, the truncated function
T_{k}(u)
belongs to
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
and satisfies

-\DIV\big({\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla T_{k}(u))\big)=\mu_{k}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

for some finite measure
\mu_{k}
with
\mu_{k}\to\mu
and
|\mu_{k}|\to|\mu|
weakly in the sense of measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then the pointwise estimate (
1.9
) holds almost everywhere for the generalized gradient
\nabla u
. In particular, any
renormalized solution
to (
1.7
) satisfies the above conditions, so the pointwise estimate always applies.
For the existence of renormalized solutions to elliptic equations with Orlicz growth and measure data, we refer to
[
6
]
. See also
[
11
]
for several equivalent definitions of renormalized solutions.

Our main results in this manuscript consist two types of regularity estimates for solutions to (
1.1
): pointwise Wolff potential estimates for the gradient in the singular regime
i_{a}\in\big(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1\big)
, and Lipschitz regularity in the regime
i_{a}\in(0,1)
. Since the gradient of solutions
\nabla u
may not belong to
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, we need to replace the standard mean oscillation

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\rho}|\,dx

by the quantity

\phi(x,\rho)=\left(\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u-q|^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}},\quad\gamma<1.

Such modifications have been carried out in
[
16
,
18
,
17
,
19
,
37
,
39
]
. To establish decay estimates for the excess functional
\phi(x,\rho)
(Propisition
3.4
), it is crucial to obtain comparison estimates of the form
\left(\fint_{B_{2r}}(\nabla u-\nabla w)^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, where
w
is a solution to the homogeneous equation

-\DIV\left({\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla w)\right)=0

with condition
w=u
on
\partial B_{2r}
(see Lemma
3.1
). We remark that in Lemma
3.1
, when
i_{a}\in(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1)
, the right-hand side
\gamma_{0}
can be taken up to
1-i_{a}
, while for
i_{a}\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2n-1})
only
\gamma_{0}<1-i_{a}
is attainable. This is the reason in the case
i_{a}\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2n-1})
, we can only derive Lipschitz estimates for solutions. Our arguments are much influenced by
[
16
,
37
]
. However, in contrast to the
p
-Laplace case, a more delicate analysis is required due to the non-homogeneity of the operator, which prevents the direct use of standard scaling arguments. Moreover, we need a reverse Hölder inequality for
\nabla w
(Lemma
3.3
), which, to the best of our knowledge, has not appeared in the literature. Achieving this requires not only adapting the classical iteration lemma to the Orlicz framework (Lemma
2.3
), but also carefully combining several additional auxiliary lemmas. In addition, the non-homogeneous nature of Orlicz growth requires an extra structural condition (
1.6
) to obtain the desired decay estimates for the excess functional
\phi(x,\rho)
(Propisition
3.4
).

A (weak) solution to (
1.1
) is a function
u\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,G}(\Omega)
such that

(1.8)

\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d\mu

holds for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Our main results are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Interior pointwise gradient estimate)
.

Let
i_{a}\in\big(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1\big)
. If
u\in W^{1,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a solution to (
1.1
),
and assume that (
1.2
)–(
1.6
) hold.
Then there exists a constant

C=C(n,i_{a},s_{a},\lambda,\Lambda,\omega),

such that for every Lebesgue point
x\in\Omega
of the vector–valued function

\nabla u
and for any
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1]
, the pointwise estimate

(1.9)

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\Big[\mathbf{W}^{R}_{i_{a},g}(|\mu|)(x)\Big]^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}+C\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x)}\left(|\nabla u(y)|+1\right)^{1-i_{a}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{1-i_{a}}}

holds almost everywhere. Here the truncated Wolff potential is defined by

\mathbf{W}^{R}_{i_{a},g}(|\mu|)(x):=\int_{0}^{R}\left(g^{-1}\!\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{\rho}(x))}{\rho^{\,n-1}}\right)\right)^{i_{a}}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}.

Theorem 1.2

(Interior Lipschitz estimate)
.

Let
i_{a}\in(0,1)
. If
u\in W^{1,G}_{\rm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a solution to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)–(
1.6
) hold, then there exists a constant
C=C(n,i_{a},s_{a},\Lambda,\lambda,\omega)
such that the estimate

(1.10)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x))}\leq C\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{R}(|\mu|)\big\|^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}+CR^{-\frac{n}{1-i_{a}}}\||\nabla u|+1\|_{L^{1-i_{a}}(B_{R}(x))}

holds for any
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1]
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the power–type case
g(t)=t^{p-1}
with
1<p<2
, the structural conditions
(
1.2
)–(
1.6
) are satisfied with

i_{a}=s_{a}=p-1.

In this situation, equation (
1.1
) reduces to the
p
–Laplace equation

-\DIV\big(a(x)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)=\mu,

where
a(x)
is a Dini continuous function in
\Omega
. Moreover, in this case the truncated Orlicz–Wolff potential

\mathbf{W}^{R}_{i_{a},g}
is exactly the truncated Riesz potential

\mathcal{I}^{R}_{1}(|\mu|)(x)=\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{\rho}(x))}{\rho^{\,n-1}}\right)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}.

Consequently, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
recover the interior pointwise gradient estimate and the interior Lipschitz estimate for solutions to the
p
–Laplace equation with measure data obtained in
[
20
]
.

2.
Preliminart Lemmas

In this section we list some definitions and preliminary lemmas. If not specified, a constant
C
is a positive constant, possibly changing line by line. We denote by
(\cdot)_{B_{R}(x)}
the average over the ball
B_{R}(x)
.

A function
G:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function
if it has the form

(2.1)

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau

for some non-decreasing, continuous function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
that is not identically zero. A Young function
G
is an
N
-function
if
0<G(t)<\infty
for all
t>0
and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{G(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{t}{G(t)}=\infty.

The
conjugate function

G^{\ast}
of an
N
-function
G
is defined by

G^{\ast}(\tau)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\bigl(t\tau-G(t)\bigr),\quad\tau\geq 0.

It is well-known that
G^{\ast}
is also an
N
-function, and Young’s inequality holds:

(2.2)

t\tau\leq\epsilon G(t)+C(\epsilon)G^{\ast}(\tau),\quad\text{for all }t,\tau\geq 0\text{ and any }\epsilon>0.

Moreover, the conjugate function satisfies

G^{\ast}(g(t))\leq C\,G(t).

A Young function
G
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition if

G(2t)\leq C\,G(t)\quad\text{for some }C>0,

and the
\nabla_{2}
-condition if

G(t)\leq\frac{G(\theta t)}{2\theta}\quad\text{for some }\theta>1\text{ and all }t>0.

From
[
10
, Proposition 2.9]
and
[
45
, Lemma 1.9]
, we know that
G\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
. The following lemma, which can be found in
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
, describes the scaling properties of
G
and
g^{-1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
G
and
g
satisfy (
1.5
). Then, for all
\lambda\leq 1
,

\lambda^{s_{a}+1}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{i_{a}+1}G(t),\quad c\,\lambda^{1/i_{a}}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/s_{a}}g^{-1}(t).

For all
\lambda\geq 1
,

\lambda^{i_{a}+1}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{s_{a}+1}G(t),\quad c\,\lambda^{1/s_{a}}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/i_{a}}g^{-1}(t).

The Orlicz space
L^{G}(\Omega)
is defined as

L^{G}(\Omega):=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:\varrho_{G}(u)<\infty\},

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{G}\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\},

where

\varrho_{G}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx

is called
\varrho_{G}
-modular. We define the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
as follows:

W^{1,G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W_{\rm{loc}}^{1,1}(\Omega):|u|,|\nabla u|\in L^{G}(\Omega)\right\},

where the gradient is understood in the distributional sense. The norm on this space is given by

\|u\|_{W^{1,G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{G}\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)+\varrho_{G}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

The space
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
denotes the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with respect to this norm.

The following lemma comes from
[
44
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies
(1.2)–(1.7)
with
s_{a}<1
, and let
G
be defined in (
2.1
). Then there exist constants
C=C(n,i_{a},s_{a})>0
, such that the following holds for all

\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

(i)

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

(2.3)

\begin{split}G(|\xi-\eta|)&\leq C\,\varepsilon^{\frac{i_{a}-1}{1+i_{a}}}\frac{g(|\xi|+|\eta|)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}\,|\xi-\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon G(|\eta|)\\
&\leq C\,\varepsilon^{\frac{i_{a}-1}{1+i_{a}}}\bigl({\mathcal{A}}(x,\xi)-{\mathcal{A}}(x,\eta)\bigr)\cdot(\xi-\eta)+\varepsilon\,G(|\eta|),\end{split}

and

(2.4)

\bigl({\mathcal{A}}(x,\xi)-{\mathcal{A}}(x,\eta)\bigr)\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq C\,|V(\xi)-V(\eta)|^{2},

where

V(z):=\sqrt{\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}}\,z.

(ii)

We have

(2.5)

C\,\frac{g(|\xi|+|\eta|)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}\,|\xi-\eta|^{2}\leq|V(\xi)-V(\eta)|^{2}\leq C\,\frac{g(|\xi|+|\eta|)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}\,|\xi-\eta|^{2},

and

(2.6)

C\,\sqrt{\frac{g(|\xi|+|\eta|)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}}\,|\xi-\eta|^{2}\leq\bigl(V(\xi)-V(\eta)\bigr)\cdot(\xi-\eta)\leq C\,\sqrt{\frac{g(|\xi|+|\eta|)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}}\,|\xi-\eta|^{2}.

The proof of the following iteration lemma in the standard case can be found in
[
23
, Lemma 6.1]
. For the sake of completeness, we include a proof in the setting of Orlicz-growth equations.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\rho_{1}>\rho>0
with
B_{\rho_{1}}\Subset\Omega
,
\xi\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,G}(\Omega)
, and let
G
be defined as above.
Assume for all
\rho\leq t<s\leq\rho_{1}
, we have

\int_{B_{t}}G(\nabla\xi)\,dx\leq\theta\int_{B_{s}}G(|\nabla\xi|)\,dx+\int_{B_{s}}G\left(\frac{A}{s-t}\right)\,dx.

with
A\geq 0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
\theta
and
s_{a}
, such that

\int_{B_{\rho}}G(\nabla\xi)\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{\rho_{1}}}G\left(\frac{A}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\right)\,dx.

Proof.

Fix a parameter
\tau<1
, to be chosen later.
Define a sequence

t_{k}:=\rho+(1-\tau^{k})(\rho_{1}-\rho),\qquad k=0,1,2,\cdots.

Then
t_{0}=\rho
,
t_{k}\to\rho_{1}
, and

t_{k+1}-t_{k}=\tau^{k}(1-\tau)(\rho_{1}-\rho).

Applying the assmution with
t=t_{k}
and
s=t_{k+1}
, we obtain

\int_{B_{t_{k}}}G(|\nabla\xi|)\,dx\leq\theta\int_{B_{t_{k+1}}}G(|\nabla\xi|)\,dx+c\int_{B_{t_{k+1}}}G\!\left(\frac{A}{\tau^{k}(1-\tau)(\rho_{1}-\rho)}\right)\,dx.

Denote

Z(t_{k})=\int_{B_{t_{k}}}G(|\nabla\xi|)\,dx.

Iterating the above inequality yields, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

Z(\rho)=Z(t_{0})\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\theta^{k}\int_{B_{\rho_{1}}}G\!\left(\frac{A\tau^{-k}}{(1-\tau)(\rho_{1}-\rho)}\right)\,dx+\theta^{m}Z(t_{m}).

Since
Z
is bounded and
\theta\in(0,1)
, letting
m\to\infty
gives

Z(\rho)\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta^{k}\int_{B_{\rho_{1}}}G\!\left(\frac{\tau^{-k}A}{(1-\tau)(\rho_{1}-\rho)}\right)\,dx.

By Lemma
2.1
, we known that there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
s_{a}
,
such that

G(\lambda t)\leq C\,\lambda^{s_{a}+1}G(t)\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\geq 1,\ t>0.

Consequently,

G\!\left(\frac{\tau^{-k}A}{(1-\tau)(\rho_{1}-\rho)}\right)\leq\frac{\tau^{-k(1+s_{a})}}{(1-\tau)^{1+s_{a}}}G\!\left(\frac{A}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\right).

Therefore,

Z(\rho)\leq\frac{C}{(1-\tau)^{1+s_{a}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\theta\,\tau^{-(s_{a}+1)}\bigr)^{k}\int_{B_{\rho_{1}}}G\!\left(\frac{A}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\right)\,dx.

Choosing
\tau>\theta^{\frac{1}{s_{a}+1}}
such that
\theta\,\tau^{-(s_{a}+1)}<1
, we deduce that

Z(\rho)=\int_{B_{\rho}}G(|\nabla\xi|)\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{\rho_{1}}}G\!\left(\frac{A}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\right)\,dx,

where
C
depends only on
\theta
and
s_{a}
.

3.
Pointwise gradient estimates

The section is devoted to the proof of pointwise gradient estimates for solutions of (
1.1
). Let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
) and
B_{2r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
. We consider the unique solution
w
to the equation

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\DIV\left({\mathcal{A}}(x,\nabla w)\right)=0&\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{2r}(x_{0}),\\
w=u&\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial B_{2r}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

We shall derive the following estimate.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
w
be a solution to (
3.1
) and assume that
i_{a}\in(0,1)
. Then for any
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1-i_{a}]
when
i_{a}\in(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1)
, or
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\frac{ni_{a}}{n-1})
when
i_{a}\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2n-1}]
, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\left(\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}|\nabla u-\nabla w|^{\gamma_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\\
&\quad\leq Cg^{-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}{r^{n-1}}\right)+C\left(g^{-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}{r^{n-1}}\right)\right)^{i_{a}}\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}(|\nabla u|+1)^{1-i_{a}}\,dx,\end{split}

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
i_{a}
and
\gamma_{0}
.

Proof.

Here, we only give the proof in the case
i_{a}\in\big(\frac{n-1}{2n-1},1\big)
. The proof for
i_{a}\in(0,\frac{n-1}{2n-1}]
is similar, with ideas inspired by
[
38
, Lemma 2.1]
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
B_{2r}(x_{0})=B_{1}
and
|\mu|(B_{2r})=1
. Indeed, this can be achieved by setting

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\bar{u}(x)=\frac{u(x_{0}+2rx)}{2Ar},\qquad\bar{\mu}(x)=\frac{2r\mu(x_{0}+2rx)}{g(A)},\\
&\bar{{\mathcal{A}}}(x,z)=\frac{{\mathcal{A}}(x_{0}+2rx,Az)}{g(A)},\qquad A:=g^{-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{2r})}{(2r)^{n-1}}\right).\end{split}

For
k>0
, using

\varphi_{1}=T_{2k}(u-w):=\max\{\min\{u-w,2k\},-2k\}

as test function in (
1.1
) and (
3.1
) and recalling (
2.4
), we have

(3.4)

\int_{C_{k}}|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|^{2}\,dx\leq Ck,

where
C_{k}:=\{x\in B_{1}:|u(x)-w(x)|<2k\}
.
According to (
1.5
), we know that
\frac{g(t)}{t}
is strictly decreasing. Thus, by the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}|\nabla(u-w)|&\leq C\left(\frac{g(|\nabla u|+|\nabla w|)}{|\nabla u|+|\nabla w|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|\\
&\leq C\left(\frac{g(|\nabla u|+|\nabla w|+1)}{|\nabla u|+|\nabla w|+1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)\right|\\
&\leq C\left(|\nabla u|+|\nabla w|+1\right)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}\left|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla v)\right|\\
&\leq C|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|(|\nabla u|+C|\nabla u-\nabla w|+1)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}\\
&\leq C|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|(|\nabla u-\nabla w|)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}+C|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|(|\nabla u|+1)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}.\end{split}

Using Young’s inequality, we find that

(3.5)

|\nabla u-\nabla w|\leq C|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|^{\frac{2}{1+i_{a}}}+C|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|(|\nabla u|+1)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}.

Now we set

E_{k}=B_{1}\cap\{k<|u-w|<2k\}\quad\quad F_{k}=B_{1}\cap\{|u-w|>k\}.

Using the Sobolev inequality, Hölder inequality and (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}&k\left|\{x:|u(x)-w(x)|>2k\}\cap B_{1}\right|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\quad\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}}|T_{2k}(u-w)-T_{k}(u-w)|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\quad\leq C\int_{E_{k}}|\nabla(u-w)|\,dx\\
&\quad\leq C\int_{E_{k}}|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|^{\frac{2}{1+i_{a}}}+|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|(|\nabla u|+1)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}\,dx\\
&\quad\leq C|E_{k}|^{\frac{i_{a}}{1+i_{a}}}\left(\int_{E_{k}}|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1+i_{a}}}\\
&\quad\quad+C\left(\int_{E_{k}}|V(\nabla v)-V(\nabla w)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{E_{k}}(|\nabla u|+1)^{1-i_{a}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

It follows from (
3.4
) that

k^{\frac{1}{2}}|F_{2k}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq Ck^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{1+i_{a}}}|E_{k}|^{\frac{i_{a}}{1+i_{a}}}+CQ_{1}^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}},

where
Q_{1}:=\||\nabla u|+1\|_{L^{1-i_{a}}(B_{1})}
. Taking the supremum over
k>0
yields

(3.7)

\|u-w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)},\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u-w\|^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2(1+i_{a})}}_{L^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2i_{a}},\infty}(B_{1})}+CQ_{1}^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}.

Since
\frac{n-1}{2n-1}<i_{a}<1
, one has

\frac{n}{2(n-1)}>\frac{1-i_{a}}{2i_{a}},

and therefore

(3.8)

\|u-w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)},\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u-w\|^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2(1+i_{a})}}_{L^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)},\infty}(B_{1})}+CQ_{1}^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}.

An application of Young’s inequality gives

(3.9)

\|u-w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{n}{2(n-1)},\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C+CQ_{1}^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}.

Set

H(u,w)=|V(\nabla u)-V(\nabla w)|^{2},\qquad q=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}.

For
k,l>0
, by Chebyshev’s inequality and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle|\{H(u,w)>l\}\cap B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{|u-w|>k\}\cap B_{1}|+|\{|u-w|\leq k,\;H(u,w)>l\}\cap B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-q}\|u-w\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{l}\!\int_{B_{1}\cap\{|u-w|\leq k\}}H(u,w)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-q}\|u-w\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(B_{1})}+\frac{Ck}{l}.

Choosing

k=\big[l\|u-w\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(B_{1})}\big]^{\frac{1}{1+q}},

one obtains

l^{\frac{q}{1+q}}|\{H(u,w)>l\}\cap B_{1}|\leq C\|u-w\|^{\frac{q}{1+q}}_{L^{q,\infty}(B_{1})}.

Taking the supremum over
l>0
gives

(3.10)

\|H(u,w)\|_{L^{\frac{q}{1+q},\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u-w\|_{L^{q,\infty}(B_{1})}.

Let
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1-i_{a}]
, then we have

\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1+i_{a}}<\frac{q}{1+q}.

Thus, using (
3.5
) and Hölder’s inequality with exponents

\frac{2}{1-i_{a}}
and
\frac{2}{1+i_{a}}
, we infer

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla(u-w)|^{\gamma_{0}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}\Big(H(u,w)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1+i_{a}}}+H(u,w)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}}(|\nabla u|+1)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}(1-i_{a})}{2}}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}H(u,w)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1+i_{a}}}\,dx+C\left(\int_{B_{1}}H(u,w)^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1+i_{a}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1+i_{a}}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}(|\nabla u|+1)^{\gamma_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1-i_{a}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|H(u,w)\|^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1+i_{a}}}_{L^{\frac{q}{1+q},\infty}(B_{1})}+C\|H(u,w)\|^{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}}_{L^{\frac{q}{1+q},\infty}(B_{1})}Q_{1}^{\frac{\gamma_{0}(1-i_{a})}{2}}.

Combining (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) yields

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla(u-w)|^{\gamma_{0}}\,dx\leq C+CQ_{1}^{\gamma_{0}(1-i_{a})},

which implies the desired result.
∎

.

w
be a solution to (
3.1

j\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(x,\varepsilon^{j}\rho)\leq\;

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\phi(x,r)+C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{H}(x,2\varepsilon^{j}\rho)

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x))}+1\big)^{1-i_{a}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{h}(x,2\varepsilon^{j}\rho)

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x))}+1\big)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\omega}^{\frac{2i_{a}}{(1+i_{a})^{2}}}(2\varepsilon^{j}\rho).

Using the definition of
\widetilde{H}
,
\widetilde{h}
and
\widetilde{\omega}
, one easily checks that there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon
,
n
,
i_{a}
,
s_{a}
and
\alpha_{1}
, such that

c_{1}f(t)\leq f(s)\leq c_{2}f(t)\qquad\text{for all }0<\varepsilon t\leq s\leq t,

for any
f\in\{\widetilde{H}(x,\cdot),\widetilde{h}(x,\cdot),\widetilde{\omega}(\cdot)\}
. Consequently, by the standard comparison between geometric sums and Riemann integrals, we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

Recall the definition of
\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}
from Section
3
.
For any
z\in B_{\varepsilon\rho}(x)
, we have

|\mathbf{q}_{x,\varepsilon\rho}-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|^{\gamma_{0}}\leq|\nabla u(z)-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|^{\gamma_{0}}+|\nabla u(z)-\mathbf{q}_{x,\varepsilon\rho}|^{\gamma_{0}}.

Averaging over
B_{\varepsilon\rho}(x)
and taking the
\gamma_{0}
-th root yield

|\mathbf{q}_{x,\varepsilon\rho}-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|\leq C\phi(x,\varepsilon\rho)+C\phi(x,\rho).

Iterating this estimate gives

|\mathbf{q}_{x,\varepsilon^{j}\rho}-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{j}\phi(x,\varepsilon^{i}\rho).

Therefore, by (
3.22
),

(4.8)

|\nabla u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(x,\varepsilon^{j}\rho),

for every Lebesgue point
x\in\Omega
of
\nabla u
.

Now we are ready to prove the interior Lipschitz estimate.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
and assume that
B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
for some
R\in(0,1]
.
Throughout the proof we suppose that the truncated Wolff potential is locally bounded, namely

(4.9)

\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{R}(|\mu|)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}<\infty.

The argument is divided into two steps. We first establish the desired estimate under a smoothness assumption on
u
, and then remove this restriction by an approximation procedure.

Step 1. Assume that
u\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})})
.
Let
x\in B_{R}(x_{0})
and
\rho\in(0,R/4]
be such that
B_{2\rho}(x)\Subset B_{R}(x_{0})
.
Combining the excess-decay estimate (
4.6
) with (
4.8
), we infer that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|\leq\;

\displaystyle C\phi(x,\rho)+C\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}(x))}+1\big)^{1-i_{a}}\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}(x))}+1\big)\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{\frac{2i_{a}}{(1+i_{a})^{2}}}(t)}{t}\,dt.

In addition, by the definition of
\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}
, we have

|\mathbf{q}_{x,\rho}|\leq C\phi(x,\rho)+C\rho^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{\rho}(x))}\leq C\rho^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{\rho}(x))}.

Combining the previous two inequalities yields

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|\leq\;

\displaystyle C\rho^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{\rho}(x))}+C\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

(4.10)

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}(x))}+1\big)^{1-i_{a}}\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle+C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}(x))}+1\big)\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{\frac{2i_{a}}{(1+i_{a})^{2}}}(t)}{t}\,dt.

Since
\widetilde{\omega}
satisfies the Dini-type condition (
1.4
),
we may choose
\rho_{0}\in(0,R/4]
sufficiently small such that

C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{\frac{2i_{a}}{(1+i_{a})^{2}}}(t)}{t}\,dt\leq 3^{-1-n/\gamma_{0}}.

Thus, for any
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
, by Young’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|\leq\;

\displaystyle C\rho^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{\rho}(x))}+C\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

(4.11)

\displaystyle+C\Big(\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}+3^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}(x))}+1\big).

Define
\rho_{k}=(1-2^{-k})R
for
k\geq 1
. Since
\rho_{k+1}-\rho_{k}=2^{-k-1}R
, we have
B_{2\rho}(x)\subset B_{\rho_{k+1}}(x_{0})
for any
x\in B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0})
and
\rho=2^{-k-2}R
. We take
k_{0}
sufficiently large such that
2^{-k_{0}-2}\leq\rho_{0}
. Then by (
4
) with
\rho=2^{-k-2}R
, we have for any
k\geq k_{0}
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}+1\leq\;

\displaystyle 3^{-n/\gamma_{0}}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{k+1}}(x_{0}))}+1\big)

\displaystyle+C\Big(\frac{2^{k}}{R}\Big)^{n/\gamma_{0}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{\rho_{k+1}}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle+C\sup_{x\in B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{R/2}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle+C\sup_{x\in B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0})}\Big(\int_{0}^{R/2}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}.

Multiplying the inequality by
3^{-nk/\gamma_{0}}

and summing over
k\geq k_{0}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}3^{-nk/{\gamma_{0}}}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}3^{-nk/{\gamma_{0}}}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}+1)+CR^{-n/{\gamma_{0}}}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+1)

\displaystyle\quad+C\sup_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{2}}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt+C\sup_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{2}}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}},

where each summation is finite. By subtracting

\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}3^{-nk/{\gamma_{0}}}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho_{k}}(x_{0}))}+1)

from both sides of the above inequality, we get the following
L^{\infty}
-estimate for
\nabla u
:

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}+1\leq CR^{-n/{\gamma_{0}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle+C\sup_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{2}}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt+C\sup_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{2}}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}.

Using the definitions of
\widetilde{H}
and
\widetilde{h}
, it is not difficult to check that

\int_{0}^{R/2}\frac{\widetilde{H}(x,t)}{t}\,dt+\Big(\int_{0}^{R/2}\frac{\widetilde{h}(x,t)}{t}\,dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}\leq C\big(\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{R}(|\mu|)(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\gamma_{0}\leq 1-i_{a}
. Thus we deduce from (
4.12
) that

(4.13)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\leq C\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{R}(|\mu|)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}+CR^{-\frac{n}{1-i_{a}}}\||\nabla u|+1\|_{L^{1-i_{a}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

Step 2. Approximation argument.

Let
r_{1}\in(0,R)
and set
r_{2}=(R+r_{1})/2
.
Choose a sequence of standard mollifiers
\{\varphi_{k}\}
and define

\mu_{k}=\mu*\varphi_{k},\qquad{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(x,\xi)=({\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,\xi)*\varphi_{k})(x)\quad\text{in }B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}).

For
k
sufficiently large,
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
satisfies the same structural conditions as
{\mathcal{A}}
in
B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, assumption (
4.9
) ensures that

\mu\in(W^{1,G}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})))^{\prime}
, and hence

\mu_{k}\to\mu\quad\text{in }(W^{1,G}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})))^{\prime}.

Let
u_{k}\in u+W^{1,G}_{0}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))
solve

-\DIV({\mathcal{A}}_{k}(x,\nabla u_{k}))=\mu_{k}\quad\text{in }B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}).

Standard energy estimates yield a uniform bound of
\{\nabla u_{k}\}
in
L^{G}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))
.
Moreover, a monotonicity argument implies

\nabla u_{k}\to\nabla u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{G}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})),

up to a subsequence.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
and
\mu_{k}
are smooth, classical regularity theory gives

u_{k}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))
.
Therefore estimate (
4.13
) applies to
u_{k}
on
B_{r_{1}/2}(x_{0})
:

\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{1}/2}(x_{0}))}\leq C\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{r_{1}}(|\mu_{k}|)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{1}}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{i_{a}}}+Cr_{1}^{-\frac{n}{1-i_{a}}}\||\nabla u|+1\|_{L^{1-i_{a}}(B_{r_{1}}(x_{0}))}.

By Fubini–Tonelli theorem and the definition of
\mu_{k}
, the Wolff potentials satisfy

\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{r_{1}}(|\mu_{k}|)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{1}}(x_{0}))}\leq\big\|\mathbf{W}_{i_{a},g}^{r_{1}}(|\mu|)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{2}}(x_{0}))}

for all large
k
.
Passing to the limit
k\to\infty
and then letting
r_{1}\to R
, we obtain the desired Lipschitz bound
(
1.10
) at
x_{0}
.
∎
