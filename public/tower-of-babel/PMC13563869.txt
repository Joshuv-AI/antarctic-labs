Integrating online learning with PBL and CBL: a promising approach to improve comprehensive learning outcomes in breast surgery for Chinese eight-year medical students
Authors: Yutong Liu, Zijun Ling, Zicheng Zhang, Xinqi Peng, Minfeng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the impact of an integrated teaching method combining an online platform, Problem-Based Learning (PBL), and Case-Based Learning (CBL) on breast surgery education for Chinese eight-year programme medical students.MethodsThis study employed a randomized controlled trial. A total of 50 eight-year medical students (2019 cohort) who completed their internship in the Department of Breast Surgery at Nanfang Hospital, Southern Medical University, from September 2024 to June 2025 were randomly assigned to two groups (n = 25 each). The control group adopted a traditional model of theoretical lectures and clinical practice, while the experimental group implemented the integrated online platform-PBL-CBL approach. Teaching effectiveness was assessed via theoretical examinations, clinical skill evaluations, and structured questionnaires.ResultsThe experimental group achieved significantly higher total theoretical examination scores than the control group (76.25 ± 4.35 vs. 73.60 ± 4.47, P = 0.039). In Mini-CEX assessments, the experimental group demonstrated superior overall clinical competence (7.32 ± 0.56 vs. 6.92 ± 0.76, P = 0.039). DOPS scores revealed significant advantages in six key dimensions: indication/contraindication comprehension, sedation/analgesia management, operational capability, aseptic technique, postoperative care, and communication skills (P < 0.05). Questionnaire results showed no statistically significant differences in individual dimensions (all P > 0.05), but a positive trend was observed.ConclusionThe integration of an online platform with PBL and CBL significantly enhances the theoretical proficiency, clinical diagnostic capabilities, and comprehensive learning satisfaction of eight-year medical programme students in breast surgery, providing a viable framework for high-level medical talent training.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09784-6.Keywords: Online Learning, Problem-based learning, Case-based learning, Medical education, Breast surgery, Eight-year medical programme

Background

As one of the key training specialties in Chinese medical education system, the eight-year medical program features stringent admission criteria and a much higher entrance exam score thresholds compared with other clinical medicine programs. It is specifically designed to deliver rigorous elite education, and cultivate physician-scientists (M.D.) with superior comprehensive competencies, leadership, innovation, and interdisciplinary expertise [1]. Students in the eight-year medical programme are encouraged to develop clinical thinking and scientific research abilities as soon as possible. The final phase of the programme emphasizes clinical rotations and research training, requiring students to integrate theoretical knowledge with hands-on skills to meet the demands of specialized medical practice [2]. However, the condensed training timeline poses challenges, as students must master extensive medical knowledge in a limited period, often hindering in-depth understanding and clinical application [3]. Therefore, continuous exploration and refinement of teaching reform are essential to enhance the quality of eight-year medical education.

At present, traditional lecture-based learning (LBL) remains the predominant teaching method in medical education due to its efficiency in delivering core knowledge to large cohorts [4]. However, mounting evidence indicates that LBL is less effective in fostering critical thinking and problem-solving skills—key competencies for medical professionals—owing to its teacher-centred nature and passive learning dynamics [5].This limitation has driven a paradigm shift towards more dynamic, student-centred instructional approaches in contemporary medical education [6].

Problem-Based Learning (PBL) and Case-Based Learning (CBL) are widely recognized innovative strategies that address these shortcomings, owing to their demonstrated effectiveness in fostering students’ active learning, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills [7]. PBL, guided by non-directive mentors, centres on clinical problems to promote independent inquiry and teamwork, thereby enhancing critical thinking and self-directed learning [8]. CBL utilizes authentic clinical cases to bridge theoretical knowledge and practice, encouraging multidisciplinary analysis to strengthen clinical decision-making abilities [9]. Numerous studies have confirmed that CBL improves clinical practice, problem-solving, and analytical skills among medical students and residents [10, 11], while systematic reviews consistently report higher student satisfaction and engagement in PBL courses compared to traditional LBL [12]. Additionally, the post-pandemic era has accelerated the digital transformation of medical education, with online teaching platforms emerging as valuable tools for resource dissemination. These platforms not only deliver textbook content but also provide real-time updates on the latest clinical guidelines and technological advancements, enabling students to access cutting-edge knowledge efficiently. Leveraging the complementary strengths of the aforementioned teaching approaches, the integration of online platforms with PBL and CBL is poised to substantially enhance the teaching effectiveness for eight-year medical students.

Breast surgery, a specialized field integrating oncology and reconstructive surgery, demands a solid theoretical foundation, proficient clinical skills, and sound decision-making capabilities. Therefore, it has become crucial to explore and implement more efficient teaching methods to enhance the effectiveness of breast surgery education. For eight-year medical programme students, who represent the elite of medical education, a more comprehensive and tailored breast surgery teaching program is essential to cultivate them into versatile top-tier professionals. The integration of an online platform with PBL and CBL offers a promising approach to enhance the integration of theory and practice in breast surgery education, deepening conceptual understanding, refining clinical reasoning, and improving problem-solving efficiency.

This study aimed to explore the application value of this integrated teaching model, evaluate its effectiveness in enhancing students’ comprehensive competencies, and establish a practice-oriented teaching system for eight-year medical programme.

Methods

Participants

A total of 50 eight-year medical students from the 2019 cohort (total 120 students), who completed their internship rotation in the Department of Breast Surgery at Nanfang Hospital between September 2024 and June 2025, were enrolled. These 50 participants selected breast surgery as their elective rotation within general surgery subspecialties (covering thyroid, gastrointestinal, colorectal, and breast surgery). All participants had completed compulsory breast disease courses and were instructed by the same faculty team to ensure comparable baseline knowledge and clinical competence. Using a random number table for simple randomization, an independent researcher numbered students 1–50 by admission order, matched random numbers, and assigned the top 25 to the experimental group and the last 25 to the control group. Group allocation was concealed in numbered sealed opaque envelopes; students opened envelopes on admission day under supervision. Assessors were blinded to group assignments. Baseline characteristics showed no statistically significant intergroup differences (P > 0.05; Table 1).

Sample size and post-hoc power analysis

This study enrolled 50 students who elected to rotate through the breast surgery department. While this represents a selected subgroup of the entire cohort, baseline characteristics between the experimental and control groups were well balanced (Table 1). A post-hoc power analysis was conducted based on the primary outcome—theoretical examination total score. Using G*Power 3.1 for an independent-samples t-test (two-tailed, α = 0.05), with a mean difference of 2.65, a pooled standard deviation of 4.41 (Cohen’s d = 0.61), and 25 students per group, the achieved statistical power was 0.52.

Study design

This study was conducted during a two-week clinical rotation in the Breast Surgery Department.

The control group received a traditional teaching model integrating theoretical lectures and clinical practice. Theoretical instruction included a review of core breast surgery knowledge (e.g., breast anatomy, disease diagnosis, and treatment principles) via classroom PowerPoint lectures to establish a systematic knowledge framework. During clinical practice, students performed supervised tasks including patient interviews, physical examinations, medical record documentation, and diagnostic test analysis, and observed basic surgical procedures. Instructors provided personalized feedback and detailed case analyses following task completion.

The experimental group implemented a blended teaching approach combining online learning, PBL, and CBL. Prior to PBL/CBL sessions, students accessed learning resources on “Aike”—an online teaching platform developed by Southern Medical University—including breast surgery courses, the latest clinical guidelines, and surgical procedure videos. Students completed independent learning of these resources during self-directed study time. After finishing the online learning module, the students took part in problem-based learning (PBL) and case-based learning (CBL) sessions focused on breast cancer. At the start of each session, instructors outlined the course structure and presented a clinical case via slides. Students then engaged in faculty-guided group discussions, formulating clinical questions based on theoretical knowledge and conducting evidence-based research using online databases and library resources. Each group designated a representative to present findings, address key questions, and identify unresolved issues. Instructors provided structured summaries, clarified complex concepts, and reinforced disease diagnosis and treatment principles. Following PBL/CBL sessions, students participated in standardized clinical activities, including patient consultations and supervised observation of basic breast surgical procedures.

Upon completion of the clinical rotation, both groups underwent theoretical examinations, clinical skill assessments, and questionnaire surveys to evaluate teaching effectiveness.

Evaluation of teaching effectiveness

Theoretical examination

The theoretical examination consisted of two sections: basic theoretical questions (50 points) and clinical case analyses (50 points). Questions were developed by instructors to assess conceptual mastery of breast surgery.

Clinical skill assessments

Clinical skills were assessed using modified Mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise (Mini-CEX) and Directly Observed Procedural Skills (DOPS) tools, which are internationally validated for clinical competence evaluation [13]. Each assessment lasted 30 min and was conducted by two non-teaching instructors using standardized scales. The Mini-CEX scale evaluates students’ clinical diagnostic and therapeutic competencies across seven criteria: interviewing skills, clinical examination skills, humanistic qualities, clinical judgement, counseling skills, organization/efficiency, and overall clinical competence [14]. The DOPS scale replicates real-world clinical practice settings by enabling instructors to directly observe students as they perform a range of clinical procedures. The evaluation criteria encompass comprehension of indications and contraindications, acquisition of informed consent, appropriate preoperative preparation, technical proficiency, adherence to aseptic principles, timely seeking of assistance, postoperative care, effective patient communication, humanistic concern and professionalism, and overall clinical competence [15]. Both scales adopt a 9-point scoring system with three grading levels: 1–3 represents unsatisfactory performance, 4–6 indicates satisfactory achievement, and 7–9 denotes excellent performance.

Questionnaire survey

An anonymous, self-administered questionnaire was used to collect subjective feedback on teaching effectiveness. The instrument assessed five domains (each scored 1–5 on a Likert scale): mastery of theoretical knowledge, enhancement of autonomous learning ability, enhancement of innovative thinking, enhancement of teamwork competence, and development of humanistic care awareness. This questionnaire was designed to assess five relatively independent dimensions of teaching effectiveness—theoretical knowledge mastery, autonomous learning ability, innovative ability, teamwork ability, and humanistic care awareness—thus constituting a formative measurement model for which high internal consistency among the items is not a requirement. An internal consistency analysis was conducted for this questionnaire.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 22.0 statistical software. Continuous variables were expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD), and group comparisons were performed using independent samples t-tests. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, with intergroup comparisons using chi-square tests. To aid result interpretation, Cohen’s d was calculated for effect size, with d = 0.2 as small, 0.5 as moderate, and 0.8 as large (Cohen, 1988). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all statistical analyses.

Results

Comparison of theoretical examination scores

The experimental group achieved significantly higher mean total theoretical examination scores compared to the control group (76.25 ± 4.35 vs. 73.60 ± 4.47, P < 0.05). Specifically, the experimental group performed better in basic theoretical questions (40.24 ± 2.29 vs. 38.77 ± 2.19, P < 0.05). For clinical case analysis, although a moderate effect size (d = 0.507) was observed, the between‑group difference was only borderline significant (P = 0.079). (Table 2)

Comparison of questionnaire survey scores

The anonymous questionnaire was fully recovered with a 100% response rate in both groups, and all data were included in the statistical analysis. Internal consistency analysis revealed a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.246 across the five items. This low value is indicative of the independence among the dimensions rather than poor reliability of the scale. Therefore, no composite total score was calculated or reported, and all questionnaire results should be considered exploratory rather than confirmatory evidence.

As shown in Table 5, although the experimental group scored higher on all five dimensions, none of the between-group differences reached statistical significance (all P > 0.05). Given the limited sample size and insufficient statistical power, these null findings do not confirm the absence of an intervention effect. For humanistic care awareness, although the between-group difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.083), it constituted a marginally significant trend with a medium effect size (d = 0.501). The effect sizes for innovative ability and teamwork ability were 0.465 and 0.454, respectively, approaching medium levels, whereas only small effect sizes were observed for theoretical knowledge mastery and autonomous learning ability (d = 0.154 and 0.207). These results suggest a favorable but preliminary trend in self‑perceived competencies, particularly in humanistic care, under the integrated online-PBL-CBL model. Given that the reliability of individual-item measurements cannot be verified, that none of the differences reached statistical significance, and that statistical power was limited, no reliable conclusions can currently be drawn regarding improvements in satisfaction or competency enhancement. Future research would adopt multi-item scales with rigorously validated reliability and validity, together with adequate sample sizes, to further examine these exploratory findings.

Discussion

LBL method promotes passive learning, limiting students’ ability to process and critically evaluate knowledge [16]. The eight-year medical programme, focused on cultivating interdisciplinary talents, requires not only solid clinical skills but also advanced competencies such as self-directed learning, teamwork, and innovative thinking. With rapid advancements in breast surgery techniques, medications, and clinical guidelines, traditional teaching models are increasingly inadequate for meeting the learning needs of eight-year medical students.

The integration of online platforms with PBL and CBL addresses these limitations through synergistic effects, which can be explained by cognitive load theory. Intrinsic cognitive load, defined as the inherent complexity of clinical knowledge, represents a key challenge in surgical education. In our model, online pre-learning helps students master basic concepts and terminology before PBL/CBL discussions, reducing intrinsic cognitive load. As foundational knowledge becomes increasingly automated, cognitive resources are redirected toward germane cognitive load, supporting deep processing, clinical reasoning, and the development of complex mental schemas. Students are thus better able to engage in the higher-order thinking required in PBL and CBL activities, such as differential diagnosis, treatment planning, and evidence-based decision-making.This sequential design creates genuine synergy: the online platform optimizes cognitive resource allocation; CBL provides authentic scenarios for knowledge transfer; and PBL promotes deep conceptual transformation. Together, they create a unified learning experience that surpasses the effect of any single component alone.

The present study demonstrated that the integrated online-PBL-CBL teaching model was associated with improved theoretical examination performance and overall clinical competence compared with traditional teaching. These improvements were reflected in higher total theoretical scores, basic theoretical knowledge, overall Mini-CEX performance, and key DOPS domains including indication recognition, sedation management, operative capability, aseptic technique, postoperative care, and communication skills. Such findings support the value of case-based and problem-oriented learning in strengthening theoretical foundation and standardized clinical operation skills. This may be attributed to the following reasons. First, online learning platforms facilitate targeted knowledge acquisition and systematic learning. Second, PBL and CBL courses support students in identifying gaps in their diagnostic reasoning and implementing targeted improvements through structured inquiry and case analysis. Third, supervised clinical practice enables the application of theoretical knowledge to real patient care, refining clinical skills. The synergistic integration of these stages across the curriculum collectively enhances the development of students’ clinical competencies. Effect size analysis shows moderate effects in clinical case analysis and clinical examination skills, indicating meaningful educational benefits despite borderline significance, which is common in small‑sample educational studies.

DOPS assessments revealed significant improvements in six procedural dimensions, highlighting the model’s effectiveness in enhancing core clinical skills. However, no statistically significant between-group difference was observed in the overall DOPS performance score. This lack of divergence can be attributed to three interrelated factors. First, the DOPS score integrates procedural fluency and clinical judgment, which require long‑term clinical experience. Achieving a measurable improvement in clinical skill mastery remains challenging in short-term interventions. Second, the DOPS employs a non‑linear scoring system where excellent overall ratings require consistent high performance across all domains; similar scores in some individual items weakened the overall between‑group difference. Third, a ceiling effect existed because the control group already achieved a high baseline score (7.60 ± 0.65), leaving limited room for further improvement. Additionally, a small sample size also had an impact on the significance of the results.

The questionnaire results revealed a consistent positive trend in student satisfaction across all five domains, with the most prominent improvement observed in the development of humanistic care awareness. Although the experimental group scored higher than the control group in all five dimensions of the questionnaire, none of these between-group differences reached statistical significance. This lack of significance may be attributed to several factors: the relatively small sample size limited statistical power; the two-week short intervention period was insufficient to produce substantial measurable changes in subjective competencies; and the low internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.246) of the questionnaire scale reduced its ability to detect group differences. Taken together, the integrated model demonstrates a favorable tendency toward improving student-perceived learning quality, particularly in humanistic care, which merits further verification in larger-sample and longer-term studies.

However, the integrated learning model may impose a higher learning burden on students and require substantially greater pedagogical investment and expertise from instructors compared with traditional teaching. Students spent an extra 3–4 h over the two-week rotation on pre-class online learning, case analysis, literature research and group discussion preparation. For instructors, the model demands additional time for case design, PBL question development, online resource updating, and small-group facilitation. Moreover, the experimental group required four experienced teachers to lead four small discussion groups separately, which is a higher faculty workload than traditional teaching. Therefore, while the integrated model improves learning outcomes, it relies on reasonable time allocation for students and sufficient faculty support, standardized case development, and targeted teacher training to achieve sustainable application.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, as a single-center investigation with a relatively small sample size (n = 25 per group), post-hoc power analysis revealed a statistical power of 0.52 for the primary outcome, below the recommended 0.80, indicating a risk of Type II error. The eight-year programme is elite and small-scale, and we enrolled all students rotating into breast surgery, limiting further sample expansion. Future multicentre randomized controlled trials are needed to validate findings across diverse educational settings. Secondly, the short internship period and lack of long-term follow-up prevent an assessment of the model’s sustained impact of students on clinical practice post-graduation. Concurrently, the study did not account for potential confounding factors such as individual differences in students’ prior academic performance, learning styles, or self-directed learning abilities, which may have influenced the observed outcomes.

Notably, despite these limitations, the blended framework is adaptable to other surgical disciplines (e.g., general surgery, orthopedics, urology) by updating discipline-specific online resources and PBL/CBL cases. It can be promoted to institutions with mature online platforms and trained faculty. Meanwhile, the online platform used in this study provides a foundation for further intelligent optimization. Future iterations of the teaching model could incorporate artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted learning, including AI-driven case recommendation, automated formative assessment, real-time clinical reasoning feedback, and personalized learning path generation. Artificial intelligence can compensate for limitations of human-led PBL/CBL by processing large volumes of clinical cases, standardizing evaluation, and providing timely feedback, thereby improving teaching efficiency and reducing variability. Integrating AI into the online-PBL-CBL framework may enhance its effectiveness and help achieve more robust improvements in clinical competence.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the integrated teaching model combining an online platform with PBL and CBL may offer advantages in breast surgery education for eight-year medical students, and was associated with enhanced theoretical knowledge mastery, clinical diagnostic capabilities, and overall learning satisfaction.
