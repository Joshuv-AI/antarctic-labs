LLMpedia: A Transparent Framework to Materialize an LLM’s Encyclopedic Knowledge at Scale
Muhammed Saeed, Simon Razniewski

Abstract
Benchmarks such as MMLU suggest flagship language models approach factuality saturation, with scores above 90%. We show this picture is incomplete. LLMpedia generates encyclopedic articles entirely from parametric memory, producing ∼ {\sim} 1M articles across three model families without retrieval. For gpt-5-mini, the verifiable true rate on Wikipedia-covered subjects is only 74.7% – more than 15 percentage points below the benchmark-based picture, consistent with the availability bias of fixed-question evaluation. Beyond Wikipedia, frontier subjects verifiable only through curated web evidence fall further to 63.2% true rate. Wikipedia covers just 61% of surfaced subjects, and three model families overlap by only 7.3% in subject choice. In a capture-trap benchmark inspired by prior analysis of Grokipedia, LLMpedia achieves substantially higher factuality at roughly half the textual similarity to Wikipedia. Unlike Grokipedia, every prompt, artifact, and evaluation verdict is publicly released, making LLMpedia the first fully open parametric encyclopedia – bridging factuality evaluation and knowledge materialization. All data, code, and a browsable interface are at https://llmpedia.net .



1
Introduction

Large language models are now used as everyday knowledge interfaces by hundreds of millions of people
20
.
Yet what these models actually
know
– and how reliably they express it in long-form text – remains poorly understood.
Two research threads address this from opposite directions:
factuality evaluation
probes models on experimenter-chosen questions;
knowledge materialization
lets models produce their own knowledge at scale.
Neither alone suffices: benchmarks miss what they never ask, and materializations lack systematic evaluation.
LLMpedia bridges the two.

Most current evaluation relies on curated benchmarks such as MMLU
8
, TruthfulQA
14
, and HLE
23
.
These are indispensable, but they share a structural limitation: they test only knowledge the evaluator thought to ask about.
This creates a form of
availability bias

30
: a model can look highly factual on a fixed set of questions while containing broad regions of weak or unstable knowledge the benchmark never reaches.
The limitation becomes sharper in real use, where people receive fluent, multi-paragraph explanations rather than short benchmark answers
24
.

.

A natural alternative is to
materialize
model knowledge at scale.

2
and GPTKB
10
showed that recursive elicitation can surface vast amounts of model knowledge, including large regions absent from Wikipedia
9
;
4
.
But GPTKB materializes triples – a format unlike how users actually interact with models
18
– and its pipeline suffers from persistent entity disambiguation failures
4
;
9
.
We instead materialize full encyclopedic articles in the free-text modality users see, making it possible to evaluate subject choice, discourse structure, entity salience, and – crucially – how much apparent knowledge survives outside the narrow corridor of benchmark questions.

This gap is large, for gpt-5-mini, the verifiable true rate on Wikipedia-covered subjects is only 74.7% – more than 15 percentage points below the 90%+ picture suggested by benchmark-style evaluation.
That gap is the main empirical signal of this paper: fixed-question benchmarks overstate open-ended factual reliability
in part
because they are shaped by availability bias in what gets asked.
Among surfaced subjects absent from Wikipedia but recoverable through curated web evidence, the true rate falls further to 63.2%.
At the same time, Wikipedia covers only 61% of surfaced subjects, and three model families overlap on just 7.3%, indicating that materialized parametric knowledge is far more model-specific than leaderboard-style evaluation implies.

This question matters beyond benchmarking.
Wikipedia’s coverage is uneven, with a well-documented Western-centric bias
33
, gender skew
32
, and non-Anglophone gaps.
If models encode reliable knowledge beyond Wikipedia, materializing it could help complement those gaps – but only if the generation process is auditable.
The most visible LLM-generated encyclopedia, Grokipedia
35
, is methodologically opaque.
Prior work reports high textual similarity to Wikipedia
36
, and we document additional evidence consistent with retrieval-shaped generation (Appendix
K
).
This creates a
capture trap
: the encyclopedia may inherit Wikipedia’s phrasing while obscuring both genuine model knowledge and its error modes.

We present
LLMpedia
, a fully auditable, open-source framework for parametric encyclopedia generation.
Starting from a seed, the system expands breadth-first through article generation and wikilink extraction, then filters candidates through a three-stage sanitization pipeline before re-enqueuing them.
Every prompt, intermediate artifact, and evaluation verdict is publicly released.
We operate in two modes:
general-domain materialization
, where BFS expands freely from a seed entity, and
topic-focused generation
, where expansion is constrained to entities associated with one of three root topics.
Both regimes use prompt templates documented in App.
J
.

Across gpt-5-mini
19
, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct
6
, and DeepSeek-V3
15
, we produce approximately one million articles.
Motivated by claims of ideological bias in Grokipedia, we additionally inject controlled editorial personas (scientific-neutral, left-leaning, conservative) across all pipeline stages in topic-focused runs, treating framing as an explicit, auditable variable rather than a hidden confounder.

Our investigation centers on three questions:

RQ1
: What do topic-focused parametric encyclopedias reveal about cross-model factuality, coverage, and the effect of editorial persona?

RQ2
: At
10^{5}
scale, how do three model families differ in knowledge breadth, overlap, and reliability? Does factuality degrade with BFS depth?

RQ3
: Does parametric generation escape the Wikipedia capture trap – producing factually competitive but structurally independent content?

Our core contributions are:

1.

A scalable, fully open-source pipeline for parametric encyclopedia construction – bridging factuality evaluation and knowledge materialization into a single framework;

2.

Evidence that open-ended parametric generation is substantially weaker than benchmark-style factuality suggests: for gpt-5-mini, true rate falls to 74.7% on Wikipedia-covered subjects, more than 15 points below the benchmark-based picture;

3.

Evidence that the beyond-Wikipedia frontier is both real and difficult: subjects verifiable only through curated web evidence reach just 63.2% true rate;

4.

A capture-trap benchmark, inspired by
36
, in which we collect the most-edited English Wikipedia articles and show that LLMpedia achieves higher factuality than Grokipedia at roughly half the textual similarity to Wikipedia.

2
Related Work

Knowledge probing and its limits.

Probing frameworks such as LAMA
21
test recall via cloze prompts; multitask suites like MMLU
8
span knowledge-heavy domains; TruthfulQA
14
targets misconceptions; HLE
22
pushes frontier difficulty.
These measure only what the experimenter thought to ask.
LLMpedia complements benchmarks by materializing knowledge at scale and decomposing outputs into true, false, and unverifiable.

From triples to articles.

2
introduced recursive elicitation via knowledge-graph expansion;
10
scaled this into GPTKB (100M+ triples), revealing that many materialized entities are absent from Wikipedia
4
.
GPTKB materializes triples; LLMpedia materializes full discourse-level articles.
This is not merely a length difference: articles make it possible to evaluate structure, subject choice, and unverifiability in free text.

Encyclopedia generation and the retrieval trap.

Early work assumed structured inputs
26
;
12
; WikiSum
16
scaled to multi-document summarization.
STORM
27
models pre-writing through web-grounded interactions but remains retrieval-dependent.

3
found that outlines improve generation; LLMpedia extends this with dynamic outline generation tailored to each entity.
Grokipedia
35
operates at the largest scale but discloses no methodology;
36
report TF-IDF cosine of 0.46–0.49 against Wikipedia, nearly double LLMpedia’s 0.26, suggesting retrieval-shaped repackaging.

Hallucination, retrieval, and provenance.

Hallucination remains a central problem for factual generation
(
11
)
.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) can improve factuality by grounding outputs in external sources
(
13
;
17
)
, while self-reflective variants and confidence elicitation can further improve reliability
(
7
;
28
)
.
But retrieval complicates provenance: once external passages are introduced, it becomes hard to separate what comes from parametric memory from what is copied or assembled from retrieved text.
In encyclopedia-style generation, this matters because retrieval can pull systems toward sources such as Wikipedia, obscuring whether the system is expressing model knowledge or producing retrieval-shaped content
(
29
;
36
)
.

3
LLMpedia Framework

Figure
2
shows the full pipeline.
From a seed entity, the system expands breadth-first through optional self-grounding, outline generation, article elicitation, and three-stage entity sanitization before enqueuing surviving children.
All prompts and intermediate artifacts are logged.
Full prompt templates for both general-domain and topic-focused runs appear in Appendix
J
.

3.1
Materialization Modes

General-domain expansion.

In the general-domain setting, BFS starts from a single seed entity –
Vannevar Bush
in all reported runs – and expands without topical restrictions.
Each article yields Wikitext with
[[wikilinks]]
; after sanitization, surviving links become the next frontier.
This mode surfaces an unrestricted knowledge graph and scales to
10^{5}
–
10^{6}
articles.

Topic-focused generation.

The topic-focused setting uses the same recursive BFS logic, but constrains expansion around one of three root topics:
Ancient Babylon
,
US Civil Rights Movement
, and
Dutch Colonization in Southeast Asia
.
We seed each run with a topic-specific subject list and instruct the model to generate articles whose
[[wikilinks]]
remain directly associated with the root topic.
Only topically relevant survivors are re-enqueued as children.
This yields a controlled version of BFS useful for cross-model comparison, persona analysis, and domain-difficulty analysis.
Prompts for both regimes are documented in Appendix
J
.

3.2
Self-Grounding

Before article generation, the model can optionally produce a structured fact sheet – summary, aliases, and predicate–object pairs with calibrated confidence scores – to anchor downstream content and reduce cross-section drift.
Facts below threshold 0.75 are flagged.
We treat self-grounding as an ablation variable (§
4.4
); the final large-scale operating point disables it for simplicity.

3.3
Dynamic Outline and Article Generation

Prior work found that outlines improve LLM-based encyclopedia generation
3
, but mainly studied fixed section templates.
LLMpedia instead uses dynamic outline generation: the model first proposes 6–7 section headings tailored to the entity type, then writes the article to match that outline.
If warranted, the model also produces an

block.

3.4
Prompting Strategies

The
baseline
prompt requests standard Wikitext with plain
[[wikilinks]]
.
The
calibrated
prompt instead requires per-entity confidence annotations (
[[Entity (0.85)]]
); entities below threshold 0.75 are discarded before NER, making coverage–factuality tradeoffs explicit
28
.
The exact prompt families for general-domain and topic-focused runs are given in Appendix
J
.

3.5
Entity Sanitization Pipeline

Both GPTKB
10
and GPTKB v1.5
9
document persistent entity disambiguation and fragmentation failures. We address these with three stages; Figure
5
illustrates the effect on the general-domain expansion graph, where only encyclopedic entities survive to spawn sub-graphs.

Stage 1: Canonical Normalization.

Unicode NFKC, case-folding, and punctuation removal map surface variants to identical keys (e.g., “John F. Kennedy”
\to

john f kennedy
). Fast and deterministic, this eliminates trivial duplicates before costlier stages.

Stage 2: LLM-Based Encyclopedic Filtering.

Not every wikilink warrants its own article. LLMs exhibit a
looping problem
: expanding generic terms like
[[engineer]]
or
[[inventor]]
(Figure
4
) yields thin articles that in turn produce equally generic wikilinks, diluting quality at each expansion layer. In contrast,
[[Office of Scientific Research and Development]]
expands into a rich, self-contained article with distinctive downstream entities. Traditional NER tools degrade on isolated entity labels; following GPTKB
10
, we use the LLM itself to classify candidate batches (prompt in Appendix
J
), retaining entities that genuinely deserve standalone encyclopedia entries.

Stage 3: Embedding-Based Deduplication.

Canonical normalization misses semantic duplicates (“Deutschland” vs. “Germany”). We maintain an embedding index (
text-embedding-3-small
) over all committed entities; candidates exceeding cosine threshold
0.90
are flagged and resolved via LLM arbitration with parent-article context. In parallel execution, concurrent NER workers may independently nominate the same candidate from different parents within the same BFS wave; within-wave cross-candidate deduplication at the similarity worker collapses these before any commit, guaranteeing the main queue contains no duplicates (Appendix
E.2
).

3.6
Architecture and Persona Injection

Each pipeline stage maintains an independent work queue with retries and exponential backoff, supporting both batch and online modes (Appendix
E.1
).
To study framing as an explicit variable, we inject three personas – scientific-neutral, left-leaning, and conservative – at the system level of
all
pipeline stages in topic-focused runs.
This changes not only article prose, but also outline generation, link proposal, and filtering behavior.

4.3
Evaluation Protocol

Our automatic verification pipeline has two steps.
First, an LLM judge (
gpt-4.1-nano
) extracts up to ten atomic, verifiable factual claims per article.
Second, each claim is checked against reference evidence and classified as
supported
,
refuted
, or
insufficient evidence
.

Crucially, factuality metrics are computed only over subjects for which reference evidence exists.
For
Tier 1
, the full Wikipedia article is retrieved via the MediaWiki API with redirect resolution.
Subjects absent from Wikipedia contribute to coverage statistics but not to precision, true rate, or false rate.
For
Tier 2
, claims about frontier subjects absent from Wikipedia are checked against curated web sources drawn from a quality-scored domain list (133 domains; Appendix
D
).
Because this evidence pipeline is intentionally strict, some Wikipedia-absent subjects still yield no usable web evidence and are excluded from Tier 2 factuality metrics.
In the capture-trap experiment (§
5.4
), both tiers run for all subjects.

Metrics.

Let
N
be the total number of extracted claims for an article, with
n_{s}
supported,
n_{r}
refuted, and
n_{u}
insufficient:

True rate

\displaystyle=n_{s}/N

(1)

False rate

\displaystyle=n_{r}/N

(2)

Unverifiable rate

\displaystyle=n_{u}/N

(3)

Precision

\displaystyle=n_{s}/(n_{s}+n_{r})

(4)

The first three partition every claim and sum to 1.
Precision excludes unverifiable claims from the denominator.
All factuality metrics are macro-averaged at the article level unless otherwise noted.

Similarity.

We compute TF-IDF cosine, token Jaccard,
n
-gram overlap (
n
=1–3), and semantic cosine (
text-embedding-3-small
) between generated articles and Wikipedia.

5
Results

5.1
Entity Sanitization at Scale

The main scaling challenge is not article generation alone, but sanitizing the much larger expansion frontier that generated articles create.
GPT-5-mini produces articles for about 740K subjects, while Llama and DeepSeek produce articles for about 120K subjects each.
Yet even these smaller open-model runs emit very large numbers of candidate expansion links: 51.6M raw wikilink candidates for GPT-5-mini, 9.8M for Llama, and 6.5M for DeepSeek.

Most of these candidates do not become new subjects.
They are removed in three stages: lexical/canonical deduplication, NER filtering, and semantic deduplication.
After these stages, 741K candidates survive as new queued subjects for GPT-5-mini, compared with 498K for Llama and 396K for DeepSeek.
These final counts are frontier nodes admitted into the BFS queue, not completed articles.

The dominant loss comes from redundancy.
Relative to raw candidates, only 3.5% of GPT-5-mini candidates survive canonical deduplication, compared with 12.7% for Llama and 13.2% for DeepSeek.
Among canonical survivors, NER accepts 71.9% for GPT-5-mini, 54.8% for Llama, and 51.7% for DeepSeek; similarity filtering then retains 60.2%, 73.0%, and 89.3%, respectively.
Overall, only 1.44% of GPT-5-mini raw candidates, 5.07% of Llama candidates, and 6.10% of DeepSeek candidates become new queued subjects.
This gap reflects the fact that each article emits many wikilinks, many of which are duplicate surface forms, generic non-entities, or semantic near-duplicates.

6
Conclusion

Benchmarks report 90%+ factuality for frontier LLMs, but materialized writing reveals a weaker picture.
On a uniform-random GPT-5-mini sample, Wikipedia covers only 61.3% of subjects, with 74.7% true rate on the covered subset – over 15 points below benchmark performance.
Web verification confirms the gap: frontier precision is 79.8%.
Three model families overlap on just 7.3% of subjects, and on 1,000 shared articles LLMpedia exceeds Grokipedia by +13 pp precision at half the textual similarity to Wikipedia.
All data, code, and interface are at
https://llmpedia.net
.

7
Limitations

Single-run conditionality and cost barrier.

Every article is the result of a single generation pass under a fixed prompt and temperature-0 decoding.
We do not claim that one pass fully characterizes what a model
knows
; it surfaces one sample from a latent knowledge distribution.
Self-consistency decoding
31
could recover additional knowledge, but the full project already costs approximately $3,000 across generation, ablation, evaluation, and preliminary testing (Appendix
B
); multi-sample exploration would multiply the dominant generation cost considerably. As shown in
5
, the choice of the seed entity is inconsequential.

Temporal snapshot.

All runs were conducted between January and March 2026 with the target models listed in
4
.
Wikipedia and web references were retrieved during the evaluation window, but both evolve continuously.
A claim judged unverifiable today may become verifiable or refutable later.

Evaluation stack and judge reliability.

Automatic LLM-based factuality evaluation is practical at our scale but not perfect.
Our pipeline relies on a single judge model,
gpt-4.1-nano
, for both claim extraction and verdict assignment, but this is common practice in research
10
;
25
.

Web evidence coverage and selection bias.

For frontier subjects, only 75.7% (292/386) yielded usable web evidence under the strict source-quality filtering described in Appendix
D
.
The remaining 24.3% are excluded from Tier 2 metrics, which may bias frontier estimates toward subjects with stronger public documentation.

Scale asymmetry.

Only gpt-5-mini reaches nearly one million articles.
The open-weight models are constrained to approximately 120K–498K, so the deepest hop-stratified analysis is GPT-5-mini specific.

Scope of measurement.

Our framework captures propositional factual support, but not everything that matters in an encyclopedia article.
It does not directly measure coherence, neutrality, salience, completeness, or omission of important facts.
An article can therefore achieve high precision while still being editorially unbalanced or incomplete.

Model and data-source limitations.

Although our goal is to analyze encyclopedic generation rather than surface or publish personal data, generated articles and public-source evidence may occasionally mention real individuals because encyclopedic subjects often include public figures. We did not design the pipeline to expose private or sensitive personal information, and any such content that is found inadvertently should be filtered from releases and removed from public-facing interfaces or downloadable artifacts.

Appendix E
Pipeline Implementation

E.1
System Configuration and Design Decisions

Execution modes.

LLMPedia supports two execution modes that trade latency against cost.
In
online mode
, API calls are issued concurrently across a multi-threaded worker pool, with all five pipeline stages running in parallel.
A global concurrency cap is enforced across all stages simultaneously to prevent rate-limit exhaustion.
In
batch mode
, requests are submitted to the OpenAI Batch API, which processes them asynchronously at a 50% cost reduction with a completion window of up to 24 hours.
The two modes are architecturally equivalent and produce identical outputs; batch mode was used in our main experiments for cost efficiency, while online mode is reserved for small-scale ablations where turnaround time matters.

Model configuration and stage independence.

Each pipeline stage—self-grounding, outline generation, article elicitation, NER filtering, and similarity arbitration—can be assigned an independent language model, temperature, and token budget.
In practice, all stages within a given run share a single model unless explicitly differentiated.
The one principled exception is the embedding model used for similarity deduplication, which is always configured independently: embedding and completion models serve fundamentally different functions and conflating their configuration would silently degrade deduplication quality.

BFS depth and article budget.

Expansion depth and total article count are both configurable hard caps.
In
topic-focused runs
(§
5.2
), depth is capped at two hops from the thematic seed to maintain topical coherence.
In
general-domain expansion
(§
5.3
), no depth cap is imposed: GPT-5-mini reaches hop 6 over 741K articles, with 94.2% of the corpus at hop 4 or beyond (Appendix
I
).
In both modes, the seed article at hop 0 is generated but excluded from evaluation metrics to avoid confounding the model’s generation behaviour with the trivially known anchor entity.

Similarity threshold and arbitration.

The cosine similarity threshold above which two entity embeddings trigger a deduplication check is set to 0.90.
This value is conservative: surface-form variants of the same entity (e.g.
United States
and
USA
) consistently fall above it, while genuinely distinct but thematically related entities consistently fall below it.
When the threshold is exceeded, a secondary LLM arbitration step is invoked rather than rejecting automatically.
This two-stage design prevents both false positives and false negatives.

NER confidence and elicitation filtering.

Under the calibrated prompt variants, both the NER stage and the elicitation stage assign explicit confidence scores.
Candidates below the NER confidence threshold are rejected before reaching the similarity stage.
Rejection at either stage is non-permanent: if a future parent article re-proposes the same entity with sufficient confidence, it re-enters the pipeline from the beginning.

Persona.

Each pipeline stage accepts a persona specification injected at the system level.
Three personas are provided: scientifically neutral, left-leaning, and conservative.
All three share identical structural prompts; only the framing and evaluative language differ.

Fault tolerance and strict-gate semantics.

Failed API calls are retried with exponential back-off up to a configurable maximum.
After all retries are exhausted, a strict-gate policy applies: a stage that cannot produce a valid output causes the corresponding entity to be
dropped
rather than passed through with a degraded result.
For NER, a parse failure produces no candidates rather than passing all candidates unchecked.
For similarity arbitration, an API failure causes the candidate to be treated as a duplicate and rejected.
This conservative policy introduces a small downward bias on recall but eliminates the risk of contaminating the corpus with candidates that bypassed quality controls.

CanonQueue
if and only if it simultaneously passes Stage 3 similarity
and
is absent from
CanonKeys
; both operations execute atomically.

Why commitment happens after similarity, not after NER.

A candidate is registered in
CanonKeys
only after it passes Stage 3.
If commitment happened at NER acceptance, an entity later rejected by similarity would be permanently suppressed even though it was never materialised.
Deferring commitment keeps future parent articles free to re-propose the same entity.

Within-batch collisions.

A single parent article may produce the same wikilink in two surface forms that canonicalize identically.
Within-wave cross-candidate deduplication handles this before any index update.

Correctness guarantee.

By induction on BFS waves, the embedding index at the start of wave
t
contains exactly the entities committed in waves
0,\ldots,t{-}1
.
The main article queue therefore contains no duplicate entities at any point during or after execution.

Appendix I
Hop-Stratified Analysis: Supplementary

I.1
Corpus Hop Distribution

The full GPT-5-mini BFS graph contains 741K surfaced subjects distributed as follows: hop 0: 1; hop 1: 58; hop 2: 2,344; hop 3: 40,923; hop 4: 318,296; hop 5+: 379,506.
Thus, the corpus is overwhelmingly deep: 94.2% of surfaced subjects lie at hop 4 or beyond.

I.2
Coverage–Error Decoupling

The central hop-level pattern is a coverage decline without a comparable explosion in false claims.
Wikipedia coverage falls from 100% at hop 1 to 60.0% at hop 5+.
Over the same range, precision declines from 96.0% to 86.6%, while false rate rises only from 4.0% to 8.8%.
What grows most sharply is the unverifiable rate, from 1.0% at hop 1 to 18.1% at hop 5+.
The main late-hop degradation is therefore not classical hallucination, but movement into regions where external evidence becomes sparse.

Appendix J
Prompt Templates

LLMPedia operates under two
generation regimes
and two

elicitation strategies
, yielding four prompt configurations.
The
general-domain
regime performs open BFS expansion from an
arbitrary seed with no topical constraint. The
topic-focused

regime also performs BFS but restricts expansion to entities with a
direct, meaningful connection to a designated root topic
(e.g.,
Ancient Babylon
). Orthogonally,
baseline

prompts request standard Wikitext with plain
[[wikilinks]]
,
while
calibrated
prompts additionally require each wikilink to
be annotated with a confidence score (
[[Entity (0.85)]]
)
reflecting the model’s certainty that the entity belongs to the
subject’s semantic neighborhood; links below threshold
\tau=0.75
are
discarded by the NER stage before reaching similarity arbitration.

Each subject passes through two prompt roles in sequence:

1.

Article elicitation
: generates the full Wikitext
article following a pre-generated section outline. The calibrated
variant requires scored wikilinks.

2.

NER filtering
: classifies each raw wikilink extracted
from the article as a true named entity worthy of BFS expansion or
a reject. The calibrated variant additionally assigns confidence
scores.

Runtime placeholders.

All prompts share the following runtime-filled placeholders:

•

{subject_name}
: the entity currently being
processed, e.g.
Vannevar Bush
.

•

{root_subject}
: the root topic constraining
topic-focused runs, e.g.
Ancient Babylon
; absent in
general-domain prompts.

•

{avg_words_per_article}
: target article length in
words (default 716, matching Wikipedia’s overall
average
34
).

•

{outline_block}
: the JSON section list produced by
the outline generation step and injected verbatim into elicitation
prompts; see Example
J
below.

•

The persona string is injected at the system level of

enabling LLMPedia to probe how the same parametric knowledge is
selected and framed under different editorial orientations.

Grokipedia’s construction process is not publicly disclosed.
Through examination of published articles, we identified indicators of retrieval-augmented rather than parametric generation.

Internal tool-calling traces.

Figure
16
shows an article where internal tool-calling dialogue is visible in published output—diagnostic of a pipeline where the generation model calls external retrieval tools at inference time.

–

These errors are characteristic of retrieval over a corpus where homonymous entities co-occur.

Consistency with capture-trap results.

These qualitative observations align with the quantitative capture-trap findings (§
5.4
): Grokipedia’s high Wikipedia similarity (TF-IDF 0.492) combined with low factual precision (76.7%) and high false rate (20.3%) is consistent with a retrieval pipeline that tracks Wikipedia’s surface form but introduces errors through entity disambiguation and passage conflation.
