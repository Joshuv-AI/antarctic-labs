A causal reinforcement learning framework for reliable gene regulatory network inference
Authors: Ruirui Ji, Wenzhuo Zhang, Yi Geng, Anjie Song, Yu Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGene regulatory network inference is crucial for revealing the mechanisms of cellular functions and the causal mechanisms of disease occurrence. However, traditional correlation-based methods struggle to identify causal directions, and deep learning methods lack structural interpretability. Moreover, neither of them can efficiently adapt to the search of the solution space for large-scale gene regulatory networks, making it difficult to accurately capture the real regulatory patterns between genes. This study proposes a gene regulatory network inference method based on causal reinforcement learning to address the above problems.ResultsExperiments on the DREAM5 dataset and scRNA-seq datasets of six cell types from BEELINE demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing mainstream baseline models in inference accuracy, structural sparsity, and biological plausibility. Further validation via GNNExplainer interpretability analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment shows that the constructed gene regulatory network clarifies causal relationships in gene regulation, enhances structural interpretability, and aligns closely with real biological networks and known biological pathways in both topological and functional aspects.ConclusionsThe proposed causal reinforcement learning-based framework realizes efficient global optimization of large-scale gene network structures, and the inferred regulatory networks possess high causal rationality and biological interpretability. These results confirm the feasibility and effectiveness of the method for real-world applications in systems biology and computational biology, providing a scalable approach for causal structure modeling of high-dimensional gene expression data.Keywords: Gene regulatory network, Causal reinforcement learning, Graph neural networks, Gene expression data, Structural causal model

Introduction

Gene Regulatory Network (GRN) refers to the collection of regulatory relationships between genes and their products [1–3]. GRN determines the activation and silencing of genes in a specific cellular environment, which is of great significance to reveal the diversity of cell functions and the mechanism of diseases caused by non-coding mutations [4, 5]. With the development of high-throughput sequencing technology, the accumulation of massive gene expression data provides data support for GRN inference [6], but it also brings challenges. Specifically, three key challenges remain unresolved, namely how to accurately distinguish the causality and correlation of regulation from high-dimensional data, how to describe the nonlinear interaction between genes under the causal framework, and how to balance the inference scale and biological interpretability. Therefore, the development of GRN inference methods with causal discrimination as the core, adaptability to large-scale data, and strong interpretability is a key issue to be urgently addressed in systems biology and computational biology.

Early GRN inference mainly relied on gene co-expression analysis or information-theoretic metrics to quantify the relationship strength between genes, and employed statistical approaches to mine association patterns from gene expression data for regulatory network reconstruction [7, 8]. For instance, WGCNA [9] calculates weighted correlations of gene expression to construct co-expression networks. ARACNe [10], by contrast, uses mutual information to quantify gene co-expression and applies the data processing inequality to eliminate indirect associations, thereby enabling regulatory network reconstruction. Another representative method, GENIE3 [11], decomposes network inference into regression problems for each gene and evaluates the feature importance of candidate regulators via the random forest algorithm. These methods are computationally efficient and exhibit a certain degree of robustness to data noise, and they have demonstrated outstanding performance in authoritative benchmark tests such as the DREAM challenges. However, such methods can only capture correlative relationships between genes. The inferred networks are often undirected or lack causal directionality, making it difficult to distinguish between direct regulation and indirect associations. To tackle this shortcoming, the research paradigm that treats GRN inference as causal structure discovery has gradually emerged [12]. Classic methods such as the PC algorithm [13] and GES/GIES algorithms [14] all assume the network to be a directed acyclic graph (DAG). Specifically, the PC algorithm prunes edges via conditional independence tests to construct a causal skeleton, while the GES/GIES algorithms perform a greedy search for the optimal DAG using a scoring function. Their core advantage lies in their ability to output directed edges, clarify regulatory directions, and theoretically reveal potential causal relationships. However, they face significant bottlenecks in practical applications. For example, under high-dimensional and small-sample scenarios, the reliability and efficiency of independence tests and searches decrease significantly, feedback loops and unobserved latent variables in real networks conflict with the DAG assumption, and classic linear models struggle to characterize the nonlinear effects of gene regulation.

To resolve the technical bottlenecks of classic causal methods, researchers have begun to integrate the strong feature learning capability of deep learning with causal modeling, leading to the development of novel GRN inference methods [15–17]. Such methods not only leverage deep learning to automatically mine potential interaction patterns from high-dimensional and complex data and effectively capture nonlinear regulatory dependencies between genes, but also anchor the directionality of regulatory relationships through causal modeling [18–20]. For example, GreyNet [21] targets time-series gene expression data. It captures dynamic interaction features of genes via an adaptive sliding window technique and combines generalized multivariate Granger causal regression to convert dynamic associations into directed causal links. This method has demonstrated excellent causal discrimination capability on datasets such as DREAM4. Another approach, GPO-VAE [22], introduces GRN-aligned parameter optimization in the latent space of a variational autoencoder (VAE). It explicitly models causal dependencies between genes through multi-hop causal accumulation and sparsity constraints, thereby enhancing the biological interpretability of inference results. Additionally, Feng et al. [23] input gene representations extracted by graph neural network (GNN) into an additive noise model (ANM) to estimate causal relationships, which significantly improves prediction accuracy across multiple datasets. Despite solving the technical bottlenecks of traditional causal methods, this type of integrated method still has limitations. On the one hand, the "black-box" nature of deep learning has not been effectively constrained by causal modeling, resulting in limited interpretability of results. On the other hand, causal modeling mostly remains limited to post-hoc verification or shallow constraints and does not permeate the entire feature learning process. Some models still rely on correlative signals for training and fail to truly integrate causal logic into the process of mining regulatory relationships [24]. Ultimately, this makes it difficult to accurately reveal the real causal mechanisms between genes. Additionally, the problem of inefficient exploration in traditional causal structure search, such as greedy search easily falling into local optima in the large solution space of gene regulatory networks (GRNs), has not been resolved by this type of integrated method.

To address the weak interpretability of the aforementioned methods that integrate deep learning with causal modeling, as well as the inefficient search issue where traditional causal structure search tends to get trapped in local optima within the large solution space of GRNs, the paradigm of causal reinforcement learning (CRL), a paradigm that combines the advantages of causal reasoning and reinforcement learning (RL), has gradually emerged [25]. The core idea of CRL is to incorporate causal relationship modeling into the RL process [26], thereby overcoming the limitations of traditional RL such as low sample efficiency and lack of interpretability in decision-making. Among existing CRL studies, the direction that treats causal structure discovery as a sequential decision-making process has shown significant potential [27]. For instance, Zhu et al. [28] proposed the first RL-based causal discovery algorithm. This algorithm converts DAG structure search into an action sequence in graph space and uses the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) score as a reward signal to achieve global optimization. Wang et al. [29] narrowed the search space through a variable ordering strategy, which improves the efficiency of large-scale data processing. Ahmed et al. [30], on the other hand, applied RL to active experimental design to maximize the identifiability of causal networks. These studies confirm that CRL not only alleviates the problems of low sample efficiency and poor interpretability but also exhibits significantly better exploration and optimization capabilities than traditional heuristic methods. GRN inference is essentially a problem that searches for and models high dimensional sparse causal structures [31]. It requires the accurate identification of regulatory relationships with clear causal directionality from large scale gene expression data and the selection of the optimal network structure that conforms to genuine biological properties in the enormous solution space. The application of CRL to GRN inference holds inherent advantages. Causal modeling can break the limitation of traditional methods that only capture correlations. It explicitly models the causal dependencies between genes to clarify regulatory directionality and distinguish direct from indirect regulation. This approach avoids the interference of spurious correlations and ensures the causal rationality and biological adaptability of the inferred results. RL is equipped with the capacity for sequential decision making and efficient exploration. This capacity can effectively tackle the challenge of the huge solution space in GRN inference. It helps to escape the dilemma that traditional exhaustive or greedy strategies are prone to local optima. RL can also adapt to the high noise characteristics of single cell data. It screens authentic regulatory signals from noisy backgrounds and removes spurious correlations through its sequential iterative exploration mechanism. Key constraints such as sparsity are integrated into the reward function to further improve the accuracy and structural rationality of the inference results.

This study proposes a GRN inference method based on CRL. This method achieves the collaborative optimization of causal modeling and RL through a modular design. Specifically, first, a state-action mapping and an experience replay mechanism are defined to represent the actions and states of GRN inference under the reinforcement learning strategy, and these elements are stored. Subsequently, a deep Q-network (DQN) composed of k-nearest neighbors (KNN), a structural causal model (SCM) layer, and a variational graph autoencoder (VGAE) is constructed as the core inference architecture to generate corresponding actions. Finally, action rewards are calculated using the BIC combined with the network loss function. This guides the model to favor causal networks with simplicity in structure and strong biological interpretability, and the inference results are ultimately output after iterative optimization. By leveraging the strong exploration capability of CRL and the interpretability advantage of causal modeling, this method ensures that the inference results conform to the characteristics of real biological networks.

The main contributions of this study are as follows.

(1) This study develops a CRL paradigm that integrates the SCM and VGAE for the task of GRN structure inference. By modeling network structure search as a Markov decision process and calculating action rewards via the BIC combined with the network loss function, this approach achieves efficient global optimization of large-scale and complex gene network structures, and provides a scalable and generalized framework for causal structure modeling of high-dimensional gene expression data.

(2) This study designs a mechanism that integrates initial graph construction via KNN, nonlinear feature compression by VGAE, and explicit SCM modeling. This mechanism effectively captures the complex and nonlinear regulatory dependencies between genes. It not only preserves the structural prior of local co-expression but also enhances the semantic expression capability of node representations. Meanwhile, it ensures that the inferred network possesses causal rationality and structural sparsity, thereby improving the accuracy and interpretability of inference results.

(3) Experimental results demonstrate that the inferred regulatory networks exhibit higher accuracy. This study further verifies through interpretability analysis and pathway enrichment analysis that the core genes identified are functionally highly consistent with known biological pathways. This confirms that the proposed method has practical biological significance in revealing potential regulatory mechanisms and identifying key regulators.

Methods

Overall model framework

The overall framework is illustrated in Fig. 1, which consists of four key components, namely the DQN, the reward feedback and experience replay mechanism, the state-action mapping, and the action execution and network update.

CRL-based GRN inference framework

Firstly, based on the gene expression matrix, an initial KNN graph is constructed via cosine similarity to preserve information on gene expression correlations. Subsequently, a VGAE is adopted to learn potential regulatory factors and capture the complex nonlinear dependency structure of gene expression. Based on latent vectors, a SCM is established. After that, a Markov decision process is defined based on RL. The state space includes the current graph structure, the noise parameters of the controlled SCM, and structural scores. The action involves adjusting the hyperparameters of the SCM layer and updating the adjacency matrix. The reward is designed to evaluate the quality of causal structures using the BIC. A DQN is used to guide action selection, strengthening the search for regulatory relationships with strong causal properties. Finally, under the guidance of causal rewards, a gene regulatory network that conforms to biological regulatory rules and exhibits high reliability is gradually inferred. The final output of the model is a binary causal regulatory adjacency matrix, where each entry indicates the presence or absence of a directed regulatory edge between the corresponding gene pair. This matrix constitutes the inferred gene regulatory network. In the unsupervised setting, the output is directly obtained from the optimized confidence matrix. In the supervised setting, the output is further aligned with gold-standard labels to improve accuracy.

Experimental process and result analysis

Experimental setup

To validate the effectiveness of the CRL-based GRN inference model proposed in this study, three experiments are conducted. These include comparative experiments, parameter analysis, interpretability analysis and biological significance analysis. This section elaborates on the experimental setup and implementation process in detail.

To evaluate the performance of the method proposed in this study, PCC [39], GENIE3 [11] and GRNRI [40] are selected as comparative methods for unsupervised learning. GENIE3 is a tree-based ensemble method that achieves network inference by decomposing the GRN inference problem into a series of regression problems. GRNRI is a GNN-based unsupervised model that uses a variational autoencoder model to infer GRN from single-cell RNA sequencing data. GNNLink [41], GENELink [42], GNE [43] and CNNC [44] are selected as comparative algorithms for supervised learning. RCL-OG [45] is selected as the RL-based comparative algorithm for supervised learning. This method obtains the inferred network by continuously sampling sequential graphs. To ensure the fairness of comparison, the order of network nodes is specified in the GRN inference task. This avoids the impact of ordering on network inference.

It is observed that the AUPRC performance varies across datasets under the unsupervised setting. The proposed method obtains a lower AUPRC on the E. coli dataset compared with the unsupervised baselines. The AUPRC on the S. cerevisiae dataset remains comparable to or slightly higher than most baselines. This variation can be well explained by the intrinsic characteristics of the AUPRC metric. AUPRC is strongly affected by the number of predicted edges and severe class imbalance in sparse biological networks. The proposed method is guided by the BIC score and explicit causal constraints in RL. It naturally favors sparse and biologically plausible network structures. The proposed method tends to produce sparser and more conservative network structures in the absence of supervised labels. This design reduces false positive predictions and improves structural plausibility. This may also lead to lower AUPRC values on certain datasets. Other methods may generate denser networks with more predicted edges. Such a pattern can achieve higher numerical AUPRC in the evaluation. The proposed method consistently achieves the best AUROC on both datasets. This result confirms its strong and stable ability to distinguish true regulatory relationships.

In summary, Table 1 demonstrates that the method proposed in this study exhibits a significant ability to outperform existing statistical and deep learning models in the unsupervised setting under gold standard data conditions. After the introduction of label supervision, the performance of the model is further improved. This indicates that the CRL mechanism possesses outstanding generality and scalability in modeling causal dependencies of gene regulation and optimizing structural accuracy.

As shown in Tables 4 and 5, module ablation experiments are conducted under both unsupervised and supervised settings on single-cell datasets including Non-Specific, Specific and STRING types, to quantitatively evaluate the independent contributions of the VGAE, SCM and RL components to GRN inference performance. Overall, the full model achieves the best AUROC on most cell types and evaluation conditions. The replacement or removal of any core component leads to obvious performance degradation and reduced stability. Specifically, when replacing the VGAE with a plain MLP encoder, the model loses the ability to model gene topological structures, resulting in average reductions of 15.9−17.3 percentage points in unsupervised AUROC and 14.2−15.2 percentage points in supervised AUROC across the TF+500 and TF+1000 scales. Completely removing the SCM deprives the model of explicit causal constraints and causes a sharp increase in spurious regulatory edges, decreasing the unsupervised and supervised AUROC by 16.1−18.5 and 13.7−14.5 percentage points on average, respectively. Replacing the RL-based adaptive structure exploration with a fixed greedy search makes the model prone to local optima and yields the most severe performance loss, with average AUROC drops of 22.6−25.9 and 24.3−27.1 percentage points under unsupervised and supervised settings, along with significantly increased prediction fluctuation and standard deviation. These trends remain highly consistent across both gene scales, demonstrating the stable and essential role of each module in high-dimensional and sparse single-cell data.

Meanwhile, it can be observed from Tables 4 and 5 that a few ablation variants achieve slightly higher AUROC than the full model on individual cell samples under both unsupervised and supervised settings. This phenomenon does not imply module ineffectiveness and mainly arises from two reasons. First, without the constraint of gold-standard regulatory labels, models with simplified structures tend to overfit local expression distributions in the unsupervised scenario, leading to metric overestimation. Second, the full model imposes multi-module joint regularization under supervision, which mildly restricts fitting capacity on samples with balanced distribution and low noise, thus causing local metric fluctuations. Nevertheless, the full model maintains remarkable advantages in terms of cross-dataset average performance, overall trends and robustness, and such local fluctuations do not affect the core conclusion.

Comprehensive statistical results from Tables 4 and 5 demonstrate that RL-driven global structure exploration serves as the primary source of model accuracy and robustness, whose adaptive search mechanism effectively alleviates the local optimum limitation of greedy search. The SCM causal layer suppresses false positive edges via explicit constraints, which is critical for ensuring the causal plausibility and biological interpretability of inferred networks. VGAE topological encoding provides high-quality node representations for causal modeling, forming the foundation of accurate GRN inference. The three components collaborate and complement each other to achieve accurate, stable and causally reliable GRN inference.

Conclusion

A GRN inference method based on CRL is proposed in this study. A DQN anchored on the VGAE and SCM is constructed to achieve efficient and accurate inference of GRN structures. First, an initial graph structure is constructed from gene expression data using the KNN module, which effectively captures the local correlation features among genes. Subsequently, feature embedding and dimensionality reduction are performed via the VGAE to generate latent vector representations of nodes, while causal relationships among genes are explicitly modeled in the SCM layer. Finally, network reconstruction is achieved using a dot product decoder, and a RL strategy is adopted with the BIC score employed as the reward function to continuously optimize the network structure. This allows the model to effectively approach the globally optimal structure and endow the inferred network with biological significance. In addition, interpretability analysis reveals that the network derived from the proposed method exhibits more complex and abundant node interaction structures, and is more consistent with the characteristics of real biological regulatory networks. Validation through KEGG pathway enrichment confirms that the inferred network is consistent with real biological networks and possesses biological significance.

Although the study achieves satisfactory performance in GRN inference, there is still room for further extension. In the future, we will extend our model from static analysis to temporal dynamic scenarios by incorporating time-series data during cell differentiation, disease progression, or drug intervention, and construct a dynamic causal inference framework to more accurately characterize the temporal changes of gene regulatory relationships.
