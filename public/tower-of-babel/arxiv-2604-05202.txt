The blow-up rate for a log non-scaling invariant semilinear wave equation in the conformal regime
Mohamed Ali Hamza

Abstract
We consider the blow-up behavior of solutions to the semilinear wave equation ∂ t 2 u − Δ ​ u = | u | p − 1 ​ u ​ ln a ⁡ ( u 2 + 2 ) , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ n × [ 0 , T ) , \partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u\ln^{a}(u^{2}+2),\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T), in the conformal case p = p c = 1 + 4 n − 1 p=p_{c}=1+\frac{4}{n-1} . Previous results in [ 22 , 23 ] show that for a ∈ ℝ a\in\mathbb{R} , solutions in the subconformal regime p < p c p<p_{c} blow up with a Type I rate at any non-characteristic point. The objective of this work is to extend this blow-up rate to the conformal regime under the assumption a < 0 a<0 . We establish an a priori upper bound for any blow-up solution and construct a Lyapunov functional in similarity variables. The resulting functional exhibits only weak dissipation, which necessitates delicate energy arguments to obtain the sharp blow-up rate in the conformal case. To the best of our knowledge, this provides the first result for the blow-up rate in a critical framework for an evolution problem where the scaling symmetry is broken.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation of the problem

This paper is devoted to the study of blow-up solutions for the
following semilinear wave equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u\ln^{a}(u^{2}+2),\qquad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u(t):x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\rightarrow u(x,t)\in{{\mathbb{R}}}.

We assume in addition that
n\geq 2
and

p=p_{c}\equiv 1+\frac{4}{n-1}.

(1.2)

The spaces
L^{2}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
H^{1}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are defined by

L^{2}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}/\sup_{d\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\int_{|x-d|\leq 1}|u(x)|^{2}dx)<+\infty\},

and

H^{1}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{u\in L^{2}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),|\nabla u|\in L^{2}_{loc,u}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\}.

Semilinear wave equations with a nonlinearity featuring a logarithmic factor have been used in various nonlinear physical models, such as nuclear physics, wave mechanics, optics, and geophysics (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
]
).

The Cauchy problem for the equation (
1.1

H^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, locally in
time. This follows from the finite speed
of propagation and the well-posedness in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

valid whenever
1<p<p_{S}
, where
p_{S}\equiv 1+\frac{4}{n-2}.

Combining the blow-up solutions for the associated ordinary differential equation of (
1.1
) and

blow-up solution
u(t)
of (
1.1
) which depends non-trivially on the space variable.

If

is an arbitrary blow-up solution of (
1.1
), we define
a 1-Lipschitz curve
\Gamma=\{(x,T(x))\}

such that

D=\{(x,t)\;|\;t<T(x)\}.

The justification of this fact can be found in Alinhac

In fact, for any

, we derive a solution

defined by

Let

denote the supremum of all such

.
This leads to two distinct scenarios:

•

Global Existence: If

, then by the geometry of light cones,
T(x)=\infty
for all

, and the solution exists globally in time.

•

Finite-time Blow-up: If
T(x)<+\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the domain

is the union of all such maximal backward light cones of the type

as described in (
1.3

The time

.
A point

is non characteristic
if there are

\delta_{0}\in(0,1)\mbox{ and }t_{0}<T(x_{0})\mbox{ such that }u\;\;\mbox{is defined on }{\mathscr{C}}_{x_{0},T(x_{0}),\delta_{0}}\cap\{t\geq t_{0}\},

where

In the case

1.1

\partial^{2}_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\,\,\,(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,T),

The blow-up rate of singular solutions to (

This specific behavior is classified as Type I blow-up (or the ODE regime).
Conversely, any solution that does not follow this rate is classified as Type II.

Specifically, if

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon_{0}(p,n)\leq(T(x_{0})-t)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},T(x_{0})-t))}}{(T(x_{0})-t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}

\displaystyle+(T(x_{0})-t)^{\frac{2}{p-1}+1}\Big(\frac{\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},T(x_{0})-t))}}{(T(x_{0})-t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}+\frac{\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},T(x_{0})-t))}}{{(T(x_{0})-t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}}\Big)\leq K_{0},

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\tilde{E}_{\alpha_{0}}(w(s))=\begin{cases}-2\alpha_{0}\displaystyle\int\frac{(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\rho_{\alpha_{0}}}{1-|y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y,&\text{if }p<p_{c},\\[15.0pt]
-\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,&\text{if }p=p_{c}.\end{cases}

Establishing (

Furthermore, the argument employs Sobolev interpolations, critical Gagliardo-Nirenberg estimates, and a geometric covering technique specifically adapted to the blow-up surface to deduce the required estimate. It is worth noting that, unlike the sub-conformal case, the dissipation in the conformal regime degenerates at the boundary, necessitating refined arguments to address this additional difficulty.

The validity of these results relies on the fact that, although (

In particular, the leading-order dynamics are governed by the ordinary differential equation

It is important to note that, in the conformal regime, the dissipation degenerates at the boundary even in the unperturbed case, and this property persists under perturbations.

The blow-up dynamics associated to equation (

Accordingly, in the subconformal and conformal regimes, it is natural to expect that the blow-up rate is dictated by the solution of the ordinary differential equation:

v_{T}^{\prime\prime}(t)=|v_{T}(t)|^{p-1}v_{T}(t)\ln^{a}\big(v_{T}^{2}(t)+2\big),\quad v(T)=\infty.

Clearly, as

In the subconformal regime, we previously investigated the blow-up dynamics for the nonlinear wave equation (

Our main strategy relies on the construction of a Lyapunov functional in similarity variables.
As previously noted, the conformal case poses significantly greater challenges than the subconformal regime, requiring a novel approach. This is precisely the difficulty addressed in the present work: the methodologies developed in

In the subconformal regime (
p<p_{c}
), the approach in
[
23
]
begins with the introduction of a functional
g_{0}(s)
associated with equation (
1.1

\frac{d}{ds}g_{0}(s)\leq-\alpha_{0}\int_{B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\frac{\rho_{\alpha_{0}}(y)}{1-|y|^{2}}dy+\frac{C}{s}g_{0}(s),

which yields a rough polynomial bound in
s
for the solution in space-time averages. Furthermore, by utilizing elementary estimates for the nonlinear term, the analysis can be reduced to the pure power case up to an
\epsilon

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into

\frac{d}{ds}g_{1}(s)\leq-\alpha_{0}\int_{B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\frac{\rho_{\alpha_{0}}(y)}{1-|y|^{2}}dy+\frac{C}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}g_{1}(s).

From this ordinary differential inequality, we naturally derive a Lyapunov functional. With this functional established, the interpolation strategy from our previous work can be adapted in a straightforward manner.

We recall that in the conformal case (
p=p_{c}
), the parameter
\alpha_{0}(p_{c})
vanishes, rendering the construction of a functional satisfying the differential inequality (
1.17

[

to the conformal case.

The first step consists of introducing a functional associated with equation (
1.1
) in similarity variables
which satisfies the following differential inequality:

\frac{d}{ds}h_{0}(s)\leq-c\eta\int_{B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\frac{\rho_{\eta}(y)}{1-|y|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}y+C\eta h_{0}(s),\qquad\forall\eta>0.

By utilizing this functional, we derive an exponential bound (in
s
) for the

norm of the solution of (
1.2
) in terms of space-time averages. Specifically, we obtain the estimates (
2.3
) and (

) below. Subsequently, we derive a first functional from a Pohozaev identity by multiplying equation (
2.2

2.2

. By combining these two functionals, we establish a new estimate that controls the time-averaged

By exploiting these exponential bounds, we construct a second functional

\frac{d}{ds}h_{1}(s)\leq-\alpha(s)\int_{B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\frac{\rho}{1-|y|^{2}}dy+\frac{C}{s}h_{1}(s).

Consequently, we establish a polynomial bound (in terms of

Using this result, we derive a Lyapunov functional for equation (

\frac{d}{ds}h_{2}(s)\leq-\alpha(s)\int_{B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}\frac{\rho}{1-|y|^{2}}dy.

Although this functional provides the foundation for our optimal estimate, the weakness of the damping term presents a novel complication. Indeed, by using the estimate related to

This complication arises because the problem resides in the logarithmically subconformal regime. This specific obstacle is particular to our study and represents a gap in the literature. To overcome this difficulty,
by expressing the equation (

over the ball, we construct a new energy functional

A deeper understanding of the dynamics allows us to establish more precise control over the damping term; specifically, we obtain the following uniform estimate:

\int_{s}^{s+1}\int_{\partial B}(\partial_{s}w)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\sigma{\mathrm{d}}\tau\leq C.

(1.18)

By virtue of this improvement, we conclude that the time average of the

M_{\eta}(w(s))
, is derived from a Pohozaev-type identity obtained by multiplying equation (
2.2
) with
(y\cdot\nabla w)\rho
. The second functional,
J_{\eta}(w(s))
, arises by multiplying equation (
2.2
) by

. We then introduce the composite functional
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}(w(s))=M_{1/2+\eta}(w(s))+bJ_{\eta}(w(s))
, where the constant

is chosen to cancel the singular term
\int_{B}|\nabla_{\theta}w|^{2}\frac{|y|^{2}\rho_{\eta}}{1-|y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}y
upon estimating the time derivative of
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}(w(s))
. This construction enables us to control the time average of the nonlinear term with a singular weight
representing one of the primary contributions of this paper.
Consequently, the preceding analysis allows us to derive a bound for the time average of the singular integral
\int_{B}|\nabla_{\theta}w|^{2}\frac{\rho_{\eta}}{1-|y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}y.
