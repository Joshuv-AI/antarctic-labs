Sticky CIR process with potential: invariant measure and exact sampling
Tony Shardlow

Abstract
We study the sticky Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) process in one dimension, a diffusion on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) with a sticky boundary condition at the origin, arising as the marginal process in a sparse Bayesian inference framework based on Hadamard–Langevin dynamics. For the parameter range δ ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \delta\in(1,2) , in which the origin is accessible but not absorbing, we prove well-posedness of the process and uniqueness of its invariant measure, which is a mixture of a point mass at zero and a weighted gamma-type density on the interior. We derive an explicit Green’s function for the resolvent in terms of confluent hypergeometric functions, and use this to construct an exact sampler for the invariant measure in the zero-potential case. For a non-trivial potential G G , we establish existence and uniqueness of the tilted invariant measure via a Girsanov change of measure, and develop two sampling algorithms: a Metropolis–Hastings corrected sampler that targets the invariant measure exactly, and an unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) that is cheaper per step but introduces an O ⁡ ( h ) O(h) bias. Numerical experiments confirm the predicted behaviour: the Metropolis–Hastings sampler achieves the target invariant measure at all step sizes, while the ULA exhibits the expected O ⁡ ( h ) O(h) bias.

Introduction

We establish well-posedness and develop sampling methods for the invariant measure of the following sticky CIR process.
For potential function
G\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, stickiness parameter
\mu>0
, dissipation parameter
\lambda>0
, CIR parameter
\delta\in(1,2)
, inverse temperature
\beta>0

\displaystyle\begin{split}du_{t}&=\left[\frac{\delta-1}{\beta\,u_{t}}-\lambda\,u_{t}-G^{\prime}(u_{t})\right]dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\,dW_{t},\\
\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{t}=0\}}\,dt&=\frac{\exp(-\beta G(0))}{\mu}d\ell_{t}^{0}(u),\end{split}

(1.1)

1.1
Motivation

The motivation for this work is the sparse sampling methodology of
5

To place this in a Bayesian inverse-problem context, consider recovering an unknown

The central observation behind our approach is that this same Gibbsian reweighting also describes the invariant measure of the one-dimensional

2.1
Scale function, speed measure, and local time

Definition 2.1
(regular
diffusion).

A
regular diffusion
on an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
in the sense of Itô–McKean, is a continuous strong Markov process

X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
where every point of
I
is
accessible
from every other point with positive probability. In terms of
\tau_{y}=\inf\{t>0\colon X_{t}=y\}
, this means, for all
x
in the interior of
I
and all
y\in I
, that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{y}<\infty\mid X_{0}=x)>0
. See
(
19
, Definition 45.2)
or
(
12
, Chapter 15.1)
or
(
18
, Chapter VII, before Proposition 3.1)
.

Every regular diffusion on
I
is associated to a strictly increasing function
s\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
(the
scale
function
) and a Radon measure
m
on
I
with full support (the

speed measure
). Explicit definitions for
s
and
m
are given in terms of hitting probabilities and expected occupation times in
(
11
, Section 4.1)
or
(
18
, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.6)
. Using
s
and
m
, the generator
\mathcal{L}
of
X
is

\mathcal{L}f=\frac{d}{dm}\!\left(\frac{df}{ds}\right),

for
f
in the domain of
\mathcal{L}
, where
df/ds
denotes the derivative of
f
with respect to the scale function (i.e.,
\lim_{y\to x}(f(y)-f(x))/(s(y)-s(x))
), and
d/dm
denotes the Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to the speed measure. This operator is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(m)
and the Lebesgue adjoint operator is

(\mathcal{L}^{*}f)(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{s^{\prime}(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{f(x)}{m^{\prime}(x)}\right),

(2.1)

where
m^{\prime}(x)
is the Lebesgue density of the speed measure
m
and
s^{\prime}(x)
is the derivative of the scale function. In the case of a smooth drift
b\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
and smooth non-zero diffusion
\sigma\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
, the diffusion with generator
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(x)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+b(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
has, for
x
in the interior of
I
,

s(x)=\int^{x}\exp\left(-2\int^{y}\frac{b(z)}{\sigma^{2}(z)}\,dz\right)dy,\quad m^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}(x)s^{\prime}(x)},

(2.2)

where the scale function is only defined up to an additive constant. See

(
18
, Chapter VII, Theorem 3.12 and Exercise 3.20)
or
(
11
, Section 4.2)
.

Intuitively, the scale function
s
captures the drift of the process so that the transformed process
s(X_{t})
is a local martingale on
s(I)
. As a consequence, by optional stopping, the hitting times
\tau_{a},\tau_{b}
to levels
a<x<b
satisfy

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{a}<\tau_{b}\mid X_{0}=x)=\frac{s(b)-s(x)}{s(b)-s(a)},

so
s
acts as a coordinate in which exit probabilities are linear in position. The speed measure
m
captures how long the process lingers in each region; areas with high density with respect to
m
are visited more frequently and for longer durations. In particular, when the speed measure has an atom at the boundary, that point is said to be
slowly reflecting
in
(
18
, Chapter VII, Definition 3.11)
or
sticky
, where the process sojourns or temporarily remains (in contrast to absorbing boundary points where the process remains forever). The speed measure also yields the invariant measure: if
m
is finite, then the invariant measure is
m
normalised to be a probability measure.

Remark 2.2
.

Some authors (e.g.,
(
12
, Chapter 15)
or
(
19
, Theorem 52.1)
) define the speed measure with an additional factor of
1/2
, so
m^{\prime}(x)=1/(\sigma^{2}(x)s^{\prime}(x))
. This changes some formulas by a factor of
2
but does not affect the underlying theory.

The sojourn condition is expressed in terms of diffusion local time
(
19
, Theorem 49.1)
.
It is the natural local time for diffusions and is defined via the speed measure.

Definition 2.3
(diffusion local time).

For a diffusion
X
with speed measure
m
, the
diffusion local time

\ell_{t}^{a}
at level
a
is defined by

\ell^{a}_{t}(X)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{m((a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon))}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{s}-a|<\varepsilon\}}\,ds.

The following occupation-time formula holds:
for measurable
f\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s})\,ds=\int f(a)\,\ell^{a}_{t}(X)\,m(da).

(2.3)

When
m
has an atom and the process sojourns at the boundary, this definition applies naturally. If
m(\{0\})=1/\mu
, for some
\mu>0
, then taking the limit gives

\ell^{0}_{t}(X)=\mu\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s}=0\}}\,ds.

This is often written
\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}=0\}}\,dt=\frac{1}{\mu}\,d\ell^{0}_{t}(X)
and known as the
“sojourn condition”
. It says the length of the sojourn at the boundary is determined by the diffusion local time and stickiness parameter
\mu
; the limit
\mu\to 0
gives absorption and infinite time at the boundary, and

\mu\to\infty
gives instantaneous reflection and zero time at the boundary.

Remark 2.4
.

Diffusion local time
\ell_{t}^{a}
differs from the local time
L_{t}^{a}
defined via the Tanaka formula
(
18
, Chapter VI, Corollary 1.2)
. In that case, the occupation-time formula
(
18
, Chapter VI, Corollary 1.6)
holds with respect to the quadratic variation: for a continuous semimartingale
X
and measurable
f\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s})\,d\langle X\rangle_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(a)L_{t}^{a}(X)\,da.

In the interior, the two local times are related by
\ell^{a}_{t}=s^{\prime}(a)L^{a}_{t}/2
, where
s
is the scale function.

2.2
Reflecting CIR
process

The Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) process
(
6
)
is defined by

{du_{t}=\left[\frac{\delta-1}{\beta\,u_{t}}-\lambda\,u_{t}\right]\,dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\,dW_{t},\qquad u_{0}=x>0,\\
}

(2.4)

with parameters
\lambda,\beta>0
and

\delta\in(1,2)
and a standard Brownian motion
W_{t}
.
By
Eq.

2.2
with
b(x)=(\delta-1)/(\beta x)-\lambda x
and
\sigma^{2}=2/\beta
, the scale and speed densities for
Eq.

2.4
are

s^{\prime}(x)=x^{1-\delta}\exp(\lambda\beta x^{2}/2),\qquad m^{\prime}(x)=\beta x^{\delta-1}\exp(-\lambda\beta x^{2}/2),\qquad x\in(0,\infty).

(2.5)

Near the origin,
s^{\prime}(x)\sim x^{1-\delta}
is integrable if and only if
\delta<2
, in which case
s(0+)>-\infty
and
\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(s(x)-s(0+))\,m(dx)<\infty
; Feller’s test
(
12
, Lemma 6.1)
then makes the boundary at
0
accessible, which is essential for the sticky case that we focus on in this paper. For
\delta\geq 2
, the boundary is inaccessible. The lower endpoint
\delta>1
ensures
m^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
, so the interior part of the invariant measure vanishes near the boundary. We restrict to
\delta\in(1,2)
throughout.

We show this equation has a
well-defined solution
u_{t}\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.5
(well-posedness of reflecting CIR process).

There exists a unique strong solution
u_{t}
of
Eq.

2.4

for
\delta\in(1,2)
. The solution
u_{t}
is non-negative for all

t\geq 0
. Further,
u=0
is accessible and
u_{t}
is
reflected instantaneously at
u=0
; equivalently, the speed measure has no atom at the boundary,
m(\{0\})=0
.

By Itô’s formula,
Y_{t}=u_{t}^{2}
satisfies

dY_{t}=\frac{2\delta}{\beta}\,dt-2\lambda Y_{t}\,dt+\sqrt{\frac{8Y_{t}}{\beta}}\,dW_{t}.

(2.6)

That is,
Y_{t}
is a mean-reverting squared Bessel process of dimension
\delta
. By the classical theory of squared Bessel processes with
\delta\in(1,2)

(
9
, Section 3)
,
Y_{t}
admits a unique non-negative strong solution and the boundary at
0
is reached and reflected instantaneously:
\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}=0\}}\,ds=0
a.s. Setting
u_{t}=\sqrt{Y_{t}}
yields a unique non-negative strong solution of
Eq.

2.4
with
\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{s}=0\}}\,ds=0
, so the occupation-time formula
Eq.

2.3
forces
m(\{0\})=0
.
∎

3
Sticky CIR process (no potential)

We turn to the CIR process with a sticky boundary condition (
Eq.

1.1
with
G=0
),

\displaystyle du_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\delta-1}{\beta\,u_{t}}-\lambda\,u_{t}\right]\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{t}>0\}}\,dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\,dW_{t},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{t}=0\}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu}d\ell_{t}^{0}(u),

with initial condition
u_{0}=x>0
. The second line is the sojourn condition where
\ell_{t}^{0}
is the diffusion
local time at
0
and
\mu>0
controls the degree of stickiness (see
Definition

2.3
).

Theorem 3.1
(well-posedness of the sticky CIR
process).

The sticky CIR process defined by
Eq.

3.1
has a unique
weak solution
u_{t}
for
\delta\in(1,2)
and any stickiness
parameter
\mu>0
. The solution
u_{t}
is a regular
diffusion process.

The Itô–McKean time-change construction of
17
for the sticky Bessel process applies verbatim, with his instantaneously reflecting Bessel process replaced by the instantaneously reflecting CIR process
\tilde{u}_{t}
of
Theorem

2.5
. A weak solution is obtained as
u_{t}=\tilde{u}_{T_{t}}
, where
T_{t}=A_{t}^{-1}
and
A_{t}=t+\mu^{-1}\ell_{t}^{0}(\tilde{u})
. Conversely, any weak solution recovers
\tilde{u}
via the inverse time change
\tilde{u}_{t}=u_{A_{t}}
, with
A_{t}=\inf\{s:T_{s}>t\}
and
T_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{s}>0\}}\,ds
; since
\ell^{0}(\tilde{u})
is locally finite, the time change is defined for all
t\geq 0
, and strong uniqueness of
\tilde{u}
(
Theorem

2.5
) gives weak uniqueness of
u
. The mean-reversion drift
-\lambda\tilde{u}_{t}
enters only through the drift of
\tilde{u}
and passes through the time change unchanged. Regularity of
u_{t}
in the sense of
Definition

2.1
follows from the general theory of time-changed strong Markov processes
(
21
)
:
\tilde{u}
is regular by
Theorem

2.5
, and time change by a continuous strictly-increasing additive functional preserves regularity.
∎

3.1
Invariant measure and
ergodicity

The next theorem gives ergodicity of the sticky CIR process, and presents the invariant measure as the sum of an atom at the boundary and a continuous part on the interior.

Theorem 3.2
(ergodicity of the sticky CIR
process).

Let
u_{t}
be the sticky CIR process defined by
Eq.

3.1

with parameters
\lambda,\beta>0
,
\delta\in(1,2)
, and
stickiness parameter
\mu\in(0,\infty)
.

1.

There exists a unique invariant
probability measure
\pi_{0}
on
[0,\infty)
given by

\pi_{0}(dx)=\frac{1}{Z}\left[\frac{1}{\mu}\delta_{0}(dx)+\beta x^{\delta-1}e^{-\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}\,dx\right]

where
Z=1/{\mu}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta x^{\delta-1}e^{-\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}\,dx<\infty
.

2.

For any initial point
x\in[0,\infty)
and
any bounded measurable function
\phi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{t}\phi(x)=\int_{[0,\infty)}\phi(y)\,\pi_{0}(dy),

where
P_{t}\phi(x)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(u_{t})\mid u_{0}=x]
.

The sticky SDE (
3.1
) shares the interior dynamics of the reflecting CIR process, so the interior scale and speed densities are given by
Eq.

2.5
.
The sticky boundary condition adds a point mass at
0
with mass

m(\{0\})=1/\mu
as discussed in
Definition

2.3
,
giving the full speed measure
m(dx)=m^{\prime}(x)\,dx+\mu^{-1}\delta_{0}(dx)
.
Since
m
is the speed measure of a regular diffusion (
Theorem

3.1
), it is an invariant

\sigma
-finite measure
(
18
, Chapter V)
. As
\delta>1
,

m([0,\infty))=\frac{1}{\mu}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta x^{\delta-1}e^{-\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}\,dx<\infty,

so
\pi_{0}=m/m([0,\infty))
is a well-defined invariant probability measure.

The process is conservative, irreducible with respect to
m
, and admits a
finite invariant measure. Hence,
14
applies and we conclude that
\pi_{0}
is the unique invariant measure and
P_{t}\phi(x)\to\pi_{0}(\phi)

for all bounded measurable
\phi
.
∎

3.2
Transition density and boundary
condition

We identify the Fokker–Planck equation for the transition density, including the boundary condition at
u=0
.

Theorem 3.3
(transition density).

On
(0,\infty)
, the Lebesgue density
f
of the CIR process defined by
Eq.

3.1
solves

\partial_{t}f=-\partial_{u}\!\left(\left(\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta u}-\lambda u\right)f\right)+\tfrac{1}{\beta}\partial_{uu}f.

If
p
denotes the density with respect to the speed measure
m
,
then at
u=0
the sticky (Wentzell) boundary condition holds

\partial_{s}p(t,0^{+})=\tfrac{1}{\mu}\,\partial_{t}p(t,0),

(3.2)

where
\partial_{s}=(1/s^{\prime})\partial_{u}
is the derivative in scale.

The Lebesgue-density
f(t,u)
of the process on
(0,\infty)
satisfies
the Fokker–Planck equation
\partial_{t}f=\mathcal{L}^{*}f
, where
\mathcal{L}^{*}
is the

L^{2}(du)
-adjoint of the generator. Substituting the coefficients

b(u)=(\delta-1)/(\beta u)-\lambda u
and
\sigma^{2}=2/\beta
into
\mathcal{L}^{*}f=-\partial_{u}(bf)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{uu}(\sigma^{2}f)

gives the stated equation.

Let
p_{0}(t)=\mathbb{P}(u_{t}=0)
denote the mass at the sticky
point. Conservation of probability gives

p_{0}(t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t,u)\,du=1.

Differentiating in
t
and substituting
\partial_{t}f=\mathcal{L}^{*}f
:

\partial_{t}p_{0}(t)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{*}f\,du=\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}J(t,u),

where
J(t,u)
is the probability flux, and we used that the flux decays at infinity.
From
Eq.

2.1
, the Lebesgue-adjoint generator is

\mathcal{L}^{*}f=\frac{d}{du}\!\bigl(\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\frac{d}{du}\frac{f}{m^{\prime}}\bigr)
,
so the flux

J(t,u)=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}(u)}\,\frac{d}{du}\frac{f(t,u)}{m^{\prime}(u)}=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}(u)}\,\partial_{u}p(t,u)=\partial_{s}p(t,u),

(3.3)

where
p=f/m^{\prime}
is the speed measure density and we used
\partial_{s}=(1/s^{\prime})\partial_{u}
.
The sticky boundary contributes a point mass
m(\{0\})=1/\mu
, so writing the boundary mass in terms of the speed measure density gives

p_{0}(t)=\mathbb{P}(u_{t}=0)={p(t,0)}/{\mu}
.
Substituting into the flux-balance equation
\partial_{t}p_{0}(t)=J(t,0^{+})
, we find

\tfrac{1}{\mu}\,\partial_{t}p(t,0)=\partial_{s}p(t,0^{+}),

which rearranges to the Wentzell condition
Eq.

3.2
.
∎

4
Sampling the invariant measure

Our sampling algorithms are based on an explicit form for the transition distribution for exponentially distributed times. This is built on the following Green’s function.

4.1
Green’s function
G_{\alpha}

For
\alpha>0
, the
\alpha
-Green’s function (or resolvent kernel) of the
reflecting CIR process
\tilde{u}
is defined by

\int_{A}G_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m(dy)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\,\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{u}_{t}\in A}\,dt\mid\tilde{u}_{0}=x\right],\qquad A\subseteq(0,\infty),

(4.1)

so
G_{\alpha}(x,y)
is the density of the discounted occupation measure with
respect to the speed measure
m
. Writing
p(t;x,y)
for the transition density of
\tilde{u}
with respect to
m
, this is equivalently the Laplace transform

G_{\alpha}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\,p(t;x,y)\,dt.

(4.2)

Alternatively, if
T_{\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
is independent of
\tilde{u}
, then

G_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m(dy)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\mathbb{P}(\tilde{u}_{T_{\alpha}}\in dy\mid\tilde{u}_{0}=x),

(4.3)

so
\alpha\,G_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m(dy)
is the transition distribution at the
random time
T_{\alpha}
.
The distributions after exponential times are computable and can be used to construct efficient sampling algorithms for the invariant measure of the sticky CIR process, as we will see in

Sections

5.1
and
4.3
.

Analytically,
f(x)=G_{\alpha}(x,y)
(as a function of
x
for fixed
y
)
solves the resolvent equation

(\alpha-\mathcal{L})\,f=\delta_{y},

(4.4)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the generator of
\tilde{u}
. This is interpreted in the speed-measure sense; that is,
\int\phi(x)(\alpha-\mathcal{L})f(x)\,m(dx)=\phi(y)
for any test function
\phi
.

Away from
x=y
,
Eq.

4.4
reduces to the
homogeneous ODE
(\alpha-\mathcal{L})f=0
, which is Kummer’s equation

f^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{\delta-1}{x}-\lambda\beta x\right)f^{\prime}-\alpha\beta\,f=0.

(4.5)

Setting
z=\lambda\beta x^{2}/2
, a direct calculation transforms
Eq.

4.5
into
z\,w^{\prime\prime}+(b-z)\,w^{\prime}-a\,w=0
, for

a=\alpha/(2\lambda)
,
b=\delta/2
. This is Kummer’s confluent hypergeometric equation, whose linearly independent solutions are the confluent hypergeometric functions of the first kind,
M(a,b,z)={}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;z)
(regular at
z=0
, with
M(a,b,0)=1
), and of the second kind,
U(a,b,z)
(decaying at
z=\infty
, with
U(a,b,z)\sim z^{-a}
); we use the notation of
16
. Therefore,
Eq.

4.5
has solutions
M(a,b,z_{x})
and
U(a,b,z_{x})
, where
z_{x}=\lambda\beta x^{2}/2
.

The Green’s function is constructed by matching at
x=y
: the decaying solution

U
is used for the larger argument and the regular solution
M
for the
smaller,

G_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{f_{0}(x\wedge y)\,U(a,b,z_{x\vee y})}{|\mathcal{W}|},

(4.6)

where
f_{0}(x)=M(a,b,z_{x})+c\,U(a,b,z_{x})
with
c
determined by the boundary condition and
|\mathcal{W}|
is the Wronskian normalisation that ensures the jump condition at
x=y
is satisfied.
See
(
19
;
4
)
for the general theory of Green’s functions for one-dimensional diffusions.

The next lemma collects three boundary quantities that recur throughout: the scale derivatives of
f_{0}
and
f_{\infty}
at
0^{+}
, used in
Lemma

4.3
to impose the Wentzell sticky condition; the Wronskian normalisation
|\mathcal{W}|
from
Eq.

4.6
; and the boundary value
G_{\alpha}(0,0)=U_{0}/|\mathcal{W}|
, which determines the leave probability
\Phi=1/G_{\alpha}(0,0)
of
Corollary

4.5
. Each is given in closed form via the gamma function.

Lemma 4.1
.

(i)

The scale derivatives of
f_{0}(x)=M(a,b,z_{x})
and

f_{\infty}(x)=U(a,b,z_{x})
at the boundary satisfy

\frac{df_{0}}{ds}(0^{+})=0,\qquad\frac{df_{\infty}}{ds}(0^{+})=-|\mathcal{W}|.

(ii)

G_{\alpha}(0,0)={U_{0}}/{|\mathcal{W}|},

where
U_{0}=U(a,b,0)=\frac{\Gamma(1-b)}{\Gamma(1+a-b)}
and

|\mathcal{W}|=\lambda\beta\,\frac{\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a)}\,\Bigl(\tfrac{\lambda\beta}{2}\Bigr)^{-b}.

(4.7)

(i) The scale density is
s^{\prime}(x)=x^{1-\delta}e^{\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}
.
Since
M(a,b,z)=1+\frac{a}{b}z+O(z^{2})
, we have

f_{0}^{\prime}(x)\sim\frac{a}{b}\lambda\beta x
as
x\downarrow 0
, so

f_{0}^{\prime}(x)/s^{\prime}(x)\sim\frac{a\lambda\beta}{b}x^{\delta}\to 0
,
confirming
(df_{0}/ds)(0^{+})=0
.

The Wronskian satisfies the identity
W_{z}[M,U]\coloneqq MU^{\prime}-M^{\prime}U=-\frac{\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a)}z^{-b}e^{z}

(
16
, §13.2.34)
. Since
dz_{x}/dx=\lambda\beta x
, this gives

f_{0}(x)f_{\infty}^{\prime}(x)-f_{\infty}(x)f_{0}^{\prime}(x)=W_{z}[M,U]\big|_{z_{x}}\,\lambda\beta x=-\frac{\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a)}\Bigl(\frac{\lambda\beta x^{2}}{2}\Bigr)^{\!-b}e^{\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}\,\lambda\beta x.

Dividing by
s^{\prime}(x)
, the exponentials cancel and the net power of
x

vanishes (since
x^{-2b}\,x\,x^{\delta-1}=1
), giving the constant

\frac{f_{0}f_{\infty}^{\prime}-f_{\infty}f_{0}^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}(x)}=-\frac{\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a)}\,\lambda\beta\left(\frac{\lambda\beta}{2}\right)^{-b}\eqqcolon-|\mathcal{W}|.

By Abel’s theorem applied to the ODE (
4.5
) with coefficient

p(x)=(\delta-1)/x-\lambda\beta x
, the Wronskian satisfies

W[f_{0},f_{\infty}](x)=C\exp(-\int p\,dx)
. Since

\exp(-\int p\,dx)=x^{1-\delta}e^{\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}=s^{\prime}(x)

by
Eq.

2.5
, the ratio
W[f_{0},f_{\infty}](x)/s^{\prime}(x)
is
constant in
x
, and the calculation above identifies this constant
as
-|\mathcal{W}|
.
Taking
x\to 0^{+}
and using
f_{0}(0)=1
,
(df_{0}/ds)(0^{+})=0
, the Wronskian identity gives

\frac{df_{\infty}}{ds}(0^{+})=-|\mathcal{W}|.

(ii) Note that
M(a,b,0)=1
and
U(a,b,0)=\Gamma(1-b)/\Gamma(1+a-b)\eqqcolon U_{0}
from
(
16
, (13.2.2) and (13.2.18))
(under the condition
b\in(0,1)
, equivalently
\delta\in(0,2)
). From
G_{\alpha}(x,y)=f_{0}(x\wedge y)\,f_{\infty}(x\vee y)/|\mathcal{W}|
, we have that

G_{\alpha}(0,0)=U_{0}/|\mathcal{W}|
. ∎

4.2
Transition distributions for the sticky CIR process

We derive the transition distribution
\mathbb{P}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in\cdot\mid u_{0}=x)

for
x\geq 0
, where
T_{\alpha}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\alpha)
is
independent of
u
. The key step is to
impose the Wentzell sticky boundary condition on the resolvent.

Lemma 4.2
(resolvent boundary condition).

The resolvent kernel
G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)
, viewed as a function
of its first argument at fixed
y\in(0,\infty)
, solves

(\alpha-\mathcal{L})G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y}
on
(0,\infty)

in the speed-measure sense, with boundary condition

\frac{dG^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(\cdot,y)}{ds}(0^{+})=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(0,y).

(4.8)

By the symmetry
G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)=G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(y,x)
, the same condition holds in the second argument.

By standard properties of Green’s functions, the function
x\mapsto G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)
solves
the resolvent ODE
(\alpha-\mathcal{L})f=\delta_{y}
. Only the boundary behaviour at
0
is
modified by stickiness, and that is what we verify here.

Fix
x\in(0,\infty)
. Recall that
G_{\alpha}^{\rm sticky}(x,y)
is given by the Laplace transform
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}p(t;x,y)\,dt
by
Eq.

4.2
(applied to the sticky process, with
m
now carrying the atom
\mu^{-1}\delta_{0}
). We apply the Laplace transform
to both sides of the time-domain Wentzell condition
Eq.

3.2
,

\partial_{s}p(t;x,0^{+})=\tfrac{1}{\mu}\,\partial_{t}p(t;x,0).

On the right, integration by parts in
t
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\,\partial_{t}p(t;x,0)\,dt

\displaystyle=\bigl[e^{-\alpha t}p(t;x,0)\bigr]_{0}^{\infty}+\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\,p(t;x,0)\,dt

\displaystyle=\alpha\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,0),

where the boundary term at
\infty
vanishes and the term at
0
equals

-p(0^{+};x,0)=0
(since
x>0
and the initial law has no mass at
0
). On the left, formally commuting
\partial_{s}
with the
t
-integral in the definition of
G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}
gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\,\partial_{s}p(t;x,0^{+})\,dt=\frac{d\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)}{ds}(0^{+}).

Equating the two transforms gives the second-argument form of
Eq.

4.8
:

\frac{d\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)}{ds}(0^{+})=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,0).

Symmetry of the resolvent kernel with respect to the speed measure
transfers this to the first argument
and gives
Eq.

4.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
(sticky resolvent).

The fundamental solutions of
(\alpha-\mathcal{L})f=0
satisfying the
boundary condition (
4.8
) are
f_{\infty}(x)=U(a,b,z_{x})

and

f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x)=M(a,b,z_{x})+c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x}),

(4.9)

where

c_{\mu}=\frac{-\alpha}{\mu\,|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}.

(4.10)

The sticky resolvent is

G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x\wedge y)\,f_{\infty}(x\vee y)}{|\mathcal{W}|},

with the same normalisation constant
|\mathcal{W}|
as the reflecting
resolvent.

The solution
f_{\infty}=U(a,b,z_{x})
decays at
\infty
and is unchanged from the reflecting case. The general solution of

(\alpha-\mathcal{L})f=0
on
(0,\infty)
regular at
0
is a linear
combination of
M
and
U
, so we write
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}
as
in
Eq.

4.9
. From
Lemma

4.1
,

(dM/ds)(0^{+})=0
and
(dU/ds)(0^{+})=-|\mathcal{W}|
, so substituting
into
Eq.

4.8
gives

-c_{\mu}\,|\mathcal{W}|=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}(1+c_{\mu}U_{0}),

and solving yields
Eq.

4.10
. The normalisation constant is unchanged
since adding
c_{\mu}f_{\infty}
to
f_{0}
contributes

c_{\mu}(f_{\infty}f_{\infty}^{\prime}-f_{\infty}f_{\infty}^{\prime})=0
to the Wronskian.
The resolvent equation
(\alpha-\mathcal{L})G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y}

then holds on
(0,\infty)
as in the reflecting case
Eq.

4.6
, and
Eq.

4.8

holds by the choice of
c_{\mu}
.
∎We recast the transition distribution in a form that can be exploited in the sampling algorithms.

Theorem 4.4
(transition distribution).

For
x>0
, the transition distribution
P_{x}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in\cdot)

admits the resolvent representation

\mathbb{P}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in A\mid u_{0}=x)=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,0)\,\mathbf{1}_{0\in A}+\alpha\int_{A\cap(0,\infty)}G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,

(4.11)

equivalent to the three-component mixture

\mathbb{P}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in A\mid u_{0}=x)=w_{0}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{0\in A}+w_{<}(x)\,\nu_{<}(A)+w_{>}(x)\,\nu_{>}(A),

(4.12)

where
\nu_{<}
and
\nu_{>}
are the probability measures with Lebesgue
densities

\frac{d\nu_{<}(y)}{dy}\propto f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,x)}(y),\qquad\frac{d\nu_{>}(y)}{dy}\propto U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,\mathbf{1}_{(x,\infty)}(y),

and the weights are

\displaystyle w_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=-c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x}),

(4.13)

\displaystyle w_{<}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,U(a,b,z_{x})}{|\mathcal{W}|}\int_{0}^{x}f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,

(4.14)

\displaystyle w_{>}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x)}{|\mathcal{W}|}\int_{x}^{\infty}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,

(4.15)

satisfying
w_{0}(x)+w_{<}(x)+w_{>}(x)=1
.

The representation
Eq.

4.11
follows from
Eq.

4.3

and the decomposition of the speed measure,

m(dy)=\mu^{-1}\delta_{0}(dy)+m^{\prime}(y)\,dy
.

For
Eq.

4.12
, evaluate
Eq.

4.11
on the atom first.
By
Lemma

4.3
with
y=0
,

G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,0)=f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(0)\,U(a,b,z_{x})/|\mathcal{W}|
.
Evaluating
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(0)=M(a,b,0)+c_{\mu}U(a,b,0)=1+c_{\mu}U_{0}

and substituting
c_{\mu}=-\alpha/(\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0})

from
Eq.

4.10
gives

f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(0)=1-\frac{\alpha U_{0}}{\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}=\frac{\mu|\mathcal{W}|}{\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}.

(4.16)

The atom weight
w_{0}(x)=(\alpha/\mu)\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,0)
then
becomes

w_{0}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\,\frac{\mu|\mathcal{W}|}{\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}\,\frac{U(a,b,z_{x})}{|\mathcal{W}|}=-c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x})>0,

where the inequality holds since
c_{\mu}<0
and
U(a,b,z_{x})>0
.

To verify
w_{0}+w_{<}+w_{>}=1
, recall that

G_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{M(a,b,z_{x\wedge y})\,U(a,b,z_{x\vee y})}{|\mathcal{W}|}

is the reflecting resolvent (the case
c_{\mu}=0
), and use the decomposition

G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)=G_{\alpha}(x,y)+\frac{c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x})\,U(a,b,z_{y})}{|\mathcal{W}|},

which follows from
f^{\rm sticky}_{0}=M+c_{\mu}U
. Since

w_{<}+w_{>}=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy
,
the decomposition gives

w_{<}+w_{>}=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\alpha}(x,y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy+\frac{\alpha c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x})}{|\mathcal{W}|}\int_{0}^{\infty}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

The first term equals
1
since
\alpha G_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)\,m^{\prime}(\cdot)

is a probability density on
(0,\infty)
(the boundary mass vanishes for
the reflecting process; see
Eq.

4.3
).
The second integral equals
|\mathcal{W}|/\alpha
by the same argument at

x=0
, where
G_{\alpha}(0,y)=U(a,b,z_{y})/|\mathcal{W}|
; that is,

\int_{0}^{\infty}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy=|\mathcal{W}|/\alpha,

(4.17)

so the second term equals
c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{x})=-w_{0}
.
Hence,
w_{0}+w_{<}+w_{>}=1
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
(leave probability and exit distribution).

Let
T_{\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
be independent of the sticky process
u
, with
u_{0}=0
.
The probability that
u_{T_{\alpha}}\neq 0
is

p_{\mathrm{leave}}=\frac{\mu}{\mu+\Phi},\qquad\Phi=\frac{1}{G_{\alpha}(0,0)},

where
G_{\alpha}
is the reflecting resolvent of
Section

4.1
.
Conditional on
u_{T_{\alpha}}\neq 0
, the exit position is drawn from
the density

\nu_{\alpha}(dy)=Z_{\nu}^{-1}\,U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,\qquad Z_{\nu}=|\mathcal{W}|/\alpha.

(4.18)

By the time-change construction
u_{t}=\tilde{u}_{T_{t}}
, the sticky process
remains at
0
until the accumulated local time
\ell^{0}_{T_{\alpha}}(\tilde{u})

exceeds an independent budget
\theta\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\mu)
.
To determine the distribution of
\ell^{0}_{T_{\alpha}}(\tilde{u})
, let

\tau_{\ell}=\inf\{t:\ell^{0}_{t}(\tilde{u})>\ell\}
be the inverse local time
of the reflecting process
\tilde{u}
at
0
; this is a subordinator with
Laplace exponent
\kappa(\alpha)
, meaning

\mathbb{E}[e^{-\alpha\tau_{\ell}}]=e^{-\ell\,\kappa(\alpha)}
.
By the standard theory of one-dimensional diffusions

(
11
, Section 6.2)
, the Laplace exponent equals

\kappa(\alpha)=1/G_{\alpha}(0,0)\eqqcolon\Phi
, where
G_{\alpha}(0,0)=U_{0}/|\mathcal{W}|

is computed in
Lemma

4.1
.
Since
T_{\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
is independent of
\tilde{u}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\ell^{0}_{T_{\alpha}}(\tilde{u})>\ell\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\ell}<T_{\alpha}\right)=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\alpha\tau_{\ell}}]=e^{-\ell\,\Phi},

so
\ell^{0}_{T_{\alpha}}(\tilde{u})\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\Phi)
, independently
of
\theta
. The leave probability then follows from

\mathbb{P}\left(E_{1}>E_{2}\right)=\mu/(\mu+\Phi)
for independent
exponentials
E_{1}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\Phi)
,
E_{2}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\mu)
.

Setting
x=0
in
Theorem

4.4
, the exit density is

\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\propto G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(0,y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy\propto U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy
, since
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(0)
is a constant
independent of
y
. The normalising constant

Z_{\nu}=|\mathcal{W}|/\alpha

follows from
Eq.

4.17
.
∎

Remark 4.6
(sampling the exit density).

To draw
y\sim\nu_{\alpha}
, we use rejection sampling. First, substitute
w=z_{y}=\lambda\beta y^{2}/2
so the speed measure density becomes
m^{\prime}(y)\,dy\propto w^{b-1}e^{-w}\,dw
(using
y^{\delta-2}\propto w^{b-1}
with
b=\delta/2
), which is proportional to the
\mathrm{Gamma}(b,1)
density. Therefore, we propose
y=\sqrt{2w/(\lambda\beta)}
for
w\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(b,1)
. The remaining factor
U(a,b,w)
in
Eq.

4.18
is bounded by
U_{0}=U(a,b,0)
since
U(a,b,\cdot)
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Hence, we accept with probability
U(a,b,w)/U_{0}
.

4.3
Sticky CIR sampler (no potential)

We construct an exact sampler for the invariant measure
\pi_{0}
of the sticky CIR process (with zero potential
G=0
). The chain samples the sticky CIR process exactly at independent exponential times: given
u_{k}=x
, draw
T_{\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
and set
u_{k+1}\sim\mathbb{P}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in\cdot\mid u_{0}=x)
. Since the continuous-time process is ergodic (
Theorem

3.2
), the chain returns
\pi_{0}
as its invariant distribution. The closed-form three-component mixture
Eq.

4.12
of
Theorem

4.4
reduces each draw to sampling from three explicit components: an atom at the boundary, a density on
(0,x)
, and a density on
(x,\infty)
, each sampled in turn below.

Two integrals are needed for each starting point
x
:

I_{>}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,\qquad I_{<}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

Since
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(y)=M(a,b,z_{y})+c_{\mu}\,U(a,b,z_{y})
, the second
integral splits as
I_{<}(x)=I_{<}^{M}(x)+c_{\mu}\,I_{<}^{U}(x)
, where

I_{<}^{M}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}M(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy,\qquad I_{<}^{U}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

Both
I_{<}^{U}
and
I_{>}
are precomputed on a truncated grid
[0,y_{\max}]
by the trapezium rule; the upper limit
y_{\max}
is chosen so that the tail
\int_{y_{\max}}^{\infty}U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy
is negligible, which is justified by the rapid decay of
U(a,b,z_{y})
as
y\to\infty
.
The remaining integral
I_{<}^{M}
is recovered without further quadrature using
the reflecting resolvent identity (i.e.,
Theorem

4.4
with
\mu\to\infty
and
c_{\mu}=0
):

\frac{\alpha}{|\mathcal{W}|}\bigl[M(a,b,z_{x})\,I_{>}(x)+U(a,b,z_{x})\,I_{<}^{M}(x)\bigr]=1,

(4.19)

which gives
I_{<}^{M}(x)=\bigl(|\mathcal{W}|/\alpha-M(a,b,z_{x})\,I_{>}(x)\bigr)/U(a,b,z_{x})
.

From
u_{k}=x>0
, the next state is drawn from the three-component mixture
Eq.

4.12
with weights
Eqs.

4.13
and
4.15
:

Atom at
0

(weight
w_{0}(x)
): set
u_{k+1}=0
.

Above
x

(weight
w_{>}(x)
): density
U(a,b,z_{y})\,m^{\prime}(y)
on
(x,\infty)
, sampled by CDF inversion against the precomputed
I_{>}
.

Below
x

(weight
w_{<}(x)
): density
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(y)\,m^{\prime}(y)
on
(0,x)
, sampled by CDF inversion against
I_{<}
(recovered from
I_{>}
and
I_{<}^{U}
via
Eq.

4.19
).

From
u_{k}=0
, the leave probability
p_{\rm leave}
and exit
distribution
\nu_{\alpha}
are given by
Corollary

4.5
: with
probability
p_{\rm leave}
the process leaves
0
and the landing
position is drawn from
\nu_{\alpha}
(using
Remark

4.6
), and with probability

1-p_{\rm leave}
it remains at
0
.

Remark 4.7
.

The functions
M(a,b,z)
and
U(a,b,z)
are the confluent hypergeometric
functions of the first and second kind, available in Python as

scipy.special.hyp1f1
and
scipy.special.hyperu

respectively. The cost per step is dominated by the offline precomputation of
I_{<}^{U}
and
I_{>}
on a grid of starting points
x
. The precomputation cost is
O(N)
evaluations
of
M
and
U
on the grid of
N
points, done once per

(\alpha,\lambda,\beta,\delta)
. The per-step cost is
O(\log N)
for the
binary-search CDF inversion, plus two evaluations of
M
and
U
at the
current state
x
. The sampler reuses the same grid for all values of

\mu
: only the scalar
c_{\mu}
and
p_{\rm leave}
change.

Extension to a potential function

We are ready to treat the full SDE (

. We assume

with

bounded below, and that

satisfies a linear growth bound:

for some

The first is well-posedness: the linear-growth assumption on
G^{\prime}
is exactly what is needed for a Girsanov change of measure against the sticky CIR process of
Theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 5.1
(well-posedness).

Under the above assumptions on
G
, there exists a unique weak solution to
Eq.

1.1
with sticky boundary at
0
.

Let
\tilde{u}_{t}
be the sticky CIR process of
Theorem

3.1
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
with driving Brownian motion

, and define the exponential local martingale

reduces Novikov’s condition to a uniform exponential second-moment bound for

on

. Taking

as a Lyapunov function gives

(cf.

), and Khasminskii’s lemma
(

is a true martingale, and Girsanov’s theorem makes

1.1

∎

The second result identifies the invariant measure: scale and speed-measure calculations from

show that the potential reweights the
G=0
invariant measure by the Gibbsian factor

, both on the interior and at the sticky atom.

Theorem 5.2
(invariant measure).

Suppose

is such that

is integrable
with respect to
\pi_{0}
. Then

has a unique invariant probability measure

\pi_{0}

\frac{d\pi}{d\pi_{0}}(u)=\frac{1}{Z}\,e^{-\beta G(u)},\qquad Z=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\beta G(u)}\,\pi_{0}(du).

A regular diffusion with finite speed measure

has unique invariant
probability measure

(

and show it has finite total mass proportional
to

.

The drift

5.2
Unadjusted Langevin sampler with mixed times

The MCMC sampler of
Section

5.1
combines a deterministic
Euler step with an exact draw from the sticky CIR resolvent, followed by
a Metropolis–Hastings correction that enforces exact invariance with
respect to
\pi
. Removing the Metropolis–Hastings correction gives an
unadjusted
Langevin algorithm
(ULA) that is cheaper per step but targets a biased
stationary distribution
\pi_{h}
.

Splitting interpretation

Decompose the generator of
Eq.

1.1
as

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}
, where

\mathcal{L}_{1}f=\frac{d}{dm_{0}}\frac{df}{ds_{0}}

is the generator of the sticky CIR process with
G\equiv 0
, and

\mathcal{L}_{2}f=-G^{\prime}(x)\,f^{\prime}(x)

is deterministic transport along
\dot{x}=-G^{\prime}(x)
on
(0,\infty)
.
Naively applying the Lie–Trotter split
e^{h\mathcal{L}}\approx e^{h\mathcal{L}_{1}}e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}
is ambiguous here because the transport flow
\Phi_{h}
of
\dot{x}=-G^{\prime}(x)
may reach
\{0\}
in finite time from states
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}\coloneqq\{x>0:\Phi_{t}(x)=0\text{ for some }t\leq h\}
, leaving
e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}
undefined there. We extend
e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}
to

[0,\infty)
by depositing such mass at the boundary:

\bigl(e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}f\bigr)(x)=\begin{cases}f(\Phi_{h}(x)),&x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}},\\
f(0),&x\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}.\end{cases}

(5.5)

This corresponds to routing states
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}

to the boundary kernel
P_{0}^{G=0}
in the MCMC proposal. The subsequent sticky CIR step

e^{h\mathcal{L}_{1}}
then evolves mass at
0
according to the sticky
boundary behaviour, so the split
e^{h\mathcal{L}}\approx e^{h\mathcal{L}_{1}}e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}
is well-defined.

We will use a first-order
mixed-time splitting
approximation with a fixed
time
h=1/\alpha
for the ODE part and an independent

\tau\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
for the CIR part:

e^{(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})h}\varphi\approx\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\left[e^{\mathcal{L}_{1}\tau}\,e^{\mathcal{L}_{2}h}\varphi\right]=\alpha(\alpha-\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}\,e^{\mathcal{L}_{2}h}\varphi.

The identity
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[e^{\mathcal{L}_{1}\tau}]=\alpha(\alpha-\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}

uses the fact that the Laplace transform of the semigroup at

\tau\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
is the resolvent. Both the fixed-time
and the exponential-time approximations agree to first order in
h
:
expanding in
h=1/\alpha
gives

\alpha(\alpha-\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}e^{\mathcal{L}_{2}h}\varphi=\varphi+h(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})\varphi+O(h^{2}),

matching
e^{(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})h}\varphi=\varphi+h(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})\varphi+O(h^{2})
. Thus, to first order, we may fix
the time for the ODE step and randomise the time for the CIR step, or vice versa,
as long as the times agree in expectation.

The ULA proposal coincides with the Metropolis–Hastings proposal of

\phi_{h}
to obtain the starting point, then draw from the sticky CIR resolvent. Removing the acceptance step gives the ULA transition: starting from

, set

u_{k+1}\sim P_{\phi_{h}(x)}^{G=0}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in\cdot)

(

\phi_{h}(x)>0
and Case 2 if
\phi_{h}(x)=0
). By

4.11

, the transition kernel is

(5.6)

where

Conclusion

This paper develops both the theory and the numerics of the one-dimensional sticky CIR process for
\delta\in(1,2)
, the regime in which the boundary is accessible but not absorbing. The invariant measure has the atom-plus-density structure required of sparse priors, and the explicit Green’s function of the reflecting CIR resolvent makes exact sampling of the
G\equiv 0
invariant measure feasible. The Gibbsian reweighting that incorporates a potential
G
(
Theorem

5.2
) is targeted exactly by the Metropolis–Hastings sampler (
), and approximately by an unadjusted Langevin scheme (
) whose bias is
O(h)
(
Theorem

5.10
).

Several directions remain for future work. First, the present analysis is
one-dimensional; extending the sticky CIR sampler to the full
Hadamard–Langevin dynamics for the posterior
\pi(d{\mathbf{u}},d{\mathbf{v}})\propto\exp(-\beta G({\mathbf{u}}\odot{\mathbf{v}}))\,\pi_{0}(d{\mathbf{u}},d{\mathbf{v}})
requires treating the

d
-dimensional product structure and the coupling between
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}

components, as outlined in
Section

1.1
. Second, we would like to sharpen the convergence analysis to the invariant measure — ideally, discharging the simplifying hypotheses
5.6
,
5.7
and
5.8
of
Theorem

5.10
, and showing a geometric rate of convergence uniform in
h
as holds for the CIR analysis in
(
5
)
. The Foster–Lyapunov
drift condition
K_{h}V\leq(1-\gamma)V+b
of
Lemma

A.1
holds
uniformly in
h
and is the main ingredient needed for uniform-in-
h

geometric ergodicity in a weighted total-variation norm via the
Hairer–Mattingly framework
(
10
)
. The missing
piece is a minorisation of
K_{h}^{n(h)}
for
n(h)=O(1/h)
steps, which remains open.

K_{h}V(x)\leq(1-\gamma)\,V(x)+b,\qquad x\geq 0,

with
\gamma=\lambda/(\alpha+2\lambda)>0
and

b=\bigl[\alpha h(2c_{1}+2C^{2})+2\delta/\beta\bigr]/(\alpha+2\lambda)<\infty
,
both independent of
h\leq h_{0}
and of the initial condition
u_{0}
.
In particular,
5.3
holds with
M=b/\gamma
.

For the sticky CIR process with
G=0
starting at
y\geq 0
, the function

t\mapsto e^{2\lambda t}E_{y}[u_{t}^{2}]-\delta/(\lambda\beta)
is a martingale, giving

E_{y}[u_{t}^{2}]=e^{-2\lambda t}y^{2}+\frac{\delta}{\lambda\beta}(1-e^{-2\lambda t}).

Averaging over
T_{\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)
, and using

E[e^{-2\lambda T_{\alpha}}]=\alpha/(\alpha+2\lambda)
,

E_{y}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2\lambda}\,y^{2}+\frac{2\delta}{\beta(\alpha+2\lambda)}.

(A.2)

For
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}
(i.e.,
x_{1}\coloneqq x-G^{\prime}(x)h>0
), the ULA uses
y=x_{1}
as starting point.
The assumption
Eq.

A.1
and
|G^{\prime}(x)|\leq C(1+x)
give

x_{1}^{2}=x^{2}-2hxG^{\prime}(x)+h^{2}G^{\prime}(x)^{2}\leq x^{2}(1+2c_{0}h+2C^{2}h^{2})+h(2c_{1}+2C^{2}h),

where we used
G^{\prime}(x)^{2}\leq 2C^{2}(1+x^{2})
. Hence, with

\kappa=c_{0}+C^{2}
and
h\leq 1
,

x_{1}^{2}\leq(1+2\kappa h)\,x^{2}+h(2c_{1}+2C^{2}).

Substituting into
Eq.

A.2
with
y=x_{1}
:

K_{h}V(x)\leq\frac{\alpha(1+2\kappa h)}{\alpha+2\lambda}\,x^{2}+b,\quad b=\frac{\alpha h(2c_{1}+2C^{2})+2\delta/\beta}{\alpha+2\lambda}.

For
h\leq h_{0}=\lambda/(2\kappa\alpha)
we have
2\kappa\alpha h\leq\lambda
, so

\frac{\alpha(1+2\kappa h)}{\alpha+2\lambda}\leq\frac{\alpha+2\kappa\alpha h}{\alpha+2\lambda}\leq 1-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+2\lambda}\eqqcolon 1-\gamma.

For
x=0
or
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}
, the ULA routes to the boundary kernel and draws directly from the resolvent at
0
, so

K_{h}V(x)=2\delta/[\beta(\alpha+2\lambda)]\leq b
.

Verification of
5.3
.

Iterating the drift condition
K_{h}V\leq(1-\gamma)V+b
gives

\mathbb{E}[u_{n}^{2}]=K_{h}^{n}V(u_{0})\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}u_{0}^{2}+\frac{b}{\gamma},

and hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\mathbb{E}[u_{n}^{2}]\leq b/\gamma

since
(1-\gamma)^{n}\to 0
. The bound
b/\gamma
depends only on

\lambda,\beta,\delta,\mu,c_{0},c_{1}
and is independent of both
h\leq h_{0}

and
u_{0}
.
∎

A.2
Regularity

We address regularity of solutions to the Poisson equation
\mathcal{L}g=\psi
and the resolvent equation
(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})v=f
.

Proposition A.2
(Poisson equation regularity).

Suppose that
G^{\prime}\in C_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
, and let
\mathcal{L}
denote the generator of the sticky CIR process with potential
G
. For any
\psi\in C_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
with mean zero with respect to the absolutely continuous part of
\pi
,

\pi_{\mathrm{ac}}(\psi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x)\,m^{\prime}(x)\,dx=0,

(A.3)

the Poisson equation
\mathcal{L}g=\psi
has a solution
g\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
, unique up to an additive constant. Normalising
g(0)=0
, we have

g^{\prime}(0)=0,\qquad g^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{\beta\,\psi(0)}{\delta},

(A.4)

together with the derivative bounds

\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\psi\|_{\infty}.

Note that
g
belongs to the domain of the generator
\mathcal{L}
if and only if
\psi(0)=0
.

The generator acts as

\mathcal{L}g=\frac{1}{\beta}g^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{\delta-1}{\beta x}-\lambda x-G^{\prime}(x)\right)g^{\prime}.

(A.5)

Notice that
\mathcal{L}
kills constants, so solutions to
\mathcal{L}g=\psi
are defined up to an additive constant.
With the speed-measure density
m^{\prime}(x)=\beta x^{\delta-1}e^{-\beta G(x)-\lambda\beta x^{2}/2}
of the generator
\mathcal{L}
(as in
Theorem

5.2
), the equation
\mathcal{L}g=\psi
takes the integrating-factor form

\frac{d}{dx}[m^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(x)]=\beta\,m^{\prime}(x)\,\psi(x).

Since
m^{\prime}(x)\asymp x^{\delta-1}
as
x\to 0^{+}
and
\delta>1
, the two
linearly independent solutions of the homogeneous equation

\frac{d}{dx}[m^{\prime}g^{\prime}]=0
near
x=0
are
g\equiv\text{const}

(regular, with
m^{\prime}g^{\prime}\to 0
) and
g^{\prime}\sim x^{1-\delta}
(singular,
with
m^{\prime}g^{\prime}\to\text{const}\neq 0
). The requirement
\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\infty
therefore forces

m^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0^{+}
and gives the unique solution

g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\beta}{m^{\prime}(x)}\int_{0}^{x}m^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(t)\,dt.

(A.6)

Since
m^{\prime}(t)\asymp t^{\delta-1}
and
m^{\prime}(x)^{-1}\asymp x^{1-\delta}
as
t,x\to 0^{+}
, expanding
Eq.

A.6
gives

g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\beta\,\psi(0)}{\delta}\,x+O(x^{2})\|\psi\|_{\infty},

which identifies the boundary values
g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}(0)=\beta\,\psi(0)/\delta
in
Eq.

A.4
.

For the behaviour at infinity of
g^{\prime}
, write
I(s)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{s}m^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(t)\,dt
. Since
\rho(x)=m^{\prime}(x)/Z
, the AC-mean-zero condition
Eq.

A.3
reads

\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(t)\,dt=Z\,\pi_{\mathrm{ac}}(\psi)=0,

so
I(\infty)=0
. Hence,
|I(s)|=|\int_{s}^{\infty}m^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(t)\,dt|\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\int_{s}^{\infty}m^{\prime}\,dt
and so
|I(s)|=O(\|\psi\|_{\infty}\,s^{\delta-2}e^{-\lambda\beta s^{2}/2})
for large
s
. Combined with
1/m^{\prime}(s)\sim s^{1-\delta}e^{\lambda\beta s^{2}/2}e^{\beta G(s)}
, we get
|g^{\prime}(s)|=O(\|\psi\|_{\infty}/s)
at infinity, so
\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\psi\|_{\infty}
. The bound on
g^{\prime\prime}
follows from the ODE (
A.5
) together with
g^{\prime}(0)=0
and the global bound on
g^{\prime}
, giving
\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\psi\|_{\infty}
. We do
not
claim
\|g\|_{\infty}<\infty
: integrating
g^{\prime}(s)=O(1/s)
at infinity,
g
may grow logarithmically.

Taking
x\to 0^{+}
in the differential operator (
A.5
) and using
g^{\prime}(0)=0
together with
g^{\prime}(x)/x\to g^{\prime\prime}(0)
gives the
consistency identity

\mathcal{L}g(0^{+})=\tfrac{1}{\beta}g^{\prime\prime}(0)+\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta}g^{\prime\prime}(0)=\tfrac{\delta}{\beta}\,g^{\prime\prime}(0)=\psi(0).

On the other hand, the Wentzell boundary condition characterising
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L})
(
Eq.

3.2
, written on the generator side) gives a
second
value for
\mathcal{L}g(0^{+})
: for
\delta>1
the scale derivative
\partial_{s}g(0^{+})=g^{\prime}(0^{+})/s^{\prime}(0^{+})
vanishes (since
s^{\prime}(0^{+})=\infty
and
g^{\prime}(0^{+})=0
), and the dual Wentzell relation then forces

\mathcal{L}g(0^{+})=\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\partial_{s}g(0^{+})=0.

Equating the two expressions for
\mathcal{L}g(0^{+})
,

g\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L})\Longleftrightarrow\psi(0)=0\Longleftrightarrow g^{\prime\prime}(0)=0.\qed

Lemma A.3
(resolvent regularity).

For every
h>0
and
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
,

v=(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}f

satisfies
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
, and
v\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
with
v^{\prime}(0^{+})=0
.

The
L^{\infty}
contraction
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
is standard since
(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}
corresponds to averaging
f
against the resolvent kernel of the sticky CIR (a probability kernel for each
x
). Smoothness of
v
on
(0,\infty)
follows from the smoothness of the kernel away from the diagonal.

For
v^{\prime}(0^{+})=0
, write the resolvent equation as
\mathcal{L}_{1}v=\alpha(v-f)
with
\mathcal{L}_{1}v=\tfrac{1}{\beta}v^{\prime\prime}+(\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta x}-\lambda x)v^{\prime}
. For
\mathcal{L}_{1}v
to remain bounded as
x\to 0^{+}
(as required for the right-hand side
\alpha(v-f)
to be bounded), the singular term
\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta x}v^{\prime}(x)
must remain bounded; since
\delta>1
, this forces
v^{\prime}(0^{+})=0
.
∎

A.3
Error analysis

The error analysis of ULA is divided into three parts: the Euler local truncation error (
Proposition

A.6
), the routing correction (
Proposition

A.7
), and the splitting error (
Proposition

A.10
).
Recall that the routing region

\mathcal{R}_{h}=\{x>0\colon x\leq G^{\prime}(x)h\}
is
the set of interior points for which the Euler step
x_{1}=x-G^{\prime}(x)h

exits the domain. The following estimate on the size of
\mathcal{R}_{h}
is used in the proof of the propositions.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\delta\in(1,2)
,
G^{\prime}\in C_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
, and let
\pi_{h}
be a stationary distribution of the ULA kernel
K_{h}
(
Theorem

5.4
). Then there exists
h_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{h\leq h_{0}}\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h})=O(h^{\delta}).

From
\pi_{h}K_{h}=\pi_{h}
and the definition of
K_{h}
:

\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h})={\pi_{h}}(K_{h}(\,\cdot\,,\mathcal{R}_{h}))\leq\sup_{x_{1}\geq 0}P_{x_{1}}^{G=0}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{R}_{h}).

Since
\mathcal{R}_{h}\subseteq(0,\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}h]
, it suffices to bound
\sup_{x_{1}}P_{x_{1}}^{G=0}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in(0,\epsilon])
with
\epsilon=\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}h
. From the resolvent formula
Eq.

4.11
, for any
x_{1}\geq 0
:

P_{x_{1}}^{G=0}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in(0,\epsilon])=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\alpha\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x_{1},y)\,m^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

By the sticky resolvent formula (
Lemma

4.3
),

G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x_{1},y)=\frac{f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x_{1}\wedge y)\,U(a,b,z_{x_{1}\vee y})}{|\mathcal{W}|}.

We bound the two factors
U(a,b,z_{x_{1}\vee y})
and
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x_{1}\wedge y)
separately, uniformly in
h\leq h_{0}
and
x_{1}\geq 0
, on the integration range
y\in(0,\epsilon]
. Since
U(a,b,\cdot)
is positive and decreasing on
[0,\infty)
(
Remark

4.6
),

U(a,b,z_{x_{1}\vee y})\leq U(a,b,0)=U_{0}
.
The argument
x_{1}\wedge y
lies in the boundary layer
[0,\epsilon]
, since
x_{1}\wedge y\leq y\leq\epsilon
, so we expand
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}
around the boundary. At the boundary,
Eq.

4.16
gives

f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(0)=p_{\rm leave}=\frac{\mu|\mathcal{W}|}{\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}.

For
x\in[0,\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}h]
and parameter values
\alpha=1/h
,
a=\alpha/(2\lambda)
,
b=\delta/2
,
z_{x}=\lambda\beta x^{2}/2
, we have
a\,z_{x}\leq\beta\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\,h/4=O(h)
, which is the natural expansion parameter for
M(a,b,z)=1+(a/b)z+\cdots
(large
a
balanced by small
z_{x}
). Choose
h_{0}\leq 4/(\beta\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2})
so that
a\,z_{x}\leq 1
on the integration range, ensuring the Taylor remainder is uniform. The bound
|c_{\mu}|U_{0}\leq 1
on the second coefficient of
f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x)=M(a,b,z_{x})+c_{\mu}U(a,b,z_{x})
keeps the
U
-contribution under control. Taylor expansion at
z=0
then yields

f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x_{1}\wedge y)=p_{\rm leave}+O(h)
,
uniformly for
h\leq h_{0}
. Since
p_{\rm leave}=\mu|\mathcal{W}|h/(\mu|\mathcal{W}|h+U_{0})\asymp h^{\delta/2}
as
h\to 0
, the remainder is absorbed into the leading term:

f_{0}^{\rm sticky}(x_{1}\wedge y)\leq C\,p_{\rm leave}

for some constant
C
independent of
h
and
x_{1}
. Substituting the two factor bounds and then the explicit form of
p_{\rm leave}
,

\alpha\,G^{\rm sticky}_{\alpha}(x_{1},y)\leq\frac{C\,\alpha\,p_{\rm leave}\,U_{0}}{|\mathcal{W}|}=\frac{C\,\alpha\,\mu\,U_{0}}{\mu|\mathcal{W}|+\alpha U_{0}}\leq C\mu,

where the last step bounds the denominator from below by
\alpha U_{0}
.
Using
m^{\prime}(y)=\beta y^{\delta-1}e^{-\lambda\beta y^{2}/2}\leq\beta y^{\delta-1}
,

P_{x_{1}}^{G=0}(u_{T_{\alpha}}\in(0,\epsilon])\leq C\mu\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\beta y^{\delta-1}\,dy=\frac{C\mu\beta\,\epsilon^{\delta}}{\delta}=O(h^{\delta}),

uniformly in
x_{1}\geq 0
, giving
\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h})=O(h^{\delta})
.
∎

Lemma A.5
(resolvent gradient bound).

Let
R_{h}\coloneqq(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}
denote the
G=0
sticky CIR resolvent. For every
h>0
and every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
,

\|(R_{h}\varphi)^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{1+\lambda h}\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

(A.7)

Set
\tilde{\varphi}\coloneqq R_{h}\varphi
, so
\tilde{\varphi}-h\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi
on
(0,\infty)
with
\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{\varphi}=\tfrac{1}{\beta}\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}+(\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta x}-\lambda x)\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime}
. Differentiating in
x
, the commutator
[\partial_{x},\mathcal{L}_{1}]\tilde{\varphi}=(\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}_{1}\tilde{\varphi})-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\partial_{x}\tilde{\varphi})=-V(x)\,\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)
with

V(x)\coloneqq\frac{\delta-1}{\beta x^{2}}+\lambda\geq\lambda\quad\text{on }(0,\infty),

using
\delta>1
. Hence
\psi\coloneqq\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime}
satisfies

(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1}+hV)\psi=\varphi^{\prime}\qquad\text{on }(0,\infty).

(A.8)

The boundary regularity
\psi(0)=\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)=0
from
Lemma

A.3
, together with the decay of the resolvent kernel at infinity, forces the supremum of
|\psi|
to be attained at some interior point
x^{*}\in(0,\infty)
(unless
\psi\equiv 0
). Suppose
M\coloneqq\psi(x^{*})>0
is the supremum (the case
\inf\psi<0
is symmetric). Then
\psi^{\prime}(x^{*})=0
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(x^{*})\leq 0
, so
\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi(x^{*})=\tfrac{1}{\beta}\psi^{\prime\prime}(x^{*})\leq 0
. Evaluating
Eq.

A.8
at
x^{*}
,

M(1+hV(x^{*}))\leq M(1+hV(x^{*}))-h\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi(x^{*})=\varphi^{\prime}(x^{*})\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

giving
M\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}/(1+hV(x^{*}))\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}/(1+h\lambda)
. Applying the same argument to
-\psi
yields
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}/(1+h\lambda)
, which is
Eq.

A.7
.
∎

Proposition A.6
(Euler local truncation error).

Let
\delta\in(1,2)
and
G^{\prime}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
. For any
h_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for all
h\in(0,h_{0}]
and
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
with
\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\infty
,

\pi_{h}\bigl(h^{-1}|K_{h}\varphi-S_{h}\varphi|\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}}\bigr)\leq C\,h\,\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Throughout, set
x_{1}=x-G^{\prime}(x)h
. Under
G^{\prime}\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))
, Taylor expansion gives the Euler error bound

\Phi_{h}(x)-x_{1}=\tfrac{h^{2}}{2}G^{\prime}(x)G^{\prime\prime}(x)+O(h^{3}),\qquad|\Phi_{h}(x)-x_{1}|=O(h^{2}),

uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}
. Both
K_{h}\varphi(x)
and
S_{h}\varphi(x)
are values of the
G=0
resolvent
R_{h}\varphi
applied at different starting points; concretely
K_{h}\varphi(x)=(R_{h}\varphi)(x_{1})
and
S_{h}\varphi(x)=(R_{h}\varphi)(\Phi_{h}(x))
when the extension
Eq.

5.5
is not invoked, and
S_{h}\varphi(x)=(R_{h}\varphi)(0)
when it is.

Split
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}
into the
bulk

\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}})^{c}
and the
shell

\mathcal{R}_{h}^{c}\cap\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
.

Bulk.
Here
x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}
ensures
x_{1}>0
, and
x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
ensures the natural flow
\Phi_{t}(x)
stays strictly positive on
[0,h]
, so the extension in
Eq.

5.5
is not invoked. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

|K_{h}\varphi(x)-S_{h}\varphi(x)|=\Bigl|\int_{\Phi_{h}(x)}^{x_{1}}(R_{h}\varphi)^{\prime}(s)\,ds\Bigr|\leq|x_{1}-\Phi_{h}(x)|\,\|(R_{h}\varphi)^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

By
Lemma

A.5
,
\|(R_{h}\varphi)^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, so

|K_{h}\varphi(x)-S_{h}\varphi(x)|\leq|x_{1}-\Phi_{h}(x)|\,\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}=O(h^{2})\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Integrating against
\pi_{h}
gives
\pi_{h}(h^{-1}|K_{h}\varphi-S_{h}\varphi|\,\mathbf{1}_{\rm bulk})=O(h)\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Shell.
Here
x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}
still gives
x_{1}>0
, but
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
means the extension in
Eq.

5.5
applies and
S_{h}\varphi(x)=(R_{h}\varphi)(0)
. Hence,

|K_{h}\varphi(x)-S_{h}\varphi(x)|=|(R_{h}\varphi)(x_{1})-(R_{h}\varphi)(0)|\leq x_{1}\,\|(R_{h}\varphi)^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq x_{1}\,\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Since
\Phi_{h}(x)\leq 0
on
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
while
x_{1}>0
, the Euler error bound forces
x_{1}\leq|x_{1}-\Phi_{h}(x)|=O(h^{2})
, hence
|K_{h}\varphi-S_{h}\varphi|=O(h^{2})\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
on the shell. The argument of
Lemma

A.4
applies equally to
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
, giving
\pi_{h}(\text{shell})\leq\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}})=O(h^{\delta})
, so
\pi_{h}(h^{-1}|K_{h}\varphi-S_{h}\varphi|\,\mathbf{1}_{\rm shell})=O(h^{\delta})\cdot h\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Adding the two contributions completes the proof.
∎

Proposition A.7
(routing region correction).

Let
g\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
with
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
,

f=e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}g
, and

\tilde{v}=(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}f
.
There exists
h_{0},C>0
such that, for all
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,

\pi_{h}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{h}}|(\mathcal{A}_{h}^{S}-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{S})g|\bigr)\leq C\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,h^{\delta}.

By linearity of the resolvent,

[(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}g](0)-\tilde{v}(x)=\underbrace{[(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}(g-f)](0)}_{\text{(A)}}+\underbrace{\tilde{v}(0)-\tilde{v}(x)}_{\text{(B)}}.

Both terms are bounded by a single device:
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
|g^{\prime\prime}|\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
give, by two integrations from
0
,

|g(z)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,z^{2}\qquad\forall z\geq 0,

(A.9)

and since
f=e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}g
acts as composition with the flow (or extension to
0
in the routing region),

|f(z)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,\Phi_{h}(z)^{2}\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigl(z^{2}+\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}h^{2}\bigr).

(A.10)

Bound on (A).
The resolvent at
0
has a probabilistic representation
[(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}\eta](0)=\mathbb{E}_{0}[\eta(u_{T_{\alpha}})]\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\eta(u_{T_{\alpha}})\mid u_{0}=0]
, where
u
is the
G=0
sticky CIR started at
0
and
T_{\alpha}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\alpha)
with
\alpha=1/h
. Hence,

|\text{(A)}|=\bigl|\mathbb{E}_{0}[(g-f)(u_{T_{\alpha}})]\bigr|\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[|g(u_{T_{\alpha}})|]+\mathbb{E}_{0}[|f(u_{T_{\alpha}})|].

Applying
Eqs.

A.9
and
A.10
and the boundary-kernel second-moment identity (
Lemma

A.1
, Case 2)

\mathbb{E}_{0}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]=2\delta/[\beta(\alpha+2\lambda)]=O(h),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[|g(u_{T_{\alpha}})|]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{0}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]=O(h)\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[|f(u_{T_{\alpha}})|]

\displaystyle\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{0}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]+\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}h^{2}\bigr)=O(h)\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Hence,
|\text{(A)}|\leq Ch\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Bound on (B).
Restricted to
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}\subseteq(0,\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}h]
, the same argument gives
\tilde{v}(y)=E_{y}[f(u_{T_{\alpha}})]
and, by
Eq.

A.10
,

|\tilde{v}(y)|\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigl(E_{y}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]+\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}h^{2}\bigr),\qquad y\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{R}_{h}.

The same second-moment identity gives
E_{y}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]=O(h)
for
y\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{R}_{h}
, hence
|\tilde{v}(y)|\leq Ch\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
and
|\text{(B)}|\leq|\tilde{v}(0)|+|\tilde{v}(x)|\leq Ch\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Combining.

h^{-1}|\text{(A)}+\text{(B)}|\leq C\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
uniformly on
\mathcal{R}_{h}
. Integrating against
\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h})\leq Ch^{\delta}
(
Lemma

A.4
),

\pi_{h}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{h}}|(\mathcal{A}_{h}^{S}-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{S})g|\bigr)\leq C\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,h^{\delta}.\qed

Before presenting
Proposition

A.10
, we require two technical lemmas.

Lemma A.8
(Euler-shift identity).

Let
K_{h}=R_{h}\circ\phi_{h}
denote the ULA kernel, where
R_{h}=(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}
is the
G=0
sticky CIR resolvent and
\phi_{h}
is the clamped Euler map, and let
\pi_{h}
be its stationary distribution. For any
\psi
in the domain of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
,

\pi_{h}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi)=h^{-1}\,\pi_{h}\bigl(\psi-\psi\circ\phi_{h}\bigr).

(A.11)

Let
\nu_{h}\coloneqq(\phi_{h})_{*}\pi_{h}
denote the pushforward of
\pi_{h}
by
\phi_{h}
, so that
\nu_{h}(\psi)=\pi_{h}(\psi\circ\phi_{h})
for any test functions
\psi
.
Since
K_{h}\varphi=(R_{h}\varphi)\circ\phi_{h}
, the stationarity condition
\pi_{h}(K_{h}\varphi)=\pi_{h}(\varphi)
can be rewritten as

\nu_{h}(R_{h}\varphi)=\pi_{h}(\varphi).

(A.12)

From
(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})R_{h}=I
, we have

R_{h}\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi=h^{-1}(R_{h}\psi-\psi)

(A.13)

for
\psi
in the domain of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
(using
R_{h}\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}R_{h}
).
Applying
Eq.

A.12
with
\varphi=\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi
, then substituting
Eq.

A.13
, and finally
Eq.

A.12
with
\varphi=\psi
:

\pi_{h}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi)=\nu_{h}(R_{h}\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi)=h^{-1}\bigl[\nu_{h}(R_{h}\psi)-\nu_{h}(\psi)\bigr]=h^{-1}\bigl[\pi_{h}(\psi)-\pi_{h}(\psi\circ\phi_{h})\bigr],

which is
Eq.

A.11
.
∎

Lemma A.9
(Integration by parts for
\pi_{h}
).

Let
\rho_{h}
denote the density of the absolutely continuous part of
\pi_{h}
with respect to Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)
. Assume that

(i)

\rho_{h}\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
and
\rho_{h}(x),x\rho_{h}(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
, uniformly in
h\in(0,h_{0}]
;

(ii)

the score
s_{h}\coloneqq\rho_{h}^{\prime}/\rho_{h}
satisfies
\pi_{h}(|s_{h}|)\leq C_{*}
uniformly in
h\in(0,h_{0}]
.

Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\bigl|\pi_{h}(\eta\cdot u^{\prime}\,\mathbf{1}_{(\xi,\infty)})\bigr|\leq C\,\|\eta\|_{C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}+|\eta(\xi)|\,|u(\xi)|\,\rho_{h}(\xi),

(A.14)

for
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,
\xi>0
,
\eta\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}([\xi,\infty))
, and
u\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}([\xi,\infty))
. Only
\|u\|_{\infty}
appears on the right – the derivative
u^{\prime}
has been moved onto
\eta
and the score
s_{h}
by the integration by parts.

Since
\xi>0
, the integral involves only the absolutely continuous part and

\pi_{h}(\eta\cdot u^{\prime}\,\mathbf{1}_{(\xi,\infty)})=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\eta(x)\,u^{\prime}(x)\,\rho_{h}(x)\,dx.

By
(i)
,
\rho_{h}
is
C^{1}
on
(\xi,\infty)
with
\rho_{h}(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
, and
\eta,u\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}
are absolutely continuous. Integration by parts gives

\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\eta\,u^{\prime}\,\rho_{h}\,dx=\bigl[\eta\,u\,\rho_{h}\bigr]_{\xi}^{\infty}-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}\,\rho_{h}+\eta\,\rho_{h}^{\prime}\bigr)\,u\,dx.

The boundary at
\infty
vanishes by
\|\eta u\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\rho_{h}(x)\to 0
, leaving

\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\eta\,u^{\prime}\,\rho_{h}\,dx=-\eta(\xi)\,u(\xi)\,\rho_{h}(\xi)-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}\,u\,\rho_{h}\,dx-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\eta\,u\,s_{h}\,\rho_{h}\,dx.

Taking absolute values and using
\pi_{h}((\xi,\infty))\leq 1
together with
(ii)
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\pi_{h}(\eta\,u^{\prime}\,\mathbf{1}_{(\xi,\infty)})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq|\eta(\xi)|\,|u(\xi)|\,\rho_{h}(\xi)+\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{\infty}\,\pi_{h}((\xi,\infty))+\|\eta\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{\infty}\,\pi_{h}(|s_{h}|)

\displaystyle\leq|\eta(\xi)|\,|u(\xi)|\,\rho_{h}(\xi)+\bigl(1+C_{*}\bigr)\|\eta\|_{C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}}\,\|u\|_{\infty},

which is
Eq.

A.14
with
C=1+C_{*}
.
∎

Proposition A.10
(splitting error bound).

Let
g\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
with
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
and the boundary regularity

g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=g^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,

(A.15)

f=e^{h\mathcal{L}_{2}}g
, and
\tilde{v}=(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})^{-1}f
. (
g
itself need not be bounded – the Poisson solution from
Proposition

A.2
may grow logarithmically at infinity.) Assume the
\pi_{0}
-relative density
w_{h}\coloneqq d\pi_{h}/d\pi_{0}
satisfies

(i)

w_{h}\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
with
\sup_{h\leq h_{0}}\|w_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq M<\infty
;

(ii)

\sup_{h\leq h_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|w_{h}^{\prime}(x)|\,\rho_{0}(x)\,dx\leq M_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
.

Then, there exists
h_{0},C>0
such that

\left|\pi_{h}\left(\frac{\tilde{v}-g}{h}-\mathcal{L}g\right)\right|\leq C\,h\,\bigl(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\bigr),\qquad\forall h\in(0,h_{0}].

(A.16)

Verification of the hypotheses of
Lemma

A.9
.
From (i),
\rho_{h}=w_{h}\rho_{0}\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
. Since
\|w_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq M
and
\rho_{0}
has super-exponential tails,
\rho_{h}(x),x\rho_{h}(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
uniformly in
h
. For the score,
s_{h}=\rho_{h}^{\prime}/\rho_{h}=w_{h}^{\prime}/w_{h}+s_{0}
with
s_{0}=\rho_{0}^{\prime}/\rho_{0}=(\delta-1)/x-\lambda\beta x
, so

\pi_{h}(|s_{h}|)\leq\int|w_{h}^{\prime}(x)|\,\rho_{0}(x)\,dx+\|w_{h}\|_{\infty}\,\pi_{0}(|s_{0}|)\leq M_{\mathrm{TV}}+M\cdot\pi_{0}(|s_{0}|),

where
\pi_{0}(|s_{0}|)<\infty
for
\delta>1
(the
1/x
singularity is integrable against
\rho_{0}(x)\sim x^{\delta-1}
). Hence,
Lemma

A.9
applies with
C_{*}=M_{\mathrm{TV}}+M\pi_{0}(|s_{0}|)
. We also note for later use that
\rho_{h}(x)=w_{h}(x)\rho_{0}(x)\leq M\cdot Cx^{\delta-1}
for
x\in(0,1]
, uniformly in
h
.

Let
w=\tilde{v}-g
and
R=(f-g-h\mathcal{L}_{2}g)/h
. From the resolvent equation
(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})\tilde{v}=f
with
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}
, we see

(I-h\mathcal{L}_{1})w=f-g+h\mathcal{L}_{1}g=h\mathcal{L}g+hR.
This can be rearranged to

\frac{\tilde{v}-g}{h}-\mathcal{L}g=\mathcal{L}_{1}w+{R}.

(A.17)

It suffices to bound each summand on the right by
C\,h\,(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})
.

Bound on
R
.
We show
\|R\|_{\infty}\leq Ch\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
. For
x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
,
f(x)=g(\Phi_{h}(x))
and Taylor expansion of
g
along the flow with second-derivative remainder gives
|R(x)|\leq Ch\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
. For
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
,
f(x)=0
by
Eq.

5.5
, so
R(x)=(-g(x)-h\mathcal{L}_{2}g(x))/h
. Since
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
, two integrations of
|g^{\prime\prime}|\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
give
|g(x)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}x^{2}
and
|g^{\prime}(x)|\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}x
, hence
|\mathcal{L}_{2}g(x)|=|G^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(x)|\leq\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}x
. Combined with
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}\subseteq(0,\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}h]
,
|R(x)|\leq C\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}(x^{2}+hx)/h\leq Ch\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

Bound on
\mathcal{L}_{1}w
.
We show
\pi_{h}(\mathcal{L}_{1}w)\leq C\,h\,(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})
. Before invoking
Lemma

A.8
, we verify
w\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
. The resolvent maps
\tilde{v}=R_{h}f
into
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
by construction, so it suffices that
g\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
. By the same argument as in
Proposition

A.2
,
g\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
is equivalent to
g^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, which is granted by
Eq.

A.15
; equivalently,
\mathcal{L}_{1}g(0^{+})=0
matches the sticky action at the atom. Hence
w=\tilde{v}-g\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
, and
Lemma

A.8
applied with
\psi=w
gives

\pi_{h}(\mathcal{L}_{1}w)=h^{-1}\,\pi_{h}\bigl(w-w\circ\phi_{h}\bigr).

(A.18)

We bound the right-hand side by splitting the state space into the routing region
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\mathrm{cts}}
and its complement.

For
x\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}=\mathcal{R}_{h}\cup\{0\}
,
\phi_{h}\equiv 0
. At
x=0
,
w(0)-w(\phi_{h}(0))=0
.
For
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}
,

w(x)-w(\phi_{h}(x))=w(x)-w(0)=\bigl(\tilde{v}(x)-\tilde{v}(0)\bigr)-\bigl(g(x)-g(0)\bigr).

By the same local kernel-moment argument used in the proof of
Proposition

A.7
(exploiting that
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}
is a neighborhood of
0
on which the boundary-kernel second moment
E_{y}[u_{T_{\alpha}}^{2}]=O(h)
uniformly, together with
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
),
|\tilde{v}(x)-\tilde{v}(0)|\leq Ch\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
; and by Taylor expansion,
|g(x)-g(0)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}x^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}
for
x\in\mathcal{R}_{h}
. Hence

\bigl|w(x)-w\circ\phi_{h}(x)\bigr|\leq Ch\,\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},\qquad\forall x\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}.

Combined with the routing-region mass bound
\pi_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h})\leq Ch^{\delta}
from
Lemma

A.4
,

h^{-1}\,\pi_{h}\bigl((w-w\circ\phi_{h})\mathbf{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}}\bigr)\leq Ch^{\delta}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},\qquad\forall h\in(0,h_{0}].

(A.19)

For
x\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}=\{x>0\colon x\notin\mathcal{R}_{h}\}
, we have
\phi_{h}(x)=x-hG^{\prime}(x)>0
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

w(x)-w\circ\phi_{h}(x)=hG^{\prime}(x)\int_{0}^{1}w^{\prime}(\phi_{h}^{(t)}(x))\,dt.

where
\phi_{h}^{(t)}(x)\coloneqq\phi_{h}(x)+t\,hG^{\prime}(x)
. Integrating over
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}
,

h^{-1}\,\pi_{h}\bigl((w-w\circ\phi_{h})\mathbf{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}}\bigr)=\int_{0}^{1}\pi_{h}\Bigl(G^{\prime}\cdot(w^{\prime}\circ\phi_{h}^{(t)})\,\mathbf{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}}\Bigr)\,dt,

(A.20)

We bound the integrand in
Eq.

A.20
uniformly in
t\in[0,1]
. On
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}
, we have

\phi_{h}^{(t)}(x)\geq 0,

with Jacobian
1-(1-t)hG^{\prime\prime}(x)=1+O(h)
. The image
\phi_{h}^{(t)}(\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c})\subseteq(\xi_{h},\infty)
for some
\xi_{h}\geq c\,h>0
(depending only on
\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
). Changing variables
y=\phi_{h}^{(t)}(x)
rewrites

\pi_{h}\bigl(G^{\prime}\cdot(w^{\prime}\circ\phi_{h}^{(t)})\,\mathbf{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}}\bigr)=\pi_{h}\bigl(\eta_{h}^{(t)}\cdot w^{\prime}\,\mathbf{1}_{(\xi_{h},\infty)}\bigr)

for an explicit
\eta_{h}^{(t)}\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}([\xi_{h},\infty))
with
\|\eta_{h}^{(t)}\|_{C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))}\leq C\|G\|_{C^{2}_{\mathrm{b}}([0,\infty))}
uniformly in
h,t
. Applying
Lemma

A.9
with
\xi=\xi_{h}
,
\eta=\eta_{h}^{(t)}
, and
u=w
,

\bigl|\pi_{h}(\eta_{h}^{(t)}\cdot w^{\prime}\,\mathbf{1}_{(\xi_{h},\infty)})\bigr|\leq C\,\|w\|_{\infty}+|\eta_{h}^{(t)}(\xi_{h})|\,|w(\xi_{h})|\,\rho_{h}(\xi_{h}),\qquad\forall h\in(0,h_{0}].

From
w=R_{h}(h\mathcal{L}g+hR)
and
\|R_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}}\leq 1
together with the bound on
R
, we see
\|w\|_{\infty}\leq h\|\mathcal{L}g\|_{\infty}+Ch^{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C\,h\,(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})
(using
|\mathcal{L}g(x)|\leq\tfrac{1}{\beta}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}+(\tfrac{\delta-1}{\beta x}-\lambda x-G^{\prime}(x))\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
and the boundary-layer cancellation
(\delta-1)g^{\prime}(x)/(\beta x)=O(\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})
from
g^{\prime}(0)=0
). For the boundary term,
\rho_{h}(\xi_{h})\leq M\,C\xi_{h}^{\delta-1}\leq Ch^{\delta-1}
by hypothesis (i) and the
x^{\delta-1}
scaling of
\rho_{0}
near
0
(as derived at the start of the proof), so

|\eta_{h}^{(t)}(\xi_{h})|\,|w(\xi_{h})|\,\rho_{h}(\xi_{h})\leq C\|G\|_{C^{1}_{\mathrm{b}}}\cdot Ch(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})\cdot Ch^{\delta-1}=Ch^{\delta}(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}).

Combining both contributions to the integrand and integrating over
t\in[0,1]
,

h^{-1}\,\pi_{h}\bigl((w-w\circ\phi_{h})\mathbf{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{h}^{c}}\bigr)\leq Ch\,(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})+Ch^{\delta}(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})

(A.21)

for
h\in(0,h_{0}]
. Combined with
Eq.

A.19
and
Eq.

A.18
,
|\pi_{h}(\mathcal{L}_{1}w)|\leq Ch\,(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty})
.

Substituting both bounds into
Eq.

A.17
with
\delta>1
gives
Eq.

A.16
.
