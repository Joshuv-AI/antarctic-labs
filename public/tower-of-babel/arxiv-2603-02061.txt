SINR Estimation under Limited Feedback via Online Convex Optimization
Lorenzo Maggi, Boris Bonev, Reinhard Wiesmayr, Sebastian Cammerer, Alexander Keller

Abstract
We introduce a novel online convex optimization (OCO) framework to estimate the user’s signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) from ACK/NACK feedback, channel quality indicator (CQI) reports, and previously selected modulation and coding scheme (MCS) values. Specifically, the proposed approach minimizes a regularized binary cross-entropy loss using mirror descent enhanced with Nesterov momentum for accelerated SINR tracking. Its parameters are tuned online via an expert-advice algorithm, endowing the estimator with continual learning capabilities. Numerical experiments in ray-traced scenarios show that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art schemes in estimation accuracy and adapts robustly to time-varying SINR regimes.

I
Introduction

Estimating the
signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR)
experienced by the
user equipments (UEs)
at the
base station (BS)
is crucial for radio resource management. Reliable SINR estimates enable the scheduler to balance spectral efficiency and reliability. Conversely, poor estimates can lead to overly aggressive MCS choices, causing retransmissions and increased latency, or overly conservative ones, resulting in reduced throughput.
Unfortunately, accurate
downlink (DL)

SINR
estimation at the
BS
is impaired by the nature of uplink feedback. ACK/NACK acknowledgments provide only indications of decoding success or failure, while
channel quality indicator (CQI)
reports may be unreliable due to quantization errors and feedback delay.
Compounding these difficulties is the non-stationary nature of the wireless channel.
UE
mobility, fading, and interference cause the SINR to evolve in complex patterns. Self-adaptivity is thus a desirable property for an
SINR
estimator to enable both fast adaptation to sudden changes in the
SINR
regime and stable operation during stationary periods.

Prior art
on
SINR
estimation from limited feedback is mainly related to adaptive
modulation and coding scheme (MCS)
schemes. The industry standard is
outer-loop link-adaptation (OLLA)

[
16
]
. Several variants of
OLLA
have been studied, including stepsize adaptation mechanisms
[
20
]
, reinforcement learning-assisted approaches
[
11
]
, and customized versions, e.g., for extended reality
[
15
]
.
The work
[
18
]
proposes
latent Thompson sampling (LTS)
to infer the
SINR
, while
[
7
]
uses a Kalman filter to infer the interference pattern from
CQI
.

Our contributions
are three-fold:

i)

We introduce a novel framework for SINR estimation from ACK/NACK and CQI reports, grounded in
online convex optimization (OCO)
.

ii)

We derive a mirror-descent
SINR
estimator augmented with Nesterov momentum enabling accelerated
SINR
tracking.
Intuitively, the estimate is adjusted according to the “surprise” of receiving an ACK/NACK, and fused with the CQI estimate via a convex combination.

iii)

We show how the estimator’s parameters can be automatically tuned online via an expert-advice algorithm.

This work extends
[
19
]
, relying on a simpler
SINR
estimator which is not based on
OCO
, does not exploit CQI report and has no self-optimization properties.

The optimal parameters
\alpha
,
\beta

A large stepsize

The observations above motivate the use of a method that tunes the parameters

V
Performance guarantees

5
5

5

In this technical section, we assume that the reader has some familiarity with the theory behind
OCO
. We refer to
[
8
]
for an excellent introduction.

The performance of our method in the
ACK/NACK
-only setting (Sec.
III-A
) can be evaluated in terms of the
regret

R_{T}
, being the difference between the cumulative loss of the algorithm and that of a candidate solution
\{g_{t}\}_{t}
over
T
slots:

R_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t}(g_{t}).

(13)

Different regret guarantees hold depending on the choice of the candidate solution.
If
g_{t}
is the best constant guess, i.e.,
g_{t}:=g
for all
t
where
g=\operatorname{arg\,min}_{g^{\prime}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t}(g^{\prime})
, then we recover the classic static regret.
Since the
SINR
varies over time and our goal is to track it, evaluating the regret with respect to a dynamic candidate is more appropriate.
The regret is typically bounded as a function of the candidate’s variability, quantified by the
path length

P_{T}:=\sum_{t=2}^{T}|g_{t}-g_{t-1}|
.
It is shown in
[
21
]
that
OGD
with a fixed learning rate
\eta
on the order of
1/\sqrt{T}
achieves a dynamic regret
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}(1+P_{T}))
against any possible candidate with path length
P_{T}
.

The acceleration induced by
momentum
in offline settings does not automatically translate into an improved online regret
[
2
]
, which typically requires extra structure such as lookahead predictions, as shown in
[
12
]
. Yet, momentum can improve
SINR
tracking in practice, as discussed in Sec.
III-A
.

When
CQI
is considered and composite mirror descent is used (Sec.
III-B
), the regret also includes the regularizer, i.e.,
R_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{t})+\Omega_{t}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{t})-\ell_{t}(g_{t})-\Omega_{t}(g_{t})
.
In this case, one can derive a bound
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})
on the static regret
[
6
]
.

Finally, we analyze the ability of the
expert-advice
,
FS
-based Alg.
3
to track the best parameter combination
(\alpha,\beta,\eta)
.
If the identity of the best expert changes
k
times over
T
slots, the regret of
FS
on the loss
\ell^{\prime}
with respect to an oracle selecting the best expert on each interval is
\mathcal{O}(k\log(\frac{NT}{k}))
, which is sublinear in
T
(see
[
8
]
, Ch. 10).
In contrast, an algorithm that converges to the best
fixed
parameters in hindsight incurs linear regret, since it cannot match the best expert on every sub-interval. This demonstrates the continual learning capability of Alg.
3
in non-stationary environments.

Spectral efficiency vs.
SINR
estimation accuracy
(Fig.
4
). Although
MCS
selection is outside the scope of this work, we show that using our
SINR
estimate to select the
MCS
induces a trade-off between
SINR
estimation accuracy and spectral efficiency.
We consider an
LA
scheme that selects in each slot
t
the highest
MCS
not exceeding an instantaneous
BLER
target
\tau_{t}
, i.e.,
u_{t}=\max\{u:\text{\small{BLER}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{t},u)\leq\tau_{t}\}
.
The target
\tau_{t}
is drawn uniformly in
[0,1]
with probability
p_{e}
, and is otherwise computed as
\mathrm{clip}(\tau+0.02(\sum_{i<t}\tau-y_{t}),0,1)
. Note that the feedback term
\sum_{i<t}\tau-y_{t}
steers the long-term
BLER
toward
\tau
, while exploratory
MCS
selection probes the channel.
MCS exploration improves channel estimation accuracy (see Fig.
4

SINR
abruptly increases (resp., decreases) and a high (resp., low)
MCS
results in an ACK (resp., NACK), the estimate quickly adapts to the new regime. Yet, spectral efficiency drops sharply for
p_{e}>0.15
.
This highlights the need for policies that balance exploration and exploitation, probing the channel when beneficial to maximize spectral efficiency, as in
[
19
]
.

VII
Conclusions

This study lays the groundwork for applying the online optimization framework to
SINR
-based resource management.
Specifically, we formulate
SINR
estimation using ACK/NACK feedback and
CQI
reports from the
UE
within the
OCO
framework (Sec.
II
). This new perspective leads to a low-complexity algorithm based on mirror descent with momentum (Sec.
III
), for which provable online regret guarantees exist (Sec.
V
).
Our method is effectively parameter-free, as we leverage the expert-advice framework to automatically tune the algorithm parameters (Sec.
IV
). This endows the estimator with continual learning capabilities in non-stationary
SINR
environments (Sec.
V
).
Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach consistently outperforms
SoA

LA
schemes in terms of
SINR
estimation accuracy, even with few experts (Sec.
VI
). These results suggest that our method provides a strong benchmark for run-time
SINR
estimation accuracy, and could be used to optimize existing link-adaptation schemes online.
Moreover, the estimated
SINR
can be directly used to select the
MCS
at each transmission slot.
In this setting, an exploration-versus-exploitation trade-off naturally arises between refining the
SINR
estimate and exploiting it to maximize spectral efficiency.
