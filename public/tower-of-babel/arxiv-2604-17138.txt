Observation of a cross-section enhancement near the t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} production threshold in s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV p ​ p pp collisions with the ATLAS detector
Janna Katharina Behr on behalf of the ATLAS Collaboration

Abstract
A significant excess of t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} events near the production threshold was observed in LHC Run-2 data by the ATLAS Collaboration. It is consistent with the formation of t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} quasi-bound states, which were first hypothesised almost 40 years ago. This contribution summarises the experimental results and outlines a path toward further characterisation of the excess.



The top quark stands out among the fermions of the Standard Model due to its large mass and consequently extremely short lifetime of approximately
5\times 10^{-25}
seconds. This lifetime is shorter than the average time required for the formation of hadronic bound states (
3\times 10^{-24}
s) and spin decorrelation (
3\times 10^{-21}
s).
This means that the top quark does not form hadronic bound states and its spin state can be accessed from the angular distributions of its decay products.
If a top-antitop quark pair (
t\bar{t}
) is produced with an invariant mass
m_{t\bar{t}}
close to the kinematic threshold
2m_{\mathrm{top}}
, the top and antitop quark typically have small relative velocities and can be described by non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD). If the
t\bar{t}
system is in a colour-singlet state, the NRQCD Coulomb potential is attractive and the
t\bar{t}
system can form short-lived quasi-bound states, colloquially dubbed “toponia”. This state does not decay via quark-antiquark annihilation but via the weak decay of one of the constituent quarks. It manifests itself as a narrow enhancement of the
t\bar{t}
cross-section below the production threshold, dominated by pseudo-scalar
{}^{1}S_{0}
states. These and other NRQCD modifications of the
t\bar{t}
threshold were predicted as early as 1987
[
7
]
but assumed to be impossible to observe at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). They were hence not fully included in standard Monte Carlo (MC) models of
t\bar{t}
production at the LHC.
Recently, the ATLAS
[
3
]
and CMS
[
6
]
Collaborations reported the observation of significant excesses of
t\bar{t}
events near the production threshold, consistent with models of
t\bar{t}
quasi-bound-state formation.

1
Data and simulated samples

The measurement is based on proton–proton (
pp
) collision data recorded with the ATLAS detector
[
4
]
at a centre-of-mass energy
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV,
with an integrated luminosity of 140 fb
-1
.

1.1
Baseline MC models of
t\bar{t}
production

In the baseline MC model,
t\bar{t}
production is modelled at next-to-leading order (NLO) in perturbative QCD (pQCD). No NRQCD effects, besides those entering at NLO in pQCD, are considered in this model.
Events are generated using the
hvq
model in
PowhegBox v2
, interfaced with
Pythia8.2
.
The sample is normalised to the inclusive cross-section of
834^{+37}_{-43}
pb, obtained at NNLO+NNLL precision.
The differential predictions for the kinematic variables of the top-quarks in this and related systematic variation samples are improved by correcting them to more accurate differential predictions calculated at NNLO-QCD+NLO-EW accuracy with
MATRIX
and
HATHOR
, respectively. The corrections are applied via a two-dimensional reweighting in
m_{t\bar{t}}
and
\cos\theta^{*}
, where
\theta^{*}
is the angle between the momentum of the top quark in the
t\bar{t}
centre-of-mass frame and the momentum of the reconstructed
t\bar{t}
system in the laboratory frame.

An alternative pQCD
t\bar{t}
sample based on the
bb4l
model in
PowhegBoxRes
is used to estimate the impact of off-shell top-quark decays. This model includes off-shell
and non-resonant contributions as well as exact spin correlations at NLO accuracy and simulates the
inclusive production of
b^{+}b^{-}\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\nu^{+}\nu^{-}
final states.

1.2
Extended MC model of
t\bar{t}
production

The extended MC model includes NRQCD effects in addition to the baseline MC model of pQCD
t\bar{t}
production.
NRQCD effects at the threshold are modelled following the approach in Ref.
[
9
]
.
In this framework, the formation of colour-singlet states is described using the NRQCD Green’s function in the Coulomb gauge. The ratio of this Green’s function to the one for free top quarks is used to reweight the LO matrix elements obtained with
MadGraph
. Only
{}^{1}S_{0}

t\bar{t}
production from gluon-gluon initial states is considered.
The Green’s function reweighting is applied only
to events with
m_{t\bar{t}}<350
GeV and a top-quark momentum magnitude
p^{*}
in the
t\bar{t}
rest frame
<50
GeV to minimise the overlap with the pQCD
t\bar{t}
samples.
This sample, which is referred to as
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
, is normalised to a cross-section of 6.43 pb from analytical calculations
[
8
]
.

2
Analysis strategy

The measurement is conducted in final states with two oppositely charged leptons (electrons and/or muons) and at least two jets.
Candidate events are required to contain exactly two oppositely charged leptons with transverse momentum
p_{T}>10
GeV, with at least one lepton satisfying
p_{T}>28
GeV, and at least two jets with
p_{T}>25
GeV. At least one of the jets must be
b
-tagged, i.e. identified as originating from a
b
-quark. Additionally, in events with same-flavour leptons, the dilepton invariant mass must be
>15
GeV, not within the range
81-101
GeV around the
Z
-boson mass, and the event is required to have
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}>60
GeV to reduce the background from
Z
+jets events. Only events passing these requirements are considered for the signal regions (SRs) of the measurement.
These contain small contaminations from non-
t\bar{t}
processes, with 4% of events in these regions arising from
tW
production, 1.5% from events with objects falsely identified as leptons (“Fakes”), and 0.8% from
Z
+jets production.

The four-vectors of the top and antitop quarks are reconstructed by first selecting the two
b
-jet candidates from their decays. If more than two of the jets in the event are
b
-tagged, the two highest-
p_{T}

b
-tagged jets are selected. If there is only one
b
-tagged jet, the highest-
p_{T}
jet among the remaining untagged ones is selected.
Next, the four-momenta of the two neutrinos from the leptonically decaying
W
-bosons are obtained from the selected
b
-candidate jets, charged leptons, and the total
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
in the event by applying constraints from the
W
boson and top quark masses in an analytical approach known as the Ellipse Method
[
2
]
.
The resolution of the reconstructed
m_{t\bar{t}}
is about 22% at the
t\bar{t}
threshold and improves to 18% around 500 GeV.

Only events with
m_{t\bar{t}}<500
GeV are considered for the measurement. The events passing all selection requirements are categorised into nine SRs based on two angular observables,
c_{\mathrm{hel}}
and
c_{\mathrm{han}}
, which are sensitive to
t\bar{t}
spin correlations. The variable
c_{\mathrm{hel}}
is the scalar product of the unit vectors of the momenta of the two leptons after Lorentz boosting the respective four-momenta first into the
t\bar{t}
centre-of-mass frame, and then separately into their parent (anti)top-quark rest frames
[
5
]
. The observable
c_{\mathrm{han}}
is the cosine of the same angle, where the sign of the
component of the lepton momentum along the top-quark flight direction is flipped
[
1
]
.
These observables serve to discriminate spin-singlet
t\bar{t}
states from pQCD
t\bar{t}
events.

(

(45^{+21}_{-20})\%

m_{t\bar{t}}

The systematic uncertainty in the fitted cross-section is dominated by modelling uncertainties in the pQCD
t\bar{t}
and
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
components of the extended model. For the
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
component, the dominant effects are the uncertainties in the modelling of final- and initial-state radiation, respectively, which are largest in the lowest
m_{t\bar{t}}
bins and decrease towards higher
m_{t\bar{t}}
. For pQCD
t\bar{t}
production, the largest uncertainty in terms of its impact on the measured cross-section is that related to the scale choice in the NNLO QCD reweighting. Only moderate shifts and constraints are observed for the nuisance parameters related to the dominant modelling and experimental uncertainties in the profile-likelihood fit.

An alternative set of results has been obtained using
bb4l
for the pQCD
t\bar{t}
prediction.
The alternative baseline model is rejected at a significance of more than
8\sigma
(
7\sigma
expected).
The
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
cross-section is measured to be
8.5^{+1.2}_{-1.1}
pb, consistent with the cross-section obtained using
hvq
for the baseline model.

For a more direct comparison with Ref.
[
6
]
, another set of results is obtained using a simplified model, in which NRQCD effects are approximated via a pseudoscalar resonance
\eta_{t}
.
Using this model along with the
hvq
baseline, a cross-section
\sigma(\eta_{t})=13.1^{+1.9}_{-1.7}
pb is obtained. The larger observed cross-section compared with the
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
model can be explained by the differences between the
m_{t\bar{t}}
distributions in the SRs, which are understood to be partially related to the fact that no upper bound is applied on the
m_{t\bar{t}}
at the parton level for the simplified model.

4
Outlook

Further efforts toward a more complete MC model of NRQCD effects near the
t\bar{t}
threshold are needed to reconcile the results obtained with the
\eta_{t}
model and the
t\bar{t}_{\text{GFRW}}
model. Future models should also include
P
-wave and colour-octet, along with higher-order NRQCD contributions.
Improved models of the pQCD
t\bar{t}
component, which include NNLO QCD corrections in addition to an accurate treatment of off-shell top-quark decays, are needed.
Importantly, an improved matching between NRQCD and pQCD predictions is essential to further characterise the nature of the excess in future precision measurements by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations using data from LHC Run 3.
