Prognostic significance of pretreatment pan-immune inflammation value in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients undergoing definitive chemoradiotherapy
Authors: Yibin Cai, Ning Wei, Jiarong Zhang, Ruirong Lin, Chao Teng, Qiwei Yao, Zhitao Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe pan-immune inflammation value (PIV) reflects the balance between host immune and inflammatory status and is a commonly used indicator for evaluating cancer prognosis. However, to date, no studies have confirmed the prognostic value of pretreatment PIV in patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) undergoing definitive chemoradiotherapy (dCRT).MethodsThe receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to determine the optimal cutoff value of the PIV for predicting prognosis. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve analysis was used to investigate the relationship between PIV and prognosis. A visual analysis was conducted using the SHAP method. Combined with TNM staging, a comprehensive risk stratification model was constructed through recursive partitioning analysis (RPA). Finally, the model was evaluated through decision curve analysis (DCA) and ROC curve.ResultsA total of 696 patients were included. The level of PIV before treatment was significantly correlated with a larger primary tumor burden. RCS analysis revealed a non-linear relationship between PIV and survival outcomes. Both univariate and multivariate Cox analyses confirmed that the pre-treatment PIV was an independent prognostic factor for overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Subgroup analysis showed that in all subgroups except those receiving ≥ 3 cycles of chemotherapy, high PIV was an adverse prognostic factor. Based on PIV and TNM stage, a new risk stratification model was constructed through RPA, which classified patients into three risk groups with significantly different OS and PFS. Compared with the traditional TNM stage, this RPA model showed significantly better predictive performance.ConclusionPretreatment PIV is an independent prognostic biomarker in locally advanced ESCC patients undergoing dCRT. The novel RPA model integrating PIV with TNM staging outperforms traditional staging, offering a refined “anatomical-biological” risk stratification tool to guide personalized treatment.Keywords: Pan-immune inflammation value, Prognosis, Recursive partitioning analysis, Definitive chemoradiotherapy, Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma

Introduction

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related death worldwide, and a substantial proportion of patients are already at a locally advanced stage at the time of initial diagnosis [1, 2]. For these patients, definitive chemoradiotherapy (dCRT) is the primary curative treatment option, offering the opportunity for organ preservation and improved survival [3, 4]. However, the clinical prognosis after dCRT exhibits significant heterogeneity, with a considerable subset of patients experiencing rapid disease progression or recurrence [5]. This heterogeneity highlights the urgent need for more reliable prognostic biomarkers, which can be used to accurately identify high-risk patients before treatment and guide the formulation of individualized treatment strategies.

The tumor microenvironment, particularly the immune status and systemic inflammatory response within it, has garnered increasing attention for its role in cancer initiation, progression, and treatment response [6, 7]. Systemic inflammation can influence tumor progression through multiple mechanisms, including promoting angiogenesis, suppressing anti-tumor immunity, and inducing therapy resistance [8, 9]. Composite scores derived from routine peripheral blood inflammatory markers, such as neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts-including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), have been extensively studied and shown to correlate with prognosis in various solid tumors [10–14]. Although inflammatory indices such as NLR, PLR, and SII have demonstrated prognostic value in various tumors, none of them incorporate monocyte counts. Monocytes are important precursors of tumor-associated macrophage, which can polarize into the M2 phenotype in the tumor microenvironment, promoting immune escape, angiogenesis, and treatment resistance through secretion of immunosuppressive factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β [15, 16]. For ESCC patients receiving dCRT, radiotherapy induces immunogenic cell death in tumor cells, releasing damage-associated molecular patterns that recruit and activate monocytes/macrophages [17]. The pan-immune inflammation value (PIV), as an emerging comprehensive index, integrates neutrophil, platelet, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts and is considered to provide a more holistic depiction of the host’s immune-inflammatory status [18]. Preliminary studies have demonstrated that PIV holds independent prognostic value in several cancers, such as colorectal cancer and non-small cell lung cancer [18, 19].

However, evidence regarding the association between PIV and prognosis after dCRT remains lacking in the specific patient population with locally advanced ESCC. To date, no study has systematically evaluated the predictive utility of pretreatment PIV levels for survival outcomes in locally advanced ESCC patients undergoing dCRT. Therefore, this study aims to explore the prognostic value of PIV pretreatment in patients with locally advanced ESCC who are undergoing dCRT.

Materials and methods

Patients

Patients with locally advanced ESCC who underwent dCRT at our institution between January 2014 and December 2020 were retrospectively reviewed. The inclusion criteria were: (1) histologically confirmed ESCC; (2) clinical stage IIB–IVA according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system; (3) receipt of platinum‑based concurrent or sequential dCRT; (4) age ≥ 18 years; and (5) availability of complete blood routine, comprehensive biochemical panel, and other required data within one week before treatment initiation. Exclusion criteria included: (1) prior esophageal cancer‑related surgery, radiotherapy, or systemic therapy; (2) presence of other primary malignancies; (3) active infection, autoimmune disease, or hematologic disorders; (4) severe hepatic or renal dysfunction; and (5) incomplete clinical data or loss to follow‑up. After screening, 696 patients met the criteria and were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Fujian Cancer Hospital’s Ethics Committee.

Data collection and definition

The relevant information of the patients was retrospectively collected from the electronic medical record system, including age, gender, tumor stage, tumor location, tumor length, tumor thickness, treatment plan, blood routine test before treatment, comprehensive biochemical test, etc. The calculation formula for PIV is as follows: PIV = (neutrophil count [×10⁹/L] × platelet count [×10⁹/L] × monocyte count [×10⁹/L]) / lymphocyte count [×10⁹/L].

Treatment

Radiotherapy was delivered following an individualized approach, with intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) or three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy (3D-CRT) as the primary techniques. The total prescribed radiation dose ranged from 50 to 70 Gy, administered in 25–34 fractions. Target volume delineation adhered to widely accepted clinical guidelines: the GTV encompassed the primary tumor and metastatic lymph nodes; the CTV was created by extending the GTV by 3 cm in the craniocaudal direction and 0.5 cm in all other directions; and the PTV was generated by expanding the CTV by 0.5–1 cm (with an additional 1 cm margin applied to nodal regions). Plan evaluation criteria required that at least 95% of the PTV volume receive the prescribed dose, while dose–volume constraints for all organs at risk were strictly respected. Regarding chemotherapy, all patients received platinum-based regimens, with the number of treatment cycles ranging from 0 to 8. The regimens were broadly categorized into two groups: type A consisted of platinum combined with a taxane (paclitaxel or docetaxel), and type B consisted of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) plus cisplatin. Before and after each chemotherapy cycle, systematic safety monitoring—including complete blood count, biochemical profile, and electrocardiogram—was performed. In cases of severe adverse events, subsequent cycle doses were adjusted accordingly.

Study endpoints and follow-up

The study evaluated two primary endpoints: overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). OS was measured from treatment initiation until death due to any cause or the final follow-up date. PFS was calculated from the start of treatment until the first occurrence of radiographic or clinical disease progression, death from any cause, or the date of last known follow-up. A standardized follow-up schedule was implemented: quarterly in the first two years, semi-annually in years three and four, and yearly thereafter. Each visit included a physical exam, laboratory workup (encompassing blood routine tests, biochemical panels, and tumor markers), and endoscopic evaluation if clinically warranted. Radiographic surveillance with neck/chest/abdomen CT and brain MRI was routinely performed; PET-CT was utilized when recurrence was suspected. The schedule was adaptable to individual clinical needs. The median follow-up period was 21.5 months.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.5.2) and SPSS (version 25.0). With the maximization of the Youden index as the endpoint and the 5-year OS as the endpoint, the optimal cut-off value was determined through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to determine the independent prognostic risk factors. The variance inflation factor (VIF) was employed for the diagnosis of collinearity. The non-linear association between PIV and survival outcomes was explored using the restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve. The survival differences were compared using the Kaplan-Meier survival curve. Based on PIV and TNM staging, a risk stratification model was constructed through Recursive Partitioning Analysis (RPA) to predict and classify patients. Finally, the prognostic performance of the RPA model was evaluated using the ROC and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA). Bootstrap resampling (1,000 resamples) and 10‑fold cross‑validation will be performed for internal validation of the RPA model, and the calibration curve and corrected concordance index (C-index) will be calculated. Key variables had no missing data; censoring was handled using the partial likelihood method, and the proportional hazards assumption was tested and verified. All p values were two-tailed, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 696 patients with locally advanced ESCC were included. 58.5% were aged ≥ 65 years. Male patients accounted for 72.4%. Weight loss was present in 53.2% of patients. The most common tumor location was the middle thoracic esophagus (47.3%). Tumor length ≥ 5.55 cm was observed in 47.4% of patients, and tumor thickness ≥ 1.25 cm in 59.3%. Regarding TNM stage, 20.1% were stage II, 30.0% stage III, and 49.9% stage IVA. T3/T4 stages comprised 94.2% of patients; 72.1% had nodal involvement (N1–3). For treatment, 71.8% received < 3 chemotherapy cycles; 42.7% underwent concurrent chemoradiotherapy. The Radiotherapy dose was ≥ 59.75 Gy in 76.6% of patients. The optimal cut-off value of PIV was 247.13, and the corresponding AUC was 0.673 (95% CI: 0.633–0.713). 40.9% of the patients had a PIV ≥ 247.13. Subgroup analyses of PIV

We also conducted subgroup analyses based on PIV to evaluate its robustness in predicting prognosis among patients with different clinical characteristics. The results showed that in all the subgroups-including those divided by age, gender, weight loss status, tumor location, tumor size, TNM stage, and treatment modality-higher PIV levels were significantly associated with poorer OS and PFS (all p < 0.05, Fig. 5), except for the subgroup with ≥ 3 chemotherapy cycles. The hazard ratios and their 95% confidence intervals for each subgroup were all greater than 1, suggesting that PIV, as a comprehensive indicator integrating inflammation and immune status, demonstrates strong and consistent adverse prognostic predictive ability in different patient populations.

Constructing the RPA model based on PIV and TNM staging

The risk stratification model constructed based on RPA is shown in Fig. 6A. This model divides 696 patients with locally advanced ESCC into the following three risk groups: RPA I group (low PIV and stage II, n = 93), RPA II group (high PIV and stage II, or low PIV and stage III/IVA, n = 365), and RPA III group (high PIV and stage III/IVA, n = 238). Compared with the traditional TNM staging, the RPA model shows a higher HR, indicating its stronger ability to distinguish risks (Fig. 6B). The Kaplan-Meier survival curve further confirms that the risk stratification based on the RPA model is superior to the TNM staging in differentiating patient prognosis (Fig. 6C, D). In the quantitative comparison of prognostic performance, the area under the curve (AUC) of the RPA model for predicting 5-year OS and PFS is higher than that of the TNM staging (Fig. 6E). Moreover, the DCA also indicates that the RPA model has better predictive accuracy in clinical net benefit (Fig. 6F). The calibration curve shows that there is a good stability between the predicted probabilities of the RPA model and the actual observed values (Fig. 6G). The C-index of the model is 0.643.

Discussion

This study is the first to systematically evaluate the prognostic value of the pre-treatment PIV in patients with locally advanced ESCC who underwent dCRT. Currently, although the TNM staging system provides a basic anatomical prognostic framework, patients still exhibit significant prognostic heterogeneity after receiving the same treatment, highlighting the urgent need to integrate host biological information for more precise risk stratification. Against this backdrop, the main findings of our study include: (1) Higher pre-treatment PIV levels were significantly associated with greater tumor burden and later TNM stages; (2) Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that high PIV was an independent poor prognostic factor for OS and PFS; (3) RCS analysis revealed a positive non-linear association between PIV and survival outcomes; (4) The RPA risk stratification model constructed based on PIV and TNM staging was significantly superior to the traditional TNM staging system in terms of prognostic discrimination efficacy.

PIV, as a composite inflammatory indicator integrating neutrophil, platelet, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts, has been proven to have significant prognostic predictive value in various solid tumors. For example, in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer, higher baseline PIV levels were independently associated with shorter OS and a poorer response rate to first-line chemotherapy, and its predictive efficacy was even better than that of traditional inflammatory indicators such as the NLR and SII [20, 21]. In the field of lung cancer, regardless of whether patients received chemotherapy or immune checkpoint inhibitors, high baseline PIV was consistently reported as an independent poor prognostic factor, significantly associated with shorter OS and PFS [22, 23]. Similarly, in locally advanced or metastatic gastric cancer, high PIV predicted a lower tumor response rate and shorter survival time, and was positively correlated with a higher lymph node metastasis burden [24, 25]. In addition, studies in breast cancer also suggested that patients with higher PIV before neoadjuvant therapy tended to have a lower pathological complete response rate and a higher recurrence risk, further demonstrating the potential of PIV in predicting treatment response [26, 27]. Consistent with the above cross-cancer research evidence, this study is the first to systematically validate the prognostic value of PIV in patients with locally advanced ESCC who underwent definitive chemoradiotherapy, confirming that high pre-treatment PIV is an independent risk factor for survival outcomes, and this association remained robust in most pre-defined clinical subgroups. In addition, SHAP analysis provides interpretability for the XGBoost model, identifying pathological N stage, PIV, tumor thickness, and radiation dose as the most influential prognostic factors. These feature importances offer actionable insights for treatment planning. For instance, patients with high PIV and advanced N stage may have poor response to standard chemoradiotherapy and could be candidates for intensified therapy (e.g., higher radiation dose or combined targeted/immunotherapy). Conversely, patients with low-risk SHAP profiles might be suitable for de-escalated strategies in future clinical trials, reducing unnecessary toxicity. Additionally, PIV, as a modifiable inflammatory marker, suggests potential for pre-treatment risk modification through anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory interventions. These explanations bridge the gap between complex machine learning predictions and clinical decision-making, enhancing the model’s practical utility.

Notably, across all subgroups (except the ≥ 3 chemotherapy cycles group), the direction of the hazard ratio for PIV is consistently > 1, which further supports the cross-subgroup robustness of PIV as an adverse prognostic factor. In the subgroup of patients receiving ≥ 3 cycles of chemotherapy, the prognostic impact of high PIV appeared inconsistent. Several biological and treatment-related factors may explain this observation. First, sustained chemotherapy (≥ 3 cycles) may partially counteract the systemic inflammatory state reflected by high PIV through continuous suppression of tumor-associated inflammation. Chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., platinum, taxanes) not only directly kill tumor cells but also induce immunogenic cell death and reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine release, thereby diminishing the prognostic weight of PIV [28]. Second, patients who receive ≥ 3 cycles typically show a favorable initial treatment response, achieving longer disease stabilization or remission; in such cases, the immunosuppressive or pro-inflammatory microenvironment represented by baseline high PIV may be overridden by the sustained anti-tumor effects of chemotherapy. Third, patients able to complete ≥ 3 cycles often have better performance status and organ function, and thus a relatively better prognosis per se, making treatment intensity a more dominant prognostic factor than baseline PIV. Additionally, the relatively small sample size of this subgroup may lead to insufficient statistical power to detect the true prognostic effect of PIV. Larger prospective studies are warranted to validate this finding.

Tumor‑induced systemic inflammation is one of the core features driving cancer progression and treatment resistance [29, 30]. This response is far from a simple local phenomenon; rather, it is a systemic process that reshapes the entire immune landscape and metabolic homeostasis. As an indicator integrating four parameters, PIV has the advantage of more comprehensively capturing this complex “inflammatory immunosuppressive” microenvironment [31, 32]. Neutrophils can directly damage tissue by releasing mediators such as reactive oxygen species and elastase, promoting genomic instability, and providing a “scaffold” for tumor cell metastasis through the formation of neutrophil extracellular trap [33–35]. Platelets not only physically protect circulating tumor cells from immune clearance by aggregation, but also release various growth factors (e.g., PDGF, TGF‑β) that directly stimulate tumor proliferation and angiogenesis [36, 37]. Monocytes infiltrate tumors under the recruitment of tumor‑derived factors (e.g., CCL2) and polarize into M2‑type tumor‑associated macrophage with immunosuppressive functions, further inhibiting T‑cell function and promoting tissue remodeling [38, 39]. Lymphocytes, particularly cytotoxic T cells and NK cells, are the core executors of antitumor immunity [40, 41]. Systemic inflammation leads to lymphopenia or functional exhaustion, signifying the failure of the immune surveillance system [42]. Therefore, high PIV quantifies the imbalance between “pro‑tumor inflammatory forces” (product of neutrophils, platelets, and monocytes) and “antitumor immune forces” (lymphocytes), serving as a concentrated manifestation of the host immune system’s “collapse,” consistent with previous findings. The biological mechanisms linking PIV to tumor burden, immune suppression, and systemic inflammation have been well recognized. Emerging evidence further highlights the role of immune-tumor crosstalk and cytokine-mediated resistance in driving poor prognosis. For example, elevated PIV may reflect an immunosuppressive microenvironment characterized by increased infiltration of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which secrete cytokines such as IL-1, TGF-β, and IL-10 to promote tumor progression and immune evasion [43, 44]. Moreover, these pro-inflammatory cytokines can activate signaling pathways (e.g., STAT3, NF-κB) that confer resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, thereby diminishing treatment efficacy [45, 46]. The interplay between systemic inflammation, local immune suppression, and therapeutic resistance provides a mechanistic rationale for the strong prognostic value of PIV in locally advance ESCC. Future studies should explore whether modulating these inflammatory pathways could improve treatment outcomes.

As a serological indicator reflecting systemic inflammatory status, PIV is easy to obtain, cost-effective, and highly reproducible, making it a promising complement to multimodal features such as radiomics, pathomics, and transcriptomics in constructing a more comprehensive prognostic prediction model for locally advanced ESCC. In recent years, studies have integrated histopathological images with clinical information through a multimodal digital biopsy framework, or employed RSA-style analysis pipelines to fuse multi-source data, both demonstrating superior predictive performance compared to single-modal models [47]. For instance, studies in gastric cancer have shown the advantages of integrating imaging and pathological features, while combining transcriptomic data further improved prognostic stratification [48, 49]. PIV can serve as a global indicator reflecting the host’s systemic inflammatory-immune status, complementing the above modalities based on local tumor characteristics, and is expected to play a key role in future multimodal frameworks.

We have further translated the biomarker PIV into a risk stratification tool with potential clinical significance. The RPA risk stratification model constructed based on PIV and TNM staging integrates biological information reflecting the host’s systemic immune‑inflammatory status with the traditional anatomical staging system, thereby shifting from “static anatomical staging” toward a more comprehensive “biological‑anatomical assessment.” This model demonstrates improved prognostic discrimination and represents a preliminary step toward an intuitive and operable clinical decision support tool. By relying solely on routinely available pre‑treatment indicators, it can identify patients at different risk levels with distinct prognoses, which may assist risk stratification. For example, the model may help recognize patients in the same TNM stage who have a poor prognosis due to a high inflammatory state, as well as those who can derive sufficient benefit from standard treatment without excessive intervention. However, these findings are based on a single‑center retrospective cohort and require prospective validation in multicenter studies before clinical implementation. The establishment and application of this model may represent a meaningful step toward more precise and efficient individualized management in locally advanced ESCC.

However, this study has some limitations. First, this study is inherently limited by its single‑center retrospective design, and potential selection biases—such as treatment protocol variations and potential confounders (such as comorbidities, infections, medication use, and nutritional status) —cannot be fully excluded. Second, although the study sample size is relatively large, all patients are from the same center, which limits the external generalizability of the model. Third, the wide 95% confidence intervals observed in some small-sample subgroups (e.g., the ≥ 3 chemotherapy cycles group) reflect reduced statistical power and should be interpreted with caution. These results, however, do not undermine the robust and precise prognostic value of PIV established in the overall multivariable analysis. Therefore, our research results urgently need to be prospectively validated in a larger-scale, multi-center, independent external cohort (with different ethnic backgrounds). Finally, the inflammatory and immune status itself is dynamically changing, and this analysis only relies on single-time point data before treatment. Longitudinal monitoring of these indicators during and after treatment is required in the future.

Conclusion

In conclusion, pre-treatment PIV is a reliable and robust independent prognostic factor for patients with locally advanced ESCC receiving dCRT. The RPA risk stratification model constructed based on PIV and TNM staging is superior to the traditional staging system in prognosis discrimination, providing a simple, effective, and easily popularized risk assessment tool for clinical practice and contributing to guiding individualized treatment decisions.
