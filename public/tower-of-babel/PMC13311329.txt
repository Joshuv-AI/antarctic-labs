Data sets on the interaction of FKBP35 from Plasmodium knowlesi and Hsp90 C-terminal pentapeptide
Authors: Cahyo Budiman, Anna Robreth Robert, Jovi Silvester, Ping-Chin Lee, Kamruddin Ahmed, Thean Chor Leow, Azzyati Mohd Padzil, Meiny Suzery, Kazufumi Takano, Asep Awaludin Prihanto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFK506-binding protein 35 from Plasmodium knowlesi (Pk-FKBP35) is a multidomain peptidyl–prolyl cis–trans isomerase (PPIase) considered a potential target for antimalarial drug development. The protein consists of an N-terminal FK506-binding domain (FKBD) and a tetratricopeptide repeat domain (TPRD) containing a calmodulin-binding motif (CBM). The TPRD domain is known to recognize the conserved MEEVD motif located at the C-terminus of heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90). This dataset describes the biochemical and biophysical characterization of Pk-FKBP35 and its interaction with the Hsp90-derived MEEVD motif. Recombinant full-length FKBP35 (FL-FKBP35) and three variants, namely Pk-FKBD, Pk-TPRD+, and a calmodulin-binding motif deletion mutant (del-CBM), were heterologously expressed in E. coli and purified to high purity. Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed the structural integrity of the recombinant proteins. PPIase activity assays using a fluorogenic substrate demonstrated that FL-FKBP35, Pk-FKBD, and del-CBM exhibited catalytic activity, with kcat/KM values of 5.04 ± 0.21 × 10–5, 4.81 ± 0.34 × 10–5, and 5.17 ± 0.17 × 10–5 M-1 s-1, respectively, whereas Pk-TPRD+ showed no detectable activity. Interactions between FKBP35 proteins and the MEEVD peptide were examined using surface plasmon resonance analysis, revealing that the dissociation constant (KD) of FL-FKBP35 (54.75 ± 5.01 µM) was comparable (P > 0.05) to that of Pk-TPRD+ (46.41 ± 5.36 µM), while Pk-FKBD alone showed no detectable binding. Functional assays further demonstrated that MEEVD binding did not significantly affect the intrinsic PPIase activity of FKBP35 (P > 0.05). These data provide detailed biochemical, biophysical, and interaction profiles of Pk-FKBP35 and its interaction with the Hsp90-derived MEEVD motif.Keywords: Plasmodium knowlesi, FKBP35, Hsp90–MEEVD interaction, Peptidyl-prolyl isomerase (PPIase), Surface plasmon resonance

Background

Malaria remains a major infectious disease in tropical regions, including Southeast Asia, where Plasmodium knowlesi has emerged as an important zoonotic malaria parasite capable of infecting humans [1]. In Malaysian Borneoand neighboring regions, epidemiological studies have reported a considerable number of P. knowlesi infections, highlighting the need to better understand the molecular mechanisms underlying parasite biology and potential therapeutic targets [1,2].

One protein of interest is FK506-binding protein 35 (FKBP35), a member of the peptidyl-prolyl cis–trans isomerase (PPIase) family that catalyzes the isomerization of Xaa–Pro peptide bonds during protein folding [2,3]. In Plasmodium, FKBP35 is a multidomain protein consisting of an N-terminal FK506-binding domain (FKBD) followed by tetratricopeptide repeat domains (TPRD) [2]. The TPR fold motif is known to mediate interactions with the molecular chaperone heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90) and Hsp70 through a conserved pentapeptide motif (MEEVD) located at the C-terminal region of both proteins [4]. This short peptide motif functions as a recognition element for TPR-containing co-chaperones and facilitates the assembly of chaperone complexes involved in protein folding and maturation [5].

This dataset was compiled to document the in vitro interaction between FKBP35 from P. knowlesi (Pk-FKBP35) and the MEEVD pentapeptide derived from the C-terminal region of Hsp90. In addition to characterizing the interaction, the dataset includes experimental measurements describing how MEEVD binding influences the catalytic PPIase activity of FKBP35. The data provide experimental evidence supporting the role of the TPR domain in modulating FKBP35 interaction with the Hsp90 recognition motif and complement previously reported structural descriptions of FKBP proteins in Plasmodium species.

Data Description

For interaction analysis, full-length Pk-FKBP35 (FL-FKBP35) and its variants were heterologously expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3). The variants included the isolated FK506-binding domain (Pk-FKBD), the isolated tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domain containing the calmodulin-binding motif (TPRD+), and a full-length Pk-FKBP35 lacking the calmodulin-binding motif (del-CBM). The schematic primary structure of the proteins is shown in Fig. 1.

Primary structure of the Pk-FKBP35 protein. The FKBD domain spans from Met1 to Ala148, the TPRD+ domain from Lys149 to Asn280, and the CBM domain from Val281 to Lys323. All proteins are attached to His-tag at their N-terminal.

All recombinant proteins were successfully expressed and purified as shown in Fig. 2. The predominant bands for each recombinant protein were observed near their expected molecular weight positions on the 15% SDS-PAGE gel. FL-FKBP35 appeared as a major band at approximately 37 kDa, whereas Pk-FKBD migrated at approximately 18 kDa. Pk-TPRD+ showed an apparent molecular weight of approximately 25 kDa, while the del-CBM variant appeared at approximately 31 kDa. The observed molecular weights of FL-FKBP35, Pk-FKBD, Pk-TPRD+, and del-CBM were consistent with the theoretical molecular weights predicted from their respective amino acid sequences.

Purity of Pk-FKBP35 proteins under 15% SDS-PAGE. The marker of proteins with known sizes (kDa) are shown on Lane M. Lane 1, 2, 3 and 4 refer to del-CBM, Pk-FKBD, Pk-TPRD+ and FL-FKBP35, respectively.

The structural integrity of the purified proteins was examined using far-UV circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy (Fig. 3). FL-FKBP35 exhibited a far-UV CD spectrum with negative ellipticity signals extending from approximately 205 to 240 nm, with the minimum signal intensity reaching approximately −15,000 deg cm2 dmol-1 near 212–215 nm. Pk-TPRD+ showed the strongest negative ellipticity among the tested proteins, with signal intensities approaching −28,000 deg cm2 dmol-1 around 210–222 nm. In contrast, Pk-FKBD displayed substantially weaker negative ellipticity signals, with minimum values of approximately −5000 deg cm2 dmol-1 within a similar wavelength region. The del-CBM variant also exhibited relatively weak negative ellipticity signals, reaching approximately −6000 deg cm2 dmol-1. The secondary structure content calculated from far-UV CD spectra indicates that FL-FKBP35 is predominantly composed of α-helical structures (48.68%), supported by a minor contribution of β-sheet content (16.44%). A strong helical dominance was also observed for Pk-TPRD+ (58.98%), with negligible β-sheet content. In contrast, FKBD is primarily β-sheet-rich (39.68%), with a relatively low α-helical content (14.28%). Interestingly, despite exhibiting a spectral profile similar to Pk-FKBD, del-CBM showed a marked reduction in both α-helical and β-sheet contents, decreasing to 6.45% and 17.92%, respectively.

Far-UV CD spectra of Pk-FKBP35 proteins. FL-FKBP35 is shown in blue line, while red, green and black lines refer to Pk-TPRD+, del-CBM and Pk-FKBD, respectively.

As result, Pk-TPRD+ exhibited no detectable catalytic activity. In contrast, FL-FKBP35, Pk-FKBD, and the del-CBM variant demonstrated the ability to catalyze cis–trans isomerization of the substrate, with kcat/KM values ranging from 4.81 × 10–5 to 5.17 × 10–5 M-1 s-1. Statistical analysis showed that the kcat/KM values of these three variants were comparable (P > 0.05), despite structural differences among the proteins. Pk-FKBD consisted only of the FKBD domain without the TPRD-CBM segment, whereas the del-CBM variant contained the FKBD and TPRD domains but lacked the CBM region.

Fig. 4 presents representative binding sensorgrams obtained from surface plasmon resonance analysis of the interaction between FKBP35 proteins and the MEEVD peptide. In these experiments, FKBP35 proteins were immobilized on the sensor surface, while the MEEVD peptide was used as the analyte. FL-FKBP35 and Pk-TPRD+ produced clear binding responses, reaching approximately 190–200 RU during the association phase, followed by a decrease in signal intensity during the dissociation phase. A transient signal increase was also observed near the end of the association phase, reaching approximately 280–290 RU for both proteins. In contrast, Pk-FKBD exhibited only baseline-level responses, remaining close to 0 RU throughout the analysis. Similarly, the del-CBM variant showed minimal signal responses of <10 RU under the same experimental conditions.

Sensorgrams obtained from Biacore analysis showing representative binding responses of MEEVD (500 µM) to immobilized Pk-FKBP35 proteins. Injections were initiated at time zero and continued for 60 s.

Further analysis of the equilibrium binding responses plotted against the concentration of MEEVD produced saturation curves, as shown in Fig. 5. For FL-FKBP35, the equilibrium binding response increased from approximately 25 RU at the lowest peptide concentration tested to approximately 200 RU at 500 µM MEEVD. Similarly, Pk-TPRD+ exhibited progressively increasing response signals, ranging from approximately 20 RU at lower peptide concentrations to approximately 190 RU at 500 µM MEEVD. Intermediate concentrations of MEEVD also produced gradual increases in response units for both proteins, with binding responses of approximately 100–120 RU observed at 100 µM and approximately 150–180 RU observed at 200 µM peptide concentration. The equilibrium binding data were fitted to a single-site binding model, from which dissociation constant (KD) values were obtained and summarized in Table 2. FL-FKBP35 exhibited a KD value of 54.75 ± 5.01 µM, whereas Pk-TPRD+ showed a KD value of 46.41 ± 5.36 µM. Statistical analysis demonstrated that the KD values obtained for FL-FKBP35 and Pk-TPRD+ were comparable (P > 0.05). Throughout the concentration range tested, FL-FKBP35 consistently produced slightly higher equilibrium response unit values than Pk-TPRD+.

To examine whether MEEVD binding influences enzymatic activity, PPIase assays were conducted in the presence of the MEEVD peptide. As shown in Fig. 6, the measured PPIase activity values of FL-FKBP35 was measured at various concentration of MEEVD concentrations. Across all tested conditions, the recorded PPIase activity values remained within a narrow range of approximately 5.0–5.1 M-1 s-1. In the absence of MEEVD, FL-FKBP35 exhibited a catalytic activity of approximately 5.0 M-1 s-1. Addition of 1 nM and 10 nM MEEVD produced activity values that remained close to the baseline measurement, with only minimal variation observed. Similarly, assays conducted in the presence of 50 nM and 100 nM MEEVD showed activity values comparable to those obtained at lower peptide concentrations and in the absence of peptide. Statistical analysis demonstrated that the catalytic activity values obtained at different MEEVD concentrations were comparable to each other (P > 0.05). No major increase or decrease in activity values was detected throughout the tested concentration range. PPIase activity assays for Pk-TPRD+ in the presence of MEEVD were not conducted because this construct showed no detectable catalytic activity in the earlier enzymatic analysis.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Protein preparation

All proteins were prepared in a His-tagged form as previously reported [2]. The protein consists of full-length Pk-FKBP35 (FL-FKBP35), Pk-FKBD, Pk-TPRD+ and Pk-CBM. The schematic primary structure of each protein is shown in Fig 1. Overproduction and purification of these proteins were carried out as described previously [2]. Production levels of the recombinant proteins in E. coli cells and their purities were analyzed by SDS-PAGE23 using 15% polyacrylamide gel, followed by staining with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R250. Protein concentrations were determined from the UV absorption on the basis that the absorbance at 280 nm of a 0.1% (1 mg/ml) solution is 0.73, 0.65, and 0.76 for full-length FL-FKBP35 and del-CBM, Pk-FKBD, and Pk-TPRD+, respectively.

PPIase catalytic activity

The peptidyl-prolyl cis–trans isomerase (PPIase) activity of Pk-FKBP35 proteins (FL-FKBP35, Pk-FKBD, Pk-TPRD+, and del-CBM) was determined using a protease-coupled assay described by Goh et al. [2]. The assay employed the fluorogenic peptide substrate N-succinyl-Ala-Leu-Pro-Phe-7-amido-4-methylcoumarin (Peptide Institute, Osaka, Japan), following the protocol reported by Ikura et al. [6]. Reactions were conducted at 25 °C with FKBP35 proteins at a final concentration of 10 nM, and fluorescence measurements were carried out using a Cary Eclipse Fluorescence Spectrophotometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The resulting kinetic profiles were analyzed using SigmaPlot v10 (Systat Software, San Jose, CA, USA) [7].

To evaluate the effect of the MEEVD peptide on FL-FKBP35 activity, the enzyme was pre-incubated with the peptide at various molar ratios relative to the enzyme (0.1-, 1-, 5-, and 10-fold). The MEEVD peptide was chemically synthesized by GenScript Biotech (Piscataway, NJ, USA). The concentration of FL-FKBP35 was maintained at 10 nM throughout the assay. Fluorescence signals corresponding to substrate turnover were monitored over time, and the kinetic curves were subsequently analyzed using SigmaPlot v10.

Circular dichroism (CD) spectra

Circular dichroism (CD) spectra were recorded at 25 °C using a J-725 automatic spectropolarimeter (JASCO, Tokyo, Japan). For far-UV CD measurements (200–260 nm), protein samples were prepared at a concentration of approximately 0.2 mg/mL, and spectra were collected using a 2 mm path length quartz cuvette. For near-UV CD measurements (240–320 nm), protein samples were adjusted to approximately 0.7 mg/mL in 20 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 8.0) and analyzed using a 10 mm path length quartz cuvette. The mean residue ellipticity (θ, expressed in deg·cm²·dmol⁻¹) was calculated according to previous methods described [[8], [9], [10], [11]].

Surface plasmon resonance

The interaction between the proteins and the MEEVD peptide was analyzed using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) with a Biacore X 100 instrument (Biacore, Uppsala, Sweden). Immobilization of the His-tagged protein onto a Ni²⁺-chelated nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) sensor chip (Biacore) was performed according to a previously described procedure [12]. The MEEVD peptide, synthesized by GenScript Biotech (Piscataway, NJ, USA), was dissolved in 20 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 8.0) containing 2 mM dithiothreitol, 100 mM NaCl, and 1 mM EDTA. The peptide solution was injected over the sensor chip surface at 25 °C with a flow rate of 10 µL min⁻¹, where the His-tagged protein had been immobilized. After each binding cycle, the sensor surface was regenerated by washing with 0.5 M EDTA. To determine the dissociation constant (KD), MEEVD was injected at a series of concentrations. The equilibrium binding responses obtained at each concentration were plotted against ligand concentration, and KD values were calculated using the steady-state affinity model implemented in the BIAevaluation software (Biacore).

Data analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Differences among the mean values of PPIase activity, KD values, and ATPase activity were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with the significance level set at P < 0.05. When a significant difference among groups was detected, Tukey’s post hoc test was applied to determine pairwise differences between the means [13]. The ANOVA analysis was performed using an online ANOVA calculator (Standard Insights, https://standard-insights.com/tools/anova-calculator/).
