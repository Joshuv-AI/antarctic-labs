A search for Hadamard matrices of Williamson type
Hadi Kharaghani, Ali Mohammadian, Behruz Tayfeh-Rezaie, [4mm] Department of Mathematics, Computer Science, University of LethbridgeLethbridge, Alberta, T1K 3M4, Canada, [2mm] School of Mathematics, Institute for Research in Fundamental Sciences (IPM)P.O. Box 19395-5746, Tehran, Iran[4mm] kharaghani@uleth.ca ali_m@ipm.ir tayfeh-r@ipm.ir

Abstract
In this article, we consider a special class of Williamson type matrices which we call them near Williamson matrices. They are in fact four n × n n\times n ( − 1 , 1 ) (-1,1) -matrices A , B , C , D A,B,C,D so that A A is circulant, B , C , D B,C,D are symmetric circulant, and they satisfy A ​ A ⊤ + B ​ B ⊤ + C ​ C ⊤ + D ​ D ⊤ = 4 ​ n ​ I AA^{\top}+BB^{\top}+CC^{\top}+DD^{\top}=4nI . Using a computer search, we find all inequivalent near Williamson matrices for all odd orders at most 35 35 . We also show that such matrices exist for all odd orders up to 63 63 . As a consequence, we find the first known example of a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order 118 118 . Keywords: Back circulant matrix, Circulant matrix, Quaternary Hadamard matrix, Williamson type Hadamard matrix. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 05B20, 15B34.

Introduction and results

A
(-1,1)
-matrix is a matrix whose entries consist of
-1
or
1
.
A
Hadamard matrix
of order
n
is an
n\times n

(-1,1)
-matrix
H
such that
HH^{\top}=nI
, where
H^{\top}
is
the transpose of
H
and
I
is the identity matrix.
It is well known that the order of a Hadamard matrix must be
1
,
2
, or a multiple of
4

[
10
]
. It is conjectured that there exists a Hadamard matrix of order
n
for every
n
divisible by
4
. The smallest order for which no Hadamard matrix is presently known is
668

[
8
]
.

A matrix
A=[a_{ij}]_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}
is said to be

circulant
if
a_{ij}=a_{0\,j-i}
for all
i
and
j
, and

back circulant
if
a_{ij}=a_{0\,i+j}
for all
i
and
j
,
where indices are calculated modulo
n
. It is obvious that circulant and back circulant matrices are uniquely determined by their first rows.
If
A
is circulant and symmetric, then
a_{0k}=a_{0\,n-k}
for all
k
.
It is easy to see that any two circulant matrices commute with each other and every back circulant matrix is symmetric. Furthermore,
if
A
is a circulant matrix and
B
is a back circulant matrix, then
AB
is back circulant and
AB=BA^{\top}
. In particular,
every symmetric circulant matrix commutes with every back circulant matrix. We denote by
R
the back circulant matrix whose first row vector is
(0,\ldots,0,1)
.

In 1944, Williamson introduced the matrix

W=\left[\begin{array}[]{rrrr}A&B&C&D\\
-B&A&D&-C\\
-C&-D&A&B\\
-D&C&-B&A\end{array}\right]

for construction of Hadamard matrices
[
19
]
.
If four
n\times n

(-1,1)
-matrices
A,B,C,D
satisfy

\displaystyle AA^{\top}+BB^{\top}+CC^{\top}+DD^{\top}=4nI

(1)

and

\displaystyle XY^{\top}=YX^{\top}\,\text{ for all }\,X,Y\in\{A,B,C,D\},

(2)

then it is straightforwardly checked that
W

is a Hadamard matrix of order
4n
. Such a quadruple
\{A,B,C,D\}
are called
Williamson type matrices
of order
n
.

Williamson
[
19
]
used symmetric circulant matrices which trivially satisfy (
2
).
Williamson
type matrices
\{A,B,C,D\}
in which

A,B,C,D
are symmetric circulant

(-1,1)
-matrices of order
n

are said to be
Williamson matrices
of order
n
.
We note that several other plug-in constructions for Hadamard matrices similar to
W
have been introduced and
investigated in the literature; see, for example,
[
11
]
.

The method of Williamson was utilized by Baumert, Golomb, and Hall
[
1
]
to discover a Hadamard matrix of order
92
that is
the smallest order for which a Hadamard matrix could not be constructed using the classical methods due to Sylvester
[
13
]
and Paley
[
10
]
.

A
quaternary Hadamard matrix
of order
n
is an
n\times n
matrix
H
with entries from
\{-\mathbbmsl{i},-1,1,\mathbbmsl{i}\}

that satisfies
HH^{*}=nI
, where
\mathbbmsl{i}
is the imaginary unit number and
H^{*}

is the conjugate transpose of
H
.
It is well known that the order of a quaternary Hadamard matrix is
1
or divisible by
2

[
18
]
. It is an open question whether there exists a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order
n
for each even number
n
. After Đoković
[
3
]
constructed
a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order
70
, the first unknown order for the existence of quaternary Hadamard matrices seems to be
94
; see Table 11.2 in
[
12
]
.
If four
n\times n

(-1,1)
-matrices
A,B,C,D
satisfy (
1
) and (
2
),
then it is easily verified that the block matrix

\tfrac{1+\mathbbmsl{i}}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}A+\mathbbmsl{i}B&C+\mathbbmsl{i}D\\
\mathbbmsl{i}C+D&-\mathbbmsl{i}A-B\end{array}\right]

(3)

is a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order
2n

[
17
]
.
Plugging in near Williamson matrices of order
59
in (
3
) gives
a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order
118
which is the first previously unknown order
[
14
]
.

Finally, we present the definition of ‘equivalence’. For an
n\times n
matrix
X=[x_{ij}]_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}

and an automorphism
\sigma
of the additive group
\mathbbmsl{Z}_{n}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, we let
X^{\sigma}=[x_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}]_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1}
.
It is routine to check that if
X
is a circulant matrix, then so is
X^{\sigma}
.
A
signed permutation matrix

P
is a
(-1,0,1)
-matrix which has exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each column.
Two quadruples
\{A,B,C,D\}
and
\{A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime},D^{\prime}\}
of circulant
(-1,1)
-matrices are said to be
equivalent
if there exist
an automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathbbmsl{Z}_{n}
and four circulant signed permutation matrices
P,Q,S,T
such that
A^{\prime}=A^{\sigma}P
,

B^{\prime}=B^{\sigma}Q
,
C^{\prime}=C^{\sigma}S
, and
D^{\prime}=D^{\sigma}T
.

2
The algorithm

In this section, we describe our search algorithm for near Williamson
matrices of odd orders. We note that it is basically similar to the algorithm presented in
[
7
]
.
Let
\{A,B,C,D\}
be a quadruple of near Williamson matrices of order
n
, where
A
is circulant and
B,C,D
are
symmetric. We assume that
n
is odd throughout this section.

Since
AA^{\top}
,
BB^{\top}
,
CC^{\top}
,
DD^{\top}
are circulant matrices, they mutually commute with each other and therefore we may
consider a common basis consisting of eigenvectors of
AA^{\top}
,
BB^{\top}
,
CC^{\top}
,
DD^{\top}
. Denote by
\lambda_{0}(X),\lambda_{1}(X),\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}(X)

the eigenvalues of
XX^{\top}
for
X\in\{A,B,C,D\}

corresponding to this set of eigenvectors. It follows from
(
1
) that

\lambda_{i}(A)+\lambda_{i}(B)+\lambda_{i}(C)+\lambda_{i}(D)=4n\,\text{ for }\,i=0,1,\ldots,n-1.

(4)

Recall that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
of a circulant matrix

X
with the first row
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
are calculated by the
formula

\lambda_{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}x_{k}\omega_{i}^{k}

for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
where
\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
are the
n
th roots of unity and
\omega_{0}=1
. From this,
one can easily find the eigenvalues of
XX^{\top}
in terms of
the inner products of rows of
X
when
X
is circulant. In fact, if
X
is a circulant matrix, then

\lambda_{i}(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}(X)\cos\left(\frac{2ik\pi}{n}\right)

(5)

for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, where

p_{k}(X)
is the inner product of row
0
and row
k
of
X
.

Equalities (
4
) are useful in pruning the search
space in our algorithm. As the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}(A)
,
\lambda_{i}(B)
,
\lambda_{i}(C)
,
\lambda_{i}(D)
are all nonnegative, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(A)\leqslant 4n,

(6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(B)\leqslant 4n,

(7)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(A)+\lambda_{i}(B)\leqslant 4n

(8)

for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
.
We use (
6
)–(
8
) to eliminate some candidates for
A
and
B
during the search process.
The most useful case is when we use (
5
) with
i=0
in (
4
). It reads as

a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}=4n,

(9)

where
a,b,c,d
are the row sums of
A,B,C,D
. The relation (
9
) considerably reduces the possible candidates
for all matrices
A,B,C,D
.
By negating the matrices, without
loss of generality, we may assume that
the first element in the first rows of
A,B,C,D
are
1
. As
B,C,D
are
symmetric circulant matrices, it follows that
b\equiv c\equiv d\equiv n\pmod{4}
.
Without loss of generality, we may also assume that
|b|\geqslant|c|\geqslant|d|
.

We first find the set
\mathcal{M}
of all symmetric
circulant
(-1,1)
-matrices of order
n
with the row sum
b
and the first
entry in the first row being
1
. We then apply
the automorphism group of
\mathbbmsl{Z}_{n}
to the set
\mathcal{M}
and select
a representative
X
from each of the orbits and check if all the
eigenvalues of
XX^{\top}
are at most
4n
. This process eliminates
some of the unnecessary cases, resulting in a set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
of all
possible candidates for
B
.
We save
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
in the memory for later use.

For every circulant
(-1,1)
-matrix

Y
of order
n
with the row sum
a
and the first
entry in the first row being
1
