Diagnostic capability of ganglion cell complex thickness using spectral domain optical coherence tomography in glaucoma
Authors: Arika Poudel, Pragati Gautam Adhikari, Biplav Ghimire, Madhu Thapa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study had two main aims: first, aimed to document the normal values of macular ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness among control eyes and glaucoma suspects and second, to evaluate the diagnostic ability of GCC parameters in distinguishing Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG), Glaucoma Suspects and Controls in a tertiary eye centre in Nepal.MethodsA hospital-based cross-sectional observational study was conducted in Kathmandu, Nepal, from June 2020 to June 2021. GCC parameters (Average GCC, Superior GCC, and Inferior GCC) were measured using spectral-domain Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT). These parameters were compared among groups using Linear Mixed-effect Models, considering considerable inter-eye correlation. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed, and area under the curve (AUC) values were calculated to assess diagnostic performance.ResultsA total of 203 patients (406 eyes) were included − 76 with POAG, 62 Glaucoma suspects, and 65 Controls. Average GCC, superior GCC and Inferior GCC thickness were 96.54 ± 10.09 μm, 96.68 ± 10.91 μm and 96.43 ± 9.60 μm, in controls; 83.30 ± 11.46 μm, 83.88 ± 12.29 μm, and 81.94 ± 13.19 μm in POAG, and 93.76 ± 7.74 μm, 94.02 ± 8.03 μm, and 93.82 ± 8.27 μm, respectively in glaucoma suspects. All GCC parameters were significantly lower in the POAG group compared with both glaucoma suspects and controls (p < 0.05), even after adjustment for age. The highest diagnostic accuracy was observed for average GCC (AUC = 0.83, 95% C.I. – 0.77–0.92) and inferior GCC (AUC = 0.83, 95% C.I. – 0.76–0.90), both demonstrating excellent discrimination between POAG and controls (AUC = 0.83).ConclusionsAll GCC parameters demonstrated a significant diagnostic capability in detecting POAG, with average GCC and inferior GCC thickness showing the best performance. The single-centre cross-sectional study design, and lack of randomisation were the main limitations of this study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-05052-9.

Background

The ganglion cell complex is a complex neuronal structure that extends from the retina to the midbrain [1]. The thickness of the ganglion cell layer is measured over the macular region, taking into account the fact that more than half of the ganglion cells are localized at the macula. The ganglion cell complex (GCC) is affected by glaucoma in all of its components: cell bodies, dendrites, and axons [2].

Depletion in retinal nerve fibre layer thickness is an early sign of glaucoma, and it can progress to a large reduction in retinal ganglion cells (RGC) density before visual field abnormalities are noticed [2]. Studies have shown that up to 25–35% of RGCs may be lost before observable visual field defects appear. To avoid visual damage, it is thus, critical to recognise the structural alterations caused by RGC loss as soon as possible [3, 4].

Several methods are available to evaluate ganglion cell damage. Optic disc evaluation shows structural losses at the optic nerve head (for example, higher C/D ratio, neuro-retinal rim thinning, or notching). Structural changes can be identified by tests like Retinal Nerve Fibre Layer (RNFL) and GCC analysis and functional assessment of ganglionic cells can be detected by automated visual field deficits. But the capacity of existing clinical methods is limited to detect ganglion cell injury until there is a significant loss [4, 5].

OCT is a non-invasive optical imaging technology that has been used to assess in vivo morphologic changes in the optic nerve head, retina, and macular region [5]. While the relationship between the RNFL and the visual field has been thoroughly documented, the predictability of GCC and its relationship with retinal sensitivity is yet to be investigated. Reliance on automated visual field testing alone might miss the diagnosis, so GCC analysis should be incorporated to evaluate the structural damage [6].

The goal of this study was to find out the normal values of GCC parameters among control eyes and glaucoma suspects, and to compare thickness of various GCC parameters assessed by spectral-domain OCT among POAG, glaucoma suspect, and control groups, and evaluate diagnostic capability of macular GCC thickness in glaucoma, which have not been previously reported from Nepal.

Methods

A hospital-based cross-sectional observational study was conducted in a tertiary eye care centre at Kathmandu, Nepal, from June 2020 to June 2021.

The patients aged 18–80 years, who visited General Ophthalmology Outpatient Department (OPD) and Glaucoma clinic (new patients as well as patients under medications), and provided consent, were included as the study population.

The subjects were divided into the POAG, Glaucoma suspects and control groups.

Glaucoma suspects

Those with IOP > 21 and/or optic nerve head changes suspicious of glaucoma (e.g. increased cup-disc ratio for the size of the optic disc, disc haemorrhages, narrow disc rim, asymmetry of disc cupping, peripapillary atrophy), but with normal visual field [9, 10].

Controls

Individuals with IOP < 21 mm Hg, normal optic nerve head, no significant anterior or posterior segment abnormality and a refractive error within +3D to -3D, were the controls.

Those with angle closure glaucoma, secondary open angle glaucoma, moderate or severe glaucomatous visual field changes as per Anderson criteria, hypertensive or diabetic retinopathies, visually significant cataracts or media opacities, other retinal or macular diseases, refractive error beyond +3D or -3D, history of intraocular surgery or cryotherapy, and those with disc hemorrhages that resolved with treatment were excluded from the study.

Sample size

Sample size was calculated using the Obuchowski method for comparing a single AUC to a null hypothesis [11, 12]. We used an expected AUC of 0.885 (based on pooled AUC reported by Kansal et al.) [13], a null AUC of 0.7, alpha of 0.05, and a power of 80%. Considering a 1:1 test to control ratio, and a 20% dropout or anticipated imaging failure, the sample size calculated was a minimum of 59 per group.

Data collection

The patients in this study were examined in general ophthalmology clinic as well as the glaucoma clinic of BPKLCOS. Patient findings were recorded at the presentation. Proforma designed for this study was used to record the relevant history and clinical findings.

History

Detailed clinical and required ophthalmic histories were taken.

All relevant risk factors for POAG were assessed, and detailed family and treatment histories of glaucoma were obtained.

Examination

Best unaided and corrected visual acuity was recorded and retinoscopy done by a qualified optometrist. These findings were also converted to logMAR for the purpose of statistical analysis [14].

Detailed ocular examination, including slit lamp examination, anterior and posterior segment evaluation, IOP measurement by Goldmann Applanation Tonometer, indirect ophthalmoscopy and pachymetry (using Bon Sirius corneal topographer).

POAG patients and glaucoma suspects underwent special clinically required investigations, namely gonioscopy, by Goldmann single mirror lens, and automated visual field analysis by SITA standard 24 − 2 perimetry (2010 Carl Zeiss Meditec HFAII 750-41686–5.1.2/ 5.1.2). Automated visual field analysis was performed only when clinically indicated. However, AVF done within 6 months prior to the study were included.

All subjects were scanned using RTVue XR Avanti Edition OCT, software version 4.0 [15, 16]. Ganglion cell complex thickness was measured in all participants by GCC scan protocol, which consisted of one horizontal line with a 7 mm scan length (934 A-scans) and 15 vertical lines with a 7 mm scan length, and a 0.5 mm interval (800 A-scans) centred at 1 mm temporally to the fovea. This off-centre positioning is inherent to the manufacturer’s protocol as it maximizes sampling of the paramacular region with ganglion cell density is the highest. A minimun image quality signal strength index was set to 30 as per manufacturer’s guidelines. GCC thickness was defined as the distance from the internal limiting membrane to the outer boundary of the inner plexiform layer and was calculated automatically by the device. Eyes were divided into two sectors, superior and inferior. GCC was expressed as the average thickness of both sectors (Avg GCC) and separately as the thickness of the superior (Sup GCC) and inferior (Inf GCC). The OCT by RTVue normative database only includes individuals between 18 and 80 years. So, we excluded subjects outside this range. The technicians performing OCT were masked to the clinical status of the subjects. However, the ophthalmologists interpreting the OCT results were not systematically masked to the diagnosis of the patients. Indeterminate/un-interpretable OCT results were excluded from the study.

Subjects were classified as POAG, Glaucoma suspects and controls after history, examination, investigation, and final opinion from any of the glaucoma specialists at the clinic.

Statistical analysis and data management

All the proformas were thoroughly reviewed for completeness of data. Data were entered in MS Excel version 10. Proper data cleaning was done. Then the data was transferred to SPSS software version 23 and analysed. Comparison of average GCC, superior GCC, inferior GCC was done by comparison of means and the difference between GCC thickness among POAG, glaucoma suspects and control. The significance of various GCC thicknesses in the diagnosis of glaucoma was evaluated by linear mixed-effects models. Linear mixed effects model was preferred as it has low false positive rates for a modest sample size, and is better to interpret individual-level findings. ROC curves were drawn for each GCC parameter of right eyes with the purpose of discrimination between POAG and control, POAG and Glaucoma suspects, and validated with findings from left eyes. Areas under the Curve (AUC) were calculated along with 95% Confidence Intervals (CI), Youden optimal cutoff, and sensitivity, specificity, likelihood ratios and predictive values at that cutoff. Significance was determined by calculating and interpreting p- values.

Each additional year was associated with small but significant reduction in GCC parameters. Similarly, the difference in GCC thickness between Glaucoma Suspects and controls were not significant (p > 0.05).

Among the evaluated parameters, superior GCC provided the highest sensitivity for detecting POAG (72.40%), whereas average GCC demonstrated the highest specificity (86.60%). Inferior GCC showed a balanced diagnostic performance with sensitivity of 68.40% and specificity of 83.50%. Positive likelihood ratios ranged from 3.29 to 4.72, indicating a moderate increase in the probability of POAG following a positive test result, whereas negative likelihood ratios ranged from 0.35 to 0.43, suggesting modest capability to rule out. All GCC parameters demonstrated excellent negative predictive values (> 99.00%), indicating excellent ability to rule out POAG in populations with a similarly low prevalence. In contrast, the PPV values ranged from 5.05 to 7.12%, mainly reflecting the low prevalence of POAG. These findings suggest that GCC measurements alone may not be sufficient as standalone diagnostic tests, particularly in low prevalence settings where PPV is inherently limited. But they can be valuable tools in conjunction with other clinical findings, consistent with the AUC values observed in this study.

Discussions

Glaucoma is a progressive optic neuropathy characterised by the gradual degeneration of neuronal tissue, including retinal ganglion cells, often preceding detectable visual field deficits [3–5]. Identifying these structural changes early is essential to prevent visual damage. In the presence of normal visual field tests, clinical evaluation can be complemented with additional evaluation of the RNFL thinning with quantitative imaging techniques. In this study, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of macular GCC thickness in differentiating POAG, glaucoma suspects, and healthy controls.

The demographic characteristics of our study population were broadly comparable to previous studies. The average age of the POAG group was older than the other two groups, a finding comparable to previous studies and consistent with the known age-related rise in glaucoma prevalence [16, 20]. POAG cases showed a slight male predominance, contrary to the normally observed higher incidence of glaucoma among females [21, 22]. This may reflect differences in healthcare access and health-seeking behaviour. The average total IOP was similar to the findings of previous studies [20, 23, 24]. The similar average IOP across the three groups, however, might be due to inclusion of known patients under treatment. The significantly lower mean CCT in the POAG group also aligns with prior findings [25, 26].

Our findings demonstrate that all GCC parameters (average, superior, and inferior) were significantly reduced in POAG compared with both glaucoma suspects and controls. These results are consistent with previous studies reporting GCC thinning as an early structural marker of glaucomatous damage [6, 23, 27–33].

Furthermore, inferior GCC and average GCC showed the highest diagnostic accuracy, with excellent AUC values for distinguishing POAG from controls. Most of these studies have also identified average GCC and inferior GCC to have the best diagnostic values. Some studies, however have identified superior GCC to have the best diagnostic performance [27]. Such variations may be due to methodological differences, disease state or patient selection.

The diagnostic performance of GCC parameters in differentiating POAG from glaucoma suspects was acceptable but lower than that observed for POAG versus controls, similar to the findings by Deshpande et al., who reported AUROC of 0.81 for inferior temporal GCC [34]. This likely reflects the intermediate structural status of glaucoma suspects, in whom early or sub-clinical changes may not yet be sufficiently pronounced.

Additionally, GCC parameters demonstrated poor discrimination between glaucoma suspects and controls, suggesting limited utility in this specific comparison. A key strength of our study is the use of linear mixed-effects models to account for inter-eye correlation. The ICC values we observed confirm meaningful inter-eye correlation, justifying our analytical approach.

The high NPV, good sensitivity and specificity of the AUC cutoff found in our study were comparable to findings of Garas et al. [33] However, the difference in PPV might be attributable to the low prevalence of POAG in Nepali population [19].

Overall, our results support measurement of GCC parameters as a valuable tool for early glaucoma detection, particularly in conjunction with other clinical and imaging findings. Singh et al. also reported increase in diagnostic accuracy of GCC when other parameters like RNFL and visual fields were included [32]. While GCC assessment could not reliably distinguish glaucoma suspects from healthy eyes, it remains valuable for identifying early structural changes before functional loss becomes evident.

Our study had several limitations. Because our study was hospital-based, we could not randomize the subjects. As the patients visiting the ophthalmology and glaucoma clinic were taken as the sample population, the controls could not be fully matched for age and sex, possibly affecting its external validity. Similarly, selection of controls from the out-patient clinics might have led to selection bias, and may impact generalisability of findings. The POAG group was older than controls and suspects, which may influence GCC thickness as age-related retinal thinning occurs. The ophthalmologists interpreting OCT reports could not be masked due to limited availability of trained and expert clinicians. A multicentric, longitudinal study with a larger sample size and a longer duration of study might be required for validation and generalisation of these findings. Moreover, the thinning of macular GCC can sometimes occur in causes other than POAG, e.g. Myopia, Macular degeneration, and thus should be interpreted with caution [31, 35].

Conclusions

This study showed that the thickness of all the GCC parameters, namely Average GCC, Superior GCC, and Inferior GCC is considerably lower in POAG eyes as compared to Control eyes and that of Glaucoma suspects, with Average GCC and Inferior GCC having the best discriminating ability. While GCC assessment cannot reliably distinguish glaucoma suspects from healthy eyes, it remains valuable for identifying early structural changes before functional loss becomes evident. Thus, GCC parameters are useful in the early identification of glaucoma suspects and glaucoma patients, particularly in conjunction with other clinical examination and imaging findings.
