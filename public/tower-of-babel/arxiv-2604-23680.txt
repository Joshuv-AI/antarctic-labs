Universal Interpretation of Hidden Zero and 2 2 -Split of Tree-Level Amplitudes Using Feynman Diagrams, Part 𝐈 \mathbf{I} : Tr ⁡ ( ϕ 3 ) {\rm Tr}(\phi^{3}) , NLSM and YM
Kang Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a universal diagrammatic interpretation of hidden zeros and 2 2 -splits of tree-level amplitudes. Originally developed for Tr ⁡ ( ϕ 3 ) {\rm Tr}(\phi^{3}) amplitudes in our previous work, this interpretation is now extended to tree-level amplitudes in Nonlinear sigma model (NLSM) and Yang-Mills (YM) theories. The interpretation is based on a certain factorization behavior of Feynman diagrams under specific kinematic constraints, which we term shuffle factorization along a specific line (SFASL). This mechanism allows us to separate Feynman diagrams along specific lines after summing over shuffle permutations. When applied to NLSM and YM amplitudes, we perform proper extensions of the SFASL used in the Tr ⁡ ( ϕ 3 ) {\rm Tr}(\phi^{3}) case. Through the SFASL, the interpretation for the hidden zeros and 2 2 -splits of tree amplitudes of Tr ⁡ ( ϕ 3 ) {\rm Tr}(\phi^{3}) , NLSM, and YM can be unified as: the hidden zeros are ascribed to the on-shell condition k j 2 = 0 k_{j}^{2}=0 of a massless particle, while the 2 2 -splits are caused by separating each Feynman diagram along two lines, akin to unzipping two zippers.

vertex vanishes. The above highly special conditions can be relaxed in two ways: (i)

(

) is greater than

(

) remains equal to

but this

-line (

-line) is an internal line. In what follows, we demonstrate that the contributions from these two cases cancel each other.

To see how this cancellation arises, let us first consider
the simple example shown in Fig.

. From the standpoint of
L_{(i,\bullet)}
, the left diagram in Fig.
11
corresponds to
n_{A}=3
, whereas the right diagram corresponds to
n_{A}=1
, with the associated
A
-line being an internal line.
By using the

-form of
V_{2n+2}
in (

), the left diagram of Fig.

can be evaluated as

\displaystyle F_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\,{-1\over 2^{2}}\,\Big(k_{A_{1}}+k_{A_{3}}+k_{B_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}

(44)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,{-1\over 2^{2}}\,\Big(k_{A_{1}A_{3}}^{2}+k^{2}_{B_{1}}\Big)\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}\,.~~

Meanwhile, the right diagram can be evaluated as

\displaystyle F_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\,{1\over 2}\Big(k_{A_{1}}+k_{A_{3}}\Big)^{2}\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}}\,\Big[\,{1\over 2}\Big(k_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}+k_{B_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}

(45)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,{1\over 2^{2}}\,k_{A_{1}A_{3}}^{2}\,{1\over s_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}}\,\Big(k^{2}_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}+k^{2}_{B_{1}}\Big)\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2^{2}}\,k_{A_{1}A_{3}}^{2}\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}\,+\,{1\over 2^{2}}\,k_{A_{1}A_{3}}^{2}\,{1\over s_{A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}}}\,k_{B_{1}}^{2}\,{1\over s_{iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}B_{1}}}\,.

As can be seen, in the summation over Feynman diagrams, the first term of

is an odd integer greater than

. The same circumstance holds for the
B
-set. Therefore, we must sum over the four diagrams shown on the l.h.s. of Fig.

in (

), as well as the observation (

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,\Big[\,{(-1)^{(n_{A}+n_{B}-2)/2}\over 2^{(n_{A}+n_{B})/2}}\,\Big(K_{A}^{2}+K_{B}^{2}\Big)\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{i\{A\}\{B\}}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,\Big[\,{(-1)^{(n_{A}-3)/2}\over 2^{(n_{A}-1)/2}}\,K_{A}^{2}\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{\{A\}}}\,\Big[\,{(-1)^{(n_{B}-1)/2}\over 2^{(n_{B}+1)/2}}\,\Big(k_{\{A\}}^{2}+K_{B}^{2}\Big)\,\Big]\,{1\over s_{i\{A\}\{B\}}}\,,

3.3
General SFASL: recursive proof

Now we present a general proof of the SFASL expressed in (
30
), for the NLSM diagrams. First, we note that our treatment in the previous subsection for the case where both
n_{A}
and
n_{B}
are odd is generic; under the kinematic constraint (
2
), this case never contributes to the amplitude. Therefore, we only need to consider the case where both
n_{A}
and
n_{B}
are even. The proof will be carried out in a recursive manner.

In subsection
3.1
, we showed that the SFASL in (
30
) holds for
p=q=1
. Moreover, when
p=0
or
q=0
, the SFASL in (
30
) trivially holds, since there are no shuffle permutations in these cases. Now we assume that (
30
) holds for
(p,q-1)
,
(p-1,q)
, and
(p-1,q-1)
. To apply this assumption, we separate the l.h.s. of (
30
) into three parts,

\displaystyle\sum_{\shuffle(p,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-N_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p,q-1)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-1-N_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

(48)

\displaystyle+\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p-1,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-1-N^{\prime}_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p-1,q-1)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-2-N^{\prime\prime}_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}|\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\,,~~~

where we have defined
\underline{\{A\}}\equiv\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{p-1}
and
\underline{\{B\}}\equiv\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{q-1}
to simplify the expression. In (
48
),
three parts correspond to three cases
(p,q-1)
,
(p-1,q)
and
(p-1,q-1)
, respectively. Using the SFASL, as well as the
K\cdot K
-form of
V_{2n+2}
in (
29
), we can evaluate the first part as,

\displaystyle F_{(p,q-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p,q-1)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-1-N_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

(49)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,\Big[\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q-1}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\Big]\,{V_{\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p-1}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q-1}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\Big]\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}\,{V_{\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+1}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~{(-1)^{m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{B_{q}}+1}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}+2}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle+C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}\,{-2k^{2}_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\,,~~

where the definitions of
{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}
,
{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}
,
m_{A_{p}}
and
m_{B_{q}}
are the same as those in subsection
3.1
.
The factor
C_{(p-1,q-1)}
is introduced for simplicity,

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}=\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p-1}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q-1}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\,.

(50)

Notice that
V_{\{A\}_{p}}
in the above is
{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}
, this is because it is taken from the factorized form in the second line. In the last step, we have used

\displaystyle k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\,\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}\,k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}+k^{2}_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\,.

(51)

As in subsection
3.1
, this decomposition aims to separate
V_{\{B\}_{q}}
into a part that is completely independent of the momentum from
A
-lines and commutes with
V_{\{A\}_{p}}
, and a part that depends on the momentum from
A
-lines and does not commute with
V_{\{A\}_{p}}
. For the convenience of the following discussion, we say that the entire
F_{(p,q-1)}
is divided into a commuting part and an non-commuting part.

The second part in (
48
) can be evaluated similarly,

\displaystyle F_{(p-1,q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p-1,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-1-N^{\prime}_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

(52)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,\Big[\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p-1}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q-1}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\Big]\,{V_{\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B_{q}\}}}\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}+2}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B_{q}\}}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle+C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}+2}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B_{q}\}}}\,{-2k^{2}_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\,.

Again,
F_{(p-1,q)}
is divided into a commuting part and an non-commuting part.
Meanwhile, the third part can be computed as

\displaystyle F_{(p-1,q-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\,\sum_{\shuffle(p-1,q-1)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-2-N^{\prime}_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\,\Big)\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}|\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

(53)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,\Big[\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p-1}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q-1}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\Big]\,{V_{\{A\}_{p}|\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}+1}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}+{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\,.

Adding
F_{(p,q-1)}
,
F_{(p-1,q)}
and
F_{(p-1,q-1)}
together, and using
s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A_{p}\}}=k^{2}_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A_{p}\}}
,

s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B_{q}\}}=k^{2}_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B_{q}\}}
,
as well as the observation

\displaystyle k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}+k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\,,

(54)

we see that
F_{(p-1,q-1)}
is canceled by non-commuting parts of
F_{(p,q-1)}
and
F_{(p-1,q)}
. The final result comes from the commuting parts of
F_{(p,q-1)}
and
F_{(p-1,q)}
,

\displaystyle F_{(p,q-1)}+F_{(p-1,q)}+F_{(p-1,q-1)}

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,{(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+m_{B_{q}}+2}}\,\Big[\,\big({\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\big)\,\big({\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\big)\,\Big]

\displaystyle\Big(\,{1\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}+{1\over s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\,\Big)\,{1\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-shuffle})}

\displaystyle\,C_{(p-1,q-1)}\,\Big({(-1)^{m_{A_{p}}}\over 2^{m_{A_{p}}+1}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{A\}_{p}}-2k_{i\underline{\{A\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}\over s_{i\underline{\{A\}}\{A\}_{p}}}\Big)\,\Big({(-1)^{m_{B_{q}}}\over 2^{m_{B_{q}}+1}}\,{{\cal V}_{\{B\}_{q}}-2k_{i\underline{\{B\}}}\cdot k_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}\over s_{i\underline{\{B\}}\{B\}_{q}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\times\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\,,~~

where we have used the observation (
11
).
The factorization formula in the last line of (
55
) completes the recursive proof. As can be seen, apart from the factor
C_{(p-1,q-1)}
, the above process is exactly the same as that in subsection
3.1
. That is, the SFASL can be achieved stepwise along
L_{(i,\bullet)}
via an unique mechanism.

3.4

In this subsection, we will interpret the hidden zeros and

3.4.1
Hidden zero

The kinematic condition for the hidden zeros of NLSM amplitudes is also given by (
12
), and the interpretation of the hidden zeros is in complete agreement with the
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
case. As in the
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
case, we can divide
\boldsymbol{A}
and

\boldsymbol{B}
separately into a series of subsets, each of which will contribute the corresponding BG current of the NLSM. Similar to Fig.
5
, for a given division, the summation over Feynman diagrams involves a summation over shuffle permutations. Applying the SFASL to the line
L_{(i,j)}
(multiplied by
D^{(i,\bullet)}_{p+q}/D^{(i,\bullet)}_{p+q}
to compensate for the difference between internal and external lines), we find that, in the NLSM case, (

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{\shuffle(p,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-N_{A|B}}\,{V^{(i,\bullet)}_{t}\over D_{t}^{(i,\bullet)}}\Big]\,s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{p}\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{q}}\,

\displaystyle\,\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{p}\,{V_{\{A\}_{\alpha}}\over s_{i\{A\}_{1}\cdots\{A\}_{{\alpha}}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{q}\,{V_{\{B\}_{\beta}}\over s_{i\{B\}_{1}\cdots\{B\}_{{\beta}}}}\Big)\,k_{j}^{2}\,.~~

(56)

Note that
p
and
q
in the above denote the numbers of
A
-sets and
B
-sets, respectively, rather than the numbers of subsets in the given division.
Although the expression becomes more complicated, the factor
k_{j}^{2}
remains unchanged. Again, the on-shell condition
k_{j}^{2}=0
indicates the vanishing of (
56
). This phenomenon holds for any divisions of
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
, thus we arrive at the following hidden zero

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{2n}^{\rm NLSM}(1,\cdots,2n)\,

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-0-scalar})}

\displaystyle\,0\,.~~

(57)

3.4.2

-split: first case

The kinematic condition for the

-split remains given by (

). To reproduce the

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{2n}^{\rm NLSM}(1,\cdots,2n)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\rm div}\boldsymbol{A}}\,\sum_{{\rm div}\boldsymbol{B}}\,\sum_{{\cal P}_{\boldsymbol{A}}}\,\sum_{{\cal P}_{\boldsymbol{B}}}\,\Big(\sum_{\shuffle(p,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-N_{A|B}}\,{V_{t}^{(i,v)}\over D_{t}^{(i,v)}}\Big)\,\Big(\sum_{\shuffle(m,l)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{m+l-N^{\prime}_{A|B}}\,{V_{t}^{(j,v)}\over D_{t}^{(j,v)}}\Big)\,V_{v}\,f^{\rm NLSM}(R)\,.

\boldsymbol{A}
into

\boldsymbol{B}
into

subsets,

with the external leg

are attached to

, which to

, and which to

. The definition of

and

and

). In (

is the contribution from the vertex

. The summation over Feynman diagrams is separated into the summation over divisions, the summation over partitions

and

Applying the SFASL to (

\displaystyle\sum_{\shuffle(p,q)}\,\prod_{t=1}^{p+q-N_{A|B}}\,{V_{t}^{(i,v)}\over D_{t}^{(i,v)}}\,

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-2split-scalar})}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{kine-condi-2split-scalar})}

Meanwhile, based on the divisions in (

\displaystyle f^{\rm NLSM}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{r}\,{\cal J}^{\rm NLSM}_{A_{\alpha}}\,{1\over s_{A_{\alpha}}}\Big)\,\Big(\prod_{{\beta}=1}^{h}\,{\cal J}^{\rm NLSM}_{B_{\beta}}\,{1\over s_{B_{\beta}}}\Big)\,,~~

The expression (

In (
83

\{\mu\}
, provided by the collection of polarization vectors and BG currents, is contracted with the set of lower indices
\{\mu\}\cup\nu
, provided by the set of vertices. Note that except for the
\eta^{\nu\rho}
carried by the last propagator, all upper indices carried by the other propagators on

have already been contracted with the lower indices of the vertices on
L_{(i,\bullet)}
, while the set
\{\mu\}
of lower indices consists of those remaining after the contraction. After the contraction between the upper
\{\mu\}
and lower
\{\mu\}

\nu

\eta^{\nu\rho}
to an upper index

\rho

L_{(i,\bullet)}

\epsilon_{i}
connected to
L_{(i,\bullet)}

\rho

\bullet
and is eventually contracted at another vertex. This is the general strategy we adopt in the remainder of this section for contracting the Lorentz index of a given vertex with other vectors.

The above way of understanding the contractions is based on the following picture. In the preceding two sections, when proving the SFASL for the
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
and NLSM diagrams using the recursive approach, we always started from the external leg

i
and progressively implemented factorization along

L_{(i,\bullet)}

n-1

n-1

L_{(i,\bullet)}
and participates in contractions at other vertices. The only exception occurs when reproducing the
2

v
where
L_{(i,v)}
,
L_{(j,v)}
, and
L_{(k,v)}
meet, we take the viewpoint that all vectors contract with all indices of the vertex
v
.

5
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we generalized a mechanism of Feynman diagrams—denoted as SFASL—to the cases of NLSM and YM. This mechanism was previously found in our earlier work
Zhou:2024ddy
to be satisfied by the Feynman diagrams of
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
model, and the generalization was achieved by extending the pattern of shuffle permutations. Through this generalized SFASL, we further interpreted the hidden zeros and
2
-split structures of tree-level amplitudes in NLSM and YM. In the extended shuffle permutations, mixed vertices
V_{\{A\}|\{B\}}
emerged. We have showed that these mixed vertices are canceled by the non-commuting parts of the unmixed vertices. Ultimately, the SFASL reduces the hidden zeros to the on-shell condition
k_{j}^{2}=0
, and causes the
2
-splits by separating each Feynman diagram along
L_{(i,v)}
and
L_{(j,v)}
. Although the verification procedure was rather involved and lengthy, the underlying picture of the SFASL and the cancellation of mixed vertices is remarkably concise. Synthesizing our present results with previous work
Zhou:2024ddy
, we now know that the hidden zeros and
2
-splits of tree amplitudes for the three models—
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
, NLSM, and YM—can all be universally interpreted from the SFASL.

We naturally expect that the SFASL can also be used to interpret the hidden zeros and
2
-splits in other models, including SG, DBI, GR, as well as YM and GR with specific higher-derivative corrections. At the very least, it is easy to verify that the mass dimensions of the vertices in these models all satisfy the required constraints (
26
). Furthermore, it is straightforward to see that for amplitudes without ordering, the shuffle permutations along
L_{(i,\bullet)}
discussed in this paper also applies, provided that the sets
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
(unordered) are appropriately chosen. This is because, one can always fix the order of
A
-sets and the order of
B
-sets, while absorbing the permutations among
A
-sets and the permutations among
B
-sets into the summation over different divisions. For standard GR amplitudes, and YM amplitudes with specific higher-derivative corrections, we have made some progress, but several technical difficulties remain unresolved. Therefore, we will report these results in a forthcoming paper.

Suppose we prove that all currently known models exhibiting hidden zeros and
2
-splits satisfy the SFASL, and that their hidden zeros and
2
-splits can be explained by it. Then, although logically the SFASL is only a sufficient condition for these structures rather than a necessary one, we can at least conclude that the SFASL governs the presence of hidden zeros and
2
-split in a large class of physical models.
As discussed in section
1
, this allows us to reverse the logical direction. Instead of starting from a model and testing the SFASL, we can ask what constraints the SFASL imposes on physical models—for example, on their Lagrangians or Feynman rules. For instance, we may ask: what requirements must the interaction vertices satisfy so that mixed vertices are canceled by the non-commuting parts of unmixed vertices? If these questions can be answered, then when encountering a new physical model, we may be able to quickly determine whether its tree-level amplitudes possess hidden zeros and
2
-split structures.

Finally, as noted in section
1
, our recent discovery of hidden zeros and
2
-split in loop-level Feynman integrands of
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
model was achieved by a method that directly generalizes the tree-level SFASL for
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})

Zhou:2026ukg
. Given that the tree-level SFASL can be extended to other models such as NLSM and YM, it is reasonable to expect that a similar generalization also holds at loop-level. This suggests that, through the SFASL, we may be able to extend the hidden zeros and
2
-split of
{\rm Tr}(\phi^{3})
Feynman integrands to other physical models.
