A Runge-type theorem by remote forcing for the linearized resistive MHD system
Mitsuo Higaki, Franck Sueur

Abstract
In this paper, we study a quantitative Runge-type global approximation theorem for the linearized magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system in bounded domains with arbitrary topology. In the context of magnetic relaxation, the interplay between the domain topology and magnetic field structure plays a crucial role. Recent studies illustrate a sharp contrast in the dynamics: while Enciso–Peralta-Salas [ 8 ] highlights that the geometric complexity of magnetic fields acts as an obstruction to relaxation in non-resistive regimes, Kozono-Shimizu-Yanagisawa [ 15 ] proves that in resistive regimes, the flow stably relaxes towards a harmonic equilibrium. Focusing on this resistive scenario, we adopt a control-theoretic viewpoint to quantitatively approximate the relaxation trajectory generated by the linearized initial-boundary value problem. Specifically, after decomposing the bounded-domain solution into the time-evolving part and the stationary part, we approximate it by a global solution on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} under a remote forcing. An explicit dependence of the forcing cost on the approximation error is provided.

Introduction and main results

The interplay between the topology of the underlying domain and the geometric structure of magnetic fields is a central theme in modern magnetohydrodynamics (MHD). A fundamental question in this field, dating back to the pioneering works of Arnold
[
2
]
, Parker
[
23
,
24
]
, and Moffatt
[
20
,
21
]
, is the problem of magnetic relaxation: determining whether and how a given magnetic field evolves towards an MHD equilibrium.

Recent mathematical results have revealed that the answer depends critically on the presence of resistivity. In the context of the non-resistive MHD system, Enciso–Peralta-Salas
[
8
]
identified obstructions to magnetic relaxation. They proved that generic magnetic fields, due to the conservation of complex geometric structures, cannot be transformed into equilibria via volume-preserving diffeomorphisms. This highlights the rigidity of magnetic field lines in the ideal MHD case, where they are transported by the fluid flow.

In sharp contrast, for the resistive MHD system, the presence of magnetic diffusivity acts as a mechanism for energy dissipation, breaking the geometric rigidity in the non-resistive case. Focusing on this dissipative regime, Kozono-Shimizu-Yanagisawa
[
15
]
established the exponential stability of harmonic magnetic fields. Although their result relies on a smallness assumption to utilize perturbation techniques, it reveals a fundamental topological selection principle: the flow does not simply decay to zero, but stably relaxes towards a non-trivial harmonic equilibrium dictated by the domain’s cohomology (see
[
27
]
).

From a control-theoretic viewpoint, it is a highly non-trivial question whether such a physically natural, dissipative relaxation process can be artificially replicated by remote forcing. In this paper, we focus on the linearized resistive MHD system and investigate the global approximation of such a relaxation process. The global approximation theorem, or the Runge property, is a cornerstone of the theory of partial differential equations, asserting that a solution defined on a subdomain can be approximated by one defined on a larger domain. While the qualitative aspects of this property have been well established through the classic works for elliptic equations
[
16
,
19
,
3
]
and the developments in parabolic equations
[
13
,
6
]
, recent research has shifted towards its quantitative aspect to determine the “cost of approximation”
[
26
,
7
,
4
]
. A compelling example of this shift is found in the study of topological transitions: Enciso–Peralta-Salas
[
7
]
leveraged a quantitative Runge-type theorem for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation to rigorously demonstrate the existence of quantum vortex reconnection. Their work highlights that the ability to precisely control the approximation error is not merely technical, but essential for capturing such structural evolutions. Inspired by this perspective, specifically the quantitative control of topological dynamics, we aim to evaluate the cost of replicating the aforementioned magnetic relaxation. Our main result, Theorem
1.1
below, provides explicit estimates that account for the hybrid nature of the dynamics, decomposed into a dissipative time-evolving part and a stationary topological part, thereby quantifying the control cost for replicating the magnetic relaxation towards an equilibrium. Although our analysis is restricted to the linearized system, this quantitative framework serves as a crucial first step towards controlling the full nonlinear MHD system.

Remark 1.2

(i)

The presence of a background magnetic field
H_{*}
makes the linearized MHD system a system with variable coefficients. In the works
[
12
,
11
]
, the global approximation was constructed explicitly by using series expansions in (vector) spherical harmonics and by solving a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs). While powerful, this ODE method relies heavily on the algebraic structure of constant coefficient operators, or separation of variables. It is inherently not robust under perturbations by variable coefficients; the loss of symmetry breaks the diagonal structure of the system, making explicit construction practically impossible for general
H_{*}
.

To overcome this limitation, we abandon constructing a global approximation that solves the homogeneous system. Instead, we adopt the “source term approach” inspired by Rüland-Salo
[
26
]
. We prove the existence of a global solution under forcing supported in a control set
Y
. Then, by the quantitative unique continuation property rather than explicit calculation, the approach in
[
26
]
offers a robust way to estimate the cost of approximation, valid even for variable coefficient operators.

(ii)

In the context of MHD, Runge approximation on compact subsets is topologically trivial. Indeed, considering the approximations on an interior set
K
of
D
as in
[
6
,
12
]
obscures the cohomological structure of the magnetic field. Locally, a harmonic magnetic field
H_{\ast}
can be expressed as a gradient of a potential, losing its global circulatory character. The richness of the MHD system in a non-simply connected domain lies precisely in the interaction between the fluid and the background magnetic field
H_{\ast}
.

(iii)

The factors
\exp(\rho_{0}t)
in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) arise from the contour integral method used in the proof in Section
5
; they are not claimed to reflect the sharp long-time behavior. The contour admissible simultaneously for
\mathbb{S}
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
, the extended whole-space operator in Section
2
, contains a low-frequency circular arc on which
\Re\lambda
may be positive, while the available resolvent information does not allow us to replace it by a common contour contained in
\{\Re\lambda\leq-c\}
for some
c>0
. Accordingly, an estimate with a factor
\exp(-ct)
for the evolutionary error would require additional low-frequency information or a different construction. Any such improvement is concerned only with the evolutionary part, since the topological part is time independent.

Remark 1.3

It is instructive to clarify the relationship between topology, forcing, and boundary conditions in the context of Runge-type global approximation.

•

Topology and Forcing
: For a bounded domain
D
, the condition
\beta_{2}(D)=0
means that
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
has no bounded connected components. This was the topological assumption used in
[
11
]
to construct a homogeneous global approximation. By allowing for forcing (source terms) supported in the complement
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, as we do in this paper following
[
26
]
, the Runge approximation becomes feasible even when
\beta_{2}(D)>0
, akin to allowing poles in classical Walsh’s theorem
[
29
]
for harmonic functions.

•

Boundary Conditions
: The emergence of a non-trivial equilibrium depends critically on the boundary conditions. For the velocity field
v
, the no-slip condition precludes the existence of non-trivial harmonic fields, meaning the velocity part of the local solution effectively has no topological part. In contrast, for the magnetic field
H
, the perfect conductor condition
H\cdot n=0
allows for non-trivial harmonic fields. Remarkably, the coupling of the MHD system dictates the necessity of the stationary velocity forcing
i
to maintain the equilibrium. As a byproduct, when
H_{*}=0
, our result covers the Stokes system, extending the scope of
[
11
]
.

For the use in the following sections, we need to extend the MHD operator
\mathbb{S}
on

Lemma 2.1

There exists an extension
\tilde{H}_{*}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
such that

\tilde{H}_{*}|_{D}=H_{*},\qquad\operatorname{div}\tilde{H}_{*}=0,\qquad\operatorname{spt}\tilde{H}_{*}\Subset B_{R}.

Moreover,

\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|H_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(D)}.

The constant
C>0
is independent of
H_{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\{h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\}
be a basis of
X_{{\rm har}}(D)
. Since
h_{j}\cdot n=0
on
\partial D
, we have

\int_{\Gamma_{\ell}}h_{j}\cdot n\,d\sigma=0,

(2.1)

for every connected component
\Gamma_{\ell}
of
\partial D
.

Fix

. Thanks to the divergence-free extension theorem of Kato-Mitrea-Ponce-Taylor
[
14
, Corollary 3.2]
with
s=2
, there exists
\tilde{h}_{j}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
such that

\tilde{h}_{j}|_{D}=h_{j},\qquad\operatorname{div}\tilde{h}_{j}=0,\qquad\operatorname{spt}\tilde{h}_{j}\Subset B_{R}.

Note that the flux coefficients
\lambda_{j}
appearing in
[
14
, Corollary 3.2]
vanish due to (
2.1
). Since
p>3
, the Sobolev embedding gives

W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Now write

H_{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}h_{j},\qquad\tilde{H}_{*}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}\tilde{h}_{j}.

Then the extension properties required in the statement follow by linearity. Moreover, since
X_{{\rm har}}(D)
is finite-dimensional, all norms are equivalent on it, and therefore

\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}|c_{j}|\leq C\|H_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(D)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2

The adjoint operator
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}
is given by

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}u\\

where the adjoint coupling operators are defined by

\begin{split}\mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}B&=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}((\operatorname{rot}B)\times\tilde{H}_{*}),\\
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}u&=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla u-(\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})^{\top}u).\end{split}

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
are self-adjoint on their respective domains.

For
\mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}
, by the identity
(A\times B)\cdot C=A\cdot(B\times C)
and integration by parts,

\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u,B\rangle&=\langle\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\times u),B\rangle\\
&=\langle\tilde{H}_{*}\times u,\operatorname{rot}B\rangle\\
&=\langle u,\operatorname{rot}B\times\tilde{H}_{*}\rangle.\end{split}

Applying the projection
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
leads to the formula of
\mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}

For
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}
, by the definition,

\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}B,u\rangle&=\langle-\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*}),u\rangle\\
&=-\langle\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B,u\rangle-\langle B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*},u\rangle.\end{split}

For the first term, since

,

\langle-\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B,u\rangle=\langle B,\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla u\rangle.

For the second term, we look at the

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(-B_{k}\partial_{k}(\tilde{H}_{*})_{i}\big)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}B_{k}\big(-\partial_{k}(\tilde{H}_{*})_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}\big)\,dx.

The term in the parenthesis is the

leads to the formula of
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}
. This completes the proof.

whenever

\|H_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}+\|\nabla H_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq\delta_{\rm bd}.

For the whole-space operator, we write

\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}:=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&0\\
0&\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbb{S}^{(1)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\\
\mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
are positive self-adjoint,
\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is sectorial on
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, standard interpolation gives, for every
\eta>0
and every
U
in
D(\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})
,

\|\mathbb{S}^{(1)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big(\eta\|\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C_{\eta}\|U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big).

Hence there exists
\delta_{\rm ws}>0
such that
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\mathbb{S}^{(1)}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
and likewise
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*}
are sectorial whenever
\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\delta_{\rm ws}
, by the perturbation theorem for sectorial operators; see
[
18
, Theorem 1.2.14]
. Finally, let
\delta_{\rm coe}>0
be a small constant ensuring the coercivity in the proof of Lemma
4.1
. We therefore fix once and for all

\delta_{*}:=\min\{\delta_{\rm bd},\,C_{\rm ext}^{-1}\delta_{\rm ws},\,C_{\rm ext}^{-1}\delta_{\rm coe}\}

and assume that

\|H_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(D)}\leq\delta_{*}.

This assumption will be used throughout whenever the smallness of
H_{*}
is required.

3
Global approximation of resolvent solutions

In this section, we present the quantitative Runge approximation for the resolvent problem associated with the linearized MHD operator
\mathbb{S}
. This analysis serves as the building block for approximating the time-evolving part
V_{{\rm evol}}
introduced in (
1.10
) in the frequency domain.

For
\delta\in(0,\pi)
, we set

\Sigma_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\operatorname{arg}z|<\delta\}.

Throughout this section, let

\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi-\delta}\cap\{|\lambda|\geq\rho_{0}\},

(3.1)

with
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
\rho_{0}>0
. We then consider
V=(v,H)
solving

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda v-\Delta v-H_{*}\cdot\nabla H-H\cdot\nabla H_{*}+\nabla q=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\lambda H+\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}H)+\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times v)=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\operatorname{div}v=\operatorname{div}H=0&\mbox{in}\ D.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

We start with the interpolation estimate for the vorticity.

Lemma 3.1

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and
Y_{0}\Subset Y
be a bounded Lipschitz subdomain. Then there exists
C>0
such that, for every
\Psi\in H^{2}(Y)^{3}
,

\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(Y_{0})}\leq C\|\mathbb{P}_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{1/2}\|\Psi\|_{H^{2}(Y)}^{1/2}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Set
b=\mathbb{P}_{Y}\Psi
. By the Helmholtz decomposition in
Y
, there exists
\chi\in H^{1}(Y)
such that
\Psi=b+\nabla\chi
. Thus
\operatorname{rot}\Psi=\operatorname{rot}b
in
Y
in the sense of distributions.

Let
\zeta\in H^{1}_{0}(Y)^{3}
. Then we have

\begin{split}\Big|\langle\operatorname{rot}\Psi,\zeta\rangle_{H^{-1}(Y),H^{1}_{0}(Y)}\Big|&=\bigg|\int_{Y}b\cdot\operatorname{rot}\zeta\,dx\bigg|\\
&\leq C\|b\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(Y)}.\end{split}

Hence

\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{H^{-1}(Y)}\leq C\|\mathbb{P}_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}
.
If
\zeta\in H^{1}_{0}(Y_{0})^{3}
, its zero
extension
\widetilde{\zeta}
to
Y
satisfies
\widetilde{\zeta}\in H^{1}_{0}(Y)^{3}
and

\|\widetilde{\zeta}\|_{H^{1}(Y)}=\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(Y_{0})}
. This implies that

\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{H^{-1}(Y_{0})}\leq C\|\mathbb{P}_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

(3.4)

On the other hand, since
\Psi\in H^{2}(Y)^{3}
,

\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{H^{1}(Y_{0})}\leq C\|\Psi\|_{H^{2}(Y)}.

(3.5)

The standard interpolation inequality on
Y_{0}
gives

\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(Y_{0})}\leq C\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{H^{-1}(Y_{0})}^{1/2}\|\operatorname{rot}\Psi\|_{H^{1}(Y_{0})}^{1/2}.

(3.6)

The desired estimate (
3.3
) follows from (
3.4
)–(
3.6
).
∎

Next we state the stability estimate for the system (
3.7
) below.

Lemma 3.2

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary,
G^{\prime}
be a subdomain of
G
satisfying
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG^{\prime}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset G
. Let
(\varphi,\omega)\in H^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(G)^{6}\cap H^{1}(G)^{6}
satisfy, for some
s\in H^{1}(G)
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda\varphi-\Delta\varphi+\omega\times\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla s=0&\mbox{in}\ G,\\
\lambda\omega-\Delta\omega+\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla\varphi-(\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})^{\top}\varphi)=0&\mbox{in}\ G,\\
\operatorname{div}\varphi=\operatorname{div}\omega=0&\mbox{in}\ G.\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

Suppose that, for
0<\eta<\mathcal{E}
, we have
\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{H^{1}(G)}\leq\mathcal{E}
and
\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(G^{\prime})}\leq\eta
. Then there exist
C,\mu>0
independent of
\lambda,\eta,\mathcal{E}
such that

\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\exp(C\langle\lambda\rangle)\,\mathcal{E}\bigg(\log\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\eta}\bigg)^{-\mu}.

(3.8)

In particular, if
(\varphi,\omega)=0
in a nonempty open subset of
G
, then
(\varphi,\omega)=0
in
G
.

Proof.

Set

(\Phi(x,t),\Omega(x,t))=\exp(t\lambda)(\varphi(x),\omega(x)),\qquad S(x,t)=\exp(t\lambda)s(x)

on
G\times(-1,1)
. Then we see that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\Phi-\Delta\Phi+\Omega\times\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla S=0&\mbox{in}\ G\times(-1,1),\\
\partial_{t}\Omega-\Delta\Omega+\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla\Phi-(\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})^{\top}\Phi)=0&\mbox{in}\ G\times(-1,1),\\
\operatorname{div}\Phi=\operatorname{div}\Omega=0&\mbox{in}\ G\times(-1,1).\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

We first derive a
3
-cylinder estimate for (
3.9
). Set
Q=\operatorname{rot}\Phi
. Since
\operatorname{div}\Phi=0
,

\Delta\Phi+\operatorname{rot}Q=0.

(3.10)

Let
\mathcal{P}(a)
denote the skew-symmetric matrix satisfying

\operatorname{Div}\mathcal{P}(a)=\operatorname{rot}a
for a vector field
a
. Then, taking the rotation of the first equation of (
3.9
) gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}Q-\Delta Q+\operatorname{Div}F=0&\mbox{in}\ G\times(-1,1),\\
\partial_{t}\Omega-\Delta\Omega+\operatorname{Div}G=0&\mbox{in}\ G\times(-1,1),\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

where

F:=\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\tilde{H}_{*}),\qquad G:=\mathcal{P}\big(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla\Phi-(\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})^{\top}\Phi\big).

Since
\tilde{H}_{*}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, we have

|F|+|G|\leq C(|\Omega|+|\nabla\Phi|+|\Phi|).

The system (
3.10
)–(
3.11
) has the same diagonal principal part as the generalized non-stationary Stokes system in
[
17
]
, while the remaining terms are bounded first- and zero-order perturbations allowed in the argument of
[
17
]
. Hence, applying the parabolic and elliptic Carleman estimates yields the following three-cylinder inequality: for
0<t_{0}<T<1
and concentric balls
B_{R_{1}}\subset B_{R_{2}}\subset B_{R_{3}}\subset G
under radius conditions,

\begin{split}&\int_{-T+t_{0}}^{T-t_{0}}\int_{B_{R_{2}}}\big(|\Phi|^{2}+|\Omega|^{2}\big)\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\big(|\Phi|^{2}+|\Omega|^{2}\big)\,dx\,dt\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{3}}}\big(|\Phi|^{2}+|\Omega|^{2}\big)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1-\theta},\end{split}

(3.12)

where
\theta\in(0,1)
depends on
G,t_{0},T,R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
and
\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
.

Returning to the time-independent field
(\varphi,\omega)
by using

\int_{-a}^{a}\int_{B_{R}}(|\Phi|^{2}+|\Omega|^{2})\,dx\,dt=\bigg(\int_{-a}^{a}\exp(2t\Re\lambda)\,dt\bigg)\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}

for
a\in(0,1)
and ball
B_{R}\subset G
, gives the
3
-sphere estimate

\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{2}})}\leq C\exp(C\langle\lambda\rangle)\,\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{1}})}^{\theta}\|(\varphi,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{3}})}^{1-\theta},

for concentric balls
B_{R_{1}}\subset B_{R_{2}}\subset B_{R_{3}}\subset G
. The standard propagation-of-smallness argument of
[
1
]
then yields (
3.8
). The qualitative unique continuation follows by applying the estimate with
\eta\downarrow 0
along a chain of balls from the open set where
(\varphi,\omega)
vanishes.
∎

We now proceed with the approximation of
V=(v,H)
by a global solution under forcing supported in
Y
in (
1.9
). Let
\mathcal{X}_{\rm restr}
denote the subspace of
L^{2}(D)^{6}

\mathcal{X}_{\rm restr}=\overline{\{a|_{D}\mid a\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}}^{L^{2}(D)^{3}}\times\overline{\{a|_{D}\mid a\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}}^{L^{2}(D)^{3}}.

(3.13)

Then we define
\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
as the space of target functions by

\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}=\Big\{V\in\mathcal{X}_{\rm restr}\cap H^{2}(D)^{6}~\big|~\mbox{
$V=(v,H)$ solves \eqref{eq.MHD.resol.loc} with $q\in H^{1}(D)$
}\Big\}.

(3.14)

Note that the normal trace of
(v,H)\in\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
satisfies

\langle\gamma_{n}v,1\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=\langle\gamma_{n}H,1\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=0

(3.15)

for every connected component
\Gamma_{\ell}
of
\partial D
.

We also define the linear operator

T:L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)\to L^{2}(D)^{6}

as follows: for
F=(f,g)\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
,

TF=\big((\lambda+{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}J_{Y}F\big)\big|_{D},\qquad J_{Y}F:=(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathtt{e}_{Y}f,\mathtt{e}_{Y}g).

(3.16)

Moreover,

\mathcal{X}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\operatorname{Ran}(T)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{L^{2}(D)^{6}}.

(3.17)

Let us denote by
\mathcal{K}
the kernel of
T
and by
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
the orthogonal space of
\mathcal{K}
in
L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
. Finally, let us denote by
A
the restriction of
T
to
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 3.3

The following hold.

(1)

For
V=(v,H)\in\mathcal{X}
, set

(\varphi,\Psi)=(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*})^{-1}\Pi_{D}V,\qquad\Pi_{D}V:=(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathtt{e}_{D}v,\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathtt{e}_{D}H).

(3.18)

Then the adjoint
A^{*}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
of
A
is given by

A^{*}V=\big(\varphi|_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\big).

(2)

The operator
A^{*}A
is positive, compact, and self-adjoint on
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
.

(3)

The range of
A
is dense in
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
is contained in
\mathcal{X}
.

(4)

There exist orthonormal bases

\{F_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{K}^{\perp},\qquad\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{X}

and positive constants
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots>0
and
\alpha_{j}\to 0
such that

AF_{j}=\alpha_{j}V_{j},\qquad A^{*}V_{j}=\alpha_{j}F_{j}.

Proof.

We first prove (
1
). Let
V=(v,H)\in\mathcal{X}
and set

W=(\varphi,\Psi)=\big((\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{*})^{-1}\big)\Pi_{D}V.

For every
F=(f,g)
in
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
, putting

U=(\lambda+{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}J_{Y}F,

we compute

\langle AF,V\rangle_{D}=\langle U|_{D},V\rangle_{D}=\langle U,\mathtt{e}_{D}V\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\langle U,\Pi_{D}V\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\langle J_{Y}F,W\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

By the definition of
J_{Y}
, we see from
g\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
that

\begin{split}\langle J_{Y}F,W\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&=\langle\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathtt{e}_{Y}f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\langle\mathtt{e}_{Y}g,\Psi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\\
&=\langle f,\varphi|_{Y}\rangle_{Y}+\langle g,\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\rangle_{Y}\\
&=\big\langle F,\big(\varphi|_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\big)\big\rangle_{Y}.\end{split}

Moreover, if
G\in\mathcal{K}
, then
TG=0
, and the same computation gives

\big\langle G,\big(\varphi|_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\big)\big\rangle_{Y}=\langle TG,V\rangle_{D}=0.

Thus

\big(\varphi|_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\big)

belongs to
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
, and the formula for
A^{*}V
follows.

For (
2
), the resolvent estimate gives

\|TF\|_{H^{2}(D)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

for
\lambda
in the fixed sector with
|\lambda|\geq\rho_{0}
with
C
depending on
\rho_{0}
. By the Rellich theorem,
A
is compact from
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
into
\mathcal{X}\subset L^{2}(D)^{6}
. Consequently
A^{*}A
is positive, compact, and self-adjoint on
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
.

We now prove (
3
). The density is due to the definition (
3.17
). Next we show that
\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}\subset\mathcal{X}
. For
V=(v,H)\in L^{2}(D)^{6}
orthogonal to
\mathcal{X}
, let
(\varphi,\Psi)
be defined by (
3.18
). Since the computation used in the proof of (
1
) is valid for every

F\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
,

0=\langle TF,V\rangle_{D}=\big\langle F,\big(\varphi|_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})\big)\big\rangle_{Y}.

Consequently,

\varphi|_{Y}=0,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\Psi|_{Y})=0.

Set

\omega=\operatorname{rot}\Psi.

Then the second equality implies that
\Psi|_{Y}
is locally of gradient form, and hence
\omega|_{Y}=0
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{M}
be the connected components of
B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, and choose nonempty subdomains
Y_{k}\Subset Y\cap\mathcal{O}_{k}
. Since the forcing
\mathtt{e}_{D}V
vanishes in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, the pair
(\varphi,\omega)
satisfies (
3.7
). Lemma
3.2
therefore gives
(\varphi,\omega)=0
in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
for every
k
, and thus

\varphi|_{B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}=0,\qquad\omega|_{B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}=0.

We claim that every
U\in\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
is orthogonal to the subspace
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
. We may show that
\langle U,V\rangle_{D}=0
holds for every
V\in\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
, and therefore conclude that
U\in(\mathcal{X}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathcal{X}
. Let
U=(u,B)
be associated with a pressure
p\in H^{1}(D)
, that is,
U=(u,B)
satisfies

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda u-\Delta u-H_{*}\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla H_{*}+\nabla p=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\lambda B+\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)+\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times u)=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}B=0&\mbox{in}\ D.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.19)

On the other hand, there exist
s_{1},s_{2}\in H^{1}(B^{\prime})
such that

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\varphi-\Delta\varphi+(\operatorname{rot}\Psi)\times\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla s_{1}=\mathtt{e}_{D}v&\mbox{in}\ B^{\prime},\\
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Psi+\operatorname{rot}\omega+\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla\varphi-(\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})^{\top}\varphi+\nabla s_{2}=\mathtt{e}_{D}H&\mbox{in}\ B^{\prime},\\
\operatorname{div}\varphi=\operatorname{div}\Psi=0&\mbox{in}\ B^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.20)

Blockwise application of the Green formula to (
3.19
)–(
3.20
) gives

\langle U,V\rangle_{D}=\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Stokes}}(u,p,\varphi)+\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Mag}}(B,\Psi)+\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Coup}}(U,W)+\mathcal{B}_{{\rm pr},2}(B,s_{2}),

(3.21)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Stokes}}(u,p,\varphi)&:=\int_{\partial D}\Big((\partial_{n}u-pn)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(u\cdot n)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-u\cdot\partial_{n}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,d\sigma,\\
\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Mag}}(B,\Psi)&:=\int_{\partial D}\Big((B\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\omega\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(\operatorname{rot}B\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,d\sigma,\\
\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Coup}}(U,W)&:=-\int_{\partial D}(u\cdot n)(H_{*}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\,d\sigma,\\
\mathcal{B}_{{\rm pr},2}(B,s_{2})&:=\int_{\partial D}(B\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma.\end{split}

(3.22)

The detailed derivation of (
3.21
) is given in Appendix
A
.

Because
(\varphi,\omega)=0
in
B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and
(\varphi,\Psi)
is in
H^{2}(B^{\prime})
, the trace of
(\varphi,\omega)
and the first-order derivatives of
\varphi
vanish on
\partial D
when approached from the interior. Moreover, since
H_{*}\cdot n=0
on
\partial D
, the vector identity
(a\times b)\times c=(a\cdot c)b-(b\cdot c)a
gives

\begin{split}\operatorname{rot}B\times n-(u\cdot n)H_{*}=(\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\times n\end{split}

on
\partial D
. Thus the equality (
3.21
) is reduced to

\begin{split}\langle U,V\rangle_{D}&=\int_{\partial D}(u\cdot n)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma\\
&\quad+\int_{\partial D}((\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial D}(B\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma.\end{split}

(3.23)

Let us show that the right-hand side is zero. In
B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, we see from (
3.20
) that

\nabla s_{1}=0,\qquad\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Psi+\nabla s_{2}=0.

(3.24)

Thus
s_{1}
is constant on each connected component of
B^{\prime}\setminus\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, and the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.23
) vanishes by the zero-flux condition
\langle\gamma_{n}u,1\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=0
on every component
\Gamma_{\ell}
of
\partial D
due to
(u,B)\in\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
. Moreover, by the Green formula and
\operatorname{div}B=0
,

\int_{\partial D}(B\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma=\int_{D}B\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx.

Then, by the second equation of (
3.19
) and the rot Green formula,

\begin{split}\int_{D}B\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx&=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\partial D}((\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\times n)\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma\\
&=-\int_{\partial D}((\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma.\end{split}

Here we note that the boundary pairing only involves the tangential trace of
\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
; using
\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=-\lambda\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
on the exterior of
\partial D
gives the last equality. Hence we have

\int_{\partial D}((\operatorname{rot}B+H_{*}\times u)\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial D}(B\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma=0.

Hence
\langle U,V\rangle_{D}=0
for any

, and therefore
U\in(\mathcal{X}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathcal{X}
.

Finally, (
4
) follows from the spectral theorem applied to the compact positive self-adjoint operator
A^{*}A
on
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

Proof.

Proposition 3.6

There exists
C>0
depending on
\rho_{0}
in (

In this section, we present the quantitative Runge approximation for the part

in (

We then observe that the pair

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta v-H_{*}\cdot\nabla H-H\cdot\nabla H_{*}+\nabla q=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}H)+\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times v)=0&\mbox{in}\ D,\\
\operatorname{div}v=\operatorname{div}H=0&\mbox{in}\ D.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

Indeed, by the equality for vector fields
A,B

\nabla(A\cdot B)=A\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla A+A\times(\operatorname{rot}B)+B\times(\operatorname{rot}A),

and by
H_{*},H\in X_{{\rm har}}(D)

V
and
q
satisfies (
4.1
).

Recall from (
1.9
) that
Y
is the control set. We start with the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u-\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla p=\mathtt{e}_{Y}i&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)+\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\times u)=\mathtt{e}_{Y}j&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}B=0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Lemma 4.1

If
\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, for every
F=(i,j)\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
, there exists a unique weak solution
U=U_{F}=(u_{F},B_{F})\in\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{6}
of (
4.2
) with an associated pressure
p\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Remark 4.2

The same argument below applies, without any change, when the right-hand side
(\mathtt{e}_{Y}i,\mathtt{e}_{Y}j)
is replaced by an arbitrary pair in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with compact support. In addition, the argument applies to the adjoint form as well.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
:

For
U=(u,B),W=(\varphi,\Psi)
in
\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{6}
, we define

\begin{split}a(U,W)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname{rot}B\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*})\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tilde{H}_{*}\times u)\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx.\end{split}

We also define the linear form

\ell_{F}(W)=\int_{Y}i\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{Y}j\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx.

Then the weak formulation of (
4.2
) is simplified to

a(U,W)=\ell_{F}(W).

Since
Y
is a bounded domain and
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

|\ell_{F}(W)|\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Moreover,

|a(U,W)|\leq C\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Since
B\in\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the whole-space identity

\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\|\operatorname{rot}B\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

holds. Hence, for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,

\begin{split}\Re a(U,U)&\geq C_{1}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}-C_{2}\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, if
\|\tilde{H}_{*}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is sufficiently small, then

\Re a(U,U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Hence the Lax-Milgram theorem yields a unique
U=(u,B)
in
\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{6}
satisfying

\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Recovering the pressure
p
is routine work. The proof is complete.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We first approximate the time-evolving part by the Dunford integral representation together with the resolvent approximation in Section
3
, and then we treat the stationary topological part by the result of Section
4
. We conclude by combining the two constructions.

Let
V(t)
be a solution to the linearized MHD system (
MHD
) written as

V(t)=V_{{\rm evol}}(t)+V_{{\rm topo}},\qquad V_{{\rm evol}}(t)=e^{-t{\mathbb{S}}}V_{0},\qquad V_{{\rm topo}}=(0,H_{*}^{\prime}),

(5.1)

for
V_{0}\in D({\mathbb{S}})
and
H_{*}^{\prime}\in X_{{\rm har}}(D)
.

Recall that
\tilde{H}_{*}
denotes the extended background field in Lemma
2.1
.

Approximation of the time-evolving part.

We start by approximating the time-evolving part
V_{{\rm evol}}
in (
5.1
). First we fix a solenoidal whole-space extension of the initial data. Since
V_{0}=(v_{0},H_{0})\in D(\mathbb{S})
, both
v_{0}
and
H_{0}
are divergence-free
H^{2}
-fields on
D
with zero normal trace on
\partial D
. By the divergence-free extension theorem of Kato-Mitrea-Ponce-Taylor
[
14
, Corollary 3.2]
, applied with
s=2
and
p=2
, there exists
\tilde{V}_{0}=(\tilde{v}_{0},\tilde{H}_{0})\in H^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\times H^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
with compact support in a fixed ball containing
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
such that

\tilde{V}_{0}|_{D}=V_{0},\qquad\|\tilde{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)},\qquad\|\tilde{V}_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|V_{0}\|_{H^{2}(D)}.

(5.2)

By elliptic regularity for the Stokes block and
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
for the magnetic block, the graph norm of
\mathbb{S}
is equivalent on
D(\mathbb{S})
to the
H^{2}(D)
-norm. Since moreover
0\in\rho(-\mathbb{S})
,

\|V_{0}\|_{H^{2}(D)}\leq C\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

(5.3)

Instead of approximating
V_{{\rm evol}}
directly, we introduce the reference solution

U_{{\rm ref}}(t):=e^{-t\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\tilde{V}_{0},

where
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is the global MHD operator in Section
2
, and consider the difference

E(t):=V_{{\rm evol}}(t)-U_{{\rm ref}}(t).

Fix
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
arbitrarily. Using the Dunford integral, we represent
E(t)
as

E(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma}\exp(\lambda t)V(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad V(\lambda):=(\lambda+\mathbb{S})^{-1}V_{0}-\big((\lambda+\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}\tilde{V}_{0}\big)|_{D}.

Here
\gamma
is chosen as follows. By the sectoriality of both
\mathbb{S}
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
, there exists
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
such that

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma_{\pi-\delta}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{0\}\subset\rho(-\mathbb{S})\cap\rho(-\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})

holds; see Remark
2.3
and
[
18
, Proposition 1.2.13]
. We then let
\gamma
denote the oriented (counterclockwise) contour

\gamma=\Big\{|\operatorname{arg}z|=\pi-\delta,\mkern 9.0mu|z|\geq\rho_{0}\Big\}\cup\Big\{|\operatorname{arg}z|\leq\pi-\delta,\mkern 9.0mu|z|=\rho_{0}\Big\}.

We split the integral into high- and low-frequency parts with a parameter
L>\rho_{0}
:

\begin{split}E(t)&:=E_{{\rm high}}(t)+E_{{\rm low}}(t)\\
&:=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma\cap\{|\lambda|>L\}}\exp(\lambda t)V(\lambda)\,d\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma\cap\{|\lambda|\leq L\}}\exp(\lambda t)V(\lambda)\,d\lambda.\end{split}

For the high-frequency part
E_{{\rm high}}
, we apply the argument in
[
11
]
. We write

V(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big((\lambda+\mathbb{S})^{-1}\mathbb{S}V_{0}-\big((\lambda+\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{V}_{0}\big)|_{D}\Big).

This, together with the standard sectorial resolvent estimate
\|(\lambda+\mathbb{S})^{-1}\|\leq C|\lambda|^{-1}
and its analogue for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
(see
[
18
, Proposition 1.2.13]
and
[
25
, Chapter 2]
) yields

\begin{split}\|V(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|^{2}}\big(\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}+\|\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big)\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|^{2}}\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)},\end{split}

where we have used (
5.2
)–(
5.3
). Consequently,
E_{{\rm high}}
is estimated as

\begin{split}\|E_{{\rm high}}(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}&\leq C\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\int_{\gamma\,\cap\{|\lambda|>L\}}\frac{\exp(t\Re\lambda)}{|\lambda|^{2}}\,d|\lambda|\\
&\leq\frac{C\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}}{L}\exp(-tL\cos\delta),\quad t\geq 0.\end{split}

(5.4)

For the low-frequency part
E_{{\rm low}}
, we set

\gamma_{L}=\gamma\cap\{|\lambda|\leq L\}.

For every
\lambda\in\gamma_{L}
, using
J_{Y}
in (
3.16
), we define the operator

T_{\lambda}F=\big((\lambda+{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}J_{Y}F\big)\big|_{D},\quad F\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y).

The map
\lambda\mapsto T_{\lambda}
is analytic on
\gamma_{L}
as a bounded operator from
L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
to
L^{2}(D)^{6}
. Since
\tilde{V}_{0}|_{D}=V_{0}
and
\tilde{H}_{*}=H_{*}
on
D
, the field
V(\lambda)
satisfies the homogeneous local resolvent system (
3.2
) in
D
. Moreover, it holds that
V(\lambda)\in\mathcal{X}^{\rm tgt}
by a similar argument when extending
V_{0}
to
\tilde{V}_{0}
. In addition, the resolvent estimates and (
5.2
)–(
5.3
) imply

\displaystyle\|V(\lambda)\|_{H^{2}(D)}+\|q(\lambda)\|_{H^{1}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq C\langle\lambda\rangle\big(\|V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}+\|\tilde{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\langle\lambda\rangle\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)},\quad\lambda\in\gamma_{L},

with the pressure normalized by
\int_{D}q(\lambda)\,dx=0
. Therefore Proposition
3.6
yields that, for every
\eta>0
and every
\lambda\in\gamma_{L}
, there exists
F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}
in
L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
such that

\|V(\lambda)-T_{\lambda}F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C\eta\langle\lambda\rangle\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)},

satisfying, using resolvent estimates and (
5.2
),

\|F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq M\|V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)},\qquad M:=\exp\bigg(C\Big(\frac{\exp(C\langle L\rangle)}{\eta}\Big)^{4/\mu}\bigg).

We now replace the noncanonical choice
F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}
by a canonical one that varies continuously with
\lambda
. For each
\lambda
in
\gamma_{L}
and each
\tau>0
, we consider the Tikhonov functional

J_{\lambda,\tau}(F):=\|V(\lambda)-T_{\lambda}F\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\tau\|F\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2},\quad F\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y).

This functional is strictly convex and coercive, hence it has a unique minimizer. Since its Euler-Lagrange equation reads, letting
T_{\lambda}^{*}
denote the adjoint of
T_{\lambda}
,

(T_{\lambda}^{*}T_{\lambda}+\tau I)\mathsf{F}_{\tau}(\lambda)=T_{\lambda}^{*}V(\lambda),

the minimizer is equivalently given by

\mathsf{F}_{\tau}(\lambda)=T_{\lambda}^{*}(T_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}+\tau I)^{-1}V(\lambda).

Choosing
\tau=\eta^{2}L^{2}/M^{2}
, we see that

\begin{split}J_{\lambda,\tau}(F_{\lambda}^{\sharp})&\leq\|V(\lambda)-T_{\lambda}F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\tau\|F_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\\
&\leq C\eta^{2}L^{2}\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\tau M^{2}\|V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\\
&\leq C\eta^{2}L^{2}\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}.\end{split}

From now on we set

\mathsf{F}(\lambda):=\mathsf{F}_{\tau}(\lambda),\qquad U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda):=(\lambda+\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}J_{Y}\mathsf{F}(\lambda).

By minimality,

J_{\lambda,\tau}(\mathsf{F}(\lambda))\leq J_{\lambda,\tau}(F_{\lambda}^{\sharp})
. Thus we have

\|V(\lambda)-U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C\eta L\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}

and, using the definition of
\tau
,

\|\mathsf{F}(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\tau^{-1/2}\eta L\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq CM\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

The point of this replacement is that the minimizer is now canonical. Since
\lambda\mapsto T_{\lambda}
and
\lambda\mapsto V(\lambda)
are analytic on
\gamma_{L}
, the map
\lambda\mapsto T_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}+\tau I
is continuous and takes values in boundedly invertible operators. By the explicit formula for the minimizer and continuity of inversion on the open set of boundedly invertible operators, it follows that

\lambda\mapsto\mathsf{F}(\lambda),\qquad\lambda\mapsto U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda)

are continuous on
\gamma_{L}
. In particular, the Bochner integrals below are well defined.

Using
U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda)
, we define the correction term in the time domain as

U_{{\rm corr}}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(\lambda t)U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Then the error is bounded by

\begin{split}\|E_{{\rm low}}(t)-U_{{\rm corr}}(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}&\leq\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(t\Re\lambda)\|V(\lambda)-U^{\prime}_{{\rm corr}}(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\,d|\lambda|\\
&\leq C\eta L\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(t\Re\lambda)\,d|\lambda|\\
&\leq C\eta L^{2}\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\exp(\rho_{0}t),\quad t\geq 0.\end{split}

(5.5)

Set

U_{{\rm evol}}(t)=U_{{\rm ref}}(t)+U_{{\rm corr}}(t).

Then
U_{{\rm evol}}=(u,B)
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u-\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla p=\mathtt{e}_{Y}f&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}B+\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)+\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\times u)=\mathtt{e}_{Y}g&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}B=0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty).\end{array}\right.

(5.6)

Here the forcing
(f,g)
is defined by

(f,g)(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(\lambda t)\mathsf{F}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Since
\gamma_{L}
is compact and

\lambda\mapsto\mathsf{F}(\lambda)

is continuous, differentiation under the integral sign shows that
(f,g)
belongs to
C^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y))
. It is estimated as

\begin{split}\|(f,g)(t)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}&\leq\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(t\Re\lambda)\|\mathsf{F}(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\,d|\lambda|\\
&\leq CM\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\int_{\gamma_{L}}\exp(t\Re\lambda)\,d|\lambda|\\
&\leq\exp\bigg(C\Big(\frac{\exp(C\langle L\rangle)}{\eta}\Big)^{4/\mu}\bigg)\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\exp(\rho_{0}t),\quad t\geq 0.\end{split}

(5.7)

Approximation of the topological part.

We next approximate the topological part
V_{{\rm topo}}
in (
5.1
). We apply Proposition
4.6
: for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
(i,j)\in L^{2}(Y)^{3}\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(Y)
such that the solution
U_{{\rm topo}}=(u,B)
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u-\tilde{H}_{*}\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla\tilde{H}_{*}+\nabla p=\mathtt{e}_{Y}i&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)+\operatorname{rot}(\tilde{H}_{*}\times u)=\mathtt{e}_{Y}j&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}B=0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(5.8)

approximates
V_{{\rm topo}}
in
D
as

\|V_{{\rm topo}}-U_{{\rm topo}}\|_{L^{2}(D)}<\varepsilon\|H_{*}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

(5.9)

Moreover,

\|(i,j)\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq\exp\bigg(C\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{4/\mu}\bigg)\|H_{*}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

(5.10)

Conclusion.

Now we define the total global approximation
U=U(t)
by

U(t)=U_{{\rm evol}}(t)+U_{{\rm topo}}.

Then
U
solves (
1.11
) due to (
5.6
) and (
5.8
). Combining the high and low frequency estimates (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), respectively, with (
5.9
), and then using (
5.2
)–(
5.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\|V(t)-U(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{L}\exp(-tL\cos\delta)+\eta L^{2}\exp(\rho_{0}t)\bigg)\|\mathbb{S}V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}+C\varepsilon\|H_{*}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(D)}.\end{split}

Choosing
L=\varepsilon^{-1}
and
\eta=\varepsilon^{3}
, we arrive at (
1.12
). The estimate of the forcing
(f,g)
is verified by (
5.7
) and that of
(i,j)
by (
5.10
). The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.

\Box

Appendix A
Proof of (
3.21
)

In this appendix, we derive the equality (
3.21
).

Proof of (
3.21
):

Since
\tilde{H}_{*}=H_{*}
in
D
and
\mathtt{e}_{D}V=V
in
D
, the conjugate of (
3.20
) gives

\begin{split}\langle U,V\rangle_{D}&=\int_{D}u\cdot\Big(\lambda\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-\Delta\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\times H_{*}+\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{D}B\cdot\Big(\lambda\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+H_{*}\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-(\nabla H_{*})^{\top}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx.\end{split}

On the other hand, testing (
3.19
) by
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
yields

\begin{split}0&=\int_{D}\Big(\lambda u-\Delta u-H_{*}\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla H_{*}+\nabla p\Big)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{D}\Big(\lambda B+\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)+\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times u)\Big)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx.\end{split}

Subtracting this identity from the previous one, the
\lambda
-terms cancel and we obtain

\langle U,V\rangle_{D}=I_{{\rm Stokes}}+I_{{\rm Mag}}+I_{{\rm Coup}}+I_{{\rm pr},2},

where

\displaystyle I_{{\rm Stokes}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{D}\Big(u\cdot(-\Delta\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-(-\Delta u+\nabla p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{{\rm Mag}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{D}\Big(B\cdot\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{{\rm Coup}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{D}B\cdot\Big(H_{*}\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-(\nabla H_{*})^{\top}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{D}(H_{*}\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla H_{*})\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{D}u\cdot\big((\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\times H_{*}\big)\,dx-\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times u)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{{\rm pr},2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{D}B\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx.

We use the following Green formulas, with
n
denoting the outward unit normal to
D
:

\displaystyle\int_{D}a\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{D}(\operatorname{div}a)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{\partial D}(a\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\int_{D}a\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}a\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{\partial D}(a\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}a\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}a\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx+\int_{\partial D}(n\times a)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma.

Since
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}\varphi=0
in
D
, two integrations by parts for the Laplacian terms and one integration by parts for the pressure terms give

\begin{split}I_{{\rm Stokes}}&=\int_{\partial D}\Big((-pn)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(u\cdot n)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-u\cdot\partial_{n}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\partial_{n}u\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,d\sigma\\
&=\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Stokes}}(u,p,\varphi).\end{split}

Similarly, the rot Green formulas give

\begin{split}I_{{\rm Mag}}&=\int_{D}B\cdot\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\,dx-\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}(\operatorname{rot}B)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx\\
&=\int_{\partial D}\Big((B\times n)\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(\operatorname{rot}B\times n)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,d\sigma\\
&=\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Mag}}(B,\Psi).\end{split}

The coupling part is simplified as follows. By the convention for
(\nabla H_{*})^{\top}
, we have

B\cdot\big((\nabla H_{*})^{\top}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\big)=(B\cdot\nabla H_{*})\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.

Thus the corresponding two terms cancel. The remaining
\varphi
-coupling is

\begin{split}&\int_{D}\Big(B\cdot(H_{*}\cdot\nabla\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+(H_{*}\cdot\nabla B)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big)\,dx\\
&=\int_{D}\operatorname{div}\big((B\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)H_{*}\big)\,dx=\int_{\partial D}(H_{*}\cdot n)(B\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\varphi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\,d\sigma=0,\end{split}

where we have used
\operatorname{div}H_{*}=0
in
D
and
H_{*}\cdot n=0
on
\partial D
. For the remaining
\Psi
-coupling, the scalar triple product identity gives

u\cdot\big((\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\times H_{*}\big)=(H_{*}\times u)\cdot\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.

Therefore, by the rot Green formula,

\begin{split}&\int_{D}u\cdot\big((\operatorname{rot}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\times H_{*}\big)\,dx-\int_{D}\operatorname{rot}(H_{*}\times u)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,dx\\
&=\int_{\partial D}\big((H_{*}\times u)\times n\big)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma.\end{split}

Since
H_{*}\cdot n=0
on
\partial D
, the identity

(a\times b)\times c=(a\cdot c)b-(b\cdot c)a
yields

(H_{*}\times u)\times n=(H_{*}\cdot n)u-(u\cdot n)H_{*}=-(u\cdot n)H_{*}

on
\partial D
. Consequently,

I_{{\rm Coup}}=-\int_{\partial D}(u\cdot n)(H_{*}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\,d\sigma=\mathcal{B}_{{\rm Coup}}(U,W).

Finally, since
\operatorname{div}B=0
in
D
, the gradient Green formula gives

I_{{\rm pr},2}=\int_{\partial D}(B\cdot n)\,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mus_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\,d\sigma=\mathcal{B}_{{\rm pr},2}(B,s_{2}).

Combining the four identities for
I_{{\rm Stokes}}
,
I_{{\rm Mag}}
,
I_{{\rm Coup}}
, and
I_{{\rm pr},2}
proves (
3.21
).
