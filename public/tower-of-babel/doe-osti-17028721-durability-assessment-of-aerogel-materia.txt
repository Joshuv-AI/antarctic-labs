 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 The NLR core window technologies research program supports multiple capabilities for
 assessing the durability of advanced window materials. The CU-Boulder team has developed a
 new transparent aerogel material under an ARPA-e SHIELD program award. NLR will work
 with CU-Boulder to assess the durability of their material based on relevant window industry
 testing methods.

 Purpose:

 NREL will conduct durability testing of emerging fenestration technology prototypes produced
 by CUBoulder. This will help the CU-Boulder team gain insight into the durability of their
 technology as well as possible failure modes to assist them in improving performance.

 Task 1: Durability Assessment of Prototype Devices

 NREL will perform a durability assessment of prototype samples generated under the proposed
 CU-Boulder project entitled “Advancing Scalable Insulation Retrofits from Flexible Inexpensive
 Lucid Materials for Single-Pane Windows.” CU-Boulder will provide at least five prototype
 samples of their highly insulating window film product. NREL will perform initial
 characterization of optical properties and then place samples into an exposure chamber being
 maintained at conditions consistent with the ASTM E-2141 durability standard for
 electrochromic windows. NREL will remove samples and perform identical optical
 characterization to the initial measurements after 2000, 4000, and 5000 hours of simulated
 exposure. NREL will supply all data and a final written report to CU-Boulder.

 Varied transparent aerogel materials were integrated into insulating glass units by the team at
 CU-Boulder and provided to NREL for evaluation. Optical characterization was conducted with
 simple visual observation. This is consistent with industry practices and based upon evaluation of
 non-uniformity and optical distortions sensitive to the human eye under the ASTM E2189
 method. Samples had been characterized for thermal performance previously by the team at CU-
 Boulder using in-house developed specialized techniques.

 NREL placed the samples into an Atlas XR-260 weathering chamber held at a temperature of

 sources in accordance with exposure conditions used under the ASTM E2141 method. Samples
 were removed from the XR-260 at 2000-hour exposure intervals and underwent visual inspection
 and were transferred back to the CU-Boulder team for thermal characterization using their
 methods. Samples were then returned to NREL and replaced in the chamber until a total of 5000
 hours of exposure was achieved. Samples were then inspected visually and returned to CU-
 Boulder for final characterization. NREL did not observe any changes to the samples throughout
 the exposure process.
