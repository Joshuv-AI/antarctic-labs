The multidimensional signaling of ELANE: from congenital hematopoietic failure to immune microenvironment crosstalk and targeted interventions
Authors: Fu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMutations in the neutrophil elastase gene (ELANE) have classically been established as the primary genetic etiology of severe congenital neutropenia (SCN) and cyclic neutropenia (CyN), characterized by early-stage granulocytic maturation arrest and a predisposition to life-threatening infections. However, recent high-throughput transcriptomic, multi-omics, and functional studies have unveiled a paradigm-shifting, multidimensional role for neutrophil elastase (NE) that extends far beyond the confines of bone marrow failure. As a highly active serine protease, NE has emerged as a central hub in regulating recently discovered forms of regulated cell death (RCD), including pyroptosis, ferroptosis, and NETosis, and orchestrating the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). Within the bone marrow, mutant NE triggers intense proteotoxic stress, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, and a unique proapoptotic “aggrephagy” (the selective autophagic degradation of misfolded protein aggregates), driving granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) resistance and clonal evolution toward acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Extracellularly, NE released via neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) acts as a fascinating double-edged sword: it possesses the intrinsic capability to selectively eradicate genetically diverse cancer cells by cleaving the CD95 death domain and interacting with histone H1 isoforms, yet it concomitantly mediates immunosuppression, chemoresistance, and systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) in sepsis by reprogramming macrophage polarization. This review systematically synthesizes the intricate molecular mechanisms of NE, emphasizing the profound crosstalk between hematopoietic failure, inflammatory cell death pathways, and tumor immunology. Furthermore, this review highlights the translational potential of these discoveries, exploring cutting-edge therapeutic strategies, including CRISPR/Cas9-based precise base editing, dual-nickase promoter targeting, and selective allosteric small-molecule inhibitors, ultimately aiming to bridge the critical gaps from bench to bedside.Keywords: Severe Congenital Neutropenia, ELANE Mutation, Neutrophil Extracellular Traps, Regulated Cell Death, Tumor Immune Microenvironment, Targeted Therapy

Introduction: the dual nature of ELANE in maintaining immune homeostasis and driving pathogenesis

Neutrophils serve as the indispensable vanguard of the innate immune system, representing the most abundant circulating leukocytes responsible for the rapid clearance of invading pathogens. The homeostasis of neutrophil production, maturation, and apoptosis is tightly regulated by highly coordinated transcriptional and proteolytic networks within the bone marrow [1–4]. The ELANE gene, which encodes the serine protease neutrophil elastase (NE), is predominantly expressed during the promyelocyte stage of granulopoiesis. Historically, missense and frameshift heterozygous mutations in the ELANE gene have been universally recognized as the most frequent genetic aberration underlying severe congenital neutropenia (SCN) and cyclic neutropenia (CyN) [5–7]. This leads to a profound maturation arrest of myeloid progenitors, subjecting patients to recurrent, often fatal, bacterial and fungal infections [8–15].

However, the functional landscape of NE has recently undergone an unprecedented expansion. Accelerating evidence from single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), spatial transcriptomics, and degradomics reveals that mutant NE is not merely an intracellular structural misfolded pathological protein responsible for hematopoietic failure [16, 17]. Rather, it is a versatile and highly active signaling protease that profoundly influences systemic inflammatory cascades and the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) [18–22]. Upon release into the extracellular space, often extruded as a key functional component of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), NE actively participates in the regulation of recently discovered regulated cell death (RCD) pathways [23]. Recent breakthroughs have firmly established its direct involvement in initiating and propagating pyroptosis and ferroptosis across various pathological models, ranging from metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) to acute myeloid leukemia (AML), gastric cancer, non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and colorectal cancer [24–30].

In the complex milieu of cancer, NE exhibits a paradoxical and context-dependent dual role. On one hand, it possesses a remarkable intrinsic capacity to selectively execute genetically diverse cancer cells and attenuate primary tumorigenesis without harming healthy host tissues [31–35]. On the other hand, within specific highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, NE is hijacked by cancer cells to promote stemness, induce resistance to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and orchestrate immune evasion via the exclusion of cytotoxic T lymphocytes [36–39].

This comprehensive review aims to integrate these cross-disciplinary findings, intentionally shifting the scientific focus from traditional hematological manifestations to the broad, systemic spectrum of NE-mediated cell death and immune remodeling. By delineating the underlying molecular logic, examining the mechanisms of leukemic transformation, and identifying current research gaps, this review provides a forward-looking perspective on translational interventions. These range from targeted pharmacological therapies, such as specific NE and cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) inhibitors [40, 41], to the most advanced CRISPR/Cas9-mediated precise gene editing platforms [42, 43], offering a comprehensive roadmap for future clinical applications.

Disaster in the bone marrow: the molecular logic of hematopoietic failure and clonal evolution

Intracellular toxicity: proteotoxic stress, aggrephagy, and metabolic reprogramming

To date, a widespread spectrum of ELANE variants has been characterized in clinical cohorts, with systematic screenings identifying over 100 distinct pathogenic mutations. Extensive genetic mapping confirms that these variants encompass multiple molecular categories, predominantly comprising missense mutations, nonsense mutations, frameshifts, and in-frame deletions [44]. The specific nature and intra-genic localization of these mutations only partially determine the definitive clinical phenotype. While certain variants are more frequently associated with the severe clinical course of SCN and carry a significantly higher cumulative risk of secondary CSF3R mutations and malignant leukemic transformation, others manifest as CyN; notably, the functional mutation severity and clinical trajectories largely overlap between these two patient populations [45]. Understanding this complex mutational landscape is fundamental for deciphering the heterogeneous intracellular stress responses.

The canonical pathomechanism of ELANE-associated SCN postulates that misfolded NE proteins, directly resulting from these genetic mutations, accumulate within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), triggering a severe unfolded protein response (UPR). This sustained ER stress subsequently activates downstream apoptotic pathways, leading to the premature death of neutrophil precursors and consequent peripheral neutropenia [4, 46]. However, detailed functional models utilizing primary patient cells, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), as well as murine (e.g., 32Dcl3) and human (e.g., HL-60) myeloblast cell lines, suggest a substantially more heterogeneous and complex intracellular disaster [47–49].

Surprisingly, unlike canonical UPR-inducing mutations (e.g., p.S97L), specific pathogenic variants challenge the universal applicability of the UPR model. For instance, the p.G185R mutation, which is clinically associated with greater disease severity and a higher propensity for leukemogenesis, has been shown to impair granulocytic differentiation by downregulating the mRNA expression of critical hematopoietic transcription factors (Gfi1, Cebpe, Cebpd, and Spi1) and cell surface receptors (Csf3r) in murine models, without significantly eliciting a classical UPR or ER stress response [48, 50]. Conversely, mutations affecting the ELANE translation initiation codon (start codon mutations) result in the formation of nuclear N-terminally truncated NE aggregates. Unlike canonical UPR-inducing mutations, these distinct aggregates activate a unique proapoptotic “aggrephagy” pathway. This pathological process is characterized by a sharply decreased BAG1/BAG3 ratio (co-chaperones regulating protein quality control), direct colocalization of the misfolded NE with BAG3, localized expression of autophagic LC3B, and marked upregulation of the RNA-chaperone protein SERF1, which directly interacts with cytoplasmic NE to induce neutrophil precursor cell death [51].

Concurrently, the presence of mutant NE induces the excessive accumulation of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) in hematopoietic progenitor cells (HPCs). This acute oxidative stress promotes the aberrant formation of promyelocytic leukemia (PML) nuclear bodies, which intrinsically control the metabolic state of HPCs and create a deleterious feed-forward loop that further exacerbates ELANE expression and accelerates the disease course [52, 53]. Furthermore, high-resolution analyses of bone marrow subpopulations have revealed that primitive early hematopoietic stem cells (eHSCs, defined as CD49f+) appear to escape the UPR induced by mutated NE due to an initial lack of ELANE expression. However, in SCN patients, these CD49f+ cells fail to appropriately upregulate essential HSC-specific transcription factors (e.g., C/EBPα, MLL1, HOXA9) in response to physiological cues, further contributing to the profound maturation block [54].

Research gap

The specific biochemical thresholds and structural determinants that dictate whether a distinct ELANE missense, frameshift, or nonsense mutation triggers classic ER stress, ROS-mediated PML activation, or SERF1-driven aggrephagy (Fig. 1A) remain unmapped. Additionally, the precise spatiotemporal mechanisms by which primitive subsets, such as CD49f+ eHSCs, evade mutant NE toxicity while simultaneously failing to mount an effective granulopoietic differentiation response require deeper investigation utilizing single-cell multi-omics tracking.

Mechanisms of G-CSF resistance and the clonal drive toward leukemia

Long-term, high-dose administration of recombinant human G-CSF is the standard of care for alleviating neutropenia in SCN and CyN patients. However, approximately 15% of patients exhibit primary or secondary G-CSF refractoriness, and a similarly alarming proportion eventually develops myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or AML [55–59]. The transition from a hypoproliferative bone marrow failure state to a hyperproliferative myeloid malignancy is a stepwise, Darwinian evolutionary process, predominantly characterized by the acquisition of somatic mutations in the G-CSF receptor (CSF3R) and the transcription factor RUNX1 [53, 59–63].

Recent studies have radically updated our understanding of this leukemogenic transformation. It has been elucidated that the activation of truncated CSF3R mutants in SCN-HPCs does not merely provoke immediate clonal outgrowth through unrestrained hyperproliferation, but paradoxically induces a sustained, pathological pro-inflammatory state (Fig. 1B) [60]. This chronic inflammatory microenvironment, driven by acquired secondary driver mutations (such as in CXXC4) and the subsequent downregulation of TET2 activity, acts as a critical catalyst for malignant transformation [64].

Moreover, advanced iPSC modeling of the transition from SCN to AML (SCN/AML) has identified the dramatic upregulation of the BAALC oncogene and the consequent downstream phosphorylation of MK2a (MAPKAPK2, a critical kinase regulating inflammatory mRNA stability) as pivotal, early leukemogenic events. Notably, this renders primary SCN/AML blasts highly sensitive to CMPD1, a selective inhibitor of MK2a phosphorylation, highlighting a new targetable vulnerability [65]. In parallel, scRNA-seq of leukemic populations has identified specific subsets, such as the CD34 + CD117dim blast cluster, which exhibit partial maturation features, overexpress granule genes including ELANE and AZU1, and are highly correlated with chemoresistance and poor clinical outcomes (Fig. 1B and C) [66].

Research gap

The exact sequence of molecular events linking chronic G-CSF exposure, intrinsic ROS/inflammatory stress (e.g., TET2 suppression), and the eventual acquisition of CSF3R and RUNX1 mutations is not fully elucidated. There is a critical, unmet clinical need for longitudinal, multi-omics surveillance studies to define the temporal window of pre-leukemic clonal dominance. Defining this window would enable the implementation of preemptive, targeted therapies (such as MK2a inhibitors) before overt leukemic transformation permanently alters the bone marrow architecture.

Transcending boundaries: NE, NETs, and systemic inflammatory networks

Beyond the confines of the bone marrow, NE functions as a highly potent extracellular mediator of systemic immunity. This is predominantly achieved through its active extrusion within NETs and via direct proteolytic signaling cascades that affect a wide array of host and pathogen targets.

Macrophage-neutrophil crosstalk in sepsis and acute organ damage

In acute, life-threatening systemic inflammatory syndromes such as sepsis, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), severe COVID-19, and major burn shock, the dysregulated, explosive release of NE-rich NETs directly amplifies pathological tissue damage [67–73]. Extensive transcriptomic and scRNA-seq analyses of peripheral blood from septic shock patients have consistently identified ELANE, alongside LCN2, LTF, and MPO, as core hub genes and highly robust prognostic biomarkers [74–77]. Their overexpression is tightly linked to hyperactive neutrophil degranulation, massive systemic inflammation, impaired T-cell activation, and significantly increased 28-day mortality rates [20, 78–80].

The pathogenic amplification loop of sepsis is heavily dependent on aberrant macrophage-neutrophil crosstalk orchestrated by NE. For instance, severe inflammation induces hyper-ferritinemia, which significantly upregulates the expression of the macrophage scavenger receptor (MSR). This upregulation subsequently promotes massive, uncontrolled NET formation in a peptidylarginine deiminase 4 (PAD4)-, NE-, and ROS-dependent manner, severely exacerbating acute lung injury [81, 82]. Furthermore, extracellular NE does not merely act as a bystander protease; it actively drives the cytokine storm by directly promoting M1 macrophage polarization (characterized by high CD86 + expression) and stimulating the massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β and TNF-α (Fig. 2A) [83]. This positions NE at the apex of the inflammatory cascade during septic shock and extends its relevance to other inflammatory and degenerative contexts, including experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), rosacea, pediatric septic arthritis, spinal cord injury, and intervertebral disc degeneration [84–89].

Central regulator of newly discovered cell death: ferroptosis and pyroptosis

A true paradigm-shifting discovery in recent years is the definitive identification of NE as a critical, direct regulator of non-apoptotic, inflammatory cell death pathways, aligning perfectly with the evolving landscape of cell death research.

In the context of MAFLD, NE actively promotes rapid and severe hepatocyte ferroptosis. Mechanistically, NE disrupts the physiological binding between P62 (a classical autophagy receptor) and KEAP1 (a key sensor for oxidative stress), thereby enhancing KEAP1 protein stability by inhibiting its lysosomal degradation. This pathological stabilization subsequently enhances NRF2 ubiquitination, which decisively suppresses the protective NRF2/GPX4 antioxidant axis. The ultimate result is massive, lethal lipid peroxidation and widespread ferroptotic cell death in hepatocytes (Fig. 2B, left) [24].

Simultaneously, NE functions as a potent executioner of pyroptosis. In the treatment of AML, a novel combination regimen of G-CSF and recombinant human thrombopoietin (rhTPO) has been shown to drastically improve curative effects in elderly patients by triggering robust pyroptosis in leukemia cells, a process directly and mechanistically mediated by NE [27]. Similarly, in solid tumors such as gastric cancer and glioma, ELANE expression strongly correlates with pyroptotic gene signatures, and the NE protein physically interacts with gasdermin family proteins (e.g., GSDMC), serving as a highly promising prognostic biomarker for pyroptosis-mediated tumor necrosis and immune infiltration [25, 90]. Moreover, advanced diagnostic models based on pyroptosis-related genes (including ELANE, GSDMD, NLRP1, and CASP4) have successfully stratified sepsis patients, identifying specific GSDMD-expressing macrophage subsets that are intrinsically linked to poor survival outcomes (Fig. 2B, right) [91–93]. Recent studies also suggest that microRNAs, such as miR-608, can post-transcriptionally regulate ELANE expression, adding another layer of complexity to its inflammatory control [94, 95].

Research gap

Substantial biochemical and cellular evidence, such as the seminal work by Kambara et al. [96], has elegantly demonstrated that NE can directly cleave Gasdermin D (GSDMD) to generate pore-forming, active N-terminal fragments in a caspase-independent manner. Therefore, the capability of NE to cleave gasdermins is established. However, the broader in vivo physiological relevance of this pathway remains incompletely defined. Specifically, it is still unclear whether NE functions as a primary executioner or plays a more context-dependent, auxiliary role alongside canonical caspase-mediated mechanisms during complex inflammatory responses. Furthermore, the full spectrum of direct NE cleavage targets across other regulated cell death pathways requires comprehensive profiling.

Remodeling the Tumor Immune Microenvironment (TIME): the tug-of-war between “tumor-promoting” and “tumor-suppressing” effects of NE

The presence of tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) and their associated proteases in the TIME has long been a subject of intense debate. NE perfectly epitomizes this paradox, exerting profound but diametrically opposed effects depending on the specific cellular context, spatial localization, and tumor stage.

Tumor-specific killing: the potential of ne as a broad-spectrum anticancer factor

Recent groundbreaking studies have unveiled the astonishing intrinsic capacity of human NE to selectively eradicate genetically diverse cancer cells while completely sparing non-cancerous host cells, circumventing the broad toxicity associated with traditional chemotherapies. Mechanistically, NE proteolytically liberates the CD95 death domain. This liberated fragment then interacts specifically with histone H1 isoforms (H1.0 and H1.2), proteins that are frequently and significantly elevated in a wide variety of cancers, to trigger selective, highly immunogenic cancer cell death (immunogenic apoptosis) (Fig. 2C, left) [31, 34, 35].

This potent cytotoxic property has catalyzed the rapid development of innovative, targeted therapies. The intratumoral administration of an engineered therapeutic elastase (e.g., N17350) not only induces rapid, genotype-independent tumor regression but also stimulates robust CD8 + T-cell activation, generating a systemic abscopal effect (a phenomenon where localized treatment triggers a systemic anti-tumor immune response causing regression of distant, untreated metastases) that overcomes resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors in refractory models [35]. Moreover, a highly sophisticated systemic delivery approach utilizing lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-encapsulated mRNA encoding ELANE or porcine pancreatic elastase (PPE) has demonstrated remarkable efficacy. The SELECT (Simplified LNP with Engineered mRNA for Cell-type Targeting) platform leverages specific microRNA target sites incorporated into the mRNA scaffold to restrict NE translation exclusively to tumor cells. In vivo models have shown that this strategy significantly inhibited lung metastasis in aggressive melanoma models and powerfully promoted anti-tumor CD8 + T-cell infiltration without demonstrating any acute systemic toxicity [33, 97].

Accomplice to immunosuppression and metastasis: NETs and TIME reprogramming

Conversely, when successfully hijacked by the tumor, NE-rich NETs and TANs act as potent drivers of immunosuppression, angiogenesis, and metastasis. In HER2-positive breast cancer, soluble DLL4 released by tumor cells activates the Notch signaling pathway in adjacent neutrophils, heavily inducing the formation of NETs enriched with NE, MPO, and PDIA4. These NETs form a dense physical and biochemical barrier that aggressively excludes cytotoxic T-cell infiltration (Fig. 2C, right), ultimately culminating in severe resistance to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (e.g., THP regimens) [21, 36]. Similar mechanisms influence therapy responses in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) patients undergoing anti-TNF treatments, highlighting the pervasive nature of NE’s immune-modulating capability [98].

Furthermore, extensive scRNA-seq atlases of EGFR-wildtype lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) have identified a specific, highly pro-metastatic NE+ neutrophil subset driven by the central transcription factor RELB. This unique subpopulation strongly correlates with advanced lymph node metastasis, poor immunotherapeutic response, and the creation of a highly immunosuppressive niche [39]. Similarly, in LUAD, tumor cells downregulate the expression of TPM2 to activate the p38/MAPK signaling pathway in neutrophils, significantly increasing NE secretion. This secreted NE, in turn, promotes tumor progression via the Hippo pathway and chemoattracts additional neutrophils via IL-1β and IL-8, forming a vicious pro-tumorigenic cycle [37]. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), cancer cells have even been shown to upregulate their own endogenous ELANE expression, subsequently releasing NE-rich exosomes that forcefully polarize surrounding macrophages toward a pro-tumorigenic, immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, thereby drastically accelerating tumor growth (Fig. 2C, right). Notably, this pathological cross-communication can be effectively interrupted by CCK-BR antagonists like proglumide [99].

Research gap

The spatial and temporal dynamics that determine whether NE acts as an “anti-tumor lytic agent” (via CD95/Histone H1 cleavage) or a “pro-tumor immunosuppressive accomplice” (via NET-mediated T-cell exclusion) are currently undefined. Identifying the critical concentration thresholds of NE, characterizing the specific local co-factors in the TIME that mediate this functional switch, and utilizing high-definition spatial transcriptomics are absolutely essential steps for the safe and effective translation of NE-based cancer therapies.

Paradigm shift: frontier intervention strategies targeting ELANE and neutrophil defects

The standard, lifelong management of congenital neutropenia heavily relies on G-CSF; however, the persistent, looming risk of leukemic transformation and the existence of entirely G-CSF-refractory cases necessitate a paradigm shift toward definitive precision medicine. Remarkable advances in understanding the molecular pathogenesis of ELANE have recently catalyzed the rapid development of sophisticated gene therapies and novel pharmacological interventions aimed at directly rescuing progenitor cell death and restoring physiological immune homeostasis.

The clinical translation of advanced gene editing technologies

The advent of CRISPR/Cas9 technology has fundamentally revolutionized the therapeutic landscape for ELANE-associated SCN, powerfully shifting the focus from mere symptomatic relief to curative genetic correction [43, 56]. Early ex vivo approaches demonstrated that standard CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of the mutant ELANE allele in patient-derived CD34 + HSPCs effectively abolished the production of the misfolded, toxic NE protein. This intervention robustly rescued granulocytic differentiation and fully restored normal functional attributes, such as phagocytosis, ROS production, and chemotaxis, in the resulting neutrophils [56]. To rigorously preserve the wild-type allele’s critical antimicrobial function, highly refined monoallelic knockout strategies have been developed. These strategies selectively target heterozygous single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) closely linked to the mutant allele, which elegantly circumvents the loss of essential native protein functions [108].

Furthermore, homology-directed repair (HDR) strategies utilizing CRISPR-Cas9 combined with an adeno-associated virus 6 (AAV6) donor template have achieved precise correction of specific disease-causing mutations (e.g., L172P) with up to 56% efficiency, successfully restoring differentiation in rigorous in vivo humanized mouse xenograft models [42, 109]. To mitigate the well-documented risks of double-strand break (DSB)-induced genotoxicity and large chromosomal deletions, a “spacer-nick” approach combining the Cas9D10A nickase with a pair of PAM-out sgRNAs has been utilized, significantly reducing non-homologous end joining(NHEJ)-mediated on-target mutations [110].

Even more advanced next-generation editing platforms are rapidly moving toward clinical application. The MILESTONE approach utilizes a CRISPR-Cas9D10A nickase to introduce two specific single-strand DNA breaks at opposing strands of the ELANE promoter TATA box, highly efficiently suppressing ELANE mRNA expression without generating deleterious chromosomal translocations [101]. Pushing the boundaries further, the PRECISE strategy employed a cutting-edge adenine base editor (ABE) delivered via ribonucleoproteins (RNPs) to precisely edit target nucleotides in the ELANE promoter. This non-DSB(non-double-strand break) approach achieved over 68% editing efficiency in primary patient HSPCs, flawlessly rescuing granulocytic differentiation while maintaining pristine genome integrity with virtually zero detectable off-target effects [102]. Comprehensive head-to-head comparisons of these strategies strongly underscore the necessity of personalized, patient-specific off-target assessments (e.g., utilizing GUIDE-Seq and CAST-Seq on patient-derived iPSCs) to ensure the absolute clinical safety of these advanced therapeutics prior to human trials [111].

Discovery of innovative targets for pharmacological intervention

For patients unsuitable for complex gene therapy or allogeneic stem cell transplantation, innovative pharmacological agents offer highly promising, non-invasive alternatives to bypass the maturation arrest and prevent premature progenitor cell death [40].

Small-molecule NE inhibitors have garnered significant attention in this space. Interestingly, high-throughput screening has revealed that not all NE inhibitors are effective in the unique context of SCN. While agents like sivelestat are potent for controlling extracellular systemic inflammation (e.g., in ARDS), they completely fail to rescue neutrophil differentiation in patient-derived in vitro models. In stark contrast, MK0339, an orally absorbed, highly cell-permeable NE inhibitor that selectively binds to an alternative, stabilizing site on the NE protein, successfully alleviates ER stress (indicated by the marked correction of GRP78/BIP upregulation). MK0339 restores normal neutrophil maturation and full bactericidal capacity, presenting a highly viable, daily pharmacological equivalent to gene editing for preventing mutant ELANE toxicity [41, 103].

Beyond direct protease inhibition, modulating the downstream transcriptional and survival network offers another powerful therapeutic avenue. Sophisticated in silico drug repurposing utilizing transcriptomic data from SCN-iPSCs successfully identified flavopiridol, an FDA-approved pan-CDK inhibitor. Treatment with ultra-low-dose flavopiridol effectively rescued defective granulopoiesis across diverse inherited ELANE mutations by restoring the previously suppressed CEBPA signaling pathway, a known defective molecule in SCN and CyN, completely bypassing the need for exogenous G-CSF signaling and avoiding its associated leukemogenic risks [104].

Additionally, precision pharmacological treatments are expanding across the broader spectrum of inherited neutropenias. For example, the use of SGLT-2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin) has shown remarkable efficacy in ameliorating neutropenia and systemic inflammation in G6PC3 deficiency and GSD Ib by clearing toxic metabolic intermediates (e.g., 1,5-anhydroglucitol) [112, 113]. Similarly, CXCR4 antagonists (such as plerixafor and mavorixafor) have demonstrated significant success in correcting myelokathexis, restoring absolute neutrophil counts, and mitigating severe infection susceptibility in patients with WHIM (Warts, Hypogammaglobulinemia, Infections, and Myelokathexis) syndrome and CXCR2 loss-of-function variants [105–107]. For cases completely refractory to pharmacological interventions, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) remains a definitive curative option, significantly improving outcomes and resolving immunodeficiency in complex syndromes like JAGN1-deficiency and WHIM syndrome, alongside vigilant multidisciplinary management (e.g., dental and periodontal care) [114–117].

Research gap

Although pre-clinical iPSC and murine models of gene editing and novel small molecules demonstrate robust restoration of granulopoiesis, long-term clinical endpoints evaluating their true efficacy in preventing clonal evolution and leukemogenic transformation (MDS/AML) over decades are completely lacking. Furthermore, the systemic immunological consequences of permanently knocking out or chronically chemically inhibiting NE (particularly regarding the patient’s susceptibility to specific intracellular pathogens or potential alterations in the tumor immune microenvironment) remain an open question requiring extensive, long-term in vivo and clinical validation.

Conclusion and future directions

The scientific narrative of ELANE has evolved profoundly over the past decade, from being strictly and narrowly defined as a monogenic structural driver of severe congenital neutropenia to being universally recognized as a master orchestrator of the immune microenvironment and a central executor of previously identified regulated cell death pathways. Within the bone marrow, mutant NE instigates severe proteotoxic stress, aggrephagy, and ROS accumulation, serving as the proximal trigger for differentiation arrest, secondary G-CSF resistance, and ultimate leukemic transformation. Yet, in the periphery and within the complex tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), NE exerts a highly context-dependent dual role: it acts as a potent, tumor-specific lytic agent through the selective cleavage of the CD95 death domain and interaction with histone H1, while simultaneously acting as an immunosuppressive accomplice via NET-induced T-cell exclusion and macrophage reprogramming.

The future of ELANE-targeted research relies heavily on the integration of high-resolution multi-omics technologies. scRNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and advanced degradomics will be indispensable for deciphering the precise biochemical thresholds and specific cleavage substrates that dictate whether ELANE promotes protective, anti-tumor pyroptosis or detrimental, pro-tumor systemic inflammation. Clinically, the rapid advancement of highly targeted mRNA-LNP delivery systems for cancer therapy, extremely precise base-editing tools (like PRECISE) for SCN, and highly selective allosteric inhibitors (like MK0339) are rapidly bridging the gap from bench to bedside. Ultimately, unraveling the multidimensional network of ELANE will not only permanently transform the therapeutic paradigm for congenital bone marrow failure syndromes but will also unlock unprecedented, highly targeted avenues for systemic immunomodulatory and broad-spectrum anticancer therapies.
