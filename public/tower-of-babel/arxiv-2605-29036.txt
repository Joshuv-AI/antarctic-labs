On the existence of Markovian measures on continuous paths
Jules Pitcho

Abstract
. Let 𝜼 \eta be a positive Radon measure on the space of continuous paths from ℝ \mathbb{R} into a locally compact Polish space Y Y , and assume that 𝜼 \eta admits an invariant measure. We explicit conditions on 𝜼 \eta such that successive Markovianisation of 𝜼 \eta over any dense and countable set of times have all limit points (in the weak-star topology on Radon measures) satisfying the strong Markov property. We show that if Y Y is a locally compact Polish group, and 𝜼 \eta is left or right translation invariant, then 𝜼 \eta satisfies such conditions. Our proof uses the Zermalo-Fraenkel and Axiom of Dependant Choice axiomatisation of set theory, in which countable products of compacts are compact.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

It is known since the nineteenth century that spatially Lipschitz vector fields admit a unique flow. In the late twentieth century and the early twenty first century, vector fields with only one weak spatial derivative – whether a locally integrable function or a Radon measure – have been shown to admit an almost everywhere unique flow in
[
12
,
3
,
4
]
(see also
[
10
]
for a quantitative stability theory, and
[
5
]
for a regularity theory). For rougher vector fields, superposition principles
[
2
,
17
]
guarantee existence of measures concentrated on integral curves called
Lagrangian representations
.

The structure theory of Lagrangian representations is however little understood. Results are known in the case vector fields singular at the initial time
[
14
,
15
]
, in the case of scalar conservation laws
[
7
,
6
]
or in the case of autonomous one-dimensional ODE’s
[
9
]
.

An interesting question is then whether Lagrangian representations can be memoryless, that is whether knowledge of the present state of a Lagrangian representation suffices to uniquely determine its future evolution, without any further information on its past. This property is expressed mathematically through a Markov property.
Motivated by this question, we directly study measures on the space of continuous paths valued in a locally compact Polish space, and ask which such measures admit a modification satisfying a Markov property.

1.2.
Our contribution in a nutshell

Aim of this paper is to construct a Markovian version of any measure on the space of continuous paths valued in a locally compact Polish space satisfying a regularity property, and admitting an invariant measure. We also show that translation invariant measures satisfy this regularity property.

More precisely, we will define the Markov operator
M_{t}
at a time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which maps a measure
\eta
on the space of continuous paths to the unique measure on the space of continuous paths
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
, for which the distribution observed before time
t
and after time
t
coincide with that of
\eta
, but for which the distribution before and after time
t\in\mathbb{R}
are independant.
\eta
is then said to be
Markovianised
at time
t
, if
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
. We will further say that
\eta
satisfies the Markov property if it is Markovianised at every time
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We will then show that successively applying the Markov operator at a finite number of times gives a unique measure, which does not depend on the ordering of these times, thereby showing that Markovianisation on a finite set of times is well-defined. The Markov hull of
\eta
will be defined as the set of all measures reachable in the weak-star limit by successive Markovianisation of
\eta
over any countable and dense subset of
\mathbb{R}
.

We will prove two results. Firstly, we will show that under a regularity hypothesis on
\eta
, every element of the (non-empty) Markov hull of
\eta
satisfies the (strong) Markov property. Secondly, we will show that every translation invariant
\eta
satisfies the aforementioned regularity property whereby every element in its Markov hull satisfies the (strong) Markov property.

1.4.
Physical interpretation

Let us propose the following dictionary to interpret Corollary

in the language of physics. We translate
\mu
-invariant Radon measure on the space of continuous paths to realisation of incompressible Lagrangian turbulence; we translate translation invariant to homogenous; we translate Markov property to memoryless; and finally an element of the Markov hull is translated to a model.

We then loosely rephrase Corollary
1.5
as
‘every translation invariant and
\mu
-invariant Radon measure on the space of continuous paths has each element of its (non-empty) Markov hull satisfying the Markov property.’ Following the dictionary proposed above, this translates to the following.

Every realisation of incompressible, homogeneous Lagrangian turbulence admits a memoryless model.

1.5.
Outline of ideas

We now present the main ideas to prove Theorem
1.2
.

1.5.1.
Uniform spaces

Our proof uses the theory uniform spaces introduced by André Weil
[
18
]
and subsequently developed by Nicolas Bourbaki
[
8
]
.
Let us motivate the concept of a uniform spaces.

A sequence of pairs of points in a metric space will be called uniformly close, if there is a finite upper bound to the distance between each pair of points. Uniform closeness of a sequence of pairs of points is a property invariant under uniform equivalence of the metric. It is however not expressible solely in terms of the topology structure induced by the metric – that in terms of the family of open balls induced by the metric. In other words, sequences of pairs of points in a metric space which are uniformly close cannot be distinguished from those which are not solely by the topology induced by the metric.

As a topology, a uniformity is expressed in a set-theoretic language, wherein contrary to a metric space structure, no reference is made to the real line. However, as an enrichement over a topology, a uniformity induced by a metric distinguishes sequences of pairs of points which are uniformly close from those which are not. In other words, a uniformity is sufficiently rich to express the property of uniform closeness of a sequence of pairs of points. Concepts such as uniform continuity or equicontinuity can thus be defined for maps from a topological space into a uniform space.
In particular, Ascoli’s compactness criterion for the uniform topology on real-valued continuous functions can be generalised to functions valued in uniform space.

1.5.2.
Tensor product of measures through a map and Markovianisation

To define the Markovianisation of a measure on the space of paths valued in a locally compact Polish space, we will first define the tensor product of two measures through a map, and give a characterising property of this object. Given a measure
\eta_{t,x}
concentrated on a set of paths, which are all valued at
x\in Y
for some time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we will define its Markovianisation at time
t
as the tensor product
\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,x}\otimes_{(t,x)}\eta_{t,x}^{[t,+\infty)}
, which is the unique probability measure on the space of continuous, whose distribution before time
t
coincides with that
\eta
, whose disitribution after time
t
coincides with that of
\eta
, but – contrary to
\eta
– the distribution before and after time
t
are independant.

Given now a measure
\eta
on the space of paths, whose one-marginal at time
t\in\mathbb{R}
is the positive Radon measure
\mu
on
Y
, denoting by
\{\eta_{t,x}\}_{x\in Y}
a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
, we then define its Markovianisation
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
at time
t
as the following integral

(1.2)

M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})=\int_{Y}\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,x}\otimes_{(t,x)}\eta_{t,x}^{[t,+\infty)}\mu(dx).

Whenever
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
, we will say that
\eta
satisfies the Markov property at time
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We will then show that successive Markovianistion of
\eta
over a finite set of times does not depend on the ordering of these times, thereby showing that Markovianisation over a finite set of times is well-defined. To do so, we will have to prove properties of bilinearity and associativity of the tensor product of measures. We will then define the Markov hull of a
\mu
-invariant measure
\eta
to be the set of all accumulation points – in the weak-star topology on Radon measures – of sequences of successive Markovianisation of
\eta
over any countable and dense subset of
\mathbb{R}
. The Markov property however does not pass into the weak-star limit.

1.5.3.
The space of disintegrations
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with respect to
\mu
and the disintegration map

We define the set of maps from
\operatorname{supp}\mu
into probability measures on the space of paths, for which there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
the image of
x
through such a map is concentrated on paths whose value at time
t
is
x
. We then identity such maps which coincide
\mu
-a.e. and form the set of equivalence classes
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. On this set, we will define integrals, which consist in averaging the values of a (equivalence class) map
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with respect to a class of nonnegative and continuous probability density on
\operatorname{supp}\mu
. We then endow
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with the initial uniformity with respect to all such integrals, which is the coarsest uniformity on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
making all these integrals uniformly continuous.
We further consider the topological space
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
consisting of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
endowed with the topology of compact convergence, and observe that precompact sets are characterised by Ascoli’s theorem.

We then define the disintegration map, mapping a
\mu
-invariant measure
\eta
on the space of continuous paths to a continuous map from
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
, which sends every
t\in\mathbb{R}
to be the equivalence class of disintegrations (restricted to
\operatorname{supp}\mu
) of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
. We will show that the disintegration map has precompact image in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
using Tychonov’s theorem for countable products (which can be proved in the Zermalo-Fraenkel and Axiom of Dependant Choice axiomatisation of set theory as shown in
[
13
, Proposition 4.72]
), thus enriching the information on the convergence to elements of the Markov hull. However this will still not be sufficient in order pass into the limit in the Markov property.

1.5.4.
The space of continuous disintegrations
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
with respect to
\mu

We define the set
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
of continuous maps from
\operatorname{supp}\mu
into probability measures on the space of paths (endowed with the vague uniformity inducing the weak-star topology), for which there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
the image of
x
through such a map is concentrated on paths whose value at time
t
is
x
. We endow this set with the uniform structure of compact convergence, and show that the canonical injection of
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is uniformly continuous.

Assuming now that
\eta
is
Markov regular
will ensure that disintegrations of
\eta
with respect to
\mu
of successive Markovianisations of
\eta
are always contained in some compact subset of
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
. Since on a compact space, there exists a unique uniformity compatible with the topology, we will deduce that the image through the disintegration map of any successive Markovianisations of
\eta
is precompact in the topological space
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
endowed with the topology of compact convergence.

Therefore, we will be able to upgrade the convergence to an element

in the Markov hull: for every

, we will have that the unique continuous disintegration (restricted to
\operatorname{supp}\mu
) with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
of the successive Markovianisations of
\eta
converges in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
to the unique continuous disintegration of
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
. This convergence will be strong enough to pass into the limit in the Markov property, and to further propagate the Markov property to all times (not only the dense and countable set of times of successive Markovianisation). It will in fact be shown that
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
satisfies the
strong Markov property
.

1.5.5.
Translation invariant measures

We now consider the set of (left or right) translation invariant measures on a space of continuous paths valued in a locally compact Polish group. We will show that for any compact time interval
I
in
\mathbb{R}
, and there exists a compact set
K
in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
such that for any translation invariant
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},
there exists a disintegration
\{\eta_{t,x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}
of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
, which belongs to

. We will further show that the set of translation invariant measures is invariant by Markovianisation, thereby showing that all its elements are Markov regular.

1.6.
Organisation of this paper

In Section

Secondly we will present the key concept of an initial uniformity, and the uniformity induced by a family of pseudometrics. Fourthly, we will show how to endow a function space with different uniformities such as the uniformity of uniform convergence, the uniformity of

In Section

2.1.
Neighbourhoods

We recall the notion of neighborhood of a point from
[

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a topological space and

. A neighbourhood of

is any subset of

which contains an open set containing

. The neighborhoods of a subset

consisting of a single point are also called neighbourhoods of the point

Let us denote by

the set of all neighbourhoods of

. The set

have the following properties:

(V_{1})

Every subset of
X
which contains a set belonging to
\mathfrak{B}(x)
itself belongs to
\mathfrak{B}(x).

(V_{2})

Every finite intersection of sets of
\mathfrak{B}(x)
belongs to
\mathfrak{B}(x).

(V_{3})

The element

is in every set of
\mathfrak{B}(x)
.

(V_{4})

If
V
belongs ot
\mathfrak{B}(x)
, then there exists a set

belonging to
\mathfrak{B}(x)
such that for every

,

belongs to
\mathfrak{B}(y)
.

Proposition 2.2

If to each element

of a set

, there corresponds a set

of subsets of

such that the properties

.

Remark 2.4
.

In a topological space space

We can now give the definition of a uniform structure.

Definition 2.5

A uniform structure (or uniformity) on a set

\mathcal{U}
of

which satisfy

and
(F_{2})
and the following axioms:

(U_{1})

Every set belonging to

(U_{2})

Remark 2.6

The composite of two subsets

(2.1)

U\circ V=\{(x,z)\;:\;\text{there exists $y\in X$ such that $(x,y)\in U$ and $(y,z)\in V$}\}.

The inverse of

is defined by

(2.2)

\overset{-1}{V}=\{(y,x)\in X\times X\;:\;(x,y)\in V\}.

A uniformity can be uniquely determined by a subfamily of entourages.

Definition 2.7

A fundamental system of entourages of a uniformity is any set
\mathfrak{B}
of entourages such that every entourage contains a set belonging to

Given a subset

.

Proposition 2.8

Let

, and for every

be the set of subsets of

of

is the neighbourhood filter of

This is topology is called

.
Finally let us state a criterion for a uniform space to be Hausdorff.

Theorem 2.9

A uniform space

2.3.
Uniformly continuous functions

We now define morphisms between uniform spaces. These are uniformly continuous functions.

Definition 2.10

A mapping

2.4.
Comparison of uniformities

The class of uniform structures on a given set is a partially ordered set. Let us define the order relation.

If

The following proposition asserts that a finer uniformity contain more entourages.

Proposition 2.13

If
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and

The following shows that the corresponding order relation induced between topologies coincides with the usual order relation between topologies.

Let

3.
Measure theory on topological spaces

In this section, we first present some useful results of measures theory of topological spaces. A detailed presentation is given in Laurent Schwartz’s book
[
16
]
. We then specify to measures where the underlying topological space is a space of continuous paths valued in a locally compact Polish space.

3.1.
Radon measures on topological spaces

Let
X
be a locally compact Polish space and let
\mathscr{B}(X)
be its Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Let
C_{c}(X)
be the space of compactly supported, real-valued continuous functions on
X
. It is a Banach space under the
\sup
norm. A functional on
C_{c}(X)
is a map from
C_{c}(X)
to the real numbers. A functional
\lambda
is said to be
positive
, if
\Phi\geq 0
implies that
\lambda(\Phi)\geq 0
.

3.1.1.
Basic facts on Radon measures

By convention, a Radon measure will always be assumed to be positive.
Let us begin with the definition of Radon measures as a real-valued set map.

Definition 3.1
.

A map
\nu:\mathscr{B}(X)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is said to be a Radon measure on
X
, if

(i)

\nu
is outer regular on all Borel sets, i.e.

(3.1)

\nu(A)=\inf\{\nu(B)\;:\;B\text{ open and }A\subset B\};

(ii)

\nu
is inner regular on all Borel sets, i.e.

(3.2)

\nu(A)=\sup\{\nu(F)\;:\;F\subset A,\text{ $F$ compact and measurable}\};

(iii)

\nu
is finite on compacts, i.e.
\nu(K)<+\infty
for every
K
compact in
X
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(X)
be the set of Radon measures on
X
.
Every Radon measure on
X
induces a continous linear functional on
C_{c}(X)
through the formula

(3.3)

\lambda(\Phi)=\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu(dx)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(X).

Conversely, by the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem, every Radon measure arises as a positive continuous linear functional on
C_{c}(X)
through the formula (
3.3
).

3.1.2.
Vague uniformity on Radon measures

We henceforth equip the set
\mathcal{M}(X)
with the uniform structure induced by the family of pseudometrics

(3.4)

{\rm d}_{\Phi}(\nu^{1},\nu^{2})=\Big|\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu^{1}(dx)-\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu^{2}(dx)\Big|,

where
\Phi
runs in
C_{c}(X)
. This uniform structure will be called the
vague uniformity
, and the induced topology will be called the
vague topology
. A fundamental system of entourage of the vague uniformity on
\mathcal{M}(X)
is given by
\bigcap_{\Phi\in J}{\rm d}^{-1}_{\Phi}([0,\alpha])
where
J
runs through all the finite subsets of
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, and
\alpha
runs in the nonnegative real numbers.

Any subset
B
of
\mathcal{M}(X)
equipped with the uniformity induced from the vague uniformity will also be said to be equipped with the vague uniformity. A fundamental system of entourage of the vague uniformity on

is then given by
\bigcap_{\Phi\in J}{\rm d}^{-1}_{\Phi}([0,\alpha])\cap B\times B
where

runs through all the finite subsets of
C_{c}(\Gamma)
.

3.1.3.
Separability and metrizability

By Thereom
2.25
, there exists a dense and countable subset
\mathscr{D}\subset C_{c}(X)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M>0
. Then the vague uniformity on
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
has a countable fundamental system of entourages, and is metrizable.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Chapter IX, 2.4]
, it suffices to show that the family of pseudometrics
\{{\rm d}_{\Phi}\}_{\Phi\in\mathscr{D}}
induces a uniformity on
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
, which is isomorphic to the vague uniformity of
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
.
By the description of the fundamental system of entourages of
\mathcal{M}(X)
above, we will thus have to show that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and every
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
, there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and
\bar{\Phi}\in\mathscr{D}
such that

(3.5)

{\rm d}^{-1}_{\bar{\Phi}}([0,\beta])\subset{\rm d}^{-1}_{\Phi}([0,\alpha]).

Let
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Choose
\bar{\Phi}\in\mathscr{D}
such that
\sup_{x\in X}|\bar{\Phi}(x)-\Phi(x)|\leq\alpha/4M
and
\beta\in(0,\alpha/2)\cap\mathbb{Q}
.
Then we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu^{1}(dx)-\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu^{2}(dx)\Big|&\leq\int_{X}|\Phi(x)-\bar{\Phi}(x)|\nu^{1}(dx)+\int_{X}|\Phi(x)-\bar{\Phi}(x)|\nu^{2}(dx)\\
&\quad+\Big|\int_{X}\bar{\Phi}(x)\nu^{1}(dx)-\int_{X}\bar{\Phi}(x)\nu^{2}(dx)\Big|\\
&\leq\alpha/2+\Big|\int_{X}\bar{\Phi}(x)\nu^{1}(dx)-\int_{X}\bar{\Phi}(x)\nu^{2}(dx)\Big|.\end{split}

Therefore, if
(\nu^{1},\nu^{2})\in{\rm d}_{\bar{\Phi}}^{-1}([0,\beta])
, then
(\nu^{1},\nu^{2})\in{\rm d}_{\Phi}^{-1}([0,\alpha])
, which proves the thesis.
∎

We describe a countable fundamental system of entourages of the vague uniformity of
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
by

(3.7)

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\rm d}_{\Phi_{i}}^{-1}([0,\alpha]),

where

runs in
\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha
runs in
\mathbb{Q}^{+}
and
\Phi_{i}
run in
\mathscr{D}
. Denoting by

the canonical injection, a metric inducing the vague uniformity is then given by

{\rm d}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm d}_{\Phi_{k}}\circ(\iota\times\iota)/2^{k}.

The vague topology is thus characterised by the

of sequences: we shall say that
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges vaguely to

as

(3.8)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu_{n}(dx)=\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu(dx)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(X).

Recall that a topological space is called
\sigma
-compact
, if it admits a countable exhaustion by compacts. Note that a locally compact Polish space is always
\sigma

The following lemma shows that metrizability holds for a larger class of subsets of subsets of

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(A_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable exhaustion by compacts of

(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of positive real numbers. Then the vague uniformity on

B:=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(A_{k})=M_{k}\}

Proof.

The case

For each

(3.9)

r_{k}:\mathcal{M}(X)\ni\nu\longmapsto\nu\lfloor_{A_{k}}\in\mathcal{M}(A_{k}).

In view of Lemma
3.2
, the vague uniformity on
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(A_{k})\;:\;\nu(A_{k})=M_{k}\}
is metrizable, on which we let
{\rm d}_{k}
be a metric. We now claim that
{\rm d}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm d}_{k}\circ(r_{k}\times r_{k})/2^{k}
is a metric inducing the vague unifomity

∎

3.2.
Vague compactness of Radon measures

Let

be a locally compact Polish space. We begin by showing that on a compact space, the space of Radon measures with constant mass is vaguely compact.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
X
is a compact topological space. Then the set
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
is vaguely compact.

Proof.

Observe that the function which is identically equal to

\mathbb{1}_{X}
, belongs to

. To show that
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}

(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
and assume that
\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to

as

Then we have

(3.10)

M=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\nu_{n}(X)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\mathbb{1}_{X}(x)\nu_{n}(dx)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\mathbb{1}_{X}(x)\nu^{\prime}(dx)=\nu^{\prime}(X).

This shows that
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
is vaguely closed. It suffices to show that
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
is vaguely precompact, and since
\mathcal{M}(X)
is metrizable, it is enough to show sequential vague precompactness. By Theorem
2.25
, there exists a countable and dense subset
\mathscr{D}
of
C_{c}(X)
. Let
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(X)=M\}
. For every
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}
, we have

(3.11)

\Big|\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu_{n}(dx)\Big|\leq M\|\Phi\|_{C^{0}}.

By a diagonal argument, there exists an increasing map

\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and a countinuous linear map
\Lambda:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.12)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu_{\delta(n)}(dx)=\Lambda(\Phi)\qquad\forall\Phi\in\mathscr{D}.

\Lambda
admits a unique continuous extension
\bar{\Lambda}
to
C_{c}(X)
, for which
\Phi\geq 0
implies
\bar{\Lambda}(\Phi)\geq 0
. Therefore by the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem, there exists a unique Radon measure
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(X)
such that

(3.13)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu_{\delta(n)}(dx)=\bar{\Lambda}(\Phi)=\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu^{\prime}(dx)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(X).

∎

We then have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(A_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable exhaustion by compacts of
X
, and let
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequence of nonnegative real numbers. Then the set

B:=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Big\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(A_{k})=M_{k}\Big\}

is vaguely compact.

Proof.

The case when
B=\emptyset
is trivial.
Observe that for every
A_{k}
, the restriction map
\mathcal{M}(X)\ni\nu\longmapsto\nu\lfloor_{A_{k}}\in\mathcal{M}(A_{k})
is vaguely continuous.
Therefore, in view of Proposition
3.4
, the set
\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;:\;\nu(A_{k})=M_{k}\}
is vaguely closed, whence

B
is also vaguely closed. As
B
is metrizable by Lemma
3.3
, it is now enough to show that
B
is sequentially vaguely precompact in
\mathcal{M}(X)
. Let
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
B
. In view of Proposition
3.4
,
(\nu_{n}\lfloor_{A_{k}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is vaguely sequentially precompact in
\mathcal{M}(A_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By a diagonal argument, there thus exists an increasing map
\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\nu_{\delta(n)}\lfloor_{A_{k}}
vaguely converges to
\nu\lfloor_{A_{k}}
as
n\to+\infty
in
\mathcal{M}(A_{k})
. Observe that
\nu\lfloor_{A_{k_{1}}}\lfloor_{A_{k_{0}}}=\nu\lfloor_{A_{k_{0}}}
if
k_{0}\leq k_{1}
. Denoting by
\iota_{A_{k}}:A_{k}\hookrightarrow X
the canonical injections, we can then set
\nu=\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\iota_{A_{k}})_{\#}\nu_{A_{k}}
in
\mathcal{M}(X)
. Let us finally show that
\nu_{\delta(n)}
vaguely converges to
\nu
in
\mathcal{M}(X)
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
\Phi\in C_{c}(X)
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset A_{k}
, whence we have

(3.14)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu_{\delta(n)}(dx)&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A_{k}}\Phi(\iota_{A_{k}}x)\nu_{\delta(n)}\lfloor_{A_{k}}(dx)\\
&=\int_{A_{k}}\Phi(\iota_{A_{k}}x)\nu\lfloor_{A_{k}}(dx)\\
&=\int_{X}\Phi(x)\nu(dx).\end{split}

The thesis follows.
∎

3.3.
Disintegration of Radon measures

Let us now present a useful tool for our purpose: the disintegration of a measure with respect to a Borel map and a target measure used in the study of linear transport in
[
1
,
4
,
14
,
15
]
. A presentation with proofs of the disintegration of a measure is given in
[
11
]
.
Let
X
and
Y
be a locally compact Polish spaces,
\mu
a Radon measure on
X
,
\nu
a Radon measure on
Y
and
f:X\to Y
a Borel map such that
f_{\#}\mu=\nu
. Then there exists a Borel family of probability measures
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
of measures on
Y
such that

(i)

\mu_{y}
is concentrated on the level set
E_{y}:=f^{-1}(y)
for every
y\in Y
;

(ii)

the measure
\mu
can be decomposed as
\mu=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\nu(dy)
, which means that for every Borel set
A
contained in
X
, we have

(3.15)

\mu(A)=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}(A)\nu(dy).

Any family satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
is called a
disintegration
of
\mu
with respect to
f
and
\nu
. The disintegration is essentially unique in the following sense: for any other disintegration
\{\tilde{\mu}_{y}:y\in Y\}
, it holds
\mu_{y}=\tilde{\mu}_{y}
for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in Y
.
We also have

(3.16)

\int_{X}\phi d\mu=\int_{Y}\Big[\int_{E_{y}}\phi d\mu_{y}\Big]\nu(dy)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
.

We now give a useful fact.
Let
g:X\to X
and
h:X\to Y
be Borel maps such that:

(P)

h_{\#}\mu=\nu
and for every
y\in Y
, we have
g^{-1}((h^{-1}(y))^{c})=(f^{-1}(y))^{c}
.

The following is true.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
is a disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
f
and
\nu
, then
\{g_{\#}\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
is a disintegration of
g_{\#}\mu
with respect to
h
and
\nu
.

Proof.

Let
y\in Y
. Observe that
g_{\#}\mu_{y}((h^{-1}(y))^{c})=\mu_{y}(g^{-1}(h^{-1}(y))^{c})=\mu_{y}((f^{-1}(y))^{c})=0
where we have used (P) in the second to last equality and that
\mu_{y}
is concentrated on
f^{-1}(y)
in the last equality.
So
g_{\#}\mu_{y}
is supported on
h^{-1}(y)
, and since
y
was arbitrary, this proves
(i)
.

Let
A
a Borel set in
X
. Then as
g^{-1}(A)
is a Borel set in
X
, it follows that

(3.17)

g_{\#}\mu(A)=\mu(g^{-1}(A))=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}(g^{-1}(A))\nu(dy)=\int_{Y}g_{\#}\mu_{y}(A)\nu(dy),

which gives
(ii)
.
∎

Let us denote by the support of
\nu
by
\operatorname{supp}\nu
, which is the smallest closed set such that
\nu(Y\backslash\operatorname{supp}\nu)=0
.
By a slight abuse of language, the restriction
\{\mu_{y}\;:\;y\in\operatorname{supp}\nu\}
of
\{\mu_{y}\;:\;y\in Y\}
to
\operatorname{supp}\nu
will also be called a disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
f
and
\nu
.

3.4.
Measures on the space of continuous paths

Let
Y
be a locally compact Polish space. Consider the uniformity of compact convergence
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
on the set of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
Y
, which is Hausdorff and complete by Theorem
2.21
. Precompact sets with respect to the uniformity of compact convergence are characterised by Ascoli’s theorem.

3.4.1.
The space of paths
\Gamma

Given
h\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the map
b_{h}(\gamma(\cdot))=\gamma(\cdot-h)
from the set of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
Y
, into the same set.
Let
\Gamma
be a subset of
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
such that:

(Comp-
\Gamma
)

for each compact
K\subset Y
, and each compact
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
\cup_{t\in I}e_{t}^{-1}(K)\cap\Gamma
is compact in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y);

(t-trans-
\Gamma
)

for every
h\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
b_{h}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
.

We endow
\Gamma
with the induced uniformity.

•

\Gamma
is locally compact. Indeed, let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
Y
is locally compact, there exists an open set
U
in
Y
such that
\gamma(0)\in U
, and a compact set
K
in
Y
contaning
U
, whence we have

\gamma\in e_{0}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma\subset e_{0}^{-1}(K)\cap\Gamma,

and by continuity of
e_{0}
, the set
e_{0}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma
is open in
\Gamma
, and
e_{0}^{-1}(K)\cap\Gamma
is compact in
\Gamma
by (Comp-
\Gamma
).

•

\Gamma
is metrizable. This follows directly from Theorem
2.25
.

•

\Gamma
is completely metrizable.
Let
{\rm d}
be a metric compatible with the uniform structure on
\Gamma
. Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
(\Gamma,{\rm d})
. Then
(e_{0}(\gamma_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
Y
since the map
e_{0}
is uniformly continuous, and converges to some
y\in Y
by completeness of
Y
. Since
Y
is locally compact, there is an open set
U
, and a compact set
K
such that
y\in U\subset K,
and up to changing the starting index of the sequence, we can assume that
(e_{0}(\gamma_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U
. Therefore
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset e_{0}^{-1}(K)
, so by compactness of
e_{0}^{-1}(K)
, the sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
(\Gamma,{\rm d})
to some
\gamma\in e_{0}^{-1}(K)
.

•

\Gamma
is separable, since
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
is separable by Theorem
2.25
.

Therefore
\Gamma
is a locally compact Polish space.

3.4.2.
\mu
-invariant Radon measures on
\Gamma

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
Y
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
be the subset of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
such that for every
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, and every

, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\mu
.
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)

.
Observe that a fundamental system of entourages of the uniformity on

is given by

(3.18)

\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}{\rm d}^{-1}_{\Phi_{i}}([0,\alpha])\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma),

where

runs in
\mathbb{Q}^{+}
,

runs in
\mathbb{N}
, and

run in

.
We record the following properties of

.

Lemma 3.7
.

The vague uniformity on
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
is metrizable and compact.

Proof.

Let
(B_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion by compacts of
Y
.
Setting
A_{k}=e_{0}^{-1}(B_{k})
, which is compact in view of (Comp-
\Gamma
), and setting
M_{k}=\mu(e_{0}^{-1}(B_{k}))
for every

3.3
, the vague uniformity on

B:=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)\;:\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(A_{k})=M_{k}\}

. Let

∎

4.
The tensor product of measures

In the section, we will construct the tensor product of measures through a map.

4.1.
Generalities on measure theory

A
measurable space
is a pair consisting of a set and a
\sigma
-algebra over this set.
A map between two measurable spaces is said to be
measurable
, if under this map, the inverse image of every set in the
\sigma
-algebra of the codomain lies in the
\sigma
-algebra of the domain. A
measure
on a measurable space is a real-valued, nonnegative set function on the
\sigma
-algebra such that the image of the empty set is zero and which is countably additive. A measurable space endowed with a measure is called a
measure space
.

Let
(X,\Sigma)
and
(Y,\Sigma^{\prime})
be two measurable spaces, let
\nu
be measure on
(X,\Sigma)
and let
g
be a measurable map from
X
to
Y
. The
pushforward measure
of
\nu
through
g
is defined to be the unique measure
g_{\#}\nu
on
(Y,\Sigma)
such that

(4.1)

g_{\#}\nu(A)=\nu(g^{-1}(A))\qquad\forall A\in\Sigma^{\prime}.

Let
\nu^{\prime}
be a measure on
(Y,\Sigma^{\prime})
.
Assume now that
g
also satisfies the following property
g(A)\in\Sigma^{\prime}
for every
A\in\Sigma
.
The
pullback measure
of
\nu^{\prime}
through
g
is defined to be the unique measure
g^{\#}\nu^{\prime}
on
(X,\Sigma)
such that

(4.2)

g^{\#}\nu^{\prime}(A)=\nu^{\prime}(g(A))\qquad\forall A\in\Sigma.

It then holds that
g^{\#}g_{\#}\nu=\nu
if
g
is injective, and
g_{\#}g^{\#}\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime}
if
g
is surjective.

4.2.
Tensor product of measures through a map

Let
(X_{1},\Sigma_{1})
and
(X_{2},\Sigma_{2})
be two measurable spaces.
Consider the product measurable space
(X_{1}\times X_{2},\Sigma_{1}\otimes\Sigma_{2})
, where the product
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma_{1}\otimes\Sigma_{2}
is the coarsest
\sigma
-algebra, which makes the two canonical projections maps
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are measurable.
Let
X
be a set, let
f:X\to X_{1}\times X_{2}
be an injective map, and let
\Sigma
be the coarsest
\sigma
-algebra on
X
, which makes
f
measurable.
Let
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
be two measures on
(X_{1},\Sigma_{1})
and
(X_{2},\Sigma_{2})
respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that there exists
S_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
and
S_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}
such that

(i)

X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}\subset{\rm Im}f;

(ii)

\nu_{1}(S_{1})=0
and
\nu_{2}(S_{2})=0
.

Then there exists a unique probability measure
\nu
on
(X,\Sigma)
such that
f_{\#}\nu=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}
, which explicitely means

(4.3)

\nu\big((p_{1}\circ f)^{-1}(A_{1})\cap(p_{2}\circ f)^{-1}(A_{2})\big)=\nu_{1}(A_{1})\nu_{2}(A_{2})\qquad\text{for every set $A_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}$ and $A_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}$}.

\nu
is called the tensor product and
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
through
f
and denoted by
\nu_{1}\otimes_{f}\nu_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{X}:=f^{-1}(X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2})\in\Sigma
. Let
\bar{\iota}:\bar{X}\hookrightarrow X
and
\iota:X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}\hookrightarrow X_{1}\times X_{2}
be the canonical injections, and let
\bar{\Sigma}
be the initial
\sigma
-algebra on
X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}
with respect to
\iota
.
Note that there exists a unique measurable isomorphism
\bar{f}:\bar{X}\to X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}
such that

\iota\circ\bar{f}=f\circ\bar{\iota}.

Note that
\iota(A)
lies in
\Sigma_{1}\otimes\Sigma_{2}
for each set
A
in
\bar{\Sigma}
since the projection
\iota(A)
onto
X_{1}
(resp.
X_{2}
) belongs to
\Sigma_{1}
(resp.
\Sigma_{2}
). Also
\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}\lfloor_{X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}}.

Therefore by
(ii)
, we have

(4.4)

\iota_{\#}\iota^{\#}(\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2})=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}.

Define the measure
\nu=\bar{\iota}_{\#}\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}
on
X
. Since
\bar{f}
is a measurable isomorphism and by (
4.4
), we have

(4.5)

f_{\#}\nu=f_{\#}\bar{\iota}_{\#}\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=\iota_{\#}\bar{f}_{\#}\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=\iota_{\#}\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}.

Let us prove that (
4.5
) uniquely characterises
\nu
. Let
\bar{\nu}
satisfy
f_{\#}\bar{\nu}=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}
, and observe that, we have
\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}f_{\#}\bar{\nu}=\bar{\iota}^{\#}\bar{\nu}
, whenever
\bar{\nu}
satisfies
(ii)
.
Therefore,

(4.6)

\bar{\nu}=\bar{\iota}_{\#}\bar{\iota}^{\#}\bar{\nu}=\bar{\iota}_{\#}\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}f_{\#}\bar{\nu}=\bar{\iota}_{\#}\bar{f}^{\#}\iota^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2},

which shows that
\nu
is uniquely characterised.

∎

4.3.
Tensor product and composition of maps

Let
\bar{X}_{1}
and
\bar{X}_{2}
be two sets. Let
g_{1}:X_{1}\to\bar{X}_{1}
and
g_{2}:X_{2}\to\bar{X}_{2}
be two surjective maps and let
\bar{\Sigma}_{1}
and
\bar{\Sigma}_{2}
be
\sigma
-algebras on
\bar{X}_{1}
and
\bar{X}_{2}
, which respectively make
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
measurable. Under the hypothesis of Proposition
4.1
, assume that we have

(4.7)

g^{-1}_{1}(g_{1}(S_{1}))=S_{1}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad g^{-1}_{2}(g_{2}(S_{2}))=S_{2}.

Let
\bar{X}
be a set and let
g:X\to\bar{X}
be a surjective map. Let
\bar{\Sigma}
be a
\sigma
-algebra on
\bar{X}
, which makes
g
measurable. Assume further that there exists an injective and measurable map
\bar{f}:\bar{X}\to\bar{X}_{1}\times\bar{X}_{2}
such that for every
A\in\bar{\Sigma}
, we have
\bar{f}(A)\in\bar{\Sigma}_{1}\otimes\bar{\Sigma}_{2}
, and further assume that

(4.8)

\bar{f}\circ g=(g_{1}\times g_{2})\circ f.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the same hypothesis as Proposition
4.1
, the tensor product of
(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}
and
(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}
through
\bar{f}
is well-defined, denoted by
(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{\bar{f}}(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}
, and characterised by

(4.9)

\bar{f}_{\#}[(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{\bar{f}}(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}]=(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2},

and further satisfies

(4.10)

(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{\bar{f}}(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}=g_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{f}\nu_{2}.

Proof.

Set
\bar{S}_{1}:=g_{1}(S_{1})
and
\bar{S}_{2}:=g_{2}(S_{2})
and observe that
\bar{S}_{1}\in\bar{\Sigma}_{1}
and
\bar{S}_{2}\in\bar{\Sigma}_{2}
by (
4.7
), and that

(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}(\bar{S}_{1})=0=(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}(\bar{S}_{2}).

Since
g
is surjective, we have that
{\rm Im}(\bar{f}\circ g)={\rm Im}\bar{f}
.
Therefore
\bar{X}_{1}\times\bar{X}_{2}\backslash\bar{S}_{1}\times\bar{S}_{2}\subset{\rm Im}\;\bar{f}
since
g_{1}\times g_{2}
is surjective, since
X_{1}\times X_{2}\backslash S_{1}\times S_{2}\subset{\rm Im}f
, and in view of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
). Therefore the tensor product of
(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}
and
(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}
through
\bar{f}
is well-defined by Proposition
4.1
, and is characterised by

(4.11)

\bar{f}_{\#}[(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{\bar{f}}(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}]=(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}=(g_{1}\times g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}.

Also, since
\bar{f}
is injective, we have that

(4.12)

(g_{1})_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{\bar{f}}(g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{2}=\bar{f}^{\#}(g_{1}\times g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}.

Since
f
is injective, by the characterising property of tensor product of Proposition
4.1
, we have that

(4.13)

g_{\#}\nu_{1}\otimes_{f}\nu_{2}=g_{\#}f^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}.

Notice now that

(4.14)

g_{\#}f^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=\bar{f}^{\#}(g_{1}\times g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2},

holds if and only

(4.15)

(g_{1}\times g_{2})_{\#}f_{\#}f^{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}=(g_{1}\times g_{2})_{\#}\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2},

where we have used (
4.8
) and

5.3.
Associativity of the tensor product

Let us now argue that the tensor product is associative.

Lemma 5.4

(Associativity)
.

Let
t_{1}<t_{2}
be real numbers and let
x_{1},x_{2}\in Y
. Let also
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\subset\mathbb{R}
be closed intervals such that:
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\{t_{1}\}
, and
I_{2}\cap I_{3}=\{t_{2}\}
; for every
t_{1}\in_{1}
and every
t_{2}\in I_{2}
, we have
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
; for every
t_{2}\in I_{2}
and
t_{3}\in I_{3}
, we have
t_{2}\leq t_{3}
. Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma\lfloor_{I_{i}})
for

such that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}
is concentrated on
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\lfloor_{I_{1}}\;:\;\gamma(t_{1})=x_{1}\}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}
is concentrated on
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\lfloor_{I_{3}}\;:\;\gamma(t_{2})=x_{2}\}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}
is concentrated on
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\lfloor_{I_{2}}\;:\;\gamma(t_{1})=x_{1}\text{ and }\gamma(t_{2})=x_{2}\}

(5.9)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\otimes_{(t_{1},x_{1})}[{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}]=[{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\otimes_{(t_{1},x_{1})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}]\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}.

In view of the above lemma, we will write
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\otimes_{(t_{1},x_{1})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}
.

Proof.

1. Denote
I=I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}.
By the characterising property of the tensor product in Proposition
4.1
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}
is concentrated on
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\lfloor_{I_{2}\cup I_{3}}\;:\;\gamma(t_{1})=x_{1}\}
and

4.1
each side of the identity we seek to prove are uniquely characterised by

(5.10)

\begin{split}(r^{I_{1}}\times r^{I_{2}}\times r^{I_{3}})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\otimes_{(t_{1},x_{1})}[{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}]&={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3},\\
(r^{I_{1}}\times r^{I_{2}}\times r^{I_{3}})_{\#}[{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\otimes_{(t_{1},x_{1})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}]\otimes_{(t_{2},x_{2})}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3}&={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{2}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{3},\end{split}

whence they must be equal, and this proves the thesis.
∎

6.1.
The space

We endow

by

(6.2)

\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}:=Z_{\mu}/{\sim}.

Elements of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
will be designated by bold font lowercase Greek letters for instance
\nu
, and
\{\nu_{x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}
will then designate an element in the equivalence class of
\nu
.

6.1.1.
An integral on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

Let us introduce an integral on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with respect to a test function.
The class of test functions
\mathscr{T}
will be a countable family of nonnegative compactly supported functions of
Y
, whose integral against
\mu
equals one.
Indeed
C_{c}(Y)
is separable by Theorem
2.25
. Therefore the non-empty subspace
\{\phi\in C_{c}(Y)\;:\;\int_{Y}\phi(y)\mu(dy)=1,\;\phi\geq 0\}
is also separable, of which we let
\mathscr{T}
be a countable and dense subset.

Given
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, we define the integral

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}:\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}\ni{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}\longmapsto\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma),

as the unique Radon measure in
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
– which is canonically identified to continuous positive linear functional on
C_{c}(\Gamma)
– satisfying

(6.3)

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}),\Phi\rangle=\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu_{x}(d\gamma)\phi(x)\mu(dx)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma),

for some
\{\nu_{x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}
in the equivalence class of
\nu
.
We will also write

(6.4)

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})=\int_{Y}\nu_{x}\phi(x)\mu(dx).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. Then
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})
is well-defined and
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

1. Let us prove that
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})
is well-defined. Let
\{\nu_{x}^{1}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu},\{\nu_{x}^{2}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\in Z_{\mu}
such that
\nu_{x}^{1}=\nu^{2}_{x}
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
. Then we have

(6.5)

\int_{Y}\nu^{1}_{x}\phi(x)\mu(dx)=\int_{Y}\nu^{2}_{x}\phi(x)\mu(dx),

whence by Fubini’s theorem, we get

(6.6)

\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu^{1}_{x}(d\gamma)\phi(x)\mu(dx)=\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu^{2}_{x}(d\gamma)\phi(x)\mu(dx)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma),

whereby
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})
is well-defined.

2. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nu_{x}(\{\gamma(t)=x\})=1
. Let
K
be compact in
Y
such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset K
. Recall that
e_{t}^{-1}(K)
is compact by Section
3.4.1
So there exists
\Phi_{0}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
such that
\Phi_{0}\equiv 1
on
e_{t}^{-1}(K)
. Then since
\Phi_{0}\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{supp}\nu_{x}
for every
x\in e_{t}^{-1}(K)
, we have

(6.7)

\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{0}(\gamma)\nu_{x}(d\gamma)\phi(x)\mu(dx)=\int_{Y}\phi(x)\mu(dx)=1,

and also for every
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
such that
\Phi\equiv 0
on
e_{t}^{-1}(K)
, we have

(6.8)

\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu_{x}(d\gamma)\phi(x)\mu(dx)=0.

Therefore, in view of the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani characterisation of Radon measures,
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})(e^{-1}_{t}(K))=1
and
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})(e^{-1}_{t}(K)^{c})=0
, whence
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}})\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
.
∎

6.1.2.
A uniform structure on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

Let us now endow
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with the intial uniformity on the set
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
with respect to the maps
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}
where
\phi
runs through
\mathscr{T}
, i.e.

uniformly continuous for every

.

Let us describe a fundamental system of entourages of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.
For each
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, and each entourage
V
of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
, we define the set

(6.9)

V_{\phi}:=\Big\{({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{2})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}\times\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}\;:\;\Big(\int_{Y}\nu^{1}_{y}\phi(y)\mu(dy),\int_{Y}\nu^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\mu(dy)\Big)\in V\Big\}.

The family of sets
\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}}

where
V
runs through the entourages the fundamental system of entourages
\mathfrak{V}
of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
described in Section
3.4.3
,

runs through

and
\phi_{i}
run in

forms a fundamental system of entourages of the uniformity on

.

6.2.

\delta

In this section, we will define a disintegration map
\delta
on
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
and collect properties of this map.

6.2.1.
The space of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

Let
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
denote the uniform space of compact convergence on the set of continuous maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.
A fundamental system of entourage of
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
is then given by the sets
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(I;\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}})
where
\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}}
runs in the fundamental system of entourages described in Section
6.1.2
and
I
runs in the compacts in
\mathbb{R}
.

6.2.2.
Definition of the disintegration map
\delta

Given
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by essential uniqueness of the disintegration, there exists a unique element in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
, which we shall denote by
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}
, and is such that every disintegration
\{\eta_{t,x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
belongs to the equivalence class
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}
.
We now define the disintegration map
\delta:\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\longrightarrow C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
by
\delta({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}):\mathbb{R}\ni t\longmapsto{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.

6.2.3.
Properties of the disintegration map
\delta

The following lemma gives useful properties of the disintegration map
\delta
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The map
\delta
is well-defined, uniformly continuous and its image is precompact in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
.

Proof.

To prove that
\delta
is well-defined, it suffices to show that for every
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
, the map

f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}:\mathbb{R}\ni t\longmapsto{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

is continuous.
Recall that the uniformity on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is initial with respect to the maps
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}
where
\phi
runs in
\mathscr{T}
.
So by Proposition
2.15
, we have that
f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}
is continuous, if and only if
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\circ f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}
are continuous for each
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, whence it suffices to prove the latter to get well-definedness.

1. To prove that that
\delta
is well-defined – and also that the image of
\delta
is precompact in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
– we first claim that for each
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
and each compact interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the family

(6.10)

\Big\{f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}:I\ni t\ni\longmapsto\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t})\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\Big\}_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)}

is equicontinuous.
Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be compact.
Let
t_{0}\in I
and let
V\in\mathfrak{V}
be an entourage of the fundamental system of the uniformity on
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
described in Section
3.4.3
. It suffices to exhibit an open neighborhood
J\subset I
of
t_{0}
such that for every
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
, and every
t\in J
we have

(6.11)

(f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}_{\phi}(t_{0}),f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}_{\phi}(t))\in V.

Recall that
V
has the form

(6.12)

V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\Big\{({\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}^{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\;:\;\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}^{1}(d\gamma)-\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}^{2}(d\gamma)\Big|<\alpha\Big\}.

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
and define the measure
\nu:=(e_{t_{0}},e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
on
Y\times Y
. Define the projection maps

(6.13)

\begin{split}&q_{1}:Y\times Y\ni(y_{1},y_{2})\longmapsto y_{1}\in Y,\\
&q_{2}:Y\times Y\ni(y_{1},y_{2})\longmapsto y_{2}\in Y.\\
\end{split}

Let
\{\nu_{y}\}_{y\in Y}
be a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
q_{2}
and
\mu
.
Let
K
be compact in
Y
such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset K
and
e_{t_{0}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{i})\subset K
.
Then
e_{t_{0}}^{-1}(K)
is compact in
\Gamma
by Section
3.4.1
, which from we deduce the following.

(Obs)

Observe that for each entourage
V^{\prime}
of
Y
there exists an open neighborhood
J
of
t_{0}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\nu\cap K\times Y\subset V^{\prime}
whenever
t\in J
.

Let also
\{\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\}_{(x,y)\in Y\times Y}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t_{0}}\times e_{t}
and
\nu
.
Notice that for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in Y
, we have

(6.14)

\eta_{t,y}=\int_{Y}\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\nu_{y}(dx).

We then have

(6.15)

\begin{split}f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}_{\phi}(t)=\int_{Y}\eta_{t,y}\phi(y)\mu(dy)&=\int_{Y\times Y}\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\phi(y)\nu_{y}(dx)\mu(dy)\\
&=\int_{Y\times Y}\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\phi(y)\nu(dx,dy).\end{split}

Similarly, we also have

(6.16)

f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}_{\phi}(t_{0})=\int_{Y\times Y}\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\phi(x)\nu(dx,dy).

Let us define the functions
\phi_{x}(x,y):=\phi(x)
and
\phi_{y}(x,y):=\phi(y)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By uniform continuity of
\phi
on
Y
, there exists an entourage
V^{\prime}
of
Y
such that

(6.17)

\sup_{(x,y)\in V^{\prime}}|\phi_{x}(x,y)-\phi_{y}(x,y)|<\varepsilon.

In view of (Obs), there is an open neighborhood
J
of
t_{0}
such that for each
t\in J
, we have

(6.18)

\sup_{(x,y)\in\operatorname{supp}\nu\cap K\times Y}|\phi_{x}(x,y)-\phi_{y}(x,y)|<\varepsilon.

Therefore,

(6.19)

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)[f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t)](d\gamma)-\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)[f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t_{0})](d\gamma)\Big|&=\Big|\int_{Y\times Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}(d\gamma)(\phi_{x}(x,y)-\phi_{y}(x,y))\nu(dx,dy)\Big|\\
&=\Big|\int_{K\times Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}(d\gamma)(\phi_{x}(x,y)-\phi_{y}(x,y))\nu(dx,dy)\Big|\\
&\leq\varepsilon\|\Phi_{i}\|_{C^{0}}\mu(K).\end{split}

where in the second equality, we have used that
\operatorname{supp}\eta_{t_{0},x;t,y}\cap\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{i}=\emptyset
whenever
x\notin K
.
Now choose
\varepsilon>0
, and an open neighborhood
J
of
t_{0}
correspondingly such that for every
t\in J
, we have

(6.20)

\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)[f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t)](d\gamma)-\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)[f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t_{0})](d\gamma)\Big|<\alpha.

Then for every
t\in J
, we have

(6.21)

(f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t_{0}),f_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}(t))\in V.

Since
t_{0}
was arbitrary in
I
, and
\eta
was arbitrary in
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
, the claim follows. This clearly implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\circ f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}
are continuous for each
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
whereby well-definedness of
\delta
follows.

2. Let us prove that the image of
\delta
is precompact in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
. By Ascoli’s theorem, it suffices to show

(i)

equicontinuity for each
I\subset\mathbb{R}
compact of the family of maps

(6.22)

\Big\{f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}:I\ni t\longmapsto{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}\}_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)};

(ii)

for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
{\mathbf{H}}(t):=\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\;:\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\}
is precompact in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.

As
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is equipped with the initial uniformity with respect to the maps
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}
where
\phi
runs in
\mathscr{T}
, for every
I
compact in
\mathbb{R},
by Proposition
2.15
, the family
\{f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}\;:\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\}
is equicontinuous, if and only if the families
\{\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\circ f^{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},I}\;:\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\}
are equicontinuous for every
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, and the latter was shown in 1. of this proof.
Let us show
(ii)
.

2.bis. Let us show that the family
\{\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\}_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}
separates
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
such that
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{1})=\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{2})
for every
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
. Let
\mathscr{D}
be a countable and dense subfamily of
C_{c}(\Gamma)
.
Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{D},
and let
\{\nu^{1}_{x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}
and
\{\nu^{2}_{x}\}_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}
be in the equivalence class of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{1}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{2}
respectively. Observe that for every
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, we have

(6.23)

\int_{Y}\phi(x)\Big[\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu^{1}_{x}(d\gamma)\Big]\mu(dx)=\int_{Y}\phi(x)\Big[\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu^{2}_{x}(d\gamma)\Big]\mu(dx).

Therefore, as
\mathscr{T}
is dense in
\{\phi\in C_{c}(Y):\int_{Y}\phi(y)\mu(dy)=1,\;\phi\geq 0\}
, by Lusin’s theorem, there exists a set
N_{\Phi}\subset Y
such that
\mu(N_{\Phi})=0
and for every
x\in Y\backslash N_{\Phi}
, we have

(6.24)

\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu_{x}^{1}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu^{2}_{x}(d\gamma).

Let now
N:=\cup_{\Phi\in\mathscr{D}}N_{\Phi}
, which is also a
\mu
-negligeable set. Then by density of
\mathscr{D}
in
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, for every
x\in Y\backslash N
, we have
\nu^{1}_{x}=\nu^{2}_{x}.
, whence
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{1}={\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{2}
.

2.ter. Let us conclude
(ii)
. For every
\phi\in\mathscr{T}
, let
K_{\phi}\subset Y
be compact such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset K_{\phi}
. Then the set
e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi})
is compact in
\Gamma
by Section
3.4.1
, and we have that
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mathbf{H}}(t))\subset\mathcal{P}(e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi}))
. By Proposition
3.4
, the space
\mathcal{P}(e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi}))
is vaguely compact.
Note also that the product topological space
\Pi_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}\mathcal{P}(e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi}))
is compact by Tychonov’s theorem for countable product. (This theorem can be proved within Zermalo-Fraenkel and the Axiom of Dependant Choice by
[
13
, Proposition 4.72]
). Therefore
(\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mathbf{H}}(t)))_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}\subset\Pi_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}\mathcal{P}(e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi}))
is precompact.
Observe also that the map

\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}\ni{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}\longmapsto(\mathcal{I}_{\phi}({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}))_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}\in\Pi_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}\mathcal{P}(e_{t}^{-1}(K_{\phi}))

is a homeomorphism onto its image since
\{\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\}_{\phi\in\mathscr{T}}
separates
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
. Therefore
{\mathbf{H}}(t)
is precompact in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
, and so the image of
\delta
is precompact in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
.

3. Let us finally prove uniform continuity of
\delta
.
Recall the fundamental system of entourages
\mathfrak{V}
of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
described in Section
3.4.3
, and let
V\in\mathfrak{V}
.
Let
\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}}
be an entourage in the fundamental system of entourages of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
described in Section
6.1.2
. Furthermore, let
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(I;\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}})
be an entourage in the fundamental system of
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
described in Section
6.2.1
. Let
({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{2})\in{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(I;\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}})
, then we have
(\mathcal{I}_{\phi_{i}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}^{1}),\mathcal{I}_{\phi_{i}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}^{2}))\in V_{\phi_{i}}
for every
t\in I
and every
i=1,\dots,N
.

By 1. of this proof, for every
t\in I
, there exists an open neighborhood
J_{t}\subset I
containing
t
such that for every
s\in J_{t}
, every
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
, and every
i=1,\dots,N
, we have

(6.25)

\Big|\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta_{t,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)-\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta_{s,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)\Big|<\alpha/3.

Therefore, for every
t\in I
, whenever we have

(6.26)

\Big|\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta_{t,x}^{1}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)-\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta_{t,x}^{2}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)\Big|<\alpha/3,

then for every
s\in J_{t}
, we have

(6.27)

\Big|\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{1}_{s,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)-\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{2}_{s,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)\Big|<\alpha.

By compactness of
I
, there exists a finite set
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\}\subset I
such that
\{J_{t_{j}}\}_{1\leq j\leq k}
is an open cover of
I
, which is such that for every
j=1,\dots,k
, every
s\in J_{t_{j}}
, and every
({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
such that (
6.26
) holds with
t=t_{j}
, we have

(6.28)

\Big|\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{1}_{s,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)-\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{2}_{s,x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)\Big|<\alpha\quad\forall i=1,\dots,N.

Now define the following entourage of
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}({\Gamma})

(6.29)

\begin{split}V^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\Big\{&({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\;:\;\\
&\Big|\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{1}_{t_{j},x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)-\int_{Y}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(\gamma)\phi(x)\eta^{2}_{t_{j},x}(d\gamma)\mu(dx)\Big|<\alpha/3\Big\}.\end{split}

In view of the above argument,
({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{2})\in V^{\prime}
implies that for every
t\in I
, we have
({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}^{1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}^{2})\in\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}}
, whence
(\delta({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{1}),\delta({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{2}))\in{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(I;\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}})
.
As
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(I;\cap_{i=1}^{N}V_{\phi_{i}})
was an arbitrary entourage in the fundamental system of
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
described in Section
6.2.1
, the map
\delta
is uniformly continuous.

∎

6.3.
The space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
of continuous disintegrations with respect to
\mu

Let us define the set

(6.30)

\mathcal{X}_{\mu}:=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}:\operatorname{supp}\mu\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\;:\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}\text{ is continuous and $\nu_{x}(\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;:\;\gamma(t)=x\})=1$}\quad\forall x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\Big\}.

6.3.1.
A uniform structure on
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}

We endow
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
with the uniformity of compact convergence.
The sets
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(K;V)
where
V
runs in the fundamental system of entourages of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
described in Section
3.4
and
K
runs in the compacts in

form a fundamental system of entourages of the uniformity on
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
. As
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
is a metrizable and separable uniform space by Lemma
3.2
, the uniform space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
is metrizable and separable in view of Theorem
2.25
.

6.3.2.
Relation between the uniformities
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

Let

The family of of maps

6.4.
Propagating invariance under the Markov opertor

Let

and
x\in Y

Recall that
r^{I}

\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
to
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma\lfloor_{I})
for every interval

We define
\Gamma_{t,x}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;:\;\gamma(t)=x\}
which is endowed with the induced uniform structure. By a slight abuse of notation, the induced restriction map from
\Gamma_{t,x}
to
\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{I}
is also designated by

, and for every

\nu^{I}:=(r^{I})_{\#}\nu
. We then define the Markov opertor

from
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
to
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
by

M_{t,x}(\nu)=\nu^{(-\infty,t]}\otimes_{(t,x)}\nu^{[t,+\infty)}
where we recall that, in the sense of Proposition
4.1
, the tensor product here is through the map
f_{t}:\Gamma_{t,x}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{(-\infty,t]}\times\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{[t,+\infty)}
given by

, which is an isomorphism of uniform spaces.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
and let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
. Then
M_{t,x}\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to
M_{t,x}\nu
as
n\to+\infty
, if and only if
\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\nu
as
n\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Firstly by Proposition
3.4
and Section
3.4.1
, we have that
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
is vaguely compact so it suffices to check vague convergence in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
.
From the characterisation of the tensor product, for every
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
we have

(6.36)

(f_{t})_{\#}M_{t,x}\bar{\nu}=\bar{\nu}^{(-\infty,t]}\times\bar{\nu}^{[t,+\infty)},

which implies that

(6.37)

M_{t,x}\bar{\nu}=(f_{t})^{\#}\bar{\nu}^{(-\infty,t]}\times\bar{\nu}^{[t,+\infty)}.

Let
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma_{t,x})
and notice that

(6.38)

\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}}\Phi(\gamma)M_{t,x}\nu_{n}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,x}}\int_{\Gamma^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,x}}\Phi(f_{t}^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}))\nu_{n}^{(-\infty,t]}(d\gamma_{1})\nu_{n}^{[t,+\infty)}(d\gamma_{2}).

Since
f_{t}=r^{(-\infty,t]}\times r^{[t,+\infty)}
is an isomorphism of uniform spaces, the pushforward map
(f_{t})_{\#}
is an homeomorphism of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{t,x})
onto
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,x}\times\Gamma^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,x})
, whereby
\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to
\nu
, if and only if
\nu^{(-\infty,t]}_{n}\times\nu_{n}^{[t,+\infty)}
converges vaguely to
\nu^{(-\infty,t]}\times\nu^{[t,+\infty)}
, whence by Fubini’s theorem

(6.39)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}}\Phi(\gamma)M_{t,x}\nu_{n}(d\gamma)&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{[t,+\infty)}}\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{(-\infty,t]}}\Phi(f_{t}^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}))\nu_{n}^{(-\infty,t]}(d\gamma_{1})\nu_{n}^{[t,+\infty)}(d\gamma_{2})\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{[t,+\infty)}}\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}\lfloor_{(-\infty,t]}}\Phi(f_{t}^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}))\nu^{(-\infty,t]}(d\gamma_{1})\nu^{[t,+\infty)}(d\gamma_{2})\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{t,x}}\Phi(\gamma)M_{t,x}\nu(d\gamma).\end{split}

Since
\Phi
was arbitrary, it follows that
\nu_{n}^{(-\infty,t]}\times\nu_{n}^{[t,+\infty)}
converges vaguely to
\nu^{(-\infty,t]}\times\nu^{[t,+\infty)}
as
n\to+\infty
, if and only if
M_{t,x}\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to
M_{t,x}\nu
as
n\to+\infty
and the thesis follows.
∎

For every

and every interval

, denote

(Obs-
\Phi
)

Lemma 6.6
.

1.

The claim follows.

(b_{h})_{\#}
from
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)

\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)

\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)

(b_{h})_{\#}\bar{\nu}

\bar{\nu}

\Phi

\gamma

\Gamma

\Phi

converges vaguely to

(6.44)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(b_{t-t_{n}}\gamma)\nu_{n}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu(d\gamma).

3.bis. In view of Lemma
6.5

formed as the union of the Markov

-hulls of
\eta
over the family of dense and countable subsets
D\subset\mathbb{R}
. We shall denote the Markov hull of
\eta
by
\mathfrak{M}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
, and we can write it as

(6.51)

\mathfrak{M}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})=\bigcup_{D\in\mathfrak{D}}\mathfrak{M}_{D}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}),

where
\mathfrak{D}\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
is the family of subsets of
\mathbb{R}
which are dense and countable.

Recalling the canonical injection
\iota:\mathcal{X}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
, we define the induced injection

\iota^{*}:\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})\ni\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\longmapsto\{\iota{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}).

Lemma 6.14
.

The map
\iota^{*}
is well-defined and continuous. Furthermore
\iota^{*}\circ\delta^{\prime}=\delta\lfloor_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mu}}
.

Proof.

To see that
\iota^{*}
is well-defined, notice that the map
\mathbb{R}\ni t\longmapsto\iota({\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is continuous, since
\mathbb{R}\ni t\longmapsto{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
is continuous and
\iota:\mathcal{X}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
is also continuous. As
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
and
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
are metrizable, it suffices to verify the sequential characterisation of continuity. Let
(\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{n}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence converging in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
to
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Let
I
be a compact in
\mathbb{R}
, and we then have that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{n}_{t}\}_{t\in I}
converges in
\mathcal{C}_{u}(I;\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
to
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in I}
as
n\to+\infty
.
Since
\iota
is uniformly continuous, this implies that
\{\iota{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{n}_{t}\}_{t\in I}
converges in
\mathcal{C}_{u}(I;\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
to
\{\iota{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in I}
as
n\to+\infty
, whence
\{\iota{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{n}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
converges in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
to
\{\iota{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
as
n\to+\infty
. The identity
\iota^{*}\circ\delta^{\prime}=\delta\lfloor_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mu}}
can be checked directly.
∎

We now prove that every element of the Markov hull of a Markov regular element in
\mathcal{Y}_{\mu}
satisfies the strong Markov property.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu}
be Markov regular. Then every element in the Markov hull of
\eta
satisfies the strong Markov property.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
be in the Markov hull of
\eta
. Then there exists a countable, dense subset
D
of
\mathbb{R}
and a set ordering
(D_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
of
D
such that, modulo a subsequence
M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
converges vaguely to
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
as
N\to+\infty
. By Lemma
6.2
, we also have that
\delta(M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))
converges to
\delta(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
as
N\to+\infty
.

1. We claim that
\delta^{\prime}(M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))
converges to
\delta^{\prime}(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
as
N\to+\infty
.
Let
\xi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be an increasing map. Recall that the uniform space
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
is metrizable by Theorem
2.25
, so by Lemma
6.11
, there exists another increasing map
\zeta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\delta^{\prime}(M_{D_{(\zeta\circ\xi)(N)}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))
converges in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
as
N\to+\infty
to some element
{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}=\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}.
Observe that
\iota^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
and that
\delta(M_{D_{(\zeta\circ\xi)(N)}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
converges in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{Z}_{\mu})
to
\iota^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}
as
N\to+\infty
by Lemma
6.14
. Therefore, we have
\iota^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}=\delta(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}),
whence
{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}=\delta^{\prime}(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
. By the subsubsequence lemma, it follows that
\delta^{\prime}(M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))
converges to
\rho
in
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
as
N\to+\infty
.

2. Let us now show that
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
satisfies the strong Markov property.
Let
K
be a compact in
\operatorname{supp}\mu
, let
(V_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a decreasing fundamental system of entourages of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
, i.e.
V_{k+1}\subset V_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(K;V_{k})
be an entourage of
\mathcal{X}_{\mu},
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists an entourage
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}_{k}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
such that
{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}_{k}^{\prime}\circ{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}_{k}^{\prime}\subset{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(K;V_{k})
by Axiom
(U_{3})
of Definition
2.5
. Also there exists
N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
t_{k}\in D_{N_{k}}\cap[t-1,t+1]
such that:

•

t_{k}\to t
as
k\to+\infty
thanks to density of
D
in
[t-1,t+1]
;

•

(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t_{k}},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t})\in{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}_{k}^{\prime}
thanks to uniform continuity of
[t-1,t+1]\ni t\longmapsto{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
;

•

((M_{D_{N_{k}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t_{k}},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t_{k}})\in{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}_{k}^{\prime}
thanks to convergence of
\delta^{\prime}(M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))
to
\delta^{\prime}(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
in
\mathcal{C}_{u}([t-1,t+1];\mathcal{X}_{\mu})
.

Therefore, we have
((M_{D_{N_{k}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t_{k}},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t_{k}})\in{\mbox{\boldmath$W$}}(K;V_{k}),
which implies that for every
x\in K
, we have
(M_{D_{N_{k}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t_{k},x},\bar{\eta}_{t,x})\in V_{k}
. Setting
\nu_{k}=(M_{D_{N_{k}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t_{k},x}
and
\nu=\bar{\eta}_{t,x}
, the hypothesis of Lemma
6.6
are satisfied, so that we have
M_{t,x}\bar{\eta}_{t,x}=\bar{\eta}_{t,x}
for every
x\in K
. As
K
and
t
were arbitrary, this proves the thesis.
∎

Let us now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\eta
be Markov regular, and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}
be dense and countable, and let
(D_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be a set ordering of
D
. Note that
M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, whence by Lemma
3.7
,
(M_{D_{N}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has at least one accumulation point in

,
which shows that the Markov hull of

is non-empty. Lemma
6.15
then shows that every element of the Markov hull of

satisfies the strong Markov property.
∎

7.
Translation invariant measures

Let
(Y,\cdot)
be a locally compact Polish group. Let
\mu
be the left Haar measure on
Y
.
\mu
is uniquely determined up to a multiplicative constant.
Recall that
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
is the uniform space of compact convergence.
Let
\Gamma
be as in Section
3.4.1
.
Define the left action of
Y
on
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
by
Y\times\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)\ni(y,\gamma)\longmapsto y\cdot\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)
. Assume additionally that
\Gamma
is such that the induced left action of
Y
on
\Gamma
is transitive.
(All this section will study left Haar measures and left actions
Y
, but can be modified straightforwardly to address the case of right Haar measures and right actions on
Y
.)

7.1.
Left translation of paths

We define the left translation of paths

l_{y}:\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y)\ni\gamma\longmapsto y\cdot\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R};Y),

and by transitivity of the action of
Y
on
\Gamma
, the restriction
l_{y}\lfloor_{\Gamma}
of
l_{y}
to
\Gamma
is bijective onto
\Gamma
.
Denote by
(l_{y})_{\#}
the induced pushforward map from
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
to
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, and notice that
(l_{y})_{\#}\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
y\in Y
. Then the map
(l_{y})_{\#}:\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
is vaguely continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to check the sequential characterisation of continuity since
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
is metrizable by Lemma
3.2
.
Let
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
converging vaguely to
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
, and let
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
. Observe that
\Phi\circ l_{y}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
, whence

(7.1)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)(l_{y})_{\#}\nu_{n}(d\gamma)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(l_{y}\gamma)\nu_{n}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(l_{y}\gamma)\nu(d\gamma).

Thus
(l_{y})_{\#}\nu_{n}
converges vaguely to
(l_{y})_{\#}\nu
as
n\to+\infty
. This proves the thesis.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then
l_{x}\gamma
converges to
l_{y}\gamma
in
\Gamma
as
x\to y
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can take
y=e.
By continuity of the group multiplication, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
x\cdot\gamma(t)
converges to
\gamma(t)
in
Y
as
x\to e
, i.e. pointwise convergence. Since
Y
is locally compact, there exists a compact neighborhood
K
in
Y
containing
\gamma(0)
. Also
e_{0}^{-1}(K)
is compact in
\Gamma
. Therefore
l_{x}\gamma
converges
\gamma
in
\Gamma
as
x\to e
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
. Then
(l_{x})_{\#}\nu
converges vaguely to
(l_{y})_{\#}\nu
as
x\to y.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
7.1
, without loss of generality, we take
y=e
. Let
K
be a compact neighborhood of
e
, and
let
\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
. Observe that the family
\{\Phi\circ l_{x}\}_{x\in K}
is compact in
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, therefore
\Phi\circ l_{x}
converges to
\Phi
in
C_{c}(\Gamma)
as
x\to e
, which implies that

(7.2)

\lim_{x\to e}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(l_{x}(\gamma))\nu(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)\nu(d\gamma),

and the thesis follows since
\Phi
was arbitrary.
∎

7.2.
Structural property of translation invariance

Let us define translation invariant elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
.

Definition 7.4
.

We shall say that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
is (left) translation invariant, if for each
y\in Y
we have
(l_{y})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
.

The following structural result for translation invariant elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
will be useful.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
be translation invariant. Then for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique
\eta_{t,e}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
such that
\{(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,e}\}_{y\in Y}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
.

Under the hypothesis of the above lemma, in view of Lemma
7.3
, we shall call
\{(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,e}\}_{y\in Y}

the
disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a countable family of Borel sets generating the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\Gamma
. Note that such a family exists by separability of
\Gamma
. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\{\eta_{t,x}\}_{x\in Y}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
. Then we have

(7.3)

\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,x}\mu(dx)=(l_{y})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{Y}\eta_{t,x}\mu(dx).

Let
B
be a Borel set in
Y
and let
A\in\mathscr{G}
. We then have

(7.4)

\int_{Y}\eta_{t,x}(A\cap\{\gamma(t)\in B\})\mu(dx)=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,x}(A\cap\{\gamma(t)\in B\})\mu(dx).

Since
\eta_{t,x}
is concentrated on
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;:\;\gamma(t)=x\}
for every
x\in Y
, we get

(7.5)

\int_{B}\eta_{t,x}(A)\mu(dx)=\int_{y^{-1}\cdot B}(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,x}(A)\mu(dx).

Since
B
is an arbitrary Borel set in
Y
, by Lusin’s theorem there exists a
\mu
-negligeable set
N_{A}\subset Y
such that for every
x\in N_{A}
, we have

(7.6)

\eta_{t,x}(A)=(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,y^{-1}\cdot x}(A).

Define now

N:=\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{G}}N_{A},

which is also a
\mu
-negligeable set, and since
\mathscr{G}
generates the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\Gamma
, we have

(7.7)

\eta_{t,x}=(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,y^{-1}\cdot x}\qquad\forall x\in Y\backslash N.

In particular, choosing
y=x
, we get

(7.8)

\eta_{t,x}=(l_{x})_{\#}\eta_{t,e}\qquad\forall x\in Y\backslash N,

whence
\{(l_{x})_{\#}\eta_{t,e}\}_{x\in Y}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
, and by essential uniqueness of disintegrations
\eta_{t,e}
is uniquely determined.
∎

7.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Given a subset
I
of
\mathbb{R}
, we define the subset
H^{I}
of disintegrations at time
t\in I
of translation invariant elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(\Gamma)
by

(7.9)

H^{I}:=\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}\;:\;\text{ ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is translation invariant and $t\in I$}\}.

‘

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
I
be a compact interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Then the subset
H^{I}
is precompact in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

1. By Ascoli’s theorem, we have to check that:

(i)

for each compact
A
in
Y
, the set
H^{I}\lfloor_{A}
of restrictions to
A
of functions of
H^{I}
is equicontinuous;

(ii)

for each
y\in Y
, the set
H^{I}(y)\subset\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
of points
\eta_{t,y}
is vaguely precompact.

2. Let us check
(i)
. Let
A
be a compact in
Y
. Let
V
be an entourage in the fundamental system
\mathfrak{V}
of
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
described in Section
3.4.3
of the form

(7.10)

V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}{\rm d}_{\Phi_{i}}^{-1}([0,\alpha]).

Let
t\in I
, and let
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}\in H^{I}
. Then we have that
(\eta_{t,x},\eta_{t,y})=((l_{x})_{\#}\eta_{t,e},(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,e})\in V
for every
x,y\in A
, if and only if

(7.11)

\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(l_{x}\gamma)\eta_{t,e}(d\gamma)-\int_{\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(l_{y}\gamma)\eta_{t,e}(d\gamma)\Big|<\alpha\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,N.

In view of Lemma
7.2
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have that
l_{x}\gamma
converges to
l_{y}\gamma
in
\Gamma
as
x\to y
. Therefore, there is an open neighborhood
\mathscr{N}(y)\subset Y
of
y
such that for every
i=1,\dots,N
and every
x\in\mathscr{N}(y)
we have
\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\Phi_{i}(l_{x}\gamma)-\Phi_{i}(l_{y}\gamma)|<\alpha
, whence (
7.11
) holds, i.e.
(\eta_{t,x},\eta_{t,y})\in V
. As
V
is arbitrary in
\mathfrak{V}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{t}
is arbitrary in
H^{I}
and
y
is arbitrary in
Y
, it follows that
H^{I}\lfloor_{A}
is equicontinuous.

2.bis. Let us check
(ii)
. Let
y\in Y
and recall that
\bigcup_{t\in I}e_{t}^{-1}(\{y\})\subset\Gamma
is compact. Observe now that
H^{I}(y)
is contained in
\mathcal{P}(\bigcup_{t\in I}e_{t}^{-1}(\{y\}))
. By Proposition
3.4
,
\mathcal{P}(\bigcup_{t\in I}e_{t}^{-1}(\{y\}))
is compact.

This proves the thesis.
∎

We can conclude this paper with the following proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

1. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us show that if
\eta
is translation invariant, then
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
is also translation invariant. In view of Lemma
7.5
, we let
\{(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,e}\}_{y\in Y}
be the disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
. Then we have by Lemma
4.2
that

(7.12)

M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,y)}(l_{y})_{\#}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}\mu(dy)=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}]\mu(dy).

So for every
x\in Y
, and every Borel set
B
in
\Gamma
, we have

(7.13)

\begin{split}(l_{x})_{\#}M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})(B)&=\int_{Y}(l_{x})_{\#}(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}](B)\mu(dy)\\
&=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}](x^{-1}\cdot B)\mu(dy)\\
&=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}](B)(x^{-1}\cdot)_{\#}\mu(dy)\\
&=\int_{Y}(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}](B)\mu(dy)\\
&=M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})(B),\end{split}

where in the second equality, we have used the definition of
l_{x}
, in the third equality we have used the definition of the pushforward of
\mu
along the map
Y\ni y\longmapsto x^{-1}\cdot y\in Y,
and in the fourth equality we have used left translation invariance of
\mu
.
Therefore
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
is translation invariant and
\{(l_{y})_{\#}[\eta^{(-\infty,t]}_{t,e}\otimes_{(t,e)}\eta^{[t,+\infty)}_{t,e}]\}_{y\in Y}
is the disintegration of
M_{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
.

2. Let
I
be compact in
\mathbb{R}
, and let
F
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}
. By 1. of this proof
M_{F}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
is translation invariant, whence by Lemma
7.5
for every
t\in I
, we have that
\{(l_{y})_{\#}\eta_{t,e;F}\}_{y\in Y}
is the disintegration of
M_{F}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})
with respect to
e_{t}
and
\mu
for some
\eta_{t,e;F}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
, so that by Lemma
7.3
,
(M_{F}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
, and therefore
(M_{F}({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}))_{t}\in H^{I}
. By Lemma
7.6
, the closure of
H^{I}
is compact. As
I
and
F
were arbitrary, this shows that
\eta
is Markov regular.
∎
