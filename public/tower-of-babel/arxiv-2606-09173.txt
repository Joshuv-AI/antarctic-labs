Jingjing CAI, Zhihui LIU

Abstract
. We establish sharp, uniform-in-time strong error estimates for a nonlinearity-explicit tamed finite element method (FEM) applied to a class of superlinear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by multiplicative noise, including the stochastic Allen–Cahn equation with a moderately thick interface. This tamed-FEM was first introduced in [Z. Liu and J. Shen, arXiv:2502.19117] to ensure long-time unconditional stability and to preserve the Lyapunov structure of this class of SPDEs. We further prove that the scheme is exponentially ergodic and derive the convergence rate between the exact invariant measure and its numerical counterpart in the Wasserstein-2 distance. Finally, we present numerical experiments that verify the ergodicity as well as the sharpness and time-independence of the strong convergence rates for this tamed-FEM.

1.
Introduction

There is a well-developed theory of strong error estimates for numerical approximations of SPDEs with Lipschitz coefficients over a finite time horizon; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
13
]
and references therein. This theory has recently been extended to SPDEs with monotone and superlinear coefficients driven by multiplicative noise in
[
19
,
16
]
. In the past decade, many researchers have analyzed this question for SPDEs with non-Lipschitz coefficients, such as the 2D stochastic Navier–Stokes equation
[
9
]
, the 1D stochastic Burgers equation and the Cahn–Hilliard–Cook equation
[
12
]
, and the 1D stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
5
,
6
]
, by exploiting the exponential integrability property. In contrast, much less is known about strong error estimates for numerical approximations of SPDEs that remain valid over infinite time horizons.

For superlinear SPDEs, long-time analysis is particularly delicate.
It is well known that the backward Euler method studied in
[
19
]
and other implicit schemes for SPDEs often entail high computational costs; nonlinearity-explicit schemes are generally preferred to reduce these costs. However, it was demonstrated in
[
2
]
that the classical Euler–Maruyama scheme and its Galerkin-based full discretizations, when applied to a class of superlinear SPDEs, lead to a blow-up of the
p
-th moment for all
p\geq 2
.

To prevent blow-up,
[
17
]
recently constructed the first nonlinearity-explicit tamed-FEM for a class of superlinear SPDEs driven by multiplicative noise. The authors showed that this tamed-FEM is unconditionally stable over infinite time intervals and uniquely ergodic, and that it strongly converges to the original SPDE at an optimal rate on any finite time interval. However, whether these rates remain valid over an infinite time horizon remains unknown, posing an intriguing and challenging problem.

This challenge has driven significant research over the past decade, inspiring the development and long-time numerical analysis of the second-order parabolic SPDE

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(t,\xi)=(\Delta X(t,\xi)+f(X(t,\xi))){\rm d}t+g(X(t,\xi)){\rm d}W(t,\xi),\quad(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathscr{O},

with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (DBC)
X(t,\xi)=0
,
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\mathscr{O}
, and initial condition
X(0,\xi)=X_{0}(\xi)
,
\xi\in\mathscr{O}
. Here,
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) is a bounded open set with a piecewise smooth boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
;
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be of monotone type with superlinear growth, with
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
Lipschitz continuous in the standard infinite-dimensional sense; and
W
is an infinite-dimensional Wiener process (cf. Section
2
for details). Equation (
1.1
) includes the stochastic Allen–Cahn equation, which arises from phase transitions in materials science under stochastic perturbations, corresponding to
f(\xi)=\epsilon^{-2}(\xi-\xi^{3})
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\epsilon>0
is the interface thickness; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
18
,
15
,
16
,
20
]
and references therein.

To derive uniform-in-time strong error estimates of the tamed-FEM (see Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
), a key step is the rigorous analysis of its uniform-in-time unconditional stability in the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm (see Proposition
3.1
). This is followed by a sequence of new estimates for the tamed function (Lemma
3.2
), in particular the uniform upper bound (
3.8
) for
f_{\tau}^{\prime}+\tau|f_{\tau}^{\prime}|^{2}
, an estimate that is not available for
|f_{\tau}^{\prime}|
alone. To our knowledge, this is the first uniform-in-time strong error estimate for nonlinearity-explicit schemes that preserve the unique ergodicity of superlinear SPDEs driven by multiplicative noise. Combined with the exponential ergodicity of the tamed-FEM, which follows from the Lyapunov structure derived in
[
17
]
and an exponential continuity estimate with respect to initial data, we obtain an ergodic error estimate—estimate between the exact and numerical invariant measures—in the Wasserstein-2 distance (see Theorem
5.1
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some preliminaries. Several a priori uniform-in-time estimates of Eq. (
1.1
), the tamed-FEM, and a related auxiliary process are given in Section
3
. We establish the strong error estimates between the tamed-FEM and Eq. (
1.1
) in Section
4

5

6
.

2.2.
Main Assumptions

Our central assumption on the drift function

Assumption 2.1
.

f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\xi f(\xi)\leq K_{1}-K_{2}|\xi|^{q+2},

\displaystyle[1+\tau|\xi|^{2q}]f^{\prime}(\xi)-q\tau|\xi|^{2(q-1)}\xi f(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}(1+\tau|\xi|^{2q})^{3/2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle|f(\xi)|\leq L_{1}+L_{2}|\xi|^{q+1},~|f^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq L_{3}+L_{4}|\xi|^{q},~

\displaystyle|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq L_{5}+L_{6}|\xi|^{q-1}.

Denote by

\displaystyle f_{\tau}(\xi):=\frac{f(\xi)}{(1+\tau|\xi|^{2q})^{1/2}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 2.1
.

It is clear that the condition (

Throughout, we assume that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{2(q+1)}_{\xi}\hookrightarrow\;H\hookrightarrow\;\dot{H}^{-1}\hookrightarrow\;L^{2(q+1)/(2q+1)}_{\xi},\quad d=1,2,3,

hold (see
[

]

Then we can define the Nemytskii operators

\displaystyle G(u)g_{k}(\xi):

\displaystyle=g(u(\xi))g_{k}(\xi),\quad u\in H,~k\in\mathbb{N}_{+},~\xi\in\mathscr{O}.

We will frequently use the following facts about

Remark 2.2

Let (

2.3.
Tamed-FEM

Let
h\in(0,1)
,
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a regular family of quasi-uniform partitions of
\mathscr{O}
with maximal length
h
, and
V_{h}\subset\dot{H}^{1}
be the space of continuous functions on

which are piecewise linear over

and vanish on
\partial\mathscr{O}
.
Let
A_{h}:V_{h}\rightarrow V_{h}
and
\mathcal{P}_{h}:\dot{H}^{-1}\rightarrow V_{h}
be the discrete Dirichlet Laplacian and generalized orthogonal projection operators, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle\langle A_{h}u^{h},v^{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla u^{h},\nabla v^{h}\rangle,\quad u^{h},v^{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}u,v^{h}\rangle

\displaystyle={}_{-1}\langle u,v^{h}\rangle_{1},\quad u\in\dot{H}^{-1},\ v^{h}\in V_{h}.

We discretize Eq. (
2.14
) in space with the Galerkin finite element method (FEM) and in time with the nonlinearity-tamed Euler scheme, called the tamed-FEM, i.e., find
V_{h}
-valued (time-homogeneous) Markov chains
\{Y_{n}^{h},\mathscr{F}_{t_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{h}=Y_{n-1}^{h}+A_{h}Y_{n}^{h}\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})\tau+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{n-1}^{h})\delta_{n-1}W,

where
\delta_{n-1}W:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})
,
t_{n}=n\tau
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, with initial datum
Y^{h}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{h}X_{0}
.
The tamed-FEM (
2.15
) is linearly implicit, so it can be uniquely solved pathwise.

The tamed-FEM (
2.15
) can be equivalently rewritten as

(2.16)

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{h}=S_{h,\tau}Y_{n-1}^{h}+\tau S_{h,\tau}\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{n-1}^{h})+S_{h,\tau}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{n-1}^{h})\delta_{n-1}W,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},

where
S_{h,\tau}:=({\rm Id}-\tau A_{h})^{-1}
, with
{\rm Id}
denoting the identity operator in
V_{h}
, is an approximation of
S
in one step.
Iterating (
2.16

(2.17)

\displaystyle Y^{h}_{n}=S_{h,\tau}^{n}Y^{h}_{0}+\tau\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y^{h}_{i})+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}S_{h,\tau}^{n-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y^{h}_{i})\delta_{i}W,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Uniform-in-time A Priori Estimates

\phi_{\tau}(f_{\tau}^{\prime}(\xi))\leq\max\{\phi_{\tau}(-m_{\tau}),\phi_{\tau}(K_{3})\}.

For any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}:=\min\{1,4(L_{4}+\sqrt{L_{4}^{2}+4C_{0}})^{-2}\})
with
C_{0}:=\max\{0,L_{3}+qK_{1}^{+}d_{q}-K_{3}\}
, we have

\displaystyle m_{\tau}\leq L_{3}+L_{4}\tau^{-1/2}+qK_{1}^{+}d_{q}\leq K_{3}+C_{0}+L_{4}\tau^{-1/2}\leq K_{3}+\tau^{-1},

and thus
\phi_{\tau}(-m_{\tau})-\phi_{\tau}(K_{3})=(m_{\tau}+K_{3})\bigl(\tau(m_{\tau}-K_{3})-1\bigr)\leq 0
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle f_{\tau}^{\prime}(\xi)+\tau|f_{\tau}^{\prime}(\xi)|^{2}=\phi_{\tau}(f_{\tau}^{\prime}(\xi))\leq\phi_{\tau}(K_{3})=K_{3}+\tau K_{3}^{2},

thereby proving (
3.8
).

To show (
3.9
) for any
u,v\in H
, we observe that
f_{\tau}(u)-f_{\tau}(v)=M_{\tau}(u,v)(u-v)
with
M_{\tau}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\tau}^{\prime}(v+s(u-v))ds
.
Then

\displaystyle\langle F_{\tau}(u)-F_{\tau}(v),u-v\rangle+\tau\|F_{\tau}(u)-F_{\tau}(v)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}}[(f_{\tau}(u)-f_{\tau}(v))(u-v)+\tau|f_{\tau}(u)-f_{\tau}(v)|^{2}]\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}}[M_{\tau}(u,v)+\tau|M_{\tau}(u,v)|^{2}]|u-v|^{2}\,d\xi.

Here and in what follows, we omit the integration variable when integration is present to lighten the notation.
By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle F_{\tau}(u)-F_{\tau}(v),u-v\rangle+\tau\|F_{\tau}(u)-F_{\tau}(v)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}[f_{\tau}^{\prime}(v+s(u-v))+\tau|f_{\tau}^{\prime}(v+s(u-v))|^{2}]\,ds\Big)|u-v|^{2}\,d\xi.

In combination with the above inequality and (
3.8
), we conclude (
3.9
).

Let
u\in\dot{H}^{1}
.
The definition of the inner product and norm in
\dot{H}^{1}
yields that

\displaystyle\langle u,F_{\tau}(u)\rangle_{1}+\tau\|F_{\tau}(u)\|_{1}^{2}=\langle u,F_{\tau}(u)\rangle+\tau\|F_{\tau}(u)\|^{2}+\int_{\mathscr{O}}(f_{\tau}^{\prime}(u)+\tau|f_{\tau}^{\prime}(u)|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi.

For the first term, following the technique used in the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have the existence of positive constants
\tau_{0}<1
,
T_{1}
, and
T_{1}^{\prime}
such that for any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}^{\prime})
,

\langle u,F_{\tau}(u)\rangle+\tau\|F_{\tau}(u)\|^{2}\leq T_{1}-T_{1}^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}
.
For the second term, the estimate (
3.8
) implies

\int_{\mathscr{O}}(f_{\tau}^{\prime}(u)+\tau|f_{\tau}^{\prime}(u)|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq(K_{3}+K_{3}^{2}\tau)\|\nabla u\|^{2}
.
Combining the above two estimates, we conclude (
3.10
) with
\tau_{1}:=\min\{\tau_{0},\tau_{0}^{\prime}\}
.
∎

Now we can show the following required uniform-in-time estimates on the tamed-FEM (

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

,
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
, and Assumptions

+
2.2
(1) hold with

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\|Y_{n}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2p}),

for any

and
\tau\in(0,\tau_{2})
.
Moreover, if
\lambda_{1}>K_{3}+\frac{2p(q+1)-1}{2}K_{6}
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\|{\widehat{Y}}_{n}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2p(q+1)}).

Proof.

In view of (
3.3
), (
2.4
), and (

This, in combination with the uniform regularity (
3.4
), gives (
3.13
).

To show (

), we use (

) and the Poincáre inequality (
2.1
) to get

\displaystyle(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})\|Y_{j+1}^{h}\|_{1}^{2}\leq\|Y_{j}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j}^{h})\delta_{j}W\|_{1}^{2}.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, taking the
p
-th power and the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{j}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\mathscr{F}(t_{j})]
on both sides of the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})^{p}\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Y_{j+1}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Y_{j}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j}^{h})\delta_{j}W\|_{1}^{2p}.

Set

Z_{j}(t):=Y_{j}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j}^{h})(W(t_{j}+t)-W(t_{j}))
for

.
Then
Z_{j}(0)=Y_{j}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})
and
Z_{j}(\tau)=Y_{j}^{h}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j}^{h})\delta_{j}W

Applying Itô formula to

\mathbb{E}_{j}
, and utilizing the estimate
\|G(Y_{j}^{h})^{*}z\|_{U_{0}}^{2}\leq\|G(Y_{j}^{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}^{2}\|z\|_{1}^{2}
,

, and Hölder inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Z_{j}(t)\|_{1}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq p(2p-1)\|G(Y_{j}^{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Z_{j}(t)\|_{1}^{2p-2}.

By Hölder inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Z_{j}(t)\|_{1}^{2p-2}\leq(\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Z_{j}(t)\|_{1}^{2p})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
.
Substituting this into the above estimate yields

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Z_{j}(t)\|_{1}^{2p})^{1/p}\leq(2p-1)\|G(Y_{j}^{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}^{2}
.
Integrating over
[0,\tau]
yields

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Y_{j}^{h}+\tau F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})+G(Y_{j}^{h})\delta_{j}W\|_{1}^{2p}\leq(\|Y_{j}^{h}+\tau F_{\tau}(Y_{j}^{h})\|_{1}^{2}+(2p-1)\tau\|G(Y_{j}^{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}^{2})^{p}.

This, in combination with (

) and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Y_{j+1}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}\leq\frac{1}{(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})^{p}}\sum_{r=0}^{p}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2r},

,

.

\mathcal{B}_{p}(\tau)=(1+(2K_{3}+(2p-1)K_{6})\tau+2K_{3}^{2}\tau^{2})^{p}
.
When
p=1
, the sum
\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}
is empty and adopted as zero by convention, allowing us to directly obtain the final iterative inequality without further bounding.
For
p\geq 2
and
r\in\{1,\cdots,p-1\}
, we apply Young inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2r}\leq\frac{\Phi(\tau)}{2(p-1)}\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}+\frac{p-r}{p}\Big(\frac{2r(p-1)}{p\Phi(\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{r}{p-r}}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)^{\frac{p}{p-r}},

where
\Phi(\tau):=(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})^{p}-\mathcal{B}_{p}(\tau)
.
Summing over
r
then yields

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2r}\leq\frac{\Phi(\tau)}{2}\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}+\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\frac{p-r}{p}\Big(\frac{2r(p-1)}{p\Phi(\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{r}{p-r}}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)^{\frac{p}{p-r}}.

Combining the above estimates, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{j}\|Y_{j+1}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1-\frac{\Phi(\tau)}{2(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})^{p}}\Big)\|Y_{j}^{h}\|_{1}^{2p}+C(\tau),

with
C(\tau)=(\mathcal{B}_{0}(\tau)+\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\frac{p-r}{p}(\frac{2r(p-1)}{p\Phi(\tau)})^{\frac{r}{p-r}}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)^{\frac{p}{p-r}})/(1+2\tau\lambda_{1})^{p}
, with the convention that the sum vanishes if
p=1
.

Using the elementary inequality
x^{p}-y^{p}\geq p(x-y)y^{p-1}
,
p\geq 1
, we obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau)\geq p(2\tau\lambda_{1}-(2K_{3}+(2p-1)K_{6})\tau-2K_{3}^{2}\tau^{2})\geq c_{1}\tau,

for some positive constant
c_{1}
and for any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{2}^{\prime})
, with
\tau_{2}^{\prime}:=\min\{1,(\lambda_{1}-K_{3}-\frac{2p-1}{2}K_{6})K_{3}^{-2}\}\in(0,1]
as
\lambda_{1}>K_{3}+\frac{2p-1}{2}K_{6}
.
On the other hand, it is clear that
\mathcal{B}_{0}(\tau)=((2T_{1}+(2p-1)K_{5}))^{p}\tau^{p}
and
\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)\leq C\tau^{p-r}
for
r=1,\cdots,p
.
Combining these with
\Phi(\tau)>c_{1}\tau
and
\Phi(\tau)^{-\frac{r}{p-r}}\mathcal{B}_{r}(\tau)^{\frac{p}{p-r}}\leq C\tau^{p-\frac{r}{p-r}}\leq C\tau
as
1\leq r\leq p-1
when
p\geq 2
, we have the existence of a positive constant
c_{2}
such that

(3.17)

\displaystyle C(\tau)\leq c_{2}\tau.

Combining the estimates (
3.15
), (
3.16
), and (
3.17
), we conclude (
3.12
).
∎

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\|X(t_{n})-Y_{n}^{h}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2p}L_{\xi}^{2}}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1+\gamma}+\|X_{0}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2(3q+1)}})^{(q+1)(4q+1)}(h^{1+\gamma}+\tau^{1/2}).

6.
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we present numerical tests to illustrate the ergodic behavior, as well as the sharpness and time-independence of the strong convergence rates in Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
for the tamed-FEM (
2.15

All experiments are performed on the two-dimensional unit square
\mathcal{O}=(0,1)^{2}
with homogeneous DBC. To test both additive and multiplicative noise, we consider the trace-class noise coefficient
g(\xi)=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\xi

\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. We set
\varepsilon=0.25
and choose
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=(2,0)
for the additive noise case, and
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=(2,0.8)
for the multiplicative noise case.

The noise is expanded in the orthonormal sine basis
\{g_{k,\ell}\}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\{q_{k,\ell}\}
:

\displaystyle g_{k,\ell}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=2\sin(k\pi\xi_{1})\sin(\ell\pi\xi_{2}),\quad q_{k,\ell}=(1+k^{2}+\ell^{2})^{-3},\qquad k,~\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

In the simulations, we truncate the
Q
-Wiener process
W
and obtain the truncated Wiener process

W^{N}=\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq N}(1+k^{2}+\ell^{2})^{-3/2}g_{k,\ell}\beta_{k,\ell}

\{\beta_{k,\ell}\}
is a family of independent 1D Brownian motions.

Since the functions
g_{k,\ell}
are also Dirichlet eigenfunctions on
(0,1)^{2}
, we use the same notation when defining the initial data and the observables below. The temporal tests use
N=32
, whereas the spatial and ergodicity tests use
N=64
.

6.1.
Uniform-in-time strong convergence tests

To construct initial data with varying regularities, we define the following for
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\alpha,p>0
:

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,p}^{M}(\xi)=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{M}\frac{\eta_{k,\ell}g_{k,\ell}(\xi)}{(k^{2}+\ell^{2})^{\alpha}\log^{p}(e+\sqrt{k^{2}+\ell^{2}})},\qquad\bar{u}_{\alpha,p}^{M}=\frac{u_{\alpha,p}^{M}}{\|u_{\alpha,p}^{M}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}},

where
\eta_{k,\ell}\in\{-1,1\}
are fixed signs. Slower spectral decay yields a rougher profile, whereas faster decay produces a smoother one. Accordingly, we take

\displaystyle X_{0,\mathrm{time}}^{(1)}=0.8\,g_{1,1}+\bar{u}_{1,1.2}^{64},\qquad X_{0,\mathrm{time}}^{(2)}=0.8\,g_{1,1}+\bar{u}_{1.5,2}^{64},

for the temporal tests, and

\displaystyle X_{0,\mathrm{space}}^{(1)}=0.8\,g_{1,1}+\bar{u}_{1,0.51}^{512},\qquad X_{0,\mathrm{space}}^{(2)}=0.8\,g_{1,1}+\bar{u}_{1.5,1}^{512},

for the spatial tests.
Although these series are truncated, the two choices are designed to mimic data near the
\dot{H}^{1}
and
\dot{H}^{2}
regularity thresholds, respectively.

Temporal strong convergence.

We fix
h=1/32

6.2.
Ergodicity test

We take
h=1/32
,
\tau=2^{-8}
,
T=50
, and
K_{\rm mc}=1000
. The three initial states are

\displaystyle X_{0}^{(1)}=1.4g_{1,1},\qquad X_{0}^{(2)}=-1.4g_{1,1},\qquad X_{0}^{(3)}=\frac{g_{2,1}+g_{1,2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

For each Monte–Carlo path, all three initial states are driven by the same truncated Wiener path. We monitor

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\tanh(\langle u,g_{1,1}\rangle),

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\tanh\big(\langle u,g_{2,1}+g_{1,2}\rangle/\sqrt{2}\big),

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(u)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\big(-\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big).

Fig.
5
shows that the ensemble means from the three initial states rapidly merge in both the additive and multiplicative cases, illustrating the loss of memory of initial data and supporting the exponential ergodicity proved in Section
5
.
