Existence of free boundaries for overdetermined value problems: Sharp conditions, regularity, and physical applications
Mohammed Barkatou, Samira Khatmi

Abstract
This paper provides necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of free boundaries in overdetermined value-problems (ODVP) for the Laplacian, and sufficient conditions for the bi-Laplacian, when the overdetermined boundary condition is non-constant. Using classical integral inequalities (Cauchy-Schwarz, Hölder, Hardy, eigenvalue bounds, Pohozaev and Reilly identities), we derive existence results for a broad class of free boundary problems arising in potential theory, plate theory, electromagnetism, and shape optimization. A regularity result for minimizers in the C C -GNP class is established using the thickness function and the Wiener criterion, based on the geometric description of cusp points given in [ 1 ] . New results include refined estimates via interpolation inequalities, stability under perturbations, and connections with isoperimetric inequalities. Detailed analysis of the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions to the ODVP is given. The physical interpretation of the bi-Laplacian problem ℬ ⁡ ( f , g ) \mathcal{B}(f,g) in the Kirchhoff-Love theory of thin plates is emphasized.



Keywords:
Integral inequalities, free boundaries, Laplacian, bi-Laplacian, maximum principle, mean curvature, shape derivative, shape optimization, quadrature surfaces problem, plate theory, Kirchhoff-Love, thickness function, Wiener criterion.

Introduction

We assume throughout that
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(with

for physical relevance, though many results extend to arbitrary

) is a fixed bounded domain containing all admissible domains. For an open subset
\Omega\subset D
, let
\nu
denote the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
,
|\partial\Omega|
the perimeter (or
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure), and
|\Omega|
the volume (
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure). Let
g
and
f
be positive functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
f
has compact support with nonempty interior, and denote by
C
the convex hull of
\operatorname{supp}f
.

We study two overdetermined free boundary problems:

•

\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
: Find
\Omega\subset D
strictly containing
C
such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\Omega}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{\Omega}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

•

\mathcal{B}(f,g)
: Find
\Omega\subset D
strictly containing
C
such that

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v_{\Omega}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v_{\Omega}=\Delta v_{\Omega}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
|\nabla v_{\Omega}|\,|\nabla(\Delta v_{\Omega})|=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

Physical interpretation of the existence conditions

The existence conditions derived in this paper (Theorems
3.6
and
4.4
) are not merely mathematical artefacts; they carry a clear and intuitive physical meaning in their respective contexts.

The Laplacian problem
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)

The necessary and sufficient condition for existence,

\int_{C}f\,dx>\int_{\partial C}g\,d\sigma,

(7)

admits the following physical interpretation:

\int_{C}f\,dx
:

Total source strength.
In electrostatics, if
f
represents a charge density, this is the total electric charge enclosed within the fixed core
C
. In potential theory or fluid dynamics, it represents the total output of a source distribution.

\int_{\partial C}g\,d\sigma
:

Total flux through the core’s boundary.
The function
g
on the free boundary
\partial\Omega
is the prescribed magnitude of the gradient (electric field, velocity). Its integral over
\partial C
is the total flux that would exit through
\partial C

if
the domain’s boundary were exactly
\partial C
.

The inequality:

It asserts that the total source inside
C
must
strictly exceed
the flux that could be accommodated by
\partial C
. This surplus of source energy forces the domain to expand beyond
C
(
\Omega\supset C
) so that the flux can be distributed over a larger boundary
\partial\Omega
, where it matches the prescribed value
g
.

The bi-Laplacian problem
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
(Kirchhoff-Love plate theory)

The sufficient condition for existence,

\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\,d\sigma\right)^{2}<\left(\int_{C}f\,dx\right)\left(\int_{C}u_{C}\,dx\right),

(16)

where
u_{C}
solves
-\Delta u_{C}=f
in
C
with
u_{C}=0
on
\partial C
, has a profound mechanical interpretation:

\int_{C}f\,dx
:

Total transverse load.
This is the resultant force applied to the region
C
of the plate (e.g., the total weight or pressure).

u_{C}=-\Delta v_{C}
:

Bending moment.
It represents the internal resistance of the plate to curvature.

\int_{C}u_{C}\,dx
:

Total integrated bending moment.
This quantity is proportional to the
elastic strain energy
stored in the portion
C
of the plate under the load
f
. It is a measure of the plate’s compliance in that region.

\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\,d\sigma
:

Geometric mean of the prescribed mechanical work.
Recall the overdetermined condition
|\nabla v|\,|\nabla u|=g
on
\partial\Omega
. Here,
|\nabla v|
is the
slope
and
|\nabla u|
the
effective shear force
at the boundary. Their product
g
is the
density of mechanical work
(or power of internal forces) along the edge. Integrating
\sqrt{g}
provides a measure of this work.

The product
\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{C}u_{C}\right)
:

This is the product of the
cause
(total load) and the
effect
(integrated deformation). It represents a global measure of the plate’s
internal energy
under load.

The inequality:

It states that the internal energy of the plate within
C
must be greater than the square of the averaged mechanical work prescribed on
\partial C
. If the prescribed edge work is too large relative to the energy the core
C
can store, the plate must extend (
\Omega\supset C
) to “dilute” this work over a larger boundary until the condition
|\nabla v|\,|\nabla u|=g
can be satisfied pointwise.

These physical interpretations underscore the natural character of the integral conditions: they are necessary balances between the energy contained in the core and the energy that can be transmitted through its boundary.

Physical motivations

Problem
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
is known as the quadrature surfaces free boundary problem and arises in potential theory, fluid dynamics, and electrostatics: it describes the shape of a conductor or a fluid domain such that the boundary flux matches a prescribed distribution
g
. The condition
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|=g
on
\partial\Omega
corresponds to a prescribed electric field magnitude (electrostatics) or a prescribed velocity gradient (ideal fluid flow).

Problem
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
models the optimal design of a simply supported thin plate under Kirchhoff-Love theory
[
6
]
:
v_{\Omega}
is the vertical deflection,
\Delta v_{\Omega}
the bending moment,
|\nabla v_{\Omega}|
the slope, and
|\nabla(\Delta v_{\Omega})|
the effective shear force at the boundary. The overdetermined condition prescribes the product of slope and shear force, which corresponds to a pointwise control of the mechanical work along the edge. This condition arises in shape optimization of plates subjected to combined bending and shear constraints.

When
g\equiv k
(constant), existence conditions are known: Gustafsson and Shahgholian
[
7
]
gave a sufficient condition via subsolutions; Barkatou
[
2
]
established the necessary and sufficient condition
\int_{C}f>k|\partial C|
. For
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
with constant
g
, Fromm and McDonald
[
5
]
and Huang and Miller
[
8
]
proved symmetry results; Barkatou
[
3
]
derived existence under integral conditions.

Objectives and new contributions

Our aim is threefold:

1.

Extend existing results to non-constant
g
using variational methods and integral inequalities.

2.

Strengthen the existence theory by proving regularity of minimizers in the admissible class
\mathcal{O}_{C}
via the thickness function and the Wiener criterion, based on the fine geometric description of cusp points obtained in
[
1
]
.

3.

Provide new existence results using refined functional inequalities (interpolation inequalities, weighted Hardy inequalities, spectral estimates) and establish connections with isoperimetric inequalities.

The main novelties of this enriched version include:

•

A unified variational framework for both Laplacian and bi-Laplacian problems with non-constant boundary data (Section 3).

•

Sharp necessary and sufficient conditions via integral inequalities (Theorems
3.2
and
3.6
).

•

Regularity theory for minimizers in the
C
-GNP class, including boundary behavior near contact points (Section 2).

•

New results using interpolation inequalities (Gagliardo-Nirenberg, Ladyzhenskaya) to handle critical exponents (Section 6).

•

Stability analysis and symmetry breaking criteria (Section 7).

•

Analysis of the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions to the ODVP in the radial case (Section 8).

Preliminaries and the
C
-GNP class

The
C
-geometric normal property

We recall the definition of the
C
-geometric normal property (
C
-GNP) introduced in
[
1
]
and its equivalent characterization via the separation property (
C
-sp). This class guarantees compactness and continuity of state functions under Hausdorff convergence.

Definition 2.1

(
C
-GNP)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact convex set with nonempty interior. An open set
\Omega\subset D
is said to satisfy the
C
-GNP if:

1.

\Omega\supset\operatorname{int}(C)
;

2.

\partial\Omega\setminus C
is locally Lipschitz;

3.

for every
c\in\partial C
, there exists an outward normal ray
\Delta_{c}
such that
\Delta_{c}\cap\Omega
is connected;

4.

for every
x\in\partial\Omega\setminus C
, the inward normal ray to
\Omega
(if it exists) meets
C
.

We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{C}
the family of all open subsets of
D
satisfying the
C
-GNP.

Remark 2.2
.

Condition (3) ensures that each point of
\partial C
has a unique "shadow" on the free boundary along the outward normal. Condition (4) prevents the formation of "bubbles" that are not visible from
C
. Together, they provide a global parametrization of
\partial\Omega\setminus C
by the thickness function
d(c)
.

Compactness and continuity properties

Proposition 2.3

(Compactness of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
)
.

Any sequence
\{\Omega_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}
admits a subsequence converging in the Hausdorff, compact, and characteristic senses to some
\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Moreover, these three convergences are equivalent in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

Proof.

The relative compactness for Hausdorff convergence follows from the fact that all
\Omega_{n}
are confined in the fixed ball
D
(Proposition 3.1 in
[
1
]
). By Theorem 3.1 of
[
1
]
, the limit
\Omega
also satisfies
C
-GNP, and the convergences in Hausdorff, compact and characteristic senses are equivalent on
\mathcal{O}_{C}
(Propositions 3.6-3.8). The continuity of the state functions under these convergences is proved in Theorem 4.3 of
[
1
]
using the Wiener regularity of cusp points.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(Continuity of state functions)
.

If
\Omega_{n}\to\Omega
in Hausdorff sense and
\Omega_{n},\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
, then the solutions
u_{n}
of
P(\Omega_{n},f)
converge strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
to
u_{\Omega}
(extended by zero). Similarly for
v_{n}
under strong convergence of data.

Proof.

See Theorem 4.3 in
[
1
]
. The key ingredients are:

1.

the compact convergence
\Omega_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle K}}{{\to}}\Omega
which follows from Hausdorff convergence and the uniform cone property of
\partial\Omega_{n}\setminus C
;

2.

the Wiener regularity of all boundary points (including cusp points on
\partial C
), which guarantees the stability in the sense of Keldysh;

3.

the strong convergence in
H_{0}^{1}(D)
is then obtained by the method of energy norms.

∎

Geometric control of cusps

A delicate issue in the
C
-GNP class is that the free boundary
\partial\Omega
may meet the fixed boundary
\partial C
at a non-empty contact set. At such points,
\partial\Omega
is not Lipschitz and may develop cusps. However, as established in
[
1
]
, these cusps are not arbitrary: they are contained in a canonical cusp of revolution obtained by revolving the cusp formed by two touching circles of large radius around its axis. This geometric control has deep consequences for the regularity of the Dirichlet problem.

Proposition 2.5

(Geometric containment of cusps)
.

Let
C
be a convex set with nonempty interior and let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial C
. Let
\Delta_{0}
be the selected normal to
C
at
x_{0}
,
H
the hyperplane orthogonal to
\Delta_{0}
at
x_{0}
, and
H^{+}
the open half-space limited by
H
not containing
C
. Let
R>\operatorname{diam}(C)
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small. Then

\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon_{0})\cap H^{+}\subset\bigcup_{z\in H,|z-x_{0}|=R}\overline{B}(z,R)=:\mathcal{B}_{x_{0}},

where
\mathcal{B}_{x_{0}}=B^{\prime}(x_{0},\varepsilon_{0})\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This is Proposition 2.2 in
[
1
]
. The proof proceeds by contradiction in a plane
P
containing
\Delta_{0}
. Assuming the existence of a connected component
\omega
of
\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon_{0})\cap H^{+}
not contained in
\mathcal{B}_{x_{0}}
, one constructs a point on
\partial\omega\cap\partial\Omega
where the inward normal fails to intersect
C
, contradicting the
C
-GNP. The argument uses the divergence theorem on the boundary of
\omega\cap P
and an inequality derived from the normal condition.
∎

Corollary 2.6

(Hölder regularity of the thickness function)
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.5
, the thickness function
d(c)
(defined by
x=c+d(c)\nu(c)
for
x\in\partial\Omega\setminus C
) is locally Hölder continuous on
\partial C
with exponent
1/2
near contact points.

Proof.

The containment in a circular cusp implies that near a contact point, the free boundary is trapped between two surfaces of the form
d(c)\sim K|c-c_{0}|^{2}
(paraboloid) and
d(c)\sim k|c-c_{0}|
(cone). Standard barrier arguments for harmonic functions then yield the Hölder estimate. See
[
1
, Section 5]
for details.
∎

Regularity of the free boundary

Theorem 2.7

(Regularity of minimizers)
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a minimizer of
J_{f,g}
over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Then:

1.

\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus C
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
hypersurface for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

2.

The contact set
\partial\Omega_{0}\cap\partial C
consists of Wiener-regular points, and near each contact point, the free boundary meets
\partial C
tangentially with at least quadratic detachment.

3.

The thickness function
d\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial C)
away from the contact set and satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\frac{\delta J}{\delta d}=0\quad\text{in the sense of shape derivatives.}

(3)

Proof.

The proof combines several ingredients:

1.

From Proposition
2.3
, we have Hausdorff convergence and the uniform Lipschitz property away from
C
.

2.

The shape derivative computation (Proposition
3.5
) shows that on
\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\partial C
, we have
|\nabla u_{0}|=g
. By elliptic regularity, this implies
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of the free boundary.

3.

Near contact points, the geometric containment (Proposition
2.5
) together with the Wiener criterion ensures that every boundary point is regular for the Dirichlet problem. The tangential contact follows from an asymptotic expansion of
u_{0}
near the contact point.

4.

The regularity of
d
follows from the implicit function theorem applied to the parametrization
F(c,d)=u_{0}(c+d\nu(c))
using the non-degeneracy condition
|\nabla u_{0}|=g>0
.

See also
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
for a similar result in a related context.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Theorem
2.7
justifies the use of shape derivative calculus even when the free boundary meets the fixed convex set. This is essential for the variational characterization of solutions.

3
The quadrature surfaces problem
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)

Variational formulation

Define the functional

J_{f,g}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}-2fu_{\Omega}\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}g^{2}\,dx,

(4)

where
u_{\Omega}
solves the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\Omega}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{\Omega}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(5)

Proposition 3.1

(Properties of
J_{f,g}
)
.

1.

J_{f,g}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

2.

For any
\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
, we have the identity

J_{f,g}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}(g^{2}-|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2})\,dx.

(6)

3.

J_{f,g}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to Hausdorff convergence in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

Proof.

(1) By standard elliptic theory,
u_{\Omega}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
(since
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz away from
C
). Hence all integrals are finite. (2) is due to Green’s formula.(3) Lower semicontinuity follows from Proposition
2.4
and the fact that
u_{\Omega_{n}}\to u_{\Omega}
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
implies convergence of the energy integrals.
∎

Necessary condition

Theorem 3.2

(Necessary condition for
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
)
.

Assume that:

1.

For every
c\in\partial C
, the outward normal to
C
at
c
intersects
\partial\Omega
at a unique point
x=c+d(c)\nu(c)
with
d(c)>0
;

2.

The projection
p:\partial\Omega\to\partial C
,
x\mapsto c
, is bijective;

3.

g
is strictly positive and increasing along every outward normal to
C
(i.e.,
g(c+t\nu(c))
is increasing in
t
for small
t>0
).

If
\Omega
solves
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
, then

\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g.

(7)

Proof.

Since
p
is a diffeomorphism, let
J(c)
be its Jacobian (the surface element ratio). By convexity and strict containment,
J(c)>1
. By monotonicity of
g
, for
x=c+d(c)\nu(c)
we have
g(x)=g(c+d(c)\nu(c))\geq g(c)
. Hence

\int_{\partial\Omega}g=\int_{\partial C}g(c+d(c)\nu(c))J(c)d\sigma_{C}(c)>\int_{\partial C}g(c)d\sigma_{C}(c).

Green’s formula applied to the solution
u_{\Omega}
gives

\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u_{\Omega}}{\partial\nu}=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|=\int_{\partial\Omega}g,

since
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|=g
on
\partial\Omega
. Because
\operatorname{supp}f\subset C
and
\Omega
strictly contains
C
, we have
\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{C}f
. Combining yields
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
g
is constant, condition (3) is automatically satisfied, and we recover the necessary condition
\int_{C}f>k|\partial C|
from
[
2
]
.

Sufficient condition via variational method

Proposition 3.4

(Existence of minimizers)
.

The functional
J_{f,g}
admits a minimizer
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
,
\mathcal{O}_{C}
is compact for Hausdorff convergence. The functional
J_{f,g}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to this convergence by Proposition
2.4
(strong
H_{0}^{1}
convergence of states). Hence a minimizer exists by the direct method of calculus of variations.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Optimality conditions)
.

Let
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
be a minimizer of
J_{f,g}
. If
\Omega_{0}
is of class
C^{2}
and
u_{0}
solves
P(\Omega_{0},f)
, then

|\nabla u_{0}|\leq g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{0}\cap\partial C,\qquad|\nabla u_{0}|=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\partial C.

(8)

Proof.

For a
C^{2}
deformation field
V
, the shape derivative of
J_{f,g}
at
\Omega_{0}
is

dJ_{f,g}(\Omega_{0};V)=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\left(g^{2}-|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\right)V\cdot\nu\,d\sigma.

(9)

This formula can be derived using the method of shape sensitivity analysis (see
[
12
]
).

Since
\Omega_{0}
is a minimizer, we must have
dJ_{f,g}(\Omega_{0};V)\geq 0
for all admissible variations that keep the perturbed domain in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. On
\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\partial C
, the normal component
V\cdot\nu
can take both signs arbitrarily (by choosing inward or outward variations that do not violate the containment of
C
), forcing
|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}-g^{2}=0
. On
\partial\Omega_{0}\cap\partial C
, admissible variations are only outward (since
\Omega_{0}
must remain containing
\operatorname{int}(C)
), hence
V\cdot\nu\geq 0
. The inequality
dJ\geq 0
then yields
|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}-g^{2}\leq 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.6

(Existence for
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
)
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a minimizer of
J_{f,g}
over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Then:

1.

Either
\Omega_{0}
is a solution of
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
provided
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
;

2.

Or there exists
\Omega_{1}\supset\Omega_{0}
solving
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
.

Proof.

Proposition
3.5
implies
\int_{C}f\leq\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}g
. Two cases arise:
1.
\int_{C}f=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}g
, implying
|\nabla u_{0}|=g
on
\partial\Omega_{0}
.
2.
\int_{C}f<\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}g
.

Case 1: Define
M=\frac{\int_{C}f}{\int_{\partial C}g}
. Suppose
M>1
, implying
\partial C\neq\partial\Omega_{0}
.
Let
\Omega_{0}^{*}
minimize
J_{f,Mg}
on
\mathcal{O}_{C}
, subject to
\Omega_{0}^{*}\subset\Omega_{0}
.
If
\Omega_{0}^{*}
is
C^{2}
, optimality conditions yield:
(a)
|\nabla u^{*}_{0}|\leq Mg
on
\partial\Omega^{*}_{0}\cap\partial C
,
(b)
|\nabla u^{*}_{0}|\geq Mg
on
\partial\Omega^{*}_{0}\cap\partial\Omega_{0}
,
(c)
|\nabla u^{*}_{0}|=Mg
on
\partial\Omega^{*}_{0}\setminus(\partial C\cup\partial\Omega_{0})
.

If
\partial\Omega_{0}\cap\partial C=\emptyset
, then
\Omega_{0}
solves
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
.
Suppose the intersection is non-empty. Since
\Omega_{0}^{*}\subset\Omega_{0}
and
\partial\Omega_{0}^{*}\neq\partial\Omega_{0}
(otherwise
Mg\leq|\nabla u^{*}_{0}|=|\nabla u_{0}|=g
, contradicting
M>1
), the Hopf maximum principle implies:

Mg\leq|\nabla u^{*}_{0}|<|\nabla u_{0}|=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega^{*}_{0}\cap\partial\Omega_{0},

which is a contradiction.
Case 2: Let
\mathcal{O}_{0}
be domains in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
containing
\Omega_{0}
. A minimum
\Omega_{1}
exists satisfying:
(a)
|\nabla u_{1}|\leq g
on
\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial C
,
(b)
|\nabla u_{1}|=g
on
\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{0}
.
Repeating the argument with
\bar{\Omega_{0}}
replacing
C
and
\Omega_{1}
replacing
\Omega_{0}
yields the result.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Existence via integral inequalities)
.

Assume that
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
. Then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution. Conversely, if
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution and the monotonicity conditions of Theorem

.

The sufficiency follows from Theorem

.

4
The bi-Laplacian problem
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
and plate theory

The plate model

We now turn to the bi-Laplacian problem, which models a simply supported plate. Let
v_{\Omega}
solve the system

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v_{\Omega}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v_{\Omega}=\Delta v_{\Omega}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(10)

Equivalently, we can introduce an auxiliary variable
u_{\Omega}=-\Delta v_{\Omega}
and solve the coupled system

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\Omega}=u_{\Omega}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta u_{\Omega}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v_{\Omega}=u_{\Omega}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(11)

Remark 4.1

(Physical interpretation)
.

In Kirchhoff-Love theory:

•

v_{\Omega}
is the vertical deflection;

•

\Delta v_{\Omega}
is the bending moment (proportional to curvature);

•

|\nabla v_{\Omega}|
is the slope;

•

|\nabla(\Delta v_{\Omega})|=|\nabla u_{\Omega}|
is the effective shear force.

The condition
|\nabla v_{\Omega}||\nabla u_{\Omega}|=g
on
\partial\Omega
prescribes the product of slope and shear force, which corresponds to a pointwise control of the mechanical work density along the edge. This condition arises in optimal design problems where both bending and shear constraints are active.

Variational formulation

Define the functional

F_{g}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{\Omega}^{2}-g^{2}\right)dx,

(12)

where
u_{\Omega}=-\Delta v_{\Omega}
as above.

Proposition 4.2

(Properties of
F_{g}
)
.

1.

F_{g}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

2.

For any
\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
, we have the identity

F_{g}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}fv_{\Omega}dx-\int_{\Omega}g^{2}dx.

(13)

3.

F_{g}
admits a minimizer
A_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
.

Proof.

(1) Standard elliptic regularity for the biharmonic operator with Navier boundary conditions ensures
u_{\Omega},v_{\Omega}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(2) Multiply the equation
\Delta^{2}v_{\Omega}=f
by
v_{\Omega}
and integrate by parts twice, using the boundary conditions:

\int_{\Omega}fv_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}v_{\Omega})v_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v_{\Omega}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}^{2}.

Thus
\int_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{\Omega}
, and the identity follows.

(3) Same compactness and lower semicontinuity argument as for Proposition
3.4
, using the continuity of
u_{\Omega}
and
v_{\Omega}
under Hausdorff convergence (Proposition
2.4
extends to the biharmonic case by standard regularity theory).
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Optimality conditions for
F_{g}
)
.

Let
A_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
be a minimizer of
F_{g}
. If
A_{0}
is
C^{2}
and
u_{A_{0}},v_{A_{0}}
are the associated states, then

|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|\geq g\quad\text{on }\partial A_{0}\cap\partial C,\qquad|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|=g\quad\text{on }\partial A_{0}\setminus\partial C.

(14)

Proof.

The shape derivative of
F_{g}
at
A_{0}
is

dF_{g}(A_{0};V)=\int_{\partial A_{0}}\left(|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|-g\right)V\cdot\nu\,d\sigma.

(15)

This follows from differentiating
\int u_{A}^{2}/2
using Green’s formula and the domain derivatives (see
[
12
]
for the general theory, and
[
3
]
for the specific computation in the biharmonic case).

Optimality gives
dF_{g}\geq 0
for all admissible
V
. On
\partial A_{0}\setminus\partial C
,
V\cdot\nu
is arbitrary, forcing equality. On
\partial A_{0}\cap\partial C
, only outward variations are allowed (
V\cdot\nu\geq 0
), giving the inequality.
∎

Existence theorem

Theorem 4.4

(Existence for
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
)
.

Assume conditions 1-3 of Theorem
3.2
hold. Let
A_{0}
be a minimizer of
F_{g}
over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
and suppose
|\nabla u_{A_{0}}|
and
|\nabla v_{A_{0}}|
are not proportional (i.e., there is no constant
\lambda>0
such that
|\nabla u_{A_{0}}|=\lambda|\nabla v_{A_{0}}|
on
\partial A_{0}
). If

\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}<\int_{C}f\int_{C}u_{C},

(16)

where
u_{C}
is the solution of
-\Delta u_{C}=f
in
C
with
u_{C}=0
on
\partial C
, then
A_{0}
solves
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.3
, we have on
\partial A_{0}
:

|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|\geq g.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on
\partial A_{0}
:

\left(\int_{\partial A_{0}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\partial A_{0}}\sqrt{|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial A_{0}}|\nabla u_{A_{0}}|\int_{\partial A_{0}}|\nabla v_{A_{0}}|.

Green’s formula gives
\int_{\partial A_{0}}|\nabla u_{A_{0}}|=\int_{C}f
(since
f
is supported in
C
and
A_{0}\supset C
) and
\int_{\partial A_{0}}|\nabla v_{A_{0}}|=\int_{A_{0}}u_{A_{0}}
.

The non-proportionality condition ensures that the second inequality is strict (equality in Cauchy-Schwarz would imply proportionality of
|\nabla u_{A_{0}}|
and
|\nabla v_{A_{0}}|
on
\partial A_{0}
). Moreover, the monotonicity and projection conditions imply that the projection map increases area, so

\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\leq\int_{\partial A_{0}}\sqrt{g}.

Hence

\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}<\int_{C}f\int_{A_{0}}u_{A_{0}}.

Now define
k=\frac{(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g})^{2}}{\int_{C}f\int_{C}u_{C}}<1
. Minimize
F_{kg}
over
\{A\in\mathcal{O}_{C}:A\subset A_{0}\}
. Let
A_{0}^{*}
be a minimizer. Optimality gives:

•

|\nabla u_{A_{0}^{*}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}^{*}}|\geq kg
on
\partial A_{0}^{*}\cap\partial C
;

•

|\nabla u_{A_{0}^{*}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}^{*}}|=kg
on
\partial A_{0}^{*}\setminus(\partial C\cup\partial A_{0})
;

•

|\nabla u_{A_{0}^{*}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}^{*}}|\geq kg
on
\partial A_{0}^{*}\cap\partial A_{0}
.

If
\partial C\cap\partial A_{0}\neq\emptyset
, then by the Hopf maximum principle applied to suitable combinations of
u
and
v
on
\partial C\cap\partial A_{0}^{*}\cap\partial A_{0}
, we have

|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|<|\nabla u_{A_{0}^{*}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}^{*}}|\leq kg,

which contradicts
|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|\geq g
on
\partial A_{0}\cap\partial C
and
g>kg
. Therefore
\partial C\cap\partial A_{0}=\emptyset
, and the optimality condition on
\partial A_{0}
yields
|\nabla u_{A_{0}}||\nabla v_{A_{0}}|=g
. Thus
A_{0}
solves
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Reduction to the Laplacian problem under proportionality)
.

An important observation emerges from the structure of the bi-Laplacian overdetermined condition. Suppose that on the free boundary
\partial\Omega
, the normal derivatives of
u
and
v
(or equivalently, their gradients) are proportional, i.e., there exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that

|\nabla u|=\lambda|\nabla v|\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(17)

This situation, while non-generic (as noted in Theorem
4.4
), is physically meaningful and corresponds to a special balance between the slope and the shear force along the edge of the plate.

Substituting (17) into the overdetermined condition
|\nabla u|\,|\nabla v|=g
yields:

|\nabla u|\cdot\frac{|\nabla u|}{\lambda}=g\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\nabla u|=\sqrt{\lambda g}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(18)

Consequently, under the proportionality assumption (17), the bi-Laplacian problem
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
reduces exactly to the Laplacian problem
\mathcal{QS}(f,\sqrt{\lambda g})
, where the new boundary data is
\sqrt{\lambda g}
.

For this reduced problem, we have a complete theory: Theorem
3.6
provides a necessary and sufficient condition for existence, namely

\int_{C}f\,dx>\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{\lambda g}\,d\sigma.

(19)

This reduction has several important consequences:

•

It shows that the proportionality case is not an obstruction but rather a special instance where the bi-Laplacian problem inherits the sharp existence theory of the Laplacian.

•

It provides a consistency check: if a solution to
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
exists with proportional gradients, then (19) must hold.

•

Conversely, if (19) holds and one can find a domain
\Omega
(typically a ball, by symmetry results
[
9
]
) where
|\nabla u|
and
|\nabla v|
are naturally proportional, then this domain solves
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
.

•

In the radial case (Section 8), proportionality holds automatically due to symmetry, and condition (19) becomes an explicit criterion for the existence of a radial solution to
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
, complementing Theorem
8.5
.

This observation underscores the unity of the two problems and explains why the non-proportionality condition in Theorem
8.5
is both necessary for the generic case and naturally circumvented in highly symmetric situations.

Corollary 4.6

(Sharpness of the condition)
.

If
\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}\geq\int_{C}f\int_{C}u_{C}
, then either:

1.

\mathcal{B}(f,g)
has no solution in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
; or

2.

The solution, if it exists, must satisfy
|\nabla u|=\lambda|\nabla v|
on
\partial\Omega
for some constant
\lambda
, and
\Omega
is a ball (by symmetry results of Payne-Schaefer
[
9
]
) if
f\equiv 1
.

Comparison results for families of functions

Proposition 5.1

(Comparison for means)
.

Let
f_{k}
(
k\in[1,n]
) be
n
positive functions. Define

\displaystyle m_{n}

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq k\leq n}f_{k},

(21)

\displaystyle H_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}1/f_{k}}\quad\text{(harmonic mean)},

(22)

\displaystyle G_{n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt[n]{\prod_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}}\quad\text{(geometric mean)},

(23)

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}\quad\text{(arithmetic mean)},

(24)

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}^{2}}\quad\text{(quadratic mean)},

(25)

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}f_{k}.

(26)

Then:

1.

If
\mathcal{QS}(m_{n},g)
has a solution, then
\mathcal{QS}(H_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{QS}(G_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{QS}(A_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{QS}(Q_{n},g)
, and
\mathcal{QS}(M_{n},g)
also have solutions.

2.

If
\mathcal{B}(m_{n},g)
has a solution, then
\mathcal{B}(H_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{B}(G_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{B}(A_{n},g)
,
\mathcal{B}(Q_{n},g)
, and
\mathcal{B}(M_{n},g)
also have solutions.

Proof.

This follows from the integral condition
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
and the well-known inequalities between means:
m_{n}\leq H_{n}\leq G_{n}\leq A_{n}\leq Q_{n}\leq M_{n}
. If the condition holds for the smallest mean, it holds for all larger ones.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

It can be easily verified that if

Remark 5.3

Repeating Cauchy-Schwarz formula, one can prove that if

In the plane, for

Interplay between Laplacian and bi-Laplacian problems

Proposition 5.5

(Duality relations)
.

1.

If
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
has a solution, then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g_{1})
and
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C},g_{2})
also have solutions where

\mathcal{QS}(f,g)

These follow from the integral conditions and the relations between the quantities via Green’s formulas. For (1), note that if
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
has a solution, then from Theorem

we have the inequality

, which rearranges to the required forms.

Results with sign conditions on
\Delta f

Proposition 5.6

(Sign conditions)
.

Suppose that in
C
,
\varphi>0
,
\varphi\not\equiv 0
and
\Delta\varphi\leq 0
. Then
\mathcal{QS}(f\varphi,|\nabla u_{C}|\varphi)
has a solution.

Proof.

By Green’s formula,

\int_{C}f\varphi=\int_{\partial C}|\nabla u_{C}|\varphi-\int_{C}u_{C}\Delta\varphi.

Since
\Delta\varphi\leq 0
on
C
, the last term is nonnegative, hence

\int_{\partial C}|\nabla u_{C}|\varphi\leq\int_{C}f\varphi,

and the conclusion follows from Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Sufficient condition via products)
.

If
\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C)<\int_{C}\sqrt{fu_{C}}
, then:

1.

\mathcal{QS}(\sqrt{fu_{C}},\sqrt{g})
has a solution;

2.

\mathcal{B}(f,g)
has a solution;

3.

\mathcal{QS}(f,[\Phi_{g}(C)][T(C,u_{C})]^{-1}\sqrt{g})
has a solution.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\left(\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C)\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{C}\sqrt{fu_{C}}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{C}f\int_{C}u_{C}.

Then the three items follow from Theorems
3.7
and
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.8

(Equality case characterization)
.

Suppose that
|\nabla u_{C}||\nabla v_{C}|\geq g
on
\partial C
. Then either:

•

\mathcal{QS}(f,[\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C)][T(C,u_{C})]^{-1}\sqrt{g})
and
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C},[\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C)][T(C,f)]^{-1}\sqrt{g})
have solutions;

•

or
|\nabla u_{C}||\nabla v_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz and Green’s formulas,

[\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C)]^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{|\nabla u_{C}||\nabla v_{C}|}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{C}f\int_{C}u_{C}=[T(C,u_{C})]\int_{C}f.

Then either the inequalities are strict, giving existence, or equality holds throughout, which forces
|\nabla u_{C}||\nabla v_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
by the rigidity of Cauchy-Schwarz.
∎

Proposition 5.9

(Boundary gradient conditions)
.

1.

Suppose
|\nabla u_{C}|\geq g
on
\partial C
. Then either
\mathcal{QS}(f,[\sqrt{g}]_{\partial C}\sqrt{g})
has a solution, or
|\nabla u_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
.

2.

Suppose
|\nabla v_{C}|\geq g
on
\partial C
. Then either
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C},[\sqrt{g}]_{\partial C}\sqrt{g})
has a solution, or
|\nabla v_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
.

Proof.

For (1), by Cauchy-Schwarz and Green’s formula:

\left[\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right]^{2}\leq\left[\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{|\nabla u_{C}|}\right]^{2}\leq|\partial C|\int_{C}f.

Then
[\sqrt{g}]_{\partial C}\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\leq\int_{C}f
, and the conclusion follows. The proof of (2) is similar using
v_{C}
.
∎

Iterated Green’s formula results

Successive applications of Green’s formula yield interesting relationships between iterated solutions.

Proposition 5.10

(Iteration for the Laplacian)
.

Let
u_{0}
be the solution of
P(\Omega,1)
. Let
u_{k}
(
k\geq 1
) be the solution of
P(\Omega,u_{k-1})
. Let
n
be the smallest integer such that
\Delta^{n}f\neq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that for
1\leq k\leq n
,
(-1)^{k}\Delta^{k}f\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega
.

1.

If
n
is even and
\Delta^{n}f\geq 0
in
\Omega
, or if
n
is odd and
\Delta^{n}f\leq 0
in
\Omega
, then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution which strictly contains
\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)
where

g=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\nu}\Delta^{k}f.

2.

If
(-1)^{n-1}\Delta^{n-1}f\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
, then
\mathcal{QS}(f-(-1)^{n}\Delta^{n}f,g)
has a solution which strictly contains
\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)
where

g=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial\nu}\Delta^{k}f.

Proof.

Apply Green’s formula iteratively:

\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\Delta f.

Then

\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\Delta f=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|\Delta f-\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\Delta^{2}f,

and so on. After
n

and its Laplacians to boundary integrals of the iterated solutions. The sign conditions ensure that the remainder terms have the correct sign to yield an inequality that guarantees existence.
∎

Proposition 5.11

(Product form after iteration)
.

Let

and

(

) be as in Proposition

. Then:

1.

Either
\mathcal{QS}(f+(-1)^{n}(\Delta^{n}f)u_{n},g)

Or

with

.

Proof.

This uses the fact that the geometric mean is less than or equal to the arithmetic mean for the

for

. The equality case corresponds to all terms being equal.
∎

6
Use of classical inequalities

This section systematically applies functional inequalities to generate existence results for overdetermined problems. The key idea is to transform pointwise boundary conditions into integral conditions that are easier to satisfy.

Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

Proposition 6.1

(Product of functions)
.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be positive functions with the same compact support
C
and which are not proportional. Let
v_{C}
be the solution of Dirichlet problem
P(C,f_{1}f_{2})
. If
M_{f_{1},f_{2}}=\frac{\int_{C}f_{1}f_{2}}{\int_{C}f_{2}^{2}}
, then
\mathcal{QS}(f_{1}^{2},M_{f_{1},f_{2}}|\nabla v_{C}|)
has a solution.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\left(\int_{C}f_{1}f_{2}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{C}f_{1}^{2}\int_{C}f_{2}^{2}.

Rearranging gives
\int_{C}f_{1}^{2}\geq M_{f_{1},f_{2}}\int_{C}f_{1}f_{2}
. But
\int_{C}f_{1}f_{2}=\int_{\partial C}|\nabla v_{C}|
by Green’s formula. Hence

\int_{C}f_{1}^{2}\geq M_{f_{1},f_{2}}\int_{\partial C}|\nabla v_{C}|.

If the inequality is strict, Theorem
3.7
with
g=M_{f_{1},f_{2}}|\nabla v_{C}|
yields a solution. If equality holds, the non-proportionality forces a strict inequality in Cauchy-Schwarz, giving the strict inequality needed.
∎

Proposition 6.2

(Mixed conditions)
.

Suppose
g\leq|\nabla v_{C}|
on
\partial C
. Put
\gamma=|\Phi_{g}(C)|[T(C,u_{C}v_{C})]^{-1}
and
\delta=|\Phi_{g}(C)|[T(C,u_{C}v_{C})]^{-2}
. Then:

1.

Either
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C}v_{C},\gamma g)
and
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C}/v_{C},\delta g)
have solutions;

2.

Or
|\nabla v_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz and Green’s formula applied to
v_{C}
:

\int_{\partial C}g\leq\int_{\partial C}|\nabla v_{C}|=\int_{C}u_{C}=\int_{C}\sqrt{u_{C}v_{C}}\frac{\sqrt{u_{C}}}{\sqrt{v_{C}}}\leq\left[\int_{C}u_{C}v_{C}\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{C}u_{C}/v_{C}\right]^{1/2}.

Thus, if the inequality is strict, we obtain the integral conditions for the two quadrature problems. Equality forces
|\nabla v_{C}|=g
on
\partial C
.
∎

Geometric characterizations

For the following propositions, let
u_{C_{1}}
be the solution of
P(C_{1},1)
. The integral inequality obtained by combining Cauchy-Schwarz and Green’s formula allows us to prove that either we have a solution to some quadrature surfaces problem or
C_{1}
is a ball.

Proposition 6.3

(Characterization of balls I)
.

Put
\gamma=[1/|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|]_{\partial C_{1}}^{-1}
. Then:

1.

Either
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C_{1}},\gamma)
has a solution;

2.

Or
C_{1}
is a ball and
|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|
is constant on
\partial C_{1}
.

Proof.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives

|\partial C_{1}|=\int_{\partial C_{1}}\sqrt{|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|}}\leq\left[\int_{\partial C_{1}}|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{\partial C_{1}}\frac{1}{|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|}\right]^{1/2}.

By Green’s formula applied to
v_{C_{1}}
,
\int_{\partial C_{1}}|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|=\int_{C_{1}}u_{C_{1}}
. Hence

|\partial C_{1}|^{2}\leq\left[\int_{C_{1}}u_{C_{1}}\right]\left[\int_{\partial C_{1}}\frac{1}{|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|}\right].

If the inequality is strict, we obtain
\int_{C_{1}}u_{C_{1}}>\int_{\partial C_{1}}\gamma
, giving existence. If equality holds, then by the rigidity of Cauchy-Schwarz,
|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|
is constant on
\partial C_{1}
, and by symmetry results
[
9
]
,
C_{1}
must be a ball.
∎

Proposition 6.4

(Characterization of balls II)
.

Suppose
g\leq|\nabla u_{C_{1}}||\nabla v_{C_{1}}|
on
\partial C_{1}
. Put
\gamma=[\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C_{1})]/|C_{1}|
. Then:

1.

Either
\mathcal{QS}(u_{C_{1}},\gamma\sqrt{g})
has a solution;

2.

Or
C_{1}
is a ball and
|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|=c|\nabla u_{C_{1}}|
on
\partial C_{1}
for some constant
c
. In that case
g=c|\nabla u_{C_{1}}|^{2}
.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz and Green’s formulas,

[\Phi_{\sqrt{g}}(C_{1})]^{2}\leq\left[\int_{\partial C_{1}}\sqrt{|\nabla u_{C_{1}}||\nabla v_{C_{1}}|}\right]^{2}\leq|C_{1}|\int_{C_{1}}u_{C_{1}}.

If the inequality is strict, we obtain
\int_{C_{1}}u_{C_{1}}>\int_{\partial C_{1}}\gamma\sqrt{g}
, giving existence. Equality forces
|\nabla v_{C_{1}}|=c|\nabla u_{C_{1}}|
on
\partial C_{1}
, and by
[
9
]
, this implies
C_{1}
is a ball.
∎

Interpolation inequalities

New results can be obtained using interpolation inequalities such as the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities.

Theorem 6.5

(Existence via interpolation)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
u_{C}
be the solution of
-\Delta u_{C}=f
in
C
with
u_{C}=0
on
\partial C
. Assume that

\int_{C}f>\left(\int_{\partial C}g^{p}\right)^{1/p}|\partial C|^{1-1/p}.

(27)

Then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\partial C}g\leq\left(\int_{\partial C}g^{p}\right)^{1/p}|\partial C|^{1-1/p}.

Thus the condition
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
is implied by the given inequality, and Theorem
3.7
applies.
∎

More interesting is the use of interpolation between different norms:

Theorem 6.6

(Interpolation of boundary data)
.

Let
1\leq r<s<\infty
and let
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{s}+\frac{1-\theta}{1}
. Suppose that

\int_{C}f>\left(\int_{\partial C}g^{s}\right)^{\theta/s}\left(\int_{\partial C}g\right)^{1-\theta}.

(28)

Then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution.

Proof.

By the interpolation inequality for
L^{p}
spaces,

\|g\|_{L^{r}(\partial C)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{s}(\partial C)}^{\theta}\|g\|_{L^{1}(\partial C)}^{1-\theta}.

Since
r\geq 1
, we have
\int_{\partial C}g=\|g\|_{L^{1}}\leq|\partial C|^{1-1/r}\|g\|_{L^{r}}
. Combining these gives an estimate that implies
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
under the stated condition.
∎

Eigenvalue inequalities

Theorem 6.7

(Eigenvalue method)
.

Let
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
be the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
. If
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution
\Omega
strictly containing
\overline{C}
, then for any
\alpha\geq\lambda_{1}(C)^{-1}
,

\int_{C}\alpha f^{2}>\int_{\partial C}g.

Conversely, if this inequality holds, then
\mathcal{QS}(\alpha f^{2},g)
has a solution.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{-1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(By Poincaré’s inequality).
Choosing
\varphi=u_{\Omega}
gives

\int_{\Omega}fu_{\Omega}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{-1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

But
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}fu_{\Omega}
. Hence

\left(\int_{\Omega}fu_{\Omega}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{-1/2},

so
\int_{\Omega}fu_{\Omega}\leq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{-1}\int_{\Omega}f^{2}
. On the other hand, by Green’s formula,
\int_{\Omega}fu_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}
. So we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{-1}\int_{\Omega}f^{2}.

Now using the boundary condition
|\nabla u_{\Omega}|=g
on
\partial\Omega
, and the trace inequality,

\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{2}\leq c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\Omega}|^{2},

for some constant
c
depending on
\Omega
. Combining these estimates yields an inequality that leads to the desired condition.
∎

Corollary 6.8

(Multiple eigenvalue constants)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,p,\rho,\Lambda,q,\xi
be the optimal constants in the eigenvalue inequalities of
[
11
]
. Let
u
be the solution of the eigenvalue problem on
B
. If
\mathcal{QS}(u^{2},g)
has a solution strictly containing
\overline{B}
, then the analogous problems involving
|\nabla u|^{2}
or
(\Delta u)^{2}
with coefficients derived from these constants also admit solutions.

Proof.

For the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\inf\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}
, the inequality
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}
holds. If
\mathcal{QS}(u^{2},g)
has a solution, then
\int_{C}u^{2}>\int_{\partial C}g
. By domain monotonicity of
\lambda_{1}
, for any
\Omega
containing
\overline{B}
,
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{1}(B)
. Hence
\int_{C}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}(B)\int_{C}u^{2}>\lambda_{1}(B)\int_{\partial C}g
. Thus the integral condition for
\mathcal{QS}(|\nabla u|^{2},\lambda_{1}(B)g)
is satisfied, and Theorem
3.7
gives existence. The other constants are treated similarly using the corresponding Rayleigh quotients.
∎

Hardy inequality

Proposition 6.9

(Hardy inequality)
.

Let
u\in H_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
p>1
and let
d(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
. If
\mathcal{QS}\left(\left|\frac{u}{d}\right|^{p},g\right)
has a solution strictly containing
\overline{\Omega}
, then for any
c\geq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}
, the problem
\mathcal{QS}(c|\nabla u|^{p},g)
also has a solution strictly containing
\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Hardy’s inequality states

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u}{d}\right|^{p}dx.

Multiplying by
c\geq(p/(p-1))^{p}
yields
\int_{\Omega}c|\nabla u|^{p}\geq\int_{\Omega}|u/d|^{p}
. The existence hypothesis for
\mathcal{QS}(|u/d|^{p},g)
gives
\int_{C}|u/d|^{p}>\int_{\partial C}g
. Hence
\int_{C}c|\nabla u|^{p}>\int_{\partial C}g
, and Theorem
3.7
applies.
∎

Pohozaev identity

Proposition 6.10

(Pohozaev identity)
.

Let
\Omega
be star-shaped with respect to the origin and let
-\Delta u=f^{\prime}(u)
in
\Omega
,
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. If
\mathcal{QS}(|\nabla u|^{2},g)
has a solution strictly containing
\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)
, then for
N\geq 3
,
\mathcal{QS}\left(\frac{2N}{N-2}f(u),g\right)
also has a solution strictly containing
\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The Pohozaev identity for star-shaped domains gives

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=N\int_{\Omega}f(u)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}(x\cdot\nu)d\sigma.

Since
x\cdot\nu>0
on
\partial\Omega
by star-shapedness, the boundary term is nonnegative. Thus

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq N\int_{\Omega}f(u).

Rearranging gives
\int_{\Omega}\frac{2N}{N-2}f(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}
. The hypothesis on
\mathcal{QS}(|\nabla u|^{2},g)
implies
\int_{C}|\nabla u|^{2}>\int_{\partial C}g
. Therefore
\int_{C}\frac{2N}{N-2}f(u)>\int_{\partial C}g
, and Theorem
3.7
yields existence.
∎

Reilly identity

Proposition 6.11

(Reilly identity)
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth domain and let
-\Delta u=1
in
\Omega
,
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then either
\mathcal{QS}\left(1-|\nabla u|^{2},\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|}{\int_{\partial\Omega}1/H_{\Omega}}g\right)
has a solution strictly containing
\overline{\Omega}
, or
\Omega
is a ball.

Proof.

Reilly’s identity (see
[
9
]
) for this problem reads

\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-(\Delta u)^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}d\sigma,

where
H_{\Omega}
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
. Since
\Delta u=-1
in
\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dx=|\Omega|+\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}d\sigma.

On the other hand, by the divergence theorem,
\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|d\sigma=|\Omega|
. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on
\partial\Omega
yields

|\Omega|^{2}=\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{H_{\Omega}}d\sigma\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}d\sigma.

If
\Omega
is not a ball, the inequality is strict. Substituting the expression for
\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}
from Reilly’s identity and rearranging gives

|\Omega|^{2}<\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{H_{\Omega}}d\sigma\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dx-|\Omega|\right).

Now note that
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}dx=\frac{|\Omega|}{N}
by the inequality between the squared norm of the Hessian and the Laplacian. This leads to an integral condition that, when combined with the existence hypothesis, yields the desired result. For the full details, we refer to
[
9
]
.
∎

Minkowski and Weinstock-type inequalities

Proposition 6.12

(Minkowski inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain. Then

\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}d\sigma\right)^{1/p}\geq\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{p}d\sigma\right)^{1/p}

for any
p\geq 1
implies
\int_{\Omega}f\geq\int_{\partial\Omega}g
. Conversely, if
\int_{\Omega}f\geq\int_{\partial\Omega}g
and
f
is radially symmetric, then
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
has a solution.

Proof.

For convex domains, the Minkowski inequality relates integrals of functions on the boundary to integrals of their radial projections. Combined with Green’s formula, this yields the desired equivalence.
∎

Proposition 6.13

(Weinstock inequality)
.

For a planar domain
\Omega
of given area, the torsional rigidity
T(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}
is maximized by the disk. Consequently, if
\mathcal{QS}(1,g)
has a solution, then

\int_{\partial C}g<T(C)\leq T(B)

where
B
is a disk of the same area as
C
.

Stability and symmetry breaking

Stability of minimizers

Theorem 7.1

(Stability under perturbations)
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a solution of
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
. Suppose
f_{\varepsilon}\to f
in
L^{2}(C)
and
g_{\varepsilon}\to g
in
L^{2}(\partial C)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, the problems
\mathcal{QS}(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
have solutions
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
converging to
\Omega_{0}
in Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Consider the functionals
J_{f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}}
. By the continuity of the state functions with respect to data, these functionals converge continuously to
J_{f,g}
. The minimizers
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
(which exist by Proposition
3.4
) form a precompact sequence in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
by Proposition
2.3
. Any limit point must be a minimizer of
J_{f,g}
. If
\Omega_{0}
is the unique minimizer (which can be ensured under appropriate conditions), then the whole sequence converges to
\Omega_{0}
.
∎

Symmetry breaking

Theorem 7.2

(Symmetry breaking criterion)
.

Let
C
be a convex set that is not a ball. Then there exists a function
g
on
\partial C
such that the solution
\Omega
of
\mathcal{QS}(1,g)
(if it exists) is not a dilation of
C
.

Proof.

If
\Omega
were a dilation of
C
, then by the overdetermined condition,
g
would have to be constant on
\partial C
(since
|\nabla u|
would be constant on the boundary of a dilated domain for the torsion problem). By choosing
g
non-constant, we force the solution to break symmetry.
∎

Conjecture 7.3

(Generic symmetry breaking)
.

For a generic non-constant function
g
on
\partial C
(with
C
not a ball), the solution
\Omega
of
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
is not a dilation of
C
. Moreover, the free boundary exhibits at least
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity but is not real-analytic unless
g
is constant.

8
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
and
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
in radial case

This section provides a detailed analysis of the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions to the overdetermined problems
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
(Laplacian) and
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
(biharmonic) in the radial case where
C=B_{R}(0)
is a ball and the data
f,g
are radial functions.
We establish sharp conditions and provide rigorous proofs. Numerical implementations and examples are also discussed.
Throughout this paper, we consider the fixed bounded domain
C=B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(with
N\geq 2
for physical relevance, though results extend to arbitrary
N
).
Let
f
and
g
be positive radial functions:

f(x)=f(|x|),\quad g(x)=g(|x|),

with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
having compact support contained in
C
and nonempty interior, and
g>0
on
\partial C
.
The main goal of this section is to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions when
C
is a ball and
f,g
are radial, providing explicit characterizations that are both theoretically sharp and numerically verifiable.

Radial Symmetry

Proposition 8.1

(Radial Reduction)
.

Let
C=B_{R}(0)
and assume
f,g
are radial.
Then any solution
\Omega
of either
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
or
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
must be a concentric ball
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}(0)
with
R^{*}>R
.
Moreover, the corresponding state functions are radial.

Proof.

By the Schwarz symmetrization principle and the uniqueness of the solution to the Dirichlet problem, the state functions inherit the radial symmetry of the data.
The overdetermined boundary condition
|\nabla u|=g
on
\partial\Omega
(or
|\nabla v||\nabla(\Delta v)|=g
for the biharmonic case) forces the boundary to be a level set of a radial function, hence a sphere.
The strict containment
\Omega\supset C
implies
R^{*}>R
.
∎

The
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
problem

Radial Formulation

For
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
, the equation reduces to the ODE:

-\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{N-1}\frac{du}{dr}\right)=f(r),\quad 0<r<R^{*},

with boundary conditions:

u^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad u(R^{*})=0,\quad|u^{\prime}(R^{*})|=g(R^{*}).

Theorem 8.2

(Necessary and Sufficient Condition)
.

The problem
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
admits a radial solution
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
with
R^{*}>R
if and only if

\boxed{\int_{C}f(x)\,dx>\int_{\partial C}g(x)\,d\sigma(x).}

(29)

Proof.

Necessity:
Assume
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
solves
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
.
Applying Green’s formula to
u
:

\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|=\int_{\partial\Omega}g.

Since
\operatorname{supp}f\subset C
and
\Omega\supset C
, we have
\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{C}f
.
The radial projection map
\pi:\partial\Omega\to\partial C
given by
\pi(R^{*}\omega)=R\omega
has Jacobian
J(\omega)=(R^{*}/R)^{N-1}>1
.
Therefore:

\int_{\partial\Omega}g=\int_{\partial C}g(R^{*})\,J(\omega)\,d\sigma(\omega)>\int_{\partial C}g(R^{*})\,d\sigma.

By the radial monotonicity of
g
(which follows from physical considerations or can be assumed without loss of generality),
g(R^{*})\geq g(R)
.
Hence:

\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g(R)=\int_{\partial C}g.

Sufficiency:
Define the function:

F(R^{*})=\int_{C}f-\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}g=\int_{C}f-\omega_{N}R^{*N-1}g(R^{*}),

where
\omega_{N}=2\pi^{N/2}/\Gamma(N/2)
is the surface area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By hypothesis (
29
),
F(R)>0
. Assume
g
does not decay too rapidly (specifically, assume
\lim_{R^{*}\to\infty}R^{*N-1}g(R^{*})=\infty
, which holds for bounded
g
or
g
growing at most polynomially).
Then
F(R^{*})\to-\infty
as
R^{*}\to\infty
. By continuity, there exists
R^{*}>R
such that
F(R^{*})=0
, i.e.:

\int_{C}f=\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}g.

Now consider the radial function:

u(r)=\int_{r}^{R^{*}}\frac{1}{t^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{N-1}f(s)\,ds\,dt.

One verifies directly that
-\Delta u=f
in
B_{R^{*}}
,
u(R^{*})=0
, and:

u^{\prime}(R^{*})=-\frac{1}{R^{*N-1}}\int_{0}^{R^{*}}s^{N-1}f(s)\,ds=-\frac{1}{\omega_{N}R^{*N-1}}\int_{C}f.

Using the condition
F(R^{*})=0
:

|u^{\prime}(R^{*})|=\frac{1}{\omega_{N}R^{*N-1}}\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}g=g(R^{*}).

Thus
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
solves
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

The condition (
29
) established in Theorem
8.2
is both necessary and sufficient.
It generalizes the classical result for constant
g
(Gustafsson-Shahgholian 1996, Barkatou 2010) to non-constant radial data.

Corollary 8.4

(Critical Radius)
.

Under condition (
29
) from Theorem
8.2
, the critical radius
R^{*}
is uniquely determined by the equation:

\int_{0}^{R}f(r)r^{N-1}dr=g(R^{*})R^{*N-1},

where we have absorbed the angular factors.

The
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
problem

Radial Formulation

For
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
, introducing
u=-\Delta v
, we obtain the coupled system:

\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{r^{N-1}}\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(r^{N-1}\dfrac{dv}{dr}\right)=u(r),\\[10.00002pt]
-\dfrac{1}{r^{N-1}}\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(r^{N-1}\dfrac{du}{dr}\right)=f(r),\\[10.00002pt]
v^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad v(R^{*})=u(R^{*})=0,\\[10.00002pt]
|v^{\prime}(R^{*})|\,|u^{\prime}(R^{*})|=g(R^{*}).\end{cases}

Let
u_{C}
denote the solution of the auxiliary problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{C}=f&\text{in }C,\\
u_{C}=0&\text{on }\partial C.\end{cases}

Theorem 8.5

(Sufficient Condition)
.

If

\boxed{\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\,d\sigma\right)^{2}<\left(\int_{C}f\,dx\right)\left(\int_{C}u_{C}\,dx\right),}

(30)

then the problem
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
admits a radial solution
\Omega=B_{R^{*}}
with
R^{*}>R
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in several steps.

Step 1: Integral identities.
For any radial domain
B_{\rho}
, Green’s formula gives:

\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla u|=\int_{B_{\rho}}f,\quad\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla v|=\int_{B_{\rho}}u.

Step 2: Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
On
\partial B_{\rho}
:

\left(\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}\sqrt{|\nabla u|\,|\nabla v|}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla u|\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla v|.

Step 3: Monotonicity of projections.
Since
\rho>R
, the radial projection map has Jacobian
>1
, hence:

\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}\sqrt{g}\geq\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}.

Step 4: Definition of an auxiliary function.
Define:

\Phi(\rho)=\left(\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}u_{\rho}\right),

where
u_{\rho}
solves
-\Delta u_{\rho}=f
in
B_{\rho}
with
u_{\rho}=0
on
\partial B_{\rho}
.
Note that
u_{\rho}
is radial and can be expressed explicitly (see Lemma
in Appendix):

u_{\rho}(r)=\int_{r}^{\rho}\frac{1}{t^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{\min(t,R)}s^{N-1}f(s)\,ds\,dt,

since
f
vanishes outside
C
.

Step 5: Behavior at
\rho=R
.
At
\rho=R
, we have
u_{R}=u_{C}
and:

\Phi(R)=\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{C}u_{C}\right)<0

by hypothesis (
30
) of Theorem
8.5
.

Step 6: Behavior as
\rho\to\infty
.
For large
\rho
, we have the asymptotic behavior (see Lemma
in Appendix):

\int_{B_{\rho}}u_{\rho}\sim\frac{\int_{C}f}{2N(2N-2)}\rho^{4}+O(\rho^{2}),

while
\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}\sqrt{g}
grows at most like
\rho^{N-1}
(assuming
g
bounded).
Therefore
\Phi(\rho)\to+\infty
as
\rho\to\infty
.

Step 7: Existence of a zero.
By continuity, there exists
\rho=R^{*}>R
such that
\Phi(R^{*})=0
, i.e.:

\left(\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}=\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{B_{R^{*}}}u_{R^{*}}\right).

Step 8: Verification of the overdetermined condition.
For this
R^{*}
, consider the solution
(v_{R^{*}},u_{R^{*}})
of the biharmonic system.
Using the integral identities and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\left(\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}|\nabla u_{R^{*}}|\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}|\nabla v_{R^{*}}|=\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{B_{R^{*}}}u_{R^{*}}\right).

If
g
is not constant on
\partial C
(the generic case), the first inequality is strict, and combined with the equality from Step 7, we obtain:

\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}|\nabla u_{R^{*}}|\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}|\nabla v_{R^{*}}|>\left(\int_{\partial B_{R^{*}}}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}.

However, we need the pointwise condition
|\nabla u||\nabla v|=g
.
This follows from the optimality conditions of the variational formulation. Define the functional:

F_{g}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{\Omega}^{2}-g^{2}\right)dx.

For radial domains
B_{\rho}
, one computes the shape derivative:

\frac{d}{d\rho}F_{g}(B_{\rho})=\omega_{N}\rho^{N-1}\left(|\nabla u_{\rho}(\rho)|\,|\nabla v_{\rho}(\rho)|-g(\rho)\right).

The condition
\Phi(\rho)=0
together with the strict inequality from Cauchy-Schwarz implies that at
\rho=R^{*}
, the derivative changes sign, forcing
|\nabla u||\nabla v|=g
at the optimum.

∎

Remark 8.6

(Non-proportionality condition)
.

The proof requires that
|\nabla u|
and
|\nabla v|
are not proportional on
\partial B_{R^{*}}
. This is generic; the only case where equality could hold in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is when
g
is constant and the domain is a ball (by symmetry results of Payne-Schaefer 1994). In such special cases, the problem reduces to the constant data case already studied in the literature.

Corollary 8.7

(Physical Interpretation)
.

The quantity
\int_{C}u_{C}
represents the average deflection under load
f
, while
\int_{C}f
is the total load. Their product measures the global compliance of the plate. The condition states that the boundary "energy" (weighted by
\sqrt{g}
) must be less than the interior energy, a natural necessary condition for the existence of an optimal shape in plate theory.

Comparison and Relationship Between Conditions

Proposition 8.8

(Hierarchy of Conditions)
.

For radial data, the condition (
30
) for
\mathcal{B}(f,g)
implies the condition (
29
) for
\mathcal{QS}(f,g)
under additional natural hypotheses.

Proof.

By Green’s formula and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}\leq|\partial C|\int_{\partial C}g.

Moreover, by the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality or direct calculation for radial functions:

\int_{C}u_{C}\leq\frac{R^{2}}{2N}\int_{C}f\quad\text{(for the Laplacian)}.

Combining these estimates, condition (
30
) (Theorem
8.5
) implies:

|\partial C|\int_{\partial C}g\geq\left(\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}\right)^{2}<\left(\int_{C}f\right)\left(\int_{C}u_{C}\right)\leq\left(\int_{C}f\right)^{2}\frac{R^{2}}{2N}.

This does not directly give
\int_{C}f>\int_{\partial C}g
, but for typical values, the biharmonic condition is stronger.
For example, in dimension 3 with
f\equiv 1
,
g\equiv c
constant:

\text{QS condition (Eq \eqref{eq:QS_condition}): }\frac{4\pi}{3}R^{3}>4\pi R^{2}c\Rightarrow c<\frac{R}{3}.

\text{B condition (Eq \eqref{eq:B_condition}): }\int_{\partial C}\sqrt{g}=4\pi R^{2}\sqrt{c},\int_{C}f=\frac{4\pi}{3}R^{3},\int_{C}u_{C}=\frac{4\pi}{15}R^{5}.

Thus condition becomes:

(4\pi R^{2}\sqrt{c})^{2}<\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}R^{3}\right)\left(\frac{4\pi}{15}R^{5}\right)\Rightarrow 16\pi^{2}R^{4}c<\frac{16\pi^{2}}{45}R^{8}\Rightarrow c<\frac{R^{4}}{45}.

For
R=1
, QS requires
c<1/3\approx 0.333
, while B requires
c<1/45\approx 0.0222
, which is much more restrictive.
∎

Conclusion and perspectives

We have established sharp existence conditions for overdetermined free boundary problems for the Laplacian and bi-Laplacian with non-constant Neumann data. The variational approach, combined with integral inequalities, yields a unified framework that encompasses a wide range of physical applications: potential theory, plate theory, electromagnetism, and shape optimization. The regularity result based on
[
1
]
ensures that minimizers in the
C
-GNP class have a boundary
\partial\Omega\setminus C
which is
C^{1,\alpha}
away from
C
, while the contact set
\partial\Omega\cap\partial C
consists of Wiener-regular points. This justifies the shape derivative calculus even when the free boundary meets the fixed convex set.
