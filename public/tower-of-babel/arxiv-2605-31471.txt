Convergence Rates of Continuous-Time Random Walks to Time-Fractional Diffusions with Unbounded Coefficients
Artur Sidorenko, Vasilii Kolokoltsov

Abstract
We investigate uniform weak convergence rates for probabilistic numerical methods applied to backward time-fractional diffusion equations whose dynamics are driven by diffusions with possibly unbounded coefficients, such as the Geometric Brownian Motion. The fractional structure is represented through a random time-change by the inverse of a stable subordinator. To approximate the underlying fractional dynamics, we combine discrete Markov chain schemes for the diffusion component with heavy-tailed random walk approximations of the time change. Our analysis builds on Feller semigroup techniques and a high-order sensitivity framework for diffusion semigroups based on the Kunita stochastic flows and tensor fields. We derive uniform bounds for all orders of sensitivities, establish a quasi-contraction property for the associated semigroup, and transfer these estimates to the fractional setting via the convolution representation with the inverse subordinator. As a result, under killing conditions which dominate at least the base-space semigroup growth, we obtain weak convergence rates for the combined continuous-time-random-walk scheme to the time-fractional diffusion, with a logarithmic regime before the discount dominates the stronger smooth-space growth.

Introduction

We consider the following time-fractional Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}^{\beta}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\bigl(Lu(t,x)-c\,u(t,x)\bigr),\qquad u(0,x)=f(x),

(1)

where
c\geq 0
, and

\partial_{t}^{\beta}g(t)=D_{0+\star}^{\beta}g(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}g^{\prime}(s)\,ds

(2)

is the Caputo–Dzherbashian fractional derivative acting in the time variable
t
, whereas
L
is the generator of a diffusion process acting in the space variable
x
.

The solution to (
1
) admits the following probabilistic representation (see, e.g. the monographs
Kolokoltsov 2011
;
Kolokoltsov 2010
)

u(t,x)={\mathbb{E}}_{x}\bigl[e^{-c\hat{\sigma}_{t}}f(X_{\hat{\sigma}_{t}})\bigr],

(3)

where
X
is a diffusion process with generator
L
, and
\hat{\sigma}_{t}
is the inverse of a
\beta
-stable subordinator.
The representation (
3
) leads to numerical schemes based on continuous-time random walks (CTRWs).
The main question of interest is to obtain quantitative convergence rates of these CTRW-based approximations to the true solution
u
. The equation itself is meaningful for
c\geq 0
; the fractional convergence theorem proved below is stated in the killed regimes where the discount parameter
c
dominates at least the base-space growth rate, with a logarithmic rate before it dominates the stronger smooth-space growth rate.

For bounded diffusion coefficients, weak convergence and convergence rates of CTRW approximations were obtained in
Kolokoltsov 2023
.
In this paper we extend these results to the case of unbounded diffusion coefficients.
This setting includes affine coefficients. These examples are covered in
Theorem
12
and Corollary
15
.

We work with continuous-time random walk (CTRW) approximations.
CTRW processes, originally introduced in physics
Montroll and Weiss 1965
, are known to have scaling limits described by Markov processes with a random time change given by inverse stable subordinators
Kolokoltsov 2009
.
Numerical methods for time-fractional PDEs have been developed both in the deterministic and probabilistic settings; here we use the probabilistic CTRW approximation framework of
Kolokoltsov 2023
.

Our analysis relies on the Feller semigroup techniques of
Kolokoltsov 2011
;
Kolokoltsov 2010
and on CTRW approximations of stable subordinators proposed in
Kolokoltsov 2023
.
To obtain convergence rates for our CTRW approximations, we analyse high-order sensitivities of the diffusion semigroup,

\mathrm{D}^{k}F_{t}f(x),

and establish a quasi-contraction property for the diffusion Feller semigroup
F_{t}
on spaces of smooth functions with bounded (weighted) derivatives.
We use Kunita’s stochastic flow theory
Kunita 1984
;
Kunita 1990
;
Kunita 2019
, which allows for pathwise analysis of derivatives.
Under mild conditions higher-order derivatives can be organised into tensor fields.
We employ a recent chain rule for tensor fields
Licht 2024
, originating from the Faà di Bruno formula
Comtet 2012
;
Savits 2006
.

Our main contributions are as follows:

1.

We obtain convergence rate estimates for CTRW schemes approximating the probabilistic solution (
3
) of the killed time-fractional Cauchy problem (
1
) with unbounded diffusion coefficients, including a logarithmic fractional rate when the killing dominates the base-space growth but not the stronger smooth-space growth.

2.

We prove uniform convergence rates for random-walk approximations of diffusions with unbounded coefficients, at the level of Feller semigroups.

3.

We develop a methodology for investigating sensitivities of diffusion processes

\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x,\omega)

with respect to the initial condition
x
as random tensor fields, based on Kunita’s stochastic flow theory and the tensorial chain rule, and derive corresponding growth estimates.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
Section
2
contains the precise setting and the formulation of the main results.
Section
3
introduces the stochastic tensor field notation, the tensorial chain rule, and the basic elements of Kunita’s theory used in this work.
In Section
4
we study the sensitivities of the diffusion semigroup with respect to the initial point and establish the quasi-contraction property.
Section
5
records the weighted Feller facts used for unbounded observables.
Section
6
contains the proofs of the main results, including the weak convergence rates for the time-fractional diffusion.

3
Random Tensor Fields and Kunita Stochastic Flows

3.1
Tensor fields

Let
X={\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
Y={\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
be finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces.
For integers
m\geq 0
, denote by

\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,Y)

the space of continuous
m
-linear mappings

A:X^{m}\to Y.

In coordinates (for simplicity, when
Y={\mathbb{R}}
) an element
A\in\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
is represented by

\bigl(a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\bigr),\qquad 1\leq i_{p}\leq k,

and acts via

A[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{k}a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\,x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{i_{m}}.

To avoid ambiguity, we put the arguments of a tensor inside square brackets.

For
A\in\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,Y)
, define the operator (spectral) norm

\|A\|:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in X\\
x_{i}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|A(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|}{\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{m}\|}.

(20)

Because all norms in finite dimensions are equivalent, the operator norm
is comparable to the Frobenius norm

\|A\|_{F}^{2}:=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\bigl(a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\bigr)^{2}.

For elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in X
, we denote their tensor product by

x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{m}
or
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}
.
The tensor product of normed linear spaces
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
is denoted by

X_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes X_{n}
or
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
.
The
m
-th tensor power of
X
is denoted by
X^{\otimes m}
.

For an element
u\in X_{1}\otimes X_{2}
, define the
projective tensor norm

\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
by

\|u\|_{\pi}:=\inf\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x_{1,i}\|\,\|x_{2,i}\|\ \Big|\ u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{1,i}\otimes x_{2,i},\;x_{1,i}\in X_{1},\;x_{2,i}\in X_{2},\;N\in\mathbb{N}\Bigr\}.

In the finite-dimensional case,
X_{1}\otimes X_{2}
equipped with the projective tensor norm
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
is complete.

Any tensor
A\in\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,Y)
can be viewed as an element of

\mathcal{L}(X^{\otimes m},Y)
.
In fact, the tensor norm on the former coincides with the operator norm on the latter (up to equivalence of norms in finite dimensions).

For a tensor
A\in\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,Y)
, define its symmetrisation as

\operatorname{Sym}(A)[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]:=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Perm}(m)}A[x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(m)}].

We define the symmetric product
x_{1}\vee x_{2}
as

\operatorname{Sym}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})
, and in general

x_{1}\vee\dots\vee x_{m}:=\operatorname{Sym}\bigl(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{m}\bigr).

When all arguments coincide, we also write

v^{\vee m}:=v\vee\dots\vee v
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a function
f\in C^{m}(X)
(scalar-valued), the tensor

\nabla f(x)\in\mathfrak{L}_{1}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
is defined as the first-order directional derivative

\nabla f(x)[u]:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{f(x+\varepsilon u)-f(x)}{\varepsilon},

and the tensor
\mathrm{D}^{m}f(x)\in\mathfrak{L}_{m}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
is the iterated
m
-th order directional derivative. In coordinates,

\mathrm{D}^{m}f(x)[u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{k}\frac{\partial^{m}f(x)}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\dots\partial x_{i_{m}}}\,u^{i_{1}}_{1}\dots u^{i_{m}}_{m}.

The tensor
\mathrm{D}^{m}f(x)
is symmetric, so we also write

\mathrm{D}^{m}f(x)\bigl[x_{1}\vee\dots\vee x_{m}\bigr]

instead of
\mathrm{D}^{m}f(x)[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
.

3.2
Kunita’s theory in tensor notation

We view the derivatives
\mathrm{D}^{j}X_{t}(x,\omega)
with respect to the initial point as random tensor fields and recall the needed parts of Kunita’s stochastic flow theory
Kunita 1984
in tensor notation.

We recall
(
Kunita 1984
, Theorem 3.1)
in the notation used here.

Theorem

\@upn
16
.

Consider the SDE (
4
).
Let Assumption
1
(items
(1)
–
(2)
) hold.
Assume that
\nabla\sigma_{j}(x)
is well-defined and (globally)

-Hölder continuous for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and all

Then the process

\nabla X_{t}(x)=e+\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\nabla b\bigl(X_{s}(x)\bigr)\bigr)\,\nabla X_{s}(x)\,ds+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\nabla\sigma_{l}\bigl(X_{s}(x)\bigr)\bigr)\,\nabla X_{s}(x)\,dW_{s}^{l},

(21)

where

is the identity mapping on
X
.
Moreover, for every
u\in X
and
p\geq 2
we have

\nabla X_{t}(x)[u]\in L^{p}
.

For higher-order derivatives we use a tensorial Faà di Bruno formula.
For a finite set
A
, denote by
\mathcal{P}(A,k)
the set of partitions of
A

into
k
disjoint non-empty subsets:

\mathcal{P}(A,k):=\left\{P=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}:A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}P_{i},\;P_{i}\cap P_{j}=\varnothing,\ i\neq j,\;P_{i}\neq\varnothing,\ i=1,\dots,k\right\}.

For brevity, write
\mathcal{P}(m,k):=\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,m\},k)
.

We use the following tensor form of the Faà di Bruno formula; cf.
(
Licht 2024
, Proposition 3.1)
.

Proposition

\@upn
17
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be Banach spaces and let
f\colon X\to Y
,

g\colon Y\to Z
.
Let
x\in X
and set
y=f(x)
.
Assume that
f
and
g
have continuous derivatives up to order
m
in a neighbourhood of
x
and
y
, respectively. Then, for

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{m}(g\circ f)(x)[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\ \sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)}\mathrm{D}^{k}g\bigl(f(x)\bigr)\Bigl[\mathrm{D}^{|P_{1}|}f(x)\bigl[\bigvee_{i\in P_{1}}v_{i}\bigr],\dots,\mathrm{D}^{|P_{k}|}f(x)\bigl[\bigvee_{i\in P_{k}}v_{i}\bigr]\Bigr].

(22)

This is the tensor form of the Faà di Bruno formula. The classical and multivariate versions are discussed in
Comtet 2012
;
Constantine and
Savits 1996
; see also
Savits 2006
for probabilistic applications.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
C^{m,\alpha}
and
C^{m,\alpha}_{g}
denote the classes of maps
f:X\to X
whose derivatives up to order
m
are continuous and whose
m
-th derivatives are locally, respectively globally,
\alpha
-Hölder continuous.

We also use
(
Kunita 1984
, Theorem 3.3)
.

Theorem

\@upn
18
.

Consider the SDE (
4
).
Let
m\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and suppose

b,\sigma_{j}\in C^{m,\alpha}_{g}
for all
j=0,\dots,d
.
Assume that all derivatives of
b
and
\sigma_{j}
up to order
m
are bounded.
Then, for each
t\geq 0
, the mapping
x\mapsto\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)
is of class
C^{m,\beta}
almost surely, for any
\beta<\alpha
.
Furthermore,
\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)
satisfies an SDE of the form

\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\mathrm{D}^{m}b\bigl(X_{s}(x)\bigr)\bigr)\bigl[\cdot\bigr]\,ds+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\mathrm{D}^{m}\sigma_{l}\bigl(X_{s}(x)\bigr)\bigr)\bigl[\cdot\bigr]\,dW_{s}^{l},

(23)

where the dots indicate multilinear dependence on

\mathrm{D}^{k}X_{s}(x)
,
k=1,\dots,m
, given explicitly via Proposition

.
Moreover, for any
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in X
and

,

\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)[u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]\in L^{p}.

Equation (

) involves the lower-order derivatives

\mathrm{D}^{k}X_{t}(x)
,
k=1,\dots,m-1
. In particular, by
Proposition

, the coefficients can be written in closed form in terms of the tensors
\mathrm{D}^{k}b
and
\mathrm{D}^{k}\sigma_{j}
.

4.1
First-order derivative

Lemma

\@upn
19
.

Consider the SDE (
4
) under Assumption
1
.
Let
p\geq 2
and
v\in{\cal X}
with
|v|=1
. Then there exists a constant

\mu_{1,p}=\mu_{1,p}(K,d,p)>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl|\nabla X_{t}(x)[v]\bigr|^{p}\;\leq\;e^{\mu_{1,p}t},\qquad t\geq 0.

(24)

Proof.

For
p\geq 2
, the map
y\mapsto|y|^{p}
on
{\cal X}
is twice continuously differentiable.
Set

Y_{t}:=\nabla X_{t}(x)[v].

By Theorem
16
,
Y_{t}
satisfies the linear SDE

dY_{t}=\bigl(\nabla b(X_{t}(x))\,Y_{t}\bigr)\,dt+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\bigl(\nabla\sigma_{l}(X_{t}(x))\,Y_{t}\bigr)\,dW_{t}^{l},\qquad Y_{0}=v.

Applying Itô’s formula to
t\mapsto|Y_{t}|^{p}
gives

d|Y_{t}|^{p}=p|Y_{t}|^{p-2}\langle Y_{t},dY_{t}\rangle+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}|Y_{t}|^{p-2}d\langle Y\rangle_{t},

where
\langle Y\rangle_{t}
is the quadratic variation of
Y
. Taking expectations and using the boundedness of
\nabla b
and
\nabla\sigma_{l}
from Assumption
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\mathbb{E}}|Y_{t}|^{p}

\displaystyle=p\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[|Y_{t}|^{p-2}\big\langle Y_{t},\nabla b(X_{t})Y_{t}\big\rangle\Bigr]+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[|Y_{t}|^{p-2}\,\big|\nabla\sigma_{l}(X_{t})Y_{t}\big|^{2}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,p}\,{\mathbb{E}}|Y_{t}|^{p},

where
C_{1,p}=C_{1,p}(K,d,p)
is finite and can be computed explicitly by Young and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities. Since
Y_{0}=v
and
|v|=1
, Grönwall’s lemma yields

{\mathbb{E}}|Y_{t}|^{p}\leq e^{C_{1,p}t},\qquad t\geq 0.

Setting
\mu_{1,p}:=C_{1,p}
gives (
24
).
∎

4.2
Higher-order derivatives

We derive inductive bounds for higher-order derivatives of the flow.

Proposition

\@upn
20
.

Consider the SDE (
4
) under Assumption
1
.
Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\geq 2
. Then there exist functions

A_{m}(\cdot,p):[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that, for all

v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in{\cal X}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]\bigr\|^{p}\;\leq\;A_{m}(t,p)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|v_{j}\|^{p},\qquad t\geq 0.

(25)

Moreover, there exist constants
\mu_{m,p}>0
and
\lambda_{m,p}>0

depending only on
K,d,m,p
such that

\displaystyle A_{1}(t,p)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\mu_{1,p}t},

(26)

\displaystyle A_{m}(t,p)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{m,p}\bigl(e^{\mu_{m,p}t}-1\bigr),\qquad m\geq 2.

(27)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
.
The case
m=1
is given by Lemma
19
, which yields

A_{1}(t,p)\leq e^{\mu_{1,p}t}
.

Let
m\geq 2
and suppose the claim holds for all orders

1,\dots,m-1
. For brevity, set

Z_{t}^{(m)}:=\mathrm{D}^{m}X_{t}(x)[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}].

By Kunita’s theory and the tensorial Faà di Bruno formula
(Proposition
17
),
Z_{t}^{(m)}
satisfies a linear SDE whose drift and diffusion coefficients at time
s
are finite linear combinations of terms of the form

\mathrm{D}^{k}b(X_{s}(x))\Bigl[\mathrm{D}^{|P_{1}|}X_{s}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{1}}v_{i}],\dots,\mathrm{D}^{|P_{k}|}X_{s}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{k}}v_{i}]\Bigr],

and similarly with
\sigma_{l}
instead of
b
, where

1\leq k\leq m
and
P=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
runs over partitions

P\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)
.

Using the boundedness of the derivatives of
b
and
\sigma_{l}
in Assumption
1
, we have for each such term

\Bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{k}\sigma_{l}(X_{s}(x))\bigl[\mathrm{D}^{|P_{1}|}X_{s}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{1}}v_{i}],\dots,\mathrm{D}^{|P_{k}|}X_{s}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{k}}v_{i}]\bigr]\Bigr\|\leq K\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{|P_{j}|}X_{s}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{j}}v_{i}]\Bigr\|.

In this expansion the single-block partition

produces the term

\mathrm{D}b(X_{s}(x))\,Z_{s}^{(m)}
, and likewise
\mathrm{D}\sigma_{l}(X_{s}(x))\,Z_{s}^{(m)}

in the diffusion part; these are the only terms linear in
Z_{s}^{(m)}
, they
are bounded by Assumption
1
, and we keep them on the
homogeneous side. Every remaining partition has
k\geq 2
blocks, and since

\sum_{j}|P_{j}|=m
each such block has size at most

.
Applying Itô’s formula to

, taking expectations and dropping martingale terms, we obtain an integral inequality of the form

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl\|Z_{t}^{(m)}\bigr\|^{p}\leq C_{m,p}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl\|Z_{t}^{(m)}\bigr\|^{p}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{|P_{j}|}X_{t}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{j}}v_{i}]\bigr\|^{p}\Bigr],

where
C_{m,p}
depends only on
K,d,m,p
.

Fix a partition
P=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)
with

2\leq k\leq m
. Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponents
(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})

given by
q_{j}:=k
to the product of
k

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{|P_{j}|}X_{t}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{j}}v_{i}]\bigr\|^{p}\Bigr]\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\bigl\|\mathrm{D}^{|P_{j}|}X_{t}(x)[\vee_{i\in P_{j}}v_{i}]\bigr\|^{kp}\Bigr)^{1/k}.

Since
|P_{j}|\leq m-1
for every block, the induction hypothesis applied
with exponent
kp\geq 2
gives

{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathrm{D}^{|P_{j}|}X_{t}(x)[\vee v_{i}]\|^{kp}\leq A_{|P_{j}|}(t,kp)\prod_{i\in P_{j}}|v_{i}|^{kp}
,
and therefore

\frac{d}{dt}A_{m}(t,p)\leq C_{m,p}\,A_{m}(t,p)+\tilde{C}_{m,p}\sum_{k=2}^{m}\sum_{P\in{\cal P}(m,k)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}A_{|P_{j}|}(t,kp)^{1/k},

where
\tilde{C}_{m,p}
depends only on

. Each

is, by the induction hypothesis,
bounded by
\lambda_{l,kp}(e^{\mu_{l,kp}t}-1)
for
l\geq 2
and by

e^{\mu_{1,kp}t}
for

. Substituting these bounds and applying

yields

A_{m}(t,p)\leq\lambda_{m,p}\bigl(e^{\mu_{m,p}t}-1\bigr),

for suitable constants
\lambda_{m,p},\mu_{m,p}>0
.
This completes the induction.
∎

Each partition
P\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)
with
k<m
contains at least one block of size
\geq 2
, so we obtain an upper bound of the form

\bigl|\mathrm{D}^{m}F_{t}f(x)[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]\bigr|\leq C_{m}e^{\bar{\mu}_{m}t}\|f\|_{m},

for suitable constants
C_{m},\bar{\mu}_{m}>0
depending only on
K,d,m
.
Taking the supremum over all unit vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
and
x\in{\cal X}

yields (
28
).
∎

4.4
Renorming and weighted estimates

The previous lemma gives growth bounds, but not a quasi-contraction in the natural norm. We use the equivalent renorming below.

Proposition

\@upn
22
.

Let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup on a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
.
Assume there exist constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|P_{t}\|_{X\to X}\;\leq\;Me^{\omega t},\qquad t\geq 0.

(29)

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an equivalent norm

\|\cdot\|_{*}
on
X
such that

\|P_{t}x\|_{*}\;\leq\;e^{(\omega+\varepsilon)t}\,\|x\|_{*},\qquad x\in X,\;t\geq 0.

(30)

In particular, with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{*}
, the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is quasi-contractive.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and define

\|x\|_{*}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)t}\,\|P_{t}x\|.

(31)

\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a norm. Moreover,

\|x\|_{*}\;\geq\;\|P_{0}x\|=\|x\|,

and by (
29
),

\|x\|_{*}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)t}Me^{\omega t}\|x\|=M\|x\|.

Thus
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|
.

For
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|P_{s}x\|_{*}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)t}\,\|P_{t}(P_{s}x)\|=\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)t}\,\|P_{t+s}x\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\geq s}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)(r-s)}\,e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)s}\|P_{r}x\|\leq e^{(\omega+\varepsilon)s}\,\sup_{r\geq 0}e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)r}\|P_{r}x\|

\displaystyle=e^{(\omega+\varepsilon)s}\,\|x\|_{*},

which gives (
30
).
∎

We need the following polynomial Lyapunov bound.
For an integer
q\geq 1
define

V_{q}(x):=1+|x|^{2q},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k},

and the weighted norm

\|f\|_{V_{q}}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\frac{|f(x)|}{V_{q}(x)}.

Lemma

\@upn
23
.

Let
X_{t}^{x}
solve (
4
) under Assumption
1
, and let
V_{q}(x)=1+|x|^{2q}
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there are constants
C_{q},D_{q}>0
, depending only on
q,K,d
, such that

LV_{q}(x)\;\leq\;C_{q}\,V_{q}(x),\qquad x\in{\cal X},

(32)

and

\bigl|L^{2}V_{q}(x)\bigr|\;\leq\;D_{q}\,V_{q}(x),\qquad x\in{\cal X}.

(33)

Consequently, for every measurable
f
with
\|f\|_{V_{q}}<\infty
,

\|F_{t}f\|_{V_{q}}\;\leq\;e^{C_{q}t}\,\|f\|_{V_{q}},\qquad t\geq 0.

One can take

C_{q}:=(4qK+6q^{2}K^{2})\,d,\qquad D_{q}:=C_{*}\,q^{4}K^{4}\,d^{2}

for a universal constant
C_{*}>0
independent of
q,K,d
, and
k
.

Proof.

We first estimate
LV_{q}
. For

V_{q}(x)=1+|x|^{2q},

one has

\nabla V_{q}(x)=2q\,|x|^{2q-2}x,

and

\mathrm{D}^{2}V_{q}(x)[u,v]=2q\,|x|^{2q-2}\langle u,v\rangle+4q(q-1)\,|x|^{2q-4}\langle x,u\rangle\langle x,v\rangle.

|\nabla V_{q}(x)|\leq C_{q}(1+|x|^{2q-1}),\qquad\|\mathrm{D}^{2}V_{q}(x)\|\leq C_{q}(1+|x|^{2q-2}).

(34)

Using the linear growth of
b,\sigma
gives

LV_{q}(x)=\nabla V_{q}(x)[b(x)]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{D}^{2}V_{q}(x)[\sigma_{j}(x),\sigma_{j}(x)]\leq C_{q}V_{q}(x),

which is (
32
). By Dynkin’s formula and Grönwall’s lemma,

{\mathbb{E}}[V_{q}(X_{t}^{x})]\leq e^{C_{q}t}V_{q}(x),

and therefore

\|F_{t}f\|_{V_{q}}\leq e^{C_{q}t}\|f\|_{V_{q}}.

To estimate

For every multiindex
\alpha
with

Because

of order at most four, bounded derivatives of
b,\sigma_{j}
, and at most one undifferentiated factor of
b
or two undifferentiated factors of
\sigma_{j}

C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

|g(x)|\leq C_{0}(1+|x|^{2q}),\qquad|\nabla g(x)|\leq C_{1}(1+|x|^{2q-1}),\qquad\|\mathrm{D}^{2}g(x)\|\leq C_{2}(1+|x|^{2q-2}).

(36)

Applying
L
once more gives

L^{2}V_{q}(x)=\nabla g(x)[b(x)]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{D}^{2}g(x)[\sigma_{j}(x),\sigma_{j}(x)].

Using (
36
) and the linear growth of
b,\sigma

\displaystyle|L^{2}V_{q}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|b(x)|\,|\nabla g(x)|+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{D}^{2}g(x)\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\sigma_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K(1+|x|)\,C_{1}(1+|x|^{2q-1})+\frac{1}{2}dK^{2}(1+|x|)^{2}\,C_{2}(1+|x|^{2q-2})

\displaystyle\leq D_{q}(1+|x|^{2q})=D_{q}V_{q}(x),

which proves (

C_{*}
if needed, one may take

D_{q}\leq C_{*}q^{4}K^{4}d^{2}.

∎

Function Spaces and Semigroups for Unbounded Functions

We record the weighted semigroup facts needed in Section
6
. The point is to work on Banach spaces that are invariant under
F_{t}
and on which
F_{t}f\to f
strongly as
t\downarrow 0
. Under these conditions the generator is well defined on a dense domain; see
(
Kolokoltsov 2010
, Theorem 2.4)
.

For a weight
V\geq 1
with
V(x)\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
, put

C_{V,\infty}({\cal X}):=\Bigl\{f\in C({\cal X}):\|f\|_{V}:=\sup_{x\in{\cal X}}\frac{|f(x)|}{V(x)}<\infty,\quad f/V\in C_{\infty}({\cal X})\Bigr\}.

We use the following weighted Feller consequence of
(
Kolokoltsov 2010
, Theorem 5.2)
.

Lemma

\@upn
25
.

Let
(X_{t}^{x})_{t\geq 0}
satisfy Assumption
1
, and suppose that a twice continuously differentiable weight
V\geq 1
satisfies

V(x)\xrightarrow[|x|\to\infty]{}\infty,\qquad LV\leq cV

for some
c\geq 0
. Then
(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a bounded strongly continuous semigroup on
C_{V,\infty}({\cal X})
and

\|F_{t}f\|_{V}\leq e^{ct}\|f\|_{V},\qquad\|F_{t}f-f\|_{V}\xrightarrow[t\downarrow 0]{}0.

Proof.

The Lyapunov bound and Dynkin’s formula give
{\mathbb{E}}V(X_{t}^{x})\leq e^{ct}V(x)
. The cited weighted Feller theorem then gives invariance of
C_{V,\infty}({\cal X})
and strong continuity in the weighted norm.
∎

The next lemma records the escape-to-infinity property of the diffusion that
underlies the vanishing-at-infinity bookkeeping in
Section
6
. It is the only place where we use that the process,
started far away, does not return to a fixed compact set within a bounded time.

Lemma

\@upn
26
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then, for every compact

K\subset{\cal X}
and every
T>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}P_{x}(X_{t}\in K)\xrightarrow[\;|x|\to\infty\;]{}0.

Proof.

Fix
p>0
and consider the bounded weight

w_{-p}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-p/2}
, which vanishes at infinity. Writing

\nabla w_{-p}(x)=-p(1+|x|^{2})^{-p/2-1}x
and

\mathrm{D}^{2}w_{-p}(x)=-p(1+|x|^{2})^{-p/2-1}I+p(p+2)(1+|x|^{2})^{-p/2-2}\,x\otimes x
,
the linear growth
|b(x)|,|\sigma_{\ell}(x)|\leq K(1+|x|)
together with

|x|(1+|x|)\leq C(1+|x|^{2})
gives, after collecting terms, a constant

c_{p}=c_{p}(K,d,p)
with

Lw_{-p}(x)\leq c_{p}\,w_{-p}(x),\qquad x\in{\cal X}.

Since
w_{-p}
is bounded with bounded derivatives, Dynkin’s formula and
Grönwall’s lemma yield
{\mathbb{E}}w_{-p}(X_{t}(x))\leq e^{c_{p}t}w_{-p}(x)
. On the
compact set
K
the weight is bounded below, so

\mathbf{1}_{K}\leq(\inf_{K}w_{-p})^{-1}w_{-p}
, and therefore

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}P_{x}(X_{t}\in K)\leq\frac{e^{c_{p}T}}{\inf_{K}w_{-p}}\,w_{-p}(x)\xrightarrow[\;|x|\to\infty\;]{}0.\qed

Proposition

\@upn

(39)

\displaystyle+e^{\bar{m}t}h(l+\epsilon_{h})\|f\|_{D}.

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t}\left\|e^{-c\lfloor s/h\rfloor h}(U_{h})^{\lfloor s/h\rfloor}f-e^{-cs}F_{s}f\right\|_{B}

Remark

6
.

Contrary to

Proof.

The estimate (

(41)

This proves (

The semigroup

The next lemma computes the error of a random walk scheme.

Lemma

\@upn
35
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Consider a random walk with a one-step transition operator
U_{h}
satisfying Assumption
3
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(K,d,M_{4})>0
such that for all
f\in D_{4}
and all
0<h\leq 1
,

\left\|\left(\frac{U_{h}-I}{h}-L\right)f\right\|_{W}\;\leq\;C\,h\,\|f\|_{4},

(45)

The constant depends only on
K,d
and
M_{4}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in{\cal X}
and abbreviate

B:=b(x),\qquad S_{l}:=\sigma_{l}(x),\qquad\eta:=\eta_{h}(x).

We write

\eta=hB+\sqrt{h}\,Y,\qquad Y:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}(x)\xi_{j}.

We have
{\mathbb{E}}[Y]=0
, and by the third–moment assumption

{\mathbb{E}}[Y^{\wedge 3}]=0.

The tensor Taylor expansion with remainder gives

\displaystyle f(x+\eta)

\displaystyle=f(x)+\nabla f(x)[\eta]+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[\eta,\eta]+\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)[\eta,\eta,\eta]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{24}\mathrm{D}^{4}f(x+\theta\eta)[\eta,\eta,\eta,\eta],

for some (random)
\theta\in(0,1)
.
Let us take expectations of these values from the Taylor extension.
Since
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi]=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{l}\xi_{m}]=\delta_{lm}
,

{\mathbb{E}}[\eta]=hB,\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[\eta\wedge\eta]=h\sum_{l=1}^{d}S_{l}\wedge S_{l}+h^{2}B\wedge B.

Hence

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[f(x+\eta)]

\displaystyle=f(x)+h\nabla f(x)[B]+\frac{h}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[S_{l},S_{l}]

\displaystyle+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[B,B]+R_{3}(x)+R_{4}(x),

where

R_{3}(x):=\frac{1}{6}{\mathbb{E}}[\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)[\eta,\eta,\eta]],\qquad R_{4}(x):=\frac{1}{24}{\mathbb{E}}[\mathrm{D}^{4}f(x+\theta\eta)[\eta,\eta,\eta,\eta]].

The generator is

Lf(x)=\nabla f(x)[B]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[S_{l},S_{l}].

Thus

(U_{h}f)(x)-f(x)-hLf(x)=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[B,B]+R_{3}(x)+R_{4}(x).

Using
\|B\|\leq K(1+\|x\|)
,

\left|\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[B,B]\right|\leq h^{2}\|f\|_{4}\,K^{2}(1+\|x\|)^{2}.

Dividing by
hW(x)
and using

(1+\|x\|)^{2}\leq 2(1+\|x\|^{4})=2W(x)
,

\frac{1}{W(x)}\left|\frac{1}{h}\cdot\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[B,B]\right|\leq 2K^{2}h\,\|f\|_{4}.

Since
\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)
is a symmetric tensor,

R_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)\big[{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})\big].

We compute
{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})
explicitly. With
\eta=hB+\sqrt{h}Y
,

\eta^{\wedge 3}=(hB+\sqrt{h}Y)^{\wedge 3}=h^{3}B^{\wedge 3}+3h^{2}\sqrt{h}\,B^{\wedge 2}\wedge Y+3h(\sqrt{h})^{2}B\wedge Y^{\wedge 2}+(\sqrt{h})^{3}Y^{\wedge 3}.

Taking expectations and using
{\mathbb{E}}[Y]=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}[Y^{\wedge 3}]=0
,

{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})=h^{3}B^{\wedge 3}+3h^{2}\,B\wedge{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{\wedge 2}).

Since
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{i}\xi_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
,

{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{\wedge 2})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sigma_{l}(x)\vee\sigma_{l}(x)
is
deterministic, with norm bounded by

\|{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{\wedge 2})\|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|\sigma_{l}(x)\|^{2}\leq dK^{2}(1+\|x\|)^{2},

and
\|B\|\leq K(1+\|x\|)
, so

\|{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})\|\leq(1+3d)K^{3}h^{2}(1+\|x\|)^{3}.

Since
(1+\|x\|)^{3}\leq 8(1+\|x\|^{4})=8W(x)
, we get

\|{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})\|\leq 8(1+3d)K^{3}h^{2}W(x).

Thus,

\frac{|R_{3}(x)|}{hW(x)}\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)\|\,\|{\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{\wedge 3})\|\leq\frac{4}{3}(1+3d)K^{3}h\,\|f\|_{4}.

The fourth-order term

|R_{4}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{24}\|f\|_{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\|\eta\|^{4}.

Using the vector inequality
\|u+v\|^{4}\leq 8(\|u\|^{4}+\|v\|^{4})
,

{\mathbb{E}}\|\eta\|^{4}\leq 8h^{4}\|B\|^{4}+8h^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|^{4}.

For the drift part, we obtain

8h^{4}\|B\|^{4}\leq 8h^{4}K^{4}(1+\|x\|)^{4}\leq 64K^{4}W(x)h^{4}.

Now we proceed with the diffusion part. Thanks to the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}\right)^{2}\leq d\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}^{2}.

Therefore,

{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|^{4}\leq d^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\sigma_{j}(x)|^{4}|\xi_{j}|^{4}\leq 2d^{4}M_{4}K^{4}(1+|x|^{4}).

Combining the two contributions gives (recall that
h\leq 1
)

{\mathbb{E}}\|\eta\|^{4}\leq CK^{4}\,(1+d^{4}M_{4})\,W(x)\,h^{2},

Collecting the three contributions, we obtain for all
x\in{\cal X}
and
0<h\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{W(x)}\left|\left(\frac{U_{h}-I}{h}-L\right)f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2K^{2}+\frac{4}{3}(1+3d)K^{3}+CK^{4}(1+d^{4}M_{4})\Big)h\,\|f\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C(K,d,M_{4})\,h\,\|f\|_{4},

which finishes the proof.
∎

\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
C_{c}^{\infty}({\cal X})
is dense
in
{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
with respect to

\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,r}
. In particular,

{\cal C}^{m}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
is dense in

{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
whenever
m\geq r
.

Proof.

Let
f\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
and choose

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\cal X})
with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi=1
on

B_{1}
, and
\chi=0
outside
B_{2}
. Put

\chi_{R}(x)=\chi(x/R)
and
f_{R}=\chi_{R}f
. For
0\leq j\leq r
,
Leibniz’ rule gives

\mathrm{D}^{j}(f-f_{R})=(1-\chi_{R})\mathrm{D}^{j}f+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}C_{j,\ell}\,\mathrm{D}^{\ell}(1-\chi_{R})\,\mathrm{D}^{j-\ell}f.

The first term tends to zero in the weighted norm because

\mathrm{D}^{j}f/w_{\alpha-j}
vanishes at infinity. The remaining terms
are supported in
\{R\leq|x|\leq 2R\}
and satisfy

\|\mathrm{D}^{\ell}\chi_{R}(x)\|\leq C_{\ell}R^{-\ell}\leq C_{\ell}w_{-\ell}(x)

there. Since

w_{-\ell}w_{\alpha-(j-\ell)}=w_{\alpha-j}
, these terms also tend
to zero uniformly after division by
w_{\alpha-j}
. Hence

\|f_{R}-f\|_{\alpha,r}\to 0
.

For fixed

f_{R}
by a standard mollifier

\rho_{\varepsilon}
. The functions

f_{R,\varepsilon}:=\rho_{\varepsilon}*f_{R}
belong to

C_{c}^{\infty}({\cal X})
and converge to
f_{R}
in the ordinary
C^{r}
norm
on a fixed compact set. On that compact set the weighted norm is
equivalent to the usual
C^{r}
norm, so

\|f_{R,\varepsilon}-f_{R}\|_{\alpha,r}\to 0
. This proves density.

Lemma

\@upn
39
.

Fix
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

, and assume
Assumption

. Then there exists

C_{\alpha,r}>0
such that, for every

,

\|Lf\|_{\alpha,r+2}+\|L^{2}f\|_{\alpha,r}\leq C_{\alpha,r}\|f\|_{\alpha,r+4}.

In particular,

is a bounded operator.

Proof.

We write

Lf=\mathrm{D}f[b]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\mathrm{D}^{2}f[\sigma_{\ell},\sigma_{\ell}].

Assumption

gives

\|\mathrm{D}^{j}b(x)\|\leq Cw_{1-j}(x)

\|\mathrm{D}^{j}a(x)\|\leq Cw_{2-j}(x),\qquad 0\leq j\leq r+4.

For

\mathrm{D}^{q}(\mathrm{D}f[b])

w_{\alpha-(q-\ell+2)}w_{2-\ell}=w_{\alpha-q}

\|Lf\|_{\alpha,r+2}\leq C\|f\|_{\alpha,r+4}
. The same
derivative expansion proves the vanishing-at-infinity condition:
after division by

each term contains a weighted
derivative ratio of
f
, evaluated at

Lf\in{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}
.
Applying the already proved bound with

in place of

Lemma

\@upn
40
.

Fix
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and assume
Assumption
2
. The diffusion semigroup

(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
leaves
{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
invariant and is
strongly continuous on this space. If
A_{\alpha,r}
denotes its
generator on
{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
, then

{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})\subset\operatorname{Dom}(A_{\alpha,r}),\qquad A_{\alpha,r}f=Lf.

The space
{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
is dense in

{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
and is an invariant core for

A_{\alpha,r}
.
Moreover, for each
T>0
there is
M_{\alpha,r,T}
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|F_{t}g\|_{\alpha,r}\leq M_{\alpha,r,T}\|g\|_{\alpha,r}.

Proof.

We first record the weighted flow estimates used below. The
Lyapunov calculation for the weights
w_{\gamma}
gives, for every

\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\geq 1
, and
T>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}w_{\gamma}(X_{t}(x))^{p}\leq C_{\gamma,p,T}w_{\gamma}(x)^{p}.

Kunita’s equations for the flow jets, combined with

\|\mathrm{D}^{q}\sigma_{\ell}(y)\|\leq Cw_{1-q}(y)
, yield by induction

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathrm{D}X_{t}(x)\|^{p}\leq C_{p,T},\qquad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathrm{D}^{j}X_{t}(x)\|^{p}\leq C_{j,p,T}w_{1-j}(x)^{p},\quad j\geq 2.

Indeed, in the
j
th jet equation each forcing term associated with
a partition
P=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{q}\}
has weight

w_{1-q}(X_{s})\prod_{i=1}^{q}w_{1-|P_{i}|}(X_{s}),

and after Hölder’s inequality, the Lyapunov estimate, and the
induction hypothesis this becomes
w_{1-j}(x)
because

\sum_{i}|P_{i}|=j
. Grönwall’s lemma then gives the displayed bound.

Let
g\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
. The derivative
representation for
F_{t}g
follows from Kunita’s flow theorem and
Proposition
17
. For
1\leq j\leq r
,

\mathrm{D}^{j}F_{t}g(x)={\mathbb{E}}\sum_{q=1}^{j}\sum_{P\in{\cal P}(j,q)}\mathrm{D}^{q}g(X_{t}(x))\big[\mathrm{D}^{|P_{1}|}X_{t}(x),\dots,\mathrm{D}^{|P_{q}|}X_{t}(x)\big],

while
F_{t}g(x)={\mathbb{E}}g(X_{t}(x))
for
j=0
. Hölder’s inequality and
the estimates above give

\|\mathrm{D}^{j}F_{t}g(x)\|\leq C_{\alpha,r,T}\|g\|_{\alpha,r}w_{\alpha-j}(x),\qquad 0\leq j\leq r,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

This proves the boundedness estimate on
{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha}
.

We next prove the vanishing condition and strong continuity.
First let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\cal X})
. The same derivative
representation and the continuity of the stochastic flow jets at

t=0
give local uniform convergence

\mathrm{D}^{j}F_{t}g\to\mathrm{D}^{j}g
for
0\leq j\leq r
. To control the tail, choose

\beta<\alpha
. Since
g
is compactly supported,

g\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\beta,\infty}({\cal X})
, and the estimate already proved
gives, for
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\frac{\|\mathrm{D}^{j}F_{t}g(x)\|}{w_{\alpha-j}(x)}\leq C\|g\|_{\beta,r}w_{\beta-\alpha}(x),\qquad|x|\ \text{large}.

The right-hand side tends to zero as
|x|\to\infty
, uniformly in

t\in[0,1]
. Thus
F_{t}g\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}
and

\|F_{t}g-g\|_{\alpha,r}\to 0
for compactly supported smooth
g
.
Lemma
38
and the boundedness estimate extend
both statements to arbitrary

g\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
.

If
f\in{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
, then
Lemma
39
gives

Lf\in{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X})
. Applying Itô’s formula to the
stopped diffusion and then removing the stopping by the weighted
Lyapunov estimates gives

F_{t}f-f=\int_{0}^{t}F_{s}Lf\,ds\quad\text{in }{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}({\cal X}),

and therefore

\frac{F_{t}f-f}{t}-Lf=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(F_{s}Lf-Lf)\,ds\to 0

in
{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}
by strong continuity. This identifies
the generator. Density follows from Lemma
38
.
Invariance of
{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}
follows from the same
argument applied at derivative order
r+2
. Hence

{\cal C}^{r+2}_{\alpha,\infty}
is a dense invariant subspace contained
in
\operatorname{Dom}(A_{\alpha,r})
, and the semigroup
core criterion makes it an invariant core.
∎

Lemma

\@upn
42
.

Fix
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}

Put

\eta_{h}(x):=hb(x)+\sqrt{h}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\sigma_{\ell}(x)\xi_{\ell}.

Lemma
37
gives the weight comparability,
the derivative bounds for
\eta_{h}
, and the preservation of

{\cal C}^{r}_{\alpha,\infty}
by

. The displayed stability estimate
is precisely Assumption

. It remains

For consistency, use Taylor’s formula with integral fourth-order

\displaystyle f(x+\eta_{h})

\displaystyle=f(x)+\mathrm{D}f(x)[\eta_{h}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[\eta_{h},\eta_{h}]+\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)[\eta_{h},\eta_{h},\eta_{h}]

The coefficient

.
Let

The moment assumptions give

and, because all third moments of

where

denotes the finite symmetrised sum of

in one of the slots. Hence

\displaystyle(U_{h}-I-hL)f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)[b,b]+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)[\mathcal{S}(b\otimes a)]

\displaystyle+\frac{h^{3}}{6}\mathrm{D}^{3}f(x)[b,b,b]+R_{4,h}f(x),

where

\mathrm{D}^{j}\{\mathrm{D}^{2}f[b,b]\}
is a finite sum of contractions of

\mathrm{D}^{j_{0}+2}f

\mathrm{D}^{j_{1}}b
and
\mathrm{D}^{j_{2}}b
,

. After division by

The same calculation applies to

\|\mathrm{D}^{\ell}a(x)\|\leq Cw_{2-\ell}(x)
, and to

. Thus these three deterministic

for
0\leq j\leq r
.

For the integral remainder, apply Proposition
17

and Leibniz’ rule to

\mathrm{D}^{4}f(x+\theta\eta_{h})[\eta_{h},\eta_{h},\eta_{h},\eta_{h}].

Every
j
th derivative is a finite sum of contractions containing one

\mathrm{D}^{4+q}f(x+\theta\eta_{h}(x))
, with
q\leq j
, several
derivatives of
\varphi_{h}^{\theta}(x)=x+\theta\eta_{h}(x)
, and four
factors obtained by differentiating the four copies of
\eta_{h}
.
Lemma
37
gives the weight comparison

w_{\alpha-(4+q)}(x+\theta\eta_{h}(x))\leq Cw_{\alpha-(4+q)}(x)
and the bounds

\|\mathrm{D}^{\ell}\eta_{h}(x)\|\leq C\sqrt{h}\,w_{1-\ell}(x)
.
Since four copies of
\eta_{h}
are present before differentiation,
every term contains the factor
h^{2}
and its coefficient weights
multiply exactly to
w_{\alpha-j}(x)
. Boundedness of
\xi
permits
taking expectation inside the same estimate. Therefore

\|\mathrm{D}^{j}R_{4,h}f(x)\|\leq C_{\alpha,r}h^{2}\|f\|_{\alpha,r+4}w_{\alpha-j}(x).

The same product estimates also show that each weighted derivative

derivative ratio of

evaluated at
x
or at

and the latter tends to infinity uniformly in
the bounded increment variable. Combining the deterministic and

Dividing by
h
and taking the maximum over
j
proves the local
error estimate.
