COVID-19 cluster outbreaks and infection control measures in nursing homes in Okinawa: protocol for a retrospective observational study
Authors: Shuhei Ideguchi, Yoshihiro Takayama, Nanae Ikemiyagi, Fuki Ujimiya, Kazutaka Yamaniha, Miki Imura, Kosuke Tanabe, Kazuko Yamamoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionFollowing the reclassification of COVID-19 as a category V infectious disease in Japan in May 2023, systematic public health monitoring of case numbers and cluster outbreaks was discontinued. Consequently, infection control measures, responses to cluster outbreaks and vaccination coverage in long-term care facilities were largely left to the discretion of individual facilities, and the understanding of the current situation in these settings remains insufficient. This observational study aims to retrospectively investigate COVID-19 cluster outbreaks and the resulting burdens experienced by facilities after reclassification, focusing on special nursing homes (Tokubetsu Yogo Rojin Home) for older adults, a population at particularly high risk for COVID-19-associated hospitalisation and mortality. By comprehensively evaluating facility and resident characteristics, vaccination coverage and infection prevention and control measures, this study seeks to generate evidence to inform future infection control strategies in long-term care facilities.Methods and analysisThis retrospective observational study targets special nursing homes in Okinawa Prefecture and residents living in these facilities. Data for a target sample of nine nursing homes with approximately 900 residents will be collected from existing facility and medical records using the Research Electronic Data Capture system. The study was initiated in June 2025 and completion is anticipated by March 2027. The primary outcomes are the occurrence of COVID-19 cluster outbreaks in nursing homes between May 2023 and August 2025 and the facility-level burden experienced during outbreaks, including operational and economic impacts. A cluster outbreak is defined as the occurrence of ≥2 cases of the same infectious disease within a 10-day period. Key variables of interest include baseline characteristics of facilities and residents, vaccination coverage among staff and residents and infection prevention and control measures. Additionally, the study explores potential factors associated with residents’ clinical outcomes and facility burden using logistic and linear regression models, respectively, with facility-level random intercepts if deemed appropriate. Prespecified supplementary analyses include one-way analysis of variance and χ2 tests.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University of the Ryukyus in June 2025 (approval number: 25-2486-00-00-00). The findings will be disseminated at academic conferences and in peer-reviewed medical journals.Keywords: COVID-19, Infection control, Vaccination, Nursing Homes, Disease Outbreaks

Introduction

While the COVID-19 pandemic has subsided, SARS-CoV-2 continues to cause periodic epidemics regionally.1 2 It has been reported that COVID-19-related hospitalisation and fatality rates were higher than those of seasonal influenza.3–5 These rates were particularly pronounced among older adults,3 5–7 who tend to have weaker immune systems and more underlying health conditions. Recent reports from the USA have indicated that among adults aged ≥65 years, the hospitalisation rate for COVID-19 was approximately five times higher than that for influenza6 and the fatality rate among hospitalised patients was about 1.8 times higher.3

Okinawa Prefecture has experienced large-scale COVID-19 outbreaks compared with other prefectures in Japan. Since the emergence of the Omicron variant, older adults have remained at an increased risk of hospitalisation, severe illness and death,8–10 and cluster outbreaks in long-term care facilities have become a major public concern. However, following the reclassification of COVID-19 as a category V infectious disease on 8 May 2023 under the Act on the Prevention of Infectious Diseases and Medical Care for Patients with Infectious Diseases (the Infectious Disease Control Act), monitoring data on the number of infected cases and cluster occurrences were no longer systematically collected by public health authorities. Consequently, infection control measures, responses to cluster outbreaks and vaccination coverage in long-term care facilities were left to the discretion of each facility, resulting in limited visibility into the current situation across facilities. Various initiatives for early detection and response support have been implemented, including the Facility Epidemic Surveillance System for the Elderly in the jurisdictions of two public health centres and nationwide infection surveys among member facilities of the Japan Association of Geriatric Health Services Facilities. However, these initiatives remain fragmented and limited in scope, and challenges persist in implementing nationwide and systematic monitoring. Furthermore, findings from these initiatives have not been sufficiently used to inform evidence-based preventive measures or policy decisions.

Additionally, Okinawa has been reported to have one of the lowest vaccination rates in Japan, with only approximately 15%–45% of the older populations (aged ≥60 years) receiving a vaccine in Autumn/Winter 2023 compared with approximately 29%–77% nationwide.11 12 From April 2024 onwards, the national routine immunisation programme for COVID-19 has been implemented under the classification of Category B diseases in the Immunisation Act, with a primary focus on preventing severe illness among individuals. COVID-19 vaccines are currently subsidised for individuals who are either aged ≥65 years or those aged 60–64 years with underlying health conditions.13 Under this framework, vaccination decisions are largely voluntary for individuals and facility operators, and the estimated national coverage was only 17.9% in the inaugural 2024–2025 season.14 However, vaccination uptake among residents and staff in long-term care facilities after the reclassification of COVID-19 remains unclear. As long-term care facilities are among the high-risk settings for COVID-19 outbreaks,15 16 vaccination uptake, along with the implementation of effective infection prevention and control measures, has important implications for protecting residents, minimising outbreak-related disruptions and sustaining essential healthcare and caregiving services. In regions such as Okinawa, where both high disease burden and low vaccination uptake have been reported, clarifying the current situation in long-term care facilities through comprehensive epidemiological investigation is particularly important.

This study aims to retrospectively investigate COVID-19 cluster outbreaks and the resulting burdens on facilities during the post-reclassification period, as well as the facility and resident characteristics, vaccination status among residents and staff, and infection prevention and control measures. Additionally, the study explores potential factors associated with the size of COVID-19 cluster outbreaks and facility burden. The findings of this study will clarify the current challenges faced by long-term care facilities and provide evidence to support the development of effective infection control strategies for future COVID-19 epidemics nationwide.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

This retrospective observational cohort study targets special nursing homes (Tokubetsu Yogo Rojin Home) in Naha City and Urasoe City, Okinawa, Japan. These nursing homes provide long-term care under the Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI) system in Japan. The study was initiated on 11 June 2025, the date of approval by the head of the University of the Ryukyus following the ethical approval granted by the university’s institutional review board (IRB). The study is anticipated to be completed by 31 March 2027. The study design and operational structure are shown in figure 1.

Operational structure of the study. IRB, institutional review board; REDCap, Research Electronic Data Capture.

Participating facilities and study population

The study includes special nursing homes located in two target cities, each with a capacity of ≥50 residents, that agreed to participate in this study. The study population comprises residents of consenting nursing homes who resided in the facilities at any time from May 2023 onwards. Residents or legal representatives who opt out of data inclusion will be excluded from the study.

Data collection

Following the reclassification of COVID-19 to a category V infectious disease, public health centres generally only required the reporting of large clusters (typically ≥10 cases), and smaller clusters may not be officially recorded at the facility-level. To overcome the documentation gap and to capture all cluster outbreaks, two layers of data will be collected and cross-referenced. For example, COVID-19 cluster occurrence at each facility will be identified from the facility data, and it will be also ascertained by the dates of infection among residents. The facility-level data, including data on COVID-19 and influenza clusters from May 2023 to August 2025, will be retrospectively collected from administrative logs, financial records and staff shift logs (figure 2). The resident-level clinical data will be extracted from individual medical records maintained at each facility.

The data collection workflow will proceed as follows: first, the study protocol will be explained to the target facilities to obtain institutional consent, and opt-out procedures will be implemented for the residents (details are provided in the Ethical approval section). The data will be collected by research collaborators, graduate students and medical students affiliated with the University of the Ryukyus. All data collectors will receive standardised training prior to data collection. Trained investigators will visit participating facilities. During these visits, data will be manually entered into a secure, multisite-access web application system, the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap), with a server managed by the University of the Ryukyus under a licence agreement with Vanderbilt University. The collected data will be anonymised to remove identifiable information. Details of data handling are provided in the Data management section. Data collection is currently ongoing, and the data analysis is anticipated to commence in May 2026 for facility-level data and September 2026 for resident-level data.

The target sample size is approximately 900 residents. The sample size was determined on the basis that each facility accommodates 100 residents and that there are nine special nursing homes with a capacity of ≥50 residents in Naha City and Urasoe City. Residents of special nursing homes account for approximately 60% of all users of facility-based services under the Japanese LTCI system17 (table 1). No formal statistical power calculation was performed because this study is exploratory in nature.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes are the occurrence of COVID-19 cluster outbreaks in long-term care facilities and the associated facility-level burden experienced during outbreaks. A cluster outbreak is defined as the occurrence of ≥2 cases of the same infectious disease within a 10-day period. To accurately capture cluster outbreaks, the cluster occurrence will be extracted from facility-level data and also be ascertained based on the overlap of COVID-19 infection dates among residents. Cluster characteristics will be assessed in terms of the number of cluster occurrences, the number and proportion of residents involved and residents’ clinical outcomes. The facility-level burden will be evaluated in terms of both operational and economic burden, including revenue loss in JPY resulting from temporary bed vacancies due to resident transfers to hospitals, expenses incurred for in-facility care and costs associated with infection prevention and control measures. Operational burden will include the total number of workdays lost owing to staff absences related to cluster outbreaks defined above (person-days) and the temporary suspension of care services for infected residents. Staff absence will be evaluated alongside the cluster occurrence. All outcomes will be assessed for each COVID-19 cluster.

Key variables of interest that complement the primary outcomes include baseline characteristics of facilities and residents, vaccination coverage among staff and residents during this study and infection prevention and control measures implemented at each facility.

Statistical analysis

In addition to descriptive statistics, exploratory analyses will be conducted. As this study aims to comprehensively investigate the previously uncharacterised real-world situations in long-term care facilities, no predefined hypotheses have been established. One of the analyses related to COVID-19 cluster outbreaks will evaluate residents’ clinical outcomes (eg, presence or absence of severe illness requiring oxygen therapy) in relation to key facility characteristics including vaccination rates among staff and residents, facility type, visitation restrictions, interactions with other facilities on the same compound (eg, daycare services) and the presence or absence of infection prevention and control measures, using a logistic regression model. The explanatory variables will be carefully selected based on clinical relevance rather than solely relying on stepwise selection or p values. To evaluate potential factors associated with facility-level burden, linear regression models will be fitted using costs or total staff absences associated with cluster outbreaks (person-days) as response variables and explanatory variables including the number of cluster occurrences and infected residents. For the statistical models, to account for potential clustering within facilities, the inclusion of facility-level random intercepts will be considered and incorporated if deemed appropriate for model stability. Between-facility differences in outcomes will be assessed using one-way analysis of variance or χ2 tests, as appropriate.

Unless otherwise stated, statistical tests will be two-sided with a significance level of 0.05 and 95% CIs. Because this study is exploratory in nature, no adjustments for multiple comparisons will be applied. Missing data will not be imputed. All analyses will be conducted using SAS V.9.4 or later (SAS Institute).

Patient and public involvement

Residents targeted for data collection, their representatives and members of the public will not be involved in this study.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval

The study protocol was approved by the IRB of the University of the Ryukyus on 11 June 2025 and subsequently by the head of the university (approval number: 25-2486-00-00-00). This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as amended in October 2024) and Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects (as amended in March 2023). To ensure transparency and allow eligible residents to opt out, an opt-out notice will be posted on the websites of the University of the Ryukyus Hospital (First Department of Internal Medicine) and the participating special nursing homes. The opt-out notice will include information on the purpose of the study, procedures for the use of resident information, the expected start date of data use, the scope of data use and the procedures by which residents or their legal representatives may request discontinuation of data use. As this study is observational in nature, involves no intervention, and uses existing information obtained from facilities in which the eligible residents reside, no health-related harm attributable to this study is expected to occur. Regarding data for facility staff, no direct health-related risks attributable to study inclusion are anticipated. No personally identifiable information of facility staff will be collected, and individual informed consent is therefore not required in accordance with the Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects.

Data management

The REDCap system will be used for data collection and secure data exchange between participating facilities and the University of the Ryukyus Hospital, as described above. To manage and analyse resident data obtained from participating facilities, the facility-level operational data and the individual resident-level clinical data will be linked within the REDCap system using unique facility identifiers. Then, the data will be anonymised using unique identifiers and stored on an offline computer at the First Department of Internal Medicine, Division of Infectious, Respiratory, and Digestive Medicine, University of the Ryukyus Graduate School of Medicine. The correspondence table used for anonymisation will be stored separately in a locked room within the department with restricted access. No personal identifiers will be shared with external contractors, including data analysis companies. All study data will be retained for at least 5 years after study completion or for 3 years after the final dissemination of study findings, whichever is longer. After the retention period, data will be securely disposed of in accordance with institutional policies.

Dissemination of findings

The study findings will be reported to the IRB and disseminated through presentations at academic conferences and in peer-reviewed medical journals. Only aggregated and anonymised data will be presented, and no identifiable individual-level or facility-level information will be disclosed. Furthermore, the collected data will not be used beyond the scope of the approved study protocol.
