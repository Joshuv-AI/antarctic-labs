DAN-YOLO-L: a lightweight YOLO approach for efficient infrared small object detection from UAV perspectives
Authors: Kai Feng, Guojun Lin, Tong Lin, Zhongqiang Luo, Run Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSmall object detection in drone aerial photography faces challenges such as small scales, inconspicuous features, and complex backgrounds. To address these issues, this paper proposes an improved detection model based on the YOLO framework. First, a multi-channel feature extraction module—Deconvolutional Network combined with the C3 module (deconv-c3k2)—is designed to enhance feature extraction and multi-scale representation capabilities. Second, an enhanced Auxiliary Head detection module is introduced to improve feature interaction and collaboration across different levels. Concurrently, the NWD-Inner-CIoU loss function is adopted to mitigate the impact of IoU on small target localization offset, thereby boosting detection accuracy. To meet real-time embedded deployment requirements, an L1 pruning strategy is employed to reduce the model parameter size. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms baseline models on the HIT-UAV dataset, achieving 81.9% on mAP@0.5 and 51.4% on mAP@0.5:0.95, respectively. Inference speed increases by 8.32%, while parameter count decreases by 20.8%. Computational load was reduced by 0.36 GFLOPs, and model size shrinks to 79.2% of the original. Furthermore, experiments on the IRay Infrared Dataset validate the method’s generalization capability. Overall, the proposed approach demonstrates distinct advantages in detection accuracy, real-time performance, and lightweight design, while exhibiting stability and practical value.Keywords: Small object detection, UAV infrared images, DAN-YOLO-L, NWD-Inner-CIoU lossSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) technology, UAVs are increasingly applied in fields such as remote sensing, traffic monitoring, and disaster relief1–3. Particularly in complex environments, detecting small targets within UAV aerial imagery poses significant challenges.According to standard benchmarks such as VisDrone and MS COCO, small targets typically exhibit minimal pixel dimensions (e.g., less than 16× 16 or 32× 32 pixels)4,5, resulting in blurred features and insufficient information. Consequently, existing detection methods often demonstrate low accuracy and high false positive rates when handling such targets. To cope with this drawback, enhancing the accuracy of small target detection while reducing computational resource consumption has emerged as a key research problem. In recent years, with the widespread application of deep learning technologies, object detection methods based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs)6 have made significant progress. The YOLO series of models7, in particular, has demonstrated balance between accuracy and computational complexity. Despite its superior balance between detection speed and accuracy in general scenarios, the YOLO framework, like most single-stage detectors, still encounters specific performance bottlenecks when handling small objects in complex environments, particularly regarding feature preservation and localization stability. First, traditional feature extraction methods often neglect multi-scale feature fusion, leading to suboptimal model performance across different scales of small objects. Second, positioning errors in small objects cause instability in Intersection over Union (IoU)8 calculations. Consequently, traditional IoU-based loss functions frequently fail to meet the demands of small object detection, resulting in increased training difficulty and reduced convergence speed. To address these challenges in small object detection, this paper proposes an innovative detection framework with significant improvements in four key aspects:

Multi-scale feature fusion enhancement: To overcome feature loss caused by scale differences in traditional detection frameworks, this paper integrates deconvolution9 with the C3k2 architecture to enhance multi-scale feature10 extraction and fusion capabilities. This integration not only improves feature representation of small objects across scales but also effectively enhances the model’s sensitivity to low-resolution small targets. This method is particularly suitable for detecting small objects in drone aerial imagery, significantly improving detection accuracy and robustness.

Related work

Object detection, as a significant research topic in computer vision, has undergone a developmental journey from shallow to deep approaches. Early methods primarily relied on manually designed features and traditional classifiers, such as Haar features combined with Adaboost, HOG with SVM14–16, and sliding window detection. While these methods advanced object detection applications at the time, their limited feature representation capabilities made them ill-suited for complex backgrounds and small objects. Additionally, their computational efficiency was relatively low, failing to meet real-time requirements. With the rise of deep learning, detection frameworks based on convolutional neural networks gradually replaced traditional approaches. Two-stage models like R-CNN17, Fast R-CNN18, and Mask R-CNN19 achieved significant accuracy improvements by performing detection in two steps: candidate region generation and classification/regression. However, these methods generally incurred high computational overhead and slow inference speeds, making them unsuitable for drone aerial photography tasks requiring rapid response. To address this speed bottleneck, researchers proposed one-stage detectors like SSD20 and the YOLO series. YOLO transforms detection into a regression problem, directly predicting categories and bounding boxes on feature maps, greatly enhancing real-time performance. Through iterations from the original YOLO to the latest YOLOv11, the network has continuously optimized its backbone architecture, feature fusion, and detection head design, achieving an optimal balance between speed and accuracy. Consequently, it has gradually become a key benchmark framework for small object detection in UAV applications. Researchers have also introduced targeted improvements for small object detection. For instance, Dual-YOLO21 enhances multi-scale representation through dual-path and feature fusion mechanisms. SOD-YOLO22 introduced cross-domain attention modules to enhance small object discrimination in aerial imagery. IPD-YOLO23 designed novel detection heads and loss functions for infrared scenarios, improving localization accuracy for weakly featured targets. NOC-YOLO24 attempted to balance lightweight architecture with detection precision, demonstrating feasibility in infrared small-object vehicle detection.Recently, Transformer-based approaches have also demonstrated a growing impact on small object detection tasks. For instance, recent studies, such as the contrast-aware and uncertainty-optimized transformer model, have shown that Transformer architectures can significantly improve small object representation and localization in complex high-resolution scenes25. Similarly, recent advancements have explored lightweight transformer models incorporating adaptive rotational convolutions to specifically address aerial object detection, further demonstrating the potential of self-attention mechanisms in extracting robust features from complex UAV perspectives26. However, while Vision Transformers excel in capturing global context and fine-grained representations, their high computational complexity and substantial memory footprint often limit their practical deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms that require real-time inference. Therefore, lightweight CNN architectures, particularly the YOLO series, remain a more feasible and highly efficient framework for UAV-based small object detection. These efforts lay the groundwork for small object detection, yet challenges persist in complex environments–including insufficient accuracy, unstable features, and the difficulty of balancing lightweight design with performance. Infrared imaging, while effective for nighttime and adverse weather conditions, often suffers from low signal-to-noise ratios and a lack of texture information. As highlighted in recent studies on infrared shape and motion modeling27,28, small targets in these images are frequently submerged in complex, cluttered backgrounds, such as clouds, buildings, or sea waves. These factors make it extremely difficult for conventional networks to distinguish genuine targets from high-intensity noise or thermal artifacts

Proposed method

This study builds upon the YOLOv11n framework to address the challenges of insufficient feature representation and high model complexity in small object detection. By refining the network architecture and optimization strategies, we developed a lightweight and efficient detection model, DAN-YOLO-L, whose structural diagram is shown in Fig. 1. During feature extraction, the C3k2 module is combined with a deconvolution (Deconv) structure to enhance feature recovery and detail representation, mitigating feature loss in small objects caused by downsampling. The detection component employs an auxiliary detection head (Aux Head), introducing additional supervisory signals at intermediate layers to promote feature optimization and improve detection stability. For the loss function, NWD-Inner-CIoU is introduced. By integrating normalized Wasserstein distance with the CIoU metric, it simultaneously accounts for differences in object center position, scale, and shape, thereby improving localization accuracy for small targets. Finally, L1 sparse pruning removes redundant channels, effectively reducing model parameters and computational overhead to achieve lightweight deployment.

Loss function optimization: NWD-Inner-CIoU loss function for small object detection

To further enhance detection accuracy and regression stability, this study introduces a combined NWD-Inner-CIoU optimization scheme within the existing detection framework. Traditional IoU-based losses (e.g., GIoU, DIoU, CIoU) are overly sensitive to positional offsets in small objects and yield zero gradients when predicted boxes do not intersect or completely enclose ground-truth boxes.To address these issues, the Normalized Gaussian Wasserstein Distance (NWD) is adopted as a scale-normalized similarity metric, providing smooth and consistent gradients under varying object sizes and displacements.In parallel, the Inner-CIoU loss refines the geometric alignment between anchor boxes and target boxes by introducing an inner-box constraint that focuses optimization on the most representative overlap regions.This dual-loss design allows NWD to improve metric stability while Inner-CIoU enhances localization precision, particularly for small or densely packed targets. Figure 7 illustrates the geometric mechanism of the proposed Inner-CIoU loss, showing how inner and outer bounding regions are defined for both targets and anchors. The purple boxes correspond to the target-related regions (ground-truth and its corresponding anchor box), while the orange boxes denote their inner counterparts used to constrain relative positional alignment. During training, the proposed Inner-CIoU mechanism refines the traditional CIoU formulation by introducing an inner bounding region within both the target and anchor boxes.This inner region focuses the regression process on the core overlap area, allowing effective gradient propagation even when boxes are partially misaligned or non-overlapping. Guided by this geometry-aware constraint, the adaptive anchor–target assignment achieves more stable and precise localization.When combined with the NWD term, the overall framework maintains smooth differentiability and enhanced robustness across various object scales.

Experimental dataset description

To validate the detection performance of the proposed model under diverse scenarios, this paper selected two representative public datasets: HIT-UAV32 and the IRAY Infrared Dataset.Both datasets focus on infrared small-object detection but offer different perspectives: the former emphasizes high-altitude UAV aerial thermal imagery, while the latter focuses on multi-scale target detection in diverse ground-based infrared surveillance scenarios. Together, they provide a solid basis for evaluating the model’s generalization performance under complex thermal conditions, jointly providing a solid basis for evaluating the model’s generalization performance under complex conditions. (1) The HIT-UAV dataset, proposed by Harbin Institute of Technology, aims to advance research in infrared small object detection. It is constructed from infrared video sequences captured by a low-altitude drone platform, covering diverse complex scenarios including campuses, roads, grasslands, built-up areas, and nighttime environments. A total of 2898 thermal infrared image frames with a resolution of 384 × 288 were extracted from the raw videos, with precise bounding box annotations provided for each target. Target types primarily include pedestrians, vehicles, and low-altitude aircraft, with sizes typically occupying less than 1% of the image area, demonstrating the characteristic features of small objects. As illustrated in Fig. 8a, the HIT-UAV dataset’s high background noise and low signal-to-noise ratio characteristics impose stringent demands on the multi-scale modeling capabilities of detection algorithms. Consequently, it is widely adopted for evaluating infrared small target detection performance. (2) The IRay Infrared Dataset, released by China’s IRay Optoelectronics, serves as a universal dataset for validating infrared intelligent perception algorithms. It encompasses surveillance images across multiple scenarios under both daytime and nighttime conditions, covering typical thermal target categories such as pedestrians, vehicles, and small aerial targets. The IRay dataset analysis diagram is shown in Fig. 8b. The dataset comprises 11,045 infrared images, with 8753 used for training and 2292 for testing. All images share a uniform resolution of 640 × 512 pixels. Significant challenges exist within the dataset, including thermal radiation interference, edge blurring, and target occlusion, making it a representative benchmark for validating model robustness and infrared feature recognition capabilities. During preprocessing, images undergo grayscale normalization and histogram-based heat enhancement to mitigate sensor noise effects. To ensure experimental fairness and reproducibility, all experiments follow identical training strategies with input images uniformly resized to 640 × 640. Training and validation data were split in an 8:2 ratio, with the objective function optimized using a joint loss formulation.

Comparative experiment

To validate the comprehensive performance of the improved model DAN-YOLO-L, this paper selected multiple mainstream object detection algorithms as comparison models and conducted systematic comparative experiments. The experiments comprehensively evaluated performance across multiple dimensions, including detection accuracy (mAP@0.5, mAP@0.5:0.95), detection reliability (P, R), inference speed (FPS), and computational complexity (GFLOPs). The results demonstrate that DAN-YOLO-L achieves the highest detection accuracy. Its mAP@0.5 reached 0.819 and mAP@0.5:0.95 reached 0.514, both surpassing the other comparison models. Specifically, mAP@0.5 improved by approximately 6.4% and 6.1% compared to Faster RCNN (0.770) and SSD (0.772), respectively, significantly enhancing the feature separation capability of traditional detectors in complex backgrounds. Compared to lightweight models like YOLOv5n (0.770), YOLOv6n (0.751), and YOLOv8n (0.797), DAN-YOLO-L achieves superior results on several key metrics while maintaining overall balanced performance., achieving the highest detection accuracy among lightweight models. This demonstrates that structural optimization significantly enhances feature extraction and multi-scale fusion capabilities. Regarding recall (R), DAN-YOLO-L achieves 0.795, representing an approximately 15.6% improvement over YOLOv11n (0.682), thereby achieving higher detection completeness while maintaining high accuracy. Compared to YOLOv10n (0.743) and YOLOv9-t (0.733), recall improved by 7% and 8% respectively, fully demonstrating DAN-YOLO-L’s more robust detection performance for small and weakly textured objects. In terms of inference speed (FPS), DAN-YOLO-L achieves 92.28 FPS, comparable to YOLOv8n (89.53 FPS) and YOLOv9-t (95.0 FPS), while significantly outperforming traditional algorithms like Faster RCNN (25 FPS) and SSD (45 FPS), demonstrating robust real-time detection capabilities. Its computational complexity is 2.84 GFLOPs, slightly lower than YOLOv11n’s 3.2 GFLOPs, and significantly lower than RT-DETR (50.3 GFLOPs) and IRSTD-YOLO (20.0 GFLOPs), achieving an outstanding balance between performance and energy consumption. Notably, the comparison with IRSTD-YOLO33—a dedicated model for infrared small object detection–is particularly striking. DAN-YOLO-L achieves a 3% improvement in mAP@0.5 while significantly reducing computational complexity to approximately one-fifth that of IRSTD-YOLO, with inference speed increasing by about 1.5 times. This demonstrates DAN-YOLO-L’s superior computational efficiency and universal adaptability, particularly in scenarios demanding rapid response and high-density small object detection. Specifically, while IRSTD-YOLO requires 20.04 GFLOPs, our DAN-YOLO-L operates with only 2.84 GFLOPs, representing a reduction in computational complexity by approximately 85.8%. This dramatic improvement in efficiency, achieved without sacrificing detection precision, underscores the superiority of DAN-YOLO-L for deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms. In summary, DAN-YOLO-L achieves a remarkable equilibrium across detection accuracy, recall, and real-time performance. By introducing multi-scale attention enhancement and lightweight feature fusion mechanisms, it not only effectively improves confidence and robustness in small object detection but also substantially reduces computational resource consumption while maintaining high inference speed. Across all metrics, DAN-YOLO-L demonstrates the optimal performance-efficiency ratio among comparable models, making it particularly suitable for resource- and speed-sensitive application environments such as embedded devices, edge computing terminals, and real-time monitoring scenarios.

Comparison of pruning experiments

To enhance model inference speed and reduce model size, this study applied L1 pruning optimization to the DAN-YOLO model. It should be emphasized that the L1 pruning optimization was specifically executed on the final, fully-integrated DAN-YOLO model rather than on each intermediate ablation variant. The rationale behind this design is that the primary objective of pruning is to achieve the optimal balance between accuracy and inference efficiency for actual deployment. By performing compression on the highest-performing configuration (which integrates all proposed modules), we ensure that the model retains the maximum representational capacity before undergoing structural regularization. This targeted optimization aligns with the practical goal of providing the most efficient version of our best-performing detector for resource-constrained UAV platforms. L1 pruning evaluates the magnitude of each connection’s weight, removing connections that contribute minimally to the model’s output. This reduces computational load and optimizes the model structure. We performed pruning based on the L1 norm of connections across layers and recovered any potential performance loss through fine-tuning. In experiments, we defined the Speed_up ratio as the computational load of the pruned model divided by that of the original model. By comparing pruning effects at different Speed_up levels, Table 5 shows that when Speed_up reaches 1.6, the pruned model demonstrates significant improvements across multiple performance metrics. Specifically, mAP@0.5 improved from 0.799 to 0.819, while the model’s parameter count and memory footprint decreased by 1.19 M and 2.5 M, respectively. Inference speed also accelerated significantly, validating pruning’s computational efficiency optimization.This slight increase in mAP@0.5 from 0.799 to 0.819 after pruning is primarily attributed to the fine-tuning recovery phase and the structural regularization effect. During fine-tuning, the model re-optimizes the remaining essential weights within a more compact architecture, better capturing the discriminative features of infrared small targets. Additionally, L1-norm pruning filters out redundant channels and parameters that may contribute to background noise or overfitting, leading to a more robust representation. Figure 10 illustrates the comparison of channel counts across layers before and after pruning. It is evident that the channel count in the pruned model is significantly reduced, particularly in high-dimensional layers (e.g., “model_5” to “model_7”), where redundant channels are effectively eliminated. This optimization reduces computational overhead and substantially enhances inference speed. In the figure, yellow bars represent channel counts before pruning, while red bars indicate channel counts after pruning. It is observed that channel counts decreased in most layers, with channel distribution becoming more compact post-pruning, indicating effective model structure refinement. Although mAP@0.5 decreased when the acceleration ratio exceeded 1.6, other performance metrics of the pruned model remained at high levels, particularly inference speed and model size. Therefore, an acceleration ratio of 1.6 was adopted as the final pruning configuration in this study. At this ratio, pruning not only accelerated inference but also effectively reduced model size while maintaining a good balance in accuracy.

Conclusions

This paper addresses issues such as feature loss, inaccurate localization, and limited real-time performance caused by scale differences in small object detection by proposing the improved model DAN-YOLO. The model integrates deconv and C3k2 modules to enhance multi-scale feature extraction, introduces an improved Auxiliary Head for efficient multi-layer feature fusion, and designs an NWD-Inner-CIoU loss function combining Wasserstein distance with an inner auxiliary box mechanism to boost small object localization accuracy and robustness. Additionally, the proposed L1 pruning strategy effectively reduces computational complexity, improving inference speed while maintaining accuracy. Experiments on the Hit-UAV and IRay Infrared Dataset demonstrate that DAN-YOLO outperforms YOLOv11n in metrics including mAP@0.5, mAP@0.5:0.95, Precision, and Recall, particularly excelling in small object detection within complex backgrounds and low-contrast scenarios. The model maintains high efficiency in terms of GFLOPs and FPS, validating its potential for real-time applications in drones and embedded devices. Future work will focus on further enhancing the model’s generalization capabilities and lightweight design to accommodate more complex environments and multi-scenario tasks.
