Abstract
In this paper, we study Riemannian metrics on the three-dimensional lens spaces that are deformations of the standard Riemannian metric along the fibers of the Hopf fibration. In other words, these metrics are axisymmetric. There is a one-parametric family of such metrics. This family tends to an axisymmetric sub-Riemannian metric. We find the cut loci and the cut times using methods from geometric control theory. It turns out that the cut loci and the cut times converge to the cut locus and the cut time for the sub-Riemannian structure, that was already studied. Moreover, we get some lower bounds for the diameter of these Riemannian metrics. These bounds coincide with the exact values of diameters for the lens spaces L ⁡ ( p , 1 ) L(p;1) . Keywords : Lens space, Berger sphere, cut locus, diameter, geometric control theory. AMS subject classification : 53C30, 53C17, 49J15.

2
Axisymmetric Riemannian metrics on lens spaces

Let us recall the notion of a lens space.

Definition 1
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be coprime numbers. Consider a three dimensional sphere
S^{3}=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1\}
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-action on
S^{3}
via the formula:

[k]\circ(z,w)=\left(e^{i\frac{2\pi k}{p}}z,e^{i\frac{2\pi kq}{p}}w\right),\qquad\text{where}\qquad[k]\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ (z,w)\in S^{3}.

The orbit space
L(p;q)=S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is called
a lens space
.
We denote by
\Pi:S^{3}\rightarrow L(p;q)
the natural projection.

We need some model of the lens space
L(p;q)
. Let
z=q_{0}+iq_{3}
,
w=q_{1}+iq_{2}
.

Proposition 1
(See, for example,
[
8
]
, Prop. 2).

Assume that
p>1
. Consider the subset

L=\left\{(z,w)\in S^{3}\,\Bigm|\,q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+\frac{q_{3}^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}}\leqslant 1,\ q_{0}\geqslant 0\right\}\subset S^{3}.

Let us glue the points
(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\sim(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime},q_{3}^{\prime})
of the boundary
\partial L
if one of the following conditions holds
:

(1)

q_{3}>0
,
q_{3}^{\prime}=-q_{3}
and
z^{\prime}=q_{1}^{\prime}+iq_{2}^{\prime}=e^{\frac{2\pi q}{p}}z
, where
z=q_{1}+iq_{2}
,

(2)

q_{3}=q_{3}^{\prime}=0
, and
z^{\prime}=e^{\frac{2\pi k}{p}}z
for some
k=1,\dots,p
.

The resulting topological space is homeomorphic to
L(p;q)
, see Fig.

Remark 1
.

It is not difficult to see that there are homeomorphisms

L(p;q)\simeq L(-p;q)\simeq L(p;-q)\simeq L(p;kp+q)\qquad\text{for}\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let us mention some particular cases.
First, note that the Lie group

\mathrm{SU}_{2}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}z&w\\
-\bar{w}&\bar{z}\\
\end{array}\right)\,\Bigm|\,z,w\in\mathbb{C},\ |z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1\right\}

is homeomorphic to
S^{3}
.
Second, the lens spaces
L(p;1)
are
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-homogeneous.
Indeed, the action of the generator of the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
can be represented by the left multiplication by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{p}}&0\\
0&e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{p}}\\
\end{array}\right)

which commutes with the right action of the group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
.
Similarly, the lens space
L(p;-1)
is
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-homogeneous, since the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-generator’s action can be represented by the right multiplication by the same matrix that commutes with the left action of the group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
.

Obviously,
L(1;1)=S^{3}=\mathrm{SU}_{2}
and
L(2;1)=\mathbb{R}P^{2}=\mathrm{SO}_{3}
.
The lens space
L(4;1)
is homeomorphic to the projectivization of the tangent bundle of the two dimensional sphere.
It may be a useful model for an anthropomorphic contour recovery for spherical images avoiding cusps, compare with
[
10
,
11
]
.

Now we define a Riemannian metric we deal with.

Notice that the left action of the circle group

\mathrm{U}_{1}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\varphi}&0\\
0&e^{-i\varphi}\\
\end{array}\right)\,\Bigm|\,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathrm{SU}_{2}

on
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
commutes with the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-action. This implies, that the group
\mathrm{U}_{1}
acts also on the lens space
L(p;q)
.
The factor is homeomorphic to
S^{2}
, since it is a complex projective line
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with homogeneous coordinates
[z:w]
factorized by cyclic group generated by rotation by the angle
\frac{2\pi(1-q)}{p}
around zero in the affine chart
w\neq 0
.

Definition 2
.

The natural projection
L(p;q)\rightarrow L(p;q)/\mathrm{U}_{1}\simeq S^{2}
is called
the Hopf fibration
.
Its fibers are circles.

We consider the symmetric metric on
S^{3}
deformed along the fibers of the Hopf fibration and then transferred to
L(p;q)
.

More precisely, we consider a
\mathrm{SU}_{1}
-left-invariant and
\mathrm{U}_{1}
-right-invariant Riemannian metric on
S^{3}
, in other words,
a left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
on the Lie group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
that in the tangent space of the identity reads as

g(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=I_{1}u_{1}^{2}+I_{1}u_{2}^{2}+I_{3}u_{3}^{2},\qquad\text{where}\qquad(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\mathfrak{su}_{2}=T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{SU}_{2},

and
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
are coordinates in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}_{2}
corresponding to the basis

e_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right),\qquad e_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right),\qquad e_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}i&0\\
0&-i\\
\end{array}\right).

Remark 2
.

Notice that if
I_{1}=I_{3}
, then
g=4I_{1}s
, where
s
is the standard metric on the unit sphere
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
(i.e., restricted from the Euclidian structure of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
).

Proposition 2
.

The group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
acts on
S^{3}\simeq\mathrm{SU}_{2}
by isometries.

Proof.

Define by
f:S^{3}\rightarrow S^{3}
the action of the generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, i.e.,
f:(z,w)\mapsto(\varepsilon z,\varepsilon^{q}w)
,
where
\varepsilon=e^{i\frac{2\pi}{p}}
.
It is easy to check that

df\circ dL_{(z,w)}(i\xi,\zeta)=dL_{f(z,w)}(i\xi,\varepsilon^{q-1}\zeta),

where
L_{(z,w)}
denotes the left-shift by an element
\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}z&w\\
-\bar{w}&\bar{z}\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SU}_{2}

and
(i\xi,\zeta)
is a tangent vector to
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
at the identity point, i.e.,
\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}i\xi&\zeta\\
-\bar{\zeta}&-i\xi\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathfrak{su}_{2}
.
Since our metric is axisymmetric, i.e.,
\mathrm{U}_{1}
-invariant, then
(i\xi,\zeta)\mapsto(i\xi,\varepsilon^{q-1}\zeta)
is local isometry.
∎

Proposition
2
implies that we can transfer axisymmetric Riemannian metric from
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
to the lens space
L(p;q)
.
We call the resulting metric
the Berger metric
on
L(p;q)
by analogue with the Berger sphere.
Let as introduce a parameter that measures the oblateness of the metric:

\eta=\frac{I_{1}}{I_{3}}-1>0.

When
\eta\rightarrow-1
, or, equivalently,
I_{3}\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain sub-Riemannian structure on
L(p;q)
.
The corresponding sub-Riemannian distribution is the projection of the left-invariant distribution on the Lie group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}

defined by the subspace
\sspan{\{e_{1},e_{2}\}}\subset\mathfrak{su}_{2}
.
This sub-Riemannian structure was studied by U. Boscain and F. Rossi
[
8
, Sec. 4]
.

3
Some necessary definitions and known results

Any geodesic in the lens space is a projection of a geodesic in the group
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
.
We use the Hamiltonian approach to describe geodesics in
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
, see
[
1
, Sec. 2]
for details.
Consider the cotangent bundle
\pi:T^{*}\mathrm{SU}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{SU}_{2}
.
Any geodesic is a projection of a trajectory of the Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{H}
in
T^{*}\mathrm{SU}_{2}
corresponding to the Hamiltonian

H=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{I_{1}}+\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{I_{1}}+\frac{h_{3}^{2}}{I_{3}}\right)
, where
h_{i}(\lambda)=\langle dL_{\pi(\lambda)}e_{i},\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\lambda\in T^{*}\mathrm{SU}_{2}

are linear on the fibers of the cotangent bundle Hamiltonians.
Any arclength parameterized geodesic starting from the point
\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{SU}_{2}
is determined by its initial covector
h\in C\subset T^{*}_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{SU}_{2}\simeq\mathfrak{su}_{2}^{*}
,
where
C=\{h\in\mathfrak{su}_{2}^{*}\,|\,H(h)=\frac{1}{2}\}
is a level surface of the Hamiltonian
H
.

Definition 3
.

(1)
The exponential map
is the map

\Exp:C\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow L(p;q),\qquad\Exp(h,t)=\Pi\circ\pi\circ e^{t\vec{H}}h,\qquad(h,t)\in C\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
e^{t\vec{H}}
is the flow of the Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{H}
and
\Pi:\mathrm{SU}_{2}\rightarrow L(p;q)
is the factorization by the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-action.

(2) A pair of diffeomorphisms
s:C\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
S:L(p;q)\rightarrow L(p;q)
, where
s
keeps the time, is called
a symmetry of the exponential map

if
\Exp\circ s=S\circ\Exp
.

(3) A point
m\in L(p;q)
is called
a Maxwell point
if two different geodesics of the same length starting from the point
o=\Pi(\mathrm{id})
meet one another at the point
m
.

(4) A critical value of the exponential map is called
a conjugate point
.

(5) If a geodesic starting from the point
o
is optimal up to the point
c
on it and is not optimal after this point, then the point
c
is called
a cut point
along this geodesic. The set of the cut points along all geodesics starting from the point
o
is called
the cut locus

\mathrm{Cut}_{o}
.

Usually Maxwell points appear due to symmetries of the exponential map. If
\mathrm{Sym}
is some group of symmetries, then by
t_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{Sym}}
we denote the first time when a Maxwell point corresponding to symmetry appears and call it
the first Maxwell time for the symmetry group

\mathrm{Sym}
. Analogically one can define
the first conjugate time
and
the cut time
. The first Maxwell time, the first conjugate time and the cut time are the functions of initial covector of a geodesic:

t_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{Sym}}:C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},\qquad t_{\mathrm{conj}}:C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},\qquad t_{\mathrm{cut}}:C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}.

We will find the set of first Maxwell points corresponding to symmetries
M^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{o}
and prove that the exponential map is a diffeomorphism of the following domains:

\Exp:\{(h,t)\in C\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\,|\,0<t<t_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{Sym}}(h)\}\rightarrow L(p;q)\setminus\left(\closure{M^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{o}}\cup\{o\}\right),

where
\closure{M^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{o}}
is the closure of
M^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{o}
.
This will imply that
\mathrm{Cut}_{o}=\closure{M^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{o}}
. To prove this diffeomorphism we will need the inequality
t_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{Sym}}(h)\leqslant t_{\mathrm{conj}}(h)
for any
h\in C
.

Also we need geodesic equations and the equations for the conjugate time obtained in
[
1
,
12
]
.

Introduce the following notation. If
h=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in\mathfrak{su}_{2}^{*}
are coordinates in the basis dual to the basis
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathfrak{su}_{2}

|h|=\sqrt{h_{1}^{2}+h_{2}^{2}+h_{3}^{2}},\qquad\bar{h}_{i}=\frac{h_{i}}{|h|},\ i=1,2,3,\qquad\tau=\frac{2I_{1}t}{|h|}.

4
Maxwell strata

The exponential map for an axisymmetric Riemannian problem on the lens space
L(p;q)
has the same symmetry group as in the cases of
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{3}
.
The group of symmetries is
\mathrm{Sym}\simeq\mathrm{O}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we refer for details to
[
1
, Sec. 4]
.
The generators of this group act in the pre-image and in the image of the exponential map in the following way:

(s1)

s
is a rotation around
h_{3}
-axis and
S
is a rotation around
q_{3}
-axis in
(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
-space;

(s2)

s
is a reflection with respect to a plane containing
h_{3}
-axis and
S
is a reflection with respect to a plane containing
q_{3}
-axis;

(s3)

s
is the reflection with respect to the
(h_{1},h_{2})
-plane and
S
is the reflection with respect to the
(q_{1},q_{2})
-plane.

It is already known that the first Maxwell time
t_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{Sym}}
for
p>1
depends on the Maxwell time corresponding to the rotations (s1) and compositions of the rotations (s1) with the reflection (s3), see
[
1
, Prop. 4]
. It is easy to see from the geodesic equations (Proposition
3
) that the Maxwell time for rotations corresponds to
\tau=\pi
, while the compositions of the rotations by angles
\frac{2\pi kq}{p}
with the reflection (s3) give Maxwell points that are determined by topology of the lens space
L(p;q)
. These Maxwell points form the surface
\partial L
with identified points as described in Proposition
1
.
The corresponding
\tau
for this Maxwell time is the first positive root with respect to the variable
\tau
of the equation of the surface
\partial L
where the parametric geodesics equations are substituted.
Thus, the comparison of this first positive root with
\pi
plays a key role in the analysis of Maxwell points.

Remember that the surface
\partial L\subset S^{3}
for
p>1
has the equation

q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+\frac{q_{3}^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}}-1=-q_{0}^{2}-q_{3}^{2}+\frac{q_{3}^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}}=\frac{1}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}}\left(q_{3}^{2}\cos^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}-q_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}\right)=

=\frac{1}{\sin^{2}{\frac{\pi}{p}}}\left(q_{3}\cos{\frac{\pi}{p}}-q_{0}\sin{\frac{\pi}{p}}\right)\left(q_{3}\cos{\frac{\pi}{p}}+q_{0}\sin{\frac{\pi}{p}}\right)=0.

Let us introduce the function
\ell(q)=\ell_{-}(q)\ell_{+}(q)
, where

\begin{array}[]{cclcl}\ell_{-}(q)&=&q_{3}\cos{\frac{\pi}{p}}-q_{0}\sin{\frac{\pi}{p}}&=&\cos{\tau}\sin{\left(\tau\eta\bar{h}_{3}-\frac{\pi}{p}\right)}+\bar{h}_{3}\sin{\tau}\cos{\left(\tau\eta\bar{h}_{3}-\frac{\pi}{p}\right)},\\
\ell_{+}(q)&=&q_{3}\cos{\frac{\pi}{p}}+q_{0}\sin{\frac{\pi}{p}}&=&\cos{\tau}\sin{\left(\tau\eta\bar{h}_{3}+\frac{\pi}{p}\right)}+\bar{h}_{3}\sin{\tau}\cos{\left(\tau\eta\bar{h}_{3}+\frac{\pi}{p}\right)}.\\
\end{array}

(2)

Denote by
\tau_{\ell}
the first positive root of
\ell(q_{0}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{1}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{2}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{3}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau))
with respect to variable
\tau
and fixed
\bar{h}_{3}
,
Obviously,
\tau_{\ell}=\min{(\tau_{\ell}^{-},\tau_{\ell}^{+})}
, where
\tau_{\ell}^{-}
and
\tau_{\ell}^{+}
are the first positive roots of the equations (respectively):

\ell_{-}(q_{0}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{1}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{2}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{3}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau))=0,\qquad\ell_{+}(q_{0}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{1}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{2}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau),q_{3}(\bar{h}_{3},\tau))=0.

We regard
\tau_{\ell}^{-}
and
\tau_{\ell}^{+}
as functions of
\bar{h}_{3}
, so
\tau_{\ell}^{-},\tau_{\ell}^{+}:[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Remark 3
.

Note that if
p=1
, then
\ell(q)=q_{3}^{2}
and
\tau_{\ell}(\bar{h}_{3})=\tau_{\ell}^{-}(\bar{h}_{3})=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
is the first positive root of the equation

. This Maxwell time corresponding to the reflection with respect to the plane

, i.e., the symmetry (s3), plays the key role in the Maxwell time analysis in the case

, see

\tau_{\ell}
is universal and doesn’t depend on

, while the surface
\ell=0
is the surface of fixed point for different symmetries that depend on

.

Lemma 1
.

The following expression for
\tau_{\ell}
is satisfied
:

\tau_{\ell}(\bar{h}_{3})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tau_{\ell}^{-}(\bar{h}_{3}),&\text{if}&\bar{h}_{3}\geqslant 0,\\
\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3}),&\text{if}&\bar{h}_{3}<0.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Note that
\ell_{-}(-\bar{h}_{3})=-\ell_{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
. This implies that
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(-\bar{h}_{3})=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
, see Fig.
3
.
If
p=1
or
p=2
, then
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(\bar{h}_{3})=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
.
Assume that
p>2
. We have
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(0)=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(0)=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(1)=\frac{\pi}{p(1+\eta)}<\frac{(p-1)\pi}{p(1+\eta)}=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(0)
.
If
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(\bar{h}_{3})=\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
for some
\bar{h}_{3}
, then
q_{0}(\bar{h}_{3})=q_{3}(\bar{h}_{3})=0
, this implies that
\bar{h}_{3}=0
.
So,
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(\bar{h}_{3})\leqslant\tau_{\ell}^{+}(\bar{h}_{3})
for

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
\eta\geqslant-\frac{p-1}{p}
. Then
t_{\mathrm{cut}}(0)\geqslant t_{\mathrm{cut}}(1)
iff
\eta\geqslant\frac{4}{p^{2}}-1
.
This inequality is satisfied automatically if
p\geqslant 4
.

Proof.

First,
|h|=\sqrt{I_{1}}
for
\bar{h}_{3}=0
and
|h|=\frac{\sqrt{I_{1}}}{\sqrt{1+\eta}}
for
\bar{h}_{3}=1
by formula (
4
).
Second,
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(0)=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\tau_{\ell}^{-}(1)=\frac{\pi}{p(1+\eta)}
due to formula (
2
).
Third,

t_{\mathrm{cut}}(0)=\frac{2I_{1}\tau_{\ell}^{-}(0)}{|h|}=\pi\sqrt{I_{1}},\qquad t_{\mathrm{cut}}(1)=\frac{2I_{1}\tau_{\ell}^{-}(1)}{|h|}=2\sqrt{I_{1}}\frac{\pi}{p\sqrt{1+\eta}}.

(5)

Finally,

t_{\mathrm{cut}}(0)\geqslant t_{\mathrm{cut}}(1)\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad p\sqrt{1+\eta}\geqslant 2\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\eta\geqslant\frac{4}{p^{2}}-1.

This inequality is satisfied automatically if
