Final Technical Report
DOE Award Number: DE SC0022526​
Project Title: California Bridge to the EIC: Building a Diverse Workforce for Nuclear
Physics Research at the Electron Ion Collider​
Principal Investigator: Prof. Miguel Arratia, University of California, Riverside

1. Executive Summary
The California Bridge to the Electron Ion Collider traineeship program established an
integrated workforce development effort connecting University of California campuses,
California State University Minority Serving Institutions, and DOE national laboratories.
The program expanded participation in nuclear physics research among students from
underrepresented and socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds while
strengthening collaborative activities aligned with the future Electron Ion Collider.

During the award period, trainees conducted experimental, theoretical, and
computational research, participated in consortium meetings and national laboratory
collaborations, and received structured mentoring and professional development. The
program achieved strong outcomes in graduate school placement, STEM career
transitions, and sustained engagement with DOE Nuclear Physics research.

2. Consortium Activities and Training Model
The consortium included UC campuses, CSU institutions, Lawrence Berkeley National
Laboratory, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. Activities were concentrated in
Southern California with UCLA and UC Riverside serving as primary research nodes.

Trainees participated in detector development, nuclear physics data analysis, and
computational modeling related to EIC science. Training occurred through hands on
laboratory research, remote computational work, and collaborative projects utilizing
national laboratory facilities and high performance computing resources.

The consortium hosted two multi day meetings per year and maintained biweekly
remote seminars. These meetings provided opportunities for trainee presentations,
technical exchange, and cohort building, creating a collaborative environment that
complemented traditional conference participation.

3. Recruitment and Participation
Recruitment followed Cal Bridge best practices and focused on CSU MSI campuses
and UC Riverside students located near UCLA and UCR. The program launch occurred
out of phase with the primary Cal Bridge recruitment calendar, which reduced the
availability of some candidates. To maintain participation, the consortium adopted a
hybrid engagement model that combined remote and in person research activities.

Students were recruited from CSU San Luis Obispo, CSU Northridge, CSU San
Bernardino, CSU Long Beach, CSU Dominguez Hills, and UC Riverside. Several
trainees were first generation college students or transfers from community colleges.

6. Program Impact
The award strengthened collaboration among DOE nuclear physics researchers across
UC campuses and national laboratories while building a pipeline of diverse trainees
entering graduate education and technical careers. The integrated mentoring structure
fostered a collaboration within a collaboration that increased trainee retention and
engagement.

 3

Participants gained experience in experimental nuclear physics, computation, and
interdisciplinary teamwork, positioning them for continued involvement in EIC related
research and the broader DOE Nuclear Physics mission.

7. Challenges and Mitigation
Pandemic related restrictions limited access to national laboratory facilities during
portions of the award period. Remote research activities were implemented but did not
fully replace in person laboratory experiences. Some trainees encountered
infrastructure challenges related to workspace and internet access.

The consortium mitigated these issues through hybrid training models, flexible
scheduling, and increased mentoring interactions, which allowed trainees to remain
engaged throughout the program.

8. Conclusion
The California Bridge to the EIC traineeship program successfully demonstrated a
scalable model for expanding participation in nuclear physics research among
underserved student populations. The program produced measurable outcomes in
graduate school placement, workforce development, and cross institutional
collaboration. Through sustained research engagement and mentorship, the award
contributed to preparing a diverse cohort of scientists aligned with the scientific goals of
the Electron Ion Collider and DOE Nuclear Physics.
