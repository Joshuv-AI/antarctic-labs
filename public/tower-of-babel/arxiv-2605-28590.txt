Two-loop QCD corrections to 𝑯 → 𝒃 + 𝒃 ¯ + 𝒈 H\rightarrow b+\bar{b}+g at higher powers in the dimensional regulator
Pulak Banerjee, Chinmoy Dey, M. C. Kumar, Vaibhav Pandey, and V. Ravindran

Abstract
We compute the two-loop massless QCD corrections to the amplitude of Higgs boson decay to bottom quark pair and a gluon ( H → b + b ¯ + g H\rightarrow b+\bar{b}+g ) in the higher powers of the dimensional regularization parameter ϵ \epsilon . The calculation is performed by projecting the amplitude onto the appropriate Lorentz structures related to the process. We also show the numerical behaviour of the form factors for a few sample phase-space points. These amplitudes are necessary ingredients for computing the three-loop virtual corrections to bottom-quark annihilation to Higgs plus jet production at the hadron collisions.

2
Higgs decay amplitudes

We consider the production of a bottom quark pair in association with a gluon via the decay of a Higgs boson, and for the calculation purpose, we follow the same notation as in Ref.
8
:

\displaystyle H(q)\rightarrow b(p_{1})+\bar{b}(p_{2})+g(p_{3}),

(1)

where
q^{2}=M_{H}^{2}
and
p_{i}^{2}=0
for
i=1,2,3
.
With Mandelstam variables defined as

\displaystyle s=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2},\quad t=(p_{2}+p_{3})^{2},\quad u=(p_{1}+p_{3})^{2},

(2)

We have the relation

\displaystyle s+t+u=M_{H}^{2}.

(3)

We define the scattering amplitudes for this process as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\epsilon^{*~\mu}(p_{3}),

(4)

where
\epsilon^{\mu}
is the polarization vector of the outgoing gluon.
The gluon polarization sum is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon_{\lambda}^{*~\mu}(p_{3})\epsilon_{\lambda}^{\nu}(p_{3})=-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{p_{3}^{\mu}n^{\nu}+p_{3}^{\nu}n^{\mu}}{p_{3}\cdot n},

(5)

where
n^{\mu}
is an arbitrary light-like massless four vector. For our calculation we choose
n^{\mu}=p_{1}^{\mu}
.

To calculate the partonic current, it is convenient to express it in a tensor decomposition.
Following the tensorial structure of the
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
, Lorentz invariance and transversality condition (
p_{3}\cdot\epsilon=0
) of external gluon allows us to write the amplitude as a linear combination of the following set of three tensor structures:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=\overline{u}(p_{1})\left[\mathcal{F}_{1}~p_{1}^{\mu}+\mathcal{F}_{2}~p_{2}^{\mu}+\mathcal{F}_{3}~\not{p}_{3}\gamma^{\mu}\right]v(p_{2}).

(6)

Further QCD ward identity
\left(p_{3~\mu}A^{\mu}=0\right)
reduces the three form factors (
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}_{3}
) to only two independent form factors (
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=\overline{u}(p_{1})\left[A_{1}\left(p_{2}\cdot p_{3}~p_{1}^{\mu}-p_{1}\cdot p_{3}~p_{2}^{\mu}\right)+A_{2}~\not{p}_{3}\gamma^{\mu}\right]v(p_{2}).

(7)

The equation
4
can be written as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\equiv A_{1}T_{1}+A_{2}T_{2}

(8)

where,

\displaystyle T_{1}=\overline{u}(p_{1})\left[p_{2}\cdot p_{3}~p_{1}^{\mu}-p_{1}\cdot p_{3}~p_{2}^{\mu}\right]v(p_{2})\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(p_{3}),

\displaystyle T_{2}=\overline{u}(p_{1})\not{p}_{3}\gamma^{\mu}v(p_{2})\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(p_{3}).

(9)

The original form-factors
\mathcal{F}_{i}
can be written in terms of
A_{i}
as,
\mathcal{F}_{1}=(p_{2}\cdot p_{3})~A_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{3}=A_{2}
.
We note that the tensor decomposition (
7
) of the amplitude is completely general and valid to all orders in perturbation theory, as it is based only on Lorentz symmetry and gauge invariance.
The dimensionality of the space-time is taken as
d=4+\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is the dimensional regularization parameter.

The form factors
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
can be extracted from the amplitudes using appropriate projectors.
The projectors can be written as

\displaystyle P_{1}=C_{1,1}T^{\dagger}_{1}+C_{1,2}T^{\dagger}_{2},

\displaystyle P_{2}=C_{2,1}T^{\dagger}_{1}+C_{2,2}T^{\dagger}_{2}.

(10)

The coefficients
C_{i,j}
are determined by imposing the conditions :

\displaystyle\sum_{spins}P_{1}\mathcal{A}=A_{1},

\displaystyle\sum_{spins}P_{2}\mathcal{A}=A_{2}.

(11)

The Projectors are found to be,

\displaystyle P_{1}=\frac{1}{stu(d-3)}\left[\frac{2}{s}(d-2)T^{\dagger}_{1}+T^{\dagger}_{2}\right],

\displaystyle P_{2}=\frac{1}{stu(d-3)}\left[T^{\dagger}_{1}+\frac{s}{2}T^{\dagger}_{2}\right].

(12)

The form-factors (
A_{i}
) can be perturbatively expanded in terms strong coupling constant

(
a_{s}=g_{s}^{2}/16\pi^{2}
) as,

\displaystyle A_{i}=B_{f}\left[A_{i}^{(0)}+a_{s}A_{i}^{(1)}+a_{s}^{2}A_{i}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(a_{s}^{3})\right],\quad i=1,2.

(13)

Here
B_{f}
is the overall Born factor defined as,

\displaystyle B_{f}=4\pi\sqrt{a_{s}}~\lambda~(T^{a})_{ji},

(14)

where
\lambda=-im_{b}/v
is the Yukawa coupling of the Higgs boson to bottom quarks.
Here,
v
is the vacuum expectation value,
m_{b}
is the bottom-quark mass, and
(T^{a})_{ji}
is the Gell-Mann matrix in the fundamental representation of the SU(3) colour group.
The form factors can be obtained by applying the projectors (
12
) to the amplitude, order by order in perturbation theory, as we discuss in the next section.

Once the expressions for the form factors are obtained in terms of master integrals, we substitute the integrals that are known in the literature

3.1
UV Renormalization and IR subtraction

The unrenormalized form factors contain ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) divergences which appear as poles in
\epsilon(=d-4)
in dimensional regularization with regularization scale
\mu_{0}
.
We have performed UV renormalization of the form factors in the modified minimal-subtraction (
\overline{MS}
) scheme. For the process under consideration, both the strong coupling constant and the Yukawa coupling of the Higgs boson to bottom quarks require renormalization. We follow the renormalization procedure as described in
8
. For completeness, we also describe the renormalization procedure here.

The bare strong coupling constant
a^{b}_{s}
is related to the renormalized strong coupling constant
a_{s}\equiv a_{s}(\mu^{2})
at the renormalization scale
\mu
as,

\displaystyle\frac{a_{s}^{b}}{\mu_{0}^{\epsilon}}S_{\epsilon}=\frac{a_{s}}{\mu^{\epsilon}}\left[1+a_{s}Z_{1}+a_{s}^{2}Z_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(a_{s}^{3}\right)\right]

(16)

where,

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\frac{2\beta_{0}}{\epsilon},

\displaystyle Z_{2}=\left(\frac{4\beta_{0}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\epsilon}\right).

(17)

The Yukawa coupling renormalization is given as,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{b}}{\mu_{0}^{\epsilon}}S_{\epsilon}=\frac{\lambda}{\mu^{\epsilon}}\left[1+a_{s}Z_{\lambda,1}+a_{s}^{2}Z_{\lambda,2}+\mathcal{O}\left(a_{s}^{3}\right)\right]

(18)

where,

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda,1}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(6C_{F}\right),

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda,2}=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(18C_{F}^{2}-6\beta_{0}C_{F}\right)+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{3}{2}C_{F}^{2}+\frac{97}{6}C_{F}C_{A}-\frac{5}{3}n_{f}C_{F}\right)

(19)

with
S_{\epsilon}=\exp\left[\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(\gamma_{E}-\ln 4\pi\right)\right]
, where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
The
C_{F}
and
C_{A}
are the Casimir invariants of the SU(3) colour group in the fundamental and adjoint representation,
n_{f}
is the number of active quark flavours. The coefficients of QCD beta function
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
are given as,

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{2}{3}n_{f},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{34}{3}C_{A}^{2}-\frac{10}{3}C_{A}n_{f}-2C_{F}n_{f}.

(20)

The renormalized form factors are obtained by substituting eqs. (
16
) and (
18
) in the unrenormalized form factors and re-expanding in powers of renormalized strong coupling constant
a_{s}
. The renormalized form factors at different orders in
a_{s}
can be written as,

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{i}^{b,(0)},

(21)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left[A_{i}^{b,(1)}+\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\left(\beta_{0}+6C_{F}\right)A_{i}^{b,(0)}\right],

(22)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}\bigg[A_{i}^{b,(2)}+\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\left(3\beta_{0}+6C_{F}\right)A_{b}^{b,(1)}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}\left\{\frac{3}{2}\beta_{0}+12\beta_{0}C_{F}+18C_{F}^{2}+\epsilon\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}+\frac{97}{6}C_{A}C_{F}+\frac{3}{2}C_{F}^{2}-\frac{5}{3}C_{F}n_{f}\right)\right\}A_{i}^{b,(0)}\bigg].

(23)

After UV renormalization, the form factors still contain IR divergences as poles in
\epsilon
. In any IR-safe observable, these IR divergences will cancel against those coming from the real emission contributions as per the KLN theorem
65
;
68
.
The structure of these IR divergences is universal and is well understood
32
. We follow the subtraction procedure as described in
49
;
8
to obtain the finite form factors. The renormalized form factors can be written in terms of finite form factors as,

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=2\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)A_{i}^{(0)}+A_{i}^{(1),fin},

(24)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=4\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(2)}(\epsilon)A_{i}^{(0)}+2\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)A_{i}^{(1)}+A_{i}^{(2),fin},

(25)

where the operators
\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)
and
\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(2)}(\epsilon)
are given as,

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\exp[-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\gamma_{E}]}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\Bigg[\left(\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{3}{\epsilon}\right)\left(C_{A}-2C_{F}\right)\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{s}\right)^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}C_{A}+\frac{3}{2\epsilon}C_{A}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{2\epsilon}\right)\left\{\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{t}\right)^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}+\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{u}\right)^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\right\}\Bigg],

(26)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(2)}(\epsilon)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)\left(\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(\epsilon)-2\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)+\frac{\exp[{\frac{\epsilon}{2}\gamma_{E}}]}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)}\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+\mathbf{K}\right)\mathbf{I}_{b}^{(1)}(2\epsilon)

\displaystyle+\left(2~\mathbf{H}_{q}^{(2)}(\epsilon)+\mathbf{H}_{g}^{(2)}(\epsilon)\right),

(27)

where,

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}=\left(\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)C_{A}-\frac{5}{9}n_{f},

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{q}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigg[C_{A}C_{F}\left(\frac{245}{432}+\frac{23}{16}\zeta_{2}-\frac{13}{4}\zeta_{3}\right)+C_{F}^{2}\left(\frac{3}{16}-\frac{3}{2}\zeta_{3}+3\zeta_{3}\right)+C_{F}n_{f}\left(\frac{25}{216}-\frac{1}{8}\zeta_{2}\right)\Bigg],

(29)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{g}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Bigg[C_{A}^{2}\left(-\frac{5}{24}-\frac{11}{48}\zeta_{2}-\frac{1}{4}\zeta_{3}\right)+C_{A}n_{f}\left(\frac{29}{54}+\frac{1}{24}\zeta_{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}C_{F}n_{f}-\frac{5}{54}n_{f}^{2}\Bigg].

(30)

4
Summary

In this work, we computed the two-loop massless QCD correction to the Higgs boson decay to a bottom quark pair in association with a gluon (
H\rightarrow b+\bar{b}+g
) at higher powers of the dimensional regularization parameter
\epsilon
.
We have used the projection method to extract the form factors from the amplitude. Our results of these amplitudes are crucial to construct N
3
LO QCD correction to the Higgs boson production in the bottom anti-bottom annihilation process in association with a jet.
We have confirmed that our results reproduce the expected infrared structure as predicted by Catani’s IR subtraction operators.
We observe that due to the presence of the higher weight GPLs in the coefficients of
\epsilon^{2}
, the computational time for the form-factors rapidly increases compared to that of
\epsilon^{0}
.
The two-loop amplitudes computed in this work provide essential ingredients for future high-precision (three-loop) calculations of Higgs boson production in bottom quark-initiated channels at hadron colliders.

Appendix A
GPLs

We recall here that the GPLs are defined as iterated integrals over the rational functions as,

\displaystyle G(l_{1},l_{2},...,l_{n};x)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{t-l_{1}}~G(l_{2},...,l_{n};t),

(A.1)

\displaystyle G(\underbrace{0,0,..,0}_{n};x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\text{ln}^{n}(x),~G(x)=1;

(A.2)

We write few weight-1 GPLs in terms of logarithms
44
,

\displaystyle G(1;y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln(1-y)\;,

\displaystyle G(0;y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln y\;,

\displaystyle G(-1;y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln(1+y)

\displaystyle G(1-z;y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(1-\frac{y}{1-z}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle G(z;y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{z-y}{z}\right).

(A.3)

The derivative of the GPLs can be written as,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(a,\vec{b};y)=f(a,y)G(\vec{b};y)

(A.4)

Where,
f(a,y)=\frac{1}{a-y}
.
