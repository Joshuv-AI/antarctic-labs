Operational criterion for Wigner function negativity
Paolo Solinas, Beatrice Donelli, Stefano Gherardini

Abstract
We introduce an operational criterion to identify Wigner function (WF) negativity for an arbitrary quantum state within the framework of quantum non-demolition measurements. This criterion corresponds to experimentally accessible schemes that enable a direct measurement of the WF, and establishes the coherent-state basis as a privileged basis for determining when the WF exhibits negative regions. We show that the absence (presence) of coherent superpositions in the coherent-state basis provides direct information about the positivity (negativity) of the WF. In particular, the absence of such superpositions constitutes a sufficient condition for WF positivity. Although a general proof of necessity remains elusive, we demonstrate that this condition is also necessary in two relevant cases: Schrödinger-cat states and higher-order cat states on a circle. More precisely, for Schrödinger-cat states we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the positivity of the WF in full generality, whereas for high-order cat states on a circle we derive an analogous condition in the limit of a large number of densely packed coherent states.



Discriminating whether a system behaves classically or exhibits quantum features is of paramount importance, both for the foundations of quantum mechanics and for quantum technologies. From a foundational perspective, identifying when quantum behavior in a system vanishes allows us to understand whether it undergoes a quantum-to-classical transition

The boundary between the quantum and classical worlds is defined by the emergence of non-classical behaviors. While inequalities such as those by Bell

The WF

This link with QNDM suggests the following experimental protocol: the system is sequentially coupled in time to the two detectors

so that information about the system’s position and momentum is encoded in the detectors’ phases. By varying the coupling strength
\lambda
and
\eta

,
Solinas et al. 2021
,
Solinas et al. 2022
,
Solinas and Gherardini 2025

and

A more intriguing interpretation is suggested by Eq. (
2

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}
can be seen as a tool to extract information about the non-Hermitian adjoint operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}

Kraus 1983
,
Dürr et al. 1992
,
Peres 1993

Quantum coherence in the coherent-state basis—

As discussed in Refs.
Solinas and Gherardini 2025
,
Solinas and Gherardini 2026
, in a finite-dimensional space with an orthonormal basis, the negativity of the QNDM quasi-probability distribution arises from the presence of off-diagonal density-matrix elements in the eigenbasis of the measured observable. In the present case of an infinite-dimensional space, it is natural to ask to what extent a similar statement can be formulated. Considering the second term in Eq. (
2
), we identify the annihilation operator
\hat{a}
as the measured observable and, consequently, the coherent states
|\beta,\sigma\rangle
as the privileged basis, since
\hat{a}\mathinner{|{\beta,\sigma}\rangle}=\alpha\mathinner{|{\beta,\sigma}\rangle}

Glauber 1963
,
Sudarshan 1963
,
Klauder and Skagerstam 1985
.

There is strong evidence that the CSB plays a privileged role in the description of the WF. First, Hudson’s theorem
Hudson 1974
affirms that a pure quantum state has a positive WF
if and only if
it is Gaussian, i.e., a coherent state. Second, Diósi and Kiefer
Diósi and Kiefer 2000
,
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
, as well as Brody et al.
Brody et al. 2025
, have shown that the pointer basis for the open-system dynamics of the WF is the Gaussian (coherent-state) basis.

Since the CSB depends on a length scale
\sigma
(i.e., the Gaussian width), one may further ask how
\sigma
should be chosen when decomposing the system’s density matrix over the CSB. It turns out that, although both the decomposition and the density matrix’s off-diagonal elements depend on
\sigma
, the WF and its negativity do not. In fact, as discussed in Ref.
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
and Appendix, the WF is invariant under the rescaling by
c
:
X\rightarrow cX
and
P\rightarrow P/c
. Since this transformation corresponds to a change in
\sigma
, it follows that the quasi-characteristic function
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}
in Eq. (
2
) can be decomposed in any CSB (which depends on
\hat{a}
through
\sigma
). This invariance is implicit in Hudson’s theorem
Hudson 1974
.

In some physical situations, determining the length scale
\sigma
is natural, for instance, in the case of a cat or higher-order cat state (see discussion below). Moreover, there exists a broad class of systems for which
\sigma
is dictated by the physical problem itself, namely systems interacting with an environment, where the natural length scale is set by system-environment interactions
Diósi and Kiefer 2000
,
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
,
Brody et al. 2025
. In all these cases, it is convenient to use the CSB associated with this specific length scale.

More generally, the system may involve multiple length scales, as in the case of a superposition of two coherent states with different
\sigma
values. However, the invariance of the WF under length rescaling ensures that any CSB can be used. In the following, to simplify the discussion, we restrict our analysis to cases in which the system is characterized by a single, known length scale
\sigma
.

Sufficient condition for Wigner function negativity—
The absence of quantum coherence in the CSB
\mathinner{|{\beta,\sigma}\rangle}\equiv\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}
constitutes a sufficient condition for the positivity of the WF. To see this, consider a system prepared in an incoherent mixture of coherent states. In this case, the density matrix is diagonal in the CSB and can be written as
\hat{\rho}_{\rm diag}=\int d^{2}\beta\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\hat{\rho}|\beta}\rangle}\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}
with
d^{2}\beta=d{\rm Re}\beta~d{\rm Im}\beta
and
\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\hat{\rho}|\beta}\rangle}\geq 0

Glauber 1963
.
Then, the corresponding quasi-characteristic function reads
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}=\int d^{2}\beta\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\hat{\rho}|\beta}\rangle}
.
By integrating
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}
over the variables
\lambda,\eta
, one can straightforwardly show that the WF is positive; refer also to the Appendix for more details. This result naturally includes the sufficient condition of Hudson’s theorem
Hudson 1974
, corresponding to the case of a single coherent state,
\hat{\rho}=\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}
.

a) and see Appendix for more details]. Therefore, if
\Delta\geq\overline{\Delta}_{c}
, the Wigner function is negative.

In the highly packed limit, the upper bound
\overline{\Delta}_{c}
well-approximates
\Delta_{c}
(boundary between positive and negative regions of the WF), as it exhibits the same trend with
d
. This is shown in Fig.
2
b), where we compare
\overline{\Delta}_{c}
with the values of
\Delta_{c}
computed numerically by searching for the minimum of WF over the full
X
-
P
space.

Similarly to the previous case, when
d
is much larger compared to the characteristic length scale of the problem (
d\gg\sigma
),
\Delta_{c}
becomes exponentially small. As a result, in such a regime, the presence of coherent superpositions in the system’s density matrix within the CSB practically represents a necessary and sufficient condition for the WF negativity.

Conclusions—

We have introduced an operational criterion to determine the negativity of the WF for a generic density matrix. This criterion is associated with a well-defined protocol (the QNDM scheme) that has already found application in many contexts
Guerlin et al. 2007
,
Deléglise et al. 2008
,
Geremia et al. 2003
,
Guerlin et al. 2007
,
Solinas et al. 2021
,
Niemietz et al. 2021
,
Solinas et al. 2022
,
Yanagimoto et al. 2023
and it identifies the coherent states basis as a privileged one to establish the condition for a negative WF. Starting from Refs.
Solinas and Gherardini 2025
,
Solinas and Gherardini 2026
, we studied to what extent the presence of coherent superpositions in this basis provides direct information about the WF negativity.

Sufficient condition for the WF negativity can be readily proven; that is, the absence of coherent-basis superpositions implies the positivity of the WF. Then, for the Schrödinger-cat states and high-order cat states on a circle, relevant case-studies in the literature, we also discuss the necessary condition that guarantees negativity. In fact, we identify a critical residual coherence above which the WF exhibits negativity. This result is analytically exact for the Schrödinger-cat state, and approximate for high-order cat states.

The presence of a critical residual coherence
\Delta_{c}
is directly related to the decomposition of the quantum state in the CSB, which is continuous and overcomplete. However, given the exponential reduction of
\Delta_{c}
with the system’s length scale, in many cases one can argue that the mere presence of quantum superpositions in the CSB yields a negative WF.

Our results provide a new perspective, both theoretically and experimentally, for analyzing the WF and its properties. These ideas could be exploited to track the quantum-to-classical transition in many experimental set-ups
Arndt et al. 1999
,
Guerlin et al. 2007
,
Deléglise et al. 2008
,
Gerlich et al. 2011
,
Vlastakis et al. 2013
,
Aspelmeyer 2022
,
Fuchs et al. 2024
,
Pedalino et al. 2026
, or used to certify the presence of quantum effects in quantum continuous variable systems
Mu et al. 1996
,
Braunstein and van Loock 2005
,
Weedbrook et al. 2012
,
Adesso et al. 2014
. Nevertheless, a significant question remains open, namely, to understand whether the necessary condition can be extended to more complex discrete configurations or to the continuous case, while still dealing with an arbitrary density matrix.

(S6)

II
Invariance of the Wigner function under length rescaling

Let us consider the definition of the Wigner Function (WF) in terms of the quasi-characteristic function:

W(X,P)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int d\lambda~d\eta\,e^{-i\lambda X}e^{-i\eta P}\Tr[e^{i(\lambda\hat{x}+\eta\hat{p})}\hat{\rho}].

(S8)

Following Ref.
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
, we rescale all the lengths by a factor
c
:
X\rightarrow cX
,
P\rightarrow P/c
,
\hat{x}\rightarrow c\hat{x}
,
\hat{p}\rightarrow\hat{p}/c
, such that the rescaled Wigner function reads as

W\left(cX,\frac{P}{c}\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int d\lambda~d\eta~e^{-ic\lambda X}e^{-i\eta\frac{P}{c}}\Tr[e^{i(c\lambda\hat{x}+\eta\frac{\hat{p}}{c})}\hat{\rho}].

(S9)

By changing the integration variables to
\Lambda=c\lambda
and
\Gamma=\eta/c
, the extremes of integration do not change, and thus we can write:

W\left(cX,\frac{P}{c}\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int d\Lambda~d\Gamma~e^{-i\Lambda X}e^{-i\Gamma P}\Tr[e^{i(\Lambda\hat{x}+\Gamma\hat{p})}\hat{\rho}]=W(X,P).

(S10)

Therefore, the Wigner function is invariant under canonical linear transformation. This result was already discussed by Diósi and Kiefer in Ref.
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
.

III
Sufficient condition for the Wigner function negativity

Let us introduce the creation and annihilation operators that are linear combinations of
\hat{x}
and
\hat{p}
:

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\Big(\hat{x}+i\sigma^{2}\hat{p}\Big)

(S11)

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\Big(\hat{x}-i\sigma^{2}\hat{p}\Big),

(S12)

where
\hat{x}=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}+\hat{a}_{\sigma}\right)
and
\hat{p}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}-\hat{a}_{\sigma}\right)
are the inverse relations. Eqs. (
S11
)-(
S12
) obey the usual commutation relations
[\hat{a}_{\sigma},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}]=1
, given that
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i
(
\hbar=1
).

In the following, we rescale all the lengths over
\sigma
that is equivalent to take
\sigma=1
in the above formulas. The exponential operator
e^{i(\eta\hat{p}+\lambda\hat{x})}
can be written as

\displaystyle e^{i(\eta\hat{p}+\lambda\hat{x})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\frac{i\lambda-\eta}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\frac{i\lambda+\eta}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}\right\}=\exp\left\{\frac{i\lambda-\eta}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right\}\exp\left\{\frac{i\lambda+\eta}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}\right\}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}+\eta^{2}}{4}\right\}=

(S13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right\}\exp\left\{-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}\right\}\exp\left\{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{4}\right\},

where
\alpha\equiv(i\lambda-\eta)/\sqrt{2}
,
{\rm Re}\alpha=-\eta/\sqrt{2}
and
{\rm Im}\alpha=\lambda/\sqrt{2}
.

If the system’s density matrix is diagonal in the coherent state
\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}
, we can write it as
\hat{\rho}=\int d^{2}\beta~\rho_{\beta\beta}\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}
with
d^{2}\beta=d{\rm Re}\beta~d{\rm Im}\beta
and
\rho_{\beta\beta}=\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\hat{\rho}|\beta}\rangle}\geq 0

Glauber 1963
. Therefore, the quasi-characteristic function simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}=e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\Tr\left[e^{-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}\hat{\rho}\,e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\right]=e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\int d^{2}\beta~\rho_{\beta\beta}\,e^{-\alpha^{*}\beta+\alpha\beta^{*}},

(S14)

where we have used the fact that the coherent states are eigenstates of
\hat{a}
, i.e.,
e^{-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}}\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}=e^{-\alpha^{*}\beta}\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{\langle{\beta}|}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}=\mathinner{\langle{\beta}|}e^{\alpha\beta^{*}}
, and that
\Tr\left[\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}\right]=1
.
Expressing the integrals in
\lambda
and
\eta
in terms of
{\rm Re}\alpha
and
{\rm Im}\alpha
, the WF
W(X,P)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int d\lambda~d\eta\,e^{-i\lambda X}e^{-i\eta P}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\eta}
can be written as

W(X,P)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{2}\beta~\rho_{\beta\beta}\int d{\rm Re}\alpha~e^{-\frac{({\rm Re}\alpha)^{2}}{2}}e^{i\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Re}\alpha\left[P-\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Im}\beta\right]}\int d{\rm Im}\alpha~e^{-\frac{({\rm Im}\alpha)^{2}}{2}}e^{-i\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Im}\alpha\left[X-\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Re}\beta\right]}.

(S15)

Thus, by performing the Gaussian integrations, we obtain:

W(X,P)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^{2}\beta~\rho_{\beta\beta}\,e^{-\left(X-\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Re}\beta\right)^{2}}e^{-\left(P-\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Im}\beta\right)^{2}}.

(S16)

Since all the integrand terms are positive, the WF (
S16
) is positive.

III.1
Remark on the sufficient condition for Wigner function negativity

Albeit straightforward, we would like to stress that the result about the presence of a privileged basis, i.e., the coherent state basis, and the sufficient condition for WF negativity is not as trivial as it might seem. Even for one-dimensional systems, indeed, there are three natural bases in which to decompose the system’s density matrix associated with the operators
\hat{x}
,
\hat{p}
and
\hat{a}
, the latter being a linear combination of
\hat{x}
and
\hat{p}
. Any diagonal density matrix in one of these bases would lead to a positive WF. However, a density matrix diagonal in the
\hat{x}
basis would yield an unphysical and unnormalized WF.

To show this, we start from an alternative definition of the WF
Wigner 1932
,
Hillery et al. 1984
:
W(X,P)=\int dYe^{iYP}\mathinner{\langle{X-\frac{Y}{2}|\hat{\rho}|X+\frac{Y}{2}}\rangle}
, and we assume that the density matrix is diagonal in the position basis, i.e.,
\hat{\rho}=\int dx\rho(x,x)\outerproduct{x}{x}
. Using the orthogonality of position eigenstates, i.e.,
\mathinner{\langle{x|y}\rangle}=\delta(x-y)
, we obtain:

W(X,P)=\int dYe^{iYP}\int dx\,\rho(x,x)\,\delta\left(x-X+\frac{Y}{2}\right)\delta\left(x-X-\frac{Y}{2}\right)=\rho(X,X).

(S17)

The WF (
S17
) is nonphysical because it is not normalizable: the integration over
P
is not bounded. Indeed, it is known in the literature that the WF cannot be arbitrarily squeezed in phase space
Hillery et al. 1984
, as it is in Eq. (
S17
).

In conclusion, despite formally leading to positive WF, the cases in which the density matrix becomes diagonal in the basis of
\hat{x}
or
\hat{p}
are unphysical and must be regarded as mathematical limits. This observation is related to the findings in Refs.
Diósi and Kiefer 2000
,
Diósi and Kiefer 2002
,
Brody et al. 2025
, where, for open systems, it is shown that the density matrix reaches a diagonal form only in the mathematical limit of infinite-time evolution.

V
Schrödinger-cat states: Necessary condition for Wigner function negativity

We denote the coherent state associated with the complex number
\beta
and variance
\sigma^{2}
as
\mathinner{|{\beta,\sigma}\rangle}
. With this notation, let us consider a system prepared in a superposition of two coherent states.
The density matrix can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{N}\Big(\rho_{1,1}\outerproduct{\beta_1, \sigma}{\beta_1, \sigma}+\rho_{2,2}\outerproduct{\beta_2, \sigma}{\beta_2, \sigma}+\rho_{1,2}\outerproduct{\beta_1, \sigma}{\beta_2, \sigma}+\rho_{2,1}\outerproduct{\beta_2, \sigma}{\beta_1, \sigma}\Big),

(S21)

where the normalization constant is

N=\rho_{1,1}+\rho_{2,2}+\rho_{1,2}e^{-\frac{|\beta_{1}|^{2}+|\beta_{2}|^{2}}{2}}e^{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}^{*}}+\rho_{2,1}e^{-\frac{|\beta_{1}|^{2}+|\beta_{2}|^{2}}{2}}e^{\beta_{2}\beta_{1}^{*}}\,.

(S22)

As discussed in the main text, we restrict our analysis to the case
X_{1,2}=\pm\sqrt{2}\,\text{Re}\beta
and
P_{1,2}=0
. Using the expression of the Wigner function as a sum [see Eq. (
S20
)], we obtain two diagonal contributions (
i=j=1,2
):

W_{1,1}=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-P^{2}}e^{-(X-\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta)^{2}};\qquad W_{2,2}=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-P^{2}}e^{-(X+\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta)^{2}},

(S23)

and two off-diagonal contributions (
i=1,j=2
and
i=2,j=1
):

W_{1,2}=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-P^{2}}e^{-X^{2}}e^{-i2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta P}=W_{2,1}^{*}\,.

(S24)

Using the self-adjoint property of the off-diagonal elements, the total Wigner function can be written as

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left[\rho_{1,1}W_{1,1}+\rho_{2,2}W_{2,2}+\rho_{1,2}W_{1,2}+\rho_{2,1}W_{2,1}\right]=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left[\rho_{1,1}W_{1,1}+\rho_{2,2}W_{2,2}+2{\rm Re}(\rho_{1,2}W_{1,2})\right].

(S25)

Let us now parametrize the density matrix with the angles
\theta
and
\Delta
as

\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}\cos^{2}{\theta}&\Delta\sin{\theta}\cos{\theta}e^{i\phi}\\
\Delta\sin{\theta}\cos{\theta}e^{-i\phi}&\sin^{2}{\theta}\end{pmatrix}

(S26)

where
0\leq\Delta\leq 1
is a parameter that accounts for the degree of coherence of the system.

Substituting it into the Wigner function yields:

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi N}\left[\cos^{2}{\theta}\,e^{-P^{2}}e^{-(X-\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta)^{2}}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\,e^{-P^{2}}e^{-(X+\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta)^{2}}+2\Delta{\rm Re}\left(\sin{\theta}\cos{\theta}e^{i\phi}e^{-P^{2}}e^{-X^{2}}e^{-i2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta P}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-X^{2}-P^{2}}}{\pi N}\left[\cos^{2}{\theta}\,e^{2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta X-2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\,e^{-2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta X-2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}+\Delta|\sin{2\theta}|\cos{\left(\phi- 2\sqrt{2} \text{Re}\beta P \right)}\right]=

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-X^{2}-P^{2}-2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}}{\pi N}\left[\cos^{2}{\theta}\,e^{2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta X}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\,e^{-2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta X}+e^{2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}\Delta|\sin{2\theta}|\cos{\left(\phi- 2\sqrt{2} \text{Re}\beta P \right)}\right]\,.

(S27)

Defining
\tilde{\varphi}\equiv\phi-2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta P
and
Z\equiv e^{2\sqrt{2}\text{Re}\beta X}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-X^{2}-P^{2}-2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}}{\pi N}\left[\cos^{2}{\theta}\,Z+\sin^{2}{\theta}\,Z^{-1}+e^{2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}\Delta|\sin{2\theta}|\cos{\tphi}\right]=

(S28)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-X^{2}-P^{2}-2\text{Re}\beta^{2}}}{\pi NZ}\left[\cos^{2}{\theta}\,Z^{2}+b\,Z+\sin^{2}{\theta}\right]\,,

(S29)

where we have introduced
b\equiv e^{2(\text{Re}\beta)^{2}}\Delta|\sin{2\theta}|\cos{\tphi}
.
The last expression coincides with Eq. (5)
of the main text. The zeros of the Wigner function are obtained by imposing that the quadratic polynomial inside the brackets vanishes, i.e.
\mathcal{P}(Z)=\cos^{2}{\theta}\,Z^{2}+b\,Z+\sin^{2}{\theta}=0
, yielding

Z_{\pm}=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4\sin^{2}{\theta}\cos^{2}{\theta}}}{2\cos^{2}{\theta}}\,.

(S30)

Since
\cos^{2}\theta>0
, the parabola
\mathcal{P}(Z)
is concave up, and its minimum occurs at the middle point of the two solutions
Z_{m}=(Z_{+}+Z_{-})/2
. Therefore, one finds that the parabola at its vertex assumes the minimum value

\mathcal{P}(Z_{m})=2\sin^{2}\theta\left(1-\Delta^{2}\cos^{2}\tilde{\varphi}~e^{4{\rm Re}\beta^{2}}\right)\,.

(S31)

As the prefactor
(e^{-X^{2}-P^{2}-2{\rm Re}\beta^{2}})/(\pi NZ)
is non-negative, the condition to have a negative WF for a cat state is given by the inequality
\mathcal{P}(Z_{m})<0
, which corresponds to
\Delta>e^{-2{\rm Re}\beta^{2}}/|\cos\tilde{\varphi}|
. We have excluded the case
\sin{\theta}=0
, which would correspond to a density matrix with all the population in the first coherent state and zero population in the other one.

The strongest bound is obtained when the cosine term is maximal, i.e.,
\cos\tilde{\varphi}=1
, corresponding to momenta satisfying
\tilde{\varphi}=2\pi k
. In this case, the condition for Wigner-function negativity reduces to

\Delta>\Delta_{c}\equiv e^{-2(\mathrm{Re}\,\beta)^{2}},

(S32)

which coincides with Eq. (
8
) of the main text.

Higher-order cat states on a circle

Let us consider the Wigner function of
M

We are interested in the highly packed states limit, which formally corresponds to
M\gg d^{2}
. Similarly to the cat-state case, we parametrize the density matrix with a coherence factor
\Delta
multiplying the off-diagonal elements
\rho_{j,k}=[(1-\Delta)\delta_{jk}+\Delta]/M
with
0\leq\Delta\leq 1
equal for all the off-diagonal elements.

Let us restart from the total Wigner function, defined in Eqs. (3)-(4) of the main text, and rewrite it in polar coordinates using the above definitions

\displaystyle W(R,\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi NM}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[(1-\Delta)\delta_{jk}+\Delta\right]\exp\left(-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{j,k}}+r\cos(\phi-\Lambda_{j,k})e^{i\Phi_{j,k}}\right)=

\displaystyle=(1-\Delta)\frac{e^{-R^{2}-2d^{2}}}{\pi NM}\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{r\cos(\phi-\theta_j)}+\Delta\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi NM}\sum_{j,k=1}^{M}e^{-d^{2}e^{i2\Phi_{j,k}}+r\cos(\phi-\Lambda_{j,k})e^{i\Phi_{jk}}}\,,

(S33)

where we have defined
r=2\sqrt{2}Rd
,
\Lambda_{j,k}=(\theta_{j}+\theta_{k})/2
and
\Phi_{j,k}=(\theta_{j}-\theta_{k})/2
.
Introducing the index
\ell\equiv j-k
, we obtain
\Phi_{\ell}=\pi\ell/M
and
\Lambda_{\ell,k}=(\ell+2k)\pi/M
, so that

W(R,\phi)=(1-\Delta)\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-2d^{2}}}{\pi NM}\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{r\cos(\phi- \theta_j)}+\Delta\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi NM}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,e^{re^{i\Phi_{\ell}}\cos(\Lambda_{\ell,k} - \phi)}

(S34)

It is worth noting that this parametrization is valid only if the function
f(j,k)
, defined as the argument of the double sum, is periodic with period
M
in its first index. In particular, we require
f(j+M,k)=f(j,k)
, indeed

\displaystyle f(j+M,k)

\displaystyle=\exp{{-d^2 e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}(j-k+M)}} \, + r e^{i \frac{\pi}{M}(j-k+M)} \cos\left(\phi- \frac{\pi}{M} (j+k+M) \right) }=

\displaystyle=\exp{{-d^2 e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}(j-k)}} \, + r (-1) e^{i \frac{\pi}{M}(j-k)} \cos\left(\phi- \frac{\pi}{M} (j+k) -\pi\right) }=f(j,k)\,,

(S35)

where we have used
e^{i\pi}=-1
and
\cos{(x-\pi)=-\cos(x)}
. This ensures that the two sums can be treated as independent.
At this point, we perform a Jacobi-Anger expansion over the modified Bessel functions
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
:

e^{\alpha_{\ell}\cos(\Lambda_{\ell,k} - \phi)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(\alpha_{\ell})\,e^{in(\Lambda_{\ell,k}-\phi)}

(S36)

where
\alpha_{\ell}\equiv re^{i\Phi_{\ell}}
.
Using the identity

\sum_{p=1}^{M}\left(e^{in\frac{2\pi}{M}}\right)^{p}=\begin{cases}M&\text{if }n=qM\quad\text{with }q\in\mathbb{Z}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}\,,

(S37)

we find that the sum over
k
becomes

\sum_{k=1}^{M}e^{in\Lambda_{\ell,k}}=e^{in\ell\pi/M}\sum_{k=1}^{M}e^{ink2\pi/M}=e^{in\ell\pi/M}M\delta_{n,qM}\,.

(S38)

Hence, the double sum can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,e^{re^{i\Phi_{\ell}}\cos(\Lambda_{\ell,k} - \phi)}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(\alpha_{\ell})\,e^{in(\Lambda_{\ell,k}-\phi)}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(\alpha_{\ell})\,e^{-in\phi}e^{in\ell\pi/M}M\delta_{n,qM}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(\alpha_{\ell})\,e^{-iqM\phi}e^{iq\ell\pi}M=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\Phi_{\ell}}}\,\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(\alpha_{\ell})\,e^{-iqM\left(\phi-\frac{\pi}{M}\ell\right)}M.

(S39)

An analogue calculation applies also to the diagonal contribution

\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{r\cos(\phi- \theta_j)}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(r)\,e^{in(\phi-\theta_{j})}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(r)\,e^{in\phi}\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{in\frac{2\pi j}{M}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{n}(r)\,e^{in\phi}M\delta_{n,qM}=\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(r)\,e^{iqM\phi}M.

(S40)

Putting all the calculations together, the Wigner function becomes:

W(R,\phi)=(1-\Delta)\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-2d^{2}}}{\pi N}\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(r)\,e^{iqM\phi}+\Delta\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell}}\,\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(re^{i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell})\,e^{-iqM\left(\phi-\frac{\pi}{M}\ell\right)}.

(S41)

In both diagonal and off-diagonal contributions, the angular dependence appears through terms of the form

\sum_{q=-\infty}^{+\infty}I_{qM}(x)\,e^{iqMy}\,,

(S42)

which reflects the underlying rotational symmetry of the state.

We now identify the dominant contribution to the sum over modified Bessel functions. As known, in the limit
x\rightarrow 0
the only surviving contribution is the zero Bessel function
I_{0}(x)
. More precisely, for
0<\absolutevalue{\alpha_{\ell}}\ll\sqrt{qM+1}
, we have

I_{qM}(\alpha_{\ell})\sim\frac{1}{\Gamma(qM+1)}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\ell}}{2}\right)^{qM}=\frac{1}{(qM)!}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\ell}}{2}\right)^{qM}.

(S43)

Using the Stirling’s approximation
n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}
,
I_{qM}(\alpha_{\ell})
simplifies to

I_{qM}(\alpha_{\ell})\sim\left(\frac{\alpha_{\ell}}{2}\right)^{qM}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi qM}}\left(\frac{e}{qM}\right)^{qM}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi qM}}\left(\frac{e\,\alpha_{\ell}}{2qM}\right)^{qM}\underset{qM\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,.

(S44)

Such a limit is valid when
M>|\alpha_{l}|=r
, which gives us a condition on the radial coordinate
R<M/(2\sqrt{2}d)
.

Therefore, all terms with
q\neq 0
are exponentially suppressed, and the leading order approximation consists in retaining only the
q=0
contribution:

W(R,\phi)=(1-\Delta)\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-2d^{2}}}{\pi N}I_{0}(r)+\Delta\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell}}\,I_{0}(re^{i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell})\,.

(S45)

A further approximation can be performed on the sum over
\ell
. The larger contribution arises when the exponential is maximal, i.e., when

e^{2i\pi/M\ell}=-1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\ell=\frac{M}{2}\quad(\text{for even }M).

(S46)

We thus make the substitution
\ell=M/2+x
, which entails:

S\equiv\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}e^{-d^{2}e^{2i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell}}\,I_{0}\left(re^{i\frac{\pi}{M}\ell}\right)=\sum_{x=-M/2}^{M/2-1}e^{d^{2}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}x}}\,I_{0}\left(i\,r\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{M}x}\right).

(S47)

Since
\left|{\rm exp}(d^{2}e^{i\Theta x})\right|=\left|{\rm exp}(d^{2}\cos(\Theta x))\,{\rm exp}(id^{2}\sin(\Theta x))\right|\leq e^{d^{2}}
, the largest contribution is tightly localized around
x=0
and we can approximate the slowly varying modified Bessel function by its value at the maximum reached for
x=0
. In fact, from the Taylor expansion around
x=0
, we have:

I_{0}(i\,r\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{M}x})\approx I_{0}(i\,r)+i\frac{\pi rx}{M}I_{1}(i\,r)=J_{0}(r)+i\frac{\pi rx}{M}J_{1}(r)\,,

(S48)

where we have used the properties of the Bessel function
I_{0}(ir)=J_{0}(r)
and
I_{1}(ir)=J_{1}(r)
valid for
r\in\mathbb{R}
. To neglect the contribution linear in
x
, we need that

\left|\frac{\pi rx}{M}\right|\ll\left|\frac{J_{0}(r)}{J_{1}(r)}\right|<1\,,

(S49)

which is valid for
M\gg d
.

Hence, we can factorize the dominant contribution
J_{0}(r)
out of the summation in Eq. (
S47
) such that

S\approx J_{0}(r)\sum_{x=-M/2}^{M/2-1}e^{d^{2}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}x}}.

(S50)

The exponential inside the summation can be expanded as a power series, and we get:

S\approx J_{0}(r)\sum_{x=-M/2}^{M/2-1}\sum_{n}\frac{\left(d^{2}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}x}\right)^{n}}{n!}=J_{0}(r)\sum_{n}\frac{d^{2n}}{n!}\sum_{x=-M/2}^{M/2-1}\left(e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}n}\right)^{x}=J_{0}(r)\sum_{n}\frac{d^{2n}}{n!}M\delta_{n,qM}=J_{0}(r)M\sum_{q}\frac{d^{2qM}}{(qM)!}\,,

(S51)

where we have used the identity (
S37
). The summation
\sum_{q}\frac{d^{2qM}}{(qM)!}
evaluated in
q=0
gives exactly
1
, while the term with
q=1
is equal to
d^{2M}/M!
. The latter is thus suppressed in the highly-packed limit
M\gg d^{2}
. Terms with
q
larger than
1
are even less relevant in the highly-packed limit.

Therefore,
S\approx J_{0}(r)M
and the Wigner function simplifies to:

W(R,\phi)=W(R)=(1-\Delta)\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-2d^{2}}}{\pi N}I_{0}(r)+\Delta\,\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi N}MJ_{0}(r)=\frac{e^{-R^{2}-d^{2}}}{\pi N}\left[(1-\Delta)\,e^{-d^{2}}I_{0}(r)+\Delta\,M\,J_{0}(r)\right].

(S52)

Solving
(1-\Delta)\,e^{-d^{2}}I_{0}(r)+\Delta\,M\,J_{0}(r)=0
for
\Delta
and minimizing over
r
provides us the upper bound
\overline{\Delta}_{c}
of
\Delta_{c}
for
d\geq 1
:

\overline{\Delta}_{c}=\min_{r}\frac{1}{1-e^{d^{2}}M\frac{J_{0}(r)}{I_{0}(r)}}=\frac{1}{1-e^{d^{2}}M\frac{J_{0}(\pi)}{I_{0}(\pi)}}=\frac{1}{1+0.056\,e^{d^{2}}M},

(S53)

given that stationary points (i.e., where the first derivative vanishes) of the ratio
\frac{J_{0}(r)}{I_{0}(r)}
occur at
r=0
,
r=\pm 3.19622
,
r=\pm 6.30644
,
r=\pm 9.43950\dots
. As these values are well approximated by
r\cong n\pi
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the minimum of
\frac{J_{0}(r)}{I_{0}(r)}
is attained near
r=\pi
, for which
J_{0}(\pi)/I_{0}(\pi)\cong-0.056
.

In conclusion, if
\Delta\geq\overline{\Delta}_{c}
, then the Wigner function is surely negative.
