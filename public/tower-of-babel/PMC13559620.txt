Integrative analysis of single-cell sequencing identifies CD8+ TIM3+ CD101+ T cell-associated genes as prognostic biomarkers in breast cancer
Authors: Xuran Zhang, Runfang Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer is a prevalent and deadly malignancy that significantly impacts women’s quality of life and imposes financial burdens. Despite therapeutic advancements, tumour heterogeneity and frequent relapses remain major challenges. Accordingly, this study aimed to characterize immune features associated with CD8+ TIM3+ CD101+ T cells and develop a prognostic signature for breast cancer.MethodsThis study integrated single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets to characterize CD8+ TIM3+ CD101+ T cell (CCT)-related immune features and construct a prognostic signature in breast cancer. Single-cell RNA-seq data were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository, and bulk transcriptomic data were from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and GEO databases. Analytical methods included pseudo-time trajectory reconstruction (Monocle2), intercellular signalling analysis (CellChat), functional enrichment (ClusterProfiler), and immune profiling (ssGSEA). Prognostic modeling was conducted using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression, with validation via Kaplan-Meier and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses.ResultsSingle-cell analysis identified 17 clusters spanning seven cell types, including T cells, myeloid cells, and epithelial cells. T-cell sub-clustering revealed four subtypes. Pseudotime analysis suggested a potential state-transition relationship between CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ and CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T-cell states. A total of 121 differentially expressed genes were enriched in vital biological processes. An 11-gene prognostic model showed strong predictive power across cohorts. Single-cell T-cell reclustering identified a CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T-cell subpopulation, which was primarily characterized by the expression of markers such as CD101 and HAVCR2/TIM3.ConclusionsThis study maps cellular heterogeneity and molecular networks in breast cancer, offering insights for targeted therapy and improved prognosis.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most prevalent malignancy among women and is the primary cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. This neoplastic disease has a significant epidemiological prevalence and a substantial oncological mortality burden across international healthcare systems (1). Recent epidemiological data indicate that approximately 2.3 million new cases and 670,000 deaths occurred in 2022 (2). Although modern medicine has made significant progress in screening technologies and therapeutic interventions, patient survival rates demonstrate considerable variability across demographics, underscoring the persistent complexity of managing this disease (3). Currently, several treatments, including surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation therapy, have improved survival rates (4); however, substantial gaps remain in the ability to predict patient responses and manage therapy-induced resistance. Furthermore, existing treatments often fail to address the heterogeneity of breast cancer, necessitating personalised and precise approaches. Consequently, identifying innovative biomarkers and developing targeted therapeutic strategies have become imperative for advancing diagnostic precision, prognostic assessment, and clinical management of invasive breast carcinoma (BRCA). This critical gap in the current oncological practice underscores the necessity for molecular discoveries that could transform patient stratification and personalised intervention approaches.

The CD8+ TIM3+ CD101+ T cell (CCT) lymphocyte population constitutes a unique subpopulation of T lymphocytes identified by the simultaneous co-expression of three membrane markers, CD8, CD101, and TIM3, which display critical functional roles within the tumour microenvironment (TME). CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cells may represent a phenotype associated with T-cell dysfunction or exhaustion under persistent antigen stimulation; however, the co-expression of CD101 and TIM3 alone cannot directly establish that this population is in a terminally differentiated state. Investigations in tumour immunotherapeutic research highlight the immunological relevance of CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T lymphocytes, offering essential perspectives for evaluating T-cell functional dynamics within malignant lesions and predicting clinical responses to immune-modulating therapies. Experimental evidence has revealed that PD-1+ progenitor CD8+ T lymphocytes when exposed to persistent pathogenic challenges progressively differentiate into transitional effector phenotypes. Such differentiated populations display characteristic gene expression patterns linked to pathogen containment mechanisms, particularly those marked by upregulated CX3CR1 chemotactic receptors, enhanced secretion of inflammatory cytokines, and elevated granzyme B production. The combined blockade of the PD-1/TIM-3 signalling axes has proven effective in reversing T cell anergy states, while potentiating antigen-specific immune activation during dendritic cell-tumour vaccine co-administration. These molecular insights underscore the clinical significance of CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cell profiling, which advances both conceptual models and therapeutic strategies in cancer immune modulation. The experimental findings have thereby revealed critical pathways that bridge fundamental immunology and translational oncology, and provide actionable targets for the development of precision immunotherapy.

The emergence of single-cell transcriptomic profiling (scRNA-seq) platforms coupled with advanced computational analytics has created transformative potential for decoding the molecular signatures of various immune cell subtypes within the neoplastic microenvironment (TME) (5). Emerging research has indicated that molecular profiling of immune populations through single-cell transcriptomic analysis has significant potential as a predictive methodology for predicting clinical outcomes and immunotherapy efficacy in gynaecological malignancies (6,7). These findings highlight the potential for identifying novel therapeutic targets.

Thus, through systematic multi-omics profiling, in this study, we mapped TME dynamics at single-cell resolution, uncovering critical cellular interaction networks, core genomic drivers, and regulatory pathways underlying mammary carcinoma progression. By synthesising multidimensional omics datasets using computational biology approaches, we achieved a comprehensive characterisation of the breast cancer ecosystem architecture, yielding actionable biological insights for novel therapeutic development. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0674/rc).

Methods

Acquisition and computational preprocessing of single-cell transcriptomic datasets

The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository hosts several single-cell genomic datasets. For this investigation, we acquired scRNA-seq profiles of treatment-naïve BRCAs from the GEO accession GSE248288, comprising samples from four primary tumour specimens. Raw sequencing files were initially processed using Seurat’s computational framework (version 4.2.0) in the R statistical environment (8). The initial quality control used the following filtering parameters: (I) exclusion of cells expressing <200 genes; (II) removal of genes detected in <1 cell; (III) retention of cells containing 300–5,000 expressed genes; (IV) preservation of cells with UMI counts (log10GenesPerUMI) <30,000; and (V) selection of cells exhibiting mitochondrial gene content <30%. After normalisation, we employed a variance-stabilising transformation to identify genes with variable expression patterns using mean-dispersion equilibrium analysis. Dimensionality reduction was conducted using principal component analysis, and statistically significant principal components were used for graph-theoretic clustering. Batch corrections across datasets were implemented using harmony integration. Cellular clustering analysis was conducted using the FindClusters function (resolution =0.5), employing shared nearest-neighbour modularity optimisation across 30 principal components, yielding 17 discrete clusters. Dimensional reduction was performed using the RunUMAP algorithm (n_neighbours =30) to project cell distributions onto two-dimensional embeddings (UMAP-1 versus UMAP-2). Cluster-defining differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using Seurat’s FindAllMarkers function in normalised expression matrices (logfc threshold =0.25). Subsequent cell-type annotation leveraged the established marker gene signatures, followed by proportional quantification of subpopulation distributions within biological specimens.

Bulk transcriptome data acquisition and preprocessing

This study employed open-access genomic resources obtained from established biomedical repositories, including The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The analytical framework incorporated these comprehensive molecular datasets to ensure a robust investigation of oncogenic mechanisms through multi-platform genomic evidence integration. Transcriptomic datasets (transcripts per million-normalised) (TPM-normalised), along with clinicopathological annotations and somatic mutation catalogues for BRCA, were acquired through bioinformatics pipelines employing TCGA biolinks (version 2.25.0) (8) in R, utilising VarScan2 variant detection protocols. Complementary GEO repository data integration was conducted using the GEO query package to enable efficient retrieval of expression profiles and matched clinical documentation. Our integrated cohort incorporated 1,231 TCGA-BRCA samples (113 demographically matched normal tissues and 1,118 tumour specimens) with extended clinicogenomic metadata available for 1,061 malignancies. This core dataset was augmented with 327 clinically characterised samples from the GSE20685 cohort. All analytical procedures adhered rigorously to the data access regulations and ethical governance frameworks mandated by the source repositories.

Constructing single-cell trajectories in pseudo-time

Cellular differentiation trajectories were reconstructed using Monocle 2 (9), an algorithmic framework that combines graph-based manifold learning with predefined transcriptomic signatures to generate pseudo-time projections that delineate linear and branching differentiation pathways. The preliminary processing phase comprised expression normalisation using size factor estimation to mitigate batch effects. Gene expression features exhibiting significant variability across biological replicates (selection thresholds: normalised dispersion >1.5-fold baseline variation coupled with mean expression ≥0.1 TPM) were selected as model inputs (10). Dimension reduction employed the DDRTree algorithm under standardised parameter configurations. Branching dynamics were characterised using Monocle 2 branching analysis of the expression pattern module, which is a computational module designed to detect genes displaying branch-dependent expression patterns (9). Lineage-associated transcriptional signatures were mapped using hierarchical clustering visualisation tools.

Cell communications analysis and ligand-receptor expression

Intercellular communication profiling involves the systematic evaluation of ligand-receptor coexpression patterns across cellular subpopulations to delineate context-dependent signalling networks (11). To systematically characterise the bidirectional signalling activity across cell types, we implemented the CellChat analytical framework. This computational framework enables quantitative evaluation of interaction topology, measurement of pathway-specific signalling flux, and probabilistic modelling of cellular crosstalk (12). Our methodology applied CellChat (version 1.1.3) to single-cell transcriptomic profiles of breast cancer specimens processed using the Seurat toolkit. A focused investigation of CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cell interactions quantified pathway-specific communication strength by utilising the platform’s built-in metrics with a specialised analysis of immune checkpoint modulation. Key signalling pathways were visualised in detail to resolve spatial interaction dynamics. All computational workflows adhered to the default parameters with statistical significance thresholds (P<0.05), incorporating the Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple hypothesis testing.

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses

Functional enrichment analysis was performed to characterise biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), and cellular components (CC) through GO (13) annotations. Pathway perturbation analysis was performed using the KEGG (14) as a bioinformatics framework to identify the dysregulated metabolic and signalling cascades. For breast cancer-associated DEGs (P<0.05), ontological and pathway enrichment evaluations were implemented using the “clusterProfiler” R package (version 4.2.2) (15), enabling systematic integration of both GO term classification and KEGG pathway mapping.

Development and validation of prognostic models

The prognostic evaluation of CCT-associated DEGs in breast cancer was performed using univariate Cox proportional hazards regression to quantify the association between transcriptional features and overall survival (OS) within the tumour cohort. Genes that met the significance threshold (P<0.05) were designated as survival-associated candidates for downstream modelling. Thereafter, stratified randomisation into training (n=730) and validation (n=331) cohorts was performed for the clinically annotated tumour samples using 7:3 partitioning. Feature selection and prognostic signature construction leveraged least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-regularised Cox regression modelling using the “glmnet” R package with iterative variable shrinkage to optimise model generalisability. Risk stratification of the training cohort specimens was performed through optimal threshold determination using the surv_cutpoint function, categorising patients into low- and high-risk subgroups. Prognostic stratification efficacy was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curve generation, and intergroup differences were statistically validated using log-rank testing. Model discriminatory performance was quantified using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, where area under the curve (AUC) values (range, 0.5–1.0) quantified classification efficacy, with superior performance indicated by elevated AUC metrics. For external validation, a training-derived risk stratification threshold was applied to independent validation cohort specimens, enabling consistent partitioning into low- and high-risk prognostic categories. The candidate genes used for prognostic modeling in this study were initially identified at the single-cell level. Specifically, they were cell-subpopulation-associated differentially expressed genes obtained by comparing CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cells with other cell types or subpopulations, rather than genes directly derived from differential expression analysis of whole-tissue bulk transcriptomic data.

Construction and validation of the nomogram

Demographic and clinicopathological parameters (age, sex, and tumour stage) were acquired from the TCGA database. The prognostic significance of the model-derived risk scores was evaluated using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses incorporating clinical covariates. A predictive nomogram integrating risk scores with clinical variables was developed using the “RMS” R package to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities. Time-dependent ROC analysis was performed to quantify the discriminatory performance of the nomogram across survival intervals.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA (16) is an analytical framework that evaluates the coordinated differential expression patterns of predefined gene collections across biological states. Comparative transcriptomic profiling the high- and low-risk groups was analyzed using the “limma” R package (version 3.50.0) (17), quantifying expression magnitude differences through log2 fold change (FC) calculations. Rank-ordered gene lists (sorted by log2FC values) underwent GSEA processing using “clusterProfiler” (version 4.2.2), employing 1,000 phenotype permutations to establish statistical significance thresholds. Reference pathways were curated from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (18,19), focusing on the “c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1. symbol” annotation subsets. Enrichment significance was defined as a false discovery rate (FDR)-rate-corrected P value threshold of <0.05.

Gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

Biological pathway heterogeneity between the control and breast cancer cohorts was investigated through GSVA, implemented using the “c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1. symbols” KEGG pathway annotations, conducted through the “GSVA” R package (version 1.42.0). Analytical outcomes underwent heatmap visualisation employing the “pheatmap” graphical toolkit (version 1.0.12) to resolve functional enrichment patterns across comparative groups”.

Immune infiltration analysis [single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)]

ssGSEA (20) extends the conventional GSEA methodology through sample-wise enrichment scoring by assigning pathway activation metrics to individual specimens rather than to grouped cohorts. This single-sample adaptation method calculates enrichment metrics representing coordinated transcriptional modulation (upregulation or suppression) within annotated gene sets for each biological sample. Unlike conventional GSEA group-level pathway enrichment assessments (control versus disease comparisons across predefined pathways), ssGSEA generates pairwise enrichment indices that link discrete samples to specific functional gene modules.

Using the Tumor and Immune System Interactions Database (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/index.php) (21), we systematically profiled 28 immune cell subpopulations (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-1.xlsx), including CD8+ T cell subsets (activated, central memory, and effector memory), CD4+ T cell subtypes (activated, central memory, effector memory, follicular helper, gamma delta, Th1, Th17, Th2, and regulatory), B cell lineages (activated, immature, and memory), innate immune components [natural killer cells (CD56bright/dim), natural killer T (NKT) cells, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs)], dendritic cell variants (activated, plasmacytoid, and immature), and myeloid populations (macrophages, eosinophils, mast cells, monocytes, and neutrophils). Immune infiltration was quantified by deconvolution of breast cancer transcriptomic data to derive cell-type-specific enrichment scores. Differential infiltration patterns between risk-stratified cohorts were graphically represented employing the “ggplot2” visualisation toolkit (version 3.3.6), contrasting immune microenvironment landscapes across prognostic groups” (22).

Assessment of drug susceptibility

Therapeutic drug response prediction was based on half-maximal inhibitory concentration metrics from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/) (23), combined with transcriptomic profiles. Computational pharmacogenomic analysis was performed using the “oncoPredict” R package (version 0.2) (24), enabling systematic evaluation of drug response profiles for clinically relevant agents across prognostically stratified cohorts with breast cancer. This in silico framework quantifies therapeutic vulnerabilities by correlating molecular signatures with pharmacologically stratified risk subgroups.

Somatic mutation analysis

Genomic aberrations were characterised by integrative analysis of somatic mutations and copy number variation profiles. The “maftools” computational framework (25) enabled the systematic annotation of molecular variants across molecular subtypes, including single-nucleotide polymorphisms, insertion-deletion mutations, tumour mutational burden (TMB), and variant allele frequencies. Consistent with the oncogenic selection patterns, recurrently altered genes (defined as the top 20 genes by mutational prevalence) have been designated as putative driver genes underlying tumourigenesis (26).

Immunotherapy responses

The tumour immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE, http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) algorithm was used to evaluate the likelihood of an immunotherapy response (27).

Expression of immune checkpoint genes

Immunoregulatory checkpoints are molecular complexes expressed on immune cells that modulate activation thresholds through homeostatic signalling. These mechanisms serve as critical safeguards against the pathological overactivation of immune responses. Comparative transcriptional profiling of clinically validated immune checkpoint genes was performed across stratified cohorts to quantify the differential regulatory pathway activity.

Statistical analysis

Computational analyses were performed in R (version 4.1.2), leveraging the survival analytics toolkit (“survival” and “survminer” packages) for K-M curve generation. Bivariate associations were quantified using non-parametric Spearman’s rank-order correlations within the statistical framework. Group-wise comparisons employed a tiered approach: Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for dual cohorts and Kruskal-Wallis H-tests for multi-group contrasts. Significance thresholds adhered to a two-tailed α-level of 0.05, with P values below this cutoff indicating statistically significant findings.

Pseudo-time trajectories analysis

By analysing the identified CCT-related cell populations, we constructed a pseudo-temporal trajectory to uncover trajectory-associated gene expression patterns linked to breast cancer progression. Transcriptional states along this trajectory suggested dynamic transcriptional state changes. Monocle2 pseudotime analysis revealed distinct distributions of CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cells and CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cells along the trajectory, suggesting a potential state transition relationship between them (Figure 4A-4C). However, it was unable to confirm that CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cells represent a definite terminal differentiation endpoint. Genes highly expressed in the pre-branch phase were primarily associated with pathways, including ‘T cell receptor complex’, ‘MHC protein complex binding’, ‘MHC class II protein complex binding’, ‘response to unfolded protein’, and ‘heat shock protein binding’, as identified through GO BP analysis. In contrast, genes enriched in pathways such as ‘antigen processing and presentation’, ‘positive regulation of cytokine production’, ‘antigen processing and presentation of endogenous peptide antigen’, and ‘positive regulation of leukocyte-mediated cytotoxicity’ were highly expressed in Branch 2. Genes related to ‘cytokine binding’ were overexpressed in Branch 1 (Figure 4D and table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-4.xlsx).

Spatial interaction patterns (Figure 5A,5B) revealed distinct cellular crosstalk hierarchies, with CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cells predominantly functioning as signalling recipients between cohorts. Among them, the MIF-related ligand-receptor axes mainly included MIF–(CD74+CXCR4), MIF–(CD74+CD44), and MIF–(CD74+CXCR2) (Figure 5C-5E). MIF-related signaling mainly involved communication between Myeloid, Macrophage cells and CD8+ T-cell subpopulations, particularly interactions directed toward CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cells (Figure 5F). Enrichment analysis of DEGs associated with CCT cells in breast cancer

A total of 121 CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cell subpopulation-associated DEGs were obtained. These genes were identified from single-cell data by comparing CD8+CD101+TIM3+ T cells with other cell types or subpopulations (P<0.05, |Log2FC| >0.5; Figure S1A and table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-5.xlsx).

To investigate the biological functions associated with key genes, enrichment analyses of GO entries (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-6.xlsx) and KEGG pathways (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-7.xlsx) were performed. GO analysis indicated that these genes were associated with ‘response to a virus’, ‘T cell activation’, ‘defence response to virus’ (BP), and other biological processes. ‘Focal adhesion’, ‘cytosolic ribosome’, ‘cell-substrate junction’ (CC); ‘proton-transporting ATP synthase activity, rotational mechanism’, ‘proton channel activity’, ‘double-stranded RNA binding’ (MF); and other molecular functions were enriched (Figure S1B). The enriched KEGG pathways included ‘measles’, ‘coronavirus disease-COVID-19’, ‘T cell receptor signalling pathway’, ‘Epstein–Barr virus infection, natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and primary immunodeficiency’, ‘Th17 cell differentiation’, ‘PD-L1 expression and PD-1 checkpoint pathway in cancer’, and ‘Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation’ (Figure S1C).

Construction and verification of the prognostic risk model

Univariate Cox analysis was employed to identify the DEGs (P<0.05), ultimately revealing 30 genes linked to breast cancer prognosis (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-8.xlsx). Subsequently, the above single-cell-derived CCT subpopulation-associated DEGs were mapped to the TCGA/GEO bulk transcriptomic cohorts, and prognostic genes were screened using univariate Cox regression and LASSO Cox regression. From the BRCA samples (n=1,061), a random sampling approach allocated 7/10 of the data to the training set (n=730) and 3/10 to the validation set (n=331). LASSO regression analysis was performed on the training set to eliminate redundant genes with a random number seed set at 146. Eleven genes were associated with the prognosis of patients with BRCA (CXCL13, MTRNR2L12, CD7, RGS1, SH2D2A, ISG20, STAT1, SIRPG, ICOS, BATF, TFF3) (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-9.xlsx, Figure S2A,S2B). The robustness of the model based on these 11 gene features was assessed by stratifying the samples into high- and low-risk groups using the optimal cut-off value determined using the surv_cutpoint function. K-M survival curves were generated for both the training and validation cohorts. The results indicated that patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly poorer prognoses than those in the low-risk group across all cohorts (Figure S2C,S2D). The predictive accuracy of the model was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. In the training cohort, the AUC values for the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates were 0.642, 0.726, and 0.692, respectively (Figure S2E). In the validation cohort, the corresponding AUC were 0.631, 0.668, and 0.626, respectively (Figure S2F).

We also examined the impact of various clinical characteristics on the prognosis. The analysis revealed that age (age_high ≥65, age_low <65 years), tumour stage (stage_high: Stage III-Stage X; stage_low: Stage I-Stage II), nodal stage (Nstage_high: N2-NX; Nstage_low: N0-N1), and metastasis stage (Mstage_high: M1 and MX; Mstage_low: M0) significantly influenced prognosis (Figure S3A-S3D). Furthermore, we explored the effects of prognosis-related gene expression on patient outcomes. The results demonstrated that MTRNR2L12, CXCL13, BATF, RGS1, SH2D2A, TFF3, SIRPG, and STAT1 had notable impacts on prognosis (Figure S4A-S4H).

Construction and verification of nomogram

To evaluate the independent prognostic capacity of the risk score, we implemented univariate and multivariate Cox regression models that integrated key clinical parameters (age, sex, and tumour stage). The analytical outcomes revealed that the risk score retained prognostic independence across both regression methodologies (Figure S5A,S5B). A prognostic nomogram incorporating the risk score demonstrated significant predictive capacity for the clinical endpoints (Figure S5C). ROC analysis revealed robust discriminatory power, with AUC values of 0.838, 0.784, and 0.733 at the 1-, 3-, and 5-year intervals, respectively (Figure S5D). These metrics confirm the efficacy of the model in stratifying both near-term and extended survival probabilities.

Immune infiltration analysis

To delineate immunological heterogeneity across breast cancer risk cohorts, we performed ssGSEA-based quantification of 28 leukocyte subsets. High-risk patients displayed a significant depletion in cytolytic effector populations, including activated B lymphocytes, CD4+/CD8+ T cells, CD56dim NK cells, and MDSCs, consistent with an immunosuppressive phenotype (P<0.05; Figure S7A). Conversely, the high-risk group exhibited immunomodulatory expansion of γδ T cells, immature dendritic cells, NKT cells, and specialised T helper subsets (Th17/Th2/Tfh), indicative of a proinflammatory microenvironment (P<0.05; Figure S7A). Pan-leukocyte correlation analysis revealed a predominantly coordinated immune activity, although antagonistic relationships emerged between CD56dim NK cells and effector memory CD8+ T cells (Figure S7B).

Furthermore, we examined the relationship between risk scores and immune cells and identified significant correlations (P<0.001). The nine most strongly correlated pairs are shown in Figures S8A-S8I. The risk score demonstrated a significant negative correlation with activated CD4 T cells (R=–0.205, Figure S8A) and activated CD8 T cells (R=–0.2336; Figure S8B). The risk score demonstrated a strong positive correlation with γδ T lymphocytes (R=0.3509; Figure S8C) and a pronounced inverse association with immature B-lineage cells (R=–0.2218; Figure S8D). While immature dendritic populations displayed a marked positive correlation (R=0.156; Figure S8E), MDSCs exhibited a substantial negative correlation (R=–0.1928; Figure S8F). Coordinated pro-inflammatory interactions were further evidenced by robust positive associations with follicular helper T cells (R=0.2658; Figure S8G), NKT cell subsets (R=0.2432; Figure S8H), and plasmacytoid dendritic lineages (R=0.2479; Figure S8I).

TMB and drug susceptibility analysis

We analysed specific gene mutations in BRCA and identified the top 20 genes with the highest mutation frequencies, which were then visualised. TP53 exhibited the highest mutation frequency in both groups, followed by PIK3CA (Figures S9A,S9B). TMB, a key determinant of immunotherapy efficacy, was significantly higher in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group, owing to somatic mutations associated with breast cancer (P<0.05; Figure S9C). Additionally, we investigated whether the risk score could effectively predict chemotherapy sensitivity in patients with BRCA (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-12.xlsx). The results demonstrated that individuals with a low-risk score were more likely to exhibit positive responses to several chemotherapeutic agents, including telomerase inhibitor IX_1930 (Figure S9D), sabutoclax_1849 (Figure S9E), podophyllotoxin bromide_1825 (Figure S9F), MG-132_1862 (Figure S9G), epirubicin_1511 (Figure S9H), dactinomycin _1811 (Figure S9I), BMS-754807_2171 (Figure S9J), and AZD8055_1059 (Figure S9L). In contrast, individuals with a high-risk score were more likely to exhibit a positive response to BI-2536_1086 (Figure S9K). These findings suggest that chemotherapy holds significant promise for patients in low-risk groups.

Immune checkpoints and TIDE analysis

We analysed the expression levels of immune checkpoints in the high- and low-risk groups and identified significant differences in the expression of CD274, CD28, CTLA4, HAVCR2, LAG3, PDCD1, SIGLEC10, TIGIT and TNFRSF4 (Figure S10A). To assess the potential clinical efficacy of immunotherapy in both groups, we used the TIDE algorithm (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0674-13.xlsx). A higher TIDE prediction score suggests an increased likelihood of immune evasion, indicating that patients may benefit less from immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. Our findings revealed that TIDE and the exclusion and dysfunction scores were lower in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group (Figure S10B). Additionally, a greater proportion of immunotherapy responses were observed in the low-risk group (Figure S10C).

Discussion

Breast malignancy remains the leading oncological pathology among women worldwide and accounts for substantial morbidity and mortality in the global disease burden. Its pervasive clinical trajectory profoundly compromises quality of life parameters while imposing a significant socioeconomic burden, positioning it as a multidimensional public health challenge. Despite progress in treatment methods, the high variability and propensity for breast cancer recurrence pose significant obstacles to achieving a cure. Advancements in scRNA-seq technology have provided the opportunity to gain a comprehensive understanding of the molecular characteristics of tumour-infiltrating immune cells (28). Advancing cancer therapeutics requires two imperatives: elucidating the fundamental pathobiology of oncogenesis at the molecular level and pioneering precision-targeted intervention paradigms. This dual-axis approach forms the cornerstone of translational research, and addresses the current limitations of precision oncology.

Prior investigations have established the molecular signature of CCT as a negative prognostic biomarker for immune checkpoint inhibitor response (29), with mechanistic studies revealing its translational relevance. The sustained co-expression of TIM3 and CD101 has emerged as a defining molecular signature of CD8+ T cell dysfunctional exhaustion, providing novel insights into therapeutic resistance mechanisms (30,31). The CD101+TIM3+ phenotype in the TME may be associated with T cell exhaustion, dysfunction, and decreased immune treatment response. It may also overlap with the characteristics of tissue-resident memory-like T cells. Therefore, in this study, it is interpreted as a phenotype related to the functional state of T cells, rather than a single terminal differentiation endpoint (32,33). Although this study identified a CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T-cell subpopulation, CD101 and HAVCR2/TIM3 were not included in the final prognostic model. Therefore, this study does not interpret CD101 as a validated independent prognostic factor. Our pseudo-time trajectory analysis identified a temporal sequence in which CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T cells and CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cells occupied related transcriptional states, suggesting a potential state-transition relationship between these two T-cell subsets. However, Monocle2-based pseudo-time inference cannot independently confirm lineage directionality, nor does it establish CD8+CD101+TIM3+ T cells as a definite terminal differentiation endpoint. These findings therefore provide a hypothesis-generating framework for future studies to investigate the functional transition between CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ and CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T-cell states and to identify potential regulatory nodes that may influence immunotherapy responses.

Enrichment analyses were then performed to characterize the transcriptional programs associated with these T-cell states. Genes associated with the CD8+ CD101− TIM3+ T-cell state were enriched in cytokine binding, whereas genes associated with the CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T-cell state were enriched in antigen processing and presentation pathways. Sustained T-cell receptor signalling and persistent antigenic stimulation may promote the expression of immune checkpoint molecules and contribute to T-cell dysfunction in chronic infection and the TME. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies showing that persistent antigen exposure and inhibitory receptor signalling are closely linked to progressive T-cell functional impairment (34,35). Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted as evidence of a dysfunction-associated transcriptional phenotype rather than proof of a fixed terminal differentiation state. We also identified the pivotal role of the Macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF)−(CD74+CXCR4) and MIF−(CD74+CD44) ligand-receptor axes, which served as the main molecular pathways. MIF-related communication may contribute to the formation of an immunosuppressive TME and may be associated with CD8+T-cell dysfunction. MIF, IL2, GALECTIN, and IL4 were the primary incoming signals, whereas IL2, GALECTIN, and IL4 were the dominant outgoing signals. This interpretation is consistent with our findings that the high-risk group exhibited increased TIDE, Dysfunction, and Exclusion scores, as well as a negative correlation between the risk score and the infiltration level of activated CD8+ T cells. Since MIF, CD74, CXCR4, CD44, and CXCR2 were not included in the final prognostic model, this study does not interpret MIF signaling as a direct prognostic model gene or a validated prognostic factor. Instead, it is described only as a potential immunoregulatory signal suggested by the cell-cell communication analysis.

Emerging translational evidence has established that immunophenotypically defined CD8+CD101hiTIM3+ (CCT T) lymphocyte populations are strongly associated with suboptimal therapeutic outcomes after immune checkpoint blockade regimens for non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC). This cellular subset has potential as a biomarker for stratifying patients with diminished immunological responsiveness to contemporary immunotherapies (36). However, the presence of CD8+CD101hiTIM3+ cells in breast cancer has not yet been documented, highlighting the innovative and forward-looking nature of this study. CD8+CD101hiTIM3+ is a subtype of CD8+ T cells that has been associated with cell exhaustion and has demonstrated much lower frequencies in NSCLC responders to immunotherapy (37).

CXCL13 has emerged as a prominent component of the 11-gene signature, demonstrating dysregulated expression in BRCA histopathology along with elevated systemic concentrations in metastatic cohorts (38). These findings suggest that CXCL13 is a critical mediator of breast oncogenesis and is a dual-utility biomarker for therapeutic targeting and diagnostic stratification. This chemokine, also known as a B lymphocyte chemoattractant, critically regulates B cell trafficking and lymphoid tissue compartmentalisation. In mammary malignancies, CXCL13 drives the pathological remodelling of the TMEs through immune cell orchestration, directly correlating with disease progression and clinical trajectories. In addition to breast cancer, CXCL13 overexpression exhibits pan-cancer relevance with prognostic associations documented in pulmonary adenocarcinomas and head/neck epithelial tumours, where it potentiates immunocyte infiltration and augments checkpoint inhibitor responsiveness (39). Spatial transcriptomic analyses have consistently shown that CXCL13 localises to functionally impaired T cell niches, where it facilitates B lymphocyte recruitment to nodal structures and sustains germinal centre maturation (40). Functional studies have demonstrated CXCL13-mediated activation of tumourigenic signalling cascades in penile squamous carcinomas, revealing conserved oncogenic mechanisms across malignancies (41). The quantification of CXCL13 within tumour ecosystems has demonstrated its prognostic utility in predicting immunotherapy efficacy and survival outcomes. The ability of chemokines to mediate lymphocyte crosstalk establishes them as master regulators of breast cancer immunodynamics, rendering them a strategic focus for immunomodulatory interventions aimed at restoring antitumour competence.

STAT1 transcription factor paradoxically participates in the malignant progression of breast cancer subtypes (42). However, its expression is markedly attenuated in oestrogen receptor-positive (ERα+) tumour cells, implying that its downregulation is a critical determinant of oncogenesis (43). Our investigation identified an inverse relationship between STAT1 transcriptional activity and tumour-infiltrating immune cell indices. As a crucial transducer of interferon signalling cascades, STAT1 has been extensively investigated in oncology. Experimental evidence substantiates its canonical role as a tumour-suppressive mediator in malignant transformation (44-47), with pan-cancer loss-of-function phenotypes observed through multiple molecular mechanisms, including (I) proteasomal degradation pathways, (II) epigenetic silencing by promoter hypermethylation, (III) dysregulation of STAT1-inhibitory pathways, and (IV) STAT3-mediated cross-inhibition through constitutive activation (48-50). Murine models with STAT1 ablation exhibit increased vulnerability to carcinogen-induced tumourigenesis and spontaneous development of mammary adenocarcinomas/ovarian teratomas (51).

Conversely, pathological STAT1 hyperactivation is manifested in diverse malignancies, including mammary carcinomas, pleural mesotheliomas, head squamous neoplasms, and lymphoproliferative disorders (52-54). Although enhanced STAT1 transcriptional activity has been associated with favourable prognoses in most studies (55-58), paradoxical observations link elevated STAT1/pSTAT1 levels in neoplastic tissues to adverse clinical trajectories. Mechanistically, TNBC microenvironments exhibit distinctive enrichment of CXCL16+ myeloid populations coupled with amplified STAT1 regulon activity, which is a hallmark of PD-L1+ immature myeloid populations that may orchestrate immunosuppressive niches (59).

Comprehensive immune profiling using ssGSEA revealed significant disparities in leukocyte infiltration patterns between breast cancer risk cohorts, delineating a heterogeneous immunological terrain stratified by disease aggressiveness (60). High-risk patients exhibit a diminished prevalence of key effector populations, including activated B lymphocytes, CD4+/CD8+ T cells, CD56dim NK cells, and MDSCs, suggesting a systemic compromise in antitumour immunosurveillance capacity. Paradoxically, this group demonstrated elevated infiltration of γδ T cells, immature dendritic cells, and specialised T helper subsets, which may indicate tumour-permissive immunomodulatory networks.

Genomic profiling of BRCA cases revealed TP53 and PIK3CA as the predominant mutational hotspots, establishing their roles as pathogenic drivers of mammary oncogenesis. The elevated TMB observed in the high-risk cohorts correlated with amplified neoantigen presentation potential, suggesting enhanced immunotherapeutic vulnerability. Conversely, low-risk patients exhibited preferential sensitivity to conventional cytotoxic agents, including epirubicin, sabutoclax, and telomerase inhibitor IX_1930. This therapeutic dichotomy advocates risk-stratified treatment algorithms, immunotherapy prioritisation for hypermutated tumours, and chemotherapy optimisation for genomically stable malignancies.

Limitations

Although this comprehensive investigation provided valuable mechanistic insights, certain methodological constraints merit acknowledgement. The analytical framework primarily leveraged computational biology approaches and algorithmic modelling techniques, with the absence of experimental validation, representing a critical gap in confirming biological plausibility. This limitation underscores the need for orthogonal validation through benchtop assays to substantiate computational predictions and enable mechanistic elucidation of the observed phenomena. Second, although the sample size was considerable, it may still have failed to capture the complete heterogeneity of cancer, which could have influenced the generalisability of the results. Third, the absence of clinical validation indicates that predictive models and identified biomarkers require further evaluation in clinical settings to confirm their effectiveness. Fourth, although MIF-related communication was highlighted by the cell-cell communication analysis, MIF, CD74, CXCR4, and CD44 were not retained in the final prognostic signature. Therefore, this study does not interpret MIF signaling as a direct prognostic factor or as a component of the prognostic model, and its association with patient prognosis still requires further validation. Fifth, Monocle2’s pseudotime analysis infers cellular state continuity based on gene expression similarity and dimensionality reduction, but cannot independently confirm the true differentiation direction; further validation through RNA velocity, lineage tracing, or functional experiments remains necessary. Besides, due to the limited sample size of the single-cell dataset used in this study and the lack of complete survival outcome information matched to each single-cell sample, we were unable to directly validate the association between the proportion of CD8+ CD101+ TIM3+ T cell subpopulations and patient survival. Finally, integrating multiple datasets from various sources may lead to batch effects. However, efforts have been made to normalise and standardise the data, and these variations could affect the outcomes.

Conclusions

In this study, we performed an extensive examination of the molecular dynamics and cellular ecosystems through systematic characterisation of breast oncogenesis. By leveraging integrated single-cell transcriptomic datasets coupled with computational biological approaches, we characterised 17 unique cellular populations, mapped their functional contributions to malignant niches, and developed a predictive framework anchored to critical gene expression signatures. Our findings highlight the crucial involvement of discrete cellular subtypes and oncogenic signalling cascades, revealing novel perspectives on targetable therapeutic vulnerabilities.
