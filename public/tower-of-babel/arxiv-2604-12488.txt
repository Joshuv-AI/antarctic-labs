Depth of powers of the edge ideal of an increasing weighted path Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author. Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13B22, 13F20; Secondary 05C99, 05E40 Thanks: Keywords: Depth, edge-weighted graph, edge ideal
Jiaxin Li, Dancheng Lu, Thanh Vu, Guangjun Zhu ∗

Abstract
. We provide exact formulas for the depth of the quotient ring of powers of the edge ideal of an increasing weighted path.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. Suppose that
{\bf w}\colon E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is a weight function defined on the edges of
G
, and we denote the pair
(G,{\bf w})
by
G_{\bf w}
. We call
G_{\bf w}
an
edge-weighted graph
with
G
as its underlying graph.
For an edge-weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
with vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, its
edge ideal
was first introduced in
[
15
]
. This ideal is a monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, which is defined by

I(G_{\bf w})=\bigl((x_{i}x_{j})^{{\bf w}(x_{i}x_{j})}\mid x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G)\bigr).

If there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
such that
{\bf w}(e)\geq 2
, then
G_{\bf w}
is said to be
nontrivially weighted
, and
e
is referred to as a
nontrivially weighted edge
. Otherwise,
G_{\bf w}
is
trivially weighted
, and every edge of
G_{\bf w}
is a
trivially weighted edge
. In this trivial case,
I(G_{\bf w})
coincides with the classical edge ideal of the underlying graph
G
, an ideal that has been extensively studied in the literature
[
1
,
5
,
7
,
13
,
14
]
.

For a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
, Brodmann
[
2
]
showed that, for
t\gg 0
, the depth
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
becomes eventually constant. The smallest integer
t_{0}
such that

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})=\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t_{0}})\text{\ for all }t\geq t_{0}

is called the
index of depth stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\operatorname{dstab}(I)
.
Trung
[
17
]
determined the limit depth for arbitrary edge ideals of graphs and described the index of depth stability for certain classes of graphs. More recently, Lam, Trung, and Trung
[
11
]
provided a complete combinatorial characterization of the index of depth stability for edge ideals of arbitrary graphs.

It is also worth noting that, although the index of depth stability of edge ideals is now well understood, the exact values of
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
for intermediate powers of edge ideals have only recently been computed for a limited number of graph classes; see, for example,
[
1
,
7
,
14
]
.

Turning to edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs, far less is known regarding both the limit depth and the index of depth stability. One notable result in this direction was recently established by Hien, Li, Trung, and Zhu
[
9
]
for the case of increasing weighted trees.

The depth of powers of edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs has also been computed for some special classes of graphs; see, for example,
[
12
,
18
,
19
]
. Notably, the limit depth remains unknown even for edge ideals of arbitrary edge-weighted paths.

In this paper, we take a further step toward understanding the depth of powers of edge ideals of arbitrary edge-weighted paths by focusing on the case of increasing weighted paths. First, we introduce some notation for convenience. Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
denote an edge-weighted path with vertex set
V(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\}
and edge set

E(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})=\{e_{i}\mid e_{i}:=x_{i}x_{i+1}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}.

We say that
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
is an
increasing weighted path
if
{\bf w}(e_{i})\leq{\bf w}(e_{i+1})
for all
i
.

Given a weighted path
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
, let

\Delta=\{\,i\in[n-2]\mid{\bf w}(e_{i})={\bf w}(e_{i+1})\,\}.

The set
\Delta
can be decomposed into maximal blocks

\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{p},

where each
\delta_{i}
consists of consecutive indices and satisfies

\min(\delta_{i+1})-\max(\delta_{i})\geq 2.

A block
\delta
is said to be of type
j
(for
j=0,1,2
) if
|\delta|\equiv j\pmod{3}
. Adjacent blocks of type
1
and type
2
can be
glued
together when the distance between them is exactly
2
. Using this gluing operation,
\Delta
can be decomposed as

\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{q},

where each
\Delta_{i}
is either a block of type
0
or a maximal extended block formed by gluing together type
1
and type
2
blocks.

For
i=1,\ldots,q
, let
t_{i}
and
s_{i}
denote the numbers of type
1
and type
2
blocks in
\Delta_{i}
, respectively, and let
r_{i}\in\{0,1\}
be the residue of
t_{i}
modulo
2
. We define

a(\Delta)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}r_{i},\quad b(\Delta)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}s_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{t_{i}-r_{i}}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad c(\Delta)=\frac{|\Delta|-a(\Delta)-2b(\Delta)}{3}.

Let
k(\Delta)=a(\Delta)+b(\Delta)+c(\Delta)
. We then define the piecewise function

d(\Delta,t)=\begin{cases}k(\Delta)-t+2,&\text{if }1\leq t\leq a(\Delta),\\[6.0pt]
k(\Delta)+1-\left\lfloor\frac{t+a(\Delta)-1}{2}\right\rfloor,&\text{if }a(\Delta)+1\leq t\leq a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta),\\[8.0pt]
k(\Delta)+1-\left\lfloor\frac{t+2a(\Delta)+b(\Delta)-1}{3}\right\rfloor,&\text{if }a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+1\leq t\leq|\Delta|,\\[8.0pt]
1,&\text{if }t\geq|\Delta|+1.\end{cases}

The main result of this paper is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
be an increasing weighted path. Then, for any
t\geq 1
,

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})=d(\Delta,t).

Example 1.2
.

Let

I=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{2}x_{4}^{2},x_{4}^{2}x_{5}^{2},x_{5}^{2}x_{6}^{2},x_{6}^{3}x_{7}^{3},x_{7}^{3}x_{8}^{3},x_{8}^{3}x_{9}^{3},x_{9}^{4}x_{10}^{4},x_{10}^{4}x_{11}^{4},x_{11}^{5}x_{12}^{5}).

Then

\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\delta_{2}\cup\delta_{3}\cup\delta_{4},

where
\delta_{1}=\{1\},\delta_{2}=\{3,4\},\delta_{3}=\{6,7\},\delta_{4}=\{9\}
. Note that
\min(\delta_{i+1})-\max(\delta_{i})=2
for
i=1,2,3
. Thus,
\Delta
consists of one extended block containing two type 1 blocks and two type 2 blocks. Therefore,
a(\Delta)=0
,
b(\Delta)=3
, and
c(\Delta)=0
. Consequently, the depth sequence
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
for
t=1,\ldots,7
is

\{4,4,3,3,2,2,1\}.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
introduces the basic facts that are used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some basic facts that will be used throughout this paper. Detailed information can be found in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
.
For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the depth of
L
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{depth}(L)=\min\{i\mid H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(L)\neq 0\},

where
H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. For any subset
A
of
V(G)
,
G[A]
denotes the
induced subgraph
of
G
on the set
A
; that is, for any
u,v\in A
,
\{u,v\}\in E(G[A])
if and only if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, its
neighborhood
and
closed neighborhood
are defined by
N_{G}(v):=\{u\in V(G)\mid\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
and
N_{G}[v]:=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
, respectively. By abuse of notation, we also write
uv
for the edge
\{u,v\}
of
G
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Any concept valid for
G
extends
naturally to
G_{\mathbf{w}}
. For example, for a subset
A\subseteq V(G)
,
the
induced subgraph

G_{\mathbf{w}}[A]
is the graph with underlying
graph
G[A]
, where the weight of each edge in
G_{\mathbf{w}}[A]
is
identical to its weight in
G_{\mathbf{w}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{m+1},\dots,x_{n}]
be two polynomial rings over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
S=S_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}S_{2}
and
I\subset S_{1}
,

J\subset S_{2}
be two nonzero homogeneous ideals. Then

\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I+J)\bigr)=\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/J).

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
)

Let
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0
be an exact
sequence of finitely generated graded
S
-modules. Then

(1)

\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(N),\operatorname{depth}(P)+1\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(N)\neq\operatorname{depth}(P)
;

(2)

\operatorname{depth}(N)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(M),\operatorname{depth}(P)\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(P)\neq\operatorname{depth}(M)-1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
16
, Corollary 1.3]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
f\notin I
be a monomial. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\leq\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I:f)\bigr).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal and
f
be a nonzero homogeneous form of
S
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f)),\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,f))\}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/(I:f)\rightarrow S/I\rightarrow S/(I,f)\rightarrow 0,

we obtain the conclusion.

\square

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I,J
be nonzero homogeneous ideals of
S
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I\cap J))\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/I),\operatorname{depth}(S/J),\operatorname{depth}(S/(I+J))+1\}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/(I\cap J)\rightarrow S/I\oplus S/J\rightarrow S/(I+J)\rightarrow 0,

we obtain the conclusion.

\square

Recall that a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
is said to be
nontrivially weighted
if there is at least one edge with weight greater than
1
; otherwise, it is said to be
trivially weighted
. An edge
e\in E(G_{\bf w})
is
nontrivially weighted
if
{\bf w}(e)\geq 2
; otherwise, we say that
e
has
trivial weight
.

In this paper, we extend a lemma from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G_{\bf w}
be an edge-weighted graph, and let
e=x_{n-1}x_{n}
be a leaf edge of
G
, that is,
x_{n}
has a unique neighbor, namely
x_{n-1}
. If
{\bf w}(e)\leq{\bf w}(x_{n-1}x_{j})
for all
x_{j}\in N_{G}(x_{n-1})
, then for all
t\geq 2
, we have

\bigl(I(G_{\bf w})^{t}:(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\bf w}(e)}\bigr)=I(G_{\bf w})^{t-1}.

Proof.

Clearly, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side. It therefore suffices to prove that if
f
is a minimal generator of
I(G_{\bf w})^{t}
, then

g=f/\gcd(f,(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\bf w}(e)})\in I(G_{\bf w})^{t-1}.

To this end, we can write
f=f_{1}\cdots f_{t}
, where each
f_{i}
is a minimal generator of
I(G_{\bf w})
. If
x_{n}
divides
f_{j}
for some
j
, then
f_{j}=(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\bf w}(e)}
since
x_{n}
is a leaf of
G
. Hence,

g=f/\gcd(f,(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\bf w}(e)})=f_{1}\cdots f_{j-1}f_{j+1}\cdots f_{t}\in I(G_{\bf w})^{t-1}.

Now assume that
x_{n}
does not divide
f_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq t
. Since
{\bf w}(e)\leq{\bf w}(x_{n-1}x_{j})
for all
x_{j}\in N_{G}(x_{n-1})
,
\gcd(f,(x_{n-1}x_{n})^{{\bf w}(e)})
is either
1
or
x_{n-1}^{{\bf w}(e)}
. In the first case,
g=f\in I(G_{\bf w})^{t}\subseteq I(G_{\bf w})^{t-1}
(as
t\geq 2
). In the second case, there exists some
j
such that
f_{j}
is divisible by
x_{n-1}
; hence,

g\text{ is divisible by }f_{1}\cdots f_{j-1}f_{j+1}\cdots f_{t}\in I(G_{\bf w})^{t-1}.

The conclusion follows.

\square

We use the following notation throughout the remainder of this paper. Let
m
and
n
be integers with
m\leq n
. By convention, the notation
[m,n]
denotes the set
\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
. In particular, if
m=n
, then
[m,n]=\{m\}
, and
[n]
is defined as
[1,n]
. Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
be a nontrivially weighted path, and let
i
and
j
be integers in
[n+1]
. If
i\leq j
, then
P_{\bf w}^{[i,j]}
denotes the induced subpath of
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
on the vertex set
\{x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{j}\}
; otherwise,
P_{\bf w}^{[i,j]}
is defined to be the empty graph.
For a subset
A\subseteq[n]
, we define

\max(A)=\max\{a\mid a\in A\}\quad\text{and}\quad\min(A)=\min\{a\mid a\in A\}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
T\subseteq[n]
be a nonempty subset. For integers
p,q
with
1\leq p\leq q\leq n
, the interval
[p,q]
is called a
maximal block
of
T
if the following three conditions hold:

(1)

[p,q]\subseteq T
,

(2)

p-1\notin T
,

(3)

q+1\notin T
.

By the above definition,
T
can be uniquely expressed as a disjoint union of maximal blocks:

T=\delta_{1}\sqcup\delta_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\delta_{s}.

This decomposition is referred to as the
maximal block decomposition
of
T
.

Definition 2.8
.

The type of a block
\delta=[p,q]
is defined as the residue of
|\delta|
modulo
3
, which takes values in
\{0,1,2\}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\delta_{1}=[p_{1},q_{1}]
and
\delta_{2}=[p_{2},q_{2}]
be two blocks with
q_{1}<p_{2}
. We say that
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are
gluable
if
|\delta_{1}|\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,
|\delta_{2}|\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, and
p_{2}-q_{1}=2
. Let
T\subseteq[n]
be a nonempty subset with maximal block decomposition
T=\delta_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\delta_{s}
. A union
M=\delta_{j}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{k}
is called a
maximal extended block
of
T
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\delta_{\ell}
is of type
1
or type
2
for all
\ell=j,\ldots,k
,

(2)

\delta_{\ell}
and
\delta_{\ell+1}
are gluable for all
\ell=j,\ldots,k-1
,

(3)

either
j=1
or
\delta_{j-1}
is not gluable to
\delta_{j}
,

(4)

either
k=s
or
\delta_{k}
is not gluable to
\delta_{k+1}
.

.

Let
\Delta\subseteq[n]
be a subset of indices, and suppose
\Delta
admits the decomposition

\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{q},

where each
\Delta_{i}
is either a block of type

For

, let
t_{i}

and type

r_{i}\in\{0,1\}

modulo

. We define

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq[n]
be a nonempty set of indices and let
\Gamma_{1}=\Delta\setminus\{\min(\Delta)\}
. Then for all
t\geq 1
,

d(\Delta,t)\leq\min\{d(\Gamma_{1},t-1),\;d(\Gamma_{1},t)+1\}.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{p}
be the decomposition of
\Delta
into maximal blocks, where
\delta_{1}
is the first maximal block.
Then the blocks of
\Gamma_{1}
are exactly
\delta_{1}\setminus\{\min(\Delta)\},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{p}
and
|\Gamma_{1}|=|\Delta|-1
. We distinguish three cases.

Case 1. Suppose that
\delta_{1}
is of type
2
. If
\delta_{2}
is not gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then direct computation yields

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})-1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
, and the desired inequality holds.

If
\delta_{2}
is gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})-1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
if
t_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, and

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
if
t_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
The conclusion follows similarly.

Case 2. Assume that
\delta_{1}
is of type
1
. If
\delta_{2}
is not gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
, and the claim holds.

If
\delta_{2}
is gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})-1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
if
t_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, and

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})+1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})
if
t_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
The result is again straightforward.

Case 3. Assume that
\delta_{1}
is of type
0
. Then
\Delta_{1}=\delta_{1}
and
t_{1}=0
. Thus,

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{1})
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{1})-1
,
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{1})+1
.
The inequality follows immediately.

\square

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq[n]
be a set of indices with
1\in\Delta
, and let
\Gamma_{2}=\Delta\cap[3,n]
. Then for all
t\geq 1
, we have

d(\Delta,t)\leq d(\Gamma_{2},t)+1.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{p}
be the decomposition of
\Delta
into maximal blocks, where
\delta_{1}
denotes the first maximal block.
We proceed by considering the following cases.

Case 1. Suppose
|\delta_{1}|=1
. Then
2\notin\Delta
, and
\Gamma_{2}=\Delta\setminus\{1\}=\Delta\setminus\{\min(\Delta)\}
. By Lemma
2.11
, the desired inequality holds immediately.

Case 2. Suppose
|\delta_{1}|\geq 2
. Then the blocks of
\Gamma_{2}
are exactly
\delta_{1}\setminus\{1,2\},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{p}
, and
|\Gamma_{2}|=|\Delta|-2
. We further distinguish three subcases:

(i) If
\delta_{1}
is of type
2
, then
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2})+1
, and
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})
, so the claim follows.

(ii) Assume
\delta_{1}
is of type
1
. If
\delta_{2}
is not gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})+1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2})-1
, and
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})+1
, and the inequality holds.

If
\delta_{2}
is gluable to
\delta_{1}
, then

\begin{cases}a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})-1,\;b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2}),\;c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})+1&\text{if }t_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\

The conclusion is clear in both subcases.

(iii) Assume
\delta_{1}
is of type
0
. Then
\Delta_{1}=\delta_{1}
and

.
If
\min(\delta_{2})-\max(\delta_{1})\geq 3
, then
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})-1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2})
, and
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})+1
, so the claim is verified.

If
\min(\delta_{2})-\max(\delta_{1})=2
, then

\begin{cases}a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})-1,\;b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2}),\;c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})+1&\text{if }t_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{2})+1,\;b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{2})-1,\;c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{2})+1&\text{if }t_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

The inequality holds in this case as well.

\square

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq[n]
be a set of indices with
1\in\Delta
, and let
\Gamma_{3}=\Delta\cap[4,n]
. Then for all
t\geq 1

d(\Delta,t)\leq d(\Gamma_{3},t)+1.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{p}
be the decomposition of
\Delta
into maximal blocks, where
\delta_{1}
denotes the first maximal block.
We consider the following cases.

Case 1. Suppose
|\delta_{1}|=1
.
If
2,3\notin\Delta
, then
\Gamma_{3}=\Delta\setminus\{1\}=\Delta\setminus\{\min(\Delta)\}
. By Lemma
2.11
, the desired inequality holds.

If
2\notin\Delta
and
3\in\Delta
, then
the blocks of
\Gamma_{3}
are exactly
\delta_{2}\setminus\{3\},\delta_{3},\ldots,\delta_{p}
and
|\Gamma_{3}|=|\Delta|-2
.
We distinguish three subcases.

(i) If
\delta_{2}
is of type
2
, then
\delta_{2}\setminus\{3\}
is of type
1
.
It follows that
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{3})
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{3})+1
, and
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{3})
. The claim holds.

(ii) If
\delta_{2}
is of type
1
, then
a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{3})
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{3})+1
, and
c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{3})
. The result is straightforward.

(iii) If
\delta_{2}
is of type
0

a(\Delta)=a(\Gamma_{3})+1
,
b(\Delta)=b(\Gamma_{3})-1

c(\Delta)=c(\Gamma_{3})+1

Case 2. Suppose
|\delta_{1}|=2

3\notin\Delta
and
\Gamma_{3}=\Delta\setminus\{1,2\}

2.12
, the inequality holds immediately.

Case 3. Suppose
|\delta_{1}|\geq 3
.
Then the blocks of
\Gamma_{3}
are exactly

and

Since

, and

The desired estimate follows at once.

\square

3.
Main results

In this section, we provide the exact formula for the depth of an increasing weighted path
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
.

If
n\leq 2
, then
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
is a star graph. By
[
18
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3]
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})=1
for all
t\geq 1
.

Thus, we assume
n\geq 3
. For simplicity, we set
{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}(e_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
. In this case, if
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
is a strictly increasing weighted path (i.e.,
{\bf w}_{i}<{\bf w}_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-2]
), then by
[
9
, Proposition 3.8]
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})=1
for all
t\geq 1
. From this point onward, we therefore assume that
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
is an increasing but not strictly increasing weighted path. That is, there exists some
i\in[n-2]
such that
{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i+1}
. Let
\Delta=\{i\in[n-2]\mid{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i+1}\}
; then
\Delta\neq\emptyset
.

In the following, unless otherwise specified, we will assume that
f_{i}=(x_{i}x_{i+1})^{{\bf w}_{i}}
for all
i\in[n]
.

By
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})=1
for all
t\geq|\Delta|+1
. Therefore, it suffices to determine
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})
for all
t\in[1,|\Delta|]
.

First, we establish the lower bound. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
. Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
be a weighted path such that
{\bf w}_{1}={\bf w}_{2}
. Denote by
I=I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})
and
J=I(P_{\bf w}^{[4,n+1]})
. Then for all
t\geq 1
we have

(I^{t}:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}})I^{t-1}+J^{t}.

Proof.

Clearly the left hand side contains the right hand side. Now assume that
g=(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{t}f_{i_{j}})/\gcd(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{t}f_{i_{j}},x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})
is a minimal generator of

(I^{t}:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})
.
We may assume that
i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{t}
.
If
i_{1}>2
, then
g=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{t}f_{i_{j}}\in J^{t}+x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}I^{t-1}.

If
i_{1}=1
, then
f_{i_{1}}=x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}
hence
g=x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{t}f_{i_{j}}\in x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}I^{t-1}
.

If
i_{1}=2
, then
f_{i_{1}}=x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}
hence,
g=x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{t}f_{i_{j}}\in x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}I^{t-1}
. The conclusion follows.

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})\geq d(\Delta,t)
for all
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
I=I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})
. We proceed by induction on
n
and
t
. The base case
n\leq 2
is trivial. We now assume
n\geq 3
and consider two cases:

(I) Assume
{\bf w}_{1}<{\bf w}_{2}
. First, let
t=1
. Assume that
L=I(P_{\bf w}^{[2,n+1]})
. Then

I=(L,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})\cap(L,x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})
. By Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\operatorname{depth}\bigl(\frac{S}{I}\bigr)\geq\min\Bigl\{\operatorname{depth}\Bigr(\frac{S}{(L,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})}\Bigr),\,\operatorname{depth}\Bigl(\frac{S}{(L,x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})}\Bigr),\,\operatorname{depth}\Bigl(\frac{S}{(L,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})}\Bigr)+1\Bigr\}.

Note that the set of indices
\Delta
is the same for
I
and
L
. Hence, by induction,
\operatorname{depth}(S/(L,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\geq d(\Delta,t)
. The conclusion follows from the induction hypothesis on
n
and Lemmas
2.1
,
2.11
, and
2.12
.

Next, assume that
t>1
. Let
f=(x_{1}x_{2})^{{\bf w}_{1}}
, then
(I^{t}:f)=I^{t-1}
and
(I^{t},f)=(I^{t},x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})\cap(I^{t},x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})
. By similar arguments to the preceding paragraph, we deduce that
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t},f))\geq d(\Delta,t)
. By Lemma
2.4
, the induction hypothesis on
t
, we deduce

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\geq\min\left\{\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I^{t}:f)\bigr),\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I^{t},f)\bigr)\right\}\geq d(\Delta,t).

(II) Assume
{\bf w}_{1}={\bf w}_{2}
. For
t=1
, we have
(I:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=I(P_{\bf w}^{[4,n+1]})+(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{2}})
and
(I,x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=I(P_{\bf w}^{[3,n+1]})+(x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})
. By the induction hypothesis on
n
and Lemmas
2.1
,
2.12
, and
2.13
, we have

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))=d(\Delta_{1},1)+1\geq d(\Delta,1)

and

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))=d(\Delta_{2},1)+1\geq d(\Delta,1),

where
\Delta_{1}=\Delta\cap[4,n-2]
and
\Delta_{2}=\Delta\cap[3,n-2]
. Applying Lemma
2.4
, we obtain
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\geq d(\Delta,1)
.

Now assume that
t>1
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t},x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})),\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\}.

By induction and Lemma
2.11
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t},x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\geq d(\Delta,t)
. Now, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

K=(I^{t}:x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}})I^{t-1}+J^{t}
, where
J=I(P_{\bf w}^{[4,n+1]})
.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/K)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/(K,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})),\operatorname{depth}(S/(K:x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\}
. Now,

(K:x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=I^{t-1}
and
(K,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}})+x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}L^{t-1}+J^{t}
.
By induction on
t
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/(K:x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\geq d(\Delta,t)
.
By induction on
n
, Lemmas
2.2
,
2.11
and
2.13
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S/(K,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/(K,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}})),\operatorname{depth}(S/((K,x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}):x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{1}}))\}

\displaystyle\geq d(\Delta,t).

The conclusion follows.

\square

Theorem 3.3
.

\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})\bigr)=k(\Delta)+1
, where
k(\Delta)=a(\Delta)+b(\Delta)+c(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Let
I=I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})
. By Lemma
3.2
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\leq k(\Delta)+1
. We proceed by induction on
n
. The base case
n\leq 2
is trivial, so we assume
n\geq 3
.

Let

f=x_{n+1}^{{\bf w}_{n}-1}\biggl(\prod_{k=m+2}^{n}x_{k}^{{\bf w}_{k-1}}\biggr),

where
m=\max(\Delta)
. Then

(I:f)=I(P_{\bf w}^{[1,m]})+\bigl(x_{m+1}^{{\bf w}_{m+1}},x_{m+2}^{{\bf w}_{m+2}-{\bf w}_{m+1}},x_{m+3}^{{\bf w}_{m+3}-{\bf w}_{m+2}},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{{\bf w}_{n-1}-{\bf w}_{n-2}},x_{n}^{{\bf w}_{n}-{\bf w}_{n-1}}x_{n+1}\bigr).

By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to prove that
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f))\leq k(\Delta)+1
. We consider two cases:

(i) If
m\in[1,3]
, then
k(\Delta)=1
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f))=2=k(\Delta)+1
.

(ii) If
m\geq 4
, let
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\{i\in[m-3]\mid{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i+1}\}
. By the induction hypothesis on
n
and Lemma
2.1
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(R/I(P_{\bf w}^{[1,m]}))\leq k(\Delta^{\prime})+1
. Moreover, Lemma
2.13
gives
k(\Delta^{\prime})\leq k(\Delta)-1
. Combining these results with Lemma
2.1
, we deduce

\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I:f)\bigr)=\operatorname{depth}\bigl(R/I(P_{\bf w}^{[1,m]})\bigr)+1\leq k(\Delta)+1.

This completes the proof.

\square

In the following, we consider the case where
t\geq 2
. If
|\Delta|=1
, then, by
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
,

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})=1
for all
t\geq 2
. Hence, we always assume that
|\Delta|\geq 2
.
We will prove that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t})\leq d(\Delta,t)
. To this end, we construct a monomial
f
such that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\leq\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I^{t}:f)\bigr)=d(\Delta,t)
. The construction of
f
relies on the following definition.

Let
\Delta=\delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{q}
be the decomposition of
\Delta
into maximal blocks. We define a partition

\Delta=A(\Delta)\sqcup B(\Delta)\sqcup C(\Delta).

It suffices to recursively define
A(\Delta)
and
B(\Delta)
; the set
C(\Delta)
is then given by
C(\Delta)=\Delta\setminus(A(\Delta)\cup B(\Delta))
.

For convenience, we write each block as
\delta_{j}=[m_{j},n_{j}]
for
j=1,\ldots,q
.

Base case.

If
\Delta
consists of a single block
\delta_{1}
, then

A(\Delta)=\begin{cases}\{n_{1}\},&\text{if $\delta_{1}$ is of type $1$},\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\;\;B(\Delta)=\begin{cases}\{n_{1}-1,n_{1}\},&\text{if $\delta_{1}$ is of type $2$},\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Recursive step.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}=\delta_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{q}
.

•

If
\delta_{1}
is of type
0
or type
2
, then

A(\Delta)=A(\Delta^{\prime}),\qquad B(\Delta)=B(\Delta^{\prime})\cup B(\delta_{1}).

•

If
\delta_{1}
is of type
1
and not gluable to
\delta_{2}
, then

A(\Delta)=A(\Delta^{\prime})\cup A(\delta_{1}),\qquad B(\Delta)=B(\Delta^{\prime}).

•

If
\delta_{1}
is of type
1
and gluable to
\delta_{2}
, set

\Gamma=\delta_{2}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup\delta_{q-1}\cup\delta_{q},\quad\text{where }\delta_{2}^{\prime}=\delta_{2}\setminus\{m_{2}\}.

We then define

A(\Delta)=A(\Gamma),\qquad B(\Delta)=B(\Gamma)\cup\{n_{1},m_{2}\}.

It is easy to see that the cardinalities of
A(\Delta)
,
B(\Delta)
, and
C(\Delta)
are related to the previously defined
a(\Delta)
,
b(\Delta)
, and
c(\Delta)
as follows:

|A(\Delta)|=a(\Delta)
,
|B(\Delta)|=2b(\Delta)
and
|C(\Delta)|=3c(\Delta)
.

We illustrate the construction with the following example.

Example 3.4
.

Let

\Delta=\{1\}\cup\{3,4\}\cup\{6\}\cup\{8,9\}\cup\{11,12,13\}\cup\{15\}.

The first block
\{1\}
is of type
1
and gluable to the subsequent block
\{3,4\}
. Thus,
\{1,3\}\subseteq B(\Delta)
, and we proceed with the remaining blocks
\{4\}\cup\{6\}\cup\{8,9\}\cup\{11,12,13\}\cup\{15\}
.

The block
\{4\}
is of type
1
and gluable to the subsequent block
\{6\}
, so
\{4,6\}\subseteq B(\Delta)
.

The block
\{8,9\}
is of type
2
, hence
\{8,9\}\subseteq B(\Delta)
.

Next, the block
\{11,12,13\}
is of type
0
, so
\{11,12,13\}
contributes to
C(\Delta)
.

Finally, the remaining singleton block
\{15\}
contributes to
A(\Delta)
.

We therefore have

A(\Delta)=\{15\},\qquad B(\Delta)=\{1,3,4,6,8,9\},\qquad C(\Delta)=\{11,12,13\}.

Now, we label the elements of
\Delta
by first listing the elements of
A(\Delta)
, followed by those of
B(\Delta)
, and finally those of
C(\Delta)
. Each set is arranged in descending order. We denote this labeling by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{|\Delta|}
. For the example above, we have

\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{10}\}=\{15,9,8,6,4,3,1,13,12,11\}.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
t\in[2,|\Delta|+1]
, and let
g_{t}
denote the product of the monomials corresponding to the weighted edges
e_{\mu_{j}+1}
for all
j=1,\ldots,t-1
. By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{depth}\bigl(S/(I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t}:g_{t})\bigr)\leq d(\Delta,t)
. However, the colon ideal
\bigl(I(P_{\bf w}^{n+1})^{t}:g_{t}\bigr)
is often too complex to directly verify the depth formula.

For example, let
{\bf w}=(1,1,2,2,2,2,4,4,6,6,6)
. Here,
\Delta=\{1,3,4,5,7,9\}
. If we set
g_{2}=f_{2}
, the resulting colon ideal is
(I^{2}:g_{2})=I+(x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}^{2})
. Note that the generator
x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}^{2}
has support on three variables, which makes it difficult to compute the depth of this ideal. However, if we multiply
g_{2}
by
x_{3}
, the colon ideal becomes more tractable, and its depth is easier to compute. We therefore adjust the definition of the product of edges as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
be an increasing weighted path. For each
i\in[n-1]
, let
f_{i}=(x_{i}x_{i+1})^{{\bf w}_{i}}
. We define the modified monomial
\tilde{f}_{i}
by

\tilde{f}_{i}=\begin{cases}f_{i},&\text{if ${\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i+1}$},\\
f_{i}\cdot\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}x_{j}^{{\bf w}_{j}-1},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\ell
denotes the largest index such that
{\bf w}_{i}<{\bf w}_{i+1}<\cdots<{\bf w}_{\ell}
.
Furthermore, let

\Delta=\bigl\{\,i\in[n-2]\mid{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i+1}\,\bigr\}.

Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{|\Delta|}
be the labeling of the elements of
\Delta
as defined above. For each integer
t
with
2\leq t\leq|\Delta|+1
, we define the monomial
g_{t}\in S
by

g_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}\tilde{f}_{\mu_{i}+1}.

Remark 3.7
.

Continuing with the example in Remark
3.5
, we see that
g_{3}=(x_{2}x_{3}^{2})\cdot(x_{10}x_{11})^{6}
and
g_{4}=(x_{2}x_{3}^{2})\cdot(x_{10}x_{11})^{6}\cdot\bigl((x_{8}x_{9})^{4}x_{9}^{5}\bigr)

[
7
]
and
[

]
to compute the colon ideal.
For a trivially weighted path, the colon ideal is obtained by bipartite completion of the closed neighborhood of edges. For a subset
\{e_{i}\mid i\in A\}
of edges of an increasing weighted path

f_{i}
and the modified monomial
\tilde{f}_{i}
. In the following, we compute the colon ideals of products of these
\tilde{f}_{i}
. For convenience, we introduce some notation:

For a monomial
f

\min(f)=\min(\operatorname{supp}(f))
and
\max(f)=\max(\operatorname{supp}(f))
,
where
\operatorname{supp}(f)=\{i\mid x_{i}\text{ divides }f\}

\operatorname{odd}(A)

A

\operatorname{odd}(A)=1
; otherwise,
\operatorname{odd}(A)
is the maximal odd integer in
A

\operatorname{even}(A)

A

\operatorname{even}(A)=0

\operatorname{even}(A)

\bar{D}=\prod\limits_{j\in A}f_{j}

\operatorname{odd}(D)=\operatorname{odd}(\operatorname{esupp}(D))

\operatorname{even}(D)=\operatorname{even}(\operatorname{esupp}(D))

\displaystyle U(D)

{\bf w}_{j},&\text{if }j\in\bigl([\min(D)-1,\max(D)+1]\cap[n]\bigr)\setminus\operatorname{esupp}(D),\\
{\bf w}_{n},&\text{if }j=n+1.\end{cases}

We also define

E(D)=\bigl(x_{j}^{a_{j}}\mid j\in[\min(D)-1,\operatorname{odd}(D)-1]\text{ and }j\text{ is even}\bigr),

and

O(D)=\bigl(x_{j}^{a_{j}}\mid j\in[\min(D)-1,\operatorname{even}(D)-1]\text{ and }j\text{ is odd}\bigr).

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n+1}
be an increasing weighted path, and let

be defined as in Definition

\Gamma_{i}=E(D_{i})+O(D_{i})+(U(D_{i})\cap V(D_{i}))

Let

I\subseteq(I^{t}:g_{t})

M_{t}=\bigl\{i\in[t_{k}]\mid\max(D_{i})\in C(\Delta)\sqcup\{n-1,n\}\bigr\}
,
J_{i}=(U^{\prime}(D_{i})\setminus O^{\prime}(D_{i}))\cap(V^{\prime}(D_{i})\setminus E^{\prime}(D_{i}))
,

K=\begin{cases}(x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{if ${\bf w}_{n-1}<{\bf w}_{n}$ or $n=b_{2b(\Delta)-1}+3$},\\
(x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
P^{\prime}
is an induced subpath of
P_{n+1}
on the set
C(\Delta)\sqcup\Pi_{1}
, where
\Pi_{1}=\{j\in A(\Delta):\tilde{f}_{j+1}\nmid g_{t}\text{ and }j\in C(\Delta)\}
.

To compute
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))
, it is enough to compute
\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/H_{1})
.
Applying
[
12
, Lemma 2.10]
to the edge
e_{c_{1}}
, we have

\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/H_{1})\leq\begin{cases}\operatorname{depth}(S_{11}/H_{11})+1,&\text{if $c_{1}+4\notin\Pi_{1}$},\\
\operatorname{depth}(S_{12}/H_{12})+2,&\text{if $c_{1}+4\in\Pi_{1}$},\end{cases}

where each
S_{1j}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{q}\mid q\in\operatorname{supp}(H_{1j})]
,

H_{1j}=\begin{cases}\bigl(\sum\limits_{i\in M_{t}}J_{i}\bigr)+K+I(P^{\prime}_{j}),&\text{if $\max(D_{j})\neq c_{1}$ for all $j\in M_{t}$},\\
\bigl(\sum\limits_{i\in M_{t}\setminus\{\ell\}}J_{i}\bigr)+K+I(P^{\prime}_{j}),&\text{if $\max(D_{\ell})=c_{1}$ for some $\ell\in M_{t}$},\\
\end{cases}

P^{\prime}_{1}
is an induced subpath of
P_{n+1}
on the set
(C(\Delta)\setminus\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\})\sqcup\Pi_{1}
and
P^{\prime}_{2}
is an induced subpath of
P_{n+1}
on the set
(C(\Delta)\setminus\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\})\sqcup(\Pi_{1}\setminus\{c_{1}+4\})
.

Now we need to compute
\operatorname{depth}(S_{1j}/H_{1j})
for
j\in[2]
. Applying
[
12
, Lemma 2.10]
successively to the edges
e_{c_{4}},e_{c_{7}},\ldots,e_{c_{3c(\Delta)-5}},e_{c_{3c(\Delta)-2}}
yields

\operatorname{depth}(S_{11}/H_{11})\leq c(\Delta)+|\Pi_{1}|
and
\operatorname{depth}(S_{12}/H_{12})\leq c(\Delta)+|\Pi_{1}|-1
.
Note that from the definition of
\Pi_{1}
, we have
|\Pi_{1}|=a(\Delta)-(t-1)-|\Pi|
.
Therefore,

\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/H_{1})\leq c(\Delta)+|\Pi_{1}|+1=c(\Delta)+a(\Delta)-|\Pi|-t+2
. Thus,
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))\leq k(\Delta)-t+2=d(\Delta,t)
.

(2) If
t\in[a(\Delta)+1,a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+1]
, then
d(\Delta,a(\Delta)+2j+1)=d(\Delta,a(\Delta)+2j+2)
for any
0\leq j\leq b(\Delta)-1
.
Then by
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
, we only need to consider the case where
t=a(\Delta)+2u+1
with
0\leq u\leq b(\Delta)
. The case
u=0
follows from (1). In the following, we assume that
u\in[b(\Delta)]
.
By Lemma
3.12
,
(I^{t}:\rho_{t})=I(P_{n+1})+\Phi_{t}+\Psi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t_{k}}\Upsilon_{i}
and
\Phi_{t}=(\{x_{b_{2i-1}},x_{b_{2i-1}+2}\}:i\in[b(\Delta)-u])
.
Easy to check that

[n+1]\setminus\Omega=\left(\operatorname{supp}(I^{t}:\rho_{t})\setminus\Omega\right)\sqcup\left(\{b_{2i-1}+1\mid i\in[b(\Delta)-u]\}\right)
and

\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(I^{t}:\rho_{t})
.
Therefore,

[n+1]=\operatorname{supp}(I^{t}:\rho_{t})\sqcup\left(\{b_{2i-1}+1\mid i\in[b(\Delta)-u]\}\right)
.
Since
|\{b_{2i-1}+1\mid i\in[b(\Delta)-u]\}|=b(\Delta)-u
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))=b(\Delta)-u+\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/H_{2})
, where
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{i}\mid i\in\operatorname{supp}(H_{2})]
,

\displaystyle H_{2}=\bigl(\sum\limits_{i\in[t_{k}]}J_{i}\bigr)+I(P^{\prime\prime})+\begin{cases}(x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{if ${\bf w}_{n-1}<{\bf w}_{n}$},\\
(x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{if ${\bf w}_{n-1}={\bf w}_{n}$},\end{cases}

and
P^{\prime\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
P_{n+1}
on the set
C(\Delta)
. By similar arguments as the case (1), we can deduce that
\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/H_{2})\leq c(\Delta)+1
. Therefore,
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))\leq b(\Delta)-u+c(\Delta)+1=d(\Delta,t)
.

(3) If
t\in[a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+1,|\Delta|]
, then we also have that
d(\Delta,a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+3j+1)=d(\Delta,a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+3j+2)=d(\Delta,a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+3j+3)
for any
0\leq j\leq c(\Delta)-1
.
As a result, we only need to consider the case where
t=a(\Delta)+2b(\Delta)+3v+1
with
0\leq v\leq c(\Delta)-1
. The case
v=0
follows from (2). In the following, we assume that
v\in[c(\Delta)-1]
. In this case,
(I^{t}:\rho_{t})=I(P_{n+1})+\Psi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t_{k}}\Upsilon_{i}
and
\Phi_{t}=(0)
.
By the definition of
g_{t}
and
\rho_{t}
, we have

\tilde{f}_{j+1}\mid\rho_{t}
for any
j\in A(\Delta)\cup B(\Delta)\cup\{c_{3(c(\Delta)-v)+1},c_{3(c(\Delta)-v)+2},\ldots,c_{3c(\Delta)}\}
. Therefore,
(I^{t}:\rho_{t})=\Psi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t_{k}}(O^{\prime}(D_{i})+E^{\prime}(D_{i}))+H_{3}
, where

\displaystyle H_{3}=\bigl(\sum\limits_{i\in[t_{k}]}J_{i}\bigr)+I(P^{\prime\prime\prime})+\begin{cases}(x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{if ${\bf w}_{n-1}<{\bf w}_{n}$},\\
(x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}x_{n+1}),&\text{if ${\bf w}_{n-1}={\bf w}_{n}$},\end{cases}

and
P^{\prime\prime\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
P_{n+1}
on the set
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{3(c(\Delta)-v)}\}
.
Since the ideal
\Psi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t_{k}}(O^{\prime}(D_{i})+E^{\prime}(D_{i}))
is generated by variables,
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))=\operatorname{depth}(S_{3}/H_{3})
, where
S_{3}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{i}\mid i\in\operatorname{supp}(H_{3})]
. By similar arguments as the case (1), we can deduce that
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:\rho_{t}))=\operatorname{depth}(S_{3}/H_{3})\leq c(\Delta)-v+1=d(\Delta,t)
.

\square
