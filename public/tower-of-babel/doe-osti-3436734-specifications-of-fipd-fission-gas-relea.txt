 1. INTRODUCTION
All fission gas release data stored in the Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD) [1-3] was
originally measured using the Gas Assay, Sample and Recharge (GASR) System in the Hot Fuel
Examination Facility (HFEF) [4]. It is therefore called GASR data in FIPD. During the
measurement of a sample, such as an irradiated EBR-II fuel element/capsule, a pinhole-sized
region near the top of the element plenum was melted by a laser. Plenum gas then expanded into
a calibrated volume (note: in this document, “sample” and “capsule/element” are used
interchangeably consistent with GASR documents in FIPD). The pressure rise in the volume was
recorded. Helium backfilling and expansion was then performed to determine the sample (e.g.,
fuel element plenum) volume using Boyle’s Law and assuming ideal gas behavior at constant
temperature. With the plenum volume and the recorded pressure rise, the sample (e.g., fuel element
plenum) pressure was derived with assumption of ideal gas law behavior. The plenum volume and
pressure as well as the cladding temperature during the measurement were collected (GASR data
in FIPD). Other records associated with the fission gas release data include: raw GASR data
records including volumes and post-puncture pressures of seal head/sealing head and manifold,
calibration data, backfilling gas pressure data, and the data analysis records. A sample(s) of the
fission gas released from the plenum was collected by the GASR system into sample bottles. The
chemical and isotopic composition of the gas sample was analyzed separately from GASR data,
and will be discussed in a separate specification. The plenum volume, pressure, and cladding
temperature during the measurement are typically utilized to determine the number of moles of
gas in the plenum. This quantity is often compared to the number of moles of gas generated by
fission events. However, calculating these values and their associated uncertainty is beyond the
scope of this document, as it necessitates additional assumptions.
The most important document to understand the FIPD fission gas data is the GASR operational
manual (title: Gas Assay, Sample and Recharge System (GASR) operation and maintenance
manual, HFEF/N OMM 4381, DOC. NO. W0018-0032-ES-00) [5]. This manual provides: (1)
description of the GASR and the functions of each component (laser drilling, welding, seal
head/sealing head, manifold, vacuum system, sample system, purge and gas tag system, etc.); (2)
step-by-step guidance on calibrations, operations, and measurements; and (3) maintenance
procedures and other details relating to the structure and operation of the GASR. Note that the
original GASR operated until 2020. A new GASR with the same design and measurement
methodology was installed in 2021 [6]. The specifications of the GASR presented on the HFEF
website [4] at this time are consistent with the ones given in the operational manual [5]. The
methods to calculate the plenum volume and pressures were not included in the operational manual,
but were recorded in the legacy data analysis files [7, 8]. Details of the methods are given in
Chapter 3.

 2. FISSION GAS PRESSURE AND PLENUM VOLUME MEASUREMENTS
The GASR system operation is complex and includes the process of loading the fuel element, laser
operation and puncture, evacuation and pressurization, calibration, backfilling, etc. However,
many procedures (e.g., laser puncturing, gas sampling, backfilling tag gas, leak tests, and system
cleanup) are not needed for the determination of plenum volume and pressure of a fuel element,
and therefore are not included in this specification (details of those procedures can be found in ref.
[5]).
Note that the GASR measurement is only for the volume and pressure that communicates with the
plenum where the fuel pin is punctured. For sodium bonded fuel, the sampled volume is typically
above the fuel and sodium bond, and the sodium bond has largely been displaced above the fuel
column except for sodium in fuel porosity or in the fuel-clad gaps. In a small number of fuel pins,
sodium bond was displaced above the fuel column up into the plenum and effectively reduced the
volume characterized by GASR data.
Some fuel elements were prepared by in-cell impact treatment at 500°F before GASR
measurements. This heat treatment was to ensure that sodium traps above the top of the fuel
consolidate and to provide a single plenum of sufficient size for accurate measurement. The GASR
measurement were performed after fuel element was cooled and bond sodium solidified [9].
The GASR system calibration and verification is given below:
 1. The volumes of the seal head/sealing head and manifold have been ascertained by out-of-
 cell studies and verified by INL Analytical Laboratory personnel. GASR system
 component volumes without an assigned uncertainty are provided in the procedures.
 Documented volume characterization performed more recently report uncertainty of ±1%
      or less [10].
 2. Two thermocouples are installed in the system: one monitors the temperature near the
 manifold, which is recorded as cabinet temperature in the records (for more details, refer
 to Chapter 4), while the other is in contact with the cladding of the capsule/element to
 monitor the temperature rise of the element during drilling or welding operations (recorded
 as capsule/element temperature, for more details, refer to Chapter 4).
 3. Out-of-cell measurement and test equipment (i.e., pressure gauges) calibration [5, 11, 12].
 a. Calibration and control of HFEF out-of-cell measuring and test equipment is
 administered by the HFEF Instrumentation and Control (I&C) Group Supervisor
 b. The pressure gauge calibration is performed at the prescribed intervals, which has
 been defined between one and three months [5, 11, 12].
 c. Calibration standards are traceable to standards maintained by a national accredited
 organization (e.g., the National Bureau of Standards), defined by accepted values
 of physical constants, or derived by ratio-type self-calibration techniques.
 d. The out-of-cell pressure gauge calibration verifies accuracy of ±0.1%.

4. Discussion of Ideal Gas Law Assumption
 Note that for the algebraic substitution and the slope method, the calculation of 𝑉𝑐𝑎𝑝
 doesn’t rely on the pressure measurements from initial laser puncture and expansion, but
 the calculation of 𝑃𝑐𝑎𝑝 uses the measured expansion pressure from the initial laser puncture.
 And for either method, the basic assumption used to calculate 𝑉𝑐𝑎𝑝 is that the backfilled

helium is acting as an ideal gas (see Eq. 4). This is reasonable because the backfilled gas
pressure is low enough. However, the basic assumption of calculating 𝑃𝑐𝑎𝑝 is that both the
backfilled helium and the plenum gas are ideal gases. The fission gas (comprised mainly
of Kr and Xe) is usually in the range of hundreds of psi. In a recent study, fission gas was
simulated by a three-component gas (11.3%He, 12.9%Kr, and 75.8%Xe) to study the
compressibility. [15]. A polynomial equation of the compressibility factor was developed
by fitting the experimental data:

 4. SPECIFICATION OF FISSION GAS RELEASE DATA
Some key details about gas release measurements were extracted from available literature about
the GASR [5]. Combined with the data records and other documents in FIPD, a specification for
interpretating the fission gas release data was established:
 1. Raw measurement record
 The measured helium gas pressures during the pre-puncture calibration were recorded in a
 table (see example in Figure 1). The data from 1/6/84 to 9/25/89 were collected, digitized,
 and preserved in ref [14].

 The pressure measurements during the laser puncture run and helium backfilling process
 were recorded using a pressure/volume determination form (Figure 7). Note that the
 parameters for the fuel element rewelding process were also recorded in the form but are
 not needed for plenum volume and pressure determination. FIPD contains the raw
 measurement records for only several subassemblies (e.g., X448 and X448A). Figure 8
 shows an example of a raw measurement record stored in FIPD. Note that in Figure 7(a)
 and (b), "IN-CELL TEMP" and "Cap/El Temp" refer to the readings of the thermocouple
 in contact with the cladding of the capsule/element, while "cabinet temp" in Figure 7 refers
 to the reading of the thermocouple that monitors the temperature near the manifold.
