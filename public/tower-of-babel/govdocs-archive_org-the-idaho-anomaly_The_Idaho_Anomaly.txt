The geopolitical and physical geography of the American
West has long provided a sanctuary for the development,
testing, and deployment of the United States' most advanced
military and aerospace technologies. Within this strategic
paradigm, the state of Idaho occupies a uniquely critical po-
sition. Far removed from the coastal vulnerabilities of the
Pacific seaboard and insulated by rugged mountainous ter-
rain and expansive high deserts, Idaho has evolved over the
past century into a pivotal node for aerospace defense, nu-
clear research, and acoustic naval engineering. Simultane-
ously, and perhaps not coincidentally, Idaho operates as the
historical ground zero for the modern Unidentified Aerial
Phenomenon (UAP) era—formerly classified as Unidenti-
fied Flying Objects (UFOs).
This report provides an exhaustive, forensic exploration
of Idaho’s military infrastructure, tracing the historical evo-
lution of its installations from frontier militias to modern
hubs of electronic warfare and stealth submarine research.

The Establishment of the Idaho National

Upon achieving statehood in 1890, the state constitution
mandated the creation of a permanent military apparatus.
This directive led to the organization of the First Regiment
of Idaho Infantry in 1890 and the official legislative approval
of the Idaho National Guard on March 14, 1891, establishing
it as the official state militia.2
Since its inception, the Idaho National Guard has main-
tained an unbroken record of participation in every major
military conflict involving the United States. The Guard's
first federal deployment occurred on May 7, 1898, when in-
fantry units were dispatched to the Philippines during the
Spanish-American War.2 Subsequent deployments included
border security operations in Nogales, Arizona, during the
1916 Pancho Villa Expedition, and extensive service in both
the Pacific and European theaters of World War II, during
which every unit in the state was activated.2
The operational legacy of these units is highly distin-
guished. Notably, the 116th Engineer Battalion remains the
only National Guard unit in the entire country to have de-
ployed to both the Korean and Vietnam conflicts.2 Today,
