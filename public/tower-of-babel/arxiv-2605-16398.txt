Support-Safe Variational Hybrid Filtering for Contact-Mode and Sparse-Law Recovery
Marios Papamichalis, Regina Ruane

Abstract
Contact-rich robot dynamics are hybrid: a single observation can match several latent states and contact regimes (free, impact, stick–slip). A standard amortized filter that places no probability on a feasible contact transition will permanently lose the branch the robot actually follows. We introduce VHyDRO , a variational hybrid dynamics learner that prevents this branch loss. At each step, VHyDRO mixes the learned proposal with a feasible transition law before sampling and importance weighting, ensuring that every transition retained by the model-feasible carrier remains covered. VHyDRO jointly infers a continuous latent state and a discrete contact mode, and fits a sparse port-Hamiltonian law to each recovered regime. On top of this, three guarantees connect: support coverage stabilizes filtering, the stabilized filter concentrates the discrete contact posterior on coherent regimes, and mode-pure segments admit sparse port-Hamiltonian recovery. The recovery error separates cleanly into filtering, derivative, mode-impurity, and physics-residual parts. Three empirical findings track the same mechanism. Under heavy occlusion the support-safe filter stays usable while a non-defensive proposal collapses. On ManiSkill demonstrations and on four Sawyer/BridgeData task families the discrete state forms temporally coherent contact-regime segments that the discrete state yields a stronger joint profile across ARI, change-point F1, and segment purity than post-hoc and mode-free baselines. On hybrid systems with known equations the mode-conditioned sparse fit recovers the active physical terms; purely predictive baselines do not.

Despite this progress, the ingredients needed for contact-rich hybrid filtering

scalable perception, interpretable dynamics, uncertainty diagnostics, and
model-relative support-safe inference.

We propose
VHyDRO
(
variational hybrid dynamics with robust proposal support
), a support-safe hybrid variational state-space model for
partially observed contact dynamics.
VHyDRO
represents the manipulation scene by a continuous latent state
z_{t}
, and represents the active predictive regime by
a discrete mode
s_{t}
. The transition associated with each mode is
parameterized by a port-Hamiltonian core with sparse library corrections,
yielding dynamics that are physically structured and inspectable.

Inference in
VHyDRO
uses a support-safe variational family. A standard
amortized proposal may assign zero or very small probability to model-feasible
contact states, producing unstable importance weights and degrading learning.

VHyDRO
mitigates this failure mode by mixing the learned proposal with a
feasible transition carrier. The resulting mixture satisfies
P_{t}\ll Q_{t,\lambda}
and gives positive
Q_{t,\lambda}
-mass to every measurable transition set with positive
P_{t}
-mass.

The contribution is not a new defensive-IS inequality; it is a deployable support-safety mechanism for amortized hybrid filtering: choose the mixture mass before sampling, sample from the same mixture used in the importance weight, and audit the resulting representation through filtering stability, mode coherence, and sparse-law recovery.

Theory

Classical tools appear here as certifiable components, but the way they are wired together is specific to
VHyDRO
. Theorem
1
turns defensive mixing into a history-conditioned, pre-sampling IW/FIVO support gate; the same realized
\lambda_{t}
is used in both proposal and weight. Theorem
2
states the predictive-separation condition under which the discrete posterior concentrates on a coherent mode using only likelihood-ratio evidence. Theorem

Model and training.

VHyDRO
parameterizes a hybrid latent state
x_{t}=(s_{t},z_{t})
, where

z_{t}
is a continuous belief state and
s_{t}
is a discrete predictive
contact regime. The amortized proposal
Q_{t}
is observation-adaptive and
sharp. The carrier
P_{t}
is the model’s one-step transition law before
conditioning on
o_{t+1}
:

P_{t}(dx_{t+1})=\pi_{\theta}(ds_{t+1}\mid x_{t},a_{t})\,p_{\theta}(dz_{t+1}\mid s_{t+1},z_{t},a_{t}).

Its discrete support is the model-feasible mode support, and its continuous
support is the corresponding mode-conditioned transition support with the
validation-fixed noise floor shared across variants. Thus
P_{t}
is not
assumed to be the true physical law; it declares the model-feasible support
that the learned proposal is not allowed to delete. Training samples from

Q_{t,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)Q_{t}+\lambda P_{t},

and weights particles using the same realized mixture density. Support safety
is therefore model-relative:
VHyDRO
preserves every measurable transition
set assigned positive mass by
P_{t}
, but it cannot recover true physical
branches omitted by
P_{t}
. After filtering, mode-pure segments are fit with a
sparse port-Hamiltonian library by regularized regression. The mode count and
candidate library are fixed in the present experiments.

The learned parameters are optimized with the sequential IW/FIVO objective:
at step
t
, the one-step normalizer estimate is

\widehat{Z}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{\theta}(o_{t+1}\mid z_{t+1}^{i})\frac{dP_{t}}{dQ_{t,\lambda_{t}}}(x_{t+1}^{i}),\qquad x_{t+1}^{i}\sim Q_{t,\lambda_{t}},

and training maximizes the corresponding sum of log normalizer increments.
Thus the same realized mixture is used for sampling and weighting in both
training and evaluation. The theorem requires an
h_{t}
-measurable upper
certificate
\bar{\rho}_{t}\geq\rho_{t}
before particles are drawn. In the
experiments,
\lambda_{t}
is selected by a validation-frozen rule before
held-out particles are sampled; certificate plots are diagnostics of the
realized variance frontier and are not used for post-hoc retuning. When the
requested budget cannot be certified, the filter marks it uncertified and uses
a validation-fixed fallback
\lambda_{\rm fb}
, which is not itself claimed
to certify the requested
\tau
.

VHyDRO
combines support-safe hybrid filtering, mode concentration, and
sparse-law recovery; Theorems

–

1.

Choose

\lambda_{\min,t}\leq 1

2.

Sample each next particle from

Return the filtered particles and the mode posterior

Theorem 1

(Support-safe budgeted IW/FIVO increment)
.

Assume
0<Z_{t}<\infty
and
M_{2,t}<\infty
. Then
P_{t}\ll Q_{t,\lambda}
,
\Pi_{t}\ll Q_{t,\lambda}
, and

0\leq\frac{dP_{t}}{dQ_{t,\lambda}}(x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\qquad Q_{t,\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

(2)

Consequently, for the one-step weight
W_{t}(x):=g_{t}(x)dP_{t}/dQ_{t,\lambda}(x)
,

\chi^{2}(\Pi_{t}\|Q_{t,\lambda})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\rho_{t}-1,\qquad\rho_{t}:=\frac{M_{2,t}}{Z_{t}^{2}}.

(3)

Since
M_{2,t}\geq Z_{t}^{2}
by Jensen’s inequality,
\rho_{t}\geq 1
, so the
right-hand side is nonnegative for
\lambda\in(0,1]

If
X^{1:N}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}Q_{t,\lambda}
and
\widehat{Z}_{t}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{t}(X^{i})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{t}\mid h_{t}]

\displaystyle=Z_{t},

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}_{t}\mid h_{t})}{Z_{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\rho_{t}}{\lambda}-1\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{ESS}_{N}}{N}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{1+\chi^{2}(\Pi_{t}\|Q_{t,\lambda})}\geq\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{t}}.

\lambda\geq\frac{\bar{\rho}_{t}}{1+N\tau^{2}},\qquad\frac{\bar{\rho}_{t}}{1+N\tau^{2}}\leq 1.

(5)

Relation to prior bounds.

The result is a Lasso oracle bound applied to a mode-conditioned
port-Hamiltonian library with plug-in errors. The four-term decomposition is
used as claim bookkeeping: it identifies the aggregate sources of perturbation
that can affect sparse recovery, but the experiments measure aggregate sparse
support and coefficient recovery rather than separately certifying each
perturbation component.

The four components of

\nu_{m}=\nu_{m}^{\text{filt}}+\nu_{m}^{\text{der}}+\nu_{m}^{\text{mode}}+\nu_{m}^{\text{pH}}

are defined in Appendix
B.3
. They should be read
as an aggregate error accounting device, not as four independently measured
empirical certificates.

Assumptions and claim boundary.

All three guarantees are model-relative. The carrier
P_{t}
must assign positive mass to the physical branch one wants to preserve; a branch omitted by
P_{t}
cannot be recovered by support mixing. The mode set and sparse library are finite and fixed in the present experiments. The action-cancellation condition in Theorem
2
matches the logged-intervention evaluation protocol used here; mode-dependent closed-loop policies require including the policy likelihood in the mode odds. Finally, the results below audit filtering, segmentation, and sparse-law recovery, not certified closed-loop control.

3
Experiments

The experiments audit the three mechanism links: support-safe IW/FIVO under occlusion, proxy-label contact-regime segmentation, and sparse-law recovery on known-equation systems. We report ESS/N, relative weight variance, empirical relative variance, NLL, ECE, Cov90, Mode F1, ARI, change-point F1, segment purity, support F1, coefficient error, physical-constant error, and vector-field NRMSE.

Shared protocol and matched ablations.

Each applicable method/task/condition cell uses
20
independent seeds, with no seed pruning. Within each comparison, variants share architecture family, latent dimension, particle count, training budget, observation likelihood, resampling rule, occlusion schedule when applicable, and proxy-label construction. The
\lambda=0
/ no-support ablation removes only defensive support mass; the smooth/no-mode/no-sparsity/no-pH ablations remove the indicated mechanism. Proxy labels are deterministic functions of recorded state/action fields and are used only for evaluation. These are mechanism-isolation audits, not closed-loop task-success, contact-force-ground-truth, or real-robot claims.

4
Conclusion and Limitations

Amortized variational filters can catastrophically delete feasible contact
branches under partial observation.
VHyDRO
prevents this by using a
model-relative defensive transition carrier with a pre-sampling variance budget.
This support repair stabilizes filtering, and the stabilized filter yields
coherent predictive modes that can support downstream sparse-law recovery.
Technically, the defensive mixture ensures
P_{t}\ll Q_{t,\lambda}
and bounds
the transition-to-proposal density ratio for transition sets retained by the
feasible carrier
P_{t}
. Empirically, the paper tests the three links of this
mechanism chain: high-occlusion IW/FIVO diagnostics, proxy-label
contact-regime segmentation, and known-equation sparse-law recovery.

Limitations.

The guarantees are model-relative: if the carrier
P_{t}
omits a real physical branch,
VHyDRO
cannot recover it. The mode set and sparse library are fixed in the present experiments. The action-cancellation assumption in Theorem
2
matches logged interventions or mode-independent behavior policies; mode-dependent closed-loop policies require a different likelihood-ratio statement. The experiments are matched mechanism audits, not claims of closed-loop task-success dominance, external SOTA ranking, simulator contact-force ground-truth recovery, real-robot transfer, or real-robot sparse-law identification. A natural follow-up is closed-loop MPC with a held-out predictive KL or calibration certificate, which would let recovered mode-wise laws support chance-constrained planning rather than only offline mechanism audits.

A.2
Experiment 2: ManiSkill contact-regime segmentation audit with proxy labels

Protocol.

Experiment 2 uses released ManiSkill HDF5 demonstrations from PushCube-v1, PokeCube-v1, PullCubeTool-v1, and PegInsertionSide-v1. The evaluation labels are denoised kinematic proxies derived from raw HDF5 states and actions. They distinguish free motion, impact-like transitions, and
stick/slip-like contact using smoothed object/end-effector kinematic
changes. They are used for validation diagnostics and held-out evaluation only; the model is not trained with simulator contact-force labels. Hyperparameters are selected on validation episodes, and all reported metrics are computed on held-out test episodes.

Kinematic proxy-label construction.

For each trajectory, the deterministic proxy uses only recorded state/action
fields and is shared by all methods. Let
p_{t}^{\rm obj}
and

p_{t}^{\rm ee}
denote object and end-effector positions, and let
a_{t}

denote the action. We compute normalized finite-difference terms

r_{t}^{\rm obj}=\frac{\|\Delta p_{t}^{\rm obj}\|_{2}}{\operatorname{MAD}(\|\Delta p^{\rm obj}\|_{2})+\epsilon},\qquad r_{t}^{\rm ee}=\frac{\|\Delta p_{t}^{\rm ee}\|_{2}}{\operatorname{MAD}(\|\Delta p^{\rm ee}\|_{2})+\epsilon},\qquad r_{t}^{a}=\frac{\|\Delta a_{t}\|_{2}}{\operatorname{MAD}(\|\Delta a\|_{2})+\epsilon}.

The scalar score is

c_{t}=\alpha_{\rm obj}r_{t}^{\rm obj}+\alpha_{\rm ee}r_{t}^{\rm ee}+\alpha_{a}r_{t}^{a}.

The weights
\alpha
, smoothing window, minimum-run denoising length, and
thresholds
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
are selected on validation episodes and then
frozen. After smoothing, labels are assigned as free if
c_{t}<\theta_{1}
,
impact-like if
c_{t}\geq\theta_{2}
, and stick/slip-like otherwise, followed by
the fixed minimum-run denoising. Clipping at the 99.5th percentile is used only
for plotting histograms, not for metric computation. The test split is never
used to choose thresholds. Proxy labels are used only for metric computation
after label-permutation matching; they are not provided to the
VHyDRO
mode
posterior during training.

A.4
Experiment 3: sparse physical-law recovery audit

This appendix audits the sparse-law link in the theory chain:

mode concentration
\Rightarrow
sparse physical-law recovery
. The
benchmark uses known sparse port-Hamiltonian laws, so support recovery,
coefficient error, vector-field error, and physical-constant error can be
measured directly.

Protocol.

We evaluate four controlled known-equation hybrid systems with known sparse laws: a bouncing puck with wall contacts, a sliding block with Coulomb friction, a pendulum with contact stops, and a planar pushing system. The same twenty-seed evaluation protocol is applied to every method and includes observation noise, missing data, hidden velocities, numerical derivative noise, and small mode-assignment perturbations. We compare
VHyDRO
-full,
no-mode
,
no-sparsity
, and
no-pH
. The first three produce port-Hamiltonian coefficients and can therefore be evaluated by sparse support recovery, relative coefficient error, and physical-constant error; the no-pH baseline is evaluated only as a vector-field predictor. The candidate libraries contain at most
p=12
terms per system, and the
true active support has size at most
k=3
. These values match the
low-dimensional sparse setting used when interpreting
Theorem
3
.

Summary metrics.

Table
13
is the main quantitative audit.
VHyDRO
is best on every metric that reflects sparse physical-law recovery: support F1, relative coefficient error, vector-field NRMSE, and physical-constant error. The no-mode ablation merges incompatible regimes and degrades both support recovery and coefficient estimation. The no-sparsity ablation predicts more accurately than no-mode, but the dense fit obscures the active support. The no-pH baseline reaches a moderate vector-field error but has no sparse port-Hamiltonian coefficients to inspect.

The no-mode ablation confounds regimes, the no-sparsity ablation predicts with dense coefficients but loses sparse support recovery, and the no-pH ablation fits a vector field without recoverable port-Hamiltonian coefficients.

Interpretation.

The sparse-law benchmark is designed so that a purely predictive model can
perform well without identifying the governing mechanism. The no-pH baseline
demonstrates this possibility: it achieves a moderate vector-field error, yet
it offers no recoverable port-Hamiltonian coefficients, no sparse support, and
no physical constants. On this controlled known-equation audit,
VHyDRO

simultaneously predicts accurately and recovers the sparse, mode-wise physical
mechanism.

B.1
Proof of Theorem
1
: support-safe budgeted IW/FIVO increment

Proof.

The theorem is stated around the defensive proposal in Eq. (
1
). We prove, in order, the domination and Radon–Nikodym bound in Eq. (
2
), the chi-square bound in Eq. (
3
), the unbiasedness/variance/ESS statement in Eq. (
4
), and the budgeted choice of
\lambda
in Eq. (
5
).

All quantities are conditional on the realized particle history

h_{t}=(s_{t},z_{t},a_{t},o_{\leq t+1}).

(23)

For readability, write

P:=P_{t},\qquad Q:=Q_{t},\qquad Q_{\lambda}:=Q_{t,\lambda},\qquad g:=g_{t},\qquad Z:=Z_{t},\qquad M_{2}:=M_{2,t}.

(24)

With this notation, Eq. (
1
) becomes

Q_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)Q+\lambda P.

(25)

Defensive-mixture domination.

For any measurable set
A
, Eq. (
25
) gives

Q_{\lambda}(A)=(1-\lambda)Q(A)+\lambda P(A)\geq\lambda P(A).

(26)

Hence, if
Q_{\lambda}(A)=0
, then
P(A)=0
. Therefore

P\ll Q_{\lambda}.

(27)

By the Radon–Nikodym theorem, the derivative

r(x):=\frac{dP}{dQ_{\lambda}}(x)

(28)

exists
Q_{\lambda}
-almost surely.

Bounded prior-to-proposal ratio.

The domination inequality (
26
) implies

P(A)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}(A)\qquad\forall A.

(29)

We claim that

r(x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\qquad Q_{\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

(30)

Suppose, for contradiction, that

B:=\left\{x:r(x)>\frac{1}{\lambda}\right\}

(31)

has positive
Q_{\lambda}
-measure. Then

P(B)=\int_{B}r(x)\,Q_{\lambda}(dx)>\frac{1}{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}(B),

(32)

which contradicts Eq. (
29
). Thus Eq. (
30
) holds. Together with nonnegativity of Radon–Nikodym derivatives, this is precisely the main-text bound Eq. (
2
).

Domination of the filtering target.

Define the unnormalized target measure

\widetilde{\Pi}(A):=\int_{A}g(x)\,P(dx).

(33)

Because
g\geq 0
, if
P(A)=0
, then
\widetilde{\Pi}(A)=0
. Hence
\widetilde{\Pi}\ll P
. Since
0<Z<\infty
, the normalized measure

\Pi(A):=\frac{\widetilde{\Pi}(A)}{Z}=\frac{1}{Z}\int_{A}g(x)\,P(dx)

(34)

is a probability measure. Moreover,

\Pi\ll P\ll Q_{\lambda},

(35)

so
\Pi\ll Q_{\lambda}
. This proves the target-support part of Theorem
1
.

Derivative of the filtering target.

Since

\Pi(dx)=\frac{g(x)}{Z}P(dx)

(36)

and

P(dx)=r(x)Q_{\lambda}(dx),

(37)

the Radon–Nikodym chain rule gives

\frac{d\Pi}{dQ_{\lambda}}(x)=\frac{g(x)}{Z}r(x)\qquad Q_{\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

(38)

The assumption that
g(x)<\infty

(P+Q)
-a.s. implies
g(x)<\infty

Q_{\lambda}
-a.s., because
Q_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)Q+\lambda P
. Together with Eq. (
30
), this gives

W(x):=g(x)r(x)<\infty,\qquad 0\leq W(x)\leq\frac{g(x)}{\lambda}\qquad Q_{\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

(39)

This is the full SI version of the finite-weight statement summarized in Theorem
1
.

Chi-square identity.

By definition,

1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})=\int_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\frac{d\Pi}{dQ_{\lambda}}(x)\right)^{2}Q_{\lambda}(dx).

(40)

Using Eq. (
38
),

1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{Z^{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}g(x)^{2}r(x)^{2}\,Q_{\lambda}(dx).

(41)

Guide-independent chi-square bound.

Since
0\leq r(x)\leq\lambda^{-1}

Q_{\lambda}
-a.s. by Eq. (
2
),

r(x)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}r(x)\qquad Q_{\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

(42)

Therefore,

\displaystyle 1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda Z^{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}g(x)^{2}r(x)\,Q_{\lambda}(dx)

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda Z^{2}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}g(x)^{2}\,P(dx)=\frac{M_{2}}{\lambda Z^{2}}.

Equivalently,

\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{M_{2}}{Z^{2}}-1.

(44)

Since
Z=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)]
,
M_{2}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)^{2}]
, and
\rho_{t}=M_{2,t}/Z_{t}^{2}
, Eq. (
44
) is exactly the main-text chi-square bound Eq. (
3
).

Second-moment identity.

From Eq. (
38
),

W(x)=g(x)r(x)=Z\frac{d\Pi}{dQ_{\lambda}}(x).

(45)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\lambda}}[W(X)^{2}]

\displaystyle=Z^{2}\int_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\frac{d\Pi}{dQ_{\lambda}}(x)\right)^{2}Q_{\lambda}(dx)

(46)

\displaystyle=Z^{2}\left(1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})\right).

Combining Eq. (
46
) with Eq. (
43
) gives

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\lambda}}[W(X)^{2}]\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}M_{2}<\infty.

(47)

Unbiasedness of the one-step normalizer estimate.

Let
X\sim Q_{\lambda}
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\lambda}}[W(X)]=\int_{\mathsf{X}}g(x)r(x)\,Q_{\lambda}(dx)=\int_{\mathsf{X}}g(x)\,P(dx)=Z.

(48)

Thus, for conditionally i.i.d. samples
X^{1},\ldots,X^{N}\sim Q_{\lambda}
and

\widehat{Z}^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W(X^{i}),

(49)

we have

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t}]=Z.

(50)

This is the first line of Eq. (
4
).

Variance identity.

Because
X^{1},\ldots,X^{N}
are conditionally i.i.d. given
h_{t}
,

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t})=\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{\lambda}}(W(X)).

(51)

Using Eqs. (
46
) and (
48
),

\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{\lambda}}(W(X))=Z^{2}\left(1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})\right)-Z^{2}=Z^{2}\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda}).

(52)

Therefore,

\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t})}{Z^{2}}=\frac{1}{N}\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda}).

(53)

Applying Eq. (
44
) yields

\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t})}{Z^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{N}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)]^{2}}-1\right],

(54)

which is the variance part of Eq. (
4
).

Asymptotic normalized ESS.

Let

W_{i}:=W(X^{i}),\qquad\overline{W}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{i},\qquad\overline{W^{2}}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}^{2}.

(55)

By Eqs. (
46
) and (
48
),

\mathbb{E}[W_{i}]=Z\in(0,\infty),\qquad\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{2}]<\infty.

(56)

The strong law of large numbers gives

\overline{W}_{N}\to Z,\qquad\overline{W^{2}}_{N}\to Z^{2}\left(1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})\right)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(57)

The limiting denominator is strictly positive because
Z>0
. Hence, on the almost-sure event where both limits hold,

\frac{\operatorname{ESS}_{N}}{N}=\frac{\left(N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}\right)^{2}}{N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}^{2}}=\frac{\overline{W}_{N}^{2}}{\overline{W^{2}}_{N}}\to\frac{1}{1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})}.

(58)

The finite-
N
convention
\operatorname{ESS}_{N}=0
when
\sum_{i}W_{i}^{2}=0
does not affect the limit because
\overline{W}_{N}\to Z>0
. From Eq. (
43
),

\frac{1}{1+\chi^{2}(\Pi\|Q_{\lambda})}\geq\lambda\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)^{2}]}.

(59)

Equations (
58
)–(
59
) are exactly the ESS portion of Eq. (
4
).

Variance-budget certificate.

Define

\rho:=\frac{M_{2}}{Z^{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[g(X)]^{2}}.

(60)

Because
P
is a probability measure and
g\in L^{2}(P)
, Jensen’s inequality gives
M_{2}\geq Z^{2}
. Thus
\rho\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\bar{\rho}\geq\rho
be a certified upper bound. From Eq. (
54
),

\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t})}{Z^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\rho}{\lambda}-1\right)\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\lambda}-1\right).

(61)

Therefore the guide-independent bound certifies

\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{Z}^{(N)}\mid h_{t})}{Z^{2}}\leq\tau^{2}

(62)

whenever

\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\lambda}-1\right)\leq\tau^{2}.

(63)

This is equivalent to

\lambda\geq\frac{\bar{\rho}}{1+N\tau^{2}}.

(64)

If
\bar{\rho}\leq 1+N\tau^{2}
, then the right-hand side of Eq. (
64
) is at most one, so every

\lambda\in\left[\frac{\bar{\rho}}{1+N\tau^{2}},1\right]

(65)

certifies the target budget. If
\bar{\rho}>1+N\tau^{2}
, then no
\lambda\in(0,1]
can certify the target using this guide-independent upper bound. The function

\lambda\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\lambda}-1\right)

(66)

is decreasing on
(0,1]
, so the best certified bound from this inequality is attained at
\lambda=1
and equals

\frac{1}{N}(\bar{\rho}-1).

(67)

The sufficient condition Eq. (
64
), together with feasibility
\bar{\rho}/(1+N\tau^{2})\leq 1
, is the main-text budget rule Eq. (
5
).

Density-level version.

This paragraph is an implementation-level SI extension of Theorem
1
; it is not needed to state the compact main-text theorem, but it justifies the density formula used in code. Assume that
P
and
Q
admit tractable densities
p
and
q
with respect to a common
\sigma
-finite reference measure
\mu

Q_{\lambda}
admits the density

q_{\lambda}(x)=(1-\lambda)q(x)+\lambda p(x).

(68)

Since

q_{\lambda}(x)\geq\lambda p(x)\qquad\mu\text{-a.e.},

(69)

we have, with the convention
0/0:=0
,

\frac{p(x)}{q_{\lambda}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\qquad q_{\lambda}\text{-a.e.}

(70)

Moreover, for any measurable

\int_{A}\frac{p(x)}{q_{\lambda}(x)}q_{\lambda}(x)\,\mu(dx)=\int_{A}p(x)\,\mu(dx)=P(A),

(71)

so
p/q_{\lambda}
is a valid density-level version of
dP/dQ_{\lambda}
. Therefore the density-level importance weight

W(x)=g(x)\frac{p(x)}{q_{\lambda}(x)}

(72)

agrees with the measure-theoretic weight above up to
Q_{\lambda}
-null sets.

State-dependent or adaptive
\lambda

This is another SI extension of the compact theorem. The preceding argument was conditional on
h_{t}
and on a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1]

is chosen as an

-measurable, possibly random quantity before drawing

The result is therefore a one-step conditional statement for the proposal-and-weighting increment. In an IW/FIVO/SMC implementation, it applies to each particle ancestor after conditioning on that ancestor and on the information available before sampling the next particle.

B.2
Proof of Theorem
2
: Exponential concentration of predictive modes

Proof.

The main text defines the clamped-mode posterior in Eq. (
6
) and the predictive log-ratio in Eq. (
7
). We prove the sharp posterior bound Eq. (
8
), its simplified exponential form Eq. (
9
), and the variational transfer bound Eq. (
10
).

All probabilities and expectations are taken under the true data-generating law
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
for the considered constant-mode segment, conditional on the realized action process when actions are treated as interventions. Fix a wrong mode
s\neq m
and define

S_{L,s}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}R_{\ell,s},

(73)

where
R_{\ell,s}
is the main-text log-ratio in Eq. (
7
). By definition,

R_{\ell,s}=\mu_{\ell,s}+\xi_{\ell,s},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\star}[\xi_{\ell,s}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell}]=0.

(74)

Moreover, by the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption,

\mathbb{E}_{\star}\!\left[\exp\{t\xi_{\ell,s}\}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\right]\leq\exp\!\left(\frac{t^{2}\sigma_{\ell,s}^{2}}{2}\right)\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

(75)

The variable
\xi_{\ell,s}
is measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
and the next observation
O_{\ell+1}
, hence with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}
under the filtration above. Therefore the partial sums

M_{j,s}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\xi_{\ell,s},\qquad j=0,\ldots,L,

(76)

form a martingale with conditionally sub-Gaussian increments.

We first prove a lower-tail inequality for
S_{L,s}
. By cumulative predictive separation,

\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\mu_{\ell,s}\geq\Gamma_{L,s}\qquad\text{almost surely}.

(77)

Hence, for any
u>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[S_{L,s}\leq\Gamma_{L,s}-u\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}(\mu_{\ell,s}+\xi_{\ell,s})\leq\Gamma_{L,s}-u\right]

(78)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\xi_{\ell,s}\leq-u\right].

Assume first that
V_{s}>0
. For any
\eta>0
, Markov’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\xi_{\ell,s}\leq-u\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[\exp\left\{-\eta\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\xi_{\ell,s}\right\}\geq e^{\eta u}\right]

(79)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\eta u}\mathbb{E}_{\star}\!\left[\exp\left\{-\eta\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\xi_{\ell,s}\right\}\right].

Using the tower property and applying Eq. (
75
) backwards from
\ell=L-1
to
\ell=0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\star}\!\left[\exp\left\{-\eta\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\xi_{\ell,s}\right\}\right]\leq\exp\left(\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sigma_{\ell,s}^{2}\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\eta^{2}V_{s}}{2}\right).

(80)

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[S_{L,s}\leq\Gamma_{L,s}-u\right]\leq\exp\left(-\eta u+\frac{\eta^{2}V_{s}}{2}\right).

(81)

Optimizing over
\eta>0
with
\eta=u/V_{s}
yields

\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[S_{L,s}\leq\Gamma_{L,s}-u\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2V_{s}}\right).

(82)

If
V_{s}=0
, then
\sigma_{\ell,s}=0
for every
\ell
. In this case, for any fixed nonzero
t
, Jensen’s inequality and Eq. (
75
) imply

1=\exp\!\left(t\,\mathbb{E}_{\star}[\xi_{\ell,s}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell}]\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\star}[\exp\{t\xi_{\ell,s}\}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell}]\leq 1.

(83)

Thus equality holds in Jensen’s inequality. Since the exponential function is strictly convex,
\xi_{\ell,s}
must be conditionally almost surely constant given
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
. Its conditional mean is zero, so
\xi_{\ell,s}=0
almost surely. Consequently,

(84)

Thus the desired lower bound also holds in the degenerate case
V_{s}=0
.

Now define, for every
s\neq m
,

u_{s}:=\sqrt{2V_{s}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}.

(85)

For modes with
V_{s}>0
, Eq. (
82
) gives

\mathbb{P}^{\star}\!\left[S_{L,s}\leq\Gamma_{L,s}-u_{s}\right]\leq\frac{\delta}{M-1}.

(86)

For modes with
V_{s}=0
, the event
S_{L,s}\geq\Gamma_{L,s}-u_{s}
holds almost surely because
u_{s}=0
. Hence, by a union bound over the
M-1
wrong modes, with probability at least
1-\delta
, the event

\mathcal{E}:=\left\{S_{L,s}\geq\Gamma_{L,s}-\sqrt{2V_{s}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\quad\forall s\neq m\right\}

(87)

holds.

On
\mathcal{E}
, Bayes’ rule and the constant-segment posterior from Eq. (
6
) give, for each
s\neq m
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Pi_{L}(S=s)}{\Pi_{L}(S=m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{s}\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-1}p_{s}(O_{\ell+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell})}{\pi_{m}\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-1}p_{m}(O_{\ell+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell})}

(88)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{s}}{\pi_{m}}\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\log\frac{p_{m}(O_{\ell+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell})}{p_{s}(O_{\ell+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\ell})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{s}}{\pi_{m}}\exp(-S_{L,s})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(B_{s}-\Gamma_{L,s}+\sqrt{2V_{s}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\right).

Therefore,

\sum_{s\neq m}\frac{\Pi_{L}(S=s)}{\Pi_{L}(S=m)}\leq\sum_{s\neq m}\exp\left(B_{s}-\Gamma_{L,s}+\sqrt{2V_{s}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\right)=A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}.

(89)

Let

T_{L}:=\sum_{s\neq m}\frac{\Pi_{L}(S=s)}{\Pi_{L}(S=m)}.

(90)

Since

1=\Pi_{L}(S=m)+\sum_{s\neq m}\Pi_{L}(S=s)=\Pi_{L}(S=m)(1+T_{L}),

(91)

we have

\Pi_{L}(S\neq m)=\sum_{s\neq m}\Pi_{L}(S=s)=\frac{T_{L}}{1+T_{L}}.

(92)

On
\mathcal{E}
,
T_{L}\leq A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}
. Since
x\mapsto x/(1+x)
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
,

\Pi_{L}(S\neq m)\leq\frac{A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}}{1+A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}}\leq A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}.

(93)

This is exactly the sharp exact-posterior recovery bound in Eq. (
8
).

We now prove the simplified bound. If

\Gamma_{L,s}\geq L\Delta,\qquad B_{s}\leq B,\qquad V_{s}\leq V_{\max},

(94)

for all
s\neq m
, then

\displaystyle A_{L,\delta}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\neq m}\exp\left(B_{s}-\Gamma_{L,s}+\sqrt{2V_{s}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\right)

(95)

\displaystyle\leq(M-1)\exp\left(B-L\Delta+\sqrt{2V_{\max}\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\right)=A_{L,\delta}.

Since
x\mapsto x/(1+x)
is increasing,

\Pi_{L}(S\neq m)\leq\frac{A_{L,\delta}}{1+A_{L,\delta}}\leq A_{L,\delta}.

(96)

If additionally
\sigma_{\ell,s}\leq\sigma
for all
\ell
and
s\neq m

V_{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sigma_{\ell,s}^{2}\leq L\sigma^{2},

(97)

so

, which gives

\Pi_{L}(S\neq m)\leq(M-1)\exp\left(B-L\Delta+\sigma\sqrt{2L\log\frac{M-1}{\delta}}\right).

(98)

This is the main-text simplified bound Eq. (
9
).

It remains to transfer the bound from the constant-segment model posterior
\Pi_{L}
to the amortized variational posterior
q_{\phi}
. Suppose that, on the realized observations,

\mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_{\phi}(S\mid O_{1:L},A_{0:L-1})\,\middle\|\,\Pi_{L}(S)\right)\leq\varepsilon_{q}.

(99)

Using the convention

\operatorname{TV}(P,Q):=\sup_{\mathcal{A}}|P(\mathcal{A})-Q(\mathcal{A})|,

(100)

Pinsker’s inequality gives

\operatorname{TV}(q_{\phi},\Pi_{L})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{KL}(q_{\phi}\|\Pi_{L})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{q}}{2}}.

(101)

Apply this to the event
\mathcal{A}:=\{S\neq m\}
. Then

q_{\phi}(\mathcal{A})\leq\Pi_{L}(\mathcal{A})+\operatorname{TV}(q_{\phi},\Pi_{L}).

(102)

Combining Eq. (

(103)

This is exactly the variational transfer statement in Eq. (
10

gives the simplified variational bound

q_{\phi}(S\neq m\mid O_{1:L},A_{0:L-1})\leq A_{L,\delta}+\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{q}}{2}}.

(104)

Finally, if the KL condition in Eq. (

\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (

. This last union-bound variant is a useful SI extension of the compact theorem statement.

Proof of Theorem

Q_{a}

(139)

(141)

For

and a predictable action value

, the infinitesimal generator corresponding to Eq. (

\mathcal{L}_{m_{j}}^{a_{t}}H(z)=\nabla H(z)^{\top}\Big[(J-R)\nabla H(z)+Ga_{t}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma\nabla^{2}H(z)\right).

The skew-symmetry of

\nabla H(z)^{\top}J\nabla H(z)=0.

(143)

Since

,

-\nabla H(z)^{\top}R\nabla H(z)\leq-r\|\nabla H(z)\|_{2}^{2}.

The input term is bounded by Cauchy’s inequality and Young’s inequality:

\nabla H(z)^{\top}Ga_{t}\leq\|\nabla H(z)\|_{2}\,\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|a_{t}\|_{2}\leq gA\|\nabla H(z)\|_{2}\leq\frac{r}{2}\|\nabla H(z)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{g^{2}A^{2}}{2r}.

For the diffusion term, because
\Sigma\succeq 0
,
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\leq\nu^{2}

(146)

Combining Eqs. (
142

146
) yields

\mathcal{L}_{m_{j}}^{a_{t}}H(z)\leq-\frac{r}{2}\|\nabla H(z)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{g^{2}A^{2}}{2r}+\frac{L_{H}\nu^{2}}{2}.

(147)

\mathcal{L}_{m_{j}}^{a_{t}}U(z)=\mathcal{L}_{m_{j}}^{a_{t}}H(z).

Therefore, for all

\alpha

(151)

At a genuine switching time
\tau_{j}

\Delta_{j}:=U_{s_{\tau_{j}}}(z_{\tau_{j}})-U_{s_{\tau_{j}^{-}}}(z_{\tau_{j}^{-}}).

(152)

By the passive-switch assumption,

\Delta_{j}\leq 0\qquad\text{a.s.}

(153)

T

\Delta_{j}\leq 0

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})]\leq\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{0}}(z_{0})]+\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\alpha\,\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{t}}(z_{t})]+C_{E}\right)dt.

(155)

Define

\psi(t):=\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{t}}(z_{t})].

(156)

Then Eq. (
155
) reads

\psi(T)\leq\psi(0)+\int_{0}^{T}(-\alpha\psi(t)+C_{E})dt.

(157)

By the integral form of Gronwall’s inequality,

\psi(T)\leq e^{-\alpha T}\psi(0)+\frac{1-e^{-\alpha T}}{\alpha}C_{E}.

(158)

Substituting back the definition of
\psi
proves

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})]\leq e^{-\alpha T}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{0}}(z_{0})]+\frac{1-e^{-\alpha T}}{\alpha}C_{E},

(159)

which is the main-text energy certificate Eq. (
19
).

The following tail bound is more detailed than the compact main-text statement but is useful for experiments that measure high-energy failures. Define

\overline{U}_{T}(a):=e^{-\alpha T}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{0}}(z_{0})]+\frac{1-e^{-\alpha T}}{\alpha}C_{E}.

(160)

Since
U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})\geq 0
, Markov’s inequality gives, for every
B>0
,

Q_{a}\!\left(U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})\geq B\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})]}{B}\leq\frac{\overline{U}_{T}(a)}{B}.

(161)

We now prove the KL-robust risk and MPC claims. Let

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q):=\sup_{\mathcal{B}}|P(\mathcal{B})-Q(\mathcal{B})|

(162)

denote total variation distance. By Pinsker’s inequality and the uniform predictive calibration assumption,

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{a}^{\star},Q_{a})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{a}^{\star}\|Q_{a})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{H}}{2}}.

(163)

Since total variation is always at most one,

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{a}^{\star},Q_{a})\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{H}}{2}}\right\}\qquad\forall a\in\mathcal{A}.

(164)

Therefore, for every measurable event
\mathcal{F}
,

P_{a}^{\star}(\mathcal{F})\leq Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})+d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{a}^{\star},Q_{a})\leq Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})+\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}.

(165)

Applying this to the high-energy event
\mathcal{E}_{B}=\{U_{s_{T}}(z_{T})\geq B\}
and using Eq. (
161
) gives the SI-level true high-energy certificate

P_{a}^{\star}(\mathcal{E}_{B})\leq\min\left\{1,\frac{\overline{U}_{T}(a)}{B}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}\right\}.

(166)

This bound is additional theory beyond the compact theorem statement, but it follows immediately from Eq. (
19
) and the KL calibration assumption.

Next, let
C(\tau)\in[0,C_{\max}]
be any bounded measurable planning cost. Since
C/C_{\max}\in[0,1]
, the variational characterization of total variation gives

\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C(\tau)]-\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[C(\tau)]\right|\leq C_{\max}\,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{a}^{\star},Q_{a})\leq C_{\max}\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}\qquad\forall a\in\mathcal{A}.

(167)

Using Eq. (
167
) for
\widehat{a}
gives

\mathbb{E}_{P_{\widehat{a}}^{\star}}[C]\leq\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\widehat{a}}}[C]+C_{\max}\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}.

(168)

By
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
-optimality of
\widehat{a}
under the
VHyDRO
predictive law,

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\widehat{a}}}[C]\leq\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[C]+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}.

(169)

Moreover, for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
, Eq. (
167
) also gives

\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[C]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C]+C_{\max}\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}.

(170)

Taking the infimum over
a\in\mathcal{A}
yields

\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{a}}[C]\leq\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C]+C_{\max}\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}.

(171)

Combining Eqs. (
168
), (
169
), and (
171
) gives

\mathbb{E}_{P_{\widehat{a}}^{\star}}[C]-\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C]\leq 2C_{\max}\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}},

(172)

which is the main-text MPC bound Eq. (
20
). Since
\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{H}/2}
, Eq. (
172
) also implies the slightly looser form

\mathbb{E}_{P_{\widehat{a}}^{\star}}[C]-\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C]\leq 2C_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{H}}{2}}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}.

(173)

If the true infimum is attained by
a^{\star}
, then

\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{a}^{\star}}[C]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{a^{\star}}^{\star}}[C],

(174)

which gives the oracle-comparison form.

For the deterministic chance constraint, let
\mathcal{F}
be any measurable failure event. If

Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})\leq\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}},\qquad\delta\in[\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}},1],

(175)

then Eq. (
165
) gives

P_{a}^{\star}(\mathcal{F})\leq Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})+\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\delta.

(176)

This is the deterministic implication in Eq. (
21
).

It remains to prove the rollout certificate and connect it to Eq. (
22
). Let

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}=\{a^{1},\ldots,a^{L}\}

(177)

be the finite evaluated action set. For each
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}
, define

Y_{a,i}:=\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{a}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{F}\},\qquad\tau_{a}^{(i)}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}Q_{a}.

(178)

Then
Y_{a,i}\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{E}[Y_{a,i}]=Q_{a}(\mathcal{F}),\qquad\widehat{Q}_{a,n}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{a,i}.

(179)

For a fixed
a
, Hoeffding’s inequality gives

\Pr\left(Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})>\widehat{Q}_{a,n}(\mathcal{F})+\epsilon_{n}(L,\beta)\right)\leq\exp\!\left(-2n\epsilon_{n}(L,\beta)^{2}\right)=\frac{\beta}{L},

(180)

where

\epsilon_{n}(L,\beta):=\sqrt{\frac{\log(L/\beta)}{2n}}.

(181)

Taking a union bound over the
L
evaluated actions yields, with probability at least
1-\beta
, the simultaneous event

Q_{a}(\mathcal{F})\leq\widehat{Q}_{a,n}(\mathcal{F})+\epsilon_{n}(L,\beta)\qquad\forall a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}.

(182)

On this simultaneous event, if the possibly data-dependent selected action
\widetilde{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eval}}
satisfies

\widehat{Q}_{\widetilde{a},n}(\mathcal{F})\leq\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}-\epsilon_{n}(L,\beta),

(183)

then

Q_{\widetilde{a}}(\mathcal{F})\leq\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}.

(184)

Applying the deterministic chance certificate in Eq. (
21
) to
\widetilde{a}
gives

P_{\widetilde{a}}^{\star}(\mathcal{F})\leq\delta.

(185)

Equations (
181
)–(
185
) prove the Monte Carlo margin statement Eq. (
22
), completing the proof of Theorem
4
.
