Let

\widetilde{X}
is called the inverse of
\widetilde{A}

\widetilde{A}^{-1}

\widetilde{X}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times m}

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}\widetilde{X}\widetilde{A}=\widetilde{A},\ \ \widetilde{X}\widetilde{A}\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{X},\ \ (\widetilde{A}\widetilde{X})^{\top}=\widetilde{A}\widetilde{X},\ \ (\widetilde{X}\widetilde{A})^{\top}=\widetilde{X}\widetilde{A},

(2.5)

then

is called the Moore-Penrose inverse of

If the Moore-Penrose inverse of a hyper-dual number matrix exists, it is unique
[
32
]

In order to derive the resistance distance and Kirchhoff index of a hyper-dual number weighted graph, we next give the existence and explicit representation of solutions to hyper-dual linear equations.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\widetilde{A}\in\mathbb{H}^{m\times n}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathbb{H}^{m}
. Suppose that

exists. Then the following statements hold.

(a)
The solution to the hyper-dual equation

\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
exists if and only if

\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}.

(b)
If the solution to the hyper-dual equation
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
exists, then its general solution is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}+(I-\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{A})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},

(2.6)

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is arbitrary.

Proof.

(a)
Suppose
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
is a solution to
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
. Then

\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=(\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}})\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}.

Conversely, if

\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
, it is clear that
\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
is a solution to
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
.

(b)
From
(a)
, we have
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
. For any
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{H}^{m}
,

\widetilde{A}(\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}+(I-\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{A})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}})=\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}.

Hence,
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}+(I-\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{A})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}
is a solution to
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}

On the other hand, let

\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
be a solution to
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}

Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}+\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}-\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}+(I-\widetilde{A}^{{\dagger}}\widetilde{A})\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}.

Therefore, every solution to
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
can be written in the form Eq. (
2.6
).
∎

(c)
From part
