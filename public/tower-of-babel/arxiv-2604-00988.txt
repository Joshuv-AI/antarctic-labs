A Discontinuous Galerkin Scheme for the Cahn-Hilliard Equations with Discrete Maximum Principle for Arbitrary Polynomial Order Thanks: Submitted to the editors August 10, 2026.
Jimmy Kornelije Gunnarsson, Robert Klöfkorn

Abstract
We propose a structure-preserving discontinuous Galerkin scheme for the Cahn–Hilliard equations with degenerate mobility based on the Symmetric Weighted Interior Penalty formulation. By evaluating the mobility at cell averages rather than as a piecewise polynomial, the proposed scheme preserves strict degeneracy and yields a coercivity constant that is independent of the mobility, removing the need for regularisation. Moreover, we establish existence of discrete solutions even with degeneracy via a Leray–Schauder fixed-point argument, and show that the scheme satisfies a provable discrete maximum principle at arbitrary polynomial order p p when combined with the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter for p > 0 p>0 and from the scheme alone for p = 0 p=0 . Mass conservation and energy dissipation are established for the unlimited scheme; for the limited variant, we discuss observed energy dissipation for p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 and potential theoretical solutions. Numerical experiments confirm optimal convergence rates of order p + 1 p+1 in L 2 L^{2} and validate structure-preserving properties with numerical results.

Introduction

Consider the Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\{1,2,3\}
with boundary

\partial\Omega
and time domain
[0,T]
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
; for convenience,
we write
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times[0,T]
. The Cahn–Hilliard (CH) equations
for a phase-field
\psi:\Omega_{T}\to[-1,1]
read:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\nabla\cdot(M(\psi)\nabla\upsilon),

(1)

\displaystyle\upsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(\psi)-\mathrm{Cn}^{2}\Delta\psi,

(2)

with initial and boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\psi(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{0},

(3)

\displaystyle\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(4)

\displaystyle M(\psi)\nabla\upsilon\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(5)

where
\upsilon
is the chemical potential,
\mathrm{Pe}>0
is the Péclet
number,
\mathrm{Cn}>0
is the Cahn number,
M(\psi)=\max\{1-\psi^{2},0\}
is the degenerate
mobility, and
W
is a double-well potential. There are two popular choices for the
double-well potential, one is the quartic potential
[
1
,
24
,
23
]
:

W(\psi)=\frac{1}{4}(\psi^{2}-1)^{2},

(6)

while the original formulation is the logarithmic potential
[
15
]
,

W(\psi)=\frac{\theta}{2}((1+\psi)\ln(1+\psi)+(1-\psi)\ln(1-\psi))-\frac{\theta_{c}}{2}(1-\psi^{2}),

(7)

for temperatures
\theta_{c}>\theta>0
. For the logarithmic potential in Eq. (
7
),

\psi
is confined to
(-1,1)

[
15
]
, whereas for the quartic potential
in Eq. (
6
)
the solution is not a priori bounded, since fourth-order equations do not admit a
strong maximum
principle
[
15
]
,
[
14
, Remark 3.5]

(in contrast to second-order in space problems such as the heat equation
[
28
]

or the Allen–Cahn equation

[
27
]
). Consequently, discrete formulations of the CH equations demand
carefully constructed numerical schemes that enforce a weak maximum principle for
quartic potentials
[
23
]
in the hope of satisfying a weak maximum principle
as proven in
[
15
, Theorem 1]
, namely for the quartic potential in Eq. (
6
)
alongside the mobility
M
for
\psi^{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
|\psi^{0}|\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega
then
|\psi(\cdot,t)|\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega\times[0,T]
. Further analysis
in the weak case has also been carried out in
[
10
]
.
Constructing provable bound-preserving schemes is, in general, a highly nontrivial
task, as highlighted in
[
14
, Remark 3.5]
and references therein.
As demonstrated in
[
1
,
20
,
23
]
the theorem in
[
15
, Theorem
1]
does not translate directly to standard discretization methods such
as the Finite Element Method (FEM) and the Symmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (SIPG)
discretization, both of which violate a discrete maximum principle in numerical simulations.
The limited variants FEM-L and SIPG-L introduced in
[
23
]
restore
boundedness in numerical experiments, yet neither is supported by a formal proof.
For a discrete maximum principle,
only recently have rigorous results emerged, either through upwind flux design
[
1
]

or through limiter-based approaches
[
21
,
25
]
for the quartic case.
Maximum principle schemes have also been developed for the logarithmic potential
[
8
]
.
We also note that the analysis in
[
15
]
does not account for advection;
this has since been addressed at the discrete level in
[
1
]
by means
of upwinding and piecewise constant basis functions, and we discuss this extension
in Remark
20
.

A key limitation of, for instance, the FEM-L, SWIP-L and SIPG-L schemes in
[
23
]

is that their fluxes across cell intersections do not readily ensure a discrete maximum
principle for the cell averages. In particular, the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter employed
in
[
24
,
23
]
(and as a second step in
[
25
]
)
requires that the cell averages themselves satisfy a discrete maximum principle.
This motivates the use of the Symmetric Weighted Interior
Penalty (SWIP) formulation, which strictly imposes zero flux on intersections
where the cell average attains a global maximum or minimum by exploiting the degeneracy
of the mobility
M
. The SWIP discretization was originally discussed in
[
7
]
for isotropic diffusion
and subsequently generalized to the anisotropic setting in
[
17
]
. A key
assumption in
[
17
]
is to consider a piecewise constant diffusion tensor.
Although the SWIP discretization was briefly discussed in
[
20
]
and
for the first time applied to the CH equations in
[
24
,
23
]
,
the prescribed formulations in the latter employ a piecewise polynomial mobility evaluation
that necessitates a regularisation assumption. This requirement can be circumvented
by evaluating the mobility at the cell averages, thereby permitting strict piecewise
degeneracy. As demonstrated in Theorem
19
,
this modification enables a provable discrete maximum principle at the cell-average
level, and thus, also for the phase-field approximation following a scaling limiter
discussed in Section
4.1
.
In the present work, we propose a novel DG formulation – the SWIP Diffusive Projection (SWIPDP) scheme combined
with the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter (SWIPDP-L) – that provably satisfies a discrete
maximum principle for the CH equations at arbitrary polynomial order (Theorem
19
).
We establish existence of solutions for the unlimited SWIPDP scheme and, by virtue
of an energy estimate, for the limited variant as well. This guarantee comes at the
expense of not necessarily ensuring monotone energy dissipation for
p>0
,
although this property is consistently observed in numerical experiments. The resulting
scheme furnishes a robust framework for simulating the CH equations, including advective
extensions, and can be naturally incorporated into coupled formulations as in
[
24
,
23
]
.

2
Discretization

In this section, we present the spatial and temporal discretization of the CH equation
system. The primary objective is to motivate the design of the SWIPDP-L scheme and
to establish
coercivity of the resulting trilinear form, boundedness, and
energy dissipation. The overall coercivity proof and the remaining details follow
a similar procedure to that in
[
23
]
.

2.1
Notation

Let the spatial domain
\Omega
be partitioned into a union of
M
non-intersecting
elements
K
forming a mesh
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\cup_{i=1}^{M}K_{i}
. Then we denote
by
\Gamma_{i}
with unit normal
\boldsymbol{n}
the set of all intersections between
two
elements of the grid
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, and the set of all
intersections, also with the boundary of the domain
\Omega
, is denoted by

\Gamma
.
For an intersection
e\in\Gamma
we denote the adjacent elements with
K^{-}_{e}

and

K^{+}_{e}
(
K^{-}_{e}=K^{+}_{e}
for
e\in\Gamma\cap\Gamma_{i}
)
and
define
h_{e}
as the harmonic mean of the
neighboring element areas divided by the edge length:

h_{e}:=\frac{2|K^{+}_{e}||K^{-}_{e}|}{|e|(|K^{-}_{e}|+|K^{+}_{e}|)}\quad\forall e\in\Gamma.

(8)

The global mesh width is then defined as
h=\max_{e\in\Gamma}h_{e}
.
Moreover, we make a regularity assumption on the mesh
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
in the sense that we
will only consider meshes which are shape-regular.
Such regularity is achieved, for instance, by quadrilaterals and right-angled isosceles
triangles for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and by hexahedra and right-angled isosceles tetrahedra
for

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, the theorems we present hold for general meshes with minor modifications.
For the time discretization we consider a uniform partition of the time interval

[0,T]
with time increment
\tau=\frac{T}{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
denote the time levels by
t_{n}=n\cdot\tau
for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
. For brevity,
we also denote time-dependent variables by a subscript
n
, i.e.,
\psi^{n}=\psi(\cdot,t_{n})
.

Following standard FEM notation, we consider a general-order FEM formulation
for the space of trial and test functions:

V_{h}^{p}=\{\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}}):\varphi|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(K),\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\},

(9)

where
\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)
denotes a polynomial space of order at most
p

on the
element
K
. Note that this makes
V_{h}^{p}
a broken polynomial space over
the grid
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. We also introduce the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{K}

for
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
as

\left\langle\varphi,\phi\right\rangle_{K}=\int_{K}\varphi\phi\,d\boldsymbol{x},\qquad\left\langle\varphi,\phi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left\langle\varphi,\phi\right\rangle_{K},

(10)

for scalar functions
\varphi,\phi\in V_{h}^{p}
,
inducing the
L^{2}
-norm
\left\langle\varphi,\varphi\right\rangle_{K}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}
.
For brevity, we omit the subscript
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
in the inner product when it is clear
from context.
We introduce the
operators
[{\cdot}]_{e},\{{\cdot}\}_{e}
, and

\langle{\cdot}\rangle_{e}
for

e\in\Gamma_{i}
as

\begin{split}[{\varphi}]_{e}=\varphi_{|{K^{-}_{e}}}-\varphi_{|{K^{+}_{e}}},\qquad\{{\varphi}\}_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi_{|{K^{-}_{e}}}+\varphi_{|{K^{+}_{e}}}\right),\qquad\langle{\varphi}\rangle_{e}=\frac{2\,\varphi_{|{K^{+}_{e}}}\,\varphi_{|{K^{-}_{e}}}}{\varphi_{|{K^{+}_{e}}}+\varphi_{|{K^{-}_{e}}}},\\
\end{split}

(11)

for some
\varphi
, where
[{\cdot}]_{e}
,
\{{\cdot}\}_{e}
, and
\langle{\cdot}\rangle_{e}

denote the jump, arithmetic average,
and harmonic average across intersection
e
, respectively. For brevity, we drop
the subscript
e
for those and adopt the notation
\varphi^{\pm}:=\varphi_{|K_{e}^{\pm}}
.
Moreover, we introduce the notation
\varphi_{\oplus}:=\max{\{0,\varphi\}}

and
\varphi_{\ominus}:=\min{\{0,\varphi\}}
to denote the positive and negative
parts of a function, respectively. This notation is employed in particular for
upwinding.
Finally, we introduce the following notation for the set of
functions in
L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
that take values in
[-1,1]
a.e. in
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
:

L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}}):=\{\varphi\in L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}}):\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1\},

(12)

and denote set membership in
L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
by the following notation:

\|\varphi\|_{L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}=\begin{cases}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}&\text{if }\varphi\in L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}}),\\
\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(13)

and we say that
\varphi
is bounded in
L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})

if
\|\varphi\|_{L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}<\infty
.

2.2
Discontinuous Galerkin

We proceed by constructing a general formulation for the spatial discretization that
is suitable for practical implementation.

Definition 1
(Cell–averaged projection).

Consider
\varphi\in V_{h}^{p}
and
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. We define the cell–averaged
projections
\Pi_{K}^{0}:\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)\to\mathbb{P}^{0}(K)
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}:V_{h}^{p}\to V_{h}^{0}
as

\Pi_{K}^{0}\varphi=|K|^{-1}\int_{K}\varphi\,d\boldsymbol{x},\qquad\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\Pi_{K}^{0}\varphi\,\chi_{K}(\boldsymbol{x}),

(14)

where

\chi_{K}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\boldsymbol{x}\in K,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(15)

Lemma 2
(Orthogonality).

Suppose that we have an orthogonal basis
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
with respect to
the L
2
inner product for
\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)
for each
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
such
that
\left\langle\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\right\rangle_{K}=\delta_{ij}|K|
and
\varphi_{0}=1
. Then for any
\phi\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)
such that
\phi=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}\varphi_{j}
we have that
\Pi_{K}^{0}\phi=c_{0}
and
\left\langle\phi-\Pi_{K}^{0}\phi,\varphi_{0}\right\rangle_{K}=0
.

Proof.

We prove the first statement. By definition,

\Pi_{K}^{0}\phi=|K|^{-1}\int_{K}\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}\varphi_{j}\,d\boldsymbol{x}=|K|^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}\left\langle\varphi_{j},\varphi_{0}\right\rangle_{K}=c_{0}.

(16)

The second statement follows immediately from the orthogonality of the basis.

Remark 3
(Zero-mean decomposition).

As a consequence of Lemma
2
, any
\varphi\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(K)

can be decomposed into a cell-averaged part
\overline{\psi_{K}}=\Pi_{K}^{0}\varphi
and a
zero-mean part
\tilde{\varphi}_{K}=\varphi_{K}-\overline{\psi_{K}}
. More generally,
we write
\tilde{\varphi}=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\tilde{\varphi}_{K}\chi_{K}

and note that
\tilde{\varphi}\in V_{h}^{p}\setminus V_{h}^{0}
for
p>0
; recall that
V_{h}^{p}
is a broken polynomial space since continuity across
cell interfaces is not imposed.

Let
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
denote a fixed discrete phase-field. Following the formulation
in

[
17
]
, we introduce a piecewise constant mobility
M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})

that serves as an isotropic piecewise degenerate diffusion coefficient. Note that

M(\overline{\psi_{K}})\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(K)

and
M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})\in L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
by definition. In the spirit of
the SWIP formulations in
[
24
,
23
]
, we introduce
the trilinear form
b:L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})\times V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}),\upsilon_{h},\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})\,\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,d\boldsymbol{x}

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\Gamma}\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\left(\frac{\eta_{e}}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}}][{\varphi}]-\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}}\}[{\varphi}]-\{{\nabla\varphi}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]\right)ds,

where
\eta_{e}>0
is a penalty parameter over
e\in\Gamma_{i}

which we establish in Theorem
4
. Moreover, the flux
\langle{\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}}\rangle

follows from choosing the weights
w_{e}^{\pm}=\frac{M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})}{M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{+}}})+M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{-}}})}
using SWIP, as suggested in
[
17
]
for treating
a diffusion-type formulation
for DG.

Coercivity of
\tilde{b}(\cdot,\cdot)=b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}),\cdot,\cdot)
for a fixed

\psi_{h}
can only be established over the set

\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}:={\mathcal{T}_{h}}\setminus\{K:M(\overline{\psi_{K}})=0\}=\{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}:M(\overline{\psi_{K}})>0\},

(18)

and, without loss of generality, we assume that
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\neq\emptyset
. This
is a natural condition required to obtain meaningful bounds for the corresponding
constants.

Theorem 4
(Coercivity).

Let
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
be a fixed discrete phase-field and suppose that there
exists at least one intersection
e
with
|\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}}|<1
, so that
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\neq\emptyset
. Then the bilinear form
\tilde{b}(\cdot,\cdot):=b(M(\psi_{h}),\cdot,\cdot)

defined in Eq. (
17
)
is coercive over the space

\tilde{V}_{h}^{p}=\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}):\varphi|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(K),\forall K\in\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\}\setminus\{1\},

(19)

provided that the
penalty parameter
\eta_{e}>0
is chosen sufficiently large, independently of the mobility
M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})
.

Proof.

We consider the case with
p>0
. We introduce the weighted DG semi-norm:

\|\upsilon_{h}\|^{2}_{DG}:=\|\sqrt{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\frac{\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}}]^{2}ds,

(20)

and consider the inequality:

\tilde{b}(\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})\geq\tilde{C}\|\upsilon_{h}\|^{2}_{DG},\quad\forall\upsilon_{h}\in\tilde{V}_{h}^{p},

(21)

for some
\tilde{C}>0
. For the trilinear form Eq. (
17
) we have:

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})

\displaystyle=\|\sqrt{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\frac{\eta_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}}]^{2}ds

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}2\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]ds.

First, we bound the cross term
\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]ds
in absolute value. Applying the triangle inequality,
we obtain:

\left|\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]ds\right|\leq\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\left|\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]ds\right|,

(22)

and an application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives for
\beta\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}[{\upsilon_{h}}]ds|

(23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{1-\beta}\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{\beta}[{\upsilon_{h}}]\|_{L^{2}(e)},

(24)

for every
e\in\Gamma_{i}
. Then, by Young’s
inequality, we obtain the intersection-wise estimate:

\displaystyle 2\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{1-\beta}\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{\beta}[{\upsilon_{h}}]\|_{L^{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\leq h_{e}\epsilon_{e}\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{1-\beta}\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{e}h_{e}}\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{\beta}[{\upsilon_{h}}]\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2},

for an arbitrary
\epsilon_{e}>0
on each intersection

e\in\Gamma_{i}
. Then we seperate out the mobility

\|\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{1-\beta}\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}=\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{2(1-\beta)}\|\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2},

(25)

and expanding the average gives:

\|\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla\upsilon_{h}^{+}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+\|\nabla\upsilon_{h}^{-}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)

(26)

The trace inequality with
C_{T}:=\frac{k(k+d-1)}{d}

[
3
,
26
]

then gives:

\|\nabla\upsilon_{h}^{\pm}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{h_{e}}\|\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K_{e}^{\pm})}^{2}.

To recover the weighted volumetric term, we observe that since
M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})>0
for
K\in\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
we have that

\|\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}=\frac{\|\sqrt{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}}{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}.

(27)

We note that
\langle{M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})}\rangle\leq 2M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})
for
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
,
which yields the following limit

\lim_{|\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}}|\to 1}\frac{\langle{M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})}\rangle^{2(1-\beta)}}{M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})}\leq\lim_{|\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}}|\to 1}2M(\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}})^{1-2\beta},

(28)

This constrains our choices to
\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
for consistency,
while the limit
|\overline{\psi_{K}}|\to 1
removes the restriction to
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
.
For simplicity, we proceed with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
,
which removes the explicit dependence on the mobility
M(\cdot)
in the remaining
terms.
Since
M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})>0
for
K\in\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}

and the contributions from
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{\prime}=\{K:M(\overline{\psi_{K}})=0,K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\}
vanish,
we sum over
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
without loss of generality.
For the DG semi-norm in Eq. (
20
), we obtain
the following volumetric term estimate:

\|\sqrt{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\left(1-\sum_{e\in\partial K\cap\Gamma_{i}}C_{T}\epsilon_{e}\right)\geq 0\qquad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

(29)

The inequality in Eq. (
29
) is trivially satisfied for
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{\prime}
. For
K\in\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
, it is
strictly positive provided
\epsilon_{e}=\frac{1}{2m_{K_{e}}C_{T}}
, where

m_{K_{e}}=\sum_{e\in\partial K\cap\Gamma_{i}}1
. This choice
yields
for the penalty term:

\int_{e}\left(\frac{\eta_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle}{h_{e}}-\frac{\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle^{2\beta}}{2\epsilon_{e}h_{e}}\right)[{\upsilon_{h}}]^{2}ds\geq 0,

(30)

with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
as established above. Since there exists an intersection

e
with
|\overline{\psi_{K_{e}^{\pm}}}|\neq 1
by assumption, we have
\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle>0
for at least one intersection
e
, and we choose
\eta_{e}
such
that

\eta_{e}\geq\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{e}}=m_{K_{e}}C_{T},

(31)

in agreement with standard estimates for the penalty parameter (see
[
3
,
26
,
16
]
).
There then exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

b(M(\psi_{h}),\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})\geq C_{1}\|\sqrt{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\nabla\upsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}+C_{2}\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\frac{\eta_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}}]^{2}ds,

(32)

with
C_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
and
C_{2}=\underset{e\in\Gamma_{i}}{\max}\{\eta_{e}-m_{K_{e}}C_{T}\}
and
\tilde{C}=\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
we arrive at

b(M(\psi_{h}),\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})\geq\tilde{C}\|\upsilon_{h}\|^{2}_{DG},\quad\forall\upsilon_{h}\in\tilde{V}_{h}^{p},

(33)

which completes the proof.

The significance of Theorem
4
becomes evident in the derivation
of energy dissipation for the SWIPDP scheme in Theorem
10
, which
requires that
b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}),\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})
be semi-positive
for
\upsilon_{h},\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
. We note that this follows likewise
from Eq.(
33
) since, due to loss of coercivity over
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}^{\prime}
,
the inequality holds by taking
\eta=\eta_{e}
large enough (which is found with
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}={\mathcal{T}_{h}}
). In particular, since the constants in Eq. (
32
)
can be precomputed independently of the data by virtue of Theorem
4
,
the resulting estimate is robust owing to the weights. Moreover, as discussed in

[
23
, Remark 3.4]
, this formulation avoids the need to estimate
unknown ratios and ambiguities arising in
[
24
,
23
]

in the choice of the penalty parameter
\eta_{e}
.

Remark 5
(Semi-positivity of
\tilde{b}(\cdot,\cdot)
over
V_{h}^{0}
).

For the lowest polynomial order case,
p=0
, we have
\nabla\upsilon_{h}=0
on each element

K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. Consequently, the gradient terms vanish and the DG semi-norm in
Eq. (
33
) reduces to

\tilde{b}(\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})=\|\upsilon_{h}\|_{DG}^{2}=\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\frac{\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h})}\rangle}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}}]^{2}\,ds,\quad\forall\upsilon_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},

(34)

which vanishes for any globally constant
C_{R}\in V_{h}^{0}\cap\mathbb{R}
and locally whenever

M(\Pi_{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}^{0}\psi_{h})=0
.

Analogously to Eq. (
17
), we introduce the
bilinear form
a:V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
for the discretization
of the Laplacian operator in Eq. (
2
):

\displaystyle a(\psi_{h},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\nabla\psi_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\sum_{e\in\Gamma}\int_{e}\frac{\eta_{e}^{\Delta}}{h_{e}}[{\psi_{h}}][{\varphi}]

(35)

\displaystyle-\{{\nabla\psi_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}}\}[{\varphi}]-\{{\nabla\varphi\cdot\boldsymbol{n}}\}[{\psi_{h}}]ds,

where
\eta_{e}^{\Delta}\geq\eta_{e}
is a local penalty parameter for the
Laplacian operator to assure coercivity (following any standard SIPG formulation
in for instance
[
3
,
26
]
). Note that
a(\cdot,\cdot)=b(1,\cdot,\cdot)
, which reduces directly to a SIPG formulation; consequently,
coercivity of
a
follows by an analogous argument to that for
b
. The superscript

\Delta
distinguishes the penalty
parameter for the Laplacian from that of the mobility term in Eq. (
17
);
the two need not be equal, as discussed in
[
24
,
23
]
. However, we will proceed with
\eta_{e}^{\Delta}=\eta_{e}=\eta
with
\eta
sufficiently large.

3
Scheme

We now introduce the time discretization of our proposed scheme. To handle the nonlinear
potential
W
, we employ the Eyre splitting
[
19
]
:

\Phi^{+}(\psi)=\psi_{h}^{3},\quad\Phi^{-}(\psi)=-\psi_{h},

(36)

with
W^{\prime}(\psi_{h})=\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h})
. The SWIPDP scheme is to find
(\psi_{h}^{n+1},\upsilon_{h}^{n+1})\in V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left\langle\psi_{h}^{n+1}-\psi_{h}^{n},\varphi\right\rangle+\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n+1}),\upsilon_{h}^{n+1},\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(37)

\displaystyle\left\langle\upsilon_{h}^{n+1},\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{n+1})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{n}),\xi\right\rangle-\mathrm{Cn}^{2}a(\psi_{h}^{n+1},\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(38)

for all
\xi,\varphi\in V_{h}^{p}
as solutions at time
t=(n+1)\tau
. In the special case of
V_{h}^{0}
,
the SWIPD scheme introduced in
[
24
]
and SWIPDP coincide, since

\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}
is the identity map
on
V_{h}^{0}
.

In what follows, we establish several fundamental properties of the SWIPDP scheme
and demonstrate that any solution satisfies the corresponding physical constraints.
In particular, the energy dissipation established in Corollary
11

serves as an a priori estimate for the existence result.

Definition 6
(Phase-field mass).

We refer to the quantity

m_{\psi_{h}}=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{h}\,dx

(39)

as the phase-field mass.

Lemma 7
(Mass conservation).

Suppose that there exists a solution
(\psi_{h}^{n+1},\upsilon_{h}^{n+1})\in V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
to the SWIPDP scheme in Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
).
Then the phase-field mass is conserved in time, i.e.
m_{\psi_{h}^{n+1}}=m_{\psi_{h}^{n}}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Testing Eq. (
37
) with
\varphi=1
yields the desired result.

Lemma 8
(Coercivity domain).

The coercivity domain
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
defined in Eq. (
18
) is non-empty
for any
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
such that
m_{\psi_{h}}\in(-1,1)
.

Proof.

By Definition
6
,

m_{\psi_{h}}=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{h}\,dx=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\int_{K}\psi_{h}\,dx=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\frac{|K|}{|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}\overline{\psi_{K}},

(40)

so that
m_{\psi_{h}}\in(-1,1)
implies the existence of at least one cell
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
with
|\overline{\psi_{K}}|<1
, and hence
M(\overline{\psi_{K}})>0
, yielding
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\neq\emptyset
.

As a consequence of Lemma
8
, one can choose initial data

\psi_{h}^{0}
such that
m_{\psi_{h}^{0}}\in(-1,1)
to ensure that the coercivity domain is
non-empty. Moreover, this property persists at subsequent time steps by the mass
conservation established in Lemma
7
. For the remainder of this paper,
we assume that
\psi_{h}^{0}
is chosen so that
m_{\psi_{h}^{0}}\in(-1,1)
, which is expected with Theorem
19
.

3.1
Energy formulation and a priori bounds

In this subsection,
we derive a priori bounds exploiting the energy dissipation property of the SWIPDP scheme. We begin by introducing the following discrete energy functional.

Definition 9
(Discrete energy).

\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}]=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Cn}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}W(\psi_{h})d\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{\mathrm{Cn}^{2}}{2}a(\psi_{h},\psi_{h})\,

(41)

as the discrete energy.

With Definitions
1
and
9
in hand, we establish
the following discrete energy dissipation result for the SWIPDP scheme.

Theorem 10
(Discrete energy dissipation for SWIPDP).

Suppose that there exists a solution
(\psi_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\in V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
to the SWIPDP scheme
in Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
) at time
t=\tau
and that
a
is coercive and

\tilde{b}(\varphi,\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in V_{h}^{p}
. Then

\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{1}]\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}].

(42)

Proof.

Testing Eq. (
37
) with
\varphi=\upsilon_{h}^{1}
and
Eq. (
38
) with
\xi=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}(\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left\langle\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0},\upsilon_{h}^{1}\right\rangle+\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\,b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left\langle\upsilon_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle

(43)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{Cn}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pe}}\,a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Adding the two equations yields:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Cn}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pe}}\,a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\,b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\leq 0,

where the inequality follows from
\tilde{b}(\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\geq 0
.
Since
\Phi^{+}(\psi)=\psi^{3}
is convex and
\Phi^{-}(\psi)=-\psi
is concave,
we have

\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle\geq\left\langle W^{\prime}(\psi_{h}^{1}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle.

(45)

Substituting into Eq. (
44
),

\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left\langle W^{\prime}(\psi_{h}^{1}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle+\frac{\mathrm{Cn}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pe}}\,a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})\leq 0.

(46)

Next, we note that

\left\langle W(\psi_{h}^{1})-W(\psi_{h}^{0}),1\right\rangle\leq\left\langle W^{\prime}(\psi_{h}^{1}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle,

(47)

by convexity. Using
the polarization identity,

\displaystyle a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\psi_{h}^{0})=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1})-a(\psi_{h}^{0},\psi_{h}^{0})

(48)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a(\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1})-a(\psi_{h}^{0},\psi_{h}^{0})\bigr),

(49)

and multiplying Eq. (
46
) with
\frac{\mathrm{Pe}}{\mathrm{Cn}}
we arrive at the desired results following Definition
9
to complete
the proof.

Corollary 11
(Energy estimate).

Under the assumptions of Theorem
10
, a solution
(\psi_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})
to Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
)
satisfies

\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{1}]+\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}],

(50)

and, moreover
\tilde{C}\|\upsilon_{h}^{1}\|^{2}_{DG}\leq\mathrm{Pe}\,\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}]
.

Proof.

From Eq. (
44
),

\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1})-\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0}\right\rangle+\frac{\mathrm{Cn}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pe}}\,a(\psi_{h}^{1},\psi_{h}^{1}-\psi_{h}^{0})+\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\,b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})=0.

(51)

Applying the coercivity estimate
b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\geq\tilde{C}\,\|\upsilon_{h}^{1}\|^{2}_{DG}
from Theorem
4
, the Eyre
splitting inequality, and the polarization identity as in the proof of Theorem
10
,
we obtain Eq. (
50
). The bound on
\|\upsilon_{h}^{1}\|^{2}_{DG}
then
follows from
b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\geq\tilde{C}\,\|\upsilon_{h}^{1}\|^{2}_{DG}
together with
\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{1}]\geq 0
.

Lemma 12
(Uniform
L^{2}
-bound for
\psi_{h}^{1}
).

Under the assumptions of Theorem
10
, if
\psi_{h}^{1}
is a solution
to Eq. (
37
), then

\|\psi_{h}^{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}\leq 2\mathrm{Cn}\,\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}]+\frac{|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}{2}=:C_{\psi}(\psi_{h}^{0},\mathrm{Cn},|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|),

(52)

where
C_{\psi}(\psi_{h}^{0},\mathrm{Cn},|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|)
is a constant depending only on the initial
phase-field
\psi_{h}^{0}
, the Cahn number
\mathrm{Cn}
, and the measure of the domain
|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|
.

Proof.

By Corollary
11
,
\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{1}]\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}]
.
Since
a(\psi_{h},\psi_{h})\geq 0
:

\frac{1}{\mathrm{Cn}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}W(\psi_{h}^{1})\,dx\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{1}]\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}].

(53)

For the quartic double-well potential
W(\psi)=\frac{1}{4}(\psi^{2}-1)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\psi^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}
, Young’s inequality gives
\frac{1}{4}\psi^{4}\geq\psi^{2}-1
, so that
W(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\psi^{2}-\frac{3}{4}
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{\mathrm{Cn}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\psi_{h}^{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}-\frac{3|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|}{4}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Cn}}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}W(\psi_{h}^{1})\,dx\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}],

(54)

which rearranges to Eq. (
52
).

3.2
Existence

In this section we use the results from
Section
3.1
to establish the existence of solutions to the SWIPDP scheme in Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
).

Theorem 13
(Existence of solutions to SWIPDP).

Let
\psi_{h}^{0}\in V_{h}^{p}
be a given initial phase-field. Then there exists
a solution
(\psi_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})\in V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
to the SWIPDP scheme in Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
).

Proof.

It is sufficient to establish existence for
n=0
.
Following an approach analogous to
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
15
, Theorem
2, Lemma 3]
, and
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, we first solve a regularized
problem with strictly positive mobility and subsequently pass to the degenerate limit.
For
\delta\in(0,1)
, define
M_{\delta}(\psi)=\max\{M(\psi),\delta\}
and
consider the regularized scheme: find
(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta},\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta})\in V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left\langle\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}-\psi_{h}^{0},\varphi\right\rangle+\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}b(M_{\delta}(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}),\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta},\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(55)

\displaystyle\left\langle\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta},\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\xi\right\rangle-\mathrm{Cn}^{2}a(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta},\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(56)

for all
\varphi,\xi\in V_{h}^{p}
. Since
M_{\delta}(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1,\delta})\geq\delta>0
, Theorem
4
applies with
\tilde{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}={\mathcal{T}_{h}}
.
Let
N_{h}=\dim(V_{h}^{p})
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
be the piecewise orthogonal
basis of the broken polynomial space of
V_{h}^{p}
from Lemma
2
.
Testing Eq. (
56
) with
\xi=\phi_{j}
and using orthogonality gives

c_{j}=|K_{j}|^{-1}\bigl(\left\langle\Phi^{+}(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta})+\Phi^{-}(\psi_{h}^{0}),\phi_{j}\right\rangle+\mathrm{Cn}^{2}a(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta},\phi_{j})\bigr),

(57)

where
K_{j}
is the element supporting
\phi_{j}
. We note that, by construction,

\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}c_{j}\phi_{j}=:\mathcal{S}[\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}:\psi_{h}^{0}]
is determined by
\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}
given
\psi_{h}^{0}
, and
\mathcal{S}:V_{h}^{p}\to V_{h}^{p}
is continuous. Substituting into Eq. (
55
)
reduces the system to a single equation in
\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}
. Moreover, identifying

V_{h}^{p}\cong\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
via the basis
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
, we write

\psi_{h}^{1,\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}d_{j}\phi_{j}
for a coefficient vector
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{N_{h}})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
. To prove existence, we apply the Leray–Schauder
fixed-point theorem (see
[
22
, Theorem 11.3]
and
[
1
, Theorem 2.6]
)
following similarly to the proof in
[
1
, Theorem 2.5]
. For
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we reformulate Eqs.(
55
)–(
57
) to finding
\mathbf{d}^{(\lambda)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
such that

d_{j}^{(\lambda)}=d_{j}^{0}-\frac{\lambda\tau\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}b\bigl(M_{\delta}(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{(\lambda)}),\mathcal{S}[\psi_{h}^{(\lambda)}:\psi_{h}^{0}],\phi_{j}\bigr)}{|K_{j}|},\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{h},

(58)

where, implicitly,
\psi_{h}^{(\lambda)}=\sum_{i}d_{i}^{(\lambda)}\phi_{i}
defines a
fixed-point formulation for the map
T_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
satisfying

T_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)})=\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}
. We now verify the hypotheses
of the Leray–Schauder fixed-point theorem. First,
T_{\lambda}
is continuous for
each
\lambda\in[0,1]
, since it is a composition of continuous functions of
d^{(\lambda)}_{j}
.
Moreover, the map
(\lambda,\boldsymbol{d})\mapsto T_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
is jointly continuous,
as the right-hand side of Eq. (
58
) depends continuously on both

\lambda
and
\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}
. Since
V_{h}^{p}
is finite-dimensional, every continuous map
on a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
has a compact image; hence
T_{\lambda}
is compact
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Second, at
\lambda=0
the map
T_{\lambda}
simplifies
to
T_{0}(\boldsymbol{d})=\boldsymbol{d}^{0}
for all
\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
, so that
\boldsymbol{d}^{0}
is
trivially a fixed point of
T_{0}
. Third, we show that the set of fixed points
\{\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}:\lambda\in[0,1]\}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
. To establish this,
we note that the energy identity underlying Corollary
11
remains
valid for the regularized mobility
M_{\delta}
with the rescaling
\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\to\lambda\mathrm{Pe}^{-1}
, since
0<\delta\leq M_{\delta}\leq 1
uniformly in

\delta
. For
\lambda=0
the bound is immediate as
\psi_{h}^{(0)}=\psi_{h}^{0}
. Next we establish an a priori
bound for the coefficient vector
\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}
. Lemmas
2

and
12
then give the inequality chain

|K||\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}|^{2}\leq\|\psi_{h}^{(\lambda)}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}\leq C_{\psi}[\psi_{h}^{0},\mathrm{Cn},|{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|],

(59)

uniformly in
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. Setting
R=\sqrt{|K|^{-1}C_{\psi}}+1
, the solution set
\{\boldsymbol{d}^{(\lambda)}:\lambda\in[0,1]\}
is contained in the closed ball
\overline{B_{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
induced by the Euclidian norm in Eq.(
59
).
By the Leray–Schauder theorem formulation in
[
22
, Theorem 11.3]
,
there exists a fixed point, and thus, a solution
(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta},\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta})

to the regularized problem in Eqs. (
55
)–(
56
).

We now establish existence for the degenerate case by passing to the limit
\delta\to 0^{+}
. Consider a sequence
(\delta_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
with
\delta_{0}=\delta
from the regularized problem,

\delta_{i}\in(0,1)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and limit
\delta_{i}\to 0^{+}
. Since the uniform bound

\|\psi_{h}^{1,\delta_{i}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}^{2}\leq C_{\psi}
and the compact embedding of
\overline{B_{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
established above are both
\delta
-independent, and the image
\mathcal{S}(\overline{B_{R}}:\psi_{h}^{0})
is compact in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by continuity of
\mathcal{S}
, the existence of
a sequence
(\delta_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
is established such that
(\psi_{h}^{1,\delta_{i}},\upsilon_{h}^{1,\delta_{i}})\to(\psi_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})
in
V_{h}^{p}\times V_{h}^{p}
.
It remains to verify that all terms in Eqs.(
55
)–(
56
) pass
to the limit. Since
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}
is a continuous linear projection,
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1,\delta_{i}}\to\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}
in
V_{h}^{0}
, and
M_{\delta_{i}}(s)=\max\{1-s^{2},\delta_{i}\}\to M(s)
pointwise for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For the harmonic
averages, the bound
\min\{M_{\delta_{i}}^{+},M_{\delta_{i}}^{-}\}\leq\langle{M_{\delta_{i}}}\rangle\leq 2\min\{M_{\delta_{i}}^{+},M_{\delta_{i}}^{-}\}
on each
e\in\Gamma_{i}

gives
\langle{M_{\delta_{i}}}\rangle\to\langle{M}\rangle
. In particular, if
\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}>0
this follows from continuity of
\langle{\cdot}\rangle
, while for
\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}=0
the bound gives
\delta_{i}\leq\langle{M_{\delta_{i}}}\rangle\leq 2\delta_{i}

which passes to the limit following a comparison, as
\delta_{i}\to 0^{+}
. Since all integrals
in
b
are uniformly bounded, the dominated convergence theorem
[
18
, E.3 Theorem
5]
yields convergence of
b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
. The remaining terms
are continuous or polynomial in
\psi_{h}^{1,\delta_{i}}
and thus pass to the limit.
Therefore
(\psi_{h}^{1},\upsilon_{h}^{1})
solves Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
).

Remark 14
(Uniqueness).

Uniqueness does not follow directly from Theorem
13
; however, it was
proven to be
\tau
-independent for a similar formulation (see for instance the discrete

[

]
), and the Eyre splitting in Eq. (
36
)
gives unique solutions
[
19
]
. We therefore rely on these results to allude
to uniqueness of the SWIPDP scheme without providing a formal proof. Regardless,
in Section
5.2
we will still restrict ourselves to conservative
choices of
\tau
.

Following the establishment of existence in Theorem
13
and our discussion about uniqueness in Remark
14
we proceed with the main results. In Section
4
, we describe how to obtain boundedness in
L_{1}^{\infty}
for
solutions
\psi_{h}^{1}
by applying a scaling limiter, while the cell-averaged bound

|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}|\leq 1
a.e. in
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
follows automatically from
|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{0}|\leq 1
a.e. in
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. The existence result in Theorem
13
for the
SWIPDP scheme enables the application of this theory.

4
Discrete maximum principle

To obtain boundedness in
L_{1}^{\infty}
for
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
which are solutions to the SWIPDP scheme, it suffices
to ensure boundedness in
L_{1}^{\infty}
for the cell averages

\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}
and subsequently apply a scaling limiter introduced in Section

4.1
to enforce the point-wise bound.
However, this approach comes at the cost that a monotone energy dissipation
law can no longer be formally guaranteed for the limited scheme as we discuss in Remark
21
,
although a discrete energy estimate remains available for
p\geq 1
, wherein
the SWIPDP-L scheme is needed for a discrete maximum principle. However, for
p=0
the SWIPDP scheme gives provable discrete maximum principle, mass conservation, and energy dissipation which means that it has provable
structure preservation.

4.1
Scaling limiter

The Zhang–Shu scaling limiter was first introduced in
[
30
]
and successfully
applied
in
[
9
,
11
,
23
,
25
]
.
The underlying idea is to rescale the phase-field on each element so that prescribed
minimum and maximum values are satisfied.
For
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, we denote by
\psi_{K}={\psi_{h}}_{|K}
the restriction of
\psi_{h}

to element
K
and define the projection operator

\Pi_{s}:V_{h}^{p}\longrightarrow V_{h}^{p}
by

\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\int_{K}\Pi_{s}[\psi_{K}]\cdot\varphi=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\int_{K}\widehat{\psi_{K}}\cdot\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in V_{h}^{p}

(60)

with the scaled phase-field

\widehat{\psi_{K}}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\overline{\psi_{K}}+\alpha_{K}\tilde{\psi}_{K},

(61)

and the scaling factor
\alpha_{K}
is defined as

\alpha_{K}:=\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\hat{K}}\left\{1,\left|\frac{\overline{\psi_{K}}-\psi_{\min}}{\overline{\psi_{K}}-\psi_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|,\left|\frac{\overline{\psi_{K}}-\psi_{\max}}{\overline{\psi_{K}}-\psi_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|\right\}

(62)

where
\hat{K}
is a sampling set, in principle taken as
\overline{K}:=K\cup\partial K
,
but in practice is typically replaced by a finite set of evaluation points for computational
efficiency – for instance, one may take
\hat{K}
as the set of quadrature
points
\Lambda_{K}\subset\overline{K}
or
\Lambda_{K}=\partial K
for
p=1
such as in
[
23
]
. Since we seek a phase-field
\psi_{h}\in L_{1}^{\infty}
,
we set
\psi_{\min}=-1
and
\psi_{\max}=1
.

Lemma 15
(Scaling limiter bounds).

Suppose that
\overline{\psi_{K}}\in L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
for each
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and that

\psi_{K}
is not necessarily bounded in
L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
for some
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. Then
\widehat{\psi_{K}}\in L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
.

Proof.

Since the scaling limiter acts locally, it is enough to consider a single
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and assume that
\overline{\psi_{K}}\in L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
while
\psi_{K}
is unbounded
in
L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
. The
|\overline{\psi_{K}}|=1
case is trivial, suppose that
|\overline{\psi_{K}}|<1
and that
\psi_{K}
is unbounded in
L_{1}^{\infty}(K)
. Then, by Eqs. (
61
)
and (
62
),

-\alpha_{K}|\tilde{\psi}_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\widehat{\psi_{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})-\overline{\psi_{K}}\leq\alpha_{K}|\tilde{\psi}_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})|

(63)

and thus
|\widehat{\psi_{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq 1
for almost all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\hat{K}
. We refer
to the original paper
[
30
]
for details.

Remark 16
(Bound equivalences).

As a direct consequence of Lemma
15
, if
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}

is bounded in
L_{1}^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
,
then the limited phase-field
\widehat{\psi_{h}}
also satisfies
\|\widehat{\psi_{h}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
. Hence, to enforce the bound on the cell averages via the SWIPDP scheme,
i.e., to verify that
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
prior to applying
the limiter, then
|\widehat{\psi_{h}}|\leq 1
a.e. in
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
.

In view of Remark
16
, it remains to verify that the limited scheme
SWIPDP-L, which applies the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter after each successful time
step of the SWIPDP scheme, admits a valid initialization for the SWIPDP scheme
in Eqs.(
37
)–(
38
) after limiting. Since the proof of
Theorem
13
relies on the bound in Lemma
12
,
it suffices to show that the energy remains bounded after applying the scaling limiter
to a phase-field
\psi_{h}
whose energy
\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}]
is bounded.

Lemma 17
(Boundedness of limited energy).

Let
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
and suppose that
\mathcal{E}[\psi^{n}_{h}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

\mathcal{E}[\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proof.

We consider only the case
p>0
since for
p=0
, the result is trivial as we do not perform limiting. For
p>0
, first, we note that
\|\widehat{\psi_{h}}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq\|\psi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

for each
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
by construction of the scaling limiter, which implies
that
\widehat{\psi_{h}}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
if
\psi_{h}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
. By an argument analogous
to that in the proof of Lemma
12
, and owing to the equivalence
of norms in finite-dimensional spaces, there exists a constant
C[{\mathcal{T}_{h}},h]>0

such that

\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}W(\widehat{\psi_{h}})d\boldsymbol{x}\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\frac{C[{\mathcal{T}_{h}},h]\|\widehat{\psi_{h}}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(K)}}{4}+\frac{|K|}{4}\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\frac{C[{\mathcal{T}_{h}},h]\|\psi_{h}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(K)}}{4}+\frac{|K|}{4},

(64)

for some constant
C[{\mathcal{T}_{h}},h]>0
depending on the grid
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and mesh size
h
,
and is bounded since
\psi_{h}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
by Lemma
12
. Similarly
for
a
, due to equivalences of norms in finite-dimensional spaces and using the trace inequality, there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{inv}}>0
such that

a(\widehat{\psi_{h}},\widehat{\psi_{h}})\leq\max_{e\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left\{h_{e}^{-2}\right\}C_{\mathrm{inv}}\|\widehat{\psi_{h}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq\max_{e\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left\{h_{e}^{-2}\right\}C_{\mathrm{inv}}\|\psi_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}.

(65)

Hence, each term in the energy
\mathcal{E}[\widehat{\psi^{n}_{h}}]
is bounded, and consequently

\mathcal{E}[\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Lemma 18
(Limited mass conservation).

Suppose that
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
and let
\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}
be the limited version of
\psi_{h}
.
Then
\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\widehat{\psi_{h}}d\boldsymbol{x}=\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{h}d\boldsymbol{x}
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{K}
is constant on each element
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
by construction in
Eq. (
62
):

\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\widehat{\psi_{h}}d\boldsymbol{x}=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\int_{K}\overline{\psi_{K}}+\alpha_{K}\tilde{\psi}_{K}d\boldsymbol{x}=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}|K|\overline{\psi_{K}}=\int_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{h}d\boldsymbol{x},

(66)

where each equality follows from Eq.(
61
) and Lemma
2
.

4.2
Cell-averaged bounds

For
n\geq 0
, the proof of
Theorem
13
only requires that the energy at the previous step satisfies

\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Therefore, the same argument shows existence of
solutions to the SWIPDP-L scheme, in which the previous solution
\psi_{h}^{n}
is replaced
by the limited version
\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}
. Since
\mathcal{E}[\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
whenever
\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
m_{\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}}=m_{\psi_{h}^{n}}

by Lemmas
17
and
18
, respectively, mass
is preserved under limiting and the energy remains bounded.
In Theorem
19
we show that the cell averages
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n}

for
n\geq 0
remain bounded in
L_{1}^{\infty}
for the SWIPDP scheme if
|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{0}|\leq 1
a.e., which in turn implies that the limited scheme SWIPDP-L yields
boundedness in
L_{1}^{\infty}
for limited solutions
\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}\in V_{h}^{p}
.

Theorem 19
(Boundedness in
V_{h}^{p}
for SWIPDP-L).

Suppose that the initial phase-field
\psi_{h}^{0}\in V_{h}^{p}
satisfies

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
. Then the cell-averaged solution

\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n}
of the SWIPDP scheme in Eqs. (
37
)–(
38
)
satisfies
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
for all
n\geq 0
, unconditionally. Consequently,
the limited version
\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n+1}}
of the solution
\psi_{h}^{n+1}
to the SWIPDP-L scheme
satisfies
\|\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n+1}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and with the assumption that

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
. Suppose, for the sake
of contradiction, that there exists an element
K^{\star}\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
such that

\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}=1+\varepsilon=\max_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\overline{\psi^{1}_{K}},

(67)

for some
\varepsilon>0
given a solution
\psi_{h}^{1}\in V_{h}^{p}
to the SWIPDP scheme in Eqs.(
37
)–(
38
) for
n=0
. The rest follows by induction. We test Eq. (
37
) with
the test function

\varphi=(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}\,\chi(K^{\star})\in V_{h}^{0}\subset V_{h}^{p},

(68)

to obtain

\frac{|K^{\star}|}{\tau}\left\langle\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-\overline{\psi^{0}_{K^{\star}}},(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}\right\rangle=-b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}\,\chi(K^{\star})),

(69)

where, by the choice of the test function
\varphi
in Eq. (
68
),

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}\,\chi(K^{\star}))\noindent

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\partial K^{\star}\cap\Gamma_{i}}\int_{e}\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1})}\rangle(\frac{\eta_{e}}{h_{e}}[{\upsilon_{h}^{1}}]-\{{\nabla\upsilon_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}}\})(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}ds,

(71)

Since
\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}=\underset{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}{\max}\overline{\psi^{1}_{K}}

and, consequently,
M(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}})=\underset{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}{\min}M(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K}})
,
the harmonic average satisfies

M(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}})\leq\langle{M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1})}\rangle\leq 2M(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}).

(72)

Using the comparison in Eq.(
72
) it follows from
M(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}})=0
that

b(M(\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}),\upsilon_{h}^{1},(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}\,\chi(K^{\star}))=0,

(73)

which gives

\frac{|K^{\star}|}{\tau}(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-\overline{\psi^{0}_{K^{\star}}})(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}=0.

(74)

Eq.(
74
) implies that

\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}=\overline{\psi^{0}_{K^{\star}}}
or
(\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}-1)_{\oplus}=0
, either of which contradicts the assumption that
\overline{\psi^{1}_{K^{\star}}}=1+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
. We conclude that
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
, and by induction,
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
for all
n\geq 0
. Finally, since
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
, we obtain from Lemma
15
that
\|\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n+1}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\leq 1
for all

. An analogous argument applies for the global minimum.

Remark 21
(Limited energy dissipation).

While the unlimited SWIPDP scheme satisfies monotone energy dissipation – that is,

\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{n+1}]\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{n}]\leq\ldots\leq\mathcal{E}[\psi_{h}^{0}]

for a sequence of phase-fields
(\psi_{h}^{n})_{n\geq 0}

which are solutions to the SWIPD scheme in Eqs.(
37
)–(
38
) for
n>0

by a simple induction argument based on Theorem
10
– the
same cannot be established directly for the limited scheme
SWIPDP-L (or any other limited scheme) due to the interaction of the limiter coefficients

\alpha_{K}
with the
penalty and consistency terms
and the data-dependent quantities in
W
. A practical trade-off thus arises: for

\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, both a discrete maximum principle and energy dissipation can be
proven,
and with

p>0
, boundedness is guaranteed under the limiter but energy dissipation
is not. One possible
remedy is to study the optimization problem associated with the scaling limiter,
namely to find
\tilde{\alpha}_{K}\in[0,\alpha_{K}]
satisfying

\mathcal{E}[\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\overline{\psi^{n+1}_{K}}+\tilde{\alpha}_{K}\tilde{\psi}^{n+1}_{K}]\leq\mathcal{E}[\widehat{\psi_{h}^{n}}],\qquad n\geq 0,

(77)

since these are the steps at which the energy is evaluated.
Nevertheless, it remains unclear how to enforce such a constraint, as it constitutes
a global condition on a non-convex problem. Numerical experiments, however, suggest
that this is generally not an issue, and in particular, that energy dissipation is
still observed in practice as is found and discussed in Section
5.2
.

4.3
Comparison to other schemes

Drawing on the numerical observations reported in
[
24
,
23
]
,
we summarize below the advantages of the proposed SWIPDP-L scheme:

1.

Both the proposed SWIPDP-L scheme and the schemes in
[
25
]
admit
a provable discrete maximum principle for arbitrary polynomial order
p
. However,
the scheme in
[
25
, Section 1.5]
relies on a two-step limiter, which consists
of solving a DG scheme, then solving a constrained convex problem, and finally applying
a Zhang–Shu scaling limiter. In contrast, the SWIPDP-L scheme requires only a single
application of the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter after each successful time step, which
is a more efficient procedure. Moreover, it is unclear whether the scheme in
[
25
]

satisfies an energy law, see for instance Remark
21

for more details, or to what extent it remains competitive in that regard.

2.

As noted above, the proposed scheme shares the property with
[
1
]

of providing boundedness for the cell-averaged solution
\Pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{0}\psi_{h}
of the
SWIPDP scheme, which in turn yields a discrete maximum principle for the limited
scheme SWIPDP-L. However, the scheme in
[
1
]
is defined only for
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, whereas SWIPDP-L accommodates
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
with arbitrary
polynomial order
p
while retaining the bound-preserving property. The formulation
in
[
1
,
2
]
(and related applications in
[
2
]
)
is notably elegant in that one can directly establish a discrete maximum principle
and energy laws even for a coupled scheme. We defer to future work the proof of analogous
results for the SWIPDP-L scheme, whose analysis is more involved due to the necessity
of employing a limiter for boundedness in Theorem
19
for
p>0

and the uncertainty regarding energy dissipation discussed in Remark
21
.

3.

For the FEM-L, SIPG-L, and SWIP-L schemes in
[
23
]
, it is
unclear whether the procedure satisfies a discrete maximum principle or an energy
law; only numerical evidence is provided. It is also unclear how to properly treat the coercivity constant, as can be seen by comparing Theorem
4

to
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
. A similar remark holds for the SWIPD-L scheme
in
[
24
, Theorem 2]
for
p>0
. In contrast, the coercivity result in Theorem

4
does not require explicit estimates of a data-dependent constant
to ensure semi-positivity of
\tilde{b}(\upsilon_{h},\upsilon_{h})
for
\upsilon_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
.

5
Numerics

We begin by stating a remark regarding some difficulties from
translating the theoretical results to the numerical setting, which may affect the
results in practice.

Remark 22
(Numerical considerations and limitations).

Below we outline some key numerical considerations and limitations of the proposed
scheme which may affect the results in practice:

1.

The SWIPDP scheme is almost fully implicit and can therefore be computationally
expensive. Moreover, since the proof of Theorem
19
relies on strict
degeneracy of the mobility, regularization cannot be used without potentially compromising
the discrete maximum principle of the SWIPDP-L scheme. However, without a small regularization
parameter, the resulting nonlinear system might become ill-conditioned due to the
degeneracy of the mobility.

2.

Similarly, standard solvers for the nonlinear system require a finite tolerance

TOL
for both the Newton and linear iterations, which may also affect the discrete
maximum principle of the SWIPDP-L scheme.

3.

In our Zhang–Shu scaling limiter implementation, we add a tolerance
\text{TOL}_{\lim}=5\cdot 10^{-16}
to avoid division by zero in Eq. (
62
). We also artificially set
\alpha_{K}=0
if
|\overline{\psi_{K}}|>1-\text{TOL}_{\text{avg}}
, with a threshold of
\text{TOL}_{\text{avg}}=10^{-14}
to limit numerical overflow.

In view of Remark
22
, we also note that the SWIPDP-L scheme does not necessarily satisfy a monotone energy dissipation law for polynomial
order
p>1
due to the scaling limiter, as noted in Remark
21
.
However, this property is observed in practice as seen in Figs.
1

and
4
.

6
Summary and Outlook

In this paper we constructed a novel scheme which unconditionally satisfies
a discrete maximum principle for arbitrary polynomial order
p
using a scaling
limiter. Numerical examples in Section
5

validate the theoretical results presented in Sections
2
and
3
.
Shortcomings are discussed in Remark
22
and
optimal order of convergence of the proposed scheme is demonstrated in
Table
1
.

A key theoretical contribution is the mobility-free constant constraint
on the coercivity of the SWIPDP, which removes the need for a regularization
parameter as required in
[
24
,
23
]
.
Moreover, it reduces the complexity of the limiter process in
[
25
]
by
eliminating the need to adjust the cell-averages, and one simply obtains the desired
constrained bounds thanks to the scheme in Theorem
19
.
Moreover, we also proved existence of a solution and highlighted energy stability
properties of the proposed scheme, despite only being provable to be unconditionally
monotone decreasing for the very special case when
p=0
, while for
p=1
this
is only supported by numerical evidence.
It is of interest to further investigate the energy stability following Remark
21
,
and to explore the possibility of a provable energy stability result for higher-order
polynomial degrees while preserving boundedness. Moreover, it would be of
interest to see how competitive this scheme is against other provably bound-preserving
schemes such as
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
, and to explore the possibility
of extending the proposed scheme to the coupled Cahn–Hilliard–Navier–Stokes system,
or other coupling schemes as a potential extension aided by Remark
20

and
hp
-adaptive configurations with a theoretically valid scheme.
