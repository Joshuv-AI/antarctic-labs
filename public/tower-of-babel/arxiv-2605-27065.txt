A continuum of Small-cap decouplings and Exponential Sums for the Moment Curve in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}
Jacob Glidewell

Abstract
. We use the high-low method and wavepacket pruning to prove new small-cap decoupling estimates for the moment curve in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} . As an application, we verify a conjecture of Demeter regarding the L 12 L^{12} square-root cancellation of exponential sums associated with the moment curve in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} . This provides a continuum of square-root cancellation estimates that connects the Vinogradov MVT in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with a result of Bourgain, related to improving the best known estimate for the Lindelöf hypothesis.

Introduction

We are motivated to attack the following conjecture of Demeter
[
6

for the case of the moment curve
\Phi(t)=(t,t^{2},t^{3},t^{4})

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let

. Assume
a\geq b\geq 0

\int_{\Omega}\left|\sum_{n\sim N}e(x\cdot\Phi\left(\frac{n}{N}\right))\right|^{p}dx\lessapprox N^{\frac{p}{2}}|\Omega|.

Some special cases of this are known. Notably, the case

corresponds to the Vinogradov Mean Value Theorem in

(

Integrating in

\int_{\Omega}\left|\sum_{n\sim N}e(x\cdot\tilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right))\right|^{12}dx\lessapprox N^{6}|\Omega|

[

and

Bourgain’s original approach in
[
3

relies on a geometric observation that a bilinear version of (

Notation and Outline

We write
A\lesssim B
or
A=O(B)
if
A\leq cB
for some universal constant (possibly large)
c>0
. Also, we write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. We write
A\lessapprox B
if
A\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}B
for any
\epsilon>0
. Finally, we write
A\approx B
if
B\lesssim A\lessapprox B
.

Now, we define the operational notation for our argument. Fix
\epsilon>0
. Define intermediate scales
R_{k}=R^{k\epsilon}
for
k=0,\dots,K
where
R_{K}\sim R^{\beta}
with
K\sim\frac{1}{\epsilon}
. Now, we define the intermediate caps.

i)

For
R_{k}\leq R^{1/4}
, let
\gamma_{k}
be the caps with dimensions

\sim R_{k}^{-1}\times R_{k}^{-2}\times R_{k}^{-3}\times R_{k}^{-4}.

ii)

For
R^{1/4}<R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
, let
\gamma_{k}
be the caps with dimensions

\sim R_{k}^{-1}\times R_{k}^{-2}\times R_{k}^{-3}\times R^{-1}.

iii)

Finally, for
R^{1/3}<R_{k}\leq R^{\beta}
, let
\gamma_{k}
be the caps with dimensions

\sim R_{k}^{-1}\times R_{k}^{-2}\times R^{-1}\times R^{-1}.

These coincide with the cap sizes in
[
8
]
in the first three dimensions. Fix
A>0
a constant to be determined, depending on
\epsilon
. There are two places in the argument (namely, Lemma
5.2
and estimating the low piece in Section
7
) where the size of
A
is determined. Fix
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4}
caps with
|\tau_{i}|\sim 1
and
1
-separated. For
\lambda>0
, let

U_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega_{R}:\lambda\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}(x)|\leq 2\lambda\}.

We normalize so that
\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}=1
. By dyadic pigeonholing, we have for some
0<\lambda\leq R^{\beta}
,

(3)

\|\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lessapprox\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}|.

We assume without loss of generality that
\lambda\geq R^{\beta/2}
. Otherwise, Theorem
1.3
is immediate. We will make use of this assumption in Section
8
.

At this point, let us give a heuristic overview of the proof of Theorem
1.3
; the broad-to-linear reduction for Theorem
1.2
is standard and is given in Section
9
. The argument for Theorem
1.3
nicely decomposes into two major phases: (a) finding local square-root cancellation and (b) gaining globally with a high-low decomposition.

Phase (a):

Our initial goal is to identify where in
\Omega_{R}
square-root cancellation is occurring locally. This is quantified by square functions at each scale
R_{k}
. Indeed, we would like
F
to be comparable to

\left(\sum_{\gamma}|F_{\gamma}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

However, this is not always true. Instead, we pick out the spatial places where intermediate (squared) square functions are comparable to square-root cancellation; that is,

(4)

g_{k}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\sim R^{\beta}.

Each of these square functions are essentially constant on balls of radius
R_{k}
, so we decompose via a stopping time selection
\Omega_{R}=\bigcup\Lambda_{k}
with each
\Lambda_{k}
a union of
R_{k}
-cubes where (
4
) occurs. To pass from
F
to these intermediate square functions, we use the Multilinear Restriction Theorem for the moment curve (see
[
5
, Ex. 6.33]
or Theorem
3.1
in the next section). This preserves the square-root cancellation from
F
to the intermediate square function on
\Lambda_{k}
, giving the estimate which "morally" says

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{4\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

Phase (b):

Now, it suffices to get a gain on the size of
\Lambda_{k}
. Because of the stopping time selection and our expectation that
F
satisfies square-root cancellation on most of
\Omega_{R}
, we expect
|\Lambda_{k}|
to decrease as
R_{k}
increases. For instance, when
p=12
, we would like to show

|\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox\frac{R^{2\beta}}{\lambda^{4}}|\Omega_{R}|.

As we will see in Lemma
5.3
, the critical
\lambda
to consider is
\lambda\sim R_{k}^{1/2}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.
Thus, the estimate we would like to show is "morally"

|\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox\frac{1}{R_{k}^{2}}|\Omega_{R}|,

which matches our expectation. To prove such an estimate, we convert to estimating a
L^{q}
-moment of the intermediate (squared) square function:

R^{q\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|\sim\int_{\Lambda_{k}}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}\right)^{q}=\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}|^{q}.

Because of the stopping time selection of
\Lambda_{k}
, we have control over the next intermediate square function

g_{k+1}=\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}|^{2}.

We relate the two square functions by the following identity:

\displaystyle g_{k}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\gamma_{k+1},\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k+1}}\overline{F_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k+1}}\overline{F_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}}+\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\not\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k+1}}\overline{F_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}},

where
\sim
is the adjacency relation (i.e
\gamma_{k+1}\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}
if they are the same or adjacent). We can bound the first term by
g_{k+1}
; this is called the "low" piece. It suffices to control the second term; this is called the "high" piece. We will explain in Section
7
that the terms in the high piece exhibit additional orthogonality, due to the fact that their frequency supports mimic that of cone planks. This is the main mechanism of our proof, mirroring that of
[
8
]
. Since we are proving estimates over a range of parameters, some combinations of
\alpha
and
\beta
have access to better or worse outcomes from the Multilinear Restriction Theorem (Part (a)) and cone decouplings (Part (b)). From this obstacle, we finally need to show how these estimates combine to give the correct estimates for Theorem
1.3
. We must consider three different regimes of
R_{k}
where slightly different arguments are needed.

A key technical tool used in the argument is "wavepacket pruning" (see Lemma
4.1
). This tool is commonly used in tandem with the high-low stopping time argument. It "prunes off" irrelevant wavepackets with large amplitudes relative to each intermediate square function. One should observe that wavepacket pruning is not strictly needed for every iteration of this kind of argument. For instance,
[
9
]
does not make use of any pruning. For similar reasons, Theorem
1.2
with
\beta=\frac{1}{3}
does not need wavepacket pruning. However, the other cases
\beta>\frac{1}{3}
need this tool and so we use it for all cases. One should note that pruning causes the need for a
4
-broad formulation, rather than a
4
-linear formulation; see Lemma
5.3
.

A striking similarity between our argument here and Bourgain’s original argument is that the
x_{4}
variable becomes irrelevant after an initial "local" step. In Bourgain’s argument, the local step was an initial application of the
L^{6}
decoupling inequality on
R^{1/2}
-cubes while in our argument, this was part (a) described above. It remains unclear exactly why (other than for ad-hoc reasons) one may ignore
x_{4}
in some steps and still produce sharp estimates. One may hope that a general theory of these decouplings may shed some light on this dependence.

The order of this paper is as follows. Section

gives a review of the known decouplings we will be using for our argument. Sections
4
and
5

will present how we use decoupling for the cone in

to enhance our estimate from

4.
Wavepacket Pruning

We first prune wavepackets to create the functions
F^{(k)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}
, identically as in
[
8
, Lemma 1]
. We record the key properties here. The main point is
F^{(k)}
has a similar Fourier support to
F
with a much better
L^{\infty}
size, suitable for transferring to smaller exponents where lower dimensional decoupling may be used.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
\gamma_{k}
,

a)

|F^{(k)}_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|\lesssim|F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|\leq\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\subset\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}(x)|,

b)

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\min\left\{\frac{R^{\beta}}{\lambda},\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}\right\},

c)

\text{supp }\widehat{F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}}\subset\text{supp }\widehat{F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}}\subset C\gamma_{k}.

Proof.

We perform an iterative scheme. Define
F_{\gamma_{K}}^{(K)}=F_{\gamma}
and
F_{\gamma_{K-1}}^{(K)}=F_{\gamma_{K-1}}
. For each
1\leq k\leq K-1
, write the wavepacket decomposition dual to
\gamma_{k}
:

F^{(k+1)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{T_{\gamma_{k}}}\psi_{T_{\gamma_{k}}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}.

We split the
T_{\gamma_{k}}
into two collections: good wavepackets and bad wavepackets. The good ones are defined by

\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{k}}^{g}=\{T_{\gamma_{k}}:\|\psi_{T_{\gamma_{k}}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}\|_{\infty}\leq A^{K-k+1}\frac{R^{\beta}}{\lambda}\}.

Now define

F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}=\sum_{T_{\gamma_{k}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{k}}^{g}}\psi_{T_{\gamma_{k}}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)},\quad F_{\gamma_{k-1}}^{(k)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}\subset\gamma_{k-1}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}.

Property a) follows by noting that since
\{T_{\gamma_{k}}\}
are a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, either
|F^{(k)}_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|=0
or
|F^{(k)}_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|\sim|F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k}}(x)|.

The properties c), and the first bound in b) are immediate from the construction. The latter bound in b) follows from iterating property a).
∎

It is not immediately clear which bound in b) is the "better"
L^{\infty}
estimate. For some estimates,
\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}
will suffice. However, as we will see in Lemma
5.3
, the critical value of
\lambda
we consider at this scale is
R_{k}^{1/2}R^{\beta/2}
and so we morally have from b),

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{R^{\beta/2}}{R_{k}^{1/2}}=\left(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}\right)^{1/2}.

This is the desired square-root cancellation bound.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any scale
R_{k}\leq R^{1/2}
, we have

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}\lessapprox|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}\right)^{1/2}.

For
R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
, we have

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}\lessapprox\min\left\{\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}R_{k}^{\frac{1}{4}}},\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta}}{R^{\frac{1}{12}}R_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\}|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{12}}.

Proof.

Note by a) of Lemma
4.1
and
L^{6}
cylindrical decoupling with Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon^{100}}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\subset\gamma_{k}}\|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon^{100}}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\subset\gamma_{k}}\|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We iterate this until
k=K
to get

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon^{99}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\subset\gamma_{k}}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, this gives

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}\lessapprox\left(\frac{R_{k}^{-1}}{R^{-\beta}}|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Similarly, note by a) of Lemma
4.1
and
L^{12}
cylindrical decoupling with Theorem
3.2
, we have

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon^{100}}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\subset\gamma_{k}}\|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We iterate this until
k=k_{0}
with
R_{k_{0}}\approx R^{1/3}
to get

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon^{99}}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k_{0}}\subset\gamma_{k}}\|F_{\gamma_{k_{0}}}^{(k_{0})}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By b) in Lemma
4.1
and the previous
L^{6}
inequality with
k=k_{0}
, we have

\|F_{\gamma_{k_{0}}}^{(k_{0})}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}^{12}\lesssim\min\left\{\frac{R^{6\beta}}{\lambda^{6}},\frac{R^{6\beta}}{R^{2}}\right\}\|F_{\gamma_{k_{0}}}^{(k_{0})}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}^{6}\\
\lessapprox\min\left\{\frac{R^{6\beta}}{\lambda^{6}},\frac{R^{6\beta}}{R^{2}}\right\}|\Omega_{R}|\left(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R^{1/3}}\right)^{3}=\min\left\{\frac{R^{9\beta}}{\lambda^{6}R},\frac{R^{9\beta}}{R^{3}}\right\}|\Omega_{R}|.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\lessapprox\left(\frac{R_{k}^{-1}}{R^{-1/3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\min\left\{\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{1}{12}}},\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta}}{R^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right\}|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{12}}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta+\frac{1}{12}}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}R_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}},\frac{R^{\frac{3}{4}\beta}}{R^{\frac{1}{12}}R_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\}|\Omega_{R}|^{\frac{1}{12}}.

We can actually do better than the first bound by going straight to
L^{6}
. Indeed,

\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}^{12}\lesssim\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}^{6}\|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k)}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{R})}^{6}\lessapprox\frac{R^{6\beta}}{\lambda^{6}}\left(\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}}\right)^{3}|\Omega_{R}|=\frac{R^{9\beta}}{\lambda^{6}R_{k}^{3}}|\Omega_{R}|.

∎

5.
Square Function Stopping Time

Let

g_{k}(x)=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}(x)|^{2}.

We perform a stopping time decomposition on
\Omega_{R}
. The decomposition has two regimes:
R_{k}>R^{\alpha}
and
R_{k}\leq R^{\alpha}
. In the large scale regime
R_{k}>R^{\alpha}
, we will split into boxes of sidelengths

R_{k}\times R_{k}\times R_{k}\times R^{\alpha}

while for the small scale regime
R_{k}\leq R^{\alpha}
, we will split into cubes of sidelength
R_{k}
. We will call both "boxes" of sidelength
R_{k}
; however, the meaning of "boxes" is as above, depending on the size of
R_{k}
relative to
R^{\alpha}
.
Split
\Omega_{R}
into "boxes" of sidelength
R_{K-1}
. We say one of these "boxes"
B
is
(K-1)
-bad if

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}g_{K-1}^{2}\geq A^{2}R^{2\beta}.

Let
\Lambda_{K-1}
be the union of the
(K-1)
-bad "boxes". We form
\Lambda_{k}
similarly. We split the remaining "boxes" after forming
\Lambda_{k+1}
into smaller "boxes" of sidelength
R_{k}
. We say one of these "boxes"
B
is
k
-bad if

(5)

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}g_{k}^{2}\geq A^{2K-2k}R^{2\beta}.

Let
\Lambda_{k}
be the union of these
k
-bad "boxes". Finally, let
\Lambda_{0}
be the union of the cubes with sidelength
1
which remain after forming
\Lambda_{1}
. Note by Hölder’s inequality,

g_{k}\leq R^{\epsilon}g_{k+1}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
k\geq 1
, we have

\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}|^{2}\geq A^{2K-2k}R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|,\quad\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k+1}|^{2}\lesssim A^{2K-2k-2}R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

Moreover, for
x\in\Lambda_{k}
,

g_{k}(x)\approx A^{K-k}R^{\beta}

and for
m>k
,

g_{m}(x)\lesssim A^{K-m}R^{\beta}.

For
k=0
, the second inequality still holds and for
x\in\Lambda_{0}
,
g_{0}(x)\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R^{\beta}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from summing over all
R_{k}
-"boxes"
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
with the definition (
5
) of
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
. For the second inequality, first observe that
g_{k}
is locally constant on balls of radius
R_{k}
; indeed,
g_{k}
is Fourier supported on a ball of radius
O(R_{k}^{-1})
about the origin. This follows since each
|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}
has Fourier support on
C(\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k})
by Lemma
4.1
. Now note that if
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
, then
B
must lie in a
(k+1)
-good "box". Therefore, for
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
,

\int_{B}|g_{k+1}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{|B|}{|B^{\prime}|}\int_{B^{\prime}}|g_{k+1}|^{2}\leq A^{2K-2k-2}R^{2\beta}|B|,

where
B^{\prime}
is the
(k+1)
-good parent of
B
. Now, we sum over
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
to finish.
For the pointwise estimate, we use Hölder’s inequality as above and the locally constant property. Further, for
m>k
, for the
R_{m}
-"box" containing
x
, call it
B
, we have

\int_{B}|g_{m}|^{2}\leq A^{2K-2m}R^{2\beta}|B|.

By the locally constant property,
g_{m}(x)\lesssim A^{K-m}R^{\beta}
.
∎

We relate each
\Lambda_{k}
to the pruning, identical to
[
8
, Lemma 5]
. Define

F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}\subset\tau_{i}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
\tau_{i}
and each
x\in\Lambda_{k}
,

|F_{\tau_{i}}(x)-F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}(x)|\leq\frac{\lambda}{1000}.

Proof.

Fix
\tau_{i}
. Write

F_{\tau_{i}}=F^{(k+1)}_{\tau_{i}}+\sum_{m=k+1}^{K-1}(F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m+1)}-F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m)}).

Further for each term, we have

F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m+1)}-F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m)}=\sum_{\gamma_{m}\subset\tau_{i}}F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}-F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m)}.

We bound each term of this sum. Let
\mathcal{T}^{b}_{\gamma_{m}}
be the set of bad wavepackets as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
. Note that since
|F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}|
is locally constant on each
T_{\gamma_{m}}
, we have

\displaystyle|F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}-F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m)}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{\gamma_{m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{b}_{\gamma_{m}}}\psi_{T_{\gamma_{m}}}|F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T_{\gamma_{m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{b}_{\gamma_{m}}}A^{-(K-m+1)}\frac{\lambda}{R^{\beta}}\psi_{T_{\gamma_{m}}}|F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{-(K-m+1)}\frac{\lambda}{R^{\beta}}|F_{\gamma_{m}}^{(m+1)}|^{2}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m+1)}-F_{\tau_{i}}^{(m)}|\lesssim A^{-(K-m+1)}\frac{\lambda}{R^{\beta}}g_{m}.

So, for
x\in\Lambda_{k}
, by Lemma
5.1
, we have
g_{m}(x)\lesssim A^{K-m}R^{\beta}
. Now, we may choose
A
sufficiently large such that we get the desired inequality.
∎

We also have the following bound on
\lambda
, which is analogous to
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
. This is the step where the
4
-broad formulation is needed; passing to the pruned
F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}
is not available for the
4
-linear version.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}
is nonempty, then

\lambda\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}^{1/2}R^{\beta/2}.

Proof.

Say,
x\in U_{\lambda}\cap B
where
B\subset\Lambda_{k}
is a "box" with sidelength
R_{k}
. By Lemma
5.2
, we may pass to
F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}\subset\tau_{i}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}
:

\lambda\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}(x)|\leq\frac{\lambda}{1000}+\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}(x)|.

Then, using Lemma
5.1
,

\lambda\lesssim\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}(x)|\leq\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}(x)|\leq R_{k}^{1/2}g_{k}^{1/2}(x)\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}R_{k}^{1/2}R^{\beta/2}.

∎

6.
Multilinear Restriction Estimates

Recall from (
3
), it suffices to show

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}|=\sum_{k}\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

We will eventually get a uniform estimate for
\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|
, for each
k
. In this section, we will get an
L^{8}
estimate on
\Lambda_{k}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

(6)

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}R^{4\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

By Lemma
5.2
, we can pass to
F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}\subset\tau_{i}}F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}
, just as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
. Moreover, we can bound by the geometric mean

\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{1/4}.

We split into two regimes.

First, consider
R_{k}\leq R^{\alpha}
. By the
L^{8}
multilinear restriction estimate Theorem
3.1
,

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim\sum_{B=B_{R_{k}}\subset\Lambda_{k}}\int_{B}\prod_{i=1}^{4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{B\subset\Lambda_{k}}\frac{1}{|B|^{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left(\int_{B}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right).

By
L^{2}
orthogonality and Hölder’s,

\int_{B}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{B}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}=\int_{B}g_{k}\lesssim|B|^{1/2}\left(\int_{B}|g_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim|B|R^{\beta+O(\epsilon)}.

Therefore, we have

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R^{4\beta+O(\epsilon)}|\Lambda_{k}|.

Now, consider
R^{\alpha}<R_{k}\leq R^{\beta}
. If we ignore
x_{4}
and only use the
L^{6}
trilinear restriction estimate Theorem
3.1
, we get by the same argument as in the previous case that

(7)

\lambda^{6}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R^{3\beta+O(\epsilon)}|\Lambda_{k}|.

However, we can do better; we can apply
L^{8}

4
-linear estimate Theorem
3.1
at the scale
R^{\alpha}
and using the locally constant property, we can then further use the
L^{6}
trilinear estimate Theorem
3.1
. This is similar to the argument in
[
9
, Section 4.1]
. This gives

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim\sum_{B\subset\Lambda_{k}}\sum_{B_{R^{\alpha}}\subset B}\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{B_{R^{\alpha}}\subset\Lambda_{k}}\frac{1}{|B_{R^{\alpha}}|^{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left(\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right).

By
L^{2}
orthogonality and Hölder’s and the locally constant property of
g_{k}
,

\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}|F_{\tau_{i}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha},\gamma^{\prime}\subset\tau_{i}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\lesssim R_{k}R^{-\alpha}\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}\sum_{\gamma_{k}}|F_{\gamma_{k}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\\
=R_{k}R^{-\alpha}\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}g_{k}\lesssim R_{k}R^{-\alpha}|B_{R^{\alpha}}|^{1/2}\left(\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}|g_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim R_{k}R^{-\alpha}|B_{R^{\alpha}}|R^{\beta+O(\epsilon)}.

Therefore, we have

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R_{k}R^{\beta-\alpha+O(\epsilon)}\sum_{B_{R^{\alpha}}\subset\Lambda_{k}}\frac{1}{|B_{R^{\alpha}}|^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{B_{R^{\alpha}}}\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha},\gamma^{\prime}\subset\tau_{i}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right).

Similarly to the local constancy of
g_{k}
, we have that

\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}

are roughly constant on cubes
B_{R^{\alpha}}
. Hence,

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R_{k}R^{\beta-\alpha+O(\epsilon)}\sum_{B\subset\Lambda_{k}}\int_{B}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha},\gamma^{\prime}\subset\tau_{i}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right).

By the
L^{6}
trilinear restriction estimate Theorem
3.1
, we have

\int_{B}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha},\gamma^{\prime}\subset\tau_{i}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{B}\sum_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=R^{-\alpha}}|F_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}|^{2}\right).

By the same orthogonality argument as above, we have

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R_{k}R^{4\beta-\alpha+O(\epsilon)}|\Lambda_{k}|.

In summary,

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}|\Lambda_{k}|\begin{cases}R^{4\beta},&R_{k}\leq R^{\alpha}\\
R_{k}R^{4\beta-\alpha},&R_{k}>R^{\alpha}\end{cases}=R^{O(\epsilon)}\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}R^{4\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

For clarity moving forward, we will omit
R^{O(\epsilon)}
as it is harmless for our argument.

7.
Adding exponents: from
L^{8}
to
L^{p}

The
L^{p}
estimate

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|

follows by combining (
6
) with an improved estimate for
|\Lambda_{k}|
.
We can first quickly dispense with
k=0
. Indeed, by Lemma
5.3
and (
6
), we use the trivial estimate
|\Lambda_{0}|\leq|\Omega_{R}|
and we are done. Now, we focus on
k\geq 1
.
To get such an estimate, we decompose
g_{k}
into a high and low piece. Define the adjacency relation
\gamma_{k}\sim\gamma_{k}^{\prime}
if
\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k}^{\prime}
are the same or adjacent caps. Let

g_{k}^{h}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\not\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}\overline{F_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}},

and

g_{k}^{l}=\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}}F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}\overline{F_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}}.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have

A^{2K-2k}R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}^{l}|^{2}+\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{2}.

We will show
g_{k}^{l}
is comparable to
g_{k+1}
.
Note that

g_{k}^{l}(x)\leq g_{k+1}(x)+\left|\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{k+1}\sim\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}\\
\gamma_{k+1},\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}adjacent\end{subarray}}F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k+1}}(x)\overline{F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}(x)}\right|.

Thus, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

|g_{k}^{l}(x)|\leq g_{k+1}(x)+\sum_{\gamma_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{k+1},\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset\gamma_{k}\\
\gamma_{k+1},\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}adjacent\end{subarray}}\frac{|F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k+1}}(x)|^{2}+|F^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_{k+1}^{\prime}}(x)|^{2}}{2}\lesssim g_{k+1}(x).

By Lemma
5.1
, we have

\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}^{l}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k+1}|^{2}\lesssim A^{2K-2k-2}R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

Therefore, we can choose
A
sufficiently large such that

\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}^{l}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}A^{2K-2k}R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|.

Hence, the high piece dominates so that

R^{2\beta}|\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim\int_{\Lambda_{k}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{2}\leq|\Lambda_{k}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{q}\right)^{2/q}

which gives for
q\geq 2
,

R^{\beta q}|\Lambda_{k}|\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{R}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{q}.

From this point further, we ignore
x_{4}
and only use
3
-dimensional considerations; namely, the decoupling of the cone into planks Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
. We use these decouplings of the cone to get the following improved orthogonality for
g_{k}^{h}
. The high piece has frequency supported on differences of separated
\gamma_{k+1}
. After rescaling, these difference sets are roughly cone planks in the first three frequency variables. We refer to
[
8
, Lemma 7, Lemma 8]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

For
4\leq\frac{2}{\beta}\leq q\leq 6
, and
R_{k}=R^{2/q}\in[R^{1/3},R^{\beta}]
,

\int_{\Omega_{R}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{q}\lessapprox R_{k}^{-1}R\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}|^{2q}.

For
R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
,

\int_{\Omega_{R}}|g_{k}^{h}|^{6}\lessapprox\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k+1}}\|F_{\gamma_{k+1}}^{(k+1)}\|_{L^{12}(\Omega_{R})}^{4}\right)^{3}.

Proof.

We deviate from the presentation in
[
8
, Section 2.3-2.4]
for clarity. In particular, their arguments are completely geometric. We reformulate in terms of the extension operator, and simplify the argument down to a simple sequence of variable changes. Fix
x_{4}
and only consider
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the first three variables; integrating over
x_{4}
and Minkowski’s inequality at the end will give the inequalities over
\Omega_{R}
.

Let
E
be the extension operator associated with the moment curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e. for any interval
J\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
f_{J}:J\to\mathbb{C}
,

Ef_{J}(x)=\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1]}f_{J}(t)e(x\cdot(t,t^{2},t^{3}))\;dt.

Let
J_{k}
be the interval of length
R_{k}^{-1}
associated with the cap
\gamma_{k}
. Consider the analog of the high piece

G(x)=\sum_{J_{k}}\sum_{J_{k+1}\not\sim J_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset J_{k}}Ef_{J_{k+1}}(x)\overline{Ef_{J_{k+1}^{\prime}}}(x).

After an affine rescaling, we will see that for each
J_{k}
,

G_{J_{k}}(x)=\sum_{J_{k+1}\not\sim J_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset J_{k}}Ef_{J_{k+1}}(x)\overline{Ef_{J_{k+1}^{\prime}}}(x)

is Fourier supported on a thin neighborhood of a cone plank. Indeed, by two changes of variables,

\displaystyle G_{J_{k}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J_{k+1}\not\sim J_{k+1}^{\prime}\subset J_{k}}\int_{J_{k+1}\times J_{k+1}^{\prime}}f(t)\overline{f(s)}e(x\cdot(t-s,t^{2}-s^{2},t^{3}-s^{3}))\;dtds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{k+1}\not\sim J_{k+1}^{\prime}\\
\subset J_{k}\end{subarray}}\int_{[R_{k+1}^{-1},R_{k}^{-1}]}\int_{J_{k+1}+J_{k+1}^{\prime}}g(u,v)e(x\cdot(u,uv,\frac{u^{3}}{4}+\frac{3}{4}uv^{2}))\;dvdu

\subset J_{k}\end{subarray}}\int_{[R^{-\epsilon},1]}\int_{J_{k+1}+J_{k+1}^{\prime}}g(R_{k}u^{\prime},v)e(\frac{1}{R_{k}}x\cdot(u^{\prime},u^{\prime}v,\frac{(u^{\prime})^{3}}{4R_{k}^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}u^{\prime}v^{2}))\;dvdu^{\prime},

\frac{R}{R_{k}}

R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}

R_{k}^{2}

3.3

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|G(x)|^{6}\;dx=R_{k}^{3}\int_{B_{R/R_{k}}}|\tilde{G}(y)|^{6}\;dy

\displaystyle=R_{k}^{3}\sum_{B_{R_{k}^{2}}\subset B_{R/R_{k}}}\|\tilde{G}\|_{L^{6}(B_{R_{k}^{2}})}^{6}

\displaystyle\lessapprox R_{k}^{3}\sum_{B_{R_{k}^{2}}\subset B_{R/R_{k}}}\left(\sum_{J_{k}}\|\tilde{G}_{J_{k}}\|_{L^{6}(B_{R_{k}^{2}})}^{2}\right)^{3}.

\int_{B_{R}}|G(x)|^{6}\;dx\lessapprox\left(\sum_{J_{k}}\|G_{J_{k}}\|_{L^{6}(B_{R})}^{2}\right)^{3}\lesssim R^{O(\epsilon)}\left(\sum_{J_{k+1}}\|Ef_{J_{k+1}}\|_{L^{12}(B_{R})}^{4}\right)^{3}.

R^{-1}

\frac{1}{R_{k}^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{R}{R_{k}}\right)^{-1}
in this regime. By the same computation as in the previous case, we get the desired inequality.

8.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Fix
p\in(10,14]
with
\alpha=\left(\frac{p-6}{2}\right)\beta-1
,
\max\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{p-6}\}\leq\beta\leq\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{p-8}\}
. The main strategy is to combine (
6
) with Corollary
7.2
. In some cases, we do not need the full strength of
(
6
)
, only the
L^{6}
estimate (
7

R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
. Case II verifies for the intermediate scales and Case III verifies for the largest scales.

8.1.
Case Ia:
R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
,
p\leq 12

By Corollary
7.2
and (
7
), we get

\lambda^{6}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{3\beta}\frac{R^{3\beta}}{\lambda^{6}}|\Omega_{R}|

which gives

\lambda^{12}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{6\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

By our assumption in Section
2
that
\lambda\geq R^{\beta/2}
, we get

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

8.2.
Case Ib:
R_{k}\leq R^{1/3}
,
p>12

If
R_{k}\leq R^{\alpha}
, then by Corollary
7.2
and (
6
), we get

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{4\beta}\frac{R^{3\beta}}{\lambda^{6}}|\Omega_{R}|.

By our assumption in Section
2
that
\lambda\geq R^{\beta/2}
, we get

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

Alternatively, now suppose
R_{k}>R^{\alpha}
if any scales exist. By Corollary
7.2
and (
6
), we get

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{4\beta}\frac{R^{3\beta-\alpha}}{R_{k}^{2}R}|\Omega_{R}|.

By Lemma
5.3
, this gives

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|

\displaystyle\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(7-\frac{p}{2})-\alpha-1}}{R_{k}^{2}}\lambda^{p-8}|\Omega_{R}|

\displaystyle\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(7-\frac{p}{2})-\alpha-1}}{R_{k}^{2}}(R_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}})^{p-8}|\Omega_{R}|

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R_{k}^{\frac{p}{2}-6}}{R^{\alpha+1-3\beta}}|\Omega_{R}|.

Note
\alpha+1=\beta\left(\frac{p}{2}-3\right)
. Since
p>12
, this gives

\frac{R_{k}^{\frac{p}{2}-6}}{R^{\alpha+1-3\beta}}=\left(\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-6}\leq 1.

Hence, we get
\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

8.3.
Case II:
R^{1/3}<R_{k}<R^{\frac{4}{p-2}}

Let
R_{k}=R^{\frac{2}{q}}
with
\frac{p-2}{2}\leq q\leq 6
. We split into two subcases depending on the size of
\lambda
. First, suppose
\lambda\geq R_{k}
. By Corollary
7.2
and (
6
), we get

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{4\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q-3)+1}}{R_{k}^{3}\lambda^{2q-6}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}.

Since
q\geq\frac{p-2}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|

\displaystyle\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q+1-\frac{p}{2})+1}}{R_{k}^{3}\lambda^{2q-p+2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q+1-\frac{p}{2})+1}}{R_{k}^{3}R_{k}^{2q-p+2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q+1-p)+\frac{p}{2}\beta+1}}{R_{k}^{2q-p+5}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q+1-p)+3\beta+\alpha+2}}{R_{k}^{2q-p+5}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}

R_{k}^{q}=R^{2}
and that

\frac{R^{\alpha}}{R_{k}}\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}\leq\max\left\{\frac{R^{\beta}}{R_{k}},1\right\}.

For
p\geq 11\geq q+5

\left(\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\beta}}\right)^{p-q-5}\leq 1.

These give
\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.
Also for

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|\left(\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\beta}}\right)^{p-q-4}\leq R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}|\Omega_{R}|.

\lambda^{8}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{4\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q-3)+1}}{R_{k}^{2q-3}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}.

\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|\lessapprox R^{\frac{p}{2}\beta}\frac{R^{\beta(q+1-\frac{p}{2})+1}}{R_{k}^{2q-p+5}}|\Omega_{R}|\max\left\{1,\frac{R_{k}}{R^{\alpha}}\right\}

8.4.
Case III:
R_{k}>R^{\frac{4}{p-2}}

Let

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}|U_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k}|

\frac{R_{k}^{\frac{p}{2}-4}}{R}\leq\frac{(R^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta}-1}}{R}=\frac{R^{\alpha}}{R^{\beta}},

9.
Broad-to-linear Reduction - Proof of Theorem
1.2

Up to this point, we have shown Theorem
1.3
. Let us restate: for any
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{4}
caps with
|\tau_{i}|\sim 1
and
\sim 1
-separated, we have

(8)

\|\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lessapprox R^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

To show the linear inequality for
p\geq 11
, we follow an argument similar to
[
9
, Section 2]
. Let
C(R)
be the best constant satisfying

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\leq C(R)R^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

By iterating the following inequality, we have the desired conclusion
C(R)\lessapprox 1
.

Lemma 9.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
,

C(R)\lesssim_{\epsilon}C(R^{1-\epsilon})+R^{1000\epsilon}.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
K\sim R^{\epsilon\beta}
. By a standard broad-narrow argument, we have

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{|\tau|=K^{-1}}\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}+K^{100}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\tau_{i}|=K^{-1}\\
d(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})\geq\frac{1}{10}K^{-1}\end{subarray}}\|\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p},

where the first sum is over all caps
\tau
with length
\frac{1}{K}
and the second sum is over all quadruples of caps
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})
with length
\frac{1}{K}
and
\frac{1}{10K}
separated. The narrow term we compute by rescaling and the induction hypothesis. Indeed, fix
\tau
and let
T
be an affine transformation rescaling
\tau
to the full moment curve over
[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
\widehat{G}(\xi)=\widehat{F}(T^{-1}\xi)
. Note
|\det T|\sim K^{10}
. This gives

\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lesssim\frac{1}{K^{10(p-1)}}\|G\|_{L^{p}(T^{-1}(\Omega_{R}))}^{p}.

Note that
T^{-1}(\Omega_{R})
is roughly a

\frac{R}{K}\times\frac{R}{K^{2}}\times\frac{R}{K^{3}}\times\frac{R^{\alpha}}{K^{4}}

regular box. We will trivially extend this to a

\frac{R}{K}\times\frac{R}{K^{2}}\times\frac{R}{K^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\times\frac{R^{\alpha}}{K^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}

regular box. Applying the induction, we get

\displaystyle\|G\|_{L^{p}(T^{-1}(\Omega_{R}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\frac{R}{K^{\frac{1}{\beta}}})\left(\frac{R}{K^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\right)^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}\frac{|\Omega_{R}|}{K^{3+\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta}}}\max_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=KR^{-\beta}}\|G_{\gamma^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}

\displaystyle=C(\frac{R}{K^{\frac{1}{\beta}}})\frac{R^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}}{K^{p}}|\Omega_{R}|\max_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=KR^{-\beta}}\|G_{\gamma^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

By reversing the change of variables, we get

\max_{|\gamma^{\prime}|=KR^{-\beta}}\|G_{\gamma^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}\lesssim K^{10p}\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

We combine the previous inequalities to get

\sum_{|\tau|=K^{-1}}\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lesssim\frac{1}{K^{p-11}}C(R/K^{1/\beta})R^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

Since
p\geq 11
, we get

\frac{1}{K^{p-11}}\leq 1,

and so, we get the correct bound for the narrow term. By a similar rescaling argument and using (
8
), we also get

K^{100}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\tau_{i}|=K^{-1}\\
d(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})\geq\frac{1}{10}K^{-1}\end{subarray}}\|\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}|F_{\tau_{i}}|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}^{p}\lessapprox K^{O(1)}R^{\beta\frac{p}{2}}|\Omega_{R}|\max_{\gamma}\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{p}.

∎
