Topological invariant of periodic many body wavefunction from charge pumping simulation
Haoxiang Chen, Yubing Qian, Weiluo Ren, Xiang Li, Ji Chen

Abstract
Many-body topological quantum states host exotic quantum phenomena and lie at the forefront of developing next-generation quantum technologies. Recently emerged neural network wavefunction methods have established themselves as a powerful computational framework for accessing these states, enabling the variational machine learning calculation of the system’s ground state wavefunction. However, reliable computation of topological invariants remains an open challenge when the whole deterministic energy spectrum is not available. In this work, we introduce a robust approach to determining topological invariant based on simulating the charge pumping process, by monitoring the response of polarization upon flux insertion. By applying this method, we accurately extract the Chern numbers for Abelian fractional Chern insulators. Our approach also enables the first neural-network-wavefunction-based identification of anomalous composite Fermi liquid states. Our work resolves a key bottleneck in applying neural network wavefunctions to correlated topological matter, and the method proposed is also generally applicable to other many-body approaches, thereby opening up new avenues for future research in this field.

Theoretically, the many-body Chern number
C
is defined by the full space integral

C=\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}{{\bm{\uptheta}}}}{2\pi\mathbbm{i}}\left[\langle\partial_{\theta_{y}}{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}|\partial_{\theta_{x}}{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{\theta_{x}}{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}|\partial_{\theta_{y}}{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle\right],

(1)

where
|{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle
is a periodic state obtained from
|{\Psi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle
via a gauge transformation.
Here,
|{\Psi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle
denotes the original many-body ground state wavefunction at twist boundary condition
{\bm{\uptheta}}=(\theta_{x},\theta_{y})
.
The transformation is given by
|{\Phi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle=\exp(-\frac{\mathbbm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{a=x,y}\theta_{a}{\mathbf{B}}_{a}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})|{\Psi_{\bm{\uptheta}}}\rangle
, where
\hat{\mathbf{r}}
is the electron position operator and
{\mathbf{B}}_{x},{\mathbf{B}}_{y}
are the supercell reciprocal lattice vectors.

To calculate the many-body Chern number via this definition,
the effective Brillouin zone are divided into plaquettes,
and the Berry curvature are computed for each plaquette using finite difference methods
12
.
This calculation is usually done with exact diagonalization (ED)
7
,
where wavefunctions and the overlap between them are available deterministically.
Ref.
19
simplifies the calculation by introducing the one-plaquette formula,
where the Berry curvature itself is proven to be virtually quantized and
the whole integration in Eq.
1
can be replaced by calculating the integrand on a single plaquette.
However, in the presence of ground state degeneracy, this method requires access to all degenerate ground states.
Alternatively, the Chern number can be determined from other global properties of wavefunction and without integration.
When the system respects certain rotational or inversion symmetry,
the topological invariant can be obtained using wavefunctions only at high-symmetric points in the effective Brillouin zone
31
;
22
.
However, this approach is constrained by the system geometry and has not yet been extended to fractional Chern insulators.
Other methods draw upon the modern polarization theory
18
;
42
,
using Resta’s polarization operator
\hat{Z}_{{\mathbf{B}}}=\exp(\mathbbm{i}{\mathbf{B}}\cdot\sum_{i}\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{i})
to determine the topological invariants.
For instance, Ref.
14
demonstrates that by using polarization along three specific directions,
the topological invariant can be determined up to modulo 2.
Another approach is adopted in Ref.
17
,
tracing the polarization phase shift when inserting magnetic flux.
The authors numerically calculated for the infinite cylinder geometry and also introduced the charge pumping for torus.
So far these polarization-based approaches are still confined to situations where topological invariants are integer.

Consequently, none of the methods mentioned above are readily compatible with neural network wavefunctions
when it comes to the FCI states.
To fully unleash the potential of neural networks on precision and scalability,
especially its advantage of automatically taking band-mixing effect into account
25
;
30
,
here we introduce a new method inspired by
the concept of topological charge pumping
34
;
20
;
17
,
to calculate the topological invariant for FCI states.

We demonstrate that, despite the ground state degeneracy inherent to FCI states,
the topological invariant can still be
robustly extracted from the evolution of the polarization during charge pumping simulating.
As illustrated in Fig.
1
d-f,
the phase shift of polarization at phase twist
{\bm{\uptheta}}=(\theta_{x},\theta_{y})
is

\mathcal{C}(\theta_{x})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{Im}\ln\frac{\langle\Psi_{\theta_{x},0}|\hat{Z}_{{\mathbf{B}}_{y}}|\Psi_{\theta_{x},0}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{0,0}|\hat{Z}_{{\mathbf{B}}_{y}}|\Psi_{0,0}\rangle}.

(2)

The wavefunction at twisted boundary condition
{\bm{\uptheta}}
can be acquired by numerical simulation,
and the polarization phase is tracked for states with intermediate twists, and the continuous branch is chosen of the logarithm (see SI.
II A
).
When a unit flux is inserted,
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{x}=2\pi)
is fractionally quantized, reflecting the topological invariant.
Here we evaluate the polarization along the supercell reciprocal lattice vectors
{\mathbf{B}}_{y}
,
chosen to ensure that
Z_{{\mathbf{B}}_{y}}
does not change the total momentum,
i.e.,
N_{e}{\mathbf{B}}_{y}=m{\mathbf{b}}_{x}+n{\mathbf{b}}_{y}
,
where
N_{e}
is the electron number,
m,n
are integer numbers,
and
{\mathbf{b}}_{x}
,
{\mathbf{b}}_{y}
are primitive cell reciprocal basis vectors.
We also note that
the proposed method can be generalized to other twist directions (see SI.
III A
)
and polarization operator with other wave vectors in the effective Brillouin zone (see SI.
III B
).

The phase shift
\mathcal{C}(\theta_{x}=2\pi)
obtained in Eq.
2
can be justified as a reliable approximation of the Chern number
C
via topological charge pumping.
Following the generalized Laughlin’s argument,
the system’s topological property can be determined with the pumped charge upon adiabatically inserting a unit flux.
As illustrated in Fig.
1
c, consider the adiabatic
insertion of a magnetic flux along the
x
-direction in a toroidal geometry,
the current is given by the time derivative of the polarization

J=\text{d}P/\text{d}t=\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{d}~\text{Im}\ln\langle\hat{Z}_{\mathbf{B}}\rangle/\text{d}t
,
according to the modern polarization theory
18
;
42
.
The total pumped charge for unit flux insertion is thus
Q=\int\text{d}tJ=\frac{1}{2\pi}\text{Im}\ln\langle\hat{Z}_{\mathbf{B}}\rangle~|_{\theta=0}^{2\pi}
,
Laughlin’s argument states that tracing the flux insertion process enables the identification of whether the system is topologically non-trivial.
For trivial and integer quantum Hall states in Fig.
1
d-e,
the total pumped charge should be
0
and
1
, and the phase shift of polarization is
0
and
2\pi
, respectively.

For FCI states in Fig.
1
f, the ground states are nearly-degenerate,
and unit flux insertion does not guarantee the ground state return to the initial states,
for which the charge center do not return to its initial position at zero flux and the pumped charge is non-integer.
However the connection between polarization phase and topological invariant stays valid.
An intuition can be acquired from spectral flow,
where the nearly-degenerate FCI ground states flow into each other upon flux insertion
41
;
4
.
With
q
unit fluxes inserted, the ground state can go back to the initial one and the polarization phase should consequently shift by multiples of
2\pi
.
Averaging over this process, the polarization phase shift per unit flux insertion is
2\pi\frac{p}{q}
, which directly encodes the fractional topological order, where
p
and
q
are integers.
A detailed discussion of the theory is provided in SI.

Our charge pumping method can be applied to individual momentum sectors, and directly confirming the existence of non-trivial topological property.

Other CFL degenerate states at

It is worth noting that for a gapless system such as the CFL phase, the topological invariant is not well-defined. Nevertheless, the Hall conductance remains fractionally quantized, and our method remains applicable in cases where the polarization does not vanish, for example, in small systems.
These results exemplify the charge pumping as a robust identifier for the putative CFL from normal fermi liquid.

To summarize, we demonstrate that the polarization operator

\hat{Z}_{\mathbf{B}}
,
when tracked under adiabatic flux insertion, serves as a robust estimator of topological invariants for fractional anomalous Hall states in strongly interacting flat-band systems.
We re-examine the Abelian FCI states in the twisted
\text{MoTe}_{2}
system, and further extend the applicability of this method to CFL states.
Notably, the charge pumping process can also be generalized to probe the whole anyonic statistics.
When choosing other wave vector
b
in the Brillouin zone,
the polarization operator
\hat{Z}_{\mathbf{B}}
generally maps one ground state to other within the manifold.
The full anyonic statistics is encoded in the matrix representation of
\hat{Z}_{\mathbf{B}}
, while in this work we only calculated for the diagonal component.
For future works, the mixed estimator of polarization,
along with adiabatic flux insertion,
can be a direct probe to anyonic statistics.
