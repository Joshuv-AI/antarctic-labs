The applicability of pregnancy diagnosis using interferon-stimulated genes in embryo-transferred Japanese Black cattle
Authors: Hitomi Yoshino, Toh-ichi Hirata, Hideo Matsuda, Tadayuki Yamanouchi, Yutaka Hashiyada, Kei Imai, Toru Takahashi, Toshina Ishiguro-Oonuma, Kazuyoshi Hashizume, Keiichiro Kizaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The early pregnancy diagnosis method by measuring interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) in peripheral blood can be used in cattle after artificial insemination (AI) with high accuracy. ISGs are genes whose expression is induced by interferon tau (IFNT) in the trophectoderm of the conceptus during early pregnancy in cattle. We evaluated the applicability of ISG-based pregnancy diagnosis in embryo-transferred (ET) cattle. ISG levels in the peripheral blood were measured on days 19 and 21 of gestation in ET cattle, and diagnostic accuracy was calculated. The diagnostic potential of ISG was assessed using previously established threshold values for AI cattle. The diagnostic threshold values for ET cattle were also calculated using the Youden index from the receiver operating characteristic curve. Regardless of the threshold values used, the results showed significantly higher ISG expression in pregnant than in non-pregnant cattle on day 21, but not on day 19. Pregnancy diagnosis in ET cattle resulted in reduced accuracy, particularly on day 19, compared to AI cattle. These findings suggest that the causes of non-conception may differ between AI and ET cattle. These findings highlight the limitations of using ISG-based early pregnancy diagnosis during the preimplantation period in ET cattle.

Introduction

Shortening the nonconception period in cattle is important for maintaining productivity. Commonly used methods for pregnancy diagnosis in cattle include transrectal ultrasonography after day 30, non-return to estrus, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays of milk and/or peripheral blood for detecting pregnancy-associated glycoproteins (PAGs), which can be used as early as 24 days of gestation (Oliveira et al. 2020; Wallace et al. 2015). If pregnancy can be diagnosed before the next estrus, reinsemination can be performed at the subsequent estrus in nonconceived cattle. This approach relies on the early detection of gestational status.

Several studies have confirmed that the expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) increases in peripheral blood leukocytes during early pregnancy in cattle (Gifford et al. 2007; Green et al. 2010; Kizaki et al. 2013; Shirasuna et al. 2012). Among leukocytes, ISGs are primarily expressed in granulocytes and can be measured after isolating the granulocyte fraction. A significant increase in ISG expression distinguishes pregnant from non-pregnant cattle by day 14 after estrus (Kizaki et al. 2013). However, granulocyte isolation is labor-intensive and impractical for routine diagnosis to enable a more practical approach, we have conducted studies aiming to diagnose early pregnancy by measuring ISG expression in RNA derived from whole blood collected using PAXgene RNA tubes. This method has demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy at 21 days after artificial insemination (AI) and is applicable to both Holstein and Japanese Black (JB) breeds, regardless of breed-specific differences (Dalmaso et al. 2020; Pugliesi et al. 2014; Yoshino et al. 2018, 2020). Furthermore, we previously proposed ISG expression cutoffs between days 18 and 21 after AI in JB cattle, achieving positive and negative predictive values exceeding 80% on each day (Yoshino et al. 2020). In contrast, for embryo transfer (ET) cattle, diagnosis has been reported on day 21 after estrus (14 days after ET) (Yoshino et al. 2023). As the diagnosis on day 21 after estrus may have already begun the next estrus of the unconceived cattle, it is desirable to make an earlier diagnosis in cattle.

ET involves the transfer of embryos with confirmed developmental competence, produced either in vivo or in vitro, into synchronized recipient cattle. Fertilization and subsequent differentiation into inner cell mass and trophoblast cells are confirmed prior to transfer. Therefore, the causes of nonconception may differ between AI and ET cattle. Findings from AI studies may not be directly applicable to ET cattle, and their generalizability requires further evaluation. In this study, we applied previously established ISG cutoffs, interferon-stimulated gene 15 ubiquitin-like modifier (ISG15) and MX dynamin-like GTPases MX1 and MX2, that diagnose pregnancy with high accuracy on days 19 and 21 after estrus in AI cattle, to ET cattle, and evaluated their diagnostic performance. In addition, we calculated the threshold values of ISGs for ET cattle using the Youden index from the receiver operating characteristic curve and evaluated the diagnostic accuracy.

Materials and methods

The study included three heifers and 24 parous JB cattle from an experimental farm at our institution. The 27 JB cattle were fitted with a PRID DELTA device (containing 1.55 g of progesterone and a capsule containing 10 mg of estradiol benzoate; Aska Pharmaceutical, Tokyo, Japan) for nine days. Cloprostenol (500 µg), a prostaglandin F2alpha analog (Estrumate; Nagase Medicals, Itami, Japan), was administered intramuscularly two days before PRID DELTA removal. Fertirelin acetate (100 µg), a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) analog (Conceral; Nagase Medicals), was administered intramuscularly 36 h after PRID DELTA removal. Estrus was induced the following day, which was designated as day 0. JB embryos produced in vitro, following the method described by Hirata et al. (Hirata et al. 2008), were used for ET on day 7. The embryos were fresh, stage of blastocysts, and the quality was classified as code 1, according to the protocol of the International Embryo Technology Society (IETS). After ET, whole blood samples were collected from the jugular vein using PAXgene RNA blood collection tubes (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany) on days 19 and 21 (i.e., 12 and 14 days after ET, respectively). A total of 27 cows were used in this study and blood was collected from 16 animals on day 19 and 11 animals on day 21. Pregnancy was confirmed by ultrasonography at approximately 42 days of gestation. The outcomes of pregnancy diagnosis were as follows: on day 19, six pregnant and 10 non-pregnant JB cattle; on day 21, seven pregnant and four non-pregnant JB cattle.

Peripheral blood RNA was extracted using the PAXgene Blood RNA kit (QIAGEN). RNA quantity and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (ND-1000; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Reverse transcription was performed using 1 µg of total RNA, random primers, and a high-capacity reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Quantitative RT-PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed to detect ISGs (ISG15, MX1, and MX2) and the internal control gene glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), as described previously (Yoshino et al. 2023). GAPDH was used to normalize the mRNA expression levels. GAPDH, ribosomal protein L27, and beta-actin were evaluated as reference genes for RT-qPCR, and no significant differences were observed among them (data not shown).

Diagnostic threshold values were calculated using the Youden index based on previously reported data from JB cattle after AI (Yoshino et al. 2020). The threshold values on day 19 were 0.168 for ISG15, 0.121 for MX1 and 0.257 for MX2, respectively. The values on day 21 were 0.346, 0.203, and 0.29 for ISG15, MX1, and MX2, respectively. Diagnostic threshold values were calculated using the Youden index from the receiver operating characteristic curve based on the ISG expression level in JB cattle after ET in this examination. Sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated using the procedure described previously (Yoshino et al. 2020). The sensitivity, TP/ (TP + FN) × 100 (the probability of a diagnosis of pregnancy among cows that were truly pregnant); specificity, TN/ (TN + FP) × 100 (the probability that cows that were diagnosed as non-pregnant were truly non-pregnant); PPV, TP/ (TP + FP) × 100; and NPV, TN/ (TN + FN) × 100, were calculated. The accuracy of pregnancy determination, (TP + TN)/ (TP + FP + TN + FN) × 100, was evaluated using the diagnostic accuracy.

Results

The mRNA expression levels of ISG15, MX1, and MX2 in pregnant and non-pregnant JB cattle on days 19 and 21 are shown in Fig. 1. On day 21, expression levels of all three genes differed significantly between pregnant and non-pregnant cattle. In contrast, on day 19, expression levels of the three ISGs in peripheral blood were similar between the two groups. Table 1 presents the sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, PPV, and NPV calculated using the diagnostic threshold values reported previously (Yoshino et al. 2020). On day 19, the sensitivities of ISG15, MX1, and MX2 exceeded 80%, whereas specificity was limited to 50%. The PPV and NPV values for ISG15, MX1, and MX2 on day 19 were 50%, 50%, and 54.5%, and 83.3%, 83.3%, and 100%, respectively. On day 21, accuracy indices for all ISGs, including sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, PPV, and NPV, were high. Based on the ISG expression in ET cattle (Fig. 1), the diagnostic threshold values were calculated using the Youden index. The threshold values on day 19 were 0.159 for ISG15, 0.199 for MX1 and 0.281 for MX2, respectively. The threshold values on day 21 were 0.496 for ISG15, 0.309 for MX1 and 0.651 for MX2, respectively. Table 2 presents the diagnostic accuracy calculated using these threshold values. On day 19, the accuracy trend did not differ from that in Table 1, showing low specificity and PPV. On day 21, the results were also similar to those in Table 1, and further improvements in accuracy were observed.

Discussion

It has been reported that ISG expression increases in leukocytes of pregnant cattle in response to interferon tau (IFNT), enabling early pregnancy diagnosis in AI cattle (Green et al. 2010; Yoshino et al. 2018, 2020). However, although limited studies have examined about ET cattle, findings cannot be considered equivalent, as the underlying causes of non-conception differ between AI and ET. For instance, fertilization failure does not occur in ET.

The mRNA expression levels of the three ISGs in ET cattle are shown in Fig. 1. In AI cattle, the expression levels of ISGs were significantly increased in the peripheral blood leukocytes of pregnant cattle on day 18 after AI (Gifford et al. 2007; Green et al. 2010). No differences in ISG expression were observed between pregnant and non-pregnant cattle on day 19 in ET cattle, indicating that the ISG expression pattern may differ between AI and ET.

Table 1 shows the diagnostic accuracy for each day after estrus when the diagnostic threshold values established for AI cattle were applied to expression levels in ET cattle, as shown in Fig. 1. A key observation in ET cattle was that many non-conceptive animals showed equivalent ISG expression levels to those of pregnant cattle on day 19. This trend was consistent across all three genes, with half of the non-conceptive cattle showing expression levels above the cutoff values. Consequently, the PPV on day 19 was as low as 50% (Table 1). By contrast, on day 21, the PPV for MX1 was slightly lower (77.8%), but both the PPV and NPV of ISG15 and MX2 were high, and diagnostic accuracy improved. This trend was similar when we used the diagnostic threshold values established for ET cattle (Table 2). In a previous report on AI cattle (Yoshino et al. 2020), both PPV and NPV exceeded 80% for ISG15 on day 19, confirming high diagnostic efficacy for ISG15 at that time point. A comparison between the previous and current studies is valid because expression values in both were normalized using GAPDH as a reference gene. Pregnancy diagnosis on day 21, at the period of implantation, can thus be performed with high accuracy in ET cattle. The reason for the low specificity of MX1 on day 21 remains unclear but may be attributable to the small sample size.

ISG expression is correlated with IFNT concentrations secreted by trophoblasts (Matsuyama et al. 2012), and it is likely that half of the embryos in non-conceptive cattle produced comparable amounts of IFNT on day 19. However, because these cattle ultimately failed to conceive, the cause likely occurred after day 19. Implantation in cattle begins on day 19 of pregnancy, when the trophoblast contacts the uterine epithelium, with fusion to the chorion occurring at approximately 21–22 days (King et al. 1980). The results of this study indicate that ISG levels fluctuate during this period, suggesting that implantation failure may be one of the causes of non-conception in ET cattle. Casano et al. (Casano et al. 2023) reported that ISG expression was moderate in cattle that experienced embryonic death. Embryonic death in that study was defined as the absence of an embryo or heartbeat, or the presence of only fetal membranes at pregnancy diagnosis approximately 30 days after insemination (Casano et al. 2023). The exact timing of embryonic death was unclear in the present study. Further research is needed to investigate the relationship between ISG expression and the timing of embryonic loss.

Even in ET cattle, the NPV is sufficiently high, the prediction of non-pregnant cattle based on ISG expression is useful to rebreeding during the next estrus. However, due to the low PPV, there is a risk of mistakenly keeping some non-pregnant cows as if they were pregnant, thereby extending the non-pregnant period. These findings suggest that the causes of non-conception may differ between AI and ET cattle. Further research in this area could provide fundamental insights for improving conception rates using ET techniques.
