Introduction

1.1
Main Result

The Riemann zeta function is defined in the region
{\rm Re}\ s>1
of
\mathbb{C}
by

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-s}.

(1.1)

It admits a meromorphic continuation to the complex plane with a pole at
s=1
and satisfies the functional equation
\zeta(s)=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\sin(\pi s/2)\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(1-s)
.
The function thus admits trivial zeros at negative even integers. The Riemann Hypothesis asserts that all other zeros must lie on the critical line
{\rm Re}\ s=1/2
.

This paper is concerned with the distribution of large values of the zeta function occurring on the critical line.
The main result is an upper bound on the measure of level sets of
\zeta
in the interval
[T,2T]
for values of the order
\log\log T
, conditionally on the Riemann Hypothesis:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
k>0
and
V=V(T)\sim k\log\log T
. We have for
T
large enough

\frac{1}{T}\text{\rm meas}\Big\{t\in[T,2T]:\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|>V\Big\}\leq C_{k}\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}},

where
C_{k}=\exp(e^{ck})
for some absolute constant
c>0
.

An upper bound of this form was proved unconditionally on the range
0<k<2
in
[
1
]
.
A matching lower bound with constant
(k^{2}\log k)^{-k^{2}+{\rm o}(1)}
is proved in
[
5
]
for all
k>0
.
These results are to be contrasted with Selberg’s Central Limit Theorem which states that for any
v\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\text{\rm meas}\Big\{t\in[T,2T]:\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|>v\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\log\log T}\Big\}=\int_{v}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\rm d}x.

Theorem
1.1
and the corresponding lower bound show that Gaussian fluctuations extend to large deviations for the whole range of order
\log\log T
, up to constant.
The interest for this regime of values stems from its relation to the moments.
In the seminal paper
[
19
]
, Soundararajan proved the following bound for
k\geq 0
under the Riemann Hypothesis:

\frac{1}{T}\text{\rm meas}\Big\{t\in[T,2T]:|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|>(\log T)^{k}\Big\}=(\log T)^{-k^{2}+{\rm o}(1)}.

(1.2)

Since the measure of level sets and the
2k
-moments satisfy the relation

\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|^{2k}{\rm d}t=2k\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2kV}\text{\rm meas}\Big\{t\in[T,2T]:|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|>e^{V}\Big\}{\rm d}V,

(1.3)

the bound (
1.2
) then implies that the
2k
-moment is
\leq(\log T)^{k^{2}+\varepsilon}
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, with the dominant contribution coming from
V
around
k\log\log T
.
In the breakthrough paper
[
10
]
, Harper sharpened this bound to

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|^{2k}{\rm d}t\leq e^{e^{\OO(k)}}(\log T)^{k^{2}}.

(The effective constant can be taken to be
e^{e^{18.63k}}
as shown in
[
21
]
.)
Theorem
1.1
and the relation (
1.3
) imply Harper’s bound:

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
k\geq 2
. We have for
T
large enough

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|^{2k}{\rm d}t\ll e^{e^{ck}}(\log T)^{k^{2}},

for the same absolute constant
c
as Theorem
1.1
.

The proof is identical to
[
1
, Corollary 1.2]
with Theorem
1.1
as the new input.
Unconditionally, upper bounds for
0\leq k\leq 2
were proved in
[
12
]
, as well as lower bounds for the whole range
k\geq 0
in
[
13
]
.
The
Moments Conjecture
of Keating & Snaith
[
16
]
predicts that the moments are asymptotic to
a_{k}g_{k}(\log T)^{k^{2}}
with explicit arithmetic constant
a_{k}
and geometric constant
g_{k}
.
Only the cases
k=1
and
k=2
are known from the classical works of Hardy & Littlewood
[
9
]
and Ingham
[
15
]
.
As argued in
[
18
]
, the optimal
C_{k}
in Theorem
1.1
should also be
a_{k}g_{k}
.
Note that as
k\to\infty
, we have the asymptotic behavior (see for example
[
7
,
14
]
)

a_{k}=(\log k)^{-k^{2}+{\rm o}(1)},\qquad g_{k}=k^{-k^{2}+{\rm o}(1)}.

(1.4)

Hence the constant in Theorem
1.1
is still far off the predicted value as it should decay to
0
sharply as
k\to\infty
.
For more information on the moments problem, we refer to the survey of Florea
[
8
]
, and to the review paper of Soundararajan
[
20
]
for the broader question of the distribution of values of
L
-functions. In Section
3
, we discuss the consequences of Theorem
1.1
to the statistics of large values on short intervals of the critical line.

As in
[
19
]
and
[
10
]
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the following conditional pointwise bound of Soundararajan:

Proposition 1.3

(
[
19
]
)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
T
be large enough and
t\in[T,2T]
. Consider
2\leq x\leq T^{2}
. Let
\lambda_{0}=0.4912\dots
denote the unique positive real solution to
e^{-\lambda}=\lambda+\lambda^{2}/2
. For all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
we have

\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t)|\leq{\rm Re}\ \sum_{n\leq x}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma+{\rm i}t}\log n}+\frac{(1+\lambda)}{2}\cdot\frac{\log T}{\log x}+\OO\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big),

(1.5)

where
a(n)=\frac{\log(x/n)}{\log x}
and
\sigma=\sigma(x,\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\log x}
.

The main challenge in estimating with good accuracy the moments or the large deviations is to take
x
large in the above bound, while maintaining sufficient control of the Dirichlet polynomial to prove Gaussian behavior.
In
[
19
]
, this was done up to
x=T^{\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\frac{\log_{3}T}{\log_{2}T}}
, and
x=T^{e^{-1000k}}
in
[
10
]
.
As explained in the next section, we rely on a random walk heuristic based on the recursive scheme of
[
3
]
which allows for a control of the partial sums up to a threshold similar to
[
10
]
.
The asymptotic (
1.4
) for
g_{k}
would be a consequence of Gaussian fluctuations in the range of primes between
T^{1/k^{2}}
and
T^{1/k}
, but this seems currently out of reach.

1.2
The Recursive Scheme

Throughout the paper, we will use the probabilistic notation where
\tau
denotes a random variable uniformly distributed on
[T,2T]
, with
\mathbf{P}
standing for the corresponding distribution and
\mathbf{E}
, for the expectation.
We will also follow the probabilistic convention of omitting the dependence on
\tau
in random variables and events.
In this notation, the proof of Theorem
1.1
amounts to estimating
for fixed positive
k
and
V\sim k\log\log T
, the probability
\mathbf{P}(H)
of the event

H=\{\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}\tau)|>V\}.

The recursive scheme of
[
3
]
consists of decomposing
H
into decreasing good events
G_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 1
, so that

\mathbf{P}(H)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\mathcal{L}+1}\mathbf{P}(H\cap G_{\ell-1}\setminus G_{\ell}),

(1.6)

with the convention that
G_{0}=[T,2T]
and
G_{\mathcal{L}+1}=\emptyset
.
The events restrict the values of the partial sums at checkpoints indexed by
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
defined by

T_{0}=1
,
T_{1}=\exp(\sqrt{\log T})
and

T_{\ell}=T^{\beta_{\ell}},\quad\beta_{\ell}=\frac{e^{\ell-1}}{\sqrt{\log T}},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L},

(1.7)

with

\mathcal{L}=1+\max\{\ell:\beta_{\ell}\leq e^{-10^{4}k}\}.

With Proposition
1.3
in mind, we define for
\lambda=1/2
,
1\leq j,\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, the partial sums

S_{\ell}^{(j)}={\rm Re}\sum_{p\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{a_{j}(p)}{p^{\sigma_{j}+{\rm i}\tau}}+{\rm Re}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p^{2}\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{a_{j}(p^{2})}{p^{2\sigma_{j}+2{\rm i}\tau}},

(1.8)

where

\sigma_{j}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\log T_{j}}\qquad a_{j}(n)=\frac{\log(T_{j}/n)}{\log T_{j}}.

The moments of
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
are estimated in Appendix
A
. In particular, the variance is approximately
\frac{1}{2}\log\log T_{\ell}
.
It is expected that the random phases
p^{-i\tau}
become essentially independent in the limit of large
T
for distinct primes, thus that the partial sums ranging in
\ell
behave like random walks with time index
t_{\ell}=\log\log T_{\ell}
.
Note that each abscissa
\sigma_{j}
gives rise to a random walk
(S_{\ell}^{(j)},1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L})
, which complicates the analysis.
Using this heuristic as a guide, if
\log|\zeta|
achieves a value of
V\sim k\log\log T
, then the value of
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
at
T_{\ell}
is most likely very close to
\kappa\log\log T_{\ell}

where
\kappa
is the gradient

\kappa=\frac{V}{\log\log T}.

The good events
G_{\ell}
for
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
are defined as to reflect the random walk behavior:

G_{\ell}=\{S_{\ell}^{(j)}\in[L_{\ell},U_{\ell}],\ \forall j\geq\ell\}\cap G_{\ell-1},

(1.9)

with

L_{\ell}=\kappa\log\log T_{\ell}-c_{\ell},\qquad U_{\ell}=\kappa\log\log T_{\ell}+c_{\ell}.

(1.10)

The parameters
c_{\ell}
capture the fluctuations around the linear interpolation.
For a random walk achieving a value
V
at time
\frac{1}{2}\log\log T
, the fluctuations at time
\frac{1}{2}\log\log T_{\ell}
are of the order

c_{\ell}\approx\Big(\log\log T-\log\log T_{\ell}\Big)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\approx(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

As seen from the proof, our current approach forces us to take a larger quantity

c_{\ell}=\beta_{\ell}^{-\gamma},\quad\gamma=1/25.

(1.11)

(The analysis allows for any
\gamma
smaller than
1/20
, cf. Equation (
B.8
).)
Note that
c_{\ell-1}=e^{\gamma}c_{\ell}>c_{\ell}
, narrowing the barriers at each subsequent checkpoint.
With these definitions, we have the following main proposition:

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
k>0
and
V=V(T)\sim k\log\log T
.
For all
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, we have for some absolute constant
c>0
and
T
large enough

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(H\cap G_{\ell-1}\setminus G_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(H\cap G_{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\leq e^{e^{ck}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

(1.13)

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof is straightforward from the above proposition, after noticing that
e^{-c_{\ell}}
is summable in
\ell
. The dominant term in the sum is (
1.13
).
∎

The proof of Proposition
1.4
uses an approximation of indicator functions in terms of Dirichlet polynomials, as detailed in Appendix
B
.
The left-hand side of (
1.12
) is smaller than

\mathbf{P}\Big(G_{\ell-1}\cap\{\exists j\geq\ell:S_{\ell}^{(j)}>U_{\ell}\}\Big)+\mathbf{P}\Big(H\cap G_{\ell-1}\cap\{\exists j\geq\ell:S_{\ell}^{(j)}<L_{\ell}\}\Big).

(1.14)

We refer to these events as the ‘upper’ and ‘lower’ barrier bounds respectively. The main new input of this paper is the estimate of the second term, which is done in Section
2.3
.
In
[
1
]
, a similar estimate was handled using a twisted fourth moment, which is insufficient when
k\geq 2
.
We rely instead in Section
2.3
on the bound (
1.5
).
An
a priori
bound is needed for this estimate and is proved in Section
2.1
. The first term in (
1.14
) is dealt with similarly in Section
2.2
.
In each case, we estimate the probability for fixed
j\geq\ell
and use a union bound on
j
. The total contribution will be negligible since

\#\{j\geq\ell\}=\mathcal{L}-\ell+1=\log\frac{\beta_{\mathcal{L}}}{\beta_{\ell}}+1\leq\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1}+1,

(1.15)

which will be absorbed by the probability yielding (
1.12
).

As in
[
19
,
10
]
, we expect that the method of proof of Theorem
1.1
can be adapted to yield sharp bounds for the large deviations and moments of certain families of Dirichlet
L
-functions.

Notation
.
We will use Vinogradov’s notation with a slight convenient twist. Let
f,g
functions of
T
. For
k
fixed, we write
f\ll g
whenever

\limsup_{T\to\infty}|f/g|\leq e^{ck^{2}},\ \text{ for some absolute constant $c>0$.}

(1.16)

We write
f\asymp g
if
f\ll g
and
f\gg g
.
To simplify the notation, we will sometimes in the proof express the checkpoints in the
\log\log
-scale, i.e.,

t_{\ell}=\log\log T_{\ell}.

Proof of Proposition
1.4

We assume throughout that
k\geq 2
. We consider
V\sim k\log\log T
and we write
\kappa=V/\log\log T
for the exact gradient.
The estimate (
1.12
) is done by splitting the probability into two estimates for the upper and lower barrier as in (
1.14
).
The results are respectively given in Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
below. For an accurate estimate, an
a priori
bound in Lemma
2.1
is first needed.

2.1
A Priori Bound

We start by proving a bound for the complement of the event

A_{\ell}=\{|S_{\ell}^{(j)}-S_{\ell-1}^{(j)}|\leq 2c_{\ell},\ \forall j\geq\ell\},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, we have

\mathbf{P}(G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}^{\rm c})\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

Proof.

As noticed in (
1.15
),
it suffices to bound the probability uniformly in
j
. Henceforth, we omit the notation by writing
S_{\ell}^{(j)}=S_{\ell}
.
We first evaluate
\mathbf{P}(A_{1}^{\rm c})
, which is simply

\mathbf{P}(A_{1}^{\rm c})\ll\log\beta_{1}^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{P}(|S_{1}|>2c_{1})\leq\frac{\log\beta_{1}^{-1}}{(2c_{1})^{2q}}\cdot\mathbf{E}[|S_{1}|^{2q}],

using Markov’s inequality for some
q>1
. Equation (
A.6
) of the appendix is applicable with the choice of
q=\lceil 4c_{1}^{2}\rceil
since
2q\beta_{1}\leq 10\beta_{1}^{1-2\gamma}\leq 1/100
for
T
large enough.
This gives

\mathbf{P}(A_{1}^{\rm c})\ll\sqrt{\log\log T}\cdot e^{-4c_{1}^{2}}\ll e^{-c_{1}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

For
\ell>1
we decompose the event in terms of the value of
S_{\ell-1}
, so that

\mathbf{P}(G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}^{\rm c})\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\sum_{u\in[L_{\ell-1},U_{\ell-1}]}\mathbf{P}\Big(\{S_{\ell}-S_{\ell-1}>2c_{\ell},S_{\ell-1}\in[u-1,u]\}\cap G_{\ell-1}\Big).

By writing

G_{\ell-1}(u)=G_{\ell-2}\cap\{S_{\ell-1}\in[u-1,u]\},

(2.1)

the above is

\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\sum_{u\in[L_{\ell-1},U_{\ell-1}]}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{|S_{\ell}-S_{\ell-1}|^{2q}}{(2c_{\ell})^{2q}}\mathbf{1}(G_{\ell-1}(u))\right].

We choose
q=\lceil 4c_{\ell}^{2}\rceil
. The polynomial
(S_{\ell}-S_{\ell-1})^{q}
has then length at most
T^{2q\beta_{\ell}}
which is
\leq T^{1/100}
for
T
large enough.
We now apply Lemma
B.1
of the appendix to get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}^{\rm c})

\displaystyle\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\cdot\sum_{u\in[L_{\ell-1},U_{\ell-1}]}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{|S_{\ell}-S_{\ell-1}|^{2q}}{(2c_{\ell})^{2q}}\right]\cdot\frac{e^{-u^{2}/t_{\ell-1}}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}

\displaystyle\ll e^{-4c_{\ell}^{2}}\cdot(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\cdot c_{\ell-1}\cdot\frac{e^{-L_{\ell-1}^{2}/t_{\ell-1}}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}},

where we used a trivial bound over
u
and Equation (
A.5
).
Since
L_{\ell-1}=\kappa t_{\ell-1}-c_{\ell-1}
, this is

\displaystyle\ll\exp\Big(\kappa^{2}(t-t_{\ell-1})+2\kappa c_{\ell-1}-c_{\ell-1}^{2}\Big)\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}},

as claimed.
∎

2.2
Upper Barrier

We observe that on the event
G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}
,
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, we have

S_{\ell}^{(j)}\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},U_{\ell}^{\prime}],\ \forall j\geq\ell,

(2.2)

for the slightly more forgiving barriers

L^{\prime}_{\ell}=\kappa t_{\ell}-4c_{\ell}\qquad U^{\prime}_{\ell}=\kappa t_{\ell}+4c_{\ell}.

(2.3)

Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
restore the uniformity of the restrictions in
\ell
.
We prove:

Lemma 2.2
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, we have

\mathbf{P}\big(G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}\cap\{\exists j\geq\ell:S_{\ell}^{(j)}>U_{\ell}\}\big)\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we do an union bound on
j
, using (
1.15
), and write
S_{\ell}^{(j)}=S_{\ell}
. The case
\ell=1
is identical to the one in Lemma
2.1
.
For
\ell>1
, using Lemma
2.1
, we only have to prove the case when
S_{\ell}\in[U_{\ell},U_{\ell}^{\prime}]
.
We can apply Lemma
B.1
to get that the probability in question is

\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\sum_{u\in[U_{\ell},U_{\ell}^{\prime}]}\frac{e^{-u^{2}/t_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}}\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\cdot c_{\ell-1}\cdot\frac{e^{-U_{\ell}^{2}/t_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}}\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

2.3
Lower Barrier

We prove the bound for the second term in (
1.14
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
, we have

\mathbf{P}\big(H\cap G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}\cap\{\exists j\geq\ell:S_{\ell}^{(j)}<L_{\ell}\}\big)\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, a union bound on
j
allows us to consider some fixed
j\geq\ell
. It will be beneficial to retain the superscript notation on
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
in the following, see in particular the argument around (
2.3
).

We condition on the position at time
\ell
, and find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(H\cap G_{\ell-1}\cap A_{\ell}\cap\{S_{\ell}^{(j)}<L_{\ell}\})\ll\sum_{u\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},L_{\ell}]}\mathbf{P}(\{\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}\tau)|-S_{\ell}^{(j)}>V-u\}\cap G_{\ell}(u)),

using (
2.2
) and the notation (
2.1
). For
\ell=1
, we take
G_{1}(u)=\{S_{1}^{(j)}\in[u-1,u]\}
.

Now, we use Proposition
1.3
to replace
\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}\tau)|
with a Dirichlet polynomial of length
x
to be determined.
This truncation ought to be dependent on the magnitude of the deviation
V-u
.
We take
x=T^{\beta_{m}}
where
m
is the smallest index such that

\beta_{m}>c_{\ell}^{-1}=\beta_{\ell}^{\gamma}.

(2.4)

Applying Proposition
1.3
, we hence find

\displaystyle\{\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}\tau)|-

\displaystyle S_{\ell}^{(j)}>V-u\}

\displaystyle\subset\Big\{{\rm Re}\sum_{n\leq T^{\beta_{m}}}\frac{a(n)}{n^{\sigma_{m}+{\rm i}\tau}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log n}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\beta_{m}^{-1}+\OO(1/\sqrt{\log T})\Big\}.

For the right-hand side event, the powers of primes greater than
2
can be absorbed into the lower bound. It follow that

\Big\{\log|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}\tau)|-S_{\ell}^{(j)}>V-u\Big\}\subset\Big\{S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}\geq\frac{1}{5}(V-u)\Big\}.

To resolve the discrepancy between the abscissae
\sigma_{j}
and
\sigma_{m}
when comparing
S_{m}^{(m)}
and
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
, we first trivially write

S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}=S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)}+(S_{\ell}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}).

This implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\{S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}\geq\frac{1}{5}(V-u)\}\cap{G_{\ell}(u)}\Big)\leq

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\{S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)}>\frac{1}{10}(V-u)\}\cap{G_{\ell}(u)}\Big)+\mathbf{P}\Big(\{S_{\ell}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(j)}>\frac{1}{10}(V-u)\}\cap{G_{\ell}(u)}\Big).

We begin with bounding the first probability on the right-hand side. In this case, the abscissae are identical. The proof then follows similarly to Lemma
2.2
, using additionally that the Dirichlet polynomials
S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)}
and
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
are supported on disjoint sets of primes. Indeed, we have using a Markov inequality and Lemma
B.1
that for
W=(V-u)/10

\mathbf{P}(\{S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)}>W\}\cap G_{\ell}(u))\ll\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}}{W^{2q}}\right]\frac{e^{-u^{2}/t_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}},

(2.6)

provided
\mathcal{Q}=(S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)})^{q}
has length not exceeding
T^{1/2}
. The length is then at most
T^{2q\beta_{m}}
so the choice
q=\lceil W/5\rceil
is permissible since for
u\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},L_{\ell}]
we have, using (
2.3
) and (
2.4
)

2q\beta_{m}<\frac{2e}{50}\frac{(V-u)}{c_{\ell}}<\frac{2e}{50}\left(\kappa\beta_{\ell}^{\gamma}\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1}+4\right)<\frac{1}{2}\ .

An application of Lemma
A.1
and Stirling’s approximation yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}}{W^{2q}}\right]\ll\exp\left(q\log q-q-q\log W^{2}+q\log(t_{m}-t_{\ell})\right)\ll\exp\left(-\frac{1}{10}W\log W\right),

where we used the fact that
W^{1/2}>t_{m}-t_{\ell}
.
Writing
\bar{u}=u-\kappa t_{\ell}
, the first probability in (
2.5
) is then

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\kappa^{2}/t_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}}\sum_{\bar{u}\in[-4c_{\ell},-c_{\ell}]}e^{2\kappa|\bar{u}|}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{100}|\bar{u}|\log|\bar{u}|}\ll\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\cdot e^{\kappa^{2}(t-t_{\ell})}\sum_{\bar{u}\in[-4c_{\ell},-c_{\ell}]}e^{2k|\bar{u}|-\frac{1}{100}|\bar{u}|\log|\bar{u}|}.

The exponent
2k-\frac{1}{100}\log|\bar{u}|
is negative when
\bar{u}>e^{200k}
. Recall that
\bar{u}\in[c_{\ell},4c_{\ell}]
, so indeed
c_{\ell}\geq c_{\mathcal{L}}=\beta_{\mathcal{L}}^{-\gamma}>e^{200k}
by the choice of
\mathcal{L}
. Therefore the contribution from the first event in the right-hand side of (
2.5
) is

\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}.

Returning to the second probability in (
2.5
), we introduce a further decomposition and bound as

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},L_{\ell}],w\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},U^{\prime}_{\ell}]\\
|w-u|>\frac{1}{10}(V-u)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{P}(\{S_{\ell}^{(m)}\in[w-1,w]\}\cap\{S_{\ell}^{(j)}\in[u-1,u]\}\cap G_{\ell-1}).

This is now in the form to apply the two-point estimate of Lemma
B.2
. This yields

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},L_{\ell}],w\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},U^{\prime}_{\ell}]\\
|w-u|>\frac{1}{10}(V-u)\end{subarray}}e^{-\frac{(u+w)^{2}}{4t_{\ell}}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}(w-u)^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll(\log\beta_{\ell}^{-1})\cdot\frac{e^{-{L_{\ell}^{\prime}}^{2}/t_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{t_{\ell}}}\cdot e^{-c_{\ell}^{2}/800},

using that
V-u>c_{\ell}
for
u\in[L_{\ell}^{\prime},L_{\ell}]
. This is
\ll e^{-c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{e^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}
concluding the proof.
∎

3
Applications to Short Intervals

In
[
1
, Corollary 1.3]
, it was shown that a large deviation estimate together with an appropriate discretization yields a probabilistic upper bound for the maximum of
|\zeta|
in an interval
of length
(\log T)^{\gamma}
for some exponent
\gamma\geq 0
. The unconditional result was then limited to
\gamma<3
, because of the restriction
k<2
on the values
V\sim k\log\log T
.
The same proof together with now Theorem
1.1
extends this bound conditionally to all
\gamma\geq 0
:

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
\gamma\geq 0
and
y>0
such that
y={\rm o}\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. We have

\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\gamma}}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t+{\rm i}h)|\leq e^{y}\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\gamma}}}{(\log\log T)^{1/(4\sqrt{1+\gamma})}},

(3.1)

for all
t\in[T,2T]
except on a set of Lebesgue measure
\ll e^{-2y\sqrt{1+\gamma}}e^{-y^{2}/\log\log T}
.

The bound is expected to be sharp for
\gamma>0
. The case
\gamma=0
corresponds to the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating Conjecture and was proved in
[
3
]
.
The large deviation estimate can also be used exactly as in
[
1
, Corollary 1.4]
to yield a conditional upper bound on the moments in short intervals.

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Let
\gamma\geq 0
. For all
\beta\geq 0
, we have for
A>1

\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\gamma}}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t+{\rm i}h)|^{\beta}{\rm d}h\leq A(\log T)^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}+\gamma},

(3.2)

for all
t\in[T,2T]
except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure
\ll 1/A
.

For
\beta>\beta_{c}=2\sqrt{1+\gamma}
, a sharper bound holds:

\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\gamma}}|\zeta(1/2+{\rm i}t+{\rm i}h)|^{\beta}{\rm d}h\leq C_{A,\beta}\cdot(\log\log T)^{-\tfrac{\beta}{2\beta_{c}}}\cdot(\log T)^{\tfrac{\beta_{c}}{2}\beta-1},

(3.3)

for all
t\in[T,2T]
except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure
\ll 1/A
, where
C_{A,\beta}
is an explicit constant dependent on
A
and
\beta
.

The result should be sharp for
\gamma>0
, up to constant. In the critical case
\beta_{c}=2
for
\gamma=0
, Harper in
[
11
]
proved that the moment is stochastically bounded by
\log T/\sqrt{\log\log T}
.
See also
[
6
]
for a different proof.

Appendix A
Moments of
S_{\ell}^{(j)}

The moments of the partial sums (
1.8
) can be estimated by comparison with the Steinhaus model.
Namely, consider
(X(p),p\text{ primes})
independent random variables uniformly distributed on the unit circle. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the random variable
X(n)
multiplicatively according to the prime factorization of
n
.
Then the mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials, see for example
[
17
]
, can be stated as follows: for a sequence of complex numbers
(b_{n},n\geq 1)
, and
\tau
uniform on
[T,2T]

\mathbf{E}\left[\Big|\sum_{n\leq N}b_{n}n^{-{\rm i}\tau}\Big|^{2}\right]=\Big(1+\OO(N/T)\Big)\mathbf{E}\left[\Big|\sum_{n\leq N}b_{n}X(n)\Big|^{2}\right]=\Big(1+\OO(N/T)\Big)\sum_{n\leq N}|b_{n}|^{2}.

(A.1)

In particular, this directly implies that for two Dirichlet polynomials with complex coefficients
(b_{n},n\geq 1)
and
(c_{n},n\geq 1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\Big(\sum_{n\leq N}b_{n}n^{-{\rm i}\tau}\Big)\overline{\Big(\sum_{n\leq N}c_{n}n^{-{\rm i}\tau}\Big)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\Big(\sum_{n\leq N}b_{n}X(n)\Big)\overline{\Big(\sum_{n\leq N}c_{n}X(n)\Big)}\right]+\OO\Big(\frac{N}{T}\sum_{n\leq N}|b_{n}|^{2}+|c_{n}|^{2}\Big).

(A.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq N}b_{n}\overline{c_{n}}+\OO\Big(\frac{N}{T}\sum_{n\leq N}|b_{n}|^{2}+|c_{n}|^{2}\Big).

These formulas can be applied to get the variance of the real sums
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
defined in (
1.8
): for
j\geq\ell
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[(S_{\ell}^{(j)})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{a_{j}(p)^{2}}{p^{2\sigma_{j}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{p^{2}\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{a_{j}(p^{2})^{2}}{p^{4\sigma_{j}}}+{\rm o}(1)

(A.3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\log T_{\ell}+1,

by Mertens’s estimates and the definition of
\sigma_{j}
and
a_{j}
. The covariance at two abscissae
i,j\geq\ell
is computed similarly

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}[S_{\ell}^{(i)}S_{\ell}^{(j)}]\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{p\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{a_{i}(p)a_{j}(p)}{p^{\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j}}}\right|+\OO(1)

(A.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j}}}+\OO\Big(\sum_{p\leq T_{\ell}}\frac{\log p}{\log T_{i}}+\frac{\log p}{\log T_{j}}\Big)+\OO(1)=\frac{1}{2}\log\log T_{\ell}+\OO(1).

The comparison with Steinhaus random variables can also be used to obtain upper bound for the moments of
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
.
The following is Lemma 16 of
[
3
]
. Note that in their case
a_{j}(p)=1
and
\sigma_{j}=1/2
, but the same upper bound still holds.

Lemma A.1
.

Let

be as in (

) for

with

\mathbf{E}[\big(S_{\ell}^{(j)}-S_{k}^{(j)}\big)^{2q}]\ll\frac{(2q)!}{2^{q}q!}\left(\frac{\log\beta_{\ell}-\log\beta_{k}}{2}\right)^{q}.

(A.5)

Moreover, there exists

(A.6)

The following upper bound for the moment generating function of the Steinhaus version of (

(A.7)

Gaussian Comparison on the Good Events

We prove the lemma for the events

defined in (

L_{\ell}^{\prime}
,

defined in (

Lemma B.1
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{Q}
be of the form
\mathcal{Q}=(S_{m}^{(m)}-S_{\ell}^{(m)})^{q}
for
q\in\mathbb{N}
, and

such that

Consider the event
G_{\ell}(u)=G_{\ell-1}\cap\{S_{\ell}^{(j)}\in[u-1,u]\}
with

and some

. We have

\mathbf{E}\Big[|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}\mathbf{1}(G_{\ell}(u))\Big]\ll\mathbf{E}\Big[|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}\Big]\cdot\frac{e^{-u^{2}/\log\log T_{\ell}}}{\sqrt{\log\log T_{\ell}}}.

(B.1)

Proof.

To simplify the notation in the proof, we write
[L_{\ell},U_{\ell}]
for the interval
[u-1,u]
.
We also fix
\sigma_{j}
in
S_{\ell}^{(j)}
throughout and drop it from the notation.
The first step is to express the restriction on the partial sums in terms of their increments

Y_{j}=S_{j}-S_{j-1},\quad 1\leq j\leq\mathcal{\ell}.

(B.2)

We now partition
[L_{j},U_{j}]
in intervals of the form
[u_{j},u_{j}+\Delta_{j}^{-1}]
where

u_{j}\in\Delta_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}\qquad\Delta_{j}=c_{j},

(B.3)

with
c_{j}
given in (
1.11
). By definition, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{L}}\Delta_{j}^{-1}=\OO(1)
.
We consider the values of
u_{j}
’s that are relevant for the restricted sums. Namely, we consider
\ell
-tuples
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell})
in the set

\mathcal{I}=\{{\bf u}:\sum_{i=1}^{j}u_{i}\in[L_{j}-1,U_{j}],\ \forall j\leq\ell\}.

(B.4)

We then have the inclusion

\{S_{j}\in[L_{j},U_{j}],j\leq\ell\}\subset\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{I}}\{Y_{j}\in[u_{j},u_{j}+\Delta_{j}^{-1}],j\leq\ell\}.

(B.5)

The definition of
\mathcal{I}
implies the bound

|u_{j}|\leq 4\Delta_{j}.

(B.6)

With these considerations, the left-hand side of (
B.1
) is

\leq\sum_{\bf u\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{1}\big(Y_{j}\in[u_{j},u_{j}+\Delta_{j}^{-1}]\big)\right].

We approximate the indicator function above by a polynomial as follows.
Following Lemma 2 in
[
2
]
, for
a>2
,
X>1
and
\Delta
large enough, there exists a polynomial
\mathcal{D}
of degree smaller than
100X\Delta^{3a}
with
\ell
-th coefficient bounded by
\frac{(2\pi)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\Delta^{2a(\ell+2)}
such that for
|x|\leq X
we have

\mathbf{1}(x\in[0,\Delta^{-1}])\Big(1-e^{-\Delta^{a-2}}\Big)\leq|\mathcal{D}(x)|^{2}\leq\mathbf{1}(x\in[-\Delta^{-a},\Delta^{-1}+\Delta^{-a}])+e^{-\Delta^{a-2}}.

(B.7)

We pick
a=5
. From the restriction (
B.6
) for fixed
j\leq\ell
, we can pick
X=10\Delta_{j}
. With these choices, the polynomial has degree less than
10^{3}\Delta_{j}^{3a+1}\leq\Delta_{j}^{20}
.
We write
\mathcal{D}_{j}
for this polynomial.

We apply the mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials to
\mathcal{D}_{j}(Y_{j}-u_{j})^{2}
.
Note that this is not a Dirichlet polynomial
per se
since
Y_{j}
is the real part of a Dirichlet polynomial.
However, by expanding the powers of
\mathcal{D}_{j}
, it can be re-expressed in a form where the theorem as in (
A.2
) can be applied.
The length of the polynomial is then at most

(T_{\ell})^{\Delta_{\ell}^{20}}=T^{\beta_{\ell}(\beta_{\ell}^{-20\gamma})},

(B.8)

which is small enough for
\gamma<1/20
.
Define
\mathcal{Y}_{j}
as in (
B.2
) with the
p^{-{\rm i}\tau}
’s replaced by independent Steinhaus random variables
X(p)
, i.e. uniformly distributed on the unit circle.
This shows so far that

\mathbf{E}\left[|Q|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{1}\big(Y_{j}\in[u_{j},u_{j}+\Delta_{j}^{-1}]\big)\right]\ll\mathbf{E}[|Q|^{2}]\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{D}_{j}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j})|^{2}\right].

(B.9)

For each
j
, we decompose the expectation onto the events
|\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}|\leq\Delta_{j}^{6a}
and
|\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}|>\Delta_{j}^{6a}
.
On the first, we get from (
B.7
) that

\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{D}_{j}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j})|^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}|\leq\Delta_{j}^{6a})\right]=\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}\in[-\Delta_{j}^{-a},\Delta_{j}^{-1}+\Delta_{j}^{-a}])+e^{-\Delta_{j}^{a-2}}.

By Gaussian approximation (see for example Lemma 8 and 9 in
[
2
]
), the probability is equal to
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}_{j}-u_{j}\in[-\Delta_{j}^{-a},\Delta_{j}^{-1}+\Delta_{j}^{-a}])+\OO(\exp(-ce^{t_{j-1}/2}))
for
j\geq 2
, and with
\asymp
for
j=1
. Here,
\mathcal{N}_{j}
is defined as
Y_{j}
with the
p^{-{\rm i}\tau}
’s now replaced by independent complex standard Gaussian random variables
Z(p)
. In particular, since
|u_{j}|\Delta_{j}^{-a}\leq 1
, the overspill is negligible and we have

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}_{j}-u_{j}\in[-\Delta_{j}^{-a},\Delta_{j}^{-1}+\Delta_{j}^{-a}])=(1+\OO(\Delta_{j}^{1-a}))\cdot\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}_{j}-u_{j}\in[0,\Delta_{j}^{-1}]).

(B.10)

Moreover, by a Gaussian estimate, this probability is larger than the error term
e^{-\Delta_{j}^{a-2}}
for the choice
a=5
. Therefore, we have shown

\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{D}_{j}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j})|^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}|\leq\Delta_{j}^{6a})\right]=(1+\OO(\Delta_{j}^{-1}))\cdot\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}_{j}-u_{j}\in[0,\Delta_{j}^{-1}]).

The contribution on the event

is estimated by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a Chernoff bound

\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{D}_{j}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j})|^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j}|>\Delta_{j}^{6a})\right]\ll\mathbf{E}\left[|\mathcal{D}_{j}(\mathcal{Y}_{j}-u_{j})|^{4}\right]^{1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{j}^{6a}}

using the estimate (
A.7

B.6
).

which shows that (

It remains to take the product over

of the above and apply the reverse inclusion (

\mathbf{E}\Big[|Q|^{2}\mathbf{1}(G_{\ell}(u))\Big]\ll\mathbf{E}[|Q|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{P}\Big(\sum_{j\leq\ell}\mathcal{N}_{j}\in[u-2,u+1]\Big),

(B.13)

where we dropped the intermediate restrictions on the sums of
