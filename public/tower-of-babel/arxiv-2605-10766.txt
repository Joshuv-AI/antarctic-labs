Some properties of Fourier quasicrystals and measures on a strip
Sergii Favorov, Özkan Deǧer

Abstract




Abstract.

In our paper we extend some results of the theory of Fourier quasicrystals on the real line to a horizontal strip of finite width. For measures in a strip we use a natural generalization of the usual Fourier transform
for measures on the line. We consider positive or translation bounded measures
\mu
on a strip whose Fourier transform is a pure point measure
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}
(as usual,
\delta_{\gamma}
is the unit mass at the point
\gamma
).
We prove that the measure
\nu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|b_{\gamma}|^{2}\delta_{\gamma}
has the exponential growth. Moreover, if for some
\eta>0
the points of
\Gamma
in every interval of length
\eta
are linearly independent over integers, then the measure
\hat{\mu}
also has the exponential growth.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 52C23, 42A38, 42A75

Keywords: tempered distribution, Fourier quasicrystal in the horizontal strip, c-Fourier transform of a measure in a strip, pure point spectrum

1.
Introduction

A complex measure
\mu
on a Euclidean space with discrete locally finite support is called
crystalline
,
if
\mu
is a tempered distribution and its Fourier transform in the sense of distributions
\hat{\mu}

is also a measure with discrete locally finite support;
\mu
is called a
Fourier quasicrystal
if both measures
|\mu|
and
|\hat{\mu}|
are also tempered distributions.
Here and below
|\nu|(E)
denotes the variation of the complex measure
\nu
on the set
E
.

Fourier quasicrystals and crystalline measures have been studied very actively in the recent years. Many works are devoted to investigations their properties (see, for example, the papers
[
15
,
11
,
1
]
). In fact, Fourier quasicrystals are forms of Poisson formulas, which were used, in particular, by D. Radchenko and M. Viazovska in
[
18
]
.

The first nontrivial example of Fourier quasicrystals, which are sums of unit masses

(1)

\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\delta_{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},

was found by P.Kurasov and P.Sarnak
[
10
]
. In
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
A. Olevskii and A. Ulanovskii established a 1-1 connection between such Fourier quasicrystals
and sets of zeros of real-rooted exponential polynomials. In
[
5
]
this result was generalized to the zero sets of real-rooted absolutely convergent Dirichlet series. F. Goncalves
[
8
]
and F. Goncalves, G. Vedana
[
9
]
studied arbitrary measures
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
with pure point measures
\hat{\mu}
and obtained an analog of Olevskii and Ulanovskii’s result.

Measures of the form (
1
) with support
\Lambda
in a horizontal strip of finite width appeared in
[
6
]
, where an analog of Olevskii and Ulanovskii’s result was found. Also in
[
7
]
, the technique of Fourier quasicrystals in a strip was used to provide a criterion for an entire almost periodic function to be a finite product of sines.

Returning to the definition of a Fourier quasicrystal, it is natural to ask whether we need additional conditions on the variations
of the measures
\mu
and
\hat{\mu}
in order to prove that a crystalline measure is a Fourier quasicrystal. The answer is positive; in the paper
[
4
]
a crystalline measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
was constructed
that is not a Fourier quasicrystal. In the paper
[
3
]
some conditions were found for a crystalline measure to be a Fourier quasicrystal. In particular, it was proved that
if a measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is positive or translation bounded and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
is a pure point measure

(2)

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma},

then the measure

\nu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|b_{\gamma}|^{2}\delta_{\gamma}

is also translation bounded, and if the points of
\Gamma
in every interval of length
\eta
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
\hat{\mu}
is also translation bounded.

The set
\Gamma
in (
2
) is called
spectrum
of the measure
\mu
. Recall also that a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
is
translation bounded
if its variations on intervals of length
1
are uniformly bounded.

In our paper we show that analogs of these results do not valid for measures on a horizontal strip of finite width. Nevertheless we prove that for non-negative or translation bounded measures
\mu
in a strip
the measures
\nu
as above and the measure
\hat{\mu}
in the case of locally independent set
\Gamma
have at most exponential growth.
The precise formulations are presented in Section
2
after giving necessary definitions. An example of calculating the Fourier transform of a measure in a strip is given in Section
3
. In Sections
4
and
5
, we give some auxiliary results. In Section
6
, we present the proofs of the main theorems.

2.
Necessary notions and formulations of the main results

Let
\mu
be a measure with support in
\mathcal{S}_{H}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,|\text{I}m\,z|\leq H\}
.
Suppose that

(3)

|\mu|(\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,|\text{R}e\,z|\leq r\})\leq C_{0}\max\{1,r^{\rho}\}

with some positive constants
C_{0},\ \rho
.
We will say that the measure
\mu
is
translation bounded
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
if

(4)

\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}|\mu|(\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,s\leq\text{R}e\,z\leq s+1\})=C_{1}<\infty.

It is easy to check that inequality (
3
) with
\rho=1,\ C_{0}\leq 3C_{1}
is valid for translation bounded measures.

Set for a function
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support

(5)

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi izt}dt,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Clearly,
\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)
is an entire extension of the usual Fourier transform

\hat{\varphi}(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi ixt}dt,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

and

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)=\widehat{(\varphi(t)e^{2\pi yt})}(x),\quad z=x+iy.

Define
the c-Fourier transform
of a measure
\mu
by the equality

(6)

(\hat{\mu}^{c},\varphi)=\int\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz),

where
\varphi
belongs to the space
\mathcal{D}
of all
C^{\infty}
-functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
with compact support.
Below we will show that
\hat{\mu}^{c}
is well-defined as a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{D}
.
We will also show there that the c-Fourier transform of the measure
\mu_{0}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{n+i}
is the measure
\hat{\mu}^{c}_{0}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{2\pi n}\delta_{n}
,
which is not translation bounded. Namely,

\log\hat{\mu}^{c}_{0}(-r,r)=\log\sum_{|n|\leq r}e^{2\pi n}\sim 2\pi r\qquad(r\to\infty).

Hence the exact analog of results in
[
3
]
is incorrect. To formulate
our generalization of these results we recall the following definition (
[
12
, Ch.1]
):

A measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
has a type
\sigma
with respect to the exponential growth if

\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{-1}\log\nu(-r,r)=\sigma.

In our paper we obtain the following results:

Theorem 1
.

If a positive measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
satisfies (
3
) and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
is also a measure, then
\mu
is translation bounded.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}^{c}
be a pure point measure of the form (
2
).
Then the measure

(7)

\nu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|b_{\gamma}|^{2}\delta_{\gamma}

has the property

(8)

\nu(t-1,t+1)=O(e^{4\pi H|t|})\qquad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

and has the type with respect to the exponential growth at most
4\pi H
.
The same statement is true for any translation bounded measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
with a pure point measure
\hat{\mu}^{c}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}^{c}
be a pure point measure of the form (
2
).
If there exists
\eta>0
such that sets
\Gamma\cap(t-\eta,t+\eta)
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
then the measure
\hat{\mu}^{c}
has the property

(9)

|\hat{\mu}^{c}|(t-1,t+1)=O(e^{2\pi H|t|})\qquad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

and has the type with respect to the exponential growth at most
2\pi H
. The same statement is true for any translation bounded measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}

with a pure point measure
\hat{\mu}^{c}
satisfying the condition of the theorem.

Recall that the set
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}
if for any finite number of elements
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in X

and arbitrary
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the equality
m_{1}t_{1}+\dots+m_{n}t_{n}=0
implies
m_{1}=\dots=m_{n}=0
.

Note that the condition on
\Gamma
be satisfy in the case
|t-t^{\prime}|>\eta
for all
t,\,t^{\prime}\in\Gamma,\ t^{\prime}\neq t
. Thus, the above example of the measure
\mu_{0}
shows that estimates (
8
)
and (
9
) are optimal.

3.
An example of c-Fourier transform for a measure in a strip

Let’s find the c-Fourier transform of the measure
\mu_{0}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{n+i}
in the strip
|\text{I}m\,z|\leq 1
.

First note that zeros of exponential polynomial

\sin\pi(z-i)=(i/2)e^{-\pi}e^{-i\pi z}-(i/2)e^{\pi}e^{i\pi z}

correspond to support of this measure. The logarithmic derivative of
\sin\pi(z-i)
has the form

\frac{\pi\cos\pi(z-i)}{\sin\pi(z-i)}=\pi i\frac{e^{\pi i(z-i)}+e^{-\pi i(z-i)}}{e^{\pi i(z-i)}-e^{-\pi i(z-i)}}.

Let
\text{I}m\,z>1
. We get
\left|e^{\pi i(z-i)}\right|<1,\ \left|e^{-\pi i(z-i)}\right|>1
and

\frac{\pi\cos\pi(z-i)}{\sin\pi(z-i)}=-\pi i\frac{1+e^{2\pi i(z-i)}}{1-e^{2\pi i(z-i)}}=-\pi i-2\pi i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{2\pi n}e^{2\pi inz}.

If
\text{I}m\,z<-1
, then we get
\left|e^{\pi i(z-i)}\right|>1,\ \left|e^{-\pi i(z-i)}\right|<1
and

\frac{\pi\cos\pi(z-i)}{\sin\pi(z-i)}=\pi i\frac{e^{-2\pi i(z-i)}+1}{1-e^{-2\pi i(z-i)}}=\pi i+2\pi i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi n}e^{-2\pi inz}.

It was proved in
[
7
]
that for the case of
\supp\mu\subset\mathcal{S}_{H}
the measure
\hat{\mu}^{c}
has the form

\hat{\mu}^{c}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}\frac{ih^{+}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\delta_{\gamma}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\frac{ih^{-}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\delta_{\gamma},

where
\Gamma^{*}\subset[0,\infty),\ \Gamma_{*}\subset(-\infty,0]
and
h^{+}
and
h^{-}
are the coefficients of the Diriclet series for the logarithmic derivative of the
corresponding exponential polynomial for the measure
\mu
in the cases
\text{I}m\,z>H
and, respectively,
\text{I}m\,z<-H
. Hence,

\hat{\mu}^{c}_{0}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{2\pi n}\delta_{n}.

4.
Some propeties of the c-Fourier transform

The next three equalities for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mu
satisfying (
3
) follow immediately from the definition of c-Fourier transform:

(10)

\widehat{(e^{2\pi i\tau t}\varphi(t))^{c}}=(\hat{\varphi}^{c})(z-\tau),\qquad z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\quad\tau\in{\mathbb{R}},

(11)

(\hat{\varphi}^{c}_{\tau})(z)=e^{-2\pi i\tau z}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\quad\text{for}\quad\varphi_{\tau}(t)=\varphi(t-\tau),\qquad z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\quad\tau\in{\mathbb{R}},

(12)

(\widehat{\mu_{\tau}})^{c}(t)=e^{-2\pi i\tau t}\hat{\mu}^{c}(t)\quad\text{for}\quad\mu_{\tau}(E)=\mu(E-\tau),\qquad\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
\varphi(t)
be an
m
-differentiable function with compact support such that
\varphi^{(m)}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Integrating (
5
) by parts, we obtain

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)=(2\pi iz)^{-m}\int_{\supp\varphi}\varphi^{(m)}(t)e^{-2\pi itz}dt,\quad z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}\setminus\{0\},

therefore, for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}

(13)

|\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)|\leq C_{3}(\max\{1,|z|\})^{-m},

where the constant
C_{3}
depends on
\varphi
and
m
.

Let a measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
satisfy (
3
) with some
\rho<m
. Denote

M_{\mu}(r):=|\mu|(\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,|z|\leq r\}),

Using (
13
) and integrating by parts, we get

\left|\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz)\right|\leq C_{3}M_{\mu}(1)+C_{3}\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},|z|>1}|z|^{-m}|\mu|(dz)\leq C_{3}m\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-m-1}M_{\mu}(r)dr.

Therefore the integral in (
6
) is finite, and the distribution
\hat{\mu}^{c}
is well-define on the space of all
m
-differentiable continuous functions with compact support. Since for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}

|e^{2\pi isz}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)|\leq e^{2\pi|s|H}|\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)|\leq C_{3}e^{2\pi|s|H}(\max\{1,|z|\})^{-m},

we see that the same arguments prove the estimate

(14)

\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}e^{2\pi isz}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz)\right|\leq C_{4}e^{2\pi|s|H},

where the constant
C_{4}
does not depend on
s
.

If the measure
\mu
is translation bounded, then for any shift of the measure
\mu_{\tau}(E)=\mu(E-\tau)
we have

|\mu_{\tau}|(\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,|z|\leq r\})\leq 3C_{1}\max\{1,r\},

where
C_{1}
is the constant from (
4
). We obtain from (
14
) with
m=2

(15)

\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}e^{2\pi isz}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu_{\tau}(dz)\right|\leq C_{5}e^{2\pi|s|H},

where the constant
C_{5}
does not depend on either
s
or
\tau
.

We will also use the following proposition

Proposition
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is a function
\Psi_{m}(t)
with compact support such that its Fourier transform
\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)
satisfies (
13
)
and
\text{R}e\,\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)>0
for all
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
.

Proof
. Set
\theta(t)=1
for
|t|\leq 1
and
\theta(t)=0
for
|t|>1
. The c-Fourier transform of the function
\theta\star\theta
equals
\sin^{2}(2\pi z)/(2\pi z)^{2}
,
therefore it is non-negative for all
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
and vanishes only at the points
z=k/2,\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
. Consider the function

\psi(t)=(\theta\star\theta)(t-1/8)+(\theta\star\theta)(t+1/8).

It has compact support and c-Fourier transform

\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)=\sin^{2}(2\pi z-\pi/4)/(2\pi z-\pi/4)^{2}+\sin^{2}(2\pi z+\pi/4)/(2\pi z+\pi/4)^{2},

that is strictly positive on the real axis. The simple calculation shows that

\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)=\frac{i}{4}\left(e^{4\pi iz}-e^{-4\pi iz}\right)\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi z-\pi/4)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2\pi z+\pi/4)^{2}}\right]\\
+\frac{1}{4}\left\{\frac{2}{(2\pi z-\pi/4)^{2}}+\frac{2}{(2\pi z+\pi/4)^{2}}\right\}.

The expression in the square brackets behaves like
z^{-3}
, and the expression in the braces behaves like
(\pi z)^{-2}
as
z\to\infty
. Hence for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\ z\to\infty
,

\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)=O(z^{-3})+\frac{1+o(1)}{4\pi^{2}z^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}(\text{R}e\,z)^{2}}(1+o(1)).

Define the convolution of
n\geq m/2
functions
\psi(t)
with
\Phi(t)
. Clearly, this function has compact support. Also, its c-Fourier transform

\hat{\Phi}^{c}(z)=\left[\sin^{2}(2\pi z-\pi/4)/(2\pi z-\pi/4)^{2}+\sin^{2}(2\pi z+\pi/4)/(2\pi z+\pi/4)^{2}\right]^{n}

satisfies (
13
) and strictly positive on the real axis. Therefore
\text{R}e\,\hat{\Phi}^{c}(z)
is strictly positive in a neighborhood of
{\mathbb{R}}
. We also have for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\ z\to\infty

\hat{\Phi}^{c}(z)=\frac{1}{4^{n}\pi^{2n}(\text{R}e\,z)^{2n}}(1+o(1)),

hence there is
N<\infty
such that
\text{R}e\,\hat{\Phi}^{c}(z)>0
for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\ |\text{R}e\,z|\geq N
. On the other hand, there is
\eta>0
such that
\text{R}e\,\hat{\Phi}^{c}(z)>0
for
|\text{I}m\,z|\leq\eta,\ |\text{R}e\,z|\leq N
.
Consequently, for the compactly supported function
\Psi_{m}(t):=(H/\eta)\Phi(Ht/\eta)
we obtain

\text{R}e\,\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)=\text{R}e\,\hat{\Phi}^{c}(\eta z/H)>0

for all
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
.
\Box

5.
Some properties of almost periodic functions

The proofs of our theorems are based on some results of the classical theory of almost periodic functions
(see
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
).

Recall that a continuous function
f(t)
on the real line
{\mathbb{R}}

is called
almost periodic
if for any
\varepsilon>0
the set of its
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}|f(t+\tau)-f(t)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense, i.e.,
E_{\varepsilon}\cap(t,t+L)\neq\emptyset
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and some
L=L(\varepsilon)
.

Each almost periodic function in
{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded, a finite sum or product of almost periodic functions is also almost periodic,
and the uniform limit on
{\mathbb{R}}
of almost periodic functions is almost periodic too. In particular,
every absolutely convergent Dirichlet series

(16)

D(t)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_{\omega}e^{2\pi it\omega},\qquad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|a_{\omega}|<\infty,

with a countable
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is almost periodic. It is easy to check that the usual Fourier transform of
D(t)
is a measure with a finite total mass

\hat{D}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_{\omega}\delta_{\omega}.

For every almost periodic
f(t)
and every
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
one considers
the Fourier coefficients
c_{\omega}(f)\in{\mathbb{C}}
as

c_{\omega}(f)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(t)e^{-2\pi it\omega}dt.

The set
\{\omega:\,c_{\omega}(f)\neq 0\}
is at most countable.
It is easy to check that the uniform convergence of the series (
16
) implies
c_{\omega}(D)=a_{\omega}
for all
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Next, the Parseval identity

(17)

\sum_{\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}}|c_{\omega}(f)|^{2}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f(t)|^{2}dt

holds.

Note that by Y.Meyer (cf.
[
14
, Theorem 3.8]
), if the Fourier transform of the measure
f(t)dt
for an almost periodic function
f
is also a measure, then

\sum_{|\omega|<r}|c_{\omega}(f)|<\infty,\quad\forall\,r<\infty,\qquad\text{and}\quad\widehat{f(t)dt}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}(f)\delta_{\omega}.

We will also use the Kroneker lemma (see
[
13
, Chapter II]
):

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
be linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}
real numbers and
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N}
be arbitrary real numbers. Then for every
\varepsilon>0

there exist
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
j=1,\ldots,N
, such that

|tx_{j}-\theta_{j}-p_{j}|<\varepsilon,\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

6.
Proofs of theorems

Proof of Theorem
1
. Let the measure
\mu
be satisfied (
13
) with some
\rho<\infty
, and
\Psi_{m}
be the function from the Proposition with
m>\rho
.
Set
\eta=\inf_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,0\leq\text{R}e\,z\leq 1}\text{R}e\,\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)
. Using (
12
), we get for any fixed
s\in{\mathbb{R}}

\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,s\leq\text{R}e\,z\leq s+1}\mu(dz)\leq\eta^{-1}\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}:\,0\leq\text{R}e\,z\leq 1}\text{R}e\,\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)\mu_{-s}(dz)\leq\eta^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}\text{R}e\,\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)\mu_{-s}(dz)\\
=\eta^{-1}\text{R}e\,\int_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)\mu_{-s}(dz)\leq\eta^{-1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}_{H}}\hat{\Psi}_{m}^{c}(z)\mu_{-s}(dz)\right|=\eta^{-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{m}(t)\hat{\mu}_{-s}^{c}(dt)\right|=\\
\eta^{-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{m}(t)e^{2\pi ist}\hat{\mu}^{c}(dt)\right|\leq\eta^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi_{m}(t)||\hat{\mu}^{c}|(dt).

Since
\Psi_{m}
is compactly supported and
|\hat{\mu}^{c}|
is a measure, we see that the last integral is finite, and the first integral is bounded by the independent on
s
constant.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
2
. By Theorem
1
, it is enough to prove Theorem
2
for translation bounded measures
\mu
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
be a non-negative even function such that
\varphi(t)\equiv 1
for
|t|<1
. Note that
\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)
is also even, and for any
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\tau-z)=\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z-\tau).

Using (
10
) and the definition of the Fourier transform of measure on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
, we get for every
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle(\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c})(\tau)=\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\tau-z)\mu(dz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\widehat{(e^{2\pi i\tau t}\varphi(t))^{c}}(z)\mu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)e^{2\pi i\tau t}\hat{\mu}^{c}(dt)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\supp\varphi}b_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma)e^{2\pi i\tau\gamma}.

Since
\hat{\mu}^{c}
is a measure, we see that
\sum_{\gamma\in\supp\varphi}|b_{\gamma}|<\infty
and the last sum converges absolutely. Therefore,

(\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c})(\tau)
is an almost periodic function on
{\mathbb{R}}
. The above equality also implies (see Section
5
) that the usual Fourier transform of this function is a measure

\widehat{\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\supp\varphi}\varphi(\gamma)b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}.

By Meyer’s result, the Fourier coefficients of the function
(\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c})(\tau)
are

c_{\gamma}(\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c})=b_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma).

Using Parseval’s equality (
17
), we obtain that mass of the measure
\nu
from (
7
) on
[-1,1]
can be estimated as follows

\nu[-1,1]=\sum_{|\gamma|<1}|b_{\gamma}|^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}}|\varphi(\gamma)b_{\gamma}|^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}}|c_{\gamma}(\mu\star\hat{\varphi}^{c})|^{2}\\

then by (

. By Theorem

, it is enough to prove Theorem

\mu
.

C_{5}

Suppose that (

and

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap I_{n}}|b_{\gamma}|=|\hat{\mu}^{c}|(I_{n})>5C_{5}e^{2\pi H|t_{n}|}.

If the set
\Gamma\cap(t_{n}-\eta,t_{n}+\eta)
is finite for some

, then we set

, we
choose
A_{n}

\sum_{\gamma\in A_{n}\cap I_{n}}|b_{\gamma}|>4C_{5}e^{2\pi H|t_{n}|}.

Using the Kroneker lemma, we can find
s_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
