Whole genome sequence of a superbug-Escherichia coli strain KAB-AI-497 isolated from the vagina of a 20 year old pregnant woman with premature rupture of membrane (PROM) in a resource limited setting, Kabale Regional Referral Hospital, in Uganda
Authors: Ismail Abiola Adebayo, Leila Abdukadir Abdullahi, Chrispuss Ngabirano, Irene Mukenya Mutuku, Wasiu Abayomi Moyosore, Muritala Issa Bale, Ganiyat Odunayo Ganiyu, Rasheed Omotayo Adeyemo, Saheed Adekunle Akinola, John Chrysostom Lule

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe objective of the study is to sequence the whole genome of multidrug resistant E. coli strain KAB-AI-497 that causes bacterial vaginosis and implicated in premature rupture of membrane in pregnant woman.Data descriptionThe DNA of the E. coli strain KAB-AI-497 was extracted using the MagAttract HMW DNA Kit, and the extracted DNA was sequenced using an MGI DNBSEQ G99ARS platform. FastQC was used to perform quality control analysis and the reads were trimmed by Trimmomatic. De novo genome assembly was performed by SPAdes and it resulted to a draft assembled genome that has 5.1 Mb genome size, 153 contigs, and 50.5% GC content. Quality analysis of the assembled genome revealed it has 98.46% completeness and 0.97% contamination. The closest E. coli strain to this strain KAB-AI-497 in terms of similarity was Escherichia coli SMS-3-5 with an average nucleotide identity of 98.43% and genome coverage of 86.19%, which confirmed the species level identity of the strain. The assembled genome was annotated using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline which identified 4,726 protein coding genes in the strain genome. Furthermore, the annotation revealed the genome has resistant genes responsible for resistance against many antibiotic classes such as tetracycline, fluoroquinolone, and penicillin.

Objective

Bacterial vaginosis (BVS) is one of the major risk factors of premature rupture of membranes (PROM) in pregnant women [1–3]. E. coli is one of the most prominent causes of BVS [4, 5]. The health concern is more worrisome and PROM becomes more difficult to treat and manage when BVS is caused by multidrug-resistant (MDR) strains. The objective of the study is to sequence the whole genome of multidrug-resistant E. coli strain KAB-AI-497, which causes bacterial vaginosis (BVS) and is implicated in premature rupture of membranes (PROM) in pregnant woman. Therefore, the study aims to have a clear insight into the whole resistome of the MDR E. coli strain KAB-AI-497, in an attempt to guide medics, medical researchers and other health workers in understanding the resistance and susceptibility patterns of the MDR strain, which is an important strategy to effectively combat the menace of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and PROM in pregnant women [6–8]. The mitigation is even much needed in a resource limited regions of the world such as Kabale Municipality of the Western Uganda where the isolate was gotten [8].

The data has not yet been published as at this time when this data note manuscript is written, however, the data is part of large-scale whole genome data that are currently being collected for the purpose of making comparative genome analyses of MDR pathogenic strains in resource limited Kabale Municipality of Western region of Uganda.

Data description

The data sets presented in this manuscript include the whole genome sequence of Escherichia coli strain KAB-AI-497 isolated from the vagina of a 20 years old pregnant woman that suffered premature rupture of membrane due to bacterial vaginosis (BVS). The data include forward and reverse raw sequence reads (Data set 1), draft assembled genome sequence (Data set 2), and the annotated genome data by Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP). All the data are deposited in NCBI.

For sample collection, sterile swab was aseptically inserted 3 to 5 cm in to the vagina of the pregnant woman and it was gently rotated for about 10 to 30 s after which it was gently withdrawn and carefully placed in a transport tube. The collected sample was cultured on MacConkey agar and it was incubated at 37° C for 24 h under aerobic condition. Pink to red colonies which appeared on the agar suspected to be E. coli are sub cultured for purity. Pure isolate of the strain was cultured in Sulfide Indole Motility (SIM), and triple sugar iron (TSI) agars for identity confirmation. Furthermore, specific biochemical tests which are Gram staining, citrate test, and urea test were performed to confirm the isolate identity as E. coli.

DNA was extracted from the bacterial cells using the The MagAttract HMW DNA Kit (QIAGEN, Germany) by following the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality of the extracted DNA was assessed by 1% w/v agarose gel electrophoresis. The extracted DNA was quantified using NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Library preparation was performed using MGI Fast FS DNA Library Prep Kit (MGI Tech Co., Ltd., China) and sequencing was conducted on an MGI DNBSEQ G99ARS platform to generate 150 bp paired-end (PE150) reads.

FastQC (v0.12.1) was used to process the raw sequencing reads for quality control [9] and the reads were trimmed by Trimmomatic (v0.39) [10] to remove low-quality reads and adapters. De novo genome assembly was performed by SPAdes (v4.2.0) [11]. The assembled genome has 5.1 Mb genome size, 153 contigs, 50.5% GC content, N50 of 165.7 kb, L50 of 9, and genome coverage of 50×. For species and strain identification, the sequence was analyzed by Kraken2 (v2.17.1) [12], followed by Bracken (v3.1) [13], and then FastANI (v1.3), and it was revealed that the E. coli has closest similarity to the strain [14].

The assembled genome was annotated using the Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) [15] provided by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). The results revealed that the strain genome has 5,047 genes, which include 4,726 protein coding genes, 84 tRNA genes, 15 rRNA genes, and 217 pseudogenes. In addition, several resistant genes responsible for resistance to tetracycline, sulfonamide, fluoroquinolone, and penicillin antibiotic classes among others were identified as part of the resistome of the strain (E. coli strain KAB-AI-497). Virulence factor database (VFDB) analysis revealed the strain genome has several virulence factor genes which include rscB, entS, fepC, fepE, fes, and cfaC. Furthermore, the strain genome has repA and sopA plasmid genes. CheckM analysis (v1.2.4) [16] was performed which revealed that the assembled genome has 98.46% completeness and 0.97% contamination.
