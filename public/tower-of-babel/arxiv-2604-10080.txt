Abstract
. We establish comparison and divisibility properties for crossed product C*-algebras arising from automorphisms of algebras C ⁡ ( X , D ) C(X,D) which lie over minimal homeomorphisms, from actions of compact groups which have finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers, and from actions of compact groups which have the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison. We deduce that these crossed products we consider are pure, and conclude they have stable rank one, and in certain cases have real rank zero. We give examples in which these properties do not follow from previous results, in the case of C ⁡ ( X , D ) C(X,D) due to the lack of 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stability of D D , the underlying topological spaces not being finite dimensional, or both.

1.
Introduction

Significant progress has been made on the classification of
crossed product C*-algebras arising from minimal dynamical systems.
The unpublished long preprint
[
26
]
of Q. Lin
and N. C. Phillips (see also the survey articles

[
24
]
and
[
25
]
) provides a
description of the transformation group
C*-algebras arising from minimal diffeomorphisms of
finite dimensional smooth compact manifolds in
terms of a direct limit decomposition.
In
[
23
]

and
[
40
]
, it is shown that crossed
products arising from minimal homeomorphisms
of infinite compact metrizable spaces with finite covering
dimension are classified by their ordered K-theory in the
presence of sufficiently many projections (for instance,
when projections separate traces).
In
[
40
]

it is further proved that crossed products by such minimal
homeomorphisms have finite nuclear dimension, and
hence absorb the Jiang-Su algebra
{\mathcal{Z}}
tensorially
(that is, are
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable).
Finally, G. A. Elliott and
Z. Niu (
[
10
]
) have shown that crossed products
by minimal homeomorphisms of compact metric spaces
with mean dimension zero (including all minimal
homeomorphisms of finite dimensional compact
metric spaces) are
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable, from which
it follows that they are classifiable in the sense of the
Elliott program by Corollary D of Theorem A of
[
11
]
.

There is also substantial work on crossed products

C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},A,\alpha)
when
A
is simple.

Much less is known when the C*-algebra
A
is
neither commutative nor simple.
The easiest more general
case to consider is presumably
A=C(X,D)
, in which
X

is a compact metric space and
D
is a simple unital C*-algebra.
In this case, every
automorphism of
A
“lies over” a homeomorphism
h
of
X
.
(See
[
6
]
.)
We want the crossed product to be simple,
and this happens if and only if
h
is minimal.
Hua (
[
20
]
) has
shown that such crossed products have tracial rank zero
when moreover
X
is the Cantor set,
D
has tracial rank
zero, and some additional K-theoretic assumptions are made.
This case was more systematically studied in
[
6
]
,
in which several results were given showing that if
D
has
good properties (for example, has stable rank one, has strict
comparison of positive elements, or is tracially
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable),
and other conditions are satisfied, then
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)

has good properties.
In particular, by
[
6
, Theorem 4.5]
,
if
D
is tracially
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable, then so is
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)
,
even if
{\operatorname{mdim}}(h)>0
.
If
D
is nuclear, it then follows from

[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
that
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)
is
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable.
Therefore
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)
is classifiable
in the sense of the Elliott program.
However, there are many
interesting examples of crossed products of this sort in which

D
is not nuclear and not even tracially
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable.
Many examples are given in
[
6
, Section 6]
.
Many of the results there are limited to the case

\dim(X)\leq 1
or even
\dim(X)=0
, and are
therefore somewhat artificial.
The reason is lack of
sufficient information about algebras of the form
C(Y,D)
,
or recursive subhomogenous algebras over
D
, and their
direct limits, when the dimensions of the spaces are greater
than
1
.

This is where pure C*-algebras come into play.
Following Winter
[
43
, Definition 3.6]
, a C*-algebra is said to be pure if it has strict comparison and is
almost divisible.
More generally, weaker notions of comparison and divisibility, namely
m
-comparison and
n
-almost divisibility, suffice: if
A
has
m
-comparison and is
n
-almost divisible for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
A
is pure by
[
4
, Theorem 5.7]
.
See Section
2
for the relevant definitions and further details.
We warn that
m
-comparison as used here is a much weaker condition
than the property
with the same name used to define the radius of comparison,
as for example in
[
39
, Definition 6.1]
.
However,
0
-comparison is the same for both definitions.

Pureness is closely connected to
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability.
By
[
37
]
,
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability implies pureness, and for separable C*-algebras, the two notions coincide at the level of central sequence algebras.
(See
[
31
]
.)
In the nonnuclear setting,
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability is often unavailable, whereas pureness remains accessible and continues to play a useful role in establishing strict comparison.
It is therefore natural to view pureness as one plausible extension of
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability to the case of nonnuclear simple C*-algebras.
Two other possible extensions are
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability itself and (in the unital case)
tracial
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability as in
[
21
, Definition 2.1]
.
Certainly
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability implies tracial
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability.
It seems likely that tracial
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability implies
pureness, although, as far as we know, nobody has
actually proved this.
For simple separable nuclear
unital C*-algebras, tracial
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability implies

{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability (
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
).
The Elliott classification conjecture would imply that
pureness implies
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability.
Without nuclearity,
both reverse implications are false.
It is shown in

[
30
, Theorem 2.2]
that tracial
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability, even
tracial rank zero, does not imply
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability.
The
reduced C*-algebras of nonabelian free groups are pure but
certainly not
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable.
(See
[
2
, Theorem A]
and

[
4
, Proposition 5.13]
.)

Coming back to algebras of the form
C(Y,D)
, it is well known that
C(Y,D)
need not have strict comparison, even when
D
does.
Positive results are available under additional assumptions, for instance, when
\dim(Y)=1
.
(See
[
3
, Theorem 2.6]
.)
However, recent work in
[
38
]
shows that, under suitable divisibility assumptions on
D
, one can obtain strict comparison for
C(Y,D)
, and in fact establish pureness for arbitrary compact metric spaces
Y
.
(See
[
38
, Corollary 5.9 and Theorem 5.11]
.)

In this paper, we use these results on pureness of algebras
of the form
C(Y,D)
to prove that,
if
D
is pure and
h\colon X\to X
is a minimal homeomorphism (even with

{\operatorname{mdim}}(h)>0
), then
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)

is pure.
Since many reduced free products are pure, in
particular including
C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{n})
when

n\geq 2
(see
[
2
, Theorem A]
and

[
4
, Proposition 5.13]
), and simple stably finite
pure C*-algebras have stable rank one (
[
22
, Corallary 1.3]
), we are
able to deduce pureness, in particular, strict comparison of
positive elements, as well as stable rank one, for much
more natural versions of some of the examples in

[
6
, Section 6]
.
In some cases we are also
able to deduce real rank zero.

Related methods also give results for two other kinds of crossed products.
The first arises from actions of compact groups with finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers, and the second from actions with the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison.
Actions of the first type were introduced and studied by Gardella (
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
), generalizing the notion introduced by Hirshberg, Winter, and Zacharias for finite group actions.
Crossed products by such actions enjoy strong permanence properties:
several important classes of C*-algebras, including
\mathcal{D}
-absorbing C*-algebras (where
\mathcal{D}
is strongly self-absorbing), stable C*-algebras, C*-algebras with finite nuclear dimension (or decomposition rank), and C*-algebras with finite stable rank (or real rank), are preserved.
(See
[
15
, Theorem 4.17]
.)
The second class was introduced in
[
28
]
by the third and fourth named authors, where similar preservation results are obtained.
It is proved there that fixed point algebras under such actions,
and, in appropriate cases, the corresponding crossed products,
preserve properties such as simplicity, Property (SP), tracial rank zero, tracial rank at most one, the Popa property, tracial
\mathcal{Z}
-stability,
\mathcal{Z}
-stability in the nuclear case, infiniteness, and pure infiniteness.
In
[
29
]
the authors construct three types of examples that have the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison but do not have finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers.

We show that pureness is likewise preserved under crossed products in these settings.
Let
A
be a simple separable stably finite unital C*-algebra,
let
G
be a second countable compact group,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of
G
on
A
.
Suppose that
\alpha
has
the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison,
in the sense of
[
28
, Definition 2.1]
,
or that
G
is a Lie group
and
\alpha
has finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers,
in the sense of
[
14
, Definition 3.2]
.
We prove that if
A
is pure, then so is
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
.

The main step
for pureness of
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},C(X,D),h)
is showing
that if
B
is a large subalgebra of
A
, in the
sense of
[
34
, Definition 4.1]
, and if
B

is pure, then
A
is pure.
The reverse implication
is also true.
The proof depends on the fact that the
nonprojection parts of the Cuntz semigroups
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
and
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
are
isomorphic.
Assuming instead the restricted tracial
Rokhlin property with comparison, or finite Rokhlin
dimension with commuting towers, we compare the
fixed point algebra
A^{\alpha}
with
A
.
The
isomorphism statement above is no longer true,
but enough of it is that the same underlying ideas
can be made to work.
In all three cases, under
suitable additional assumptions, we can say
something about
m
-comparison and
n
-almost
divisibility separately, at the cost of allowing
m

and
n
to increase.
Thus, the fact that

m
-comparison and
n
-almost divisibility
imply pureness plays a key role.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we establish the relevant notation, definitions, and results
regarding Cuntz subequivalence, the Cuntz semigroup, and

n
-comparison and
m
-almost divisibility of both
semigroups and associated C*-algebras.
In particular, we
define the key property of pureness for a C*-algebra.
In
Section
3
, we establish relationships
between
n
-comparison and
m
-almost divisibility for
a large subalgebra and its containing algebra, and use these
to prove that if a C*-algebra contains a pure large subalgebra,
then the containing algebra is itself pure.
In Section
4
, we show that

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)
is pure
when
D
is pure and the action of
\alpha
lies over a
minimal homeomorphism.
It follows that such
crossed products have stable rank one and, in certain cases,
real rank zero.
In Section
5
, we
establish pureness for

C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)
when
G
is a compact
Lie group and
\alpha
has finite Rokhlin dimension with
commuting towers.
In Section
6
, we
establish pureness for

C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)
where
G
is a
second countable compact
group and
\alpha
has the tracial Rokhlin property with
comparison.
Finally, in Section
7
we give
examples of crossed product C*-algebras of the type described
in Section
4
.
These examples are not
covered by the results and examples in
[
6
]
due
to either the algebra
D
in
C(X,D)
not being
\mathcal{Z}
-stable,
the space
X
not being finite dimensional, or both.
In some of our examples the underlying dynamical system need
not even have mean dimension zero.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cuntz Subequivalence, Comparison, and
Divisibility

We recall some basic definitions and terminology related to
Cuntz subequivalence and the Cuntz semigroups
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
and

{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
for a C*-algebra
A
.
We include the algebra
A
in the notation
a\precsim_{A}b

and
\langle a\rangle_{A}
,
because we frequently work simultaneously with both
A

and a subalgebra
B\subseteq A
,
usually a large subalgebra in the sense of
[
34
]

or the fixed point algebra of an action of a compact group on
A
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra.

(1)

For
a,b\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
, we say that
a
is

Cuntz subequivalent
to
b
over
A
, written

a\precsim_{A}b
, if there is a sequence
(v_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

in
A\otimes K
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}bv_{n}^{*}=a
.

(2)

We say that
a
and
b
are
Cuntz equivalent
in
A
,
written
a\sim_{A}b
, if
a\precsim_{A}b
and
b\precsim_{A}a
.
This relation is an equivalence relation, and we write
\langle a\rangle_{A}

for the equivalence class of
a
in
A
.
When there is no
possibility of confusion, we sometimes just write

a\precsim b
,
a\sim b
, and
\langle a\rangle
.

(3)

The
Cuntz semigroup
of
A
is

{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)=(A\otimes K)_{+}/\sim_{A},

together with the semigroup operation
\langle a\rangle_{A}+\langle b\rangle_{A}=\langle a\oplus b\rangle_{A}
and the partial order given by

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}
if and only if
a\precsim_{A}b
.

(4)

We define a subsemigroup
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
of
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
by

{\operatorname{W}}(A)=M_{\infty}(A)_{+}/\sim_{A},

which is the “classical” Cuntz semigroup of
A
.
We also denote the class of
a
in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
by
\langle a\rangle_{A}
,
except in Remark
2.2

and Section
2.2
, where we use
[a]_{A}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The operations and order in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
are the same as in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
By Remark 1.2 of
[
34
]
,
if
a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
, then
\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}
in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)

if and only if
[a]_{A}\leq[b]_{A}
in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.
The notational distinction between
\langle a\rangle_{A}
and
[a]_{A}
will only be
used in Section
2.2
.

The semigroup
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
tends to have better properties than

{\operatorname{W}}(A)
, such as the existence of certain suprema
(Theorem 4.19 of
[
5
]
), which will be important for
Definition
2.7
, and behavior with respect to
direct limits (Theorem 4.35 of
[
5
]
).
We work in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)

when possible, but use
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
when certain results in the
literature are formulated in those terms.

We next recall the definitions of divisibility and comparison definitions for
abstract semigroups, with the cases of interest being

{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
and
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.
Much of the notation and terminology
follows
[
4
]
, although many of the definitions used
are not original there.

Definition 2.3

(Before Proposition 2.3 of
[
4
]
)
.

For an ordered semigroup
S
and
\xi,\eta\in S
, write

\xi<_{\operatorname{s}}\eta
if
(k+1)\xi\leq k\eta
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

Definition 2.4
.

An ordered semigroup
S
has
n
-comparison
if
given
\xi,\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in S
,
the relation
\xi<_{\operatorname{s}}\eta_{j}

for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
implies
\xi\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\eta_{j}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra.
We say
A
has
n
-comparison

if
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has
n
-comparison in the sense of Definition

2.4
.
If
A
has 0-comparison, we say that
A
has

strict comparison
.

Strict comparison as in Definition
2.5
agrees
with the usual notion of strict comparison of positive elements
by quasitraces for a C*-algebra
A
, and this property holds
if and only if
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
is almost unperforated.
For details of
these relationships (and relationships with other version of
comparison), see Section 3.4 of
[
4
]
.

We caution that
n
-comparison in the sense of
Definition
2.5
is
different from
r
-comparison (for
r\in[0,\infty)
)
as used in the definition of the radius of comparison, in the sense
of Definition 6.1 of
[
39
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be an ordered semigroup, and let
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in S
.
We write
\xi^{\prime}\ll\xi
if for every increasing sequence

(\xi_{n})
in
S
satisfying
\xi\leq\sup_{n}\xi_{n}
, there
is
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
\xi^{\prime}\leq\xi_{N}
.
We call
\ll

the
way below
relation (or
compact containment

relation) and say
\xi^{\prime}
is way below
\xi

(or
\xi^{\prime}
is compactly contained in
\xi
).

For this definition to be useful, we need to know that
suprema of increasing sequences in the ordered semigroup

S
actually exist.
For the main semigroup of interest here,
this is in fact the case.

Definition 2.7
.

An ordered semigroup
S
is said to be a
{\operatorname{Cu}}
-semigroup

if the following properties hold:

(1)

If
(\xi_{n})
is an increasing sequence in
S
,
then
\sup_{n}\xi_{n}
exists.

(2)

For any
\xi\in S
there exists a sequence
(\xi_{n})

such that
\xi_{n}\ll\xi_{n+1}
for all
n
and

\xi=\sup_{n}\xi_{n}
.

(3)

If
\xi_{1}\ll\xi_{2}
and
\eta_{1}\ll\eta_{2}
in
S
,
then
\xi_{1}+\eta_{1}\ll\xi_{2}+\eta_{2}
.

(4)

If
(\xi_{n})
and
(\eta_{n})
are increasing sequences
in
S
, then
\sup_{n}(\xi_{n}+\eta_{n})=\sup_{n}\xi_{n}+\sup_{n}\eta_{n}
.

The Cuntz semigroup
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
is a
{\operatorname{Cu}}
-semigroup
for a C*-algebra
A
, by the theorem at the start of Section 2
of
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
S
be a
{\operatorname{Cu}}
-semigroup, and let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We say that
S
is
m
-almost divisible
if
for any
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in S
such that
\xi^{\prime}\ll\xi

and any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, there exists
\eta\in S
such that

N\eta\leq\xi\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad\xi^{\prime}\leq(N+1)(m+1)\eta.

We say that
S
is
almost divisible
if
S
is

0
-almost divisible.

Other closely related notions of almost divisibility exist.
See the discussion after Definition 4.1 of
[
4
]

for details.

Applying Definition
2.8

to
S={\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
for a C*-algebra
A
generalizes
Definition 2.5(i) of
[
43
]
, as in Section 2.3 of
[
36
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra, and let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We say that

A
is
m
-almost divisible
if
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
is
m
-almost
divisible in the sense of
Definition
2.8
.
We say that
A
is

almost divisible
if
A
is 0-almost divisible.

The following definition first appeared in
Definition 2.6 of
[
43
]
, with the additional
assumptions the C*-algebra involved is separable, simple, and
unital.
In Section 2.3 of
[
36
]
it is generalized to
arbitrary C*-algebras, and in
Definition 5.1 of
[
4
]
to
{\operatorname{Cu}}
-semigroups.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra and let
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We say that

A
is
(n,m)
-pure
if has
n
-comparison in the
sense of Definition
2.5
and is
m
-almost divisible
in the sense of Definition
2.8
.
We say that
A
is
pure
if it is
(0,0)
-pure.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
A
be a simple stably finite unital C*-algebra which is not of
Type I.
Let
p\in A\otimes K
be a nonzero projection,
let
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
and let
\xi\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)\setminus\{0\}
.
Then there exist
x,y\in(p(A\otimes K)p)_{+}
and

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that:

(1)

{\operatorname{sp}}(x)={\operatorname{sp}}(y)=[0,1]
.

(2)

x\precsim_{A}p
.

(3)

p\precsim_{A}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\oplus(y-\varepsilon)_{+}
.

(4)

N\langle y\rangle_{A}\leq\xi
.

(5)

N\langle y\rangle_{A}\leq\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}
.

Proof.

Choose
a\in(A\otimes K)_{+}\setminus\{0\}

such that
\langle a\rangle_{A}=\xi
.
Use Lemma 2.4 of
[
34
]
to choose
orthogonal nonzero positive elements

b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{N}\in p(A\otimes K)p

such that
b_{0}\sim_{A}b_{1}\sim_{A}b_{2}\sim_{A}\cdots\sim_{A}b_{N}

and orthogonal nonzero positive elements

c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\ldots,c_{N}\in{\overline{a(A\otimes K)a}}

such that
c_{1}\sim_{A}c_{2}\sim_{A}\cdots\sim_{A}c_{N}
.
By Lemma 2.6 of
[
34
]
, there is

d\in\bigl({\overline{b_{0}(A\otimes K)b_{0}}}\bigr)_{+}\setminus\left\{0\right\}

such that
d\precsim_{A}c_{1}
.
Use Lemma
2.12
to choose

y\in\bigl({\overline{d(A\otimes K)d}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(y)=[0,1]
.
Define
x=p-y
and
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}
.
We show that these choices satisfy the conclusion of the lemma.

Part (
1
) is clear.
For (
2
), we in fact have
x\leq p
.
For (
4
), we have

y\precsim_{A}d\precsim_{A}c_{1}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad N\langle c_{1}\rangle_{A}=\langle c_{1}\rangle_{A}+\langle c_{2}\rangle_{A}+\cdots+\langle c_{N}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a\rangle_{A}=\xi.

We prove (
3
).
Set
r=(x-\varepsilon)_{+}+(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\in p(A\otimes K)p
.
Then

\|r-p\|\leq\bigl\|(x-\varepsilon)_{+}-x\bigr\|+\bigl\|(y-\varepsilon)_{+}-y\bigr\|\leq\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}.

Therefore
r
is invertible in
p(A\otimes K)p
.
So

p\sim_{A}r=(x-\varepsilon)_{+}+(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\oplus(y-\varepsilon)_{+},

as desired.

Finally, we prove (
5
).
Define a continuous function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
by

f(\lambda)=\begin{cases}0&\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{3}\\
3\lambda-1&\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{1}{3}<\lambda\leq\frac{2}{3}\\
1&\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{2}{3}<\lambda.\end{cases}

Then
(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\sim f(x)
.
Since

p-x=y\in{\overline{d(A\otimes K)d}}\subseteq{\overline{b_{0}(A\otimes K)b_{0}}},

we have
xb_{j}=b_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,N
.
Therefore also
f(x)b_{j}=b_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,N
.
Now

N\langle b_{0}\rangle_{A}=\langle b_{1}\rangle_{A}+\langle b_{2}\rangle_{A}+\cdots+\langle b_{N}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle f(x)\rangle_{A}=\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}.

Since

y\in{\overline{b_{0}(A\otimes K)b_{0}}}
,
we have
y\precsim_{A}b_{0}
,
and (
5
) follows.
∎

2.2.
Comparison and Divisibility in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)

In this section, we show that
n
-comparison and

m
-almost divisibility of
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
are equivalent to
comparison and divisibility properties in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.
For the clarity of the reader, we distinguish between the class
of an element
a\in A
when viewed as in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
or
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
,
as noted in Remark
2.2
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra and let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Then
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has
n
-comparison
if and only if
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
has
n
-comparison.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has
n
-comparison.
Let
a,a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
satisfy

[a]_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}[a_{j}]
in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
.
Thus, there are
k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

such that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

(k_{j}+1)[a]_{A}\leq k_{j}[a_{j}]_{A}.

For
j=0,1,\ldots,n
, we have

(k_{j}+1)\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
By
n
-comparison for
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
,

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

By Remark
2.2
, it follows that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

[a]_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}[a_{j}]_{A},

showing
n
-comparison for
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.

Let us now assume that
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
has
n
-comparison.
Let
a,a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
,
and suppose that there are
k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that

(k_{j}+1)\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}

for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
\delta_{j}>0
such that in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
we have

(k_{j}+1)\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle(a_{j}-\delta_{j})_{+}\rangle_{A}.

By Lemma 1.9 of
[
34
]
, there exist

c,c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that

(2.1)

\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}=\langle c\rangle_{A}

and for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
we have

(2.2)

\langle(a_{j}-\delta_{j})\rangle_{A}=\langle c_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Thus, in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
we have

(k_{j}+1)\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle c_{j}\rangle_{A}

and hence, by Remark
2.2
, in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

(k_{j}+1)[c]_{A}\leq k_{j}[c_{j}]_{A}.

By
n
-comparison for
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
, we see that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

(2.3)

[c]_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}[c_{j}]_{A}.

Using (
2.1
) at the first step,
(
2.3
) and Remark
2.2
at the second step,
and (
2.2
) at the third step,
we get the following inequalities in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
:

\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}=\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle c_{j}\rangle_{A}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle(a_{j}-\delta_{j})\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
,
proving
n
-comparison for
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
∎

Next, we show that
m
-almost divisibility in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)

is equivalent to a divisibility condition in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.
We recall the following standard result.

Lemma 2.16

(Proposition 4.3 of
[
16
]
)
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra and let
a,b\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
.
Then
\langle a\rangle_{A}\ll\langle b\rangle_{A}
if and only if there is an
\varepsilon>0
such
that
a\precsim_{A}(b-\varepsilon)_{+}
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra and let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Then
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
is
m
-almost divisible if and only if
for every
a\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
, every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, and every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

k[b]_{A}\leq[a]_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad[(a-\varepsilon)_{+}]_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)[b]_{A}.

Proof.

Let
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
be
m
-almost divisible.
Let
a\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
and let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
Since

\left\langle\left(a-\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\ll\left\langle\left(a-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}
,
and
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
is
m
-almost divisible,
there exists
b\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
such that

(2.4)

k\langle b\rangle_{A}\leq\left\langle\left(a-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad\left\langle\left(a-\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\langle b\rangle_{A}.

Choose
\delta>0
such that

(2.5)

\left\langle\left(a-\varepsilon\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\langle(b-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A}.

By Lemma 1.9 of
[
34
]
,
there exists
c\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that

\langle(b-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A}=\langle c\rangle_{A}.

Hence, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we see that

k\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a\rangle_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\langle c\rangle_{A}.

Finally, by Remark
2.2
, it follows that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

k[c]_{A}\leq[a]_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad[(a-\varepsilon)_{+}]\leq(k+1)(m+1)[c]_{A}.

This is the desired relation.

Conversely, assume that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, and
a\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
,
there exists
b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

k[b]_{A}\leq[a]_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad[(a-\varepsilon)_{+}]\leq(k+1)(m+1)[b]_{A}.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and let
\xi,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
satisfy
\xi\ll\eta
.
Choose
a\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
such that

(2.6)

\langle a\rangle_{A}=\eta.

By Lemma
2.16
, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

(2.7)

\xi\leq\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}.

By Lemma 1.9 of
[
34
]
,
there exists
c\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that

\langle c\rangle_{A}=\left\langle\left(a-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}.

Choose
\delta>0
such that

(2.8)

\left(a-\varepsilon\right)_{+}\precsim_{A}(c-\delta)_{+}.

By hypothesis, there exists
b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
such that
in
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
we have

k[b]_{A}\leq[c]_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\left[\left(c-\delta\right)_{+}\right]_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)[b]_{A}.

In
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
we now get,
using Remark
2.2
and (
2.6
),

k\langle b\rangle_{A}\leq\langle c\rangle_{A}=\left\langle\left(a-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq\eta

and using (
2.7
), (
2.8
),
and Remark
2.2
,

\xi\leq\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq\left\langle\left(c-\delta\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\langle c\rangle_{A}.

This proves
m
-almost divisibility for
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
∎

3.
Pureness for Large Subalgebras

In this section, we prove that if
B\subseteq A
is a large subalgebra
the sense of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
,
then
A
is pure if and only if
B
is pure.
We obtain results for comparison and almost divisibility
independently, with an increase of the parameter in the case
of almost divisibility.
For our applications, we do not need to know that pureness of
A

implies pureness of
B
.

3.1.
Preliminary Results

We start by collecting material needed for both
comparison and almost divisibility.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
A
be a stably finite simple unital C*-algebra.
Following Definition 3.1 of
[
34
]
,
we let
{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)
denote the set of
\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
such that

\eta
is not the class of a projection;
that is,
\eta
is purely positive in the sense of
[
5
]
.
See also
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem 6.8 of
[
34
]
)
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional stably finite simple
unital C*-algebra, and let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra
which is large in the sense of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
.
Let
\iota\colon B\to A
be the inclusion map.
Then
\iota_{*}
defines an order and semigroup isomorphism
from
{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(B)\cup\left\{0\right\}
to
{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)\cup\left\{0\right\}
.

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let the assumptions be as in Theorem
3.2
.
Then for any
x\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
for which

\langle x\rangle_{A}
is not the class of a projection in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
,
there is a
y\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
such that
x\sim_{A}y

and
\langle y\rangle_{B}
is not the class of projection in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
.

The appropriate specialization of Lemma
2.12
is as follows.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional stably finite simple
unital C*-algebra, let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra
which is large in the sense of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]

(possibly
B=A
),
and let
C\subseteq B
be a nonzero hereditary subalgebra.
Then there exists
a\in C_{+}
such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(a)=[0,1]
.

Proof.

The algebra
B
is simple by Proposition 5.2 of
[
34
]
,
infinite dimensional by Proposition 5.5 of
[
34
]
,
and unital.
Therefore
B
is not of Type I, so
C
is also is not of Type I.
Thus Lemma
2.12
applies.
∎

3.2.
Comparison for Large Subalgebras

In this subsection, we show that a
large subalgebra has
m
-comparison if and only if
the containing algebra does.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A
be a simple separable infinite dimensional stably
finite unital C*-algebra, let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra
which is large in the sense of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
,
and let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Then
B
has
m
-comparison if and only if
A
has
m
-comparison.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
m
-comparison for
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
implies

m
-comparison for
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
Let
x,a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in(A\otimes K)_{+}

satisfy
\langle x\rangle_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
for

j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
We must show that

(3.1)

\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

If
\langle x\rangle_{A}=0
, this is immediate, so we assume
that
\langle x\rangle_{A}\neq 0
.
By the definition of
<_{\operatorname{s}}
(Definition
2.3
),
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
there is
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

such that

(3.2)

(k_{j}+1)\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

First assume
\langle x\rangle_{A}
is not the class of a projection.
Use Corollary
3.3

to choose
y\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
such that
\langle y\rangle_{A}=\langle x\rangle_{A}

and
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(y)
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. For
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
we will find
b_{j}\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
such that

(3.3)

\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Since
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(y)
,
there is
\rho\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing
.
Choose
\lambda\in{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)
.
Choose a continuous function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}(f)\subseteq(\rho,\varepsilon)
and
f(\lambda)\neq 0
.

Define

I=\bigl\{j\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}\colon{\mbox{$\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}$ is the class of a nonzero projection}}\bigr\}.

For
j\not\in I
, Corollary
3.3
provides

b_{j}\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
such that

\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}=\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
Use Lemma
3.4
to choose

c\in\bigl({\overline{f(y)(B\otimes K)f(y)}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(c)=[0,1]
.
Set
z_{j}=(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c
.
Then
(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{B}z_{j}\precsim_{B}y
.
We have

(k_{j}+1)\langle z_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq(k_{j}+1)\langle y\rangle_{A}=(k_{j}+1)\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}=k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Since
0
is a limit point of both
{\operatorname{sp}}(z_{j})
and
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
,
Theorem
3.2
gives the second step in
the calculation

(k_{j}+1)\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq(k_{j}+1)\langle z_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.

It follows that
\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
,
and we already have
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}=\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
So (
3.3
) holds.

Now suppose
j\in I
.
We may as well assume that
a_{j}
is a projection.
Use Lemma
2.14
to choose

r_{j},s_{j}\in(a_{j}(A\otimes K)a_{j})_{+}
and
\delta\in(0,1)

such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(r_{j})={\operatorname{sp}}(s_{j})=[0,1],\qquad r_{j}\precsim_{A}a_{j},

a_{j}\precsim_{A}(r_{j}-\delta)_{+}\oplus(s_{j}-\delta)_{+},\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 2k_{j}\langle s_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle(r_{j}-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A}.

Corollary
3.3
yields
b_{j}\in(B\otimes K)_{+}

such that
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
and
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}=\langle r_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
Use Lemma 2.6 of
[
34
]
to choose

d_{j}\in\bigl({\overline{f(y)(A\otimes K)f(y)}}\bigr)_{+}\setminus\{0\}

such that
d_{j}\precsim_{A}s_{j}
.
Use Lemma
3.4
to choose

g_{j}\in\bigl({\overline{d_{j}(A\otimes K)d_{j}}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(g_{j})=[0,1]
.
Corollary
3.3
yields
c_{j}\in(K\otimes B)_{+}

such that
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(c_{j})
and
\langle c_{j}\rangle_{A}=\langle g_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
Set
z_{j}=(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\oplus c_{j}
.
We have
c_{j}\precsim_{A}f(y)
, so

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)\langle z_{j}\rangle_{A}&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle y\rangle_{A}=(2k_{j}+2)\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle(r_{j}-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A}+2k_{j}\langle(s_{j}-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A}\\
&\leq(2k_{j}+1)\langle r_{j}\rangle_{A}=(2k_{j}+1)\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}.\end{split}

Since
0
is a limit point of both
{\operatorname{sp}}(z_{j})
and
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
,
Theorem
3.2
gives the second step in
the calculation

(2k_{j}+2)\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle z_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq(2k_{j}+1)\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.

It follows that
\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.
We already have
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}=\langle r_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
So (
3.3
) holds in this case too.

Since (
3.3
) holds for
j=0,1,\ldots,m

and
B
has
m
-comparison, it follows that

\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.

Using the second part of (
3.3
), we get

\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get

\langle y\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Combining this with
\langle x\rangle_{A}=\langle y\rangle_{A}
gives (
3.1
)
and completes the proof of this direction in the case that
\langle x\rangle_{A}

is not the class of a projection.

Now assume that
\langle x\rangle_{A}
is the class of a nonzero
projection in
A\otimes K
.
Since
x\neq 0
, we have
a_{j}\neq 0
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Use Lemma 2.6 of
[
34
]
to choose

a\in(A\otimes K)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Set
k=\max(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})
.
By Lemma
2.13
, there exist
\mu,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)

such that

\mu\leq\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq\mu+\eta\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(2k+2)\eta\leq\langle a\rangle_{A}.

For
j=0,1,\ldots,m
, we then have,
also using (
3.2
) at the second step,

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)(\mu+\eta)&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle x\rangle_{A}+(2k_{j}+2)\eta\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}+\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}+\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}=(2k_{j}+1)\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.\end{split}

Hence
\mu+\eta<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
Since
\mu+\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)
, the previous case yields
the second step in the calculation

\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq\mu+\eta\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A},

which is (
3.1
).
This completes the proof that if
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
has

m
-comparison, then so does
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.

The reverse implication uses the same pieces, but arranged differently.
Assume that
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has
m
-comparison;
we show that
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
has
m
-comparison as well.
To this end, let

y,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
satisfy

\langle y\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Without loss of generality
y\neq 0
.
By the definition of
<_{\operatorname{s}}
(Definition
2.3
),
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
there is
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

such that
(k_{j}+1)\langle y\rangle_{B}\leq k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.
In particular,
b_{j}\neq 0
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.

First assume
\langle y\rangle_{B}
is not the class of a projection.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. We will find
z\in(B\otimes K)_{+}

and for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
, we will find
a_{j}\in(B\otimes K)_{+}

such that
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)
,

(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{B}z
,
and, for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
\langle z\rangle_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
,

0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(a_{j})
, and
\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.

Since
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(y)
,
there is
\rho\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing
.
Choose
\lambda\in{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)
.
Choose a continuous function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}(f)\subseteq(\rho,\varepsilon)
and
f(\lambda)\neq 0
.
Use Lemma
3.4
to choose

c\in\bigl({\overline{f(y)(B\otimes K)f(y)}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(c)=[0,1]
.
Set
z=(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c
.
Then
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)
and

(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{B}z\precsim_{B}y
.

Define

I=\bigl\{j\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}\colon{\mbox{$\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}$ is the class of a nonzero projection}}\bigr\}.

For
j\not\in I
, set
a_{j}=b_{j}
.
Then
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(a_{j})
.
Also,

\langle z\rangle_{B}\leq\langle y\rangle_{B}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(k_{j}+1)\langle y\rangle_{B}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}.

The second says that
\langle z\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}
.

Now suppose
j\in I
.
We may as well assume that
b_{j}
is a projection.
Use Lemma
2.14
to choose

a_{j},s_{j}\in(b_{j}(B\otimes K)b_{j})_{+}
and
\delta\in(0,1)

such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(a_{j})={\operatorname{sp}}(s_{j})=[0,1],\qquad a_{j}\precsim_{B}b_{j},

b_{j}\precsim_{B}(a_{j}-\delta)_{+}\oplus(s_{j}-\delta)_{+},\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 2k_{j}\langle s_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq\langle(a_{j}-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{B}.

Then

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)\langle z\rangle_{B}&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle y\rangle_{B}\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}+2k_{j}\langle s_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq(2k_{j}+1)\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}.\end{split}

Thus, here too
\langle z\rangle_{B}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}
,

0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(a_{j})
,
and
\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}
because
\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.

Passing to
A
, we have

\langle z\rangle_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Since
A
has
m
-comparison,
it follows that

\langle z\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}.

Since
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)

and of
{\operatorname{sp}}(a_{j})
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
Theorem
3.2
implies that

\langle z\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}.

Therefore

\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq\langle z\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.

We have proved that for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.

So
\langle y\rangle_{B}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.
This completes the case where
\langle y\rangle_{B}
is not the class of a projection.

Now assume that
\langle y\rangle_{B}
is the class of a nonzero
projection in
B\otimes K
.
Use Lemma 2.6 of
[
34
]
to choose

b\in(B\otimes K)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\langle b\rangle_{B}\leq\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Set
k=\max(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})
.
By Lemma
2.13
, there exist
\mu,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(B)

such that

\mu\leq\langle y\rangle_{B}\leq\mu+\eta\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(2k+2)\eta\leq\langle b\rangle_{B}.

For
j=0,1,\ldots,m
, we then have

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)(\mu+\eta)&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle y\rangle_{B}+(2k_{j}+2)\eta\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}+\langle b\rangle_{B}\leq 2k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}+\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}=(2k_{j}+1)\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}.\end{split}

Hence
\mu+\eta<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{B}
.
Since
\mu+\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(B)
, the previous case yields
the second step in the calculation

\langle y\rangle_{B}\leq\mu+\eta\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{B}.

This completes the proof that if
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has

m
-comparison, then so does
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
.
∎

3.3.
Almost Divisibility for Large Subalgebras

In this subsection, we prove that
if
A
is a simple stably finite unital C*-algebra and
B\subseteq A
is a
large subalgebra which is
m
-almost divisible, then
A
is

(2m+1)
-almost divisible.
In the opposite direction, if
A
is

m
-almost divisible, then
B
is
(4m+3)
-almost divisible.
This differs from the behavior of
m
-comparison
as proved in Section
3.2
,
where the parameter
m
remains unchanged when
passing between
A
and
B
.
We don’t know whether the stronger version is true for

m
-almost divisibility.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple stably finite unital C*-algebra,
and let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra
which is large in the sense of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
.
Let
\iota\colon B\to A
be the inclusion map.
Let
\xi,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
satisfy
\iota_{*}(\xi)\ll\iota_{*}(\eta)
.
Then
\xi\ll\eta
.

Proof.

Choose
x,y\in B\otimes K
such that such that

\langle x\rangle_{B}=\xi
and
\langle y\rangle_{B}=\eta
.
If there is a
projection
p\in B
such that
\xi=\langle p\rangle_{B}
, then

\xi=\langle p\rangle_{B}\ll\langle p\rangle_{B}=\xi\leq\eta,

which is the desired conclusion.
The conclusion follows the
same way if
\eta
is the class of a projection.
Therefore we may
assume that
0
is a limit point of both
{\operatorname{sp}}(x)
and

{\operatorname{sp}}(y)
.

Since
\langle x\rangle_{A}\ll\langle y\rangle_{A}
, Lemma
2.16

provides
\varepsilon>0
such that
x\precsim_{A}(y-\varepsilon)_{+}
.
Since
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(y)
, there is

\rho\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing
.
Choose

\lambda\in{\operatorname{sp}}(y)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)
.
Choose a continuous function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}(f)\subseteq(\rho,\varepsilon)
and
f(\lambda)\neq 0
.
Use Lemma
3.4
to choose

c\in\bigl({\overline{f(y)(B\otimes K)f(y)}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(c)=[0,1]
.
Then

(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\leq(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c\precsim_{B}(y-\rho)_{+}.

It follows that

x\precsim_{A}(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\leq(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c.

Since
0
is a limit point of both
{\operatorname{sp}}(x)

and
{\operatorname{sp}}\bigl((y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c\bigr)
,
Theorem
3.2
implies that

x\precsim_{B}(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c
.
Combining this with

(y-\varepsilon)_{+}+c\precsim_{B}(y-\rho)_{+}
,
we get
x\precsim_{B}(y-\rho)_{+}
.
So

\langle x\rangle_{B}\ll\langle y\rangle_{B}
by
Lemma
2.16
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple stably finite unital C*-algebra,
and let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra which is large in the sense
of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
.
Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Suppose that
B
is
m
-almost divisible.
Then
A
is
(2m+1)
-almost divisible.

Proof.

Let
\iota\colon B\to A
be the inclusion map.
Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Let
\xi,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
satisfy
\xi\ll\eta
.
Choose
a,b\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
such that

\langle a\rangle_{A}=\xi
and
\langle b\rangle_{A}=\eta
.

First suppose that
a=0
or
0
is a limit point of

{\operatorname{sp}}(a)
, and that
b=0
or
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b)
.
We claim that in this case, we
actually get
\gamma\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
such that

(3.4)

k\gamma\leq\eta\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\xi\leq(k+1)(m+1)\gamma.

This claim certainly implies the desired conclusion for
this case, which has merely
k\gamma\leq\eta
and

\xi\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\gamma
.

To prove the claim, use Corollary
3.3

to find
\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)

such that
\iota_{*}(\xi_{0})=\xi
and
\iota_{*}(\eta_{0})=\eta
.
Then
\xi_{0}\ll\eta_{0}
by Lemma
3.6
.
So the hypothesis provides
\gamma_{0}\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)

such that
k\gamma_{0}\leq\eta_{0}
and

\xi_{0}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\gamma_{0}
.
Set
\gamma=\iota_{*}(\gamma_{0})
.
Then (
3.4
) holds, as claimed.

Otherwise, one or both of
a
and
b
is nonzero but
its spectrum does not have
0
as a limit point.
Therefore there is a nonzero projection
p\in A\otimes K

such that
\langle p\rangle_{A}=\langle a\rangle_{A}
or there is a nonzero projection

p\in A\otimes K
such that
\langle p\rangle_{A}=\langle b\rangle_{A}
.
In either case, it suffices to prove the desired
conclusion when
a=b=p
.

The case
N=1
of Lemma
2.14
provides

x,y\in(p(A\otimes K)p)_{+}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(x)={\operatorname{sp}}(y)=[0,1],\quad x\precsim_{A}p,\quad p\precsim_{A}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\oplus(y-\varepsilon)_{+},\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad y\precsim_{A}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}.

Lemma
2.16
tells us that

\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\ll\langle x\rangle_{A}
.
Since
\varepsilon<1
,
it follows that
0
is a limit point of

{\operatorname{sp}}((x-\varepsilon)_{+})
as well as of
{\operatorname{sp}}(x)
.
By the claim above (see (
3.4
)),
there is
\gamma\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
such that

(3.5)

k\gamma\leq\langle x\rangle_{A}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(m+1)\gamma.

Now

k\gamma\leq\langle x\rangle_{A}\leq\langle p\rangle_{A}

and, using
y\precsim_{A}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}
at the second
step and the second part of (
3.5
) at the
third step,

\langle p\rangle_{A}\leq\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}+\langle(y-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq 2\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\gamma.

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We now give the the analog of
Proposition
3.7
in the other direction.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple stably finite unital C*-algebra,
and let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra which is large in the sense
of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
.
Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Suppose that
A
is
m
-almost divisible.
Then
B
is
(4m+3)
-almost divisible.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is similar to
that of Proposition
3.7
.
Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and let
\xi,\eta\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
satisfy

\xi\ll\eta
.
Choose
a,b\in(B\otimes K)_{+}

such that
\langle a\rangle_{B}=\xi
and
\langle b\rangle_{B}=\eta
.

First suppose that
a=0
or
0
is a limit point of

{\operatorname{sp}}(a)
, and that
b=0
or
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b)
.
In this case, we will prove the following stronger conclusion:
for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
there is
\gamma\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
such that

(3.6)

k\gamma\leq\langle b\rangle_{B}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle a\rangle_{B}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\gamma.

Since
\langle a\rangle_{B}\ll\langle b\rangle_{B}
and homomorphisms preserve the
relation
\ll
(this is obvious from Lemma
2.16
),
we have
\langle a\rangle_{A}\ll\langle b\rangle_{A}
in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
Using
m
-almost divisibility of
A
, we obtain

c\in(A\otimes K)_{+}
such that

(3.7)

2k\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq(2k+1)(m+1)\langle c\rangle_{A}.

Suppose that
\langle c\rangle_{A}\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)\cup\left\{0\right\}
,
so that
2\langle c\rangle_{A}\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)\cup\left\{0\right\}
.
By Corollary
3.3
, there is
d\in(B\otimes K)_{+}

such that
\langle d\rangle_{A}=\langle c\rangle_{A}
and
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(d)
.
Using (
3.7
), we have

k\langle d\rangle_{A}\leq 2k\langle d\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}

and

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq(2k+1)(m+1)\langle d\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\langle d\rangle_{A}.

By Theorem
3.2
, these relations
also hold for the classes in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
.
This is (
3.6
) with
\gamma=\langle d\rangle_{B}
.

Let us now suppose that
\langle c\rangle_{A}
is a class of nonzero projection.
By Lemma
2.13
, there exist

\mu,\kappa\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)
such that

\mu\leq\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq\mu+\kappa\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa\leq\langle c\rangle_{A}.

Then
\mu+\kappa\in{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)
, and from (
3.7
)
we see that

k(\mu+\kappa)\leq 2k\langle c\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)(\mu+\kappa).

Corollary
3.3
provides

d\in(B\otimes K)_{+}
such that
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(d)

and
\langle d\rangle_{A}=\mu+\kappa
, and Theorem
3.2
implies that

k\langle d\rangle_{B}\leq\langle b\rangle_{B}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\langle a\rangle_{B}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\langle d\rangle_{B}.

This is (
3.6
) with
\gamma=\langle d\rangle_{B}
.

It remains to consider the case in which
one or both of
a
and
b
is nonzero but
its spectrum does not have
0
as a limit point.
In this case, there is a nonzero projection
p\in B\otimes K

such that
\langle p\rangle_{B}=\langle a\rangle_{B}
or there is a nonzero projection

p\in B\otimes K
such that
\langle p\rangle_{B}=\langle b\rangle_{B}
.
In either case,
\langle a\rangle_{B}\leq\langle p\rangle_{B}\ll\langle p\rangle_{B}\leq\langle b\rangle_{B}
,
and it suffices to prove the desired
conclusion when
a=b=p
.
By Lemma
2.14
, there exist

x,y\in(p(B\otimes K)p)_{+}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(x)={\operatorname{sp}}(y)=[0,1],\quad x\precsim_{B}p,\quad p\precsim_{B}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\oplus(y-\varepsilon)_{+},\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad y\precsim_{B}(x-\varepsilon)_{+}.

Since
\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\ll\langle x\rangle_{B}
and
0
is a
limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}((x-\varepsilon)_{+})
as well as of

{\operatorname{sp}}(x)
, by the previous case (see (
3.6
)),
we obtain
\gamma\in{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
such that

k\gamma\leq\langle x\rangle_{B}\leq\langle p\rangle_{B}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq(k+1)(2m+2)\gamma.

Now

\langle p\rangle_{B}\leq 2\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{B}\leq(k+1)(4m+4)\gamma.

Thus
B
is
(4m+3)
-almost divisible.
∎

3.4.
Pureness for Large Subalgebras

We put the results of Section
3.2

and Section
3.3
together to get the the following result.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple stably finite unital C*-algebra,
and let
B\subseteq A
be a subalgebra which is large in the sense
of Definition 4.1 of
[
34
]
.
Then
A
is pure if and
only if
B
is pure.

Proof.

If
B
is pure, then
B
has
0
-comparison and is
0
-almost
divisible.
So
A
has
0
-comparison by
Theorem
3.5
and is
1
-divisible by
Proposition
3.7
.
Together, these imply
A
is pure by Theorem
2.11
.

Conversely, suppose that
A
is pure.
Then
A
has

0
-comparison and is
0
-almost divisible.
So
B
has
0
-comparison by Theorem
3.5

and is
3
-almost divisible by Proposition
3.8
.
Together, these imply
B
is pure by Theorem
2.11
.
∎

4.
Pureness of Crossed Products by
Automorphisms of
C(X,D)

We establish pureness for crossed product C*-algebras

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)
where
D

is a simple unital pure C*-algebra,
X
is a compact metric
space, and
\alpha
induces a minimal homeomorphism
h\colon X\to X
.
Consequently, such crossed
products will have stable rank one, and under suitable
conditions will also have real rank zero.

Notation 4.1
.

For a locally compact Hausdorff space
X

and a C*-algebra
D
,
we identify
C_{0}(X,D)=C_{0}(X)\otimes D

in the standard way.
For an open subset
U\subseteq X
,
we use the abbreviation

C_{0}(U,D)=\big\{a\in C_{0}(X,D)\colon{\mbox{$a(x)=0$ for all $x\in X\setminus U$}}\big\}\subseteq C_{0}(X,D).

This subalgebra is of course canonically isomorphic to
the usual algebra
C_{0}(U,D)
when
U
is considered
as a locally compact Hausdorff space in its own right.

In particular,
if
Y\subseteq X
is closed, then

(4.1)

C_{0}(X\setminus Y,\,D)=\big\{a\in C_{0}(X,D)\colon{\mbox{$a(x)=0$ for all $x\in Y$}}\big\}.

In Lemma
4.2
, we recall from
[
6
]

a general construction for automorphisms of
C_{0}(X,D)

which lie over homeomorphisms of
X
in the sense
of Definition 1.2 of
[
6
]
. (This means that there
is a homeomorphism
h\colon X\to X
such that the
corresponding action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
on
C_{0}(X)
is generated by

f\mapsto f\circ h^{-1}
, for
f\in C_{0}(X)
).
This will be used repeatedly in the examples of
Section
7
.

Lemma 4.2

(Lemma 1.4 of
[
6
]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, let
h\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism,
and let
D
be a C*-algebra.
Then there is a one to one correspondence
between actions of
{\mathbb{Z}}
on
C_{0}(X,D)
that lie over
h
and
continuous functions from
X
to
{\operatorname{Aut}}(D)
, given as follows.

For any function
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}
from
X
to
{\operatorname{Aut}}(D)

such that
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}(d)
is continuous for all
d\in D
,
there is an automorphism
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(C_{0}(X,D))

given by
\alpha(a)(x)=\alpha_{x}(a(h^{-1}(x)))
for all

a\in C_{0}(X,D)
and
x\in X
, and this automorphism lies
over
h
.

Conversely, if
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(C_{0}(X,D))
lies over
h
,
then there is a function
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}
from
X
to

{\operatorname{Aut}}(D)
such that
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}(d)
is continuous for
all
d\in D
and such that

\alpha(a)(x)=\alpha_{x}(a(h^{-1}(x)))
for all

a\in C_{0}(X,D)
and
x\in X
.

The following definition was introduced in Definition 2.3
of
[
6
]
, and is the analog of
Definition 7.3 of
[
34
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space,
let
h\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism,
let
D
be a C*-algebra,
and let
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(C_{0}(X,D))

be an automorphism which lies over
h
.
Let
u
be
the standard unitary implementing the action of

\alpha
in the crossed product C*-algebra

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C_{0}(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)
.
Let
Y\subseteq X
be a nonempty closed subset,
and, following (
4.1
) in
Notation

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A
and
B
be simple unital C*-algebras.
Assume
B
has a unique tracial state
\sigma
.
Then the map
\rho\mapsto\rho\otimes\sigma

is a bijection from
T(A)
to
T(A\otimes_{\min}B)
.

Proof.

Let
\tau
be a tracial state on
A\otimes_{\min}B
.
For fixed
a\in A_{+}
, define a map
\omega_{a}
on

B
by
\omega_{a}(b)=\tau(a\otimes b)
.
Then

\omega_{a}
is a bounded linear functional on
B
,
and is positive.
For any
b,c\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{a}(bc)-\omega_{a}(cb)

\displaystyle=\tau(a\otimes bc)-\tau(a\otimes cb)

\displaystyle=\tau(a\otimes bc-a\otimes cb)

\displaystyle=\tau((a\otimes b)(1_{A}\otimes c)-(1_{A}\otimes c)(a\otimes b))=0

and so
\omega_{a}
has the trace property.
Therefore,
it is a nonnegative multiple of
\sigma
, say

\omega_{a}(b)=\rho(a)\sigma(b)
.
Writing

a\in A
as a linear combination of positive
elements allows the extension of
\rho
to a map

A\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
Positivity and the condition
that
\rho(1)=1
are immediate.
The trace
property of
\rho
is easily verified via the
calculation

\rho(xy)=\rho(xy)\sigma(1_{B})=\omega_{xy}(1_{B})=\tau(xy\otimes 1_{B})=\tau(yx\otimes 1_{B})=\rho(yx)\sigma(1_{B})=\rho(yx).

Additivity of
\rho
on
A_{+}
follows from the
observation (using the linearity of
\tau
) that

\displaystyle\rho(a_{1}+a_{2})\sigma(b)=\omega_{a_{1}+a_{2}}(b)

\displaystyle=\tau((a_{1}+a_{2})\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\tau(a_{1}\otimes b)+\tau(a_{2}\otimes b)=\rho(a_{1})\sigma(b)+\rho(a_{2})\sigma(b)

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{+}
, and linearity
of
\rho
follows.
Hence
\rho
is a tracial state
on A.
That
\rho\otimes\sigma=\tau
follows from
density of the linear span of the elementary tensors.
This gives surjectivity of the map

\rho\mapsto\rho\otimes\sigma
,
and injectivity follows easily by evaluation on

a\otimes 1_{B}
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
D
be a simple unital C*-algebra, let

be
a compact metric space, let

be
a minimal homeomorphism, and obtain an action

as in

.
For every

and every

, there exist an

and

such that

\lVert s_{k}(x)\rVert=1
for all

, and

\Big\lVert E(a)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}as_{k}^{*}\Big\rVert<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Choose
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
f_{k}\in C(X,D)
for

-N\leq k\leq N
such that

b=\sum_{k=-N}^{N}f_{k}u^{k}
satisfies

\lVert a-b\rVert<\varepsilon/2
.
Set

.
By Proposition 11.1.19
of
[

, there exist
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and

such that,,
for

|t_{k}(x)|=1
for all

and such that, for all

and

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}(x)\overline{t_{k}(h^{-j}(x))}=0.

Set

Define

\displaystyle P(f_{0})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}f_{0}s_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(t_{k}\otimes 1_{D})f_{0}(t_{k}\otimes 1_{D})^{*}

\displaystyle=f_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(t_{k}\otimes 1_{D})(t_{k}\otimes 1_{D})^{*}=f_{0}=E(f_{0}).

It is easy to show that, for

and any

and hence, for any

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}(x)\alpha^{j}(s_{k}^{*})(x)=\Big[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}(x)\overline{t_{k}(h^{-j}(x))}\Big]\cdot 1_{D}=0.

It follows that, for any

Now,

\displaystyle=\lVert E(a)-E(b)\rVert+\lVert E(b)-P(b)\rVert+\lVert P(b)-P(a)\rVert

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/2+0+\varepsilon/2

∎

5.
Pureness of Crossed products by Compact Group Actions with
finite Rokhlin Dimension with commuting towers

In this section, we apply approximation results for crossed products,
where the action has finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers,
in order to establish pureness for certain crossed products.
The main result we use, due to Gardella, Hirshberg, and Santiago,
states that if
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)

has finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers,
then the crossed product
C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)

can be locally approximated by a continuous
C(X)
-algebra
whose fibers are isomorphic to
A\otimes K(L^{2}(G))
.

We begin with the following notation and definitions.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
G
be a compact group.
We write
\mathtt{Lt}\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(C(G))

for the left action of
G
on
C(G)
,
given by
\mathtt{Lt}_{g}(f)(t)=f(g^{-1}t)
.

The following lemma for finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers,
due to Gardella,
characterizes it in terms of elements of the C*-algebra itself.
See Definition 3.2 and Lemma 3.7 of
[
14
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a second countable compact group. Let
A
be a separable C*-algebra,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action.
Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.
Then
{\operatorname{dim}}_{\operatorname{Rok}}^{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha)\leq d
if and only if, for any finite sets

F\subseteq A
and
S\subseteq C(G)
and any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist
d+1
completely positive contractive maps

\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{d}\colon C(G)\to A

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

For any
a\in F
and
f\in S
,

\|[\psi_{j}(f),a]\|<\varepsilon
for
j=0,1,\ldots,d
.

(2)

For any
f\in S
and
g\in G
,

\|\alpha_{g}(\psi_{j}(f))-\psi_{j}(\mathtt{Lt}_{g}(f))\|<\varepsilon

for
j=0,1,\ldots,d

(3)

\|\psi_{j}(f_{1})\psi_{j}(f_{2})\|<\varepsilon

whenever
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
S
are orthogonal.

(4)

For any
a\in F
,
\|\sum_{j=0}^{d}\psi_{j}(1_{C(G)})a-a\|<\varepsilon
.

(5)

For any
f_{1},f_{2}\in S
,

\|[\psi_{j}(f_{1}),\psi_{k}(f_{2})]\|<\varepsilon
for
j,k=0,1,\ldots,d
.

The following result is an important structure theorem
due to Gardella, Hirshberg, and Santiago.

Proposition 5.3

(Corollary 4.6 of
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be a second countable compact group,
let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space,
and let
A
be a separable C*-algebra.
Let
G\curvearrowright X
be a free continuous action of
G
on
X
,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of
G
on
A
.
Equip the C*-algebra
C(X,A)
with the diagonal action of
G
,
denoted by
\gamma
.
Then the crossed product C*-algebra

C^{*}\bigl(G,\,C(X,A),\,\gamma\bigr)

is a continuous
C(X/G)
-algebra,
each of whose fibers is isomorphic to
A\otimes K(L^{2}(G))
.
If
G
is furthermore a Lie group,
then
C^{*}\bigl(G,\,C(X,A),\,\gamma\bigr)
is the
section algebra of a locally trivial bundle over
X/G

with fibers isomorphic to
A\otimes K(L^{2}(G))
.

We now recall the definition of sequentially split homomorphisms
(Definition 2.1 of
[
7
]
).

Definition 5.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be two C*-algebras. A homomorphism

\varphi\colon A\rightarrow B
is
sequentially split
,
if there exists a commutative diagram of homomorphisms
of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

where the horizontal map
\iota
is the canonical inclusion.

The following theorem, which is Proposition 4.11 of
[
15
]
,
is a key approximation result used to establish permanence properties
for crossed products by actions with finite Rokhlin dimension with
commuting towers. Its statement involves the notion of a

positively existential embedding
, which, in the setting of
separable C*-algebras, coincides with that of a sequentially
split homomorphism.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action
of a second countable compact group on a separable C*-algebra
such that
{\operatorname{dim}}_{\operatorname{Rok}}^{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha)<\infty
.
Then there exist a compact metric space
X

and a free action
G\curvearrowright X
such that, by endowing
C(X,A)
with the diagonal
G
-action
\gamma
, the
canonical embedding

\rho\colon C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)\to C^{*}\bigl(G,\,C(X,A),\,\gamma\bigr)

is sequentially split.
Furthermore, if
G
is finite dimensional,
then
X
may be chosen to be finite dimensional.

In
[
7
]
, it is shown that the existence of a sequentially split homomorphism from the C*-algebra
A
to the C*-algebra
B

implies that several regularity properties pass from
B
to
A
.
The following results show that this is also the case for pureness.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
A
and
B
be separable C*-algebras, and

\varphi\colon A\rightarrow B
be a sequentially split homomorphism. If
B
is pure, then so is
A
.

Proof.

First observe that if
\varphi\colon A\to B
is a sequentially split homomorphism, then the induced (amplified) homomorphism

\tilde{\varphi}\colon A\otimes K\to B\otimes K

is again sequentially split. By a mild abuse of notation, we continue to denote this amplified map by
\varphi
. We thus obtain a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A\otimes K)_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\otimes K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

where
\iota
denotes the canonical inclusion of
A\otimes K
into
(A\otimes K)_{\infty}
as constant sequences.

Since
B
is pure,
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
has
0
-almost comparison and is
0
-almost divisible. Moreover,
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
has
0
-almost comparison by Theorem 2.9 of
[

. It remains to show that

is

,

, and

. Fix

to be a representative

. Since

(\psi\circ\varphi)(a)=\iota(a)
, we can choose

,
such that for all

Thus, for all

, we get the following subequivalences

Furthermore, since
{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)
is

such that

(5.2)

k\langle b\rangle_{B}\leq\left\langle\left(\varphi(a)-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{B}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\langle\left(\varphi(a)-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{B}\leq(k+1)\langle b\rangle_{B}.

From the second inequality in the above equation, we obtain

\left(\varphi(a)-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}\precsim_{B}b\otimes 1_{k+1},

and hence

\left((\psi\circ\varphi)(a)-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}\precsim_{{(A\otimes K)}_{\infty}}\psi(b)\otimes 1_{k+1}.

Let
e\in(A\otimes K)_{\infty}
be such that

\left\|e^{*}\left(\psi(b)\otimes 1_{k+1}\right)e-\left((\psi\circ\varphi)(a)-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{32}.

Fix representative sequences
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
for

and
\psi(b)
, respectively, and choose

such that, for all
n\geq N_{2}
,

\left\|e_{n}^{*}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})e_{n}-\left(a_{n}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{32}.

It follows that, for all
n\geq N_{2}
,

(5.3)

\left(\left(a_{n}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{16}\right)_{+}\precsim_{A}\left(e_{n}^{*}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})e_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\right)_{+}.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
be such that
\|e_{n}\|^{2}\leq M
for all
n
. By
Lemma 1.4(8) of
[
34
]
,

(5.4)

\left(\left(a_{n}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\right)_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{16}\right)_{+}=\left(a_{n}-\frac{7\varepsilon}{16}\right)_{+},

and, by Lemma 1.4(6) of
[
34
]
at the second step and
Lemma 1.7 of
[
34
]
at the third step, we obtain

(5.5)

\begin{split}\left(e_{n}^{*}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})e_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\right)_{+}&=\left(e_{n}^{*}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})^{1/2}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})^{1/2}e_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\right)_{+}\\
&\sim_{A}\left((b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})^{1/2}e_{n}e_{n}^{*}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})^{1/2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\right)_{+}\\
&\precsim_{A}\left(\|e_{n}\|^{2}(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1})-\frac{\varepsilon}{32}\right)_{+}\\
&\precsim_{A}\left(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}\right)_{+}.\end{split}

From (
5.3
), (
5.4
), and (
5.5
), we see that

(5.6)

\left(a_{n}-\frac{7\varepsilon}{16}\right)_{+}\precsim_{A}\left(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k+1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}\right)_{+}.

Moreover, the first inequality in (
5.2
) implies that

\psi(b)\otimes 1_{k}\precsim_{(A\otimes K)_{\infty}}\left((\psi\circ\varphi)(a)-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}.

Choose
c\in(A\otimes K)_{\infty}
such that

\left\|c^{*}\left((\psi\circ\varphi)(a)-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}c-\psi(b)\otimes 1_{k}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}.

Let
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a representative sequence for
c
, and let
N_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
be such that, for all

,

(5.7)

\left\|c_{n}^{*}\left(a_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}c_{n}-b_{n}\otimes 1_{k}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}.

Then, for all

(5.8)

\left(b_{n}\otimes 1_{k}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}\right)_{+}\precsim_{A}\left(a_{n}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}.

Finally, let
l:=\max\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\}
. Using the first part of (
5.1
) together with (

), we obtain

k\left\langle\left(b_{l}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq\left\langle\left(a_{l}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq\left\langle a\right\rangle_{A},

and by second part of (
5.2
) together with (
5.6
), we obtain

\left\langle(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq\left\langle\left(a_{l}-\frac{7\varepsilon}{16}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A}\leq(k+1)\left\langle\left(b_{l}-\frac{\varepsilon}{32M}\right)_{+}\right\rangle_{A},

which gives
0
-almost divisibility of
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
.
∎

6.
Pureness of Crossed Products by Compact Group
Actions with the Tracial Rokhlin Property

In this section, we prove that pureness passes to crossed products
by actions of compact groups which have
the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison,
in the sense of Definition 2.1 of
[
28
]
.
We give independent results for comparison and almost divisibility.
We use
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
in the proofs since the theorems we cite
are stated for
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
. The third and fourth author in
[
28
]
defined the (restricted) tracial Rokhlin property with comparison for compact group actions on unital simple C*-algebras. Examples that satisfy this definition are in
[
29
]
. We begin with the following definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple unital C*-algebra,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be
an action of a second countable compact group
G
on
A
.
The action
\alpha
has the

restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison

if for every finite set
F\subseteq A
,
every finite set
S\subseteq C(G)
,
every
\varepsilon>0
, every
x\in A_{+}\setminus\{0\}
,
and every
y\in(A^{\alpha})_{+}\setminus\{0\}
,
there exist a projection
p\in A^{\alpha}
and a
unital completely positive map

\varphi\colon(C(G),\mathtt{Lt})\to(pAp,\alpha)

such that the following hold.

(1)

\varphi
is an
(S,F,\varepsilon)
-approximately equivariant
central multiplicative map (Definition 1.5 of
[
28
]
).

(2)

1-p\precsim_{A}x
.

(3)

1-p\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}y
.

(4)

1-p\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}p
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A
be a stably finite simple separable infinite dimensional
unital C*-algebra,
let
G
be a second countable compact group, and let

\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action
which has the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose
A
has
m
-comparison.
Then
A^{\alpha}
has
m
-comparison.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof that
m
-comparison passes from
a simple C*-algebra to a large subalgebra, part of Theorem
3.5
.
By Proposition
2.15
, it suffices to prove

m
-comparison in
{\operatorname{W}}(A^{\alpha})
.

Let
x,a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))_{+}
satisfy

\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Without loss of generality
x\neq 0
.
By the definition of
<_{\operatorname{s}}
(Definition
2.3
),
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
there is
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

such that
(k_{j}+1)\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.
In particular,
a_{j}\neq 0
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.

First assume
\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
is not the class of a projection.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
We will find
z\in(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))_{+}

and, for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
we will find
b_{j}\in(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))_{+}

such that
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)
,

(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}z
,
and, for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
\langle z\rangle_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}
,

0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
, and
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.

Choose
\rho\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(x)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing
.
Choose
\lambda\in{\operatorname{sp}}(x)\cap(\rho,\varepsilon)
.
Choose a continuous function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}(f)\subseteq(\rho,\varepsilon)
and
f(\lambda)\neq 0
.
The algebra
A^{\alpha}
is simple
by Theorem 3.1 of
[
28
]
and not of Type I
by Proposition 3.2 of
[
28
]
.
Therefore Lemma
2.12
provides

c\in\bigl({\overline{f(x)(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))f(x)}}\bigr)_{+}

such that
{\operatorname{sp}}(c)=[0,1]
.
Set
z=(x-\varepsilon)_{+}+c
.
Then
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)
and

(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}z\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}x
.

Define

I=\bigl\{j\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}\colon{\mbox{$\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}$
is the class of a nonzero projection}}\bigr\}.

For
j\not\in I
, set
b_{j}=a_{j}
.
Then
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
.
Also,

\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(k_{j}+1)\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

The second part says that
\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.

Now suppose
j\in I
.
We may as well assume that
a_{j}
is a projection.
Use Lemma
2.14
to choose

b_{j},s_{j}\in(a_{j}(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))a_{j})_{+}
and
\delta\in(0,1)

such that

{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})={\operatorname{sp}}(s_{j})=[0,1],\qquad b_{j}\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}a_{j},

a_{j}\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}(b_{j}-\delta)_{+}\oplus(s_{j}-\delta)_{+},\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 2k_{j}\langle s_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle(b_{j}-\delta)_{+}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

Then

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq 2k_{j}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}+2k_{j}\langle s_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq(2k_{j}+1)\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Thus, here too
\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
,

0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
,
and
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A}
because
\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.

Passing to
A
, we have
\langle z\rangle_{A}<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Since
A
has
m
-comparison, it follows that

\langle z\rangle_{A}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A}
.
Since
0
is a limit point of
{\operatorname{sp}}(z)

and of
{\operatorname{sp}}(b_{j})
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
,
Proposition 4.10 of
[
28
]
implies that

\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.
Therefore

\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle z\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

We have proved that for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\langle(x-\varepsilon)_{+}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

So
\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.
This completes the case that
\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
is not the class of a projection.

Now assume that
\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
is the class of a nonzero
projection in
M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha})
. By Theorem 3.1 of

[
28
]
,
A^{\alpha}
is simple. Now, use Lemma 2.6 of
[
34
]
to choose

b\in(M_{\infty}(A^{\alpha}))_{+}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\langle b\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
for

j=0,1,\ldots,m
.
Set
k=\max(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})
.
By Lemma
2.13
, there exist
\mu,\eta\in{\operatorname{W}}(A^{\alpha})
,
both not the classes of projections,
such that

\mu\leq\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\mu+\eta\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad(2k+2)\eta\leq\langle b\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

For
j=0,1,\ldots,m
, we then have

\begin{split}(2k_{j}+2)(\mu+\eta)&\leq(2k_{j}+2)\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}+(2k_{j}+2)\eta\\
&\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}+\langle b\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq 2k_{j}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}+\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}=(2k_{j}+1)\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Hence
\mu+\eta<_{\operatorname{s}}\langle a_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}
.
Since
\mu+\eta
is not the class of a projection,
the previous case yields the second step in the calculation

\langle x\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}\leq\mu+\eta\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle b_{j}\rangle_{A^{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof that if
A
has

m
-comparison, then so does
A^{\alpha}
.
∎

Proposition 4.19 of
[

says that if

has the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison,

We need a more careful version of a consequence of that statement,

.
Its role in the next proof is to avoid rather awkward notation.
We will not need all parts of the conclusion.
(We give a nearly full list for possible use elsewhere.)
In our application,
x
and
y
will be in
A
and
A^{\alpha}
,
embedded in
M_{N}(A)
and

as the upper left corners.

To prove the full version, including the condition

\|\psi(a)\|>\|a\|-\varepsilon
for all
a\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}

(omitted below), the strategy is as follows.
The proof of Proposition 4.19 of
[

]

gives a map of the form
{\operatorname{id}}_{M_{N}}\otimes\varphi_{0}
.
Use this map in the proof of Theorem 2.14 of
[

.
It is awkward to write a careful proof this way,

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A
be an infinite dimensional simple separable unital C*-algebra,
let
G
be a second countable compact group, and let

\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action which has the
restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison.
Let
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
every compact subset
F_{1}\subseteq M_{N}(A)
,
every compact subset
F_{2}\subseteq M_{N}(A^{\alpha})
,
every
x\in M_{N}(A)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
,
and every
y\in M_{N}(A^{\alpha})_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, there exist
a projection
p_{0}\in A^{\alpha}
and a
unital completely positive contractive map

\psi_{0}\colon A\to p_{0}A^{\alpha}p_{0}
such that,
taking
p=1_{M_{N}}\otimes p_{0}\in M_{N}(A^{\alpha})

and
\psi={\operatorname{id}}_{M_{N}}\otimes\psi_{0}\colon M_{N}(A)\to pM_{N}(A^{\alpha})p
,
the following hold:

(1)

\psi
is an
(n,F_{1}\cup F_{2},\varepsilon)
-approximately
multiplicative map in the sense of Definition 1.5 of
[
28
]
.

(2)

\|pa-ap\|<\varepsilon

for all
a\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}
.

(3)

\|\psi(a)-pap\|<\varepsilon

for all
a\in F_{2}
.

(4)

1_{M_{N}(A)}-p\precsim_{A}x
.

(5)

1_{M_{N}(A)}-p\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}y
.

(6)

1_{M_{N}(A)}-p\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}p
.

Proof.

We will systematically write elements

as matrices
b=(b_{j,k})_{j,k=1}^{N}
.
We also use standard matrix unit notation, so that

(b_{j,k})_{j,k=1}^{N}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}e_{j,k}\otimes b_{j,k}
.
Define compact subsets
S_{1}\subseteq A
and
S_{2}\subseteq A^{\alpha}
by

S_{\nu}=\bigl\{a_{j,k}\colon{\mbox{$a\in F_{\nu}$ and $j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}$}}\bigr\}

for
\nu=1,2
.
Following the argument in the proof of
Proposition 4.19 of
[
28
]
, use Lemma 3.8 of
[
33
]
,
then Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 of
[
28
]

and Lemma 2.4 of
[
34
]
,
to get
x_{0}\in A_{+}\setminus\{0\}

and
y_{0}\in(A^{\alpha})_{+}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(6.1)

1_{M_{n}}\otimes x_{0}\precsim_{A}x\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 1_{M_{n}}\otimes y_{0}\precsim_{A^{\alpha}}y.

Further set

\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon}{N^{n+1}+1}.

Apply Theorem 2.14 of
[
28
]

with
\varepsilon_{0}
in place of
\varepsilon
,
with
n
as given,
with
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in place of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
,
and with
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
in place of
x
and
y
.
Let
p_{0}\in A^{\alpha}
and
\psi_{0}\colon A\to p_{0}A^{\alpha}p_{0}

be the resulting projection and
(n,S_{1}\cup S_{2},\varepsilon_{0})
-approximately
multiplicative unital completely positive contractive map.
Define
p=1_{M_{N}}\otimes p_{0}
and
\psi={\operatorname{id}}_{M_{N}}\otimes\psi_{0}
.

We verify the conclusions of the lemma.
We start with (
1
).
An argument by induction on
m
shows that every
product
b=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{m}

with
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}
has the form

(b_{j,k})_{j,k=1}^{N}
in which, for
j,k=1,2,\ldots,N
,
the element
b_{j,k}
is a sum of
N^{m-1}
products
of length
m
of elements of
S_{1}\cup S_{2}
.
The matrix entries of
c=\psi(a_{1})\psi(a_{2})\cdots\psi(a_{m})

are sums of the same products, except that

\psi_{0}(z)
.
When

\begin{split}\|(\psi(b)-c)_{j,k}\|&=\|\psi_{0}(b_{j,k})-c_{j,k}\|\\
&\leq N^{m-1}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}\in S_{1}\cup S_{2}}\bigl\|\psi_{0}(z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{m})-\psi_{0}(z_{1})\psi_{0}(z_{2})\cdots\psi_{0}(z_{m})\bigr\|\\
&\leq N^{m-1}\varepsilon_{0}.\end{split}

Therefore

\|\psi(b)-c\|\leq N^{2}\cdot N^{m-1}\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon.

This proves (

For (

For (
4
), we have, using (

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
A
be a simple unital separable infinite dimensional stably finite
C*-algebra.
Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of a second countable
compact group which has the tracial Rokhlin property with comparison
but does not have the Rokhlin property.
If
A
is pure, then the crossed product

C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)
is pure.

If
G
is a compact Lie group, and
\alpha
has the Rokhlin property,
then
C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)
is pure by
Theorem
5.8
.

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

It follows from Proposition
6.4
,
Proposition
6.2
,
and Theorem

that

is pure.
Now, Corollary 3.9 of
[
28
]
implies that

is strongly
Morita equivalent to
A^{\alpha}
. Therefore

C^{*}\bigl(G,\,A,\,\alpha\bigr)
is pure.
∎

\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}\bigl(C(X)\otimes C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{\infty})\bigr)

be the tensor product of the automorphism

f\mapsto f\circ h^{-1}
of
C(X)
and
\sigma
.
Then

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X)\otimes C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{\infty}),\,\alpha\bigr)

is simple by Proposition 1.6 of
[
6
]

and
D
is pure by Proposition
7.3
,
so the crossed product is pure by Theorem
4.6

and has stable rank one by Corollary
4.7
.

If
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},X,h)
has real rank zero,
for example if
h
is an irrational rotation, then

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X)\otimes C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{\infty}),\,\alpha\bigr)

has real rank zero by Corollary
4.13
.

The next several examples give actions which can be viewed
as various forms of noncommutative Furstenberg transformations.
They are more natural versions of this idea than the
actions in Examples 6.8, 6.9 and 6.12 of
[
6
]
.

Example 7.8
.

Fix any
n\in\left\{2,3,\ldots,\infty\right\}
and take

D=C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{n})
.
Adopt Notation
7.1
.
Take
X=S^{1}
, let

\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
h\colon X\to X
be the
irrational rotation
\zeta\mapsto e^{2\pi i\theta}\zeta
.
Following Notation
7.1
for the
generators of
D
, for
\zeta\in X
let

\alpha_{\zeta}\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(D)
be determined by

\alpha_{\zeta}(u_{k})=\zeta u_{k}
for

k=1,2,\ldots,n
(or
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
if
n=\infty
).
Apply Lemma
4.2
to get an action

\alpha\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\operatorname{Aut}}\big(C(X,D)\big)
.
The algebra

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)

is simple by Proposition 1.6 of
[
6
]
and

D
is pure by Proposition
7.3
,
so the crossed product is pure by
Theorem
4.6
and has stable
rank one by Corollary
4.7
.
It is well known that the irrational rotation
algebra
C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},X,h)
has real rank zero.
(The range of the canonical map is the set

\left\{m+n\theta\colon m,n,\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
, which is
dense in
{\mathbb{R}}
since
\theta
is irrational.)
It follows from Corollary
4.13
that

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)
has real rank zero.

Example 7.9
.

Fix
n\in\{2,3,\ldots,\infty\}
and take

D=C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{n})
.
Fix
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(or
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots\in{\mathbb{R}}
)
such that
1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n}

(or
1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots
)
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Take
X=(S^{1})^{n}
, and let
h\colon X\to X
be

h(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\bigl(e^{2\pi i\theta_{1}}\lambda_{1},\,e^{2\pi i\theta_{2}}\lambda_{2},\,\ldots,\,e^{2\pi i\theta_{n}}\lambda_{n}\bigr)

for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in S^{1}
,
or, if
n=\infty
,

h(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)=\bigl(e^{2\pi i\theta_{1}}\lambda_{1},\,e^{2\pi i\theta_{2}}\lambda_{2},\,\ldots,\bigr)

for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots\in S^{1}
.
Following
Notation
7.1
for the generators of
D
,
for
\zeta\in X
let
\alpha_{\zeta}\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(D)

(called
\varphi_{\zeta}
in Notation
7.1
) be
determined by
\alpha_{\zeta}(u_{k})=\zeta_{k}u_{k}
for

k=1,2,\ldots,n
(or
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
if
n=\infty
).
Apply Lemma
4.2
to get an action

\alpha\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\operatorname{Aut}}\bigl(C(X,\,C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{n}))\bigr)
.
Then

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,\,C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{n})),\,\alpha\bigr)

is simple, pure, and has stable rank one as in
Example
7.8
.
The transformation group C*-algebra

C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},X,h)
again has real rank zero.
The range of the canonical map

\rho\colon K_{0}(C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},X,h))\to{\operatorname{Aff}}(T(C^{*}({\mathbb{Z}},X,h)))

is the set

\left\{m_{0}+m_{1}\theta_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}\theta_{n}\colon m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

(analogously if
n=\infty
)
which is dense in
{\mathbb{R}}
so long as
\theta_{k}
is irrational
for at least one
k
.
(Linear independence over
{\mathbb{Q}}
of

1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n}
, or of
1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots
,
is needed for minimality, but not here.)
It follows from Corollary
4.13
that

C^{*}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\bigr)
has real rank zero.

Example 7.10
.

Let
X
be any compact metric space, and let
h
be an
arbitrary minimal homeomorphism of
X
.
Following Notation
7.1
for the generators
of
C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{2})
, let

\gamma\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{2}))
be
given by
\gamma(u_{1})=u_{1}
and
\gamma(u_{2})=u_{1}u_{2}
.
Let

\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}\big(C(X)\otimes C^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(F_{2})\big)

be the tensor product of the automorphism

f\mapsto f\circ h^{-1}
of
C(X)
and
\gamma
.
The algebra

C^{*}\big({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\big)

is simple by Proposition 1.6 of
[
6
]
and

D
is pure by Proposition
7.3
,
so
C^{*}\big({\mathbb{Z}},\,C(X,D),\,\alpha\big)
is pure by
Theorem
4.6
and has stable rank
one by Corollary
4.7
.

The mapping
u_{1}\mapsto u_{1}
and
u_{2}\mapsto u_{1}u_{2}

really is an automorphism of
F_{2}
, because it has the
inverse
u_{1}\mapsto u_{1}
and
u_{2}\mapsto u_{1}^{-1}u_{2}
.
