Maxfaces with infinitely many Swallowtails
Anu Dhochak

Abstract
. In this article, we discuss the existence of a 1-parameter infinite genus family of maxfaces having infinitely many planar (spacelike) ends and infinitely many swallowtails. In particular, we show the existence of : (1) a period-2 family of maxface with infinitely many planar ends and alternating singularity types—every odd-layer neck has exactly four swallowtails, while each even-layer neck is almost conical, for n = 2 n=2 the fundamental piece is a genus-1 Wei-type maxface; (2) a period-3 family where every neck carries four swallowtails (24 per period); (3) a period-2 family of maxface with an almost conical singularity on every neck. All families of maxfaces are embedded in a wider sense.

1.
Introduction

Maximal surfaces are zero–mean–curvature spacelike immersions in the Lorentz–Minkowski space
\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}
. Like their Euclidean minimal counterparts, they arise as critical points of the area functional and admit Weierstrass–Enneper representations. In sharp contrast with the abundance of complete minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the only complete maximal immersion in
\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}
is the plane
[
9
]
.

To overcome this rigidity, one allows admissible singularities and works with
maximal maps
and, more specifically,
maxfaces
in the sense of Umehara–Yamada
[
9
]
. Complete non–planar maxfaces necessarily have a compact singular set, and embeddedness has to be understood in the wider sense (embedded outside a compact set); we adopt this convention throughout (cf.
[
9
,
3
]
).

Classical and recent examples already show a rich landscape: the Lorentzian catenoid and the Kim–Yang toroidal example
[
3
]
; the families in
[
2
]
; and related constructions of complete maxfaces with few ends. Nevertheless, higher–genus maxfaces in the literature typically had only two or three (catenoidal) ends, and very few are embedded in the wider sense.

Recently, Chen-Dhochak-Kumar-Mohanty
[
1
]
, adapted Traizet’s
node–opening
technique
[
7
,
6
]
, Weierstrass gluing framework to the Lorentzian setting and produced many arbitrary finite genus families of maxfaces with many spacelike ends, including Lorentzian Costa and Lorentzian Costa–Hoffman–Meeks type examples. Their work demonstrated that the balance/non-degeneracy conditions for the node–opening technique coincide with those in the minimal case.

The present article pushes the node–opening method beyond the finite–genus regime under natural boundedness and balancing hypotheses (in the spirit of Morabito–Traizet’s infinite–genus minimal surfaces
[
5
]
), and in Section
3
, we give many examples of infinite genus maxfaces. From
[
1
]
, it is clear that
infinite
node–opening scheme will work verbatim to construct a real
1
–parameter family of complete maxfaces that are embedded (in the wider sense), have
infinite genus
, and carry
infinitely many
spacelike planar ends. Since the calculations follow the same pattern, we do not explicitly rewrite the proofs. However, the final results are interesting; we don’t see it in the literature, so in section
2
we state the existence results with the necessary modifications required.

On these new infinite genus maxfaces, as in
[
1
]
, the singular set consists of curves located along the waist of each neck. To determine the nature of singularities, in
[
1
]
, the authors introduced the functions
R_{l,k}^{(r)}(\theta)
and analyzed the behavior of this function. Here, in our infinite-genus case, due to slight modifications in the node–opening process, we have to modify the functions to
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)
accordingly. In Subsection
2.1
, we calculate it and recall all related singularities as in
[
1
]
.

We see many interesting behaviors of singularities in the infinite genus case in compare to the finite genus case as in
[
1
]
, in particular, in
[
1
]
, the singularities were analysed when for some
r>0
,
R_{l,k}^{(r)}(\theta)\not\equiv 0
with
\theta
identifying a singularity. In contrast, in Proposition
3.1
, we show that
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)\equiv 0
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
for certain configurations.

We introduce the notion of an
almost–conical
singularity, defined by the vanishing
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)\equiv 0
for all
r
. This helps us single out the non-collapsed, highly isotropic case where the waist (singular curve) stays for
t>0
but the
\theta
-dependence disappears at
every
order in
t
. In simple terms, the singularity behaves like a cone to infinite order, without creating a true cone tip. This notion is natural for our periodic and quasi-periodic families, where such waists appear. We will see such examples in Section
3
.

We summarize the main findings of this article. We give explicit constructions of periodic/quasi-periodic maxfaces with infinitely many planar ends with a mix of singularities. More precisely, we have

(1)

Period-
2
family with alternating swallowtails and almost conical singularities-
We give a family of complete, embedded (in the wider sense) maxfaces with infinitely many planar ends. In this family, every neck in an odd-numbered layer produces exactly four swallowtails, while each neck in an even-numbered layer is almost conical. For
n=2
, the fundamental piece is a
genus-
1
Wei-type
maxface.

(2)

Period-
3
family with four swallowtails on each neck-
We obtain a complete, embedded (in the wider sense), even genus family of maxfaces of period
3
. A direct computation shows that each of the six necks in one period carries four swallowtails, i.e.,
6\times 4=24
swallowtails per period, hence
infinitely many
on the whole surface.

(3)

Every neck with an almost conical singularity-
It is a family of zero genus maxafces where every layer contains only one neck with
an almost conical
singularity. Geometrically, the picture strongly resembles the classical
Riemann-type
maximal surfaces
[
4
]
, now in a periodic setting.

2.
Maxface: existence and singularity part

We first recall the classical Weierstrass representation for maxfaces
[
9
]
. Let
M
be a Riemann surface (possibly punctured at the ends), let
g
be a meromorphic function on
M
, and let
dh
be a holomorphic
1
-form on
M
satisfying:

•

Divisor condition:
Away from the punctures, we must have

(g)_{0}-(g)_{\infty}=(dh)_{0}.

•

Period Condition:
For all closed curves
C
on
M
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{C}g^{-1}dh+\overline{\int_{C}gdh}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\int_{C}dh

\displaystyle=0.

•

Regularity condition:

|g|
is not identically
1
.

Then
X:M\to\mathbb{E}_{1}^{3}
defined by

(2.3)

M\ni z\mapsto\operatorname{Re}\int^{z}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(g^{-1}+g),\frac{i}{2}(g^{-1}-g),1\Big)dh\in\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1},

gives a maxafce. Here
(M,\,g,\,dh)
is called the Weierstrass data of the maxface. Conversely, for every maxface there exists a meromorphic function
g
and a holomorphic 1-form
dh
such that (
2.3
) represents it.

For our construction of an infinite genus family of maxfaces, we adopt the infinite configuration terminology of Morabito–Traizet
[
5
]
and solve the period problem for the configuration. We now recall the infinite configuration in the maxface settings.

Configurations and Forces:

Let
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of positive integers, and let
(p_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
be a sequence of complex numbers. We refer to the sequence
(p_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
as a
configuration
of type
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Define
c_{k}=\frac{1}{n_{k}}
. For each
(k,i)
, we define the
force

F_{k,i}:=\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n_{k}}\frac{2c_{k}^{2}}{p_{k,i}-p_{k,j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k+1}}\frac{c_{k}c_{k+1}}{p_{k,i}-p_{k+1,j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{c_{k}c_{k-1}}{p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,j}},

and assume that all the points
p_{k,\,i}
and
p_{k\pm 1,\,j}
are pairwise distinct for each
k
.

Following Morabito–Traizet
[
5
]
, we introduce the following change of variables that includes translations and scalar multiplication:

(2.4)

\begin{cases}u_{k,i}:=(-1)^{k}(p_{k,i}-p_{k,1}),&1\leq i\leq n_{k},k\in\mathbb{Z},\\
l_{k}:=(-1)^{k}(p_{k,1}-p_{k-1,1}),&k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

By definition,
u_{k,1}=0
for all
k
. Let
\boldsymbol{U}
denote the infinite sequence

(2.5)

\ldots,\ell_{k},u_{k,2},\dots,u_{k,n_{k}},\ell_{k+1},u_{k+1,2},\ldots,,

which encodes the configuration up to translation and scaling. In terms of the parameter
\boldsymbol{U}
, we rewrite the forces as

F_{k,i}=\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n_{k}}\frac{2(-1)^{k}c_{k}^{2}}{u_{k,i}-u_{k,j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k+1}}\frac{(-1)^{k}c_{k}c_{k+1}}{u_{k,i}+l_{k+1}+u_{k+1,j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{(-1)^{k}c_{k}c_{k-1}}{u_{k,i}+l_{k}+u_{k-1,j}}.

We also define the maps

G_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{c_{k}c_{k-1}}{p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{(-1)^{k}c_{k}c_{k-1}}{u_{k,i}+l_{k}+u_{k-1,j}}.

In terms of
F_{k,i}
and
G_{k}
we say:

Definition 2.1
.

A configuration is said to be
balanced
if the sequence

\tilde{F}:=(...,G_{k},F_{k,2},...,F_{k,n_{k}},G_{k+1},F_{k+1,2},...,F_{k+1,k_{k+1}},...)

is zero, and we call it
non-degenerate
if the differential of
\tilde{F}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{U}
is an isomorphism from
l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
to itself.

In terms of configuration, our existence result is as follows

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider a balanced, non-degenerate configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
of type
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(1)

the sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is bounded,

(2)

the sequence
\boldsymbol{U}
takes a finite number of values,

(3)

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}p_{k,i}\neq\frac{1}{n_{k-1}}p_{k-1,i}.

Then there exists a 1-parameter family
(M_{t})_{0<t<\epsilon}
of complete, embedded (in a wider sense) maxfaces with infinitely many planar ends.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation of
[
5
]
and was verified for the finite configuration by Chen, Dhochak, Kumar, and Mohanty in
[
1
]
. Since it is verbatim, we omit the proof here and outline the Weierstrass data of the constructed surfaces.

Riemann surface:
Consider an infinite chain of Riemann spheres
{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}_{k}},\text{ for }{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. In each copy
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}_{k}
, we fix distinct finite points:

a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,n_{k}}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{k},\qquad b_{k,1},\ldots,b_{k,n_{k}}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{k+1}.

where
{\mathbb{C}}_{k}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}_{k}\setminus{\infty_{k}}
. Identifying
a_{k,i}=b_{k,i}
for each

gives a nodal curve
\Sigma_{0}
. We then apply the classical node-opening construction
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
, which we outline below.

Define a meromorphic function on each copy of Riemann sphere
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}_{k}}={\mathbb{C}}_{k}\cup\{\infty_{k}\}
by

(2.6)

g_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{\beta_{k-1,i}}{z-b_{k-1,i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\frac{\alpha_{k,i}}{z-a_{k,i}},

where the new parameters
\alpha:=(\alpha_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}\text{ and }\beta:=(\beta_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
are complex numbers.

Moreover, for each parameter we define initial value parameters
a^{0},b^{0},\alpha^{0},\text{ and }\beta^{0}
such that all parameters vary in a small neighbourhood of their initial value parameter. We also assume that all initial value parameter sequences

follow finiteness hypotheses, i.e., the set
\{u^{0}_{k,i}\}
is finite. In terms of the configuration data, define:

a^{0}_{k,i}=(-1)^{k}\text{conj}^{k}(l^{0}_{k}+u^{0}_{k,i}),\quad\text{ and }\quad b^{0}_{k,i}=(-1)^{k}\text{conj}^{k+1}(u^{0}_{k,i}).

Thus, in each copy of a complex plane for all
1\leq i\leq n_{k}
, and
1\leq j\leq n_{k-1}
, the central values corresponding to the parameters
a
and
b
are distinct. We take variable
\alpha
and
\beta
those satisfying

\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\alpha_{k,i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k,i}=1,

with the central values

\alpha^{0}=(\alpha^{0}_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}=\beta^{0}=(\beta^{0}_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}=\frac{1}{n_{k}}.

Define local coordinates around the points
a_{k,i}
and

by
v_{k,i}=\frac{1}{g_{k}}
and
w_{k,i}=\frac{1}{g_{k+1}}

and

respectively, and identify the annular regions

t^{2}/\rho<|v_{k,i}|<\rho,\qquad t^{2}/\varepsilon<|w_{k,i}|<\rho,

by

v_{k,i}(z)w_{k,i}(z^{\prime})=t^{2}.

Geometrically, this creates a neck connecting
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}_{k}
and
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}_{k+1}
. The resulting Riemann surface, we denote by
\Sigma_{t}
.

Gauss map:

The Gauss map

is given by

g(z):=\begin{cases}tg_{k}(z)&\text{if }z\in\overline{{\mathbb{C}}_{k}}\text{ and $k$ is even,}\\
1/(tg_{k}(z))&\text{if }z\in\overline{{\mathbb{C}}_{k}}\text{ and $k$ is odd.}\end{cases}

The height differential:
Define a holomorphic form
\omega

2.1.
About Singularities

The singular set of a maxface is characterized by the condition
|g|=1
. In our family of maxfaces, the singular set is given by the union of connected curves represented by

\mathcal{S}_{k,i}\;:=\;\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{k}:|v_{k,i}(z)|=t\}\;=\;\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{k+1}:|w_{k,i}(z)|=t\},

where
v_{k,i}
and
w_{k,i}
are the local coordinates on the two halves of the neck. Thus geometrically, each singular point of a maxface lies on the “waist” of some neck.

The key tool used in
[
1
]
to analyze singularities is a family of real-valued functions
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)
depending on an index
r\in\mathbb{N}
and a neck labeled by
(k,i)
. The main difference between the finite (as in
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
) and the infinite configurations
[
5
]
is the location of the node. In
[
1
]
, given a finite configuration
(p_{i})
, the Riemann surface was obtained by removing the node at the points
p_{i}
, but in the infinite dimensional case, we remove the nodes at
a^{0}_{k,i}
and
b^{0}_{k,i}
, which are related to the configuration
(p_{k,i})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
as follows:

(2.7)

a^{0}_{k,i}=\mathrm{conj}^{k}(p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,1})\,\text{ and }\,b^{0}_{k,i}=\mathrm{conj}^{k+1}(p_{k,i}-p_{k,1}).

Consequently, the expression of
R^{(r)}_{l,k}
in
[
1
]
will change, and we write it here with proper modification. In terms of the configuration
(p_{k,i})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
it is given by

(2.8)

R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Im}\!\Bigl(e^{(r+1)i\theta}\overline{\mathrm{Res}_{p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}}\;-\;e^{-(r+1)i\theta}\mathrm{Res}_{p_{k,i}-p_{k,1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k+1}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}\Bigr),&k\text{ odd},\\[6.0pt]
\mathrm{Im}\!\Bigl(e^{(r+1)i\theta}\mathrm{Res}_{p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}\;-\;e^{-(r+1)i\theta}\overline{\mathrm{Res}_{p_{k,i}-p_{k,1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k+1}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}}\Bigr),&k\text{ even},\end{cases}

where

(2.9)

\omega_{k}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k-1}}\frac{c_{k-1}}{z-(p_{k-1,i}-p_{k-1,1})}\;-\;\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\frac{c_{k}}{z-(p_{k,i}-p_{k-1,1})}.

With these functions
R_{k,i}^{(r)}(\theta)
, the proof of results in
[
1
, Section 2.2]
is verbatim. We write the required points with proper modification for our setup (infinite genus).

Theorem 2.3
.

For all sufficiently small nonzero
t
, the singular set of
M_{t}
consists of infinite disjoint closed curves, one for each neck
(k,i)
. Moreover:

(1)

All singular points are nondegenerate (i.e. fronts, no cross‐caps).

(2)

If
R^{(1)}_{k,i}(\theta)\not\equiv 0
at
t=0
, then each neck
(k,i)
carries exactly four swallowtail singularities.

(3)

If a neck is fixed by a vertical reflection, the fixed singular point is non‐cuspidal (a swallowtail or higher
A_{2m+1}
); if fixed by a horizontal reflection, the entire waist collapses to a cone singularity.

The preceding theorem describes the generic situation, namely that along each neck one obtains a closed singular curve and, unless a symmetry forces a collapse, the function
R^{(1)}_{k,i}(\theta)
detects four swallowtails. However, in the infinite–genus/node–opening situation that we are using, there are natural configurations (see in particular the periodic and quasi–periodic ones constructed later) for which
all
the higher–order quantities
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)
vanish simultaneously for a given neck for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\theta
. In such a case, the singular curve is still present for
t>0
, but the usual criterion “first nonzero
R^{(r)}_{k,i}
determines the type” no longer distinguishes the singularity. To single out precisely this highly isotropic, non-collapsing regime—visible only in the infinite concatenations—we introduce the following notion.

Definition 2.4

(Almost Conical singularity)
.

Each point
\theta
of a connected singular curve around the waist of a neck is called an almost conical singular point if
R^{(r)}_{k,i}(\theta)\equiv 0
for all
\theta
and for all
r
.

Geometrically, for
t>0
we still get a small closed curve as the singular set, but its profile does not depend on
\theta
even to higher orders in
t
. Thus, the singularity looks “cone–like” in the whole Taylor expansion, but the waist actually stays of positive size and does
not
collapse.

3.
Periodic Maxface with infinitely many swallowtails and almost conical singularities

In this section, we give examples of maxfaces having infinite genus, infinitely many swallowtails, and other singularities. These are constructed by concatenating a finite balanced and non-degenerate configuration into an infinite configuration.

In
[
5
]
, the authors define periodic and quasi-periodic configurations for a minimal surface. Here we adapt those definitions for the case of maxface and recall them here.

Definition 3.1
.

A configuration of type
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is called periodic if there exists a positive integer
T
such that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}},n_{k+T}=n_{k},l_{k+T}=l_{k}
and
u_{k+T,i}=u_{k,i}
for all
2\leq i\leq n_{k}
. A maxface corresponding to a periodic configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
is said to be a periodic maxface.

Definition 3.2
.

A maxface is said to be quasi-periodic if its configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
is quasi-periodic, i.e., there exists a diverging sequence of integers
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exists an integer
N
such that for
n\geq N
,

n_{k+T_{n}}=n_{k},\quad l_{k+T_{n}}=l_{k}\text{ and }u_{k+T_{n},i}=u_{k,i}\text{ for }2\leq i\leq n_{k}.

Let
h
be a positive integer, call it height, and for this
h
, let
(n_{0},n_{1},...,n_{h})
be a finite sequence of necks such that
n_{0}=n_{h}=1
and let
(p_{k,i})_{0\leq k\leq h,1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
be a finite configuration of height
h
. The corresponding finite parameter sequence
\boldsymbol{U}
, as define in (
2.5
), is given by
(l_{1},u_{1,2},...,u_{1,n_{1}},l_{2},u_{2,2},...,l_{h})
. In the following paragraph, as in
[
5
]
, we define an infinite balanced and non-degenerate configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
of type
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}=(...,n_{h-1},1,n_{1},...,n_{h-1},1,n_{1},...)
by concatenating a finite balanced non-degenerate configuration of height
h
.

Observe that the change of parameters in (
2.4
) follows translations as well as scalar multiplication, so we need to modify the concatenation conditions as in Section 3 of
[
5
]
, so that the end point
p_{h,1}
and the first point
p_{0,1}
coincide during concatenation. Define the configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
of type
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
as:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{for, }m\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq k\leq h,\quad l_{mh+k}:=\begin{cases}l_{k}\quad\text{ if }h\text{ is odd}\,\\
(-1)^{m}l_{k}\quad\text{if }h\text{ is even}\end{cases},

\displaystyle\text{for, }m\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq k\leq h-1,2\leq i\leq n_{h},u_{k,i}:=\begin{cases}u_{k,i}\quad\text{if h is even },\\
(-1)^{m}u_{k,i}\quad\text{if }h\text{ is odd},\end{cases}

which follows
l_{k+T}=l_{k}
and
u_{k+T,i}=u_{k,i}
. The corresponding neck positions
(p_{k,i})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
satisfy
p_{k+T,i}=p_{k,i}+C
for some constant
C\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Similarly, we can concatenate a sequence of different height finite balanced and non-degenerate configurations, yielding a periodic and quasi-periodic family of maxfaces.

3.1.
Period-2 maxfaces with swallowtail and almost conical singular points.

In this subsection we explain a simple height–2 example where the singularities in the two layers behave differently. For every small
t>0
we get a maxface
M_{t}
which is periodic of period
2
. In all odd layers each neck produces exactly four swallowtails, while in all even layers the unique neck has an almost conical singularity.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the height–2 configuration of type
(1,n,1)
given by

p_{0,1}=0,\quad p_{2,1}=2\sqrt{-1},\quad p_{1,j}=\sqrt{-1}+\cot\bigl(\tfrac{j\pi}{n+1}\bigr)\quad(1\leq j\leq n).

Since
u_{1,1}=0
, the corresponding finite configuration
\boldsymbol{U}
is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}

\displaystyle=(l_{1},u_{1,2},\dots,u_{1,n},l_{2})

\displaystyle=\bigl(-(\sqrt{-1}+\cot(\tfrac{\pi}{n+1})),\;\cot(\tfrac{\pi}{n+1})-\cot(\tfrac{2\pi}{n+1}),\;\dots,\;\sqrt{-1}-\cot(\tfrac{\pi}{n+1})\bigr).

It is proved in
[
5
,
8
]
that this configuration is balanced and non–degenerate, and moreover it is unique up to the natural permutation symmetry of the
n
necks.

Using the above concatenation procedure, we extend this to an infinite configuration
(p_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
, where the layers alternate between
n
necks and one neck; that is,
n_{k}=n
for odd
k
and
n_{k}=1
for even
k
. Concretely,

p_{0,1}=0,\qquad(p_{1,i})_{1\leq i\leq n}=\sqrt{-1}+\cot\bigl(\tfrac{i\pi}{n+1}\bigr),\qquad p_{2,1}=2\sqrt{-1},

and inductively

p_{k+2,i}=p_{k,i}+2\sqrt{-1}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence, for each small
t>0
, the maxface given by Theorem
2.2
is
2
–periodic and has infinitely many ends.

When
n=2
, this gives the Lorentzian analogue of a genus–
1
“Wei–type” surface, i.e. a
3
–ended maxface of genus
1
. For general
n
we get higher–genus examples with the same alternating pattern of ends.

From a direct computation one obtains, for odd
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the functions
R_{k,i^{\prime}}^{(1)}(\theta)
from (
2.8
):

R_{k,i^{\prime}}^{(1)}(\theta)\;=\;\frac{6\cos 2\theta}{n\bigl(1+\cot^{2}(\tfrac{i^{\prime}\pi}{n+1})\bigr)^{2}}\,\Bigl(\cot\tfrac{i^{\prime}\pi}{n+1}-R\Bigr)\not\equiv 0,

where

R=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1,\neq i^{\prime}}^{n}\frac{\sin\frac{(j-i^{\prime})\pi}{n+1}}{\sin\frac{i^{\prime}\pi}{n+1}\sin\frac{j\pi}{n+1}}.

For each
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n
, the function
R_{k,i^{\prime}}^{(1)}(\theta)
has exactly four simple zeros in
\theta
, equally spaced. Each zero corresponds to a swallowtail. Hence every neck in an odd layer produces
four
swallowtails.

For the even layers (i.e.
k
even) we have only one neck, and in this case

(3.2)

R_{k,1}^{(r)}(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\Big[e^{(r+1)\sqrt{-1}\theta}\operatorname{Res}_{p_{k,1}-p_{k-1,1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}-e^{-(r+1)\sqrt{-1}\theta}\overline{\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{\omega_{k+1}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}}\Big].

For convenience we denote
\operatorname{Res}_{0}\bigl(\frac{\omega_{1}}{dz}\bigr)^{r+2}
by
R
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
R_{0,1}^{(r)}(\theta)\equiv 0
. In fact
R_{k,1}^{(r)}(\theta)\equiv 0
for all even
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover,
R
is purely imaginary when
r
is even, and
R
is real when
r
is odd.

Proof.

The proof uses the symmetry of the points
(p_{k,i})_{1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
and the periodicity
p_{k+2,i}=p_{k,i}+2\sqrt{-1}
. Since each
p_{k,1}
is a vertical translate of
p_{0,1}
, it is enough to work with
p_{0,1}
. From (
2.9
), for
k=1
,

\omega_{1}\;=\;\frac{c_{0}}{z-(p_{0,1}-p_{0,1})}\;-\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{c_{1}}{z-(p_{1,i}-p_{0,1})},\qquad c_{0}=1,\ c_{1}=\frac{1}{n},\ p_{0,1}=0.

Thus

\displaystyle R=\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{\omega_{1}}{dz}\Big)^{r+2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{z}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(1/n)}{z-p_{1,i}}\Big)^{r+2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{-1}{p_{1,i}}\Big)\frac{1}{1-\frac{z}{p_{1,i}}}\Big)^{r+2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\Big)^{r+2},\quad a_{k}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(p_{1,i})^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{z^{r+2}}+\binom{r+2}{1}\frac{A(z)}{z^{r+1}}+\dots+(A(z))^{r+2}\Big),

where
A(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
. Write
(A(z))^{j}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{j,m}z^{m}
with
c_{j,m}
polynomial in the
a_{k}
’s.

The
z^{-1}
–coefficient is

(3.3)

R=\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\binom{r+2}{j}c_{j,r+1-j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\binom{r+2}{j}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{j}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=r+1-j\end{subarray}}a_{k_{1}}\cdots a_{k_{j}}\Bigg].

A similar formula holds for
\bigl(\frac{\omega_{0}}{dz}\bigr)^{r+2}
, with coefficients
b_{k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(p_{-1,i})^{k+1}}
. By symmetry
b_{k}=\overline{a_{k}}
, so
\operatorname{Res}_{-p_{-1,i}}\bigl(\frac{\omega_{k}}{dz}\bigr)^{r+2}=(-1)^{r+2}\overline{R}
. Plugging this into (
3.2
) for
k=0
gives

R_{0,1}^{(r)}(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\Big[e^{\sqrt{-1}(r+1)\theta}(-1)^{r+2}\bar{R}-e^{-\sqrt{-1}(r+1)\theta}\bar{R}\Big].

To finish, we show
R
is purely imaginary for
r
even and real for
r
odd, exactly as in the original argument, using the pairing

(p_{1,n-j})_{0\leq j\leq\frac{n}{2}}=-\overline{(p_{1,i})_{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}}}

when
n
is even (and the similar statement when
n
is odd). This gives the parity properties of
a_{k}
and hence of
R
; substituting back shows
R_{0,1}^{(r)}(\theta)\equiv 0
for all
r
.
∎

By Definition
2.4
, this shows that all even layers have almost conical singularities, while all odd layers have four swallowtails on each neck. Thus this provides an example with infinitely many swallowtails and infinitely many almost conical singularities.

3.2.
Period-3 maxfaces with swallowtails in every neck.

We now describe a height–3 example in which
every
neck produces swallowtail singularities.

Consider the height–3 configuration of type
(1,2,2,1)
given by

\displaystyle p_{0,1}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad p_{3,1}=3\sqrt{-1},

\displaystyle p_{1,1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\sqrt{-1},\qquad p_{1,2}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\sqrt{-1},

\displaystyle p_{2,1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+2\sqrt{-1},\quad\;p_{2,2}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+2\sqrt{-1}.

With these neck positions the finite configuration is balanced and non–degenerate (as in the previous height–2 case).

By applying the same concatenation procedure as before, we obtain an infinite configuration

\boldsymbol{U}=(\dots,2,1,2,2,1,2,\dots),

i.e. the sequence of numbers of necks is periodic of period
3
: one layer with
2
necks, the next with
1
neck, then again two layers with
2
necks, and so on. More precisely, the parameters satisfy

l_{k+3}=l_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{k+3,i}=u_{k,i}\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z},\;2\leq i\leq n_{k}),

so we get a
3
–periodic balanced and non–degenerate configuration. In particular,

\boldsymbol{U}=(\dots,\,l_{1}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\sqrt{-1},\;u_{1,2}=-\sqrt{2},\;l_{2}=\sqrt{-1},\;u_{2,2}=\sqrt{2},\;l_{3}=-(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{-1}),\;l_{4},\dots),

and hence, by Theorem
2.2
, this produces a complete, embedded (in the wider sense) maxface of even genus, periodic of period
3
. The associated neck positions
(p_{k,i})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq n_{k}}
are exactly the above ones for
k=0,1,2,3
, and for all other layers are determined by the vertical periodicity

p_{k+3,i}=p_{k,i}+3\sqrt{-1}\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z}).

Singularities.

This configuration has a vertical reflection symmetry (the
yz
–plane) which passes through all the necks
(p_{3k,1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. By Proposition 5.4 of
[
1
]
, at the fixed points of this vertical reflection the singularities are automatically non–cuspidal. A direct (but slightly long) computation of the functions
R_{k,i}^{(1)}(\theta)
in (
2.8
) at each neck gives:

\displaystyle R_{0,1}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{6}(1.4\sin 2\theta+2.4\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta),

\displaystyle R_{3,1}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{6}(1.4\sin 2\theta-2.4\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta),

\displaystyle R_{1,1}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}(0.6\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta+0.16\sin 2\theta),

\displaystyle R_{1,2}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3}{8}(0.05\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta-1.2\sin 2\theta),

\displaystyle R_{2,1}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{384}(108.2\sin 2\theta+85.3\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta),

\displaystyle R_{2,2}^{(1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{384}(142.3\sin 2\theta-10.8\sqrt{2}\cos 2\theta).

(Here we are simply writing the coefficients exactly as they come out of the computation.)

Notice that
none
of the above six functions is identically zero. Hence, by Theorem
2.3
, each of the six necks appearing in the first three layers (from
k=0
to
k=3
) has exactly four swallowtail singularities for sufficiently small
t>0
. Thus in one period we see
6\times 4\;=\;24

swallowtail singularities, and all the other points on the singular curves are cuspidal edges. Because the configuration is
3
–periodic, this pattern repeats for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, so the surface has infinitely many swallowtails.

This height–3 construction is useful because it gives a very symmetric maxface of even genus, with infinitely many planar ends, and with the singularities completely under control: every neck produces the “four swallowtails” pattern.

3.3.
Maxfaces with an almost conical singularity in every neck

We also record the opposite extreme. Take any height
h
and consider the configuration

p_{k,1}=ka\qquad(0\leq k\leq h),

with a fixed
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, and of type

(n_{0}=1,\,1,\,\dots,\,1,\,n_{h}=1),

so that each layer has exactly one neck. By (
3.1
), the corresponding sequence
\boldsymbol{U}
satisfies

l_{k+2}=l_{k},

hence it is balanced and non–degenerate and gives a
2
–periodic maxface. For this family we have, for
every
neck and for
every
order
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

R^{(r)}_{k,1}(\theta)\equiv 0\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z}),

so each neck carries an almost conical singularity (in the sense of Definition
2.4
). This family of examples is very close, in its appearance, to the classical Riemann–type maximal surfaces constructed in
[
4
]
.
