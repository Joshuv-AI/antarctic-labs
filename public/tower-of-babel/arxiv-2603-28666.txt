Abstract
We propose that changing-look AGN transitions arise from size variability of the inner ADAF disk. The AGN accretion disk comprises an outer thin disk and an inner thick ADAF component whose size is intrinsically unstable and evolves with time. The size variations of ADAF are accompanied by changes in accretion rate and outflow quantity, with the latter governing line-of-sight absorption. From this variable ADAF perspective, changing-state and changing-observation AGN represent two faces of the same coin. We further discuss gigahertz-peaked and compact steep-spectrum radio sources as potential manifestations of small-to-intermediate scale ADAFs. Finally, we propose that AGN unification models should incorporate both orientation and ADAF size as key parameters.



1
Introduction

Changing-look active galactic nuclei (CL-AGNs) are a remarkable class of
extragalactic sources that exhibit dramatic spectral transitions between
different AGN types on timescales of months to decades. First identified
in the 1970s with the discovery of Mrk 1018’s transition from Seyfert
1.9 to type 1
(
21
)
, these systems challenge the classical
unification model by demonstrating that AGN classification is not solely
determined by orientation. The transitions manifest as the appearance or
disappearance of broad emission lines—most notably Balmer lines—which
respond to changes in the accretion rate and inner disk geometry

(
19
)
. Two primary mechanisms are invoked to explain these
phenomena: intrinsic changes in the accretion state, where the inner
disk transitions between a standard thin disk and a radiatively inefficient
flow
(
24
)
, and variable obscuration by clumpy material in
the broad line region or torus
(
20
)
. The most compelling case for intrinsic
transitions is 1ES 1927+654, which transformed from a “naked” Seyfert 2
to a broad-line AGN within months in 2017–2018, with newly formed broad
line clouds detected on eccentric orbits
(
22
)
.
Recent systematic surveys have identified hundreds of CL-AGNs, revealing
that such transitions may occur in 10–20% of AGN over cosmic time

(
18
;
2
)
, fundamentally altering our understanding
of black hole growth and AGN duty cycles.

From a theoretical perspective,
26
extended the advection-dominated accretion flow (ADAF;
11
;
12
) to a more general framework. By assuming that the binding energy released in the disk can be temporarily stored in turbulence, an ADAF disk can form without requiring the two-temperature assumption for ions and electrons. This newly developed ADAF disk is dense, geometrically thick, and optically thick, making it applicable to diverse accretion systems including AGNs, X-ray binaries, and young stellar objects. Central funnels are established within the thick ADAF disk, where jets may form and become collimated. In a subsequent paper,
27
proposed that the ADAF disk is intrinsically unstable; its radial extent may vary due to instabilities. This variable ADAF disk can explain numerous observed behaviors in black hole X-ray binaries, including: (i) the X-ray burst light curves and the q-shaped tracks in hardness–intensity diagrams, both related to changes in ADAF size; (ii) the frequencies of quasi-periodic oscillations, tied to the ADAF scale; (iii) the hard X-ray power-law spectrum, connected to the Kolmogorov spectrum of turbulence; and (iv) variations in accretion rate, linked to ADAF size. The variability of the ADAF disk can also account for many observed phenomena in changing-look AGNs, as discussed in Section 2. Conclusions and further discussions are presented in Section 3.

2
Model description and comparison with observations

The formation of a collimated jet requires a large, thick ADAF disk, enabling deep funnels to develop around the central black hole in AGNs
(
26
)
. When the ADAF is small—for instance, during its initial formation from a thin disk or following shrinkage from a larger state—the central funnel becomes shallow with a wide opening angle, and the horizontal pressure gradient available to accelerate gas blobs weakens proportionally with disk thickness. Consequently, accelerated blobs emerging from such shallow funnels produce only wide-angle, V-shaped outflows rather than collimated jets. The region above the funnel and accretion disk becomes very cloudy. True jet collimation is achieved only when the ADAF attains a critical size. These jets and their associated shocks contribute some of the observed radio, X-ray and optical emissions.

The accretion rate onto the central black hole depends on the size of the ADAF disk too. As the ADAF expands, the fractional contact area between the ADAF disk and the black hole decreases, while more material is carried away by outflows or jets. As a result, the true accretion rate must decline. Such a decrease in the accretion rate is directly observed in the neutron star system Her X-1, where the anomalous low state corresponds to a reduced accretion rate (
4
;
25
). This anomalous low state is interpreted as a large ADAF size that persists over a long period (
27
). Although Her X-1 is a neutron star system, the same reasoning should apply to black hole systems. The evolution of the outer thin disk is unlikely to be significantly affected by changes in the inner ADAF disk.

The broad emission lines in active galactic nuclei originate from photoionized gas in the broad line region (BLR), which lies at distances of about 10 to 1,000 gravitational radii. In this region, the high-velocity Keplerian motion around the central supermassive black hole causes Doppler broadening of recombination and collisionally excited lines such as H
\alpha
and H
\beta
. The BLR is thought to be located in several types of environments: one is scattered blobs around the central black hole
(
13
;
14
;
15
)
, and another is the inner part of the thin disk
(
8
;
5
;
6
)
. The blobs in the BLR are mainly supplied by outflows from the central funnel or from the ADAF itself.

Here we present some estimations of the ADAF size. The inner radius of the ADAF is approximately the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO), about
3R_{\rm s}=6GM/c^{2}
, where
M
is the mass of the central black hole. The outer radius of the ADAF ranges from the ISCO to about
50R_{\rm s}
. This value is scaled from the black hole XRB GX 339-4, as the truncated thin disk there is measured to extend beyond
27.5R_{\rm s}

(
3
)
; we adopt roughly twice this value as the outer boundary of the large ADAF. The height is difficult to estimate. We may simply assume it to be 5–10 times the radius in the inner region, as inferred from opening angles of about 10–20 degrees (e.g., M87;
9
), and approximately unity (i.e.,
H/R\sim 1
) in the middle part of the ADAF. If no jet is formed, the ADAF is expected to be much smaller and flatter.

With these basic principles established, interpreting the changing-look AGN (CL-AGN) phenomena within the variable ADAF disk scenario
(
27
)
becomes very straightforward.
19
provided an excellent summary of changing-look AGN in his review, and many of the observational facts discussed below are drawn directly from that work.

Assuming the AGN initially resides in a thin-disk state and appears as a Type 1 AGN, certain instabilities trigger the formation of an ADAF disk. This newly formed ADAF is small, with its central funnel widely open. Outflows intensify and cover a large area over the disk, serving two roles: increasing the number of BLR clouds and enhancing the clouds responsible for eclipses.

As the ADAF grows, it may push the thin disk outward, reducing the disk-originating component of the broad-line emission. Additionally, the ADAF, optically thick, may occult the central X-ray source, diminishing the illumination and thereby reducing the total broad-line emission. These processes lead to the AGN becoming a changing-observation AGN (CO-AGN). The growing ADAF also lowers the accretion rate, causing significant variability in X-ray, optical, UV, and infrared luminosities. The broad lines weaken or disappear (turning into Type 2 AGN) due to the occultation of the illuminating X-rays by the expanding ADAF. Collectively, these effects render the source a changing-state AGN (CS-AGN).

CO-AGN and CS-AGN do not necessarily occur simultaneously; typically, the CO-AGN phase precedes the CS-AGN phase as the ADAF grows. From the perspective of the variable ADAF disk scenario, CO-AGN and CS-AGN are simply two sides of the same coin — two accompanying effects of an evolving ADAF disk. When the ADAF expands to a certain size and the central funnel deepens, radio jets may form. The reverse process can also occur, as the ADAF contracts from a large to a small size. Given that the ADAF is not stable, its size may temporarily change in the opposite direction during phases of expansion or contraction.

Some sources, such as SDSS J101152.98+544206.4, even underwent a Type 1
\rightarrow
Type 1.9
\rightarrow
Type 1 cycle within a period of about 20 years
(
10
)
. During the ADAF growth phase, the accretion rate may indeed decrease, or the opposite may occur during the ADAF contraction phase, as suggested by the infrared flux variations in this source. Some sources exhibit radio activity in conjunction with CS-AGN variability. Radio observations of the UV/optical-flaring AGN 1ES 1927+654—corresponding to a phase of decreasing ADAF—revealed a reduction in radio power by a factor of about four, followed by a rebrightening
(
7
;
19
)
. All of these findings are consistent with the variable ADAF disk model.

The peaked-spectrum (PS, some of it called gigahertz-peaked spectrum) and compact steep-spectrum (CSS) sources may also be related to the size of the ADAF. Two excellent review papers exist in this research field
(
17
;
16
)
. The detection of interactions between the radio source and the emission-line gas, the presence of dust in the emission-line regions, and the existence of significant gas columns toward the nuclei in these sources
(
17
)
are all consistent with the small or intermediate ADAF disk scenario discussed earlier.

3
Summary and Discussions

The variable ADAF disk model successfully accounts for a range of observational phenomena in X-ray binary systems
(
26
)
, and appears capable of explaining most CL-AGN phenomena as well. The CL-AGNs exhibit a limited range of ADAF variability compared to black hole XRBs, where cycles of X-ray bursts and decays span the full range of ADAF size variations. In this framework, CO-AGN and CS-AGN represent two manifestations of the same CL-AGN phenomena, driven by variability in the ADAF size. PS and CSS radio sources are considered as young AGNs or transient sources
(
17
)
, or interpreted as small to intermediate sized ADAF. Together, PS, CSS, and CL-AGN may introduce a temporal dimension to the existing orientation-based AGN unification model
(
1
;
23
)
, offering a more comprehensive picture of AGN.

While the variable ADAF disk model appears successful in explaining phenomena in both X-ray binaries and CL-AGNs, the trigger for ADAF variability remains poorly understood. The range of sizes that an ADAF can attain or vary across is also unclear. The critical ADAF size required to transform the outflow into a jet still requires investigation. The relative size of the ADAF is a key parameter governing jet formation, accretion rate, and outflows. Further theoretical and observational studies are urgently needed.
