HIGHER REGULARITY OF SOLUTIONS OF AN ITERATIVE FUNCTIONAL EQUATION
Liang Feng, Xiao Tang

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the existence of C n C^{n} , n ∈ ℕ + n\in\mathbb{N}^{+} , solutions for a class of second-order iterative functional equations involving iterates of the unknown function and a nonlinear term. Applying the Fiber Contraction Theorem and Faà di Bruno’s Formula, we establish the existence of bounded C n C^{n} solutions with bounded derivatives of order from 1 1 to n n .

39B12, 39B22

1.
Introduction

Iterative functional equations, which involve iterations of unknown functions (
[
2
,
8
]
and references therein), have been widely investigated.
This class includes iterative roots (
[
9
,
10
]
and references therein) and polynomial-like iterative equations (
[
12
,
19
]
and references therein). The fundamental challenge stems from nonlinearity of the iteration operator.

In 2000, N. Brillouët-Belluot (
[
3
]
) first introduced the second-order iterative functional equation

\phi^{2}(x)=\phi(x+a)-x,

(1.1)

motivated by the multi-variable functional equation

x+\phi(y+\phi(x))=y+\phi(x+\phi(y)).

(1.2)

It is easy to see that (
1.2
) with
y=0
is reduced to (
1.1
) with
a=\phi(0)
.
Since the existence of solutions to functional equations must be re‑examined even under small changes, many researchers studied both equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) extensively; see
[
1
,
4
,
7
,
13
,
15
,
18
]
. By
[
5
, Corollary 3.8]
,
[
11
, Theorem 11]
or
[
17
, Theorem 5]
, equation (
1.1
) has no continuous solutions on
\mathbb{R}
when
a=0
.

In 2010, N. Brillouët-Belluot and W. Zhang (
[
4
]
) considered a general version of equation (
1.1
),

\phi^{2}(x)=\lambda\phi(x+a)+\mu x,

(1.3)

where
\lambda,a
and
\mu
are real constants such that
a\lambda\neq 0
.
They investigated the existence of Lipschitz solutions of equation (
1.3
) on any compact interval and gave a construction of piecewise continuous solutions of equation (
1.3
) on a bounded interval. Later, Y. Zeng and W. Zhang (
[
18
]
) proved that equation (
1.3
) has no continuous solutions on
\mathbb{R}
when
\lambda=1
and
\mu\leq-1
. They also provided a sufficient condition for the existence of continuous solutions of equation (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{R}
.

In 2018, X. Tang and W. Zhang (
[
15
]
) generalized equation (
1.3
) to the more general form:

\phi^{2}(x)=h(\phi(f(x)))+g(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.4)

where
h,f
and
g
are given functions, and
\phi
is an unknown one.
They proved the existence of bounded Lipschitz solutions to equation (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{R}
under Lipschitz condition when
g
is bounded while the existence of unbounded Lipschitz solutions to the same equation on
\mathbb{R}
when
g
is unbounded under additional bounded nonlinearity conditions.
Furthermore, without Lipschitz condition, they constructed continuous solutions on
\mathbb{R}
.
In 2024, W. Shi and X. Tang (
[
14
]
) further studied the
C^{1}
solutions of equation (
1.4
) using the Fiber Contraction Theorem.

In this paper, we continue to investigate
C^{n}
,
n\geq 2
being an integer, solutions of equation (
1.4
). Imposing constraints on derivatives of the given functions and applying the Fiber Contraction Theorem (
[
16
]
) presented in Section

Theorem 1
.

Assume that functions
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are of class
C^{n}
such that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h^{\prime}(x)|\geq m,~\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(x)|\geq\alpha,

(1.5)

\max\Big\{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g^{\prime}(x)|,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g^{\prime\prime}(x)|,...,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g^{(n)}(x)|\Big\}~\leq\beta,~\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g(x)|<+\infty,~

(1.6)

where
m>1
,
\alpha>0
are real constants, and
\beta>0
is a sufficiently small real constant.
Furthermore,

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\Big\{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h^{(k)}(x)|,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{(k)}(x)|\Big\}<+\infty.

(1.7)

Then the functional equation (
1.4
)
has a bounded
C^{n}
solution whose the first up to
n
-th derivatives are bounded.

Remark:
In Theorem (
1

solving (
3.6
) and a
\rho_{1}
solving (
3.7

3.8

Lemma 1

( ​
[

)
Let

(y_{0\infty},y_{1\infty},y_{2\infty},...,y_{n\infty})\in Y_{0}\times Y_{1}\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\times Y_{n}.

We define a bundle map

\Gamma:C_{b}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};L)\times C_{b,\rho_{1}}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\dots\times C_{b,\rho_{n}}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow C_{b}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};L)\times C_{b,\rho_{1}}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\dots\times C_{b,\rho_{n}}^{0}(\mathbb{R})

for

where

is defined by

\Lambda(\phi):=h^{-1}\circ(\phi^{2}-g)\circ f^{-1}\qquad\text{~for all ~~}~~\phi\in C_{b}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};L),

(3.3)

and with

\Lambda_{k}(\phi,\phi^{\prime},\dots,\phi^{(k)}):=(\Lambda(\phi))^{(k)}\qquad\text{for each }1\leq k\leq n,

\Psi_{k}(\phi,\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k})
is defined by replacing
\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime},\dots,\phi^{(k)}
with
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...,\phi_{k}
in

(\phi,\phi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\phi_{k})\in C_{b}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};L)\times C_{b,\rho_{1}}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\dots\times C_{b,\rho_{k}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}).

Namely, according to Faà di Bruno’s formula given in Lemma
2
, for each
1\leq k\leq n

is defined by

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(\phi,\phi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\phi_{k}):=\Lambda_{k}(\phi,\phi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\phi_{k})=(h^{-1})^{\prime}\circ(\phi^{2}-g)\circ f^{-1}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\bigg((\phi_{k}\circ\phi\circ f^{-1})\cdot(\phi_{1}\circ f^{-1})^{k}+(\phi_{1}\circ\phi\circ f^{-1})\cdot(\phi_{k}\circ f^{-1})\bigg)\cdot((f^{-1})^{\prime})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad+H_{k}(\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{k-1}),

\displaystyle H_{k}(\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{k-1}):=

\displaystyle(h^{-1})^{\prime}\circ(\phi^{2}-g)\circ f^{-1}\cdot\bigg\{\phi_{1}\circ\phi\circ f^{-1}\cdot\bigg(\sum_{h_{1}+2h_{2}+\dots+kh_{k}=k\atop h_{1}\neq k}\frac{k!}{h_{1}!h_{2}!\dots h_{k}!}\phi_{h_{1}+h_{2}+\dots+h_{k}}\circ

\displaystyle f^{-1}\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}\big(\frac{(f^{-1})^{(m)}}{m!}\big)^{h_{m}}\bigg)-(g\circ f^{-1})^{(k)}+

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1}+2t_{2}+\dots+(k-1)t_{k-1}=k-1}\frac{(k-1)!}{t_{1}!t_{2}!\dots t_{k-1}!(1!)^{t_{1}}(2!)^{t_{2}}\dots{((k-1)!)}^{t_{k-1}}}\phi_{t_{1}+t_{2}+\dots+t_{k-1}}\circ

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}+2l_{2}+\dots+(k-1)l_{k-1}=k-1}\frac{(k-1)!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\dots l_{k-1}!(1!)^{l_{1}}(2!)^{l_{2}}\dots((k-1)!)^{l_{k-1}}}\cdot(h^{-1})^{(l_{1}+l_{2}+\dots+l_{k-1})}\circ

\displaystyle\phi\circ f^{-1}\cdot\prod_{u=1}^{i}\bigg(\sum_{v_{1}+2v_{2}+\dots+uv_{u}=u}\frac{u!}{v_{1}!v_{2}!\dots v_{u}!}\phi_{v_{1}+v_{2}+\dots+v_{u}}\circ f^{-1}\cdot\prod_{w=1}^{u}(\frac{(f^{-1})^{(w)}}{w!})^{v_{w}}\bigg)^{s_{u}}

\displaystyle-(g\circ f^{-1})^{(i)}\bigg\}^{l_{i}}

for all
