Finite-sample bounds for regularized optimal transport
Alberto González-Sanz, Marcel Nutz, Austin J. Stromme

Abstract
We study the sample complexity of regularized optimal transport for general convex regularizations including the Kullback–Leibler divergence and L p L^{p} penalties. Our main results are non-asymptotic bias and variance bounds for the empirical cost, with explicit dependence on the regularization parameter and on the intrinsic dimension of the marginals. Our approach simultaneously improves, unifies, and extends existing finite-sample bounds. In particular, we improve the state of the art for entropic optimal transport, and we obtain the first fully quantitative results for L p L^{p} regularization with 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . For the quadratic transport cost, we deduce that quadratically regularized optimal transport (i.e., L 2 L^{2} regularization) estimates the unregularized optimal transport cost at rate n − 2 / ( d + 4 ) n^{-2/(d+4)} , the fastest non-asymptotic rate currently available for any estimator based on regularized optimal transport.



Keywords
Central Limit Theorem; Optimal Transport; Sparse Regularization; Sample Complexity

Introduction

Optimal transport (OT) is a widely used method for comparing probability distributions (e.g.,
[
49
,
46
,
43
]
). Given probability measures
P
and
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a cost function
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the optimal transport problem is

{\rm OT}:={\rm OT}(P,Q)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(P,Q)}\int c\,d\pi,

where
\Pi(P,Q)
denotes the set of all couplings between
P
and
Q
.
In many applications of OT, the marginals
P
and
Q
are not explicitly known; instead, one has access to i.i.d. samples. Given independent random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
with
X_{i}\sim P
and
Y_{i}\sim Q
, denote the associated empirical measures by
\widehat{P}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
and
\widehat{Q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{Y_{j}}
. Then the empirical OT problem is

\widehat{\rm OT}:={\rm OT}(\widehat{P},\widehat{Q})=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\widehat{P},\widehat{Q})}\int c\,d\pi.

It is well known that OT suffers from the
curse of dimensionality
: in general, the rate at which the empirical cost
\widehat{\rm OT}
converges to its population counterpart
{\rm OT}
is of order
n^{-1/d}

[
15
,
18
]
, limiting the applicability of OT in high-dimensional problems.

Regularization by a strictly convex penalty is a common approach to remedy the curse of dimensionality while also reducing the computational burden of OT. Given a regularization parameter
\varepsilon>0
and a strictly convex function
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, we study the regularized optimal transport (ROT) problem

\displaystyle{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}:={\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}(P,Q)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(P,Q)}\int c\,d\pi+{\varepsilon}D_{\phi}(\pi|P\otimes Q),

(1)

where

D_{\phi}(\pi|P\otimes Q):=\int\phi\biggl(\frac{d\pi}{d(P\otimes Q)}\biggr)d(P\otimes Q),

as well as its empirical counterpart
\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}:={\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}(\widehat{P},\widehat{Q})
. The most popular choice for
\phi
is
\phi_{1}(t):=t\log t
, leading to Kullback–Leibler divergence
D_{\text{KL}}
and the
entropic optimal transport
(EOT) problem
{\rm EOT}:={\rm OT}_{\phi_{1},\varepsilon}
. Indeed, EOT was popularized on computational grounds—the solution to its dual problem can be computed efficiently via Sinkhorn’s algorithm
[
9
]
, which enjoys linear convergence (see
[
19
,
6
,
8
]
, among many others). Starting with
[
22
]
, several works demonstrated that entropic regularization
also evades the
curse of dimensionality (for fixed
\varepsilon>0
). In general, the dual problem of (
1
) is

\displaystyle\sup_{(f,g)\in L^{\infty}(P)\times L^{\infty}(Q)}\Phi_{\phi,\varepsilon}(f,g),

(2)

\Phi_{\phi,\varepsilon}(f,g):=\int\biggl\{f(x)+g(y)-\varepsilon\cdot\psi\biggl(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)\biggr\}d(P\otimes Q)(x,y),

where
\psi(s):=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\phi(t)\}
is the convex
conjugate of
\phi
. For EOT, the conjugate is
\psi(s)=e^{s-1}
, and the smoothness of this dual problem is the basis of
[
22
]
. While the constant in their bound was exponential in
1/\varepsilon
, this was subsequently improved to polynomial for smooth costs by
[
39
]
and to Lipschitz costs by
[
47
]
, whereas
[
44
]
obtained a bound with exponential dependence for bounded measurable cost.

Starting with
[
40
,
4
,
17
,
38
]
, a growing literature studies alternative regularizations, in particular quadratically regularized optimal transport (QOT) which uses the squared
L^{2}
norm
\phi_{2}(t):=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}
(or, equivalently up to a constant, the
\chi^{2}
divergence
\frac{1}{2}(t^{2}-1)
). This literature observed that the resulting transport plans exhibit sparsity, in the sense that the support converges to the support of the unregularized OT plan (which is typically a graph) as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in contrast to the EOT plans which have full support for any
\varepsilon>0
. Sparsity is shared by the
L^{p}
regularizer
\phi_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}
for
p\in(1,\infty)
(or equivalently the
p
-Tsallis divergence), which for
p\in(1,2)
interpolates between EOT and QOT. See also
[
50
,
27
,
42
,
26
]
for recent analytic results on sparsity, and
[
28
,
29
]
for algorithms with linear convergence guarantees.

For such regularizers, the conjugate
is
\psi_{p}(s)=\frac{(s)_{+}^{q}}{q}
if
p>1
, where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
denotes the conjugate exponent and
(s)_{+}=\max(0,s)
the positive part. The
limited smoothness of the resulting dual problem (
2
) might lead one to conjecture that such ROT problems have significantly worse sample complexity than EOT, or even suffer from a direct curse of dimensionality, as suggested by the bounds in
[
2
]
. Recently,
[
30
,
25
]
obtained central limit theorems with the usual rate
\sqrt{n}
for
p\in(1,2]
, giving the first indications that less-smooth penalties still have a strong regularizing effect in terms of sample complexity. Specifically,
[
30
]
covered Lipschitz costs
c
and divergences for which
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
—thus including
p\in[1,2)
, but not
p\geq 2
—and exploited this regularity in a Z-estimation approach. QOT, corresponding to the boundary case of
p=2
where
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
, was covered in
[
25
]
, which restricts to the quadratic cost
c(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{2}
for technical reasons. These central limit theorems imply sample complexity results at rate
n^{-1/2}
, which are however qualitative in that the leading constants are not quantified. The latter is inherent to the proof technique using compactness arguments; in
particular, a direct comparison with the
sample complexity of EOT was not possible. For
p>2
, parametric sample complexity was not known before the present work, regardless of quantification of constants.

For further background on statistical
optimal transport, we refer the reader to the comprehensive surveys
[
13
,
1
,
7
]
.

1.1
Summary of contributions

We develop new approaches to sample complexity which simultaneously improve, unify, and extend the previous results. In particular, we

•

recover and improve the sharpest known sample complexity results for EOT;

•

give the first fully quantitative results for
L^{p}
regularization with
p\in(1,2]
, and in particular for QOT;

•

give the first parametric results for general regularizations with conjugate
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
, including
L^{p}
for arbitrary
p\in(1,\infty)
.

The following paragraphs summarize a selection of the take-aways from our main results in the body of the text; the underlying proof approaches are explained in the subsequent
Section

1.2
. For simplicity of exposition, we focus on the
p
-Tsallis (or
L^{p}
) entropies for
p\in[1,\infty)
, including EOT for
p=1
and QOT for
p=2
, even though some results in the body are more general. Moreover, we may focus on the bias: the mean squared error admits the bias–variance decomposition (
12
) and we will see in
Proposition

3.3
that the variance admits a very general
n^{-1}
rate with coefficient independent of
\varepsilon
and
d
.

Our first main result establishes an
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
rate of the bias (the so-called slow rate) with polynomial dependence on the regularization
1/\varepsilon
. For
p=1
(EOT), this result recovers the best known constant (see
[
47
]
). For
p\in(1,2]
, and in particular for QOT, it is the first parametric sample complexity result with quantification of the constant, and for
p>2
, it is the first parametric rate at all.

Theorem 1.1

(Slow rate)
.

Assume that
P
and
Q
are supported on bounded sets
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
with Minkowski dimensions
{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
and
{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega^{\prime})
(as defined in
Section

3.4
). Assume further that
c
is Lipschitz. Then, for every
\alpha>\min({\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega),{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega^{\prime}))
,

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{\alpha}{2(1+(p-1)\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{n}},\quad\text{for }p\in[1,\infty).

Theorem

1.1
also shows for the first time that the constant for ROT depends only on the
minimum
of the dimensions of
P
and
Q
; for instance, in the extreme case of semi-discrete transport where one marginal is discrete, the dimension disappears completely (
Section

3.3
).
This phenomenon was first identified
for unregularized OT,
where it was called the lower complexity adaptation principle (LCAP)
[
35
]

and then
for the entropically regularized
problem, where it was dubbed minimum intrinsic dimension scaling
[
47
,
34
]
.

Theorem

1.1
shows that
this phenomenon holds for general ROT problems.

A second, more subtle, implication
of
Theorem

1.1
is that the statistical complexity of EOT (
p=1
)
is significantly more sensitive to the dimension than estimators with
L^{p}
penalties for
p>1
. Indeed, the exponent of
1/\varepsilon
is
\alpha/2
for
p=1
, hence linear in the dimension
\alpha
, whereas for fixed
p>1
, the exponent tends to the constant
\frac{1}{2(p-1)}
for large
\alpha
. This might appear counterintuitive at first glance, given that EOT leads to the smoothest plans. The resolution of this paradox lies in the trade-off between statistical complexity and regularization bias (see
Section

3.5
): the meaning of
\varepsilon
is not directly comparable across the problems.

For the first time, our quantitative results enable a more meaningful way of comparing different regularizations by directly evaluating the estimation of the unregularized OT problem. Indeed, tuning
\varepsilon
as a function of
n
,
Theorem

1.1
implies a rate for the approximation of the unregularized OT problem. For instance, for EOT, we recover

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm EOT}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{{\alpha}+2}}\,(\log n)^{\frac{{\alpha}}{{\alpha}+2}},

and for QOT we obtain the comparable rate

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm QOT}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{{\alpha}+2}}.

However, it turns out that, specifically for QOT, this rate can be significantly improved in the key case where
c
is the quadratic cost
c(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{2}
. Here, our result (see
Proposition

3.23
) is

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm QOT}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{2}{d+4}}.

Remarkably, this bound is strictly better than any previously known rate for regularized OT derived from a non-asymptotic bound for general
\varepsilon
—EOT included—and thus positions QOT as a competitive alternative. Moreover, this bound almost achieves
n^{-2/d}
, which is the minimax rate for estimating
{\rm OT}
in this setting (for
d>4
).

To conclude our discussion of the slow rate, we mention a central limit theorem for the empirical cost (
Theorem

3.4
), centered at its population counterpart and with parametric rate
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. It generalizes the central limit theorems of
[
30
,
25
]
for the empirical cost to more general divergences and transport costs, with a completely different proof. In EOT, central limit theorems are well established:
[
39
]
and
[
12
,
24
]
proved central limit theorems for the fluctuations and empirical cost, respectively, assuming smooth transport costs, later generalized to bounded transport costs in
[
31
]
. See also
[
32
,
23
]
for central limit theorems for the empirical plans and potentials,
[
36
]
for multimarginal EOT, and
[
3
]
for the discrete case.

Our second main result establishes an
n^{-1}
rate for the bias, the so-called fast rate, under stronger assumptions on the marginals and for
p\in[1,2]
. This faster rate comes at the expense of a larger constant (compared to
Theorem

1.1
); the constant remains polynomial in
1/\varepsilon
but is now exponentially growing in the dimension even for
p>1
. Our result improves upon the best previous result for EOT; for other regularizations, there is no comparable previous result.

Theorem 1.2

(Fast rate)
.

Assume that
P
and
Q
are supported on bounded convex sets with densities bounded away from zero and infinity. Assume further that
c
is Lipschitz. Then

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-(3d+2+\frac{d}{1+d(p-1)})}}{n},\quad\text{for $p\in[1,2]$}.

For
p=1
, this specializes to

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm EOT}\bigr]-{\rm EOT}\bigr|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-(4d+2)}}{n}

and this leading term is strictly smaller than the
\varepsilon^{-9d-6}
obtained in
[
47
, Theorem 12]
. Moreover, our result allows for the practical case of marginal supports with boundary, whereas
[
47
]
assumes manifolds without boundary. We note that, although

Theorem

1.2
involves the ambient dimension
d
, it is consistent with
the lower complexity adaptation principle because the
measures are indeed supported on
d
-dimensional sets.

As we discuss in more detail below,
the improved rate in
Theorem

1.2

is enabled by a fine analysis of the convergence
of the dual potentials. It shows that the empirical potentials
(\widehat{f},\widehat{g})
converge to the potentials
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
at a parametric rate.

Theorem 1.3

(Rate for potentials)
.

Assume that
P
and
Q
are supported on bounded convex sets with densities bounded away from zero and infinity. Assume further that
c
is Lipschitz. Then

\mathbb{E}\biggl[\bigl\|\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}^{2}\biggr]\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-(6d+4+\frac{d}{1+d(p-1)})}}{n},\quad\text{for $p\in[1,2]$}.

We mention that the convexity assumption on the marginal supports could be relaxed to connected domains with Lipschitz boundary in both
Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
, at the expense of an additional domain-dependent constant. Regarding prior results, the central limit theorems of
[
30
,
25
]
also imply parametric convergence rates for the potentials in their respective settings, but as mentioned above, their techniques do not allow for any quantification of the constant, while that is our main objective
in the current work.

1.2
Proof strategies

The following paragraphs highlight some of the techniques used in our proofs. In view of the bias–variance decomposition (
12
) we start with a general bound (
Proposition

3.1
) controlling the bias in terms of the potentials and the density
\rho_{\varepsilon}
(cf. (
9
)) of the optimal regularized plan
\pi_{\varepsilon}
,

(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}]-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon})^{2}\leq 2\min_{a,b\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|f_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{f}-a\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}+\|g_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g}-b\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}^{2}\Bigr]{\rm Var}_{{P\otimes Q}}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]}{n}.

(3)

The proof is based on the convexity technique introduced in
[
47
]
. However, in contrast to the bound in
[
47
, Eq. (5.4)]
, (
3
) involves a term including the potentials. This term could immediately be replaced by a constant upper bound, but the term itself tends to zero, and this will be fundamental for some of the developments below, including the central limit theorem in
Theorem

3.4
where (
3
) is used to show that the limiting Gaussian variable is centered.

On the other hand, we bound the variance by

{\rm Var}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]\leq\frac{4\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}}{n}

in
Proposition

3.3
. The proof uses a strategy based on the Efron–Stein inequality that was introduced by
[
14
]
in the context of unregularized optimal transport. Note that, in contrast to the variance bound established in
[
47
]
, our bound does not depend on the regularization parameter
\varepsilon
. The same Efron–Stein strategy is also used in the proof of the central limit theorem.

Next, we turn to the fast rate (
Theorem

4.8
) and the convergence of the potentials (
Theorem

4.5
), where the proofs are substantially more involved and arguably the most innovative. We begin by defining the convex function

\Gamma(t)=-\widehat{\Phi}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigl((1-t)({\widehat{f}},{\widehat{g}})+t\,({f}_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})\bigr),\quad t\in[0,1],

where
\widehat{\Phi}_{\phi,\varepsilon}
is the empirical dual objective function. As shown in
Lemma

C.1
, this function admits the second-order Taylor expansion

\Gamma(t)=-\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})drds,

where

\gamma_{r}=\frac{\bigl((1-r){\widehat{f}}+rf_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl((1-r){\widehat{g}}+rg_{\varepsilon}\bigr)-c}{\varepsilon}.

The core of our argument is to establish a coercivity bound of the form

\widehat{\beta}\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})\lesssim\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q}),

(4)

for some
\widehat{\beta}>0
, possibly
random, and for sufficiently small
r
. This is related to an empirical version of a PL inequality for the dual problem. (A deterministic PL inequality was separately presented in
[
29
]
, but we mention that key ideas first arose in the context of the present work.) An inequality related to (
4
) was established in
[
47
]
, but for the semi-dual objective which involves only one of the two potentials. That approach fails for the more general regularizations
considered here, and also leads to slower rates for EOT. In the present work, we develop a finer analysis studying the full dual objective
\Gamma(t)
.

The first step towards (
4
) is to prove that

\widehat{\alpha}\,{\rm Var}_{\widehat{P}}\bigl[\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr]{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}\lesssim\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})

(5)

for a controlled random variable
\widehat{\alpha}>0
, where the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\biggl\{\text{there exists $\hat{T}$ s.t.~$\hat{T}_{\#}P=\widehat{P}$ and $\|\hat{T}-{\rm I}\|_{L^{\infty}(P)}\leq C\varepsilon$}\biggr\}.

To establish (
5
), we first demonstrate in
Lemma

C.3
that for every
u,v
,

\frac{\widehat{\alpha}_{1}}{n^{2}}\sum_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq C\varepsilon}\bigl(u(X_{i})-u(X_{\ell})\bigr)^{2}\lesssim\int\bigl(u\oplus v\bigr)^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q}),

for another controlled random
\widehat{\alpha}_{1}>0
. The left-hand side of this inequality is related to the graph Laplacian of a random geometric graph with edge weights
{\bf 1}_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\varepsilon}
. To bound it from below, we adapt an approach of
[
20
]
which was developed for random geometric graphs with i.i.d. data on manifolds without boundary. Specifically, under the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, we define the isometry

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}:L^{2}(\widehat{P})

\displaystyle\to L^{2}(P)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i}f(X_{i}){\bf 1}_{T^{-1}(X_{i})}=f\circ T,

for
T
pushing
P
forward to
\widehat{P}
. In
Lemma

C.6
we then verify the approximation

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\delta}\bigl(u(X_{i})-u(X_{\ell})\bigr)^{2}\approx\int\int_{\mathbb{B}_{{\delta}}(x)}\bigl((\Pi_{T}u)(x)-(\Pi_{T}u)(z)\bigr)^{2}dP(x)dP(z).

(6)

Lower bounds on the right-hand side are closely related to Poincaré inequalities and connectedness of the support.
In particular, we use
a technical result from
[
29
]
which states
that when
P
has bounded convex support, then for any
h\in L^{2}(P)
and
\delta>0
,

\int\int\bigl(h(x)-h(z)\bigr)^{2}dP(x)dP(z)\lesssim\delta^{-d-2}\int\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)}\bigl(h(x)-h(z)\bigr)^{2}dP(x)dP(z).

(7)

The estimates (
6
) and (
7
), together with the fact that
\Pi_{T}
is an isometry, imply (
5
).

The second step is to derive (
4
) from (
5
) and a spectral analysis of the operator associated with the bilinear form

\bigl((u,v),(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\bigr)\mapsto\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})(u\oplus v)(\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q});

see
Proposition

C.8
. Finally, we show that
\widehat{\beta}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}
is close to its population counterpart (see
\beta_{n,\varepsilon}
in
Theorem

4.5
) with sufficiently high probability. This uses standard arguments of empirical process theory and a control on the
\infty
-Wasserstein distance proved in
[
21
]
.

2
Preliminaries

The following is in force throughout the paper.

Standing assumption 2.1

(Regularization)
.

The function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly convex with
\phi(x)/x\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
.
Furthermore, the conjugate

y\mapsto\psi(y):=\phi^{*}(y):=\sup_{x\geq 0}\{xy-\phi(x)\}

belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies

(i)

\psi^{\prime}(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
;

(ii)

there exist
t_{\psi}>0
and
\delta_{\psi}>0
such that
\psi^{\prime}(t_{\psi})=1
and
\psi
is strictly convex on
[t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi},\infty)
.

Remark 2.2
.

As can be observed directly
from its definition,
\psi
is
non-decreasing, so that

\psi^{\prime}\geq 0
everywhere. Moreover, under Assumption
2.1
, the function
\phi
is differentiable on
(\psi^{\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}),\infty)
, and in particular on
[1,\infty)
, due to the strict convexity of
\psi
on
[t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi},\infty)
. We note that
\phi^{\prime}(1)=t_{\psi}>0
.

For two probability measures
\mu\ll\nu
on the same space, we then define

D_{\phi}(\mu|\nu):=\int\phi\biggl(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\biggr)d\nu,

and we set
D_{\phi}(\mu|\nu):=\infty
if
\mu\not\ll\nu
. We note that
D_{\phi}
is a bona fide divergence only if
\phi(1)=0
, which leads to
D_{\phi}(\nu|\nu)=0
. Changing
\phi
by an additive constant changes
D_{\phi}
and
\psi
only by an additive constant, and that does not materially affect the results in this paper. We did not include a normalization for
\phi
in
2.1
in order to cover both the
L^{p}
and
p
-Tsallis regularizations.

Note that our assumptions on the divergence relax those of
[
30
]
. Indeed, we assume
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
rather than
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and this is necessary to cover the Tsallis entropies for
p\geq 2
. We also impose the growth condition that
\psi^{\prime}(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, instead of the stronger condition that
\psi^{\prime}(x)\geq x
for all sufficiently large
x
.

The following basic result will be used both for the population marginals
(P,Q)
and the empirical marginals
(\widehat{P},\widehat{Q})
. For a function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote
\|h\|_{\infty,\Omega}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|h(x)|
, and we simply write
\|h\|_{\infty}
when the domain is clear from context.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P,Q
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with supports contained in given compact sets
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
. Moreover, let
c:\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and measurable.

(i)

The primal problem (
1
) admits a unique optimizer
\pi\in\Pi(P,Q)
.

(ii)

Strong duality holds, i.e., the value of the primal problem (
1
) equals the value of the dual problem (
2
).

(iii)

The dual problem (
2
) admits a (non-unique) optimizer
(f,g)\in L^{\infty}(P)\times L^{\infty}(Q)
.

(iv)

A pair
(f,g)\in L^{\infty}(P)\times L^{\infty}(Q)
is an optimizer of the dual problem (
2
) if and only if it satisfies the first-order condition

\begin{cases}&\int\psi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{f(\cdot)+g(y)-c(\cdot,y)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)dQ(y)=1\quad P\text{-a.s.},\\[1.00006pt]
&\int\psi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{f(x)+g(\cdot)-c(x,\cdot)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)dP(x)=1\quad Q\text{-a.s.}\end{cases}

(8)

(v)

Any dual optimizer
(f,g)
yields a primal optimizer via the density

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\frac{d\pi}{d(P\otimes Q)}(x,y)=\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\biggr),

(9)

where
\psi^{\prime}
is the derivative of
\psi
.

(vi)

Let
(f,g)\in L^{\infty}(P)\times L^{\infty}(Q)
satisfy (
8
). Then there exists a unique bounded measurable extension
(f,g)
to
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
that is a version of the given equivalence classes and satisfies

\begin{cases}&\int\psi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)dQ(y)=1\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\\[1.00006pt]
&\int\psi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)dP(x)=1\quad\text{for all }y\in\Omega^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(10)

For such a version
(f,g)
, the uniform bound
\|f\oplus g\|_{\infty}\leq 5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)<\infty
holds on
\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}
.
Moreover, if
c(\cdot,y)

admits a uniform modulus of continuity
\omega
uniformly in
y
, then
f
admits
\omega
as well. Similarly for
g
. In particular, if
c\in\mathcal{C}(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime})
admits a uniform modulus of continuity
\omega
, then
f
and
g
both admit
\omega
.

(vii)

If
\spt P
is connected,
c
is continuous,
\spt P=\Omega
and
\spt Q=\Omega^{\prime}
, then the solution of (
10
) is unique up to additive constant: the solution set is
\{(f+a,g-a):a\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where
(f,g)
is as in
(vi)
.

In the following, when referring to potentials
(f,g)
associated with
(P,Q)
, we always choose the version/extension mentioned in (vi), and moreover center
f
w.r.t. the first marginal,

\int fdP=0.

(11)

This uniquely specifies the potentials
when they are determined up to additive constants.

The following consistency result states that
convergence of the marginals
implies convergence of
the associated dual potentials.

Lemma 2.4

(Consistency of potentials)
.

Let
P,Q
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact supports
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
. Assume that
\Omega
is connected and
c\in\mathcal{C}(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime})
. Let
\{P_{n}\}
and
\{Q_{n}\}
be sequences of measures supported in
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
, respectively, converging in distribution to
P
and
Q
, respectively. Let
(f_{n},g_{n})
be potentials associated with
(P_{n},Q_{n})
, and let
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
be the (unique in the sense of
Proposition

2.3
(vii)) potentials associated with
(P,Q)
. Then

\|f_{n}\oplus g_{n}-f_{\varepsilon}\oplus g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty,\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}}\to 0.

Throughout the paper, we will use the following
assumptions.

Standing assumption 2.5

(Bounded supports)
.

The population measures
P
and
Q
have compact supports
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Standing assumption 2.6

(Bounded cost)
.

The cost function
c:\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded and measurable.

We also fix a probability space
(\bm{\Omega},\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
on which the i.i.d. samples
X_{i}\sim P
and
Y_{i}\sim Q
are defined. We assume that both samples are independent. The associated empirical measures are denoted
\widehat{P}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
and
\widehat{Q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{Y_{j}}
. We choose and fix associated dual potentials
(\widehat{f},\widehat{g})
that are
\mathcal{A}
-measurable.

3
Statistical complexity of the cost

In this section we state the main results on the statistical complexity of the cost where the bias has the slow rate
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Proofs are postponed to
Appendix

B
.

3.1
General cost bounds

We start with general (if partly crude) bounds for the two terms in the bias–variance decomposition

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\bigr]=\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}}_{\text{bias}}+\underbrace{{\rm Var}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]}_{\text{variance}}.

(12)

The first result is a parametric rate for the bias term, valid for bounded measurable transport cost
c
and general regularization
\phi
.

Proposition 3.1

(Bias bound)
.

We have

\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\leq 2\min_{a,b\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|f_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{f}-a\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}+\|g_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g}-b\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}^{2}\Bigr]{\rm Var}_{{P\otimes Q}}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]}{n},

where
\rho_{\varepsilon}
denotes the density (
9
) of the optimal plan
\pi_{\varepsilon}
for
(P,Q)
. As a consequence,

\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\leq 8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{{\rm Var}_{{P\otimes Q}}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]}{n}.

The second display is obtained from the first via the bound of
Proposition

2.3
(vi) for the potentials. As mentioned in
Section

1.2
, the first display is significantly stronger since the difference of the potentials tends to zero, and this will be crucial to obtain the fast rate in
Section

4.2
.

Meanwhile, using
Proposition

2.3
(v),(vi) to bound
{\rm Var}_{{P\otimes Q}}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]
yields our first explicit bound on the sample
complexity of regularized OT.

Corollary 3.2
.

We have

\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\leq 8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{\psi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{6\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}}{n}.

In the case of Tsallis entropies this yields

\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\leq\begin{cases}8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{(6\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon)^{2/(p-1)}}{\varepsilon^{2/(p-1)}\,n},&p>1\\[8.00003pt]
8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{\exp(12\|c\|_{\infty}/\varepsilon)}{n}&p=1.\end{cases}

(13)

In particular,
Corollary

3.2
already implies bounds with dimension-free and polynomial
dependence on
1/\varepsilon
for a broad class of divergences, including Tsallis entropies of any order
p\in(1,\infty)
. For EOT, however, the corresponding bound exhibits an exponential dependence on
1/\varepsilon
. We will see in
Section

3.2
how this bound
can be improved for Lipschitz transport costs.

Next, we turn to the variance of
\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}
and show that it is of order

1/n
under our general assumptions, with no dependence on the regularization parameter
\varepsilon
.

Proposition 3.3

(Variance bound)
.

We have

{\rm Var}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr]\leq\frac{4\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}}{n}.

Independence of the regularization parameter
\varepsilon
is expected, as the variance bound also holds for unregularized optimal transport
[
10
]
. By contrast, the bias bound in
Proposition

3.1
depends on
\varepsilon
through the variance
\mathrm{Var}_{P\otimes Q}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]
.

Before proceeding to more refined sample complexity results, we state a central limit theorem for the regularized cost.

Theorem 3.4

(Central limit theorem)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is connected and
c\in\mathcal{C}(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime})
. Consider the estimator

\widehat{W}=\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}-\int h_{\varepsilon}(x,y)d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})(x,y),

where

h_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-\varepsilon\psi\biggl(\frac{f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\biggr).

Then
n{\rm Var}[\widehat{W}]\to 0
, and as a consequence,

\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}-\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}])
converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable with mean zero and variance given by

{\rm Var}\Biggl[f_{\varepsilon}(X)+g_{\varepsilon}(Y)-\varepsilon\Bigl(\int\psi(\xi_{\varepsilon}(X,y))\,dQ(y)+\int\psi(\xi_{\varepsilon}(x,Y))\,dP(x)\Bigr)\Biggr],

where
\xi_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=(f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-c(x,y))/\varepsilon
and
(X,Y)\sim P\otimes Q
.

3.2
Bounds on the variance of the density for Lipschitz costs

In
Corollary

3.2
,
\mathrm{Var}_{P\otimes Q}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]

was bounded crudely in terms of
\|c\|_{\infty}
.
We now strengthen this bound when the cost
is Lipschitz, obtaining refined results for the sample complexity. We write
\mathbb{B}_{R}(x_{0})
for the open ball of radius
R>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{R}(x_{0})
for its closure.

Assumption 3.5

(Cost is Lipschitz in first
argument)
.

There exists
L>0
such that

|c(x,y)-c(z,y)|\leq L\|x-z\|\quad\text{for all }x,z\in\Omega\text{ and }y\in\Omega^{\prime}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
3.5
hold. Then

\int\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)^{2}dP(x)dQ(y)\leq\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x).

Remark 3.7
.

Lemma

3.6
involves only one of the two marginals (the one referenced in
3.5
). If the cost function is
L
-Lipschitz in both variables, then by symmetry, the lower complexity adaptation principle holds:

\int\bigl(\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\bigr)^{2}dP(x)dQ(y)\leq\min_{\mu\in\{P,Q\}}\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{\mu(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)d\mu(x).

Combining
Lemma

3.6
with
Propositions

3.1
and
3.3
gives the following bound.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
3.5
hold. Then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\\
\leq\frac{8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}}{n}\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x)+\frac{4\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}}{n}.

This abstract right-hand side will be made more concrete in the next two subsections, which will make clear that

Corollary

3.8
is indeed a significant strengthening of
Corollary

3.2
.

3.3
Semi-discrete regularized optimal transport

Semi-discrete transport is the setting where
P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
is supported on a set of distinct atoms
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(whereas
Q
is arbitrary). Then, the unregularized OT problem does not suffer from the curse of dimensionality
[
11
,
45
,
35
]
. In EOT,
[
47
]
and
[
34
]
discovered independently that

\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{\rm EOT}-{\rm EOT}|]\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, where the hidden constant is independent of
\varepsilon
. More specifically,
[
34
]
showed this for bounded costs, and
[
47
]
for Lipschitz costs, but the proof of

[
47
]
only uses Lipschitz continuity in one variable. In fact, in the semi-discrete case with
\Omega=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}
, any bounded cost satisfies
3.5
: if
N\geq 2
, for
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
and
y\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

\bigl|c(x_{i},y)-c(x_{j},y)\bigr|\leq 2\|c\|_{\infty}\leq L\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|,\quad L:=\frac{2\|c\|_{\infty}}{\min_{k\neq\ell}\|x_{k}-x_{\ell}\|}.

The case
N=1
is trivial and any
L>0
may be chosen.
The right-hand side of
Lemma

3.6
can then be bounded via

\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x)\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}\bigl(p_{i}^{-1}\bigr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)p_{i}=N.

Therefore,
Corollary

3.8
yields the following bound, generalizing the aforementioned results for EOT to general regularizations.

Corollary 3.9

(Semi-discrete)
.

Assume that the support of
P
has cardinality
N<\infty
. Then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\leq\frac{8\bigl(5\|c\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\phi^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}N}{n}+\frac{4\|c\|_{\infty}^{2}}{n}.

3.4
Bounds on the variance of the density for Tsallis entropies

In this section we develop the bound
of
Lemma

3.6
in the case of the
L^{p}
(equivalently, up to additive constants, Tsallis) regularizers, namely

\phi_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{t^{p}-1}{p}&\text{if }p\in(1,\infty),\\
t\log t&\text{if }p=1.\end{cases}

Definition 3.10

(Covering numbers)
.

A
\delta
-cover
of
\Omega
is a set of points
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset\Omega
such that
\Omega\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x_{i})
. The
\delta
-covering number

\mathcal{N}(\Omega,\delta)
of
\Omega
is the smallest cardinality
N
of a
\delta
-cover of
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Fix
\phi=\phi_{p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
3.5
hold.

(i)

If
p\geq 2
,

\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x)\leq\inf_{r>0}\biggl\{\mathcal{N}\Bigl(\Omega,\frac{r}{4}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\biggr\}.

(ii)

If
1<p<2
,

\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x)\leq 2^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\inf_{r>0}\biggl\{\mathcal{N}\Bigl(\Omega,\frac{r}{4}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\biggr\}.

(iii)

If
p=1
,

\int\inf_{r>0}\psi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\phi^{\prime}_{p}\biggl(\frac{1}{P(\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(x))}\biggr)+\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r\biggr)dP(x)\leq\inf_{r>0}\mathcal{N}\Bigl(\Omega,\frac{r}{4}\Bigr)e^{\frac{2L}{\varepsilon}r}.

Next, we recall the definition of Minkowski dimension.

Definition 3.12
.

The (upper)
Minkowski dimension
of
\Omega
is

{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\mathcal{N}(\Omega,\delta)}{-\log\delta}.

(The lower Minkowski dimension is defined with
\liminf
, but since we will only use the upper dimension, for brevity, we simply call that the Minkowski dimension.)

The following observation is a direct consequence of
Lemmas

3.6
and
3.11
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Fix
\phi=\phi_{p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
3.5
hold. For any real number
d_{P}>{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
, there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

{\rm Var}_{P\otimes Q}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{d_{P}}{1+d_{P}(p-1)}}.

Remark 3.14
.

The assumption
d_{P}>{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
ensures that
\mathcal{N}(\Omega,\delta)\leq C^{\prime}\delta^{-d_{P}}
, for
\delta
small enough and some
C^{\prime}>0
. In fact, the latter inequality is a sharper sufficient condition for the result. In most cases of interest, for instance when
\Omega
is a Lipschitz submanifold, this sharper condition is also satisfied for
d_{P}={\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
.

The following example shows that our bound on
{\rm Var}_{P\otimes Q}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]
in
Lemma

3.13
is sharp.

Example 3.15
.

Let
P=Q
be the uniform measure on
[0,1]^{d}
and

c(x,y)=d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y)=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|x-y-z\|.

Then there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

{\rm Var}_{P\otimes Q}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]\geq\frac{1}{C}\cdot\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{1+(p-1)d}}&\text{if $p\in(1,\infty)$},\\
\varepsilon^{-d}&\text{if }p=1.\end{cases}

Finally, we combine
Propositions

3.1
,
3.3
and
3.13
to obtain the main result for the general
p
-Tsallis family.

Corollary 3.16
.

Fix
\phi=\phi_{p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
3.5
hold. For any
d_{P}>{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
, there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{d_{P}}{1+d_{P}(p-1)}}}{n}.

Remark

3.14
applies to
Corollary

3.16
as well, and this is the form we have used in the Introduction.

We observe that
Corollary

3.16
indicates a much stronger dependence on the (intrinsic) dimension for the entropic regularization than the Tsallis entropies for

. The next subsection explains this phenomenon.

3.5
Trade-off between regularization bias and statistical complexity

We start by recalling a different notion of dimension. Denote by
\mathcal{W}_{2}(P,Q)
the 2-Wasserstein distance between
P
and
Q
.

Definition 3.17

(Quantization dimension)
.

We say that
P
has
quantization dimension
smaller than
D_{P}>0
if there exist
C>0
and a sequence of probability measures
\{P_{n}\}_{n}
, with
{\rm supp}(P_{n})
having cardinality at most
n
, such that
\mathcal{W}_{2}(P_{n},P)\leq C\,n^{-1/D_{P}}
for all
n
large enough. We say that
P
has
uniform quantization dimension
smaller than
D_{P}>0
if additionally the sequence
\{P_{n}\}_{n}
can be chosen such that

\liminf_{n}\frac{\min_{x\in{\rm supp}(P_{n})}P_{n}(x)}{\max_{x\in{\rm supp}(P_{n})}P_{n}(x)}>0.

(14)

In this subsection we assume that the transport cost function is Lipschitz.

Assumption 3.18

(Lipschitz cost)
.

The cost
c
is Lipschitz on
\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}
with constant
L>0
.

Theorem 3.3 and Example 3.4 in
[
16
]
show that, if
P
has quantization dimension smaller than
D_{P}
and
p\in[1,2]
, there exists a constant
C_{p}
depending on
\Omega
,

,

and the Lipschitz constant of the cost such that for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

\bigl|{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}\bigr|\leq\begin{cases}C_{p}\,\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{(p-1)D_{P}+1}}&\text{for }p>1,\\
C_{1}\,\varepsilon|\log(\varepsilon)+1|&\text{for }p=1.\end{cases}

(15)

For
p>2
, the bound (
15
) holds if
P
has uniform quantization dimension smaller than
D_{P}
.
1
1

1

[
16
]
it is shown that (

) holds for

if the

is smaller than

in

.

Lemma 3.20

(Regularization error)
.

Let

with

. The following hold for

(i)

If
p\in[1,2]

\bigl|{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{(p-1)d_{P}+1}}&\text{for }p>1,\\
-\varepsilon\log(\varepsilon)&\text{for }p=1.\end{cases}

(ii)

If

and (

\bigl|{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{(p-1)d_{P}+1}}.

We observe that for EOT, the regularization error

up to multiplicative constants, while for

the dependence appears in the exponent of the regularization parameter. This also explains why the influence of the dimension on EOT is much more pronounced in

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
3.18
hold, let
d_{P}>{\rm dim}_{M}(\Omega)
, and fix
\phi=\phi_{p}
with

. The following hold for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

(i)

If
p\in[1,2]
,

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim\begin{cases}\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{d_{P}}{2(1+(p-1)d_{P})}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{(p-1)d_{P}+1}}&\text{for }p>1,\\
\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{d_{P}}{2}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\varepsilon\log(\varepsilon)&\text{for }p=1.\end{cases}

(ii)

If

and (
17
) holds,

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{p},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{d_{P}}{2(1+(p-1)d_{P})}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{(p-1)d_{P}+1}}.

Remark

3.14
applies to both

3.20
and
3.21
.

Next, we tune

as a function of

to optimize the rate.

Remark 3.22
.

For the case of QOT (i.e.,
p=2
), the optimal choice of
\varepsilon
in

3.21
to estimate OT is
\varepsilon\asymp n^{-\frac{1+{d_{P}}}{{d_{P}}+2}},
which yields the rate

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{2},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{{d_{P}}+2}}.

For EOT the optimal choice is
\varepsilon\asymp n^{-\frac{1}{{d_{P}}+2}}\,(\log n)^{-\frac{2}{{d_{P}}+2}}

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{1},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{{d_{P}}+2}}\,(\log n)^{\frac{{d_{P}}}{{d_{P}}+2}}.

In the case of QOT with quadratic transport cost, the
L^{2}
structure of the penalty allows the regularization error to control the variance of the optimal density, and the quadratic cost yields a sharper approximation estimate. Combining these two facts, we can further improve our bound as follows, now with the ambient dimension.

Proposition 3.23

(Improved

Let

\phi=\phi_{2}
and

Then for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{2},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{2d+4}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{d+2}}.

The optimal choice of

\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\rm OT}_{\phi_{2},\varepsilon}\bigr]-{\rm OT}\bigr|\lesssim n^{-\frac{2}{d+4}}.

We remark that when

the OT cost under the assumptions
of

is

[

Proposition

To the best of our knowledge,

Using the concavity of the integrand, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl(\widehat{\Phi}_{\phi,\varepsilon}({\widehat{f}},\widehat{g})-\widehat{\Phi}_{\phi,\varepsilon}({f}_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})\bigr)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}\biggl[\int\bigl[\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\bigr]\bigl(1-\psi^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{f_{\varepsilon}\oplus g_{\varepsilon}-c}{\varepsilon}\biggr)\bigr)\,d\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q}\biggr]

since

\mathbb{E}\biggl[\iint\xi\,d\widehat{Q}d\widehat{P}\biggr]=0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\int(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon})\int\xi\,d\widehat{Q}d\widehat{P}\biggr]

\frac{m_{i}}{\varepsilon}\geq t_{\psi}
,

\displaystyle a_{i,j}a_{\ell,j}

\displaystyle\geq\Bigl(\psi^{\prime\prime}\biggl(\frac{m_{i}-2L\bigl(\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|+\|Y_{j(i)}-Y_{j}\|\bigr)}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\biggr)\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)^{2}{\bf 1}_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}{\bf 1}_{\|Y_{j(i)}-Y_{j}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}.

Thus (
20
) yields

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})\bigl(u\oplus v\bigr)^{2}d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi})^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j,i,\ell}\bigl(u(X_{i})-u(X_{\ell})\bigr)^{2}\frac{{\bf 1}_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}{\bf 1}_{\|Y_{j(i)}-Y_{j}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}}{\sum_{k}\pi_{k,j}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi})^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,\ell}\bigl(u(X_{i})-u(X_{\ell})\bigr)^{2}{\bf 1}_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}\sum_{j}\frac{{\bf 1}_{\|Y_{j(i)}-Y_{j}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}}{\sum_{k}\pi_{k,j}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2}}\Biggl(\frac{\min_{j}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(Y_{j})\Bigr)}{\max_{j}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}\pi_{k,j}^{(1)}}\Biggr)\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi})^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,\ell}\bigl(u(X_{i})-u(X_{\ell})\bigr)^{2}{\bf 1}_{\|X_{i}-X_{\ell}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\Biggl(\frac{\min_{j}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(Y_{j})\Bigr)}{\max_{j}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}\pi_{k,j}^{(1)}}\Biggr)\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi})^{2}\widehat{\Theta}_{\varepsilon}(u,u).

This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition C.4
.

For all

\widehat{\Theta}_{\varepsilon}(u,u)\geq{\frac{\lambda_{P}^{2}}{\Lambda_{P}^{2}\Bigl(\Bigl\lceil\frac{16L{\rm diam}(\Omega)}{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}\Bigr\rceil\Bigr)^{d+2}}}{\rm Var}_{\widehat{P}}[u]{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}.

The proof will be stated at the end of this subsection, after some auxiliary results. For a measurable map

.

Note

C.2
Coercivity of the random bilinear form

Next, we show the coercivity of
\widehat{B}_{r}
under the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
. The idea of the proof is to show that
\widehat{B}_{r}
defines a nonnegative operator
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}
which admits an eigenvalue decomposition. Then, we bound the eigenvalues on the strength of
Proposition

C.2
.

Proposition C.8
.

For
0\leq r\leq t_{\varepsilon}
,
we have

\widehat{B}_{r}(u\oplus v,u\oplus v)\geq\widehat{\beta}\|u\oplus v\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}^{2}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}},

where

\widehat{\beta}=\min\biggl(\frac{\widehat{\alpha}\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))}{\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))+4\gamma(n,\varepsilon)},\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))\biggr).

As a consequence,

\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})\geq\frac{\widehat{\beta}}{2}\int\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{f}-f_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\widehat{g}-g_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q}){\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}.

Proof.

The bilinear form

\widehat{B}_{r}(u\oplus v,\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v})=\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r})\bigl(u\oplus v\bigr)\bigl(\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v}\bigr)d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})

is symmetric and nonnegative. Hence, there exists an operator

\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}:\widehat{L}_{\oplus}^{2}\to\widehat{L}_{\oplus}^{2}

such that

\widehat{B}_{r}(u\oplus v,\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v})=\bigl\langle\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}\bigl(u\oplus v\bigr),\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v}\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})},

for all
u\oplus v,\tilde{u}\oplus\tilde{v}\in\widehat{L}_{\oplus}^{2}
. The operator
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}
admits an eigenvalue decomposition, and all eigenvalues are nonnegative since
\widehat{B}_{r}
is symmetric and nonnegative. Let
\lambda\geq 0
be a nontrivial eigenvalue with associated eigenvector
u_{0}\oplus v_{0}\neq 0
. Assume furthermore that
\mathcal{E}_{n}
holds. We can assume that
u_{0}
is centered with respect to
\widehat{P}
, as this does not change the value of
u_{0}\oplus v_{0}
.

Fixing
u\in L^{2}(\widehat{P})
with
\int ud\widehat{P}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\bigl\langle u_{0},u\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigl\langle u_{0}\oplus v_{0},u\oplus 0\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}\bigl(u_{0}\oplus v_{0}\bigr),u\oplus 0\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}

\displaystyle=\int u(x)\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,y))(u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y))d(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int u(x)\biggl(u_{0}(x)\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,y))d\widehat{Q}(y)+\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,y))v_{0}(y)d\widehat{Q}(y)\biggr)d\widehat{P}(x).

Since
u\in L^{2}(\widehat{P})
with
\int ud\widehat{P}=0
was arbitrary and
u_{0}
is centered, we get that, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\lambda u_{0}(X_{i})=u_{0}(X_{i})\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(X_{i},y))d\widehat{Q}(y)+\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(X_{i},y))v_{0}(y)d\widehat{Q}(y)\\
-\int\biggl(u_{0}(x)\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,y))d\widehat{Q}(y)+\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,y))v_{0}(y)d\widehat{Q}(y)\biggr)d\widehat{P}(x).

Furthermore, fixing
v\in L^{2}(\widehat{Q})
and using that
\int u_{0}d\widehat{P}=0
, we derive the identity

\lambda\langle v_{0},v\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}=\lambda\langle u_{0}\oplus v_{0},0\oplus v\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}.

Therefore, repeating the same argument as before,

\lambda v_{0}(Y_{j})=v_{0}(Y_{j})\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)+\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))u_{0}(x)d\widehat{P}(x),

and in particular

v_{0}(Y_{j})=\frac{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))u_{0}(x)d\widehat{P}(x)}{\lambda-\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)},

whenever the denominator is
nonzero.
There are two cases,

0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}\min_{j}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda>\min_{j}\frac{1}{2}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x),

which we analyze separately.

Case 1:
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}\min_{j}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)
.
We use the fact that
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is monotone and
0\leq r\leq t_{\varepsilon}
to see that for some
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\lambda>\frac{1}{2}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)

and

\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)\geq\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\biggl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\biggr)d\widehat{P}(x).

Arguing as in the proof of
Lemma

C.3
, it follows that

\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\biggl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\biggr)d\widehat{P}(x)\geq\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\frac{\#\bigl\{i:\|X_{i}-X_{i(j)}\|\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}\bigr\}}{n},

where

i(j)\in\argmax_{i}\{\widehat{f}(X_{i})+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(X_{i},Y_{j})\}.

This implies

\displaystyle\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\biggl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\biggr)\,d\widehat{P}(x)\geq\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi})\min_{i}\widehat{P}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i})\Bigr),

(22)

which yields

\displaystyle\lambda\geq\frac{1}{2}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\biggl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\biggr)d\widehat{P}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i})\Bigr).

In this case, the proof is complete here.

Case 2:
0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}\min_{j}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)
.
By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigl(v_{0}(Y_{j})\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))u_{0}(x)d\widehat{P}(x)}{\lambda-\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\biggl(\frac{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))u_{0}(x)d\widehat{P}(x)}{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)}\biggr)^{2}\leq 4\frac{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))\bigl(u_{0}(x)\bigr)^{2}d\widehat{P}(x)}{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{r}(x,Y_{j}))d\widehat{P}(x)}.

We use the fact that
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is monotone and
0\leq r\leq t_{\varepsilon}
to find

\displaystyle\bigl(v_{0}(Y_{j})\bigr)^{2}\leq 4\frac{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\Bigl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\Bigr)\bigl(u_{0}(x)\bigr)^{2}d\widehat{P}(x)}{\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\Bigl(\frac{\widehat{f}(x)+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(x,Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\Bigr)d\widehat{P}(x)}.

Applying (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq 4\frac{\int u_{0}^{2}\int\psi^{\prime\prime}\Bigl(\frac{\widehat{f}\oplus\widehat{g}-c}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta_{\psi}}{4}\Bigr)d\widehat{Q}(y)d\widehat{P}(x)}{\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\gamma(n,\varepsilon)}{\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}.

(23)

Note that this implies that
\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}\neq 0
.
Since
u_{0}\oplus v_{0}
is an eigenvector of
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lambda\|u_{0}\oplus v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle u_{0}\oplus v_{0},\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}\bigl(u_{0}\oplus v_{0}\bigr)\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}\geq\widehat{\alpha}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}},

where in the last inequality we used
Proposition

C.2
and the fact that
u_{0}
is centered. Using again that
u_{0}
is centered, we get
\|u_{0}\oplus v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P}\otimes\widehat{Q})}^{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}^{2}
, which in turn yields

\lambda\geq\widehat{\alpha}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{P})}^{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}\,.

(24)

Plugging (
C.2
) into (
24
), we derive

\lambda\geq\frac{\widehat{\alpha}\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))}{\psi^{\prime\prime}\bigl(t_{\psi}-\delta_{\psi}\bigr)\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))+4\gamma(n,\varepsilon)}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}

for any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\widehat{\mathbb{M}}_{r}
under Case 2. Combining this with the lower bound obtained in Case 1 yields
\lambda\geq\widehat{\beta}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}
for every eigenvalue
\lambda
. This completes the proof.
∎

C.3
Replacing random bounds by deterministic ones

There are three random terms in
Proposition

C.8
:

\min_{i}\widehat{P}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i})\Bigr)
,
\min_{j}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(Y_{j})\Bigr)
, and
{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}
. In this subsection we provide bounds for these quantities.

C.3.1
Probability of empirical balls

The following result shows that the empirical measures of the balls are close to their population counterparts. In particular, we lower bound the probability of the events

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\biggl\{\min_{j}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(Y_{j})\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\biggr\}

and

\mathcal{G}_{n}=\biggl\{\min_{i}\widehat{P}(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{x\in\Omega}P\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(x)\Bigr)\biggr\}.

The proof, which is standard, follows from VC-theory and Dudley’s integral entropy bound.

Proposition C.9
.

There exists a constant
C\geq 0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\mathbb{P}\biggl(\min_{j}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(Y_{j})\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\biggr)\geq 1-\frac{C}{n\,\Bigl(\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}}

and

\mathbb{P}\biggl(\min_{i}\widehat{P}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(X_{i})\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{x\in\Omega}P\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(x)\Bigr)\biggr)\geq 1-\frac{C}{n\,\Bigl(\min_{x\in\Omega}P\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(x)\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}}.

Proof.

The collection
\mathcal{B}
of all open balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a VC-class of order
d+2
by
[
48
, Lemma 2.6.15]
. Hence, by Theorem 2.6.4, ibid, there exists a dimensional constant
C
such that, for any probability measure
\mu
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B},L^{2}(\mu),\delta)\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2(d+1)}}.

Here
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B},L^{2}(\mu),\delta)
denotes the minimal number of balls of radius
\delta>0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
necessary to cover
\{{\bf 1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}
. Hence, the bound

\int_{0}^{1}\sup_{\mu}\sqrt{\log\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B},L^{2}(\mu),\delta)\bigr)}d\delta\leq C

(25)

on the metric entropy holds
for a possibly different dimensional constant
C
. Define the stochastic process

\{{\bf 1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}\ni{\bf 1}_{B}\mapsto\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})={Q}({\bf 1}_{B})-\widehat{Q}({\bf 1}_{B})

over the metric space
(\{{\bf 1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\},L^{2}(Q))
. We define the symmetrized version of the process as

\{{\bf 1}_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}\ni{\bf 1}_{B}\mapsto\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}({\bf 1}_{B})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\epsilon_{j}{\bf 1}_{B}(Y_{j}),

where
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i}
is an i.i.d. sequence of Rademacher random variables independent of
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
. First note that the random variable

\sup_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})
can be written as
\sup_{B\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the collection of all open balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with rational radii and centers. (This avoids measurability issues in the sequel.) The symmetrization lemma yields

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\sup_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\leq 4\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\sup_{B\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}({\bf 1}_{B})\bigr)^{2}\bigr].

(26)

Hence, by Hoeffding’s inequality, the process
\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}
satisfies

\mathbb{P}\biggl(\sqrt{n}\bigl|\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}({\bf 1}_{B_{1}})-\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}({\bf 1}_{B_{2}})\bigr|\geq s\,\bigm|Y_{1},\dots Y_{n}\biggr)\leq 2e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2\|{\bf 1}_{B_{2}}-{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{Q})}}},

i.e.,
\sqrt{n}\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}|Y_{1},\dots Y_{n}
is sub-Gaussian with respect to the
L^{2}(\widehat{Q})
-norm. Hence, Dudley’s integral entropy bound (cf.
[
48
, Corollary 2.2.8]
) yields

n\,\mathbb{E}\biggl[\bigl(\sup_{B\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{S}({\bf 1}_{B})\bigr)^{2}\bigm|Y_{1},\dots Y_{n}\biggr]\lesssim\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\log\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B},L^{2}(\widehat{Q}),\delta)\bigr)}d\delta\biggr)^{2}\leq C^{2},

where the final constant is finite and deterministic by (
25
). Taking expectations and using (
26
), we conclude that

n\,\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\sup_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})\big)^{2}\big]=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\biggl(\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}\widehat{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\biggl(\sup_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\leq\frac{1}{2}\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\biggl(\sup_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)>\frac{1}{2}\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{4\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\sup_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}}\mathbb{Q}_{n}({\bf 1}_{B})\bigr)^{2}\bigr]}{\Bigl(\min_{y\in\Omega^{\prime}}{Q}\Bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{8L}}(y)\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2}},

completing the proof. The estimate for
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is obtained by the identical argument with
P,X_{i},\Omega
in place of
Q,Y_{j},\Omega^{\prime}
.
∎

C.3.2
The
\infty
-Wasserstein distance

In this section we recall some of the results from
[
21
]
regarding the empirical
\infty
-Wasserstein distance, defined as

\widehat{W}_{\infty}=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(P,\widehat{P})}\bigl\|x-y\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\pi)}.

Because
P
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, the infimum is attained by a map, in the sense that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a transport map
\hat{T}
with
\hat{T}_{\#}P=\widehat{P}
and

\widehat{W}_{\infty}=\|\hat{T}-I\|_{L^{\infty}(P)}.

As a consequence, we have the equality

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\Bigl\{\widehat{W}_{\infty}\leq{\frac{\delta_{\psi}\varepsilon}{32L}}\Bigr\}.

We define the sequence

\alpha_{n,d}=\begin{cases}(\log(n))^{\frac{1}{2}}{n^{-\frac{1}{2}}}&\text{if }d=1,\\
{(\log(n))^{\frac{3}{4}}}{n^{-\frac{1}{2}}}&\text{if }d=2,\\
{(\log(n))^{\frac{1}{d}}}{n^{-\frac{1}{d}}}&\text{if }d>2.\end{cases}

Theorem C.10

(
[
21
]
and
[
37
]
)
.

There exist constants
C,A\geq 0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq A\alpha_{n,d}\bigr)\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}}.

Corollary C.11
.

Fix
{u>\max(d,2)}
. Then
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{W}_{\infty}^{{u}}\bigr]\leq{\frac{\delta_{\psi}^{{u}}}{n(32L)^{{u}}}}.

As a consequence, for any
{u>\max(d,2)}
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c})\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{{u}}n}.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{W}_{\infty}\leq{\rm diam}(\Omega)
and for
n
sufficiently large
A\alpha_{n,d}\leq{\rm diam}(\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{W}_{\infty}^{u}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{u}}\bigr)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\bigl({\rm diam}(\Omega)\bigr)^{u}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{u}}\bigr)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\bigl(A\alpha_{n,d}\bigr)^{u}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{u}}\bigr)d\lambda+\int_{\bigl(A\alpha_{n,d}\bigr)^{u}}^{\bigl({\rm diam}(\Omega)\bigr)^{u}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{u}}\bigr)d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(A\alpha_{n,d}\bigr)^{u}+\bigl({\rm diam}(\Omega)\bigr)^{u}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\widehat{W}_{\infty}\geq A\alpha_{n,d}\bigr)\leq\frac{\delta_{\psi}^{u}}{n(32L)^{u}},

where the last inequality holds for
n
sufficiently large, by
Theorem

C.10
. Hence, the first claim holds. The second is derived from the first via Markov’s inequality.
∎

belongs to

(2,\infty)
. We have

and

. Moreover,

\left(a+\frac{1}{8}\right)_{+}^{q-2}\leq C_{q}\left(a_{+}^{q-2}+1\right),\qquad a\in\mathbb{R},

(27)

\left(a+\frac{1}{8}\right)_{+}\leq a_{+}+\frac{1}{8},

.

\pi_{i,j}^{(1)}\leq(q-1)C_{q}\left[\biggl(\frac{\widehat{f}(X_{i})+\widehat{g}(Y_{j})-c(X_{i},Y_{j})}{\varepsilon}\biggr)_{+}^{q-2}+1\right].
