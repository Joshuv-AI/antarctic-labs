Reply To Comment on “Langmuir Adsorption Kinetics in Liquid Media: Interface Reaction Model”
Authors: Md. Akhtarul Islam, Myisha Ahmed Chowdhury, Md. Salatul Islam Mozumder, Md. Tamez Uddin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the mentioned paper, an interface reaction model (IRM) was proposed to determine sorption–desorption rate constants independently of operating parameters. Recently, commentary has been published that discusses some implicit assumptions made in developing the model, and at the end, the dimensional homogeneity of the IRM equations has been questioned. In response, the authors agree with the simplification made in developing the model but show that the model equations are dimensionally consistent. To diffuse any confusion in the future regarding dimensional consistency, the dimension of each term in IRM has been exclusively discussed.

Introduction

Islam et al. derived a Langmuir adsorption kinetic equation based on IRM, in which the interface is defined as a sharp boundary between two phases. It is assumed that mass exchange between two immiscible phases is realized through interface reaction. Unlike the chemical kinetic equation for a homogeneous system, in which the rate is expressed in terms of “rate of change in mass per unit volume or weight”, the interface reaction rate is expressed as the “rate of change of mass” in each of the contacting phases. The proposed model gives sorption–desorption rate constants independently of operating parameters, which the existing literature models failed to ensure.

Very recently, Hasan and Revellame, in the form of commentary, raise questions about some important elements of the model development and with strong statements reject the applicability of the model. As the developers of the model, we would like to give some explanation of the raised issues and to disperse any confusion that may arise from them.

First of all, the commentators mention some implicit assumptions that have been made in developing the model. Then they claim that the basic model equations are dimensionally inconsistent, and finally, they propose corrections to the model equations. We feel that “dimensional inconsistency” is a serious issue that would make a model invalid, and for this reason, we shall focus on this issue.

Implicit Assumptions and Use of the Model-Title “Interface Reaction”

The commentators mention that in developing the model, bulk properties have been considered instead of interface ones, and thus, different steps in the adsorption process like bulk liquid transport, film diffusion, and pore diffusion have been ignored. The commentators are right in this respect. In developing the model, it is stated, “Assuming that there is no diffusion layer, and the interface represents a sharp boundary between the solid and liquid phases (i.e., the process is adsorption kinetics-limited), the adsorption kinetics is represented by a reversible interface reaction...”

In studying the adsorption process for technological purposes, the exact mechanism of sorption–desorption is not studied in depth. The concentration in the liquid phase is determined analytically, but that in the solid phase is never measured (which is definitely difficult). The concentration of the adsorbate in the solid phase is estimated based on the material balance, following the change in the concentration in the liquid phase only. In fact, no information is available about the distribution of the adsorbate on the solid surface or in the bulk. Ignoring the existence of any diffusion layer, the concentration of the adsorbate in the liquid bulk is taken to be the same as that at the interface (which is considered to be a sharp boundary).

Correction to the IRM Proposed by the Commentators

In this section, a brief discussion is used to distinguish between the conventional adsorption kinetic model (CAKM, also used by Liu and Shen) and the interface reaction model (IRM) proposed by Islam et al. Then we shall try to find what position the proposed correction occupies with respect to the CAKM and the IRM.

The unit of k a(0) is m3/(kg·s). It may also be written as m3/(kg A·s) if the investigator decides to show how the unit is formed. It appears that the commentators prefer writing in the second format. This is a question of choice. The unit of q is kg adsorbate/kg adsorbent. Practically, this means that q is dimensionless and q ∞ is also dimensionless. The terms “A”, “adsorbent”, “adsorbate”, and so on do not contribute to the formation of the unit of a material property. But still researchers express the same unit in a different way. Conclusions

Equations 11 and 12 in Islam et al. are dimensionally consistent, and the IRM could not be devaluated based on dimensional homogeneity. The commentators have rightly noticed that the model equations have been developed by ignoring phenomena like bulk liquid transport, film diffusion, and pore diffusion. The model is simplified by the assumption of the existence of a sharp boundary between the solid and liquid phases. New experimental data treated with IRM would show how much the neglected phenomena deviate from the model prediction.
