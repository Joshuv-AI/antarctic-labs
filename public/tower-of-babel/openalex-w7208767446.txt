Upcycling industrial by-products into water treatment materials is a promising strategy for simultaneously
achieving resource circularity and water puriﬁcation. In this study, a hydroxyapatite-zeolite (HAp-ZE) composite upcycled from blast furnace slag, an abundant industrial by-product, was investigated as a sorbent for
toxic cobalt ions (Co2+). HAp-ZE removed Co2+ from the solution within a short contact time of 1 h and achieved
a removal eﬃciency of approximately 100 % at Co2+ concentrations of up to 0.4 mmol/L. The sorption isotherm
of HAp-ZE ﬁtted well with the Langmuir model. Furthermore, the maximum Co2+ sorption capacity of HApZE, estimated from the sorption isotherm, was comparable to that of a physical mixture of high-purity zeolite
and HAp. Sodium ions (Na+) and calcium ions (Ca2+) were released from HAp-ZE during the Co2+ sorption
tests. Elemental analysis revealed that Co was distributed in both the zeolite and HAp phases of HAp-ZE. These
results suggest that Co2+ removal occurs through cation-exchange reactions in both the zeolite and HAp phases
of HAp-ZE. This study demonstrates that the low-cost HAp-ZE produced from blast furnace slag exhibits a
Co2+ removal performance comparable to that of its high-purity counterpart, highlighting its potential as a
sustainable and economically attractive water puriﬁcation material.

1. Introduction
The conservation and recycling of water resources have
become critical global challenges owing to rapid industrialization, population growth, and increasing freshwater
demand. Ensuring access to clean water requires the
development of eﬃcient and economically viable water
puriﬁcation materials capable of removing harmful contaminants from water environments.1,2) Heavy metals are a
serious concern among various pollutants because they
pose long-term risks to both human health and ecosystems.
Therefore, signiﬁcant eﬀorts have been made to develop
high-performance sorbents with low production costs.3–5)
In particular, cobalt is not only a toxic metal that poses
risks to human health and the environment but also a

2.2

Synthesis of HAp-ZE

HAp-ZE was synthesized from blast furnace slag according to the previous reports.13,14) 5.0 g of the ball-milled
slag was dissolved in 22 mL of 1.0 mol/L H3PO4 aqueous
solution for 2 h. 50 mL of 3.0 mol/L NaOH aqueous
solution was added dropwise into the suspension. The
mixture was then vigorously stirred for 3 h at 80 °C in a
water bath. The mixture was transferred to a Teﬂon bottle
and aged in an electric oven at 90 °C for 2 days. The
obtained slurry was then ﬁltered, washed with distilled
water, and dried overnight at 110 °C.

et al. demonstrated that hydroxyapatite (HAp) and zeolite
composites (HAp-ZE) can be synthesized from blast furnace slag under mild conditions using phosphoric acid,
which is highly reactive with calcium.13,14) HAp and
zeolite are well-known as promising water puriﬁcation
materials for toxic metal cations.15,16) Therefore, HAp-ZE
produced from blast furnace slag is expected to be a promising and cost-eﬀective water treatment material. Although
several studies have reported the metal cation removal
properties of slag-derived HAp-zeolite composites, their
cobalt ion (Co2+) removal performance has not yet been
investigated.18,19) Furthermore, no studies have systematically clariﬁed the removal mechanism of slag-derived
HAp-zeolite composites through detailed post-sorption
characterization.
Herein, we investigated the Co2+ removal performance
of a HAp-ZE composite upcycled from blast furnace slag
(Fig. 1). The Co2+ sorption capacity of HAp-ZE was
quantitatively evaluated and compared with those of reference zeolite and HAp to assess the applicability of the upcycled composite as a Co2+ sorbent. In addition, changes
in the concentrations of host metal cations released from
HAp-ZE during Co2+ sorption were examined to evaluate
the contribution of cation-exchange reactions to Co2+
removal.

2. Materials and methods
2.1

Materials

Phosphoric acid (H3PO4) and sodium hydroxide
(NaOH) were purchased from Nacalai Tesque, Japan.
Cobalt nitrate hexahydrate [Co(NO3)2·6H2O] and nitric
acid (HNO3) were purchased from FUJIFILM Wako Pure
Chemical Corporation, Japan. The blast furnace slag was
consisted of 41.5 wt % CaO, 35.8 wt % SiO2, 15.3 wt %
Al2O3, 5.48 wt % MgO, and 1.58 wt % Fe2O3, with the
remaining fraction consisting of minor components.13,14)
Zeolite (X-type zeolite, zeolite 13X) was obtained from
Tosoh Corporation, Japan. Hydroxyapatite (HAp, HAP100) was obtained from Taihei Chemical Industrial Co.,
Ltd., Japan. All reagents were of analytical grade and used
without further puriﬁcation.

2.3

Characterization

Powder X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) patterns were recorded
using a D8 ADVANCE (Bruker AXS GmbH, Germany)
with Cu K¡ radiation (­ = 1.5418 ¡) at 40 kV and 40 mA.
Nitrogen (N2) adsorption–desorption isotherm data were

was measured using a pH meter (D-52, HORIBA, Ltd.,
Japan).

2.4

Cobalt sorption test

The Co2+ aqueous solution from 0.2 to 4.0 mmol/L was
prepared using Co(NO3)2·6H2O without pH adjustment
(pH = 5.6–5.8). HAp-ZE and references (Zeolite and
HAp) were used as sorbents in the sorption tests. Sorbents
(30.0 mg) were placed in plastic tubes, and 20 mL of

speed of 150 rpm in a mechanical shaker (Bio Shaker, BR43FL, TAITEC Corporation, Japan) at 25 °C for 1 day.
Subsequently, the sorbent and solution were separated by
ﬁltration, and the sorbents were freeze-dried to obtain
powdered samples.

3.2

Cobalt sorption behavior of HAp-ZE

The heavy metal removal capacity of HAp-ZE was
evaluated using Co2+ as a probe. Figure 4(a) shows the
time dependence of Co2+ removal from HAp-ZE using 1.0
mmol/L Co(NO3)2 aqueous solution. Co2+ was fast removed within the ﬁrst hour and reached a plateau, with the
removal remaining almost constant for up to 24 h. This
result suggests that HAp-ZE possesses an aﬃnity for Co2+
and enables its rapid removal from the solution.
The Co2+ sorption tests were conducted by shaking the
samples at 25 °C for 24 h in a 0.2–4.0 mmol/L Co(NO3)2
aqueous solution. Figure 4(b) presents the removal eﬃciencies of Co2+ on HAp-ZE and the reference samples.
HAp-ZE showed approximately 100 % removal at Co2+
concentrations up to 0.4 mmol/L when 30.0 mg of adsorbent and 20 mL of Co(NO3)2 aqueous solution were used.

HAp and zeolite are widely known as inorganic ionexchangers capable of removing cations through ionexchange reactions between the host metal ions of the
sorbents. Na-type zeolite ion-exchanges between host

a solution. Calcium ions (Ca2+) in HAp exchange with
divalent metal cations in the solution. Figures 5(a) and
5(b) show the concentrations of Na+ and Ca2+ released

HAp phase to Co2+ removal at higher Co2+ concentration.

4. Conclusions
In this work, we investigated the Co2+ sorption capacity
of HAp-ZE converted from blast furnace slag using batch
tests with 0.2–4.0 mmol/L Co(NO3)2 aqueous solution.
HAp-ZE removed nearly all of the Co2+ from the test
solution within the range of initial Co2+ concentrations up
to 0.4 mmol/L. The sorption isotherm for HAp-ZE was
well-ﬁtted to the Langmuir model, and the calculated
maximum sorption capacity (Qmax) was 0.69 mmol/g. The
Co2+ removal performance was in the order of Zeolite >
HAp-ZE > HAp. Notably, HAp-ZE exhibited a Co2+
sorption capacity equivalent to that of a physical mixture
of high-purity Zeolite and HAp. During the Co2+ sorption
tests, HAp-ZE released Na and Ca into solutions. Furthermore, FE-SEM and EDS analyses of HAp-ZE after the
tests revealed that no detectable precipitates were observed
and that Co was uniformly distributed throughout the particles. These results indicate that Co2+ removal by HApZE mainly occurs via an ion-exchange mechanism involving Na+ from the zeolite phase and Ca2+ from the HAp
phase. This study demonstrates that an upcycled HAp-ZE
composite exhibits Co2+ sorption performance comparable
to that of high-purity reference materials and clariﬁes its
ion-exchange-based removal mechanism through systematic post-sorption characterization. These ﬁndings highlight the potential of slag-derived HAp-ZE as a costeﬀective and sustainable sorbent for toxic metal ion
removal.
