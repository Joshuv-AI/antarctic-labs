Derivative estimates for SDEs with singular and unbounded coefficients Thanks: This work is supported by the National Key R&D Program of China (No. 2023YFA1010103) and the NNSF of China (No. 12471140, 12401182, 12595282).
Pengcheng Xia, Longjie Xie and Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. We develop a unified PDE-probabilistic framework for pointwise gradient and Hessian estimates of Markov semigroups associated with stochastic differential equations with singular and unbounded coefficients. Under mild local structural assumptions on the diffusion matrix and integrability/regularity conditions on the drift, we obtain quantitative sharp short-time regularization estimates as well as long-time decay bounds (including exponential and polynomial rates) for the first and second spatial derivatives of the semigroup. A distinctive feature of our results is the explicit dependence of these estimates on local norms of the coefficients (through scale-invariant quantities), without requiring any global smoothness, boundedness or uniform ellipticity. In particular, our approach allows for degenerate or highly irregular behavior at infinity, subject to suitable local ellipticity and Lyapunov/ergodicity controls. As applications, we establish solvability and regularity results for Poisson equations on the whole space with singular coefficients, and we derive pointwise gradient estimates for SDEs with distributional drifts via a Zvonkin-type transform. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60H10, 60F17, 35B40, 35B30. Keywords: Gradient estimate, Hessian estimate, Kolmogorov equation, Poisson equation, unbounded coefficients.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Derivative (or gradient) estimates for diffusion semigroups are fundamental in stochastic analysis and partial differential equation (PDE) theory. They provide
pointwise
control of the spatial derivatives of
\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi
in terms of
\varphi
itself, quantify the instantaneous regularization of the underlying stochastic dynamics, and, from the PDE viewpoint, correspond to a priori estimates for solutions of Kolmogorov equations. Beyond regularization, gradient estimates have far-reaching implications: they typically imply the strong Feller property; through their deep connection with the Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition, they lead to Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, which in turn govern the long-time behavior, convergence to equilibrium, and ergodic properties of the underlying stochastic system; see
[
1
]
and the references therein.

Consider the following time-homogeneous stochastic differential equation (SDE) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
W
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. Whenever (
1.1
) admits a weak solution
X_{t}(x)
for each
x
, the associated Markov semigroup is defined by

(1.2)

\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x):=\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi(X_{t}(x))\big],\quad t\geqslant 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A basic problem is to determine whether one has pointwise bounds of the form

(1.3)

|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)|\leqslant C(t,x)\,\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

and, more importantly for applications, to understand
quantitatively
how
C(t,x)
depends on the local behavior of the coefficients. For instance, if
\sigma={\mathbb{I}}_{d}
is the identity matrix and
|b(x)|\leqslant\kappa(1+|x|)
for some
\kappa>0
,
is it possible to establish the gradient estimate:

|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)|\leqslant C(1+|x|+t^{-1/2})\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\quad t\in(0,1),\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}?

Several influential approaches have been developed to establish such estimates.

(1) Malliavin calculus and Bismut formula.

Under global non-degeneracy and sufficient smoothness assumptions on the coefficients, Malliavin calculus leads to Bismut-type derivative formulas expressing
\nabla{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi
in terms of
\varphi(X_{t})
itself rather than derivatives of
\varphi
. More precisely, one has

(1.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X_{t}(x))\int^{t}_{0}(\sigma^{-1}(X_{s}(x))J_{s}(x))^{*}\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right],

where the asterisk denotes the transpose of a matrix, and
J_{t}(x):=\nabla_{x}X_{t}(x)=J_{t}
is the Jacobian flow satisfying

J_{t}=I_{d}+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla b(X_{s})J_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla\sigma(X_{s})J_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s},

and
I_{d}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix. This idea goes back to Bismut
[
3
]
and was developed systematically in the works of Elworthy–Li and many others; see
[
11
,
40
]
and the references therein. Such formulas yield short-time bounds of order
t^{-1/2}
and provide a versatile probabilistic representation for derivatives of solutions to linear and nonlinear PDEs. Using this formula, Thalmaier and Wang
[
41
, Theorem 6.1]
established gradient estimate (
1.3
) with explicit dependence of
C(t,x)
on the local
L^{\infty}
-norm of
b
and the local Ricci curvature bound around
x
. Moreover, under the global assumption
\nabla\sigma,b\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p>d
, a Bismut formula like (
1.4
) was established in
[
43
]
. Recently, Wang and Zhao
[
42
]
also derived gradient estimates for killed SDEs with irregular drift.

(2) Coupling methods.

This probabilistic approach estimates the finite difference

|{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)-{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(y)|/|x-y|

by constructing a coupling
(X_{t},Y_{t})
whose marginals solve the original diffusion started from
x
and
y
, and which coalesce after a random coupling time. It bypasses the need for differentiable coefficients, making it powerful for low-regularity settings. The seminal work of Priola and Wang
[
37
]
established a rigorous framework for coefficients that are unbounded and merely Hölder continuous. The key assumptions are the uniform ellipticity of
\sigma(x)
and a structural assumption like strict monotonicity:

\sup_{|x-y|=r}r^{-1}\big[\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|^{2}+\langle b(x)-b(y),x-y\rangle\big]\leqslant g(r),

where
g
is integrable near zero. This assumption is strictly weaker than global Lipschitz continuity. Under this framework, sharp gradient estimates of the form
\|\nabla{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leqslant c_{t}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
are obtained. Notably, when
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)ds<\infty
, one recovers the characteristic short-time bound
C/\sqrt{t}
. In
[
37
]
, these results also extend to Dirichlet problems and yield new Liouville-type theorems.

(3) Analytic/PDE methods.

The Bernstein method is a classical analytic technique used to derive gradient estimates for solutions of PDEs, particularly in the context of parabolic problems with unbounded coefficients. The core idea is to construct an auxiliary function that combines the solution and its gradient, and then apply the maximum principle to this auxiliary function. Formally,
u(t,x):={\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)
solves the following Kolmogorov equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)={\mathscr{L}}u(t,x),

where for
a(x)=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{*}
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}u(x):={\rm tr}\big(a(x)\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}u(x)\big)+b(x)\cdot\nabla_{x}u(x).

Using Bernstein’s technique, gradient estimates for
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi
with unbounded coefficients have been studied in
[
2
,
6
,
24
,
28
]
, see also
[
5
, Chapter 1]
. However, higher-order regularity of the coefficients are generally assumed in these works as their methods require differentiating the equations directly.

Despite the substantial literature, most existing works rely on global assumptions such as uniform ellipticity, global smoothness, or boundedness of coefficients, thereby excluding a wide range of physically and biologically relevant models where coefficients display localized singularities or superlinear growth at infinity. In these regimes, classical tools become much harder to apply, and there is a pressing need for
localized and quantitative
derivative estimates whose constants depend explicitly on
local behavior
of the coefficients. At the same time, while short-time gradient bounds with
t^{-1/2}
scaling are by now well understood in several frameworks,
long-time derivative estimates
are considerably less developed, especially for systems involving unbounded or singular coefficients. Under global smoothness and dissipativity assumptions, long-time gradient bounds have been obtained relatively recently, see e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, and such estimates are shown to play an essential structural role in uniform-in-time convergence results in various settings, such as Euler schemes and averaging limits
[
7
,
8
,
27
]
. For SDEs with unbounded, locally regular, and possibly singular coefficients, the lack of global smoothness obstructs Bismut-type formulas, while the absence of strong confinement limits classical coupling and spectral methods. Consequently, ,establishing long-time gradient estimates under merely local regularity remains a significant challenge.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we develop a unified quantitative framework that yields both
sharp short-time
regularization bounds and
long-time
decay estimates for the gradient and Hessian of the semigroup associated with (
1.1
), allowing for locally singular and unbounded coefficients. A key novelty is that the constants in our estimates depend
explicitly
, and only, on
local
norms of the coefficients and local ellipticity ratios, without imposing uniform ellipticity, global smoothness, or boundedness.

To formulate the assumptions and main results, for a function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
we introduce the weighted space

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\rho}:=\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Bigl\{\varphi:\ \|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\varphi(x)|}{\rho(x)}<+\infty\Bigr\}.

In particular, by (
1.8
), for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
, the semigroup (
1.2
) is well defined. For
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
B_{r}(x)
for the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at
x
with radius
r
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha\geqslant 0
, we denote by
L^{p}(B_{r}(x))
and
\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(B_{r}(x))
the usual
L^{p}
and Hölder spaces over
B_{r}(x)
, and similarly use
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for local spaces; see Section 2 for precise definitions.

We work under the following local assumptions and a non-explosion/moment condition:

(H
{}_{b}^{\sigma}
)

Suppose that
\sigma\in\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
and
b\in L^{p_{b}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p_{b}\in(d,\infty]
,
and there are two functions
0<\lambda(x)\leqslant\Lambda(x)<\infty
such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lambda(x)|\xi|^{2}\leqslant|\sigma(x)\xi|^{2}\leqslant\Lambda(x)|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there is a unique global solution
(X_{t}(x))_{t\geqslant 0}

to SDE (
1.1
),
and there exist two functions
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)

and an increasing continuous function
\ell_{0}:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\rho_{0}(X_{t}(x))\leqslant\ell_{0}(t)\,\rho_{1}(x),\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Remark 1.1
.

Under the above regularity assumptions on
\sigma
and
b
, together with the local ellipticity condition (
1.7
), local well-posedness of (
1.1
) is standard; see, e.g.,
[
43
]
. If
\rho_{0}=\rho_{1}\equiv 1
, then (
1.8
) holds trivially. More generally, if
\rho_{0}
is a Lyapunov-type function (i.e.,
\rho_{0}(x)\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
), then (
1.8
) implies non-explosion of solution.

Our first main result is the following estimates, which are sharp in the short-time scaling and whose constants depend explicitly, and locally, on the coefficients.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(H
{}_{b}^{\sigma}
)
holds, and
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
.

(i)

(Gradient estimates)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,d,p_{b},\alpha)>0

such that for all
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)|\lesssim_{C}\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\Gamma_{t}(x)}{\sqrt{t\wedge 1}}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{3\varepsilon+d/p_{b}}\ell_{0}(2t)\,\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho_{0}}},

where
\Lambda_{1}(x):=\Lambda(x)+1
, and for
a(x)=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{*}
and
\theta_{b}=1-d/p_{b}
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}(x):=\sqrt{t\wedge 1}\left[\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)[a]^{1/\alpha}_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}}{\lambda^{1+1/\alpha}(x)}+\frac{\|b\|^{1/\theta_{b}}_{L^{p_{b}}(B_{1}(x))}}{\lambda(x)^{1/\theta_{b}}}\right]+\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}.

(ii)

(Hessian estimates)

If, in addition,
b\in\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the same
\alpha
as in
(H
{}_{b}^{\sigma}
)
,
and for some
c_{0}\in(0,1)
,

c_{0}\lambda(x)\leqslant\inf_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\lambda(y)\leqslant\sup_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\Lambda(y)\leqslant c_{0}^{-1}\Lambda(x),

then for
\beta\in\{0,\alpha\}
,
there exists a constant
C=C(c_{0},d,\alpha)>0
such that for all
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}(B_{1/2}(x))}\lesssim_{C}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{t}(x)}{\sqrt{t\wedge 1}}\right)^{2+\beta}\ell_{0}(2t)\,\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho_{0}}},

where

(1.10)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{t}(x):=\sqrt{t\wedge 1}\left[\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)[a]^{1/\alpha}_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}}{\lambda^{1+1/\alpha}(x)}+\frac{\|b\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}}{\lambda(x)}\right]+\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}.

Remark 1.3
.

A noteworthy feature of the bounds in
Theorem

1.2
is their
local
dependence on the coefficients: the right-hand sides involve only local norms on
B_{1}(x)
, the local ellipticity ratio
\Lambda(x)/\lambda(x)
and the lower bound
\lambda(x)
. Of course, the semigroup value
\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_{t}(x))]
depends globally on the dynamics, but its
spatial derivatives at a point
can still be controlled by local information. Moreover,
Theorem

1.2
implies the sharp short-time asymptotics: for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\limsup_{t\to 0}\sqrt{t}\,\big|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)\big|\lesssim_{C}\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{1+3\varepsilon+d/p_{b}}\|\varphi\|_{\infty},

and

\limsup_{t\to 0}t\,\big|\nabla_{x}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)\big|\lesssim_{C}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{2}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

In particular, these leading-order short-time constants do not involve any norms of
a
or
b
.

Remark 1.4
.

A special case of part (i) above is that if
a(x)=\lambda I_{d}
and
b\in L^{p_{b}}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p_{b}\in(d,\infty]
, then there is a constant
C=C(d,p_{b},\lambda)>0
such that for all
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)|\lesssim_{C}\ell_{0}(2t)\,\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t\wedge 1}}+\|b\|_{L^{p_{b}}(B_{1}(x))}^{1/\theta_{b}}\right)\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho_{0}}}.

In particular, if, in addition,
b
has polynomial growth, i.e.,
|b(x)|\lesssim_{C}1+|x|^{m}
, then

|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}\varphi(x)|\lesssim_{C}\ell_{0}(2t)\,\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t\wedge 1}}+|x|^{m}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Although the above estimates hold for all
t\geqslant 0
, the function
\ell_{0}
does not usually decay as
t\to\infty
. To establish decay estimates, we impose an additional, natural ergodicity assumption:

(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′

In addition to
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
, there exists a unique invariant probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)<\infty
, and a decreasing function
\ell_{1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\ell_{1}(t)=0
such that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
,

(1.11)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)-\mu(\varphi)|\leqslant\ell_{1}(t)\,\rho_{1}(x)\,\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}},\ (t,x)\in(1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}
are the same weights as in (
1.8
).

The next theorem shows that the gradient and Hessian of the semigroup decay at the same rate as
\ell_{1}(t)
, thereby quantitatively connecting short-time regularization with long-time stabilization.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
(H
{}_{b}^{\sigma}
)
′
holds, and
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
.

(i)

(Long-time gradient estimates)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,d,p_{b},\alpha)>0

such that for all
(t,x)\in(2,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)\right|\lesssim_{C}\,\ell_{1}(t-1)\,\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{3\varepsilon+d/p_{b}}\Gamma_{1}(x)\,\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}},

where
\ell_{1}
comes from (
1.11
) and
\Gamma_{1}(x)
is given by (
1.9
) with
t=1
.

(ii)

(Long-time Hessian estimates)

If, in addition, we assume the same conditions as in Theorem
1.2
(ii), then for
\beta\in\{0,\alpha\}
there exists a constant
C=C(c_{0},d,\alpha)>0
such that for all
(t,x)\in[2,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi]_{\mathbf{C}^{\beta}(B_{1/2}(x))}\lesssim_{C}\ell_{1}(t-1)\,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}^{2+\beta}(x)\,\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}},

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}(x)
is given by (
1.10
) with
t=1
.

Our method to prove the above estimates is quite unified, and both the regularity assumptions on the coefficients and the dissipative condition for the long-time estimates are much weaker than existing results in the literature. In fact, we work at the level of the more general time-inhomogeneous Kolmogorov equation

(1.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+{\mathscr{L}}_{t}u(t,x)+f(t,x)=0,

with

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{t}u(x):={\rm tr}\big(a(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}u(x)\big)+b(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}u(x).

The backbone of our argument is to establish pointwise control of
|\nabla_{x}u(t,x)|
and
|\nabla^{2}_{x}u(t,x)|
for solutions to (
1.14
) in terms of
u(t,x)
itself; see Theorems
3.3
and
3.5
below. This is achieved by deriving new a priori local
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
maximal regularity and local Schauder estimates for (
1.14
) with unbounded coefficients, where the dependence on coefficients is made explicit via scaling and interpolation, and then applying embedding theorems on domains with a novel scale selection procedure that determines the intrinsic radius based on local ellipticity and regularity norms. The short-time estimates in Theorem
1.2
follow via probabilistic representations of solutions. As a consequence, we also obtain short-time gradient estimates for time-inhomogeneous SDEs; see Theorems
4.6
and
4.7
below. The long-time estimates in Theorem
1.5
follow from the quantitative short-time bounds, the semigroup property, and the ergodic decay assumption, thereby linking short-time regularization and long-time stabilization.

(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}

, in particular the ergodic bound (

.

(Lyapunov condition and exponential ergodicity)

Example 1.7
.

(Geometric drift condition and polynomial ergodicity)

Assume that
\sigma(x)
is uniformly elliptic, i.e., there exists
0<\lambda<1
such that

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leqslant|\sigma(x)\xi|^{2}\leqslant\lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and the drift

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\langle}x,b(x){\rangle}=-\infty.

Then
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′
holds with
\rho_{0}(x)=1+|x|^{m}
for any
m>0
,
\ell_{1}(t)=t^{-\gamma}
for any

, and
\rho_{1}(x)=c(1+|x|^{m+\beta})

\mathbb{E}|X_{t}(x)|^{m}\leqslant C_{d}(1+|x|^{m}).

\beta=n/q
, and deduce that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{t}\varphi(x)-\mu(\varphi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)\big[\mathbb{P}\circ X_{t}(x)^{-1}(\mathrm{d}y)-\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\big]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\big[\mathbb{P}\circ X_{t}(x)^{-1}(\mathrm{d}y)+\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\big]\right)^{1/p}\,\|\mathbb{P}\circ X_{t}(x)^{-1}-\mu\|_{\rm var}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{d}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}(1+|x|^{mp})^{1/p}(1+|x|^{n})^{1/q}(1+t)^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{d}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}(1+|x|^{m+\beta})(1+t)^{-\gamma},

which yields (
1.11
).

1.3.
Applications and future perspectives

The explicit and local nature of our estimates is particularly useful in inhomogeneous, unbounded or singular environments: it enables robust control under truncation, scaling, and localization, and is therefore well-suited to problems in homogenization and averaging, stability analysis, and numerical approximation of singular stochastic systems. Below, we first highlight two concrete applications and then outline several methodological directions enabled by our framework.

1.3.1.
Poisson equation in the whole space.

The long-time derivative estimates allow us to study the Poisson/corrector equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.17)

\displaystyle-{\mathscr{L}}u(x)=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
{\mathscr{L}}
is given by (
1.5
) and is the generator of (
1.1
). Equations of the form (
1.17
) are also referred to as the cell problems in periodic homogenization, and are known to be a key tool in stochastic averaging, diffusion approximation, moderate and large deviations, and numerical approximation of invariant measures for SDEs; see, e.g.,
[
29
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
. The well-posedness and regularity of
u
were studied in
[
32
,
33
]
; see also
[
38
]
and references therein. However, these works typically impose boundedness-type assumptions, which restrict applicability. More recently, under dissipativity assumptions,
[
7
]
studied (
1.17
) with unbounded coefficients, but still required uniform ellipticity and higher-order regularity of
\sigma
,
b
, and the forcing
f
.

We establish well-posedness of (
1.17
) in local Sobolev spaces for singular and unbounded coefficients, and provide quantitative gradient and Schauder-type bounds for the solution
u
with explicit dependence on local coefficient norms and local ellipticity ratios; see Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 below. These quantitative estimates are crucial in proving uniform-in-time convergence of multi-scale problems: in averaging/homogenization and diffusion approximation, effective coefficients and error terms are typically expressed through correctors solving (
1.17
) (see
[
44
]
).

1.3.2.
SDEs with distributional drifts.

As an illustration of the scope of our localized framework, we apply the main results to derive gradient estimates for SDEs with distributional drift:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=(b_{1}+b_{2})(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

where
b_{1}
belongs to a negative-order Sobolev/Bessel potential class and
b_{2}
has at most linear growth. Such models arise naturally in fluid dynamics and rough path theory, and classical approaches to gradient estimates break down due to the lack of pointwise meaning of the drift (see
[
4
,
14
,
17
]
and references therein). Using a Zvonkin-type transform, we remove the singular component
b_{1}
and reduce the equation to an SDE with Hölder diffusion and unbounded drift, to which our theorems apply. This yields, to the best of our knowledge, the first pointwise gradient estimate for semigroups associated with SDEs whose drift lies in negative-order Sobolev spaces; see Theorem 6.3 below.

1.3.3.
Methodological perspectives

Beyond the specific applications presented above, our framework provides a foundation for several research directions:

•

Nonlinear PDEs via linearization.

Many nonlinear (quasilinear) parabolic equations reduce locally to Kolmogorov-type operators after linearization. Quantitative a priori bounds with explicit dependence on local coefficient norms provide natural input for bootstrap and continuation arguments. This perspective is closely related to the classical theory of linear and quasilinear parabolic equations
[
25
,
26
]
and to modern
L^{p}
-based regularity theories for rough coefficients
[
21
,
20
]
.

•

Homogenization, averaging, and diffusion approximation.

Quantitative weak convergence often reduces to controlling correctors and remainder terms expressed through Poisson equations and semigroup expansions. Our localized short-time derivative bounds enable precise error control in multi-scale expansions even when global ellipticity or regularity fails; see
[
18
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
for representative frameworks where Poisson equations are structural. Moreover, the precise long-time bounds fit naturally into error decompositions where the time horizon must be controlled uniformly, see
[
44
]
.

•

Quantitative numerical analysis.

Weak error analysis for discretization schemes requires derivative estimates for backward Kolmogorov equations. Our results naturally feed into local weak error bounds under unbounded coefficients via Lyapunov weights, and into long-time error estimates (such as approximation of invariant measures) when combined with ergodic decay. For classical weak error expansions and numerical perspectives, see
[
29
,
39
,
36
]
and references therein.

•

Sensitivity and robustness.

Pointwise gradient bounds quantify the stability of expectations under perturbations, which is crucial for parameter sensitivity and robustness of Markov semigroups under coefficient perturbations. Because our constants are local, stability can be proved by localization and patching arguments, a viewpoint compatible with Harris-type ergodic frameworks and perturbation arguments; see e.g.
[
10
,
30
]
.

•

Stochastic control with rough coefficients.

In stochastic control, verification and regularity of feedback controls depend on spatial derivatives of the value function (solutions to HJB equations). Localized semigroup regularization estimates provide building blocks for mollified verification and stability of near-optimal controls when coefficients are unbounded or rough; see, e.g.,
[
13
]
for the viscosity-solution approach.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces notation and analytic tools. Section
3
develops local PDE estimates for Kolmogorov equations. Section 4 derives pointwise gradient and Hessian estimates for the SDE semigroup. Sections 5 and 6 present the applications to Poisson equations and SDEs with distributional drifts, respectively.

C
denotes a positive constant whose value may change from line to line. We write

to mean

for some inessential constant

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the local Sobolev and Hölder spaces, together with several interpolation inequalities that will be used repeatedly below.

2.1.
Local Sobolev spaces and anisotropic interpolation

Let
Q=I\times\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval and
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open domain. For
q,p\in[1,\infty]
, we define the anisotropic Lebesgue norm

\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}_{p}(Q)}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\mathcal{O}))}:=\left(\int_{I}\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(t,x)|^{p}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{q/p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q},

with the usual modifications when
q=\infty
or
p=\infty
. When
Q=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we simply write

\|f\|_{q,p}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},\qquad\|f\|_{\infty}:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Let
{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p}(Q)
denote the anisotropic Sobolev space consisting of all measurable functions
f
on
Q
such that
\partial_{t}f
and
\nabla_{x}^{2}f
exist in the weak sense and

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p}(Q)}:=\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}_{p}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}_{p}(Q)}+\|\nabla_{x}^{2}f\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}_{p}(Q)}<\infty.

We also introduce the local Sobolev space

{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;\mathrm{loc}}(Q):=\big\{u:\ u\phi\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p}(Q)\ \text{for all }\phi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(Q)\big\}.

Similarly,
{\mathbb{W}}^{2}_{p}(\mathcal{O})
and
{\mathbb{W}}^{2}_{p;\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{O})
denote the global and local Sobolev spaces over
\mathcal{O}
, respectively.

We record the following Gagliardo–Nirenberg type anisotropic interpolation inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1.

Let
q_{0}\in[q,\infty]
and
p_{0}\in[p,\infty]
, and define

\theta:=1-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}+\frac{2}{q_{0}}.

Then there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(d,p,q,p_{0},q_{0})>0
such that for
any
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
,

(2.1)

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{q_{0},p_{0}}\lesssim_{C_{1}}\varepsilon_{1}\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p}+\varepsilon_{2}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{q,p}+\varepsilon_{1}^{(d/p_{0}-1-d/p)/\theta}\varepsilon_{2}^{(2/q_{0}-2/q)/\theta}\|u\|_{q,p}.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
and define the parabolic rescaling

\widetilde{u}(t,x):=u(\lambda_{1}t,\lambda_{2}x).

Since
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
, it is known that there exists a constant
C=C(d,p,q,p_{0},q_{0})>0
such that (see
[
23
, Lemma 10.2]
)

(2.2)

\|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{u}\|_{q_{0},p_{0}}\lesssim_{C}\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}+\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}+\|\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}.

A direct computation gives the scaling relations

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{u}\|_{q_{0},p_{0}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}^{1-d/p_{0}}\lambda_{1}^{-1/q_{0}}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{q_{0},p_{0}},\qquad\|\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}=\lambda_{2}^{-d/p}\lambda_{1}^{-1/q}\|u\|_{q,p},

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}^{2-d/p}\lambda_{1}^{-1/q}\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p},\qquad\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}\|_{q,p}=\lambda_{2}^{-d/p}\lambda_{1}^{1-1/q}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{q,p}.

Substituting into (
2.2
) yields

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{q_{0},p_{0}}&\lesssim_{C}\lambda_{2}^{1+d/p_{0}-d/p}\lambda_{1}^{1/q_{0}-1/q}\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p}\\
&\quad+\lambda_{2}^{d/p_{0}-d/p-1}\lambda_{1}^{1+1/q_{0}-1/q}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{q,p}\\
&\quad+\lambda_{2}^{d/p_{0}-d/p-1}\lambda_{1}^{1/q_{0}-1/q}\|u\|_{q,p}.\end{split}

Now choose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that

\lambda_{2}^{1+d/p_{0}-d/p}\lambda_{1}^{1/q_{0}-1/q}=\varepsilon_{1},\qquad\lambda_{2}^{d/p_{0}-d/p-1}\lambda_{1}^{1+1/q_{0}-1/q}=\varepsilon_{2}.

Solving these equations gives

\lambda_{2}=\varepsilon_{1}^{(1+1/q_{0}-1/q)/\theta}\,\varepsilon_{2}^{(1/q-1/q_{0})/\theta},\qquad\lambda_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}^{(1+d/p-d/p_{0})/\theta}\,\varepsilon_{2}^{(1+d/p_{0}-d/p)/\theta},

where
\theta=1-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}+\frac{2}{q_{0}}
.
Consequently,

(2.4)

\lambda_{2}^{d/p_{0}-d/p-1}\lambda_{1}^{1/q_{0}-1/q}=\varepsilon_{1}^{(d/p_{0}-1-d/p)/\theta}\,\varepsilon_{2}^{(2/q_{0}-2/q)/\theta}.

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) gives the desired estimate (
2.1
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1
.
For
p_{0}\in[p,\infty]
, the estimate

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{q,p_{0}}\lesssim_{C}\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p}+\varepsilon^{(d/p_{0}-1-d/p)/\theta_{0}}\|u\|_{q,p},\quad\theta_{0}:=1-\tfrac{d}{p}+\tfrac{d}{p_{0}},

follows from the classical (spatial) Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality applied to
u(t,\cdot)
for a.e.
t
and then integrated in time; see, e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.2.
Hölder spaces in a domain

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open domain. For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
we use
\mathbf{C}^{k+\alpha}(\mathcal{O})
to denote the Hölder spaces over
\mathcal{O}
with norm

\|f\|_{\mathbf{C}^{k+\alpha}(\mathcal{O})}:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}+\cdots+\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}+[\nabla^{k}f]_{\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O})},

where

[\nabla^{k}f]_{\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O})}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{|\nabla^{k}f(x)-\nabla^{k}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

We shall use the conventions

[f]_{\alpha}:=[f]_{\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\qquad\|f\|_{\alpha}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+[f]_{\alpha}.

The local Hölder space is defined by

\mathbf{C}^{k+\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{O}):=\{f:\ f\phi\in\mathbf{C}^{k+\alpha}(\mathcal{O})\ \text{for all }\phi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{O})\}.

We recall the following iteration lemma (cf.
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
), which will be used for proving our local
L^{p}
-maximal regularity estimates and Schauder’s estimates.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0\leqslant\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}\leqslant 1
,
A,B>0
, and let
h:[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]\to[0,\infty)
be bounded. Suppose that for some
\gamma\geqslant 0
and
\vartheta\in(0,1)
,

h(\tau)\leqslant\vartheta\,h(\tau^{\prime})+(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)^{-\gamma}A+B,\qquad\tau_{1}\leqslant\tau<\tau^{\prime}\leqslant\tau_{2}.

Then there exists
C=C(\gamma,\vartheta)>0
such that for all
\tau_{1}\leqslant\tau<\tau^{\prime}\leqslant\tau_{2}
,

h(\tau)\lesssim_{C}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)^{-\gamma}A+B.

We also need the following interpolation inequality in Hölder spaces, which is used in local Schauder estimates.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0\leqslant\beta\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
. For any
j=0,1,2
,
there exist constants
C=C(d,j,\beta,\alpha)>0

and
\gamma=\gamma(j,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that for all
u\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2+\alpha}(B_{1})
,

\varepsilon>0
and
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma<\tau\leqslant 1
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle[\nabla^{j}u]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}(B_{\sigma})}\lesssim_{C}\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{\tau})}+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}[\varepsilon^{2+\alpha}(\tau-\sigma)^{-\gamma}+1]\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\tau})}.

Proof.

It is well known that the following global interpolation inequality holds (cf.
[
19
, Theorem 3.2.1]
):
for some
C=C(d,j,\beta,\alpha)>0
,

[\nabla^{j}u]_{\beta}\lesssim_{C}[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha}+\|u\|_{\infty}.

For
\varepsilon>0
define

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=u(\varepsilon x).

Then

[\nabla^{j}u_{\varepsilon}]_{\beta}=\varepsilon^{j+\beta}[\nabla^{j}u]_{\beta}.

Thus

\varepsilon^{j+\beta}[\nabla^{j}u]_{\beta}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2+\alpha}[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha}+\|u\|_{\infty}.

Dividing both sides by
\varepsilon^{j+\beta}
, we derive

(2.6)

\displaystyle[\nabla^{j}u]_{\beta}\lesssim_{C}\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}\|u\|_{\infty}.

Fix
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma<\tau\leqslant 1
.
Let
\eta_{0}(x)
and
\eta_{1}(x)
be smooth cut-off functions supported in
B_{(\tau+\sigma)/2}

and
B_{\tau}
, respectively, and satisfy that

0\leqslant\eta_{0}\leqslant 1\,\,\,{\text{in}}\,\,\,B_{(\tau+\sigma)/2},\ \ \eta_{0}\equiv 1\,\,{\text{in}}\,\,B_{\sigma},

and

0\leqslant\eta_{1}\leqslant 1\,\,\,{\text{in}}\,\,\,B_{\tau},\ \ \eta_{1}\equiv 1\,\,{\text{in}}\,\,B_{(\tau+\sigma)/2},

and for any
j=0,1,2
and
\beta\in[0,1]
, there is a
C=C(j,\beta,d)>0
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle[\nabla^{j}\eta_{i}]_{\beta}\leqslant C(\tau-\sigma)^{-j-\beta},\ \ i=0,1.

Let
\upsilon:=(\tau+\sigma)/2
and write

\|\cdot\|_{\beta;\sigma}:=\|\cdot\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}(B_{\sigma})},\ \ \|\cdot\|_{\infty;\sigma}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\sigma})}.

Now by (
2.6
), the chain rule and (
2.7
), we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle[\nabla^{j}u]_{\beta;\sigma}

\displaystyle\leqslant[\nabla^{j}(u\eta_{0})]_{\beta}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}[\nabla^{2}(u\eta_{0})]_{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}\|u\eta_{0}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}\left([\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\upsilon}+[\nabla_{x}u]_{\alpha;\upsilon}\|\nabla\eta_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\infty;\upsilon}[\nabla\eta_{0}]_{\alpha}\right)+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}\|u\|_{\infty;\upsilon}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}\left([\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\upsilon}+(\tau-\sigma)^{-1-\alpha}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\upsilon}\right)+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}\|u\|_{\infty;\upsilon}.

In particular, if one takes
j=1,\beta=\alpha
and replaces
\varepsilon
by
(\varepsilon(\tau-\sigma))^{1+\alpha}
, then

[\nabla_{x}u]_{\alpha;\sigma}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}\left([\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\tau}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\tau}\right)+(\varepsilon(\tau-\sigma))^{-(1+\alpha)^{2}}\|u\|_{\infty;\tau};

and if one takes
j=1,\beta=0
and replaces
\varepsilon
by
\varepsilon(\tau-\sigma)
, then

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\infty;\sigma}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}\left([\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\tau}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\tau}\right)+(\varepsilon(\tau-\sigma))^{-1}\|u\|_{\infty;\tau}.

Combining the above two estimates and taking
\varepsilon
to be small enough, we obtain
that for some
C=C(d,\alpha)>0
and all
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma<\tau\leqslant 1
,

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\sigma}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\tau}+[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\tau}+C(\tau-\sigma)^{-(1+\alpha)^{2}}\|u\|_{\infty;\tau}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we get

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\alpha;\upsilon}\lesssim[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{\alpha;\tau}+(\tau-\sigma)^{-(1+\alpha)^{2}}\|u\|_{\infty;\tau}.

Substituting this into (
2.8
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

By (
2.5
), for any
0\leqslant\beta\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
,
j=0,1,2
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

[\nabla^{j}u]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}(B_{1/2})}\lesssim_{C}\varepsilon^{2-j+\alpha-\beta}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1})}+\varepsilon^{-j-\beta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

3.
Kolmogorov equations with unbounded coefficients

This section is devoted to establishing a priori local regularity estimates for solutions to time-inhomogeneous Kolmogorov equations with unbounded and possibly singular coefficients. A principal novelty is that all bounds come with explicit dependence on local norms of the coefficients, which is crucial for applications.

Let
Q:=I\times\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a space-time domain, where
I=(S,T)\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval and
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open domain. We consider the time-inhomogeneous Kolmogorov equation in
Q
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+{\mathscr{L}}_{t}u(t,x)+f(t,x)=0,

where

{\mathscr{L}}_{t}u(x):={\rm tr}\big(a(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}u(x)\big)+b(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}u(x).

Here
a:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a symmetric matrix-valued measurable function, and
b:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable vector field. Throughout this section we impose the following local regularity and ellipticity assumption on
a
:

(H
{}^{\alpha}_{a}
)

Suppose that
a\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}\big(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{C}_{\text{loc}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and there exist two functions
0<\lambda(x)\leqslant\Lambda(x)<\infty
such that,
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lambda(x)|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\langle a(t,x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leqslant\Lambda(x)|\xi|^{2}.

We first define strong solutions to (
3.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty)
. A function
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;{\rm loc}}(Q)
is called a strong solution
of (
3.1
) in
Q
if
{\mathscr{L}}_{\cdot}u+f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Q)
and for every

\phi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(Q)
,

\langle\!\langle u,\partial_{t}\phi\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle{\mathscr{L}}_{\cdot}u+f,\phi\rangle\!\rangle,

where

\langle\!\langle f,g\rangle\!\rangle:=\int_{Q}f(t,x)g(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

Equivalently, for every
g\in\mathbf{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
and for a.e.
S<t<s<T
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle u(s),g\rangle=\langle u(t),g\rangle-\int_{t}^{s}\langle{\mathscr{L}}_{r}u(r)+f(r),g\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

where

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathcal{O}}f(x)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Remark 3.2
.

If (
3.2
) holds with
s=T
(in the above sense) and
u(T)=\varphi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{O})
, then
u
is called a solution to (
3.1
) with terminal value
u(T)=\varphi
.

To state the main results, we recall the parabolic cylinder with center
(t,x)
and radius
R>0
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle{Q}_{R}(t,x):=I_{R}(t)\times B_{R}(x):=(t-R^{2},t+R^{2})\times B_{R}(x).

Moreover, we also use the following notations:

\|f\|_{{{\mathbb{L}}}^{q}_{p}(Q_{R}(t,x))}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}(I_{R}(t);L^{p}(B_{R}(x)))}

and

[f]_{{L}^{\infty}\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(Q_{R}(t,x))}:=\sup_{s\in I_{R}(t)}[f(s,\cdot)]_{\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(B_{R}(x))}.

The first main result of this section provides an a priori pointwise gradient bound for the solution with constants depending explicitly on local norms of the coefficients.

Theorem 3.3

(Gradient estimate)
.

Suppose that
(H
{}^{\alpha}_{a}
)
holds, and
b\in L^{q_{b}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};L^{p_{b}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
q_{b},p_{b}\in(1,\infty]
with

\theta_{b}:=1-\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\frac{2}{q_{b}}>0
. Let

q\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,q_{b}]
and
p\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,p_{b}]
satisfy
\theta:=1-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}>0.

For given
S<T
, there exists a constant
C=C(d,p,q,p_{b},q_{b},\alpha)>0
such that for any solution

u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;{\rm loc}}\big((S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
of PDE (
3.1
) and
(t,x)\in[\frac{T+S}{2},T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}u(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\Lambda^{1/q}_{1}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)R}\right)^{2-\theta}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R}(t,x))}+\frac{R^{\theta}\Lambda_{1}^{1/q}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R}(t,x))},

where
\Lambda_{1}(x):=\Lambda(x)+1
,

R_{0}:=\frac{\sqrt{T-t}}{\sqrt{T-S}\vee 1}
and

Remark 3.4
.

Suppose that

and

It is worth noting that by linearization, the above estimate could be used to derive some a priori regularity estimates for HJB equations (see

3.1.
Local
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-maximal regularity and Schauder’s estimates

The purpose of this subsection is to establish a priori local Sobolev (maximal
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-regularity) and Schauder-type estimates for solutions to (
3.1
). These estimates will later be combined with embedding arguments to derive pointwise bounds on
\nabla u
and
\nabla^{2}u
.

Let
a(t):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, symmetric, positive-definite matrix-valued function. For
t<s
define

X_{t,s}:=\int^{s}_{t}\sqrt{2a(r)}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r},

where
(W_{r})_{r\in\mathbb{R}}
is a Brownian motion, and for
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

P_{t,s}\varphi(x):=\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(x+X_{t,s}).

Itô’s formula yields, for Lebesgue almost every
s>t
,

\partial_{s}P_{t,s}\varphi(x)={\rm tr}\big(a(s)\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x}P_{t,s}\varphi(x)\big),

and for Lebesgue almost every
t<s
,

\partial_{t}P_{t,s}\varphi(x)=-{\rm tr}\big(a(t)\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x}P_{t,s}\varphi(x)\big).

Moreover, if
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R};C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and we set for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

u(t,x):=\int^{\infty}_{t}P_{t,s}f(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\int^{\infty}_{t}\mathbb{E}\,f(s,x+X_{t,s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

then
u
satisfies the following equation on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+{\rm tr}\big(a(t)\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x}u(t,x)\big)+f(t,x)=0.

We first employ probabilistic methods to study solutions of (
3.10
). The novelty lies in the explicit dependence of the estimates on the ellipticity constant.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that for some
0<\lambda\leqslant\Lambda<\infty
,

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\langle a(t)\xi,\xi\rangle\leqslant\Lambda|\xi|^{2},\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(i)

(
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-maximal regularity estimate)

For any
q,p\in(1,\infty)
there exists a constant
C=C(d,q,p)>0
such that for all
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p}\lesssim_{C}\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_{q,p}.

(ii)

(Schauder estimate)

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
C=C(d,\alpha)>0
such that for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R};{\mathbf{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle[\nabla_{x}^{2}u]_{\infty,\alpha}\lesssim_{C}\lambda^{-1}[f]_{\infty,\alpha},

where
[f]_{\infty,\alpha}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}[f(t,\cdot)]_{\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Proof.

(i) By a standard density argument we may assume
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R};C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
First consider the case
a(t)=\lambda I_{d}
with
\lambda>0
. Define

u_{\lambda}(t,x):=u(t,\sqrt{\lambda}x),\qquad f_{\lambda}(t,x):=f(t,\sqrt{\lambda}x).

The chain rule gives

\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}(t,x)+\Delta u_{\lambda}(t,x)+f_{\lambda}(t,x)=0.

By
[
20
, p.107, Theorem 7]
there exists a constant
C=C(d,q,p)>0
such that

\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u_{\lambda}\|_{q,p}\lesssim_{C}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{q,p}.

A change of variables then yields, for every
\lambda>0
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p}\lesssim_{C}\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_{q,p}.

Now let
W^{(1)}_{t}
and
W^{(2)}_{t}
be two independent
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motions. For
t<s
set

X^{(1)}_{t,s}:=\int^{s}_{t}\sqrt{2\bigl(a(r)-\lambda I_{d}\bigr)}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{(1)}_{r},\quad X^{(2)}_{t,s}:=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,(W^{(2)}_{s}-W^{(2)}_{t}).

Since the ellipticity lower bound implies
a(r)-\lambda I_{d}
is positive semidefinite,
\sqrt{a(r)-\lambda I_{d}}
is well-defined.
For
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we also set

P^{(i)}_{t,s}\varphi(x):=\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(x+X^{(i)}_{t,s}),\quad i=1,2.

Since
X^{(1)}_{t,s}
and
X^{(2)}_{t,s}
are independent,

P_{t,s}\varphi(x)=P^{(1)}_{t,s}P^{(2)}_{t,s}\varphi(x).

Hence

u(t,x)=\int^{\infty}_{t}P_{t,s}f(s,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\int^{\infty}_{t}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(P^{(2)}_{t,s}f\bigl(s,x+X^{(1)}_{0,s}-X^{(1)}_{0,t}\bigr)\Bigr){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=:\mathbb{E}\,\widetilde{u}\bigl(t,x-X^{(1)}_{0,t}\bigr),

where we adopt the convention
X_{0,s}:=-X_{s,0}
for
s<0
, and

\widetilde{u}(t,x):=\int^{\infty}_{t}P^{(2)}_{t,s}f\bigl(s,x+X^{(1)}_{0,s}\bigr){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Using Fubini’s theorem and estimate (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u(t)\|^{q}_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}\widetilde{u}(t,\cdot+X^{(1)}_{0,t})\|^{q}_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}\widetilde{u}(t)\|^{q}_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-q}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(s,\cdot+X^{(1)}_{0,s})\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)=\lambda^{-q}\|f\|^{q}_{q,p},

which proves (
3.11
).

(ii) Estimate (
3.12
) follows by the same decomposition argument, combined with the Schauder estimate for the constant diffusion
\lambda I_{d}
and the stability under translations; see
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
. The factor
\lambda^{-1}
is obtained via the same scaling as in (i).
∎

Remark 3.7

Theorem 3.8

(Local

Lemma

If

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}^{2}u\|_{q,p;3/4}\lesssim_{C_{1}}\frac{\Lambda^{2}_{1}}{\lambda^{2}}\|{u}\|_{q,p;1}+\frac{\|f\|_{q,p;1}}{\lambda}.

Step 5. Parabolic scaling.

∎

Theorem 3.9

\displaystyle[\nabla_{x}^{2}u]_{\infty,\alpha;{R/2}}\lesssim_{C}\Bigg(\frac{\Lambda_{1}/R+\|b\|_{\infty;R}+R^{\alpha}[b]_{\infty,\alpha;R}}{\lambda}\Bigg)^{2+\alpha}\|u\|_{\infty;R}+\frac{\|f\|_{\infty;R}+R^{\alpha}[f]_{\infty,\alpha;R}}{R^{\alpha}\lambda},

Proof.

Step 1. Reduction to

)–(

Besides (

\displaystyle[\eta]_{\alpha}\leqslant C(\tau-\sigma)^{-\alpha},\ [\nabla\eta]_{\alpha}\leqslant C(\tau-\sigma)^{-1-\alpha},\ [\nabla^{2}\eta]_{\alpha}+[\partial_{t}\eta_{0}]_{\alpha}\leqslant C(\tau-\sigma)^{-2-\alpha}.

solves on

Step 2. Estimating the forcing term

Using the product estimate for time-uniform Hölder seminorms,

\displaystyle[\widetilde{g}]_{\infty,\alpha;\upsilon}\lesssim\;

\displaystyle[g]_{\infty,\alpha;\upsilon}+(\tau-\sigma)^{-\alpha}\|g\|_{\infty;\upsilon}+\Lambda_{1}(\tau-\sigma)^{-2}[u]_{\infty,\alpha;\upsilon}+\Lambda_{1}(\tau-\sigma)^{-2-\alpha}\|u\|_{\infty;\upsilon}

(3.35)

\displaystyle+\Lambda_{1}(\tau-\sigma)^{-1}[\nabla_{x}u]_{\infty,\alpha;\upsilon}+\Lambda_{1}(\tau-\sigma)^{-1-\alpha}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\infty;\upsilon}.

\displaystyle+[f]_{\infty,\alpha;\tau}+(\tau-\sigma)^{-\alpha}\|f\|_{\infty;\tau}\Big\},

where

\frac{|\nabla_{x}^{2}u(t,y)-\nabla_{x}^{2}u(t,y^{\prime})|}{|y-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\leqslant[\nabla_{x}^{2}u(t,\cdot)]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{R_{2}/2}(y))}.

Applying (
3.40
) to the cylinder
Q_{R_{2}}(t,y)
, and by (
3.45
) and (
3.44
),
we get

\displaystyle[\nabla_{x}^{2}u(t,\cdot)]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{R_{2}/2}(y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(y)}{\lambda(y)R_{2}}\Big)^{2+\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R_{2}}(t,y))}+\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R_{2}}(t,y))}}{\lambda(y)R_{2}^{\alpha}}+\frac{[f]_{L^{\infty}{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(Q_{R_{2}}(t,y))}}{\lambda(y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)R}\Big)^{2+\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{Q}_{R}(t,x))}+\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{Q}_{R}(t,x))}}{\lambda(x)R^{\alpha}}+\frac{[f]_{L^{\infty}{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(\tilde{Q}_{R}(t,x))}}{\lambda(x)}.

Combining the above two cases,
we obtain the desired estimate (
3.9
).
∎

4.
Pointwise gradient and Hessian estimates for SDEs

In this section we combine the local PDE estimates obtained in Theorems
3.3
and
3.5

with probabilistic representations to derive pointwise gradient and Hessian bounds for solutions of SDEs
whose coefficients may be unbounded or singular.

4.1.
Probabilistic solutions of Kolmogorov equations

Throughout this and the next subsection, we fix
-\infty<S<T<\infty
and a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion

(W_{t})_{t\in[S,T]}
.
Consider the time-inhomogeneous SDE

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t,s}(x)=b\big(s,X_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}\,\sigma\big(s,X_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},\qquad X_{t,t}(x)=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
S\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T
, and

b:[S,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\sigma:[S,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable.
We always set

a(t,x):=(\sigma\sigma^{*})(t,x).

We begin with a local Sobolev regularity statement for probabilistic solutions.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and let
(q_{b},p_{b})\in(1,\infty]
satisfy
\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}+\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}<1
.
Assume that
\sigma
is uniformly elliptic, i.e.,
\lambda I_{d}\leqslant a(t,x)\leqslant\Lambda I_{d}
, and

\sigma\in L^{\infty}\!\big([S,T];\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),\qquad{b\in L^{q_{b}}\big([S,T];L^{p_{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big).}

Let
q\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,q_{b}]
and
p\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,p_{b}]
satisfy

\tfrac{d}{p}+\tfrac{2}{q}<2.

Given
f\in L^{q}([S,T];L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

\displaystyle u(t,x):=\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f\big(s,X_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

Then

u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;\mathrm{loc}}\big((S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
solves (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
Moreover, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\bigl(0,\frac{T-S}{2}\bigr)
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u\|_{L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m}))}\lesssim_{C_{t,S,T,m}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T]\times B_{2m})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}([S,T];L^{p}(B_{2m}))},

where
C_{t,S,T,m}>0
depends only on
t,S,T,m,p,q,p_{b},q_{b},d
,
the ellipticity constants of
\sigma
on
[S,T]\times B_{2m}
,
and the quantities

\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T];\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}(B_{2m}))}
and

\|b\|_{L^{q_{b}}([S,T];L^{p_{b}}(B_{2m}))}
.

Proof.

Step 1. Smooth approximation.

Let
\sigma_{n},b_{n}
and
f_{n},g_{n}
be smooth approximations of
\sigma,b
and
f,g
, respectively, obtained via standard spatial mollification, so that

\sigma_{n},b_{n},g_{n}\in L^{\infty}\big([S,T];C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),\ \ f_{n}\in L^{q}\big([S,T];C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),

and

{(\sigma_{n},b_{n},f_{n},g_{n})\to(\sigma,b,f,g)\ \text{locally in the natural norms as }n\to\infty.}

Denote by
X_{t,s}(x)
and
X^{n}_{t,s}(x)
the weak solutions of (
4.1
) corresponding to

(\sigma,b)
and
(\sigma_{n},b_{n})
, respectively.
By
[
43
]
, for each
S\leqslant t<s\leqslant T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t,s}(x)\to X_{t,s}(x)\quad\text{{in law} as }n\to\infty,

and
X^{n}_{t,s}(x)
admits a transition density
p_{n}(t,x;s,y)
satisfying Gaussian two-sided bounds with constants depending only on the ellipticity and the local
L^{q_{b}}_{t}L^{p_{b}}_{x}
-norm of
b
; namely,
for all
S\leqslant t<s\leqslant T
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}}{(s-t)^{d/2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}|x-y|^{2}/(s-t)}\leqslant p_{n}(t,x;s,y)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{(s-t)^{d/2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{1}|x-y|^{2}/(s-t)},

with constants
C_{0},C_{1},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}
independent of
n
.

Define
a_{n}:=\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n}^{*}
and

u_{n}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}\big[g_{n}\big(X^{n}_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}\big(s,X^{n}_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

Then
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}([S,T];C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and solves (
3.1
) with coefficients
(a_{n},b_{n})
and source term
f_{n}
.

Step 2. Local
{\mathbb{W}}^{2,p}
-bounds via Theorem
3.8
.

Fix
(t,x)\in(S,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Choose

{0<R<\sqrt{T-t}\wedge\sqrt{t-S}\wedge 1}

small enough independent of
n
so that
Q_{R}(t,x)\subset(S,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{(s,y)\in Q_{R}(t,x)}\|a_{n}(s,y)-a_{n}(s,x)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leqslant R^{\alpha}\sup_{s\in[S,T]}[a_{n}(s,\cdot)]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}\leqslant\delta\,\lambda,

where
\delta
is the constant from (
3.14
).
Applying (
3.15
) to
u_{n}
on
Q_{R}(t,x)
yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R/2}(t,x))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[1+\|b_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{p_{b}}(Q_{R}(t,x))}^{2/\theta_{b}}\Big]\|u_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R}(t,x))}+\|f_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R}(t,x))},

where
\theta_{b}:=1-\frac{2}{q_{b}}-\frac{d}{p_{b}}
and the implicit constant depends only on

R,p,q,p_{b},q_{b},d,\alpha
and the ellipticity constants of
\sigma
on
[t-R^{2},t+R^{2}]\times B_{2}(x)
.
Since mollification does not increase local
L^{q_{b}}_{t}L^{p_{b}}_{x}
norms,
a finite covering argument over
[S+t,T-t]\times B_{m}
then yields: for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,\frac{T-S}{2})
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m}))}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T]\times B_{2m})}+\|f\|_{L^{q}([S,T];L^{p}(B_{2m}))},

with the stated dependence of constants.
Note that by (
4.4
) and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}\big(s,X^{n}_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{n}\big(s,y\big)\frac{C_{1}}{(s-t)^{d/2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{1}|x-y|^{2}/(s-t)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{-d/(2p)}\|f_{n}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}([S,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where in the last step we have used that
d/p+2/q<2
.
Thus, there is a constant
C>0
independent of
n
such that

\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|g\|_{\infty}+C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}.

Hence, by (
4.5
),

\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}([S,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where the implicit constant does not depend on
n
.

Step 3. Passage to the limit.

By (
4.3
) and dominated convergence (using the uniform Gaussian bounds (
4.4
)),
for each
(t,x)\in(S,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
one has
u_{n}(t,x)\to u(t,x)
.
Moreover, (
4.5
) implies that (up to a subsequence) for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,\frac{T-S}{2})
,

\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}_{x}u\quad\text{weakly in }L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m})),

and by the interpolation inequality,

{\nabla_{x}u_{n}\rightarrow\nabla_{x}u\ \text{strongly in $L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m}))$}.}

Now take
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((S+t,T-t)\times B_{m})
. Since
u_{n}
solves the weak form of (
3.1
) with coefficients
(a_{n},b_{n})
and source
f_{n}
,

\langle\!\langle u_{n},\partial_{t}\phi\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}+f_{n},\phi\rangle\!\rangle,

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{n}\varphi(t,x):={\rm tr}\big(a_{n}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}\varphi(x)\big)+b_{n}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)
.
Using
a_{n}\to a
and
b_{n}\to b
strongly in local
L^{q_{b}}_{t}L^{p_{b}}_{x}
,
together with the weak convergence of
\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}
and the strong convergence of
\nabla_{x}u_{n}
,
we may pass to the limit in each term and conclude that
u
satisfies (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
Finally, taking weak lower semicontinuity limits in (
4.5
) yields (
4.2
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If, in addition to the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
,
we also suppose that
b,f\in L^{\infty}([S,T];{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
;
then by using the Schauder estimate
in Theorem
3.9
,

u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((S,T);{\mathbf{C}}^{2+\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\bigl(0,\frac{T-S}{2}\bigr)
,

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}_{x}u]_{L^{\infty}([S+t,T-t];{\mathbf{C}}^{2+\alpha}(B_{m}))}\lesssim_{C_{t,S,T,m}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T]\times B_{2m})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([S+t,T-t];{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{2m}))}.

Next we introduce a Lyapunov-type hypothesis which provides global moment control and will be used to handle unbounded coefficients on the whole space.

({\bf\widetilde{H}}^{\sigma}_{b})

Assume
\sigma
satisfies
(H
{}^{\alpha}_{a}
)
with
a=\sigma\sigma^{*}
,
and
b\in L^{q_{b}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R};L^{p_{b}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
q_{b},p_{b}\in(1,\infty]
with

\theta_{b}:=1-\frac{d}{p_{b}}-\frac{2}{q_{b}}>0
.
Moreover,
for every
(t,x)\in[S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
the solution of SDE (
4.1
) does not explode,
and there exist two functions
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{S\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\,\rho_{0}\big(X_{t,s}(x)\big)\leqslant\rho_{1}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Recall that the space
\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
is defined by (
1.6
). We can now state the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
({\bf\widetilde{H}}^{\sigma}_{b})
holds. For every
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}([S,T];\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}})
, define

(4.7)

\displaystyle u(t,x):=\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f\big(s,X_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

Then for any
q\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,q_{b}]
and
p\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,p_{b}]

satisfying
\tfrac{d}{p}+\tfrac{2}{q}<2
, we have

u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;\mathrm{loc}}\big((S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
and
u
satisfies PDE (
3.1
).

Proof.

We argue in two steps by localizing the coefficients.

Step 1. Bounded data.

Assume first that
f
and
g
are bounded measurable.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\chi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be nonnegative with

\chi_{n}(x)=1
for
|x|\leqslant n
and
\chi_{n}(x)=0
for
|x|>n+2
.
Define

\sigma_{n}:=\sqrt{a_{n}}:=\sqrt{a\chi_{n}+(1-\chi_{n})I_{d}},\qquad b_{n}:=b\chi_{n}.

By the Lipschitz continuity of the matrix square-root on uniformly elliptic matrices,
\sigma_{n}
inherits the local
C^{\alpha}
-regularity from
a
.
These coefficients satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, and for
|x|\leqslant n
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(t,x)=\sigma(t,x),\quad b_{n}(t,x)=b(t,x).

Let
X^{n}_{t,s}(x)
be the solution to (
4.1
) associated with
(\sigma_{n},b_{n})
, and set

\tau_{n}:=\inf\{s>t:|X^{n}_{t,s}(x)|\geqslant n\},\qquad\hat{\tau}_{n}:=\inf\{s>t:|X_{t,s}(x)|\geqslant n\}.

The nonexplosion condition together with
[
35
, p.32, Theorem 10.4]
gives

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{n}<T)=0,\qquad{\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{n}<T)=0,}

and for each
S\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.10)

X^{n}_{t,{s\wedge\tau_{n}}}(x)
has the same law as
X_{t,s\wedge\hat{\tau}_{n}}(x)
.

Define

u_{n}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X^{n}_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f\big(s,X^{n}_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

Since
|u_{n}(t,x)|\leqslant\|g\|_{\infty}+(T-S)\|f\|_{\infty}
,
(
4.8
) and Theorem
4.1
imply that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,\frac{T-S}{2})
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geqslant 2m}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{q}([S+t,T-t];L^{p}(B_{m}))}<\infty,

and for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((S+t,T-t)\times B_{m})
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n},\partial_{t}\phi\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}+f,\phi\rangle\!\rangle,

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{n}\varphi(t,x):={\rm tr}\big(a_{n}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}\varphi(x)\big)+b_{n}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(x)
.
Moreover, for each
(t,x)\in(S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u_{n}(t,x)\to u(t,x)
pointwise.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X^{n}_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\bigl|\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X^{n}_{t,T\wedge\tau_{n}}(x)\big)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[g\big(X_{t,T\wedge\hat{\tau}_{n}}(x)\big)\big]\bigr|+2\|g\|_{\infty}\bigl[\mathbb{P}(\tau_{n}<T)+\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{n}<T)\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Con1}}}{{=}}2\|g\|_{\infty}\bigl[\mathbb{P}(\tau_{n}<T)+\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}_{n}<T)\bigr]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{DS2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

and similarly for the integral term involving
f
.
Using (
4.11
) and passing to the limit in (
4.12
) exactly as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we conclude that
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;{\rm loc}}
and solves (
3.1
).

Step 2. General data in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
.

Let
g_{n}:=g\chi_{n}
and
f_{n}:=f\chi_{n}
. Then
g_{n}
and
f_{n}
are bounded, and by Step 1 the corresponding probabilistic solution

u_{n}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}\big[g_{n}\big(X_{t,T}(x)\big)\big]+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}\big(s,X_{t,s}(x)\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

belongs to
{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;{\rm loc}}
and solves (
3.1
) with source term
f_{n}
.
Moreover, since
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}([S,T];\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}})
,

\displaystyle|u_{n}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}\mathbb{E}\big[\rho_{0}(X_{t,T}(x))\big]+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T];\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}})}\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{t}^{T}\rho_{0}(X_{t,s}(x)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Lya1}}}{{\leqslant}}\bigl(\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}+(T-t)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([S,T];\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}})}\bigr)\,\rho_{1}(x).

In particular, for each fixed
m
the family
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is uniformly bounded on
[S,T]\times B_{2m}
, hence the local estimate (
4.2
) applies uniformly (with
f_{n}
in place of
f
).
Finally, since
g_{n}\to g
and
f_{n}\to f
pointwise and
|g_{n}|\leqslant|g|
,
|f_{n}|\leqslant|f|
,
dominated convergence (using (
4.6
)) yields
u_{n}(t,x)\to u(t,x)
pointwise.
Passing to the limit in the weak formulation as in Step 1 gives that
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;{\rm loc}}
solves (
3.1
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In general, for
u
defined by (
4.7
) one cannot expect the identity (
3.2
) to extend to
s=T
without additional terminal regularity.
Consequently, uniqueness of such probabilistic solutions for (
3.1
) is not addressed here.

Remark 4.5
.

In the situation of Theorem
4.3
, if we assume
b\in L^{\infty}([S,T];{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

and
f\equiv 0
, then by using remark
4.2
, one can show
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((S,T);{\mathbf{C}}^{2+\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

4.2.
Derivative estimates for time-inhomogeneous SDEs

Under the hypothesis
({\bf\widetilde{H}}^{\sigma}_{b})
, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
and
t\in[S,T]
we define

\mathcal{T}_{t,T}\varphi(x):=\mathbb{E}\,\varphi\big(X_{t,T}(x)\big).

A direct combination of Theorems
4.3
and
3.3
yields the following short-time gradient bound.

Theorem 4.6

(Short-time gradient estimate)
.

Assume that
({\bf\widetilde{H}}^{\sigma}_{b})
holds.
For any
q\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,q_{b}]
and
p\in(1,\infty)\cap(1,p_{b}]
satisfying

\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<1,

there exists a constant
C=C(d,q,p,q_{b},p_{b},\alpha)>0
such that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
and every

(t,x)\in\big[\tfrac{T+S}{2},T\big)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t,T}\varphi(x)\big|\lesssim_{C}\;\frac{(\sqrt{T-S}\vee 1)\,\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)}{\sqrt{T-t}}\,\Lambda_{1}^{1/q}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}}\sup_{s\in[S,T]}\big\|\mathcal{T}_{s,T}\varphi\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))},

where
\Lambda_{1}(x):=\Lambda(x)+1
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)
is defined by (
3.5
).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
, the function
u(t,x):=\mathcal{T}_{t,T}\varphi(x)
belongs to

{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{q,p;\mathrm{loc}}\big((S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
and solves (
3.1
) with
f\equiv 0
.
Hence we may apply (
3.4
) to obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t,T}\varphi(x)\big|\lesssim\Lambda^{1/q}_{1}(x)\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)R}\Big)^{1+\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}}\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\cdot,T}\varphi\big\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{R}(t,x))},

where
R_{0}:=\frac{\sqrt{T-t}}{\sqrt{T-S}\vee 1}
and

R:=\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)R_{0}}{\lambda(x)\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)}\leqslant R_{0}
.

By the definition of
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}(Q_{R}(t,x))
and the bound
L^{\infty}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
on bounded sets,

\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\cdot,T}\varphi\big\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{p}(Q_{R}(t,x))}\lesssim R^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\cdot,T}\varphi\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{R}(t)\times B_{R}(x))}.

Since
\theta=1-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{2}{q}
, inserting this bound into (
4.13
) yields

In the short-time estimate (

A natural question is whether, for Hölder terminal data, one can improve the time singularity.
To this end, we introduce the following class of weights.

Let

denote the class of functions

such that there exist constant

For any

and

Given

and

{\mathbf{C}}_{\rho}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in{\mathbf{C}}_{loc}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}}{\rho(x)}<\infty\right\}.

For

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho}}\asymp\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\rho(x)}+[f]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho}},\qquad[f]_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{|y|\leqslant 1}\frac{|f(x+y)-f(x)|}{\rho(x)|y|^{\alpha}}.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\beta\in[0,1]
and

Let

there exists

such that for all

5.
Poisson equations on the whole space

In this section we apply the regularity estimates obtained in Sections 3 and 4
to study solvability and regularity of Poisson equations on the whole space.
More precisely, we consider

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}u(x)={\rm tr}\big(a(x)\cdot\nabla^{2}_{x}u(x)\big)+b(x)\cdot\nabla_{x}u(x)=-f(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We impose the following standing assumption on the coefficients.

(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′′

Assume that
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′
holds and, in addition, the function
\ell_{1}
appearing in (
1.11
)
satisfies
\ell_{1}\in L^{1}([0,\infty))
.

Recall that
{\mathscr{L}}
is the generator associated with (
1.1
), and
\mu
is the unique invariant probability
measure of the semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. In particular,

{\mathscr{L}}^{*}\mu=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathscr{L}}\psi(x)\,\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=0\quad\text{for all suitable test functions }\psi.

Hence a necessary solvability condition for (
5.1
) is the centering condition

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mu(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=0.

Throughout this section we assume (
5.2
).
We then define the candidate solution

(5.3)

\displaystyle u(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{t}f(x)\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

Under
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′′
the integral in (
5.3
) is finite for every
x
and moreover

|u(x)|\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{T}_{t}f(x)|\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\lesssim\rho_{1}(x)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell_{1}(t)\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\lesssim\rho_{1}(x)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}.

We can now state the first main result of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′′
holds.
Let

satisfy (
5.2
) and define

by (
5.3
).
Then for every
p>d
we have

, and
u
solves the Poisson equation (

)
in the weak sense.
Moreover, for any

there exists a constant

such that for all

,

(5.4)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}u(x)|\lesssim_{C}\;\Gamma_{1}(x)\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{3\varepsilon+d/p_{b}}\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}},

where
\Lambda_{1}(x):=\Lambda(x)+1
and
\Gamma_{1}(x)
is given by (
1.9
) with

.

Proof.

Step 1. Gradient bound.

Using the short-time gradient estimate for

and the long-time decay estimate for

,
we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}u(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{t}f(x)\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right|\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}f(x)|\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{T}_{t}f(x)|\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\Gamma_{1}(x)\Lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}(x)}{\lambda(x)}\right)^{3\varepsilon+d/p_{b}}\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1/2}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}\ell_{1}(t)\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

. This yields (

Step 2. Local

regularity and identification of the Poisson equation.

Fix an arbitrary bounded smooth domain
{\mathcal{O}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Consider the Dirichlet problem

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathscr{L}}\hat{u}(x)=-f(x),&x\in{\mathcal{O}},\\[2.84526pt]
\hat{u}=u,&x\in\partial{\mathcal{O}}.\end{array}\right.

Since
u
is continuous, standard elliptic theory (e.g.
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
) implies that for every
p>d
there exists a unique solution

\hat{u}\in{\mathbb{W}}^{2}_{p}({\mathcal{O}})\cap C(\overline{{\mathcal{O}}})
to (
5.5
).

Let
\tau_{\mathcal{O}}:=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:X_{t}(x)\notin{\mathcal{O}}\}
be the first exit time from
{\mathcal{O}}
.
Applying Itô’s formula to
\hat{u}(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}(x))
and taking expectations, we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{u}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\hat{u}\big(X_{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}(x)\big)-\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathscr{L}}\hat{u}(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,u\big(X_{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}(x)\big)+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}f(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

On the other hand, by the definition of
u
and the strong Markov property,

(5.7)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}f(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)+\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}^{\infty}f(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}f(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}f\big(X_{s}(x^{\prime})\big)\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X_{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}(x)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}f(X_{s}(x))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)+\mathbb{E}\,u\big(X_{\tau_{\mathcal{O}}}(x)\big).

Combining (
5
) and (
5.7
) yields
\hat{u}(x)=u(x)
for all
x\in{\mathcal{O}}
.
As
{\mathcal{O}}
is arbitrary, this shows that
u\in{\mathbb{W}}^{2}_{p;\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
p>d
and that
u
solves (
5.1
).
∎

Under strengthened regularity assumptions, we obtain a Schauder-type estimate.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′′
and (
3.8
) hold.
Suppose
b,f\in\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}
satisfies (
5.2
).
Then there exists a constant
C=C(d,\alpha,\ell_{0},\ell_{1})>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{2+\alpha}(B_{1/2}(x))}\lesssim_{C}\Bigl[\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}^{\,2+\alpha}(x)\,\rho_{1}(x)+\rho_{0}(x)/\lambda(x)\Bigr]\,\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho_{0}}},

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}(x)
is defined by (
1.10
) with
t=1
.

Proof.

For
t>0
we split
u
as

u(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}_{s}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{\infty}_{t}\mathcal{T}_{s}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=:u_{0}(t,x)+u_{1}(t,x).

Take
t=1
. For the short-time part
u_{0}
, since by (
1.10
) and (
3.8
),

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,2]\times B_{1}(x))}\lesssim\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}\lesssim\rho_{1}(x)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}},

arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
and employing Theorems
4.3
and
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{0}(1,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1/2}(x))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\widetilde{\Gamma}^{2+\alpha}_{1}(x)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,2]\times B_{1}(x))}+\lambda(x)^{-1}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{2+\alpha}_{1}(x)\,\rho_{1}(x)+\rho_{0}(x)/\lambda(x)\bigr)\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}_{\rho_{0}}}.

For the long-time part
u_{1}
, estimate (
1.13
) yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{x}u_{1}(1,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{1}^{\infty}\|\nabla^{2}_{x}\mathcal{T}_{s}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\widetilde{\Gamma}^{2+\alpha}_{1}(x)\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho_{0}}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\ell_{1}(s-1){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

The integral is finite because
\ell_{1}\in L^{1}([0,\infty))
under
(H
{}^{\sigma}_{b}
)
′′
. Hence both the short-time and the long-time contributions are finite, and combining the two estimates gives (
5.8
).
∎

6.
Gradient estimates for SDEs with distributional drifts

As an application of the pointwise gradient estimates derived in the previous sections,
we establish a short-time gradient bound for the Markov semigroup associated with SDEs
whose drift contains a distributional component, a class of highly singular coefficients.

For
(\alpha,p)\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z})\times[1,\infty]
, let
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{p}
denote the Bessel potential space

\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{p}:=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{p}}:=\|(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}}<\infty\Big\},

where
(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is defined via Fourier transform by

(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f:=\Big((1+|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha/2}\widehat{f}(\xi)\Big)^{\check{}}.

Fix
d\geqslant 2
and consider the following SDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(6.1)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

where the drift
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
satisfies:

(A)

There exist
\alpha_{b}\in(-1,-\tfrac{1}{2}]
and
p_{b},q_{b}\in[2,\infty]
such that

\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}+\tfrac{2}{q_{b}}<1+\alpha_{b},\qquad b_{1},\ \mathord{{\rm div}}b_{1}\in\bigcap_{T>0}L^{q_{b}}\big([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\alpha_{b}}_{p_{b}}\big),

and there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1}\geqslant 0
such that

|b_{2}(t,x)|\leqslant c_{0}+c_{1}|x|,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
b_{1}
is a distribution, the term
b_{1}(t,X_{t})
is not classically defined.
Following
[
17
]
, solutions are introduced through mollification.
Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a nonnegative mollifier with
\int\phi=1
and set
\phi_{n}(x):=n^{d}\phi(nx)
.
Define the smooth approximation of
b_{1}
by

b_{1;n}(t,x):=(b_{1}(t,\cdot)*\phi_{n})(x).

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We begin by introducing the concept of weak solutions for the SDE (
6.1
) with a distributional drift.

Definition 6.1

(Weak solutions)
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a stochastic basis and
let
(X,W)
be
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued, continuous,
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted processes.
We call
(\mathfrak{F},X,W)
a weak solution of (
6.1
) with initial law
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if

W
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion,
\mathbb{P}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\mu
, and for all
t\in[0,T]
,

X_{t}=X_{0}+A^{b_{1}}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{2}(s,X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}\,W_{t},\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

where

A^{b_{1}}_{t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}b_{1;n}(s,X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\mbox{ exists in $L^{2}(\Omega)$.}

We recall the following result from
[
17
]
.

Theorem 6.2

(Existence, uniqueness, and Krylov estimate)
.

Assume
(A)
. Then for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a weak solution
(\mathfrak{F},X,W)
of (
6.1
)
starting from
x
in the sense of Definition
6.1
, and it is unique in the class of solutions satisfying the Krylov estimate:
for any
(\alpha,p,q)\in[\alpha_{b},0]\times[2,\infty]^{2}
with

\tfrac{q_{b}}{2}\leqslant q\leqslant q_{b},\qquad p\leqslant p_{b},\qquad\alpha-\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{2}{q}\geqslant\alpha_{b}-\tfrac{d}{p_{b}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{b}},

and for any
T,m>0
, there exist
\theta>0
and
C=C(T,m,\alpha,p,q)>0
such that for all

f\in L^{q}([0,T];\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha})\cap L^{q}([0,T];C_{b}^{\infty})
and
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}f(s,X_{s})\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1+\theta}{2}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q}([0,T];\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha})}.

Now we can show the following gradient estimate.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume
(A)
. For every
T>0
there exists a constant
C>0

such that
for all
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\nabla_{x}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(X_{t}(x))|\leqslant C\left(1+|x|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

For any
T>0
,
by
[
17
, Section 4]
, there is a measurable function
\Phi_{t}(x):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

x\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x)
forms a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism and

(6.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Phi_{t}(x)|,\ |\nabla_{x}\Phi^{-1}_{t}(x)|\leqslant 4,\ \ t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
X
be the unique weak solution of (
6.1
) satisfying the Krylov estimate (
6.2
),
and define the transformed process

Y_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(X_{t}).

By
[
17
, Lemma 4.5]
,
Y
solves an SDE of the form

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}=\tilde{b}(t,Y_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}\tilde{\sigma}(t,Y_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

where

\tilde{b}(t,y):=\lambda(y-\Phi^{-1}_{t}(y))+(b_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{t})(\Phi^{-1}_{t}(y)),\quad\tilde{\sigma}(t,y):=\nabla\Phi_{t}(\Phi^{-1}_{t}(y)).

Moreover,
\tilde{b}
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are measurable, and there exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
C_{T}>0
such that
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(6.4)

\displaystyle|\tilde{b}(t,y)|\leqslant C_{T}(1+|y|),\qquad|\tilde{\sigma}(t,y)-\tilde{\sigma}(t,y^{\prime})|\leqslant C_{T}|y-y^{\prime}|^{\gamma},

and the diffusion matrix is uniformly elliptic:

(6.5)

\displaystyle|\xi|/4\leqslant|\tilde{\sigma}(t,y)\xi|\leqslant 4|\xi|,\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Fix
t\in(0,T]
. In order to apply the backward-in-time gradient estimate from Section 4
(which is stated on a symmetric time interval around
0
), we extend the coefficients

(\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma})
from
[0,t]
to
[-t,t]
by freezing them for negative times:

\tilde{b}(s,\cdot):=\tilde{b}(0,\cdot),\qquad\tilde{\sigma}(s,\cdot):=\tilde{\sigma}(0,\cdot),\qquad s\in[-t,0].

This extension preserves the bounds (
6.4
)–(
6.5
) on
[-t,t]
.

Let
\psi_{t}:=\varphi\circ\Phi_{t}^{-1}
. Then
\|\psi_{t}\|_{\infty}=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
, and

\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(X_{t}(x))=\mathbb{E}\,\psi_{t}(Y_{t}(\Phi_{0}(x))).

By Theorem
4.6
with
(S,t,T)=(-t,0,t)
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{y}\mathbb{E}\,\psi_{t}(Y_{0,t}(y))\big|\leqslant C_{T}\left(1+|y|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\|\psi_{t}\|_{\infty}=C_{T}\left(1+|y|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

By the chain rule and (
6.3
),

\nabla_{x}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(X_{t}(x))=\nabla_{y}\Big(\mathbb{E}\,\psi_{t}(Y_{0,t}(y))\Big)\Big|_{y=\Phi_{0}(x)}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Phi_{0}(x),

hence

|\nabla_{x}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(X_{t}(x))|\leqslant\|\nabla_{x}\Phi_{0}\|_{\infty}\,\big|\nabla_{y}\mathbb{E}\,\psi_{t}(Y_{0,t}(y))\big|_{y=\Phi_{0}(x)}.

Combining (
6.6
) with (
6.3
) and using
|\Phi_{0}(x)|\leqslant 4|x|+C_{T}
, we obtain
the desired estimate.
∎
