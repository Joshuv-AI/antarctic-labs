Vertex-colored Turán theorems with applications in extremal hypergraph problems
Wanfang Chen, Jinghua Deng, Jianfeng Hou, Xizhi Liu, Yixiao Zhang

Abstract
Balogh, Clemen, and Lidický [ 2 ] proved that the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm Turán problem for K 5 3 K_{5}^{3} is asymptotically solved by the balanced bipartite construction, and they further conjectured that this construction is uniquely extremal for all sufficiently large n n . We confirm this conjecture. We also determine exactly the maximum number of cliques in an n n -vertex K 5 3 K_{5}^{3} -free 3 3 -uniform hypergraph for all sufficiently large n n , thereby verifying the corresponding case of a conjecture of Frankl, Gryaznov, and Talebanfard [ 11 ] . The main ingredients are Turán-type theorems for vertex-colored graphs forbidding balanced cliques, including an edge bound, an ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm bound, and a sharp crossing-triangle theorem in the two-colored balanced K 4 K_{4} -free case. We also use a local modification procedure within the stability method. This reduces the exact hypergraph problems to proving that the relevant objective function increases under suitable local changes near the bipartite construction.

1  Introduction

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph (henceforth an
r
-graph)
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets of some set
V
. We call
V
the vertex set of
\mathcal{H}
and denote it by
V(\mathcal{H})
. When
V
is understood, we often identify
\mathcal{H}
with its edge set.
Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs, an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph. A central problem in extremal combinatorics is to determine extremal properties of
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graphs on
n
vertices. In particular, the Turán problem asks for the Turán number

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max\left\{|\mathcal{H}|:|V(\mathcal{H})|=n\text{ and }\mathcal{H}\text{ is }\mathcal{F}\text{-free}\right\}.

For
r=2
, the asymptotic behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is described by the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem
[
8
]
(see also
[
7
]
).
Denote by
K_{\ell}^{r}
the complete
r
-graph on
\ell
vertices, with the superscript omitted when
r=2
.
The classical theorem of Turán
[
18
]
determines the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\ell})
for every
\ell\geq 3
, with the case
\ell=3
proved earlier by Mantel
[
15
]
.
In contrast, for
r\geq 3
, determining
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{\ell}^{r})
even asymptotically is notoriously difficult in general.
Erdős
[
9
]
offered $500 for determining the Turán density of
K_{\ell}^{r}
for any single pair
\ell,r
with
\ell>r\geq 3
. This prize remains unclaimed. We refer the reader to
[
1
,
6
,
12
,
14
]
for further background on hypergraph Turán theory.

In this paper, we study two exact extremal problems for
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graphs: the Turán problem in the
\ell_{2}
-norm, where the number of edges is replaced by the sum of squared codegrees, and the problem of maximizing the number of cliques.
Following Balogh, Clemen, and Lidický
[
2
]
, for an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
, we write

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{r-1}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)^{2},

where
d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)
denotes the codegree of the
(r-1)
-set
e
. The corresponding extremal quantity and density are

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\ell_{2}}(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max\left\{\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}:|V(\mathcal{H})|=n\text{ and }\mathcal{H}\text{ is }\mathcal{F}\text{-free}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\pi_{\ell_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{ex}_{\ell_{2}}(n,\mathcal{F})}{\binom{n}{r-1}(n-r+1)^{2}},

respectively.
The existence of this limit follows from the standard averaging argument.
Recently, Balogh, Clemen, and Lidický
[
2
]
established the
\ell_{2}
-norm Turán density of the tetrahedron
K_{4}^{3}
to be
1/3
.
Subsequently, Bodnár et al.
[
3
]
determined the exact
\ell_{2}
-norm Turán number of
K_{4}^{3}
for all sufficiently large
n
.

For the
\ell_{2}
-norm Turán problem of
K_{5}^{3}
, the relevant extremal construction is the balanced complete bipartite
3
-graph.
Given two disjoint sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, let
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
denote the
3
-graph on
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
consisting of all triples that intersect both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.
When
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=[n]
is a balanced partition of
[n]
, we write
\mathbb{B}_{n}
for
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
.
The construction
\mathbb{B}_{n}
shows that
\pi_{\ell_{2}}(K_{5}^{3})\geq 5/8
.
Using flag algebras, Balogh, Clemen, and Lidický
[
2
]
proved the matching upper bound
\pi_{\ell_{2}}(K_{5}^{3})\leq 5/8
, establishing the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

It holds that
\pi_{\ell_{2}}(K_{5}^{3})=5/8
.

They further conjectured that the corresponding exact statement holds for all sufficiently large
n
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
2
, Conjecture 1.4]
)
.

There exists
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\ell_{2}}(n,K_{5}^{3})=\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2},

and
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is the unique
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph on
n
vertices
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}=\mathrm{ex}_{\ell_{2}}(n,K_{5}^{3})
.

Our first main result confirms this conjecture.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, every
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2},

and equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

We next turn to the clique-counting problem in hypergraphs.
Given an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
, a clique in
\mathcal{H}
is a subset
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
such that either
|S|<r
, or
|S|\geq r
and every
r
-subset of
S
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
.
We write
k(\mathcal{H})
for the number of cliques in
\mathcal{H}
.
For an integer
t\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle V(x,t)\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{x}{j}.

In particular, when
x
is a positive integer,
V(x,t)
represents the size of the Hamming ball of radius
t
in the
x
-dimensional hypercube.
Frankl, Gryaznov, and Talebanfard
[
11
]
proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
11
, Conjecture 14]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex
r
-graph containing no clique of size
\ell+1
.
Then the total number of cliques in
\mathcal{H}
is bounded by

\displaystyle k(\mathcal{H})\leq V\!\left(\frac{(r-1)n}{\ell},r-1\right)^{\frac{\ell}{r-1}}.

Furthermore, when
r-1\mid\ell
and
\ell\mid(r-1)n
, the unique extremal case is the
n
-vertex
\frac{\ell}{r-1}
-partite
r
-graph whose edge set consists of all
r
-sets that intersect each part in at most
r-1
vertices.

When
(r,\ell)=(3,4)
, the corresponding construction is the balanced complete bipartite
3
-graph
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Thus, in our setting, the clique-counting problem again points to the same bipartite construction.
Bodnár
[
4
]
proved an asymptotic version of the corresponding statement, and our second theorem gives the exact result for all sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, every
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

\displaystyle k(\mathcal{H})\leq k(\mathbb{B}_{n}),

and equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

The main auxiliary input comes from Turán-type problems in vertex-colored graphs.
The point is that, once a
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph is close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the relevant link graphs carry a natural two-part vertex coloring induced by the underlying bipartition.
Moreover, every balanced copy of
K_{4}
in such a link graph must be accounted for by a missing triple of the bipartite template. Otherwise it would extend to a copy of
K_{5}^{3}
.
Thus these link graphs have few balanced copies of
K_{4}
, and the required local estimates reduce to Turán-type statements for vertex-colored graphs.
This leads us to study the following general setup.
Let
G
be a graph on vertex set
V
, and let
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{s}
be a fixed partition.
For integers
1\leq q\leq s
and
t\geq 1
, we call a clique
K\subseteq G
a
(q,t)
-balanced clique if there exists an index set
I\subseteq[s]
with
|I|=q
such that

\displaystyle|V(K)\cap V_{i}|=t\quad\text{for every }i\in I,\quad\text{and}\quad K\cong K_{qt}.

When
q=s
, this is simply a balanced
K_{st}
.

We establish several Turán-type results for vertex-colored graphs that are useful in extremal problems for hypergraphs.
In particular, we prove an edge bound and an
\ell_{2}
-norm bound for graphs with no balanced clique, as well as a sharp theorem on crossing triangles in the two-colored balanced
K_{4}
-free case.
The precise statements are given in Section
2
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we prove Turán-type results for vertex-colored graphs.
In Section
3
, we collect the common structural properties of
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graphs that are close to the bipartite construction
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
These properties will be used in both exact arguments.
In Section
4
, we combine the vertex-colored graph results with the common structural lemmas to prove Theorem
1.3
.
In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.5
.

Both exact proofs follow the same general mechanism.
A stability theorem first gives a partition with respect to which the hypergraph is close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
The common structural results, together with objective-specific local degree estimates, then give the required local control of bad edges and missing edges.
Finally, we apply a local modification procedure: for a suitable pair, we replace the bad edges containing this pair by the corresponding missing edges from the bipartite template.
The key point is that this operation strictly increases the objective function, namely the
\ell_{2}
-norm in Section
4
and the clique-counting objective in Section
5
.
This rules out all bad edges. The remaining conclusion then follows from extremality and from the fact that the complete bipartite construction is maximized by a balanced partition.

2  Turán-type theorems in vertex-colored graphs

In this section, we first state and then prove several Turán-type results for vertex-colored graphs.
Let
G
be a graph on vertex set
V
, and let
V\coloneqq V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{s}
be a fixed partition of
V
(equivalently, an
s
-coloring of the vertices of
G
).
We recall that, for integers
1\leq q\leq s
and
t\geq 1
, a clique
K\subseteq G
is called a
(q,t)
-balanced clique if there exists an index set
I\subseteq[s]
with
|I|=q
such that

\displaystyle|V(K)\cap V_{i}|=t\quad\text{for every }i\in I,\quad\text{and}\quad K\cong K_{qt}.

When
q=s
, we simply call
K
a balanced
K_{st}
.

Throughout this section, we assume that

\displaystyle|V_{i}|=n\quad\text{for all }i\in[s].

When cyclic notation is used, the relevant indices are taken modulo the specified range.

For a vertex set
S\subseteq V
, we use
G[S]
to denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
.
For pairwise disjoint sets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\subseteq V
, we write
G[S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}]
for the induced
k
-partite subgraph of
G
with parts
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
.

2.1  Extremal results

In this subsection, we state the vertex-colored extremal results used later.
We first give an edge bound for graphs with no balanced
K_{st}
, then an
\ell_{2}
-norm bound in the case
t=2
, and finally a sharp bound on crossing triangles in the two-colored balanced
K_{4}
-free case.

2.1.1  Edge Bound

We first introduce the extremal construction.
Let
V_{1}=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{t-1}
be a partition of the set
V_{1}
such that the sizes of
S_{i}
differ by at most one.
We define
\Lambda[S_{1},\ldots,S_{t-1};V_{2},\ldots,V_{s}]
to be the graph on the vertex set
V
with edge set

\displaystyle\Lambda[S_{1},\ldots,S_{t-1};V_{2},\ldots,V_{s}]\coloneqq\binom{V}{2}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[t-1]}\binom{S_{i}}{2}.

Observe that the graph
\Lambda
is balanced
K_{st}
-free, as the part
V_{1}
does not contain a clique of size
t
.
A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle|\Lambda[S_{1},\ldots,S_{t-1};V_{2},\ldots,V_{s}]|=\binom{sn}{2}-\binom{n}{2}+t_{t-1}(n)=\frac{(sn)^{2}}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(t-1)}+o(n^{2}),

where
t_{t-1}(n)
denotes the number of edges in the balanced
(t-1)
-partite Turán graph on
n
vertices.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
s,t\geq 2
. If
G
is balanced
K_{st}
-free, then

\displaystyle|G|\leq\frac{(sn)^{2}}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(t-1)}+s^{2}n.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
t=s=2
and
|V_{i}|=n
for
i\in[2]
.
If
G
is balanced
K_{4}
-free, then

\displaystyle|G|\leq\frac{3n^{2}}{2}+4n.

2.1.2
\ell_{2}
-Norm Bound

We now turn to the
\ell_{2}
-norm. In the case
t=2
, the graph
\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2},\ldots,V_{s}]
has
V_{1}
independent.
The subgraph induced by
V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{s}
is complete, and all edges between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{s}
are present. Hence

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2},\ldots,V_{s}]\rVert_{2}=(s-1)n\cdot(sn-1)^{2}+n\cdot(sn-n)^{2}.

This leads to the following upper bound for balanced
K_{2s}
-free graphs.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
s\geq 2
. If
G
is balanced
K_{2s}
-free, then

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}\leq(s^{2}(s-1)+(s-1)^{2})n^{3}+4s^{3}n^{2}.

In the case
s=2
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
s=2
and
|V_{i}|=n
for
i\in[2]
.
If
G
is balanced
K_{4}
-free, then

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}\leq 5n^{3}+32n^{2}.

2.1.3  Crossing Triangles

Finally, we consider crossing triangles in the case
s=2
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)
denote the number of crossing triangles in
G
, that is, triangles containing vertices from both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. For the graph
\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2}]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},\Lambda)=n\binom{n}{2}.

The next theorem shows that this is best possible, and the following proposition gives the corresponding stability statement.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
s=2
,
n\geq 3
, and
|V_{i}|=n
for
i\in[2]
.
If
G
is a balanced
K_{4}
-free graph, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\leq n\binom{n}{2}.

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
\eta>0
, there exist
\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\coloneqq\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}(\eta)>0
and
N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\coloneqq N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}(\eta)
such that the following holds for all
n\geq N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
.
Suppose that
G
is a balanced
K_{4}
-free graph on the partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
with
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=n
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\geq n\binom{n}{2}-\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}.

Then
G
can be transformed into either
\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2}]
or
\Lambda[V_{2};V_{1}]
by changing at most
\eta n^{2}
edges.

2.2  Local Symmetrization

In this subsection, we record a slight variant of Zykov symmetrization
[
19
]
. It
will be used in the proofs of the edge bound and the
\ell_{2}
-norm bound, and it will also
provide a convenient monotonicity statement for crossing triangles.
Given nonadjacent vertices
u,v\in V
, denote by
G_{u\to v}
the graph obtained from
G
by
symmetrizing
u
to
v
, that is,

\displaystyle G_{u\to v}\coloneqq\big(G\setminus\left\{uw\colon w\in N_{G}(u)\right\}\big)\cup\left\{uw\colon w\in N_{G}(v)\right\}.

We say that
u
and
v
are equivalent, denoted
u\sim v
, if
u
and
v
lie in the same part
V_{i}
for some
i\in[s]
and
N_{G}(u)=N_{G}(v)
(and hence
\{u,v\}\notin G
).
For
i\in[s]
and
v\in V_{i}
, write

\displaystyle[v]\coloneqq\{u\in V_{i}\colon u\sim v\}

for the equivalence class of
v
in
V_{i}
(with respect to
\sim
in
G
).

We say that
G
is locally symmetrized if, for each
i\in[s]
, any two nonadjacent vertices in
V_{i}
are equivalent.

Suppose that
u
and
v
lie in the same part and
uv\notin G
. Then there are no edges between the classes
[u]
and
[v]
. Let
G_{[u]\to[v]}
be the graph obtained by symmetrizing every vertex in
[u]
to the neighborhood pattern of a vertex in
[v]
.

Each such step reduces the number of equivalence classes by one, so after finitely many steps we obtain a locally symmetrized graph. Moreover, if
G
is balanced
K_{st}
-free, then so is
G_{[u]\to[v]}
. Indeed, suppose that
G_{[u]\to[v]}
contains a balanced
K_{st}
, say
K
. If
K
avoids
[u]
, then
K
already appears in
G
, a contradiction. Hence
K
contains a vertex from
[u]
. Since
[u]
remains independent and has no edges to
[v]
after the symmetrization,
K
contains exactly one vertex from
[u]
and no vertex from
[v]
. Replacing this vertex by
v
gives a balanced
K_{st}
in
G
, a contradiction.
The next two propositions show that, under a suitable such symmetrization, neither the number of edges nor the
\ell_{2}
-norm nor the number of crossing triangles decreases.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
u
and
v
be nonadjacent vertices lying in the same part.

\displaystyle|G|\leq\max\left\{|G_{[u]\to[v]}|,~|G_{[v]\to[u]}|\right\},\quad\lVert G\rVert_{2}\leq\max\left\{\lVert G_{[u]\to[v]}\rVert_{2},~\lVert G_{[v]\to[u]}\rVert_{2}\right\}.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
u
and
v
be nonadjacent vertices lying in the same part.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\leq\max\left\{\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G_{[u]\to[v]}),~\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G_{[v]\to[u]})\right\}.

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

For a vertex
x\in V
, let
\tau(x)
denote the number of crossing triangles containing
x
.
Since vertices in the same equivalence class have the same neighborhood,
\tau(x)
is constant on each of the classes
[u]
and
[v]
. Let
\tau_{u}
and
\tau_{v}
denote these values, and assume without loss of generality that
\tau_{u}\leq\tau_{v}
.
Since there are no edges between
[u]
and
[v]
, and since
[u]
remains independent after the symmetrization, every crossing triangle affected by the passage from
G
to
G_{[u]\to[v]}
contains exactly one vertex of
[u]
. For each such vertex, the number of crossing triangles changes from
\tau_{u}
to
\tau_{v}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G_{[u]\to[v]})-\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)=|[u]|(\tau_{v}-\tau_{u})\geq 0.

This proves the proposition.

Therefore, in the proofs of Theorems
2.1
,
2.3
, and
2.5
, we may assume that
G
is locally symmetrized.
The following fact follows directly from the definition of locally symmetrized.

Fact 2.9
.

Suppose that
G
is locally symmetrized. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

Each equivalence class is an independent set in
G
.

(ii)

For each
i\in[s]
and every pair of distinct equivalence classes
[x],[y]\subseteq V_{i}
, the bipartite graph between
[x]
and
[y]
is complete.

2.3  Proof of the Edge Bound

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
G
be an extremal balanced
K_{st}
-free graph with respect to the partition
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{s})
, where
|V_{i}|=n
for all
i\in[s]
.
Suppose to the contrary that

\displaystyle|G|>\binom{sn}{2}-\binom{n}{2}+t_{t-1}(n)+s^{2}n.

We first show that extremality forces every transversal choice of one vertex from each part to have large total degree.

Claim 2.10
.

For any choice of
s
vertices
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[s]}d_{G}(v_{i})>s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}.

Proof of Claim
2.10
.

We proceed by contradiction. Suppose there exists a tuple
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{G}(v_{i})\leq s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}
.
Let us label vertices in
V_{1},\ldots,V_{s}
by
V_{i}=\{v_{i}^{1},\ldots,v_{i}^{n}\}
, for all
i\in[s]
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume the tuple yielding the contradiction is
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})=(v_{1}^{1},\ldots,v_{s}^{1})
.
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}\big(d_{G}(v_{1}^{i})+\cdots+d_{G}(v_{s}^{i})\big)=2|G|

\displaystyle>2\left(\binom{sn}{2}-\binom{n}{2}+t_{t-1}(n)+s^{2}n\right)

\displaystyle=s^{2}n^{2}-sn-n^{2}+n+2t_{t-1}(n)+2s^{2}n.

Recall that the balanced
(t-1)
-partite Turán graph on
n
vertices has minimum degree

n-\left\lceil n/(t-1)\right\rceil
. In particular, its average degree satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2t_{t-1}(n)}{n}\geq n-\left\lceil\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rceil\geq\frac{t-2}{t-1}n-1.

Hence, by the pigeonhole principle, there exists a tuple
(v_{1}^{i_{0}},\ldots,v_{s}^{i_{0}})
such that

\displaystyle d_{G}(v_{1}^{i_{0}})+\cdots+d_{G}(v_{s}^{i_{0}})\geq\frac{2|G|}{n}

\displaystyle>s^{2}n-s-n+1+\frac{2t_{t-1}(n)}{n}+2s^{2}

\displaystyle\geq s^{2}n-s-n+1+\left(\frac{t-2}{t-1}n-1\right)+2s^{2}

\displaystyle>s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}+s^{2}.

If
i_{0}=1
, then the last displayed inequality contradicts the choice of
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})
. Hence
i_{0}\neq 1
.
We now construct a new graph
\tilde{G}
from
G
by a sequence of symmetrization operations.
Set
G^{(0)}\coloneqq G
.
For
j=1,\ldots,s
, let
G^{(j)}
be the graph obtained from
G^{(j-1)}
by deleting all edges incident with
v_{j}
and then joining
v_{j}
to every vertex in
N_{G^{(j-1)}}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})\setminus\{v_{j}\}
.
Finally, set
\tilde{G}\coloneqq G^{(s)}
.
We claim that every graph
G^{(j)}
is balanced
K_{st}
-free.
This is clear for
j=0
.
Suppose that
G^{(j-1)}
is balanced
K_{st}
-free and that
G^{(j)}
contains a balanced
K_{st}
, say
K
.
Since the
j
-th step changes only edges incident with
v_{j}
, the clique
K
must contain
v_{j}
.
By construction,
v_{j}v_{j}^{i_{0}}\notin G^{(j)}
, so
v_{j}^{i_{0}}\notin V(K)
.
For every
x\in V(K)\setminus\{v_{j}\}
, the edge
v_{j}x
in
G^{(j)}
implies that
v_{j}^{i_{0}}x
is an edge of
G^{(j-1)}
.
Replacing
v_{j}
by
v_{j}^{i_{0}}
therefore gives a balanced
K_{st}
in
G^{(j-1)}
, a contradiction.
Thus
\tilde{G}
is balanced
K_{st}
-free.
Moreover, before the
j
-th step only the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{j-1}
have been changed, and hence

\displaystyle d_{G^{(j-1)}}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})\geq d_{G}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})-(j-1)\quad\text{and}\quad d_{G^{(j-1)}}(v_{j})\leq d_{G}(v_{j})+(j-1).

In the
j
-th symmetrization, at most one neighbor of
v_{j}^{i_{0}}
, namely
v_{j}
, is not copied to
v_{j}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle|G^{(j)}|-|G^{(j-1)}|

\displaystyle\geq d_{G}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})-d_{G}(v_{j})-(2j-1).

Summing this over
j\in[s]
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{G}|=|G^{(s)}|

\displaystyle\geq|G|+\sum_{j\in[s]}\bigl(d_{G}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})-d_{G}(v_{j})-(2j-1)\bigr)

\displaystyle=|G|-\sum_{j\in[s]}d_{G}(v_{j})+\sum_{j\in[s]}d_{G}(v_{j}^{i_{0}})-s^{2}

\displaystyle>|G|-\left(s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}\right)+\left(s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}+s^{2}\right)-s^{2}=|G|,

contradicting the maximality of
G
.

For a set
S\subseteq V(G)
and an index
i\in[s]
, we define the common neighbors of
S
in
V_{i}
as

\displaystyle N_{i}(S)\coloneqq\bigcap_{v\in S}\bigl(N_{G}(v)\cap V_{i}\bigr).

Additionally, let
\nu(S)
denote the number of local equivalence classes contained in
S
.
We may assume throughout the proof that
\nu(V_{i})\geq t
for all
i\in[s]
.
Indeed, if
\nu(V_{i})<t
for some
i
, then
|G[V_{i}]|\leq t_{t-1}(n)
.
In this case, the total number of edges in
G
is bounded by

\displaystyle|G|\leq\binom{sn}{2}-\binom{n}{2}+|G[V_{i}]|\leq\binom{sn}{2}-\binom{n}{2}+t_{t-1}(n)\leq\frac{(sn)^{2}}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(t-1)}+s^{2}n,

in which case Theorem
2.1
follows immediately.
Therefore, we proceed under the assumption that

\displaystyle\nu(V_{i})\geq t\quad\text{for all }i\in[s].

(1)

We next grow a balanced clique as far as possible while keeping enough local classes in every unused part.
Let
\tau
be the maximum integer for which there exists a set
S\subseteq V(G)
satisfying:

(i)

S
induces a
(\tau,t)
-balanced clique in
G
,

(ii)

\nu(N_{i}(S))\geq t
for every index
i\in[s]
such that
S\cap V_{i}=\emptyset
,

where we adopt the convention that
N_{i}(\emptyset)\coloneqq V_{i}
.

Let
S
be a set that realizes this maximum
\tau
.
To simplify notation, we may assume without loss of generality that
S
intersects exactly the last
\tau
parts.
Letting
\bar{\tau}\coloneqq s-\tau
, this implies that

\displaystyle S\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{if and only if }i\in[\bar{\tau}+1,s].

Moreover, by defining
S_{i}\coloneqq S\cap V_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle|S_{i}|=t\qquad\text{for every }i\in[\bar{\tau}+1,s].

Claim 2.11
.

\bar{\tau}\in[2,s]
.

Proof of Claim
2.11
.

Since
\bar{\tau}\coloneqq s-\tau
, it suffices to show that
\tau\in[0,\,s-2]
.
We first verify the lower bound,
\tau\geq 0
. The trivial choice of
\tau=0
and
S=\emptyset
satisfies condition
(i)
, while condition
(ii)
holds by (
1
). Since
\tau
is defined as the maximum integer admitting such a set, we conclude
\tau\geq 0
.

For the upper bound, suppose for contradiction that
\tau\geq s-1
. If
\tau=s
, then condition
(i)
gives a balanced
K_{st}
in
G
, a contradiction. Thus
\tau=s-1
. Let
i
be the unique index with
S\cap V_{i}=\emptyset
. By
(ii)
, we have
\nu(N_{i}(S))\geq t
. Choose
t
vertices in
N_{i}(S)
from distinct local equivalence classes. These vertices are adjacent to every vertex of
S
, and Fact
2.9
implies that they form a clique inside
V_{i}
. Hence they extend
S
to a
(s,t)
-balanced clique, contradicting the
(s,t)
-balanced clique freeness of
G
.
Thus
\tau\leq s-2
.

By
(ii)
, we have
\nu\bigl(N_{i}(S)\bigr)\geq t
for each
i\in[\bar{\tau}]
.
For each such
i
, choose one vertex from each of the
t
largest local equivalence classes in
N_{i}(S)
, and denote these vertices by

\displaystyle S_{i}\coloneqq\{v_{1}^{i},\ldots,v_{t}^{i}\},

so that
[v_{1}^{i}],\ldots,[v_{t}^{i}]
are the
t
largest local equivalence classes in
N_{i}(S)
.
For any
i\in[\bar{\tau}]
, the set
S\cup S_{i}
induces a
(\tau+1,t)
-balanced clique in
G
.
Indeed, each vertex of
S_{i}
lies in
N_{i}(S)
, and the vertices of
S_{i}
lie in distinct local equivalence classes, so Fact
2.9
implies that they form a clique inside
V_{i}
.
For each unused part, the maximality of
\tau
gives an obstruction to extending
S
, and we record one such obstruction by the map
f
.
Thus, for each such
i
, there must exist an index
f(i)\in[\bar{\tau}]\setminus\{i\}
such that

\displaystyle\nu\bigl(N_{f(i)}(S\cup S_{i})\bigr)\leq t-1.

Since
f
maps the finite set
[\bar{\tau}]
to itself and has no fixed point, it contains a directed cycle of length at least two.
By relabeling the indices in
[\bar{\tau}]
, we may assume that this cycle is of the form

\displaystyle 1\to 2\to\cdots\to r\to 1\text{\quad for some $r\in[\bar{\tau}]$ with $r\geq 2$.}

In particular, for every
i\in[r]
, we have

\displaystyle\nu\bigl(N_{i+1}(S\cup S_{i})\bigr)\leq t-1,

where the index
i+1
is taken modulo
r
.
We split the degree sum according to the cyclic obstruction, the remaining unused parts, and the original clique
S
.
We define the following three partial sums of degrees:

\displaystyle e_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[r]}\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v),\quad e_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[r+1,\bar{\tau}]}\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v)\quad\text{and}\quad e_{3}\coloneqq\sum_{v\in S}d_{G}(v).

Claim 2.12
.

e_{1}\leq srtn-\sum_{i\in[r]}|N_{i}(S)|.

Proof of Claim
2.12
.

Throughout this claim, the index
i+1
is taken modulo
r
.
For each
i\in[r]
, we estimate the number of edges between
S_{i}
and
V_{i+1}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of local equivalence classes contained in
N_{i+1}(S\cup S_{i})
.
By the definition of
f
along the cycle, we have
|\mathcal{C}|\leq t-1
.
Every vertex in
N_{i+1}(S)
outside the classes in
\mathcal{C}
is adjacent to at most
t-1
vertices of
S_{i}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
consists of at most
t-1
classes contained in
N_{i+1}(S)
, its total size is at most the total size of the
t
largest classes in
N_{i+1}(S)
, which is
\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i+1}]|
by the choice of
S_{i+1}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S_{i}}|N_{i+1}(v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in S_{i}}(|V_{i+1}\setminus N_{i+1}(S)|+|N_{i+1}(v)\cap N_{i+1}(S)|)

\displaystyle\leq|S_{i}|(n-|N_{i+1}(S)|)+(|S_{i}|-1)|N_{i+1}(S)|+|[v_{1}^{i+1}]|+\cdots+|[v_{t}^{i+1}]|

\displaystyle=tn-|N_{i+1}(S)|+\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i+1}]|.

Since
G
is locally symmetrized, the sum of degrees within
V_{i}
for vertices in
S_{i}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S_{i}}|N_{i}(v)|=(n-|[v_{1}^{i}]|)+\cdots+(n-|[v_{t}^{i}]|)=tn-\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i}]|.

Consequently, the total number of edges between
S_{i}
and
V_{i}\cup V_{i+1}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S_{i}}(|N_{i}(v)|+|N_{i+1}(v)|)\leq 2tn-|N_{i+1}(S)|+\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i+1}]|-\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i}]|.

Including the trivial upper bound
|N_{j}(v)|\leq n
for the remaining
s-2
indices
j\notin\{i,i+1\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v)\leq stn-|N_{i+1}(S)|+\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i+1}]|-\sum_{j\in[t]}|[v_{j}^{i}]|.

Summing this inequality over all
i\in[r]
, the terms involving the sizes of equivalence classes telescope and cancel out, yielding

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[r]}\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v)\leq r\cdot stn-\sum_{i\in[r]}|N_{i+1}(S)|=srtn-\sum_{i\in[r]}|N_{i}(S)|.

This completes the proof.

Using the trivial upper bound
d_{G}(v)\leq sn
, we estimate the second sum as

\displaystyle e_{2}=\sum_{i\in[r+1,\bar{\tau}]}\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v)\leq(\bar{\tau}-r)t\cdot sn.

(2)

For the third sum
e_{3}
, we observe that for
i\in[r]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}|N_{i}(v)|\leq(|S|-1)n+|N_{i}(S)|.

This yields

\displaystyle e_{3}=\sum_{v\in S}d_{G}(v)=\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{j\in[r]}|N_{j}(v)|+\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{j\in[r+1,s]}|N_{j}(v)|\leq s\tau tn+\sum_{i\in[r]}\bigl(|N_{i}(S)|-n\bigr).

(3)

Together with the original sets
S_{i}=S\cap V_{i}
for
i\in[\bar{\tau}+1,s]
, the sets
S_{i}
are now defined for every
i\in[s]
, and each has size
t
.
Combining Claim
2.12
, (
2
), and (
3
), the total degree sum over all
S_{i}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[s]}\sum_{v\in S_{i}}d_{G}(v)

\displaystyle=e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\left(srtn-\sum_{i\in[r]}|N_{i}(S)|\right)+(\bar{\tau}-r)tsn+\left(s\tau tn+\sum_{i\in[r]}\bigl(|N_{i}(S)|-n\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\leq s^{2}tn-rn.

By averaging, there exists a tuple
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})\in S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{s}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[s]}d_{G}(v_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{2}tn-rn}{t}=s^{2}n-\frac{rn}{t}.

Since
r\geq 2
, this is at most
s^{2}n-\frac{2n}{t}
, contradicting Claim
2.10
.

2.4  Proof of the
\ell_{2}
-Norm Bound

For a graph
G
, let

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v)^{2},\qquad s_{G}(v)\coloneqq\lVert G\rVert_{2}-\lVert G-v\rVert_{2}.

Then

\displaystyle s_{G}(v)=d_{G}(v)^{2}+\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}(2d_{G}(u)-1).

(4)

Summing (
4
) over
v
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)}s_{G}(v)=3\lVert G\rVert_{2}-2|G|.

Indeed, the first term contributes
\lVert G\rVert_{2}
, while each vertex
u
contributes
d_{G}(u)(2d_{G}(u)-1)
to the second term. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)}\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}(2d_{G}(u)-1)=2\lVert G\rVert_{2}-\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)=2\lVert G\rVert_{2}-2|G|,

which gives the desired identity.
If
S=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}\subseteq V(G)
and
G_{i}\coloneqq G-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}
with
G_{0}\coloneqq G
, then

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}-\lVert G-S\rVert_{2}=\sum_{i\in[s]}\bigl(\lVert G_{i-1}\rVert_{2}-\lVert G_{i}\rVert_{2}\bigr)=\sum_{i\in[s]}s_{G_{i-1}}(v_{i})\leq\sum_{v\in S}s_{G}(v),

since
s_{G^{\prime}}(v)\leq s_{G}(v)
whenever
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
.

We also record the corresponding identity for
3
-graphs, which will be used later.
For a
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and a vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, let

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}-\lVert\mathcal{H}-v\rVert_{2},\qquad s(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\frac{1}{|V(\mathcal{H})|}\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}s_{\mathcal{H}}(v).

Then, as in
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
,

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\rVert_{2}+\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(2d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1).

(5)

Indeed, let
\mathcal{P}_{v}\coloneqq\bigl\{\{v,u\}\colon u\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}\bigr\}
. Then

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{P}_{v}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)^{2}+\sum_{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}}{2}}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)^{2}-d_{\mathcal{H}-v}(e)^{2}\right).

If
e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}}{2}
, then
d_{\mathcal{H}-v}(e)=d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1
when
e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
, and
d_{\mathcal{H}-v}(e)=d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)
otherwise. Hence

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(vu)^{2}+\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(2d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1)

\displaystyle=\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\rVert_{2}+\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(2d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1),

since
d_{L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(u)=d_{\mathcal{H}}(vu)
for every
u\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}
.

Summing (
5
) over
v
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}s_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=4\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}-3|\mathcal{H}|.

(6)

Indeed,
\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\rVert_{2}=2\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}
, and each pair
e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{2}
contributes
d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)\bigl(2d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1\bigr)
to the second term. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(2d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1)=2\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}-\sum_{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)=2\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}-3|\mathcal{H}|,

which gives (
6
).
We now prove the
\ell_{2}
-norm bound for balanced
K_{2s}
-free graphs.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

We proceed by induction on
n
.
The induction deletes one vertex from each part. If a low-degree vertex exists, the loss of the
\ell_{2}
-norm is small. Otherwise the edge bound controls the total degree defect.
For
n=2
,

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}\leq|V(G)|^{3}=(2s)^{3}=2s^{3}n^{2},

so the statement holds.

Now let
n\geq 3
, assume that the statement holds for
n-1
, and let
G
be a balanced
K_{2s}
-free graph with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{s}
, each of size
n
.
By Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle|G|\leq\frac{(s^{2}-1)n^{2}}{2}+s^{2}n.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V(G)}d_{G}(x)=2|G|\leq(s^{2}-1)n^{2}+2s^{2}n.

(7)

Suppose first that there is a vertex
v_{1}\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(v_{1})<(s-1)n
. By symmetry, we may assume that
v_{1}\in V_{1}
, and choose arbitrary vertices
(v_{2},\ldots,v_{s})\in V_{2}\times\cdots\times V_{s}
.
By (
4
),

\displaystyle s_{G}(v_{1})\leq d_{G}(v_{1})^{2}+\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v_{1})}2d_{G}(u)\leq d_{G}(v_{1})^{2}+2sn\,d_{G}(v_{1})\leq(s-1)^{2}n^{2}+2s(s-1)n^{2}.

For each
i\in[2,s]
, we similarly have

\displaystyle s_{G}(v_{i})\leq d_{G}(v_{i})^{2}+\sum_{u\in V(G)}2d_{G}(u)\leq s^{2}n^{2}+2(s^{2}-1)n^{2}+4s^{2}n=(3s^{2}-2)n^{2}+4s^{2}n,

where we used
d_{G}(v_{i})\leq sn
and (
7
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}-\lVert G-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[s]}s_{G}(v_{i})

\displaystyle\leq(s-1)^{2}n^{2}+2s(s-1)n^{2}+(s-1)\bigl((3s^{2}-2)n^{2}+4s^{2}n\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq(3s^{3}-6s+3)n^{2}+4s^{3}n.

Writing
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}
, we note that
G^{\prime}
remains balanced
K_{2s}
-free with parts
V_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
, each of size
n-1
.
Applying the induction hypothesis to
G^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert G^{\prime}\rVert_{2}+(3s^{3}-6s+3)n^{2}+4s^{3}n

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(s^{2}(s-1)+(s-1)^{2}\bigr)(n-1)^{3}+4s^{3}(n-1)^{2}+(3s^{3}-6s+3)n^{2}+4s^{3}n

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(s^{2}(s-1)+(s-1)^{2}\bigr)n^{3}+4s^{3}n^{2}.

The last inequality follows by expanding. The difference between the final right-hand side and the preceding expression is

\displaystyle(s^{3}+6s-3)n-(3s^{3}+2s-1)\geq 0

for
s\geq 2
and
n\geq 3
.
Thus the theorem holds in this case.

We may therefore assume that
(s-1)n\leq d_{G}(v)\leq sn
for every
v\in V(G)
. Since

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v)^{2},

it remains to bound the degree square sum under these constraints.

If
n\leq 2s^{2}
, then the trivial bound
d_{G}(v)\leq sn
gives

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v)^{2}\leq sn\cdot(sn)^{2}=s^{3}n^{3}\leq(s^{2}(s-1)+(s-1)^{2})n^{3}+4s^{3}n^{2},

where the last inequality follows from
n\leq 2s^{2}
, since it is equivalent to
(2s-1)n\leq 4s^{3}
.
Thus we may assume that
n>2s^{2}
.

For each vertex
v
, let
\delta_{v}\coloneqq sn-d_{G}(v)
. Then
0\leq\delta_{v}\leq n
, and by (
7
),

\displaystyle\sum_{v}\delta_{v}=s^{2}n^{2}-\sum_{v}d_{G}(v)\geq s^{2}n^{2}-\bigl((s^{2}-1)n^{2}+2s^{2}n\bigr)=n(n-2s^{2}),

Since
\sum_{v}\delta_{v}^{2}\leq n\sum_{v}\delta_{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v}d_{G}(v)^{2}=\sum_{v}(sn-\delta_{v})^{2}=s^{3}n^{3}-2sn\sum_{v}\delta_{v}+\sum_{v}\delta_{v}^{2}\leq s^{3}n^{3}-(2s-1)n\sum_{v}\delta_{v}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\lVert G\rVert_{2}=\sum_{v}d_{G}(v)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq s^{3}n^{3}-(2s-1)n\sum_{v}\delta_{v}

\displaystyle\leq s^{3}n^{3}-(2s-1)n^{2}(n-2s^{2})

\displaystyle=(s^{2}(s-1)+(s-1)^{2})n^{3}+(4s^{3}-2s^{2})n^{2}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

2.5  Proof of the Crossing-Triangle Bound

For a locally symmetrized graph
G
, let

\displaystyle V_{1}=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V_{2}=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{q}

be the decompositions into equivalence classes, and write

\displaystyle a_{i}\coloneqq|A_{i}|,\qquad b_{j}\coloneqq|B_{j}|.

We define an auxiliary bipartite graph
H
with vertex classes
[p]
and
[q]
, regarded as disjoint copies, by declaring that
ij\in H
if and only if
G[A_{i},B_{j}]
is complete.

Lemma 2.13
.

The auxiliary graph
H
is
K_{2,2}
-free.

Proof of Lemma
2.13
.

Suppose that
i_{1},i_{2}\in[p]
and
j_{1},j_{2}\in[q]
span a copy of
K_{2,2}
in
H
.
Choosing arbitrary vertices
u_{t}\in A_{i_{t}}
and
v_{t}\in B_{j_{t}}
for
t\in[2]
, we obtain a balanced
K_{4}
in
G
, a contradiction.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

By Proposition
2.8
, we may assume that
G
is locally symmetrized.
The auxiliary graph converts balanced
K_{4}
-freeness into a
K_{2,2}
-free condition, which lets us count crossing triangles through common neighborhoods in
H
.
Let
T_{2,1}
and
T_{1,2}
denote the numbers of crossing triangles with two vertices in
V_{1}
and in
V_{2}
, respectively.
Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)=T_{2,1}+T_{1,2}.

For a fixed class
B_{j}
, every crossing triangle counted by
T_{2,1}
that uses a vertex of
B_{j}
arises from a pair of distinct classes
A_{i},A_{k}
with
i,k\in N_{H}(j)
.
Conversely, every such choice gives crossing triangles, since
G[A_{i},A_{k}]
is complete by Fact
2.9
, while
G[A_{i},B_{j}]
and
G[A_{k},B_{j}]
are complete by the definition of
H
.
Hence

\displaystyle T_{2,1}=\sum_{j\in[q]}b_{j}\sum_{\{i,k\}\subseteq N_{H}(j)}a_{i}a_{k}.

Let
a\coloneqq\max_{i\in[p]}a_{i}
and
b\coloneqq\max_{j\in[q]}b_{j}
.
Since
H
is
K_{2,2}
-free by Lemma
2.13
, we have

\displaystyle|N_{H}(i)\cap N_{H}(k)|\leq 1\qquad\text{for all }1\leq i<k\leq p.

It follows that

\displaystyle T_{2,1}

\displaystyle\leq b\sum_{j\in[q]}\sum_{\{i,k\}\subseteq N_{H}(j)}a_{i}a_{k}

\displaystyle=b\sum_{1\leq i<k\leq p}a_{i}a_{k}\cdot|N_{H}(i)\cap N_{H}(k)|

\displaystyle\leq b\sum_{1\leq i<k\leq p}a_{i}a_{k}=b\left(\binom{n}{2}-\sum_{i\in[p]}\binom{a_{i}}{2}\right)\leq b\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right).

By symmetry,

\displaystyle T_{1,2}\leq a\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{b}{2}\right).

Since
n\binom{n}{2}
is the number of crossing triangles in each of the split graphs
\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2}]
and
\Lambda[V_{2};V_{1}]
, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle n\binom{n}{2}-(T_{1,2}+T_{2,1})

\displaystyle\geq n\binom{n}{2}-\left(b\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right)+a\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{b}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(a+b-n)(a+b-n-1)}{2}+\frac{(n-a)(n-b)(a+b-2)}{2}\geq 0.

The last inequality holds because
a+b-n
is an integer and
1\leq a,b\leq n
, so both terms in the final expression are nonnegative.
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\leq T_{1,2}+T_{2,1}\leq n\binom{n}{2}.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

The proof first shows that near-extremality forces one color class to contain a large independent set and the other color class to be almost complete.
Let
T_{2,1}
and
T_{1,2}
denote the numbers of crossing triangles in
G
with two vertices in
V_{1}
and in
V_{2}
, respectively.
Let
\alpha_{1}\coloneqq\alpha(G[V_{1}])
and
\alpha_{2}\coloneqq\alpha(G[V_{2}])
, where
\alpha(F)
denotes the independence number of a graph
F
.
For every edge
e\in G[V_{1}]
, the set
N_{G}(e)\cap V_{2}
is independent in
G[V_{2}]
, since otherwise
e
together with an edge of
G[V_{2}]
inside this common neighborhood would span a balanced copy of
K_{4}
in
G
.
Therefore

\displaystyle T_{2,1}\leq\alpha_{2}|G[V_{1}]|\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{1,2}\leq\alpha_{1}|G[V_{2}]|.

Also,

\displaystyle|G[V_{i}]|\leq\binom{n}{2}-\binom{\alpha_{i}}{2}\qquad\text{for }i\in[2].

Set
x\coloneqq\alpha_{1}/n
and
y\coloneqq\alpha_{2}/n
. Then the hypothesis gives

\displaystyle n\binom{n}{2}-\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)=T_{2,1}+T_{1,2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{2}\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{\alpha_{1}}{2}\right)+\alpha_{1}\left(\binom{n}{2}-\binom{\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{3}}{2}\bigl(y(1-x^{2})+x(1-y^{2})\bigr)=\frac{n^{3}}{2}(x+y)(1-xy).

We shall choose
N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
at the end so that
1/n\leq\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
. Since

\displaystyle n\binom{n}{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2n}\right)n^{3},

it follows that
(x+y)(1-xy)\geq 1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
.

Write
S\coloneqq x+y
and
P\coloneqq xy
. Since

\displaystyle 1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq S(1-P)\leq S,

we have
S\geq 1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
. If
S\leq 1
, then
|S-1|\leq 3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
. If
S\geq 1
, then
(1-x)(1-y)\geq 0
gives
P\geq S-1
, and hence

\displaystyle 1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq S(1-P)\leq S(2-S)=1-(S-1)^{2}.

Thus
S\leq 1+2\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}
. Returning to
1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq S(1-P)
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-P\geq\frac{1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}}{S}\geq\frac{1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}}{1+2\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}},

and therefore
P\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}
for some absolute constant
C
.
In the rest of the proof,
C
denotes a sufficiently large absolute constant, which may be enlarged finitely many times.

By symmetry, it suffices to treat the case
x\geq y
. If
\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq 1/9
, then
x\geq S/2\geq(1-3\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}})/2\geq 1/3
, so

\displaystyle y=\frac{P}{x}\leq 3C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x=S-y\geq 1-C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2},

after enlarging
C
if necessary.

Let
r\coloneqq n-\alpha_{1}
. Then
r\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n
, and hence

\displaystyle|G[V_{1}]|\leq\binom{n}{2}-\binom{\alpha_{1}}{2}\leq rn\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n^{2}.

Consequently,
T_{2,1}\leq\alpha_{2}|G[V_{1}]|\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}
, and therefore

\displaystyle T_{1,2}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)-T_{2,1}\geq n\binom{n}{2}-C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}.

(8)

Since every edge of
G[V_{2}]
lies in at most
n
crossing triangles of type
V_{1}V_{2}V_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|G[V_{2}]|\geq\frac{T_{1,2}}{n}\geq\binom{n}{2}-C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{2}.

Let
\overline{G}
denote the complement of
G
on
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
.
Thus

\displaystyle|\overline{G}[V_{2}]|=\binom{n}{2}-|G[V_{2}]|\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{2}.

It remains to control the missing cross edges.
For each
b\in V_{2}
, set

\displaystyle x_{b}\coloneqq\bigl|\{a\in V_{1}\colon ab\notin G\}\bigr|,

and thus

\displaystyle|\overline{G}[V_{1},V_{2}]|=\sum_{b\in V_{2}}x_{b}.

An absent triangle of type
V_{1}V_{2}V_{2}
is a triple
\{a,b,b^{\prime}\}
with
a\in V_{1}
and
bb^{\prime}\in G[V_{2}]
that is not a triangle of
G
, which happens exactly when at least one of
ab
and
ab^{\prime}
is missing.
Thus the number of absent triangles of type
V_{1}V_{2}V_{2}
is
n|G[V_{2}]|-T_{1,2}
.
Charging each absent triangle to its missing cross edges, each absent triangle is charged at most twice. Hence, by (
8
),

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in V_{2}}x_{b}d_{G[V_{2}]}(b)\leq 2\bigl(n|G[V_{2}]|-T_{1,2}\bigr)\leq 2C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}.

(9)

Let

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigl\{b\in V_{2}\colon d_{G[V_{2}]}(b)\geq n-1-C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n\bigr\}.

Then for any
b\in V_{2}\setminus V_{2}^{\prime}
, we have
n-1-d_{G[V_{2}]}(b)>C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n
.
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in V_{2}}(n-1-d_{G[V_{2}]}(b))=2|\overline{G}[V_{2}]|\leq 2C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{2},

and every vertex
b\in V_{2}\setminus V_{2}^{\prime}
contributes more than
C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n
to this sum, we obtain

\displaystyle|V_{2}\setminus V_{2}^{\prime}|\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n,

after enlarging
C
if necessary.
Hence the number of missing cross edges incident to
V_{2}\setminus V_{2}^{\prime}
is at most
n|V_{2}\setminus V_{2}^{\prime}|\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n^{2}
.
For each
b\in V_{2}^{\prime}
, if
C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}\leq 1/4
and
n\geq 4
, then

\displaystyle d_{G[V_{2}]}(b)\geq n-1-\frac{n}{4}\geq\frac{n}{2}.

Hence, by (
9
),

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}\sum_{b\in V_{2}^{\prime}}x_{b}\leq\sum_{b\in V_{2}^{\prime}}x_{b}d_{G[V_{2}]}(b)\leq 2C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{3}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in V_{2}^{\prime}}x_{b}\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}n^{2},

after enlarging
C
if necessary, and hence

\displaystyle|\overline{G}[V_{1},V_{2}]|=\sum_{b\in V_{2}}x_{b}\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n^{2}.

Thus
G
can be transformed into
\Lambda[V_{1};V_{2}]
by deleting all edges of
G[V_{1}]
, adding all missing edges inside
V_{2}
, and adding all missing cross edges between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. The total number of changes is at most

\displaystyle|G[V_{1}]|+|\overline{G}[V_{2}]|+|\overline{G}[V_{1},V_{2}]|\leq C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n^{2}.

If the original graph has
y>x
, the same argument with the roles of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
exchanged shows that
G
can be transformed into
\Lambda[V_{2};V_{1}]
by changing at most
C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}n^{2}
edges.
We now choose the parameters so that all smallness assumptions used above and the required edit-distance bound hold.
At this point
C
is fixed. Choose
\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{9},\frac{1}{16C^{2}},\frac{\eta^{2}}{C^{2}}\right\}.

Then
C\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}^{1/2}\leq\min\{\eta,1/4\}
, and the condition
\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\leq 1/9
used above holds.
Finally, choose
N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
such that

\displaystyle N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}\geq\max\left\{4,\left\lceil\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}}\right\rceil\right\}.

Then
1/n\leq\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
and
n\geq 4
for all
n\geq N_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}
.
This completes the proof of Proposition
2.6
.

3  Local structure near the bipartite construction

In this section, we collect the common structural facts for
3
-graphs close to the bipartite construction. These facts do not depend on the objective function. They will be used in both the
\ell_{2}
-norm problem and the clique-counting problem.

We first introduce the notation used in this section.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-graph. Given a bipartition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
, let
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
denote the complete bipartite
3
-graph with parts
V_{1},V_{2}
, whose edges are the triples of type
V_{1}V_{1}V_{2}
or
V_{1}V_{2}V_{2}
.
We write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\cap\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}].

We say that
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
is a maximum partition of
\mathcal{H}
if it maximizes
|\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]|
over all bipartitions of
V(\mathcal{H})
.
With respect to the same partition, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}[V_{1},V_{2}]\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}[V_{1},V_{2}]\coloneqq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\setminus\mathcal{H}.

We call the edges in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}[V_{1},V_{2}]
bad edges, and the edges in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{H}}[V_{1},V_{2}]
missing edges.
When there is no ambiguity, we write simply
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}
.

For pairs, we use

\displaystyle K[V_{1},V_{2}]\coloneqq\bigl\{\{x,y\}\colon x\in V_{1},\ y\in V_{2}\bigr\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]\coloneqq\binom{V_{1}}{2}\cup\binom{V_{2}}{2},

so
K[V_{1},V_{2}]
is the set of cross pairs and
\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]
is the set of same-side pairs.

For a family
\mathcal{F}
of triples and a set
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(S)\coloneqq\{F\in\mathcal{F}\colon S\subseteq F\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{\mathcal{F}}(S)\coloneqq|\mathcal{F}(S)|.

If
e
is a pair, we also write

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(e)\coloneqq\{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\colon e\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{F}\},

so that
d_{\mathcal{F}}(e)=|N_{\mathcal{F}}(e)|
. The maximum degree of
\mathcal{F}
is

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{F}}(v).

Let
\partial\mathcal{H}
denote the shadow of
\mathcal{H}
. For a vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, its link graph is

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq\{xy\in\partial\mathcal{H}\colon vxy\in\mathcal{H}\}.

For convenience, we view
L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
as a graph on
V(\mathcal{H})
.
If
L
is a graph and
X,Y
are disjoint vertex sets, then
L[X]
denotes the subgraph of
L
induced by
X
, and
L[X,Y]
denotes the bipartite subgraph of
L
induced between
X
and
Y
.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{n}
denote the balanced complete bipartite
3
-graph on
n
vertices. A simple calculation gives

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}_{n}|=\frac{n-2}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Let
\delta>0
. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is
\delta
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
if there exists a balanced partition
U_{1}\cup U_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
, that is,
\bigl||U_{1}|-|U_{2}|\bigr|\leq 1
, such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}[U_{1},U_{2}]|\leq\delta n^{3}.

By comparing the crossing triples containing a vertex before and after moving it to the other part in a maximum partition, we obtain the following fact.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
3
-graph, and let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
be a maximum partition. Fix
i\in[2]
and
v\in V_{i}
, and let
L\coloneqq L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
. Then

\displaystyle|L[V_{3-i}]|\geq|L[V_{i}]|.

The next lemma records the elementary consequences of being close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
with respect to a maximum partition.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\delta>0
be sufficiently small, and let
n
be sufficiently large.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex
3
-graph which is
\delta
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
be a maximum partition of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}
be the bad and missing edges with respect to this partition.
Then we have

(i)

\max\{|\mathcal{B}|,|\mathcal{M}|\}\leq 2\delta n^{3}
,

(ii)

\bigl||V_{i}|-n/2\bigr|\leq 10\delta^{1/2}n
for each
i\in[2]
,

(iii)

\bigl||\mathcal{H}|-|\mathbb{B}_{n}|\bigr|\leq\delta n^{3}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is
\delta
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, there is a balanced partition
U_{1}\cup U_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}[U_{1},U_{2}]|\leq\delta n^{3}.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{\ast}\coloneqq\mathbb{B}[U_{1},U_{2}]
.
Then
\mathbb{B}^{\ast}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
, and hence
|\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|=|\mathbb{B}_{n}|
.

The symmetric-difference bound gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\geq|\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|-|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\geq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\delta n^{3}.

Since
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
is a maximum partition, it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]|\geq|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\geq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\delta n^{3}.

(10)

Since
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|=n
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]|=\frac{|V_{1}||V_{2}|(n-2)}{2}.

This is maximized when the partition is balanced. Hence, we have
|\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]|\leq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|
.
It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|=|\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]|-|\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]|\leq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\bigl(|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\delta n^{3}\bigr)=\delta n^{3}.

Also,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\leq|\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|+|\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\leq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|+|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\leq|\mathbb{B}_{n}|+\delta n^{3}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}|=|\mathcal{H}|-|\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]|\leq\bigl(|\mathbb{B}_{n}|+\delta n^{3}\bigr)-\bigl(|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\delta n^{3}\bigr)=2\delta n^{3}.

This proves part
(i)
.

It remains to bound the two parts.
Since
|\mathbb{B}_{n}|=\frac{n-2}{2}\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
and
\mathcal{H}[V_{1},V_{2}]\subseteq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
, the lower bound in (
10
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{|V_{1}||V_{2}|(n-2)}{2}\geq\frac{n-2}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-\delta n^{3}.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-|V_{1}||V_{2}|\leq\frac{2\delta n^{3}}{n-2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{4}-|V_{1}||V_{2}|=\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4}-\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\right)+\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-|V_{1}||V_{2}|\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2\delta n^{3}}{n-2}.

Since
|V_{2}|=n-|V_{1}|
, this implies

\displaystyle\left(|V_{1}|-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{4}-|V_{1}|(n-|V_{1}|)=\frac{n^{2}}{4}-|V_{1}||V_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2\delta n^{3}}{n-2}\leq 100\delta n^{2}.

The last inequality holds because
\delta
is sufficiently small and
n
is sufficiently large. Therefore
\bigl||V_{1}|-n/2\bigr|\leq 10\delta^{1/2}n
. The same estimate holds for
V_{2}
, since
|V_{2}|=n-|V_{1}|
. Hence part
(ii)
follows.

Finally,

\displaystyle\bigl||\mathcal{H}|-|\mathbb{B}_{n}|\bigr|=\bigl||\mathcal{H}|-|\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\bigr|\leq|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}^{\ast}|\leq\delta n^{3}.

This proves part
(iii)
, and completes the proof.

We next record the colored removal lemma in the form needed below.

Lemma 3.3

(Colored Graph Removal Lemma
[
10
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and every pair of positive integers
f,k
, there exists
\delta_{\ref{LEM:common-colored-removal}}\coloneqq\delta_{\ref{LEM:common-colored-removal}}(\varepsilon,f,k)>0
such that the following holds.
Let
\phi:E(F)\to[k]
be a
k
-edge-coloring of a graph
F
on
f
vertices, and let
\psi:E(G)\to[k]
be a
k
-edge-coloring of a graph
G
on
n
vertices.
If the number of copies of
F
with coloring
\phi
in the coloring
\psi
of
G
is at most
\delta_{\ref{LEM:common-colored-removal}}n^{f}
, then all such copies can be removed by deleting at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges of
G
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
X\cup Y
be a bipartition of
V(G)
. A copy of
K_{4}
in
G
is balanced with respect to the partition
X\cup Y
if it has two vertices in
X
and two vertices in
Y
.
The following lemma explains why balanced
K_{4}
’s in the link graphs are controlled by missing edges.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\{\{x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\}:x,x^{\prime}\in V_{1},\ y,y^{\prime}\in V_{2},\ xx^{\prime}\in G[V_{1}],\ yy^{\prime}\in G[V_{2}]\}.

in

4.3  The local replacement step

We first record the bipartite calculation needed at the end of the exact argument. It says that among all
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graphs, the complete balanced bipartite construction has maximum
\ell_{2}
-norm.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
n\geq 4
, and let
\mathcal{G}
be an
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graph. Then

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{G}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2},

and equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is an
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graph with bipartition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{G})
. Since adding edges cannot decrease the
\ell_{2}
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{G}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\rVert_{2},

and equality in this inequality is possible only when
\mathcal{G}
is the complete bipartite
3
-graph with this bipartition, namely
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
.

Put
m\coloneqq|V_{1}||V_{2}|
. In
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
, pairs inside
V_{1}
have codegree
|V_{2}|
, pairs inside
V_{2}
have codegree
|V_{1}|
, and cross pairs have codegree
n-2
. Hence

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\rVert_{2}=\binom{|V_{1}|}{2}|V_{2}|^{2}+\binom{|V_{2}|}{2}|V_{1}|^{2}+|V_{1}||V_{2}|(n-2)^{2}=m\left(m-\frac{n}{2}+(n-2)^{2}\right).

For
n\geq 4
, the expression on the right is strictly increasing in
m
. Since
m\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
, with equality if and only if the bipartition is balanced, we have
\lVert\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2}
. Equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
and the bipartition is balanced. This proves Lemma
4.6
.

We also need the local replacement estimate. It will be applied only to
3
-graphs obtained during the replacement procedure, so no forbidden-subgraph condition is needed in its statement.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists

\mathcal{M}
be the corresponding sets of bad and missing edges. Lemma
3.2
, together with the choice of
\delta_{0}
, gives

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[2]}\left||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq 10\delta_{0}^{1/2}n\leq\xi n.

(25)

Lemma
4.5
and the choice of
\eta
give

\displaystyle\max\{\Delta(\mathcal{B}),\Delta(\mathcal{M})\}\leq 2\eta n^{2}\leq\xi n^{2}.

(26)

By Lemma
3.5
, every bad edge
E\in\mathcal{B}
contains a pair
e\subseteq E
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq n/10
. Moreover, for every
e\in\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq d_{\mathcal{B}}(e)-7\eta^{1/2}n=d_{\mathcal{B}}(e)-\xi n.

(27)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{B}\neq\emptyset
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]:d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq\frac{n}{10}\text{ and }\mathcal{B}(e)\neq\emptyset\right\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}.

Since every bad edge lies inside one part, the preceding paragraph shows that every bad edge contains some pair from
\mathcal{I}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}
. For
j\in[k]
, once
\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{j-1}
have been defined, we proceed as follows. If
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})=\emptyset
, then set
\mathcal{H}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}_{j-1}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{j-1}
. Otherwise set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}\coloneqq(\mathcal{H}_{j-1}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j}))\cup\mathcal{M}_{j-1}(e_{j}),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{j}\setminus\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}],\qquad\mathcal{M}_{j}\coloneqq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\setminus\mathcal{H}_{j}.

This procedure only deletes bad edges and only adds edges of
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{k}.

Every edge of
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
contains exactly one same-side pair. Hence, before step
j
, the additions made at pairs different from
e_{j}
do not affect the missing edges containing
e_{j}
. Consider a step
j
with
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Then
e_{j}\in\partial\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
, and

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{M}_{j-1}}(e_{j})=d_{\mathcal{M}}(e_{j})\geq\frac{n}{10}\geq 47\xi^{1/2}n,\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}_{j-1}}(e_{j})\geq d_{\mathcal{B}_{j-1}}(e_{j})-\xi n.

The second inequality follows from the equality above, (
27
), and the inclusion
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. Together with (
25
) and (
26
), the inclusions
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
show that Lemma
4.7
applies to
\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
with the fixed partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and the pair
e_{j}
. Hence
\lVert\mathcal{H}_{j}\rVert_{2}>\lVert\mathcal{H}_{j-1}\rVert_{2}
for every step with
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
, while the other steps leave the graph unchanged.

At least one step is nontrivial. Indeed, choose an original bad edge
E\in\mathcal{B}
and a pair
e_{j}\subseteq E
with
e_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
. If
E
is still present before step
j
, then
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Otherwise,
E
was removed in an earlier nontrivial step. Therefore

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{H}_{k}\rVert_{2}>\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}.

Since each original bad edge contains a pair from
\mathcal{I}
, it is removed when one such pair is processed, unless it was removed earlier. Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{k}
has no bad edge, and so
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
. By Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}<\lVert\mathcal{H}_{k}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2},

a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{B}=\emptyset
.

If
\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
, then adding any edge of
\mathcal{M}
keeps the graph bipartite and hence
K_{5}^{3}
-free. It also strictly increases the
\ell_{2}
-norm, since the codegrees of the three pairs contained in this edge all increase by one. This contradicts the extremality of
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
, and so
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
. Finally, Lemma
4.6
gives
\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\rVert_{2}
, while extremality gives the reverse inequality. Hence equality holds in Lemma
4.6
, and so
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
. This proves that every extremal graph is isomorphic to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, and completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

5  Counting cliques in
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graphs

For a fixed
3
-graph
F
, the number of induced copies of
F
in
\mathcal{H}
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\left|\left\{S\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{|V(F)|}\colon\mathcal{H}[S]\cong F\right\}\right|.

For complete
3
-graphs, induced and non-induced copies coincide, and we write
\mathrm{N}(K_{t}^{3},\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{ind}}(K_{t}^{3},\mathcal{H})
.
Since a
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph has no cliques of size at least
5
, we have

\displaystyle k(\mathcal{H})=1+n+\binom{n}{2}+|\mathcal{H}|+\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H}).

(28)

Thus, by (
28
), to prove Theorem
1.5
, it is enough to maximize

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq|\mathcal{H}|+\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H}).

In this section, we first establish a stability result for
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graphs with many copies of
K_{4}^{3}
. We then apply a local modification argument as in Section
4
and finally prove Theorem
1.5
.

5.1  The edge-stability result

We first prove the stability statement that will be used in the exact argument.
Let
E_{t}
denote the empty
3
-graph on
t
vertices. Let
K_{4}^{3-}
and

be obtained from the complete

and

-vertex

be the

-vertex

The certificate in Bodnár

t\coloneqq|x-1/2|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{64}-\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{128}\leq\frac{1}{n^{4}}\binom{|A|}{2}\binom{|B|}{2}\leq\frac{(|A||B|)^{2}}{4n^{4}}\leq\frac{x^{2}(1-x)^{2}}{4}=\frac{1}{64}-\frac{t^{2}}{8}+\frac{t^{4}}{4}\leq\frac{1}{64}-\frac{t^{2}}{16}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left||A|-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq 4\left(\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{128}\right)^{1/2}n.

Move at most
4(\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}+\varepsilon^{2}/128)^{1/2}n+1
vertices from the larger part to the smaller one, and let
U_{1}\cup U_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
be the resulting balanced partition. Moving one vertex changes at most
n^{2}/2
edges of the corresponding bipartite
3
-graph. Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}\triangle\mathbb{B}[U_{1},U_{2}]|\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{128}+2\left(\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{128}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2n}\right)n^{3}\leq\varepsilon n^{3},

by the choice of
\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}
and
N_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}
. This proves that
\mathcal{H}
is
\varepsilon
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

5.2  Local degree control near
\mathbb{B}_{n}

We next prove the local control needed for the clique-counting replacement argument. The first input is the corresponding near-regularity statement for the number of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
containing a fixed vertex.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
K_{5}^{3}
-free
n
-vertex
3
-graph such that

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H})=\max\{\Psi(\mathcal{G}):\mathcal{G}\text{ is a }K_{5}^{3}\text{-free }3\text{-graph on }n\text{ vertices}\}.

For each
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, let
\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
denote the number of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
in
\mathcal{H}
containing
v
. Then for every vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\left|\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-\frac{4\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H})}{n}\right|\leq 2n^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

Fix distinct vertices
u,v\in V(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{u\to v}
be the
3
-graph obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by deleting all edges containing
u
, and then adding
e\cup\{u\}
for every pair
e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{u,v\}}{2}
such that
e\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{H}
. This operation preserves
K_{5}^{3}
-freeness. Indeed, no edge of
\mathcal{H}_{u\to v}
contains both
u
and
v
, and any copy of
K_{5}^{3}
using
u
would give a copy of
K_{5}^{3}
in
\mathcal{H}
after replacing
u
with
v
.

All edges and copies of
K_{4}^{3}
avoiding
u
remain present. Moreover, every edge and every copy of
K_{4}^{3}
counted at
v
whose vertex set avoids
u
gives, after replacing
v
with
u
, the corresponding edge or copy of
K_{4}^{3}
in
\mathcal{H}_{u\to v}
. The number of edges counted by
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
that also contain
u
is at most
n
, and the number of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
counted by
\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
that also contain
u
is at most
\binom{n}{2}
. Hence

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{u\to v})\geq\Psi(\mathcal{H})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(u)-\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(u)+d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-n^{2}.

By the extremality of
\mathcal{H}
, we have
\Psi(\mathcal{H})\geq\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{u\to v})
. Since
0\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(x)\leq\binom{n-1}{2}
for every vertex
x\in V(\mathcal{H})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\geq\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-2n^{2}.

Interchanging
u
and
v
gives
|\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(u)-\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|\leq 2n^{2}
. Finally,
\sum_{x\in V(\mathcal{H})}\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(x)=4\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H})
, and the lemma follows.

The next lemma is the clique-counting analogue of Lemma
4.5
. It shows that, once an extremal graph is known to be close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, no vertex is incident to many bad edges or many missing edges with respect to a maximum partition.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\eta>0
be sufficiently small. There exist
\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}\coloneqq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}(\eta)>0
and
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}\coloneqq N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}(\eta)
such that the following holds for all
n\geq N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph with

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H})=\max\{\Psi(\mathcal{G}):\mathcal{G}\text{ is a }K_{5}^{3}\text{-free }3\text{-graph on }n\text{ vertices}\}.

Suppose further that
\mathcal{H}
is
\delta
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
for some
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
.
Let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
be a maximum partition of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}
be the bad and missing edges with respect to this partition. Then

\displaystyle\max\{\Delta(\mathcal{B}),\Delta(\mathcal{M})\}\leq 2\eta n^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Choose
\alpha>0
sufficiently small so that
\alpha\leq\frac{\eta}{100}
and
\alpha\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}(\eta/8)}{100}
.
Choose

sufficiently small so that Lemma
3.2
is applicable with parameter
\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
, and so that

\displaystyle\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:common-balanced-k4-missing}}(\alpha),\qquad\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2},\qquad 50\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2}+5\alpha\leq\min\left\{\frac{\eta}{4},\frac{\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}(\eta/8)}{16}\right\}.

Choose
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
sufficiently large so that all results used below apply.

Let

, and let
\mathcal{H}
satisfy the assumptions. Since

is

-free, the extremality of
\mathcal{H}
gives

. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H})=\Psi(\mathcal{H})-|\mathcal{H}|\geq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})-|\mathcal{H}|\geq\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathbb{B}_{n})+|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\binom{n}{3}.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is
\delta
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
\delta\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
, it is also
\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Applying Lemma
3.2
to the maximum partition

gives

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathcal{B}|,|\mathcal{M}|\}\leq 2\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}n^{3},\qquad\left||V_{i}|-n/2\right|\leq 10\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2}n\text{ for each }i\in[2].

(32)

The lower bound above and Lemma
5.5
show that, for every vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\geq\frac{4\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H})}{n}-2n^{2}\geq\frac{4}{n}\left(\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathbb{B}_{n})+|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\binom{n}{3}\right)-2n^{2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{16}-\alpha\right)n^{3}.

(33)

Here the last inequality follows from the choice of
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
.

Fix
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
. By symmetry, assume that
v\in V_{1}
, and put

. By Fact
3.1
, we have
|L[V_{2}]|\geq|L[V_{1}]|
.
Since
\delta\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:common-balanced-k4-missing}}(\alpha)
, Lemma

gives a set of at most

edges whose deletion makes

balanced

-free with respect to

. Choose subsets

and
V_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{2}
with common size

by deleting vertices from the larger of

and

. By (
32
), at most

vertices are deleted. Let

-free graph on

obtained from

by deleting at most

Every copy of

containing

gives a triangle in

. If this triangle is not crossing with respect to

, then the other three vertices form a bad edge of

. Therefore (

) and (

) imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},L)\geq\kappa_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-|\mathcal{B}|\geq\left(\frac{1}{16}-2\alpha\right)n^{3}.

Passing from

to

22\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2}n
vertices and at most

edges, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{16}-3\alpha-22\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2}\right)n^{3}.

Also
m\leq n/2
, while
m\geq(\frac{1}{2}-11\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2})n
for

sufficiently large. By the choices of

,
\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}
, and

, this gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cr}}(K_{3},G)\geq m\binom{m}{2}-\varepsilon_{\ref{PROP:2-colored-K4-triangle-stability-sym}}(\eta/8)m^{3}.

Proposition
2.6
, applied to

with parameter
\eta/8
and part size

, gives that

can be transformed into either
\Lambda[V_{1}^{\prime};V_{2}^{\prime}]
or

by changing at most

edges.

could be transformed into

, contradicting the maximality of the partition. Hence

can be transformed into

edges.

The edit distance from

to

contributes at most
\eta n^{2}/8
to the corresponding three-term sum. Passing from

restores at most

deleted edges, and restoring the vertices outside

contributes at most

. Therefore, by the choice of parameters,

\displaystyle|L[V_{1}]|+\bigl((|V_{1}|-1)|V_{2}|-|L[V_{1},V_{2}]|\bigr)+\left(\binom{|V_{2}|}{2}-|L[V_{2}]|\right)\leq\left(\frac{\eta}{8}+\alpha+22\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}^{1/2}\right)n^{2}\leq 2\eta n^{2}.

The first term is

, while the last two terms sum to

. Thus

and

. The same argument applies when

, and therefore

.

5.3  The local replacement step

We first record the bipartite calculation needed at the end of the exact argument. It says that among all
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graphs, the complete balanced bipartite construction has maximum
\Psi
-value.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
n\geq 4
, and let
\mathcal{G}
be an
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graph. Then

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{G})\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n}),

and equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Proof of Lemma
5.7
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is an
n
-vertex bipartite
3
-graph with bipartition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{G})
. Since adding any missing edge increases
|\mathcal{G}|
and cannot decrease the number of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{G})\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]),

and equality in this inequality is possible only when
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
.

Put
m\coloneqq|V_{1}||V_{2}|
. In
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
, the edges are precisely the triples meeting both parts, and copies of
K_{4}^{3}
are precisely the four-sets with two vertices in each part. Hence

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}])=\frac{m(n-2)}{2}+\binom{|V_{1}|}{2}\binom{|V_{2}|}{2}=\frac{m(m+n-3)}{4}.

For
n\geq 4
, the expression on the right is strictly increasing in
m
. Since
m\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
, with equality if and only if the bipartition is balanced, we have
\Psi(\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}])\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. Equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
and the bipartition is balanced. This proves Lemma
5.7
.

We also need the local replacement estimate. The next lemma is the clique-counting analogue of Lemma
4.7
.

Lemma 5.8
.

There exists
\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}>0
such that for every
0<\xi\leq\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
there exists
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}\coloneqq N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}(\xi)
such that the following holds for all
n\geq N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-graph with a bipartition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{G})
, and let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}
be the bad and missing edges with respect to this partition.
Let
e_{\ast}\in\partial\mathcal{G}
be a pair in the shadow such that
e_{\ast}\in\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]
. Suppose that
e_{\ast}
satisfies

(i)

\bigl||V_{i}|-n/2\bigr|\leq\xi n
for
i\in[2]
,

(ii)

\max\{\Delta(\mathcal{B}),\Delta(\mathcal{M})\}\leq\xi n^{2}
,

(iii)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(e_{\ast})\geq 47\xi^{1/2}n
,

(iv)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(e_{\ast})\geq d_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{\ast})-\xi n
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}\coloneqq(\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{B}(e_{\ast}))\cup\mathcal{M}(e_{\ast})
. Then

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{G}^{\ast})>\Psi(\mathcal{G}).

Proof of Lemma
5.8
.

Choose
\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}>0
sufficiently small. Fix
0<\xi\leq\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
, and choose
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
sufficiently large.
By symmetry, assume that
e_{\ast}=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq V_{1}
.
Put

\displaystyle X\coloneqq N_{\mathcal{M}}(e_{\ast}),\qquad Y\coloneqq N_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{\ast}).

Then
X\subseteq V_{2}
,
Y\subseteq V_{1}
, and assumptions
(iii)
and
(iv)
give

\displaystyle|X|\geq 47\xi^{1/2}n\qquad\text{and}\qquad|Y|\leq|X|+\xi n.

(34)

Let
\mathcal{T}^{+}
be the family of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
which are present in
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
but not in
\mathcal{G}
, and let
\mathcal{T}^{-}
be the family of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
which are present in
\mathcal{G}
but not in
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
.
Since only edges containing
e_{\ast}
are changed, every copy in
\mathcal{T}^{+}\cup\mathcal{T}^{-}
contains
e_{\ast}
.

Define a graph
G_{\ast}
on
V_{2}
by declaring

if and only if
u_{1}xx^{\prime},u_{2}xx^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
.
The graph

is unchanged when passing from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
.
Every copy in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
contains an added edge
u_{1}u_{2}x
with
x\in X
, and its fourth vertex must lie in
V_{2}
, since every triple of the form
u_{1}u_{2}y
with
y\in V_{1}
is absent from
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
.
Thus every copy in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
is of the form
\{u_{1},u_{2},x,x^{\prime}\}
, where
xx^{\prime}\in G_{\ast}
and
\{x,x^{\prime}\}\cap X\neq\emptyset
.
Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}^{+}|=\sum_{x\in X}d_{G_{\ast}}(x)-|G_{\ast}[X]|.

For each
x\in X
, a vertex
x^{\prime}\in V_{2}\setminus\{x\}
fails to be adjacent to
x
in
G_{\ast}
only if one of
u_{1}xx^{\prime}
and
u_{2}xx^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{M}
. Each missing edge counted in the following sum contains
u_{1}
or
u_{2}
, and is counted at most twice. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}d_{G_{\ast}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq|X|(|V_{2}|-1)-\sum_{x\in X}\bigl(d_{\mathcal{M}}(u_{1}x)+d_{\mathcal{M}}(u_{2}x)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq|X|(|V_{2}|-1)-2d_{\mathcal{M}}(u_{1})-2d_{\mathcal{M}}(u_{2})\geq|X|(|V_{2}|-1)-4\xi n^{2},

where the last inequality uses assumption
(ii)
.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}^{+}|\geq|X|(|V_{2}|-1)-4\xi n^{2}-\binom{|X|}{2}=|X||V_{2}|-\frac{|X|(|X|+1)}{2}-4\xi n^{2}.

(35)

We now bound the copies destroyed by the replacement.
Every copy in
\mathcal{T}^{-}
contains a deleted edge
u_{1}u_{2}y
with
y\in Y
, and so it either has three vertices in
V_{1}
and one in
V_{2}

V_{1}

If a copy in
\mathcal{T}^{-}

V_{2}

Thus the number of such copies is at most
|Y|(|V_{2}|-|X|)
.
If a copy in
\mathcal{T}^{-}
is contained in
V_{1}
, then it has vertex set

with

and

. The edge

determines the copy, and hence assumption

gives at most

such copies.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}^{-}|\leq|Y|(|V_{2}|-|X|)+\xi n^{2}.

(36)

Combining (

), (
35
), and (
36
), and using

, we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}^{+}|-|\mathcal{T}^{-}|

\displaystyle\geq|X||V_{2}|-\frac{|X|(|X|+1)}{2}-4\xi n^{2}-(|X|+\xi n)(|V_{2}|-|X|)-\xi n^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|X|^{2}-|X|}{2}-\xi n(|V_{2}|-|X|)-5\xi n^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|X|^{2}-|X|}{2}-6\xi n^{2}\geq\xi n^{2},

where the last two inequalities follow from

,
|X|\leq n
, and the choice of
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{G}^{\ast})-\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{G})=|\mathcal{T}^{+}|-|\mathcal{T}^{-}|\geq\xi n^{2}.

Also, by (
34
),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}|-|\mathcal{G}|=|X|-|Y|\geq-\xi n.

By the choice of
N_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
, we get

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{G}^{\ast})-\Psi(\mathcal{G})=|\mathcal{G}^{\ast}|-|\mathcal{G}|+\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{G}^{\ast})-\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{G})\geq\xi n^{2}-\xi n>0.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.8
.

5.4  Proof of Theorem
1.5

We now give the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}}
be the constant from Lemma

. Choose
\eta>0
sufficiently small so that Lemma

. Set

, and assume, by decreasing

\displaystyle\xi<\xi_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-switching}},\qquad 47\xi^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{10},\qquad 2\eta\leq\xi.

\displaystyle\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:K53-count-local-degree-control}}(\eta),\qquad\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{\ref{LEMMA:common-local-bad-missing-control}}(\eta),\qquad 10\delta_{0}^{1/2}\leq\xi.

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathbb{B}_{n})+|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\binom{n}{3}\geq\left(\frac{1}{64}-\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}(\delta_{0})\right)n^{4}.

Let
n\geq n_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph maximizing
\Psi
. Since
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is
K_{5}^{3}
-free, the extremality of
\mathcal{H}
gives
\Psi(\mathcal{H})\geq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathcal{H})=\Psi(\mathcal{H})-|\mathcal{H}|\geq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})-|\mathcal{H}|\geq\mathrm{N}(K_{4}^{3},\mathbb{B}_{n})+|\mathbb{B}_{n}|-\binom{n}{3}\geq\left(\frac{1}{64}-\delta_{\ref{THM:K53-count-edge-stability}}(\delta_{0})\right)n^{4}.

By Theorem
5.4
, the graph
\mathcal{H}
is
\delta_{0}
-close to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\mathcal{H})
be a maximum partition of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}
be the corresponding sets of bad and missing edges. Lemma
3.2
, together with the choice of
\delta_{0}
, gives

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[2]}\left||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq 10\delta_{0}^{1/2}n\leq\xi n.

(37)

Lemma
5.6
and the choice of
\eta
give

\displaystyle\max\{\Delta(\mathcal{B}),\Delta(\mathcal{M})\}\leq 2\eta n^{2}\leq\xi n^{2}.

(38)

By Lemma
3.5
, every bad edge
E\in\mathcal{B}
contains a pair
e\subseteq E
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq n/10
. Moreover, for every
e\in\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq d_{\mathcal{B}}(e)-7\eta^{1/2}n=d_{\mathcal{B}}(e)-\xi n.

(39)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{B}\neq\emptyset
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\overline{K}[V_{1},V_{2}]:d_{\mathcal{M}}(e)\geq\frac{n}{10}\text{ and }\mathcal{B}(e)\neq\emptyset\right\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}.

Since every bad edge lies inside one part, the preceding paragraph shows that every bad edge contains some pair from
\mathcal{I}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}
. For
j\in[k]
, once
\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{j-1}
have been defined, we proceed as follows. If
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})=\emptyset
, then set
\mathcal{H}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}_{j-1}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{j-1}
. Otherwise set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}\coloneqq(\mathcal{H}_{j-1}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j}))\cup\mathcal{M}_{j-1}(e_{j}),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{j}\setminus\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}],\qquad\mathcal{M}_{j}\coloneqq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]\setminus\mathcal{H}_{j}.

This procedure only deletes bad edges and only adds edges of
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{k}.

Every edge of
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
contains exactly one same-side pair. Hence, before step
j
, the additions made at pairs different from
e_{j}
do not affect the missing edges containing
e_{j}
. Consider a step
j
with
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Then
e_{j}\in\partial\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
, and

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{M}_{j-1}}(e_{j})=d_{\mathcal{M}}(e_{j})\geq\frac{n}{10}\geq 47\xi^{1/2}n,\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}_{j-1}}(e_{j})\geq d_{\mathcal{B}_{j-1}}(e_{j})-\xi n.

The second inequality follows from the equality above, (
39
), and the inclusion
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. Together with (
37
) and (
38
), the inclusions
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{M}_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
show that Lemma
5.8
applies to
\mathcal{H}_{j-1}
with the fixed partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and the pair
e_{j}
. Hence
\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{j})>\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{j-1})
for every step with
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
, while the other steps leave the graph unchanged.

At least one step is nontrivial. Indeed, choose an original bad edge
E\in\mathcal{B}
and a pair
e_{j}\subseteq E
with
e_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
. If
E
is still present before step
j
, then
\mathcal{B}_{j-1}(e_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Otherwise,
E
was removed in an earlier nontrivial step. Therefore

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{k})>\Psi(\mathcal{H}).

Since each original bad edge contains a pair from
\mathcal{I}
, it is removed when one such pair is processed, unless it was removed earlier. Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{k}
has no bad edge, and so
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
. Completing
\mathcal{H}_{k}
to
\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
cannot decrease
\Psi
, and Lemma
5.7
gives

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{H})<\Psi(\mathcal{H}_{k})\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}])\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})\leq\Psi(\mathcal{H}),

a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{B}=\emptyset
.

If
\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
, then adding any edge of
\mathcal{M}
keeps the graph bipartite and hence
K_{5}^{3}
-free. It also increases
|\mathcal{H}|
by one and cannot decrease the number of copies of
K_{4}^{3}
. This contradicts the extremality of
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
, and so
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{B}[V_{1},V_{2}]
. Finally, Lemma
5.7
gives
\Psi(\mathcal{H})\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, while extremality gives the reverse inequality. Hence equality holds in Lemma
5.7
, and so
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

We have shown that every
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph maximizing
\Psi
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Therefore every
n
-vertex
K_{5}^{3}
-free
3
-graph
\mathcal{G}
satisfies
\Psi(\mathcal{G})\leq\Psi(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, with equality only when
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathbb{B}_{n}
. By (
28
), the same statement holds for
k(\mathcal{G})
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
