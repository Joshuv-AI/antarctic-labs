Measure preserving maps with bounded total variation
Stefano Bianchini, Luca Talamini

Abstract
. Consider a piecewise affine Lipschitz map ϕ : Ω → ℝ \phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R} , where Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} is an open set, and assume that x ↦ x + t ∇ ϕ ( x ) x\mapsto x+t\nabla\phi(x) is injective for almost every t > 0 t>0 . In (J.-G. Liu, R. L. Pego, Rigidly breaking potential flows and a countable Alexandrov theorem for polytopes , Pure Appl. Anal., 7 (4), 2025) the authors conjecture that every such ϕ \phi must be locally convex. We prove the result assuming additionally ∇ ϕ ∈ B ​ V l ​ o ​ c ​ ( Ω ) \nabla\phi\in BV_{loc}(\Omega) , for a more general class of measure preserving maps.



1.
Introduction

In
[
LP25
]
the authors consider the following problem: let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set, and consider masses
\{m_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and velocities
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sum_{i}m_{i}=\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega)

where
\mathscr{L}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. It is a classical result in optimal transport
[
Bre91
]
that there is a unique convex map
\nabla\phi
such that

\mathscr{L}^{d}\Big(\big\{x\in\Omega\;|\;\nabla\phi(x)=v_{i}\big\}\Big)=m_{i}.

Since
\phi
is convex, the map
x\mapsto x+t\nabla\phi(x)
is injective for all
t>0
on the set
F\subset\Omega
, where

F=\Big\{x\in\Omega\;|\;\text{$\phi$ is differentiable at $x$}\Big\}.

Clearly
F
is of full measure in
\Omega
, i.e.
|\Omega\setminus F|=0
.

Conversely, let a partition of
\Omega
be given, up to a negligible set,

\Omega=\bigcup_{i}A_{i},\qquad\text{$A_{i}$ open sets}

together with a Lipschitz map
\phi
, affine on each
A_{i}
, such that
x\mapsto x+t\nabla\phi(x)
is injective on
F
for a.e.
t>0
. The following conjecture was put forward in
[
LP25
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Every such map
\phi
must necessarily be locally convex in
\Omega
.

Our aim here is to prove the result for maps
\phi
with
\nabla\phi\in BV_{loc}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

In the above setting, if
\nabla\phi\in BV_{loc}(\Omega)
,
\phi
must be locally convex.

Proving the result for
\nabla\phi\notin BV
is presently an almost completely open problem. The only non-
BV
case is proved in
[
LP25
]
assuming only continuity of the gradient, i.e.
\nabla\phi\in C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
3
we show that every such
\phi
must be subharmonic, this holds without the
BV
assumption. In Section
2
we conclude the proof by showing, using the theory of
BV
functions, that
\phi
must be locally convex. In Section
4
we give a different proof of the subharmonicity of
\phi
, based on optimal transport tools, which yields an additional property of
\phi
in the case
\nabla\phi\notin BV
.

2.
Measure preserving maps and main result

Our Theorem
1.2
will be a consequence of a more general result, valid for a class of measure preserving maps.

In all of the following,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open set.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T
be a measurable map
T:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
F\subset\Omega
measurable be given. We say that
T
is
measure preserving
on
F
if for all
A\subset\Omega
measurable,
T(F\cap A)
is measurable and

(2.1)

T_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner(F\cap A)=\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner T(F\cap A).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
function. We say that
\phi
satisfies the
measure preserving condition
(in short (
MPC
)) if there is a full measure set
F\subset\Omega
for which

(2.2)

T_{t}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad T_{t}(x):=x+t\nabla\phi(x)

is measure preserving on
F
for a.e.
t\geq 0
.

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a map satisfying
(
MPC
)
, and assume that
\nabla\phi\in BV_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
\phi
is locally convex in
\Omega
.

Clearly, Theorem
1.2
follows from (
2.3
). We would expect every function
\phi\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
belonging to the more general class of maps satisfying the measure preserving condition of Definition
2.2
to be locally convex, but we have been unable to prove it.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
consists of two steps. As a first point, we prove the following proposition, which is valid without the
BV
assumption.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a map satisfying
(
MPC
)
. Then
\phi
is subharmonic in
\Omega
, that is,

(2.3)

-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0\qquad\text{for all $\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)$},\qquad\psi\geq 0.

Unfortunately, it is well known that the gradient of subharmonic functions does not need to lie in
BV_{loc}
, as it can be readily seen by looking at the fundamental solution of the Poisson equation

\Delta\Phi=\delta_{0},\qquad\Phi=\frac{1}{(d-2)\omega_{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}.

Therefore presently the
BV_{loc}
assumption in Theorem
2.3
cannot be removed.

We will prove Proposition
2.4
in Section
3
, for a more general class of maps which we call
expanding
.

Using Proposition
2.4
we can prove Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

By the standard theory of
BV
functions, see
[
AFP00
]
, since by assumption
\nabla\phi\in BV_{loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can decompose the derivative
D(\nabla\phi)
as

D(\nabla\phi)=D^{a}(\nabla\phi)+D^{s}(\nabla\phi)\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

where
D^{a}(\nabla\phi)
is the absolutely continuous part of
D(\nabla\phi)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{d}
, and
D^{s}(\nabla\phi)
is the singular part. Here
\mathscr{M}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is the space of locally finite matrix valued measures. In the following we analyze them separately.

1.

(Singular part).
We have that

D^{s}(\nabla\phi)=\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}
is the polar matrix, defined for
|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|
-a.e.
x
. By the Alberti’s rank 1 theorem
[
AFP00
, Theorem 3.94]
for
|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|
a.e.
x\in\Omega
there exist
a(x),b(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}=a(x)\otimes b(x)

Since the polar matrix
\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}
must be symmetric, we deduce the existence of
\lambda(x)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\vec{n}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\lambda(x)\vec{n}(x)\otimes\vec{n}(x)=a(x)\otimes b(x)
for
|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|
a.e.
x\in\Omega
, therefore

(2.4)

D^{s}(\nabla\phi)=\lambda\vec{n}\otimes\vec{n}\;|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|

and the Laplacian of
\phi
is

\Delta\phi=\mathrm{Tr}(D^{a}(\nabla\phi))+\lambda|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|.

By Proposition
2.4
we deduce in particular that

\lambda(x)\geq 0\qquad\text{for $|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|$-almost every $x\in\Omega$}

therefore the polar
\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}
is a positive definite matrix and by (
2.4
)
D^{s}(\nabla\phi)
is a positive definite matrix valued measure, since for every continuous vector field
\xi\in C_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there holds

\int_{\Omega}\xi^{\perp}(x)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}D^{s}(\nabla\phi)}{\mathrm{d}|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|}(x)\cdot\xi(x)\;\mathrm{d}\,|D^{s}(\nabla\phi)|(x)\geq 0.

2.

(Absolutely continuous part).
We will show that in fact the absolutely continuous part is zero. We will use the following well-known Lusin-Lipschitz properties for
BV
functions (see
[
AFP00
, Theorem 5.34]
). In our setting, since
\nabla\phi\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a constant
\kappa
(depending only on the dimension
d
) such that for every
\Lambda>0
there exists a Lipschitz function
f^{\Lambda}
with Lipschitz constant less then
\kappa\Lambda
, such that

\mathscr{L}^{d}\Big(\big\{x\in\Omega\;|\;\nabla\phi(x)\neq f^{\Lambda}(x)\big\}\Big)\leq\frac{\kappa}{\lambda}|D\nabla\phi|(\Omega).

Letting
G^{\Lambda}:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;f(x)=\nabla\phi(x)\}
, we also have the property

G^{\Lambda}_{1}\subset G^{\Lambda}_{2}\qquad\text{if $\Lambda_{1}\leq\Lambda_{2}$}.

We have that
f^{\Lambda}(x)-\nabla\phi(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in G^{\Lambda}
, therefore by the locality of the gradient of
BV
functions we obtain

Df^{\Lambda}\llcorner G^{\Lambda}=D(\nabla\phi)\llcorner G^{\Lambda}

so that in particular
D^{s}(\nabla\phi)\llcorner G^{\Lambda}=0
, and moreover for the absolutely continuous part there holds

(2.5)

Df^{\Lambda}(x)=D^{a}(\nabla\phi)(x)\qquad\text{for $\mathscr{L}^{d}$ almost every $x\in G^{\Lambda}$}.

Define now the Lipschitz function

T_{t}^{\Lambda}(x):=x+tf^{\Lambda}(x).

We have now that, using the standard area formula for the Lipschitz function
f^{\Lambda}
, for every measurable
A\subset\Omega
there holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{A\cap G^{\Lambda}}|\mathrm{det}\,D^{a}T_{t}(x)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{A\cap G^{\Lambda}}|\mathrm{det}\,DT^{\Lambda}_{t}(x)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sharp\{x\in G^{\Lambda}\cap A\;|\;T_{t}^{\Lambda}(x)=y\}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sharp\{x\in G^{\Lambda}\cap A\;|\;T_{t}(x)=y\}\mathrm{d}y.

Notice that for every disjoint measurable
A,B\subset F
, the measure preserving condition (
2.2
) implies that
|T_{t}(A)\cup T_{t}(B)|=|T_{t}(A)|+|T_{t}(B)|
and therefore
T_{t}(A)
and
T_{t}(B)
are essentially disjoint; in particular this yields

\sharp\{x\in G^{\Lambda}\cap A\;|\;T_{t}(x)=y\}=1\qquad\text{for a.e. $y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

In fact, assume by contradiction that for a positive measure set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there are at least two elements
x_{1}(y)\neq x_{2}(y)
such that
T_{t}(x_{1}(y))=T_{t}(x_{2}(y))=y
. Then, by the measurable selection theorem, there are two measurable sets
A_{1},A_{2}\subset\Omega
such that
T_{t}(A_{1})=B=T_{t}(A_{2})
up to negligible sets, which is a contradiction.
Therefore we conclude, by letting
\Lambda\to+\infty
, that

\int_{A}|\mathrm{det}\,D^{a}T_{t}(x)|\mathrm{d}x=\int_{T_{t}(A)}1\mathrm{d}y=|T_{t}(A)|=|A|

which implies that

|\mathrm{det}\,D^{a}T_{t}(x)|=1\qquad\text{for a.e. $x\in\Omega$}.

Therefore we deduce that for almost every
x\in\Omega

(2.7)

1=|\det(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+tD^{a}(\nabla\phi)(x))|=\prod_{i}|(1+t\lambda_{i}(x))|

where
\lambda_{i}(x)
,
i=1,\ldots,d
are the eigenvalues of
D^{a}(\nabla\phi)(x)
. Repeating the argument for a dense and countable set of times
t_{j}\geq 0
, and using the continuity in
t
of the expressions in (
2.7
), we deduce that for
\mathscr{L}^{d}
almost every
x\in\Omega

(2.8)

1=\prod_{i}|(1+t\lambda_{i}(x))|\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$}.

This clearly implies
\lambda_{i}(x)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
. Since this holds for
\mathscr{L}^{d}
-almost every
x\in\Omega
, we deduce that
D^{a}(\nabla\phi)=0
.

Finally, using the first step, we conclude

D(\nabla\phi)=D^{s}(\nabla\phi)\geq 0.

is a positive definite matrix valued measure. It is well known that positive definite distributional Hessian of
\phi
is equivalent to
\phi
being locally convex, therefore this concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Subharmonicity and expanding maps

The aim of this Section is to provide a proof of Proposition
2.4
. We will prove it in the more general setting of
expanding maps
, that we now introduce.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a measurable map
T:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
expanding
if

(3.1)

\rho:=T_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega\leq\mathscr{L}^{d}

Remark 3.2
.

Clearly, if
T
is measure preserving on
F\subset\Omega
(Definition
2.2
) and
F
is of full measure in
\Omega
, there holds

T_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner F=T_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega=\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner T(F)\leq\mathscr{L}^{d}

therefore measure preserving maps are also expanding according to Definition
3.1
.

In the next proposition, we show that if a gradient
\nabla\phi
is such that
x+t\nabla\phi(x)
is expanding for a.e.
t\geq 0
, then
\phi
is subharmonic:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be in
\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
and assume that
T_{t}(x)=x+t\nabla\phi(x)
is expanding for a.e.
t\geq 0
. Then it holds

(3.2)

\Delta\phi\geq 0\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$}.

By Remark
3.2
, this also proves Proposition
2.4
.

Proof.

We need to show that for every
0\leq\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
, there holds

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(x)\nabla\psi(x)dx\leq 0.

Let
\rho_{t}=(T_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega
.
Since
T_{t}
is expanding, we have that for all
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\psi=0
in
\overline{\Omega^{c}}
, there holds

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\rho_{t}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\mathscr{L}^{d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\rho_{0}.

We show that for every
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
compactly supported in
\Omega
, the function
g^{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

g^{\psi}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\rho_{t}

is Lipschitz and we compute its derivative. We have for all
\delta>0

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}|\psi(x+(t+\delta)\nabla\phi(x))-\psi(x+t\nabla\phi(x))|\leq L|\nabla\phi(x)|

where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
\psi
. Then we have, using the definition of
\rho_{t}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{g^{\psi}(t+\delta)-g^{\psi}(t)}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\rho_{t+\delta}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)d\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x+(t+\delta)\nabla\phi(x))-\psi(x+t\nabla\phi(x))dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\psi(x+(t+\delta)\nabla\phi(x))-\psi(x+t\nabla\phi(x))dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x+t\nabla\phi(x))\cdot\nabla\phi(x)dx

where the penultimate equality follows from the dominated convergence theorem, which we could apply because of (
3.5
) since
|\nabla\phi|\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Now using (
3.4
) we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq g^{\psi}(t)-g^{\psi}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(g^{\psi})^{\prime}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x+s\nabla\phi(x))\cdot\nabla\phi(x)dxds.

In particular we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x+s\nabla\phi(x))\cdot\nabla\phi(x)dxds

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\psi(x+s\nabla\phi(x))dsdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(x)\nabla\psi(x)dx

where the last equality follows from the fact that

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\psi(x+s\nabla\phi(x))ds\longrightarrow\nabla\psi(x)\qquad\text{strongly in $\mathbf{L}^{1}(\Omega)$ as $t\to 0^{+}$}

because again by (
3.5
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\psi(x+s\nabla\phi(x))-\nabla\psi(x)ds\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Ls|\nabla\phi(x)|dsdx

\displaystyle\leq Lt\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi(x)|dx.

This concludes the proof.

∎

4.
Optimal transport approach

Here we provide a different proof of Proposition
2.4
based on optimal transport techniques.

Let us define the measures, for all
t>0
,

\mu:=\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega,\qquad\nu_{t}:=(T_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega.

In this section we need to assume that the first moment is finite, that is

\int|x|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu,\qquad\int|y|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}<\infty

which amounts to assume that
T_{t}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
, or equivalently
\nabla\phi\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and that

\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

From
[
Bre91
]
we know that there exists a unique convex function
f_{t}
such that
(\nabla f_{t})_{\sharp}\mu=\nu_{t}
.
As above, this means that there exists a set of full measure (which we can take to be equal to
F
, recall Definition
2.2
) such that
\nabla{f_{t}}
exists in
F
and

(\nabla{f_{t}})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}=\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{F_{t}}

where
F_{t}=\nabla f_{t}(F)
.

We recall the Polar factorization theorem by Brenier
[
Bre91
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable map in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\xi_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. There exists a unique decomposition

\xi=\nabla u\circ\Phi

were
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function and
\Phi:\Omega\to\Omega
is measure preserving (i.e.
\Phi_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega=\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega
). Moreover,
\nabla u
is the optimal map for the quadratic transport cost pushing forward
\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner\Omega
to
\xi_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\Omega
.

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of maps
X_{t}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a vector field such that

(4.1)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{X_{t}(x)-x}{t}-\mathbf{b}(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0.

Letting

X_{t}=\nabla{f_{t}}\circ\Phi_{t}

be the Polar Decomposition of
X_{t}
given by Theorem
4.1
, it holds

\nabla h_{t}:=\frac{\nabla{f_{t}}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\to\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b},\quad\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\to(\mathrm{id}-\mathtt{P}_{\nabla})\mathbf{b}\quad\text{in $\mathbf{L}^{2}$ as $t\to 0^{+}$}

where
\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the projection on the space of gradients.

Proof.

1.
Let
h_{t}
be defined, up to constants, by

\nabla h_{t}:=\frac{\nabla{f_{t}}-\mathrm{id}}{t}.

As a first step, we compute the weak limit of
h_{t}
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
For any function
\psi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, we compute

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}[(\nabla f_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}](x)-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\psi(x)+t\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla h_{t}(x)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\psi(x+t\nabla h_{t}(x))\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\psi(x)+t\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla h_{t}(x)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla h_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}[(X_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}](x)-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\psi(x)+\nabla\psi(x)\cdot(X_{t}(x)-x)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Bigg|

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\Omega}\psi(X_{t}(x))\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\psi(x)+\nabla\psi(x)\cdot(X_{t}(x)-x)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\qquad\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}\|X_{t}-\mathrm{id}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}.

Since
\nabla f_{t}
and
X_{t}
have the same image measure

(\nabla{f_{t}})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}={X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},

and using that, by optimality of
\nabla{f_{t}}
,

\|\nabla h_{t}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\bigg\|\frac{\nabla f_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{t}\|X_{t}-\mathrm{id}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

we obtain from (
4.3
), (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\bigg(\nabla h_{t}(x)-\frac{X_{t}(x)-x}{t}\bigg){\mathrm{d}x}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\bigg(\nabla h_{t}-\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\bigg(\frac{X_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg)(x)\bigg){\mathrm{d}x}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\;t\,\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}\bigg\|\frac{X_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, letting
t\to 0
we conclude

(4.4)

\nabla h_{t}\longrightarrow\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b}\quad\text{weakly in $\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})$ as $t\to 0^{+}$}

2.

The aim of this step is to show that
\nabla h_{t}
converges strongly to
\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b}
by showing that

(4.5)

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\|\nabla h_{t}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}\leq\|\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}.

In order to prove (
4.15
), we first consider the Helmhotz decomposition of
\mathbf{b}
as

\mathbf{b}=\nabla g+\mathbf{b}_{1}

where
\nabla g
, for some
g\in\mathbf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, is the
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-projection of
\mathbf{b}
on the subspace of gradients, and
\mathbf{b}_{1}
is a divergence free vector field.

Let
\mathbf{b}_{1,n}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of smooth divergence free vector fields such that

(4.6)

\|\mathbf{b}_{1}-\mathbf{b}_{1,n}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2^{-n},\quad\mathbf{b}_{1,n}=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}

and consider the corresponding flow map
\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega
, defined by

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)=\mathbf{b}_{1,n}(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)),\quad\tilde{\Phi}_{n}(0,x)=x.

Clearly,
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}^{n}
is measure preserving,

(\tilde{\Phi}_{t}^{n})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{2}\llcorner_{\Omega}=\mathscr{L}^{2}\llcorner_{\Omega}

since
\mathbf{b}_{1,n}
are divergence free. Moreover, define maps
\tilde{X}^{n}_{t}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\tilde{X}_{n}(t,x)=\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)+t\nabla g(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)).

By definition we have

{\tilde{X}^{n}_{t}}=\nabla\tilde{f_{t}}\circ{\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}},\quad\tilde{f}(t,x):=\frac{x^{2}}{2}+tg(x).

Note that there holds by triangular inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left|{\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)}+t\nabla g({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-\Big(x+t\mathbf{b}_{1,n}(x)+t\nabla g(x)\Big)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq t\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)-x}{t}-\mathbf{b}_{1,n}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+t\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-\nabla g(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Since
\mathbf{b}_{1,n}
is smooth, one clearly has

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)-x}{t}-\mathbf{b}_{1,n}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

We claim that also

(4.7)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-\nabla g(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

This can be easily seen since
\tilde{\Phi}_{t}^{n}
is measure preserving, via a standard argument as follows. Let
\varepsilon>0
, then by density there is a Lipschitz function
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\|F-\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon.

Therefore we estimate

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-\nabla g(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g(x)-F(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|F({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-F(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\mathrm{Lip}\,(F)\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\Phi}_{t}^{n}(x)-x|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=2\varepsilon

Since this holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, (
4.7
) is proved.
Therefore we have

(4.8)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}\left|{\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)}+t\nabla g({\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t}(x)})-\Big(x+t\mathbf{b}_{1,n}(x)+t\nabla g(x)\Big)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0

We thus compute

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\|X_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{n}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\|X_{t}-(\mathrm{id}+t\nabla\mathbf{b})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}\|(\mathrm{id}+t\nabla\mathbf{b})-(\mathrm{id}+t(\nabla g+\mathbf{b}_{1,n}))\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}\|(\mathrm{id}+t(\nabla g+\mathbf{b}_{1,n}))-\tilde{X}_{t}^{n}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq o(1)+2^{-n}+o(1)

where
o(1)
is a quantity approaching zero with
t\to 0
, and we used respectively (
4.1
) to estimate the first term, (
4.6
) to estimate the second term, and (
4.8
) to estimate the third term.
Then we can estimate the 2-Wasserstein distance between

{X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
and
{(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
as

(4.10)

\displaystyle\dist_{W_{2}}\big({X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},{(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)

\displaystyle=\|X_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}\leq t^{2}2^{-n+1}

for all
t>0
and sufficiently small. By the triangular inequality for the 2-Wasserstain distance, we obtain that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\dist_{W_{2}}\big({X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},\;\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\dist_{W_{2}}\big({X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},{(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)

\displaystyle+\dist_{W_{2}}\big(\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},\;(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}2^{-n+1}+t^{2}\|\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}

where we have used that

(4.12)

\dist_{W_{2}}\big(\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},\;(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)\leq t^{2}\|\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}

To see that (
4.12
) holds, first notice that since
(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t})
is measure preserving, we deduce

(4.13)

(\nabla\tilde{f}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}=(\nabla\tilde{f}_{t})_{\sharp}\circ(\tilde{\Phi}^{n}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}=(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}.

In particular
\nabla\tilde{f}_{t}
is an admissible map between
\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
and
(\tilde{X}^{n}_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
, with cost

\|\nabla\tilde{f}_{t}-\mathrm{id}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}=t^{2}\|\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}

and this proves (
4.12
).
Since
\nabla f_{t}
is the optimal map between
\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
and
(X_{t})_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}
, we deduce that for all
t>0
small

(4.14)

\|\nabla f_{t}-\mathrm{id}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}=\dist_{W_{2}}\big({X_{t}}_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega},\;\mathscr{L}^{d}\llcorner_{\Omega}\big)\leq t^{2}\|\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}^{2}+t^{2}2^{-n+1}

We thus conclude that

\limsup_{t\to 0}\int|\nabla h_{t}|_{2}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2^{-n}.

This holds for all
n
, so that

(4.15)

\limsup_{t\to 0}\|\nabla h_{t}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using that
\nabla h_{t}\rightharpoonup\nabla g
weakly, we thus conclude from (
4.15
) that the convergence is strong by the uniform convexity of
\mathbf{L}^{2}
.

3.
Notice that we have

(4.16)

\bigg\|\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|h_{t}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\bigg\|\frac{X_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\bigg\|\frac{X_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Then we deduce

\begin{split}\frac{\nabla{X_{t}}-\mathrm{id}}{t}&=\frac{\nabla{f_{t}}\circ\Phi_{t}-\Phi_{t}}{t}+\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}=h_{t}(\Phi_{t})+\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t},\end{split}

and we conclude that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{{X_{t}}-\mathrm{id}}{t}-\lim_{t\to 0}h_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}=\mathbf{b}-\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b}.\qed

where we used that since (
4.16
) holds, then
h_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}\to\mathtt{P}_{\nabla}\mathbf{b}
strongly in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
(this can done as in the proof of (
4.7
)).

Applying the result to our setting, we obtain:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that
(
MPC
)
holds. Let
\nabla f_{t},\Phi_{t}
be the maps in the polar decomposition of
T_{t}
, i.e.

T_{t}=\nabla f_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}.

Then

\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg\|\frac{{\nabla f_{t}}-\mathrm{id}}{t}-\nabla\phi\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg\|\frac{\Phi_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}\bigg\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}=0,

We also obtain a different proof of the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that
(
MPC
)
holds. Then

\Delta\phi\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
\nabla f_{t},\Phi_{t}
be as above.
Being
\nabla{f_{t}}
measure preserving and monotone, hence BV, it holds

\det\big(\mathrm{id}+tD^{a}\nabla h_{t}(x)\big)=1,

for
\mathscr{L}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where we recall

\nabla h_{t}=\frac{\nabla f_{t}-\mathrm{id}}{t}.

If
\lambda_{i}(t,x)
are the eigenvalues of
D^{a}\nabla h_{t}(x)
one gets, using the arithmetic-geometric inequality

(4.17)

1=\bigg(\prod_{i}(1+t\lambda_{i}(t,x))\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}(1+t\lambda_{i}(t,x))=1+\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(t,x).

By (
4.17
), there holds

\mathrm{Tr}(D^{a}\nabla h_{t})\geq 0

while for the singular part of the Laplacian, one has

t(\Delta h_{t})^{s}=(\Delta{f_{t}})^{s}\geq 0,

being
f_{t}
convex. Hence
\Delta h_{t}\geq 0
in the sense of distributions,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla h_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq 0\qquad\text{for all $\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)$, $\psi\geq 0$}.

Since by Corollary
4.3
we have the convergence
\nabla h_{t}\to\nabla\phi
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
, we deduce for every
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
that

0\geq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla h_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x

and this proves the result.
∎
