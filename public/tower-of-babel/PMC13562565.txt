Correlation analysis of blood metabolites and periventricular leukomalacia in preterm infants two weeks postnatally
Authors: Caiyu Zhang, Muchun Yu, Lu He, Zhihong Sun, Huiqing Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) is a neonatal brain injury disease that may cause neurodevelopmental disorders. In this study, our objective was to compare the differences in blood metabolites between preterm infants with and without PVL. We also aimed to screen for potential metabolic markers related to the early identification of PVL. Preterm infants diagnosed with periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) by cranial imaging were assigned to the PVL group (n = 32), while those without a PVL diagnosis were assigned to the control group (n = 68). Blood metabolite levels were measured at 2 weeks postnatally using ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS). Most blood metabolite concentrations were statistically different between the two groups (P < 0.05). The results of the OPLS-DA model identification analysis and validation show that the model is valid. Of the 12 blood metabolites with predicted variable importance (VIP) > 1, those with the highest VIP scores included octadecadienoylcarnitine (C18:2), 3-hydroxy-isovalerylcarnitine (C4DC), 3-oH-octadecylcarnitine (C18OH), 3-hydroxy-cetacylcarnitine (C16OH), Adipylcarnitine (C6DC), and Tyrosine (Tyr). The S-plot shows that the blood metabolites C4DC, Ornithine (Orn), C16OH, and C18:2 are biased towards the two poles. The univariate analysis for cross-validation showed that C18:2, C4DC, C18OH, and C16OH concentrations in blood metabolites were significantly different between the two groups (P < 0.001). The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that a high concentration of Tyr was negatively associated with the occurrence of PVL (P = 0.031), while a longer duration of parenteral nutrition (PN) and a higher Orn concentration were positively correlated with PVL (all P < 0.05). Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicated that C18:2 had a relatively high discriminatory ability for PVL among the detected metabolites, but it did not show independent statistical significance in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The most significant difference in the blood metabolite concentrations between the two groups was observed for C18:2. Enrichment analysis showed that fatty acid degradation is the most likely major mechanistic pathway involved in the development of PVL. There is a certain correlation between early blood metabolite levels and PVL occurrence in premature infants. Although C18:2 shows a certain discriminatory ability, the value of it as an early detection indicator still needs further research.

Introduction

Periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) is one of the most common cerebral lesions found in preterm infants and is an important risk factor for adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes1. Major contributors to PVL development include hypoperfusion, hypoxia–ischemia, metabolic insults, prematurity, seizures, and infections2. The periventricular area has a high susceptibility to injury owing to its immaturity and vascular supply3.

PVL is characterized by damage to oligodendrocytes, myelin, and axons. It occurs at the peak of myelin formation (24–32 weeks of gestational age), during which late oligodendrocyte precursors are the main cell type that constitutes the periventricular white matter and are susceptible to damage and loss caused by hypoxia, ischemia, and infections, which results in the failure of myelin formation in the white matter4. In the brain, peroxisomes are involved in lipid oxidation, phospholipid synthesis, oxidative stress, and anti-inflammatory processes5. Peroxisome dysfunction is often caused by underdeveloped liver in preterm infants, which can lead to peroxisomal dysfunction. Reduced peroxisome activity leads to inadequate plasmalogen synthesis, destabilizing oligodendrocyte membranes, and fatty acid accumulation due to abnormal fatty acid degradation, exacerbating cellular damage6,7.

Carnitine is essential for energy metabolism and has possible neuroprotective effects8. Fatty acids must be bound to L-carnitine to enter the mitochondria. During fatty acid oxidation, acylcarnitine transfers acyl groups from the cytoplasm to the mitochondria9. The first 72 h after hypoxic-ischemic brain injury are divided into primary and secondary brain injury stages, characterized by mitochondrial energy metabolism and oxidative dysfunction10. Specifically, alterations in the mitochondrial membrane and accumulation of reactive oxidants can lead to increased permeability and oxidative phosphorylation uncoupling11. Given the importance of mitochondrial metabolism in hypoxic-ischemic brain injury, acylcarnitines could serve as diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets12. As a key carrier of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondrial matrix, carnitine is essential in energy metabolism13. More and more studies have found that carnitine also has other biological functions, including neuroprotection in the developing brain14.

During fetal life, most of the transplacental supply of carnitine from the mother occurs in the last trimester of pregnancy, when fetal growth is fast15. Extremely preterm infants miss out on the last trimester of carnitine flux, resulting in low tissue stores at birth16. They are at risk of developing carnitine deficiency due to limited tissue stores, low endogenous synthesis capacity, inadequate nutrient intake, and increased carnitine requirements due to rapid growth8,13,16. Carnitine is found in human milk and formula but is typically absent from PN solutions. Current guidelines do not uniformly recommend carnitine supplementation in PN for extremely preterm infants because there is no data on whether it promotes growth or neurodevelopment. In addition to lipid metabolism and acylcarnitine abnormalities, amino acid metabolism imbalance may also be related to brain injury in premature infants17. Tyrosine, as an important aromatic amino acid, participates in protein synthesis and multiple metabolic processes18. Changes in its level can not only reflect the nutritional status, metabolic regulation and organ maturity of the body, but also be easily affected by protein intake and liver maturity19. Therefore, in premature infants, tyrosine levels not only may be associated with brain injury, but also need to be comprehensively analyzed in combination with the above nutritional and developmental factors. Therefore, we conducted metabolomic studies of blood metabolites using UPLC-MS/MS to identify significantly different metabolites in infants with PVL.

Methods

Study design

In this prospective cohort study, we enrolled infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation who were admitted to two intensive care units for neonates, namely, the neonatal intensive care unit and the preterm infant intensive care unit, at the Children’s Hospital affiliated with Zhengzhou University, Zhengzhou, China. All the included infants underwent UPLC-MS/MS testing at 2 weeks after birth. The diagnosis of PVL was mainly based on the clinical manifestations and imaging results of the infants. The screening process for cranial imaging of hospitalized premature infants was carried out according to Fig. 1. If the cranial ultrasound showed abnormally high echogenicity in the periventricular white matter area for ≥ 7 days, it could be classified as grade I imaging changes according to the de Vries classification, suggesting possible persistent periventricular high echogenicity and non-cystic white matter injury. If focal or extensive periventricular cystic changes were further observed in subsequent sequential cranial ultrasound follow-ups, the diagnosis of cystic PVL was made. For infants with suspected PVL changes indicated by cranial ultrasound but without clear cystic changes, further head MRI assessment was conducted20,21. Infants diagnosed with PVL were included in the PVL group; those without PVL lesions at 2 weeks after birth were continuously monitored with sequential cranial ultrasounds as shown in Fig. 1 and underwent head MRI at the corrected gestational age of full term to rule out late-onset PVL. Ultimately, infants who were not diagnosed with PVL, completed UPLC-MS/MS testing at 2 weeks after birth, and had similar gestational ages were included in the control group. PVL severity was classified by evaluating head magnetic resonance imaging following Choi et al. classification22. Grade I included local changes in the small capsular cavity around the ventricle (high signal on T2-weighted images and low signal on T2 fluid-attenuated inversion recovery) (Fig. 2A). Grade II included extensive cystic changes around the ventricle that could fuse into pieces (irregular ventricular walls or ventricular dilatation) (Fig. 2B). Grade III includes cystic changes in the periventricular and subcortical white matter (Fig. 2C). The Life Science Ethics Committee of the Children’s Hospital affiliated with Zhengzhou University approved this study. Study population

Eligible patients included infants with a gestational age of < 32 weeks who were admitted to the above two intensive care units between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2023. All eligible infants underwent UPLC-MS/MS at 2 weeks of age23. The micro-blood samples were collected from the heel using cards (S&S 903 Filter Paper, USA) sealed with plastic bags and stored at − 20 °C in a medical refrigerator until analysis. Infants were excluded from the study following congenital infections with overt signs at birth, significant congenital abnormalities, death, genetic metabolic diseases, lack of parental consent, and absence of UPLC-MS/MS analysis.

Metabolic data collection

Blood spots of 3.2 mm in diameter were created on dry blood filter paper using a hole punch and subsequently arranged in a 96-well culture plate. Each test comprised four controls of both high and low quality, along with two blanks, to maintain accuracy. Following this, 100 μl of the working solution was introduced into each well. The plate was then sealed with a film and incubated in a thermostatic oscillator set at 45 °C, with a speed of 700 RPM for a duration of 45 min. Next, 75 μl supernatant was extracted, added to the sample, wrapped with aluminum foil, and tested on the machine after machine balancing. Acetonitrile and pure water were used as mobile phase, and 2 μl was injected into each sample. A multi-reaction monitoring method was used for mass spectrometry.

Non-derivatization kits (PerkinElmer) were available with internal standard isotopes and high and low concentration blood films, including amino acid internal standards11 and acylcarnitine internal standards. 13 Prior to each experiment, a workup solution was prepared with internal isotope standards in a 1:1:10 10 ratio with methanol. Methanol, acetonitrile, formamide, and formic acid used as mobile phases were of high-performance liquid chromatography grade and were purchased from Sigma. Test instruments: an Agilent high-performance liquid chromatograph (Agilent 1600) and a 6460 Triple Quad MS/MS series mass spectrometer.

Metabolic data analysis

Metabolic concentrations were calculated from mass-to-charge ratios of mass spectral peaks using quantitative analysis software. For multivariate analyses, we used soft independent modeling of class analogies (v.14.1; Umetrics, ume # # < s:1 > , Sweden). Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was used to increase categorical separation, flatten the dataset, and identify potential biomarkers24. Two parameters were used to validate the quality of the model: R2Ycum (goodness of fit) and cumulative Q2 (goodness of prediction). A threshold of 0.5 is widely accepted in model classification to identify good (Q2cum ≥ 0.5) or poor (Q2cum < 0.5) predictive power24.

The OPLS-DA model was validated using 200 permutation tests, which reduced the risk of overfitting and the possibility of false-positive results. The fitted regression line connecting the observed Q2 to the center of mass of the displacement Q2 cluster is shown in the chart. The chart shows the correlation coefficient between the original Y and the arranged Y and between the current Y and Q2. The OPLS-DA model was considered valid if all Q2 values of the left-arranged dataset were lower than the Q2 values of the actual dataset on the right and if the intercept of the regression line on the Y-axis was negative24.

Moderate and strong outliers were identified using the DModX and Hotelling’s t-squared tests. Projected variable importance (VIP) was determined using a supervised survey. The VIP is a readout of the contribution of each variable to the x-axis of the model. It is summed over all components and weighted using the Y of each component25. Thus, the VIP ranking shows the contribution of the metabolite to the model. Similar to findings from other studies, metabolites with a VIP > 1 were selected as the main discriminant metabolites26.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive methods were used to summarize and compare demographic characteristics and distribution patterns of each metabolite. The t-test was used for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to assess the associations between metabolites and PVL. The discriminatory performance of potential metabolic biomarkers for PVL was assessed using ROC analysis. Area under the curve, sensitivity, and specificity of PVL principal components were calculated and compared. This approach considers both the discriminatory performance and statistical significance of individual metabolites. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses and data management were conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software (version 25.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois, USA). After normalizing the metabolic products in the blood, a comparative analysis was conducted, followed by a Pearson correlation analysis. The biological function of differentially expressed blood metabolites was analyzed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment27.

Discussion

PVL is commonly observed in preterm infants born between 24 and 32 weeks of gestation. It represents a hypoxic-ischemic injury that affects the white matter in the paraventricular watershed area of the brain. Children who survive this condition may face various outcomes, including dyskinesia, spastic cerebral palsy, and visual impairments. Carnitine exists in two forms: free carnitine and acylcarnitine. It is important in the metabolism of long-chain fatty acids by modulating their entry into the mitochondrial matrix14. Based on the premise of carnitine’s important role in energy metabolism, its use in treating critically ill preterm infants has been documented domestically and internationally. Administering carnitine can reduce the incidence of apnea and shorten the duration of mechanical ventilation28. Suvi Manninen et al.8 reported the significance of enteral feeding as a vital source of carnitine, highlighting its crucial role in supporting postnatal growth and brain development in preterm infants. Therefore, it can be inferred that postnatal carnitine deficiency in preterm infants causes damage to essential organs, including the brain.

Recently, metabolomics and tandem mass spectrometry have been increasingly used to identify and diagnose pediatric asthma, neonatal bronchopulmonary dysplasia, and various other diseases29. Few studies have reported the correlation between blood metabolite levels and PVL incidence in preterm infants. Most existing literature shows that PVL occurrence may be associated with inadequate energy supply and oxidative stress. Our study is the first to analyze the blood metabolites of PVL and non-PVL children, providing new insights into the early warning and possible pathogenesis of PVL.

The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that fatty acid degradation and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway were the most involved in blood metabolites, suggesting that they may be involved in the development of PVL. Fatty acid degradation in premature infants is inhibited due to carnitine deficiency, and large amounts of free fatty acids accumulate to form lipid droplets, most of which are located in astrocytes and microglia30. Under normal conditions, the formation of lipid droplets mitigates cellular damage induced by toxic fatty acids and provides neuroprotective effects. However, glial cells that show excessive accumulation of lipid droplets release proinflammatory factors (including IL-6 and IL-1β) and reactive oxygen species, which can harm oligodendrocytes and neurons31,32. Therefore, carnitine deficiency in preterm infants may indirectly participate in PVL pathogenesis by obstructing fatty acid metabolism, indirectly causing insufficient energy supply, oxidative stress, and inflammation.

The PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is essential in regulating blood glucose levels, fatty acid metabolism, cell proliferation, differentiation, survival, and other physiological functions33. Akt signaling is crucial for neuronal survival, growth, and synaptic plasticity, influencing brain development and function34. Findings from relevant studies showed that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway could activate vascular endothelial growth factor expression through hypoxia-inducible factor-135, insulin-like growth factor-136, and nitric oxide synthase37. Therefore, this pathway may confer neuroprotective effects through the pro-angiogenic properties of vascular endothelial growth factor. Krycer JP et al.38 showed that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway regulates the activity of sterol regulatory element-binding protein, thereby promoting lipid synthesis and driving cellular metabolism. This regulation is essential for maintaining the stability of the neuronal cell structure and function.

PI3K can be activated using two mechanisms. One involves the interaction of growth factor receptors or connexins with phosphorylated tyrosine residues. This mechanism induces a conformational change in the dimer, leading to its activation. The other mechanism involves activating PI3K through the direct binding of Ras to p110. PI3K activation generates a second messenger, PIP3, on the plasma membrane. This molecule binds to the intracellular signaling proteins Akt and 3-phosphatidylinositol-dependent protein kinase-1, which contain structural PH domains. The binding of PIP3 contributes to activating Akt through phosphorylation of Ser308 by 3-phosphatidylinositol-dependent protein kinase-139. Phosphorylation in all the aforementioned processes necessitates energy expenditure. Enrichment analysis of the KEGG pathway showed that C18:2 was mainly involved in fatty acid degradation pathway, and its level changes were simultaneously associated with the dysregulation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. The results of this study suggest that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway may be involved in the occurrence and development of PVL. This suggests that C18:2 concentration in the blood of preterm infants may be associated with PVL occurrence and development. However, research on the direct effects of C18:2 on the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is limited. However, several studies have reported that abnormal levels of carnitine, including C18:2, are frequently associated with metabolic disorders, including obesity and diabetes. Furthermore, reduced carnitine levels can impair fatty acid metabolism, leading to an inadequate energy supply and ultimately inhibiting the activity of the PI3K-Akt pathway34. Therefore, C18:2 may have an indirect association with the occurrence and development of PVL.

In this study, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that a higher concentration of tyrosine was negatively associated with the occurrence of PVL. This developmental lag renders them particularly susceptible to cell death caused by free radicals, proinflammatory cytokines, and various other factors. This susceptibility constitutes the organizational basis for neural axonal injury40. Tyrosine interacts with tyrosinase to produce melanin. This melanin cleanses free radicals and reduces inflammation41. Tyrosine is important in neurotransmitter synthesis, including dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine, and is the raw material for thyroid hormone synthesis, which promotes neuronal development. These functions collectively underscore tyrosine’s significance in developing the nervous system. A related study conducted by Cheng et al.42 reported that activating the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway enhances the metabolic rate of the pentose phosphate pathway by increasing glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase activity. This process provides nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate and essential precursors for tyrosine synthesis. When carnitine is deficient in preterm infants, the energy supply is insufficient, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is inhibited, and the rate of tyrosine synthesis decreases, exacerbating brain damage in preterm infants. Therefore, we hypothesize that reduced blood tyrosine concentrations in preterm infants are associated with PVL development.

Furthermore, the multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that a prolonged duration of PN was positively correlated with the occurrence of PVL. It is important to note that this study is observational, and the result does not imply that the duration of PN itself directly causes PVL. Instead, a longer PN exposure is more likely to reflect a more severe overall condition of the infant, a delayed establishment of full enteral feeding, and differences in the nutritional support mode. Previous studies have suggested that preterm infants who are dependent on PN for a long time may experience a decrease in free carnitine levels, which can further affect fatty acid metabolism and energy utilization43; at the same time, the insufficiency, excess, or imbalance of certain nutrients in PN may also affect the levels of blood metabolites. Therefore, the metabolic changes observed in this study may not only be related to the duration of PN exposure but also to the differences in PN composition.

This study has the following limitations: The Q2cum of the OPLS-DA model is only 0.19, indicating limited predictive performance. Although no obvious overfitting was observed in the permutation test, the results may still be affected by the small sample size and the complexity of the metabolic data. The overall condition of the PVL group was more severe (such as lower 5-min Apgar score, higher mechanical ventilation rate, longer duration of PN, later time to reach full enteral nutrition, and a higher incidence of diseases such as sepsis, etc.), so the observed metabolic differences may be partially due to the severity of the condition, inflammatory state, nutritional support, and other factors, rather than being solely driven by PVL itself. Moreover, the low levels of Tyr and C18:2, in addition to possibly being related to brain injury, may also represent non-specific metabolic changes in more severely ill preterm infants. Since carnitine is present in red blood cells, liver, and muscle, blood carnitine levels do not fully represent the total carnitine reserves in the body44. Therefore, whether there is a causal relationship between low carnitine status in preterm infants and PVL and other related diseases still requires further research. Although this study is a single-center small-sample study, more cases that meet the research criteria need to be included in the future to expand the sample size and conduct multi-center studies to enhance the universality and reliability of the conclusions.

Conclusion

This study found that the levels of blood metabolites in preterm infants two weeks after birth were correlated with the occurrence of PVL. Among them, higher Tyr levels may be negatively correlated with PVL, while longer duration of PN and higher Orn levels may be positively correlated with PVL. The study also found that although C18:2 showed some discriminatory ability, it did not demonstrate independent predictive value. These results suggest that in the clinical management of preterm infants, in addition to paying attention to conventional perinatal risk factors, early nutritional support, metabolic status, and overall disease severity should also be emphasized as potential influences on the risk of brain injury. For preterm infants with severe conditions, prolonged PN, and significant metabolic abnormalities, dynamic monitoring and neuroimaging follow-up should be strengthened to provide references for the early identification and clinical intervention of PVL.
