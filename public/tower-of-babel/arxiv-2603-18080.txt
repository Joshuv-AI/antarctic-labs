1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 1
users hold private inputs
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. In the shuffle model each user applies a local
randomizer and a trusted shuffler releases the multiset of local messages, equivalently the released histogram.
This model, initiated by the Encode–Shuffle–Analyze architecture of Bittau et al.
[
3
]
,
sits between local and central differential privacy and yields privacy amplification by anonymity

[
4
,
5
,
1
,
6
,
7
,
15
]
.

For fixed finite output alphabets the asymptotic structure of neighboring shuffle experiments is now rather
well understood. Part I of the trilogy
[
12
]
develops a sharp Gaussian/LAN/GDP theory in the
full-support regime; Part II
[
13
]
identifies critical Poisson, Skellam, and compound-Poisson
limits when the local randomizer itself becomes increasingly concentrated; Part III
[
14
]

describes the finite-alphabet dominant-block quotient geometry and its hybrid Gaussian–compound-Poisson
limit. The present paper addresses a different question, left open in the trilogy and particularly pointed in
the discussion of Part III: what happens when the output alphabet grows?

A first guess is that more output symbols should automatically help privacy. This is indeed what happens
for
d
-ary generalized randomized response (GRR): if one input changes from
a
to
b
, then only the
two output coordinates
a
and
b
carry nontrivial pairwise likelihood-ratio values, while the remaining

d-2
coordinates are pairwise neutral. Consequently the pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence is of order
d^{-1}
,
and the corresponding shuffled privacy collapses as
d\to\infty
.
This
d
-dependent improvement for
k
-ary randomized response was first established theoretically by
Feldman, McMillan, and Talwar
[
6
, Corollary 4.2]
,
who proved a blanket privacy bound of order
e^{\varepsilon_{0}}/\!\sqrt{kn}
for
k
-RR.
Our Proposition
7.1
gives the exact experiment-level counterpart.

The main message of the present paper is twofold. First, the improvement of GRR is mechanism-specific, not universal. There exist explicit

\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP channels on
d
symbols for which the pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence stays constant as

d\to\infty
, and for which the exact shuffled privacy curve of a worst-case neighboring pair is

identical
to the binary randomized-response privacy curve for every
d
.
Thus growing alphabets do not automatically amplify shuffle privacy.
Second, the shuffle model has its own optimal mechanism geometry, fundamentally different from local DP.
The locally optimal mechanism (GRR at matched budget) is
not
shuffle-optimal; the shuffle-optimal
mechanism concentrates an aggressive local signal on a random fraction of users.
In the low-budget regime this thinned augmented-GRR construction is, in fact, optimal among all
permutation-equivariant channels for the projected unbiased inverse-estimator risk, up to conditionally identical output refinements.

The correct invariant is not the raw alphabet size
d
, nor the ambient rank of the channel matrix, nor the
number of output coordinates on which rows differ. It is the pushforward law of the pairwise likelihood ratio

w_{ab,d}(y):=\frac{W_{d}(y\mid b)}{W_{d}(y\mid a)}

under the null row
W_{d}(\cdot\mid a)
. This law lives on the fixed compact interval

[e^{-\varepsilon_{0}},e^{\varepsilon_{0}}]
and completely determines the canonical one-step neighboring
shuffle experiment.

We now summarize the main results, organized into three groups: privacy structure, estimation bounds, and mechanism design.

I. Privacy structure

(A) Exact likelihood-ratio quotient compression
(Theorem
3.2
).
For the canonical neighboring pair in which one input changes from
a
to
b
,
the full histogram experiment is exactly equivalent to the multinomial experiment on the
likelihood-ratio level sets of
w_{ab,d}
. The proof is exact and finite-
n
.

(B) Universal extremal
\chi^{2}
bound
(Theorem
4.2
).
For every
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP channel and every pair
a\neq b
,

\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W_{d}(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W_{d}(\cdot\mid a)\bigr)\leq\frac{(e^{\varepsilon_{0}}-1)^{2}}{e^{\varepsilon_{0}}}.

The bound is sharp, and equality holds precisely for endpoint two-point likelihood-ratio laws.

(C) Explicit obstruction family
(Theorem
5.2
).
For every even
d
we construct a full-support cyclic half-block channel
W_{d}
for which,
along an opposite pair of inputs, the likelihood-ratio law is the same fixed two-point law for all
d
.
Hence the exact shuffled privacy curve is exactly the binary randomized-response curve for all
d
.

(D) Sharp geometric dichotomy
(Theorem
6.2
).
A channel family
\{W_{d}\}
is
diluting
iff its worst-case pairwise likelihood-ratio law collapses to

\delta_{1}
, equivalently iff its worst-case pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence tends to zero.
It is
persistent
iff some pairwise likelihood-ratio law has a nondegenerate subsequential limit,
equivalently iff the worst-case pairwise
\chi^{2}
has positive limsup.
Persistent families remain on the ordinary shuffled Gaussian/GDP scale
n^{-1/2}
, with no extra

d
-gain; diluting families yield asymptotically perfect fixed-
\varepsilon
privacy.

II. Estimation bounds

(E) Universal estimation lower bound

(Theorems
8.1
and
8.4
).
For any channel
W:[d]\to\Delta(\mathcal{Y})
with arbitrary output alphabet and any estimator

\widehat{\theta}
of the input frequency vector from the shuffled histogram,
the minimax
\ell_{2}^{2}
risk is at least of order
(d-1)/(n\chi_{\ast}(W))

in the regime
n\chi_{\ast}(W)\gtrsim d^{2}
.
We give two independent proofs: a near-vertex Cramér–Rao argument for locally unbiased
estimators, and an Assouad argument for arbitrary estimators.
The quantity
\chi_{\ast}(W)=\max_{a\neq b}\chi^{2}(W(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid a))
is therefore
not only the canonical privacy invariant but also a universal statistical obstruction.

(F) Symmetrization
(Theorem
9.1
).
Averaging any channel over all input permutations does not increase the pairwise
\chi^{2}
budget
and equalizes the Fisher eigenvalues. Hence, for the uniform-point Fisher criterion under a budget
upper bound
\chi_{\ast}(W)\leq C
, one may
restrict without loss of generality to permutation-equivariant channels.

III. Mechanism design

(G) GRR is not universally optimal; the thinning principle and low-budget symmetric optimality

(Theorem
10.2
, Corollary
10.3
, Theorem
11.2
).
Within the natural-orbit class of GRR blocks with null refinements, the optimal mechanism at canonical
shuffle budget
C
for the projected unbiased inverse-estimator risk is not the calibrated GRR channel.
For
0<C\leq C_{\ast}(d)
, the unique optimum in this class is an augmented GRR channel:
a fraction
p=C/C_{\ast}(d)
of users applies GRR with
\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1}
,
while the remaining fraction sends a common null symbol.
Thus the shuffle model rewards concentrating the local signal on a random informative subset of
messages rather than spreading noise uniformly.
Theorem
11.2
shows that the same low-budget augmented-GRR construction is
optimal among all permutation-equivariant channels with arbitrary finite output alphabet, up to
conditionally identical output refinements.

(H) GRR is the unique optimizer within subset selection

(Theorem
12.1
).
Among all subset-selection mechanisms
SS(d,s,\lambda)
calibrated to a common canonical
\chi^{2}
-budget,
the matched-budget risk is strictly increasing in the subset size
s
.
Hence GRR (
s=1
) is the unique optimizer in the entire subset-selection family.

The paper is nearly self-contained. We reproduce the canonical exact likelihood-ratio identity from
Part I for convenience. The main external probabilistic tools are the classical Berry–Esseen
inequality for i.i.d. sums and sequential compactness of probability measures on compact intervals.
The estimation lower bounds and the symmetric low-budget mechanism-design theorem are proved in full.

2.
Model and notation

Fix
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, and write
[d]=\{1,\dots,d\}
.
A local randomizer is a Markov kernel

W_{d}:[d]\to\Delta([d]),\qquad x\mapsto W_{d}(\cdot\mid x).

The released shuffle transcript is the histogram

N=(N_{1},\dots,N_{d}),\qquad\sum_{y=1}^{d}N_{y}=n,

obtained by drawing independent local outputs

Y_{i}\sim W_{d}(\cdot\mid x_{i})
and then forgetting the order.

We write

\Delta_{d}:=\Bigl\{\theta\in[0,1]^{d}:\sum_{x=1}^{d}\theta_{x}=1\Bigr\}

for the probability simplex on
[d]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The channel
W_{d}
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-locally differentially private
if

\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in[d]}\max_{y\in[d]}\frac{W_{d}(y\mid x)}{W_{d}(y\mid x^{\prime})}\leq e^{\varepsilon_{0}},

with the convention that
0/0=1
and
p/0=+\infty
for
p>0
.

Lemma 2.2

(Common support under pure LDP)
.

Let
W_{d}
be
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP with
\varepsilon_{0}<\infty
.
For each output symbol
y\in[d]
, either
W_{d}(y\mid x)=0
for every input
x
, or

W_{d}(y\mid x)>0
for every input
x
.

Proof.

If
W_{d}(y\mid x)=0
for some
x
and
W_{d}(y\mid x^{\prime})>0
for some
x^{\prime}
, then

\frac{W_{d}(y\mid x^{\prime})}{W_{d}(y\mid x)}=+\infty,

contradicting finite
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP.
∎

Henceforth we discard outputs of common zero mass and identify the output alphabet with this common support,
so that all rowwise likelihood ratios are finite.
Throughout the paper we assume
\varepsilon_{0}>0
; the case
\varepsilon_{0}=0
forces
W_{d}(\cdot\mid x)=W_{d}(\cdot\mid x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}
, giving
I_{0,d}(a,b)=0
and trivial privacy for every
d
.

Canonical neighboring pair

Fix distinct inputs
a,b\in[d]
.
The
canonical pair
is the binary experiment

P_{n,d}^{ab}\quad\text{versus}\quad Q_{n,d}^{ab},

where under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
all
n
users hold input
a
, while under
Q_{n,d}^{ab}
one distinguished user
holds input
b
and the remaining
n-1
users hold input
a
.
The released statistic is always the histogram
N
.

Write

P_{d}^{a}:=W_{d}(\cdot\mid a),\qquad P_{d}^{b}:=W_{d}(\cdot\mid b),

and define the pairwise likelihood-ratio function

w_{ab,d}(y):=\frac{P_{d}^{b}(y)}{P_{d}^{a}(y)},\qquad y\in[d].

By
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP,

e^{-\varepsilon_{0}}\leq w_{ab,d}(y)\leq e^{\varepsilon_{0}}\qquad\forall y.

We also define the pairwise chi-square divergence

I_{0,d}(a,b):=\chi^{2}\!\bigl(P_{d}^{b}\,\|\,P_{d}^{a}\bigr)=\sum_{y=1}^{d}\frac{(P_{d}^{b}(y)-P_{d}^{a}(y))^{2}}{P_{d}^{a}(y)}=\mathbb{E}_{P_{d}^{a}}\!\bigl[(w_{ab,d}(Y)-1)^{2}\bigr].

Equivalently,

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\Var_{P_{d}^{a}}(w_{ab,d}(Y)),

because
\mathbb{E}_{P_{d}^{a}}[w_{ab,d}(Y)]=1
.
In the notation of Part I,
I_{0,d}(a,b)
is the canonical Fisher constant

v^{\top}\Sigma^{+}v
for the pair
(a,b)
; see
[
12
, Sec. 2.4]
.

Finally, define the worst-case pairwise divergence

I_{d}^{\star}:=\max_{a\neq b}I_{0,d}(a,b).

Privacy curves

For a dominated binary experiment
Q\ll P
with likelihood ratio
L=\,\mathrm{d}Q/\,\mathrm{d}P
,
the one-sided privacy curve is

\delta_{Q\|P}(\varepsilon):=\sup_{A}\{Q(A)-e^{\varepsilon}P(A)\}=\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[(L-e^{\varepsilon})_{+}\bigr],\qquad\varepsilon\geq 0.

Similarly,

\delta_{P\|Q}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[(1-e^{\varepsilon}L)_{+}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\bigl[(L^{-1}-e^{\varepsilon})_{+}\bigr].

The two-sided privacy curve is

\delta_{P,Q}(\varepsilon):=\max\{\delta_{Q\|P}(\varepsilon),\delta_{P\|Q}(\varepsilon)\}.

For
\mu>0
we write

\delta_{\mathrm{GDP}(\mu)}(\varepsilon):=\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)-e^{\varepsilon}\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}-\frac{\mu}{2}\right),

the one-sided privacy curve of the Gaussian pair

\mathcal{N}(-\mu^{2}/2,\mu^{2})

versus

\mathcal{N}(\mu^{2}/2,\mu^{2})
.

3.
LR-quotient compression

We begin by reproducing the exact canonical likelihood-ratio identity from Part I; compare

[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Exact canonical likelihood ratio)
.

Fix
a\neq b
and let
L_{n,d}^{ab}
be the likelihood ratio

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N):=\frac{Q_{n,d}^{ab}(N)}{P_{n,d}^{ab}(N)}.

Then

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y=1}^{d}N_{y}\,w_{ab,d}(y).

Proof.

Write
p_{y}=P_{d}^{a}(y)
and
q_{y}=P_{d}^{b}(y)
.
Under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
the histogram law is

P_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\frac{n!}{\prod_{y=1}^{d}N_{y}!}\prod_{y=1}^{d}p_{y}^{N_{y}}.

Under
Q_{n,d}^{ab}
one user is distributed according to
(q_{y})
and the remaining
n-1
users according
to
(p_{y})
. Conditioning on the output of the distinguished user gives

Q_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\sum_{z=1}^{d}q_{z}\frac{(n-1)!}{(N_{z}-1)!\prod_{y\neq z}N_{y}!}\,p_{z}^{N_{z}-1}\prod_{y\neq z}p_{y}^{N_{y}},

where by convention the term is zero if
N_{z}=0
.
Dividing by
P_{n,d}^{ab}(N)
yields

\frac{Q_{n,d}^{ab}(N)}{P_{n,d}^{ab}(N)}=\sum_{z=1}^{d}\frac{q_{z}}{p_{z}}\frac{N_{z}}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z=1}^{d}N_{z}\,w_{ab,d}(z).

∎

Theorem 3.2

(LR-quotient compression)
.

Fix a pair
a\neq b
.
Let

\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}:=\{w_{ab,d}(y):y\in[d]\}\subset[e^{-\varepsilon_{0}},e^{\varepsilon_{0}}]

be the set of distinct pairwise likelihood-ratio values, and for each
r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}
define the
level set

B_{r}:=\{y\in[d]:w_{ab,d}(y)=r\}.

Let

M_{r}:=\sum_{y\in B_{r}}N_{y},\qquad r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d},

and let
M=(M_{r})_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}
.

Then the following hold.

(i)

Under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
, the vector
M
is multinomial:

M\sim\mathrm{Mult}\!\bigl(n;(p_{r})_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}\bigr),\qquad p_{r}:=P_{d}^{a}(B_{r}).

(ii)

The exact likelihood ratio is

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}r\,M_{r}.

(iii)

The statistic
M
is sufficient for the binary experiment

\{P_{n,d}^{ab},Q_{n,d}^{ab}\}
.
Equivalently, the full shuffled histogram experiment is exactly equivalent to the experiment on the

\lvert\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}\rvert
-dimensional quotient histogram
M
.

Proof.

(i) Under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
each local output falls in block
B_{r}
with probability

p_{r}=P_{d}^{a}(B_{r})
,
independently across users, so the block counts are multinomial.

(ii) Starting from (
3.1
) and grouping equal likelihood-ratio values,

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y=1}^{d}N_{y}\,w_{ab,d}(y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}r\sum_{y\in B_{r}}N_{y}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}r\,M_{r}.

(iii) By (ii), the likelihood ratio is measurable with respect to
\sigma(M)
.
In a dominated binary experiment, this already implies sufficiency. For completeness we verify the
conditional-law identity directly.
Fix a realization
m=(m_{r})_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}
of
M
.
Then for every histogram
N
with
M(N)=m
,

Q_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)\,P_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}r\,m_{r}\right)P_{n,d}^{ab}(N).

Summing over all histograms with the same block count vector
m
gives

Q_{n,d}^{ab}(M=m)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}}r\,m_{r}\right)P_{n,d}^{ab}(M=m),

and therefore

Q_{n,d}^{ab}(N\mid M=m)=P_{n,d}^{ab}(N\mid M=m).

Thus the backward Markov kernel
m\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{P_{n,d}^{ab}}(N\mid M=m)
, defined arbitrarily on
realizations
m
outside the support of
\mathcal{L}(M)
, reconstructs the original
histogram experiment from the quotient experiment. This is exact experiment equivalence.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(The correct invariant)
.

For the canonical neighboring pair
(a,b)
the exact experiment depends only on the pushforward law

\nu_{ab,d}:=P_{d}^{a}\circ w_{ab,d}^{-1}

on the compact interval
[e^{-\varepsilon_{0}},e^{\varepsilon_{0}}]
.
In particular, the raw alphabet size
d
is not a complete invariant of the canonical shuffle experiment.

Proof.

The quotient histogram law in Theorem
3.2
is the multinomial law generated by the
atom masses of
\nu_{ab,d}
, and the exact likelihood ratio is the linear functional

m\mapsto n^{-1}\sum_{r}rm_{r}
.
∎

4.
Universal chi-square bound and extremal channels

We next prove the universal upper bound on pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence.
The proof is a one-line variance identity usually attributed to Bhatia and Davis

[
2
]
; we include it for completeness.

Lemma 4.1

(Bhatia–Davis variance bound)
.

Let
X
be a real random variable satisfying

m\leq X\leq M\quad\text{a.s.}

and let
\mu=\mathbb{E}[X]
.
Then

\Var(X)\leq(M-\mu)(\mu-m).

Equality holds if and only if
X\in\{m,M\}
almost surely.

Proof.

Expand the nonnegative random variable
(M-X)(X-m)
:

(M-X)(X-m)=-X^{2}+(M+m)X-Mm.

Taking expectations and using

\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=\Var(X)+\mu^{2}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(M-X)(X-m)]

\displaystyle=-\Var(X)-\mu^{2}+(M+m)\mu-Mm

\displaystyle=(M-\mu)(\mu-m)-\Var(X).

Since the left-hand side is nonnegative, (
4.1
) follows.
Equality holds if and only if
(M-X)(X-m)=0
almost surely, i.e. iff

X\in\{m,M\}
a.s.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(Universal chi-square bound and extremizers)
.

Let
W_{d}
be an
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP channel and write
\lambda=e^{\varepsilon_{0}}
.
For every pair
a\neq b
,

0\leq I_{0,d}(a,b)=\chi^{2}\!\bigl(P_{d}^{b}\,\|\,P_{d}^{a}\bigr)\leq\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda}.

Equality holds if and only if, under
P_{d}^{a}
, the likelihood ratio

w_{ab,d}(Y)
takes only the two endpoint values

\lambda^{-1}
and
\lambda
with probabilities

P_{d}^{a}\!\bigl(w_{ab,d}(Y)=\lambda^{-1}\bigr)=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},\qquad P_{d}^{a}\!\bigl(w_{ab,d}(Y)=\lambda\bigr)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}.

Equivalently, the output alphabet can be partitioned as

[d]=B_{-}\sqcup B_{+}

such that

w_{ab,d}(y)=\lambda^{-1}\ \text{for }y\in B_{-},\qquad w_{ab,d}(y)=\lambda\ \text{for }y\in B_{+},

with

P_{d}^{a}(B_{-})=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},\qquad P_{d}^{a}(B_{+})=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}.

Proof.

Fix
a\neq b
and set

R:=w_{ab,d}(Y),\qquad Y\sim P_{d}^{a}.

Because
W_{d}
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP,

\lambda^{-1}\leq R\leq\lambda\qquad\text{a.s.}

Also

\mathbb{E}[R]=\sum_{y=1}^{d}P_{d}^{a}(y)\frac{P_{d}^{b}(y)}{P_{d}^{a}(y)}=\sum_{y=1}^{d}P_{d}^{b}(y)=1.

Finally,

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\mathbb{E}[(R-1)^{2}]=\Var(R),

again because
\mathbb{E}[R]=1
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
with

m=\lambda^{-1}
,

M=\lambda
,

\mu=1
,
we obtain

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\Var(R)\leq(\lambda-1)(1-\lambda^{-1})=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda}.

This proves (
4.2
).

Equality in Lemma
4.1
holds iff
R\in\{\lambda^{-1},\lambda\}
almost surely.
Since
\mathbb{E}[R]=1
, if

p=P_{d}^{a}(R=\lambda)
,
then

p\lambda+(1-p)\lambda^{-1}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}.

Hence

P_{d}^{a}(R=\lambda^{-1})=\lambda/(1+\lambda)
,
which is exactly (
4.2
). The equivalence with (
4.2
)
is immediate after setting

B_{+}=\{y:w_{ab,d}(y)=\lambda\}

and

B_{-}=\{y:w_{ab,d}(y)=\lambda^{-1}\}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3

(Universal upper bound on the Gaussian scale)
.

For every pair
(a,b)
and every
n\geq 1
, the canonical Gaussian parameter

\mu_{n,d}(a,b):=\sqrt{\frac{I_{0,d}(a,b)}{n}}

satisfies

\mu_{n,d}(a,b)\leq\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{\lambda\,n}}.

In particular,

\mu_{n,d}^{\star}:=\max_{a\neq b}\mu_{n,d}(a,b)\leq\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{\lambda\,n}}.

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Corollary
4.3
is an upper bound on the
canonical Gaussian/GDP scale
.
It does not assert finite-
n
GDP for arbitrary channels.
Section
6
proves that whenever

n\,I_{0,d}(a,b)\to\infty
,
the canonical neighboring experiment is asymptotically GDP with parameter

\mu_{n,d}(a,b)=\sqrt{I_{0,d}(a,b)/n}
.
The obstruction family below shows that (
4.3
) is sharp.

5.
An explicit obstruction family

We now construct explicit
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP channels on growing alphabets for which the exact shuffled
privacy curve is identical to the binary randomized-response curve for every
d
.
Throughout this section
\lambda=e^{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

Definition 5.1

(Half-block cyclic channel)
.

Assume
d
is even and identify
[d]
with the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.
For each
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, define the half-block

A_{x}:=\{x,x+1,\dots,x+d/2-1\}\pmod{d}.

The channel
W_{d}
is defined by

W_{d}(y\mid x)=\frac{2\lambda}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\in A_{x}\}+\frac{2}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\notin A_{x}\}.

Theorem 5.2

(Explicit obstruction family)
.

Let
W_{d}
be the half-block channel of Definition
5.1
, and fix the opposite pair

b=a+d/2\pmod{d}.

Then the following hold.

(i)

W_{d}
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP for every even
d
.

(ii)

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\chi^{2}\!\bigl(P_{d}^{b}\,\|\,P_{d}^{a}\bigr)=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda},

so the pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence is independent of
d
and saturates the universal upper bound.

(iii)

The LR quotient has exactly two atoms:

\mathcal{R}_{ab,d}=\{\lambda^{-1},\lambda\}.

The sufficient quotient statistic is

K:=\sum_{y\notin A_{a}}N_{y},

and under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
,

K\sim\mathrm{Bin}\!\left(n,\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right).

(iv)

The exact canonical likelihood ratio is

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\lambda^{-1}+\frac{K}{n}\bigl(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\bigr).

Hence the exact one-sided privacy curve is

\delta_{Q_{n,d}^{ab}\|P_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\left(\lambda^{-1}+\frac{k}{n}\bigl(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\bigr)-e^{\varepsilon}\right)_{+},

where
q=(1+\lambda)^{-1}
.
This is exactly the binary randomized-response privacy curve.

(v)

For every sequence
n\to\infty
,
the canonical Gaussian/GDP scale is

\mu_{n,d}(a,b)=\sqrt{\frac{I_{0,d}(a,b)}{n}}=\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{\lambda\,n}},

the same as for binary randomized response.
Thus growing
d
gives no additional privacy amplification along this pair.

Proof.

(i) Every entry of every row equals either

2\lambda/[d(1+\lambda)]

or

2/[d(1+\lambda)]
.
Hence for every
x,x^{\prime},y
the ratio

W_{d}(y\mid x)/W_{d}(y\mid x^{\prime})

is one of

1,\lambda,\lambda^{-1}
,
so
W_{d}
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP.

(ii) For the opposite pair
b=a+d/2
, the half-blocks are complementary:

A_{b}=A_{a}^{c}.

Therefore

P_{d}^{a}(y)=\frac{2\lambda}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\in A_{a}\}+\frac{2}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\notin A_{a}\},

while

P_{d}^{b}(y)=\frac{2}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\in A_{a}\}+\frac{2\lambda}{d(1+\lambda)}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\notin A_{a}\}.

Hence the pairwise likelihood ratio is

w_{ab,d}(y)=\lambda^{-1}\mathbf{1}\{y\in A_{a}\}+\lambda\mathbf{1}\{y\notin A_{a}\}.

Under
P_{d}^{a}
,

P_{d}^{a}(A_{a})=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},\qquad P_{d}^{a}(A_{a}^{c})=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}.

Thus

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\,(\lambda^{-1}-1)^{2}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\,(\lambda-1)^{2}=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda}.

This is (
(ii)
).

(iii) Equation (
5
) shows that the only likelihood-ratio levels are

\lambda^{-1}
and
\lambda
.
The quotient statistic can therefore be chosen as the count of outputs in the
\lambda
-block,
namely

K=\sum_{y\notin A_{a}}N_{y}.

Under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
each user falls in
A_{a}^{c}
with probability

q=P_{d}^{a}(A_{a}^{c})=\frac{1}{1+\lambda},

so
K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)
.

(iv) The exact quotient formula (
(ii)
) from
Theorem
3.2
gives

L_{n,d}^{ab}(N)=\frac{1}{n}\bigl[\lambda^{-1}(n-K)+\lambda K\bigr]=\lambda^{-1}+\frac{K}{n}(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}),

which is (
(iv)
). Plugging this into the positive-part formula for

\delta_{Q\|P}

and using (
(iii)
) yields (
(iv)
). This is exactly the classical binary
randomized-response expression.

(v) Equation (
(v)
) is immediate from (
(ii)
).
The absence of additional
d
-gain is therefore exact at the Fisher/Gaussian scale, and
Theorem
6.2
(iii) below shows that the canonical neighboring experiment remains on the
ordinary shuffled Gaussian/GDP scale.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Theorem
5.2
is a genuinely interior obstruction.
All rows have full support and no vanishing minority block is involved.
This is different from the strong-boundary obstruction of Part III

[
14
, Prop. 6.1]
, where a bounded minority block can remain visible to exact histogram tests
while disappearing in the projected weak limit.

6.
A sharp geometric dichotomy

We now characterize exactly when growing alphabets do and do not force additional privacy amplification.

Definition 6.1
.

A family of
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP channels
\{W_{d}\}_{d\geq 2}
is called

•

diluting
if

I_{d}^{\star}=\max_{a\neq b}I_{0,d}(a,b)\longrightarrow 0\qquad(d\to\infty);

•

persistent
if

\limsup_{d\to\infty}I_{d}^{\star}>0.

We begin with the exact equivalence between chi-square collapse and likelihood-ratio-law collapse.

Theorem 6.2

(Sharp dichotomy by LR-law collapse)
.

Let
\{W_{d}\}_{d\geq 2}
be an
\varepsilon_{0}
-LDP family and write
\lambda=e^{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

(i)

The family is diluting if and only if for every sequence of pairs

a_{d}\neq b_{d}

the pairwise likelihood-ratio laws satisfy

\nu_{a_{d}b_{d},d}\Rightarrow\delta_{1}\qquad(d\to\infty).

(ii)

The family is persistent if and only if there exist a subsequence
d_{m}\to\infty
and pairs

a_{m}\neq b_{m}

such that

\nu_{a_{m}b_{m},d_{m}}\Rightarrow\nu

for some probability measure
\nu
on
[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]
with
\nu\neq\delta_{1}
.

(iii)

Let
a_{d}\neq b_{d}
be any sequence of pairs, and set

I_{d}:=I_{0,d}(a_{d},b_{d}).

For the canonical neighboring experiment define

U_{n,d}:=L_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}(N)-1.

Then under
P_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}
,

U_{n,d}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i,d},\qquad Z_{i,d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}Z_{d}:=R_{d}-1,\qquad R_{d}\sim\nu_{a_{d}b_{d},d},

with

\mathbb{E}[Z_{d}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[Z_{d}^{2}]=I_{d},\qquad|Z_{d}|\leq\lambda-1\ \text{a.s.}

Define the standardized score

S_{n,d}:=\frac{U_{n,d}}{\sqrt{I_{d}/n}}.

Then the Berry–Esseen bound

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|P_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}(S_{n,d}\leq t)-\Phi(t)\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}

holds for every
n
and every pair with
I_{d}>0
.
In particular, if
nI_{d}\to\infty
, then

S_{n,d}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)\qquad\text{under }P_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}.

Moreover, under
Q_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}
,

S_{n,d}-\sqrt{\frac{I_{d}}{n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)\qquad\text{whenever }nI_{d}\to\infty.

Finally, if

\Lambda_{n,d}:=\log L_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}(N)
,
then

\frac{\Lambda_{n,d}+I_{d}/(2n)}{\sqrt{I_{d}/n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)\quad\text{under }P_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}},

\frac{\Lambda_{n,d}-I_{d}/(2n)}{\sqrt{I_{d}/n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)\quad\text{under }Q_{n,d}^{a_{d}b_{d}}.

Thus the canonical neighboring experiment is asymptotically GDP with parameter

\mu_{n,d}=\sqrt{\frac{I_{d}}{n}}

whenever
nI_{d}\to\infty
.
Here “asymptotically GDP with parameter
\mu_{n,d}
” means that
for every sequence
(n,d)
with
nI_{d}\to\infty
,

\Delta\!\bigl((P_{n,d},Q_{n,d}),\,\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{n,d}}\bigr)\to 0,

where
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:=\bigl(\mathcal{N}(-\mu^{2}/2,\mu^{2}),\mathcal{N}(\mu^{2}/2,\mu^{2})\bigr)
is the Gaussian shift
experiment.
In particular, for every fixed

\varepsilon\geq 0
the exact one-sided privacy curve satisfies

\delta_{Q_{n,d}\|P_{n,d}}(\varepsilon)\to\delta_{\mathrm{GDP}(\mu_{n,d})}(\varepsilon)
.

(iv)

If the family is persistent, then there exist
c>0
, a subsequence
d_{m}\to\infty
,
and pairs
a_{m}\neq b_{m}
such that

I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq c\qquad\text{for all }m.

Hence, along this subsequence, the canonical neighboring experiment remains on the ordinary shuffled scale

\mu_{n,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq\sqrt{\frac{c}{n}}.

In particular, growing
d
provides no automatic privacy gain beyond the usual
n^{-1/2}
shuffled scale.

(v)

If the family is diluting, then for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
and every sequence
n=n(d)\geq 1
,

\sup_{a\neq b}\delta_{Q_{n,d}^{ab}\|P_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{I_{d}^{\star}}{n(e^{\varepsilon}-1)},

\sup_{a\neq b}\delta_{P_{n,d}^{ab}\|Q_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{e^{\varepsilon}I_{d}^{\star}}{n(1-e^{-\varepsilon})}.

Consequently

\sup_{a\neq b}\delta_{P_{n,d}^{ab},Q_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow 0\qquad(d\to\infty).

That is, every diluting family yields asymptotically perfect fixed-
\varepsilon
shuffle privacy.

The proof uses three elementary lemmas.

Lemma 6.3

(Classical Berry–Esseen inequality)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}
such that the following holds.
If
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d. centered random variables with variance
\sigma^{2}>0
and finite third
absolute moment
\rho_{3}:=\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{3}
, then

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leq t\right)-\Phi(t)\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\rho_{3}}{\sigma^{3}\sqrt{n}}.

Proof.

This is the classical Berry–Esseen theorem; see, for example,
Feller
[
8
, Ch. XVI, Thm. 2]
or Petrov

[
11
, Ch. V, Thm. 3]
.
∎

Lemma 6.4

(Kolmogorov perturbation by a bounded shift)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be real-valued random variables, and let
F
be a distribution function with bounded density

f
,
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq M
.
If
|Y|\leq\eta
almost surely, then

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl|\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq t)-F(t)\bigr|\leq\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl|\mathbb{P}(X\leq t)-F(t)\bigr|+2M\eta.

In particular, for the standard normal distribution,

d_{K}(X+Y,\mathcal{N}(0,1))\leq d_{K}(X,\mathcal{N}(0,1))+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\eta.

Proof.

For every
t
,

\{X\leq t-\eta\}\subseteq\{X+Y\leq t\}\subseteq\{X\leq t+\eta\}.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}(X\leq t-\eta)-F(t)\leq\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq t)-F(t)\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq t+\eta)-F(t).

Insert and subtract
F(t\pm\eta)
and use the density bound:

F(t+\eta)-F(t-\eta)\leq 2M\eta.

Taking suprema over
t
gives (
6.4
). For the standard normal law,

M=\sup_{x}\phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}
,
which yields (
6.4
).
∎

Lemma 6.5

(Quadratic logarithmic remainder)
.

For every
u\in[-1/2,1/2]
,

\left|\log(1+u)-u+\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right|\leq|u|^{3}.

Proof.

For
|u|<1
the power series gives

\log(1+u)-u+\frac{u^{2}}{2}=\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\,u^{k}.

Hence

\left|\log(1+u)-u+\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right|\leq\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{|u|^{k}}{k}\leq\frac{|u|^{3}}{3}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|u|^{j}=\frac{|u|^{3}}{3(1-|u|)}.

For
|u|\leq 1/2
the right-hand side is at most
(2/3)|u|^{3}\leq|u|^{3}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We write simply
P_{n,d},Q_{n,d},w_{d},\nu_{d},I_{d}
when the pair
(a_{d},b_{d})
is clear from context.

Part (i).

Assume first that the family is diluting, i.e.
I_{d}^{\star}\to 0
.
Fix any sequence of pairs
(a_{d},b_{d})
.
If
R_{d}\sim\nu_{a_{d}b_{d},d}
, then

\mathbb{E}[(R_{d}-1)^{2}]=I_{0,d}(a_{d},b_{d})\leq I_{d}^{\star}\to 0.

Hence
R_{d}\to 1
in
L^{2}
, therefore also in probability, hence

\nu_{a_{d}b_{d},d}=\mathcal{L}(R_{d})\Rightarrow\delta_{1}.

Conversely, assume that every pairwise likelihood-ratio law converges weakly to
\delta_{1}
along every
sequence of pairs. If
I_{d}^{\star}\not\to 0
, then there exist
\eta>0
, a subsequence
d_{m}\to\infty
, and
pairs
(a_{m},b_{m})
such that

I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq\eta\qquad\forall m.

Because the laws
\nu_{a_{m}b_{m},d_{m}}
live on the compact interval

[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]
, Prokhorov compactness yields a further subsequence
(which we do not relabel) and a limit law
\nu
such that

\nu_{a_{m}b_{m},d_{m}}\Rightarrow\nu.

The function
r\mapsto(r-1)^{2}
is bounded and continuous on
[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]
, so

\int(r-1)^{2}\,\nu(\,\mathrm{d}r)=\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq\eta>0.

Hence
\nu\neq\delta_{1}
, contradicting the assumption. Therefore
I_{d}^{\star}\to 0
.

Part (ii).

If the family is persistent, choose
\eta>0
, a subsequence
d_{m}\to\infty
, and pairs
(a_{m},b_{m})
with

I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq\eta\qquad\forall m.

Again compactness gives a further weak limit

\nu_{a_{m}b_{m},d_{m}}\Rightarrow\nu

on
[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]
.
By bounded continuity of
(r-1)^{2}
,

\int(r-1)^{2}\,\nu(\,\mathrm{d}r)=\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})\geq\eta,

so
\nu\neq\delta_{1}
.

Conversely, if such a nondegenerate subsequential limit exists, then again by bounded continuity,

\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{0,d_{m}}(a_{m},b_{m})=\int(r-1)^{2}\,\nu(\,\mathrm{d}r)>0.

Hence
\limsup_{d}I_{d}^{\star}>0
, so the family is persistent.

Part (iii): null representation and Berry–Esseen.

Let
Y_{1,d},\dots,Y_{n,d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}P_{d}^{a_{d}}
and set

R_{i,d}:=w_{d}(Y_{i,d}),\qquad Z_{i,d}:=R_{i,d}-1.

By Lemma
3.1
,

U_{n,d}=L_{n,d}-1=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i,d}.

Also

\mathbb{E}[Z_{i,d}]=\mathbb{E}[R_{i,d}]-1=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[Z_{i,d}^{2}]=I_{d}.

Because

R_{i,d}\in[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]\qquad\text{a.s.},

we have

|Z_{i,d}|\leq\lambda-1\qquad\text{a.s.}

Therefore

\mathbb{E}|Z_{i,d}|^{3}\leq(\lambda-1)\mathbb{E}[Z_{i,d}^{2}]=(\lambda-1)I_{d}.

Applying Lemma
6.3
with

X_{i}=Z_{i,d}
,

\sigma^{2}=I_{d}
,
and (
6
) gives

\sup_{t}\left|P_{n,d}(S_{n,d}\leq t)-\Phi(t)\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}.

This proves (
(iii)
). If
nI_{d}\to\infty
, the right-hand side tends to zero, yielding
(
(iii)
).

Part (iii): alternative representation and shift.

For
n\geq 2
, under
Q_{n,d}
, let

Y_{1,d},\dots,Y_{n-1,d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}P_{d}^{a_{d}},\qquad Y_{*,d}\sim P_{d}^{b_{d}},

with
Y_{*,d}
independent of
Y_{1,d},\dots,Y_{n-1,d}
. Then

U_{n,d}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(w_{d}(Y_{i,d})-1)+(w_{d}(Y_{*,d})-1)\right).

Define

Z_{*,d}:=w_{d}(Y_{*,d})-1.

Since
P_{d}^{b_{d}}=w_{d}P_{d}^{a_{d}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{*,d}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}P_{d}^{b_{d}}(y)\bigl(w_{d}(y)-1\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}P_{d}^{a_{d}}(y)\,w_{d}(y)\bigl(w_{d}(y)-1\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(R_{d}-1)^{2}]=I_{d}.

Also
|Z_{*,d}|\leq\lambda-1
.

Let

A_{n,d}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(n-1)I_{d}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i,d},\qquad c_{n}:=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}},\qquad B_{n,d}:=\frac{Z_{*,d}-I_{d}}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}.

Then

S_{n,d}-\sqrt{\frac{I_{d}}{n}}=c_{n}A_{n,d}+B_{n,d}.

By the already proved Berry–Esseen bound,

d_{K}(A_{n,d},\mathcal{N}(0,1))\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{(n-1)I_{d}}}.

Next, the law of
c_{n}A_{n,d}
is at Kolmogorov distance at most

d_{K}(c_{n}A_{n,d},\mathcal{N}(0,c_{n}^{2}))\leq d_{K}(A_{n,d},\mathcal{N}(0,1))

by simple rescaling.
Since
\mathcal{N}(0,c_{n}^{2})
has density
\phi(t/c_{n})/c_{n}
, the distance between

\mathcal{N}(0,c_{n}^{2})
and
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
satisfies

\sup_{t}\left|\Phi\!\left(\frac{t}{c_{n}}\right)-\Phi(t)\right|\leq\frac{1-c_{n}}{c_{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x\phi(x)|\leq\frac{1}{n-1},

using
c_{n}^{-1}\leq\sqrt{2}
for
n\geq 2
and

\sup_{x}|x\phi(x)|=(2\pi e)^{-1/2}
.
Thus

d_{K}(c_{n}A_{n,d},\mathcal{N}(0,1))\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{(n-1)I_{d}}}+\frac{1}{n-1}.

Finally,

|B_{n,d}|\leq\frac{|Z_{*,d}|+I_{d}}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}\leq\frac{(\lambda-1)+(\lambda-1)^{2}/\lambda}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}\leq\frac{2(\lambda-1)}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}\qquad\text{a.s.}

Therefore Lemma
6.4
gives

d_{K}\!\left(S_{n,d}-\sqrt{I_{d}/n},\mathcal{N}(0,1)\right)\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}\frac{\lambda-1}{\sqrt{(n-1)I_{d}}}+\frac{1}{n-1}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{2(\lambda-1)}{\sqrt{nI_{d}}}.

If
nI_{d}\to\infty
, the right-hand side tends to zero, proving (
(iii)
).

Part (iii): log-likelihood expansion and GDP.

Let

\mu_{n,d}:=\sqrt{\frac{I_{d}}{n}}.

Under
P_{n,d}
, the variable
U_{n,d}
is bounded by
\lambda-1
in absolute value and
has variance
I_{d}/n=\mu_{n,d}^{2}
.
Under
Q_{n,d}
, the decomposition
U_{n,d}=\frac{1}{n}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i,d}+Z_{*,d}\bigr)

from the alternative representation gives

\Var_{Q}(U_{n,d})=\frac{(n-1)I_{d}+\Var_{Q}(Z_{*,d})}{n^{2}}\leq\frac{I_{d}}{n}+\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{n^{2}},

so
U_{n,d}=O_{P}(\mu_{n,d})
under both hypotheses.
In particular
U_{n,d}\to 0
in probability whenever
n\to\infty
.
By Lemma
6.5
,

\Lambda_{n,d}=U_{n,d}-\frac{U_{n,d}^{2}}{2}+R_{n,d},\qquad|R_{n,d}|\leq|U_{n,d}|^{3}\quad\text{on }\{|U_{n,d}|\leq 1/2\}.

Since
U_{n,d}\to 0
in probability, we have
\mathbb{P}(|U_{n,d}|>1/2)\to 0
,
so the expansion (
6
) holds on an event of probability tending to one.
Combined with
U_{n,d}=O_{P}(\mu_{n,d})
, we obtain

R_{n,d}=O_{P}(\mu_{n,d}^{3})=o_{P}(\mu_{n,d})\qquad\text{whenever }nI_{d}\to\infty.

Under
P_{n,d}
,

U_{n,d}=\mu_{n,d}S_{n,d},

so

\Lambda_{n,d}=\mu_{n,d}S_{n,d}-\frac{\mu_{n,d}^{2}}{2}S_{n,d}^{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}).

Since
S_{n,d}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, we have
S_{n,d}^{2}=1+O_{P}(1)
, hence

\mu_{n,d}^{2}(S_{n,d}^{2}-1)=O_{P}(\mu_{n,d}^{2})=o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}).

Therefore

\Lambda_{n,d}=\mu_{n,d}S_{n,d}-\frac{\mu_{n,d}^{2}}{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}),

which implies (
(iii)
).

Under
Q_{n,d}
, (
(iii)
) gives

U_{n,d}=\mu_{n,d}\left(Z_{n,d}+\mu_{n,d}\right)+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d})=\mu_{n,d}Z_{n,d}+\mu_{n,d}^{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}),

where
Z_{n,d}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.
Then

U_{n,d}^{2}=\mu_{n,d}^{2}Z_{n,d}^{2}+2\mu_{n,d}^{3}Z_{n,d}+\mu_{n,d}^{4}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}^{2})=\mu_{n,d}^{2}Z_{n,d}^{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}),

so that
\mu_{n,d}^{2}(Z_{n,d}^{2}-1)=O_{P}(\mu_{n,d}^{2})=o_{P}(\mu_{n,d})
.
Therefore

\Lambda_{n,d}=\mu_{n,d}Z_{n,d}+\mu_{n,d}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{n,d}^{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d})=\mu_{n,d}Z_{n,d}+\frac{\mu_{n,d}^{2}}{2}+o_{P}(\mu_{n,d}),

proving (
(iii)
).

The Gaussian limit (
(iii)
)–(
(iii)
) is precisely the canonical GDP limit with
parameter
\mu_{n,d}
.
Indeed, by the standard characterization of convergence of binary experiments via log-likelihood ratios
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
or Le Cam
[
9
, Ch. 8]
),
(
(iii)
)–(
(iii)
) imply that the binary experiment

(P_{n,d},Q_{n,d})
converges in Le Cam distance to the Gaussian shift experiment

\bigl(\mathcal{N}(-\mu_{n,d}^{2}/2,\mu_{n,d}^{2}),\,\mathcal{N}(\mu_{n,d}^{2}/2,\mu_{n,d}^{2})\bigr)
,
and in particular the privacy curves converge pointwise to
\delta_{\mathrm{GDP}(\mu_{n,d})}
.

Part (iv).

This is immediate from part (ii):
if the family is persistent, choose the subsequence and pairs from part (ii). Since

\int(r-1)^{2}\nu(\,\mathrm{d}r)>0
,
the corresponding
I_{d}
stay bounded below by some
c>0
eventually.
Then

\mu_{n,d}=\sqrt{\frac{I_{d}}{n}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{c}{n}}

along that subsequence. Thus there is no additional
d
-dependent privacy gain.

Part (v).

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, let
c_{\varepsilon}=e^{\varepsilon}-1>0
, and consider any pair
(a,b)
.
Under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
write
L=L_{n,d}^{ab}=1+U
.
Then

(L-e^{\varepsilon})_{+}=(U-c_{\varepsilon})_{+}\leq\frac{U^{2}}{c_{\varepsilon}}.

Indeed, if
U\leq c_{\varepsilon}
, the left-hand side is zero; if
U>c_{\varepsilon}
, then

U-c_{\varepsilon}\leq U^{2}/c_{\varepsilon}

because
U\geq c_{\varepsilon}>0
.
Taking expectations under
P_{n,d}^{ab}
gives

\delta_{Q_{n,d}^{ab}\|P_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}[(L-e^{\varepsilon})_{+}]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[U^{2}]}{e^{\varepsilon}-1}=\frac{I_{0,d}(a,b)}{n(e^{\varepsilon}-1)}.

Maximizing over pairs yields (
(v)
).

For the reverse direction,

\delta_{P_{n,d}^{ab}\|Q_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(1-e^{\varepsilon}L)_{+}\bigr].

If
1-e^{\varepsilon}(1+U)>0
, then

U<-(1-e^{-\varepsilon})
.
Set
c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=1-e^{-\varepsilon}>0
.
Pointwise,

(1-e^{\varepsilon}L)_{+}=e^{\varepsilon}(-U-c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})_{+}\leq e^{\varepsilon}\frac{U^{2}}{c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}.

Taking expectations yields

\delta_{P_{n,d}^{ab}\|Q_{n,d}^{ab}}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{e^{\varepsilon}I_{0,d}(a,b)}{n(1-e^{-\varepsilon})},

and maximizing over pairs gives (
(v)
).
Since
I_{d}^{\star}\to 0
, both upper bounds tend to zero uniformly in the pair.
∎

7.
Two poles: GRR versus half-block

This section records the two opposite geometric poles highlighted by Theorems

4.2
and
5.2
.

Proposition 7.1

(The GRR pole)
.

Fix
\lambda=e^{\varepsilon_{0}}
and consider
d
-ary generalized randomized response

W_{d}(y\mid x)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+d-1}\,\mathbf{1}\{y=x\}+\frac{1}{\lambda+d-1}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\neq x\}.

For every pair
a\neq b
,

w_{ab,d}(y)\in\{\lambda^{-1},1,\lambda\},

and under
P_{d}^{a}
,

\nu_{ab,d}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+d-1}\,\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}+\frac{d-2}{\lambda+d-1}\,\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{\lambda+d-1}\,\delta_{\lambda}.

Hence

\nu_{ab,d}\Rightarrow\delta_{1},\qquad I_{0,d}(a,b)=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(\lambda+d-1)}=\Theta(d^{-1}).

In particular, GRR is diluting.

Proof.

Fix
a\neq b
. Under
P_{d}^{a}
, the output
Y
equals
a
with probability

\alpha_{d}=\lambda/(\lambda+d-1)
,
equals
b
with probability

\beta_{d}=1/(\lambda+d-1)
,
and falls in the remaining
d-2
symbols with total probability

\rho_{d}=(d-2)/(\lambda+d-1)
.
The pairwise likelihood ratio is

w_{ab,d}(a)=\lambda^{-1},\qquad w_{ab,d}(b)=\lambda,\qquad w_{ab,d}(y)=1\ \ (y\neq a,b),

which proves (
7.1
). Since
\beta_{d}\to 0
and
\rho_{d}\to 1
, we have

\nu_{ab,d}\Rightarrow\delta_{1}
.
Finally,

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\alpha_{d}(\lambda^{-1}-1)^{2}+\beta_{d}(\lambda-1)^{2}=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(\lambda+d-1)}.

∎

Proposition 7.2

(The half-block pole)
.

For the half-block family of Theorem
5.2
, the pairwise likelihood-ratio law along an
opposite pair is

\nu_{ab,d}=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda},

independent of
d
, and

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda}.

Hence the half-block family is persistent and saturates the universal upper bound of
Theorem
4.2
.

Proof.

This is exactly the computation in the proof of Theorem
5.2
(ii).
∎

Remark 7.3

(Interpolating families)
.

The two poles can be interpolated explicitly.
Fix any sequence
\theta_{d}\in[0,1]
, choose an even number
m_{d}\leq d
, and split the output alphabet
into an informative block of size
m_{d}
and a common block of size
d-m_{d}
.
On the informative block place total mass
\theta_{d}
and use the half-block construction scaled by

\theta_{d}
; on the common block place the uniform input-independent mass
(1-\theta_{d})/(d-m_{d})
.
For the opposite informative pair one then has

\nu_{ab,d}=(1-\theta_{d})\delta_{1}+\theta_{d}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda}\right),

and therefore

I_{0,d}(a,b)=\theta_{d}\,\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda}.

Thus every asymptotic profile between the two poles is realizable:

\theta_{d}\to 0

gives dilution, while

\limsup\theta_{d}>0

gives persistence.

8.
Universal lower bound on estimation risk

From this point on,
W:[d]\to\Delta(\mathcal{Y})
denotes a channel with arbitrary finite output alphabet

\mathcal{Y}
.
As in Section 2, we discard all outputs of common zero mass, so that

\mu(y):=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x=1}^{d}W(y\mid x)>0
for every
y\in\mathcal{Y}
.
We work temporarily in the i.i.d. multinomial estimation model:

\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})\in\Delta_{d}
,

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\theta
, and

Y_{i}\sim W(\cdot\mid X_{i})
conditionally independently.
The released statistic is the shuffled histogram on
\mathcal{Y}
.
Write

q_{\theta}(y):=\sum_{x=1}^{d}\theta_{x}W(y\mid x),\qquad\chi_{\ast}(W):=\max_{a\neq b}\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid a)\bigr),

and let

P_{T}:=I_{d}-\frac{1}{d}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}

denote the orthogonal projection onto

T_{d}:=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{x}u_{x}=0\}.

Whenever the Fisher information is singular on
T_{d}
, no estimator can be locally unbiased in the missing
tangent direction, so the lower bounds below are then vacuous or trivial.
Accordingly we restrict attention to channels for which
I_{\theta}(W)
is positive definite on
T_{d}
.
This injectivity condition holds, for example, whenever the
d\times|\mathcal{Y}|
channel matrix
W

has rank
d
.

An estimator
\widehat{\theta}
is
locally unbiased at
\theta_{0}
in tangent directions
if

\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[\widehat{\theta}]=\theta_{0}
and

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}+tv}[\widehat{\theta}]\right|_{t=0}=v\qquad\text{for every }v\in T_{d}.

The Fisher information on
T_{d}
is

I_{\theta}(W)=n\,P_{T}\,W\,\diag(q_{\theta})^{-1}W^{\top}P_{T}.

Theorem 8.1

(Near-vertex Cramér–Rao bound)
.

Assume
I_{\theta}(W)
is positive definite on
T_{d}
for
\theta
near
\theta^{(\rho)}
.
Fix
0<\rho<1/(d-1)
and

\theta^{(\rho)}:=\bigl(1-(d-1)\rho,\rho,\dots,\rho\bigr).

Let
\widehat{\theta}
be any estimator that is locally unbiased at
\theta^{(\rho)}
in tangent
directions.
Then

\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{(\rho)}}\bigl\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta^{(\rho)}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{(d-1)\bigl(1-(d-1)\rho\bigr)}{n\,\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

Proof.

For
u\in T_{d}
, (
8
) gives

u^{\top}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)u=n\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\frac{\bigl(\sum_{x}u_{x}W(y\mid x)\bigr)^{2}}{q_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(y)}.

For
j=2,\dots,d
, set
w_{j}:=e_{j}-e_{1}
.
Since

q_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(y)\geq\bigl(1-(d-1)\rho\bigr)W(y\mid 1)
,
we have

\displaystyle w_{j}^{\top}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)w_{j}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\frac{\bigl(W(y\mid j)-W(y\mid 1)\bigr)^{2}}{q_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(y)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{1-(d-1)\rho}\,\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid j)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid 1)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}{1-(d-1)\rho}.

Now let

S:=\sum_{j=2}^{d}w_{j}w_{j}^{\top}.

For
u\in T_{d}
,

u^{\top}Su=\sum_{j=2}^{d}(u_{j}-u_{1})^{2}=\|u\|_{2}^{2}+d\,u_{1}^{2}\geq\|u\|_{2}^{2},

so
S\succeq I
on
T_{d}
. Therefore

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)\leq\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)S\bigr)=\sum_{j=2}^{d}w_{j}^{\top}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)w_{j}\leq\frac{n(d-1)\chi_{\ast}(W)}{1-(d-1)\rho}

by (
8
). Since
\dim T_{d}=d-1
, the arithmetic–harmonic mean inequality gives

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)^{-1}\geq\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)}\geq\frac{(d-1)\bigl(1-(d-1)\rho\bigr)}{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

Choose an orthonormal matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times(d-1)}
with image
T_{d}
, and parametrize the simplex
locally by

u\mapsto\theta^{(\rho)}+Hu
.
For the induced
(d-1)
-dimensional parametric submodel, the Fisher information is

H^{\top}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)H
, while local unbiasedness in tangent directions is exactly ordinary
local unbiasedness for the parameter
u
at
u=0
.
The multivariate Cramér–Rao inequality therefore yields

H^{\top}\Cov_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(\widehat{\theta})H\succeq\bigl(H^{\top}I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)H\bigr)^{-1}
,
equivalently

\Cov_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(\widehat{\theta})\succeq I_{\theta^{(\rho)}}(W)^{-1}

on
T_{d}
.
Taking traces on
T_{d}
proves (
8.1
).
∎

Corollary 8.2

(Locally unbiased minimax lower bound)
.

For every fixed
d
and every
0<\rho<1/(d-1)
,

\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\ \mathrm{loc.\ unbiased}}\sup_{\theta\in\Delta_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{(d-1)\bigl(1-(d-1)\rho\bigr)}{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

In particular, for fixed
d
,

\inf_{\widehat{\theta}\ \mathrm{loc.\ unbiased}}\sup_{\theta\in\Delta_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{d-1}{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}\qquad\text{as }n\chi_{\ast}(W)\to\infty.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
8.1
at the single point
\theta^{(\rho)}
.
The supremum over
\theta\in\Delta_{d}
is at least the risk at that point.
∎

Lemma 8.3

(Assouad cube lemma)
.

Let
\{P_{v}:v\in\{0,1\}^{m}\}
be a family of experiments.
Assume that for every coordinate
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and every pair

v,v^{(j)}
differing only in coordinate
j
,

\mathrm{TV}(P_{v},P_{v^{(j)}})\leq\eta.

Then every decoder
\widehat{v}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
satisfies

\sup_{v\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{v}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{\widehat{v}_{j}\neq v_{j}\}\geq\frac{m}{2}(1-\eta).

Proof.

Fix
j
. For each
u\in\{0,1\}^{m-1}
, let
P_{u,0}
and
P_{u,1}
denote the two laws obtained by
freezing all coordinates except
j
.
For testing these two simple hypotheses, every decision rule
\psi
has maximal error probability at least

\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{u,0},P_{u,1})\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-\eta).

Now take
\psi=\widehat{v}_{j}
, average over the
2^{m-1}
choices of
u
, and then sum over

j=1,\dots,m
.
This gives (
8.3
).
∎

Theorem 8.4

(Assouad lower bound for arbitrary estimators)
.

Assume
0<\chi_{\ast}(W)<\infty
.
Fix
0<\delta\leq 1/(4(d-1))
, and for
v=(v_{2},\dots,v_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}
define

\theta^{v}_{1}:=1-\sum_{j=2}^{d}\delta(1+v_{j}),\qquad\theta^{v}_{j}:=\delta(1+v_{j}),\quad j=2,\dots,d.

Then every estimator
\widehat{\theta}
based on the shuffled histogram satisfies

\sup_{\theta\in\Delta_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{d-1}{8}\,\delta^{2}\Bigl(1-\delta\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}\Bigr).

Consequently, whenever
n\chi_{\ast}(W)\geq 4(d-1)^{2}
,

\inf_{\widehat{\theta}}\sup_{\theta\in\Delta_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{d-1}{64\,n\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

Proof.

For neighboring hypercube vertices
v
and
v^{(j)}
differing only in coordinate
j
,

\theta^{v^{(j)}}-\theta^{v}=\delta(e_{j}-e_{1}).

Let
q_{v}:=q_{\theta^{v}}
.
Because
\delta\leq 1/(4(d-1))
, we have

\theta^{v}_{1}=1-\delta\sum_{j=2}^{d}(1+v_{j})\geq 1-2(d-1)\delta\geq\frac{1}{2},

hence
q_{v}(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}W(y\mid 1)
for all
y\in\mathcal{Y}
.
Therefore

\chi^{2}(q_{v^{(j)}}\|q_{v})=\delta^{2}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\frac{\bigl(W(y\mid j)-W(y\mid 1)\bigr)^{2}}{q_{v}(y)}\leq 2\delta^{2}\chi_{\ast}(W).

Since
\mathrm{KL}\leq\chi^{2}
and
\mathrm{TV}^{2}\leq\mathrm{KL}/2
,

\mathrm{TV}\!\bigl(q_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n},q_{v}^{\otimes n}\bigr)\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\,\mathrm{KL}(q_{v^{(j)}}\|q_{v})}\leq\delta\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

Let
H_{v}
denote the released histogram law under
\theta^{v}
.
The histogram is a deterministic function of
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
, so by data processing

\mathrm{TV}(H_{v^{(j)}},H_{v})\leq\mathrm{TV}\!\bigl(q_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n},q_{v}^{\otimes n}\bigr)\leq\delta\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}.

For an arbitrary estimator
\widehat{\theta}
, define the coordinate decoder

\widehat{v}_{j}:=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\widehat{\theta}_{j}\geq\frac{3}{2}\delta\right\},\qquad j=2,\dots,d.

If
\widehat{v}_{j}\neq v_{j}
, then
|\widehat{\theta}_{j}-\theta^{v}_{j}|\geq\delta/2
, hence

\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta^{v}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\mathbf{1}\{\widehat{v}_{j}\neq v_{j}\}.

Applying Lemma
8.3
with

m=d-1
and

\eta=\delta\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}

gives

\sup_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\mathbf{1}\{\widehat{v}_{j}\neq v_{j}\}\geq\frac{d-1}{2}\Bigl(1-\delta\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)}\Bigr).

Combining this with (
8
) proves (
8.4
).
Finally, if
n\chi_{\ast}(W)\geq 4(d-1)^{2}
, the choice

\delta=(2\sqrt{n\chi_{\ast}(W)})^{-1}

satisfies

\delta\leq 1/(4(d-1))
,
and substitution yields (
8.4
).
∎

9.
Symmetrization and reduction to equivariant channels

Let
W:[d]\to\Delta(\mathcal{Y})
be an arbitrary channel with finite output alphabet.
Throughout this section inverses are taken on
T_{d}
, under the positive-definiteness assumption
introduced in Section
8
.
For
\pi\in S_{d}
, define the relabeled channel

W^{\pi}(y\mid x):=W(y\mid\pi^{-1}x),

and the randomized symmetrization

\overline{W}(\pi,y\mid x):=\frac{1}{d!}\,W^{\pi}(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{d!}\,W(y\mid\pi^{-1}x),\qquad(\pi,y)\in S_{d}\times\mathcal{Y}.

Theorem 9.1

(Symmetrization)
.

For every channel
W
with
\chi_{\ast}(W)<\infty
and every pair
a\neq b
,

\chi^{2}\!\bigl(\overline{W}(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,\overline{W}(\cdot\mid a)\bigr)=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid j)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid i)\bigr)\leq\chi_{\ast}(W).

If in addition
I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)
is positive definite on
T_{d}
,
then at the uniform point
\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}=(1/d,\dots,1/d)
,

I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(\overline{W})=\frac{\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)}{d-1}\,P_{T},

and therefore

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(\overline{W})^{-1}\leq\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)^{-1}.

Hence, for the uniform-point Fisher criterion under a budget upper bound

\chi_{\ast}(W)\leq C
, one may restrict without loss of generality to
permutation-equivariant channels: the symmetrized channel
\overline{W}
is still feasible
(by (
9.1
)) and has no larger risk (by (
9.1
)).

Proof.

For the privacy statement,

1+\chi^{2}\!\bigl(\overline{W}(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,\overline{W}(\cdot\mid a)\bigr)=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\Bigl(1+\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid\pi^{-1}b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid\pi^{-1}a)\bigr)\Bigr).

For fixed
a\neq b
, the ordered pair
(\pi^{-1}a,\pi^{-1}b)
is uniformly distributed over all
ordered pairs
(i,j)
with
i\neq j
, which gives (
9.1
).

For the Fisher statement, let
P_{\pi}
denote the permutation matrix of
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
At the uniform point,

I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W^{\pi})=P_{\pi}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)P_{\pi}^{\top},

hence

I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(\overline{W})=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}P_{\pi}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)P_{\pi}^{\top}.

The operator on the right commutes with every permutation, so on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
it has the form
aI+bJ
.
Restricted to
T_{d}
, it is therefore
aI
. Taking traces on
T_{d}
in (
9
)
shows

a=\frac{\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)}{d-1},

which is exactly (
9.1
). Finally,

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(\overline{W})^{-1}=\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)}\leq\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)^{-1}

by the arithmetic–harmonic mean inequality on the eigenvalues of

I_{\theta_{\mathrm{unif}}}(W)\big|_{T_{d}}
.
∎

10.
GRR is not universally optimal. The thinning principle

We now return to the fixed-composition model.
Thus
\theta\in\Delta_{d}\cap n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is deterministic, exactly
n\theta_{x}
users hold input
x
,
and the released statistic is the shuffled histogram.
Throughout this section we assume
d\geq 3
.
The case
d=2
reduces to binary randomized response, and the low-budget branch below becomes vacuous because

C_{\ast}(2)=0
.
The trivial case
C=0
corresponds to the all-null channel and carries no estimable signal.
If one additionally fixes a local-privacy cap
\varepsilon_{0}
with
\lambda_{0}=e^{\varepsilon_{0}}
, then
Theorem
4.2
implies the ambient bound

0\leq C\leq C_{\max}(\lambda_{0}):=\frac{(\lambda_{0}-1)^{2}}{\lambda_{0}},

but the analysis below itself is purely budget-parametric.
For the symmetric families below we analyze the exact affine-projected unbiased inverse estimators.

Definition 10.1

(Augmented GRR)
.

Fix
0\leq p\leq 1
and
\lambda>1
.
The augmented GRR channel is the kernel

W^{\mathrm{aug}}_{d,p,\lambda}:[d]\to\Delta([d]\cup\{z\})

given by

\displaystyle W^{\mathrm{aug}}_{d,p,\lambda}(y\mid x)

\displaystyle=p\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+d-1}\,\mathbf{1}\{y=x\}+\frac{1}{\lambda+d-1}\,\mathbf{1}\{y\neq x,\ y\in[d]\}\right)

\displaystyle+(1-p)\,\mathbf{1}\{y=z\}.

Write

\beta_{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\lambda+d-1},\qquad\eta_{\lambda}:=\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda+d-1},\qquad C_{\lambda}:=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(\lambda+d-1)}.

Thus
C_{\lambda}
is the canonical pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence of ordinary GRR from
Proposition
7.1
.

Theorem 10.2

(GRR mixtures and null refinements)
.

Fix
m\geq 1
. Let

\mathcal{Y}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}[d]_{i}\sqcup\mathcal{Z},

where
\mathcal{Z}
is an arbitrary finite set of null symbols.
For
i=1,\dots,m
, choose
p_{i}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{i}>1
, and define

W((i,y)\mid x)=p_{i}\left(\beta_{\lambda_{i}}+\eta_{\lambda_{i}}\,\mathbf{1}\{y=x\}\right),\qquad y\in[d]_{i},

while
W(z\mid x)=r_{z}
for
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, with
\sum_{i}p_{i}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}r_{z}=1
.

Then the following hold.

(i)

For every pair
a\neq b
,

\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid a)\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{\lambda_{i}}.

(ii)

Assume
S>0
(equivalently, at least one block has
p_{i}>0
).
Let
N^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
be the block histogram on
[d]_{i}
, let

M_{i}:=\sum_{y=1}^{d}N_{y}^{(i)},\qquad S:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2},

and define the affine-projected inverse estimator

\widetilde{\theta}:=\frac{\mathbf{1}}{d}+\frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}\left(\frac{N^{(i)}}{n}-\frac{M_{i}}{nd}\,\mathbf{1}\right).

Then
\widetilde{\theta}
is unbiased,
\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\in T_{d}
, and its exact
fixed-composition risk is constant in
\theta
:

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{S}-1\right).

(iii)

Among all channels of this form with prescribed canonical budget

\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}C_{\lambda_{i}}=C,

the maximal signal coefficient
S
is

S_{\mathrm{opt}}(C)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{C}{d+2\sqrt{d-1}},&0\leq C\leq C_{\ast}(d),\\[5.16663pt]
\displaystyle\eta_{\lambda(C)}^{2},&C\geq C_{\ast}(d),\end{cases}

where

\lambda(C)\ \text{ is the unique solution of }\ C_{\lambda(C)}=C,\qquad C_{\ast}(d):=C_{\sqrt{d-1}}.

Consequently the optimal fixed-composition risk within this class is

R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(C)=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{S_{\mathrm{opt}}(C)}-1\right).

For
0\leq C\leq C_{\ast}(d)
it is attained by one-level augmented GRR with

\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1},\qquad p=\frac{C}{C_{\ast}(d)}.

For
C\geq C_{\ast}(d)
it is attained by the calibrated ordinary GRR channel with
p=1
and

\lambda=\lambda(C)
.

Proof.

For (
(i)
), fix
a\neq b
.
In block
i
, the only nontrivial likelihood-ratio values are

\lambda_{i}^{-1}\ \text{ on }(i,a),\qquad\lambda_{i}\ \text{ on }(i,b),\qquad 1\ \text{ elsewhere},

while every null symbol has ratio
1
. Summing the blockwise GRR contributions gives
(
(i)
).

For (
(ii)
), fix
u\in T_{d}
, and write

\varphi_{u}((i,y)):=\frac{\eta_{\lambda_{i}}}{S}\,u_{y},\qquad\varphi_{u}(z):=0,\quad z\in\mathcal{Z}.

Because
\sum_{y=1}^{d}u_{y}=0
,

\langle u,\widetilde{\theta}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varphi_{u}(Y_{t}).

For an input
x
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\varphi_{u}(Y)]=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}\Bigl(\beta_{\lambda_{i}}\sum_{y=1}^{d}u_{y}+\eta_{\lambda_{i}}u_{x}\Bigr)=u_{x}

because
S=\sum_{i}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}
.
Similarly,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\varphi_{u}(Y)^{2}]=\frac{1}{S^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\Bigl(\beta_{\lambda_{i}}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\eta_{\lambda_{i}}u_{x}^{2}\Bigr).

Hence

\Var_{x}(\varphi_{u}(Y))=\frac{1}{S^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\Bigl(\beta_{\lambda_{i}}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\eta_{\lambda_{i}}u_{x}^{2}\Bigr)-u_{x}^{2}.

Averaging over the fixed composition
\theta
and writing

\Gamma_{\theta}:=\Cov_{\theta}(\widetilde{\theta})
,
we obtain

u^{\top}\Gamma_{\theta}u=\frac{1}{n}\left[\frac{\sum_{i}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\beta_{\lambda_{i}}}{S^{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sum_{i}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{3}}{S^{2}}-1\right)\sum_{x=1}^{d}\theta_{x}u_{x}^{2}\right].

Taking traces on
T_{d}
and using

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}(I)=d-1,\qquad\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}\!\bigl(P_{T}\diag(\theta)P_{T}\bigr)=\frac{d-1}{d},\qquad\beta_{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{\eta_{\lambda_{i}}}{d}=\frac{1}{d},

gives

\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}\Gamma_{\theta}=\frac{d-1}{n}\left[\frac{1}{S^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\eta_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}\left(\beta_{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{\eta_{\lambda_{i}}}{d}\right)-\frac{1}{d}\right]=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{S}-1\right).

Since
\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\in T_{d}
, the total squared error equals its
T_{d}
-trace:

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{tr}_{T_{d}}\Gamma_{\theta},

which is exactly (
(ii)
).

It remains to optimize
S
at fixed budget
C
.
Define

s(\lambda):=\eta_{\lambda}^{2}=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{(\lambda+d-1)^{2}},\qquad c(\lambda):=C_{\lambda}=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(\lambda+d-1)}.

Since

c^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{(\lambda-1)\bigl(2d\lambda^{2}+d\lambda+d+\lambda^{3}-\lambda^{2}+\lambda-1\bigr)}{\lambda^{2}(\lambda+d-1)^{2}}>0,

the equation
c(\lambda)=C
has a unique solution
\lambda(C)
for every
C>0
.

Every channel in the theorem yields a convex combination

(C,S)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\bigl(c(\lambda_{i}),s(\lambda_{i})\bigr)+(1-\sum_{i}p_{i})(0,0).

Hence the optimal frontier is the upper concave envelope of the planar curve

\lambda\mapsto(c(\lambda),s(\lambda))
together with the origin.

First,

\frac{s(\lambda)}{c(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda+d-1)}.

Its derivative is

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{s(\lambda)}{c(\lambda)}=\frac{d-1-\lambda^{2}}{(\lambda+1)^{2}(\lambda+d-1)^{2}},

so the slope from the origin is maximized uniquely at

\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1}
.
Thus the line from the origin tangent to the curve meets it at

\bigl(c(\lambda_{\ast}),s(\lambda_{\ast})\bigr)=\left(C_{\ast}(d),\frac{C_{\ast}(d)}{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}\right).

Second,

\frac{ds}{dc}=\frac{2d\lambda^{2}}{(\lambda+d-1)\bigl(2d\lambda^{2}+d\lambda+d+\lambda^{3}-\lambda^{2}+\lambda-1\bigr)},

and therefore

\frac{d^{2}s}{dc^{2}}=\frac{2d\lambda^{3}P_{d}(\lambda)}{(\lambda-1)\bigl(2d\lambda^{2}+d\lambda+d+\lambda^{3}-\lambda^{2}+\lambda-1\bigr)^{3}},

where

P_{d}(\lambda)=d^{2}\lambda+2d^{2}-3d\lambda^{3}+d\lambda-4d-2\lambda^{4}+2\lambda^{3}-2\lambda+2.

For
\lambda\geq\sqrt{d-1}
,

P_{d}^{\prime}(\lambda)=d^{2}+d-2-(9d-6)\lambda^{2}-8\lambda^{3}\leq-8(d-1)^{2}-8(d-1)^{3/2}<0,

and

P_{d}(\sqrt{d-1})=2(d-1)^{3/2}(2-d)\leq 0.

Hence
P_{d}(\lambda)\leq 0
for every
\lambda\geq\sqrt{d-1}
, so

\frac{d^{2}s}{dc^{2}}\leq 0\qquad\text{for }\lambda\geq\sqrt{d-1}.

Thus the upper concave envelope consists exactly of the tangent line from the origin up to

C_{\ast}(d)
, followed by the original curve for
C\geq C_{\ast}(d)
.
This proves (
(iii)
), and (
(iii)
) follows from (
(ii)
).
The descriptions of the extremizers are immediate from the two pieces of the concave envelope.
∎

Corollary 10.3

(Augmented GRR beats calibrated GRR at low budget)
.

Let
0<C<C_{\ast}(d)
, and let
\lambda(C)
be the unique solution of
C_{\lambda(C)}=C
.
Then the optimal projected inverse-estimator risk is

R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(C)=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}{C}-1\right),

whereas calibrated GRR has projected risk

R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{GRR}}(C)=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{d+\lambda(C)+(d-1)/\lambda(C)}{C}-1\right).

Hence

\frac{R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(C)}{R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{GRR}}(C)}=\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}-C}{d+\lambda(C)+(d-1)/\lambda(C)-C}<1.

11.
Low-budget optimality among all permutation-equivariant channels

We now allow an arbitrary finite output alphabet
\mathcal{Y}
equipped with an action of
S_{d}
,
and we assume throughout this section that

W(\pi y\mid\pi x)=W(y\mid x)\qquad\text{for every }\pi\in S_{d},\ x\in[d],\ y\in\mathcal{Y}.

Write the orbit decomposition as

\mathcal{Y}=\bigsqcup_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}
,
and let

\mu(y):=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x=1}^{d}W(y\mid x)

be the row-average output law.
By equivariance,
\mu
is
S_{d}
-invariant, hence constant on each orbit.

For every orbit
\mathcal{O}
, choose a representative
y_{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{O}
,
let
p_{\mathcal{O}}:=\mu(\mathcal{O})
, and define the orbit template

a^{(\mathcal{O})}_{x}:=\frac{W(y_{\mathcal{O}}\mid x)}{\mu(y_{\mathcal{O}})},\qquad x=1,\dots,d.

If the canonical pairwise
\chi^{2}
-budget is finite, then every entry of
a^{(\mathcal{O})}
is strictly
positive, and

\sum_{x=1}^{d}a^{(\mathcal{O})}_{x}=d.

Write

t^{(\mathcal{O})}:=a^{(\mathcal{O})}-\mathbf{1}\in T_{d},\qquad A_{\mathcal{O}}:=\sum_{x=1}^{d}\frac{1}{a_{x}^{(\mathcal{O})}},\qquad B_{\mathcal{O}}:=\sum_{x=1}^{d}\bigl(a_{x}^{(\mathcal{O})}\bigr)^{2}.

Every symbol in
\mathcal{O}
carries a permutation of this template.

Proposition 11.1

(Orbit templates, projected estimator, and additive invariants)
.

For a permutation-equivariant channel
W
with finite output alphabet, define

S(W):=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}p_{\mathcal{O}}\,\frac{B_{\mathcal{O}}-d}{d(d-1)}.

Then:

(i)

For every pair
a\neq b
,

\chi^{2}\!\bigl(W(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid a)\bigr)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}p_{\mathcal{O}}\,\frac{A_{\mathcal{O}}B_{\mathcal{O}}-d^{2}}{d(d-1)}.

(ii)

Assume
S(W)>0
(equivalently, at least one orbit is informative).
Let
t(y)\in T_{d}
denote the score vector attached to
y
, obtained by permuting the relevant
template
t^{(\mathcal{O})}
on the orbit containing
y
.
Then the estimator

\widetilde{\theta}=\frac{\mathbf{1}}{d}+\frac{1}{nd\,S(W)}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}N_{y}\,t(y)

is unbiased, satisfies
\mathbf{1}^{\top}\widetilde{\theta}=1
, and has exact fixed-composition risk

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{S(W)}-1\right),

constant in
\theta
.

(iii)

If
W
is supported on a single informative orbit with template
a
(so
B>d
), then

\frac{S(W)}{\chi^{2}(W(\cdot\mid b)\,\|\,W(\cdot\mid a))}=\frac{B-d}{AB-d^{2}}.

Proof.

Fix an orbit
\mathcal{O}
, and let
m_{\mathcal{O}}:=|\mathcal{O}|
.
Since
\mu
is uniform on
\mathcal{O}
, we have

\mu(y_{\mathcal{O}})=p_{\mathcal{O}}/m_{\mathcal{O}}
.
For
y=\pi y_{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{O}
, equivariance gives

W(y\mid x)=\frac{p_{\mathcal{O}}}{m_{\mathcal{O}}}\,a^{(\mathcal{O})}_{\pi^{-1}x}=\frac{p_{\mathcal{O}}}{m_{\mathcal{O}}}\,\bigl(1+t^{(\mathcal{O})}_{\pi^{-1}x}\bigr).

Hence each row places total mass
p_{\mathcal{O}}
on
\mathcal{O}
, independently of
x
.

For (
(i)
), fix the pair
(1,2)
.
Using (
11
), the orbitwise contribution to the
\chi^{2}
-budget is

\frac{p_{\mathcal{O}}}{m_{\mathcal{O}}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{\bigl(t_{2}(y)-t_{1}(y)\bigr)^{2}}{1+t_{1}(y)}.

Averaging uniformly over the orbit is the same as averaging over all
d!
permutations of the template,
because each distinct orbit point arises the same number of times from labeled permutations.
Therefore the ordered pair of template coordinates seen at
(1,2)
is uniformly distributed over all
ordered pairs
(i,j)
with
i\neq j
, and the orbit contribution equals

p_{\mathcal{O}}\,\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{(a_{j}^{(\mathcal{O})}-a_{i}^{(\mathcal{O})})^{2}}{a_{i}^{(\mathcal{O})}}.

Now

\sum_{j\neq i}(a_{j}-a_{i})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}^{2}-2a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}+da_{i}^{2}=B-2da_{i}+da_{i}^{2}

by (
11
).
Summing over
i
gives exactly (
(i)
).

For (
(ii)
)–(
(ii)
), define

M:=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mu(y)\,t(y)t(y)^{\top}.

Because
t(\pi y)=P_{\pi}t(y)
and
\mu
is invariant,
M
commutes with every permutation matrix.
Hence

M=\alpha P_{T}

for some
\alpha\geq 0
.
Taking traces on
T_{d}
gives

\alpha=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mu(y)\|t(y)\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{\mathcal{O}}p_{\mathcal{O}}\|t^{(\mathcal{O})}\|_{2}^{2}=d\,S(W),

so

M=d\,S(W)\,P_{T}.

For an input
x
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[t(Y)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mu(y)\bigl(1+t_{x}(y)\bigr)t(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mu(y)t_{x}(y)t(y)=Me_{x}

\displaystyle=d\,S(W)\left(e_{x}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{d}\right),

where the second equality uses
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mu(y)t(y)=0
, which holds because

\mu
is
S_{d}
-invariant and
t(y)\in T_{d}
, so the sum is an
S_{d}
-invariant vector in
T_{d}

and must vanish.
Therefore
\widetilde{\theta}
in (
(ii)
) is unbiased,
and it takes values in the affine hyperplane

\{\vartheta:\mathbf{1}^{\top}\vartheta=1\}.

Next,
\|t(y)\|_{2}=\|t^{(\mathcal{O})}\|_{2}
on each orbit
\mathcal{O}
, and each row assigns total mass

p_{\mathcal{O}}
to that orbit, so for every input
x
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\|t(Y)\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}p_{\mathcal{O}}\,\|t^{(\mathcal{O})}\|_{2}^{2}=d(d-1)\,S(W).

The mean contribution of one user with input
x
is

d\,S(W)(e_{x}-\mathbf{1}/d)
,
whose squared norm is

d^{2}S(W)^{2}\left\|e_{x}-\frac{\mathbf{1}}{d}\right\|_{2}^{2}=d(d-1)\,S(W)^{2}.

Since users are independent under fixed composition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nd^{2}S(W)^{2}}\left(d(d-1)\,S(W)-d(d-1)\,S(W)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{S(W)}-1\right),

which proves (
(ii)
).
Finally, (
(iii)
) is just the quotient of (
11.1
) and (
(i)
)
for a single orbit.
∎

Theorem 11.2

(Low-budget optimality among all permutation-equivariant channels)
.

Assume
d\geq 3
and
0<C\leq C_{\ast}(d)
.
Among all permutation-equivariant channels
W:[d]\to\Delta(\mathcal{Y})
with arbitrary finite output
alphabet and canonical pairwise
\chi^{2}
-budget
C
, the minimum exact fixed-composition risk of the
projected unbiased inverse estimator is

R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{\mathrm{sym}}(C)=\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}{C}-1\right).

It is attained by augmented GRR with

\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1},\qquad p=\frac{C}{C_{\ast}(d)}.

More generally, equality holds if and only if every informative orbit is, up to a permutation of the input
labels, the singleton GRR template

a_{\ast}=\left(\frac{d\sqrt{d-1}}{\sqrt{d-1}+d-1},\frac{d}{\sqrt{d-1}+d-1},\dots,\frac{d}{\sqrt{d-1}+d-1}\right),

and the remaining mass lies on null refinements.
Equivalently, every optimizer is an augmented-GRR channel up to splitting the informative GRR symbols
and/or the null symbol into finitely many conditionally identical refinements.

Proof.

By Proposition
11.1
, minimizing the projected risk is equivalent to maximizing the
total signal coefficient
S(W)
at fixed total budget
C
.
Because both
S(W)
and the budget in (
(i)
) add over orbits, it suffices to bound the
orbitwise slope (
(iii)
).

Fix one informative orbit and abbreviate
A=A_{\mathcal{O}}
,
B=B_{\mathcal{O}}
,
a=a^{(\mathcal{O})}
.
For fixed
B
, the slope (
(iii)
) is maximized when
A=\sum_{i}1/a_{i}
is minimized subject to

a_{i}>0,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}=d,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}^{2}=B.

Since
A\to\infty
as any
a_{i}\downarrow 0
, the minimum is attained in the interior.
The KKT conditions require Lagrange multipliers
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}
with

-\frac{1}{a_{i}^{2}}+\alpha_{0}+2\beta_{0}a_{i}=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,d,

so every coordinate
a_{i}
is a positive root of the depressed cubic

2\beta_{0}x^{3}+\alpha_{0}x^{2}-1=0
.
A real cubic has at most three real roots, but the constraint
a_{i}>0

and Descartes’ rule of signs (the coefficient sequence
2\beta_{0},\alpha_{0},0,-1
has exactly one sign change
when
\beta_{0}>0
, and at most two when
\beta_{0}<0
) show that there are at most two positive roots.
(One can also reach this conclusion via Schur-convexity of
A
;
see
[
10
, Ch. 3, C.1 and Ch. 5, B.1]
.)
Hence the minimizing template has at most two distinct values.

Write these two values as
\alpha>\beta>0
, with multiplicities
s
and
d-s
, respectively.
Since
s\alpha+(d-s)\beta=d
, setting
\lambda:=\alpha/\beta>1
gives

\alpha=\frac{d\lambda}{d+s(\lambda-1)},\qquad\beta=\frac{d}{d+s(\lambda-1)}.

Such a two-level template is exactly the subset-selection template of size
s
.
A direct substitution into (
11.1
) and (
(i)
) yields

S_{s}(\lambda)=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)^{2}}{(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)^{2}},\qquad C_{s}(\lambda)=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)},

(we write
C_{s}
for
C_{d,s}
when
d
is fixed),
and therefore

\frac{S_{s}(\lambda)}{C_{s}(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)}.

For fixed
s
, differentiation gives

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{S_{s}(\lambda)}{C_{s}(\lambda)}=\frac{d-s-s\lambda^{2}}{(\lambda+1)^{2}\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)^{2}}.

If
1\leq s<d/2
, the unique critical point on
(1,\infty)
is

\lambda_{s}^{\ast}=\sqrt{(d-s)/s}>1
,
and

\max_{\lambda>1}\frac{S_{s}(\lambda)}{C_{s}(\lambda)}=\frac{1}{d+2\sqrt{s(d-s)}}.

If
s\geq d/2
, then
d-s-s\lambda^{2}\leq 0
for every
\lambda\geq 1
, so the slope in
(
11
) is non-increasing on
[1,\infty)
and its supremum is the boundary value

1/(2d)
, which is strictly smaller than
1/(d+2\sqrt{d-1})
for
d\geq 3
.
Thus every informative orbit satisfies

\frac{S_{\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\mathcal{O}}}\leq\frac{1}{d+2\sqrt{d-1}},

with equality if and only if
s=1
and
\lambda=\sqrt{d-1}
.

Summing (
11
) over the orbits and using Proposition
11.1
gives

S(W)\leq\frac{C}{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}.

Hence every permutation-equivariant channel with budget
C
obeys

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\widetilde{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}{C}-1\right).

This is (
11.2
).

Finally, augmented GRR with
\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1}
and
p=C/C_{\ast}(d)
has

S(W)=p\,\eta_{\lambda_{\ast}}^{2}=\frac{C}{d+2\sqrt{d-1}},

so it attains (
11.2
).
Equality throughout forces every informative orbit to attain equality in
(
11
), hence to be the singleton template (
11.2
) up to a
permutation of the coordinates.
All remaining mass must lie on null orbits.
This is exactly the stated refinement-class characterization.
∎

Remark 11.3

(Ordered-pair orbits)
.

The ordered-pair orbit
\{(i,j):i\neq j\}
corresponds to a three-level template

a=(u,v,w,\dots,w)

with
u+v+(d-2)w=d
.
Here

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}+(d-2)w^{2}-d}{d(d-1)},

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bigl(1/u+1/v+(d-2)/w\bigr)\bigl(u^{2}+v^{2}+(d-2)w^{2}\bigr)-d^{2}}{d(d-1)}.

Thus the ordered-pair slope is exactly
S_{\mathrm{op}}/C_{\mathrm{op}}=(B-d)/(AB-d^{2})
with

A=1/u+1/v+(d-2)/w
and
B=u^{2}+v^{2}+(d-2)w^{2}
.
The proof of Theorem
11.2
shows that, at fixed
B
, the minimizing
A
must be
two-level.
Hence an ordered-pair orbit can only attain the optimal slope on the boundary
u=w
or
v=w
,
where it collapses to a refined GRR orbit.
In particular, ordered pairs never improve on augmented GRR in the low-budget regime.

12.
GRR optimal within the subset-selection family

For
1\leq s\leq d-1
and
\lambda>1
, let
SS(d,s,\lambda)
denote the subset-selection channel on

\binom{[d]}{s}
:

W_{d,s,\lambda}(S\mid x)=\frac{d}{\binom{d}{s}\,(\lambda s+d-s)}\Bigl(\lambda\,\mathbf{1}\{x\in S\}+\mathbf{1}\{x\notin S\}\Bigr).

Write

C_{d,s}(\lambda):=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)}{\lambda(d-1)(\lambda s+d-s)}

for the canonical pairwise
\chi^{2}
divergence, and

R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{d,s}(\lambda):=\frac{d-1}{n}\,\frac{\lambda^{2}s(s-1)+2\lambda s(d-s)+(d-s)(d-s-1)}{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)^{2}}

for the exact projected fixed-composition risk.

Formula (
12
) is immediate from a four-type count.
Fix
a\neq b
.
Subsets
S\in\binom{[d]}{s}
split into the four classes

a\in S,\ b\notin S;\qquad b\in S,\ a\notin S;\qquad a,b\in S;\qquad a,b\notin S.

The first two classes each have cardinality
\binom{d-2}{s-1}
.
Under input
a
, their likelihood-ratio values relative to input
b
are

\lambda^{-1}
and
\lambda
, respectively, while the last two classes are neutral.
Using

\frac{\binom{d-2}{s-1}}{\binom{d}{s}}=\frac{s(d-s)}{d(d-1)},

one obtains (
12
).

For the inverse estimator, let

C_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{j\in S_{i}\},\qquad j=1,\dots,d,

be the coordinate-inclusion counts.
A direct count gives

\displaystyle p_{s}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}_{x}(x\in S)=\frac{\lambda s}{d+s(\lambda-1)},

\displaystyle r_{s}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}_{x}(j\in S\mid j\neq x)=\frac{s\bigl(\lambda(s-1)+d-s\bigr)}{(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\frac{C_{j}}{n}\right]=r_{s}+(p_{s}-r_{s})\theta_{j},\qquad p_{s}-r_{s}=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)}{(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)}.

Therefore

\widehat{\theta}_{j}:=\frac{C_{j}/n-r_{s}}{p_{s}-r_{s}},\qquad j=1,\dots,d,

is unbiased, and it already satisfies
\sum_{j}\widehat{\theta}_{j}=1
because
\sum_{j}C_{j}=ns
.

Equivalently,
SS(d,s,\lambda)
is the two-level orbit template from
Section
11
with

\alpha=\frac{d\lambda}{d+s(\lambda-1)},\qquad\beta=\frac{d}{d+s(\lambda-1)},

where
\alpha
appears on
s
coordinates and
\beta
on the remaining
d-s
coordinates.
Substituting this template into Proposition
11.1
yields
(
12
), and the estimator (
12
) is exactly the projected orbit estimator
(
(ii)
) written in inclusion-count coordinates.

Theorem 12.1

(GRR is the unique optimizer in the subset-selection family)
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
C>0
.
For each
s\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
, let
\lambda_{s}(C)>1
be the unique solution of

C_{d,s}\bigl(\lambda_{s}(C)\bigr)=C.

Define the matched-budget risk

\mathfrak{R}_{d,s}(C):=R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{d,s}\bigl(\lambda_{s}(C)\bigr).

Then

\mathfrak{R}_{d,1}(C)<\mathfrak{R}_{d,2}(C)<\cdots<\mathfrak{R}_{d,d-1}(C).

Equivalently, GRR (
s=1

Proof.

If
d=2
, then
s=1
is the only admissible subset size, so the claim is immediate.
Assume henceforth that
d\geq 3

Extend
C_{d,s}(\lambda)
and
R^{\mathrm{fc}}_{d,s}(\lambda)
to real

by the same formulas.
For fixed

\partial_{\lambda}C_{d,s}(\lambda)=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)\bigl(2d\lambda^{2}+d\lambda+d+\lambda^{3}s-\lambda^{2}s+\lambda s-s\bigr)}{\lambda^{2}(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)^{2}}>0.

Hence, for every fixed
C>0

\lambda_{s}(C)>1

C^{1}
on
(1,d-1)

Since monotonicity on the real interval

it suffices to differentiate the real-valued function

Using (

\mathfrak{R}_{d,s}(C)=\frac{d-1}{ndC}\left(\frac{(\lambda_{s}(C)+1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda_{s}(C)-1)\bigr)}{\lambda_{s}(C)}-C\right).

Write
\lambda=\lambda_{s}(C)
for brevity.
Since
C_{d,s}(\lambda)=C

\frac{d\lambda}{ds}=-\frac{\partial_{s}C_{d,s}(\lambda)}{\partial_{\lambda}C_{d,s}(\lambda)}=-\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)\bigl(d^{2}-2ds-s^{2}(\lambda-1)\bigr)}{s(d-s)\bigl(2d\lambda^{2}+d\lambda+d+\lambda^{3}s-\lambda^{2}s+\lambda s-s\bigr)}.

Also,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\!\left(\frac{(\lambda+1)(d+s(\lambda-1))}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{\lambda},

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\!\left(\frac{(\lambda+1)(d+s(\lambda-1))}{\lambda}\right)

Remark 12.2

(Thinned subset selection)
.

If one allows an additional null symbol and puts only a fraction
p
of the mass on

SS(d,s,\lambda)
, then Proposition
11.1
shows that the exact
fixed-composition risk of the affine-projected inverse estimator has the form

\frac{d-1}{nd}\left(\frac{1}{p\kappa_{d,s}(\lambda)}-1\right),\qquad\kappa_{d,s}(\lambda)=\frac{s(d-s)(\lambda-1)^{2}}{(d-1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)^{2}},

while the canonical budget becomes
p\,C_{d,s}(\lambda)
.
The ratio

\frac{\kappa_{d,s}(\lambda)}{C_{d,s}(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+1)\bigl(d+s(\lambda-1)\bigr)}

is maximized at

and
\lambda=\sqrt{d-1}
.
Thus even after adding null mass, the subset-selection family never beats the low-budget
augmented-GRR frontier of Sections
10
and
11
.

Discussion

The results above separate four layers.

First, Theorem
8.1
and Theorem
8.4
show that the worst pairwise
canonical quantity

\chi_{\ast}(W)

already forces a universal estimation lower bound of order

(d-1)/(n\chi_{\ast}(W))
.
Thus
\chi_{\ast}
is not only the canonical privacy invariant of the present paper; it is also a universal
statistical obstruction.

Second, Theorem
9.1
shows that, for the uniform-point Fisher criterion, asymmetry never
helps. One may average over
S_{d}
, decrease the pairwise
\chi^{2}
budget, and equalize the Fisher
eigenvalues.

Third, Theorem
10.2
identifies the exact optimal frontier inside the natural class of GRR
blocks and null refinements. The optimal projected inverse-estimator risk is attained by the thinned GRR
mechanism with

\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1}

up to the threshold
C_{\ast}(d)
, followed by calibrated GRR.
Theorem
12.1
shows that within the subset-selection family GRR is again the unique
matched-budget optimizer.

Fourth, Theorem
11.2
resolves the full permutation-equivariant problem in the
low-budget regime
0<C\leq C_{\ast}(d)
.
Every permutation-equivariant channel decomposes into orbit templates.
For the projected unbiased inverse-estimator risk, the signal coefficient and the canonical budget are
additive over orbits, and the orbitwise signal-to-budget slope is maximized only by the singleton GRR
template with
\lambda_{\ast}=\sqrt{d-1}
.
Thus low-budget optimality is not merely a feature of the GRR-block class: it is the exact symmetric
optimum, up to conditionally identical refinements of the informative or null symbols.

What remains open is the global symmetric frontier for
C>C_{\ast}(d)
.
The low-budget argument is linear in the orbitwise slope and therefore closes the problem only up to the
first tangency point of the GRR curve.
Beyond that threshold, ordered-pair orbits and higher orbit types may still contribute to the true upper
concave envelope, and a complete classification of the high-budget permutation-equivariant frontier remains
to be done.

Several further directions are immediate.
One can ask for sharp nonasymptotic minimax constants beyond the projected unbiased class, for exact
finite-
n
comparisons between the privacy curve and the estimation frontier, and for analogous orbit
classifications under additional structural constraints such as bounded message length or sparse outputs.
More broadly, the mechanism-design problem in the shuffle model appears to be governed by a finite-dimensional
orbit geometry rather than by the local-DP extremizers themselves.
