Differential graded categories in holomorphic symplectic geometry
Borislav Mladenov

Abstract
Let ( X , σ ) (\mathrm{X},\sigma) be a holomorphic symplectic manifold. We study the differential graded category of canonical Lagrangian D \mathrm{D} -branes 𝒟 Lag ​ ( X , σ ) \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma) along with its deformation quantisation, spanned by quantised orientations, 𝒟 ​ 𝒬 ​ ( X , σ ) \mathcal{DQ}(\mathrm{X},\sigma) , and the virtual de Rham category 𝒟 ​ ℛ vir ​ ( X , σ ) \mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma) . We prove the formality of these dg categories when localised at a countable collection of orientable compact Kähler Lagrangian submanifolds with pairwise clean intersections. Along the way, we define Kaledin classes of minimal A ∞ \mathrm{A}_{\infty} -categories and show that they are the obstructions to formality. In addition, we obtain a formality criterion for flat weakly proper Calabi-Yau dg categories.

Introduction

Let
(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold. The main goal of this paper is the study of various differential graded categories associated to
\mathrm{X}
and its Lagrangian submanifolds.

Lagrangian
\mathrm{D}
-branes

Let
\mathrm{L}\subset\mathrm{X}
be a compact Kähler Lagrangian submanifold equipped with a choice of a square root
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}
of its canonical bundle. Recall that there is a spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}_{2}=\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{L}/\mathbf{C})\Rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2},\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}\right).

It was shown in
[
34
]
that it collapses on the second page and moreover that the differential graded algebra

\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2},\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}\right)

is formal and quasi-isomorphic to the de Rham algebra of
\mathrm{L}
.

These results were motivated by mirror symmetry and the latter suggests that we should be working with categories rather than single objects. In the present article, we shall upgrade the above to the level of differential graded categories.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
be the full differential graded subcategory of
{\mathrm{\bf{D}}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{X})
spanned by square-roots of the canonical bundles of Lagrangian submanifolds in
\mathrm{X}
. Thus:

•

Objects in
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
are choices of square-roots
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}
where
\mathrm{L}
is a Lagrangian submanifold in
\mathrm{X}
;

•

For a pair of Lagrangian submanifolds
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
and two objects associated with these Lagrangians
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}
and
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1/2}
, the morphism spaces are given by the complexes

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2},\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1/2}\right)=\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2},\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1/2}\right).

We introduce a local version of this category. Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a collection of orientable Lagrangian submanifolds in
\mathrm{X}
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathfrak{L})
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
spanned by objects supported in
\mathfrak{L}
. Our first result goes as follows:

Theorem 1.1.1

(
Theorem

5.4.9
)
.

Let
(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold and let
\mathfrak{L}

We call an

The virtual de Rham category

In the case of a single Lagrangian submanifold, we have seen that de Rham cohomology and, somewhat more precisely, the spectral sequence which starts with de Rham cohomology and converges to the cohomology of
\mathrm{RHom}
, play a key role relating the coherent complex (counting massless states of open strings) and the deformation quantisation complex. Following this philosophy, we shall define a virtual de Rham differential graded category playing the role of de Rham cohomology in the case of categories.

Let
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
be two Lagrangian submanifolds and suppose given two smooth half-twisted
\mathrm{D}
-modules
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{M}
on
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
, respectively. Following Behrend and Fantechi, we define the virtual de Rham complex

\mathscr{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{M})\coloneq\left(\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-1.5pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{\bullet}_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{M}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{BF}}\right).

In
Section

4.1
, we introduce the Malgrange-Serre functor
\mathrm{MS}
which provides soft resolutions of coherent sheaves on a complex manifold. These are particularly well-suited for our purposes as they are functorial and multiplicative when the underlying coherent sheaves are equipped with bilinear pairings.

For a contact manifold
\mathrm{Y}
and a Legendrian
\Lambda
, the microlocalisation functor

\mu_{\Lambda}:\mathrm{Mod}_{\Lambda,\mathrm{rh}}\left(\widehat{\mathrm{E}}_{\mathrm{Y}}\right)\to\gamma_{*}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{rh}}\left(\mathrm{D}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}^{\sqrt{v}}\right)

allows us in
Section

4.3
to extend this construction to regular holonomic
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}
-modules admitting lifts along
\mu_{\Lambda}
to
\widehat{\mathrm{E}}_{\mathrm{Y}}
-modules for a (local) contactification
\mathrm{Y}\to\mathrm{X}
. We note that in general the forgetful functor induced by the inclusion
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}\xhookrightarrow{}\rho_{*}\widehat{\mathrm{E}}_{\mathrm{Y}}
is only locally essentially surjective.

Putting it all together, we obtain a differential graded category

\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)

whose objects are quantised orientation modules and, for two such modules
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{M}}
, the morphism complex is

\Gamma\left(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{MS}\left(\mathscr{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{M}}\right)\right)\right).

We then have a formality result for the local version along a collection
\mathfrak{L}
which is a dg version of the Kähler formality of
[
11
]
:

Theorem 1.3.1

(
Theorem

5.4.2
)
.

Let
(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold and let
\mathfrak{L}
be a (countable) collection of orientable compact Kähler Lagrangian submanifolds with clean pairwise intersections. Then the differential graded category
\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
is formal.

In fact,
Theorem

1.2.1
is a corollary of
Theorem

1.3.1
since, for Solomon-Verbitsky collections, we have:

Proposition 1.3.2

(
Proposition

5.4.4
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Solomon-Verbitsky collection. There is a quasi-isomorphism

\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)/\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathrm{X},\sigma)\right).

In general, the virtual de Rham complexes are closely related to the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles and we conjecture that there is a simply-graded spectral sequence whose first page is the de Rham complex which converges to the cohomology of the (shifted) perverse sheaf.

Formality criteria for differential graded categories

Proving
Theorem

1.1.1
requires a generalisation of the results of Lunts on formality of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
and differential graded algebras over general rings to
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
and differential graded categories.

For a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
over a
\mathbf{Q}
-algebra
\mathrm{R}
, we first extend the construction of Lunts’ Kaledin class
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
to categories and prove:

Theorem 1.4.1

(
Theorem

2.10.6
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category over
\mathrm{R}
and assume
\mathrm{R}
is a
\mathbf{Q}
-algebra. The following are equivalent:

1.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is formal.

2.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formal for all
n\geq 1
.

3.

For all
n\geq 3
, the truncated Kaledin class
\mathrm{k}^{\leq n}_{\mathcal{C}}
vanishes.

4.

The Kaledin class
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
vanishes.

As an easy corollary, mimicking Lunts’ case of algebras, we have:

Corollary 1.4.2

(
Corollary

2.10.8
)
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{C},m)
is a finite minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category which is flat and proper over an integral domain
\mathrm{R}
with generic point
\eta
. Assume the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is torsion-free. Then
\mathcal{C}
is formal if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
is formal.

There is a differential graded version of this, just as in the algebra case, see
Corollary

2.10.9
. The difficulty of working with categories, rather than algebras, is that the Kaledin class is never living in the compactly supported cohomology if the category is not finite, hence we lose the nice base-change properties of the compactly supported Hochschild cohomology. We can still salvage the following which is good enough for our applications:

Theorem 1.4.3

(
Theorem

2.10.10
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a flat weakly proper Calabi-Yau differential graded category over an intergral domain
\mathrm{R}
with generic point
\eta
. Assume the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is projective, where
m_{2}
is the composition in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is formal if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}=\mathrm{Ind}_{k(\eta)/\mathrm{R}}(\mathcal{C})
is formal.

Context

1.5.1
Holomorphic Floer theory

There has been a duality philosophy for some time now in the framework of holomorphic symplectic manifolds. The papers
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
are first works on the subject.
The paper
[
21
]
puts forward a conjecture that the Fukaya category of a hyperkähler variety
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I,J,K})
with symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{J}}
should be quasi-equivalent to a non-commutative deformation of the derived category on the holomorphic symplectic manifold
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I},\sigma_{\mathrm{I}}=\omega_{\mathrm{J}}+i\omega_{\mathrm{K}})
. In fact, his calculations show that this should be a formal deformation in the non-commutative Poisson bivector direction.

More generally, in the subsequent paper
[
22
]
, he upgrades the above to a duality between
\mathrm{A}
and
\mathrm{B}
branes on
(\mathrm{X},\omega_{\mathrm{J}})
and
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I},\sigma_{\mathrm{I}}=\omega_{\mathrm{J}}+i\omega_{\mathrm{K}})
, respectively. The following conjecture is a precise formulation of the above discussion.

Conjecture 1.5.1
.

Let
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I,J,K},g)
be a hyperkähler manifold. There is a quasi-equivalence

\mathcal{DF}(\mathrm{X},\omega_{\mathrm{J}})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Nov}/\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dg,h}}(\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}})\right),

between the Fukaya category
\mathcal{DF}(\mathrm{X},\omega_{\mathrm{J}})
and the differential graded category of holonomic
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}
-modules associated to the holomorphic symplectic manifold
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I},\sigma_{\mathrm{I}}=\omega_{\mathrm{J}}+i\omega_{\mathrm{K}})
.

More recently, Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
25
]
have introduced a generalised Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for a holomorphic symplectic manifold
(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
. Their global conjecture is an equivalence, after extension of scalars, between the
\mathrm{A}
-side, also called Betti, which is a global Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{glob}}
defined over the limit of rings analytic functions on punctured unit discs, and the
\mathrm{B}
-side, also known as de Rham, which is the category of global holonomic deformation quantisation modules
\mathcal{H}ol_{\mathrm{glob}}
. Their theory includes Lagrangian subvarieties and the theory of quantum wave functions is supposed to produce deformation quantisation modules supported of these singular Lagrangian subvarieties. Kontsevich and Soibelman have local versions of this correspondence which is associated with a neighbourhood of a fixed Lagrangian and seems better understood.

1.5.2
Symplectic geometry and Solomon-Verbitsky

In
[
40
]
Solomon and Verbitsky consider the (local) Fukaya category
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
of a collection of
\mathrm{I}
-holomorphic graded spin Lagrangians
\mathfrak{L}
in a hyperkähler variety
(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{I,J,K},g)
, equipped with the symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{J}}=g(\mathrm{J}\cdot,\cdot)
. Their main result shows that, generically, finite energy holomorphic curves bounding
\mathrm{I}
-holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds must be constant.

It follows that for two such Lagrangian
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
which intersect cleanly, the Floer coboundary operator
\mu_{1}(\mathrm{L,M})
of
\mathrm{CF}(\mathrm{L,M})
coincides with the de Rham differential, hence the spectral sequence

\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathrm{L}/\mathbf{C})\otimes\mathrm{Nov}\Rightarrow\mathrm{HF}^{*}(\mathrm{L,L})

collapses on the second page. In addition, the Floer composition
\mu_{2}(\mathrm{L})
of
\mathrm{CF}(\mathrm{L,L})
is the wedge product of differential forms up to sign and
\mu_{k}(\mathrm{L,L})=0
for
k\geq 3
. This implies, as in
[
11
]
, that the Floer
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathrm{CF}(\mathrm{L},\mathrm{L})
is formal. The de Rham versions of these results were obtained in
[
34
]
.

These results motivate them to state the following conjecture attributed to Ivan Smith:

Conjecture 1.5.2
.

For a collection of compact spin
\mathrm{I}
-holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds with clean pairwise intersections, the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is a formal.

Then our
Theorem

1.2.1
is the de Rham analogue of this conjecture under the generalised Riemann-Hilbert correspondence of Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
25
]
.

The category
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{L}}
is known to be (intrinsically) formal by
[
1
]
for the Slodowy slice to a nilpotent matrix with two equal Jordan blocks and the (finite) Seidel-Smith collection of distinguished Lagrangian submanifolds.

In relation to the work of Solomon and Verbitsky, we introduce also a local version of
1.5.1
which should be much more accessible.

Note that, as explained above, given a Solomon-Verbitsky collection
\mathfrak{L}
, the full differential graded subcategory
\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{s}}
of
\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}
, spanned by a choice of quantised orientation for each orientable Lagrangian submanifold, equipped with orientation data, in
\mathfrak{L}
, is independent of these choices as the morphism complexes are invariant upon chaining (quantised) orientations. Since the Solomon-Verbitsky category
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
has a single object associated to each Lagrangian in
\mathfrak{L}
, we should expect in line with
Corollary

1.2.2
and

Conjecture 1.5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Solomon-Verbitsky collection of Lagrangian submanifolds in

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Nov}/\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{s}}\right).

Graded categories

Fix a ring
\mathrm{R}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathrm{R})
the category of
\mathbf{Z}
-graded
\mathrm{R}
-modules whose objects are modules
\mathrm{M}
over
\mathrm{R}
equipped with a decomposition

\mathrm{M}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{M}_{n}.

An
\mathrm{R}
-linear map
f:\mathrm{M}\to\mathrm{N}
is said to be of degree
m
if

f(\mathrm{M}_{n})\subseteq\mathrm{N}_{n+m}.

Morphisms in the category of graded
\mathrm{R}
-modules are
\mathrm{R}
-linear maps of degree
0
, also called (co)chain maps.

A differential on a graded module
\mathrm{M}
is a degree
1
linear map
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{M}}:\mathrm{M}\to\mathrm{M}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}=0
. We call the pair
(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{M}})
a differential graded module. A morphism of differential graded modules is a degree
0
linear morphism commuting with the differential. We denote by
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{R})
the category of differential graded
\mathrm{R}
-modules.

Definition 2.1.1
.

A graded category over
\mathrm{R}
is a category
\mathcal{C}
enriched over
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathrm{R})
.

More explicitly, this means for any two objects
\mathrm{X,Y}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have a decomposition of the space of morphisms

\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})=\bigoplus_{n\in Z}\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}).

Moreover, the composition of morphisms is of degree zero.

A graded functor
\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a functor which induces a degree
0
morphism

\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}}:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{X}),\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{Y}))

for any
\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
.

For a graded category
\mathcal{C}
, the opposite graded category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
has the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
, the morphism spaces are

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathrm{X,Y})=\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Y,X})

with composition

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op},n}(\mathrm{Y,Z})\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op},m}(\mathrm{X,Y})\to\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op},n+m}(\mathrm{X,Z})

such that

g\otimes f\mapsto(-1)^{nm}f\circ g.

Differential graded categories

Definition 2.2.1
.

A differential graded category over
\mathrm{R}
is a category
\mathcal{C}
enriched over
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{R})
.

Hence the morphism spaces of a dg category
\mathcal{C}
are endowed with a structure of differential graded modules over
\mathrm{R}
, that is,

\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathrm{X,Y})

with a differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{X,Y}}
of degree
1
and the compositions

\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Y,Z})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})\to\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Z})

are morphisms of dg
\mathrm{R}
-modules.

A differential graded (dg) functor is a functor

\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}

such that

\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}}:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{X}),\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{Y}))

is a morphism of dg
\mathrm{R}
-modules, i.e. it has degree
0
and commutes with the differentials, for any
\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
. A dg functor

\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}

is quasi-isomorphism if it induces a bijection on objects

\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\cong\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{2})

and, for any two
\mathrm{X,Y}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
, a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y}}:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{X}),\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{Y})).

The opposite differential graded category of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as the opposite graded category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
with the same differential as
\mathcal{C}
.

\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
- categories

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let

-category

Example 2.3.2

1.

An
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-category
\mathcal{C}

\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra structure on the endomorphism space

2.

A graded category is an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category with

Remark 2.3.3
.

Note that an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-category needn’t be a category for its composition may not be associative and there may be no identities.

Example 2.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category. Then the first relation is

(*_{1})\quad m_{1}m_{1}=0,

i.e.
m_{1}
is a differential. The second relation is

(*_{2})\quad m_{1}m_{2}=m_{2}(m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{1}),

meaning that
m_{1}
is a derivation for the composition
m_{2}
. The third equation shows

is associative up to the homotopy

(*_{3})\quad m_{2}(m_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{2})=m_{1}m_{3}+m_{3}(m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 2}+\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 2}\otimes m_{1}).

In particular, any minimal
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-category is associative. The cohomology of an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-category is a graded associative category.

Definition 2.3.5

Example 2.3.9

Let

Cocategories

Definition 2.4.1
.

A cocategory
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathrm{R}
is given by a class of objects
\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
together with
\mathrm{R}
-linear comultiplication
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}
which is coassociative, that is,
\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\circ\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}=\left(1_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\circ\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Definition 2.4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be
\mathrm{R}
-linear cocategories. A cofunctor
\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a pair consisting of a map of objects
\mathrm{F}:\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\to\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{2})
and an
\mathrm{R}
-linear degree
0
morphism

\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathrm{FX},\mathrm{FY})

such that

\left(\mathrm{F}\otimes\mathrm{F}\right)\circ\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}=\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\circ\mathrm{F}.

The main example for us is the reduced tensor cocategory associated to a graded category (or more generally graph as we don’t need the compositions) over
\mathrm{R}
. Namely, let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{R}
-graph. Let
\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}
be the cocategory with the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and morphisms

\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\bigoplus_{\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}_{0},\cdots,\mathrm{X}_{n}=\mathrm{Y}}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{n-1},\mathrm{X}_{n})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1}).

The comultiplication is given by

\Delta:\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathrm{Z}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{Y})\otimes\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{X})

which is determined on pure tensors by

f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{i+1}\right)\otimes\left(f_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{1}\right).

In addition to the internal grading arising from
\mathcal{C}
,
\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}
has external grading by weight coming from the fact that it is cofree cocategory. The weights are given by tensor length. Hence we obtain an increasing filtration by sub cocategories

\mathrm{W}_{1}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathrm{W}_{2}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\cdots\subset\mathrm{W}_{n}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C},

where
\mathrm{W}_{n}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}
has the same objects as
\overline{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{C}
and morphisms of weight
\leq n
.

Definition 2.4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{F,G}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
be cofunctors. An
\left(\mathrm{F,G}\right)
-coderivation of degree
p
is given by a map of objects
\mathrm{D}:\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\to\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{2})
and a collection of
\mathrm{R}
-linear morphisms of degree

\mathrm{D}:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathrm{X,Y})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathrm{D}\mathrm{X},\mathrm{D}\mathrm{Y})

such that

\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\circ\mathrm{D}=\left(\mathrm{G}\otimes\mathrm{D}+\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{F}\right)\circ\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}.

We denote the graded
\mathrm{R}
-module of
(\mathrm{F,G})
-coderivations by
\mathrm{coDer}(\mathrm{F,G})
. Any increasing filtration
\mathrm{W}_{\bullet}\mathcal{C}_{1}
on
\mathcal{C}_{1}
induces a decreasing filtration
\mathrm{W}_{\bullet}\mathrm{coDer}(\mathrm{F,G})
with
\mathrm{W}_{n}\mathrm{coDer}(\mathrm{F,G})
consisting of all coderivations vanishing on
\mathrm{W}_{n-1}\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

For

\displaystyle m_{i}

Deformations of linear categories

Definition 2.6.1
.

1.

An
\mathrm{R}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
is flat if for all
\mathrm{X,Y}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, the
\mathrm{R}
-modules
\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})
are projective.

2.

An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}

\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}
is flat.

Remark 2.6.2
.

We note that
\mathcal{C}
is flat iff
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{o}p}
is flat.

Let
\varphi:\mathrm{R}\to\mathrm{S}
be a map of rings. We have an induced functor

\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}:\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{S}}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{R}}.

For an
\mathrm{S}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
, we let
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C})
be the
\mathrm{R}
-linear category with the same objects and morphisms
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C})(\mathrm{X,Y})=\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y}))
. In the opposite direction, we have

\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}\coloneqq-\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{S}:\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{R}}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{S}}

and for an
\mathrm{R}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
, we let
\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C})
be the
\mathrm{S}
-linear category with the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and

\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C})(\mathrm{X,Y})=\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{S}.

It is an easy check that
\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}
is left adjoint to
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
. Following
[
30
]
, we shall define deformations of linear categories as lifts along these functors.

Definition 2.6.3
.

Let
\varphi:\mathrm{R}\to\mathrm{S}
be a map of rings. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{S}
-linear category. An
\mathrm{R}
-linear deformation of
\mathcal{C}
is a flat
\mathrm{R}
-linear category
\mathcal{D}
equipped with an
\mathrm{R}
-linear functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C})
which induces an
\mathrm{S}
-linear equivalence
\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 2.6.4
.

In sufficiently nice circumstances, an
\mathrm{R}
-linear deformation induces an abelian deformation of the corresponding categories of modules. We refer to
[
30
]
for the definition of deformations of abelian categories and details.

Hochschild cohomology

Suppose
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are dg cocategories. That is, they are equipped with degree
1
coderivations
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\in\mathrm{coDer}(1_{\mathcal{C}_{i}})
. Then, for any two dg cofunctors
\mathrm{F,G}:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
, the space
\mathrm{coDer}(\mathrm{F,G})
is a dg
\mathrm{R}
-module with differential

\mathrm{D}\mapsto\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\circ\mathrm{D}-(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{D}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}.

Definition 2.7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category. The Hochschild cohomology complex of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\mathrm{coDer}\left(1_{\overline{\mathrm{T}}\left(\mathcal{C}[1]\right)}\right),

with differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}=[\mathrm{d}_{\overline{\mathrm{T}}\left(\mathcal{C}[1]\right)},-]
. Its cohomology is called the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{C}
and is denoted by
\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{\bullet-1}(\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C}))
.

Remark 2.7.2
.

We see immediately from the universal property of the reduced tensor cocategory that

\mathrm{CC}^{n}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}^{n}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C}\right),\mathcal{C}[1]\right)

The weight filtration on
\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
given by tensor length corresponds to the weight filtration on the space of coderivation under the above identification. Since the differential of
\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
lies in
\mathrm{W}_{1}\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
, we see that the filtration descends to cohomology that we still denote by
\mathrm{W}_{\bullet}\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
and call the (cohomological) weight filtration.

We shall later relate the second piece
\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isotopies which will be useful for applications.

Definition 2.7.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},m)
and
(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})
be two
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category structures on
\mathcal{C}
. An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isotopy between
m
and

is an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-functor
\mathrm{F}:(\mathcal{C},m)\to(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})
such that
\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})}
and for all
\mathrm{X,Y}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
we have
\mathrm{F}_{1}=1_{\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})}:\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})\to\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X,Y})
.

Remark 2.7.4
.

1.

The set of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isotopies of
\mathcal{C}
forms an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-subcategory of
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})
.

2.

Note that an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isotopy is equivalent to an element
\mathrm{F}_{+}\in\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{coDer}^{0}\left(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}\right)
given by the "coefficients"
\mathrm{F}_{i}
,

, of
\mathrm{F}

We observe that
\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
is a complete pre-Lie algebra with complete proper pre-Lie subalgebra given by
\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})_{+}=\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
. This subalgebra is pro-nilpotent In this set up we have an exponential map:

Definition 2.7.5
.

For any
\mathrm{c}\in\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
, the exponential of
\mathrm{c}
is defined as

\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{c})=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathrm{c}^{i}}{i!}.

As
\mathrm{c}\in\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})

\mathrm{c}^{i}\in\mathrm{W}_{i+1}\mathrm{CC}(\mathcal{C})
, hence the series converges with respect to the topology induced by the filtration
\mathrm{W}

Proposition 2.7.6
.

For any
\mathrm{c}
in
\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{CC}^{0}(\mathcal{C})
, the exponential
\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{c})
defines an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isotopy
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
iff
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mathrm{c})=0

is a Hochschild class in

Definition 2.7.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category. The Hochschild homology complex of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathrm{CC}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})

with differential

\displaystyle\partial(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j+k\leq n}(-1)^{\epsilon_{k}}\mathrm{d}_{j}(f_{n-k+1}\otimes\cdots f_{n}\otimes f_{0}\otimes f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{j-k-1})\otimes f_{j-k}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n-k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j+k\leq n}(-1)^{\lambda_{k}}f_{0}\otimes f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{k}\otimes\mathrm{d}_{j}(f_{k+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{k+j})\otimes f_{k+j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n},

where we let

\epsilon_{k}=\sum_{l\leq k-1}|f_{n-l}|(s_{n}-|f_{n-l}|)\text{ and }\lambda_{k}=\sum_{l\leq k}|f_{l}|\text{ for }s_{n}=\sum_{l\leq n}|f_{l}|.

The Hochschild homology of

Bi-graded and compactly supported Hochschild cohomology

We shall need variants of Hochschild cohomology. These will be mostly useful in the case of a (finite) graded category
\mathcal{C}
. We extend the following definition of Lunts from algebras to categories:

Definition 2.8.1
.

A minimal flat
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
is called finitely defined if only finitely many of its higher multiplications
m_{i}
are non-zero.

Definition 2.8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely defined
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category. Its compactly supported Hochschild cohomology complex is

\mathrm{CC}_{c}^{d}(\mathcal{C})=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\prod_{\mathrm{X}_{0},\cdots,\mathrm{X}_{p}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{R}}^{d}\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{p-1},\mathrm{X}_{p})[1]\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1})[1]),\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{p})[1]\right).

Remark 2.8.3
.

This is a subcomplex of the Hochschild complex. The reason usually one considers this subcomplex is that direct sums, unlike products, base-change. Hence, the version for categories could play that role only if
\mathcal{C}
is finite.

Let now
\mathcal{C}
be a graded category, define

\mathrm{CC}^{p,q}(\mathcal{C})=\prod_{\mathrm{X}_{0},\cdots,\mathrm{X}_{p}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{R}}^{p+q}\left(\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{p-1},\mathrm{X}_{p})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1})),\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{p})\right).

Then have a decomposition of the compactly supported Hochschild complex

\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathcal{C})=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet,q}(\mathcal{C}).

(1)

Remark 2.8.4
.

Observe that the decompostion (
1
) is a splitting of complexes which holds precisely because
\mathcal{C}
has no higher multiplications. For a general
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category, we only get a spectral sequence for the cohomologies instead.

The next results are straightforward generalisations of algebra versions of Lunts.

Proposition 2.8.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely defined
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category over
\mathrm{R}

\mathcal{C}
is finite and flat of finite type over
\mathrm{R}
. Suppose that

1.

\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C}))=\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}\left(\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathcal{C})\right)

\mathrm{HH}_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{C}))=\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathcal{C}))

Clearly it is enough to prove the first assertion. Since

In the situation of

\mathrm{R}
-linear category. Then the conclusions remain true for the bi-graded Hochschild complex and its cohomology.

Suppose that

Recall that a dg category
\mathcal{C}
over

is called proper if for all objects

is a perfect complex of
\mathrm{R}
-modules. We let

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op},\vee}:(\mathrm{X,Y})\mapsto\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Y,X})^{\vee}.

We have a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathrm{R}}(\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal{C},\mathrm{R})\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op},\vee}\right).

(2)

We denote the cohomology of the complex on the left by

.

Remark 2.9.1
.

If
\mathrm{R}
is a field, so injective over itself, we get just dual of the Hochschild homology. In general, the situation is more complicated, although in sufficiently nice circumstances, e.g. when
\mathrm{R}
has global dimension

, we can control this via spectral sequences.

Definition 2.9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a proper dg category. A weakly proper
n
-Calabi-Yau structure on

is an element in
\mathrm{HH}^{\vee}_{-n}(\mathcal{C})
such that the corresponding morphism

Proposition 2.9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a weakly proper
n
-Calabi-Yau dg category. There is a canonical identification

\mathrm{HH}^{\vee}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet+n}(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

This follows immediately from (
2
) and the definition.
∎

Corollary 2.9.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a weakly proper Calabi-Yau dg category over
\mathrm{R}
such that
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
is projective. Then for any flat
\mathrm{R}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Q}
, we have an injection

\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{Q}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{Q}).

For any projective
\mathrm{R}

\mathrm{M}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{Q}\xhookrightarrow{}\left(\mathrm{M}\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{Q}\right)^{\vee}

Kaledin classes and formality criteria

In this section, we shall generalise two important results on formality of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras due to Kaledin and Lunts.

Theorem 2.10.1

(Lunts
[
31
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{A}
be a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra over
\mathrm{R}
which is projective as an
\mathrm{R}
-module. Then
\mathrm{A}
is formal if and only if it is
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formal for all
n
.

Kaledin
[
18
]
shows that
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formality is obstructed by a Hochschild cohomology class, called the Kaledin class, and that the next holds:

Theorem 2.10.2

(Kaledin-Lunts
[
18
]
,
[
31
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{R}
be an integral domain with field of fractions
k(\eta)
. Consider a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathrm{A}
over
\mathrm{R}
which is a finite projective
\mathrm{R}
-module. Assume that the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{A}(2))
is torsion-free. If
\mathrm{A}_{\eta}=k(\eta)\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}\mathrm{A}
is formal, then
\mathrm{A}
is formal. In particular,
\mathrm{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is formal for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec(R)}
.

Consider now an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
and its associated dg cocategory
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})
. The codifferential of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})
is an element of degree
1
in
\mathrm{coDer}\left(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}\right)
. Hence we may consider its weight decomposition

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),1}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),2}+\cdots.

Definition 2.10.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category. The Kaledin class of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),3}+2\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),4}+\cdots.

Clearly
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a degree
1
element of
\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{coDer}\left(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}\right)
. This gives for all
n,m

[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),n},\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),m}]=[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),m},\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),n}].

Recalling
[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})},\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}]=0
, we get

[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})},\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}]=0.

Hence
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a cocycle and we get a well defined class in
\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{HH}^{2}(\mathcal{C})
. We shall abuse notation and denote by
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
this Hochschild cohomology class.

Remark 2.10.4
.

Similarly there are Kaledin classes associated to
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-categories. Any
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
can be considered as
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-category and the corresponding Kaledin class is the truncation of
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
at weight
n
, denoted
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n}
. It is a cocycle in
\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{coDer}(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})})/\mathrm{W}_{n+1}\mathrm{coDer}(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})})
.

Lemma 2.10.5
.

Let
m
and
m^{\prime}
be two minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures on
\mathcal{C}
. Let
F:(\mathcal{C},m)\to(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isomorphism with
F=1_{\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})}
. Then it induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{HH}(\mathcal{C},m)\to\mathrm{HH}(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})
which is compatible with the weigh filtrations. Moreover,
\mathrm{HH}(\mathrm{F})\left(\mathrm{k}_{(\mathcal{C},m)}\right)=\mathrm{k}_{(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})}
.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathrm{F}=(\mathrm{F}_{1},\mathrm{F}_{2},\cdots)
the cofunctor between the bar constructions too. Let
\mathrm{F}_{*}
and
\mathrm{F}^{*}
be post and pre-composition with the cofunctor
\mathrm{F}
. Note that these operations preserve coderivations. The quasi-isomorphism on the cochain level is defined as

\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{F})\coloneqq\mathrm{F}^{-1,*}\circ\mathrm{F}_{*}:\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C},m)\to\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime}).

It respects the weight filtrations since so do the maps
\mathrm{F}^{-1,*}
and
\mathrm{F}_{*}
.

Similarly to the construction of the Kaledin class, we let
\partial\mathrm{F}
be the
(\mathrm{F,F})
-coderivation determined by
(0,\mathrm{F}_{2},2\mathrm{F}_{3},\cdots)
. By inspecting the construction via the universal property of the reduced tensor coalgebra, this is precisely the map we obtain by changing all higher components of the cofunctor
\mathrm{F}
via the same procedure. Hence
\partial\mathrm{F}\circ\mathrm{F}^{-1}
is a degree
0
coderivation and a calculation shows that

\mathrm{CC}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{F})\left(\mathrm{k}_{(\mathcal{C},m)}\right)-\mathrm{k}_{(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})}=[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C},m^{\prime})},\partial\mathrm{F}\circ\mathrm{F}^{-1}].

∎

Theorem 2.10.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category over
\mathrm{R}
and assume
\mathrm{R}
is a
\mathbf{Q}
-algebra. The following are equivalent:

1.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is formal.

2.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formal for all
n\geq 1
.

3.

For all
n\geq 3
, the truncated Kaledin class
\mathrm{k}^{\leq n}_{\mathcal{C}}
vanishes.

4.

The Kaledin class
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
vanishes.

Proof.

The implication
1\Rightarrow 2
is trivial. Since the (truncated) Kaledin class is invariant under
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-isomorphisms, we get immediately that
2\Rightarrow 3
. By definition we have
3\Rightarrow 4
.

We turn to
4\Rightarrow 1
. Suppose now
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}=0
. We are going to construct an isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{C}
. Since the Kaledin class vanishes, we may pick
\tau^{(2)}\in\mathrm{W}_{2}\mathrm{coDer}^{0}(1_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})})
such that
[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})},\tau^{(2)}]=\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}
. In particular, for
n=3
, we get

[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),2},\tau_{2}^{(2)}]=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),3}.

Hence the map

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{2}^{(2)}\right)}\coloneqq\mathrm{exp}(-\tau_{2}^{(2)})\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}\circ\mathrm{exp}(\tau_{2}^{(2)})

is a degree
1
coderivation on
\overline{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{C}[1])
and we have an isomorphism of dg cocategories

\mathrm{exp}(\tau_{2}^{(2)}):\left(\overline{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{C}[1]),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{2}^{(2)}\right)}\right)\simeq\left(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}\right).

We have
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{2}^{(2)}\right),3}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),3}-[\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C}),2},\tau_{2}^{(2)}]=0
. Let us denote the dg cocategory on the left by
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},{\tau_{2}^{(2)}}\right)
. We may now apply the same procedure to
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},{\tau_{2}^{(2)}}\right)
to find
\tau_{3}^{(3)}
such that

\mathrm{exp}(\tau_{3}^{(3)}):\left(\overline{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{C}[1]),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{3}^{(3)}\right)}\right)\simeq\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},{\tau_{2}^{(2)}}\right).

For the dg cocategory
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},{\tau_{3}^{(3)}}\right)
, we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{3}^{(3)}\right),3}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},\tau_{3}^{(3)}\right),4}=0.

By induction, we get a sequence
\tau_{n}^{(n)}
for all
n\geq 2
such that the codifferential of
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{C},{\tau_{n}^{(n)}}\right)
vanishes in weights
\leq n+1
. The infinite composition

\mathrm{exp}\left(\tau_{2}^{(2)}\right)\circ\mathrm{exp}\left(\tau_{3}^{(3)}\right)\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{exp}\left(\tau_{n}^{(n)}\right)\circ\cdots:\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}

is the required isomorphism which is well-defined as
\tau_{n}^{(n)}
is of weight
n
, hence its exponential is identity on weights
<n
, thus for any fixed weight, we have a finite composition.
∎

Corollary 2.10.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},m)
be a minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category such that
m_{3}=\cdots=m_{n}=0
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-formal if and only if
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n+1}=[m_{n+1}]=0
in
\mathrm{HH}^{2}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
.

Proof.

This an immediate consequence of the preceding theorem and the assumptions on the vanishing of the higher multiplications.
∎

Corollary 2.10.8
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{C},m)
is a finite minimal
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-category which is flat and proper over an integral domain
\mathrm{R}
with generic point
\eta
. Assume the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is torsion-free. Then
\mathcal{C}
is formal iff
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
is formal.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{C}
is formal, then so is its
k(\eta)
base-change
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
. Suppose
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
is formal. It suffices to prove
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formal for all
n\geq 1
. We proceed by induction base case being trivially true. Assume
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-formal. Then, we may assume we are in the situation of
Corollary

2.10.7
. Hence
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-formal iff

\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n+1}=[m_{n+1}]=0

in
\mathrm{HH}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})\subset\mathrm{HH}^{2}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
Since
\mathrm{HH}_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
commutes with flat base-change, the formality of
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
implies that
\left(\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n+1}\right)_{\eta}=\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}^{\leq n+1}=0
, so
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n+1}
is torsion. By assumption
\mathrm{HH}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is torsion-free, hence
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq n+1}=0
and
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-formal.
∎

We shall formulate a dg version of
Corollary

2.10.8
for future use.

Corollary 2.10.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a flat, proper finite dg category over an integral domain
\mathrm{R}
with generic point
\eta
. Assume the

. Then

is formal.

Finally, as finite categories are very restrictive (and are essentially just algebras), we have the following formality criterion which drops the finiteness requirement at the expense of introducing other constraints.

Theorem 2.10.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a flat weakly proper
n
-Calabi-Yau differential graded category over an intergral domain
\mathrm{R}
with generic point
\eta
. Assume the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is projective, where
m_{2}
is the composition in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is formal if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}=\mathrm{Ind}_{k(\eta)/\mathrm{R}}(\mathcal{C})

Proof.

By the Calabi-Yau property, we have an isomorphism of
\mathrm{R}
-modules

\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}^{\vee}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right)\simeq\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet+n}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right).

We can calculate the left side via the spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{R}}^{p}\left(\mathrm{HH}_{q}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right),\mathrm{R}\right)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{\mathrm{R}}\left(\mathrm{CC}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right),\mathrm{R}\right).

Since
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C},m_{2})
is projective over
\mathrm{R}
, the spectral sequence collapses on the second page. Hence
\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right)
is the
\mathrm{R}
-dual of
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right)
and is, therefore, torsion-free.

Next, we claim that the natural map

\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right)\otimes_{\mathrm{R}}k(\eta)\to\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}_{\eta})

(3)

is injective. Indeed,
k(\eta)
is flat over
\mathrm{R}
, so
Corollary

2.9.4
implies the claim. Now, by
Corollary

2.10.7
, we may work by induction on
n
, then injectivity of the map (
3
) and formality of
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
imply that the obstruction classes map to
0
and hence are torsion in the
\mathrm{R}
-module
\mathrm{HH}^{2}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{C}\right)
which is torsion-free, so must vanish.
∎

Example 3.1.2

The most basic example of a holomorphic symplectic manifold is given by cotangent bundles of complex manifolds.

2.

There is a holomorphic Darboux theorem, meaning that the local models of holomorphic symplectic manifolds are open subsets of cotangent bundles.

Definition 3.1.3
.

A complex Lagrangian subvariety of
(\mathrm{X},\sigma)
is a locally closed subvariety
\mathrm{L}
of dimension
\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{X}/2
such that the restriction of the symplectic form to the regular locus vanishes, i.e.
\sigma|_{\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}}=0
. When
\mathrm{L}
is smooth, we call it a submanifold.

Similarly, there is a holomorphic version of contact geometry.

Definition 3.1.4
.

A holomorphic contact manifold is a triple
(\mathrm{Y},\mathscr{L},\alpha)
, where
\mathrm{Y}
is a complex manifold of dimension
2n+1
,
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on it and
\alpha\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Y},\Omega_{\mathrm{Y}}\otimes\mathscr{L})
is such that
\alpha\wedge(\mathrm{d}\alpha)^{n}
is non-degenerate.

We call
\mathscr{L}
the contact line bundle and
\alpha
- the contact form. A contactomorphism
\phi:(\mathrm{Y}_{1},\alpha_{1})\to(\mathrm{Y}_{2},\alpha_{2})
is a local biholomorphism such that
\phi^{*}\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}
.

Example 3.1.5
.

1.

Here the most simple example is the projectivisation of the cotangent bundle of a complex manifold.

2.

Projective spaces of odd dimension are contact.

3.

There is a contact version of the Darboux’s theorem asserting that the local models of contact manifolds are given by open subsets of projectivised cotangent bundles.

The analogue of Lagrangian subvarieties is as follows:

Definition 3.1.6

A complex Legendrian subvariety of

is a locally closed subvariety
\Lambda
of dimension

such that
\alpha|_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{sm}}}=0
.

Next, we describe how to go back and forth between the symplectic and contact geometries.

Definition 3.1.7

Let

be a contact manifold. Its canonical symplectisation is the principal

-bundle
\gamma:\tilde{\mathrm{Y}}\to\mathrm{Y}

Remark 3.1.8
.

1.

Microdifferential modules

Let
\mathrm{X}
be a complex manifold. The cotangent bundle
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
supports a filtered sheaf of
\mathbf{C}
-algebras
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
of formal microdifferential operators. Fix
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
local coordinates on
\mathrm{X}
, and write
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
for the induced coordinates on
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
. Let
\mathscr{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)
be the sheaf of homogeneous functions in the fibre coordinates on
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
of degree
m
, i.e.

\left(\sum\xi_{j}\partial/\partial\xi_{j}-m\right)f(x,\xi)=0.

We define the sheaf of formal microdifferential operators of order
\leq m
by

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)=\prod_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\mathscr{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m-j).

In order to get a sheaf globally on
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
, glue these sheaves on overlaps using the transformation rule for total symbols of differential operators. Taking the limit over
m\in\mathbf{Z}
, we get the sheaf of formal microdifferential operators on
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
:

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}=\varinjlim_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m).

Let
\sigma_{m}:\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)\to\mathscr{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)
be the principal symbol. There is an isomorphism

\mathrm{gr}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathscr{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m).

There are products
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(l)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)\to\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(l+m)
, given by

(f\star g)_{l}(x,\xi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=i+j-|\alpha|\\
\alpha\in\mathbf{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial/\partial\xi_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial/\partial\xi_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}f_{i}(x,\xi)\cdot(\partial/\partial x_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial/\partial x_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}g_{j}(x,\xi).

In particular
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
and
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)
are sheaves of (non-commutative)
\mathbf{C}
-algebras.

Remark 3.2.1
.

Notice that the total symbol of a differential operator is a polynomial in
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}
and now we are allowing symbols which are general holomorphic functions rather than just polynomials.

Let
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{X}})
be the projectivised cotangent bundle of
\mathrm{X}
. The sheaf
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
is constant on the fibres of the projection
\pi:\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}\setminus\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{X}})
and we shall use the same notation for its pushforward to
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{X}})
. We shall eventually be interested in modules over the stack of formal microdifferential operators on a general contact manifold. The next properties of modules over
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
are local and will readily extend to the stack version.

Let
\Lambda
be a Legendrian subvariety of
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{X}})
. There is a lattice in
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
associated to
\Lambda
defined as the subalgebra of
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}|_{\Lambda}
generated by

\mathrm{I}_{\Lambda}=\{P\in\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(1)|_{\Lambda}\;|\;\sigma_{1}(P)|_{\Lambda}=0\}.

We denote this subalgebra by
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Lambda/\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
.

Definition 3.2.2
.

1.

A coherent module
\mathscr{M}
over
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
is holonomic if its support is a Legendrian subvariety.

2.

A holonomic
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is regular holonomic if locally there is a coherent
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)
-module
\mathscr{M}^{0}
which generates
\mathscr{M}
over
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
and
\mathscr{M}^{0}/\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(-1)\mathscr{M}^{0}
is a
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}(0)
-module.

3.

A regular holonomic
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is simple along
\Lambda
if
\mathscr{M}^{0}/\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(-1)\mathscr{M}^{0}
is an invertible
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}(0)
-module.

Remark 3.2.3
.

Regularity is equivalent to requiring that
\mathscr{M}^{0}
be
\mathrm{I}_{\Lambda}
-invariant.

Quantisation of contact manifolds

Given a (holomorphic) contact manifold
\mathrm{Y}
, we may choose an open covering
\{\mathrm{U}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
together with contact embeddings
\rho_{i}:\mathrm{U}_{i}\to\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Omega_{\mathrm{Y}_{i}}
into projectivised cotangent bundles. The sheaves of formal microdifferential operators on these projectivised cotangent bundles do not glue and one need to rigidify the problem.

Let
\mathrm{X}
be a complex manifold. Recall that locally the formal adjoint of a microdifferential operator
\mathrm{P}=\sum p_{i}\in\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(m)
is given by
\mathrm{P}^{*}=\sum p^{*}_{i},
where

p_{i}^{*}(x,\xi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=k-|\alpha|\\
\alpha\in\mathbf{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}(\partial/\partial\xi_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial/\partial\xi_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}\cdot(\partial/\partial x_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial/\partial x_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}p_{k}(x,-\xi).

We get a map

*:\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}\to\pi^{-1}\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}\otimes_{\pi^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}\otimes_{\pi^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\pi^{-1}\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{-1}.

Hence, upon passing to half twists by the canonical bundle, we get a canonical anti-involution:

*:\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}^{\sqrt{v}}\to\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}^{\sqrt{v}}.

(

]
)

DQ-algebras

We let
\mathrm{X}
be a complex manifold. Let
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
be the ring of formal power series in
\hbar
, and
\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)
its field of fractions, i.e. the field of formal Laurent series. Define a sheaf of
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-algebras:

\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]=\varprojlim\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}\otimes_{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]/\hbar^{n}.

Definition 3.6.1
.

A star product on
\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]
is a
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-bilinear associative multiplication
\star
such that

f\star g=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{P}_{i}(f,g)\hbar^{i},\text{ where }f,g\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}},

such that
\mathrm{P}_{i}
are holomorphic bidifferential operators with
\mathrm{P}_{0}(f,g)=fg
and
\mathrm{P}_{i}(f,1)=\mathrm{P}_{i}(1,f)=0
for all
i\geq 1
. The pair
(\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!],\star)
is called a star algebra.

Definition 3.6.2
.

A deformation quantisation algebra (DQ-algebra) on a complex manifold
\mathrm{X}
is a sheaf of
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-algebras
\mathscr{A}_{\mathrm{X}}
locally isomorphic to a star algebra as a
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-algebra.

Example 3.6.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\mathrm{X}}
be a DQ-algebra on
\mathrm{X}
. Let
\pi:\mathscr{A}_{\mathrm{X}}\to\mathscr{A}_{\mathrm{X}}/\hbar\mathscr{A}_{\mathrm{X}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}
. For any
f,g\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}
, choose lifts
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}
such that
\pi(\tilde{f})=f
and
\pi(\tilde{g})=g
. Then define a bracket

\{f,g\}=\pi(\hbar^{-1}(\tilde{f}\tilde{g}-\tilde{g}\tilde{f})).

This is independent of the choices made and defines a Poisson structure on
\mathrm{X}
.

Example 3.6.4
.

Let
t
be the coordinate on
\mathbf{C}
and
(t;\tau)
- the symplectic coordinates on
\Omega_{\mathbf{C}}
. Let
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C},\tau\not=0}
be the open subset of
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}}
where
\tau\not=0
. We have a map

\rho:\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C},\tau\not=0}\to\Omega_{\mathrm{X}},\quad(x,t;\xi,\tau)\mapsto(x,\tau^{-1}\xi).

Define the subsheaf of operators independent of
t
:

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}},\widehat{t}}(0)=\{\mathrm{P}\in\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}}}(0)\text{ such that }[\mathrm{P},\partial_{t}]=0\}.

Then, letting
\hbar
act as
\tau^{-1}
, we define the canonical DQ-algebra on
\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}
by

\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)=\rho_{*}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}},\widehat{t}}(0)\subset\rho_{*}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}}}(0).

The
\hbar
-localisation of
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
.

Remark 3.6.5
.

We call the DQ algebra in the example above the canonical deformation quantisation of the cotangent bundle of a complex manifold.

1.

We have
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)/\hbar\cong\mathscr{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}
, so it’s indeed deformation quantisation.

2.

\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}|_{\mathrm{X}}\cong\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}(\!(\hbar)\!)
, that is, restricting to the zero section
\mathrm{X}
, we get differential operators over formal Laurent power series.

3.

\rho_{*}\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}\times\mathbf{C}}}(0)
is a flat
\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{X}}}(0)
-module.

Deformations and the dualising complex

Let
\mathrm{X}
be a complex manifold endowed with the DQ algebroid
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)
. Kashiwara and Schapira
[
24
]
defined a deformation
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}
of the sheaf of differential operators
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}
. It is a
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-subalgebroid of
\mathscr{E}nd_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0))
and there is an equivalence

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!].

This induces an equivalence of stacks of modules over these algebroids. Under this equivalence,
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)
, regarded as a
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}
-module, corresponds to
\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]
. On the central fibres we get

\iota^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}.

This deformation gives rise to a deformation of the canonical bundle of
\mathrm{X}
as follows. The above implies that

\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-1.5pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{p}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0),\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\right)=0

for all
p\not=n\coloneqq\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{X}
. Indeed, under the equivalence
\mathrm{D}^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]
, we have
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)\simeq\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]
, so the claim follows from the standard calculation

\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\text{\kern-3.0pt{\it{om}}}\,_{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]}\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!],\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!]\right)\simeq\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}[\![\hbar]\!][-n].

Then the deformation of the canonical bundle is defined as

\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}=\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-1.5pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{n}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0),\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\right).

This is a bi-invertible
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)\otimes\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)^{\mathrm{op}}
-module such that
\iota^{*}\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\simeq\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}
.

Definition 3.8.1
.

We define the
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)
-dualising complex of
\mathrm{X}
as

\omega^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\text{\kern-3.0pt{\it{om}}}\,_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}}\left(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0),\mathrm{D}^{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}\right)[2n]\simeq\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}[n].

By
[
24
]
, it defines a Serre functor

{\mathrm{\bf{D}}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0))\to{\mathrm{\bf{D}}}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0))\;\;\mathscr{D}\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}[n]\otimes_{\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)}\mathscr{D}.

Proposition 3.8.2
.

Let
\mathrm{X}
be holomorphic symplectic. Then we have an isomorphism of
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)\otimes\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)^{\mathrm{o}p}
-modules
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathscr{W}}\cong\hbar^{\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{X}/2}\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)

\mathscr{D}
and

\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\text{\kern-3.0pt{\it{om}}}\,_{\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{E})[n]

We are going to pair this theorem with an existence result of D’Agnolo and Schapira regarding quantised orientations of Lagrangian submanifolds. Indeed, by the classification

(D’Agnolo-Schapira

corresponding to
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}

\mathrm{exp}(2\pi i\lambda)

Remark 3.10.3
.

More generally, without assuming the orientability of

It induces a long exact sequence in cohomology which includes

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{L},\mathbf{C}^{*})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{L},\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{L}}^{*})\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{L},\mathrm{d}\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{L}})\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathrm{L},\mathbf{C}^{*}).

Recall that invertible
\mathbf{C}
-algebroids on
\mathrm{X}
are classified by

\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1/2}}
be the

-algebroid associated to the class

Then the classification

3.5.1
, we see that the theorem above remains true for

-modules, i.e. in general we get twisted

-modules.

Notice that

is trivial if and only if there exists a line bundle

such that

The complex of derived

\mathrm{Cone}\left(\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}:\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}\to\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{X}}\right),

We apply the above to the case of Lagrangian intersection. Let
\mathrm{X}
be holomorphic symplectic and suppose
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}

\mathrm{M}

1

\mathrm{U}
if necessary, we may assume

is a critical chart, called an

-critical chart in
[

]

\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
and get
\mathrm{M}

\Delta\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{X}\times\mathrm{X}

-charts and the result of Bussi is that these turn

into a

-critical locus:

(Bussi
[

]
)

Let
\mathrm{X}
be a holomorphic symplectic variety. Suppose given two Lagrangians
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{M}
in

. Then then intersection
\mathrm{L}\cap\mathrm{M}
admits a structure of a
\mathrm{d}
-critical locus

with canonical bundle

\mathrm{X}

Theorem 4.1.1

(Malgrange
[
32
]
)

-modules.

For any coherent sheaf

The above resolution of

.

\mathrm{X}
be a complex manifold. For a coherent sheaf

\mathrm{MS}:\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{X})\to\mathrm{C}^{b}(\mathrm{X})

and we use the same notation for its extension to
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\mathrm{X})
. By definition, for any
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{+}(\mathrm{X})

\mathrm{MS}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\mathscr{F}\simeq\mathrm{MS}(\mathscr{F}).

\mathrm{X}
and
\mathrm{Y}
be complex manifolds and consider a map

\mathscr{F}

We are going to be mainly interested in applications to sheaves of dg algebras and this is facilitated by the dg algebra structure on the graded sheaf

\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\bullet}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p}\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{X}}^{0,p},

given by the triple
(\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\bullet},\bar{\partial},\wedge)

\mathscr{F}

is a dg

.

Let
\mathscr{A}

Let

whose composition is the multiplication

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\mathscr{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{MS}(\mathscr{A})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{X}}}\mathrm{MS}(\mathscr{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{MS}(\mathscr{A}).}

Formality

Definition 5.4.1
.

A Solomon-Verbitsky collection
\mathfrak{L}
is a countable collection of orientable compact Kähler Lagrangian submanifolds
\mathrm{L}_{i}
,
i\in\mathrm{I}
, in
\mathrm{X}
with pairwise clean intersections.

Theorem 5.4.2
.

Let
\mathrm{X}/\mathbf{C}
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold and let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Solomon-Verbitsky collection. The virtual de Rham category
\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
of
\mathfrak{L}
is formal.

Proof.

We begin by defining two dg categories. Let
\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}
with the same objects as
\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
and set

\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)=\Gamma_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{MS}\left(\mathscr{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)\right)\right),

i.e. the subcomplex of the complex of global sections given by the
\bar{\partial}
-closed elements.

The second auxiliary category denoted
\mathrm{H}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
is the dg category with the same set of objects as
\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
and morphisms given by the quotients

\mathrm{H}_{\bar{\partial}}(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}})\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)=\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)/\bar{\partial}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)\right)

with differential induced by
\mathrm{d_{dR}}
.
There is a diagram of functors

\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\overset{\iota}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{H}_{\bar{\partial}}(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}).

The first is given by the natural inclusion, while the latter is the canonical projection. We are going to show that these induce quasi-isomorphisms and moreover
\mathrm{H}_{\bar{\partial}}(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}})
is a graded category, i.e.
\mathrm{d_{dR}}
induces the zero differential on the spaces of morphisms. Hence
\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is formal.

The differential structure on
\mathrm{H}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
is trivial: indeed pick any
\alpha
with
\bar{\partial}(\alpha)=0
. The we can apply the
\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}
-lemma to
\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)
hence getting

\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)=\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}(\gamma)

which implies that
[\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)]=0
in the quotient

\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)/\bar{\partial}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)\right).

Since both
\iota
and
\pi
are identity on objects, they are essentially surjective on the underlying homotopy categories.

Let’s begin by showing that
\iota
induces a quasi-isomorphism between complexes of morphisms. It clearly induces a surjection on cohomology since any
\mathrm{d_{dR}}+\bar{\partial}
-closed element is also
\bar{\partial}
-closed for degree reasons. To show injectivity, suppose we have a
\bar{\partial}
-closed element

\alpha=\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\beta)+\bar{\partial}(\beta).

Applying the
\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}
-lemma, we get
\alpha=\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}(\gamma)
and clearly
\bar{\partial}(\gamma)
is in the kernel of
\bar{\partial}
.

Surjectivity of
\pi
: suppose given

[\alpha]\in\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)/\bar{\partial}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)\right),

since the differential is trivial, it is enough to find a
\mathrm{d_{dR}}
-closed representative of
\alpha
. To that end, apply the
\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}
-lemma to
\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)
to find
\gamma
such that

\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)=\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}(\gamma),

so
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\bar{\partial}(\gamma)
is in the class of
\alpha
and is
\mathrm{d_{dR}}
-closed. Injectivity follows immediately from the
\mathrm{d_{dR}}\bar{\partial}
-lemma since any
\alpha
with
\bar{\partial}(\alpha)=0
and
\mathrm{d_{dR}}(\alpha)=0
which projects to
0
in

\mathcal{DR}_{\mathfrak{L}}^{\mathrm{vir},\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)/\bar{\partial}\left(\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{1}},\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\right)\right)

must be
\mathrm{d_{dR}}
-exact.
∎

Having established the formality of

, we are now going to consider the coherent category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathfrak{L})
. Before proving our next result, we will need some preliminary lemmas. The first one gives a sufficient condition for the cohomology of a perfect complex over

Lemma 5.4.6
.

Let
\iota:\mathbf{C}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
be the inclusion of the central fibre and let

\mathrm{C}\in\mathrm{Perf}\big(\mathrm{Spec}\big(\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]\big)\big).

Suppose that for all
i\in\mathbf{Z}
we have

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\big(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\iota^{*}\mathrm{C})\big)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\big(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{C})\big).

Then the cohomology
\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{C})
is free (of finite rank) over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
.

Proof.

Consider the exact triangle

\mathrm{C}\xrightarrow{\hbar}\mathrm{C}\to\iota^{*}\mathrm{C}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{C}[1].

It induces a long exact sequence in cohomology

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\xrightarrow{\hbar}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\iota^{*}\mathrm{C})\to\mathrm{H}^{i+1}(\mathrm{C})\xrightarrow{\hbar}\mathrm{H}^{i+1}(\mathrm{C}).

Hence there are exact sequences

0\to\mathbf{C}\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\iota^{*}\mathrm{C})\to\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\left(\mathbf{C},\mathrm{H}^{i+1}(\mathrm{C})\right)\to 0.

In particular, we get that

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{C}\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\right)\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\iota^{*}\mathrm{C})\right).

Since
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})
is finitely generated, we may write it as

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})=\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]^{d_{i}}\oplus\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]/\hbar^{k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]/\hbar^{k_{r_{i}}},

where
k_{1},\cdots,k_{r_{i}},r_{i}\in\mathbf{N}
. Notice that

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{C}\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\right)=d_{i}+r_{i}\text{ and }\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\right)=d_{i}.

It follows by flatness of
\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)
that

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{C})\right)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathrm{C})\right).

Hence,
r_{i}=0
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}\left(\mathrm{C}\right)
is free.
∎

Lemma 5.4.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Solomon-Verbitsky collection and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
be a finite full dg subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
. Then it is formal and quasi-isomorphic to the trivial formal deformation of
\iota^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
, where
\iota:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
is the inclusion of the central fibre.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{loc}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}\subset\mathcal{DR}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
be the full subcategory spanned by the objects of
\mathcal{D}
. We know
\mathcal{D}
is formal by
Corollary

5.4.5
. The degeneration of the local-to-global
\mathrm{Ext}
spectral sequences implies that
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is free of finite type over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
. Hence we think of it as a (flat) formal deformation of
\mathrm{H}\iota^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\cong\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
. Since
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is finite and its morphism spaces are free of finite rank over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
, upper semi-continuity gives an inequality

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathrm{HH}^{p,q}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}\right)\right)\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathrm{HH}^{p,q}\left(\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\right)\right).

(7)

On the other hand, the spectral sequence argument shows that there is a multiplicative filtration
\mathrm{F}
on
\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
such that

\mathrm{gr}_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\right)\cong\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}.

The completed Rees deformation associated to
\mathrm{F}
gives a deformation with central fibre
\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}
. Thus

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)}\left(\mathrm{HH}^{p,q}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}\right)\right)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathrm{HH}^{p,q}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}\right)\right)\geq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathrm{HH}^{p,q}\left(\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\right)\right).

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
) with
Lemma

5.4.6
, we get the compactly supported (of second kind) Hochschild cohomology
\mathrm{HH}_{c}^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\right)
is free over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
. Now formality of
\mathcal{D}
and
Corollary

2.10.9
imply
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is formal.

Abbreviate the Hochschild cohomology of the graded categories as follows:
\mathrm{HH}\coloneqq\mathrm{HH}\big(\iota^{*}\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\big)
,
\mathrm{HH}_{\hbar}\coloneqq\mathrm{HH}\big(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\big)
and let
\mathrm{HH}_{(\hbar)}
be the localisation of
\mathrm{HH}_{\hbar}
at
\hbar
. The reduction
\mathrm{mod}\,\hbar
gives a map
\mathrm{HH}_{\hbar}\to\mathrm{HH}
and since
\mathrm{HH}_{\hbar}
is free over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
, taking a section of the reduction map, we get
\mathrm{HH}_{\hbar}\cong\mathrm{HH}[\![\hbar]\!]
.

We have an isomorphism of graded categories

\varphi_{\hbar}:\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)=\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)/\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}\right).

Write
m_{\hbar}
and
m_{\mathrm{dR}}
for the compositions of
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathrm{H}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{vir}}
, respectively. Then by definition

m_{\hbar}=\varphi_{\hbar}^{-1}m_{\mathrm{dR}}(\varphi_{\hbar},\varphi_{\hbar}).

(9)

Differentiating (
9
) with respect to
\hbar
gives

m_{\hbar}^{\prime}=-\mathrm{d}_{\hbar}(\varphi_{\hbar}^{-1}\varphi_{\hbar}^{\prime}),

(10)

where
\mathrm{d}_{\hbar}
is the Hochschild differential of the graded category
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
, i.e.

[m_{\hbar}^{\prime}]=0\text{ in }\mathrm{HH}^{2,0}_{(\hbar)}.

Since we have a free
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-module, we may write

m_{\hbar}=m+m_{r}\hbar^{r}+\cdots,\,r\geq 1,

hence we see that the left side of (
10
) is

rm_{r}\hbar^{r-1}+(r+1)m_{r+1}\hbar^{r}+\cdots.

Hence we deduce that

m_{r}+(r+1)/r\cdot m_{r+1}\hbar+(r+2)/r\cdot m_{r+2}\hbar^{2}+\cdots

is an
\hbar
-torsion class in
\mathrm{HH}^{2,0}_{\hbar}
, lifting the class
[m_{r}]\in\mathrm{HH}^{2,0}
, so it must vanish. Thus
[m_{r}]=0
too. Letting
m_{r}=\mathrm{d}(\psi^{r})
, we define an automorphism

\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}

by acting as identity on objects and
\mathrm{id}-\psi^{r}\hbar^{r}
on morphisms. It kills
m_{r}
. By induction we get
\psi^{i}
for all
i\geq r
, the infinite composition

\psi_{\hbar}=\big(\big(\mathrm{id}-\psi^{r}\hbar^{r}\big)\circ\big(\mathrm{id}-\psi^{r+1}\hbar^{r+1}\big)\circ\cdots\big)

makes sense and we have
\psi_{\hbar}^{-1}m_{\hbar}(\psi_{\hbar},\psi_{\hbar})=m
, showing the triviality of
m_{\hbar}
.
∎

We immediately get the following corollary:

Corollary 5.4.8
.

The composition in
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is
\hbar
-free.

With this preliminary results in our toolkit, we are ready to prove the formality of the dg category of Lagrangian

-branes in a Solomon-Verbitsky collection.

Theorem 5.4.9
.

Let
\mathrm{X}/\mathbf{C}
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold. Suppose
\mathfrak{L}
is a Solomon-Verbitsky colllection. Then the dg category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathfrak{L})
is formal.

Proof.

The category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathfrak{L})
can be realised as the central fibre of the dg category
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
. In addition, we have

\mathrm{loc}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)\cong\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}.

By
Corollary

5.4.5
it follows that
\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is formal. The strategy therefore is to apply
Theorem

2.10.10
following the philosophy that "generically formal
\implies
formal".

Recall that for any
\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{i}}}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
, the space of morphisms

\mathrm{RHom}_{\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)}\left(\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{1}}},\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{2}}}\right)

is a perfect
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-module and
Proposition

3.8.2
implies that
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is weak proper
2n
-Calabi-Yau, and so its cohomology
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is Calabi-Yau too. It will now suffice to show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is flat and the Hochschild homology
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
of the graded category
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is a torsion-free
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-module. It is shown in
[
34
]
that for any
\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{i}}}
in
\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
, the spaces

\mathrm{Ext}_{\widehat{\mathscr{W}}_{\mathrm{X}}(0)}\left(\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{1}}},\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathrm{L_{2}}}\right)

are finite free over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
which takes care of flatness. Moreover, by
Corollary

5.4.8
, the Hochschild differential of

\mathrm{CC}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)

is
\hbar
-free, so its cohomology has no
\hbar
-torsion and it therefore torsion-free. Thus
\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}
is formal by
Theorem

2.10.10
and so is the central fibre
\iota^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Lag}}(\mathfrak{L})
.
∎

Remark 5.4.10
.

There is a more direct and somewhat self-contained version of the above proof. By the Calabi-Yau property, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-modules

\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}^{\vee}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)\simeq\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet+2n}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right).

We can calculate the left side via the spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}^{p}\left(\mathrm{HH}_{q}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right),\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]\right)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_{\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]}\left(\mathrm{CC}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right),\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]\right).

Since
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
has global dimension
1
, it degenerates on the second page. As
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
is torsion-free, we see
\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
is the
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-dual of
\mathrm{HH}_{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
, hence is torsion-free.

We claim that the natural map

\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)\to\mathrm{HH}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{H}\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}})

(11)

is injective. Indeed,
\mathbf{C}(\!(\hbar)\!)
is flat over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
and torsion-free modules over
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
are projective, so
Corollary

2.9.4
implies the claim. Now, by
Corollary

2.10.7
, we may work by induction on
n
, then injectivity of the map (
11
) and formality of
\mathcal{DQ}_{\mathfrak{L}}
imply that the obstruction classes map to
0
and hence are torsion in the
\mathbf{C}[\![\hbar]\!]
-module
\mathrm{HH}^{2}\left(\mathrm{H}\widetilde{\mathcal{DQ}}_{\mathfrak{L}}\right)
which is torsion-free, so must vanish.
