Homogenization of an optimal control problem for nonlocal semilinear elasticity with soft inclusions
Amartya Chakrabortty Abu Sufian

Abstract
This paper investigates the asymptotic analysis of an optimal control problem (OCP) posed on a high-contrast elastic medium with soft periodic inclusions, governed by a semilinear elasticity system with a nonlocal term. The domain consists of a connected matrix phase and a soft inclusion phase. The model depends on two independent small parameters: the periodicity ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 and the contrast δ > 0 \delta>0 , and the distributed control acts only in the inclusion region. We consider an L 2 L^{2} -tracking cost on the displacement and analyze the limit as ( ε , δ ) → ( 0 , 0 ) (\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0) in the regime lim ( ε , δ ) → ( 0 , 0 ) δ ε = κ ∈ ( 0 , + ∞ ] . \lim_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}=\kappa\in(0,+\infty]. First, we derive the homogenized (limit) state system associated with this scaling. We then formulate the limit OCP and prove that the limit of the microscopic optimal controls is an optimal control for the limit problem, using a Γ \Gamma -convergence approach.



Keywords:

Homogenization, Optimal Control Problem, Semi-linear Elasticity, High-Contrast Domain, Unfolding Operator.

Introduction

Smart origami-inspired metamaterials
[
21
]
and advanced textiles
[
33
]
offer a platform for designing structures with
programmable deformation patterns. A common engineering mechanism is to combine a connected stiff skeleton
(e.g. panels or fibers) with comparatively soft regions (e.g. hinges, joints, or inclusions), so that the
overall response is governed by a pronounced contrast of stiffness across the microstructure. In many
applications, one seeks to actively tune the deformation by embedding actuation or pre-strain in the
soft phase, while the stiff phase primarily carries and transmits load. This naturally leads to models in
which the control/actuation is supported only in the soft inclusions.

A widely used modeling viewpoint for such materials is to encode pre-strain (or growth/swelling) through a
multiplicative decomposition of the deformation gradient into an elastic part and a non-elastic part, see
[
23
]
.
Under small-deformation and small-prestrain assumptions, the resulting linearized equations for the
displacement contain an additive term generated by the pre-strain (see
[
5
]
), which can be recast as an effective
source term in the equilibrium equations (see
[
17
]
). In this work, we adopt this perspective and treat the resulting
effective source term as a distributed control acting only in the soft inclusions. This provides a
mathematically tractable framework for shape/response optimization while remaining consistent with common
linearization principles used in the mechanics of pre-strained or swelling media.

Beyond the contrast and microstructure, many soft active materials exhibit an additional feature: their
mechanical response may depend not only on the local strain, but also on a global measure of the
deformation amplitude. Such global coupling can arise, for instance, from an underlying interaction with a
surrounding medium (e.g. solvent redistribution in gels, network effects, or mean-field-type feedback),
or from modeling choices that penalize large overall deformation to reflect saturation/stiffening effects.
To capture this in a minimal way while keeping the elastic operator local, we include a nonlocal semilinear term of the form
\alpha\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}u
with
p\geq 2
. Equivalently, this term is generated by adding
to the total energy a convex contribution proportional to
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p+2}
, so that the resulting
Euler–Lagrange equation couples the local elastic equilibrium with a global amplitude-dependent response.

Homogenization for partial differential equations with high-contrast coefficients has attracted sustained attention over the past decades, in particular for diffusion and conductivity-type models. Early contributions include
[
34
,
35
]
, where strongly contrasting coefficients were analyzed in connection with effective properties of composite media. Since then, a broad literature has developed on high-contrast homogenization and the emergence of nonstandard (often nonlocal) effective models.

Beyond diffusion, high-contrast effects also arise in elasticity and more general variational settings. In
[
3
]
, the macroscopic behavior of an elastic composite with a strong component embedded in a weak matrix was studied; the homogenized limit exhibits nonlocal features that depend sensitively on the geometry of the stiff phase. Related high-contrast phenomena for conductivity were investigated in
[
6
]
, which considers a medium consisting of highly conductive vertical fibers embedded in a poorly conductive matrix. Further examples include
[
16
]
, addressing an elliptic variational problem on a pillar-type oscillating domain with an insulating layer surrounding a highly conductive core, and
[
24
]
, where a hyperbolic PDE with contrasting diffusive coefficients leads to a coupled two-scale limit system incorporating both macro- and micro-scale effects. For additional references on high-contrast homogenization we refer to
[
15
,
4
,
36
]
and the references therein.

The homogenization of optimal control problems (OCPs) governed by PDE constraints has also been extensively studied. For elliptic OCPs with oscillating coefficients (possibly appearing both in the state equation and in the cost functional), see for instance
[
18
,
20
]
. The homogenization of OCPs in porous media was investigated in
[
19
]
. For OCPs with high-contrast diffusion coefficients, we refer to
[
28
,
25
]
, where the limiting problems may exhibit nonlocal effects. In these works, the principal part of the state equation is given by the Laplacian; see also
[
20
,
14
,
26
]
and the references therein.

Homogenization of an approximate controllability problem (ACP) constrained by semilinear state systems was initiated in
[
13
]
on fixed domains, and later extended to perforated domains in the cases where the holes do not intersect the control region
[
11
]
and where the holes intersect the control region
[
12
]
. In these works, the semilinearity enters through a local term involving a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-function
S_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

S_{1}(0)=0,\qquad\exists\,\gamma>0\ \text{such that}\ 0\leq\frac{S_{1}(s)}{s}\leq\gamma,\quad\forall\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}.

(0.1)

Homogenization of OCPs in domains with oscillating boundaries was studied in
[
1
,
30
,
27
,
29
]
, where the control acts on the fixed (non-oscillating) part of the boundary/domain. In these works, the semilinearity is also introduced via a local term involving a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-function
S_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

S_{2}(0)=0,\qquad 0<C_{1}\leq S_{2}^{\prime}(s)\leq C_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}^{\prime\prime}\ \text{is bounded}.

(0.2)

In the analysis of above OCPs, the indirect method, which concerns the
asymptotic behavior of the corresponding optimality systems is used.
In
[
22
]
, homogenization study of a semilinear OCP is presented the direct method, relying on
\Gamma
-convergence arguments. Their semilinearity is assumed to satisfy a global growth/Lipschitz estimate of at most quadratic type,
together with monotonicity closedness. Moreover, since the leading operator is uniformly coercive/elliptic (i.e., no high-contrast degeneracy), the states admit uniform
H^{1}
-bounds, hence by the compact embedding strong
L^{2}
convergence. This strong
L^{2}
-compactness is the key ingredient to pass to the limit in such semilinear terms.

This paper studies the asymptotic behavior of an optimal control problem (OCP) posed on a high-contrast domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, governed by a semilinear elasticity system with a nonlocal (at least cubic) term, using the indirect method and a form of
\Gamma
-convergence. The high-contrast domain is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. It is decomposed into two parts: a connected stiff part
\Omega^{2}_{\varepsilon}
with relative stiffness of order
1
, and a soft part
\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}
with relative stiffness of order
\delta^{2}
. The microscopic control
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon})^{N}
acts only on the soft part
\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}
(extended by zero to
\Omega
).

The governing system (understood in the weak sense; see Section
2
for details) reads, for
p\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\sigma_{\varepsilon\delta}(u_{\varepsilon\delta})+\alpha\|u_{\varepsilon\delta}\|^{p}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,u_{\varepsilon\delta}&=f(x)+{\bf 1}_{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\theta_{\varepsilon},\quad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon\delta}&=0,\quad&&\text{on }\Gamma_{0},\\
\sigma_{\varepsilon\delta}(u_{\varepsilon\delta}){\bf n}&=0,\quad&&\text{on }\Gamma_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(0.3)

where
f=(f_{i})
denotes the body force.

The main aim of this paper is to analyze the asymptotic behavior, as
(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)
, of an OCP that seeks to match a prescribed displacement profile using external forces supported in the soft inclusions. Let
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}
be a given desired displacement. For each admissible control
\theta_{\varepsilon}
, we denote by
u_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta_{\varepsilon})\in{\bf U}
the unique weak solution of (
0.3
) (equivalently, of (
3.1
)). We consider the reduced cost functional

{\bf j}_{{\varepsilon\delta}}(\theta_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta_{\varepsilon})-u_{d}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}|\theta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx,

where
\gamma>0
is a regularization parameter. The OCP consists in finding an optimal control
\Theta_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon})^{N}
such that

{\bf j}_{{\varepsilon\delta}}(\Theta_{\varepsilon})=\inf_{\theta_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon})^{N}}{\bf j}_{{\varepsilon\delta}}(\theta_{\varepsilon}).

The results of the paper can be divided into two parts. First, we derive the limit (homogenized) state system associated with (
0.3
) in the asymptotic regime

(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0),\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa=\lim_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\in(0,+\infty].

(0.4)

Using periodic unfolding, suitable energy arguments and a form of
\Gamma
-convergence, we identify the limit displacement, the associated correctors, the limit total energy and characterize that the limit energy depends on the parameter
\kappa
. Moreover, in the case
\kappa=+\infty
we obtain a fully scale-separated homogenized system. The main tool for asymptotic analysis is the periodic unfolding operator, which was introduced in
[
7
]
and further developed in
[
8
]
. For an introduction to homogenization and elasticity, see
[
32
,
10
,
31
,
2
,
9
]
.

Second, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the microscopic OCP as
(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)
with
\delta/\varepsilon\to\kappa
. We establish existence of microscopic optimal controls and prove convergence of the adjoint state and optimal controls. Moreover, we derive the limit OCP and show that any limit point of a sequence of microscopic optimal controls is an optimal control for the limit problem. The proof relies on a
\Gamma
-convergence approach for the reduced cost functionals, together with stability and strong-convergence properties for the unfolded state variables.

Finally, we emphasize a further novelty of the present work concerns the structure of the nonlocal semilinearity.
In several related contributions on homogenization of OCPs with semilinear state equations, the nonlinearity
enters through a scalar functional of the state combined with a scalar nonlinearity (see, for example,
the functions
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in (
0.1
)–(
0.2
)), under global growth/monotonicity assumptions. In contrast,
our model involves the Hilbert-space operator

u\mapsto\alpha\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}\,u,\quad p\geq 2

which couples all spatial points through the global
L^{2}
-amplitude of the displacement.
As a consequence, passing to the limit in this term cannot be achieved from weak convergence alone; it requires
strong
L^{2}
convergence (equivalently, convergence of
L^{2}
norms), which we obtain through energy convergence
arguments in the unfolding framework. Moreover, the Fréchet derivative of this operator contains a
nonlocal rank-one contribution,

D(\|u\|^{p}u)[h]=\|u\|^{p}h+p\|u\|^{p-2}(u,h)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,u,

leading to additional global coupling in the linearized and adjoint systems.
These features, combined with the high-contrast elastic operator, make the analysis more delicate than in the
scalar diffusion setting. In particular, uniform
H^{1}
bounds typically fail because the elastic energy degenerates
in the soft phase (due to the
\delta^{2}
scaling). To overcome this difficulty, we introduce a suitable decomposition
(see Lemmas
3.2
–
3.3
) and establish convergence of the total energy via a
\Gamma
-convergence argument.

The paper is organized as follows. General notation and the description of the domain are introduced in
Sections
1
and
2
, respectively. In Section
3
, we prove existence, uniqueness, and
a priori estimates for the weak solution (equivalently, the minimizer) of the
\varepsilon
–
\delta
-dependent
semilinear elasticity system. In Section
4
, we establish existence of an optimal control for the

\varepsilon
–
\delta
-dependent OCP and show that the control-to-state mapping is globally Lipschitz. The main homogenization results for the state equation are presented in
Section
5
. Finally, in
Section
6
, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the
\varepsilon
–
\delta
OCP.

3  Existence of unique solution and a priori estimates

The problem is to determine the displacement field
u_{\varepsilon\delta}=(u_{{\varepsilon\delta},i})
satisfying the system (
0.3
). By virtue of the assumptions on
{\bf A}_{\varepsilon\delta}
, we define the bilinear form
{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}
by

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u,w)=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{{\varepsilon\delta},ij}(u)e_{ij}(w)\,dx,\quad\forall\,u,w\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{N}.

We also define the global nonlinear term as

g(u,w)=\alpha\|u\|^{p}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot w\,dx,\quad\forall\,u,w\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{N}.

So, the weak form of the problem (
0.3
) is given by: For a given function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}
and
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1})^{N}
, find the solution
{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}\in{\bf U}
such that

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u_{\varepsilon\delta},w)+g(u_{\varepsilon\delta},w)=\ell_{\varepsilon}(w)=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot w\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot w\,dx,\quad\forall\,w\in{\bf U},

(3.1)

where the set of admissible displacement is given by

{\bf U}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{N}\,|\;u=0\;\text{on $\Gamma_{0}$}\right\}.

The associated minimization problem is

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{Find $u_{\varepsilon\delta}\in{\bf U}$ such that}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt{\bf m}_{\varepsilon\delta}={\bf T}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u_{\varepsilon\delta})=\inf_{w\in{\bf U}}{\bf T}_{\varepsilon\delta}(w),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

where the total energy to this nonlinear elasticity problem is given by

{\bf T}_{\varepsilon\delta}(w)={1\over 2}{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(w,w)+{1\over p+2}g(w,w)-\ell_{\varepsilon}(w),\quad\text{for all $w\in{\bf U}$}.

Since
{\bf T}_{\varepsilon\delta}
is Fréchet differentiable and strictly convex on
{\bf U}
(for
\alpha>0
), its unique minimizer in
{\bf U}
coincides with the unique weak solution of (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

such that
\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
for all

. Hence

|B_{\varepsilon\delta}(w,v)|\leq C_{1}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall w,v\in{\bf U},

for some constant

. Similarly,

|F_{\varepsilon\delta}(w)|=|(\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}-u_{d},w)|\leq\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

so
F_{\varepsilon\delta}
is continuous on
{\bf U}
.

Since
p\geq 2
,
\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}\geq 0
and
\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p-2}\geq 0
. Thus,
for any
v\in{\bf U}
,

B_{\varepsilon\delta}(v,v)={\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(v,v)+{\alpha}\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+{\alpha}p\,\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p-2}\,(\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta},v)^{2}\geq{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(v,v).

By the coercivity assumption on
{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}
, there exists
c>0
such that

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(v,v)\geq c\,\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in{\bf U}\implies B_{\varepsilon\delta}(v,v)\geq c\,\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in{\bf U}.

Therefore,
B_{\varepsilon\delta}
is coercive on
{\bf U}
.

By the Lax–Milgram theorem, there exists a unique
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta}\in{\bf U}
such that (
6.4
) holds, i.e.

\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta}
is the unique solution of the adjoint variational problem (
6.1
).
Since
B_{\varepsilon\delta}
is coercive,
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon\delta}
is strictly convex and therefore has at most one minimizer.
Consequently, the unique solution
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta}
given by Lax–Milgram is the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon\delta}
.

Finally, taking
w=\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta}
in (
6.1
), we get

C\delta^{2}\|e(\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon})}+\|e(\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{2}_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\overline{u}_{\varepsilon\delta}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\overline{v}_{\varepsilon\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Then, with the inequality (
3.10
) and proceeding as in Lemma
3.3
, we get (
6.3
).
This completes the proof.

Step 2:

For the fixed state

Remark 2

(On sufficiency)
.

In general with the nonlinear term
\alpha\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}u
, the system (
6.6
) gives
first-order necessary conditions. Since, for
\alpha>0
the control-to-state map
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}:{\bf V}\to{\bf U}
is nonlinear (indeed, non-affine).
A quick scaling argument shows this: for simplicity we take
f\equiv 0
. If
u={\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta)
and
t>0
, then

u_{t}:={\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}(t\theta)
solves

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u_{t},w)+\alpha\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}(u_{t},w)=(t\theta,w)\quad\forall w\in{\bf U}.

If
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}
were linear, we would have
u_{t}=tu
, hence

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(tu,w)+\alpha\|tu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}(tu,w)=t\,{\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u,w)+t^{p+1}\alpha\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}(u,w),

which equals
t\big({\bf a}_{\varepsilon\delta}(u,w)+\alpha\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}(u,w)\big)
only when
\alpha=0
or
u\equiv 0
.
Thus
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}
is not linear for
\alpha>0
.
As a consequence, the reduced functional

{\bf j}_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta)-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

is, in general, non-convex for
\alpha>0
. Indeed, differentiating twice in a direction

yields

{\bf j}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)[h,h]=\|{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime}(\theta)h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big({\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}(\theta)-u_{d},\;{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)[h,h]\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and the middle term has no fixed sign in general. In the linear case
\alpha=0
we have
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0
, so

{\bf j}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)[h,h]=\|{\mathcal{S}}_{\varepsilon\delta}^{\prime}(\theta)h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\gamma\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\gamma\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

hence

is (strictly) convex and the optimal control is unique. For
\alpha>0

multiple stationary points and non-uniqueness of global minimizers.

\limsup_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\bf i}_{\varepsilon\delta}\leq\limsup_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\bf j}_{\varepsilon}(\theta_{\varepsilon})=\limsup_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon\delta},\theta_{\varepsilon})={\bf J}_{\kappa}(v_{0},V_{1},\widehat{\theta})={\bf j}_{\kappa}(\widehat{\theta}).

Since,
\widehat{\theta}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y_{1})^{N}
is arbitary, we get (
6.18
). Now, taking an optimal control of the OCP (
6.14
) in place of
\widehat{\theta}
in (
6.18
), gives (together with (
6.17
))

{\bf i}_{\kappa}\leq{\bf j}_{\kappa}(\widehat{\Theta})=\liminf_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\bf i}_{\varepsilon\delta}=\limsup_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\bf i}_{\varepsilon\delta}\leq{\bf i}_{\kappa}.

So, we obtain (
6.16
). This completes the proof.
∎

(6.21)
