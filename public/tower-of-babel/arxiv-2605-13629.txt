Orbital stability of black solitons for quasilinear Schrödinger equations with nonzero conditions at infinity
Erwan Le Quiniou

Abstract
We investigate the orbital stability of black solitons for a broad class of quasilinear Schrödinger equations in one space dimension, with nonzero boundary conditions at infinity. Namely, our framework handles general defocusing semilinear nonlinearities and focusing or defocusing quasilinear nonlinearities. First, we establish sufficient conditions on the quasilinear nonlinearities ensuring the existence of a local branch of finite-energy solitons parameterized by their speed. Within this branch, the black soliton, also called kink, corresponds to the stationary solution. Our main result is the orbital stability of the black soliton in the energy space, provided that the Vakhitov–Kolokolov (VK) slope condition holds; namely, that the derivative of the momentum with respect to the speed is negative at zero. Moreover, we derive an explicit formula for verifying this VK condition. The proof relies on the analysis of a carefully designed variational problem, which allows us to control the sup-norm of the evolution of a perturbation of the kink in terms of the energy and momentum, both of which are conserved by the flow. A delicate part of the argument is the analysis of minimizing sequences for this variational problem, since the infimum is not attained.



Keywords:

Quasilinear Schrödinger equation, Gross–Pitaevskii equation, traveling waves, black soliton, kink, nonzero conditions at infinity, orbital stability.

1.1
Solitons and their stability

To our knowledge, the first interest of the mathematical community in (
QLS
) goes back to works from H. Lange and L. Brull
[
46
,
15
]
in 1986. They obtained a sufficient condition for the existence of classical standing
and traveling-wave solutions in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
and derived formal (pseudo)-conservation laws of equation (
QLS
). Among them, let us recall the energy conservation law

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(\Psi(\cdot))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\nabla\Psi(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(|\Psi(x)|^{2})dx+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\nabla h(|\Psi(x)|^{2})|^{2}dx,

(1.2)

where
F(\sigma)=\int_{\sigma}^{r_{0}^{2}}f(w)dw
is the antiderivative of
-f
vanishing at
r_{0}^{2}
;
and the (renormalized) momentum is given by

\displaystyle P(v)=\int\operatorname{Re}(i\partial_{x}v\bar{v})\Big(1-\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{|v|^{2}}\Big)dx.

To ensure that the energy is well-defined, it is standard to work in the so-called energy space

\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\{v\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}):v^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\text{ and }|v|^{2}-r_{0}^{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}),

which coincides with the domain of
E_{\kappa}(\cdot)
in
H^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
whenever
\kappa\geq 0
. If
v\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
, then Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
provide
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|v(x)|^{2}=r_{0}^{2}
. Besides, defining the momentum of
v\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
, requires
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|v(x)|>0
, that is,
v
belongs to the nonvanishing energy space given by

\mathcal{N}\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})=\{v\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R}):\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|v(x)|>0\}.

In what follows, we endow
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
with the distance

d_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(v,w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}v-\partial_{x}w|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|v|-|w|)^{2}dx+|v(0)-w(0)|^{2}.

(1.3)

Dark and black solitons

In the setting of nonvanishing conditions at infinity, we call a dark soliton a finite energy traveling-wave solution to (
QLS
) given by

\Psi(x,t)=u_{c}(x-ct)
for some

The traveling-wave equation writes

icu_{c,\kappa}^{\prime}=u_{c,\kappa}^{\prime\prime}+u_{c,\kappa}f(|u_{c,\kappa}|^{2})+\kappa u_{c,\kappa}h^{\prime}(|u_{c,\kappa}|^{2})(h(|u_{c,\kappa}|^{2}))^{\prime\prime},\text{ in }\mathbb{R},

(TW
(c,\kappa)
)

together with the condition

Linearizing (

Then, (

A.1

QLS

1+2\kappa|\Psi|^{2}h^{\prime}(|\Psi|^{2})^{2}>0,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.4)

We say that a solution to (

\displaystyle 1+2\kappa\sigma(h^{\prime}(\sigma))^{2}>0,\quad\text{ for all }\sigma\in[0,r_{0}^{2}],

(1.5)

Going back to the expression of the energy (

(

The (orbital) stability of gray and black solitons has been analyzed in other settings: First, note that its weak formulation, equation (

(

,

)

\partial_{c}P(u_{c,\kappa})<0

\partial_{c}P(u_{c,\kappa})>0

[

in 2005, and the instability criterion was obtained by C. Audiard
[

in 2017. The VK criterion for Hamiltonian systems with a non-surjective skew-symmetric operator was obtained in a unified manner in 2020

E_{0}(u_{0,0}+\delta_{1}+i\delta_{2})=E_{0}(u_{0,0})+\begin{pmatrix}L_{+}\delta_{1}\\
\ L_{-}\delta_{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{1}\\
\delta_{2}\end{pmatrix}+o(||\delta||_{H^{1}}^{2}),

where

Let us sketch the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In a small neighborhood of the black soliton, we formally have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big||\Psi(x)|-r_{0}\Big|=r_{0}-\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big||u_{0,\kappa}(x)|-r_{0}\Big|-\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|.

Thus, for the orbital stability, it is important to track the growth of
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|

To corroborate this fact, in Proposition
3.15

\inf_{(z,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}d_{\mathcal{X}}(v,u_{0,\kappa}(\cdot-z)e^{i\varphi})\leq K(S(\Psi)+K\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi|)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(1.11)

Then, a major part of our analysis consists of establishing the functional inequality

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|^{2}\leq KS(\Psi),

(1.12)

for some
K>0
and all
\Psi\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
in a small neighborhood of

To do so, letting
\tilde{\xi}

1+2\kappa\sigma h^{\prime}(\sigma)^{2}>0,\quad\text{ and, }\quad F(\sigma)>0,\quad\text{ for all }\sigma\in(r_{0}^{2};r_{0}^{2}+\tilde{\xi}],

In the setting of vanishing condition at infinity (

It is difficult to stress how vivid the topic of standing wave solutions is in the quasilinear Schrödinger community. We shall only mention recent studies on the qualitative properties of these solutions in higher spatial dimensions and point the interested reader to the references therein

[

, and in arbitrary dimension, but for power laws nonlinearities by F. Genoud and S. Rota-Nodari
[

[

,
41

To our knowledge, the asymptotic dynamics around solitons is an open problem in general: To this day, we only have access to a handful of global well-posedness results for (

[

,
37

and small data, and two scattering results for small data: in 1d for cubic semilinear nonlinearity
