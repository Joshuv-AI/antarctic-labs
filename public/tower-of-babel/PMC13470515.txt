Paired high volume instrument (HVI) and advanced fiber information System (AFIS) dataset for global cotton characterization
Authors: Prosenjit Sen, Toufique Ahmed, Md. Sultan Mahmud, Priyanka Sarkar, Md. Abidur Rahman Nibir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The article presents a comprehensive dataset of physicochemical descriptions of commercial cotton fibers (Gossypium hirsutum L.) that grow in a wide range of agro-climatic- conditions, such as Brazil, West Africa (Mali, Benin, Burkina Faso, Ivory Coast, and Cameroon), Australia, and India. It comprises paired samples from two industry-standard characterization systems, the High Volume Instrument (HVI) to measure the bundle characteristics (e.g., Micronaire, Upper Half Mean Length, and Strength) and the Advanced Fiber Information System (AFIS) to measure the distributions of single fibers (e.g., Neps per gram, Short Fiber Content, Maturity Ratio). To ensure the retention of metadata of various originating file structures, data from industrial-quality reports were digitized with a proven Python-based Optical Character Recognition (OCR) pipeline and subsequently verified manually to achieve high accuracy. The strategic importance of this dataset is that it is the aggregation of ubiquitous HVI trade values and capital-intensive AFIS process values on identical Lot and Bale identifiers. This unique synchronization offers textile-machine learning researchers or data scientists the opportunity to explore various potential linear, non-linear and interdependencies between HVI and AFIS features. Notably, it may pave the way for predicting nep count from the HVI dataset, potentially eliminating the necessity of purchasing an expensive AFIS machine. No predictive analyses or machine learning models were conducted in this current study. Besides, it can be used for various classification-based works through standard commercial classification-level data.

Background

The global cotton industry is heavily reliant on major producing nations, such as China, India, the United States, Brazil, and Turkey. However, this dataset represents the available cotton utilized by a selected Bangladeshi spinning mill during a specific timeframe (June 2024 to June 2025), during which the factory sourced its raw cotton from Brazil and India, alongside Australia and multiple West African countries (e.g., Ivory Coast, Burkina Faso, Mali, Cameroon, and Benin).

In a spinning mill, HVI data are essential for bale purchasing, mix planning, and routine quality control, while AFIS data are critical for understanding fiber dispersion, short fibers, and neps that strongly affect spinning performance and yarn quality. HVI gives rapid measurement of bulk cotton properties such as staple length, uniformity, strength, elongation, micronaire, color, and trash for many bales every day, which supports bale classification and purchase decisions. These parameters are widely used to design bale mixes and predict yarn quality, for example using span lengths and short fiber indices derived from the HVI fibrogram [1]. AFIS measures individual fiber length distribution, short fiber content, neps, seed coat neps, fineness, and maturity, giving much more detailed information about fiber preparation quality than HVI [2]. This detail helps diagnose problems in opening, cleaning, and carding, because changes in AFIS neps or short fiber content often indicate mechanical or process setting issues that will increase yarn imperfections and end breaks [2].

Both HVI and AFIS are needed in the factory because HVI is ideal for high-throughput incoming control and long-term statistics, but it cannot fully describe fiber damage, nep generation, or micro-level dispersion through the blowroom and carding line. AFIS, although slower and more expensive per test, reveals process-generated defects and short fibers that are not visible from HVI alone, allowing targeted adjustments that improve spinning stability and yarn appearance [2]. Therefore, paired measurements are highly valuable for a cotton bale. In practice, large mills often test every bale or at least every lot on HVI to define mix windows (length, strength, MIC, trash) and then sample key points (mix, laps, card sliver, comber sliver) on AFIS for process optimization [1]. Combining both datasets lets technologists link raw material variability (from HVI) with process induced changes (from AFIS), improving settings for carding, drawing, and combing and enabling more reliable yarn quality prediction models [1]. In the same way, it provides the ground truth data to train future machine learning models for statistical analysis and predicting complex AFIS parameters from the standard HVI trade metrics.

Data Description

This article presents a structured dataset of raw cotton fiber quality parameters measured using High Volume Instrument (HVI) and Advanced Fiber Information System (AFIS) [3]. The HVI data has 14 columns namely: Lot, Bale ID, SCI, Moisture, MIC, Maturity, UHML, UI, SF, Strength, Elongation, Rd, +b, Color grade. On the other hand, the AFIS data has 6 columns namely Total Nep count, UQL, SFC_w, SFC_n, Maturity ratio, Immature fiber content. The total number of rows in the dataset is 402. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sample conditioning and preparation

Prior to AFIS and HVI testing, the raw material (cotton) was conditioned according to the ISO-139 standard. The samples were kept at a temperature of 20 ± 2 °C and a relative humidity of 65 ± 2% for a full day (24 h) to reach moisture equilibrium before any measurements were taken.

High volume instrument (HVI) testing

The fiber bundle properties are obtained using the HVI-1000 instrument. It has primarily 3 modules, namely:a)Micronaire (MIC) module: Indicates combined fiber fineness and maturity. The airflow resistance through the compressed cotton fiber determines the fineness and maturity.b)Length and Strength module: This module measures the UHML, UI, SF%, bundle strength (g/tex) and elongation of fiber.c)Color and Trash: It determines the reflectance (Rd) and yellowness (+b), color grade and trash amount by using optical technology and image analysis.

Advanced fiber information system (AFIS) testing

AFIS Pro 2 (Advanced Fiber Information System) can test bundles of fibers utilizing aeromechanical opening mechanism to physically separate and individualize fibers. The individual fibers are then drawn through an electro-optical sensor to determine the detailed profile of fibers including Neps (neps count/gram), Length (Short Fiber Content by weight (SFC_w) and Short Fiber Content by number (SFC_n), Upper Quartile Length and Mean length), Maturity and Fineness (maturity ratio, immature fiber content, and cross-sectional fineness).

Data extraction and software pipeline

The test results from HVI and AFIS were originally stored as printed/PDF reports in the factory. To generate a consolidated digital dataset a python based programmatic extraction pipeline was developed in a Jupyter Notebook environment (.ipynb) [4]. The pipeline utilized the following sequence and open-source libraries: The pdf2image library for image conversion, pytesseract for Optical Character Recognition (OCR), re (Regular Expressions) library for parsing and pattern matching and finally Pandas for data consolidation.

Data validation

The reliability of the dataset was ensured through several validation steps after data extraction. The original HVI and AFIS reports were first converted into digital data using a Python-based OCR workflow that included pdf2image, pytesseract, regular-expression parsing, and Pandas. Because OCR can introduce transcription errors when reading industrial reports, every extracted record was manually compared with the original report. Any errors found during this process were corrected before the final dataset was prepared.

After manual verification, additional quality checks were carried out. HVI and AFIS records were matched using the same Lot and Bale ID to confirm that both measurements belonged to the same cotton sample. The dataset was then checked for duplicate records, missing values, formatting errors, and data completeness. These checks confirmed that the final dataset contains 402 complete paired records, with no duplicate records and no missing values.

In addition, although formal OCR accuracy and extraction error rates were not measured during dataset development, the complete manual verification of all extracted records against the original laboratory reports, together with systematic quality checks, provides confidence in the accuracy and reliability of the dataset for statistical analysis and machine-learning applications.

Limitations

This dataset comprises industrially tested commercial cotton from multiple producing countries but may not fully represent global cotton diversity. Although all OCR-extracted records were manually verified against the original reports, isolated transcription errors cannot be entirely excluded. Furthermore, the dataset includes only paired HVI–AFIS measurements and does not incorporate spinning process parameters, yarn properties, or longitudinal observations.

Ethics Statement

The data of this article involve neither animal nor human participants. Besides, according to the company’s data distribution policy data can be shared for research and non-commercial purposes. Hence our dataset complies with the data distribution policy of the company.
