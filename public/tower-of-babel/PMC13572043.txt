Ascending Aorta Rotational Control Improves Commissural Alignment With the Navitor Vision Delivery System
Authors: Norio Tada, Yun Teng, Yusuke Enta, Masaki Nakashima, Natsuko Satomi, Daishi Tazawa, Yuhei Hasegawa, Masaki Miyasaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Ascending aortic rotation improved commissural alignment with Navitor.•Rotational timing influenced marker orientation at the annular level.•Descending aortic orientation was not consistently maintained.•Computed tomography–derived cusp overlap views guided rotational adjustment.•No rotation-related procedural complications were observed.

Commissural alignment during transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has become increasingly important to facilitate future coronary access and redo interventions.1 The optimal rotational strategy for nonspine delivery systems remains uncertain.2

The Navitor Vision valve (Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, Illinois) is delivered via the FlexNav delivery system, which employs a nonspine design that may reduce rotational fidelity during advancement through the aortic arch. We hypothesized that rotational orientation established at the descending aorta may not be maintained at the annular level and that rotational adjustment during advancement through the ascending aorta may improve commissural alignment.

We retrospectively analyzed consecutive patients who underwent TAVR using the Navitor Vision valve between April 2025 and March 2026 at our institution. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

During the early study period (April 2025 to November 2025), a descending aorta rotation strategy was used.3 Rotational orientation was established at the descending aorta to achieve a configuration of 2 radiopaque (RO) markers along the inner aortic curve and 1 marker along the outer aortic curve in the computed tomography (CT)-derived, left coronary cusp (LCC)–isolated cusp overlap view (COV), after which the delivery system was advanced and the Navitor Vision valve was deployed without further rotational manipulation.

During the later study period (November 2025 to March 2026), an ascending aorta rotation strategy was adopted (Figure 1). As described in the ALIGN-TAVR consensus document, commissural misalignment was assessed fluoroscopically in the NCC-isolated COV according to RO marker configuration.1 Commissural misalignment was graded as no, mild, moderate, or severe (ordinal score 1–4), and comparisons between groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test.

The degree of rotational adjustment, which varies depending on the patient’s unique vascular structure and catheter interactions, is a technical consideration. In some cases, only minimal rotational adjustment is necessary. In others, repeated or substantial rotational adjustment is required to achieve the desired commissural marker orientation. To minimize excessive torque accumulation or catheter kinking, continuous rotation exceeding 360° in the same direction is avoided. If the desired marker orientation is not maintained at the annular level, the delivery system is withdrawn to the sinotubular junction, and rotational adjustment is repeated. Because the left coronary artery originates from the LCC, the LCC-isolated COV may be particularly useful for commissural alignment adjustment. However, according to the double S-curve concept, substantial parallax may occur from valve positioning until before release, potentially making accurate implantation depth assessment difficult. After release, however, the valve generally better aligns with the native anatomy, and parallax becomes minimal. During valve deployment, care is taken to maintain stable rotational torque in order to preserve commissural orientation. In addition, recapture occasionally alters commissural marker orientation, requiring repeated fluoroscopic reassessment and, in some cases, repeated rotational adjustment.

A total of 36 patients were included (ascending aorta rotation [AAR], n = 13; descending aorta rotation [DAR], n = 23). Baseline characteristics were generally comparable between groups. Commissural misalignment in the NCC-isolated COV immediately before deployment was significantly lower in the AAR group (n = 13) than the DAR group (n = 15) (median [interquartile range]: 1.0 [1.0–1.5] vs. 3.0 [2.0–4.0]; p = 0.0004). Commissural misalignment after release remained significantly lower in the AAR group (1.0 [1.0–2.0] vs. 3.0 [2.0–4.0]; p = 0.0009). There were no significant differences in conduction-related outcomes between the groups, including permanent pacemaker implantation (7.1% vs. 9.1%; p = 0.83) and new-onset left bundle branch block at discharge (21.4% vs. 22.7%; p = 0.92). No complications directly attributable to rotational manipulation were observed, including vascular injury, valve embolization or migration, device malfunction, frame distortion, or abnormal tab connection/release.

The present study suggests that rotational timing is a critical determinant of commissural alignment with the Navitor Vision delivery system. Rotational orientation established at the descending aorta may not be reliably preserved during advancement through the aortic arch in nonspine systems, whereas repeated rotational adjustment through the ascending aorta may help maintain the desired commissural orientation at the annular level.

Several important limitations should be acknowledged. This was a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size. Commissural alignment was assessed fluoroscopically rather than by systematic post-TAVR CT. Missing data were present in the DAR group for predeployment marker assessment. Procedural safety and conduction outcomes require validation in larger studies.

In conclusion, rotational adjustment during advancement through the ascending aorta was associated with significantly improved commissural alignment compared with a rotation strategy at the descending aorta when using the FlexNav delivery system. Larger studies are warranted to further validate the procedural efficacy and safety of this approach.

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of Sendai Kosei Hospital.
