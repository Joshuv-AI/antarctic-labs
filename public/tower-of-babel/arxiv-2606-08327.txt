Chiaroscuro Attention: Spending Compute in the Dark Operator Routing via Spectral Entropy Across Tasks and Scales
Prateek Kumar Sikdar

Abstract
We introduce CHIAR-Former (CHIAroscuro Attention-based tRansFormer), an efficient transformer that routes each token to either DCT spectral mixing ( 𝒪 ⁡ ( d ​ log ⁡ d ) \mathcal{O}(d\log d) , sub-quadratic) or full self-attention ( 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 2 ​ d ) \mathcal{O}(n^{2}d) , quadratic in sequence length n n ) based on per-token spectral entropy H ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] H(\mathbf{x})\in[0,1] , which measures the frequency-domain complexity of each token embedding 𝐱 \mathbf{x} . We make three contributions: (1) we discover routing collapse —a three-operator system collapses to DCT+Attention, revealing the optimal operator subset; (2) we propose a learned task-level MetaRouter g = σ ⁡ ( Linear ⁡ ( 𝐱 ¯ ) ) ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] g=\sigma(\mathrm{Linear}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}))\in[0,1] , where 𝐱 ¯ \bar{\mathbf{x}} is the batch-mean embedding and g g soft-blends spectral and identity paths end-to-end; and (3) we demonstrate 35–40% FLOP reduction at 400M parameters with a 3.93 PPL cost on WikiText-103 (Test PPL 27.51 vs. 23.58). Under mixed-dataset training, CHIAR-Former dramatically outperforms full attention on small corpora, confirming the regularisation value of spectral mixing. The MetaRouter stabilises at g ≈ 0.22 g\approx 0.22 , indicating that at scale the model reaches a robust compute–quality equilibrium: attention layers absorb representational complexity while spectral preprocessing efficiently anchors low-frequency structure.

Introduction

The quadratic cost of self-attention
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}d)

[
27
]

has motivated efficient transformers
[
26
]
. Existing methods
apply fixed patterns regardless of per-token information content. We ask:
can tokens signal how much compute they need?

We formalise this via
spectral entropy
: the entropy of a token’s DCT
power spectrum
[
1
]
. Low-entropy tokens (function words) are
smooth and amenable to cheap spectral mixing; high-entropy tokens (content
words) are complex and benefit from full attention.

The name
chiaroscuro
comes from the Italian for light (
chiaro
)
and dark (
scuro
), describing the Renaissance painting technique used by
Caravaggio, Leonardo da Vinci, and Rembrandt—illuminating only where the eye
needs detail, leaving smooth regions in inexpensive shadow. CHIAR-Former
applies this principle to computation: spend the attention budget where the
signal is dark (high spectral entropy), and use cheap DCT mixing where the
signal is light (smooth, low-entropy tokens).

(2)
Theory-grounded per-token
routing via spectral entropy

(Theorem

).

(3)
Learned MetaRouter for task-level adaptation, stabilising at

g\approx 0.22
—a compute–quality equilibrium reflecting the complementary
roles of spectral structure and attention capacity.

(4)
Scale experiments at 16M and 400M demonstrating regime-dependent
efficiency–quality trade-offs.

3
CHIAR-Former Architecture

3.1
Spectral Entropy

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a token embedding vector of dimension
d
.
Its Type-II DCT spectrum
[
1
]
is

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathrm{DCT}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where each
component
\hat{x}_{i}
captures the energy in the
i
-th frequency basis.
Normalising gives a probability distribution over frequency bins:

p_{i}=\frac{\hat{x}_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}+\varepsilon},\quad i=1,\ldots,d,

(1)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small constant for numerical stability.
The
spectral entropy

[
6
]
of token
\mathbf{x}
is:

H(\mathbf{x})=-\frac{1}{\log d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\log p_{i}\;\in[0,1],

(2)

normalised by
\log d
so that
H=0
for a pure single-frequency signal
(maximally smooth) and
H=1
for a uniform spectrum (maximally complex).
Low-entropy tokens (function words, punctuation) concentrate energy in a few
low frequencies and are efficiently handled by DCT; high-entropy tokens
(domain-specific nouns, rare content words) spread energy across frequencies
and require the full expressiveness of self-attention.

3.2
Theoretical Motivation

Theorem 1

(DCT optimality for low-entropy signals)
.

For signals with approximately Toeplitz covariance (stationarity assumption),
the DCT asymptotically diagonalises the covariance matrix (Karhunen-Loève
optimality
[
6
]
). For tokens with
H(\mathbf{x})\leq\tau
,
DCT achieves near-optimal energy compaction in
\mathcal{O}(d\log d)
;
self-attention provides no additional decorrelation benefit for such tokens.

5.4
Mixed-Dataset Training and Small-Corpus Generalisation

To evaluate cross-domain robustness, we train CHIAR-Former on mixed batches
drawn from WikiText-103, WikiText-2, IMDB, and ListOps simultaneously.
On WikiText-103 this yields Test PPL 28.56 (vs. 27.51 for single-dataset
CHIAR), a modest cost of 1.05 PPL for multi-task breadth.
On WikiText-2—a small corpus of 2.4M tokens where overfitting is the
primary challenge—mixed training with spectral regularisation yields
dramatically better generalisation than full attention trained on
WikiText-2 alone, confirming that DCT’s energy compaction acts as a strong
structural prior on limited data.
These results establish two complementary operating regimes
(discussed further in Section
6
).

6.4
Operating Regime Characterisation

Two distinct regimes emerge from our experiments.

(1) Small-scale / small-data:
DCT’s energy compaction acts as a
strong structural prior, providing regularisation that full attention lacks
on limited corpora; mixed-dataset training amplifies this effect.

(2) Large-scale / large-data:
Efficiency is the primary value
proposition—37% FLOP reduction with a 3.93 PPL cost at 400M on
WikiText-103—while the MetaRouter’s stabilisation at
g\approx 0.22

confirms that spectral preprocessing remains a productive, stable
contributor to model quality at this scale.

7
Conclusion

CHIAR-Former routes tokens between DCT spectral mixing and full
self-attention based on spectral entropy
H(\mathbf{x})
, inspired
by the chiaroscuro principle of spending compute only where complexity demands it.
Key findings: routing collapse reveals DCT+Attention as the optimal operator pair;
37% FLOP reduction at 400M with a modest 3.93 PPL cost on WikiText-103;
mixed-dataset training demonstrates strong spectral regularisation on small
corpora; and the MetaRouter stabilises at
g\approx 0.22
, a robust
compute–quality equilibrium in which spectral preprocessing and attention
play complementary, stable roles at scale.

Future work: hard-gating MetaRouter at inference to realise full FLOP savings;
scaling to 1B+ parameters; fine-tuning on downstream NLP benchmarks
(GLUE, SuperGLUE).
