Discovery of FAP-Targeted Platinum(IV)-Esterified STING Agonist as Small-Molecule Dual Prodrug Conjugates for Tumor-Specific Metalloimmunotherapy
Authors: Ende He, Jia Sun, Zhengyuan Wang, Yiming Fan, Zilan Song, Ao Zhang, Chunyong Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tumor-specific activation of the stimulator of the interferon genes (STING) pathway is essential for maximizing antitumor efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity of STING agonists. Conjugating STING agonists with tumor-targeting warheads has emerged as a promising strategy in cancer immunotherapy. In this study, we designed and synthesized a series of fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-targeting platinum (Pt)­(IV)-centered STING agonist conjugates by esterifying the two axial hydroxyls of Pt­(IV) species with STING agonist MSA-2 and the FAP warhead, respectively. Among the synthesized conjugates, 21b exhibited picomolar binding affinity to FAP and preferentially accumulated in 3T3-hFAP cells. Under hypoxic tumor conditions, these conjugates release the active Pt­(II) species to induce tumor cell death and simultaneously liberate MSA-2 to activate the STING/IFN-mediated antitumor immune response. In colon tumor-bearing mice, 21b mainly distributed in tumor tissue and showed significantly enhanced antitumor activity and reduced toxicity. Compared to MSA-2 alone, it promoted more CD8+ T-cell infiltration and reduced FAP+ cell populations in tumors. Importantly, it selectively elevated TNF-α and IP-10 levels in tumor tissue without increasing serum cytokine levels, indicating tumor-specific STING pathway activation. These findings provide strong proof of concept for FAP-targeted Pt­(IV)–STING agonist conjugates as a novel tumor-specific metalloimmunotherapy.

Introduction

The stimulator of the interferon genes (STING) pathway is a critical component of the innate immune system, playing a pivotal role in the detection of cytosolic DNA and subsequent antiviral and antitumor immune responses. , Direct STING activation has been shown to increase the infiltration of activated CD8+ T cells and exerts remarkable curative effects in solid tumor mouse models, − rendering development of STING agonists a promising cancer immunotherapy over the past decade. However, the clinical development of cyclic dinucleotide (CDN) STING agonists was unsuccessful due to limited efficacy and potential safety concerns. , In-depth mechanistic studies have revealed that STING is ubiquitously expressed in both tumor and normal cells, and chronic, systemic, and unconstrained STING activation may lead to immunosuppression, thus causing autoimmunity or severe inflammation-related side effects. − Therefore, achieving tumor tissue-specific activation of STING has been proposed as a promising strategy to enhance the antitumor efficacy of conventional STING agonists while reducing their systemic toxicity. , To this end, the antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) platform has recently been adapted to deliver STING agonists by linking to tumor-targeting monoclonal antibodies. − Two such antibody-STING agonist conjugates (ASACs), HER2-targeted ASAC XMT-2056 (NCT05514717) from Mersana Therapeutics and CCR2-targeted ASAC TAK-500 (NCT05070247) from Takeda, showed promising preclinical results and have currently advanced to phase I clinical trials. − Nevertheless, these ASACs still suffers from immunotoxicity, leading to the unsatisfactory clinical outcome. As alternatives, small molecule-drug conjugates (SMDCs) have recently emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy with advantages of better solid tumor penetration, lower immunogenicity, and reduced production cost. , We recently reported two carbonic anhydrase IX-targeted SMDCs designed to selectively deliver STING agonists to tumor cells via either photocontrolled or bioreductant-dependent cleavage. , However, tumor tissue-targeting ability of these SMDCs has not been demonstrated yet in mouse models. Therefore, STING agonist SMDCs with higher tumor-specificity are still highly needed.

Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) is a surface biomarker of cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) and constitutes the majority of tumor stroma in solid cancers. In the tumor microenvironment, FAP functions as tumor-promoting and immunosuppressive effector. Importantly, FAP was found absent in healthy tissues but highly expressed in the stroma of more than 90% of the epithelial cancers, , warranting it as a promising warhead for tumor tissue-specific targeting. − On the other hand, great progress has been made in the targeted delivery of platinum-based drugs, with various delivery systems developed to improve tumor accumulation and reduce systemic toxicity. − These advanced platforms have greatly promoted the development of precision platinum chemotherapy. The combination of Pt drug and a STING agonist showed synergistic effects and extended survival compared to each monotherapy. − Nevertheless, this combination therapy suffers from different pharmacokinetics and biodistribution profiles as well as chemo- or immuno-toxicities of the individual drugs. Pt­(IV) prodrugs offer a platform allowing for conjugation of STING agonist and tumor-targeting group to the axial positions of Pt­(IV) into a single prodrug molecule. The resulting Pt­(IV) conjugate is kinetically inert in the bloodstream. Upon the reduction of Pt­(IV), the bioactive ligands are released in their active form. , In this regard, we decided to develop a new generation of SMDCs by esterifying the two axial hydroxyls of Pt­(IV) species with a FAP inhibitor warhead (OncoFAP) and a STING agonist (MSA-2), thus forming a series of Pt­(IV)-centered FAP-targeting STING agonist conjugates (Figure A). In these SMDCs, the FAP ligand enables the conjugates efficiently accumulate in tumor tissues, while the tumor bioreductive microenvironment facilitates the reduction of Pt­(IV) prodrug to the active Pt­(II) drug and simultaneously releases the prototypic STING agonist MSA-2. In the meantime, the released Pt­(II) drug can induce DNA damage, which provides additional stimuli for STING activation, thus amplifying the immune response (Figure B). This Pt­(IV)-esterified FAP-targeting STING agonist conjugate represents a new generation of SMDC featuring FAP-enabled tumor-specific binding and killing as well as bioreductive control of immune activation, thus may achieve enhanced antitumor efficacy and elevated safety profile. Herein, we report the structural design and pharmacological characterization of FAP-targeting platinum­(IV)-centered STING agonist conjugates.

Chemical Synthesis of Pt­(IV) Conjugates 9 and 13

The synthesis of compounds 9 and 13 is outlined in Scheme . Coupling reaction of MSA-2 with N-hydroxysuccinimide under EDCI afforded ester 2 in 80% yield, which was further reacted with dihydroxido Pt­(IV) complexes (4 and 7) obtained through oxidization of cisplatin or oxaliplatin by H2O2 at 60 °C. Inhibitory Activities of Conjugates 9 and 13 against FAP

Since the FAP-targeting ability of these Pt­(IV) conjugates is critical for their tumor specificity and cell uptake via FAP-mediated endocytosis, we first investigated the enzymatic inhibitory activity of conjugates 9 and 13 against FAP in a cell-based fluorescent assay with a fluorescent agent Suc-Gly-Pro-AMC as the FAP substrate. In this assay, the FAP-overexpressing U87MG cell line was used. As shown in Table , conjugate 9 with the cisplatin-based scaffold exhibited a comparable FAP inhibitory activity to that of the FAP inhibitor OncoFAP (Half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50), 49.56 vs 36.73 nM), while conjugate 13 displayed much weaker potency with an IC50 value of 303.5 nM, which is likely ascribed to a bulkier oxaliplatin scaffold that hindered its binding with FAP. Therefore, cisplatin-based complex 9 was selected for subsequent evaluation and structural optimization.

Reduction and Stability Properties of Conjugate 9

Since our SMDCs were designed to exert anticancer activity through the released STING agonist MSA-2 and the cytotoxic Pt­(II) drug cisplatin in the tumor microenvironment, we then measured the MSA-2 releasing ability of conjugate 9. As shown in Figure , under reducing conditions (10 mM sodium ascorbate in PBS/CH3OH, 9:1 v/v, pH 7.4) that mimic the tumor microenvironment, the HPLC signal for 9 (retention time, RT = 10.5 min) decreased over time. This was accompanied by the concomitant appearance of peaks corresponding to released MSA-2 (RT = 9.5 min) and OncoFAP (RT = 8.0 min). The reduction proceeded in a time-dependent manner and was complete within 6 h (Figure B). To assess specificity and exclude the possibility of nonspecific release, we evaluated the stability of 9 in PBS (pH 7.4) and in a cell culture medium (RPMI 1640). Compound 9 remained stable in both matrices for at least 24 h (Figure A). These results suggest that both MSA-2 and OncoFAP could be readily released from conjugate 9 under the reductive conditions.

FAP Inhibitory and STING Activation Activities of Conjugates 17a–c, 21a–c, 27a–c, 32, and 39

All the newly synthesized Pt­(IV) conjugates were evaluated for their FAP inhibitory activities using a cell-based fluorescent assay in U87MG human glioblastoma cells, which is widely used as a model cell for evaluating cellular FAP inhibitory activity due to endogenous overexpression of FAP. , The preliminary conjugate 9 was used as a comparison. As illustrated in Table , most of the tested conjugates demonstrated modest to high FAP inhibitory activity with nanomolar IC50 values. The linker length was found to be an important factor for the inhibitory activity. Conjugate 17b with two ethylene glycol units (ten-atom length) displayed potent inhibitory activity with an IC50 value of 38 nM against FAP, shortening (17a) or extending (17c) the linker length impaired activity. A similar structure–activity relationship (SAR) was also found in analogues 21a-c bearing alkyl linkers with different lengths. Conjugate 21b with a ten-carbon chain length displayed an IC50 value of 49 nM against FAP, while 21a with a shorter linker and 21c with a longer linker exhibited much weaker potency against FAP with IC50 values of 113 nM and >500 nM, respectively. Conjugates 27a and 27b with triazole alkyl linkers (eight- or ten-atom length) displayed comparable inhibitory activities to that of 9 with IC50 values of 67 and 57 nM, respectively. However, further increasing the length of the triazole alkyl linker decreased the activity (27c). Replacement of the alkyl linker in 27b with the oxa-alkyl linker (32) or the rigid piperidinyl ring linker (39) almost retains the FAP activity. Taken together, the Pt­(IV) SMDCs 17b and 21b with a ten-atom length glycol and alkyl linker showed compatible inhibitory activity against FAP, compared to the preliminary conjugate 9 or oncoFAP. In the meantime, we also used the THP1-dual cells bearing an IFN-stimulated response element (ISRE) promoter to test the effects of these conjugates on STING signaling and found that they did not activate STING/IRF3 signaling in THP1-dual cells. In contrast, the STING agonist MSA-2 (2) demonstrated more than 181-fold activation (Table ).

In Vitro Cellular Antiproliferative Activity

After confirming their potent FAP inhibition of conjugates 9, 17b, and 21b, we next investigated their antiproliferative activity against tumor cells to reflect their capability in releasing the cytotoxic Pt­(II) species. Two murine tumor cell lines MC38 and CT26 were selected for the CCK8 assay, and cisplatin was used as the positive control. As shown in Table , under normoxia conditions (20% O2), all of the tested conjugates exhibited much weak cytotoxicity with IC50 values ranging from 6.83 to 224.9 μM. However, under the hypoxia condition (1% O2) to mimic the progressive solid tumor microenvironment, the cellular cytotoxicity of all tested conjugates was significantly enhanced by 1.5- to 38-fold, compared to those under normoxia, with IC50 values ranging from 0.83 to 28.43 μM. Among them, 21b displayed the most potent antiproliferative effects against all tested tumor cells with IC50 values around 1.0 μM, which is 6- to 9-fold more potent than under normoxic condition. The enhanced antiproliferative activity is likely attributable to the hypoxic environment, which induced reductive cleavage of the conjugate and released cytotoxic cisplatin, thereby promoting cell death. Furthermore, we compared the cytotoxicity of 21b and cisplatin toward normal cells (L02, L929, and HaCaT cells) under normoxic condition. As shown in Table S1, 21b exhibited negligible cytotoxicity against normal cells with IC50 values greater than 100 μM, in striking contrast to the pronounced toxicity induced by cisplatin. Encouraged by these results, conjugate 21b was selected for further evaluation.

Conjugate 21b Activated STING Pathway and Induced Cell Apoptosis under Reductive Conditions

The Pt­(IV) conjugate 21b was designed to codeliver the STING agonist MSA-2 and cytotoxic Pt­(II) species into the tumor microenvironment, thereby simultaneously inducing STING activation and apoptosis. To test this mechanism, we first assessed whether 21b could activate the STING pathway under reductive conditions. In THP-1 cells pretreated with sodium ascorbateused to mimic the reductive tumor microenvironment21b (0.01 and 0.1 μM) dose-dependently triggered phosphorylation of STING and its downstream effectors TBK1 and IRF3 (Figure A). By contrast, without ascorbate pretreatment, 21b failed to activate STING signaling, indicating that reduction is required for agonist release and pathway initiation. Next, we evaluated the effect of 21b on apoptotic markers in CT26 colon cancer cells. Without sodium ascorbate, 21b had marginal effect on the expression of caspase-3, cleaved caspase-3, or cytochrome c. In the presence of sodium ascorbate, however, 21b promoted caspase-3 cleavage and significantly increased Cyt C levels (Figure B), consistent with induction of the apoptotic cascade under reductive conditions. Further supporting this, treatment of sodium ascorbate-pretreated CT26 cells with increasing concentrations of 21b (0–40 μM) led to dose-dependent apoptosis after 24 h (Figure C–F). Taken together, these data demonstrate that under reductive tumor-like conditions, conjugate 21b not only activates the STING pathway but also effectively triggers cancer cell apoptosis.

Binding Affinity for Human FAP, Cellular Uptake, and In Vivo Distribution of 21b

To measure if 21b potently binds to FAP protein to ensure its specific accumulation within tumor tissues, we measured its binding affinity with human FAP (hFAP). As shown in Figure A, conjugate 21b displayed a remarkable binding affinity to hFAP with a K D value of 0.88 nM in the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay. To further assess whether 21b accumulates in hFAP-overexpressing cells, we performed inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP–MS) to measure the platinum (Pt) content in hFAP-expressing mouse embryonic fibroblasts (3T3-hFAP) after treatment with 21b, using cisplatin as a control. As depicted in Figure B, the Pt content in 21b-treated 3T3-hFAP cells was quantified as 39.1 ng of Pt per 106 cells, which was much higher than that in the cisplatin-treated cells, indicating that 21b could be efficiently accumulated in 3T3-hFAP cells. Notably, when the cells pretreated with UAMC-1110 (a known FAP inhibitor, 0.2 μM) to block the binding of 21b to FAP, the intracellular Pt uptake was significantly reduced to the level of 20.6 ng Pt per 106 cells (Figure B), indicating that conjugate 21b preferably accumulated in 3T3-hFAP cells likely through FAP-mediated endocytosis.

To evaluate the in vivo tumor-targeting efficacy of conjugate 21b, we assessed its biodistribution in a syngeneic mouse model bearing CT26 colon tumors coimplanted with FAP-expressing NIH/3T3 fibroblasts. Pt levels in tumor and major organs were quantified using ICP–MS. 21b was administered at 10 mg/kg, and mice were euthanized at 8 and 24 h postinjection (Figure C). As shown in Figure D, 21b preferentially accumulated in tumor tissue over other organs, exhibiting a notably high tumor-to-blood ratio (12.6:1) at 8 h. By 24 h, tumor Pt levels decreased to amounts comparable to those in the spleenyet remained higher than in all other tissues examined (Figure D). These findings indicate that 21b retains in vivo targeting specificity toward FAP-expressing tumor tissue.

In Vivo Antitumor Efficacy and Toxicity of Compound 21b

The stability of Pt­(IV) complex is very critical to reduce off-tumor toxicity caused by early cleavage in blood plasma. Therefore, before in vivo antitumor study, conjugate 21b was investigated for its stability in human and mouse blood plasma by using propantheline as the control. As shown in Table S2, conjugate 21b displayed acceptable stability in both mouse and human plasma with T 1/2 of 136 and 866 min, respectively. We further investigated the pharmacokinetic properties of compound 21b in mice after intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 20 mg/kg. As shown in Table S3, it exhibited a half-life time (T 1/2) of 8.92 h, a peak plasma concentration (C max) of 54.8 ng/mL, a plasma exposure (AUCInf) of 383 h·ng/mL together with mean residence time of 11.0 h, indicating the overall PK properties of 21b are roughly suitable for further in vivo anticancer evaluation.

Human and mouse FAP share approximately 89% amino acid sequence identity with highly conserved catalytic and ligand-binding domains, and OncoFAP has been validated to exhibit potent cross-reactivity with both human and mouse FAP, supporting the use of mouse tumor models for in vivo studies. Therefore, we evaluated the antitumor efficacy of 21b in a Balb/c mouse model that bears CT26 colon tumors. When tumors reached an average volume of 50 mm3, mice were randomized into six treatment groups (n = 8 per group) and were administered intraperitoneal injections every other day for a total of seven doses (Figure A). The groups received equimolar amounts of 21b (40 mg/kg), the STING agonist MSA-2 (10 mg/kg), the FAP inhibitor OncoFAP (15 mg/kg), cisplatin (10 mg/kg), a combination of MSA-2, OncoFAP, and cisplatin, or a vehicle. As shown in Figure E, cisplatin alone and the triple-drug combination proved highly toxic, with all mice in these groups dying by day 4 and day 7 posttreatment, respectively. In contrast, mice treated with a vehicle, 21b, MSA-2, or OncoFAP alone survived throughout the study period (Figure E). Treatment with 21b significantly inhibited tumor growth (Figure B), achieving 58% tumor growth inhibition (TGI) by final tumor weight (Figure D,F), without inducing significant body weight loss (Figure C). In comparison, equimolar doses of MSA-2 or OncoFAP alone produced only minimal antitumor effects (TGI of 17.34% and 20.40%, respectively; Figure D,F). Together, these findings demonstrate that conjugate 21b exhibits potent synergistic antitumor activity while substantially reducing systemic toxicity relative to that of its individual components or their combination.

It has been reported that the STING agonist could stimulate the STING/IFN innate immune pathway to induce the infiltration of CD8+ T cells into the tumor tissue. To validate whether 21b amplified the STING-mediated antitumor immune response, an immunohistochemical assay of tumor tissues from the treated mice was performed to examine the effects on CD8+ T cells. As shown in Figure G,H, 21b displayed an enhanced ability to induce the CD8+ T-cell infiltration when compared to that by MSA-2, suggesting conjugation of MSA-2 with Pt­(IV) exhibited an amplification effect in STING-mediated antitumor immune response. In addition, the proportion of FAP+ cells in the tumor tissue was significantly decreased (Figure G,I), which might be ascribed to the FAP inhibition by the OncoFAP moiety of 21b.

In the meantime, we determined whether conjugate 21b could stimulate the STING pathway selectively within the tumor microenvironment and thereby minimize systemic immune activation, we compared its effects on downstream cytokines (IP-10 and TNF-α) in tumor tissue and blood serum with those of the free agonist MSA-2. As shown in Figure J, 21b significantly increased TNF-α and IP-10 within tumors, consistent with its preferential tumor accumulation. In contrast, serum cytokine levels remained unchanged compared to vehicle, indicating localized STING activation without systemic elevation. These results suggest that 21b selectively activates intratumoral STING signaling with a reduced risk of cytokine storm. Conversely, MSA-2 induced markedly higher serum levels of both cytokines compared to vehicle, while the cytokine levels in tumor tissues remained similar to vehicle. This profile indicates widespread systemic STING activation and highlights the potential cytokine-storm risk associated with the free agonist.

In addition, we assessed the toxicity profile of 21b (40 mg/kg) in Balb/c mice without bearing tumor cells at 48 h postadministration and compared the toxicity with that of the combination administration of MSA-2 (10 mg/kg), FAP ligand OncoFAP (15 mg/kg), and cisplatin (10 mg/kg). At the end point of this experiment, the blood serum was collected for the analysis of biochemical markers including liver damage markers aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and renal function markers uric acid (UA) and blood urea nitrogen (U-BUN). As shown in Figure S1, serum levels of all tested biochemical markers of the 21b group were comparable to those of the control group without significant variations, indicating low liver and renal damage. In contrast, the levels of ALT, AST, UA, and U-BUN in the combination group were significantly varied, indicating a potential liver and renal toxic risk. Furthermore, the histopathological examination of the heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney showed no obvious tissue damage, inflammation, or pathological lesions in the 21b-treatment group. By comparison, the mice in the combination group displayed significant structural damage and tissue abnormalities, especially in the liver and kidney. These data suggested the low toxicity of 21b.

Conclusions

Tumor-specific activation of the STING pathway is crucial for maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity. To achieve this concept, we developed a new class of small-molecule drug conjugates (SMDCs) that target tumor-specific FAP. These conjugates are constructed by esterification of a dihydroxyl Pt­(IV) species with the FAP inhibitor OncoFAP and the STING agonist MSA-2. Among these SMDCs, conjugate 21b demonstrated picomolar binding affinity (KD = 883 pM) and high inhibitory potency against FAP (IC50 = 49.99 nM). Besides, it preferentially accumulated in 3T3-hFAP cells. Under reductive conditions, this conjugate activates the STING pathway in immune cells and induces apoptosis in colon cancer cells. In immunocompetent CT-26 tumor-bearing mice, 21b mainly distributed in tumor tissue and exhibited potent antitumor activity (57.8% tumor growth inhibition) with markedly reduced systemic toxicity when compared to its individual components or their combination. Mechanistically, 21b enhanced intratumoral infiltration of CD8+ T cells while reducing the population of immunosuppressive, FAP+ cancer-associated fibroblasts. Meanwhile, 21b significantly elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IP-10) within tumors without increasing their systemic levels in serumconfirming localized STING activation and a minimized risk of cytokine storm. This is very distinction from previously reported Pt­(IV)-STING prodrugs. − Conjugate 21b also possesses significant potential for the treatment of immunologically “cold” tumors and those resistant to immune checkpoint blockade, and may act synergistically with anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 antibodies to further enhance antitumor efficacy, underscoring its high translational potential in clinical cancer immunotherapy.

In summary, we have designed an FAP-targeted, Pt­(IV)-based SMDC platform that codelivers a STING agonist and a cytotoxic agent specifically to tumor tissues. Our design represents the first example of an FAP-targeted Pt­(IV)-STING conjugate, which enables tumor-localized immune activation and cell death, offering a promising approach to enhance antitumor immunity while avoiding the systemic toxicity associated with conventional STING agonists.
