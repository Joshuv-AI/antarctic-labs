A Renormalizable and Unitary Approach to Quantum Gravity This essay received an Honorable Mention in the 2026 Essay Competition of the Gravity Research Foundation.
D. G. C. McKeon, F. T. Brandt, J. Frenkel, S. Martins-Filho

Abstract
A Lagrange multiplier field restricts the quantum corrections to the Einstein–Hilbert action at one-loop order, yielding a model that is renormalizable and unitary while reproducing the Einstein field equations in the classical limit.

Quantum gravity; renormalizability; perturbation theory; quantum field theory

Quantum mechanics and general relativity are the cornerstones of our current understanding of physics — yet these theories do not appear to work well together. In particular, the non-renormalizability of general relativity at high-energies seems to be inconsistent with local quantum field theory. There have been many attempts to solve this fundamental problem. It has been suggested that by extending the number of physical fields through supersymmetric coupling (supergravity)
[
1
]
, extending the concept of particles to strings
[
2
]
, adjusting the canonical structure of the theory (loop gravity)
[
3
]
, invoking non-perturbative behavior (asymptotic safety)
[
4
,
5
]
, or by including extra terms into the basic Einstein-Hilbert action
[
6
,
7
]
, one may obtain a realistic theory of quantum gravity. Although much progress has been made along these lines, these approaches have not yet proven to be entirely satisfactory.

When the Faddeev-Popov (FP) quantization procedure is applied to the
Einstein-Hilbert (EH) action, then at one-loop order the on-shell
condition eliminates ultraviolet divergences
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Unfortunately, renormalizability is lost beyond one-loop order,
[
11
,
12
]
or if matter fields are coupled to the graviton
[
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

For some time, it has been known that by using a
Lagrange multiplier (LM) field
\lambda
to ensure that the classical
equations of motion are satisfied, all radiative effects beyond
one-loop order are eliminated
[
16
]
. This suggests that a
LM field used in conjunction with the EH action can result in
the action being consistent with renormalizability
and unitarity while retaining standard General Relativity (GR)
in the classical limit, and at the same time allowing the
standard bosonic matter fields to couple to the metric.
A series of papers develops this approach
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]

and in this essay we will outline the steps used.

By considering a simple real integral, the essence of this
approach to the path integral (PI) used to quantize this model
is illustrated.
If
L(g)
is a sufficiently regular function of the
N
real variables
g_{i}
, with isolated non-degenerate stationary points, we find by integrating first over
\lambda
, then using the resulting
\delta
-function to integrate over
g_{i}

I\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}g_{i}\,\mathrm{d}^{N}\lambda_{i}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\left[\det\left(\frac{\partial^{2}L(g_{k})}{\partial g_{i}\,\partial g_{j}}\right)\right]^{1/2}\exp i\left[L(g_{k})+\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial L(g_{k})}{\partial g_{i}}\right],

(1)

can be written as

I\;=\;\sum_{A}\frac{\exp\bigl[i\,L\!\bigl(g_{k}^{A}\bigr)\bigr]}{\det^{1/2}\!\bigl[\partial^{2}L\!\bigl(g_{k}^{A}\bigr)/(\partial g_{i}\,\partial g_{j})\bigr]},

(2)

where
g_{k}^{A}
satisfies the condition

\frac{\partial L\!\bigl(g_{k}^{A}\bigr)}{\partial g_{i}}\;=\;0.

(3)

If the finite-dimensional integral in Eq. (
1

with

\lambda_{k}
a

that solves the classical equation of motion
(

correspond to diagrams beyond one-loop order.
The role of the factor

\det^{1/2}\bigl[\partial^{2}L(g_{k})/(\partial g_{i}\,\partial g_{k})\bigr]

and
\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle Z(j_{i},J_{i})=\int

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}g_{i}\,\mathcal{D}\lambda_{i}}\biggl[\det\biggl(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(g_{k})}{\partial g_{i}\,\partial g_{j}}\biggr)\biggr]^{\!1/2}

\displaystyle\times\exp\Biggl\{i\!\int d^{n}x\,\Bigl[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(g_{k})+\lambda_{i}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(g_{k})}{\partial g_{i}}+j_{i}g_{i}+J_{i}\lambda_{i}\Bigr]\Biggr\}.

(4)

Following Ref.
[

\bar{g}_{i}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial W}{\partial j_{i}},\qquad\bar{\lambda}_{i}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial W}{\partial J_{i}},

(5)

where
W(j_{i},J_{i})=-i\ln Z(j_{i},J_{i})

\bar{g}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i}

\displaystyle e^{i\Gamma[\bar{g},\bar{\lambda}]}=\int\!\mathcal{D}h_{i}\,\mathcal{D}H_{i}\;\det\nolimits^{1/2}\Bigl(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}}{\partial h_{i}\,\partial h_{j}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigl\{\,i\!\int d^{n}x\,\bigl[\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}(\bar{g}_{i}+h_{i})+(\bar{\lambda}_{i}+H_{i})\partial_{h_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}(\bar{g}_{i}+h_{i})+j_{i}h_{i}+J_{i}H_{i}\bigr]\Bigr\},

where now

The quantum fluctuations around the background fields
\bar{g}_{i}
and

\int d^{n}x\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}\bigl(g^{i}\bigr)

(7)

\int d^{n}x\,[\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}+\lambda_{i}\,\partial_{g_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}]

(8a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle\to\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{m}\,\frac{\partial R_{ij}(g)}{\partial g_{m}}\,\xi^{j}.

(8b)

Applying the FP quantization procedure adds to the
exponent in Eq. (

together with the usual
FP-ghost contribution.

If the gauge fixing

\displaystyle\exp\bigl[i\Gamma(\bar{g}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})\bigr]=\int\!\mathcal{D}h_{i}\,\mathcal{D}H_{i}\;\det\nolimits^{1/2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial h_{i}\partial h_{j}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{g}+h)-\frac{1}{\alpha}\,F_{ik}(\bar{g})h_{k}F_{i\ell}(\bar{g})\,h_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\times\det\bigl[F_{ij}(\bar{g})\,R_{jk}(\bar{g}+h)\bigr]\;\exp\Biggl\{i\!\int d^{n}x\,\biggl[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{g}+h)+\bigl(\bar{\lambda}_{i}+H_{i}\bigr)\,\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}}(g)}{\partial g_{i}}\right|_{g=\bar{g}+h}

(9)

The gauge fixing term to the exponential involves the introduction of a gauge parameter
\alpha
.

Bosonic matter fields
\Phi_{I}
can be coupled to the gauge fields

g_{i}
through a conventional Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{M}(\Phi_{I},g_{i})
, provided the gauge
transformation (
7

\Phi_{I}\to\Phi_{I}+R_{Ij}(\Phi)\,\xi^{j}
,
which leaves
\int d^{n}x\,\mathcal{L}_{M}(\Phi_{I},g_{i})
invariant.
We do not introduce an LM field to restrict the radiative effects of

\Phi_{I}
to one-loop order, as that would generate Landau-pole
singularities in the running gauge coupling
[
16
]
.

We now outline two approaches to the radiative corrections implied by
Eq. (
9

H_{i}
is performed first, leaving a functional

\delta
-function,

\mathop{\delta}\bigl({\partial\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}(\bar{g}+h)}/{\partial h_{i}}-{1}/{\alpha}\,F_{ij}(\bar{g})F_{jk}(\bar{g})\,h_{k}\bigr)
,
after which the sources
j_{i}
and
J_{i}
are set to zero and the integration over
h_{i}
is carried out.
This leads to the closed form
[

\displaystyle\exp\bigl[i\,\Gamma(\bar{g}_{i},0)\bigr]=\sum_{A}\det\nolimits^{-1/2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial h_{i}\,\partial h_{j}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}(\bar{g}+h^{A})-\frac{1}{\alpha}F_{ik}(\bar{g})\,h_{k}^{A}F_{i\ell}(\bar{g})\,h_{\ell}^{A}\right)\;

(10)

\displaystyle\times\det\bigl[F_{ij}(\bar{g})\,R_{jk}(\bar{g}+h^{A})\bigr]\exp\left\{\,i\!\int d^{n}x\,\Bigl[\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}(\bar{g}+h^{A})-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\bigl(F_{ij}(\bar{g})\,h_{j}^{A}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\right\},

where
h_{i}^{A}
comes from the
\delta

The background field
\bar{g}_{i}
need not satisfy the classical equation of motion.

A second approach to Eq. (
9

rules for the fields
h_{i}
and
H_{i}
and analyze the resulting
diagrams.
The terms bilinear in these fields can be written in the form

\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}h_{i}&H_{i}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{ij}&a_{ij}\\
a_{ij}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h_{j}\\
H_{j}\end{pmatrix}.

(11)

Inverting this matrix, we obtain the propagators

\begin{pmatrix}a_{ij}&a_{ij}\\
a_{ij}&0\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a^{-1}_{ij}\\
a^{-1}_{ij}&-a^{-1}_{ij}\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

Thus
the propagator for the field
h_{i}
vanishes, the propagator of the LM field

H_{i}
carries the “wrong sign”,
and
the mixed propagator for
h_{i}
-
H_{i}
is simply

, the
same as would occur for
h_{i}
alone if
H_{i}

H_{i}
enters

Combining those one-loop diagrams with the contribution from the first
functional determinant in Eq. (

the one-loop diagrams that would arise if no LM field were present.
The second determinant is

This diagrammatic approach does not determine the explicit contribution of
h_{i}^{A}
in Eq. (
10

Those classical solutions

could be said to have “dark-matter-like”
properties: they couple only through the propagation of
g_{i}
and

\Phi_{I}

We now specialize to the Einstein-Hilbert (EH) Lagrangian. Replacing

\mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}
by
\mathcal{L}_{\text{EH}}

\int d^{n}x\,[\mathcal{L}_{\text{EH}}(g)+\lambda_{i}\partial_{g_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{EH}}(g)]\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl[-g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}(g)+\lambda^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}\bigr],

(13)

with
\kappa^{2}=16\pi G_{N}
,
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}(g)-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R(g)
is the Einstein tensor and our sign conventions are such that

R^{\rho}_{~\;\sigma\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\,\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\,\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\mu}+\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\sigma\nu}\,\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\mu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\sigma\mu}\,\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\nu}
.

In the case of the Einstein–Hilbert action, the generic gauge transformation (
7
) corresponds to infinitesimal diffeomorphisms of the metric, given by

\delta g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\rho\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}+g_{\mu\rho}\,\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}+\xi^{\rho}\,\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}.

(14)

(15)

with
\varepsilon=2-\tfrac{n}{2}
.

Since

R_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2-n}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\lambda\sigma}G^{\lambda\sigma},

(16)

the divergences in Eq. (

background field
\bar{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
that accompanies

\lambda_{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (

Renormalizing the background field
\bar{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
to eliminate

distinct

in which the couplings, masses and fields that characterize a theory
absorb divergences.

To illustrate unitarity when an LM field is present,
consider a massive real scalar field

It has long been known that EH gravity is one-loop renormalizable only

on shell
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. This requirement is absent in QED or
Yang-Mills, which are renormalizable off shell
[
30
]
. In our
approach, the LM field
couples to the classical
equations of motion, so
divergences
are eliminated by absorbing them into the LM field leaving the
gravitational coupling
\kappa^{2}
untouched. Consequently
\kappa^{2}

does not “run” with the renormalization scale

\mu^{2}
, unlike the QED or Yang-Mills
coupling.
The LM field itself, and the parameters of the matter sector and the
cosmological constant,
do
depend on the scale
\mu^{2}
and vary as

\mu^{2}
changes.

Applying the same LM mechanism to Einstein-Cartan gravity should likewise suppress multi-loop graphs, yielding a renormalizable, unitary coupling of fermions to gravitons and bringing gravity into the Standard-Model fold. This issue is currently being investigated.
