Design and effectiveness evaluation of a web-based microplatform for mindfulness intervention for organ donor family members
Authors: Yuanxin Cao, Sitong Huang, Shufeng Kong, Yibo Yang, Wenzhao Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOrgan transplantation technology provides life-saving treatment for patients with end-stage organ failure. However, family members of donors face psychological harm due to the urgent decision-making process regarding organ donation and the associated stigmatization, highlighting an urgent need for mental health support.MethodsThis study used the User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS) to screen domestic mindfulness-related applications, analyzed the functions of high-quality applications, and combined with previous surveys, designed and developed a web-based mindfulness intervention micro-platform for families of organ donors based on the Information-Motivation-Behavioral Skills (IMB) model. The effectiveness of the micro-platform was verified through a pre-post self-controlled trial.ResultsThe platform consists of seven functional modules: “Understanding Mindfulness”, “Psychological Science”, “Practice Room”, “Community Exchange”, “Psychological Quiz”, “Daily Mindfulness” and “Personal Center”, and provides professional mindfulness courses and mental health education resources. The results of the effectiveness evaluation indicate that the platform has a positive effect on alleviating negative emotions such as Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), depression and anxiety among family members.ConclusionsThe present study has developed a web-based mindfulness intervention micro-platform for families of organ donors, which is effective in alleviating negative emotions such as Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), depression and anxiety among family members, as well as in improving sleep quality.

Introduction

Organ transplantation is hailed as the pinnacle of 21st-century medicine, offering hope of recovery to many patients suffering from end-stage organ failure. However, the shortage of donor organs remains a critical bottleneck hindering the development of organ transplantation, and is a challenge faced by the entire world. According to statistics, only around 173,000 organ transplant operations are performed globally each year (Global Observatory on Donation and Transplantation, 2026), and the actual number of donations falls far short of demand. As of 9 June 2026, China had recorded 66,799 organ donations, yielding 208,032 donated organs and saving the lives of over 200,000 patients (China Organ Donation Administrative Center, 2026); yet the shortfall remains substantial. The World Health Organization (WHO), the Transplantation Society (TTS) and the International Society for Organ Donation and Procurement (ISODP) have jointly formulated a strategic plan to develop post-mortem organ donation among the general public. In October 2023, China promulgated the Regulations on Human Organ Donation and Transplantation. And in May 2024, China issued the ‘Opinions on Promoting the Healthy Development of Human Organ Donation’, setting a target of achieving a per million population donation rate of 8 nationwide within the next five years. Like most countries worldwide, China requires family consent as a prerequisite for organ donation following death. In addition, researchers are actively exploring technologies such as 3D bioprinting, the construction of bioartificial livers based on stem cells (Li et al., 2023), and machine perfusion (Canizares et al., 2024) to alleviate the organ supply shortage. Due to factors such as limited knowledge and traditional beliefs, obtaining consent for organ donation from the bereaved families in China is inherently a huge challenge (He et al., 2025). Clinical practice has found that a large number of donation cases involve patients who have died from accidental injuries or unexpected diseases. In other words, most donor families have just experienced the sudden death of a loved one and are required to make a donation decision in a short period of time. This situation often leads to psychological stress reactions, causing certain psychological harm. In severe cases, it can result in a variety of psychological disorders, including depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder (Yang et al., 2026). Some scholars have found that the prevalence of depression among families of organ donors in China is approximately 31%, and the prevalence of anxiety is 43% (China Organ Donation Administrative Center, 2022). The prevalence of depression and anxiety among donor families is 10 to 15 times higher than that of the general population, and their mental health needs urgent attention and emphasis. Although studies both domestically and internationally have explored the psychological conditions of donor families, for instance, Smudla et al. found that 72.4% of Hungarian organ donor families exhibited symptoms of depression (Smudla et al., 2012), empirical research and psychological intervention models are still insufficient. Psychological interventions can be divided into early, acute, and long-term phases (Yang et al., 2016). However, existing research primarily focuses on the early stage, that is, the donation process and the first week after donation. Although theoretical discussions on intervention strategies are relatively abundant, empirical intervention studies are still insufficient, and a universally applicable intervention model has yet to be established.

Mindfulness, as an important component of psychological intervention, has gradually been integrated into mainstream therapeutic systems. With the widespread promotion and application of mindfulness interventions globally, they have demonstrated a significant regulatory role in psychological interventions. This role is not only evident in intervention studies targeting individuals with mental illnesses but is also applicable to healthy populations (Mirabito and Verhaeghen, 2023). Numerous studies have confirmed that mindfulness has significant therapeutic effects, particularly in the treatment of various clinical conditions, including mood disorders such as depression (Ruork et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2025). However, traditional face-to-face mindfulness training models are costly and face a relative shortage of qualified instructors. Meanwhile, the number of organ donations in China is on the rise, and many of these families bear heavy economic burdens. Coupled with the insufficient, imbalanced, and regionally uneven mental health service capacity in China, these factors make face-to-face mental health interventions a significant challenge for families of organ donors. Therefore, there is an urgent need to find an effective medium for delivering mindfulness interventions to assist families of organ donors who, for various reasons, are unable to proactively seek professional help or are prone to interrupting their treatment.

In December 2025, the World Health Organization (WHO) published an updated version of the Global Digital Health Strategy 2020–2027, aimed at encouraging countries to adopt digital health tools to achieve universal health coverage (World Health Organization, 2025). Internet technology has broadened the channels for mental health interventions and can leverage its technical advantages to compensate for the lack of professional resources. The effectiveness of digital mental health interventions has now been confirmed by a large number of studies (Ratheesh and Alvarez-Jimenez, 2022). Interventions based on smartphone applications may further break the temporal and spatial constraints of training, enhance training motivation, and broaden the scope of application (Jiang et al., 2019). When individuals face psychological issues but lack access to professional therapists, or are hesitant to seek help due to societal stigma surrounding mental illness, digital mental health interventions can make treatment more portable and significantly reduce the cost of therapy.

This paper investigates high-quality mindfulness-related applications in the Chinese app market and designs the functional structure of a web-based mindfulness intervention micro-platform for families of organ donors, based on the Information-Motivation-Behavioral Skill Model (IMB Model). Combining the preliminary analysis of the psychological intervention needs of organ donor families and referring to professional materials, we constructed the content framework of a web-based mindfulness intervention micro-platform for organ donor families and developed this platform. A pre-post self-controlled trial was employed to verify the effectiveness of the mindfulness intervention micro-platform for organ donor families.

Method

Web survey respondents and instruments

Web survey respondents

In November 2023, using the professional mobile application data analysis platform in China—Qimai Data, we conducted searches for relevant applications in both the Android (Google) and iOS (Apple) app stores using the keywords “mindfulness” and “meditation.” Additionally, we collected relevant WeChat mini-programs by searching for keywords such as “mindfulness” and “meditation”. The inclusion criteria for the applications ultimately selected in this study (Fig. 1) are as follows: the application should (1) be related to mindfulness and meditation; (2) use Simplified Chinese; (3) be available for use in Mainland China. The exclusion criteria are: (1) duplication; (2) relevance to games; (3) inability to download.Fig. 1Mindfulness application screening process.Fig. 1

Mindfulness application screening process.

Among the 563 applications, duplicates were excluded in the initial screening, resulting in 397 applications. Of these, 9 were mobile games, 16 were in languages other than Chinese, and 91 had content unrelated to mindfulness. After downloading and attempting to install the remaining 281 applications, 5 failed to download or launch properly. Ultimately, 276 relevant applications met all the study criteria and were assessed for quality.

Web-based survey tools

(1)The User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS)

The User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS)

This study conducted a quality assessment of applications using the User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS). The uMARS consists of an Objective Quality Scale and a Subjective Quality Scale, with 20 objective items and 6 subjective items. Each item is rated on a 5-point scale, where 1 point indicates poor, 2 points indicate fair, 3 points indicate acceptable, 4 points indicate good, and 5 points indicate excellent. The Objective Quality Scale is composed of four sections: engagement, functionality, aesthetics and information. The overall score and the score range for each specific section are from 0 to 5, with higher scores indicating better application quality. In terms of quality rating, a score of 3 is the minimum acceptable standard for applications, while a score above 4 indicates relatively high quality (Stec et al., 2019). The scale has demonstrated excellent reliability, with a Cronbach's α coefficient as high as 0.90, indicating good internal consistency (Schoeppe et al., 2017).(2)Mindfulness Application Information Extraction Form

(2)Mindfulness Application Information Extraction Form

Mindfulness Application Information Extraction Form

This study selected market-related functions for research based on previous analyses (Yin et al., 2020) and modified them according to the characteristics of the included applications. The investigation of mindfulness-related applications in this study was conducted from two aspects: basic information and specific information. Since the iOS App Store does not display download volumes, relevant data for iOS applications were not recorded.

Quality control

Before rating the applications, evaluators were required to thoroughly read and understand the content of the uMARS and follow the consensus-based rating criteria to ensure objectivity and accuracy. To avoid subjective bias, this study removed the subjective quality dimension and randomly assigned the applications to four evaluators (two groups evaluating Android and iOS applications, respectively). Evaluators independently used the uMARS, spending over 10 min on each application. Two evaluators within the same group rated the application separately, and the average of their scores was taken as the final quality score. If an application had versions for both operating systems, both were evaluated. Considering that operating system updates might affect application versions and functions, each version was treated as an independent application. To ensure the validity of the study, high-quality applications were selected as research subjects. Two evaluators independently conducted in-depth analyses and collected functional information, which was then integrated and summarized. During the evaluation process, a specially designed extraction form was used to comprehensively and systematically distill application information.

Intervention

Recruitment and eligibility criteria

The study subjects were family members of organ donors after the death of a citizen who had completed organ donation, including immediate family members such as the organ donor's spouse, adult children and parents. Study subjects were recruited through posters.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Family members who had participated in the organ donation decision-making process and for whom the organ donation had been completed; (2) Aged 18 years or older; (3) Capable of using smartphones to access online information; (4) Provided informed consent to participate in the study.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Family members of organ donors diagnosed with intellectual disabilities or specific learning disorders; (2) Family members currently receiving other psychiatric treatments or participating in other intervention programs.

Withdrawal Criteria: (1) loss of visit: Participants provided false or inaccurate contact information, resulting in an inability to maintain contact; (2) Persistent psychological deterioration during the study period, necessitating alternative interventions or medication; (3) Voluntary withdrawal from the study due to personal reasons.

Result

Web survey of mindfulness-related apps in China

Between November 20 and November 26, 2023, four evaluators conducted a comprehensive and systematic quality assessment of a total of 276 Mindfulness-Related apps with the help of the User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS). A scoring threshold was set to select apps with scores ≥ 3.5 for further information extraction.

Out of the total 64 Mindfulness-Related apps, 8 (12.5%) apps are available completely free of cost. Most of the IOS apps (88%) charged their users a partial fee. In terms of target users, 23 apps (35.9%) were aimed at adults and teens, and 39 apps (60.9%) were aimed at general adults only. Nearly 60% of the apps reported relevant information security measures. The content composition of 42.2% (27/64) of the apps was based on psychological theories or on the advice of certified professionals, including psychologists, psychotherapists, or psychiatrists. In terms of counseling features, the majority of apps (82.8%) did not provide relevant features. Only 10 (17%) apps offered some form of online counseling where staff could provide feedback and advice to users online. In terms of assessment features, almost all apps provided assessment scales in different categories, with only 15 (23.4%) apps using evidence-based scales and providing more than one type of assessment scale, 10 assessing anxiety, 10 assessing depressive symptoms, 8 assessing stress, and 5 assessing cognitive abilities. In terms of mental health education, nearly 74% of the apps (47/64) provided science-based knowledge related to mental health and mindfulness. In terms of mindfulness interventions, 47 (73%) explicitly provided one or more mindfulness therapies. Among them, the most applied therapy is Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR), with a whopping 43 (67%) apps having courses on the related therapies. In addition to this, Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) is also used more often, with related courses being found in more than half of the apps. Regarding other auxiliary functions, 58 (90.6%) apps can record users' practice data, including accumulated minutes of practice, consecutive days, and date of practice. In addition, 23 apps have an online community function, which allows users to exchange practice tips with each other and record their own training results. Design of a web-based micro-platform for organ donor family mindfulness intervention

Demand analysis

The findings of the previous research group show (Xie et al., 2024) that organ donor family members have some degree of post-traumatic stress, depression, anxiety and other symptoms, as well as high stress, social avoidance and other problems. Only a few family members can obtain psychological support through self-support, environmental support and others' support, and they prioritize information and material needs, with a weak awareness of mental health. Families have a positive attitude towards digital psychological interventions, with an expected frequency of about 30 min once a week (Yang et al., 2026). Therefore, it is believed that digital psychological interventions can be used to improve the psychological experience of family members, increase their access to psychological support, raise their awareness of mental health, and meet some of their psychological needs.

Functional design

According to the evaluation results of existing mindfulness intervention applications in the Chinese market and the three elements of the IMB model, the mindfulness intervention web-based micro-platform for families of organ donors includes the following 7 features: “Understanding Mindfulness”, “Psychological Science”, “Practice Room”, “Community Exchange”, “Psychological Quiz”, “Daily Mindfulness” and “Personal Center”. Interface design

According to the design positioning of the mindfulness intervention web-based micro-platform for organ donor families, for the specificity of mental health problems, white was chosen as the main color of the interface for a large area, and blue in the Morandi color family was chosen as the color of the key for focusing on reminders so that families of organ donors who are in a negative mood can experience a calm and warm feeling. The core layout of the micro-platform is designed with a bottom Tab navigation mode, a design that aims to optimize the interaction experience in the hot thumb area.

Micro-platform development

The researcher is responsible for the development of the web-based micro-platform development program and system requirements, and communicates with the software development professionals in real-time. The professional team is responsible for the overall development work. Among them, the user side adopts advanced H5 platform technology to provide experience, the Web side utilizes the Uni-app framework and Uview component library to realize efficient cross-platform development, and the service side uses SpringBoot as the core technology framework to ensure the stability and scalability of the system. In the front-end page design, the team used JavaScript, HTML, CSS, jQuery, and other programming languages to present a beautiful and easy-to-operate interface. At the same time, MySQL5.7 was chosen as the system database, and the RESTful interface was provided through the JSON method based on HTTPS protocol to ensure the security and efficiency of data transmission. On the H5 side, through Axios HTTPS request, the system returns the standard format JSON data, to achieve seamless interaction between H5 and the server.

After the development of the web-based micro-platform, tests revealed that the contents of “Understanding Mindfulness” and “Psychological Science” were too small, the format was not uniform, and the font needed to be enlarged to accommodate older users. Subsequently, this study employed the System Usability Scale (SUS) to evaluate the micro-platform. Developed by John Brooke (Brooke, 1996) in 1986, the SUS is a simple yet effective questionnaire tool widely used to assess system usability. The scale comprises 10 questions and employs a 5-point rating scale, with odd-numbered items scored in reverse and even-numbered items scored positively. The total score is a standardized score obtained by summing the scores of all items and multiplying by 2.5; the standardized score ranges from 0 to 100. Specifically, an overall average score between 50.9 and 71.3 indicates that the micro-platform's overall usability is average; a score of 71.4 to 85.3 indicates good usability; a score of 85.4 to 90.8 indicates very good usability; and a score greater than 90.9 indicates excellent usability (Lewis, 2018). The standard average usability score for the micro-platform in this study was 85.48. A score exceeding 85.4 indicates that the micro-platform has excellent usability and is well-suited to meet the needs of organ donation patients' families regarding the mindfulness intervention micro-platform.

Discussion

In this study, 276 mindfulness-related apps in China were screened using the User Version of the Mobile Application Rating Scale (uMARS), and 64 apps with high quality were obtained. The screening results were analyzed in terms of information and functions through a questionnaire survey, and it was found that assessment, mental health education, and mindfulness interventions were the dominant function in the domestic app market, which provided a reference basis for the subsequent design.

Based on the results of the preliminary survey, this study constructed the functional framework of the organ donor family's mindfulness intervention web-based micro-platform, including “Understanding Mindfulness”, “Psychological Science”, “Practice Room”, “Community Exchange”, “Psychological Quiz”, “Daily Mindfulness” and “Personal Center”. Based on comprehensive literature review, we developed a tailored content framework specifically designed to address the psychological needs of organ donor families as the target population.

Volunteers were recruited to validate its effectiveness. The mindfulness intervention for organ donor families web-based micro-platform was significantly effective in alleviating PTSD in family members, consistent with existing findings (Davis et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025). The reason for this may be analyzed as: PTSD and related syndromes are characterized by an overactive negative emotional system and situational insensitivity, along with avoidance of trauma-related stimuli. At the same time, this study found that this alleviating effect can be achieved by short-term interventions, with positive effects occurring as soon as the intervention is carried out up to the second week. Marx et al. (Marx et al., 2022) verified a clinically significant effect on PTSD symptoms after only two weeks of using the application. In addition to this, the web-based micro-platform significantly improved their sleep quality, which is similar to the results of previous studies. Gao et al. (Gao et al., 2022) verified the effectiveness of an app-guided mindfulness intervention in improving sleep disorders.

The web-based micro-platform for mindfulness intervention for organ donor families has good effects on mild depression and anxiety, but not on families with more severe depression and anxiety. The reason may be that compared with the traditional face-to-face mindfulness intervention, the web-based micro-platform mindfulness intervention can reach more audiences due to its convenience and popularity, but it also has the disadvantages of low social support and insufficient motivation of the users (Creswell and Goldberg, 2025). In addition to this, the web-based micro-platform was able to increase the level of trait mindfulness of family members to varying degrees, which is consistent with previous findings (Xu et al., 2025; Schillings et al., 2026). Lower trait mindfulness may lead to higher mental health problems (Yang et al., 2026). Increased levels of trait mindfulness help organ donor family members to more acutely perceive their own emotions, thoughts, and ideas as they emerge, thus enabling timely prevention of the triggering of a depressive state of mind.

This paper adheres to the principle of rigor from the preliminary literature research to the later controlled trial, but there are still some shortcomings. The Web-based Microplatform for mindfulness-based interventions designed by this study requires the families of organ donors to be able to use a smartphone and have a WeChat account. Although this approach can benefit the majority of organ donor families, a small proportion of older family members are unable to access this mental health support because they do not know how to use a smartphone to operate the micro-platform for online interventions. Furthermore, the development of this micro-platform was a lengthy and meticulous process requiring substantial funding. During the experimental phase, adhering to the principles of voluntary participation and informed consent, only four participants met the inclusion and exclusion criteria; consequently, a randomized controlled trial could not be conducted, and a pre-post controlled study was employed instead. Despite the limited sample size, user feedback was largely positive, laying the groundwork for future large-scale randomized controlled trials. Future research will further refine the platform's functionality and interface, increase funding, expand the sample size, and recruit a broader population. We aim to validate the effectiveness of the mindfulness intervention through more rigorous randomized controlled trials and conduct long-term follow-up studies to explore in depth the potential of mindfulness interventions to sustainably improve the mental health of organ donor families. In addition, future research could conduct an in-depth analysis of the mechanisms underlying the intervention's effects and explore the mediating roles of factors such as mindfulness skills, social support and coping strategies during the intervention process, thereby further advancing theoretical research in this field. Finally, the remote network management model puts high demands on family members' self-motivation, which may reveal problems such as insufficient family members' initiative and poor compliance in long-term practical application. Therefore, how to effectively enhance the initiative of family members and improve their compliance remains a topic for further in-depth research and exploration.
