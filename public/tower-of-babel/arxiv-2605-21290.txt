Serre Functors and Local Duality for Affine Quotients
Ivan Noden

Abstract




1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The purpose of this short note is to study Serre functors
of categories of the form
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

where
\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}A/G
is the quotient of
a reductive group
G
acting on
\operatorname{Spec}A
with a
unique closed orbit.

1.1.1.

Recall, if
\mathcal{C}
is a compactly generated DG category then there exists
a continuous functor
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
defined by the
property that

{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(y,\Se_{\mathcal{C}}(x))

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
compact. The functor
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}

is known as the Serre functor of
\mathcal{C}
.

Such functors have been studied since the classical
theorems of Serre and Grothendieck and their
generalisation to triangulated categories is seen
in works such as that of Bondal–Kapranov
[
1
]
.
Traditionally, in these instances, it is required
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}

be an equivalence but this assumption is often
dropped in the DG setting where such functors have been
studied and used to great effect by, for instance,
Beraldo in
[
2
]
and Gaitsgory–Yom Din in

[
3
]
.

1.1.2.

When
\mathcal{C}=\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
for
\mathcal{Y}
as above,
the Serre functor is given by tensoring
with an object
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.
We find that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}

is the local cohomology
of
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}
at the unique closed
orbit. Using this, we are able to
develop analogues of Matlis and local
duality.

In the remainder of this introduction,
we recall the classical theorems
of local and Matlis duality
in order to highlight parallels with our results on affine quotients.
After this, we briefly
study the specific example of
\mathcal{Y}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}

to illustrate these connections before stating our main results.

1.1.3.

Suppose
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local
ring and
I
is the injective hull of
k
in the
abelian category of
A
–modules. Then Matlis proves that,
for an
A
–module
M
,
if
M
is finitely generated,
\usHom_{A}(M,I)

is Artinian. Conversely, if
M
is Artinian, then
\usHom_{A}(M,I)

is finitely generated. Furthermore, if
M
is
either finitely generated or Artinian,
M\simeq\usHom_{A}(\usHom_{A}(M,I),I)
.

One notes that, if
M
is finitely generated then
its local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}M
are Artinian.
Thus, if we let
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}
denote the derived
local cohomology functor, the functor

\uHom_{A}(-,I)
defines an
involutive equivalence between

\Coh(\operatorname{Spec}A)
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Coh(\operatorname{Spec}A))
.

The local duality theorem of Grothendieck
then tells us that the functor
\uHom_{A}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(-),I)

is representable by the normalised dualising complex
\omega_{A}

of
A
.

The connection with Serre functors is that, when
the category
\QCoh(\operatorname{Spec}A)
is
reflexive

(a condition which ensures the Serre functor is nicely behaved),
then the Serre functor on
\QCoh(\operatorname{Spec}A)
is given
by tensoring with
I
.

1.1.4.

Let us now consider a baby example of the affine quotient case.
We take
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
with the usual dilation action
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Put
\pi:\mathcal{Y}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}

as the natural map and note that
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is
compactly generated by
\pi^{\ast}k_{n}
where
k_{n}

is the representation of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
with highest weight

n\in\mathbb{Z}
. As the compact objects of
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

are dualisable, the Serre functor is given by tensoring
with
\mathcal{S}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
characterised by the property
that

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\pi^{\ast}k_{n},\mathcal{S})\simeq{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\pi^{\ast}k_{n})}^{\vee}\simeq{\Hom_{\Rep(G)}(k_{0},\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}k_{n})}^{\vee}\simeq\begin{cases}{(k\cdot x^{n})}^{\vee},&\text{if }n\geq 0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

One can then observe that

\mathcal{S}\simeq\cone(\pi^{\ast}k_{1}\to\colim_{n}\pi^{\ast}k_{-n}).

This object has a geometric interpretation.
Let
j:{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be the obvious
open immersion corresponding to
\mathbb{A}^{1}-\{0\}

and define
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{0}
to be the full
subcategory given by the kernel of
j^{\ast}
.
The embedding
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{0}\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

admits a right adjoint:

\Gamma_{0}=\ker(\mathrm{id}_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}).

We remark that
\Gamma_{0}\mathcal{F}\simeq\Gamma_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\otimes\mathcal{F}

by the projection formula.
Observe then that
\mathcal{S}\simeq\Gamma_{0}(\pi^{\ast}k_{1}[1])
.
Interestingly,
\pi^{\ast}k_{1}\simeq\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})

as
\Omega_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\cdot\mathrm{d}x

and
x
has weight
1
.
Thus,

\mathcal{S}\simeq\Gamma_{0}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[1].

1.1.5.

By its defining property
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}

and since,
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})\simeq\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
, for all
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}),\mathcal{S})\simeq\mathcal{F}
.
Furthermore, for
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,
we have

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S})\simeq{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}\simeq\Gamma_{0}(\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})\otimes{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee})[1]

and

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{0}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S})\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F}\otimes{\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}[-1],\mathcal{S})\simeq{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}\otimes\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[1].

Thus,
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})

defines an involutive equivalence between

\Gamma_{0}(\Coh(\mathcal{Y}))
and
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})

1.2.
Main results

While the example of
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
relied on the smoothness of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
this assumption is not necessary. What we really use is
the existence of a unique closed orbit under the
\mathbb{G}_{m}

action. Let us spell out the more general case.

1.2.1.

Let
G
be a reductive group acting on the classical affine scheme

\operatorname{Spec}A
of finite type. Consider the stack
\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}A/G
.
The category
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is proper (that is,

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})
has finite dimensional
total cohomology whenever
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are
compact) if
\operatorname{Spec}A
has finitely many closed orbits
under the action of
G
. We focus specifically
on the case where there is a unique closed orbit,
in which case
\mathcal{Y}\simeq\operatorname{Spec}B/H
where

\operatorname{Spec}B
has a unique closed orbit under the
action of reductive
H
and, furthermore,
this closed orbit is a fixed point.
Therefore, we will assume
\operatorname{Spec}A
has
a unique closed orbit which is a fixed point.

Not only is
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
proper but its compact
objects are dualisable so the Serre functor
is given by tensoring with some
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.

1.2.2.

Let
i:BG\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be the closed immersion of the
fixed point and
j:{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
its open complement.
The full subcategory of
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
given by the kernel
of
j^{\ast}
is denoted
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
. It is
the subcategory of quasicoherent sheaves
set-theoretically supported at the fixed point.

The embedding
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

admits a right adjoint, denoted
\Gamma_{BG}

and called the
local cohomology functor
.
Explicitly,

\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\ker(\mathcal{F}\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}).

By the projection formula,
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\otimes\mathcal{F}
.

1.2.3.

Our main result is an explicit description of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
:

Theorem A
.

We have
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]
.

The main idea behind the proof is showing that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}

is supported entirely at the fixed point. From this,
its defining property allows us to characterise it
as above.

We remark that this description for
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
was
found by Beraldo in
[
2
]
in the
specific case where
\operatorname{Spec}A
is the
nilpotent cone in the Lie algebra of
G
.

1.2.4.

Using this description, we are able to prove an analogue
of Matlis and local duality. Writing

\Dmat=\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\text{ and }\Dse=\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

we prove:

Theorem B
.

For all
\mathcal{F}
in
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,

\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\simeq\Dse(\mathcal{F})[\dim G]\text{ and }\Dmat(\mathcal{F})\simeq\Gamma_{BG}(\Dse(\mathcal{F}))[\dim G],

hence
\Dmat
defines involutive equivalences

{\Coh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Gamma_{BG}(\Coh(\mathcal{Y}))
and
{\Gamma_{BG}(\Coh(\mathcal{Y}))}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

The proof of this of course uses
A
.
The main step aside from this is showing that, for

\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,
\Dse(j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\simeq 0

from which we can deduce that
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\simeq\Dse(\mathcal{F})[\dim G]
.
To do this, we show first that
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})

lands in
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
which forces
\Dse(j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.
We can then exploit
4.1.6

which tells us that
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},-)

is conservative on
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

1.2.5.

We use both
A
and

B
to also calculate
the Serre functor for
\QCoh({\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ})
in
4.4.1
.
This application recovers classical Serre duality for projective
varieties.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we outline the notations and conventions
of the paper as well as defining some basic notions and
recalling a few standard results that are worth
highlighting. It is recommended the reader
skip this section and only refer back to it when
necessary.

We always work over an algebraically closed
field
k
of characteristic zero.

2.1.
DG categories

As the existence of a Serre functor on compactly generated
DG categories follows from abstract nonsense, we provide
here some of the basics of higher category theory needed
in order to deduce the existence of the Serre functor.
We ignore all problems of a set-theoretic nature.

2.1.1.

For us, all categories are DG categories (i.e. stable
\infty
-categories
with an action of
\mathrm{Vect}
) except for a few instances where
we work with abelian categories (which we make clear).
We generally assume DG categories are cocomplete (i.e.
contain arbitrary direct sums) unless stated explicitly otherwise
or unless they are of the form
\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
or
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})

for
\mathcal{Y}
a prestack. We assume
functors between categories are exact and continuous (i.e. commute with
arbitrary direct sums) unless explicitly stated otherwise or
if they are (internal) hom functors.

The category of exact
functors between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
is denoted
\Fun(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

and the full subcategory of continuous functors is
denote
\Fun_{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
.

In general, hom-spaces are denoted by
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)

while internal homs (when they exist) are denoted by
\uHom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)
.
When we do work in an abelian category, we use
\sHom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)

and
\usHom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)
instead.

2.1.2.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. An object
c\in\mathcal{C}
is
compact

if the functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c,-)
is continuous.
The full subcategory of compact objects of
\mathcal{C}
is
denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
.

2.1.3.

A family of objects
\{c_{\alpha}\}
in
\mathcal{C}

generates

\mathcal{C}
if the family of functors
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{\alpha},-)

is a conservative family, that is
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{\alpha},c)\simeq 0

for all
c_{\alpha}
implies
c\simeq 0
. This means
that the only cocomplete full subcategory containing
the
c_{\alpha}
is
\mathcal{C}
itself. We say
\mathcal{C}
is

compactly generated
if there exists a family
of compact objects generating
\mathcal{C}
.

2.1.4.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a category which is not necessarily cocomplete.
The cocomplete category
\Ind(\mathcal{C})
is the
ind-extension

of
\mathcal{C}
. It is equipped with a functor
\mathcal{C}\to\Ind(\mathcal{C})

and is characterised by the property that, for all cocomplete
categories
\mathcal{D}
, the morphism

\Fun_{\text{cont}}(\Ind(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{D})\to\Fun(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

is an isomorphism. The lift of a functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
to
a continuous functor
\Ind(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{D}
is
called the
ind-extension
of the functor.
The category
\Fun({\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Vect})
is
a model for
\Ind(\mathcal{C})

2.1.5.

The functor
\mathcal{C}\to\Ind(\mathcal{C})
is fully faithful and its
essential image identifies with
{\Ind(\mathcal{C})}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
is cocomplete and compactly generated,
then the functor
\Ind({\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}})\to\mathcal{C}
is an
equivalence.

2.1.6.

We say
\mathcal{C}
is
proper
if
x,y\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}

implies
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\in{\mathrm{Vect}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
. Recall,

{\mathrm{Vect}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
consists of objects with finite
dimensional total cohomology. We say

\mathcal{C}
is
reflexive
if
x,y\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}

implies
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
is reflexive,
that is if each of its cohomologies
are finite dimensional.

2.2.
Algebraic geometry

2.2.1.

Let
\mathrm{pt}=\operatorname{Spec}k
. All of our prestacks are defined over
k
.
By ’stack‘ we mean ’algebraic stack‘ in the sense of
[
4
]
.

2.2.2.

For a stack
\mathcal{Y}
, one defines the category
\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.
It comes with a canonically defined functor
\Psi:\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})\to\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.
When
\mathcal{Y}
is QCA, in the sense of
[
4
]
,

\Psi
comes from ind-extending the embedding
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.

Recall, if
f\colon\mathcal{Y}_{1}\to\mathcal{Y}_{2}
is a morphism between stacks locally
almost of finite type (laft) then we have a functor
f^{!}\colon\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y}_{2})\to\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
.
If
\mathcal{Y}
is laft and
p_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathrm{pt}
is the canonical map
then we write
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}
for
p_{\mathcal{Y}}^{!}(k)\in\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.

The functor
\Psi
identifies

\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
with the left completion of
\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})
in
its t-structure and induces an equivalence
{\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{+}\to{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{+}
.
If
\mathcal{Y}
is smooth then
\Psi
is an equivalence.

Moreover, when
\mathcal{Y}
is smooth,

\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\det(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim\mathcal{Y}]

where
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is cotangent complex of
\mathcal{Y}
.

2.2.3.

If
\mathcal{Y}
is a QCA algebraic stack then the canonical
equivalence
{\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\vee}\simeq\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})

induces an involution
\Dse:{\Coh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.
Explicitly, we have
\Dse\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

[
4
, 4.4]
.

2.2.4.

For a prestack
\mathcal{Y}
, the category
\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
is the
full subcategory of
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
consisting of
dualisable objects. Following
[
5
]
,
we say
\mathcal{Y}
is
perfect
if
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is compactly
generated and
\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
and
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathrm{cpt}}

coincide.
By
[
4
, Corollary 1.4.3]
,
all QCA stacks are perfect.

2.2.5.

Except in
Section

3
,
\operatorname{Spec}A
is a
classical

affine scheme of finite type,
acted on by a fixed reductive group
G
and
\mathcal{Y}

is the quotient
\operatorname{Spec}A/G
.

We remark that
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is compactly
generated by
\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
and
\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})

is compactly generated by
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

We have a natural map
\pi:\mathcal{Y}\to BG
. As

\pi_{\ast}
is conservative,
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

is compactly generated by
{\{\pi^{\ast}V_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}\}}
.
Here,
\Lambda_{G}
is the set of dominant weights
for
G
and, for
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

V_{\lambda}
is the corresponding irreducible
representation. We write
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda)

for
\pi^{\ast}V_{\lambda}
.

The action of
G
on
\operatorname{Spec}A
induces
a
\Gamma(G,\mathcal{O}_{G})
–comodule structure
on
A
. We write
A^{G}
for the invariants
of this coaction. There is a map

p:\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}A^{G}
such that

p_{\ast}
is t-exact and preserves
coherence
[
6
]
.

We write
q:\operatorname{Spec}A\to\mathcal{Y}
for the
natural quotient/atlas map.
Note that
q^{\ast}
is
the forgetful functor from
G
–equivariant
A
–modules
to
A
–modules.

2.3.
Equivariant algebra

We will need to use some results on the category

\mathcal{C}={\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
, that is the abelian
category of
G
–equivariant
A
–modules.
These are
G
–equivariant version
of many classical algebra results which
will be useful for us to reference.

2.3.1.

A
G
–ideal of
A
is a
G
–equivariant
A
–module
contained in
A
. We say
A
is
G
–local if it has
a unique maximal
G
–ideal.

2.3.2.

The category
\mathcal{C}
has enough injectives (since
\operatorname{Spec}A/G
is algebraic
with affine diagonal) and thus every object
has an injective hull. Recall that an essential extension
of a
G
–equivariant
A
–module
M
is a module
N

containing
M
such that every other
G
–submodule of

N
intersects
M
non-trivially. The injective
hull of
M
is then an injective essential extension.

2.3.3.

We say
M\in\mathcal{C}
is
G
-free if
M\simeq\pi^{\ast}V

where
V
is a representation of
G
.
Note that for every
M\in\mathcal{C}
there
is an epimorphism from a
G
–free
module to
M
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal
G
-ideal of
A
.
Then note that
G
acts transitively on
\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{m})

so
\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{m})\simeq G/H
for some subgroup
H
.
As a result,
\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{m})/G\simeq BH

and we can conclude that every
G
–equivariant

A/\mathfrak{m}
–module is
G
–free.

2.3.4.

Let us assume that
A
is
G
–local with unique
maximal
G
–ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. If
X\in\mathcal{C}

is finitely generated and
\mathfrak{m}X=X

then, by Nakayama, there exists

m\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
mx=x

for all
x\in X
. Note, this
forces
m\in A^{G}
so
(\mathfrak{m}\cap A^{G})X=X
.
However, if
A
is
G
–local
then
A^{G}
is local so Nakayama
then implies
X=0
.

2.3.5.

As an application of this, suppose
P\in\mathcal{C}

is projective. Take
F
to be a
G
–free
module with an epimorphism
F\to P
.
Let
K
be the kernel in
\mathcal{C}
.
In the same way one proves it with
ordinary local rings, we see that

K\subseteq\mathfrak{m}F
and since
P

is projective, we deduce
that
\mathfrak{m}K=K
in
\mathcal{C}
.
Thus,
K=0
. It follows
that all projective objects in
\mathcal{C}

are
G
–free.

2.3.6.

Take
M\in\mathcal{C}
. The
G
–socle of
M
is taken to be

\Soc_{G}(M)=\usHom_{\mathcal{C}}(A/\mathfrak{m},M).

This is naturally a
G
–equivariant module
over
A/\mathfrak{m}
and hence is
G
–free.
We can also consider it as a
G
–submodule of
M
.
Hence, if
M
is
G
–Artinian,

\Soc_{G}(M)\simeq A/\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{k}V

where
V
is a finite dimensional representation
of
G
. Note that if
N
is a
G
–submodule of
M
,

N\cap\Soc_{G}(M)\simeq\Soc_{G}(N)
which is
non-zero as
N
is also
G
–Artinian
(hence it contains a simple
G
–submodule which must be

A/\mathfrak{m}
). So
M
is an essential extension of
\Soc_{G}(M)
.
They thus have the same injective hull. Write

E
for the injective hull of
A/\mathfrak{m}
.
Then the injective hull of
M
is
E\otimes_{k}V
.

3.
Serre Functors

In this section we cover some of the abstract theory
of Serre functors.

3.1.
Existence of Serre functors

3.1.1.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a compactly generated category. Then

\mathcal{C}\simeq\Ind({\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}})\simeq\Fun({({\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}})}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Vect}).

Thus, for all
x\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
the functor
{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,-)}^{\vee}

is representable by some
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
. By ind-extension,
the assignment
x\mapsto\Se_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
defines a
continuous functor
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
. By construction,
it is characterised by the property that

{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(y,\Se_{\mathcal{C}}(x))

for all
x\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
and all
y\in\mathcal{C}

although it is enough to check this property
with
y\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
. When
\mathcal{C}
is obvious
from context we just write
\Se
instead of
\Se_{\mathcal{C}}
.

3.1.2.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is equipped with a symmetric monoidal
structure with compact unit
\mathbb{1}
. Further assume
that all compact objects in
\mathcal{C}
are dualisable. Observe that

{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)}^{\vee}\simeq{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{1},{x}^{\vee}\otimes y)}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(y,\Se(\mathbb{1})\otimes x).

Hence,
\Se
is equivalent to tensoring by
\Se(\mathbb{1})
.

Notation 3.1.3
.

When
\mathcal{C}
is as above, we write
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}
for
\Se(\mathbb{1})
.

3.1.4.

In the case when
\mathcal{C}
is proper, note that, for
x\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
,
the functor
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,-)
preserves compactness and
so has a continuous right adjoint
T
. See that
T

is determined by
T(k)\in\mathcal{C}
and

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(y,T(k))\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Vect}}(\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y),k)\simeq{\Hom(x,y)}^{\vee}.

Hence,
T(k)=\Se(x)
. This provides another justification
for the existence of the Serre functor when
\mathcal{C}
is proper.

3.1.5.

The Serre functor is fully faithful on compact objects
if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is reflexive
[
3
, Proposition 1.2.6]
.
Thus, if
\mathcal{C}
is reflexive; its compact objects are dualisable
under some symmetric monoidal structure; and the monoidal unit is compact, then

\uHom_{\mathcal{C}}(x\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}},y\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}})\simeq\uHom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)

for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
.

3.2.
Serre Functors on QCoh

3.2.1.

Let us assume
\mathcal{Y}
is a perfect stack so that the
Serre functor for
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is given by tensoring with

\mathcal{S}_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}
.

Notation 3.2.2
.

When
\mathcal{Y}
is as above we write
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
for
\mathcal{S}_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}
.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be perfect stacks such that
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}

is a schematic, quasi-compact morphism.
Then
f_{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\in\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
, Observe that

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},f_{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{X}})\simeq{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{X})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}.

However,

{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}\simeq{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{X})}(f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}\simeq{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}.

Using the Serre functor on
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
we deduce that

{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}).

By assumptions on
f
, we have a projection formula and thus

{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},f^{\ast}\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})).

Thus,

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},f_{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{X}})\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

for all
\mathcal{F}\in\Perf(\mathcal{Y})
, proving that

f_{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})
.
∎

4.
Serre functors on affine quotients

4.1.
Elementary properties of affine quotients

We now focus our study on the stack
\mathcal{Y}

as defined in
Section

2.2.5
. It
is perfect so its Serre functor is
given by tensoring with
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. We would
like to consider cases when
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})

is also proper.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

If
\operatorname{Spec}A
has finitely many closed
G
–orbits then

\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is proper.

Proof.

It is enough to check that
\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu))

is compact for any
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{G}
.
By dualisability, it is enough to check

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda))
is compact
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}
.
Observe that this is the vector space underlying

p_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda)
, which is coherent by
[
6
, Theorem 4.16(x)]
,
and lands in the heart by t-exactness. Thus it is a finitely
generated
A^{G}
–module. The claim is thus proven
if
A^{G}
is finite dimensional over
k
.

Closed orbits of
\operatorname{Spec}A
correspond bijectively to
points of
\operatorname{Spec}A^{G}
(this is
[
6
, Theorem 4.16(iv)]

or
[
7
, Proposition 0.1, Theorem 1.1]
). Thus,
if
\operatorname{Spec}A
has finitely many closed orbits,
\operatorname{Spec}A^{G}

has finitely many maximal ideals.

Note
A^{G}
is a finitely generated
k
-algebra
(this is Hilbert’s 14th problem which was proven by Nagata
when
G
is linearly reductive, or it is
[
6
, Theorem 4.16(xi)]
),
thus it has finitely many maximal ideals if and only if it is
Artinian. Thus
A^{G}
is Artinian and Noetherian so
is indeed finite dimensional over
k
.
∎

4.1.2.

It thus makes sense to consider when
\operatorname{Spec}A

has finitely many closed orbits. From now on,
we will assume that, in particular,
\operatorname{Spec}A

has a unique closed orbit. Equivalently,

A
is
G
–local. Also, note
A^{G}
is local.

Take a point
x\in\operatorname{Spec}A
in this orbit
and let
H
be its stabiliser. By Luna’s slice
theorem, there is a locally closed affine
subvariety
\operatorname{Spec}B
of
\operatorname{Spec}A

containing
x
such that
\{x\}

is the unique closed orbit of
H

acting on
\operatorname{Spec}B
and
\mathcal{Y}\simeq\operatorname{Spec}B/H
.

Therefore, we assume from now on that
\operatorname{Spec}A

has a unique closed orbit and that it is a fixed point.
Note that this is equivalent to the unique

G
–maximal ideal of
A
being
maximal as an ordinary ideal.

4.1.3.

The fixed point defines a closed immersion
i:BG\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}

with open complement
j:{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
. Let
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}

be the full subcategory of
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
given by the
kernel of
j^{\ast}
. The embedding
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}

has a right adjoint, denoted by
\Gamma_{BG}

and called the local cohomology functor.
Explicitly,

\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\ker(\mathcal{F}\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}).

Proposition 4.1.4

(c.f.
[
8
, Proposition II.4.6.1.3]
)
.

The category
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
is generated by the essential image of
i_{\ast}
.

Proof.

The embedding
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
is compatible
with the t-structure since
j^{\ast}
is t-exact. Hence,

\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
is generated by
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}^{\heartsuit}
.
Any
\mathcal{F}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}^{\heartsuit}
is the colimit
of its coherent subsheaves
[
9
, Proposition 15.4]
.
If
\mathcal{F}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}^{\heartsuit}
is coherent
then it must be annihilated by some power
of the
G
–maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
A
.
Hence,
\mathcal{F}
has a filtration by objects
of the form
\mathfrak{m}^{n}\cdot\mathcal{F}
and the
quotients
\mathfrak{m}^{n}\cdot\mathcal{F}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\mathcal{F}
are
annihilated by
\mathfrak{m}
so lie in the essential
image of
i_{\ast}
.
∎

4.1.5.

Our next small result is in a similar spirit to the one above:

Proposition 4.1.6
.

The functor
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},-)
is conservative
on
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

Proof.

Take
\mathcal{G}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
and assume
\mathcal{G}\not\simeq 0
. We need to show

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\mathcal{G})

is non-zero. Notice the underlying
A
–module is

\uHom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{G})
where
\mathfrak{m}
is the
unique
G
–maximal ideal of
A
.
Thus it is enough to show the
\mathfrak{m}
–depth
of the complex
\mathcal{G}
is finite. As

\mathcal{G}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
, this follows from

[
10
, 2.5]
.
Although the argument in
loc. cit.

assumes
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is local,
it still follows in our case by

Section

2.3.4
.
∎

One consequence of this is that
any coherent sheaf must have sections supported
at the fixed point of
\mathcal{Y}
. More precisely:

Corollary 4.1.7
.

The functor
\Gamma_{BG}
is conservative on
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

Proof.

Take
\mathcal{G}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
and assume
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{G}\simeq 0
.
Then

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{G})\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\mathcal{G})\simeq 0

since
\Gamma_{BG}
is right adjoint to
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.
However, by
4.1.6
, this implies

\mathcal{G}\simeq 0
.

Equivalently, one can use
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]

and the argument in the proof above.
∎

4.2.
The Serre functor

We now look at the object
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
itself and the functor

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})
. We introduce some notation for it:

Notation 4.2.1
.

Write
\Dmat:{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
for
the functor
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})
.

Proposition 4.2.2
.

The functor
\Dmat
is t-exact.
Thus
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
and is an injective object in
this abelian category.

Proof.

First, note that, as
G
is linearly reductive,
the functor
H_{\lambda}=\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda),-)
is t-exact
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}
. Moreover, the
family of functors
\left\{H_{\lambda}\,\colon\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}\right\}

is jointly conservative. Thus
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})

is t-exact if and only if

H_{\lambda}(\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\mathcal{S}_{Y}))\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})

is t-exact for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}
. However, observe that

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq{H_{{\lambda}^{\vee}}(\mathcal{F})}^{\vee}.

Thus, as taking the linear dual is t-exact and
conservative, the claim follows again from the
t-exactness of the
H_{\lambda}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2.3
.

We have
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
.

Proof.

By
4.2.2
,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}

so we treat it as a
G
–equivariant
A
–module.
We will show it is
G
–Artinian.
Let

\mathfrak{m}
be the unique maximal
G
–ideal of
A
.

Suppose we have a chain of submodules:

\cdots\hookrightarrow M_{3}\hookrightarrow M_{2}\hookrightarrow M_{1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}.

Applying
\Dmat
yields epimorphisms:

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\twoheadrightarrow\Dmat(M_{1})\twoheadrightarrow\Dmat(M_{2})\twoheadrightarrow\Dmat(M_{3})\twoheadrightarrow\cdots.

As
A
is Noetherian, this stabilises so there exists
a\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\Dmat(M_{a})\simeq\Dmat(M_{a+1})
.
Let
N=M_{a}/M_{a+1}
.

Aiming for a contradiction, assume
N\not\simeq 0
.
Then there is a monomorphism
A/\mathfrak{m}\simeq i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}\hookrightarrow N
.
Applying
\Dmat
yields an epimorphism
\Dmat(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG})\twoheadrightarrow\Dmat(N)\simeq 0
.
However,

\Dmat(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG})\simeq i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}

by
3.2.3
. Thus, this is a contradiction.

It follows that
M_{a}\simeq M_{a+1}
so

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is Artinian. Thus it is, element-wise,
annihilated by powers of
\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

4.2.4.

It turns out
4.2.3
is the last piece we need to prove
that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]
as claimed in
A
.

Proof of
A
.

As proven in
4.2.3
,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}

and we know
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
is generated by the essential image
of
i_{\ast}
by
4.1.4
.
Thus,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is characterised by the fact that, for all
V\in{\Rep(G)}^{\mathrm{cpt}}
,
we have

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}(i_{\ast}V,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq{\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},i_{\ast}V)}^{\vee}\simeq{\Hom_{\Rep(G)}(\mathcal{O}_{BG},V)}^{\vee}\simeq\Hom_{\Rep(G)}(V,\mathcal{O}_{BG}).

Now,
\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
and

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}(i_{\ast}V,\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}V,\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

since
\Gamma_{BG}
is the right adjoint of
\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}\hookrightarrow\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
.
Then, by
(i^{\IndCoh}_{\ast},i^{!})
–adjunction,

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}V,\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\Hom_{\IndCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i^{\IndCoh}_{\ast}V,\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\Hom_{\Rep(G)}(V,\Psi(\omega_{BG})).

However,
\Psi(\omega_{BG})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{BG}[-\dim G]
and, therefore,

\Hom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}}(i_{\ast}V,\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\Hom_{\Rep(G)}(V,\mathcal{O}_{BG}[-\dim G]).

Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]
.
∎

4.2.5.

Our next minor result is how
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is the
injective hull of
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}
in the abelian
category
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
which explains
the connection to classical Matlis duality.

Proposition 4.2.6
.

In the abelian category
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}

is indecomposable, that is it cannot be written as a non-trivial
direct sum.

Proof.

Since
\usHom(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}
, if
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}

were decomposable then
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}
would be too. If
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}

were decomposable,
A
would contain a non-trivial,
homogeneous idempotent
which must be in
A^{G}
. However,
A^{G}
is local and
so cannot contain a non-trivial idempotent.
∎

Corollary 4.2.7
.

In the abelian category
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
,

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is the injective hull of
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}
.

Proof.

Since we identify
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}
with
A/\mathfrak{m}

where
\mathfrak{m}
is the unique, maximal
G
–ideal
of
A
, we see that the map
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}

is an epimorphism in
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
.
Thus
\usHom(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}

is a monomorphism.

As
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is injective,
\usHom(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\uHom(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})
which is

i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}
by
3.2.3
.
Thus we have a monomorphism
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is injective, this means
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}

is a direct summand of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
but, by the result above,

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is indecomposable so
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is an essential
extension of
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG}
. Therefore, it
is the injective hull.
∎

4.3.
Local and Matlis duality

Using the description of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}
given
by
A
,
we will now go on to prove
B

which relates
\Dmat
with classical Serre duality.
First, let us recall some notation:

Notation 4.3.1
.

We write
\Dse:{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
for the functor

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))
.

The main obstacle in proving
B

is showing that, if
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,
then
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.
For this, we need to prove that
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

lies in
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
which follows if we show
the cohomology of
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})
is coherent
and, also, that
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

is bounded above when restricted to
{\Coh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}

(see, for instance,
[
11
, Proposition II.7.20]
for details).
Before we do this, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 4.3.2
.

Let
q:\operatorname{Spec}A\to\mathcal{Y}
be the natural map. For
\mathcal{F}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
,
we have
q^{\ast}\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\Gamma_{BG}
,

q^{\ast}\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\ker(q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to q^{\ast}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}).

Let
s:U\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A
be the open complement of the
fixed point of the
G
–action and let
r:U\to{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}

be the quotient map. Then note that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

is Cartesian so
q^{\ast}j_{\ast}\simeq s_{\ast}r^{\ast}
and thus

q^{\ast}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\simeq s_{\ast}r^{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\simeq s_{\ast}s^{\ast}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
.

Now,

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\simeq\ker(q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to s_{\ast}s^{\ast}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F})

and, since all maps are from adjunction, this proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.3.3
.

The cohomology of
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Observe that, since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
,
we have a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{a,b}=H^{a}(\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(H^{-b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}))\implies H^{a+b}(\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})).

By
4.2.2
,

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(H^{-b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\usHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(H^{-b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}))
,
so it is enough to show
\usHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

is finitely generated for all
b
.

Assume for now that
H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})
is
G
–Artinian
as an object of
{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
. Then, by
Section

2.3.6

and
4.2.7
, we have a monomorphism

H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\otimes\pi^{\ast}V

for some
V\in\Rep(G)
finite dimensional. Applying

\Dmat
yields an epimorphism

\pi^{\ast}{V}^{\vee}\to\usHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})

and hence it is finitely generated.

Therefore, we just need to show
H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})

is
G
–Artinian. As
q^{\ast}
is t-exact, from
4.3.2
,

q^{\ast}H^{b}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq H^{b}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A)

and this is an Artinian
A
–module (localise and
use
[
12
, Proposition 3.5.4]
) which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.3.4
.

There exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that, for all
\mathcal{F}\in{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}
,
one has
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})^{[-n,n]}
.

Proof.

By
4.2.2
,
all we need is the cohomology
of
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}
to be bounded.
It is enough to check
q^{\ast}\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}

has bounded cohomology as
q^{\ast}

is t-exact and conservative.
From
4.3.2
,

q^{\ast}\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\ker(q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to s_{\ast}s^{\ast}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F})
,
using the notation of
4.3.2
.
Note then we just want to show
s_{\ast}s^{\ast}q^{\ast}\mathcal{F}

has bounded cohomology.
However,
\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}A,-)
is exact and conservative
so this is equivalent to checking

\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}A,s_{\ast}r^{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\simeq\Gamma(U,r^{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})

is cohomologically bounded but this clearly lies in
\mathrm{Vect}^{[0,\dim U]}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3.5
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
, then
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\otimes\mathcal{F}
, we see that

\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})).

Thus, all we need to prove is that

•

the cohomology of
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})
is coherent;

•

and the functor
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

is bounded above

(see
[
11
, Proposition II.7.20]
for details).
However, we proved these in
4.3.3
and

4.3.4
.
∎

4.3.6.

We can now prove the first part of
B
:

Proposition 4.3.7
.

For all
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
, there is a natural equivalence
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}(\mathcal{F}))\simeq\Dse(\mathcal{F})[\dim G]
.

Proof.

Observe that

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}))

since
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\otimes\mathcal{F}
. Therefore, it
is enough to show
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]
.
From
A
, we know
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]

and therefore, as
\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\in\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})_{BG}
,

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}},\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G])\simeq\Dse(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G].

Now, note

\Dse(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\cone(\Dse(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})\to\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

so the claim is proven if we show
\Dse(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})\simeq 0
.
However,
\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
and
\Dse(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})[-\dim G]\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})

by
4.3.5
so we also know

\Dse(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y}).

We are then done since

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\Dse(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}}))\simeq\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(j_{\ast}j^{\ast}i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{BG},\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq 0

by base change and this implies
\Dse(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})\simeq 0
by
4.1.6

∎

4.3.8.

To prove the other part of
B
,
we will need a straightforward but
technical lemma which we record here:

Lemma 4.3.9
.

Let
F,G:\Coh(\mathcal{Y})\to\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})
be bounded above functors and
\eta:F\to G

an arrow between them. Then
\eta
is an isomorphism
if
F(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda))\xrightarrow{\eta}G(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda))
is
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}
.

Proof.

See
[
11
, I.7.1]
. The argument is
simply an induction on amplitude. The only thing we
add is the observation that every
\mathcal{F}\in{\Coh(\mathcal{Y})}^{\heartsuit}

is the target of an epimorphism from an object of the
form
\pi^{\ast}V
with
V
a finite dimensional
representation of
G
, which can be written
as a finite direct sum of sheaves of the form
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda)
.
∎

With this, we can complete the proof:

Proof of
B
.

Take
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
,
we know that
\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{F})\simeq\Dse(\mathcal{F})[\dim G]

from
4.3.7
.
We will prove that
\Dmat(\mathcal{F})\simeq\Gamma_{BG}(\Dse(\mathcal{F}))[\dim G]

from which the rest of the statement will follow since
\Dse

is an involution on
\Coh(\mathcal{Y})
.

Observe that, as
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})[\dim G]
, we have

\Dmat(\mathcal{F})\simeq\ker(\Dse(\mathcal{F})\to\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})))[\dim G].

By the projection formula, applying
j_{\ast}j^{\ast}

to the canonical map
\Dse(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{F}\to\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})

yields a morphism

j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse(\mathcal{F})\to\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(\mathcal{F},j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})).

We will show this is an isomorphism using
4.3.9
,
which requires that both the functors
j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse
and

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

are bounded above.

First, see that

\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))\simeq\cone(\Dmat[-\dim G]\to\Dse).

Both
\Dmat
and
\Dse
are bounded above by
4.2.2
and
4.3.4

so we conclude that the functor
\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))
is bounded above.
For
j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse
,
using the same arguments of
4.3.4
shows that,
as
\Dse
is bounded above, so is
j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse
.

Therefore, for
j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse\to\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

to be an isomorphism, we need to only check it on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\lambda)

where it is clear.

Thus, the map

\Dse\to\uHom_{\QCoh(\mathcal{Y})}(-,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}))

factors through a map

\Dse\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse

and it is clear this is the map from adjunction.
So we see that

\Dmat(\mathcal{F})\simeq\ker(\Dse(\mathcal{F})\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\Dse(\mathcal{F}))[\dim G]\simeq\Gamma_{BG}(\Dse(\mathcal{F}))[\dim G]

as claimed.
∎

4.4.
Serre functor on the puncture

To end, we use
A
and
B

to find the Serre functor on
\QCoh({\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ})
.

Proposition 4.4.1
.

The Serre functor on
\QCoh({\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ})
is given by tensoring with
\Psi(\omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})[\dim G-1]
.

Proof.

By
3.2.3
,
j_{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}}\simeq\Dmat(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})
.
Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}}\simeq j^{\ast}\Dmat(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})
. Notice that

j^{\ast}\Dmat(j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})\simeq j^{\ast}\ker(\Dmat(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{BG}))\simeq\ker(j^{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to j^{\ast}\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{BG})).

However, since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Gamma_{BG}\Psi(\omega_{\mathcal{Y}})
by
A
,
we have
j^{\ast}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq 0
and thus
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}}\simeq j^{\ast}\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{BG})[-1]
.
From
B
,
j^{\ast}\Dmat(\Gamma_{BG}\mathcal{O}_{BG})[-1]\simeq\Psi(\omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}})[\dim G-1]
.
∎

Remark 4.4.2
.

Note that this recovers classical Serre duality for projective varieties by taking
\operatorname{Spec}A

to be the affine cone and
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
