Executive Summary
This white paper presents a multi-year initiative to develop practical, secure, and scalable digital
twin capabilities for engineered systems in aging and surveillance contexts—an approach
pioneered at the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) Lawrence Livermore National
Laboratory (LLNL) that maps directly onto the needs and ambitions of the Navy for ship- and
fleet-level digital twins. LLNL’s work in building part- and process-level digital twins for
advanced manufacturing, with a vision to scale up to entire factory floors and, ultimately,
enterprise-wide digital twins, offers an adaptable pathway for the Navy as it seeks to modernize
lifecycle management, readiness, and predictive maintenance across ships and fleets.
For our application, we integrate physics-based modeling with automated data ingestion,
processing, and AI-driven calibration, creating hybrid models that are both interpretable and data-
responsive. We modernized legacy workflows, established centralized data infrastructure,
automated experimental pipelines, and demonstrated end-to-end coupling of accelerated aging data
with finite element simulations via optimization and surrogate modeling. The result is a
generalizable framework that supports part-level digital twins today and lays the groundwork for
future system-level twins suitable for Navy applications.

Strategic Approach
Our digital twin architecture is built on three foundational pillars:
 1. Data Infrastructure:
 Centralized, queryable databases and APIs integrate experimental, surveillance, and
 simulation data with robust security controls. Automated data ingestion minimizes manual
 intervention, accelerates time-to-insight, and ensures traceability.
 2. Modernized Computation:
 Scripted, testable pipelines replace legacy GUI-bound or spreadsheet workflows. Parallel
 execution on secure HPC environments and efficient data formats enable large parameter
 sweeps and scalable analytics.
 3. Hybrid Modeling:
 Physics-based models serve as the backbone, with data-informed calibration and surrogate
 reduced-order models enabling rapid, age-aware predictions and “what-if” analyses.
This balanced approach combines scientific fidelity, operational efficiency, and compliance with
national security requirements, making it adaptable to diverse components and systems.
Importantly, this strategy is designed for scalability—starting at the part and process level,
extending to the factory floor, and ultimately supporting enterprise-level digital twins that could
eventually connect multiple sites. This is directly analogous to the Navy’s need to manage
complex, multi-domain data across ships, equipment, personnel, and processes, and to eventually
scale to fleet-level digital twin management.

Application to a Ship-Level Digital Twin
Our framework generalizes naturally to ship-level applications, closely paralleling the Navy’s
requirements for digital twin deployment. At the component level, automated pipelines ingest
onboard sensor data and inspection records, while physics-based models of mounts, joints,
coatings, and structural elements are calibrated to observed performance and environmental
exposure. Surrogate models provide instant predictions of degradation and remaining useful life
for parts and assemblies, enabling real-time maintenance planning.

At the subsystem level, calibrated reduced-order models feed into integrated analyses of hull girder
response, vibration, corrosion progression, and fatigue accumulation under mission profiles.
Multi-fidelity simulation covers routine operations with efficient models, while explicit solvers
address extreme events such as slamming or collision scenarios. The ship-level digital twin
aggregates these calibrated subsystems, updating state estimates continuously and projecting
readiness under planned deployments.
Just as in advanced manufacturing, where varying levels of data availability and application needs
exist—from detailed process monitoring to high-level operational scheduling—a Navy ship’s
digital twin must handle diverse data streams (personnel, equipment, maintenance, flight
operations, etc.) and processes (worker shifts, predictive maintenance, mission planning). The
same secure data infrastructure, hybrid modeling methodology, and automation strategies
demonstrated for part-level twins enable fleet-scale risk management, reduced downtime, and
optimized maintenance cycles.
Our ongoing work at NNSA is also focused on scaling beyond a single manufacturing site to
enterprise-level digital twins that connect various sites within the national security enterprise—a
scope directly analogous to a digital twin of an entire Navy fleet. While these capabilities are
promising and under active development, it is important to note that they have not yet been
demonstrated at full enterprise or fleet scale.

Key Capabilities
Our digital twin work has enable key capabilities including:
 • Centralized Data Pipelines:
 o Living databases for experimental platforms, integrating hardware-software
 interfaces to store measurements directly into secure repositories.
 o Automated analysis workflows (e.g., impedance spectra fitting, mechanical test
 processing) with robust edge-case handling and quality checks.
 o Established data ontologies and APIs for accelerated aging datasets, enabling
 immediate use in modeling pipelines.
 • Workflow Modernization:
 o Translation of complex spreadsheet-based aging models into Python, preserving
 logic while improving reliability, transparency, and scalability.
 o Parallel simulation and analytics on secure computing resources, with dashboards
 for interactive exploration and rapid decision support.
 o Surrogate models provide instant predictions within sampled domains, decoupling
 user queries from expensive full simulations.
 • Hybrid Modeling and Calibration:
 o Automated finite element pipelines for part-level models, ingesting as-designed or
 as-built geometries to reflect manufacturing realities.
 o Calibration of material and model parameters to accelerated aging data using
 optimization and surrogate modeling, reducing error and quantifying trends in age-
 sensitive properties.
 o Validation of surrogate predictions against full simulations, building trust and
 interpretability.

Methodology
Our methodology is designed to flexibly leverage available data and balance scientific rigor with
operational efficiency. The choice of modeling approach is directly informed by the quantity and
quality of data, as well as workflow requirements. Table 1 summarizes how modeling modes are
aligned to data availability and workflow needs.

This tiered approach ensures that, regardless of data regime, the digital twin framework remains
robust, interpretable, and scalable:
 • Sparse data: We rely on physics-based or hybrid models, emphasizing careful calibration,
 uncertainty management, and verification and validation (V&V). This supports
 interpretability and scenario testing grounded in physical principles.
 • Moderate data: Physics-informed machine learning (ML) and AI approaches become
 feasible, leveraging feature engineering and regularization to improve accuracy while
 retaining physical constraints.
 • Large data: High-throughput ML/AI models and surrogates enable model compression
 and rapid screening, drastically reducing computational expense and enabling scalable
 analytics.
By dynamically aligning modeling strategies to data availability, our framework supports both
near-term operational needs and long-term system evolution.

Additional Methodological Highlights:
 • Physics-First Backbone:
 Use implicit and explicit finite element methods with age-aware constitutive behavior.
 Incorporate as-built geometries from manufacturing data or computed tomography (CT)-
 derived meshes to capture defects and variability.
 • Data-Informed Calibration:
 Define robust objective functions comparing simulated and measured performance metrics.
 Apply optimization loops to align simulations with experimental or surveillance data;
 where simulation cost is high, train surrogates to accelerate calibration while maintaining
 fidelity.
 • Surrogate Reduced-Order Models:
 Train surrogates (e.g., Gaussian process, graph-based) on parameterized simulation
 datasets for fast sensitivity studies, inverse design, and real-time “what-if” assessments,
 verified against full-order models.
 • Secure, Reproducible Execution:

 • Maintain code standards, documentation, version control, and environment reproducibility.
 Use secure storage, access controls, and Authority to Operate pathways consistent with
 mission data classification.

Progress Highlights
 • Automated experimental pipelines processed accelerated aging mechanical tests into
 aligned stress-strain curves and derived performance metrics, enabling direct model
 comparison.
 • Simulation pipelines transitioned from manual GUI workflows to automated Python-
 driven execution, reducing runtimes and increasing reachable strain regimes.
 • Calibration workflows reduced error between simulations and aging datasets, and
 established consistent trends in age-sensitive properties verified against experimental
 measures.
 • Legacy aging models were migrated to modern, parallelizable implementations with
 interactive dashboards, and prepared for surrogate training to deliver instantaneous
 predictions.
 • Data infrastructure matured from file-based sharing to centralized databases with
 automated ingestion and analysis, enabling iterative experimentation guided by AI
 optimizers.

Recommendations for Navy Digital Twin Deployment
 • Build a Secure, Centralized Data Backbone:
 Define schemas and metadata for multimodal inputs (mechanical tests, non destructive
 evaluation (NDE), sensor streams, environmental logs, maintenance events). Automate
 ingestion from shipboard and shore-based instruments into secure databases; enforce
 access controls, audit trails, and interoperability across commands.
 • Modernize High-Value Models and Tooling:
 Port spreadsheet or GUI-based models to scripted pipelines with unit tests and
 documentation. Leverage high performance computing (HPC) and approved cloud for
 unclassified workloads; use parallelization to generate datasets for surrogate training and
 uncertainty quantification. Provide role-based dashboards for operators, maintainers, and
 analysts.
 • Adopt Hybrid Modeling for Aging and Surveillance:
 Use physics-based models for critical parts and subsystems; calibrate to surveillance and
 accelerated aging data through optimization and surrogate loops. Employ surrogates for
 rapid prediction at scale, with periodic revalidation against full simulations.
 • Plan Multi-Fidelity Simulation Strategies:
 Combine efficient implicit models with explicit solvers for events like buckling, slamming,
 or blast-induced dynamics. Integrate viscoelastic, corrosion, fatigue, and time-temperature
 superposition models to extrapolate long-term behavior under operational profiles.
 • Ensure Compliance and Sustainment:
 Establish governance for data classification, Authority to Operate, and software assurance;
 maintain rigorous configuration control and verification and validation workflows.
 Develop collaboration protocols among Navy labs, shipyards, and OEMs. Invest in training
 and documentation to build enduring capability.

Planned Extensions
 • Integrate multi-temperature accelerated aging to enable time-temperature superposition
 and decadal predictions of material and system behavior.
 • Connect continuous load and condition monitoring data streams to hybrid modeling
 pipelines for near real-time state updates and calibration.
 • Explore agentic AI orchestration for data ingestion, calibration workflows, and natural
 language interfaces, ensuring auditability and policy enforcement as capabilities scale.

Conclusion
The NNSA’s digital twin strategy—proven at the part and process level and designed for scalable,
secure, and automated deployment—maps directly onto the Navy’s needs for ship- and fleet-level
digital twins. Our approach accounts for the diversity of data streams and operational requirements
found in both advanced manufacturing and naval operations, and is architected to scale from single
components to enterprise or fleet-wide deployments. While full-scale, cross-enterprise digital
twins remain an aspirational goal, our work provides a practical, adaptable pathway for the Navy
to realize real-time decision-making, proactive lifecycle management, and optimized readiness
across ships and fleets.
