Construction of PANoptosis-related lncRNA prognostic model and immunotherapy sensitivity analysis in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Xiang Xiong, Jian Ding, Chuan Yao
Published: 2026

Introduction

Lung cancer is the cancer with the highest mortality rate worldwide, of which lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) accounts for 40% [1]. Most patients have symptoms at an advanced stage and the five-year survival rate is only 15% [2]. LUAD treatments include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy. However, the efficacy of these treatments for patients with intermediate and advanced LUAD is very limited. Recently, predictions based on risk scores have been widely used [3–5], which are expected to have further clinical applications. Therefore, we need more comprehensive means to improve treatment efficiency. In summary, the construction of a prognostic model can help clinicians better predict the survival rate of LUAD patients.

When the body is exposed to pathogens or stress, it triggers the innate immune system and activates various cell death pathways, including nonlytic and lytic cell death. PANoptosis, a form of nonlytic cell death, is characterized by inflammatory lytic cell death facilitated by caspases and RIPKs through the PANoptosome complex [6]. Research has identified RIPK1, AIM2, ZBP1, NLRP3, and NLRP12 as key players in the formation of the PANoptosome complex [7, 8]. Numerous studies have linked PANoptosis to inflammatory diseases, cancer, neurological disorders, and infectious diseases [9–12], underscoring the importance of developing treatments that target the molecular mechanisms of PANoptosis.

Long ncRNA (lncRNA), where the term long refers to any length of 200 nucleotides or more, regulates the protein and nucleic acid interactions of gene expression in the nucleus and cytoplasm [13, 14]. lncRNA plays a variety of biological functions in cell development and metabolism, including genetic imprinting, chromatin modification, cell cycle regulation, transcription, splicing, and translation [15]. In addition, lncRNAs are involved in post-translational modification and reprogramming of cancer energy metabolism, cancer stem cell function, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition [16, 17]. lncRNA-CDC6 acts as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) by adsorbing microRNA-215 (miR-215), leading to the regulation of CDC6 expression and facilitating the metastasis and proliferation of breast cancer cells [18]. In gastric cancer (GC), lncRNA TP53TG1 inhibits the cell cycle progression, proliferation and metastasis of GC cells by interacting with cancer inhibitor of protein phosphatase 2A (CIP2A) [19]. In addition, Chen et al. found that lncRNA SNHG1, as a regulator of M2 macrophage polarization, promotes the development of breast cancer by regulating tumor growth and angiogenesis [20]. However, the potential mechanism of lncRNA in LUAD requires further exploration.

In summary, studying PANoptosis-related lncRNA is of great significance for the diagnosis and treatment of LUAD. While prior LUAD prognostic models have focused on apoptosis or pyroptosis pathways, PANoptosis represents a coordinated interplay of pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis mediated by the PANoptosome complex [21, 22]. This integrative mechanism offers a unique advantage in capturing the dynamic crosstalk between immune evasion and tumor progression, which is underexplored in existing lncRNA-based models. Current LUAD treatments include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapies such as EGFR/ALK inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). However, resistance to targeted therapies and variable responses to ICIs in advanced stages remain significant challenges. Our study provides a novel framework to predict prognosis and immunotherapy sensitivity.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https:// portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) was used to obtain the RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq)-based transcriptome profiling data, clinical information, and mutation data, which contained a total of 59 normal individuals and 541 patients with LUAD. To identify potential PANoptosis-related lncRNAs, we used the limma software package at |R|> 0.4 and P < 0.001 to perform co-expression correlation analysis on the expression of lncRNAs and PANoptosis-related genes [23, 24]. Pearson correlation thresholds (R > 0.4, P < 0.001) were selected to balance sensitivity and specificity, ensuring robust associations while minimizing false positives, as validated in prior pan-cancer lncRNA studies [25]. Additionally, we included Supplementary Figure S1 (flowchart) detailing the stepwise identification of PANoptosis-related lncRNAs, from co-expression analysis to model construction, to enhance reproducibility.

Identification of PANoptosis-related lncRNAs

Recent studies related to PANoptosis included the relevant genes in our study [21, 26, 27]. 541 LUAD samples were randomly divided into two groups at a one-to-one ratio. In the training group, we used multivariable Cox regression analysis to obtain PANoptosis-related lncRNAs. To reduce dimensionality and prevent overfitting, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression was applied to the candidate PANoptosis-related lncRNAs using the R package “glmnet”. Ten-fold cross-validation was performed to determine the optimal penalization parameter (lambda). Specifically, the data were randomly partitioned into 10 equally sized folds. The model was trained on 9 folds and validated on the remaining fold, cycling through all 10 folds. This process was repeated to identify the lambda value that minimized the partial likelihood deviance. The LASSO algorithm, through its L1 regularization, shrinks the coefficients of less contributive lncRNAs to zero, thereby automatically selecting the most predictive features while penalizing model complexity. The best prognostic lncRNA (P < 0.05) was determined based on multivariable Cox regression analysis, and the best model parameters were used for feature construction, and then the risk score was calculated. Risk score = β lncRNA1 × Exp lncRNA1 + β lncRNA2 × Exp lncRNA2 + β lncRNA3 × Exp lncRNA3 + ••• + β lncRNAn × Exp lncRNAn.

Survival analysis and independent prognostic analysis

We used the training group to build the prognostic model and used the testing group to verify the accuracy of the model. By constructing a prognostic model, we can obtain the formula of the model. The risk score of each sample was obtained according to the model formula. Samples were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Using the “survival” package, overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) of LUAD patients were constructed in high- and low-risk groups. Furthermore, univariate and multivariable independent prognostic analyses were obtained to observe the independent prognostic value of clinical features. Based on the risk scores, we used the “pheatmap” package to visualize LUAD patients’ survival time and lncRNAs expression. Finally, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year area under the ROC curve (AUC) of clinical features in the training, testing, and all groups were obtained using the “survival ROC” package.

Construction of nomogram and model validation

Using the “survival” and “rms” packages, nomograms were obtained including age, gender, T stage, TNM stage, N stage, and risk score. Calibration curves showed the difference between the predicted and observed outcomes of the nomogram. C-index curves were used to validate the reliability of the model. Then, survival analysis of LUAD patients at stages I–II and stages III–IV stages were observed.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and functional enrichment analysis

We used the “limma” and “scatterplot3d” packages to explore the distribution of LUAD patients with high- and low-risk groups through PCA analysis. Moreover, the gene ontology (GO) and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) enrichment analysis of PANoptosis-related lncRNAs was performed using the “clusterProfiler” package, and p-value < 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched in high-and low-risk groups.

Immune cell infiltration and tumor mutation burden (TMB) analysis

Immune cell infiltration was quantified using CIBERSORT (permutations = 1000; LM22 signature matrix) with p < 0.05 for validity. TMB was calculated as somatic mutations per megabase using maftools. For immunotherapy prediction, tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) scores (combining exclusion/dysfunction signatures) were computed, and drug sensitivity (IC50) was derived from Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) via oncoPredict (pFilter = 0.001). We first used the CIBERSORT algorithm to estimate the abundance of 22 immune cells in each sample. The values ​​with a p-value < 0.05 were then filtered to perform the final analysis. Finally, we used the “parblot” package in R language to visualize the 22 kinds of immune cells and the “vioplot” package for the control analysis between low-and high-risk groups. We used the “maftools” package to compare the relationship between TMB and high and low risk. The survival software package was used to further determine the relationship between high-and low-TMB groups and LUAD patient survival rates, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Immunotherapy target analysis and immune drug prediction

We obtained TIDE data related to non-small cell lung cancer through the website (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/). The TIDE algorithm is an effective tool for predicting sensitivity to anti-tumor immunotherapy. By using the “ggpubr” and “limma” packages, we obtained the relationship curve between TMB and risk score (p < 0.05). By using “oncoPredict”, “ggplot2” and “ggpubr” packages, we further predicted anti-tumor immune drugs and their treatment sensitivity (pFilter = 0.001, corPvalue = 0.001). We obtained the GDSC database from oncoPredict’s Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/c6tfx/).

Cell culture and drug treatment

Human LUAD cell lines (A549, H1299) and normal bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) were purchased from Fuheng Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). These cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% FBS. Cells were treated with anti-PD-1 antibody (10 μg/mL, Pembrolizumab) or 5-fluorouracil (50 μM) for 48 h [28, 29].

RNA extraction and qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent. cDNA was synthesized using PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix. SYBR Green assays were performed with primers specific for the 6 lncRNAs (sequences in Supplementary Table S1). GAPDH served as the internal control. Relative expression was calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCt method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses of the data were performed using R software (version 4.1.3) in this research. Student’s t-test or a one-way ANOVA was used for statistical analysis, and p < 0.05 was considered as statistical significance.

Discussion

Lung adenocarcinoma is still one of the most common lung cancers that plagues people around the world, especially late-stage lung adenocarcinoma, which has an inferior prognosis and survival rate [30]. Lung cancer treatment now relies on early detection, such as early screening with low-dose computed tomography, but this method has certain limitations. Therefore, it is essential to find characteristic factors that affect prognosis, which has important guiding significance for our early diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma [31]. LncRNA is a functional unit of more than 200 nucleotides that does not encode a protein and is involved in almost all cellular activities [32]. Studies have found that the expression of lncRNA LINC01614 in fibroblasts in LUAD patients is increased, accompanied by cells taking up more glutamine, which further aggravates the progression of cancer [33]. PANoptosis, characterized by pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis, is a new PANoptosome-mediated cell death pattern closely related to inflammatory diseases, infectious diseases, and cancer [34–36]. Yan et al. found that PANoptosome plays a key role in ischemia/reperfusion injury of retinal neurons [37]. Moreover, ursodeoxycholic acid attenuates sepsis-induced acute lung injury by inhibiting PANoptosis-like cell death through the STING pathway [38]. However, the mechanism of PANoptosis-related lncRNAs in LUAD needs further exploration.

In our study, Lasso Cox regression analysis identified PANoptosis-related lncRNAs. A total of 6 prognostic PANoptosis-related lncRNAs, including AL596223.1, AC069368.2, LINC02518, AL161431.1, LINC00892, and FAM83A-AS1 were obtained by multivariable Cox regression analysis, and the prognostic model was constructed. ROC analysis, survival rate comparison, nomogram evaluation, and pheatmap visualization indicated that the prognostic characteristics of 6 PANoptosis-related lncRNAs were effectively differentiated between high-risk and low-risk groups, as well as stages I–II and III–IV patients, accurately predicting the outcome for LUAD patients. These lncRNAs serve as independent prognostic factors, distinct from other common clinical features. Within the 6 PANoptosis-related lncRNAs in the LUAD signal, LINC02518, AL161431.1, LINC00892, and FAM83A-AS1 were found to be involved in cancer. Knockdown of lncRNA AL161431.1 elevated cancer cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. The role of lncRNA AL161431.1 in cancer is linked to its facilitation of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition [39]. LINC00892, as a lncRNA induced by T cell activation and expressed by follicular lymphoma resident T helper cells, plays a unique biological role in sarcoma, bladder cancer, and ovarian cancer [40–43]. Recently, researchers found that FAM83A-AS1 promote LUAD progression through FAM83A-AS1/HIF-1α/glycolysis axis [44]. Moreover, FAM83A-S1 regulates the expression of FAM83A by binding to miR-495-3p, and knocking out FAM83A or FAM83AS1 inhibits the proliferation, migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition process of LUAD cells [45–47]. However, this mechanism also exists in pancreatic cancer [48]. Furthermore, the GO and GSEA pathway analysis suggested that PANoptosis-related lncRNAs may be related to LUAD development. Proposed roles for LINC02518 and FAM83A-AS1 in regulating PANoptosis via NLRP3 inflammasome activation, supported by GSEA enrichment of immune response pathways. Reducing the expression of lncRNA FLJ20021 significantly diminished the resistance to cisplatin and triggered PANoptosis in Hep-2/R cells [49]. In contrast, the knockdown of AC133552.2 enhanced cell proliferation and migration, whereas its overexpression was found to suppress tumor growth and metastasis, as demonstrated in xenograft models [50]. Previous in vitro studies have demonstrated that knockdown of the relevant lncRNA expression can indirectly influence the occurrence and progression of PANoptosis. Although the 5-year OS AUC (0.633) highlights room for improvement, this may reflect the inherent heterogeneity of LUAD. Future integration of multi-omics data could enhance long-term predictive accuracy.

We then analyzed the relationship between TMB and risk scores in LUAD patients and found that patients in the high TMB and low-risk group showed the best prognosis in our study. TMB has been proven to be an effective indicator for predicting immunotherapy in various cancers. Signature 1 High GCTMB-H can be used as a marker of poor prognosis in gastric cancer [51]. Expression of TMB and PD-L1 confirmed as potential predictive biomarkers for immunotherapy in advanced Pulmonary sarcomatoid carcinoma (PSC) [52]. Furthermore, TMB is an independent predictive factor for adjuvant anti-PD-1 therapy in patients with high-risk melanoma [53]. Our results show that patients with high TMB have a lower survival rate (P < 0.05), and the expression of TP53, TTN, and MUC16 is increased in high-risk patients. Mutations in the tumor suppressor gene TP53 promote the occurrence of tumors, and treatment of mutated TP53 is an important means [54]. p53 protein is a transcription factor involved in DNA repair, autophagy, senescence, cell cycle control and apoptosis [55]. Recent research found that upregulation of TP53 inhibited TCF4 transactivation and induced ER stress dysregulation in tumor cells, accelerating cancer cell death [56]. Conclusion

In this study, we constructed a LUAD-specific PANoptosis-related lncRNA model and verified the accuracy of the model based on the risk score, and further explored the TMB analysis and sensitivity analysis of immunotherapy, which provides a new direction for survival prediction and treatment of clinical LUAD patients.

Limitations

While our study provides a novel PANoptosis-related lncRNA prognostic model for LUAD, several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis relied solely on TCGA data, which lacks detailed treatment history and long-term follow-up information. The inclusion of multi-center cohorts with standardized treatment protocols would improve generalizability. Although we identified 6 lncRNAs as independent prognostic factors, their functional roles in regulating PANoptosis remain unvalidated. Future studies using in vitro knockdown (siRNA) or in vivo models are needed to confirm causality. The predicted drug sensitivity is based on computational models (OncoPredict), which may not fully recapitulate in vivo pharmacokinetics or tumor-stroma interactions. Preclinical validation in patient-derived organoids (PDOs) is essential. Furthermore, the computational drug predictions in the manuscript are only hypothesis-generated and do not represent clinical recommendations for LUAD. In the future, we will further stratify sensitivity analysis or prognosis based on EGFR mutation status to strengthen the clinical relevance of the gefitinib-related findings.
