Abstract
. Given a , b , c ∈ ℕ a,b,c\in\mathbb{N} , let D a , b , c D_{a,b,c} be the tournament on a + b + c a+b+c vertices obtained by replacing the vertices of the directed triangle C 3 C_{3} with transitive tournaments T ​ T a TT_{a} , T ​ T b TT_{b} , and T ​ T c TT_{c} , respectively. We generalize this result to arbitrary blow-ups D a , b , c D_{a,b,c} . We also establish a stronger stability result: if ( a + b + c ) | n (a+b+c)\mid n , then either G G contains a D a , b , c D_{a,b,c} -factor, or G G is close to an extremal graph. 1.
Introduction

Notation follows
bang2009
, so we only repeat a few definitions here (also see Section
2
). A
digraph
is not allowed to have parallel edges or loops and an
oriented graph
is a digraph with no 2-cycle. A

is an oriented graph in which every two vertices are adjacent. The
minimum semi-degree

is the minimum of all the in-degrees and out-degrees of the vertices in
G
. The
minimum total degree

\delta(G)
of

is the minimum number of edges incident to a vertex in
G
. A tournament

on
n
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
is called a
semi-regular tournament
.
When

is odd, an
n
-vertex semi-regular tournament is precisely a
regular tournament
. Let
TT_{k}
be the transitive tournament on
k
vertices. Given integers
a,b,c
with
1\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant c
, we use

to denote the tournament on
a+b+c
vertices obtained from the directed triangle
C_{3}
by replacing its vertices with
TT_{a},TT_{b}
and
TT_{c}
, respectively.
For convenience, we use

to denote

for any

. Clearly,

.

For digraphs

and

-tiling
in

is a family of pairwise disjoint copies of

in

.
An

is an

-tiling that covers all vertices of

Two fundamental problems in graph theory are to find sufficient conditions on

contains a copy of

(

) or an

-factor (

).
In this paper, we consider these two problems for oriented graphs.
We begin by reviewing some related results.

1. Turán-type problem

We say that an oriented graph

such that every

Theorem 1.1

(Bollobás, Häggkvist
bollobasJCTB50
)
.

A tournament

for some

.

Let

be the directed cycle and path of length

to

of

DeBiasio et al.

showed that Theorem

also holds for powers of directed cycles.
More precisely, they proved that given

with

is Turánable if and only if

for some

, or equivalently,

is Turánable if and only if

.
DeBiasio et al.
debiasioCPC35
also conjectured that Theorem

can be extended to all oriented graphs.
Very recently, Araujo and Xiang

disproved this conjecture by building a
5
-vertex counterexample. Our first result shows that this conjecture has many counterexamples.

Proposition 1.2
.

There exists an infinite family of Turánable oriented graphs, none of which is a subgraph of
D_{s}
for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

As mentioned above, an oriented graph is Turánable if it is contained in every sufficiently large regular tournament
G
.
It is then natural to ask for the smallest number

such that

guarantees the existence of a copy of
H
in an
n
-vertex oriented graph

. We will consider the asymptotic normalized version of the minimum semi-degree threshold. We define

\kappa^{0}(H)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g(n,H)}{n}.

When

is an oriented cycle,

is a special case of the minimum semi-degree version of the famous Caccetta–Häggkvist Conjecture

, and was studied extensively by Kühn, Osthus and Piguet

. Determining

is highly non-trivial even when

is of constant order. DeBiasio et al.

asked the following question.

Question 1

(
debiasioCPC35
)
.

Is it true that
\kappa^{0}(C_{6}^{2})=2/5
?

In
araujoARXIV2025
, Araujo and Xiang presented a construction which shows that

. In this paper, we give two lower bound constructions for

and

Proposition 1.3
.

\kappa^{0}(C_{l}^{2})\geqslant 4/9
for all

, and
\kappa^{0}(P_{l}^{2})\geqslant 3/8
for all

Moreover, there exists a

-regular
(

-regular
)

containing no

(
resp.,
P_{l}^{2}
)
.

2. Tiling problem

The tiling problem for oriented graphs has attracted considerable attention in recent years. In
keevashJCTB99
, Keevash and Sudakov proved that, for sufficiently large
n
, every
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(1/2-o(1))n
contains a
C_{3}
-tiling covering all but at most three vertices. Subsequently, Li and Molla
liEJC26
confirmed a conjecture of Cuckler
cuckler2008
and Yuster
yusterCSR1
by proving that every sufficiently large semi-regular tournament on
n\in 3\mathbb{N}
vertices contains a
C_{3}
-factor. In
wangJGT106
, Wang, Yan, and Zhang extended the result of Keevash and Sudakov

by showing that, for every integer
l\geqslant 4
and sufficiently large

, every
n
-vertex oriented graph with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(1/2-o(1))n
contains a
C_{l}
-factor.

Transitive tournament factors have also been studied extensively in recent years. Yuster
yusterorder20
showed that every sufficiently large oriented graph
G
on
n\in 3\mathbb{N}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant 5n/6
contains a
TT_{3}
-factor. Treglown
treglownJGT69
conjectured that the condition
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant 7n/18
guarantees a
TT_{3}
-factor, and he also provided an extremal example showing that this semi-degree threshold is best possible. This conjecture was later resolved for sufficiently large oriented graphs by Balogh, Lo, and Molla
baloghJCTB124
. Furthermore, DeBiasio, Lo, Molla, and Treglown

proved that
\delta(G)\geqslant(11/12+o(1))n
suffices to guarantee a
TT_{4}
-factor in an oriented graph on
n\in 4\mathbb{N}
vertices, and that this condition is asymptotically tight. In the same paper
debiasioSJDM35
, they also established the best currently known general upper bound on the minimum total degree that guarantees a
TT_{k}
-factor for all
k\geqslant 5
. We remark that the oriented Ramsey number of

plays an important role in the study of the
TT_{k}
-factor problem, see
debiasioSJDM35
.

The definition of
D_{a,b,c}
suggests that

(with

) contains a directed cycle of length

and contains a

-factor when

.
Hence,

serves as a natural bridge between the tiling problems for cycles and those for transitive tournaments. In
araujoARXIV2025
, Araujo and Xiang proved that every sufficiently large semi-regular tournament of order
n
, with

, contains a
D_{1,1,2}
-factor. They further asked for which integers
1\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant c
every sufficiently large semi-regular tournament satisfying the necessary divisibility conditions admits a
D_{a,b,c}
-factor. In this paper, we answer this question completely.

Given
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\gcd(p,q)
be the greatest common divisor between
p
and
q
. In particular,
\gcd(p,0)=p
for any positive integer

and
\gcd(0,0)
is undefined.

Theorem 1.4
.

Given
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant c
, there exists
\eta>0
such that the following holds for all sufficiently large
n\in(a+b+c)\mathbb{N}
. If
\gcd(a+b+c,c^{2}-ab)=1
, then every
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(1/2-\eta)n
contains a
D_{a,b,c}
-factor.

The remaining case

is settled by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Given
b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leqslant b\leqslant c
. If either
3\mid(1+b+c)
or
\gcd(1+b+c,c^{2}-b)=1
, then every sufficiently large semi-regular tournament on
n\in(1+b+c)\mathbb{N}
vertices contains a
D_{1,b,c}
-factor.

Recall that Li and Molla

showed that every sufficiently large semi-regular tournament on

vertices contains a

-factor, i.e.,

-factor. Theorem

clearly contains this result as a special case. Moreover, Theorem

generalizes the result of Araujo and Xiang on

-factors. Indeed, when

and

, it holds that
\gcd(a+b+c,c^{2}-ab)=1
.

As mentioned in

-factors.
The next two propositions extend this result to all

and their proofs will be presented in Appendix

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant c
. If
\gcd(a+b+c,c^{2}-ab)>1
, then there exists a semi-regular tournament on

-factor.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let

with
1\leqslant b\leqslant c
. If

and

-factor.

Motivated by Propositions

and

-tiling one can guarantee in oriented graphs that are close to regular tournaments.
Our next result shows that, for any

Theorem 1.8

Given

Proposition
1.7
.

Let
b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leqslant b\leqslant c
. If

-factors.

Proof.

Given
b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leqslant b\leqslant c

and

,
r_{2}=\lfloor h/3\rfloor
, and

Let

be a blow-up of directed triangle

obtained by replacing its vertices with three semi-regular tournaments

on

,

, and

vertices, respectively. Set

. It is not difficult to check that
T
is a semi-regular tournament on
(3k+1)h
vertices. Moreover, by an argument similar to that used in the proof of Proposition
1.6
, every copy of
D_{1,b,c}
in
T
is either of type-
(i)
or of type-
(i,i+1,i+2)
with respect to
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})
for some
i\in[3]
. Thus, the existence of a
D_{1,b,c}
-factor
\mathcal{H}
in

implies the existence of an integer solution to the following system of congruences:

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x+cy+bz\equiv r_{1}\pmod{h}\\
bx+y+cz\equiv r_{2}\pmod{h}\\
cx+by+z\equiv r_{3}\pmod{h},\end{cases}

where
x,y,
and
z
are the numbers of copies of
D_{1,b,c}
in
\mathcal{H}
that have type-
(1,2,3)
, type-
(2,3,1)
, and type-
(3,1,2)
, respectively.

Set
\Delta=c^{2}-b
. Assume that
3\nmid h
and
\gcd(h,\Delta)>1
. Let
p
be a prime divisor of
\gcd(h,\Delta)
. Clearly,
p>1
,
and
h,\Delta\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Since

, we have

, and hence

\Delta=c^{2}-b\equiv 1+b+b^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
This implies that
b^{2}\equiv-1-b\equiv c\pmod{p}
, and thus
b^{3}\equiv bc\pmod{p}
. Moreover, as
b^{3}=(b-1)(1+b+b^{2})+1
, we have

bc\equiv b^{3}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Since
1+b+b^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, we must have

. Indeed, if
b\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, then
1\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, forcing
p=1
, contrary to
p>1
. If
b\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, then
3\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, so
p=3
, which is impossible since
3\nmid h
while
p\mid h
.

Next we claim that the semi-regular tournament
T
has no
D_{1,b,c}
-factor by showing that (
3
) has no integer solution. Suppose to the contrary that
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
is an integer solution to (

). Since the congruences in (

) hold modulo

and

, reducing them modulo

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{0}+cy_{0}+bz_{0}\equiv r_{1}\pmod{p}\\

,

.

, and

,
