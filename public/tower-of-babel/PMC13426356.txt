A Functional Variant of CYP4V2 Contributes to Asthma Susceptibility Through Methylation-Mediated Regulation of the Arachidonic Acid Pathway
Authors: Xiangran Chen, Terence Yin Weng Lam, Yang Yie Sio, Yee-How Say, Kavita Reginald, Fook Tim Chew
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCYP4V2 is involved in lipid metabolism, but its contribution to asthma is poorly understood. This study aimed to investigate genetic polymorphisms and regulatory features of CYP4V2 in asthma development.MethodsWe performed an integrative multi-omics analysis combining transcriptomic, genetic, and epigenetic data from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) of individuals in the Singapore/Malaysia Cross-Sectional Genetics and Epidemiological Study (SMCSGES) cohort. The influence of CYP4V2 expression on asthma risk and expression of genes involved in arachidonic acid (AA) pathway were evaluated, followed by expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) analysis to identify regulatory variants. DNA methylation analyses were conducted to assess epigenetic regulation of CYP4V2, and promoter reporter assays were used to functionally validate candidate regulatory CpG sites.ResultsThe upregulation of CYP4V2 in PBMCs was associated with an increased risk of asthma and inversely associated with PTGER2 and ALOX5AP in the AA pathway. The SNP rs2276921 was identified as the only expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) associated with both increased CYP4V2 expression and asthma risk (FDR-adjusted p < 0.05; OR = 1.24, 95% CI = 1.10–1.40). The minor allele G of rs2276921 was associated with low methylation at cg23232844 (p = 3.94 × 10−3) and cg11969330 (p = 4.25 × 10−2), located in the promoters of the protein-coding and non-coding CYP4V2 isoforms respectively. Promoter assays indicated that these two CpG sites inhibited CYP4V2 expression, supporting the regulatory effect of rs2276921 on CYP4V2 expression may be mediated through methylation.ConclusionThis study identified rs2276921 and CpG sites cg23232844 and cg11969330 that regulate CYP4V2 expression. CYP4V2 potentially modulates the AA metabolism and contributes to asthma susceptibility. These findings suggest CYP4V2 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in asthma.

Introduction

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease that affects approximately 300 million people worldwide, with diverse epidemiological characteristics across populations.1,2 The lifetime prevalence of asthma among the Chinese population in Singapore and Malaysia is as high as 19.1%.3 Asthma is clinically heterogeneous, reflecting distinct inflammatory processes that contribute to variation in disease progression and treatment responsiveness.4,5 These inflammatory differences are broadly classified into two major endotypes: Th2-high and Th2-low. The Th2-high endotype is driven by IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13 associated eosinophilic inflammation, whereas the Th2-low endotype typically involves neutrophilic or pauci-granulocytic airway infiltration.6,7 The maturation of multi-omics technologies has enabled more comprehensive characterization of the molecular features underlying asthma, facilitating the delineation of distinct inflammatory endotypes.4 Family history of asthma is the strongest intrinsic risk factor, underscoring an important role of genetic susceptibility in disease development.8 Previous genetic studies have shown that asthma is a complex disease involving multiple interacting genes, with more than 100 susceptibility loci reported across the genome, including regions such as 5q13 and 17q21.9 However, the genetic architecture of asthma may vary across populations due to differences in environmental exposures and genetic backgrounds, highlighting the importance of population-specific studies. Such variation suggests that differences in disease susceptibility and inflammatory responses may arise from distinct regulatory mechanisms. In this context, clarifying the genetic and molecular regulation of inflammation-related genes across different populations is critical for understanding endotype diversity and may support more tailored treatment approaches.

The arachidonic acid (AA) pathway serves as a central inflammatory mediator in asthma, involving multiple lipid mediators and their associated regulatory gene networks.10–12 Dysregulation of AA metabolism has been implicated in Th2-high asthma endotypes, such as aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD), where reduced prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) and increased cysteinyl leukotriene (cysLT) production drive eosinophilic airway inflammation.7 In addition to the classical cyclooxygenases (COXs) and lipoxygenases (LOXs) pathways, members of the cytochrome P450 (CYP) family have been implicated in asthma pathogenesis, yet many of them remain functionally uncharacterized.13–15 Cytochrome P450 4V2 (CYP4V2) is an ω-hydroxylase of the CYP4 family localized to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and is involved in maintaining cellular lipid homeostasis.16,17 CYP4V2 mutants have been linked to Bietti’s crystalline corneoretinal dystrophy (BCD), accompanied by the abnormal accumulation of docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), and AA in retinal pigment epithelial cells (RPE).18,19 Beyond the ocular tissues, CYP4V2 is widely expressed in many organs including whole blood.20 Recent work has shown its marked upregulation during the differentiation of THP-1 monocytes into macrophages, suggesting a potential role for CYP4V2 in inflammatory regulation of immune cells.21 Given its role in fatty acid metabolism and emerging evidence of its involvement in immune cell differentiation, CYP4V2 may represent a previously underexplored regulator of inflammatory lipid pathways in asthma. However, the genetic and epigenetic factors influencing CYP4V2 expression, and how these regulatory layers may contribute to lipid-mediated inflammatory processes in asthma, have not been systematically investigated. Understanding how CYP4V2 is regulated may provide novel insights into biomarker development and therapeutic targeting of lipid-mediated inflammation in asthma.

In this study, we investigated the genetic association between CYP4V2 and asthma in the Singapore/Malaysia Cross-Sectional Genetic Epidemiology Study (SMCGES) cohort. We first analyzed peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) transcriptome data to evaluate the influence of CYP4V2 expression on asthma risk and the expression of genes involved in AA pathways. We then integrated genotype data to identify expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) associated with CYP4V2 expression and assessed their association with asthma susceptibility. We further examined the associations between single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), DNA methylation patterns and CYP4V2 expression. Finally, we performed an in vitro promoter reporter assay to functionally characterize the regulatory effects of key promoter CpG sites on CYP4V2 expression.

Methods

Study Participants and Phenotype Definitions

Participants were drawn from SMCSGES cohort. The recruitment process and study design for this cohort have been detailed in previous literature.3,8,22,23 Atopy was defined as a positive skin prick test (SPT) to at least one common house dust mite allergen (Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus or Blomia tropicalis), with a wheal size ≥ 3 mm.

Asthma cases were determined as individuals who reported a physician-diagnosed history of asthma and had evidence of atopy. Healthy controls were participants with no history of asthma and a negative SPT result. Asthma cases were further stratified by disease severity based on reported clinical symptoms and healthcare utilization. Severe asthma was defined as the presence of any of the following: daytime or night-time asthma attacks, school absence, general practitioner (GP) or specialist visits, accident and emergency (A&E) admission, hospitalization, or any exacerbation event. Cases not meeting these criteria were classified as mild.

PBMC RNA-Seq Processing and Expression Quantification

PBMCs were obtained from participants’ peripheral blood (10 mL) via Ficoll-Hypaque density gradient centrifugation, followed by total RNA extraction using the E.Z.N.A.® Total RNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek). mRNA was enriched using oligo (dT) magnetic beads, and sequencing libraries were constructed using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB). The libraries were sequenced at both ends using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw reads were aligned to the GRCh37 reference genome using TopHat v2.1.1, the transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) were calculated using Cufflinks v2.2.1 and further converted to the transcripts per million (TPM). To improve the comparability of expression values between samples, TPM was normalized using the GTEx protocol with inverse normal transformation (INT).24 All samples were sequenced in the same batch to reduce potential batch effects.

Genotype Data Quality Control and Filtering

Genotyping data were derived from genomic DNA extracted from oral epithelial cells by using the AxyPrep™ Multisource Genomic Miniprep DNA kit (Axygen, USA). The original genotypes were generated using multiple Illumina whole-genome arrays, including Infinium OmniZhongHua-8 (v1.3/v1.4), HumanHap550v3, Omni2.5Exome, and Global Screening Array. Subsequently, IMPUTE2 was used to uniformly imputate data based on the 1000 Genomes Project Phase 3 (CHB) reference panel to ensure consistency with allele frequencies in the Chinese population.

DNA Methylation Profiling

DNA methylation data were generated using Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip. After background and color shift correction, the raw signals were processed according to standard procedures to remove probes with insufficient detection quality, cross-reactive probes, probes affected by SNPs, and other unsuitable probes. The methylation signals were then quantified using the minfi package (R 3.6.1), and β values were calculated as the methylation level of CpG sites.

Data Subsets and Statistical Analyses

For the differential expression analysis, 145 participants with complete phenotype and PBMC expression data were included. The difference in CYP4V2 expression between asthma cases and healthy controls was assessed using a two-tailed Student’s t test. Linear regression models were used to assess the association between AA pathway gene expression (PTGER2 and ALOX5AP) and CYP4V2 expression, with CYP4V2 expression as the outcome variable. Each model included gene expression, disease status, and a gene expression × disease status interaction term. eQTL analysis was conducted on 582 participants who had both expression and genotype data (4,744,873 SNPs). Association between CYP4V2 expression and SNPs in ±50 kb flanking regions of the gene were assessed using linear regression models. The LD pattern (r2 ≥ 0.8) of eQTL was calculated, and tag SNPs were selected by Haploview (Broad Institute).

To identify the asthma susceptibility loci, disease association analysis was conducted in 2407 participants (asthma cases n = 1,328, healthy controls n = 1,079) who completed genotyping. Logistic regression models (adjusted for age and sex) were used by PLINK 1.9 to assess the relationship between eQTL tag SNPs and asthma risk.

Methylation regulation analysis was conducted on 263 participants with genotype, methylation, and expression data. One-way ANOVA was used to compare differences in CpG methylation levels among individuals with different genotypes. CpG methylation levels were then dichotomized into high and low groups based on median, and a two-tailed Student’s t test was used to assess the difference between methylation status and CYP4V2 expression. In addition, correlation analyses were performed to assess the relationship between methylation levels and CYP4V2 expression as continuous variables.

All multiple testing p values were corrected for the false discovery rate (FDR) using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Linear regression, one-way ANOVA and two-tailed Student’s t test were calculated in R 4.3.1. Scatter and dot plots were generated using the ggplot2 package in R 4.3.1. Detailed information for each subset can be found in Table S1.

In vitro Luciferase Assay

The promoter regions of CYP4V2 isoforms (GRCh37: chr4:187,110,978–187,112,977; chr4:187,123,448–187,125,447) were cloned into the promoter less Firefly luciferase reporter pGL4.10 (Promega) to construct the wild-type plasmid. Subsequently, a C to T mutation was introduced at cg23232844 and cg11969330 to generate the corresponding mutant constructs. All promoter constructs were co-transfected with the internal control plasmid Renilla luciferase reporter pGL4.74 (Promega) for signal correction.

Human leukemia monocytic (THP-1), purchasing from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, USA) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Cytiva) containing 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum and maintained at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Transient transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). To investigate the effect of CpG methylation on promoter activity, different concentrations of 5-AZa-2′ -deoxycytidine (DAC) (0 μM, 0.5 μM, 1 μM, and 5 μM) were added after transfection and incubated for 24 h.

Cells were lysed with Passive Lysis Buffer 24 h after transfection, and luciferase activity was measured using a Promega Dual-Luciferase® Reporter Assay System and a GloMax® Discover microplate reader. Firefly signals were corrected for relative luminescent units (RLUs), and empty vector pGL4.10 was used as background control. All experiments were technically replicated and performed at least three independent biological replicates.

Results

CYP4V2 Upregulation is Associated with Increased Asthma Risk

To explore the potential role of CYP4V2 in the pathogenesis of asthma, we analyzed the whole-transcriptome expression data of PBMCs from the SMCSGES cohort (n = 145, Table S1). The results showed that the expression level of CYP4V2 was significantly higher in asthma patients compared with healthy controls (t test, p = 0.005, Figure 1A). Linear regression revealed significant interaction effects between gene expression and disease status when assessing associations CYP4V2 with PTGER2 (β = 0.40, p = 0.008, Table 1) and ALOX5AP (β = 0.41, p = 0.010, Table 1). Interestingly, CYP4V2 expression exhibited opposite correlation patterns with these genes in asthma patients compared with healthy controls (Figure 1B and C). Given that PTGER2 and ALOX5AP participate in AA related inflammatory pathways, these opposite trends suggest that CYP4V2 expression may modulate inflammatory processes relevant to asthma susceptibility. rs2276921 Showed Significant Association with CYP4V2 Expression and Asthma

We next sought to elucidate the genetic polymorphisms underlying the association between CYP4V2 expression and asthma. First, we examined genetic variants situated in the ±50 kb flanking regions of the CYP4V2 gene (chr4:187,062,674–187,184,617, GRCh37/hg19) and identified 347 SNPs. 283 common SNPs were retained after applying quality control filters of MAF > 0.05 and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE, p > 0.001). Using whole-transcriptome expression and genotype data from the SMCSGES eQTL cohort (n = 582, Table S1), we performed linear regression to assess the eQTL association between genotype of the variants and CYP4V2 expression. To further evaluate whether these tag-SNPs are associated with asthma susceptibility, genotype distributions were compared among 2,407 participants from the SMCSGES disease association cohort (n = 1,328 asthma cases, n = 1,079 healthy controls, Table S1) using logistic regression analysis. Only SNP rs2276921 remained statistically significant after correcting for multiple testing (Table S2). Notably, the minor allele G of rs2276921 was significantly positively associated with the risk of asthma (FDR p = 0.0131, OR = 1.24, 95% CI = 1.10–1.40, Figure 2A). This disease association was directionally consistent with the eQTL results, as the risk allele G that increased asthma susceptibility also associated with higher CYP4V2 expression in PBMCs (FDR p = 0.005, Figure 2B). However, in the GTEx v8 database (https://gtexportal.org/), CYP4V2 expression in whole blood stratified by rs2276921 genotype revealed a significant stepwise increase from GG to GC and CC genotypes (p = 5.37 × 10−3; Figure 2C). This trend was also corroborated by meta-analysis data from the eQTLGen Consortium (https://www.eqtlgen.org/), where the allele C of rs2276921 showed a positive association with CYP4V2 expression (Z = 28.8, p = 1.12 × 10−182; Table S3). rs2276921 is Associated with cg23232844 and cg11969330 Methylation Sites at CYP4V2 Promoters

We investigated whether rs2276921 might influence CYP4V2 expression through DNA methylation, given that rs2276921 is not in LD with SNPs on the promoter of the gene (Figures S1A and B) and is located distally from the transcriptional start sites of the CYP4V2 isoforms (Figure 3A and B). Importantly, rs2276921 is not located within any transcription factor binding site or predicted to have any regulatory feature according to the functional annotation results from SNPinfo.25 Within the ±50 kb regions surrounding rs2276921, a total of 73 CpG sites were assessed for methylation levels. ANOVA analysis with FDR correction identified seven CpG sites whose methylation levels were significantly associated with both rs2276921 genotype and CYP4V2 expression in the same regulatory direction. Specifically, the minor allele G of rs2276921 was associated with lower methylation levels and increased CYP4V2 expression. Among these, two CpG sites are located within the promoter regions of distinct CYP4V2 transcripts (Figure 3A and B). The site cg11969330 was significantly associated with rs2276921 genotype (ANOVA FDR p = 0.043, Figure 4A) and showed differential CYP4V2 expression between high and low methylation groups (t test FDR p = 1.90 × 10−9, Figure 4B). Similarly, cg23232844 was associated with rs2276921 genotype (ANOVA FDR p = 3.94 × 10−3, Figure 4C) and differential CYP4V2 expression (t test FDR p = 3.69 × 10−4, Figure 4D). In addition to the group-based comparisons, we further assessed the relationship between methylation levels and CYP4V2 expression using Pearson correlation analysis. Consistent with the above findings, both cg11969330 (Pearson r = −0.500, FDR p = 6.59 × 10−16) and cg23232844 (Pearson r = −0.308, FDR p = 3.17 × 10−6) exhibited significant negative correlations with CYP4V2 expression supporting a continuous relationship between methylation and gene expression. These findings suggest that rs2276921 may regulate CYP4V2 transcription indirectly by modulating methylation at key promoter regions, contributing to its role in asthma pathogenesis. The luciferase activity of both Hap1Δ and Hap2Δ mutants were significantly higher than their respective wild-type constructs (Figure 5C), indicating that the methylation of these two CpG sites may inhibit CYP4V2 promoter activity. Consistent with this, treatment with the DNA methyltransferase inhibitor DAC induced a dose-dependent increase in luciferase activity in cells transfected with the Hap1 construct (Figure 5D), further supporting the methylation-dependent repression at the cg23232844 site. However, DAC treatment had no significant effect on the Hap2 construct (Figure 5E), suggesting that promoter activity driven by the cg11969330 region is less sensitive to DNA demethylation. Together, these findings indicate that the two CpG sites differentially regulate the promoter activities of distinct CYP4V2 transcript variants.

Discussion

Previous studies based on the SMCSGES population have functionally characterized multiple asthma susceptibility loci across several chromosomal regions and biological pathways, including 17q12–21,26–28 4q21,29 and 2q,30 as well as genes involved in AA pathway31 and allergic inflammation.32 Within the AA metabolic network, gene variants in the COX pathway were shown to be associated with asthma risk in SMCSGES cohort, supporting a genetic link between AA metabolism and asthma pathogenesis.31 However, the CYP pathway in asthma remains less defined.

In this study, higher CYP4V2 expression levels in PBMCs were associated with increased asthma risk in the SMCSGES cohort (Figure 1A), supporting a role for CYP4V2 in disease development. Within the CYP4 subfamily, CYP4A11 and CYP4F2 are known to catalyze AA to 20-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (20-HETE), a lipid mediator implicated in airway inflammation and airway hyperresponsiveness.33 Given that CYP4 family members participate in lipid metabolic pathways implicated in airway inflammation, the upregulation of CYP4V2 observed in asthma patients suggests that it may function similarly.

The opposite associations observed between CYP4V2 expression and key AA pathway genes may reflect altered regulation of the AA pathway under asthmatic conditions (Figure 1B and C). In asthma patients, CYP4V2 was inversely associated with PTGER2 and ALOX5AP, two genes representing distinct functional branches of the AA pathway. ALOX5AP is part of the LOX pathway and is required for 5-lipoxygenase–dependent leukotriene production and has been consistently associated with asthma susceptibility.34,35 PTGER2 is part of the COX pathway and encodes the EP2 receptor for prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), which contributes to immune regulation and airway protection in immune cells.36–38 These patterns suggest that CYP4V2 upregulation may alter other AA pathway components in asthma. One possible interpretation is that CYP4V2 upregulation may be linked to preferential metabolism of AA by CYP enzymes and reduced dependency on LOX and COX enzymes. This may alter the composition of downstream lipid mediators, including epoxyeicosatrienoates (EETs) and hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids (HETEs), which have been implicated in asthma-related airway inflammation.14,39 However, as lipid mediators were not directly measured in this study, the impact on downstream metabolites remains to be validated. In addition, we evaluated potential confounding effects of clinical and demographic factors, including disease severity (Figure S2A), medication use (Figure S2B–I), sex (Figure S2J), and age (Figure S2K). No significant associations with CYP4V2 expression were observed, suggesting that these factors are unlikely to be major determinants of CYP4V2 expression in this cohort.

At the genetic level, we identified rs2276921 as the only SNP significantly associated with both CYP4V2 expression and asthma susceptibility (Figure 2A and B). In the SMCSGES cohort, the G allele of rs2276921 was associated with CYP4V2 upregulation and increased asthma risk. Conversely, CYP4V2 upregulation was associated with the C allele in GTEx (Figure 2C) and eQTLGen (Table S3) which predominantly comprise individuals of European ancestry. Although eQTL effects are often shared across populations, their effect sizes and transferability may vary between ancestry groups due to differences in allele frequency, linkage disequilibrium structure, and haplotype background.40–42 Therefore, rs2276921 should be considered a candidate regulatory variant requiring further validation in independent and ethnically diverse cohorts to assess the robustness and generalizability of this association.

Previous studies have shown that non-coding regulatory variants can influence gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms, particularly by modulating DNA methylation at promoter or enhancer regions.43–45 Given that rs2276921 is located distally upstream of CYP4V2 and does not alter the coding sequence or splice structure, its regulatory effect is more likely mediated through epigenetic mechanisms. In this study, two CpG sites cg23232844 and cg11969330 within promoter regions of distinct CYP4V2 isoforms were identified as functionally relevant regulatory elements and demonstrated using reporter promoter assays (Figures 4 and 5). Mutation at cg23232844 to abolish the CpG site markedly increased promoter activity of Hap1Δ and cg23232844-containing Hap1 showed strong responsiveness to pharmacological demethylation, consistent with a methylation-dependent mode of regulation. In contrast, mutation at cg11969330 also enhanced promoter activity of Hap2Δ but cg11969330-containing Hap 2 was not responsive to demethylation, suggesting that transcriptional regulation at this site may predominated by other mechanisms, such as local chromatin organization or transcription factor binding.46

Collectively, these findings suggest that genetic and epigenetic regulation of CYP4V2 may contribute to asthma-related inflammatory processes. The association between rs2276921, promoter methylation, and CYP4V2 expression supports a possible regulatory role of this locus in asthma. Given the emerging importance of lipid metabolism in asthma heterogeneity, these findings provide a basis for future studies exploring the potential clinical relevance of CYP4V2 in asthma. However, this interpretation should be considered in light of several limitations. First, the analyses were performed using bulk PBMC data, which precludes cell-type-specific resolution and may not fully capture regulatory mechanisms occurring in disease-relevant airway epithelial cells. Although PBMCs provide a useful measure of systemic immune status and inflammatory responses, future studies incorporating airway epithelial samples together with single-cell transcriptomic or epigenomic approaches will be important to determine whether the regulatory patterns identified here are also present in airway-resident cell populations. Second, lipid mediators were not directly measured, preventing assessment of whether altered CYP4V2 expression is associated with changes in AA metabolism or downstream lipid mediator production. Future studies incorporating targeted lipidomic profiling and quantification of AA-derived metabolites, particularly in induced sputum or bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), will be required to clarify this potential functional link. Third, although the sample size was adequate for genetic association analyses, it was relatively modest for expression and methylation analyses. Therefore, validation in larger independent cohorts would strengthen the generalizability of these findings. Finally, the cross-sectional design limits assessment of temporal relationships between genetic, epigenetic, and transcriptional changes. Future longitudinal studies and experimental validation will be valuable for further characterizing the dynamics of CYP4V2 regulation in asthma.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that CYP4V2 is upregulated in PBMCs from asthma patients in SMCSGES cohort, with integrative analyses suggesting that genetic variation may influence its expression through promoter methylation. Our findings further suggest a potential link between CYP4V2 and arachidonic acid metabolic processes that may contribute to asthma-related airway inflammation. However, the downstream lipid mediators were not directly validated and the underlying mechanisms remain to be determined. In addition, the observed genetic regulatory pattern may be specific to the SMCSGES cohort, and further validation in independent and ethnically diverse cohorts will be necessary to assess the generalizability of these findings. As this study was based on bulk PBMC data and a cross-sectional design, future studies incorporating disease-relevant airway tissues, longitudinal sampling, and targeted lipidomic profiling will be important to further characterize the biological role of CYP4V2 in asthma. Together, these results suggest that CYP4V2 may serve as a potential biomarker for identifying arachidonic acid-related asthma endotypes, which may facilitate patient stratification and guide future investigations into lipid metabolism-related therapeutic approaches.
