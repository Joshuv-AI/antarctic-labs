The inhomogeneous Total Variation Flow with L 1 L^{1} -data
Marta Latorre and Sergio Segura de León

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of the Dirichlet problem for the parabolic equation driven by the 1 1 –Laplacian operator under minimal integrability assumptions. Specifically, we consider u ′ − div ​ ( D ​ u / | D ​ u | ) = f in ​ ( 0 , + ∞ ) × Ω , u^{\prime}-\hbox{\rm div\,}(Du/|Du|)=f\qquad\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,, where Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary, u 0 ∈ L 1 ​ ( Ω ) u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega) is the initial datum, and f ∈ L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( 0 , + ∞ , L 1 ​ ( Ω ) ) f\in L_{loc}^{1}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega)) is the source term. We establish the existence and uniqueness of entropy solutions in this low-regularity setting. Our approach relies on an approximation scheme and an entropy formulation adapted to the 1 1 –Laplacian structure. Additional results include comparison between solutions, further regularity when data have higher integrability and an analysis of the long-time decay of solutions in the homogeneous case.



1.
Introduction

In this work we prove the existence and uniqueness of an entropy solution to the following Dirichlet problem:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}(t,x)-\Delta_{1}u(t,x)=f(t,x)&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,,\\
u(t,x)=0&\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\partial\Omega\,,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{ in }\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geqslant 2
having a Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Here,
u^{\prime}
denotes the time derivative of
u
, while
\Delta_{1}u=\hbox{\rm div\,}(Du/|Du|)
is the so-called 1–Laplacian (the symbol
div
stands for the spacial divergence operator). We consider a source datum
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, for all
T>0
, and an initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Whenever dealing with
L^{1}
-data, the solution cannot be expected to lie in the energy space, only its truncations can be there. In such cases, classical weak formulations of solutions are no longer applicable. Thus, to analyze
L^{1}
-data, the concepts of entropy and renormalized solutions were introduced. For elliptic equations similar to those driven by the
p
–Laplacian (
p>1
), a general theory was developed by Bénilan et al.
[
9
]
, giving meaning to the gradient of the solution from the gradient of its truncations and introducing the entropy formulation in this context.
This entropy framework was later extended to the parabolic setting. Indeed, in
[
29
]
and
[
5
]
the existence and uniqueness of entropy solutions for parabolic
p
–Laplacian type equations with
L^{1}
-data were independently established. On the other hand, the renormalized formulation can be found in
[
16
]
for the elliptic case and in
[
10
]
for its parabolic adaptation (see also
[
25
,
26
]
for related results).

One of the main challenges in working with the
1
–Laplacian operator is giving a precise meaning to the quotient
\displaystyle\frac{Du}{|Du|}
. The suitable energy space to dealing with elliptic equations driven by the
1
–Laplacian is the space of functions of bounded variation; it contains those functions whose gradient is just a Radon measure, wherewith the above quotient involves Radon measures. To overcome this difficulty, in
[
17
,
2
]
(see also
[
4
]
), a bounded vector field
{\bf z}
is introduced to represent the quotient
\displaystyle\frac{Du}{|Du|}
, requiring that
\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and the identity
({\bf z},Du)=|Du|
holds, where
({\bf z},Du)
denotes the pairing supplied by Anzellotti’s theory (
({\bf z},Du)
is a Radon measure that generalizes the dot product, see
[
6
]
). On the other hand, the boundary condition cannot be satisfied in the sense of traces and so a weak sense must be introduced, which is also based on Anzellotti’s theory. Consequently, the condition
({\bf z},Du)=|Du|
must be replaced by
({\bf z},DT_{k}u)=|DT_{k}u|
for all
k>0
in the
L^{1}
-framework.

These requirements were transferred to the parabolic setting in
[
2
]
for the homogeneous case (see, for instance,
[
23
]
for the corresponding elliptic problem). Indeed, applying the Crandall-Liggett semigroup generation theorem, the existence of an entropy solution is proven in
[
2
]
. By using the technique of doubling of variables, uniqueness is obtained as well. Semigroup theory also allows handle the inhomogeneous case, when
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
(see
[
31
]
). A different approach was studied in
[
12
]
, where solutions are parabolic minimizers of a certain related functional. Existence is achieved by passing to the limit in a sequence of minimizers of approximating strictly convex functionals. Uniqueness also holds for every convex functional. It is worth noting that both approaches yield to the same solution, at least for a regular initial datum (see
[
20
]
).

Yet another way to address the non-homogeneous problem with initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
was considered in
[
30
]
and
[
22
]
using an approximation procedure. In
[
30
]
, existence and uniqueness with datum
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
(the same setting as
[
31
]
) is obtained, while the extension to
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
contained a flaw. This deficiency was corrected in the second paper through
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
. We point out that the procedure used, however, does not ensure the identity
\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),Du(t))=\int_{\Omega}|Du(t)|
for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. It is just shown that this identity holds
in mean
(see
[
22
, Lemma A.6 and Remark A.7]
). Furthermore, although the definition of solution employed is closely related to that in
[
2
]
, different formulations are compared in
[
22
, Remark 3.7]
. The last one is analogous to the weak solution of parabolic problems driven by the
p
–Laplacian (for a detailed proof that the solution found in
[
22
]
satisfies this last formulation, we refer to Proposition
3.5
below). We stress that, using it, it is very easy to prove uniqueness of solutions.

In this paper, the problem we address with
L^{1}
-data is

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}(t,x)-\Delta_{1}u(t,x)=f(t,x)&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\,,\\
u(t,x)=0&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\,,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{ in }\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

for every
T>0
.
Our aim is to extend the notions and results of
[
22
]
to this context. This extension is technically demanding because standard energy estimates fail in the
L^{1}
-setting (see, for instance,
[
24
]
for a general discussion on parabolic equations driven by the
p
–Laplacian,
p>1
, and measure data). More precisely, we introduce a formulation of entropy solution which is similar to that considered in
[
29
]
or
[
5
]
to handle
p
–Laplacian type equations, and conceptually aligned with the entropy conditions for flux-limited diffusion equations
[
14
]
. Consequently, the core of our approach relies on constructing a suitable family of test functions inherently coupled with their associated vector fields, which are provided by Proposition
3.7
below, having in mind Definition
3.1
.

Equipped with this setting up, uniqueness can be easily proven without having to doubling variables. Regarding existence, we first extend the key result
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
to our framework (see Proposition 4.1 below) and get an entropy solution by constructing a suitable sequence of approximating problems. It is worth noting that the solution we found in our
L^{1}
-environment almost satisfies the identity
\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}u(t))=\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(t)|
, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. However, we can only see it in a
mean sense
(see Remark
5.5
) as it occurs in the regular case.

Besides existence and uniqueness, we establish a comparison principle and show that if the data
f
and
u_{0}
possess higher integrability –specifically, if
f(t)
(for almost all
t>0
) and
u_{0}
belong to
L^{r}(\Omega)
with
1<r<2
–, then the entropy solution inherits this regularity, i.e. for almost every
t>0
the function
u(t)
belongs to
L^{r}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we analyze the long-time behavior of solutions in the homogeneous case by showing the decay of the solution for large
t
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the notation and preliminary results required to make the work self-contained. Section 3 gathers results for problems with regular data, which serve as the foundation for the general case. In Section 4 we present a key auxiliary result, while Sections 5 and 6 are devoted to proving the existence and uniqueness of entropy solutions, respectively. In Section 7 we establish a comparison result and analyze how the distance between initial data influences the distance between the corresponding solutions. Section 8 is concerned with improved regularity: we show that if the data possess higher integrability, the entropy solution reflects this fact. Finally, in Section 9, we analyze the long-time behavior of solutions to the homogeneous equation and establish a decay result when
t
tends to
\infty
.

2.
Preliminaries

This section collects the notation and auxiliary results used throughout the paper.

Notation

Let
T>0
be fixed and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(with
N\geqslant 2
) be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. We denote by
\nu
the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
, defined
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-almost
everywhere, where
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
stands for the
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. On the other hand, the Lebesgue measure of a measurable subset
E\subset\Omega
is denoted by
|E|
.

We adopt standard notation for function spaces, denoting by
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
the Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, respectively.

Given a Banach space
X
, the space
L^{p}(0,T;X)
consists of strongly measurable,
p
-summable
X
–valued functions
v\colon[0,T]\to X
. The space
L_{w}^{1}(0,T;X)
consists of weakly measurable functions, i.e., those
v\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;X)
such that the map
t\mapsto\langle v(t),\phi\rangle
is measurable for every
\phi\in X^{*}
. We recall that
L^{1}(0,T;X)\subset L_{w}^{1}(0,T;X)
.

When
X
is a function space, we identify
v(t)\in X
with
v(t)(x)=v(t,x)
. For further details on Banach space–valued functions, we refer to
[
19
, Appendix E]
and
[
18
]
.

Vector-valued Sobolev spaces are denoted by
W^{1,p}(0,T;X)
, or by
H^{1}(0,T;X)
when
p=2
. Their definition and main properties can be found in
[
13
, Appendix]
. We emphasize that these spaces satisfy the continuous embedding
W^{1,p}(0,T;X)\subset C([0,T];X)
.

A weakly measurable function
f\colon[0,T]\to X
is called Dunford integrable if
\langle x^{*},f(t)\rangle\in L^{1}(0,T)
for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. If, in addition, for every measurable set
E\subset(0,T)
, the Dunford integral
\int_{E}\langle x^{*},f(t)\rangle\,dt
defines an element of
X
, then
f
is said to be Pettis integrable.

As indicated previously, the symbol
u^{\prime}
represents the time derivative of function
u
in the sense of distributions. The precise definition of
u^{\prime}
used in this work is given in Definition
2.2
.

Finally, we consider the truncation operator
T_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

T_{k}(s)=\min\{|s|,k\}\,{\rm\,sign}(s),\quad\text{for all }s\in\mathbb{R}\,,

as well as function
G_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

(3)

G_{k}(s)=s-T_{k}(s)\,,\quad\text{for all }s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

A related auxiliary function is the primitive of
T_{k}
, given by

(4)

J_{k}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}T_{k}(\sigma)\,d\sigma\,,\quad\text{for all }s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Functions of Bounded Variation

The space of functions of bounded variation, denoted by
BV(\Omega)
, constitutes the natural framework for elliptic equations involving the 1–Laplacian. A function
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
belongs to
BV(\Omega)
if its distributional gradient
Dv
is a Radon measure with finite total variation. We denote the total variation of
Dv
over
\Omega
by
\int_{\Omega}|Dv|
. Accordingly, the integral of a function
\varphi
with respect to the measure
|Dv|
is denoted by
\int_{\Omega}\varphi|Dv|
.

Recall that every
v\in BV(\Omega)
admits a trace on the boundary satisfying
v\big|_{\partial\Omega}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
.

The space
BV(\Omega)
becomes a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|v\|=\int_{\Omega}|Dv|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|v|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\,.

We remark that this norm defines a functional in
BV(\Omega)
that is lower semi-continuous with respect to the
L^{1}(\Omega)
-convergence.

It is worth noting that a chain rule holds for functions of bounded variation. If
u\in BV(\Omega)
and
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with a Lipschitz constant
L
, then
\varphi(u)\in BV(\Omega)
and the inequality
|D\varphi(u)|\leqslant L|Du|
holds in the sense of measures (see
[
1
, Theorem 3.99]
and the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.96]
).

For a comprehensive study of functions of bounded variation, we refer the reader to the monographs
[
1
,
7
]
.

We frequently use the space
BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, equipped with the norm

\|v\|=\max\{\|v\|_{BV},\|v\|_{\infty}\}\,.

This is a Banach space, and we denote its duality pairing with its dual
(BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
and the space itself by

\langle\zeta,v\rangle_{\Omega},\quad\zeta\in(BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*},\;v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

Finally, given
\xi=\zeta+f
with
\zeta\in(BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we define the pairing

\langle\!\langle\xi,v\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}:=\langle\zeta,v\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx\qquad\text{ for all }\;v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

Anzellotti’s Theory

To rigorously define the quotient
Du/|Du|
and address the difficulties arising where the gradient
Du
vanishes in a set of nonzero measure,
[
2
]
introduced a vector field
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
to represent this term. In order to handle such vector fields, it is required a suitable version of Green’s formula. We rely on the theory of
L^{\infty}
–divergence–measure vector fields, originally developed by Anzellotti (see
[
6
]
) and extended by Chen and Frid (
[
15
]
).

Given
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define the pairing
({\bf z},Dv)
by

\langle\,({\bf z},Dv)\,,\varphi\,\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}v\,\varphi\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}v\,{\bf z}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\,,\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

This distribution
({\bf z},Dv)
defines a Radon measure satisfying the inequality
|({\bf z},Dv)|\leqslant\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}|Dv|
in the sense of measures. We point out that this pairing remains well-defined under weaker regularity assumptions, for instance, when
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Moreover, the following Green’s formula holds

\int_{\Omega}v\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}({\bf z},Dv)=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\,[{\bf z},\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\,,

where
[{\bf z},\nu]\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
stands for the weak trace on the boundary
\partial\Omega
of the normal component of the vector field, also introduced by Anzellotti in
[
6
]
.

A crucial step in our existence proof involves extending these results to cases where the integrability of
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is a priori unknown.

A final remark regarding truncations is in order. Assume that
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
{\bf z}
is a
L^{\infty}
–divergence–measure vector field such that
\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
. If the identity
({\bf z},Dv)=|Dv|
holds in the sense of measures, it implies
({\bf z},DT_{k}(v))=|DT_{k}(v)|
for every
k>0
. To see this claim, it is enough to fix
k>0
, split both measures:
|Dv|=|DT_{k}(v)|+|DG_{k}(v)|
and
({\bf z},Dv)=({\bf z},DT_{k}(v))+({\bf z},DG_{k}(v))
, and write

0=|Dv|-({\bf z},Dv)=\big[|DT_{k}(v)|-({\bf z},DT_{k}(v))\big]+\big[|DG_{k}(v)|-({\bf z},DG_{k}(v))\big]\geqslant 0

owing to the non-negativity of both bracketed terms. It is now straightforward that

({\bf z},DT_{k}(v))=|DT_{k}(v)|
and
({\bf z},DG_{k}(v))=|DG_{k}(v)|
holds.

Time Derivative

We now introduce the notion of time derivative employed throughout this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Psi\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.

We say that
\Psi
admits a
weak derivative
in
L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
if there exists

\Theta\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
such
that
\Psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Theta(s)ds
, where the integral is
taken as a Pettis integral.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a function
\xi\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}+L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
is the
time derivative
of
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
if

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\!\langle\,\xi(t),\Psi(t)\,\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u(t)\,\Phi(t)\,dx\,dt

for all
\Psi\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
with compact support in
(0,T)
and whose weak time derivative is
\Phi\in L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

3.
Regular data framework

A key contribution of this work lies in the use of test functions equipped with an associated vector field. This approach allows the use of approximate solutions as test functions. To this end, for a fixed
T>0
, we first consider problem (
2
) with regular data
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
. In this setting, the suitable concept of solution is defined as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
.
A function
u\in L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
(which implies
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
) is called a
weak solution
of (
2
) if
u(0)=u_{0}
and there exists a vector field
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
satisfying

(i)

u^{\prime}(t)=\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)+f(t)
in
\Omega
in the sense of distributions,

(ii)

({\bf z}(t),Du(t))=|Du(t)|
as measures in
\Omega
,

(iii)

[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-u(t))

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a. e. on
\partial\Omega
,

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
.

Furthermore, assuming
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
for all
T>0
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
, we say that
u\in L^{1}_{w}(0,+\infty;BV(\Omega))\cap C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
is a global weak solution of (
1
) if it is a weak solution to (
2
) for every
T>0
.

Remark 3.2
.

Condition (i) in Definition
3.1
implies that any
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
can be used as a test function. As a consequence, the solution itself or a truncation of it are admissible test functions.

Remark 3.3
.

Since
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, Anzellotti’s theory applies. Then, conditions (i)–(iii) of the above definition can be combined to obtain

(5)

\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\big(u(t)-\phi(t)\big)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|Du(t)|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|u(t)|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
=\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),D\phi(t))-\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi(t)[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\int_{\Omega}f(t)\big(u(t)-\phi(t)\big)\,dx

for all
\phi\in L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and almost all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and every
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
, problem (
2
) admits a unique solution in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Proposition
3.4
can be proven as in
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
where the solution is found as a limit of solutions to parabolic problems involving the
p
–Laplacian. Alternatively, the result can be derived from the arguments in
[
12
, Theorem 1.6]
, having in mind
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
. Observe that uniqueness easily follows from applying identity (
5
) to two distinct solutions, taking on account
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
.

The main advantage of Proposition
3.4
is that it provides a large amount of functions, each equipped with an associated vector field, suitable for use as test functions when more general data are considered.

Moreover, since the existence and uniqueness of solutions for
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
were established in
[
22
, Theorems 4.1 and 5.1]
, the following result shows that these solutions satisfy a formulation in which functions with an associated vector field can, indeed, be employed as test functions. This fact was announced in
[
22
, Remark 3.7]
. The main goal of this paper is to extend that result to the
L^{1}
-setting.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
.
Then, the unique solution
u\in L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
to problem (
2
) satisfies, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

(6)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u(t)-\phi(t))^{2}dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
+\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),D(u(t)-\phi(t))\big)-\int_{\partial\Omega}(u(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f(t)(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx

for every
\phi\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
which has an associated vector field
{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(a)

\|{\bf z}_{\phi}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
,

(b)

\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,

(c)

({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),D\phi(t))=|D\phi(t)|
for almost all
t
,

(d)

[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-\phi(t))
for almost all
t
.

Proof
.
Since
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.
Now, consider the approximating problems

(7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\displaystyle u_{n}^{\prime}-\Delta_{1}u_{n}&=&f_{n}(t,x)&\hbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\,,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{n}&=&0&\hbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\,,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{n}(0,x)&=&u_{0}(x)&\hbox{in }\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

which have a solution
u_{n}\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
thanks to Proposition
3.4
. Moreover, it provides a vector field
{\bf z}_{n}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\|{\bf z}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and

(1)

u_{n}^{\prime}(t)=\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}(t)+f_{n}(t)
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
,

(2)

({\bf z}_{n}(t),Du_{n}(t))=|Du_{n}(t)|
as measures in
\Omega
,

(3)

[{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-u_{n}(t))

\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
–a. e. on
\partial\Omega
,

holds for almost every
t\in(0,T)
.

We first claim that inequality (
6
) holds for each
u_{n}
. To this end, choose
\phi\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
which has an associated vector field
{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying conditions (a)–(d) of the statement. We apply
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
to get

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))^{2}dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))^{\prime}\,dx

for almost every
t\in(0,T)
. Thus,

(8)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))^{2}dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{\prime}(t)(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}(t)(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(t)(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
=-\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{n}(t),D(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t)))+\int_{\partial\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(t)(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx

due to Green’s formula.
Hence, it remains to verify that

-\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{n}(t),D(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t)))+\int_{\partial\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
\leqslant-\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),D(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t)))+\int_{\partial\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

but it is straightforward by conditions (c) and (d) of the statement. Therefore, our claim is proven.

Now, consider inequality (
6
) for
u_{n}
, apply Green’s formula, fix
0<s<t<T
and integrate from
s
to
t
(recall that
H^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\subset C([0,T];BV(\Omega))
):

(9)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}(s)-\phi(s))^{2}\,dx+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\sigma)(u_{n}(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\\
-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(\sigma)(u_{n}(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,d\sigma\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\sigma)(u_{n}(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

Since it is proven in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
converges to
u
(the solution to problem (
2
)) in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, it follows that we may let
n
go to infinity in (
9
) and it yields

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u(t)-\phi(t))^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u(s)-\phi(s))^{2}\,dx+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\sigma)(u(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\\
-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(\sigma)(u(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,d\sigma\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f(\sigma)(u(\sigma)-\phi(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

Differentiating this inequality and applying Green’s formula again, we are done.

Our next concern is to get, without any intention of generality, bounded solutions to (
2
) that can be chosen as test functions. To begin with, we consider a Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality in the setting of
BV(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
.
Then
u\in L^{\frac{N+1}{N}}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and the following inequality holds:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N+1}{N}}dx\,dt\\
\leqslant C\left[\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right)\,dt

for a certain constant
C>0
depending only on
N
and
\Omega
, where
G_{k}(s)
is defined in (
3
).

Proof
.
Applying the interpolation inequality followed by Sobolev’s inequality, we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N+1}{N}}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+1}}\leqslant\left(\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N+1}}\\
\leqslant C\left(\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{N+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right)^{\frac{N}{N+1}}

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

The result is obtained by raising both sides of the inequality to the power
\frac{N+1}{N}
and integrating over
(0,T)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

If
f\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then the unique solution to problem (
2
) is bounded.

Proof
.
Let
u
be the unique solution to problem (
2
) with associated vector field
{\bf z}
. Now fix
k>\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
. Taking
G_{k}(u)
as test function (recall Remark
3.2
), for almost every
0<t<T
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u(t))u^{\prime}(t)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\Omega}f(t)G_{k}(u(t))\,dx\\
\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx.

Taking into account
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, we have
G_{k}(u(t))u^{\prime}(t)=G_{k}(u(t))G_{k}(u(t))^{\prime}
and so we may apply
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
(alternatively, we may appeal to Proposition
4.1
below). Hence,

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u(t))^{2}\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx.

Given
0<\tau<T
, we integrate over
[0,\tau]
to deduce

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u(\tau))^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}dt\\
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u_{0})^{2}\,dx+\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\,dt=\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\,dt

due to the choice
k>\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
.
It implies

(10)

\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\tau\in(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u(\tau))^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|D(G_{k}(u(t)))|\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}dt\\
\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\,dt\,.

The next step is to apply first Lemma
3.6
, then our estimate (
10
) and finally Hölder’s inequality:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N+1}{N}}\,dx\,dt\\
\leqslant C\left[\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}G_{k}(u(t))^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2N}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|DG_{k}(u(t))|\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}dt\right]\\
\leqslant C\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|\,dx\,dt\right]^{\frac{2N+1}{2N}}\\
\leqslant C\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N+1}{N}}\,dt\right]^{\frac{2N+1}{2(N+1)}}|\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\>:\>|u(t,x)|>k\}|^{\frac{2N+1}{2N(N+1)}}

where
C
stands for different constants that only depends on
N
and
\Omega
.
Simplifying, it yields

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}(u(t))|^{\frac{N+1}{N}}\,dt\leqslant C|\{|u|>k\}|^{\frac{2N+1}{N}}

for some constant only depending on the parameters of our problem. Since
\frac{2N+1}{N}>1
, a standard procedure (see
[
21
, Theorem 6.1, Chapter II]
) allows us to conclude that
u
is bounded.

Remark 3.8
.

The above result can also be derived from formulation (
6
). Obviously,
T_{k}(u)
cannot be taken as a test function in (
6
) and an approximation with smooth functions is necessary.
Namely, for
0<\epsilon<k-\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
, we denote by
T_{k}^{\epsilon}(s)
a regularization of
T_{k}(s)
such that
T_{k}^{\epsilon}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and

\big(T_{k}^{\epsilon}\big)^{\prime}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }|s|\leqslant k-\epsilon\,,\\[5.69054pt]
0&\text{ if }|s|\geqslant k\,,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ with }\quad 0\leqslant(T_{k}^{\epsilon})^{\prime}(s)\leqslant 1\,.

Nevertheless, the subsequent process of letting
\epsilon
go to 0 is cumbersome.

4.
An auxiliary result

One of the biggest difficulties in handling the Total Variation Flow lies in the parabolic term. In the context of regular data, the evolution problem was analyzed in
[
22
]
relying on the identity

(11)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}u(t)^{2}dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}u(t)u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,.

This identity allows the use of the solution itself as a test function. However, such a property cannot be expected to hold when dealing with merely summable data. In fact, just truncations of solutions should be taken as test functions with
L^{1}
-data. Consequently, it is necessary to extend (
11
) to the form

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,,

where
J_{k}(s)
is defined in (
4
). Actually, in this section we establish this identity for a general class of non-decreasing functions that includes truncations.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
(so that
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
). Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-decreasing, bounded, and Lipschitz-continuous function such that
\varphi(0)=0
. Define

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s)\,ds\,.

Then the following identity holds:

(12)

\left(\int_{\Omega}\psi(u(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(u(t))\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,.

Moreover, for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is satisfied

(13)

\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\varphi(u(t))\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,.

Proof
.
Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
and take
\delta>0
small enough so that the following calculations can be developed.

-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)}{-\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t-\delta)}{\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)}{\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)}{\delta}\,\psi(u(t+\delta))\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)}{\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\psi(u(t+\delta))-\psi(u(t))}{\delta}\,\eta(t)\,dx\,dt\,.

We define the auxiliary function

\alpha(t,x)=\dfrac{\psi(u(t+\delta,x))-\psi(u(t,x))}{u(t+\delta,x)-u(t,x)}\quad\text{ if }u(t+\delta,x)\not=u(t,x)\,,

and
\alpha(t,x)=\varphi(u(t,x))
if
u(t+\delta,x)=u(t,x)
.
Thus, the previous equality can be written as

-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)}{-\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(t+\delta)-u(t)}{\delta}\,\alpha(t)\,\eta(t)\,dx\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(t+\delta)-u(t)}{\delta}\,\eta(t)\,\varphi(u(t))\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(t+\delta)-u(t)}{\delta}\,\eta(t)\,\big(\alpha(t)-\varphi(u(t))\big)\,dx\,dt\\
=A_{1}+A_{2}\,.

Consider now the function
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(t)=\dfrac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\,\varphi(u(s))\,ds\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, where the integral is taken in the sense of Dunford. Notice that in
[
2
, Lemmas 3–4]
, it is proven that it is actually a Pettis integral. We also remark that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Lambda_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt
is well defined since
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
.

Therefore,

\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Lambda_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,u(t)\,dx\,dt\\
=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\varphi(u(t))-\eta(t-\delta)\varphi(u(t-\delta))}{\delta}\,u(t)\,dx\,dt\\
=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\varphi(u(t))}{\delta}\,u(t)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\varphi(u(t))}{\delta}\,u(t+\delta)\,dx\,dt=A_{1}\,.

In a similar way, we define
\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\big(\alpha(s)-\varphi(u(s)\big)\,ds
which is also a Pettis integral. Let us now verify that
\Upsilon_{\delta}
is bounded on
(0,T)
. Firstly, due to the Mean Value Theorem, there exists
\beta
in the interval with endpoints
u(t+\delta)
and
u(t)
such that

\psi(u(t+\delta))-\psi(u(t))=\varphi(\beta)\big(u(t+\delta)-u(t)\big)\,.

We highlight that
\beta
depends on
x,t,\delta
and it may not be unique; nevertheless, it allows us to write
\alpha(t)
as
\varphi(\beta)
. Then it yields

|\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)|\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}|\eta(s)|\big|\varphi(\beta)-\varphi(u(s))\big|\,ds\leqslant 2\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\Upsilon_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\,u(t)\,dx\,dt\\
=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\big(\alpha(t)-\varphi(u(t))\big)-\eta(t-\delta)\big(\alpha(t-\delta)-\varphi(u(t-\delta))\big)}{\delta}\,u(t)\,dx\,dt\\
=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\big(\alpha(t)-\varphi(u(t))\big)}{\delta}\,u(t)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t)\big(\alpha(t)-\varphi(u(t))\big)}{\delta}\,u(t+\delta)\,dx\,dt=A_{2}\,.

Thus,

(14)

-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)}{-\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Lambda_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt\,.

Taking the limit as
\delta\to 0
on the left hand side, we get

\lim_{\delta\to 0}-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)}{-\delta}\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt

since the Mean Value Theorem leads to
\left|\frac{\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)}{-\delta}\right|=\left|\eta^{\prime}(\zeta)\right|\leqslant\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

On the other hand, the first term on the right hand side of (
14
) satisfies

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Lambda_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\eta(t)\,\varphi(u(t))\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,dt\,;

observe that Lebesgue’s Theorem can be applied due to
|\Lambda_{\delta}(t)|\leqslant\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Finally, we show that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt=0
. Indeed, recall that we may write
\alpha(s)=\varphi(\beta)
for some
\beta
between
u(s+\delta)
and
u(s)
. Hence, the monotonicity of
\varphi
implies

|\alpha(s)-\varphi(u(s))|=|\varphi(\beta)-\varphi(u(s))|\leqslant|\varphi(u(s+\delta))-\varphi(u(s))|\leqslant L|u(s+\delta)-u(s)|

where
L
stands for the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
. Then, having in mind
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega))
, it gives

\left|\int_{\Omega}\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\right|\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}|\eta(s)|\,L\,|u(s+\delta)-u(s)|\,|u^{\prime}(t)|\,ds\,dx\\
\leqslant L\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(u(t+\delta)-u(t)\big)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in L^{1}(0,T)\,.

As a consequence,

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Upsilon_{\delta}(t)\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,dt\right|\leqslant C\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(u(t+\delta)-u(t)\big)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\longrightarrow 0\,.

Therefore, letting
\delta
to
0
in (
14
) we get

-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\psi(u(t))\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}\eta(t)\,\varphi(u(t))\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx\,dt\,

for any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
and then (
12
) is obtained.

Identity (
13
) can be proven reasoning in a similar way.

Remark 4.2
.

We point out that the above proof also holds when
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}((\epsilon,T)\times\Omega)
for all
\epsilon>0
. This regularity occurs for solutions to problem (
2
) with data
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, as established in
[
31
]
(see also
[
30
]
and
[
22
]
).

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
, and let
u
denote the unique solution to problem (
2
).
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-decreasing, bounded, and Lipschitz-continuous function with
\varphi(0)=0
, and define

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s)\,ds\,.

Then the function
\displaystyle t\longmapsto\int_{\Omega}\psi(u(t))\,dx

is absolutely continuous on
(0,T)
.

Moreover, the function
\displaystyle t\longmapsto\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\psi(u(t))\,dx
is also absolutely continuous on
(0,T)
for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.
It is a consequence of

\left(\int_{\Omega}\psi(u(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(u(t))\,u^{\prime}(t)\,dx

since
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
\varphi
is bounded.

5.
Entropy solution

In this section, we introduce the notion of entropy solution we employ and prove a fundamental result that guarantees the existence of such a solution. Standard weak solution cannot be expected due to the non regularity of the data.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. We say that
u
is an
entropy solution
to problem (
2
) if

(i)

u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
and
T_{k}(u)\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
for all
k>0
;

(ii)

there exists
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
such that the distributional divergence
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
and, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
, the pairing
({\bf z}(t),Dv)
is a Radon measure for all
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
;

(iii)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
, the weak trace
[{\bf z}(t),\nu]
is well defined and it satisfies

(1)

\|[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
,

(2)

[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-u(t))

{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
,

(3)

the following Green’s formula holds:

\langle\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t),v\,\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),Dv)=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\,[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\quad\forall\,v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,;

(iv)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)

such that there exists
{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

(15)

\|{\bf z}_{\phi}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1\,,\qquad\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)\,

and, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

(16)

({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),D\phi(t))=|D\phi(t)|\qquad\text{ and }\qquad[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-\phi(t))

the following inequality holds:

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),DT_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t)))-\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f(t)T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

Given
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
for all
T>0
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we say that
u\in C([0,+\infty);L^{1}(\Omega))
is a global entropy solution to (
1
) if it is a solution to (
2
) for every
T>0
.

Remark 5.2
.

The symbol
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)
denotes the distributional divergence of the vector field
{\bf z}(t)
, which can be uniquely extended to an element of the dual space of
W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Consequently, we may regard
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{1}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
. By requiring that
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
, we select a specific extension, which is not necessarily unique. The extension of interest for our purposes is obtained as the limit (in some sense that will be determined) of the divergences of the vector fields associated to the approximating problems.

As expected, we verify that every solution in the sense of Definition
3.1
(corresponding to regular data) constitutes an entropy solution.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, the unique solution to problem (
2
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
is an entropy solution.

Proof
.
Only condition (iv) of Definition
5.1
has to be proven. Let
\phi
satisfy the conditions set forth in (iv).

Fixed
0<t<T
, we take the test function
T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))
in (
2
):

(17)

\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}f(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\,.

The first integral can be written as (adding
\pm\phi^{\prime}(t)
):

\int_{\Omega}u^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}(u(t)-\phi(t))^{\prime}\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
=\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\,,

where we have used Proposition
4.1
.

On the other hand, the second integral of (
17
) becomes

\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)\,dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\Big\{\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)-\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\Big\}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\,dx\\
\leqslant\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\,dx\,,

as a consequence of Green’s formula since
\big({\bf z}(t)-{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),DT_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\big)\geqslant 0
and
T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))[{\bf z}(t)-{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\leqslant 0
.
Therefore,

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\,,

which shows that
u
is also an entropy solution.

With the definition established, we proceed to prove the existence of such solutions.

Theorem 5.4
.

There exists at least one entropy solution to problem (
2
) for every source
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, this solution satisfies

(18)

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx

for all
k>0
and almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof
.
In order to prove the existence of entropy solutions, we follow the argument in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
with slight modifications which we detail below.

We begin with the approximating problems

(19)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{n}^{\prime}-\Delta_{1}u_{n}=f_{n}&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\,,\\
u_{n}=0&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\,,\\
u_{n}(0,x)=u^{0}_{n}(x)&\text{ in }\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

where we choose sequences
f_{n}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{n}^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{n}^{0}\to u_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

By Proposition
3.4
, there exist a solution
u_{n}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
with
u_{n}^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and a vector field
{\bf z}_{n}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|{\bf z}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and Proposition
3.7
guarantees that
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. In this case, Anzellotti’s theory applies (so that a Green’s formula holds) as well as, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

(20)

\displaystyle u_{n}^{\prime}(t)=\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}(t)+f_{n}(t)\text{ in the sense of distributions},

(21)

\displaystyle({\bf z}_{n}(t),Du_{n}(t))=|Du_{n}(t)|\text{ as measures},

(22)

\displaystyle[{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-u_{n}(t))\;{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\text{-a.e. on }\partial\Omega\,.

The next step is to find a function
u
as a limit of the sequence of approximate solutions
u_{n}
.

Taking the test function
T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))
in (
20
) with
f_{n},u_{n}^{0}
and also with data
f_{m},u_{m}^{0}
we get, by Proposition
4.1
,

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))^{\prime}\,dx\\
=-\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{n}(t)-{\bf z}_{m}(t),DT_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t)))+\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))\Big([{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]-[{\bf z}_{m}(t),\nu]\Big)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
+\int_{\Omega}(f_{n}(t)-f_{m}(t))\,T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))\,dx\,,

being
J_{k}(s)
the function defined in (
4
).

Once we have dropped non-positive terms on the right hand side and have integrated in
(0,t)
, the previous equality becomes

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t))\,dx\\
\leqslant\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}^{0}-u_{m}^{0})\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s))\,T_{k}(u_{n}(s)-u_{m}(s))\,dx\,ds\\
\leqslant k\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{0}-u_{m}^{0}|\,dx+k\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(t)-f_{m}(t)|\,dx\,dt\,,

since
J_{k}(s)\leqslant k|s|
and
|T_{k}(s)|\leqslant k
. Finally, dividing by
k
and passing to the limit as
k
goes to
0
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(t)-u_{m}(t)|\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{0}-u_{m}^{0}|\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(t)-f_{m}(t)|\,dx\,dt\,,

and so
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
. This fact implies that there exists
u\in C(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
such that

(23)

\displaystyle u_{n}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))\,,

(24)

\displaystyle u_{n}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{ in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)\,,

(25)

\displaystyle u_{n}(t)\longrightarrow u(t)\quad\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,\quad t\in(0,T)\,,

(26)

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,x)\longrightarrow u(t,x)\quad\text{ a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega\,.

We now verify that the function
u
satisfies all the conditions of Definition
5.1
.

Condition (i).-

We take the test function
T_{k}(u_{n}(t))
in (
20
) and, since Green’s formula holds, we deduce

\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t))\,u_{n}^{\prime}(t)\,dx\\
=-\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}_{n}(t),DT_{k}(u_{n}(t)))+\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t))[{\bf z}_{n}(t),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}+\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(t)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(t))\,dx\,.

Moreover, due to properties (
21
) and (
22
) and Proposition
4.1
, integrating over
(0,t)
(with
0<t<T
) it becomes

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t))\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u_{n}(s))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u_{n}(s))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big]\,ds\\
=\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}^{0})\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(s)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(s))\,dx\,ds\\
\leqslant k\left\{\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{0}|\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(s)|\,dx\,ds\right\}\leqslant k\,C\,,

which implies that

\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u_{n}(s))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u_{n}(s))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big]\,ds\leqslant k\,C\,.

That is, every
\{T_{k}(u_{n})\}_{n}
is bounded in
L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
.
It follows from the lower semicontinuity of the
BV
-norm with respect to the
L^{1}
-convergence and Fatou’s Lemma that

\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(s))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u(s))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big]\,ds\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u_{n}(s))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u_{n}(s))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big]\,ds\\
\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u_{n}(s))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u_{n}(s))|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\Big]\,ds\,.

As a consequence,
T_{k}(u(t))\in BV(\Omega)
for almost every
t\in(0,T)
and, by
[
4
, Lemma 5.19]
, the function
t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|
is measurable and
T_{k}(u)\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
.

Condition (ii).-

With regard to the vector field
{\bf z}
, its existence follows directly from the uniform bound
\|{\bf z}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
. Indeed, passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists
{\bf z}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\|{\bf z}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and
{\bf z}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits^{\ast}}{\bf z}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

To show that the sequence
\{\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}\}
is bounded in the space
L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
we follow the argument in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Step 6]
but with
v\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))
. Hence, we find a subnet such that
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits^{\ast}}\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}
in
L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
(here
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}
denotes a suitable extension of the distributional gradient of
{\bf z}
).

Moreover, we also define

\xi=\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}+f\;\in\;L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}+L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)\,,

and working as in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Step 7]
but with test functions
v\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, we prove that

u_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightharpoonup}\limits^{\ast}}\xi\;\in\;L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}+L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)\,.

On the other hand,
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Step 8]
implies that, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
, the equation holds in the distributional sense:

\left(\int_{\Omega}\omega\,u(t)\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\langle\!\langle\xi,\omega\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}{\bf z}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx+\int_{\Omega}f(t)\,\omega\,dx\quad\forall\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\,,

while
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Step 9]
shows that
({\bf z}(t),Dv)
is a Radon measure for all
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This completes the verification of condition (ii) in Definition
5.1
.

Condition (iii).-

Arguing as in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Steps 10-12]
we can define
[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
with
\|[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and such that a Green’s formula holds:

\langle\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t),v\,\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}{\bf z}(t)\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}v[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}

for all
v\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, we may show that
\xi
is the time derivative of
u
in the sense of Definition
2.2
following the argument of
[
22
, Theorem 4.1, Step 13]
.

To conclude the verification of condition (iii) in Definition
5.1
, we must prove that the boundary condition (iii)
(2)
holds. To this end, set
k>0
and let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
be non-negative. For every
\delta>0
small enough, define

\Psi_{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)T_{k}(u(s))\,ds\,,

that is a Pettis integral as a consequence of
[
2
, Lemmas 3-4]
.
On the other hand, observe that the integrals

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t),\Psi_{\delta}(t)\,\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f(t)\Psi_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt

are well-defined since
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t)\in L^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}
,
\Psi_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))
and
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. Hence the integral

(27)

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\!\langle\,\xi(t),\Psi_{\delta}(t)\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt

is also well-defined. To compute (
27
), apply that
\xi
is the time derivative of
u
to deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\!\langle\xi(t),\Psi_{\delta}(t)\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big(\eta(t)T_{k}(u(t))-\eta(t-\delta)T_{k}(u(t-\delta))\big)u(t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))\,u(t)\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t-\delta)\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t-\delta))\,u(t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))\,\big(u(t+\delta)-u(t)\big)\,dx\,dt\,.

The convexity of the real function
J_{k}
implies

T_{k}(u(t))\big(u(t+\delta)-u(t)\big)\leqslant J_{k}(u(t+\delta))-J_{k}(u(t))\,.

Hence

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\!\langle\xi(t),\Psi_{\delta}(t)\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\eta(t)\Big(J_{k}(u(t+\delta))-J_{k}(u(t))\Big)\,dx\,dt\\
=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\eta(t-\delta)J_{k}(u(t))-\eta(t)J_{k}(u(t))\Big)\,dx\,dt=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)\big)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt\,.

Next, we will compute (
27
) by means of Green’s formula

(28)

-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\eta(t-\delta)-\eta(t)\big)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt\leqslant-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\!\langle\,\xi(t),\Psi_{\delta}(t)\,\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}\,dt\\
=-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\Big[\langle\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}(t),T_{k}(u(s))\rangle_{\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}f(t)T_{k}(u(s))\,dx\Big]\,ds\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\Big[\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}(u(s)))-\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u(s))[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(s))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,ds\,dt\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(s))|-\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u(s))[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(s))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,ds\,dt\,.

Having in mind
[
22
, Lemma A3 and Corollary A4]
, we let
\delta
go to 0 to obtain

(29)

\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|-\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,dt\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,dt\,.

On the other hand, taking
\eta(t)T_{k}(u_{n}(t))
as test function in (
20
), it yields

-\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t))\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u_{n}(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u_{n}(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\Big]\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t))f_{n}(t)\,dx\,dt\,.

To pass to the limit in the first term we need that
J_{k}(u_{n})\to J_{k}(u)
strongly in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
. This fact is a consequence of the inequality
|J_{k}(u_{n})-J_{k}(u)|\leqslant k|u_{n}-u|
(which is easily derived from the Mean Value Theorem) on account of
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
. The second term may be handled by the lower semicontinuity of the BV-norm, while the right hand side is straightforward. Letting
n
go to infinity, we get

-\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\left[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right]dt\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx\,dt\,.

Finally, having in mind (
29
), it implies

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|-\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}(u(t))|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t))f(t)\,dx\Big]\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt\,.

It follows from (
30
) that

-\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}u(t)[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}u(t)|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\,dt\,.

Since this identity holds for every
k>0
and every non-negative
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we get

\int_{\partial\Omega}\big(|u(t)|+u(t)[{\bf z}(t),\nu]\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=0

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
, which implies the boundary condition (iii) (2).

Going back to (
30
), we also get

\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\Big[\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(t)|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|T_{k}u(t)|\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}u(t)f(t)\,dx\Big]\,dt\,.

The arbitrariness of
\eta
leads to identity (
18
).

Condition (iv).-

Let
\phi\in L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
be such that there exists
{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|{\bf z}_{\phi}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. In addition, it holds
({\bf z}_{\phi}(t),D\phi(t))=|D\phi(t)|
and
[{\bf z}_{\phi}(t),\nu]\in{\rm\,sign}(-\phi(t))
a.e. in
(0,T)
. Fixed
0<t<T
, thanks to Proposition
5.3
, we obtain

(31)

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(t)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\,,

since
u_{n}
is an entropy solution to problem (
19
).

It remains to pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
. To begin with, fix
0<s<t<T
and integrate (
31
) over
(s,t)
:

(32)

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}(s)-\phi(s))\,dx+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,dx\,d\tau\\
-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u_{n}(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(\tau)\,dx\,d\tau\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\tau)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,dx\,d\tau\,.

Recalling (
26
) and taking into account that

\displaystyle\big|J_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\big|\leqslant k\big(|u_{n}(t)|+|\phi(t)|\big)\,,

\displaystyle\big|f_{n}(t)\,T_{k}(u_{n}(t)-\phi(t))\big|\leqslant k|f_{n}(t)|\,,

(notice that the right hand sides converge in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
to
k\big(|u(t)|+|\phi(t)|\big)
and
k|f(t)|
, respectively), every term of (
32
) converges and so we may take the limits to get

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(s)-\phi(s))\,dx+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)\,T_{k}(u(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,dx\,d\tau\\
-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(\tau)\,dx\,d\tau\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}f(\tau)\,T_{k}(u(\tau)-\phi(\tau))\,dx\,d\tau\,.

Since this inequality holds for every
0<s<t<T
, we finally conclude that

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx\\
-\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{\phi}(t)\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f(t)\,T_{k}(u(t)-\phi(t))\,dx

holds for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.
Hence, the proof is complete.

Remark 5.5
.

Having in mind (
30
), when taking the limit of (
28
) as
\delta
goes to 0 we obtain

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}u(s))\,ds\,dt\\
=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(s)|\,ds\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(t)|\,dt\,.

Appealing to
[
22
, Lemma A6]
, the above limit leads to

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\left|\eta(s)\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}u(s))-\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(t)|\right|\,ds\,dt=0\,.

Thus, in a
mean sense
, it leads to

\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}u(t))\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(t)\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}u(t)|\,dt

for every non-negative
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
, so that we should infer that

(33)

\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}(t),DT_{k}(u(t))\big)=\int_{\Omega}|DT_{k}(u(t))|

holds for almost every
t\in(0,T)
. Nevertheless, we are not able to prove (
33
) since the convergences involved are not sufficient to achieve this conclusion.

We point out that the identity (
33
) is equivalent to

\langle\!\langle\xi(t),T_{k}(u(t))\rangle\!\rangle_{\Omega}=\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u(t))\,dx\right)^{\prime}.

6.
Uniqueness

In this section, we address the uniqueness of entropy solutions to problem (
2
) when the initial data and the source term are merely integrable functions. Specifically, we prove that the solution obtained as the limit of the approximating solutions in Theorem
5.4
is the unique entropy solution.

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists a unique entropy solution to problem (
2
) when
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.  In order to show uniqueness, we follow the same pattern used in the
p
–Laplacian case (
[
29
]
and
[
5
]
).

Let
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, and denote by
u
and
v
two entropy solutions to (
2
) such that
u
is obtained as a limit of the approximating problems (
19
). Hence, there exist sequences

(a)

\{u_{n}\}
in
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L_{w}^{1}(0,T;BV(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

(b)

\{f_{n}\}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,

(c)

\{u_{n}^{0}\}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}(0)=u_{n}^{0}
,

(d)

\{{\bf z}_{n}\}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\|{\bf z}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
,
\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and conditions (i)–(iii) of Definition
3.1
;

satisfying:

(e)

u_{n}\to u
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
,

(f)

f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,

(g)

u_{n}^{0}\to u_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Taking
\phi=u_{n}
in the entropy formulation of
v
and integrating between
s
and
t
(where
0<s<t<T
), it yields

(34)

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(t)-u_{n}(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(s)-u_{n}(s))\,dx+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma)T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\\
+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),DT_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\big)\,d\sigma-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))[{\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\,d\sigma\\
\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}f(\sigma)T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

On the other hand, we choose the test function
T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))
in problem (
19
) (actually in (
20
)) and apply Green’s formula to get

\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma)\,T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\\
=\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}(\sigma)\,dx\,d\sigma+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\sigma)T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\\
=-\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),DT_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\big)\,d\sigma+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))[{\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\,d\sigma\\
+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\sigma)T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

In other words:

(35)

\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma)\,T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),DT_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\big)\,d\sigma\\
-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))[{\bf z}_{n}(\sigma),\nu]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\,d\sigma=\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\sigma)T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

Joining (
34
) and (
35
), it implies

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(t)-u_{n}(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(s)-u_{n}(s))\,dx\\
\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}(f(\sigma)-f_{n}(\sigma))T_{k}(v(\sigma)-u_{n}(\sigma))\,dx\,d\sigma\,.

Taking into account the convergences satisfied by
u_{n}
and
f_{n}
, it is straightforward to let
n
go to infinity applying Lebesgue’s Theorem and so to arrive at

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(t)-u(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(v(s)-u(s))\,dx\leqslant 0\,.

Dividing by
k>0
and taking the limit as
k
goes to
0
it leads to

\int_{\Omega}|v(t)-u(t)|\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|v(s)-u(s)|\,dx\quad\text{ for }0<s<t\,,

which implies that function
t\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v(t)-u(t)|\,dx
is non-increasing. Thus, for all
t\in(0,T)
, it implies

\int_{\Omega}|v(t)-u(t)|\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|v(0)-u(0)|\,dx=0\,,

from where we deduce the uniqueness of the solution.

Since the existence and uniqueness of an entropy solution to problem (
2
) has been established for every
T>0
, the existence of a unique global entropy solution to problem (
1
) follows.

7.
Comparison between solutions

Having established the well-posedness of problem (
2
), this section is devoted to studying the stability of the solutions. We prove that the distance between two solutions in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
is controlled by the distance between their respective data. Additionally, we establish a comparison principle.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be the entropy solutions corresponding to data
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}^{1},u_{0}^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, respectively. It holds:

a)

If
u_{0}^{1}\leqslant u_{0}^{2}
and
f_{1}\leqslant f_{2}
, then
u_{1}\leqslant u_{2}
.

b)

\displaystyle\max_{t\in(0,T)}\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}|f_{1}(t)-f_{2}(t)|\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}^{1}-u_{0}^{2}|\,dx
.

Proof
.
a)
Let
u_{i}
be an entropy solution to problem (
2
) with data
f_{i}\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{i}^{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
i=1,2
.
Now, consider the sequences
\{f_{n}^{i}\}\subset L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
\{u_{n}^{0,i}\}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
with

(36)

f_{n}^{i}\longrightarrow f_{i}\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad u_{n}^{0,i}\longrightarrow u_{0}^{i}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,

such that
f_{n}^{1}\leqslant f_{n}^{2}
and
u_{n}^{0,1}\leqslant u_{n}^{0,2}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, due to Theorem
5.4
, we also know that

(37)

u_{n}^{i}\longrightarrow u_{i}\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Using the test function
T_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})
in the approximate problem (
19
) corresponding to data
f_{n}^{1}
and
u_{n}^{0,1}
and also in (
19
) with data
f_{n}^{2}
and
u_{n}^{0,2}
, and applying Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{k}\big((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+}\big)\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{\prime}\,T_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})\,dx\\
=-\int_{\Omega}\big({\bf z}_{n}^{1}(t)-{\bf z}_{n}^{2}(t),DT_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})\big)+\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})\big([{\bf z}_{n}^{1}(t),\nu]-[{\bf z}_{n}^{2}(t),\nu]\big)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
+\int_{\Omega}(f_{n}^{1}(t)-f_{n}^{2}(t))T_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})\,dx

which, dropping the non-positive terms and integrating over
(0,t)
, becomes

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}\big((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+}\big)\,dx\\
\leqslant\int_{\Omega}J_{k}\big((u_{n}^{1}(0)-u_{n}^{2}(0))^{+}\big)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}(f_{n}^{1}(s)-f_{n}^{2}(s))T_{k}((u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+})\,dx\,ds\leqslant 0\,.

Dividing by
k
and letting
k\to 0
, we get

\int_{\Omega}(u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))^{+}\,dx\leqslant 0\,.

Using the convergences in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
of the solutions
u_{n}^{1}
and
u_{n}^{2}
(see (
37
)) we get

\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t))^{+}\,dx\leqslant 0

which implies that
(u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t))^{+}=0
, and so
u_{1}(t)\leqslant u_{2}(t)
for almost every
t\in(0,T)
.

b)
The second part of the theorem is established by choosing the test function
T_{k}(u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))
in problems with data
f_{n}^{i}
and
u_{n}^{0,i}
,
i=1,2
. Making the same computations as in part a), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}J_{k}(u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t))\,dx\leqslant k\left\{\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{0,1}-u_{n}^{0,2}|\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}^{1}(s)-f_{n}^{2}(s)|\,dx\,ds\right\}\,.

Dividing by
k
and passing to the limit as
k\to 0
, we deduce

\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{1}(t)-u_{n}^{2}(t)|\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}^{0,1}-u_{n}^{0,2}|\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}^{1}(s)-f_{n}^{2}(s)|\,dx\,ds\,,

from where the result is inferred thanks to convergences (
36
) and (
37
).

As a consequence of the previous result, we obtain an inequality that provides a control on the solution
u
in terms of its
L^{1}
-norm.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
u
be the entropy solution to problem (
2
) corresponding to data
f\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\max_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,dt\,.

8.
Regularity

This section establishes
L^{r}
-regularity for the entropy solutions. We demonstrate that if
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
for some
1<r<2
, then the solution not only belongs to
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
, but also lies in
C([0,T];L^{r}(\Omega))
. This result is consistent with the regularity obtained in
[
22
]
(square-integrable data yields square-integrable solutions) and the existence result of the present work (merely summable data lead to merely summable solutions).

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
1<r<2
. Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
. Then, the unique entropy solution
u
to problem (
2
) belongs to
C([0,T];L^{r}(\Omega))
.

Proof
.
Fix
1<r<2
and functions
u_{0}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
. Due to Theorems
5.4
and
6.1
, there exists a unique entropy solution to problem (
2
). Moreover, this solution is obtained through an approximation procedure. We will show that such a solution also belongs to
C(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
.

Choosing sequences
\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
\{u_{n}^{0}\}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
and
u_{n}^{0}\to u_{0}
in
L^{r}(\Omega)
, Proposition
3.4
yields a unique solution
u_{n}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{1}_{w}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
to every problem (
7
) with data
u_{n}^{0}
and
f_{n}
. Let
{\bf z}_{n}
denote the associated vector field.

Let us define now the auxiliary function
\omega(x,t):=\omega_{n,m}(x,t)=u_{n}(x,t)-u_{m}(x,t)
and the bounded, non-decreasing, continuous function

\varphi_{k}(s)=r|T_{k}(s)|^{r-1}{\rm\,sign}(s)\,.

Taking the test function
\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))
in both problems (those corresponding to
m
and
n
) and subtracting the resulting equations, we get

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\omega^{\prime}(s)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}[\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{n}(s)-\hbox{\rm div\,}{\bf z}_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx.

Defining
\Psi_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{k}(s)\,ds
, using Proposition
4.1
and dropping non positive terms, the previous equality becomes

\left(\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx\right)^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\omega^{\prime}(s)\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx.

Now, we integrate over
(0,t)
with
t\in(0,T)
:

(38)

\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(\omega(t))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(\omega(0))\,dx\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx\,ds.

The next step is to pass to the limit as
k\to+\infty
. On the one hand, by Young’s inequality,

|\Psi_{k}(\omega(s))|\leqslant 2|\omega(s)|^{r}\leqslant 2\left(\frac{r}{2}|\omega(s)|^{2}+\frac{2-r}{2}\right)\in L^{1}(\Omega)

hence, the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(\omega(t))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\omega(t)|^{r}\,dx=\|\omega(t)\|_{r}^{r}

and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(\omega(0))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\omega(0)|^{r}\,dx=\|\omega(0)\|_{r}^{r}.

On the other hand,
|\varphi_{k}(s)|=r|T_{k}(s)|^{r-1}\leqslant r|s|^{r-1}\leqslant r\left(\frac{r-1}{2}|s|^{2}+\frac{3-r}{2}\right)
and so

|[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))|\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)

owing to
f_{n},f_{m}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
.
Using again the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]\varphi_{k}(\omega(s))\,dx\,ds=r\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Omega}[f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)]|\omega(s)|^{r-1}{\rm\,sign}(\omega(s))\,dx\,ds\\
\leqslant r\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega(s)|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\\
=r\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)\|_{r}\|\omega(s)\|_{r}^{r-1}\,ds

and inequality (
38
) becomes

\|\omega(t)\|_{r}^{r}\leqslant\|\omega(0)\|_{r}^{r}+r\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{n}(s)-f_{m}(s)\|_{r}\|\omega(s)\|_{r}^{r-1}\,ds\,.

Finally, applying a Gronwall type inequality due to Bihari
[
11
]
(see
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
) we obtain

\|\omega(t)\|_{r}\leqslant\|\omega(0)\|_{r}+\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))}\,;

and since
u_{n}^{0}\to u_{0}
and
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{r}(\Omega)
and
L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
, respectively, we conclude that
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))
. Since each
u_{n}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\subset C([0,T];L^{r}(\Omega))
, the result follows.

9.
Long term decay for the homogeneous problem

This final section analyzes the long-time behavior of solutions to the homogeneous problem. The qualitative properties and asymptotic behavior of the total variation flow have been thoroughly studied (see
[
3
]
) with bounded initial data; here we show that for initial data
u_{0}\in L^{r_{0}}(\Omega)
with
r_{0}\in(1,2)
, the solution
u
to problem (
2
) decays in norm over time in the
L^{r}(\Omega)
spaces for every
r\in(1,r_{0})
. This fact extends the main result of
[
28
]
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Fix
1<r_{0}<2
and assume
u_{0}\in L^{r_{0}}(\Omega)
and
f\equiv 0
. Then, for every
1<r<r_{0}
, the solution
u
to problem (
2
) satisfies

\|u(t)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{r_{0}}(\Omega)}^{h_{0}}}{t^{h_{1}}}\quad\text{ for every }t>0.

with
h_{0}=\frac{r_{0}(N-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}
,
h_{1}=\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}
and
C=\left(\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{N-r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}}
.

Proof
.
First, assume that
u
is the solution to problem (
2
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. In this first step, we argue as in
[
28
, Theorem 3.3]
. We begin by applying
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
which provides us with a unique solution
u\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
. As mentioned, this solution is found as a limit of solutions to parabolic problems involving the
p
–Laplacian. More precisely: there exists a family
\{u_{p}\}_{p}
, where
1<p<2
, such that each
u_{p}

is a solution to problem

(39)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}u^{\prime}_{p}-\Delta_{p}u_{p}&=&0\,,&\hbox{in }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,;\\[8.53581pt]
u_{p}&=&0\,,&\hbox{on }(0,+\infty)\times\partial\Omega\,;\\[8.53581pt]
u_{p}(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\hbox{in }\Omega\,;\end{array}\right.

(so that
u_{p}\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))\subset C([0,+\infty);L^{r}(\Omega))
)
and this family satisfies, for every
t>0
,

u_{p}(t)\to u(t)\qquad\hbox{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega)\,.

It follows from
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
that if
1<p<\frac{2N}{N+r_{0}}
and
1<r<r_{0}
, then

(40)

\|u_{p}(t)\|_{r}\leqslant C_{p}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{r_{0}}^{h_{0,p}}}{t^{h_{1,p}}}

where
h_{0,p}
,
h_{1,p}
and
C_{p}
depend only on the parameters and satisfy

\lim_{p\to 1}h_{0,p}=h_{0}:=\frac{r_{0}(N-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}\,,\,\,\lim_{p\to 1}h_{1,p}=h_{1}:=\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}\,,\,\,\lim_{p\to 1}C_{p}=C:=\left(\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{N-r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{N(r_{0}-r)}{r(N-r_{0})}}\,.

Therefore, letting
p
tend to 1 in (
40
), we get

(41)

\|u(t)\|_{r}\leqslant C\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{r_{0}}^{h_{0}}}{t^{h_{1}}}.

For the general case, let
u_{0}\in L^{r_{0}}(\Omega)
. Then Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
6.1
guarantee that there exists a sequence of approximate solutions
\{u_{n}\}\subset C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
such that

u_{n}^{0}\to u_{0}\qquad\hbox{strongly in }L^{r}(\Omega)\,,

where
u_{n}^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
denotes the initial datum of
u_{n}
, and

u_{n}(t)\to u(t)\qquad\hbox{strongly in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,

for every
t>0
.
Actually, we have deduced in the proof of Theorem
8.1
that, for every
t>0
,

u_{n}(t)\to u(t)\qquad\hbox{strongly in }L^{r}(\Omega)\,.

On the other hand, since
u_{n}\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
, for every
t>0
, we obtain

(42)

\|u_{n}(t)\|_{r}\leqslant C\frac{\|u_{n}^{0}\|_{r_{0}}^{h_{0}}}{t^{h_{1}}}

with the same constants as above.
Hence, passing to the limit as
n\to+\infty
in (
42
), the desired result is obtained.
