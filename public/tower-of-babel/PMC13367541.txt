VICTOR-CPA: Oral itraconazole versus oral voriconazole for treatment-naive patients with chronic pulmonary aspergillosis in India
Authors: Asmita A. Mehta
Published: 2026

The Oral itraconazole versus oral voriconazole for treatment-naive patients with chronic pulmonary aspergillosis in India (VICTOR-CPA) trial provides the first randomised controlled comparison of itraconazole and voriconazole for treatment-naïve chronic pulmonary aspergillosis (CPA) in India, a setting with a substantial post-tuberculosis (TB) CPA burden.[1] Its single-centre design enabled uniform application of European Respiratory Society (ERS)/ European Society of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases (ESCMID)/ European Confederation of Medical Mycology (ECMM)-aligned diagnostic criteria, consistent radiological assessment, structured adverse-event monitoring, predefined therapeutic drug monitoring and excellent follow-up retention, offering valuable comparative data for Indian CPA practice.

The aim of this commentary is not to reinterpret the trial’s findings but to place them within the broader landscape of CPA research in India. As the field evolves, areas such as detailed phenotypic characterisation of post-TB structural disease, harmonisation of clinico-radiological and ancillary endpoints and development of validated CPA-specific outcome tools remain important directions for future work. Likewise, expansion into multicentric cohorts will eventually help understand disease behaviour across India’s diverse clinical settings. These considerations reflect general priorities for CPA research rather than limitations of the VICTOR-CPA study, which contributes essential randomised evidence to an area where high-quality comparative data remain scarce.

DIAGNOSTIC PRECISION

CPA diagnosis is conventionally based on ≥3 months of symptoms, characteristic Computed Tomography (CT) abnormalities in a structurally damaged lung and microbiological or serological evidence of Aspergillus infection as outlined in international guidelines.[23] The VICTOR-CPA trial adhered to these established criteria, utilising validated A. fumigatus-immunoglobulin G (IgG) thresholds, serum and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) galactomannan cut-offs, culture results and histopathology where applicable, with systematic exclusion of competing diagnoses including active tuberculosis, Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria (NTM) infection and other Aspergillus-related syndromes.

While misclassification was not demonstrated in the VICTOR-CPA trial, the broader field continues to face challenges in high post-tuberculosis settings where radiological and clinical overlap with other chronic lung conditions can complicate disease characterisation. As CPA research expands in India, detailed phenotypic reporting and harmonised diagnostic stratification may help improve comparability across studies and support refinement of CPA subgroups for future clinical research.[45]

OUTCOME MEASURES

The VICTOR-CPA trial incorporated multiple clinical, radiological, physiological and serological assessments; however, the definition of treatment response remained appropriately clinico-radiological, requiring stability or improvement in Visual Analog Scale (VAS)-based symptom scores together with corresponding CT findings, consistent with current ERS/ECMM and CPAnet consensus recommendations.[1] Although 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT), St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ), serial A. fumigatus-IgG titres and fungal cultures were collected and reported, these measures are not currently validated or recommended as primary indicators of treatment response in CPA.[5]

Their inclusion nonetheless reflects ongoing attempts within the field to characterise disease trajectories more comprehensively. As CPA research evolves, the development and validation of CPA-specific, multidimensional outcome tools may help complement established clinico-radiological frameworks and support a more nuanced understanding of treatment effects in future trials.[56]

MICROBIOLOGICAL FOLLOW-UP

Serial Aspergillus-IgG, serum galactomannan and sputum fungal cultures are often incorporated into CPA studies to provide adjunctive information on disease evolution; however, current evidence indicates that these markers may remain elevated despite clinical and radiological improvement and are not recommended as primary indicators of treatment response.[45]

Given the limited sensitivity of sputum cultures and the imperfect correlation between serological trends and disease activity, future CPA research in India may benefit from exploring prospectively defined microbiological measures as ancillary endpoints. Such work could help clarify the role of mycological kinetics within broader multidimensional outcome frameworks, without replacing the established clinico-radiological criteria that remain central to response assessment.

SAFETY MONITORING

Long-term triazole therapy requires structured surveillance because voriconazole-associated toxicities such as hepatotoxicity, photosensitivity and neurocognitive effects are well recognised,[7] while itraconazole necessitates therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) due to variable absorption and non-linear pharmacokinetics. The VICTOR-CPA trial incorporated scheduled biochemical testing, symptom-based monitoring and predefined TDM checkpoints, which enabled consistent identification of adverse events and highlighted the higher frequency of hepatic and mucocutaneous toxicities in the voriconazole arm.

Although detailed subgroup analyses were beyond the scope of the report, the broader field would benefit from future studies that pair TDM data with exposure–toxicity modelling and explore phenotype-specific risk factors for adverse events. Such approaches could enhance understanding of variability in azole tolerability and support more individualised antifungal dosing strategies in CPA.

STUDY DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS

The single-centre execution of VICTOR-CPA[1] offered advantages in diagnostic consistency, imaging interpretation, protocol adherence and patient follow-up—elements that are particularly important in early comparative efficacy trials. As CPA research in India evolves, multicentre studies will play an important role in understanding how treatment responses vary across diverse post-tuberculosis lung disease phenotypes, regional environmental exposures and differing patterns of structural lung damage. Such work would complement the internal consistency achieved in single-centre designs and help refine the broader applicability of CPA therapeutic strategies in varied clinical settings.

CONCLUSION

CPA remains an important clinical challenge in India, particularly in the context of widespread post-tuberculosis structural lung disease. The VICTOR-CPA trial provides valuable randomised data through consistent diagnostic application, structured monitoring and careful assessment of clinico-radiological response.[1] As CPA research in India advances, broader priorities for the field include refining phenotypic characterisation of post-TB lung disease, developing validated multidimensional outcome tools, exploring microbiological measures as supportive secondary endpoints and expanding multicentric research frameworks to reflect India’s clinical diversity. These directions align with international standards while addressing locally relevant epidemiological considerations. Continued progress along these lines will help strengthen antifungal stewardship and generate evidence that supports more individualised and durable CPA care across high-burden settings.
