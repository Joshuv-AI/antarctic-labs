Sturm-Liouville problems with a boundary condition depending bilinearly on an eigenparameter This work was completed with the support of ADA University Faculty Research and Developement Fund and Baku State University.
Yagub N. Aliyev, Narmin N. Aliyeva

Abstract
. This paper studies a Sturm–Liouville boundary value problem in which one of the boundary conditions depends bilinearly on the spectral parameter. The differential equation is considered on the interval ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) with a classical boundary condition at one endpoint and an eigenparameter–dependent boundary condition at the other. Explicit formulas for the inner products and norms of eigenfunctions are obtained. These relations make it possible to analyze the structure of the system of root functions and the corresponding biorthogonal system. Using these results, the minimality of the system of root functions in L 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L_{2}(0,1) is established. Furthermore, the basis properties of the system of root functions in the spaces L p ​ ( 0 , 1 ) L_{p}(0,1) , 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , are investigated. Necessary and sufficient conditions under which the system forms a basis are derived. Special attention is given to the cases of multiple eigenvalues and the case when the eigenvalue coincides with the critical value − d / c -d/c . The obtained results reveal a symmetry between different spectral cases and provide a simpler approach that avoids the use of the exit space L 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) ⊕ ℂ L_{2}(0,1)\oplus\mathbb{C} . Several examples are presented to illustrate the theoretical results.

It was shown in

(i) all eigenvalues are real and simple;
(ii) all eigenvalues are real and, except for a single eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity 2, are simple;
(iii) all eigenvalues are real and, except for a single eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity 3, are simple;
(iv) all eigenvalues are simple, and apart from one conjugate pair of non-real eigenvalues, they are all real.

In the current paper, we study the basis properties in
L_{p}(0,1)

(1<p<+\infty)

\lambda_{n}=\begin{cases}\left(n-1\right)^{2}\pi^{2}+O(1),&\text{if }\beta\neq 0,\\[6.0pt]
\left(n-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\pi^{2}+O(1),&\text{if }\beta=0.\end{cases}

[formula]

Using (1.4) one can show that the root functions with one function removed form a system quadratically close to the trigonometric systems. 12

2.
Inner products and norms of eigenfunctions.

By (1.1)-(1.3), if
y_{n}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{n}
, then

-y_{n}^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y_{n}=\lambda_{n}y_{n},

[formula]

y_{n}^{\prime}(0)\sin\beta=y_{n}(0)\cos\beta,

[formula]

(a\lambda_{n}+b)y_{n}(1)=(c\lambda_{n}+d)y^{\prime}_{n}(1).

[formula]

As usual
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{2}
mean the inner
product and the norm, respectively, in
L_{2}(0,1)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
y_{n},y_{m}
be eigenfunctions
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
and
\lambda_{m}

such that
\lambda_{n}\neq\overline{\lambda_{m}}
.

(a) If
\lambda_{n},\lambda_{m}\neq-\frac{d}{c}
, then

\left(y_{n},y_{m}\right)=-(ad-bc)\frac{y_{n}(1)\overline{y_{m}(1)}}{\left(c\lambda_{n}+d\right)\left(c{\overline{\lambda}}_{m}+d\right)}.

[formula]

(b) If
\lambda_{n}=-\frac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{m}
, then

\left(y_{n},y_{m}\right)=-(ad-bc)\frac{{y^{\prime}_{n}}(1)\overline{y_{m}(1)}}{(a\lambda_{n}+b)(c\overline{\lambda_{m}}+d)}.

[formula]

Proof.

By Lagrange’s identity

\frac{d}{dx}(y_{n}(x)\overline{y_{m}^{\prime}(x)}-y_{n}^{\prime}(x)\overline{y_{m}(x)})=(\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}})y_{n}(x)\overline{y_{m}(x)}.

By integrating both sides of this equality from 0 to 1, we obtain

(\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}})(y_{n},y_{m})={\left.{(y_{n}(x)\overline{y_{m}^{\prime}(x)}-y_{n}^{\prime}(x)\overline{y_{m}(x)})}\right|}_{0}^{1}.

From (1.2), we obtain

y_{n}(0)\overline{y_{m}^{\prime}(0)}-y_{n}^{\prime}(0)\overline{y_{m}(0)}=0.

[formula]

Therefore, if
\lambda_{n},\lambda_{m}\neq-\frac{d}{c}
, then

(\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}})(y_{n},y_{m})=y_{n}(1)\overline{y_{m}^{\prime}(1)}-y_{n}^{\prime}(1)\overline{y_{m}(1)}

=-\frac{(\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}})y_{n}(1)\overline{y_{m}(1)}(ad-bc)}{\left(c\lambda_{n}+d\right)\left(c{\overline{\lambda}}_{m}+d\right)}.

[formula]

By cancelling
\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}}
from both sides of (2.7) we obtain (2.4). If
\lambda_{n}=-\frac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{m}
, then
y_{n}(1)=0
and

(\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}})(y_{n},y_{m})=-y_{n}^{\prime}(1)\overline{y_{m}(1)}.

[formula]

By dividing (2.8) by
\lambda_{n}-\overline{\lambda_{m}}=-\frac{c{\overline{\lambda}}_{m}+d}{c}
, we obtain (2.5).
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
\lambda_{r}
is a non-real eigenvalue, then

{\left\|{y_{r}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}=-\frac{\left|{y_{r}(1)}\right|^{2}(ad-bc)}{\left(c\lambda_{r}+d\right)\left(c{\overline{\lambda}}_{r}+d\right)}.

[formula]

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{r}
is a non-real eigenvalue,
\lambda_{r}\neq{\overline{\lambda}}_{r}
, and by (2.4),

\left(y_{r},y_{r}\right)=-\frac{y_{r}(1)\overline{y_{r}(1)}(ad-bc)}{\left(c\lambda_{r}+d\right)\left(c{\overline{\lambda}}_{r}+d\right)},

which is equivalent to (2.9).
∎

If
\lambda_{k}
is a double eigenvalue
(
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}
) or if
\lambda_{k}
is a triple eigenvalue
(
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}=\lambda_{k+2}
), then for the associated function

y_{k+1}
corresponding to the eigenfunction
y_{k}
, the
following relations hold:

{-y^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}}+q(x)y_{k+1}=\lambda_{k}y_{k+1}+y_{k},

[formula]

y_{k+1}(0)\cos\beta=y^{\prime}_{k+1}(0)\sin\beta,

[formula]

\left(a\lambda_{k}+b\right)y_{k+1}(1)+ay_{k}(1)=\left(c\lambda_{k}+d\right)y^{\prime}_{k+1}(1)+cy^{\prime}_{k}(1).

[formula]

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{k}
is a multiple eigenvalue
(\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}\leq\lambda_{k+2})
.

(a) If
\lambda_{k}\neq-\frac{d}{c}
, then

{\left\|{y_{k}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}=-(ad-bc)\frac{y_{k}^{2}(1)}{(c\lambda_{k}+d)^{2}}.

[formula]

(b) If
\lambda_{k}=-\frac{d}{c}
, then

{\left\|{y_{k}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}=-(ad-bc)\frac{(y_{k}^{\prime}(1))^{2}}{(a\lambda_{k}+b)^{2}}

[formula]

Proof.

By Lagrange’s identity and (2.10),

\frac{d}{dx}(y_{k+1}(x){y_{k}^{\prime}(x)}-y_{k}(x){y_{k+1}^{\prime}(x)})={y_{k}^{2}(x)}.

By integrating both sides of this identity from 0 to 1, and using (2.11),

\left\|y_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}={(y_{k+1}(x){y_{k}^{\prime}(x)}-y_{k}(x){y_{k+1}^{\prime}(x)}|}_{0}^{1}=y_{k+1}(1){y_{k}^{\prime}(1)}-y_{k}(1){y_{k+1}^{\prime}(1)}.

If
\lambda_{k}\neq-\frac{d}{c}
, then by (2.12),

\left\|y_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}=y_{k+1}(1)\frac{{y_{k}(1)}(a\lambda_{k}+b)}{(c\lambda_{k}+d)}-y_{k}(1)\frac{{y_{k+1}(1)}(a\lambda_{k}+b)+ay_{k}(1)-cy_{k}{\prime}(1)}{c\lambda_{k}+d}.

By simplifying further we obtain (2.13).
If
\lambda_{k}=-\frac{d}{c}
, then by (2.3),
y_{k}(1)=0
, and by (2.12),

{y_{k+1}(1)}=\frac{cy_{k}^{\prime}(1)}{a\lambda_{k}+b}.

[formula]

Therefore
\left\|y_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{c(y^{\prime}_{k}(1))^{2}}{a\lambda_{k}+b},

which proves (2.14).
∎

Let

B_{n}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|y_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{|y_{n}(1)|^{2}(ad-bc)}{(c\lambda_{n}+d)(c\overline{\lambda}_{n}+d)},&\lambda_{n}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\|y_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{\bigl(y_{n}^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}(ad-bc)}{(a\lambda_{n}+b)^{2}},&\lambda_{n}=-\dfrac{d}{c}.\end{cases}

[formula]

Lemma 2.4
.

B_{n}\neq 0
if and only if the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
is real and has multiplicity one (is simple).

Proof.

By (2.9), if
\lambda_{r}
is a non-real eigenvalue, then
B_{r}=0
. Similarly, if
\lambda_{k}
is a multiple eigenvalue, then by (2.13) and (2.14),
B_{k}=0
. Suppose now that
\lambda_{n}
is real and simple.
Let
Y(x)
be a function satisfying

-Y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)Y=\lambda_{n}Y+y_{n},

[formula]

Y(0)\cos\beta=Y^{\prime}(0)\sin\beta.

[formula]

Since
\lambda_{n}
is simple,

\left(a\lambda_{n}+b\right)Y(1)+ay_{n}(1)\neq\left(c\lambda_{n}+d\right)Y^{\prime}(1)+cy^{\prime}_{n}(1).

[formula]

By (2.17),

\frac{d}{dx}(Y{y_{n}^{\prime}}-y_{n}{Y^{\prime}})={y_{n}^{2}}.

By integrating both sides of this equality from 0 to 1, and then using (2.18) and (2.19), we obtain

\|y_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\neq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dfrac{|y_{n}(1)|^{2}(ad-bc)}{(c\lambda_{n}+d)^{2}},&\lambda_{n}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle-\dfrac{c\bigl(y_{n}^{\prime}(1)\bigr)^{2}}{a\lambda_{n}+b},&\lambda_{n}=-\dfrac{d}{c},\end{cases}

which completes the proof.
∎

The following result is proved in the same way.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\lambda_{r}
and
\lambda_{s}=\overline{\lambda_{r}}
(
s:=r+1
) form a conjugate pair of non-real eigenvalues, then

T_{s}=\overline{T_{r}}:=(y_{s},y_{r})+\frac{y_{s}^{2}(1)(ad-bc)}{(c\lambda_{s}+d)^{2}}\neq 0.

[formula]

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{r}
is a simple eigenvalue, any function
Y(x)
satisfying

-Y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)Y=\lambda_{r}Y+y_{r},

[formula]

Y(0)\cos\beta=Y^{\prime}(0)\sin\beta,

[formula]

also satisfies

\left(a\lambda_{r}+b\right)Y(1)+ay_{r}(1)\neq\left(c\lambda_{r}+d\right)Y^{\prime}(1)+cy^{\prime}_{r}(1).

[formula]

By (2.21),

\frac{d}{dx}{y_{s}^{\prime}\overline{Y}}-{\overline{Y^{\prime}}{y_{s}}}={y}_{s}\overline{y_{r}}.

By integrating both sides of this equality from 0 to 1, and then using (2.22) and (2.23), we obtain

(y_{s},y_{r})\neq-\frac{y_{s}^{2}(1)(ad-bc)}{(c\lambda_{s}+d)^{2}},

which proves (2.20). Note that since
\lambda_{s}
is not a real number,
c\lambda_{s}+d\neq 0
.
∎

3.
Inner products with the first associated function.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{k}
is an eigenvalue of multiplicity two or three
(\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}\leq\lambda_{k+2})
.

(a) If
\lambda_{k}\neq-\frac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n}
, then

\left(y_{k+1},y_{n}\right)=-(ad-bc)\left(\frac{y_{k+1}(1)}{c\lambda_{k}+d}-\frac{cy_{k}(1)}{\left(c\lambda_{k}+d\right)^{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{y_{n}(1)}{c\lambda_{n}+d}.

[formula]

(b) If
\lambda_{k}\neq-\frac{d}{c}=\lambda_{n}
, then

\left(y_{k+1},y_{n}\right)=-(ad-bc)\left(\frac{y_{k+1}(1)}{c\lambda_{k}+d}-\frac{cy_{k}(1)}{\left(c\lambda_{k}+d\right)^{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{y^{\prime}_{n}(1)}{a\lambda_{n}+b}.

[formula]

(c) If
\lambda_{k}=-\frac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n}
, then

\left(y_{k+1},y_{n}\right)=-(ad-bc)\left(\frac{y^{\prime}_{k+1}(1)}{a\lambda_{k}+b}-\frac{ay^{\prime}_{k}(1)}{(a\lambda_{k}+b)^{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{y_{n}(1)}{c\lambda_{n}+d}.

[formula]

Proof.

We start with the identity

\frac{d}{dx}\left(y_{k+1}y^{\prime}_{n}-y^{\prime}_{k+1}y_{n}\right)=(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n})y_{k+1}y_{n}+y_{k}y_{n}.

By integrating this equality from
0
to
1
, we obtain

(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n})(y_{k+1},y_{n})+(y_{k},y_{n})={\left.\left(y_{k+1}y^{\prime}_{n}-y^{\prime}_{k+1}y_{n}\right)\right|}_{0}^{1}.

[formula]

By (2.2) and (2.11),
y_{k+1}(0)y^{\prime}_{n}(0)-y^{\prime}_{k+1}(0)y_{n}(0)
.
By Lemma 2.1,

(y_{k},y_{n})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{k}(1)y_{n}(1)(ad-bc)}{(c\lambda_{k}+d)(c\lambda_{n}+d)},&\lambda_{k}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\dfrac{cy_{k}(1)y_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{c\lambda_{k}+d},&\lambda_{k}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c}=\lambda_{n},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\dfrac{cy_{k}^{\prime}(1)y_{n}(1)}{c\lambda_{n}+d},&\lambda_{k}=-\dfrac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n}.\end{cases}

[formula]

Therefore, if
\lambda_{k}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n}
, then by (2.3) and (2.12),

(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n})(y_{k+1},y_{n})=y_{k+1}(1)y^{\prime}_{n}(1)-y^{\prime}_{k+1}(1)y_{n}(1)-(y_{k},y_{n})

=y_{k+1}(1)\frac{y_{n}(1)(a\lambda_{n}+b)}{\left(c\lambda_{n}+d\right)}-\frac{{y_{k+1}(1)}(a\lambda_{k}+b)+ay_{k}(1)-cy_{k}{\prime}(1)}{c\lambda_{k}+d}y_{n}(1)-(y_{k},y_{n}).

Using (3.5), dividing by
\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}
, and simplifying we obtain (3.1).
Similarly, if
\lambda_{k}\neq-\dfrac{d}{c}=\lambda_{n}
, then by (2.3),
y_{n}(1)=0
, and therefore

(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n})(y_{k+1},y_{n})=y_{k+1}(1)y^{\prime}_{n}(1)-(y_{k},y_{n}).

Again, using (3.5), dividing by

Finally, if
\lambda_{k}=-\dfrac{d}{c}\neq\lambda_{n}

(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n})(y_{k+1},y_{n})=\frac{cy_{k}^{\prime}(1)}{a\lambda_{k}+b}\cdot\frac{y_{n}(1)(a\lambda_{n}+b)}{\ c\lambda_{n}+d}-y^{\prime}_{k+1}(1)y_{n}(1)-(y_{k},y_{n}).

[formula]

(c) If

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that all eigenvalues of problem (1.1)–(1.3) are real and simple. Then the system

\{y_{n}\}\quad(n=0,1,\ldots;\;n\neq l),

where

is a nonnegative integer, is a basis of the space

(1<p<+\infty)

Proof.

It is sufficient to establish the existence of a system

(7.8)

B_{n}\neq 0

u_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{B_{n}\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})}\begin{vmatrix}y_{n}(x)&\mathfrak{A}(y_{n})\\
y_{l}(x)&\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})\end{vmatrix}.

Using relations
(2.4)

(2.5)

(2.16)

(u_{n},y_{m})=\delta_{nm},

where

\delta_{nm}=0
for
n\neq m
and
\delta_{nn}=1
. Indeed, if
n\neq m

(u_{n},y_{m})=\left(\frac{1}{B_{n}\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})}\begin{vmatrix}y_{n}(x)&\mathfrak{A}(y_{n})\\

(u_{n},y_{n})=\left(\frac{1}{B_{n}\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})}\begin{vmatrix}y_{n}(x)&\mathfrak{A}(y_{n})\\

(y_{l},y_{n})&\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})\end{vmatrix}

-(ad-bc)\mathfrak{A}(y_{l}){{\mathfrak{A}(y_{n})}}&\mathfrak{A}(y_{l})\end{vmatrix}=1.

[

using the asymptotic formula (1.4) and Bari’s theorem

]
