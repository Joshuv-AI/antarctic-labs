Project Overview
This LDRD project addressed LLNL’s need for fast, high-fidelity atmospheric simulations
spanning the gap between radiation-hydrodynamics models and global weather and Earth
system models. Existing modeling tools had limited scalability and speed, making them too slow
for operational nuclear consequence management, nuclear forensics, and downscaling for
localized weather-impact assessments. The team extended the exascale-ready Energy Research
and Forecasting (ERF) model to meet the LLNL mission on operational timescales. New
capabilities included Lagrangian particles for aerosol-cloud and debris microphysics,
radionuclide fractionation, complex-terrain and resolved-building methods, natural-landscape
and urban energy models, and tools for coupling to radiation-hydrodynamics simulations and
downscaling global weather to urban scales.

The resulting framework achieved approximately 100-fold speedups in simulation times, with
nuclear cloud-rise simulations running faster than real time and validating well against
observations from four atmospheric nuclear tests. The project also demonstrated scalable
super-droplet microphysics, including aerosol-cloud interactions and radionuclide-related
particle processes, as well as validated land-surface, urban canopy, complex-terrain, and
building-resolving simulations. Comparisons of the ERF simulations to measurements from the
Joint Urban 2003 field campaign are the most accurate in the literature, while also using the
largest domain size. These advances provide a practical foundation for operational use at LLNL’s
National Atmospheric Release Advisory Center (NARAC), improved nuclear-effects and forensics
modeling, and rapid assessment of urban heat, infrastructure, energy, and other weather-
related impacts. The work generated publications, open-source and mission-specific code
contributions, follow-on funding, and new atmospheric modeling capability across national,
energy, biological, and environmental security missions.

Mission Impact
This research significantly advances LLNL’s mission by delivering an exascale-ready atmospheric
modeling capability for rapid nuclear cloud-rise and fallout prediction, nuclear forensics,
emergency response, and regional and urban scale weather-impact assessments. The validated
nuclear cloud rise simulations and regional to urban modeling capabilities create new
simulation tools for consequence management and technical nuclear forensics while also

enabling new research in atmospheric effects, urban heat, infrastructure resilience, and
machine-learning-based cloud modeling. The project also brought new computational,
engineering, and physical science staff and postdoctoral researchers to LLNL and generated
numerous follow-on projects with a variety of sponsors. These results support DOE/NNSA
missions to develop science and technology tools for future national-security challenges,
protect against weapons of mass destruction, strengthen nonproliferation and emergency-
response capabilities, and maintain an agile and effective nuclear deterrent. They also benefit
other federal agencies by improving tools for radiological and chemical emergency response,
critical-infrastructure and energy resilience, urban hazard assessment, and defense-related
threat analysis.
