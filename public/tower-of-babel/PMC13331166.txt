Efficacy of internal neurolysis for older patients suffering from trigeminal neuralgia without neurovascular compression
Authors: Keisuke Onoda, Kohei Hashida, Takao Zama, Jumpei Kato, Tomihiro Wakamiya, Masahiro Indou, Tatsuya Tanaka, Kimihiro Nakahara, Takashi Agari, Takashi Sugawara, Kazuaki Shimoji, Eiichi Suehiro, Hiroshi Itokawa, Akira Matsuno
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: The efficacy of internal neurolysis (IN) for older patients with trigeminal neuralgia (TN) without neurovascular compression remains unclear. We evaluated the clinical outcomes of IN in older patients with TN without neurovascular compression, comparing them with those of their younger counterparts.Methods: We retrospectively reviewed 22 patients with TN who underwent IN without neurovascular compression. They were divided into older (≥70 years, n = 11) and younger (<70 years, n = 11) groups. Clinical variables included pain type (typical vs. atypical), recurrence status, and intraoperative neurophysiological findings. Surgical outcomes were assessed using the Barrow Neurological Institute (BNI) pain scale, with favorable outcomes defined as BNI I–II.Results: The older group had a higher proportion of typical TN cases (9/11 [81.8%]), whereas atypical facial pain was more frequent in the younger group (6/11 [54.5%]). Favorable outcomes were achieved in 19/22 patients overall (86.4%) (9/11 [81.8%] older and 10/11 [90.9%] younger patients). When stratified by pain type, older patients with typical TN achieved favorable outcomes in 8/9 cases (88.9%), whereas older patients with atypical pain showed favorable outcomes in 1/2 cases (50.0%). In the younger group, favorable outcomes were observed in those with typical TN (5/5 [100%]) and in 5/6 patients with atypical facial pain (83.3%). Poor outcomes (BNI III) occurred in three patients (13.6%) (two older and one younger patient). Transient facial numbness was observed in three patients, including two younger patients (18.2%) and one older patient (9.1%), all of which resolved during the follow-up. Intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring demonstrated shortening of latency and/or increased amplitude in most of the patients who experienced favorable outcomes.Conclusions: IN achieved favorable pain control in 86.4% of those with TN without neurovascular compression. Older patients experienced outcomes comparable to those of younger ones, particularly for typical TN. IN likely represents an effective surgical option for this patient group, even in the absence of a responsible vessel.

INTRODUCTION

Trigeminal neuralgia (TN) is most commonly caused by neurovascular compression and is typically treated using microvascular decompression. However, no clear neurovascular compression is identified intraoperatively in some patients. Internal neurolysis (IN) has been proposed as an alternative surgical technique for such cases, with several studies having reported favorable outcomes.[2-4,9]

Older patients with TN may present with different clinical characteristics, including a higher prevalence of typical pain and age-related neural degeneration, which can influence the surgical outcomes of IN. However, few studies have compared the efficacy of IN between older and younger patients.[1,11]

This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of IN for TN without neurovascular compression, and to compare the clinical characteristics and surgical outcomes between older and younger patients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patient population

We retrospectively reviewed patients with TN who underwent IN at our institution, without intraoperatively identified neurovascular compression. Both primary and recurrent cases were analyzed.

The patients were categorized based on age, with older patients being defined as those aged ≥70 years. Their pain-related characteristics were classified as typical TN or atypical facial pain, according to each patient’s clinical presentation.

Surgical technique

All patients underwent retrosigmoid craniotomy. After exposing the trigeminal nerve, careful inspection was performed to identify possible neurovascular compression. When no responsible vessel was identified, IN was performed by longitudinally separating the trigeminal nerve fibers under microscopic visualization. Herein, we present the case of a 76-year-old woman with recurrent left-sided TN. The nerve was adherent and severely deformed [Figure 1A]. We therefore dissected to release the adhesions and attempted to correct the deformity [Figure 1B]. IN was induced at three sites [Figure 1C]. Decompression of the trigeminal nerve was achieved, and its width was successfully increased [Figure 1D].

Surgical views. (A) The trigeminal nerve (arrow) was highly adherent to the surrounding tissues and had become deformed. (B) The adhesions were dissected, which improved the nerve deformity. (C) Three incisions were made in the perineural membrane (internal neurolysis, indicated by the arrowhead). (D) Decompression was performed on the trigeminal nerve itself, widening its diameter.

Intraoperative monitoring

Intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring was performed in selected patients, which involved recording any changes in latency and amplitude before vs after the procedure.

Outcome assessment

Postoperative pain outcomes were evaluated using the Barrow Neurological Institute (BNI) pain intensity score. Favorable outcomes were defined as BNI I–II. Various clinical variables were analyzed, including age, pain type, recurrence, and neurophysiological changes.

This study’s protocol was approved by the institutional review board of our institution, which waived the requirement for informed consent because of its retrospective nature.

RESULTS

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrated that IN provided effective pain relief for TN without neurovascular compression, with an overall favorable outcome rate of 86.4%. Previous related studies have reported favorable outcomes of 70–90%, supporting the efficacy of this procedure.[2-4,9] Notably, the older patients achieved outcomes comparable to those of their younger counterparts (81.8% vs. 90.9%), suggesting that age alone does not negatively impact surgical efficacy.

The older group had a higher proportion of patients with typical TN, whereas atypical facial pain was more common in the younger one. This difference in clinical phenotypes likely influenced the surgical outcomes experienced by the patients. When stratified by pain type, 88.9% of the older patients with typical TN achieved favorable outcomes, which was comparable to the proportion in the younger group (100%). These findings suggest that IN is particularly effective in older patients with typical pain characteristics.

Conversely, the outcomes were less favorable in our patients with atypical facial pain, particularly those in the older group. Only 50.0% of the older patients with atypical pain achieved favorable outcomes compared to 83.3% of younger patients. These results suggest that atypical facial pain may represent a different pathophysiological entity, potentially involving central sensitization or diffuse trigeminal nerve degeneration, that could be less responsive to IN. Similar trends have been reported in previous studies that evaluated patient subgroups with atypical pain.[5-8,10] Therefore, pain characteristics appear to be important factors when selecting surgical candidates for IN, particularly for older patients.

However, the number of atypical cases in our older group was small, thus limiting the definitive conclusions that can be drawn from the data. The observed difference should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Nevertheless, the trend toward poorer IN outcomes in older patients with atypical facial pain suggests that careful patient selection is warranted when considering this approach, as alternative treatment strategies may be preferable.

Our recurrent cases also responded well to IN, supporting its role as a salvage procedure when no responsible vessel is identified. Previous reports have also demonstrated the utility of IN for recurrent TN.[11]

Intraoperative neurophysiological improvements, including latency shortening and increased amplitude, were frequently observed in our patients who achieved favorable outcomes. These findings support the hypothesis that IN improves trigeminal nerve conduction by reducing ephaptic transmission between nerve fibers, as has been suggested in prior reports.[4,7] Our previous studies demonstrated an improvement in intraoperative electrophysiological conduction following IN.[7] The present findings in older patients further support the hypothesis that IN improves trigeminal nerve conduction, even in age-related degenerative conditions.

Regarding safety, transient facial numbness was observed in three of our patients (13.6%), including two younger patients and one older one. All of these cases were mild and resolved spontaneously during follow-up. No permanent neurological deficits or major complications were observed, which is consistent with previous reports indicating that IN is a relatively safe procedure.[1,3,9]

Taken together, these findings suggest that IN is effective, regardless of age, particularly in patients with typical TN. However, the outcomes in older patients with atypical facial pain may be less favorable, so careful consideration is advised when selecting surgical candidates.

This study was subject to several key limitations worth noting. First, the sample size was relatively small, particularly in terms of older patients with atypical TN. Second, the retrospective design may have introduced some degree of selection bias. Third, the follow-up period is short. Future prospective studies with larger cohorts are therefore warranted to validate our present findings.

IN appears to represent a safe and effective surgical option for TN without neurovascular compression in both older and younger patients. Older patients, particularly those with typical TN, are likely to achieve outcomes comparable to those of their younger patients. However, the outcomes appear to be less favorable for older patients with atypical facial pain, so careful patient selection is advised.

CONCLUSION

Internal neurolysis appears to be a safe and effective treatment option for trigeminal neuralgia without neurovascular compression. Favorable outcomes were achieved in both older and younger patients, particularly among those with typical trigeminal neuralgia. Careful patient selection may be important for older patients presenting with atypical facial pain.
