Estimates of Γ \Gamma -functions and their log \log{} -derivatives
Miron Bekker, Boris Mityagin

Abstract
We provide a lower bound for a weighted ratio of Γ − \Gamma- functions and its log − \log{}- derivative. Then we apply this result to estimate the norm of the operator associated with the Cesáro operator in the Hardy space H p ​ ( ℂ + ) H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+}) , 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty .

5. Appendix.
In this Appendix we provide a brief information about Hardy spaces
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
in the upper half-plabe
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}=\{z:\im z>0\}
and the Cesáro operator in these spaces. For detailed information about Hardy spaces we refer a reader to
[
5
]
(Chapter 11),
[
7
]
(in particular, Chapter VI, Sect. C, p. 112 ). We also recall a couple of identities related to
\Gamma-
function and its
\log{}-

A.
The Hardy space
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
,
1\leq p<\infty
consists of functions
f(z)
, which are analytic in the upper half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and staitsfy condition

\left[\sup\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x+iy)|^{p}dx:y>0\right\}\right]^{1/p}<\infty.

(24)

The space
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
consists of functions which are analytic and bounded in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
.
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
are Banach spaces. For
1\leq p<\infty
the norm is defined by the left side of (
24
),
and
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{|f|:z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\right\}
. For a function
f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the
nontangential
limit
\lim\limits_{y\to 0}f(x+iy)=f^{*}(x)
exists for almost all
x
, and the function
f^{*}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
. The function
f(z)
can be restored from
f^{*}(x)
with the help of Cauchy formula.

The Cesáro operator on the Hardy space
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
in the upper half-plane was introduced in
[
4
]
. It is defined by the expression (
22
).
In
[
4
]
it was proved that for
1<p\leq\infty
the operator
C
is bounded on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
(another proof was given in
[
2
]
) and for
1<p<\infty
its spectrum
\sigma(C)
is given by

\sigma(C)=\{w:|w-\frac{p}{2(p-1)}|=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\}.

(25)

From the last formula it follows that the spectrum of the operator
V(p)
, defined by (
21
), is the unit circle.
