Automorphism groups and derivation algebras of Hamiltonian Lie algebras
Pradeep Bisht, Suman Rani, Santanu Tantubay

Abstract
. In this paper, we compute the automorphism group and derivation algebra of the Hamiltonian Lie algebra ℋ N \mathcal{H}_{N} and its derived subalgebra ℋ N ′ \mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime} , where N N is an even positive integer. The automorphism groups are shown to be 𝐆𝐒𝐩 N ​ ( ℤ ) ⋉ ( 𝕂 × ) N \mathbf{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes(\mathbb{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{N} for both Lie algebras and we prove that all derivations are inner for the Hamiltonian Lie algebra, also we compute the full derivation space for the derived subalgebra of Hamiltonian Lie algebra.



1.
Introduction

Lie algebras of Cartan type over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero, particularly those arising as derivations of polynomial or Laurent polynomial rings, continue to be a rich source of examples and technique in infinite-dimensional Lie theory. The classical Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{N}
, the special Lie algebra
\mathcal{S}_{N}
of divergence zero vector fields and the Hamiltonian Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}
(
N
even) on torus form an natural hierarchy of such algebras each equipped with a natural
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-grading and connected to geometric structures on algebraic tori. The automorphism groups of Witt algebras have been studied extensively (see, for examples
[
R86
]
,
[
Ba16
]
,
[
Ba17
]
).

The author in
[
R74
]
gave sufficient conditions for a Lie algebra to have outer derivations. The derivation algebra of generalized Witt algebra was systematically investigated by Dokovic and Zhao in
[
DZ98
]
. The derivation algebra and automorphism groups are determined for higher-rank Virasoro-like algebras in
[
TX09
]
. Recently, the derivation algebras for Lie algebras of polynomial vector fields in infinitely many variables have been explicitly described by Bezushchak and Kashuba in
[
BK25
]
.

The Hamiltonian Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}
(
N
even) defined as the subalgebra of Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{N}
as Hamiltonian vectorfields on
N
-dimensional torus with respect to standard symplectic form occupies a distinctive position within this hierarchy. It decomposes as

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}\rtimes\mathfrak{h},

where
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}=[\mathcal{H}_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N}]
is the simple [see Proposition
4
] derived subalgebra graded by
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with one dimensional non-zero graded component and
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathbb{K}^{N}
is the Cartan subalgebra of degree zero elements, which tracks the
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-gradation of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. The Lie bracket is governed by the symplectic bilinear form on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, making
\mathcal{H}_{N}
a natural infinite dimensional analogue of finite-dimensional symplectic Lie algebras.

Recently weight representation theory of these Lie algebras were studied in
[
FT25
]
,
[
RP25
]
.

While the automorphism groups and derivation algebras have received considerable attentions, the full structure of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{H}_{N}),\;\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}),\;\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})
remained open beyond low rank or special cases. The purpose of this paper is to determine these objects explicitly.

The structure of the paper is as follows.

In Section 2 we first recall the necessary definitions and terminology. Later, we recall and prove lemmas that will be used in sequel. Notably, a generating set for the derived subalgebra of Hamiltonian algebra i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
is obtained in Proposition
3
.

Section 3 is devoted to obtaining the automorphism groups of the derived subalgebra of Hamiltonian algebra(
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
) and the Hamiltonian Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. It is shown in the Lemma
7
that any automorphism of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
maps a
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-graded component of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
to another graded component. In fact, it is shown that if
\bm{r}
th-graded component is mapped to
\bm{s}
th-graded component, also this association
\bm{r}\mapsto\bm{s}
is shown to be an odd function, that is, if
\bm{r}\mapsto\bm{s}
, then
-\bm{r}\mapsto-\bm{s}
. Lemma
6
together with Lemma
7
implies that the association
\bm{r}\to\bm{s}
actually determines an automorphism of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, and hence determine a matrix
\bm{Q}\in\mathbf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
. The commutator relations in
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
imply that the matrix
\bm{Q}
lies in the group
\mathbf{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
. Also, the explicit action of automorphism is obtained on the
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-graded components of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
is obtained. The automorphism group of the Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
described in Theorem
9
. In Lemma
10
, it is shown that any automorphism of the Hamiltonian algebra maps
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
and the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
to itself. Finally, the automorphism group of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
is presented in Theorem
11
, which gives us our first theorem:

Theorem

(A)
.

Let
N\geq 2
be even, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{H}_{N})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})\cong\mathbf{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{N}
, where
\mathbf{GSp}(\mathbb{Z})
is the conformal symplectic group over
\mathbb{Z}
, consisting of matrices
\mathbf{Q}\in\bm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{J}\bm{Q}=\lambda(Q)\bm{J}\;\text{for some}\;\lambda(Q)\in\{1,-1\}
and
\bm{J}
is the standard symplectic matrix.

In Section 4, we first prove in Lemma
15
that any graded derivation of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
of nonzero degree
\bm{r}
is inner by obtaining a generating set for
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
. In Lemma
16
, we describe all graded derivations of degree
\bm{0}
by doing a series of calculations for constants
c_{\bm{r}}
, where
h_{\bm{r}}\to c_{\bm{r}}h_{\bm{r}}
for all
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, under a graded derivation of degree zero, (see (
17
) and (
18
)). Derivation algebras of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{H}_{N}
is described in Theorem
17
and Theorem
18
. Our second theorem is stated as

Theorem

(B)
.

Let
N\geq 2
be even, then
\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N})=\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{H}_{N}
. In particular all derivations for Hamiltonian Lie algebras are inner.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N},\,\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{*},\,\mathbb{K}
denote the sets of positive integers, integers, non-negative integers, nonzero integers and an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

We fix a positive integer
N\geq 2
. Let
A_{N}=\mathbb{K}[t_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,t_{N}^{\pm{1}}]
be the commutative algebra of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathcal{W}_{N}
be the Lie algebra of all derivations of
A_{N}
, known as the Witt algebra. Let
d_{i}
denote the
i
-th degree degree derivation
t_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
for every
1\leq i\leq N
and
t^{\bm{n}}=t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}}\ldots t_{N}^{n_{N}}
for
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Also, denote by
e_{i}
the column vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
(e_{i})_{j}=\begin{cases}1,\;\text{if}\;i=j\\
0,\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
.

Define the bilinear form
\bm{(}\cdot\,,\cdot\bm{)}
on
\mathbb{K}^{N}
by
\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)=\delta_{ij}
, and extending bilinearly to
\mathbb{K}^{N}
. For
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{K}^{N}
and
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, let
D(u,\bm{r})
denote the derivation
\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}t^{\bm{r}}d_{i}
. Then the Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{N}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-graded Lie algebra with the grading given by

\mathcal{W}_{N}=\bigoplus_{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\mathcal{W}_{N})_{\bm{r}},

where
(\mathcal{W}_{N})_{\bm{r}}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\{D(u,\bm{r})\mid u\in\mathbb{K}^{N}\}
for every
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then commutator relations on
\mathcal{W}_{N}
are given by

[D(u,\bm{r}),D(v,\bm{s})]=D(w,\bm{r}+\bm{s}),\;u,v\in\mathbb{K}^{N},\;\bm{r},\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

where
w=\bm{(}u,\bm{s}\bm{)}v-\bm{(}v,\bm{r}\bm{)}u
. The abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\{d_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq N\}=\{D(u,0)\mid u\in\mathbb{K}^{N}\}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{W}_{N}
. The Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}_{N}
is naturally isomorphic to the Lie algebra of vector fields on the
N
-dimensional torus
(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{N}
. Then it has an important subalgebra, the Lie algebra of divergence-zero vector fields
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, given by

\mathcal{S}_{N}=\bigoplus_{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\mathcal{S}_{N})_{\bm{r}}

where
(\mathcal{S}_{N})_{\bm{r}}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\{D(u,\bm{r})\mid u\in\mathbb{K}^{N}\;\text{with}\;\bm{(}u,\bm{r}\bm{)}=0\}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{S}_{N}
contains the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
.

For
N=2m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the Lie algebras
\mathcal{W}_{N}
as well as
\mathcal{S}_{N}
has yet another important subalgebra known as the Lie algebra of Hamiltonian vector fields on
N
-dimensional torus
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. For the defining
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, we first require some notations.

Let
\bm{J}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{O}_{m}&\bm{I}_{m}\\
-\bm{I}_{m}&\>\bm{O}_{m}\end{pmatrix}
,
where
I_{m}
denotes identity matrix of order
m
and
O_{m}
the zero matrix of order
m
. Then for any
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{m},r_{m+1},\ldots,r_{2m})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, we denote by
\overline{\bm{r}}
the vector
J\bm{r}=(r_{m+1},r_{m+2},\ldots,r_{2m},-r_{1},-r_{2},\ldots,-r_{m})^{\top}
. Note that
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, therefore
\mathbb{Z}
is canonically embedded in the field
\mathbb{K}
. Therefore, we identify
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{Z}e_{i}
in
\mathbb{K}^{N}
. Similarly,
\mathbb{Q}
is canonically embedded in
\mathbb{K}
. Let us denoted by
h_{\bm{r}}
the derivations of
A_{N}
of the form
D(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{r})
. Then the Hamiltonian Lie algebra is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\{h_{\bm{r}}\mid\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}\rtimes\mathfrak{h}

Note that the Hamiltonian algebra is not simple, whereas its derived subalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}=[\mathcal{H}_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N}]
is. Also, we note that

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\bigoplus_{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\mathcal{H}_{N})_{\bm{r}}=\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}\rtimes\mathfrak{h},\;\;\;\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}(\mathcal{H}_{N})_{\bm{r}}=\bigoplus_{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{\bm{r}},

where
(\mathcal{H}_{N})_{\bm{r}}=(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{\bm{r}}=\mathbb{K}h_{\bm{r}}
for
\bm{r}\neq 0
,
(\mathcal{H}_{N})_{\bm{0}}=\mathfrak{h}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{\bm{0}}=0
. For the later use, we record the following;

[h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}]=(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}},

where
\bm{r},\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{m+i}s_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}s_{m+i}
.

Now we recall the following groups which are important for later purpose.

For
N=2m
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\bm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
denotes the group of all
N\times N
integer matrices with determinant
\pm 1
. The symplectic group over the integers is defined as

\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})=\{\bm{Q}\in\bm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{J}\bm{Q}=\bm{J}\},

where
\bm{J}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{O}&\bm{I}_{m}\\
-\bm{I}_{m}&\bm{O}\end{pmatrix}.

The conformal symplectic group over
\mathbb{Z}
is defined as

\bm{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})=\{\bm{Q}\in\bm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{J}\bm{Q}=\lambda(Q)\bm{J}\;\text{for some}\;\lambda(Q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}=\{-1,1\}\}.

See
[
MT11
]
, where they have defined the conformal symplectic group of any field
k
.

We note that
\bm{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\cong\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\lambda:\bm{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\lambda(\bm{A})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\;\bm{A}\in\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\\
1&\text{if}\;\bm{A}\notin\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\end{cases}

is a group homomorphism. The following sequence is split exact.

1\longrightarrow\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{i}\bm{GSp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow 1

hence, we have
\bm{GSp_{N}}(\mathbb{Z})\cong\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Recall that
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is said to be primitive if
\gcd(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})=1
. The following Lemma is due to Yves Benoist.

Lemma 1
.

[[Ben25], Lemma 5.3.] The group
\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
acts transitively on the set of primitive vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Now we record the following facts in terms of lemma, which will be employed later in the paper.

Lemma 2
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{H}_{N}
and
\langle S\rangle
denotes the Lie subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
generated by
S
, then

(i)

If the
h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}\in S
, then
h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}\in\langle S\rangle
if
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
.

(ii)

If
h_{\bm{r}}\in S
and
h_{\bm{s}}\in\langle S\rangle
, then
h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}\in\langle S\rangle
if
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
.

(iii)

If
h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}\in\langle S\rangle
, then
h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}\in\langle S\rangle
if
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Follows from Lie brackets

[h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}]=(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}},

and the fact that the nonzero graded components of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
are one dimensional.
∎

For any
\{0\}\neq S
, we define
L_{S}=\{\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid h_{\bm{r}}\in\langle S\rangle\}
, then by Lemma 2.2.
L_{S}
has the property that if
\bm{r},\bm{s}\in L_{S}
and
\left(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s}\right)\neq 0
, then
\bm{r}+\bm{s}\in L_{S}
. By the definition,
\bm{0}\in L_{S}
.

We prove the following important technical Proposition, which will be very helpful while computing the automorphism group.

Proposition 3
.

If
S=\{\pm e_{i},\pm e_{j}\pm e_{k}\mid 1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j<k\leq N\}
, then
L_{S}=\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Proof.

Claim 1:
. Every vector of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
with
\epsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
lies in
L_{S}
.

For
m=1
, we are already done.

For
m=2
, if
\bm{r}_{1}=\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\epsilon_{2}e_{2}
, then
\left(\overline{\bm{r}_{1}},\epsilon_{3}e_{3}\right)=-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3}\neq 0
, by Lemma 2.2, we have
\bm{r}_{2}=\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\epsilon_{3}e_{3}\in L_{S}
. Note that
\left(\overline{\bm{r}_{3}},\epsilon_{4}e_{4}\right)=-\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{4}\neq 0
, therefore , we have
\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\epsilon_{3}e_{3}+\epsilon_{4}e_{4}\in L_{S}
.

For
m>2
, if
\bm{r}_{1}=\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\epsilon_{2}e_{2}
and
\bm{r}_{2}=\epsilon_{3}e_{3}+\epsilon_{m+1}e_{m+1}\in L_{S}
then we have
(\overline{\bm{r}_{1}},\bm{r}_{2})=-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{m+1}\neq 0
, and hence it follows by Lemma
2
that
\bm{r}_{1}+\bm{r}_{2}=\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\epsilon_{3}e_{3}+\epsilon_{m+1}e_{m+1}\in L_{S}
.

Now if we choose
\bm{r}_{3}=\epsilon_{4}e_{4}+\epsilon_{m+2}e_{m+2}
, then we have
(\overline{\bm{r}_{1}+\bm{r}_{2}},\bm{r}_{3})=-\epsilon_{2}e_{m+2}\neq 0
, and again by Lemma
2
we see that
\bm{r}_{1}+\bm{r}_{2}+\bm{r}_{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\epsilon_{m+i}e_{m+i}\in L_{S}
.

Assume as the induction hypothesis that for any
k\in\{3,\ldots,m-1\}
, we have
\bm{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\epsilon_{m+i}e_{m+i}\in L_{S}
. If
\bm{s}=\epsilon_{k+1}e_{k+1}+\epsilon_{m+k-1}e_{m+k-1}
, then
\left(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s}\right)=-e_{k-1}e_{m+k-1}\neq 0
and again by Lemma
2
gives that
\bm{r}+\bm{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{(k+1)-2}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. It follows by induction that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}\epsilon_{m+i}e_{m+i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m-2}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

Now if
\bm{r}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m-2}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
and
\bm{s}^{\prime}=\epsilon_{2m-1}e_{2m-1}
, then
\left(\overline{\bm{r}^{\prime}},\bm{s}^{\prime}\right)=-\epsilon_{m-1}\epsilon_{2m-1}\neq 0
and hence Lemma
2
implies that
\bm{r}^{\prime}+\bm{s}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m-1}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. Similarly
\bm{t}=\epsilon_{2m}e_{2m}\in L_{S}
and
\bm{r}^{\prime}+\bm{s}^{\prime}\in L_{S}
and
(\overline{\bm{r}^{\prime}+\bm{s}^{\prime}},\bm{t})=-\epsilon_{m}\epsilon_{2m}\neq 0
implies
\bm{r}^{\prime}+\bm{s}^{\prime}+\bm{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
.

Claim 2:
We have
(\mathbb{Z}^{*})^{N}\subseteq L_{S}
,
in other words for any
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
we will show that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
lies in
L_{S}
for every
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{N})^{\top}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{N}
.

It is already shown in Claim 1 that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. Now we consider
\bm{k}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
, then we have
\bm{k_{1}},\epsilon_{1}e_{1}\in L_{S}
and
\left(\overline{\bm{k}_{1}},\epsilon_{1}e_{1}\right)\neq 0
implies

\bm{k}_{2}=2\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

Using induction hypothesis we assume that for
{i}\in\{2,\ldots,r_{1}-1\}
, we have

\bm{k}_{i}=i\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}

and we show that this is also true for
i+1
.

Observe that
\left(\overline{\bm{k}_{i}},\epsilon_{1}e_{1}\right)\neq 0
,
\epsilon_{1}e_{1},\bm{k}_{i}\in L_{S}
, therefore Lemma
2
along with the induction hypothesis gives
k_{i+1}=(i+1)\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. Now by induction we have

\bm{k}_{r_{1}}=r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

For
m=1
, We have
\left(\overline{\bm{k}_{r_{1}}},\epsilon_{2}e_{2}\right)=-r_{1}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\neq 0
and Lemma
2
implies
\bm{k}_{r_{1},2}=r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+2\epsilon_{2}e_{2}\in L_{S}
. Inductively, we deduce that
\bm{k}_{r_{1},r_{2}}=r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+r_{2}\epsilon_{2}e_{2}\in L_{S}
.

For
m\geq 2
, we note that
(\overline{\bm{k}_{r_{1}}},\epsilon_{2}e_{2})=\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{m+2}\neq 0
, therefore Lemma
2
gives that
\bm{k}_{r_{1},2}=r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+2\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. Repeating this process as above, we obtain

\bm{k}_{r_{1},r_{2}}=r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+r_{2}\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

For
m=2
, this means that
r_{1}\epsilon_{1}e_{1}+r_{2}\epsilon_{2}e_{2}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
. We prove the same for any
m>2
.

For
m>2
, we assume by induction hypothesis that for any
j\in\{2,\ldots,m-1\}
,

\bm{k}_{r_{1}.r_{2},\ldots,r_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

Since
\left(k_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{j}},\epsilon_{j+1}e_{j+1}\right)=\epsilon_{j+1}\epsilon_{m+j+1}\neq 0
and the above method can be repeatedly used to obtain
\bm{k}_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{j+1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=j+2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. By induction, we have

\bm{k}_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

In each case, we see that
\left(\overline{\bm{k}_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}}},\epsilon_{m+1}e_{m+1}\right)=-r_{1}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{m+1}\neq 0
, therefore Lemma
2
implies
\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+2\epsilon_{m+1}e_{m+1}+\sum_{i=m+2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
and repeating this method as above yields
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{i=m+2}^{N}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}
. Finally, as above we use induction to obtain

\bm{k}_{r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{N}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}\in L_{S}.

Claim 3:
We have
\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}\subseteq L_{S}
.

Let
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, then we can choose

By Lemma-

\mathbb{Z}^{N}

Lemma 15

For any
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}

(\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}))_{\bm{r}}=\operatorname{ad}\;\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\bm{r}}

Proof.

\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}

\tau=\gcd(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})

\mathbb{Z}^{N}

\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})

\bm{Q}\in\bm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})

\bm{Q}e_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}

9

\bm{Q}

\sigma

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
given by
\sigma(h_{\bm{r}})=\lambda^{\bm{r}}h_{\bm{Q}\bm{r}}
. As automorphism takes a generating set of a Lie algebra to another generating set, it follows that

)=0.\langle h_{\pm\varepsilon_{i}},\,h_{\pm\varepsilon_{j}\pm\epsilon_{k}}\mid\forall\;i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\;\text{and}\;\forall j<k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\rangle=\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}.

Let
\partial\in\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{\bm{r}}
, since each nonzero graded space of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}

c\in\mathbb{K}
such that

\partial(h_{\varepsilon_{m+1}})=ch_{\varepsilon_{m+1}+\tau\varepsilon_{1}}.

We observe that

\left(\partial+\frac{c}{\tau}\operatorname{ad}(h_{\tau\varepsilon_{1}})\right)h_{\varepsilon_{m+1}}=0.

Accordingly, we define
\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}=\partial+\frac{c}{\tau}\operatorname{ad}(h_{\tau\varepsilon_{1}})
, with this choice

for all

. Now suppose that
c_{1i}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{K}

The following lemma characterizes the outer derivations of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 16
.

If
\partial\in\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{\bm{0}}
, then there exists
h=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}D(e_{k},0)\in\mathfrak{h}
such that
\partial=\operatorname{ad}(h)
on
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, since
\partial\in\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})_{0}
it follows that
\partial(h_{\bm{r}})=c_{\bm{r}}h_{\bm{r}}
for some
c_{\bm{r}}\in\mathbb{K}
. Our aim is to determine the relation of
c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}
with
c_{\bm{r}}
and
c_{\bm{s}}
. We first observe that for
\bm{r},\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, satisfying
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
, we have

(17)

c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}.

To show this, we apply
\partial
to the identity
[h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}]=(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}
to obtain
\left(c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}\right)[h_{\bm{r}},h_{\bm{s}}]=(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}h_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}
, which immediately yields
c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}
, whenever
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
.

Next, we show that
c_{-\bm{r}}=-c_{\bm{r}}
for every
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. To this end, we choose
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
. Since
\left(\overline{\bm{r}+\bm{s}},-\bm{s}\right)\neq 0
, (
17
) impies that
c_{\bm{s}}=c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}+c_{-\bm{s}}
. On the other hand as
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})\neq 0
, another application of (
17
) gives

\displaystyle c_{\bm{s}}

\displaystyle=c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}+c_{-\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}+c_{-\bm{r}},

and hence
c_{-\bm{r}}=-c_{\bm{r}}
for all
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
as claimed.

We now establish that
c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}
for all
\bm{r},\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
Note that it is enough to consider the case where
\bm{r}+\bm{s}\neq 0
and
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s})=0
. Choose
\bm{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that
(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s}^{\prime})\neq 0,\;(\overline{\bm{s}},\bm{s}^{\prime})\neq 0,\;\left(\overline{\bm{r}+\bm{s}},\bm{s}^{\prime}\right)\neq 0
. Since
\bm{r}+\bm{s}=(\bm{r}-\bm{s}^{\prime})+(\bm{s}^{\prime}+\bm{s})
and
\left(\overline{\bm{r}-\bm{s}^{\prime}},\bm{s}^{\prime}+\bm{s}\right)=\left(\overline{\bm{r}},\bm{s}^{\prime}\right)-\left(\overline{\bm{s}^{\prime}},\bm{s}\right)=\left(\overline{\bm{r}+\bm{s}},\bm{s}^{\prime}\right)\neq 0
, it follows from (
17
) that

c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}=c_{\bm{r}-\bm{s}^{\prime}}+c_{\bm{s}^{\prime}+\bm{s}}.

Using again (
17
), together with the identity
c_{-\bm{r}}=-c_{\bm{r}}
, we compute

\displaystyle c_{\bm{r}+\bm{s}}

\displaystyle=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{-\bm{s}^{\prime}}+c_{\bm{s}^{\prime}}+c_{\bm{s}}

(18)

\displaystyle=c_{\bm{r}}+c_{\bm{s}}.

Finally, let
c_{e_{i}}=c_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq N
. For any
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, a repeated application of (
18
) yields

. Consequently,
\displaystyle\partial(h_{\bm{r}})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}c_{i}\right)h_{r}
for every
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. If we set

, then it can easily be verified that
\partial=\operatorname{ad}(h)
on
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
. This completes the proof.
∎

\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})

Theorem 17
.

\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}\rtimes\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathcal{H}_{N}
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
14
, Lemma
15
and Lemma
16
, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})=\operatorname{ad}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})\oplus\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{h})
. By Proposition
4
, it is known that the Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
is a simple Lie algebra, therefore, its center
Z(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})=0
. Hence
\operatorname{ad}:\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}\to\operatorname{ad}(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime})
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. Also the map
\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{h}\to\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{h})
can be easily verified to be a Lie algebra isomorphism. Therefore, the result follows.

Theorem 18
.

\text{Der}(\mathcal{H}_{N})\cong\mathcal{H}_{N}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}
is simple and therefore it is perfect and its center is trivial. By Theorem
