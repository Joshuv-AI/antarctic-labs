Universal Relations and Correlation Analysis of Proto-Neutron Star Properties in Energy-Momentum Squared Gravity
Sayantan Ghosh

Abstract
Proto-neutron stars (PNSs) are the hot, lepton-rich remnants of the core collapse supernovae, which go through a cooling phase and become cold, stable Neutron stars (NSs). Since PNSs are also superdense objects with strong gravitational fields, we can use them to probe general relativity (GR) in the high-curvature regime, similar to NSs. In this study, we analyze the macroscopic properties like mass, radius, compactness, tidal deformability, f f -mode oscillations and gravitational binding energy of PNSs using four different relativistic mean-field (RMF) equations of state (EOSs) with fixed entropy per baryon ( S S =1, 2) and varying the lepton fractions ( Y l Y_{l} ). The variation of S S and Y l Y_{l} has a noticeable effect on these properties. Extending our study beyond GR, we explore these effects within the framework of Energy-Momentum Squared Gravity (EMSG). This modified gravity theory adds the squared energy-momentum terms to the field equations with a free parameter α \alpha . In the weak-field regimes, EMSG remains indistinguishable from GR, but in the strong-field regimes, such as PNSs or NSs, it shows measurable deviations. Varying the free parameter α \alpha , we observe significant changes in the macroscopic properties of the PNSs. After that, we focus on the universal relations of the macroscopic properties and the correlations of the universal relations. We find that, despite significant changes in the macroscopic properties induced by the variations of S S , Y l Y_{l} and α \alpha , the correlations remain strong and nearly unaffected.

, we have to solve the following system of ordinary differential equations
Sotani
et al.
(
2011
)
, which are modified by the effective energy density and pressure in EMSG, represented as,

\displaystyle\frac{dW(r)}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d{\cal E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{dP_{\mathrm{eff}}}\left[\omega^{2}r^{2}e^{\lambda(r)-2\nu(r)}V(r)+\frac{d\nu(r)}{dr}W(r)\right]

\displaystyle-l(l+1)e^{\lambda(r)}V(r)

\displaystyle\frac{dV(r)}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{d\nu(r)}{dr}V(r)-\frac{1}{r^{2}}e^{\lambda(r)}W(r),

(17)

where
\nu(r)
and
\lambda(r)
represent metric functions, with the fixed background metric as mentioned in Eq. (
13
).

In the close vicinity of the origin, the solution to Eq. (
17
) exhibits the following behaviour:

W(r)=Br^{l+1},\ V(r)=-\frac{B}{l}r^{l},

(18)

where
B
is an arbitrary constant. To ensure that the perturbation pressure becomes zero at the outer boundary of the star’s surface, we need to apply the following additional boundary condition,

\omega^{2}e^{\lambda(R)-2\nu(R)}V(R)+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\frac{d\nu(r)}{dr}\Big|_{r=R}W(R)=0.

(19)

Utilizing the boundary conditions outlined in Eq. (
18
) and Eq. (
19
), we can successfully solve Eq. (
17
) and determine the eigenfrequencies of the PNSs.

2.5
Gravitational binding energy

The gravitational binding energy (
B
) is defined as the difference between the gravitational mass (
M
) and baryonic mass (
M_{B}
) of the NS and PNS;
B=M-M_{B}
, where
M
is calculated
Glendenning (
1997
); He
et al.
(
2015
)
, and modified by effective energy density in EMSG as

M=\int_{0}^{R}dr\ 4\pi r^{2}{\cal E}_{\mathrm{eff}}(r),

(20)

and
M_{B}=Nm_{b}
, where
m_{b}
is the mass of baryons (931.5 MeV) and
N
is the number of baryons calculated by integrating over the whole volume in the Schwarzchild limit as

\displaystyle N=\int_{0}^{R}dr\ 4\pi r^{2}\Big[1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}\Big]^{-1/2}.

(21)

In our work binding energy
B
originally corresponds to
B/M
, which is more convenient for comparison purposes.

B/M
is positive for an unstable system and negative for a stable system
(
Serot_1986
)
. For the whole NS, the
B
is negative
(
Patra_1992
;
Satpathy_2004
;
Kaur_2020
)
.

Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we presented a comprehensive analysis of proto-neutron star properties within the framework of Energy-Momentum Squared Gravity (EMSG) using four different realistic finite-temperature RMF EOS models (NITR, IOPB-I, MODEL I, and IUFSU).
