Rigidity and flexibility under spectral Ricci lower bounds and mean-convex boundary
Gioacchino Antonelli, Yangyang Li, Paul Sweeney Jr

Abstract
. We study Riemannian manifolds ( M n , g ) (M^{n},g) with mean-convex boundary whose Ricci curvature is nonnegative in a spectral sense. Our first main result is a sharp spectral extension of a rigidity theorem by Kasue: we prove that under the conditions λ 1 ​ ( − γ ​ Δ + Ric ) ⩾ 0 , H ∂ M ⩾ 0 , \lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric})\geqslant 0,\qquad H_{\partial M}\geqslant 0, and in the sharp range 0 ⩽ γ < 4 0\leqslant\gamma<4 if n = 2 n=2 , and 0 ⩽ γ < n − 1 n − 2 0\leqslant\gamma<\frac{n-1}{n-2} if n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 , a (possibly noncompact) complete manifold with disconnected boundary, with at least one compact boundary component, must split isometrically as a product [ 0 , L ] × Σ [0,L]\times\Sigma . 1.
Introduction

Let
n\geqslant 2
, and let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth complete manifold, possibly with boundary. In this paper, all the manifolds are assumed to be connected. We define the function
\mathrm{Ric}:M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathrm{Ric}(x):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in T_{x}M\\
g(v,v)=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Ric}_{x}(v,v).

Note that
\Ric\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\text{loc}}(M)
. For a constant
\gamma\geqslant 0
, we say that
M
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant 0

if either of the two following equivalent conditions holds:

(i)

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(M)
it holds

(1)

\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\Ric\cdot\varphi^{2}\geqslant 0;

(ii)

there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that
u>0
,
-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric\cdot u\geqslant 0
on
M
, and
\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle=0
on
\partial M
. Here
\nu
denotes the unit outward normal to the boundary.

The equivalence of the two conditions is readily seen on compact manifolds, and follows from the argument of
[
12
]
on noncompact manifolds. The study of manifolds with Ricci lower bounds in the spectral sense has recently attracted substantial attention: see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
17
,
7
]
, and the references therein. This study has been connected to the study of stable minimal hypersurfaces, and in particular to the solution of the stable Bernstein problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; we refer to
[
9
,
8
,
24
,
5
]
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors prove that, when
n\geqslant 3
, if
(M^{n},g)
is compact then

(2)

\lambda_{1}\left(-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}\right)>0\Rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\,\text{is finite},

and the constant
\frac{n-1}{n-2}
is sharp for (
2
) to hold.
Moreover, in
[
4
]
the authors prove that, when
n\geqslant 3
, if
(M^{n},g)
is compact then

(3)

\lambda_{1}\left(-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}\right)\geqslant 0\Rightarrow b_{1}(M)\leqslant n.

In
[
3
]
, the authors prove that when
n\geqslant 3
and
\gamma>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, then the condition
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric})>0
is stable under connected sums. Thus, one can observe that also in (
3
) the constant
\frac{n-1}{n-2}
is sharp for the property to hold. These results hint at the following natural question.

Question 1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Define

\displaystyle C(n):=\Bigl\{\gamma\geqslant 0:

\displaystyle\text{Any compact $M^{n}$ admitting a $C^{\infty}$ metric $g$ with $\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta_{g}+\mathrm{Ric}_{g})\geqslant 0$,}

\displaystyle\text{also admits a $C^{\infty}$ metric $\widetilde{g}$ such that $\mathrm{Ric}_{\widetilde{g}}\geqslant 0$}\Bigr\}.

Find the value of

\gamma(n):=\sup C(n).

By using Gauss–Bonnet theorem,
\gamma(2)=+\infty
. In view of the results in

explained above, we also have

for every

One of the aims of this paper is to settle a sharp boundary analogue of

1.1.
Main results

The understanding of
1
in the setting of compact manifolds with mean-convex boundary rests on two key results. The first is a sharp spectral generalization of a rigidity theorem first proved
by Kasue
[
19
, Theorem B(1)]
(see also
[
18
]
): a complete manifold with
\Ric\geqslant 0
and disconnected
mean-convex boundary, at least one component of which is compact, must split
isometrically. See also
[
10
]
for a generalization, and the recent related work
[
11
]
. We stress that in this paper
H_{\partial M}
denotes the mean curvature with respect to the outward unit normal
\nu
to the boundary
\partial M
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete (possibly noncompact) manifold with nonempty boundary
\partial M
. Let
0\leqslant\gamma<4
if
n=2
, or
0\leqslant\gamma<\frac{n-1}{n-2}
if
n\geqslant 3
. Let us assume that:

(1)

There is
0<u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that

(4)

-\gamma\Delta u+u\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0\,\,\text{on $M$},\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle\geqslant 0\,\,\text{on $\partial M$}.

(2)

\partial M
has at least two connected components, at least one of which is compact.

Then
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0
,
H_{\partial M}\geqslant 0
, and
(M^{n},g)
splits isometrically as
([0,L]\times\Sigma^{n-1},\mathrm{d}t^{2}+g_{\Sigma})
, where
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
is a compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{\Sigma}}\geqslant 0
, and
0<L<\infty
.

Remark 1
.

The following remarks are in order.

(1)

The fact that at least one connected component of

We remark that when

(3)

(4)

Theorem

Roughly speaking, this is due to the following fact. In the presence of at least two boundary components, at least one of which (say

among curves connecting

0\leqslant\gamma<\frac{n-1}{n-2}

It is known that if a compact manifold with boundary
(M^{n},g)
has
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0
, and
H_{\partial M}\geqslant 0

Theorem 2

Let

, and let

Theorem 3
.

Let

. Let
\gamma\geqslant 0

, or
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
if

.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact manifold with nonempty boundary
\partial M
. Let
0<u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that

(5)

-\gamma\Delta u+u\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0\,\,\text{on $M$},\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle\geqslant 0\,\,\text{on $\partial M$},

and at least one of these two inequalities is strict. Then

, and

.

Proof of
Theorem

3
.

Let us first deal with the case
n=2
. Let
(M^{2},g)
be a compact surface
with nonempty boundary, and let
\gamma\geqslant 0
.
Dividing the first inequality of (
5
) by

, we obtain

\mathrm{Sect}\geqslant\gamma u^{-1}\Delta u.

Integrating by parts gives

\int_{M}\mathrm{Sect}\geqslant\gamma\int_{M}u^{-1}\Delta u=\gamma\int_{\partial M}u^{-1}\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle+\gamma\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Therefore, using the boundary assumption in (
5

2\pi\chi(M)=\int_{M}\mathrm{Sect}+\int_{\partial M}H_{\partial M}\geqslant\int_{\partial M}\left(H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle\right)+\gamma\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\geqslant 0.

Moreover, if at least one of the inequalities in
(
5
) is strict, then we get
\chi(M)>0
. Since
M
has nonempty boundary, it follows that

M
is homeomorphic to the disk. In particular,
M
admits a metric with

\mathrm{Sect}>0
and strictly mean-convex boundary
H_{\partial M}>0
.

Let us now assume
n\geqslant 3
. Thus, by
Theorem

2
, we have
\pi_{1}(M,\partial M)=0
. Hence, by
[

, for

M^{n}
admits a metric with
\mathrm{Sect}>0
and

, as desired.

(1)

Remark 4
.

An alternative approach to spectral Ricci lower bounds is through

\mu
-bubbles, as in several recent works on related problems. In this paper we instead use weighted length minimizers for the conformal metric
\widetilde{g}=u^{2\gamma}g
. This has two advantages. First, it keeps the relevant variational objects one-dimensional,
and hence avoids additional regularity issues in high dimensions. Second, the one-dimensional framework
is best suited to prove
Lemma

3
, which is a key tool to prove the results in the generality of the present paper; namely, for possibly noncompact manifolds with disconnected boundary, with at least one compact boundary component. It would be interesting to understand whether our results, in this level of generality, can be
obtained by a
\mu
-bubble argument. In particular, it seems that such an
approach would require additional input to handle the noncompactness and possible singularity formation.

Preliminaries

We will recall the first and second variation formulae for weighted geodesics. In this section,
(M^{n},g)
is a (possibly noncompact) manifold with nonempty boundary.

2.1.
First variation of weighted geodesics

Let us assume
\partial M
is not connected. Let us write
\partial M=C_{1}\sqcup C_{2}
, where
C_{1},C_{2}
are nonempty unions of connected components of
\partial M
.
Let
0<u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
, and

\sigma:[0,\ell]\to M
be a curve parameterized by
g
-arclength.

We assume
\sigma(0)\in C_{1}
,
\sigma(\ell)\in C_{2}
, and
\sigma((0,\ell))\subset M\setminus\partial M
. Moreover, we assume that
\sigma
is
\widetilde{g}
-length minimizing among compact curves
\beta
connecting
C_{1}
to
C_{2}
, where
\widetilde{g}=u^{2\gamma}g
. This is equivalent to saying that
\sigma
is a minimizer of the functional
E(\beta):=\int_{\beta}u^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}\ell_{g}
, where
\mathrm{d}\ell_{g}
is the
g
-length element, among compact curves
\beta
connecting
C_{1}
to
C_{2}
. Notice that, under these assumptions,
\sigma
must necessarily be
C^{3,\alpha}
-regular.

Let
e
be a variational field along
\sigma
. Let
\varepsilon
be the variational parameter. Then, the first variation for the energy
E
at
e
is

\delta E(e)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\gamma}\langle\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\rangle^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\gamma}\langle\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\rangle^{1/2}\Big[\gamma\langle u^{-1}\nabla u,e\rangle+\frac{\langle\nabla_{\sigma^{\prime}}e,\sigma^{\prime}\rangle}{\langle\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\rangle}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t.

From now on, we will tacitly assume that all the terms in the integrals are evaluated at
\sigma(t)
. At the initial time
\varepsilon=0
, we get that for every variational vector field
e\perp\sigma^{\prime}
along
\sigma
we have

0=\delta E(e)|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\gamma}\Big[\gamma\langle u^{-1}\nabla u,e\rangle-\langle e,\nabla_{\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime}\rangle\Big]\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\Big[(n-1)(\psi^{\prime})^{2}+\psi^{2}\Big[\Big(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\Big)u^{-2}(u^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\int_{0}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\Big[(n-1)u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-2\gamma\,u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-1}u^{\prime}\psi\psi^{\prime}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{n-1}u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-2}(u^{\prime})^{2}\psi^{2}

-\gamma\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-1}u^{\prime\prime}\psi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\Big[u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-1}u^{\prime}\psi^{2}\Big]_{0}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\quad-u(\sigma(\ell))^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\psi(\ell)^{2}H_{\partial M}(\sigma(\ell))-u(\sigma(0))^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\psi(0)^{2}H_{\partial M}(\sigma(0)).

this may also be written as

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\int_{0}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\Big[(n-1)u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\Big)u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-2}(u^{\prime})^{2}\psi^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\gamma u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-2}|\nabla^{\perp}u|^{2}\psi^{2}+\psi^{2}\Big(\gamma u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma-1}\Delta u-u^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\Ric(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\Big)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t

Appendix: Inradius and Bonnet–-Myers Type Estimates

Proposition 5
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
, let
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
,
k>0
, and let

(M^{n},g)
be a smooth complete Riemannian manifold with nonempty boundary.
Let
0<u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
, and assume that

0<\inf_{M}u\leqslant\sup_{M}u<\infty.

Assume that

-\gamma\Delta u+\mathrm{Ric}\cdot u\geqslant 0\qquad\text{on }M,

and assume that, along
\partial M
,

H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}\geqslant(n-1)k>0,

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal to
\partial M
,

u_{\nu}=\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle
, and
H_{\partial M}
is computed
with respect to
\nu
. Then

\operatorname{InRad}(M):=\sup_{x\in M}d_{g}(x,\partial M)\leqslant\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}.

Thus, if
\partial M
is compact, then
M
is compact.

Proof.

Set

\alpha:=\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma.

Since
n\geqslant 3
and
\gamma\geqslant 0
, we have
\alpha\geqslant 0
.

Fix
x\in\operatorname{int}M
. Consider the functional

E(\beta):=\int_{\beta}u^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\ell_{g},

where
\beta
is a compact curve in
M
.
Equivalently,
E
is the length functional of the conformally changed metric
\widetilde{g}:=u^{2\gamma}g
.
Since
u
is bounded above and bounded below away from zero,

(M,\widetilde{g})
is complete. Hence there exists an
E
-minimizing curve

\sigma:[0,\ell]\to M,

from
\partial M
to
x
. We parameterize
\sigma
by
g
-arclength, so that

\sigma(0)\in\partial M,\qquad\sigma(\ell)=x,\qquad|\sigma^{\prime}|_{g}=1.

In particular,

d_{g}(x,\partial M)\leqslant\ell.

Because
\sigma
minimizes
E
, the weighted second variation inequality
applies with the initial endpoint free on
\partial M
and the final endpoint
fixed at
x
. The same computations leading to Lemma
1
show that for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}([0,\ell])
, with
\psi(\ell)=0
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\Bigg[(n-1)(\psi^{\prime})^{2}+\psi^{2}\Bigg(\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\right)u^{-2}(u^{\prime})^{2}-\gamma u^{-2}|\nabla^{\perp}u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma u^{-1}\Delta u-\Ric(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\Bigg)\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-u(\sigma(0))^{\alpha}\psi(0)^{2}\left(H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}\right)(\sigma(0)).

Now choose

\psi(t):=1-\frac{t}{\ell}.

Then

\psi(0)=1,\qquad\psi(\ell)=0,\qquad\psi^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{\ell}.

Substituting this choice of
\psi
gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n-1}{\ell^{2}}\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{t}{\ell}\right)^{2}\Bigg[\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\right)u^{-2}(u^{\prime})^{2}-\gamma u^{-2}|\nabla^{\perp}u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma u^{-1}\Delta u-\Ric(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-u(\sigma(0))^{\alpha}\left(H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}\right)(\sigma(0)).

Hence, using the inequalities in the assumptions, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n-1}{\ell^{2}}\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t-u(\sigma(0))^{\alpha}\left(H_{\partial M}+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}\right)(\sigma(0))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n-1}{\ell^{2}}\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t-(n-1)k\,u(\sigma(0))^{\alpha}.

Since
\alpha\geqslant 0
, we have

\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\ell(\sup_{M}u)^{\alpha}

and

u(\sigma(0))^{\alpha}\geqslant(\inf_{M}u)^{\alpha}.

Therefore

0\leqslant\frac{n-1}{\ell}(\sup_{M}u)^{\alpha}-(n-1)k(\inf_{M}u)^{\alpha}.

Equivalently,

\ell\leqslant\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\alpha}.

Since
d_{g}(x,\partial M)\leqslant\ell
, we obtain

d_{g}(x,\partial M)\leqslant\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}.

Taking the supremum over
x\in M
gives the desired estimate. The last part of the statement is straightforward.
∎

Remark 5
.

In
Proposition

5
, when
n=2
, the same proof gives
\mathrm{InRad}(M)\leqslant\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\gamma}
, for every
\gamma\geqslant 0
. In addition, when
n\geqslant 2
and

one can prove

for an explicitly computable constant

only depending on
n,k,\gamma
. The proof is by now standard: one substitutes

Remark 6
.

The fixed-endpoint version of Lemma
1
gives a
slightly better oscillation dependence in the Bonnet–Myers type estimate than
the one obtained in
[
2
, Theorem 1(1)]
. Indeed, assume that
M
is complete,
that

0<\inf_{M}u\leqslant\sup_{M}u<\infty,

and that, for some
K>0
,

(40)

-\gamma\Delta u+u\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant(n-1)Ku.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and

\alpha:=\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma.

If
p,q\in M
, let
\sigma:[0,\ell]\to M
be an
E
-minimizer between

p
and
q
, where

E(\beta)=\int_{\beta}u^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}\ell_{g},

and parameterize
\sigma
by
g
-arclength. Choosing as variation function

\psi(t)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{\ell}\right),

and using the second variation computations in
Section

2.2
, (
40
), and
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, we obtain

0\leqslant(n-1)\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\alpha}\left[\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi t}{\ell}\right)-K\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi t}{\ell}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t.

Estimating
u^{\alpha}
by
(\sup_{M}u)^{\alpha}
in the positive term and by

(\inf_{M}u)^{\alpha}
in the negative term gives

0\leqslant(n-1)\left[(\sup_{M}u)^{\alpha}\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\ell}-K(\inf_{M}u)^{\alpha}\frac{\ell}{2}\right].

Hence

\ell\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\alpha/2}.

Since
d_{g}(p,q)\leqslant\ell
, taking the supremum over
p,q\in M
yields

\diam(M,g)\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}\left(\frac{\sup_{M}u}{\inf_{M}u}\right)^{\frac{n-3}{2(n-1)}\gamma}.

Thus the fixed-endpoint second-variation argument improves the oscillation
exponent in the corresponding diameter estimate from

\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma,

obtained in
[
2
, Theorem 1(1)]
using
\mu
-bubble techniques, to

\frac{n-3}{2(n-1)}\gamma.
