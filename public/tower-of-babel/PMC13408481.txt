A multi-criteria economic and technical power optimization in intelligent off-electrical grid considering hybrid management of consumption and centralized participation of the hydrogen system
Authors: Alisher Sherov, Khashimova Naima, Mash’al Saidov, Qodirov Farrux, Shodiev Kamoliddin, Dilnavoz Shavkidinova, Komilov Kamol
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes energy operation in an intelligent off-electrical grid (IOEG) with hybrid management of demand and employing hydrogen system by three layers multi-criteria optimization approach. In up and middle layers, hybrid management of consumption in demand side is proposed. The up layer is formulated considering offer price approach for load clipping (LC) strategy. The load shifting (LS) strategy considering optimal rate of consummation is proposed in middle layer. In low layer, maximize consumer satisfaction as social objective, improving profile of voltage as technical objective and minimizing power production costs as economic objective are optimized as multi-criteria optimization considering managed consumption in up and middle layers. In addition, hydrogen system as power storage is employed for centralized power production (CPG) in lower layer. The suggested optimization approach is formulated in GAMS software. The Shannon entropy and improved epsilon-constraint methods are used for solving decision making and multi-criteria optimization in low layer. The 33-nodes test grid is used as IOEG and implementing suggested optimization approach. In the end, the results demonstrate the optimum values of objectives with the LS, LC and CPG strategies, achieved through a comparative analysis of different case studies. With implementing LS, LC and CPG strategies, the consumer satisfaction is maximized by 9.2%, the voltage index is improved by 7.52%, and the power production cost is minimized by 9.18%.Keywords: Intelligent off-electrical grid (IOEG), Three layers multi-criteria optimization approach, Load clipping (LC) strategy, Load shifting (LS) strategy, Centralized power production (CPG)Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Aims and background

The energy resources have been widely recognized as highly significant, revolutionary, and transformative solutions in the energy sector. By enabling electricity-generating and grid-interactive assets to be deployed closer to where power is needed, distributed energy resources (DERs) substantially strengthen the overall capability of electrical energy systems. As a result, these resources improve the ability of such systems to meet the continually expanding and escalating demand for electricity, thereby supporting sustained reliability and performance in the face of growing consumption1,2. This significant improvement is accomplished by actively minimizing the negative environmental effects that can arise from energy production and consumption. This is primarily achieved through the reduction of pollutant emissions, which not only helps in protecting our planet but also contributes to better air quality and public health. Additionally, it is essential to keep energy costs affordable for consumers and businesses alike, ensuring that everyone has access to the energy they need without facing financial strain3. Furthermore, guaranteeing a dependable energy supply is crucial; it means that individuals and industries can rely on consistent energy availability, which is vital for daily operations and long-term planning4. Lastly, enhancing overall energy efficiency plays a pivotal role in this improvement, as it allows us to make the most out of the energy we consume, reducing waste and promoting sustainable practices. Together, these strategies create a comprehensive approach to energy management that benefits both the environment and society5,6. Conversely, the emergence of sophisticated smart grid technologies has significantly transformed the landscape of energy management by enabling the creation of robust and efficient communication pathways6. These pathways facilitate a seamless connection between the demand side, which encompasses consumers and their diverse energy consumption behaviors, and the generation side, which includes a variety of energy production sources such as solar, wind, and traditional fossil fuels7. This enhanced communication not only allows for real-time data exchange but also empowers consumers to make informed decisions about their energy usage, ultimately leading to a more responsive and adaptive energy system8. As a result, both sides of the energy equation can work in harmony, optimizing energy distribution and consumption while promoting sustainability and efficiency in the overall energy ecosystem9. This development significantly strengthens effective energy management approaches in modern energy systems, aiming to improve efficiency9. However, the previously mentioned advantage of smart grids can be effectively utilized to incorporate a wide range of different energy generation sources. This includes, but is not limited to, energy storage systems (ESSs), which play a crucial role in balancing supply and demand by storing excess energy for later use. Additionally, DERs such as solar panels, wind turbines, and other renewable sources can be seamlessly integrated into the grid, allowing for a more decentralized and sustainable energy production model10. Furthermore, demand management strategies, which involve adjusting consumer energy usage patterns to match supply availability, can significantly enhance the overall efficiency of the system8. Together, these elements not only improve the economic feasibility of off-grid electrical systems by reducing reliance on traditional energy sources but also enhance their technical performance11. This integration promotes a more resilient and efficient energy ecosystem, capable of adapting to changing energy demands and environmental conditions, ultimately leading to a more sustainable future for energy consumption11–13. Off-grid electrical systems are meticulously engineered to operate autonomously, meaning they can generate and supply power independently without relying on supplementary energy sources such as transmission lines that are typically associated with conventional power plants. These systems are designed to harness renewable energy sources, such as solar panels, wind turbines, or hydroelectric generators, allowing them to produce electricity in a self-sufficient manner14. This independence from the traditional grid not only enhances energy security but also promotes sustainability by reducing reliance on fossil fuels and minimizing the carbon footprint. As a result, off-grid systems are particularly beneficial in remote areas where access to conventional electricity infrastructure is limited or non-existent, providing a reliable and eco-friendly solution for powering homes, businesses, and communities15. This aspect becomes particularly vital in remote regions where the presence of traditional energy infrastructure is limited or even nonexistent, making it challenging to meet the energy needs of the local population. In such areas, the energy grids must not only be reliable but also affordable, ensuring that they can adequately satisfy user demands, even when faced with difficult and unpredictable circumstances15–17. To achieve this, it is essential to implement effective strategies that can enhance the resilience and efficiency of these energy systems. For instance, demand management techniques can play a significant role in balancing energy supply and consumption, while centralized generation methods, particularly through innovative energy storage solutions like hydrogen systems (HSs), can greatly improve the grid’s capacity to handle emergencies such as power shortages18. By addressing energy demand in this way, we can also alleviate the financial burden on consumers, helping to mitigate rising costs and reduce dissatisfaction among users19. Therefore, the integration of these advanced strategies is crucial for ensuring that energy supply remains stable and accessible, even in the most challenging environments19–21.

Contributions

This study presents energy scheduling for an intelligent off-electrical grid (IOEG) with employing a hybrid management for consumption and HS utilization through a three-layer multi-criteria energy optimization at day-ahead. A hybrid management approach for demand-side consumption is proposed in the upper and middle layers. The upper layer is designed with an offer price strategy for LC. Meanwhile, the middle layer introduces a LS strategy that focuses on the optimal consumption rate at day-ahead. In the lower layer, the objectives of maximizing consumer satisfaction, enhancing voltage profiles, and minimizing power production costs are optimized through multi-criteria optimization, taking into account the managed consumption from the upper and middle layers. Furthermore, a HS is utilized for CPG in the lower layer. To address decision-making and multi-criteria optimization in the lower layer, the Shannon entropy and an improved epsilon-constraint method are applied. Methodology

The optimization of the proposed approach in this study is carried out by GAMS software. The GAMS software is used to optimization of the up and middle layers with one criteria modeling. On the other hand, optimization of the low layer with multi-criteria such as consumer satisfaction, voltage index and power production costs are done by improved epsilon-constraint method in GAMS software. When optimizing multi-criteria, a diverse set of solutions emerges, referred to as non-dominated solutions or the Pareto front. This process considers both equality and inequality constraints that may be present in the problem. On the other hand, because the solutions generated often conflict with one another in terms of the various multi-criteria, it becomes essential for the decision maker to assess these solutions carefully. This evaluation must take into account the relative importance of each criteria, as the trade-offs between them can significantly influence the final decision. Conclusion and next studies

This paper studied a three-layer multi-criteria optimization strategy for day-ahead power scheduling in an IOEG, which involves consumer participation in load demand optimization and HS. In this model, consumers are represented in the upper and middle layers through a hybrid management of load demand, utilizing LC and LS methods, respectively. The LC is executed by the CLC, which considers the offer price to manage load demand clipping, while LS is carried out by the CLS, focusing on shifting load demand based on the optimal load rate for the day ahead. The lower layer is structured as a multi-criteria optimization aimed at maximizing consumer satisfaction, improving the voltage index within the IOEG, and minimizing power production costs. Alongside the load demand optimization performed by CLC and CLS in the upper and middle layers, the CHS contributes in the lower layer through the CPG approach to fulfill self-load requirements. The three-layer multi-criteria optimization method is implemented using GAMS software. Conversely, the low layer or multi-criteria optimization is addressed through Shannon entropy and enhanced epsilon-constraint techniques. The developed model is applied to the 33-node test system, considering three case studies to analyze the impact of the LC, LS, and CPG strategies on IOEG performance. The outcomes of the cases are as follows:

Case (A) This case is presented as a fundamental case for comparison with other instances. In Case A, CLC, CLS, and CHS did not engage in power optimization. The consumer satisfaction, voltage index, and power production cost are recorded at 87.6%, 0.86 p.u., and $2866.6, respectively.

Case (B) In Case B, the LC and LS approaches are executed by CLC and CLS. The metrics for consumer satisfaction, voltage index, and power production cost stand at 95.3%, 0.91 p.u., and $2633.8, respectively. Here, consumer satisfaction has increased by 7.7%, the voltage index has improved by 5.5%, and the power production cost has decreased by 8.12% when compared to Case A.

Case (C) The CLC, CLS, and CHS have participated in Case C. The consumer satisfaction rate stands at 96.8%, the voltage index is recorded at 0.93 p.u., and the cost of power production amounts to $2603.6. Compared to Case A, consumer satisfaction has risen by 9.2%, while the voltage index has improved by 7.52%. Furthermore, there is a 9.18% decrease in power production costs relative to Case A.

The findings from the analysis of various case studies are crucial for developing new cooperative strategies that address the complex needs of all stakeholders in the IOEG. These strategies aim to align participants’ goals while fostering collaboration, enhancing the sustainability of the IOEG. By adopting a synergistic approach, the effectiveness of parties improves, contributing to a more resilient framework for technical, social, and economic objectives. Consequently, these innovative strategies benefit individual stakeholders and promote collective essential.

The next studies regarding the proposed methodology and approaches in this paper can be continued with the new optimization models such as machine learning methods, evolutionary algorithms in new energy grids like smart buildings and multi-carries energy systems to implementing approaches.
