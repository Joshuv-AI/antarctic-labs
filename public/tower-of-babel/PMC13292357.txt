Dandelion exosome-like nanovesicles alleviate sepsis-induced acute kidney injury via SLC7A11-regulated ferroptosis
Authors: Pan Zhang, Fanfan Cao, Limin Xu, Xinghui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSepsis is a systemic inflammatory response syndrome triggered by infection, resulting in organ dysfunction. Sepsis-induced acute kidney injury (S-AKI) is one of the most prevalent potential complications. Accumulating studies found that ferroptosis plays an important role in S-AKI. However, the specific regulatory mechanism is not clear. In the present study, we isolated plant-derived exosome-like nanovesicles (PELNs, named PGY-Exo herein) from Dandelion (Taraxacum Genus) and established an in vivo S-AKI model. Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) to mimic an in vitro septic injury model for evaluating the therapeutic effects of PGY-Exo. Proteomics was used to reveal the molecular mechanism by which PGY-Exo ameliorate AKI. In this study, PKH26-labeled PGY-Exo were confirmed to be taken up by HUVECs and renal tissues. PGY-Exo treatment alleviated S-AKI by suppressing the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and multiple inflammatory factors, including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Proteomic screening found that solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) was the key downstream target of PGY-Exo. Downregulation of SLC7A11 abrogated the protective effects of PGY-Exo against LPS-induced HUVEC injury and dysfunction. Taken together, this study found that treatment with dandelion-derived exosome-like nanovesicles alleviated sepsis-induced ferroptosis and renal injury by upregulating SLC7A11 expression.Keywords: Dandelion, exosome-like nanovesicles, sepsis, renal injury, SLC7A11, ferroptosis

Introduction

Sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (S-AKI) is a common life-threatening complication in hospitalized and critically ill patients [1–3]. S-AKI increases the in-hospital mortality rate by 6 to 8 times and the risk of developing chronic kidney disease (CKD) by 3 times [4]. Furthermore, up to a quarter of patients with S-AKI will require renal replacement therapy (RRT) [5]. These molecular events drive the inflammatory cascade during the process of sepsis, eventually leading to excessive inflammation-induced apoptosis and aggravated renal injury [6,7]. A wealth of evidence supports the mitigation of SAKI through the suppression of inflammation and apoptosis [8–10].

Previous studies have confirmed that adipose-derived MSCs exosomes can improved kidney function [11]. In mice of sepsis-induce AKI, the intervention of AMSCs derived exosomes played a renal protective effect. The mechanism may be through SIRT1 signaling pathway [12]. Considering the source of stem cells, plant-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (PELNs) are widely used in disease treatment research [13]. Extracellular vesicles are subcellular structure vesicles wrapped by a phospholipid bilayer membrane, containing various carriers, including miRNA, mRNA, DNA, proteins, etc. Both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells release extracellular vesicles. Extracellular vesicles are classified into two major categories: exosomes (size: 100–500 nanometers) and microvesicles (size: 30–150 nanometers) [14]. PELN is an extracellular vesicle derived from plants, with a size ranging from 50 to 500 nm [15]. In addition to animal vesicles, peln also contains complex small RNAs, proteins, lipids and other metabolites. Nuts from various plants and fruits, such as ginger, blueberries and coconuts, all show anti-inflammatory properties [16–18].

In this study, we constructed sepsis induced renal injury mouse model and found that dandelion exosome-like nanovesicles treatment alleviates sepsis induced renal injury by regulation SLC7A11 mediated ferroptosis.

Materials and methods

Animal study

C57BL/6 mice were housed in controlled conditions (22 °C ± 2 °C, 60% ± 5% relative humidity). All experimental procedures were carried out following ethical animal research guidelines with the approval of Gongli Hospital of Shanghai Pudong New Area (2024-SZR-16-340406198707142817).

The cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) procedure was performed as described in a previous protocol [19]. 4 h post CLP surgery, the mice were intravenously injected with PBS and exosomes (2 mg/kg body weight). All mice were sacrificed by using the CO2 asphyxia method after the experiment (The air displacement rate of CO2 was 20% of the container volume per minute). The dose was based on prior data [20].

Proteomic analysis

Total protein (250 μg) from renal tissue was extracted, reduced with 10 mM DTT at 37 °C for 1 h and alkylated with 50 mM IAA at 25 °C in the dark for 1 h. Then 1.5 mL of prechilled (–20 °C) 100% acetone was added. The samples were cleared through centrifugation at 14,000 g three times for 15 min. Samples were then diluted with 500 μL NH4HCO3. Peptides were desalted with SPE C18 cartridges (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and lyophilized under vacuum before nano-HPLC-MS/MS analyses. Three biological replicates were analyzed to evaluate the reliability of protein identification, reproducibility and accuracy.

Isolation and identification of PGY-exo

In order to isolate PGY-Exo, 10–15 g of Dandelion was harvested from Shanghai, China. Dandelion was cleaned to eliminate all contaminants. After cleaning, PGY was ground in 30 mL of 1× phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) using a high-speed blender. PGY juice was then filtered and squeezed through a sieve. The collected juice was centrifuged at 2000 g for 20 min and the supernatant was transferred, centrifuged, and filtered using a 0.2-μm-pore filter (Acrodisc® Syringe Filters with Supor® Membrane, PALL, USA). The filtered juice was placed on the top of the sucrose cushion layers (27, 68% of sucrose) and centrifuged at 100000 g (Hitachi Ultracentrifuge CP100NX, Himac, Japan). The ultracentrifugation procedures were performed at 4 °C. Exo concentration was expressed as protein concentration using the BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and determined using nanoparticle tracking analysis (Malvern Instrument, UK). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was utilized to evaluate PGY-Exo.

Cell culture

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) from ScienCell were cultivated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle medium (HyClone, UT, USA) and 10% fetal bovine serum with no Exos (Gibco, CA, USA). The cells were maintained in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For sepsis induction, cells were treated with 10 ng/mL LPS.

Apoptosis detection using flow cytometry

Cell apoptosis analysis using the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection kit (BD Biosciences) was measured by flow cytometry according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After stimulation, 1.0 × 106 HUVECs cells were washed with cold PBS. Then, Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) staining were performed in the dark at room temperature for 15 min. Apoptotic cells were detected by flow cytometry.

ELISA for soluble inflammatory cytokines

The expression of inflammatory factors IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α in the cell supernatant or serum was determined using a commercially available ELISA kit (Sen-Xiong Company). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, supernatant was stored at −80 °C prior to measurement, in triplicate for both the standard and sample. The optical density (OD)450 was calculated by subtracting the background and drawing a standard curve.

Tubule formation assay

In vitro neovascularization was evaluated in human fibrin matrices. Following treatment, serum-starved HUVECs in endothelial basal medium were seeded onto plates coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) and incubated. Tubular structures were photographed using phase-contrast microscopy. The lengths of newly formed tubes in 10 randomly selected fields were measured.

Western blot (WB) analysis

Cells were lysed using cell lysis solution (RIPA). Protein concentration was determined using BCA (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). After transferring protein samples to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes and blocking in TBST, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against SLC7A11, GPX4, GSH, and GAPDH (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA). After washing (3× for 10 min) with TBST, the samples were incubated with secondary antibodies. The data were analyzed using ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Immunohistochemistry assays

Tissue samples were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution and embedded in paraffin. TUNEL staining assays were conducted for apoptosis analyses. The sections were examined using an Axiophot light microscope (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany) and photographed with a digital camera.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) activity

Intracellular ROS generation in HUVECs cells or renal tissue were captured using a dihydroethidium assay (Beyotime, China). Fluorescence was measured at 488 nm excitation and 525 nm emission using a fluorescence microplate reader (PerkinElmer, USA).

Statistical analysis

All data analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (CA, USA). Overall survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests. Two-tailed Student’s t tests were used to explore the significance between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance with the post hoc Bonferroni test was used for ≥ 3 groups. Correlations were determined using the Pearson correlation test. Data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. A p value of < 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Results

Isolation and characteristics of dandelion exosomes (PGY-exo)

In this study, dandelion exosomes (PGY-Exo) were successfully isolated utilize the ultra-high-speed centrifugation method. Transmission electron microscopy and particle size analysis found that PGY-Exo were about 190 nm (Figure 1A-C). Immunofluorescence detection shows that PKH26 labeled PGY-Exo can be intake by HUVECs (Figure 1D). In vivo imaging detection also shows that PKH26-PGY-Exo can enriched in nephridial tissue in time dependent way (Figure 1E).

(E) In vivo imaging shows the kidney aggregation of PKH67-labeled PGY-Exo at different times after tail vein injection. N = 6.

PGY-exo treatment alleviates sepsis-induced renal injury

ELISA for inflammatory factor expression detection show that PGY-Exo treatment alleviates sepsis-induced TNF-1α, IL-1β and IL-6 expression (Figure 2A-C). Immunohistochemical with TUNEL staining shows that PGY-Exo treatment alleviates sepsis-induced apoptosis in renal tissue (Figure 2D and 2E). Immunofluorescence detection for ROS deposition also shows that PGY-Exo treatment alleviates sepsis-induced ROS deposition in renal tissue (Figure 2F and 2G).

PGY-exo treatment inhibit sepsis-induced ferroptosis

In order to reveal the regulatory mechanism, proteomics was used to detect the protein expression in renal tissue. KEGG analysis (Figure 3A) and heat map (Figure 3B) show that ferroptosis plays an important role in sepsis-induced renal injury. SLC7A11 was the protein with the most significant difference (Figure 3C). Western blot detection shows that SLC7A11, GPX4 and GSH expression were decreased in the renal tissue of sepsis mice. PGY-Exo treatment restored and even promoted SLC7A11, GPX4 and GSH expression in sepsis mice (Figure 3D-3G).

Immunofluorescence for ROS deposition detection shows that LPS treatment can increase ROS deposition in HUVECs, PGY-Exo treatment decreased LPS induced ROS deposition. Downregulation SLC7A11 reversed the inhibitory effect of PGY-Exo to LPS induced ROS deposition in HUVECs (Figure 4A-4B). Flow cytometry detection shows that LPS treatment can increase HUVECs apoptosis in HUVECs, and PGY-Exo treatment decreased LPS induced HUVECs apoptosis. Downregulation of SLC7A11 reversed the inhibitory effect of PGY-Exo to LPS induced HUVECs apoptosis in HUVECs (Figure 4E-4F).

Discussion

Accumulation research confirmed that plant exosome nanovesicles (PENs) play an important role in microenvironment and disease regulation [18,21–23]. Dandelion (Taraxacum genus) is a perennial herb belonging to the Asteraceae family. As a well-known and extensively studied genus, dandelion comprises numerous species. Some species have been widely used in both complementary and alternative medicine to clear heat, detoxify, activate blood circulation, dispel stasis, and discharge urine [24,25]. Previous studies have confirmed that Dandelions play an important role in the treatment of inflammatory diseases [26]. The findings suggest that Dandelion extract reduces the LPS-induced inflammatory response in RAW264.7 cells by regulating polarization and apoptosis [26]. In this study, we found that plant exosome nanovesicles (PENs) from Dandelion (PGY-Exo) can improve renal injury induced by sepsis.

In this study, we found that PGY-Exo treatment inhibits inflammatory factors expression and ROS accumulation. Proteomic testing confirmed that PGY-Exo treatment restored SLC7A11 and inhibited ferroptosis. These results indicate that LPS might induce ferroptosis in renal tubular and inhibition of ferroptosis could protect mice from Septic nephropathy. Loss of activity of the lipid repair enzyme GPX4 and subsequent accumulation of lipid-based ROS, particularly lipid hydroperoxides, always drove Ferroptosis [27,28]. And we observed that LPS could induce Fe2+ and lipid peroxidation accumulation, which can regulate ferroptosis-mediated cell death [29]. It reduced the expression of GPX4 and xCT both in murine renal tissue [30].

In this study, we found that PGY-Exo treatment can increase SLC7A11 (also commonly known as xCT) expression. SLC7A11 is a known ferroptosis-related factor. In order to clarify the specific mechanism between SLC7A11 and ferroptosis-related acute injury diseases. HUVECs were used for the in vivo experiment. The results show that LPS treatment can induce ferroptosis and inhibit angiogenic differentiation function. PGY-Exo treatment promotes SLC7A11 and inhibits LPS-induced ferroptosis of HUVECs. SLC7A11 silence reversed the protective effect of PGY-Exo to LPS induced HUVECs injury.

Taken together, this study found that Dandelion exosome-like nanovesicles treatment alleviates sepsis induced renal injury by regulating SLC7A11 and inhibiting renal tissue ferroptosis. Suggestion that PGY-Exo may become a new plant medicine for kidney damage (Figure 6). However, it is still necessary for further in-depth research to determine which specific component or metabolite in the plant exosomes plays the main regulatory role, and how exactly this regulation occurs on SLC7A11.
