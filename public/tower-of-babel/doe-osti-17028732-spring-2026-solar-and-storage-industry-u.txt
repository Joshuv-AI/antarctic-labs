Changes in compensation policy are expected to reduce China’s solar deployment in 2026.
• Developers raced to complete projects in the first half of 2025, before the country switched new wind and solar
  capacity from fixed to market-based compensation in June 2025.
• Solar generation exceeded wind generation for the first time in 2025, accounting for 11% of total generation.
• PV investment slowed in the second half of 2025 and in early 2026, as many developers paused to wait for new
  provincial policies and to formulate investment strategies under the new compensation rules.
• BNEF projects 16% lower deployment in 2026; the China Photovoltaic Industry Association projects a 24%–43% drop.
China emphasized system integration rather than solar targets in its most recent 5-year plan (covering 2026–2030).
• The plan set no targets for solar installation by 2030, instead setting targets for technologies including offshore wind,
  nuclear, pumped hydro, and zero-carbon industrial parks.
• Years of rapid solar expansion have increased curtailment and reduced developer returns, prompting a focus on
  system integration through strategies such as grid expansion, enhanced flexibility, and energy storage.
• Still, the plan prescribes clean power additions to meet electricity demand growth (e.g., from data centers).
China eliminated export tax rebates for PV products on April 1, which may raise prices paid by importing countries.
• The change is expected to add 9% to Chinese module costs.
• China’s PV exports reached a record 68 GWdc (including wafers, cells, and modules) in March owing to the impending
  rebate change plus other factors such as higher electricity prices in many places.

 CSP Update—Ivanpah

• In 2025, PG&E and other energy offtakers of the Ivanpah CSP direct steam power towers,
  sought to end their PPAs 13 years early, citing lower-than-expected performance and pricing
  out-of-line with current market conditions.
• Its operational challenges were attributed to frequent cycling of the thermal power cycle
  caused by its lack of storage and the prevalence of lower-priced electricity from solar PV during
  periods of production.
• In December 2025, the CPUC rejected the proposal, citing the need for clean energy, rising
  electricity demand, and the loss of value in transmission investments.
• A new journal article proposes solving the dispute by replacing the steam-based receivers with
  molten salt receivers and connecting them to a molten salt thermal energy storage system—
  standard now for new CSP towers.
 • Most of the recent experience and supply chain in molten salt storage is currently in
 China. In addition to the dozens of 100-MW Chinese CSP plants installed with storage
 over the past few years, China is currently constructing a 2-GWht molten salt thermal
       energy storage system.                                                   National Laboratory of the Rockies | 12

 Thermal Storage Update

• At the end of 2025, Electrified Thermal Solutions commissioned its first commercial-scale
  thermal battery at Southwest Research Institute. The 20-MWh system charges with electricity
  from the grid, with a peak heat of 1,800°C.
• In May, U.S.-headquartered company Antora announced it was commissioning a 5-GWh,
  multiday thermal energy storage system at a biofuels production plant in South Dakota. The
  batteries store heat energy in blocks of solid carbon at temperatures up to 2,400°C.
• Solar Thermal World reported the commissioning of several medium-temperature solar
  collector systems in 2025 for industrial heat purposes. A Heineken brewery in Greece, for
  example, is currently constructing a 7.3-MW parabolic trough plant. In an oilfield wastewater
  treatment plant in New Mexico, a 100-kW linear Fresnel plant was constructed in 2025 to
  desalinate 70 barrels of water a day.
