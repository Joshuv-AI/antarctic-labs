Explicit constructions of anti-automorphisms of cyclic and generalized cyclic algebras
Susanne Pumplün

Abstract
. While involutions on central simple algebras have been studied extensively and are well understood, much less is known about general anti-automorphisms. We present norm criteria for the existence of anti-automorphisms as well as explicit constructions of anti-automorphisms, on cyclic and generalized cyclic algebras. Our approach describes anti-automorphisms as polynomial maps and unifies existing approaches. It recovers classical criteria for the existence of involutions as special cases. We obtain norm conditions for the existence of anti-automorphisms of the second kind and of infinite order on the ring of twisted Laurent series K ⁡ ( ( , , , ) ) K((t;\sigma)) over a field K K and the ring of twisted Laurent series D ⁡ ( ( , , , ) ) D((t;\sigma)) over a division algebra D D that is finite-dimensional over its center. Our construction produces all possible anti-automorphisms of proper nonassociative cyclic or generalized cyclic algebras, i.e. for special classes of Petit algebras which can be viewed as canonical generalizations of associative cyclic and generalized cyclic central simple algebras.

1.
Introduction

The existence and classification of involutions is a classical question in the theory of central simple algebras. Involutions on central simple algebras either preserve the center (involutions of the first kind) or act on the center nontrivially (involutions of the second kind). Their study involves for instance Brauer groups, and quadratic and hermitian forms
[
KMRT1998
]
.

It is well-known that a central simple algebra over
F
admits an involution of the second kind which leaves a subfield
F_{0}
of
F
fixed, if and only if its Brauer class lies in the kernel of the corestriction map

{\rm Cor}_{F/F_{0}}:\mathrm{Br}(F)\to\mathrm{Br}(F_{0}).
For cyclic algebras
(K/F,\sigma,a)
in particular, we thus know that

{\rm Cor}_{F/F_{0}}([(K/F,\sigma,a)])=[(K/F,\sigma,a\tau(a))],
where
\tau
generates the automorphism group of
F/F_{0}
,
and consequently,
(K/F,\sigma,a)
admits an involution of the second kind, if and only if

a\tau(a)\in N_{K/F}(K^{\times}).
We call such a condition a
norm condition
.

While the existence of involutions (anti-automorphisms of order two) on central simple algebras is well understood, much less is known about general anti-automorphisms of central simple algebras, see
[
Lew2006
,
Lew2003
,
LewTig1999
]
for some results. Explicit examples of central simple division algebras
over algebraic number fields
F
which have anti-automorphisms of the second kind, but no involution can be found in
[
MorST2005
]
.

We will distinguish between anti-automorphisms of a central simple algebra
A
over a field
F
which are
F
-linear (of the first kind), and
F_{0}
-linear (of the second kind), where
F_{0}
is a proper subfield of
F
. More precisely, we will investigate anti-automorphisms of the second kind that are
\tau
-semilinear for some
\tau\in\Aut(F)
with fixed field
F_{0}=F^{\tau}
. Unlike in the case of involutions, for anti-automorphisms of the second kind the extension
F/F_{0}
will generally not be quadratic étale. Also note that our definition of anti-automorphisms of the second kind differs from the one presented in
[
Lew2003
]
.

We will focus on anti-automorphisms both of cyclic algebras and of generalized cyclic algebras as defined in
[
Jac1996
]
(note also the slightly different approach described in
[
Tignol1987
]
). Generalized cyclic algebras do not need to be crossed products, e.g. see
[
Hanke2001
,
Hanke2004
,
Hanke2005
]
. However, every solvable crossed product is isomorphic to a generalized cyclic algebra by a classical result of Albert
[
Albert1939
, p. 182-187]
.

Our approach is based on describing cyclic algebras as quotient rings
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{n}-a)
, respectively generalized cyclic algebras as quotient rings
D[t;\sigma]/D[t;\sigma](t^{m}-d)
with
D
a central simple algebra, employing skew polynomials. We show that all anti-automorphisms of a cyclic algebra
(K/F,\sigma,a)
that restrict to some anti-automorphism on
K
, and under some mild conditions also all anti-automorphisms of a generalized cyclic division algebra
(D,\sigma,d)
that restrict to some anti-automorphism on
D
, are canonically induced by anti-automorphisms of the skew polynomial rings
K[t;\sigma]
, respectively
D[t;\sigma]
. They map
t
to
\alpha t^{k}
for some suitable positive integer
k
and
\alpha\in F^{\times}
(respectively in
C^{\times}
). We call an anti-automorphisms (resp., an isomorphism) mapping
t
to
\alpha t^{k}
a
monomial anti-automorphism (resp., isomorphism) of degree

k
.

In order to describe the anti-automorphisms we obtain new observations also on isomorphisms between cyclic and between nonassociative cyclic algebras. Monomial isomorphism of degree
k>1
that extend
\tau
can only exist between associative cyclic algebras (Theorem
4.2
), and all isomorphism between proper nonassociative cyclic algebras
that extend
\tau
will be monomial of degree one and require that
\sigma
and
\tau
commute.

More precisely, suppose that
\tau\in\Aut(K)
and
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
.
Let
\alpha\in K^{\times}
. Then there exists a ring isomorphism extending
\tau
that maps
t
to
\alpha t^{k}
for
k>1

if and only if
(K/F,\sigma,a)
is associative and

N_{K/F}(\alpha)b^{k}=\tau(a)

(Corollary
4.4
).
Monomial anti-automorphisms of degree
\ell>1
greater than one that extend
\tau
do not exist on cyclic algebras when

\tau\in\Aut(K)
commutes with
\sigma
(Theorem
3.2
).

For proper nonassociative cyclic algebras
(K/F,\sigma,a)
, only monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one exist that extend
\tau
, assuming there is some
\alpha\in K^{\times}
such that

N_{K/F}(\alpha)=\tau(a)a
(Theorem
3.2
(ii)), while when
(K/F,\sigma,a)
is associative, other
k>0
with
\gcd(k,n)=1
are possible, whenever there exist
\tau\in\Aut(K)
, such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
and

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}

for some integer
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
.
If additionally

N_{K/F}(\alpha)=\tau(a)a^{k}
then there exists an anti-automorphism extending
\tau
that is monomial of degree
k>2

(Theorem
4.8
).

The situation for generalized cyclic algebras is similar, with some small modifications (e.g., Theorem
6.5
, Theorem
7.5
). Most notably, when
\sigma
has order
m
, monomial anti-automorphisms of degree greater than one can only exist on associative generalized cyclic algebras, and all proper nonassociative algebras
(D,\sigma,d)
only admit monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one. The conditions for the existence of anti-automorphisms again involve a norm condition (Theorem
7.5
).

This paper grew out of our attempt to understand what turned out to be an error in the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
. This proposition displays an elegant example of a cyclic division algebra over a number field, that admits no involution but admits an anti-automorphism of the second kind and lays the groundwork for the main result
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
which contains yet another elegant construction of such a scenario, this time for a twisted Laurent series ring
K((t;\sigma))
. Both proofs explicitly write down some anti-automorphisms of the second kind, which however, are not well-defined. The proofs for both results are repaired in Section
5
and show that
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
indeed is correct (with a different proof) (stated in Theorem
5.5
) and that
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
has to be slightly adjusted (Proposition
5.1
). Indeed, we give criteria for the existence of anti-automorphisms of infinite order on the twisted ring of Laurent polynomials
K((t;\sigma))
; itself a central simple algebra over
F((t^{n}))
(Theorem
5.2
), and for the existence of anti-automorphisms of infinite order on the twisted ring of Laurent polynomials
D((t;\sigma))
for a division algebra
D
(Theorem
7.10
).

We also correct the norm criteria presented in the Special Case 1 and the General Case in
[
Hanke2007
, Section 3]
(Remark
7.8
).

Our approach can be easily generalized to anti-automorphisms of Azumaya algebras which is briefly discussed in Section
8
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Nonassociative rings and algebras

Let
R
be a unital commutative ring and let
A
be an
R
-module.
We call
A
a
(nonassociative) algebra
over
R
if there exists an
R
-bilinear
map
A\times A\mapsto A
,
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
, the
multiplication
of
A
. We will just denote this multiplication by juxtaposition. We will only consider
unital
algebras, which possess an element denoted by
1, such that
1x=x1=x
for all
x\in A
.

The associativity in
A
is measured by the
left nucleus
defined as
{\rm Nuc}_{l}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[x,A,A]=0\}
, the
middle nucleus

{\rm Nuc}_{m}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[A,x,A]=0\}
and the
right nucleus

{\rm Nuc}_{r}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[A,A,x]=0\}
, where
[x,y,z]=(xy)z-x(yz)
.
Their intersection

{\rm Nuc}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[x,A,A]=[A,x,A]=[A,A,x]=0\}
is the
nucleus
of
A
.
The
commuter
of
A
is defined as
{\rm Comm}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,xy=yx\text{ for all }y\in A\}
and the
center
of a nonassociative algebra
A
is defined as
{\rm C}(A)=\text{Nuc}(A)\cap{\rm Comm}(A)
.

Every unital nonassociative algebra is a unital nonassociative ring
(A,+,\cdot)
with the distributivity laws satisfied by definition of its multiplication. Conversely, every nonassociative ring can be viewed as a nonassociative algebra over its center, or any subring of it.

A nonassociative ring (or algebra)
A
is called a
proper
nonassociative ring (or algebra) if it is not associative.

Write
A^{op}=\{x^{op}\,|\,x\in A\}
for the opposite algebra of
A
. We recall that
A^{op}
has the same addition as
A
and multiplication given by

x^{op}\circ y^{op}=(yx)^{op}
.

Let
R=D[t;\sigma]
be the ring of skew polynomials of a central simple algebra
D
over some field. We will look at the algebras
D[t;\sigma]/D[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
. These algebras are well-known associative quotient algebras when
t^{m}-a
generates a two-sided ideal, and are the lesser known proper nonassocative Petit algebras otherwise. Their multiplicative structure is given by

g\circ h=gh\,\,{\rm mod}_{r}(t^{m}-a)

for every
g
,
h
of degree less than
m
,
where the right-hand side denotes the remainder of
gh
after right division with
t^{m}-a

[
Petit1966
]
.

2.2.
Anti-automorphisms

Let
A
be an
R
-algebra or a ring. An
anti-homomorphism
on
A
is an additive map
F:A\to A
such that

f(xy)=f(y)f(x)
for all
x,y\in
. If
f
is bijective it is called an
anti-automorphism
on
A
. The restriction of an anti-automorphism
f:A\to A
to
R
is an automorphism
\tau=f|_{R}\in\Aut(R)
. An anti-automorphism
f
is called
of the first kind
if it is
F
-linear, and
of the second kind
otherwise. (This deviates from the definition in

[
Lew2006
, p. 254]
, where anti-automorphisms of the second kind are defined to have order two when restricted to
R
.)
Put
R_{0}={\rm Fix}(\tau)=R^{\tau}
, then
R_{0}
is a subring of
R
.
If
f|_{R}
is a non-trivial
\tau\in\Aut(R)
we call
f
a
\tau
-semilinear
anti-automorphism, if we want to make clear which
\tau
is involved. A
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism is an
R_{0}
-linear anti-automorphism.

We note that odd powers of an anti-automorphism
f
are anti-automorphisms and even powers are isomorphisms.

When
A
is a central simple (associative) algebra over a field
F
and
f
is an involution of the second kind, we know that
F/F_{0}
is always a quadratic étale field extension with Galois group generated by
\tau
. When
F/F_{0}
is a Galois field extension and
D
a central division

F
-algebra such that
D\not\cong D^{op}
we also know that if every element of order two in
{\rm Gal}(F/F_{0})
extends to
an automorphism of
D
, then
D
has no involution which restricts to the identity on

F_{0}
. In particular, if
F_{0}
has no nontrivial automorphism (e.g.
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
), then
D
has
no involution at all
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.2.]
.

By
[
MorST2005
, Lemma 3.1]
, if
D
is a noncommutative division ring with an anti-automorphisms
f
then
f^{\ell}=id
implies that
\ell
must be even and if
D
has no involution it must be a power of 4.

By a well-known Theorem of Albert, every finite dimensional central division algebra
D
with
an
F
-linear anti-automorphism has an involution
[
Lew2006
, Theorem 7]
. Albert proved that a central simple
F
-algebra
A
admits an involution of the first kind if and only if it admits an anti-automorphism of the first kind, and that this in turn is equivalent to
A
and
A^{op}
being isomorphic
F
-algebras.

The composite of two anti-automorphism
f,g:A\to A
is an automorphism of
A
, hence there exists some
u\in A^{\times}
such that

f\circ g(x)=uxu^{-1}
which implies that
f
and
g
differ by an inner automorphism whenever
A
is a central simple algebra over a field
F
. When
A
is a central simple algebra over
F
and both
f
and
g
are
F
-linear involutions, then
u
satisfies
f(u)=\pm u
, whereas if both are involutions of the second type we may chose
u\in A
such that
f(u)=u
.

For an associative cyclic
R
-algebra
A
, we
write
A^{\tau}=\{x^{\tau}\,|\,x\in A\}
for the conjugate algebra

A^{\tau}
, for
\tau\in\Aut{R}
. We recall that
A^{\tau}
has the same addition and multiplication as
A
and scalar multiplication given by twisting the scalar multiplication via
ax^{\tau}=(\tau^{-1}(a)x)^{\tau}
for all
a\in R
.

It is well-known that a map
\widetilde{f}:A\to A
is a ring anti-automorphism of an associative
F
-algebra which restricts to some
\tau\in\Aut(F)
, if and only if the map
f:A^{\tau}\to A^{op}

given by
f(x^{\tau})=\widetilde{f}(x)^{op}
is an isomorphism of
F
-algebras
[
MorST2005
, p. 2]
.
This holds for algebras defined over rings a well, but we will not use this point of view.

We will take a slightly different approach. Given some
\tau\in\Aut_{R_{0}}(R)
, there is an obvious one-one correspondence between
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphisms

\widetilde{f}:A\to A
and
\tau
-linear ring isomorphisms
f:A\to A^{op}
(which can be also viewed as algebra isomorphisms between
R_{0}
-algebras) defined via
f(x)=\widetilde{f}(x)^{op}
.

2.3.
Cyclic algebras

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
with
\Gal(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Our approach will be based on viewing cyclic algebras as quotient algebras of skew polynomials. Let
(K/F,\sigma,a)
be an associative cyclic algebra over
F
, then

(K/F,\sigma,a)=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{n}-a)
, where
t^{n}-a\in K[t;\sigma]
is a skew polynomial that is two-sided, that is generates a two-sided ideal we factor out.

More generally, we can define a
nonassociative cyclic algebra

(K/F,\sigma,a)=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{n}-a)

of degree

n
as a special type of Petit algebra, for all
a\in K^{\times}

[
Petit1966
]
. This algebra is not associative if and only if
a\in K\setminus F
(e.g.
[
Steele2014
]
, where the opposite algebras of cyclic algebras are studied extensively, or
[
Pum2021
]
), and the usual classical simple cyclic algebra otherwise. We will treat both cases simultaneously where possible, referring to “cyclic algebras” when doing so in the following.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
K/F
be a Galois field extension of degree
n
with automorphism group generated by
\sigma
and suppose that
\tau\in\Aut(K)
commute with
\sigma
. By
[
Pum2025
, Theorem 4.4]
, the map

G_{\tau,\alpha,1}:K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)\to K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-b)

defined via

G_{\tau,\alpha,1}(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(d_{i})(\alpha t)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau(d_{i})N_{i}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{i},

is a ring isomorphism between the (potentially nonassociative rings)
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
and
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-b)
if and only if

G_{id,\alpha}:K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)^{\tau}\to K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-b)

is a ring isomorphism.
This corresponds to the point of view employing conjugate algebras in
[
MorST2005
, p. 2]
. Note that here
(K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a))^{\tau}=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-\tau(a))
.

Let
(K/F,\sigma,a)=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{n}-a)
be a cyclic algebra of degree
n
over
F
.
Define

{\rm Aut}(K)_{\sigma}=\{\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K)\mid\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(i) The elements of the group
{\rm Aut}(K)_{\sigma}
are
automorphisms of
K
that preserve the subfield
F
(that is, that restrict to automorphisms of
F
).

(ii) For every automorphism
\tau
of
K
that restricts to an automorphism of
F
there exists a positive integer
k
,
\gcd(k,n)=1
, such that
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
\Aut(K)_{\sigma}
is the centralizer subgroup
{\rm Cent}_{\Aut(K)}(\sigma)
, it is a group. As
\sigma
generates
\Gal(K/F)
, it follows that for any

, we have

if and only if
\sigma(a)=a
. Now suppose
\tau\in\Aut(K)_{\sigma}
. Then for any

, we have

\tau(a)=\tau(\sigma(a))=\sigma(\tau(a)),

and therefore

if and only if
\tau(a)\in F
, so

is preserved by

.

(ii)
Suppose
\tau\in\Aut(K)

\tau(\sigma(\tau^{-1}(a)))=a
for all

, hence
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}\in\Gal(K/F),
so there exists an integer
k
with
\gcd(k,n)=1
, such that

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
.
∎

For any

,
\tau\in\Aut(K)
and

N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)=\tau^{i-1}(\beta)\cdots\tau(\beta)\beta.

Note that

and thus

(2.1)

N_{i+j}^{\tau}(\beta)=\tau^{i}(N_{j}^{\tau}(\beta))\cdot N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)

for all

. Since

is a Galois field extension with cyclic Galois group generated by

,
N_{n}^{\sigma}=N_{K/F}
.

Let
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K)
and let
F_{0}=F^{\tau}
be the fixed field of
\tau|_{F}
.
By abuse of notation, for
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K)
we denote
\tau|_{F}
by
\tau
as well.

3.
Monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one on associative cyclic algebras

In order to classify anti-automorphisms of a cyclic algebra
(K/F,\sigma,a)
of degree
n
, we will use the ring isomorphisms between

(K/F,\sigma,a)
and its opposite algebra
(K/F,\sigma,a)^{op}=(K/F,\sigma,a^{-1})
.

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
with
\Gal(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. We will study the anti-automorphisms of the Petit algebras

In this section we focus on the case that

.

such that

. In other words, every other anti-automorphism can be obtained from one that fixes
K
, e.g. from
\widetilde{G}_{\tilde{\tau},\alpha,k},
by applying an inner automorphism
a\mapsto uau^{-1}
. This inner automorphism will map
K
to some field
K^{\prime}
via
a\mapsto u\tau(a)au^{-1}
and
t
to
\beta t^{k}
with
\beta=u\alpha\sigma^{-k}(u)
, so will be again of the type
\widetilde{G}_{\tilde{\gamma},\beta,k}
with
\tilde{\gamma}(a)=u\tau(a)u^{-1}
.

Alternatively, the computations up to now, both for isomorphisms and anti-automorphisms, hold analogously when we only assume that
\tau:K\to K^{\prime}
is a field automorphism and use
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
as generators for the respective Galois groups of
K/F
and
K^{\prime}/F
.

5.
Examples of anti-automorphisms of the second kind and of infinite order on algebras without involutions

In this section we will repair two of the proofs given in
[
MorST2005
]
and in the process also look at anti-automorphisms of the twisted Laurent series ring
K((t;\sigma))
.

5.1.
Anti-automorphisms of infinite order on cyclic algebras

Let
m>0
,
n>2
, and let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
with Galois group
\Gal(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Let
G_{0}
be the metacyclic group of order
mn
generated by
\sigma
and
\tau\in\Aut(K)

with
\sigma^{n}=id
,
\tau^{4m}=id
and
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma
. Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension of degree
n
with Galois group
G_{0}
.
So here
\Gal(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
is a subgroup of the non-abelian group
G_{0}
.

Let
D=(K/F,\sigma,a)
. In the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
(and under the additional assumption that
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
), it is claimed that
f:D\to D

f(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{i}(\tau(d_{i}))t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}t^{i}\tau(d_{i}),

is an anti-automorphism of
D
that restricts to
\tau
on
K
. This is not correct, as we know that this requires
\tau=\sigma\tau
which is not the case here.
As a consequence, unfortunately the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
also does not hold anymore the way it currently stands, but we can repair it.

Let
(K/F,\sigma,a)
be an associative cyclic algebra and let
\alpha\in K^{\times}
as usual, and
\tau\in\Aut(K)
.
We have
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
, so we can apply Theorem
4.8
for
k=n-1
. Thus there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}:(K/F,\sigma,a)\to(K/F,\sigma,a),

\widetilde{G}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tau(c_{i})(\alpha t^{n-1})^{i},

if and only we have

N_{K/F}(\alpha)=\tau(a)a^{n-1}.

Put
\alpha=1
as suggested in
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
, then there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,1,n-1}:(K/F,\sigma,a)\to(K/F,\sigma,a)

if and only

\tau(a)a^{n-1}=1.

Thus anti-automorphisms
G
such that
G|_{K}=\tau
do exist for the right choice of
a\in F^{\times}
, but these map
t
to
t^{n-1}
and not to
t
as claimed.

Suppose that additionally
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
as assumed in
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
, then there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,1,n-1}:(K/F,\sigma,a)\to(K/F,\sigma,a)

if and only
a^{n}=1.

But for
a=1
which is the only option here, we obtain the trivial case that
(K/F,\sigma,1)
is split. Note that we run into the same problem when we choose any
a\in F^{\times}
such that
\tau(a)=a
, since also in that case there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,1,n-1}
if and only if
a^{n}=1
, forcing
a=\zeta_{n}
to be a root of unity, hence we end up again in the trivial case that
(K/F,\sigma,\zeta_{n})
is split.

If
D
is a division algebra, then
D\not\cong D^{op}
as
F
-algebras, since its degree is
n>2
hence so is its exponent, as we work over number fields here. Therefore
D
does not admit an involution, as already observed in the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
.

We now obtain an explicit construction of a cyclic algebra with an anti-automorphism of the second kind, which has no involution if it is a division algebra, and repair
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 3.4]
as follows. Note that we need an additional norm condition for the anti-automorphism to exist.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
m>0
,
n>2
, and let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
with Galois group
\Gal(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Let
G_{0}
be the metacyclic group generated by
\sigma
and
\tau\in\Aut(K)
with
\sigma^{n}=id
,
\tau^{4m}=id
and
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma
. Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension of degree
mn
with Galois group
G_{0}
.

Then the cyclic algebra
D=(K/F,\sigma,a)
admits an anti-automorphism of the second kind
G_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
that restricts to
\tau
and maps
t
to
\alpha t^{n-1}
for some
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, if and only if

N_{K/F}(\alpha)=\tau(a)a^{n-1}.

The anti-automorphisms
G_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
have infinite order.
If
D
is a division algebra, then it does not admit an involution.

5.2.
Anti-automorphisms on the ring twisted Laurent series

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
with Galois group generated by
\sigma
, and let
K((t;\sigma))
be the division ring of twisted Laurent series in the indeterminate
t
with the usual addition and multiplication given by
ta=\sigma(a)t
.

Then
K((t;\sigma))=(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})
is a cyclic division algebra of degree
n
over its center
F((t^{n}))
, where we write
\sigma
also for the unique extension of
\sigma
to
K((t^{n}))
that leaves
t
invariant.

By
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 4.3]
, every anti-automorphism
f
on
K((t;\sigma))
satisfies
\bar{f}\sigma(\bar{f})^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}
, where
\bar{f}
denotes the canonical automorphism on the residue field
K
that is induced by
f
.
This obviously implies that for every anti-automorphism
f
on
K((t;\sigma))
that restricts to some
\tau\in\Aut(K)
, we have
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}
, equivalently
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
.

Suppose that
\tau\in\Aut(K)
. Then either
\tau
and
\sigma
permute or there exists some
k\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
and
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
. Every automorphism
\tau
extends canonically to some unique
\tau\in\Aut(K((t^{n})))
that maps
t^{n}
to
t^{n}
.
Let
\alpha\in K((t^{n}))^{\times}
and put
x=t^{n}
. An element in
(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})
has the form

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}c_{i,{j_{i}}}x^{j_{i}})t^{i}.

By Theorem
4.8
,
there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}:(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})\to(K((t^{n}))/F((x)),\sigma,t^{n}),

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}c_{i,{j_{i}}}x^{j})t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})x^{j_{i}})(\alpha t^{k})^{i}

generated by
\widetilde{G}(c)=\tau(h)
for all
c\in K((t^{n}))
and
\widetilde{G}(t)=\alpha t^{nk}
,
if and only if

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=\tau(t^{n})t^{nk}=t^{n(k+1)}.

Additionally, if
\sigma
and
\tau
commute, there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,1}:(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})\to(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n}),

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,1}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}c_{i,{j_{i}}}x^{j_{i}})t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})x^{j_{i}})(\alpha t)^{i}

if and only if

N_{K((x))/F((x))}(\alpha)=\tau(t^{n})t^{n}=t^{2n}.

We compute explicitly that here

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}c_{i,{j_{i}}}x^{j_{i}})t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,j})x^{j_{i}})(\alpha t^{k})^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})x^{j_{i}})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ik}

=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})t^{n{j_{i}}}N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{ik}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})\sigma^{n{j_{i}}}(N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))t^{n{j_{i}}}t^{ik}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{n{j_{i}}+ik}.

The last equality holds because
\sigma
has order
n
.

By
[
MorST2005
, Proposition 4.3]
, however, as observed above, the existence of such an anti-automorphism
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}
forces
k=n-1
, other exponents
k
cannot exist; and so
there exists a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
on
(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})

if and only if

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=\tau(t^{n})t^{n(n-1)}=t^{n^{2}}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
with Galois group generated by
\sigma
. Suppose that
\alpha\in K((t^{n}))^{\times}
.
The map
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}:K((t;\sigma))\to K((t;\sigma))
,

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\sum_{j_{i}}c_{i,{j_{i}}}x^{j_{i}})t^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j_{i}}\tau(c_{i,{j_{i}}})N_{i}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{n{j_{i}}+ik}

is a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism if and only if
k=n-1
,
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
, and

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=t^{n^{2}}.

When
n>2
, every anti-automorphism
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
has infinite order.

In particular, if
\sigma
and
\tau
commute then
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}
is an anti-automorphism if and only if
n=2
and
k=1
. Conversely, if
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,1}
is an anti-automorphism, then
\sigma
and
\tau
must commute.

Proof.

For every
\tau\in\Aut(K)
there exists some

k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
with
\gcd(k,n)=1
such that
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
.

Let
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}:K((t;\sigma))\to K((t;\sigma))
be an anti-automorphism.
This forces
k=n-1
,
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
, and
N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=t^{n^{2}}
as observed above.

Conversely, if
\tau\sigma=\sigma^{k}\tau
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=t^{n^{2}}
then map
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}
is an anti-automorphism on
K((t;\sigma))
by Theorem
4.8
. The fact that it restricts to
\tau\in\Aut(K)
yields
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
, so
k=n-1
.

When
n>2
, an anti-automorphism
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
is of infinite order since it maps
t
to
\alpha t^{n-1}
.

In particular, if
\sigma
and
\tau
commute, we must have
\sigma=\sigma^{n-1}
, or
n-1\equiv 1\mod n
, which only holds when
n=2
.
And so if
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,1}
is an anti-automorphism then
\sigma
and
\tau
commute.
∎

We now first restrict our considerations to the special case that
\alpha\in K^{\times}
. Then

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=N_{K/F}(\alpha)\in F^{\times}

which is not equal to
t^{n^{2}}
, so when
\alpha\in K^{\times}
there are no anti-automorphisms
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
on
(K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n})),\sigma,t^{n})
.
Anti-homomorphisms
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}
with
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, however, do exist.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\tau\in\Aut(K)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose we only allow that
\alpha\in K^{\times}
. Then the map
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}

defines a ring anti-homomorphism on
K((t;\sigma))
if and only if
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
.
In particular, if
\tau
and
\sigma
commute then this occurs if and only if
k\equiv 1\mod n
.

Proof.

The map
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,k}

will be an anti-homomorphism if and only if the canonically induced map
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
between
K((t;\sigma))
and its opposite algebra
K((t;\sigma^{-1}))
is multiplicative which is the case, if and only if
G(t)G(c)=G(\sigma(c))G(t)
for all
c\in K
.
We compute

G(t)G(c)=\alpha t^{k}\tau(c)=\alpha\sigma^{k}(\tau(c))t^{k}

and

G(\sigma(c))G(t)=\tau(\sigma(c))\alpha t^{k}.

Since we assume that
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, this holds for all
c\in K
if and only if
\tau\sigma=\sigma^{k}\tau
.
In particular, if
\sigma
and
\tau
commute, we must have
\sigma=\sigma^{k}
, or
k\equiv 1\mod n
.
∎

Right before
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
, a map
f:K((t;\sigma))\to K((t;\sigma))
is defined that maps
t
to
t
via

f(\sum_{i}a_{i}t^{i})=\sum_{i}t^{i}\tau(a_{i})=\sum_{i}\sigma^{i}(\tau(a_{i}))t^{i}.

This map is not a anti-homomorphism as it is not well-defined. For it to be well-defined, we would need that
\tau\sigma^{-i}=\tau
for all
i
.
Thus the map
f
cannot be used in the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
. Neither is the obvious alternative choice of the similar looking map
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
for
\alpha=1
or in fact for any
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, as we just saw this will never be an anti-automorphism.

Repairing the proof of
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
requires the existence/construction of some suitable
\alpha\in K((t))\setminus K
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i\geq s}\alpha_{i}x^{i}\in K((t^{n}))^{\times}
.

(i) If
N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=t^{n^{2}}
then
N_{K/F}(\alpha_{s})=1
and
s=1
.

(ii) If
\alpha_{1}\in K^{\times}
such that
N_{K/F}(\alpha_{1})=1
, then
N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha_{1}t^{n})=t^{n^{2}}
.

Proof.

(i)
We have

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=N_{K/F}(\alpha_{s})t^{sn^{2}}+\dots

where
N_{K/F}(\alpha_{s})t^{sn^{2}}
is the term with the lowest power of
t^{n^{2}}
and the remaining terms all involve higher powers of
t^{n^{2}}
. The condition

N_{K((t^{n}))/F((t^{n}))}(\alpha)=t^{n^{2}}
hence implies that
N_{K/F}(\alpha_{s})=1
and
s=1
.

(ii)
is clear.
∎

The order of the anti-automorphisms constructed in the above proof is infinite when
n>2
. For
n=2
(i.e., when
\sigma
and
\tau
commute which is the only time this can happen) and
\alpha\in K((t^{n}))^{\times}
, we have

\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,1}^{\ell}=\widetilde{G}_{\tau^{\ell},\beta,1}
with
\beta=\tau^{\ell}(\alpha)\cdots\tau(\alpha)\alpha
. In particular,
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,1,1}
has the same order as
\tau
. However, even when
n=2
, the order of
\widetilde{G}_{\tau^{\ell},\beta,1}
depends on the order of
\tau
and the choice of
\beta
and is either a multiple of the order of
\tau
or infinite.

We can now prove a slightly different version of
[
MorST2005
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
m>0
,
n>2
be integers, and let
G_{0}
be the metacyclic group generated by
\sigma
and
\tau\in\Aut(K)
with
\sigma^{n}=id
,
\tau^{4m}=id
and
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma
.
Let
K/F_{0}
be a Galois extension of degree
mn
with Galois group
G_{0}
, and let
F={\rm Fix}(\sigma)
.

Then
K((t;\sigma))
has a
\tau
-semilinear anti-automorphism given by
G_{\tau,t^{n},n-1}
; indeed for any
\alpha\in K((t))\setminus K
such that
\alpha=\alpha_{1}t^{n}
and
N_{K/F}(\alpha_{1})=1
,
\tau
extends to an anti-automorphism
\widetilde{G}_{\tau,\alpha,n-1}
.

The ring

does not admit an involution
f
such that
\bar{f}(a)=a
for all

. In particular, if

has no non-trival automorphism (e.g.,

has no involution at all.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that for any such

extends to an anti-automorphism

on

since

(Theorem

).

The rest of the assertion is shown in the proof of

, Theorem 4.4]
.
∎

For general anti-homomorphisms on
K((t;\sigma))
that restrict to some
\tau\in\Aut(K)
on
K
we cannot say much.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
with Galois group generated by
\sigma
.
Let
\widetilde{G}:K((t;\sigma))\to K((t;\sigma))
be an anti-automorphism such that
G|_{K}=\tau\in\Aut(K)
. Then

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}
and

\widetilde{G}(t)=\sum_{(n-1)i}\alpha_{(n-1)i}t^{(n-1)i}

for some
\alpha_{(n-1)i}\in K
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{G}:K((t;\sigma))\to K((t;\sigma))
be an anti-automorphism such that
\widetilde{G}|_{K}=\tau\in\Aut(K)
. For every
\tau\in\Aut(K)
there exists some

k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
with
\gcd(k,n)=1
such that
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{k}
. In this case, we just saw that since
\widetilde{G}
is an anti-automorphism, here
k=n-1
, applying
[
MorST2005
]
.
Suppose
\widetilde{G}(t)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}t^{i}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in K
. Consider the isomorphism
G
that is canonically induced by
\widetilde{G}
between
K((t;\sigma))
and its opposite algebra.
Then we have

(5.1)

G(tz)=G(t)G(z)=(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}t^{i})\tau(z)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\sigma^{i}(\tau(z))t^{i}

and

(5.2)

G(tz)=G(\sigma(z)t)=\sum_{i}\tau(\sigma(z))\alpha_{i}t^{i}

for all
z\in K
. Comparing the coefficients of
t^{i}

in (
4.3
) and
(
4.4
) we obtain

(5.3)

\alpha_{i}\sigma^{i}(\tau(z))=\tau(\sigma(z))\alpha_{i}

for all

and all

. This implies

\alpha_{i}(\tau\sigma^{i}(z)-\tau\sigma(z))=0

for all
i
and all
z\in K
. Since

k=n-1
,

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{n-1}

and
\sigma
has order
n
, this implies that

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}\not=\sigma^{i}

for all

so that
\alpha_{i}=0
for
all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
with

(we read the

’s modulo
n
here). Therefore
we get that

\widetilde{G}(t)=\sum_{(n-1)i}\alpha_{(n-1)i}t^{(n-1)i}

for some
\alpha_{(n-1)i}\in K.

∎

6.
Monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one on generalized cyclic algebras

6.1.
Nonassociative generalized cyclic algebras

Generalized cyclic algebras are special examples of central simple algebras which do not have to be crossed products (for interesting examples, see
[
Hanke2001
,
Hanke2004
,
Hanke2005
]
).

Let
D
be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over
C={\rm Cent}(D)
of degree
r
and

\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(D)
such that
\sigma|_{C}
has finite order
m
and fixed field
F={\rm Fix}(\sigma)
.
In this setup,
C/F
is automatically a cyclic Galois field extension of
degree
m
and
\mathrm{Gal}(C/F)=\langle\sigma|_{C}\rangle
. Then there exists
u\in D^{\times}
such that
\sigma^{m}=i_{u}
and
\sigma(u)=u
. These two relations determine
u
up to multiplication with elements in
F^{\times}
.

Let
f(t)=t^{m}-d\in D[t;\sigma]
be a two-sided skew polynomial, that means
d\in F^{\times}
. The associative quotient algebra
D[t;\sigma]/D[t;\sigma](t^{m}-d)

is called a
generalized cyclic algebra
and denoted by
(D,\sigma,d)
following Jacobson
[
Jac1996
, p. 19]
.

The generalized cyclic algebra

(D,\sigma,d)
is a central simple algebra over
F
of degree
mr
. Generalized cyclic algebras are special examples of crossed product algebras where the crossed product is taken using
D
and the cyclic group
\langle\sigma\rangle
. Every solvable crossed product division algebra is a generalized cyclic algebra by Albert.

When
D=C
and
f(t)=t^{m}-d\in F[t;\sigma]
, we obtain the cyclic algebra

Since the theory we developed is bases on skew polynomials it can be canonically extended to cyclic and generalized cyclic Azumaya algebras over rings.

Let

with center
C

Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(D)
be a ring automorphism, such that

. We will assume that

is a cyclic Galois ring extension of degree

has constant rank

-module.

Let

with

is a nonassociative (Petit) algebra over its center

and an associative Azumaya algebra over

of constant rank

if and only if

which is called a
nonassociative generalized cyclic Azumaya algebra
when

.

If

is a commutative ring,

is a cyclic Galois extension of rings of degree
m
with Galois group generated by
\sigma
, and

. In this case
(C/S_{0},\sigma,d)=C[t,\sigma]/C[t;\sigma](t^{m}-d)
is an Azumaya algebra which is called an associative

It is straightforward to check that the results from the last section also hold for (generalized) cyclic Azumaya algebras, since for the right choice of
\alpha\in C^{\times}
and
\tau\in\Aut(D)
, the correspondingly defined maps
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
and
\widetilde{G}_{\tau\tau,\alpha,k}
are also isomorphism/anti-automorphisms for generalized Azumaya algebras.

This broadly ties in with problems studied in
[

B

C

B\otimes_{C}B

C

B
is not Brauer-equivalent to
C

B

C
