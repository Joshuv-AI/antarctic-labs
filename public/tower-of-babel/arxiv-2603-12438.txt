Determinantal formulas for Sklyanin–Whittaker integrals
Taro Kimura

Abstract
. We study multi-variable integrals, that we name Sklyanin–Whittaker integrals, and prove their determinantal formulas. We also discuss a q q -deformation, a determinantal point process, and associated Mellin–Barnes integrals.



1.
Introduction and summary

This paper is devoted to integrals associated with the so-called Sklyanin measure, which was originally introduced by Sklyanin
[
Skl85
]
in the context of the quantum integrable system (quantum Toda chain).
Let
G
be a real reductive group and and let
H_{\mathbb{C}}
be its complex Cartan torus.
We denote by
A\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
the split real form of the Cartan torus and
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Lie}A\simeq\mathbb{R}^{\operatorname{rk}G}
its split Cartan subalgebra.
We denote the set of positive (negative, resp.) roots associated with
G
by
R_{G}^{+}
(
R_{G}^{-}
) and set
R_{G}=R_{G}^{+}\cup R_{G}^{-}
.
We denote the Weyl group by
W_{G}
.
Let
\differential{x}
be a Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{a}
.
Then, the Sklyanin measure of type
G
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\differential{S_G(x)}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\alpha(x)}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}\differential{x}\,,

where we set
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
and
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function.
Since we only use the root system data for the definition, we apply the root system terminology to classify the Sklyanin measure.
In this paper, we focus on the classical root systems of type
A_{n-1}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
.

Let
\differential{\mu(x)}=w(x)\differential{x}
be a weighted measure on
\mathbb{R}
, where
w
decays sufficiently fast at infinity.
Then, we are primarily interested in the following integral, that we call the Sklyanin–Whittaker (SW) integral,

(1.2)

\displaystyle Z_{G}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\alpha(x)}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(x_i)}\,.

This type of integrals appears in various contexts of mathematics and physics in addition to the original context in the quantum Toda system, including directed polymers
[
O’C09
]
, quantum spin chains
[
KKN13
,
CK20
]
, and supersymmetric gauge theories
[
HO13
,
HR13
,
FKNO15
]
.

The SW integral is a natural generalization of the well-known Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) integral (see, e.g.,
[
Meh04
,
For10
,
EKR15
]
),

(1.3)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{GUE}}=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{j}-x_{i}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x_{i}^{2}}\differential{x}_{i}\,,

which appears particularly in the study of random matrices and related studies.
Although it is a multi-variable integral, one can explicitly evaluate the GUE integral thanks to the underlying determinantal structure, in particular, the Vandermonde determinant formula,
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{j}-x_{i})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i}^{j-1}
.
The GUE integral can be straightforwardly generalized to general root systems and also general weight functions instead of the Gaussian weight, which do not spoil the determinantal structure.
The crucial update for the SW integral compared with the GUE integral is the gamma function analogue of the Vandermonde determinant.
Apparently there is no direct determinantal formula for this case, so that one cannot apply various established techniques of GUE to the SW integral.
However, in this paper, we show a simple trick to convert the SW integral to a determinantal form, which leads to the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Proposition
2.3
)
.

For a classical root system, the SW integral is given by a determinant.

The key idea to obtain a determinantal expression is as follows.
Using the reflection formula of the gamma function,
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin\pi z
, we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}z}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}=\frac{z}{4\pi}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{z}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{z}{2}}\right)\,,

Hence, the Sklyanin measure is proportional to
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\alpha(x)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{\alpha(z)}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{\alpha(x)}{2}}\right)
, where we can apply the generalized Vandermonde determinant formulas for classical root systems for both additive and multiplicative variables.
For example, the multiplicative part is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{\alpha(x)}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{\alpha(x)}{2}}\right)=\mathrm{e}^{\rho(x)}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(x)}\right)\,,

where we denote the Weyl vector by
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\alpha
.
The Weyl denominator formula gives us the determinantal expression of the multiplicative Vandermonde determinant, which allows us to use Andréief’s formula (Lemma
2.1
).
This is a sketch of the derivation of the determinantal formula of the SW integral.

Determinantal point process

An immediate application of the determinantal formula of the SW integral is the determinantal point process.
Based on the Sklyanin measure, we consider the following point process.

Let
\differential{\mu(x)}=w(x)\differential{x}
be a weighted measure on
\mathbb{R}
as before, where
w
decays sufficiently fast at infinity.
For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define an
n
-point process on
\mathbb{R}
, that we call the Sklyanin–Whittaker (SW) ensemble of type
G
, given by the following probability measure,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\differential{P_G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{Z_{G}}\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\alpha(x)}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(x_i)}\,.

where
Z_{G}
is the corresponding SW integral.
For
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
, we also assume that
w(x)=w(-x)
so that the measure is invariant under the Weyl group action.

Although it may seem not to be determinantal at first, as discussed above, it turns out that the SW ensemble is categorized into a class of determinantal point processes, called biorthogonal ensemble, introduced by Borodin
[
Bor98
]
, so that it is indeed determinantal.

Proposition 1.2

(Proposision
2.10
)
.

The SW ensemble is a determinantal point process.

The corresponding correlation kernel is not symmetric for two variables, but it is still self-reproducing.
It would be interesting to study its asymptotic behavior, which we leave for a future study.

q
-deformation

In this paper, we also study a
q
-deformed version of the SW integral.
We write the
n
-dimensional torus,
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{z\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\mid|z_{i}|=1,i=1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
|q|<1
, and
(z;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n\geq 0}(1-zq^{n})
.
For
z\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and a root
\alpha
, we write
z^{\alpha}=\mathrm{e}^{\alpha(\log z)}
.
Let
w
be a function on the unit circle admitting a Fourier expansion,
w(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{n}z^{n}
, with sufficiently fast decay of the Fourier coefficients.
For
z\in\mathbb{T}
, we define
\differential{\mu(z)}=w(z)\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}
, which is a weighted Haar measure on the unit circle.
Then, we define the following integral, that we call the
q
-Sklyanin–Whittaker (
q
-SW) integral,

(1.7)

\displaystyle Z^{(q)}_{G}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}(z^{\alpha};q)_{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(z_i)}\,.

This type of integral has a close relation to the so-called
q
-Whittaker function
[
GLO08a
,
GLO08b
,
GLO08c
]
, which is given by a limit of the Macdonald function
[
Mac95
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Proposition
2.13
)
.

The
q
-SW integral associated with a classical root system is given by a Toeplitz–Hankel type determinant.

The idea is the same as before.
Let
\theta(z;q)=(z;q)_{\infty}(q/z;q)_{\infty}
be the theta function.
Then, we have

(1.8)

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}(z^{\alpha};q)_{\infty}(z^{-\alpha};q)_{\infty}=\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}(1-z^{-\alpha})\theta(z^{\alpha};q)\,,

which is a product of the multiplicative and the elliptic Vandermonde determinants.
The elliptic one is indeed the Macdonald denominator associated with the corresponding affine root systems, which has also the determinantal formulas given by Rosengren and Schlosser
[
RS05
]
.
We remark that there are three more types of reduced affine root systems in addition to the classical
ABCD
types, i.e.,
B_{n}^{\vee}
,
C_{n}^{\vee}
, and
BC_{n}
, which are reduced to either
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
in the limit
q\to 1
.

Mellin–Barnes integral

Another example that we study in this paper is the Mellin–Barnes integral,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}w(x_{i})\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,,

where
\{a_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{r}
and
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{s}
are parameters and
\mathsf{w}
is a linear form associated with the irreducible representation of the corresponding Lie group.
The weight function
w
is chosen so that the integrand is invariant under the Weyl group action.
This is understood as a multi-variable contour integral, so that the determinantal formula mentioned above cannot be directly applied.
For the case
n=1
, it is essentially the ordinary Mellin–Barnes integral, which gives rise to the hypergeometric function.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorems
3.2
,
3.4
)
.

The Mellin–Barnes SW integral of a classical root system is given by a Wronskian of the hypergeometric function.

We also study a
q
-deformation of the Mellin–Barnes SW integral, for which we prove similar determinantal formulas (Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
).

Lemma 2.1

(Andréief’s formula)
.

For families of integrable functions,
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
, with respect to the measure
\mu
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\int\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}f_{i}(x_{j})\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}g_{i}(x_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(x_i)}=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\int f_{i}(x)g_{j}(x)\differential{\mu(x)}\right)\,.

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
EKR15
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N_{G}=\frac{1}{2}|R_{G}|=|R_{G}^{+}|
.
We write
\operatorname{sh}z=2\sinh\left(\dfrac{z}{2}\right)=\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{z}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{z}{2}}
.
Then, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\alpha(x)}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{N_{G}}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\alpha(x)\operatorname{sh}\alpha(x)\,.

Proof.

It follows from the reflection formula of the gamma function,
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin\pi z
.
∎

We have two types of the product of positive roots for additive and multiplicative variables, which we can write as a determinant.

Additive formulas:
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\alpha(x)

(2.4a)

\displaystyle A_{n-1}:

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i}^{n-j}

(2.4b)

\displaystyle B_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(x_{i}+x_{j})

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i}^{2n-2j+1}

(2.4c)

\displaystyle C_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}2x_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(x_{i}+x_{j})

\displaystyle=2^{n}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i}^{2n-2j+1}

(2.4d)

\displaystyle D_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(x_{i}+x_{j})

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i}^{2n-2j}

Multiplicative formulas:
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\operatorname{sh}\alpha(x)

(2.5a)

\displaystyle A_{n-1}:

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\mathrm{e}^{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-j\right)x_{i}}

(2.5b)

\displaystyle B_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{sh}x_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}+x_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\mathrm{e}^{(n+\frac{1}{2}-j)x_{i}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(n+\frac{1}{2}-j)x_{i}}\right)

(2.5c)

\displaystyle C_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{sh}2x_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}+x_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\mathrm{e}^{(n+1-j)x_{i}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(n+1-j)x_{i}}\right)

(2.5d)

\displaystyle D_{n}:

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)\operatorname{sh}\left(x_{i}+x_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\mathrm{e}^{(n-j)x_{i}}+\mathrm{e}^{-(n-j)x_{i}}\right)

).

Proposition 2.3
.

For
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define

(2.6)

\displaystyle M_{i,j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{i}\mathrm{e}^{jx}\differential{\mu(x)}\,,

which is finite for any
i
and
j
under the assumption that
w
decays sufficiently fast at infinity.
Then, the SW integral of classical root systems is given by a determinant as follows,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle Z_{A_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}M_{i,j-\frac{n-1}{2}}

(2.7b)

\displaystyle Z_{B_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n^{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{2i+1,i+\frac{1}{2}}-M_{2i+1,-i-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(2.7c)

\displaystyle Z_{C_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{(4\pi)^{n^{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{2i+1,i+1}-M_{2i+1,-i-1}\right)

(2.7d)

\displaystyle Z_{D_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n(n-1)}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{2i,i}+M_{2i,-i}\right)

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, the integrand of the SW integral is written as a product of additive and multiplicative Vandermonde determinants, which are given in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
Then, applying Andréief’s formula (Lemma
2.1
), we obtain the result.
∎

We show an alternative form of the determinantal formula.
Let
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
be monic polynomials of degree
k
.
Recalling that
\cosh(nx)
is given by the Chebyshev polynomial of
\cosh(x)
of degree
n
,
\cosh(nx)=2^{n-1}\cosh^{n}x+\cdots
, we have

(2.8a)

\displaystyle Z_{A_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x)q_{j}(\mathrm{e}^{x})\differential{\mu_{A_{n-1}}(x)}\right)\,,

(2.8b)

\displaystyle Z_{B_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{n^{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x^{2})q_{j}(\cosh(x))\differential{\mu_{B_n}(x)}\right)\,,

(2.8c)

\displaystyle Z_{C_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{n^{2}}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x^{2})q_{j}(\cosh(x))\differential{\mu_{C_n}(x)}\right)\,,

(2.8d)

\displaystyle Z_{D_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{n(n-1)}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x^{2})q_{j}(\cosh(x))\differential{\mu_{D_n}(x)}\right)\,,

where

(2.9a)

\displaystyle\differential{\mu_{A_{n-1}}(x)}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}x}\differential{\mu(x)}\,,\quad\differential{\mu_{B_n}(x)}=x\sinh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\differential{\mu(x)}\,,

(2.9b)

\displaystyle\differential{\mu_{C_n}(x)}=2x\sinh\left(x\right)\differential{\mu(x)}\,,\quad\differential{\mu_{D_n}(x)}=\differential{\mu(x)}\,.

We remark that, for
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
, the measure
\differential{\mu_G}
does not depend on the rank, and the integral in the determinant is invariant under the change of variable
x\mapsto-x
since
w(x)=w(-x)
in those cases.

2.1.
Gaussian integral

We consider the simplest example of the SW integral with the normalized Gaussian weight,
w(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}}/\sqrt{2\pi}
, such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\differential{\mu(x)}=1
.
We remark that
w(x)=w(-x)
.
Hence, the integrand is invariant under the Weyl group action for any
G
.

Let
\{H_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be the monic Hermite polynomials obeying the orthogonality condition,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{k}(x)H_{l}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}}\frac{\differential{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=k!\delta_{k,l}\,.

We prepare the following Lemmas to evaluate the Gaussian SW integral.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle M^{H}_{i,j}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{i}(x)\mathrm{e}^{jx}\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}}\frac{\differential{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{j^{2}}{2}}j^{i}\,.

Proof.

It follows from the following identity,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{n}(x+t)\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}}\frac{\differential{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=t^{n}\,,

which can be obtained by the change of variable
y=x+t
, and then applying the expansion of the generating function,
\mathrm{e}^{xt-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}
, together with the orthogonality of the Hermite polynomials.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be the Barnes
\mathsf{G}
-function and
a\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then, the following identity holds,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}(j+a)^{2i}=\frac{2^{(n-1)(n+2a-1)}}{\pi^{\tfrac{n-1}{2}}}\mathsf{G}(n+1)\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+a)\mathsf{G}(n+a+\frac{1}{2})\mathsf{G}(1+2a)}{\mathsf{G}(1+a)\mathsf{G}(\frac{3}{2}+a)\mathsf{G}(n+2a)}\,.

Proof.

The determinant is indeed a Vandermonde determinant,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}(j+a)^{2i}=\prod_{0\leq i<j\leq n-1}((j+a)^{2}-(i+a)^{2})\,.

Then, applying the functional relation of the
\mathsf{G}
-function,
\mathsf{G}(z+1)=\Gamma(z)\mathsf{G}(z)
, together with the Legendre duplication formula,
\Gamma(2z)=\frac{2^{2z-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(z)\Gamma\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)
, we obtain the result.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

The SW integral of the Gaussian weight
w(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}}/\sqrt{2\pi}
is given as follows,

(2.15a)

\displaystyle Z_{A_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{n(n^{2}-1)}{24}}}{2^{n(n-1)}\pi^{\tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}}}\mathsf{G}(n+1)

(2.15b)

\displaystyle Z_{B_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{n(4n^{2}-1)}{24}}}{2^{n(n+1)}\pi^{\tfrac{(n+1)(2n-1)}{2}}}\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+1)\mathsf{G}(n+\frac{3}{2})}{\mathsf{G}(\frac{3}{2})}

(2.15c)

\displaystyle Z_{C_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{12}}}{2^{n(n-1)}\pi^{\tfrac{n(2n+1)}{2}}}\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+1)\mathsf{G}(n+\frac{3}{2})}{\mathsf{G}(\frac{3}{2})}

(2.15d)

\displaystyle Z_{D_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{n(n-1)(2n-1)}{12}}}{2^{n^{2}-1}\pi^{\tfrac{(n-1)(2n+1)}{2}}}\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+\frac{1}{2})\mathsf{G}(n+1)}{\mathsf{G}(\frac{3}{2})}

Proof.

We can use the determinantal formula of the SW integral (Proposition
2.3
).
Since the Hermite polynomial of odd (even, resp.) degree contains only odd (even) degree monomials, the determinant is invariant under the replacement of
M_{i,j}
by
M_{i,j}^{H}
.
Then, we obtain the result by applying Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
.
∎

We remark that the exponential factor appearing in the formula is in general written using the Weyl vector.
Let
h^{\vee}
be the dual Coxeter number of
G
.
Then, applying the Freudenthal–de Vries strange formula,
12\rho^{2}=h^{\vee}\dim G
, we have
\exp(\frac{1}{2} \rho^2)=\exp(\frac{1}{24} h^\vee\dim G)
.

2.2.
Matrix integral with an external source

The SW integral has a close relation to the matrix integrals.
We show that it will be identified with the matrix integral with the so-called external source. See, e.g.,
[
BH16
]
for details.

We focus on type
A_{n-1}
.
Let
\mathsf{H}_{n}=\{H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\mid H=H^{\dagger}\}
be the set of Hermitian matrices of size
n
.
For
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define a diagonal matrix
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
We consider a Hermitian matrix integral with an external source
A
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathsf{H}_{n}}(a)=\int_{\mathsf{H}_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\tr V(H)+\tr HA}\differential{H}\,,

where
V
is the potential function ensuring convergence of the integral.

Lemma 2.7

(Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber (HCIZ) formula of type
A_{n-1}
)
.

Let
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
,
B=\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
, and we denote the associated Vandermonde determinant by
\Delta(A)=\Delta(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}-a_{j})
and
\Delta(B)=\Delta(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(b_{i}-b_{j})
.
Then, under the normalization of the Haar measure
\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\differential{U}=1
, the following integral formula holds,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\mathrm{e}^{\tr UAU^{\dagger}B}\differential{U}=\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+1)}{\Delta(A)\Delta(B)}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\mathrm{e}^{a_{i}b_{j}}\,,

where
\mathsf{G}(n+1)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}k!
(Barnes
\mathsf{G}
-function).

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
EKR15
]
.
∎

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be eigenvalues of
H\in\mathsf{H}_{n}
and denote the Vandermonde determinant by
\Delta(x)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})
.
Applying the HCIZ formula, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathsf{H}_{n}}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+1)}{n!\Delta(A)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta({x})\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\mathrm{e}^{x_{i}a_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-V(x_{i})}\differential{x_i}

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{G}(n+1)}{\Delta(A)}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{i-1}\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)+xa_{j}}\differential{x}\right)\,.

Proposition 2.8
.

The SW integral of type
A_{n-1}
agrees with the Hermitian matrix integral with an external source
A=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with the corresponding Weyl vector
\rho_{i}=\frac{n+1}{2}-i
under the identification
\differential{\mu(x)}=\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)}\differential{x}
.

Proof.

Consider the Vandermonde determinant
\Delta(A)=\Delta(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
for
A=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\Delta(A)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(j-i)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}i!=\mathsf{G}(n+1)\,.

Then, we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle Z_{H_{n}}(\rho)=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{i-1}\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)+x\rho_{j}}\differential{x}\right)\,,

which, up to a constant, agrees with
Z_{A_{n-1}}
under the identification
\differential{\mu(x)}=\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)}\differential{x}
.
∎

We have a similar structure for
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
.
In these cases, we should consider an integral over the corresponding Lie algebra instead of the Hermitian matrix of the case
G=A_{n-1}
.
For example, for
G=B_{n},D_{n}
, it is an integral over skew-symmetric matrices, which can be transformed into the normal form via the orthogonal transform.
The corresponding Jacobian will be given by the product over the root system.
In the presence of an external source, after applying the Harish-Chandra formula, a half of the root product is canceled and the source term is given by the determinant of
\cosh
or
\sinh
(see, e.g.,
[
FEDFZ06
,
FILZ17
]
), which agrees with Proposition
2.3
.

2.3.
Determinantal point process

Motivated by the discussion above, we study a point process, that we call the Sklyanin–Whittaker ensemble.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\differential{\mu(x)}=w(x)\differential{x}
be a weighted measure on
\mathbb{R}
, where
w
decays sufficiently fast at infinity.
For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the SW ensemble of type
G
of
n
points on
\mathbb{R}
, given by the following probability measure,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\differential{P_G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{Z_{G}}\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\alpha(x)}{2\pi}\right)\right|^{-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(x_i)}\,.

where
Z_{G}
is the corresponding SW integral.
For
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
, we also assume that
w(x)=w(-x)
so that the measure is invariant under the Weyl group action.

Rewriting the gamma factor in the joint distribution function as
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}\alpha(x)\sinh\left(\tfrac{\alpha(z)}{2}\right)
together with the determinant formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we immediately identify the SW ensemble as a special case of the so-called biorthogonal ensemble introduced by Borodin
[
Bor98
]
, which is a determinantal point process.

We define a pairing matrix for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

(2.23)

\displaystyle M_{i,j}^{G}=\left<p_{i},q_{j}\right>_{G}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x)q_{j}(\mathrm{e}^{x})\differential{\mu_{G}(x)}&(G=A_{n-1})\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{i}(x^{2})q_{j}(\cosh(x))\differential{\mu_{G}(x)}&(G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n})\end{cases}

where the measure
\mu_{G}
is defined in (
2.9
).
By the assumption on the weight
w
, decaying sufficiently fact at infinity, there exists the inverse of this matrix denoted by
\check{M}^{G}
.
We denote the joint probability density function of the SW ensemble with respect to the measure
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu_G(x_i)}
by
p_{G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, i.e.,
\differential{P_G}=p_{G}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu_G(x_i)}
.
Then, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle p_{G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}K_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})\,,

where the kernel is given by

(2.25)

\displaystyle K_{G}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}p_{i}(x)\check{M}^{G}_{i,j}q_{j}(\mathrm{e}^{y})&(G=A_{n-1})\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}p_{i}(x^{2})\check{M}^{G}_{i,j}q_{j}(\cosh(y))&(G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n})\end{cases}

This kernel is not symmetric, but self-reproducing,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{G}(x,x)\differential{\mu_G(x)}=n\,,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{G}(x,y)K_{G}(y,z)\differential{\mu_G(y)}=K_{G}(x,z)\,.

We define the
k
-point correlation function of the SW ensemble,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\rho_{G,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}):=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-k}}p_{G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}\differential{\mu_G(x_i)}\,.

Then, we have the following formula.

Proposition 2.10
.

The correlation function of the SW ensemble is given by a determinant of the corresponding kernel,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\rho_{G,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}K_{G}(x_{i},x_{j})\,,

i.e., it is a determinantal point process,

Proof.

It directly follows from the self-reproducing property of the kernel.
∎

2.4.
q
-Sklyanin–Whittaker integral

Let
w
be a function on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
admitting a Fourier expansion,
w(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{n}z^{n}
, with sufficiently fast decay of the Fourier coefficients.
We assume
w_{n}=w_{-n}
for
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
.
For
z\in\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\mid|z|=1\}
, we define
\differential{\mu(z)}=w(z)\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}
, which is a weighted Haar measure on the unit circle.
We define the
q
-SW integral as follows,

(2.29)

\displaystyle Z^{(q)}_{G}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}(z^{\alpha};q)_{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(z_i)}\,.

By the definition of the theta function (
A.3
), we may write

(2.30)

\displaystyle Z^{(q)}_{G}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}^{+}}(1-z^{-\alpha})\theta(z^{\alpha};q)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(z_i)}\,.

As in the previous case, we have two types of Vandermonde determinants in the integrand.
We write
\theta(x)=\theta(x;q)
and define the following products of theta functions, that we call the elliptic Vandermonde determinants,

(2.31a)

\displaystyle W_{A_{n-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{j}\theta(x_{i}/x_{j})

(2.31b)

\displaystyle W_{B_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta(x_{i})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}^{-1}\theta(x_{i}/x_{j})\theta(x_{i}x_{j})

(2.31c)

\displaystyle W_{C_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{-1}\theta(x_{i}^{2})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}^{-1}\theta(x_{i}/x_{j})\theta(x_{i}x_{j})

(2.31d)

\displaystyle W_{D_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}^{-1}\theta(x_{i}/x_{j})\theta(x_{i}x_{j})

There is an elliptic generalization of the Vandermonde determinant formula shown by Rosengren and Schlosser.

Proposition 2.11

(Rosengren–Schlosser
[
RS05
]
)
.

The following determinant formulas hold,

(2.32a)

\displaystyle\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(x_{i}^{j-1}\theta((-1)^{n-1}q^{j-1}tx_{i}^{n};q^{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}{(q^{n};q^{n})_{\infty}^{n}}\theta(tx_{1}\cdots x_{n})W_{A_{n-1}}(x)

(2.32b)

\displaystyle\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(x_{i}^{j-n}\theta(q^{j-1}x_{i}^{2n-1};q^{2n-1})-x_{i}^{n+1-j}\theta(q^{j-1}x_{i}^{1-2n};q^{2n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}{(q^{2n-1};q^{2n-1})_{\infty}^{n}}W_{B_{n}}(x)

(2.32c)

\displaystyle\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(x_{i}^{j-n-1}\theta(-q^{j}x_{i}^{2n+2};q^{2n+2})-x_{i}^{n+1-j}\theta(-q^{j}x_{i}^{-2n-2};q^{2n+2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}{(q^{2n+2};q^{2n+2})_{\infty}^{n}}W_{C_{n}}(x)

(2.32d)

\displaystyle\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(x_{i}^{j-n}\theta(-q^{j-1}x_{i}^{2n-2};q^{2n-2})+x_{i}^{n-j}\theta(-q^{j-1}x_{i}^{2-2n};q^{2n-2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}{(q^{2n-2};q^{2n-2})_{\infty}^{n}}W_{D_{n}}(x)

For
G=A_{n-1}
, we have an auxiliary parameter
t
, which is called the norm in
[
RS05
]
.
We assume
|t|<1
to apply the following Lemma concerning the theta function inverse.

Lemma 2.12
.

For
|q|<|z|<1
, we have

(2.33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta(z;q)}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n+1\choose 2}}{1-zq^{r}}-z^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n+1\choose 2}}{1-z^{-1}q^{r}}\right)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}z^{m}\,,

where

(2.34)

\displaystyle c_{m_{\geq 0}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{n+1\choose 2}q^{mn}\,,\qquad c_{m_{<0}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{n\choose 2}q^{(|m|-1)(n+1)}\,.

Proof.

See
[
And84
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

The
q
-SW integral of type
A_{n-1}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
is given by a Toeplitz–Hankel determinant,

(2.35a)

\displaystyle Z_{A_{n-1}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sum_{k,m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{nm}q^{(j-1)m+n{m\choose 2}}t^{k+m}c_{k}w_{i-j-nm-k}

(2.35b)

\displaystyle Z_{B_{n}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}q^{(j-1)m+(2n+1){m\choose 2}}\left(w_{i-j-m(2n+1)}-w_{2n+1-i-j-m(2n+1)}\right)

(2.35c)

\displaystyle Z_{C_{n}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{jm+(2n+2){m\choose 2}}\left(w_{i-j-m(2n+2)}-w_{2n+2-i-j-m(2n+2)}\right)

(2.35d)

\displaystyle Z_{D_{n}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(q;q)_{\infty}^{n}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{(j-1)m+(2n-2){m\choose 2}}\left(w_{i-j-m(2n-2)}+w_{2n-i-j-m(2n-2)}\right)\,.

Proof.

Together with the multiplicative determinantal formula (
2.5
), the Vandermonde factors can be computed as follows,

(2.36a)

\displaystyle A_{n-1}:\qquad

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\right)\theta\left(\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\right)=W_{A_{n-1}}(z)\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}z_{i}^{-j+1}\,,

\displaystyle B_{n}:\qquad

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}^{-1})\theta(z_{i})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{i}z_{j}}\right)\theta\left(\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\right)\theta(z_{i}z_{j})

(2.36b)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}W_{B_{n}}(z)\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(z_{i}^{n+\frac{1}{2}-j}-z_{i}^{-n-\frac{1}{2}+j}\right)\,,

\displaystyle C_{n}:\qquad

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}^{-2})\theta(z_{i}^{2})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{i}z_{j}}\right)\theta\left(\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\right)\theta(z_{i}z_{j})

(2.36c)

\displaystyle=W_{C_{n}}(z)\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(z_{i}^{n+1-j}-z_{i}^{-n-1+j}\right)\,,

\displaystyle D_{n}:\qquad

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{i}z_{j}}\right)\theta\left(\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\right)\theta(z_{i}z_{j})

(2.36d)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}W_{D_{n}}(z)\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left(z_{i}^{n-j}+z_{i}^{-n+j}\right)\,.

Then, by Proposition
2.11
, we can apply Andréief’s formula to rewrite the
n
-variable integral as a determinant.
Each element of the corresponding matrix is a single-variable contour integral on
\mathbb{T}
, yielding the constant term of the integrand.
By the series expansion of the theta function (
A.3
) (together with Lemma
2.12
for type
A_{n-1}
), we obtain the result.
We also remark that
w_{n}=w_{-n}
for
G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
.
The infinite series appearing in the determinant converge since the Fourier coefficients of
w
decay sufficiently fast.
∎

In the limit
q\to 0
, the integral is reduced to the ordinary Cartan torus integral associated with the classical groups,

(2.37)

\displaystyle Z^{(q=0)}_{G}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}(1-z^{\alpha})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{\mu(z_i)}\,,

for which the Toeplitz–Hankel type determinantal formulas are known
[
Joh97
,
BR99
,
GGT19
]
.
The formula shown in Proposition
2.13
is a natural generalization of these formulas.

3.
Mellin–Barnes integral

Set
r,s,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
0\leq s\leq r
and
0\leq n\leq r
.
Let
I
be an injective map,
I:\{1,\ldots,n\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
For
G=A_{n-1},B_{n},C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
, we denote by
\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal{R})
the set of weights of the irreducible representation
\mathcal{R}
of the corresponding Lie group.
For the defining/vector representation, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})=\begin{cases}\{e_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}&(A_{n-1})\\
\{\pm e_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}\cup\{0\}&(B_{n})\\
\{\pm e_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}&(C_{n})\\
\{\pm e_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}&(D_{n})\\
\end{cases}

We define the Mellin–Barnes SW integral of type
G
as follows,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G,I}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}\in\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}w(x_{i})\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,,

where we define with a parameter
z
as

(3.3)

\displaystyle w(x)=\begin{cases}z^{-x}&(A_{n-1})\\
z^{-x}+z^{x}&(B_{n},C_{n},D_{n})\end{cases}

The integrand is invariant under the Weyl group action.
We assume that the parameters
\{a_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1,\ldots,r}
and
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1,\ldots,s}
are generic, such that none of the poles of
\Gamma(\pm x-a_{\alpha})
are canceled by those of
\Gamma(\pm x-b_{\alpha})
.
The integration contour is given as follows:
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}
be a contour enclosing the poles of
\Gamma(\pm x-a_{\alpha})
located at
\pm(a_{\alpha}-m)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We define the
n
-variable integral contour
\mathcal{C}_{I}=\mathcal{C}_{I(1)}^{+}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{C}_{I(n)}^{+}
.
Since the integrand is invariant under the symmetric group action, the contours associated with
I
and
I\circ\sigma
yield the same integral for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Then, the integral is understood as follows:

•

G=A_{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{G,I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{I\circ\sigma}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}\in\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{-x_{i}}\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}

(3.4a)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{I}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}\in\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{-x_{i}}\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}

•

G=B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{G,I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}=\pm 1\\
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{I\circ\sigma(1)}^{s_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{C}_{I\circ\sigma(n)}^{s_{n}}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}\in\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{-s_{i}x_{i}}\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}

(3.4b)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}n!}{|W_{G}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{I}}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}\prod_{\mathsf{w}\in\operatorname{wt}(\text{def})}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(\mathsf{w}(x)-b_{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{-x_{i}}\frac{\differential{x}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,.

Hence, we use the same contour and
w(x)=z^{-x}
for any
G=A_{n-1},B_{n},C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
.
By construction, we have
\Psi_{I}=\Psi_{I\circ\sigma}
for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, i.e., it depends only on the image of
I
.
In other words, we may interpret
I:\{1,\ldots,n\}/\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
Since
I
is injective, each contour is taken by no more than single variable.
If the same contour is applied for different variables, the residue will be zero due to the product over the roots,
\prod_{\alpha\in R_{G}}\Gamma(\alpha(x))^{-1}
.
The integral converges when
|z|<1
.

3.1.
Type
A_{n-1}

We study the following Mellin–Barnes SW integral of type
A_{n-1}
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{I}(z)=\Psi_{A_{n-1},I}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix};z\right)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{I}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Gamma(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{r}\Gamma(x_{i}-a_{\alpha})}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Gamma(x_{i}-b_{\alpha})}z^{-x_{i}}\frac{\differential{x_i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,.

As before, we assume that
0\leq s\leq r
,
0\leq n\leq r
, and parameters
\{a_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1,\ldots,r}
and

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumption above, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{\prod_{\beta(\neq\alpha)}^{r}\Gamma(a_{\alpha}-a_{\beta})}{\prod_{\beta=1}^{s}\Gamma(a_{\alpha}-b_{\beta})}z^{-a_{\alpha}}{}_{s}F_{r-1}\left(\begin{matrix}\{1+b_{\beta}-a_{\alpha}\}_{\beta=1}^{s}\\

Recalling that the residue of the gamma function is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname*{Res}_{x=m}\Gamma(-x)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{-a_{\alpha}+m}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\prod_{\beta(\neq\alpha)}^{r}\Gamma(a_{\alpha}-a_{\beta}-m)}{\prod_{\beta=1}^{s}\Gamma(a_{\alpha}-b_{\beta}-m)}
