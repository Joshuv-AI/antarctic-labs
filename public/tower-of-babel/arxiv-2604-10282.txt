The automorphism group of countable recursively saturated models of Peano arithmetic and strong cuts
Saeideh Bahrami bahrami.saeideh@gmail.com

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the concept of a Lascar generic automorphism in the setting of models of Peano arithmetic ( PA \mathrm{PA} ) to the subgroup of the automorphism group of a countable recursively saturated model ℳ \mathcal{M} of PA \mathrm{PA} that fixes pointwise a strong cut I I of ℳ \mathcal{M} , denoted by ( Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ) ) ( I ) (\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)} . Then, we prove that: (1) ( Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ) ) ( I ) (\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)} has the small index property. (2) The cofinality of ( Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ) ) ( I ) (\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)} is uncountable. (3) Any nontrivial normal subgroup of ( Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ) ) ( I ) (\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)} is meagre in it. In particular, the infinite cyclic group ℤ \mathbb{Z} is not a homomorphic image of ( Aut ⁡ ( ℳ ) ) ( I ) (\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)} .

3
Small index property

In this section, we begin by generalizing the argument originally used by Lascar in
[
15
]
to prove the SIP for the automorphism group of countable arithmetically saturated models of
\mathrm{PA}
. We then apply this generalized method to establish the small index property for the subgroup of the automorphism group of a countable recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}
that pointwise stabilizes a strong cut of the model.

3.1
Lascar’s method

Here, we define a notion based on Lascar’s proof of Theorem 2.5:

Suppose
\mathbb{L}
is a countable language,
\mathcal{M}
is a countable
\mathbb{L}
-structure, and
\mathrm{G}
is the Polish group of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
. We say that the triple
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\{Y_{K}:\ K\in\mathcal{A}\})
is a
Lascar generic system
for
\mathrm{G}
if:

(1)

\mathcal{X}:=\bigcup_{n>0}\mathcal{X}_{n}
, where each
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is a comeagre subset of
\mathrm{G}^{n}
(with product topology).

(2)

(
n>0
) For every
(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in\mathcal{X}
and for each
h\in\mathrm{G}
, it holds that
(f_{1}^{h},...,f_{n}^{h})\in\mathcal{X}
.

(3)

(
n>0
) For every
(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in\mathcal{X}
the following set is a comeagre subset of
\mathrm{G}
:

\{g\in\mathrm{G}:\ (f_{1},...,f_{n},g)\in\mathcal{X}_{n+1}\}.

(4)

\mathcal{A}
is a family of subsets of
M
such that
\mathrm{G}_{(K)}
is open in
\mathrm{G}
for every
K\in\mathcal{A}
.

(5)

For every
K\in\mathcal{A}
and for each
h\in\mathrm{G}
,
h(K)\in\mathcal{A}
.

(6)

For every
K\in\mathcal{A}
, let
Y_{K}:=\bigcup_{n>0}Y_{K,n}
, where each
Y_{K,n}
is a set of
n
-tuples such that each element of the
n
-tuple is the restriction to
K
of an automorphism
g
of
\mathcal{M}
satisfying
g(K)=K
.

(7)

(
n>0
) For every tuple
(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in\mathcal{X}
, for each
\bar{a}\in M
and for every
K\in\mathcal{A}
there exists some
L\in\mathcal{A}
containing
\bar{a}
and
K
such that
(f_{1}\upharpoonright_{L},...,f_{n}\upharpoonright_{L})\in Y_{K,n}
.

(8)

(
n>0
) For each tuple
(f_{1},...,f_{n})\in\mathcal{X}
and for every
h\in\mathrm{G}
, if
K\in\mathcal{A}
such that
{(f_{1}\upharpoonright_{K},...,f_{n}\upharpoonright_{K})\in Y_{K,n}}
, then
(f_{1}^{h}\upharpoonright_{h^{-1}(K)},...,f_{n}^{h}\upharpoonright_{h^{-1}(K)})\in Y_{K,n}
.

(9)

(
n>0
) For every two
n
-tuples
(f_{1},...,f_{n}),(g_{1},...,g_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
if there exists some
K\in\mathcal{A}
such that
(f_{1}\upharpoonright_{K},...,f_{n}\upharpoonright_{K})\in Y_{K,n}
and
f_{i}\upharpoonright_{K}=g_{i}\upharpoonright_{K}

for all
i=1,...,n
, then there exists some
h\in\mathrm{G}_{(K)}
such that
f_{i}=g_{i}^{h}
for all
i=1,...,n
.

We will show that any automorphism group possessing a Lascar generic system has the SIP. For this purpose we need the following lemmas:

Lemma 3.1

(Hodges-Hodkinson-Lascar-Shelah
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathrm{G}
is a Polish group. Then the index of any meagre subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
is
2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(n\in\omega)
Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a countable
\mathbb{L}
-structure (for a given countable language
\mathbb{L}
) and
\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\{Y_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{A}\})
be a Lascar generic system for
\mathrm{G}
and
(g_{0},...,g_{n})\in\mathcal{X}
. Then:

(1)

If
\mathrm{H}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
whose index is less than
2^{\aleph_{0}}
, then there exists some
g\in\mathrm{H}
such that
(g_{0},...,g_{n},g)\in\mathcal{X}
.

(2)

If
\mathrm{H}
is a nonopen subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
and
\mathrm{O}
is a nonempty open subset of
\mathrm{G}
, then there exists some
g\in\mathrm{O}\setminus\mathrm{H}
such that
(g_{0},...,g_{n},g)\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose not. So by the third condition in the definition of a Lascar generic system,
\mathrm{H}
is meagre in
\mathrm{G}
; but this is in contradiction with the previous lemma.

(2)

By the third condition in the definition of a Lascar generic system and Lemma 2.8.

∎

We are now ready to outline a generalization of the steps used by Lascar in his proof:

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a countable
\mathbb{L}
-structure (for a given countable language
\mathbb{L}
) and
\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
such that it possesses a Lascar generic system
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\{Y_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{A}\})
. Then
\mathrm{G}
has the SIP.

Sketch of proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{H}
is a nonopen subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
whose index is less than
2^{\aleph_{0}}
. By using Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2, we will inductively build sequence
(M_{s}:\ s\in 2^{<\omega})
of elements of
\mathcal{A}
, and sequences
(g_{s}:\ s\in 2^{<\omega})
and
(h_{s}:\ s\in 2^{<\omega})
of elements of
\mathrm{G}
such that:

(1)

For every
\sigma\in 2^{\omega}
it holds that
M=\bigcup_{n>0}M_{\sigma\upharpoonright_{n}}
.

(2)

For every
s,t\in 2^{<\omega}
if
s\sqsubset_{e}t
(i.e.,
s
is an initial segment of
t
) then
M_{s}\subseteq M_{t}
.

(3)

For every
s\in 2^{<\omega}
, the tuple
(g_{\emptyset},g_{s\upharpoonright_{1}},g_{s\upharpoonright_{2}},...,g_{s})
is in
\mathcal{X}
.

(4)

For every
s,t,t^{\prime}\in 2^{<\omega}
if
s\sqsubset_{e}t
and
s\sqsubset_{e}t^{\prime}
then
h_{t}\upharpoonright_{M_{s}}=h_{t^{\prime}}\upharpoonright_{M_{s}}
.

(5)

For every
s,t,t^{\prime}\in 2^{<\omega}
if
s\sqsubset_{e}t\sqsubset_{e}t^{\prime}
then
g_{s}^{h_{t}}=g_{s}^{h_{t^{\prime}}}

(6)

g_{s}^{h_{s^{\smallfrown}0}}\in H
and
g_{s}^{h_{s^{\smallfrown}1}}\notin H
, for every
s\in 2^{<\omega}
(here, by
s^{\smallfrown}0
and
s^{\smallfrown}1
, we mean the sequences which is obtained from appending
0
and
1
to
s
, respectively).

Suppose we have built such sequences. As a result, for every
\sigma\in 2^{\omega}
the sequence
\{h_{\sigma\upharpoonright_{n}}:\ n\in\omega\}
is Cauchy. So, we put
{h_{\sigma}:=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{\sigma\upharpoonright_{n}}}
. From the above statements, we conclude that
h_{\sigma}H\neq h_{\tau}H
for every distinct
\sigma,\tau\in 2^{\omega}
, which contradicts the assumption that
[\mathrm{G}:\mathrm{H}]<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

In order to construct the aforementioned sequences, suppose
\{a_{n}:\ n\in\omega\}
is an enumeration of
M
.
For the first step of the inductive construction, since we have assumed that
{[\mathrm{G}:\mathrm{H}]<2^{\aleph_{0}}}
, by Lemma 3.2(1), there exists some
g_{\emptyset}\in\mathrm{H}\cap\mathcal{X}
. Then by the definition of a Lascar generic system, there exists some
M_{\emptyset}\in\mathcal{A}
containing
a_{0}
such that
g_{\emptyset}\upharpoonright_{M_{\emptyset}}\in Y_{M_{\emptyset}}
. Moreover, put
h_{\emptyset}=h_{0}=id
. Then, by Lemma 3.2(2) there exists some
f\in g_{\emptyset}\mathrm{G}_{(M_{\emptyset})}\setminus\mathrm{H}
such that
f\in\mathcal{X}
. Again, by the definition of a Lascar generic system, there exists some
h_{1}\in\mathrm{G}_{(M_{\emptyset})}
such that
g_{\emptyset}^{h_{1}}=f
.

For the induction step, suppose
s\in 2^{<\omega}
is given. Let
n\in\omega
be the length of
s
and suppose
g_{s}
,
M_{s}
,
h_{s}
,
h_{s^{\smallfrown}0}
, and
h_{s^{\smallfrown}1}
has been built. Let
t:=s^{\smallfrown}0
or
t:=s^{\smallfrown}1
. So
[\mathrm{G}:\mathrm{H}^{h_{t}}]<2^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then, by Lemma 3.2(1) there exists some
g_{t}\in\mathrm{H}^{h_{t}}
such that
{(g_{s\upharpoonright_{0}},g_{s\upharpoonright_{1}},...,g_{s},g_{t})\in\mathcal{X}}
. Then, put
{h_{t^{\smallfrown}0}:=h_{t}}
. By the definition of a Lascar generic system
(g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{0}},g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{1}},...,g^{h_{t}}_{s},g_{t}^{h_{t}})\in\mathcal{X}
. As a result, there exists some
K\in\mathcal{A}
such that
{(g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{0}}\upharpoonright_{K},g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{1}}\upharpoonright_{K},...,g^{h_{t}}_{s}\upharpoonright_{K},g_{t}^{h_{t}}\upharpoonright_{K})\in Y_{K}}
and
K
contains
h_{t}(a_{n+1})
and
h_{t}(M_{s})
. Now, let
M_{t}:=h_{t}^{-1}(K)
. Again, by Lemma 3.2(2) there exists some
f^{\prime}\in g_{t}^{h_{t}}\mathrm{G}_{(M_{t})}\setminus\mathrm{H}
such that
(g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{0}},g^{h_{t}}_{s\upharpoonright_{1}},...,g^{h_{t}}_{s},f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{X}
. Thus, by the definition of a Lascar generic system there exists some
h\in\mathrm{G}_{(M_{t})}
such that
f^{\prime}=g_{t}^{h_{t}h}
and
g_{s\upharpoonright_{m}}^{h_{t}}=g_{s\upharpoonright_{m}}^{h_{t}h}
for all
m\leq n
. Finally, put
h_{t^{\smallfrown}1}:=h_{t}h
.
∎

3.2
The SIP for
(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)}

Through this subsection, suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a countable recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}
,
I
is a strong cut of
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
. This subsection is devoted to constructing a Lascar generic system for the Polish group
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}
(as a closed subgroup of the Polish group
\mathrm{G}
). To formulate the problem in the framework introduced in the previous subsection, let
\mathbb{L}
be the language of
\mathbb{L}_{A}
augmented with countably many constant symbols naming elements of
I
. Then by considering the expansion
\mathcal{M}^{*}
of
\mathcal{M}
to the language
\mathbb{L}
, the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}^{*})
is precisely
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}
. Our goal is to construct a Lascar generic system for this automorphism group. For this purpose, we will generalize the notions of an existentially closed and a Lascar generic automorphism:

Definition 3
.

(
n\in\omega
) Suppose
\mathcal{K}\prec\mathcal{M}
is
I
-small, and
(g_{0},...,g_{n})\in(\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{K}))^{n+1}
. We say
(g_{0},...,g_{n})
is
I
-existentially closed
(abbreviated by
I
-e.c.
) if for every tuple
{(f_{0},...,f_{n})\in(\mathrm{G}_{(I)})^{n+1}}
such that
f_{k}\upharpoonright_{K}=g_{k}\upharpoonright_{K}
for all
k=0,...,n
, for every
\mathbb{L}_{A}
-formula
\varphi(x,y_{0},...,y_{n},\bar{z})
, and for each
\bar{c}\in K
, if
\mathcal{M}\models\exists x\ \varphi(x,f_{0}(x),...,f_{n}(x),\bar{c})
, then
{\mathcal{M}\models\varphi(d,g_{0}(d),...,g_{n}(d),\bar{c})}
for some
d\in K
.

Let
\mathrm{EC}_{I}(\mathcal{K})
be the family of all finite tuples of
I
-e.c. elements of
\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{K})
.

First, using an argument analogous to the one establishing the existence of existentially closed automorphisms when the standard cut is strong in
\mathcal{M}
, we will prove that if
I
is a strong cut of
\mathcal{M}
there exist plenty of
I
-existentially closed automorphisms in
\mathcal{M}
:

Theorem 3.4
.

(
n\in\omega
) Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a countable recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}
,
I
is a strong cut of
\mathcal{M}
, and
a,b_{0},...,b_{n}\in M
such that
\mathrm{tp}(a,i)=\mathrm{tp}(b_{k},i)
for every
k=0,...,n
and for all
i\in I
. Then there exists some
I
-small elementary submodel
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{M}
and some
I
-e.c. tuple
(g_{0},...,g_{n})\in(\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{K}))^{n+1}
such that
a,b_{0},...,b_{n}\in K
and
g_{k}(a)=b_{k}
for every
k=0,...,n
.

Proof.

We will prove the theorem for the case
n=0
, and the remaining cases follow by a similar argument. So for simplicity, let
b:=b_{0}
.

Let
S
be an inductive satisfaction class for
\mathcal{M}
such that
(\mathcal{M};S)
is also recursively saturated. So by Lemma 2.2(1), there exists some
I
-small elementary submodel
(\mathcal{K};S^{\prime})
of
(\mathcal{M};S)
containing
a,b
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\{(\alpha)_{i}:\ i\in I\}
for some
\alpha\in M
. Moreover, let
a=(\alpha)_{i_{a}}
and
b=(\alpha)_{i_{b}}
for some
i_{a},i_{b}\in I
. Now, we define:

\Delta(x,y):=\mathrm{max}\{z:\ \forall\langle w,i\rangle<z\ S(\bm{\phi}_{w}(x,i))\leftrightarrow S(\bm{\phi}_{w}(y,i))\};

and

\Lambda(\langle i,j\rangle):=\Delta((\alpha)_{i},(\alpha)_{j}).

Since
I
is strong in
\mathcal{M}
, there exists some
\epsilon>I
such that
\Lambda(i)\in I
iff
\Lambda(i)<\epsilon
for all
i\in I
. Then, let
\langle\varphi_{r}((\alpha)_{i},x,y):\ r,i\in M\rangle
be a canonical enumeration of
\mathbb{L}_{A}
-formulas of the given form with
(\alpha)_{i}
s as parameters. Moreover, let
\Theta(i,u,v,x,y)
be the following
\mathbb{L}
-formula (where
\mathbb{L}:=\mathbb{L}_{A}\cup\{S\}
):

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S(\varphi_{(i)_{0}}((\alpha)_{(i)_{1}},(\alpha)_{x},(\alpha)_{y}))\ \wedge\\
\forall z,w\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(\alpha)_{z}=(\alpha)_{u}^{\frown}(\alpha)_{x}\ \wedge\ (\alpha)_{w}=(\alpha)_{v}^{\frown}(\alpha)_{y}\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\Lambda(\langle z,w\rangle)>\epsilon\end{array}\right).

Now, we inductively define
\mathbb{L}
-terms
\Omega(i)
and
\bar{\Omega}(i)
in
\mathcal{M}
as follows:

\Omega(0):=\langle i_{a},i_{b}\rangle
, and

\bar{\Omega}(0):=\langle\textbf{u},\textbf{v}\rangle\in I
such that
(\alpha)_{\textbf{u}}=\langle a\rangle
and
(\alpha)_{\textbf{v}}=\langle b\rangle
.

and

\Omega(i+1):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mu_{\langle x,y\rangle}\Theta(i,(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0},(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1},x,y)&\text{if }\scriptstyle(\mathcal{M};S)\models\exists x,y\ \Theta(i,(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0},(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1},x,y)\\
\langle i_{a},i_{b}\rangle&\text{o.w.}\end{array}\right.

\bar{\Omega}(i+1):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mu_{\langle u,v\rangle}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(\alpha)_{u}=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0}})^{\frown}(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i+1))_{0}}\ \wedge\\
(\alpha)_{v}=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1}})^{\frown}(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i+1))_{1}}\end{array}\right)&\text{if }\scriptsize\exists u,v\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(\alpha)_{u}=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0}})^{\frown}(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i+1))_{0}}\ \wedge\\
(\alpha)_{v}=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1}})^{\frown}(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i+1))_{1}}\end{array}\right)\\
\epsilon&\text{o.w.}\end{array}\right.

Again, since
I
is strong in
\mathcal{M}
there exists some
\eta,\varepsilon>I
such that
\Omega(i)\in I
iff
{\Omega(i)<\varepsilon}
, and
\bar{\Omega}(i)\in I
iff
\bar{\Omega}(i)<\eta
for all
i\in I
. Note that
\Omega(i)<\varepsilon
and
\bar{\Omega}(i)<\eta
for all
i\in I
: we use induction to show this fact. Suppose we have shown
\Omega(i)<\varepsilon
and
\bar{\Omega}(i)<\eta
for
i\in I
. By Lemma 2.2(3), it suffices to show that
\Omega(i+1)<\varepsilon
. Suppose
{(\mathcal{M};S)\models\exists x,y\ \Theta(i,(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0},(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1},x,y)}
(for the other case, it is clear that
\Omega(i+1)<\varepsilon
). So, for every
s\in I
it holds that:

(1):\ \ \

(\mathcal{M};S)\models\exists x,y\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S(\varphi_{(i)_{0}}((\alpha)_{(i)_{1}},x,y))\ \wedge\\
\forall u,v\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0}})^{\frown}x\ \wedge\\
v=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1}})^{\frown}y\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\forall\langle r,z\rangle<s\ S(\bm{\phi}_{r}(u,z))\leftrightarrow S(\bm{\phi}_{r}(v,z))\end{array}\right)
.

So since
(\mathcal{K};S^{\prime})\prec(\mathcal{M};S)
, statement (1) implies that for every
s\in I
we have:

(2):\ \ \

(\mathcal{K};S^{\prime})\models\exists x,y\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S^{\prime}(\varphi_{(i)_{0}}((\alpha)_{(i)_{1}},x,y))\ \wedge\\
\forall u,v\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{0}})^{\frown}x\ \wedge\\
v=((\alpha)_{(\bar{\Omega}(i))_{1}})^{\frown}y\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\forall\langle r,z\rangle<s\ S^{\prime}(\bm{\phi}_{r}(u,z))\leftrightarrow S^{\prime}(\bm{\phi}_{r}(v,z))\end{array}\right)
.

As a result, by using Overspill principle over
I
in
(\mathcal{K};S^{\prime})
and Lemma 2.2(3), we infer that
\Omega(i+1)\in I
.

Now, we define the following recursive type for every
s\in M
:

\displaystyle p_{s}(x,y):=

\displaystyle\{\forall i<s\ \phi(x,i)\leftrightarrow\phi(y,i):\ \phi\text{ is an }\mathbb{L}_{A}\text{-formula}\}\cup

\displaystyle\left\{\forall i<s\ ((x)_{i}=(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i))_{0}}\ \wedge\ (y)_{i}=(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i))_{1}})\right\}.

First, we will prove that there exists some
s>I
such that
p_{s}(x,y)
is finitely satisfiable. For this purpose, define:

\Psi(m):=\max\left\{s:\exists x,y\ \psi(x,y,s,m,\alpha)\right\}
, where
\psi(x,y,s,m,\alpha)
is the following
\mathbb{L}
-formula:

\forall i<s\ \forall z<m\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(S(\bm{\phi}_{z}(x,i))\leftrightarrow S(\bm{\phi}_{z}(y,i)))\ \wedge\\
((x)_{i}=(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i))_{0}}\ \wedge\ (y)_{i}=(\alpha)_{(\Omega(i))_{1}})\end{array}\right).

Let
e>I
be the witness of strength of
I
in
\mathcal{M}
for the definable function
\Psi
. We will show that
p_{e}(x,y)
is finitely satisfiable. For this purpose, it suffices to prove that
\Psi(m)>e
for every
m\in\omega
. So, suppose
m\in\omega
be arbitrary. By the way we defined
\Omega(i)
and by using induction in
(\mathcal{M};S)
, we can show that
{(\mathcal{M};S)\models\exists x,y\ \psi(x,y,s,m,\alpha)}
for every
s\in I
. So, by Overspill principle over
I
in
(\mathcal{M};S)
, we have
\Psi(m)>e
.

As a result, we can find some
\lambda,\xi\in M
such that
(\lambda,\xi)
realizes
p_{e}(x,y)
. Now, put:

g:=\bigcup_{i\in I}(\lambda)_{i}\mapsto(\xi)_{i}.

Then, we have the following claim:

Claim:
For every
m\in\omega
and for each
i\in I
, if
\mathcal{M}\models\exists x\ \varphi_{m}((\alpha)_{i},x,f(x))
for some
f\in\mathrm{G}_{I}
such that
g\subseteq f
, then
\mathcal{M}\models\varphi_{m}((\alpha)_{i},(\lambda)_{j},(\xi)_{j})
for some
j\in I
.

The claim implies that, first
\mathrm{Dom}(g)=K
: note that since
\Omega(i)\in I
for all
i\in I
, it holds that
\mathrm{Dom}(g)\subseteq K
. To see
K\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}(g)
, let
i\in I
be arbitrary and consider the
\mathbb{L}_{A}
-formula
(x=(\alpha)_{i}\ \wedge\ y=y)
and
f\in\mathrm{G}_{(I)}
such that
f(\lambda)=\xi
in the claim. So we conclude that
(\alpha)_{i}\in\mathrm{Dom}(g)
. In a similar way,
\mathrm{Range}(g)=K
. Secondly, the claim implies that
g\in\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{K})
is
I
-e.c.

In order to prove the claim, suppose we have the assumptions of the Claim. Thus, by iterating similar arguments to statements (1) and (2), we conclude that
\mathcal{M}\models\varphi_{m}((\alpha)_{i},(\lambda)_{j},(\xi)_{j})
where
j:=\Omega(\langle m,i\rangle+1)
.
∎

Now, we will adapt the notion of a Lascar generic automorphism:

Definition 4
.

(
n\in\omega
) A tuple
(g_{0},...,g_{n})\in(\mathrm{G}_{(I)})^{n+1}
is called
Lascar
I
-generic
if:

(i)

For every finite tuple
\bar{a}\in M
there exists some
I
-small elementary submodel
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\bar{a}\in K
and
(g_{0}\upharpoonright_{K},...,g_{n}\upharpoonright_{K})\in(\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{K}))^{n+1}
is
I
-e.c.

(ii)

For every two
I
-small elementary submodel
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{M}
, and for each tuple
(f_{0},...,f_{n})\in(\mathrm{Aut}_{(I)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{L}))^{n+1}
, if the following conditions
(\spadesuit)
hold, then there exists some
h\in\mathrm{G}_{(K)}
such that
f_{k}=g_{k}^{h}\upharpoonright_{L}

.

, and

5
The cofinality of
(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)}

As mentioned in the Introduction, the cofinality of a group
\mathrm{G}
, denoted by
\mathrm{cf}(\mathrm{G})
, is the least cardinal number
\kappa
such that
\mathrm{G}
is the union of an increasing chain of
\kappa
many proper subgroups. In
[
10
]
, Kossak and Schmerl proved that
for a countable recursively saturated model
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathrm{PA}
the standard cut is strong in
\mathcal{M}
iff
\mathrm{cf}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))>\aleph_{0}
.
In this this section, using similar arguments, we generalize their result to a given cut which is not
\omega
-coded from above:

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a model of
\mathrm{PA}
and
I
is a cut of
\mathcal{M}
. We say
I
is
\omega
-coded from above
if there exists some
a\in M
such that
{I=\inf\{(a)_{n}:\ n\in\omega\}}
.
It is easy to see that if
\mathcal{M}
is recursively saturated and
I
is not
\omega
-coded from above in
\mathcal{M}
, then for every
a\in M\setminus\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{M};I)
, there exists some
b\in M
such that
a\neq b
and
\mathrm{tp}(a,i)=\mathrm{tp}(b,i)
for all
i\in I
. In particular,
(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I)}\neq(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}))_{(I\cup\{a\})}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a countable and recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}
,
{\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})}
, and
I
is a cut of
\mathcal{M}
which is not
\omega
-coded from above. Then

is strong in
\mathcal{M}
iff for every open subgroup
\mathrm{H}
of
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}

such that
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}=\langle\mathrm{H}\cup\{g\}\rangle

Let
S
be an inductive satisfaction class for
\mathcal{M}
such that

I
is strong in
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathrm{H}
is an open subgroup of
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}

a\in M
such that
\mathrm{G}_{(I\cup\{a\})}\leq\mathrm{H}

\mathrm{tp}(a,i)=\mathrm{tp}(b,i)

and
\mathrm{G}_{(I)}=\langle\mathrm{G}_{(I\cup\{a\})}\cup\mathrm{G}_{(I\cup\{b\})}\rangle

. So

, first let

I
in
\mathcal{M}
, there exists some

such that

iff
\langle r,i\rangle<e
for all

\displaystyle p_{s}(x):=

\forall k<r\ \forall i<s\ (\Phi(\langle r,i\rangle)>e\rightarrow S(\bm{\phi}_{r}(a,x,i)))\end{array}\right)\right\}.

which witnesses the strength of
