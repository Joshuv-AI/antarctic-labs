Introduction

In the paper we analyze
subspaces of modulation spaces
of the form

M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
where the short-time Fourier
transforms of involved functions and
distributions should obey certain mild
vanishing properties at infinity.
(See
[
34
]

or Section
1
for notations.)
For polynomial moderate weights we show that
these subspaces, denoted by

M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
are exactly the completions of
the Schwartz space with respect
to the
M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}

norm. For
general weights, we show that the same
holds true after the Schwartz spaces are
replaced by suitable Fourier invariant
Gelfand-Shilov spaces, which are densely
embedded in the Schwartz spaces.

In the second part of the paper, we
apply our results to deduce compactness
for pseudo-differential operators
with symbols in
M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}

spaces, when
q\in(0,1]
.
These investigations also include
compactness investigations for Toeplitz
operators with symbols in

M^{\sharp,\infty}_{(\omega)}
spaces.

In the family of modulation spaces,
the so-called Sjöstrand classes
is of peculiar interest, especially
in pseudo-differential calculus and
Gabor frame theory. For any
q\in(0,1]

and suitable weight function
\omega
on
the phase space
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, the
corresponding Sjöstrand class

M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists
of all functions or (ultra-)distributions

f
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\|f\|_{M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\left\|\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\,\cdot\,)\omega(x,\,\cdot\,)|\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}<\infty.

(0.1)

Here
V_{\phi}f
is the short-time Fourier
transform of
f
, with respect to the (fixed)
window function
\phi
.

In pseudo-differential calculus,
it is often convenient to use Sjöstrand
classes and some of their subspaces as
symbol classes for the pseudo-differential
operators.
Firstly, such operators possess
convenient mapping properties, especially
when acting on (other) modulation spaces.
(See for classical
but restrictive results, or
for recent and general
results on this.) We remark that these mapping
properties immediately give the classical
Calderon-Vailancourt’s theorem. That is,
if
a
is smooth
and bounded together with its
derivatives, then the
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}(\mathfrak{a})
is
continuous on
L^{2}
. (See .)

Secondly, for suitable restrictions on
the weight function
\omega
, corresponding
family of pseudo-differential operators
are inverse closed on
L^{2}
, which can
also be expressed that they form a so-called
Wiener algebra. For example, if
\omega=1
and

a\in M^{\infty,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
satisfies that

\operatorname{Op}(\mathfrak{a})
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
then its inverse
\operatorname{Op}(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}
is equal to

\operatorname{Op}(\mathfrak{b})
, for some
a\in M^{\infty,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

(see e. g.
[
29
]
).

Thirdly, any Gabor frame operator in
Gabor analysis is a pseudo-differential
operator with symbols in Sjöstrand
classes. Some tracks on
this property can be found
already in the analysis in
[
40
]
,
and an explicit verification of
this in unweighted case, is given in

[
29
]
. Because of all these
roles and properties, several attentions
are paid to Sjöstrand classes.

There are useful modulation space which stays in the
shadow in the limelight of Sjöstrand classes.
In the paper we focus on one type of them,
where, in additional to the conditions in
(
0.1
), one also imposes
the condition

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|\sup_{|x|>R}|V_{\phi}f(x,\,\cdot\,)\omega(x,\,\cdot\,)|\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=0.

(0.2)

For convenience we let

M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

be the set of all
f
which satisfy
these conditions. That is,

M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\equiv\{\,f\in M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\,;\,f\ \text{satisfies \eqref{Eq:DefSjosSubClassIntro}}\,\}.

We perform detailed analysis of

spaces. In the

Thereafter we apply our results to show

and

1.1.
Weight functions

For non-negative functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
,

g_{1}(x)\leq C\cdot g_{2}(x)

g_{1}\lesssim g_{2}\lesssim g_{1}

A

\mathbf{R}^{d}

\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

If there is a weight
v

\mathbf{R}^{d}

\omega(x+y)\leq C\omega(x)v(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

\omega
is called

moderate
, or
v
-moderate

By (
1.1
)

C^{-1}v(-x)^{-1}\leq\omega(x)\leq Cv(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(1.2)

for some
C\geq 1
.
We let
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set of all moderate weights on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
We say that the weight
\omega
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is polynomially moderate
if

\omega(x+y)\leq C\omega(x)(1+|y|)^{r},\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(
1.1
)
′

for some constants
C,r>0
. The set of polynomially moderate weights
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is denoted by

. Evidently,

\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(1.3)

We also consider various classes of exponential
type moderate weights.
In fact, suppose that
s>0
. Then the weight

\omega
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is called
s
-moderate
if there are constants
C,r>0

\omega(x+y)\leq C\omega(x)e^{r|y|^{\frac{1}{s}}},\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

If for every

-moderate.
The sets of

-moderate

We say that a weight

v(x+y)\leq v(x)v(y)\quad\text{and}\quad v(-x)=v(x),\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

If

Therefore, if

We also observe that (

\displaystyle=\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{P}_{0,s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{P}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad s_{1}<1<s_{2}.

In the sequel,

1.2.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

We recall that the Gelfand-Shilov space

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of all

f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{[h]}\equiv\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}}\left(\frac{\|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}f\|_{L^{\infty}}}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}\alpha!\beta!}\right)

is finite for every
h>0
. It follows that

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is a Fréchet space
with projective topology defined by the
semi-norms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{[h]}
.

One has that
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is
continuously embedded and dense
in the Schwartz space
of smooth rapidly decreasing functions

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, is invariant under
Fourier transforms, and is an algebra
under multiplications and under
convolutions.

It can be shown that
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
satisfies
the hypothesis of
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
,
wherefrom we get the following.

Lemma 1.2
.

Suppose
f\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then
for some
g_{j},h_{j}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
j=1,2
,
it holds

f=g_{1}\cdot g_{2}=h_{1}*h_{2}.

In what follows we let
\mathscr{F}
be the
Fourier transform which takes the form

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx

when
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the usual
scalar product
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The map
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to a homeomorphism on the space of tempered distributions

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
to a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and restricts
to a homeomorphism on the Schwartz space

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
We observe that with our choice of the Fourier
transform, the usual
convolution identity for the Fourier transform
takes the forms

\mathscr{F}(f\cdot g)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{F}(f*g)=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{f}\cdot\widehat{g}

when

Since we are interested in general weights
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, which is natural when dealing with weights of polynomial growth,
we consider Gelfand-Shilov spaces
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and their dual spaces of (ultra-)distributions

and

,

In order to avoid technical issues related to the usual definition of the spaces of Gelfand-Shilov type and their distribution spaces, we introduce

and

in terms of

and

, then

) (

), if and only if

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad|f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-r|x|^{\frac{1}{s}}},

\displaystyle\ |\widehat{f}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-r|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}},

for all

\displaystyle\ r

\displaystyle>0,

(1.11)

and

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad|f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-r|x|^{\frac{1}{s}}},

\displaystyle\ |\widehat{f}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-r|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}},

for some

\displaystyle\ r

\displaystyle>0,

[

1.4.
Modulation spaces

For the definition of classical
modulation spaces, we need to discuss
mixed norm spaces of Lebesgue types.
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a
(Lebesgue) measurable set in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
and let
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be the set of all
complex-valued (Lebesgue)
measurable functions on
\Omega
.

For any
p,q\in(0,\infty]
, the mixed norm
space

L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

of Lebesgue type, is given by

\displaystyle L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle=\{\,F\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\,;\,\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}<\infty\,\}

(1.27)

for some
p,q\in(0,\infty]
and

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Here

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\|G_{F,\omega,p}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d})},

where

\displaystyle G_{F,\omega,p}(\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv\|F(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\omega(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})},\qquad F\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

We put

L^{p,q}=L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}\quad\text{when}\quad\omega\equiv 1.

We also put
L^{p}_{(\omega)}=L^{p,p}_{(\omega)}
,
and observe that
L^{p}=L^{p,p}
.

In our investigations, (weighted versions of)
the subclass
L^{\sharp}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
of

L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
will play a fundamental role.
The space
L^{\sharp}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of
all
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{R\to\infty}(\underset{|x|\geq R}{\sup}\,|f(x)|)=0,

and if

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
then

f\in L^{\sharp}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

if
f\omega\in L^{\sharp}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
In addition,

L^{\sharp,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
denotes the set of all

F\in L^{\infty,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

which satisfy

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|\sup_{|x|\geq R}\left|F(x,\cdot)\right|\right\|_{L^{q}}=0,

and if
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
then

\displaystyle F\in L^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle F\cdot\omega\in L^{\sharp,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
,
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and

\phi\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
.

(1)

The classical modulation space

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists
of all

such that

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\|V_{\phi}f\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

(1.28)

is finite. The topology of
M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is defined by
the quasi-norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}
;

\|H_{f,\omega,p}\|_{L^{q}}<\infty,\quad\text{where}\quad H_{f,\omega,p}(\xi)\equiv\|V_{\phi}f(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\cdot\omega(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\|_{L^{p}}.

(1.29)

(2)

the modulation spaces

M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of
all

f\in M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|\sup_{|x|\geq R}\Big|V_{\phi}f(x,\,\cdot\,)\omega(x,\,\cdot\,)\Big|\right\|_{L^{q}}=0.

(1.30)

The topology is defined through
the (quasi-)norm

\|f\|_{M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}}\equiv\|f\|_{M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}},\qquad f\in M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(1.31)

The (classical) modulation spaces, are
essentially
introduced in
[

]
by Feichtinger.
(See e.g.
[

By (

is strongly related to

M^{\infty,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
We note that modulation spaces similar to

M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are studied
in
[
7
]
.

Evidently, by Definition

it follows
that

f\in M^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

if and only if
V_{\phi}f\in L^{\sharp,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

1.5.
Invariant quasi-Banach
function spaces

The functional

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{B}}
, defined on the
vector space
\mathscr{B}
is called a

quasi-norm
of order

r_{0}\in(0,1]
, or an
r_{0}
-norm
, if the conditions

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}},

\displaystyle\quad f,g

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{B}.

(1.32)

\displaystyle\|\alpha\cdot f\|_{\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle=|\alpha|\cdot\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\quad\alpha

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{C},\quad f\in\mathscr{B},

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f=0,

hold true. A vector space which contains
a quasi-norm (of order
r_{0}
)
is called a quasi-norm space (of order

r_{0}
), or an
r_{0}
-normed space.
A complete quasi-norm space
(of order
r_{0}
) is called a

quasi-Banach space
(of order
r_{0}
),
or an
r_{0}
-Banach space
.

The quasi-Banach
space
\mathscr{B}
is called a

quasi-Banach function
space
(or
QBF space
) on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
, whenever
\mathscr{B}
is
contained in

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
It is here
understood that the zero element
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(which is
an equivalent class), is the zero
element in
\mathscr{B}
.

We always assume that the QBF spaces
are
solid
, which is described
in the following definition.
(See e.g. (A.3) in
[
15
]
.)

Definition 1.8
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a

QBF space
on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Then
\mathscr{B}
is
called
solid
, if the
following conditions hold:

(1)

for any

f,g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

which satisfy

g\in\mathscr{B}
and
|f|\leq|g|
, then
f\in\mathscr{B}
and

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathscr{B}},

(1.33)

for some constant
C>0
which is
independent of
f
and
g
above;

(2)

for any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
there is a constant
c>0
and a function

f\in\mathscr{B}
such that
|f|>c
on a convex
set in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
which is not a zero set,
and which contains
x
.

In the following definition
we put needed restrictions
on QBF spaces.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a solid QBF space of
order
r_{0}\in(0,1]
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,

C>0
be the same as in
(
1.33
),
and let

be submultiplicative.

(1)

The space
\mathscr{B}

is called a

solid translation invariant

\mathbf{R}^{d}

(with respect to
r_{0}
and
v_{0}
), or

invariant QBF space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
if for any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
and

, then
f(\,\cdot\,-x)\in\mathscr{B}
, and

\|f(\,\cdot\,-x)\|_{\mathscr{B}}\leq Cv_{0}(x)\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\text{;}

(1.34)

(2)

The space
\mathscr{B}

is called a
solid translation
invariant Banach function space on

\mathbf{R}^{d}

(with respect to
v_{0}
), or

invariant BF space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,
if
\mathscr{B}
is a Banach space and
an invariant QBF space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

with respect to
v_{0}
.

(3)

The space
\mathscr{B}

is called a
solid translation
invariant normal quasi-Banach function
space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
(with respect to
r_{0}
and
v_{0}
), or

a normal QBF space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,

\mathscr{B}_{0}
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

with respect to
v_{0}^{r_{0}}
such that

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\equiv\|\,|f|^{r_{0}}\,\|_{\mathscr{B}_{0}}^{1/r_{0}},\quad f\in\mathscr{B}.

(1.35)

Evidently,
\mathscr{B}
in
Definition
1.9
(3),
is an invariant QBF space with respect
to
r_{0}
and
v_{0}
.

Suppose that

\mathscr{B}
is a quasi-Banach function
space on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
with respect
to
r_{0}\in(0,1]

5.

First we recall some facts about distribution

Suppose

uniquely defined by the formula

from

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

[
54
]

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

Next we recall the definition of pseudo-differential

\mathbf{R}

[

\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

Compactness properties for

In this section we first review some facts about
Toeplitz operators. Thereafter we deduce
continuity for Toeplitz operators with symbols
in suitable modulation spaces, when acting
between other modulation spaces. As in

[
6
,
43
,
30
,
44
,
50
]
,
the main idea is to
express the Toeplitz operators as
pseudo-differential operators
with symbols being a convolution
between the Toeplitz symbol
and a convenient function.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Then the Toeplitz
operator
\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})
,
with symbol
\mathfrak{a}
, and window functions

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, is the continuous
operator on
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, defined by
the formula

(\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})f_{1},f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=(\mathfrak{a}\cdot V_{\phi_{1}}f_{1},V_{\phi_{2}}f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}

(6.1)

when
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. That is,

\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})

\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})f=V_{\phi_{2}}^{*}(\mathfrak{a}\cdot V_{\phi_{1}}f).

(6.2)

The definition of
\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})

extends uniquely to a continuous operator from

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
For convenience we write

\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1}}(\mathfrak{a})

for

\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{1}}(\mathfrak{a})
.

We may interpret the operator in
(

the fact that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{a}\ast u)\quad\text{when}\quad u=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\check{W}_{\phi_{2},\phi_{1}},

(6.3)

which follows by straight-forward application of
Fourier’s inversion formula. Here

\check{F}(x,\xi)=F(-x,-\xi)

on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
. By

the mappings

(6.4)

from
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

Proposition 6.1

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\ \phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\text{;}

Evidently, for the symbol classes in

Proof.

The assertion (1) and its proof are given
in e. g.
[
24
,
32
,
43
]
.

The assertion (2) essentially also
follows from these facts. In fact,
suppose that

and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Choose

|\mathfrak{a}(X)|\leq\varepsilon(1+\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}})^{-1}(1+\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}})^{-1},\qquad|X|\geq R.

0,&|X|\geq R,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{a}_{2}(X)=\begin{cases}0,&|X|<R,\\[4.30554pt]

\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{1}=\mathfrak{a}_{2}
