Executive Summary
This report presents the initial 24 hour results for IER-634, the 2026 international nuclear accident
dosimetry intercomparison performed at the Armed Forces Radiobiology Research Institute
(AFRRI). Nine groups deployed dedicated dosimetry (ﬁve DOE laboratories, two international
groups, one DOW group, and one private industry group) in a week-long exercise in early June 2026.
For these groups, three days of irradiations were performed, and participants reported results
within 24 hours of the irradiations on the ﬁrst two days. Results were compared to the reference
values and the performance criteria of ANSI/HPS N13.3-2013 (R2025). 46% of the reported results
met the performance criteria on the ﬁrst irradiation day and 100% met the criteria the second
irradiation day. This suggests the intercomparisons remain valuable for identifying gross,
correctible errors in the NAD system. Additionally, two biodosimetry labs (one DOW and one DOE)
participated in the exercise remotely and received irradiated blood samples from two irradiation
days and reported results outside the 24 hour period. For the DOE laboratories held to the
ANSI/HPS N13.3 standard, the intercomparison can serve to satisfy the performance veriﬁcation
requirement in §6.1 of the standard.

Introduction
From June 8 to June 12, 2026, the Armed Forces Radiobiology Research Institute (AFRRI) in
Bethesda, MD hosted an international nuclear accident dosimetry intercomparison. AFRRI, a US
Department of War facility operated by the Uniformed Services University, houses a TRIGA reactor
suitable for high-dose neutron exposures. This is the second nuclear accident intercomparison to
take place at AFRRI. The previous exercise occurred in 2024 as part of IER-6021. The exercise plan
and dedicated dosimetry for each group is described in the IER-634 CED 3a report2.

This exercise was designed to test the participant’s nuclear accident dosimetry systems under two
irradiation conditions. The ﬁrst (Tuesday) was a known irradiation where the dosimeter placement
on the phantoms (e.g. front of phantom, back of phantom) and phantom locations with respect to
the irradiation source were provided to the participants. The second (Wednesday) irradiation was
blinded, where participants were only informed about dosimeter placement but not phantom
locations. The phantoms for these irradiations were either saline-ﬁlled humanoid phantoms, which
represents a typical worker exposure geometry, or aluminum plates, which provides a surrogate for
in-air measurement. The phantoms were placed in a conﬁguration previously characterized in
August 2023 as part of IER-484; the characterized positions are depicted in Figure 1. Participants
reported preliminary neutron dose estimates 24 hours after the exposure and were compared to the

A third, optional set of irradiations was performed on Thursday to allow testing of experimental
dosimeter construction or activation of personal items. The phantom conﬁguration was the same
as the ﬁrst irradiation. No results were required to be reported.

Five Department of Energy (DOE) groups, two international groups, one DOW group, and one private
industry group deployed dedicated dosimeters for the intercomparison; two additional
biodosimetry laboratories participated by receiving irradiated human blood samples. The DOE
groups were Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), Hanford Site, Los Alamos National
Laboratory (LANL), the Y-12 National Security Complex (Y-12), and Idaho National Laboratory (INL).
The international participants were the Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) from the UK and the
Autorité de Sûreté Nucléaire et de Radioprotection (ASNR) from France. The DOW participant was
the Naval Dosimetry Laboratory (NDC). The private industry group was SHINE. The two
biodosimetry laboratories were Dr. William Blakely’s group from AFRRI and Dr. Adayabalam Balajee
from the Cytogenetic Biodosimetry Laboratory, a REAC/TS team based at the Oak Ridge Institute for
Science and Education (ORISE). Both biodosimetry laboratories can serve as national resources for
the DOE or DOW.

Irradiation Conﬁguration
For each experimental set, a setup run without phantoms was performed to calibrate AFRRI’s
stand-mounted paired ion chamber and ceiling- mounted paired ion chamber system to the
desired dose target and dose rate at a reference location. The stand-mounted ion chambers were
placed at 150 cm distance from the core, and using the characterization developed by AFRRI sta
and IER-484, the doses at the phantom positions were determined. Target doses are provided in
Table 1.

Doses and dose rates between the Tuesday and Wednesday irradiation were selected to provide
nearly equivalent irradiation time to not inadvertently reveal the blinded doses.

but di erent phantoms/stands conﬁguration. However, the ﬁrst irradiation experienced a reactor
power overshoot during the ramp phase, resulting in overexposure; this delivered approximately

All BOMABs had hair samples in glassine envelopes taped to the sides, top, and back of the head.
The central, chest bottle of each BOMAB was ﬁlled with saline; all other bottles were ﬁlled with
deionized water. After each BOMAB irradiation, each group had the option of receiving saline or hair
samples for activation analysis. Additionally, each group was able to send several members to
directly survey the activated BOMABs following the irradiation and NAD removal.

For the Tuesday and Wednesday runs, human 10 mL blood samples were collected each morning
from consenting volunteers. The blood was stored in dark green top sodium heparin tubes and
stored at 18-25°C prior to irradiation. Following the irradiation, samples were counted in-house by
the AFRRI sta or sent to ORISE via FedEx. For the BOMAB irradiation, the blood was mounted in a
modiﬁed head phantom. The head was a solid acrylic cylinder with two holes to contain the blood
tubes (Figure 2). For the FIA irradiations, tubes were taped to the stand plate.

Results
ANSI/HPS N13.3 §4.1.2 requires that preliminary total absorbed dose values be provided within
24 hours following an exposure. For the purpose of this exercise, only neutron dose was requested
to be reported within 24 hours because most participants do not have access to a dosimeter reader
at the intercomparison location. Even though the majority of groups requested saline, hair, or direct
phantom measurements, results from these methods were not reported within the 24 hour
timeframe. The NDC only deployed dosimeters on a single BOMAB and FIA stand for each day.
While they provided results to the exercise coordinators within the required 24 hour period, they will
not be reported here. Results were not required for the Thursday exposures. For the biodosimetry
laboratories, results were not available within 24 hours due to the long duration required for
processing cell samples. The group numbers were arbitrarily assigned to anonymize the results.
Results for the Tuesday, unblinded irradiations are presented in Table 5; results for the Wednesday,
blinded irradiation are presented in Table 6. Green highlighted cells indicate a performance
statistic, B, meeting the ANSI requirement. The performance statistic is the relative di erence
between the measured dose and the delivered dose.

Discussion and Conclusion
The unblinded irradiation performed on Tuesday had overall low passing rates, with only 11 of 24
(46%) of the reported results meeting the performance criteria. Several groups (4,5 and 6) reported
signiﬁcantly erroneous results. Discussion with group leads identiﬁed multiple causes: new group
members, NAD system changes, or ﬁrst-time participation. For the blinded irradiation however, all
groups met the performance criteria. This suggests the intercomparisons remain valuable for
identifying gross, correctible errors in the NAD system. Additionally, for the DOE laboratories held to
the ANSI/HPS N13.3 standard, the intercomparison can serve to satisfy the performance
veriﬁcation requirement in §6.1 of the standard.

Due to low reporting of non-dedicated NAD results, future intercomparisons should emphasize
biodosimetry (blood/saline, hair, direct dose rate) measurements over dedicated NADs.
