Mapping class group action on the cohomology of the SL n \operatorname{SL}_{n} character variety
Anne Larsen

Abstract
. We describe the mapping class group action on the cohomology of the twisted SL n \operatorname{SL}_{n} -character variety of a surface Σ g \Sigma_{g} of genus g g . Our main tool is a relative version of the endoscopic decomposition of Maulik-Shen [ 16 ] ; this allows us to reduce the problem to the mapping class group action on the cohomology of a canonical finite cover of Σ g \Sigma_{g} , which was studied by Looijenga [ 13 ] .



1.
Introduction

Fix relatively prime integers
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, as well as a genus
g\geq 2
. In this paper we examine the “twisted” character variety of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
-local systems on a once-punctured genus
g
surface
\Sigma_{g}\setminus p
with monodromy
e^{2\pi id/n}\Id_{n}
on the loop
\gamma_{p}
around the puncture, i.e., the quotient

M^{\operatorname{SL}}:=\{\phi\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p),\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})):\phi(\gamma_{p})=e^{2\pi id/n}\Id_{n}\}/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

\cong\{A_{1},B_{1},\cdots,A_{g},B_{g}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):[A_{1},B_{1}]\cdots[A_{g},B_{g}]=e^{2\pi id/n}\Id_{n}\}/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}),

where the adjoint group
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts on the target
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by conjugation.
The mapping class group
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
acts naturally on this space by pullback of local systems, i.e., by its outer action on
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
, and our goal is to describe the induced action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
.

In order to state the theorem, we recall that subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}
of a group
G
are said to be
commensurable
if
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
is of finite index in each
H_{i}
. Also, let
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g}^{n}\to\Sigma_{g}
be the finite cover corresponding to the canonical surjection
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}/n)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The kernel of the
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}},\mathbb{Z})
is commensurable to the kernel of the
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
action on
H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g}^{n},\mathbb{Z})
.

By work of Looijenga on the mapping class group action on cohomology of finite abelian covers of
\Sigma_{g}

[
13
]
, this then leads to the following concrete description of the algebraic monodromy group (i.e., the identity component of the Zariski closure of the image of
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
in
\operatorname{GL}(H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}},\mathbb{C}))
):

Corollary 1.2
.

If
g>2
, or if
g=2
and
(n,6)=1
, then the algebraic monodromy group of
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
acting on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}},\mathbb{C})
is the commutator subgroup of
\Sp_{\Gal}(H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g}^{n},\mathbb{C}))
, the subgroup of invertible linear transformations respecting both the intersection form and the Galois action of
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}/n)
on
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g}^{n}
.

Although the discussion until now has been purely topological, our proof goes via the nonabelian Hodge correspondence, which provides a diffeomorphism between
M^{\operatorname{SL}}
and the moduli space of stable
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
-Higgs bundles of degree
d
on any complex algebraic curve
C
of genus
g
. In general, moduli spaces of Higgs bundles tend to be more amenable to algebro-geometric methods, and there are results on the cohomology of character varieties for which the only known proof uses the Higgs bundle description in an essential way.

For example, if we consider the analogously defined twisted character variety
M^{\operatorname{GL}}
, work of Markman on moduli of stable sheaves on surfaces, as applied to moduli spaces of Higgs bundles
[
15
, Theorem 3]
, implies the following:

Fact 1.3
.

The kernel of the
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{GL}},\mathbb{Z})
is the kernel of the surjection

\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)\twoheadrightarrow\Mod(\Sigma_{g})\twoheadrightarrow\Sp(H^{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})).

In particular, the monodromy group is
\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
.

The proof runs as follows:
there is a set of “tautological” classes in
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{GL}})
defined by taking Künneth components of Chern classes of the universal local system on
(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)\times M^{\operatorname{GL}}
, and Markman’s result is that these tautological classes generate
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{GL}})
.
Since the mapping class group action preserves the universal local system and hence its Chern classes, an immediate corollary is a description of the
\Mod(\Sigma_{g}\setminus p)
action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{GL}})
in terms of the action on
H^{*}(\Sigma_{g})
. But now it is a classical fact that the mapping class group action on
H^{*}(\Sigma_{g})
factors through a surjection to
\Sp(H^{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}))
. (For a more careful exposition, see, e.g.,
[
1
, §4.1]
.)

Returning to the case of
\operatorname{SL}
, Theorem
1.1
shows that the mapping class group action is now no longer controlled by
H^{1}(\Sigma_{g})
but rather
H^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g}^{n})
, reflecting the fact that the cohomology
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
is not generated by tautological classes. The issue is the following:

Observation 1.4
.

The finite group
\Hom(\pi_{1}(C\setminus p),\mu_{n})\cong H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)=:\Gamma
acts on
M^{\operatorname{SL}}
via the action of
\mu_{n}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
by scalar multiplication.

On
M^{\operatorname{GL}}
, this extends to an action of the connected group
\Hom(\pi_{1}(C\setminus p),\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2g}
, and so the corresponding action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{GL}})
is trivial. In particular, since the tautological classes on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
are pulled back along the inclusion
M^{\operatorname{SL}}\subset M^{\operatorname{GL}}
, they belong to
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})^{\Gamma}
.

The existence of
\Gamma
-variant classes in
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
was first pointed out by Hausel and Thaddeus
[
8
]
, who interpreted them in terms of stringy cohomology of the corresponding
\PGL
space in the context of SYZ mirror symmetry. In general, these variant classes are not so well understood; unlike the tautological classes, there is no obvious geometric construction, and so, for example, we do not fully understand the Hodge structure on the variant part of
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
, although some information on dimensions can be recovered via point-counting techniques
[
17
]
. However, more recently Maulik and Shen
[
16
]
were able to relate the variant classes of the moduli space of
\operatorname{SL}
Higgs bundles on a curve
C
to the cohomology of moduli spaces of
\operatorname{GL}_{n/m}
-Higgs bundles on
m
-fold covers of
C
. The main technical tool of this paper is a relative (i.e., compatible with the monodromy or mapping class group action) version of their construction, which occupies §
2
, and the implications for the mapping class group action on
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
, along the lines of the proof of Fact
1.3
above, are described in §
3
.

2.
Relativizing the Maulik-Shen construction

In this section we prove that the endoscopic correspondence of
[
16
, Theorem 0.4]
, which is a surjection of cohomology groups of Higgs moduli spaces for a single curve, can be upgraded to a surjection of local systems over a cover of
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
.

2.1.
Set-up and notation

In this section we will work over the base space
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
, where by
\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
we mean the moduli space of curves with level
n
structure introduced in
[
4
, (5.14)]
. (We note that
\mathcal{M}
is scheme if
n\geq 3
, and the reader may assume this is always the case by either restricting to the dense open schematic locus or using
\mathcal{M}_{g}[4]
when
n=2
.) Let
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{M}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
be the universal curve, and
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
the projection map.
Also, let
s:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{C}
be the section pulled back from the canonical section of
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There is a subgroup
S
of the group of
n
-torsion line bundles on
\mathcal{C}
such that the restriction to any fiber

\Pic(\mathcal{C})[n]\to\Pic([C]\times_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{C})[n]

defines an isomorphism
S\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pic(C)[n]
.

Proof.

The projection
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}[n]
gives, by definition, a canonical isomorphism
R^{1}\pi_{*}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2g}
of étale sheaves with alternating form.
Consider the morphism

(1)

(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2g}=H^{0}(\mathcal{M},R^{1}\pi_{*}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{M},R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})=\Pic_{\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M})

induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Since
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
has geometrically integral fibers, using the section
s
described above, we identify
\Pic_{\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M})
with the subspace of
\Pic(\mathcal{C})
consisting of line bundles
\mathcal{L}
such that
s^{*}\mathcal{L}\in\Pic(\mathcal{M})
is trivial
[
22
, Tag 0D28]
.
Under this identification, for each
\gamma\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2g}
we take
L_{\gamma}\in\Pic(\mathcal{C})
to be the image of
\gamma
under (
1
). Note that by construction,
L_{\gamma}
is an
n
-torsion class. Finally, for each
i:p\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}
, by proper base change we have
i^{*}R\pi_{*}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})=R\pi_{p*}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, we get a morphism from (
1
) to the corresponding diagram for
\pi_{p}:\mathcal{C}_{p}\to p
, which is the inclusion
\Pic^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{p})[n]\to\Pic^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{p})

[
22
, Tag 03RQ]
.
Thus each
L_{\gamma}
restricts to the corresponding element of
\Pic^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{p})[n]
.
∎

For the rest of this section, we fix an element
\gamma\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2g}
of order
m
, and let
r:=n/m
. We define
\pi_{\gamma}:\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\to\mathcal{C}
to be the
m
-fold étale cover corresponding to the
m
-torsion line bundle
L_{\gamma}
, i.e.,
V(t^{m}-1)\subset|L_{\gamma}|
, where
t
is the tautological section on the pullback of
L_{\gamma}
to the total space
|L_{\gamma}|
. We let
G_{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
denote the Galois group, which acts by scaling the fiber coordinate on
|L_{\gamma}|
by elements of
\mu_{m}
. On each fiber
C
, the above gives a canonical identification
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{2g}=\Pic^{0}(C)[n]=H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)=:\Gamma
, and the covering space
\pi_{\gamma}
corresponds to a canonical map
H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)\to G_{\gamma}
whose kernel consists of elements pairing trivially with
\gamma
. Also, via the intersection pairing on
H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)
, the choice of
\gamma\in\Gamma
gives a canonically defined element
\kappa:=\langle\gamma,-\rangle\in\hat{\Gamma}
.

Now we are ready to define the various moduli spaces of Higgs bundles arising in the proof.

•

Let
M_{n,d}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{M}
be the (coarse) moduli space of fiberwise stable (
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
) Higgs bundles of rank
n
and relative degree
d
on
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}/\mathcal{M}
; this was constructed by Simpson via GIT in
[
19
, Theorem 4.7]
.

•

Let

be the relative Hitchin base and

the (proper) relative Hitchin morphism. We also take
\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{C},n)\subset\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{C},n)
to be given by the inclusion of the zero section
\mathcal{M}\xhookrightarrow{0}|\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}|
.

•

Given an element
\mathscr{L}\in\Pic_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M})
, we define the coarse moduli space of stable
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
Higgs bundles as follows: consider

M^{0}_{n,d}(\mathcal{C}):=M_{n,d}(\mathcal{C})\times_{h,\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{C},n),0}\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{C},n)

the moduli space of trace-free stable Higgs bundles. Then

\widecheck{M}_{n,\mathscr{L}}(\mathcal{C}):=M_{n,d}^{0}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\det,\Pic_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}},\mathscr{L}}\mathcal{M},

where the map
M_{n,d}^{0}(\mathcal{C})\to\Pic_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}
is given by
(E,\theta)\mapsto\det E
. For the purposes of this paper, we fix
\mathscr{L}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(ds(\mathcal{M}))
, where
s(\mathcal{M})
is the image of the section described above.

•

Given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, tensor product by
L_{\gamma}
defines an endomorphism of
\widecheck{M}_{n,\mathscr{L}}(\mathcal{C})
. Let
M_{\gamma}(\mathcal{C})
be the fixed locus, and let
\mathbb{A}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{C},n)
be the image of
M_{\gamma}(\mathcal{C})
under
h
.

•

Given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we define a space

M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}):=\mathcal{M}_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})\times_{\mathcal{M}_{n,d}(\mathcal{C})}\widecheck{M}_{n,\mathscr{L}}(\mathcal{C}),

where the first map is given by pushforward

\mathcal{M}_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{n,d}(\mathcal{C}),(E,\theta)\mapsto(\pi_{\gamma*}E,\pi_{\gamma*}\theta)

and the second by the inclusion
\widecheck{M}_{n,\mathscr{L}}(\mathcal{C})\subset M_{n,d}(\mathcal{C})
. We also define
\mathbb{A}_{\pi}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma},r)
to be the image of
M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})\subset M_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})
under the relative Hitchin map.

Remark 2.2
.

In §
2.3
we will also use the following generalization of the definitions above:
if
\mathscr{D}\in\Pic(\mathcal{C})
is of relative degree
>2g-2
on the fibers of

or

, and

.

.

We defined all these spaces for

, but for any morphism

instead of

, and the

[

[

]
.

2.2.
Preparatory steps

We begin by describing the relative versions of the more or less routine steps of the
[
16
]
construction.
In what follows, we use
\pi
to denote all maps to the base
\mathcal{M}
(or suitable covers) when there seems to be no risk of confusion. We also use
\mathbb{Q}(X)
to denote the constant sheaf on
X
and
D^{b}(X,\mathbb{Q})
for the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces on
X
.

Proposition 2.4
.

We have

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}))\cong R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))\otimes(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})))^{\Gamma}

in
D^{b}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

We start by noting that tensor product with
L_{\delta}
for
\delta\in\Gamma
(respectively,
\pi_{\gamma}^{*}L_{\delta}
) gives an action of
\Gamma
on
M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})
(respectively,
M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})
), fiberwise with respect to the projection maps to
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, as in the fiberwise case, the product map

M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{M}}M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})\to M_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}),\,\,((L,s),(E,\theta))\mapsto(\pi_{\gamma}^{*}L\otimes E,\pi_{\gamma}^{*}s\otimes 1+1\otimes\theta)

is a quotient by the diagonal action of
\Gamma
. Thus, we have

(2)

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}))\cong(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{M}}M_{\pi}(C_{\gamma}))^{\Gamma}.

Next, we note that
M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})=\Pic^{0}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}\times_{\mathcal{M}}|\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}|
is topologically locally trivial over
\mathcal{M}
, and so
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))
is a local system for each
i
. Since
\mathcal{M}
and the fibers
M_{1,0}(C)\cong\Pic^{0}(C)\times H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
and

are smooth
[
16
, Proposition 1.1]
, by smooth relative Künneth (see, e.g.,
[

(3)

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{M}}M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})\cong R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))\otimes R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})).

Now, we note that
R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))
is
\Gamma
-invariant: indeed, by the local triviality, it suffices to see that fiberwise, the
\Gamma
action extends to an action of the connected group
\Pic^{0}(C)
and thus is trivial on cohomology. Taking
\Gamma
invariants of both sides of (
3
) in combination with (

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

We have

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))\cong\oplus_{i=0}^{2g}\bigwedge\nolimits^{i}R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))[-i]

in
D^{b}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Q})
.
In particular, there is a well-defined, split map

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))\otimes(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})))^{\Gamma}\to(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})))^{\Gamma}.

Proof.

Using the description of
M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C})
above, we see that

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(M_{1,0}(\mathcal{C}))=R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}(\Pic^{0}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}),

where
\Pic^{0}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{M}
is an abelian scheme of dimension
g
.
∎

Recall that the space
M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})
is equipped with commuting actions of
\Gamma
(by tensor product with the line bundles
\pi_{\gamma}^{*}L_{\delta}
) and
G_{\gamma}
(by pullback under deck transformations, which preserve the line bundles

Since both groups act fiberwise with respect to the projection

We begin with a description of the action of

Lemma 2.6
.

Fiberwise,
there is a canonical morphism

\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma/\ker(\kappa)\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{0}(\Prym(C_{\gamma}/C)),\delta\mapsto[\pi_{\gamma}^{*}L_{\delta}^{\otimes r}].

Given the choice of

Moreover, letting

to

act on

induced by the natural

Proof.

The description of morphism (

As for

∎

In particular, using the identification

Proposition 2.7
.

After passing to a finite cover
S\to\mathcal{M}
, there is an automorphism of
R\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}(M_{\pi}(C_{\gamma}))
inducing isomorphisms

(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})))_{(\alpha,\beta)}\cong(R\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}(M_{\pi}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma})))_{(\alpha+\kappa,\beta+\kappa_{G})}

in

, for each

Proof.

Given a smooth family
X\to\mathcal{M}

3.

Either because of the existence of a smooth compactification with smooth boundary (see

on

Lemma 3.1
.

For each

\ker(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})\acts H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma})))=\ker(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})).

We recall several well-known facts, referring to
[
1
, §3-4]
for proofs or references. First of all, we have

[

is a relative moduli space of

-Higgs bundles. If

On the other hand, since also

2.22
, we have

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{n}))\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))).

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
2.22
that we have a surjection
\oplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))\twoheadrightarrow H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))
of (complex)
\pi_{1}(W)
-representations, and so

(15)

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts\oplus_{\gamma}H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma})))\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))).

Next, we have

(16)

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts\oplus_{\gamma}H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))))=\cap_{\gamma}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma})))\\
=\cap_{\gamma}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})))

by Lemma
3.1
.

Now, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma=H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)^{*}
we have a covering map
C_{n}\to C_{\gamma}
corresponding to
H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}\mathbb{C}^{*}
. This gives a
\hat{\Gamma}=H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}/n)=\Gal(C_{n}/C)
and
\pi_{1}(W)
-equivariant identification
H^{1}(C_{\gamma})\cong H^{1}(C_{n})^{\ker\gamma}
. Moreover, the
\pi_{1}(W)
and
\hat{\Gamma}
actions on
H^{1}(C_{n})
commute, as
\hat{\Gamma}
is trivialized over
W
. Breaking down
H^{1}(C_{n})
according to characters of
\hat{\Gamma}
, we
conclude that

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{n})))=\cap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts(H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma})=\cap_{\gamma}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})),

as for each
\gamma
we have

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma}))=\cap_{i=1}^{|\gamma|}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{n})_{i\gamma}).

Combining this with (
15
) and (
16
) above, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\kappa\neq 0\in\hat{\Gamma}
we have

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))^{\Gamma}\oplus H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))_{\kappa})\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})_{-\kappa_{G}}),

where
\kappa_{G}\in\hat{G}_{\gamma}
is as defined before Proposition
2.7
.

Proof.

By the results of the previous section, we have isomorphisms of complex
\pi_{1}(W)
-representations

H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))_{\kappa}\overset{\ref{cor: last step},\ref{prop: Gpi quotient}}{\cong}H^{*}(M_{\pi}(C_{\gamma}))_{\kappa}^{G_{\gamma}}\overset{\ref{prop: st = kappa}}{\cong}H^{*}(M_{\pi}(C_{\gamma}))^{\Gamma}_{-\kappa_{G}}

and

H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}}\overset{\ref{prop: step 1}}{\cong}H^{*}(M_{1,0}(C))\otimes H^{*}(M_{\pi}(C_{\gamma}))^{\Gamma}_{-\kappa_{G}}.

Furthermore, from the proof of Lemma
3.1
(applied in the case of
\kappa=0
) we see that

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))^{\Gamma})=\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C))=\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts M_{1,0}(C)),

(where the first equality uses the identification
H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))^{\Gamma}=H^{*}(\widehat{M}_{n,d}(C))
),
and so

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))^{\Gamma}\oplus H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))_{\kappa})\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}}).

Next, note that the
G_{\gamma}
-equivariant map

\det:M_{r,d}(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,W})\to\Pic^{d}_{C_{\gamma,W}/W},(E,\theta)\mapsto\det E

induces an injection of
\pi_{1}(W)
-representations
H^{*}(\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}))\hookrightarrow H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))
. (Indeed, for any choice of
(E,\theta)\in M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma})
, we can compose the determinant map with

\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma})\to M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}),A\mapsto(A\otimes E,1\otimes\theta)

to get the isogeny
\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma})\to\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}),A\mapsto A^{\otimes r}\otimes\det E
.)
We therefore conclude that

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(M_{r,d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}})\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}})

\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}}).

Finally, recall that choosing local trivializations of the
\Pic^{0}_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,W}/W}
-torsor
\Pic^{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,W}/W}
gives a natural isomorphism of
\pi_{1}(W)
-representations
H^{*}(\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}))\cong H^{*}(\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma}))

[
2
, Lemma 1.3.5]
. We note that this isomorphism is also
G_{\gamma}
-equivariant: choosing any
A\in\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma})
to trivialize the torsor, we see that
G_{\gamma}
acts on
\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma})
via its action on
\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma})
multiplied by
\xi\mapsto\xi(A)\otimes A^{-1}\in\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma})
, and since
\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma})
is a connected group, this second part acts trivially on cohomology. Thus

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(\Pic^{d}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}})=\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(\Pic^{0}(C_{\gamma}))_{-\kappa_{G}})

=\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})_{-\kappa_{G}}),

completing the proof.
∎

Recall that subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}\subset G
are commensurable if
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
is of finite index in each
H_{i}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The subgroups

\ker(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C),\mathbb{Z}))\,\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\,\ker(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})\acts H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{Z}))

are commensurable in
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
.

Proof.

First of all, since the torsion subgroup of
H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C),\mathbb{Z})
is finite, it suffices to work with cohomology over
\mathbb{C}
.
Also, since passing to a finite cover has the effect of restricting to a finite index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
and restricting to a Zariski open set induces a surjection on
\pi_{1}
, it suffices to prove that on
\pi_{1}(W)
the two kernels are commensurable.

By Corollary
3.2
we have

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{n}))\subset\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))).

On the other hand, since
\Gamma
is trivialized over
W
, we have

\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)))=\bigcap_{\kappa\neq 0\in\hat{\Gamma}}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))^{\Gamma}\oplus H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))_{\kappa})

(17)

\subset\bigcap_{\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}}\ker(\pi_{1}(W)\acts H^{1}(C_{\gamma})_{-\kappa_{G}})

by Lemmas
3.1
(for
\kappa=0
) and
3.3
(for
\kappa\neq 0
). Considering the splitting

H^{1}(C_{n})=\oplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}=\oplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}H^{1}(C_{n}/(\ker\gamma))_{\gamma}=\oplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}H^{1}(C_{\gamma})_{\gamma}

we see that the intersection (
17
) runs over all possible characters. (Recall that we defined
\kappa_{G}\in\widehat{G}_{\gamma}\subset\Gamma
to match
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma/\ker(\kappa)}\subset\widehat{\Gamma}
via the isomorphism of Lemma
2.6
. Both the cover
C_{\gamma}\to C
and this isomorphism are independent of the choice of generator of
\langle\gamma\rangle
, and so as
\kappa
ranges over
\widehat{\Gamma}
, correspondingly
-\kappa_{G}
ranges over
\Gamma
.)
∎

The
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
action on
H_{1}(C_{n})
is described in
[
13
]
, apart from a few exceptions in genus 2; the problem arises in the argument that the monodromy representations on the different isotypic components of
H_{1}(C_{n})
, each of which is understood individually
[
13

, are (virtually) independent. (The Hodge-theoretic big monodromy result in
[
12
]
, which only describes algebraic monodromy but holds also for nonabelian Galois groups, only applies in genus
>2
.)

Corollary 3.5
.

If either
g>2
or
g=2
and
(n,6)=1
, then the algebraic monodromy group of
\pi(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})\acts H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C),\mathbb{C})
is the derived subgroup of
\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C}))
, i.e.

\begin{cases}\operatorname{SL}_{2(g-1)}(\mathbb{C})^{\times(n^{2g}-2^{2g})/2}\times\Sp_{2(g-1)}(\mathbb{C})^{\times(2^{2g}-1)}\times\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{C})&n\equiv 0(2)\\
\operatorname{SL}_{2(g-1)}(\mathbb{C})^{\times(n^{2g}-1)/2}\times\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{C})&n\equiv 1(2).\end{cases}

If
g=2
and
(n,6)\neq 1
, then the algebraic monodromy is contained in this group.

Proof.

First, by Theorem
3.4
, and the fact that the
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
action on
H^{1}(C_{n})
factors through
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
(i.e., that the quotient
C_{n}\to C
is canonical, and thus extends to a well-defined cover over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
), it suffices to compute the algebraic monodromy group of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})\acts H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})
. Assuming that
g
and
n
are as described, Looijenga’s result is that this is as big as possible, given that the monodromy action is compatible with the intersection form and (after passing to a finite index subgroup) the
\widehat{\Gamma}
action on
C_{n}
, and must be semisimple
[
5
]
; more precisely, the image of a finite-index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
is an arithmetic subgroup of the commutator subgroup of
\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{Q}))

[
13
, Corollary 2.6]
. By the Borel density theorem, an arithmetic subgroup of this semisimple group is Zariski dense, and so the closure over
\mathbb{C}
is
[\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})),\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C}))]\subset\operatorname{GL}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C}))
. (On the other hand, if
g=2
and
(n,6)\neq 1
, the algebraic monodromy may be a proper subgroup.)

This group may be described explicitly as follows: first,
an automorphism of
H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})
commutes with
\widehat{\Gamma}
exactly if it fixes all isotypic components
H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})_{\gamma}
, and since the intersection form is
\widehat{\Gamma}
-invariant, each
H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})_{\gamma}
pairs nontrivially exactly with
H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})_{-\gamma}
. In other words, we have

\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbb{C}))=\prod_{\{\gamma,-\gamma\}\subset\Gamma}\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}+H^{1}(C_{n})_{-\gamma})

where

\Sp_{\hat{\Gamma}}(H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}+H^{1}(C_{n})_{-\gamma})=\begin{cases}\operatorname{GL}(H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma})&\gamma\neq-\gamma\\
\Sp(H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma})&\gamma=-\gamma.\end{cases}

(Indeed, in the first case, a
\widehat{\Gamma}
-equivariant transformation will fix
H^{1}(C_{n})_{\pm\gamma}
, which are dual under the intersection pairing, so a symplectic action on the sum is determined by an automorphism on
H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}
. In the second case, the intersection form restricts nondegenerately to
H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}
, and any action on this space is
\widehat{\Gamma}
-invariant.) Finally, we note that we have
[\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})]=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
[\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{C}),\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{C})]=\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{C})
and that
\dim H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}=2(g-1)
for
\gamma\neq 0
and
2g
for
\gamma=0
(by
[
13
, Proposition 4.2]
and the fact that
H^{1}(C_{n})_{\gamma}\cong H^{1}(C_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
).
∎

3.1.
Back to the character variety

It now remains to show that the
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})=\Mod(C\setminus p)
actions on
H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))
and
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
agree. This is presumably well-known to experts, but it is nevertheless not immediate from Simpson’s original nonabelian Hodge correspondence, which only describes the case
d=0
.

Recall that
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})=\Mod(C\setminus p)
acts on

M^{\operatorname{SL}}=\{\phi\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(C\setminus p),\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})):\phi(\gamma_{p})=e^{2\pi id/n}\Id_{n}\}/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

(where
\gamma_{p}
is a counterclockwise loop around the puncture
p
)
via its outer action on
\pi_{1}(C\setminus p)
. (See
[
1
, Definition 2.3]
for more discussion.)
We also consider the “degree
d
”
\PGL_{n}
character variety

M^{\PGL}:=M^{\operatorname{SL}}/\Hom(\pi_{1}(C\setminus p),\mu_{n}),

where the action of
\Hom(\pi_{1}(C\setminus p),\mu_{n})
is defined by the action of
\mu_{n}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
by scalar multiplication. The
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
action on
H^{*}(M^{G})
can be described as a monodromy action on the “local system of varieties”
M^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1}):=\mathcal{T}_{g,1}\times_{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})}M^{G}
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
, which is a quotient of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g,1}
by the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
.

As mentioned in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, there is also a moduli space
\widehat{M}_{n,d}(C):=\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)/\Pic^{0}(C)[n]
of “degree
d
”
\PGL
Higgs bundles, where
\Pic^{0}(C)[n]=\Gamma
acts by tensor product. Similarly, there is a relative version
\widehat{M}_{n,d}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1})
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
, and it follows from Simpson’s degree 0 relative nonabelian Hodge correspondence
[
20
, Theorem 9.11, Lemma 9.4]
that there is a canonical homeomorphism of
M^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1})
and
\widehat{M}_{n,d}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1})
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
. (See
[
1
, §3]
for a discussion of the relative
\PGL
nonabelian Hodge correspondence in this form.)

Proposition 3.6
.

The two monodromy representations of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
on
H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))\cong H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
agree on a finite index subgroup.

Proof.

As described, e.g., in
[
8
, §2]
, over each point
(C,p)
of
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
there is a (not necessarily canonical) nonabelian Hodge homeomorphism
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)\cong M^{\operatorname{SL}}(C\setminus p)
descending to the canonical homeomorphism
\widehat{M}_{n,d}(C)\cong M^{\PGL}(C\setminus p)
under the quotient map induced by
\operatorname{SL}_{n}\to\PGL_{n}
on each side of the correspondence.

Now, since the
\Gamma
action on
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)
is generically free and thus
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)\to\widehat{M}_{n,d}(C)=\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)/\Gamma
is étale over a Zariski open set (whose preimage is dense in the Euclidean topology on
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)
), we see that isomorphisms of
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)
over the identity map of
\widehat{M}_{n,d}
are determined by their restrictions to this open subset and thus indexed by the covering group
\Gamma
. Then there is an
n^{2g}
-fold cover
S
of
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
whose fiber over
(C,p)
consists of the fiberwise homeomorphisms
\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C)\cong M^{\operatorname{SL}}(C\setminus p)
over the canonical homeomorphism
\widehat{M}_{n,d}(C)\cong M^{\PGL}(C\setminus p)
given by Simpson’s nonabelian Hodge correspondence, and by construction there is a relative homeomorphism of
\widecheck{M}_{n,\mathscr{L}}(\mathcal{C}_{S})
and
M^{\operatorname{SL}}(\mathcal{C}_{S})
over
S
; in particular, the monodromy representations of
\pi_{1}(S)
on
H^{*}(\widecheck{M}_{n,L}(C))
and
H^{*}(M^{\operatorname{SL}})
agree.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The nonabelian Hodge correspondence of
[
8
]
is not a priori canonical because, as described in
[

in the case of

In fact, there should be a canonical degree

]

[
10
]
and

]
