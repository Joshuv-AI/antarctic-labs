Investigation into the effects of pregabalin on motor nerves and their underlying mechanisms
Authors: Junfei Yi, Shuming Li, Hui Peng, Xuewen Liu, Xiaotong Xi, Dongsheng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pregabalin, a typical representative of pharmaceuticals and personal care products (PPCPs), has seen its environmental residue issue become increasingly prominent due to its widespread and high-volume clinical use. While its potential neurotoxic risks have attracted attention, the specific mechanisms underlying its induction of neurotoxicity have not yet been fully elucidated. This study investigated the neurotoxic effects of pregabalin using a zebrafish model: first, it predicted the potential mechanisms and key targets of pregabalin-induced neurotoxicity utilizing network toxicology techniques, followed by verification via experimental methods such as reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The results showed that pregabalin exposure binds to key receptors such as GRIA2, interferes with the glutamatergic synaptic pathway, thereby triggering neuronal apoptosis, and ultimately leading to neurodevelopmental impairment in zebrafish larvae — specifically a significant 8.8% reduction in neural axon length, accompanied by abnormal motor behavior, namely a significant 33.3% decrease in movement distance and a 33.4% decrease in movement speed, respectively. These findings not only provide key experimental evidence for the in-depth analysis of the neurotoxic effects and molecular mechanisms of environmental pregabalin exposure but also offer theoretical support for the systematic assessment of its eco-neurotoxic risks to ecosystems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58388-z.

Introduction

Pharmaceuticals and Personal Care Products (PPCPs) are a class of emerging contaminants, encompassing human and veterinary drugs (used for preventing or treating diseases) as well as ingredients in personal care products and household chemicals (such as disinfectants, fragrances, etc.)1. Their main pathways into the environment include: direct discharge without sufficient transformation or without any transformation during pollution treatment processes; direct input of drugs in aquaculture; indirect input in agricultural activities; and emissions from places such as pharmaceutical factories, hospitals, and nursing homes. Due to the widespread use and persistence of PPCPs, they are often detected in aquatic environments, attracting global attention2.

Pregabalin is a prescription drug commonly used to treat neuropathic pain and belongs to the class of gamma-aminobutyric acid analogues3. Initially developed as an anticonvulsant, it is currently classified under PPCPs. Clinically, it is mainly used in the treatment of postherpetic neuralgia, fibromyalgia, and neuropathic pain, and can also assist in controlling partial epileptic seizures4. Due to its rapid absorption and high bioavailability, the issues of overuse and misuse have become increasingly prominent in recent years5. Environmental monitoring data indicate that pregabalin has clear residues in sewage treatment systems: one study measured its average influent concentration at 2.5 ± 0.6 µg/L and average effluent concentration at 1.1 ± 0.1 µg/L6; concurrent monitoring at the Kaditz Wastewater Treatment Plant in Dresden showed an influent concentration range of 2015.0–2357.5ng/L and an effluent concentration of 810.0–839.3ng/L7. Each clinically used pregabalin capsule contains 25 mg of the active pharmaceutical ingredient8. As it is not easily hydrolysed in water and has strong structural stability, there is a risk of continuous increase in its environmental concentration, which may threaten the survival of organisms in the ecosystem. Toxicity studies have further revealed its potential hazards: at an exposure dose of 300 mg/kg・bw/day, pregabalin exhibited significant toxicity in both male and female rats, specifically including weight loss, reproductive function damage, enhanced oxidative stress, and upregulation of apoptosis-related indicators9; at a dose of 1250 mg/kg, rabbits showed developmental toxicity, manifested as reduced activity, decreased fetal and placental weights, and delayed skeletal ossification10. In addition, high doses of pregabalin can reduce the levels of dopamine, glutamate, and norepinephrine in the brain, causing oxidative stress damage and inducing neuronal apoptosis11. The above-mentioned mammalian studies suggest that pregabalin has potential developmental and neurotoxic risks. Population exposure data are also noteworthy: data from U.S. poison centres between 2012 and 2022 showed that pregabalin-related exposures accounted for 14.1% of total exposure cases (124161 cases in total)12. Therefore, in-depth exploration of the neurotoxicity of pregabalin and its mechanism of action holds significant scientific and practical importance.

Network toxicology integrates multiple disciplinary fields and related technologies such as bioinformatics, big data analysis, and genomics13,14. By constructing a multi-layer interaction network of “compound-target-pathway-phenotype”, it provides a powerful method to overcome the limitations of traditional research15. It focuses on exploring the toxic pathways of compounds and understanding the molecular mechanisms related to diseases, and explains the complex interactions between genes, proteins, and metabolites involved in diseases and drugs from a network perspective16. Molecular docking technology reveals toxic mechanisms and assesses potential hazards by simulating the interactions between pollutants (small molecule ligands) and biological macromolecules (targets such as enzymes and receptors). It provides an efficient computational tool for analyzing the toxic mechanisms of pollutants at the molecular level, helping to accurately identify toxic targets, clarify action pathways, and offer important support for the toxicity assessment, prevention, and control of pollutants17. Therefore, in this study, we use network toxicology and molecular docking technologies to predict the neurotoxicity of pregabalin and its potential mechanisms.

Zebrafish have significant advantages as a model for studying neurodevelopment. Their neural development process is highly conserved with that of humans, and they are convenient for observation and experimental manipulation18. With the development of genetic technology, an increasing number of transgenic zebrafish are being used to observe neural development. In these fish, similar to endogenous genes, transgenic expression is significantly induced in the developing and regenerating nervous system19. In this study, we use transgenic zebrafish Tg (hb9: eGFP) with fluorescently labeled nerves to explore the effect of pregabalin on neurodevelopment in zebrafish larvae.

The main objective of this study is to explore the neurotoxic effects of pregabalin based on the zebrafish model, and further predict and verify the potential toxic mechanism and toxic targets of pregabalin using technical methods such as network toxicology, molecular docking, and RT-qPCR. At present, as a type of PPCPs, research on pregabalin not only helps us better understand its mechanism of action, but also can provide key evidence for the safe use of related products and environmental risk assessment, thus safeguarding ecological balance and human health.

Materials and methods

Chemical substances

Pregabalin (CAS number: 148553-50-8) was purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. SYBR® Green Master Mix and the reverse transcription kit were procured from Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd (Nanjing, China). The mother liquor prepared in the experiment was stored in a refrigerator at 4 degrees Celsius.

Breeding and rearing of zebrafish

The zebrafish used in the experiment include the wild-type AB strain and the transgenic strain Tg (huc: eGFP), both of which were purchased from the Institute of Hydrobiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (IHB; located in Wuhan, China). The obtained adult zebrafish were reared in a standard recirculating aquaculture system, with the water temperature strictly maintained at 28 ± 0.5 °C, and the daily light cycle was set to 14 h of light alternating with 10 h of darkness. To obtain the fertilized eggs required for the experiment, on the evening before the planned spawning, sexually mature zebrafish were paired at a male-to-female ratio of 1:2 and placed in a dedicated breeding tank, which was equipped with a removable partition to temporarily separate male and female fish. The next morning, after the start of illumination, the partition was removed to allow male and female fish to mate and lay eggs naturally under light conditions. After spawning, the fertilized eggs in the breeding tank were collected in a timely manner. Subsequently, these fertilized eggs were carefully examined under a microscope, and embryos with normal morphology and healthy development were strictly selected; these qualified embryos were used for subsequent exposure experiments. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the provided guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals, and all methods employed in the experiments complied with relevant guidelines and regulations. After the end of the experiment (144 hpf) or when the larvae are no longer needed for further analyses, they are placed in an ice-water mixture (2–4 °C) to ensure death.

Construction of targets for pregabalin and nerve injury

The potential common targets of pregabalin acting on nerve injury were identified in this study through the following steps: first, the accurate 2D structural SDF file of pregabalin was obtained from PubChem and submitted to the Swiss Target Prediction platform (Uniform Resource Locator (URL): http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) for target prediction, with the results downloaded as the pregabalin target set; second, for nervous system injury, a search was conducted in the GeneCards database (URL: http://www.genecards.org/) using “nervous system diseases” as the keyword to construct a nerve injury-related target set; finally, an intersection analysis of the above two target sets was performed with the help of an online Venn diagram tool (URL: http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/), and the resulting intersection was the common targets of the drug and the disease.

Construction of PPI network and KEGG pathway analysis

The construction of the PPI network mainly relies on the STRING database (URL: https://cn.string-db.org/). The specific operation is as follows: upload the overlapping targets obtained from the Venn diagram of pregabalin and nerve injury to this database, set the species as human, and obtain the PPI network diagram after submission. Afterwards, Cytoscape software is used to analyze and optimize the network structure and key nodes, among which the selection criterion for core targets is that the degree value is not less than twice the median. The specific data can be found in Table S1.

To explore the biological processes and pathways involved in pregabalin in terms of nerve injury, it is necessary to use the DAVID database (URL: https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. During the operation, the intersection targets in the Venn diagram are uploaded to this database, the species is also set as human, and the analysis results are obtained after submission. From the obtained results, the top 10 contents in the KEGG catalog will be selected and arranged in ascending order of P-Value.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was employed to further analyze the intermolecular interactions between pregabalin and the screened core target proteins by predicting their binding modes and affinity. The primary software used included AutoDockTools-1.5.6 and PyMOL 2.3.4. The specific procedures were as follows: First, a suitable ligand with a resolution higher than 2.0 Å and sourced from Homo sapiens was retrieved from the PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org). The ligand was hydrogenated and stripped of water molecules using AutoDockTools-1.5.6, then saved in PDBQT format. Subsequently, water molecules and any existing ligands were removed from the protein structure using PyMOL, and the cleaned protein was saved in PDB format. This processed protein file was then converted into the PDBQT format using AutoDockTools-1.5.6 to prepare the receptor. Finally, molecular docking between the receptor and ligand was performed by executing AutoDockTools-1.5.6 via the Windows Command Prompt (CMD). The visualization of the docking results was accomplished using Discovery Studio 2024 and PyMOL 2.3.4.

Pregabalin exposure and general developmental toxicity testing

In this study, it was found that exposure to pregabalin at concentrations ≥ 100 mg/L did not cause death in zebrafish embryos. Therefore, based on environmental concentrations, the pregabalin concentrations were set at 0, 0.01, 0.1, and 1 mg/L, which include environmental concentrations. Ten wild-type zebrafish embryos at 2 hpf were placed into wells of a culture plate containing 5 mL of aerated water, with three biological replicates per concentration group. During the experiment, a fluorescent stereomicroscope was used to record the mortality and hatching rate every 24 h. At 24, 72, and 144 h post-fertilization (hpf), photographs were taken to measure and calculate the following parameters: at 24 hpf, yolk sac area and embryonic movement of zebrafish embryos; at 72 hpf, body length of zebrafish; and at 144 hpf, body length, eye circumference area, and swim bladder area of zebrafish larvae (n = 12).

Behavioral tests of pregabalin exposure

The behavioral test for larval fish was conducted with reference to a previously published method20, using alternating light-dark-light cycle stimulation to examine the locomotor behavior of 6 days post-fertilization (dpf) zebrafish larvae. The specific procedures were as follows: 12 zebrafish larvae were selected from each treatment group and transferred to a 24-well plate (one larva per well) with 3mL of test water added; after acclimating for 10 min in an environment at 28 ± 0.5 °C, their locomotor activity was recorded. The test conditions involved alternating light-dark stimulation (10 min of light alternates with 10 min of darkness, totaling 40 min). Locomotor behavior was recorded via DanioVision software, and indicators such as freezing time activity, acceleration, and movement frequency of zebrafish under light and dark conditions were calculated and statistically analyzed using the animal movement trajectory tracking system XT15 software. Each experiment was repeated three times (n = 12).

Study on neurodevelopmental toxicity in zebrafish larvae

Based on the observed adverse effects of pregabalin on zebrafish movement, the transgenic zebrafish strain Tg (hb9:eGFP) was exposed to different concentrations of pregabalin to evaluate its impact on neuronal development. The specific procedures were as follows: 12 larvae were randomly selected from each treatment group (n = 3) and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 5 min; a fluorescent stereomicroscope (Nikon SMZ25, Japan) was used to observe the larvae exposed to pregabalin until 72 hpf and 144 hpf, and the axon length of transgenic Tg (hb9:GFP) was statistically analyzed; the axon length in zebrafish larvae was quantitatively analyzed using NIS-Elements D software.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from 100 zebrafish embryos in each group exposed to pregabalin until 144 hpf using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA), with three replicates per group. cDNA synthesis was performed using the PrimeScript® RT kit. Gene expression analysis was carried out on a Bio-Rad CFX Connect Real-Time System (Bio-Rad, USA) using the SYBR Green detection method (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The expression levels of genes related to neurodevelopment (elav3, mbp, syn2a, and gap43), genes associated with neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction and glutamatergic synapse signaling pathways (gria2a, htr2a, and grm5a), and genes related to apoptosis (p53, bax, and bcl-2) were quantitatively analyzed using primers synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (see Table S2). β-actin was used as an internal reference gene, and the 2-ΔΔCt method was employed to calculate relative RNA levels.

Determination of the expression of related proteins via WB analysis

Total protein and nuclear protein were extracted according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Equal amounts of protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred to a PVDF membrane under ice-cold conditions. Subsequently, the membrane was blocked in TBST buffer containing 5% non-fat milk at room temperature for 2 h, then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibody: GluR2 Antibody (Gria2) (T57063, Abmart Pharmaceutical Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., China), P53 antibody (60283-2-Ig, Sanying Biotechnology (Wuhan) Co., Ltd.) and BCL2A Antibody (AZQ564A4, Boster Biological Technology (Wuhan) Co., Ltd.). The next day, the membrane was washed three times with TBST and incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) secondary antibody (A0208, diluted 1:1000, Beyotime, Shanghai) at 37 °C for 1 h. Finally, immunoreactive bands were detected using a gel imaging system (Servicebio, Wuhan, China), and quantitative analysis was performed with ImageJ software. Original blots/gels are shown in Supplemental Figure S2.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess normality of the data distribution. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was applied to determine significant differences between groups. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, with significance levels indicated as: p < 0.001 (***), p < 0.01 (**), and p < 0.05 (*).

Results

Effects of pregabalin exposure on early development of zebrafish larvae

Pregabalin exerts adverse effects on the early growth and development of zebrafish larvae(Fig. 1B). The results showed that compared with the control group, there was no significant difference in the survival rate and hatching rate of zebrafish embryos exposed to pregabalin at ≤ 1 mg/L (Fig. 1A). At 24 hpf, exposure to varying concentrations of pregabalin significantly reduced the number of spontaneous movements per minute in zebrafish embryos starting from 1 mg/L (Fig. 1C, p < 0.05), while no significant change was observed in yolk sac area (Fig. 1D). Compared with the control group, when exposed until 72 hpf and 144 hpf, the body length development was inhibited at exposure concentrations of ≥ 0.1 mg/L and 1 mg/L, respectively (Fig. 1E, F); moreover, at the exposure concentration of 1 mg/L, the swim bladder area was significantly reduced (Fig. 1H).

Effects of pregabalin on locomotor behavior in zebrafish larvae

Pregabalin exerts adverse effects on the locomotion of zebrafish larvae. In this study, embryos were exposed until 144 hpf, and the behavioral indicators of zebrafish larvae were observed. Figure 2A shows the movement trajectories of zebrafish larvae across the entire testing period under light-dark stimulation at 144 hpf. The results demonstrated that when the exposure concentration was ≥ 0.1 mg/L, pregabalin caused a decrease in movement distance, movement speed, and movement duration of larvae under alternating light-dark stimulation (Fig. 2B-D); the freezing time increased significantly starting from the 0.1 mg/L concentration group (Fig. 2E, p < 0.05); while the activity and active duration of zebrafish larvae decreased significantly starting from the 1 mg/L and 0.1 mg/L concentration groups, respectively (Fig. 2F and G, p < 0.05).

Effects of pregabalin on neural development in zebrafish larvae

To further investigate the impact of pregabalin on the nervous system of zebrafish larvae, transgenic zebrafish Tg (hb9:eGFP) with motor neuron labeling were exposed to pregabalin until 144 hpf post-fertilization. A fluorescent stereomicroscope was used to observe and quantify the effects of pregabalin on the nervous system at 72 hpf and 144 hpf (Fig. 3A, B). The results showed that in Tg (hb9:eGFP) zebrafish exposed until 72 hpf and 144 hpf, when the concentration of pregabalin was ≥ 0.1 mg/L, the axon length of motor neurons was significantly shortened (Fig. 3C, D, p < 0.05). Additionally, the expression of neurodevelopment-related genes (elavl3, gfap, syn2a, and gap43) was significantly inhibited (Fig. 3E), which further verified that pregabalin exerts adverse effects on the neurodevelopment of zebrafish larvae.

Identification of neurotoxicity induced by pregabalin

In this study, we identified 100 potential targets of pregabalin through the SwissTarget Prediction database and 468 targets related to neurological disease damage via the Genecards database. After integrating and deduplicating these two sets of targets, a total of 11 intersection targets were obtained, which can serve as potential targets for pregabalin-induced neurotoxicity (Fig. 4A).

(C) KEGG enrichment analysis of potential targets (Red represents the highest level of enrichment, with the color gradually transitioning to green as the enrichment decreases from top to bottom.).

To investigate the potential toxic pathways of pregabalin, we constructed a PPI network using the STRING database and analyzed the topological properties of network nodes—including degree and betweenness centrality—with Cytoscape software. Based on this network analysis, eight core targets involved in pregabalin-induced neurological damage were identified (results are presented in Table S1). Notably, according to degree values, the core targets were determined to be Glutamate Ionotropic Receptor AMPA Type Subunit 2 (GRIA2) and Glutamate Metabotropic Receptor 5 (GRM5).

Subsequently, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed on these eight core targets using the DAVID database to identify the specific signaling pathways in which they are involved. A statistical bubble chart of significance was generated to visually display the top 10 KEGG signaling pathways ranked by P-value from smallest to largest (Fig. 4B). The results showed significant enrichment in the neuroactive ligand–receptor interaction pathway and the glutamatergic synapse signaling pathway. Furthermore, based on the above analytical results, a visually optimized network diagram of key protein–protein interactions was generated (Fig. 4C).

Molecular docking between pregabalin and core target proteins associated with nerve injury

To investigate the interaction between pregabalin and two core target proteins (GRIA2 and GRM5), molecular docking analysis was performed. The specific procedures were as follows: first, the three-dimensional structure of the pregabalin small molecule ligand was obtained from the PubChem database and optimized; meanwhile, the crystal structure of the target protein receptor was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank; subsequently, molecular docking between the small molecule ligand and the protein receptor was conducted using AutoDockTools software (Scripps Research Institute, USA). Visualization images of the lowest binding energy between targets and the compound were generated using Pymol and Discovery Studio software (Fig. 5). The results showed that the binding energy of GRIA2 was − 5.80 kcal/mol, indicating that pregabalin has a stronger affinity for GRIA2, suggesting that it plays a more important role in pregabalin-induced neurotoxicity and related molecular mechanisms.

Inhibitory effect of pregabalin on relevant signaling pathways in zebrafish larvae

To further explore the potential mechanism by which pregabalin affects neurodevelopment in zebrafish larvae, zebrafish embryos were exposed to pregabalin until 144 hpf. RNA was extracted, and the expression levels of genes related to neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction and glutamatergic synapses were quantitatively analyzed. The results showed that at an exposure concentration of 0.01 mg/L, the expression level of the core target gene gria2a decreased significantly compared to the control group (Fig. 6A); concurrently, the expression of htr2a and grm5a genes also began to be significantly inhibited (Fig. 6B, C, p < 0.001). These results indicate that pregabalin can inhibit signal transduction in zebrafish larvae. In addition, the expression levels of pro-apoptotic genes (p53 and bad) increased with the increase of exposure concentration, while the expression level of anti-apoptotic gene (bcl-2) decreased with the increase of concentration (Fig. 6D-F), suggesting that pregabalin induces neural cell apoptosis in zebrafish larvae.

Western blot results

To investigate the effects of pregabalin on the expression of related proteins in zebrafish larvae, Western blot was performed to detect the protein levels of GRIA2, P53, and BCL-2 after exposure to different concentrations of pregabalin, with β-actin used as the loading control. As shown in Fig. 7, compared with the control group, GRIA2 protein expression in the pregabalin-treated groups showed a concentration-dependent decrease, with a statistically significant difference beginning at 0.1 mg/L (Fig. 7A and B, p < 0.05). Meanwhile, P53 protein expression levels were significantly upregulated with increasing exposure concentrations (Fig. 7A and C, p < 0.05). The expression of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2 showed a concentration-dependent decrease, with a significant difference observed between the 0.01 mg/L group and the control group (Fig. 7A and D, p < 0.05). These results indicate that pregabalin suppresses GRIA2 expression, upregulates p53, and downregulates BCL-2 protein levels, suggesting that it may induce neurodevelopmental toxicity through activation of apoptotic pathways.

Discussion

Our study found that pregabalin exposure significantly induced neurodevelopmental toxicity in zebrafish larvae, as evidenced by reduced embryonic spontaneous movement, inhibited body length and swim bladder development, markedly decreased locomotor activity, and significantly shortened motor neuron axon outgrowth. Network toxicology and molecular docking confirmed that GRIA2 is the core target of pregabalin-induced neurotoxicity. Further mechanistic studies revealed that pregabalin interferes with the glutamatergic synapse pathway and the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway, thereby regulating the expression of apoptosis-related genes and proteins, inducing neuronal apoptosis, and ultimately leading to abnormal motor neuron development in zebrafish. These findings clarify the neurotoxic effects and molecular mechanisms of pregabalin on aquatic organisms, providing experimental evidence for its environmental risk assessment.

Zebrafish are widely used in the identification of teratogens, elucidation of toxicant mechanisms, and studies on tissue-specific effects of toxicants in vertebrates. They possess a unique medium-throughput system, along with advantages such as low cost and high fecundity, enabling rapid assessment of compound toxicity and investigation of specific molecular mechanisms21. Consequently, zebrafish have gradually become an important model in biomedical research and drug screening. Embryonic development marks the initiation of the entire developmental process, and abnormal spontaneous embryonic movement arises from defects in any component of the motor system pathway, including the central and peripheral nervous systems, neuromuscular junctions, and muscles22. In this study, compared with the control group, zebrafish embryos exposed to pregabalin until 24 hpf exhibited a significant reduction in the number of spontaneous movements. Furthermore, at 72 hpf and 144 hpf, the body length of zebrafish larvae was significantly shortened starting from the 0.1 mg/L and 1 mg/L concentration groups, respectively. At 144 hpf, the swim bladder area in the 1 mg/L group was significantly reduced, which may affect the motor behaviour of zebrafish larvae. In summary, pregabalin exposure exerts adverse effects on the early growth and development of zebrafish.

Motor behaviour in zebrafish, as a key indicator of neurodevelopment, is widely used to evaluate the neurotoxicity of various pollutants. The movement of zebrafish larvae is controlled by spinal neurons, making motor behavioural indices important for assessing neurotoxicity induced by environmental pollutants23. The results of this study demonstrated that pregabalin exposure led to decreased locomotor activity and speed, increased pause duration during movement, and reduced activity in zebrafish larvae. Previous studies have shown that endocrine-disrupting chemicals affect the motor behaviour of zebrafish larvae24,25, consistent with the findings of this experiment, indicating that pregabalin inhibits the motor behaviour of zebrafish larvae. Based on the results of early development and motor behaviour, it is reasonable to infer that pregabalin may affect the nervous system of zebrafish larvae.

The correlation between neuroanatomical structure, physiological characteristics, and behaviour in zebrafish is highly consistent with findings in mammals, making zebrafish a reliable and relevant model for studying neurological disorders26,27. The application of transgenic technology in fish represents a significant recent advancement, facilitating the elucidation of genetic mechanisms and developmental processes, and providing a more comprehensive system for assessing the health impacts of chemicals28. Its use in zebrafish has also extended to research on nervous system development and regeneration19. The genetically engineered enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP)29 can be regulated in two ways: first, by integration into the promoter region of the elavl3 gene (huc), which encodes a neuron-specific RNA-binding protein30; second, by being driven by hb9, a key gene in motor neuron development31. Additionally, syn2a is a critical gene involved in zebrafish neurodevelopment and synaptic function32; Gap43 can guide axon growth and regulate synapse formation33; Gfap is primarily expressed in astrocytes—cells that, as the most abundant glial subtype in the spinal cord, perform multiple physiological functions in neurological disorders34. This study utilised Tg(hb9:eGFP) transgenic zebrafish to evaluate the effects of pregabalin on neurodevelopment in zebrafish larvae, revealing that neural axon length was significantly inhibited at 72 hpf and 144 hpf. This is consistent with previous research using Tg(hb9:eGFP) to investigate the motor neurotoxicity of abamectin in zebrafish embryos31, both indicating inhibited development of motor nervous system neurons. Concurrently, the significant downregulation of neurodevelopment-related genes in this study further verified a positive correlation between pregabalin-induced motor behaviour inhibition and motor nerve damage in zebrafish larvae.

To further explore the mechanism of pregabalin-induced neurotoxicity in zebrafish larvae, this study combined network toxicology and molecular docking predictions, confirming gria2 as the core target gene. Gria2, a key component of ionotropic glutamate receptors (AMPA), is an important molecule in neuroscience and molecular biology. It regulates spinal motor neuron differentiation during embryonic development, and its deletion leads to abnormal development of motor nuclei; studies have shown that gria2 knockout mice exhibit motor coordination deficits35. Molecular docking is an effective method for studying interactions between chemicals and their biological receptors, simulating binding sites and interaction types, evaluating the stability of binding complexes, and providing a basis for revealing the toxic mechanisms of compounds in target organisms36. This technique has been widely used to predict and analyse potential mechanisms of toxicity induced by endocrine-disrupting chemicals37–39. The results of this study showed that pregabalin bound to gria2 protein with a binding energy of −5.80 kcal/mol, forming a relatively stable complex, suggesting a moderate interaction between them. Despite their importance in toxicological research and drug development, network toxicology and molecular docking have limitations such as data dependence, constraints in mechanistic inference, and computational resource requirements. Thus, experimental validation is necessary to enhance result reliability.

Meanwhile, KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway and glutamatergic synapse pathway were significantly enriched, with glutamate ionotropic receptor AMPA type subunit 2 A(gria2a), 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 2 A ༈htr2a༉, and glutamate receptor, metabotropic 5 A ༈grm5a༉genes distributed in both pathways. Among these, gria2, as a key subunit of ionotropic glutamate receptors (iGluRs), not only directly mediates glutamate signal transmission from the synaptic cleft to postsynaptic neurons but also regulates AMPA receptor calcium permeability, which is crucial for synaptic plasticity and thereby affects signal transmission efficiency40. The htr2a, a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR), regulates neurotransmission related to mood, cognition, perception, and sleep by binding serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine)41. The grm5a gene, belonging to the metabotropic glutamate receptor (mGluR) family, is a key regulator of glutamate signalling in the nervous system42. This study found that the expression levels of gria2a, htr2a, and grm5a were significantly downregulated at exposure concentrations ≥ 0.01 mg/L. Previous studies have confirmed that reduced gria2a expression or htr2a deficiency triggers massive Ca²⁺ influx, activating calcium-dependent proteases and pro-apoptotic signals, leading to motor neuron death43,44; conversely, grm5a knockout mice exhibit severe motor behavioural defects42. Therefore, it is hypothesized that pregabalin exposure may indirectly induce neurotoxicity in zebrafish larvae by triggering excessive Ca²⁺ influx and disrupting neuroactive ligand‒receptor interactions as well as glutamatergic synaptic pathways. The Western blot results of this study showed that pregabalin exposure concentration-dependently reduced the protein expression level of GRIA2 in zebrafish larvae, while upregulating P53 protein and downregulating BCL-2 protein expression. qPCR results also revealed that the expression of the pro-apoptotic genes p53 and bad was significantly upregulated, whereas the expression of the anti-apoptotic gene bcl-2 was markedly downregulated, suggesting that pregabalin exposure induces excessive neuronal death in zebrafish larvae. These protein changes were consistent with the qPCR results, further validating the neurodevelopmental toxicity of pregabalin and its underlying apoptotic induction mechanism at the protein level. Notably, even at environmentally relevant concentrations, pregabalin may still pose a neurodevelopmental risk to aquatic organisms. Moreover, the mutual corroboration between Western blot and qPCR results enhances the reliability of the conclusions. However, the specific molecular mechanism remains to be further investigated.

Conclusion

In this study, zebrafish were used as a model organism to predict the potential toxicity of pregabalin by integrating network toxicology and molecular docking techniques. Additionally, fluorescence qPCR was employed to further investigate its early neurodevelopmental toxicity and underlying mechanisms. The results showed that exposure to pregabalin disrupted the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway and the glutamatergic synapse signaling pathway in zebrafish, induced neuronal apoptosis, impaired motor neuron development, and ultimately led to abnormal locomotor behavior in larvae. By utilizing network toxicology and molecular docking approaches, the study systematically predicted and elucidated the neurotoxic mechanisms of pregabalin from multiple dimensions, while comprehensively assessing its neurotoxic effects on zebrafish larvae. It should be noted that molecular docking, as a predictive screening tool in ecotoxicological research, generates binding energy data whose biological significance requires further validation through in vivo experiments in zebrafish. In summary, this study not only provides a scientific basis for assessing the potential hazards of pregabalin to aquatic organisms in real-world environments but also offers new insights into the molecular mechanisms by which pollutants interfere with neurodevelopment in aquatic species. Furthermore, it provides critical data and a valuable reference for future research in related fields.
