Quantum plasmonics with N N emitters: bright hybrid continuum selection Preprint: APS/123-QED
Georgii Semin, Hans-Rudolf Jauslin, Gérard Colas des Francs, Stéphane Guérin

Abstract
We construct mode-selective effective models describing the interaction of the quantum plasmon-polariton field supported by a finite dielectric medium and one or several quantum emitters. The construction of the effective model is based on the decomposition of the field into bright modes relevant to the interaction with the emitters and dark modes, which do not interact with the emitters. We show that the quantum plasmon-polariton field can be represented equivalently by a double-continuum spectrum or by a single hybrid continuum spectrum for each emitter. The system of the electromagnetic field coupled to a finite medium is composed of two families of continuum modes, each of them with an infinite degeneracy. The two families are deformations of the free electromagnetic field and the free medium, induced by the interaction between them, as described by the Lippmann-Schwinger equations. We show that if there are N N emitters interacting with this plasmon-polariton field, the effective interaction involves a much smaller set of bosonic continuum modes: the interacting part of the continuum can be described by N N non-degenerate one-dimensional continua, one for each emitter. The representation of the interaction in terms of a single hybrid continuum spectrum coincides with the one within the macroscopic Langevin model with bulk medium. This coincidence is explained by an exact compensation of two terms, one in the coupling term of the Hamiltonian and the other one in a Green tensor identity.

In this article, we present the construction of effective (and exact) Hamiltonians characterizing the interaction of one or several quantum emitters with finite nanostructures via plasmon-polariton fields.

, the
N
-emitter formalism was adapted to describe both emitters and detectors.

In references

;
Miano et al. 2025b
;
Miano et al. 2026
, the DBM decomposition was partially explored in the modified Langevin noise model proposed in

;

;

Consider the Hamiltonian of the coupled QEs and the quantum plasmon polariton (QPP) field in the general form

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\text{QE}}+\hat{H}_{\text{QPP}}+\hat{H}_{\text{int}},

(1)

where
\hat{H}_{\text{QE}}
is the free Hamiltonian of the quantum emitters,
\hat{H}_{\text{QPP}}
is the free Hamiltonian of the QPP field, and
\hat{H}_{\text{int}}
describes the interaction between QEs and the QPP field. One can rewrite the interaction in terms of the operators of the collective modes of the QPP field, whose set does not coincide with the full set of the field model. We call these operators
bright operators
and the corresponding modes, which interact with the emitters,
bright modes
. The remaining modes, which do not interact with the emitters and are not visible to them, are called
dark modes
. The free Hamiltonian of the QPP field, decomposed on the bright and dark modes, takes the form

\hat{H}_{\text{QPP}}=\hat{H}^{\text{bright}}_{\text{QPP}}+\hat{H}^{\text{dark}}_{\text{QPP}}.

(2)

Since we have, by construction,
[\hat{H},\hat{H}^{\text{dark}}_{\text{QPP}}]=0
, the dark modes are not coupled with the other modes and are not affected by the interaction with the emitter, which allows one to drop the
\hat{H}^{\text{dark}}_{\text{QPP}}
term in (
2
) and define the exact reduced Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}=\hat{H}^{\text{bright}}_{\text{QPP}}+\hat{H}_{\text{QE}}+\hat{H}^{\text{bright/QE}}_{\text{int}}

(3)

with the interaction part
\hat{H}^{\text{bright/QE}}_{\text{int}}
written in terms of the bright modes remaining equivalent to its initial form
\hat{H}_{\text{int}}
.

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{m}=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\mu\,\hbar\nu_{\mu}\hat{C}^{m\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{C}^{m}_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\hbar\hat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\nu\,\Omega^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})+h.c.\right].

(28)

As shown below, the free QPP energy includes dark modes, i.e., those that are not involved in the coupled dynamics. We construct the corresponding dark operators
\hat{d}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}
, which will be eliminated from the dynamics,
by subtracting from the field operator
\hat{C}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}
its projection on the subspace generated by the orthogonal set of operators
\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})
, in a similar way of a step in a Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization procedure
Dzsotjan et al. 2016
;
Rousseaux et al. 2016
;
Castellini et al. 2018
:

\displaystyle\hat{d}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}):=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}[\hat{C}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}},\hat{b}^{m\dagger}_{\nu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})]\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}-\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})h^{m\ast}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}).

(29)

The dark operators satisfy the following commutation properties

\displaystyle\left[\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),\hat{d}^{m\dagger}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),\hat{d}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\right]=0,

(30a)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{d}^{m}_{\mathbf{x},\nu,j}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),\hat{d}^{m\dagger}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\right]

\displaystyle=\delta(\nu-\nu^{\prime})\delta_{jj^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times\big[\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-h^{m}_{\mathbf{x},\nu,j}\!(\mathbf{r}_{0})h^{m\ast}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\!(\mathbf{r}_{0})\big].

(30b)

The dark modes defined by (
II.3.1
) are neither orthogonal to each other nor normalized, which leads to the generalized commutation relations (
30b
). One could apply the orthogonalization procedure to obtain standard canonical quantization relations, but since these operators will not appear in the reduced model, there is no need to do it.

The free QPP energy associated with the medium can then be decomposed into the bright and dark terms as

\displaystyle\sum_{d_{\mu}}\hat{C}^{m\dagger}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}=\hat{b}_{\nu}^{m\dagger}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})+\sum_{d_{\mu}}\hat{d}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}^{m\dagger}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{d}^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}).

(31)

Omitting the dark modes, we define the effective Hamiltonian from the above decomposition as formally presented in (
3
):

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{m}_{\text{eff}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\nu\,\hbar\nu\,\hat{b}_{\nu}^{m\dagger}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle+\hbar\hat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\nu\,\Omega^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{b}^{m}_{\nu}(\mathbf{r}_{0})+\text{h.c.}\right].

(32)

II.3.2
DBM decomposition for the e-component

The term
-\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{e+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

-\hat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{e+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle=\hbar\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\,\Omega^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{b}^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0}).

(33)

One can construct similarly as before a normalized
bright annihilation operator
for the e-component, that sums over the degeneracy indices

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}h^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}},

(34a)

\displaystyle h^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=\frac{g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})}{\Omega^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})}

(34b)

with the coefficient
g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

), and the coupling strength
\Omega^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle g^{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}\hbar\omega_{\kappa}}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),

(35a)

\displaystyle|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}|g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2},

(35b)

and a dark operator

\displaystyle\hat{d}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}):=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}[\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}},\hat{b}^{e\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})]\hat{b}^{e}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),

(36)

such that

\displaystyle\left[\hat{b}^{e}_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0}),\hat{b}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{e\dagger}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\right]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime}),

(37a)

II.4
Merging of the two bright continua into a single hybrid continuum

We can now construct an effective Hamiltonian that contains a single hybrid continuum spectrum by merging the e- and m-continua, applying a secondary DBM decomposition. We define a normalized hybrid bright operator

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{\omega}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\omega}}\left(\Omega^{e}_{\omega}\hat{b}_{\omega}^{e}+\Omega^{m}_{\omega}\hat{b}^{m}_{\omega}\right),

(41a)

\displaystyle[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{B}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime}),

(41b)

with the coupling
\Omega_{\omega}

|\Omega_{\omega}|^{2}=|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}|^{2}+|\Omega^{m}_{\omega}|^{2}

(42)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\omega}=\sqrt{|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}|^{2}+|\Omega^{m}_{\omega}|^{2}}.

(43)

All the quantities in (
41

43
) depend on the QE position
\mathbf{r}_{0}

Due to the merging of two continua into a single hybrid continuum, we have to construct the secondary dark operators (also dependent on the position
\mathbf{r}_{0}

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{e}_{\omega}:=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\omega}^{e}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}[\hat{b}_{\omega}^{e},\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}]\hat{B}_{\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\omega}^{e}-\hat{B}_{\omega}\frac{\Omega^{e\ast}_{\omega}}{\Omega^{\ast}_{\omega}},

(44a)

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{m}_{\omega}:=

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{m}_{\omega}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}[\hat{b}_{\omega}^{m},\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}]\hat{B}_{\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{m}_{\omega}-\hat{B}_{\omega}\frac{\Omega^{m\ast}_{\omega}}{\Omega^{\ast}_{\omega}},

(44b)

which, combined with the definition (

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{e\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{b}^{e}_{\omega}+\hat{b}_{\omega}^{m\dagger}\hat{b}^{m}_{\omega}=\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{B}_{\omega}+\hat{D}^{e\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{D}^{e}_{\omega}+\hat{D}^{m\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{D}^{m}_{\omega}.

(45)

Excluding the dark modes, the final reduced exact Hamiltonian reads then

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\text{hyb})}_{\text{eff}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\,\hbar\omega\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{B}_{\omega}+\hbar\omega_{eg}\hat{\sigma}_{ee}

\displaystyle+\hbar\hat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\left[\!\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\,\Omega_{\omega}\hat{B}_{\omega}+\text{h.c.}\right].

(46)

It shows the involvement of a single effective hybrid continuum via the effective annihilation operator
\hat{B}_{\omega}
composed by a superposition of the two original types of annihilation operators
\hat{C}^{e}_{\kappa}
and
\hat{C}^{m}_{\mu}
via (

), (

\hat{B}_{\omega}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\omega}}\biggl[\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}g^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}\biggr].

(47)

One can rewrite the electric field part
\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{e+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})+\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{m+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_{0}\omega}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl(\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\mathbf{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}\mathbf{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}\biggr),

(48)

II.5
Direct DBM decomposition

Instead of obtaining the effective Hamiltonian with the double continuum and merging two continua sequentially, one can avoid the intermediate steps and obtain the effective Hamiltonian with a hybrid single continuum immediately. Here, we show that we can obtain Eq. (
46
) by a direct approach of the DBM decomposition, starting from the Hamiltonian (
6
), written in terms of the creation-annihilation operators
\hat{C}^{e}_{\kappa}
,
\hat{C}^{e\dagger}_{\kappa}
,
\hat{C}_{\mu}^{m}
,
\hat{C}_{\mu}^{m\dagger}
, and obtainig the effective Hamiltonian of the form (
46
). First, we rewrite the interaction part of the Hamiltonian (
6
), expanding the electric-field operator by (
II.2
), as follows

\displaystyle-\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{+}(\mathbf{r}_{0})

\displaystyle=\hbar\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega\biggl[\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}g^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}^{m}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\hbar\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega\,\Omega_{\omega}\hat{B}_{\omega}.

(49)

Here, we introduced the hybrid bright operator, defined by

\hat{B}_{\omega}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\omega}}\biggl[\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}g^{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}\biggr],

(50)

which has exactly the same form as (
47
). It satisfies the following commutation relation

\left[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}\right]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime}).

(51)

The coupling
\Omega_{\omega}
is chosen such that it satisfies the normalizing condition by the commutation relation, and the coupling reads

\displaystyle|\Omega_{\omega}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}|g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}|g^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}|^{2}+|\Omega^{m}_{\omega}|^{2},

(52)

where we used (
26b
) and (
35b
). One can see that the coupling has the same form as (
42
).

Introducing the dark mode operators by

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}:=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}[\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}},\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}]\hat{B}_{\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{c}\frac{g^{e*}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})}{\Omega^{*}_{\omega}}\hat{B}_{\omega},

(53a)

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}:=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}-\int^{+\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}[\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}},\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime}}]\hat{B}_{\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}-\frac{g^{m*}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})}{\Omega^{\ast}_{\omega}}\hat{B}_{\omega},

(53b)

such that

\displaystyle\left[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{D}^{e}_{\omega^{\prime},d_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\right]=\left[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{D}^{e\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime},d_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\right]=0,

(54a)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{D}^{m}_{\omega^{\prime},d_{\mu^{\prime}}}\right]=\left[\hat{B}_{\omega},\hat{D}^{m\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime},d_{\mu^{\prime}}}\right]=0.

(54b)

Then, the non-interacting QPP term of the Hamiltonian can be rewritten in terms of the bright and dark mode operators as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\hat{C}^{e\dagger}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}\hat{C}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}\hat{C}^{m\dagger}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}\hat{C}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\omega}\hat{B}_{\omega}+\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\hat{D}^{e\dagger}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}\hat{D}^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}+\sum_{d_{\mu}}\hat{D}^{m\dagger}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}\hat{D}^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}.

(55)

Omitting the dark mode operators since they do not participate in the dynamics of QE, one obtains the effective Hamiltonian (
46
).

II.6
Connection to the imaginary part of the Green tensor

From the DBM decomposition, the effective exact coupling reads from (
42
)

\displaystyle|\Omega_{\omega}|^{2}=|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}|^{2}+|\Omega^{m}_{\omega}|^{2},

(56)

where the coupling of the e-continuum can be expressed in terms of the e-coefficients, using Eq. (
35
), as follows

\displaystyle|\Omega^{e}_{\omega}|^{2}=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}|g^{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\frac{\pi c}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}|\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\biggl[\frac{\pi c}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\mathbf{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\biggr]\cdot\mathbf{d}_{eg},

(57a)

and the coupling of the m-continuum can be expressed in terms of the m-coefficients or in terms of the Green tensor and e-coefficients, using Eq. (
26
), expression of the m-coefficient in terms of the Green tensor (
13
), and expressing the integral of the Green tensor using Green tensor LDOS identity (
II.2
), as follows

\displaystyle|\Omega^{m}_{\omega}|^{2}=\sum_{d_{\mu}}|g^{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\biggl[\frac{\pi c^{2}}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{d_{\mu}}|\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\pi c^{2}}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{d_{\mu}}\mathbf{m}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\otimes\mathbf{m}^{*}_{\nu,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\bigg]\cdot\mathbf{d}_{eg}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\int_{V_{m}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\,\varepsilon_{i}(\mathbf{x},\omega)

\displaystyle\times\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r}_{0},\mathbf{x},\omega)\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r}_{0},\omega)\bigg]\cdot\mathbf{d}_{eg}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\bigg[\text{Im}\,\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r}_{0},\mathbf{r}_{0},\omega)

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi c}{2\omega^{3}}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\mathbf{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{0})\bigg]\cdot\mathbf{d}_{eg}.

(57b)

Then, due to the exact compensation of the term (
57
) with that of the same form, appearing in (
57
), the effective coupling, given by the sum (
56
), reads

|\Omega_{\omega}(\mathbf{r}_{0})|^{2}=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\hbar\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}\mathbf{d}_{eg}\cdot\text{Im}\,\bar{\bar{G}}_{m+}(\mathbf{r}_{0},\mathbf{r}_{0},\omega)\cdot\mathbf{d}_{eg}.

(58)

This result, which connects the coupling to the imaginary part of the Green tensor, has been used in the literature
Hümmer et al. 2013
;
Feist et al. 2020
where it was based on the bulk-medium approach
Gruner and Welsch 1996
;
Dung et al. 1998
;
Knoll et al. 2003
;
Scheel and Buhmann 2008
;
Buhmann 2013
, but has not yet been justified for finite-size nanostructures. We have proved this result by combining the correct expressions containing the two e- and m-contributions of the electric field (
12
) and the Green tensor LDOS identity (
II.2
), including the boundary term.

\hat{a}^{m(k)}_{\omega}
satisfy the commutation relations for each emitter

\displaystyle\left[\hat{a}^{e(i)}_{\omega},\hat{a}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{e(j)\dagger}\right]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})M^{e({ij})}_{\omega},

(63a)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{a}^{m(i)}_{\omega},\hat{a}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{m(j)\dagger}\right]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})M^{m(ij)}_{\omega}

(63b)

with

\displaystyle M^{e(ij)}_{\omega}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{d_{\kappa}}h^{e(i)}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}h^{e(j)*}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}\hbar\omega}\frac{\sum_{d_{\kappa}}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}_{eg}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{i})\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*}_{\omega,d_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{r}_{j})\cdot\mathbf{d}^{(j)}_{eg}}{\Omega^{e(i)}_{\omega}\Omega^{e(j)*}_{\omega}},

(64a)

\displaystyle M^{m(ij)}_{\omega}=

\displaystyle\sum_{d_{\mu}}h^{m(i)}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}h^{m(j)*}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}\hbar\omega}\frac{\sum_{d_{\mu}}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}_{eg}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{i})\otimes\mathbf{m}^{*}_{\omega,d_{\mu}}(\mathbf{r}_{j})\cdot\mathbf{d}^{(j)}_{eg}}{\Omega^{m(i)}_{\omega}\Omega^{m(j)*}_{\omega}},

(64b)

which are standard bosonic commutation relation when
i=j
since
M^{e(ii)}_{\omega}=M^{m(ii)}_{\omega}=1
. When
i\neq j
, these commutation relations reflect the fact that the operators
\hat{a}^{e(k)}_{\omega}
and
\hat{a}^{m(k)}_{\omega}
are not orthogonal in the QE-index since, in general,
M^{e(ij)}_{\omega}\neq 0
and
M^{m(ij)}_{\omega}\neq 0
. The
M^{e(ij)}_{\omega}
and
M^{m(ij)}_{\omega}
are called overlap coefficients (which are also called the elements of Gram matrices or metric matrices).

III.3
Direct single hybrid continuum orthogonalization

Here, we construct the effective Hamiltonian with a single hybrid continuum for each emitter, starting from the initial model with Hamiltonian (

Instead of the orthogonalization of the bright modes operators of the e- and m-continua separately, one can introduce a bright operator of a

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{int}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{(k)}\otimes\left[\mathbf{d}_{eg}^{(k)}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}^{+}(\mathbf{r}_{k})+\text{h.c.}\right]

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{(k)}\otimes\Bigg[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\omega

IV
Conclusions

In this article, we have derived exact mode-selective effective models of the QPP field coupled to one or several quantum emitters using the dark and bright mode decomposition of the three-dimensional quantum plasmonic-polaritonic field. The construction of the bright modes operators for a system with several emitters requires the orthonormalisation of the bright operators, which one can provide using, for example, Gram-Schmidt or Löwdin methods.

We constructed the effective models in two ways: by preserving the two-continua structure of the QPP spectrum and by introducing a single hybrid continuum. The coupling between the quantum emitters and the hybrid bright modes of the QPP is given by the Green tensor of the medium, which follows from the Green tensor LDOS identity, proven in Refs.
Semin et al. 2024
;
Semin et al. 2025
. Finally, we showed that one can always represent the two-continua DBM decomposition as the hybrid continuum decomposition. The resulting hybrid non-degenerate continuum Hamiltonians, see (
46
) and (
III.3.2
) for a single emitter and
N
emitters, respectively,
have the same form as the effective bright Hamiltonians obtained from the Langevin noise approach in
Hümmer et al. 2013
;
Castellini et al. 2018
;
Feist et al. 2020
. This result can be seen as a justification of its use.

This construction shows that the subset of effectively interacting models can be described by
N
one-dimensional, nondegenerate continuum bosonic modes, one for each emitter. We emphasize that the effective Hamiltonians with a single hybrid continuum contain fewer bright modes compared to the double-continuum effective Hamiltonians, which makes the former a preferable choice for numerical analysis due to lower computational resources required. Once the Hamiltonian is written in this form, one can apply some methods to select the dominant modes to construct simple approximations involving only finite-dimensional matrices, like e.g., quasi-normal modes
Sauvan et al. 2013
;
Franke et al. 2019
;
Ge and Hughes 2015
;
Hughes et al. 2018
, or other
Dzsotjan et al. 2016
;
Rousseaux et al. 2016
;
Castellini et al. 2018
;
Varguet et al. 2019
;
Feist et al. 2020
.

Appendix A
Löwdin orthogonalization for the bright operators

This Appendix is based on the Löwdin orthogonalization presented in
Castellini et al. 2018
.

The generalization of the construction of the effective Hamiltonians is not direct for
N>1
emitters since the set of operators
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
, defined in Eqs. (
61a
), (
61c
), or (
79
), is not orthogonal, and one can see it through the commutation relation

[\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega},\hat{a}^{(i)\dagger}_{\omega}]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})M^{(ij)}_{\omega}

(103)

with the overlap coefficient
M^{(ij)}_{\omega}
. To solve this problem, we construct a set of bright operators that are mutually orthonormal by taking suitable linear combinations of the
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
:

\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{\omega}^{(ji)}\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega},\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{ind}}

(104)

where
N_{ind}\leq N
is the number of linearly independent operators
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
, and the coefficients
\beta_{\omega}^{(ij)}
are chosen such that the new operators satisfy the orthonormality condition:

\displaystyle{\left[\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(i)},\hat{b}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{(j)\dagger}\right]}

\displaystyle=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\delta_{ij},

(105)

\displaystyle{\left[\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(i)},\hat{b}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{(j)}\right]}

\displaystyle=0.

For this purpose, we discuss the Löwdin orthogonalization method below.

In order to implement the orthonormalization procedure, we have to identify the number of linearly independent operators
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
. In
Dzsotjan et al. 2016
, it has been shown that if
|M_{\omega}^{(ij)}|=1
with
i>j
, then it means that
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
and
\hat{a}^{(j)}_{\omega}
overlap completely, i.e. the are the same operators up to a phase factor. Multiplying the commutation relation (
103
) by
M_{\omega}^{(ij)*}
leads to

\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}=M_{\omega}^{(ij)}\hat{a}^{(j)}_{\omega}.

(106)

Thus, we can identify a pair of linearly dependent field operators. There is also the possibility that one operator can be expressed as a linear combination of two or more other operators. To identify the number
N_{\text{ind}}
, we define for the
N
bright operators
\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega}
, the
N\times N
overlap matrix
M_{\omega}
via the overlap coefficients
M_{\omega}^{(ij)}
:

M_{\omega}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&M_{\omega}^{(2,1)}&\cdots&M_{\omega}^{(N,1)}\\
M_{\omega}^{(1,2)}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&M_{\omega}^{(N,N-1)}\\
M_{\omega}^{(1,N)}&\cdots&M_{\omega}^{(N-1,N)}&1\end{pmatrix}.

(107)

The overlap matrix
M_{\omega}
is Hermitian and positive semi-definite, so we can diagonalize it through a
N\times N
unitary matrix
T_{\omega}
:

\displaystyle D_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\text{diag}\left(\lambda_{1,\omega},\ldots,\lambda_{N_{\mathrm{ind}},\omega},0,\ldots,0\right)

(108)

\displaystyle=T_{\omega}^{\dagger}M_{\omega}T_{\omega}.

(109)

We implement Löwdin’s canonical orthonormalization, obtaining a new set of bright operators

\mathbf{B}_{\omega}=\mathbf{A}_{\omega}T_{\omega}D^{-1/2}_{\omega}

(110)

with

D^{-1/2}_{\omega}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1,\omega}^{-1/2},\ldots,\lambda_{N_{\text{ind}},\omega}^{-1/2},0,\ldots,0\right)

(111)

through the one-row arrays

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\left(\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(1)},\ldots,\hat{b}_{\omega}^{\left(N_{\text{ind}}\right)},0,\ldots,0\right),

(112)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\left(\hat{a}^{(1)}_{\omega},\ldots,\hat{a}^{(N)}_{\omega}\right).

(113)

Substituting the expressions of the matrices in (
110
), each bright operator
\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(i)}
of the orthogonal set can be expressed in terms of the old bright operators as follows

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(j)}=\lambda_{j,\omega}^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{\omega}^{(ij)}\hat{a}^{(i)}_{\omega},

\displaystyle\qquad j\in\left[1,N_{\mathrm{ind}}\right],

(114a)

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\omega}^{(j)}=0,

\displaystyle\qquad j\in\left[N_{\mathrm{ind}}+1,N\right],

(114b)

where
T_{\omega}^{(ij)}
are the elements of
T_{\omega}
. Thus, one can define the orthogonalization coefficients

\beta_{\omega}^{(ji)}:=\lambda_{j,\omega}^{-1/2}T_{\omega}^{(ij)},

(115)

which give the linear combination in (
104
).

Compared with the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, the Löwdin orthogonalization provides simple, compact formulas and an algorithm that is more stable in numerical implementations
Trefethen and Bau 1997
, allowing, in principle, the treatment of a large number of emitters. This can be seen by inverting (
110
),

\mathbf{A}_{\omega}=\mathbf{B}_{\omega}D^{1/2}_{\omega}T_{\omega},

(116)

which has the form of a singular value decomposition, which has good numerical stability properties.
