SUBJECT: H. R. 1;723, A Bill to Consolidate Laws Relating to

Dual Employment

1. Pursuant to your verbal request on 16 June 195k, this Office
reviewed the subject bill to determine its effect upon the employment

of Agency personnel. The bill was scrutinized with particular reference
to your question on the desirability of ; repealing P, L. 53 - 82d Congress
in the event H. ft. U723 is enacted.

2. The proposal provides in part that an individual, unless
specifically exempted by law, will be prohibited from receiving the
salaries of two Federal offices if the combined compensation exceeds the
rate of $6,250. If the combined rate ware to exceed this amount, how-
ever, the onployee would be entitled to ielect the greater of the two
compensations, although he could not receive both.

a. These provisions would replace the following sections of
the Act of 31 July I 89 U and the Economy Act of 30 June 1932 which
currently apply:

(1) The Act of 31 July 1691; states that no person can
be appointed or hold two or more offices when the salary of
either exceeds the sum of $2,500 (unless specifically
authorized by law). Retired eitlisted men and officers
retired for combat disability dr line of duty are exempt
from this restriction by an amendment to the Act.

(2) Section 212 of the Ecdnomy Act of 30 June 1932
limits the combined rate of compensation which can be received
in a civilian position and fro .a retired pay for commissioned
service to $3,000 per annum. However, if the rate is in
excess of $3,000, the officer may elect the greater of the
two rates of pay. Retired enlisted men and officers retired
for combat disability are not subject to the requirements of
this section.

b. Public Law 53 - 82d Congress authorizes the Agency to
employ 15 officers retired, for longevity without regard to the
Act of 31 July 189U. However, off dicers hired by the Agency under
the authority of P. L. 53 are required ta forfeit either the
civilian pay or the retirement income for the duration of their
employment.

c. Since the subject bill would repeal the restrictive pro-
visions of the Act of 189U, this Agency would apparently have no
further requirement for its special authority in P. L. 53. More-
over, the authority in P. L. 53 is [Limited to the employment of
15 officers, whereas the proposed bill would permit CIA and other
Federal agencies to hire an unlimited number of commissioned
personnel, subject to the restriction on the amount of combined
pay that such personnel could receive. As indicated above, the
bill would increase the maximum combined amount that could be
retained by retired officers employed by the Agency from $3,000 to
$6,250.
