A frequency-saliency guided multi-scale feature fusion network for robust UAV object detection
Authors: Lin Cheng, Fan Guo, Zedong Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based object detection plays an important role in intelligent surveillance and traffic monitoring. However, small object scales, complex backgrounds, dense distributions, and occlusions make it difficult to balance detection accuracy and real-time performance. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a frequency-saliency guided multi-scale feature fusion network based on YOLO11 for UAV object detection. Specifically, a Global Frequency Feature Extraction module is designed by integrating saliency enhancement and Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform to separate high-frequency target details from low-frequency background information. In addition, a lightweight multi-scale feature fusion module with soft gating and spatial attention is introduced to enhance feature representation across scales, and a Scale-Adaptive Dropout Feature Pyramid Network is employed to enhance robust multi-scale feature learning. Experimental results on the HIT-UAV dataset show that FSMF-YOLO achieves 91.6% precision, 83.5% recall, and 89.3% mAP@50 at 101 FPS, improving mAP@50 by 2.6% over YOLO11n while maintaining real-time inference. These results suggest that frequency-saliency guidance improves small-object representation in complex UAV scenes and provides a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off compared with lightweight YOLO-based detectors.

Introduction

In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely used in many fields because of their low cost, high mobility, and flexible deployment. UAV-based imaging has shown particular potential for aerial object detection1. UAVs equipped with high-resolution imaging devices can provide flexible aerial observations for surveillance, traffic monitoring, and emergency response. In aerial surveillance tasks, UAVs are capable of efficiently acquiring overhead imagery, thereby providing high-precision data for object detection and tracking, while UAV-based aerial data offer indispensable support for real-time intelligence collection2. Nevertheless, UAV aerial images often encounter challenges such as complex backgrounds, extremely small object scales, dense object distributions, and highly variable viewpoints. These characteristics significantly degrade the performance of traditional object detection algorithms when applied to such data3. Consequently, enhancing aerial object detection techniques, especially precise object recognition and tracking under complex environmental conditions-has become a major focus of current research4.

Deep learning methods based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have dominated the field of aerial image object detection5. In particular, single-stage detectors represented by the YOLO series, owing to their streamlined network architectures and efficient inference speeds, have achieved a favorable balance between accuracy and speed in industrial applications and are therefore widely adopted in scenarios with stringent real-time requirements6,7. However, the inherent inductive bias and limited local receptive fields of CNNs constrain their performance ceiling. When processing aerial imagery, objects often exhibit extreme scale variations and are highly susceptible to environmental occlusions8.

When dealing with tiny objects in complex backgrounds or targets exhibiting large-span spatial relationships, CNNs often struggle to effectively capture long-range dependencies, resulting in insufficient recognition capability and a higher likelihood of missed detections or false positives9. Transformer-based detectors, such as DETR and its variants, can model global relationships through self-attention. However, their computational complexity is high for high-resolution UAV images, and their inference speed is often insufficient for real-time deployment10,11. Therefore, despite the advantages of Transformers in global modeling, they still face practical limitations in terms of computational resources and real-time performance in real-world applications.

Although CNNs continue to dominate object detection, particularly in aerial imagery, they still face several limitations12. First, traditional CNNs primarily focus on local feature representation and exhibit inherent limitations in modeling long-range dependencies. This shortcoming becomes especially pronounced in complex scenes where objects are densely distributed or exhibit large spatial separations, often leading to the neglect of distant targets. Second, CNNs generally demonstrate limited adaptability to scale variations. In aerial images, the same object may undergo significant scale changes due to differences in shooting altitude or viewing angles. Although techniques such as multi-scale feature fusion have introduced certain improvements13, CNN-based models may still suffer from degraded detection accuracy when handling such variations. Moreover, CNN architectures typically demand substantial computational resources, particularly when processing high-resolution imagery, where both computational cost and memory consumption increase considerably. This further intensifies the trade-off between inference speed and resource utilization in real-time applications. Therefore, enhancing the global modeling capability of CNNs while improving their robustness to multi-scale variations and geometric deformations remains a critical challenge in current object detection research. Recent lightweight CNN-based detectors have further improved the deployment feasibility of aerial object detection by reducing model parameters and computational cost. For example, lightweight semantic feature extraction with direction awareness has been explored for aerial traffic object detection, showing the importance of efficient semantic representation in aerial scenes. In addition, recent CNN feature representation studies also indicate that enhancing convolutional feature modeling remains an important direction for improving visual recognition performance. However, most lightweight CNN-based methods still mainly operate in the spatial domain and do not explicitly distinguish high-frequency target details from low-frequency background responses. For UAV small object detection, this limitation may weaken the representation of tiny objects in complex backgrounds.

In UAV images, small objects usually occupy only a limited number of pixels and are easily submerged by complex background textures, thermal noise, occlusions, and dense object distributions. Conventional CNN-based detectors mainly rely on spatial-domain convolutional responses. Although such detectors are efficient, they do not explicitly distinguish target-related high-frequency details, such as edges and local textures, from low-frequency background structures. As a result, weak small-object responses may be suppressed during feature extraction and multi-scale fusion. This limitation motivates the introduction of a frequency-aware feature extraction strategy that can enhance discriminative high-frequency target cues while reducing redundant background interference.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes FSMF-YOLO, a frequency-saliency guided multi-scale feature fusion network improved based on YOLO11. The proposed method introduces a global frequency feature extraction module (GFFE), a lightweight multi-scale feature fusion module (LMFF), and a Scale-Adaptive Dropout Feature Pyramid Network (SA-DropFPN) into the YOLO11 framework, thereby enhancing small object feature representation, improving robustness under complex backgrounds and occlusion, and maintaining efficient real-time inference performance. It should be noted that the proposed method does not claim saliency enhancement, wavelet decomposition, attention, or dropout as isolated new techniques. Instead, the main contribution lies in a task-oriented integration of frequency-saliency enhancement, lightweight adaptive multi-scale fusion, and scale-adaptive pyramid perturbation for UAV small object detection. Different from existing YOLO-based improvements that mainly enhance spatial convolutional features, FSMF-YOLO explicitly introduces frequency-domain decomposition to separate target-related high-frequency cues from low-frequency background responses and combines this process with saliency-guided feature enhancement. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

(1) GFFE combining saliency feature enhancement and frequency feature fusion: By integrating saliency feature enhancement (SFE) with Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT), the module is designed to enhance small-object details in complex scenes and improve the response to discriminative regions.

(2) LMFF is designed to improve adaptive cross-scale interaction. Instead of directly concatenating or adding multi-level features, LMFF uses soft gating, grouped interaction, spatial attention, and detail enhancement to dynamically regulate feature fusion with limited computational overhead.

(3) SA-DropFPN is developed to improve robust pyramid-level feature learning during training. Different from conventional dropout applied to the backbone or detection head, SA-DropFPN introduces stochastic perturbation into pyramid-level feature fusion and uses scale-dependent dropout probabilities, thereby encouraging the detector to learn complementary multi-scale representations without increasing inference cost.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: In the Related Work section, we discuss previous research on aerial image object detection, small object detection, and object detection in complex scenes. In the Method section, we provide a detailed introduction to the improved model and methods. In the Experiments section, we present extensive experiments on multiple datasets and perform ablation studies to further validate the proposed approach. Finally, in the Conclusion section, we analyze the proposed method and provide a summary.

Related work

Aerial image object detection

Aerial object detection, particularly the detection of small objects, remains a significant challenge in the field of remote sensing. This is primarily due to the lower resolution of aerial images captured at high altitudes, the complexity of backgrounds, and the large variations in object sizes. In recent years, deep learning-based methods have made significant progress in detecting small objects, especially in Unmanned Aerial Vehicle(UAV)-captured remote sensing images. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various approaches, including multi-scale fusion, context information integration, and attention mechanisms. Multi-scale feature fusion has played a key role in improving small object detection. For example, ASOD-YOLOX14 integrates CrissCross Attention and the Bidirectional Adaptive Layer Fusion Network to combine multi-scale information and contextual features. Similarly, SMBlock, a dynamic scale-matching module proposed in15, can adaptively adjust the receptive field based on the needs of different objects, reducing information loss during the convolution process. These techniques enhance the representation of small objects, allowing for effective detection even in the presence of significant background noise. Additionally, Context-aware and Frequency Refinement Networks proposed in16 combine local and global feature extraction, boundary context-awareness modules, and self-attention frequency refinement modules to improve the feature representation of small objects. Moreover, CPDD-YOLOv817, which integrates the Global Attention Mechanism and a dynamic head structure, adaptively selects different feature layers to improve the integration of contextual information for small object detection. This method has shown effectiveness for small object detection. Attention mechanisms have been widely applied to refine feature maps, enhancing small object detection performance. For instance, CrissCross Attention in ASOD-YOLOX improves the feature representation of small objects. MFEL-YOLO18 introduces multi-stage feature enhancement and cross-layer feature fusion, improving small object detection performance, especially in terms of small object feature extraction in complex backgrounds. ADD-YOLO19, which combines AKConv, C2f_DRAC, and DABFPN structures, further improves small object detection capabilities, particularly for multi-scale feature extraction and fusion in complex backgrounds. SFFN-YOLO20 optimizes small object feature enhancement and fusion effects by introducing SFEM and ICARAFE modules, thereby enhancing detection accuracy for small objects. These studies demonstrate that integrating multi-scale feature fusion, context learning, and attention mechanisms is crucial for improving the detection of small objects in aerial images. Despite significant progress, achieving efficient real-time detection without sacrificing accuracy, particularly in complex backgrounds and occluded environments, remains a key challenge for future research. Lightweight CNN-based detection has also become an important direction for aerial object detection, especially for UAV platforms with limited computational resources. Recent studies have explored lightweight semantic feature extraction and direction-aware feature modeling to improve aerial traffic object detection efficiency21. Other CNN feature representation methods have also shown that strengthening convolutional feature modeling can improve visual recognition performance. Nevertheless, these methods mainly focus on efficient spatial feature extraction or general CNN representation enhancement22. They usually do not explicitly model the frequency-domain separation between small-object details and background structures. Therefore, how to preserve fine-grained target cues while maintaining lightweight inference remains an important issue in UAV small object detection.

Object detection in complex scenes

Object detection in complex scenes is a challenging task, especially when dealing with dense small objects, multi-scale targets, and complex backgrounds. With the rapid development of deep learning technologies, object detection methods based on CNNs and Transformer architectures have become a research hotspot. First,23 proposed an improved remote sensing image detection method based on a modified Transformer architecture. The method introduced the MogaC2f. module to enhance feature extraction efficiency and utilized the ContextGuidedBlock module to implement a context-aware mechanism, effectively improving the detection performance of small objects. In addition, the AIFIMSMHSA module, which combines multi-scale multi-head self-attention mechanisms, further enhanced spatial awareness and improved object localization accuracy.24 proposed the ICSnet model for industrial complex scenes, combining the Sobel operator and Wavelet Pooling modules to successfully improve object detection accuracy in complex backgrounds. The ICSnet network optimized the detection ability for large-scale object deformations with its innovative feature fusion and adaptive convolution modules, demonstrating excellent performance in industrial applications. In dense small object detection,25 proposed a fusion detection framework based on YOLOv7, combining the Self-Attention mechanism with the SSD algorithm. By using multi-scale feature extraction and adaptive convolution modules, the framework significantly improved the model’s detection accuracy and computational efficiency for dense small objects.26 introduced a lightweight model designed to tackle efficient detection in remote sensing scenarios. The model incorporated a Feature Aggregation Diffusion Network, which enhanced the detection of small objects through effective multi-scale context feature propagation. The Central Convolutional Detection Head combined with detail-enhancing convolutions reduced the model parameters by 23.4%, while maintaining competitive detection performance. At the same time,27 proposed a new object detection method based on a multi-layer attention mechanism, which combines Faster R-CNN and Feature Pyramid Network (FPN). By introducing a hierarchical attention module, the method optimizes multi-scale feature fusion, significantly improving detection accuracy for both small and large targets in complex scenes.28 proposed a method that combines GhostNet with attention-based feature fusion, reducing model complexity while improving detection performance in complex scenes. The architecture successfully handled various object sizes and occlusion issues through a combination of lightweight convolution operations and attention mechanisms, particularly excelling in small object detection. This method improved both speed and accuracy while maintaining low computational demands.29 proposed an improved object detection algorithm that combines deep learning and traditional image processing techniques, aimed at solving the target detection problem in complex scenes. By integrating FPN and introducing new loss functions (Focal Loss and GIoU Loss), the algorithm enhances detection accuracy. Despite the significant progress made in various application scenarios, challenges remain, such as target occlusion in complex backgrounds, the difficulty of detecting small objects in low-resolution images, and computational efficiency issues in high-resolution images. Therefore, designing more efficient and accurate detection models, particularly to cope with various interference factors in complex scenes, remains an important and unsolved problem in the field of object detection.

Research motivation

Although existing UAV object detection methods have improved small object detection through multi-scale fusion, attention mechanisms, and lightweight convolutional designs, several limitations remain.

(1) Most multi-scale fusion methods mainly aggregate spatial features across adjacent pyramid levels. They improve cross-scale information interaction, but they do not explicitly distinguish target-related high-frequency details from background-dominated low-frequency structures. As a result, weak small-object responses may still be suppressed by complex textures or thermal background interference.

(2) Attention-based methods can enhance salient regions, but they may also amplify noisy responses when targets are extremely small, partially occluded, or visually similar to the background. This problem is particularly evident in UAV infrared images, where background regions with similar thermal responses may produce false positives.

(3) Lightweight detectors reduce parameters and computational cost through compact convolution, pruning, or simplified detection heads. However, excessive compression may weaken fine-grained spatial details that are essential for small object localization. Therefore, improving efficiency alone is insufficient for UAV small object detection; the detector should also preserve discriminative details and robust multi-scale representations.

Motivated by these limitations, FSMF-YOLO introduces three complementary designs. GFFE performs frequency-saliency guided enhancement to strengthen high-frequency target cues and suppress redundant background responses. LMFF adaptively fuses cross-scale features using soft gating and grouped spatial interaction rather than simple feature concatenation. SA-DropFPN introduces scale-adaptive stochastic perturbation into pyramid-level feature fusion during training, encouraging the detector to learn more stable and complementary multi-scale features.

Method

The overall architecture of the proposed FSMF-YOLO is illustrated in Fig. 1. The network is improved based on the YOLO11 framework and is designed for lightweight UAV small object detection. It aims to enhance small-object feature representation under complex backgrounds while maintaining real-time inference performance. Compared with conventional YOLO-based improvements, FSMF-YOLO differs in three aspects. First, GFFE performs joint saliency-frequency modeling rather than simple spatial attention enhancement or convolutional feature refinement. Second, LMFF conducts lightweight adaptive fusion using soft gating and grouped spatial interaction, rather than directly concatenating or adding multi-scale features. Third, SA-DropFPN applies scale-adaptive stochastic perturbation to pyramid features during training, rather than using conventional dropout in the backbone or detection head. These designs are integrated into the YOLO11 framework to improve UAV small object representation while preserving real-time inference.Fig.1Overall architecture of the proposed FSMF-YOLO.

Global frequency feature extraction module

Feature extraction from images is a critical step in modern computer vision tasks. To better capture key regions and detail information in images, this paper proposes a multi-scale feature extraction module that combines SFE and DTCWT. The module aims to significantly improve the model’s performance in complex scenes by leveraging the synergistic enhancement of saliency features and frequency features. The structure is shown in Fig. 2. The proposed GFFE is not a direct application of DTCWT to feature maps. Instead, it combines saliency-guided spatial reweighting with frequency-domain decomposition. The saliency branch highlights candidate target regions, while the DTCWT branch separates high-frequency details and low-frequency structural responses. Their fusion allows the network to enhance small-object edges and texture cues while reducing the influence of background-dominated low-frequency information.Fig.2Structure diagram of GFFE.

Saliency feature enhancement

The core idea of the SFE module is to enhance the features of the salient regions in an image, allowing the network to focus more on these key areas. In traditional CNNs, although convolution operations can extract spatial features, in complex scenes, detailed information is often overshadowed by the background. Therefore, to enhance the network’s response to critical regions and suppress redundant background interference, a lightweight and efficient saliency feature enhancement module is designed. This module uses a three-stage processing flow-saliency map generation, gated fusion, and feature enhancement to adaptively reweight the input features, explicitly highlighting the discriminative spatial and channel regions. Lightweight multi-scale feature fusion module

This module combines multiple techniques, including a soft fusion gating mechanism, grouped interactions, spatial attention, detail enhancement, and semantic enhancement, to improve the network’s representational capability while maintaining computational efficiency. The design of this module decomposes the network into modular components, making it easier for subsequent applications and extensions. The structure is shown in Fig. 5. Different from conventional feature pyramid fusion that directly adds or concatenates multi-level features, LMFF introduces an adaptive fusion strategy. This process enhances the feature response in the salient regions, as described below:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A_{k} = {\mathrm{softmax}}\left( {Conv\left( {f_{g}^{k} } \right)} \right)$$\end{document}11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{k}^{{\mathrm{int}}} = f_{g}^{k} \odot A_{k}$$\end{document}

Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{g}^{k}$$\end{document} represents the features of the k group, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{k}^{{\mathrm{int}}}$$\end{document} is the fused feature. Finally, the features from all groups \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{k}^{{\mathrm{int}}}$$\end{document} are concatenated to obtain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f^{{\mathrm{int}}}$$\end{document}. To further stabilize the network’s training process, GroupNorm is applied to normalize the features. This operation helps improve the convergence speed and stability of the network during training.

To further enhance the detail information in the feature maps, we designed a detail enhancement path, which primarily includes depthwise separable convolution (DWConv) to optimize the features. Depthwise separable convolution is first used to extract local detail features, followed by pointwise convolution for channel integration. A gating mechanism is then applied to modulate the enhanced detail features.12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g = {\mathrm{Sigmoid}}\left( {Conv_{1 \times 1} \left( {GAP\left( {f_{g} } \right)} \right)} \right)$$\end{document}13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{d} = Conv_{1 \times 1} \left( {Conv_{3 \times 3} \left( {f_{g} } \right)} \right) \odot g$$\end{document}

This operation allows the model to dynamically adjust the intensity of detail enhancement, effectively improving the network’s response to small objects and detailed regions. Finally, a 1 × 1 convolution is applied to obtain semantic features, resulting in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f_{s}$$\end{document}. The final step involves performing a weighted sum of the features from the detail enhancement path, semantic enhancement path, and the context-normalized features, producing the final output feature map:14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{out} = Conv\left( {f_{d} + f_{s} + f_{n} } \right)$$\end{document}

Scale-adaptive dropout feature pyramid network

Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) is an effective multi-scale feature representation structure and has been widely used in object detection tasks. By integrating high-level semantic information with low-level spatial details, FPN enables the detector to handle objects with different scales. However, in UAV-based small object detection, the feature pyramid is easily affected by complex backgrounds, dense object distributions, and partial occlusions. In particular, small objects usually occupy only a few pixels in aerial images, and their feature responses are weak and sparse. During multi-scale feature fusion, the detection head may over-rely on several dominant feature channels or specific pyramid levels, which reduces the robustness of the model when object scale, viewpoint, or background texture changes.

To address this problem, this paper proposes a Scale-Adaptive Dropout Feature Pyramid Network (SA-DropFPN). Different from directly applying conventional Dropout to the backbone or detection head, the proposed SA-DropFPN introduces stochastic feature perturbation into the feature pyramid fusion process. The key idea is to randomly suppress partial feature responses during training, forcing the detection head to learn more stable and complementary representations from different pyramid levels. In this way, the model is encouraged to exploit multi-scale discriminative cues rather than relying on a fixed set of local responses, thereby improving the robustness of small object detection under complex backgrounds and occlusion conditions.

Given the multi-level feature maps extracted by the backbone and neck, the feature pyramid can be denoted as:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P = \left\{ {P_{1} ,P_{2} , \cdots ,P_{L} } \right\}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{l} \in R^{{C_{l} \times H_{l} \times W_{l} }}$$\end{document} represents the feature map at the l-th pyramid level, and L denotes the number of feature pyramid levels. In the proposed network, four levels of feature maps are used for pyramid feature fusion, and the final detection is performed on three output scales, corresponding to small, medium, and large object detection branches.

For each pyramid feature \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{l}$$\end{document}, a stochastic perturbation mask \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M_{l}$$\end{document} is generated during training. The perturbation operation can be formulated as:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tilde{P}_{l} = \frac{{M_{l} \odot P_{l} }}{{1 - p_{l} }}$$\end{document}17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M_{l} \sim {\mathrm{Bernoulli}}\left( {1 - p_{l} } \right)$$\end{document}where ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M_{l}$$\end{document} is the randomly generated binary mask, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_{l}$$\end{document} represents the dropout probability of the l-th pyramid level. The scaling factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{1}{{1 - p_{l} }}$$\end{document} is used to keep the expectation of the perturbed feature consistent with the original feature distribution, which can reduce the training-inference discrepancy.

Considering that different pyramid levels contain different types of information, a fixed dropout probability is not optimal for UAV small object detection. Shallow high-resolution feature maps contain more fine-grained spatial details, which are crucial for small object localization. If excessive feature dropping is applied to these layers, weak small-object responses may be damaged. In contrast, deeper low-resolution feature maps contain stronger semantic information but are more likely to include redundant background responses.

Experiments

Evaluation metrics

To fully evaluate various object detection methods, we used several evaluation metrics, including precision, recall, and mAP@50. Specifically, mAP@50 refers to the mean average precision at an IoU threshold of 0.5. The calculation formula is as follows:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Precision = \frac{TP}{{(TP + FP)}}$$\end{document}20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Recall = \frac{TP}{{(TP + FN)}}$$\end{document}21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$AP = \int_{0}^{1} {P(R)dR}$$\end{document}22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$mAP = \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N} {AP_{i} }$$\end{document}

Dataset and implementation details

The HIT-UAV dataset is a high-altitude UAV-based infrared thermal object detection dataset. According to the official dataset description, it contains 2,898 infrared thermal images extracted from 43,470 UAV video frames. The images were collected from various outdoor scenarios, including schools, parking lots, roads, and playgrounds. The dataset provides flight-related metadata for each image, including flight altitude, camera perspective, shooting date, and daylight intensity. The flight altitude ranges from 60 to 130 m, and the camera perspective ranges from 30° to 90°. The dataset covers both daytime and nighttime conditions and includes five categories: Person, Car, Bicycle, OtherVehicle, and DontCare. In our experiments, the DontCare category was not used as a target category for model training and evaluation.

The VisDrone2019 dataset is a large-scale drone-based visual benchmark collected by the AISKYEYE team at Tianjin University. According to the official dataset description, the benchmark consists of 288 video clips with 261,908 frames and 10,209 static images captured by various drone-mounted cameras. The data cover 14 different cities in China and include diverse locations, environments, object categories, and object densities, such as urban and rural scenes, pedestrians, vehicles, bicycles, sparse scenes, and crowded scenes. The dataset was collected using different drone platforms under different scenarios and various weather and lighting conditions. Important attributes, including scene visibility, object category, and occlusion, are also provided for better data utilization. For the object detection task, VisDrone2019-DET focuses on ten predefined categories: pedestrian, person, car, van, bus, truck, motor, bicycle, awning-tricycle, and tricycle.

All experiments were conducted under the same training and evaluation configuration to ensure a fair comparison. The experiments were performed on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080Ti GPU with 11 GB memory. The implementation was based on Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.1.2 and CUDA 11.8. The input images were uniformly resized to 640 × 640. The batch size was set to 8, the number of training epochs was set to 300, and the number of data loading workers was set to 2. The AdamW optimizer was adopted with an initial learning rate of 0.001, a weight decay of 0.0005, and momentum parameters of 0.937. The random seed was fixed at 2026 for all experiments. FPS was measured on the same NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080Ti GPU with batch size 1 after model warm-up.

Conclusion

The experimental results show that FSMF-YOLO exhibits stable and balanced performance advantages on both typical UAV datasets. This result is primarily attributed to the synergistic effect of saliency enhancement, frequency-domain feature decomposition, and lightweight multi-scale fusion. Compared to traditional detection networks that rely solely on spatial convolution features, the introduction of frequency-domain modeling allows for the explicit separation of high-frequency target information and low-frequency background regions at early stages, enhancing the separability and discriminative power of small targets in complex environments. Meanwhile, the saliency feature enhancement module dynamically adjusts feature response intensity through a gating mechanism, effectively suppressing noise regions and avoiding false positives caused by excessive feature enhancement. In the VisDrone2019 dataset, which involves complex urban scenes, the overall accuracy metrics still maintain a leading advantage, indicating that the proposed network has strong generalization capabilities under multi-scale target distributions and occlusion interference conditions. Additionally, the scale-adaptive dropout strategy itself introduces no additional learnable parameters and helps improve the robustness of pyramid feature learning. This is particularly significant for resource-constrained embedded deployment scenarios.

However, the current method still has some limitations. First, under extreme low-resolution or severe occlusion conditions, the semantic information of small targets may still be insufficient, thus limiting the detection upper bound. Second, frequency-domain transformations theoretically introduce some computational overhead. Although the overall GFLOPs are kept at a relatively low level, there is still room for optimization in ultra-high-frame-rate scenarios. Future work can explore directions such as multi-modal information fusion, adaptive frequency selection mechanisms, and dynamic structural compression to further improve the model’s adaptability and real-time performance in complex environments and edge computing platforms.
