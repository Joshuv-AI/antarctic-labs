MRI features outperform histologic grade for outcome prediction in translocation-driven enriched soft-tissue sarcoma
Authors: Han-Ying Lin, Ching-Lan Wu, Chih-Hsueh Chen, Chueh-Chuan Yen, Chao-Ming Chen, Chien-An Liu, Po-Kuei Wu, Hung-Ta Wu, Chung-Jung Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMost MR-based prognostic series in soft-tissue sarcoma (STS) were derived in cohorts dominated by complex-karyotype histologies; whether conventional MR descriptors retain prognostic value in translocation-driven enriched populations is unclear. Two radiologists blinded to outcome independently scored 13 prespecified MR features. Cox regression adjusted for age, sex, maximum tumor diameter, and Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer (FNCLCC) grade was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs); a sensitivity analysis added translocation-driven histology.ResultsA total of 158 patients (mean age, 48 years ± 19 [SD]; 84 men) were evaluated; 61 of 152 classifiable patients (40%) had translocation-driven tumors. Background

Soft-tissue sarcomas (STS) are a biologically heterogeneous group of mesenchymal malignancies that account for fewer than 1% of adult cancers but contribute disproportionately to morbidity because they are frequently diagnosed at a locally advanced stage and recur despite aggressive multimodal therapy [1]. Preoperative MRI is central to defining local extent and guiding surgical planning [2–4], yet only a subset of conventional MR descriptors has been shown to carry prognostic weight [5–7].

Most published MR-prognostic series were derived in cohorts dominated by older adults and complex-karyotype histologies [6, 8, 9]. Translocation-driven subtypes (for example synovial sarcoma, myxoid liposarcoma, alveolar soft-part sarcoma, clear-cell sarcoma, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, and low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, each defined by a recurrent gene fusion) — which can behave differently biologically — are under-represented in these cohorts, and the MR descriptors that retain independent prognostic value in translocation-driven enriched populations have not been systematically tested [10–12].

We therefore re-examined a prespecified panel of 13 conventional MR features in a single-center cohort that included a higher-than-usual proportion of translocation-driven sarcomas. We deliberately analyzed the full sarcoma cohort, enriched for but not restricted to translocation-driven subtypes, so that the resulting descriptors could be applied prospectively at the time of MRI — before histologic and molecular classification are known — and would remain generalizable to unselected sarcoma populations. Our aim was to identify descriptors that retain independent prognostic value after adjustment for age, sex, tumor size, and Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer (FNCLCC) grade. We hypothesized that two binary visual descriptors — peritumoral edema and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement — would independently predict overall survival and local recurrence-free survival and that this signal would be independent of histologic grade and provide information that grade does not capture.

Methods

Study design and cohort

This retrospective single-institution study was approved by our institutional review board, which waived the requirement for informed consent. Consecutive adults (≥ 18 years) with histologically confirmed soft-tissue sarcoma who underwent contrast-enhanced MRI before any treatment between January 2008 and December 2024 were eligible. Exclusion criteria were head-and-neck, visceral, or retroperitoneal location; Ewing sarcoma; prior resection or chemoradiation; suboptimal imaging quality (e.g., lack of contrast-enhanced sequences); and missing survival data. After applying these criteria, 158 patients remained for analysis (Fig. 1). Clinical variables — age, sex, maximum tumor diameter, anatomic site, histologic subtype, FNCLCC grade, resection margin status, and neoadjuvant and adjuvant therapies — were abstracted from the electronic medical record. All patients were treated per contemporary ESMO and NCCN guidelines [13–16]. This study is reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement.

Patient selection and analysis flow

MR protocol and image analysis

MR examinations were performed on 1.5-T or 3-T scanners, at our institution or at outside facilities, before any treatment. Each examination included at minimum axial T1-weighted, T2-weighted with or without fat suppression, coronal or sagittal T2-weighted with or without fat suppression, and post-gadolinium T1-weighted sequences. Studies were reviewed on a dedicated workstation by two radiologists (reader 1, 15 years of musculoskeletal MRI experience; reader 2, 6 years), blinded to clinical outcome and histologic grade.

Readers independently scored 13 prespecified MR features [8, 17]: T1 signal heterogeneity, T2 signal heterogeneity, heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement (each defined as visually discernible spatial variation in signal intensity involving more than 50% of the tumor cross-section on a representative slice), peritumoral enhancement, peritumoral edema, intratumoral necrosis, intratumoral hemorrhage, tumor margin (well-defined vs. infiltrative) in T1 and T2, neurovascular encasement, bone invasion, nodal metastasis, and tail sign. The 13 features were prespecified from descriptors previously reported to carry prognostic value in soft-tissue sarcoma, so that any prognostic signal identified could be translated into practice without specialized acquisition [6–7, 9]. Each feature except tumor margin was scored as present or absent. Disagreements were resolved by consensus. Reader 1 rescored all examinations after a minimum interval of 8 weeks between the two scoring sessions, to estimate intra-reader agreement and reduce the chance that the reader recalled the first reading. Inter- and intra-reader agreement was summarized with the Cohen κ overall and per feature; per-feature values are reported in Additional Table A1. Maximum tumor diameter was measured on the coronal or sagittal plane best capturing the long axis.

Histology classification

Histologic diagnoses were established on surgical specimens by two dedicated bone-and-soft-tissue pathologists using the 2020 WHO classification [5], with discordant cases resolved by joint review (concordance, 149 of 152; 98%); six cases with uncertain molecular classification were excluded from histology-adjusted analyses. Whenever available, diagnoses were confirmed via molecular testing, such as fluorescence in situ hybridization or reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction, identifying characteristic fusion transcripts [18]. Tumors were classified as translocation-driven when the diagnosis corresponded to a sarcoma subtype defined by a recurrent, characteristic gene fusion — for example synovial sarcoma (SS18::SSX), myxoid liposarcoma (FUS::DDIT3), alveolar soft-part sarcoma (ASPSCR1::TFE3), clear-cell sarcoma (EWSR1::ATF1), dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (COL1A1::PDGFB), or low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (FUS::CREB3L2) — confirmed by fluorescence in situ hybridization or reverse transcription–polymerase chain reaction whenever molecular testing was available [19, 20]; all remaining subtypes, characterized by complex, non-recurrent genomic alterations rather than a single driver fusion (e.g., undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma and spindle-cell sarcoma not otherwise specified), were classified as complex karyotype [10, 21].

Outcomes

Three prespecified endpoints were overall survival (OS; time from MRI to all-cause death), local recurrence-free survival (LRFS; time to biopsy-proven or imaging-confirmed recurrence at the primary site), and metastasis-free survival (MFS; time to first distant metastasis). Patients without events were censored at the date of last clinical contact.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are summarized as mean ± SD or median (IQR); categorical variables as counts and percentages. Between-group differences were assessed with the two-sample t test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, χ² test, or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier estimates were compared with the log-rank test. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs for each of the 13 prespecified MR features. The primary multivariable model adjusted for age, sex, maximum tumor diameter, and FNCLCC grade; ridge penalization (λ = 0.01) was applied to stabilize estimates. A sensitivity analysis added translocation-driven histology as a fifth covariate. Translocation-driven histology was modeled as a covariate rather than as an inclusion criterion so that the prognostic value of the MR features could be assessed across the full sarcoma spectrum and tested for independence from molecular class. Proportional hazards assumptions were assessed with Schoenfeld residuals; no violation was detected for the primary multivariable covariates (all global tests P > .10). To test whether heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement acted as a grade surrogate, we fit a logistic regression of FNCLCC grade 3 (vs. grade 1–2) on the 13 MR features with age, sex, and maximum diameter; discrimination was summarized as area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Per the prespecified Statistical Analysis Plan, peritumoral edema and peritumoral enhancement were not entered simultaneously because of collinearity; peritumoral edema was the primary peritumoral-domain covariate, and peritumoral enhancement was tested in a drop-one sensitivity model. Median follow-up was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method [22]. All P values are two-sided; P < .05 was considered significant. Analyses were performed with Python 3.11.8 (lifelines 0.27.8, scikit-learn 1.4.1, pandas 2.2.0, NumPy 1.26.3).

Results

Patient characteristics

The study included 158 patients (mean age, 48 years ± 19 [SD]; 84 men [53%]). Median tumor diameter was 77 mm (IQR: 48, 114), and 124 of 158 patients (79%) had FNCLCC grade 3 disease. The most common histologic subtypes were synovial sarcoma (34 of 158 [22%]), undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (30 of 158 [19%]), and spindle-cell sarcoma not otherwise specified (21 of 158 [13%]). Molecular status was classifiable in 152 patients, of whom 61 (40%) had translocation-driven tumors — predominantly synovial sarcoma (34 of 61 [56%]) and myxoid liposarcoma (13 of 61 [21%]); the full subtype breakdown appears in Table 1. Heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement was present in 118 of 158 patients (75%) and peritumoral edema in 105 of 158 (66%). Patients with heterogeneous enhancement were older (mean, 51 vs. 41 years; P = .015), had larger tumors (median, 86 vs. 52 mm; P = .001), and were more often men (58% vs. 38%; P = .035); those with peritumoral edema were also older (51 vs. 44 years; P = .04) and had larger tumors (86 vs. 69 mm; P = .01). FNCLCC grade 3 proportion did not differ by either feature (Table 1).

Follow-up and survival

Median reverse Kaplan–Meier follow-up was 68 months for OS, 52 months for LRFS, and 65 months for MFS. During follow-up, 58 patients died, 53 developed local recurrence, and 73 developed distant metastasis. Kaplan–Meier curves stratified by the four MRI phenotypes defined jointly by peritumoral edema and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement separated progressively for both endpoints (Fig. 2). For overall survival (Fig. 2a), 5-year estimates were 95% in patients with neither feature, 81% with peritumoral edema only, 64% with heterogeneous enhancement only, and 42% with both features (log-rank P < .001). For local recurrence–free survival (Fig. 2b), the 5-year estimates were 87%, 58%, 75%, and 48%, respectively (log-rank P = .005). The two single-feature phenotypes ranked discordantly across endpoints, consistent with peritumoral edema indexing local aggressiveness and heterogeneous enhancement indexing systemic risk. OS estimates beyond 120 months reflect tail-of-curve extrapolation (fewer than 20% of patients remained at risk).

MR features and overall survival

Inter-reader agreement for the 13 features was substantial (κ = 0.86); intra-reader agreement was 0.91 for reader 1 and 0.84 for reader 2 (Additional Table A1). At univariable Cox analysis, the strongest MR predictors of shorter OS were heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement (HR, 4.4 [95% CI: 2.0, 9.8]; P < .001), vascular–nerve invasion (HR, 2.4; P = .002), intratumoral necrosis (HR, 2.2; P = .004), peritumoral edema (HR, 2.1; P = .02), and T1/T2 heterogeneity (HR ≈ 2.0; both P < .05). Maximum diameter (HR per 10-mm increment, 1.03 [95% CI: 1.01, 1.05]; P < .001), male sex (HR, 2.1; P = .008), and age (HR per year, 1.02; P = .008) were also significant; FNCLCC grade 3 was not (HR, 1.4; P = .38). In the primary multivariable model (n = 151; 54 events; C-index, 0.77), heterogeneous enhancement (HR, 3.9 [95% CI: 1.4, 10.7]; P = .008) and peritumoral edema (HR, 2.0 [95% CI: 1.0, 3.7]; P = .04) remained independently associated with shorter OS (Table 2).

MR features and local recurrence–free survival

At univariable analysis, the strongest LRFS predictors were peritumoral edema (HR, 2.5 [95% CI: 1.3, 4.8]; P = .007) and peritumoral enhancement (HR, 2.4 [95% CI: 1.2, 4.5]; P = .009) — correlated manifestations of the peritumoral microenvironment (φ, 0.48; variance inflation factor, 1.5). Heterogeneous enhancement was a weaker univariable signal (HR, 2.0 [95% CI: 1.0, 3.9]; P = .06), and FNCLCC grade was not significant. In the prespecified multivariable model (n = 154; 50 events; C-index, 0.65), peritumoral edema was the only independent predictor (HR, 2.3 [95% CI: 1.2, 4.5]; P = .01); heterogeneous enhancement did not reach independent significance (HR, 1.7 [95% CI: 0.8, 3.4]; P = .14). A supplementary model substituting peritumoral enhancement for peritumoral edema yielded a comparable independent effect (HR, 2.2 [95% CI: 1.1, 4.2]; P = .02), indicating that the peritumoral-domain signal is robust to feature choice (Table 3).

MR features and metastasis-free survival

Univariable MFS predictors were heterogeneous enhancement (HR, 3.1; P = .001), intratumoral necrosis (HR, 2.3; P = .002), vascular–nerve invasion (HR, 1.9; P = .02), maximum diameter (HR per 10-mm increment, 1.02 [95% CI: 1.01, 1.04]; P = .004), and male sex (HR, 1.8; P = .03); FNCLCC grade was not significant. In the multivariable model (n = 145; 60 events; C-index, 0.70), heterogeneous enhancement was a trend-level independent predictor (HR, 2.0 [95% CI: 0.9, 4.5]; P = .08); no feature reached the prespecified significance threshold, consistent with a lower event-per-variable ratio (Table 4).

Histology-adjusted sensitivity analysis

When translocation-driven histology was added as a fifth covariate, heterogeneous enhancement remained an independent OS predictor (HR, 3.0 [95% CI: 1.2, 7.9]; P = .02); peritumoral edema attenuated to a trend for OS (HR, 1.8; P = .10) after exclusion of six patients with uncertain histologic class but retained independence for LRFS (HR, 2.4 [95% CI: 1.2, 4.9]; P = .01). Translocation-driven histology itself was not independently prognostic for any endpoint. A triple-forced model (Additional Table A2) simultaneously entering age, sex, maximum diameter, FNCLCC grade 3, heterogeneous enhancement, surgical margin, and translocation-driven histology confirmed heterogeneous enhancement as an independent predictor of OS and MFS and a trend-level predictor of LRFS.

Histology grade-surrogate analysis

Logistic regression of FNCLCC grade 3 on the 13 MR features, adjusted for age, sex, and maximum diameter, showed poor grade discrimination (AUC, 0.59); no MR feature was independently significant (all P > .10), including heterogeneous enhancement (odds ratio, 0.74; P = .22), and peritumoral edema (odds ratio, 1.18; P = .68). The same panel modestly discriminated translocation-driven from complex-karyotype histology (AUC, 0.71), driven predominantly by younger age in translocation-driven cases (Additional Table A3).

Discussion

In 158 adults with contrast-enhanced MRI of newly diagnosed soft-tissue sarcoma, two binary visual descriptors — peritumoral edema and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement — were independently and complementarily associated with shorter overall and local recurrence-free survival. Both findings held after adjustment for age, sex, tumor size, FNCLCC grade, and, in sensitivity analysis, translocation-driven histology. Neither FNCLCC grade nor translocation-driven class was independently prognostic, and a 13-feature MR panel failed to discriminate grade (AUC, 0.59), arguing against a latent grade-surrogate interpretation for either descriptor. The OS hazard ratio of heterogeneous enhancement is comparable in magnitude to the deep-radiomic risk clusters reported in recent series [23, 24], and the qualitative descriptor is independent of the translocation-driven versus complex-karyotype distinction [11, 25]. These results identify two compact, readily reported MR phenotypes — a peritumoral signal indexing local biologic aggressiveness and an intratumoral vascular-heterogeneity signal indexing systemic aggressiveness — that together outperformed conventional histologic-grade and molecular stratifiers in a translocation-driven enriched cohort. Three aspects of these findings extend prior work rather than restate it: which MR descriptors retained independent value depended on case-mix in this translocation-enriched cohort; the two descriptors showed an endpoint-specific division of labor, with peritumoral edema predicting local recurrence and heterogeneous enhancement predicting overall and metastatic outcome; and neither acted as a surrogate for histologic grade, since a 13-feature panel did not discriminate grade (AUC, 0.59). These descriptors are pan-histologic imaging traits rather than markers specific to translocation-driven tumors; their value here lies in retaining prognostic information in a population in which histologic grade and molecular class did not.

Heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement has a direct pathophysiologic rationale: regions of perfusion mismatch on post-contrast imaging reflect disorganized angiogenesis, variable interstitial pressure, and microscopic necrosis — hallmarks of biologic aggressiveness. This interpretation aligns with the dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) MRI literature: Li et al. demonstrated that DCE pharmacokinetic parameters, particularly K-trans and Ve, track with histologic grade and short-term outcome in soft-tissue sarcoma [26]. Our result extends these findings by showing that a simple binary visual call, recorded on routine post-gadolinium images without pharmacokinetic modeling, captures much of the prognostic signal that DCE parameters describe quantitatively. The failure of a 13-feature MR panel to discriminate histologic grade (AUC, 0.59) further supports that heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement is not acting as a grade surrogate; its prognostic signal likely reflects a vascular-disorganization trait that crosses histologic and molecular lines. Pragmatically, this means the pattern can be incorporated into standard MR reports without specialized acquisitions, extending access beyond centers with DCE expertise (Fig. 2).

The endpoint-specific profile is clinically informative: heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement reflects a systemic-risk phenotype, and peritumoral edema reflects a local biologic-aggressiveness phenotype. The dominant role of surgical factors in local control is well established [27]. Combining the two descriptors stratifies patients into four pragmatic risk groups; the highest-risk group (both features positive) carried the lowest 5-year OS and LRFS estimates, whereas the lowest-risk group (both features negative) carried the highest.

The independent prognostic value of peritumoral edema is consistent with the edema-radiomics literature [28, 29], which has argued that the peritumoral region — not just the tumor core — contains biologically active tissue whose imaging features shape outcome. Peritumoral T2 hyperintensity in soft-tissue sarcoma likely reflects a composite of microscopic tumor infiltration beyond the gross margin, lymphatic obstruction, and neovascular leakage, all of which plausibly drive local recurrence [30]. The joint four-group stratification in Fig. 2 makes this division of labor visible: heterogeneous enhancement alone conferred the second-lowest 5-year overall survival (64%) but the second-highest local recurrence–free survival (75%), whereas peritumoral edema alone showed the reverse (81% and 58%). This discordant ranking is most parsimoniously explained by two biologically distinct processes — peritumoral edema indexing a peripheral, infiltrative phenotype that drives local relapse, and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement indexing intratumoral vascular disorganization that drives distant progression and death. Operationally, peritumoral edema flags risk of local recurrence and supports heightened local surveillance, whereas heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement flags systemic risk and supports intensified metastatic screening. Inter-reader agreement for both descriptors was substantial (κ = 0.86), supporting reproducibility in routine practice on conventional sequences without specialized postprocessing (Fig. 3).

Several MR-prognostic signals reported in earlier cohorts attenuated in our analysis. Crombé et al. [9] reported that peritumoral enhancement, intratumoral necrosis, and more than 50% T2 heterogeneity were independently associated with metastasis; in our translocation-driven enriched cohort, these descriptors attenuated after adjustment, while heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement retained significance. Peeken et al. and Yan et al. [31, 32], reported MR-radiomic grade classifiers with validation AUCs of 0.78–0.88; our grade logistic AUC of only 0.59 demonstrates that those pipelines operate in a different part of the feature space than our case-mix permits. Fernebro et al. [9] reported that diffusely infiltrative peripheral growth on MRI was strongly associated with recurrence in a population-based Scandinavian cohort dominated by complex-karyotype histologies, yet tumor margin was not prognostic in our translocation-driven enriched cohort. Instead, peritumoral edema and enhancement better reflected tumor behavior and predicted patient outcomes than did histology or tumor margin on T1- or T2-weighted sequences [33, 34]. For example, alveolar soft-part sarcoma characteristically displays infiltrative margins that reflect its distinctive vascular biology rather than conventional prognostic determinants [35]. The most parsimonious explanation is case-mix: translocation-driven tumors are younger, smaller, and less frequently necrotic, and the descriptors that survive multivariable adjustment depend on the histologic substrate. Three features of our cohort help explain the divergence: the mean age (48 years) is roughly a decade younger than typical adult STS series; 40% of patients had translocation-driven tumors — roughly double the proportion in many Western tertiary referral cohorts; and restriction to contrast-enhanced examinations enriched for larger tumors [11]. Together, these features explain the preserved signal for heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement (a pan-histologic vascular-disorganization trait) and the attenuation of descriptors that track more tightly with complex-karyotype biology.

Clinical implications

Our findings support adding two binary descriptors — presence of peritumoral edema and presence of heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement — to standard preoperative MR reports in soft-tissue sarcoma. Both can be recorded rapidly on conventional sequences, require no specialized acquisitions, and demonstrated substantial inter-reader agreement. Operationally, peritumoral edema supports intensified local surveillance (closer-interval MRI; lower threshold for biopsy of suspicious peritumoral changes), and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement supports closer metastatic screening. Combining both descriptors stratifies patients into four pragmatic risk groups that could inform individualized surveillance intensity, particularly in translocation-driven tumors where FNCLCC grade offers limited prognostic resolution. Prospective multicenter validation is required before these descriptors inform treatment-escalation decisions.

Limitations

This study has limitations. It was retrospective, single-center, and modest in size. Our institution is a tertiary sarcoma referral center; patients reached the service predominantly through external surgical referral rather than a population-based catchment, enriching the cohort for young adults (mean age, 48 years), high-grade disease (79% grade 3), and translocation-driven histology (40%) relative to population-based series, in which FNCLCC grade 3 typically accounts for 30–50% and translocation-driven tumors for roughly 20%. This referral-driven case-mix both defines the strength of our argument and limits direct generalizability to unselected cohorts; findings should be confirmed in population-based or multicenter series. Because the study was designed and powered for the full enriched cohort, it was not powered for a translocation-restricted analysis; subgroup-specific effect estimates within the translocation-driven tumors await a larger multicenter cohort. MR acquisitions spanned two field strengths and a 16-year period; although this reflects real-world practice, unmeasured protocol differences may have introduced noise. Restriction to contrast-enhanced examinations excluded 22 patients and may have further enriched for larger tumors. Confidence intervals for several effect estimates were wide; prospective multicenter validation is needed. Finally, we did not include quantitative DCE or diffusion-weighted parameters; integrating those with the two visual descriptors may further improve risk stratification.

Conclusion

In 158 adults with contrast-enhanced MRI of newly diagnosed, translocation-driven enriched soft-tissue sarcoma, peritumoral edema and heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement were independent, complementary MR predictors of outcome. Peritumoral edema indexed local biologic aggressiveness; heterogeneous gadolinium enhancement indexed systemic risk. Both were independently prognostic where FNCLCC grade and translocation-driven histology were not in this cohort, and neither acted as a grade surrogate. Incorporating these two binary descriptors into standard MR reports could inform risk-stratified surveillance in this subgroup.
