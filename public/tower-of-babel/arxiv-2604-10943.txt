On sections of Lefschetz fibrations and bundles over 2-complexes
Jonathan A. Hillman, Riccardo Pedrotti

Abstract
. We address the question of existence of sections of fibrations in two settings. First, we show that a bundle with base a finite 2-complex admits a section if and only if the inclusion of the fiber is π 1 \pi_{1} -injective and the associated short exact sequence of fundamental groups splits. Second, for Lefschetz fibrations over the disk we provide a complete algebraic criterion characterizing which loops in the boundary mapping torus extend to continuous or smooth sections over the disk. Finally, we apply our results to achiral Lefschetz fibrations over the sphere obtained by doubling along the vertical boundary, and give a criterion ensuring the existence of at least two homologically distinct sections.

Introduction

Given
p:E\to B
a fibration, a section is a map
s:B\to E
such that

p\circ s=\Id_{B}:B\to B.

For bundles over
2
-complexes, the existence of a section is essentially governed by homotopy theory as a consequence of the Homotopy Lifting Property (HLP). This is made precise in our first result.

Theorem
2.1
.

Let
p:E\to{B}
be a fibre bundle with base
B
a finite
2
-complex, and let
\ast_{B}
and
\ast_{E}
be base points such that
p(\ast_{E})=\ast_{B}
. Then there is a map
s:B\to{E}
such that
ps=id_{B}
if and only if the inclusion of the fibre
F=p^{-1}(\ast_{B})
into
E
induces a monomorphism from
\pi_{1}F
to
\pi_{1}E
and there is a homomorphism
\mathfrak{s}:\pi_{1}B\to\pi_{1}E
such that
p_{*}\mathfrak{s}=id_{B}
. If so, we may assume that
s_{*}=\mathfrak{s}
.

For Lefschetz fibrations, the question of the existence of a section is more geometric in nature, since homotopy theory alone does not fully capture the topological structure of the spaces involved. At the time of writing, it is still unknown whether any (closed) Lefschetz fibration over the sphere admits a section (
[
1
, Problem 2.22]
). A potential approach to solve this problem is the following:

(1)

We divide the base sphere into two disks,

, one containing all the critical values of
\pi
(the
critical disk
) and the other not (the

(2)

After choosing a trivialization

there is no obstruction to finding a section over

as a preimage

(3)

We determine whether the loop

can be extended as a section over

.

In the second part of our paper, we completely solve step

Our algebraic criterion reads as follows. Let
\pi:E\to D
be a Lefschetz fibration over the disk with
k

, regular fiber a smooth closed orientable surface

Theorem

.

Let
\pi:E\to D
be a Lefschetz fibration as above. Let

be the mapping torus of

There exists a continuous section

choose a compatible factorization of the monodromy

\tau=\tau_{V_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}}.

Then, as a based loop in
\Sigma

\alpha\sim\alpha_{1}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{2}\star\cdots\star(\tau_{V_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}})^{-1}\alpha_{k},

where each

\alpha_{i}\sim\zeta_{i}\star\widetilde{V_{i}}^{m_{i}}\star\tau_{V_{i}}^{-1}(\overline{\zeta_{i}}).

is smoothable if and only if
\forall i\ m_{i}\in\{0,1\}

We remark that a compatible positive factorization of the monodromy, arising for example after choosing an ordered set of vanishing paths, is an auxiliary choice in our theorem. If the loop

can be successfully decomposed with respect to one positive factorization, then it can be decomposed for any other factorization obtained via Hurwitz moves. Therefore, the property of being

In
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, the author constructed an example of an achiral Lefschetz fibration whose fiber

has arbitrarily high genus, as a double of a Lefschetz fibration

In this paper we start with a Lefschetz fibration over the disk

Theorem

If there exists an index

Let

3.1.1.
Lefschetz fibrations over the disk with a single critical value

Our construction of smooth and continuous sections for a Lefschetz fibration over the disk requires an explicit model for a Lefschetz fibration over the disk. The following construction taken from
[
8
]
will be used. We included some details we believe might be of independent interest to the reader.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
V
be an essential curve in
\Sigma
. Fix some radius
r_{0}>0
. There is a symplectic Lefschetz fibration
(E^{V},\pi^{V})
over
D^{V}(r_{0})
(the disk of radius
r_{0}
) together with an symplectomorphism between the regular fiber over
r_{0}\in\partial D^{V}(r_{0})
with its induced symplectic form and a surface
\Sigma
with appropriate volume form

such that if
\rho^{V}
is the symplectic monodromy around
\partial D^{V}(r_{0})
, then
{\tau_{V}=\phi^{V}\rho^{V}(\phi^{V})^{-1}}
is a Dehn twist along

. The Lefschetz fibration
(E^{V},\pi^{V})
has a single critical point
x_{0}
over
0\in D^{V}(r_{0})
.

Idea of proof.

For all the details, see
[
8
, Prop 1.11]
. The construction consists in gluing an appropriate local model
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
around the critical point
x_{0}
together with the quadratic complex projection map

, to a chosen neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(V)
of the vanishing cycle
V
in the reference fiber
\Sigma
. Using Darboux coordinates, we can identify
\mathcal{N}(V)
with
T^{*}_{\leq\lambda}(S^{1})
, the subbundle of the cotangent bundle whose vectors have norm at most
\lambda>0
.

The model
M
is defined via a diffeomorphism
\Phi
fibered over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
8
, Eq. 1.13]
):

M:=\Phi^{-1}\big(D\times(T^{*}_{\leq\lambda}(S^{1})\setminus S^{1})\big)\cup\Delta.

The diffeomorphism is given explicitly as

(4)

\begin{split}\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\Delta&\to\mathbb{C}\times(T^{*}(S^{1})\setminus S^{1})\\
\textbf{x}&\mapsto(q(\textbf{x}),\cos(\alpha/2)u-\sin(\alpha/2)\|u\|v+\sin(\alpha/2)\frac{u}{\|u\|})\end{split}

where
se^{i\alpha}=q(\textbf{x})
,

and
v:=\frac{\Re(e^{-i\alpha/2}\textbf{x})}{\|\Re(e^{-i\alpha/2}\textbf{x})\|}

Finally

E^{V}:=M\ \bigcup_{{\sim}}\ D^{V}(r_{0})\times(\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{N}(V))

∎

The following is a simple but useful observation

Lemma 3.10
.

If
\ast_{\Sigma}\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)
, then for any class

there is a smooth section

realizing the class

([\alpha],1)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{f},\ast_{\Sigma})\rtimes_{f_{*}}\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Any section of the mapping torus of

\Omega_{f}(\Sigma):=\{\rho:[0,1]\to\Sigma\mid\rho(1)=f\rho(0)\}.

The lemma is then an immediate consequence of the equivalence

to denote that

and

Lemma 3.11
.

Given two based sections

of the mapping torus

representing in

([\beta],1)
and

respectively, then TFAE:

(a)

(b)

[\alpha]=[\zeta]\cdot[\beta]\cdot f^{-1}_{*}([\overline{\zeta}])
in
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})
.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma(\Sigma_{f})=\Omega_{f}(\Sigma)
. An homotopy through sections corresponds to a path in
\Omega_{f}(\Sigma)
. Two elements are in the same connected component of
\Omega_{f}(\Sigma)
if and only if exists
[\zeta]\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})
such that

([\alpha],1)=([\zeta],0)\cdot([\beta],1)\cdot([\overline{\zeta}],0).

This is a consequence of the long exact sequence of homotopy groups for the fibration
\Omega(\Sigma)\to\Omega_{f}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma
. Spelling out the operation group operation completely we get

concluding the proof.
∎

Let

3.2.2.
Sections of concantenated mapping torii

In order to characterize the loops that can arise as boundary of sections in
\Gamma\big({\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bigvee_{\ast_{D}}D_{i}}}\big)
or
\Gamma_{0}\big({\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bigvee_{\ast_{D}}D_{i}}}\big)
, we need a suitable description of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau},\ast_{\Sigma})
.

Let
F_{k}
be the free group generated by the set
\{[\gamma_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{k}
. Let

\displaystyle\rho:F_{k}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Aut}\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})\big)

\displaystyle\rho([\gamma_{i}])([\alpha])

\displaystyle={\tau_{V_{i}}}_{*}^{-1}([\alpha]),

using this representation we can define the semidirect product
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})\rtimes_{\rho}F_{k}
. We will use the symbol “
\bullet
” to denote its group operation. An inductive application of Seifert-Van Kampen theorem reveals that

\pi_{1}({\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bigvee_{\ast_{D}}\gamma_{i}}},\ast_{E})\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})\rtimes_{\rho}F_{k}.

Choose a geometric basis
\vec{\gamma}:=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})
of
D\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}
. We define the map

\partial D\hookrightarrow D\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}\simeq\bigvee_{\ast_{D}}^{k}\gamma_{i}.

On total spaces this gives us the inclusion

\Sigma_{\tau}\cong{\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial D}}\hookrightarrow{\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\bigvee_{\ast_{D}}\gamma_{i}}}

which induces the following map at the level of fundamental groups:

(9)

\begin{split}\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\rtimes_{\tau_{*}}\mathbb{Z}&\to\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})\rtimes_{\rho}F_{k}\\
([\alpha],0)&\mapsto([\alpha],0)\\
(0,1)&\mapsto(0,[\gamma_{1}])\bullet\cdots\bullet(0,[\gamma_{k}]).\end{split}

The following lemma describes how to
concatenate
homotopy classes of sections of
\Sigma_{\tau_{V_{i}}}
to obtain a well-defined homotopy class of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau},\ast_{\Sigma})
.

Lemma 3.18
.

The map
\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}
from (
9
) is well defined and injective. Given a sequence of sections
s_{i}:S^{1}\to\Sigma_{\tau_{V_{i}}}
, representing homotopy classes

([\alpha_{i}],1)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau_{V_{i}}},\ast_{\Sigma}),

there is a unique class
([\alpha],1)\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau},\ast_{\Sigma})

which represents the homotopy class of the concatenation of the
s_{i}
as a loop in
\Sigma_{\tau}
and whose representative
\alpha
must satisfy

\alpha\sim\alpha_{1}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{2}\star\cdots\star(\tau_{V_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}})^{-1}\alpha_{k}\text{ in }\Sigma.

Proof.

To prove well-definedness, the only relation to check is

(0,1)([\alpha],0)(0,-1)=\big(\tau^{-1}_{*}([\alpha]),0\big).

The map
\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}
maps the LHS of the previous equation to

\big(0,[\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{k}]\big)\bullet\big([\alpha],0\big)\bullet\big(0,([\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{k}])^{-1}\big)=\big({\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}}_{*}\cdots\ {\tau_{V_{k}}^{-1}}_{*}([\alpha]),0\big)=\big(\tau_{*}^{-1}([\alpha]),0\big).

To show injectivity, every element of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau},\ast_{\Sigma})
can be written as
([\alpha],\,n)
with
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Assume

\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}([\alpha],n)=([\ast_{\Sigma}],e),

where
e\in F_{k}
is the trivial letter. By definition
[\alpha]=[\ast_{\Sigma}]\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma,\ast_{\Sigma})
and
([\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{k}])^{n}=e\in F_{k}
. Since the word
[\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{k}]
is clearly reduced in
F_{k}
, it has to have infinite order in it. Therefore
n=0
, proving injectiviness.

Let
\alpha:=\alpha_{1}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{2}\star\cdots\star(\tau_{V_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}})^{-1}\alpha_{k}
, given the sequence of sections
s_{i}
, by applying
\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}
we have:

\displaystyle\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}\big([s_{1}\star\cdots\star s_{k}]\big)

\displaystyle=\big([\alpha_{1}],[\gamma_{1}]\big)\bullet\big([\alpha_{2}],[\gamma_{2}]\big)\bullet\cdots\bullet\big([\alpha_{k}],[\gamma_{k}]\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\alpha_{1}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}\alpha_{2}\star\cdots\star(\tau_{V_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}})^{-1}\alpha_{k},[\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{k}]\big)

\displaystyle=\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}([\alpha],1)

By injectiviness of
\iota_{\vec{\gamma}}
we conclude.
∎

3.2.3.
Sections of Lefschetz fibrations over the disk with several critical values

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
\pi:E\to D
be a Lefschetz fibration as above. Let
\Sigma_{\tau}
be the mapping torus of
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
identified with
{\left.\kern-1.2ptE\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\partial D}}

be a section representative for it.

There is a continuous section

of

if and only if the following condition is met:

\tau

can be decomposed as

\forall i\ m_{i}\in\{0,1\}

for

(\Leftarrow)

3.17

The conditions required by Theorem

If

or

3.14
and Lemma
3.17
.

(\Rightarrow)

Let

3.17
,

\Sigma

By Theorem

, direct substitution shows

\displaystyle([\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}]^{m_{1}},[{\gamma_{1}}^{\prime}])\bullet([\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime}]^{m_{2}},[{\gamma_{2}}^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=([\widetilde{V}_{2}]^{m_{1}},[{\gamma_{2}}])\bullet([\tau_{V_{2}}(V_{1})]^{m_{2}},[{\gamma_{2}}^{\prime}])

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{V}_{2}^{m_{1}}\star\widetilde{V}_{1}^{m_{2}}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}(\widetilde{V}_{2}^{-m_{1}})

3.3.
Homologically distinct sections of some achiral Lefschetz fibrations over the sphere

We give here a criterion for the existence of homologically distinct sections of the
achiral
Lefschetz fibration
D(\pi):D(E)\to S^{2}
arising as the double of a given Lefschetz fibration over the disk
\pi:E\to D
with closed fibers and monodromy
\tau
. Recall that

D(E)=E\bigsqcup_{\partial E}\overline{E},

where the gluing takes place along the boundary using the identity map. The second copy inherits the opposite orientation hence the result is an achiral Lefschetz fibration.

Choose a factorization
\tau=\tau_{V_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}}
, let
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. We define the based loop
V^{\textbf{m}}
in
\Sigma
as follows:

V^{\textbf{m}}:=\widetilde{V}^{m_{1}}_{1}\star\tau_{V_{1}}^{-1}\widetilde{V}^{m_{2}}_{2}\star\cdots\star(\tau_{V_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{V_{1}})^{-1}\widetilde{V}^{m_{k}}_{k}.

It is immediate that
([V^{\mathbf{m}}],1)
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
3.19
and hence there exists a section
\sigma_{\textbf{m}}\in\Gamma(E)
whose restriction is a representative for
([V^{\mathbf{m}}],1)
.

In the following,
[\cdot]
will denote an equivalence class in homology, rather than in homotopy. Since there will be no further mentions to homotopy classes this should not cause any confusion.

Theorem 3.21
.

For any section
\sigma_{\textbf{m}}\in\Gamma_{0}(E)
constructed as above, its double
D(\sigma_{\textbf{m}})
is a section of
D(E)
. If there exists an index
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and an integer
m
such that

m\cdot[V_{j}]\notin\mathrm{Im}(\Id_{\ast}-\tau_{*})\subset H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}),

then
D(E)
admits at least two homologically distinct sections.

Proof.

The fact that a section
\sigma
of
E
gives rise to a section
D(\sigma)
of
D(E)
is immediate.

Consider the boundary map arising from the M-V sequence applied to the decomposition
D(E)=E\bigsqcup_{\partial E}\overline{E}

\delta:H_{2}(D(E);\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau};\mathbb{Z})\cong{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\text{Im}(\Id_{*}-\tau_{*})$}\right.};

two sections
s_{1},s_{2}
of
D(E)
are homologically distinct if

\delta([s_{1}])\neq\delta([s_{2}]).

The first section we consider is
D(\sigma_{\textbf{0}})
, i.e. the section associated with all
m_{i}=0
. Up to Hurwitz moves, we can assume that
j=1
in the assumption of the Theorem; there is an integer
m
such that

m\cdot[V_{1}]\notin\mathrm{Im}(\Id_{\ast}-\tau_{*}).

Consider the section
D(\sigma_{m\cdot{e_{1}}})
. The boundary map
\delta
sends
D(\sigma_{\textbf{0}})
to
0
, while it is easy to see via the Picard Lefschetz formula that
\delta\big(D(\sigma_{m\cdot{e_{1}}})\big)=m\cdot[V_{1}]\neq 0
as an element of
H_{1}(\Sigma_{\tau};\mathbb{Z})
, hence the two sections are homologically distinct.
∎

In the case of
\tau\sim\mathrm{Id}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
, then
0=\text{Im}(\Id_{*}-\tau_{*})
and by results of I. Smith (
[
9
]
) there must be at least one homologically essential vanishing cycle among the

’s. Hence we have the following corollary:

Corollary 3.22
.

Let

as above. If
\tau\sim\id
in

, then

admits countably many homologically distinct sections.
