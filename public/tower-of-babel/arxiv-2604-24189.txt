Malliavin calculus and densities for chaos-driven stochastic differential equations
Laurent Loosveldt, Yassine Nachit, Ivan Nourdin

Abstract
We study stochastic differential equations driven by finite-order chaos processes on abstract Wiener spaces, with pathwise Riemann–Stieltjes integration. The driving noise is an ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} -valued chaotic process given by multiple Wiener-Itô integrals of fixed order, allowing for non-Gaussian dynamics. Under mild smoothness assumptions on the coefficients and Hölder-type regularity of the noise, we establish existence and uniqueness of solutions. We then prove Malliavin differentiability and absolute continuity of the law of the solution. Since the usual Gaussian isonormal framework is unavailable, we rely on the Kusuoka-Stroock approach to Malliavin calculus and develop a Taylor expansion for multiple integrals under Cameron-Martin shifts. Under suitable ellipticity, independence, and non-degeneracy conditions, the Bouleau–Hirsch criterion yields density results. Applications to multidimensional Hermite-driven equations are provided.



Keywords
: Malliavin calculus, Stochastic differential equations, Wiener chaos, Density of laws, Non-Gaussian noise, Hermite process.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the stochastic differential equation

X^{k}_{t}=x_{0}^{k}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{k}(X_{s})ds+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k,\ell}(X_{s})dF_{s}^{\ell},

(1)

studied in the following general framework:

•

x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the initial value of the process

;

•

the stochastic integral in (
1
) is understood pathwise, in the Riemann–Stieltjes sense (see e.g.
[
31

);

•

the driving process
F
is an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued chaos process defined on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
(see definition (

•

(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}

(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,1\leq\ell\leq m}
are (smooth) deterministic functions, as made precise by hypothesis

The question of the existence of a density for the solution to (

is a multidimensional Brownian motion was, in fact, Malliavin’s original motivation in

]

\mathbb{E}[B_{s}^{H,\ell}B_{t}^{H,\ell^{\prime}}]=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}(|s|^{2H}+|t|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}),

(2)

see e.g.
[

7
,

,
10
,

,

,

. Among these contributions,

[
24
]
subsequently turned out to be key ingredients in several further developments: smoothness of the density under a Hörmander-type hypoellipticity condition
[
2
]
, upper bounds for the density
[
3
]
, asymptotic expansions for expectations of smooth functionals of the solution
[

, rates of convergence and asymptotic error distribution of Euler approximation schemes
[

]
, and statistical inference
[

. The strategy introduced in
[

]

[

[

The purpose of the present paper is to push this theory further, in the general context of chaotic processes on abstract Wiener spaces. More precisely, we show that, under mild conditions on the coefficients
(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
and
(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,1\leq\ell\leq m}
(hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{1})}
in Subsection

(hypothesis

Let us stress at once a central difficulty in this last task. In order to establish the absolute continuity of the law of the solution
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
, we rely on the Bouleau–Hirsch criterion (Theorem
5.1

There, however, the analysis is considerably simplified by the fact that Malliavin calculus can be set up via an isonormal process canonically associated with the underlying Gaussian process, which greatly facilitates the computation of the Malliavin derivatives of the quantities involved. This favourable picture breaks down as soon as the driving process
F
is no longer Gaussian. Consequently, the classical methods developed in the Gaussian setting are not directly available to us, and new strategies have to be devised. Our main tool is an alternative definition of Malliavin calculus, which is presented in Subsection

The paper is organised as follows. Section

2
Background and strategies

2.1
Abstract Wiener spaces

An abstract Wiener space is a quadruple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
where

(a)

\Omega
is a separable Banach space;

(b)

\mathbb{P}
is a Gaussian measure on
\Omega
with full support;

(c)

\mathcal{F}
is the completion with respect to
\mathbb{P}
of the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
;

(d)

(\mathfrak{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}})
is a separable Hilbert space that is continuously and densely embedded in
\Omega
via an injection
j\,:\,\mathfrak{H}\to\Omega
.

The image
j(\mathfrak{H})
is usually called the Cameron–Martin space. By the Riesz representation theorem, one identifies
\mathfrak{H}
with its dual and considers the adjoint operator
j^{*}\,:\,\Omega^{*}\to\mathfrak{H}
of
j
, which is characterised by

\langle j^{*}(y),h\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=y(j(h))

(3)

for
y\in\Omega^{*}
and
h\in\mathfrak{H}
. We further assume that, for every
y\in\Omega^{*}
,

\int_{\Omega}e^{iy(\omega)}d\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\|j^{*}(y)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\right).

(4)

The subspace
j^{*}(\Omega^{*})
is dense in
\mathfrak{H}
, and this allows one to define, for every
g\in\mathfrak{H}
, a centered Gaussian random variable
X_{g}
. Whenever
g=j^{*}(y)
for some
y\in\Omega^{*}
, it suffices, in view of (
4
), to set
X_{g}=y
. The same identity shows that the map
g\mapsto X_{g}
from
j^{*}(\Omega^{*})
to
L^{2}(\Omega)
is continuous (with respect to the norm of
\mathfrak{H}
), and therefore extends continuously to the whole space
\mathfrak{H}
. The resulting Gaussian process
\{X_{g}\,:\,g\in\mathfrak{H}\}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is
isonormal
, in the sense that

\mathbb{E}[X_{g}X_{g^{\prime}}]=\langle g,g^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\qquad\text{for all }g,g^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}.

Such a process is the natural framework in which to set up Malliavin calculus. Below, we collect the main ingredients needed in the present paper and refer to
[
20
,
22
]
for details and proofs.

Malliavin derivatives and Sobolev-type spaces.

Throughout this paper, we adopt the usual notation
\otimes
for the tensor product of elements of
\mathfrak{H}
, and
\odot
for the symmetric tensor product, that is, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathfrak{H}
,

h_{1}\odot\dots\odot h_{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}h_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{\sigma(n)},

with
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the set of all permutations of
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

For each
q\geq 1
, we write
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}
for the
q
th tensor product of
\mathfrak{H}
,
\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
for its
q
th
symmetric
tensor product, and
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q})
for the space of
\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
-valued random elements
F
that are
\mathcal{F}
-measurable and satisfy
\mathbb{E}[\|F\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}}^{2}]<\infty
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the class of cylindrical random variables of the form

F=f(X_{h_{1}},\ldots,X_{h_{n}}),

(5)

where
n\geq 1
,
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathfrak{H}
, and
f
is infinitely differentiable with all partial derivatives of polynomial growth. For such an
F
, the
q
th Malliavin derivative is the element of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q})
given by

D^{q}F=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{q}=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{q}f}{\partial\ell_{1}\ldots\partial\ell_{k}}(X_{h_{1}},\ldots,X_{h_{n}})h_{\ell_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{\ell_{q}}.

(6)

The operator
D^{q}
is closable (see e.g.
[
20
, Proposition 2.3.4]
); for every
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
p\geq 1
, the Sobolev-type space
\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
is then defined as the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\|\cdot\|_{m,p}\,:\,F\mapsto\left(\mathbb{E}[|F|^{p}]+\sum_{q=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}[\|D^{q}F\|^{p}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(7)

Finally, we set
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}=\bigcap_{p\geq 1}\bigcap_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
.

Divergence operator and multiple integrals.

The divergence operator of order
q
, denoted by
\delta^{q}
, is defined as the adjoint of
D^{q}
. It is an unbounded operator from
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q})
into
L^{2}(\Omega)
whose domain
\Dom(\delta^{q})
consists of those
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q})
for which there exists a constant
c>0
such that
|\mathbb{E}[\left<D^{q}F,u\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}]|\leq c\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for every
F\in\mathbb{D}^{q,2}
. For
u\in\Dom(\delta^{q})
,
\delta^{q}(u)
is the unique element of
L^{2}(\Omega)
characterised by the duality relation

\mathbb{E}[F\delta^{q}(u)]=\mathbb{E}[\left<D^{q}F,u\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}],\quad\text{for all}\;F\in\mathbb{D}^{k,2}.

(8)

A direct consequence of this definition is the “integration by parts” formula
[
20
, Proposition 2.5.4]
: if
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
u\in\Dom(\delta)
are such that
\mathbb{E}[F^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{H}}]
,
\mathbb{E}[F^{2}\delta(u)^{2}]
and
\mathbb{E}[\langle Df,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}]
are all finite, then
Fu\in\Dom(\delta)
and

\delta(Fu)=F\delta(u)-\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}.

(9)

The divergence operator is the key ingredient in the construction of multiple stochastic integrals, which play a central role in our work.

Let
q\geq 1
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
. The multiple Wiener-Itô integral of order
q
of
f
is the centered random variable

I_{q}(f):=\delta^{q}(f).

(10)

Among the many properties of multiple Wiener-Itô integrals, the following ones will be used repeatedly.

•

Hypercontractivity.
For every
q\geq 1
,
p\in[2,\infty)
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|I_{q}(f)\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq(p-1)^{q/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|I_{q}(f)\right|^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

(11)

•

Isometry formula
(when
p=q
) and
orthogonality
(when
p\neq q
): for
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot p}
,
g\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
and
p,q\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)\right]=\begin{cases}p!\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes p}}&\text{ if }p=q,\\
0&\text{ if }p\neq q.\end{cases}

(12)

•

Product formula for multiple Wiener–Itô integrals:
for any
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot p}
and
g\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
,

I_{p}(f)\,I_{q}(g)=\sum_{r=0}^{p\wedge q}r!\binom{p}{r}\binom{q}{r}\,I_{p+q-2r}\big(f\widetilde{\otimes}_{r}g\big),

(13)

where
f\widetilde{\otimes}_{r}g
denotes the symmetrised
r
th contraction of
f
and
g
.

Hilbert-space-valued random variables.

We shall also need random variables taking values in a separable Hilbert space, and we thus extend the above definitions accordingly. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a separable Hilbert space. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{U}}
the class of
\mathcal{U}
-valued smooth random variables of the form
F=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}\,u_{j}
, with
F_{j}\in\mathcal{S}
and
u_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
. For any such
F
and any
q\geq 1
, the
q
th Malliavin derivative of
F
is the
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}\otimes\mathcal{U}
-valued random element

D^{q}F=\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{q}F_{j}\otimes u_{j}.

As before, one shows that, for every
p,q\geq 1
,
D^{q}
is closable from
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{U}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{U})
into
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}\otimes\mathcal{U})
. This allows us to introduce the analogous Sobolev-type spaces
\mathbb{D}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})
and
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
, together with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{m,p,,\mathcal{U}}\,:\,u\mapsto\left(\mathbb{E}[\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{p}]+\sum_{q=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}[\|D^{q}u\|^{p}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{U}}]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(14)

Similarly, for
q\geq 1
, if
u\in\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}
has the form
u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}\times h_{j}
with
u_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
and
h_{j}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}
, we set

\delta^{q}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}\delta^{q}(h_{j}),

and
\delta^{q}
extends to a bounded operator from
\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}
to
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{U})

[
20
, Section 2.6]
. In particular, for any
p>q
, the identification
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes p}=\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes{(p-q)}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}
gives meaning to
\delta^{q}(f)
for
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes p}
. Extending definition (
10
) in this way, we obtain two further properties of multiple Wiener-Itô integrals that will be used in the sequel:

•

Malliavin derivative of a multiple Wiener-Itô integral.
For every
p,q\geq 1
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
, one has
I_{q}(f)\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty,p}
, and, for every
r\geq 1
,

D^{r}I_{q}(f)=\begin{cases}\frac{q!}{(q-r)!}I_{q-r}(f)&\text{ if }r\leq q,\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(15)

where
I_{q-r}(f)
is computed with respect to the first
q-r
variables.

•

Reintegration formula:
for every
q\geq 1
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
,

I_{q}(f)=\delta(I_{q-1}(f)).

(16)

2.2
Existence of the solution and statement of the main conditions

From now on, we fix an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
and assume that the driving process
F
of (
1
) takes the following form.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued stochastic process
\{F_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called a
chaotic process
(over the abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
) if there exists
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
t\geq 0
and every
1\leq\ell\leq m
, one can find
f_{t}^{\ell}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
satisfying

F_{t}=(I_{q}(f_{t}^{1}),\dots,I_{q}(f_{t}^{m})).

(17)

The aim of the present subsection is to discuss the existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
1
). For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\mathcal{C}^{p}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the set of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are
p
-times continuously differentiable with bounded partial derivatives up to order
p
. We now impose the following assumption on the coefficients
(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
and
(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,1\leq\ell\leq m}
of (
1
).

\mathbf{(H_{1})}
For all
1\leq k\leq d
, and
1\leq\ell\leq m
,
b_{k},\sigma_{k,\ell}\in\mathcal{C}^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In
[
23
]
, the authors consider the deterministic differential equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
driven by an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued function
\varphi
,

x_{t}^{k}=x_{0}^{k}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{k}\left(x_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k,\ell}\left(x_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi_{s}^{\ell},

(18)

with
1\leq k\leq d
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and they establish its existence and uniqueness in terms of the function spaces recalled below.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T>0
. For
\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we introduce the following spaces of measurable functions:

(a)

the space
W_{1}^{\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
of functions
f:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\|f\|_{\alpha,1}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(|f(t)|+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha+1}}\mathrm{\penalty\ d}s\right)<\infty;

(b)

the space
W_{2}^{1-\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
of functions
\varphi:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying

\|\varphi\|_{1-\alpha,2}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\left(\frac{|\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)|}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}+\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|\varphi(\tau)-\varphi(s)|}{(\tau-s)^{2-\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)<\infty;

(c)

the
\theta
-Hölder space
C^{\theta}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
of functions
f:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\|f\|_{\theta}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f(t)|+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{(t-s)^{\theta}}<\infty.

Remark 2.3
.

For any
T>0
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\gamma>0
such that
1-\alpha+\gamma\leq 1
, one has the continuous embeddings

C^{\alpha+\gamma}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset W_{1}^{\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

(19)

and

C^{1-\alpha+\gamma}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\subset W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\subset C^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right).

(20)

Our starting point is
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
, which asserts that, for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and under assumptions on the coefficients weaker than
\mathbf{(H_{1})}
, if
\varphi\in W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
, then the deterministic differential equation (
18
) admits a unique solution
x\in W_{1}^{\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap C^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. To apply this result to the stochastic differential equation (
1
), we need to ensure that (a version of) the driving process
F
has trajectories in some space
W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
. This leads us to impose the following assumption on the chaotic process
F
given by (
17
).

\mathbf{(H_{2})}
There exists
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
such that, for any
1\leq\ell\leq m
, there exists a deterministic constant
c_{\ell}>0
for which, for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

\|f^{\ell}_{t}-f^{\ell}_{s}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}\leq c_{\ell}\,|t-s|^{H}.

(21)

Whenever
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
holds, the isometry formula (
12
) together with the hypercontractivity property (
11
) yield, for every
1\leq\ell\leq m
,
0\leq j\leq q
,
p\geq 2
and
s,t\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|D^{j}(F_{t}^{\ell}-F_{s}^{\ell})\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes j}}^{p}\right]\leq c_{\ell,j,p}\,|t-s|^{pH}.

(22)

From (
22
) and Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, we deduce that, for every
0\leq j\leq q
, the process
\{D^{j}F_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
admits a version whose trajectories are
\theta
-Hölder continuous as maps from
[0,T]
to
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes j}
for every
\theta\in(0,H)
. In the sequel, whenever
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
is in force, we shall always work with these versions, still denoted
\{D^{j}F_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. In particular, for any
\beta\in(0,H-\frac{1}{2})
and
\gamma\in(0,\beta)
, we have
H-\beta+\gamma\leq 1
, and the trajectories of
\{F_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
therefore belong to
C^{H-\beta+\gamma}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\subseteq W_{2}^{H-\beta}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
, so that
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
can be applied with
1-\alpha=H-\beta
.

We close this subsection by recalling
[
24
, Proposition 4]
, which plays a key role throughout our analysis.

Proposition 2.4
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
T>0
, and let
x(\varphi)
denote the solution to (
18
) driven by
\varphi\in W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
. Under hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{1})}
, the mapping

\Psi\,:\,W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\to W_{1}^{\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\,:\,\varphi\mapsto x(\varphi)

(23)

is Fréchet differentiable and for any
\varphi,\psi\in W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
the Fréchet derivative of
\Psi
at
\varphi
in the direction

2.3
Roadmap through an alternative definition of Malliavin calculus

In Subsection
2.1
, we sketched Shigekawa’s definition (originally introduced by Malliavin
[
17
]
and Shigekawa
[
27
]
) of the Sobolev-type spaces
\mathbb{D}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})
, where
\mathcal{U}
is a separable Hilbert space. While this is the most commonly used definition in the literature, these spaces admit several equivalent characterisations. The one we shall exploit here is due to Stroock
[
28
]
and Kusuoka
[
13
]
(see also Sugita
[
29
]
), who introduced the Sobolev spaces
\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})
(see the definition below). Their definition is based on the following two properties.

Denoting by
\mathcal{L}_{HS}(j(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{U})
the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators from
j(\mathfrak{H})
to
\mathcal{U}
, these two properties read as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

A
\mathcal{U}
-valued random variable
F:\Omega\to\mathcal{U}
is said to be

(a)

ray absolutely continuous

(RAC)
if, for every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, there exists a
\mathcal{U}
-valued random variable
\hat{F}_{h}:\Omega\to\mathcal{U}
such that
F=\hat{F}_{h}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, the map
\mathbb{R}\ni\varepsilon\mapsto\hat{F}_{h}(\omega+\varepsilon\,j(h))
is absolutely continuous;

(b)

stochastically Gâteaux differentiable

(SGD)
if there exists a random variable
G:\Omega\to\mathcal{L}_{HS}(j(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{U})
such that, for every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(F(\bullet+\varepsilon\,j(h))-F)\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\longrightarrow}G(\bullet)[j(h)]\qquad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(28)

The operator
G
is unique
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., and is denoted by
\hat{D}F
. Higher-order derivatives are defined inductively: if
\hat{D}^{n-1}F
is
(SGD)
, then
\hat{D}^{n}F:=\hat{D}(\hat{D}^{n-1}F)
.

The Sobolev-type spaces
\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})
, for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
1<p<\infty
, are then defined inductively. First, for
m=1
,

\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{1,p}(\mathcal{U})=\{F\in L^{p}(\mathcal{U})\,;\;F\text{ is RAC and SGD, }\hat{D}F\in L^{p}\left(\mathcal{L}_{HS}(j(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{U})\right)\},

(29)

and, for
m>1
, one then sets analogously

\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})=\{F\in\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m-1,p}(\mathcal{U})\,;\;\hat{D}F\in\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m-1,p}\left(\mathcal{L}_{HS}(j(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{U}))\right)\}.

(30)

The following theorem shows that Kusuoka-Stroock approach and Shigekawa’s definition lead to the same spaces.

Theorem 2.7

(Theorem 3.1 in
[
29
]
)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
1<p<\infty
, we have
\hat{\mathbb{D}}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{D}^{m,p}(\mathcal{U})
, and for any
F
in this space, we have, for a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, and for any
h\in\mathfrak{H}
,

\hat{D}^{k}F(\omega)[j(h),\ldots,j(h)]=\left<D^{k}F(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}},\qquad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq m.

(31)

In the present work, we shall rely on the Kusuoka-Stroock approach to establish the Malliavin differentiability of the solution
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to the stochastic differential equation (

Strategy for
(SGD)
.

For any

,

\frac{X_{t}^{k}(\bullet+\varepsilon j(h))-X_{t}^{k}(\bullet)}{\varepsilon}.

Unfortunately, this strategy is hopeless in our general setting. A good illustration is given by the standard case of the canonical Brownian motion on

\displaystyle:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\;\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N},\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)=\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{I}_{q}(f)(\omega)\text{ and }\right.

(42)

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 142.26378pt\lim_{n\to\infty}D^{\ell}F_{n}(\omega)=\widetilde{D}^{\ell}F(\omega)\right\},

\Omega_{F}^{\prime}=\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\cap\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\Omega_{F_{n}}^{\prime},\quad\Omega_{F}=\Omega_{1}\cap\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\Omega_{F_{n}}

and, for
0\leq\ell\leq q
the random variables

\begin{cases}\widetilde{F}_{\ell}(\omega)&:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega),\quad\text{ for all }\omega\in\Omega_{F},\\
\widetilde{F}_{\ell}(\omega)&:=0,\quad\text{ for all }\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{F}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega_{F}
and
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{F}^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{F})=1
. We now show that one can take
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F}_{0}
. Since
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})=1
, we obtain
\widetilde{F}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{D}}^{\infty}
, with
\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F}_{\ell}
for
0\leq\ell\leq q
and
\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}=0
for
\ell>q
. Moreover, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
and every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{I}_{q}(f_{n})(\omega)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}D^{\ell}F_{n}(\omega)=D^{\ell}F(\omega).

To conclude, we note that the previous case of elements of the form (
41
) gives, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
,
h\in\mathfrak{H}
,
n\geq 1
and
0\leq\ell\leq q
,

\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{I}_{q}(f_{n})(\omega+j(h))=\sum_{k=0}^{q-\ell}\frac{1}{k!}\langle\widetilde{D}^{k+\ell}\widetilde{I}_{q}(f_{n})(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}.

It follows that
\omega+j(h)\in\Omega_{F}
, and hence
\Omega_{F}+j(\mathfrak{H})\subseteq\Omega_{F}
. For such
\omega
and
h
, we also get, for all
0\leq\ell\leq q
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}(\omega+j(h))=\widetilde{F}_{\ell}(\omega+j(h))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{q-\ell}\frac{1}{k!}\langle\widetilde{F}_{\ell+k}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{q-\ell}\frac{1}{k!}\langle\widetilde{D}^{\ell+k}\widetilde{F}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}.

∎

For a random variable
F=I_{q}(f)
with
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
, the events
\Omega_{F}
and
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
appearing in Theorem
3.2
have the advantage of being independent of
h\in\mathfrak{H}
; however, the associated Taylor expansion (
37
) holds only for a random variable
\widetilde{F}
almost surely equal to
F
. If instead we fix the vector
h\in\mathfrak{H}
beforehand (which is enough to verify the
(SGD)
condition) then the Taylor expansion can be written directly for
F
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
be fixed, and set
F=I_{q}(f)
. For every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, there exists an event
\Omega_{h}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
and
0\leq\ell\leq q
,

D^{\ell}F(\omega+j(h))=\sum_{k=0}^{q-\ell}\frac{1}{k!}\langle D^{k+\ell}F(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{F}
be the events given by Theorem
3.2
. Fix
h\in\mathfrak{H}
and consider the translation
T_{h}\,:\,\Omega\to\Omega\,:\,\omega\mapsto\omega+j(h)
, together with the induced pushforward measure

\mathbb{P}_{h}=(T_{h})_{\ast}\mathbb{P}.

The Cameron-Martin theorem
[
6
]
ensures that
\mathbb{P}_{h}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative

\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{h}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\exp\left(X_{h}-\frac{1}{2}\|h\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\right).

(43)

Consequently, the event

\{\omega\,:\,\omega+j(h)\in\Omega_{F}^{\prime}\}=\Omega_{F}^{\prime}-j(h)

has probability
1
. The set
\Omega_{h}:=(\Omega_{F}^{\prime}-j(h))\cap\Omega_{F}^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{F}
is therefore of probability
1
, and the conclusion follows from (
37
) combined with the identity
\widetilde{D}^{\ell}\widetilde{F}=D^{\ell}F
, valid on
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

3.2
Towards (SGD): construction of the processes
S
and
\Gamma

Combining the results of Subsection
3.1
with Proposition
2.4
, we are now in a position to construct the processes
\{S_{t,\varepsilon}^{h}\,:\,t\in[0,T],\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
and
\{\Gamma_{t,\varepsilon}^{h}\,:\,t\in[0,T],\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
announced at the end of Subsection
2.3
. In what follows, given
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
h\in\mathfrak{H}
and
1\leq k\leq q
, the notation
\left<D^{k}F_{t},h^{\otimes k}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}
stands for

\displaystyle\left(\left<D^{k}F^{1}_{t}),h^{\otimes k}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}},\ldots,\left<D^{k}F^{d}_{t},h^{\otimes k}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}\right),

while we set
\left<D^{0}F_{t},h^{\otimes 0}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes 0}}:=F_{t}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
F=\{F_{t}=(I_{q}(f_{t}^{1}),\ldots,I_{q}(f_{t}^{m}))\,,\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued chaos process on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
, and assume that
F
satisfies hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
with
H\in(\tfrac{1}{2},1)
.
Let
R=\{R^{h}_{t,\varepsilon}\,;\;t\in\mathbb{R},\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
and
S=\{S^{h}_{t,\varepsilon}\,;\;t\in\mathbb{R},\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
be the
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued stochastic processes defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle R_{t,\varepsilon}^{h}=F_{t}(\bullet+\varepsilon\,j(h))\quad\text{ and }\quad S^{h}_{t,\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{\varepsilon^{k}}{k!}\langle D^{k}F_{t},h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}.

(44)

Then the following hold:

(a)

S
is a version of
R
;

(b)

for every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, almost every
\omega\in\Omega
and every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, one has
S^{h}_{\star,\varepsilon}(\omega)\in W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
;

(c)

for every
\beta\in(0,H-\tfrac{1}{2})
and
1-\alpha=H-\beta
, and for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the map from
\mathbb{R}
into
W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
defined by
\varepsilon\mapsto S^{h}_{\star,\varepsilon}(\omega)
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. Moreover, for every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{d^{\ell}}{d\varepsilon^{\ell}}S^{h}_{\star,\varepsilon}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=l}^{q}\frac{\varepsilon^{k-l}}{k!}\left<D^{k}F_{\star}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}},&\text{if }&0\leq\ell\leq q\\
0,&\text{if }&\ell>q.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Point (a) is a direct consequence of Corollary
3.3
. Point (b) follows from hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
(see the discussion following inequality (
22
) above) together with the embedding (
20
). Point (c) is a straightforward consequence of elementary differential calculus.
∎

Building on this proposition, we define, for each
h\in\mathfrak{H}
,

\Phi_{h}\,:\,\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\,:\,(\varepsilon,\omega)\mapsto S^{h}_{\star,\varepsilon}(\omega).

With these notations in hand, we set, for all
h\in\mathfrak{H}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega
,

\Gamma_{h,t}(\varepsilon,\omega):=\Psi_{t}(\Phi_{h}(\varepsilon,\omega)),

(45)

where
\Psi
is the mapping introduced in (
23
). Recall also that
\mathcal{D}\Psi(\varphi)[\psi]
denotes the Fréchet derivative of
\Psi
at
\varphi
in the direction
\psi
.

We now verify that the process
\{\Gamma_{h,t}(\varepsilon,\bullet)\,:\,t\in[0,T],\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},\,h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
enjoys the required regularity properties.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
F=\{F_{t}=(I_{p}(f_{t}^{1}),\ldots,I_{p}(f_{t}^{m}))\,,\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued chaos process on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
, satisfying hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
, and assume that the coefficients
(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
and
(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,\,1\leq\ell\leq m}
of the stochastic differential equation (
1
) satisfy hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{1})}
.
Then, for every
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, every
t\in[0,T]
and every
1\leq k\leq d
, the map
\Gamma_{h,t}^{k}(\star,\omega)
is differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
, with derivative given, for every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, by

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Gamma_{h,t}^{k}(\varepsilon,\omega)=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}(S_{\star,\varepsilon}^{h}(\omega))\left[\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}S_{\star,\varepsilon}^{h}(\omega)\right].

(46)

In particular, for
\varepsilon=0
, we get

\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Gamma_{h,t}^{k}(\varepsilon,\omega)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}(F_{\star}(\omega))\left[\langle DF_{\star}(\omega),h\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right].

(47)

Proof.

Since
F
satisfies hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
, point (c) of Proposition
3.4
ensures that, for each
h\in\mathfrak{H}
and almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the mapping
\Phi_{h}(\star,\omega)
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. On the other hand, Proposition
2.4
guarantees that
\Psi_{t}^{k}
is Fréchet differentiable for every
t\in[0,T]
and every
1\leq k\leq d
. The chain rule then yields, for all
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, every
t\in[0,T]
and every
1\leq k\leq d
, the differentiability of
\Gamma^{k}_{h,t}(\star,\omega)
. Moreover, under the same assumptions and for any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, the chain rule gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Gamma_{h,t}^{k}(\varepsilon,\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}(\Psi_{t}^{k}\circ\Phi_{h}(\star,\omega))[\varepsilon]

(48)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}\left(\Phi_{h}(\varepsilon,\omega)\right)[\mathcal{D}\Phi_{h}(\star,\omega)[\varepsilon]]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}\left(S_{\star,\varepsilon}^{h}(\omega)\right)\left[\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}S_{\star,\varepsilon}^{h}(\omega)\right].

This concludes the proof of the first part of Proposition
3.5
. Equation (
47
) is then a direct consequence of (
45
) and point (c) of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

3.3
Towards (RAC): a refined version of the driving chaotic process

In this subsection, we construct a version of the driving chaotic process that enjoys strong continuity and smoothness properties. More precisely, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F
be a real-valued chaos process on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
.
Assume that
F
verifies the hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
, with
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
For any
h\in\mathfrak{H}
, there exists a stochastic process
(\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that:

(a)

For any
t\in[0,T]
,
\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}=F_{t}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.;

(b)

For all
\omega\in\Omega
, the map

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\ni(t,\varepsilon)\longmapsto\widetilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}\big(\omega+\varepsilon\,j(h)\big)

is continuous.

(c)

Let
\beta\in(0,H-\frac{1}{2})
and
1-\alpha=H-\beta
. For all
\omega\in\Omega
, the mapping from
\mathbb{R}
into
W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
defined by
\varepsilon\mapsto\tilde{F}^{h}_{\star}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}
.

The proof of Theorem
3.6
relies on the following lemma. As its proof is somewhat tangential to the main thread of this paper, we have chosen to postpone it to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
q
be a positive integer,
e_{0}\in\mathfrak{H}
, with
\|e_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1
, and
(f_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a family of vectors in
\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
.
We denote by
V
the orthogonal complement of
\operatorname{span}\{e_{0}\}
in
\mathfrak{H}
.
Hence, there exist
(f_{t,q-k})_{t\in[0,T]}\subset V^{\odot{(q-k)}}
, with
0\leq k\leq q
, such that

I_{q}(f_{t})=\sum_{k=0}^{q}I_{k}(e_{0}^{\otimes k})I_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k}),

(49)

where
I_{0}(h^{\otimes 0})=1
. Furtheremore, for each
0\leq k\leq q
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

\|f_{t,q-k}-f_{s,q-k}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes{(q-k)}}}\leq C\|f_{t}-f_{s}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}.

(50)

We now have all the ingredients needed to prove Theorem
3.6
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Let
h\in\mathfrak{H}
be fixed. The case
h=0
is trivial, so we assume
h\neq 0
and set
e_{0}:=h/\left\|h\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Let
V
be the orthogonal complement of
\operatorname{span}\{e_{0}\}
in
\mathfrak{H}
and
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
an orthonormal basis of
V
.
Then
(e_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{H}
.
Let
X=\{X_{g}\,:g\in\mathfrak{H}\}
denote the isonormal Gaussian process on the abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
introduced in Subsection
2.1
, and let
\tilde{X}=\{\tilde{X}_{g}\,:\;g\in\mathfrak{H}\}
be its version provided by Proposition
3.1
, whose notations we adopt here. Consider first the event

\Omega_{h}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\Omega_{g_{n}}.

One clearly has
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
and
\Omega_{h}+\mathbb{R}j(h)\subset\Omega_{h}
, where

\Omega_{h}+\mathbb{R}j(h):=\{\omega+\varepsilon j(h)\,:\,\omega\in\Omega_{h},\,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Moreover, by equation (
34
), for every
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{e_{0}}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))=\tilde{X}_{e_{0}}(\omega)+\varepsilon\left\|h\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2},\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{X}_{e_{n}}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))=\tilde{X}_{e_{n}}(\omega),

(51)

for
n\geq 1
.

Since Proposition
3.1
ensures that
\tilde{X}_{g}=X_{g}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for every
g\in\mathfrak{H}
, we immediately obtain, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle F_{t}=I_{q}(f_{t})=\tilde{I}_{q}(f_{t})\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(52)

Furthermore, Lemma
3.7
yields the existence of families
(f_{t,q-k})_{t\in[0,T]}\subset V^{\odot{(q-k)}}
, for
k=0,\ldots,q
, such that, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\tilde{I}^{h}_{q}(f_{t})=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\tilde{I}_{k}(e_{0}^{\otimes k})(f_{t,q-k})\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(53)

Consider now the
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{G}:=\sigma(\tilde{X}_{e_{n}}:\,n\geq 1).

For every
t\in[0,T]
and
0\leq k\leq q
, since
f_{t,q-k}\in V^{\odot{(q-k)}}
and
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
V
, there exists a sequence of polynomials
(P_{n_{\ell}})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
(P_{n_{\ell}}(\tilde{X}_{e_{1}},\dots,\tilde{X}_{e_{n_{\ell}}}))_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
converges almost surely to
\tilde{I}_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k})
; see for instance the proof of
[
21
, Lemma 4.3]
.
Introduce the event

\Omega_{h,t}:=\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,(P_{n_{\ell}}(\tilde{X}_{e_{1}}(\omega),\dots,\tilde{X}_{e_{n_{\ell}}}(\omega)))_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ converges}\}.

It is clear that
\Omega_{h,t,k}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h,t})=1
. Accordingly, the random variable

Y^{h}_{t,k}\,:\,\omega\mapsto\begin{cases}\lim_{\ell\to+\infty}P_{n_{\ell}}(\tilde{X}_{e_{1}}(\omega),\dots,\tilde{X}_{e_{n_{\ell}}}(\omega))&\text{ if }\omega\in\Omega_{h,t,k}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

is
\mathcal{G}
-measurable and almost surely equal to
\tilde{I}_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k})
.
Combining the hypercontractivity property for multiple Wiener–Itô integrals (
11
), the isometry formula (
12
), inequality (
50
) and Hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
, we deduce that, for every
0\leq k\leq q
,
p\geq 2
and
s,t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle[|Y^{h}_{t,k}-Y^{h}_{s,k}|^{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{I}^{h}_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k})-\tilde{I}^{h}_{q-k}(f_{s,q-k})|^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)^{p(q-k)/2}\big\{\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{I}^{h}_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k})-\tilde{I}^{h}_{q-k}(f_{s,q-k})|^{2}]\big\}^{p/2}

\displaystyle=(p-1)^{p(q-k)/2}\big((q-k)!\big)^{p/2}\|f_{t,q-k}-f_{s,q-k}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes{q-k}}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(p-1)^{p(q-k)/2}\big((q-k)!\big)^{p/2}\|f_{t}-f_{s}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(p-1)^{p(q-k)/2}\big((q-k)!\big)^{p/2}|t-s|^{pH}.

Hence, for any
\beta\in(0,H-\frac{1}{2})
, the Kolmogorov continuity theorem applied on the abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P},V)
yields, for each
0\leq k\leq q
, a
\mathcal{G}
-measurable stochastic process
(\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k})_{t\in[0,T]}
with
(H-\beta)
-Hölder continuous sample paths and such that, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}=\tilde{I}^{h}_{q-k}(f_{t,q-k})\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(54)

We are now ready to introduce the stochastic process
(\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
defined, for every
t\in[0,T]
, by

\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}:=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\tilde{I}_{k}(e_{0}^{\otimes k})\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{h}}.

(55)

It remains to check that it enjoys the announced properties.

First, combining (
52
), (
53
), (
54
) and (
55
) with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
, we see that
\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}=F_{t}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Secondly, the
\mathcal{G}
-measurability of
(\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k})_{t\in[0,T]}
, together with (
51
), yields, for every
0\leq k\leq q
,
t\in[0,T]
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))=\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}(\omega),

while the first part of the proof of Theorem
3.2
, combined with equality (
15
), gives, for the same
\varepsilon
and
\omega
,

\tilde{I}_{k}(e_{0}^{\otimes k})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\frac{\varepsilon^{\ell}}{\ell!}\langle\tilde{I}_{k-\ell}(e_{0}^{\otimes k})(\omega),h^{\otimes\ell}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes\ell}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\frac{\varepsilon^{\ell}}{\ell!}\|h\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{\ell}\tilde{I}_{k-\ell}(e_{0}^{\otimes{k-\ell}})(\omega).

Altogether, since
\Omega_{h}+\mathbb{R}j(h)\subset\Omega_{h}
and
\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}
vanishes outside
\Omega_{h}
for every
t\in[0,T]
and
0\leq k\leq q
, we obtain, for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,

\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\frac{\varepsilon^{\ell}}{\ell!}\|h\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{\ell}\tilde{I}_{k-\ell}(e_{0}^{\otimes{k-\ell}})(\omega)\right)\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k}(\omega).

(56)

This formula, together with the
(H-\beta)
-Hölder continuity of the sample paths of
(\tilde{Y}^{h}_{t,k})_{t\in[0,T]}
for every
0\leq k\leq q
, entails that, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, the map
(t,\varepsilon)\mapsto\tilde{F}^{\,h}_{t}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))
is continuous, and the map from
\mathbb{R}
into
W_{2}^{1-\alpha}\left(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
defined by
\varepsilon\mapsto\tilde{F}^{h}_{\star}(\omega+\varepsilon j(h))
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
∎

4
Malliavin smoothness of the solution

The tools built in Section
3
bring together all the quantities announced in the roadmap at the end of Subsection
2.3
. We can now prove that the solution of the stochastic differential equation (
1
) is smooth in the Malliavin sense.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F=\{F_{t}=(I_{q}(f_{t}^{1}),\ldots,I_{q}(f_{t}^{m}))\,,\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued chaos process on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
. Assume that,
F
verifies the hypotheses
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
with

5
Absolute continuity of the law of the solution

In this section, we show that the law of the solution to the stochastic differential equation (
1
) is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Our main tool is the Bouleau–Hirsch criterion, a cornerstone of Malliavin calculus
[
5
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and let
\mathbf{Y}=\left(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}\right)
be a random vector satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

Y_{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, for some
p\in(1,\infty)
;

(ii)

\operatorname{det}\left(\Gamma_{\mathbf{Y}}\right)>0

\mathbb{P}
-a.s., where
\Gamma_{\mathbf{Y}}=\left(\left\langle DY_{i},DY_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
denotes the Malliavin matrix of
\mathbf{Y}
.

Then the law of
\mathbf{Y}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The absolute continuity of the law of the solution to a stochastic differential equation is typically obtained under various non-degeneracy conditions, imposed both on the coefficients and on the driving process. In what follows, we take as a starting point the formulation of these conditions proposed in
[
7
]
within the framework of rough path theory.

We first impose the following ellipticity assumption on the coefficients
(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
and
(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,1\leq\ell\leq m}
of (
1
).

\mathbf{(H_{3})}
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the vector space spanned by
\{\sigma_{1,\ell}(x),\ldots,\sigma_{d,\ell}(x)\,,\;1\leq\ell\leq m\}
is equal
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The conditions imposed on the driving process in
[
7
]
are specific to the Gaussian rough path setting. We first recall them before proposing their counterparts in our non-Gaussian framework.

\mathbf{(GRPH_{4})}
The components of the driving Gaussian process are independent.

\mathbf{(GRPH_{5})}
For any
g=(g^{1},\dots,g^{d})\,:\,[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with bounded
p
-variation
2
2

2

In
[
7
]
, the driving process is assumed to be
p
-rough path.
,

\left(\int_{0}^{T}g_{s}dh_{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}g_{s}^{k}dh_{s}^{k}=0\penalty\ \forall\,h\in j(\mathfrak{H})\right)\Rightarrow g=0.

In our setting, we use conditions analogous to
\mathbf{(GRPH_{4})}
and
\mathbf{(GRPH_{5})}
, but formulated at the level of Malliavin derivatives. For the independence assumption, we adopt the following hypothesis.

\mathbf{(H_{4})}
The components of the driving process in (
1
) are Malliavin independent: for all
1\leq\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\leq m
and
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely,

\langle DF_{t}^{\ell},DF_{t}^{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=0.

When
F
is Gaussian, it is straightforward to check that
\mathbf{(GRPH_{4})}
and
\mathbf{(H_{4})}
are equivalent. As for
\mathbf{(GRPH_{5})}
, the bounded
p
-variation assumption is natural in the rough path setting; in our context, it is more natural to work with a Hölder-type condition.

\mathbf{(H_{5})}
The driving process in (
1
) is non-degenerate, if for any fixed
t\in(0,T]
, and any
\mathbb{R}^{m}
valued stochastic process
(Y_{r})_{r\in[0,t]}
such that the sample paths of
Y
are
\gamma
-Hölder continuous, for some
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\exists\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{F},\text{ with }\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})>0,\text{ s.t., }\forall\omega\in\Omega_{0},\text{ and }1\leq\ell\leq m,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left\|\int_{0}^{t}Y^{\ell}_{s}(\omega)dDF^{\ell}(s,\omega)\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=0\bigg)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\bigg(\exists\Omega_{1}\in\mathcal{F},\text{ with }\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})>0,\;\exists s\in[0,t],\text{ s.t., }\forall\omega\in\Omega_{1}\,Y_{s}(\omega)=0\;\;\;\bigg).

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
F=\{F_{t}=(I_{q}(f_{t}^{1}),\ldots,I_{q}(f_{t}^{m}))\,,\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued chaos process on an abstract Wiener space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})
, satisfying hypotheses
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
,
\mathbf{(H_{4})}
and
\mathbf{(H_{5})}
. Assume moreover that the coefficients
(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}
and
(\sigma_{k,\ell})_{1\leq k\leq d,\,1\leq\ell\leq m}
of the stochastic differential equation (
1
) satisfy hypotheses
\mathbf{(H_{1})}
and
\mathbf{(H_{3})}
.
Then, for every
t\in(0,T]
, the law of
X_{t}
, where
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is the unique solution to (
1
), is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Parts of the proof are inspired by that of
[
24
, Theorem 8]
. Fix

. By Theorem
4.1
,

for every

. In view of the Bouleau–Hirsch criterion (Theorem

), it thus suffices to show that the determinant of the Malliavin matrix
\Gamma_{X_{t}}
of

is almost surely positive.
Let

be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{H}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{X_{t}}=(M_{k,k^{\prime}})_{1\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq d},

Assume, for contradiction, that

\Omega_{0}
. On the other hand, we may rewrite

\displaystyle a^{T}\Gamma_{X_{t}}a

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\left<\left<DX_{t},e_{n}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}},a\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right|^{2},

(69)

where

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

By (

), for almost every

\displaystyle\left<DX_{t}^{k}(\omega),e_{n}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}}=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}(F_{\star}(\omega))\left[\langle DF_{\star}(\omega),e_{n}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right].

Moreover, using (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\Psi_{t}^{k}(F_{\star}(\omega))\left[\langle DF_{\star}(\omega),e_{n}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right]

where

is the operator defined in (

and every

\displaystyle\left<\left(\mathcal{D}_{2}T(0,X(\omega))^{-1}\circ\mathcal{D}_{1}T(0,X(\omega))\right)\left[\langle DF_{\star}(\omega),e_{n}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right]_{t},a(\omega)\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}T(0,X(\omega))\left[\langle DF_{\star}(\omega),e_{n}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\right]_{t},a(\omega)\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k,\ell}(X_{s}(\omega))d\left<DF^{l}(s,\omega),e_{n}\right>_{\mathfrak{H}}\right)a^{k}(\omega)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}a^{k}(\omega)\sigma_{k,\ell}(X_{s}(\omega))dDF^{\ell}(s,\omega)\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}=0.

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\mathfrak{H})

\mathbb{P}

This formalization allows us to write, for every

\left<DF\,,\,DG\right>_{\mathfrak{H}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{(\ell)}_{r}F\,D^{(\ell)}_{r}G\,dr.

Throughout this section,

denotes the

) with respect to the isonormal Gaussian process (

Let us first consider the case

Definition 6.1

\displaystyle Z^{H,q}_{t}=I_{q}(L_{t}^{H,q})\qquad\text{where}\qquad L_{t}^{H,q}(\xi)=c(H,q)\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(s-\xi_{j}\right)_{+}^{H_{0}-\frac{3}{2}}ds,

(73)

H_{0}=1+\frac{H-1}{q}\in\left(1-\frac{1}{2q},1\right),\quad\text{and}\quad c(H,q)=\sqrt{\frac{H(2H-1)}{q!\,\beta^{q}\left(H_{0}-\frac{1}{2},2-2H_{0}\right)}}.

Here
\beta
stands for the Beta function and we use the convention, for each
(\theta,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\theta^{\alpha}_{+}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\theta^{\alpha},&\text{if }\theta>0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The family of Hermite processes provides paradigmatic examples of chaotic random processes. It includes the Rosenblatt process, obtained for
q=2
, and the fractional Brownian motion, obtained for
q=1
; the latter is the only Gaussian element of this family. A direct computation (see e.g.
[
30
, Proposition 2.1]
) shows that, for every
s,t\geq 0
,

\langle L_{t}^{H,q},L_{s}^{H,q}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{q},\mathbb{R})}=\frac{q!}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right),

which yields, by the polarization formula,

\|L_{t}^{H,q}-L_{s}^{H,q}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{q},\mathbb{R})}\leq q!\,|t-s|^{2H}.

(74)

In particular, hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{2})}
is satisfied by the one-dimensional Hermite process
Z^{H,q}
. We refer the reader to Tudor’s monograph
[
30
]
for a detailed presentation of this class of processes and of their properties, which include stationarity of increments and
H
-self-similarity. For our purposes, the key feature is that
(Z_{t}^{H,q})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is adapted to the filtration defined, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\{B(\mathbbm{1}_{A})\,:A\text{ is a Borel }\lambda\text{-finite subset of }(-\infty,t]\}\vee\mathcal{N},

where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the collection of
\mathbb{P}
-null subsets of
\Omega
and
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. In view of
[
22
, Corollary 1.2.1]
, we therefore have, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

D_{r}Z_{t}^{H,q}=0\qquad\text{for }\mathbb{P}\otimes\lambda\text{-a.e.\ }(\omega,r)\in\Omega\times(t,\infty).

(75)

We now turn to the definition of the multidimensional Hermite process.

Fix

(76)

the process

Recall that, with

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{j,q}^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}}=\begin{cases}\hat{h}_{j,q}^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}}=h_{q},&\text{ if }i_{1}=\ldots=i_{q}=\ell,\\

A direct verification shows that, if

Proposition 6.3

Let

and, for

\displaystyle I_{q}(\hat{h}_{\ell,q})=I_{q}^{B^{\ell}}(h_{q}).

We first treat the case where

78

\displaystyle I_{q+1}(\hat{h}_{\ell,q+1})=

\displaystyle I_{q+1}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q+1}\sum_{k=1}^{q+1}\delta\left(I_{q}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}\right),

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\left(I_{q}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=I_{q}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})\delta(\tilde{g}_{k,\ell})

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\langle DI_{q}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell}),\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}

\displaystyle=I_{q}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})I_{1}(\tilde{g}_{k,\ell})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\neq k\end{subarray}}^{q+1}I_{q-1}(\tilde{g}_{1,\ell}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{n,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}}\odot\dots\odot\tilde{g}_{q+1,\ell})\left\langle\tilde{g}_{n,\ell},\tilde{g}_{k,\ell}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}

\displaystyle I_{q+1}(\hat{h}_{\ell,q+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q+1}\sum_{k=1}^{q+1}\left(I_{q}^{B^{\ell}}(\tilde{g}_{1}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{g_{k}}\odot\dots\odot g_{q+1})I_{1}^{B^{\ell}}(g_{k})\right.

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\neq k\end{subarray}}^{q+1}\left.I_{q-1}^{B^{\ell}}(g_{1}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{g_{n}}\odot\dots\odot\widehat{g_{k}}\odot\dots\odot g_{q+1})\left\langle g_{n},g_{k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})}\right).

Running the same argument backwards, we deduce the claimed identity

The general case then follows by a density argument.
∎

Lemma 6.5

Let

\displaystyle\int_{t-\varepsilon}^{t}|D_{r}Z^{H,q}_{t}-D_{r}Z^{H,q}_{t-\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dr\overset{\text{law}}{=}\varepsilon^{2H}\int_{0}^{1}|D_{r}Z^{H,q}_{1}|^{2}\,dr.

(82)

Proof.

Fix
t>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,t)

\displaystyle\int_{t-\varepsilon}^{t}dx|D_{x}Z^{H,q}_{t}-D_{x}Z^{H,q}_{t-\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{h}^{\varepsilon+h}d{x}|D_{x}Z^{H,q}_{h+\varepsilon}-D_{x}Z^{H,q}_{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\,\int_{h}^{\varepsilon+h}d{x}\left|I_{q-1}\left(L_{h,h+\varepsilon}^{H,q}({x},\star)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\,\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}dr\left|I_{q-1}\left(L_{h,h+\varepsilon}^{H,q}(r+h,\star)\right)\right|^{2},

(83)

where the last equality follows from the change of variable

. Moreover,

\displaystyle L_{h,h+\varepsilon}^{H,q}(r+h,\star)

\displaystyle=c(H,q)\,\int_{h}^{h+\varepsilon}ds\left(\left(s-r-h\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}\left(s-\star_{j}\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(84)

where the last equality was obtained by the change of variable

, and

is the constant defined in Definition

. Combining (
6
) and (
6
), the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle q^{2}c^{2}(H,q)\,\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}dr\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{q-1}dB_{\xi_{i}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}d\theta\left(\left(\theta-r\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}\left(\theta+h-\xi_{j}\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle q^{2}c^{2}(H,q)\,\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}dr\,\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{q-1}d{B}_{\xi_{i}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}d\theta\left(\left(\theta-r\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}\left(\theta-\xi_{j}\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}

(86)

\displaystyle q^{2}c^{2}(H,q)\,\varepsilon^{\frac{2(H-1)}{q}+2}\,\int_{0}^{1}ds\,

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{q-1}d{B}_{\xi_{i}}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\left(\left(\tau-s\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}\left(\varepsilon\,\tau-\xi_{j}\right)_{+}^{\frac{H-1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
Z^{H,q}=(Z^{H,q,1},\ldots,Z^{H,q,m})
be the
m
-dimensional Hermite process of order
q\geq 1
and self-similarity parameter
H\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
defined in (
81
). Then
Z^{H,q}
satisfies hypothesis
\mathbf{(H_{5})}
.

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,T]
and let
Y=(Y_{r})_{r\in[0,t]}
be a real-valued stochastic process with
\gamma
-Hölder continuous trajectories, for some
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
. It suffices to show that, given a one-dimensional Hermite process
Z=\{Z_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
of order
q\geq 1
and self-similarity parameter
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, the existence of some
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}(\omega)dDZ_{s}(\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}}=0\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\Omega_{0}

(88)

implies the existence of
\Omega_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})>0
such that

\displaystyle Y_{t}(\omega)=0\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\Omega_{1}.

(89)

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and set
h=t-\varepsilon
. By (
88
), we have, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
,

\displaystyle 0=\left\|\int_{0}^{h}Y_{s}(\omega)dDZ_{s}(\omega)+\int_{h}^{\varepsilon+h}Y_{s}(\omega)dDZ_{s}(\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

(90)

Observe now that, by (
75
), for any
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and
s\in[0,h]
one has
D_{r}Z_{s}(\omega)=0
for
\lambda\otimes\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
(r,\omega)\in[h,h+\varepsilon]\times\Omega
. Consequently, there exists
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime}_{0})>0
such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}Y_{s}(\omega)dDZ_{s}(\omega)=0\qquad\text{in }L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R}).

(91)

Combining (
90
) and (
91
), we obtain, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{h}^{\varepsilon+h}Y_{s}(\omega)dDZ_{s}(\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

(92)

Applying the Sewing lemma
[
15
, Proposition 2.1]
together with (
92
) yields, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left\|Y_{h}(\omega)\big(DZ_{\varepsilon+h}(\omega)-DZ_{h}(\omega)\big)\right\|_{L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\omega}\varepsilon^{\gamma+H-\beta},

\beta\in(0,H\wedge\alpha)
. We thereby infer that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle|Y_{h}(\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\omega}\,\varepsilon^{\gamma+H-\beta}}{\left\|DZ_{\varepsilon+h}(\omega)-DZ_{h}(\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R})}}.

(93)

By Lemma
6.5
, we have, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,t)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma+H-\beta}}{\left\|DZ_{\varepsilon+h}-DZ_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}([h,h+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R})}}\overset{\text{law}}{=}\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma-\beta}}{\left\|DZ_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}}.

(94)

as

such that, for almost every

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{\gamma+H-\beta}}{\left\|DZ_{t}(\omega)-DZ_{t-\varepsilon_{n}}(\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}([t-\varepsilon_{n},t],\mathbb{R})}}=0.

(95)

Combining (

\Omega_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
with

such that

, which proves (

Proof of Lemma
3.7

Let
(\mathfrak{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}})
be a real separable Hilbert space, and fix
e_{0}\in\mathfrak{H}
with
\|e_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1
. Let
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of
\operatorname{span}\{e_{0}\}

\mathfrak{H}

(e_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{H}
. Every

then admits the expansion

f=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}=0}^{\infty}\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q})\,e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{q}},

\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q})=\big\langle f,\,e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{q}}\big\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}.

Since
f
is symmetric,
\lambda
is invariant under permutations of its arguments: for every permutation
\sigma

\{1,\dots,q\}

\lambda(f;j_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,j_{\sigma(q)})=\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}).

For notational convenience, given
e_{j_{1}},\ldots,e_{j_{q}}\in\mathfrak{H}
we set

\displaystyle e_{j_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot e_{j_{q}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{\sigma}e_{j_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{\sigma(q)}},

where the sum runs over all permutations
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,q\}
. We shall also use the shorthand

\displaystyle e_{0}^{\odot k}\odot e_{j_{1}}\odot\ldots\odot e_{j_{q-k}}

\displaystyle:=\underbrace{e_{0}\odot\cdots\odot e_{0}}_{k\text{ times}}\odot e_{j_{1}}\odot\ldots\odot e_{j_{q-k}}.

Equivalently (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Eq. (B.3.1), p. 206]
), the expansion (
96
) can be rewritten in terms of symmetric tensors as

f=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}=0}^{\infty}\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q})\,e_{j_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot e_{j_{q}}.

(97)

Grouping the terms according to the number of indices equal to
0
, we may rewrite (
97
) as

f=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)e_{0}^{\odot k}\odot e_{j_{1}}\odot\ldots\odot e_{j_{q-k}},

(98)

where

\displaystyle a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)

\displaystyle:=\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k\text{ times}}).

Before turning to the proof of Lemma
3.7
, we recall the notion of contraction of tensors. Fix
r\in\{0,1,\dots,m\wedge n\}
. We first define the
r
th contraction on elementary tensors. For
r\geq 1
, we set

\displaystyle(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}})\otimes_{r}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{m}})

\displaystyle:=\Bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}\langle e_{j_{\ell}},e_{k_{\ell}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\Bigg)\,e_{j_{r+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}}\otimes e_{k_{r+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{m}}\;\in\;\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes(m+n-2r)}.

For
r=0
, we use the convention

(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}})\otimes_{0}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{m}})=e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}}\otimes e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{m}}.

In particular, when
m=n=r
,

(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}})\otimes_{n}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{n}})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\langle e_{j_{\ell}},e_{k_{\ell}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=\big\langle e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}},\,e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{n}}\big\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}}.

The definition is then extended to general tensors by bilinearity and continuity. If
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}
and
g\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes m}
admit expansions as in (
96
), their
r
th contraction is given by

\displaystyle f\otimes_{r}g

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\geq 1}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\geq 1}\lambda(f;j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\,\lambda(g;k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})

(99)

\displaystyle\times(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{n}})\otimes r(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{m}}).

By construction,
f\otimes_{r}g\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes(m+n-2r)}
for every
r\leq m\wedge n
. In general,
f\otimes_{r}g
is not symmetric, and we denote by

f\widetilde{\otimes}_{r}g\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot(m+n-2r)}

its symmetrization.

In the canonical case
\mathfrak{H}=L^{2}(T,\mathcal{B},\mu)
, with
\mu

\sigma
-finite and non-atomic, formula (
99
) takes the form, for
\mu^{m+n-2r}
-a.e.
(t_{1},\dots,t_{m+n-2r})\in T^{m+n-2r}
,

\displaystyle(f\otimes_{r}g)(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+n-2r})

(100)

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{r}}f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-r})\,

\displaystyle\times g(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r},t_{n-r+1},\ldots,t_{m+n-2r})\,\mu^{\otimes r}(du).

We are now in a position to prove Lemma
3.7
, which is a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, and hence also in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Choose an orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
V
. By (
98
), we have the expansion

f_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f_{t};j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)e_{0}^{\odot k}\odot e_{j_{1}}\odot\ldots\odot e_{j_{q-k}},

(101)

where

\displaystyle a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f_{t};j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)

\displaystyle:=\lambda(f_{t};j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k\text{ times}}).

Since
f_{t}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
, we have, for every
k=0,\ldots,q
,

\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f_{t};j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)\right|^{2}<\infty.

For each
k=0,\ldots,q
, we may therefore define the vector
f_{t,q-k}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot{q-k}}
by

\displaystyle f_{t,q-k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{(q)}\left(f_{t};j_{1},\ldots,j_{q-k}\right)e_{j_{1}}\odot\ldots\odot e_{j_{q-k}}.

Plugging this definition into (
101
), we obtain the tensor decomposition in
\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}

f_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}e_{0}^{\otimes k}\odot f_{t,q-k}.

(102)

Moreover,

\|f_{t}-f_{s}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|e_{0}^{\otimes k}\odot(f_{t,q-k}-f_{s,q-k})\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes q}},

(103)

from which the inequality (
50
) follows directly.

It remains to establish the decomposition (
49
). To this end, observe that

I_{q}(f_{t})=\sum_{k=0}^{q}I_{q}\left(e_{0}^{\otimes k}\odot f_{t,q-k}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star)}}{{=}}\sum_{k=0}^{q}I_{k}\left(e_{0}^{\otimes k}\right)I_{q-k}\left(f_{t,q-k}\right),

where
(\star)
follows from the product formula (
13
), combined with the vanishing of the contractions
e_{0}^{\otimes k}\otimes rf_{t,q-k}
for
k=1,\ldots,q-1
and
r=1,\ldots,k\wedge(q-k)
. This concludes the proof.
