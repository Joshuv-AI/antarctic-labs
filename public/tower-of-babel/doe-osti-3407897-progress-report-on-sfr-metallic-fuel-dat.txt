 ABSTRACT

This report summarizes the progress of SFR metallic fuel qualification related activities, which are
focused on providing quality assurance relevant information applicable to experiments irradiated
during the Integral Fast Reactor (IFR) program. An overview of the metallic fuel performance data
and the associated databases, including the EBR-II Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD),
Out-of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD), and TREAT Experimental Relational Database (TREXR)
is included. The legacy data in the databases, including as-built, post-irradiation examination (PIE),
operating parameters, and out-of-pile experiment post-test data are introduced. The SFR metallic
fuel Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP) and its implementation to qualify these legacy data
is described in detail. Important PIE data QA documents and the specifications of seven types of
PIE measurements (contact profilometry, laser profilometry, neutron radiography, gamma scan,
fission gas release fission gas chemistry, and metallography) are provided. Examples of the
implementation of the QAPP to qualify each of those types of PIE data are provided.

 Progress Report on SFR Metallic Fuel Data Qualification
 1 INTRODUCTION
The purpose of this technical report is to summarize Sodium-cooled Fast Reactors (SFR) metallic
fuel qualification related activities which are focused on providing quality assurance relevant
information on the Integral Fast Reactor (IFR) program experiments currently available in the
Experimental Breeder Reactor II (EBR-II) Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD) [1], Out-
of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD) [2], and the Transient Test Reactor Facility (TREAT)
Experimental Relational Database (TREXR) [3]. The IFR test assemblies were irradiated in EBR-
II to demonstrate different aspects of the U-Zr based metallic fuel performance. Some of these
EBR-II irradiated pins were part of an out-of-pile testing program that subjected them to out-of-
pile furnace overheating tests to evaluate transient fuel performance. Also included is a summary
of the quality assurance program that is being implemented to qualify the data.

1.1 Overview of Metallic Fuel Irradiation Test Program

The Integral Fast Reactor (IFR) metallic fuel testing program was initiated in 1984 to develop and
demonstrate metallic alloy fuels for sodium-cooled fast reactors with a closed fuel cycle. The
Experimental Breeder Reactor-II (EBR-II), a 62.5 MWt sodium-cooled fast reactor at Argonne
National Laboratory – West (ANL-West 1950’s – 2005) (currently Materials and Fuels Complex
of Idaho National Laboratory), served as the primary irradiation test bed. A 10 wt% zirconium
addition was selected as the reference alloying agent, and the EBR-II core was gradually converted
from earlier U-5Fs driver fuel to Mark-III (U-10Zr with D-9 or SS-316 cladding) and then Mark-
IV (U-10Zr with HT-9 cladding) configurations. The Experimental Fuels Laboratory, established
in 1984, fabricated plutonium-bearing ternary fuel, U-xPu-10Zr (3% ≤ x ≤ 28%). In total, 16,811
U-Zr and 660 U-Pu-Zr fuel pins were irradiated in EBR-II before its shutdown in September 1994,
with the test matrix spanning multiple cladding materials, pin diameters (4.4–12.9 mm), zirconium
contents (2–12%), and smeared densities (70–85%), achieving peak burnups up to 20 at%. To
address concerns that the excellent EBR-II results might not scale to full-length commercial pins,
complementary tests were conducted in the Fast Flux Test Facility (FFTF), a 400 MWt fast-
neutron test reactor at the Hanford Site. The IFR-1 assembly (U-Pu-Zr fuel) reached 10.2 at% peak
burnup, and the MFF series — six full and two partial assemblies of U-10Zr/HT-9 for the FFTF
Series III.b core conversion qualification — all exceeded 10 at% peak burnup, with the lead test
reaching 16% without a breach. Additional information about the available data from these test
can be found in section 2.2.1.

1.2 Overview of Out-of-Pile Furnace Test Program

An extensive out-of-pile furnace test program was conducted at Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility
(AGHCF) at Argonne National Laboratory (ANL) from 1987-1994 to evaluate the fuel/cladding
compatibility and performance of fast reactor metallic fuels. These tests, conducted in furnace
apparatuses built in the AGHCF, overheated irradiated fuel pin segments and some whole pins
from EBR-II to elevated temperatures from 625 °C to 850 °C for durations of a few minutes up to
36 hours. Post-test metallographic examinations were made of the samples to examine the extent
of fuel liquefaction as well as the maximum rate and depth of eutectic penetration into the cladding.
Most of the tests were conducted on ~8mm tall fuel pin segments taken from various locations
along the length of EBR-II pins following their in-pile service. These tests, conducted in the fuel
 1

behavior test apparatus (FBTA), established the time- and temperature-dependence of eutectic
cladding penetration in the tested samples as well as rough threshold conditions for eutectic
liquefaction. Additional information on the FBTA tests can be found in section 2.2.2.

Seven whole-pin furnace (WPF) tests were also conducted on whole, intact irradiated fuel pins to
study synergistic effects of fission gas pressure loading and fuel-cladding interaction. Pressure
transducers at the top of the test section provided means to detect pin breach by measuring the
pressure rise due to fission gas release. The first four WPF tests, FM-1 through FM-4, were run
until pin failure and terminated upon detection of the pin breach. Tests FM-5 through FM-7 ran
for a set duration and the pins remained intact. Additional information on the WPF tests can be
found in section 2.2.2.

Post-test examination (PTE) of the FBTA tests focused primarily on metallography in the form of
microphotography, with occasional SEM/EDX or microhardness measurements taken on samples.
Posttest microphotographs were examined to determine the nature, depth, and extent of cladding
attack. Furnace tests on whole pins in the WPF also included posttest gamma scans, cladding
diameter measurements for computing cladding strain, and neutron radiography.

1.3 Overview of Metallic Fuel Transient Test Program

The M5, M6, and M7 tests, conducted at the Transient Reactor Test (TREAT) facility in 1986–
1987, were the first transient-overpower experiments on modern IFR-type metallic fuel alloys. A
total of six EBR-II-irradiated fuel pins were tested in Mark-IIIC flowing-sodium loops: five were
ternary U-19Pu-10Zr alloy clad in D9 stainless steel with peak burnups ranging from 0.8 to 9.8
at.%, and one was binary U-10Zr clad in HT9 at 2.9 at.% burnup. Each test subjected two pins
simultaneously to an exponential power rise on an 8-second period at prototypical thermal-
hydraulic conditions (full sodium coolant flow, ~630 K inlet temperature), with the transient
terminated at or just before cladding failure—resulting in overpower levels of roughly four times
nominal.

The tests established the cladding failure threshold for IFR metallic fuel and demonstrated two key
safety-favorable behaviors: significant pre-failure axial fuel expansion (providing negative
reactivity feedback before cladding breach) and strongly dispersive post-failure fuel motion away
from the core midplane. The TREAT fast-neutron hodoscope tracked real-time fuel relocation with
~1 g mass and ~3 cm spatial resolution, confirming that fuel driven to failure produced net negative
reactivity worth changes. Post-test destructive examinations revealed limited cladding breach
extents, evidence of fuel melting and fuel-cladding chemical interaction zones, and fission gas
release data from unfailed pins. These results, now organized as benchmark specifications in ANL-
ART-174 [4], continue to serve as the primary validation database for metallic fuel transient
behavior codes.

1.4 Overview of Existing Metallic Fuel Performance Databases

The operations of EBR-II [5] and the Fast Flux Test Facility (FFTF) [6], starting in the early 1960’s
for EBR-II and 1984 for FFTF, provided major domestic knowledge and data for sodium cooled
fast reactor (SFR) fuels, including both metallic and oxide fuels. For metallic fuel, extensive data
were generated during the IFR program [7, 8]. This knowledge base includes general reactor

 2

information, Argonne National Laboratory (ANL) reports, IFR reports, run reports (note: a run is
an EBR-II operating cycle), memos, post irradiation examination (PIE) reports, drawings, design
specifications, experiment qualification reports, publications in journals and conferences,
information regarding measured properties (e.g., IFR metallic fuels handbook [9, 10]) , and
documents related to out of pile experiments. To collect the information, a database: the IFR
Materials Information System (IMIS) [11, 12] was developed at the time, but could not be
completed due to the end of the IFR program. This early program termination limited the
information that was stored in the IMIS database, especially details on the fuel operating conditions,
PIE data, and documents. In addition, a quality assurance program was not implemented on the
data stored in that database. Thus, several metallic fuel databases have been developed as part of
US-DOE Office of Nuclear Energy Advanced Reactor Technology (ART) Fast Reactor Program’s
Fast Reactor Database collection (https://frdb.ne.anl.gov).

Fast reactor metallic fuels databases that compile accurate and detailed operating and performance
parameters are important to industry and different institutions who have shown interest in
developing metallic-fuel-based fast reactors. Those potential end-users have interests related to
establishing a safety case for this type of fuel within a given operational envelope in a commercial
reactor. The database can be used for demonstrating to the licensing authorities that well-
established performance information is available to support safe reactor operations. In addition,
the industry and relevant institutions have interest in developing or using existing fuel performance
codes that are validated with data large body of legacy data to provide best estimate descriptions
of fuel behavior in a reactor.

Argonne’s highest-interest databases capturing fast reactor metallic fuel performance data in
support of fast reactor research and development are Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD)
[1], Out-of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD) [2], and TREAT Experimental Relational Database
(TREXR) [3]:

 • FIPD is an organized collection of EBR-II test pin data and documentation. The database
 includes pin operation conditions calculated using a collection of ANL analysis codes
 developed during the IFR program, including axial distributions for power, temperatures,
 fluences, burnup, and isotopic densities. The database also contains pin measured data from
 post-irradiation examination, including fission gas release and gas chemistry
 measurements, cladding profilometry, gamma scans, and dimensional measurements
 derived from neutron radiography. There is also an extensive collection of documents
 associated with different pins and experiments, including raw PIE data, design descriptions,
 safety analysis, and operational reports.

 • OPTD is an organized, searchable archive of information about out-of-pile furnace
 transient tests conducted on metallic fuel samples at the AGHCF at ANL. Fuel pins or pin
 segments previously irradiated in EBR-II were placed into one of two furnace apparatuses
 and overheated to examine the fuel-cladding compatibility of each sample, margins to
 failure, and failure mechanisms. Records describing the design, planning, and execution of
 the tests are included in the database as well as test summary reports, instrument records,
 and experimenter’s notes. Pre- and post-test examination data is also available, including
 cladding diameter measurements, gamma scans, neutron radiography, and metallography.

 3

 • TREXR is an organized, searchable collection of information that describes the hundreds
 of experiments conducted on nuclear reactor fuels at the TREAT at ANL-West beginning
 in 1960. The experiments generally investigated the response of nuclear fuel samples to
 severe conditions similar to those associated with reactor accidents. The TREAT reactor
 was specifically designed and operated to provide such conditions. Investigation,
 understanding, and predictability of the transient response of nuclear fuels is important in
 supporting the design and licensing of safe nuclear reactors.

1.5 Overview of ANL QAPP and Implementation for Qualification of Legacy Data

Argonne established a Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP) to qualify legacy metallic fuel
data [13]. The QAPP uses the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Standard
NQA-1 (NQA-1) 2008/2009. In some cases, specific provisions and measures have been prepared
to meet some aspects of 10CFR50 Appendix B, “Quality Assurance Criteria for Nuclear Power
Plants and Fuel Reprocessing Plants.” The NRC reviewed the QAPP in June 2021 and determined
it satisfies the quality assurance requirements of Appendix B to Title 10 of the Code of Federal
Regulations (10 CFR) Part 50 [14]. The data qualification process described in the QAPP includes
the following qualification methods:
 - QA Program Equivalency: Determine if the acquisition, processing and archiving of data
 have been performed in accordance with sound technical, administrative practices in
 compliance with NRC requirements and guidance.
 - Peer Review: Independent evaluation of data to determine if the originally employed QA
 methodology is acceptable and confidence is warranted in the data acquisition.
 - Data Corroboration: Determine if subject matter data comparisons can be shown to
 substantiate or confirm parameter values.

An initial consideration of the given methods of qualification led to the conclusion that the peer
review method is the appropriate method for the legacy data qualification effort, based on available
records associated with the data. Thus, the data qualification activities described in this report
follow the peer review method processes to perform the data qualification activities, which involve
a subject matter expert (SME) evaluation of the data. In some cases, elements of data corroboration
or program equivalency are considered during the peer review process, for example, to compare
measurements made by alternative researchers or techniques, or in consideration of applicable
procedures, standards, or equipment calibrations.

 2 DATA QUALIFICATION OVERVIEW

2.1 ANL QAPP for SFR Metallic Fuel Data Qualification

Managing the massive quantity of data in the databases generated during the US IFR program
presents a significant challenge for retaining data integrity and availability. The American Society
of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Standard NQA-1 (NQA-1) 2008/2009 Addenda provides
appropriate requirements to assess data integrity, completeness, and availability. Accordingly,
Argonne established a Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP) for the SFR Metallic Fuel Data
in FIPD using the ASME NQA-1 2008/2009 Addenda standard [13]. This QAPP is the top-level
policy document that establishes the way in which quality is achieved in the SFR legacy fuel data.
Implementing documents prescribe more detailed responsibilities and requirements and define the
organizational interfaces involved within the scope of the QAPP. The Quality Assurance and
Vendor Inspection Branch (QVB) of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) has completed
its review and determined that the Argonne QAPP for SFR Metallic Fuel Data Qualification
satisfies the quality assurance requirements of Appendix B to Title 10 of the Code of Federal
Regulations (10 CFR) Part 50. The implementation of this QAPP is essential to ensure the quality
of data in FIPD, and it will be one of the key efforts in the on-going FIPD development. In addition
to the plan to qualify the data, another plan has been developed to qualify the software associated
with the database. This plan is designated the Nuclear Fuel Codes and Data (NFD) Software
Quality Assurance Program Plan (SQAPP) [15]. The relation between the QAPP and SQAPP for
metallic fuel data qualification can be seen in Figure 1. More details about the SQAPP can be
found in ref. [16].

The current QA program aims at supporting industry efforts for licensing advanced SFR designs
that utilize metallic fuels as well as other stakeholders performing R&D related to metallic fuel,
including DOE programs and universities. Different end users can require different sets of
information. As shown in Figure 1, the industry users will require more detailed documentation,
including all available records of the data. Less detail is needed for other end users such as the
Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS) program for modeling and
simulation or users conducting non-NRC based qualifications and other R&D activities. The
primary data needs of those end users are the PIE data, operating parameters, and fabrication data
for the experiments.

2.3 PIE and PTE Data Flow from Generation to Database

PIE & PTE measurements were originally performed at ANL, ANL-West (Argonne National
Laboratory-West, now the Materials and Fuels Complex of Idaho National Laboratory), and Idaho
National Laboratory (INL). Most of the raw measurement data were printed as hard-copy
documents and, when originated elsewhere, were transmitted to ANL for various analyses. These
hard copy records were later scanned into PDF format and preserved in the database document

 14

libraries. The scanned data were further digitized into numeric data files (typically CSV formatted)
using the principles described in the applicable specifications. The digitized data allows users to
plot and perform in-depth analysis such as fuel performance model validation and benchmarking.

2.3.1 Contact Profilometry Data

The contact profilometry data preserved in FIPD for as-fabricated and as-irradiated pins were
originally generated at HFEF at ANL-West. The data were transmitted to ANL after the
measurements and digitized at ANL. Both hard-copy and digitized data are stored in FIPD.

Contact profilometry measurements for WPF test pins in OPTD were made and recorded in hard
copy at AGHCF at ANL [18]. The cladding diameter profiles were digitized at ANL after scanning
the hard copy records.

Data qualifications were performed at ANL by implementation of the QAPP (see section 4.2 for
details). The qualified data are now available to users of the metallic fuel data via FIPD and OPTD.

2.3.2 Laser Profilometry Data

The laser profilometry data are available only for a limited number of experiments (e.g., X425)
and were originally generated at HFEF at ANL-West. The data were transmitted to ANL after the
measurements and digitized at ANL. Both hard-copy and digitized data are stored in FIPD.

Data qualifications were performed in ANL by implementation of the QAPP (see section 4.3 for
details). The qualified data are available to users of the metallic fuel data via FIPD.

2.3.3 Manual Micrometer Measurements of Cladding Diameter

Some AGHCF measurements of cladding diameter on out-of-pile furnace test pins were conducted
using a manually operated micrometer rather than by continuous contact profilometry [18]. The
manual measurements were recorded by hand in notebooks at ANL and later transcribed digitally.
Qualified data are available to users via OPTD.

2.3.4 NRAD Data

The NRAD data/images were originally generated at HFEF at ANL-West. The data were scanned
into high-resolution digital images and stored in JPG or TIFF formats. The images were then
transmitted to ANL and are stored in FIPD and OPTD. Based on the images, two types of
measurements were performed: (1) axial fuel length measurements; and (2) fuel diameter
measurements (only applied to X423 experiment). The axial fuel length measurements on NRAD
images were performed at INL [19]. The measured data were transmitted to ANL and are stored
in FIPD. The diameter measurements on NRAD images were performed at both INL and ANL
(see section 4.4 for more details). The measured fuel diameters are stored in FIPD.

 15

Qualifications of the NRAD data (including the measured fuel length and diameters) were
performed at ANL by implementation of the QAPP (see section 4.4 for details). The qualified data
are now available to users of the metallic fuel data via FIPD and OPTD.

2.3.5 Isotopic Gamma Scan Data

The isotopic gamma scan data were originally generated at HFEF at ANL-West and AGHCF (for
out-of-pile test pins). Data originating at HFEF at ANL-West were transmitted to ANL after the
measurements and were digitized at ANL. Both hard-copy and digitized data are stored in FIPD.

Data qualifications of as-irradiated and post-transient-test gamma scans were performed at ANL
by implementation of the QAPP (see section 4.5 for details). Qualified data are available to users
via FIPD.

2.3.6 Fission Gas Data

The fission gas release and chemistry data were originally generated at ANL-West. and AGHCF
(for out-of-pile test pins). The data were transmitted to ANL after the measurements and digitized
at ANL. Both hard-copy and digitized data are stored in FIPD.

Data qualifications were performed in ANL by implementation of the QAPP (see sections 4.6 and
4.7 for details). The qualified data are available to users of the metallic fuel data via FIPD.

2.3.7 Metallography Data

Metallography data was collected at HFEF at ANL-West and at the AGHCF at ANL and at INL.
Photo prints of the microphotography images were kept in binders on site. Some negatives were
also archived. Available prints have been scanned into PDF and high-resolution TIF format and
preserved in the FIPD and OPTD libraries. SMEs have checked magnification of metallography
images collected at AGHCF and added scale bars to the digital image files; more detail can be
found in Section 2.5.7. Qualified metallography data are now available in TREXR and OPTD.

Post-transient data in OPTD also includes quantitative estimates of eutectic penetration depth into
cladding and fraction of liquid-containing phase in the fuel. The estimates were made by original
experimenters manually measuring contrast features on the printed metallography images. The
area fraction of liquid-containing phase was independently measured by SMEs using modern
digital image analysis software and evaluated for concurrence with original estimates to qualify
them alongside the metallography images.

2.4 Procedures and Forms

The qualification of data under the QAPP is governed by the Data Qualification Methods
procedure (NFD-PRO-5.3-01). This procedure defines the scope, responsibilities, work process,
and records created as a result of the work process. The primary output of the Data Qualification
Methods procedure is a QA record based on the completion of the Data Qualification Report form
(NFD-FRM-5.3-01). In general, one data qualification report is created for each unique
combination of data type and experimental subassembly. For example, neutron radiography for

 16

experiment X425, which consisted of four subassembly loadings (X425, X425A, X425B, and
X425C), would produce four data qualification reports. Reports are generated in this manner based
on the calculation and PIE measurement procedures, which are typically done per subassembly,
though this is not always the case. For example, in experiments that contain both binary (U-Zr)
and ternary (U-Pu-Zr) fuel pins, PIE or analysis would sometimes be performed separately for
each fuel type. Revision 2 of this procedure and form are provided in Appendix C-1.

2.5.1 HFEF Contact Profilometry

The specification of EBR-II Contact Profilometry Data (ANL/CFCT-22/20) [20] was developed
for interpreting and qualifying contact profilometry data of EBR-II irradiated fuel pins stored in
the FIPD. Contact profilometry measures the outer diameter profile of fuel pin cladding along the
axial length by passing the pin between two opposing contact rollers. The measurement provides
a continuous trace of cladding diameter as a function of axial position, which is critical for
assessing fuel-cladding mechanical interaction, cladding strain, and fuel swelling.

The report covers two types of contact profilometry data recording systems. The first is a strip-
chart recorder system, where the profilometry trace was recorded on paper chart rolls. These
records were later scanned and stored as PDF images in FIPD, and the plotted data has been
digitized for numerical analysis. The second system used a COMPRO computer to digitally record
the measurements, with data printed out and later scanned into PDF format. For the computer-
recorded data, some original digital files are still available in CSV format.

Each contact profilometry data image contains four key sections: an information line identifying
the subassembly and element, the profilometry trace showing cladding diameter variation, the axial
position readout in inches, and the fuel pin diameter scale. The Y-axis directly reports cladding
diametral values with a measurement uncertainty of less than ±0.0002 inches. The X-axis
represents fuel pin axial position, with the known error source being ±0.25% of span from the
recorder. The report also describes how to determine the bottom of the fuel slug position from the
profilometry trace by identifying characteristic blips caused by the contact rollers traversing the
cladding tube-to-plug weld area.

2.5.2 HFEF Laser Profilometry

The specification of EBR-II Laser Profilometry Data (ANL/CFCT-22/21) [21] was developed
for interpreting and qualifying laser profilometry data from irradiated EBR-II fuel elements
stored in FIPD. All laser profilometry data was originally measured using the element laser
profilometer system in HFEF. Data acquisition and instrument operation were performed
automatically via the HFEF Data Acquisition and Process Control System (DAPCS), and results
were recorded in digital form on magnetic tapes. Since the raw digital data on magnetic tapes
was not recoverable after more than 30 years, the available data in FIPD is based on hard-copy
plots that were printed at the time, scanned, and saved in PDF format. Most of these hard-copy
images have been digitized using the Fast Reactor Data Digitization System (FRDDS) and stored
in CSV format.

Each laser profilometry data record sheet contains: (a) title and relevant information including the
capsule/item element number, profile date, and data plotting date; (b) measurement rotation angle
in degrees; (c) the profilometry trace; (d) a Y-axis scale representing pin diameter in inches; and
(e) an X-axis scale representing fuel pin axial position in inches. Measurements were typically
performed at multiple rotation angles per fuel pin, with a rotation accuracy of ±1.0 degrees. Some
subassemblies include an additional dataset showing the average of all rotations.

The measurement accuracy of the laser profilometer system changed over time due to aging of
mechanical components. For the Y-axis (diameter), the accuracy was ±0.0001 inches before
 18

12/01/1986, +/- 0.0002 inches from 12/01/1986 to 01/04/1990, and ±0.0003 inches after
02/05/1990 (all for specimens up to 0.3 inches in diameter; slightly larger uncertainties applied to
larger specimens). The X-axis (axial position) uncertainty is ±0.003 inches. The bottom of the fuel
slug position is determined by identifying a sudden large blip in the trace (caused by the laser
hitting the reduced area in the lower end plug) and adding the known distance from the weld area
to the top of the end plug. The data recording direction for most records is "POSITION FROM
TOP OF ELEMENT," though exceptions exist for some subassemblies (e.g., X421A) where the
data was recorded in an inverted direction. The report documents laser profilometry data
availability across multiple subassemblies (X419B, X420A, X420B, X421, X421A, X425, X429,
X429B, X431, X435, X441, X441A, X447, and X447A) with profile dates spanning from 1987 to
1991.

2.5.3 Neutron Radiography

The specifications of EBR-II Neutron Radiography Method Description and Digitization
Approach (ANL/CFCT-22/22) [22] were developed for interpreting and qualifying neutron
radiography (NRAD) data of post-irradiated and post-tests fuel pins. Neutron radiography was
performed at the Neutron Radiography Facility (NRAD) using a TRIGA reactor as the neutron
source. The technique produces transmission images of fuel pins by exposing them to a thermal
neutron beam and recording the attenuation pattern on imaging media, enabling non-destructive
examination of internal fuel structures.

The report describes two generations of imaging systems used at NRAD. The original system
employed dysprosium or indium foils as converter screens with industrial X-ray film, producing
high-resolution images that were later digitized from the original films. The newer direct imaging
system, implemented around 2010, uses gadolinium oxysulfide scintillator screens coupled to a
digital camera, producing images directly in digital format. The NRAD images in FIPD span both
eras, with the legacy film-based images forming the majority of the dataset.

Key specifications are provided for interpreting the images, including the geometric unsharpness
of the imaging system, the relationship between image density and fuel characteristics, and the
methods for extracting quantitative measurements. The report details how fuel slug diameter and
axial fuel length can be measured from NRAD images, with fuel column length uncertainty
estimated at approximately ±0.04 inches. The digitization approach for legacy film images is also
described, including scanning resolution, image format, and the conversion of analog film density
to digital pixel values for quantitative analysis.

2.5.4 Gamma Scan

The specification of EBR-II Gamma Scan Data from the Precision Gamma Scanner
(ANL/CFCT-22/19) [23] was developed for interpreting and qualifying gamma scan data
collected by the Precision Gamma Scanner (PGS) at the HFEF. The gamma scan data, stored in
FIPD, was acquired from 1985 to 1993 using the PGS-II instrument. The PGS was originally
manufactured in 1971 and has undergone two major upgrades—to PGS-II (before 1984) and
PGS-III (1996)—with improvements to the detector, collimator slit, positioning system, and
control electronics over time.

 19

The PGS system consists of a germanium gamma ray detector, a variable-slit collimator that
isolates the target region, and a computer-controlled positioning stage capable of translating a fuel
pin along multiple axes. Data is acquired point-by-point: the sample is moved to a desired position,
gamma counts are accumulated for a fixed live time, and the process repeats across the scan length.
The scanner can operate in either axial mode (along the fuel rod) or horizontal mode (across it),
recording either gross gamma activity or isotope-specific counts at characteristic photon energies.
The original data was recorded in two distinct plot formats depending on the time period: one used
from August 1985 through November 1987, and an updated format used thereafter.

Each gamma scan data record sheet contains a header with metadata (scan type, sample designation,
subassembly, scan date, live time, slit width, and isotope/energy), a y-axis reporting measured
counts per second, an x-axis showing position in inches, and the data trace plot itself. The gamma
spectrum data is primarily intended for qualitative analysis, including studies of cladding failure,
fuel melting, phase changes, and fission product migration. It can also be used for irradiated fuel
slug length determination.

The specification also documents the accuracy of the key instrument parameters across its three
generations. The slit width accuracy is consistently ±0.001 inches for all versions. Positioning
accuracy along the z-axis improved from ±0.01 inches (PGS and PGS-II) to ±0.005 inches (PGS-
III), while x-axis accuracy remained at ±0.003 inches throughout.

2.5.5 Fission Gas Release

The specification of FIPD Fission Gas Release Data (ANL/CFCT-23/15) [24] was developed for
interpreting and qualifying fission gas release data stored in FIPD. The fission gas data was
collected using the Gas Assay, Sample and Recharge (GASR) system at the HFEF. During
measurement, a laser punctures a pinhole in the fuel element plenum, releasing fission gas into a
calibrated volume where the pressure rise is recorded. Helium backfilling and expansion is then
performed to determine the plenum volume using Boyle's Law under the assumption of ideal gas
behavior.

The report details the GASR measurement process, including pre-puncture calibration of in-cell
pressure gauges, the laser puncture run, and the helium backfilling and expansion procedure. Three
methods for calculating plenum volume and pressure from the raw data are described: the
simultaneous equation method (a simple reference calculation), the algebraic substitution method
(the default method since 1985, used for the majority of FIPD data), and the slope method
(developed around 1990, which uses linear regression of multiple pressure measurements for
improved precision). Uncertainties for all methods are reported as 2 sigma standard deviations,
and the GASR system verification has demonstrated accuracy of ±1% for number of moles and
±5% for volume and pressure for volumes greater than 0.4 cubic inches.

The specification also addresses the validity of the ideal gas law assumption. While the assumption
holds well for helium backfilling at low pressures, the fission gas (primarily krypton and xenon)
at operating conditions may deviate from ideal behavior. A compressibility factor correction is
provided, showing that the pressure reported in FIPD assuming ideal gas behavior can be up to 15%
higher than the corrected value near 500 psi. The fission gas release data for each fuel element

 20

includes the calculated plenum volume, pressure, cladding temperature during measurement, and
associated uncertainties.

2.5.6 Fission Gas Chemistry

The specification of FIPD Fission Gas Chemistry Data (ANL/CFCT-23/16) [25] was developed
for interpreting and qualifying fission gas chemistry data stored in FIPD. The data encompasses
two main sets: data collected during the IFR program from 1984 to 1994, analyzed by gas mass
spectrometry at the Analytical Laboratory (AL) at Argonne National Laboratory-West, and data
collected at INL after the IFR program, analyzed at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory
(PNNL). Many fuel pins irradiated in EBR-II and the FFTF were measured.

The fission gas was sampled by the GASR system at HFEF and transferred to either AL or PNNL
for mass spectrometric analysis. The typical data package includes gas composition (mole
percentages of hydrogen, helium, nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide, krypton, and xenon)
and isotopic analysis of xenon and krypton expressed in atom percentages. During the IFR era, the
analysis was performed using a Varian MAT CH7 and/or a Nuclide Corporation 6-60 isotope ratio
mass spectrometer at AL. In the post-IFR period, PNNL used a Finnigan MAT-271 quantitative
mass spectrometer.

The specification documents data formats across different time periods, noting that the format
evolved from combined multi-pin tables (pre-1989) to standardized single-table formats (1989-
1991) and individual "Analytical Sample Record" sheets (post-1992). Data uncertainty is specified
as ±5% for measurements conducted under the Mass Spectrometry Measurement Control Plan.
For data reported prior to 1989 with uncertainties below 5%, a conservative ±5% error should be
applied. The post-IFR data from PNNL reports uncertainties as 2% for minor gas constituents and
uses root-sum-of-squares for major constituents.

2.5.7 Metallography

Metallography data represents one of the most important types of post-irradiation and post-test
examination data for understanding fuel microstructure. The databases contain three main sets:
metallography from EBR-II and FFTF irradiated pins examined at HFEF ANL-W and INL,
metallography from EBR-II irradiated pins examined at the Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility
(AGHCF), and metallography from transient-tested fuel pins also examined at AGHCF. Three
reports have been generated to develop a comprehensive guidance for interpreting and qualifying
legacy metallography data of U(Pu)Zr metallic fuel pins.

The first report titled “Magnification Determination for AGHCF Metallographic Images”
(ANL/NSE-23/90) [26], describes methods for checking and determining the magnification of
metallographic images generated at the AGHCF. Individual high-magnification micrographs (50X
to 500X) were recorded as Polaroid photographs labeled with AGHCF identification number,
magnification, and surface condition, while low-magnification composite images (photomosaics,
approximately 25X to 100X) were constructed by pasting together higher-magnification images
and re-photographing the assembly. Most individual images lack scale bars, and only a few
composite images contain them. The metallographic images examined in this work were generated

 21

during the 1989 to 1993 timeframe for cross sections of U-10Zr/HT9 fuel rods irradiated in EBR-
II and for samples following out-of-pile Fuel Behavior Test Apparatus (FBTA) tests.

The report details the equipment and procedures used at AGHCF, including the Leitz MM-5RT
Research Metallograph (purchased in 1967) and the Bausch and Lomb (B&L) Metallograph.
Magnification calibration and verification were performed using a microslide standard with
parallel lines at 1 mm, 0.1 mm, and 0.01 mm spacings. Routine magnification checks were
conducted every two months prior to 1989, and semi-annually thereafter. The report provides
Excel-determined magnifications compared to values recorded in the FBTA Notebook, showing
that the Leitz 50X and 150X settings produced images at their nominal magnification, the Leitz
250X setting generated images at approximately 260X, and the 500X settings produced images at
approximately 520X (Leitz) and 510X (B&L).

The second report titled “Specifications of Legacy U(Pu)Zr Metallography Data” (ANL-ART-296)
[27], describes sample preparation procedures including sectioning, epoxy mounting, mechanical
grinding, polishing, and etching. Metallographic examination was performed using Leitz MM-
5RT and Bausch and Lomb metallographs operating at magnifications from 20X to 800X in inert
atmosphere containment boxes. The AGHCF specification covers the sample naming system (A/G
number format), cutting diagrams, sectioning schemes, hard-copy records (positive photos and
negative films), and digitized records. Hard-copy data were digitized using an Epson Perfection
V850 Pro scanner at 600 DPI for positive photographs and 2400 DPI for negative films, saved in
TIFF format. Aggregated images for each test pin or test segment were also saved in PDF format.

The specification details the information tag present on each metallography image, which records
fields such as MCT/MST number, identification number, A/G number, magnification, etchant
condition, date, and remarks about the photograph location. For HFEF legacy metallography, only
a limited number of datasets have been recovered to date. The report also covers photomosaic
(composite) images, which are large-area views assembled from multiple individual photographs,
and the scale bar and magnification verification procedures that were routinely performed on the
microscopes.

The third report titled “Determination of Scale Bar for AGHCF Metallography Data”
(ANL/CFCT-25/37) [28] provides guidance for determining scale bars for recently digitized high-
resolution metallography images of U(Pu)Zr fuel pins from AGHCF. Many legacy metallography
images record magnification information but lack physical scale bars, which are essential for
quantitative microstructural analysis such as measuring grain sizes, porosity, fuel-cladding
chemical interaction (FCCI) layer thicknesses, and bubble distributions. The analysis focuses on
fuel pin DP11 (U-10Zr/HT9) from EBR-II subassembly X447A as a representative case.

For high-magnification photographs (250X and 500X), pixel sizes are calculated directly from the
known magnification and scanning resolution (600 DPI), yielding 0.1693 micrometers/pixel at
250X and 0.0847 micrometers/pixel at 500X. These calculated scale bars were validated by
measuring cladding thickness from the images and comparing to the nominal value of 381 ± 13
micrometers, showing agreement within 2-5%. Magnification uncertainties were estimated by
comparing instrument settings to verified magnification values from calibration records, yielding
conservative uncertainties of 4.4% for 250X and 4.0% for 500X images.

 22

For composite (photomosaic) images, where the original magnification cannot be directly used
due to the multi-step assembly and re-photographing process, pixel sizes are estimated using
physical reference features. The report evaluates three reference methods: nominal cladding
thickness, measured cladding outer diameter, and characteristic microstructural features identified
in both high-magnification images and composites. The microstructural feature-based method is
recommended as the most reliable approach for radial strip composites, as it directly links
composite pixel sizes to the calibrated high-magnification images. The report provides systematic
procedures for determining scale bars for four types of composites: radial strip, axial strip, whole
radial cross-section, and axial cross-section composites.

2.5.8 AGHCF Cladding Diameter

The specification of Cladding Diameter Measurements Conducted at AGHCF (ANL-ART-292)
[18] was developed for interpreting and qualifying fuel element cladding diameter measurement
data collected at AGHCF before and after out-of-pile furnace transient tests. The data records
have been collected and preserved in the Out-of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD) and are used to
determine transient-induced changes in fuel element diameter, or cladding strain, for the Whole
Pin Furnace (WPF) tests (FM-1 through FM-7). The specification was developed using the
AGHCF Operations Manual (Doc. No. IPS-2-00-00, dated June 1989) and recovered
measurement records in consultation with subject matter experts.

Fuel element diameter was measured by two methods: contact profilometry and manual
micrometer. Contact profilometry was conducted using a lab-built rig employing a continuously
moving linear variable differential transducer (LVDT) operating against a fixed sapphire anvil,
recording continuous diameter traces along the fuel pin length at specified rotational orientations.
The data was originally recorded on paper strip charts, with the x-axis representing axial position
in inches and the y-axis representing diameter in mils, zeroed to the standard as-manufactured
cladding diameter of 0.230 or 0.290 inch (230 or 290 mils). Between August 1991 and April 1992,
an updated automated profilometer was introduced that recorded data digitally. Manual
micrometer measurements were taken at discrete axial locations (generally every ~3 inches) using
a one-inch spindle type micrometer with 0.001-inch vernier division, read through a transit
telescope from outside the hot cell through shielding solution and glass.

Each profilometry record consists of a strip chart trace, an accompanying record sheet (containing
Alpha-Gamma ID, subassembly/pin ID, reference orientation, scan angles, calibration standard,
and strip chart scale), and a calibration check record. The calibration check establishes the y-axis
scale of the diameter trace and verifies LVDT linearity using a certified thickness shim (generally
5 mils). Calibration standards for the WPF tests were drill rods within 0.003 inches of the outer
cladding design diameters. The estimated accuracy of the lab-built profilometer is ± 0.003 inches,
and the profilometer zero shift was verified to be less than 0.002 inches. Micrometer accuracy is
expected to be within 0.0005 inches (half the vernier division), though environmental limitations
such as cladding ovality and viewing through shielding solution may affect results.

The availability of diameter data varies by test: pretest and posttest profilometry and/or micrometer
data exist for tests FM-1 through FM-5, while only computed strain values (from presentations)

 23

are available for FM-6 and FM-7 since primary measurement data was not recovered. Digitized
cladding diameter data for each test is stored in CSV files with the format
"testname_claddiam_time", containing columns for series label, axial position (x), and measured
outer diameter or strain (y). The data was digitized with profilometry strip chart scans converted
to point data and micrometer measurements manually transcribed into digital tables.

2.5.9 As-built Fabrication Data

For as-built data, the specification of EBR-II fabricated fuel diameter data (ANL/CFCT-23/45)
[29] has been developed to interpret and qualifying fabricated fuel slug diameter data stored in
FIPD. The fabricated fuel slug diameter is a key parameter for understanding fuel swelling
behavior in EBR-II metallic fuel pins, as it serves as the pre-irradiation baseline against which
post-irradiation diameter measurements are compared. However, direct records of fabricated fuel
slug diameters are not uniformly available across all subassemblies in the database.

Three methods are described for recovering fabricated fuel slug diameter values. The first and
most direct method involves extracting diameters from existing fabrication records,
characterization reports, or fuel alloy summary sheets where the diameter was explicitly reported.
The second method uses a correlation between fuel slug length and diameter, exploiting the fact
that fuel slugs were injection cast to a target weight and alloy composition, making the slug length
inversely related to the cross-sectional area and therefore the diameter. The third method relies on
batch and identification number correlations, where the fabricated diameter of a fuel slug can be
inferred from slugs known to come from the same casting batch.

The report documents the data formats, sources, and uncertainties associated with each method.
The fuel slug diameter is typically reported in inches, with casting mold inner diameters ranging
from 0.1405 inches to 0.173 inches depending on the experiment. The specification notes that
some slugs were centerless ground after casting, which removed material and reduced the diameter
from the as-cast value. The length-diameter correlation method is validated using data from
multiple subassemblies and shows good agreement with directly reported values.

Most other fabrication data besides fuel slug diameter is reported in a more straightforward way,
and the digitization is generally more straightforward. They are reported in different formats and
different layouts, but generally only require some basic correlation between pin labels, batch labels,
slug labels, etc. to compile the data up correctly. These correlations are documented and included
as part of the data fabricated data package.

2.5.10 Digitized Data

Most of the legacy data—including fabrication, post-irradiation examination, and post-test
examination records—exist in hard-copy formats (scanned PDF files originally derived from paper
documents). The majority of these data have now been processed and digitized into a structured
digital format (CSV). A report titled “Qualification of Digitized Legacy Fast Reactor Data (ANL-
ART-295) [30]” has been developed to provide the methodology for qualifying digitized legacy
fast reactor fuel performance data. The qualification of scanned records does not automatically

 24

extend to digitized data files derived from them; the digitized data must undergo a separate peer
review process.

The report describes two categories of data digitization. For typed or hand-recorded tabular data
(such as fission gas release and chemistry data, fuel slug diameters, and cladding measurements),
values are directly transcribed from hard copy to digital format (typically CSV files) and verified
by a separate reviewer against the original record. For plotted data (such as gamma scans, contact
profilometry, and laser profilometry traces), digitization is performed using the Fast Reactor Data
Digitization System (FRDDS), a custom MATLAB-based tool that converts scanned plot images
into point data.

An uncertainty analysis was conducted using 100 repeated digitizations of a contact profilometry
record, revealing measurement uncertainties of approximately ±0.1 inch in the x-axis (axial
position) and ±0.02 mil in the y-axis (cladding diameter), which are negligible relative to the fuel
slug length (~14 inches) and pin diameter (~231.5 mil). The final digitized data file must be
approved by a separate reviewer through a data qualification report (DQR) using the "peer review
method" before it can be designated as qualified data.

3.1 Calculations Flow

For each experiment listed in Table 1, the operating parameters were calculated by Argonne’s
physics and thermal hydraulics codes which have been used in the past in relation to EBR-II
simulation. Those codes include REBUS [31], RCT [32], and a version of SUPERENERGY-II
(SE2) code [33, 34]. REBUS produces whole core neutronics and performs depletion calculations,
providing information for subassembly power, burnup, and nuclide densities for three axial regions
in each of the core and blanket subassemblies. REBUS assumes that the fuel has experienced 6%
axial growth after irradiation. RCT uses these results and performs an intra-assembly
reconstruction, providing pin-wise power, flux, burnup, and nuclide densities for the 3 axial
regions. SE2P (coupled SE2+RCTP) receives the RCT output data files and are then used by RCTP
to produce axial interpolations of the pin power, flux, burnup, and nuclide densities [35, 36]. The
generated axial power profiles are then used by SE2 to produce detailed calculations of coolant
temperatures surrounding each pin in the subassembly. These results are then averaged to provide
a single axial coolant temperature profile for each pin and are used to calculate axial temperature
profiles at radial points of interest within the cladding and fuel. SE2P also calculates the cycle
DPA based on the given flux using multigroup damage cross-sections for the cladding constituents.
This workflow is shown in Figure 3.

The operating conditions dataset in FIPD is based largely on a collection of legacy datasets from

calculations from REBUS, and pin-wise reconstructions from RCT, as shown by the dark grey
shaded region in Figure 3. This part of the pipeline is not currently being rerun, and these files are
generally taken as legacy data. The final SE2P part of the pipeline was added in more recent years.
Legacy sets of binary RCT output files for each experiment irradiated in EBR-II during the IFR
program were available by the end of the IFR program. The SE2 input files were originally
manually assembled for these RCT output files and then used to calculate the detailed operating
parameters for the IFR experimental pins. However, through multiple QA efforts several issues

available in FIPD.

3.3 Operating Data Version Control

Much of the past work on the calculated operating data was done in an ad-hoc fashion. This
presented quality assurance issues in terms of consistency and reproducibility. To resolve these
issues, it was decided to consolidate all materials into version-controlled repositories.

Recently, a GitLab [39] instance for hosting of Git repositories was deployed at Argonne and it
was chosen as the hosting solution for this purpose. The GitLab platform offers a variety of benefits.
Being based on the Git version control system [40], the repositories enjoy features like cheap local
branching, convenient staging areas, and support for multiple workflows. The GitLab platform
extends Git’s source management to a centralized DevOps platform, allowing for convenient
collaboration between developers, issue tracking, security, and CI/CD. GitLab can be hosted in the
cloud but can also be deployed locally. The Argonne GitLab instance is deployed locally at
Argonne and managed by the directorate IT services, secured behind the Argonne VPN, requires
two-factor authentication, and is approved for storage of sensitive information. The functionality
in the GitLab platform also helps satisfy the requirements of the SQAPP relating to software
configuration management, including configuration identification, configuration change control,
and configuration status control.

To encapsulate data and better facilitate collaboration, the calculated operating data and related
processing scripts were split up into several repositories and linked together with submodule
relationships. The PIE data and QA document repository were also migrated from their previous
locations into GitLab repositories so that all FIPD-related content could be stored and managed
together. These repositories are shown in Figure 6.

3.4 Physics Software QA Evaluation

The initial software quality assurance evaluation for the relevant physics codes, REBUS, RCT,
and SE2P (also referred to as SE2RCT) is covered in [41]. To briefly summarize these codes,
REBUS produce whole core neutronics and perform depletion calculations, providing information
for subassembly power, burnup, and nuclide densities. The REBUS output datasets currently being
used in FIPD are considered legacy results from calculations performed in the past. REBUS is not
currently being run/rerun to verify these results. RCT uses these results and produces an intra-
assembly reconstruction, providing pin-wise power, flux, and nuclide densities, at 3 axial points.
Version v11.0-r3161-d2021-01-14 was used to regenerate the RCT files after corrections were
made as previously discussed. SE2P (SE2+RCTP) then takes these results, where RCTP is used to
produce axial interpolations of the pin power, flux, and nuclide densities. The generated axial
power profiles are then used by SUPERENERGY-II to produce detailed calculations of coolant,
cladding, and fuel temperatures for each pin in the subassembly.

unfailed test pins was < 1%.

The QA process for all available pre-test and post-test cladding diameter measurement data on
out-of-pile furnace tested pins is complete and was recorded for each test on a Data Qualification
Report form (NFD-FRM-5.3-01). An example for test FM-1 (T-418, X425 tested at 820 °C) is
shown in Appendix A-9.

 5 SUMMARY
This technical report summarizes the relevant quality assurance information available for IFR
experiments currently stored in the FIPD and OPTD. Background describing metallic fuel data
generated during the IFR program, including as-built, PIE, operating parameters, out-of-pile
experiment data and other relevant information were presented. The FIPD and OPTD databases
built on such data were introduced. To manage the data and associated documents, a quality
assurance program plan for the metallic fuel data was established using the ASME NQA-1
2008/2009 Addenda standard. After review by the NRC, the plan was determined to satisfy the
quality assurance requirements of Appendix B to Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations (10
CFR) Part 50. The quality assurance of software associated with the metallic data is guided by a
separate software quality assurance program plan. By implementing both plans, the metallic fuel
data, including the operating parameters and PIE data are being qualified. The available data for
the prioritized experiments are summarized in this report. The specifications for post-irradiation
and post-test data, as-built data and digitized data, which are the foundation for metallic fuel legacy
data qualification, are presented in this report. Implementation of the quality assurance program
plan to qualify these data was demonstrated by presenting examples to qualify each type of data.

APPENDIX B-1: NFD-PRO-5.3-01 REV. 3 – DATA QUALIFICATION METHODS

C-1.1 Purpose

Establish the process for qualification of existing data, including data of indeterminate quality, for use in activities under the control of
both the Quality Assurance Program Plan for SFR Metallic Fuel Data Qualification, ANL/NE-16/17 and the Software Quality Assurance
Program Plan (SQAPP) for Nuclear Fuel Codes and Data (NFD), ANL/NE-17/7.

C-1.2 Scope

C-1.2.1 Applicability

The Argonne Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP) allows issuance of separate QA programs and procedures so long as they are
compatible in intent and do not compromise the Argonne QAPP. Accordingly, this procedure is implemented under both the QAPP for
SFR Metallic Fuel Data Qualification, ANL/NE-16/17 and the SQAPP for NFD, ANL/NE-17/7.
C-1.2.2 Content

This procedure prescribes measures for data qualification planning, evaluating and establishing data quality under controlled methods,
and documenting the results.
C-1.2.3 Responsibilities

C-1.2.3.1 Subject Matter Expert (SME)
 • Performs data qualification by either Quality Assurance Program Equivalency, Data Corroboration, or Peer Review as assigned
 by the FDQ Manager.
 • Prepares the data qualification report for the assigned methods.
C-1.2.3.2 Project Quality Assurance Representative (Project QAR)
 • Oversees data qualification by either Quality Assurance Program Equivalency, Data Corroboration, or Peer Review as assigned
 by the FDQ Manager.
 • Prepares the data qualification report for the assigned method.
C-1.2.3.3 Software Integration Manager (SIM)
 • Reviews the data qualification report for consistency with respect to NFD configuration management.
 • Recommends the disposition of the data based on the qualification results.

 105

C-1.2.3.4 Fuel Development and Qualification Manager (FDQ Manager)
 • Designates the disposition of the data and approves the data qualification report.
