A Note on the Perturbative Expansion of the Schwinger Model on S 2 S^{2}
Joseph Smith

Abstract
The Schwinger model is perhaps the simplest non-trivial exactly-solvable QFT. In this note we examine the perturbative structure of the theory on the sphere and show that its quantum corrections match those predicted by the expansion of the exact solution.

The focus of this note will be the structure of perturbation theory for the Schwinger model on
S^{2}

However, if we are interested in computing quantum corrections in theories where an exact solution is not available then this viewpoint is flipped: as we have a prediction for the computation of any observable in the theory we can use the Schwinger model as a testing ground for perturbative methods on

In the spirit of experimentation we shall calculate both using two methods. The first employs the position-space representations of propagators and the interaction vertex in stereographic coordinates on the sphere. This has the benefit of having a similar structure to position-space perturbation theory in flat space; however, the resulting integrals we shall find are complicated, and we must be content with a numerical evaluation. The second method is an expansion in angular momentum eigenstates. The difficulty here lies in the structure of the interaction vertex, which is determined by an integral over three of the relevant eigenfunctions. Once this has been ascertained, though, the sums that must be computed for each diagram are relatively simple and can (to an extent) be done explicitly.

The Schwinger Model on
S^{2}
: A Brief Review

The Schwinger model consists of a two-dimensional
U(1)
gauge field coupled to a massless Dirac fermion, with the action

\displaystyle S[A,\bar{\psi},\psi]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\left(\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}+iqA_{\mu}\right)\psi\right)\ .

(1)

Our fermion conventions are given in appendix
A

S^{2}

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}d\mathbf{x}\cdot d\mathbf{x}\ ,

(2a)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{2R^{2}}{R^{2}+\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\ .

(2b)

In order to work with (

d*A=0\ ,

(3)

for which we locally have the solution

A=R*d\beta\ ,

(4)

in terms of a prepotential
\beta
, where the factor of
R
is inserted to keep
\beta

3

\displaystyle F\wedge*F

\displaystyle=R^{2}\left(d*d\beta\right)\wedge\left(*d*d\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{S^{2}}R^{2}\left(\nabla^{2}\beta\right)^{2}

when written in terms of
\beta

S[\beta,\bar{\psi},\psi]=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla^{2}\beta\right)^{2}+\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi+iqR\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}g^{\nu\rho}\partial_{\rho}\beta\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)\ .

(6)

As we shall be interested in performing perturbative calculations using the action (
6

\beta

G(x,y)=\langle\beta(x)\beta(y)\rangle_{0}\ ,

(7)

\nabla^{4}_{x}G(x,y)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta^{(2)}(x-y)}{\sqrt{g_{x}}}-\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}}\right)\ ,

(8)

where the constant offset to the delta function cancels the zero-mode piece in its spherical harmonic expansion. This is most easily solved by expanding both sides in spherical harmonics (for which our conventions are given in appendix
A

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(x)\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(y)}{l^{2}(l+1)^{2}}+c_{0}\ ,

(9)

c_{0}

\beta

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(1-\frac{u(x,y)}{2}\right)+1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\ ,

(10)

where
u(x,y)
is the
SO(3)

u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\frac{2R^{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}{(R^{2}+\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{x})(R^{2}+\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{y})}\ .

(11)

This function is finite as we take the two points to coincide, with the value

G(x,x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\ .

(12)

Next, let us consider the fermion’s propagator. This is simplified by making the field reparameterisation

\psi=\Omega^{-1/2}\chi\ ,

(13)

which transforms the curved-space fermion’s action into that of a free massless fermion on the flat two-dimensional plane,

\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi=\int d^{2}x\,\bar{\chi}\sigma^{a}\partial_{a}\chi\ .

(14)

The two-point function of
\psi

\displaystyle S(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\langle\psi(\mathbf{x})\bar{\psi}(\mathbf{y})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega(\mathbf{x})\Omega(\mathbf{y})}}\langle\chi(\mathbf{x})\bar{\chi}(\mathbf{y})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\Omega(\mathbf{x})\Omega(\mathbf{y})}}\frac{\sigma^{a}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})^{a}}{(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}\ ,

(15)

where we’ve used the well-known propagator for a flat free-fermion in two-dimensions. When needed we will use the notation

S(x,y)=\frac{S_{0}(x,y)}{\sqrt{\Omega(x)\Omega(y)}}

(16)

to isolate the non-flat contribution to the propagator.

Perturbation Theory in Stereographic Coordinates

3.1
Two-Loop Partition Function and Regularisation

The first object we will be interested in computing is the first quantum correction to the sphere partition function, defined by

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{DAD\bar{\psi}D\psi}{\text{vol}(\mathcal{G})}\,e^{-S[A,\bar{\psi},\psi]}\ ,

(29)

where
\text{vol}(\mathcal{G})
is the volume of the gauge group, or, after gauge-fixing using (
3
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits D\beta D\bar{\psi}D\psi\,e^{-S[\beta,\bar{\psi},\psi]}\ .

(30)

This was computed exactly in
5
; one can then expand it perturbatively in
qR
, where we find the first correction

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{q}}{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\right)\bigg\rvert_{q^{2}R^{2}}=\frac{q^{2}R^{2}\left(2\ln\epsilon+1-2\gamma\right)}{2\pi}+O(\epsilon)\ .

(31)

Here
\epsilon\sim\left(\Lambda_{UV}R\right)^{-1}
is a UV cut-off. The finite terms in this expression can be altered by a rescaling of the cut-off, so have no physical meaning; we will therefore focus on recovering the coefficient of the logarithm.

We would like to recover this by directly performing an asymptotic expansion of (
30

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}\sim\mathcal{Z}_{0}\,{\sum}_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-iqR)^{n}}{n!}

\mathcal{Z}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}\sim\mathcal{Z}_{0}\,{\sum}_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-iqR)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits d^{2}\mathbf{x}_{1}...d^{2}\mathbf{x}_{n}\,\epsilon_{a_{1}b_{1}}\,...\,\epsilon_{a_{n}b_{n}}

which matches the term (

In fact, as integrating out the fermion is one-loop exact the only diagrams that contribute to the partition function are photon loops with repeated insertions of the vacuum polarisation diagram. As this takes an incredibly simple form we can easily compute further corrections. For instance, the next term in the expansion comes from the diagram

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{4}R^{4}}{4\pi^{2}}{\sum}_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{2l+1}{l^{2}(l+1)^{2}}

(98)

which again matches the

(101b)

5
Conclusion

In this note we have examined the perturbative structure of the Schwinger model on
S^{2}
through computations of the one-loop correction to the photon propagator and the partition function. We did this using both a numerical position-space computation in stereographic coordinates and a semi-analytic calculation in momentum-space. In both cases we found agreement with the exact solution of
28
;
5
once a gauge-invariant regularisation scheme was used. The two methods were seen to have differing strengths; the position-space calculation, though simpler, only yielded numerical results, whereas the momentum-space approach relied on a somewhat convoluted determination of the interaction vertex but yielded the exact result for each observable.

In our momentum-space calculation we restricted to the case
m=0
for simplicity, which led us to conjecture the values (
78
) and (
80
) for
\mathcal{I}
in the interaction vertex. It would be of interest to extend this to find the values of
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
for any
m
in order to completely characterise the momentum-space vertex. This would, for example, allow us to show explicitly that the vacuum polarisation diagram is
m
-independent. Additionally, a complete result for the interaction vertex would also allow for computations of higher-loop corrections; predictions for these can be found through an expansion of the known exact results, and it would be instructive to check that the two match.

While we focused here on the Schwinger model, a similar momentum-space expansion structure will occur in any theory on the sphere with a cubic coupling between a scalar field and fermionic fields. One may then hope that the computation of the partition function performed here could be easily adapted to other theories of interest for which an exact result is not known, such as the
dS_{2}
supergravity theories proposed in
7
.

Appendix B
The Gauge Anomaly and the Partition Function

In this appendix we shall review gauge anomalies in the Schwinger model and how they affect the computation of the partition function. To do this, let us first discuss the case of a free massless fermion coupled to a background
U(1)
gauge field,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{F}[\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits D\bar{\psi}D\psi\,e^{-S_{F}[\bar{\psi},\psi,\mathcal{A}]}\ ,

(121a)

\displaystyle S_{F}[\bar{\psi},\psi,\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits d^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}+iqR\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\right)\psi\ .

(121b)

We shall be interested in the one-point function of the
U(1)
current which couples to the gauge field; a perturbative expansion of

\displaystyle\langle J^{\mu}(x)\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}[\mathcal{A}]}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits D\bar{\psi}D\psi\,\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)(x)\,e^{-S[\bar{\psi},\psi,\mathcal{A}]}

(122)

\displaystyle\big\langle\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)\big\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{\Omega(\mathbf{x})^{2}}\partial_{a}\bigg(\Omega(\mathbf{x})^{2}\big\langle J^{a}(\mathbf{x})\big\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg)\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega(\mathbf{x})^{2}\big\langle\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)\big\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\Big\rvert_{qR}=iqR\,\partial_{a}\bigg(\int d^{2}\mathbf{y}\,\mathcal{A}_{b}(\mathbf{y})\tr\left(\sigma^{a}S_{0}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\sigma^{b}S_{0}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})\right)\bigg)\ .
