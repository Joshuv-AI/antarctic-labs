Abstract
. Using model theory and differential algebra, we give necessary conditions for algebraic ordinary differential equations to have a complex Pfaffian solution on some complex domain. These tools also allow us to give many examples of algebraic ordinary differential equations that do not have real Pfaffian solution on any open interval. We also give a sufficient condition for a function to be d d -irreducible, in the sense of Nishioka. These characterizations are used to give several answers to questions of Bianconi (2016) and strengthen a theorem of Nguyen (2009).

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we use model theory and differential algebra to study the classical problem of characterizing the type of functions that can appear as solutions to a given algebraic differential equation. We will mainly focus on determining whether the solutions satisfy two main defining properties, namely, whether the functions are complex Pfaffian and whether they are
d
-reducible for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.1.
Pfaffian

Pfaffian functions are defined as functions solutions to certain triangular systems of polynomial ordinary differential equations. Let
I
be an open real interval. A
Pfaffian chain
of real analytic functions on
I
is a sequence
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}
such that there are polynomials
P_{1},\cdots P_{n}
such that the following system of equations is satisfied on
I
:

\begin{cases}f_{1}^{\prime}=P_{1}(x,f_{1})\\
f_{2}^{\prime}=P_{2}(x,f_{1},f_{2})\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}^{\prime}=P_{n}(x,f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})\end{cases}

A real analytic function
f:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is Pfaffian (or
\mathbb{R}
-Pfaffian) if it is part of a Pfaffian chain. There is a natural generalization of this definition for functions of multiple variables, see subsection
3.1
. Moreover, replacing
I
by a complex domain and real analytic, real polynomials by complex analytic and complex polynomials, we obtain the class of
complex Pfaffian
(or
\mathbb{C}
-Pfaffian) functions, which are more suited to the methods of this article. Finally, if we no longer insist that the system if triangular, e.g. the equations are of the form
f_{i}^{\prime}=P_{i}(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
, we obtain the class of
Noetherian functions
.

Pfaffian functions were introduced by Khovanskii
[
18
]
where he shows that systems of equations with Pfaffian functions have a bound (computable from the data of the Pfaffian chains) on the number of isolated zeros. These developments played a large role in model theoretic results about extensions of the real field (cf.
[
20
]
and
[
36
]
). The effective bounds for sets defined by zeros of Pfaffian systems have also proved useful in a number of diophantine type results, especially in conjunction with the Pila-Wilkie theorem (cf.
[
2
,
16
]
). So, there is powerful motivation to understand which analytic functions are Pfaffian, and hence to understand which differential equations have Pfaffian solutions.

Despite this, until
[
12
]
, the literature essentially included only two ways of showing a function is not Pfaffian - the two fairly obvious ones
1
1

1

Many experts might also have noticed one other roundabout way of seeing that some differentially algebraic function is not Pfaffian based on the fact that there is no maximal o-minimal expansion of the reals

(1)

Show that the function oscillates too much to satisfy the kind of bounds that zeros of Pfaffian systems satisfy (usually too much to even be o-minimally definable). The sine function on

(2)

Show that the function satisfies no differential equation. The
\Gamma

While the first technique is quite sensitive to the domain of the function (for example the sine function can be put in a Pfaffian chain on a domain where the tangent function is defined), the second obstruction is more robust but applies to few classical functions of interest. In this paper we will be interested in obstructions which are robust to shrinking the domain of the function. Indeed, when discussing Pfaffian functions, we will always be talking about functions which are Pfaffian on
some

Functions satisfying a (nonlinear) differential equation of order strictly larger than one, which is moreover strongly minimal, cannot be Pfaffian. In fact, it should be clear to experts that for a function to be Pfaffian, the Lascar rank (or Morley rank) of the system of differential equations that the function satisfies must be equal to the order of the system. Then if one wants to give a characterization of which functions are Pfaffian, two obstacles

One is also interested in understanding which complex analytic functions have real and imaginary parts which are Pfaffian, as well as which real analytic functions are Pfaffian, in the more classical real sense. 1.2.
Solvability and Reducibility

The class of Pfaffian functions is one of several generalizations of the class of Liouvillian functions introduced by Joseph Liouville in the late 1800’s. In the direction of higher order linear equations, the class of
d
-solvable functions has been studied classically starting with Fuchs (also in the late 1800’s), and more recently by Singer
[
35
]
, Nguyen
[
26
]
, and others. Roughly, a function is

-solvable if it can be obtained by successively solving homogeneous linear differential equations of order less or equal to
d

More generally, one might allow extensions by arbitrary nonlinear equations of order at most
d

d
-reducibility
[
27
,
28
]
. Recently, work of
[
24
]
was combined with
d
-irreducibility to give proofs of the strong minimality of various differential equations
[
4
]
. Those familiar with the notion of
d
-solvability might view
d
-reducibility as the general non-linear version of
d
-solvability.

We use the recent work of Freitag and Moosa
[
11
]
on generic
n
-transitivity to give a condition for the generic solution of a differential equation which is internal to the constants to have a solution which is not
d
-reducible. This allows us to strengthen results of Singer
[
34
,
35
,
26
]
by showing that certain solutions of linear differential equations are not only not
d
-solvable, but are also not
(d-1)
-reducible, a more general class. This is Theorem
4.15
below:

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be the solution set of a homogeneous linear differential equation over
K
with differential galois group
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then the generic solution of
X
is
n-1
-reducible but not
n-2
-reducible.

2.2.
Differential equations on algebraic curves

In this subsection, we briefly recall the formalism of
D
-varieties, which will be useful throughout the text, as well as some specific results on differential equations on curves. The reader may consult
[
25
]
for more details on this perspective. We work in a fixed model
(\mathcal{U},\delta)
of
\mathrm{DCF}_{0}
.

Consider a differential field
K
, and
V\subset\mathcal{U}^{n}
some affine variety defined over
K
. The
prolongation
of
V
is the variety
\tau V\subset\mathcal{U}^{2n}
defined by the following equations:

\begin{cases}P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=0\\
P^{\delta}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial P}{\partial X_{i}}U_{i}=0\end{cases}

for each
P\in I(V)
. The polynomial
P^{\delta}
is obtained by applying the ambient derivation to the coefficients of
P
. If
K<\mathbb{C}
, we recover the classical tangent bundle
TV
. The prolongation is a torsor for the tangent bundle.

By patching, we can define the prolongation of any algebraic variety.

Definition 2.15
.

A
D
-variety
(V,s)
is a variety
V
, equipped with a rational section
s:V\rightarrow\tau V
of its prolongation, meaning that
\pi\circ s=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
, where
\pi:\tau V\rightarrow V
is the natural projection. Note that it is sometimes required that
s
is regular in the literature, but we do not make that assumption.

As proved in
[
13
]
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between order one differential equations and
D
-varieties
(C,s)
, where
C
is an algebraic curve. If
C
is defined over
\mathbb{C}
, there is also a one-to-one correspondence between rational sections
s
of the tangent bundle and rational differential form in
\Omega(C)
, given by associating to
s
the unique
\omega
with
\omega(s)=1
. If
C
is defined over a non-constant differential field
K
, the correspondence is between sections of the prolongation and the space
\Omega^{\tau}_{K(C)/K}
of KS-forms on
C
instead, see
[
7
]
.

We make
D
-varieties correspond to definable sets in
\mathrm{DCF}_{0}
via the notion of
D
-points. It is straightforward to check that for any
a\in V(\mathcal{U})
, the derivative
\delta(a)
belongs to
\tau V
. This leads to the following:

Definition 2.16
.

Let
(V,s)
be a
D
-variety over some differential field
K
. Its set of
D
-points
is the set

(V,s)^{\sharp}=\{a\in V(\mathcal{U}):s(a)=\delta(a)\}\text{ .}

It is a definable subset of
V
.

In particular, we can define the generic type of a
D
-variety:

Fact 2.17
.

Let
(V,s)
be a
D
-variety over a differential field
K
. There is a unique complete type
p(x)\in S(K)
asserting that:

•

x\in(V,s)^{\sharp}
,

•

x
does not belong to any proper subvariety of
V
.

A proof of this fact can be found in
[
25
]
, in the discussion following Lemma 3.3. This type is called the
generic type
of
(V,s)
. Crucially, many types are interdefinable with generic types of
D
-varieties.

More precisely, we call a type
p
over some differential field
K

finite-dimensional
if for any
a\models p
, the differential field
K\langle a\rangle
has finite transcendence degree over
K
. We then have the following, see
[
25
, Theorem 3.7]
for a proof.

Fact 2.18
.

Any finite-dimensional type
p\in S(K)
is interdefinable with the generic type of a
D
-variety over
K
.

Note that in particular, we can associate to every finite-dimensional type a birational equivalence class, and any birational invariant can be used as an invariant of the type.

We also have a notion of
D
-morphism:

Definition 2.19
.

A
D
-morphism between two
D
-varieties
(V,s)
and
(W,r)
is a rational map
\phi:V\rightarrow W
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

where

is the differential of

.

This makes

-varieties into a category, which is proven in
[

]
to be equivalent to the category of types of finite rank and definable maps between them.

3.
Pfaffian types and functions

3.1.
Preliminaries on Pfaffian functions

We start by recalling the classic definition of Pfaffian chains and functions:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. A finite sequence
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})
of analytic functions
f_{j}:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
Pfaffian chain
if there are polynomials with real coefficients,
P_{ij}(\bar{x},y_{1},\ldots,y_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
so that

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}=P_{ij}(\bar{x},f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}).

We say a function
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
Pfaffian
if it is in a Pfaffian chain.

Replacing
\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{C}
and real analytic with complex analytic, one obtains the definition of
\mathbb{C}
-Pfaffian.

There are numerous variations of the definition considered in the literature. For instance, when
\mathcal{R}
is an o-minimal expansion of the reals,
[
23
]
replaces the polynomial functions in the above definition with the more general class of functions definable in
\mathcal{R}
. In this work, we will use two definitions adapted to the context of differentially closed fields. For rest of this subsection,
K
with be a differential subfield of
\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
K<\mathcal{U}
be a differential field. A finite sequence
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})
of elements
a_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
is a
B
-definable (rational) Pfaffian chain
if for all
1\leq i\leq N
there is a polynomial
P_{i}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i})
(resp. a rational function) with coefficients in
K
such that for all
i
:

a_{i}^{\prime}=P_{i}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{i})\text{ .}

The number
N
is called the
order
of the chain.

We say that
a\in M
is
B
-definably (rationally) Pfaffian
if there is a
B
-definable Pfaffian chain
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
(resp. definable rational Pfaffian chain) and some
P\in K[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
such that
a=P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
. We say that
p\in S(B)
is
(rationally) Pfaffian
if some (any)
a\models p
is
B
-definably (rationally) Pfaffian.

The connection between the usual pfaffian definition and the definable version is reasonably clear, but we state and prove it for completeness:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p\in S(F(t))
, where
F<\mathbb{C}
is a finitely generated extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
p
is (rationally) Pfaffian if and only if there are some domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}
and some holomorphic (rationally)
\mathbb{C}
-Pfaffian function
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
f\models p
(viewing
f
as an element of the differential field
\mathrm{Mer}(U)
).

Proof.

First assume that
p
is Pfaffian, and fix some
F
-definable Pfaffian chain
a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}
with
a=P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})
. Then
F\langle a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}\rangle
is a finitely generated differential field, and by Seidenberg’s embedding theorem there are some domain
U
of
\mathbb{C}
and an embedding
\sigma:F\langle a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}\rangle\rightarrow\mathrm{Mer}(U)
. Potentially restricting
U
, we may assume
f=\sigma(a)
is holomorphic. Then
\sigma(a_{1}),\cdots,\sigma(a_{N})
is a Pfaffian chain, witnessing that
f
is
\mathbb{C}
-Pfaffian.

Conversely, assume that there are some open
U\subset\mathbb{C}
and some holomorphic
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
f\models p
. We embed
\mathrm{Mer}(U)
into some differentially closed field
M
, and pick
a
to be the image of
f
.

The same proof works for rationally Pfaffian.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(b_{n},\cdots,b_{n})
be a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain. Any element of
K\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\rangle
is part of a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain.

Proof.

The differential field
K\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\rangle
is generated by taking field operations and derivatives. Therefore, all that is needed is to prove that these operations, when applied to elements of Pfaffian chains, gives elements of Pfaffian chains. For addition and multiplication, this is immediate. For taking multiplicative inverse, note that if
b
is in a Pfaffian chain, we obtain a Pfaffian chain for
\frac{1}{b}
of length one more by using that
(\frac{1}{b})^{\prime}=-(\frac{1}{b})^{2}b^{\prime}
.

Now assume that
d_{m}
is an element of some Pfaffian chain
(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m})
so that
d_{m}^{\prime}=P(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m})
, for some
P\in K[x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}]
. Then we have:

d_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\partial P}{x_{i}}(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m})d_{i}^{\prime}+P^{\delta}(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m})

where
P^{\delta}
is obtained from
P
by applying
\delta
to its coefficients. Since the
d_{i}
form a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain, the right hand side is equal to some polynomial with coefficient in
K
applied to
d_{1},\cdots,d_{m}
. Thus
b^{\prime}
is also in a Pfaffian chain.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in S(K)
and
f:p\rightarrow f(p)
a
K
-definable map. Then
p
is definably Pfaffian if and only if
f(p)
and
\operatorname{tp}(a/f(a)K)
are both definably Pfaffian, for some (any)
a\models p
.

Proof.

The left to right direction is immediate: adding parameters to the base preserves Pfaffianess, and in
\mathrm{DCF}_{0}
, definable closure is generated by field operations and derivatives, all of which preserve Pfaffianess, by Lemma
3.4
.

Conversely, suppose that both
f(p)
and
\operatorname{tp}(a/f(a)K)
are definably Pfaffian. So
f(a)
is part of a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain. As
a
is
K\langle f(a)\rangle
definably Pfaffian, we can extend this chain to a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain containing
a
.

∎

As a corollary, we obtain:

Corollary 3.6
.

A type
p\in S(K)
is
K
-definably Pfaffian if and only if each step in any semiminimal analysis over
K
is
K
-definably Pfaffian.

Because of this result, we will focus our efforts on semi-minimal types. Note however that computing a semi-minimal analysis of a type is a highly non-trivial problem.

3.2.
Types internal to the constants

We can start with the case of a type internal to the constants. Again, we fix a base differential field

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

Before the moreover part, this is a well-known result, see

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

and let

The left to right direction is because projection maps can be used at the last step of a Pfaffian chain, the right to left because any Pfaffian chain for

∎

As discussed before, we call a
\mathbb{C}
-internal type

if the action of the binding group

on

is free. The type
p
is weakly
\mathbb{C}
-orthogonal if and only if that action is transitive. Let
p\in S(K)
be any
\mathbb{C}
-internal type. By Lemma

, determining if
p
is
K

\mathbb{C}
-orthogonal type is
K
-definably Pfaffian. By Lemma

, we can replace

the type of any tuple of its realizations. In particular, by
[

and the discussion preceding it, we can assume that

is fundamental, without changing the binding group.

We now recall the following fact, due to Kolchin
[
19
, §VI.9]
, but written in this form by Jaoui-Moosa
[
14
]
:

Fact 3.9
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field, and
p\in S(K)
a
\mathbb{C}
-internal, fundamental and weakly
\mathbb{C}
-orthogonal type. Then there is a connected algebraic group
G
, defined over
\mathbb{C}\cap K
, such that

is interdefinable with the generic type of a full logarithmic-differential equation on
G
over

. Moreover, the group
G(\mathbb{C})
is definably isomorphic to the binding group of

.

This allows us to show that internal types with specific binding groups are Pfaffian:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
p\in S(K)
be fundamental, weakly
\mathbb{C}
-orthogonal, with
K
algebraically closed, and let
G
be its binding group. If
G
is finite or definably isomorphic to either
\mathbb{G}_{a}(\mathbb{C})
,
\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
,
\mathbb{G}_{a}(\mathbb{C})\mathchar 9583\relax\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, then

is
K
-definably Pfaffian.

Proof.

Assume first that
G
is finite. Let

, by stable embeddedness we have
a\in K(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{alg}}
. So there is
P\in K(\mathbb{C})[x]\setminus K(\mathbb{C})
such that
P(a)=0
. Taking derivatives, we obtain

, where
P^{\delta}
is obtained by applying the ambient derivative to the coefficients of

. Because
P(a)=0
, we have
K(\mathbb{C})(a)=K(\mathbb{C})[a]
, thus
\frac{1}{\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}(a)}
is a polynomial in

. Therefore

is
K
-Pfaffian.

Now assume that
G
is infinite. First note that interdefinability of types in
S(K)
preserves
K
-definable Pfaffianess, and thus by Fact
3.9
, we may assume that
p
is the generic type of a full logarithmic differential equation on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
,
\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
\mathbb{G}_{a}\mathchar 9583\relax\mathbb{G}_{m}
or

over

.

Over
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, these are given by

and

, respectively, for some

. These obviously have

-definably Pfaffian generic types.

For the

and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}

must then be interdefinable with the generic type of a Ricatti equation

∎

Before proving our main result, we need some observation about algebraic groups:

Fact 3.11
.

Let
H
be an algebraic subgroup of a cartesian product of copies of
\mathbb{G}_{a},\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{G}_{a}\mathchar 9583\relax\mathbb{G}_{m}
or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
. Then
H
has a subnormal series in which each quotient is finite or definably isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a},\mathbb{G}_{m}
or
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{G}_{a},\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{G}_{a}\mathchar 9583\relax\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}
correspond exactly, up to isomorphism, to the algebraic subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. Therefore, we may assume that
H
is a subgroup of a cartesian power of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
.

We induct on the number
n
of factors. If
n=1
, then
H
is an algebraic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, and the previous paragraph gives the result.

Now assume
n\geq 1
and the result is correct for all

. Let
\pi
be the projection on the first coordinate and
\rho
the projection on the last

[

for example), there are an algebraic group

and surjective morphisms

and
\theta:\rho(H)\rightarrow K
such that

.

In particular, the morphism

has kernel
\{(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n}):\phi\circ\pi(g_{1})=\theta\circ\rho(g_{2},\cdots,g_{n})=\operatorname{id}\}
, which is isomorphism to

and

is a quotient group of a subgroup of

, it is also of the required form, and we obtain the result.

∎

Remark 3.12
.

In

We now prove (1) implies (2) by induction. Our induction hypothesis is that if some (any) realization of

For

There is a finite tuple

The type

is definably Pfaffian if and only if every type in this semiminimal analysis is.

If it is internal to
\mathbb{C}
, Theorem
3.13
gives us the restriction on its binding group. If not, by the results of
[
12
]
, the type
r
must be of order one, and we conclude by Theorem
3.20
.

∎

We end this subsection by discussing the difference between Pfaffian types and rationally Pfaffian types. By our previous work, these two notions coincide for
\mathbb{C}
-internal types. Thus, the only types in the semi-minimal analysis that may be rationally Pfaffian but not Pfaffian are interalgebraic with Morley products of order one types, which must be the generic type of a
D
-variety on a curve. We will focus here on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and thus will work with generic types of equations of the form
y^{\prime}=f(y)
, for some rational function
f
.

First note that such a type can be Pfaffian, even if
f
is not a polynomial: consider the equation
y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2y}
, its solutions are locally given by
\sqrt{c+t}
, which are Pfaffian. For a non-algebraic example, consider the (multivalued) Lambert function, which is defined as the inverse of the function
w\rightarrow we^{w}
. Pick
W
to be any branch of this multivalued function. An elementary computation shows that
W
satisfies

W^{\prime}=\frac{W}{z(1+W)}

where
z
is a complex variable. One can check that
W
is part of the Pfaffian chain over
\mathbb{Q}(z)

\begin{cases}y_{1}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{z}(1-y_{1})y_{1}^{2}\\
y_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{z}y_{1}y_{2}\end{cases}

for which
y_{1}=\frac{1}{1+W}
and
y_{2}=W
is a solution.

However, we will show here that these two classes of types are different, at least over constant parameters. Since both are associated to rational
1
-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, genus arguments will not work. We start with a lemma:

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
K<\mathbb{C}
be algebraically closed, and
p\in S(K)
be the generic type of
y^{\prime}=f(y)
for some
f\in K(x)
, which we assume to be strictly disintegrated. If
p
is Pfaffian then there are a field
K<L
, some
R,S\in L(x)
such that, with
h=\frac{R}{S}
, we have that
f(h(x))S^{2}
is a polynomial in
L[x]
and
h
is non-constant.

Proof.

Assume
p
is Pfaffian, there must be
a\models p
and a
K
-definable Pfaffian chain
b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}
such that
a\in\operatorname{acl}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{k},K)
. We may assume
k
is minimal. Let
L=K\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{k-1}\rangle^{\mathrm{alg}}
.

By minimality, we have that
\operatorname{tp}(b_{k}/L)
is minimal,
a
and
b_{k}
are interalgebraic over
L
, and
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt\displaystyle{}}{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt\textstyle{}}{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt\scriptstyle{}}{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt\scriptscriptstyle{}}}_{K}L
. Since non-forking extensions of strictly disintegrated types are strictly disintegrated, Fact
2.13
gives that
a\in\operatorname{dcl}(b_{k}L)
. In other words, there is
h\in L(x)
such that
a=h(b_{k})
. Let
P\in L[x]
be such that
b_{k}^{\prime}=P(b_{k})
and write
h=\frac{R}{S}
for some
R,S\in L[x]
. Also denote
R^{\delta}
and
S^{\delta}
the polynomials obtained by applying the derivation of
\mathcal{U}
to the coefficients of
R
and
S
. We compute:

\displaystyle f(h(b_{k}))

\displaystyle=f(a)

\displaystyle=a^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h(b_{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{R(b_{k})}{S(b_{k})}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(R^{\delta}(b_{k})+P(b_{k})\frac{\partial R}{\partial x}(b_{k})\right)S(b_{k})-R(b_{k})\left(S^{\delta}(b_{k})+P(b_{k})\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}(b_{k})\right)}{S(b_{k})^{2}}

which gives

f(h(b_{k}))S(b_{k})^{2}=\left(R^{\delta}(b_{k})+P(b_{k})\frac{\partial R}{\partial x}(b_{k})\right)S(b_{k})-R(b_{k})\left(S^{\delta}(b_{k})+P(b_{k})\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}(b_{k})\right)\text{ .}

Note that by minimality of
k
, we have
b_{k}\not\in L=L^{\mathrm{alg}}
. Thus, we must have the equality

f(h(x))S(x)^{2}=\left(R^{\delta}(x)+P(x)\frac{\partial R}{\partial x}\right)S(x)-R(x)\left(S^{\delta}(x)+P(x)\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}\right)

in
L(x)
, giving the result.
∎

Here is our criterion:

Theorem 3.25
.

Let
f=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}x-\alpha_{i}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}x-\beta_{j}}
for some complex numbers
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\beta_{j}
for all
i,j
, and fix
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m)^{\mathrm{alg}}
. Let
p
be the generic type of
y^{\prime}=f(y)
over
K
, which we assume to be strictly disintegrated. If either two
\beta_{j}
are non-equal, or
0<m<n-2
, then
p
is not Pfaffian.

Proof.

Consider any extension
K<L
, let
h\in L(x)
be non-constant, and write
h=\frac{R}{S}
for some
R,S\in L[x]
relatively prime. We can compute

\displaystyle f(h(x))S^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R}{S}-\alpha_{i}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{R}{S}-\beta_{j}}S^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}R-\alpha_{i}S}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}R-\beta_{j}S}S^{m+2-n}

Assume first that two
\beta_{j}
are non-equal, which we may take to be
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
. For
f(h(x))S^{2}
to be a polynomial, we must have that
R-\beta_{1}S
divides the numerator. Note that
\gcd(R-\beta_{1}S,S)=\gcd(R,S)=1
, and also that for all
i
we have
\gcd(R-\beta_{1}S,R-\alpha_{i}S)=\gcd(R,S)
as
\alpha_{i}\neq\beta_{1}
. Thus, the polynomial
R-\beta_{1}S
divides the numerator if and only if
R-\beta_{1}S
is constant. By the same reasoning
R-\beta_{2}S
must be constant, and thus
(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})S
must be constant. As
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
, we obtain that
S
is constant, and therefore that
R
is constant. So
f(h(x))S^{2}
cannot be a polynomial, thus
p
is not Pfaffian by Lemma
3.24
.

Now assume that
2<m+2<n
. Continuing the previous computation, we have

\displaystyle f(h(x))S^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}R-\alpha_{i}S}{S^{n-m-2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}R-\beta_{j}S}

Again, note that
\gcd(S,R-\alpha_{i}S)=\gcd(R,S)=1
, thus for
S
to divide the numerator, it must be constant. Say
S=c
for some
c\in L\setminus\{0\}
. Then we can write

\displaystyle f(h(x))S^{2}

\displaystyle=c^{m+2-n}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}R-\alpha_{i}c}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}R-\beta_{j}c}\text{ .}

Note that
R
cannot be constant too, as
h=\frac{R}{S}
is non-constant. Because
m>0
, we can look at the polynomial
R-\beta_{1}c
, which must have a root
d
. But it cannot be a root of the numerator, as then
R(d)-\alpha_{i}c=R(d)-\beta_{1}c
for some
i
, which would imply that
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i}
. Therefore
f(h(x))S^{2}
cannot be a polynomial, so
p
is not Pfaffian by Lemma
3.24
.
∎

Before applying this theorem to obtain examples of rationally Pfaffian types that are not Pfaffian, let us comment on its limitations and potential generalizations.

First, it is unclear if a necessary and sufficient condition could be obtained from these techniques. The problem is that there seems to be no way to bound the length
k
of the Pfaffian chain in Lemma
3.24
. If
k=1
could be obtained, then one could reverse the computations of Theorem
3.25
to describe a necessary and sufficient condition, as long as the type is strictly disintegrated. Note that it is also possible to reverse these computations to obtain many examples of equations
y^{\prime}=f(y)
for
f\in\mathbb{C}(x)\setminus\mathbb{C}[x]
with Pfaffian generic type.

Finally, it is unclear if one could obtain general results regarding types that are not strictly disintegrated. In that case, one must replace the rational function
h
with a finite-to-finite correspondence, which may make the computations trickier.

We end this section by proving that the two classes are different:

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
a,b
be two non-equal complex numbers different from
0
and
1
, and with
\frac{a(a-1)}{b(b-1)}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
. The generic type
p\in S(\mathbb{Q}(a,b)^{\mathrm{alg}})
of

y^{\prime}=\frac{(y-a)(y-b)}{y(y-1)}

is not pfaffian.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\frac{(x-a)(x-b)}{x(x-1)}
. According to Theorem
3.25
, the generic type
p
of
y^{\prime}=f(y)
over
K=\mathbb{Q}(a,b)^{\mathrm{alg}}
will not be Pfaffian if it is strictly disintegrated. According to
[
13
, Lemma 2.23]
, this is the case if
\frac{1}{f}
has at least two simple poles and no two residues are rational multiple of each other. The poles of
\frac{1}{f}
are at
a
and
b
, are both simple with residue
\frac{a(a-1)}{a-b}
and
\frac{b(b-1)}{b-a}
. It is therefore enough to pick
a,b
such that the ratio
-\frac{a(a-1)}{b(b-1)}
of these two quantities is irrational to obtain a non-Pfaffian type.
∎

A similar proof, but using the other case of Theorem
3.25
, gives us:

Corollary 3.27
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
be three non-equal, non zero complex numbers, and with
\frac{a_{l}}{a_{k}}\frac{\prod\limits_{i\neq k,l}{a_{k}-a_{i}}}{\prod\limits_{j\neq k,l}a_{l}-a_{j}}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
for all non-equal
l,k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. The generic type
p\in S(\mathbb{Q}(a,b)^{\mathrm{alg}})
of

y^{\prime}=\frac{(y-a_{1})(y-a_{2})(y-a_{3})(y-a_{4})}{y}

is not pfaffian.

The following is left unanswered by our work here:

Question 3.28
.

Can we provide a necessary and sufficient criteria for an order one type to be Pfaffian?

If this can be done, a necessary and sufficient condition for any finite rank type to be Pfaffian could be given, in the spirit of Theorem
3.23
.

3.5.
Real consequences

Historically, complex analytic Pfaffian functions were much less studied than real analytic Pfaffian functions. Indeed, the applications of Pfaffian functions to model theory are all in the context of extensions of the real field. Moreover, the bounds on the number of zeroes do not hold for complex Pfaffian functions (as an example, the complex exponential has infinitely many zeroes). In this short subsection, we give consequences of our results for the non-existence of real analytic Pfaffian solutions to some algebraic ordinary differential equations, starting with the internal case.

Proposition 3.29
.

Let
L(y)=0
be a monic linear differential equation of order
n
defined over some field
K<\mathbb{R}(t)
, with Galois group
\mathrm{Sl}_{n}
for
n>2
. For any open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the only Pfaffian solution of
L(y)=0
on
I
is the zero function.

Proof.

Assume, on the contrary, that it does have a non-zero real analytic Pfaffian solution
f
on some open interval
I
. The ring of real analytic functions on
I
is a differential integral domain, which can thus be embedded in a differentially closed field
\mathcal{U}
, which we may as well assume to be
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, with field of constants equal to
\mathbb{C}
. The image
a
of
f
under this embedding must be
K
-definably Pfaffian, where we identify
K
with its image under the embedding. We must also have
L(a)=0
and
a\neq 0
.

Consider the generic type
q
of
L(y)=0
. Note that the set of solution of
L(y)=0
forms an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, and its set of non-zero elements is acted upon transitively by its Galois group
\mathrm{Sl}_{n}
. In particular, we see that
q
is weakly orthogonal to
\mathbb{C}
, and isolated by
L(y)=0\wedge y\neq 0
. As
a\neq 0
, we must have
a\models q
, and in particular
\operatorname{tp}(a/K)
has binding group
\mathrm{Sl}_{n}
. By Theorem
3.13

a
is not
K
-definably pfaffian, a contradiction.
∎

The case of minimal types has a similar proof:

Proposition 3.30
.

Let
P(y^{(n)},\cdots,y^{\prime},y)=0
be an algebraic differential equation defined over
K<\mathbb{R}
, and
p
its generic type over
K
, which we assume to be minimal. If
p
is not Pfaffian, then
P(y^{(n)},\cdots,y^{\prime},y)=0
has no non-constant real analytic Pfaffian solution, on any open real interval.

Proof.

Suppose that it does have a real analytic, non-constant Pfaffian solution
f
. Again embedding the situation into a differentially closed field
\mathcal{U}
, we see that the image of
a
of
f
under the embedding is a solution of
P(y^{(n)},\cdots,y^{\prime},y)=0
that does not belong to
K^{\mathrm{alg}}
. By minimality, this implies that
a\models p|_{K^{\mathrm{alg}}}
. But
a
is also
K
-definably Pfaffian, which implies that
p
is Pfaffian.
∎

Remark 3.31
.

Note that the same proof works for rationally Pfaffian.

We can use this to obtain non-Pfaffian real analytic functions:

Corollary 3.32
.

Any real analytic, non-constant solution, on any open real interval, of either

•

a Weierstrass equation with real coefficients,

•

an equation of the form
y^{\prime}=\frac{(y-a)(y-b)}{y(y-1)}
with
a,b
non-equal real numbers with
a,b\neq 0,1
and
\frac{a(a-1)}{b(b-1)}\not\in\mathbb{Q}

is not Pfaffian.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.30
together with Theorem
3.13
and Corollary
3.26
, respectively.
∎

4.
Reducibility and solvability

4.1.
Preliminaries on reducibility and solvability

The special classes of functions particular to this section have their origins in a question of Fuchs from the late 19th century, see
[
10
]
. Given some linear differential equation of order
n
, when is it possible to express all of the solutions of the equation in terms of solutions of linear differential equations of order
d<n
? This corresponds to the following:

Definition 4.1
.

A differential field extension
F\supset k
is
d
-solvable
if there is a tower of differential fields
k=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset K_{2}\ldots\subset K_{r}
with
F\subset K_{r}
and for each
i\leq r
, one of these two possibilities is true:

•

K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is a finite algebraic extension.

•

K_{i}
is a PV-extension of
K_{i-1}
for a homogeneous linear differential equation over
K_{i-1}
of order at most
d
.

We call a type
p\in S(K)

d
-solvable if some (any) of its realizations is contained in a
d
-solvable extension of
K
. A realization of such a type
p
is said to be
d
-solvable over
K
.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
26
, page 422]

Let
F/k
be a PV-extension with constant field
\mathcal{C}(k).
If
Aut(F/k)\subset GL_{m}
, then there is a linear differential equation
X
of order at most
m
such that
F
is the PV-extension of
X.

This proposition can be used to verify that the definition of
d
-solvability in Definition
4.1
might have alternatively have been taken to be
K_{i+1}/K_{i}
is a PV extension and
\mathrm{Aut}(K_{i+1}/K_{i}))\subset GL_{d}.
There is no direct analog of Proposition
4.2
in the nonlinear case. Part of the difficulty associated with the nonlinear generalizations of
d
-solvability is the fact that there is less control over the group of automorphisms of the equations. For example, there is a priori no bound on their dimension in terms of the order of the equation.

Definition 4.3
.

When
G
is a linear algebraic group over
C
, we say that
G
is
d
-solvable
if there is a chain of algebraic subgroups
G=G_{0}\supset G_{1}\supset\ldots G_{s}=\operatorname{id}_{G}
with the property that
G_{i}
is normal in
G_{i-1}
and
G_{i-1}/G_{i}
is either finite or a quotient of a subgroup of
GL_{d}
.

Given a short exact sequence
1\rightarrow G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}\rightarrow G_{3}\rightarrow 1
,
G_{2}
is
d
-solvable if and only if
G_{1}
and
G_{3}
are each
d
-solvable.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
26
, page 423]

A PV-extension is
d
-solvable if and only if its differential Galois group is
d
-solvable.

In
[
34
]
,
2
-solvable extensions are called
Eulerian
, where the previous theorem was established. We note in particular the following consequence of Theorem
3.13
:

Corollary 4.5
.

The generic solution of a linear differential equation is pfaffian if and only if it is
2
-solvable.

We also recall the definition of
n
-reducibility developed over a series of papers of Nishioka
[

,
28
]
:

Definition 4.6
.

An tuple

is

We will say that a type

is

4.2.
A condition for non-

-reducibility

Consider the family of types
\mathfrak{P}_{K,d}
consisting of types over some differential field extension

such that some (any) realization has transcendence degree less or equal to

over

. This is a

Proposition 4.7
.

If a type

is
d
-reducible, then it is
\mathfrak{P}_{K,d}
-analyzable.

Proof.

We use induction on the number

of differential field extensions
K=K_{1}<\cdots<K_{l}
such that

for some

. If

, then

is the type of some element of

, so satisfies an equation of order

.

Now assume that there is a chain
K=K_{1}<\cdots<K_{l}
such that some

belongs to
K_{l}
, and that we have proven the result up to

. Consider

, where

is some element of

such that

over

over

We know that

A

The proof is essentially the same as for Theorem

Corollary 4.9

Let

•

Remark that we only give the definition for binding groups here, as it is all we need, but it can in general be defined for any finite Morley rank group action

Taking the image of a type can only raise the degree of generic transitivity:

Lemma 4.11

Let

Proof.

Since generic

Theorem 4.12
.

Let

be non-algebraic

-internal type. If

, then

-reducible.

Proof.

By Proposition

, we know that any

-analyzable. We prove by induction on the length

]
.

Now let

We next point out some connections between

-solvability and

-reducibility when

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
X
be the solution set of an order
n
linear homogeneous differential equation
L(y)=0
over
K
. The image of
X\setminus\{0\}
under the logarithmic derivative map
\mathrm{dlog}:y\rightarrow\frac{y^{\prime}}{y}
is the zero set of an order
n-1
differential equation.

Proof.

The fiber of a given point
\mathrm{dlog}^{-1}(\alpha)
is in bijection with
\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
: if
\mathrm{dlog}(y)=\alpha
, then
\mathrm{dlog}(cy)=\alpha
for any

. Since
X
is the zero-set of an homogeneous linear equation, this fiber is contained in
X
. As each fiber is of order one, it must be that the image of the generic point generates a differential field of transcendence degree
n-1
over
k.

∎

In particular, this gives us the following:

Corollary 4.14
.

Fix

and let
p\in S(K)
. If some (any) realization
a
of
p
is
d
-solvable, then it is also
d-1
-reducible.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
X
be the solution set of a homogeneous linear differential equation over

is

-reducible but not

-reducible.

Proof.

That it is

-reducible follows from Proposition

.

To prove that it is not

-reducible, first note that we may assume that the linear differential equation has order

(see

\mathrm{Gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
on

Consider the logarithmic derivative map
\mathrm{dlog}
on

, the image

is the solution set of a differential equation of order

, and is definably isomorphic to

We can restrict

to a definable map

from the generic type of

to the generic type of

∎
