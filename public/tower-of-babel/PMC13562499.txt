Prediction of physicochemical properties of organic compounds using degree-based topological indices and machine learning models
Authors: Sadia Noureen, Nazma Ashraf, Saood Azam, Adnan Aslam, Keneni Abera Tola
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a quantitative structure-property relationship (QSPR) framework that integrates graph theory with machine learning to predict key physicochemical properties of diverse organic compounds. A data set of 275 structurally diverse organic compounds, including alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, cyclic systems, and aromatic hydrocarbons–was represented as molecular graphs, and their topological features were encoded using two complementary degree-based indices: the Sombor index (SO) and the Modified inverse degree index (ID). These indices were employed to model the octanol-water partition coefficient (LogP), calculated LogP (CLogP), molar refractivity (MR), and critical pressure (CP). Linear regression established baseline correlations, while Random Forest and XGBoost regression models were implemented to capture nonlinear relationships and enhance predictive accuracy. Models were evaluated using a 70%/30% train-test split and five-fold cross-validation. The predicted values show good correlation with Actual values. Machine learning models are outperforming than linear regression. XGBoost has the highest prediction performance for all properties, with a best value larger than 0.97. Well-chosen topological indices and machine learning models, provide a highly efficient and effective way to predict the physicochemical properties of organic compounds with diverse scaffolds.Keywords: Chemical graph theory, Topological indices, QSPR modeling, Machine learning predictionSubject terms: Chemistry, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Graph theory was recognized long time ago as an effective tool for a mathematical description of chemical molecules in terms of graphs where atoms of an inorganic molecule are paired off to form vertices of a graph, and chemical bonds corresponding to edges, so that atoms separated by very short paths correspond to nearest neighbors6,7,22. Chem graph theory is now proven to be fundamental tool in chemical studies, especially in so-called chemical graph theory, and in so called quantitative structure-property relationship (QSPR) studies8,13,20. In QSPR studies a structure of a molecule is transformed into a number of QSPR descriptors, or QNs, which in a form of vector can be used to predict certain measurable, physicochemical properties of that molecule8,15,17.

Topological indices are numerical invariants of molecular graphs which encode information concerning the structure of molecules. In the last three decades, a large number of topological indices have been defined3,19. Many of them are degree-based indices, which are simple, easy to calculate and have predictive power16,25. In recent years (2020-2024), some new topological indices such as Sombor-type, eccentricity-based and geometric-arithmetic degree, and polynomial degree-based topological indices have been defined, which provide new way for the structural characterization. On the other hand, the topological indices are used in QSPR modeling, where machine learning methods are also incorporated. For instance, Chu et al. (2021) systematically investigated degree and irregularity-based molecular descriptors for benzenoid systems, demonstrating strong correlations with electronic properties and providing a theoretical foundation for predicting the behavior of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)26. Similarly, Julietraja and Venugopal employed M-polynomial techniques to compute degree-based descriptors for coronoid PAHs, revealing that connectivity indices such as Randic and Zagreb indices exhibit significant correlations with total π-electron energy, a finding directly relevant to the synthesis of novel coronoid structures27. More recently, Julietraja et al. extended neighbourhood sum-based descriptors to sodalite zeolite systems, showing that these indices effectively capture the structural complexity of cage-and-tunnel frameworks, thereby establishing a basis for predicting adsorption and sieving properties in porous materials28.

In the present investigation, two degree-based topological descriptors i.e., Sombor index (SO) and modified inverse degree-based index (ID) have been utilized for this purpose1,4,21. The choice of these two descriptors is based on the reason that, these two descriptors are pairwise dependent descriptors, which provide a complementary view of molecular structure. They have low computational time and provide a global and local view of molecular graph, as compared to higher dimensional descriptors. Sombor index is global degree-based descriptor that captures the degree of heterogeneity in the degrees of vertices in the graph. On the other hand, modified inverse degree descriptor is based on local structure and emphasizes the influence of small valency vertices in the graph and hence is sensitive to fine variations in the local structure of molecules. Hence, it shows stronger predictivity as compared to Sombor index. Utilization of these two descriptors provide a compact and complementary representation of the molecular topology which improves the interpretability and the predictive capability of developed QSPR models5,9,18.

The data set under consideration includes organic compounds (hydrocarbons, i.e., aliphatic compounds) of a large diversity. They are presented in a convenient two dimensional form in the shape of plans, including straight chain, branched chain, cyclic and polycyclic structures10,11,14. Many of these compounds are commercially available and are known as test molecules in many literature e.g. n-alkanes, cycloalkanes, unsaturated aliphatic (alkenes and alkynes) compounds, aromatic (substituted benzene ring) compounds. A large set of data of such compounds are commonly used in QSPR studies to establish relationships between various topological descriptors and physico-chemical properties of compounds. These compounds include linear molecules (acene type), branched molecules (indacene type), cyclic (naphthalene type) and polycyclic (phenanthrene type) molecules etc. The simple structural profiles and their easy characterization make them ideal molecules to test performance of topological indices and to establish meaningful structure-property relationship. Many studies have been reported in literature for such set of data10,20,21.

While numerous QSPR studies have individually employed degree-based indices or machine learning methods for property prediction, the present work advances the field in three specific directions. First, unlike previous studies that typically rely on single topological descriptors, we provide a systematic justification for the complementary pairing of the Sombor index (capturing global degree heterogeneity) and the modified inverse degree index (sensitive to local low-vertex effects), demonstrating that their combination yields superior predictive performance () compared to either descriptor alone. Second, to our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive application of XGBoost regression using this specific descriptor pair for simultaneous prediction of LogP, CLogP, MR, and critical pressure across 275 structurally diverse hydrocarbons, achieving error reductions of approximately 40–60% relative to linear baselines. Third, beyond mere application, this work provides a quantitative benchmarking framework that reveals ID substantially outperforms SO across all properties (feature importance: 0.95–0.98 vs. 0.02–0.05), offering a novel insight that local structural encoding is more predictive for these physicochemical properties than global connectivity patterns. These findings advance the state of molecular property prediction by establishing that carefully paired degree-based indices with gradient boosting can approach experimental accuracy levels () without recourse to expensive quantum-chemical calculations or high-dimensional fingerprint representations.

In this study, the degree-based topological indices for all of the investigated molecular frameworks are computed. Statistical regression techniques are then employed to obtain a link between these calculated parameters and several significant experimental physicochemical properties2,23,24. These selected properties are then used as a target for a Random Forest and XGBoost regressions in order to exploit higher level, non-linear structure-property relationships and to improve the accuracy of the prediction models12. A comparison between the traditional statistical methods, and state of the art machine learning approaches is systematically carried out to evaluate their prediction abilities through various bench marking parameters. The approach presents a promising avenue to predict the properties of organic compounds using a set of topological indices and a good learning approach.

Evaluation of degree-based molecular descriptors

In chemical graph theory, a molecular structure is modeled by a simple connected graph G = (V, E), where the set of vertices V(G), also referred to as atoms and denoted by V, and the set of edges E(G), also referred to as chemical bonds and denoted by E, together represent the molecular topology. The degree of a vertex i ∈ V(G) equals the number of bonds including atom i. The degree of an adjacent vertex j∈ V(G) is the number of bonds that contain atom j. Degree-based topological indices are valuable molecular descriptors calculated on the basis of the degree sequence of molecular graphs and describe the characteristics associated to the chemical structure and responsible for several physicochemical properties.

In the present study, two degree-based topological indices Sombor index (SO) and modified Inverse Degree index (ID) have been utilized due to their considerable structural sensitivities and predicting abilities (Table 1). The edges in Sombor index (SO) contribute different values on the basis of the degrees of end vertices of that edge. Since Modified Inverse Degree (ID) index is calculated based on vertex degree distributions in molecular graph, there are occurrences of identical values of this index. These repetitions do not refer to any error, since the different chemical compounds can get identical topological structures which consequently would have identical descriptor values.

Assessment of size effects and model robustness

To examine the potential influence of size-driven correlations in the data set, additional baseline analyses were performed using simple descriptors such as carbon count and molecular weight. Although these descriptors exhibit expected trends with physicochemical properties, their predictive performance was comparatively lower than that obtained using the Sombor and Modified Inverse Degree indices. Moreover, Y-randomization tests were performed to test model robustness by randomly permuting the response variables, and resulting models demonstrated a marked decrease in predictive accuracy. This result suggests that the derived models specifically go beyond trivial size scaling relationships.

Machine learning-based modeling

Machine learning techniques were occupied to study non-linear structure-property relationships using degree-based topological indices as input descriptors. XGBoost and Random Forest methods were used to develop models with high accuracy for prediction on structurally diverse compounds.

To ensure reproducibility a fixed random number generator seed was set to 42. All models were implemented in Python 3.9 using scikit-learn (version 1.2.2) for Random Forest and data splitting, and XGBoost (version 1.7.5) for XGBoost regression. No feature scaling or normalization was applied because tree-based models are invariant to monotonic transformations. The 70/30 train-test split was performed using train_test_split with random_state=42. Hyperparameters were optimized using five-fold cross-validation exclusively on the training set; the test set was never used during hyperparameter tuning. The final hyperparameters are reported in Table 12. The best XGBoost parameters were estimators 200, max depth 6, learning rate 0.1, the rest of the XGBoost parameters were set to their default values and early stopping was set to False. The Random Forest model utilized 200 trees with automatic feature selection at each split.

Random Forest regression is applied for the modeling of intricate and nonlinear dependencies between molecular topology and physicochemical properties. On the other hand, Random Forest, which is a multi-tree method, relies on the creation of a group of decision trees due to the random selection of training data and features. The final forecast is made by combining the predictions made by all the individual trees, which leads to an increase in predictive stability and a decrease in sensitivity to the noise in the data.

In this paper, topological indices based on the degree are used as the input descriptors for the Random Forest model that the algorithm can learn structure-property relationships through non-linearity which cannot be represented well by linear assumptions. XGBoost regression

The XGBoost regression developed yields the best possible prediction results by discovering the complex structure–property relations through the boosting technique. The ensemble averaging approach is not used in XGBoost. Decision trees are built one after another and the new tree being built at that time is focused on correcting the errors of prediction of the previous trees. The modeling process carried out in the above manner allows the model to detect even the most subtle nonlinear effects that are normally neglected by simpler regression methods.

As of watershed moment in this work, the topological indices based on the degree are the input features for the XGBoost model. The optimization of the algorithm is performed by the iterative method on the objective function during the training phase. The function is designed to measure prediction accuracy against the complexity of the model. The regularization methods, which are part of the algorithm, limit the maximum height of the trees, thus reducing the problem of overfitting and increasing the model’s robustness when applied to different types of structurally diverse organic compounds in the structure. Model robustness assessment via cross-validation and bootstrapping

In addition to the train/test split, the robustness of the models were validated using a five-fold cross-validation approach. The entire dataset was first split into five roughly equal-sized subsets. For each validation, one subset was used for evaluation, and the remaining four subsets were used for training a model for that fold. This cross-validation procedure ensured that all compounds were used to train and validate the models without bias from a single data split.

The cross-validation performance is shown in Table 18 as the mean with standard deviation of the bootstrapped values. Conclusions

In this study, we present a graph-theoretical and machine learning approach to investigate the correlation between molecular structure and four physicochemical properties of many organic compounds. We represent the molecule in a chemical space abstracted to chemical graphs, where molecular structures are described by degree-based topological descriptors. Two degree-based topological descriptors, the Sombor index and the modified inverse degree index, are selected because of their special features to capture different aspects of molecular structures, including connectivity and branching and without redundancy.

In order to establish structure–property relationships, initial quantitative correlations were obtained using linear regression analysis as a reference. In order to remove the limitations associated with linear assumptions, predictions using Random Forest and XGBoost regression models were also performed. The machine learning models under all studied properties showed improved prediction accuracy and stability.

Based on evaluations performed on a 70% for training, 30% for testing split, as well as evaluations based on five-fold cross-validation including multiple numerical tests and visualizations, the method exhibits great generalization capability and stability in prediction accuracy. Both the numerical results and visualizations demonstrate the validity of the approach. Within the investigated domain of hydrocarbons (275 compounds spanning alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, cycloalkanes, and aromatics), accurate and robust physical-chemical property predictions can be achieved by selecting topological descriptors well. The methodology shows promise for transferability to other molecular data sets, but further validation on compound classes containing heteroatoms, polar functional groups, or complex ring systems is necessary before generalizing the findings. Therefore, the conclusions of this study are limited to the hydrocarbon chemical space.. The approach is extended in the article to explore predictions using fingerprint representations.

We acknowledge that our dataset contains homologous series that may introduce size-dependent correlations. While our linear and machine learning models show strong predictive performance, future work will systematically address this limitation by (i) partial correlation analysis to isolate structural effects from size, (ii) stratified validation across structural families, (iii) external validation using a newly curated set of diverse hydrocarbons, and (iv) leave-one-scaffold-out cross-validation to ensure generalization across distinct molecular topologies. These analyses will provide a more rigorous assessment of true model generalization beyond size-driven trends.
