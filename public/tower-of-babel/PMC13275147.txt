Entirely Robotic-assisted Microsurgical Anastomoses in Autologous Breast Reconstruction
Authors: Andreas Arkudas, Raymund E. Horch, Luca Kümmerl
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Robot-assisted microsurgical arterial anastomosis has become increasingly common in autologous breast reconstruction in recent years. The aim of this study was to perform both arterial and venous microsurgical anastomoses using the da Vinci robotic system in autologous breast reconstruction. Preliminary tests were conducted to determine the optimal instruments, handling techniques, and procedural steps for robot-assisted microsurgical anastomosis of both the artery and the vein. The patient was a 39-year-old woman who underwent a skin-sparing mastectomy for breast cancer followed by expander placement. Robot-assisted microsurgical anastomosis of a muscle-sparing type II transverse rectus abdominis myocutaneous flap was successfully performed using the da Vinci Xi robot without complications. A 3.0-mm coupler system was used for the venous anastomosis, and 8-0 sutures were used for the arterial anastomosis. Anastomotic patency was verified intraoperatively using the da Vinci Firefly indocyanine green function. The intraoperative and postoperative courses were uneventful, with no complications observed. In addition to its established applications in autologous breast reconstruction, the da Vinci robotic system is also suitable for performing microsurgical anastomoses. We were able to show that both arterial and venous anastomoses in autologous breast reconstruction can be performed safely using the da Vinci robot. Compared with other microsurgical robotic platforms, key advantages of the da Vinci system include its integrated camera, 4-port configuration, and integrated indocyanine green function.

Robot-assisted microsurgery has found its way into microsurgical flap transplantation in recent years.1 The Symani microsurgical robotic system (MMI, Wilmington, DE), has already been used in the fields of nerve and lymphatic surgery and flap transplantation.2–4

The da Vinci robotic system (Intuitive Surgical, Sunnyvale, CA) was introduced 25 years ago primarily for urological and visceral surgery, among other indications.5,6 Microsurgical flap transplantation has also been described using the da Vinci system in head and neck reconstruction.7 This robotic system has also been used for deep inferior epigastric perforator flap harvest and for subcutaneous mastectomy.8,9

The purpose of this study was to perform entirely robotic-assisted microsurgical anastomoses (ERAMA) using the da Vinci system in autologous breast reconstruction.

PATIENT AND METHODS

The study received ethical approval from the ethics committee of the University of Erlangen-Nürnberg (reference no. 25-508-ANF). Written informed consent for the procedure and publication of the case, including clinical images, was obtained from the patient preoperatively. Autologous breast reconstruction was performed in a 39-year-old woman at the Department of Plastic and Hand Surgery, University Hospital Erlangen. The patient had previously undergone skin-sparing mastectomy and expander-based reconstruction (Fig. 1).

RESULTS

A 30-degree lens was used for the camera, placed in port 3, with the viewing direction oriented medially. (See figure, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which displays the port placement and configuration, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E979.) Black Diamond forceps were placed in ports 1 and 4 (also used as a needle holder), and Potts scissors were positioned slightly back in port 2, whereas the lens was placed slightly more medially than the forceps.

After the muscle-sparing type II transverse rectus abdominis myocutaneous flap was divided, the da Vinci robot was then brought into the operative field, the trocars were placed (without sealing), and the instruments and the optic were inserted and adjusted to the predefined configuration established during preoperative testing. A motion scaling of 3:1 was selected.

Venous Coupler Anastomosis

The inferior epigastric vein was first inserted into the first ring of the coupler device (Synovis MCA, St. Paul, MN) with robotic assistance. (See Video 1 [online], which displays the robot-assisted venous coupler anastomosis in autologous breast reconstruction.)

Here, the vein was guided over the individual pins with robotic assistance, and the assistant used the coupler forceps to push the vein down onto the pins. After all pins had been loaded, the internal mammary vein was inserted into the second ring in the same manner. The assistant then closed and released the coupler and compressed the ring again with forceps to ensure secure approximation. The venous anastomosis required 8 minutes.

Arterial Anastomosis

A robot-assisted arteriotomy was performed using Potts scissors. (See Video 2 [online], which displays the first half of the robot-assisted arterial anastomosis in autologous breast reconstruction.) (See Video 3 [online], which displays the second half of the robot-assisted arterial anastomosis in autologous breast reconstruction.) Black Diamond micro forceps were used to dilate both the inferior epigastric artery and the internal mammary artery, and the adventitia of both vessels was trimmed with Potts scissors.

The robot-assisted arterial anastomosis was performed in the same manner as a manual anastomosis using 8-0 suture without an approximator. The open-book technique was used, placing the most challenging stitch at the posterior wall first and avoiding corner sutures.

Suture ends were shortened by the surgeon using Potts scissors. The remaining sutures were placed sequentially from the posterior to the anterior wall. The final 3 stitches were placed consecutively to maintain optimal visualization of the vessel lumen and were then tied individually. The arterial anastomosis required 32 minutes.

Total ischemia time was 75 minutes. Anastomotic patency was confirmed intraoperatively using the da Vinci Firefly indocyanine green imaging function.

The rest of the procedure was performed in the standard manner under loupe magnification. Total operative time was 5 hours and 49 minutes. The postoperative course was uneventful, with normal perfusion of the flap at all times.

DISCUSSION

We were able to show that in autologous breast reconstruction, microsurgical anastomosis of both arterial and venous anastomoses can be performed completely using the da Vinci robot. The advantages of the da Vinci robotic system lie in its 4 ports with the option of inserting scissors alongside the needle holder and forceps, enabling the surgeon to independently cut the sutures, trim the adventitia, and perform an arteriotomy. In addition, the integrated camera eliminates the need for a surgical microscope or exoscope. Furthermore, the integrated Firefly function allows indocyanine green examination of the anastomotic patency.10 Also, the use of the da Vinci robot in particular makes it possible to combine robot-assisted flap harvest and/or mastectomy and microsurgical anastomosis with the same robotic system.

Disadvantages of the robotic systems include significant costs for sterile drapes for the robot arms and expensive instruments with their limited uses. Furthermore, the ischemia time is considerably longer compared with manual techniques. This is due, on the one hand, to robot setup during the ischemia time and, on the other hand, to a lack of experience with the system and the learning curve. Furthermore, due to the lack of haptics, it is somewhat more difficult to perform the anastomosis compared with manual anastomosis, which also leads to a corresponding learning curve. The limitations of the da Vinci robot lie in the field of supermicrosurgery, where special microsurgical robotic systems such as the Symani system may offer advantages due to their higher motion scaling. Because this report represents a single-case feasibility study, larger case series and comparative studies will be necessary to develop structured training protocols and to better define the benefits, limitations, and safety of this approach.

CONCLUSIONS

We were able to demonstrate that the da Vinci robotic system can be used to perform entirely robot-assisted microsurgical anastomoses in autologous breast reconstruction and that robot-assisted venous coupler anastomoses are feasible.
