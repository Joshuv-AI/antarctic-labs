Non-Abelian Dirac oscillator in a uniform Yang–Mills background: spin–isospin mixing and singlet–triplet splitting
Abdelmalek Boumali[0.3em] ORCID: 0000-0003-2552-0427, [0.3em] Laboratory of Theoretical, Applied Physics, Echahid Cheikh Larbi Tebessi University, Algeria

Abstract
We investigate a planar Dirac oscillator coupled to a spatially uniform U ⁡ ( 2 ) = U ⁡ ( 1 ) × SU ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathrm{U}(2)=\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2) Yang–Mills background. The gauge configuration, adapted from the Dossa–Avossevou construction, contains an Abelian magnetic field B B , a non-Abelian spatial amplitude β \beta , and a non-Abelian scalar amplitude ρ \rho . Within the Pauli-reduced formulation, the non-Abelian field strength produces a constant operator on ℂ spin 2 ⊗ ℂ iso 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{iso}} . This operator contains a diagonal internal-Zeeman contribution proportional to σ 3 ​ T 3 \sigma^{3}T^{3} and an off-diagonal spin–isospin term proportional to σ 1 ​ T 1 + σ 2 ​ T 2 \sigma^{1}T^{1}+\sigma^{2}T^{2} . Its diagonalization gives a doubly degenerate aligned branch and two mixed branches with eigenvalues λ FM = g 2 ​ β 2 4 ​ m , λ S = − g 2 ​ β ​ ( β − 2 ​ ρ ) 4 ​ m , λ T = − g 2 ​ β ​ ( β + 2 ​ ρ ) 4 ​ m . \lambda_{\mathrm{FM}}=\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{4m},\qquad\lambda_{S}=-\frac{g^{2}\beta(\beta-2\rho)}{4m},\qquad\lambda_{T}=-\frac{g^{2}\beta(\beta+2\rho)}{4m}. Consequently, the aligned internal-Zeeman scale is quadratic in β \beta , whereas the singlet–triplet separation is linear in β ​ ρ \beta\rho . The revised formulation makes the sign conventions explicit, verifies the main limiting cases, distinguishes the Pauli-reduced spectrum from a full first-order Dirac diagonalization, and clarifies the physical meaning of the numerical illustrations.



Keywords:
Dirac oscillator; non-Abelian gauge field; Yang–Mills background; Pauli interaction; spin–isospin coupling; singlet–triplet splitting; graphene bilayers.

1
Introduction

The Dirac oscillator is a paradigmatic exactly solvable relativistic model. It is obtained by introducing a linear non-minimal coupling in the Dirac equation and combines a discrete oscillator-like spectrum with a transparent spin structure
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Its two-dimensional version is especially useful as an analytical benchmark for graphene-like systems, magnetic confinement, and relativistic quantum simulations
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. In the Abelian case, the oscillator is introduced through

\bm{p}\longrightarrow\bm{p}-\mathrm{i}m\omega\beta_{D}\bm{r},

(1.1)

where
m
is the mass,
\omega
is the oscillator frequency, and
\beta_{D}=\gamma^{0}
.

A natural extension is obtained when the oscillator is coupled to a matrix-valued gauge connection. In a non-Abelian theory, a spatially uniform connection may still generate a nonzero field strength because the Yang–Mills tensor contains the commutator of the gauge potentials. This mechanism has no Abelian analogue and can reorganize the internal degeneracy of the Dirac oscillator. In an earlier aligned construction
[
9
]
, a single internal generator produced an internal-Zeeman splitting. The purpose of the present work is to analyze the more general situation in which the background excites several directions of the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
algebra and, as a result, mixes spin and isospin states.

The gauge field used here is the uniform
\mathrm{U}(2)
background introduced by Dossa and Avossevou
[
10
]
. It contains an Abelian magnetic sector and a non-Abelian sector governed by two real constants,
\beta
and
\rho
. The parameter
\beta
enters the spatial components of the non-Abelian vector potential, while
\rho
enters the temporal component. Their simultaneous presence generates off-diagonal terms of the form
\sigma^{1}T^{1}+\sigma^{2}T^{2}
in the Pauli-reduced operator. These terms resolve the internal sector into aligned and mixed branches and lead to a singlet–triplet splitting proportional to
\beta\rho
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
defines the conventions and evaluates the Yang–Mills field strength. Section
3
derives and diagonalizes the spin–isospin operator. Section
4
presents the Pauli-reduced spectrum, checks the main limits, and discusses the figures. Section
5
summarizes the physical interpretation and states the domain of validity of the result.

2
Model, conventions, and field strength

We use natural units,
\hbar=c=1
. Greek indices
\mu,\nu=0,1,2
denote spacetime components, Latin indices
i,j=1,2
denote planar spatial components, and adjoint indices
a,b,c=1,2,3
label the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
directions. The wave function belongs to

\Psi\in\mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{iso}}.

(2.1)

The
\mathrm{SU}(2)
generators are taken in the fundamental representation,

T^{a}=\frac{\tau^{a}}{2},\qquad[T^{a},T^{b}]=\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{abc}T^{c},

(2.2)

where
\tau^{a}
are Pauli matrices acting in isospin space. The planar Dirac matrices act only on the spinor index. We choose the sign convention in which the Pauli-reduced magnetic spin operator is represented by
\sigma^{3}
and the aligned limit reproduces the internal-Zeeman scale of Ref.
[
9
]
.

Following standard non-Abelian gauge-field conventions
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
, the full
\mathrm{U}(2)
connection is written as

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=eA^{(0)}_{\mu}\mathbb{I}_{2}+gA^{a}_{\mu}T^{a},

(2.3)

with Abelian and non-Abelian coupling constants
e
and
g
, respectively. The field strength is

\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}=eF^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\mathbb{I}_{2}+gF^{a}_{\mu\nu}T^{a},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle F^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A^{(0)}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{(0)}_{\mu},

(2.5)

\displaystyle F^{a}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}-g\epsilon^{abc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}.

(2.6)

The last term in Eq. (
2.6
) is the commutator contribution and remains nonzero for constant non-commuting gauge potentials.

The Abelian sector is chosen in the symmetric gauge,

A^{(0)}_{0}=0,\qquad A^{(0)}_{i}=-\frac{B}{2}\epsilon_{ij}x_{j},

(2.7)

which gives

F^{(0)}_{12}=B,\qquad F^{(0)}_{0i}=0.

(2.8)

The non-Abelian sector is specified by the Dossa–Avossevou ansatz
[
10
]
,

A^{a}_{i}=-\beta\epsilon_{ia},\qquad A^{a}_{0}=\rho\delta^{a3}.

(2.9)

Equivalently, the only nonzero components are

A_{1}^{2}=-\beta,\qquad A_{2}^{1}=+\beta,\qquad A_{0}^{3}=\rho.

(2.10)

Since
\beta
and
\rho
are constants, the derivative terms in Eq. (
2.6
) vanish. Direct substitution gives

\displaystyle F^{3}_{12}

\displaystyle=-g\beta^{2},

(2.11)

\displaystyle F^{1}_{01}

\displaystyle=-g\beta\rho,

(2.12)

\displaystyle F^{2}_{02}

\displaystyle=-g\beta\rho,

(2.13)

with all other non-Abelian components equal to zero. Thus
F^{3}_{12}
is a commutator-generated non-Abelian magnetic component quadratic in
\beta
, whereas
F^{1}_{01}
and
F^{2}_{02}
are electric-type non-Abelian components proportional to
\beta\rho
. For
\rho=0
, only the diagonal generator
T^{3}
remains. For
\rho\neq 0
, the additional generators
T^{1}
and
T^{2}
are activated and produce spin–isospin mixing.

3
Pauli-reduced spin–isospin operator

In the Pauli-reduced form of the Dirac-oscillator problem, the non-Abelian field strength contributes a constant matrix acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{iso}}
. With the sign convention stated above, this matrix is

\Lambda_{\mathrm{NA}}=\frac{g^{2}}{2m}\left[\beta^{2}\,\sigma^{3}\otimes T^{3}-\beta\rho\left(\sigma^{1}\otimes T^{1}+\sigma^{2}\otimes T^{2}\right)\right].

(3.1)

The first term is diagonal in the
\sigma^{3}
and
T^{3}
basis and represents the internal-Zeeman contribution. The second term is off-diagonal because it flips spin and isospin simultaneously.

In the ordered basis

\{|+,+\rangle,\ |+,-\rangle,\ |-,+\rangle,\ |-,-\rangle\},

(3.2)

where the first sign denotes the spin projection and the second sign denotes the isospin projection, Eq. (
3.1
) becomes

\Lambda_{\mathrm{NA}}=\frac{g^{2}}{4m}\begin{pmatrix}\beta^{2}&0&0&0\\
0&-\beta^{2}&-2\beta\rho&0\\
0&-2\beta\rho&-\beta^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&\beta^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.3)

The aligned states
|+,+\rangle
and
|-,-\rangle
are eigenstates with the doubly degenerate eigenvalue

\lambda_{\mathrm{FM}}=\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{4m}.

(3.4)

The remaining two states are diagonalized by the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations

\displaystyle|S\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|+,-\rangle-|-,+\rangle\right),

(3.5)

\displaystyle|T_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|+,-\rangle+|-,+\rangle\right),

(3.6)

with eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{S}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g^{2}\beta(\beta-2\rho)}{4m},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{T}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g^{2}\beta(\beta+2\rho)}{4m}.

(3.8)

Here the labels
S
and
T_{0}
refer to the antisymmetric and symmetric spin–isospin combinations in Eq. (
3.5
)–(
3.6
); they should not be interpreted as an exactly degenerate
\mathrm{SU}(2)
multiplet of the full anisotropic operator.

The resulting mixed-branch separation is

\Delta\lambda_{ST}=\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{T}=\frac{g^{2}\beta\rho}{m}.

(3.9)

This is the central mathematical result. The aligned internal-Zeeman effect is quadratic in
\beta
, while the singlet–triplet splitting is linear in the product
\beta\rho
.

4
Spectrum, limiting checks, and numerical illustration

The Abelian magnetic field and the Dirac-oscillator coupling combine into the effective scale

\Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=m\omega+\frac{eB}{2}.

(4.1)

The corresponding planar magnetic Dirac-oscillator backbone is

\left(E_{n,s}^{(0)}\right)^{2}=m^{2}+2\Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(2n+1-s),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,\qquad s=\pm 1.

(4.2)

Within the factorized Pauli-reduced spectral problem, the internal eigenvalues of Eq. (
3.1
) enter as additive mass-squared shifts:

E_{n,s,k}^{(\pm)}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2\Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(2n+1-s)+2m\lambda_{k}},\qquad k\in\{\mathrm{FM},S,T\}.

(4.3)

The radicand must be non-negative. This condition is automatically satisfied in the weak-field regime but can restrict the triplet branch when
\beta\rho>0
is sufficiently large.

The standard limits are recovered consistently. If
\beta=\rho=B=0
, Eq. (
4.3
) reduces to the planar Dirac-oscillator spectrum

E_{n,s}^{(\pm)}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2m\omega(2n+1-s)}.

(4.4)

If
\beta=\rho=\omega=0
, one obtains the relativistic Landau-type spectrum in
(2+1)
dimensions,

E_{n,s}^{(\pm)}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+eB(2n+1-s)}.

(4.5)

For
\beta=\rho=0
, the non-Abelian sector decouples and the magnetic field shifts the oscillator scale according to Eq. (
4.1
), in agreement with the magnetic Dirac-oscillator interpretation of Refs.
[
7
,
8
]
.

The aligned non-Abelian limit is obtained by setting
\rho=0
. Then

\lambda_{S}=\lambda_{T}=-\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{4m},\qquad\lambda_{\mathrm{FM}}=+\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{4m},

(4.6)

so the internal separation is

\lambda_{\mathrm{FM}}-\lambda_{S,T}=\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{2m}\equiv\zeta.

(4.7)

Equation (
4.7
) reproduces the internal-Zeeman scale used in the aligned construction. However, Eq. (
4.3
) is a square-root spectrum of the Pauli-reduced second-order operator, whereas the aligned result of Ref.
[
9
]
was written as a first-order channel shift. The consistent weak-background comparison is therefore

E_{n,s,k}^{(+)}=E_{n,s}^{(0)}+\frac{m\lambda_{k}}{E_{n,s}^{(0)}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda_{k}^{2}),

(4.8)

which gives

E_{n,s,\mathrm{FM}}^{(+)}-E_{n,s,S/T}^{(+)}=\frac{m\zeta}{E_{n,s}^{(0)}}+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{2}).

(4.9)

For
\rho\neq 0
, the exact positive-energy singlet–triplet difference is

\Delta E_{ST}^{(+)}=\sqrt{\left(E_{n,s}^{(0)}\right)^{2}+2m\lambda_{S}}-\sqrt{\left(E_{n,s}^{(0)}\right)^{2}+2m\lambda_{T}},

(4.10)

and its weak-field form is

\Delta E_{ST}^{(+)}\simeq\frac{m(\lambda_{S}-\lambda_{T})}{E_{n,s}^{(0)}}=\frac{g^{2}\beta\rho}{E_{n,s}^{(0)}}.

(4.11)

Thus the leading observable separation is linear in
\beta\rho
and suppressed by the relativistic backbone energy.

5
Discussion and conclusion

The extended Yang–Mills background converts a purely diagonal internal-Zeeman problem into a mixed spin–isospin problem. In the aligned limit, the non-Abelian connection selects the generator
T^{3}
, and the internal sector separates into two independent branches. When
\rho\neq 0
, the temporal component of the connection does not commute with the spatial components. The commutator part of the field strength then generates the operators
\sigma^{1}T^{1}
and
\sigma^{2}T^{2}
, which couple
|+,-\rangle
and
|-,+\rangle
and produce the mixed states in Eqs. (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).

This structure is relevant as an analytically solvable benchmark for systems with matrix-valued gauge potentials. In bilayer graphene, for example, layer or sublattice degrees of freedom may play the role of an internal pseudospin, and effective non-Abelian gauge potentials can arise from interlayer coupling or strain-related mechanisms
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. Similar ideas also appear in cold-atom realizations of synthetic non-Abelian gauge fields
[
19
,
20
]
. The present model should not be read as a microscopic description of a particular device; rather, it identifies the algebraic mechanism by which a uniform matrix-valued background splits internal branches.

The scope of Eq. (
4.3
) should also be emphasized. It is the spectrum of the factorized Pauli-reduced operator. If one starts instead from a fully first-order Dirac Hamiltonian with non-commuting spin-dependent potentials, the full spinor–orbital Hamiltonian must be diagonalized directly or treated perturbatively. The present result is therefore a controlled Pauli-reduced spectral model and a diagnostic of the splitting generated by the Yang–Mills commutator.

In summary, the non-Abelian Dirac oscillator in the uniform
\mathrm{U}(2)
background possesses one aligned internal branch and two mixed branches. The aligned internal-Zeeman scale is

\zeta=g^{2}\beta^{2}/(2m)
, while the mixed-branch splitting is

\Delta\lambda_{ST}=g^{2}\beta\rho/m
. The latter is absent in the purely aligned model and provides the characteristic signature of the off-diagonal Yang–Mills components. Natural extensions include higher isospin representations, non-uniform backgrounds
\beta(\bm{x})
and
\rho(\bm{x})
, and microscopic realizations in graphene-inspired or cold-atom platforms.
