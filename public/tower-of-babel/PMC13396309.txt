Head-to-head comparison of 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT and 68Ga/PSMA-11 PET/CT for primary staging of high-risk prostate cancer: a phase II study
Authors: Aws Saudi, Malin Ståhlgren, Prab Takhar, Pernilla Norberg, Martin Holmbom, Julia Dahl, Mostafa Afshar, Isidro Albert-García, Jon Forsberg, Marcus Ressner, Mathias Elgland, Veronica Sanchez-Rodriguez, Firas Aljabery, Miguel Ochoa-Figueroa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate whether 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT is non-inferior to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT for metastases detection in patients with newly diagnosed high risk prostate cancer (PCa). Patients with newly diagnosed high-risk PCa (age range 50–79 years, mean 66) underwent three imaging modalities: 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT, 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT (contrast-enhanced CT), and 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT. Metastatic involvement of lymph nodes (LN) and bones were recorded. Repeated-measures ANOVAs and post-hoc analyses were used to compare modality performance. Sensitivity and specificity for LN and skeletal lesions were calculated based on PET scans as the reference standard. 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT-based therapy decisions were compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT-based decisions.ResultsSensitivity of 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT for metastasis detection at the patient level was 81% for LN metastasis and 85% for bone metastases, compared with 57% for bone metastases by 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT. There were no false positive findings by 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT; specificity was 100% compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT. At lesion level analysis, 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT had significantly higher detection rate of LN metastases compared with CE-CT (p = 0.026), but a lower detection rate compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT (p = 0.003). Classification of metastasis burden into high volume metastasis (HVM) and low volume metastasis (LVM) was concordant in 85% of cases based on 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT, while conventional imaging agreed in 64% of cases. Therapy decisions based on 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT were concordant with those based on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT in 91% (30/33) of patients.Conclusions99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT demonstrates strong diagnostic capability for metastatic staging and therapy planning in newly diagnosed high-risk PCa, outperforming conventional imaging. While 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT identified more additional lesions, concordance in treatment decisions remained high. Considering its broader accessibility, 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT may represent a practical alternative for primary staging, especially in settings with limited PET/CT availability. The study was approved by the Swedish Ethical Review Authority (approval number 2021 − 01642) and the Swedish Medical Products Agency (EudraCT-number 2021-001059-15). Trial registration date 16th February 2022.Keywords: Prostate cancer, PSMA-targeted radiotracers, Biomarker imaging, Radioligand imaging, PSMA PET/CT, SPECT/CT, Comparative effectiveness, PSMA-RADS, Clinical Trials, Personalized medicine

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) remains the second most common malignancy in men and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide [1]. Despite careful patient selection for curative treatment, whether either radical prostatectomy or radiotherapy, the recurrence rate remains high, up to 30–50% within 10 years [2, 3]. This highlights the frequent downstaging of the disease by current modalities and underscores the need for novel diagnostic tools with improved staging accuracy to optimize treatment strategies and patient outcomes.

Imaging plays a pivotal role in the primary staging of PCa, guiding therapeutic decisions, especially in patients with high-risk disease. Despite advances in conventional imaging such as computed tomography (CT) and 99mTc-HDP single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), a substantial proportion of patients are still inaccurately staged, emphasizing the need for more precise imaging techniques.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) is a highly specific, well-characterized cell-surface glycoprotein that is markedly overexpressed in prostate tumor cells but minimally expressed in normal tissues. Four PSMA-targeting radiopharmaceuticals are currently available for positron emission tomography (PET) imaging: PSMA-11, PSMA-1007, PSMA-I&T, and DCFPYL. Among these, 68Ga-PSMA-11 (also known as [68Ga] Ga-gozetotide) is the most widely used tracer in the European Union [4–6]. Strong evidence indicates that PSMA PET/CT has superior accuracy for primary staging of PCa, compared to conventional imaging modalities, 92% vs. 65% respectively [7]. In direct comparison, PSMA PET outperforms conventional imaging in the detection of extra prostatic extension, seminal vesicle invasion, lymph node and bone metastasis [8]. However, several PSMA-targeting radiotracers for PET require cyclotron access, or a 68Ga generator, making tracer selection dependent on local infrastructure. Each approach has advantages and limitations in terms of diagnostic performance, cost, and availability of cyclotrons/generators [9, 10]. These limitations of PET remain a significant barrier to its widespread use worldwide.

Technetium-99m (99mTc) remains the most widely used radio nuclide in clinical nuclear medicine due to its availability, relatively long half-life, low radiation burden, and lower costs. Recent advances in radio pharmacy have led to the development of Technetium-99m-PSMA-targeted radiopharmaceuticals suitable for SPECT/CT imaging, such as 99mTc-MIP-1404. 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT has demonstrated high detection rates in patients with biochemical recurrence, even at low prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels [11, 12]. In a meta-analysis [99mTc] Tc-PSMA SPECT/CT had a pooled estimated detection rate of 79.7% for primary staging and 75.4% in biochemical recurrence [13]. Comparison of SPECT/CT and PET have shown some promising and comparable results particularly for skeletal metastasis [14, 15]. These findings are worth further examination as SPECT/CT is a cost-efficient imaging method, widely available and accessible in almost every part of the world.

Modern SPECT/CT systems employing Cadmium Zinc Telluride (CZT) detectors have demonstrated substantial improvements in energy resolution, count rate performance, and image contrast compared with conventional SPECT cameras, resulting in enhanced image quality and shorter scan times [16]. The combination of a 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT agent with CZT-based SPECT/CT scanners represents a promising approach that may improve PCa staging compared to conventional radiology and potentially offer diagnostic performance comparable to PET/CT [17].

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the performance of 99mTc-MIP-1404 using a commercially available digital CZT SPECT/CT system, in comparison with the established clinical standards of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT, CECT, and 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT (bone scan), for the detection of lymph node and skeletal metastases in patients with newly diagnosed high-risk PCa.

The secondary objective was to study whether treatment decisions would be changed based on 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT compared to decisions based on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

The study was approved by the Swedish Ethical Review Authority (approval number 2021 − 01642) and the Swedish Medical Products Agency (EudraCT-number 2021-001059-15). Trial registration date 16th February 2022. All patients gave written informed consent for the purpose of anonymized evaluation and publication of their data.

Study population

Patients were prospectively recruited by the Department of Urology at Linköping University Hospital, from January 2023 to December 2024. Eligible participants were men aged > 50 years, prostate cancer diagnosed within the preceding 12 weeks, high-risk prostate cancer was defined by at least one of the following criteria: PSA ≥ 20 ng/mL, clinical stage T3, or ISUP grade group 3–5, and patients deemed suitable for radical prostatectomy or curative radiotherapy.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded if they were receiving ongoing anti-androgen therapy, had a second primary malignancy diagnosed within the previous two years, or presented with tumors containing predominantly sarcomatous, neuroendocrine, or spindle cell components.

Imaging protocols

All patients underwent 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT, 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT, and 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT within a 30-day interval. All imaging procedures followed the current guidelines at the time of imaging [18]. Contrast-enhanced CT (CE-CT) images were acquired as part of the PET/CT studies and were not performed as separate examinations.

99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT bone scan settings

A whole-body scan was performed after intravenous administration of a mean dose of 500 MBq 99mTc-HDP using a VERITON-CT 360° CZT SPECT/CT scanner (Spectrum Dynamics Medical, Caesarea, Israel) for all studies. A CT for attenuation correction (ACCT) was acquired with 120 kV, 15 effective mAs, 10.0 mm slice thickness, 10.0 mm increment, 650 mm DFOV, 23.5 s scan time, and 1 s delay, covering a trans-axial FOV of 1910 mm. SPECT acquisition was performed as a 360° 3D scan, matrix 256 × 256, voxel size 2.46 × 2.46 mm, FOV 314 mm, with a total acquisition time of at least 24 min (1440 s), reducible to 16–22 min (1000–1200 s) depending on body composition. Reconstruction was performed with OSEM (4 iterations × 8 subsets) including CT-based attenuation correction, partial volume correction, and PSF recovery. An intra-iteration convolution filter (cut-off, order 2) and a 3 × 3 median post-filter was applied.

99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT settings

Three to 4 h after the intravenous administration of 8 MBq / kg of 99mTc-MIP-1404 a whole-body scan was performed using a VERITON-CT 360° CZT SPECT/CT scanner for all studies. The radiosynthesis of 99mTc-MIP-1404 was performed as previously described [12]. CT settings (ACCT) were 120 kV, 15 effective mAs, 10.0 mm slice thickness, 10.0 mm increment, 650 mm DFOV, 23.5 s scan time, and 1 s delay (transaxial FOV 1910 mm). SPECT acquisition was performed as a 360° 3D scan, matrix 256 × 256, voxel size 2.46 × 2.46 mm, FOV 314 mm, with acquisition times of 24 min (1440 s) reduced to 16–22 min (1000–1200 s) when possible. Reconstruction was performed using OSEM (8 iterations × 8 subsets) with CT-based attenuation correction and PSF recovery. Filtering included a 3 × 3 median post-filter, while no intra-iteration filter was applied.

68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT settings

Contrast-enhanced PET/CT was performed from skull base to proximal thighs after administration of a mean dose of 220 MBq [^68Ga] Ga-PSMA-11 using a GE Discovery D710 (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA). CT scans were performed with intravenous contrast (70 ml for < 70 kg; 90 ml for > 70 kg) and 80 s delay. The Q.AC (Quantification Achieved Consistently) CT was acquired with 120 kV, 560 mA, 22.8 effective mAs, 3.27 mm interval, 3.75 mm thickness, 700 mm DFOV, and 18.2 s exposure. The CT was acquired from skull base to proximal thighs at 120 kV, 560 mA, 22.8 effective mAs, 1.25 mm slice thickness, 0.625 mm interval, 500 mm DFOV, 18.2 s exposure, with a trans-axial coverage of 1000–1200 mm. PET was acquired in 3D mode with a 256 × 256 matrix (in-plane pixel spacing 2.73 × 2.73 mm), axial FOV 157 mm, 6–8 bed positions at 2 min/bed position (total 12–16 min). Reconstruction used OSEM (3 iterations × 18 subsets) with CT-based attenuation correction, TOF and PSF modeling enabled, followed by a Gaussian 5.5 mm FWHM post-filter (Table 1).

Data and statistical analyses

Statistical power was primarily calculated to include 200 patients anticipated to undergo all scans. Due to logistical and financial difficulties, the inclusion frequency was too slow, hence the study was terminated early, finally including only 33 patients as a pilot study.

As indicated by the power analysis, the statistical power of the study was weak. To retain as much statistical power as possible for each statistical test, missing values were handled through pairwise deletion. Mean differences between the number of detected 68Ga-PSMA-11 and 99mTc-MIP-1404 positive lymph nodes were tested through repeated-measures ANOVAs and post-hoc dependent-samples t-tests. The number of detected lymph nodes and skeletal metastases were not normally distributed (for both measures, skewness > 2.5), however the sampling distribution of mean values for these measures was assumed to be approximately normally distributed given that the sample size exceeded 30 participants [20]. To retain maximal statistical power, unadjusted p-values were used for the post-hoc dependent-samples t-tests.

The sensitivity and specificity were calculated by simple chi-square for 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT and 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT at patient level and not lesion level because of the lack of histological results for true negative LN and bone lesions. Results of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT were considered as the reference standard. Confidence intervals for sensitivity and specificity were measured through the Clopper-Pearson method.

Therapy decisions for patients were taken based on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT, 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT and CE-CT scans, but not on 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT. To avoid interpretation bias, analysis of 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT scans was performed after the study was closed and all therapy decisions had been taken.

To compare 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT based therapy decisions to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT based decisions, cases were classified as either non metastatic disease (N0M0), low-volume metastatic disease (LVM: ≤ 4 metastasis with no visceral metastasis, no bone metastasis outside axial skeleton, inclusive N1M0) and high-volume metastatic disease (HVM: ≥ 4 metastases with at least one outside axial skeleton or visceral metastasis).

Results

Between January 2023 and December 2024, 38 patients were prospectively enrolled. Five patients were excluded due to protocol deviations or incomplete imaging, leaving 33 evaluable participants with high-risk prostate cancer. The baseline characteristics of the study population are summarized in (Table 1).

All patients completed imaging with 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT, 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT, and 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT within a 30-day interval. Contrast-enhanced CT (CE-CT) images were obtained from the PET/CT examinations and were not performed separately.

Detection of lymph node and skeletal metastases

99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT detected metastatic disease in 14 of 33 patients (43%) with a total of 111 lesions (67 lymph node and 44 bone lesions).

68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT detected metastases in 16 of 33 patients (48%) with a total of 407 lesions (262 lymph node and 145 bone lesions). Conventional imaging was markedly less sensitive: 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT identified 11 bone metastases in 4 patients, and contrast-enhanced CT detected 33 lymph node metastases, for a total of 44 lesions.

Diagnostic performance of 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT, 68Ga-PSMA and Conventional imaging

At the patient level, using 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT as the reference standard, 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT demonstrated an overall sensitivity of 81% for lymph nodes detection, both pelvic and distant, and 85.0% for bone metastases detection, while 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT showed 57% sensitivity for bone lesions. Specificity was high across all modalities, reflecting a low false-positive rate.

68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT detected significantly more lymph node metastases than 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT (mean ± SD: 8.19 ± 13.71 vs. 2.09 ± 3.44; p < 0.001, repeated-measures ANOVA). No significant difference was observed for skeletal metastasis detection (p = 0.318). Post hoc analyses confirmed that 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT identified more lymph node lesions than 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT (t [31] = 3.28, p = 0.003) and CECT (t [31] = 3.18, p = 0.003). 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT also detected significantly more lymph node metastases than CECT alone (t [31] = 2.34, p = 0.026) (Tables 2 and 3).

Disease classification and Therapeutic decision

When comparing disease classification across imaging modalities, differences emerged (Table 5). Among 33 patients, 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT identified 17 (51%) as non-metastatic, 7 (21%) as low-volume metastatic (LVM), and 9 (27%) as high-volume metastatic (HVM). 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT yielded similar results with 19 (57%) non-metastatic, 6 (18%) LVM, and 8 (24%) HVM. In contrast, conventional imaging (bone scan and CE-CT) classified 20 patients (61%) as non-metastatic, 11 patients (33%) as low-volume metastatic (LVM), and only 2 patients (6%) as high-volume metastatic (HVM). Overall, 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT staging was concordant with 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CE-CT in 28 of 33 patients (85%), whereas conventional imaging showed concordance in 21 of 33 patients (64%). For 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT three patients were downgraded from HVM on CE-CT PET to LVM on SPECT, and two from LVM on CE-CT PET to non-metastatic on SPECT. Conventional imaging overestimated non-metastatic disease in three patients, LVM in four, and dramatically underestimated HVM in 7 patients.

Discussion

Our findings indicate that 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT detects fewer lymph node and skeletal metastasis compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT. Despite the lower sensitivity of 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT classification of patients into high and low metastatic disease was concordant in 85% compared to 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT. Therapy decision was similar in 91% of cases based on 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT compared to 68 Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT with the caveat regarding the sample size of the study (Fig. 1).

The advent of PSMA-targeted PET/CT has established a new benchmark in prostate cancer imaging, consistently outperforming conventional radiology with substantially higher sensitivity and specificity [8, 21, 22].

Consequently, PSMA PET/CT has been incorporated into current clinical guidelines for patients with prostate cancer, both in the settings of biochemical recurrence and primary staging [18]. PSMA SPECT/CT has also been used for staging PCa, but to a much more limited extent [11, 23, 24]. In a prospective multicenter Phase 2 study for intermediate- and high-risk PCa patients scheduled for radical prostatectomy, 99mTc-MIP-1404 SPECT/CT detected LN metastasis in 33.3% at a patient-level and sensitivity increased to 50.0% when patients with ongoing androgen-deprivation therapy were excluded [24].These values are in a similar range to those of the approved PSMA-PET tracers for detecting pelvic lymph node disease with a sensitivity of 41.5% for 68Ga−ΠΣΜΑ−11 [25, 26]. In line with these results, data from the current study showed that 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT had a patient-level sensitivity of 81% for LN and 85% for bone metastases compared to 68Ga-PSMA-PET/CE-CT as the reference standard. The presence or absence of metastasis by radiological staging is crucial for clinical decisions. Despite that 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT detected far fewer metastases compared to PET, treatment decisions were largely similar in 91% of cases, indicating that 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT detects a smaller number of lesions mainly in patients with high volume of metastases, which had less impact on therapy decisions. In contrast, conventional imaging demonstrated sensitivity of 50% for lymph node metastases and 57% for bone lesions. As a result, conventional imaging underestimated the presence and the burden of metastases in the same group of patients much more than PET/CT and 99mTc-MIP-404 CZT SPECT/CT (Tables 5 and 6).

Consistent with our study, Farkas et al. retrospectively evaluated PSMA SPECT/CT in 28 prostate cancer patients, 24 (85%) with high-risk disease (PSA > 20 ng/mL, Gleason 8–10 [ISUP 4–5], or clinical stage ≥ T2c) prior to definitive treatment. Based on post-treatment follow-up as the reference, PSMA-SPECT/CT demonstrates high reliability and clinical utility as a non-invasive imaging tool for the primary staging of prostate cancer [27]. In a recent study from Australia [28], PSMA SPECT was used to assess the clinical impact on primary staging of PCa and revealed that SPECT is a reliable method and patient management decisions were affected in 41% of the cohort, however, no comparison to PET was performed in this study.

Our results are concordant with the direct comparison of 99mTc-PSMA SPECT/CT and 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT [14, 29, 30], which revealed higher detection of LN and bone metastasis by PET compared to SPECT. Despite the difference in detection of both LN and bone metastasis, our study reveals a significant difference for LN but not for skeletal detection. Our study revealed the clear superiority of PET followed by SPECT and lastly conventional imaging in the detection rate of lesions. The total number of LN lesions detected was 262, 67 and 33 by PET, SPECT and CECT respectively and for bone lesions 144, 44 and 12 by PET, SPECT and HDP SPECT respectively.

A meta-analysis by Wang Q. et al. [31] showed that pooled sensitivity and specificity of [99mTc]Tc-PSMA SPECT/CT for diagnosis and staging of PCa were 0.89 (95% CI, 0.84–0.93) and 0.92 (95% CI, 0.67–0.99), respectively. Our study shows comparable sensitivity of 0.81 (95% CI 0.53–0.95) for LN detection and 0.85 (95% CI 0.42–0.99) for bone metastases. Another two meta-analyses [8, 32] comparing 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT to conventional modalities, bone scans and SPECT /CT, demonstrate the superior performance of PET. Our results also revealed the superiority of 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT compared to 99mTc-MIP-404 CZT SPECT/CT, 99mTc-HDP CZT SPECT/CT and CECT.

The definition of oligometastatic disease or LVM remains uncertain in literature as the number of metastases, localization and multiple radiological modalities used in different studies varies greatly. Our definition of LVM versus HVM, as revealed in statistical analysis above, is consistent with the guidelines of the European Association of Urology and Swedish guidelines for prostate cancer treatment and is based on studies that reveal positive survival outcome by the addition of local palliative radiotherapy of the prostate in patients with LVM [33–35] and survival benefit by triple combination therapy of docetaxel, abiraterone and ADT for patients with HVM [36–39]. Based on this classification our PET-based therapy was compared to SPECT-based therapy, revealing comparable classification to non-metastatic, LVM and HVM in 85% of cases and comparable therapy decisions in 91%. It is important to emphasize that the main difference in the total number of detected lesions was for patients with > than 4 metastases in both modalities and that difference did not impact therapy decisions, except in 3 cases. One of these 3 patients classified as HVM with PET was treated with triple therapy, while SPECT revealed LVM and the patient would not have received docetaxel. In the other 2 out of these 3, 68GaPSMA-11 PET/CT revealed two pelvic LN metastases in each patient, with short-axis diameters of 5–7 mm, and they received palliative radiotherapy + ADT + abiraterone (6 Gy × 6 according to STAMPEDE), while SPECT revealed no metastasis in these 2 cases, and they would have undergone curative therapy. Micrometastases remain a challenging question for radiologists even in the era of PSMA PET/CT [40], and a clinical challenge for urologists and oncologists because of the lack of knowledge regarding how suspicious lymph node metastases on PET/CT can affect therapy decisions and long-term oncological outcomes.

Regarding imaging evaluation, although 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT identified a greater number of lesions in both locoregional and distant locations, both tracers detected pathological lesions PSMA-RADS 4 or 5 in the same locations (locoregional or distant metastasis) within individual patient evaluations. It is important to highlight that despite the higher lesion count with 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT, this did not translate into a meaningful change in treatment decisions in our study cohort (Fig. 2). Our study revealed high specificity of 99mTc−ΜΙΠ−1404 SPECT/CT with no false-positive lesions identified (based on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT as reference standard). This is a considerably important advantage in primary staging where a false-positive result can preclude curative intent treatment (e.g. radical prostatectomy).

An important consideration for adopting 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT is availability and cost. Access to PET/CT imaging and associated radiotracer production is limited, especially in low- and middle-income countries, as well as the high costs of PET scans [14]. In our institution the cost of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT can be up to 3–4 times higher than SPECT/CT. Our results suggest that given its broader availability, 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT holds promise as a valuable tracer for primary staging of PCa. However, larger-scale studies are required to determine whether 99mTc-MIP-1404 and 68Ga-PSMA-11 are truly equivalent in terms of clinical impact. We suggest that 99mTc-MIP-1404 may present a pragmatic and accessible option to extend precise PSMA-based imaging to a broader patient population, particularly in healthcare settings lacking PET/CT infrastructure.

Our study has several limitations. An important consequence of the small sample size is the uncertainty surrounding the observed treatment concordance between modalities. Although treatment recommendations differed in only three patients, this proportion may not accurately reflect the true frequency of clinically meaningful discordance in a larger population. Patient selection was not randomized and was limited to a single institution, which may explain the high rate of metastasis detection by PET/CT and the high overall metastatic burden. This may have affected the sensitivity of SPECT/CT, indicating a risk of selection bias that limits generalizability to the broader population. Furthermore, as histopathological verification and long-term outcome data were unavailable, we could not determine whether lesions detected exclusively by PET/CT represented true disease that would ultimately alter patient prognosis. Therefore, while our findings suggest that 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT may provide clinically useful staging information when PET/CT is unavailable, the present study does not establish equivalence between the two modalities. Larger prospective studies with outcome validation are required before definitive conclusions regarding clinical interchangeability can be drawn.

Conclusion

In newly diagnosed high-risk PCa patients, 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT demonstrates superior diagnostic performance compared with conventional imaging, but inferior to PET. Furthermore, it may serve as a viable alternative for primary staging, particularly in settings with limited PET/CT availability or where timely imaging is needed to guide treatment decisions. Further studies with larger cohorts are needed to strengthen its diagnostic performance and confirm the potential of 99mTc-MIP-1404 CZT SPECT/CT.
