Comparison of Motivic Homotopy Theories
Tianjian Tan

Abstract
We construct a comparison functor from the dual category of motivic homotopy category 𝒮 ​ ℋ \mathcal{SH} to the category of 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant localizing motives Mot loc 𝔸 1 \operatorname{Mot}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}} in the sense of Blumberg, Gepner and Tabuada (with 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariance imposed). We as well construct its non- 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant analogue: a functor from the dual category of Annala-Iwasa-Hoyois’s non- 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant motivic homotopy category ℳ ​ 𝒮 \mathcal{MS} to Mot loc \operatorname{Mot}_{\operatorname{loc}} . After the Barr-Beck argument, these functors factor through categories of modules over a dual version of ( 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant) K-theory spectrum KGL ( 𝔸 1 ) \operatorname{KGL}^{(\mathbb{A}^{1})} . Over a field that admits resolution of singularities, we show that the 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant factored functor is fully-faithful, while the non- 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant one is not in general.

2.1.1
Formal inversion of an object

In this section, we recall the techniques on formally inverting objects in a symmetric monoidal category. In the
\infty
-category setting, this is due to Robalo in
[
22
]
. For a presentably symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
and an object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
, we want to formally inverting
X
in a universal way. More precisely, we are hoping to find a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]
and a strongly monoidal functor

\operatorname{L}^{\mathcal{C}}_{X}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}],

such that
\operatorname{L}^{\mathcal{C}}_{X}
sends
X
to an invertible object and is initial among all such functors.

The category of spectra
\operatorname{Sp}
is an example of this construction: it is the formal inversion of the category of pointed anima
\operatorname{An}_{\ast}
with respect to
(\mathbb{S}^{1},1)
. Recall that besides the universal characterization,
\operatorname{Sp}
admits an explicit model

\operatorname{Sp}=colim(\cdots\xrightarrow{-\otimes\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{An}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{-\otimes\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{An}_{\ast}\xrightarrow{-\otimes\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{An}_{\ast}),

in which the colimit is computed in
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}

X
, the model could be described in a similar way as that of
\operatorname{Sp}
above, which we will call a telescope model. While for more general
X
, the model is due to Annala and Iwasa in
[
2
]
, which is more subtle.

\mathcal{C}
is a small symmetric monoidal category. That is the same data as an object in the category
\operatorname{CAlg(\mathsf{Cat})}

2.1.2
Dualizable objects

Recall that an object
X
in a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is dualizable if there exists some
X^{\vee}\in\mathcal{C}
and (co)evaluation morphisms

ev:X^{\vee}\otimes X\rightarrow 1_{\mathcal{C}}\quad coev:1_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow X^{\vee}\otimes X

satisfy triangle identities. More precisely, that says the following compositions

\begin{gathered}X\xrightarrow{\text{ coev }\otimes id}X\otimes X^{\vee}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\text{ id }\otimes\mathrm{ev}}X\\
X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\text{ id }\otimes\text{ coev }}X^{\vee}\otimes X\otimes X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\text{ ev }\otimes\text{ id }}X^{\vee}\end{gathered}

are homotopic to the identity.

If the ambient symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is closed, and
X
is a dualizable object in
\mathcal{C}
, then the evaluation map uniquely determines these structure maps, hence witnesses the inner hom
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,1)
as a dual object of
X
. And when the dual object exists, it is unique. Throughout the thesis, we mostly handle the dualizable objects in the subcategory
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
of
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
spanned by stable categories and exact functors (and inheriting Lurie tensor product). In particular, if
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
is dualizable, then its dual is
\operatorname{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{C},\operatorname{Sp})
.

A theorem of Lurie gives a full characterization of dualizable objects in
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
. Before stating the result, we recall the definition of compactly assembled categories.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
is strongly compact if for every filtered colimit
Z=colimZ_{i}
, there is a lift rendering the following diagram commutative (up to homotopy):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{colim\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z_{i})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{colim\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z_{i})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}}

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category and
X\in\mathcal{C}
. We say that
X
is strongly compactly exhausted if it is can be written as a sequential colimit

X=colim(X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}\rightarrow\cdots)

where all the transition maps
X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
are strongly compact morphisms.

Definition 2.11
.

A presentable category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be compactly assembled if it is generated under colimits by strongly compactly exhausted objects.

Proposition 2.12
.

(
[
18
, Proposition D.7.3.1]
)
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable and presentable category, the following are equivalent

•

\mathcal{C}
is dualizable in
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
;

•

\mathcal{C}
is compactly assembled;

•

\mathcal{C}
is a retract in
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
of some compactly generated category.

The right notion of functors between compactly assembled categories is compactly assembled functors. In the stable setting, they could be characterized as

•

strongly left adjoint functors, that is, left adjoint functors such that the right adjoint admits further a right adjoint, or,

•

functors that preserve strongly compact morphisms.

In the stable compactly generated setting, the equivalent conditions above are further equivalent to

•

compactness-preserving functors.

We denote by
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}
the (non-full) subcategory of
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
spanned by stable, compactly assembled categories and compactly assembled functors between them. This category in fact inherits a symmetric monoidal structure from

, which is a little subtle since it is not a full subcategory. We refer to

Denote by

the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}
spanned by compactly generated categories, and denote by
\mathsf{Cat}^{\operatorname{perf}}
the category spanned by stable, idempotent complete categories and exact functors. We have the following well-known result:

Lemma 2.13
.

The Ind-completion functor

induces an equivalence of

2.1.3
A compatibility result of dual operation and formal inversion

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})
and
X\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
. The main goal of this section is to show that taking dual commutes with formal inversion. Note first that we have the following commutative diagram by the combination of the last lemma in the last section and
[
14
, Construction 2.9.21]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Cat}^{\operatorname{perf}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{cg}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Cat}^{\operatorname{perf}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{cg}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ind}(-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{op}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ind}(-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where all functors are symmetric monoidal. For a compactly assembled category
\mathcal{C}
, the vertical functor
(-)^{\vee}
sends it to
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\simeq\operatorname{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{C},\operatorname{Sp})
, while for a strongly left adjoint functor

,

is given by the precomposition with the right adjoint
R
of

. Equivalently, it is the left adjoint of the functor given by precomposite with
L
. In particular, for
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}
and
X\in\mathcal{C}
compact, the colimits-preserving functor

\operatorname{Sp}\xrightarrow{X}\mathcal{C}

is strongly left adjoint, hence dualizes to a functor

\operatorname{Sp}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\vee}.

Since
\operatorname{Sp}\simeq\operatorname{Sp}^{\vee}
, we precomposite it with the functor above, and the composition

\operatorname{Sp}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\vee}

is determined by a single element in
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
, which we denote by

.

The first observation is that the formal inversion of a compactly assembled (resp. compactly generated) category with respect to a compact object is still compactly assembled (resp. compactly generated). We can see this from the c-spectra model of formal inversion. In fact, it suffices to show that the localization functor
\operatorname{L}
preserves weakly compactly exhaustible objects (resp. compact objects). This is true since the right adjoint preserves filtered colimits, as proved in
[
2
, Lemma 1.5.2]
.

Combining all the discussions so far, the question whether taking dual commutes with formal inversion makes sense. We give a positive answer:

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable, compactly assembled, presentably symmetric monoidal category. Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
be a compact object. Then

\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]^{\vee}

holds in
\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}]/}
. A fortiori, they are the same as presentably symmetric monoidal category.

Proof.

Note first that
\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]^{\vee}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
commutative algebra via the dual of the canonical monoidal functor
\operatorname{L}_{X}^{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]
. Again,
\operatorname{L}_{X}^{\mathcal{C}}
is strongly left adjoint by
[
2
, Lemma 1.5.2]
. This ring map factors through
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}]
by the next lemma and the universal property of formal inversion. On the other hand, since symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca}
is restricted from
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{st}
, Lemma
2.8
remains valid in compactly assembled world. The Yoneda lemma then applies:

\displaystyle\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}]/}(\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}],\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}^{\vee}/}(\mathcal{C}^{\vee},\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}/}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}^{\vee})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]/}(\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}],\mathcal{D}^{\vee})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})^{X}_{\mathcal{C}/}(\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}],\mathcal{D}^{\vee})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})^{X^{dual}}_{\mathcal{C}^{\vee}/}(\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]^{\vee},\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})_{\mathcal{C}^{\vee}[(X^{dual})^{-1}]/}(\mathcal{C}[X^{-1}]^{\vee},\mathcal{D}),

where the third and the fifth isomorphisms follow again from the next lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{ca})
with

a compact object in
\mathcal{C}
. Let

dualizes to
-\otimes X^{dual}:\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\vee}.

Proof.

Since

preserves compact morphisms, hence is strongly left adjoint.

For a presentable category
\mathcal{D}
, we denote by

the natural map from
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
to
\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D}
. We consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{E},\operatorname{Sp})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E},\operatorname{Sp})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{E},\operatorname{Sp})},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\boxtimes X^{dual}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces mult^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{id}\boxtimes X)^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces mult^{\ast}}

where

is the monoidal structure map on

induced by that on

On the other hand, we claim that

The left adjoint of that is the dual of

2.1.4
Factorization of adjunction pair through category of modules

This section follows from
[
8
]
. Let
\gamma^{\ast}:\mathcal{C}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}:\gamma_{\ast}
be an adjunction between symmetric monoidal categories and we assume the left adjoint functor
\gamma^{\ast}
to be strongly symmetric monoidal, then the adjunction pair factors through the following commutative diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes-}(\mathcal{C})},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\gamma^{\ast})^{enh}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

in which the notation
\operatorname{LMod}
means the category of modules over a monad. Each of the arrows above admits a right adjoint, and if we replace all arrows by their right adjoints, the diagram remains commutative. In the following part of this section, we explain the diagram and give conditions for

,
\alpha
to be equivalences.

The factorization through
\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{C})
is true even without any symmetric monoidal structure. The idea is that the universal property of the monad
\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}
provides a factorization

\mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{(\gamma_{\ast})^{enh}}\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{U}}\mathcal{C}.

Informally speaking,
(\gamma_{\ast})^{enh}
sends

to
\gamma_{\ast}(d)
, and the monad
\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}
acts on
\gamma_{\ast}(d)
via the counit of the adjunction. We refer to
[
17
, proposition 4.7.3.3]
for a detailed discussion. Moreover, the two maps involved above are both right adjoints. And we denote by
(\gamma^{\ast})^{enh}
the left adjoint of the first functor. The Lurie-Barr-Beck theorem gives an necessary and sufficient condition for
(\gamma^{\ast})^{enh}
to be an equivalence:

Lemma 2.17
.

(
[
17
, Theorem 4.7.3.5]
)
Let
\gamma^{\ast}:\mathcal{C}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}:\gamma_{\ast}
be an adjunction. The following are equivalent:

1.

The functors
(\gamma_{\ast})^{enh}
and
(\gamma^{\ast})^{enh}
are mutually inverse.

2.

The functor
\gamma_{\ast}
satisfies the following conditions:

•

(\gamma_{\ast})^{enh}
is conservative;

•

For all
\gamma_{\ast}
-split simplicial object
X
in
\mathcal{D}
,
X
admits a colimit in
\mathcal{D}
. Furthermore,
\gamma_{\ast}
preserves and reflects
X
-indexed colimits.

The factorization through
\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes-}(\mathcal{C})
is induced by a map of monads

a:\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes-\rightarrow\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}.

More precisely,
a
is the composite of the following maps of monads:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes-

\displaystyle\simeq\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes\operatorname{id}(-)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta}\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(-)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mu}\gamma_{\ast}(\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes\gamma^{\ast}(-))

\displaystyle\simeq\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast},

where

is given by the lax monoidal structure on
\gamma_{\ast}
. This

induces a functor
\alpha_{\ast}:\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\operatorname{LMod}_{\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}(1)\otimes-}(\mathcal{C})

becomes an equivalence.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable, compactly generated, presentably monoidal category. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is rigidly generated if
\mathcal{C}
is generated under filtered colimits by (a small set of) compact, dualizable objects.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\gamma^{\ast}:\mathcal{C}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}:\gamma_{\ast}
be an adjunction of symmetric monoidal categories. Suppose that
\gamma^{\ast}
is monoidal,
\mathcal{C}
is rigidly generated,
\mathcal{D}
has compact unit, and colimits distribute over tensor product in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, then
\alpha
is fully faithful. Moreover, if
\gamma_{\ast}
is conservative, then
\alpha
is an equivalence.

Proof.

We give a sketch of proof. The idea follows
[
4
, Appendix A]
.

It suffices to show the projection formula holds. That is, for all
X
and
Y
,

p:\gamma_{\ast}Y\otimes X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta}\gamma_{\ast}Y\otimes\gamma_{\ast}\gamma^{\ast}X\xrightarrow{\mu}\gamma_{\ast}(Y\otimes\gamma^{\ast}X)

is an isomorphism. This is true for dualizable

, since by the Yoneda lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(Z,\gamma_{\ast}Y\otimes X)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(Z\otimes X^{\vee},\gamma_{\ast}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{D}(\gamma^{\ast}Z\otimes\gamma^{\ast}(X^{\vee}),Y)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{D}(\gamma^{\ast}Z\otimes(\gamma^{\ast}X)^{\vee},Y)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{D}(\gamma^{\ast}Z,Y\otimes\gamma^{\ast}X)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(Z,\gamma_{\ast}(Y\otimes\gamma^{\ast}X)).

Moreover, by a diagram chasing, we see that the above isomorphism is induced precisely by

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
commute with tensor product and
\mathcal{C}
is rigidly generated, it reduces to show that

preserves colimits. But this is true since
\gamma^{\ast}
sends compact dualizable objects (which form a family of generators) to compact objects by the compactness of unit in
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Finally, we have a general equivalence between categories of modules over an algebra and that over a monad of a certain form:

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category and

-algebra in
\mathcal{C}

\beta:\operatorname{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}_{A\otimes-}(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

Apply
[
17

to the forgetful functor
\operatorname{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
.∎

and the compact generation of

We begin by recalling some topos theory. Let
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}

on

A presheaf

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Mot}(S)_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{perf}_{X}),1)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{KH}(\operatorname{perf}_{X}^{op}).

And when
\mathcal{SH}
is rigidly generated, for instance, over a field
k
and after inverting the exponential characteristic
e
,
\Phi_{\ast}(1)
is identified with
\operatorname{KGL}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
via the canonical equivalence witnessing its self-duality (see 9.2.1 of
[
9
]
)

\displaystyle\dagger:\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]}(1,X\otimes-).

To see this, note first that for smooth projective scheme
X
,
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X
is dualizable in
\mathcal{SH}
as discussed in
[
21
]
, hence it remains so in the dual category
\mathcal{SH}^{\vee}
. We have the following equivalences of mapping spectra:

\displaystyle\mathcal{SH}^{\vee}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X^{dual})^{\vee},\dagger\operatorname{KGL}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{SH}(\dagger(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X^{dual})^{\vee},\operatorname{KGL}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{SH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X,\operatorname{KGL}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{KH}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{SH}^{\vee}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X^{dual})^{\vee},\Phi_{\ast}(1).

The second isomorphism above follows a combination of Remark
2.16
and
[
9
, Lemma 9.2.4]
. Now since
\mathcal{SH}^{\vee}
is rigidly generated, it is generated under suspensions, loops, finite colimits and filtered colimits by objects of the form

, showing that
\dagger\operatorname{KGL}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\simeq\Phi_{\ast}(1)
by Yoneda lemma.

The main result of this section is the fully-faithfulness of
\widetilde{\Phi}
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Over a field
k
and after inverting the exponential characteristic
e
, the functor

\widetilde{\Phi}:\operatorname{Mod}_{\Phi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})\rightarrow\operatorname{Mot}(k)^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\operatorname{loc}}[\frac{1}{e}]

is fully faithful on the level of mapping spectra.

Proof.

In the proof, when we use the notation
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X
, we actually mean the further localization inverting
e
of
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X\in\mathcal{SH}(k)
.

We first claim that this is true for objects of the form
\Phi_{\ast}(1)\otimes(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Z)^{dual}
with
Z
smooth and projective. Such assumptions on
Z
provide sufficient dualizibility:

•

(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Z)^{dual}
is dualizable in
\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}
;

•

\operatorname{perf}_{Z}
is saturated, that is, dualizable in the ambient symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{cg}\simeq\mathsf{Cat}^{\operatorname{perf}}
. This follows
[
20
, Proposition 3.31]
: For
k
-scheme
Z
of finite type and separated,
\operatorname{perf}_{Z}
is smooth (resp. proper) if and only if
Z
is smooth (resp. proper).

Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{\Phi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})(\Phi_{\ast}(1)\otimes(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{dual},\Phi_{\ast}(1)\otimes(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y)^{dual})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{dual},\Phi_{\ast}(1)\otimes(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y)^{dual})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{dual}\otimes((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y)^{dual})^{\vee},\Phi_{\ast}(1))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mot}(k)^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Phi^{\ast}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{dual}\otimes((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y)^{dual})^{\vee}),1)[\frac{1}{e}]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mot}(k)^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Phi^{\ast}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{dual}\otimes\Phi^{\ast}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y)^{dual})^{\vee},1)[\frac{1}{e}]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mot}(k)^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathcal{U}\operatorname{perf}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{perf}_{Y}^{op}),1)[\frac{1}{e}]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mot}(k)^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathcal{U}\operatorname{perf}_{X},\mathcal{U}\operatorname{perf}_{Y})[\frac{1}{e}].

On the other hand, it suffices to prove this. By rigid generation,
\Phi_{\ast}(1)\otimes(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Z)^{dual}
is dualizable in
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Phi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{SH}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})
and generates the whole category under suspensions, loops, finite colimits and filtered colimits. Applying
[
22
, Proposition 2.7]
, it reduces to show that
\widetilde{\Phi}
preserves compact objects, which is true by the compactness of unit in
\operatorname{Mot}(k)_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}[\frac{1}{e}]
.
∎

4.2
Fully-faithfulness of the factored functor, non-
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant case

One of the differences between
\mathcal{MS}
and
\mathcal{SH}
is that, even over a field
k
and after inverting the exponential characteristic,
\mathcal{MS}
fails to be rigidly generated. In particular, the categories of modules
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Psi_{\ast}\Psi^{\ast}}(\mathcal{MS}^{\vee})
and
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Psi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{MS}^{\vee})
are different in general.

We will show that the functor

\widetilde{\Psi}:\operatorname{Mod}_{\Psi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{MS}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})\rightarrow\operatorname{Mot}(k)_{\operatorname{loc}}[\frac{1}{e}]

induced as in Section
2.1.4
is not always fully faithful. In fact, as we will see, over a countable field,
\pi_{0}
of the mapping spectrum between the affine line and any non-zero ring
R
is always countable in the left hand side, while it is always uncountable in
\operatorname{Mot}_{\operatorname{loc}}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
k
be a countable field and
e
be the exponential characteristic of
k
. Then the full subcategory of
\mathcal{MS}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}
spanned by compact objects is countable, in the sense that the
\pi_{0}
of all the mapping spectra are countable.

Proof.

It suffices to show for
\mathcal{MS}(k)
. We first show that
\mathcal{P}sh_{\operatorname{Nis},\operatorname{ebu}}(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k))
has countable subcategory of compact objects. By
[
19
, Theorem I.3.3]
and
[
3
, Proposition 5.6]
, we see that
\mathcal{P}sh_{\operatorname{Nis},\operatorname{ebu}}(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k))
is the Verdier quotient of

\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}sh(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k)),

where
\mathcal{A}
is spanned by, for all Nisnevich squares and elementary blowup squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}

the cofibers of

j(U)\amalg_{j(W)}j(V)\rightarrow j(X).

Moreover, the mapping spectra in
\mathcal{P}sh_{\operatorname{Nis},\operatorname{ebu}}(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k))
between
\operatorname{L}\circ j(X)
and
\operatorname{L}\circ j(Y)
is computed by

colim_{Z\in\mathcal{A}_{/Y}}\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{P}sh(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k))}(j(X),\operatorname{cofib}(Z\rightarrow j(Y))),

hence is countable as a countable colimit of countable sets.

To pass to
\mathcal{MS}(k)
, we consider the c-spectra model. It directly follows Definition
2.6
that
\operatorname{Sp}^{lax}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{P}sh_{\operatorname{Nis},\operatorname{ebu}}(Sm^{\operatorname{fp}}(k)))
has countable mapping spectra between compacts. Applying
[
19
, Theorem I.3.3]
again shows that
\mathcal{MS}(k)
has countable mapping spectra between compacts, as desired.
∎

Since the mapping spectra between representables in
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Psi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{MS}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})
is computed in
\mathcal{MS}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee}
up to tensoring the target with the
\Psi_{\ast}(1)
, and the latter one is a
\omega_{1}
-compact object as a dual version of
\operatorname{KGL}
, we conclude that the mapping spectra between representables in
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Psi_{\ast}(1)}(\mathcal{MS}(k)[\frac{1}{e}]^{\vee})
is countable as a
\omega_{1}
-small colimit of countable sets.

On the other hand, for a non-zero
k
-algebra
R
,
[
7
, Theorem 9.1]
shows that

\mathbb{W}(R)\simeq\pi_{0}\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Mot}_{\operatorname{loc}}}(\mathcal{U}\operatorname{perf}_{k[t]},\mathcal{U}\operatorname{perf}_{R}),

where
\mathbb{W}(R)
is the ring of big Witt vectors. The underlying set of
\mathbb{W}(R)
is
(1+tR[[t]])^{\times}
. In particular, it is uncountable.
