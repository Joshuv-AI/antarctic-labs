Simple unital Jordan superalgebras
Ivan Shestakov, Efim Zelmanov

Abstract
We prove that a simple unital Jordan superalgebra of arbitrary dimension belongs to the list of known simple unital superalgebras or lies in a certain proper subvariety.

Introduction

Throughout the paper, if otherwise is not stated, all algebras are considered over a field
F
of characteristic
\neq 2
.

A (linear )
Jordan algebra
is a vector space
J
with a binary bilinear operation
xy
satisfying the identities

\displaystyle xy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yx,

\displaystyle(x^{2}y)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2}(yx).

A canonical example of a Jordan algebra is the algebra
A^{(+)}
obtained from an associative algebra
A
by introducing the new product
a\cdot b=\tfrac{1}{2}(ab+ba)
. Algebras of this type and their subalgebras are called
special
.

An important example is a
Hermitian algebra

H(A,*)
of self-adjoint elements in an associative algebra
A
with involution
*
. They include algebras of type
A^{(+)}
since
A^{(+)}\cong H(B,*)
, where
B=A\oplus A^{\circ}
with
A^{\circ}
anti-isomorphic to
A
and
(a,b)^{*}=(b,a)
.

The Jordan algebra
H(A,*)
is simple if and only if
(A,*)
is
*
-simple.

Another important algebra is an
algebra of a bilinear form

J(V,f)
or
Jordan Clifford algebra
. Let
V
be a vector space with a symmetric bilinear form
f:V\times V\rightarrow F
. Consider the vector space direct sum

J(V,f)=F\cdot 1\oplus V
and define a product on it by setting
1=1_{J(V,f)}
and for
u,v\in V

\displaystyle{u\cdot v=f(u,v)1}.

The algebra
J(V,f)
is simple
if and only if the form
f
is nondegenerate and
\dim_{F}V>1
.

Clearly, it lies in the associative Clifford algebra
Cl(V,f)
of the bilinear form
f
, hence it is special.

Not all Jordan algebras are special; the most important example of a non-special (or
exceptional
) Jordan algebra is the algebra
H(\mathbf{O}_{3})
of hermitian
3\times 3
-matrices over the algebra
\mathbf{O}
of octonions (with respect to the symmetric product
a\cdot b
).

The algebra
H(\mathbf{O}_{3})
is simple. Its exceptionality was proved in 1934 by A.Albert. This algebra and its forms are called now
Albert algebras
.

It was proved in
[
30
]
that

Every simple Jordan algebra is either special or is isomorphic to a form of the algebra
H(\mathbf{O}_{3})
(an
Albert algebra
).

The objective of our paper are
Jordan superalgebras
. They were introduced in 1970-s by V.Kac
[

]
and I.Kaplansky
[

. The class is rich in interesting examples and has deep connections to Lie superalgebras and to Mathematical Physics (see

Consider the definition and main examples of Jordan superalgebras.

Let
V
be a vector space of countable dimension and let
G(V)
be the Grassmann (or exterior) algebra over
V
. If
\{e_{i},\ i\geq 1\}
is a basis of the space
V
, then the algebra
G(V)
is presented by generators
e_{i},\ i\geq 1
, and relations
e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=0,\ i.j\geq 1
. The set of ordered products
1,\,e_{i},\,\ldots,e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}},\ i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k},
is a basis of
G(V)
.

The algebra
G(V)
is
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-graded,
G(V)=G(V)_{\bar{0}}+G(V)_{\bar{1}}
. The even part
G(V)_{\bar{0}}
is the span of products of even length:
1,e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{2k}},\ i_{1}<\cdots<i_{2k}
, whereas
G(V)_{\bar{1}}
is the space of all products of odd length
e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{2k+1}},\ i_{1}<\cdots<i_{2k+1}
.

By a
superalgebra
we mean an algebra

\displaystyle A=A_{\bar{0}}+A_{\bar{1}}

that is
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-graded. The elements from
A_{\bar{0}}
are usually called
even
and the elements from
A_{\bar{1}}
are called
odd
.

Given a variety
\mathcal{V}
of algebras defined by homogeneous identities (see
[
7
,
39
]
) we say that a superalgebra
A=A_{\bar{0}}+A_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathcal{V}
-superalgebra if its
Grassmann envelope

\displaystyle G(A)=A_{\bar{0}}\otimes G(V)_{\bar{0}}+A_{\bar{1}}\otimes G(V)_{\bar{1}}

belongs to the variety
\mathcal{V}
.

Knowing the identities defining the variety
\mathcal{V}
, one can easily write down the
superidentities
defining the
\mathcal{V}
-superalgebras.
Thus an associative superalgebra is just a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-graded associative algebra. A
commutative superalgebra
is a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-graded algebra
A=A_{\bar{0}}+A_{\bar{1}}
that satisfies
the superidentity

\displaystyle xy=(-1)^{\bar{x}\bar{y}}yx,

where for an element
a\in A_{\bar{0}}\cup A_{\bar{1}}
the symbol
\bar{a}
denotes its parity,
\bar{a}=0
or 1.
In particular, the Grassmann algebra is a commutative superalgebra.

A superalgebra

is a

if it satisfies the superidentities

\displaystyle xy=(-1)^{\bar{x}\bar{y}}yx,

\displaystyle((xy)z)t+(-1)^{\bar{y}\bar{z}+\bar{y}\bar{t}+\bar{z}\bar{t}}((xt)z)y+(-1)^{\bar{z}\bar{t}}x((yt)z)

\displaystyle=(xy)(zt)+(-1)^{\bar{y}\bar{z}}(xz)(yt)+(-1)^{\bar{t}(\bar{y}+\bar{z})}(xt)(yz).

(1.2)

Consider the main examples of Jordan superalgebras.
Similarly to the case of algebras, we have the following three classes of superalgebras:

Example 1.

Let

be an associative superalgebra. The new operation

\displaystyle a\circ b=\tfrac{1}{2}(ab+(-1)^{\bar{a}\bar{b}}ba)

.

Example 2.
(

is a Jordan subsuperalgebra of

.

Example 3.
(

-graded vector space over

We refer to this superalgebra as

Now let us discuss infinite dimensional simple Jordan algebras and superalgebras.

We will formulate a slightly modified conjecture on classification of simple (finite or infinite dimensional) Jordan superalgebras that is due to N. Cantarini and V. Kac

Conjecture 1.1
.

A simple Jordan superalgebra over a field
F
of zero characteristic (with a nonzero odd part) is isomorphic to one of the following superalgebras:

I)

H(A,*)
, where
(A,*)
is a
*
-simple associative superalgebra with a superinvolution
*
and with a noncommutative even part (see
[

5

);

II)

a Jordan superalgebra of a superform or
k_{3}
, or
D_{t}
, or
K_{10}
over some extension of the ground field
F
;

III)

a Kantor double
Kan\,(A,[,])
or a twisted Kantor double.

IV)

a Jordan Cheng-Kac superalgebra
JCK(A,d)
or a twisted subsuperalgebra of
JCK(A,d)
.

N. Cantarini and V. Kac
[
1
]
proved the Conjecture for linearly compact simple Jordan superalgebras. V. Kac, C. Martínez and E. Zelmanov
[
10
]
proved the Conjecture for superconformal Jordan algebras, i.e. graded Jordan superalgebras
J=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{Z}}J_{i}
that are simple and have all dimensions
\dim_{F}J_{i}
uniformarly bounded.

A Jordan superalgebra
J
is called
special
if it is embeddable in
A^{(+)}
for some associative superalgebra
A
. A superalgebra is
i
-special
if it is a homomorphic image of a special superalgebra. Equivalently, a superalgebra is
i
-special if it satisfies all identities that are satisfied by all special Jordan superalgebras.

It is easy to see that examples of types I – III, exept for
K_{10}
, are special. Yu. Medvedev and E. Zelmanov
[
21
]
proved that the superalgebra
K_{10}
is not even
i
-special.
K. McCrimmon
[
19
]
(see also
[
25
]
) proved that a Kantor double
K(A,[,])
of a bracket of vector type is special. He also proved that the Kantor double of the classical Poisson bracket is not special. On the other hand, I. Shestakov
[
27
,
17
]
showed that Kantor doubles are always
i
-special. C. Martinez, I. Shestakov and E. Zelmanov
[
17
]
showed that Jordan Cheng-Kac superalgebras are special.

A weaker form of the above Conjecture can be formulated as follows:

A simple Jordan superalgebra over a field of zero characteristic with a nonzero odd part is either
{i}
-special or isomorphic to
K_{10}
.

In
[
33
]
the second author constructed a proper variety of Jordan algebras
Var
satisfying the ideal of identities
X
. In an arbitrary Jordan superalgebra
J
the ideal
X(J)
has bounded multiplicative length (see section 1). Among finite dimensional simple Jordan algebras only Jordan algebras of symmetric bilinear form
lie in
Var
.

In this paper we show that counterexamples to the Conjecture, if any, lie in the variety
Var
.

Main Theorem

Let
J
be a simple unital Jordan superalgebra over a field of zero characteristic. Then either
J
is isomorphic to one of the superalgebras I – V above or
J\in Var
.

We recall that in a recent paper
[
28
]
we classified simple Jordan superalgebras with the even part being a sum of Jordan algebras of nondegenerate symmetric forms. The proof of lemma 4.4 in
[
28
]
contains a mistake; lemmas 4.4 and 4.5 of
[
28
]
should be substituted by lemma
6.3
below.

The main technical assertion of the paper refers to a wider class of primitive Jordan superalgebras of finite multiplicative length.

Main Proposition

Let
J
be a unital primitive Jordan superalgebra with nonzero odd part of finite multiplicative length over an algebraically closed field of zero characteristic. Then either
J
is
i
-special or
J\cong K_{10}
.

\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m}\}=\{i,j\}
;

(b) If
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{2m+1}\leq 2n
then
a_{ij}D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots D(x,v_{i_{2m+1}})\in M\cdot L
unless
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m+1}\}=\{i\}
or
\{j\}
.

Proof.
(a)
a_{ij}D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots D(x,v_{i_{2m}})\sim v_{i}v_{j}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{2m}}
.
Since the length of this product is even, it can not be equal to
\pm v_{k},\ 1\leq k\leq 2n.
Since the length is less then
2n
, it can not be equal to
v_{1}\cdots v_{2n}
.
Hence unless
v_{i}v_{j}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{2m}}=\pm 1
, the element
a_{ij}D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots D(x,v_{i_{2m}})
lies in
L
. This completes the proof of the assertion (a).

If
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m+1}\}\neq\{i\}
or
\{j\}
, then there exists
k\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m+1}\},\ k\not\in\{i,j\}
. We have

\displaystyle a_{ij}D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots D(x,v_{i_{2m+1}})=\pm a^{\prime}D(x,v_{k}),

where
a^{\prime}=a_{ij}D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots\widehat{D(x,v_{k})}\cdots D(x,v_{i_{2m+1}})
.
We have

a^{\prime}\sim v_{i}v_{j}v_{i_{1}}\cdots\widehat{v_{k}}\cdots v_{i_{2m+1}},

hence
a^{\prime}
commutes with
v_{k}
. If
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m+1}\}\setminus\{v_{k}\}\neq\{i,j\}
then
a^{\prime}\in L
by (a). We have

\displaystyle a^{\prime}D(x,v_{k})=-(a^{\prime}\cdot v_{k})\cdot x\in M\cdot L.

Let
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m+1}\}=\{i,j,k\}
. Then
a^{\prime}=1+b,\ b\in L,\,b\sim 1
. Again

a^{\prime}D(x,v_{k})=bD(x,v_{k})=-(bv_{k})x\in M\cdot L
.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

\Box

Lemma 7.10
.

\displaystyle a_{12}R(a_{34})\cdots R(a_{2t-1,2t})D(x,v_{1})\cdots D(x,v_{t})=1\ (mod\ L).

Proof.
For a subset
\pi=\{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq 2t\}
denote
D_{\pi}=D(x,v_{i_{1}})\cdots D(x,v_{i_{m}})
. Then

\displaystyle a_{12}R(a_{34})\cdots R(a_{2t-1,2t})D(x,v_{1})\cdots D(x,v_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum(a_{12}D_{\pi_{1}})R(a_{34}D_{\pi_{2}})\cdots R(a_{2t-1,2t}D_{\pi_{t}}),

where the summation is done over decompositions
\pi_{1}\dot{\cup}\pi_{2}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}\pi_{d}=\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
. By Lemma
7.9

a_{2t-1,2t}D_{\pi_{t}}\in L
or
a_{2t-1,2t}D_{\pi_{t}}\in M\cdot L
or
\pi_{t}=\{2t-1,2t\}
.
In the first two cases

(a_{12}D_{\pi_{1}})R(a_{34}D_{\pi_{2}})\cdots R(a_{2t-1,2t}D_{\pi_{t}})\in L.

In the third case this element is equal to

(a_{12}D_{\pi_{1}})R(a_{34}D_{\pi_{2}})\cdots R(a_{2t-3,2t-2}D_{\pi_{t-1}})\ (mod\ L).

We repeat the above argument for the element
a_{2t-3,2t-2}D_{\pi_{t-1}}
and so on. After
t-1
steps we get

\displaystyle a_{12}R(a_{34})\cdots R(a_{2t-1,2t})D(x,v_{1})\cdots D(x,v_{t})

\displaystyle\equiv a_{12}D(x,v_{1})D(x,v_{2})\equiv 1\ (mod\ L).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
