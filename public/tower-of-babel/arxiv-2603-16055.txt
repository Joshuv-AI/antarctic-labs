Strategies in POMDPs with Stage Duration
Ivan Novikov, Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne

Abstract
Partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) with stage duration provide a framework for approximating continuous-time behavior by scaling transition probabilities with a stage duration parameter h ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] h\in(0,1] . While previous literature has primarily focused on the limit of the discounted value as the stage duration h h vanishes, this paper investigates the global behavior of the asymptotic value, V ⁡ ( h ) V(h) , across varying stage durations. Our main result demonstrates that any strategy in a POMDP with stage duration h h can be mimicked in the base POMDP ( h = 1 h=1 ). Specifically, we provide an explicit construction showing that for any strategy in the POMDP with stage duration h h , there exists a strategy in the base POMDP that secures the same asymptotic payoff. As a consequence of this theorem, we establish that the value function V ⁡ ( h ) V(h) is nondecreasing with respect to h h , and that the continuous-time limit lim h → 0 V ⁡ ( h ) \lim_{h\to 0}V(h) exists.

Introduction

Partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs)
were introduced by
Drake 1962
. In this paper, we consider the special case where the signal depends only on the current state and is deterministic. Such a POMDP proceeds in discrete time as follows. In the beginning, the initial state is drawn according to some probability distribution, after which a deterministic signal is generated based on that state. At each stage, the decision maker observes the signal, recalls all previous actions and signals, and chooses an action. This action determines the stage payoff. The next state is then drawn randomly, conditional on the current state and action. The goal of the decision maker is to maximize his overall payoff.

POMDPs can be generalized to the case of two decision makers with opposite interests. In this case, we refer to them as zero-sum stochastic games. In this framework, we can distinguish between the cases of full state observation, where the state is fully observed by both decision makers (introduced by
Shapley 1953
); public signals, where the signal is seen by both decision makers (see, e.g.,
Ziliotto 2016
); and private signals, where each decision maker observes his own signal (see, e.g.,
Renault 2006
). Note that in POMDPs, there is no difference between public and private signals because there is only one decision maker.

POMDPs with stage duration
were introduced by
Neyman 2013
in the context of zero-sum stochastic games with fully observed state. Given a stochastic game
G_{1}
,
Neyman 2013
considers a family
G_{h}
of stochastic games in which the leaving probabilities

2
POMDPs with stage duration

A
partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP)
is a 7-tuple
(\Omega,A,S,f,g,P,p_{1})
, where
\Omega
is the finite set of states,
A
is the finite set of actions,
S
is the finite set of signals,
f:\Omega\to S
is the function giving a signal for each state,
g:\Omega\times A\to\mathbb{R}
is the stage payoff function,
P:\Omega\times A\to\Delta(\Omega)
is the transition probability function, and
p_{1}\in\Delta(\Omega)
is the initial distribution on the states. The POMDP
(\Omega,A,S,f,g,P,p_{1})
proceeds as follows. An initial state
\omega_{1}
is drawn from
p_{1}
, and the decision maker receives the signal
f(\omega_{1})
. At each stage
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the decision maker chooses an action
a_{n}\in A
and receives the unobserved payoff
g(\omega_{n},a_{n})
. The next state
\omega_{n+1}
is drawn according to
P(\omega_{n},a_{n})
, and the decision maker receives the signal
f(\omega_{n+1})
.

A history of length
t\in\mathbb{N}
in the POMDP
(\Omega,A,S,f,g,P,p_{1})
is

(s_{1},a_{1},s_{2},a_{2},\ldots,s_{t-1},a_{t-1},s_{t})
.
The set of all histories of length
t
is

H_{t}:=S\times(A\times S)^{t-1}
.
A
(behavior) strategy
of the decision maker is a function

\sigma:\bigcup_{t\geq 1}H_{t}\to\Delta(A)
.
The decision maker’s strategy induces a probability distribution on the set
S\times(A\times S)^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
. (Indeed, the strategy induces a probability distribution on the set
H_{1}
, then on the set
H_{2}
, etc. By the Kolmogorov extension theorem, this probability can be extended in a unique way to the set

S\times(A\times S)^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
). In particular, given an initial probability distribution
p_{0}\in\Delta(\Omega)
, a strategy

\sigma:\bigcup_{t\geq 1}H_{t}\to\Delta(A)
,
and the induced probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{p_{0}}_{\sigma}
on
S\times(A\times S)^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
, we can consider the expectation
\mathbb{E}^{p_{0}}_{\sigma}
of any random variable defined on
\bigcup_{t\geq 1}H_{t}
.

Definition 1

(POMDP with stage duration,
(
Novikov 2024
, Definition 1)
and
(
Neyman 2013
, p. 240)
)
.

Fix a POMDP

G_{1}=(\Omega,A,S,f,g,P,p_{1}).

The
POMDP with stage duration

h\in(0,1]
is the POMDP

G_{h}=(\Omega,A,S,f,g,P_{h},p_{1}),

with

P_{h}(\cdot\mid\omega,a)=hP(\cdot\mid\omega,a)+(1-h)\delta_{\omega}(\cdot),

where

\delta_{\omega}(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\omega^{\prime}=\omega;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In
G_{h}
, we consider the asymptotic value

V(h):=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\left[\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\lambda h\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda h)^{i-1}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\right].

In the above definition, we use
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}
to denote the expectation generated by the strategy
\sigma
in
G_{h}
. Formally, this expectation also depends on the initial probability distribution
p_{0}
, but since this distribution is independent of the stage duration
h
, we omit
p_{0}
from the notation.

Remark 1
.

Definition
1
coincides with the one given in
Novikov 2024
except that the latter considers the
\lambda
-discounted payoff

V_{\lambda}(h):=\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\lambda h\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda h)^{i-1}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right),

where
\lambda\in(0,1],

whereas we consider its limit
V(h)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}V_{\lambda}(h).

Definition
1
is also similar to the one introduced in
Neyman 2013
, except that the latter considers the case of full state observation, and uses
V_{\lambda}(h)
instead of
V(h)
.

\triangle

We will need an alternative expression for
V(h)
. To this end, we provide the following definition.

Definition 2

(cf.
(
Arapostathis et al. 1993
, p. 286)
)
.

For each
h\in(0,1]
and each strategy
\sigma
in
G_{h}
, we define the
expected long-run average payoff
as

R(\sigma,h):=\liminf_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right).

Remark 2
.

We have

\displaystyle V(h)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\left[\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\lambda h\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda h)^{i-1}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\left[\sup_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\lambda)^{i-1}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma}\left[\liminf_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\right],

where the first equality is obtained by using the change of variable
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/h
, and the last equality follows from
Rosenberg
et al. 2002
, see also
(
Chatterjee
et al. 2022
, Remark 2.1)
.

\triangle

Remark
2
implies that
V(h)=\sup_{\sigma}R(\sigma,h).

4.1
Construction of the strategy
\widehat{\sigma}

We first construct the strategy
\widehat{\sigma}
. We fix
h\in(0,1)
and a strategy
\sigma
in
G_{h}
.

Definition 3
.

1.

We denote by
X=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots)
the stochastic process of i.i.d. random variables, where

\Big(\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1)=h\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=0)=1-h\Big)\iff X_{i}\sim Bernoulli(h).

2.

We denote by
T=(T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots)
the stochastic process, in which
T_{i}
is the random variable

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle=0;

\displaystyle T_{i}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n>T_{i-1}\mid X_{n}=1\}\quad\text{ for }i>0.

3.

We denote by
N=(N_{1},N_{2},\ldots)
the stochastic process of i.i.d. random variables, where

\Big(N_{i}=T_{i}-T_{i-1}\Big)\iff\forall i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\;\;N_{i}=k\text{ with probability }h(1-h)^{k-1}\iff N_{i}\sim Geometric(h).

Remark 4

(Interpretation of
X,T,
and
N
)
.

In
G_{h}
, the next state is chosen according to

hP(\cdot\mid\omega,a)+(1-h)\delta_{\omega}(\cdot).

This means that at the end of each stage, the next state is drawn according to
P
with probability
h
; otherwise, the state remains unchanged (drawn according to
\delta_{\omega}
) with probability
1-h
. Hence:

1.

X_{i}
indicates whether the transition at the end of the
i
-th stage is governed by
P
(if
X_{i}=1
) or by
\delta_{\omega}
(if
X_{i}=0
).

2.

T_{i}
is the stage number at which state transition is governed by
P
for the
i
-th time.

3.

N_{i}
is the duration of the
i
-th epoch. It represents the number of stages between the
(i-1)
-th and
i
-th time the transition is governed by
P
, consisting of
N_{i}-1
self-transitions (
\delta_{\omega}
) followed by one
P
-transition.

\triangle

Remark 5
.

This remark summarizes some useful properties of
T
and
N
that will be used in subsequent proofs. For
N
, we have

\mathbb{P}(N_{j}\geq m)=(1-h)^{m-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}(N_{j})=\frac{1}{h}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{Var}(N_{j})=\frac{1-h}{h^{2}}.

For
T
, we have

T_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}N_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}(T_{j})=\frac{j}{h}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{Var}(T_{j})=\frac{1-h}{h^{2}}j.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. For a fixed infinite history
\mathcal{H}=(s^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2},\ldots)
in
G_{h}
,
we denote the filtered history
\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}
as the random vector

\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=(s^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{T_{1}},s^{\prime}_{T_{1}+1},a^{\prime}_{T_{2}},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{T_{k-1}},s^{\prime}_{T_{k-1}+1}).

The probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}
is defined on an extended joint probability space that encompasses both the histories of
G_{h}
and the auxiliary stochastic processes
X,T,
and
N
.
Similarly, we use
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}
to denote the expectation of random variables defined on this probability space.

Definition 4
.

Let
\eta_{k}=(s_{1},a_{1},s_{2},\ldots,a_{k-1},s_{k})
be a history of length
k
in
G_{1}
, and let
\widehat{a}\in A
. The strategy
\widehat{\sigma}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}(\eta_{k})(\widehat{a})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}^{h}_{\sigma}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{a}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k}).

If
\mathbb{P}^{h}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k})=0
, then we define
\widehat{\sigma}(\eta_{k})
as an arbitrary fixed mixed action.

\triangle

Remark 6

(Intuition behind the construction of
\widehat{\sigma}
)
.

The strategy
\widehat{\sigma}
is designed to simulate in
G_{1}
the strategy
\sigma
from
G_{h}
. In
G_{h}
, the state only truly transitions (according to
P
) at the random stages
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots
. Between these stages, the state is frozen.

The decision maker in
G_{1}
does not experience these frozen periods; instead, every stage in
G_{1}
corresponds to a true transition. Therefore, to replicate
\sigma
, the decision maker in
G_{1}
examines the current history
\eta_{k}
and asks:
“If I were playing
G_{h}
and the sequence of true transitions perfectly matched
\eta_{k}
, what action would I play at the (random and unobserved) moment
T_{k}
when the state is finally about to change?”

As the durations of the frozen periods (
N_{i}
) and the intermediate actions taken during them are absent in
G_{1}
,
\widehat{\sigma}
effectively “filters out” this information. It calculates the expected mixed action that
\sigma
would play at stage
T_{k}
, conditioned on the fact that the filtered history
\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}
of true transitions aligns with the history
\eta_{k}
observed in
G_{1}
.

\triangle

Remark 7

(Strategy in the state-blind case)
.

In the case of a single signal, any strategy
\sigma
in
G_{h}
becomes a sequence of (mixed or pure) actions, i.e.,
\sigma=(a^{1},a^{2},\ldots)
. In this case, the strategy
\widehat{\sigma}
in
G_{1}
is given by

\widehat{\sigma}=(\widehat{a}^{1},\widehat{a}^{2},\ldots)
, where
\widehat{a}^{i}=\mathbb{E}^{h}_{\sigma}(a_{T_{i}}).
Note that, in general, the actions
\widehat{a}^{i}
are mixed, even if
\sigma
is a pure strategy.

\triangle

Proof of Theorem 1 and Corollaries

We first state four technical lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}g(\omega_{k},a_{k})=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega_{T_{k}},a_{T_{k}}).

Lemma 2
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\sum_{j=T_{k-1}+1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega_{T_{k}},a_{T_{k}}).

Lemma 3
.

Let
t_{k}=\lfloor k/h\rfloor
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{t_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{h}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right).

Lemma 4
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence and
\{x_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a subsequence of
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Suppose that

\lim_{j\to\infty}(x_{j+1}-x_{j})=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|n_{j+1}-n_{j}|\leq M\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

We then have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}x_{n_{k}}.

Proof of Theorem
1
modulo technical lemmas.

To show that

R(\sigma,h)
and
R(\widehat{\sigma},1)

We start with

, we have

\displaystyle R(\widehat{\sigma},1)=\liminf_{T\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T}g(\omega_{k},a_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{T\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}g(\omega_{k},a_{k})\right)

(1)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{T\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega_{T_{k}},a_{T_{k}})\right).

Consider the sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
and the subsequence
\{x_{t_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of

, where

x_{t}:=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad t_{k}=\lfloor k/h\rfloor.

Let
M=\sup_{\omega,a}g(\omega,a)
. We have

\displaystyle\left|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{t+1}\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})\right)\right|

(2)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{-1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega_{i},a_{i})+\frac{1}{t+1}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega_{t+1},a_{t+1})\right|\leq\frac{Mt}{t(t+1)}+\frac{M}{t+1}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0.

(3)

Now Lemma

(4)

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{t_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{h}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{K\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{h}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=T_{k-1}+1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)

4.3
Proof of Lemma
1

To avoid ambiguity between the two distinct stochastic processes, we adopt the following notational convention in the proof of Lemma
1
.
The state
\omega_{k}
(resp. action
a_{k}
, signal
s_{k}
) refers exclusively to the
k
-th stage state (resp. action, signal) in
G_{1}
.
Conversely, the state
\omega^{\prime}_{k}
(resp. action
a^{\prime}_{k}
, signal
s^{\prime}_{k}
) refers exclusively to the
k
-th stage state (resp. action, signal) in
G_{h}
.

In the proof of Lemma
1
, when manipulating conditional probabilities, we adopt the standard convention that
0\times\text{undefined}=0
. Consequently, whenever we evaluate a conditional probability of the form
\mathbb{P}(A\mid B)
, it is implicitly assumed that
\mathbb{P}(B)>0
. In cases where
\mathbb{P}(B)=0
, the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}(A\mid B)
is technically undefined; however, because such terms only appear multiplied by
\mathbb{P}(B)=0
(such as in the law of total probability or Bayes’ theorem), they contribute zero to the overall expression. Thus, we do not need to explicitly evaluate or restrict our summations to exclude these zero-probability events.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

We will prove a more general statement: for any
\omega\in\Omega,a\in A,k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and any history
\eta_{k}
of length
k
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\omega,a_{k}=a)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=a).

(6)

This identity implies the lemma. Indeed, by (
6
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\omega_{k}=\omega,a_{k}=a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Histories }\eta_{k}\text{ of}\\
\text{length }k\text{ in }G_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\omega,a_{k}=a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Histories }\eta_{k}\text{ of}\\
\text{length }k\text{ in }G_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=a)

(7)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=a).

Subsequently, we obtain by (
7
)

\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}g(\omega_{k},a_{k})=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,a\in A}\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\omega_{k}=\omega,a_{k}=a)\cdot g(\omega,a)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,a\in A}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=a)\cdot g(\omega,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}g(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}).

The rest of the proof is dedicated to justifying (
6
). The proof is by induction on
k
. The base case
k=1
holds because the initial probability distribution is the same in both
G_{1}
and
G_{h}
.

We now suppose that (
6
) holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and we will prove that it also holds for
k+1
. Let
\eta_{k+1}=(\eta_{k},\overline{a},\overline{s}).

In
G_{1}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k+1},\omega_{k+1}=\omega,a_{k+1}=a)=\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k+1},\omega_{k+1}=\omega)\cdot\widehat{\sigma}(\eta_{k+1})(a).

(8)

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k+1},\omega_{k+1}=\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\widehat{\omega},a_{k}=\overline{a},\omega_{k+1}=\omega,s_{k+1}=\overline{s})

(9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\widehat{\omega},a_{k}=\overline{a})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\omega_{k+1}=\omega,s_{k+1}=\overline{s}\mid\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\widehat{\omega},a_{k}=\overline{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k},\omega_{k}=\widehat{\omega},a_{k}=\overline{a})\cdot P(\omega\mid\overline{a},\widehat{\omega})\cdot\mathbf{1}_{f(\omega)=\overline{s}}

In
G_{h}
, it follows from the definition of the conditional probability that

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a)=\\
\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega).

(10)

Because the state is frozen between stage
T_{k}+1
and stage
T_{k+1}
, we have

\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}.

(11)

By the construction of the event
\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1}
, we can write it as a union of disjoint events:

\left\{\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1}\right\}=\bigcup_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{\omega},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\overline{a},s^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\overline{s}\right\}.

(12)

Using (
11
) and (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega)=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega,\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{\omega},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\overline{a},s^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\overline{s})

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{\omega},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\overline{a})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega,s^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\overline{s}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{\omega},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\overline{a}).

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k}=\eta_{k},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\widehat{\omega},a^{\prime}_{T_{k}}=\overline{a})\cdot P(\omega\mid\overline{a},\widehat{\omega})\cdot\mathbf{1}_{f(\omega)=\overline{s}}

Thus, by (
9
), (
13
), and the induction hypothesis, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k+1},\omega_{k+1}=\omega)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega)

(14)

By the law of total probability, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)=\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Histories }\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\text{ of}\\
\text{length }T_{k+1}\text{ in }G_{h}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega),

(15)

Since
a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}
depends by the definition only on the history of length
T_{k+1}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}).

(16)

By the Bayes’ theorem, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})}{\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})}.

(17)

Note that
\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}
contains all the information in
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{fil}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{rem}
denote the supplementary information in
\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}
not present in
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{fil}
, consisting specifically of the exact holding durations
N_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k+1
, and the intermediate actions
a^{\prime}_{j}
taken during those waiting periods. By the construction, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{rem},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})

(18)

We claim that
\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}
is conditionally independent of
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{rem}
given
\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1}
; that is

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{rem},\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1}),

(19)

This conditional independence holds due to the structure of the delayed transitions. By definition, the waiting periods
N_{i}
are generated by an independent sequence of geometrically distributed random variables, and are therefore independent of the underlying state sequence. Furthermore, during any waiting period
N_{i}
, the state remains constant. Consequently, the signals observed during these periods simply repeat the last signal recorded in the filtered history
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{fil}
.

Because the decision maker only observes signals and not the state itself, the intermediate actions in
\mathcal{H}^{rem}_{k+1}
generated by the strategy
\sigma
depend only on the sequence of received signals and past actions. Since these signals are completely determined by the filtered history
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{fil}
and the state-independent waiting periods
N_{i}
, the generation of
\mathcal{H}^{rem}_{k+1}
is conditionally independent of the true, unobserved state
\omega_{T_{k}+1}^{\prime}
. Thus, conditioning on
\mathcal{H}^{rem}_{k+1}
provides no additional information regarding the distribution of
\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}
, implying that the equality (
19
) holds.

Thus we have by (
17
), (
18
), and (
19
)

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1}).

(20)

From (
15
), (
16
), (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k}+1}=\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Histories }\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\text{ of}\\
\text{length }T_{k+1}\text{ in }G_{h}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\eta^{\prime}_{k+1})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\eta^{\prime}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a\mid\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1})=\widehat{\sigma}(\eta_{k+1})(a).

Finally, by combining (
8
), (
10
), (
14
), and (
21
), we have

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\sigma}}^{1}(\eta_{k+1},\omega_{k+1}=\omega,a_{k+1}=a)=\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}^{h}(\mathcal{H}^{fil}_{k+1}=\eta_{k+1},\omega^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=\omega,a^{\prime}_{T_{k+1}}=a).\qed

Let

By the triangle inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right|\right)\leq M\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\big(|T_{k}-t_{k}|\big).

By Jensen’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\frac{h}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})-\frac{1}{t_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left[\frac{h}{k}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)\right]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\left(\frac{h}{k}-\frac{1}{t_{k}}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left[\frac{h}{k}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)\right]\right|\leq\frac{h}{k}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}^{h}\left(\left|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{T_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{t_{k}}g(\omega_{j},a_{j})\right)\right|\right)

5
Concluding remarks

5.1
Fully observed state

This subsection is dedicated to the case where the signal fully reveals the state, meaning that
S=\Omega
and
f(\omega)=\omega
. In such a case,
(
Sorin and
Vigeral 2016
, Proposition 5.2)
proves that for all
\lambda\in(0,1]
and
h\in(0,1]
, we have

V_{\lambda}(h)=V_{\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda-\lambda h}}(1).

This implies that for any
h\in(0,1]
, we have

V(h)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}V_{\lambda}(h)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}V_{\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda-\lambda h}}(1)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}V_{\lambda}(1)=V(1).

Hence,
V
is a constant function in this particular case.

5.2
Continuity of
V(h)

In this subsection, we examine whether the function
h\mapsto V(h)
is continuous.

Proposition 1
.

The function
h\mapsto V(h)
is lower semi-continuous on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

If
h^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, then the support of the transition probabilities of the POMDPs in the family
\{G_{h}\}_{h\in(h^{\prime}-\varepsilon,h^{\prime}+\varepsilon)}
is identical. The proposition now follows directly from
(
Chatterjee
et al. 2022
, Corollary 3.10)
.
∎

However, it is possible to introduce new transitions when moving from
h=1
to
h<1
, which implies that
Chatterjee
et al. 2022
cannot be applied. In fact, it is possible that
V(h)
is not lower semi-continuous at
1
, even in the state-blind case.
