Flowing with Displacements and Tilts: Surface Operators in O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) Models
Jake Belton, Nadav Drukker, Biswajit Sahoo

Abstract
Defect conformal field theories have special operators of protected dimension known as displacements and tilts. They arise due to the breaking of global symmetries by the defect and the normalisations of their two-point functions are characteristics of the defect. In the case of surface defects, these normalisations are related to some of the anomaly coefficients in the surface effective action. To study these operators and their flows between different defect renormalization group fixed points we present an elegant approach using conformal perturbation theory that easily reproduces the known examples from the critical Wilson–Fisher O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model in 4 − ε 4-\varepsilon dimensions and allows us to construct new ones in other multiscalar theories. In all the systems that we study the flows are short and under full control, as is the change of the displacement and tilt normalizations. We point out some novel features like the existence of vortices when the defect conformal manifold is not simply connected. In addition to regular human labour, this work relied heavily on generative AI; see full disclosure in methodology section.

Introduction

Conserved stress tensors and symmetry currents are of paramount
importance in local conformal field theories. When introducing defects
into a theory, the defect dynamics are themselves often non-local,
as they can be mediated via bulk interactions. Still, instead
of the conserved currents, they have distinguished operators
known as displacements
\mathbb{D}
and tilts

and internal symmetries, respectively.

The displacements represent a small deformation of a defect away from
a symmetric configuration (plane or sphere). The tilts arise from
localised deviation from a uniform coupling of a defect to operators
charged under a global symmetry. For the formal definitions, see
the top of Section

For a

protected dimensions
\Delta_{\mathbb{D}}=p+1
and
\Delta_{\mathbbm{t}}=p

The normalizations of these operators
are physically meaningful quantities characteristic of the
particular defects

2.2
Surface perturbation and fixed points

Our main objects of study are flat surface operators achieved
by perturbing the bulk theory by the scalar and symmetric
traceless operators

S_{\rm def}=S_{\rm UV}+\mu^{-\gamma_{S}}h^{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\tau\,S+\mu^{-\gamma_{T}}h^{T}_{ij}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\tau\,T_{ij}.

Here

The beta functions take their usual form in conformal perturbation

\displaystyle\beta^{S}

\displaystyle=\mu\frac{dh^{S}}{d\mu}=\gamma_{S}h^{S}+\pi C_{SSS}(h^{S})^{2}+\pi C_{STT}h^{T}_{ij}h^{T}_{ij}+O(h^{3},\varepsilon h^{2}),

\displaystyle\beta^{T}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mu\frac{dh^{T}_{ij}}{d\mu}=\gamma_{T}h^{T}_{ij}+2\pi C_{STT}h^{S}h^{T}_{ij}+\pi C_{TTT}\left(h^{T}_{ik}h^{T}_{kj}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{N}h^{T}_{kl}h^{T}_{kl}\right)+O(h^{3},\varepsilon h^{2}).

One case when

because there are no possible singlet contractions.

) fully fix

It is worth mentioning here that these expressions are significantly

. We keep the general form and specialise
to WF only in Section

2.3
Defect conformal manifold

In the case of the symmetry breaking defects, the choice
of a diagonal

in (

This is an
nm

tilt operators
{\mathbbm{t}}
in the bifundamental representation.

One point to notice is that for

the Grassmannian has
fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gr(n,\mathbb{R}^{N}))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, so the defect has

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charged local vortex operators. Those are configurations
where encircling the vortex once, the choice of representative
defect from the coset traverses the noncontractible cycle.

For
N=2
and
n=1
, the defect conformal manifold is simply

\Gr(1,\mathbb{R}^{2})=S^{1}
with fundamental group
\mathbb{Z}
, so in this case
the vortices are integer valued.

It would be interesting to study these vortices, which can be thought
of as defects within defects
[
38
,
39

40

2.4.1
Reality and stability analysis

The stability analysis of such defects was performed in
[
36
]
,
generalising Michel’s theorem
[
42
]
.
At this order the beta functions (
2.17
),
(
2.18
) are gradient,

\beta^{I}=\partial A/\partial h^{I}
, with

A=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{I}(h^{I})^{2}+\frac{\pi}{3}C_{IJK}\,h^{I}h^{J}h^{K},\qquad I\in\{S,T_{ij}\}.

(2.45)

The function
A
decreases monotonically towards the IR and stable fixed points
minimise
A
. Furthermore, the theorem states that if the quadratic part of

A
is negative definite, meaning
\gamma_{S},\gamma_{T}<0
,
there is at most one totally stable defect and, if it exists, it preserves

O(N)
symmetry, so is
{\cal D}_{N}
.
Since the potential is cubic, it certainly is not a global minimum.

Looking at (
2.39
), we see
that the symmetric fixed point
{\cal D}_{N}
is stable
iff
\gamma_{T}C_{SSS}>2\gamma_{S}C_{STT}
. This is clearly
correct if
\gamma_{T}>0
(assuming
\gamma_{S}<0
, or else there
would be no flow).

The situation is more interesting when both
\gamma_{T}
and
\gamma_{S}

are negative. In that case, if
\gamma_{T}C_{SSS}>2\gamma_{S}C_{STT}
,
then it is easy to see that all of
{\cal D}_{n}
with
n\neq m

are real saddle points. We note that the discriminant in
(
2.27
) can be rewritten in terms of
\gamma_{T}^{{\cal D}_{N}}

(
2.39
) as

\sigma_{n}^{2}=\frac{9\nu^{2}}{N^{2}}-\frac{36nm}{N}\frac{C_{STT}C_{SSS}}{C_{TTT}^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{T}}{\gamma_{S}^{2}}\gamma_{T}^{{\cal D}_{N}}.

(2.46)

Given that
\gamma_{T}<0
, the sign of the second term depends entirely on

\gamma_{T}^{{\cal D}_{N}}
. If
{\cal D}_{N}
is stable, then the second term in
(
2.46
) is non-negative and

\sigma_{n}^{2}\geq 9\nu^{2}/N^{2}
for all
n\neq m
.

Also for
n=m
in the even
N
case, the reality condition of this
fixed point
(
2.31
) is exactly the same as the
{\cal D}_{N}
stability condition.
This is because the
n=m=N/2
branch meets the symmetric fixed
point
{\cal D}_{N}
exactly when

h_{N/2}
coupling in (
2.30
) vanishes, which is
the same as
\gamma_{T}^{{\cal D}_{N}}=0
.
For the ordinary WF theory this
happens at
N=6
:
{\cal D}_{6}^{\rm WF}
meets

{\cal D}_{3}^{\rm WF}
. Resolving this degeneracy requires going to
higher orders in perturbation theory, as done
in
[
37
]
. We note further such examples in
the long-range WF theory in Section
5.2.2
.

For
\gamma_{S}<0<\gamma_{T}
, the symmetric point
{\cal D}_{N}
is stable
but a possible
n=m
saddle is complex. There may be real saddle
points for other values of
n
.
For
\gamma_{T}<0<\gamma_{S}
, the fixed point
{\cal D}_{N}
exists only as
a formal solution, as
S
is irrelevant.
The
n=m
case is complex, but there could be real fixed points
for small or large

, but again, they are unstable.

4.3
Protected two-point functions along flows

Having determined the normalizations at fixed points, we now consider
the protected two-point functions along the RG flows.
To be concrete,
we look at the scalar–tensor system and restrict to a two-dimensional
subspace in the space of coupling with
O(n)\times O(m)
symmetry
along the entire flow. This is the same subspace as in
Section
2.6
and it includes

{\cal D}_{0}
,
{\cal D}_{N}
and two points on the
{\cal D}_{n}
defect conformal
manifold.

This space consists of the running scalar coupling
\bar{h}^{S}(s)

with
s=\mu|\tau|
and the running tensor coupling

\bar{h}^{T}_{ij}(s)=\bar{h}_{n}(s)\diag\big(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{n},\underbrace{-\tfrac{n}{m},\cdots,-\tfrac{n}{m}}_{m}\big).

(4.27)

In the rest of this subsection, all occurrences of the barred couplings

\bar{h}^{S}
,
\bar{h}^{T}_{ij}
and
\bar{h}_{n}
should be
viewed as the running coupling, which is a function of
s
. Those
without a bar are still the renormalised couplings at a scale
\mu
.

The running of the couplings
\bar{h}^{S}(s)
and
\bar{h}_{n}(s)
is
controlled by the beta functions

\displaystyle-s\frac{d\bar{h}^{S}(s)}{ds}

\displaystyle=\beta^{S}\equiv\gamma_{S}\bar{h}^{S}+\pi C_{SSS}(\bar{h}^{S})^{2}+\pi C_{STT}\frac{nN}{m}\bar{h}_{n}^{2},

(4.28)

\displaystyle-s\frac{d\bar{h}_{n}(s)}{ds}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\equiv\gamma_{T}\bar{h}_{n}+2\pi C_{STT}\bar{h}^{S}\bar{h}_{n}+\pi C_{TTT}\frac{m-n}{m}\bar{h}_{n}^{2}.

These carry an extra minus sign relative to the beta functions
(
2.17
), (
2.18
),
which govern the running with the energy scale,

\beta=\mu\,dh/d\mu
. In (
4.28
) the
couplings are functions of the dimensionless distance

s=\mu|\tau|
, and the scale probed at separation
|\tau|
is

1/|\tau|\propto 1/s
. Increasing
s
therefore lowers the energy,

d\ln(\text{scale})=-d\ln s
, so that
s\,d\bar{h}/ds=-\beta
.
Equivalently, the
flow runs from the UV (
s\to 0
) to the IR (
s\to\infty
).
Note that all couplings on the
right-hand side are evaluated at the same scale
s
.

In addition to the singlet and this particular component of the tensor,
we follow the fields in the bifundamental representation of

O(n)\times O(m)
. In the absence of fields beyond
S
and

T_{ij}
, this sector is
characterized by a single running anomalous dimension

\gamma_{T_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath}}}(s)
, which
vanishes at the
{\cal D}_{n}
fixed-point (
2.40
).
Its value along the flow is also determined by the tensor beta functions
(
2.18
)

\gamma_{T_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath}}}(s)=\gamma_{T}+2\pi C_{STT}\bar{h}^{S}(s)+\pi C_{TTT}\frac{m-n}{m}\bar{h}_{n}(s).

(4.29)

The tilt in (
4.5
) has the
prefactor
\bar{h}_{n}-\bar{h}_{m}=\frac{N}{m}\bar{h}_{n}
, so its full evolution is
governed by (
4.29
) together with the evolution
of
\bar{h}_{n}
, which is

-\beta_{n}
(
4.28
). These two exactly
cancel each other and the protected tilt operator has zero anomalous
dimension at all scales. A similar argument applies to the displacement
operator and the RG-improved two-point functions take the form

\displaystyle\big\langle{\mathbbm{t}}_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath}}(\tau){\mathbbm{t}}_{\hat{k}\check{l}}(0)\big\rangle_{\rm RG}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{{\mathbbm{t}}}(s,h^{S},h_{n})\delta_{\hat{\imath}\hat{k}}\delta_{\check{\jmath}\check{l}}}{|\tau|^{4}},

(4.30)

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathbb{D}_{r}(\tau)\mathbb{D}_{s}(0)\big\rangle_{\rm RG}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\mathbb{D}}(s,h^{S},h_{n})\delta_{rs}}{|\tau|^{6}}.

The normalizations

and

can be read off directly from

4.5
),
(
4.6
) at the running
couplings and using the fixed-point normalizations

), (

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{D}_{r}(\tau)\mathbb{D}_{s}(0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{rs}}{|\tau|^{6}}\left[2\Delta_{S}\big(\bar{h}^{S}(\mu|\tau|)\big)^{2}+2\Delta_{T}\frac{nN}{m}\bar{h}_{n}(\mu|\tau|)^{2}\right],

(4.31)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbbm{t}}_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath}}(\tau){\mathbbm{t}}_{\hat{k}\check{l}}(0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2\frac{N^{2}}{m^{2}}\bar{h}_{n}(\mu|\tau|)^{2}\delta_{\hat{\imath}\hat{k}}\delta_{\check{\jmath}\check{l}}}{|\tau|^{4}},

The bilinear operators in the

\mathbf{T}_{2}

antisymmetric tensor with a pseudoscalar

\begin{gathered}S=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{N}\,\kappa_{d}}\phi_{ia}\phi_{ia},\qquad T_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}}\left(\phi_{ia}\phi_{ja}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{N}\phi_{ka}\phi_{ka}\right),\qquad Z_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}}P^{(N)}_{ij,kl}\epsilon_{ab}\phi_{ka}\phi_{lb}.\\

\end{gathered}

with
\epsilon_{12}=1

for

\displaystyle\gamma_{S,\pm}^{\rm N2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon+\frac{2(N+1)}{3}\lambda_{*\pm}-\frac{N-1}{3}\zeta_{*\pm}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(5.50)

\displaystyle\gamma_{T,\pm}^{\rm N2}

\displaystyle\gamma_{T_{2},\pm}^{\rm N2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{*\pm}+\frac{N-1}{3}\zeta_{*\pm}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{Y,\pm}^{\rm N2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{*\pm}-\frac{1}{3}\zeta_{*\pm}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{Z,\pm}^{\rm N2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{*\pm}-\zeta_{*\pm}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

The beta functions for a surface deformation is as in

)–(

T_{2}
and
Y
with the appropriate indices.
When the two of them mix, we use the collective name
T_{2}Y_{\pm}
.

Under the symmetry breaking
O(N)\times O(2)\to U(p)\times O(n)\times SO(2)
,
they decompose as (c.f. (
3.16
))

\displaystyle T_{2}:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{1}_{N},\mathbf{T}_{2})

\displaystyle\to(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\otimes\mathbf{T}_{2},

(5.81)

\displaystyle Y:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{T}_{N},\mathbf{T}_{2})

\displaystyle\to\big((\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\mathbf{p},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n})\oplus(\bar{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{n})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{T}_{n})\oplus(\mathbf{Adj}_{p},\mathbf{1})\big)\otimes\mathbf{T}_{2}.

Both are
SO(2)
doublets, but we omit the indices below for simplicity.

The
O(N)
singlet component of
Y
mixes with
T_{2}
. In the basis

\left(T_{2,ab},\,\frac{h^{T,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}_{ij}Y_{ij,ab}}{|h^{T,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}|}\right),

(5.82)

the anomalous dimensions are the eigenvalues of

\Gamma_{T_{2}Y}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\gamma_{T_{2}}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}&\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}\big|h^{T,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\big|\\[6.00006pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}\big|h^{T,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\big|&\displaystyle\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}-4\pi\,\frac{\varpi}{n}h_{2p}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\end{pmatrix},

(5.83)

and recall from (
3.8
) and tracelessness that

\big|h^{T,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\big|^{2}=\frac{2pN}{n}\big(h_{2p}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\big)^{2}.

(5.84)

The remaining components of
Y
do not mix and have the anomalous dimensions

\displaystyle\gamma_{Y_{n}}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}+4\pi h_{n}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}.

(5.85)

\displaystyle\gamma_{Y_{\rm sym},\pm}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}+4\pi h_{2p}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\pm 4\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\eta^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}.

\displaystyle\gamma_{Y_{\rm bifund},\pm}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}-2\pi\,\frac{\varpi}{n}h_{2p}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}\pm 2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\eta^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}.

\displaystyle\gamma_{Y_{\rm adj}}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}+4\pi h_{2p}^{{\cal C}_{p,\pm},{\rm N2}}.

The
\pm
subscripts on the symmetric and bifundamental fields correspond
to the real and imaginary parts of these complex fields that get
different contributions from the coupling to
Z
.

For the
U(N/2)
symmetric
{\cal C}_{N/2}^{\rm N2}
saddle, one simply sets

h^{T,{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=0
and
h^{S,{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}
is in
(
5.69
). There are no bifundamental fields since
n=0

and no mixing among the singlet components.
The anomalous dimensions are

\gamma_{T_{2}}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\gamma_{T_{2}}^{\rm N2}-\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2},\quad\gamma_{Y_{\rm sing}}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\gamma_{Y_{\rm adj}}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}-\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2},\quad\gamma_{Y_{\rm sym},\pm}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\gamma_{Y}^{\rm N2}-\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2}\pm 4\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\eta^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}.

(5.86)

Stability in the full space requires all anomalous dimensions
in this
T_{2},Y
sector, in addition to the
S
,
T
,
Z
eigenvalues above, to
be nonnegative.

Transverse vector operators of dimension near three at

{\cal C}_{p,\pm}
are gotten from Section
3.3
.
As with dimension near two operators, one should also
consider the operators arising from the descendants of

T_{2}
and
Y
. They decompose as in
(
5.81
), do not mix with

V_{r}
,
U_{ij,r}
and
W_{ij,r}
, mix among themselves
in the singlet sector and the symmetric and bifundamental
components of
Y
get different corrections for their real
and imaginary parts. All very analogously to the
discussion above.

5.3.5
Displacements and tilts

The displacement and tilt normalisations follow from the formulas in
Section
4.2
with the structure constants
(
5.68
) and fixed-point couplings
in (
5.69
) and (
5.71
)–(
5.74
).
As elsewhere in the
O(N)\times O(2)
analysis we keep the
bulk dimensions
\gamma_{S}^{\rm N2}
,
\gamma_{T}^{\rm N2}
,

\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2}
unevaluated,
and since the fixed-point couplings are of order

\varepsilon
we replace
\Delta_{S}
,
\Delta_{T}
,
\Delta_{Z}
by their leading
value
2
.

At the
O(N)
symmetric fixed point (
5.56
) the
tensor and antisymmetric couplings vanish, so

C_{{\mathbbm{t}}}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm N2}}=0
and, from (
4.13
),

C_{\mathbb{D}}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm N2}}=2\Delta_{S}\big(h^{S,{\cal D}_{N},{\rm N2}}\big)^{2}=\frac{N}{\pi^{2}}\big(\gamma_{S}^{\rm N2}\big)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

(5.87)

The expressions at the symmetry breaking points
{\cal D}_{n}
do not
simplify. See (
4.8
), (
4.11
).

Using
C_{TTT}^{\rm N2}=2
and
C_{STT}^{\rm N2}=2/\sqrt{N}
in
(
4.10
), the tilt normalisation can be written as

C_{{\mathbbm{t}}}^{{\cal D}_{n},{\rm N2}}=\frac{N^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\nu^{2}}\left[\gamma_{T}^{\rm N2}+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{N}}h^{S,{\cal D}_{n},{\rm N2}}\right]^{2}.

(5.88)

For the
{\cal C}_{p,\pm}

fixed points with
h^{Z}\neq 0
the conformal manifold is

O(N)/(U(p)\times O(n))
, whose tangent space
(
4.17
) splits into the bifundamental and
wedge directions, giving two distinct tilt families.

At the
U(N/2)
symmetric fixed point
{\cal C}_{N/2}^{\rm N2}

(
5.69
),
the bifundamental directions are absent and the tensor coupling
vanishes, so only the wedge tilt survives. Equations
(
4.23
), (
5.69
) give

C_{{\mathbbm{t}}_{\wedge}}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2}\big(2\gamma_{S}^{\rm N2}-\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2}\big),

(5.89)

while (
4.25
) with
h^{T}=0
collapses to

C_{\mathbb{D}}^{{\cal C}_{N/2},{\rm N2}}=\frac{N}{2\pi^{2}}\gamma_{S}^{\rm N2}\gamma_{Z}^{\rm N2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

(5.90)

At the generic
U(p)\times O(n)
fixed point
{\cal C}_{p,\pm}^{\rm N2}
both
tilt families are present. There are no particular simplifications, so
see (
4.19
), (
4.23
),
and (
4.26
).

5.4
Tricritical Wilson–Fisher theory in
d=3-\varepsilon

Lastly we look at the tricritical

O(N)
WF theory. This theory has a sextic potential and a
perturbative bulk fixed point in
d=3-\varepsilon
dimensions.
The
S
and
T_{ij}
operators in this model
are quartics of the fundamental field with
[
33
]

\gamma_{S}^{\rm tri}=-\frac{2(N+14)}{3N+22}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\qquad\gamma_{T}^{\rm tri}=-\frac{4(N+7)}{3N+22}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(5.91)

and the current is just as in (
5.7
)
but has
\Delta_{j}^{\rm tri}=2-\varepsilon
.
The four index symmetric traceless tensor has

\gamma_{T_{4}}^{\rm tri}=-\frac{6(N+6)}{3N+22}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})
, but we do not
turn it on to make the analysis of fixed points a bit more manageable.

With tree-level normalised operators

S=\frac{(\phi_{k}\phi_{k})^{2}}{\sqrt{8N(N+2)}\,\kappa_{3}^{2}},\qquad T_{ij}=\frac{\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\phi_{k}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{N}(\phi_{k}\phi_{k})^{2}}{2\sqrt{N+4}\,\kappa_{3}^{2}},\qquad\kappa_{3}=\frac{1}{4\pi}+O(\varepsilon),

(5.92)

and the unnormalised current, we find the
structure constants to leading order

C_{SSS}^{\rm tri}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,(N+8)}{\sqrt{N(N+2)}}+O(\varepsilon),\qquad C_{STT}^{\rm tri}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,(N+16)}{\sqrt{N(N+2)}}+O(\varepsilon),\qquad C_{TTT}^{\rm tri}=\frac{4(5N+32)}{(N+4)^{3/2}}+O(\varepsilon).

(5.93)

The current two-point function is still given by
(
5.8
), now evaluated at
d=3
,

C_{j}^{\rm tri}=4\kappa_{3}^{2}+O(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}+O(\varepsilon).

(5.94)

Now from the Ward identity (
2.15
),
in the tricritical theory

C_{TjU}^{\rm tri}=\sqrt{C_{j}^{\rm tri}}+O(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+O(\varepsilon).

(5.95)

C_{Sjj}
and
C_{Tjj}
vanish at leading order. This is easy to
see because at leading order the only allowed Wick contractions
are between all the fields in the two
j
s and the quartics
S
and

T_{ij}
. Given that
j
is antisymmetric and
S
,
T_{ij}

are symmetric, this vanishes.
Because of this we cannot use conformal perturbation theory to
reliably deform the theory by the current. The entire framework
in sections
2
and
3

relies on finite structure constants so the beta function equations
are solved with
h\sim\varepsilon
. With
C_{Sjj}\sim\varepsilon
,
the beta functions do not describe short flows, so we do not
turn on
j
deformations.

5.4.1
Fixed points

In this case the
O(N)
symmetric fixed point (
2.21
) is

{\cal D}_{N}^{\rm tri}:\qquad h^{S,{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}=\frac{\sqrt{2N(N+2)}(N+14)}{2\pi(N+8)(3N+22)}\varepsilon,\qquad h^{T,{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}_{ij}=0.

(5.96)

For
n\neq m
, the discriminant obtained from
(
2.27
) is

\sigma_{n}^{2}=\frac{9\nu^{2}}{N^{2}}-\frac{18nm(N+4)^{3}(N+7)(N+16)(N^{2}-112)}{N^{2}(N+2)(N+14)^{2}(5N+32)^{2}}.

(5.97)

The fixed point is real when
\sigma_{n}^{2}
is nonnegative,
in particular for
N^{2}<112
.
Unlike in the ordinary WF theory,
\sigma_{n}=0
does not occur for any
integer
N
in the
\varepsilon
expansion.

We do not plug in the values for all variables at these general
fixed points. It is easy enough to do, but the resulting expressions
are long and unilluminating.

5.4.2
Operators of dimension near two

At the symmetric tricritical fixed point,
(
2.39
) gives

\gamma_{S}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}=\frac{2(N+14)}{3N+22}\varepsilon,\qquad\gamma_{T}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}=-\frac{2(N^{2}-112)}{(N+8)(3N+22)}\varepsilon.

(5.98)

In the tricritical theory the current has dimension
2-\varepsilon
.
In the O(N) symmetric fixed point the current is conserved so does
not develop an anomalous dimension. In the symmetry breaking fixed
points the
O(n)
and
O(m)
adjoint components are still conserved,
while the bifundamental components get anomalous dimensions.
Because of the transverse vector index,

j_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath},r}^{\rm tri}
does not mix with the tilt

{\mathbbm{t}}_{\hat{\imath}\check{\jmath}}^{\rm tri}
originating from
T_{ij}
.

The symmetric fixed point
{\cal D}_{N}^{\rm tri}
is perturbatively stable when

N^{2}<112
.
Thus, it is stable for
N\leq 10
and unstable for

N\geq 11
. All real symmetry-breaking fixed points are saddles by the
analysis of Section
2.4.1
.

For
N\leq 10
, all branches are real. For
N=11
, the real branches with

n\neq m
are
n=1,2,3,8,9,10
. For
N=12,13
, they are

n=1,2,N-2,N-1
. For
14\leq N\leq 19
, they are
n=1,N-1
. For

N\geq 20
, no branch with
n\neq m
is real. When
N
is even, the

n=m=N/2
branch is real only for
N\leq 10
.

As usual, we choose not to write the dimensions of operators close
to three, as they are not particularly illuminating. The exception
is of course the displacement.

Displacements and tilts

The tricritical theory has only the scalar and tensor defect couplings,

(

5.93

At the symmetric fixed point (
5.96
) the tensor
coupling vanishes, so
C_{{\mathbbm{t}}}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}=0
and, from
(

C_{\mathbb{D}}^{{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}=2\Delta_{S}\big(h^{S,{\cal D}_{N},{\rm tri}}\big)^{2}=\frac{2N(N+2)(N+14)^{2}}{\pi^{2}(N+8)^{2}(3N+22)^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

(5.99)

The values of
C_{\mathbbm{t}}^{{\cal D}_{n},{\rm tri}}
and

6
Discussion

We presented a unified approach to studying surface defects at first order
in conformal perturbation theory. This reproduces many of the results
in the Wilson–Fisher
O(N)
model found in
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, but extended the results
to many other settings including the long range model,
overlapping with
[
32
]
, the chiral
O(N)\times O(2)

model and the tricritical
O(N)
model. As an example, in the
last case we find that the
O(N)
symmetric defect is stable for

N\leq 10
and unstable otherwise.
The same techniques could be carried over to other theories, like those
studied in
[
31
]
.

In addition to studying deformation of the trivial UV defect, we
looked at deformations away from an existing defect in
Section
2.6
. In particular,
we show that under certain assumptions, requiring an
O(N)

invariant IR fixed point constrains the starting point to be equivalent to
one of the
{\cal D}_{n}
defects that arise from a deformation of
the bulk theory.

Results at higher loop order in the
\varepsilon
expansion were
presented in
[
30
,
37
]
. This extension
is beyond the scope of the present work, though it should not be hard
to adapt those calculations to the other models in
d=4-\varepsilon
.
In particular, we present a rich family of fixed-point collisions in the
long range model in Section
5.2.2
, which would
be corrected at higher orders in perturbation theory.
Such degenerations appear at the boundaries of stability
regions analysed in extensive detail in a unified manner
in Section
2.4.1
and then applied to the
different models. Since the long-range model has a continuous
parameter
\alpha
, the degenerations would remain, but at
shifted values of
\alpha
. So these are defect marginal
operators that exist to all orders in the
\varepsilon

expansion, but presumably are not exactly marginal.

Going to higher orders in perturbation theory may also be useful
to try to make connections with
O(N)
models in three dimensions,
which possess varied universality classes of surface
defects
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

Tilt and displacement operators are nonperturbatively protected.
Tilts are exactly marginal
and move the defect along the defect conformal manifold, which in our
cases are either the Grassmannian

\Gr(n,\mathbb{R}^{N})=O(N)/(O(n)\times O(m))
or

O(N)/(U(p)\times O(N-2p))
,
a
O(2p)/U(p)
bundle over
\Gr(2p,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In particular the second
case is an example of a homogeneous but not symmetric space.
While this is a natural consequence of complicated symmetry
breaking patterns, in practice few have been studied in detail
(but see e.g.
[
59
]
).

In particular, in this case the theory has two different types of
tilt operators, those responsible for motion along the base
and those along the fibre. These surface operators
offer a tractable example of such rich defect conformal manifolds.

We also noted that the real Grassmannian is not simply connected,
so there are natural local vortex operators on them, which warrant
further study. In certain cases the space of defects is not connected,
allowing for line interfaces between defects on the two
connected components. Of course line interfaces can exist also between
two surface operators on the same connected component, or
between completely inequivalent surface operators.
Yet another generalisation of connecting two semi-infinite surfaces
is to glue them along a “crease,” as already considered in
this context in
[
27
]
. This fits within the
more general framework of defect fusion, as discussed in

[
43
,
44
]
and it would
be interesting to explore it in detail for our examples.

For all the models and all the fixed points we evaluated the
normalisation constants
of the displacements and tilts:
C_{\mathbb{D}}
,
C_{\mathbbm{t}}
respectively. We also
examined their behaviour under RG flows showing in detail the
protection of the dimension at all scales. In the
simple one-dimensional system of a symmetry preserving
flow we solved for the explicit
flow. In other cases the explicit flows can generically only be
solved numerically.
