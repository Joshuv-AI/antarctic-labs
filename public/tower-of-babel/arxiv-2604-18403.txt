Nested Sequents for Horn-Characterizable Quantified Modal Logics with Equality via Reachability Rules
Tim S. Lyon, Eugenio Orlandelli

Abstract




Abstract.
We introduce cut-free nested sequent systems for a broad class of quantified modal logics (QMLs). The QMLs we consider are semantically defined using relational models that assign both an inner and outer domain to each world. This rich model structure enables the specification of various QMLs by enforcing different frame conditions, including increasing, decreasing, constant, and empty domains, as well as general path conditions and seriality. We extend the usual notion of nested sequent to include signatures, i.e., multisets of terms, which let us naturally define rules capturing the aforementioned domain conditions. A distinctive feature of our nested sequent systems is the use of reachability rules—inference rules parameterized by formal grammars (viz., semi-Thue systems). These rules operate by propagating or consuming formulae or terms along certain paths within a nested sequent, where paths are encoded as strings generated by a parameterizing grammar. This paper is the first to provide sound and complete nested systems for QMLs semantically characterized by models using both inner and outer domains. We analyze the proof-theoretic properties of these systems, identify a number of admissible structural rules, establish the invertibility of all rules, and prove a non-trivial syntactic cut-elimination theorem. We also observe that the standard universal quantifier rule used in nested systems subsumes the Extended Barcan Rule, which forces nested systems to capture QMLs with constant outer domains.

§1. Introduction

Since Gentzen’s seminal work on proof theory
[
12
,
13
]
,
analytic proof systems
have played a central role in both the study and application of logics. These systems (de)compose logical theorems step by step using inference rules, making them especially effective for establishing non-trivial properties such as interpolation and decidability, as well as for enabling automated reasoning. One of the most influential frameworks for constructing such systems is Gentzen’s
sequent calculus

[
12
,
13
]
, which represents proofs via (pairs of) multisets of formulae known as
sequents
. Despite the utility of sequent systems, it is well-known that certain logics resist presentation as an analytic sequent calculus. This has led to numerous generalizations of Gentzen’s formalism to supply ever more expressive logics with analytic proof systems. Such extensions are broadly called
multisequent systems
and include
display calculi

[
2
]
,
hypersequent calculi

[
1
]
,
labeled calculi

[
37
,
41
]
, and
nested calculi

[
5
,
17
]
. This proof-theoretic paradigm has proven highly effective, being used to establish properties of logics such as decidability
[
37
,
39
]
, interpolation
[
9
,
23
]
, and complexity-hardness
[
22
]
.

While multisequent systems for
propositional
non-classical logics have been extensively studied, the literature on such systems for
quantified
non-classical logics remains comparatively sparse. The quantified setting proves challenging because standard inference rules often prove too crude to capture the nuances inherent in non-classical quantifiers. For example, if we add the standard rules for the universal quantifier to a nested system for a quantified modal logic (QML), then both the Barcan formula (
\mathbf{BF}
)
\forall x\Box\varphi\supset\Box\forall x\varphi
and its converse (
\mathbf{CBF}
)
\Box\forall x\varphi\supset\forall x\Box\varphi
become provable, meaning, the nested system necessarily captures a QML with
constant domains
(see
[
24
]
). In the context of display calculi, Belnap has noted that this phenomenon occurs “because these rules for the quantifiers are
structure free
[
\ldots
], which is an indication of an unrefined account”
[
2
, p. 409]
. One can bypass this problem by incorporating
signatures
(i.e., multisets of terms) into multisequents, which implicitly encode a (local) existence predicate
\mathcal{E}
and restrict how quantifiers are introduced; see
[
31
,
24
,
33
,
40
]
. This allows for one to capture QMLs with alternative domain conditions such as increasing or decreasing domains (cf.
[
33
,
26
]
).

In the context of QMLs, the labeled sequent formalism stands out as one of the most studied approaches, having been used to provide well-behaved calculi for a range of QMLs (e.g.,
[
33
,
34
,
32
,
41
]
). As shown in
[
34
]
, the labeled formalism enables the construction of (cut-free) sound and complete calculi for a broad class of semantically defined QMLs—many of which fail to have a known axiomatic system. Although the labeled sequent formalism is known to be quite general, uniform, and modular (see Lyon et al.
[
21
]
for a discussion), the formalism does have significant drawbacks. In particular, labeled sequent systems often introduce redundant syntactic structures in sequents and superfluous inferences in proofs, unnecessarily inflating proof size in many cases
[
30
,
25
]
. Also, in contrast to other multisequent formalisms (e.g., hypersequents and nested sequents), labeled sequents typically fail to have a known formula interpretation, and in some cases, it is even known that labeled sequents fail to have a formula interpretation altogether
[
21
]
. To rectify these drawbacks, we study nested sequent systems for QMLs in this paper.

A
nested sequent
is a tree of Gentzen sequents. The creation of the nested sequent formalism is often attributed to Bull
[
5
]
and Kashima
[
17
]
, though later work by Brünnler
[
4
]
and Poggiolesi
[

]
was instrumental to the popularity of the formalism.

It should be noted that Leivant
[

introduced a notational variant of nested sequents in 1981 (which prefixes formulae with so-called

) in his proof-theoretic work on propositional dynamic logic.
This formalism is rather elegant, yielding proof calculi that require minimal syntactic bureaucracy, have compact proofs, and where termination of proof search is more easily obtained
[
28
,
25
]
. Moreover, the rules of such systems are typically height-preserving invertible, which allows for counter-models to be extracted from failed proof search, and usually nested systems admit syntactic cut-elimination. Due to these nice aesthetic and computational properties, nested sequent systems have found a range of applications, being used in knowledge integration algorithms
[
22
]
, serving as a basis for constructive interpolation and decidability techniques
[
27
,
23
,
39
]
, and even being used to solve open questions about axiomatizability
[
16
]
. See
[
20
]
for a survey on nested sequent systems and their applications.

In this paper, we define and study nested sequent systems for a broad class of QMLs. The QMLs we consider subsume those treated by Corsi
[
7
]
, and are semantically defined using relational models that assign both an inner and outer domain of elements to each world. Distinct QMLs are then characterized by imposing frame conditions on the accessibility relation of a model (as usual), or by imposing conditions on the inner domains associated with worlds (cf.
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
). Regarding the latter, one obtains distinct QMLs depending on if domains are permitted to be empty, if domains are permitted to increase, decrease, or be constant along the accessibility relation, or if inner and outer domains are required to be equal. The QMLs we consider consists of extensions of a base logic
\mathsf{Q}^{\circ}_{=}.\mathsf{K}
with seriality, general path conditions, and the aforementioned domain conditions (cf.
[
7
]
).

In order to give this class of logics a uniform nested sequent presentation, we use signatures (i.e., multisets of terms) in our nested sequents, which are used to process quantificational data. Additionally, we make use of
reachability rules
, which possess two kinds of functionality: such rules may (1) propagate formulae or terms along paths in a nested sequent and/or (2) search for data along paths in a nested sequent. The first kind of functionality is exhibited by the well-known class of
propagation rules

[
10
,
15
]
, which have proven vital for the provision of nested sequent systems for propositional modal and constructive logics
[
6
,
14
]
. Rules exhibiting the second kind of functionality have only been defined more recently in the context of first-order constructive logics
[
11
,
29
,
26
]
. We use these rules in our setting to capture reasoning with the diverse class of frame conditions we consider.

This paper serves as a journal version of the conference paper
[
24
]
, and makes the following new contributions:

(1)

We simplify our nested systems by using one-sided nested sequents for a language in negation normal form.

(2)

We provide cut-free nested sequent calculi for a significant extension of the QMLs considered in
[
24
]
, permitting QMLs characterized by general path conditions of the form
\forall w,u,v\in\mathcal{W}(w\mathcal{R}^{n}u\ \&\ w\mathcal{R}^{k}v\rightarrow u\mathcal{R}v)
.

(3)

We define and incorporate (new) reachability rules into our nested systems, which allow for a unified and modular treatment of all QMLs within a single nested sequent presentation.

(4)

We observe that the
Extended Barcan Rule

\mathbf{EBR}
(see Corsi
[
7
]
) is an instance of the universal quantifier rule in the nested sequent setting. This forces the QMLs we consider to have constant outer domains, suggesting that nested sequents ‘naturally capture’ this class of QMLs, even with the use of signatures in nested sequents.

(5)

We identify and prove a selection of structural rules height-preserving admissible in our nested systems, show that the rules of all nested systems are height-preserving invertible, and establish a non-trivial syntactic cut-elimination theorem. Our cut-elimination theorem is interesting in at least two respects: first, our use of reachability rules allows for the proof to be
uniform
in the sense that
ad hoc
elimination strategies are not needed to deal with special logics; cf.
[
4
,
24
]
. Second, cut-elimination relies on the height-preserving admissibility of a novel structural rule, referred to as the
shift rule
, which propagates nestings along paths of a nested sequent. The shift rule serves as a generic structural rule capturing all general path conditions imposed on a QML simultaneously.

Paper Organization.

In Section
2
, we define the class of QMLs we consider, as well as provide the grammar theoretical foundations for the formulation of our reachability rules. In Section
3
, we introduce our nested sequent calculi for QMLs and prove them sound relative to the semantics used by Corsi
[
7
]
. We also establish completeness by generalizing and adapting a method of Kripke
[
18
]
to our nested sequent setting. To be more precise, we show how to extract counter-models from failed attempts to find a nested sequent proof of a given formula. In Section
4
, we establish our height-preserving admissibility and invertibility results, which are used to prove the admissibility of the Extended Barcan Rule
\mathbf{EBR}
in
every
nested system we have defined. We also identify the novel ‘shift’ structural rule that allows for a uniform proof of syntactic cut-elimination. In the final section (Section
5
), we discuss future work and conclude.

We let

2.3. Grammar-Theoretic Preliminaries

As will be seen later on, a significant aspect of our nested calculi is the incorporation of inference (viz. reachability) rules whose applicability depends upon certain strings generated by a formal grammar. Therefore, the current section introduces the grammar-theoretic notions required to properly define such rules.

We define our
alphabet

\Upsigma:=\{R,\bar{R}\}
to be the set of
characters

R
and
\bar{R}
, and we let
X\in\Upsigma
. We make use of the symbols
R
and
\bar{R}
to encode information in inference rules about worlds in the
future
and
past
(resp.) of an accessibility relation
\mathcal{R}
in a model. We say that
R
and
\bar{R}
are
converses
, and define the
converse operation

{X}^{-1}
(on characters) as
{R}^{-1}:=\bar{R}
and
{\bar{R}}^{-1}:=R
.

We let
\cdot
denote the typical
concatenation operation
with
\varepsilon
the
empty string
. The set
\Upsigma^{*}
of
strings over
\Upsigma
is defined to be the smallest set satisfying the following conditions: (i)
\Upsigma\cup\{\varepsilon\}\subseteq\Upsigma^{*}
, and (ii)
\text{If }\mathtt{s}\in\Upsigma^{*}\text{ and }X\in\Upsigma\text{, then }\mathtt{s}\cdot X\in\Upsigma^{*}
. We use
\mathtt{s}
,
\mathtt{t}
,
\mathtt{r}
,
\ldots
(potentially annotated) to denote strings from
\Upsigma^{*}
. Also, we will often write the concatenation of two strings
\mathtt{s}
and
\mathtt{t}
as
\mathtt{s}\mathtt{t}
as opposed to
\mathtt{s}\cdot\mathtt{t}
, and for the empty string
\varepsilon
, we have
\mathtt{s}\varepsilon=\varepsilon\mathtt{s}=\mathtt{s}
. The converse operation on strings (adapted from
[
39
]
) is defined accordingly: (i)
{\varepsilon}^{-1}:=\varepsilon
, and (ii)
\text{If }\mathtt{s}=X_{1}\cdots X_{n}\text{, then }{\mathtt{s}}^{-1}:={X}^{-1}_{n}\cdots{X}^{-1}_{1}
.

We now define
\Upsigma
-systems
, which are special kinds of
semi-Thue systems

[
36
]
that encode information about the conditions imposed on frames and models, and which will parameterize inference rules in our proof systems later on.

Definition 2.9

(
\Upsigma
-System)
.

We define a
\Upsigma
-system
to be a set
\mathrm{S}
of
production rules
of the form
X\longrightarrow\mathtt{s}
, where
X\in\Upsigma
and
\mathtt{s}\in\Upsigma^{*}
.

Specific
\Upsigma
-systems will be of use to us in this paper. In particular, for a set
\mathbf{G}
of generalized path conditions, we define the
\Upsigma
-system
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
as follows:

(R\longrightarrow\bar{R}^{n}R^{k}),(\bar{R}\longrightarrow\bar{R}^{k}R^{n})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})

iff

\mathbf{G}(n,k)\in\mathbf{G}
.

We also define the specific
\Upsigma
-systems
\mathrm{S4}
and
\mathrm{S5}
:

\bullet

\mathrm{S4}:=\{R\longrightarrow\varepsilon,\bar{R}\longrightarrow\varepsilon,R\longrightarrow RR,\bar{R}\longrightarrow\bar{R}\bar{R}\}
;

\bullet

\mathrm{S5}:=\{R\longrightarrow\varepsilon,\bar{R}\longrightarrow\varepsilon,R\longrightarrow\bar{R}R,\bar{R}\longrightarrow\bar{R}R\}
.

Since
\mathrm{S4}
and
\mathrm{S5}
are
\Upsigma
-systems, any property that holds for an arbitrary
\Upsigma
-system holds for
\mathrm{S4}
and
\mathrm{S5}
as well. We will describe the significance of these two
\Upsigma
-systems shortly. First, let us make the notion of a
derivation
and
language
precise in the context of
\Upsigma
-systems.

Definition 2.10

(Derivation, Language)
.

Let
\mathrm{S}
be a
\Upsigma
-system. We write
\mathtt{s}\longrightarrow\mathtt{t}
and say that the string
\mathtt{t}
may be derived from the string
\mathtt{s}
in
\Upsigma^{*}
in
one-step

iff
there are strings
\mathtt{s}^{\prime},\mathtt{t}^{\prime}\in\Upsigma^{*}
and
X\longrightarrow\mathtt{r}\in\mathrm{S}
such that
\mathtt{s}=\mathtt{s}^{\prime}X\mathtt{t}^{\prime}
and
\mathtt{t}=\mathtt{s}^{\prime}\mathtt{r}\mathtt{t}^{\prime}
. We define the
derivation relation

\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathrm{S}}
to be the reflexive and transitive closure of
\longrightarrow
. For
\mathtt{s},\mathtt{t}\in\Upsigma^{*}
, we call
\mathtt{s}\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathrm{S}}\mathtt{t}
a
derivation of
\mathtt{t}
from
\mathtt{s}
, and define the
length
of a derivation to be the minimal number of one-step derivations required to derive
\mathtt{t}
from
\mathtt{s}
in
\mathrm{S}
. Moreover, we define the
language

L_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathtt{s}):=\{\mathtt{t}\ |\ \mathtt{s}\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathrm{S}}\mathtt{t}\}
, where
\mathtt{s}\in\Upsigma^{*}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a set of generalized path conditions.

(1)

\mathtt{s}=X_{1}\cdots X_{n}\in L_{\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})}(X)
iff
{\mathtt{s}}^{-1}={X}^{-1}_{n}\cdots{X}^{-1}_{1}\in L_{\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})}({X}^{-1})
.

(2)

If
L=L_{\mathrm{S4}\cup\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})}(X)
, then either
L=L_{\mathrm{S4}}(X)
or
L=L_{\mathrm{S5}}(X)
.

Proof.

Claim (1) follows from the definition of
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
since for each production rule
X\longrightarrow\mathtt{s}\in\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
there is a production rule
{X}^{-1}\longrightarrow{\mathtt{s}}^{-1}\in\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
. To prove claim (2), we first observe that
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
either contains a production rule of the form
X\longrightarrow\mathtt{s}{X}^{-1}\mathtt{t}
or it does not. Since
R\longrightarrow\varepsilon,\bar{R}\longrightarrow\varepsilon\in\mathrm{S4}
, we have that
X\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{S4}\cup\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})}^{*}{X}^{-1}
, which, along with the other rules of
\mathrm{S4}
, will permit us to derive any string from
X
, i.e.
L=L_{\mathrm{S5}}(X)
. If
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
does not contain a production rule of the form
X\longrightarrow\mathtt{s}{X}^{-1}\mathtt{t}
, then it will also not contain a production rule of the form
{X}^{-1}\longrightarrow\mathtt{r}X\mathtt{r}^{\prime}
by the same reason justifying claim (1) above. Therefore, every production rule of
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
must be of the form
R\longrightarrow R^{n}
or
\bar{R}\longrightarrow\bar{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
X^{0}=\varepsilon
. Observe that all production rules in
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
can be derived in
\mathrm{S4}
, i.e.
R\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{S4}}^{*}R^{n}
or
\bar{R}\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{S4}}^{*}\bar{R}^{n}
, showing that
L=L_{\mathrm{S4}}(X)
as
\mathrm{S4}
can already derive whatever can be derived with
\mathrm{S}(\mathbf{G})
.
∎

\mathrm{S4}
encodes a notion of
directed
reachability and
\mathrm{S5}
encodes a notion of
undirected
reachability. Given a frame, recognizing what worlds are reachable from one another along paths of the accessibility relation
\mathcal{R}
is relevant in our context as frame conditions such as
\mathbf{G}(n,k)
connect initial and terminal worlds, and
\mathbf{ID}
and
\mathbf{DD}
require inner domains to contain certain elements along
\mathcal{R}
-paths. To see how
\mathrm{S4}
and
\mathrm{S5}
encode notions of (un)directed reachability, let us think of
R
as representing a forward move along the accessibility relation of a model, and
\bar{R}
as representing a backward move along the accessibility relation. Then,
\mathrm{S4}
defines the languages
L_{\mathrm{S4}}(R)=\{R^{n}\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
L_{\mathrm{S4}}(\bar{R})=\{\bar{R}^{n}\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where each string
R^{n}
and
\bar{R}^{n}
(denoting a sequence of
n

R
’s and
n

\bar{R}
’s, respectively, with
R^{0}=\bar{R}^{0}=\varepsilon
) ‘connects’ a world
w
and
u
if
u
can be reached in
n
forward steps or backward steps (resp.) along the accessibility relation from
w
. Similarly,
\mathrm{S5}
defines the language
L_{\mathrm{S5}}(R)=L_{\mathrm{S5}}(\bar{R})=\Upsigma^{*}
where each string
X_{1}\cdots X_{n}
‘connects’ a world
w
to
u
if
u
is reachable from
w
in
n
steps (forward and/or backward).

§3. Nested Sequent Systems

We now present our nested sequent systems for QMLs. Let
\vec{\varphi}
be a (finite) multiset of formulae and
\vec{t}\subset\mathit{Ter}
be a (finite) multiset of terms, which we refer to as a
signature
. We define
nested sequents
accordingly:

1.

Each
flat sequent
of the form
\vec{t},\vec{\varphi}
is a nested sequent;

2.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, if
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is a nested sequent, then
\vec{t},\vec{\varphi},[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\ldots,[\mathcal{H}_{n}]
is a nested sequent.

In the context of nested sequents, we use
\Gamma
,
\Delta
,
\Sigma
,
\ldots
to denote flat sequents,
\vec{t}
,
\vec{s}
,
\vec{r}
,
\ldots
to denote signatures,
\vec{\varphi}
,
\vec{\psi}
,
\vec{\xi}
,
\ldots
to denote multisets of formulae, and
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{K}
,
\ldots
to denote nested sequents. The use of signatures in nested sequents is crucial for encoding the
\mathbf{ID}
,
\mathbf{DD}
,
\mathbf{CD}
, and
\mathbf{NE}
conditions in inference rules. Signatures have been used in nested sequents for other first-order non-classical logics
[
29
,
26
]
to encode similar conditions.

For a nested sequent
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma,[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\ldots,[\mathcal{H}_{m}]
, we define a
component
of the nested sequent to be a flat sequent appearing in
\mathcal{G}
, that is, a component of
\mathcal{G}
is an element of the multiset
c(\mathcal{G})
, where the function
c
is recursively defined as follows with
\uplus
denoting the multiset union:

c(\Gamma,[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\ldots,[\mathcal{H}_{m}])=\{\Gamma\}\uplus\!\!\biguplus_{1\leq i\leq m}\!\!c(\mathcal{H}_{i}).

We let
\mathit{Names}
be a denumerable set of pairwise distinct labels and use
w
,
u
,
v
,
\ldots
(occasionally annotated) to denote them. For each nested sequent
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma,[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\ldots,[\mathcal{H}_{m}]
, we call
\Gamma
the
root
of
\mathcal{G}
and assume that every component of
\mathcal{G}
is assigned a unique name from
\mathit{Names}
. The incorporation of names in our nested sequents is crucial for the definition of our reachability rules below. A
context
is a nested sequent with holes; a hole
\{\}
takes the place of a formula in a nested sequent and
\mathcal{G}\{\mathcal{H}\}
denotes the sequent obtained by filling the hole in the context
\mathcal{G}\{\}
with the nested sequent
\mathcal{H}
. As usual, we write
\mathcal{G}\{\emptyset\}
to denote the removal of a hole
\{\}
in a context
\mathcal{G}\{\}
. For example, if
\mathcal{G}\{\}=t,p(x),[s,q(a),\{\}]
and
\mathcal{H}=x,r(x,y),[\exists xp(x)]
, then
\mathcal{G}\{\mathcal{H}\}=t,p(x),[s,q(a),x,r(x,y),[\exists xp(x)]]
and
\mathcal{G}\{\emptyset\}=t,p(x),[s,q(a)]
. We sometimes refer to a component as a
w
-component
if
w
is the name of that component, and we use the notation
\mathcal{G}\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\}_{w_{1}}\cdots\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\}_{w_{n}}
to indicate that
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is ‘rooted at’ the
w_{i}
-component of
\mathcal{G}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Often, we will more simply write
\mathcal{G}\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\}\cdots\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\}
if the names of the components are not of relevance. Observe that every nested sequent encodes a
tree
whose nodes are (named) flat sequents.

Definition 3.1

(Tree of a Nested Sequent)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\Gamma,[\mathcal{H}_{1}]_{w_{1}},\ldots,[\mathcal{H}_{m}]_{w_{n}}
be a nested sequent with
w
the name of the component
\Gamma
. We recursively define the tree
\mathtt{tr}(\mathcal{G})=(V,E)
as follows:

V=\{(w,\Gamma)\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}\qquad E=\{(w,w_{i})\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}

such that
\mathtt{tr}(\mathcal{H}_{i})=(V_{i},E_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

As is typical of nested sequents, they admit interpretation directly on models
and
possess an equivalent formula interpretation, that is, every nested sequent is equivalent to a formula in the language
\mathcal{L}
. Both interpretations are useful, and so, we present both interpretations below.

Definition 3.2

(Sequent Semantics)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a nested sequent with
w
the name of the root,
\mathtt{tr}(\mathcal{G})=(V,E)
,
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{I}\rangle
be a model, and
\sigma
be an
\mathcal{M}
-assignment. We define an
\mathcal{M}
-interpretation
to be a function
\iota:\mathit{Names}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
. We say that
\mathcal{G}
is satisfied on
\mathcal{M}
with
\sigma
and
\iota
, written
\mathcal{M},\sigma,\iota\Vdash\mathcal{G}
,
iff
if conditions (1) and (2) hold, then (3) holds, where conditions (1)–(3) are listed below:

(1)

for each
(u,v)\in E
,
\iota(u)\mathcal{R}\iota(v)
;

(2)

for each
(u,\vec{t},\vec{\varphi})\in V
,
\mathcal{I}_{\iota(u)}^{\sigma}(\vec{t})\in\mathcal{D}_{\iota(u)}
;

(3)

for some
(v,\vec{t},\vec{\varphi})\in V
,
\mathcal{M},\iota(v),\sigma\Vdash\bigvee\vec{\varphi}
.

We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
valid
w.r.t. a class of frames
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{C})

iff
for every model
\mathcal{M}
based on a frame
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{C})
,
\mathcal{M}
-assignment
\sigma
, and
\mathcal{M}
-interpretation
\iota
,
\mathcal{M},\sigma,\iota\Vdash\mathcal{G}
. We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
invalid
otherwise.

Definition 3.3

(Formula Interpretation)
.

We define the formula interpretation
\mathtt{fm}(\mathcal{G})
of a nested sequent
\mathcal{G}
accordingly:

\mathtt{fm}(\vec{t},\vec{\varphi},[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\dots,[\mathcal{H}_{n}]):=(\bigvee_{t\in\vec{t}}\dot{\neg}\mathcal{E}t\lor\bigvee_{\varphi\in\vec{\varphi}}\varphi)\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\Box\,\mathtt{fm}(\mathcal{H}_{i})

where
\bigvee\emptyset:=\bot
, as usual.

Observe that
\mathtt{fm}(\mathcal{G})
requires the existence predicate
\mathcal{E}
to be expressible in the object language, which holds when the language contains the equality, as is the case for
\mathcal{L}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{I}\rangle
be a model,
\sigma
be an
\mathcal{M}
-assignment,
\iota
be an
\mathcal{M}
-interpretation, and
\mathcal{G}=\vec{t},\vec{\varphi},[\mathcal{H}_{1}],\dots,[\mathcal{H}_{n}]
be a nested sequent with
w
the name of the root
\vec{t},\vec{\varphi}
. Then,
\mathcal{M},\sigma,\iota\Vdash\mathcal{G}
iff
\mathcal{M},\iota(w),\sigma\Vdash\mathtt{fm}(\mathcal{G})
.

,

,

,

The
domain propagation rule

\mathsf{dp}
semantically captures the fact that if a term is interpreted in the inner domain of a world, then it is interpreted in the inner domains of all future worlds, all past worlds, or all worlds, depending on if the model has increasing, decreasing, or constant inner domains, respectively. The
classical domains rule

\mathsf{cd}

\mathsf{nd}

non-empty domains rule

\mathsf{nd}

some
object exists in the inner domain of each world. Last, the
seriality rule

\mathsf{d}
corresponds to the seriality condition. The exact semantic reading of each inference rule is made clear in the proof of soundness (see Theorem
3.14
).

Remark 3.5
.

Observe that when the premise

Completeness is proven by extracting a counter-model from failed (potentially non-terminating) proof-search. To be specific, we assume we are given an unprovable nested sequent
\mathcal{G}
and (bottom-up) apply rules from
\mathsf{N}\mathsf{Q}^{\circ}_{=}.\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{C})

\mathcal{G}
. As
\mathcal{G}
is unprovable in
\mathsf{N}\mathsf{Q}^{\circ}_{=}.\mathsf{K}(\mathcal{C})

\mathsf{ax}
. Using this branch, a model can be extracted that falsifies
\mathcal{G}
, which proves the contrapositive of the completeness statement below (Theorem
3.15
).

Although this completeness strategy is common in the domain of proof theory, the actual implementation of this strategy in our setting proves to be rather complex. This is due to the large number of details one must keep track of and the non-triviality of extracting a counter-model from an infinite branch of nested sequents. Due to the length of the completeness proof, we have deferred it to the appendix; the reader can consult Appendix
A
for the full details.

24

§5. Conclusion

In this work, we unified Horn-characterizable QMLs supporting equality within a single nested sequent framework. This was achieved by incorporating signatures in nested sequents and using reachability rules in nested systems. The latter rules are unique in that they propagate or consume formulae or terms along paths in a nested sequent, permitting one to toggle between various QMLs by simply changing the parameterizing
\Upsigma
-systems. This allowed us to provide the first sound and cut-free complete nested systems for a broad class of QMLs characterized by relational models that assign inner and outer domains to worlds (cf. Corsi
[
7
]
), and which are subject to a variety of distinct frame and domain conditions.

In future work, we plan to investigate whether the formula interpretation of nested sequents can be leveraged to extract axiomatic systems for QMLs, in the spirit of
[
16
]
, which applied a similar approach to sub-intuitionistic predicate logics. We note that the specification of axiomatic systems for various QMLs remains open.

Another promising line of research involves generalizing our nested sequent framework to capture broader and alternative classes of QMLs. Three directions are particularly noteworthy. First, as discussed throughout the paper, the Extended Barcan Rule (
\mathbf{EBR}
) is entailed by the standard universal quantifier rule, thereby limiting our nested systems to QMLs characterized by models with constant outer domains. It would be interesting to find an alternative
\forall
rule that allows cut-free nested sequent systems that are sound and complete relative to classes of normal models with non-constant outer domains (cf.
[
7
]
). Second, one could investigate nested systems for QMLs with non-rigid—and possibly non-denoting—terms, drawing inspiration from treatments of definite descriptions based on
\lambda
-abstraction, such as that of Fitting and Mendelsohn
[
8
]
, which has been explored in the context of labeled calculi
[
33
]
. Third, it may be fruitful to extend the framework to capture generalizations of the relational semantics considered in this paper, such as counterpart-theoretic semantics (cf.
[
3
,
34
]
).
