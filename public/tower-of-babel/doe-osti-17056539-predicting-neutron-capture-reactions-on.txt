Project Overview
Neutron capture reactions are central to element synthesis in supernovae, neutron-star mergers,
and nuclear devices. This project addressed a lack of reliable data for unstable nuclei by
developing a computational capability to predict neutron-capture rates, with quantified
uncertainties, across an astrophysically significant and mission-relevant region of the nuclear
chart.
Using machine learning techniques, we developed an uncertainty-quantified effective nuclear
interaction for large-scale shell-model calculations of nuclear level densities and gamma-ray
strength functions. We also developed POTLUQ, a global optical-model potential tailored to the
region of interest. Together, these components enabled statistical reaction calculations that
produce capture rates with quantified uncertainties and correlations. We integrated the rates
into astrophysical simulations of elemental abundances in the weak r-process, supplied reaction
data for LLNL applications, and conducted stockpile-stewardship impact studies.
The project established an improved method for generating realistic capture rates and
propagating their uncertainties into multiphysics applications. The resulting capability
strengthens connections between nuclear physics and astrophysical observations, supports
interpretation of historical radiochemical data, enhances nuclear forensics, and identifies
reaction rates with the greatest influence on astrophysical or application-specific observables.

Mission Impact
This project has developed science and technology tools and capabilities to meet future national
security challenges. It has advanced predictive capabilities in nuclear theory and strengthened
LLNL’s Nuclear, Chemical, and Isotopic Science core competency area. Its activities are well
aligned with community whitepapers in nuclear structure, reactions, and astrophysics, which
inform the National Science Advisory Committee’s long-range plans and thus support LLNL’s S&T
mission. Multiple early-career scientists have been trained at the intersection of fundamental
and applied science relevant to LLNL’s programmatic missions. The newly developed capabilities
have demonstrated impact in radiochemical activation studies of programmatic interest, from
the perspectives both of physical modeling and computational infrastructure. The outcomes of
the project are highly relevant for maintaining and enhancing the safety, security, and
effectiveness of the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile, and for addressing DOE's energy and
environmental security missions.
