1.
Introduction

Since the 1970s, Whittaker modules have been introduced and have attracted considerable attention. As a class of modules that are not weight modules, they play a significant role both in the representation theory of Lie algebras and in physics.
For a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and Lie algebra homomorphism
\eta:\mathfrak{n}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, a
\mathfrak{g}
-module is called a Whittaker module if
x-\eta(x)
acts locally nilpotently on it for any
x\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.
Whittaker modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
were constructed by Arnal and Pinzcon
[
1
]
.
Kostant introduced and studied Whittaker modules for all finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
. The map
\eta
is called non-singular if
\eta(x_{\alpha})\neq 0
for all simple root vectors of
\mathfrak{g}
.
He showed that simple non-singular Whittaker modules are in one-to-one correspondence with the maximal ideals of the center

Z(\mathfrak{g})
of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}

[
17
]
. Further work related to singular Whittaker modules has been carried out by E. Backelin
[
2
]
and E. McDowell
[
26
]
. In
[
10
]
, it was proved that the category of Whittaker modules, for any reductive
\mathfrak{g}
, is extension full in the category of all
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
Whittaker modules have been extensively studied across various algebraic structures, including the Virasoro algebra and its related algebras
[
21
,
22
,
29
,
30
,
38
]
, quantum groups
[
28
,
35
,
39
]
, generalized Weyl algebras
[
6
]
, affine Lie algebras
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
, and Lie superalgebras
[
7
]
. For Lie algebras lacking triangular decomposition,
P. Batra and V. Mazorchuk provided a general framework for studying Whittaker modules, covering simple Lie algebras of Cartan type. They described basic properties, including a block decomposition of the Whittaker category
[
5
]
.

The representation theory of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras has been extensively studied.
A key example is the Witt algebra
W_{n}
, which is the derivation Lie algebra of the polynomial algebra
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}]
.
A central topic in this field is the study of Harish-Chandra modules, which are weight modules with finite-dimensional weight spaces. For
W_{1}
, these modules were classified by O. Mathieu
[
24
]
. The theory of Harish-Chandra modules for the Witt algebra
W_{n}
has also been well developed.
In 1974-1975, A. N. Rudakov studied irreducible smooth representations of Cartan type Lie algebras over the formal power series
\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]]
(not over polynomials), see
[
33
,
34
]
.
In 1999, I. Penkov and V. Serganova determined the supports of all simple weight modules over
W_{n}
and
\bar{S}_{n}

[
31
]
.
Shen and Larsson independently introduced the tensor
W_{n}
-modules
T(P,V)
from a Weyl module
P
and a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V

[
18
,
36
]
.
In
[
20
]
, the simplicity of any
T(P,V)
was completely determined.
Y. Xue and R. Lü obtained the classification of all simple bounded weight modules of
W_{n}

[
40
]
.
Based on this result, D. Grantcharov and V. Serganova shown that any simple Harish-Chandra
W_{n}
-module is isomorphic to a simple quotient of some
T(P,V)

[
14
]
.
In
[
41
]
, the authors investigated the Whittaker category over
W_{n}
. For more related results, we refer to
[
4
,
13
,
23
,
37
]
.

For Cartan type Lie algebras not of type
W
, little progress has been made concerning their modules, especially Whittaker modules. The present paper is devoted to the study of Whittaker modules over a Cartan type Lie algebra of type
S
.
Let
\bar{S}_{n}(n\geq 2)
denote the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with constant divergence. The simplicity of tensor modules of

was studied in
[
12
,
16
,
19
]
.
Recently, the simple Harish-Chandra module of

was classified in
[

]

In this paper, we study the category of Whittaker modules for

Denote
\Delta_{2}=\text{span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{1}}},\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{2}}}\}

Then

is a Whittaker pair in the sense of
[

]

An

Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some necessary preliminaries, including
the Cartan type Lie algebra
\bar{S}_{2}
,
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-modules,
Whittaker modules.

2.1.
Cartan type Lie algebra
\bar{S}_{2}

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the vector space of
n
-dimensional complex vectors. Denote its standard basis by
\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{n}\}
. Let
A_{n}
be the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}]
in the commuting variables
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
,
\cdots
,
t_{n}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
\alpha_{i}
be the
i
-th componont of
\alpha
and
|\alpha|=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}
. For convenience, let
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
and
d_{i}=t_{i}\partial_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
and
t^{\alpha}=t^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}t^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}\cdots t^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}
,
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}
.
The Witt algebra
W_{n}
is the Lie algebra of derivations of
A_{n}
, i.e.,

W_{n}=\text{span}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{i},\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i=1,2,\cdots,n\}.

It has the following Lie bracket:

[t^{\alpha}\partial_{i},t^{\beta}\partial_{j}]=\beta_{i}t^{\alpha+\beta-e_{i}}\partial_{j}-\alpha_{j}t^{\alpha+\beta-e_{j}}\partial_{i},

where all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i,j=1,\dots,n
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\bar{S}_{n}
be the Lie subalgebra of
W_{n}

consisting of all derivations with constant divergence, i.e.,

\bar{S}_{n}=\{\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}\partial_{i}\in W_{n}\mid\sum^{n}_{i=1}\partial_{i}(p_{i})\in\mathbb{C}\}

with
p_{i}\in A_{n}
. Recall that
S_{n}=[\bar{S}_{n},\bar{S}_{n}]
is the simple ideal of codimension 1 in
\bar{S}_{n}
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq-1}
, let
L_{\alpha}=(1+\alpha_{2})t^{\alpha+e_{1}}\partial_{1}-(1+\alpha_{1})t^{\alpha+e_{2}}\partial_{2}
, then

S_{2}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}L_{\alpha},

with brackets

[L_{\alpha},L_{\beta}]=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\alpha_{2}&1+\alpha_{1}\\
1+\beta_{2}&1+\beta_{1}\end{array}\right|L_{\alpha+\beta},\forall\alpha,\beta\in\Phi,

where
\Phi=\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq-1}\setminus\{(-1,-1)\}
. Note that
\bar{S}_{2}=\mathbb{C}d\ltimes S_{2}
for
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
, and the subspace
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}d_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}d_{2}
is the Cartan subalgebra of
\bar{S}_{2}
. Denote
h^{\mathbf{m}}=d_{1}^{m_{1}}d_{2}^{m_{2}}
for
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
.
The Lie algebra
\bar{S}_{2}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}}\bar{S}_{2}(i)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra, where
\bar{S}_{2}(i)=\{u\in\bar{S}_{2}(i)|[d,u]=iu\}
.
Note that
\bar{S}_{2}(0)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
.

Regarding
A_{2}
as an
\bar{S}_{2}
-module via the derivation action, we define the semidirect product Lie algebra
\widetilde{S}_{2}:=\bar{S}_{2}\ltimes A_{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\bar{S}_{2}
-module
M
is called a weight module if the action of
\mathfrak{h}
on
M
is diagonalizable, i.e.,
M=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{2}}M_{\lambda}
, where

M_{\lambda}=\{v\in M\mid d_{i}v=\lambda_{i}v,i=1,2\}.

The space
M_{\lambda}
is called the weight space with weight
\lambda
and
\emph{supp}(M):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{2}|M_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
is called
the support of
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
\widetilde{S}
-module
V
is called
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-module if
A_{2}
acts on
V
associatively, i.e.,

t^{\alpha}(t^{\beta}v)=t^{\alpha+\beta}v,\,t^{0}v=v,\,\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}.

2.2.
Whittaker module of
\bar{S}_{2}

Recall the definition of the Whittaker pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
introduced by
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
is called a Whittaker pair if
\mathfrak{n}
is a quasi-nilpotent subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{n}
on the
\mathfrak{n}
-module
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is locally nilpotent.

For Lie algebra
\bar{S}_{2}
, let
\Delta_{2}=\text{span}\{\partial_{1},\partial_{2}\}
which is an abelian subalgebra. We can check that the adjoint action of
\Delta_{2}
on
\bar{S}_{2}/\Delta_{2}
is locally nilpotent, so
(\bar{S}_{2},\Delta_{2})
is a Whittaker pair. An
\bar{S}_{2}
or
\widetilde{S}_{2}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module if the action of
\Delta_{2}
on
M
is locally finite. For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we can define a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}:\Delta_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(\partial_{i})=a_{i}
for
i=1,2
. For any Whittaker module
M
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we can define the subspace

M(\mathbf{a})=\{v\in M\mid(x-\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x))^{k}v=0,\text{for some}\ k\in\mathbb{N},\forall\ x\in\Delta_{2}\}

which is a submodule
M
. It is easy to see that
M=\oplus_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}}M(\mathbf{a})
.
A Whittaker module
M
is of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
if
M=M(\mathbf{a})
. We define the subspace of
M
as follows:

\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid xv=\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x)v,\forall\,x\in\Delta_{2}\}.

An element of
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is called a Whittaker vector. For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we call it non-singular if
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i=1,2
.

Let
\Omega^{\bar{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}
(resp.
\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}
) denote the category of Whittaker
\bar{S}_{2}
(resp.
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
)-module
M
of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
such that
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)<+\infty
.

3.
The category of Whittaker
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-modules

In this section, we study Whittaker
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-modules.
Let
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}:=\oplus_{i=0}^{+\infty}\bar{S}_{2}(i)
which is a subalgebra of
\bar{S}_{2}
.
We will establish an equivalence between
\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}
and the category of finite-dimensional
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-modules.

3.1.
Whittaker module over
\mathcal{D}_{2}

Let
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be the Weyl algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, which is generated by
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
,
\partial_{1}
,
\partial_{2}
with the relations

[t_{i},t_{j}]=[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,[\partial_{i},t_{j}]=\delta_{i,j},\,\,\,\,i,j=1,2.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
a
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module of type
\mathbf{a}
if for any
v\in M
there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\partial_{i}-a_{i})^{k}v=0
for
i=1,2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{D}_{\mathbf{a}}
be the category consisting of finite length Whittaker
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-modules of type
\mathbf{a}
. Let
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
be the algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{D}_{2}
defined by

t_{i}\rightarrow t_{i},\,\partial_{i}\rightarrow\partial_{i}+a_{i},\,i=1,2.

The
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-module
A_{2}
can be twisted by
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
to be a new
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-module
A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
and the action of
\mathcal{D}_{2}
on
A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
is defined by

x\cdot f(t)=\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}(x)f(t),\,f(t)\in A_{2},\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{2}.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
41
]

(a) Any simple module in
\mathcal{V}^{D}_{\mathbf{a}}
is isomorphic to
A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
.

(b) The category
\mathcal{V}^{D}_{\mathbf{a}}
is semi-simple.

3.2.
The Whittaker category
\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}

Since
A_{2}
is a left module algebra over the Hopf algebra
U(\bar{S}_{2})
, we have the smash product algebra
A_{2}\#U(\bar{S}_{2})
. An
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-module is actually a module
over
A_{2}\#U(\bar{S}_{2})
.

The following isomorphism was given by Hu and Lu.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
15
]

There is an associative algebra isomorphism

\phi:A_{2}\#U(\bar{S}_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{2}\otimes U(\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0})

defined by

\phi(t^{\beta})=t^{\beta}\otimes 1,\quad\phi(d_{i})=d_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes d_{i},

\phi(L_{\alpha})=L_{\alpha}\otimes 1+\sum_{0<r\leq\alpha+e_{1}+e_{2}}\binom{\alpha+e_{1}+e_{2}}{r}t^{r}\otimes L_{\alpha-r},

for
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2},\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq-1}\setminus\{(-1,-1)\}
.

For any
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-module
P
, and
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-module
V
, the tensor product
P\otimes V
becomes an
(A_{2},\bar{S}_{2})
-module
T(P,V)
under the map

\phi
. Let
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}\text{-fmod}
denote the category of finite-dimensional
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-modules.
One can verify that for any finite-dimensional
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-module
V
, the module
T(A_{2}^{\mathbf{a}},V)\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}

and admits
V
as its space of Whittaker vectors, i.e.,
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(T(A_{2}^{\mathbf{a}},V))=V
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
M\in\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, the Whittaker vector subspace
\emph{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is an
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-module
V
and
T(A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2},V)\cong M
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.3
, we can view
M
as a module over
\mathcal{D}_{2}\otimes U(\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0})
.
Note that in
\mathcal{D}_{2}\otimes U(\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0})
, we have
[\mathcal{D}_{2},\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}]=0
. Therefore, for any
v\in\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
, we have
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}v\subseteq\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
,
which implies that
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is an
\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}
-module. Next, we prove that the
\mathcal{D}_{2}\otimes U(\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0})
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\otimes\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)

\displaystyle\rightarrow M,

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}\otimes v

\displaystyle\mapsto t^{\alpha}v,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+},

is an isomorphism.
Since
M\in\mathcal{V}^{D}_{\mathbf{a}}
when it was retricted to
\mathcal{D}_{2}
,
by Lemma
3.2
,
\psi
is surjective.
Suppose that
w=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda}t^{\beta}\otimes v_{\beta}\in\ker\psi
(
\Lambda
is a finite set). For any fixed
\alpha\in\Lambda
, since
A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
is simple, and by Density theorem, there exists some
x\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
such that
xt^{\alpha}=1
,
xt^{\beta}=0
for all
\beta\neq\alpha
,
\beta\in\Lambda
. Then we have
0=x\psi(w)=\psi(xw)=\psi(1\otimes v_{\alpha})=v_{\alpha}
, i.e.,
v_{\alpha}=0
, thus
w=0
. Hence
\psi
is injective.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\bar{S}_{2}^{\geq 0}\emph{-fmod}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto T(A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2},V)

is an equivalence of the two categories.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.4
, for any
M\in\Omega^{\widetilde{S}_{2}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, we have that
\mathcal{F}(V)\cong M
, where
V=\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M))
.
Since

is a simple
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-module, by Schur’s Lemma, we have
\text{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(A^{\mathbf{a}}_{2})=\mathbb{C}
. From

is an isomorphism, for any

-fmod, therefore

is a category equivalence.
∎

g_{\alpha}(d_{1},d_{2})=1,T_{\mathbf{0}}=1,T_{\beta}=L_{\beta},0\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|,\beta\neq\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1},d_{2})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{2}+1)\prod_{i=-1}^{\alpha_{1}-1}(d_{1}-i)\prod_{j=0}^{\alpha_{2}-1}(d_{2}-j)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|\alpha|-1}(\alpha_{1}+1)\alpha_{2}\prod_{i=0}^{\alpha_{1}-1}(d_{1}-i)\prod_{j=-1}^{\alpha_{2}-1}(d_{2}-j),

and

g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})=(-1)^{|\alpha-\beta|}\binom{\alpha+\mathbf{1}}{\beta+\mathbf{1}}\prod_{m_{1}=0}^{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}-1}(d_{1}-m_{1})\prod_{m_{2}=0}^{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{2}-1}(d_{2}-m_{2}).

Using
(\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1})\binom{\alpha_{1}+1}{\beta_{1}+1}=(\beta_{1}+2)\binom{\alpha_{1}+1}{\beta_{1}+2}
, we can check that

g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1},d_{2})+2(d_{1}-d_{2})g_{1,0}(d_{1},d_{2})-2g_{1,-1}(d_{1},d_{2}+1)=0,

and

g_{\beta}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})+(\beta_{1}+2)g_{\beta+e_{1}}(d_{1},d_{2})=0,

for
0\leq|\beta|<|\alpha|
with
\beta\neq\mathbf{0}
.

Then

\displaystyle\ [\partial_{1},Y_{\alpha}]=

\displaystyle[\partial_{1},\sum_{0\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}T_{\beta}g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})\partial^{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\atop\beta\neq\mathbf{0}}(1+\beta_{1})L_{\beta-e_{1}}g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})\partial^{\beta}+(g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{e_{1}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\atop\beta\neq\mathbf{0}}L_{\beta}(g_{\beta}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{\beta+e_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq|\beta|<|\alpha|\atop\beta\neq\mathbf{0}}L_{\beta}(g_{\beta}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})+(\beta_{1}+2)g_{\beta+e_{1}}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{\beta+e_{1}}

\displaystyle+2L_{(0,-1)}g_{(1,-1)}(d_{1},d_{2})\partial^{e_{1}-e_{2}}+L_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\alpha}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{\alpha+e_{1}}

\displaystyle+2L_{\mathbf{0}}g_{(1,0)}(d_{1},d_{2})\partial^{e_{1}}+(g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{e_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq|\beta|<|\alpha|\atop\beta\neq\mathbf{0}}L_{\beta}(g_{\beta}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\beta}(d_{1},d_{2})+(\beta_{1}+2)g_{\beta+e_{1}}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{\beta+e_{1}}

\displaystyle+(2L_{\mathbf{0}}g_{(1,0)}(d_{1},d_{2})-2g_{(1,-1)}(d_{1},d_{2}+1)+g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1}+1,d_{2})-g_{\mathbf{0}}(d_{1},d_{2}))\partial^{e_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Similarly, we can compute that
[\partial_{2},Y_{\alpha}]=0
.
∎

It is easy to verify that
\bar{S}_{2}
is generated by the elements

L_{(0,-1)},L_{(-1,0)},d_{2},L_{(1,-1)}
,
L_{(-1,1)},L_{(1,0)}
and
L_{(0,1)}
.
Let
B
be the subalgebra of
U_{(-1)}

generated by
\partial_{i},d_{i},\partial_{i}^{-1},i=1,2
.
The following result gives a tensor product decomposition of
U_{(-1)}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

U_{(-1)}=BH\cong B\otimes H
.

Proof.

Clearly
BH\subseteq U_{(-1)}
. To show that
U_{(-1)}\subseteq BH
, it is sufficient to prove that the generators of
\bar{S}_{2}
belong to
BH
. However,
\bar{S}_{2}
is generated by
L_{(0,-1)},L_{(-1,0)},d_{2},L_{(1,-1)}
,
L_{(-1,1)},L_{(1,0)}
and
L_{(0,1)}
. These generators belong to

BH
, since

\displaystyle L_{(0,-1)}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{2}\in BH,\quad L_{-1,0}=\partial_{1}\in BH,\quad d_{2}\in BH,

\displaystyle L_{(1,-1)}

\displaystyle=-2t_{1}\partial_{2}=Y_{(1,-1)}\partial_{1}^{-1}\partial_{2}-2(t_{1}\partial_{1})\partial_{1}^{-1}\partial_{2}\in BH,

\displaystyle L_{(-1,1)}

\displaystyle=2t_{2}\partial_{1}=Y_{(-1,1)}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}^{-1}+2(t_{2}\partial_{2})\partial_{1}\partial_{2}^{-1}\in BH,

\displaystyle L_{(1,0)}

\displaystyle=Y_{(1,0)}\partial_{1}^{-1}-2t_{1}\partial_{2}(t_{2}\partial_{2})\partial_{1}\partial_{2}^{-1}\partial_{1}^{-1}+(t_{1}\partial_{1})^{2}\partial_{1}^{-1}+t_{1}\partial_{1}\partial_{1}^{-1}\in BH,

\displaystyle L_{(0,1)}

\displaystyle=Y_{(0,1)}\partial_{2}^{-1}+2t_{2}\partial_{1}(t_{1}\partial_{1})\partial_{2}\partial_{1}^{-1}\partial_{2}^{-1}-(t_{2}\partial_{2})^{2}\partial_{2}^{-1}-t_{2}\partial_{2}\partial_{2}^{-1}\in BH.

So
U_{(-1)}=BH
.
Since
[B,H]=0
, we can define the algebra homomorphism

\gamma:B\otimes H\rightarrow U_{(-1)}
such that

\gamma(b\otimes u)=bu
for any
b\in B,u\in H
.
Since
U_{(-1)}=BH
,
\gamma
is surjective. Since

B
is a simple algebra,
\ker\gamma=B\otimes I
for some ideal of

I
of
H
. Since
\gamma|_{H}=\mathrm{Id}_{H}
, we must have that
I=0
.
Thus
\ker\gamma=0
, and hence
\gamma
is injective. Therefore
\gamma
is isomorphic.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

We have that
H\cong H_{\mathbf{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
B_{\mathbf{1}}=B\otimes_{U(\Delta_{2})}\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}}
. We know that
B_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathbb{C}[d_{1},d_{2}]v_{\mathbf{1}}
as a vector space. Since

(\partial_{i}-1)g(d_{1},d_{2})v_{\mathbf{1}}=(g(d_{1}+\delta_{1,i},d_{2}+\delta_{2,i})-g(d_{1},d_{2}))v_{\mathbf{1}},

where
g(d_{1},d_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[d_{1},d_{2}]
,
i=1,2
, any nonzero
B
-submodule
V^{\prime}
of
B_{\mathbf{1}}
must contain
v_{\mathbf{1}}
. So
V^{\prime}=B_{\mathbf{1}}
and
B_{\mathbf{1}}
is an irreducible
B
-module.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.4
,
we obtain that the module
Q_{\mathbf{1}}
is isomorphic to
B_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes H
. According to Schur’s Lemma, we have
\text{End}_{B}(B_{\mathbf{1}})\cong\mathbb{C}
, thus
H_{\mathbf{1}}\cong\text{End}_{B\otimes H}(B_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes H)^{\text{op}}\cong\text{End}_{H}(H)^{\text{op}}\cong H
.
∎
