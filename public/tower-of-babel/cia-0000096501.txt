WASHINGTON 28, D. Cc

OFFICE OF THE DIRECTOR

21 AUG 1958

MEMORANDUM FOR THE PRESIDENT

SUBJECT: Progress Report on the Implementation of Recommendation
No. 2 of the Report to the President by the President's
Board of Consultants on Foreign Intelligence Activities
dated 20 December 1956

On 20 December 1956 the President's Board of Consultants on Foreign
Intelligence Activities made the following recommendation:

~

"To insure the proper functioning of the Intelligence Cammnity
and to guarantee proper coordination with the military in time

of war, we suggest that a realistic appraisal of the Community's
organization and responsibilities be undertaken at once. This

should be undertaken directly by the Director of Central Intel-
ligence, and he should report his appraisal and recommendations
to the Board of Consultants on Foreign Intelligence Activities.”

In my interim report to you on 30 April 1957 I pointed out that the
organization of the intelligence community in time of war or peace depends
on the collection of directives issued to it by the National Security
Council, and that those directives were under detailed review in compliance
with Recommendation No. 9 of the Board. This review has now been completed
and coordinated drafts of new National Security Council Intelligence Direc-
tives have been submitted to the National Security Council. Approval of
these directives will leave the intelligence community with a clear set of
instructions concerning its organizations and functions and will enable
the intelligence conmmnity to begin the development of detailed plans to
implement these functions in time of ver.

During the time since my last report, the decisions taken in re-
vising the National Security Council Intelligence Directives have sub-
stantially clarified the role of Director of Central Intelligence in
providing "strong centralized direction". This clarification will have
an important bearing on the development of our plans for wartime opera-
tions.

a. The development by the intelligence community of a new

authorities. This system was put into effect on 21 July 1958 using
existing communication facilities and has already resulted in sub-
stantial savings in the time required to report items of critical
intelligence. It is anticipated that further additional amounts
of time in the handling of critical intelligence will be saved as
communications facilities are developed specifically for the hand-
