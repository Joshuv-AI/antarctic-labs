 Capstone Project Report Outline
Title
 Preliminary Proof of Concept for Community Use of Artificial Intelligence During Air
 Quality Event Response
Author and affiliations

 Kyle Fuhrer, Oregon State University Graduate School, Environmental Science,
 Professional Science Master’s Program, and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory
 (LLNL), Livermore, CA
Introduction
 Context and motivation

 • Artificial Intelligence (AI) capabilities are expanding and can enhance
 organizational performance and individual productivity
 • LLNL is investing in AI-enabled research areas such as high-performance
 computing, advanced materials/manufacturing, earth and atmospheric science
 Problem statement/opportunity

 • Beneficial AI applications for environmental monitoring and response,
 specifically during elevated Air Quality Index (AQI) events, are
 underexplored
 • Better AQI event response can help LLNL and the public adapt to impacts
 from natural disasters and climate change
 Project purpose

 • Provide proof of concept showing how AI can enhance environmental and
 emergency response during elevated AQI events
Literature Review

 AI & big data for air quality prediction

 • Fu et al. (2023) as a key example of:
 o A hybrid machine learning model predicting pollutant concentrations
 o A deep learning model integrating time, space, and meteorology to
 forecast pollutants
 o Pollutants forecasted under heavy conditions: PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, NO₂, SO₂,
 CO, O₃ across 15 provinces in China
 o Key takeaway emphasized: future monitoring may need multi-model
 approaches that process big data, rather than single model approaches

 3

 o Relevance to this project: AI can improve monitoring accuracy and
 potentially strengthen policy and response.
AI in disaster management across event phases

 • Linardos et al. (2022) synthesis of Machine Learning/Deep Learning
 (ML/DL) applications across disaster management:
 o Examples: rainfall warning, earthquake signal recognition, flood and
 wildfire image detection
 o Observed research emphasis: post-disaster response and damage
 assessment are most studies
 o Identified gap relevant to this project: opportunity to enhance
 preparedness and mitigation, not just emergency response
 • Gan et al. (2024) comparison of ChatGPT vs Google Bard (Gemini) on Mass
 Casualty Incident (MCI) triage, AI performance in emergency management
 decision tasks:
 o Both models trained to apply MCI Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment
 (START) methodology in 15 scenarios
 o Compared to performance from 315 medical students
 o Bard outperformed ChatGPT consistently, with differing strengths
 based on each task component
 o Relevance: commonly available AI tools may support emergency
 response, but models differ and need further evaluation
LLNL-relevant research avenues for AQI event protection

 • Potential opportunity within US Department of Energy and LLNL to improve
 ES&H and Emergency Management preparedness, mitigation, and response
 • Dillon et al. (2022) building occupant protection model:
 o Indoor/outdoor exposure ratio parameters by particle size, building
 type, filtration
 o Creation of Regional Shelter Analysis
 o Proposed future extensions (conceptual, for later work):
 o Use ML to enhance parameters for RSA
 o Scenario simulations, real-time sensor analysis during AQI events
 • Massey et al. (2021) on N95 reuse after heat treatment during COVID-19:
 o Motivated by PPE shortages, addressed supply chain issues, limited
 resources
 o Potential AI extension ideas:
  Optimize heat treatment protocols.
  Scale PPE management strategies during AQI events

 Environmental costs of AI

 • Kshetri (2024) outlines three phases of GPT-3 development:
 o Training, inference, hardware/infrastructure production
 o Impact framing used for accountability
 o Inference phase electricity and water use figures (as a cautionary
 reference).
 o Relevance: encourages carbon footprint awareness during project’s
 inference/use phase.
Objectives
 Objective 1: Define user community

 • Under LLNL ES&H guidance, define the LLNL user community that could
 benefit from AI use during AQI events
 Objective 2: AI Model performance evaluation

 • Evaluate how well ChatGPT, Claude, and LLNL LivChat can:
 o Accurately process AQI event data
 o Validate wildfire incidents
Methods
 Consulting the User Community

 • Early interviews/consultations with field professionals to ground methods in
 real practices
 • Consultations intended for insights and method development only, not
 qualitative research
 Participants

 • Participants were contacted at the recommendation of the LLNL Emergency
 Operations Center ESH Deputy Lead
 • Six professionals consulted including department managers, emergency
 operations personnel, environmental professionals
 • Helped identify suitable and accessible resources for methods and data
 collection
 • For consultation purposes only, no qualitative data, no IRB requirement
 Consulting process

 • Email request for consultation permission, project proposal attached

 5

 • In-person or Cisco Webex; ~30 to 60 minutes each
 • No formal protocol for interview structure, discussion prompts used
 • Sample topics for discussion prompts included:
 o Suitable wildfire incident resources for training an AI chatbot
 o Air monitoring databases for AI chatbot to learn
 o AQI resources used in daily work
 o Examples of relevant work tasks
 • Consultation notes recorded in electronic word processing document
 • Follow-up emails contributed additional resources
 • Consultant informed resource summary presented in table
 o Show participants’ roles, suggested resources, access level
 (public/private), and intended integration (yes/no)
 • Resources highlighted as candidates for project integration
 o AirNow (EPA), Purple Air, Cal Fire InciWeb
 • Resources discussed but not integrated
 o NARAC, DataMiner, NOAA Amazon Web Services NBM, ArcGIS
 (NNSA/DOE), LandFire, LLNL AQI, NIFC AGOL tool
Materials

 • AI models utilized and evaluated:
 o Open AI ChatGPT-4
 o Anthropic Claude
 o LLNL LivChat (Version 2026.92.2), LivChat can toggle between
 internal and external sources, this project used external only

AI Prompt Design

 • Five-part prompt including ten steps, built to train and test AQI and wildfire
 data gathering
 • Resources used in prompt/training (based on consultations):
 o AirNow (AQI data and Smoke/Fire map)
 o PurpleAir
 o Cal Fire incident database
 o InciWeb
 • Prompt parts:
 o Part I: learn AQI scale and categories from AirNow
 o Part II: locate/access/summarize real-time AirNow Smoke and Fire
 Map data (includes Purple Air integrated sensor data)
 o Part III: use CAL FIRE incidents data (GIS, name, county, start date,
 acreage, containment)
 o Part IV: use InciWeb incident data (name, type, state, size, status)

 6

 o Part V: combine/interpret AQI + wildfire data for California (analysis
 step, described later as not completed due to limitations)
Data Collection

 • Tested each chatbot’s ability to access, interpret, and analyze AQI and wildfire
 information
 • Initial prompts used to learn behavior and limitations
 o Used incremental probing, refined instructions to guide responses
 • Responses collected, exported and recorded into Microsoft Word table
Data Analysis
 Scoring framework

 • Binary rubric for 10 steps:
 o Yes (1) or No (0) for completeness of required tasks
 Evaluation criteria

 • Completeness included all required tasks in each step
 o Scoring required completion without human intervention and using
 only specified resources
 o No human subjects data collection or analysis, not subject to IRB
 review

Results
 Overall scoring summary, presented as table

 • Reported totals
 o ChatGPT: 3/10
                       o Claude: 1/10
 o LivChat: 2/10
 Results by model

 • ChatGPT
 o Completed Part I (steps 1 and 2): accessed AQI scale and summarized
 PM2.5 categories with examples
 o Part III partially successful: located Cal Fire incidents page and data
 o Limitations encountered:
  Could not view EPA AirNow Smoke/Fire Map real-time data
 (JavaScript-based)
  Could not summarize Cal Fire incidents table due to JavaScript
 viewing limitations

 7

  Could not access InciWeb incident database, attributed to
 “security/antiscraping protections”
 o Workarounds provided (but not scored as success):
  Provided user instructions to manually access maps/data, then
 summarize
 • Claude
 o Part I, step 2 successfully summarized and provided examples of AQI
 scale
 o Claude was unsuccessful with all remaining parts
 o Limitations encountered:
  Like ChatGPT, JavaScript-sources inaccessible (AirNow map,
 CAL FIRE interface)
 o Key scoring issue:
  Used resources outside the prompt requirements, which caused
 “failed” scoring despite otherwise accurate AQI scale content
 • LivChat
 o Successfully completed Part I, steps 1& 2, provided EPA AQI scale
 and examples
 o Limitations encountered:
  Live web browsing restrictions prevented completion of Parts
 II through V
Discussion, Summary & Conclusions

 • LLNL user community defined and consulted
 o Informed approach helped understand roles at the lab
 o Connections formed to determine next iteration of AI use during
 wildfire events
 • Project focused on “required elements of success” rather than analysis of
 response quality
 o This approach can help inform ESH and EM users of the “do’s and
 don’ts” when utilizing AI models
 • Despite limitations, all models provided detailed instructions about data
 access, collection, analysis, and presentation
 • Future research can focus on:
 o AI/ML model development with LLNL HPC or Data Science Institute
 o Creating user instructions for guided AI model use during wildfire or
 other elevated AQI incidents
