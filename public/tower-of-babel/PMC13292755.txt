Phylogenetic analysis of CIAV and pathogenies in embryo eggs
Authors: Yunshu Xie, Quansheng Chen, Zexin Chen, Huiping Liang, Zheng Chen, Huali Xie, Huansheng Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, clinical samples of positive CIAV were collected for whole genome sequencing, and the key amino acid variation characteristics were analyzed. One of the CIAV was inoculated into 5-day-old embryo eggs to hatch, and the pathogenicity of the isolated virus was systematically evaluated. The results showed that the whole genome of 12 strains of CIAV was sequenced, and there were many amino acid mutation sites in the VP1 gene, mainly concentrated in the hypervariable regions such as 75-97, 139-157, 287-294 and 406-439. The pathogenicity test showed that the weight of chick embryos in the infected group was significantly lower than that in the control group, and the hatching rate decreased. After hatching, chicks showed symptoms such as bursa atrophy, liver bleeding and limb paralysis. HE staining showed pathological changes such as degeneration and necrosis of hepatocytes, lymphocytopenia and renal tubular dilatation. In summary, the molecular characteristics of CIAV epidemic strains in Jiangxi province of China are clarified, which makes up for the shortcomings of the lack of field wild virus background in the existing embryo pathogenicity research.

Introduction

Chicken infectious anemia virus (CIAV) is an immunosuppressive pathogen that mainly infects chicks aged 1-3 weeks. After infection, it can induce aplastic anemia and systemic lymphatic exhaustion, which will seriously reduce the host's immune function and hinder its growth, and significantly increase the risk of secondary infection such as virus, bacteria and fung . It was first discovered and reported by Japanese scholar Yuasa in 1979 (Zhou and Shen et al., 1997). At present, CIAV is widely popular all over the world. CIAV was first reported in China in 1992, and subsequent serological and etiological investigations showed that the infection of CIAV was widespread in chickens in Shandong, Anhui, Henan, Hebei, Jiangxi, Fujian and other places, and the epidemic range became more and more extensive.

CIAV, belonging to the genus Gyrovirus of Anelloviridae, is an icosahedral virus with an average diameter of 25 nm. The genome of CIAV is a single circular DNA with a length of about 2.3 kb, which contains three overlapping open reading frames (ORFs), encoding three viral proteins: one structural protein (VP1) and two unstructured proteins (VP2 and VP3). Among these protein, VP1 is a major capsid protein and a highly mutated protein, which plays a key role in virus replication, transmission and virulence (Renshaw and Soine et al., 1996). However, in recent years, the genetic evolution analysis of CIAV epidemic strains in Jiangxi province is lacking. To fill these research gaps, 12 CIAV field isolates were recovered from clinically affected poultry farms in Jiangxi Province. All isolates were subjected to whole-genome sequencing, with particular focus on characterizing VP1 amino acid mutation patterns of prevailing field strains.

Vertical transmission via fertilized eggs represents a important route for CIAV to infect developing embryos and newly hatched chicks, resulting in congenital growth retardation and post-hatch immunosuppression. Most CIAV embryonic pathogenicity investigations adopt laboratory-adapted reference strains, with pathological assessments restricted exclusively to embryonic development and lacking sequential monitoring of progressive post-hatch lesions following natural vertical transmission. In view of the above research blank, this study selected 5-day-old SPF chicken embryos to challenge virus, and continuously monitored the pathological changes of organs from embryo to 9 days after hatching. The amino acid mutation characteristics of VP1 epidemic strain of CIAV in Jiangxi province in recent years were improved, and the new pathogenic characteristics of chicken embryos infected vertically by wild virus were supplemented.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals and reagents

SPF chicken embryos were purchased from Nanjing Tegeli Planting Cooperative, DNA/RNA co-extraction kit was purchased from TOROIVD TECHNOLOGY COMPANY (Shanghai, China), DH5α competent cells (CC96102) were purchased from TOLOBIO (Shanghai, China), and DNA marker (SM221) was processed from Seven Biotechnology (Bei Jing, China).

Primer design and synthesis

Referring to the published nucleotide sequence of CIAV, three pairs of primers were designed for CIAV detection (CIAV-F:TAGGTACCCCCACCATGCACGCGAACG;CIAV-R:AGCCTCGAGTCAACACTATACGTACCGGGG) , amplification of VP1 and 3′UTR (VP1-F:ATGGCAAGACGAGCTCGCAGAC;VP1-R:GATTGTGCGATAAAGCCATTTG) , and amplification of VP2 (including VP3) and 5′UTR (VP2-F:GCATTCCGAGTGGTTACTATTCC;VP2-R:TTACACTATACGTACCGG) respectively.

Sample source and collection

In 2025, clinical samples of broilers showing CIAV clinical symptoms were collected from several poultry farms in Jiangxi Province. Take a sample of soybean tissue and put it into a grinding tube, add 1 mL of normal saline and a grinding bead, and put it into a grinder for grinding. Freeze-thaw the ground liquid for 3 times, and centrifuge at 4°C for 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes. Take the supernatant and extract DNA with DNA/RNA co-extraction kit. The reaction conditions were: 95°C, 10 min; 95°C, 30 s, 56°C, 30 s, 72°C, 45 s, 25 cycles; 72°C, 10 min.

Virus genome amplification

The positive DNA was used to amplify the complete sequence of virus genome by PCR with primers (VP1-F, VP1-R, VP2-F, VP2-R). The reaction conditions were: 95°C, 10min;; 95°C, 30 s, 50°C, 30 s, 72°C, 90 s, 35 cycles; 72°C, 10 min. Then, we purified and recovered the PCR products, and connected with the vector pMD18T.

Virus isolation

The supernatant of liver grinding fluid which through a 0.22 μm filter was inoculated into the allantoic cavity of 9-day-old SPF chicken embryo. When the chick embryo grows to 14 days old, the allantoic fluid is collected.

Chicken embryo inoculation experiment

Forty-five SPF embryos were randomly divided into two groups: 30 for infected groups and 15 for control groups. On the fifth day of chicken embryo development, 0.2 mL CIAV diluent was inoculated into the allantoic cavity of infected chicken embryos, and 0.2 mL phosphate buffered saline (PBS) was inoculated into the allantoic cavity of control chicken embryos, and the death of chicken embryos was recorded every day. On the 9th day (ED9), ED12, ED15, ED17 and ED19, the chickens of three infected groups and one control group were autopsied. At the age of 21 days, the chicken embryos hatched one after another. On the 3rd, 6th and 9th day after hatching, the chicken embryos of three infected groups and one control group were autopsied, and the liver, heart, kidney, bursa of fabricius and spleen were stained with HE.

Statistical analysis

In order to process and analyze the sequence, we use Snapgene to splice the sequencing results and upload all virus genome sequences to NCBI.

Results and discussion

Sequence analysis

The results showed that the complete genome of 12 isolates was 2298 bp, in which the VP1 gene was 1350 bp, the VP2 gene was 651 bp and the VP3 gene is 366 bp. The two ends of the genome are 5′ and 3′ non-coding regions, with the length of 358 bp and 117 bp respectively, which is consistent with the known CIAV genome structure, and there is no deletion or insertion. The homology between the isolated strain and the reference strain is 96.48%−99.59%. The reference strain JL14023 from Harbin, China has the highest homology (99.59%) with the isolate H3 JIANGXI, the reference strain G17.33.3 from Viet Nam has the lowest homology (96.48%) with the isolate H4 JIANGXI, and the three isolates have the highest homology (99.56%) with the CQ21313 from Guangdong, China Province, and the five isolates are simultaneously with CK from Hebei, China Province.

VP1 protein of CIAV is not only the key factor of virus assembly and infection, but also the main determinant of genetic evolution and virulence difference (College of Veterinary Medicine and An et al., 2019; Chen and Yuan et al., 2023). Then, the mutation of amino acid sites in the VP1 sequence of the isolated strain was analyzed. These mutation sites are mainly concentrated in 75-97, 139-157, 287-294 and 406-439. The most common mutations are: H22N, V75I, D95G, L97M, K129Q, E144Q, M157V, T287S, T287A, P290A, H294Q, A370G, A370T, A370S, L376I, E406K, A413A and I426V. The mutations of individual strains are: R36G, R41H, F45L, F49S, H50L, R53Q, G92S, K110R, F12S, L125I, FI81S, N248T, M268R, R352G, N379H, A415P, Q429H, N437K and A439D. The effect of the above mutation on the strain needs further study.

Characterization of CIAV-infected chicken embryos

After the embryos were inoculated with CIAV diluent, the chicken embryos that died within 24 h were discarded. Fig. 1B shows that the weight of chicken embryos in the control group is significantly higher than that in the infected group, which indicates that CIAV infection will lead to obvious retardation of chicken embryo development and weight loss. The effect of virus infection on chicken embryos began to appear from ED9, and the survival rate decreased most obviously in the incubation stage, and then stabilized (Fig. 1C). In ED9-D1, the chicken embryos in the control group developed normally, and the shape of the embryos was complete, the size increased synchronously with the embryo age, and the skin was ruddy, without obvious bleeding, edema or deformity. After hatching, the chicks are lively, with uniform and shiny feathers and normal limb development. In the infected group, the chicken embryos were easy to die in the early stage (ED9-12), and the dead chicken embryos showed obvious developmental retardation, and the embryos were not formed. In the middle and late stage (ED12-ED19), the embryo size is slightly smaller than that of the control group, with slight congestion on the skin and head and few feathers. When chicken embryos hatch, some chicken embryos are difficult to hatch, and the hatching rate is 9/12(75%). At hatch (Day 1 post-hatching), CIAV-infected chicks exhibited marked lethargy, ruffled and dull feathers, and stunted growth. Several individuals displayed limb paralysis and difficulty standing, progressing to a moribund state, in stark contrast to the healthy, active chicks in the control group. (Fig. 1D). This shows that CIAV has interfered with the normal development in the embryonic stage, showing developmental retardation and abnormal morphology, which is related to the vertical transmission of the virus. Gross pathological examination of chickens at 3, 6, and 9 days revealed distinct organ lesions in CIAV-infected birds compared with controls group. In the liver, control chicks exhibited a uniform orange color, soft and elastic texture, and a smooth capsule, with no evidence of hemorrhage or necrosis. In contrast, infected chicks developed progressive hepatic lesions: at Day 3, only a few pinpoint hemorrhages were observed; by Day 6, livers were enlarged, pale yellow, and displayed increased subcapsular hemorrhages; at Day 9, livers were yellow-brown, friable, and showed surface hemorrhage. For the Bursa of Fabricius (BF), control chicks had well-developed, plump, pale milky-white bursae with uniform texture that grew normally with age. In infected chicks, BF atrophy was evident as early as Day 3, with slight shrinkage and reddened discoloration. Lesions progressed to severe atrophy, softening, and surface hemorrhages by Day 6, culminating in extreme shrinkage, shriveling, and increased hemorrhages at Day 9, indicating severe damage to this primary immune organ. The spleen of control chicks was uniformly dark red and plump. In infected chicks, the spleen was enlarged at Day 3, became discolored and shrunken by Day 6, and developed a soft, flaccid texture at Day 9 (Fig. 1E). At the age of 9 days, the intestine swelled, and the yolk in the abdominal cavity was not absorbed, leaving a large number of yellow yolk sacs, while the yolk sacs of normal chicks at the age of 6-7 days after hatching could be absorbed by more than 95%, suggesting that virus infection seriously interfered with the normal development and nutritional metabolism of chickens (Fig. 1F). Hematoxylin-Eosin staining

HE staining results revealed that obvious lymphocyte depletion, loss of mucosal epithelial villous structure, as well as atrophy and thinning of muscular layer were observed in the bursa of Fabricius of CIAV-infected group from embryonic day 19 unhatched embryos to 3-9-day-old hatched chicks. In control group, the follicular structure of the bursa of Fabricius was intact with densely distributed lymphocytes, and the epithelial and muscular layers presented clear morphology without any pathological changes (Fig. 2A). For the liver tissues of infected chicks, mild hydropic degeneration of hepatocytes and focal inflammatory lesions were observed at Day 3. At Day 6, hepatocellular hydropic degeneration, necrosis and serous exudation occurred, accompanied by inflammatory cell infiltration within veins. At Day 9, extensive fatty degeneration was found in hepatic lobules, together with dilation and congestion of hepatic sinusoids. By contrast, hepatocytes in the control group maintained normal morphology with regularly arranged hepatic cords, and no degeneration, necrosis or inflammatory infiltration was detected at all time points (Fig. 2B). The liver is a core organ responsible for substance metabolism and detoxification in chicks (Narayani and C., 2018). The pathological changes of liver tissues developing from mild hydropic degeneration at Day 3 to fatty degeneration, congestion and necrosis at Day 9 suggested that these lesions were not simply caused by direct viral injury, but resulted from the combined effects of viral replication-mediated cytotoxicity and host immune pathological injury.

In the infected group, splenic lymphocytes presented fusiform morphology at Day 3. At Day 6, obvious proliferation of fibroblasts and connective tissue as well as capsular hyperplasia were observed in the spleen, along with extravascular congestion, lymphocyte reduction and inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 2C). At Day 9, the normal histological structure of the spleen was completely destroyed, and connective tissue hyperplasia was found in the splenic capsule and arterial walls. In the control group, the white pulp and red pulp of the spleen were well-organized with evenly distributed lymphocytes, and no hyperplasia, atrophy or inflammatory lesions were observed. As a major peripheral immune organ, the dynamic pathological progression of the spleen reflected the transition from effective immune response to immune exhaustion in host body. The morphological alteration of splenic lymphocytes at Day 3 indicated that host immune function was already inhibited at this stage. Capsular connective tissue hyperplasia and lymphocyte loss at Day 6 implied that the spleen attempted to maintain structural integrity through interstitial proliferation, while persistent lymphocyte depletion reflected continuous destruction of immune cells by CIAV. The complete structural collapse and extensive fibrosis of the spleen at Day 9 manifested severe functional failure of peripheral immune organs.

In 9-day-old infected chicks, renal lesions were characterized by dilation, deformation, necrosis, mineralization and basophilic degeneration of renal tubules and collecting ducts. Abundant exfoliated cell debris and proteinaceous substances accumulated in the tubular lumen. In addition, glomerular atrophy, mesangial cell proliferation, inflammatory cell infiltration as well as capsular hyperemia and hemorrhage were also observed. The kidneys of control chicks possessed complete tissue structure with normal morphology of renal tubules and glomeruli, without degeneration, necrosis or cast formation (Fig. 2D). In this study, the systematic multi-organ pathological changes fully elucidate the pathological mechanism underlying clinical symptoms including growth retardation, messy plumage, limb paralysis and high mortality in infected chicks.

Conclusion

In this study, the whole genomes of 12 strains of CIAV were successfully amplified and sequenced in clinical samples collected in Jiangxi Province in 2025, and the amino acid mutation sites in VP1 gene were analyzed, and the distribution characteristics of these mutations in different isolates were preliminarily clarified. The results of pathogenicity showed that after inoculation with CIAV, the chick embryos showed the phenomena of delayed development, weight loss and decreased hatching rate. After hatching, the chicks showed symptoms such as bursa atrophy, liver bleeding and limb paralysis. HE staining further observed pathological changes such as degeneration and necrosis of liver cells, lymphopenia and renal tubular dilatation.
