Generalized Entropies and Black Hole Area Quantization from Landauer’s Principle
Jorge Ananias Neto, Ronaldo Thibes

Abstract
We investigate black hole area quantization by imposing Landauer’s principle on the discrete entropy change between consecutive area levels. The elementary transition is identified with the entropy cost of erasing one bit of information, Δ ​ S = k B ​ ln ⁡ 2 \Delta S=k_{B}\ln 2 . For the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, this gives the standard Bekenstein–Mukhanov value of the area spectrum parameter, which is used as the reference limit. The same discrete construction is then applied to generalized entropy functionals. For Barrow entropy, the parameter γ \gamma becomes level dependent, while the relative separation between adjacent area levels still vanishes for large n n . For the modified Rényi entropy, the nonsingular branch has vanishing relative spacing at large n n , whereas the singular branch develops a finite-level pole. For the modified Kaniadakis entropy, the small κ \kappa expansion shows that a fixed deformation parameter prevents the relative area spacing from vanishing in the large n n limit. Overall, the results suggest that Landauer’s principle provides a useful framework for analyzing generalized entropic extensions of the Bekenstein–Mukhanov area spectrum.

II
Landauer principle

Landauer’s principle provides a direct link between information theory and thermodynamics
landauer
;
bri
;
land2
. It states that erasure of one bit of
information requires a minimum energy cost, according to the inequality

\displaystyle\Delta E\geq k_{B}T\ln 2\,,

(6)

where
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant and
T
is the temperature. The factor
\ln 2
reflects the entropy change associated with the loss of a single bit,
while the corresponding energy is dissipated as heat. This principle has been supported by both experimental and theoretical results

;
jgb
;
yxr
;
rw
;
ebor
;
ebor2
,
and is now regarded as a basic result in the thermodynamics of information.

Landauer’s principle has also been applied in several other important contexts in gravitational physics. In particular, it has been argued that Hawking evaporation
satisfies the Landauer bound in its saturated form
cl
. Related discussions have connected this principle to Hawking temperature
abreu
,
cosmological horizon

, and Immirzi parameter in Loop Quantum Gravity

In the black hole case, the same relation follows directly from the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. Adopting a system of units in which
\hbar=c=1
, one has

\displaystyle S_{BH}=4\pi k_{B}GM^{2}\,,

(7)

where
G
is Newton’s constant and
M
is the black hole mass. Differentiating with respect to
M
, one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{BH}}{dM}=\frac{1}{T}=8\pi k_{B}GM\,.

(8)

For a small mass variation
\Delta M
, this gives

\displaystyle\Delta S_{BH}=8\pi k_{B}GM\Delta M\,.

(9)

If this entropy change is associated with the erasure of one bit,
Eq. (
5
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta M=\frac{\ln 2}{8\pi G}\frac{1}{M}\,,

(10)

which, using Eq. (
8
), can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta M=k_{B}T\ln 2\,.

(11)

Thus, an elementary entropy variation associated with Hawking evaporation is consistent with the saturation of the Landauer bound.

To apply Landauer’s principle to the Bekenstein–Hawking area law,
Eq. (
3
), we first rewrite it in terms of the discrete area
spectrum, Eq. (
1
), which gives

S_{BH}=\frac{k_{B}\,\gamma\,n}{4}\,.

(12)

The exact entropy variation between two consecutive levels is then

\Delta S_{BH}=S_{BH}(n+1)-S_{BH}(n)=\frac{k_{B}\,\gamma}{4}\,.

(13)

Combining Eq. (
13
) with the Landauer prescription,
Eq. (
5
), we obtain the expression for the parameter

\gamma
appearing in Eq. (
1
),

\displaystyle\gamma=4\ln 2\,.

(14)

Thus, the Landauer prescription reproduces the Bekenstein–Mukhanov area spacing.

Substituting Eq. (
14
) into Eq. (
1
), the spectrum becomes

\displaystyle A_{n}=\left(4\,\ell_{P}^{2}\,\ln 2\right)n\,.

(15)

The corresponding area spacing

\Delta A_{n}=4\,\ell_{P}^{2}\,\ln 2

(16)

leads to a
1/n
relative separation between adjacent levels, i.e.,

\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}=\frac{4\,\ell_{P}^{2}\,\ln 2}{\left(4\,\ell_{P}^{2}\,\ln 2\right)n}=\frac{1}{n}\,.

(17)

Consequently, for large

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}=0\,.

(18)

Therefore, although the area spectrum remains discrete, the relative spacing between adjacent area levels becomes negligible for
large
n
. At macroscopic scales, the relevant behavior is controlled by the vanishing of the relative spacing rather than by the individual
level structure.

Barrow entropy

Modified Rényi entropy

To introduce the modified Rényi entropy, we first briefly review Tsallis statistics.
Tsallis statistics
tsallis
;
t2
;
t3
provides a nonextensive extension of the usual
Boltzmann–Gibbs (BG) framework, in which the standard definition of entropy is generalized

q
. The corresponding entropy is given by

\displaystyle S_{q}=k_{B}\,\frac{1-\sum_{i=1}^{W}p_{i}^{q}}{q-1}\,,

where
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant,
p_{i}
denotes the probability of the
i
th microstate,

q
is the nonextensivity entropic parameter, and
W
represents the total number of accessible
microstates. In the limit
q\to 1
, this expression reduces to the standard BG entropy.
Although Tsallis entropy preserves the main properties expected of an entropy measure, it is
not additive in the usual sense. Instead, it satisfies a pseudo additivity relation.
This formalism has found applications in a wide variety of physical contexts, including Lévy-type anomalous diffusion
t4

turbulence in pure electron plasmas

, position-dependent mass formalisms
daCosta:2018qsp
;
Borges
;
Thibes:2025
;
Thibes:2025b

systems
t6A
;
t6B

Abrahao:2016hnh
;
piz

In the microcanonical ensemble, for which all microstates are equally probable, the Tsallis entropy takes the simpler form

\displaystyle S_{q}=k_{B}\,\frac{W^{1-q}-1}{1-q}\,.

(32)

As expected, Eq. (
32

when

We now assume that the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy

k_{B}\,\frac{W^{1-q}-1}{1-q}=S_{BH}.

Solving Eq. (

\displaystyle W=\left(1+\lambda\frac{S_{BH}}{k_{B}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\,,

where we have defined

Using this expression in the standard BG relation

\displaystyle S_{R}=\frac{k_{B}}{\lambda}\ln\left(1+\lambda\frac{S_{BH}}{k_{B}}\right)\,.

At this point, it is useful to recall that Rényi entropy and related deformations are widely used in quantum information theory as generalized
measures of uncertainty and entanglement. They also arise naturally in the replica construction of the von Neumann entropy in conformal field
theory and have been applied to entanglement spectra in systems ranging from black hole horizons to quantum many body systems. See, for instance,

\gamma

S_{R}(n)=\frac{k_{B}}{\lambda}\ln\left(1+\lambda\frac{\gamma\,n}{4}\right)\,,

\Delta S_{R}=\frac{k_{B}}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{1+\lambda\frac{\gamma}{4}(n+1)}{1+\lambda\frac{\gamma}{4}n}\right)\,.

By imposing Landauer’s principle,

\gamma_{R}=\frac{4\left(2^{\lambda}-1\right)}{\lambda\left[1-n\left(2^{\lambda}-1\right)\right]}\,.

In the limit

From Eq. (

Thus, for

Dividing Eq. (

Modified Kaniadakis entropy

Kaniadakis statistics defines a nonextensive extension of the standard
Boltzmann–Gibbs (BG) framework through a real deformation parameter

\displaystyle S_{\kappa}=-k_{B}\sum_{i=1}^{W}\frac{p_{i}^{1+\kappa}-p_{i}^{1-\kappa}}{2\kappa}\,,

(46)

where
p_{i}
denotes the probability of the
i
th microstate and
W
is
the total number of accessible states. The standard BG entropy is recovered
in the limit
\kappa\to 0
. Owing to its deformed structure, this entropy has
found applications in several areas, including relativistic systems,
gravitation, and cosmology

;

;
aabn

In the microcanonical case, where all microstates are equally probable, the
above expression reduces to

\displaystyle S_{\kappa}=k_{B}\frac{W^{\kappa}-W^{-\kappa}}{2\kappa}\,.

(47)

This expression reduces to
S=k_{B}\ln W
for
\kappa\to 0
, and the
deformation parameter is usually restricted to
0\leq\kappa<1
.

In order to construct an entropy analogous to the MRE within the Kaniadakis framework,
the authors of Ref.
aa
proposed a modified Kaniadakis entropy as follows.
They identified the microcanonical Kaniadakis entropy with the Bekenstein–Hawking
entropy, Eq. (
3
), namely

k_{B}\frac{W^{\kappa}-W^{-\kappa}}{2\kappa}=S_{BH}\,.

(48)

Solving for
W
, one obtains

W=\left(\kappa\frac{S_{BH}}{k_{B}}+\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\frac{S_{BH}^{2}}{k_{B}^{2}}}\right)^{1/\kappa}\,.

(49)

Substituting this result into the relation
S=k_{B}\ln W
yields the modified
Kaniadakis entropy (MKE)

S_{\kappa}^{*}=\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa}\ln\left(\kappa\frac{S_{BH}}{k_{B}}+\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\frac{S_{BH}^{2}}{k_{B}^{2}}}\right)\,.

(50)

In the limit
\kappa\to 0
, this expression reduces to the standard
Bekenstein–Hawking entropy,
S_{BH}
. The MKE may
therefore be regarded as a relativistic analogue of Rényi-type constructions.
The thermodynamics induced by this entropy differs from that of the Bekenstein–Hawking case.
In particular, the associated Hawking temperature may lead to a positive heat capacity for Schwarzschild black holes.
As a result, above a critical mass, the temperature increases with the black hole mass.
The corresponding Hawking mass loss rate is also found to be equal to or larger than the standard one, leading to a shorter evaporation time.
This feature may be relevant for discussions of the non-observation of primordial black holes.
Since black hole evaporation is a quantum effect, these results suggest that the MKE provides an effective parametrization of quantum corrections to black hole
thermodynamics. Further details can be found in Refs.

aaacnt

In order to determine the parameter
\gamma

S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)=\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa}\ln\left[\kappa\frac{\gamma n}{4}+\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\frac{\gamma^{2}n^{2}}{16}}\right].

At this point, the exact discrete implementation of Landauer’s prescription leads to algebraically involved expressions. Since the physically
relevant regime must recover the Bekenstein–Hawking result as

Expanding Eq. (

S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)\simeq k_{B}\left[\frac{\gamma n}{4}-\frac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{3}n^{3}}{384}\right]\,.

The corresponding discrete entropy variation is

\Delta S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)=S_{\kappa}^{*}(n+1)-S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)\,,

(53)

which gives

\Delta S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)\simeq k_{B}\left[\frac{\gamma}{4}-\frac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{3}}{384}\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)\right]\,.

(54)

By imposing Landauer’s principle,
\Delta S_{\kappa}^{*}(n)=k_{B}\ln 2
, we obtain

\frac{\gamma}{4}-\frac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{3}}{384}\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)=\ln 2\,.

(55)

Solving this equation perturbatively up to order

\gamma_{\kappa}(n)=\gamma_{0}+\kappa^{2}\gamma_{2}+O(\kappa^{4})\,,

\gamma_{0}=4\ln 2

\gamma_{2}=\frac{2}{3}\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)(\ln 2)^{3}\,.

Thus, the Landauer selected parameter in the MKE framework is

Substituting Eq. (

A_{n}\simeq 4\ell_{P}^{2}n\ln 2\left[1+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}}{6}\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)\right].

\Delta A_{n}=A_{n+1}-A_{n}\,.

Using Eq. (

\Delta A_{n}\simeq 4\ell_{P}^{2}\ln 2\left[1+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}}{6}\left(9n^{2}+15n+7\right)\right].

(62)

Therefore, the relative area spacing is

\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}\simeq\frac{1+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}}{6}\left(9n^{2}+15n+7\right)}{n\left[1+\frac{\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}}{6}\left(3n^{2}+3n+1\right)\right]}.

(63)

Expanding this expression consistently up to order
\kappa^{2}
, one obtains

\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}\simeq\frac{1}{n}\left[1+\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}\left(n^{2}+2n+1\right)\right].

(64)

Equivalently,

\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}\simeq\frac{1}{n}+\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}\left(n+2+\frac{1}{n}\right).

(65)

For large
n
, the dominant behavior is

\frac{\Delta A_{n}}{A_{n}}\simeq\frac{1}{n}+\kappa^{2}(\ln 2)^{2}n\,.

(66)

Therefore, for fixed
\kappa
, the second term prevents the relative spacing from vanishing in the large
n
limit.
One possible way to make the relative spacing vanish at large
n
is to allow
the deformation parameter to be interpreted as an effective scale-dependent quantity,

\kappa=\kappa(n)
, decreasing with
n
in such a way that

\kappa(n)\sqrt{n}\ll 1\qquad(n\gg 1)\,.

(67)

This condition is equivalent to
\kappa^{2}(n)n\ll 1
and ensures that
\Delta A_{n}/A_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
This asymptotic interpretation is restricted to the regime in which the small-
\kappa
expansion remains controlled.
Thus, in the MKE framework, a fixed deformation parameter does not make the relative spacing vanish in the large
n
regime.
This difficulty can be avoided, for example, by treating
\kappa
as an effective scale-dependent parameter rather than as a fixed
fundamental constant.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we examined the implications of Landauer’s principle for the quantization
of black hole horizon area in different entropic frameworks. The analysis was performed at
the exact discrete level by identifying the entropy variation between two neighboring area
levels with the elementary one-bit entropy increment. This procedure provides a direct
way to determine the parameter
\gamma
appearing in the area spectrum without assuming,
from the beginning, a specific microscopic degeneracy for the horizon states.

For Barrow entropy, the coefficient
\gamma_{B}
becomes dependent on the area level

n
and on the deformation parameter
\Delta
. The resulting spectrum is no longer
uniformly spaced. Nevertheless, the relative separation between neighboring levels
vanishes in the large
n
regime. Therefore, the fundamental discreteness of the spectrum
is preserved, although the separation between consecutive levels becomes negligible at
macroscopic scales.

For the modified Rényi entropy, the Landauer prescription separates two branches.
For
\lambda>0
, the coefficient
\gamma_{R}
itself develops a finite-level pole and
becomes negative beyond it, so that this branch does not define a positive area spectrum
past the pole. By contrast, for
\lambda<0
, no divergence occurs,
\gamma_{R}
remains
positive for all
n
, and the area spectrum approaches a finite asymptotic value.
In this branch, the relative area spacing also vanishes as
n\to\infty
, so that the
macroscopic regime becomes insensitive to the underlying level structure.

For the modified Kaniadakis entropy, the small
\kappa
analysis shows that the deformation
produces a correction to the relative area spacing that grows with the area level. As a result,
a fixed deformation parameter does not make the relative spacing vanish in the macroscopic
regime. This behavior can nevertheless be avoided if
\kappa
is regarded as an effective
scale-dependent parameter whose contribution is sufficiently suppressed at large
n
.

Taken together, our results show that Landauer’s principle can be used beyond the
recovery of the Bekenstein–Mukhanov result. It also provides a useful framework for
analyzing generalized entropic extensions, distinguishing regular branches from singular
ones and clarifying the behavior of the level spacing in the large
n
regime.
