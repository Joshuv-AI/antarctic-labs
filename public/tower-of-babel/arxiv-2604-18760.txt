A universal complementarity identity for polarized double-slit interferometry
José J. Gil

Abstract
We establish an exact identity among four dimensionless invariants accessible by standard polarimetric and interferometric measurements in a polarized double-slit experiment: the in-phase and quadrature components V A V_{A} and V N V_{N} of fringe visibility, the path predictability 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , and the mixedness ℐ \mathcal{I} of the path-reduced state satisfy V A 2 + V N 2 + 𝒫 2 + ℐ 2 = 1 V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{I}^{2}=1 . The identity is a universal algebraic consequence of the positivity of the reduced state and holds for every normalized path–polarization density matrix. It unifies the Englert–Greenberger–Yasin and Jakob–Bergou relations, separates the two operationally distinct components of visibility measurable by phase-shifted interferometry, and admits a natural interpretation within the Jaynes maximum-entropy framework: the three path invariants parametrize the minimal exponential family on the accessible algebra, while ℐ 2 \mathcal{I}^{2} emerges as the residual mixedness that saturates the positivity bound. The separation V 2 = V A 2 + V N 2 V^{2}=V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2} identifies the antisymmetric sector of the coherence matrix ρ = A + i ​ N \rho=A+iN as the specific substrate of phase-sensitive information and permits a sector-resolved diagnosis of environmental coupling.



Wave–particle complementarity in double-slit interferometry has been given quantitative content by a sequence of increasingly refined inequalities, from the original Englert–Greenberger–Yasin relation
V^{2}+\mathcal{D}^{2}\leq 1
between fringe visibility and which-path distinguishability
[
1
,
2
]
, to its completion into the tripartite equality
V^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{C}^{2}=1
by Jakob and Bergou for pure states of a path–marker system
[
3
]
, and to its reformulation as an entropic uncertainty relation
[
4
]
. In parallel, the quantification of coherence as the
\ell_{1}
norm of the off-diagonal density-matrix elements
[
5
]
has given the wave attribute an intrinsic resource-theoretic basis independent of interferometric visibility. These developments have extended naturally to the polarized case, in which the state of polarization of light passing through the slits serves as a marker of the path
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

The present Letter derives a compact identity that unifies these strands and refines them in one specific direction. We consider the joint path–polarization state of a beam incident on a two-slit mask, described by a
4\times 4
coherence matrix
\rho
in the basis
\{|1,H\rangle,|1,V\rangle,|2,H\rangle,|2,V\rangle\}
, where
\{|1\rangle,|2\rangle\}
label the two slits and
\{|H\rangle,|V\rangle\}
label two orthogonal linear polarizations defined by the analyzer. The object
\rho
generalizes the two-point coherence-polarization matrix of Wolf
[
7
]
to a quantum-mechanical joint description and reduces to it in the semiclassical limit. The only structural requirements on
\rho
are Hermiticity, positivity and unit trace.

Any Hermitian matrix admits the unique decomposition
\rho=A+iN
with
A=A^{\top}
real symmetric and
N=-N^{\top}
real antisymmetric. For an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix this partitions the
n^{2}
real parameters into
n(n+1)/2
in
A
and
n(n-1)/2
in
N
; for the present case, ten parameters in
A
and six in
N
. The decomposition, standard in the algebra of coherence matrices, acquires operational content once the algebra of accessible observables is itself partitioned along the same lines. Concretely, the Pauli tensors
\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes\sigma_{\nu}^{\mathrm{pol}}
with
\mu,\nu\in\{0,1,3\}
, together with
\sigma_{2}^{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes\sigma_{2}^{\mathrm{pol}}
, have real symmetric matrices in the ordered product basis and thus couple to
A
; they correspond to intensity measurements with fixed phase (populations, in-phase interferometric correlations, and linear polarimetric analyses
I
,
Q
,
U
). The remaining six tensors, containing a single factor of
\sigma_{2}
, are purely imaginary antisymmetric and couple to
N
; they require either quadrature phase control or circular polarimetric analysis (
V
component) for their detection. Full enumeration and classification of the 16 tensors are given in the Supplement.

Using the identity
\operatorname{Tr}(M_{S}M_{A})=0
for any real symmetric
M_{S}
and real antisymmetric
M_{A}
of compatible size, the expectation value of any Hermitian observable
\hat{O}
with matrix
O=O_{S}+iO_{A}
factors cleanly as

\langle\hat{O}\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(AO_{S})-\operatorname{Tr}(NO_{A}).

(1)

Symmetric and antisymmetric observables couple exclusively to the
A
and
N
sectors of the state. This decoupling is the structural reason why the two sectors carry physically distinct information.

The reduced state on the path degree of freedom,
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{pol}}\rho
, is a
2\times 2
density matrix whose Hermitian decomposition inherits the partition
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=A_{\mathrm{cam}}+iN_{\mathrm{cam}}
. Parametrizing
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+\vec{r}\cdot\vec{\sigma})
with
\vec{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define

V_{A}\equiv r_{x},\qquad V_{N}\equiv r_{y},\qquad\mathcal{P}\equiv r_{z},

(2)

i.e. the three invariants are the Cartesian components of the Bloch vector of the reduced path state. Equivalently,
V_{A}=2\operatorname{Re}\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}=2A_{\mathrm{cam},12}
and
V_{N}=-2\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}
, which identifies
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
with the real and imaginary parts of the interpath coherence and hence with the symmetric and antisymmetric sectors of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
;
\mathcal{P}=\rho_{\mathrm{cam},11}-\rho_{\mathrm{cam},22}
is the difference of integrated intensities emerging from the two slits. All three are standard observables requiring no polarimetric resolution at detection. The total fringe visibility integrated over polarization is
V=|2\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}|
and obeys
V^{2}=V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}
, the statement that
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
are the two Cartesian components of a single complex coherence amplitude.

The positivity of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
, which follows automatically from the positivity of
\rho
, is equivalent to
|\vec{r}|\leq 1
. Defining
\mathcal{I}^{2}=1-|\vec{r}|^{2}=4\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=2(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}^{2})
, we obtain the central identity of this Letter,

\\
;V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{I}^{2}=1.\;

(3)

The identity is exact for every normalized state, pure or mixed, and saturates the positivity bound by construction. Equation (
3
) recovers the Englert–Greenberger–Yasin relation
V^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}\leq 1
when the polarization sector is inactive, and the Jakob–Bergou complete-complementarity equality
V^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{C}^{2}=1
for globally pure states, where
\mathcal{C}
is the path–polarization concurrence of Wootters
[
9
]
, and in that case
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{C}
. For mixed global states,
\mathcal{I}\geq\mathcal{C}
, the additional slack reflecting classical residual mixedness beyond genuine path–polarization entanglement.

The decomposition of visibility into its in-phase and quadrature components admits a direct experimental reading. If
I(\phi)
is the intensity recorded at a fixed screen point when a preparatory phase shift
|1\rangle\to e^{i\phi}|1\rangle
is imposed on one of the slits, standard interferometry gives
I(\phi)=I_{0}[1+V_{A}\cos\phi+V_{N}\sin\phi]
, with
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
separately extractable by four-point phase-shifting
[
10
]
. The sum
V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}
is invariant under rotations of the preparatory phase
\phi\mapsto\phi+\phi_{0}
, which rotate
(V_{A},V_{N})
on the equatorial plane of the Bloch sphere while leaving
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{I}
untouched. The phase choice therefore redistributes the informational content between the
A
and
N
sectors without altering the total, a fact consistent with (
3
).

The identity (
3
) admits a natural interpretation within the Jaynes maximum-entropy framework. Given measured expectation values
\{o_{k}\}
for a finite set of observables
\{\hat{O}_{k}\}
, the Jaynes principle selects the state of maximum von Neumann entropy compatible with the data
[
11
]
; grouping the observables according to their membership in the symmetric or antisymmetric sector, the resulting Gibbsian state
\rho^{*}\propto\exp(-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\hat{O}_{k})
inherits the partition. If no observables in the antisymmetric sector are constrained,
\rho^{*}
is real symmetric and the
N
sector vanishes identically, so that
V_{N}=0
regardless of the remaining data:
N
is, in this precise sense, the footprint of phase-sensitive observables on the state. Within this framework, the three path invariants
(V_{A},V_{N},\mathcal{P})
parametrize the minimal exponential family on the accessible algebra, and
\mathcal{I}^{2}
emerges as the residual mixedness that saturates the positivity bound. An operational corollary is that
\mathcal{I}
requires no independent tomographic reconstruction: it can be obtained from the same phase-shifted and population measurements that determine
V_{A}
,
V_{N}
and
\mathcal{P}
via
\mathcal{I}^{2}=1-(V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2})
, or equivalently as
\mathcal{I}^{2}=4\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
. Equation (
3
) is itself universal: as an algebraic consequence of the positivity of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
, it holds on every normalized state regardless of whether it is of maximum-entropy form.

As a concrete illustration, consider the separable pure state
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle+|2\rangle)\otimes(\cos\theta|H\rangle+\sin\theta|V\rangle)
subjected to a preparatory phase
\phi
between slits and to the path-dephasing channel
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(\rho)=(1-\gamma)\rho+\gamma(\sigma_{z}^{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes I)\rho(\sigma_{z}^{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes I)
with
\gamma\in[0,1/2]
. The four invariants become

\displaystyle V_{A}

\displaystyle=(1-2\gamma)\cos\phi,\quad V_{N}=(1-2\gamma)\sin\phi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mathcal{I}^{2}=4\gamma(1-\gamma),

(4)

and their squared sum equals unity for all
\gamma
and
\phi
. The polarization angle
\theta
does not appear: it affects only the components of
\rho
that are traced out when forming
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
. The geometrical content of (
4
) is that preparatory phase
\phi
moves the state on the equator of the Bloch sphere without changing any invariant other than the partition between
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
, while phase noise
\gamma
draws the state radially inward along a chord whose endpoint at
\gamma=1/2
is the center of the sphere, where
\mathcal{I}=1
and all three coherence and predictability invariants vanish.

.

Two observations consolidate the interpretation. First, the identity is universal in the sense that it holds on any normalized state of the
2\otimes 2
system regardless of preparation, measurement protocol, or the specific form of environmental coupling. Second, and as a consequence, the loss of interferometric visibility under any noise process admits an exact accounting: the four invariants redistribute among themselves while their squared sum remains fixed, so any decrease of
V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}
is exactly compensated by a combined change of
\mathcal{P}^{2}
and
\mathcal{I}^{2}
. In particular, for the path-dephasing channel of (
4
) the redistribution is entirely from coherence to mixedness with
\mathcal{P}
unchanged, so the visibility lost is fully absorbed by
\mathcal{I}^{2}
and remains in principle recoverable from
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
. More generally,
\mathcal{I}
represents information that has migrated between the sectors of identity (
3
) rather than information that has been irretrievably lost to the environment.

The two components of visibility
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
behave differently under environmental coupling. Phase noise modeled as random dephasing of the path sector acts multiplicatively on both with the same factor
1-2\gamma
, so that the ratio
V_{N}/V_{A}
is preserved and the rotation symmetry of the equatorial plane is respected. Processes that break this symmetry—correlated polarization–path noise, birefringent path-dependent delays, frequency-dependent phase shifts—generically decouple the degradation rates of
V_{A}
and
V_{N}
. The identity (
3
) provides an exact bookkeeping under any such process, and the ratio
V_{N}^{2}/(V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2})
may serve as a candidate indicator of the symmetry class of the noise, of potential utility in the characterization of decoherence channels in optical quantum devices. The
2\otimes 2
framework adopted here assumes that path and polarization exhaust the relevant degrees of freedom; in experiments where spectral, temporal, or transverse-mode correlations carry significant weight, equation (
3
) applies to the corresponding marginal obtained after tracing over the additional sectors.

The reduction of the joint coherence matrix to the path sector discards the polarimetric information at the two slits, which is carried by the diagonal-by-path blocks of
A
and
N
. Extending equation (
3
) to the full
4\times 4
object and using the Fano parametrization of the joint state yields the compact identity
V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+P_{\mathrm{pol}}^{2}+\|T\|_{F}^{2}=3P_{\Delta}^{2}
, where
P_{\mathrm{pol}}
is the global degree of polarization,
\|T\|_{F}
is the Frobenius norm of the path–polarization correlation tensor, and
P_{\Delta}
is the overall polarimetric purity index of the coherence matrix
[
12
,
13
]
. The sum of the five local invariants equals three times the squared overall polarimetric purity, a direct algebraic bridge between the present result and the established formalism of polarimetric purity. The integer coefficient
3
admits a transparent reading as the number of independent informational directions of the
2\otimes 2
state in the sector accessible by local observables and bipartite correlations. Detailed derivations are given in the Supplement.

The Hermitian decomposition
\rho=A+iN
on which the present identity rests has scope beyond the optical setting: the partition into phase-insensitive and phase-sensitive sectors applies to density operators in broader contexts, where the antisymmetric sector retains its role as the substrate of phase-oriented observables
[
14
,
15
]
. The polarized double-slit experiment provides its optically accessible minimal realization.

In conclusion, we have established an exact identity relating four experimentally accessible invariants of the polarized double-slit experiment, derived it as a consequence of the Hermitian decomposition of the joint coherence matrix and the positivity of the reduced state, shown that it arises naturally from maximum-entropy inference on the partitioned algebra of observables, and identified the
N
sector of the coherence matrix as the specific locus of phase-sensitive information. The identity unifies previous complementarity relations, refines the measurement-level content of fringe visibility into two operationally distinct components, and provides a quantitative account of the informational bookkeeping under decoherence. The decomposition principle on which it rests has scope beyond the present optical context, where it admits an interpretation within a broader information-theoretic framework whose consequences are explored elsewhere.

S1
Algebraic derivations

S1.1
Hermitian decomposition and the trace-orthogonality lemma

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian matrix,
\rho^{\dagger}=\rho
. Writing
\rho=R+iJ
with
R,J\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
real, Hermiticity imposes
R^{\top}+iJ^{\top}=R-iJ
, whence
R=R^{\top}
(real symmetric) and
J=-J^{\top}
(real antisymmetric). Identifying
A\equiv R
and
N\equiv J
, the decomposition

\rho=A+iN,\qquad A=A^{\top},\qquad N=-N^{\top}

(S1)

is unique. For
n=4
,
A
has ten independent real parameters (four diagonal plus six above-diagonal) and
N
has six (diagonal identically zero by antisymmetry, six above-diagonal).

Lemma (trace orthogonality).
For any
M_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
M_{S}=M_{S}^{\top}
and
M_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
M_{A}=-M_{A}^{\top}
, one has
\operatorname{Tr}(M_{S}M_{A})=0
.

Proof.

\operatorname{Tr}(M_{S}M_{A})=\operatorname{Tr}\big((M_{S}M_{A})^{\top}\big)=\operatorname{Tr}\big(M_{A}^{\top}M_{S}^{\top}\big)=\operatorname{Tr}\big(-M_{A}M_{S}\big)=-\operatorname{Tr}(M_{S}M_{A})
, using cyclicity of the trace in the last step. The quantity equals its own negative and therefore vanishes.
\square

Corollary (decoupling identity).
For any Hermitian operator
\hat{O}
with matrix
O=O_{S}+iO_{A}
in the same basis,

\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\rho}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho O)=\operatorname{Tr}(AO_{S})-\operatorname{Tr}(NO_{A}),

(S2)

since the cross terms
i\operatorname{Tr}(AO_{A})
and
i\operatorname{Tr}(NO_{S})
vanish identically by the Lemma. Real-symmetric observables read out
A
; imaginary-antisymmetric observables read out
N
.

S1.3
Bloch parametrization of the reduced state

The reduced path state is

\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{pol}}\rho=\sum_{p\in\{H,V\}}\langle p|_{\mathrm{pol}}\rho|p\rangle_{\mathrm{pol}}.

(S3)

The
2\times 2
complex blocks of
\rho
associated with path pairs
(i,j)
are
\rho_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
, and the reduced state is
\rho_{\mathrm{cam},ij}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{ij})
, which is manifestly linear in
\rho
and preserves Hermiticity. The Hermitian decomposition commutes with partial tracing, so that
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=A_{\mathrm{cam}}+iN_{\mathrm{cam}}
with
A_{\mathrm{cam}}
real symmetric
2\times 2
and
N_{\mathrm{cam}}
real antisymmetric
2\times 2
.

Parametrizing
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+\vec{r}\cdot\vec{\sigma})
with
\vec{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define the three path invariants
V_{A}\equiv r_{x}
,
V_{N}\equiv r_{y}
,
\mathcal{P}\equiv r_{z}
as the Cartesian components of the Bloch vector. Their explicit relation with the matrix elements of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
reads

\displaystyle V_{A}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}\sigma_{1})=\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}+\rho_{\mathrm{cam},21}=2\operatorname{Re}\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}=2A_{\mathrm{cam},12},

(S4)

\displaystyle V_{N}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}\sigma_{2})=i(\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}-\rho_{\mathrm{cam},21})=-2\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\mathrm{cam},12}=2N_{\mathrm{cam},21},

(S5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}\sigma_{3})=\rho_{\mathrm{cam},11}-\rho_{\mathrm{cam},22}.

S1.4
Derivation of the central identity

The positivity of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
is equivalent to both of its eigenvalues being non-negative, which for a
2\times 2
Hermitian matrix with unit trace reduces to
\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}\geq 0
. Explicit calculation gives

\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=\tfrac{1}{4}(1-|\vec{r}|^{2}).

(S8)

Defining

\mathcal{I}^{2}\equiv 1-|\vec{r}|^{2}=4\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=2\big(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}^{2}\big),

(S9)

one obtains the exact identity

V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{I}^{2}=1

(S10)

as a direct consequence of
|\vec{r}|^{2}=V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}
. Equation (
S10
) is independent of the polarization sector of
\rho
and holds for every normalized state.

S1.5
Relation to entanglement measures

For pure states
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{pol}}
, the Schmidt decomposition writes
|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|e_{1},f_{1}\rangle+\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}|e_{2},f_{2}\rangle
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
and Wootters concurrence
\mathcal{C}=2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
. The reduced state has eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}
and determinant
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
, whence

\mathcal{I}^{2}=4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=\mathcal{C}^{2}\qquad\text{(pure states)}.

(S11)

The identity (
S10
) reduces to
V^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+\mathcal{C}^{2}=1
, the Jakob–Bergou complete-complementarity relation.

For mixed states, the Wootters concurrence is defined as
\mathcal{C}=\max\{0,\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}-\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}-\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{3}}-\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{4}}\}
with
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}
the eigenvalues in decreasing order of
\rho(\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{2})\rho^{*}(\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{2})
. The inequality
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathcal{I}
, with equality on pure states, follows directly from the bound
\mathcal{C}^{2}\leq 4\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
valid for any two-qubit state
[
9
,
16
]
: since
\mathcal{I}^{2}=4\det\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
by definition, this is precisely the stated inequality. The slack
\mathcal{I}^{2}-\mathcal{C}^{2}\geq 0
quantifies the residual mixedness of the reduced state beyond what is explained by path–polarization entanglement, and corresponds to classical mixtures, environmental correlations, or loss-of-track processes that do not generate genuine quantum correlations
[
17
]
.

S2
Maximum-entropy derivation

S2.1
Variational setup

Given a set of
n
accessible observables
\{\hat{O}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
with measured expectation values
\{o_{k}\}
, the von Neumann entropy
S(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\ln\rho)
is maximized subject to the constraints
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\hat{O}_{k})=o_{k}
and
\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1
. The Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}[\rho]=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\ln\rho)-\lambda_{0}(\operatorname{Tr}\rho-1)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\big(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\hat{O}_{k})-o_{k}\big).

(S12)

The variation
\delta\mathcal{L}/\delta\rho=0
gives
-\ln\rho-I-\lambda_{0}I-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\hat{O}_{k}=0
, hence

\rho^{*}=\frac{1}{Z(\vec{\lambda})}\exp\Big(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\hat{O}_{k}\Big),\quad Z(\vec{\lambda})=\operatorname{Tr}\exp\Big(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\hat{O}_{k}\Big),

(S13)

with
\vec{\lambda}
determined by the
n
constraint equations
\partial\ln Z/\partial\lambda_{k}=-o_{k}
.

S2.2
Structural role of the sector decomposition

Partitioning the observables into those with
S
-type (real symmetric) matrix in the ordered product basis and those with
A
-type (imaginary antisymmetric) matrix,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\hat{O}_{k}=\hat{L}_{S}+i\hat{L}_{A},

(S14)

where
\hat{L}_{S}
is real symmetric and
\hat{L}_{A}
is real antisymmetric. The matrix exponential
\exp(-\hat{L}_{S}-i\hat{L}_{A})
is Hermitian, but its decomposition
\rho^{*}=A^{*}+iN^{*}
does not in general coincide with the partition of the exponent except when
[\hat{L}_{S},\hat{L}_{A}]=0
.

Nevertheless, the following implication holds unconditionally and is the structural claim of the variational derivation.

Proposition.
If no observables of
A
type are constrained, i.e. if
\hat{L}_{A}=0
, then
\rho^{*}
is real symmetric and
N^{*}=0
. In particular
V_{N}^{*}=0
regardless of the data on the
S
-type observables.

Proof.
If
\hat{L}_{A}=0
, the exponent is real symmetric, and
\exp(-\hat{L}_{S})=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-\hat{L}_{S})^{k}/k!
is a convergent series of real symmetric matrices and hence itself real symmetric; this conclusion is independent of whether the individual operators
\hat{O}_{k}
that make up
\hat{L}_{S}
commute with one another, since the sum
\hat{L}_{S}
is itself real symmetric and the exponential depends only on
\hat{L}_{S}
. Normalization by the scalar
Z^{-1}
preserves reality and symmetry. The Hermitian decomposition of
\rho^{*}
therefore has
N^{*}=0
, from which
V_{N}^{*}=-2N^{*}_{\mathrm{cam},12}=0
follows immediately.
\square

Converse.
If any
A
-type observable is constrained with a non-zero value, then
N^{*}\neq 0
necessarily. Indeed,
A
-type observables have identically zero expectation on real symmetric states by the decoupling identity (
S2
), so a real-symmetric
\rho^{*}
cannot satisfy a non-trivial constraint on the antisymmetric sector. The
A
sector of
\rho^{*}
is populated specifically by constraints of the
A
type, which is the operational content of the claim that the antisymmetric sector of the coherence matrix is the footprint of phase-sensitive observables.

S2.3
Minimal example: three constraints on the path sector

Constraining the three path observables
\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{cam}}\otimes I
for
\mu=1,2,3
with values
(V_{A},V_{N},\mathcal{P})
, the MaxEnt state over the joint
4
-dimensional space factorizes as
\rho^{*}=\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}^{*}\otimes(I/2)
(no polarization information constrained, hence maximally mixed polarization). The reduced path state is

\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}^{*}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+V_{A}\sigma_{1}+V_{N}\sigma_{2}+\mathcal{P}\sigma_{3}),

(S15)

which is positive iff
V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}\leq 1
. The saturation condition is equivalent to
\mathcal{I}^{2}=0
, and at saturation the state is pure. This minimal example shows that the identity (
S10
) is the unique non-trivial algebraic constraint relating the three path invariants to the remaining mixedness of the reduced state.

S3
Extension to the full
4\times 4
coherence matrix

S3.1
Fano parametrization

A general
2\otimes 2
state admits the Fano parametrization

\rho=\tfrac{1}{4}\Big(I\otimes I+\vec{r}_{\mathrm{cam}}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\otimes I+I\otimes\vec{r}_{\mathrm{pol}}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+\sum_{ij=1}^{3}T_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\Big),

(S16)

with
\vec{r}_{\mathrm{cam}},\vec{r}_{\mathrm{pol}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the marginal Bloch vectors of path and polarization, and
T_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
the components of the
3\times 3
correlation tensor. The global purity is

\mu^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}\Big(1+|\vec{r}_{\mathrm{cam}}|^{2}+|\vec{r}_{\mathrm{pol}}|^{2}+\|T\|_{F}^{2}\Big),

(S17)

with
\|T\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{ij}T_{ij}^{2}
the Frobenius norm.

S3.2
Generalized identity

Using
|\vec{r}_{\mathrm{cam}}|^{2}=V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}
and writing
P_{\mathrm{pol}}^{2}=|\vec{r}_{\mathrm{pol}}|^{2}
, one obtains

V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+P_{\mathrm{pol}}^{2}+\|T\|_{F}^{2}=4\mu^{2}-1,

(S18)

exact for every normalized state. Equation (
S18
) generalizes (
S10
) by including the global polarization information
P_{\mathrm{pol}}
and the path–polarization correlations
\|T\|_{F}
. Its right-hand side is bounded by
3
(pure states,
\mu=1
) and
0
(maximally mixed,
\mu=1/2
).

The reduced identity (
S10
) is recovered from (
S18
) by expressing
|\vec{r}_{\mathrm{cam}}|^{2}+\mathcal{I}^{2}=1
, which is equivalent to the positivity of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
. The reduced identity is therefore an exact algebraic consequence of the positivity of
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}
rather than a truncation of (
S18
).

S3.3
Connection with polarimetric purity indices

The coherence matrix of the full joint state admits analysis through the polarimetric purity indices
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
introduced in Refs.
[
12
,
13
]
for the characterization of partially polarized systems. These indices, defined from the ordered eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\lambda_{4}\geq 0
of the coherence matrix (with
\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}=1
), read

P_{1}=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\;P_{2}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-2\lambda_{3},\;P_{3}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}-3\lambda_{4},

(S19)

and satisfy
0\leq P_{1}\leq P_{2}\leq P_{3}\leq 1
, with
P_{3}=1
characterizing states whose coherence matrix has rank strictly less than four.

The overall degree of polarimetric purity of Gil–Bernabéu
[
12
,
13
]
is defined as

P_{\Delta}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\Big(4\mu^{2}-1\Big)=\frac{4}{3}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{4}\lambda_{k}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\Big),

(S20)

normalized so that
P_{\Delta}=1
for pure states and
P_{\Delta}=0
for the maximally mixed state. Substituting
4\mu^{2}-1=3P_{\Delta}^{2}
into (
S18
) gives the compact form

V_{A}^{2}+V_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}+P_{\mathrm{pol}}^{2}+\|T\|_{F}^{2}=3P_{\Delta}^{2},

(S21)

which states that the sum of the five local invariants of the joint path–polarization state equals three times the squared overall polarimetric purity of its coherence matrix. This is an exact equality and, for pure states (
P_{\Delta}^{2}=1
), recovers the maximal value
3
of the five-invariant sum. The integer coefficient
3
admits a transparent reading as the number of independent informational directions of the
2\otimes 2
state in the sector accessible by local observables and bipartite correlations. The finer decomposition of
P_{\Delta}
into the indices
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
refines this accounting at the spectral level but does not alter the total on the right-hand side of (
S21
).

S4
Numerical verification

We have verified the identity (
S10
) on the following representative set of states. In all cases the squared sum equals unity to machine precision.

Case 1. Product pure state.

|\psi\rangle=|1\rangle\otimes|H\rangle
. Invariants:
V_{A}=V_{N}=0
,
\mathcal{P}=1
,
\mathcal{I}=0
. Sum:
1
.

Case 2. Path superposition, fixed polarization.

|\psi\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle+|2\rangle)\otimes|H\rangle
.
V_{A}=1
,
V_{N}=\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}=0
. Sum:
1
.

Case 3. Path superposition with
\pi/2
phase.

|\psi\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle+i|2\rangle)\otimes|H\rangle
.
V_{A}=\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}=0
,
V_{N}=1
. Sum:
1
.

Case 4. Unbalanced coherent superposition.

|\psi\rangle=(\cos\alpha|1\rangle+\sin\alpha\,e^{i\beta}|2\rangle)\otimes|H\rangle
. Direct computation gives
V_{A}=\sin 2\alpha\cos\beta
,
V_{N}=\sin 2\alpha\sin\beta
,
\mathcal{P}=\cos 2\alpha
,
\mathcal{I}=0
. Sum:
\sin^{2}2\alpha+\cos^{2}2\alpha=1
.

Case 5. Bell-type entangled state.

|\psi\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1,H\rangle+|2,V\rangle)
.
V_{A}=V_{N}=\mathcal{P}=0
,
\mathcal{I}^{2}=1
. Sum:
1
.

Case 6. Partially entangled state.

|\psi\rangle=\cos\chi|1,H\rangle+\sin\chi|2,V\rangle
.
V_{A}=V_{N}=0
,
\mathcal{P}=\cos 2\chi
,
\mathcal{I}^{2}=\sin^{2}2\chi=\mathcal{C}^{2}
. Sum:
1
.

Case 7. Maximally mixed state.

\rho=I/4
. All invariants vanish except
\mathcal{I}^{2}=1
. Sum:
1
.

Case 8. Path-dephased coherent superposition.

\rho=\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|)
with
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle+e^{i\phi}|2\rangle)\otimes(\cos\theta|H\rangle+\sin\theta|V\rangle)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
as defined in the Letter. Invariants:
V_{A}=(1-2\gamma)\cos\phi
,
V_{N}=(1-2\gamma)\sin\phi
,
\mathcal{P}=0
,
\mathcal{I}^{2}=4\gamma(1-\gamma)
. Sum:
(1-2\gamma)^{2}+4\gamma(1-\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma,\phi,\theta
.

Case 9. Werner-type mixture.

\rho=p|\Phi^{+}\rangle\langle\Phi^{+}|+(1-p)I/4
with
|\Phi^{+}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1,H\rangle+|2,V\rangle)
and
p\in[0,1]
. Direct computation gives
V_{A}=V_{N}=\mathcal{P}=0
and
\mathcal{I}^{2}=1
for all
p
, since
\rho_{\mathrm{cam}}=I/2
is invariant under the mixture. Sum:
1
. The concurrence
\mathcal{C}(p)=\max\{0,(3p-1)/2\}
varies with
p
, illustrating
\mathcal{I}\geq\mathcal{C}
with strict inequality for
p<1
.

Case 10. Generic mixed state (numerical).
A random Hermitian positive matrix of trace one, generated by drawing a
4\times 4
complex Gaussian matrix
G
, forming
\rho=GG^{\dagger}/\operatorname{Tr}(GG^{\dagger})
, and computing the four invariants. Over
10^{4}
samples, the squared sum equals unity to within numerical precision (
|1-\sum|<10^{-14}
in IEEE double precision).

All cases confirm that equation (
S10
) is an exact algebraic identity holding unconditionally on normalized states of the composite
2\otimes 2
system.

S5
Remarks on the decomposition principle

The identification of the Hermitian decomposition
\rho=A+iN
as the natural coordinate on the state space, structured by the partition of the algebra of accessible observables into
S
-type and
A
-type, extends beyond the optical setting of this Letter. In broader information-theoretic contexts, the same decomposition has been shown to organize the substrate of observables sensitive to phase orientation
[
14
,
15
]
. The present polarized double-slit experiment constitutes the simplest non-trivial optical realization in which both sectors of the decomposition are operationally accessible and experimentally distinguishable, and the identity (
S10
) is the corresponding exact statement at the level of laboratory-accessible invariants.
