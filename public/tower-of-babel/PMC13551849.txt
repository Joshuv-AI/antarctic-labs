Insufficient mpox knowledge among public health professionals involved in infectious disease prevention and control in China: a large-scale online cross-sectional survey
Authors: Minxia Hua, Wenqian Zhu, Zewei Chen, Yuelin Wu, Shiqing Liang, Yiting Xie, Xiaoli Yue, Jing Li, Jiahui Zhang, Xiangdong Gong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe World Health Organization has declared mpox a Public Health Emergency of International Concern twice. In China, public health professionals (PHPs) involved in infectious disease prevention and control are responsible for mpox control, case reporting, and health communications during outbreaks. This study aimed to assess mpox knowledge and factors associated with sufficient mpox knowledge among PHPs in China.MethodsThis study analyzed PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control from a large-scale online cross-sectional survey of healthcare workers conducted through a nationwide platform. An e-questionnaire was used to collect data on demographic and institutional characteristics, attention to mpox, mpox information sources, training history and 37 mpox knowledge items covering etiology, clinical features, transmission, and prevention. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with sufficient mpox knowledge.ResultsA total of 1,601 PHPs from 28 of 31 provincial-level regions in China completed the survey, and 1,568 valid responses were included. Among the 1,568 PHPs, 91.3% (1,432/1,568) were aware of mpox, and 61.7% (967/1,568, 95% CI: 59.3%–64.1%) had sufficient mpox knowledge. 44.6% (95% CI: 42.1%–47.0%), 74.2% (95%CI: 72.1%–76.4%) and 47.0% (95% CI: 44.5%–49.5%) PHPs aware of mpox had sufficient knowledge regarding etiology, clinical features and transmission and prevention, respectively. Multivariable logistic regression analysis showed that the associated factors with sufficient mpox knowledge included gender (female: OR = 1.34, 95% CI: 1.03–1.74), professional title (intermediate: OR = 1.85, 95% CI: 1.40–2.45, senior: OR = 2.05, 95% CI: 1.44–2.91), institution (CDC: OR = 2.07, 95% CI: 1.60–2.67), attention toward mpox (occasional: OR = 2.59, 95% CI: 1.72–3.90; daily/frequent: OR = 3.70, 95% CI: 2.39–5.73), mpox information sources (two sources: OR = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.14-2.00; three or more sources: OR = 1.77, 95% CI: 1.31–2.39), and training (OR = 2.44, 95% CI: 1.89–3.15).ConclusionThis study found insufficient mpox knowledge among the surveyed PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control across multiple regions of China, especially among males, those with junior professional titles, limited information access, low attention to mpox, no prior training, and hospital-based staff. Targeted training, continuing education, and multi-channel information dissemination should be strengthened to support mpox preparedness among frontline PHPs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-28260-w.Keywords: Mpox, Public health professionals, Knowledge, Associated factors, Training

Background

Mpox is a zoonotic orthopoxviral disease caused by the mpox virus (MPXV), clinically characterized by fever, vesiculopustular rash, and lymphadenopathy [1, 2]. Before 2022, mpox was primarily endemic to Central and West Africa, with spillover transmission from infected animals and human-to-human transmission through close contact, particularly sexual contact [1, 3, 4]. The first human case was identified in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 1970 [5], while the first outbreak outside Africa occurred in the United States in 2003 following contact with imported prairie dogs [6].

In May 2022, a multi-country mpox outbreak emerged across Europe and the Americas, with more than 81,000 laboratory-confirmed cases reported globally by December 2022 [7]. This unprecedented transcontinental spread, which disproportionately affected men who have sex with men (MSM) in non-endemic regions, prompted the World Health Organization (WHO) to declare mpox a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) in July 2022 [8]. Subsequently, a resurgence of mpox led WHO to issue a second PHEIC declaration in August 2024 [9].

In China, the first imported human mpox case on the mainland was confirmed on September 16, 2022 [10]. According to relevant laws and regulations in China, mpox cases are required to be reported to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDCs). Although current epidemiological indicators suggest sustained low-level community transmission, extensive sexual contact networks among MSM populations may maintain transmission risks, highlighting the need for enhanced surveillance and targeted prevention measures [11, 12].

Healthcare workers (HCWs), including public health professionals (PHPs), clinical physicians and nurses, are recognized by WHO as a group at increased risk of infectious disease exposure [13]. In China, PHPs work in CDCs and in public health departments of hospitals at national, provincial, prefectural, and county levels. PHPs in CDCs are responsible for local disease surveillance, disease prevention and control, outbreak response, as well as infectious disease information release and health education. PHPs in hospitals are responsible for infectious disease case reporting and management within their respective institutions.

At the beginning of the mpox outbreak, WHO released epidemic updates and prevention and control guidance. China also formulated and issued relevant official documents and timely epidemic updates, which were disseminated to provincial, prefectural, and county-level medical institutions and CDCs. However, the level of mpox-related knowledge among PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control remained unclear. Meanwhile, existing studies have focused mainly on mpox knowledge among clinical physicians [14, 15]. Therefore, based on a large-scale online survey of HCWs conducted through a nationwide platform, this study focused on PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control to assess their mpox knowledge and explore factors associated with sufficient knowledge.

Methods

Study population and recruitment

This cross-sectional study was conducted using an online survey among HCWs in China from September 16 to September 29, 2022. The study population was PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control, a subset of the cross-sectional survey participants. Participants were recruited using a non-random convenience sampling approach via the Hand in Hand Medical Visit (Xieshou Yifang, in Chinese) platform, an official national health communication and service initiative for sexually transmitted disease (STD) prevention and control. This platform was launched in 2019, administered by the National Health Commission of China and developed by the National Center for STD Control at the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. As of September 2022, 126,363 HCWs had registered on the platform. Invitation messages were sent to all registered HCWs to invite voluntary participation in the survey.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were: (1) PHPs working in CDCs or public health departments of hospitals at national, provincial, prefectural, or county levels; (2) PHPs working in CDC divisions responsible for STD/AIDS, infectious disease control and related sectors, as well as hospital PHPs involved in infectious disease case reporting and management; and (3) voluntary participation in the online survey. The exclusion criteria were: (1) not involved in infectious disease prevention and control, including those working in chronic disease management and maternal and child health services; (2) clinical physicians or nurses; and (3) non-voluntary participation in the online survey. Questionnaire and design

We developed a structured questionnaire based on authoritative guidelines, including the Interim Rapid Response Guidelines for Mpox (WHO) [18], the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Mpox (2022 edition) [19], and the Technical Guidelines for Mpox Prevention and Control (2022 edition) [20]. The questionnaire showed good reliability (α = 0.87) and validity (KMO = 0.98, P < 0.001). The questionnaire has been detailed in a previous Chinese publication [21], with the English supplementary version provided in the Supplementary File 1.

The questionnaire collected demographic and institutional characteristics, mpox information sources, attention to mpox and mpox-related training history. Mpox knowledge was assessed using 37 items across three domains: etiology (8 items, 8 points), clinical features (15 items, 15 points), and transmission and prevention (14 items, 14 points), yielding a total score of 37 points. For multiple-choice questions, responses were scored as correct only when all correct options were selected without any incorrect options.

Mpox knowledge was categorized according to modified Bloom’s taxonomy, which has been widely used in educational and knowledge assessment studies to classify levels of mastery [22]. Based on previous knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) studies and mpox-related knowledge studies using Bloom’s taxonomy-based cutoff criteria, sufficient mpox knowledge was operationally defined as correctly answering at least 70% of the 37 knowledge items [23–25]. Therefore, participants with a total score of ≥ 26 were classified as having sufficient mpox knowledge, whereas those scoring < 26 were classified as having insufficient mpox knowledge.

A pre-specified logical skip rule was applied. Participants who selected “never heard of mpox” were not asked the detailed knowledge questions or subsequent questions on information sources, attention to mpox, and training history. They were assigned a total knowledge score of zero for the overall knowledge assessment but were excluded from item-specific and multivariable regression analyses because the detailed knowledge items and related explanatory variables were not collected.

To ensure data quality, all key questions were mandatory, logical skip rules were embedded in the questionnaire, and each mobile number was allowed to submit only one response to prevent duplicate submissions.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 30.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were reported as mean ± SD, non-normally distributed variables as median (IQR), and categorical variables as n (%). Univariable logistic regression analyses were first performed to screen candidate variables. Variables with P < 0.250 in univariable analyses were considered for multivariable logistic regression to avoid prematurely excluding potentially relevant variables that might become significant after adjustment [26, 27]. A forward stepwise logistic regression model was used to identify factors associated with sufficient mpox knowledge and to obtain a parsimonious explanatory model. Backward stepwise logistic regression was also performed to examine the consistency of model selection. Nominal variables were dummy-coded, ordinal predictors were assessed using trend tests, and all tests were two-sided with α = 0.05.

Results

Demographic and institutional characteristics of the study population

A total of 1,601 PHPs from 28 of 31 provincial-level regions, excluding Hainan, Xizang, and Shanghai, voluntarily completed the online survey. After excluding invalid responses, 1,568 valid questionnaires were included in the final analysis, representing a valid response rate of 97.9%. The demographic and institutional characteristics of the 1,568 PHPs are presented in Table 1. PHPs from the Central-South, North, Northeast, East, and West regions accounted for 22.8% (357/1,568), 13.1% (206/1,568), 40.4% (633/1,568), 11.1% (174/1,568), and 12.6% (198/1,568), respectively. The mean age was 39.30 ± 9.88 years, with the youngest being 20 years old and the oldest being 61 years old. 71.0% (1,113/1,568) were females. The majority of the PHPs had bachelor’s degrees, accounting for 84.0% (1,317/1,568). PHPs with junior, intermediate, and senior professional titles accounted for 52.4% (821/1,568), 30.0% (471/1,568), and 17.6% (276/1,568), respectively. PHPs working in CDCs represented 61.5% (964/1,568).

Among 1,568 PHPs, 91.3% (1,432/1,568) were aware of mpox and 8.7% (136/1,568) were unaware. Participants unaware of mpox were older (41.10 ± 10.30 years) than those aware (39.13 ± 9.82 years) (P = 0.026). The distribution of mpox awareness differed by region (P = 0.034). Among participants unaware of mpox, the largest proportion was from the Northeast region (55/136, 40.4%), while the East region accounted for the smallest proportion (6/136, 4.4%). The distribution of education level also differed by mpox awareness status (P < 0.001). Participants with a diploma or below accounted for a larger proportion among those unaware of mpox (34/136, 25.0%) than among those aware of mpox (135/1,432, 9.4%).

Time of first awareness and attention to mpox

Among 1,432 PHPs aware of mpox, 47.8% (684/1,432) first heard of mpox before April 2022, while 40.7% (583/1,432) first heard of it between April 2022 and the survey period. As for recent attention to mpox, 40.5% (580/1,432) reported frequently engaging with such content over the past six months, whereas 59.5% (852/1,432) indicated they only occasionally or rarely followed updates on the topic.

Mpox information source

Among PHPs aware of mpox, 87.2% (1,249/1,432) obtained mpox information through online new media platforms such as WeChat, Weibo, and Douyin. Another 63.0% (902/1,432) acquired it via traditional channels like television, newspapers, and radio. 32.1% (459/1,432) obtained the information from professional websites, and 17.4% (249/1,432) learned from relatives, friends, or neighbors through word-of-mouth. In total, 39.3% (563/1,432) of participants reported a single information source, 30.6% (438/1,432) reported 2 sources, and 30.1% (431/1,432) reported 3 or more sources.

Mpox-related training

Among PHPs aware of mpox, 55.9% (801/1,432) reported that their institutions had provided mpox-related training or lectures, while 44.1% (631/1,432) reported no such training.

Knowledge level regarding mpox

Among 1,568 PHPs, 61.7% (967/1,568, 95% CI: 59.3%–64.1%) had sufficient mpox knowledge, and only 0.38% (6/1,568, 95% CI: 0.1%–0.8%) participants correctly answered all the knowledge questions. Correct responses for each knowledge item were calculated among the 1,432 participants aware of mpox and completed the knowledge questionnaire.

Etiology of mpox

The median score for this section was 5 (3, 7), accounting for only 62.5% (5/8) of this domain. Only 44.6% (95% CI: 42.1%–47.0%) PHPs aware of mpox had sufficient knowledge regarding etiology. While 86.1% correctly identified “Effective inactivation methods for MPXV”, only 43.2% recognized “MPXV and varicella-zoster virus are not in the same family” (Table 2).

Factors associated with mpox knowledge among PHPs

Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed among 1,432 PHPs who were aware of mpox and had complete data on the core variables. The 136 participants who had never heard of mpox were excluded from these analyses because the detailed knowledge items and related explanatory variables were not collected due to the questionnaire skip rule. Results of the univariable logistic regression analysis showed that variables such as region, age, gender, educational level, professional title, institution, institution level, attention to mpox, mpox information sources, and mpox-related training had P < 0.250 and were considered candidates for multivariable analysis.

The variables retained were consistent between the forward and backward stepwise models; therefore, the forward stepwise results are presented in Table 5. In the final multivariable model, gender, professional title, institution, attention to mpox, mpox information sources, and mpox-related training were significantly associated with sufficient mpox knowledge. Specifically, females were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge than males (OR = 1.34, 95% CI: 1.03–1.74). PHPs with intermediate (OR = 1.85, 95% CI: 1.40–2.45) and senior professional titles (OR = 2.05, 95% CI: 1.44–2.91) were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge than those with junior professional titles. Compared with hospital-based PHPs, those working in CDCs had higher odds of sufficient mpox knowledge (OR = 2.07, 95% CI: 1.60–2.67). Participants who occasionally paid attention to mpox (OR = 2.59, 95% CI: 1.72–3.90) or paid daily/frequent attention to mpox (OR = 3.70, 95% CI: 2.39–5.73) were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge than those who rarely paid attention to mpox. Accessing two sources (OR = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.14–2.00) or three or more sources of mpox information (OR = 1.77, 95% CI: 1.31–2.39) was associated with higher odds of sufficient mpox knowledge compared with accessing a single source. Participants who had received mpox-related training were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge than those without training (OR = 2.44, 95% CI: 1.89–3.15).

Discussion

This study assessed mpox knowledge among 1,568 surveyed PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control in China and explored factors associated with sufficient mpox knowledge. We found that only 61.7% of the surveyed PHPs had sufficient mpox knowledge. Although this proportion was higher than that reported among physicians (55.0%) and nurses (58.0%) [28, 29], it remained insufficient among PHPs responsible for infectious disease prevention and control. Notably, only 0.38% of participants correctly answered all the knowledge questions, indicating a very low proportion of complete mastery among the surveyed PHPs.

In this study, we found that region, age, and education level were associated with mpox unawareness among surveyed PHPs. Among PHPs aware of mpox, those with intermediate or senior professional titles, employment in CDCs, multiple mpox information sources, higher attention to mpox, and mpox-related training were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge. Notably, PHPs with intermediate and senior professional titles were more likely to have sufficient mpox knowledge than junior PHPs, which is consistent with China’s competency-based professional certification system [30]. However, due to the cross-sectional design, these associations should not be interpreted as causal effects. These findings identify priority groups for future training and information dissemination, particularly PHPs with junior professional titles, limited information access, low attention to mpox, and no prior mpox-related training.

Moreover, in the overall knowledge assessment among all surveyed PHPs, a higher proportion of PHPs working in CDCs (66.6%) had sufficient mpox knowledge than hospital-based PHPs (53.8%), which is consistent with the findings of Peng et al. [31]. Hospital-based PHPs are often involved in infectious disease case reporting and management within healthcare facilities. Therefore, the relatively lower level of mpox knowledge observed among hospital-based PHPs may indicate potential gaps in preparedness for case identification, reporting, and initial response. These findings suggest that targeted mpox-related training and continuing education should be strengthened for hospital-based PHPs.

Enhancing mpox knowledge among PHPs is important for strengthening preparedness for mpox prevention and control. Effective public health surveillance, risk communication, and early response depend partly on the professional capacity of PHPs, underscoring the need for a comprehensive understanding of the disease [32]. In our study, only 44.6% and 47.0% of participants demonstrated sufficient knowledge of mpox etiology and transmission and prevention, respectively. These findings indicate important knowledge gaps in domains closely related to case identification, risk assessment, and prevention practices. Therefore, targeted and timely educational interventions may be warranted [33]. Healthcare and public health institutions should consider integrated, systematic, and multi-channel training and continuing education programs through both traditional and digital platforms, with tailored content for PHPs in hospitals, CDCs, and other settings, to support sustained attention to and accurate understanding of mpox.

For future practice, continuous education and targeted training for frontline PHPs should be strengthened to support early detection, risk communication, and emergency response for mpox and other emerging infectious diseases. Our findings highlight the importance of routine training, standardized professional development, and multi-channel information dissemination in strengthening mpox preparedness and supporting the prevention and control of zoonotic diseases in China.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Therefore, the observed associations between mpox knowledge and related factors, including mpox-related training, information sources, and attention to mpox, should be interpreted as exploratory rather than causal. Second, although invitation messages were distributed through a nationwide online platform and valid responses were obtained from 28 of 31 provincial-level regions, participants were recruited using a voluntary convenience sampling strategy. PHPs who were more familiar with online professional platforms or more interested in emerging infectious diseases may have been more likely to participate, which may have introduced selection bias and potentially overestimated the mpox knowledge level. In addition, several provincial-level regions were not represented, and no weighting or post-stratification adjustment was performed. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as reflecting the surveyed PHPs rather than nationally representative estimates for all PHPs in China. Third, participants who had never heard of mpox were assigned a total knowledge score of zero for the overall knowledge assessment, which may have affected the estimated proportion with sufficient mpox knowledge. They were excluded from item-specific and regression analyses because relevant knowledge items and explanatory variables were not collected due to the questionnaire skip rule. Therefore, the regression findings should be interpreted as applying to PHPs who were aware of mpox. Finally, the use of an online self-administered questionnaire may have introduced information bias, although several quality control measures were applied. This study focused only on PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control, and did not include clinical physicians, nurses, or PHPs not involved in infectious disease prevention and control. In addition, some potentially relevant factors, such as work experience, specific job responsibilities, previous outbreak response experience, and self-efficacy in mpox prevention and control, were not collected, which may have led to residual confounding.

Conclusions

This study found insufficient mpox knowledge among the surveyed PHPs involved in infectious disease prevention and control across multiple regions of China, particularly among males, PHPs with junior professional titles, those with limited information access, low attention to mpox, no prior mpox-related training, and hospital-based staff. These findings identify priority groups for future capacity-building efforts and highlight the need for targeted continuing education, specialized training, and multi-channel information dissemination to support mpox preparedness among frontline PHPs.
