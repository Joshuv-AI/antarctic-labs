Large sum-free sets in finite vector spaces II.
Christian Reiher, Sofia Zotova

Abstract
. Answering a question of Leo Versteegen, we prove that for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 every sum-free set A ⊆ 𝔽 5 n A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n} with | A | ≥ 28 ⋅ 5 n − 3 |A|\geq 28\cdot 5^{n-3} is either contained in the union of two parallel hyperplanes, or isomorphic to Λ × 𝔽 5 n − 3 \Lambda\times{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n-3} , where Λ ⊆ 𝔽 5 3 \Lambda\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{3} denotes a certain sum-free set of size 28 28 discovered by Vsevolod Lev and Leo Versteegen.

1.
Introduction

A subset
A
of an abelian group
G
is called
sum-free
if there are no
solutions of
x+y=z
with
x,y,z\in A
. The study of such sets has a long history,
which can be traced back to work of Issai Schur
[
14
]
motivated by Fermat’s last
theorem.

Given a finite abelian group
G
we write
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)
for the maximum cardinality of
a sum-free subset of
G
. Building on earlier work by many researchers, the determination
of this group invariant was completed by Ben Green and Imre Ruzsa
[
4
]
.
Once
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)
is known, one can ask further for a classification
of the collection

\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}(G)=\{A\subseteq G\colon\text{$A$ is sum-free and }|A|=\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)\}

of all maximum sum-free sets. The solution of this problem was completed by Balasubramanian,
Prakash, and Ramana
[
1
]
. The next natural question is to determine the largest
possible size
\mathrm{sf}_{1}(G)
of a sum-free set
A\subseteq G
that it not contained in
any set from
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}(G)
.

In an early contribution, Davydov and Tombak
[
2
]
solved this problem for binary vector
spaces, where it has connections to coding theory.
We have
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{2}^{n})=2^{n-1}
for every
n\geq 1
and the only maximum sum-free
sets are affine hyperplanes not containing the origin. For
{n\leq 3}
all sum-free subsets
can be covered by such hyperplanes, while for
n\geq 4
Davydov and Tombak
proved
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{2}^{n})=5\cdot 2^{n-4}
.

For ternary vector spaces, the analogous problem was solved by Vsevolod Lev
[
9
]
.
The maximum sum-free subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n}
are again affine hyperplanes not passing through
the origin, so they have size
3^{n-1}
. In the nontrivial case
n\geq 3
the main result
of
[
9
]
states
{\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n})=5\cdot 3^{n-3}}
.

Continuing this line of research, Vsevolod Lev
[
10
]
, followed by Leo
Versteegen
[
15
]
, initiated the investigation of
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})
.
Perhaps interestingly, for all primes
p\geq 7
and all dimensions
n\geq 1

the invariant
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
has been determined in the meantime
(see
[
12
]
and the references therein), so
p=5
is the last open case.
It is well known that
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})=2\cdot 5^{n-1}
and that the only
maximum sum-free subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
are sets of the form
H\cup(-H)
, where
H

denotes an affine hyperplane not containing the origin (cf. Fact
2.3
).
A sum-free set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}

is called
normal
if it is a subset of such a set. Thus
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})
is the largest
cardinality of a non-normal sum-free subset of
{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
. Since all sum-free subsets
of
{\mathds{F}}_{5}
are normal, this invariant is only interesting for
{n\geq 2}
.
Leo Versteegen
[
15
]
showed
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}^{2}_{5})=5
and
based on his analysis it is easy to classify all extremal examples.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Kneser’s theorem

Given two subsets
A
and
B
of an abelian group
G
we write

A+B=\{a+b\colon a\in A\text{ and }b\in B\}

for their
sum set
. So
A
is sum-free if and only if
(A+A)\cap A=\varnothing
.
We need a general lower bound on the cardinalities of such sum sets due to
Kneser
[
7
,
8
]
. Its formulation involves the concept of the

symmetry set
of an arbitrary subset
X\subseteq G
, which is defined
by

\mathrm{Sym}(X)=\{g\in G\colon g+X=X\}\,.

Clearly, this set is a subgroup of
G
and
X
is a union of cosets of
\mathrm{Sym}(X)
.

Theorem 2.1

(Kneser)
.

If
A
and
B
denote two finite nonempty subsets of an abelian group
G

and
K=\mathrm{Sym}(A+B)
, then

|A+B|\geq|A+K|+|B+K|-|K|\,.

The right side is clearly a multiple of
|K|
and, if
G
is finite, so is
|G|
.
Therefore, Kneser’s theorem immediately implies the following result, which could also
be proved in a much more direct manner.

Corollary 2.2
.

If two subsets
A
and
B
of a finite abelian group
G
satisfy
|A|+|B|>|G|
,
then
A+B=G
.

Later on, we shall encounter more substantial applications of Kneser’s theorem. To illustrate
the general idea, we quickly derive the well-known formula for
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})
from this
result.

Fact 2.3
.

If
A,B,C\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
are three nonempty subsets satisfying
(A+B)\cap C=\varnothing
,
then

|A|+|B|+|C|\leq 6\cdot 5^{n-1}\,.

In particular, we have
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})=2\cdot 5^{n-1}
for every natural number
n
.

Proof.

Consider the symmetry set
K=\mathrm{Sym}(A+B)
. This is a linear subspace of
{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}

and
{A+B}
is a union of translates of
K
. Since
A+B
is disjoint to the nonempty
set
C
, the dimension of
K
can be at most
n-1
, whence
|K|\leq 5^{n-1}
.
Moreover, Kneser’s theorem yields

5^{n}\geq|A+B|+|C|\geq|A+K|+|B+K|-|K|+|C|\geq|A|+|B|+|C|-5^{n-1}

and the desired upper bound
|A|+|B|+|C|\leq 6\cdot 5^{n-1}
follows.

In the special case
A=B=C
this tells us
|A|\leq 2\cdot 5^{n-1}

for every sum-free subset
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
, which proves the
upper bound
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})\leq 2\cdot 5^{n-1}
. In the other direction,
for any affine hyperplane
H
not passing through the origin the sum-free set

H\cup(-H)
exemplifies the lower bound
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})\geq 2\cdot 5^{n-1}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
assuming Proposition
2.10
.

We will establish the following statement by induction on
n\geq 2
.

Every sum-free set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
of size
|A|\geq 28\cdot 5^{n-3}

is normal or
\mathrm{VL}
.

The base case,
n=2
, amounts to the fact that every sum-free set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{2}

with at least six elements is normal, which is a simple consequence of Theorem
1.1
.
In the inductive step from
{n-1}
to
n
we consider any sum-free set

A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
of size
{|A|\geq 28\cdot 5^{n-3}}
, where
n\geq 3
.
Notice that every two-dimensional subspace
E\leq{\mathds{F}}^{n}_{5}
contains
24

nonzero vectors, while each nonzero
v\in{\mathds{F}}^{n}_{5}
is contained in the same number
of two-dimensional subspaces
E
. So because of
0\not\in A
an averaging
argument yields a two-dimensional subspace
{E\leq{\mathds{F}}^{n}_{5}}
such that

|A\cap E|\geq\left\lceil\frac{24|A|}{5^{n}-1}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{24\cdot 28}{125}\right\rceil=6\,.

By Theorem
1.1
the sum-free subset
A\cap E
of
E
is normal and, hence, there
is a one-dimensional space
L\leq E
intersecting
A
in a set of the
form
A\cap L=\{v,-v\}
. We now want to find a hyperplane
H
such
that
L\leq H\leq{\mathds{F}}^{n}_{5}
and
|A\cap H|
is large.
Since all vectors in
{{\mathds{F}}^{n}_{5}\smallsetminus L}
are in the same number
of such hyperplanes, another averaging argument provides
a hyperplane
H
satisfying

\displaystyle|A\cap H|

\displaystyle\geq 2+\left\lceil\frac{(|A|-2)(5^{n-1}-5)}{5^{n}-5}\right\rceil\geq 2+\frac{(28\cdot 5^{n-3}-2)\cdot(5^{n-1}-5)}{5^{n}}

\displaystyle>2+28\cdot 5^{n-4}-\frac{38}{25}>28\cdot 5^{n-4}\,.

Since the last inequality is strict, the induction hypothesis applied in

shows that
{A\cap H}
is a normal subset of

.
This means that there exists an affine hyperplane

. Now

satisfies the hypothesis
of Proposition
2.10
and, consequently,

is normal or

\mathrm{VL}
-set.
∎

5.
Applications of Kneser’s theorem

5.1.
Hyperplanes

In this subsection we study dense sum-free sets contained in the union of a small number
of parallel hyperplanes.

Lemma 5.1
.

If for some
n\geq 2
a sum-free set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
of size
|A|>5^{n-1}

can be covered by two parallel hyperplanes, then it is normal.

Proof.

Suppose that for some hyperplane
H\leq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}
, vector
v\in{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}\smallsetminus H
,
and scalars
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathds{F}}_{5}
we have

A\subseteq(\lambda v+H)\cup(\mu v+H)\,.

Due to
|A|>|H|
the sets
A_{\lambda}=A\cap(\lambda v+H)
and
A_{\mu}=A\cap(\mu v+H)

are nonempty. The group
{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{\times}
acts by multiplication on the two-element subsets
of
{\mathds{F}}_{5}
, with three orbits represented by
\{0,1\}
,
\{1,2\}
, and
\{1,-1\}
.
We can therefore suppose that
\{\lambda,\mu\}
is one of these three sets.
In the last case,
A
is clearly normal, so it suffices to show that the first two cases
lead to contradictions.

Suppose first that
\lambda=0
and
\mu=1
. Since
A
is sum-free,
we have
{(A_{0}+A_{1})\cap A_{1}=\varnothing}
. But now

5^{n-1}=|H+v|\geq|A_{0}+A_{1}|+|A_{1}|\geq|A_{0}|+|A_{1}|=|A|
contradicts our assumption
on the size of
A
.

The case
\lambda=1
and

Lemma 5.2

Let

The notation for sum-free sets employed in the introduction
suggests the following hierarchy of sum-free sets introduced
in
[
12
]
*Definition 1.2.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. Starting with

\mathrm{SF}_{0}(G)=\{A\subseteq G\colon A\text{ is sum-free}\}

we define by recursion on
k
the numbers and sets

•

\mathrm{sf}_{k}(G)=\max\{|A|\colon A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)\}
,

•

\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{k}(G)=\{A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)\colon|A|=\mathrm{sf}_{k}(G)\}
,

•

\mathrm{SF}_{k+1}(G)=\{A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)\colon\text{there
is no $B\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{k}(G)$ with $A\subseteq B$}\}
.

This is to some extent inspired by a similarly defined hierarchy for intersecting set
systems, which has a vast literature (see e.g.,
[

5
,
6

11

). As we have
already pointed out,

is known for every finite abelian group

). Research on

has so far mainly focused on vector spaces

and even

were known for all

for broader classes of abelian groups as well.

For
