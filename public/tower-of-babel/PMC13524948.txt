A novel DIO optimized sigmoid-activated 2DOF-PID controller for robust control of magnetic levitation systems
Authors: İrfan Ökten
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Among the classical testbeds in control engineering, magnetic levitation systems occupy a prominent place owing to their strongly nonlinear character and intrinsic open-loop instability. Stabilizing a freely suspended ferromagnetic object requires careful regulation of the electromagnetic force, which makes these systems particularly demanding for controller design. To address this challenge, the present work introduces a sigmoid-activated two-degree-of-freedom PID (SA-2DOF-PID) controller, wherein a sigmoid-based nonlinear term is embedded within a standard 2DOF-PID architecture. This integration yields smoother actuation signals and increases overall closed-loop robustness. Controller parameter selection is carried out via the dholes-inspired optimization (DIO) algorithm, a recently proposed nature-inspired method. A multi-objective fitness function incorporating peak overshoot, settling time, and cumulative tracking error guides the tuning procedure. Simulation experiments performed under both nominal plant conditions and deliberate parametric perturbations serve to assess the proposed scheme. Quantitative comparisons against secant optimization algorithm (SOA)-, honey badger algorithm (HBA)-, and particle swarm optimization (PSO)-based controllers reveal that the DIO-tuned SA-2DOF-PID achieves markedly lower overshoot, quicker settling, and higher steady-state precision. Robustness evaluations involving intentional variations in system parameters further confirm that the closed-loop behavior remains well-damped with only marginal deterioration. Additional benchmarking against whale optimization algorithm combined with simulated annealing (WOA-SA)-based proportional–integral–derivative (PID), opposition-based artificial electric field algorithm (ObAEF)-based fractional-order PID (FOPID), and manta ray foraging optimization (MRFO)-based real PID with second-order derivative (RPIDD²) controllers corroborates the practical advantages of the proposed approach. Taken together, the study demonstrates that the SA-2DOF-PID framework tuned by DIO constitutes an effective, structurally lean, and computationally tractable strategy for managing nonlinear and open-loop unstable plants.

Introduction

Reliable position control of magnetically suspended objects has attracted sustained research interest, primarily because such systems exhibit pronounced nonlinear behavior coupled with open-loop instability that renders them inherently difficult to regulate1,2. The operating principle relies on continuously adjusting the current through an electromagnet so that the resulting attractive force keeps a ferromagnetic ball at a prescribed height against gravity3. Departure from the target position, however small, triggers divergent dynamics unless a feedback mechanism intervenes in time, which places strict demands on both controller structure and tuning accuracy. As a result, magnetic ball suspension setups have become a standard vehicle for validating advanced control algorithms under genuinely challenging plant conditions4–7. Against this backdrop, the present study develops a sigmoid-activated two-degree-of-freedom proportional–integral–derivative (SA-2DOF-PID) controller whose parameters are systematically determined by the dholes-inspired optimization (DIO) algorithm8. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, no prior work has employed DIO for tuning a sigmoid-augmented 2DOF-PID controller on this particular benchmark. The architecture merges a smooth, bounded nonlinear preprocessing layer with an efficient population-based optimizer, simultaneously targeting robustness, noise attenuation, and transient quality. Through a carefully formulated cost function that penalizes both peak deviations and residual error accumulation, the tuned controller surpasses classical tuning approaches by a considerable margin.

Despite the diversity of available control paradigms, proportional–integral–derivative (PID) regulators continue to dominate industrial practice, largely because of their uncomplicated structure, straightforward commissioning, and adequate closed-loop behavior across a wide range of plants9–12. Their shortcomings become apparent, however, when the plant is as demanding as a magnetic levitation system: excessive transient overshoot, sluggish recovery, and poor attenuation of disturbances frequently accompany naive PID designs in this setting13. To overcome these deficiencies, a variety of enriched PID variants have been explored, among them fractional-order PID (FOPID)14,15, the two-degree-of-freedom PID (2DOF-PID), and higher-order derivative extensions such as real PID with second-order derivative (RPIDD²)16. Each variant broadens the design space, yet the associated parameter sets grow correspondingly more difficult to tune by conventional means17–19.

Over the past decade, metaheuristic search algorithms have emerged as highly competitive alternatives to gradient-based and manual procedures for controller parameter selection20–23. Drawing on analogies from physical processes or collective animal behavior, these methods explore high-dimensional, multimodal landscapes without needing derivative information and are thus well-suited to the non-convex tuning problems that arise in advanced PID design. Representative examples include particle swarm optimization (PSO)24, ant colony optimization (ACO)25 and teaching-learning-based optimization (TLBO)26, all of which have been successfully deployed on magnetic levitation control benchmarks, yielding measurable gains in both transient dynamics and long-run tracking precision. A persistent limitation across these algorithms, however, is their sensitivity to the exploration–exploitation trade-off: an imbalance in either direction risks premature stagnation at inferior solutions.

In this respect, the DIO algorithm stands out as a relatively recent addition to the metaheuristic toolbox, notable for its self-adjusting search intensity and reliable convergence profile. The algorithm abstracts the coordinated pack-hunting and vocal-communication behaviors of dholes, channeling population diversity during early iterations into focused exploitation as the search matures. Notwithstanding its promising theoretical properties, the potential of DIO in closed-loop control design—and for magnetic levitation regulation in particular—has so far remained largely unexamined.

Complementary to the selection of the optimization algorithm, embedding nonlinear elements directly into the controller structure provides an additional and independent mechanism for improving closed-loop performance. Sigmoid-type mappings, in particular, compress large error signals into bounded, smoothly varying outputs, thereby preventing abrupt control actions that could excite high-frequency resonances or destabilize sensitive plants27. Coupling such mappings with a PID skeleton typically yields tighter transient suppression and better noise immunity without materially complicating the overall architecture.

Drawing on these observations, the present paper proposes a Sigmoid-Activated Two-Degree-of-Freedom PID (SA-2DOF-PID) controller tailored to the stabilization of a magnetic ball suspension system. By marrying the dual-channel flexibility of the 2DOF-PID layout with a sigmoid-driven nonlinear corrective term, the design offers richer shaping of both reference-tracking and disturbance-rejection dynamics. Optimal selection of the resulting parameter set is entrusted to the DIO algorithm, whose adaptive vocalization-inspired update rules sustain a productive balance between global search and local refinement.

The central ambition of this work is to build a controller that retains architectural transparency while delivering superior dynamic and steady-state metrics under both idealized and perturbed plant conditions. Accordingly, the SA-2DOF-PID scheme is subjected to an extensive simulation-based evaluation and placed in direct competition with several optimization-tuned benchmarks drawn from the recent literature. The experimental evidence consistently shows that the proposed design achieves faster settling, curtailed overshoot, and greater immunity to parameter drift than its competitors.

Dholes-inspired optimization (DIO)

The DIO algorithm is a biologically based meta-heuristic optimization method developed by drawing inspiration from the advanced social organization, cooperative hunting behavior, and adaptive communication mechanisms of Asian wild dogs8,28,29. The intra-pack coordination and rapid adaptation to environmental conditions of dholes directly correspond to achieving the exploration-exploitation balance encountered in complex and high-dimensional optimization problems. At the heart of the algorithm lies the concept of a Lead Vocalizer, which directs the pack but is not dominant. This leader structure represents the best available solution in the search space and directs other candidate solutions to promising regions. Adaptively adjusting the leader’s influence throughout iterations allows the algorithm to perform large-scale exploration in the early stages and focus on local exploitation in later stages. This approach ensures the preservation of solution diversity while reducing the risk of early convergence.

The coordination achieved by dholes through vocal communication is modeled in the DIO algorithm through dynamic control parameters. This mechanism allows for the gradual reduction of the leader effect depending on the progress of the search process, enabling the population to exhibit more flexible search behavior. In addition, the cooperative hunting strategies of dholes are algorithmically represented by updating solution candidates according to both the leader position and the positions of other individuals. Finally, the territorial behavior of dholes is modeled with boundary constraints that ensure solutions remain within the defined search area. By combining these biological principles in a computational framework, DIO stands out as an algorithm that preserves solution diversity, effectively manages the balance between exploration and exploitation, and exhibits strong convergence properties in multimodal optimization problems.

Rooted in the rich social ecology of dholes, the DIO algorithm translates three key behavioral patterns—pack-wide vocal signaling, coordinated prey pursuit, and territory defense—into a rigorous computational search procedure capable of tackling high-complexity optimization tasks. Modeling of magnetic levitation system

Owing to the characteristics of magnetic fields, it is possible to lift and suspend objects with ferromagnetic properties without physical contact. Based on this principle, a magnetic levitation system controls the position of a ferromagnetic object by regulating the current flowing through an electromagnet via an input voltage \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$v\left(t\right)$$\end{document}. A schematic representation of this system is illustrated in Fig. 2. In such a system, the current supplied to the coil enables it to function as an electromagnetic actuator. The generated electromagnetic force can counterbalance the gravitational force acting on the ferromagnetic object, allowing the object to remain suspended in a stable position.

Nevertheless, controlling magnetic levitation systems is challenging because they inherently exhibit nonlinear and unstable dynamics. For this reason, the implementation of a feedback control mechanism is essential to ensure system stability. The proposed SA-2DOF-PID controller offers several advantages compared to conventional PID-based control strategies. The two-degree-of-freedom structure allows independent adjustment of the reference tracking and disturbance rejection characteristics of the system. In addition, the inclusion of the sigmoid-based nonlinear compensation term enhances the controller’s capability to handle nonlinearities inherent in the magnetic levitation system. This additional term contributes to smoother control action and improves the overall stability and transient performance of the system. Consequently, the proposed controller aims to achieve faster response, reduced overshoot, and improved robustness against system uncertainties and external disturbances.

Application of DIO to SA-2DOF-PID controlled system

The present section examines the application of the SA-2DOF-PID controller to the magnetic ball suspension plant, a well-established testbed in the control engineering literature that combines open-loop instability with pronounced nonlinear dynamics. The main objective of this implementation is to regulate the vertical position of the levitated ball by appropriately adjusting the control voltage supplied to the electromagnet. To achieve high-performance control, the parameters of the controller defined in Eq. (13) are tuned using the DIO algorithm. The parameter optimization process is based on a comprehensive fitness function (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${F}_{fit}$$\end{document}) structured in the following Eq. In this formulation, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${t}_{f}=5s$$\end{document} denotes the total simulation time, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M}_{p}$$\end{document} is the peak value of the system output y and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T}_{s}$$\end{document} represents the settling time, defined as the time required for the output response to enter and remain within a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 2\%$$\end{document} tolerance band around the reference value r (assumed to be 1 in normalized units). The instantaneous tracking error is defined as e=r-y. The proposed fitness function is structured to balance three important control objectives: limiting the overshoot, reducing the settling time, and minimizing the accumulated tracking error over the simulation interval. The first component, weighted by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{1}$$\end{document} and expressed as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{1}\cdot \left({M}_{p}-r\right)$$\end{document}, penalizes excessive peak values that exceed the desired steady-state reference level. The adopted weighting coefficients are selected as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{1}={\rho }_{2}=0.2$$\end{document}, providing a balanced emphasis on overshoot limitation, fast settling, and low overall error accumulation.

Pairing an adaptive cost function with the systematic tuning capacity of the DIO algorithm yields a coherent and practically oriented design pathway for the SA-2DOF-PID controller. Together, these two components allow the closed-loop system to sustain reliable, high-quality operation when confronted with the nonlinear dynamics characteristic of magnetic levitation plants. The underlying design rationale is straightforward: a sigmoid-based preprocessing stage is introduced ahead of the conventional PID paths to ensure that the generated control signal remains smooth and confined within a bounded range, thereby avoiding the sharp actuation jumps that would otherwise risk destabilizing the suspended ball. Beyond this, the inherent saturation property of the sigmoid mapping confers a degree of inherent immunity to sensor noise and suppresses the tendency toward oscillatory behavior in the closed-loop response.

The DIO algorithm is employed to determine the optimal controller parameters, allowing the control structure to adapt effectively to parameter variations and external disturbances. Through this optimization-based tuning approach, the controller can maintain stable performance under changing operating conditions. Consequently, the proposed framework addresses several limitations commonly encountered in traditional PID, FOPID, and RPIDD² based controllers. Conclusion

This paper has presented a sigmoid-activated two-degree-of-freedom PID (SA-2DOF-PID) controller for position regulation of a magnetic ball suspension system, where a sigmoid-driven nonlinear compensation branch is embedded within the conventional 2DOF-PID skeleton to improve actuation smoothness and closed-loop robustness. All free parameters of the controller were determined through the DIO algorithm, whose population-based search balances broad exploration against focused exploitation throughout the iterative process. The design was subjected to a thorough simulation campaign covering both nominal plant conditions and deliberate parametric perturbations. Across every tested scenario, the SA-2DOF-PID tuned by DIO returned notably shorter settling durations, sharply curtailed peak overshoot, and negligible steady-state error relative to counterpart controllers calibrated with SOA, HBA, and PSO. Statistical repeatability tests further revealed that DIO converges to competitive fitness values more consistently than the competing optimizers, exhibiting tight inter-run dispersion. When the winding resistance and inductance were each varied by ± 10% from their nominal values, the proposed controller retained stable, well-damped transients with only marginal degradation, whereas alternative approaches showed appreciably larger overshoots and prolonged recovery times. Head-to-head comparisons with WOA-SA-tuned PID, ObAEF-tuned FOPID, and MRFO-tuned RPIDD² confirmed that the proposed method achieves the best overall settling speed, damping quality, and tracking precision across the full set of test cases.

Disturbance rejection tests showed that the regulated output recovers swiftly from externally induced deviations without exciting sustained oscillations, affirming the practical utility of the proposed framework in environments where load variations and unexpected inputs are unavoidable. These collective findings underscore the capability of the DIO-tuned SA-2DOF-PID to handle real-world control challenges that go beyond the idealized simulation setting. A notable constraint of the current work is its reliance on a linearized plant model, which accurately captures small-signal dynamics around the nominal operating point but cannot fully represent large-amplitude nonlinearities or actuator saturation. Prospective extensions therefore include validation on the complete nonlinear model, experimental deployment on physical hardware, and exploration of hybrid or alternative nonlinear activation functions that could yield further transient improvements. On balance, the DIO-based SA-2DOF-PID offers a well-rounded control solution—structurally compact, computationally affordable, and demonstrably robust—for the class of nonlinear, open-loop unstable systems exemplified by magnetic levitation platforms.
