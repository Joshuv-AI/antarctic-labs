Degenerate 3-evolution equations in Gevrey classes
Alexandre Arias Junior, Alessia Ascanelli

Abstract
. We consider the Cauchy problem for third-order evolution differential operators with variable coefficients, depending on time t ∈ [ 0 , T ] t\in[0,T] and space x ∈ ℝ x\in\mathbb{R} , where the leading coefficient a 3 ​ ( t ) a_{3}(t) vanishes at t = 0 t=0 with finite order. 1.
Introduction and state of the art

In this paper we deal with the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}P(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})u(t,x)=f(t,x)&(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\cr u(0,x)=g(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

for the operator

(1.2)

\displaystyle P=D_{t}+a_{3}(t)D^{3}_{x}+a_{2}(t,x)D^{2}_{x}+a_{1}(t,x)D_{x}+a_{0}(t,x),

with
a_{3}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R})

a_{j}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})),j=0,1,2
, and
\mathcal{B}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of complex-valued functions bounded on
\mathbb{R}
together with all their derivatives. The operator
P
is known in the literature as a
3-
evolution operator with real characteristics
, cf.
[
17
]
; indeed, the principal symbol (in the Petrowski sense)
p_{3}(t,\tau,\xi)=\tau+a_{3}(t)\xi^{3}
admits the real characteristic root
\tau=-a_{3}(t)\xi^{3}
. The assumption
a_{3}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
agrees with the necessary condition
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits a_{3}(t)\leq 0\ \forall t\in[0,T]
for well-posedness in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{R}}H^{m}(\mathbb{R})
given by
[
17
, Theorem 3, page 31]
. The fact that the lower order terms
a_{j}
are complex-valued makes the problem challenging.

In this paper we are mainly interested in the case when the leading term
a_{3}(t)
of the equation may vanish at some
t\in[0,T]
, that is, the equation degenerates and the behavior of the lower order terms
a_{j}(t,x),\ j=1,2,
determines the space where the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well-posed: as we will see, we can have no
H^{\infty}
well-posedness because of a degeneration with respect to
t
, or because of the behaviour at
|x|\to\infty
of the coefficients, or both. In such cases, we shall seek well-posedness results in the Gevrey-type space

{H}^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}):=\bigcup_{\rho>0}H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R}),

where, given
\theta\geq 1,m,\rho\in\mathbb{R},

H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Gevrey-Sobolev space

H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\{u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\langle D\rangle^{m}e^{\rho\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}

and
\langle D\rangle^{m}
,
e^{\rho\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}
denote the Fourier multipliers with symbols
\langle\xi\rangle^{m}
and
e^{\rho\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}
respectively. These spaces are Hilbert spaces with inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}}=\,\langle\langle D\rangle^{m}e^{\rho\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}u,\langle D\rangle^{m}e^{\rho\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}v\rangle_{L^{2}},\quad u,v\in H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R}).

The relation between
H^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
and the Gevrey classes is given by
G_{0}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\subset{H}^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\subset G^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}),
where
G^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of all smooth functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.3)

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h^{-|\alpha|}\alpha!^{-\theta}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|<+\infty

for some
h>0
, and
G_{0}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all compactly supported functions contained in
G^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
. Notice that
H^{m}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R})
recaptures the the Sobolev space
H^{m}(\mathbb{R})
when
\rho=0
.

If
a_{3}(t)
never vanishes, i.e., under the assumption
a_{3}(t)\geq C>0
(or
a_{3}(t)\leq-C<0
)
\forall t\in[0,T]
, the
H^{\infty}
theory for the class of operators (
1.2
) is well-estabilished: necessary and sufficient conditions for

well-posedness have been given, respectively, in
[
7
]
(where a more general class of
p
-evolution equations is considered),
[
9
]
and
[

. Roughly speaking, these conditions state that if the coefficient
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits a_{2}
decays as

In this second situation, necessary and sufficient decay conditions for well-posedness in

with
\theta>1
of (
1.1
) have been recently stated, again under the assumption
a(t)_{3}\geq C>0

\forall t\in[0,T]
. On the one hand, from
[

for some

and the Cauchy problem (

\Xi:=\max\{2(1-\sigma_{2}),2(1-\sigma_{1})-1\}\leq\frac{1}{\theta}.

Testing this condition on the model operator

,

Coming now to the case where

The problem of a vanishing leading term in
p
-evolution equations has been considered at the moment only in the case of Schrödinger type equations, that is,

\sigma,c,C>0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\ell},\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits a_{1}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq

in space (

Main result

Let us consider the Cauchy problem (

\displaystyle c_{a_{3}}t^{\ell}\leq|a_{3}(t)|

\displaystyle C_{a_{3}}t^{\ell},\quad 0<c_{a_{3}}\leq C_{a_{3}},

\displaystyle t^{k}C^{\beta+1}_{a_{2}}\beta!\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma_{2}},

(2.6)

We are mainly interested in the "effective" degeneracy in time, that is

Before stating the main result of this paper, we define the two following numbers depending on the vanishing order

and the decay rates

(2.8)

\displaystyle q_{2}=q_{2}(\ell,k,\sigma_{2})=\begin{cases}2\frac{(\ell-k)\sigma_{2}+(k+1)(1-\sigma_{2})}{\sigma_{2}(\ell-k)+(k+1)}=2\left(1-\frac{\sigma_{2}(k+1)}{\sigma_{2}(\ell-k)+(k+1)}\right),\quad\sigma_{2}\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\
\frac{2(\ell-k)}{\ell+1},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\sigma_{2}\geq 1,\end{cases}

(2.9)

\displaystyle q_{1}=q_{1}(\ell,k^{\prime},\sigma_{1})=\begin{cases}\frac{(k^{\prime}+1)(1-2\sigma_{1})+\sigma_{1}(\ell-k^{\prime})}{\sigma_{1}(\ell-k^{\prime})+(k^{\prime}+1)}=1-\frac{2\sigma_{1}(k^{\prime}+1)}{\sigma_{1}(\ell-k^{\prime})+(k^{\prime}+1)},\quad\sigma_{1}\in(0,1),\\
\frac{\ell-2k^{\prime}-1}{\ell+1},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\,\sigma_{1}\geq 1.\end{cases}

We then set

3.
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to give some definitions and results that we are going to use throughout the text. We remark that all the results stated in this section are valid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, however we state them with

3.1.
Pseudodifferential operators of finite order

As usual, for any
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
stands for the space of all functions
p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

(3.14)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\partial^{\beta}_{x}p(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\alpha}.

The topology in
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is induced by the following family of seminorms

|p|^{(m)}_{\ell}:=\max_{\alpha\leq\ell,\beta\leq\ell}\sup_{x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\partial^{\beta}_{x}p(x,\xi)|\langle\xi\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|},\quad p\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}),\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We associate to every symbol
p\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the continuous operator
p(x,D):\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
(Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions), known as pseudodifferential operator, defined by

p(x,D)u(x)=\int e^{i\xi x}p(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)d\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\scriptstyle-\!$}}\xi,\quad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\hat{u}
denotes the Fourier transform of
u
and
d\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\scriptstyle-\!$}}\xi=(2\pi)^{-1}d\xi
. We will often denote the operator
p(x,D)
by
op(p(x,\xi))
referring to its symbol. The next result gives the action of operators coming from symbols
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
in the standard sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}

3.2.
Pseudodifferential operators of infinite order

In this subsection we will recall the main results concerning pseudodifferential operators of infinite order. First we define the Gelfand-Shilov spaces and then we will pass to the classes of symbols. For the proofs of the results here stated we refer to Chapter
2
of
[
1
]
and to Appendix A of
[
8
]

The Gelfand-Shilov spaces have been originally introduced in the book
[
12
]
, making part of larger class called spaces of type
\mathcal{S}
. More to the point, given
\theta\geq 1
and
A,B>0
we say that a smooth function
f
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{\theta,A}_{\theta,B}(\mathbb{R})
if there is a constant
C>0
such that

|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}f(x)|\leq CA^{\alpha}B^{\beta}\alpha!^{\theta}\beta!^{\theta},

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The norm

\|f\|_{\theta,A,B}\,=\sup_{\overset{x\in\mathbb{R}}{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}}|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}f(x)|A^{-\alpha}B^{-\beta}\alpha!^{-\theta}\beta!^{-\theta}

turns
\mathcal{S}^{\theta,A}_{s,B}(\mathbb{R})
into a Banach space. We define

\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\bigcup_{A,B>0}\mathcal{S}^{\theta,A}_{s,B}(\mathbb{R})

and we can equip it with the inductive limit topology of the Banach spaces
\mathcal{S}^{\theta,A}_{\theta,B}(\mathbb{R})
. We also consider the projective version, that is

\Sigma_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\bigcap_{A,B>0}\mathcal{S}^{\theta,A}_{\theta,B}(\mathbb{R})

equipped with the projective limit topology. The following inclusions are continuous (for every
\varepsilon>0
)

\Sigma_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\subset\Sigma_{\theta+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}).

Concerning the action of the Fourier transform we have the following isomorphisms

\mathcal{F}:\Sigma_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\to\Sigma_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}).

Now we pass to the symbol classes of finite and infinite order with Gevrey regular symbols.

The order of

e^{k(t)\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}
; this means that, looking into the asymptotic expansion of

P_{k,\Lambda}:=e^{k(t)\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}\circ e^{\Lambda}\circ P\circ(e^{\Lambda})^{-1}\circ e^{-k(t)\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}

(that contains the term

coming out from
e^{k(t)\langle D\rangle_{h}^{1/\theta}}\circ D_{t}\circ e^{-k(t)\langle D\rangle_{h}^{1/\theta}}
) we will be able to absorb errors of order
q
in the estimates. The function
k(t)
will be of the form

(4.18)

k(t)=\rho_{0}(T+1-t),

for some fixed
\rho_{0}>0
.

The main role of this change of variable is to make the transformed Cauchy problem

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}P_{k,\Lambda}(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})v(t,x)=f_{k,\Lambda}(t,x)&(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\cr v(0,x)=g_{k,\Lambda}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

with

f_{k,\Lambda}=e^{k(t)\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}e^{\Lambda}f,\quad g_{k,\Lambda}=e^{k(t)\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}e^{\Lambda}g,

well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
If we prove well-posedness in
L^{2}
for problem (
4.19
), then we immediately obtain for (
1.1
) the well-posedness in
H^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
for
\theta\in(1,\frac{1}{q})
by the inversion of formula (
4.17
). Indeed, if in (
1.1
) we take
f,g\in H^{0}_{\rho;\theta}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\rho>0
, in view of Theorem
3.22
(since
q<\frac{1}{\theta}
) we have

f_{k,\Lambda},g_{k,\Lambda}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

as long as we choose
\rho_{0}<\frac{\rho}{T+1}
.
Hence there is a unique
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
solution to (
4.19
). The inverse of the operator
e^{k(t)\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}e^{\Lambda}
has the following shape

\{e^{k(t)\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}e^{\Lambda}\}^{-1}=(e^{\Lambda})^{-1}e^{-k(t)\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}}.

It turns out (see the upcoming Lemma
4.2
) that
(e^{\Lambda})^{-1}=\,^{R}e^{-\Lambda}\circ r
, for some zero order operator

. In this way, again in view of Theorem
3.22
, we have that

, which is the solution to (
1.1
), belongs to
H^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore (
1.1
) is
H^{\infty}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R})
well-posed for all
\theta\in(1,\frac{1}{q})
.

Remark 4.1

4.2.
Invertibility of the operator
e^{\Lambda}

Since
\Lambda(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{q,0}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by Proposition
3.21
we have
e^{\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}\in SG^{\infty,0}_{\mu;\frac{1}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The calculus for this class of symbols (see Section
3
) is defined for
1/q>1
. This is the reason for the restriction (
2.11
) in Theorem
2.2
, see also Remark
2.1
.

Since
\Lambda(t,x,\xi)
is real-valued the reverse operator
{}^{R}e^{-\Lambda}
coincides with the
L^{2}
-adjoint of
e^{-\Lambda}
and by Theorem
3.17
we have

{}^{R}e^{-{\Lambda}}(t,x,D)=a(t,x,D)+r(t,x,D),

\displaystyle a(t,x,\xi)\sim\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}D^{\alpha}_{x}e^{-{\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}}\ {\rm in}\ FSG^{\infty,0}_{\mu;1/q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;r\in\mathcal{S}_{2\mu-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Therefore (again by Theorem
3.17
) we get

\displaystyle(e^{\Lambda}\circ^{R}e^{-\Lambda})(t,x,D)=q(t,x,D)+r_{\infty}(t,x,D),

where
r_{\infty}(t,x,\xi)\in\mathcal{S}_{2\mu-1}
and

q(t,x,\xi)\sim\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\{e^{\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}D^{\alpha}_{x}e^{-\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}\}\quad\text{in}\quad FSG^{\infty,0}_{\mu;\frac{1}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Standard computations give that

|\partial^{\gamma+\alpha}_{\xi}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\{e^{\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}D^{\alpha}_{x}e^{-\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}\}|\leq C^{\gamma+\beta+2\alpha+1}(\gamma!\beta!\alpha!^{2})^{\mu}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(1-q)\alpha-\gamma}_{h}\langle x\rangle^{-\beta-\alpha}.

In this way we obtain that
q(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{0,0}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

(e^{\Lambda}\circ^{R}e^{-\Lambda})(t,x,D)=q(t,x,D)+r_{\infty}(t,x,D)=1+r(t,x,D)+r_{\infty}(t,x,D),

where
r(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{-(1-q),-1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
r_{\infty}(t,x,\xi)\in\mathcal{S}_{2\mu-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Setting
\bar{r}=r+r_{\infty}
we get

(4.50)

\displaystyle(e^{\Lambda}\circ^{R}e^{-\Lambda})(t,x,D)=1+\bar{r}(t,x,D),

and the
0-
seminorms of the symbol
\bar{r}
can be estimated by

(4.51)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\partial^{\beta}_{x}\bar{r}(t,x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(1-q)-\alpha}_{h}\leq h^{-(1-q)}C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha}_{h}.

Calderon-Vaillancourt theorem then implies that the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
operator norm of
\bar{r}
is strictly less than
1
, provided that
h\geq h_{0}
for some
h_{0}
depending on a finite number of the constants
C_{\alpha,\beta}
appearing in (
4.51
). Therefore, the operator
1+\bar{r}
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and we have

(1+\bar{r})\circ\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{r})^{j}=1.

Notice that since the constants
C_{\alpha,\beta}
depend on the constants
M_{2}
and
M_{1}
, then
h_{0}
depends on
M_{2}
and
M_{1}
as well. Finally we get by (
4.50
)

e^{\Lambda}\circ^{R}e^{-\Lambda}\circ\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{r})^{j}=1.

Using Theorem
9
in
[
2
]
we obtain that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{r})^{j}
is an operator given by a symbol of the form
q_{1}+\bar{r}_{\infty}
, where
q_{1}(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{0,0}_{\mu}
and
\bar{r}_{\infty}(t,x,\xi)\in\Sigma_{\kappa}
for every
\kappa>2\mu-1
. We have so proved the following lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu>1
. There is
h_{0}=h_{0}(M_{2},M_{1})
such that for all
h\geq h_{0}
, the operator
e^{\Lambda}(t,x,D)
is invertible
and its inverse is given by

\{e^{\Lambda}(t,x,D)\}^{-1}=\thinspace^{R}\{e^{-\Lambda}\}(t,x,D)\circ\sum_{j\geq 0}(-\overline{r}(t,x,D))^{j},

for some
\bar{r}=r+r_{\infty}
, where
r(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{-(1-q),-1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
r_{\infty}(t,x,\xi)\in\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for every

and

r(t,x,\xi)-\sum_{1\leq\gamma\leq N}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\partial^{\gamma}_{\xi}\{e^{\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}D^{\gamma}_{x}e^{-\Lambda(t,x,\xi)}\}\in SG^{-(N+1)(1-q),-N-1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\forall N\geq 1.

Moreover,
\sum(-\overline{r}(t,x,D))^{j}
has symbol in
SG^{0,0}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})+\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for every
\kappa>2\mu-1
. Finally, we have

(4.52)

\{e^{\Lambda}(t,x,D)\}^{-1}=\thinspace^{R}\{e^{-\Lambda}\}(t,x,D)\circ\textrm{op}(1-i\partial_{\xi}\partial_{x}{\Lambda}+q_{-2}+r_{\infty}),

where
q_{-2}(t,x,\xi)\in SG^{-2(1-q),-2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
r_{\infty}(t,x,\xi)\in\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for all
\kappa>2\mu-1

4.3.
Conjugation

This section is devoted to compute the conjugation

P_{\Lambda}=(e^{\Lambda})\circ P\circ(e^{\Lambda})^{-1}.

The coefficients of the operator
P
belong to the class
S^{j}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for
j=0,1,2,3
, so we start by computing
(e^{\Lambda})p(e^{\Lambda})^{-1}
where
p(t,x,\xi)\in S^{m}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the inverse of
e^{\Lambda}
contains the reverse operator we first calculate

e^{\Lambda}(t,x,D)\circ p(t,x,D)\circ\,^{R}e^{-\Lambda}(t,x,D)=q(t,x,D)+r_{\infty}(t,x,D),

Standard computations at symbols level give

|\partial^{\alpha+\gamma}_{\xi}\partial^{\lambda}_{x}\{\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}e^{\Lambda}D^{\beta}_{x}pD^{\alpha}_{x}e^{-\Lambda}\}|\leq C^{\gamma+\lambda+2(\alpha+\beta)+1}(\gamma!\lambda!\alpha!^{2}\beta!^{2})^{\mu}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-(1-q)(\alpha+\beta)-\gamma}_{h}.

and

where

and

,

4.54

4.5.
Lower bound estimates and energy estimate

In this section we shall use sharp-Gårding and Fefferman-Phong inequalities in order to get a priori energy estimates for the Cauchy problem (
4.19
). We first write

\displaystyle iP_{k,\Lambda}(t,x,D)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}+\rho_{0}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}-\partial_{t}\Lambda_{e}(t,D)-\partial_{t}\Lambda_{\psi}(t,D)

\displaystyle+ia_{3}(t)D^{3}_{x}-a_{3}(t)op(3\xi^{2}\partial_{x}\Lambda)

\displaystyle+ia_{2}(t,x)D^{2}_{x}+ir_{2}(t,x,D)

\displaystyle+ia_{1}(t,x)D_{x}+ir_{1}(t,x,D)+r_{0}(t,x,D).

Since we want to apply Fefferman-Phong inequality at level 2, we split the imaginary part of the terms of order
2
into its hermitian and anti-hermitian parts:

and we rewrite the operator

as

\displaystyle iA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iA+(iA)^{*}}{2}+\frac{iA-(iA)^{*}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}op\left(\sum_{\gamma\geq 1}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\partial^{\gamma}_{\xi}D^{\gamma}_{x}\overline{iA}\right)+A_{2}+r

for all

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}+\rho_{0}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{\theta}}_{h}-\partial_{t}\Lambda_{e}(t,D)-\partial_{t}\Lambda_{\psi}(t,D)

\displaystyle+ia_{3}(t)D^{3}_{x}+A_{2}(t,x,D)

\displaystyle-Im\,a_{2}(t,x)D_{x}^{2}-Im\,r_{2}(t,x,D)+\tilde{r}_{2}(t,x,D)-a_{3}(t)op(3\xi^{2}\partial_{x}\Lambda)
