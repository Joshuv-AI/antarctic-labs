Equivalence Checking of Quantum Circuits via Path-Sum and Weighted Model Counting
Wei-Jia Huang, Christophe Chareton, Yu-Fang Chen, Kai-Min Chung, Min-Hsiu Hsieh, Alfons Laarman, Jingyi Mei

Abstract
Equivalence checking of quantum circuits is a central verification task in quantum computing, ensuring the correctness of circuit optimizations, hardware mappings, and compilation pipelines. Among the primary symbolic methods for this purpose, the path-sum formalism provides a compact representation with powerful reduction rules that yield a canonical form for the classically simulable Clifford fragment, but confluence fails beyond the Clifford fragment. We introduce a new weighted model counting (WMC) encoding for path-sums and combine it with the existing path-sum reductions to obtain a verifier that is both complete and efficient. Our method applies reductions whenever possible and invokes the WMC-based decision procedure on the residual path-sum, yielding a complete semantic check up to a global phase. We implement the approach and evaluate it on standard benchmarks. Results show that the hybrid method outperforms either component in isolation and competes with state-of-the-art tools.

Quantum computation stores and processes information in systems governed by quantum mechanics

Quantum circuits are typically executed on a coprocessor architecture: a classical host emits instructions that initialize a quantum register and apply these gates.

Before giving the corresponding WMC example, we recall the definition of weighted model counting.

Definition 1
(Weighted Model Counting)

Let
\Gamma
be a Boolean formula over variables
V

W:V\times\{0,1\}\to\mathbb{A}
be a weight function that assigns
to each literal
(v,b)
a weight

in some commutative semiring

The

\mathit{WMC}(\Gamma,W)\;=\;\sum_{\tau\models\Gamma}\;\prod_{v\in V}W\!\big(v,\tau(v)\big),

where the sum ranges over all satisfying assignments

Example 2
(Reducing Path-Sum Reasoning to Weighted Model Counting)

Rather than applying rewrite rules, we encode the problem of checking whether the path-sum for

The weight function is defined as follows. For Boolean variable

W(v,b)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\in\{x,y,z\}$},\\[4.0pt]
(-1)^{b}&\text{if $v\in\{s_{1},s_{2}\}$}.\end{cases}

Below, we explain the idea behind the encoding

1

Finally, the constraint

A one-

The dynamics of a quantum state are governed by

.
A unitary

is a linear map on
\mathcal{H}
with
U^{\dagger}U=I
, where

denotes the

Elementary quantum

is a finite sequence of gates; each gate is a unitary acting on one or more qubits, and the whole circuit denotes a unitary matrix

.
Parallel composition of two circuits corresponds to their

and sequential composition to their

.
Two circuits

, where

for some real
\theta
.

Equivalently, to check if two circuits
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are equivalent, it suffices to verify that
U[C_{2}]U[C_{1}]^{\dagger}=I
. They are equivalent up to global phase iff
U[C_{2}]U[C_{1}]^{\dagger}=e^{i\theta}I
for some real
\theta
.

3
Technical Contribution

Our goal is to verify the equivalence of two
n
-qubit circuits
C_{1},C_{2}
.
Due to reversibility of the circuits,
this reduces to checking whether

C:=C_{1}C_{2}^{\dagger}

is equal to
I
(exact equivalence) or to
e^{i\theta}I
for some real
\theta
(equivalence up to global phase).
We construct the path-sum
PS(C)
and then proceed in two steps:

1.

apply path-sum reductions (Fig.
1
) to simplify
PS(C)
, and

2.

encode the residual instance as a weighted model counting (WMC) problem and decide it exactly.

Step 1 largely follows the standard path-sum calculus, but requires some refinements.
In particular, reduction rules depend on recognizing syntactic patterns in path-sum formulae, yet many equivalent expressions do not match these patterns directly.
In practical use, one needs to add additional rules to reorganize the phase polynomials and Boolean output functions to enable pattern matching.
We also slightly relaxed the constraints for
\mathsf{Rz}(\theta)
and
\mathsf{CRz}(\theta)
gate constructions to permit arbitrary rotation angles.
These relaxations integrate smoothly with the path-sum formalism of
[
4
]
, but, as expected, the reduction rules cannot handle interference on arbitrary angles and therefore remain incomplete.
This motivates Step 2, where we derive a sound and complete equivalence-checking procedure for path-sum formulae through a reduction to WMC.

(\frac{1}{2}y_{0}(y_{1}+z)+\frac{1}{2}x(y_{1}+z))\cdot 2\pi
,
where
Q=z
,
R=\frac{1}{2}x(y_{1}+z)
,
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=(x,y_{1})
.
So
y_{0}\notin var(Q)\cup var(R)\cup var(\mathcal{O})
,
the phase polynomial is then reduced to be
\left\langle\emptyset,R[y_{1}\leftarrow\bar{Q}]\cdot 2\pi,\mathcal{O}[y_{1}\leftarrow\bar{Q}]\right\rangle
,
where
R[y_{1}\leftarrow\bar{Q}]\cdot 2\pi=\frac{1}{2}x(z+z)\cdot 2\pi\equiv 0\quad(\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})[y_{1}\leftarrow z]=|x,z\rangle
.

Thus we have the reduction:
Equation
(\ref{eq:ps_after})\xrightarrow{[\mathsf{H}\mathsf{H}]}\left\langle\emptyset,0,|x,z\rangle\right\rangle
.∎

In
[
4
]
, completeness is established only for the Clifford fragment of the calculus. To extend it to general quantum circuits, we reduce the problem to weighted model counting (WMC).

3.1
Encoding path-sum equivalence into WMC

We reduce equivalence to a check on diagonal amplitudes. For

{C}=C_{2}C_{1}^{\dagger}
and basis
|\mathbf{x}\rangle
, we assume
PS({C})
is

|\mathbf{x}\rangle~\mapsto~\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{|\mathbf{y}|}}}\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathbf{y}|}}e^{i\,\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}|\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\rangle_{.}

and slightly abuse notation and write
PS(C)
also for its
matrix representation
. Formally, it defines the matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{|\mathbf{y}|}}}\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathbf{y}|}}e^{i\,\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}|\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{x}\right|
.

Because
PS(C)
is unitary, for each basis state
|\mathbf{x}\rangle
, the output
PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle
has
L_{2}
-norm equals 1.
Therefore
PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=|\mathbf{x}\rangle
iff its
diagonal amplitude
satisfies

\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=1
.
For equivalence up to a global phase, it suffices to require

\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=e^{i\theta}

Lemma 1

PS(C)
is the identity if and only if,
for every basis state
|\mathbf{x}\rangle
, its diagonal amplitude satisfies

\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=1.

Corollary 1

Let
PS(C)
act on
n
qubits.
Then
PS(C)
is equivalent to identity up to global phase if and only if

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}\,|\,PS(C)\,|\,\mathbf{x}\rangle\;=\;2^{n}\cdot e^{i\theta}\text{ for a given }\theta.

Proof

The “only if” direction is immediate: if
PS(C)=e^{i\theta}\cdot I
, for every
|\mathbf{x}\rangle
,

\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=e^{i\theta}\cdot\langle\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{x}\rangle=e^{i\theta}
, thus
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}\,|\,PS(C)\,|\,\mathbf{x}\rangle\;=\;2^{n}\cdot e^{i\theta}
.

For the “if” direction, suppose
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=2^{n}\cdot e^{i\theta}
.
Since
PS(C)
is unitary, for each basis state
\mathbf{x}
we have

|\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle|\leq 1
. Therefore,

2^{n}=\Big|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle\Big|\;\leq\;\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\big|\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle\big|\;\leq\;\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}1=2^{n}.

Since the first and last terms of the chain are equal, both inequalities must in fact be equalities, Thus
|\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle|=1
for every
\mathbf{x}
and since the modulus of total sum is the maximum
2^{n}
,
that global phase must be the same, i.e.
\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=c
for all
\mathbf{x}
.
So
\langle\mathbf{x}|e^{-i\theta}\cdot PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=1
.
Based on Lemma
1
,
we conclude
e^{-i\theta}\cdot PS(C)=I
thus
PS(C)=e^{i\theta}\cdot I
.∎

Next we show how to use WMC to compute the sum of the diagonal values
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}\,|\,PS(C)\,|\,\mathbf{x}\rangle
.
For the encoding, we first rewrite the
phase polynomial

\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
of
PS(C)
to a finite sum of monomial terms

\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{j\in J}\texttt{Mon}_{j},\qquad\texttt{Mon}_{j}=k_{j}\cdot\texttt{bc}_{j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Here
J
is the set of monomial index, each
\texttt{Mon}_{j}
is the product of a real coefficient
k_{j}

and a Boolean condition
\texttt{bc}_{j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.
The Boolean condition indicates
when
this monomial contributes:
it evaluates to
1
if the assignment
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
activates the term,
and
0
otherwise.

Example 5

Considering the path-sum in Equation (
5
) of Example
4
:

PS(C_{2};C_{1}^{\dagger})=\left\langle\{y_{0},y_{1}\},{(xz+zy_{0}+{(x+y_{0}-2xy_{0})}y_{1})\cdot\pi},|x,y_{1}\rangle\right\rangle

Here, the phase term can be written as
{(y_{0}y_{1}+y_{0}z+xy_{1}+xz-2xy_{0}y_{1})\cdot\pi}
, which expands as a monomials sum of 5 monomial terms, with the following definition and constraints:

\begin{array}[]{rcl|rcccl|rcccl}\lx@intercol\hfil\textbf{Monomial definition}\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol&\lx@intercol\hfil\textbf{Boolean constraint}\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol&\lx@intercol\hfil\textbf{Real Coefficient}\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\texttt{Mon}_{1}&:=&{y_{0}y_{1}\pi}&\texttt{bc}_{1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})&:=&y_{0}\wedge y_{1}&&&k_{1}&:=&\pi\\
\texttt{Mon}_{2}&:=&y_{0}z\pi&\texttt{bc}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})&:=&y_{0}\wedge z&&&k_{2}&:=&\pi\\
\texttt{Mon}_{3}&:=&xy_{1}\pi&\texttt{bc}_{3}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})&:=&x\wedge y_{1}&&&k_{3}&:=&\pi\\
\texttt{Mon}_{4}&:=&{xz\pi}&\texttt{bc}_{4}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})&:=&x\wedge z&&&k_{4}&:=&\pi\\
\texttt{Mon}_{5}&:=&{-2xy_{0}y_{1}\pi}&\texttt{bc}_{5}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})&:=&x\wedge y_{0}\wedge y_{1}&&&k_{5}&:=&-2\pi\end{array}

∎

To bring this into WMC form, we proceed monomial by monomial.
For each
j\in J
we introduce a fresh Boolean variable
s_{j}
,
referred to as a
monomial variable
, together with a constraint

\gamma_{j}:\quad s_{j}\leftrightarrow\texttt{bc}_{j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Thus,
s_{j}
explicitly represents whether the Boolean condition

\texttt{bc}_{j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
holds.
We then attach to
s_{j}
a literal weight function

W(s_{j},0)=1,\qquad W(s_{j},1)=e^{ik_{j}}.

Intuitively, if the condition is false, the term contributes nothing, while if it is true, we multiply by the corresponding root of unity. We set the weight of all other literals to 1, as they are irrelevant to the sum of diagonal amplitude values.

From

PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle_{=}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m}}}\sum_{\mathbf{y}}e^{2\pi i\,\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}|\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\rangle_{,}

we obtain

\langle\mathbf{x}|PS(C)|\mathbf{x}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m}}}\sum_{\mathbf{y}}e^{2\pi i\,\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\langle\mathbf{x}|\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m}}}\sum_{\mathbf{y}:\,\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{x}}e^{2\pi i\,\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}.

Here, as observed in
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
,
we have that by orthonormality

\langle\mathbf{x}|\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\rangle

equals the Kronecker delta
\delta_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\,\mathbf{x}}
, equals
1
if
x=O(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and
0
otherwise.
Hence, in the WMC encoding, we include the formula

\beta:\;\;\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})_{i}\leftrightarrow x_{i}\big),

so that only diagonal paths are counted.
Finally, we form the global Boolean constraint

\Gamma:=\beta\wedge\ \bigwedge_{j\in J}\gamma_{j},

which enforces both the path restriction and the link between each
s_{j}

and its underlying condition.

With this construction, the sum of diagonal amplitude is obtained as

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{x}\,|\,PS(C)\,|\,\mathbf{x}\rangle\;=\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m}}}\,\mathrm{WMC}(\Gamma,W),

where
\mathrm{WMC}(\Gamma,W)
denotes the weighted model count of
\Gamma

under the literal weights
W
.

5
can be encoded as a weighted

5
:

\Gamma\;=\;\bigwedge_{j=1}^{5}\!\bigl(s_{j}\leftrightarrow\texttt{bc}_{j}\bigr)\;\wedge\;\bigl(y_{1}\leftrightarrow z\bigr).

Since
k_{j}=\pi
for all
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
and
k_{5}=-2\pi
, the weight function
W
is

W(s_{j},0)=1,\qquad W(s_{j},1)=(-1)^{\delta(j,5)}

(where shaded rows are the satisfying assignments), we obtain

\mathrm{WMC}(\Gamma,W)\;=\;\sum_{\tau\models\Gamma}\;\prod_{j=1}^{5}W\!\bigl(s_{j},\tau(s_{j})\bigr)\;=\;8.

Since
m=2
and
n=2
,

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{2}}\bigl\langle\mathbf{x}\,\big|\,PS(C)\,\big|\,\mathbf{x}\bigr\rangle\;=\;4\;=\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\,\mathrm{WMC}(\Gamma,W).

Therefore, the circuit
C_{1}
is equivalent to the circuit
C_{2}
. Note that WMC solvers normally do not perform such exhaustive enumeration, we include it here to help the reader verify the correctness of the results.

Comparison of Operation Modes.

Our tool is publicly available under the MIT license. All experiments were conducted on an Azure Standard E8ads v5 server (8 vCPUs, 64 GiB memory).

The
QuPRS
tool supports three operation modes. Assume that we are checking the equivalence of two circuits
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, all modes first construct the path-sum formula of
C_{2}(C_{1})^{\dagger}
:

•

RR (Reduction-rules)
Applies only rule-based reductions during the path-sum formula construction. If the resulting formula differs from that of an identity matrix, the tool reports “unknown.”

•

WMC (Weighted Model Counting)
Constructs the path-sum formula without applying any reduction rules. It uses weighted model counting to determine if the final path-sum formula corresponds to the identity.

•

Hybrid
Initially applies reduction rules to simplify the formula. If the resulting formula is not syntactically identical to that of the identity, weighted model counting is employed to check for semantic equivalence.

6
Conclusion

We presented a hybrid verifier for quantum circuit equivalence that combines path-sum reductions with a new weighted model counting (WMC) encoding. The reductions aggressively simplify circuits when algebraic structure is present, and the WMC back end provides a complete semantic check up to global phase on the residual path-sum. We implemented the approach in
QuPRS
, which supports reduction-only, WMC-only, and hybrid modes.
On standard benchmarks, the hybrid mode consistently outperforms either component alone and is competitive with state-of-the-art tools. It is especially effective on QFT and phase estimation, where correctness depends on many distinct rotation angles and precise phase accumulation. The robustness study with injected random rotations shows the highest error-detection rate among the compared tools.
Overall,
QuPRS
demonstrates that coupling symbolic reductions with complete model-based reasoning yields a practical and scalable path to quantum circuit equivalence checking.
