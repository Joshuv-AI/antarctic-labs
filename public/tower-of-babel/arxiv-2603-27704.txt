Auto-Stabilized Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods for Biot’s consolidation model on Non-Convex Polytopal Meshes
Chunmei Wang, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. This paper presents an auto-stabilized weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for the Biot’s consolidation model within the classical displacement-pressure two-field formulation. Unlike traditional WG approaches, the proposed scheme achieves numerical stability without the requirement of traditional stabilizers. Spatial discretization is performed using weak Galerkin finite elements for both displacement and pressure approximations, while a backward Euler scheme is employed for temporal discretization to ensure a fully implicit and stable formulation. We establish the well-posedness of the resulting linear system at each time step and provide a rigorous error analysis, deriving optimal-order convergence. A significant merit of this WG scheme is its flexibility on general shape-regular polytopal meshes, including those with non-convex geometries. By utilizing bubble functions as a primary analytical tool, the method produces stable, oscillation-free pressure approximations without specialized treatment. Numerical experiments are presented to validate the theoretical convergence rates and demonstrate the computational efficiency and robustness of the auto-stabilized formulation.

Introduction

Based on Biot’s theoretical framework, poroelasticity provides a coupled mathematical description of fluid flow within deformable porous media. This modeling is indispensable to various geophysical sectors, including the detection of gas hydrates. While early investigations into this coupling can be traced back to the one-dimensional studies of Terzaghi
[
42
]
, the generalized mathematical theory was established by Maurice Biot through several foundational publications
[
3
,
4
]
. Biot’s model has since been extensively employed for the quantitative and qualitative analysis of poroelastic phenomena. In recent years, the numerical simulation and theoretical study of Biot’s equations have seen a significant resurgence, driven by diverse applications in biomechanics, medical science, petroleum engineering, and food processing.

In this study, we consider a porous medium that is linearly elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic, saturated by an incompressible Newtonian fluid. Under these assumptions, the quasi-static Biot model is formulated as a time-dependent system of partial differential equations (PDEs) for the solid displacement vector,
\mathbf{u}
, and the fluid pressure,
p
, defined on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq d\leq 3
with a regular boundary
\Gamma
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}},

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla\cdot(K\nabla p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g,

where
{\bm{\sigma}}=2\mu\epsilon({\mathbf{u}})+\lambda\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}I
and
\epsilon({\mathbf{u}})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla{\mathbf{u}}^{T})
represent the effective stress and strain tensors, respectively. The coefficients
\lambda
and
\mu
denote the Lamé parameters,
K
is the hydraulic conductivity tensor,
{\mathbf{f}}
is the density of body forces, and the source term
g
accounts for fluid extraction or injection. The partial derivative with respect to time for the displacement is denoted by
\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}}
.

To ensure a well-posed coupled system, specific boundary and initial conditions are required. A standard configuration of boundary conditions is defined as:

{\mathbf{u}}=0,\qquad K\nabla p\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0,\qquad\text{on}\ \Gamma_{c},

{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}={\bm{\beta}},\qquad p=0,\qquad\text{on}\ \Gamma_{t},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
is the unit outward normal vector. The boundary
\Gamma
is partitioned into disjoint subsets

and
\Gamma_{c}
of non-null measure such that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{t}\cup\Gamma_{c}
. Furthermore, the following incompressibility constraint is imposed at the initial time

(1.3)

(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}})({\mathbf{x}},0)=0,\qquad{\mathbf{x}}\in\Omega.

The Weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method offers a sophisticated framework for solving PDEs by approximating differential operators through a distribution based approach for piecewise polynomials. This paper introduces a simplified, auto-stabilized WG formulation that supports both convex and non-convex polytopal elements. This approach has recently demonstrated success in Poisson

We develop this WG method specifically for the Biot system (

The well-posedness of the system is established through a backward Euler temporal discretization. We prove optimal-order convergence under standard regularity assumptions and numerically demonstrate that the scheme produces oscillation-free pressure approximations without requiring mass lumping or manually tuned stabilization parameters.

2.
Weak Operators and Discrete Formulations

This section reviews the definitions of the weak gradient, weak divergence and weak strain tensor operators, along with their corresponding discrete formulations, as introduced in
[
51
,
18
]
.

2.1.
Weak Function Spaces

Consider a polytopal element
T
with boundary
\partial T
. A weak function on
T
is defined as a pair
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}
, where
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d}
represents the interior values and
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}
represents the boundary values. It is important to note that
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
is treated as a variable independent of the trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
on
\partial T
.

The space of all weak functions on
T
, denoted by
V(T)
, is defined as follows:

V(T)=\left\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}\right\}.

Similarly, the scalar weak function space
W(T)
is defined as:

W(T)=\left\{q=\{q_{0},q_{b}\}:q_{0}\in L^{2}(T),q_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)\right\}.

2.2.
Weak Differential Operators

The weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear operator mapping
V(T)
to the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d}
. For any
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
,
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
is defined as a bounded linear functional satisfying:

(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the unit outward normal vector to
\partial T
.

The weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear operator mapping
V(T)
to the dual space of
H^{1}(T)
. For
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
, the operator is defined such that:

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(T).

Furthermore, the weak gradient of a scalar
q\in W(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w}q
, is a linear operator mapping
W(T)
to the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, satisfying:

(\nabla_{w}q,{\mathbf{w}})_{T}=-(q_{0},\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{w}})_{T}+\langle q_{b},{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\mathbf{w}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}.

2.3.
Discrete Weak Operators

For any non-negative integer
r\geq 0
, let
P_{r}(T)
denote the space of polynomials on
T
with total degree at most
r
. The discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
is the unique polynomial in
[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}
satisfying:

(2.1)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

If
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, applying integration by parts to the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.1
) yields:

(2.2)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

The discrete weak strain tensor is then defined as:

\epsilon_{w,r,T}({\mathbf{v}})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}+\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}^{T}).

The discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,r,T}q
for
q\in W(T)
is the unique polynomial in
[P_{r}(T)]^{d}
such that:

(2.3)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}q,{\mathbf{w}})_{T}=-(q_{0},\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{w}})_{T}+\langle q_{b},{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\mathbf{w}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}.

If
q_{0}\in H^{1}(T)
, integration by parts leads to:

(2.4)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}q,{\mathbf{w}})_{T}=(\nabla q_{0},{\mathbf{w}})_{T}+\langle q_{b}-q_{0},{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\mathbf{w}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d}.

Finally, the discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
is the unique polynomial in
P_{r}(T)
satisfying:

(2.5)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

For
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, this can be reformulated as:

(2.6)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},w)_{T}+\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

3.
Auto-Stabilized Weak Galerkin Algorithms

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
denote a finite element partition of the domain

into polytopal elements, which satisfies the shape regularity conditions established in

We define the following constrained Sobolev spaces:

\displaystyle[H_{c}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}:{\mathbf{v}}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{c}\},

\displaystyle H_{t}^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{q\in H^{1}(\Omega):q=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{t}\}.

The weak formulation of (

\displaystyle 2\mu(\epsilon{\mathbf{u}},\epsilon{\mathbf{v}})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}})-(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}},p)

\displaystyle=({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}})+({\bm{\beta}},{\mathbf{v}})_{\Gamma_{t}},\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in[H_{c}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d},

\displaystyle-(\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}},q)-(K\nabla p,\nabla q)

\displaystyle=(g,q),\quad\forall q\in H_{t}^{1}(\Omega).

3.1.
Weak Finite Element Spaces

Let
k\geq 1
. To approximate the displacement
{\mathbf{u}}
, we introduce the local weak finite element space:

V(T)=\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[P_{k}(e)]^{d},e\subset\partial T\}.

The global weak finite element space
V_{h}
is constructed by assembling the local spaces
V(T)
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, while enforcing the continuity of the boundary component
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
across the interior interfaces

:

V_{h}=\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}|_{T}\in V(T),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

The subspace of
V_{h}
incorporating the essential boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{c}
is defined as:

V_{h}^{c}=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}:{\mathbf{v}}_{b}|_{\Gamma_{c}}=0\}.

For the pressure field
p
, we define the local weak space:

W(T)=\{q=\{q_{0},q_{b}\}:q_{0}\in P_{k}(T),q_{b}\in P_{k}(\partial T)\}.

Similarly, the corresponding global space
W_{h}
and its constrained subspace
W_{h}^{t}
are given by:

\displaystyle W_{h}

\displaystyle=\{q=\{q_{0},q_{b}\}:\{q_{0},q_{b}\}|_{T}\in W(T),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

\displaystyle W_{h}^{t}

\displaystyle=\{q\in W_{h}:q_{b}|_{\Gamma_{t}}=0\}.

3.2.
Discrete Operators and Bilinear Forms

For simplicity, we let the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
, the discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
, and the discrete weak strain tensor
\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
denote the operators
\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}
,
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
, and

, respectively, as defined in (

) and (

(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,r,T}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),\quad(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),\quad(\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\epsilon_{w,r,T}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}).

Following the auto-stabilization strategy in

]
, the degree

for non-convex elements and

for convex elements, where

denotes the number of faces/edges of the element

[

We define the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle a({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle=2\mu(\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{u}},\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{v}})+\lambda(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}),

\displaystyle c(p,q)

\displaystyle=(K\nabla_{w}p,\nabla_{w}q),

\displaystyle b({\mathbf{v}},q)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},q).

3.3.
Fully Discrete Scheme

We propose a weak Galerkin method for the Biot’s equations, employing the WG method for spatial discretization and the backward Euler scheme for temporal discretization. The full discretization is defined as follows:
Find
{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n}=\{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}^{n},{\mathbf{u}}_{b}^{n}\}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
p_{h}^{n}=\{p_{0}^{n},p_{b}^{n}\}\in W_{h}^{t}
such that

(3.1)

\begin{cases}a({\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-b({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p_{h}^{n})=({\mathbf{f}}(t_{n}),{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+\langle{\bm{\beta}}(t_{n}),{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}},&\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{c},\\
-b(\bar{\partial}_{t}{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n},q_{h})-c(p_{h}^{n},q_{h})=(g(t_{n}),q_{0}),&\forall q_{h}\in W_{h}^{t},\end{cases}

where
\bar{\partial}_{t}{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n}=\frac{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}
and
\Delta t
is the time step size.

At each time step

, the solution is obtained via the following scheme:

Auto-Stabilized WG Algorithm 3.1
.

Find
{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
p_{h}\in W_{h}^{t}
such that

(3.2)

\begin{cases}a({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-b({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p_{h})=({\mathbf{f}}(t_{n}),{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+\langle{\bm{\beta}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}},&\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{c},\\
-b({\mathbf{u}}_{h},q_{h})-\Delta tc(p_{h},q_{h})=(\hat{g},q_{0}),&\forall q_{h}\in W_{h}^{t},\end{cases}

where the superscript

is omitted for brevity and

.

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}
and
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}
define norms on
V_{h}^{c}
and
W_{h}^{t}
, respectively.

Proof.

Assume
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}=0
for some
{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}^{c}
. By the norm equivalence (
4.7
), we have
\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{1,h}=0
. This implies
\epsilon{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
and
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
in each element
T
, with
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}={\mathbf{v}}_{b}
on
\partial T
. Consequently,
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in RM(T)
on each element, where
RM(T)
is the space of rigid body motions defined by:

RM(T)=\{\textbf{a}+\eta\textbf{x}:\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\eta\in so(d)\},

where
so(d)
is the space of skew-symmetric
d\times d
matrices. The condition
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}|_{e}={\mathbf{v}}_{b}
implies the continuity of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
across the entire domain
\Omega
. The boundary condition
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=0
on
\Gamma_{c}
ensures
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
on
\Gamma_{c}
. By the second Korn’s inequality, it follows that
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
in
\Omega
, which implies
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=0
and thus
{\mathbf{v}}\equiv 0
.

Next, we show that
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}
is a norm on
W_{h}^{t}
. Assume
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}q{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}=0
for
q\in W_{h}^{t}
. By (
4.8
), we have
\|q\|_{1,h}=0
, implying
\nabla q_{0}=0
on each
T
and
q_{0}=q_{b}
on
\partial T
. Thus,
q_{0}
is constant on each element. The interface condition
q_{0}=q_{b}
ensures that
q_{0}
is constant throughout
\Omega
. Since
q_{b}=0
on
\Gamma_{t}
, it follows that
q_{0}\equiv 0
and
q_{b}\equiv 0
, yielding
q\equiv 0
.
∎

Finally, the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded as follows:

(4.12)

b({\mathbf{v}},q)\leq C{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}\|q\|.

By applying Lemmas
4.3
,
4.4
, and inequality (
4.12
), and invoking standard theory
[
51
]
, we establish that the linear system (
3.2
) is well-posed. We define the following comprehensive bilinear form for
({\mathbf{u}},p),({\mathbf{v}},q)\in V_{h}^{c}\times W_{h}^{t}
:

T({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)=a({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})-b({\mathbf{v}},p)-b({\mathbf{u}},q)-\Delta tc(p,q),

associated with the weighted norm:

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{v}},q){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}^{2}={|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}+\|q\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}q{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}^{2}.

Lemma 4.6
.

The bilinear form
T(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the inf-sup condition:

(4.13)

\sup_{({\mathbf{v}},q)\in V_{h}^{c}\times W_{h}^{t}}\frac{T({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)}{{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{v}},q){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}}\geq\zeta{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{u}},p){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t},

where
\zeta>0
is independent of
h
and
\Delta t
. Consequently, the discrete system (
3.2
) is well-posed.

Proof.

From the inf-sup condition (
4.11
), for any
p=\{p_{0},p_{b}\}\in W_{h}^{t}
, there exists
{\mathbf{w}}\in V_{h}^{c}
such that:

b({\mathbf{w}},p)\geq\alpha\|p\|^{2},\quad\text{with }{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{w}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}=\|p\|.

For
{\mathbf{u}}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
p\in W_{h}^{t}
, we choose
{\mathbf{v}}={\mathbf{u}}-\theta{\mathbf{w}}
and
q=-p
. Then:

\begin{split}T({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)&=a({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}}-\theta{\mathbf{w}})-b({\mathbf{u}}-\theta{\mathbf{w}},p)+b({\mathbf{u}},p)+\Delta tc(p,p)\\
&={|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}-\theta a({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{w}})+\theta b({\mathbf{w}},p)+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}^{2}\\
&\geq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{w}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{V_{h}}+\theta\alpha\|p\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{W_{h}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}+\left(\theta\alpha-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\right)\|p\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{W_{h}}.\end{split}

Setting
\theta=\alpha
, we obtain:

T({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)\geq\frac{1}{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{W_{h}}\geq\zeta_{1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{u}},p){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}^{2},

where
\zeta_{1}=\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\}
. Furthermore, we observe that:

\begin{split}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{v}},q){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}^{2}&={|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{u}}-\theta{\mathbf{w}},-p){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}^{2}\\
&\leq 2{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{V_{h}}+2\theta^{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{w}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}^{2}+\|p\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}^{2}\\
&\leq 2{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{V_{h}}+(2\alpha^{2}+1)\|p\|^{2}+\Delta t{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}_{W_{h}}\leq\zeta_{2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{u}},p){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}^{2},\end{split}

where
\zeta_{2}=\max\{2,2\alpha^{2}+1\}
. Thus, (
4.13
) holds with
\zeta=\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}^{-1/2}
. The continuity of
T(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
is readily verified.

Furthermore, it is easy to show that the blinear form
B({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)
is continuous, i.e.,

B({\mathbf{u}},p;{\mathbf{v}},q)\leq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{u}},p){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}({\mathbf{v}},q){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{\Delta t}.

By Babuska theory, the linear system (
3.2
) is well-posed.
∎

5.
Error Equations

In this section, we derive the error estimates for the fully discrete scheme (
3.1
). We assume that the initial data
\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}
satisfies the discrete divergence-free condition
\nabla_{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}=0
as described in (
1.3
); however, alternative initial conditions may be considered without altering the fundamental analysis.

We begin by establishing the error equations for our model. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto the space
P_{r}(T)
, where the degree
r
is defined as:

•

r=k-1+2N
for non-convex elements
T
,

•

r=k-1+N
for convex elements
T
,

where
N
represents the number of faces (or edges) of the element
T
.

For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, let
Q_{0}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(T)
, and for each edge or face
e\subset\partial T
, let
Q_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(e)
. For any
{\mathbf{v}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
, the projection
Q_{h}{\mathbf{v}}
is defined by:

(Q_{h}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}:=\{Q_{0}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),Q_{b}({\mathbf{v}}|_{\partial T})\},\quad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

An analogous definition holds for any
q\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

(Q_{h}q)|_{T}:=\{Q_{0}(q|_{T}),Q_{b}(q|_{\partial T})\},\quad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

The following lemma summarizes the key projection properties required for the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
51
]

For
\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
and
p\in H^{1}(T)
, the following commutative properties hold:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{h}(\nabla\mathbf{u}),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{h}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}),

(5.3)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{w}(Q_{h}\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{h}(\epsilon(\mathbf{u})),

(5.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}p)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{h}(\nabla p).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},p)
be the exact solutions to the Biot’s consolidation model equations (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). For any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
q_{h}\in W_{h}^{t}
, the following error equations hold:

(5.5)

\displaystyle a(Q_{h}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})-b(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathcal{Q}_{h}p)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{0})+\langle\bm{\beta},\mathbf{v}_{b}\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}+\ell_{1}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})+\ell_{2}(\mathbf{v}_{h},p),

(5.6)

\displaystyle-b(\partial_{t}Q_{h}\mathbf{u},q_{h})-c(Q_{h}p,q_{h})

\displaystyle=(g,q_{0})-\ell_{3}(p,q_{h}),

where the linear functionals
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
, and
\ell_{3}
represent the consistency errors defined as:

\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}2\mu\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
+\lambda\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},

\ell_{2}({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},

\ell_{3}(p,q_{h})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-\langle q_{b}-q_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)K\nabla p\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Proof.

Using (
5.3
), standard integration by parts, and setting
{\bm{\varphi}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}
in (
2.2
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}2\mu(\epsilon_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}2\mu({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}),\epsilon_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}2\mu(\epsilon{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}+2\mu\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}2\mu(\epsilon{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\epsilon{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+2\mu\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-2\mu({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot(\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}))+2\mu\langle\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}+2\mu\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\

where we used
\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=\langle\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=\langle\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}
since
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=0
on
\Gamma_{c}
.

Using (
5.2
), standard integration by parts, and setting
{\bm{\varphi}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\epsilon{\mathbf{u}}
in (

), we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\lambda(\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}\\

=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\lambda(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}+\lambda\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\

where we used

since
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=0
on
\Gamma_{c}
.

Using standard integration by parts and setting

in (

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p)_{T}\\

where we used

on

and

Adding the above three equations and using (

\begin{split}&a(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-b({\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p)\\

This completes the proof of (
5.5

Using (
5.2
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot\partial_{t}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},q_{0})_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}}),q_{0})_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}},q_{0})_{T}.\end{split}

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot\partial_{t}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},q_{0})_{T}-(K\nabla_{w}Q_{h}p,\nabla_{w}q_{h})_{T}\\

1.2
).
This completes the second equation of (

This concludes the proof.

Lemma 6.2

(6.4)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}\leq Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}.

In the second example, we find the steady-state of
the Biot’s model (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) on the unit square domain

\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)
, where
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}
,
g=0
and

(7.3)

\displaystyle K=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{in }(0,\frac{1}{4})\times(0,1),\\
K_{0}&\hbox{in }[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]\times(0,1),\\
1&\hbox{in }(\frac{3}{4},1)\times(0,1).\end{cases}

The boundary conditions are

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbf{u}&=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(\pi y)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\ \hbox{on}\ \{1\}\times(0,1),\\
\mathbf{u}&=\mathbf{0}\ \hbox{on}\ (0,1)\times\{0,1\},\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u}&=0\ \hbox{on}\ \{0\}\times(0,1),\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} p&=0\ \hbox{on}\ \{1\}\times(0,1),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}p&=0\ \hbox{on}\ (0,1)\times\{0,1\}\ \hbox{and}\ \{0\}\times(0,1).\end{aligned}\right.

The initial conditions are

(7.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{u}&=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(\pi y)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\ \hbox{and}\ p=0.\end{aligned}
