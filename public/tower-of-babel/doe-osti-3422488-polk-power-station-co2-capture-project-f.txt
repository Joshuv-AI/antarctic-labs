 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
 Major Goals and Objectives
 The overall objective of the project was to perform the preliminary development work for an
 integrated CO2 capture project at the Polk Power Station (PK). During the project, the team
 applied its extensive knowledge base, built from previous Front End Engineering and Design
 (FEED) studies and recent power generation major construction projects, to complete the
 conceptual design of a carbon capture system capable of capturing a minimum of 95% of the CO2
 emissions. The FEED study for the integrated CO2 capture project included significant design
 deliverables consistent with the level needed to develop an AACE Class 3 estimate with an
 accuracy of ±15% for the entire scope of the capture facility and balance of plant systems and
 expanded upon the work being completed under award DE-FE0032224. In addition to furthering
 the FEED study for the CO2 capture system, the project team planned to develop a FEED study
 for the CO2 transportation infrastructure needed to move the product gas from the compression
 island to the Class VI Sequestration wells.

 The scope of the project also included development of the major permits needed for the
 integrated CO2 capture project to move forward, such as the air, site, and Class VI well permits.
 The project also included development and execution of the community benefits plan.

 Impact
 The completed FEED study included the design of a cost-effective Carbon Capture and
 Sequestration (CCS) system that maintains the necessary flexibility of a dispatch-based
 generating asset, pushes the boundaries of scalability, maximizes energy efficiency, and utilizes
 a transformational solvent, while maintaining a robust and flexible system. This information will
 be used to develop a business case analysis for implementation at Polk Unit 2 (PK2).

 Furthermore, the results of the study will have broad applicability for the US power generating
 fleet for multiple reasons. First, the PK CT technology is the most widely deployed CTs in the US.
 Second, TEC’s generating system has a significant portion of non-dispatchable renewable energy
 that will increase in the future, which is similar to many other regions in the US. This FEED study
 will demonstrate how a large and critical unit, such as PK2, can be equipped with CCS to operate
 within and support a decarbonized electric grid in the future.
