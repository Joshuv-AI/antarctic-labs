Higher-spin algebras from soft theorems I: the wedge condition
Mathias Charbonnier, Javier Peraza

Abstract
In this article we use the sub n -soft graviton theorems to construct the map 𝕋 \mathbb{T} from the spin-graded set of holomorphic functions on local celestial sphere patches to differential operators acting on the asymptotic data for massless particles at ℐ + \mathcal{I}^{+} , in analogy with previous results in the literature for the sub n -soft photon theorems. The result is an explicit closed-form formula. We show that the wedge subalgebras for both Yang-Mills and gravity are the natural domain on which 𝕋 \mathbb{T} becomes a representation.

Introduction

The study of symmetries is still a fruitful avenue in the search for new insights into the quantum properties of physical theories. In particular, over the past years, the study of symmetries in the infrared limit of gravity and gauge theories allowed to match three a priori unrelated phenomena: the soft limits of tree-level scattering amplitudes, the extension of phase spaces that accommodate asymptotic symmetries, and the memory effect. These three topics are related by different integral transformations in the well-known IR triangle
Strominger:2017zoo
. The extension of this triangle to different contexts and beyond tree-level is an ongoing active area of research.

In this work, we are interested in the symmetries arising from the sub
n
-soft theorems, first proposed in
Hamada:2018vrw
;
Li:2018gnc
. We will use a simple model of gauge theory/gravity coupled to a massless scalar, and derive the action of the symmetry in the hard charge as a differential operator acting on the scalar particle variables. We show that these operators contain all the information needed to reconstruct the algebraic properties of the charges and their generators.

For Maxwell theory, in
Campiglia:2018dyi
the authors constructed the map
\mathbb{T}
from holomorphic functions on local celestial sphere patches with arbitrary spin
-s
,
\tau_{s}
, to differential operators acting on the hard particle data at
\mathcal{I}^{+}
. The definition of the hard charge in terms of these operators was given, and it was shown to be classically realized and conserved. A similar study for scalar soft theorems has been done in
Briceno:2025ivl
, where the authors constructed the tower of hard charges associated to the scalar soft theorems.

More recently, and from a different perspective, a tower of conserved quantities for gravity

;
Donnay:2024qwq
;
Kmec:2026dis
and Yang-Mills

;

Preliminaries

We will use flat Bondi coordinates,

ds^{2}=-dudr+r^{2}dzd\bar{z},

(5)

where the complex coordinates parametrize

to the celestial direction,

q^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z}).

(6)

Here, we are identifying the point

with

. We will use this coordinate patch, instead of the usual stereographic projection

p^{\mu}(E,z,\bar{z})=Eq^{\mu}(z,\bar{z}).

(7)

Since we are mapping to flat coordinates, the missing point corresponds to a null direction that can be used to define a complete null basis for Minkowski space,

\{q^{\mu},\partial_{z}q^{\mu},\partial_{\bar{z}}q^{\mu},k^{\mu}\},

(8)

where

p^{\mu}(\rho,E,z,\bar{z})=Eq^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})+\rho k^{\mu}.

With these definitions, we have the pull back of

\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}=-q_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}-k_{\mu}\partial_{E}+E^{-1}\partial_{\bar{z}}q^{\mu}\partial_{z}+E^{-1}\partial_{z}q^{\mu}\partial_{\bar{z}}.

(10)

We will use the following convention for the notation: variables
(\omega,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
will parametrize the energy and direction of the soft particle momenta, while

will parametrize the hard particles energy and direction.

Negative and positive helicities of the soft particle are defined by

\epsilon^{-}_{\mu}=\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}q_{\mu},\quad\epsilon^{+}_{\mu}=\partial_{\zeta}q_{\mu},

(11)

respectively. In this work we use negative helicities, although results are analogous for positive ones. Observe that
\partial^{2}_{\zeta}q^{\mu}=\partial^{2}_{\bar{\zeta}}q^{\mu}=\partial^{2}_{\zeta}\epsilon^{\pm}_{\mu}=\partial^{2}_{\bar{\zeta}}\epsilon^{\pm}_{\mu}=0

. Indeed, we are “pulling back” the operator commutator via
\mathbb{T}
to some bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
on
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathbb{T}
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathcal{W},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket)
.

Outside the condition
C=0
, when considering deformations of the wedge algebra due to covariance under supertranslations, one expects to recover the results established in those works by deforming the map
\mathbb{T}
.
