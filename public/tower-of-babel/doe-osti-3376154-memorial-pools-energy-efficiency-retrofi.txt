 The 9/11 Memorial & Museum

Executive Summary

 The National September 11 Memorial & Museum, a nonprofit organization
located at the World Trade Center in New York City, bears solemn witness to the
terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001 and February 26, 1993. The organization
honors the 2,983 victims of these attacks, those who risked their lives to save others,
and all who demonstrated extraordinary compassion in the aftermath through
commemoration, exhibitions and educational programs that tell the story of the attacks
and explore the continuing global impact of 9/11 and the consequences of terrorism on
individual lives and communities.
 One prominent aspect of the site is the two Memorial Pools, located in the
footprints of the original North and South Twin Towers. During nighttime hours, early
mornings, and cloudy days each pool is illuminated with 136 custom submergible LED
light fixtures. These fixtures create a magnificent golden effect illuminating the waterfalls
cascading over the ground level pool troughs where the names of all victims are
enshrined.
 The lighting fixtures at the base of each pool consist of light strips encased in a
waterproof plastic enclosure; inside these casings are the pool lights. The North and
South Memorial Pools each have four sides and there are 32 60-inch fixtures per side
and two 42-inch fixtures per pool side, for a total of 34 fixtures per pool side (32 (60'
fixtures) + 2 (42" fixtures) = 34 (Lights per side of pool)). The pre-existing LED lights
reached the end of their useful life and began burning out or dimming, leaving sections
of the pool dark or discolored.
 The Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency project allowed for a retrofit of all pool
fixtures with new, highly efficient LED light strips. Rather than performing a costly full
replacement of the fixtures, each light was retrofitted by a manufacturer to reuse viable
components while replacing anything that was compromised. Fixtures that experienced
technical issues were removed, examined by experts, and reinstalled. This process
allowed for significant cost savings, produced far less waste, and aligned with the
institution’s sustainability goals helping the Memorial Plaza maintain its LEED Gold
certification.
 As the Memorial Plaza is open to the public 24 hours a day, seven days a week,
the Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency Retrofit project directly impacted the institution’s
ability to carry out our mission to honor the 2,983 victims of the attacks on September
11, 2001 and February 26,1993, those who risked their lives to save others and all who
demonstrated extraordinary compassion in the aftermath.
 The retrofit improved the overall aesthetic appearance of an important place of
remembrance for New Yorkers, Americans and visitors from around the world. By
properly illuminating the pools we are paying respect to those that lost their lives on
September 11th and the World Trade Center bombing, further supporting our goal to
never forget the lives lost on September 11, 2001 and February 26, 1993.

Background

 In April 2003, the Lower Manhattan Development Corporation - established to
oversee the revitalization of the downtown area after 9/11 - launched an international
competition to choose a design for a permanent memorial at the World Trade Center
site. The competition was open to adults 18 years or older, without regard for nationality
or professional accreditation, and yielded 5,201 submissions from 63 countries. In
January 2004, the design submitted by architect Michael Arad and landscape architect
Peter Walker, Reflecting Absence, was chosen as the winning entry. Their design
features twin waterfall pools surrounded by bronze parapets that list the names of the
victims of the 9/11 attacks and the 1993 World Trade Center bombing. The pools are
set within a plaza where roughly 400 swamp white oak trees grow.
 Today, the 9/11 Memorial Plaza features twin waterfall pools surrounded by
bronze parapets that list the names of the victims of the September 11, 2021 attack and
the 1993 World Trade Center bombing. These two one-acre pools are located within the
exact footprints of the original Twin Towers; they honor the victims and provide a space
for visitors to reflect and remember, and represent a promise made to never forget.
 The interior perimeter at the bottom of each pool (30 feet below the surface) is
lined with light fixtures that illuminate the pools at night. The current lights have been in
use since the Memorial opened in September 2011, and the LED strips are nearing the
end of their lifespan.
 These submergible fixtures were custom made for the Memorial Pools, and to
make repairs, retrofits, or replacements, the entire unit – LED strips and all – must be
sent to the original fixture manufacturer for updating. The custom nature of these units
meant that the 9/11 Memorial & Museum needed to maintain partnership with the
original manufacturer, Acuity Brands in Minnesota, for the duration of the project.

Project Objectives
 The 9/11 Memorial & Museum is committed to addressing energy consumption at
the pace and speed required by the Paris Climate Agreement. To that end, the
institution works collaboratively with the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey on
their Net Zero Initiative with the goal of achieving net zero greenhouse gas emissions by
2050. As such, the Memorial Plaza was consciously designed to conserve energy,
water, and other natural resources. In July of 2015, it was awarded Leadership in
Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) Gold Certification.
 When we realized that many of the Memorial Pool lights had reached the end of
their useful life, the Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency Retrofits project was conceived to
update all the pool lights to LEDs, thus making the pools more energy efficient while
also avoiding outages. The completion of this project was essential to ensuring that the
Memorial Pools can operate at the highest capacity to properly honor those who lost
their lives on September 11, 2001 and in the 1993 World Trade Center attack.
 To complete and track our goals, we had four tasks and several corresponding
milestones that comprised the energy efficiency project.

Project Results and Discussion
 By the end of the Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency Retrofit project, all light
fixtures on the eight individual walls were successfully retrofitted and reinstalled in the
North and South Memorial Pools. This was all completed by the dates noted in the
Quarter One Department of Energy Research Performance Progress Report (August
27th, 2025). The last milestone, “Milestone 4.2 “Second of two Memorial Pool wall’s light
fixtures to be uninstalled, shipped to Minnesota, retrofitted, and reinstalled” was
completed on August 27th, 2025. On this date, we reached 100 percent completion of
Milestone 4.2 which signified the conclusion of the Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency
Project.
 Upon the 100 percent completion of Milestone 4.2, the project was 100 percent
complete by Quarter Two and as a result no work will be done in the remaining quarters
of the project.
 The work done in the first quarter of the project was successful. As such, there
were zero “no-go” milestones. This success and completion of the project is
represented in the significant accomplishment listed for Quarter One: the purchase of
wooden crates, deinstallation of eight sides of submergible pool light fixtures (34 lights
per side), the shipment of the fixtures to the original manufacturer in Minnesota,
examination and retrofit of the lights, return shipment, and reinstallation.
 As of August 27, 2025 all of the lights in both the North and South Memorial
Pools have been retrofitted and reinstalled.
 Due to extenuating circumstances, the 9/11 Memorial & Museum received a pre-
award cost authorization on July 28, 2023. The authorization approved costs incurred
on or after December 29, 2022. Upon receipt of this approval, work began while the
Memorial & Museum remained in negotiations with the Department of Energy. Project
work began on September 21, 2023 as originally planned although the official start of
the project was September 9, 2025.
 With this in mind, all our tasks and milestones were 100 percent completed within
their originally planned start and finish dates. There were no changes to our tasks or
milestones.

 Despite not having any changes to our tasks or milestones, we did have to
implement a change in approach. Previously, the plan was to remove and install all
lights for each wall of each pool on the plaza one at a time. This would have been a
more time-consuming method but would have allowed work to go at a pace. Ultimately,
after an assessment took place, the project team received approval from executive staff
to drain the Memorial Pools during portions of the process. This resulted in us being
able to execute two walls at a time, saving us time and making our work much more
efficient.
 Assessing the success of the project’s sustainability goal is complicated. It is
difficult to quantify the decrease in energy consumption before and after the retrofit
because the North and South Memorial Pools are not metered separately from the rest
of Memorial and Museum site. While we cannot measure the pools specifically, we can
examine the energy usage at the entire site as a whole.
 The standard unit to measure electricity for billing is a Kilowatt-Hour (kWh). This
figure measures total electricity consumption over time. In September 2025 our sitewide

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions

 There are two major accomplishments associated with the Memorial Pools
Energy Efficiency Retrofit project.
 The first is a decrease in the overall energy consumption by the 9/11 Memorial &
Museum. As stated previously, upon project completion energy consumption at the site
was down seven percent. This is a significant accomplishment as the institution strives
towards a net zero greenhouse gas emission by 2050 in partnership with the Port
Authority of New York and New Jersey.
 The Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency Retrofit Project is just one of many steps
the 9/11 Memorial & Museum is taking towards reaching this goal. Another major
initiative is a comprehensive assessment and rehousing of the Museum’s collection in
partnership with the National Endowment for Humanities under the Sustaining Cultural
Heritage program. The results of that project will not be known until 2027, but it was
designed to similarly reduce energy consumption onsite by implementing a more energy
efficient housing of the collection while also reducing the amount of hazardous waste
we produce.
 The second significant accomplishment of the Memorial Pools Energy Efficiency
Retrofit project is of course, the full illumination of the North and South Memorial Pools.
Fully functioning light fixtures are an essential component of the Memorial Plaza. These
new LED strips allow us to pay homage through the illumination of the North and South
Memorial Pools for those who lost their lives on September 11, 2001, as well as those
who lost their lives on February 26th ,1993.
 As previously discussed, the timely nature of the 25th anniversary of the
September 11, 2001 attacks was a key motivating factor for this work. It was extremely
important that the North and South Memorial Pools both be fully functional and lit for this
important and historic milestone. The powerful symbolism evoked by the illuminated
Memorial Pools is extremely meaningful to all who visit and we expect that number to
be significant on September 11 of this year.
 One positive outcome of the change in our planned methodology was the speed
at which we were able to get the work done. During the project, the approach shifted
from removing pool lights one at a time to removing multiple sets simultaneously.
 Initially, the plan was to remove one side of lights and replace it with a spare set
of lights during the retrofit process to ensure the pools always remained lit. The original
plan would have drastically added time to the project schedule and would not have
allowed work to be completed by September 11th, 2025.
 There was additional work scheduled for the pools entirely unrelated to the light
fixtures. This other work resulted in the pools needing to be drained and taken offline for
extended periods of time. The retrofit project team saw this other work as an opportunity

to remove multiple sides of the pool lights at once rather than one at a time. This altered
approach resulted in an elimination of down time between fixture work and significantly
condensed the project schedule.
 A key lesson in this phase of pool drainage was realizing that removal and
deinstallation was much faster when the North and South pools were empty. While this
seems elementary, our original project plan involved removing and installing fixtures in
an active pool. The active pools had 18 inches of water and we had to remove and
install the lights one set at a time. Due to concurrent projects, we were able to remove
multiple sets at once in a dry condition which led to quicker work onsite and quicker turn
arounds from the factory.
 A key challenge the project team encountered was weather conditions. The
Memorial Pools are located on the Plaza which is an outdoor green space. Working
outside in general makes meeting schedules very difficult and presented a challenge to
properly deploy manpower to ensure work was completed in the fullest extent even in
the best weather conditions. Furthermore, removing and installing light fixtures outside
in inclement weather—including rain, snow, heat, and cold—was difficult for both
Memorial & Museum staff and ABM contractors.
 The removal and installation processes necessitate removing stones on the
plaza to access and feed wiring beneath the pools. The fixtures also need to be bolted
into place. All of this amounts to laborious work that must be done by hand. This work is
also very intricate and requires precise movements that are difficult to execute while
wearing bulky gloves and heavy rain, snow, and general cold weather gear.
 Cold weather was not the only issue; extreme heat poses its own challenges. In
warmer months, the bottoms of the pools are significantly hotter than normal outside
temperatures. The heat can cause delays of regular pool maintenance as well as
special project work and extreme heat might even require a cessation of all work until it
cools down.
 The project team encountered a second challenge when working with our
contractors to enhance in-shop testing procedures: some of the lights were leaking after
installation.
 In the early phases of the project, we received multiple sets of lights from the
manufacturer that leaked after being reinstalled into the North and South pools.
Typically, the fixtures are designed to be airtight as they contain electrical components
and operate underwater. The leaking fixtures needed to be sent back to the
manufacturer in Minnesota. This incident had the potential to cause severe scheduling
delays or even project failure if the leak could not be resolved.
 The 9/11 Memorial & Museum project team in conjunction with our ABM
contractors worked with the manufacturer to develop enhanced testing procedures at
their warehouse. During the testing phase each fixture was vacuum sealed and filled
with nitrogen to ensure there was no leakage or latent moisture inside the device. The
fixtures were then submerged off site for an extended period of time, prior to shipment,
to ensure there would be no leakage when installed in the Memorial Pools.
 The third and final challenge was enhancing shipping and packaging methods to
avoid damage to fixtures during transit.
 The custom-made fixtures are very fragile and must be handled with extreme
care to avoid compromising the watertight nature of the electronics. During an early

 Page 9 of 15

 The 9/11 Memorial & Museum

project phase, we received fixtures with damaged power chords that exposed the
electrical wiring to moisture upon installation. The project team determined that the
rubber casing around the chords was compromised during transit, likely due to the way
the fixtures were packaged. We believe that the rubber chord was coming into contact
with a jagged metal edge of the fixture’s footing.
 In order to address this concern, the 9/11 Memorial & Museum purchased
custom crates complete with specifically designed spots for the chords to be wrapped
and thus avoid getting damaged during transit. Since the light fixtures are custom made
it made sense that we needed to have custom shipping containers as well.
 Furthermore, handling the light fixtures required immense caution and care. The
lights could lose their airtight seal if handled incorrectly during packaging or
unpackaging. Although this issue only affected a couple of fixtures, it was noted and
special care was taken to properly handle fixtures at all stages of the project moving
forward.
 As with all project challenges, any fixture that was found damaged upon arrival or
install was shipped back to the manufacturer and repaired. This resulted in some
schedule delays due to shipping, extra work needing to be completed in the factory, and
reinstallation on site.
 In this phase of receiving the shipped pool lights, we learned the importance of
proper shipping and handling techniques to ensure that fixtures arrived onsite,
undamaged, and watertight. This includes not twisting the fixture lenses to ensure the
vacuum seal remains in effect and procuring custom shipping crates with specific
protected areas to store chords; this avoids the lights becoming damaged while in
transit.

Path Forward
 While we are continuously exploring new ways to make our site more energy
efficient and have an overarching goal to upgrade all lighting to high powered LED,
there is currently no plan for further work on the Memorial Pools or their lighting
systems.
