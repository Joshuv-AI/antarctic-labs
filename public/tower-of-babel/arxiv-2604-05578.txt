Abstract
. We consider asymptotic behavior of solutions to the oblique-Dirichlet mixed boundary conditions without the strict monotonicity of the equation in the variable corresponding to the unknown function for ”thin domains” i.e. when the N + 1 N+1 dimensional domains collapse to an N N dimensional domain. A global ellipticity condition in the limit equation is introduced.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of solutions to fully nonlinear elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs) in thin domains with oblique and Dirichlet boundary conditions. Specifically, we consider the following boundary value problem (D
ε
), with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, on a domain
\Omega_{\epsilon}\subset\Omega\times[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}

(D
ε
)

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} &F(D^{2}u^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},z)=0\ \text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\gamma^{+}\cdot Du^{\varepsilon}=\beta^{+}\ \ \text{ on }\partial_{\mathrm{T}}\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\gamma^{-}\cdot Du^{\varepsilon}=\beta^{-}\ \ \text{ on }\partial_{\mathrm{B}}\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&u^{\varepsilon}(z)=\beta(z)\text{ on }\partial_{\mathrm{L}}\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

where
the domain

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is defined as

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{z=(x,y)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,\varepsilon g^{-}(x)<y<\varepsilon g^{+}(x)\},

\Omega
is a given bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
g^{\pm}
,
are given continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}

satisfying
g^{-}<g^{+}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Here,
the boundary
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is divided into three parts, called the top, bottom, and lateral boundaries, which are denoted by
\partial_{\mathrm{T}}\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
\partial_{\mathrm{B}}\Omega
,
and
\partial_{\mathrm{L}}\Omega
, respectively, and they are defined as

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathrm{T}}\Omega=\{(x,y)\,:\,x\in\overline{\Omega},\,y=\varepsilon g^{+}(x)\},\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathrm{B}}\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\,:\,x\in\overline{\Omega},\,y=\varepsilon g^{-}(x)\},\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathrm{L}}\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\,:\,x\in\partial\Omega,\,\varepsilon g^{-}(x)\leq y\leq\varepsilon g^{+}(x)\}.

Furthermore,

F
is a Bellman-Isaacs type operator, on
\mathcal{S}(N+1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1]
,
of the form

(1)

F(X,p,r,z)=\inf_{\lambda\in L}\sup_{\mu\in M}(-\tr\sigma_{\lambda\mu}^{\intercal}\sigma_{\lambda\mu}X-b_{\lambda\mu}\cdot p+c_{\lambda\mu}r-f_{\lambda\mu}),

where
\mathcal{S}(N+1)
denotes the space of symmetric matrices of size
N+1
,

L
and
M
are given nonempty sets,
and
\sigma_{\lambda\mu}
,

b_{\lambda\mu}
,
c_{\lambda\mu}
, and
f_{\lambda\mu}
are functions on

\overline{\Omega}\times[-1.1]

taking values in
k\times(N+1)
matrices for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{R}
, respectively.

Further
and
detailed requirements on
F
and
\gamma^{\pm},\beta^{\pm},\beta
will be provided later on
(see (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
9
),
(
10
), (
11
), and (
12
) below).

This work is a direct continuation of the Dirichlet lateral boundary condition part of our previous study (see
[
3
]
), where we established results for similar problems under the assumption of strict monotonicity of the operator
F(X,p,r,z)
in the variable
r
. In the present paper, we relax this requirement. Instead of strict monotonicity, we assume only that
F
is non-decreasing in
r
, which corresponds to the condition
c_{\lambda\mu}(z)\geq 0
for all
(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M
(see (
7
)).

Let us mention that typical condition for existence of solutions for Neumann boundary problem associated with fully nonlinear degenerate operators requires strict monotonicity in the
dependence on the solution see e.g.
[
1
,
6
]
. Here, the lateral condition is a Dirichlet condition and this gives some room. Nonetheless to compensate for the lack of strict
monotonicity, we introduce a requirement on the ellipticity of
F
in the limit domain
\Omega
. This request which reinforce the degenerate ellipticity is much less than the strict ellipticity.

The precise condition is that there exists a function
s\in C^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

(2)

\inf_{(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M,\ x\in\overline{\Omega}}\,\left|(Ds(x),-Ds(x)\gamma_{0}^{T}(x))A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\right|>0,

where
A_{\lambda\mu}:=\sigma_{\lambda\mu}^{\intercal}\sigma_{\lambda\mu}
and

\gamma_{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 1
.

The existence of
s\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})
in (
2
)
or (
26
) is a global condition. To simplify the situation, we consider a smooth function

A:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathcal{S}(N)
satisfying
A(x)\geq 0
. If

(3)

A(x)\not=0\quad\text{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega},

then, for each point
y\in\overline{\Omega}
, one can choose a linear function
s_{y}
for which

Ds_{y}(x)A(x)Ds_{y}^{\intercal}(x)>0
in a neighborhood of
y
. But, in general, condition (
3
)
does not imply the existence of a
C^{2}
-function
s
on
\overline{\Omega}
such that

(4)

Ds(x)A(x)Ds^{\intercal}(x)>0\quad\text{ for }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

The existence of such a function
s
over the entire domain
\Omega
is non-trivial, more is explained in Section
3
to illustrate this phenomenon.

Under these revised hypotheses, our main result, Proposition
2
, establishes the existence and uniform boundedness of viscosity solutions
u^{\varepsilon}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Our proof relies on the construction of appropriate strict sub- and super-solutions (Lemma
2
). In the proof of Lemma
2
, we employ a coordinate transformation to distort the system so that the boundary conditions match the simplified case where
\gamma_{0}=0
. Once the existence result of Proposition
2
is proved, to obtain our main convergence result for the limiting thin domain, we only need to follow the convergence argument in
[
2
,
3
]
.

Standing assumptions

\bullet
Assumptions on

:

has the form:

(5)

F(X,p,r,z)=\inf_{\lambda\in L}\sup_{\mu\in M}F_{\lambda\mu}(X,p,r,z)\ \ \text{ on }\mathcal{S}(N+1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],

where

F_{\lambda\mu}(X,p,r,z)=-\tr\sigma_{\lambda\mu}^{\intercal}\sigma_{\lambda\mu}X-b_{\lambda\mu}\cdot p+c_{\lambda\mu}r-f_{\lambda\mu},

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{\lambda\mu}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathcal{M}(k,N+1)),\quad b_{\lambda\mu}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),\quad

\displaystyle c_{\lambda\mu},\,f_{\lambda\mu}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathbb{R}).

In the above,

and
\mathcal{S}(m)
the set of all symmetric matrices in
\mathcal{M}(m,m)
,

The following boundedness

(6)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &\max\{|\sigma_{\lambda\mu}(z)|,|b_{\lambda\mu}(z)|,|c_{\lambda\mu}(z)|,|f_{\lambda\mu}(z)|\}\leq C_{F},\\
&\max\{|\sigma_{\lambda\mu}(z)-\sigma_{\lambda\mu}(z^{\prime})|,|b_{\lambda\mu}(z)-b_{\lambda\mu}(z^{\prime})|\}\leq C_{F}|z-z^{\prime}|,\\

where
C_{F}
is a positive constant,
\omega_{F}
is a modulus of continuity.
Additionally,

(7)

c_{\lambda\mu}(z)\geq 0\ \ \text{ for }z\in\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1].

\bullet
Assumptions on
\Omega
:
The set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded

domain and satisfies the

(8)

B_{r_{0}}(x+r_{0}\nu(x))\cap\Omega=\emptyset\ \ \ \text{ for }x\in\partial\Omega.

where

\bullet
Assumptions on
\beta,\,\gamma^{\pm},\,\beta^{\pm}
: The following

(9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\beta\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathbb{R}),\qquad\gamma^{\pm}=(\gamma_{1}^{\pm},\gamma_{2}^{\pm})\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),\quad\\
&\beta^{\pm}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1],\mathbb{R}).\end{aligned}\right.

It is natural to assume that
\pm\gamma_{2}^{\pm}>0
on
\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1]
, and in fact, by normalization, we assume that

(10)

\gamma_{2}^{\pm}=\pm 1.

Furthermore, we assume that there exist functions

(11)

\gamma_{o}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\beta_{o}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}),\quad k^{\pm}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \ \text{ and }\ \ l^{\pm}\in C(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}),

such that

(12)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &\gamma_{1}^{\pm}(x,y)=\pm\gamma_{o}(x)+k^{\pm}(x)y+o(|y|),\\
&\beta^{\pm}(x,y)=\pm\beta_{o}(x)+l^{\pm}(x)y+o(|y|),\end{aligned}\right.

as
y\to 0
, where
o(|y|)/|y|\to 0
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
as
y\to 0
.
These imply:

(13)

\beta^{+}(x,0)=-\beta^{-}(x,0)=:\beta_{o}(x)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \gamma_{1}^{+}(x,0)=-\gamma_{1}^{-}(x,0)=:\gamma_{o}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

In
[

, the regularity assumption on
\gamma_{0},\,\beta_{0}
is that

(\gamma_{0},\beta_{0})\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.
Here, we assume (

on
\gamma_{0},\,\beta_{0},\,k^{\pm}

than that in
[
3
]
, for brevity of presentation.

\bullet
Ellipticity assumptions: We set
A_{\lambda\mu}:=\sigma_{\lambda\mu}^{\intercal}\sigma_{\lambda\mu}
.

(14)

\left\{\,\begin{minipage}[293.2521pt]there

$$\inf_{(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M}\left|(Ds(x),-Ds(x)\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x))A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\right|>0.$$\end{minipage}\right.

Furthermore for any

,

(15)

\inf_{(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M}\left|(\nu(x),-\nu(x)\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}(x))A_{\lambda,\mu}(x,0)\right|>0.

Indeed, recall here the condition

3.
Around the ellipticity condition

Before entering into the proofs and statements of the core results of this paper we wish to dwell on the ellipticity condition in order to
show how the condition (
14
) affects the choice of the matrices
A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\geq 0
allowed.

So precisely we consider
\mathcal{S}(N)
-valued function
A
on

\overline{\Omega}
, having the property,
A(x)\geq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, such that there exists a function
s\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})

(18)

Ds(x)A(x)Ds(x)^{T}>0.

It is quite obvious that if
A(x)
is any non negative matrix such that at least one eigenvalue is positive, and its corresponding eigenvector
v(x)

is a gradient field in
\Omega
with potential
s(x)
then, (
18
) is satisfied.

On the other hand having one positive eigenvalue is not enough. As mentioned in Remark
1
,
we now give an example of an
\mathcal{S}(N)
-valued function
A
on

\overline{\Omega}
, with one eigenvalue equal to
1
for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, but such that
(
18
) is not satisfied for any
s\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that includes the unit circle
\partial B_{1}(0)
.
For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, set

\displaystyle R(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle A(\theta)

\displaystyle:=R(\theta)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}R(\theta)^{-1}=R(\theta)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}R(\theta)^{\intercal}.

For the moment, we consider
A
as an
\mathcal{S}(2)
-valued function on
\partial B_{1}(0)
by associating

A(\theta)
to point
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\in\partial B_{1}(0)
.
Set

v(\theta):=(1,0)R(\theta)^{-1}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\qquad w(\theta):=(0,1)R(\theta)^{-1}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),

and note that
v(\theta),w(\theta)
are (unit) eigenvectors of
A(\theta)
. Indeed,

v(\theta)A(\theta)=0
and
w(\theta)A(\theta)=w(\theta)
, and the associated eigenvalues are
0
and
1
, respectively.
It is clear that
A(\theta)
are all non-negative definite and non-zero (i.e., (
3
) is satisfied).

To proceed, we pick any
s\in C^{1}(\partial B_{1}(0),\mathbb{R})
. Let
x(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
, and
note that

\frac{d}{d\theta}s(x(\theta))=Ds(x(\theta))\cdot\dot{x}(\theta)=Ds(x(\theta))\cdot(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta))=Ds(x(\theta))\cdot w(\theta).

Now, since
s(x(0))=s(x(\pi))
, by the Mean Value Theorem, there is a
\theta_{0}\in(0,2\pi)
such that
(d/d\theta)s(x(\theta))\big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=0
. Thus, we have

Ds(x(\theta_{0}))\cdot w(\theta_{0})=0,

which implies, together with the two-dimensionality of the space, that

Ds(x(\theta_{0}))=rv(\theta_{0})
for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
and moreover,
Ds(x(\theta_{0}))A(\theta_{0})=0
.
This shows that for the
\mathcal{S}(2)
-valued function
A
on
\partial B_{1}(0)
, (
4
) does not hold for any
s\in C^{1}(\partial B_{1}(0),\mathbb{R})
. It is worth to remark that the function
A
can be extended
smoothly and boundedly to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that
A(x)\in\mathcal{S}(2)
and
A(x)\geq 0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
To see this, let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
be such that

\chi(r)\geq 0
for
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\chi(r)=1
for
r<1/2
, and
\chi(r)=0
for
r\geq 1
. Then, for

x=r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define

A(x)=R(\theta)\begin{pmatrix}\chi(r)&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}R(\theta)^{-1}.

It is clear this
A
is an extension of the original
A
defined on
\partial B_{1}(0)
.

Noting that
A(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
for
x\in B_{1/2}(0)
,
we easily deduce that
A
is well-defined and smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
It is clear that
A(x)\in\mathcal{S}(2)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\chi(|x|)
and
1
are the eigenvalues of
A(x)
, which implies that
A
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and non-negative definite everywhere.

We wish to mention that the example was inspired by
[
5
]
.

Main result

4.1.
Limit problem

Although the settings are slightly different from those in
[
3
]
, it will turn out that the limit problem, i.e., the
PDE problem for the limit function of
u^{\varepsilon}
to (D
ε
), is identical.
Recall (see
[
3
, (1.13), (1.14)]
) that
the limit problem is stated as

(D
0
)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &G(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=\beta(x,0)\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
G
has the form: for

(X,p,r,x)\in\mathcal{S}(N)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}
,

(19)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &G(X,p,r,x):=\inf_{\lambda\in L}\sup_{\mu\in M}G_{\lambda\mu}(X,p,r,x),\\
&G_{\lambda\mu}(X,p,r,x):=-\tr(\widetilde{A}_{\lambda\mu}(x)X)-\tilde{b}_{\lambda\mu}(x)\cdot p+\tilde{c}_{\lambda\mu}(x)r-\tilde{f}_{\lambda\mu}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

The coefficients in the above are as follows: It has been shown (see
[
3
]
) that for all

(X,p,r,x)\in\mathcal{S}(N)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}
,

G(X,p,r,x)=F(A+B+C,(p,\beta_{0}-\gamma_{0}\cdot p),r,(x,0)),

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}X&-X\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\\
-\gamma_{0}(x)X&\gamma_{0}(x)X\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&-(pD\gamma_{0}(x))^{\intercal}\\
-pD\gamma_{0}(x)&b(x)\cdot p\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&D\beta_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\\
D\beta_{0}(x)&c(x)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}D\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}-\frac{1}{g^{+}-g^{-}}(g^{+}k^{+}+g^{-}k^{-}),

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{0}D\beta_{0}+\frac{1}{g^{+}-g^{-}}(g^{+}l^{+}+g^{-}l^{-}).

(The functions
\gamma_{0},\,\beta_{0},\,k^{\pm},\,l^{\pm}
are those in (
12
).)
It follows from the above that

(20)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{A}_{\lambda\mu}(x)&=\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&-\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\end{pmatrix}A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&-\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\end{pmatrix}^{\intercal},\\
\tilde{b}_{\lambda\mu}(x)&=b_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&-\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\end{pmatrix}^{\intercal}\\
&\quad-2e_{N+1}A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\begin{pmatrix}D\gamma_{0}(x)&0\end{pmatrix}^{\intercal}+e_{N+1}A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)e_{N+1}^{\intercal}b(x),\\
\tilde{c}_{\lambda\mu}(x)&=c_{\lambda\mu}(x,0),\\
\tilde{f}_{\lambda\mu}(x)&=f_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)+b_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\beta_{0}(x)+\tr A_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\begin{pmatrix}0&D\beta_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\\
D\beta_{0}(x)&c(x)\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
e_{N+1}
denotes the unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, with its
(N+1)^{\,\text{th}}
entry
being unity. Setting

\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}(x)=\sigma_{\lambda\mu}(x,0)\begin{pmatrix}I_{N}&-\gamma_{0}^{\intercal}(x)\end{pmatrix}^{\intercal}\ \ \text{ for }x\in\overline{\Omega},

we have
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda\mu}=\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}^{\intercal}\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
It follows from (
6
), (
11
), and (
17
)
that for
x,x^{\prime}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M
,

(21)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &\max\{|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}(x)|,|\tilde{b}_{\lambda\mu}(x)|,|\tilde{c}_{\lambda\mu}(x)|,|\tilde{f}_{\lambda\mu}(z)|\}\leq\widetilde{C}_{F},\\
&\max\{|\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}(x)-\tilde{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}(x^{\prime})|,|\tilde{b}_{\lambda\mu}(x)-\tilde{b}_{\lambda\mu}(x^{\prime})|\}\leq\widetilde{C}_{F}|x-x^{\prime}|,\\
&\max\{|\tilde{c}_{\lambda\mu}(x)-\tilde{c}_{\lambda\mu}(x^{\prime})|,|\tilde{f}_{\lambda\mu}(x)-\tilde{f}_{\lambda\mu}(x^{\prime})|\}\leq\tilde{\omega}_{F}(|x-x^{\prime}|),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\widetilde{C}_{F}
is a positive constant and
\tilde{\omega}_{F}
is a modulus of continuity.

5.
A key proposition for the oblique-Dirichlet problem

In our study of (D
ε
),
unlike in the previous work
[
2
,
3
]
, we do not assume strict monotonicity of
F(X,p,r,z)
in
r
,
and, by assuming instead (
14
), which is a sort of ellipticity of
F
,
we establish a proposition similar to
[
3
, Proposition 2]
.

Since we are interested in the asymptotic behaviour, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, of the solutions to the boundary value problem (
Dε
), we may focus our
considerations on small
\varepsilon
, say
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, which guarantees that

(22)

\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\subset\overline{\Omega}\times[-1,1].

We remark that the inequality in (
14
) is equivalent that for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
