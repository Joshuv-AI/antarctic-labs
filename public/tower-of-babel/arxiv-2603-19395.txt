Numerical Analysis of a Coupled 3D-1D Transport Problem
Alyssa M. Taylor-LaPole, Uzochi Gideon, Beatrice Riviere, Duygu Vargun

Abstract
A finite element solution coupled with an interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin solution are defined for the approximation of the coupled 3D-1D solute transport problem. Under sufficient regularity for the weak solutions, optimal error bounds are derived for the 3D concentration and 1D concentration, that are optimal with respect to the time step size and the mesh sizes. Numerical results verify the theoretical results.



keywords
finite element method, interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method, well-posedness, error bounds

1
Introduction

Coupled 3D–1D partial differential equations are primarily used to model multiscale problems in which thin, lower-dimensional structures are embedded within a three-dimensional bulk domain. These models are applied across various scientific and engineering disciplines, ranging from biomedical and physiological sciences
Cattaneo and Zunino 2014
;
Notaro et al. 2016
;
Köppl et al. 2020
;
Fritz et al. 2022
to geosciences and material sciences
Gjerde et al. 2020
;
Malenica et al. 2018
;
Cerroni et al. 2019
;
Gjerde et al. 2018
;
Llau et al. 2016
, to address problems characterized by significant disparities in the scales of the domains that constitute the model.
Mixed dimensional models avoid the prohibitively high cost of creating a 3D mesh fine enough to resolve the tiny radius of a capillary or well within a large volume, by reducing thin 3D inclusions to 1D manifolds via topological model reduction techniques proposed
D’Angelo and Quarteroni 2008
;
Laurino and Zunino 2019
.

In this work, we formulate and analyze a numerical method to solve the coupled 3D-1D time-dependent convection diffusion equations, introduced in
Masri et al. 2024a
. Solute exchange between the 1D and 3D domains is made possible through a flux that is proportional to a permeability function and the difference between the
1D concentration and the lateral average of the 3D concentration. Our scheme combines the backward Euler method in time with the finite element method and with the interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method in space. Optimal error bounds are derived for the concentration of the solute.
Our analysis is valid in the general case where the permeability of the inclusion wall is a non negative function along the 1D domain; this allows for parts of the wall of the blood vessel (or wall of the inclusion) to be impermeable. To our knowledge, our work is the first error analysis of the 3D-1D transport problems.

The numerical analysis of coupled 3D-1D flows is introduced in
Laurino and Zunino 2019
where the elliptic problem is discretized by the finite element method. Convergence is obtained by deriving a priori error estimates. The authors derive the 3D-1D mixed dimensional model from
the fully 3D-3D model and they show that the 3D-1D model has a modeling error that depends on the radius of the tubular inclusion. The coupled 3D-1D elliptic problem is discretized by the interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method in
Masri et al. 2024b
. Because of the coupling term involving the 1D centerline, the 3D solution exhibits low regularity and consistency of the DG scheme does not hold.
The case of numerical discretization of the time-dependent coupled 3D-1D problems has not been studied in the literature. As mentioned above, the work
Masri et al. 2024a
contains the derivation of the 3D-1D transport problem by starting from the 3D-3D models of solute transport and by applying a similar topological reduction to the one in
Laurino and Zunino 2019
. The authors also show that the modeling error decreases with the inclusion diameter.

An outline of the paper is as follows. We first introduce the model problem and the scheme in Section
2
and Section
3
. Existence, uniqueness
and stability bounds are obtained in Section
4
. Convergence is proved by deriving error bounds in the following section. Numerical results are given in Section
6
and conclusions follow.

3
Numerical scheme

The proposed scheme employs the finite element method to solve (
1
) and the interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) method to solve (
2
). The time-stepping method is the backward Euler method.
The scheme is defined in this section and its derivation is given in Appendix
8
.

Let
\tau>0
denote the time step value and
let
t^{n}=n\tau
denote the discrete time value for any
n\geq 0
.
The domain
\Omega
is partitioned into tetrahedra elements
K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\Omega}
. The mesh size is defined by
h_{\Omega}=\max\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\Omega}}\mathrm{diam}(K)
. Let
V^{\Omega}_{h}
be the finite dimensional space of continuous piecewise polynomials of degree
k_{1}\geq 1
that vanish on the boundary:

V^{\Omega}_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\,v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k_{1}}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\Omega}\}.

Using the parametrization
\lambda
, we partition the curve
\Lambda
by defining a partition
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{N}=L
denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}^{h}
, with mesh size
h_{\Lambda}
.
Let
H^{1}(\mathcal{T}^{h}_{\Lambda})
be the space of broken
H^{1}
functions on
\Omega
and
V^{\Lambda}_{h}
be the finite dimensional space of discontinuous piecewise polynomials of degree
k_{2}:

V^{\Lambda}_{h}=\{\hat{v}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Lambda),\,\hat{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k_{2}}((s_{i-1},s_{i}))\ \forall i=1,..,N\}.

The jump and average operators are defined across interior nodes
s_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N-1
as, for any function
\hat{v}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}^{h})
:

\displaystyle\hat{v}(s_{i}^{-})=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0,\epsilon>0}\hat{v}(s_{i}-\epsilon),\ \ \hat{v}(s_{i}^{+})=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0,\epsilon>0}\hat{v}(s_{i}+\epsilon),

\displaystyle[\hat{v}]_{s_{i}}=\hat{v}(s_{i}^{-})-\hat{v}(s_{i}^{+}),\quad\{\hat{v}\}_{s_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{v}(s_{i}^{-})+\hat{v}(s_{i}^{+})\right).

Let
\sigma>0
denote the penalty parameter used in the IPDG scheme.
We define a semi-norm for the space
V_{h}^{\Lambda}
:

|\hat{v}_{h}|_{\mathrm{DG}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\frac{\partial\hat{v}_{h}}{\partial s}\|_{L^{2}([s_{i-1},s_{i}])}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{h_{\Lambda}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}[\hat{v}_{h}]_{s_{i}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We now present the scheme.
For all
n\geq 1
, given
(c_{h}^{n-1},\hat{c}_{h}^{n-1})\in V_{h}^{\Omega}\times V_{h}^{\Lambda}
, find
(c_{h}^{n},\hat{c}_{h}^{n})\in V_{h}^{\Omega}\times V_{h}^{\Lambda}
such that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(c_{h}^{n}-c_{h}^{n-1})v_{h}+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla c_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{U}}c_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla v_{h}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\overline{c_{h}^{n}}-\hat{c}_{h}^{n}\right)\overline{v_{h}}=\int_{\Omega}f(\cdot,t^{n})v_{h},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Omega},

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Lambda}|\mathcal{D}|(\hat{c}_{h}^{n}-\hat{c}_{h}^{n-1})\hat{v}_{h}+

\displaystyle a_{\Lambda}(\hat{c}_{h}^{n},\hat{v}_{h})+b_{\Lambda}(\hat{c}_{h}^{n},\hat{v}_{h})+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\hat{c}_{h}^{n}-\overline{c_{h}^{n}}\right)\hat{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\hat{f}(\cdot,t^{n})\hat{v}_{h}+|\mathcal{D}(0)|\hat{U}\,c^{\mathrm{in}}(t^{n})\hat{v}_{h}(0),\quad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Lambda},

(12)

where
a_{\Lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b_{\Lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined as follows for any

:

\displaystyle a_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi},\hat{v})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{s_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}|\mathcal{D}|\hat{\kappa}\frac{\partial\hat{\xi}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial s}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\{|\mathcal{D}|\hat{\kappa}\frac{\partial\hat{\xi}}{\partial s}\}_{s_{i}}\,[\hat{v}]_{s_{i}}

\displaystyle-\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\{|\mathcal{D}|\hat{\kappa}\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial s}\}_{s_{i}}\,[\hat{\xi}]_{s_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sigma}{h_{\Lambda}}[\hat{\xi}]_{s_{i}}\,[\hat{v}]_{s_{i}},

\displaystyle b_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi},\hat{v})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{s_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}|\mathcal{D}|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial s}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}\hat{\xi}(s_{i}^{-})[\hat{v}]_{s_{i}}+|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(L)\hat{v}(L).

We remark that

is the standard IPDG discretization of the diffusion operator and

. The parameter

To start the scheme, we choose

is the Scott-Zhang interpolant of

Throughout the paper,

Assume that

Error analysis

To handle the coupling term in the analysis below, we will make use of the following result (see Lemma 3.4 in

Lemma 2

The following inequality holds

\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega),\quad\||\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\overline{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{1}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

L^{2}(\Gamma)
to
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Now, under sufficient regularity for the weak solution, we state and prove an error bound that is optimal in time and space with respect to the gradient norm.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(c,\hat{c})
be the weak solution and assume that

c\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\Omega))
, for
1<r<3/2
,

\hat{c}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
,

\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\Omega))
and

\frac{\partial^{2}c}{\partial t^{2}}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega).

Assume
\tau
is small enough, namely
\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
There exists a constant
M>0
independent of
h_{\Omega},h_{\Lambda}
and
\tau
such that for all
m\geq 1

\displaystyle\|c(\cdot,t^{m})-c_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\hat{c}(\cdot,t^{m})-c_{h}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{m}\|\nabla c(\cdot,t^{n})-\nabla c_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{m}|\hat{c}(\cdot,t^{n})-\hat{c}_{h}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}\leq M(\tau^{2}+h_{\Omega}^{2r-2}+h_{\Lambda}^{2}).

(19)

Proof.

We will first obtain error bounds for the errors between the numerical solutions and optimal approximations of the weak solutions. We introduce the notation:

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 0,\quad e^{n}=c_{h}^{n}-\Pi c(t^{n}),\quad\hat{e}^{n}=\hat{c}_{h}^{n}-\pi\hat{c}(t^{n}),

\displaystyle\forall t\geq 0,\quad\xi(t)=c(t)-\Pi c(t),\quad\hat{\xi}(t)=\hat{c}(t)-\pi\hat{c}(t),

where for all
t
,
\Pi c(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is the Scott-Zhang interpolant of
c(t)
and
\pi\hat{c}(t)
is the L
2
projection of
\hat{c}(t)
on
V_{h}^{\Lambda}
. These approximations satisfy the following optimal bounds
Bernardi et al. 2024
:

\displaystyle\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Mh_{\Omega}^{r}\|c(t)\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)},\,\,\|\partial_{t}\xi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Mh_{\Omega}^{r}\|\partial_{t}c(t)\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)},

(20)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\xi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Mh_{\Omega}^{r-1}\|c(t)\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)},

(21)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle\,\|\hat{\xi}(t)\|_{L^{2}([s_{i-1},s_{i}])}\leq Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\hat{c}(t)\|_{H^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i}])},

(22)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle\,\|\partial_{t}\hat{\xi}(t)\|_{L^{2}([s_{i-1},s_{i}])}\leq Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\partial_{t}\hat{c}(t)\|_{H^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i}])},

(23)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle\,\|\frac{\partial\hat{\xi}(t)}{\partial s}\|_{L^{2}([s_{i-1},s_{i}])}+h_{\Lambda}\|\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{\xi}(t)}{\partial s^{2}}\|_{L^{2}([s_{i-1},s_{i}])}\leq Mh_{\Lambda}\|\hat{c}(t)\|_{H^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i}])}.

(24)

The bounds hold for the time derivatives because
\partial_{t}\Pi c=\Pi\partial_{t}c
and
\partial_{t}\pi c=\pi\partial_{t}c
.

With the regularity assumptions on the weak solution, it is easy to see that it satisfies for all
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}(t^{n})v_{h}+\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla c(t^{n})\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{U}}c(t^{n})\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\overline{c(t^{n})}-\hat{c}(t^{n})\right)\overline{v_{h}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(\cdot,t^{n})v_{h},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Omega},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}|\mathcal{D}|\frac{\partial\hat{c}(t^{n})}{\partial t}\hat{v}_{h}+a_{\Lambda}(\hat{c}(t^{n}),\hat{v}_{h})+b_{\Lambda}(\hat{c}(t^{n}),\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\hat{c}(t^{n})-\overline{c(t^{n})}\right)\hat{v}_{h}=|\mathcal{D}(0)|\hat{U}\,c^{\text{in}}(t^{n})\hat{v}_{h}(0)+\int_{\Lambda}\hat{f}(\cdot,t^{n})\hat{v}_{h},\quad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Lambda}.

Therefore we have the following error equations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(e^{n}-e^{n-1})v_{h}+\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla e^{n}\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{U}}e^{n}\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n}\right)\overline{v_{h}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}(t^{n})-\frac{1}{\tau}(\Pi c(t^{n})-\Pi c(t^{n-1}))\right)v_{h}+\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\xi(t^{n})\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{U}}\xi(t^{n})\cdot\nabla v_{h}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\overline{\xi(t^{n})}-\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\right)\overline{v_{h}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Omega},

(25)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Lambda}|\mathcal{D}|(\hat{e}^{n}-\hat{e}^{n-1})\hat{v}_{h}+a_{\Lambda}(\hat{e}^{n},\hat{v}_{h})+b_{\Lambda}(\hat{e}^{n},\hat{v}_{h})+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\hat{e}^{n}-\overline{e^{n}}\right)\hat{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}|\mathcal{D}|\left(\frac{\partial\hat{c}}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{\tau}(\pi\hat{c}(t^{n})-\pi\hat{c}(t^{n-1}))\right)\hat{v}_{h}+a_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi}(t^{n}),\hat{v}_{h})+b_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi}(t^{n}),\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\hat{\xi}(t^{n})-\overline{\xi(t^{n})}\right)\hat{v}_{h},\quad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\Lambda}.

(26)

Choosing
v_{h}=e^{n}
in (
25
) and
v_{h}=\hat{e}^{n}
in (
26
), summing the two resulting equations, using coercivity of
a_{\Lambda}
, property (
17
) and the positivity of
b_{\Lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|e^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}-\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{k_{0}}{2}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{a}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\ |\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}([\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{D}(0)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(0))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(L))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{dc}{dt}(t^{n})-\frac{1}{\tau}(\Pi c(t^{n})-\Pi c(t^{n-1}))\right)e^{n}+\int_{\Lambda}|\mathcal{D}|\left(\frac{d\hat{c}}{dt}-\frac{1}{\tau}(\pi\hat{c}(t^{n})-\pi\hat{c}(t^{n-1}))\right)\hat{e}^{n}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\kappa\nabla\xi(t^{n})\cdot\nabla e^{n}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{U}}\xi(t^{n})\cdot\nabla e^{n}+a_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi}(t^{n}),\hat{e}^{n})+b_{\Lambda}(\hat{\xi}(t^{n}),\hat{e}^{n})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Lambda}\gamma|\partial\mathcal{D}|\left(\overline{\xi(t^{n})}-\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\right)(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n})

\displaystyle=T_{1}+\dots+T_{7}.

We now bound each term
T_{i}
. For the first two terms
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we will use the well-known equality for any function
w
of one variable
t
:

\frac{1}{\tau}(w(t^{n})-w(t^{n-1})=w^{\prime}(t^{n})-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t^{n-1}}^{t^{n}}(s-t^{n-1})w^{\prime\prime}(s)ds.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, Young’s inequality with a positive
\epsilon_{1}
parameter, (
10
) and the approximation bound (
20
), we have for
T_{1}
:

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{dc}{dt}(t^{n})-\frac{1}{\tau}(\Pi c(t^{n})-\Pi c(t^{n-1}))\right)e^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{dc}{dt}(t^{n})-\frac{1}{\tau}(c(t^{n})-c(t^{n-1}))\right)e^{n}+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\xi(t^{n})-\xi(t^{n-1})e^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\sqrt{\tau}\|e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}c}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}([t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}e^{n}\left(\int_{t^{n-1}}^{t^{n}}\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{M}{2\epsilon_{1}}\tau\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}c}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}([t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\Omega)}^{2}+M\frac{h_{\Omega}^{2r}}{\epsilon_{1}\tau}\left\|\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}(t^{n-1},t^{n};H^{r}(\Omega))}^{2}.

The second term is bounded in a similar fashion as the first term, except we do not use Poincaré’s inequality. The approximation bound (
23
) and Young’s inequality with parameter
\epsilon_{2}>0
are used.

\displaystyle T_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}\|\hat{e}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{M}{2\epsilon_{2}}\tau\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{c}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}([t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\Lambda)}^{2}+M\frac{h_{\Lambda}^{4}}{2\epsilon_{2}\tau}\left\|\frac{\partial\hat{c}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}(t^{n-1},t^{n};H^{2}(\Lambda))}^{2}.

The third term is bounded using Cauchy-Schwarz’s and
Young’s inequalities and the approximation bound (
21
):

\displaystyle T_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{3}}{2}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{3}}k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla\xi(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{3}}{2}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{Mk_{1}^{2}}{2\epsilon_{3}}h_{\Omega}^{2r-2}\|c(t^{n})\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Similarly, with Cauchy-Schwarz’s, Young’s inequalities and the approximation property (
20
), we have for the fourth term:

\displaystyle T_{4}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{4}}{2}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{4}}\|{\boldsymbol{U}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\xi(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{4}}{2}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{M}{2\epsilon_{4}}\|{\boldsymbol{U}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{\Omega}^{2r}\|c(t^{n})\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Using standard DG techniques to bound the terms in the DG bilinear form (
Riviere 2008
, see Appendix
11
):

\displaystyle T_{5}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{5}}{2}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{5}}Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}.

For the sixth term, we have with (
22
), (
24
),
a trace inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz’s and Young’s inequalities:

\displaystyle T_{6}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{s_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}|\mathcal{D}|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\frac{\partial\hat{e}^{n}}{\partial s}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(s_{i}^{-},t^{n})\,[\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}}

\displaystyle+|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(L,t^{n})\,(\hat{e}^{n}(L))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{U}\|\frac{\partial\hat{e}^{n}}{\partial s}\|_{L^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i})}\|\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i})}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}[\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\hat{U}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{\xi}(s_{i}^{-},t^{n})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(L))^{2}+|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(L,t^{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{6}}{2}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}+\frac{d_{1}^{2}}{2\epsilon_{6}}\hat{U}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}(s_{i-1},s_{i})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}[\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\hat{U}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{\xi}(s_{i}^{-},t^{n})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(L))^{2}+|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}\,\hat{\xi}(L,t^{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{6}}{2}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}+M\frac{d_{1}^{2}}{2\epsilon_{6}}\hat{U}^{2}h_{\Lambda}^{4}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}\,[\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(L))^{2}+Md_{1}\hat{U}h_{\Lambda}^{3}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}.

For the last term, we first write

\displaystyle T_{7}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{\xi(t^{n})}-\hat{\xi}(t^{n}))\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}

Using triangle inequality, Lemma
2
, and approximation results (
22
), (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{\xi(t^{n})}-\hat{\xi}(t^{n}))\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\||\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\overline{\xi(t^{n})}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}+\gamma_{1}\||\partial D|^{1/2}\hat{\xi}(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}C_{1}\|\xi(t^{n})\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{1}d_{1}Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}C_{1}Mh_{\Omega}^{r-1}\|c(t^{n})\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{1}d_{1}Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}.

Therefore a bound for
T_{7}
is

T_{7}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+Mh_{\Omega}^{2r-2}\|c(t^{n})\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}^{2}+Mh_{\Lambda}^{4}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}.

We choose the following values for the parameters
\epsilon_{i}
:

\epsilon_{1}=\frac{k_{0}}{12},\quad\epsilon_{2}=d_{0},\quad\epsilon_{3}=\epsilon_{4}=\frac{k_{0}}{6},\quad\epsilon_{5}=\epsilon_{6}=\frac{C_{a}}{2},

and combine the bounds above to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|e^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}-\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\gamma^{1/2}|\partial\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}(\overline{e^{n}}-\hat{e}^{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{k_{0}}{4}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{C_{a}}{2}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|\mathcal{D}(s_{i})|\hat{U}([\hat{e}^{n}]_{s_{i}})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{D}(0)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(0))^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{D}(L)|\hat{U}(\hat{e}^{n}(L))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+M\tau\left(\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}c}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}([t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{c}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}([t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\Lambda)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+M\frac{h_{\Omega}^{2r}}{\tau}\left\|\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}([^{n-1},t^{n};H^{r}(\Omega))}^{2}+M\frac{h_{\Lambda}^{4}}{\tau}\left\|\frac{\partial\hat{c}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}(t^{n-1},t^{n};H^{2}(\Lambda))}^{2}

\displaystyle+Mh_{\Omega}^{2r-2}\|c(t^{n})\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}^{2}+Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\|\hat{c}(t^{n})\|_{H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}.

(27)

The rest of the proof follows a standard technique. After dropping some positive terms in the left-hand side, we multiply (
27
) by
2\tau
,
sum from
n=1
to
n=m
for any
m\geq 1
, assume that
\tau\leq 1/2
and apply Gronwall’s lemma to obtain:

\displaystyle\|e^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+\frac{k_{0}}{2}\tau\sum_{n=1}^{m}\|\nabla e^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{a}\tau\sum_{n=1}^{m}|\hat{e}^{n}|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\||\mathcal{D}|^{1/2}\hat{e}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}+M\tau^{2}\left(\|\frac{\partial^{2}c}{\partial t^{2}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{c}}{\partial t^{2}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Lambda)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+Mh_{\Omega}^{2r-2}\left(\|\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|c\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{r}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+Mh_{\Lambda}^{2}\left(\|\frac{\partial\hat{c}}{\partial t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Lambda))}^{2}+\|\hat{c}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}\right).

The final result is obtained by first using the approximation bounds (
20
) and (
22
) for the initial errors
e^{0}
and
\hat{e}^{0}
, and second by using triangle inequalities and optimal bounds for the approximation errors.

∎

Conclusion

A numerical scheme is analyzed for the coupled 3D-1D transport problem. The concentration in the 3D domain is approximated by continuous piecewise polynomials and the concentration in the 1D domain is approximated by discontinuous polynomials. Error bounds that combine both 3D and 1D errors in
L^{2}
in time and gradient in space are optimal with respect to the time step and the regularity of the weak solution. In addition, bounds of the errors in
L^{\infty}(L^{2})
are also derived.

Because of the use of finite elements for the 3D problem, the velocity field is assumed to be either divergence free or to be bounded by the diffusion coefficient in the 3D domain. In order to remove these assumptions, the 3D transport problem should be discretized by a discontinuous Galerkin method. The lack of consistency for the DG method in the 3D domain is a challenge. This will be the object of future work.
