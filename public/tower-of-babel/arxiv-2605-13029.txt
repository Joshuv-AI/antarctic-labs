On the additivity of projective presentations of maximal rank
Grzegorz Bobiński, Jan Schröer

Abstract
. We study projective presentations of finite-dimensional modules over finite-dimensional algebras. We discuss if projective presentations of maximal rank behave additively. More precisely, we ask if the direct sum of copies of a projective presentation of maximal rank is again of maximal rank. The modules which have a projective presentation of maximal rank are exactly the τ \tau -regular modules. This class of modules can be seen as a generalization of modules of projective dimension at most one, and of τ \tau -rigid modules. The τ \tau -regular modules form open subsets of module varieties. Their closures are therefore unions of irreducible components, which are called generically τ \tau -regular. We discuss when a τ \tau -regular module or a generically τ \tau -regular component can be reduced to a module or component of projective dimension at most one, and we show that this is closely related to the question on the additivity of maximal rank presentations.

\tau

This article deals with

1.3.
Hierarchy of modules

The
\tau
-regular modules are a generalization of modules of projective dimension at most one, and of
\tau
-rigid modules.
Recall that
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
is
rigid
if
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M)=0
.
A rigid module
M
is a
partial tilting module
if

\operatorname{proj.dim}(M)\leq 1
.
Partial tilting modules are the direct summands of
tilting modules, which are the corner stone of classical tilting theory.

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid M\text{ is projective}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\leq 1}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid\operatorname{proj.dim}(M)\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{rigid}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M)=0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{partilt}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M)=0,\;\operatorname{proj.dim}(M)\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{{\tau-rigid}}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\tau_{A}(M))=0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\tau-reg}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)\mid M\text{ is $\tau$-regular}\}.

We have the following hierarchy of classes of modules:

\textstyle{\operatorname{\tau-reg}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{rigid}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{P}}_{\leq 1}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{{\tau-rigid}}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{partilt}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{proj}(A)}

We have

\operatorname{partilt}(A):={\mathcal{P}}_{\leq 1}(A)\cap\operatorname{rigid}(A)={\mathcal{P}}_{\leq 1}(A)\cap\operatorname{{\tau-rigid}}(A).

\displaystyle:=\min\{\dim({\mathcal{Z}})-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\mid M\in{\mathcal{Z}}\},

\displaystyle e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}})

\displaystyle:=\min\{\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M)\mid M\in{\mathcal{Z}}\},

\displaystyle e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\min\{\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M^{\prime})\mid(M,M^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{Z}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle E_{A}({\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\min\{E_{A}(M,M^{\prime})\mid(M,M^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{Z}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\}.

be the simple
A
-modules, up to isomorphism.

As defined already above,
let

2.2.
Direct sums of irreducible components

For
1\leq i\leq m
let

{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,\mathbf{d}_{i})
.
Set
\mathbf{d}:=\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{m}
.
We get a morphism

\displaystyle\eta\colon\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})

\displaystyle(g,M_{1},\ldots,M_{m})

\displaystyle\mapsto g.(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{m}).

The image of
\eta
is denoted by
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
.
This is an irreducible constructible subset of
\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})
, which is called the

of the components

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
.
The direct sum of

{\mathcal{Z}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
.
Note that the closure

\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}

is in general not an irreducible component of
\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
CBS02
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For
1\leq i\leq m
let

{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,\mathbf{d}_{i})
.
Then the following are equivalent

(i)

\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
;

(ii)

e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i},{\mathcal{Z}}_{j})=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
with
i\not=j
.

A component
{\mathcal{Z}}
is
indecomposable
if the indecomposable modules
form a dense subset of
{\mathcal{Z}}
.
Each component
{\mathcal{Z}}
is (the closure of) a direct sum of indecomposable components in a
unique way, up to permutation of the summands.
This is then called the
canonical decomposition
of
{\mathcal{Z}}
.
We refer to
[
CBS02
]
for details.

Here is an analogous result for generically
\tau
-regular component.
Its proof is based on Theorem

.

Theorem 2.2

(
[

, Theorem 1.3]

.

For
1\leq i\leq m
let

(ii)

for all

The following lemma should be well known.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
1\leq i\leq m
let

{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,\mathbf{d}_{i})

such that

{\mathcal{Z}}:=\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A).

Then

c_{A}({\mathcal{Z}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})\text{\hskip 10.22217ptand \hskip 10.22217pt}e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{d}:=\mathbf{d}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{m}
.
We consider the morphism

\eta\colon\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}\to{\mathcal{Z}}

of affine varieties
which sends
(g,M_{1},\ldots,M_{m})
to
g.(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{m})
.
For
M
generic in
{\mathcal{Z}}
we can assume that

M=M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{m}
with
M_{i}
generic in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}

for each
1\leq i\leq m
.
We get

\displaystyle\dim\eta^{-1}(M)

\displaystyle=\dim\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{i}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle c_{A}({\mathcal{Z}})

\displaystyle=\dim({\mathcal{Z}})-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}

\displaystyle=\left(\dim\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\dim({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})-\dim\eta^{-1}(M)\right)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}

\displaystyle=\left(\dim\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\dim({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})-\dim\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(M)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{i}}\right)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}).

The equality

e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})

follows from Theorem
2.1
, which says that

e_{A}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i},{\mathcal{Z}}_{j})=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
with
i\not=j
.
∎

2.3.

We recall some results from

.

For each pair

with

This is a slightly modified version of the canonical decomposition introduced and studied in groundbreaking work by

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

(i)

\operatorname{proj.dim}(\mathcal{Z})\leq 1

(ii)

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii):
Assume that
\operatorname{proj.dim}({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 1
.
We always have
{\mathcal{P}}_{\leq 1}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{\tau-reg}(A)
.
Assume now
M\in{\mathcal{Z}}\cap\operatorname{\tau-reg}(A)
.
Let

P_{1}\xrightarrow{f}P_{0}\to M\to 0

be a minimal projective presentation of
M
.
We know from Fei’s Lemma
[
BS25
, Lemma 3.2]
that there is a generic
N\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, which has
a projective presentation of the form

P_{1}\xrightarrow{g}P_{0}\to N\to 0.

, we get an isomorphism

(P_{1}\xrightarrow{g}P_{0})\cong(P_{1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{g^{\prime}}P_{0}^{\prime})\oplus(P\xrightarrow{\cong}P)\oplus(P^{\prime}\to 0)

of

is a minimal projective presentation of

is a monomorphism.
We also know that
\operatorname{rk}(g)=\operatorname{rk}(f)=r(P_{1},P_{0})
, since

is
\tau
-regular.
This implies
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P^{\prime},N)=0
.
Since
{\mathcal{Z}}
and therefore also

is sincere, this implies

.
It follows that
g
and therefore also

(ii)
\implies
(i):

5.2.
Faithful components

Mousavand and Paquette
[
MP23
]
initiated the study of faithful
irreducible components.
We recall and generalize some of their results.

Proposition 5.2

(
[
MP23
, Proposition 5.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
.
Then

\{M\in\mathcal{Z}\mid M\text{ is faithful}\}

is an open subset of
\mathcal{Z}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
, and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a dense open subset
of
\mathcal{Z}
.
Then there exist
M_{1},\ldots,M_{t}\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that

I_{\mathcal{Z}}=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq t}I_{M_{i}}=I_{M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t}}.

Proof.

We have

I_{\mathcal{Z}}=I_{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcap_{M\in{\mathcal{U}}}I_{M}.

Since the ideals
I_{M}
are subspaces of the finite-dimensional
vector space
A
, there exist finitely many modules
M_{1},\ldots,M_{t}\in{\mathcal{U}}

with

I_{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq t}I_{M_{i}}.

Finally, it follows from the definitions that

\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq t}I_{M_{i}}=I_{M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t}}.\qed

Corollary 5.4

(
[
MP23
, Proposition 5.4]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
be faithful. Then there exist generic modules

M_{1},\ldots,M_{r}
in
\mathcal{Z}
such that

M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t}
is faithful.

Proof.

Combine Proposition
5.3
with Proposition
5.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For
{\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
with

{\mathcal{Z}}=\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}

we have

I_{\mathcal{Z}}=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq m}I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}.

Proof.

This is obvious, since

I_{\mathcal{Z}}=I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}.\qed

(

Theorem 5.7

Let

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\operatorname{proj.dim}(\mathcal{Z})\leq 1

(ii)

-regular for all

Proof.

(i)
\implies

This is part of Lemma

(ii)
\implies
(i):
Since

is faithful, we can apply Corollary

and get generic modules

The

6.1.

Let
I
be an ideal of
A
, and let
B=A/I
.
Then we can identify
\operatorname{mod}(B)
with the full subcategory of all

with
IM=0

When we consider such an

as a

we speak of the

of

to

(or to a

-module).
Similarly, if
{\mathcal{Z}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
with

{\mathcal{Z}}
as a component in
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
, and we speak
again of a

of
{\mathcal{Z}}
to
B
(or to a component
in
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
).

Lemma 6.1
.

As above, let
B=A/I
, and let
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
with
IM=0
.
Then

c_{B}(M)\leq c_{A}(M),\quad e_{B}(M)\leq e_{A}(M),\quad E_{B}(M)\leq E_{A}(M).

Proof.

We have
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})
with
\mathbf{d}=\underline{\dim}(M)
.
We can interpret
\operatorname{mod}(B,\mathbf{d})
as a closed subset of

\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})
.
This implies
c_{B}(M)\leq c_{A}(M)
.

Note that
\operatorname{mod}(B)
is an exact subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(A)
.
It follows that
e_{B}(M)\leq e_{A}(M)
.

By
[
AR77
, Proposition 4.2]
we know that
\tau_{B}(M)
can be seen
as a submodule of
\tau_{A}(M)
.
This obviously implies

\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(M,\tau_{B}(M))=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\tau_{B}(M))\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\tau_{A}(M))

and therefore
E_{B}(M)\leq E_{A}(M)
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

As above, let

, and let

6.4.
Reduction to components of projective dimension
at most one

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
.
Assume that there is some
M\in{\mathcal{Z}}
such that
M^{t}
is
\tau
-regular for all
t\geq 1
.
For each such
t
there is a unique
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
such that

M^{t}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

We have
I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t+1}}\subseteq I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{d}_{t}:=\underline{\dim}(M^{t})
.
Let

{\mathcal{U}}
be the image of the morphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbf{d}_{t+1}}\times{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{M}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d}_{t+1})

\displaystyle(g,M_{1},M_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto g.(M_{1}\oplus M_{2}).

Then
{\mathcal{U}}
is an irreducible constructible subset of

\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d}_{t+1})
.
Furthermore,
{\mathcal{U}}
is
\tau
-regular, since
M^{t+1}\in{\mathcal{U}}
.
Thus the
\tau
-regular modules form a dense subset of
{\mathcal{U}}
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}_{t+1}
.
Thus all modules from
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
appear as direct summands of modules
from
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t+1}
.
This implies
I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t+1}}\subseteq I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}}
.
∎

By Lemma
6.7
,
there is a chain

\cdots\subseteq I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t+1}}\subseteq I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}}

of ideals of
A
.
For dimension reasons there is some number
s({\mathcal{Z}})
such that

I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}}=I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{s({\mathcal{Z}})}}

for all
t\geq s({\mathcal{Z}})
.
Then

I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\infty}}:=\bigcap_{t\geq 1}I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}}=I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{s({\mathcal{Z}})}}.

Define

B:=A/I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\infty}}.

The next lemma follows now immediately.

Lemma 6.8
.

With the notation above,
for all
t\geq s({\mathcal{Z}})
,
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
is a faithful component in
\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(B)
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

Assume that
I
is an ideal of
A
with
I{\mathcal{Z}}=0
, and let

B=A/I
.
If

\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 1,

then
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
is
\tau_{A}
-regular for all

t\geq 1
.

(ii)

Assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
is
\tau_{A}
-regular for all
t\geq 1
,
let
I=I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\infty}}
, and let
B=A/I_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{\infty}}
.
Then
\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 1
.

Proof.

(i):
Assume that
\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 1
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
is
\tau_{B}
-regular for
all
t\geq 1
.
Let
M
be generic in
{\mathcal{Z}}
.
We get

\displaystyle c_{A}(M^{t})

\displaystyle\geq c_{B}(M^{t})=E_{B}(M^{t})=t^{2}E_{B}(M)=t^{2}c_{B}(M)

\displaystyle=t^{2}c_{A}(M)=t^{2}E_{A}(M)=E_{A}(M^{t}).

This implies
c_{A}(M^{t})=E_{A}(M^{t})
.
Thus
M^{t}
and therefore also
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
is
\tau_{A}
-regular
for all
t\geq 1
.

(ii):
Assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}
is
\tau_{A}
-regular for all
t\geq 1
.
Then
{\mathcal{Z}}^{t}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
for some unique

{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
.

By Proposition
6.6
,
each
\tau_{A}
-regular
M\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
is also
\tau_{B}
-regular.
Thus
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
is
\tau_{B}
-regular for all
t\geq 1
.
Let
t\geq s({\mathcal{Z}})
.
By Lemma
6.8
,

{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}
is faithful (as a component of
B
-modules),
and
({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})^{s}
is generically
\tau_{B}
-regular
for all
s\geq 1
.
(Each
\tau_{A}
-regular module in
({\mathcal{Z}}^{t})^{s}={\mathcal{Z}}^{ts}
is
\tau_{B}
-regular
in
({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})^{s}
, by Proposition
6.6
.)
Thus
\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})\leq 1
by Theorem
5.7
.
It follows from
Theorem
5.1

that there is a
\tau_{B}
-regular
module
M=M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t}
in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}

with
M_{i}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
for all
1\leq i\leq t
and
\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}(M)\leq 1
.
This implies
\operatorname{proj.dim}_{B}({\mathcal{Z}})\leq 1
.
∎

.

.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii):

(ii).

(ii)
\implies
(i):
