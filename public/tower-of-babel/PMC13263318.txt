Prognostic significance of intratumoral neutrophil extracellular traps in pulmonary pleomorphic carcinoma: comparison with adenocarcinoma
Authors: Makoto Yoden, Ryosuke Kaku, Daigo Ishihara, Kohei Shibata, Keiko Ueda, Takuya Shiratori, Yoko Kataoka, Yo Kawaguchi, Jun Hanaoka, Fumihiro Shoji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPleomorphic carcinoma (PC) of the lung is a rare, aggressive non-small cell lung cancer. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are linked to tumour progression, but their relevance in pulmonary PC is unknown. We evaluated the prognostic impact of intratumoral NETs and CD66b+ neutrophil infiltration in PC versus adenocarcinoma.MethodsAmong 1148 patients who underwent pulmonary resection for primary lung cancer, 813 patients with adenocarcinoma and 17 with PC were analysed. Clinicopathological factors and survival were compared and propensity score matching was applied. NETs and neutrophils were quantified by CD66b immunohistochemistry and citrullinated histone H3/myeloperoxidase immunofluorescence.ResultsPC was associated with heavier smoking, more vascular invasion, and more advanced stage than adenocarcinoma (all p < 0.001). The patient characteristics were comparable after matching. Overall and recurrence-free survival were both worse in the PC group (both p < 0.001) and remained poorer after matching. Cox analysis identified NET-positive area (% per high-power field) as an independent predictor of worse overall survival (hazard ratio 1.14, 95% confidence interval 1.01–1.30; p = 0.033).ConclusionsPulmonary PC shows intense neutrophil infiltration and abundant intratumoral NETs, both associated with poor survival. Quantitative assessment of NETs may serve as a biomarker and therapeutic target in PC.

Background

Pleomorphic carcinoma of the lung is a rare and aggressive subtype of non-small cell lung cancer, characterized by histological heterogeneity and poor clinical outcomes compared with conventional adenocarcinoma. According to the 2021 WHO Classification of Thoracic Tumors, pleomorphic carcinoma is categorized under sarcomatoid carcinomas, defined as a non-small cell carcinoma containing spindle and/or giant cell components, often admixed with adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma [1]. Despite advances in surgical techniques and perioperative management, patients with pleomorphic carcinoma often experience early recurrence and limited survival [2]. This tumour entity exhibits unique biological behaviours, with frequent vascular invasion, necrosis, and resistance to systemic therapy, suggesting that its aggressive phenotype may be driven by distinctive tumour microenvironment interactions.

The tumour microenvironment has recently emerged as a critical determinant of tumour progression, immune evasion, and response to therapy. In particular, neutrophil-mediated inflammation plays a multifaceted role in cancer biology by promoting angiogenesis, extracellular matrix remodelling, and metastatic dissemination. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are web-like structures composed of DNA and granular proteins, such as myeloperoxidase (MPO) and citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3). In the present study, the term ‘NETs’ refers specifically to neutrophil extracellular traps and not to neuroendocrine tumours. NETs have been implicated in tumour invasion, immune evasion, and postoperative recurrence across various malignancies [3]. Experimental studies have demonstrated that NETs can trap circulating tumour cells [4], induce epithelial–mesenchymal transition [5], and impair cytotoxic T-cell function [6].

Although the involvement of NETs in lung cancer has been increasingly recognized, their specific contribution to the pathobiology of pleomorphic carcinoma remains poorly characterized. Adenocarcinoma was selected as the comparator in this study because it represents the most prevalent and well-characterized subtype of non-small cell lung cancer, allowing for a controlled comparison within a shared epithelial lineage. Moreover, adenocarcinoma provides an appropriate clinical and biological reference because of its distinct molecular background, established diagnostic criteria, and the availability of large, well-documented patient cohorts suitable for statistical matching. In addition, adenocarcinoma is often the predominant epithelial component within pleomorphic carcinoma, suggesting a close histogenic relationship between the two subtypes. Previous studies, however, have been limited by small sample sizes and the lack of appropriately matched comparator cohorts, thus hindering a robust evaluation of NETs in this rare tumour type.

The current study aimed to elucidate the clinical and prognostic relevance of intratumoral NET formation and CD66b⁺ neutrophil infiltration in pleomorphic carcinoma compared with adenocarcinoma, using propensity score matching to minimize baseline differences between groups. By integrating immunohistochemical and quantitative immunofluorescence analyses, we sought to clarify if NET formation represents a key biological mechanism underlying the aggressive clinical behaviour of pulmonary pleomorphic carcinoma.

Methods

Patient selection

This retrospective study included patients who underwent pulmonary resection for primary lung cancer at Shiga University of Medical Science between 2011 and 2024, including patients with adenocarcinoma or pleomorphic carcinoma. Patients with other histological subtypes, such as small cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, large-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, and others, were excluded (Fig. 1). All diagnoses were confirmed by at least two experienced pathologists according to the WHO Classification of Thoracic Tumors applicable at the time of diagnosis [1]. Clinicopathological data were retrieved from electronic medical records and pathology reports.Fig. 1Flow diagram of patient selection.

Flow diagram of patient selection.

Tissue preparation

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections (4 µm) were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval using Target Retrieval Solution, pH 9 (10×) (Dako, Agilent Technologies, Glostrup, Denmark) in a microwave oven. Endogenous autofluorescence was minimized by cooling the slides and carrying out glycine quenching in 0.1 mol/L glycine in phosphate-buffered saline for 10 min at room temperature prior to antibody incubation.

Immunohistochemistry for CD66b

Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with a peroxidase blocking reagent, and nonspecific binding was suppressed with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in phosphate-buffered saline. The sections were then incubated with anti-CD66b antibody (Cat. 555723, BD Pharmingen, San Diego, CA, USA) for 1 h at room temperature and visualized using a 3,3′-diaminobenzidine substrate kit (Dako) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Hematoxylin was used for nuclear counterstaining.

The number of CD66b⁺ neutrophils was counted manually in three representative high-power fields (HPFs, ×200) within the tumour stroma, and the mean value was used for statistical analysis. Quantification was performed in a blinded manner to minimize assessment bias.

Immunofluorescence staining

To detect NETs, sections were cooled after antigen retrieval and incubated with 0.1 M glycine in phosphate-buffered saline for 15 min at room temperature to quench residual aldehydes, followed by blocking with 1% bovine serum albumin in phosphate-buffered saline for 30 min. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against CitH3 (NB100-57135, Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA) and MPO (AF3667, R&D Systems Minneapolis, MN, USA). After washing, the slides were incubated with Alexa Fluor 647 donkey anti-rabbit IgG (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA) and NorthernLights NL557 donkey anti-goat IgG (R&D Systems) for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole and mounted using ProLong Gold Antifade Mountant (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Fluorescent images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) and analysed with ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Co-localization of CitH3 and MPO was regarded as evidence of NET formation. NET-positive area (% per HPF) was quantified in three representative high-power fields (×200) selected from viable intratumoral stromal regions corresponding to the areas evaluated on histological review, while avoiding necrotic regions, tissue folds, and artefactual fluorescence. Fluorescence images were acquired using the same microscope and comparable exposure conditions. Image analysis was performed using ImageJ software. Threshold adjustment was performed for each image to appropriately identify CitH3- and MPO-positive signals while excluding background fluorescence, and the area showing overlap of CitH3 and MPO signals was defined as NET-positive area. The mean value from three fields was used for analysis. Quantification was performed in a blinded manner.

Propensity score matching

Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to minimize baseline differences between the groups, using logistic regression including age, sex, smoking history, performance status, pathological stage, and adjuvant chemotherapy as covariates. One-to-one nearest-neighbor matching without replacement was applied.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using Wilcoxon’s rank-sum test, while categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test. Overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Because survival differences were expected to appear predominantly in the early postoperative period, the generalized Wilcoxon (Breslow) test was also applied as a sensitivity analysis. Prognostic factors were evaluated using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p values were derived from Wald tests. For multivariate analysis in the PSM cohort, age, pathological stage, lymphatic invasion, vascular invasion, CD66b+ neutrophil density, and NET-positive area were included a priori, with NET-positive area entered as the main variable of interest. All statistical analyses were conducted using JMP (SAS Institute Cary, NC, USA). A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient selection and clinicopathological characteristics

A total of 1148 patients underwent pulmonary resection for primary lung cancer at Shiga University of Medical Science Hospital between 2011 and 2024. Among them, 830 patients with adenocarcinoma (n = 813) or pleomorphic carcinoma (n = 17) were included in the present study (Fig. 1).

Before PSM, patients with pleomorphic carcinoma showed significantly higher rates of smoking (Brinkman index, p < 0.001), more advanced pathologic T category (p < 0.001), a higher incidence of vascular invasion (p < 0.001), and higher pathological stage (p < 0.001) compared with patients with adenocarcinoma (Supplementary Table S1). After PSM, 17 matched pairs were analysed, with no significant difference in age, sex, smoking history, performance status, pathological T and N categories, pathological stage, or adjuvant therapy between the two groups (Table 1), indicating that the baseline characteristics were well balanced. The median age of the entire cohort was 70 years (IQR 64–76 years), and 58.4% were male. Representative immunofluorescence images demonstrated co-localization of CitH3 and MPO, confirming NET structures predominantly within pleomorphic carcinoma tissues (Fig. 2).

These findings indicate that pleomorphic carcinoma harbours a highly inflamed tumour microenvironment enriched with activated neutrophils and extensive NET formation. The spatial overlap of CitH3⁺ and MPO⁺ signals suggested functional activation of neutrophils within the tumour stroma, rather than passive accumulation. These differences were visualized by box-and-whisker plots comparing CD66b⁺ neutrophil counts and NET-positive areas between pleomorphic carcinoma and adenocarcinoma samples (Fig. 2).

Notably, the magnitude of NET formation showed greater intertumoural variability in patients with pleomorphic carcinoma than in patients with adenocarcinoma, suggesting heterogeneous but overall enhanced neutrophil activation within this histological subtype.

Collectively, these findings indicate that intratumoral NET formation, rather than neutrophil density alone, is a key determinant of an adverse prognosis in patients with pleomorphic carcinoma. The prognostic impact of NETs persisted even after adjusting for established pathological factors, thus highlighting the biological relevance of NET-mediated tumour–immune interactions.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that pleomorphic carcinoma was associated with markedly higher neutrophil infiltration and extensive formation of NETs throughout the tumour microenvironment compared with conventional adenocarcinoma, even after rigorous adjustment using PSM. This finding indicates that the distinct inflammatory landscape of pleomorphic carcinoma cannot be explained solely by clinicopathological differences between histologic subtypes. By using adenocarcinoma as a comparator, this study distinguished NET-related inflammatory signatures intrinsic to pleomorphic carcinoma from those reflecting general tumour aggressiveness. Although the survival differences between the groups were not significant after PSM, patients with pleomorphic carcinoma consistently showed a trend towards poorer outcomes, together with pronounced neutrophil activation and NET formation, suggesting that its aggressive phenotype cannot be solely attributed to tumour stage or smoking exposure.

Quantitative analysis in the PSM cohort revealed that the NET-positive area was independently associated with OS, indicating that intratumoral NET formation is not merely an epiphenomenon of inflammation, but may play an active biological role in the aggressive behaviour of pleomorphic carcinoma. A recent study by Oi et al. [7] reported that NET formation, defined by co-localization of CitH3 and MPO, was significantly associated with a poor prognosis in patients with pulmonary pleomorphic carcinoma. The current findings are consistent with this previous study and provide complementary insights into tumour-associated NETs. By applying PSM and including adenocarcinoma as a comparator, we demonstrated that pleomorphic carcinoma is characterized by markedly higher CD66b⁺ neutrophil infiltration and NET formation. Although CD66b⁺ cell density was elevated, it was not independently associated with survival in multivariate analysis, suggesting that the detrimental impact of neutrophil activity may depend more on NET formation than on neutrophil infiltration itself.

This contrast between pleomorphic carcinoma and adenocarcinoma indicates that the inflammatory landscape varies substantially among tumour subtypes. Pleomorphic carcinoma was characterized by more intense neutrophil infiltration and abundant NET formation, suggesting a highly activated inflammatory microenvironment. These findings imply that the biological impact of neutrophilic inflammation may depend not only on the degree of cell infiltration, but also on the extent of NET formation. The transition from neutrophil activation to NETosis represents a shift towards an inflammation-driven milieu that could facilitate tumour progression through tissue remodelling, proteolytic activity, and immune modulation. Consistent with this idea, our quantitative analysis identified the NET-positive area as an independent prognostic factor for OS, highlighting the prognostic significance of NET formation beyond morphological observations.

Mechanistically, NETs can promote tumour progression via multiple mechanisms [3], including induction of epithelial–mesenchymal transition [5], enhancement of vascular invasion [4], and suppression of cytotoxic T-cell activity [6]. The marked co-localization of CitH3 and MPO in pleomorphic carcinoma tissues observed in the present study further supports the concept that NET formation contributes to the highly invasive phenotype of this histological subtype. Given that vascular invasion was more frequent in pleomorphic carcinoma, NETs may contribute to tumour dissemination through mechanisms involving endothelial damage and increased vascular permeability, as suggested by previous experimental studies [4]. In addition, NETs have been shown to remodel the tumour microenvironment through endothelial damage, cytokine release, and modulation of immune cell activity, leading to a more permissive and inflamed stromal milieu [8]. Such interactions may be particularly relevant in pleomorphic carcinoma, which exhibits extensive stromal reaction and heterogeneous inflammatory cell infiltration.

In addition to these tumour-promoting effects, recent clinical evidence in non-small cell lung cancer demonstrated that elevated circulating NETs were associated with a poor response and poor survival in patients treated with immune-checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [9]. Although this evidence does not specifically address pleomorphic carcinoma, it suggests that excessive NET formation may contribute to an immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment that limits ICI efficacy. Given that pleomorphic carcinoma typically exhibits strong programmed death-ligand 1 expression [10] but often shows limited and short-lived responses to ICIs [11], it is possible that intratumoral NETs and programmed death-ligand 1-mediated pathways act cooperatively to promote immune evasion. This dual immunosuppressive axis comprising high programmed death-ligand 1 expression and abundant NETs may partly explain the poor and transient clinical responses to ICIs observed in patients with pleomorphic carcinoma.

Beyond its association with baseline tumour aggressiveness, NET formation may also influence the dynamics of postoperative recurrence. Previous studies suggested that surgery-induced inflammation and neutrophil activation could transiently elevate systemic NET levels, potentially facilitating the survival and seeding of residual tumour cells [12, 13]. This raises the possibility that pleomorphic carcinoma, characterized by pre-existing NET enrichment, could be particularly susceptible to postoperative micrometastatic progression. Future studies integrating perioperative NET monitoring and longitudinal sampling may clarify if NETs can serve not only as a prognostic marker, but also as a modifiable mediator of recurrence.

From a clinical perspective, the quantification of NET formation may serve as a novel biomarker for postoperative prognosis. The identification of NETs as an independent prognostic factor raises the possibility that therapeutic strategies targeting NETs, such as DNase treatment [14], peptidylarginine deiminase 4 inhibition [15], or perioperative modulation of neutrophil activity, could potentially improve outcomes in patients with pleomorphic carcinoma. Moreover, combining NET-targeting approaches with immune-checkpoint blockade may enhance antitumor immunity by relieving physical and biochemical suppression imposed by NETs. These concepts warrant further translational and clinical investigations. In parallel, growing evidence across thoracic malignancies has underscored the importance of tumour–immune interactions and the immune microenvironment in shaping disease behaviour and therapeutic responsiveness. In particular, recent studies in thoracic and neuroendocrine tumours have highlighted that the composition of the immune microenvironment and the balance between immune activation and immune suppression may substantially influence the efficacy of emerging immunotherapeutic strategies [16]. In this broader context, our findings support the relevance of investigating NET-related immune modulation as a potential contributor to the aggressive biology of pulmonary pleomorphic carcinoma.

This study had several limitations. First, it was a retrospective single-institution analysis, and the propensity score-matched cohort was small (17 pairs) because of the rarity of pleomorphic carcinoma. This limited sample size likely reduced the statistical power to detect survival differences after matching and also constrained the robustness of the prognostic analyses. In particular, because the number of outcome events was limited relative to the number of covariates entered into the multivariate Cox model, the model may have been unstable and prone to overfitting. Therefore, the observed independent association between NET-positive area and overall survival should be interpreted cautiously as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, rather than definitive. Larger multicentre studies are needed to validate the reproducibility and generalizability of these findings. Second, we did not conduct any mechanistic experiments to demonstrate a direct causal relationship between NET formation and tumour progression. Further multicentre studies and in vivo functional analyses are therefore needed to validate the current findings and to clarify the molecular pathways by which NETs promote tumour aggressiveness.

This study demonstrated that pleomorphic carcinoma of the lung is characterized by prominent neutrophil infiltration and abundant NET formation, and that increased NET formation is associated with a poor prognosis. Quantitative assessment identified the NET-positive area as an independent prognostic factor within pleomorphic carcinoma, emphasizing the pathological and clinical importance of tumour-associated NETs in this rare subtype. These findings suggest that targeting NET formation may provide a novel therapeutic approach and prognostic biomarker for improving outcomes in patients with pleomorphic carcinoma.
