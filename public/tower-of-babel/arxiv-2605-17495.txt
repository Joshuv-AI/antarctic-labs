Maximally almost periodic subgroups of Abelian groups of prime exponent ⋆
Ol’ga Sipacheva

Abstract
. It is proved that any infinite Abelian topological group of prime exponent has an infinite maximally almost periodic subgroup.

This paper is concerned with Hausdorff Abelian topological groups of prime
exponent
p
. It is well known that, algebraically, any such group
G
is a direct sum of copies of the cyclic
group
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
of order
p
(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 8.5]
). Since
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is
a vector space over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, it follows that so is
G
. Therefore,
G
has a basis
E
, and every
nonzero element of
G
can be uniquely (up to the order of terms) represented as a linear combination of elements
of
E
with nonzero coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
; in other words,
G=\bigoplus_{e\in E}\langle e\rangle
, where
\langle e\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by
e
(this is the cyclic group of
order
p
). Thus, on any Abelian topological group
G
of prime exponent
p
with basis
E
, there exists the
natural topology induced by the Tychonoff product topology of
\prod_{e\in E}\langle e\rangle
. In what follows,
we refer to this topology as the
product topology on
G
associated with
E
.

Although the algebraic structure of
G
is simple, its topology may be very complex and strongly differ from
a product topology. For example, it may happen that any continuous character of
G
, that is, any continuous
homomorphism of
G
to the circle group
\mathbb{T}
, is trivial
[
8
, Example 5]
, i.e., has kernel

G
. Recall that the intersection of the kernels of continuous characters of a topological group is called the

von Neumann kernel
of
G
and denoted by
n(G)
; a group
G
with
n(G)=G
is said to be
minimally
almost periodic
and a group
G
with trivial
n(G)
is said to be
maximally almost
periodic

[
7
]
.

Remark 1
.

A Hausdorff Abelian topological group
G
of prime exponent is maximally almost periodic if and only if there
exists a basis
E
of
G
such that the product topology on
G
associated with
E
is coarser than the original
topology of
G
.

Indeed,
G
is maximally almost periodic if and only if the family
\mathscr{C}
of all nontrivial continuous
characters on
G
separates the points of
G
, that is, the continuous diagonal homomorphism
\operatornamewithlimits{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\Delta$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\large$\Delta$}}}{\Delta}{\Delta}}\mathscr{C}\colon G\to\mathbb{T}^{|\mathscr{C}|}
is injective and
G
admits a continuous isomorphism onto a topological
subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{|\mathscr{C}|}
. Clearly, this subgroup carries the product topology associated with some
basis.

Yet another obvious criterion for
G
to be maximally almost periodic is that the intersection of open subgroups
of finite index in
G
is trivial, because the image of any character of
G
is the discrete cyclic group

\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and its kernel is an open subgroup of
G
of index
p
. On the other hand, every
open subgroup of finite index in
G
is an intersection of open subgroups of index
p
, each of which is the
kernel of a continuous character.

Example 5 of
[
8
]
proves the existence of a Hausdorff minimally almost periodic Abelian topological group of
any prime exponent. In this paper, we show that any infinite Abelian topological groups of prime exponent
contains an infinite maximally almost periodic subgroup, that is, a subgroup whose topology is finer than the
product topology associated with some basis of this subgroup. We also prove that any countable Abelian
topological group of prime exponent has a closed discrete basis.

In what follows, by
\mathbb{N}
we denote the set of positive integers and by
|X|
, the cardinality of a
set
X
. The subgroup of a group
G
generated by a set
A\subset G
is denoted by
\langle A\rangle
; when
G

is treated as a vector space,
\langle A\rangle
is the linear subspace of
G
generated by
A
. We assume that

\langle\varnothing\rangle
is the trivial subgroup (subspace). All notions related to topological and metric
spaces can be found in
[
4
]
and to topological groups, in
[
2
]
. We assume all topological groups
under consideration to be Hausdorff.

A
norm
on a group
G
with neutral element
e
is a function
\lVert\boldsymbol{\cdot}\rVert\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

(1)

\|g\|=0
if and only if
g=e
;

(2)

\|g^{-1}\|=\|g\|
;

(3)

\|gh\|\leq\|g\|+\|h\|
(the triangle inequality for norms).

We say that a topology of a topological group
G
is
generated
by a norm
\lVert\boldsymbol{\cdot}\rVert

if the sets
\{x\in G:\|x\|<\varepsilon\}
,
\varepsilon>0
, form a neighborhood base at
e
for the topology
of
G
.

Definition
.

A subset
X
of a group
G
with neutral element
e
is said to be
independent
if, given any

n\in\mathbb{N}
, any pairwise distinct
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in A
, and any
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

k_{1}=\dots=k_{n}=0
whenever
x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot x_{n}^{k_{n}}=e
.

In other words,
X\subset G
is independent if
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle X\setminus A\rangle=\{e\}
for any
A\subset X
.

Definition
.

We say that a subset
X
of a group
G
is a
basis
of
G
if
X
is independent and
\langle X\rangle=G
.

Not every group has a basis, but when a basis exists, it is almost never unique. As mentioned above, any Abelian
group
G
of prime exponent does have a basis
E
and every element
g
of such a group can be written as a
linear combination of elements of
E
with nonzero coefficients in a unique way (up to permutation of terms). We
refer to this linear combination as a
decomposition
of
g
in
E
and to the number of terms in
it as the
length
of
g
.

Definition
.

A subset
X
of a topological group
G
with neutral element
e
is said to be
topologically independent

if, given any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, any pairwise distinct
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in A
, any
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and any neighborhood
U
of
e
, there exists a neighborhood
V
of

e
such that
x_{1}^{k_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{k_{n}}\in U
whenever
x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot x_{n}^{k_{n}}\in V
.

Note that any Tychonoff space
X
is a basis of the free, free Abelian, and free Boolean topological groups

F(X)
,
A(X)
, and
B(X)
on
X
, but it is never topologically independent in these groups (unless
X
is
discrete). Moreover, no subspace
Y
of
X
with nondiscrete closure is topologically independent. Indeed, let

G
be any of the groups
F(X)
,
A(X)
, and
B(X)
; we denote the neutral element of
G
by
e
. The subroup
H

of all words of even length is open in
G
, because this is the kernel of the continuous homomorphism
H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
extending the map
X\to\{0,1\}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defined by
x\mapsto 1
for each
x\in X
. Let
U=H
, and let
g
be any limit point of
Y
. Since
g^{-1}\cdot g=e
and the group operations are continuous, it
follows that, given any neighborhood
V
of
e
, there exists a neighborhood
W
of
g
in
G
such that

u^{-1}\cdot v\in V
for all
u,v\in W
. In particular, we have
x^{-1}\cdot y\in V
for any
x,y\in W\cap Y
.
On the other hand,
x^{-1}
and
y
do not belong to
U
, and hence
Y
is not topologically independent.

Proposition 1
.

Any metrizable countable Abelian topological group of prime exponent contains an infinite topologically
independent set.

Proof.

Let
G
be a metrizable countable Abelian topological group of prime exponent
p
with zero element
{\mathbf{0}}
, and
let
E=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots\}
be any basis of
G
. For convenience, we will treat
G
as a vector space over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
and its elements as linear combinations of elements of
E
.

Since the group
G
is first-countable, its topology is generated by a norm
\lVert\boldsymbol{\cdot}\rVert
(see,
e.g.,
[
3
]
). Consider the set
E^{\prime}=\{e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},\dots\}
defined recursively as follows:

•

e^{\prime}_{1}=e_{1}
;

•

if
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}
are already defined, then
e^{\prime}_{n+1}
is
a linear combination
\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}e^{\prime}_{n}+\lambda_{n+1}e_{n+1}
, where
\lambda_{n+1}\neq 0
, of minimum norm (if there are
several linear combinations of minimum norm, then we take any of them).

It is easy to see that
E^{\prime}
has the following properties:

(i)

for each
n
, the linear span of
\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}\}
equals that of
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
;

(ii)

E^{\prime}
is a basis in
G
;

(iii)

if
n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}
, then
\|e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}\|\leq\|\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{k}e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}\|
for any
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Indeed, (i) is proved by simple induction: for every
n
, we have

e^{\prime}_{n}=\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}e^{\prime}_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}e_{n},

where
\lambda_{n}\neq 0
. Therefore,
(p-\lambda_{n})e_{n}=\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}e^{\prime}_{n-1}+(p-1)e^{\prime}_{n}
and hence

e_{n}
can be represented as a linear combination of
e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}
. This easily implies (i).

The set
E^{\prime}
is linearly independent, because the decomposition of every
e^{\prime}_{n}
in
E

contains
e_{n}
with nonzero coefficient, which does not occur in the decompositions of
e^{\prime}_{k}
with
k<n
. Since

E
is a basis of
G
, it follows from (i) that
E^{\prime}
spans
G
. This implies (ii).

Property (iii) follows directly from the definition of
e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}
, because

\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{k}e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}
equals a linear
combination of
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n_{k}}
(since so does each
e^{\prime}_{n_{i}}
), and the coefficient of
e_{n_{k}}

in this linear combination is nonzero (since
e_{n_{k}}
occurs in the decomposition of
e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}
in

E
with nonzero coefficient and does not occur in the decompositions of
e^{\prime}_{k}
with
k<n
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
w=\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{i_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{n}e^{\prime}_{i_{n}}
,

where
n,i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,

i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{n}
,
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Then

\|\mu e^{\prime}_{i_{n-k}}\|\leq p^{k}\cdot 2^{k}\cdot\|w\|\qquad\text{for each}\quad k=0,\dots,n-1\quad\text{and
any}\quad\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. Note that, for any
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x\in G
, we have
\|\mu x\|\leq p\cdot\|x\|
. Thus, the required inequality for
k=0
follows from property (iii) of
E^{\prime}
. Suppose that

0<l<n
and the inequality holds for any
k<l
and
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Applying the triangle inequality for
norms, we obtain

\|(p-\lambda_{n-l+1})e^{\prime}_{i_{n-l+1}}+(p-\lambda_{n-l+2})e^{\prime}_{i_{n-l+2}}+\dots+(p-\lambda_{n})e^{\prime}_{i_{n}}\|\leq\\
\leq p^{l-1}\cdot(2^{l-1}+2^{l-2}+\dots+1)\cdot\|w\|=p^{l-1}\cdot(2^{l}-1)\cdot\|w\|.

Noting that

\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{i_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{l}e^{\prime}_{i_{l}}=\\
=w+(p-\lambda_{n-l+1})e^{\prime}_{i_{n-l+1}}+(p-\lambda_{n-l+2})e^{\prime}_{i_{n-l+2}}+\dots+(p-\lambda_{n})e^{\prime}_{i_{n}}

and
applying the triangle inequality once more, we arrive at

\|\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{2}e^{\prime}_{i_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{l}e^{\prime}_{i_{l}}\|\leq\|w\|+p^{l-1}\cdot(2^{l}-1)\cdot\|w\|\leq p^{l-1}\cdot 2^{l}\|w\|.

By property (iii) we have
\|e^{\prime}_{i_{l}}\|\leq p^{l-1}\cdot 2^{l}\|w\|
and
\|\mu e^{\prime}_{i_{l}}\|\leq p^{l}\cdot 2^{l}\|w\|
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Each Cauchy sequence in
E^{\prime}
converges to
{\mathbf{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
(e^{\prime}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence. Then, for
any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|e^{\prime}_{n_{k^{\prime}}}+(p-1)e^{\prime}_{n_{k^{\prime\prime}}}\|<\varepsilon
for any
k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\geq N
. But if
n_{k^{\prime\prime}}>n_{k^{\prime}}
, then

\|e^{\prime}_{n_{k^{\prime\prime}}}\|\leq\|e^{\prime}_{n_{k^{\prime}}}+(p-1)e^{\prime}_{n_{k^{\prime\prime}}}\|
by property (iii) of
E^{\prime}
.
Therefore,
\|e^{\prime}_{n_{k}}\|<\varepsilon
for any
k>N
.
∎

Let
\max\colon G\to\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
be the function defined by
\max({\mathbf{0}})=0
and

\max(\lambda_{1}e^{\prime}_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{k}e^{\prime}_{n_{k}})=n_{k}\quad\text{for $n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}$ and $\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}$}.

In particular,
\max(e^{\prime}_{n})=n
for each
e^{\prime}_{n}
.

If
G
is nondiscrete, then there exists a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise distinct
elements of
g
converging to
{\mathbf{0}}
. We may assume without loss of generality that the sequence

\bigl(\max(g_{n})\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
strictly increases and that
\|g_{n}\|<1/(4p)^{n}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
k_{n}=\max(g_{n})
and
a_{n}=e^{\prime}_{k_{n}}
. Then
\|a_{n}\|<1/(4p)^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
by property
(iii). Consider any linear combination
w=\lambda_{1}a_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{m}a_{n_{m}}
, where
n_{1}<\dots n_{m}

(and hence
n_{i}\geq i
for
i\leq m
). For every
l<m
, we have
w=w^{\prime}+w^{\prime\prime}
and
\|w^{\prime}\|\leq\|w\|+\|(p-1)w^{\prime\prime}\|
,
where
w^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}a_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{l}a_{n_{l}}
and
w^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{l+1}a_{n_{l+1}}+\dots+\lambda_{m}a_{n_{m}}
. Clearly,

\|(p-1)w^{\prime\prime}\|\leq p\cdot(\|a_{l+1}\|+\dots+\|a_{m}\|)<1/(4p)^{l}

and
\|w^{\prime}\|\leq\|w\|+1/(4p)^{l}
. By Lemma
1
we have

\|a_{n_{i}}\|\leq(2p)^{l}\cdot\|w^{\prime}\|
for each
i\leq l
. Therefore, if
\|w\|<1/(4p)^{l}
, then

\|a_{n_{i}}\|\leq(2p)^{l}/(4p)^{l}+1/(4p)^{l}<1/2^{l-1}
for
i\leq l
. We also have
\|a_{n_{i}}\|<1/(4p)^{i}<1/2^{l-1}

for
i>l
by the definition of the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, if
\varepsilon>0
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
1/2^{l-1}<\varepsilon
, then
\|a_{n_{i}}\|<\varepsilon
,
i\leq m
, whenever
w=\lambda_{1}a_{n_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{m}a_{n_{m}}
and
\|w\|<1/(4p)^{l}
. This means that the independent set
\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is topologically independent.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Any infinite Abelian topological group of prime exponent contains an infinite maximally almost periodic subgroup.

Proof.

Let
G
be an infinite Abelian topological group of prime exponent
p
with zero element
{\mathbf{0}}
, and let
H
be
an arbitrary countable subgroup of
G
. The network weight of any countable topological space is countable, and
any topological group of countable network weight admits a not strictly coarser second-countable Hausdorff group
topology
[
1
, Theorem 2]
. Let
\tau
be a second-countable Hausdorff group topology on
H

coarser than the induced topology. By Proposition
1

(H,\tau)
contains an infinite topologically
independent set
A=\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Since
A
is independent, it
follows that the subgroup
\langle A\rangle
of
G
generated by
A
is isomorphic to the direct sum

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\langle a_{n}\rangle\subset\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\langle a_{n}\rangle
of the finite
groups
\langle a_{n}\rangle
. According to
[
6
, Proposition 4.7]
, the product topology on
\langle A\rangle

associated with
A
is coarser than the topology induced from
(H,\tau)
; hence it is coarser than that induced
from
G
. According to Remark
1
,
\langle A\rangle
is maximally almost periodic.
∎
