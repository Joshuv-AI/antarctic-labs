Interpretation of Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) as an S S -Wave B 1 ​ B ¯ B_{1}\bar{B} – B 1 ​ B ¯ ∗ B_{1}\bar{B}^{*} Molecular State
Qing Lu, Cai Cheng, Yin Huang

Abstract
Although heavy-quark symmetry implies a B 1 ​ B ¯ B_{1}\bar{B} molecular partner of the D 1 ​ D ¯ D_{1}\bar{D} molecule, no such state has been observed experimentally to date. In this work, we propose that the experimentally observed Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) may be a candidate for such a molecular state, although it may also contain a B 1 ​ B ¯ ∗ B_{1}\bar{B}^{*} component. To explore whether the Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) can be interpreted as an S S -wave B 1 ​ B ¯ B_{1}\bar{B} – B 1 ​ B ¯ ∗ B_{1}\bar{B}^{*} molecule, we calculate its strong decay widths within this molecular scenario using the compositeness condition and effective Lagrangians. The coupling of the Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) to its constituents B 1 B_{1} and B ¯ ( ∗ ) \bar{B}^{(*)} is determined by fitting the available experimental data on Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) → e + ​ e − \Upsilon(11020)\to e^{+}e^{-} and Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) → χ b ​ J ​ π ​ π ​ π \Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\pi\pi\pi decays. With the extracted coupling, we compute the partial decay widths of the Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) into B ( s ) ( ∗ ) ​ B ¯ ( s ) ( ∗ ) B^{(*)}_{(s)}\bar{B}^{(*)}_{(s)} , π ​ π ​ Υ ​ ( n ​ S ) \pi\pi\Upsilon(nS) , π ​ π ​ h b ​ ( n ​ P ) \pi\pi h_{b}(nP) , and π ​ π ​ π ​ χ b ​ 1 \pi\pi\pi\chi_{b1} via hadronic loops, as well as the three-body B ∗ ​ π ​ B ¯ ( ∗ ) B^{*}\pi\bar{B}^{(*)} decays via tree-level diagrams. Our results suggest that the Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) can indeed be interpreted as an S S -wave B 1 ​ B ¯ B_{1}\bar{B} – B 1 ​ B ¯ ∗ B_{1}\bar{B}^{*} molecule with a dominant B 1 ​ B ¯ B_{1}\bar{B} component, whose main decay channel is B s ∗ ​ B ¯ ∗ B_{s}^{*}\bar{B}^{*} . Moreover, the partial widths for Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) → π ​ π ​ Υ ​ ( n ​ S ) \Upsilon(11020)\to\pi\pi\Upsilon(nS) and Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) → π ​ π ​ h b ​ ( n ​ P ) \Upsilon(11020)\to\pi\pi h_{b}(nP) are found to be only a few eV. In contrast, the widths for Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) → π ​ π ​ π ​ χ b ​ J \Upsilon(11020)\to\pi\pi\pi\chi_{bJ} are considerably larger, with π ​ π ​ π ​ χ b ​ 1 \pi\pi\pi\chi_{b1} reaching up to 0.167 MeV, while the yet-unobserved π ​ π ​ π ​ χ b ​ 0 \pi\pi\pi\chi_{b0} channel could be as large as 0.754 keV. These distinctive decay patterns could serve as experimental signatures of the molecular nature of the Υ ⁡ ( 11020 ) \Upsilon(11020) , and their confirmation would provide a test of the applicability of heavy-quark symmetry.

Introduction

With the progress of experimental studies, several hundred hadrons have been discovered to date

Among them, many can be interpreted within the conventional quark model as mesons composed of a quark–antiquark pair

This discovery, together with the subsequent observation of many other exotic candidates, has raised fundamental questions about the

QCD permits hadrons to realize a variety of configurations as long as they satisfy the color-singlet condition,

Consequently, uncovering the configurations of these exotic hadrons has become a central challenge in contemporary hadron physics.

IV
Summary

In this work, we investigate whether the experimentally observed
\Upsilon(11020)
can be interpreted as an
S
-wave
B_{1}\bar{B}
–
B_{1}\bar{B}^{*}
molecular state. Using the compositeness condition and effective Lagrangians, we calculate the strong decay widths of
\Upsilon(11020)
within the molecular framework. By fitting existing experimental data, including
\Upsilon(11020)\to e^{+}e^{-}
and
\Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\pi\pi\pi
decays, we extract the couplings of
\Upsilon(11020)
to its constituents
B_{1}
and
\bar{B}^{(*)}
.

With the extracted coupling, we calculate the partial decay widths of the
\Upsilon(11020)
into
B^{(*)}_{(s)}\bar{B}^{(*)}_{(s)}
,
\pi\pi\Upsilon(nS)
,
\pi\pi h_{b}(nP)
, and
\pi\pi\pi\chi_{b1}
via hadronic loops, as well as the three-body
B^{*}\pi\bar{B}^{(*)}
decays through tree-level diagrams (see Fig.
1
). The results indicate that the
\Upsilon(11020)
is predominantly a
B_{1}\bar{B}
molecular state, accounting for approximately 75.4% of its total composition. Its primary decay channel is the strange
B_{s}^{*}\bar{B}^{*}_{s}
mode, which has not yet been observed experimentally. Notably, the partial widths for
\pi\pi\Upsilon(nS)
and
\pi\pi h_{b}(nP)
are only a few eV:
8.242
,
29.715
, and
161.592
eV for
\pi\pi\Upsilon(1S)
,
\pi\pi\Upsilon(2S)
, and
\pi\pi\Upsilon(3S)
, respectively, and
37.281
eV and
0.117
keV for
\pi\pi h_{b}(1P)
and
\pi\pi h_{b}(2P)
. In contrast, the
\pi\pi\pi\chi_{bJ}
channels, especially
\pi\pi\pi\chi_{b1}
, are significantly enhanced, reaching up to
0.167
MeV.
The yet-unobserved
\pi\pi\pi\chi_{b0}
channel could be as large as 0.754 keV. These features differ substantially from the predictions of conventional quark models and provide a clear experimental signature for the molecular interpretation of
\Upsilon(11020)
.

Such distinctive decay patterns could be probed at LHCb and other experimental facilities. Once experimentally confirmed, they would further deepen our understanding of heavy-quark symmetry.

Appendix A
Decay Width Formula for
\Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\pi\pi\pi

In the narrow-width approximation (NWA),
\Gamma_{X}\ll M_{X}
(valid for the
\omega
meson)
and neglecting vertex dependence on the virtuality of
X
, the cascade decay factorizes.
For the chain

\displaystyle\Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\,\omega\to\chi_{bJ}\,\pi\pi\pi,

(40)

let
A\equiv\Upsilon(11020)
,
X\equiv\omega
(mass
M_{X}
, width
\Gamma_{X}
),
B\equiv\chi_{bJ}
,
and
n
-body final state (
n=3
) with invariant mass
M_{n}^{2}\equiv(p_{\pi_{1}}+p_{\pi_{2}}+p_{\pi_{3}})^{2}
.
The total amplitude via
X
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{A\to B+1\cdots n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{A\to BX}(M_{n})\frac{1}{M_{n}^{2}-M_{X}^{2}+iM_{X}\Gamma_{X}}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{M}_{X\to 1\cdots n}(M_{n}),

(41)

and the
(n+1)
-body width is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A\to B,1\cdots n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2M_{A}}\int d\Phi_{n+1}\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal{M}_{A\to B,1\cdots n}|^{2}.

(42)

Decomposing the phase space,

\displaystyle d\Phi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=d\Phi_{2}(A\to BX)\frac{dM_{n}^{2}}{2\pi}d\Phi_{n}(X\to 1\cdots n),

(43)

yields

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{A\to B,1\cdots n}}{dM_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{A\to BX}(M_{n})\,\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{M_{n}\Gamma_{X\to 1\cdots n}(M_{n})}{(M_{n}^{2}-M_{X}^{2})^{2}+M_{X}^{2}\Gamma_{X}^{2}}.

(44)

Changing variables to
M_{n}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{A\to B,1\cdots n}}{dM_{n}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{A\to BX}(M_{n})\,\frac{2M_{n}^{2}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{X\to 1\cdots n}(M_{n})}{(M_{n}^{2}-M_{X}^{2})^{2}+M_{X}^{2}\Gamma_{X}^{2}}.

(45)

Under the NWA and assuming weak
M_{n}
dependence, evaluating at
M_{n}=M_{X}
and using

\displaystyle\int\frac{dM_{n}^{2}}{\pi}\frac{M_{X}\Gamma_{X}}{(M_{n}^{2}-M_{X}^{2})^{2}+M_{X}^{2}\Gamma_{X}^{2}}

\displaystyle=1,

(46)

gives the factorized width

\displaystyle\Gamma(A\to B,1\cdots n)

\displaystyle\approx\Gamma(A\to BX)\frac{\Gamma(X\to 1\cdots n)}{\Gamma_{X}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(A\to BX)\,{\rm Br}(X\to 1\cdots n),

(47)

and for the specific case,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\pi\pi\pi)

\displaystyle\approx\Gamma(\Upsilon(11020)\to\chi_{bJ}\omega)\,{\rm Br}(\omega\to 3\pi).

(48)
