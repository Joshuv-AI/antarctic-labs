Identification of Estrogen-Related Biomarkers in Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease Through Machine Learning and Single-Cell Analysis
Authors: Jiajing Chao, Zhangmin Tan, Yiwen Zhang, Qian Gao, Zhe Li, Chengfang Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
INTRODUCTION:Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly known as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), is a prevalent chronic liver disease linked to metabolic risk factors. Sex hormones, particularly estrogen, seem to influence MASLD development, as premenopausal women are relatively protected. The aim of this study was to identify estrogen-related biomarkers of MASLD using integrated bioinformatics, machine learning, and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) approaches.METHODS:Six public transcriptomic data sets (5 training, 1 validation) were analyzed to find differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in MASLD vs controls. Estrogen-related genes (ERGs) were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) and intersected with DEGs to define estrogen-associated DEGs (DEERGs). Machine learning algorithms—Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator regression and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)—were applied to select key feature genes, which were then validated in an independent cohort. Immune cell infiltration was evaluated by cell-type identification by estimating relative subsets of RNA transcripts (CIBERSORT) and single-sample gene set enrichment analysis. Consensus clustering based on feature genes defined molecular subtypes. In addition, a human liver scRNA-seq data set was analyzed to map cell-type-specific expression and pathway activity. A competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions was constructed for the diagnostic genes.RESULTS:Fourteen DEERGs were identified, and 2 diagnostic biomarkers—IGFBP2 and P4HA1—showed high diagnostic accuracy. Both genes were associated with immune infiltration and defined 2 estrogen-related molecular subtypes with distinct immune microenvironment features. Single-cell analysis localized IGFBP2 and P4HA1 to hepatocytes, implicating estrogen-related regulation in metabolic and fibrotic remodeling. CellChat analysis revealed weakened hepatocyte-centered signaling in MASLD. The ceRNA network suggested that multiple lncRNAs (e.g., NEAT1, MALAT1, XIST) may modulate these biomarkers through shared miRNAs.DISCUSSION:Through integrated multicohort analysis, we identified IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as novel estrogen-related tissue biomarkers of MASLD. Preliminary exploration suggests their potential detectability in peripheral blood, though large-scale tissue and protein-level validations are required. Our findings provide insight into sex hormone-linked mechanisms in MASLD and propose candidate molecular targets for improved risk stratification and therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly known as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), has emerged as the most prevalent chronic liver disease worldwide (1). It encompasses a spectrum from simple steatosis to metabolic steatohepatitis (MASH), which can progress to fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (2). Over the past decades, the global prevalence of MASLD has risen to ∼30% and continues to increase in parallel with the epidemics of obesity and type 2 diabetes (3). Notably, MASLD is now recognized as the fastest-growing cause of hepatocellular carcinoma and a leading indication for liver transplantation (4). These trends highlight an urgent need to elucidate pathogenic mechanisms and identify reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis and risk stratification, as no pharmacotherapy has yet been approved for MASLD/nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) management (5).

Sex differences in MASLD incidence and outcomes suggest a key role for sex hormones in its pathogenesis (6). Epidemiologically, men exhibit higher MASLD prevalence and tend to develop more severe fibrosis than premenopausal women, but this female advantage diminishes after menopause (7). Estrogens are thought to confer protection against hepatic steatosis and fibrosis: Experimental and clinical data indicate that estrogen sufficiency attenuates lipogenesis, inflammation, and fibrogenesis in the liver (8). For example, premenopausal women (with higher estradiol levels) have significantly lower MASLD and MASH risk compared with age-matched men or postmenopausal women (9). Conversely, states of estrogen deficiency or relative androgen excess are associated with increased MASLD incidence. Women with polycystic ovary syndrome—a condition of hyperandrogenism and low sex hormone-binding globulin—show higher rates of NAFLD/NASH than body mass index-matched controls (10). Similarly, postmenopausal status is a risk factor of MASLD progression, and longer duration of estrogen deficiency correlates with greater fibrosis severity (11). Mechanistically, estrogens through estrogen receptor-α (ERα) in hepatocytes suppress lipogenic genes (e.g., SREBP1c, fatty acid synthase [FAS]) and promote fatty acid oxidation and very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) export, while also exerting anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects in the liver microenvironment (12). In animal models, loss of estrogen signaling leads to worsened steatosis, oxidative stress, and hepatic collagen accumulation, whereas estrogen supplementation or estrogen receptor (ER) agonists can ameliorate metabolic liver injury (13,14). These observations underscore the hormone-liver axis as an important modulator of MASLD pathogenesis and suggest that estrogen-related genes and pathways might yield valuable biomarkers or therapeutic targets for MASLD (8).

In this study, we applied a multiomics strategy combining bulk transcriptomics, machine learning, immune infiltration profiling, molecular subtyping, and single-cell analysis to identify estrogen-related biomarkers in MASLD. We further validated their diagnostic performance, investigated their immune associations, and predict upstream regulatory noncoding RNAs through a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network construction. By integrating bulk and single-cell analyses, our aim was to provide a comprehensive view of how estrogen-related transcriptional dysregulation contributes to MASLD pathogenesis, and to propose novel biomarkers that could improve disease diagnosis or enable personalized therapeutic strategies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Data collection

Six transcriptomic data sets related to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), including GSE48452 (control = 14, MASLD = 32), GSE63067 (control = 7, MASLD = 11), GSE66676 (control = 34, MASLD = 33), GSE89632 (control = 24, MASLD = 39), GSE107231 (control = 5, MASLD = 5), and GSE164760 (control = 6, MASLD = 74). Five data sets (GSE48452, GSE63067, GSE66676, GSE89632, and GSE107231) were merged into a training cohort after removing batch effects using the “sva” R package, whereas GSE164760 served as an independent validation cohort. For single-cell analysis, the single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data set GSE174748 (15) containing human liver samples from patients with MASLD and healthy controls was obtained from the GEO database. To identify estrogen-related genes (ERGs), the keyword “estrogen” was searched in the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). This search strategy systematically captured gene sets reflecting both direct targets of estrogen receptors and genes involved in broader estrogen-responsive signaling pathways (e.g., Hallmark Estrogen Response, gene ontology [GO] regulation of estrogen receptor activity), yielding 8,374 ERGs.

Differential expression and functional analysis

Differential expression analysis between MASLD and control samples was performed using the “limma” R package. Genes with an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) greater than 0.585 and an adjusted P value of <0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed. The intersection between the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and the previously identified ERGs was obtained to define the differentially expressed estrogen-related genes (DEERGs). Pairwise Pearson correlation coefficients among DEERGs were calculated and displayed as a heatmap to assess their coexpression relationships. Furthermore, GO enrichment analysis was conducted using the “clusterProfiler” package to identify biological processes and functional pathways associated with these DEERGs.

Machine learning-based feature selection and diagnostic model construction

To identify robust diagnostic biomarkers for MASLD, DEERGs were analyzed using 2 complementary machine learning approaches: Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). LASSO was selected for its superior dimensionality reduction capabilities, effectively handling multicollinearity among coexpressed genes by penalizing absolute coefficients. XGBoost was chosen to capture potential nonlinear relationships between gene expression and disease status while providing reliable feature importance ranking. LASSO regression was performed with the “glmnet” package to minimize model overfitting, and the optimal penalty parameter (λ) was determined through 10-fold cross-validation (α = 1). The XGBoost model was trained using the “xgboost” package, with classification labels (MASLD vs control) as the binary outcome. The hyperparameters were set as follows: maximum tree depth = 5, learning rate (η) = 0.3, number of iterations = 25, and objective function = binary logistic regression. After model optimization, genes with a feature importance (gain) > 0.15 were considered significant contributors. The intersection of genes from both algorithms yielded the final diagnostic feature genes.

To ensure generalizability, their diagnostic performance was validated using an independent data set (GSE164760), and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted using the “pROC” package. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated to quantify diagnostic efficiency, and gene expression levels were further compared between MASLD and normal samples to assess consistency across cohorts.

Immune infiltration analysis

The immune infiltration profile of MASLD was characterized using the cell-type identification by estimating relative subsets of RNA transcripts (CIBERSORT) algorithm integrated within the “IOBR” R package, which estimates the relative abundance of 22 immune cell subsets in each sample. Differences in immune cell proportions between MASLD and control groups were visualized as boxplots. To further assess immune activity, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed with the “GSVA” package to calculate immune cell enrichment scores and immune-related functional pathway activity. These analyses collectively revealed alterations in the hepatic immune microenvironment associated with MASLD.

Molecular subtype identification based on estrogen-related genes

To explore molecular heterogeneity associated with ERGs in MASLD, consensus clustering was performed using the “ConsensusClusterPlus” R package based on the expression levels of the 2 diagnostic feature genes. The optimal cluster number (k) was selected according to the cumulative distribution function curve and delta area analysis, yielding 2 stable molecular subtypes. To further investigate immunological differences between the subtypes, immune cell infiltration and immune-related functional signatures were assessed using ssGSEA and the CIBERSORT algorithm implemented in the “IOBR” package.

Single-gene gene set enrichment analysis

To further explore the biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of each diagnostic feature gene, single-gene gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed. This method evaluates the association between individual gene expression and predefined biological pathways. GO and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) gene sets were retrieved from the molecular signatures database (MSigDB, v2025.1) (c5.go.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt and c2.cp.kegg_legacy.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt). Enrichment analysis was conducted using the “GSEA” function in the “clusterProfiler” R package, enabling the identification of biological processes and signaling pathways significantly correlated with each key gene.

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

The scRNA-seq data set of MASLD was obtained from the GEO database and analyzed using the “Seurat” R package. Cells with <500 unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) or >20% mitochondrial gene expression were excluded. Only cells expressing between 300 and 7,000 genes, and genes detected in ≥3 cells, were retained. After quality control, 19,193 cells (9,816 from healthy and 9,377 from MASLD samples) were included.

Data were log-normalized, and 2,000 highly variable genes were identified using FindVariableFeatures. Dimensionality reduction was performed by principal component analysis, followed by batch correction with the “Harmony” package to mitigate intersample variability. Uniform manifold approximation and projection was used for visualization, and clusters were annotated based on canonical markers obtained from the CellMarker database.

To associate single-cell populations with disease phenotypes, the “Scissor” package was employed using bulk RNA-seq-derived MASLD phenotypic information. Estrogen-related gene module scores were calculated for each cell using the “AddModuleScore” function and visualized across cell types. Intercellular communication and signaling network alterations between MASLD and control groups were investigated using the “CellChat” package.

Construction of the ceRNA network

To investigate the potential post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms underlying the identified diagnostic genes, miRNAs interacting with these genes were predicted using the “multiMiR” R package, which integrates data from the TargetScan database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/). lncRNA-miRNA interaction data were downloaded from the Encyclopedia of RNA Interactomes (ENCORI) database (The Encyclopedia of RNA Interactomes, https://rnasysu.com/encori/), and interactions with clipExpNum > 15 were retained as high-confidence pairs. The resulting lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory relationships were assembled into a ceRNA network and visualized using Cytoscape (version 3.10.2) to illustrate potential interaction axes among noncoding RNAs and diagnostic genes.

Preliminary pilot exploration of circulating biomarkers via reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction

While our scRNA-seq analysis localized the diagnostic genes primarily to hepatocytes, we conducted a preliminary pilot study to explore whether the transcript levels of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 could be detected in peripheral blood, reflecting their potential as noninvasive circulating biomarkers (liquid biopsy). Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR) was conducted on peripheral whole blood samples collected from a small cohort of 10 participants (5 MASLD patients and 5 healthy controls). Total RNA was extracted from whole blood samples using TRIzol reagent (Ambion, Austin, TX), following the manufacturer's protocol. Reverse transcription was performed using the First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) to synthesize cDNA from purified RNA. Subsequently, quantitative PCR amplification was performed using 2× Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as the internal reference control. Specific sequences for IGFBP2 and P4HA1 are listed in Table 1. All reactions were performed in triplicate, and gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. This pilot experiment was intended as a proof-of-concept for peripheral detectability, complementing the primary validation performed on the large independent tissue data set (GSE164760).

RESULTS

Identification of estrogen-related differential genes

Differential gene expression analysis between MASLD and control liver samples identified 19 DEGs using thresholds of |log2FC| > 0.585 and adjusted P < 0.05 (see Supplementary Table S1, Supplementary Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/CTG/B550). To determine estrogen-related alterations, the intersection of the 19 DEGs with 8,374 ERGs from the MSigDB database yielded 14 DEERGs (Figure 1a). The correlation heatmap among the 14 DEERGs revealed strong coexpression relationships (Figure 1b). Expression profiling demonstrated that most DEERGs were significantly dysregulated in MASLD compared with normal controls (Figure 1c). To explore their biological relevance, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted. The DEERGs were predominantly enriched in biological processes related to skeletal-muscle cell differentiation, miRNA transcription, peptide-hormone response, and regulation of miRNA metabolic processes, whereas KEGG pathways were mainly associated with mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling, growth-hormone synthesis and secretion, and hormone-related cancers such as thyroid and breast cancer (Figure 1d). These findings suggest that estrogen-associated transcriptional changes in MASLD are tightly linked to hormone-responsive and signal-transduction pathways involved in hepatic metabolic regulation.

Construction and validation of the diagnostic model for MASLD

To identify potential diagnostic biomarkers, the DEERGs were subjected to 2 complementary machine-learning algorithms. First, LASSO regression was performed to reduce dimensionality and avoid overfitting, yielding 7 candidate feature genes at the optimal penalty parameter λ determined by 10-fold cross-validation (Figure 2a,b). Next, the XGBoost model was applied to rank gene importance according to feature gain. Genes with a gain score > 0.15 were retained as high-impact predictors (Figure 2c). Integration of the 2 algorithms identified IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as the most robust diagnostic feature genes (Figure 2d). To ensure their diagnostic reliability, we further evaluated the individual predictive performance of these genes in both the training and validation cohorts. Genes with an AUC value below 0.7 were excluded, yielding 2 high-confidence diagnostic biomarkers, IGFBP2 and P4HA1 (see Supplementary Figure S1, Supplementary Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/CTG/B549). ROC analysis demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance of the 2-gene model in the training cohort, with AUC values of 0.762 for IGFBP2, 0.768 for P4HA1, and 0.798 for the combined model (Figure 2e). In the independent validation data set (GSE164760), both genes maintained high discriminative ability (AUC = 0.858 for IGFBP2, 0.804 for P4HA1, and 0.881 for the combined model) (Figure 2f). Consistent with these findings, the expression of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 was significantly lower in MASLD liver tissues compared with controls in both the training and validation cohorts (Figure 2g).

Furthermore, to provide an intuitive clinical tool for predicting the likelihood of MASLD, a nomogram was developed based on the 2 diagnostic biomarkers, IGFBP2 and P4HA1 (Figure 3a). Model calibration and clinical utility were subsequently evaluated. The decision curve analysis further confirmed that the two-gene nomogram provided a substantial net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities (Figure 3b), outperforming both individual predictors and default “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies. The calibration curve demonstrated excellent agreement between predicted and observed probabilities (Figure 3c), indicating strong model calibration without evidence of overfitting. Collectively, these results highlight that integrating IGFBP2 and P4HA1 into a nomogram enhances diagnostic interpretability and clinical applicability for MASLD risk assessment.

Immune landscape associated with diagnostic genes

To elucidate the immune microenvironmental differences between MASLD and control samples, the CIBERSORT algorithm was used to estimate the relative fractions of 22 immune cell subsets. As shown in Figure 4a, both groups exhibited complex immune compositions; however, the proportions of several immune cell types differed significantly. Specifically, MASLD samples displayed increased infiltration of M1 macrophages and T cells CD4 memory resting, whereas plasma cells, activated natural killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, and resting dendritic cells were relatively reduced (Figure 4b). ssGSEA further confirmed that MASLD livers exhibited higher cytolytic activity, inflammation-promoting, and macrophage, accompanied by reduced Th1 compared with normal controls (Figure 4c). To investigate the immunological relevance of the diagnostic biomarkers, a correlation heatmap was constructed linking IGFBP2 and P4HA1 expression levels with immune-cell infiltration profiles. IGFBP2 expression showed a significant negative correlation with NK cells resting and T cells gamma delta but a positive association with Mast cells activated, whereas P4HA1 exhibited positive correlation with mast cells activated and neutrophils (Figure 4d). These findings indicate that estrogen-related biomarkers are intricately associated with hepatic immune remodeling in MASLD.

Functional enrichment analysis of diagnostic genes

To explore the potential biological functions and signaling mechanisms associated with the diagnostic biomarkers IGFBP2 and P4HA1, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed. GO analysis indicated that genes positively correlated with IGFBP2 were mainly involved in processes related to collagen containing extracellular matrix, acyl-CoA metabolic process, and external encapsulating structure organization, whereas negatively correlated genes were enriched in organophosphate biosynthetic process (Figure 5a). Similarly, P4HA1-associated genes were significantly enriched in ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, mRNA processing, and regulation of translation (Figure 5b). Subsequent GSEA provided additional insight into signaling pathway activation patterns. For IGFBP2, the top enriched KEGG pathways included base excision repair, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, and Janus kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling (Figure 5c). For P4HA1, enriched pathways were predominantly associated with complement and coagulation cascades, spliceosome, and transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) signaling pathway (Figure 5d).

Identification of estrogen-related molecular subtypes in MASLD

To further explore molecular heterogeneity among MASLD samples, consensus clustering was performed based on the expression profiles of the 2 diagnostic genes (IGFBP2 and P4HA1). The cumulative distribution function and delta area curves indicated that the optimal clustering stability was achieved at k = 2 (Figure 6a). Accordingly, all MASLD samples were classified into 2 distinct molecular subtypes, designated as Cluster 1 and Cluster 2 (Figure 6b). Boxplot analysis revealed significant differences in the expression levels of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 between the 2 subtypes, with both genes showing higher expression in Cluster 2 (Figure 6c). Principal component analysis further confirmed a clear separation between the 2 clusters (Figure 6d).

The immune infiltration landscape, evaluated using the CIBERSORT algorithm, demonstrated distinct immune compositions between the subtypes (Figure 6e). Correlation analysis revealed close associations between IGFBP2/P4HA1 expression and specific immune-cell subsets, including T cells follicular helper and monocytes (Figure 6f). Specifically, Cluster 2 exhibited higher proportions of monocytes and lower proportions of M2 macrophages compared with Cluster 1 (Figure 6g). Moreover, ssGSEA analysis revealed significant differences in immune functional activity between the subtypes: Cluster 2 showed increased immune-related scores in chemokine receptor signaling, T-cell costimulation, Tfh, and Tregs, whereas Cluster 1 displayed higher NK-cell and cytolytic activity scores (Figure 6h).

These findings indicate that the 2 estrogen-related subtypes of MASLD possess distinct gene expression and immune-infiltration characteristics, suggesting potential differences in immune-metabolic regulation across subgroups.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis of ERGs in MASLD

To further dissect the cellular heterogeneity and tissue-level localization of ERGs in MASLD, scRNA-seq data from liver samples were analyzed. After quality control and batch correction, a total of 19,193 cells were included, comprising 9,816 from healthy controls and 9,377 from patients with MASLD. Uniform manifold approximation and projection revealed 8 major cell populations, including hepatocytes, macrophages, hepatic stellate cells (HSCs), endothelial cells, cholangiocytes, B cells, T cells, and NK cells (Figure 7a). Each cluster was annotated using canonical marker genes (Figure 7b). The distribution of these marker genes across individual single-cell clusters, demonstrating the correspondence between clusters and annotated hepatic cell types, is presented in Supplementary Figure S2 (see Supplementary Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/CTG/B549). The cell-type composition differed between MASLD and control samples, with hepatocytes and macrophages representing the dominant cell types (Figure 7c). Hepatocytes were characterized by the abundant expression of albumin (ALB), apolipoprotein E (APOE), APOB, and CYP3A4; macrophages by MARCO, C1QA, CD163, and C1QB. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses across clusters indicated that HSCs were enriched in pathways associated with extracellular matrix structural constituent and cadherin binding, whereas hepatocytes were enriched in complement and coagulation cascades, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling, and drug metabolism pathways (Figure 7d,e). To evaluate the overall estrogen-related transcriptional activity, an estrogen score was calculated for each cell using the AddModuleScore function. The score was highest in hepatocytes, indicating that these 2 cell types exhibit the strongest estrogen-related gene expression signatures (Figure 7f). Expression analysis showed that P4HA1 and IGFBP2 were broadly expressed across hepatocytes, and comparison between groups revealed decreased expression of both genes in MASLD relative to controls (Figure 7g).

Cell-cell communication analysis reveals disrupted intercellular signaling in MASLD

To further investigate alterations in intercellular communication associated with MASLD, CellChat analysis was performed to infer ligand-receptor interactions among major hepatic cell types. As shown in Figure 8a, both the number and overall strength of inferred interactions were markedly reduced in MASLD compared with healthy liver tissue, suggesting a global attenuation of cell-cell signaling activity. Network visualization revealed that in both groups, hepatocytes and endothelial cells served as central communication hubs. However, in MASLD, the graph was sparser, with fewer and thinner edges, indicating reduced connectivity among cell types. Besides, in the differential networks, healthy livers showed markedly higher numbers and stronger interactions among cell types—especially between hepatocytes, endothelial cells, HSC, and B cells—compared with MASLD (Figure 8b). Quantitative analysis of incoming and outgoing interaction strength demonstrated that hepatocytes exhibited the strongest outgoing signaling activity in both conditions, while endothelial cells and cholangiocytes displayed the highest incoming strength (Figure 8c). Further analysis of ligand-receptor pairs identified substantial remodeling of communication pathways between hepatocytes and other cell types (Figure 8d). In healthy livers, interactions such as nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT)-INSR and PLG-PARD3 were dominant.

Construction of the ceRNA regulatory network

To further explore the post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of the 2 diagnostic biomarkers, a ceRNA network was constructed. Using the multiMiR and ENCORI databases, miRNAs targeting P4HA1 and IGFBP2 were predicted, and the corresponding interacting lncRNAs were subsequently identified. As shown in Figure 9, the final network consisted of 2 mRNAs (P4HA1 and IGFBP2), 10 miRNAs (hsa-miR-30e-5p, hsa-miR-122-5p, hsa-miR-126-3p, hsa-miR-145-5p, hsa-miR-204-5p, hsa-miR-320a, hsa-miR-335-5p, hsa-miR-491-3p, hsa-miR-877-3p, hsa-miR-941), and 9 lncRNAs (NEAT1, MALAT1, X inactive specific transcript (XIST), KCNQ1OT1, SNHG1, SNHG14, OIP5-AS1, PWAR5, and MIR3142HG). This network suggests that these lncRNAs may competitively bind shared miRNAs to modulate the expression of P4HA1 and IGFBP2, potentially influencing estrogen-related metabolic and fibrotic pathways in MASLD.

Pilot validation of circulating IGFBP2 and P4HA1 expression by RT-qPCR

To explore the feasibility of detecting these hepatocyte-enriched markers noninvasively, we performed a pilot RT-qPCR analysis of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 expression in peripheral blood samples from a small cohort of patients with MASLD and healthy controls (N = 10). Interestingly, consistent with the bioinformatics predictions derived from liver tissues, the mRNA levels of both IGFBP2 and P4HA1 were significantly downregulated in the peripheral blood of the MASLD group compared with controls (P < 0.01, Figure 10). Although blood mRNA primarily originates from circulating leukocytes and cell-free RNA rather than direct liver tissue, this preliminary result suggests that the systemic suppression of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 might reflect hepatic states, highlighting their potential translational value as noninvasive circulating biomarkers.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we identified and validated 2 estrogen-related genes, IGFBP2 and P4HA1, as potential diagnostic biomarkers for MASLD through integrated transcriptomic and machine-learning analyses. Both genes were among the estrogen-associated transcripts most significantly dysregulated in MASLD livers, and a diagnostic model combining IGFBP2 and P4HA1 achieved high accuracy in distinguishing MASLD from healthy controls. Notably, the expression of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 was markedly downregulated in patients with MASLD compared with controls across multiple cohorts, consistent with emerging evidence that these genes play protective roles in hepatic metabolic homeostasis (16). IGFBP2 (insulin-like growth factor binding protein 2) is a liver-enriched secretory protein that has recently been shown to function as an endogenous inhibitor of steatogenesis (17). Zhai et al demonstrated that IGFBP2 is the most significantly downregulated gene in human NAFLD liver tissues, and that IGFBP2 deficiency in mice exacerbates hepatic steatosis and inflammation by unleashing epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-STAT3 signaling and increasing SREBP1-mediated lipogenesis (16). Concordantly, clinical studies report that patients with MASLD tend to have lower circulating IGFBP2 levels, and that IGFBP2 may exert a protective effect against disease progression (18). Our finding of reduced IGFBP2 in MASLD aligns with these observations and further implicates loss of IGFBP2 as a feature of the estrogen-related transcriptional alterations in MASLD. Notably, estrogen status may regulate IGFBP2: Estradiol has been shown to increase IGFBP2 expression in certain contexts, which could partially explain sex differences in IGFBP2 levels and NAFLD severity (19).

P4HA1 (prolyl-4-hydroxylase subunit α1) is an enzyme involved in collagen maturation and extracellular matrix remodeling (20). We found P4HA1 expression to be significantly downregulated in MASLD, and it emerged as a top predictor in both LASSO and XGBoost models. In a comprehensive meta-analysis, P4HA1 was identified as a hub gene consistently decreased (∼95% down) in NAFLD livers, and its expression showed a significant positive correlation with neutrophil infiltration and other immune/inflammatory pathways (21). Furthermore, overexpression of miR-122 (an abundant liver microRNA) in HSCs can suppress P4HA1 expression and thereby reduce collagen deposition and fibrosis (22). Thus, P4HA1 downregulation in our MASLD cohort (which included many noncirrhotic patients) could represent a response to limit fibrosis, potentially mediated by microRNAs or estrogen-related signals. Overall, the robust performance of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as a diagnostic duo (AUC ∼0.88 in validation) underscores their significance. Both proteins have mechanistic links to metabolic and fibrotic pathways in the liver—IGFBP2 through modulation of growth factor signaling and lipogenesis (23), and P4HA1 through collagen turnover and possibly HIF-1α pathway interactions (23,24)—making them biologically credible markers of MASLD pathophysiology.

Our study also provides insight into the immune dysregulation associated with MASLD and its estrogen-related biomarkers. Using CIBERSORT and ssGSEA, we profiled the hepatic immune cell landscape in MASLD vs normal samples. Consistent with previous reports, MASLD livers exhibited an inflammatory immune milieu characterized by increased proinflammatory macrophages and T-cell subsets alongside reductions in certain innate cells (25). We observed a rise in M1-polarized macrophages in MASLD, which aligns with the known role of recruited monocyte-derived macrophages in driving NASH progression (26,27). These macrophages produce inflammatory cytokines and perpetuate liver injury; indeed, activation of Kupffer cells and infiltration of bone marrow-derived macrophages are hallmark events in MASLD progression (28). Concurrently, patients with MASLD in our cohort showed decreased proportions of resting NK cells and dendritic cells relative to controls. This finding is noteworthy, as NK cells normally have antifibrotic and immunosurveillance functions in the liver (e.g., killing activated stellate cells), and reduced NK cell activity has been linked to more severe fibrosis (29,30). Our data support the notion that MASLD entails a state of NK cell dysfunction or numeric decline, which may allow fibrogenesis to progress unchecked. Similarly, neutrophil fractions were somewhat lower in MASLD livers here, though neutrophils are often elevated in circulation during NASH. It is possible that neutrophils had already transmigrated or degranulated in hepatic tissue, contributing to damage without appearing in high numbers. In fact, recent work indicates neutrophils can exacerbate NASH by releasing reactive oxygen species and proteases; for example, Kupffer cell/macrophage NCF1 was shown to induce Kupffer cell ferroptosis and worsen liver injury in MASH (31,32). Thus, even a moderate neutrophil presence can have outsized pathogenic effects.

Importantly, we found that expression of the 2 diagnostic genes correlated with specific immune cell alterations. IGFBP2 levels showed a positive correlation with anti-inflammatory immune signatures (e.g. activated mast cells) but a negative correlation with proinflammatory cells such as gamma-delta T cells and resting NK cells (in MASLD, where NK cells are reduced). This aligns with IGFBP2's putative anti-inflammatory role—patients with higher IGFBP2 tended to have a more quiescent immune profile (16). P4HA1 expression correlated positively with neutrophil and monocyte scores, suggesting that P4HA1 might be induced as a counter-response in more immune-active livers (or, alternatively, lower P4HA1 permits a heightened inflammatory state) (33). These immune-gene associations highlight that the identified biomarkers are not just passive bystanders but are intertwined with the remodeling of the hepatic immune microenvironment in MASLD. Notably, estrogen signaling can modulate immunity—estrogen has been reported to skew macrophages toward an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype and enhance NK-cell cytotoxicity (34,35). The loss of estrogen in postmenopausal patients with MASLD might therefore promote the proinflammatory, NK-depleted state we and others observe (36). This immunological dimension reinforces the need to consider sex hormone status in MASLD immunopathology and suggests that IGFBP2 and P4HA1 could also serve as indicators of immune state or fibrotic activation.

By leveraging single-cell transcriptomics, we further dissected the cellular source and context of the 2 biomarker genes and broader estrogen-related transcriptional changes. We identified all major liver cell types and observed MASLD-associated cell type-specific disruptions. The findings confirmed that hepatocytes and HSCs scored highest for estrogen-responsive gene signatures, indicating these cells are principal targets of estrogen-related pathways in the liver. This is in line with known distribution of estrogen receptors: hepatocytes (and Kupffer cells) predominantly express ERα, whereas HSCs express ERβ (37). The high “estrogen module” score in HSCs and hepatocytes suggests that hormonal dysfunction could directly affect metabolic and fibrogenic programs in these cells (38). IGFBP2 and P4HA1 are mainly expressed by hepatocytes in normal liver, and their expression is diffusely reduced in MASLD hepatocytes (compared to control), mirroring the bulk RNA findings. Lower hepatocytic IGFBP2 may result in diminished secretion of this protective protein into the circulation, potentially contributing to systemic metabolic disturbances (such as insulin resistance) often seen in patients with MASLD (39,40). We also observed that HSCs in MASLD samples were activated (showing up-regulation of collagens and extracellular matrix [ECM] genes, not surprisingly), and interestingly, they too had lower P4HA1 expression on average, despite being key collagen-producing cells. One possibility is that P4HA1 downregulation reflects a feedback mechanism or exhaustion in fibrogenic cells—perhaps early in disease, the stellate cells have not yet strongly induced P4HA1 (23). Alternatively, post-transcriptional suppression by microRNAs in HSCs (such as miR-122 or others) could be at play (41). These findings confirm that IGFBP2 and P4HA1 are predominantly hepatocellular genes whose diminution in MASLD occurs in tandem with profound cellular and intercellular perturbations. It also demonstrates the utility of scRNA-seq in confirming cell origin of biomarkers and linking molecular changes to specific pathogenic cell types (e.g., activated HSCs).

Regarding clinical relevance, the identification of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 holds significant translational potential. The rising global burden of MASLD necessitates reliable, noninvasive diagnostic tools, particularly to replace invasive liver biopsies. The integration of these 2 genes into a diagnostic nomogram demonstrated a high clinical net benefit for distinguishing MASLD from healthy individuals. In clinical practice, these markers could be particularly valuable for risk stratification in populations with altered sex hormone profiles, such as postmenopausal women or patients with polycystic ovary syndrome, who are at a higher risk of rapid fibrosis progression. Furthermore, to explore the feasibility of detecting these hepatocyte-enriched markers noninvasively, we conducted a preliminary pilot RT-qPCR analysis using peripheral blood. Our data showed significant downregulation of IGFBP2 and P4HA1 transcripts in the blood of patients with MASLD. Although whole blood mRNA primarily originates from circulating leukocytes or low-abundance cell-free RNA, and does not directly mirror hepatocyte intracellular expression, this preliminary finding suggests that the systemic suppression of these genes may reflect hepatic metabolic states. Consequently, they offer an intriguing avenue for further exploration as noninvasive circulating biomarkers (liquid biopsy).

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, bulk transcriptomic analysis lacked cell-type resolution, although scRNA-seq was used to partially address this. Second, patient cohorts were derived from public data sets with heterogeneity in demographics and sample handling. Third, while we performed a preliminary RT-qPCR validation using peripheral blood, the sample size (N = 10) is statistically underpowered and subject to individual variation. Importantly, there is an inherent biological mismatch between the primary target tissue (hepatocytes) and the validation sample (whole blood). Whole blood mRNA cannot serve as a direct surrogate for liver tissue expression. Therefore, our blood qPCR data must be interpreted strictly as a preliminary proof-of-concept for circulating biomarker detectability, rather than a definitive tissue-level validation. Fourth, this study is correlative and does not establish causality for IGFBP2 or P4HA1 in MASLD. Functional studies using knockout or overexpression models are needed. Moreover, the regulatory relationship between these genes and estrogen signaling remains to be clarified, including potential ERα/ERβ binding. In addition, future validations must incorporate protein-level assessments, such as immunohistochemistry on human liver biopsies and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays on patient serum, to verify the translational reliability of IGFBP2 and P4HA1. Last, the ceRNA network is predictive and requires experimental validation of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions.

In conclusion, our integrative machine learning and single-cell analysis identified IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as estrogen-related biomarkers that are consistently downregulated in MASLD and closely linked to disease status. These genes and their regulatory networks (e.g., miR-122 and lncRNA NEAT1/MALAT1) illuminate the interplay between metabolic dysfunction, hormonal signaling, and immune-fibrotic activation. Our findings offer molecular insight into the loss of estrogen's hepatoprotective effects in MASLD and provide a basis for developing precision diagnostic and personalized therapeutic strategies, including hormone-targeted or gene-targeted interventions. Further large-scale prospective validations, including tissue and circulating protein assessments, along with rigorous mechanistic studies will be essential to advance these biomarkers toward clinical application.

MASLD is a multifactorial liver disease influenced by metabolic and hormonal dysregulation. In this study, we identified IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as estrogen-related biomarkers whose downregulation reflects the loss of estrogen's hepatoprotective effects. Incorporation of these genes into a diagnostic model significantly improved MASLD detection. Single-cell analysis confirmed their hepatocellular origin and association with a proinflammatory and profibrotic microenvironment. Furthermore, our ceRNA network suggested that dysregulated noncoding RNAs, including NEAT1, MALAT1, and miR-122, may contribute to their suppression. These findings highlight IGFBP2 and P4HA1 as promising tools for improving MASLD diagnosis and risk stratification, particularly in populations with altered estrogen status.
