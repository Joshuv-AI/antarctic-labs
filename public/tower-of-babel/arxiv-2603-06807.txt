1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper, we investigate the critical behavior of the degenerate parabolic equation with forcing

(1.1)

|x|^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}=\Delta u+|x|^{\sigma_{2}}|u|^{p}+t^{\varrho}\mathbf{w}(x),\qquad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
N\geqslant 1
,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>-2
,
p>1
,
\varrho>-1
, and
\mathbf{w}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function given.
We also consider the unforced version,

(1.2)

|x|^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}=\Delta u+|x|^{\sigma_{2}}|u|^{p},\qquad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Equation (
1.2
) fits into the more general class of nonhomogeneous parabolic equations

(1.3)

\rho(x)u_{t}=\Delta u+\mathbf{q}(x)|u|^{p},\qquad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where the positive function
\rho
represents the density of the medium and
\mathbf{q}
is a prescribed continuous function.
As explained in
[
5
]
, the model (
1.3
) describes heat transfer in a nonhomogeneous medium of density
\rho
, subject to a nonlinear temperature–dependent source. Standard
assumptions include positivity, boundedness, and smoothness of
\rho
, together with asymptotic behavior

(1.4)

\rho(x)\sim|x|^{-\sigma}\qquad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.

The linear counterpart of (
1.3
),

\rho(x)u_{t}=\Delta u,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),

was studied in
[
16
]
; see also
[
15
,
21
]
.
For the nonlinear problem with
\mathbf{q}\equiv 1
, it is shown in
[
5
]
that when
p>1
and
0<\sigma<2
, the Fujita exponent is

p_{F}(N,\sigma)=1+\frac{2}{N-\sigma}.

Under a stronger condition on
\rho
, blow-up occurs whenever the initial data satisfy (see
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{\frac{\sigma}{p-1}}u_{0}(x)=\infty.

Further results for the cases
p=1
and
0<p<1
are also obtained in
[
5
]
.
In
[
17
]
, the equation (
1.3
) is treated under the assumption
\mathbf{q}(x)\sim|x|^{-m}
as
|x|\to\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, leading to the Fujita exponent

p_{F}(N,\sigma,m)=1+\frac{2-m}{N-\sigma}.

In the radial case,
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
shows that the first and second critical Fujita exponents for (
1.2
) are

(1.5)

\displaystyle p_{F}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2+\sigma_{2}}{N+\sigma_{1}},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mu^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2+\sigma_{2})}{(2+\sigma_{1})(p-1)}.

The
second critical
exponent
\mu^{*}>0
is the threshold governing the influence of spatial decay of initial data:
solutions blow up in finite time when
u_{0}(x)\sim C|x|^{-\mu}
with
0<\mu<\mu^{*}
, while global solutions exist for
\mu>\mu^{*}
.

Although it is sometimes asserted that the first Fujita exponent for (
1.2
) follows immediately from scaling, this is misleading.
Indeed, the equation is invariant under

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2+\sigma_{2}}{p-1}}u\!\left(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}+2}t,\lambda x\right),\qquad\lambda>0,

and the only Lebesgue space preserved by this scaling is
L^{p_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

p_{c}=\frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}}.

The condition
p_{c}=1
corresponds to
p=1+\frac{2+\sigma_{2}}{N}
, which does
not
match the true Fujita exponent (
1.5
).
Thus scaling alone does not reveal the correct threshold.

Following
[
10
]
, a key tool in the radial setting is the transformation

u(t,r)=\mathsf{v}(\tau,z),\qquad r=|x|,\quad z=r^{\theta},\quad\tau=\Lambda t,

where
\Lambda>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
are chosen appropriately.
Assuming
\mathbf{w}(x)=\mathbf{w}(r)
, a direct computation gives

(1.7)

\begin{split}\Lambda\,\mathsf{v}_{\tau}&=\theta^{2}z^{2-\frac{2+\sigma_{1}}{\theta}}\mathsf{v}_{zz}+\theta(\theta+N-2)z^{1-\frac{2+\sigma_{1}}{\theta}}\mathsf{v}_{z}+z^{\frac{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}{\theta}}|\mathsf{v}|^{p}\\
&\quad+\Lambda^{-\varrho}\tau^{\varrho}z^{-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\theta}}\mathbf{w}\!\left(z^{1/\theta}\right).\end{split}

Introduce the parameters

\theta=1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2},\qquad\sigma=\frac{2(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})}{2+\sigma_{1}},\qquad\overline{N}=\frac{2(N+\sigma_{1})}{2+\sigma_{1}},\qquad\Lambda=\theta^{\frac{2\sigma}{2+\sigma}},

and apply the change of variables

s=\theta^{-\frac{2}{2+\sigma}}z.

Then (
1.7
) becomes

(1.8)

\mathsf{v}_{\tau}=\mathsf{v}_{ss}+\frac{\overline{N}-1}{s}\mathsf{v}_{s}+s^{\sigma}|\mathsf{v}|^{p}+\tau^{\varrho}\mathbf{W}(s),

with

\mathbf{W}(s)=\Lambda^{-\varrho-1}s^{-\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{2+\sigma_{1}}}\mathbf{w}\!\left(\theta^{\frac{2}{\theta(2+\sigma)}}s^{1/\theta}\right).

Equation (
1.8
) corresponds to the transformed version of

(1.9)

v_{t}=\Delta v+|x|^{\sigma}|v|^{p}+t^{\varrho}\mathbf{w}(x)

in radial coordinates for an effective spatial dimension
\overline{N}
.
According to
[
18
]
, the first Fujita exponent for (
1.1
) is

(1.10)

p^{*}=\frac{\overline{N}-2\varrho+\sigma}{\overline{N}-2\varrho-2}

whenever
-1<\varrho\leqslant 0
and
\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{w}(x)\,dx>0
.

The transformation leading to (
1.9
) is introduced to shed light on the origin of the critical exponent and to place our problem within the framework of weighted Hardy–Hénon type equations, for which the critical behavior is well understood. This reformulation provides useful structural insight and helps motivate the identification of the threshold exponent.

However, we stress that equation (
1.9
) is not used in subsequent proofs. The preceding discussion and the transformation itself are intended solely as a heuristic argument to determine the critical exponent
p^{*}
.

In order to formulate the problem in a weak framework compatible with the weighted structure of (
1.1
), we introduce the following notion of a weak global solution.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
is a weak global solution of (
1.1
), with initial data
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
, if it satisfies

\begin{split}&u_{0}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&|x|^{\sigma_{2}}|u|^{p}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad|x|^{\sigma_{1}}u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{split}

and, for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-|x|^{\sigma_{1}}\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi\right)u\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\sigma_{1}}u_{0}(x)\psi(0,x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x|^{\sigma_{2}}|u|^{p}+t^{\varrho}\mathbf{w}(x)\right)\psi\,dt\,dx

Our first main result establishes (
1.10
) without requiring radial symmetry. More precisely, we have the following statement.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>-2
,
p>1
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
\mathbf{w}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, assume that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{w}(x)\,dx>0.

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\varrho>0
, then the problem (
1.1
) admits no global-in-time weak solution for any
p>1
.

(ii)

If
-1<\varrho<0
and

(1.11)

p<p^{*}:=\frac{N+\sigma_{2}-\varrho(2+\sigma_{1})}{\,N-2-\varrho(2+\sigma_{1})\,},

then the problem (
1.1
) admits no global-in-time weak solution.

(iii)

If
\varrho=0
,
N\geqslant 2
, and

p\leqslant\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{\,(N-2)_{+}\,},

then the problem (
1.1
) admits no global-in-time weak solution.

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

For related results in the case
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
14
,
18
]
. In particular,

p^{*}=\frac{N-2\varrho}{N-2\varrho-2},

which is consistent with
[
14
, Theorem 1.1, (1.8)]
.

(ii)

When
\varrho=0
and
N=2
, the critical exponent
p^{*}
defined in (
1.11
) becomes

p^{*}=\infty.
Consequently, in this case, there is no global-in-time weak solution for any
p>1
.

(iii)

When
\varrho=0
and
\sigma_{2}=0
, the critical exponent
p^{*}
defined in (
1.11
) reduces to

p^{*}=\frac{N}{N-2},\quad N\geqslant 3,

regardless of the value of
\sigma_{1}>-2
. This coincides with the well-known Fujita exponent for the semilinear heat equation with a forcing term, which in the non-degenerate case
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
has been established in
[
2
]
. Consequently, for time-independent forcing (
\varrho=0
) and an unweighted nonlinearity (
\sigma_{2}=0
), the degenerate or singular coefficient
|x|^{\sigma_{1}}
does not influence the critical threshold for blow-up.

(iv)

In the critical case
\varrho=0
and
p=\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{N-2}
, the nonexistence result of Theorem
1.1
(iii) extends the classical blow-up phenomenon for the Hardy–Sobolev type equations. The logarithmic cut-off function used in the proof is essential to handle the borderline decay.

We now turn to the global theory for exponents
p
exceeding the critical value
p^{*}
given in (
1.11
).
To this end, we study global-in-time mild solutions to the Cauchy problem

u_{t}=|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta u+|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\,|u|^{p}+t^{\varrho}\,|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\,\mathbf{w}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

under suitable conditions on the parameters
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
,
\varrho
, and
p
, together with a smallness assumption on
u_{0}
and
\mathbf{w}
.
Our analysis relies on the semigroup estimates for the operator
|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta
established in Proposition
2.1
, combined with a fixed-point argument in a weighted-in-time Lebesgue space.
The precise statement is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
and assume

-2\;<\;\sigma_{2}\;<\;\sigma_{1}\;\leqslant\;0,\qquad-1\;<\;\varrho\;<\;0,\qquad p\;>\;1.

Define the critical exponents
p_{c},r_{c},p^{*}
by

(1.12)

\begin{split}p_{c}\;&=\;\frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}},\\[5.69054pt]
p^{*}\;&=\;\frac{N+\sigma_{2}-\varrho(2+\sigma_{1})}{\,N-2-\varrho(2+\sigma_{1})\,},\\[5.69054pt]
r_{c}\;&=\;\frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}+(1+\varrho)(2+\sigma_{1})(p-1)}.\end{split}

Assume, in addition, that

p>p^{*}.

Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, for every initial data

u_{0}
and forcing term
\mathbf{w}
satisfying

(1.13)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}+\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{r_{c}}}<\varepsilon,

the mild formulation

(1.14)

\nu(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\,|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\,ds+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(s^{\varrho}\,|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}w\bigr)\,ds

admits a unique global-in-time mild solution

u\in L^{\infty}\!\bigl((0,\infty);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr)
satisfying
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r}}<\infty
, where
r
and
\mu
are defined in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) below.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

It is straightforward to verify that any mild solution of (
1.14
) is also a weak solution. Consequently, all conclusions of Theorem
1.1
apply directly to every mild solution.

(ii)

Note that Theorem
1.2
does not address the case
\varrho=0
,
p>\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{N-2}
and
N\geqslant 3
. A complete analysis of this regime lies beyond the scope of the theorem and requires separate investigation; see
[
24
]
for the case
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
.

(iii)

The smallness condition (
1.13
) on the initial data and the forcing term is natural for the fixed-point argument. It would be interesting to investigate whether global solutions can exist for large data when
p>p^{*}
, or if blow-up may occur in a different manner. Furthermore, the critical case
p=p^{*}
is left open and deserves further study.

Finally, we present a local existence result obtained by using a fixed point argument.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
and assume that the parameters satisfy

-2<\sigma_{2}\leqslant\sigma_{1}\leqslant 0,\qquad-1<\varrho\leqslant 0,\qquad p>1.

Let
q\geqslant 1
be such that

q>\max\!\left\{\frac{Np}{N+\sigma_{2}},\ \frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}}\right\},\qquad q\geqslant p.

Assume that the initial data and the forcing term satisfy

u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Let
(\mathbf{S}(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
be the
C_{0}
–semigroup generated by the operator

|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta
.
Then there exists a time
T>0
such that the integral equation

(1.15)

u(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\,|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(s^{\varrho}\,|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}(x)\bigr)\,ds.

admits a unique mild solution

u\in C\bigl([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr).

Remark 1.3
.

(i)

The unforced case, namely
\mathbf{w}=0
, commonly referred to as the Hardy–Hénon equation, was studied in
[
3
]
for
\sigma_{1}=0
, where the parameter
\gamma
corresponds to
-\sigma_{2}
in the present notation.

(ii)

The condition

q>\frac{Np}{N+\sigma_{2}}

ensures that the weighted semigroup estimate in Proposition 2.1 can be applied to the nonlinear term

|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}|u|^{p}
with
(q_{1},q_{2})=(q/p,q)
.

(iii)

The restriction

q>\frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}}

guarantees that the associated time singularity
(t-s)^{-\alpha}
arising in the convolution kernel
is integrable, which is essential for the fixed-point argument. Here
\alpha
is given by (
5.1
) below.

(iv)

The assumption
q\geqslant p
ensures that
|u|^{p}\in L^{q/p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
whenever
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(v)

Unlike the global existence result in Theorem
1.2
, the present local theory allows the
borderline case
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}
, since no decay in time is required for the nonlinear term.

(vi)

No sign condition on
\mathbf{w}
is imposed in the local existence result; only the integrability condition

|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is needed.

2.
Useful tools & Auxiliary results

In this section, we introduce the notation used throughout the paper and present several auxiliary results and estimates.

We begin by recalling several fundamental estimates for the heat semi-group
associated with the operator
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta
, where
\alpha\in(0,2)
.
This is relevant in our setting because equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(2.1)

u_{t}=|x|^{\alpha}\Delta u+|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\,|u|^{p}+t^{\varrho}\,|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\,\mathbf{w}(x),

with the identification
\alpha=-\sigma_{1}<2
.
Thus, the linear diffusion part in (
2.1
) falls precisely within the
framework of the degenerate operator
|x|^{\alpha}\Delta
.

Theorem 2.1

(
L^{a}
–
L^{b}
estimates for degenerate heat semigroups)
.

Let
N\geqslant 1
,
0\leqslant\alpha<2
, and
1<a,b<\infty
. Suppose the exponents
a,b
satisfy

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{b}<\frac{1}{a}<2-\alpha,&\text{if }N=1,\\[6.45831pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{b}<\frac{1}{a}<1-\frac{\alpha}{N},&\text{if }N\geqslant 2.\end{cases}

Then the degenerate operator
L:=|x|^{\alpha}\Delta
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathbf{S}(t))_{t>0}
, and there exists a constant
C=C(N,\alpha,a,b)>0
such that for all
t>0
and
\varphi\in L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(2.2)

\|\mathbf{S}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\,t^{\,-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\right)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

Theorem
2.1
corresponds to
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
.

(ii)

The validity of the smoothing estimate (
2.2
) depends essentially on the parameter
\alpha
.
For the remaining cases—namely the singular case
\alpha<0
, the borderline case
\alpha=2
, and the super
-
degenerate case
\alpha>2
—a summary of the situation can be found in
[
12
]
.

(iii)

For further details and other heat kernel estimates, we refer to
[
12
,
19
,
22
,
20
,
6
]
.

We record below a smoothing estimate for the semigroup generated by the degenerate operator

|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta
, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
,
-2<\sigma_{1}\leqslant 0
, and let
\mathbf{S}(t)
be the
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by
|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta
.
Assume
0\leqslant\gamma<N
and let
1<q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
satisfy

(2.3)

0<\frac{1}{q_{2}}<\frac{\gamma}{N}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N}.

Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
t>0
and every
\varphi\in L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(2.4)

\big\|\mathbf{S}(t)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\big\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\leqslant C\,t^{-\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{q_{2}}\right)-\frac{\gamma}{2+\sigma_{1}}}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{q_{1}}}.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
follows the general
strategy of
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
, where the weight
|x|^{-\gamma}

is decomposed and controlled by suitable Hölder estimates. In the present
setting, the argument is further combined with the homogeneity properties
and the
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
bounds for the semigroup
(\mathbf{S}(t))_{t>0}

derived in
[
12
]
, which capture the scaling effects generated by
the degenerate coefficient
|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}
. For completeness, a full
proof is given below.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The proof proceeds in two steps: first we establish the estimate at
t=1
, then we extend it to arbitrary
t>0
by a scaling argument.

Step 1: The case
t=1
.

Set
m=N/\gamma
(if
\gamma=0
the statement is trivial).
Choose
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that
\varepsilon<m
, and

(2.5)

\frac{1}{q_{2}}<\frac{1}{m+\delta}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}<\frac{1}{m-\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N}.

Such
\varepsilon,\delta
exist because of (
2.3
).
Decompose the potential as

|\cdot|^{-\gamma}=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2},

with
\psi_{1}\in L^{m-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\psi_{2}\in L^{m+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Define

(2.6)

\frac{1}{r_{1}}=\frac{1}{m-\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{q_{1}},\qquad\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{m+\delta}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}.

By construction,
0<\frac{1}{r_{1}},\frac{1}{r_{2}}<1
and
\frac{1}{q_{2}}<\frac{1}{r_{2}}<\frac{1}{r_{1}}
.
Moreover, by taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small we can ensure that

(2.7)

\frac{1}{r_{1}}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N}.

Combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and (
2.7
), we are in a position to apply the standard smoothing estimate (
2.2
) to the pairs
(a,b)=(q_{2},r_{1})
and
(a,b)=(q_{2},r_{2})
. As a consequence, we obtain

(2.8)

\|\mathbf{S}(1)f\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{r_{i}}},\qquad i=1,2.

Next, we decompose the weight and estimate

\|\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant\|\mathbf{S}(1)(\psi_{1}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}+\|\mathbf{S}(1)(\psi_{2}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}.

For
i=1,2
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\|\psi_{1}\varphi\|_{r_{1}}\leqslant\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{m-\varepsilon}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}},\quad\|\psi_{2}\varphi\|_{r_{2}}\leqslant\|\psi_{2}\|_{L^{m+\delta}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}.

Applying (
2.8
) we obtain

\|\mathbf{S}(1)(\psi_{1}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant C\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{m-\varepsilon}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}},\quad\|\mathbf{S}(1)(\psi_{2}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant C\|\psi_{2}\|_{L^{m+\delta}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}.

Summing the two contributions yields

(2.9)

\|\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant C_{1}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}},

with
C_{1}=C(\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{m-\varepsilon}}+\|\psi_{2}\|_{L^{m+\delta}})
.

Step 2: Scaling argument.

Let
D_{\lambda}
be the dilation operator
D_{\lambda}\varphi(x)=\varphi(\lambda x)
,
\lambda>0
.
One has
\|D_{\lambda}\varphi\|_{r}=\lambda^{-N/r}\|\varphi\|_{r}
and

D_{\lambda}(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)=\lambda^{-\gamma}|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}\varphi
.
A direct computation based on the homogeneity of the operator yields the following scaling property (see also
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
).

(2.10)

D_{\lambda}^{-1}\mathbf{S}(t)D_{\lambda}=\mathbf{S}(\lambda^{2+\sigma_{1}}t).

Take
\lambda=t^{-1/(2+\sigma_{1})}
; then
\lambda^{2+\sigma_{1}}t=1
and (
2.10
) gives

\mathbf{S}(t)=D_{\lambda}\mathbf{S}(1)D_{\lambda}^{-1}.

Now compute

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(t)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda}\mathbf{S}(1)D_{\lambda}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda}\mathbf{S}(1)\bigl(\lambda^{\gamma}|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\gamma}D_{\lambda}\bigl[\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi)\bigr].

Taking the
L^{q_{2}}
-norm,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}(t)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\gamma}\|D_{\lambda}[\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi)]\|_{q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\gamma}\lambda^{-N/q_{2}}\|\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}.

Applying (
2.9
) to
D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi
,

\|\mathbf{S}(1)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant C_{1}\|D_{\lambda}^{-1}\varphi\|_{q_{1}}=C_{1}\lambda^{N/q_{1}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}.

Hence,

\|\mathbf{S}(t)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}\leqslant C_{1}\lambda^{\gamma+N(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}.

Substituting
\lambda=t^{-1/(2+\sigma_{1})}
we finally obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}(t)(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\varphi)\|_{q_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\,t^{-\frac{1}{2+\sigma_{1}}\bigl[\gamma+N\bigl(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{q_{2}}\bigr)\bigr]}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}\,t^{-\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\bigl(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{q_{2}}\bigr)-\frac{\gamma}{2+\sigma_{1}}}\|\varphi\|_{q_{1}}.

Thus (
2.4
) holds with
C=C_{1}
.
∎

The next lemma plays a key role in building the functional setting needed to apply the fixed-point argument for establishing global existence of mild solutions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
,
\sigma_{1}>-2
,
\sigma_{2}>-2
, and
-1<\varrho<0
.
Define

A:=2+\sigma_{1}>0,\qquad f(p)=\varrho Ap^{2}-(N-2+\varrho A)p+(N+\sigma_{2}),

and

p^{*}=\frac{N+\sigma_{2}-\varrho A}{\,N-2-\varrho A\,}.

Then

f(p)<0\qquad\text{for all }p\geqslant p^{*}.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Since
\varrho<0
and
A>0
, we have
\varrho A<0
, so the quadratic polynomial
f
is concave.

We first verify that
f(p^{*})<0
and
f^{\prime}(p^{*})<0
. A straightforward substitution yields

f(p^{*})=\frac{\varrho A\,(\sigma_{2}+2)^{2}}{(N-2-\varrho A)^{2}}<0,

where the inequality follows immediately from
\varrho A<0
. Moreover,

f^{\prime}(p^{*})=\frac{2\varrho A(N+\sigma_{2})-(\varrho A)^{2}-(N-2)^{2}}{\,N-2-\varrho A\,}\equiv\frac{B}{\,N-2-\varrho A\,}.

The numerator
B
can be expressed as

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=-\Big[(\varrho A)^{2}+(N-2)^{2}-2\varrho A(N+\sigma_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=-\Big[(N-2-\varrho A)^{2}-2\varrho A(\sigma_{2}+2)\Big].

Since
\sigma_{2}+2>0
and
\varrho A<0
, the term
-2\varrho A(\sigma_{2}+2)
is positive, which implies
B<0
and therefore
f^{\prime}(p^{*})<0
.

Because
f
is concave and satisfies
f^{\prime}(p^{*})<0
, it is nonincreasing on
[p^{*},\infty)
. Combined with
f(p^{*})<0
, we conclude that

f(p)<0\qquad\text{for all }p\geqslant p^{*}.

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\psi^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\right)\right|\leqslant CT^{-1}\psi^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\left|\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\right|,

4.
Global existence

To carry out the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we will make use of the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geqslant 2
and assume that the parameters satisfy

-2<\sigma_{2}<\sigma_{1}\leqslant 0,\qquad-1<\varrho<0,\qquad p>p^{*},

where
p^{*}
is given in (
1.12
). Then there exists
r\in(1,\infty)
such that

(4.1)

\max\Bigl\{\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{2+\sigma_{1}}{Np},\;\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{\varrho(2+\sigma_{1})}{N}\Bigr\}\;<\;\frac{1}{r}\;<\;\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{p_{c}},\;\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{Np}\Bigr\}.

Define

(4.2)

\mu:=\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr),

(4.3)

\beta:=\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr),

and

(4.4)

\delta:=\frac{N(p-1)}{(2+\sigma_{1})r}-\frac{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}{2+\sigma_{1}},

where
p_{c},r_{c}
are given by (
1.12
).
Then the quantities
\mu
,
\beta
, and
\delta
satisfy

(4.5)

0<\mu<\frac{1}{p},\qquad 0<\beta<1,\qquad 0<\delta<1,

and moreover,

(4.6)

1-p\mu-\delta=-\mu=\varrho+1-\beta.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
will be given later. For the moment, we simply record the required parameter choices and resume the main argument for Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For the parameters
r
and
\mu
specified in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we introduce the Banach space

\mathbf{X}:=\Bigl\{u\in L^{\infty}\!\bigl((0,\infty);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr)\;:\;\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}:=\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r}}<\infty\Bigr\}.

We seek a mild solution of (
1.14
) inside the closed ball

\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)\subset X
, where
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen sufficiently small.

Define the operator
G:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{X}
by

G(u)(t):=\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}+F(u)(t)+H(t),

where

\displaystyle F(u)(t)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\,ds,

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(s^{\varrho}|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\bigr)\,ds.

Since
p>p^{*}
and (
4.1
) imply

\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1}{p_{c}}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N},

we may apply the unweighted estimate (
2.4
) with
q_{1}=p_{c}
,
q_{2}=r
, and use (
4.2
) to obtain

\|\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\,t^{-\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}=C\,t^{-\mu}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}.

Thus,

(4.7)

\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}.

For the nonlinear term
F(u)
, we apply the weighted estimate (
2.4
) with

q_{1}=r/p
,
q_{2}=r
, and
\gamma=\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}>0
, yielding

\|F(u)(t)\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\delta}\|u(s)\|_{L^{r}}^{p}\,ds,

where
\delta
is given by (
4.4
).

Multiplying by
t^{\mu}
, using the definition of
\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}
and (
4.5
), we find

\displaystyle t^{\mu}\|F(u)(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p}\,t^{\mu}\!\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\delta}s^{-p\mu}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p}\,t^{\mu+1-p\mu-\delta}\!\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{-\delta}\tau^{-p\mu}\,d\tau,

and the last integral is finite by (
4.5
). Hence

(4.8)

\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|F(u)(t)\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p}.

A standard Lipschitz estimate based on

|\,|a|^{p}-|b|^{p}|\leqslant C(|a|^{p-1}+|b|^{p-1})|a-b|
gives

(4.9)

\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leqslant C\big(\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p-1}\big)\|u-v\|_{\mathbf{X}}.

Next, applying the unweighted estimate (
2.4
) with
q_{1}=r
,
q_{2}=r_{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|H(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{r_{c}}}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}s^{\varrho}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{r_{c}}}t^{\varrho+1-\beta}\!\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{-\beta}\tau^{\varrho}\,d\tau,

where
\beta
is defined in (
4.3
).
Using (
4.5
)–(
4.6
) and
\varrho>-1
, the last integral is finite, and thus

(4.10)

\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|H(t)\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\left\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{r_{c}}}.

Combining (
4.7
), (
4.8
), and (
4.10
), we obtain for every

u\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)
,

\displaystyle\|G(u)\|_{\mathbf{X}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigl(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}+\left\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{r_{c}}}\bigr)+C\|u\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon+C(2C\varepsilon)^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\varepsilon,

provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen sufficiently small.
Thus
G
maps
\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)
into itself whenever

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}+\left\||\cdot|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{r_{c}}}\leqslant\varepsilon
.

Finally, by (
4.9
), for all
u,v\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)
,

\|G(u)-G(v)\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leqslant 2C(2C\varepsilon)^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{\mathbf{X}}.

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that

2C(2C\varepsilon)^{p-1}<1
, the operator
G
becomes a contraction on

\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)
.
By the Banach fixed-point theorem,
G
admits a unique fixed point

u\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}(0,2C\varepsilon)
, which is the desired global mild solution of (
1.14
) in
\mathbf{X}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

.

Proof of Lemma

.

.

1. Existence of

, both

\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{\varrho A}{N}<\frac{1}{p_{c}},\qquad\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{A}{Np}<\frac{1}{p_{c}},

hold automatically. Hence the left-hand side of (
4.11
) is strictly smaller than
1/p_{c}
.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that for all
p\geqslant p^{*}
,

f(p)=\varrho Ap^{2}-(N-2+\varrho A)p+(N+\sigma_{2})<0.

The inequality

\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{\varrho A}{N}<\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{Np}

is equivalent to
f(p)<0
; hence it holds whenever
p>p^{*}
.

Next, the inequality

\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{A}{Np}<\frac{N+\sigma_{2}}{Np}

is equivalent to

p>\frac{N+2+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}{N+\sigma_{1}}.

A direct computation shows that

p^{*}\;>\;\frac{N+2+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}{N+\sigma_{1}},

so this inequality is also satisfied for all
p>p^{*}
.

2. Bounds on
\mu,\beta,\delta
.

Since
1/r<1/p_{c}
, we have
\mu>0
.
The inequality

\frac{1}{r}>\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{A}{Np}

gives

\mu=\frac{N}{A}\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)<\frac{1}{p}.

For

\beta=\frac{N}{A}\!\left(\frac{1}{r_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\right),

we use the identity

\frac{1}{r_{c}}=\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{(1+\varrho)A}{N}.

Since
\varrho>-1
, we have
1/r_{c}>1/p_{c}>1/r
, hence
\beta>0
.

Moreover,

\frac{1}{r}>\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{\varrho A}{N}=\frac{1}{r_{c}}-\frac{A}{N},

which implies
\beta<1
.
Thus
0<\beta<1
.

To control
\delta
, use the identity

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{A}{N}\mu.

Substituting into

\delta=\frac{N(p-1)}{Ar}+\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{A}

gives

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{N(p-1)}{A}\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{A}{N}\mu\Bigr)+\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{2+\sigma_{2}}{A}+\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{A}-(p-1)\mu

\displaystyle=1-(p-1)\mu.

Since
0<\mu<1/p
, we have
(p-1)\mu<1-1/p
, hence

0<\delta<1.

3. Algebraic identities.

Using
\delta=1-(p-1)\mu
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-p\mu-\delta

\displaystyle=1-p\mu-\bigl(1-(p-1)\mu\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\mu.

For the second identity,

\displaystyle\mu-\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{A}\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{1}{r_{c}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{A}\Bigl(-\frac{(1+\varrho)A}{N}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-(1+\varrho),

hence

-\mu=\varrho+1-\beta.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

5.
Local existence

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
The argument is based on a fixed-point method in the Banach space

X_{T}:=\Bigl\{u\in C\bigl([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr):\|u\|_{X_{T}}<\infty\Bigr\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{T}}:=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We introduce the operator

F(u)(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}\,|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(s^{\varrho}\,|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\bigr)\,ds.

Our goal is to show that, for a suitable choice of
T>0
and for an appropriate radius
M>0
, the map
F
is a contraction on the closed ball

B_{M}:=\bigl\{u\in X_{T}:\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant M\bigr\}.

Since
\mathbf{S}(t)
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\|\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}},\qquad t\geqslant 0.

For the nonlinear term, we apply Proposition 1 with

\gamma=\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}>0,\qquad q_{2}=q,\qquad q_{1}=\frac{q}{p}.

Under this choice, condition (
2.3
) reads

0<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{N}+\frac{p}{q}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N}.

The right-hand inequality implies
q>\frac{Np}{N+\sigma_{2}}
, which is ensured by our assumptions.
The corresponding time-decay exponent given by (
2.4
) is

(5.1)

\alpha:=\frac{N}{2+\sigma_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{p}{q}-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)+\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{2+\sigma_{1}}=\frac{N(p-1)}{q(2+\sigma_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{2+\sigma_{1}}.

The integrability condition
\alpha<1
is equivalent to

\frac{N(p-1)}{q}<2+\sigma_{2},

that is,
q>\frac{N(p-1)}{2+\sigma_{2}}
, which again follows from the choice of
q
.

Concerning the forcing term, we set

f:=|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\mathbf{w}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

so that the forcing takes the form
s^{\varrho}f(x)
.
Applying Proposition
2.1
with
\gamma=0
and
q_{1}=q_{2}=q
requires

0<\frac{1}{q}<1+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{N},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\frac{N}{N+\sigma_{1}}<q<\infty.

This condition is automatically satisfied since
p>1
and
\sigma_{2}<\sigma_{1}
imply

\frac{Np}{N+\sigma_{2}}>\frac{N}{N+\sigma_{1}}.

Hence,

\bigl\|\mathbf{S}(t-s)(s^{\varrho}f)\bigr\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C\,s^{\varrho}\|f\|_{L^{q}}.

In what follows, the constant
C
denotes a generic positive constant that may vary from line to line.

Let
u\in X_{T}
.
The linear term satisfies

\|\mathbf{S}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}.

For the nonlinear contribution, Proposition
2.1
yields

\bigl\|\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|u(s)\|_{L^{q}}^{p},

and therefore

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}|u(s)|^{p}\bigr)\,ds\Bigr\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\,t^{1-\alpha}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p}.

For the forcing term we obtain

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(s^{\varrho}f\bigr)\,ds\Bigr\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\frac{C}{\varrho+1}\,t^{\varrho+1}\|f\|_{L^{q}}.

We now fix

M:=2C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}},\qquad R(T):=C_{1}T^{1-\alpha}M^{p}+C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{q}}T^{\varrho+1},

with
C_{1}=\frac{C}{1-\alpha}
and
C_{2}=\frac{C}{\varrho+1}
.
Choosing
T>0
sufficiently small so that
R(T)\leqslant\frac{M}{2}
, which is possible since
1-\alpha>0
and
\varrho>-1
, we obtain for all
u\in B_{M}
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\|F(u)(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+R(T)\leqslant M.

Hence,
F(B_{M})\subset B_{M}
.

To prove the contraction property, let
u,v\in B_{M}
.
Using the inequality

\bigl||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\bigr|\leqslant p\bigl(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1}\bigr)|u-v|

together with Hölder’s inequality, we deduce

\|\,|u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\|_{L^{q/p}}\leqslant 2pM^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{L^{q}}.

Applying Proposition
2.1
once again yields

\bigl\|\mathbf{S}(t-s)\bigl(|x|^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p})\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant 2pCM^{p-1}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|u-v\|_{L^{q}}.

Consequently,

\|F(u)(t)-F(v)(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\frac{2pC}{1-\alpha}\,M^{p-1}t^{1-\alpha}\|u-v\|_{X_{T}},

and hence

\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant C_{3}M^{p-1}T^{1-\alpha}\|u-v\|_{X_{T}},\qquad C_{3}:=\frac{2pC}{1-\alpha}.

By possibly reducing
T
further so that
C_{3}M^{p-1}T^{1-\alpha}<1
, the map
F
becomes a contraction on
B_{M}
.

The Banach fixed point theorem then guarantees the existence of a unique fixed point of
F
in
B_{M}
, which is exactly a mild solution
u\in C\bigl([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr)

of the integral equation (
1.15
).

6.
Concluding remarks

This work is devoted to the analysis of the critical behavior of a degenerate parabolic equation with a space–time-dependent forcing term, namely equation (
1.1
). We establish the existence of a sharp threshold that separates finite-time blow-up from global-in-time existence, characterized by the critical exponent
p^{*}
defined in (
1.11
).

The analysis combines scaling arguments, semigroup estimates associated with the degenerate operator
|x|^{-\sigma_{1}}\Delta
, and a fixed-point method in suitably time-weighted Lebesgue spaces. The resulting critical exponent
p^{*}
extends the classical Fujita exponent and reflects the subtle interplay between the degeneracy parameters
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, the temporal growth rate
\varrho
, and the spatial dimension
N
.

Several natural questions remain open. In particular, the behavior at the critical threshold
p=p^{*}
is not covered by the present results and is expected to require more refined analytical tools. The stationary forcing case
\varrho=0
, as well as the possibility of global existence for large initial data when
p>p^{*}
, also deserve further investigation. Moreover, allowing sign-changing or non-radial forcing terms may significantly alter the dynamics and lead to new critical phenomena. Extending the analysis to more general degenerate or singular weights beyond the range
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>-2
is another challenge.

Overall, this study contributes to the qualitative theory of degenerate parabolic equations with time-dependent forcing. The explicit form of the critical exponent
p^{*}
highlights how temporal growth, spatial degeneracy, and weighted nonlinearities interact. The techniques developed here, particularly the use of weighted semigroup estimates combined with fixed-point arguments, are flexible and may be adapted to other nonlinear evolution equations with variable coefficients and external sources.
