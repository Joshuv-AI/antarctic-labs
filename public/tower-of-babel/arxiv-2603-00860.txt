Geometric Multigrid solvers for hybrid high-order methods on polytopal meshes
Santiago Badia †, Jordi Manyer †

Abstract
. We propose the first optimal geometric multigrid solver for hybrid high-order discretizations that can handle arbitrary polytopal agglomeration hierarchies in both two and three dimensions. The key ingredient is the use of modified skeleton spaces, which naturally accommodate non-planar interfaces arising during coarsening while reducing the number of degrees of freedom. We prove robust convergence with respect to the mesh size and the number of levels, and we validate our results numerically on a range of agglomeration-based mesh hierarchies. The approach extends naturally to other hybrid discretizations such as hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin and Weak Galerkin methods.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded polytopal domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the Poisson problem with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(2.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a given source term. The extension to the non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions is straightforward. The weak formulation reads: Find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(2.2)

a(u,v)\doteq\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}fv,\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

By the Lax-Milgram theorem, problem (
2.2
) admits a unique solution. We assume that the solution satisfies the elliptic regularity, i.e.,
u
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies
\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Let
\ell=0,\ldots,L
denote levels and
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
be a collection of partitions of the domain
\Omega
at each level, each consisting of a disjoint set of open polygonal/polyhedral elements
t
with diameters
h_{t}=\operatorname{diam}(t)
, and let
h_{\ell}=\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}h_{t}
denote the mesh size of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. We will limit ourselves to nested meshes built by agglomeration, so that each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
is obtained by agglomerating elements of the finer mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. That is, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
the set
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)=\{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}:{t}\subset{T}\}
is such that
\overline{T}=\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}\overline{t}
. We assume that the mesh hierarchy is such that for all
\ell=1,\ldots,L

(2.3)

h_{\ell-1}/h_{\ell}\simeq 1

The cell agglomeration induces a set of interfaces between cells:

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\doteq\{F=\overline{t_{1}}\cap\overline{t_{2}}:\ t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\ ,\ \overline{t_{1}}\cap\overline{t_{2}}\neq\emptyset\}.

In general,
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
denotes interfaces between cells, which are piecewise-linear curves for
d=2
and polygonal surfaces for
d=3
. We will still refer to these interfaces as
faces
for brevity. We can proceed analogously to create coarse edges
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
(by using the same construction above starting with the face agglomerates
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
) and vertices
\mathcal{V}_{\ell}
(from the agglomerated edges
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
).

We say that
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
is associated to a cell
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
if
F\subset\partial T
. The set of faces associated to a cell
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)
. We assume that the mesh hierarchy is such that for all
\ell=1,\ldots,L
and all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
the number of faces in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)
is uniformly bounded by a constant independent of
\ell
and
t
. This assumption is satisfied for typical agglomeration strategies and, notably, does not impose any limit on the number of planar faces composing an interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
.
We distinguish between interior faces
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{{\rm in}}\doteq\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}:F\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset\}
and boundary faces
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{{\rm bd}}\doteq\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}:F\subset\partial\Omega\}
.
Given two consecutive levels
\ell
and
\ell-1
, we introduce the set of fine faces in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
that belong to coarse faces in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}
as
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})\doteq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(F)
. We will, for brevity, write
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}
for
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})
(fine faces fully contained in a coarse cell) and
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}
for
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})
(fine faces lying on coarse faces).

Given a geometric entity
X
, e.g. a cell
t
or a planar face
f
, let
\mathbb{P}^{k}(X)
denote the space of polynomials of total degree at most
k
on
X
, with local
L^{2}
-orthogonal projectors onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(X)
given by
\pi_{X}^{k}:L^{2}(X)\to\mathbb{P}^{k}(X)
. Similarly, given
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
a collection of geometrical entities, e.g.
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, we define the corresponding broken polynomial space
\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{X}_{\ell})=\bigtimes_{X\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbb{P}^{k}(X)
and
\pi_{\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}^{k}
the broken projector acting element-wise, that is
\pi_{\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}^{k}v|_{X}=\pi_{X}^{k}(v|_{X})
for all
X\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
. Analogously, we define
\pi_{X}^{1,k}
the elliptic projector onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(X)

[
39
, Definition 1.39]
and
\pi_{\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}^{1,k}
its broken counterpart.
For any measurable set
X
, we write
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}
for the
L^{2}(X)
inner product and
\|\cdot\|_{X}
for the associated norm. For a collection
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
, these are extended element-wise:
(u,v)_{\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\doteq\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}(u,v)_{X}
. We also denote by
|u|_{1,X}
and
|u|_{1,\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}
the
H^{1}
seminorm on
X
and the broken
H^{1}
seminorm on
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
.

2.1.
HHO discretisation

To simplify expressing the various operators, we will use a mixed-order
hho
formulation, where the polynomial degree of the cell unknowns is one degree higher than that of the face unknowns
[
15
]
.
To each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, we associate an
hho
discrete space
\underline{U}_{\ell}
defined as

(2.4)

\underline{U}_{\ell}\doteq U_{\ell}\times M_{\ell}=\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\times\mathcal{P}^{k}_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}),

where
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})=\{v\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}):v|_{F}=0\ \forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{{\rm bd}}\}
and
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
is a space of polynomial traces on an interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
that we will define in Sect.
2.2
. When
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)=\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)
, we recover the standard HHO discretisation. We define
\pi_{F}^{k}
the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector onto
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
, and
\pi_{\partial t}^{k}
the broken projector acting face-wise on
\partial t
, that is
\pi_{\partial t}^{k}v|_{F}=\pi_{F}^{k}(v|_{F})
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)
.

Remark 2.1
.

To ease on notation, we will consider
k\doteq k_{\ell}
for all levels
\ell
. This choice does not, however, affect the analysis since we will not be tracking explicitly the dependence of the constants on the polynomial order; in fact, the analysis can be trivially extended to the case where
k_{\ell}
varies with
\ell
.

Given an element
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, we denote by
\underline{u}_{t}=(u_{t},u_{\partial t})
the restriction of a discrete function
\underline{u}_{\ell}\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
to the element
t
, where
u_{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(t)
is the element unknown and
u_{\partial t}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t))
collects the face unknowns on the boundary
\partial t
of the element.
We will use the typical
hho
norm on
\underline{U}_{\ell}
, defined for all
\underline{u}_{\ell}\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
by

(2.5)

\|\underline{u}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{1,\ell}}^{2}\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\|\underline{u}_{t}\|_{\underline{1,t}}^{2}\ ,\quad\|\underline{u}_{t}\|_{\underline{1,t}}^{2}\doteq|u_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}+h_{t}^{-1}\|u_{\partial t}-u_{t}\|_{\partial t}^{2}.

We define a local reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}
that maps the discrete unknowns
\underline{u}_{t}
to a polynomial
\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}(\underline{u}_{t})\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(t)
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{t},\nabla w)_{t}

\displaystyle=(\nabla u_{t},\nabla w)_{t}+(u_{\partial t}-u_{t},\nabla w\cdot\bm{n})_{\partial t},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{t},1)_{t}

\displaystyle=(u_{t},1)_{t},\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(t).

The local bilinear form
\underline{a}_{\ell,t}
is then defined by

(2.7)

\underline{a}_{\ell,t}(\underline{u}_{t},\underline{v}_{t})\doteq(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{t},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{v}_{t})_{t}+\underline{s}_{\ell,t}(\underline{u}_{t},\underline{v}_{t}),

where
\underline{s}_{\ell,t}
is a stabilisation term defined as

(2.8)

\underline{s}_{\ell,t}(\underline{u}_{t},\underline{v}_{t})\doteq h_{t}^{-1}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)}(u_{\partial t}-\pi_{F}^{k}u_{t},v_{\partial t}-\pi_{F}^{k}v_{t})_{F},

with
\pi_{F}^{k}
being the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector onto
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
.
The global bilinear form
\underline{a}_{\ell}
is then

(2.9)

\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{v}_{\ell})\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\underline{a}_{\ell,t}(\underline{u}_{t},\underline{v}_{t})\ ,\quad\underline{b}_{\ell}(\underline{v}_{\ell})\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}(f,v_{t})_{t}.

The discrete problem then reads: Find
\underline{u}_{\ell}\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
such that for all
\underline{v}_{\ell}\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
it holds

(2.10)

\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{v}_{\ell})=\underline{b}_{\ell}(\underline{v}_{\ell}).

Similarly to
[
38
]
, we can statically condense our bulk unknowns to obtain a global problem posed only on the face unknowns. To do this, for each level
\ell
we define the local inverse static condensation operators
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,t}:M_{\ell}(t)\to U_{\ell}(t)
and
\mathcal{V}_{\ell,t}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to U_{\ell}(t)
as

(2.11)

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{\ell,t}((\mathcal{U}_{\ell,t}\lambda,0),(w,0))=-\underline{a}_{\ell,t}((0,\lambda),(w,0))\quad\forall w\in U_{\ell}(t),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{\ell,t}((\mathcal{V}_{\ell,t}f,0),(w,0))=(f,w)_{t}\quad\forall w\in U_{\ell}(t).

We define global operators
\mathcal{R}_{\ell}:\underline{U}_{\ell}\to\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(t)
and
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}:M_{\ell}\to U_{\ell}
by applying the respective local operators element-wise. We can then define the hybrid
hho
norm on
M_{\ell}
for all
\lambda\in M_{\ell}
as

(2.13)

\|\lambda\|_{1,\ell}^{2}\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\|\lambda\|_{1,t}^{2}\ ,\quad\|\lambda\|_{1,t}^{2}\doteq\|(\mathcal{U}_{\ell,t}\lambda,\lambda)\|_{\underline{1,t}}^{2}.

Analogously, the global skeletal bilinear and linear forms are then defined for all
\lambda,\mu\in M_{\ell}
as

(2.14)

\displaystyle a_{\ell}(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\doteq\underline{a}_{\ell}((\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda,\lambda),(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\mu,\mu)),\qquad f_{\ell}(\mu)\doteq\underline{a}_{\ell}((\mathcal{V}_{\ell}f,0),(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\mu,\mu)).

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{a_{\ell}}
the energy norms associated to the bilinear forms
\underline{a}_{\ell}
and
a_{\ell}
, respectively. We also introduce the notation
\|\cdot\|_{\underline{a_{\ell},X}}
,
\|\cdot\|_{a_{\ell},X}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\underline{1,\ell,X}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,\ell,X}
to denote the respective norms restricted to a subset
X
of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
.

The traditional analysis of multigrid methods relies on the use of an inner product on the space of unknowns. For hybrid methods, this inner product has to be defined for skeletal functions. We take a different approach to the one taken in
[
20
]
, and define the skeleton inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\ell}
and its associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell}
for
\lambda,\mu\in M_{\ell}
as

(2.15)

\langle\lambda_{\ell},\mu_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\doteq(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\mu_{\ell})_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\ ,\quad\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}\doteq\langle\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}.

Using the above definition, we can define the operator
A_{\ell}:M_{\ell}\to M_{\ell}
associated to the bilinear form
a_{\ell}
and the right-hand side
b_{\ell}
as

a_{\ell}(\lambda,\mu)=\langle A_{\ell}\lambda,\mu\rangle_{\ell}\ ,\quad\forall\lambda,\mu\in M_{\ell},\qquad b_{\ell}(\mu)=\langle f_{\ell},\mu\rangle_{\ell}\ ,\quad\forall\mu\in M_{\ell}.

It is a well know result
[
20
, Section 6.7]
that the maximal eigenvalue
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})
of the operator
A_{\ell}
satisfies

(2.16)

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})\simeq h_{\ell}^{-2}.

2.2.
Polynomial spaces on interfaces

We now discuss the choice of the interface polynomial spaces
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
. As noted earlier, the most natural choice is to consider discontinuous polynomials on each face composing the interface, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)=\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)
, which yields the standard
hho
discretisation. This has the advantage of being quite straightforward to implement in existing
hho
codes. This choice, however, has important drawbacks in the context of multigrid methods. As we aggregate elements to form coarse elements, the number of faces composing an interface increases. This leads to a suboptimal reduction in the number of face unknowns on coarse levels. Indeed, having many small faces on coarse levels does not improve the approximation properties of the method, since these are tied to the element size
h_{t}
. This means many face unknowns are essentially redundant, leading to an increased computational cost and memory consumption that does not translate into better accuracy.
Moreover, it has been observed in practice
[
20
,
22
,
38
]
that traditional face-block smoothers perform poorly for this choice of interface polynomial space, with convergence deteriorating as the number of levels increases. This stems from the fact that, despite coarsening the elements, the unknowns in a single face remain associated to high-frequency modes, leading to ineffective smoothing on coarser levels. This suggests that some form of coarsening of the face unknowns is necessary to obtain an efficient multigrid method. In
[
20
,
22
,
38
]
, the authors achieve this by coarsening under the impractical assumption that coarse faces are planar. This severely limits the possible hierarchies of meshes that can be considered. The work in
[
20
,
21
]
tries to circumvent this issue with the use of non-nested hierarchies. However, the proposed algorithms rely on complex prolongation operators that are computationally expensive and have only been implemented in 2D. Overall, an efficient and elegant solution that allows for agglomeration-based coarsening strategies has not yet been proposed.

In this work, we propose an alternative coarsening strategy for face unknowns that does not require planar coarse faces. To achieve this, we draw inspiration from recent works on
hho
methods for curved domains
[
51
]
.
In
[
51
]
, it is shown that optimal approximation properties can be obtained by defining
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)
as

(2.17)

\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)=\mathbb{P}^{0}(F)+\nabla\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\Omega)\cdot\bm{n_{F}},

where
\bm{n_{F}}
is a unit normal vector to the interface
F
. On each interface, this space has a maximum dimension that is independent of the number of faces composing the interface, thus maintaining efficiency as we agglomerate elements. Moreover, if the interface
F
is planar, we recover the standard HHO space
\mathcal{P}^{k}(F)=\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)
.
Although we do not go into detail here, these spaces are built by performing a local orthogonalisation process on each interface
[
51
]
. This process can also be done hierarchically, by using
M_{\ell}
within the construction of the basis for
M_{\ell-1}
, for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
.

3.
Geometric multigrid solver

In this section, we propose a geometric multigrid solver for the
hho
discretisation. We refer, e.g., to
[
28
]
for an introduction to these techniques. The two key ingredients to define a geometric multigrid solver are the intergrid transfer operators and the smoothing operators.

First, we define the intergrid transfer operators. The prolongation operator maps coarse-level functions in
\ell-1
to fine-level functions in
\ell
; it is denoted by
I_{\ell}:M_{\ell-1}\to M_{\ell}
and is defined in Sect.
3.1
. The restriction operator, mapping fine-level functions to coarse-level functions, is defined as the adjoint of the prolongation with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product, i.e.,

\langle I_{\ell}^{\prime}v,w\rangle_{\ell}=\langle v,I_{\ell}w\rangle_{\ell}\quad\forall v\in M_{\ell},\ w\in M_{\ell-1}.

The smoother on level
\ell
is denoted by
R_{\ell}:M_{\ell}\to M_{\ell}
and is defined in Sect.
3.2
.

Given these ingredients, we can now recursively define the multigrid operator on level
\ell
, denoted by

B_{\ell}:M_{\ell}\to M_{\ell},\quad b_{\ell}\mapsto B_{\ell}b_{\ell},

with
m_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
smoothing steps. On the coarsest level
\ell=0
, let
B_{0}\doteq A_{0}^{-1}
be the exact inverse of the coarse grid operator. For every other level
\ell=\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
B_{\ell}
is defined as the operator that given
b_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
performs the following steps:

•

Sets
x^{(0)}=0\in M_{\ell}

•

Performs
m_{\ell}
smoothing steps

x^{(i+1)}=x^{(i)}+R_{\ell}(b_{\ell}-A_{\ell}x^{(i)})\quad i=0,\dots,m_{\ell}-1

•

Applies the coarse grid correction on level
\ell-1

x^{(m_{\ell}+1)}=x^{(m_{\ell})}+I_{\ell}B_{\ell-1}I_{\ell}^{\prime}(b_{\ell}-A_{\ell}x^{(m_{\ell})})

•

Performs
m_{\ell}
smoothing steps, to maintain symmetry of the operator

x^{(i+1)}=x^{(i)}+R_{\ell}(b_{\ell}-A_{\ell}x^{(i)})\quad i=m_{\ell}+1,\dots,2m_{\ell}

•

Returns
B_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}=x^{(2m_{\ell}+1)}

If
m_{\ell}=m
is constant for all levels, we obtain the standard multigrid V-cycle. Otherwise, we assume that
m_{\ell}
fulfills the following condition:

(3.1)

\rho_{1}m_{\ell}\geq m_{\ell-1}\geq\rho_{2}m_{\ell}\quad\forall\ell\leq L,

for some constants
0<\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{2}
.

The complete solver can be written as

B_{\ell}=(I-R_{\ell}^{T}A_{\ell})^{m_{\ell}}(I-I_{\ell}B_{\ell-1}I_{\ell}^{\prime})(I-R_{\ell}A_{\ell})^{m_{\ell}}.

In practice, access to the right-hand sides
b_{\ell}
at every level
\ell
is not required, but rather we implement
B_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell},r_{\ell})
where
r_{\ell}=b_{\ell}-A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}
is the residual at level
\ell
and update both the correction and residual simultaneously.

3.1.
Intergrid transfer operators

We explore two different choices for the prolongation operator
I_{\ell}:M_{\ell-1}\to M_{\ell}
, which were first proposed in
[
20
,
31
,
32
]
, and we adapt them here to our interface spaces. The idea behind these operators is to reconstruct a higher-order polynomial on coarse cells that is then projected onto the fine interfaces.

We start by defining some useful intermediary operators. First, we define
{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}:M_{\ell}\rightarrow\underline{U}_{\ell}
the hybrid extension of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}
, namely

(3.2)

{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\doteq(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})\quad\forall\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}.

We denote by
\gamma_{\ell}:\underline{U}_{\ell}\to M_{\ell}
the trace operator on level
\ell
, namely

(3.3)

\gamma_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}\doteq\lambda_{\ell}\text{ for }\underline{u}_{\ell}=(u_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})\in\underline{U}_{\ell}.

We also define
\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}:U_{\ell-1}\rightarrow M_{\ell}
as a weighted

-orthogonal projector onto fine interfaces, given by

(3.4)

\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}(v)|_{F}\doteq\begin{cases}\pi_{F}^{k}(v|_{T})|_{F}&\text{if }F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1},\\
\alpha_{FT}\pi_{F}^{k}(v|_{T})|_{F}+\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}\pi_{F}^{k}(v|_{T^{\prime}})|_{F}&\text{if }F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}.\end{cases},

where
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
are the two coarse elements sharing the coarse interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}
and the weights
\alpha_{FT}
and
\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}
are chosen as

(3.5)

\alpha_{FT}=\frac{|T|}{|T|+|T^{\prime}|},\quad\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}=\frac{|T^{\prime}|}{|T|+|T^{\prime}|},

thus
\alpha_{FT}+\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}=1
. However, the analysis applies to any partition of unity.
For convenience, we define
\underline{\Pi}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\doteq{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}:U_{\ell-1}\to\underline{U}_{\ell}
as the discrete harmonic extension of
\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}
to the full
hho

Definition 3.1
.

We define
I^{U}_{\ell}\doteq\Pi^{\mathrm{av}}_{\ell}\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to M_{\ell}
and

.

For both prolongation operators, we define their natural extension to the full

3.2.
Smoothing operators

In this work, we use a family of so-called star-patch smoothers
[
24
]
, which are block-Jacobi relaxation methods in which each block corresponds to the face unknowns in a patch of cells around mesh topological entities (e.g., vertices, edges, and faces), excluding those on the boundary of the patch that are fixed. These relaxation methods can be viewed as subspace correction methods
[
50
]
induced by a space decomposition of the form

M_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\ell}}N_{i},\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,L,

where each subspace
N_{i}
is constructed by gathering the degrees of freedom associated with a given subset of topological entities in the mesh and
n_{\ell}
is the number of such subspaces (patches). The coarse space is not decomposed into patches. These smoothers have been successfully used in multigrid methods to obtain mesh-, order-, and parameter-independent convergence rates for conforming discretisations of the
H(\text{div},\Omega)
and
H(\text{curl},\Omega)
Riesz maps
[
5
,
6
]
, for incompressible linear elasticity
[
44
]
and for the Navier-Stokes equations
[
25
]
.

In this work, we will use the additive variant of space correction methods, referred to as parallel subspace correction methods in
[
50
]
. The parallel subspace correction preconditioner associated with the decomposition
\{N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\ell}}
is given by

(3.6)

R_{\ell}\doteq\omega\tilde{R_{\ell}}\ ,\quad\tilde{R_{\ell}}\doteq\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\ell}}I_{i}A_{i}^{-1}I_{i}^{\prime},\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,L,

where
\omega
is a constant damping factor,
I_{i}:N_{i}\to M_{\ell}
is the canonical embedding and
A_{i}:N_{i}\to N_{i}^{\prime}
is the local operator defined as

\langle A_{i}u_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{\ell}=a_{\ell}(u_{i},v_{i})\quad\forall u_{i},v_{i}\in N_{i}.

The operator
R_{\ell}
is then used as a preconditioner for the system matrix
A_{\ell}
in a Richardson iteration as described in Sect.
3
. We consider two different types of star-patch smoothers:

•

Face-star patch
smoothers: To correctly capture the error modes associated with each level, we need the span of the blocks to include all face unknowns associated with a given interface. Thus, for each interface
\overline{F}=\overline{T}\cap\overline{T^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, we define the interface-star patch as the set of face unknowns supported on
F
.
In order to follow the standard auxiliary space notation, we consider a numbering of the faces in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{{\rm in}}
, and denote its elements by
F_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n_{\ell}\doteq|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{{\rm in}}|
. Then, we define the corresponding patches as
N_{i}=M_{\ell}(F_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n_{\ell}
.
The solution in each patch can then be seen as a local problem on
\Omega_{i}
being the interior of
\overline{T}\cup\overline{T^{\prime}}
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega_{i}
.

•

Vertex-star patch
smoothers: We can improve the smoothing properties of the face-star patch smoother by enlarging the patches to include all face unknowns belonging to the interfaces connected to a given vertex. For each vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\ell}
, we define the vertex-star patch as the set of face unknowns supported on all interfaces
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
such that
v\in\overline{F}
. In 3D, we can define the
edge-star patch
smoother in a similar way, by gathering all face unknowns supported on interfaces connected to a given edge
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
.

A schematic representation of the two star patch smoothers considered is shown in Figure
1
.

.

Generating interfaces are represented as thick colored lines. For the vertex-interface-star patch the generating vertex is represented as a colored square. Note that only skeletal

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})

K_{\ell}\doteq(\mathbbm{1}-(\mathbbm{1}-R_{\ell}A_{\ell})(\mathbbm{1}-R_{\ell}^{\prime}A_{\ell}))A_{\ell}^{-1}.

(Boundedness of

For all

(Smoothing hypothesis)
.

The iteration operator
\mathbbm{1}-R_{\ell}A_{\ell}
has positive spectrum. Moreover, for all
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}

\frac{\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})}\leq C_{3}\langle K_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}.

where

\delta=\frac{C_{1}C_{3}}{m-C_{1}C_{3}}\quad\text{with}\quad m>2C_{1}C_{3}.

Proof.

This result is an immediate consequence of
[

, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

4.1
,

4.1.
Proof of Assumptions

and

We introduce three new assumptions on the operator

, that we will prove in Sect.
5
for the specific case of the two operators defined in Sect.

. First, we will replace Assumption
4.1
with the following assumption on the boundedness of the energy of
I_{\ell}
:

Assumption 4.6

(Prolongation stability)
.

The prolongation operator
I_{\ell}
satisfies

(4.3)

\|I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell}}\leq C_{I}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}\quad\forall\lambda_{\ell-1}\in M_{\ell-1}.

To prove Assumption
4.1
, we also introduce two new assumptions on the approximation properties of
I_{\ell}
:

Assumption 4.7
.

For all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that
I_{\ell}
fulfils the following bound:

\|\underline{J}_{\ell}z-I_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Assumption 4.8
.

For all
\lambda_{\ell-1}\in M_{\ell-1}
, we have that
I_{\ell}
fulfils the following bound:

\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

The fact that Assumption
4.6
implies Assumption
4.1
is shown by the following lemma:

Lemma 4.9
.

Under Assumption
4.6
, we have that for all
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{I}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{I}^{2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{I}^{4})^{1/2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

Proof.

To prove the first bound, we use the definition of
P_{\ell-1}
together with a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Assumption
4.6
to obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=a_{\ell-1}(P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})=a_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell},I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\leq C_{I}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

Dividing by
\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}
gives the desired bound. The second bound is a consequence of the first result and Assumption
4.6
:

\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\leq C_{I}\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}\leq C_{I}^{2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}.

Finally, for the last bound, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}

\displaystyle=a_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}-2a_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell},I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})+\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}-2a_{\ell-1}(P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})+\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}+\|I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}

where we have used that
a_{\ell-1}(P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})=\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}^{2}\geq 0
. The result then follows from the first two bounds.
∎

After proving Assumption
4.1
, we now focus on the proof of Assumption
4.2
.

Definition 4.10

(Consistency error)
.

For all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
and
\mu_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
, denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(z;\mu_{\ell})
the consistency error
[
39

, that is

(4.7)

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(z;\mu_{\ell})\doteq(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\mu_{\ell})-\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{J}_{\ell}z,{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\mu_{\ell}).

The following bound holds for the
hho
consistency error. For all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mu_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}

[
39
, Lemma 2.18]
,

(4.8)

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(z;\mu_{\ell})\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})}\|\mu_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}.

Theorem 4.11
.

Under Assumptions
4.7
and
4.8
, for all
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
the following bound holds:

\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}.

Proof.

Consider the dual problem: find
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta z=\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}).

Then by adding and subtracting
(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell})-(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle=(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})+(-\Delta z,\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\mathfrak{T}_{2}

The first term can be bounded in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(z;\lambda_{\ell})-\mathcal{E}_{\ell-1}(z;P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})+\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{J}_{\ell}z,{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell})-a_{\ell-1}(\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z,P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(z;\lambda_{\ell})-\mathcal{E}_{\ell-1}(z;P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})+\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{J}_{\ell}z-I_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z,{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}+h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}+h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}

where we have used the consistency error bound (
4.8
) for the first two terms and a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality together with Assumption
4.7
for the last term, followed by the continuity of
P_{\ell-1}
.

The second term can be bounded in the following way:

\mathfrak{T}_{2}\leq|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}.

where we have used Assumption
4.8
, followed by the continuity of
P_{\ell-1}
.

Putting things back together and using elliptic regularity of

, we have that

\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}},

and dividing by
\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
concludes our proof.
∎

Theorem 4.12

(Approximation property)
.

Let
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
. Under Assumptions
4.7
and
4.8
, we have the following bound:

|a_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})|\lesssim\frac{\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})}.

Proof.

We use the definitions of
A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\ell}
to write

|a_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})|=\langle\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell},A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\leq\|\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}

Using Theorem

, we obtain

\|\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{a_{\ell}}=h_{\ell-1}\langle A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}^{1/2}\leq h_{\ell-1}\|A_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{1/2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{1/2}.

Then, we obtain the following bound

\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}=\langle\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}=\langle A_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\leq\|A_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\lesssim\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})^{-1}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}.

Then
\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\lesssim\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})^{-1}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}
. Using (

) and (

) gives

. Thus,

\|\lambda_{\ell}-I_{\ell}P_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})^{-1/2}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}\lesssim\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})^{-1}\|A_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}.

4.2.
Proof of Assumption
4.3

This section is devoted to proving Assumption
4.3
for the two star-patch smoothers defined in Sect.
3.2
. We will, however, recast it within the framework of subspace correction methods
[
50
]
.

Given the patch-based smoother
R_{\ell}
defined in (
3.6
), proving Assumption
4.3
boils down to studying the spectrum of the operator
D_{\ell}\doteq\tilde{R_{\ell}}A_{\ell}=\omega^{-1}R_{\ell}A_{\ell}
. In particular, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 4.13
.

Assume that there exist constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
such that, for all
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}

(4.9)

a_{\ell}(D_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},D_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell})\leq c_{0}a_{\ell}(D_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}),

and

(4.10)

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{i}\in N_{i}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\|\lambda_{i}\|_{A_{i}}^{2}\leq c_{1}h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}.

Then
R_{\ell}
fulfills Assumption
4.3
by choosing
\omega<c_{0}^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have that (
4.9
) implies that the spectrum of
D_{\ell}
is bounded by
c_{0}
. By taking
\omega<c_{0}^{-1}
, we have that
\mathbbm{1}-R_{\ell}A_{\ell}
has a positive spectrum, as desired.

For the upper spectrum bound, we use a well-known identity in subspace correction literature
[
49
, Lemma 2.4]
, that is

a_{\ell}(D_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{i}\in N_{i}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\|\lambda_{i}\|_{A_{i}}^{2}.

Moreover, notice that

\langle R_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle=\omega^{-1}a_{\ell}(A_{\ell}^{-1}\tilde{R_{\ell}}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})=\omega^{-1}a_{\ell}(D_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}).

Using the stable decomposition in (
4.10
) we obtain

\langle R_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\leq\omega^{-1}c_{1}h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}.

Recall that by standard inverse estimates, the maximal eigenvalue of
A_{\ell}
scales as
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})\simeq h_{\ell}^{-2}
. In particular, there exists a constant
c_{2}
such that
h^{-2}\leq c_{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})
. Then

\langle R_{\ell}^{-1}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\omega c_{2}}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}.

Because
R_{\ell}
and its inverse are symmetric positive definite with respect to the
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\ell}
inner product, an upper bound on the maximal eigenvalue of the inverse strictly defines a lower bound on the minimal eigenvalue of the operator itself, that is

\langle R_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}\rangle_{\ell}\geq\frac{\omega c_{2}}{c_{1}}\frac{\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\ell}^{2}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A_{\ell})}.

Finally, to prove the above bound for
K_{\ell}
instead of
R_{\ell}
, we can proceed as in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

The first condition (
4.9
) measures the interaction between the subspaces
N_{i}
. It was shown in
[
50
, Theorem 4.1, Lemma 4.6]
that a bound for
c_{0}
is given by the spectral radius of the so-called interaction matrix
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{S}\times N_{S}}
, where its entries
\Theta_{ij}
are defined as the smallest constants satisfying a strengthened Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

a_{\ell}(D_{i}u,D_{j}v)\leq\Theta_{ij}(D_{i}u,u)_{a_{\ell}}^{1/2}(D_{j}v,v)_{a_{\ell}}^{1/2}\quad\forall u,v\in V,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,N_{\ell},

with
D_{i}\doteq I_{i}A_{i}^{-1}I_{i}^{\prime}A_{\ell}
.
It can then be shown that (see
[
50
, Lemma 4.6]
,
[
24
, Equation 2.15]
)

(4.11)

c_{0}\leq\rho(\Theta)\leq N_{O},

where
N_{O}
is the maximum number of overlapping subspaces
N_{i}
. For our star-patch-based smoothers, this number is uniformly bounded independently of the mesh size and the level
\ell
, as as consequence of the number of interfaces per element being uniformly bounded. The condition on the damping factor
\omega
, found in Theorem
4.13
, then becomes
\omega<1/N_{O}
.

The second condition (
4.10
) follows from a stable decomposition of the space
M_{\ell}
into the subspaces
N_{i}
. We will first prove it for the simplest case of the edge-based patches (which form a sort of partition of unity for the interfaces), and then give a proof for more general patches.

Lemma 4.14
.

For each interface
F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, take
\tau_{j}
to be the patch formed by the (at most two) elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
sharing

\eta_{j}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{F}&\text{ on }F=F_{j}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}.

Then the following bound holds:

\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{1,h,\tau_{j}}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{j})}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{j})}^{2}.

Proof.

On each element
t\in\tau_{j}
, we have that the hybrid function associated to
\eta_{i}
, i.e.,
\underline{u}_{j}=(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\eta_{j},\eta_{j})
, is the energy minimizer among all hybrid functions with the same trace on
\partial t
. Thus,

\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{t}}^{2}\leq\|(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\eta_{j})\|_{\underline{a}_{t}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell},\eta_{j})\|_{\underline{1,t}}^{2}=\|\nabla\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{t}^{2}+h_{t}^{-1}\|\lambda-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{F_{j}}^{2}+h_{t}^{-1}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t\setminus F_{j}}^{2}.

By summing over all elements in
\tau_{j}
, then using the inverse trace inequality, we obtain

\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}=\sum_{t\in\tau_{j}}\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{t}}^{2}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{1,h,\tau_{j}}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{j})}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{j})}^{2},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.15

(Patch stable decomposition)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
denote the set of interfaces generating the patch associated to
N_{i}
, and let
\alpha_{i,F}>0
denote weights such that

\sum_{i:F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F}=1\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}.

Given
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
, we consider the decomposition
\lambda_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\lambda_{i}
with

\lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{i,F}\lambda_{\ell}|_{F}&\text{ on }F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\\
0&\text{ elsewhere }\end{cases}.

Then, the following bound holds:

(4.12)

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\|\lambda_{i}\|_{a_{i}}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

By construction, we have that
\lambda_{i}=\sum_{F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F_{j}}\eta_{j},
where
\eta_{j}
is defined as in Lemma
4.14
. Since
0<\alpha_{i,F_{j}}\leq 1
, we can bound
\sum_{i:F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F}^{2}\leq\sum_{i:F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F}=1
.
Then using Lemma
4.14
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\|\lambda_{i}\|_{a_{i}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\sum_{F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F_{j}}^{2}\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}=\sum_{F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}\left(\sum_{i:F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}\alpha_{i,F}^{2}\right)\|\eta_{j}\|_{a_{\ell}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{j})}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we have used that the number of overlapping
\tau_{j}
on each element (i.e faces around an element) is uniformly bounded independently of the mesh size and the level
\ell
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

The analysis is independent of the choice of face space, and thus applies equally to the classical
hho
space
\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}
, provided the smoother blocks contain all
dof
associated to each interface. However, since the number of degrees of freedom per interface grows with the number of planar faces, such smoothers are not scalable in general. Reducing the smoother blocks to only containing
dof
on a single planar face will not yield a scalable smoother as discussed in
[
20
]
.

5.
Proofs of the prolongation assumptions

This section will be devoted to the proof of Assumptions
4.6
,
4.7
and
4.8
for the prolongation operators
I^{U}_{\ell}
and
I^{R}_{\ell}
defined in Sect.
3.1
. We start by introducing some operators that will be used within the analysis, followed by technical lemmas and finally the proof of the assumptions.

5.1.
Broken
hho
interpolator

First, we define a broken version of the standard
hho
interpolation operator, and state a useful property we will be using later.

Definition 5.1

(Broken
hho
interpolation operator)
.

We define the broken
hho
interpolation operator
\underline{J}_{\ell,T}:H^{1}(T)\to\underline{U}_{\ell,T}
at each coarse cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
as the restriction of the usual
hho
interpolation operator
\underline{J}_{\ell}

[
39
, Equation (2.8)]
, that is

\underline{J}_{\ell,T}v_{T}=((\pi_{t}^{k+1}v_{T})_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)},(\pi_{F}^{k}v_{T})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(T)}).

Lemma 5.2

(
hho
interpolation boundedness)
.

Given
v_{T}\in H^{1}(T)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
, the following bound holds:

\|\underline{J}_{\ell,T}v_{T}\|_{1,h,T}=\sum_{t\in T}\left(|\pi_{t}^{k+1}v_{T}|_{1,t}+h_{t}^{-1}\|\pi_{F}^{k}v_{T}-\pi_{t}^{k+1}v_{T}\|_{\partial t}\right)\lesssim|v_{T}|_{1,T}.

Proof.

This is a standard result for the standard
hho
interpolation operator. The proof is analogous to that of
[
39
, Proposition 2.2]
.
∎

For a scalar function
v\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
, we define the jump of
v
across a face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
as
\llbracket v\rrbracket=v|_{t}-v|_{t^{\prime}}
, where
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
are the two elements sharing face
F
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
z\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have the following bound:

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\|\underline{J}_{\ell,T}(z-\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z)\|_{1,\ell,T}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

Proof.

For the first bound, we can use Lemma
5.2
together with the approximation properties of the elliptic projection
\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}

[
39
, Theorem 1.48]
to write

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\|\underline{J}_{\ell,T}(z-\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z)\|_{1,\ell,T}\lesssim\|\nabla(z-\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}=\|\nabla(z-\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the second bound, we add and subtract
z
on each face, then using a triangle inequality, and finally the approximation properties of
\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}

[
39
, Theorem 1.48]
, we can write

h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z\rrbracket\|_{F}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|z-\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z\|_{\partial T}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-1/2}h_{\ell-1}^{3/2}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

By assumption, we have
h_{\ell-1}/h_{\ell}\simeq 1
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Averaging interpolation operator

Second, we will study the stability of a particular averaged interpolation operator from broken, locally
H^{1}
functions to the
hho
space. This operator will be used as an intermediate step to prove the stability of
I^{U}_{\ell}
and
I^{R}_{\ell}
.

Definition 5.4
.

Define the operator
\underline{W}_{\ell}:H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})\to\underline{U}_{\ell}
such that, given
z_{\ell-1}=(z_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})
,
\underline{w}_{\ell}=\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}
has components

(5.1)

w_{t}=\pi_{t}^{k+1}z_{T}\quad t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)\ ,\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1},

and

(5.2)

w_{F}=\begin{cases}\pi_{F}^{k}z_{T}&if $F\in(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})(T),$\\
\pi_{F}^{k}(\alpha_{FT}z_{T}+\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}z_{T^{\prime}})&if $F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1},F\subset\partial T\cap\partial T^{\prime}.$\end{cases},

where
\alpha_{FT}
and
\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}
are the weights defined in (
3.5
).

Remark 5.5
.

The operator
\underline{W}_{\ell}
is close to the operators
\underline{\Pi}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}
defined in Sect.
3.1
. Indeed, we have that for all
z_{\ell-1}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})
,

(5.3)

\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}=(\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k+1}z_{\ell-1},\Pi^{\mathrm{av}}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}),

and thus the two operators only differ in the bulk component, where
\underline{\Pi}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}
uses the discrete harmonic extension of
\Pi^{\mathrm{av}}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}
instead of the local
L^{2}
projection of
z_{\ell-1}
.

Remark 5.6
.

Within each subdomain
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
, the operator
\underline{W}_{\ell}
is close to the broken
hho
interpolation operator
\underline{J}_{\ell,T}
defined in Definition
5.1
. Indeed, we have that for all
z_{\ell-1}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})
,

(5.4)

(\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1})|_{T}=\underline{J}_{\ell,T}z_{T}+\underline{\xi}_{T},

with
\underline{\xi}_{T}=(0,\xi_{T})
a purely skeleton function that only depends on the jumps of
z_{\ell-1}
across the faces of
T
, namely

(5.5)

\xi_{T,F}=\begin{cases}0&if $F\in(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})(T)$\\
\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}\pi_{F}^{k}\left(z_{T^{\prime}}-z_{T}\right)&if $F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1},F=\partial T\cap\partial T^{\prime}$\end{cases}.

This is a very useful property that will be used extensively in the analysis.

Lemma 5.7
.

For any
z_{\ell-1}\in H^{1}(T)
, the following bound holds:

(5.6)

\|\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}\|_{1,\ell}^{2}\lesssim|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{2}.

Proof.

From (
5.4
) and the triangle inequality, we have that

\|\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}\|_{1,\ell}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\|\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}\|_{1,\ell,T}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\left(\|\underline{J}_{\ell,T}z_{T}\|_{1,\ell,T}^{2}+\|\underline{\xi}_{T}\|_{1,\ell,T}^{2}\right).

The first term can be bounded using Lemma
5.2
. For the second term, we use the definition of
\underline{\xi}_{T}
and the fact that
\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
to obtain

\|\underline{\xi}_{T}\|_{1,\ell,T}^{2}=\sum_{F\in\partial T}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\xi_{T,F}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\partial T}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{F}^{k}(z_{T^{\prime}}-z_{T})\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\partial T}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|z_{T^{\prime}}-z_{T}\|_{F}^{2},

where we have used the stability of
\pi_{F}^{k}
. Summing over all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}
and using the definition of the jump, we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.3.
Bubble function bounds

We now introduce three lemmas that bound the energy of bubble functions within
hho
spaces:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\underline{\delta}_{\ell}=(\delta_{\ell},0)\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
be a bubble function on level
\ell
. Then, we have the following bound:

\|\delta_{\ell}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell}\|\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}.

Proof.

On each
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality gives

\|\delta_{t}\|_{t}\lesssim h_{t}\|\nabla\delta_{t}\|_{t}+h_{t}^{1/2}\|\delta_{t}\|_{\partial t}=h_{t}\|\underline{\delta}\|_{1,t},

where the equality stems from the fact
\underline{\delta}_{\ell}
has zero traces. Summing over all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and using the energy norm equivalence concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
z\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. For some level
\ell
, define two hybrid functions:

\underline{u}_{\ell}\doteq\underline{J}_{\ell}z=(\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k+1}z,\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}^{k}z)\ ,\quad\underline{v}_{\ell}\doteq{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\gamma_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell}z=(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}^{k}z,\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}^{k}z).

Then, we have the following bound:

\|\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We define the bubble function
\underline{\delta}_{\ell}=\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}=(\delta_{\ell},0)
. Then, by a-orthogonality of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}
with bubble functions, we have

\|\underline{\delta}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}^{2}=\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{\delta}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})=\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})=\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{\ell},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\right)_{t}\right]+s_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell}).

The consistency term can be bounded by remembering that
\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{\ell}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{J}_{\ell}z=\pi_{t}^{1,k+1}z
and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{u}_{\ell},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\pi_{t}^{1,k+1}z,\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\right)_{t}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla z,\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\Delta z,\delta_{t}\right)_{t}+(\nabla\pi_{t}^{1,k+1}z\cdot n,0)_{\partial t}\leq|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\delta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\underline{\delta}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

where we have used that
(\nabla\pi_{t}^{1,k+1}z,\nabla w)_{t}=(\nabla z,\nabla w)_{t}
for all
w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(t)
, then the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}
, then a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and finally Lemma
5.8
on the bubble
\underline{\delta}
.

The stabilisation term can be bounded as follows:

s_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})\leq s_{\ell}(\underline{J}_{\ell}z,\underline{J}_{\ell}z)^{1/2}s_{\ell}(\underline{\delta}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})^{1/2}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

where we have first used a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and then the consistency of the stabilisation
[
39
, Lemma 2.14]
.
Putting everything together and dividing by
\|\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
z_{\ell-1}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})
be a cell-wise broken polynomial function on the coarse mesh. Define two hybrid functions on the fine mesh:

\underline{u}_{\ell}\doteq\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}=\left(\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k+1}z_{\ell-1},\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}z_{\ell-1}\right)\ ,\quad\underline{v}_{\ell}\doteq{\underline{\Pi}}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}z_{\ell-1}=\left(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}z_{\ell-1},\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}z_{\ell-1}\right)

.
We have the following bounds:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{F}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell}|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{F}

Proof.

Note that
\underline{\delta}_{\ell}=\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}=(\delta_{\ell},0)
is a bubble function on level
\ell
. By a-orthogonality of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}
with bubble functions, we have

\|\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}^{2}=\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{\delta}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})=\underline{a}_{\ell}(\underline{u}_{\ell},\underline{\delta}_{\ell})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}a_{t}(\underline{u}_{t},\underline{\delta}_{t}).

In each subdomain
T
, we can decompose
\underline{u}_{\ell}|_{T}
as in Remark
5.6
to get
\underline{u}_{\ell}|_{T}=\underline{J}_{\ell,T}z_{\ell-1}+\underline{\xi}_{T}
with
\underline{\xi}_{T}=(0,\xi_{T})
a purely skeleton function given by

(5.9)

\xi_{T,F}=\begin{cases}0&if $F\in(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1})(T)$\\
\alpha_{FT^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{F}^{k}z_{T^{\prime}}-\pi_{F}^{k}z_{T}\right)&if $F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1},F=\partial T\cap\partial T^{\prime}$\end{cases}

Then we can use the linearity of
a_{t}
to write

\|\underline{\delta}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}^{2}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}a_{t}(\underline{J}_{\ell,t}z_{\ell-1},\underline{\delta}_{t})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}a_{t}(\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t},\underline{\delta}_{t}).

For the first terms, we can use the polynomial consistency of the stabilisation
[
39
, Proposition 2.13]
, then the fact that
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{J}_{\ell,t}z_{\ell-1}=\nabla\pi_{t}^{1,k+1}z_{\ell-1}=\nabla z_{\ell-1}
on polynomials, to write

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}a_{t}(\underline{J}_{\ell,t}z_{\ell-1},\underline{\delta}_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{J}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}\right)_{t}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla z_{\ell-1},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle\leq|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}|\delta_{h}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})}\leq|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\|\underline{\delta}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

which is what (
5.7
) requires. For (
5.8
), we can develop the expression further by using the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}
, then a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on the
\underline{a}_{\ell}
norm, and finally Lemma
5.8
to write

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}a_{t}(\underline{J}_{\ell,t}z_{\ell-1},\underline{\delta}_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{J}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}\right)_{t}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\left(\nabla z_{\ell-1},\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}\underline{\delta}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}-\left(\Delta z_{\ell-1},\delta_{t}\right)_{t}+(\nabla z_{\ell-1}\cdot n,0)\leq|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\|\delta_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\|\underline{\delta}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

For the second term, we can use a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to write

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}a_{t}(\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t},\underline{\delta}_{t})\leq\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}a_{t}(\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t},\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t})\right)^{1/2}\cdot\|\underline{\delta}_{\ell}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}},

and then

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}a_{t}(\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t},\underline{\xi}_{T}|_{t})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(T)}h_{t}^{-1}\|\xi_{T}\|_{\partial t}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-1}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\|\xi_{T}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-1}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}

where we have used that
\underline{\xi}_{T}
has zero bulk and zero interior traces in each subdomain
T
. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.4.
Abstract framework for prolongation operators

We can now use the previous lemmas to propose a unified framework for prolongation operators that are defined by the averaging on
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
of reconstructed coarse bulk quantities, namely
I_{\ell}\doteq\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\Theta_{\ell-1}
for some reconstruction operator
\Theta_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to U_{\ell-1}
.

Lemma 5.11

(Abstract framework for Assumptions
4.6
and
4.8
)
.

Let
\Theta_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to U_{\ell-1}
be an operator such that for all
\lambda_{\ell-1}\in M_{\ell-1}
, we have that

|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}\ ,\quad\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{F}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

Define the prolongation operator
I_{\ell}\doteq\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\Theta_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to M_{\ell}
. Then for all
\lambda_{\ell-1}\in M_{\ell-1}
we have the following bounds:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}

Proof.

Let
z_{\ell-1}=\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}
, and define the hybrid functions
\underline{u}_{\ell}=\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}
and
\underline{v}_{\ell}=\underline{\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}
.
Lemma
5.10
can be applied to
\underline{u}_{\ell}
and
\underline{v}_{\ell}
to write

\|\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\|\llbracket\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{F}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}},

where we have used the lemma hypotheses for the last pass. This proves (
5.10
).

To prove (
5.11
), we can use the triangle inequality to write

\|{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\leq\|\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}+\|\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}},

where we have used (
5.10
) for the first term, and Lemma
5.7
together with the lemma hypotheses for the second term.

To prove (
5.12
), we notice that
\underline{u}_{\ell}-\underline{v}_{\ell}
is a bubble function on level
\ell
, and that the bulks of
\underline{u}_{\ell}
and
\underline{v}_{\ell}
are given, respectively, by

u_{\ell}=\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k+1}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}=\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\ ,\quad v_{\ell}=\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}.

Thus we can use Lemma
5.8
to write

\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell}\|\underline{W}_{\ell}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.12

(Abstract framework for Assumption
4.7
)
.

Let
\Theta_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to U_{\ell-1}
be an operator such that for all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have:

|\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Define the prolongation operator
I_{\ell}\doteq\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\Theta_{\ell-1}:M_{\ell-1}\to M_{\ell}
. Then
I_{\ell}
fulfils the bound in Assumption
4.7
, that is, for all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that
I_{\ell}
fulfils the following bound:

\|\underline{J}_{\ell}z-{\underline{I}}_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
z_{\ell-1}=\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z
. We define the following hybrid functions:

\underline{u}_{1}\doteq\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}\ ,\quad\underline{u}_{2}\doteq{\underline{\Pi}}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}z_{\ell-1}.

By adding and subtracting
\underline{u}_{1}
and
\underline{u}_{2}
, then using a triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\underline{J}_{\ell}z-{\underline{I}}_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\leq\|\underline{J}_{\ell}z-\underline{u}_{1}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}+\|\underline{u}_{1}-\underline{u}_{2}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}+\|\underline{u}_{2}-{\underline{I}}_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\doteq\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\mathfrak{T}_{2}+\mathfrak{T}_{3}.

For the first term, we use the split of
\underline{W}_{\ell}
in Remark
5.6
, followed by Lemma
5.3
, to write

\mathfrak{T}_{1}=\|\underline{J}_{\ell}z-\underline{W}_{\ell}z_{\ell-1}\|_{1,h}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}\|\underline{J}_{\ell,T}(z-z_{\ell-1})\|_{1,h,T}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the second term, we observe that
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
matches the application of Lemma
5.10
with
z_{\ell-1}
. We can then use Lemma
5.3
to bound the jump term, and the continuity of
\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}
to write

\mathfrak{T}_{2}=\|\underline{u}_{1}-\underline{u}_{2}\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z_{\ell-1}|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, the last term
\mathfrak{T}_{3}
can be bounded by noticing that

{\underline{I}}_{\ell}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z={\underline{\Pi}}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\Theta_{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z={\underline{\Pi}}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z,

and then using the stability of
\underline{\Pi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}}
together with Lemma
5.9
:

\mathfrak{T}_{3}=\|{\underline{\Pi}}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{av}}(z_{\ell-1}-\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z)\|_{\underline{a}_{\ell}}\lesssim|z_{\ell-1}-\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket z_{\ell-1}-\Theta_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}.

Thus,
\mathfrak{T}_{3}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
by the lemma hypotheses. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.5.
Verification of the assumptions for
I^{U}_{\ell}
and
I^{R}_{\ell}

The only missing ingredient to apply the previous lemma to our prolongation operators
I^{U}_{\ell}
and
I^{R}_{\ell}
is to show that the chosen operators
\Theta_{\ell-1}
, respectively
\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}
, satisfy the required bounds for Lemmas
5.11
and
5.12
. For both operators, the first assumption in Lemma
5.11
is a standard result (see
[
39
, Proposition 2.13]
). The second assumption in Lemma
5.11
, concerning the convergence of the jumps, is a bit more involved and will be the target of Lemma
5.14
. Finally, the assumption in Lemma
5.12
will be the target of Lemma
5.15
.
First, we prove a small technical lemma which borrows ideas from
[
39
, Section 2.3.2]
.

Lemma 5.13
.

For any
\underline{u}_{\ell}=(u_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})\in\underline{U}_{\ell}
, we have the following bound:

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\|\underline{u}_{\ell}\|_{1,\ell}^{2}.

Proof.

For any
w\in H^{1}(t)
,
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{-1}\|w-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h_{\ell}^{-1}\|w-\pi_{t}^{0}w\|_{\partial t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{t}^{0}w-\pi_{\partial t}^{k}w\|_{\partial t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}w-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}

\displaystyle=h_{\ell}^{-1}\|w-\pi_{t}^{0}w\|_{\partial t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}(w-\pi_{t}^{0}w)\|_{\partial t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}w-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell}^{-1}\|w-\pi_{t}^{0}w\|_{\partial t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}w-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}w-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2},

where we have used the fact that
\pi_{\partial t}^{k}\pi_{t}^{0}w=\pi_{t}^{0}w
by definition of the face projection at all levels, the stability of
\pi_{\partial t}^{k}
and a local Poincaré inequality.
Then, we add and subtract
\lambda_{\ell}
, use the triangle inequality, rearrange terms by element and apply the above result to obtain

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}\|_{t}^{2}+h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\pi_{\partial t}^{k}\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\underline{u}_{\ell}-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}\lesssim\|\underline{u}_{\ell}\|_{1,\ell}^{2}

where in the last step we have the same arguments as in
[
39
, Proposition 2.13, (i)]
and
[
39
, Lemma 2.31]
to bound the first and second terms respectively.
∎

Lemma 5.14

(Convergence of the jumps)
.

Given
\lambda_{\ell}\in M_{\ell}
. Then, the following bounds hold:

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{1,\ell}^{2},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathcal{R}_{\ell}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{1,\ell}^{2}.

Proof.

To show (
5.13
), we add and subtract
\lambda_{\ell}|_{F}
, use the triangle inequality and rearrange terms by element to obtain

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\rrbracket\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}h_{\ell}^{-1}\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}-\lambda_{\ell}\|_{\partial t}^{2}\lesssim\|\lambda_{\ell}\|_{1,\ell}^{2},

where we have used the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{1,\ell}
.
To show (
5.14
), we use Lemma
5.13
with
\underline{u}_{\ell}={\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

For all
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, the following bound holds:

(5.15)

\displaystyle|\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

(5.16)

\displaystyle|\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
z_{\ell-1}\doteq\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z
. Define
E^{R}\doteq z_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z
. Recalling that the local reconstruction operator satisfies
\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z=z_{\ell-1}
, we have that

E^{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}(\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z)=\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}(\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z).

Then, we can use the stability of
\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}
(see
[
39
, Proposition 2.13, (i)]
) and Lemma
5.13
to write

|E^{R}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket E^{R}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim\|\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\|_{1,\ell-1}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used Lemma
5.9
in the last pass. This concludes the proof of the first result. For the second result, define
E^{U}=z_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z
. We add and subtract
\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z
and obtain

E^{U}=(z_{\ell-1}-\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z)+(\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z)\doteq E^{U}_{1}+E^{U}_{2}.

For the first term, we use that
\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z=\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z
and thus
E^{U}_{1}=\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}(\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-z)
. Then, we can use the continuity of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}
, together with the same arguments as in Lemma
5.3
to write

|E^{U}_{1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket E^{U}_{1}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim|\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-z|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket\pi^{1,k}_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}z-z\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}|z|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the second term, we observe that
E^{U}_{2}
is the bulk of the bubble function
\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z
. Thus, we use the triangle inequality on the jumps and rearrange terms by element to write

\displaystyle|E^{U}_{2}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\llbracket E^{U}_{2}\rrbracket\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}|\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z|_{H^{1}(T)}+h_{\ell}^{-1/2}\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}^{k}z-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}}^{k}z\|_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z-{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}\gamma_{\ell-1}\underline{J}_{\ell-1}z\|_{1,\ell-1}

Then using Lemma
5.9
we conclude the proof.
∎

We can now proceed to show that the prolongation operators
I^{U}_{\ell}
and
I^{R}_{\ell}
satisfy the Assumptions
4.6
,
4.7
and
4.8
.

Theorem 5.16

(Proof of assumptions for
I^{U}_{\ell}
)
.

The operator
I^{U}_{\ell}
fulfils Assumptions
4.6
,
4.7
and
4.8
.

Proof.

The prolongation operator
I_{\ell}\doteq I^{U}_{\ell}
is given by taking
\Theta_{\ell-1}=\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}
in Lemma
5.11
. By Lemma
5.14
,
\Theta_{\ell-1}
clearly fulfils the assumptions in Lemma
5.11
. Then (
5.11
) provides Assumption
4.6
and (
5.12
) becomes Assumption
4.8
, that is

\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

Finally, Lemma
5.15
ensures that
\Theta_{\ell-1}
fulfils the assumptions of Lemma
5.12
, which provides Assumption
4.7
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.17

(Proof of assumptions for
I^{R}_{\ell}
)
.

The operator
I^{R}_{\ell}
fulfils Assumptions
4.6
,
4.7
and
4.8
.

Proof.

The prolongation operator
I_{\ell}\doteq I^{R}_{\ell}
is given by taking
\Theta_{\ell-1}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\ell-1}
in Lemma
5.11
. By Lemma
5.14
,
\Theta_{\ell-1}
clearly fulfils the assumptions in Lemma
5.11
. Then Assumption
4.6
is given by (
5.11
). For Assumption
4.8
, we can add and subtract
\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}
then use the triangle inequality to write

\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\leq\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}.

The first term is bounded by (
5.12
), that is

\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell}I_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

The second term can be bounded by noticing that
\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}^{0}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}=\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}^{0}\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}
by definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\ell-1}
and thus we can use a triangle inequality followed by the approximation properties of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}^{0}
to write

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}^{0}\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}-\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1}}^{0}\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\left(|\Theta_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}+|\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\lambda_{\ell-1}|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell-1})}\right)\lesssim h_{\ell-1}\|\lambda_{\ell-1}\|_{a_{\ell-1}}.

Finally, Lemma
5.15
ensures that
\Theta_{\ell-1}
fulfils the assumptions of Lemma
5.12
, which provides Assumption
4.7
. This concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Numerical results

In this section, we validate the geometric multigrid solver of Sect.
3
through numerical experiments. We verify mesh-independence (iteration counts bounded independently of hierarchy depth
\ell
) and
h
-independence (iteration counts bounded independently of fine mesh size) for different prolongation operators and patch-based smoothers.

Figure 2
.
Illustration of the considered families of meshes in 2D: Rep-tile meshes (top row), and agglomerated Voronoi meshes (bottom row). Each row shows consecutive levels in the mesh hierarchy, with finer meshes on the left and coarser meshes on the right. Note that interfaces between coarse cells retain all vertices from the agglomeration process.

In two dimensions, we consider three different families of mesh hierarchies:

Cartesian meshes
: Given the unit
d
-cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
and an initial Cartesian mesh with
n
cells in each direction, where
n
is even, we generate a coarser mesh by agglomerating blocks of
2\times 2
quadrilateral elements into identical octagonal elements. The resulting mesh has
n/2
octagonal elements in each direction, arranged in a Cartesian fashion. Note that this process is fundamentally different from the one described in
[
20
, Section 3.1]
as we do not coarsen the octagon faces into quadrilaterals, but rather keep the extra vertices. This process can be repeated until the desired number of levels is reached, where in each level
\ell
the elements can be seen as squares whose edges have been subdivided into
2^{(\ell-1)}
segments.

Rep-tile meshes
: Next, we consider meshes composed of rep-tiles, i.e., polygons that can be subdivided into smaller copies of themselves. This allows us to test our algorithm with meshes having non-straight interfaces while keeping a regular coarsening strategy. We consider a rectangular domain
\left[0,3/2\right]\times\left[0,1\right]
tiled procedurally with L-shaped polygons. The coarsening is performed by grouping four identical L-shaped elements into a larger L-shaped element, as illustrated in Figure
2
. This process can be repeated to generate multiple levels of coarsening. As with the above Cartesian meshes, straight interfaces are not coarsened.

Agglomerated Voronoi meshes
: Finally, we consider mesh hierarchies obtained by arbitrary agglomeration of polygonal meshes. Starting from a Cartesian mesh of the unit
d
-cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
, we subdivide each quadrilateral into two identical triangles. The polygonal mesh is then generated by computing the Voronoi tessellation of the simplexified mesh, associating each Voronoi cell with a Cartesian node. The coarsening is performed by agglomerating connected clusters of cells into arbitrary polygonal elements. Note that we do not require that the resulting elements are convex or shape-regular. Finding optimal agglomeration strategies for polytopal meshes remains an open research question
[
26
,
3
]
. Within this work, the colouring of the mesh into clusters is performed using a hierarchical clustering algorithm with complete-linkage clustering and optimal branch ordering
[
8
]
, provided by the Julia package
Clustering.jl

[
29
]
. Several other clustering strategies, including K-means, R-trees and graph-based methods (using Metis) were also considered but produced worse agglomerates when compared visually. The coarsening process is illustrated in Figure
2
.

In three dimensions, we limit our study to Cartesian meshes of the unit
d
-cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
, coarsened in a similar way as in two dimensions by agglomerating blocks of
2\times 2\times 2
polyhedral elements.

On each mesh family, we solve the Poisson problem (
2.1
) with unit diffusion and source term

f(x,y)=-\Delta(\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2\pi y)x(x-L_{x})y(y-L_{y})),

where
(L_{x},L_{y})=(1,1)
for unit square domains and
(L_{x},L_{y})=(3/2,1)
for rep-tile domains.

The resulting linear systems are solved using FGMRES
[
43
]
preconditioned with a single iteration of the Multigrid V-cycle from Sect.
3
. Each V-cycle uses
m_{\ell}=5
pre- and post-smoothing steps with a constant damping parameter
\omega=0.2
, and a direct LU solve at the coarsest level. All experiments are implemented in Julia
[
10
]
within the open source Gridap ecosystem
[
7
,
46
]
, making use of the recent library expansion for hybrid discretisations
[
34
]
and the Julia-native solvers provided by GridapSolvers
[
33
]
. The features described in
[
34
]
became available for Gridap
v0.19.8
.

We test two prolongation operators,
I^{R}_{\ell-1}
and
I^{U}_{\ell-1}
from Definition
3.1
, and two patch smoothers: face-star patch (FP) and vertex-star patch (VP). In 3D, we also test the edge-star patch (EP) smoother. For each family of meshes, we create hierarchies with
L=2,\ldots,6
levels for different finest mesh sizes
n_{c}
(number of cells in each direction for the finest mesh). We use the
hho
spaces from Sect.
2
with
k\in\{0,1,2\}
. Table
1
reports the number of skeletal DOFs (interface unknowns
M_{\ell}
) after static condensation eliminates cell unknowns for different types of meshes in 2D and 3D.

For each combination of parameters, we report the number of FGMRES iterations required to reduce the relative residual by a factor of
10^{-8}
for different levels
\ell
and finest mesh sizes
n_{c}
. The results are presented in Table
2
. We do not run the case
L=6
for the smallest agglomeration-based mesh in 2D for lack of cells to coarsen. We also do not show the vertex-star patch case for
k=2
and the largest mesh in 3D, which does not converge for the current choice of damping parameter
\omega
(increased overlapping of the vertex-patch and hidden
k
-dependence of the constants require more damping to achieve convergence).
For fixed polynomial order
k
and finest mesh size
n_{c}
, the iteration counts remain stable across all number of levels
L=2,\ldots,6
(horizontal lines). Similarly for fixed
k
and
L
, as
n_{c}
increases the iteration counts remain constant, validating
h

7.
Conclusion

We have constructed and theoretically analyzed the first optimal fully-hybrid
gmg
solver for
hho
discretisations capable of handling arbitrary polytopal agglomeration hierarchies in both 2D and 3D.
A key ingredient of this approach is a
minimal

hho
face space that can naturally handle non-flat interfaces while reducing the number of
dof
. This construction removes the restriction present in prior GMG approaches for hybrid methods, which were mostly confined to nested multigrid mesh hierarchies with planar faces at all levels, drastically reducing their applicability.
Numerical experiments confirm the robust convergence of the proposed solver with respect to mesh size and number of multigrid levels, in both 2D and 3D. While we focus on
hho
methods, the techniques developed here can be extended to other hybrid methods such as
hdg
. This will be the subject of future work, along with the extension of the framework to other problems such as incompressible flows.
