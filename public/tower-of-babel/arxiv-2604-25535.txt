The SK model with a sparse variance profile: free energy and AMP algorithm for TAP equations at high temperature
Walid Hachem

Abstract
A generalization of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick (SK) model for spin glasses is considered, in which the interaction matrix is endowed with a variance profile that has no particular structure and may be sparse. In the first part of the paper, an asymptotic equivalent of the free energy is derived at sufficiently high temperatures, regardless of the signature of the variance profile matrix. In the second part, the mean of the spin vector under the Gibbs measure is estimated using an Approximate Message Passing algorithm based on the Thouless–Anderson–Palmer equations. The dynamical approach of Adhikari et al. (J. Stat. Phys., 2021), originally developed for the classical SK model, is adapted to the present setting to obtain these results.

Assumption 2

Denoting as usual as

[
9

. The second purpose of this paper is to generalize

2

,

We begin with an approximation result related with the function

Lemma 9
.

Let
C>0
. For each
e>0
, there exists a polynomial
p_{e}
such
that
p_{e}(0)=\Tanh(0)
,

\max_{\alpha\in[0,C]}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left(p_{e}(\alpha\xi)-\Tanh(\alpha\xi)\right)^{2}\leq e\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{\alpha\in[0,C]}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left(p^{\prime}_{e}(\alpha\xi)-\Tanh^{\prime}(\alpha\xi)\right)^{2}\leq e.

Proof.

Given a small
\delta>0
, it is known, see
[
22
, Th. 1]

or
[
15
]
, that there exists a polynomial
u
on
{{\mathbb{R}}}
such that

\forall x\in{{\mathbb{R}}},\ \left|u(x)-\Tanh^{\prime}(x)\right|\leq\delta\exp(\delta x^{2}).

Defining the polynomial
U
as

U(x)=\Tanh(0)+\int_{0}^{x}u(s)\,ds

we obtain that

|U(x)-\Tanh(x)|=|\int_{0}^{x}(u(s)-\Tanh^{\prime}(s))\,ds|\leq\delta|x|\exp(\delta x^{2})
. Therefore, given
\alpha>0
not too large, we
have after a simple derivation that

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{E}}}(u(\alpha\xi)-\Tanh^{\prime}(\alpha\xi))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}/\sqrt{1-\delta\alpha^{2}},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{E}}}(U(\alpha\xi)-\Tanh(\alpha\xi))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\alpha^{2}/(1-\delta\alpha^{2})^{3/2}.

By assumption,
0\leq\alpha^{2}\leq C^{2}
. Thus, by setting
\delta
small
enough, we can take
p_{e}=U
.
∎

Recall the expression of

We now show that

This is trivial for

Proposition 13

For each
b>0

\varphi\in\PL

We notice that

The moment bound (
18
) can be proven by recurrence on
l
. For

,
consider a set
A_{1}
and an index

. Since

18
) holds true for
l=1
. Assuming (
18
) is true
for

{{\mathbb{E}}}\left|\check{x}^{l+1}_{(A_{l+1}),i}\right|^{b}={{\mathbb{E}}}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\left|\check{x}^{l+1}_{(A_{l+1}),i}\right|^{b}\ |\ {\mathscr{F}}_{-i}\right]={{\mathbb{E}}}(\varsigma_{(A_{l+1}),i}^{l+1})^{b}{{\mathbb{E}}}|\xi|^{b}

which is bounded by the recurrence assumption.

We now prove by recurrence on
l
the convergence (
19
) as well as

{{\mathbb{E}}}(\check{x}^{l}_{(A_{l}),r}-\check{x}^{l}_{(A_{l}\cup\{i\}),r})^{2}\to 0

(20)

for all sequences
(A_{l}^{(n)})
,
(i_{n})
with
i_{n}\not\in A_{l}^{(n)}
,
and
(r_{n})
with
r_{n}\not\in A_{l}^{(n)}\cup\{i_{n}\}

We first notice that at the left hand side of (

have a negligible contribution. Thus, in all the remainder

Let us start our recurrence with

. As above, consider a set
A_{1}
and an

{{\mathbb{E}}}\varphi(\check{x}^{1}_{(A_{1}),i})={{\mathbb{E}}}\varphi\left(f(0)\sum_{r\not\in A_{1}}W_{ir}\right)={{\mathbb{E}}}\varphi\left(\varsigma_{(A_{1}),i}^{1}\xi\right).

We also have
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq C|x-y|(1+|x|^{a}+|y|^{a})
for
some

. Recalling that
{\widecheck{X}}^{l}=[{\widecheck{X}}^{l}_{i}]\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\diag(\check{q}^{l}))

. Note also that

(A_{1}^{(n)})
and sequence
of indices
(i_{n})
. We now show that

\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{S}}|^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{S}}}\beta_{i}\varphi(\check{x}^{1}_{(A_{1}),i})-\beta_{i}{{\mathbb{E}}}\varphi({\widecheck{X}}^{1}_{i})\right)^{2}\to 0.

, and

.

We also use the following notations:
\overline{{\mathcal{T}}}^{k}
is the set of
such labelled trees, with depth

\widecheck{\mathcal{T}}^{k}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{T}}}^{k}

in
which there exists two identical labels
\ell(\cdot)
. Furthermore,

{\mathcal{T}}^{k}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{T}}}^{k}
is the subset that satisfies the following

, then the corresponding sequence of labels
\ell(\cdot)

non-backtracking. This means that for each

{\mathcal{T}}^{k}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{T}}^{k}

for which the label

of the root is

\widecheck{\mathcal{T}}_{i}^{k}
. Notice that
\widecheck{\mathcal{T}}_{i}^{k}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{i}^{k}
. Such a

Given a tree

, write

W(T)=\prod_{(u\to v)\in E(T)}W_{\ell(u)\ell(v)},\quad\Gamma(T)=\prod_{(u\to v)\in E(T)}\alpha_{c(u)},\quad\text{and}\quad\check{x}(T)=\prod_{v\in L(T)}(\check{x}^{0}_{\ell(v)})^{c(v)},

Given an integer

and
b

Let

Proof.

{{\mathbb{E}}}\|W^{(n)}\|\leq T^{(n)},

T^{(n)}=(1+\delta)\left(2\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert{S^{(n)}}\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{1/2}+\frac{6}{\sqrt{\log(1+\delta)}}(\|S^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\log n)^{1/2}\right)

for an arbitrary
\delta>0
. Furthermore, by Gaussian concentration,

{{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\|W^{(n)}\|\geq T^{(n)}+t\right]\leq\exp(-t^{2}/(2\|S^{(n)}\|_{\infty})^{2}),

for
\delta\in(0,1/2]
, as given by
[
4
, Cor. 3.9]
. By
consequence, since
K_{n}\geq\log n
, it holds that there exists
C_{W}>0
for
which
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}\to_{n}1
almost surely. This implies that

\limsup_{n}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left\|m-\Tanh(x^{k})\right\|_{n}^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon+4(\log 2)^{k},

thus,
\limsup_{k}\limsup_{n}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left\|m-\Tanh(x^{k})\right\|_{n}^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, Theorem
5

holds true.

We close the paper with some remarks.

Remark 1
.

The proof for the bound (
12
) requires the Gaussian assumption on
the entries of the matrix
W
. This assumption is not essential for the rest of
the proof which is based on the approach of
[
8
,
18
]
. If
we manage to generalize the bound (
12
) to the non-necessarily
Gaussian case, the proof above will continue to work after some easy
adaptations.

Remark 2
.

The condition
K_{n}\geq\log n
is an artifact of our proof due to the
fact that we needed to bound the spectral norm
\|W\|
in order to approximate
our AMP algorithm with a polynomial activation function with the AMP
algorithm with the
\Tanh
activation function. Another possible proof
technique would be to pass from the iterates
\tilde{x}^{l}
to the iterates
x^{l}

without the need of introducing the polynomial intermediates. This is left for
future research.

A.2
Proof of Corollary

We take out the superscripts

.
Recall that
S=[\psi(|i-j|)]
. For

be the
circulant deformation of

given as the

matrix which first row is

\begin{bmatrix}\psi(0)&\cdots&\psi(K_{n})&0&\cdots&0&\psi(K_{n})&\cdots&\psi(1)\end{bmatrix}.

Given a

let

where

. Define the Hamiltonian

\widetilde{H}(\sigma)=\sigma^{\top}\widetilde{W}\sigma/2+h\left(\sigma\cdot 1\right)

3

3
) holds true for

\widetilde{F}_{n}

.
We can write

where

\widetilde{W}_{ij}

{{\mathbb{E}}}\widetilde{W}_{ij}^{2}=t\psi(|i-j+n|)
.

, thus,
\widetilde{F}_{n}\geq F_{n}
.

{{\mathbb{E}}}E(\sigma)^{2}=t\sum_{i=1}^{K_{n}}\sum_{j=n-K_{n}+i}^{n}\psi(|i-j+n|)\leq tK_{n}.

Jensen’s inequality applied to the expectation with respect to the

leads to

\widetilde{F}_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}{{\mathbb{E}}}\log\sum_{\sigma}e^{H(\sigma)+{{\mathbb{E}}}E(\sigma)^{2}/2}\leq\frac{tK_{n}}{2n}+F_{n},

and the corollary is established.

Proof of Lemma

Let us denote as
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
the set of rooted, undirected, labelled and
connected graphs such that
\ell(\circ)=i
,

,
and the property

\{u,v\}\in E(G)\ \Rightarrow\ \ell(u)\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell(v)}.

, let us denote as

. With these notations, we have

\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|=\sum_{\bar{G}\in{\mathcal{R}}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\,:\\
\boldsymbol{U}(G)=\bar{G}\end{subarray}}\ \left|\left\{(T_{1},\ldots,T_{b})\in{\mathcal{A}}\,:\,\boldsymbol{G}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{b})=G\right\}\right|.

(26)

.
We need to show that

(27)

Given
\bar{G}\in{\mathcal{R}}
, denote as
\circ
the root node of

\bar{G}
, write
M=|V(\bar{G})|-1
, and write

. Recalling that
\bar{G}
is connected,

\circ

\boldsymbol{\pi}(v)

v

,

\left|\left\{G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\,:\,\boldsymbol{U}(G)=\bar{G}\right\}\right|\leq\left|\left\{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{M})\in[n]^{M}\,:\,\forall k\in[M],\ j_{k}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{j_{\boldsymbol{\pi}(k)}}\right\}\right|.

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{M})\in[n]^{M}\,:\,\forall k\in[M],\ j_{k}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{j_{\boldsymbol{\pi}(k)}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\ldots,j_{M}\in[n]\,:\\
