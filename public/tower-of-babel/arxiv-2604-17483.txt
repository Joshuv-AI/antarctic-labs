Permutation, stabilization and decomposition
Paul Balmer, Martin Gallauer

Abstract
. Informed by our understanding of the tt-geometry of permutation modules, we investigate the proper definition of the ‘stable permutation category’ of a finite group. Then we prove that this category decomposes over cyclic and generalized quaternion groups and only in those cases.

Introduction

For the whole paper,
G

.
We introduce the

of
G
with coefficients in

\StPerm(G;k).

It is to the usual stable module category
\StMod(kG)
, of
kG
-modules modulo projectives, what permutation modules are to general modules.
However
\StPerm(G;k)
is
not
defined as the additive quotient of the category of permutation modules by the subcategory of projectives.
We explain in

why this definition would not be very interesting.
In fact, our stable permutation category
\StPerm(G;k)
is not even a subcategory of
\StMod(kG)
but rather an intermediate localization between the

\DPerm(G;k)

[
BG23b
]

Conjecturally,

With the definition established and justified by these alternate interpretations, we investigate when this stable permutation category

is indecomposable as a tt-category. This question might come as a surprise since the other three types of categories in (

) are always indecomposable, for instance because the ring of endomorphisms of their

And yet the stable permutation category can indeed decompose, in very special cases.

1.2 Theorem

(
6.1
)
.

The stable permutation category
\StPerm(G;k)
is indecomposable unless the
p
-Sylow of

is cyclic or generalized quaternion.

We obtain this result by proving that the spectrum is connected as a topological space. Conversely, for

p

is disconnected and this forces

to be a product of two tt-categories, or more.
Amusingly, the connected components of the spectrum that appear in those cases are reminiscent of spectra of other tt-categories and this observation led us to
predict
what the decompositions of
\StPerm(G;k)
should be at the categorical level. In both cases, our geometric guess was confirmed:

1.3 Theorem

(

)
.

The stable permutation category
\StPerm(C_{p^{n}};k)
of the cyclic group of order

is tt-equivalent to the product of the

\StMod(kC_{p})\times\cdots\times\StMod(kC_{p^{i}})\times\cdots\times\StMod(kC_{p^{n}})
.

1.4 Theorem

(

)
.

For

, the stable permutation category
\StPerm(Q_{2^{n}};k)
of the

of order

is tt-equivalent to the product

\StPerm(D_{2^{n-1}};k)\times\StMod(kQ_{2^{n}})
of the stable permutation category of the dihedral group
D_{2^{n-1}}
of order

and the usual stable module category of

. Both of these factors are indecomposable.

2.
Definition via equivariantly perfect complexes

We write
\modname(kG)
for the abelian category of finitely generated
kG
-modules and write
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
for its bounded derived category.
Before turning to permutations, let us remind the reader of the usual stable
module
category.

2.1 Recollection
.

The
stable module category

\stmod(kG)
is the additive quotient of the category
\modname(kG)
by the subcategory of projective modules.
(Additive quotients by a subcategory are obtained by keeping the same objects and by modding out the morphisms that factor via an object of the subcategory.)
It is triangulated by Happel
[
Hap88
]
. In fact, by Rickard
[
Ric89
]
, we have a triangular-equivalence

(2.2)

\stmod(kG)\cong\Der_{\smallb}(kG)/\Der_{\smallperf}(kG)

with the Verdier quotient of the derived category
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
by the thick subcategory of perfect complexes.
The category
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)/\Der_{\smallperf}(kG)
is effortlessly triangulated, as any Verdier quotient. In contrast, we need
kG
to be Frobenius to obtain the equivalence (
2.2
) and to show that the additive quotient
\stmod(kG)
is triangulated.

The problem we want to address is to find the correct analogue of the stable module category
\stmod(kG)
in the context of
permutation modules
.
We assume that the reader is aware of the importance of permutation modules in representation theory and beyond,
e.g.
in the theory of motives. See
[
BG23b
]
if necessary.

2.3 Recollection
.

The full additive subcategory
\perm(G;k)
of
\modname(kG)
of
permutation

kG
-modules consists of those isomorphic to
k(X)
for a finite
G
-set
X
.
We slightly enlarge it by including direct summands
\perm(G;k)^{\natural}\subseteq\modname(kG)
; the latter are called
p
-permutation
or
trivial source

kG
-modules.
(If
G
is a
p
-group then
\perm(G;k)^{\natural}=\perm(G;k)
.)
As
\perm(G;k)^{\natural}
is just an additive category, its bounded derived category is simply its bounded homotopy category, no need to invert quasi-isomorphisms. We shall denote it
\mathscr{K}(G)
as we did in
[
BG25a
]
:

\mathscr{K}(G):=\Der_{\smallb}(\perm(G;k)^{\natural})=\Komp_{\smallb}(\perm(G;k)^{\natural}).

The ‘big’
derived category of permutation modules

\DPerm(G;k)
of
[
BG25a
]
is a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category whose compact part is the above
\mathscr{K}(G)
.
This ‘Ind-completion’ can be realized for instance inside the unbounded homotopy category
\Komp(\Perm(G;k))
of not necessarily finitely generated permutation modules, as the localizing subcategory generated by
\perm(G;k)
.
Its rigid-compact objects is indeed the subcategory
\DPerm(G;k)^{c}=\textrm{thick}(\perm(G;k))=\mathscr{K}(G)
.

Alternatively,
\DPerm(G;k)
is the homotopy category of modules over the Bredon cohomology spectrum
H\underline{k}
in genuine
G
-spectra, see
[
Fuh25
]
.

2.4 Remark
.

The inclusion
\perm(G;k)^{\natural}\subseteq\modname(kG)
does not yield an inclusion on derived categories.
Quite the opposite, the canonical functor, that we denote
\Upsilon\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\to\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
, is actually a Verdier quotient by
[
BG23a
, Theorem 5.13]
.
As
\stmod(kG)
is a further Verdier quotient of
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
, we immediately obtain:

2.5 Corollary
.

The thick subcategory of
\stmod(kG)
generated by permutation modules is the whole
\stmod(kG)
.
∎

2.6 Remark
.

Since free modules are permutation, the category
\perm(G;k)^{\natural}
contains the subcategory
\proj(kG)
of finitely generated projective
kG
-modules.
One can then form the
additive
quotient

(2.7)

\frac{\perm(G;k)^{\natural}}{\proj(kG)}.

This additive category has been called the ‘stable category of
p
-permutation modules’ in (unpublished) literature. We shall avoid this terminology.
The additive quotient (
2.7
) is clearly a full subcategory of the stable module category
\stmod(kG)
.
However (
2.7
) is not triangulated in any evident way.
In fact (
2.7
) generates the whole
\stmod(kG)
as a thick triangulated subcategory by
2.5
.

In view of the above discussion, we propose to define
\stperm(G;k)
as a suitable localization of the bounded derived category of
p
-permutation modules
\mathscr{K}(G)
. We present justifications in the subsequent
Section

3
.

2.8 Recollection
.

Let
H\leq G
be a
p
-subgroup and write
{G}/\!\!/{H}=N_{G}(H)/H
for the Weyl group of
H
in
G
.
There exists (see
[
BG25a
, § 5]
) a tensor functor

\Psi^{H}\colon\perm(G;k)^{\natural}\to\perm({G}/\!\!/{H};k)^{\natural}

characterized by the property that
\Psi^{H}(kX)\cong k(X^{H})
for every finite
G
-set
X
.
It is called
modular
H
-fixed-points
or
Brauer quotient
. It induces a tt-functor on homotopy categories (applying
\Psi^{H}
degreewise on complexes) that we still denote
\Psi^{H}\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\to\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{H})
. Composed with the canonical localization of
2.4
we obtain a tt-functor to the derived category of the Weyl group

\check{\Psi}^{H}\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\xrightarrow{\Psi^{H}}\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{H})\overset{\ \Upsilon\ }{\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}}\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H})).

2.9 Definition
.

A bounded complex
C\in\mathscr{K}(G)
of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules is called
equivariantly perfect
if its image under modular
H
-fixed-points functor
\Psi^{H}(C)
is a perfect complex over the Weyl group of
H
, for every
p
-subgroup
H\leq G
.
This means that
\Psi^{H}(C)
is
quasi-isomorphic
to a bounded complex of finitely generated projective
k({G}/\!\!/{H})
-modules, or in formula
\check{\Psi}^{H}(C)\in\Der_{\smallperf}(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))
, for every
H
.
Let us denote by

\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)=\DPerm(G;k)^{c}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}

the subcategory of
\mathscr{K}(G)=\DPerm(G;k)^{c}
consisting of equivariantly perfect complexes.
Equivalently,

\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)=\Ker\left(\mathscr{K}(G)\xrightarrow{(\Psi^{H})}\prod_{H\leq G}\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))\xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:Rickard}}\prod_{H\leq G}\stmod(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))\right)

can be written as the kernel of a tt-functor (where
H
runs through the (conjugacy classes of)
p
-subgroups).
In particular, it is a tt-ideal.

2.10 Example
.

Every bounded complex of projective
kG
-modules is equivariantly perfect. Indeed,
\Psi^{H}(kG)
is
kG
for
H=1
and zero for
H\neq 1
.

2.11 Example
.

Let
G=C_{p}
and
C=0\to k\to kC_{p}\to kC_{p}\to k\to 0
the usual acyclic complex. For
H=1
, the complex
\Psi^{1}(C)=C
is quasi-isomorphic to zero hence it is perfect as complex of
kG
-modules. And for
H=C_{p}
, the complex
\Psi^{C_{p}}(C)=0\to k\to 0\to 0\to k\to 0
is perfect over the trivial group
{G}/\!\!/{H}=1
, as every complex of
k
-vector spaces is. It follows that
C
is equivariantly perfect.

2.12 Remark
.

Let us dispel any possible confusion between the equivariantly perfect complexes of
2.9
and two adjacent notions

\Komp_{\smallb}(\proj(kG))\ \subseteq\ \mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)\ \subseteq\ \Upsilon^{-1}(\Der_{\smallperf}(kG)).

(a)

Every bounded complex of projective
kG
-modules is equivariantly perfect (
2.10
). It follows that, inside
\mathscr{K}(G)
, we have an inclusion of tt-ideals
\langle kG\rangle=\Komp_{\smallb}(\proj(kG))\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)
. It is usually a proper inclusion, as shown in
2.11
.

(b)

By definition (take
H=1
in
2.9
), every equivariantly perfect complex is perfect as a complex of
kG
-modules. The converse is true for groups with very small
p
-Sylow but is false in general. Indeed, suppose that
G
has order divisible by
p^{2}
. Pick
H
a copy of
C_{p}
in the center of a
p
-Sylow of
G
. Our assumption implies that
p
divides the order of
{G}/\!\!/{H}
.
Let
C={}^{\otimes}\Ind_{1}^{G}(0\to k\xrightarrow{1}k\to 0)
be the ‘Koszul object’ of
[
BG25a
, 3.15]
, where it is denoted
\kosname_{G}(1)
.
This complex is acyclic, hence trivially perfect as a complex of
kG
-modules. However,
\Psi^{H}(C)
generates
\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{H})
as a tt-ideal, by
[
BG25a
, Lemma 5.21 (for
K=1
)]
. Since
{G}/\!\!/{H}
has order divisible by
p
, the category
\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{H})
cannot consist only of perfect complexes (for
\stmod(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))\neq 0
). Therefore
\Psi^{H}(C)
is not perfect over
{G}/\!\!/{H}
.

We are now ready for our central definition.

2.13 Definition
.

The
stable permutation category
of
G
is the idempotent-completion of the Verdier quotient of the derived permutation category
\mathscr{K}(G)=\Komp_{\smallb}(\perm(G;k)^{\natural})
by the tt-ideal of equivariantly perfect complexes (
2.9
)

\stperm(G;k)=\bigg(\frac{\mathscr{K}(G)}{\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)}\bigg)^{\natural}.

It is therefore an idempotent-complete rigid tensor-triangulated category in such a way that the
\natural
-localization functor
p\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\to\stperm(G;k)
is a tt-functor.

Let
\StPerm(G;k)=\DPerm(G;k)/\Locname(\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G))
be the corresponding finite localization of the ‘big’ derived permutation category
\DPerm(G;k)
at the tt-ideal
\mathscr{K}_{\textrm{eq-perf}}(G)
of its compacts
\DPerm(G;k)^{c}=\mathscr{K}(G)
. We baptize
\StPerm(G;k)
the
‘big’ stable permutation category
of the group
G
, with coefficients in
k
.
By Neeman-Thomason localization
[
Nee92
]
, we can identify
\StPerm(G;k)^{c}
with
\stperm(G;k)
– hence the idempotent-completion in the latter.

2.14 Remark
.

We do not know whether the idempotent-completion is necessary in the definition of
\stperm(G;k)
.
The definition of
\stmod(kG)
, either as an additive (
2.1
) or triangulated quotient through Rickard’s Theorem (
2.2
), does not involve an idempotent-completion because one can show that it is already idempotent-complete.
It is conceivable that the same holds true for permutation modules but it would require an argument.

2.15 Proposition
.

We have the commutative diagrams of tt-functors (
1.1
).
The stable module category
\StMod(G;k)
is the finite localization of the stable permutation category
\StPerm(G;k)
away from the tt-ideal of compacts consisting of complexes of
p
-permutation modules that are perfect as complexes of
kG
-modules.

Proof.

By
2.12
(
b
), the equivariantly perfect complexes are perfect. This means that the kernel of
p
is sent to zero by
q\circ\Upsilon
and therefore
q\circ\Upsilon
localizes along
p
, to yield the unique factorization
\bar{\Upsilon}
. The kernel of the localization
\bar{\Upsilon}
is then the image under
p
of the kernel of
q\circ\Upsilon
, that is, the localizing subcategory generated by complexes of
p
-permutation modules that are perfect in
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
.
∎

3.
Justifications of the definition

We want to justify our definition of the stable permutation category given in
Section

2
, from various tt-geometric perspectives.
We remind the reader of the stable module category precedent.

3.1 Recollection
.

By
[
BCR97
]
the spectrum of
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
is homeomorphic, via the comparison map, to the homogeneous spectrum of the cohomology (see also
[
Bal10
, Proposition 8.5]
):

(3.2)

\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}(kG))\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\Spech(\Hm^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(G,k)).

This space is local: Its unique closed point is the tt-ideal
\mathscr{M}=(0)
of
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
on the left-hand side of (
3.2
), which corresponds to
\Hm^{+}(G,k)
on the right-hand side. In the language of projective algebraic geometry,
\Hm^{+}(G,k)
is the ‘irrelevant’ ideal and removing it yields the usual projective variety
\Proj(\Hm^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(G,k))
.
At the level of the derived category
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
, the closed point
\mathscr{M}=(0)
is exactly the support of the tt-ideal
\Der_{\smallperf}(kG)
of perfect complexes. Removing this support, the open complement
\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}(kG))\smallsetminus\{\mathscr{M}\}
becomes the spectrum of the corresponding localization
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)/\Der_{\smallperf}(kG)
.
And the latter is the stable module category by (
2.2
).
Away from the ‘irrelevant’ closed points, the homeomorphism (
3.2
) restricts to

(3.3)

\Spc(\stmod(kG))\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\Proj(\Hm^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(G,k)).

In summary, the stable module category
\stmod(kG)
is obtained by a localization of the usual derived category
\Der_{\smallb}(kG)
away from the irrelevant ideal,
i.e.
away from the unique closed point of its spectrum.

A similar pattern holds for the stable permutation category, with ‘local’ replaced by ‘semi-local’. Let us recall some tt-geometry from
[
BG25a
]
.

3.4 Recollection
.

The main result of
[
BG25a
, § 7]
identified the spectrum of the tt-category
\mathscr{K}(G)=\DPerm(G;k)^{c}
of
2.3
.
This spectrum admits a stratification by locally closed subsets indexed by conjugacy classes of
p
-subgroups

(3.5)

\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))=\coprod_{(H)\in\Sub_{p}(G)/G}\mathscr{V}_{{G}/\!\!/{H}}

where
\mathscr{V}_{{G}/\!\!/{H}}\cong\Spech(\Hm^{*}({G}/\!\!/{H};k))\cong\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H})))
is the (extended) cohomological support variety associated with the Weyl group
{G}/\!\!/{H}
, also known as the cohomological open in
\Spc(\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{H}))
. More precisely, for each
p
-subgroup
H\leq G
, the tt-functor
\check{\Psi}^{H}\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\to\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))
of
2.8
induces a continuous map
\check{\psi}^{H}=\Spc(\check{\Psi}^{H})
that we prove to be injective
\check{\psi}^{H}\colon\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H})))\hookrightarrow\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))
and whose image is our
\mathscr{V}_{{G}/\!\!/{H}}
in (
3.5
).
In particular, the closed point
(0)
of each
\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H})))
as in
2.1
gives a closed point

\mathscr{M}(H)=\mathscr{M}_{G}(H):=\check{\psi}^{H}(0)=(\check{\Psi}^{H})^{-1}(0)=\Ker(\check{\Psi}^{H}\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\to\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H})))

that belongs to the stratum
\mathscr{V}_{{G}/\!\!/{H}}
. We proved in
[
BG25a
, Corollary 7.31]
that these
\mathscr{M}(H)
are all the closed points of
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))
.
In other words,
\mathscr{K}(G)
is ‘semi-local’ in the sense that its spectrum admits only finitely many closed points
\mathscr{M}(H)
, one for every
G
-conjugacy class of
p
-subgroups
H\in\Sub_{p}(G)
.

3.6 Proposition
.

An object
C\in\mathscr{K}(G)=\DPerm(G;k)^{c}
is equivariantly perfect in the sense of
2.9
if and only if its support is contained in the subset
\big\{\,\mathscr{M}(H)\,\big|\,H\in\Sub_{p}(G)\,\big\}
of closed points of
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))
.

Proof.

As with any tt-functor the support of the image
\check{\Psi}^{H}(C)
of an object
C
is the preimage
(\check{\psi}^{H})^{-1}(\supp(C))
of its support under the induced map
\check{\psi}^{H}=\Spc(\check{\Psi}^{H})
.
It follows from (
3.5
) that
C
has support in
\big\{\,\mathscr{M}(H)\,\big|\,H\in\Sub_{p}(G)\,\big\}
if and only if every
\check{\Psi}^{H}(C)\in\Der_{\smallb}(k({G}/\!\!/{H}))
has support in the closed point
(0)
of
\Spc(\Der_{\smallb}({G}/\!\!/{H}))
, which is equivalent to
\check{\Psi}^{H}(C)
being perfect over
{G}/\!\!/{H}
by
2.1
.
∎

3.7 Remark
.

2.13
is an instance of a very general tt-construction. For any Thomason subset
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
of the spectrum of a tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, we form the
localization of
\mathscr{K}
on the complement
U=Y^{\complement}
by localization-idempotent-completion

\mathscr{K}|_{U}:=(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{K}_{Y})^{\natural}

We typically do this for
Y
closed with quasi-compact open complement
U
. This tt-category
\mathscr{K}|_{U}

4.
General properties

Recall that the assignment
G\mapsto\DPerm(G;k)
comes with restriction functors along any group homomorphisms
u\colon G\to G^{\prime}
, that have an ambidextrous adjoint in case
u
is injective, namely (co)induction. This data forms a Mackey 2-functor in the sense of
[
BD20
]
.
We claim that the 2-functor
G\mapsto\StPerm(G;k)
inherits a structure of Mackey 2-functor but only for restriction along injective homomorphisms.
The restriction and induction functors are compatible with those of
\DPerm
under the localizations
\DPerm(G;k)\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}\StPerm(G;k)
.
This is reminiscent of what happens with the passage from the usual derived category to the stable module category, see
[
BD20
, Example 4.2.6]
.
In the language of
[
BD20
]
, this
G\mapsto\StPerm(G;k)
is a Mackey 2-functor defined on the 2-category of finite groupoids and faithful 1-cells.
The analogous statements hold for the 2-functor
G\mapsto\stperm(G;k)
of subcategories of compacts.

4.1 Proposition
.

The equivariantly perfect complexes of
p
-permutation modules (
2.9
) are preserved by conjugation by elements in
G
, by restriction to subgroups and by induction from subgroups. They are also preserved by modular fixed-points functors with respect to every
p
-subgroup.

Proof.

For restriction
\Res^{G}_{K}
to a subgroup
K\leq G
, we can use its compatibility with modular fixed points (
[
BG25a
, Proposition 5.15]
): for every
p
-subgroup
H\leq K
we have
\check{\Psi}^{H}\circ\Res^{G}_{K}\cong\Res^{{G}/\!\!/{H}}_{{K}/\!\!/{H}}\circ\check{\Psi}^{H}
. Since
{K}/\!\!/{H}
is a subgroup of
{G}/\!\!/{H}
, the restriction of a perfect complex over
{G}/\!\!/{H}
remains perfect over
{K}/\!\!/{H}
.

[
BG25a
, Proposition 5.15]
also deals with conjugation, in the same way.

For modular fixed-point, the argument is similar, using
[
BG25a
, Proposition 5.17]
instead. Note that there is also a restriction to a subgroup involved in that case.

Finally, for induction, one can argue by means of supports, using
3.6
. It suffices to check that, for every
K\leq G
and every
C\in\mathscr{K}(K)
, the support of the induced complex
\Ind_{K}^{G}(C)
is equal to the image of the support of
C
under
\Spc(\Res^{G}_{K})
. This is a general tt-fact about the image of a rigid object by a right adjoint to a separable extension
[
Bal16
, Theorem 3.4 (c)]
. It then suffices to use that
\Spc(\Res^{G}_{K})
maps closed points to closed points
[
BG25a
, Lemma 11.9 (b)]
.
∎

4.2 Corollary
.

Let
H\leq G
be a subgroup. There is a well-defined coproduct-preserving tt-functor
\Res^{G}_{H}\colon\StPerm(G;k)\to\StPerm(H;k)
compatible with the functor
\Res^{G}_{H}\colon\DPerm(G;k)\to\DPerm(H;k)
under localization, meaning that the following diagram commutes up to isomorphism

\textstyle{\DPerm(G;k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{\Res^{G}_{H}}
\textstyle{\DPerm(H;k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\StPerm(G;k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Res^{G}_{H}}
\textstyle{\StPerm(H;k).\!}

Restriction admits a two-sided adjoint
\Ind_{H}^{G}\colon\StPerm(H;k)\to\StPerm(G;k)
, compatible with
\Ind_{H}^{G}\colon\DPerm(H;k)\to\DPerm(G;k)
under localization (as above).

Let
H\leq G
be a
p
-subgroup. There exists a tt-functor of ‘stable’ modular
H
-fixed-points
\Psi^{H}\colon\StPerm(G;k)\to\StPerm({G}/\!\!/{H};k)
, compatible with the original
\Psi^{H}\colon\DPerm(G;k)\to\DPerm({G}/\!\!/{H};k)
under localization (as above again).

All the above functors preserve compact objects and give similar statements for
\stperm
instead of
\StPerm
,
mutatis mutandis
.

Proof.

Once the original functors preserve the tt-ideals of compacts that we quotient out (equivariantly perfect complexes), these functors pass to the finite localizations of the ‘big’ categories in a unique way. Natural transformations are preserved, and therefore adjunctions follow. Since the original functors preserve compacts, so do the new ones on stable categories.
∎

4.3 Corollary
.

The 2-functors
G\mapsto\StPerm(G;k)
and
G\mapsto\stperm(G;k)
(with respect to restriction) are Mackey 2-functors on the 2-category of finite group(oid)s and
faithful
1-cells.

Proof.

All properties, in particular Ambidexterity and the Mackey formula
[
BD20
, Definition 1.1.7]
are inherited by localization since all units and counits come from the Mackey 2-functor
\DPerm(-;k)
, as long as we only use restriction under faithful homomorphisms as in
4.2
. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

4.4 Corollary
.

For every subgroup
H\leq G
the category
\StPerm(H;k)
is equivalent to the category of
A^{G}_{H}
-modules in
\StPerm(G;k)
where the tt-ring
A^{G}_{H}=\Ind_{H}^{G}(\mathbb{1})
is
k(G/H)
with the usual multiplication coming from
\perm(G;k)
(making all
\gamma\in G/H
orthogonal idempotents). The same holds on subcategories of compacts.

Proof.

Again, this passes under localization or holds by general 2-Mackey theory
[
BD20
, Theorem 2.4.1]
. One obtains
\Ind_{H}^{G}(\mathbb{1})=k(G/H)
from
\DPerm
.
∎

4.5 Remark
.

One can actually show that the Mackey 2-functor
\StPerm(-;k)
is

in the sense of
[

]
, meaning that for every subgroup

the composite
\Id\to\Ind_{H}^{G}\Res^{G}\to\Id
, of the unit for the

, is equal to multiplication by

. Again, this is immediate from the fact that the original

is itself cohomological and from the fact that the units and counits pass to the localization.

4.6 Remark
.

It follows from

that restriction

is a faithful functor whenever the index

.

4.7 Remark
.

In the same vein, one can prove that the Mackey 2-functor

.
This follows from the fact that

We can adapt the Colimit Theorem from the case of the derived permutation category
[

]
to the stable permutation category.

4.8 Recollection
.

The category
\mathcal{E}_{p}(G)
of elementary abelian subquotients of
G
is defined as follows. Its objects are pairs
(H,K)
where
K\trianglelefteqslant H
is a section of
G
and
H/K
is elementary abelian.
A morphism
(H,K)\to(H^{\prime},K^{\prime})
is an element
g\in G
such that
K^{\prime}\leq K^{g}
and
H^{g}\leq H^{\prime}
. By
[
BG25a
, Construction 11.4]
each such morphism

yields a tt-functor

(4.9)

\mathscr{K}(g)\colon\mathscr{K}(H^{\prime}/K^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\Psi^{\bar{K}}}\mathscr{K}(H^{\prime}/K^{g})\xrightarrow{\Res}\mathscr{K}(H^{g}/K^{g})\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\mathscr{K}(H/K)

combining modular fixed points with respect to the
p
-subgroup
\bar{K}:=K^{g}/K^{\prime}\triangleleft H^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
, restriction to the subgroup
H^{g}/K^{g}\leq H^{\prime}/K^{g}
and conjugation by

.
Applying
\Spc
gives a continuous map
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(H/K))\to\Spc(\mathscr{K}(H^{\prime}/K^{\prime}))
and the colimit of this diagram of topological spaces is
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))
by
[
BG25a
, Theorem 11.10]
:

(4.10)

\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{(H,K)\in\mathcal{E}_{p}(G)}\Spc(\mathscr{K}(H/K))\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G)).

The comparison maps from each
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(H/K))
to
\Spc(\mathscr{K}(G))
are similarly induced by the tt-functor
\mathscr{K}(G)\xrightarrow{\Psi^{K}}\mathscr{K}({G}/\!\!/{K})\xrightarrow{\Res}\mathscr{K}(H/K)
.

We now prove the analogous statement for the stable permutation category.

4.11 Theorem
.

Modular fixed points and restrictions yield as above a homeomorphism of topological spaces

(4.12)

\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{(H,K)\in\mathcal{E}_{p}(G)}\Spc(\stperm(H/K;k))\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\Spc(\stperm(G;k)).

Proof.

In view of
3.8
, it suffices to verify that for each of the various functors
f^{*}
discussed above (namely modular fixed points, restriction and conjugation) with induced map
f=\Spc(f^{*})
on spectra, we have
f^{-1}(\{\text{closed points}\})=\{\text{closed points}\}
.
For then the homeomorphism (
4.12
) is the restriction of the homeomorphism (
4.10
) to the open complement of the closed points.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from
3.6
and
4.1
.
The converse inclusion is
[
BG25a
, Lemma 11.9 (b)]
.
∎

5.
Bottleneck subgroups

In this short preparatory section,
P
is a
p
-group.
We discuss when
P
admits a ‘bottleneck’ subgroup
H
by which we mean a proper non-trivial subgroup
1\neq H<P
that cannot be surrounded by a section
K^{\prime}<H<H^{\prime}
with
K^{\prime}\trianglelefteqslant H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
elementary abelian of
p
-rank at least two. In other words, we discuss when every subgroup
H^{\prime}
containing
H
as a subgroup of index
p
has only
H
as index-
p
subgroup.

Bottleneck subgroups can exist at the top and at the bottom of a
p
-group:

5.1 Remark
.

It is well-known that a
p
-group that contains a
unique index-
p
subgroup

H
must be cyclic. Indeed,
H
must contain any central element of order
p
and, modding it out, one concludes by induction on the order of the group.

5.2 Remark
.

At the other end, a
p
-group that has a
unique subgroup of order
p
must be either a cyclic group or a generalized quaternion group
Q_{2^{n}}
of order
2^{n}
.
This is more involved, see
[
Bro82
, Theorem 4.3, p. 99]
.
Recall that for
n\geq 3
, the
generalized quaternion group
is presented as follows

(5.3)

Q_{2^{n}}=\langle x,y\mid x^{2^{n-1}}=1,\ y^{2}=x^{2^{n-2}},yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}\rangle.

Its unique subgroup of order 2 is the center
Z(Q_{2^{n}})=\langle y^{2}\rangle
.

Note that in those examples, both in the cyclic case and in the generalized quaternion case, the unique subgroup of order
p
is central.

We are going to show that these are the only examples.

5.4 Lemma
.

Let
P
be a
p
-group that contains a non-trivial proper subgroup
H
with the following property: For every subgroup
H^{\prime}\leq P
containing
H
with
[H^{\prime}\!:\!H]=p
, the group
H^{\prime}
is cyclic. Then
P
is cyclic or generalized quaternion.

Proof.

By induction, we can assume the result for every
p
-group of order
|P|/p
.
Recall that for every proper subgroup
H
of a
p
-group
P
, there exists
H<H^{\prime}\leq P
with
[H^{\prime}\!:\!H]=p
. It follows from our assumption (
H^{\prime}
being cyclic) that
H
is cyclic.

Observation:
Let
z\in Z(P)
be a central element of order
p
. Then
z\in H
.

Indeed, if
z
was not in
H
then the subgroup
H^{\prime}=\langle H,z\rangle\simeq H\times C_{p}
would contradict our hypothesis. Let us pick such a
z\in Z(P)\cap H
.

We distinguish two cases. First, suppose that
H=\langle z\rangle
. Then
H\leq Z(P)
is central. For every element
g\in P
of order
p
, the subgroup
H^{\prime}:=\langle z,g\rangle
cannot be bigger than
H
otherwise it would be isomorphic to
C_{p}\times C_{p}
and contradict our hypothesis again. It follows that
g\in H
. Consequently,
H
is the only subgroup of
P
of order
p
. By
5.2
we obtain that
P
is cyclic or generalized quaternion.

The second option is that
H
is strictly larger than the central cyclic subgroup
\langle z\rangle
.
Consider then the quotient
\bar{P}=P/\langle z\rangle
and
\bar{H}=H/\langle z\rangle\lneq\bar{P}
.
One easily verifies that
\bar{H}
still satisfies the same hypothesis in
\bar{P}
as
H
did in
P
.
By induction hypothesis, we know that
\bar{P}
is either cyclic or generalized quaternion, and in particular admits a unique subgroup of order
p
, which is central and therefore necessarily in
\bar{H}
by the above Observation (applied to
\bar{P}
and
\bar{H}
).

Let
g\in P
be an element of order
p
. We claim that
g\in H
. Its class
\bar{g}\in\bar{P}
modulo
\langle z\rangle
has order
1
or
p
hence belongs to
\bar{H}
by the above discussion. It follows from this and from
\langle z\rangle\leq H
that
g\in H
as claimed.

Consequently,
H
contains every subgroup of
P
of order
p
and since the cyclic group
H
has a unique subgroup of order
p
the same holds for
P
.
We conclude that
P
must be cyclic or generalized quaternion by
5.2
.
∎

We shall use the above result in the following form:

5.5 Proposition
.

Let
P
be a
p
-group that is neither cyclic nor generalized quaternion and let
1\neq H<P
be a proper non-trivial subgroup.
Then there exist a section
K^{\prime}\trianglelefteqslant H^{\prime}\leq P
and proper inclusions
K^{\prime}<H<H^{\prime}
, with

elementary abelian (necessarily of
p
-rank at least two).

Proof.

By
5.4
there exists a
non-cyclic
subgroup
H^{\prime}>H
with
[H^{\prime}\!:\!H]=p
. Since
H^{\prime}
is not cyclic, it has more than one subgroup of index
p
(
5.1
). We get the result with
K^{\prime}=\bigcap_{[H^{\prime}\,:\,N]=p}N
the Frattini subgroup of

.
∎

6.

We can apply

7.
Decomposition over cyclic and generalized quaternion

We want to prove a converse to
6.1
. If the
p
-Sylow of

is cyclic or generalized quaternion (for

in the latter case) then

will decompose and we want to identify the components.

We are going to use a general fact about ‘big’ tt-categories

,
i.e.
compactly-rigidly generated tensor-triangulated categories. Recall from
3.7
that
\mathscr{T}|_{U}:=\mathscr{T}/\Locname(\mathscr{T}^{c}_{Y})
when

is the complement of a Thomason subset (
e.g.
a quasi-compact open).
Any coproduct-preserving tt-functor

between big tt-categories induces a continuous map
f:=\Spc(f^{*})\colon\Spc(\mathscr{S}^{c})\to\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
and the functor
f^{*}
sends
\mathscr{T}_{Y}
into
\mathscr{S}_{f^{-1}(Y)}
by
[

, Theorem 6.3]
. In particular
f^{-1}(U)^{\complement}=f^{-1}(U^{\complement})
is Thomason in
~\Spc(\mathscr{S}^{c})
.
It follows that
f^{*}
induces a tt-functor on the localizations
\bar{f}^{*}\colon\mathscr{T}|_{U}\to\mathscr{S}|_{f^{-1}(U)}
, that we can denote

.

7.1 Proposition
.

With above notation for
f^{*}\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
and
U\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
, suppose that
f^{*}
is fully faithful. Then the induced functor

f^{*}|_{U}\colon\mathscr{T}|_{U}\to\mathscr{S}|_{f^{-1}(U)}

remains fully faithful. Moreover, suppose in addition that
\mathscr{S}
is generated as a tensor-triangulated category by
f^{*}(\mathscr{T})
and some collection of objects supported outside of
f^{-1}(U)
, that is, objects in
\Locname(\mathscr{S}^{c}_{f^{-1}(U)^{\complement}})
. Then
f^{*}|_{U}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

The full-faithfulness of
f^{*}|_{U}
is
[
BG25b
, Lemma 5.1]
. For the moreover part, the generators supported outside of
f^{-1}(U)
become zero in
\mathscr{S}|_{f^{-1}(U)}
by definition. It follows that
f^{*}|_{U}
is also essentially surjective.
∎

7.2 Remark
.

We are going to apply the above to
f^{*}
given by inflation along
G\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}G/N
for a particular normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteqslant G
. Note that inflation does not descend to stable permutation categories, very much for the same reasons as with the usual stable module category: It does not preserve equivariantly perfect complexes. (For instance
k(G/N)
is inflated from a
G/N
-perfect but if
N
admits a non-trivial
p
-subgroup

the object
\Psi^{H}(k(G/N))\simeq k^{[G:N]}
is not perfect.) However, the version of inflation that we shall use is the one on derived permutation categories
\Infl^{G/N}_{G}\colon\DPerm(G/N;k)\to\DPerm(G;k)
, which is fine
[
BG25a
, § 4]
.

7.3 Proposition
.

For every normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteqslant G
, the coproduct-preserving tt-functor
\Infl^{G/N}_{G}\colon\DPerm(G/N;k)\to\DPerm(G;k)
is fully faithful.

Proof.

As with any coproduct-preserving tt-functor one can verify full-faithfulness on compacts (check that the unit
\Id\to f_{*}f^{*}
is an isomorphism and use cocontinuity of
f^{*}
and
f_{*}
). Here, the functor
\Infl^{G/N}_{G}\colon\Komp_{\smallb}(\perm(G/N;k)^{\natural})\to\Komp_{\smallb}(\perm(G;k)^{\natural})
is fully faithful because so is
\Infl^{G/N}_{G}\colon\perm(G/N;k)\to\perm(G;k)
, the latter because it is the restriction of the usual restriction-of-scalars functor on modules
\Infl^{G/N}_{G}\colon\modname(k(G/N))\to\modname(kG)
along a ring epimorphism
kG\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}k(G/N)
.
∎

Let us start discussing our examples of cyclic or generalized quaternions groups.

k(N_{G}(H)/P)
since
({G}/\!\!/{H})/(P/H)\cong N_{G}(H)/P
.
Now the functor

maps

to
k\big((G/P)^{H}\big)=k(N_{G}(H,P)/P)
and crucially
N_{G}(H,P)=\big\{\,g\in G\,\big|\,{}^{g}H\leq P\,\big\}
is equal to
N_{G}(H)
since
H
is the
unique
cyclic subgroup of order 2 in the generalized quaternion group
P
. It follows that the top functor in (
7.7
) maps
A=k(G/P)
to
(\Psi^{H}(k(G/P)),\bar{\Upsilon}(k(G/P)))=(k(N_{G}(H)/P),k(G/P))=B
and therefore, by general nonsense, it is also an equivalence. (If a tensor functor
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
sends a ring
A
to
F(A)=B
and if the induced functor
F\colon\Mod_{\mathscr{T}}(A)\to\Mod_{\mathscr{S}}(B)
is an equivalence then
F
induces an equivalence
\Desc_{\mathscr{T}}(A)\overset{\sim}{\,\to\,}\Desc_{\mathscr{S}}(B)
; therefore, if
A
and
B
satisfy descent then the original
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
was an equivalence.)
∎

7.10 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a finite group whose
p
-Sylow subgroup is cyclic of order
p^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Choose a tower
1=H_{n}<H_{n-1}<\ldots<H_{1}<H_{0}\leq G
of cyclic subgroups
H_{i}
of order
p^{n-i}
.
Then the stable permutation category
\StPerm(G;k)
is equivalent to the product of the stable
module
categories
\StMod(k({G}/\!\!/{H_{i}}))
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. The components
\StPerm(G;k)\to\StMod(k({G}/\!\!/{H_{i}}))
of this equivalence are given by the (stable) modular
H_{i}
-fixed points functors
\Psi^{H_{i}}
composed with the canonical localization
\bar{\Upsilon}\colon\StPerm({G}/\!\!/{H_{i}};k)\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}\StMod(k({G}/\!\!/{H_{i}}))
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to the proof of
7.6
: One proves it for the
p
-Sylow and one reduces to that case by descent. For the
p
-group case (the Sylow) one proceeds by an easy induction on
n
using
7.4
, since in this case
G/N
is cyclic of order
p^{n-1}
. For the descent argument one uses once again the little miracle about the uniqueness of the subgroup
H
of a certain order in the
p
-Sylow, which gives us
N_{G}(H,P)=N_{G}(H)
for every
H=H_{1},\ldots,H_{n-1}
.
Details are left to the interested reader.
∎

and

1.4
and
1.3
in the introduction in the case of
p
-groups.

7.11 Remark
.

In the above two proofs, we use central cyclic subgroups
N\triangleleft P
of the
p
-Sylow of
G
, typically their center
N=Z(P)
. Note that we cannot apply
7.4
directly to
N
in
G
as there is no guarantee that
N
remains normal in
G
. For instance, for
P=Q_{8}=\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}
the usual quaternion group, we can find a finite abelian group
A
of odd order on which
Q_{8}
acts in such a way that the action of
N=\{\pm 1\}
is non-trivial, for instance the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vectorspace
A=\mathbb{F}_{\!\!3}^{2}
with the action of
Q_{8}
via the usual embedding
Q_{8}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\!\!3})
given by
i\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
j\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. In such a case, if we let
G=Q_{8}\ltimes A
then the
2
-Sylow
P=Q_{8}\times\{1\}
is not normal and the element
(-1,a)
conjugates
N
to a different cyclic subgroup, for any
a\in A
not fixed by
-1
.
