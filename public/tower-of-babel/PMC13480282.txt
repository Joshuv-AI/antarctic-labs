Chronic Total Occlusion Recanalization in a 14-Year-Old Girl With Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum
Authors: Evandro Martins Filho, Fabio Rodrigo Furini, Bruna Pedroso Pereira, Selma Rodrigues Chaves, Alessandra Teixeira Oliveira, Paulo Eduardo Ballve Behr, Luisa Pigatto Kalil, Carlos Collet, Taito Arai, Valter Correia de Lima
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE) is a hereditary disorder causing progressive arterial mineralization that may cause premature coronary disease in children.Case SummaryA 14-year-old girl with exertional angina since age 8 was diagnosed with PXE after her mother used an artificial intelligence tool to identify skin lesions. Coronary imaging revealed mid-left anterior descending artery chronic total occlusion without CT macrocalcification. Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) demonstrated concentric high-echoic wall thickening without acoustic shadowing, consistent with mineralized elastic lamina rather than atherosclerosis. A stentless drug-coated balloon strategy was chosen. At 10 months, she was asymptomatic with sustained left anterior descending artery patency by scheduled angiography.DiscussionThis is the first reported IVUS-guided chronic total occlusion recanalization in a pediatric PXE patient, illustrating how intravascular imaging can characterize disease biology and guide individualized device selection.Take-Home MessagesIn children with unexplained exertional symptoms and skin lesions, PXE should be considered. When IVUS identifies a nonatherosclerotic substrate, a stentless strategy may preserve vessel growth potential.

History of Presentation

For 6 years, a girl's progressive exertional chest pain, dyspnea, and presyncope had been attributed to anxiety. By age 14, she could no longer tolerate minimal physical activity. Treadmill stress testing finally objectified the problem: 3-mm ST-segment depression with severe chest pain and near-syncope during recovery. Dobutamine stress echocardiography confirmed extensive anterior-wall ischemia with ejection fraction declining from 64% to 54% at peak stress.Take-Home Messages•In children with exertional chest pain and objective ischemia, heritable connective tissue disorders such as pseudoxanthoma elasticum should be considered, particularly when characteristic skin lesions are present.•When intravascular imaging identifies a nonatherosclerotic mineralizing substrate in pseudoxanthoma elasticum–related coronary chronic total occlusion, a stentless strategy with cutting balloon and drug-coated balloon may preserve the vessel for future interventions in this progressive disease.

The diagnostic breakthrough came from the patient's mother. Clusters of yellowish papules had been spreading across the girl's cervical and flexural regions for 5 years (Figure 1); a skin biopsy in 2020 had been nondiagnostic. Driven by persistent concern, the mother photographed the lesions and submitted them to an artificial intelligence–based tool, which suggested pseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE). She sought targeted dermatologic consultation, and a repeat biopsy revealed fragmented and calcified elastic fibers within the reticular dermis, confirming PXE (Figure 2). Genetic testing identified compound heterozygous ABCC6 nonsense variants (c.3971 G > A, p.Trp1324Ter and c.3421 C > T, p.Arg1141Ter), establishing the molecular diagnosis. Past Medical History

The patient had no cardiovascular risk factors, systemic inflammatory disease, or familial hypercholesterolemia. There was no history of Kawasaki disease, congenital heart defects, or prior cardiac interventions.

Differential Diagnosis

In an adolescent with objective myocardial ischemia and no traditional risk factors, the differential included congenital coronary anomalies, Kawasaki disease, vasculitis, and heritable mineralization disorders. The characteristic cutaneous papules directed evaluation toward PXE, confirmed by histopathology and genetic testing.

Investigations

Laboratory evaluation excluded conventional atherosclerotic risk factors: lipid profile was normal (low-density lipoprotein 84 mg/dL), glycated hemoglobin 4.9%, and autoimmune screening negative. Mild normocytic anemia and subclinical calcium-phosphorus axis disturbance (low intact parathyroid hormone, reduced 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D) were consistent with PXE-related ectopic mineralization (Supplemental Table 2). Dedicated coronary computed tomography angiography analysis using a computational plaque characterization platform (QAngioCT, Medis Medical Imaging) revealed left-dominant circulation with mid-left anterior descending artery (LAD) chronic total occlusion (CTO) (length 10 mm, total lesion length 56 mm), a 23-mm myocardial bridge overlying the occluded segment, retrograde distal filling via collaterals, and multivessel disease (Figure 4A). Three-dimensional reconstruction and plaque characterization identified fibrous, fibrofatty, necrotic core, and calcified components with an overall coronary calcium burden <1%, with the LAD territory representing 62.8% of the myocardial mass at risk (Figures 4B to 4D). Invasive angiography confirmed mid-LAD CTO with a blunt proximal cap and well-developed septo-septal collaterals (Rentrop grade 3), significant marginal branch disease of the dominant left circumflex, and diffuse narrowing of the nondominant right coronary artery (Figure 5). This pattern of multivessel obstruction without fluoroscopic calcification was inconsistent with atherosclerosis. Management

A multidisciplinary heart team favored percutaneous revascularization of the LAD over surgical bypass given the patient's age and anticipated somatic growth; left circumflex and right coronary disease were deferred to medical therapy. A stentless drug-coated balloon (DCB) strategy was planned as definitive therapy, reserving stent implantation as bailout only.

Through right femoral access, an 8F EBU 3.5 guiding catheter engaged the left coronary system (Table 1). Diagnostic angiography localized the mid-LAD CTO (Figure 6A). An intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) catheter was delivered into a septal branch adjacent to the occlusion, while an intermediate tip-load (6g) tapered guidewire under microcatheter support was positioned at the proximal cap (Figure 6B). Precrossing IVUS from the septal branch localized the cap at the 10-o'clock position, defining the initial wire vector (Figure 6C). After cap penetration, IVUS confirmed the intraplaque wire position (Figure 6D). Lesion preparation consisted of noncompliant balloon inflation (2.25 × 30 mm, 24 atm) followed by sequential cutting-balloon angioplasty (2.0 × 15 and 2.5 × 15 mm, 22 atm). Postcrossing IVUS revealed the central finding: concentric, high-echoic arterial wall thickening without acoustic shadowing, in the absence of lipid-rich plaque, consistent with mineralization of the internal elastic lamina rather than atherosclerosis (Figure 6H). A hypoechoic muscular band suggestive of an intramyocardial course was also identified (Figure 6I), and controlled distal dissection was noted following cutting-balloon inflation (Figure 6J). Definitive treatment used a paclitaxel-coated balloon (2.5 × 30 mm, 6 atm, 60 seconds) (Figure 6K). Final angiography demonstrated TIMI flow grade 3 with no residual stenosis (Figure 6L).

Outcome and follow-up

The procedure was completed without complications (peak troponin I 0.8 ng/mL). The patient was discharged on dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin 100 mg and clopidogrel 75 mg) for 6 months and rosuvastatin 20 mg (for pleiotropic vascular protection given multivessel disease). At 6-month follow-up, exertional angina had nearly resolved (Canadian Cardiovascular Society class I, previously class III), and she had returned to school sports including volleyball (Figure 7). Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first CTO recanalization in a pediatric patient with PXE combining IVUS-guided crossing with a stentless DCB strategy. Takamatsu et al1 recently reported stentless DCB treatment in a 14-year-old male with ABCC6 variants and multisegment right coronary artery stenosis, but that case involved focal lesions rather than CTO. PXE is caused by biallelic ABCC6 loss-of-function variants that impair hepatic pyrophosphate secretion, leading to progressive ectopic mineralization of elastic fibers in skin, retina, and arterial media.2,3 Coronary involvement typically presents in adulthood3,4; CTO during adolescence is exceedingly rare.5,6

The central contribution of this case is the demonstration that IVUS can identify the disease substrate and directly inform device selection. The circumferential high-echoic wall thickening without acoustic shadowing, in the absence of lipid-rich plaque, is consistent with fragmented, calcified elastic fibers of the internal elastic lamina, a morphology described by Fujisaki et al5 and Anzai et al6 in adult PXE patients. Notably, macrocalcification was absent on conventional CT despite extensive obstruction, although dedicated computational plaque characterization identified calcified components at a coronary calcium burden <1%, reinforcing that PXE-related mineralization occurs at a microscale below CT spatial resolution.6,7 This imaging-characterized substrate drove the decision to avoid stent implantation: In a vessel undergoing progressive medial mineralization with unpredictable remodeling, a permanent metallic scaffold risks late malapposition, a concern amplified in a growing adolescent. DCB angioplasty preserved native vessel geometry and future revascularization options.8 The coexistence of a 23-mm myocardial bridge overlying the CTO segment suggests that localized mechanical stress may have contributed to premature focal obstruction.

CTO intervention in PXE poses specific technical challenges related to stiff mineralized walls.9 IVUS-guided proximal cap localization, real-time intraplaque position verification, and three-dimensional wiring with controlled incremental advancement were essential for safe crossing.9,10 Several limitations should be acknowledged: This is a single case with medium-term follow-up, serial invasive imaging was not performed, optical coherence tomography was not used and could have provided complementary elastic lamina characterization, and the described IVUS phenotype requires validation in larger series.

Conclusions

In a growing adolescent with PXE and LAD CTO, IVUS identified a nonatherosclerotic mineralizing arteriopathy and guided a vessel-preserving stentless strategy. This case illustrates how disease biology assessed through intravascular imaging can inform individualized interventional decisions in heritable coronary arteriopathies.
