Cloning Encrypted Quantum States in Arbitrary Dimensions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Filip-Ioan Ceară

Abstract
Recently, Yamaguchi and Kempf proved that encrypted qubits can be cloned. In this work, we generalize the encrypted cloning protocol and prove that it also applies to higher-order quantum systems. Given that a straightforward generalization of the protocol using the exponential of the shift and phase operators fails to satisfy the unitary requirement for a quantum gate, we propose a different approach. We introduce a new operator to be used in the encryption process and show that it is unitary. We adapt the decryption operator from the reference paper to fit in the framework of multi-level quantum systems. We analyze the circuit implementation of the proposed operators and show that the overhead imposed by larger dimensions scales linearly with qudit dimension.



I
Introduction

Generalizing qubit algorithms or protocols to qudits is a challenging task in quantum information theory [
10
]. The main advantage of multi-dimensional quantum states can especially be observed in the field of quantum communication [
4
].
A qudit can implement more efficient quantum communication protocols because it has an inherently larger alphabet size and is more robust to noise [
3
]. This increased “noise threshold” makes qudit-based systems more suitable for long-distance communication
Furthermore, multi-qudit systems allow more complex entanglement structures, thus enabling more sophisticated secret-sharing schemes [
8
].

Recently, Yamaguchi and Kempf [
11
] presented a protocol that allows the cloning of encrypted states of a qubit, without violating the no-cloning theorem. We solve one of the open problems stated in their paper, showing that the cloning of encrypted states is not restricted to qubit-based systems. Thus, we establish that this protocol is intrinsic to quantum information encoded in finite dimensions. This result highlights the protocol’s scalability and relevance. Thus, one can leverage the protocol’s scalability and the advantages of qudit systems to propose new schemes in quantum cryptography.

In this work, we discuss the fact that the natural generalization of the encryption operator, as the exponential of a generalized Pauli operator, cannot be performed for
d\geq 3
. Thus, we propose a generalization of the encryption operator based on a constant-amplitude zero-autocorrelation (CAZAC) sequence. For the decryption matrix, we generalize the form given in [
11
] to the qudit case. We show that both of the proposed matrices are unitary. We demonstrate that, within the protocol, the proposed matrices meet the imposed requirements. Thus, the encryption operator creates a state indistinguishable from a maximally mixed state, while the decryption operator successfully retrieves the information originally stored in the qudit of interest, as detailed in the protocol.

The structure of the article is the following.
In Section
II
, we define the problem statement and introduce the notations that will be used throughout the paper. In Section
III
, we perform the analysis of the proposed matrices for encryption and decryption operations. The methods for implementing the unitary matrices are analyzed in Section
IV
, where we also evaluate the gate complexity and assess the number of one- and two-qudit gates required to implement the protocol. Finally, we summarize the work in Section
V
. All the proofs of the statements that we have used during this work are detailed in the appendices.

II
Preliminaries

A qudit represents a multi-dimensional quantum state that generalizes the qubit (which can be represented mathematically as a vector in a two-dimensional Hilbert space). Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
be a Hilbert space with dimension
d=\dim\mathcal{H}_{d}
. Thus, any qudit
\left|\psi\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
can be written as
\left|\psi\right\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}\left|i\right\rangle
, using the generalized computational basis
\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|1\right\rangle,\dots,\left|d-1\right\rangle\}
[
9
].

We define the generalized Pauli operators, often called Weyl operators, in the computational basis as:

\displaystyle X_{d}\left|k\right\rangle=\left|k\oplus_{d}1\right\rangle

(1)

\displaystyle Z_{d}\left|k\right\rangle=\omega^{k}\left|k\right\rangle,

(2)

where
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}
is a root of unity of order
d
and
\oplus_{d}
represents the addition modulo
d
. Respectively, the notation
\ominus_{d}
is equivalent to subtraction modulo
d
. The
X_{d}
is also called the shift operator because it acts as a permutation in the computational basis, while the
Z_{d}
is often named the phase operator. It is clear that
X_{d}^{d}=Z_{d}^{d}=I_{d}
, where
I_{d}
represents the identity matrix of dimension
d
. It can be easily shown that
X_{d}^{\dagger}=X_{d}^{-1}
and
Z_{d}^{\dagger}=Z_{d}^{-1}
. Thus, unlike the case of
d=2
, where
X^{-1}=X
, and
Z^{-1}=Z
, for any
d\geq 3
the generalized Pauli operators are no longer hermitian.

The Fourier transform
F
maps the two generalized Pauli operators

X_{d}=F^{\dagger}Z_{d}F,

(3)

and is defined in [
5
] as

F\left|k\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\left|j\right\rangle.

(4)

For an arbitrary one-qudit gate
U
, we can define two types of controlled gates. The first one is defined as the
C(U)
gate [
3
], acting on the control qudit
\left|j\right\rangle
and the target qudit
\left|k\right\rangle
, as

C(U)\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\right\rangle=\left|j\right\rangle U^{j}\left|k\right\rangle.

(5)

The second is the
p
-controlled gate, which is denoted as
C_{p}(U)
and is acting as

C_{p}(U)\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\right\rangle=\left|j\right\rangle U^{j\delta_{p,j}}\left|k\right\rangle.

(6)

In this case, the quantum gate
U
is applied to the target qudit only when
j=p
, as specified by the Kronecker-delta function
\delta_{j,p}
.
Using the definition in (
5
), the generalized controlled Pauli operators
C(X_{d})
and
C(Z_{d})
are

\displaystyle C(X_{d})\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\right\rangle=\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\oplus_{d}j\right\rangle,

(7)

\displaystyle C(Z_{d})\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\right\rangle=\omega^{jk}\left|j\right\rangle\left|k\right\rangle.

(8)

The quantum state

\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle\left|p\right\rangle

(9)

represents the generalized Bell state and could be easily obtained by applying the
F
and
C(X_{d})
gates, as

C(X_{d})\cdot(F\otimes I_{d})\left|00\right\rangle=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle.

(10)

The generalization that we are proposing for the encryption cloning protocol, which is developed in [
11
], assumes the existence of a data qudit
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
and
n
pairs of maximally entangled states
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{i},N_{i}}=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle
. The
S_{i}
qudits represent those used to clone the encrypted state, while the
N_{i}
qudits are stored locally and used to retrieve the initial information stored in the data qudit. In the encryption process, a unitary gate will be applied to the data qudit
A
and to all the
S_{i}
qudits. The application of the encryption matrix should produce a quantum state that prevents any measurement of any of the
S_{i}
qudits from revealing information about the data qudit. Thus, the qudits
S_{i}
could be shared among
n
parties, while keeping all
N_{i}
qudits locally, where the data qudit originated. After choosing one of the qudits
S_{i}
, applying the decryption operator to that qudit and all of the
N_{i}
qudits allows a single party to have access to the original quantum state stored in the data qudit, while all of the remaining
n-1
parties obtain only a maximally mixed state.

III
Operator definitions

In this section, we provide definitions of the encryption and decryption operators and prove that both are indeed unitary matrices. Moreover, we show that both matrices satisfy the functional requirements stated in Section
II
.

III.1
Encryption operator

Similar to [
11
], we define the operators
P_{X}
and
P_{Z}

\displaystyle P_{X}=X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}\otimes\dots\otimes X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{n})}

(11)

\displaystyle P_{Z}=Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}\otimes\dots\otimes Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{n})},

(12)

where the
X_{d}^{(i)}
, and
Z_{d}^{(i)}
are the generalized Pauli operators described in (
1
), and (
2
) respectively. We use the notation
X_{d}^{(i)}
to illustrate the application of the
X_{d}
to the qudit labeled with
i
. We express the encryption gate as in the following expression:

U_{enc}^{(n)}=V(P_{X})V(P_{Z}),

(13)

using a generic map
V
. Therefore, we define the required properties of the operator
V(P)
, such that the encrypted state in each of the
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{i}}
reveals no information about the original state of the data qudit. The natural generalization of the operator
V(P)
is the one described in the following equation

V(P)=e^{-i\theta P},

(14)

which, for the case of
\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}
, results in the same form of the operators as in the original paper [
11
]. For the case of
d=2
the matrices
X_{2}
and
Z_{2}
are hermitian matrices, resulting that the operator in (
14
) can be written as

V(P)=\cos{\theta}\cdot I_{2}-i\sin{\theta}\cdot P.

(15)

For the case of
d\geq 3
, due to the property of
X_{d}
and
Z_{d}
not being hermitian matrices, the operator described in (
14
) yields a non-unitary matrix [
5
]. Thus, this natural generalization cannot be used for qudits.

To achieve the condition that the operator is unitary, we propose the operator

V(P)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}c(k)P^{k},

(16)

where the
c(k)
represents a Chu Sequence, a particular case of a Zadoff-Chu Sequence [
1
]. All Zadoff-Chu sequences are CAZAC, meaning they have a perfectly flat power spectrum and their periodic autocorrelation is a Kronecker-delta function. The general expression for this type of sequence is given in the following equation

zc(k)=\exp\Big({-i\frac{\pi uk(k+c_{f}+2q)}{d}}\Big),

(17)

with the requirement that
c_{f}=d\pmod{2}
, and
gcd(u,d)=1
. The
c(k)
sequence is obtained by choosing the integer parameters
u=1
and
q=0
. Thus, we obtain the following operator that will be used in the encryption gate

V(P)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{-i\frac{\pi k(k+d\%2)}{d}}P^{k},

(18)

where we have defined
\%
as the remainder of division by 2.
Using the expression of the the operator
V(P)
as expressed in (
18
), with
P_{X}
, and
P_{Z}
, as described in (
11
) and (
12
), respectively, the encryption matrix has the following expression

U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{-i\frac{\pi k(k+d\%2)}{d}}{(P_{X})}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-i\frac{\pi l(l+d\%2)}{d}}{(P_{Z})}^{l}.

(19)

Following the form of the encryption gate in (
19
), expressing the operators
P_{X},P_{Z}
by indicating the qudit they are being applied to, and using the notation
c_{kl}=c(k)\cdot c(l)=e^{-i\frac{\pi k(k+d\%2)}{d}}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi l(l+d\%2)}{d}}
, we obtain the following expression

U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}({X_{d}^{(A)}})^{k}({Z_{d}^{(A)}})^{l}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}({X_{d}^{(S_{i})}})^{k}({Z_{d}^{(S_{i})}})^{l}.

(20)

It can be easily seen that, for each of the coefficients
c_{kl}
,
|c_{kl}|=1
, thus resulting in an equal superposition of operators. One property of the Zadoff-Chu sequences is that they exhibit orthogonality between cyclically shifted versions of themselves [
1
]. Thus, each sequence of coefficients
c(k)
exhibits similar properties to those of white noise, as the autocorrelation is a Kronecker-delta function. It has been shown that the Kronecker product of two CAZAC sequences is also a CAZAC sequence [
7
]. Thus, when using the coefficients
c_{kl}
, we are obtaining a sequence whose autocorrelation is equal to
0
everywhere except the origin. We can observe the behavior in
Figure

1
, and conclude that the coefficients that we use are similar to samples extracted from a pseudorandom generator, and can successfully be used for the encryption operator.

Figure 1:
2D Autocorrelation for
c_{kl}
coefficients, for different values of the qudit dimension. All the plots show a maximum value of
1
at
(0,0)
and the value
0
for all the other combinations.

We further show that the proposed operator from (
20
) is a consistent generalization because it produces the same expression as that given in the original paper. Thus, for the case of
d=2
, the coefficients
c_{kl}
have the following values
c_{00}=1,c_{01}=-i,c_{10}=-i,c_{11}=-1
, and thus the encryption unitary is given as

\displaystyle U_{enc_{2}}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(I_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}I_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}+(-i)\cdot Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}Z_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}

\displaystyle+(-i)\cdot X_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}+

\displaystyle(-1)(-i)^{n+1}\cdot Y_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}Y_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}\Big),

resulting in the same operator as the one given in
11
.

Recalling from (
19
), the encryption operator can be written as the product of two matrices
A_{X}
and
A_{Z}
, as shown

\displaystyle{U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{-i\frac{\pi k(k+d\%2)}{d}}P_{X}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-i\frac{\pi l(l+d\%2)}{d}}P_{Z}^{l}

\displaystyle=A_{X}\cdot A_{Z}.

To prove that
U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}
is unitary, we show that each of the matrices
A_{X}
and
A_{Z}
is unitary. Each of the two matrices is the sum of
d-1
unitary terms; thus, it is not trivial that the matrix is unitary. The proof that each of the matrices
A_{X}
and
A_{Z}
is unitary is given in Appendix
B
and is based on the orthogonality of terms in the Zadoff-Chu sequence. As a consequence, the matrix could be implemented as a gate and applied to a quantum circuit.

III.2
Decryption operator

For the decryption matrix, we follow the construction mechanism from [
11
], but adapt it for any dimension
d\geq 3
. In the original paper, the authors used the following result

\Big(\sigma_{\mu}^{(N_{i})}{}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\sigma_{\mu}^{(S_{i})}\Big)\left|\Phi_{2}\right\rangle=\left|\Phi_{2}\right\rangle,\forall\mu\in\{0,\dots,3\},

(21)

where the operators
\sigma_{\mu}
represent the Pauli operators
\{I_{2},X_{2},Y_{2},Z_{2}\}
. For
d\geq 3
, this result does not apply; thus, we propose the following theorem, whose proof is provided in Appendix
E.1
.

Theorem III.1
.

The following identity

\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(Q_{1})}}^{k_{1}}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(Q_{1})}}^{-k_{2}}\otimes{X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(Q_{2})}}^{k_{1}}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(Q_{2})}}^{k_{2}}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

holds for any
d\geq 2
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0\dots d-1\}
, where
Q_{1},Q_{2}
represent the two qudits involved.

Taking into account
Theorem

III.1
and using the notations
\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}}^{l}\otimes{I_{d}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{1})}\Big)\equiv O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}
and
(I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}\otimes{F^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{1})}}^{2})\left(\textstyle\sum_{c=0}^{d-1}\left(X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}\right)^{2c}\otimes(|c\rangle\langle c|)^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{1})}\right)\equiv C^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}
, we propose the following decryption operator:

U_{dec_{d}}^{(n)}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}^{-1}\text{SWAP}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}C^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1}N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right>\left<\Phi_{d}\right|{O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1}N_{1})}}^{\dagger}\\
\otimes\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{-l}\Big).

(22)

Hence, we defined the decryption operator that retrieves the information stored originally in the
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
to the
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{1}}
. Without loss of generality, the construction can be easily modified so that the information retrieval can be made on any other
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{i}}
. This transformation is possible because the state obtained after the encryption process is symmetrical, in the sense that the same operators have been applied to all the
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{i}}
. The construction that we proposed for the decryption operator is equivalent to a SWAP gate between the data qudit
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
and
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{N_{1}}
, as we have proved in Appendix
A.2
. Unlike the expression in [
11
], multi-dimensional quantum states require an additional SWAP gate and a gate
C
, composed of two one-qudit and two two-qudit gates.

As the expression given for
U_{dec_{d}}^{(n)}
in (
22
) is a sum of unitary terms, it is not trivial that the result is also unitary. Thus, we provide in Appendix
C
the proof that the matrix is indeed unitary, using the property that the generalized Pauli matrices form an orthonormal basis. Therefore, proving that the proposed matrix is unitary allows us to easily implement it as a gate in a qudit quantum circuit.

III.3
Correctness of the unitaries

As stated in Section
II
, the quantum state obtained after applying the encryption unitary to the initial state should be an encrypted state that reveals no information to an external observer. Furthermore, applying the decryption unitary to the encrypted state must recover the original information stored in the data qudit to the chosen party, specifically
S_{1}
in our case. If both requirements are satisfied, we assume that the two unitaries are suitable within the scope of the protocol. To prove this, we propose
Theorem

III.2
and
Theorem

III.3
. We provide the corresponding proofs in Appendices
E.2
and
E.3
.

Theorem III.2
.

For any operator
O_{1},O_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}}
,

\text{Tr}_{B}\Big((O_{1}\otimes I_{B})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{A,B}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{A,B}(O_{2}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\Big)=\frac{1}{d}O_{1}O_{2}^{\dagger}.

Theorem III.3
.

For all
k,l,m,n\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
,

\text{Tr}\Big((X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle k}Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle l}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|(Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle-n}X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle-m}\otimes I_{d})\Big)=\delta_{k,m}\delta_{l,n}.

To analyze whether the encrypted state provides no information about the data qudit, we compute the partial trace over a specific subsystem and show that it equals the maximally mixed state. In this scenario, we can be sure that no adversary can retrieve any information. We perform the computation on subsystem
S_{1}
. We proved that the expected result is obtained without imposing any additional constraints on the other subsystems. Thus, the same argument can be applied to any other
S_{i}
.

To show that the proposed decryption gate performs the correct operation, we begin with the encrypted state and analyze its evolution. Since we selected the subsytem
(S_{1})
as the designated party, we demonstrate that the final state consists of a generalized Bell state
\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle
shared between qudits
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A},\text{ and }\left|\psi\right\rangle_{N1}
, while the data qudit is transferred to
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{1}}
. All other pairs are likewise left in the generalized Bell state
\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle
. Since this result is obtained without imposing any additional constraints, we can assume, without loss of generality, that the proposed unitary correctly implements the desired operation.

IV.2
Implementation of decryption operator

As the decryption operator has been constructed as the one in [
11
], the implementation of the unitary follows the same principle. Thus, we write the decryption unitary as

U_{dec_{d}}^{(n)}=c_{00}\cdot{\overline{T}^{\dagger}_{S_{1},N_{1}}}\cdot T_{d^{2}-1}\cdot...\cdot T_{2}\cdot T_{1}\cdot\overline{T}_{S_{1},N_{1}}.

(27)

The gate
\overline{T}_{S_{1},N_{1}}
performs the mapping

V
Conclusions

We have solved the open problem posed by Yamaguchi and Kempf and proved that the protocol generalizes successfully to qudits. As the Pauli operators are not hermitic for any
d\geq 3
, we have constructed an operator based on CAZAC sequences. The proposed encryption operator is a consistent generalization of the encryption gate in the original paper, as we have proved. The decryption matrix follows the construction architecture from the qubit case and is adjusted to account for the differences introduced by qudits. The additional overhead introduced by the quantum system’s dimensionality is linear in the qudit dimension for both encryption and decryption.

Useful Lemmas for multi-dimensional quantum states

Lemma A.1
.

For any operator
U\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}}
, the following equality is true.

(U\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle=(I_{d}\otimes U^{\mathsf{T}})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

Proof.

We prove the lemma by computing the LHS and the RHS and observing that they are equal. Firstly, we express
U
by means of an orthonormal basis. Thus, we can express
U
and its transpose as:

U=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}U_{ij}\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle j\right|,\text{ and }U^{\mathsf{T}}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}U_{ji}\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle j\right|.

The LHS has the following derivation

\displaystyle(U\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\Big(\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}U_{ij}\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle j\middle|p\right\rangle\Big)\left|p\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p,i=0}^{d-1}U_{ip}\left|i\right\rangle\left|p\right\rangle.

Similarly, the RHS of the equation has the following value

\displaystyle(I_{d}\otimes U^{\mathsf{T}})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle\Big(\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}U_{ji}\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle j\middle|p\right\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p,i=0}^{d-1}U_{pi}\left|p\right\rangle\left|i\right\rangle.

We can clearly observe that the two states are equal; thus, we can claim that the statement is true.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\otimes I_{d}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\Big(\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle\left|p\right\rangle\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle

We start by using the Lemma
A.1
for rewriting the left-hand side of the equation, using the property that

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i,p,c=0}^{d-1}Q_{i}\left|2i\oplus_{d}p\right\rangle X_{d}^{2c}\left|p\right\rangle\left|c\right>\left<c\right|\left|i\right\rangle

∎

Unitarity of encryption operator

To prove that
U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}
is unitary, we show that each of
A_{X}
and
A_{Z}
is unitary.
We prove
A_{X}
is unitary, by performing the notation
\alpha_{k}=e^{-i\frac{\pi ik(k+d\%2)}{d}}

\displaystyle A_{X}A_{X}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}P_{X}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{*}P_{X}^{-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}\alpha_{j}^{*}P_{X}^{(k-j)}

We perform the change of variable
k-j\mapsto m

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{j+m}\alpha_{j}^{*}\Big)P_{X}^{m}

Using Lemma
A.6
, we simplify

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\delta_{m,0}P_{X}^{m}

\displaystyle=P_{X}^{0}=I_{d}.

Following the same principle, we show that
A_{Z}
is unitary, using the same notation
\alpha_{k}=e^{-i\frac{\pi ik(k+d\%2)}{d}}
:

\displaystyle A_{Z}A_{Z}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}P_{Z}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{*}P_{Z}^{-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}\alpha_{j}^{*}P_{Z}^{(k-j)}

We perform the change of variable
k-j\mapsto m

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{j+m}\alpha_{j}^{*}\Big)P_{Z}^{m}

Using Lemma
A.6
, we simplify

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\delta_{m,0}P_{Z}^{m}

\displaystyle=P_{Z}^{0}=I_{d}.

We showed that

, and

are unitary matrices, thus their product,

If we recall (

is a unitary operator, we focus just on proving the other component, namely the operator

Because the operator is constructed by the summation of matrices that are obtained using tensor products of square matrices, it is obvious that the decryption operator is a square matrix. Thus, it is sufficient to show that

.

\displaystyle AA^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}^{-1}O_{kl}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|O_{kl}^{\dagger}\otimes U_{kl}\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{====}\cdot\sum_{m,n=0}^{d-1}{(c_{mn}^{-1})}^{*}O_{mn}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|O_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes U_{mn}^{\dagger}

Using the Lemma
A.4
,we simplify,

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}^{-1}{(c_{kl}^{-1})}^{*}O_{kl}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|O_{kl}^{\dagger}\otimes U_{kl}U_{kl}^{\dagger}

Since each

is unitary and

,

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\Big(O_{kl}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|O_{kl}^{\dagger}\Big)\otimes I_{d}^{\otimes(n-1)}

Using Lemma
A.5
, we find that

\displaystyle=I_{d}^{\otimes(2)}\otimes_{d}^{\otimes(n-1)}

We have shown that our proposed decryption operator is indeed unitary.

Appendix D
Correctness proof

The initial state of the quantum circuit is given as

\left|\psi\right\rangle_{init}=\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\Big(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{S_{i},N_{i}}\Big).

(32)

The encrypted state is obtained by applying the encryption unitary to the initial state. Thus, we obtain the state

\displaystyle\left|\psi\right\rangle_{enc}

\displaystyle=U_{enc_{d}}^{(n)}\otimes I_{d}^{\otimes(n)}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{init}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{l}\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\otimes

(33)

\displaystyle\phantom{===}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{l}\otimes I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{i})}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle.

The encryption operator should produce a quantum state that does not leak any information regarding the original
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
to any external party that might have access to any of the
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{i}}
. To show that the proposed unitary achieves this goal, we compute the partial trace on the
S_{1}
subsystem, and show that the result is the maximally mixed state
\frac{1}{d}I_{d}
. We are tracing out the density matrix
\rho_{enc}=\left|\psi\right\rangle_{enc}\left\langle\psi\right|_{enc}
. We can express the density matrix of the state as

\displaystyle\rho_{enc}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l,m,n=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}c_{mn}^{*}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle\left\langle\psi\right|A_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes

\displaystyle\phantom{====}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}(S_{kl}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|(S_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d}),

(34)

using the notations
A_{kl}={X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{l}
, and
S_{kl}={X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{l}
. The partial trace can be calculated as

\displaystyle\rho_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\overline{S_{1}}}\rho_{enc},

(35)

where
\overline{S_{1}}=A,S_{2},\dots,S_{n},N_{1},\dots,N_{n}
. One intermediary step is to take the partial trace over all of
S_{i},N_{i},i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
. Thus, the result is presented as follows.

\displaystyle\rho_{A,S_{1},N_{1}}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{S_{2},\dots,S_{n},N_{2},\dots,N_{n}}(\rho_{enc})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\cdot\sum_{k,l,m,n=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}c_{mn}^{*}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle\psi\right|_{A}A_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes

\displaystyle\phantom{=}(S_{kl}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{1},N_{1}}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{1},N_{1}}(S_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d})\otimes

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big(\bigotimes_{i=2}^{n}(S_{kl}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{i},N_{i}}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{i},N_{i}}(S_{mn}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d})\Big)

Using the linearity of the trace and

the result from
Theorem

III.3
,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\cdot\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}|c_{kl}|^{2}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle\psi\right|_{A}A_{kl}^{\dagger}\otimes

\displaystyle\phantom{=}(S_{kl}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{1},N_{1}}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{\scriptscriptstyle S_{1},N_{1}}(S_{kl}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d})\cdot 1

(36)

Taking the result of
\rho_{A,S_{1},N_{1}}
from (
D
), we can calculate the
\rho_{S_{1}}
, by taking the partial trace over the subsystems
A
and
N_{1}
. Using the result from
Theorem

III.2
, and the properties of the trace, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}|c_{kl}|^{2}\cdot\text{Tr}\Big(A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle\psi\right|_{A}A_{kl}^{\dagger}\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{Tr}_{N_{1}}\Big((S_{kl}\otimes I_{d})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{S_{1},N_{1}}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{S_{1},N_{1}}(S_{kl}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d})\Big)\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}1\cdot\text{Tr}\Big(\left\langle\psi\right|_{A}A_{kl}^{\dagger}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\Big)\cdot\frac{1}{d}S_{kl}S_{kl}^{\dagger}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}1\cdot 1\cdot\frac{1}{d}I_{d}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}I_{d}.

(37)

We proved that the encryption matrix we proposed indeed generates an encrypted state. We did not impose additional conditions on the
S_{1}
subsystem, which means that the same result will be obtained for any
\rho_{S_{i}}
.

The decrypted state is obtained by applying the unitary matrix proposed for the decryption operation to the encrypted state. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\psi\right\rangle_{dec}

\displaystyle=U_{dec_{d}}^{(n)}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{enc}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}^{-1}\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1}N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right>\left<\Phi_{d}\right|{O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1}N_{1})}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\dagger}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{-l}\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\cdot\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m,n=0}^{d-1}c_{mn}A_{mn}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}O_{mn}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{j},N_{j})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle,

(38)

where we have used the notations
O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}=({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1})}}^{l}\otimes{I_{d}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{1})})
,
A_{kl}={X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{l}
, and
\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}=\text{SWAP}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\cdot C^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}
. To show that the decryption unitary correctly retrieves in
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S1}
the information originally stored in the data qudit
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, we simply perform a step-by-step evolution of the state in (
D
). Thus, we calculate and obtain the following result.

\displaystyle\left|\psi\right\rangle_{dec}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\sum_{m,n=0}^{d-1}c_{kl}^{-1}c_{mn}\cdot\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}A_{mn}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\otimes

\displaystyle\phantom{==}O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|{O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}}^{\dagger}O_{mn}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle\otimes

\displaystyle\phantom{==}\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{-l}{X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{m}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{n}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{Using the result from Lemma \ref{th_orthonormal_basis}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}|c_{kl}|^{2}\text{SWAP}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{==}\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\Big({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{j})}}^{-l}{X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{k}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{i})}}^{l}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{Using the result from Theorem \ref{th_2}},

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}A_{kl}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}O_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{====}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{We use the property from Lemma \ref{th_swap}},

\displaystyle=\text{SWAP}^{\scriptscriptstyle(S_{1},N_{1})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle_{A}\left|p\right\rangle_{S_{1}}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{N_{1}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle_{A}\left|\psi\right\rangle_{S_{1}}\left|p\right\rangle_{N_{1}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=2}^{n}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle.

(39)

We showed that the information stored in qudit
A
is transferred now to
S_{1}
, as needed, without imposing any supplementary restriction on the other

Appendix E
Proof of the theorems

E.1
Proof of the Theorem
III.1

Let
\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle
be the generalized Bell state and
X_{d},Z_{d}
be the generalized Pauli operators. In this Appendix, we provide the derivation for the qudit identity

\left({X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{k_{1}}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}}^{-k_{2}}\otimes{X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)}}^{k_{1}}{Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)}}^{k_{2}}\right)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle,

(40)

for any dimension
d\geq 2
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by fixing one of the operators, constructing the other, and showing that we obtain the same result as in the theorem statement. Specifically, we need to calculate the
O_{A}
, and
O_{B}
, such that

\displaystyle\Big(O_{A}\otimes{X_{d}^{k_{1}}}{Z_{d}^{k_{2}}}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle,\text{and}

\displaystyle\Big({X_{d}^{k_{1}}}{Z_{d}^{-k_{2}}}\otimes O_{B}\Big)\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle.

For the calculation of
O_{A}
, we consider that there is no operator applied on the
(A)
subsystem. We observe the effect of the gates on the
(B)
subsystem, and then we compute the gates that cancel that effect.

\displaystyle I_{d}\otimes X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{k_{2}}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{k_{2}}\left|p\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\left|p\right\rangle\omega^{k_{2}p}\left|p\oplus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle

We perform :
m\mapsto p\oplus_{d}k_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\omega^{(m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle\left|m\right\rangle

Thus, we must find the operator that performs the following mapping
O_{A}(\omega^{m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle)=\left|m\right\rangle.

We can easily find that this operator is indeed
O_{A}=X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle-k_{2}}
because

\displaystyle X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{-k_{2}}\omega^{(m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=X_{d}^{k_{1}}\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle=\left|m\right\rangle

For the calculation of
O_{B}
, we consider that there is no operator applied to the
(B)
subsystem. We observe the effect of the gates applied to the
(A)
subsystem, and then we compute the gates that cancel that effect.

\displaystyle X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle k_{1}}Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle{-k_{2}}}\otimes I_{d}\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}X_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle k_{1}}Z_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle-k_{2}}\left|p\right\rangle\left|p\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{p=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\scriptscriptstyle-k_{2}p}\left|p\oplus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle\left|p\right\rangle

We perform :
m\mapsto p\oplus_{d}k_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\scriptscriptstyle-(m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m\right\rangle\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle

Thus, we must find the operator that performs the following mapping

O_{B}(\omega^{-(m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m\ominus_{d}k_{1}\right\rangle)=\left|m\right\rangle.

We can easily find that this operator is indeed
O_{B}=X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{k_{2}}
because

X_{d}^{k_{1}}Z_{d}^{k_{2}}\omega^{-(m-k_{1})k_{2}}\left|m-k_{1}\right\rangle=X_{d}^{k_{1}}\left|m-k_{1}\right\rangle=\left|m\right\rangle.

Considering that using the constructions presented above, we derive the form of the operators in the theorem statement, we have proved that the construction holds
\forall(k_{1},k_{2})\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}^{2}
.
∎

E.2
Proof of the Theorem
III.2

Let
\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle
be the generalized Bell state,
O_{1},O_{2}
be two general one-qudit operators and
I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)}
be the identity applied on the subsystem B.
We prove the identity

\text{Tr}_{B}\Big((O_{1}\otimes I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)})\left|\Phi_{d}\right\rangle_{A,B}\left\langle\Phi_{d}\right|_{A,B}(O_{2}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)})\Big)=\frac{1}{d}O_{1}O_{2}^{\dagger}.

(41)

Proof.

\displaystyle LHS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{l,p_{1},p_{2}=0}^{d-1}(I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\left\langle l\right|_{B})(O_{1}\otimes I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)})\left(\left|p_{1}\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle p_{2}\right|_{A}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{=======}\cdot\left(\left|p_{1}\right\rangle_{B}\left\langle p_{2}\right|_{B}\right)(O_{2}^{\dagger}\otimes I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(B)})(I_{d}^{\scriptscriptstyle(A)}\left|l\right\rangle_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{l,p_{1},p_{2}=0}^{d-1}O_{1}\left|p_{1}\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle p_{2}\right|_{A}O_{2}^{\dagger}\left\langle l\middle|p_{1}\right\rangle_{B}\left\langle p_{2}\middle|p_{1}\right\rangle_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}O_{1}\left|l\right\rangle_{A}\left\langle l\right|_{A}O_{2}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}O_{1}O_{2}^{\dagger}

∎
