The conformal limit for Nakajima quiver varieties
Sotiria Chatzimarkou and Panagiotis Dimakis

Abstract
. Inspired by Gaiotto’s conformal limit construction for Higgs bundles we define and study a conformal limit construction for Nakajima quiver varieties. We prove that the conformal limit is indeed a limit of a one-parameter family of points inside a specified quiver variety and that it gives a biholomorphic map between holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds foliating two different quiver varieties. In the last part of the paper we discuss the analog of Simpson’s conjecture on the completeness of these holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds.

1.
Introduction

On a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus
g\geq 2
equipped with a complex vector bundle
E
of rank
n
and degree zero one can define the Hodge moduli space
M_{Hod}
parametrizing polystalbe
\lambda
-connections.
M_{Hod}
admits a natural
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action and a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
equivariant map

\pi:M_{Hod}\to\mathbb{C}.

The fiber
\pi^{-1}(0)
is the moduli space
M_{H}
parametrizing polystable Higgs bundles and the fiber
\pi^{-1}(1)
is the de Rham moduli space
M_{dR}
parametrizing completely reducible complex flat connections. Given a stable Higgs bundle
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
with harmonic metric
h
consider the twistor line

[(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E}+\lambda\Phi^{\dagger_{h}},\lambda\partial_{E}^{\dagger_{h}}+\Phi)]\in\pi^{-1}(\lambda).

Combining the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action and the existence of twistor lines we can perform the following set of operations

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bar{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E}+\lambda R\Phi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}},\lambda\partial^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}+R\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1,\bar{\partial}_{E}+\lambda R\Phi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}},\partial^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}+\lambda^{-1}R\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{R,\hbar}=\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}+\hbar R^{2}\Phi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}+\hbar^{-1}\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hbar:=\lambda R^{-1}}

in order to obtain a family of complex flat connections indexed by
R>0
and
\hbar
a fixed positive constant. It was conjectured by Gaiotto in
7
and later proven in
6
that when
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
lies in the Hitchin section, the limit

\lim\limits_{R\to 0}\nabla_{R,\hbar}

exists and provides a biholomorphism from the Hitchin section to the space of Opers in
M_{dR}
. In
3
this theorem was generalized to the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
be the set of points in
M_{Hod}
whose
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-limit is the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-fixed point
[(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
with harmonic metric
h_{0}
. The restrictions
W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})=W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})\cap\pi^{-1}(0)
and
W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})=W(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})\cap\pi^{-1}(1)
are holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds of
M_{H}
and
M_{dR}
respectively, for
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
the conformal limit exists and takes the following expression

(1.2)

\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi):=\lim\limits_{R\to 0}\nabla_{R,\hbar}=\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{0}^{\dagger_{h_{0}}}+\hbar\Phi_{0}^{\dagger_{h_{0}}}+\hbar^{-1}\Phi

and it is a biholomorphic map from
W^{0}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
to
W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
.

More general conformal limit constructions have recently been obtained in
2
for parabolic Higgs bundles, in
8
for polystable fixed points and in
9
for Higgs bundles associated to real components. Apart from its inherent beauty and simplicity, the conformal limit map provides a way to endow the manifolds
W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
with coordinates, a fact which was essential in the proof in rank two
5
of Simpson’s conjecture
14
that the
W^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
are complete submanifolds of
M_{dR}
.

Even though the existence of a conformal limit was conjectured only in the realm of Higgs bundles, it is apparent from (
1.1
) that an analog of the family
\nabla_{R,\hbar}
can be constructed for any hyperkähler manifold admitting a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action with the extra property that this
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action extends to its twistor space. Thus, given such a hyperkähler manifold, it is meaningful to ask whether a conformal limit exists and whether it has certain desirable properties.

The purpose of the present paper is to carry out the construction of a conformal limit for a class of hyperkähler manifolds called Nakajima quiver varieties
12
. We chose to focus on Nakajima quiver varieties primarily because they are the only general family of hyperkähler manifolds with geometric features very similar to Higgs bundle moduli spaces whose structure is relatively well understood.

Nakajima quiver varieties are constructed as hyperkähler quotients of a vector space
\mathbb{M}
parametrizing all framed representations of a doubled quiver. Specifically,
\mathbb{M}
is hyperkähler and admits a compatible group action by
G_{\mathbf{v}}
. As a result admits a hyperkähler moment map

\mu=(\mu_{\mathbb{R}},\mu_{\mathbb{C}}):\mathbb{M}\longmapsto\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}

Given
(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})\in Z\oplus(Z\otimes\mathbb{C})
where
Z
is the center of
G_{\mathbf{v}}
, we define the Nakajima quiver variety as

\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})}:=\{\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})\}/G_{\mathbf{v}}.

Under a suitable genericity assumption on
(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})
these are complete hyperkähler manifolds and when
\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}=0
they admit a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action. The analogue of the Hodge moduli space is given by

M^{Q}_{Hod}:=\bigcup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{M}_{((1-|\xi|^{2})\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},-2i\xi\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}

and the twistor line for
(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})\in\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(0)
is given by

(B_{h}-\xi B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger},i_{k}-\xi j_{k}^{\dagger},B_{\bar{h}}+\xi B_{h}^{\dagger},j_{k}+\xi i_{k}^{\dagger})\in\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}((1-|\xi|^{2})\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(-2i\xi\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}).

Therefore, we can build the same family as in (
1.1
) for quiver varieties

(1.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B_{h},i_{k},RB_{\bar{h}},Rj_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B_{h}-\xi RB_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}},i_{k}-\xi Rj_{k}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}},RB_{\bar{h}}+\xi B_{h}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}},Rj_{k}+\xi i_{k}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B_{h}-\hbar R^{2}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger_{h_{R,\xi}}},i_{k}-\hbar R^{2}j_{k}^{\dagger_{h_{R,\xi}}},\hbar^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}+B_{h}^{\dagger_{h_{R,\xi}}},\hbar^{-1}j_{k}+i_{k}^{\dagger_{h_{R,\xi}}})=F_{R,\hbar}(p).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hbar=\xi R^{-1}}

and ask whether the limit exists and what properties it may possess.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that going from the second to the third and from the third to the fourth line the metric changes. This happens because the real moment map is not preserved by the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action so in both steps the metric changes in order to satisfy the appropriate real moment map. The fact that the final metric
h_{R,\hbar}
depends on both
R
and
\hbar
is the reason why the conformal limit will involve a gauge transformation which depends on both
\hbar
and the initial point unlike the Higgs bundle conformal map (
1.2
).

In order to answer the question we follow the paradigm of
3
and define a
Hodge slice

S_{p}
in the space
\mathbb{M}
which via a Kuranishi map provides a local coordinate chart of
\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
about the point
[p]
. If furthermore
[p]=[p_{0}]=[(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0})]
is a fixed point of the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action then the deformation theory allows us to define a natural subvariety
S^{+}_{p_{0}}\subseteq S_{p_{0}}
which we call the Białynicki-Birula (BB) slice. If
W(p_{0})
is the set of points in
M^{Q}_{Hod}
whose
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-limit is the point
p_{0}
then we will show that the restrictions
W^{0}(p_{0})=W(p_{0})\cap\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
and
W^{1}(p_{0})=W(p_{0})\cap\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
are holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds of
M_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
and
M_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
respectively and that the BB slice is biholomorphic to both of them. Moreover we will show that for every
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
there exists
g_{A}\in G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}
such that
g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A)\in M_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
. This will allow us to prove

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
[p_{0}]
and
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
the conformal limit

(1.4)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A)):=\lim\limits_{R\to 0}F_{R,\hbar}(g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A))=\\
&g_{A,\hbar}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})\end{split}

is a biholomorphism from
W^{0}(p_{0})
to
W^{1}(p_{0})
.

Remark 1.4
.

The point

p_{A}:=(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})

satisfies the complex moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{A})=-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
and
g_{A,\hbar}\in G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the unique complex gauge transformation for which the real moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A})=0
is satisfied.

Remark 1.5
.

By abuse of notation, we will write
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(p_{0}+A)
instead of
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A))
since
g_{A}
is uniquely determined.

Finally, we find it meaningful to formulate an analogue of Simpson’s conjecture for quiver varieties, namely

Conjecture 1.6
.

W^{1}(p_{0})
are complete submanifolds of
\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
for any fixed point
p_{0}
.

We prove part of the conjecture. In particular, we show that if
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
is such that
p_{0}+A
is not nilpotent then the line
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(p_{0}+A)
leaves every compact subset of
\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
. This implies the following:

Theorem 1.7
.

W^{1}(p_{0})
is a complete submanifold of
\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
for every
very stable
fixed point
p_{0}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the first part of this section we introduce Nakajima quiver varieties and present some of their basic properties, necessary for the later parts of the paper
12
. In the second part of the section we explain the core ideas behind the proof that the conformal limit for Higgs bundles exists
3
that we will generalize to the quiver variety setting.

2.1.
The geometry of Nakajima quiver varieties

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, non-empty graph with
n
vertices and let
H
be the set of pairs consisting of an edge together with an orientation. From here on, we fix an orientation of the graph
\Gamma
. By this we mean a subset
\Omega\subseteq H
such that
\bar{\Omega}\cup\Omega=H
,
\Omega\cap\bar{\Omega}=\emptyset
. For
h\in H
, we denote
\text{in}(h)
the incoming vertex and
\text{out}(h)
the outgoing vertex. We also denote
\bar{h}
the edge with its orientation reversed, meaning that
\text{in}(\bar{h})=\text{out}(h)
and
\text{out}(\bar{h})=\text{in}(h)
. Two different vertices may be joined by several edges, but no edge may join a vertex to itself (no loops are allowed). We number the vertices and identify the set of vertices with the set
{1,2,...,n}
. Let
(V_{k},W_{k})
be pairs of hermitian vector spaces for every vertex
k\in{1,2,...n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
Nakajima quiver representation space
:

(2.1)

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\left(\bigoplus_{h\in H}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{\text{out}(h)},V_{\text{in}(h)})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(W_{k},V_{k})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(V_{k},W_{k})\right),

where
\mathbf{v}=(\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}V_{1},...,\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}V_{n})
and
\mathbf{w}=(\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}W_{1},...,\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}W_{n})
denote the dimensions of the hermitian vector spaces.
We denote the elements of
\mathbf{M}
by triples
(B,i,j)
where
B_{h}\in\operatorname{Hom}(V_{\text{out}(h)},V_{\text{in}(h)})
with
B=(B_{h})_{h\in H}
,
i_{k}\in\operatorname{Hom}(W_{k},V_{k})
with
i=(i_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}
and
j_{k}\in\operatorname{Hom}(V_{k},W_{k})
with
j=(j_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}
.

The space
\mathbf{M}
carries a natural symplectic form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
given by:

(2.2)

\omega_{\mathbb{C}}((B,i,j),(B^{\prime},i^{\prime},j^{\prime})):=\sum\limits_{h\in H}\operatorname{Tr}(\varepsilon(h)B_{h}B_{\bar{h}})+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Tr}(i_{k}j^{\prime}_{k}-i^{\prime}_{k}j_{k}),

where
\varepsilon(h)=1
if
h\in\Omega
and
\varepsilon(h)=-1
if
h\in\bar{\Omega}
.

The symplectic vector space
\mathbf{M}
decomposes into the sum of Lagrangian subspaces
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M_{\Omega}}\oplus\mathbf{M_{\bar{\Omega}}}
where:

\begin{split}\mathbf{M_{\Omega}}:=&\bigoplus_{h\in\Omega}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{\text{out}(h)},V_{\text{in}(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(W_{k},V_{k})\\
\mathbf{M_{\bar{\Omega}}}:=&\bigoplus_{h\in\bar{\Omega}}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{\text{out}(h)},V_{\text{in}(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{k},W_{k}).\end{split}

Since
V,W
are hermitian vector spaces there exists a hermitian inner product on
\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)
defined by
(f,g)=\operatorname{Tr}(fg^{\dagger})
, where
(\bullet)^{\dagger}
denotes the hermitian adjoint. Using this inner product we define a second complex structure
J
on
\mathbf{M}
given by:

J(m,m^{\prime})=({-m^{\prime}}^{\dagger},m^{\dagger})\text{ for }(m,m^{\prime})\in\mathbf{M_{\Omega}}\oplus\mathbf{M_{\bar{\Omega}}}.

Combining the symplectic form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
and the complex structure
J
we define the metric

(2.3)

g((B,i,j),(B^{\prime},i^{\prime},j^{\prime})):=\omega_{\mathbb{C}}((B,i,j),J\cdot(B^{\prime},i^{\prime},j^{\prime}))=\sum\limits_{h\in H}\operatorname{Tr}(B_{h}B_{h}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger})+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Tr}(i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}+j_{k}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}j_{k}).

The two complex structures define a hyperkähler structure on
\mathbf{M}
. The gauge group
G_{\mathbf{v}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{U}(\operatorname{dim}V_{k})
acts on
\mathbf{M}
by:

g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},j_{k})=(g_{\text{in}(h)}B_{h}g_{\text{out}(h)}^{-1},g_{k}i_{k},j_{k}g_{k}^{-1}),

preserving the hyperkähler structure and the hermitian inner product on
\mathbf{M}
.
Let
\mu
be the corresponding hyperkähler moment map and
\mu_{\mathbb{R}},\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
the real and complex components of
\mu
. They have the following explicit forms:

(2.4)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(B,i,j)&=\frac{i}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}B_{h}B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}+i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}-j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\right)_{k}\in\bigoplus_{k}\mathfrak{u}(V_{k})=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\\
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(B,i,j)&=\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(h)B_{h}B_{\bar{h}}+i_{k}j_{k}\right)_{k}\in\bigoplus_{k}\mathfrak{gl}(V_{k})=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C},\end{split}

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
is the Lie algebra of
G_{\mathbf{v}}
. Above we have identified
\mathfrak{g}
with its dual
\mathfrak{g^{*}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
be the center of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
and let
\zeta=(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})
be an element of
Z\oplus(Z\otimes\mathbb{C})
. We define the Nakajima quiver variety as the hyperkähler quotient
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}
of
\mathbb{M}
by
G_{\mathbf{v}}
:

(2.5)

\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}=\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\{(B,i,j)\in\mathbf{M}|\mu(B,i,j)=\zeta\}/G_{\mathbf{v}}.

We denote by
[(B,i,j)]
the
G_{\mathbf{v}}
-orbit of
(B,i,j)
considered as a point on
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}
.

Depending on the choice of
\zeta
the space
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}
may have singularities. The subset

(2.6)

\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}^{reg}=\{(B,i,j)\in\mu^{-1}(-\zeta)|\text{ the stabilizer of }(B,i,j)\text{ in }G_{\mathbf{v}}\text{ is trivial}\}/G_{\mathbf{v}}

is a generally incomplete nonsingular hyperkäler manifold with dimension:

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{M_{\zeta}^{reg}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}M-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}G_{v}=2^{t}\mathbf{v}(2\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{Cv}),

where
C=2I-A
is the generalized Cartan matrix. The natural symplectic form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
descends to define a symplectic form on
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}^{reg}
which we also denote by
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
. Now, let:

\begin{split}R_{+}&=\{\theta=(\theta_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}|^{t}\theta\mathbf{C}\theta\leq 2\}\backslash\{0\}\\
R_{+}(\mathbf{v})&=\{\theta\in R_{+}|\theta_{k}\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}V_{k}\text{ for every }k\}\\
D_{\theta}&=\{x=(x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sum\limits_{k}x_{k}\theta_{k}=0\}.\end{split}

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem
2.8
in
12
)
.

Suppose

(2.7)

\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\bigcup\limits_{\theta\in R_{+}(\mathbf{v})}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus D_{\theta}

then the regular locus
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}^{reg}
coincides with
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}
thus making
\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}
a complete hyperkähler manifold.

Definition 2.4
.

We call
\zeta

generic
if it satisfies condition (
2.7
).

In order to define the Hodge and the Białynicki-Birula slice below we need to also define the Nakajima quiver variety as a GIT quotient by the complexified gauge group
G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Fix
\zeta=(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})
. The set of stable points is defined as:

H_{\zeta}^{s}=\{m\in\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})~|\text{ the }G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}\text{-orbit through }m\text{ intersects the set }\mu_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})\}.

Theorem 2.6

(Theorem
3.2
in
12
)
.

Let
\zeta=(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})
generic, then:

i) we have a homeomorphism

\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}\cong H_{\zeta}^{s}/G_{v}^{\mathbb{C}},

ii)
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(-\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})\backslash H_{\zeta}^{s}
is a complex subvariety of
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}(-\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}).

2.2.
The conformal limit for Higgs bundles

In order to discuss the conformal limit we need to introduce the notions of Higgs bundle and
\lambda
-connection. For this we need to fix a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus
g\geq 2
equipped with a complex vector bundle
E
of rank
n
and degree zero.

Definition 2.7
.

A
Higgs bundle
is a pair
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
consisting of a Dolbeault operator
\bar{\partial}_{E}
which induces a holomorphic structure on
E
and a
1
-form
\Phi\in H^{0}(\Sigma,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes K)
called the
Higgs field
satisfying the compatibility condition

\bar{\partial}_{E}\Phi=0

i.e., the Higgs field is required to be holomorphic. Here
K
denotes the canonical line bundle over
\Sigma
.

Definition 2.8
.

A Higgs bundle
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is

•

stable
if for every non-zero proper
\Phi
-invariant holomorphic subbundle
F\subseteq E
, we have:

(2.8)

\frac{\deg(F)}{\operatorname{rk}(F)}<\frac{\deg(E)}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}.

•

poly-stable
if it is a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{ps}
denote the set of poly-stable Higgs bundles. The complex gauge group
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{C}}=\Gamma(\Sigma,\operatorname{GL}(E))
acts naturally on
\mathcal{H}^{ps}
:

g\cdot(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi):=(g^{-1}\circ\bar{\partial}_{E}\circ g,g^{-1}\circ\Phi\circ g).

We define the moduli space of Higgs bundles as equivalence classes of polystable Higgs bundles under the gauge group action
M_{H}=\mathcal{H}^{ps}/\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

The moduli of Higgs bundles
M_{H}
fits into a larger family of objects known as
\lambda
-connections which we now define.

Definition 2.9
.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, a
\lambda
-connection
on
E
is a triple
(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})
where
\bar{\partial}_{E}
is a Dolbeault operator on
E
and
\nabla_{\lambda}:\Omega^{0}(E)\rightarrow\Omega^{1,0}(E)
is a differential operator, subject to:

(i)

a
\lambda
-twisted Leibniz rule:

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
s\in\Omega^{0}(E)
:

\nabla_{\lambda}(fs)=\lambda\partial f\otimes s+f\nabla_{\lambda}(s),

(ii)

a holomorphicity condition
[\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda}]=0
.

In order to obtain a geometrically well behaved moduli space we restrict ourselves to
\lambda
-connections satisfying a stability condition similar to definition
2.7
:

Definition 2.10
.

A
\lambda
-connection
(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})
is

•

stable
if for any
\nabla_{\lambda}
-invariant subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
it holds that
\deg\mathcal{F}<0
,

•

polystable
if it is a direct sum of stable
\lambda
-connections.

We denote the space of polystable
\lambda
-connections by
\mathcal{M}^{ps}
. It admits an action by the gauge group
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{C}}
given by

g\cdot(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda}):=(\lambda,g^{-1}\circ\bar{\partial}_{E}\circ g,g^{-1}\circ\nabla_{\lambda}\circ g).

Definition 2.11
.

We define the
Hodge moduli space

M_{Hod}:=\mathcal{M}^{ps}/\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The Hodge moduli space admits a natural
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action

(2.9)

\xi\cdot[(\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]:=[(\xi\lambda,\bar{\partial}_{E},\xi\nabla_{\lambda})],

as well as a

Remark 2.12

When

with values in

Note that

Theorem 2.13

(The non-abelian Hodge correspondence)
.

There exists a bijective correspondence between the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{ps}_{H}
of polystable Higgs bundles and the space of equivalence classes of completely reducible complex flat connections on
E

\mathcal{M}_{H}^{ps}\cong M_{dR},

constructed as follows. A Higgs bundle
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is polystable if and only if there exists a hermitian metric
h
on
E
that satisfies the Hitchin equation:

F_{(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)}+[\Phi,\Phi^{\star_{h}}]=0,

where
F_{(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)}
is the curvature of the Chern connection
\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{h}
associated to the pair
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
and
\Phi^{\star_{h}}
is the adjoint of
\Phi
with respect to
h
. We call such a metric
harmonic
. Given such a harmonic metric it turns out that the connection

D=\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{h}+\Phi+\Phi^{\star_{h}}

is a completely reducible flat connection which is irreducible if and only if
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is stable. Conversely, a flat connection is completely reducible if and only if it comes from a polystable Higgs bundle through the above procedure.

We are ready to describe the conformal limit. Let
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a stable Higgs bundle. Act by the

-action to obtain the stable Higgs bundle

for any

The equivalence class

is part of the real twistor line in

passing through
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\phi)

\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action to obtain

The term on the right represents a particular element in
M_{\operatorname{dR}}

\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}+\xi^{-1}R\Phi+\xi R\Phi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}

is flat. Let
\hbar:=R^{-1}\xi
and write

The limiting flat connection

Here,

The

for

. Then it holds that

(Theorem 1.1 in

)
.

W^{i}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})\in\pi^{-1}(i)
are holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds. The

Arguably the biggest novelty in

Let
(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
a stable Higgs bundle with Chern connection
\bar{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}

where

and

. The authors in

start by looking at the deformation complex at a stable Higgs bundle:

(2.11)

C(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi):\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\xrightarrow{D^{\prime\prime}}\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\xrightarrow{D^{\prime\prime}}\Omega^{1,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E)).

Since the operators satisfy the Kähler identities:

(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}=-i[\Lambda,D^{\prime}],(D^{\prime})^{*}=i[\Lambda,D^{\prime\prime}]

we can conclude that
\ker(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}=\ker(D^{\prime})

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi):=\{(\beta,\phi)\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))|&\\

The right arrow in (

) is given by the linearization of the Higgs bundle equation
\bar{\partial}_{E}\Phi=0
. In order to define the

one replaces the linearization with the actual Higgs bundle equation:

.

The Hodge slice is given by:

(2.12)

S_{(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)}:=\{(\beta,\phi)\in\Omega^{(0,1)}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{(1,0)}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))|D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\Phi)+[\beta,\Phi]=0,D^{\prime}(\beta,\Phi)=0\}.

is a subvariety of
\mathcal{H}^{ps}
as opposed to
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\bar{\partial}_{E},\Phi)

\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-fixed point the induced

-action on

introduces a grading compatible with the mapping properties of

and

(\beta,\phi)
which are in the positive and non-negative pieces of the grading respectively and thus define

The Białinicky-Birula slice is given by:

(2.13)

\begin{split}S^{+}_{(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})}:=\{(\beta,\phi)\in\Omega^{0,1}(N^{+})\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(L\oplus N^{+})~|~D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\phi)+[\beta,\phi]=0,D^{\prime}(\beta,\phi)=0\}.\end{split}

by the conformal limit map.

4.
The Białynicki-Birula slice

The other main ingredient in the proof of existence of the conformal limit for Higgs bundles is the existence of the Białynicki-Birula (BB) slice, a finite dimensional subvariety of
\mathcal{H}^{ps}
diffeomorphic to
W^{0}_{a}(\bar{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
. In order to define the BB slice, we first define a different slice which we call the Hodge slice and then we restrict to a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-fixed point in order to define the BB slice.

4.1.
The Hodge slice

In order to define the Hodge slice we first need to understand the deformation complex at a stable point of a quiver variety. To do this we have to use the definition of the quiver variety through the GIT quotient construction. Recall that this is given by

\mathfrak{M}_{\zeta}\cong H_{\zeta}^{s}/G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}.

Therefore, at a stable point
p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})
the deformation complex is given by

(4.1)

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C}\xlongrightarrow{l_{p}}\mathbb{M}\xlongrightarrow{\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,\star)}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Here,
l_{p}
is the linearization of the gauge group action

(4.2)

g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})=(g_{in(h)}B_{h}g_{out(h)}^{-1},g_{k}i_{k}g_{out(h)B_{\bar{h}}}g_{in(h)}^{-1},j_{k}g_{k}^{-1}),

and writing
g_{k}=e^{t\xi_{k}}
, it takes the form:

(4.3)

l_{p}(\xi):=\frac{\,d}{\,dt}\Bigg|_{t=0}e^{t\xi}\cdot p=(\xi_{\text{in}(h)}B_{h}-B_{h}\xi_{\text{out}(h)},\xi_{k}i_{k},\xi_{\text{out}(h)}B_{\bar{h}}-B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{\text{in}(h)},-j_{k}\xi_{k}).

In order to obtain a representation of the tangent space at
[p]
it is most convenient to work with the adjoint
l_{p}^{\star}
of
l_{p}
. Since the explicit form of the adjoint will be essential later on we give a careful derivation of its particular form. The pairing of
l_{p}(\xi)
with an element
q=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})
of the quiver representation space
\mathbb{M}
is given by:

(4.4)

\langle l_{p}(\xi),q\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}((\xi_{\text{in}(h)}B_{h}-B_{h}\xi_{\text{out}(h)})A^{\dagger}_{h}+(\xi_{\text{out}(h)}B_{\bar{h}}-B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{\text{in}(h)})A^{\dagger}_{\bar{h}})+\sum\limits_{k}\xi_{k}i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\xi_{k}\right).

To calculate the adjoint
l_{p}^{*}(\xi)
we expand both sides of the definition

(4.5)

\langle l_{p}(\xi),q\rangle=\langle\xi,l_{p}^{*}(q)\rangle.

Using equation (
4.4
) we have:

(4.6)

\begin{split}\langle l_{p}(\xi),q\rangle=&\operatorname{Tr}(\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}(\xi_{in(h)}B_{h}-B_{h}\xi_{out(h)})A_{h}^{\dagger})+\\
+&\operatorname{Tr}(\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}(\xi_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}-B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{in(h)})A_{\bar{h}})+\operatorname{Tr}(\sum\limits_{k}\xi_{k}i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\xi_{k})=\\
=&\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}[\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{in(h)}B_{h}A_{h}^{\dagger})-\operatorname{Tr}(B_{h}\xi_{out(h)}A_{h}^{\dagger})+\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger})-\\
-&\operatorname{Tr}(B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{in(h)}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger})]+\operatorname{Tr}(\sum\limits_{k}\xi_{k}i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\xi_{k}).\end{split}

Observing that

\begin{split}\operatorname{Tr}(B_{h}\xi_{out(h)}A_{h}^{\dagger})&=\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{out(h)}A_{h}^{\dagger}B_{h})\\
\operatorname{Tr}(B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{in(h)}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger})&=\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{in(h)}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}})\\
\operatorname{Tr}(J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\xi_{k})&=\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{k}J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}),\end{split}

we can rewrite equation (
4.6
) as:

\begin{split}\langle l_{p}(\xi),q\rangle=&\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}\left(\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{in(h)}B_{h}A_{h}^{\dagger})-\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{out(h)}A_{h}^{\dagger}B_{h})\right)+\sum\limits_{h\in\Omega}\left(\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger})-\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{in(h)}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}})\right)+\\
+&\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sum\limits_{k}\xi_{k}(i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k})\right)=\\
=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k=in(h)\\
h\in\Omega\}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{k}(B_{h}A_{h}^{\dagger}-A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}))+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k=out(h)\\
h\in\Omega\}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Tr}(\xi_{k}(B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}-A_{h}^{\dagger}B_{h}))+\\
+&\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sum\limits_{k}\xi_{k}(i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k})\right)=\\
=&\sum\limits_{k}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\xi_{k}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k=in(h)\\
h\in\Omega\}\end{subarray}}B_{h}A_{h}^{\dagger}-A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k=out(h)\\
h\in\Omega\}\end{subarray}}B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}-A_{h}^{\dagger}B_{h}+i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\right)\right).\end{split}

Define

M_{k}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=in(h)\\
h\in\Omega\end{subarray}}B_{h}A_{h}^{\dagger}-A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=out(h)\\
h\in\Omega\end{subarray}}B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}-A_{h}^{\dagger}B_{h}+i_{k}I_{k}^{\dagger}-J_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}.

Using this, (
4.5
) can be rewritten as:

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k}\operatorname{Tr}\xi_{k}M_{k}&=\sum\limits_{k}\operatorname{Tr}\xi_{k}(l_{p}^{*}(q))_{k}^{\dagger}\Rightarrow\\
M_{k}&=(l_{p}^{*}(q))_{k}^{\dagger}\Rightarrow\\
l_{p}^{*}(q)=M_{k}^{\dagger}&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=in(h)\\
h\in\Omega\end{subarray}}A_{h}B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=out(h)\\
h\in\Omega\end{subarray}}A_{\bar{h}}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}-B_{h}^{\dagger}A_{h}+I_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}-j_{k}^{\dagger}J_{k}=\\
&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=in(h)\\
h\in\Omega\end{subarray}}A_{h}B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}A_{\bar{h}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=in(h)\\
h\in\bar{\Omega}\end{subarray}}A_{h}B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}A_{\bar{h}}+I_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}-j_{k}^{\dagger}J_{k}=\\
&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in H\\
k=in(h)\end{subarray}}A_{h}B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}A_{\bar{h}}+I_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}-j_{k}^{\dagger}J_{k}.\end{split}

We are now in position to define the tangent space of
\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
at
[p]
. It is given by

(4.7)

T_{[p]}\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{R},0)}=\{q\in\mathbb{M}~|~\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,q)=0\text{ and }l^{\star}_{p}(q)=0\}.

Following the same intuition as in
3
we define the
Hodge slice
by changing the linearized equation
\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,q)=0
with the original quadratic in
q
complex moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p+q)=0
:

Definition 4.1
.

Given
p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})\in\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
the Hodge slice for Nakajima quiver varieties is given by:

(4.8)

S_{p}=\{q=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in\mathbb{M}~|~\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p+q)=0,l_{p}^{*}(q)=0\}.

Proposition 4.2
.

The map
P
defined by
q\to[p+q]
is a homeomorphism from a neighborhood of the origin in
S_{p}
to a neighborhood of
[p]\in\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
.

Proof.

Consider the following map

P:S_{p}\to T_{[p]}\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{R},0)}

given by

P(q)=q+(\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}})^{\star}G(-\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,q))

where
G
is the inverse of
\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,*)\circ(\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,*))^{\star}
defined on the image
\mathrm{Im}(\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p,*))
. It is rather straightforward to show that it is invertible and that
\,d\mu(p,P(q))=0
. Therefore this map is a diffeomorphism from
S_{p}
to
T_{[p]}\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{R},0)}
and composing it with the exponential map gives the required local diffeomorphism.
∎

4.2.
Białynicki-Birula slice

Finally, we are ready to define the Białynicki-Birula slice. Let
p_{0}=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0})
be an element of the quiver representation space
\mathbb{M}
, which is a representative of a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. This in particular implies that for all
t\in S^{1}
there exists a gauge transformation
g_{t}\in G_{\mathbf{v}}
such that

(4.9)

g_{t}\cdot(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},tB_{\bar{h}}^{0},tj_{k}^{0})=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0}).

Clearly
g_{t}
depends on the fixed point but we choose to suppress this dependence in the notation. The family
g_{t}
acts on the vector spaces
V_{k}
and decomposes them into direct sums of eigenspaces

(4.10)

V_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{m}V_{k}^{m}.

This decomposition induces decompositions of
\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}
and
\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}
:

\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=-a}^{b+1}\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{i}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{j=-b}^{a+1}\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{j}

for some positive integers
a,b
where
S^{1}
acts for example on
\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{i}
as multiplication by
t^{i}
. The particular summation endpoints come from Theorem
3.4

(\text{iii})

3.4
that the symplectic form transforms as
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}\to t\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
under the
S^{1}
-action and because the symplectic form is non-degenerate.
g_{t}
also acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
by conjugation giving a similar decomposition as above:

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\bigoplus\limits_{-c}^{c}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{c}.

For all graded objects
G=\bigoplus\limits_{m}G^{m}
let us fix the notation
G^{(d)}:=\bigoplus\limits_{d\leq i}G^{i}
. Observe also that
p_{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{-1}
. Therefore for
p_{0}
we get a refinement for the Hodge slice coming from the following diagram

(4.11)

(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{(1)}\xlongrightarrow{l_{p_{0}}(*)}\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{(0)}\xlongrightarrow{\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(P+*)}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{(0)}.

Definition 4.3
.

We define the Białynicki-Birula slice

(4.12)

S^{+}_{p_{0}}:=\{q=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{(0)}~|~\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}+q)=0\text{ and }l^{\star}_{p_{0}}(q)=0\}.

Proposition 4.4
.

S^{+}_{p_{0}}
is half the dimension of
\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
.

Proof.

This is a combination of the following observations. First, the Kuranishi map of Proposition
4.2
restricted to
S^{+}_{p_{0}}
provides an in fact global homeomorphism to

(4.13)

T_{p_{0}}^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}:=\{q=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{(0)}~|~\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0},q)=0\text{ and }l^{\star}_{p_{0}}(q)=0\}.

The local part is trivial and the global follows from the fact that both spaces scale in the same way when acted by the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action and the Kuranishi map is
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-equivariant. Second, stability of the point
p_{0}
implies that the maps
\,d\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0},q)
and
l^{\star}_{p_{0}}(*)
are surjective. For the second one this can be checked using the explicit formula we derived in the previous subsection and the first is similar. Third, by duality,
\operatorname{dim}((\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{(1)})+\operatorname{dim}((\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{(0)})=\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbb{C})=2\operatorname{dim}G_{\mathbf{v}}
. Fourth, the dimension of
\mathbb{M}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{M}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{(0)}
is half that of
\mathbb{M}
. This follows since the former space is isotropic with respect to
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
and adding anything to it will by duality pair non-trivially with an element in the space.
∎

We close this subsection with a lemma explaining exactly how the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action acts on the BB slice. This will be useful later on.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}
. Then
A_{R}:=g_{R}\cdot(A_{h},I_{k},RA_{\bar{h}},RJ_{k})\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}.

Proof.

We start from the complex moment map. Recall that
g_{R}\cdot(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},RB_{\bar{h}}^{0},Rj_{k}^{0})=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0}).
Therefore,

\begin{split}p_{0}+A_{R}&=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0})+g_{R}\cdot(A_{h},I_{k},RA_{\bar{h}},RJ_{k})=\\
&=g_{R}\cdot(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},RB_{\bar{h}}^{0},Rj_{k}^{0})+g_{R}\cdot(A_{h},I_{k},RA_{\bar{h}},RJ_{k})=\\
&=g_{R}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})).\end{split}

Since the complex moment map is invariant under complex gauge transformation and equivariant with respect to the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action, we have that:

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}+A_{R})&=\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(g_{R}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})))=\\
&=\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k}))=\\
&=R\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}},j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})=0,\end{split}

since
(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}
. Therefore
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}+A_{R})=0.

We proceed with the real moment map. We want to prove that:

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}&g_{R}^{in(h)}A_{h}(g_{R}^{out(h)})^{-1}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}(Rg_{R}^{out(h)}A_{\bar{h}}(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}+\\
&+g_{R}^{k}I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}RJ_{k}(g_{R}^{k})^{-1}=0\end{split}

Using the identification
g_{R}^{in(h)}=g_{R}^{k}
we can write:

(4.14)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}&g_{R}^{in(h)}A_{h}(g_{R}^{out(h)})^{-1}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}(Rg_{R}^{out(h)}A_{\bar{h}}(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}+\\
+&g_{R}^{k}I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}RJ_{k}(g_{R}^{k})^{-1}=\\
=&g_{R}^{k}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}A_{h}(g_{R}^{out(h)})^{-1}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{in(h)}-R(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{out(h)}A_{\bar{h}}+\\
+&I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{k}-R(g_{R}^{k})^{-1}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}J_{k}\bigg)(g_{R}^{k})^{-1}.\end{split}

But now we can use the following equalities:

(4.15)

(g_{R}^{out(h)})^{-1}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{in(h)}=\big(g_{R}^{in(h)}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}(g_{R}^{out(h)})^{-1}\big)^{\dagger}=(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},

(4.16)

\begin{split}R(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{out(h)}&=R(g_{R}^{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1})^{\dagger}=R(R^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}=(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},\end{split}

(4.17)

\begin{split}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}g_{R}^{k}=(g_{R}^{k}i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}=(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\end{split}

(4.18)

\begin{split}R(g_{R}^{k})^{-1}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}=R(j_{k}^{0}(g_{R}^{k})^{-1})^{\dagger}=R(R^{-1}j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}=(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}.\end{split}

Plugging the equations (
4.15
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) in (
4.14
) we obtain:

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in H\\
k=in(h)\end{subarray}}A_{h}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}A_{\bar{h}}+I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}J_{k}=l_{p_{0}}^{*}(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})=0

since
(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}.

∎

4.3.
A technical lemma

The aim of this subsection is to show that the BB slice
S^{+}_{p_{0}}
and the holomorphic Lagrangian
W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
can be related by a complex gauge transformation and obtain a quantitative estimate on the form of this transformation for points
A_{R}
with
R
small.

Let us start with a comment. While the group action by
G_{\mathbf{v}}
preserves both the real and the complex moment map i.e.

(4.19)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))&=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\\
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))&=\zeta_{\mathbb{C}},\end{split}

For every
g\in G_{\mathbf{v}}
, the group action by
G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}
only preserves the complex moment map. Therefore when we want to find a solution to both moment maps it is customary to start with a pair satisfying the complex moment map and then search for a complex gauge transformation
g
for which

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

This observation has been explored to great length in order to find solutions to the so called equations carrying a Hermitian Yang–Mills structure. It is also the underlying reason why the Hodge and BB slices are comprized of points which already satisfy the complex moment map.
Before we state the main lemma of this subsection, we show how, assuming we have a point which satisfies the complex moment map and almost satisfies the real moment map, to apply the inverse function theorem in order to get an exact solution. Specifically, let
p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})\in\mathbb{M}
such that

(4.20)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})&=\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}\\
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})&=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}+\text{small error}.\end{split}

The goal is to find a complex gauge transformation
g\in G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(4.21)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

More explicitly:

(4.22)

\begin{split}\frac{i}{2}&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}g_{in(h)}B_{h}g_{out(h)}^{-1}(g_{out(h)}^{-1})^{\dagger}B_{h}^{\dagger}g_{in(h)}^{\dagger}-(g_{in(h)}^{-1})^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}g_{out(h)}^{\dagger}g_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}g_{in(h)}^{-1}+\\
&g_{k}i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}g_{k}^{\dagger}-(g_{k}^{-1})^{\dagger}j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}g_{k}^{-1}=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}^{k}.\end{split}

If we conjugate this equality with
g_{in(h)}=g_{k}
then since
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
is in the center,
g_{k}^{-1}\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}^{k}g_{k}=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
and the above equality can be rewritten as

(4.23)

\begin{split}\frac{i}{2}&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}B_{h}g_{out(h)}^{-1}(g_{out(h)}^{-1})^{\dagger}B_{h}^{\dagger}g_{in(h)}^{\dagger}g_{in(h)}-g_{in(h)}^{-1}(g_{in(h)}^{-1})^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}g_{out(h)}^{\dagger}g_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}+\\
&i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}g_{k}^{\dagger}g_{k}-g_{k}^{-1}(g_{k}^{-1})^{\dagger}j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}^{k}.\end{split}

Using the notation
h:=(g_{1}^{\dagger}g_{1},...,g_{n}^{\dagger}g_{n})=(h_{1},...,h_{n})
we can further rewrite the above equation as

(4.24)

\frac{i}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}B_{h}h_{out(h)}^{-1}B_{h}^{\dagger}h_{in(h)}-h_{in(h)}^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}h_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}+i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}h_{k}-h_{k}^{-1}j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}^{k}.

Now,
h_{k}
are hermitian matrices and as such, we can write them as
h_{k}=e^{2\xi_{k}}
where
\xi_{k}\in i\mathfrak{g}_{k}
so that
\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n})\in i\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
. Therefore, we are looking for a
\xi
which solves the equation

(4.25)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\xi}\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

To find such
\xi
we want to apply the inverse function theorem to the non-linear map

(4.26)

i\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}\ni\xi\xrightarrow{F}-2i\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\xi}\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}))\in i\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}.

We have added the
-2i
to cancel the
i/2
in front of the real moment map so as to make the map go into
i\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
. Assuming that the original error is sufficiently small, what we need is to show that the linearization of the map
F
at
\xi=0
is bijective. The linearization of
F
has the following explicit form:

(4.27)

\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi)_{k}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}B_{h}(B_{h}^{\dagger}\xi_{in(h)}-\xi_{out(h)}B_{h}^{\dagger})-(B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}\xi_{out(h)}-\xi_{in(h)}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger})B_{\bar{h}}+i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}\xi_{k}+\xi_{k}j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}.

It is easy to check that this map is self-adjoint which means that in order to show that it is bijective it is enough to show that it is injective. Assume that it is not. This means that there exists a
\xi
such that
\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi)_{k}=0
, for all
k
. We take the inner product of this quantity against
\xi
itself:

(4.28)

\langle\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi),\xi\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\sum\limits_{k}(\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi)^{\dagger}_{k}\xi_{k}\rangle.

If we can show this is positive then we get a contradiction. Let us make an observation.

(4.29)

\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi)^{\dagger}_{k}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}(\xi_{in(h)}B_{h}-B_{h}\xi_{out(h)})B_{h}^{\dagger}-B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}(\xi_{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}-B_{\bar{h}}\xi_{in(h)})+\xi_{k}i_{k}i_{k}^{\dagger}+j_{k}^{\dagger}j_{k}\xi_{k}

which from Section
3
is exactly
l^{\star}_{p}(l_{p}(\xi))
with
p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})
. Therefore,

(4.30)

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi),\xi\rangle&=\langle l^{\star}_{p}(l_{p}(\xi)),\xi\rangle\\
&=\langle l_{p}(\xi),l_{p}(\xi)\rangle\\
&=\|l_{p}(\xi)\|^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore
\mathcal{L}((B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k}),\xi)_{k}=0
implies
l_{p}(\xi)=0
. But we have assumed that
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
is generic which implies that the map
l_{p}(*)
is injective and therefore
\xi
must be zero. Therefore we have arrived at a contradiction so the map is indeed bijective and therefore if the error is small enough, the inverse function theorem immediately produces a small
\xi
for which (
4.25
) holds.

While this is the main idea of how to construct solutions, in order to prove that the conformal limit converges, we need to prove a more quantitative estimate for
\xi
in terms of the error in our set up. First we fix some notation. Let

(4.31)

\operatorname{End}(V_{k})=\bigoplus\limits_{m}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}

where

(4.32)

\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}=\bigoplus\limits_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{k}^{j},V_{k}^{j+m}).

Denote the set of functions which take values in
\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}
and are of order
\mathcal{O}(R^{j})
as
\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{j})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
A=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}
and write
(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})=p_{0}+A_{R}
. Then there exists
R_{A}>0
sufficiently small such that for all
R\leq R_{A}
there exists a
\xi_{R}\in i\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{v}}
which satisfies the real moment map equation

(4.33)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\xi_{R}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}))_{k}=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}^{k}

and

(4.34)

(\xi_{R})_{k}\in\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{0}(R^{2})+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R^{3})+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M+2})

with
M
being the maximal positive integer for which
\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{M}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

We use the ansatz

(4.35)

\xi_{R}=R^{2}\xi_{0}+...+R^{M+2}\xi_{M}

with
\xi_{j}\in\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq j}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}
. We start by plugging in
p_{0}+A_{R}
into the real moment map. The error that this will produce is of the form

(4.36)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{0}+A_{R})&=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}+\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{0}(R^{2})+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R^{3})+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M+2})\\
&=E_{0}^{(1)}+E_{1}^{(1)}+...+E_{M}^{(1)}.\end{split}

The reason there is no linear error term in
R
is because the linear term turns out to be exactly equal to
l_{p_{0}}^{\star}(A_{R})+l_{p_{0}}^{\star}(A_{R})^{\dagger}
. This term is zero since
A
and thus also
A_{R}\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
and therefore
l_{p_{0}}^{\star}(A_{R})=0
by definition. Now, the linearization of the real moment map at
p_{0}+A_{R}
satisfies

(4.37)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(p_{0}+A_{R},\star)=&\mathcal{L}(p_{0},\star)+\\
+&\|\star\|\left(\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq 1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R)+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M})\right).\end{split}

Now comes a crucial observation. Notice that

(4.38)

\mathcal{L}(p_{0},\star):\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq j}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}\to\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq j}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}

due to the particular form of
p_{0}
. Also since
p_{0}
is stable,
\mathcal{L}(p_{0},\star)
is always invertible. Therefore we can find a unique
\xi_{0}
to solve away the
E_{0}^{(1)}
error term in (
4.36
). This means that we find a solution
\mathcal{L}(p_{0},R^{2}\xi_{0})=E_{0}^{(1)}
. This is the first step. Now, plug into the real moment map the point
e^{R^{2}\xi_{0}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R})
. The error produced is going to be of the form

(4.39)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{R^{2}\xi_{0}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}))&=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq 1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R^{3})+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M+2})\\
&=E_{1}^{(2)}+...+E_{M}^{(2)}.\end{split}

The linearization of the moment map satisfies

(4.40)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(e^{R^{2}\xi_{0}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}),\star)=&\mathcal{L}(p_{0},\operatorname{Id},\star)+\\
+&\|\star\|\left(\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq 1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R)+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M})\right).\end{split}

Thanks to (
4.38
) we can solve away the error
E_{1}^{(2)}
meaning finding
\xi_{1}\in\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq 1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})
such that
\mathcal{L}(p_{0},R^{3}\xi_{1})=E_{1}^{(2)}
. This is the second step. Repeating the above procedure we iteratively generate
\xi_{j}
for
j
up to
M-1
. Then it holds that for

e^{R^{M+1}\xi_{M-1}}\cdot...\cdot e^{R^{2}\xi_{0}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R})
the error from the real moment map will be

(4.41)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{R^{M+1}\xi_{M-1}}\cdot...\cdot e^{R^{2}\xi_{0}}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}))=\bigoplus\limits_{|m|\leq M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M+2}).

At this point we choose
R
to be appropriately small so that we can just apply the inverse function theorem since the correction produced will be of the order
\mathcal{O}(R^{M+2})
and therefore will be the last piece from the ansatz
R^{M+2}\xi_{M}
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

For any
A=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
there exists a unique gauge transformation
g_{A}
such that
g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A)\in W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
. Conversely, for any point in
W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
there exists a unique gauge transformation which takes it to an element
p_{0}+A
with
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
.

Proof.

We already know this when the norm of
A
is small by Lemma
4.6
above. Therefore assume that we know that
[p_{0}+A]\in W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
. Now by Lemma
3.4
and the definition of
W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
we know that this implies that
(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k}))
is also in the complex gauge orbit of a point in
W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
. This implies that there exists a unique
g^{1}_{A}
such that

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g^{1}_{A}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

But then for
g_{A}=g^{1}_{A}g^{-1}_{R}
it holds that

\begin{split}\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}&=\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g^{1}_{A}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k},R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}}),R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})))\\
&=\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R})).\end{split}

Therefore, once the theorem hods for small
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
we can use the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action to scale it to any
A
.
∎

5.
Conformal limit for quiver varieties

We are finally in position to prove that the conformal limit exists. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p_{0}=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0})
a fixed point and
A=(A_{h},I_{k},A_{\bar{h}},J_{k})
an element of
S_{p_{0}}^{+}
. Then the element
p_{A}=(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-{\hbar}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},{\hbar}^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})
of the quiver representation space
\mathbb{M}
satisfies the equation:

(5.1)

\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{A})=-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

Recall that
A\in S_{p_{0}}^{+}
, is equivalent to the conditions:

(5.2)

\begin{split}&l_{p_{0}}^{*}(A)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}A_{h}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})A_{\bar{h}}+I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}J_{k}=0\end{split}

and

(5.3)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}+A)_{k}&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h})(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(i_{k}^{0}+I_{k})(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})\\
&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+B_{h}^{0}A_{\bar{h}}+A_{h}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{h}A_{\bar{h}})+i_{k}^{0}j_{k}^{0}+i_{k}^{0}J_{k}+I_{k}j_{k}^{0}+I_{k}J_{k}=0.\end{split}

Using these identities we calculate the complex moment map:

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0})_{k}=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger})(\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger})+\\
&+(i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})(\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})=\\
=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger})(\hbar^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}A_{\bar{h}}+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger})+\\
&+(i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})(\hbar^{-1}j_{k}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}J_{k}+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})=\\
=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(\hbar^{-1}B_{h}^{0}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}B_{h}^{0}A_{\bar{h}}+B_{h}^{0}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}+\hbar^{-1}A_{h}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}A_{h}A_{\bar{h}}+A_{h}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-\\
&-\hbar\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}-\hbar\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}A_{\bar{h}}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger})+\hbar^{-1}i_{k}^{0}j_{k}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}i_{k}^{0}J_{k}+i_{k}^{0}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}+\\
&+\hbar^{-1}I_{k}j_{k}^{0}+\hbar^{-1}I_{k}J_{k}+I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-\hbar\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}j_{k}^{0}-\hbar\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}J_{k}=\\
=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(A_{h}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}A_{\bar{h}}+I_{k}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}J_{k}+\\
&+\hbar^{-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}B_{\bar{h}}^{o}+B_{h}^{0}A_{\bar{h}}+A_{h}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{h}A_{\bar{h}})+i_{k}^{0}j_{k}^{0}+i_{k}^{0}J_{k}+I_{k}j_{k}^{0}+I_{k}J_{k}+\\
&+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(B_{h}^{0}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger}-(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+i_{k}^{0}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}-(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}j_{k}^{0})+\\
&+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{h\in H\\
k=in(h)\}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(h)(-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger}(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger})-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger})=\\
=&l_{p_{0}}^{*}(A)+\hbar^{-1}\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}+A)+\frac{2}{i}\mu_{R}(p_{0})-\hbar\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0}^{\dagger}).\end{split}

Using the equations (
5.2
),(
5.3
) and the fact that
p_{0}
satisfies the equations
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{0})=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{0})=0
) we obtain:

\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(p_{A})=-2i\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(p_{0})=-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}.

∎

It follows from Lemma
5.1
and the genericity of
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
that there exists a complex gauge transformation
g_{A,\hbar}
such that

g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}
satisfies the real moment map equation:

(5.4)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A})=0

and therefore
g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}\in\mu^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}}(0)\cap\mu^{-1}_{\mathbb{C}}(-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})
.
Now, suppose we start with a point
p\in W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
. From Lemma
4.7
we know that there exists a unique gauge transformation such that
g_{A}^{-1}\cdot p=p_{0}+A
with
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
. Therefore, we might as well start with a
p_{0}+A
with
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
and consider the conformal limit of
g_{A}\cdot(p_{0}+A)
which we denote as
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(p_{0}+A)
.

Recall from (
1.3
) that the first step in defining the family of points whose limit we seek to show exists is to act by the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action through multiplication by
R
. Since we can already assume
R
to be appropriately small, we know from Lemma
4.6
that there exists a gauge transformation
g_{A,R}
such that

(5.5)

\begin{split}h_{A,R}:=&g_{A,R}^{\dagger}g_{A,R}\\
=&\operatorname{Id}+\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{0}(R^{2})+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=1}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})(R^{3})+...+\bigoplus\limits_{|m|=M}\operatorname{End}(V_{k})_{m}(R^{M+2})\end{split}

and which satisfies

(5.6)

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g_{A,R}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}))&=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\\
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(g_{A,R}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R}))&=0.\end{split}

Next, after considering the twistor line for
\xi
and acting through the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action by
\xi^{-1}
we get the family of points

(5.7)

\begin{split}P_{R}:=(&G_{A,R}^{in(h)}B_{h}(G_{A,R}^{out(h)})^{-1}-\hbar R^{2}(G_{A,R}^{in(h),\dagger})^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}(G_{A,R}^{out(h)})^{\dagger},G_{A,R}^{k}i_{k}-\hbar R^{2}((G_{A,R}^{k})^{-1})^{\dagger}j_{k}^{\dagger},\\
&\hbar^{-1}G_{A,R}^{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}(G_{A,R}^{in(h)})^{-1}+((G_{A,R}^{out(h)})^{-1})^{\dagger}B_{h}^{\dagger}(G_{A,R}^{in(h)})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}j_{k}(G_{A,R}^{k})^{-1}+i_{k}^{\dagger}(G_{A,R}^{k})^{\dagger})\end{split}

where we have defined
G_{A,R}=g_{A,R}\cdot g_{R}
so that
g_{A,R}\cdot(p_{0}+A_{R})=G_{A,R}\cdot(B_{h},i_{k},RB_{\bar{h}},Rj_{k})
.
This family of points satisfies the complex moment map equation
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(P_{R})=-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}
by construction. However, it is quite far from satisfying the real moment map equation and therefore we need to modify it before there is any hope of applying the inverse function theorem. Thus we consider the modified family of points

(5.8)

\begin{split}F_{R}:=g_{A,\hbar}\cdot G_{A,R}^{-1}\cdot P_{R}=g_{A,\hbar}\cdot(&B_{h}-\hbar R^{2}H_{A,R,in(h)}^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}^{\dagger}H_{A,R,out(h)},~i_{k}-\hbar R^{2}H_{A,R,k}^{-1}j_{k}^{\dagger},\\
&\hbar^{-1}B_{\bar{h}}+H_{A,R,out(h)}^{-1}B_{h}^{\dagger}H_{A,R,in(h)},~\hbar^{-1}j_{k}+i_{k}^{\dagger}H_{A,R,k}),\end{split}

where
H_{A,R}=g_{R}h_{A,R}g_{R}
. We claim that the limit

(5.9)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{R\to 0}F_{R}=&g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}=\\
&g_{A,\hbar}\cdot(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-{\hbar}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~{\hbar}^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}),\end{split}

and therefore for
R
appropriately small, we will be able to apply the inverse function theorem to correct
F_{R}
to a point
\widetilde{F_{R}}
which actually satisfies both moment map equations and such that

\lim\limits_{R\to 0}\widetilde{F_{R}}=g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}.

That the limit is of the required form follows from the particularly nice properties of the metric
h_{A,R}
which was constructed in Lemma
4.6
. Using these properties it is straightforward to check the above claim. Let us do this for the first term since the rest can be done in essentially the same way. What we show is that

H_{A,R,out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}H_{A,R,in(h)}^{-1}=R^{-2}B^{0}_{\bar{h}}+\mathcal{O}(1)
. In order to do this we have to analyze how

(5.10)

H_{A,R,out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}H_{A,R,in(h)}^{-1}=g_{R}^{out(h)}h_{A,R}^{out(h)}g_{R}^{out(h)}B_{\bar{h}}(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}(h_{A,R}^{in(h)})^{-1}(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}

acts on an element
v\in V_{in(h)}^{j}
. Clearly
h_{A,R}^{-1}
satisfies the same nice bounds (
4.34
) as
h_{A,R}
. We know that
(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}
sends
v
to
R^{-j}v
. We know that the part of
(h_{A,R}^{in(h)})^{-1}
which sends
V_{in(h)}^{j}\to V_{in(h)}^{j\pm n}
for some positive
n
sends
R^{-j}v
to
R^{-j+n+2}v_{1}\in V_{in(h)}^{j\pm n}
with
v_{1}
bounded. Then
(g_{R}^{in(h)})^{-1}
sends
R^{-j+n+2}v_{1}
to
R^{-j+n+2-(j\pm n)}v_{1}=R^{-2j+n+2\mp n}v_{1}
. Now, we know that if
B_{\bar{h}}
sends
V_{in(h)}^{a}\to V_{out(h)}^{b}
then
a\leq b+1
. Therefore, it is enough to study the worst case which is when
b=a-1
. Otherwise the error will be decaying faster in
R
. So assume
B_{\bar{h}}
sends
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n}v_{1}
to
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n}v_{2}\in V_{out(h)}^{j\pm n-1}
with
v_{2}
bounded. We know that
g_{R}^{out(h)}
sends
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n}v_{2}
to
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)}v_{2}
. We know that the part of
h_{A,R}^{out(h)}
which sends
V_{out(h)}^{j\pm n-1}\to V_{out(h)}^{j\pm n-1\pm d}
for some positive integer
d
sends
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)}v_{2}
to
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)+m+2}v_{3}
with
v_{3}\in V_{out(h)}^{j\pm n-1\pm d}
bounded. Finally
g_{R}
sends
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)+m+2}v_{3}
to
R^{-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)+d+2+(j\pm n\pm d-1)}v_{3}
. In total the dependence in
R
is given by

(5.11)

-2j+n+2\mp n+(j\pm n-1)+d+2+(j\pm n\pm d-1)\geq 2.

The only cases not covered by our calculation are when both
n=d=0
in which case we get the term
R^{-2}B_{\bar{h}}^{0}
and a term of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Applying the same analysis on the other terms we obtain

(5.12)

F_{R}=g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}+\mathcal{O}(R^{2}).

Therefore we may apply the inverse function theorem discussed in Section
4.3
to correct this family to a family
\widetilde{F_{R}}\in\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
which converges to
g_{A,\hbar}\cdot p_{A}
. Thus we have proven:

Theorem 5.2
.

Given an arbitrary point
p\in\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
its conformal limit exists. Given that
p\in W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
its
\hbar
-conformal limit is given by

(5.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(p)=g_{A,\hbar}\cdot(&B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-{\hbar}(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\\
&{\hbar}^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}),\end{split}

where
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
is the unique element such that
p_{0}+A
is in the gauge orbit of
p
. Finally, the conformal limit maps
W_{a}^{0}(p_{0})
biholomorphically to
W_{a}^{1}(p_{0})
.

6.
Simpson conjecture

The aim of this last section is to discuss the analog of Simpson’s conjecture in the context of Nakajima quiver varieties and provide some positive partial results towards the conjecture.

6.1.
Away from the nilpotent cone

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following theorem

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p_{0}=(B_{h}^{0},i_{k}^{0},B_{\bar{h}}^{0},j_{k}^{0})
be any
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-fixed point in
\mathfrak{M}_{(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}},0)}
. Given any
A\in S^{+}_{p_{0}}
such that
p_{0}+A
is not nilpotent, the line
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(p_{0}+A_{R})
leaves every compact subset of
\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
as
R\to\infty
.

Before we give a proof, we need some preparation. Given a quiver
\Gamma
with oriented edge set
H
, we augment the set
H
by adding all edges connecting
V_{k}
to
W_{k}
which we denote by
\hat{k}
. We also include all edges connecting
W_{k}
to
V_{k}
and denote them as
\check{k}
. Let
\hat{H}:=H\cup\{\hat{1},...,\hat{n},\check{1},...,\check{n}\}
. A path
\mathfrak{p}
is a sequence of edges
\{h_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
in
\hat{H}
such that
out(h_{i+1})=in(h_{i})
. A closed
\Gamma
-loop
\gamma
is a path such that
h_{i}\in H
for all
i
and
in(h_{m})=out(h_{1})
. An admissible path is a path which starts at some
W_{i}
and ends at some
W_{j}
. Given an admissible path
\mathfrak{p}
starting at
W_{k}
and ending at
W_{l}
and an element
p=(B_{h},i_{k},B_{\bar{h}},j_{k})\in\mathbb{M}
, we can associate to it the matrix
M_{p}(\mathfrak{p}):=j_{m}B_{h_{j-1}}...B_{h_{1}}i_{k}
with
out(h_{1})=k
and
in(h_{j-1})=m
. Similarly for a loop
\gamma
we can associate to it the matrix
M_{p}(\gamma)=B_{h_{m}}...B_{h_{1}}
.

Given this notation, we have the following variant of the Le Bruyn–Procesi theorem
10
,
(
4
, Theorem 10.2.2. and Exercise 10.2.2.)
due to Lusztig:

Theorem 6.2

(Lusztig
11
, Theorem
1.3
)
.

The ring of invariants
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{M}]^{G_{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathbb{C}}}
is generated by the polynomials

•

\varphi(M(\mathfrak{p}))
for an admissible path
\mathfrak{p}
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(W_{k},W_{l})^{\star}
.

•

\operatorname{Tr}(M(\gamma))
for a closed path.

Proof.

First, let us make an observation. The invariants defined in Theorem
6.2
are invariant under complex gauge transformations and thus only depend on the complex gauge orbit of
p
. As such we are free to use any representative from this orbit to calculate them. Now, since we have assumed that
p_{0}+A
is not nilpotent, Theorem
6.2
says that there exists either an admissible path or a loop such that the associated invariant is not zero. Without loss of generality assume it is a loop
\gamma
. Then we know that
\operatorname{Tr}(M_{p}(\gamma))\neq 0
.

Now let us focus on the point

g_{A_{R},\hbar}\cdot p_{A_{R}}

It is complex gauge equivalent to the point

(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-R^{-1}\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-R^{-1}\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}R(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}R(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}).

Thus by renaming
\hbar=\hbar R^{-1}
we need to study the behavior of the point

P_{A,\hbar}:=(B_{h}^{0}+A_{h}-\hbar(B_{\bar{h}}^{0})^{\dagger},i_{k}^{0}+I_{k}-\hbar(j_{k}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(B_{\bar{h}}^{0}+A_{\bar{h}})+(B_{h}^{0})^{\dagger},\hbar^{-1}(j_{k}^{0}+J_{k})+(i_{k}^{0})^{\dagger}).

We consider the operator
M_{P_{A,\hbar}}(\gamma)
. Recall that the orientation
\Omega
for our quiver does not contain any oriented cycles and therefore, at least one of the edges of the loop must lie in
\overline{\Omega}
. This implies that the leading order of
\operatorname{Tr}(M_{P_{A,\hbar}}(\gamma))
in terms of
\hbar
is given by
\hbar^{-M}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{p}(\gamma))
where
M
is the number of edges in
\overline{\Omega}
appearing in the loop
\gamma
. Since
M>0
we see that this invariant of the point
P_{A,\hbar}
becomes unbounded as
\hbar\to 0
and therefore the one parameter family of points
[P_{A,\hbar}]\in\mathfrak{M}_{(0,-2i\zeta_{\mathbb{R}})}
has to escape any compact set. Therefore we are done.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If instead of a loop
\gamma
the nontrivial invariant was given by an admissible path then this path would have to be a multiple of the matrix
j_{k}^{0}+J_{k}
and therefore
M=1
which would again give the required result.

Remark 6.4
.

Notice that these invariants function in exactly the same way as the trace of the holonomy of a complex flat connection, which was the invariant used to prove Simpson’s conjecture in the Higgs bundle case
13
;
5
.
