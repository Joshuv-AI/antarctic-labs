Abstract
. Current post-training methodologies for adapting Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) generally fall into two distinct paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Despite their prevalence, both approaches suffer from significant inherent inefficiencies when applied in isolation. SFT forces the model’s generation along a single, deterministic expert trajectory, often inducing catastrophic forgetting of general multimodal capabilities due to distributional shifts. Conversely, pure RL explores multiple generated trajectories but frequently encounters optimization collapse—a "cold-start" problem where an unaligned model fails to spontaneously sample any domain-valid trajectories in sparse-reward visual tasks. In this paper, we propose Supervised Group Relative Policy Optimization (S-GRPO), a unified post-training framework that seamlessly integrates the definitive guidance of imitation learning into the multi-trajectory exploration of preference optimization. Tailored for direct-generation visual tasks, S-GRPO introduces Conditional Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection (CGI). When a binary verifier detects a complete exploratory failure within a sampled group of trajectories, CGI dynamically injects the verified ground-truth trajectory into the candidate pool. By assigning a deterministic maximal reward to this injected anchor, S-GRPO enforces a strictly positive signal within the group-relative advantage estimation. This mechanism reformulates the supervised learning objective as a high-advantage component of the policy gradient, compelling the model to dynamically balance between exploiting the expert trajectory and exploring novel visual concepts. Theoretical analysis and empirical results demonstrate that S-GRPO gracefully bridges the gap between SFT and RL, drastically accelerates convergence, and achieves superior domain adaptation while flawlessly preserving the base model’s general-purpose capabilities.

In stark contrast, RL algorithms—such as Group Relative Policy Optimization

multiple diverse trajectories

. In specialized visual domains evaluated by strict binary verifiers, the base model struggles to spontaneously generate highly accurate trajectories. When using group-based RL, if all sampled trajectories in a group are incorrect, the reward variance collapses to zero, leading to vanishing gradients and stalled learning. Consequently, practitioners face a fundamental dilemma: SFT limits optimization to a single trajectory causing catastrophic forgetting, while pure RL explores multiple trajectories but suffers from optimization collapse due to reward sparsity.

We argue that the dichotomy between SFT and RL is artificial. Mathematically, SFT can be viewed as an RL process where the singular ground-truth trajectory is exclusively assigned a maximal reward. This perspective suggests that the transition from supervised learning to reinforcement learning should be a continuous spectrum rather than a disjointed sequence of stages. If we could integrate this strong, unambiguous supervision signal directly into the multi-trajectory online exploration phase of RL, we could theoretically synthesize the strengths of both paradigms. By treating the ground-truth trajectory as an active participant in the preference comparison process—an "anchor" with a guaranteed high reward—we can leverage the contrastive nature of policy gradients to seamlessly unify the definitive expert imitation of SFT with the autonomous multi-trajectory self-exploration of RL within a single optimization landscape.

Based on this insight, we propose Supervised Group Relative Policy Optimization (S-GRPO), a unified post-training framework tailored for LVLMs. S-GRPO introduces a dynamic mechanism:
Conditional Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection (CGI)
. During online sampling, if a simple binary verifier detects that the policy has failed to generate any valid trajectories within an exploratory group, S-GRPO conditionally injects the verified ground-truth trajectory into the candidate pool. The relative advantage is then computed against this mixed group. The injected trajectory acts as a high-advantage anchor, guiding the gradient steeply toward the correct multimodal distribution. As the model improves and begins generating correct trajectories autonomously, the CGI mechanism gracefully phases out, allowing training to seamlessly transition from supervised bootstrapping to pure self-exploratory reinforcement learning.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

Unified Post-Training Framework
: We formalize S-GRPO, an algorithm that elegantly integrates the objectives of SFT and preference optimization, eliminating the need to choose between isolated paradigms and mitigating catastrophic forgetting in LVLMs.

•

Mechanism Design
: We propose the Conditional Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection (CGI) strategy, which dynamically solves the exploration cold-start problem in binary-verifier settings by guaranteeing positive learning signals within multi-trajectory updates.

•

Empirical Performance
: We demonstrate that S-GRPO significantly accelerates convergence in sparse-reward visual domains and achieves superior domain-specific performance while preserving broad general capabilities compared to other methods.

This gracefully bridges the SFT and RL paradigms without the overhead of curriculum design, maximizing sample efficiency while preserving the structural integrity and general capabilities of the base LVLM.

3.
Preliminary

3.1.
Problem Formulation

Consider a pre-trained LVLM denoted as
\pi_{\theta_{\text{pre}}}
, which exhibits strong general-purpose multimodal capabilities. We are given a target domain-specific dataset
\mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}=\{(x_{i},y_{gt,i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
x_{i}=(v_{i},q_{i})
represents a multimodal query (an image
v_{i}
paired with a task prompt
q_{i}
) and
y_{gt,i}
is the ground-truth response containing domain-specific knowledge. The objective is to adapt
\pi_{\theta_{\text{pre}}}
into a domain-adapted model
\pi_{\theta}
through a post-training procedure, such that
\pi_{\theta}
achieves high performance on
\mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}
while maximally preserving the general capabilities of the base model. This defines the fundamental challenge in domain-adaptive LVLM alignment: integrating novel domain knowledge without catastrophic forgetting.

3.2.
Standard Paradigms and Their Limitations

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
SFT adapts model parameters
\theta
by maximizing the likelihood of expert demonstrations in the target dataset. Its objective is:

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{\text{SFT}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{gt})\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{target}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{gt}|}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{gt,t}\mid x,y_{gt,<t})\right].

While SFT effectively injects domain-specific knowledge via imitation learning, it forces the model’s generation along a single deterministic trajectory. When target domain data differs substantially from pretraining data, this strict teacher-forcing aligns the model exclusively with the narrow target distribution, leading to catastrophic forgetting of multimodal generalizability.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
To mitigate the forgetting induced by SFT, preference-based RL methods like GRPO attempt to align models via multi-trajectory exploration. Given a query
x
, GRPO samples a group of
G
trajectories
O_{gen}=\{o_{1},o_{2},\dots,o_{G}\}
from the old policy
\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}
. A verifier (reward function/model) assigns a scalar reward
r_{i}
to each
o_{i}
. The advantage estimate
\hat{A}_{i}
is computed relative to the group peers:

(2)

\hat{A}_{i}=\frac{r_{i}-\text{mean}(\{r_{1},\dots,r_{G}\})}{\text{std}(\{r_{1},\dots,r_{G}\})+\epsilon},

where
\epsilon
is a small constant for numerical stability. The GRPO objective maximizes the expected clipped advantage while regularizing against a reference policy
\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}
:

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,O_{gen}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\min\Big(\rho_{i}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i},

\displaystyle\text{clip}\left(\rho_{i}(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon\right)\hat{A}_{i}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle-\beta\mathbb{D}_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}),

where
\rho_{i}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i}|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{i}|x)}
is the importance sampling ratio. Here,
\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}
denotes the behavior policy used for trajectory sampling, while
\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}
is typically a frozen initial policy (e.g., the SFT model) strictly maintained to prevent excessive policy drift via the KL penalty.

3.3.
The Cold-Start Challenge and Optimization Collapse

While GRPO effectively explores multiple trajectories, it heavily relies on the assumption that the unaligned base policy can spontaneously generate at least some correct trajectories within a group to establish a meaningful contrastive gradient.

However, in specialized visual domains evaluated by strict verifiers, the base model suffers from a severe
cold-start problem
. The probability of spontaneously generating a valid response is infinitesimally small. Consequently, all
G
sampled trajectories are likely to fail, resulting in uniformly zero or negative rewards
(e.g.,r_{i}=0\text{ for all }i)
. When this occurs, the group statistics collapse:

\text{mean}(\{r_{i}\})\approx 0,\quad\text{std}(\{r_{i}\})\approx 0

According to Eq. (
2
), this leads to uninformative, near-zero advantage estimates (
\hat{A}_{i}\approx 0
). The contrastive signals vanish, causing an
optimization collapse
where learning completely stalls. This fundamental limitation motivates our proposed unified approach, which actively injects supervisory signals into the RL exploration loop to dynamically break the optimization deadlock.

Methodology

Building upon the limitations identified in the preliminary analysis, we introduce Supervised Group Relative Policy Optimization (S-GRPO). As illustrated in
Figure 2
, S-GRPO elegantly unifies the strengths of both SFT and GRPO into a single on-policy stage, resolving the cold-start challenge without decoupled training phases.

4.1.
Backbone and Reward Formulation

Following
(
45
)
, we focus on non-deep thinking Visual Question Answering (VQA). Therefore, we use GRPON (GRPO for Non-deep thinking models
(
45
)
). In our framework, a rule-based reward function or an external evaluator
R(o,y_{gt})
is utilized to assign scores to the generated trajectories. Specifically, we utilize Qwen3-4B-Instruct
(
37
)
as our reward verifier to conduct semantic consistency evaluation for complex visual tasks.

For integration into S-GRPO, the continuous score returned by the verifier is typically normalized or binarized (e.g.,
r\in\{0,1.0\}
) to provide clear guidance during advantage estimation. This similarity score is then converted into a binary reward using a threshold
\delta
:

(4)

R(o,y;\delta)=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if}~S(o,y)\geq\delta,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

4.2.
Supervised GRPO via Trajectory Injection

To resolve the optimization collapse caused by reward sparsity, S-GRPO actively shapes the advantage estimation landscape by dynamically modifying the composition of the multi-trajectory sampling group.

4.2.1.
Mechanism: Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection

Instead of relying solely on self-generated candidate trajectories, S-GRPO introduces an injection mechanism. For a given training query
x
associated with a known ground-truth expert trajectory
y_{gt}
:

•

Online Exploration
: We sample
G-1
exploratory trajectories from the current policy:
\{o_{1},\dots,o_{G-1}\}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|x)
.

•

Expert Trajectory Injection
: We inject the ground-truth trajectory
y_{gt}
into the set, creating a mixed candidate group. The sample size of
O_{mixed}
is strictly maintained as G to match GRPO’s group scale.

O_{mixed}=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{G-1}\}\cup\{y_{gt}\}

•

Deterministic Reward Assignment
: Crucially, the injected expert trajectory
y_{gt}
is assigned a guaranteed maximal reward
R_{max}=1.0
, acting as an intrinsic reference anchor. The
G-1
generated trajectories are evaluated by the verifier.

4.2.2.
The Unified S-GRPO Formulation

Consider the mixed group
O_{mixed}
in a severe cold-start scenario where all
G-1
exploratory trajectories fail (
r=0
) and the injected expert trajectory succeeds (
r=R_{max}=1.0
). The group statistics intrinsically shift:

\text{mean}=\frac{1}{G},\quad\text{std}=\sqrt{\frac{G-1}{G^{2}}}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}

The injection of a single high-reward anchor mathematically guarantees a non-zero standard deviation (
\text{std}>0
). Consequently, the relative advantages are forcefully segregated:

•

For Generated (Incorrect) Trajectories
o_{gen}
:

\hat{A}_{gen}=\frac{0-(1/G)}{\text{std}}<0\quad\text{(Penalizing self-generated errors)}

•

For the Injected (Expert) Trajectory
y_{gt}
:

\hat{A}_{gt}=\frac{1-(1/G)}{\text{std}}\gg 0\quad\text{(Strongly positive; Imitating reference)}

This stark contrast prevents gradient vanishing. The relative advantages are forcefully segregated:

•

For Generated (Incorrect) Trajectories
o_{gen}
:

(5)

\hat{A}_{gen}=\frac{0-(1/G)}{\text{std}}<0\quad\text{(Penalizing self-generated errors)}

•

For the Injected (Expert) Trajectory
y_{gt}
:

(6)

\hat{A}_{gt}=\frac{1-(1/G)}{\text{std}}\gg 0\quad\text{(Strongly positive; Imitating reference)}

This stark contrast prevents gradient vanishing. The unified S-GRPO objective function is formally optimized over this mixed group:

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{S-GRPO}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,O_{mixed}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\min\Big(\rho_{i}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i},

\displaystyle\text{clip}\left(\rho_{i}(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon\right)\hat{A}_{i}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle-\beta\mathbb{D}_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}),

where all notations and hyperparameters retain their definitions from
Equation 3
. Maximizing this objective naturally increases the log-probability of the expert trajectory (effectively executing an on-policy behavioral cloning step) while actively decreasing the probabilities of self-generated erroneous trajectories.

4.3.
Conditional Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection (CGI)

While baseline injection seamlessly mitigates the cold-start problem, unconditionally injecting the ground-truth trajectory throughout the entire training process may inadvertently reintroduce the strict teacher-forcing bias prevalent in SFT. To balance the exploration-exploitation trade-off, we propose a refined variant:
Conditional Ground-Truth Trajectory Injection (CGI)
.

CGI employs the ground-truth trajectory
y_{gt}
strictly as a dynamic fallback mechanism, triggered exclusively when the policy experiences a complete exploratory failure. For a query
x
, the process is as follows:

(1)

Initial Exploration:
We sample a full group of
G
candidate trajectories strictly from the current policy:
O_{gen}=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{G}\}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|x)
.

(2)

Verification and Failure Detection:
The verifier evaluates all
G
trajectories to assign rewards
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{G}\}
. We define a
group failure
condition as the scenario where all generated trajectories fail to yield a positive reward (e.g.,
\max(\{r_{1},\dots,r_{G}\})=0
).

(3)

Conditional Fallback:

•

Condition Met (Complete Failure):
If triggered, the model is trapped in a cold-start state. We discard one generated trajectory (typically the one with the lowest generation probability) and replace it with
y_{gt}
, forming the mixed group
O_{mixed}
. The policy update proceeds using the unified S-GRPO formulation (
Equation 7
).

•

Condition Not Met (Successful Exploration):
If at least one generated trajectory receives a positive reward from the verifier (
r>0
), the model possesses sufficient capability to learn from its own exploratory variance. No injection occurs, and the policy is updated using the standard GRPO objective (
Equation 3
) over the original set
O_{gen}
.

Unlike GCPO’s unconditional injection (which introduces SFT-like teacher-forcing bias) and RCPA’s curriculum-dependent prefix injection (which requires manual design), S-GRPO’s CGI mechanism dynamically triggers ground-truth injection only when necessary (complete exploration failure). This design achieves a seamless transition from supervised bootstrapping to pure reinforcement learning in a single stage, eliminating external dependencies (e.g., curriculum modules) while balancing exploration and exploitation.

5.
Experiments

In this section, we empirically validate the effectiveness of S-GRPO on Large Vision-Language Models. Our experiments are designed to address three key questions:

(1)
Can S-GRPO successfully train a visual generation policy from a "cold-start" state where a strict binary verifier causes standard RL methods to collapse?

(2)
How does the unified single-stage S-GRPO compare against the prevalent two-stage pipeline (SFT followed by GRPO) in terms of domain-specific performance and general capability retention?

(3)
What is the impact of the Conditional Ground-Truth Injection (CGI) mechanism on learning dynamics?

5.2.
Performance Comparison

Main experimental results are summarized in
Table 1
. S-GRPO delivers superior domain-specific performance while successfully bypassing the alignment tax associated with SFT.

•

FFT Induces Catastrophic Forgetting:
While FFT achieves peak domain scores (e.g., 1.0172 CIDEr on COCO), it severely corrupts the model’s general capabilities, with drastic declines of 8.78% on MMMU, a drop to 735.10 on MME, and over 36% degradation on IFEval-I. This highlights the dangers of pure MLE over-optimization.

•

Failure of RL-based methods:
Standard GRPO completely stalls when applied directly to the base model. Due to the strict binary verifier, the model fails to generate initial rewardable trajectories, yielding a dismal 0.2245 BLEU-1 on COCO. This confirms the necessity of our advantage-anchoring mechanism.

•

S-GRPO Superiority over Two-Stage Pipelines:
S-GRPO achieves comparable performance to or slightly exceeds the sophisticated two-stage (SFT
\rightarrow
GRPO) pipeline in domain metrics (e.g., scoring 1.0106 CIDEr vs 0.9921 on COCO) without requiring a separate, computationally expensive SFT phase. Crucially, because S-GRPO maintains on-policy KL-regularization from the very first step, it preserves general instruction-following capabilities far better than SFT, achieving 0.7387 on IFEval-I.

Consistent trends are observed on Geo170K and OpenI:

Geo170K
: S-GRPO achieves a 2.3008 CIDEr, comparable to FFT’s 2.3109, while significantly outperforming FFT in general understanding (MMMU: 0.5211 vs. 0.4667).

OpenI
: In medical diagnostics, S-GRPO maintains a 0.3376 BLEU-1 and 0.2388 ROUGE-L, while preserving a 0.7102 IFEval-I score, far exceeding FFT’s 0.6367.

5.3.
Ablation Study

Effectiveness of Conditional Injection.
To validate the core innovation of S-GRPO, we ablate the Ground-Truth Injection mechanism on the COCO Caption dataset. As shown in
Table 1
, we compare three settings to demonstrate the impact of our proposed Conditional Ground-Truth Injection (CGI):

(1)

No Injection (Standard GRPO)
: Yields near-zero learning progress due to severe reward sparsity.

(2)

Unconditional Injection (S-GRPO wo CGI)
: The ground-truth trajectory is injected into every single sampling group regardless of the policy’s success. As indicated by the experimental results, while this aggressive injection strategy helps solve the cold-start problem and can even further boost domain-specific performance, it inevitably compromises the model’s general capabilities. The constant presence of the ground truth introduces a strong "teacher-forcing" bias, severely limiting the model’s autonomous exploration.

(3)

Conditional Ground-Truth Injection (CGI - Ours)
: By utilizing the binary verifier to detect group failures and triggering injection strictly as a fallback, CGI achieves the optimal balance. It bootstraps the gradient during early cold-start phases to ensure domain-specific learning, and gracefully steps aside when unnecessary. This allows pure preference optimization to dominate, effectively protecting the model’s general capabilities while maintaining high domain performance.

5.4.
Parameter Study

Impact of the Control Threshold (
\delta
).
The hyperparameter
\delta
regulates the strictness of the Conditional Ground-Truth Injection (CGI) mechanism. As shown in
Table 2
, increasing
\delta
inherently raises the difficulty of achieving autonomous rewards, thereby triggering the ground-truth injection more frequently. This aggressive bootstrapping forces the model to exploit the provided trajectories, which substantially accelerates the acquisition of domain-specific knowledge (e.g., CIDEr improves dramatically from 0.2417 at
\delta=0
to 1.0170 at
\delta=1
). However, this mechanism introduces a clear trade-off. An overly stringent threshold (e.g.,
\delta=1
) induces a persistent "teacher-forcing" effect that suppresses the model’s autonomous exploratory capacity. Consequently, this over-reliance exerts a severe negative impact on the preservation of pre-trained general capabilities, as evidenced by the sharp degradation in MME (dropping to 2225.65) and IFEval-I (dropping to 0.6888). Our empirical results demonstrate that setting
\delta=0.9
achieves the optimal Pareto balance: it yields near-peak domain-specific performance (1.0106 CIDEr) while robustly preserving the model’s structural integrity and general instruction-following abilities.

5.5.
Convergence & Learning Dynamics

Demonstrating stability and computational efficiency,

Providing high-quality anchor trajectories during the cold-start phase smooths the non-convex optimization landscape. This targeted injection breaks the symmetry of zero-reward samples, guaranteeing directional policy gradients when vulnerable to reward sparsity. As capabilities improve, CGI dynamically scales back, enabling a seamless transition from supervised bootstrapping to pure preference optimization without gradient shocks.

Its learning curve suffers a severe "flatline" optimization collapse, remaining trapped in a zero-gradient plateau. While standard GRPO may occasionally exhibit sporadic reward spikes from rare exploratory successes, these noisy signals are overwhelmingly diluted by the vast majority of failed trajectories within the sampling group. Consequently, the contrastive advantage remains uninformative, rendering these fleeting successes insufficient to drive systemic policy updates. Ultimately, the standard RL paradigm wastes computational resources on unrewarded samples, fundamentally failing to accumulate the necessary gradients to assimilate the requisite domain-specific knowledge.

6.
Conclusion

We propose Supervised Group Relative Policy Optimization (S-GRPO), a novel unified framework that harmonizes domain knowledge injection and preference optimization into a single on-policy post-training stage. By introducing the Conditional Ground-Truth Injection (CGI) mechanism alongside a strict binary verifier, S-GRPO effectively resolves the optimization collapse and reward sparsity problems inherent in cold-start RL. Extensive experiments across diverse non-deep-thinking visual tasks demonstrate that S-GRPO achieves domain adaptation competitive with full fine-tuning, while significantly outperforming established two-stage pipelines in preserving the structural integrity and general-purpose capabilities of LVLMs. S-GRPO provides a robust, computationally elegant pathway for scaling specialized multimodal alignment.
