Modular Properties of Symplectic Fermion Generalised Gibbs Ensembles
Faisal Karimi, and Gérard M.T. Watts

Abstract
The symplectic fermion is a much-studied non-unitary conformal field theory with c = − 2 c=-2 and is known to contain an infinite family of mutually commuting conserved charges. We derive expressions for the conserved charges on the cylinder and use these to construct Generalised Gibbs Ensembles (GGEs) in the particular case of the 𝒲 ⁡ ( 1 , 2 ) \mathcal{W}(1,2) triplet model. We derive exact expressions for the modular S S -transforms in each sector of the symplectic fermion (and so of the whole GGE) and further extract the expressions in the asymptotic regime where the chemical potentials go to zero. Subsets of the conserved charges are known to reproduce the KdV and Boussinesq hierarchies. For the case in which the charge is identified with the zero mode W 0 W_{0} of the W 3 W_{3} algebra, we obtain asymptotic behaviour in precise agreement with the conjecture proposed in our companion paper 20 ; for the KdV subset we obtain results which exactly mirror the case for a single free fermion. Finally we identify the GGE with a translation invariant and purely transmitting defect for the symplectic fermion fields, and make some comments on the relation to other W n W_{n} algebras.

More surprisingly, it is also always possible to find infinite sets of charges which mutually commute, since the Virasoro algebra gives rise to the well-known KdV hierarchy of charges (and two further distinct hierarchies). If a theory has an extended symmetry, there can be further hierarchies - the

In this work we focus on the symplectic fermion with

This model has been studied for a long time, and an infinite set of fields of all integer weights greater than 1 have been constructed

Given a set of commuting conserved quantities, it is natural to replace the standard partition function, representing the standard Gibbs ensemble for a system at finite temperature, by a Generalised Gibbs ensemble (GGE) where chemical potentials are introduced for each of the charges.

The standard partition function of a CFT is defined as a trace over the full Hilbert space
\cal H
.
If the system is formulated on a circle of
length
R
, with the Hamiltonian
H
and momentum generator
P
given by the integrals around the cylinder of the conserved energy and momentum densities,
then the partition function is given as

\displaystyle Z=\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}(e^{-\beta H+i\gamma P})\;,

(1)

where
\beta=1/T
and
\gamma
is to accommodate systems with non-zero total momentum.
To calculate this expression, one must map the energy and momentum densities to the plane with the result

\displaystyle H=\frac{2\pi}{R}(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12})\;,\;\;\;\;P=\frac{2\pi}{R}(L_{0}-\bar{L}_{0})\;.

(2)

where
L_{0}
and
\bar{L}_{0}
are elements of the Virasoro algebra. The partition function can then be interpreted as the partition function on a torus with complex structure parametrised by
\tau
,

\displaystyle Z=\Tr_{\cal H}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}-c/24})\;.

(3)

where
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
and

\tau=(\gamma+i\beta)/R
.

1

\beta

u

v=-iu+R

v^{\prime}=v/R

\tau=(\gamma+i\beta)/R

z=\exp{(2\pi iv^{\prime})}=\exp(2\pi u/R)

Given a set of conserved currents
J_{i}
with corresponding charges
Q_{i}
, a simple generalisation of (
3
) is

\displaystyle\Tr_{\cal H}\left(e^{\sum_{i}\mu_{i}Q_{i}}\right)\;,

(4)

where we have incorporated the energy and momentum operators as

Q_{1}=L_{0}=c/24
and
Q_{-1}=\bar{L}_{0}-c/24
and
the function
(
4
) is well-defined for suitable conditions on the
\mu_{i}
.

If the charges commute, then we can interpret (
4
) as a GGE and the constants
\mu_{i}
as chemical potentials.

GGEs are of interest because they play an important role in understanding the thermalisation properties of many body systems with additional conserved quantities, see
30
. They are also related to understanding higher-spin black holes
40
;
35
;
29
.

In 2D CFTs, the partition function is a modular invariant. This raises the natural question: does the GGE have any “nice” modular properties?
The question of the modular properties of thermal correlation functions and GGEs has been of interest for a long time. See
40
;
35
;
29
for the context of higher spin black holes,
43
for an earlier look at insertions of
W_{3}
charges,
49
for KdV thermal correlation functions, and
28
;
27
;
26
;
8
for the large central charge limit of KdV GGEs.

This problem has been studied previously at
c=\frac{1}{2}

23
;
24
;
25
, where exact expressions for the modular transform of GGEs with KdV charges were obtained and interpreted in terms of defect insertions, and it was shown that
the modular transform has the asymptotic expansion as a GGE where the charges are in the same hierarchy – but this is not the general result.
The modular properties of KdV GGEs were investigated more broadly for KdV charges using the Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz in
21
where it was shown that, in general, the modular transform of a GGE is not another GGE. The asymptotic modular transform takes the form (
4
) but for charges
\{Q_{i}\}
which do not all mutually commute.

This is also true for a Boussinesq GGE - in our companion work
20
, we
considered the particular GGE

\displaystyle\Tr_{\cal H}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}-c/24}\,e^{\alpha W_{0}})\;,

(5)

and proposed an expression for the modular transformation of this GGE in the regime
\alpha\to 0
, which was again of the form (
4
) but where the
Q_{i}
do not, in general, all mutually commute (the proposed expression was conjectured in
20
and has now been proven
55
).

In this paper, we
consider the symplectic fermion which has
c=-2
. One can define various theories constructed from the symplectic fermion but we will restrict ourselves to the simplest modular invariant theory, the
\mathcal{W}(1,2)
triplet model
37
.

Earlier evidence from
21
suggested that this may be a special case where the thermal correlators of KdV charges close under modular transformations, which is a pre-requisite for the asymptotic modular transform of the GGE to be itself a GGE of the same hierarchy. We confirm that this is the case at
c=-2
. Furthermore, we show that this is also true of a large set of charges
\{\mathbf{Q}_{n}\}
associated to fields bilinear in the symplectic fermion, where
n\geq 1
is the spin of the charges.
The KdV charges are the subset of these of odd spin,
\{\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}\}
. The full set are the integrals of the chiral (and anti-chiral) fields of the linear
W_{\infty}
algebra identified in
56
.

Since the basis of currents in
56
differs from our normalisation, we
re-derive the expressions for the currents in section
3.1
.
We also need the charges, which are the result of mapping the integrals of the conserved currents on the cylinder to the plane. We do this in two independent ways in section
3.3
.
Since the resulting charges are bilinear in the fermion modes, their action on the Hilbert space is easy to calculate (although they are not all diagonal due to the complicated ground state structure of the triplet model) and it is easy to find the exact expression for the GGE.

Given the expression for the GGE, we are able to find and prove an exact expression for its modular transform, in the style of
23
;
21
.
We show that, as with the free fermions at
c=1/2
, the modular transform of a GGE constructed from these charges has an asymptotic expansion as a GGE of the same set of charges and
show that it can also be expressed as the partition function of the symplectic fermion system in the presence of a purely transmitting defect, as was the case for the free fermion.
We also show that in the particular case of the
W_{0}
charge of the
W_{3}
algebra, this agrees with the expressions in
20
;
55
.

The paper is structured as follows:

Section
2
reviews the Symplectic Fermion model, its representations, and its relation to the triplet model.
Although the triplet model and the symplectic fermion have been covered extensively in the literature, we think it is helpful to review those aspects we need, in particular the untwisted and half-twisted sectors, which are central to our modular analysis, define their (super)characters and give their modular properties.

In Section
3

6

15

In Sections

and
4.1
we define the Generalised Gibbs Ensemble (GGE) of the Symplectic Fermion by inserting charges from the bilinear hierarchy into the partition function and the exact modular
S

.

Section

Finally in section

2
The Symplectic Fermion and Representations

At central charge
c=-2
there exists a rich free field theory known as the Symplectic Fermion
46
. This theory provides a realisation of a wide class of logarithmic CFTs (see, e.g.,
12
). In this work, we restrict attention to the sectors of the Symplectic Fermion with relatively “simple” modular properties, namely the untwisted and half-twisted sectors; these are directly related to the representations of the Triplet Model
36
.

The fundamental fields are fermionic currents
\chi^{\alpha}(z)
(
\alpha=\pm
) with operator product expansions

\chi^{\alpha}(z)\chi^{\beta}(w)\sim\frac{\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}}{(z-w)^{2}},\qquad\epsilon^{+-}=-\epsilon^{-+}=1,\;\epsilon^{++}=\epsilon^{--}=0\;,

(6)

where we define the symbol “
\sim
” to mean that we have dropped terms of
O(1)
.

The fields
\chi^{\pm}
have the mode expansions

\displaystyle\chi^{\pm}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\mp\lambda}\chi^{\pm}_{m}\,z^{-m-1}\;,

(7)

where we have labelled the sectors by
\lambda
where
\lambda=0
is the untwisted sector and
\lambda=1/2
is the half-twisted sector.

The operator product expansions (
6
) imply the mode algebra

\{\chi^{\alpha}_{m},\chi^{\beta}_{n}\}=m\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,\delta_{m+n,0}.

(8)

The fields
\chi^{\pm}(z)
are Virasoro primary fields of weight
1
with respect to modes
L_{m}
of the stress tensor

[L_{m},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]=-n\chi^{\pm}_{m+n}\;,\;\;T(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{m}\,z^{-m-2}\;,\;\;T(z)=\big(\chi^{-}\chi^{+}\big)(z)\;.

(9)

where the brackets denote conformal normal ordering of fields, see
\lx@sectionsign
6.5 of
14
. In particular, in the untwisted sector we have

L_{m}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi^{-}_{m-n}\chi^{+}_{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi^{+}_{-n}\chi^{-}_{m+n}\;,\;\;L_{0}=\chi^{-}_{0}\chi^{+}_{0}+\ldots,

(10)

We can also write an expression for
L_{m}
valid in both sectors, depending on the choice of
r
and
s
,

L_{r+s}=\tfrac{1}{2}r(r+1)\,\delta_{r+s,0}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\big\{\chi^{-}_{s-1-j}\chi^{+}_{r+1+j}-\chi^{+}_{r-j}\chi^{-}_{s+j}\big\}

(11)

The Virasoro algebra has integer moding, so although
r
and
s
need not be integers, one requires that
r+s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The existence of a GGE requires a well-defined partition function, in as much it is an extension of a partition function. As emphasised in
46
;
12
, the symplectic fermion and its many twisted representations can realise infinitely many modular invariants. However, the simplest case, the case which we will focus on, is the modular invariant triplet model. Thus, the representations (more specifically, characters of representations)
of the symplectic fermion algebra (
8
) of principal
interest to us are the irreducible vacuum module
\mathbb{L}_{0}
(to be introduced below)
in which the modes in (
7
) have integer labels, and the irreducible half-twisted module
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}
in which the modes are half-integers. We will explain the connection now between the symplectic fermion and triplet model.

First, the representations of the symplectic fermion. The irreducible vacuum module
\mathbb{L}_{0}
is built on a primary state
|0\rangle

satisfying

\displaystyle\chi^{\pm}_{m}|0\rangle=0\;,\;\;m\geq 0

(12)

and admits the Fock space construction

\mathbb{L}_{0}=\text{span}\left\{\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}\dots\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}\dots\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}|0\rangle\,\,\textbf{;}\,n_{i}>n_{i{+}1},\\
m_{i}>m_{i{+}1},\,n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}{}^{\vphantom{|}}_{>0}\right\}\;.

(13)

The half-twisted module
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}

is built on a primary state
|{-}\tfrac{1}{8}\rangle
of conformal weight
h=-1/8
satisfying

\displaystyle\chi^{\pm}_{m}|{-}\tfrac{1}{8}\rangle=0\;,m>0\;.

(14)

and admits the Fock space construction

\mathbb{L}_{1/2}=\text{span}\left\{\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}\dots\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}\dots\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}|{-}\tfrac{1}{8}\rangle\,\,\textbf{;}\,n_{i}>n_{i{+}1},\\
m_{i}>m_{i{+}1},\,n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\vphantom{!}}_{\geq 0}{+}\tfrac{1}{2}\right\}\;.

(15)

In the untwisted sector, the existence of the zero modes
\chi^{\pm}_{0}
allows one to consider larger, reducible but indecomposable representations, the largest of which we denote
\mathbb{S}_{0}
, containing
\mathbb{L}_{0}
as a submodule. The ground state space of
\mathbb{S}_{0}
is four-dimensional, consisting of the vacuum
|0\rangle
together with its Jordan partner
|\Omega\rangle
satisfying

L_{0}|\Omega\rangle=|0\rangle,

(16)

and the states
|\theta^{\pm}\rangle
generated by the fermion zero-modes,

|\theta^{\pm}\rangle=\chi^{\pm}_{0}|\Omega\rangle\;,\;\;|0\rangle=\chi^{-}_{0}\chi^{+}_{0}|\Omega\rangle=\chi^{-}_{0}|\theta^{+}\rangle=-\chi^{+}_{0}|\theta^{-}\rangle.

(17)

There are several further representations generated by subsets of the states
\{|\theta^{\pm}\rangle\}
, but these are reducible with respect to the symplectic fermion algebra; the representations
\mathbb{L}_{0}
and
\mathbb{S}_{0}
are special in that they are the irreducible representation and the maximal reducible representation respectively. Acting with
non-positive fermion modes on
|\Omega\rangle
generates
\mathbb{S}_{0}
:

\mathbb{S}_{0}=\text{span}\left\{\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}\dots\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}\dots\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}|\Omega\rangle\,\,\textbf{;}\,n_{i}>n_{i{+}1},\\
m_{i}>m_{i{+}1},\,n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}{}^{\vphantom{|}}_{\geq 0}\right\}\;.

(18)

The structure of its ground states is illustrated in Fig.

2.1
The Triplet Algebra

The Triplet algebra,
\mathcal{W}(1,2)
, is defined as the bosonic subalgebra of the symplectic fermion (the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
orbifold). It is generated by the stress tensor and three additional weight 3 fields

W^{\pm}(z)=(\chi^{\pm}\partial\chi^{\pm})(z)\quad,\quad W(z)=\frac{1}{2}((\partial\chi^{-}\chi^{+})-(\chi^{-}\partial\chi^{+})).

(19)

We can decompose the symplectic fermion representations into even and odd subspaces that form representations of the Triplet algebra.

\begin{split}\mathbb{L}_{0}={W}_{0}\oplus{W}_{1}\quad&,\quad\mathbb{L}_{1/2}={W}_{-1/8}\oplus{W}_{3/8}\end{split}

(20)

Here, the modules are labelled by the conformal dimensions of their ground states. The modules
{W}_{h}
is irreducible with respect to the Triplet algebra. The weight
0
and weight
-1/8
modules
{W}_{0}
and
{W}_{-1/8}
have one-dimensional ground state spaces, whereas
{W}_{1}
and
{W}_{3/8}
have two-dimensional ground state spaces.

The Hilbert space of the triplet model was constructed in
36
;
37
and is of the form

{H}_{\text{triplet}}=({W}_{-1/8}\otimes\overline{{W}_{-1/8}})\oplus({W}_{3/8}\otimes\overline{{W}_{3/8}})\oplus{B}\;,

(21)

where
{B}
is an appropriate quotient of the bosonic subspace of
\mathbb{S}_{0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{S}_{0}}
and contains the irreducible triplet vacuum module
{W}_{0}\otimes\overline{{W}_{0}}
. Rather than repeat the construction of
{B}
, we will instead simply give a basis. We start from two bosonic states, which with a slight abuse of notation we call
\{|\Omega\rangle,|0\rangle\}
, and two fermionic states
\{|\psi^{+}\rangle,|\psi^{-}\rangle\}
which are annihilated by all the positive symplectic fermion modes and satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\psi^{\pm}\rangle&=\chi_{0}^{\pm}|\Omega\rangle=\overline{\chi}_{0}^{\pm}|\Omega\rangle\;,\;\;\;\;\\
\chi_{0}^{\alpha}|\psi^{\beta}\rangle&=\overline{\chi}_{0}^{\alpha}|\psi^{\beta}\rangle=\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}|0\rangle\;,\;\;\epsilon^{+-}=-\epsilon^{-+}=1\;,\;\;\epsilon^{++}=\epsilon^{--}=0\;,\\
L_{0}|0\rangle&=L_{0}|\psi^{\pm}\rangle=\overline{L}_{0}|0\rangle=\overline{L}_{0}|\psi^{\pm}\rangle=0\;,\;\;\;\;L_{0}|\Omega\rangle=\overline{L}_{0}|\Omega\rangle=|0\rangle\;.\end{split}

(22)

Note that the states do not factorise into a tensor product of left and right moving modes acting on a single vacuum since
|\psi^{\alpha}\rangle=\chi^{\alpha}_{0}|\Omega\rangle=\bar{\chi}^{\alpha}_{0}|\Omega\rangle
. A basis of
{B}
is then given by

{B}=V^{e}_{|\Omega\rangle}\oplus V^{e}_{|0\rangle}\oplus V^{o}_{|\psi^{+}\rangle}\oplus V^{o}_{|\psi^{-}\rangle}\;,

(23)

where
V^{e/o}_{|v\rangle}
are given by

V^{e/o}_{|v\rangle}=\text{span}\{\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}...\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}...\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-\overline{n}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-{\overline{n}}_{\sigma}}\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{\rho}}|v\rangle,\nu+\mu+\sigma+\rho=\text{even/odd}\},

(24)

and
n_{i}>n_{i{+}1}
,
m_{i}>m_{i{+}1}
,
\bar{n}_{i}>\bar{n}_{i{+}1}
,
\bar{m}_{i}>\bar{m}_{i{+}1}
, and
n_{i},m_{i},\bar{n}_{i},\bar{m}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Let us turn to modular invariance - we give a brief introduction in appendix
A
. We will see that the modular invariant partition function of the triplet model state space (
21
) is expressible in terms of only characters and super-characters of the untwisted and half-twisted symplectic fermion modules
\mathbb{L}_{0}
and
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}
respectively.

We introduce the total fermion number operator,
(-1)^{F}
, which induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading on the relevant modules
\mathbb{L}_{0}
and
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}
and the quotiented space of
\mathbb{S}_{0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{S}_{0}}
.

\begin{split}&\{\chi^{\alpha}_{m},(-1)^{F}\}=\{\overline{\chi}^{\alpha}_{m},(-1)^{F}\}=0,\\
&(-1)^{F}|0\rangle=|0\rangle,\quad(-1)^{F}|-\tfrac{1}{8}\rangle=|-\tfrac{1}{8}\rangle,\quad(-1)^{F}|\Omega\rangle=|\Omega\rangle.\end{split}

(25)

We define traces and super-traces, that is traces with and without a
(-1)^{F}
insertion, in the case of the irreducible symplectic fermion modules,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\lambda,1/2}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}}(\mathcal{O}),\qquad\mathrm{Tr}_{\lambda,0}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}}\big((-1)^{F}\mathcal{O}\big).

(26)

The corresponding characters and super-characters are

\chi_{\lambda,s}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\lambda,s}(q^{L_{0}-c/24})=q^{h(\lambda)+\tfrac{1}{12}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}-\lambda}\big(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n}\big)^{2},

(27)

with
h(0)=0
and
h(1/2)=-1/8
(and in generic twisted sectors
h(\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda-1)/2
). We have also defined
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
.

Using these expressions, we can write the traces over the spaces (
20
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Tr_{{W}_{0}}(q^{L_{0}+1/12)})&=\tfrac{1}{2}(\chi_{0,1/2}+\chi_{0,0})\;,\;\;\Tr_{{W}_{1}}(q^{L_{0}+1/12)})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\chi_{0,1/2}-\chi_{0,0})\;,\;\;\\
\Tr_{{W}_{-1/8}}(q^{L_{0}+1/12)})&=\tfrac{1}{2}(\chi_{1/2,1/2}+\chi_{1/2,0})\;,\;\;\Tr_{{W}_{3/8}}(q^{L_{0}+1/12)})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\chi_{1/2,1/2}-\chi_{1/2,0})\;.\;\;\end{split}

(28)

We find that the contribution to the modular invariant from
({W}_{-1/8}\otimes\overline{{W}_{-1/8}})\oplus({W}_{3/8}\otimes\overline{{W}_{3/8}})
, the irreducible part of the state space (
21
), is

\tfrac{1}{2}\,|\chi_{1/2,1/2}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,|\chi_{1/2,0}|^{2}

(29)

When we consider
{B}
,
L_{0}
and
\bar{L}_{0}
are diagonalisable on
V^{o}_{|\psi^{+}\rangle}\oplus V^{o}_{|\psi^{-}\rangle}
, but on
V^{e}_{|\Omega\rangle}\oplus V^{e}_{|0\rangle}
, they are not. On the basis states

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}...\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}...\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-\overline{n}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-{\overline{n}}_{\sigma}}\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{\rho}}|0\rangle\\
\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}...\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}...\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-\overline{n}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{-}_{-{\overline{n}}_{\sigma}}\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{1}}...\overline{\chi}^{+}_{-\overline{m}_{\rho}}|\Omega\rangle\end{pmatrix}\;,

(30)

with

\displaystyle N=\sum_{i}n_{i}\,{+}\sum_{j}m_{j}\;,\;\;\bar{N}=\sum_{i}\bar{n}_{i}\,{+}\sum_{j}\bar{m}_{j}\;,

(31)

they take the form

\displaystyle L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}N&0\\
1&N\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\;\;\;\bar{L}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{N}&0\\
1&\bar{N}\end{pmatrix}\;.\;\;

(32)

Using
q^{a}=e^{a\log q}
, we get

\displaystyle q^{L_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}q^{N}&0\\
\log(q)\,q^{N}\;\;&q^{N}\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\;\;\;q^{\bar{L}_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}q^{\bar{N}}&0\\
\log(q)\,q^{\bar{N}}\;\;&q^{\bar{N}}\end{pmatrix}\;.

(33)

and the off-diagonal terms do not contribute to any traces.
Hence, using a combination of traces and super-traces in the bulk module
\mathbb{S}_{0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{S}_{0}}
with the relations (
22
), one can see that the contribution of the bulk triplet module
{B}
to the modular invariant is

\Tr_{B}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\overline{q}^{\overline{L}_{0}-c/24})=2\,|\Tr_{\mathbb{L}_{0}}(q^{L_{0}-c/24})|^{2}=2|\chi_{0,1/2}|^{2}.

(34)

We emphasise now that the form of this relation will be unchanged when considering GGEs later. Looking at the expression for charges in the bilinear hierarchy
(
94
), one sees that they commute with
L_{0}
and
\bar{L}_{0}
and furthermore are diagonalisable, and so

\Tr_{B}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\overline{q}^{\overline{L}_{0}-c/24}e^{\alpha\mathbf{Q}_{n}+\alpha^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{n}})=2\Tr_{\mathbb{L}_{0}}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{\alpha\mathbf{Q}_{n}})\times\Tr_{\mathbb{L}_{0}}(\bar{q}^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{{\alpha}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{n}})

(35)

Piecing this all together tells us that the modular invariant partition function of the triplet model is

Z_{\text{triplet}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\tfrac{1}{2}\,|\chi_{1/2,1/2}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,|\chi_{1/2,0}|^{2}+2\,|\chi_{0,1/2}|^{2}.

(36)

This is a well-defined partition function, and will serve as the basis for a GGE. It is clear from the discussion that we need only consider the case of the traces of the modules
\mathbb{L}_{0}
and
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}
.

One can indeed check that (
36
) is a modular invariant. This follows from the modular properties of the characters and super-characters of the irreducible symplectic fermion modules
\mathbb{L}_{0}
and
\mathbb{L}_{1/2}
which we elaborate here.

The action of the generators of the modular group on the modular parameter are
T:\tau\mapsto\tau+1
,
S:\tau\mapsto-1/\tau
. The characters
\chi_{0,1/2},\chi_{1/2,1/2}
and
\chi_{1/2,0}
transform in the three dimensional representation

\displaystyle T:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{0,1/2}(\tau+1)\\
\chi_{1/2,1/2}(\tau+1)\\
\chi_{1/2,0}(\tau+1)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{i\pi}{6}}&0&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{i\pi}{12}}&0\\
0&0&e^{-\frac{i\pi}{12}}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{0,1/2}(\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,1/2}(\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,0}(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\;\;\;

(37)

\displaystyle S:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{0,1/2}(-1/\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,1/2}(-1/\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,0}(-1/\tau)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}~0&~~0~~&\tfrac{1}{2}\\
~0&1&~0~\\
~2&0&~0~\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{0,1/2}(\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,1/2}(\tau)\\
\chi_{1/2,0}(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\;.

(38)

The character
\chi_{0,0}
however transforms as

\displaystyle\chi_{0,0}(\tau+1)=e^{i\pi/6}\chi_{0,0}(\tau)\;,\;\;\chi_{0,0}(-1/\tau)=-i\tau\chi_{0,0}(\tau)\;.

(39)

These are closed as functional equations but to get a proper linear representation of the modular group one must consider in addition the pseudo-trace
g(\tau)=\tau\chi_{0,0}(\tau)

39

\displaystyle T:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{0,0}(\tau+1)\\

(40)

3
Hierarchy of Bilinear Fields and Charges

It has long been known that one can construct an infinite tower of bosonic fields which are bilinear in the symplectic fermions
56
.
Since these fields are bilinear, their zero modes act linearly on the modes of the symplectic fermions and hence the zero modes all mutually commute.

In this paper, we are interested not so much in the zero modes of the bilinear fields, as in the corresponding charges which are the expressions for the integrals of the bilinear fields around a cylinder, which requires us to map them to the complex plane using the method of
38
.
In this section we recall the form of the explicit basis of quasi-primary bilinear fields
and find the explicit expressions for the corresponding charges.

The charges are easily determined up to the addition of a constant, and we show how one can fix this constant in two separate ways.

3.1
Quasi-Primary Bilinear Fields

An infinite set of quasi-primary fields of all integer weights greater than or equal to two was already found at
c=-2
in
56
,
where they were identified as the generators of
the “linear”
W_{\infty}
algebra.
The fields are realised in
56
as a set of quasi-primary fields which are bilinear in the fields of a
bc
ghost system. The weight 1 and weight 0 ghost fields were denoted
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
respectively, but the fields of the
W_{\infty}
algebra are expressed only in terms of
\psi
and
\partial\bar{\psi}
, which can then be identified with the symplectic fermion fields
\chi^{+}
and
\chi^{-}
.
Rewriting the results in
56
for later convenience, we find that the quasi-primary bilinear field of weight
n
is

B_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}A_{n,k}(\partial^{n-2-k}\chi^{-}\partial^{k}\chi^{+})(z),

(42)

where

A_{n,k}=\left[\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\right]^{-1}\binom{n}{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n-2-k)!k!}.

(43)

One can simplify this expression by collecting most of the terms into a total derivative

B_{n}(z)=(\partial^{n-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)\text{ mod }\partial,\quad n=2,3,4,...

(44)

Here,
\text{ mod }\partial
means that we have dropped total derivative terms. One can easily check that
B_{n}(z)
is a quasi-primary field by calculating

L_{1}|B_{n}\rangle=0.

(45)

where
|B_{n}\rangle
is the state associated to the field
B_{n}(z)
. We now turn to the charges associated to the fields
B_{n}
.

3.2
Charges associated to quasi-primary fields

Suppose that
J(w)
is a quasi-primary field with conformal weight
n
. The charge
I
associated to
J(w)
is obtained by integrating the field on a fixed spatial slice of a cylinder.
It will be convenient to take
w=2\pi u/R
so that the cylinder has
circumference
2\pi
and is given by the strip in the complex plane
0\leq\text{Im}(w)<2\pi
with periodic boundary conditions in the imaginary direction.
We then have

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi i}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}J(w).

(46)

We can express
J(w)
in terms of fields on the plane by mapping from the cylinder with coordinate
w
to the complex plane with the map
z=e^{2\pi u/R}=e^{w}
.
The result, using, for example, the technique in
38
, is

J(w)=[z^{L_{0}}\cdot\hat{J}](z),

(47)

where
\hat{J}(z)
is a sum of local fields on the plane with weight up to and including
n
.
It will be important for us later that the leading term in
\hat{J}
is the field
J
itself, all other contributions being of lower weight.
The hat notation is introduced in appendix I of
20
to avoid clutter with many powers of
z
appearing.
The notation for the action
L_{0}\cdot
on a local field in (
47
) is a shorthand for

[L_{0}\cdot\varphi](z)=V(L_{0}|\varphi\rangle,z)\;,

(48)

where we have used the vertex operator notation,
V(|\phi\rangle,z)
, denoting the local field that corresponds to the state
|\phi\rangle
.
Note that
the charge on the cylinder
is given by

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi i}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}J(w)=\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}\,[z^{L_{0}}\cdot\hat{J}](z)=\hat{J}_{0}\;.

(49)

and will amount to being a sum of zero-modes of fields on the plane, including a possible constant term.

3.3
Expressions for the Charges in the Bilinear Hierarchy

We now apply the previous section to the currents
B_{n}
. Our first aim is to find explicit expressions for the associated charges,

\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}=(\hat{B}_{n})_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi i}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}B_{n}(w)\;.

(51)

We will do this in two ways: firstly in section

3.3.1
using means of modular forms, and then in section

3.3.4
by means of zeta-function regularisation.

3.3.1
The Way of Modular Forms

Reviews of modular forms can be found in many places; we will use the conventions of the appendix of
23
, which we repeat in appendix
A
for convenience.

In
20
;
15
, it is argued that if

\{J_{i}\}
are a set of quasi-primary fields of weights
n_{i}
, and the zero modes

\{(\hat{J}_{i})_{0}\}

are mutually commuting, then

\langle{(\hat{J}_{i_{1}})_{0}(\hat{J}_{i_{2}})_{0}...(\hat{J}_{i_{N}})_{0}}\rangle,

(52)

is a quasi-modular form of weight
\sum_{i}n_{i}
and depth
N-1
.

We can use this fact to fix the form of the charges
\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}
as follows. Since we know the explicit form of
B_{n}
, we know both the explicit forms of the leading term of
\hat{B}_{n}
and of
\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}=(\hat{B}_{n})_{0}
and the general form of the sub-leading terms. Requiring the correct modular properties will fix the sub-leading terms uniquely.

The map from
B_{n}(w)
to
\hat{B}_{n}(z)
only uses the action of the Virasoro algebra positive modes on the state
|B_{n}\rangle
and so the result will again be a bilinear field in the symplectic fermion modes (possibly with an additional constant term), but this time a sum over fields of all weights up to
n
, of the form

\hat{B}_{n}(z)=(\partial^{n-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}C^{n}_{k}(\partial^{k-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+C^{n}_{0}+\partial(\ldots).

(53)

We can further restrict this form by considering the symmetry of the symplectic fermion algebra,

\displaystyle\chi^{-}\to\chi^{+}\;,\;\;\chi^{+}\to-\chi^{-}\;.

(54)

The term
\partial^{n}\chi^{-}\chi^{+}+\partial(\ldots)
picks up a sign
(-1)^{n}
under this symmetry, and so

B_{n}(z)\mapsto(-1)^{n}B_{n}(z)
while
the Virasoro algebra is invariant,
so the only terms possible in (
53
) are those with the same parity under the symmetry, and we can refine the answer to

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{2n}(z)

\displaystyle=(\partial^{2n-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}C^{2n}_{2k}(\partial^{2k-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+C^{2n}_{0}+\partial(\ldots)\;,

(55)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{2n+1}(z)

\displaystyle=(\partial^{2n-1}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}C^{2n+1}_{2k+1}(\partial^{2k-1}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})(z)+\partial(\ldots).

(56)

Note that there is no constant term in (
56
).
When we take the zero mode of
\hat{B}_{n}
, we then find

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}=(\hat{B}_{2n})_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}\Big(j^{2n-2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{2n}_{2k}\,j^{2k-2}\Big):\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\;+\;H^{2n}_{0}\;,

(57)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{2n}=(\hat{B}_{2n+1})_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}\Big(j^{2n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{2n+1}_{2k+1}\,j^{2k-1}\Big):\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\;,

(58)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{2}=(\hat{B}_{3})_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\;,

(59)

where
:\cdots:
is normal ordering and we adopt the convention that

:\!\chi^{-}_{0}\chi^{+}_{0}\!\!:\;\;=-:\!\chi^{+}_{0}\chi^{-}_{0}\!\!:\;\;=\chi^{-}_{0}\chi^{+}_{0}.

(60)

We could, in principle, calculate the constants
C^{n}_{k}
and
H^{n}_{k}
directly, but it is much easier to use the modular properties of the
n
-point functions of the charges, as these determine them uniquely.
This argument follows very much the lines in
23
, but with one extra important subtlety.
For odd weight charges,
we know that the torus one-point functions
\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{n}}\rangle
are modular forms and these can be calculated as traces of the symplectic fermion Hilbert space and this fixes the charges uniquely once the leading term is known.
However, the traces

\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{n}}\rangle
vanish identically for even spin charges and we have to consider not only these one-point functions, but the traces
\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{n_{1}}\mathbf{Q}_{n_{2}}}\rangle
as well.

We consider the cases of fields of even and odd weight, with charges of odd and even weight respectively in the next two sections.

3.3.2
Even Weight Fields and Odd Spin Charges

As is argued in, for example,
23
, the torus one point function of zero-modes of quasi-primary fields
are
modular forms. These one-point functions are written in terms of traces over the Hilbert space of the theory which is expressed as traces over the representations

\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}
.

To this end,
we will now introduce a shorthand notation for these traces.
We define

\langle{\,\mathcal{O}\,}\rangle_{\lambda,s}(\tau)=\Tr_{\lambda,s}(\,\mathcal{O}\,q^{L_{0}-c/24})\,\;,

(61)

with

q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\;,\quad\quad\lambda,s\in\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}\;.

(62)

In this notation, the characters are

\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}
.

Following the arguments of section
2.1
, the one point function of a chiral operator
\mathcal{O}
is then given as

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}\,\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{\,\mathcal{O}\,}\rangle_{1/2,1/2}\bar{\chi}_{1/2,1/2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle{\,\mathcal{O}\,}\rangle_{1/2,0}\bar{\chi}_{1/2,0}+2\langle{\,\mathcal{O}\,}\rangle_{0,1/2}\bar{\chi}_{0,1/2}\;.

(63)

For this to transform as a weight
n
modular form, we need
the individual traces to transform in the same way as the characters, up to a factor of
\tau^{2n}
, that is (including the sector
(\lambda,s)=(0,0)
for completeness),

\left\langle\,\mathcal{O}\,\right\rangle_{\lambda,s}(-\tfrac{1}{\tau})=\tau^{2n}\,\frac{2^{\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,1/2}}}{2^{\delta_{s,1/2}\delta_{\lambda,0}}}\left(\frac{\tau}{i}\right)^{\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}}\left\langle\,\mathcal{O}\,\right\rangle_{s,\lambda}(\tau).

(64)

Note that we have packaged the S-transformations of all the sectors (
37
) (
38
) (
40
) (
41
) into one expression.
This has been done so that when we look at the exact transformation of the GGE later, we can treat all sectors at once. It is important to remember that only three of these form a linear representation, and that
\lambda=s=0
belongs to a slightly different representation (
40
), (
41
).

Now denote by
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}
just the single piece appearing in the ansatz for the charge (
57
):

\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j^{2k-2}:\chi^{-}_{j}\chi^{+}_{-j}:\;.

(65)

We have

[\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]=-n^{2k-1}\chi^{\pm}_{n},

(66)

so that:

\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=q^{h(\lambda)+\frac{1}{12}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}e^{\mu{(n-\lambda)}^{2k-1}})^{2}.

(67)

Then we can determine
\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle
by:

\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=\frac{d}{d\mu}\biggl|_{\mu=0}\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}.

(68)

Doing this calculation yields:

\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=-e^{2\pi is}2\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(m-\lambda)^{2k-1}q^{m-\lambda}}{1-e^{2\pi is}q^{m-\lambda}}\right)\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}

(69)

Looking at each case in turn, we find that we can write the prefactor in front of
\langle{1}\rangle
in terms of the Eisenstein series.

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{0,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2k)\big[\left(E_{2k}(\tau)-2E_{2k}(2\tau)\right)+1\big]\langle{1}\rangle_{0,1/2}\;,

(70)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2k)\Big[\frac{1}{2^{2k-1}}\left(E_{2k}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})+2^{2k}E_{2k}(2\tau)-(2^{2k-1}+2)E_{2k}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2k-1}-1\right)\Big]\langle{1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\;,

(71)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},0}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2k)\left[\frac{1}{2^{2k-1}}\left(2^{2k-1}E_{2k}(\tau)-E_{2k}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})\right)+\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2k-1}-1\right)\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},0}\;,

(72)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{2k-1}^{\prime}}\rangle_{0,0}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2k)(1-E_{2k}(\tau))\langle{1}\rangle_{0,0}\;.

(73)

In each case, we have a weight
2n
modular form (under the group
\Gamma(2)
(defined in appendix
A
) which we shall denote by
\mathcal{F}_{2k}^{\lambda,s}(\tau)
and some additional constant given by:

{\bf c}_{2k-1}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\zeta(1-2k)&\lambda=0,\\
\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2k-1}-1\right)\zeta(1-2k)&\lambda=1/2.\end{cases}

(74)

In essence, we find:

\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=\left(\mathcal{F}_{2k}^{\lambda,s}+{\bf c}_{2k-1}(\lambda)\right)\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}

(75)

Plugging these results into (
57
), we find:

\begin{split}\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}&=\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2n-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{2n}_{2k}\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}+\langle{H_{0}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}\\
&=\left[H_{0}+\mathcal{F}_{2n}^{\lambda,s}+{\bf c}_{2n-1}(\lambda)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{2n}_{2k}\left(\mathcal{F}_{2k}^{\lambda,s}+{\bf c}_{2k-1}(\lambda)\right)\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}.\\
\end{split}

(76)

This is a weight
2n
vector valued modular form if and only if:

H_{k>0}=0\quad,\quad H_{0}=-{\bf c}_{2n-1}(\lambda).

(77)

So the final result is that the charge
\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}
is:

\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j^{2n-2}:\chi^{-}_{j}\chi^{+}_{-j}:-{\bf c}_{2n-1}(\lambda).

(78)

and

\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=\mathcal{F}_{2n}^{\lambda,s}\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}.

(79)

3.3.3
Odd Weight Fields and Even Spin Charges

We would like to repeat the above process for the odd weight fields, which give even spin charges:

\mathbf{Q}_{2n}=(\hat{B}_{2n+1})_{0}

(80)

We define now the auxiliary expression similarly to the previous section,

\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j^{2k-1}:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\;,

(81)

(from (
59
) it is obvious that
\mathbf{Q}_{2}=\mathbf{Q}_{2}^{\prime}
). One can verify that:

[\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]=\mp n^{2k}\chi^{\pm}_{n}\;.

(82)

Using this, one can calculate the expectation value:

\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=q^{\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{2}+\frac{1}{12}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}e^{\mu{(n-\lambda)}^{2k}})(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}e^{-{\mu(n-\lambda)}^{2k}}).

(83)

Then we find that the one-point function of the even spin auxiliary charge vanishes:

\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=\frac{d}{d\mu}\biggl|_{\mu=0}\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=0.

(84)

Since the one point functions do not give a useful result,
we now demand, instead, that
\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{2n}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle
is a weight
2n+4
depth
1
quasi-modular form.
This fixes the coefficients
H^{2n+1}_{2k+1}
as follows.

We need to consider the pieces
\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle
.
We have the following trace:

\begin{split}\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}+\nu\mathbf{Q}_{2}}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}=q^{h(\lambda)+\frac{1}{12}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}&(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}e^{\mu{(n-\lambda)}^{2k}+\nu(n-\lambda)^{2}})\\
&\quad\times(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}e^{-{\mu(n-\lambda)}^{2k}-\nu(n-\lambda)^{2}}).\end{split}

(85)

We can obtain our desired two-point function by taking derivatives as before:

\begin{split}\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}&=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\mu\,\partial\nu}\biggl|_{\mu,\nu=0}\langle{e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}+\nu\mathbf{Q}_{2}}}\rangle_{\lambda,\pm}\\
&=-e^{2\pi is}2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{(n-\lambda)^{2k+2}q^{n-\lambda}}{1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda}}+e^{2\pi is}\frac{(n-\lambda)^{2k+2}q^{2n-2\lambda}}{(1-e^{2\pi is}q^{n-\lambda})^{2}}\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{\lambda,s}\\
\end{split}.

(86)

Recall from Appendix
A
,

D_{r}=q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}-\frac{r}{12}E_{2}(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{d\tau}-\frac{r}{12}E_{2}(\tau),

(87)

the covariant Serre derivative. This has the properties that if
F_{r}
denotes a weight
r
modular form, then
D_{r}F_{r}
is a weight
r+2
modular form. We can see by definition that
D_{0}F_{r}
is also a weight
r+2
and depth
1
quasi-modular form. We can break the two-point function down to each sector and rewrite them in terms of Eisenstein series.

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{0,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2(k+1))\left[D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tau)-4D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(2\tau)\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{0,\frac{1}{2}},

(88)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2(k+1))\biggl[(4^{-k-1}D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})

\displaystyle\quad-2^{-2k}\left(2^{2k}+1\right)D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tau)+4D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(2\tau)\biggr]\langle{1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}},

(89)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},0}

\displaystyle=\zeta(1-2(k+1))\left[-4^{-k-1}D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})+D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tau)\right]\langle{1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},0},

(90)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{0,0}

\displaystyle=-\zeta(1-2(k+1))D_{0}E_{2(k+1)}(\tau)\langle{1}\rangle_{0,0}.

(91)

Now we have shown that
\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle_{\lambda,s}
forms a weight
2k+4
depth
1
vector-valued quasi-modular form. Given that we know

\langle{\mathbf{Q}_{2n}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2n}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle+\sum_{k}H^{2n+1}_{2k+1}\langle{\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}_{2k}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}\rangle,

(92)

is a weight
2n+4
depth
1
form, we can conclude that all the coefficients
H^{2n+1}_{2k+1}
are zero and the charge takes the form:

\mathbf{Q}_{2n}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j^{2n-1}:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:.

(93)

Combining the Results

It is now not difficult to see that the expressions (
78
) and (
93
) can be packaged into one neat formula:

\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}j^{n-2}:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\;-\;{\bf c}_{n-1}(\lambda)\;,\quad\quad\lambda\in\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}\;.

(94)

In the above expression we have:

{\bf c}_{n-1}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\zeta(1-n)&\lambda=0,\\
\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}-1\right)\zeta(1-n)&\lambda=1/2.\end{cases}

(95)

In the above, we have used the fact that the Riemann zeta function vanishes when evaluated at negative even integers.

3.3.4
The Way of the Zeta Function

In this section we will obtain the same result as in the previous section, but for arbitrarily twisted representations using zeta-function regularisation.

A Symplectic Fermion representation with arbitrary twist
0<\lambda<1
has conformal weight
h(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{2}
. The mode expansions are given by

\chi^{\pm}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mp\lambda}\chi^{\pm}_{n}z^{-n-1}.

(96)

Notice that the fields
\chi^{+}
and
\chi^{-}
have different mode indices now. One obtains a simple irreducible representation of the symplectic fermion algebra, a Fock space as follows:

\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}=\left\{\chi^{-}_{-n_{1}}\dots\chi^{-}_{-n_{\nu}}\chi^{+}_{-m_{1}}\dots\chi^{+}_{-m_{\mu}}|h(\lambda)\rangle\,\,\textbf{;}\,\,\begin{matrix}\mu\geq 0,\\
\nu\geq 0,\end{matrix},\begin{matrix}n_{i}>n_{i+1}>0,\\
m_{i}>m_{i+1}>0,\end{matrix}\,\,n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\pm\lambda\right\}.

(97)

We will take the integral on the cylinder and attempt to regularise it directly. The integral on the cylinder would yield

\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}j^{n-2}\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}.

(98)

This expression can be found by looking at (
44
) and noticing that the total derivative terms will not contribute to the integral (
51
), and then noticing that on the cylinder with coordinate
w
, related to the plane with coordinate
z
by
z=e^{w}
, the fields have the following expansion

\chi^{\pm}(w)=z\chi^{\pm}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mp\lambda}\chi^{\pm}_{n}e^{-wn}.

(99)

By carefully massaging this expression to obtain a normal ordered product we find

\begin{split}\mathbf{Q}_{n-1}&=-(-1)^{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}(m+\lambda)^{n-1}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}j^{n-2}:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:\\
&=-(-1)^{n}\zeta(1-n,\lambda)+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}j^{n-2}:\chi^{-}_{-j}\chi^{+}_{j}:.\end{split}

(100)

In the above we have noticed that the constant but divergent sum can be formally expressed in terms of the Hurwitz Zeta function. This function is the meromorphic continuation to all
z\neq 1
of the following infinite sum, defined for
\text{Re}(z)>1

\begin{split}\zeta(z,a)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+a)^{z}}\quad,\quad\text{Re}(z)>1,a\neq 0,-1,...\;.\\
\end{split}

(101)

It is related to the Riemann zeta function
\zeta
for the following special values:

\zeta(z,1)=\zeta(z),\quad\zeta(z,\tfrac{1}{2})=(-1+2^{s})\zeta(z)\;.

(102)

For details on the Hurwitz Zeta function, see §25.11 of
16
. A fact that we will use later is that when
z
is given by the odd integer
1-2n
and
a
is given by
1-s
, with
s=0,1/2
, then the Hurwitz zeta function has the following integral representation

\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2}(2\pi)^{n}\zeta(1-2n,1-s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2n-1}}{e^{2\pi is+u}-1}du.

(103)

One finds that in a generic twisted sector, that:

{\bf c}_{n-1}(\lambda)=(-1)^{n}\zeta(1-n,\lambda)=(-1)^{n}\zeta(1-n,1-\lambda).

(104)

Notice that indeed when
\lambda=0,\frac{1}{2}
we replicate exactly the result in (
94
).

4
The Symplectic Fermion GGE

The result of the previous section is the existence of a hierarchy of conserved charges given by the zero-modes of quasi-primary fields that are bilinear in the symplectic fermion fields. These charges are denoted by
\mathbf{Q}_{n}
and are defined in (
94
). When the subscript
n=2k
is even, the commutation relations with the symplectic fermion modes are

[\mathbf{Q}_{2k},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]=\mp n^{2k}\chi^{\pm}_{n},

(105)

while for odd subscripts
n=2k-1
the relations are

[\mathbf{Q}_{2k-1},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]=-n^{2k-1}\chi^{\pm}_{n}.

(106)

We now construct the extended characters by inserting the first
N
charges into the trace, each accompanied by its own chemical potential

\Psi_{\lambda,s}(\tau,\mu_{0},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{N}):=\Tr_{\lambda,s}\!\left(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}e^{\sum_{k\geq 2}\mu_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k}}\right),\quad\lambda,s=0,\tfrac{1}{2}.

(107)

Using the commutation relations above, we obtain exact expressions for the GGEs

\Psi_{\lambda,s}(\bm{\mu})=e^{-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu_{n}{\bf c}_{n}(\lambda)}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N-\lambda}}\left(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{k}n^{k}}\right)\left(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{-\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\mu_{k}n^{k}}\right),

(108)

where, for conciseness, we have defined
\mu_{1}=2\pi i\tau
.

It is also important to discuss convergence. We will always assume that there are only a finite number of non-zero chemical potentials, and further that at least one non-zero odd spin charge in the GGE.
If the highest-spin charge inserted is odd,
then convergence requires

\displaystyle\text{Re}(\mu_{2K-1})<0\;.

(109)

If, in addition, there is a finite set of higher even-spin charges
\mathbf{Q}_{2n_{i}}
with
2n_{i}>2K-1
, we further require

\text{Re}(\mu_{2n_{i}})=0\;,

(110)

so that the GGE is convergent by the terms in it being exponentially suppressed.

We will derive the exact modular transformation of this GGE in the next section and also analyse its asymptotic behaviour.

4.1
Exact Transformation of Symplectic Fermion GGE

In this section we will obtain an exact transformation of the GGE. We highlight in advance the final result (
188
) and (
189
). This proof follows those in
25
;
60
, but with some important differences in the details.

It will be helpful to introduce constants
\alpha_{j}
and parametrise the
chemical potentials by

\mu_{k}=2\pi i\alpha_{k}\tau^{k}\;.

(111)

For generality, we will leave
\alpha_{1}
free, but later, when we perform an asymptotic analysis of the transformation, we will set
\alpha_{1}=1
.
For the sake of compactness, we introduce the following notation:

\prod_{\pm}S_{\pm}=S_{+}S_{-}\;,\quad\sum_{\pm}S_{\pm}=S_{+}+S_{-}\;.

(112)

In terms of these, the GGE (
108
) becomes

\Psi_{\lambda,s}(\tau,\bm{\alpha})=e^{-2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}{\bf c}_{j}(\lambda)}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N-\lambda}>0}\prod_{\pm}\left(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}}\right).

(113)

For reasons that will become clear in the subsequent paragraphs, let us introduce a real, positive parameter
\theta
into the GGE, (and suppress for the moment the dependence on
\tau
)

\Psi_{\lambda,s}(\theta|\bm{\alpha})=e^{-2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}{\bf c}_{j}(\lambda)}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N-\lambda}>0}\prod_{\pm}\left(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}}\right).

(114)

We can recover our original GGE simply by setting
\theta=1
. The convergence conditions
(
109
) and (
110
)
become, where
K
is the largest value such that
\alpha_{2K-1}
is non-zero,

\begin{split}&\text{Im}(\alpha_{2K-1}\tau^{2K-1})>0\;,\\
&\text{Im}(\alpha_{2n_{i}}\tau^{2n_{i}})=0\;,\quad 2n_{i}>2K-1.\end{split}

(115)

However, in this calculation we will work in a more restricted domain, where all the chemical potentials are constrained.
We always require there to be only a finite set of non-zero
\mu_{n}
. If
K
is the largest value such that
\alpha_{2K-1}
is non-zero,
then we require

\begin{split}&\text{Im}(\,\alpha^{\vphantom{\phi}}_{2K-1}\tau^{2K-1})>0,\\
&\text{Im}(\,\alpha_{2n-1}\tau^{2n-1})\geq 0,\quad\forall\,\,\,n<K,\\
&\text{Im}(\,\alpha_{2n}\tau^{2n})=0,\quad\forall\,\,\,n.\\
\end{split}

(116)

At the end our result still makes sense for the larger domain (
115
) and we can argue that by analytic continuation it is correct for that larger domain.

We take the log of the expression (
114
), to obtain a sum over logs and a sum of Hurwitz zeta functions. We will denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}
the sum over the logs.

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}&=\log(\Psi_{\lambda,s}(\theta|\bm{\alpha}))+2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{K}\tau^{2j-1}\alpha_{2j-1}\zeta(1-2j,1-\lambda)\\
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda>0}\sum_{\pm}\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}}).\end{split}

(117)

As before,
2K-1
is the spin of the highest odd-spin charge in the GGE. Here we will pick the branch of
\log
such that
\log(-1)=-\pi i
, although it will not matter in the end since we will exponentiate our answer. To apply the Poisson resummation formula, we need to rewrite this as a sum over the entire integer lattice, and not just
\mathbb{Z}-\lambda>0
. Formally, we can use

\displaystyle\log(1+e^{x})=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+e^{x})+\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+e^{-x})+\tfrac{1}{2}\log x\;,

(118)

to rewrite

\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}
(where
\lambda\in\{0,1/2\}
) as

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}&=\frac{1}{2}{\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}}\sum_{\pm}\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\theta\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}})\\
&+\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,\frac{1}{2}}\log(2)+\pi i\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda<0}\left[2s-1+\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1-(-1)^{j})\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}\right].\end{split}

(119)

The prime indicates that the case
n=0,s=0
is excluded. However, the sums in the first and second lines both diverge as
n\to-\infty
.
It is here where the introduction of the parameter
\theta
becomes useful, as we can differentiate twice with respect to it to do away with the divergent piece. First, we take two derivatives of
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}
in (
117
), and only then rewrite it as a sum over the lattice which gives

\partial^{2}_{\theta}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}=\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}\sum_{\pm}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}n^{j}})

(120)

We then introduce the functions
p_{\theta,s}^{\pm}
and
F_{\theta,s}^{\pm}
,

p_{\theta,s}^{\pm}(t)=1-e^{2\pi is}e^{\pm 2\pi i\theta\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}(it)^{j}\alpha_{j}}\;,\quad\quad F_{\theta,s}^{\pm}(t)=\partial^{2}_{\theta}\log(p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t))\;,

(121)

where the variable
t
is related to
\tau
by
\tau=it
. We can rewrite the infinite series as

\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}=\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}\sum_{\pm}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\log(p_{\theta,s}^{\pm}(nt))=\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}\sum_{\pm}F_{\theta,s}^{\pm}(nt).

(122)

The above series converges in the domain (
115
) with
\tau=it
. The Poisson resummation formula for a function
f(\nu t)
on the integer shifted lattice is

\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}+\lambda}f(\nu t)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{f}\left(\frac{m}{t}\right)e^{-2\pi im\lambda}\;,

(123)

where the tilde denotes the Fourier transformation

\widetilde{f}(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{2\pi ity}\,dt\;.

(124)

To use (
123
) in the case
\lambda=s=0
, we have to sum over the whole integer lattice with
f(x)
a smooth function, so we add in the limiting term as
n\to 0
of
F^{\pm}_{\theta,0}(nt)
, and define

\displaystyle F^{\pm}_{\theta,0}(0)=\lim_{x\to 0}F^{\pm}_{\theta,0}(x)=-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}

(125)

and now we consider

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,s}=2\partial^{2}_{\theta}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,s}-\frac{2}{\theta^{2}}\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}\\
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-\lambda}\sum_{\pm}F_{\theta,s}^{\pm}(nt)\;.

(126)

Based on (
122
), we need to calculate the fourier transforms
\widetilde{F}^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(y)
in the regimes
y<0
,
y=0
and
y>0
, which we do separately now.

\bullet\hskip 11.49994pty\neq 0

We integrate by parts to obtain

\widetilde{F}^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\log(p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t))\,e^{2\pi ity}\,dt=-\frac{1}{2\pi iy}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\left(\frac{\dot{p}^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t)}{p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t)}\right)\,e^{2\pi ity}\,dt,

(127)

where the dot on
\dot{p}^{\pm}_{\theta,s}
denotes a
t
derivative. Depending on whether
y>0
or
y<0
, we will extend the integral into the upper or lower half plane so that the integral on the contour going out to infinity is exponentially suppressed by
e^{2\pi ity}
.

\bullet\hskip 11.49994pty>0

We can close the contour in the upper half plane and the integral is given by a sum over residues at poles in the upper half plane. By inspection, poles can only come from zeroes of
p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t)
, and around each pole the
\theta
-derivatives can be pulled outside the integral making the result immediate: if
p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(t)
has a zero at
t=t_{0}^{\pm}
of order
M
, then the contribution to the integral (
127
) from the residue at
t_{0}^{\pm}
, coming from an anti-clockwise contour is

\frac{-M}{y}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\left(e^{2\pi it_{0}^{\pm}y}\right)\;.

(128)

The condition for

t_{0}^{\pm}
to be a zero of
p^{\pm}_{\theta,s}
is

\pm\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\alpha_{j}(it_{0}^{\pm})^{j}=\frac{n}{\theta},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}+s.

(129)

Rather than trying to keep track of the order of any particular zero, we will simply sum (with multiplicity) and obtain

\widetilde{F}^{\pm}_{\theta,s}(y)=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+s}\sum_{\widetilde{\omega}^{\pm}_{l}(n/\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}}\frac{1}{y}\partial^{2}_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\widetilde{\omega}_{l}^{\pm}(n/\theta)y},

(130)

where
\widetilde{\omega}_{j}^{\pm}(\kappa)
satisfies

\omega=\widetilde{\omega}_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa):\quad\pm\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\alpha_{j}(i\omega)^{j}=\kappa

(131)

Here
l
labels the roots of the equations,
l=0,...,N-1
, and we also define

\mathbb{H}^{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\text{Im}(z)>0\}\quad,\quad\mathbb{H}^{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\text{Im}(z)<0\}.

(132)

Let us emphasise, that by the sum over the roots we mean

\sum_{\widetilde{\omega}^{\pm}_{l}(n/\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}}=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
\widetilde{\omega}^{\pm}_{l}(n/\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}\end{subarray}}^{N-1},

(133)

that is, this notation is a shorthand for a sum over all the roots that lie in the upper half plane of both associated polynomials. We’ll use a similar expression for
\mathbb{H}^{-}
.

\bullet\hskip 11.49994pty<0

For the case of
y<0
, one performs similar steps in the lower half plane with clockwise contours to obtain instead

\bullet\hskip 11.49994pt\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,s}

We will need in this section some facts from Appendix B of

Fact 1:

Fact 2:
If we start with a function

\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{l}^{+}(x)dx=-2\pi\int_{-i\omega_{l}^{+}(0)}^{-i\omega_{l}^{+}(\infty)}\frac{x(iw)}{e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{2\pi w}-1}dw+2\pi\int_{i\omega_{l}^{-}(0)=-i\omega_{l}^{+}(0)}^{i\omega_{l}^{-}(\infty)}\frac{x(iw)}{e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{2\pi w}-1}dw.

(166)

, a fact apparent from (

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,s}^{(0)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-s>0}\log(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}^{-}(n\Theta)})+\log(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}^{+}(n\Theta)})\\
\end{split}

G_{\lambda}(t)\sim 2\log(2)+2\delta_{\lambda,0}\log\left(\frac{\pi it}{\alpha_{1}\tau}\right)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}O_{2n-1}t^{2n-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}^{\lambda}_{2n}t^{2n},

(175)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,s}^{(0)}&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-s>0}G_{\lambda}(n\Theta)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}((m+s)\Theta)\\

&+\log(2)(\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,\frac{1}{2}}-\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,\frac{1}{2}}.)\end{split}

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}(x)dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}^{-}(x)})+\log(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}^{+}(x)})dx\\

(178)

Recall that the roots
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(x)
satisfy

\pm\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau^{j}\alpha_{j}(\pm 1)^{j}(\omega^{\pm}_{0}(x))^{j}=x,

(179)

which we can use to define

and

(181)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,s}&\sim-\theta 2\pi i\sum_{n=1}^{K}\tau^{2n-1}\alpha_{2n-1}\zeta(1-2n,1-\lambda)-\pi i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta(1-2n,1-s)O_{2n-1}\theta^{1-2n}\\
&-\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}\log\left(\frac{i}{\alpha_{1}\tau\theta}\right)+\log(2)(\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,\frac{1}{2}}-\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,\frac{1}{2}})\end{split}

(182)

where we’ve remembered that

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,s}&=\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}\log\left(\frac{i}{\alpha_{1}\tau}\right)+\log(2)(\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,\frac{1}{2}}-\delta_{s,\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{\lambda,0})\end{split}

For simplicity, throughout the whole of this section, we will assume that

is purely imaginary.

5.1
Asymptotic Check with
\mathbf{Q}_{2}

Let us begin this section by reiterating the main result for a GGE with just the spin-2 charge inserted. With
\hat{\tau}=-1/\tau
,

\begin{split}\Tr_{\lambda,s}(\hat{q}^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{\mu_{2}\mathbf{Q}_{2}})=\frac{2^{\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,1/2}}}{2^{\delta_{s,1/2}\delta_{\lambda,0}}}\left(\frac{\tau}{i}\right)^{\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}}\prod_{\pm}&\exp\biggl\{\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{2\pi i(\hat{\tau}x\pm\hat{\tau}^{2}x^{2}\alpha_{2})}))dx\biggr\}\\
&\times\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-s}\prod_{\omega^{\pm}_{l}(n)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}}(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{2\pi i\omega_{l}^{\pm}(n)})\right),\end{split}

(190)

where we have that the roots
\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa)
satisfy:

\omega=\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa):\qquad(\hat{\tau}\omega)\pm\alpha_{2}(\hat{\tau}\omega)^{2}=\kappa,

(191)

and we also have that
\mu_{2}=:2\pi i\hat{\tau}^{2}\alpha_{2}
. First we will perform an asymptotic expansion of this result around
\mu_{2}\to 0
. Then we will check that this is what we expect from perturbative analysis.

The problem of asymptotically expanding the above result is broken down into two parts. The first part is dealing with the integral expression to obtain ground state energies. The second part is dealing with the roots of the polynomials appearing in the infinite product, corresponding to excited state energies. We do not need to deal with the constant prefactor since this is just a compact encoding of the
S
-transformation of the symplectic fermion characters (
37
), (
38
), (
40
), (

).

5.1.1
Ground State Energies

The integral at hand is

\prod_{\pm}\exp\biggl\{\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{2\pi i(\hat{\tau}x\pm\hat{\tau}^{2}x^{2}\alpha_{2})}))dx\biggr\}=:q^{h_{0}^{(s)}(\tau,\mu_{2})},

(192)

where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. Perform now the substitution
u=-2\pi i\hat{\tau}x
. Then one finds that

h_{0}^{(s)}(\tau,\mu_{2})=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\pm}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{-u\pm\mu_{2}(\frac{u\tau}{2\pi i})^{2}})du.

(193)

Now on each integral we perform another substitution. That is

u=:tf_{\pm}(t),\quad-(tf_{\pm}(t))\pm\mu_{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}(tf_{\pm}(t))^{2}=-t.

(194)

We choose
f_{\pm}(t)
such that

\begin{split}tf_{\pm}(t)\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{-t})&\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 0,\\
tf_{\pm}(t)\log(1-e^{2\pi is}e^{-t})&\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,\\
tf_{\pm}(t)\to\infty\quad&\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.\\
\end{split}

(195)

Such an
f_{\pm}(t)
can be found explicitly

f_{\pm}(t)=\mp\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\mu_{2}\tau^{2}t}\left(1-\sqrt{1\pm\frac{t\mu_{2}\tau^{2}}{\pi^{2}}}\right).

(196)

Then the integral becomes

h_{0}^{(s)}(\tau,\mu_{2})=\frac{e^{2\pi is}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{e^{t}-e^{2\pi is}}(f_{+}(t)+f_{-}(t))dt.

(197)

Now we perform a series expansion of the integrand and integrate each term. Since
\mu_{2}
is constrained to be purely imaginary, we find that such an expansion is at best an asymptotic expansion. The result is

h_{0}^{(s)}(\tau,\mu_{2})\sim-\frac{1}{\mu_{2}\tau^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-4m-2}(2\pi i)^{2m+2}}{(2m+1)}\binom{4m}{2m}\left(\frac{\mu_{2}\tau^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\right)^{2m+1}\mathbf{c}_{2m+1}(s),

(198)

where
{\bf c}_{n}(\lambda)
is defined in (
94
). We can now move on to the excited state energies.

5.1.2
Excited State Energies

The excited state energies appear in the product

\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}-s}\prod_{\omega^{\pm}_{l}(n)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}}(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{2\pi i\omega_{l}^{\pm}(n)}).

(199)

The roots satisfy

\omega=\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa):\qquad(\hat{\tau}z)\pm\alpha_{2}(\hat{\tau}\omega)^{2}=\kappa.

(200)

What is convenient for us is that this is just a quadratic equation and we can solve this directly and simply. As stated earlier, for simplicity, we will assume
\hat{\tau}
is pure imaginary
\hat{\tau}=i\hat{\tau}_{2}
(so that
\tau=\frac{i}{\hat{\tau}_{2}}
). The
(+)

\omega^{+}_{0}=-\frac{i\left(\sqrt{4\alpha_{2}\kappa+1}-1\right)}{2\alpha_{2}\hat{\tau}_{2}},\quad\omega^{+}_{1}=\frac{i\left(\sqrt{4\alpha_{2}\kappa+1}+1\right)}{2\alpha_{2}\hat{\tau}_{2}}

(201)

and the
(-)
equation also has two solutions:

\omega^{-}_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{4\alpha_{2}\kappa-1}-i}{2\alpha_{2}\hat{\tau}_{2}},\quad\omega^{-}_{1}=-\frac{\sqrt{4\alpha_{2}\kappa-1}+i}{2\alpha_{2}\hat{\tau}_{2}}.

(202)

We choose these solutions so that
\omega_{0}
is the solution continuously connected to zero when

\alpha_{2}

\omega_{1}^{\pm}(\kappa)\sim\frac{\mp i}{\hat{\tau}_{2}\alpha_{2}}+O(\alpha_{2})\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha_{2}\to 0.

(203)

It is clear then that
\omega_{1}
is either not in the upper half plane, or it is in the upper half plane but diverges as

(1-e^{2\pi i\lambda}e^{2\pi i\omega_{1}})\to 1,

(204)

so there will be no overall contribution from

in the
\alpha_{2}\to 0

It is not too difficult then to calculate

2\pi i\omega_{0}^{\pm}(n)\sim 2\pi i\tau n+nf(\mp n),

(205)

where

f(\kappa)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{i2^{m+2}\pi^{-m}\tau\kappa^{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m+1}\left(-i\mu_{2}\tau^{2}\right)^{m+1}}{(2)_{m+1}}.

(206)

The product asks for solutions in the upper-half plane which occurs if
\kappa>0
, so we find that the product becomes

\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}-s}^{\infty}(1+q^{n}e^{nf(n)})(1+q^{n}e^{nf(-n)}).

(207)

5.1.3
The Complete Asymptotic Expression

We can carefully massage the expressions (

\Tr_{\lambda,s}(\hat{q}^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{\mu_{2}\mathbf{Q}_{2}})\sim\frac{2^{\delta_{s,0}\delta_{\lambda,1/2}}}{2^{\delta_{s,1/2}\delta_{\lambda,0}}}\left(\frac{\tau}{i}\right)^{\delta_{\lambda,0}\delta_{s,0}}\Tr_{s,\lambda}(q^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{\mu_{2}\tau^{3}\text{{\boldmath{$Q$}}}_{2}}),

(208)

where

\text{{\boldmath{$Q$}}}_{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{\mu_{2}\tau^{2}}{4\pi i}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{2^{k+1}(2k+1)!}{(k+2)!}\mathbf{Q}_{k+2}\right).

(209)

5.1.4
Perturbative Check

We can perform a perturbative check by expanding directly in terms of
\mu_{2}
the expression:

\Tr_{\lambda,s}(\hat{q}^{L_{0}-c/24}e^{\mu_{2}\mathbf{Q}_{2}}),

(210)

and transforming each term separately, and then picking out the terms lowest order in

(211)

One can find the transformations of

(212)

and then matching perturbatively against the

5.2
Asymptotic Check with
\mathbf{Q}_{2n-1}

The Symplectic Fermion GGE with KdV charges is simply the square of the Free Fermion GGE with KdV charges. In this way, our exact transformation with odd-spin charges matches exactly the result proved in
25
. The free fermion has two sectors, the half-integer moded Neveu-Schwarz sector, and the integer moded Ramond sector. If
\mathcal{O}
is an operator in the free fermion, then the following traces are defined

\begin{split}\Tr^{\text{FF}}_{0,1/2}(\mathcal{O})=\Tr_{\text{R},+}(\mathcal{O}),\quad&\Tr^{\text{FF}}_{1/2,1/2}(\mathcal{O})=\Tr_{\text{NS},+}(\mathcal{O}),\quad\Tr^{\text{FF}}_{1/2,0}(\mathcal{O})=\Tr_{\text{NS},-}(\mathcal{O})\\
&\Tr_{0,0}^{\text{FF}}(\mathcal{O})=\Tr_{\text{R},-}(\mathcal{O})\end{split}

(219)

where the trace
\Tr_{\text{R},-}
is taken over the Ramond sector but omitting the zero mode
\psi_{0}
. Denote by
\mathbf{I}_{2n-1}^{\text{FF}}
the KdV charges in the theory of the Free Fermion
23
. Then one can easily see when we insert the first
N
KdV charges into the GGE we have the relations

\Tr_{\lambda,s}(e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{2k-1}\mathbf{Q}_{2k-1}})=\left[\Tr^{\text{FF}}_{\lambda,s}(e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{2k-1}\mathbf{I}^{\text{FF}}_{2k-1}})\right]^{2}.

(220)

In
23
, they calculated what the asymptotic expression for the transformed GGE should be for the charge
\mathbf{I}^{\text{FF}}_{2m-1}
. This transformation was done for a generic
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformation:

\hat{\tau}=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}.

(221)

Their result was as follows:

\Tr^{\text{FF}}(\hat{q}^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}e^{\mu\mathbf{I}^{\text{FF}}_{2m-1}})\sim\Tr^{\text{FF}}(e^{\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\mu_{2p+1}^{(m)}\mathbf{I}^{\text{FF}}_{2p+1}}).

(222)

Here we have suppressed indices and resulting prefactors associated to the modular transformation to avoid clutter. The terms
\mu_{2p+1}^{(m)}
are defined below, noting that if
p
is divisible by
m-1
then
n:=p/(m-1)
:

\begin{split}\mu_{1}^{(m)}&=2\pi i\tau\\
\mu_{2p+1}^{(m)}&=\delta_{p,n(m-1)}\,\,\frac{\mu^{n}}{n!(2\pi i)^{n-1}}\frac{(n(2m-1))!c^{{n-1}}}{(2n(m-1)+1)!}(c\tau+d)^{n(2m-1)+1}.\end{split}

(223)

That is, the terms vanish when
m-1
does not divide

.

\Tr(\hat{q}^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}e^{\mu\mathbf{Q}_{2m-1}})\sim\Tr(e^{\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\mu_{2p+1}^{(m)}\mathbf{Q}_{2p+1}}).

(224)

and

(225)

\mathbf{J}_{5}
is the zero-mode of a weight-6 quasi-primary field which is linearly independent of the current that gives the KdV charge. At

this quasi-primary field differs from the KdV current by a null field, and at

From (

and

.

6
Relation to
W_{n}
algebras and other hierarchies

As we have already said, the symplectic fermion’s relation to the KdV and Boussinesq hierarchy were shown a long time ago in

.
In this section we demonstrate that the bilinear quasi-primary fields introduced earlier realise both the KdV hierarchy and the Boussinesq hierarchy and make some comments on the relation to the hierarchies of other

6.1
The KdV Hierarchy

The KdV charges are defined as zero-modes of even-spin quasi-primary fields constructed solely from the stress tensor:

\mathbf{I}_{2n-1}^{\text{KdV}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi i}\!\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\,J^{\text{KdV}}_{2n}(w),

(226)

where the charges
\mathbf{I}_{2n-1}^{\text{KdV}}
mutually commute. These are defined at generic central charge
5
, and below we have written the first two currents in the series, having added ourselves the total derivative pieces necessary to make these fields quasi-primary.

\begin{split}J^{\text{KdV}}_{2}&=T(z),\\
J^{\text{KdV}}_{4}&=\Lambda(z)=(TT)(z)-\tfrac{3}{10}\,\partial^{2}T(z).\end{split}

(227)

In
13
it was already shown that the KdV hierarchy can be expressed in terms of bilinears of symplectic fermion fields. What they call
\phi
and
\psi
, we have denoted by
\chi^{-}
and
\chi^{+}
. The result is that, at
c=-2
, they find a set of weight
2n
fields in the Virasoro algebra, whose spin
2n-1
zero-modes commute with one another, expressed simply as left normal orderings of the stress tensor
(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})
.

(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})=(\cdots(((TT)T)T)\cdots T)\quad(n\ \text{factors}).

(228)

Since the KdV hierarchy is the unique set of such fields/charges, they conclude that these left normal ordered stress tensors correspond to the KdV currents. That is, up to total derivatives,

c=-2:\quad(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})\propto J^{\text{KdV}}_{2n}

(229)

In terms of the symplectic fermion, they show that these KdV currents can be written simply as bilinear fields.

(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})=\frac{n}{2^{n}}\left((\partial^{2n-2}\chi^{-}\chi^{+})+(\chi^{-}\partial^{2n-2}\chi^{+})\right).

(230)

Based on (
42
), we can write

B_{2n}=\frac{A_{2n,0}2^{n}}{n}(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})+\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}A_{2n,k}(\partial^{2n-2-k}\chi^{-}\partial^{k}\chi^{+}).

(231)

It is easy to check from this expression that
B_{2n}
is proportional to
(\overleftarrow{T^{n}})
up to total derivatives.

triplet model consists of all bosonic fields constructed from the symplectic fermions, and in particular includes (as a subalgebra) the

algebra, also known as the “Singlet Algebra” at

The generators are

Equivalently, in terms of modes:

\begin{split}[L_{m},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]&=-n\,\chi^{\pm}_{m+n},\\
[W_{m},\chi^{\pm}_{n}]&=\mp\tfrac{1}{2}n(m+2n)\chi^{\pm}_{m+n}.\end{split}

(236)

We will not discuss the details of the representation theory of the singlet algebra here, although we direct the reader to both our earlier brief discussion of the generic twisted sectors of the Symplectic Fermion to which they are related
3.3
, and to
12
for details. We will also briefly discuss
W_{3}
representation theory in Appendix
C
. At the moment, we just note that the generic twisted representations do not enter the modular invariant partition function of the triplet model (
36
) considered in this work.

In analogy with the KdV case, the Boussinesq charges are associated with spins that are integers not divisible by
3
, but in the
W_{3}
algebra rather than Virasoro,

\mathbf{I}_{n}^{\text{Bou.}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi i}\!\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\,J_{n+1}(w),\qquad n=1,2\pmod{3}.

(237)

The first three Boussinesq currents are given at generic central charge by
4
;
48

\begin{split}J^{\text{Bou.}}_{2}(z)&=T(z),\\
J^{\text{Bou.}}_{3}(z)&=W(z),\\
J^{\text{Bou.}}_{5}(z)&=(TW)(z)-\tfrac{3}{14}\,\partial^{2}W(z).\end{split}

(238)

where again we have added total derivative terms to make the fields quasi-primary without affecting the integrals of motion. We have already shown that the first two are bilinear fields, (
232
):

T(z)=B_{2}(z),\qquad W(z)=B_{3}(z).

(239)

For the next charge, applying the state-operator correspondence as before yields

\begin{split}J^{\text{Bou.}}_{5}&=\tfrac{5}{84}(\partial^{3}\chi^{-}\,\chi^{+})-\tfrac{5}{14}(\partial^{2}\chi^{-}\,\partial\chi^{+})+\tfrac{5}{14}(\partial\chi^{-}\,\partial^{2}\chi^{+})-\tfrac{5}{84}(\chi^{-}\,\partial^{3}\chi^{+})\\
&=\tfrac{5}{6}\,B_{5}.\end{split}

(240)

Hence the first three Boussinesq currents are proportional to the bilinear fields, and therefore so are their charges. Explicit checks up to spin
13
give

\mathbf{I}_{n}^{\text{Bou.}}=\mathbf{Q}_{n},\qquad n=1,2\pmod{3},\quad n\leq 13.

(241)

We therefore expect that the full Boussinesq hierarchy is realised at
c=-2
, with currents constructed from bilinear Symplectic Fermion fields. It should be possible to mirror the analysis of
13
and show that there exists a set of fields composed of
T(z)
and
W(z)
such that the spins of their charges are exactly
1,2\pmod{3}
and when written in terms of symplectic fermions they are proportional to the bilinear fields presented here.

6.3
The relation of KdV and Boussinesq charges

The KdV and Boussinesq charges are distinct for general values of
c
, and in particular the Boussinesq hierarchy does include
\Lambda_{0}
,
a charge of weight 3, whereas the KdV hierarchy does; and conversely the Boussinesq hierarchy has a weight two charge
W_{0}
which the KdV hierarchy does not. If one computes their commutator, one gets

\displaystyle[W_{0},\Lambda_{0}]=-\frac{1}{5}(12(T^{\prime}W)-8(W^{\prime}T)+W^{\prime\prime\prime})_{0}\;,

(242)

and so it would appear that these charges do not commute, not even for
c=-2
,
This is in contradiction with the statement that all the KdV and Boussinesq charges are found as bilinears in the symplectic fermion and all mutually commute.

We show in appendix
C
that the right hand side of (
242
) should be set to zero, and the vanishing of the corresponding field puts a constraint on the representations of the
W_{3}
algebra at
c=-2
which is precisely satisfied by those that can be constructed in the symplectic fermion model.

6.4
Relation to other hierarchies

The central charge
c=-2
plays a special role for all the
W_{n}
algebras.
These algebras each have minimal models where the central charge is given in terms of two co-prime integers
p
and
q

31
:

\displaystyle c_{n}(p,q)=(n-1)\left(1-n(n+1)\frac{(p-q)^{2}}{pq}\right)\;,\;\;\;\;p,q\geq n\;.

(243)

In each case we see that
c=-2
appears as the non-minimal value

\displaystyle c_{n}(n-1,n)=-2\;.

(244)

We have already seen that the symplectic fermion realises the KdV hierarchy related to the Virasoro (or
W_{2}
algebra) and the Boussinesq hierarchy related to the
W_{3}
algebra. The same does not seem to be true for the higher
W_{n}
algebras, at least not straightforwardly, since these all have a non-trivial primary field of weight 4 which is easily seen to be absent from the symplectic fermion model. Some of the structure constants involving the weight 4 field have been worked out in general

34
;
42
, and the weight 4 field decouples from the OPE algebra of the weight 3 field at
c=-2
: in the notation of
42
,
C_{33}^{4}
and
C_{34}^{3}
both vanish at
c=-2
, and so it seems likely that symplectic fermion model, if it is a realisation of the
W_{n}
algebras at all, is one in which all fields with spin greater than 3 are set to zero.

That this is the case for the
W_{4}
algebra is clear since all the structure constants were found in
7
;
45
, and for the
W_{5}
algebra in
41
. In the first two papers it was stated that the
W_{4}
algebra is not defined at
c=-2
owing to the divergence of the
C_{44}^{(33)}
structure constant. An alternative viewpoint is that in order for the structure constants of the algebra to be non-singular at
c=-2
, the spin 4 field must be rescaled by a factor of
\sqrt{c+2}
, and hence itself becomes null at
c=-2
.
With this choice of normalisation,
c=-2
is a perfectly regular value for the
W_{4}
algebra, just one for which the rescaled weight 4 field has zero norm and can be set to zero.

This is the same mechanism for which
c=-22/5
is a perfectly regular value of the central charge for the
W_{3}
algebra, so long as the field
W(z)
is rescaled, and in which the rescaled weight 3 field has zero norm and can be set to zero.

As a consequence, we believe that the symplectic fermion provides realisations of the integrable hierarchies of the
W_{n}
algebras for
n>3
, but in which all the fields of spins greater than 3 are set to zero.

7
The GGE as a line defect

As with the free fermion and Lee-Yang models, the GGE in the symplectic fermion can also be described in the crossed-channel
(in which the Hamiltonian propagates along the defect - see e.g.
24
)
as a purely transmitting defect for the symplectic fermion fields.
This can be seen directly form the exact modular transform (
188
), (
189
). In the case
\lambda=1/2
, the trace without the insertion of
(-1)^{F}
, the transformed partition function can be seen as derived from a set of free fermionic modes with energies determined by a twisted quantisation condition.

We can identify the individual terms in the product, in the case of a right torus,
\hat{\tau}=iL/R
, as coming from a fermion with energy
E^{\pm}_{n}
propagated along the length
L
,

\displaystyle(1+e^{2\pi i\omega^{\pm}_{l}(n)})=(1+e^{-LE^{\pm}_{n}})\;.

(245)

This gives

\displaystyle E^{\pm}_{n}=-\frac{2\pi}{R}{\hat{\tau}\omega^{\pm}(n)}\;.

(246)

If the fermion has the standard dispersion relation,
then the energies are quantised according to

\displaystyle iPR+i\delta_{\pm}(PR)=2\pi ni\;,

(247)

which is the quantisation condition for a free fermion moving on a circle of length
R
in the presence of a purely transmitting defect which imparts a phase factor

\displaystyle i\delta_{\pm}(x)=\pm(2\pi)\sum_{j=2}^{N}(\pm 1)^{j}\alpha_{j}\left(\frac{x}{2\pi}\right)^{j}\;.

(248)

It is natural to associate the transmission factor
\exp(i\delta_{\pm})
with the fields
\chi^{\pm}
, but simply from the partition function we cannot decide which phase factor is associated to which fermion.

8
Outlook

In this work we treated the “other easy case” of constructing the KdV hierarchy and deriving the modular transformation of the associated GGEs, emphasising the necessity of enlarging the set of integrable charges to include the bilinear hierarchy. The first “easy” case was the free fermion studied in
23
, and evidence for the Symplectic Fermion case was given in
21
.

The
W_{1+\infty}
algebra at
c=1
admits a realization in terms of bilinear
bc
ghost fields
2
, and the central charge can be shifted by deforming the stress tensor with the
U(1)
generator without changing the
W_{1+\infty}
algebra itself. Hence the Symplectic Fermion GGE furnishes a
c=-2
realization of the
W_{1+\infty}
characters; constructions at negative integral central charge are discussed in
44
. Dijkgraaf studied the asymptotic modular behaviour of these characters by exploiting a pre-Lie algebra structure that yields a master equation for the modularly transformed characters
15
.
By contrast, equation (
188
) gives an exact expression for the modular
S
-transformation of these characters.

The result (
188
) is proved rigorously here, but only for the half-twisted and untwisted Symplectic Fermion representations; we have not included the
U(1)
charge required to isolate the characters that form the modular-invariant bulk state spaces of the Singlet Model. A natural next step is to determine the transformation properties of the generic twisted sectors, with particular attention to the
U(1)
-neutral sectors of the GGE and relations to the analyses in
37
;
52
.

While the conjecture in
20
for the asymptotic behaviour of the GGE was limited to the case of the
W_{3}
algebra, it has now been proven in full generality
55
, removing the constraint from Dijkgraaf’s analysis in
15
of the existence of a pre-Lie algebra. It still remains a formula for the asymptotic behaviour though, not the exact transform as we have found here. It would obviously be good to try to extend these asymptotic results to exact results.

It would also be worthwhile to extend this programme to other logarithmic theories, such as the symplectic boson or fractional-level WZW models. An argument analogous to Section
4.1
may be unavailable there, but a thermodynamic Bethe ansatz approach (
21
) could serve as an alternative route.

Based on the analysis of Section
6
, it should further be possible to demonstrate that at
c=-2
one can directly show the integrability of the quantum Boussinesq hierarchy in the sense of
13
.

Another concrete direction is to construct explicitly the spectrum and Hamiltonian of the defect Hilbert space associated with the modular transformation, following the methods of
24
.

It would also be compelling to use our transformation formula to derive a Cardy-like formula for the GGE with
W_{0}
inserted and compare it to the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
black hole entropy of
40
. Our results provide a possibility of calculating it at finite
c
.

It would be useful to understand how the Symplectic Fermion GGE is realised on the lattice. In
53
the integrals of motion are understood directly on the lattice (Critical Dense Polymers). Earlier we interpreted the GGE as a translation-invariant line defect, motivated by the free-fermion GGE as a defect
24
. A lattice interpretation of this defect, or an interpretation in terms of the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
spin chain at
q=i
, would therefore be desirable (see e.g.
11
;
3
).

In
22
the thermal correlators of bilinear tensor currents in massive free fermions were shown, in the conformal limit, to correspond to the KdV hierarchy of the
c=\tfrac{1}{2}
free-fermion CFT, and to be described by massive Maass–Jacobi forms. It should be possible to analyse massive symplectic fermions similarly and to obtain analogous modular-type results for currents associated not only with KdV but also with the full Bilinear hierarchy.

Recent work
9
studies GGEs with fractional-spin charges arising in graded integrable field theories with an internal
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
structure. It would be interesting to explore this framework for the Triplet model/Symplectic Fermions, in close analogy with the graded Ising/free-fermion example in that paper. For the graded Lee–Yang model the fractional GGE admits a TBA description derivable from a deformed cubic oscillator via the ODE/IM correspondence
50
; the authors of
9
suggest extending this correspondence to other graded theories. If a graded Triplet model exists, one might therefore expect a corresponding ODE description.

It is natural to ask whether the ODE/IM correspondence can be adapted to GGEs involving KdV charges. The KdV GGE transformation is now known exactly at
c=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
c=-2
(both free theories), so a deformed ODE/IM approach may be tractable in these cases. Both central charges are associated with ODEs having a quadratic potential
17
, suggesting a unified treatment. Indeed,
c=-2
was noted as an “easy” case in
6
(the “free-fermion point”).

The Symplectic Fermion realisation of the
W_{3}
algebra permits the construction of GGEs tied to
W
-algebra hierarchies. Since the ODE/IM correspondence for
W_{N}
algebras is well developed (see e.g.
19
;
18
) and allows extraction of integrals-of-motion eigenvalues
47
, it is natural to seek an extension of ODE/IM to GGEs based on
W
-algebras. The polynomial relations (
189
) (and their analogues in the
c=\tfrac{1}{2}
free-fermion case
23
) admit a TBA interpretation; combined with discussions of reversing the ODE/IM map
33
, this could lead to a precise correspondence for both KdV GGEs and
W
-algebra GGEs.

Appendix A
(Quasi-) Modular Forms

In this appendix we will list the relevant facts about modular forms that appear in this paper. Proofs of the following statements can be found in
59
and most of the notation will be the same.

The full modular group will be denoted by

\Gamma_{1}=\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}).

(249)

We define the congruence subgroup
\Gamma(N)
by

\Gamma(N):=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}|\quad\gamma\equiv\mathbb{I}_{2},\mod N\}.

(250)

Here
\mathbb{I}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix.

A holomorphic function
f(\tau)
define on the upper half plane is said to be a modular form of weight
k
for the group
\Gamma
, which for us is
\Gamma_{1}
or
\Gamma(2)
, if it has the transformation property

f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{k}f(\tau),\quad\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

(251)

The space of weight
k
modular forms for
\Gamma
we call
M_{k}(\Gamma)
. The group
\Gamma_{1}
is generated by the matrices

T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\;,\quad S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\;,

(252)

hence we only need to check that a function transforms as a modular form under

T:\tau\mapsto\tau+1\;,\quad S:\tau\mapsto\frac{-1}{\tau}\;,

(253)

to verify it is an element of
M_{k}(\Gamma_{1})
. The group
\Gamma(2)
is generated by the matrices

\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\;,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\;-\mathbb{I}_{2},

(254)

hence we only need to check that a function transforms as a modular form under

\widetilde{T}:\tau\mapsto\tau+2\;,\quad\widetilde{S}:\tau\mapsto\frac{\tau}{2\tau+1}\;,

(255)

to verify that is belongs to
M_{k}(\Gamma(2))
. The space
M_{2k}(\Gamma_{1})
is generated by the Eisenstein series (see below) and
the space
M_{2k}(\Gamma(2))
is generated by the Jacobi theta functions (see
59
for definitions of the
Jacobi theta functions).

The Eisenstein series
E_{2k}(\tau)
are elements of
M_{k}(\Gamma_{1})
,
k=2,3,...
, and they are defined by

E_{2k}(\tau)=1+\frac{2}{\zeta(1-2k)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2k-1}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\;,\;\;\;q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\;.

(256)

The
k=1
Eisenstein series’,
E_{2}(\tau)
is quasi-modular. It has the following modular transformation property under
\Gamma_{1}

E_{2}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{2}E_{2}(\tau)-\frac{6i}{\pi}c(c\tau+d)\;.

(257)

It is worth noting that, given
\widetilde{T}=T^{2}
and
\widetilde{S}=(TST)^{2}
, one can show that

E_{2k}(2\tau),\;E_{2k}(\tau),\;E_{2k}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})\in M_{2k}(\Gamma(2)).

(258)

We use
E_{2}
to define higher quasi-modular forms by the following. The space of quasi-modular forms of weight
k
and depth
p
for a group
\Gamma
,
\widetilde{M}^{(p)}_{k}(\Gamma)
is define to be

\widetilde{M}^{(p)}_{k}(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{p}M_{k-2r}(\Gamma)\cdot E_{2}^{r}.

(259)

The above is understood in such a way that any element of that set should have a coefficient of
E_{2}^{p}
non-zero. Finally we define the Serre derivative. The Serre derivative acting on a modular form
F_{k}(\tau)
of weight
k
is defined to be

D_{k}F_{k}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{d\tau}F_{k}(\tau)-\frac{k}{12}E_{2}(\tau)F_{k}(\tau)\;.

(260)

By using the transformation of
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{d\tau}=q\frac{d}{dq}
under a modular transform we can see that
D_{k}F_{k}(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
k+2
. From this it is not difficult to see that

D_{0}F_{k}\in\widetilde{M}_{k+2}^{(1)}(\Gamma).

(261)

Appendix B
Properties of Roots

The roots
\omega^{\pm}(\kappa)
satisfy the equation

\omega=\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa):\quad\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{n+1}(\tau^{n}\alpha_{n}\omega^{n})=\kappa.

(262)

We’re assuming that we’re working in the restricted domain (
116
), which we repeat here. Let
\mathbf{Q}_{2K-1}
be the highest odd-spin charge appearing the GGE (
113
), and let there be only finitely many even spin charges appearing in the GGE
\{\mathbf{Q}_{2n_{i}}\}
with spin greater than
2K-1
. Then the restricted domain is the one which has constrained chemical potentials for all of the chemical potential and not just the highest ones.

\begin{split}&\text{Im}(\alpha_{2n-1}\tau^{2n-1})\geq 0,\quad\forall\,\,\,n<K,\\
&\text{Im}(\alpha_{2K-1}\tau^{2K-1})>0,\\
&\text{Im}(\alpha_{2n}\tau^{2n})=0,\quad\forall\,\,\,2n<N.\\
\end{split}

(263)

It is easy to show that if
\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa)
solves the above equations, then so does
-\omega_{l}^{\mp}(-\kappa)
, that is we can pair up roots so that

\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa)=-\omega_{l}^{\mp}(-\kappa).

(264)

We call by
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)
the distinguished root continuously connected to
0
when
\kappa=0
.

Let us show that for all
l
, and all
\kappa>0
, that the roots never hit the real axis. We can show this by contradiction, if any roots were to hit the real axis, then for some
\kappa>0
we would have
\text{Im}(\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa))=0
. Taking the imaginary part of (
262
), we see that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\pm 1)^{n+1}\text{Im}(\tau^{n}\alpha_{n})(\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa))^{n}=0,

(265)

and since we work in the domain (
263
), we require
z_{j}^{\pm}(\omega)=0
, which can only happen if
\omega=0
, a contradiction.

Let us show that for
l=0
, and all
\kappa>0
, that the root
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)
always has negative imaginary part. Assuming
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)
has a series expansion in
\kappa
, we can show by substitution into (
262
) that the leading piece is
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{\alpha_{1}\tau}+O(\kappa^{2})
. Therefore, the domain (
263
) tells us that for small enough
\kappa
,
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)
will have a negative imaginary part, and since the roots never cross the real axis,
\omega_{0}^{\pm}(\kappa)
will always have negative imaginary part.

Let us show that the roots
\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa)
will have opposite sign imaginary parts for
\kappa>0
and
l>0
. This simply follows from that fact that we know that for
\kappa>0
, the roots never hit the real axis, and that
\omega_{l}^{\pm}(0)=-\omega_{l}^{\mp}(0)
. Therefore if
\text{Im}(\omega^{\pm}_{l}(\kappa))>0
, then
\text{Im}(\omega^{\mp}_{l}(\kappa))<0
.

Let us find the asymptotic behaviour of the roots as
\kappa\to\infty
. We can assume that the leading behaviour as
\kappa
becomes large is some power law

\omega_{l}^{\pm}(\kappa)=A^{\pm}\kappa^{\nu_{\pm}}.

(266)

Substituting in to (
263
) gives us the leading order behaviour

(\pm 1)^{N+1}\alpha_{N}(\tau A^{\pm}\kappa^{\nu_{\pm}})^{N}\sim\kappa.

(267)

Hence

\begin{split}\nu_{\pm}&=\frac{1}{N},\quad A^{+}=\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{N}}}{(\alpha_{N}\tau^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}},\quad A^{-}=\frac{e^{\frac{(i\pi+2\pi ik)(N+1)}{N}}}{(\alpha_{N}\tau^{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}},\\
k&=1,...,N-1.\end{split}

(268)

The important thing in our calculation is that the indeed the roots do grow to infinity, and as shown previously, maintain the sign of the imaginary parts as
\kappa>0
grows.

Appendix C
[W_{0},\Lambda_{0}]
at
c=-2

The fact that both the KdV and Boussinesq hierarchies appear as subsets of the same bilinear hierarchy at
c=-2
suggests an apparent contradiction, namely

[W_{0},\Lambda_{0}]=0,

(269)

which is not generically true. We will show that this relation indeed holds only at
c=-2
, and only in representations connected to the Symplectic Fermion (and hence to related orbifold models
12
).

It is well known that the commutator of two zero modes can be expressed as the zero mode of a field. Let
A(z)
and
B(w)
be fields of weight
h_{A}
and
h_{B}
, then by rewriting the planar zero-modes as contour integrals of the fields, and looking at the OPE between
A
and
B
, it is not difficult to show that

[A_{0},B_{0}]=X_{0},

(270)

where
X(z)
is the field associated to the state

|X\rangle=\sum_{p=0}^{h_{A}-1}(-1)^{p}\frac{(1-h_{A})_{p}}{p!\,(2-h_{A}-h_{B})_{p}}L_{-1}^{p}A_{p-h_{A}+1}|B\rangle.

(271)

Setting
A=W
, and
B=\Lambda=(TT)-\frac{3}{10}\partial^{2}T
, we find that

|X\rangle=-\frac{12}{5}\left(L_{-3}W_{-3}-\tfrac{2}{3}W_{-4}L_{-2}+\tfrac{1}{2}W_{-6}\right)|0\rangle.

(272)

The crucial observation is that at
c=-2
, this state becomes null, and so
[W_{0},\Lambda_{0}]=0
. By null we mean that the state satisfies the same conditions of a highest weight state that generates a highest weight representation of the
W_{3}
algebra.

W_{n>0}|X\rangle=L_{n>0}|X\rangle=0,\quad L_{0}|X\rangle=h_{X}|X\rangle,\quad W_{0}|X\rangle=w_{X}|X\rangle

(273)

One can show that there exists another null vector at level
6
. That is

\left(\tfrac{7}{4}L_{-4}L_{-2}+\tfrac{19}{32}L_{-3}^{2}+L_{-2}^{3}-\tfrac{9}{8}W_{-3}^{2}-2L_{-6}\right)|0\rangle.

(274)

Acting with the planar zero-mode of the associated field on a generic highest weight state with weights
h
and
w
and setting the result to zero imposes the following constraint

h^{2}+8h^{3}-9w^{2}=0.

(275)

This is confirmation of an earlier study of
W_{3}
representations
57
at
c=-2
. For a generic twisted representation
\mathbb{L}_{\lambda}
of the Symplectic Fermion, the conformal weight is

h(\lambda)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda),

(276)

and from (
104
) the
W_{0}
eigenvalue is

w(\lambda)=-\tfrac{1}{6}\lambda(\lambda-1)(2\lambda-1).

(277)

It is straightforward to check that these satisfy the above constraint, so indeed all Symplectic Fermion representations of the
W_{3}
algebra exhibit
[W_{0},\Lambda_{0}]=0
.

From this perspective, it becomes natural to ask whether it is “obvious” within the
W_{3}
algebra that all KdV charges commute with
W_{0}
. One definition of the KdV charges is via perturbation of the CFT
51
by the primary field with Kac labels
(1,3)
, denoted
\phi_{(1,3)}
, so that

[\mathbf{I}^{\text{KdV}}_{2n-1},\int\phi_{(1,3)}\,d^{2}z]=0.

(278)

This field has conformal weight

h_{(1,3)}=2t-1,

(279)

where
t
is defined by

c=13-6\left(t+\tfrac{1}{t}\right).

(280)

At
c=-2
, this yields

h_{(1,3)}=0\quad\text{or}\quad 3.

(281)

For generic central charge the two choices are equivalent in that they provide the same set of conserved densities. However, in our case the weight-
3
primary is precisely the field
W(z)
. Thus all KdV charges commute with the mode
W_{0}
.

Appendix D
Pre-Lie Algebra

In this section we will show that the bilinear fields (

For the pre-lie algebra structure, one is only interested in the first and second order poles in the OPE. Consider the following OPE
