Temporal and spatial changes of ecosystem services and their influencing factors in Shule River Basin
Authors: Jianjun Zeng, Ziyang Cheng, Yanqiang Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, the water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage and habitat quality of Shule River Basin were simulated by using InVEST model. Using the method of cold hot spot analysis, the spatial variation characteristics of four ecosystem services in Shule River Basin were revealed. Finally, the dominant and interactive factors of water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage and habitat quality were explored based on the geodetector model. The main research contents and conclusions are as follows: (1) The simulation results showed that the four types of ecosystem services except soil conservation and habitat quality in the Shule River Basin increased to varying degrees during 1980–2020. Water yield increased from 1.9314 × 109 m3 to 5.2143 × 109 m3, an increase of 3.2829 × 109 m3; Soil retention decreased from 5.9453 × 109 t to 5.8708 × 109 t, decreased by 7.4567 × 106 t. Carbon storage increased from 9.4266 × 109 t to 9.5157 × 109 t, an increase of 8.9090 × 106 t; The habitat quality index decreased by 0.0013 from 0.1711 to 0.1698. (2) Ecosystem services show significant spatial differentiation. The cold spots and general areas of ecosystem services are concentrated in the wasteland in the northwest and north of the study area, and the hot spots are mainly distributed in the high-altitude mountains in the southeast and southwest of the study area. (3) Annual precipitation (q = 0.5668), potential evapotranspiration (q = 0.5497) and elevation (q = 0.5471) had great influence on the spatial differentiation of Water yield services, and the explanatory power was more than 50%. It is slope that plays a leading role in influencing the spatial distribution of soil conservation services. The main factors affecting the spatial differentiation of ecosystem services are land use, climate and topography, while the population and social economy have relatively little influence on the spatial differentiation of ecosystem services.Keywords: Ecosystem services, InVEST model, Spatial analysis, Geographic detectorSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences

Introduction

The services provided by natural ecosystems refer to the various forms in which human beings benefit directly or indirectly from their functions, including but not limited to material products and cultural emotions. The scope of services covers food supply, water resource regulation, air purification, climate regulation, disaster prevention, soil conservation, biodiversity maintenance, etc.1–3. The purpose of assessing ecosystem services is to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the state of ecosystems and the interactions between ecosystems and human activities, as well as the degree of human dependence on natural resources. Ecosystem services provide various benefits and benefits for human beings and are the basis for human survival and development4,5

At present, due to the diversity of scholars’ views and research priorities in the process of ecosystem services evaluation, a unified classification method of ecosystem services has not been formed so far. Future research is needed to further refine the classification system while exploring the complex relationship between ecosystem services and human well-being to ensure that ecosystem services can be used sustainably. The InVEST model is a set of model developed by the World Wide Fund for Nature and Stanford University for the Natural Capital project to simulate and evaluate the quality and value of ecosystem service functions6. The InVEST model consists of nearly 20 modules including habitat quality, soil and water conservation, Water yield model and ecosystem services, covering numerous aspects of ecosystem services and meeting the needs for evaluation, simulation and prediction of different functions; moreover, it has strong characteristics such as spatialization, dynamics and visualization. In addition, the InVEST model is also widely used in the study of ecosystem.

The Shule River Basin is located in the westernmost part of the Hexi Corridor in Gansu Province7,8 and is one of the three major inland river basins. The climate of the region is typically continental, with hot summers and cold winters. Due to the scarcity of natural vegetation, low precipitation and high evaporation, the ecological environment is extremely fragile. With the development of society and the intensification of human activities, land and water resources have been extensively exploited and utilized, which has provided more ecosystem services9, but also led to environmental problems such as land desertification and groundwater level decline. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the ecosystem service function of the region10, explore its change rule, and analyze its main influencing factors for promoting the sustainable development of the region’s economy, society and ecology. In order to understand the spatio-temporal evolution of ecosystem services in Shule River Basin. In this study, the ecosystem service function of Shule River Basin in 1980, 2000 and 2020 was simulated and evaluated using InVEST 3.11.0 model. Through the analysis of hot and cold spots, the regions with high (low) values of ecosystem services in Shule River Basin were identified, and the main factors affecting ecosystem services were explored by using the geographic detector model. The research results provide an important theoretical basis for local governments to formulate optimal management strategies for ecosystem service zones.

Research methods

Overview of the study area

The Shule River basin is located in the arid area of northwest China, 92°22′–98°59′ east longitude, 38°1′–42°47′ north latitude (Fig. 1). Shule River Basin is one of the three major inland river basins in Hexi region, covering Yumen City, Dunhuang City, Guazhou County and Subei Mongolian Autonomous County. It borders Qinghai Province to the south, Zhangye City and Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region to the east, Hami City of Xinjiang to the west, and Mongolia to the north. The southern part is located in the Qilian Fold belt, with higher terrain, mainly including middle-high mountain areas with an altitude of 2000–5000 m and 3000 m above sea level. The Shule River Basin belongs to a typical temperate extreme arid climate: the average annual precipitation is 20–50 mm (up to 100–200 mm in the mountainous areas upstream), and the average annual evaporation is 303–3246 mm; the average annual temperature is 7–9 °C, with long hours of sunshine, large temperature differences, and frequent sandstorms. Vegetation is strictly controlled by the water line, showing a significant zonal decrease from the upstream to the downstream and from the riverbank to the Gobi. The runoff is mainly replenished by glacial meltwater and precipitation in the mountainous areas, and the distribution of runoff within the year is extremely uneven. The region has perennial snow, modern glaciers, abundant precipitation and developed river valleys, and is an important birthplace of Shule River. The central part is located in the Hexi Corridor depression zone, the terrain is relatively low. The northern part is located in the Ma Manshan fault block belt, with relatively gentle terrain11–13.

Ecosystem service function of Shule River basin

Interannual variation

Interannual variation of Water yield. Using ArcGIS 10.7 and InVEST model, the spatial distribution maps of Water yield in 1980, 2000 and 2020 of Shule River Basin were generated (see Fig. 2). The total Water yield of Shule River basin in 1980, 2000 and 2020 is 1.9314 × 109m3, 2.1276 × 109 m3 and 5.2143 × 109 m3, respectively, showing an overall increase trend. The water yield per unit area is 15,516.62 m3/km2, 17,092.86 m3/km2 and 41,891.01 m3/km2, respectively. The growth rate of water yield from 1980 to 2000 is small, while the growth rate from 2000 to 2020 is significantly larger. Overall, the water yield increased by 3.2829 × 109 m3 from 1980 to 2020, and the average water yield based on grid cells was 15.51 mm, 17.09 mm and 41.88 mm, respectively, showing an overall upward trend. Average Water yield has increased by 26.23 mm between 1980 and 202037,38.

Spatial distribution of ecosystem services in Shule River Basin from 1980 to 2020.

Interannual variation of soil conservation. The simulation results using the soil conservation module of InVEST model showed that the total soil conservation of Shule River Basin in 1980, 2000 and 2020 was 59.4534 million tons, 59.9514 million tons and 58.7077 million tons, respectively. Soil conservation capacity per unit area was 4776.41t/km2, 4787.49t/km2 and 4716.50t/km2, respectively. The total amount and unit area of soil conservation showed a trend of "first increase and then decrease", the total decrease was 7.4567 million t, and the unit area decreased by 59.91t/km2. From 1980 to 2000, soil conservation increased slightly, with a total increase of 1,559,600 t and an increase of 11.08t/km2 per unit area. However, between 2000 and 2020, soil conservation decreased significantly, with a total decrease of 8,836,300 t and a unit area decrease of 70.99t/km2.

Interannual change of carbon stocks. The carbon reserves of Shule River Basin in 1980, 2000 and 2020 were 942,652,200 tons, 941,842,100 tons and 951,565,200 tons, respectively, showing an overall increasing trend, with a cumulative increase of 8,90,018 tons. This suggests that the increase in carbon stocks within the basin is mainly due to the increase in carbon stocks between 2000 and 2020. The carbon storage per unit area of each period was 7573.17t/km2, 7566.63t/km2 and 7644.75t/km2, respectively, showing an overall increasing trend with an increase of 71.58t/km2.

Interannual variation of habitat quality. From the perspective of time scale, the average habitat quality index in 1980, 2000 and 2020 was 0.1711, 0.1707 and 0.1698, respectively, indicating that the habitat quality was at a low level, and the habitat quality index showed a trend of “gradually decreasing”, indicating that the spatial distribution difference of habitat quality in the Shule River Basin was constantly expanding.

Driving force analysis of ecosystem services in Shule River Basin

Driver selection and processing

Ecosystem services form spatial differentiation characteristics under the dual effects of natural environmental factors and human factors. Natural environmental factors (climate, altitude, terrain, etc.) determine the spatial distribution characteristics of ecosystem services on a large scale, while human factors (population, GDP, etc.) mainly change the way of land use. Based on previous studies40–42, A total of 9 influencing factors including natural environmental factors and social and economic factors were selected: elevation (DEM), Slope, Aspect, average annual temperature (tem), annual precipitation (Pre), potential evapotranspiration (Pet), population (Pop), gross regional product (GDP) and land use type (LUCC).

The driving forces of four ecosystem services, namely annual water output, soil conservation, carbon storage and habitat quality in Shule River Basin in 2020, were analyzed by using the geographical detector model, and factor detection, ecological detection and interactive detection were respectively carried out. In the process of data preparation, GDP elements were rasterized first, and all data projection coordinate systems were unified into Krasovsky_1940_Albers. Secondly, a grid of 3000 m × 3000 m was created in ArcGIS according to the scope of the Shule River basin, and a total of 32,111 fishing points were generated. After that, the driver data (independent variables) and the simulation results of four ecosystem services (dependent variables) were extracted to the corresponding points using the multi-value extraction to the point tool, and the data was imported into Excel. Save as csv format.

Single factor detection

The single factor detection results of ecosystem Water yield services in Shule River Basin are shown in Fig. 6, and each factor has a significant driving effect on the spatial differentiation of Water yield services (result significance index p = 0). By analyzing the driving factors of the spatial differentiation of Water yield service, it is found that annual precipitation has the greatest influence on the spatial differentiation of Water yield, and the explanatory power is 56.68%. The explanatory power of potential evapotranspiration, elevation and average annual temperature was 54.97%, 54.71% and 53.59%, respectively. Slope also has a certain influence on the spatial differentiation of Water yield service, and the explanatory power of the influence factor is 27.89%. Land use type, gross regional product, population and slope direction were less influential, and the explanatory power was lower than 17%.

Single factor detection results.

In addition, although land use type ranked lower in the single factor detection, its interaction with other factors was significantly enhanced, especially the interaction with annual precipitation accounted for 97.84% of the spatial difference of water yield. The interaction between land use type and slope direction was the weakest, with q value of 0.15251.

Interactive detection results.

The detection results of the interaction of driving factors of soil conservation services in the ecosystem of Shule River Basin are shown in Fig. 8, including 36 groups of double factor enhancement and 13 groups of nonlinear enhancement. There was significant interaction between other factors and slope, and it was double factor enhanced with land use type, and q value reached 0.68019. Secondly, the interaction between elevation, average annual temperature, annual precipitation, potential evapotranspiration and land use type is also very strong, and the interaction type is mostly double-factor enhancement.

The detection results of the interaction of driving factors of carbon storage services in the ecosystem of the Shule River Basin are shown in Fig. 8, indicating that the spatial differentiation of carbon storage services in the ecosystem of the Shule River Basin is influenced by both natural and human factors. Among them, the influence of the results is more than 10% of annual precipitation and potential evapotranspiration. In addition, land use type and potential evapotranspiration are the main driving factors for the spatial differentiation of carbon storage services. The interaction detection results show that the q value between them is 0.98423, and the explanatory power is as high as 98.42%, indicating that their interaction has a significant impact on the spatial differentiation of carbon storage services.

The detection results of driver interaction of ecosystem habitat quality services in Shule River Basin have been shown in Fig. 8. The results showed that the effects of each factor on the spatial distribution of habitat quality were nonlinear or double-factor enhanced. Among them, the largest reciprocal detection q value is between climate type factor and other factors, which is between 0.15477 and 0.22062, reflecting the decisive role of climate on the spatial distribution of habitat quality. Slope aspect plays an important role in enhancing the interaction of other factors on the spatial differentiation of habitat quality.

Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, four ecosystem services in Shule River Basin were quantitatively simulated by InVEST model, and the spatial distribution characteristics were studied by ArcGIS spatial statistical method of cold and hot spot analysis. At the same time, the main influencing factors of each ecosystem service are identified with the geographical detector model.

Conclusion

The simulation results showed that the four types of ecosystem services except soil conservation and habitat quality in the Shule River Basin increased to varying degrees during 1980–2020. From 1980 to 2020, the water yield increased from 1.9314 × 109 m3 to 5.2143 × 109 m3, an increase of 3.2829 × 109 m3, indicating the improvement of water resources management, including soil and water conservation measures and effective implementation of water resources management. In the upper reaches of the Shule River in the Qilian Mountains, a ban on development and restoration efforts, as well as reforestation and grass planting, have been implemented to protect natural forests, shrublands and grasslands, and to enhance water conservation and soil erosion control capabilities. Along both banks of the river and around reservoirs, protective forests and aquatic plants have been planted to stabilize the riverbanks and purify the water quality. The entire basin has fully adopted efficient water-saving technologies such as drip irrigation, pipe irrigation, micro-irrigation, water and fertilizer integration, ridge-film and furrow irrigation, and has established water-saving demonstration areas. The Water yield capacity of different land types in descending order is unused land, grassland, forest land, cultivated land, river/water area. Soil conservation decreased from 5.9453 × 109 t to 5.8708 × 109 t, a decrease of 7.4567 × 106 t. The decrease in soil conservation may reflect land use changes, indicating that soil conservation is higher in the southern region, such as agricultural expansion, urbanization or land degradation. The central and northern regions showed low soil retention. With the increase of precipitation, soil conservation decreased. With the increase of altitude, soil retention increased. Carbon storage increased from 9.4266 × 109 t to 9.5157 × 109 t, an increase of 8.9090 × 106 t. The increase of carbon storage was due to forest regeneration or improvement of forest management practices and implementation of forest protection measures. The increase in vegetation area leads to an increase in carbon storage. The Habitat quality index decreased by 0.0013 from 0.1711 to 0.1698, indicating degradation or destruction of ecosystems, such as loss of biodiversity, land degradation or pollution problems. During the study period, the habitat quality in the Shule River Basin improved slightly on the whole, but the increase rate was relatively small, showing a high and low distribution in space. Forest land, grassland, cultivated land, river/water area, unused land, and construction land are ranked according to the average annual habitat quality index of different land classes. The results showed that the habitat quality index remained unchanged in 93.19% of the Shule River Basin region, improved in 3.14% of the region, and decreased in 3.64% of the region, indicating that the overall habitat quality was declining. Previous studies have also shown that the Water yield in this region has significantly increased under the combined influence of climate and LULC changes43 (Yue et al., 2022).This might be related to the influencing factors of the Water yield volume in this basin. Some studies also suggest that the influencing factors of Water yield, ranked by importance, are as follows: precipitation > Digital Elevation Model (DEM) > temperature > slope > gross domestic product > land use type > slope direction > population density. The variation in Water yield is consistent with the results of predecessors, which can demonstrate the reliability of the results.

This is mainly because the Qilian Mountains are distributed in the south of the study area, and the Mamane Mountains are distributed in the north. The mountain area is dominated by a large area of woodland and grassland, so the quality of various ecosystem services is high. The analysis of the influencing factors of four ecosystem services in the Shule River Basin in 2020 by using the geographical detector model found that land use type and climate factors were the main factors affecting the spatial differentiation of ecosystem services in the Shule River Basin. The single factor detection results showed that annual precipitation (q = 0.5668), potential evapotranspiration (q = 0.5497) and elevation (q = 0.5471) had strong explanatory power for the spatial differentiation of Water yield services, and the explanatory power was more than 50%. Land use type greatly shaped the spatial distribution of carbon storage and habitat quality services. Slope is the main factor affecting soil conservation services. Through interaction analysis, it is found that annual precipitation and potential evapotranspiration are the main interaction factors affecting the spatial distribution of Water yield services.

Discussion

Integrated InVEST model was used to evaluate water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage and habitat quality, and cold hot spot analysis and geographic detector model were used to reveal the spatial heterogeneity of different ecosystem services. This provides a scientific basis for regional ecological management. In the future, it is necessary to fully grasp the integrity of ecosystem services in Shule River basin.

Integrated InVEST model was used to evaluate water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage and habitat quality, and cold hot spot analysis and geographic detector model were used to reveal the spatial heterogeneity of different ecosystem services. This provides a scientific basis for regional ecological management. In the future, it is necessary to fully grasp the integrity of ecosystem services in Shule River basin.

Data from 1980, 2000 and 2020 were used in this study. Such long-term data analysis can reveal the dynamic change rules of ecosystem services in different periods, and provide historical data support for ecological protection. In the future, more time nodes can be added to reflect the dynamic changes of ecosystem services more comprehensively. The model has high requirements for the accuracy and consistency of the input data, especially for spatial data such as land use, meteorology and soil. In this study, due to the accuracy issues of some historical data, it may lead to certain uncertainties in the model’s output results. At the same time, the water conservation module of the InVEST model relies on a simplified hydrological process and fails to fully consider complex hydrological processes such as groundwater recharge and precipitation variability, which may result in an underestimation or overestimation of the water conservation results. Therefore, future research needs to combine other hydrological models and ecological process models to further comprehensively evaluate the ecosystem service functions of the watershed, in order to reduce uncertainties, improve assessment accuracy and reliability.

From a modeling perspective, soil erosion within the watershed also includes wind erosion and freeze–thaw erosion, but in this study, only the results of precipitation erosion were considered; the carbon storage and habitat quality modules only took into account the mutual influence and interaction between different land types, without considering other biological factors. Since the Water yield, soil conservation, carbon reserves, and habitat quality in the InVEST model are calculated independently in separate modules, the relationships among them are not clearly defined in the study. Although soil is the largest carbon reservoir, in this study it was not possible to determine the relationship between carbon storage and soil retention.
