Orderings of Generalized k k -Markov Numbers
Esther Banaian, Min Huang

Abstract
A k k -Markov number is a positive integer that appears in a positive integral solution to the Diophantine equation x 2 + y 2 + z 2 + k ⁡ ( x ​ y + x ​ z + y ​ z ) = ( 3 + 3 ​ k ) ​ x ​ y ​ z x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+k(xy+xz+yz)=(3+3k)xyz . This equation was introduced by Gyoda and Matsushita. When k = 0 k=0 , this definition recovers that of ordinary Markov numbers. The set of k k -Markov numbers can be indexed by pairs of coprime positive integers. There is a consistent way to label non-coprime pairs with positive integers as well, yielding a larger set of “generalized k k -Markov numbers.” In this paper, we classify lines along which the generalized k k -Markov numbers grow monotonically, extending work in the ordinary case by Lee-Li-Rabideau-Schiffler and by the second author. We find that, as k k grows, the k k -Markov numbers are more likely to be monotonic along a random line. This gives evidence that a k k -version of Frobenius’ uniqueness conjecture, which has been proposed by Gyoda and Maruyama, could be true.

Introduction

A
Markov number‌
(or Markoff number) is a positive integer that appears in a solution to the ‌
Markov equation‌

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=3xyz,

first investigated by Markoff in his seminal works of 1879 and 1880. A triple which satisfies the Markov equation is called a
Markov triple
. As a classical subject in number theory, Markov numbers exhibit deep connections to a diverse array of mathematical disciplines, including combinatorics, hyperbolic geometry, Diophantine approximation theory, and cluster algebras.

A driving force in the study of Markov numbers is the uniqueness conjecture, posed by Frobenius in 1913, which states that every Markov number is the largest number in a unique Markov triple. The conjecture remains open today, although interesting solutions to partial results have been plentiful; see
[

There is a common method for indexing Markov numbers with the numbers in
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
. One way in which this can be described is by comparing a tree structure associated to each set. First, Markov triples can be ordered using
Vieta jumps
. It is straightforward to verify that, if

is a Markov triple, then so is

(x,\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{z},y)=(x,3xy-z,y).

Using such replacements, Markov triples populate a nearly 3-regular tree with root

The Uniqueness Conjecture would imply that Markov numbers induce a total ordering on

Preliminaries

2.1
Fence Posets

Throughout the paper, we fix a nonnegative integer
k
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the lattice in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
consisting of all lines of slope
0,-1,
and
\infty
which pass through integral points. We will regard each line as a sequence of line segments between consecutive integral points.

A
fence poset
is one whose Hasse diagram is a path graph. We will associate one fence poset
\mathcal{P}_{(p,q)}
to each point
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In the next section, we will use these posets to associate a positive integer to each integral point.

First, we will assign a fence poset to any curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
based on its intersections with
\mathcal{L}
. We will always consider curves to have an orientation.

Definition 2
.

Let
\gamma
be a curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The following process will define a fence poset
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma},\succeq)
.
For clarity, in the construction we will label elements of
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
by
(\tau,i)
where
\tau
is a line segment in
\mathcal{L}
which
\gamma
crosses and
0\leq i\leq k
.

(1)

For every intersection of
\gamma
with a line segment in
\mathcal{L}
, we will have a chain of
k+1
elements. If this intersection point lies strictly closer to the endpoint of
\tau
which lies to the right of
\gamma

(\tau,0)\succ(\tau,1)\succ\cdots\succ(\tau,k)
. Otherwise, the chain is of the form
(\tau,0)\prec(\tau,1)\prec\cdots\prec(\tau,k)
.

(2)

Suppose
\gamma
crosses line segments
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
consecutively in this order. If the common endpoint of
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
lies to the right of
\gamma
, then we introduce the relation
(\tau,k)\succ(\tau^{\prime},0)
. Otherwise, the common endpoint of
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
lies to the left of
\gamma
and we introduce the relation
(\tau,k)\prec(\tau^{\prime},0)

In particular, if
\gamma
does not intersect any line segments in
\mathcal{L}
, then
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}=\emptyset
.

We will be interested in the posets associated to a family of arcs, one for each pair of points in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and we will be particularly interested in those where one of the points is the origin.

If
\gcd(|p|,|q|)=1

.

A key tool for the proofs of our main results is the following generalized Ptolemy relation which the

Proposition 14

Let

be four distinct points such that one of the following holds.

(a)

The segments
\overline{AB}
,
\overline{BC}
,
\overline{CD}
, and
\overline{DA}

form a convex quadrilateral with diagonals
\overline{AC}
and
\overline{BD}

(b)

,

, and

are non-collinear (so that

\overline{BD}

(c)

, and

lies on
\overline{BD}

Then,

Our proof for this critical proposition will require several tools. Part (a) of Proposition
14
was shown in
[
6
]
using “poset skein relations”. In order to show part (b), we will introduce one of these relations.

In order to concisely reference elements and subposets of a fence poset, we introduce an indexing. If
\mathcal{P}
is a fence poset on
h
elements, we refer to these elements as
\mathcal{P}(1),\mathcal{P}(2),\ldots,\mathcal{P}(h)
in such a way that
\mathcal{P}(i)
has cover relations with
\mathcal{P}(i-1)
and
\mathcal{P}(i+1)
, if these elements exist. Unless
h=1
, there are two ways to introduce this indexing; informally, these consist of reading the Hasse diagram of the fence poset left-to-right or right-to-left. Using this indexing, we can define subposets
\mathcal{P}[a,b]=\{\mathcal{P}(i):a\leq i\leq b\}
. In the following, we define
\mathcal{P}[b+1,b]=\emptyset
. The phrasing in the following definition is meant to match previous work by the first author on which this is based (see
[
6
,
3
]
).

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be two fence posets. Let

. Choose an integer

. Assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{P}_{1}(j)\succ\mathcal{P}_{1}(j+1)
. Define the

of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
with respect to
j
to be the collection of posets
\mathcal{P}_{3},\mathcal{P}_{4},\mathcal{P}_{5},
and
\mathcal{P}_{6}
defined as follows.

•

Let
\mathcal{P}_{3}
be the transitive closure of taking the subposets
\mathcal{P}_{1}[1,j]\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
and setting
\mathcal{P}_{1}(j)\prec\mathcal{P}_{2}(1)
.

•

Let

be the smallest integer such that
\mathcal{P}_{1}(j+1)\not\succ\mathcal{P}_{1}(u)

. Let
\mathcal{P}_{4}
be the subposet
\mathcal{P}_{1}[u,h_{1}]

•

Let

and setting
\mathcal{P}_{1}(j+1)\succ\mathcal{P}_{2}(1)

.

Let the set of order ideals of a poset

be

This can be shown by translating the results in

to fence posets. For the benefit of the reader, we include a proof. Let

\displaystyle|AD|_{k}\cdot|BC|_{k}=\alpha_{(p,q)}^{2}\big(\beta_{(p,q)}^{s+2t+u}+\beta_{(p,q)}^{-s-2t-u}-\beta_{(p,q)}^{s+u}-\beta_{(p,q)}^{-s-u}\big).

Therefore, we can conclude in this case that
|AC|_{k}\cdot|BD|_{k}=|AB|_{k}\cdot|CD|_{k}+|AD|_{k}\cdot|BC|_{k}
.
∎

4
Special Subsequences

The region
\mathcal{R}
is bordered by the lines
y=0
and
y=x
. It will be advantageous to consider the generalized
k
-Markov numbers along and near these lines.

We begin by considering the
k
-Markov numbers sequences labeled by the sequences
\{\frac{1}{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{\frac{n}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0}
. It is well-known that the numbers
m^{(0)}_{\frac{1}{n}}
are odd-indexed Fibonacci numbers and
m^{(0)}_{\frac{n}{n+1}}
are odd-indexed Pell numbers. When
k=0
, Proposition
19
recovers well-known recurrences and formulae concerning these numbers.

In the following, given two infinite sequences
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
, we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=1
.

Proposition 19
.

Let
k\geq 0
.

(1)

The sequence
\{f_{n}\}
with
f_{n}:=m_{\frac{1}{n}}^{(k)}
is given by initial conditions
f_{0}=1,f_{1}=k+2
and for all
n\geq 2
,

f_{n}=(2k+3)f_{n-1}-f_{n-2}-k.

Consequently, if
\alpha:=4k^{2}+12k+5
,
f_{n}
is given by

\frac{k}{1+2k}+\frac{2k^{2}+3k+1+(1+k)\sqrt{\alpha}}{2(1+2k)\sqrt{\alpha}}\bigg(\frac{3+2k+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{n}+\frac{-(2k^{2}+3k+1)+(1+k)\sqrt{\alpha}}{2(1+2k)\sqrt{\alpha}}\bigg(\frac{3+2k-\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{n}

so that asymptotically,

f_{n}\sim\frac{2k^{2}+3k+1+(1+k)\sqrt{\alpha}}{2(1+2k)\sqrt{\alpha}}\bigg(\frac{3+2k+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{n}.

(2)

The sequence
\{g_{n}\}
with
g_{n}:=m_{\frac{n}{n+1}}^{(k)}
is given by initial conditions
g_{0}=1,g_{1}=2k^{2}+6k+5
and for all
n\geq 2
,

g_{n}=(3k^{2}+8k+6)g_{n-1}-g_{n-2}-k(k+2).

Consequently, if
\beta:=3k^{2}+8k+6
and
\delta:=3k^{4}+17k^{3}+34k^{2}+28k+8
,
g_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{k}{3k+2}

\displaystyle+\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(\beta^{2}-4)+\delta\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{(\beta-2)(\beta^{2}-4)}\bigg(\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(\beta^{2}-4)-\delta\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{(\beta-2)(\beta^{2}-4)}\bigg(\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{n}

so that asymptotically,

g_{n}\sim\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(\beta^{2}-4)+\delta\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{(\beta-2)(\beta^{2}-4)}\bigg(\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{n}.

Proof.

First, consider part (1). Since for
n\geq 2
,
(\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{n-1},\frac{1}{n-2})
and
(\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n-1})
are adjacent Farey triples and
m_{\frac{0}{1}}^{(k)}=1
, by
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
we have the following relationship among
f_{n-2},f_{n-1},
and
f_{n}
,

f_{n}=\frac{f_{n-1}^{2}+kf_{n-1}+1}{f_{n-2}}.

(3)

Since
(1,f_{n-1},f_{n-2})
is a
k
-Markov triple, we have

f_{n-1}^{2}+kf_{n-1}+1=(2k+3)f_{n-2}f_{n-1}-f_{n-2}^{2}-kf_{n-2},

and by substituting the right hand side into Equation
3
the first part of the claim follows. The remainder of the statement can be shown with standard methods involving recurrence relations. Part (2) can be proven similarly, using Farey triples
(\frac{n-1}{n},\frac{n}{n+1},\frac{1}{1})
.

∎

In
[
15
]
, the second author showed the even-indexed Fibonacci and Pell numbers coincide with generalized Markov numbers
\{m^{(0)}_{(n,0)}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{m^{(0)}_{(n,n)}\}_{n\geq 0}
respectively. We will consider the analogue of these sequences for general
k
. First, we introduce a general recurrence for generalized Markov numbers along a fixed line.

Lemma 20
.

(1)

The sequence
m^{(k)}_{(n,0)}
is given by initial conditions
m^{(k)}_{(0,0)}=0,m^{(k)}_{(1,0)}=1
and for all
n\geq 2
,

m^{(k)}_{(n,0)}=(2k+3)m^{(k)}_{(n-1,0)}-m^{(k)}_{(n-2,0)}.

(2)

The sequence
m^{(k)}_{(n,n)}
is given by initial conditions
m^{(k)}_{(0,0)}=0,m^{(k)}_{(1,1)}=k+2
and for all
n\geq 2
,

m^{(k)}_{(n,n)}=(3k^{2}+8k+6)m^{(k)}_{(n-1,n-1)}-m^{(k)}_{(n-2,n-2)}.

Proof.

Both parts are consequences of Proposition
18
.
∎

In the ordinary case, the numbers
m^{(0)}_{(n,1)}
and
m^{(0)}_{(n,0)}
intertwine as the Fibonnaci numbers while the numbers
m^{(0)}_{(n,n-1)}
and
m^{(0)}_{(n,n)}
intertwine as the Pell numbers. We show an analogous relationship in our setting.

Proposition 21
.

For all
n\geq 1
, we have

m^{(k)}_{(n,1)}=(k+1)m^{(k)}_{(n,0)}+m^{(k)}_{(n-1,1)}

and

m^{(k)}_{(n+1,n)}=2(k+1)m^{(k)}_{(n,n)}+m^{(k)}_{(n,n-1)}.

Proof.

One can check directly that each statement is true for
n=1,2
, and show the inductive step using Proposition
19
and Lemma
20
.
∎

Now, with our understanding of the generalized
k
-Markov numbers near the boundary of
\mathcal{R}
, we define two important bounds.
For any two coprime positive integers
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, define the constants
S(a_{1},a_{2})_{\pm}
by

S(a_{1},a_{2})_{-}:=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{m^{(k)}_{\bigl(q(1+a_{1})+a_{2},\;1\bigr)}}{m^{(k)}_{\bigl(q(1+a_{1}),\;1+a_{1}\bigr)}},\qquad S(a_{1},a_{2})_{+}:=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{m^{(k)}_{\bigl((q+1)(1+a_{1}+a_{2}),\;q(1+a_{1}+a_{2})\bigr)}}{m^{(k)}_{\bigl(q(1+a_{1}+a_{2})+a_{1}+1,\;q(1+a_{1}+a_{2})+a_{1}\bigr)}}.

(4)

Lemma 22
.

Let
\alpha=4k^{2}+12k+5
,
\beta=3k^{2}+8k+6
and
\delta=3k^{4}+17k^{3}+34k^{2}+28k+8
as in Proposition
19
and let
A:=\frac{2k^{2}+3k+1+(1+k)\sqrt{\alpha}}{2(1+2k)\sqrt{\alpha}}
,
B:=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(\beta^{2}-4)+\delta\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{(\beta-2)(\beta^{2}-4)}
.
Then, for any two coprime positive integers
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, we have

S(a_{1},a_{2})_{-}=((3+3k)A)^{-a_{1}}\bigg(\frac{3+2k+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{a_{2}}

(5)

and

S(a_{1},a_{2})_{+}=((3+3k)B)^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\bigg(\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{-a_{1}}.

(6)

Proof.

We first look at
S(a_{1},a_{2})_{-}
. By Proposition
19
, we have

m^{(k)}_{(q(1+a_{1})+a_{2},1)}\sim A\bigg(\frac{3+2k+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{q(1+a_{1})+a_{2}}.

Next,
m^{(k)}_{(q(1+a_{1}),1+a_{1})}
is the
(1+a_{1})
-th term in the sequence
m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}=m^{(k)}_{(q,1)},m^{(k)}_{(2q,2)},\ldots
. By Proposition
18
, if
\eta=(3+3k)m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}-k
, we have

m^{(k)}_{(q(1+a_{1}),1+a_{1})}=\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\bigg(\frac{\eta+\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{1+a_{1}}-\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\bigg(\frac{\eta-\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{1+a_{1}}

Now, as a function in positive integers
q
, we have

\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\left(\frac{\eta-\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}{2}\right)^{1+a_{1}}}{\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\left(\frac{\eta+\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}{2}\right)^{1+a_{1}}}=0

so that

m^{(k)}_{(q(1+a_{1}),1+a_{1})}\sim\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\bigg(\frac{\eta+\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}{2}\bigg)^{1+a_{1}}\sim\frac{m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}}\eta^{1+a_{1}}}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}-4}}\sim(3+3k)^{a_{1}}(m^{(k)}_{\frac{1}{q}})^{1+a_{1}}

and we conclude using Proposition
19
again

m^{(k)}_{(q(1+a_{1}),1+a_{1})}\sim(3+3k)^{a_{1}}A^{a_{1}+1}\bigg(\frac{3+2k+\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)^{q(1+a_{1})}.

At this point, the final statement follows readily.

The identity (
6
) for
S(a_{1},a_{2})_{+}
can be proved by an analogous argument.
∎

5
Monotonicity of the generalized
k
-Markov numbers

5.1
Monotonicity along horizontal and vertical lines

In this subsection, we study the monotonicity of the generalized
k
-Markov numbers along horizontal lines
\ell:y=b
and vertical lines
\ell:x=n
, where
b,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. This extends the main result of the first author
[
6
]
to generalized
k
-Markov numbers.

For any
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
p\geq 0,q\geq 0
and
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
,
we define the horizontal and vertical ratios

h(p,q):=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}},\qquad v(p,q):=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}.

Lemma 23
.

If
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
p\geq 0
and
q>0
, then

h(p,q)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}<\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+2,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}=h(p+1,q).

and if
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
p>0
and
q\geq 0
, then

v(p,q)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}<\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+2)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}=v(p,q+1).

Proof.

Consider the points
A=(0,0)
,
B=(p+1,q)
,
C=(p+2,q)
, and
D=(1,0)
where
(p,q)
is as in the statement.
Since
q>0
, these four points form a convex quadrilateral satisfying condition (a) of Proposition
14
, and from this Proposition we have

|AC|_{k}\cdot|BD|_{k}>|AB|_{k}\cdot|CD|_{k},

since all terms are positive and
|AD|_{k}\cdot|BC|_{k}>0
.
Using the identities

|AC|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p+2,q)},\quad|BD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q)},\quad|AB|_{k}=|CD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)},

we can rearrange our inequality to find

\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}<\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+2,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}},

which is exactly the inequality
h(p,q)<h(p+1,q)
.

For the second inequality, set
A=(0,0)
,
B=(p,q+1)
,
C=(p,q+2)
, and
D=(0,1)
.
Again, since
p>0
, these points form a convex quadrilateral satisfying condition (a) of Proposition
14
.
The Ptolemy inequality gives

|AC|_{k}\cdot|BD|_{k}>|AB|_{k}\cdot|CD|_{k},

and with

|AC|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q+2)},\quad|BD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q)},\quad|AB|_{k}=|CD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)},

we deduce

\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}<\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+2)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}},

i.e.,
v(p,q)<v(p,q+1)
, as required.
∎

Notice that if we considered the case
q=0
in part (1) of Lemma
23
, then the statement would be false. For example, if
k=0
, so that
m_{(n,0)}=F_{2n}
, i.e. the
(2n)
-th Fibonacci number, we have the well-known identity
F_{2n+2}F_{2n-2}-F_{2n}^{2}=-1
, implying the
h(p,0)
sequence decreases when
k=0
.

Lemma 24
.

If
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
p\geq 0,q\geq 0
and
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
, then

m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}\,m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}>m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}\,m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q+1)}.

Consequently,

h(p,q)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}>\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}=h(p,q+1),

and

v(p,q)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}}>\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q+1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}}=v(p+1,q).

Proof.

Let
A=(0,0)
,
B=(p+1,q+1)
,
C=(p,q+1)
, and
D=(0,1)
.
These points satisfy condition (i) (in case
q>0
) or (ii) (in case
q=0
) of Proposition
14
.
Applying the Ptolemy inequality yields

|AC|_{k}\cdot|BD|_{k}>|AB|_{k}\cdot|CD|_{k}.

Using the identifications

|AC|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)},\quad|BD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)},\quad|AB|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q+1)},\quad|CD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{(p,q)},

we obtain the desired inequality

m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q)}\,m^{(k)}_{(p,q+1)}>m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}\,m^{(k)}_{(p+1,q+1)}.

Dividing both sides appropriately yields the stated comparisons for
h(p,q)
and
v(p,q)
.
∎

Corollary 25
.

For every
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}
, the following bounds hold:

h(q,q)\leq h(p,q)\leq h(p,1),\qquad v(p,0)\leq v(p,q)\leq v(p,p-1).

(7)

In particular, for all
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}
,
h(p,q)>1
and
v(p,q)>1
.

Proof.

The inequalities in (
7
) follow by iteratively applying Lemmas
23
and
24
. Specifically, we do the following.

•

Fixing
q
, we can use Lemma
23
to show
h(p,q)<h(p-1,q)<\cdots<h(q,q)
. Similarly fixing
p
we can use Lemma
24
to show
h(p,q)>h(p,q-1)>\cdots>h(p,1)
.

•

A similar argument applies to
v(p,q)
, yielding
v(p,0)\leq v(p,q)\leq v(p,p-1)
.

To establish the strict lower bounds, we observe that by Lemma
21
(see also
[
6
]
), the boundary terms satisfy

h(q,q)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(q+1,q)}}{m^{(k)}_{(q,q)}}>1\quad\text{and}\quad v(p,0)=\frac{m^{(k)}_{(p,1)}}{m^{(k)}_{(p,0)}}>1.

Combining these strict inequalities with the bounds in (
7
) immediately yields
h(p,q)>1
and
v(p,q)>1
for all
(p,q)\in\mathcal{R}
.
∎

The following proposition is the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 26
.

Let
\ell
be a horizontal line
y=b
or a vertical line
x=n
, where
b,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then the generalized
k
-Markov numbers are strictly increasing along
\ell
with respect to the enumeration of points in
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

The ratio of consecutive points on the line
y=b
, i.e.
(p,b)
and
(p+1,b)
, is
h(p,b)
, and by Corollary
25
, the sequence is strictly increasing. The same can be said for points along the line
x=n
, using the fact that
v(n,q)>1
for all
(n,q)\in\mathcal{R}
with
q<n
.
∎

One can also use Lemma
20
to see that the sequence
m^{(k)}_{(p,0)}
is increasing.
The following corollary is immediate from Proposition
26
.

Corollary 27
.

Let
\ell:y=ax+b
be a line with rational slope and intercept, i.e.,
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
.
If
a>0
, then the generalized
k
-Markov numbers are strictly increasing as functions of the
x
-coordinate along
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
.

One can also use Lemma
20
to see that the sequence
m^{(k)}_{(p,p)}
is increasing.

5.2
Monotonicity along arbitrary lines

In this section, we focus on characterizing all lines
\ell:y=ax+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
along which the
k
-Markov numbers are monotonic.
By Proposition
26
(covering horizontal lines) and Corollary
27
(which establishes strict increase for lines with positive slope), it suffices to consider the case
a<0
.
Moreover, we may assume
b>0
, since otherwise
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
.

We can therefore write
a=-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
for some coprime positive integers
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Because
a<0
, the intersection
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
is finite; denote its cardinality by
n(\ell):=|\ell\cap\mathcal{R}|.

Throughout this subsection, we order the points of
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
using their
x
-coordinates. That is, set

\mathbf{p}_{1}=(p_{1},q_{1}),\ \mathbf{p}_{2}=(p_{2},q_{2}),\ \dots,\ \mathbf{p}_{n(\ell)}=(p_{n(\ell)},q_{n(\ell)}),

so that
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{n(\ell)}
.

Since the slope
a
is rational and
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the set
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
forms an arithmetic progression in the integer lattice. Specifically, for any three consecutive points

\mathbf{p}_{i}=(p_{i},q_{i}),\quad\mathbf{p}_{i+1}=(p_{i+1},q_{i+1}),\quad\mathbf{p}_{i+2}=(p_{i+2},q_{i+2}),

with
1\leq i\leq n(\ell)-2
, we have

p_{i+1}=p_{i}+a_{2},\quad q_{i+1}=q_{i}-a_{1},\qquad p_{i+2}=p_{i+1}+a_{2},\quad q_{i+2}=q_{i+1}-a_{1}.

For each index
i
with
1\leq i<n(\ell)
, we define the
i
-th ratio of the generalized
k
-Markov numbers along
\ell
by

\operatorname{Ratio}_{i}(\ell):=\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}}.

We will be particularly interested in the
first
and
last ratios
:

\operatorname{Ratio}_{\mathrm{first}}(\ell):=\operatorname{Ratio}_{1}(\ell),\qquad\operatorname{Ratio}_{\mathrm{last}}(\ell):=\operatorname{Ratio}_{\,n(\ell)-1}(\ell).

(8)

Proposition 28
.

Let
\ell:y=ax+b
be a line with rational slope and intercept, where
a=-\dfrac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
for coprime positive integers
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then for every index
i
with
1\leq i\leq n(\ell)-2
, we have

\operatorname{Ratio}_{i}(\ell)<\operatorname{Ratio}_{i+1}(\ell).

Consequently, the sequence of generalized
k
-Markov numbers along
\ell\cap\mathcal{R}
is strictly increasing (respectively, strictly decreasing) as a function of the
x
-coordinate if and only if

\operatorname{Ratio}_{\mathrm{first}}(\ell)>1\quad\text{(respectively, }\operatorname{Ratio}_{\mathrm{last}}(\ell)<1\text{)}.

Proof.

Set
A=O=(0,0)
,
B=\mathbf{p}_{i+1}
,
C=\mathbf{p}_{i+2}
, and
D=(a_{2},-a_{1})
.
These four points form a convex quadrilateral satisfying condition (a) of Proposition
14
. Applying the Ptolemy inequality yields

|AC|_{k}\cdot|BD|_{k}>|AB|_{k}\cdot|CD|_{k}.

Using the identifications

|AC|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+2}},\quad|BD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i}},\quad|AB|_{k}=|CD|_{k}=m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}},

we obtain

m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+2}}\,m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}>\bigl(m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}}\bigr)^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}\,m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}}>0
, we deduce

\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+2}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}}}>\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i+1}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}},

which is precisely
\operatorname{Ratio}_{i+1}(\ell)>\operatorname{Ratio}_{i}(\ell)
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 29
.

Let
\mathbf{p}_{1}=(p_{1},q_{1})
,
\mathbf{p}_{2}=(p_{2},q_{2})
,

\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}=(p^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{1})
, and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}=(p^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime}_{2})

be four points in
\mathcal{R}
such that

p_{1}<p_{2},\quad q_{1}>q_{2},\qquad p^{\prime}_{1}<p^{\prime}_{2},\quad q^{\prime}_{1}>q^{\prime}_{2},\qquad\text{and}\quad p_{2}<p^{\prime}_{2}.

Assume further that
\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}
form a parallelogram; equivalently,

\mathbf{p}_{2}-\mathbf{p}_{1}=\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}.

Then the following hold:

(1)

If the segments
\overline{O\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}
and
\overline{O^{\prime}\mathbf{p}_{2}}
intersect in their interiors or at
\mathbf{p}_{2}
, where
O=(0,0)
and
O^{\prime}=(p_{2}-p_{1},\,q_{2}-q_{1})
(for example, when the common slope of
\overline{\mathbf{p}_{1}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{p}_{2}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}
is
0
), then

\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{2}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}}<\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}}}.

(2)

If the segments
\overline{O\mathbf{p}_{2}}
and
\overline{O^{\prime}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}
intersect in their interiors or at
\mathbf{p}_{2}
, where
O=(0,0)
and
O^{\prime}=(p_{2}-p_{1},\,q_{2}-q_{1})
(for example, when the common slope of
\overline{\mathbf{p}_{1}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{p}_{2}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}
is
1
), then

\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{2}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}}>\frac{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{2}}}{m^{(k)}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{1}}}.

Proof.

(1). Let

This data reflects general properties of these functions. Both functions are increasing for

and both converge to

. Therefore, the “constant sum” ordering is a sort of extreme instance of a monotonically increasing line. The fact that the two functions converge to the same value implies the difference between their values,

, converges to 0. Therefore, as
k
increases past

, the

-Markov numbers are more likely to grow monotonically along an arbitrary line.

This behavior makes a
k
-version of the Uniqueness Conjecture even more likely to be true than the ordinary version. For example, in
[
18
, Corollary 1.5]
, Lee, Li, Rabideau, and Schiffler give an upper bound for the number of generalized Markov numbers
m_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
which could be equal to a fixed number
m_{(p,q)}
. This is a function of
p
and
q
and comes from estimating the number of integral points in
\mathcal{R}
which lie in a wedge with vertex
(p,q)
and bounded by lines of slopes
-\frac{5}{4}
and
-\frac{8}{7}
, close approximations to
L(0)
and
U(0)
. Therefore, for any fixed Markov number, one can compute its uniqueness with a finite calculation.

We can repeat these calculations for general
L(k)
and
U(k)
, and this possible number of integral points will decrease. For example, from
[
18
, Corollary 1.5]
, there are at most 832 Markov numbers equal to
m_{\frac{1009}{9973}}
, but the number of
10000
-Markov numbers which could be equal to
m^{(10000)}_{\frac{1009}{9973}}
is at most 23.

Recall one way to prove the Uniqueness Conjecture would be to show that the partial order
(\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1],\prec)
, given by
\frac{q}{p}\prec\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}
whenever
m_{\frac{q}{p}}<m_{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}}
, is a total order. Showing the generalized conjecture in
[
18
]
would entail exhibiting such a total order on all points in
\mathcal{R}
.

Gyoda and Maruyama conjectured that the Uniqueness Conjecture remains true for
k
-Markov numbers in
[
12
, Conjecture 1.8]
. Accordingly, one can consider the partial order
(\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1],\prec^{(k)})
given by
\frac{q}{p}\prec^{(k)}\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}
whenever
m^{(k)}_{\frac{q}{p}}<m^{(k)}_{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}}
. The first author asked whether the partial orders

were the same for varying

in
[

. For instance, by
[
12
, Theorem 2.9]
,

, so it is immediate that
\prec^{(0)}
and
\prec^{(2)}
coincide. However, the results provided here show that the answer to this Question in general is no. We end with a few examples illustrating some differences.

Example 33
.

For another example, consider the coprime pairs
(8,7)
and
(13,1)
. The slope of the line connecting these two points is
-\frac{6}{5}
, which lies in the the gray zone for the first few values of
k
. The numbers
m^{(0)}_{\frac{7}{8}}
and
m^{(0)}_{\frac{1}{13}}
are Pell and Fibonacci numbers respectively and one can calculate
m^{(0)}_{\frac{7}{8}}=195025
and
m^{(0)}_{\frac{1}{13}}=196418
. In particular,
m^{(0)}_{\frac{7}{8}}<m^{(0)}_{\frac{1}{13}}
. Using Proposition
19
, one can check again
m^{(1)}_{\frac{7}{8}}<m^{(1)}_{\frac{1}{13}}
and
m^{(2)}_{\frac{7}{8}}<m^{(2)}_{\frac{1}{13}}
. The latter is indeed immediate from
[
12
, Theorem 2.9]
. However, at
k=3
, the behavior swaps. We have

m^{(3)}_{\frac{7}{8}}=1188752792899

and

m^{(3)}_{\frac{1}{13}}=1108609632005

so that
m^{(2)}_{\frac{7}{8}}>m^{(2)}_{\frac{1}{13}}
.

Based on this data and these investigations, we conclude with some items to consider in the future. We begin by conjecturing a strengthening of
[
12
, Conjecture 1.8]
.

Conjecture 34
.

For all
(p,q),(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
and for all
k\geq 0
,
m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}=m^{(k)}_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
implies
(p,q)=(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
.

Next, based on the behavior of
U(k)
and
L(k)
as
k
grows, we ask about the following relationship amongst the behavior of the orderings
\prec^{(k)}
in their respective “gray zones.”

Question 35
.

Do there exist
(p,q),(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
with
p<p^{\prime}
and
1<k_{1}<k_{2}
such that (1)
L(k_{i})<\frac{q^{\prime}-q}{p^{\prime}-p}<U(k_{i})
for
i=1,2
, (2)
m^{(k_{1})}_{(p,q)}>m^{(k_{1})}_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
, and (3)
m^{(k_{2})}_{(p,q)}<m^{(k_{2})}_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
?

Question
35
asks about how the orderings in the intersection of the gray zone of two values of
k
can compare. We weakly expect that no such points can exist, since, if
L(k_{2})<\frac{q^{\prime}-q}{p^{\prime}-p}<U(k_{2})
, then there exists some
k_{3}>k_{2}
such that
\frac{q^{\prime}-q}{p^{\prime}-p}<L(k)
for all
k\geq k_{3}
. That is, we expect that, once we have
m^{(k)}_{(p,q)}>m^{(k)}_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
, then for all larger
k^{\prime}>k
,
m^{(k^{\prime})}_{(p,q)}>m^{(k^{\prime})}_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}
.
