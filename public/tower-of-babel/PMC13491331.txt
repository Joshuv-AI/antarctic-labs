Concomitant Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion and Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Krishna S Gajula, Shanmukh Sai Pavan Lingamsetty, Hrushikesh R Pamreddy, Akshat Banga, Sahas R Jitta, Mohamed Doma, Krishna Cherukuri, Kash Vahdat, Eddison Ramsaran, Andrew M Goldsweig
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAtrial fibrillation (AF) is prevalent among patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and increases risks of thromboembolism and bleeding with chronic anticoagulation. Concomitant left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) during TAVR may reduce long-term stroke and bleeding risks.MethodsPubMed, Embase, and Cochrane databases were searched through May 2025 for studies comparing TAVR + LAAO versus TAVR + standard medical therapy in patients with atrial fibrillation. Random-effects models were used to pool risk ratios (RRs) or mean differences with 95% CIs.ResultsFour studies (2 randomized controlled trials, 2 observational; n = 563, mean age 82 years) were included; 279 patients (49.5%) underwent TAVR + LAAO. Thirty-day outcomes showed no differences in mortality (RR 0.91; 95% CI, 0.28-3.01), stroke/transient ischemic attack (RR 0.27; 95% CI, 0.04-1.66), or major bleeding (RR 0.27; 95% CI, 0.04-1.66). Over a 9.8-month to 2-year follow-up, all-cause mortality (RR 0.95; 95% CI, 0.68-1.32), cardiac death (RR 0.95; 95% CI, 0.60-1.50), stroke/transient ischemic attack (RR 1.33; 95% CI, 0.30-5.83), and major bleeding (RR 1.06; 95% CI, 0.71-1.59) remained similar. Contrast use and hospital length of stay were comparable.ConclusionsConcomitant TAVR plus LAAO appears feasible, with no statistically significant differences in short-term outcomes versus TAVR plus standard medical therapy; however, certainty is low, and findings are hypothesis-generating, warranting larger randomized trials and prospective registries.Keywords: atrial fibrillation, concomitant procedure, left atrial appendage occlusion, major bleeding, stroke prevention, transcatheter aortic valve replacement, transcatheter aortic valve intervention

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) often coexists with severe aortic stenosis (AS) in elderly patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). In contemporary TAVR populations, the prevalence of preexisting AF ranges from approximately 26% to 44%, and new-onset AF occurs in 7% to 10% of patients during the index TAVR hospitalization or early follow-up.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Patients with AF after TAVR often require oral anticoagulation therapy (OAC) to prevent stroke; however, OAC carries a high risk of late bleeding, particularly gastrointestinal hemorrhage, which leads to increased morbidity and mortality.6, 7, 8 These competing risks underscore the need for strategies to provide effective stroke prevention while mitigating long-term bleeding.

In patients undergoing TAVR who meet the standard eligibility criteria for percutaneous left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO), the integration of LAAO into the same procedural setting represents a rational and patient-centered approach. This strategy aims to achieve durable stroke prevention while mitigating the bleeding risks as well as the cumulative procedural risks associated with separate interventions. Given the advanced age and comorbidity burden of the TAVR population, a single-session combined procedure aligns with the broader goal of optimizing the safety, efficiency, and long-term outcomes in this high-risk cohort.

However, the data on the safety and efficacy of this concomitant approach remain limited by small sample sizes, procedural variability, and heterogeneous antithrombotic regimens. Therefore, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to synthesize contemporary evidence comparing clinical outcomes of concomitant TAVR + LAAO versus TAVR plus standard medical therapy (SMT).

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to the recommendations of the Cochrane Collaboration Handbook and reported according to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines (Supplemental Methods S1 and S2).9 The protocol was registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; ID CRD420251176165). The protocol was exempt from the requirements of the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center Institutional Review Board because the data were previously published and deidentified.

Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible for inclusion if they (1) were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or comparative observational studies; (2) enrolled patients with a history of paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent nonvalvular AF; and (3) presented data on outcomes following concomitant TAVR and LAAO.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) nonoriginal studies (reviews, letters to the editor, abstracts, commentaries, case reports, case series, and animal studies); (2) studies that included patients with severe mitral stenosis (valvular AF) or other indications for OAC; (3) studies where LAAO was not performed during the index procedure; and (4) studies that did not report data on outcomes of interest.

Study selection and data extraction

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases were systematically searched from inception to May 2025. The search strategy included keywords of Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement” OR “Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation” OR “Aortic Valve Stenosis” AND “Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion” OR “Left Atrial Appendage Closure” OR “LAAO” OR “LAAC” OR “Watchman” OR “Amplatzer Amulet” AND “Atrial Fibrillation” OR “Non-Valvular Atrial Fibrillation” OR “AF.” After removing duplicate records, 2 authors independently screened the titles and abstracts using the inclusion criteria. Potentially eligible studies underwent full-text screening, and conflicts were resolved through discussions with the senior author. Data were extracted from each study in a standardized form to record baseline characteristics and outcomes. The details of the search strategy are reported in Supplemental Method S3.

Outcomes

Short-term outcomes included 30-day all-cause mortality, stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA), major bleeding, and major vascular complications. Long-term outcomes, including all-cause mortality, cardiac death, stroke, and major bleeding, were extracted from each study at the longest available follow-up. Additionally, procedural outcomes such as contrast volume, length of stay in days, and procedural time were recorded. Outcomes were defined by the individual studies according to the Valve Academic Research Consortium 2 statement.10

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of the included studies was assessed using prespecified risk-of-bias assessment tools. RCTs were evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 (RoB 2) across 5 domains and classified as having low, moderate, or high risk of bias.11 Nonrandomized studies were assessed using the ROBINS-I tool across 7 domains and categorized as having a low, moderate, serious, or critical risk of bias.12 Potential publication bias was explored by visually inspecting the funnel plots of the effect estimates plotted against study weights.

Statistical analysis

Continuous outcome data were pooled using Mantel-Haenszel random-effects models to obtain the mean differences (MDs) and 95% CIs. Binary outcomes were aggregated using Mantel-Haenszel random-effects models to generate risk ratios (RRs) and 95% CIs. The DerSimonian and Laird method was used to calculate the heterogeneity variance tau2.13 Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochrane’s Jackson method I2 statistics. The heterogeneity between studies was interpreted as I2 ≤25% (low), ≤50% (moderate), and >50% (substantial).14 Statistical significance was set at P < .05. Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was performed to assess the influence of individual studies on the overall effect estimates and identify potential sources of heterogeneity. All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).15 The packages used were “meta” and “dmetar.”

Results

The initial literature search yielded a total of 555 articles (Figure 1). After removing duplicates and screening titles and abstracts, 521 articles were retrieved, and full texts were reviewed for eligibility. Notably among the studies, Wong et al16 was excluded as staged LAAO was performed 4-10 days after TAVR rather than an index concomitant strategy. Reasons for full-text exclusion are summarized in Supplemental Table S1. Finally, 4 studies17, 18, 19, 20 (2 RCTs and 2 observational studies) met the inclusion criteria and were included in the meta-analysis. Of 563 patients with AF undergoing TAVR, 279 (49.5%) underwent concomitant LAAO. The mean age was 82.4 ± 6.0 years, with 42% women, an average CHA2DS2-VASc score of 4.3 ± 1.1, a HAS-BLED score of 2.9 ± 1.0, and a Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) risk score for surgical aortic valve replacement of 6.1 ± 4.5. 30-day outcomes

Compared with TAVR+SMT, TAVR+LAAO demonstrated no significant difference in 30-day all-cause mortality (RR 0.91; 95% CI, 0.28-3.01; P = .88; I2 = 0%), rates of stroke or TIA (RR 0.27; 95% CI, 0.04-1.66; P = .15; I2 = 0%), major bleeding (RR 0.27; 95% CI ,0.04-1.66; P = .15; I2 = 0%), or major vascular complications (RR 3.51; 95% CI, 0.41-29.69; P = .26; I2 = 0%) (Figure 2).

Forest plots showing the 30-day safety outcomes of TAVR+LAAO compared with TAVR+SMT. IV, inverse variance; LAAO, left atrial appendage occlusion; RR, risk ratio; SMT, standard medical therapy; TAVR, transcatheter aortic valve replacement.

Long-term outcomes

Additionally, long-term follow-up outcomes (9.8 months to 2 years), including all-cause mortality (RR 0.95; 95% CI, 0.68-1.32; P = .75; I2 = 0%) (Figure 3A) and cardiac death (RR 0.95; 95% CI, 0.60-1.50; P = .83; I2 = 0%) (Figure 3B), showed no difference between the groups. Similarly, the rate of stroke or TIA (RR 1.33; 95% CI, 0.30-5.83; P = .70; I2 = 48.5%) (Figure 3C), major bleeding events were comparable (RR 1.06; 95% CI, 0.71-1.59; P = .77; I2 = 0%) (Figure 3D) and pericardial effusion or cardiac tamponade needing cardiocentesis were comparable across the groups (RR 1.89; 95% CI, 0.49-7.32; P = .36; I2 = 0%) (Figure 3E). The pooled composite end point (all-cause mortality, stroke, and major or life-threatening bleeding) did not differ significantly between TAVR+LAAO and TAVR+SMT (HR 0.90; 95% CI, 0.67-1.22; P = .51), with no between-study heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) (Supplemental Figure S1).

LAAO, left atrial appendage occlusion; SMT, standard medical therapy; TAVR, transcatheter aortic valve replacement; TIA, transient ischemic attack.

Procedural outcomes

The length of hospital stay did not differ between the groups (MD −0.22 days; 95% CI, −0.9 to 0.5; P = .54; I2 = 0%) (Figure 4A). The contrast volume used during combined TAVR + LAAO was comparable to that used during TAVR + SMT (MD 31.79; 95% CI, −1.43, 65.0; P = .06; I2 = 87%) (Figure 4B). Additionally, the procedural time was significantly higher with TAVR+LAAO compared with TAVR+SMT (MD 28.6; 95% CI, 19.43 -37.77; P < 0.01; I2 = 48%) (Figure 4C).

Quality assessment

Risk-of-bias assessment with RoB 2 revealed that the 2 RCTs, by Jakob et al20 (TAVI-LAAO) and Kapadia et al19 (WATCH-TAVR), had “low” risk of bias. The 2 observational studies, by Attinger-Toller et al17 and Gilhofer et al,18 assessed using ROBINS-I, had a “moderate” risk of bias and “serious” risk of bias, respectively, driven mainly by potential confounding (Supplemental Figures S2 and S3).

Publication bias

The funnel plots were symmetrical, suggesting no evidence of publication bias in the included studies (Supplemental Figure S4).

Leave-one-out analysis

The leave-one-out method identified studies that contributed to heterogeneity across outcomes and ensured that the overall effect size was not disproportionately influenced by any single study (Supplemental Figure S5). For 30-day mortality (Attinger-Toller et al, Gilhofer et al, and Jakob et al), the pooled direction of effect was unchanged after sequential omission of any single study, and similarly, 30-day stroke, major bleeding, and vascular complications (Attinger-Toller et al and Gilhofer et al) remained unchanged. For long-term mortality, cardiac death, stroke/TIA, and major bleeding (Attinger-Toller et al, Kapadia et al, and Jakob et al), the pooled direction of effect was unchanged after sequential omission of any single study; however, omission of Kapadia et al resulted in wider confidence intervals.

Subgroup analysis: procedural vs nonprocedural bleeding

To characterize the nature of bleeding events, we stratified bleeding into procedural and nonprocedural components. In the subgroup population, neither nonprocedural (RR 0.90; 95% CI, 0.23-3.42; P = .87; I2 = 46%) nor procedural bleeding (RR 2.14; 95% CI, 0.78-5.88; P = .138; I2 = 0%) differed significantly between TAVR+LAAO and TAVR+SMT, with no significant subgroup interaction (P = .31) (Supplemental Figure S6). In the sensitivity analysis restricted to patients in the TAVR+LAAO arm initiated on DAPT rather than OAC, nonprocedural bleeding (RR 0.59; 95% CI, 0.02-19.38; P = .77; I2 = 73%) and procedural bleeding (RR 1.82; 95% CI, 0.59-5.69; P = .30; I2 = 0%) remained comparable to TAVR+SMT, with no significant subgroup interaction (P = .55) (Supplemental Figure S7). Procedural bleeding demonstrated no between-study heterogeneity in either the overall (I2 = 0%) or DAPT-restricted (I2 = 0%) analyses.

Discussion

This meta-analysis represents the most comprehensive synthesis to date evaluating the combined strategy of TAVR and LAAO in patients with severe AS and AF. Pooled data from 2 RCTs and 2 observational studies demonstrated that concomitant TAVR and LAAO were associated with comparable rates of all-cause mortality, stroke, and major bleeding compared with TAVR and SMT during 30-day and long-term follow-up (Central Illustration).

The WATCH-TAVR trial demonstrated that TAVR with concomitant LAAO was noninferior to TAVR followed by SMT for the composite end point of death, stroke, and major or life-threatening bleeding, confirming the procedural safety and midterm efficacy of this dual approach. Similarly, the randomized TAVI-LAAO pilot trial, the first to evaluate the Amulet device in this population, provided complementary findings by showing comparable cerebrovascular and bleeding outcomes despite the higher procedural complexity.20 Collectively, these findings extend the initial feasibility experience reported by Attinger-Toller et al and the fluoroscopy-guided workflow described by Gilhofer et al, both of which established a foundation for procedural integration under conscious sedation.

Bleeding remains one of the most important determinants of morbidity and mortality in TAVR patients. Late bleeding events occur predominantly from gastrointestinal sources and are accentuated in patients with AF.5,6 In contemporary post-TAVR AF populations, intensified antithrombotic regimens have consistently led to increased bleeding risk without improving ischemic outcomes. Evidence from major trials, including ENVISAGE-TAVR AF and POPULAR-TAVR, emphasizes a simplified anticoagulation strategy to reduce gastrointestinal and major bleeding events.21,8 These data support the hypothesis that de-escalation of antithrombotic therapy after LAAO may be particularly beneficial in this frail, high-bleeding risk population. Indeed, in high-risk non-TAVR AF cohorts, LAAO compared to direct oral anticoagulation resulted in lower major bleeding rates without excess ischemic events. In our pooled data, the absence of major bleeding at 30-day and long-term follow-ups in the TAVI-LAAO cohort suggests that early transition to antiplatelet therapy after occlusion may mitigate late gastrointestinal bleeding risk, consistent with the de-escalation strategies reported in the TAVI-LAAO 2025 trial.20

Stroke risk has been a procedural concern during combined interventions, and in the TAVI-LAAO trial,20 a numerically higher rate of periprocedural strokes has been noted, although most events occurred before LAAO deployment. The study implies this finding aligns with prior observations that predilatation and specific valve platforms, such as the self-expanding Portico valve, may confer a higher periprocedural stroke risk.22, 23, 24 Nevertheless, stroke rates in the per-protocol population were comparable between groups and were consistent with the findings of the WATCH-TAVR trial,19 suggesting that the combined strategy does not intrinsically increase embolic risk. Our study findings build on this and importantly demonstrate that the follow-up stroke rates were similar to those of SMT and aligned with published clinical trials, signifying the efficacy of the combined procedure.

Procedural aspects have evolved over the past decade. The growing use of intracardiac echocardiography-guided and fluoroscopy-guided techniques has reduced dependence on general anesthesia during LAAO in experienced centers.25,26 Gilhofer et al showed that fluoroscopy-guided procedures maintain comparable safety and efficacy while minimizing anesthesia exposure. Although this procedural evolution offers potential workflow advantages, anesthetic strategy should remain individualized based on patient profile and operator experience. Our analysis found no evidence of increased procedural contrast use or prolonged length of hospital stay with the combined procedure, underscoring the safety profile of this integrated approach.

The LAAO device-level variance must be addressed to further support the robustness of this strategy. Trials included in our analysis, WATCH-TAVR and TAVI-LAAO, used WATCHMAN and Amulet devices, respectively. However, with prior dedicated LAAO device trials, including Amulet IDE and SWISS-APERO, both the WATCHMAN and Amulet systems have shown equivalent safety and efficacy in stroke prevention.27,28 Importantly, the ever-improving designs of newer-generation TAVR prostheses and LAAO devices may continue to optimize outcomes in future studies.

Surgical exclusion of the left atrial appendage has long been incorporated into cardiac procedures as an effective adjunct for stroke prevention in AF, supported by growing evidence of its clinical benefit.29 Although this approach has become standard in the surgical setting, its translation into the percutaneous space is still evolving. Integrating LAAO with transcatheter interventions such as TAVR offers a logical next step, aligning established surgical principles with minimally invasive practices. For patients eligible for LAAO, performing both procedures in a single session provides seamless transcatheter adaptation, combining stroke prevention and valve intervention within 1 efficient workflow.

Future implications

Taken together, the current evidence supports concomitant TAVR and LAAO as viable, safe, and clinically efficient alternatives to staged or medical-only management in patients with severe aortic stenosis and AF. This approach consolidates the procedures into a single session, avoiding repeated vascular access, minimizing anesthesia exposure, and simplifying long-term antithrombotic management. The evidence for concomitant LAAO performed at the time of TAVR remains limited, with WATCH-TAVR representing the only randomized multicenter comparative trial and other available reports reflecting smaller pilot or single-center experiences. Accordingly, the present systematic review and meta-analysis are intended to be hypothesis-generating. By pooling the currently available comparative data, we aimed to quantify the direction and precision of effect estimates and to identify specific knowledge gaps to inform future trial design and prospective registry efforts. Future multicenter randomized trials using standardized antithrombotic protocols and newer-generation devices are essential to guide whether this procedural synergy translates into durable improvements in survival and quality of life.

Limitations

Our meta-analysis has several limitations. First, the sample size and number of included studies were limited; several key end points were informed by only 2 studies, resulting in imprecise estimates with wide confidence intervals, and this limits the overall generalizability of the findings. Therefore, neutral findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of equivalence between strategies. Future adequately powered randomized trials and prospective registries with standardized end point definitions are needed to refine estimates and determine which patient subgroups may benefit most from an index concomitant approach. Second, there was substantial heterogeneity in postprocedural antithrombotic regimens across studies. The WATCH-TAVR trial used the first-generation WATCHMAN 2.5 device and mandated warfarin with aspirin for the first 45 days, followed by dual antiplatelet therapy, whereas the more recent TAVI-LAAO trial predominantly employed dual antiplatelet therapy alone. Such protocol differences likely contribute to the variability in bleeding and thromboembolic outcomes. Second, device heterogeneity further complicates the interpretation. A critical limitation of this meta-analysis is that a substantial proportion of included studies employed device platforms that are no longer representative of contemporary clinical practice. On the TAVR side, several valves including the Lotus, SAPIEN XT, Portico, Acurate TA, and Direct Flow Medical platforms are no longer commercially available or have been substantially redesigned. On the LAAO side, the Amplatzer Cardiac Plug and first-generation WATCHMAN 2.5 device differ meaningfully from current-generation systems in terms of design, delivery, and postprocedural management. This limits the applicability of our pooled findings to contemporary practice, and future clinical trials should specifically evaluate outcomes with current-generation devices under strict protocols. Third, imaging guidance was also inconsistent, with some centers utilizing transesophageal echocardiography, others using intracardiac echocardiography, and some relying solely on fluoroscopic guidance, potentially influencing procedural time, contrast use, and anesthesia requirements, and contributing to heterogeneity in procedural outcomes. Fourth, the inclusion of studies with varied follow-up and observational studies adds to inherent biases. Finally, publication bias cannot be reliably assessed with fewer studies per outcome. Accordingly, funnel plots were generated as descriptive visuals only and were not used to draw inferential conclusions regarding publication bias.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis consolidates the growing body of evidence suggesting that combining TAVR and LAAO in a single session is technically feasible with outcomes comparable to TAVR alone. This combined procedural strategy may benefit patients by reducing the bleeding risks associated with antithrombotic therapy as well as reducing cumulative procedural risk. These results are hypothesis-generating and support the need for adequately powered randomized trials and standardized prospective registries.
