Global Smooth Solutions to a Thermoelastic Cauchy Problem in Phase Transitions
M. Affouf

Abstract
We study one-dimensional viscoelastic phase transitions modeled by a Ginzburg–Landau energy with a non-convex cubic stress-strain law. Extending the isothermal model, we couple the momentum equation to a heat equation for the temperature field, giving a thermoelastic system with viscous, capillary, and thermal-diffusion terms. We prove global existence and uniqueness of classical smooth solutions for the Cauchy problem, using a travelling-wave decomposition, an exponential transformation of the mechanical perturbation, and coupled energy estimates at successive regularity levels. Under additional integrability and small-data assumptions, the temperature perturbation decays algebraically. Key Words: Global existence, thermoelastic phase transitions, thermal equilibration, decay rates, energy estimates, shock layers, Ginzburg–Landau theory. AMS Subject Classifications. Primary 35G30, 35L65, 35Q74; Secondary 74N30, 35K55.

Introduction

We study the thermoelastic system

\displaystyle u_{tt}+\tau(u_{x},\theta)_{x}

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon\,u_{xxt}\;-\;\delta\,u_{xxxx},

(1)

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-\kappa\,\theta_{xx}

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon\,(u_{xt})^{2}\;-\;\gamma\,\theta_{0}\,u_{xt},

(2)

which models dynamical phase transitions in shape-memory alloys.
Here
u(x,t)
is the displacement,
u_{x}(x,t)
the strain,
\theta(x,t)

the temperature,
\theta_{0}
the reference temperature, and the
coefficients
\varepsilon,\delta,\kappa,\gamma>0
denote viscosity, capillarity,
thermal conductivity, and thermoelastic coupling.
The stress is

\tau(u_{x},\theta)\;=\;u_{x}-u_{x}^{3}-\gamma(\theta-\theta_{0});

(3)

see Falk
[
4
]
and Sprekels–Zheng
[
17
]
.
The two source terms in (
2
) are the viscous dissipation

\varepsilon\,(u_{xt})^{2}
and the Kelvin–Voigt thermoelastic coupling

-\gamma\theta_{0}\,u_{xt}
.

Hagan–Slemrod
[
7
]
and Slemrod
[
15
]
showed
that (
1
)–(
2
) admits travelling-wave solutions

U(x-st)
with asymptotic states
U(\pm\infty)=U^{+},U^{-}
satisfying the
Rankine–Hugoniot condition. We treat the Cauchy problem for
perturbations of such a wave, with initial data

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle\;=\;U(x)+\rho_{0}(x),\qquad\rho_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}),

(4)

\displaystyle\theta(x,0)

\displaystyle\;=\;\theta_{0}+\phi(x),\qquad\phi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\;\;\phi(x)>-\theta_{0}\;\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5)

The condition
\phi>-\theta_{0}
keeps the absolute temperature positive.

Sprekels and Zheng
[
17
]
proved global existence for a
similar Ginzburg–Landau thermoelastic system on a bounded domain, where
Poincaré’s inequality and compact Sobolev embeddings are available.
Other bounded-domain results include
Hoffmann–Niezgódka–Sprekels (see
[
22
]
),
Watson
[
19
]
(without capillarity),
Pawłow–Zajączkowski
[
12
]

(three-dimensional Kelvin–Voigt, no smallness),
Mielke–Roubiček
[
11
]

(quasistatic large strains), and
Winkler
[
20
]

(temperature-dependent viscosity).
None of these treats the Cauchy problem on
\mathbb{R}
with the non-convex
Ginzburg–Landau energy and capillarity term.

Our main result (Theorem
3.1
) is global existence and
uniqueness of classical solutions for this Cauchy problem under
smallness conditions on the coupling
\gamma
. The proof uses the
travelling-wave decomposition of
[
7
,
15
]
and
the exponential transformation
\rho=e^{t/\varepsilon}v
from
[
1
]
,
which removes the negative contribution in the mechanical energy
estimate.

In Section
2
,we set up the perturbation system and introduce the exponential transformation. In Section
3
, we prove the global
existence theorem. Section
4
is devoted to proving the algebraic decay of the temperature perturbation. Finally, in Section
5
, we discuss two related questions.

2
Formulation

We set up the perturbation system and the energy functionals used in the proof.

2.1
Travelling-wave decomposition

We seek a solution of (
1
)–(
2
) in the form

u(x,t)\;=\;U(x-st)+\rho(x,t),\qquad\theta(x,t)\;=\;\theta_{0}+\Theta(x,t),

(6)

where
U(x-st)
is the travelling-wave solution of the isothermal
equation

u_{tt}\;=\;\tau(u_{x})_{x}-\varepsilon\,u_{txx}+\delta\,u_{xxxx}

(7)

with wave speed
s
satisfying the Rankine–Hugoniot
admissibility condition

s^{2}\;=\;-\frac{\tau(U^{+})-\tau(U^{-})}{U^{+}-U^{-}},

(8)

and
\rho(x,t)
,
\Theta(x,t)
are the mechanical and thermal perturbations
respectively. The asymptotic states
U^{-},U^{+}
satisfy the admissibility
condition for shock or phase-transition layers.

We use the isothermal travelling-wave profile
U
as the background.
This corresponds to
\theta(\pm\infty)=\theta_{0}
, so the thermal contribution

\gamma(\theta_{+}-\theta_{-})
to the full thermoelastic Rankine–Hugoniot
relation vanishes and (
8
) reduces to the isothermal form.
The thermoelastic system (
1
)–(
2
) does not in
general admit travelling waves with constant asymptotic temperature:
substituting
u=U(x-st)
and
\theta=\theta_{0}
into (
2
) produces
a non-zero source
S_{0}(x,t)
(see (
11
) below). This
residual reflects our use of the isothermal profile as the background;
it is controlled in the analysis through its uniform
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
bounds.

Constructing thermoelastic traveling waves with non-constant temperature profiles has been investigated by Hagan–Slemrod
[
7
]
and Slemrod
[
15
]
for the van der Waals pressure equation of state.

We assume throughout that the wave profile satisfies

3M_{U}^{2}\;<\;1,\qquad M_{U}\;=\;\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|U^{\prime}(x)\right|,

(9)

which ensures that the linearised stress coefficient
1-3U^{\prime 2}
is
strictly positive, with
1-3U^{\prime 2}\geq 1-3M_{U}^{2}>0
uniformly in
x
.
This positivity provides the leading coercive contribution to the
mechanical energy functional
E_{1}
defined in (
19
)
below; in particular, the term
\tfrac{1-3U^{\prime 2}}{2}v_{x}^{2}
in
E_{1}

is bounded below by a positive multiple of
v_{x}^{2}
.
Condition (
9
) is a property of the wave profile itself
(not merely of the end states
U^{+},U^{-}
), and it holds for admissible
phase-transition layers connecting states in the spinodal region; we
refer to Hagan–Slemrod
[
7
]
and
Slemrod
[
15
]
for the construction and regularity properties
of such profiles, which in particular satisfy
U^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with exponential decay at
\pm\infty
.

We also define, for use in the corrected perturbation equations below,

W(x,t)\;:=\;-s\,U^{\prime\prime}(x-st),

(10)

which represents the contribution of the wave profile curvature to the
mixed derivative
u_{xt}=W(x,t)+\rho_{xt}
. Since
U^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
(with exponential decay at
\pm\infty
), the functions

W(\cdot,t)
and

S_{0}(x,t)\;:=\;\varepsilon\,s^{2}\,[U^{\prime\prime}(x-st)]^{2}+\gamma\theta_{0}\,s\,U^{\prime\prime}(x-st)

(11)

satisfy
W(\cdot,t),\,S_{0}(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with norms
that are independent of
t
.

2.2
Perturbation equations

Substituting (
6
) into (
1
)–(
2
) and
using the fact that
U(x-st)
solves the isothermal equation (
7
),
we obtain the perturbation system.

Derivation of the momentum equation.

Since
U
solves (
7
) with stress
\tau(U_{x})
, and the
thermoelastic stress is
\tau(u_{x},\theta)=\tau(u_{x})-\gamma(\theta-\theta_{0})=\tau(u_{x})-\gamma\Theta
, substituting
u_{x}=U^{\prime}+\rho_{x}
and
expanding the cubic gives (
12
) directly.

Derivation of the thermal equation.

Note that the background pair
(U(x-st),\theta_{0})
does
not
solve
the thermoelastic system (
2
), since the heat equation with

u=U
and
\theta=\theta_{0}
gives a non-zero right-hand side. The correct
procedure is to substitute
u=U(x-st)+\rho
and
\theta=\theta_{0}+\Theta

into (
2
) directly. Using
u_{xt}=W(x,t)+\rho_{xt}

(where
W
is defined in (
10
)) and expanding:

\varepsilon\,(u_{xt})^{2}-\gamma\theta_{0}\,u_{xt}\;=\;\varepsilon\,(\rho_{xt})^{2}-\gamma\theta_{0}\,\rho_{xt}\;+\;2\varepsilon\,W\,\rho_{xt}\;+\;S_{0}(x,t),

where
S_{0}
is the fixed source defined in (
11
).
The perturbation system is therefore:

\displaystyle\rho_{tt}-\bigl(\rho_{x}-\rho_{x}^{3}-3U^{\prime\,2}\rho_{x}-3U^{\prime}\rho_{x}^{2}-\gamma\Theta\bigr)_{x}

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon\,\rho_{xxt}-\delta\,\rho_{xxxx},

(12)

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}-\kappa\,\Theta_{xx}

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon\,(\rho_{xt})^{2}-\gamma\theta_{0}\,\rho_{xt}+2\varepsilon\,W\,\rho_{xt}+S_{0},

(13)

with initial conditions
\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x)\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R})
and

\Theta(x,0)=\phi(x)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The condition
\phi>-\theta_{0}
(cf. (
5
)) ensures that the
initial absolute temperature is strictly positive; since (
13
)
is parabolic with globally bounded source terms, the maximum principle
preserves
\Theta(x,t)>-\theta_{0}
for all
t>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

The bilinear term
2\varepsilon W\rho_{xt}
in (
13
) is linear in

\rho_{xt}
with coefficient
W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
decaying exponentially,
and is controlled by the existing dissipation. The fixed source

S_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
has time-independent
L^{2}
norm and
contributes only a constant absorbed into the Gronwall bound.

2.3
Exponential transformation

We apply the transformation

\rho(x,t)\;=\;e^{t/\varepsilon}\,v(x,t)

(14)

to the mechanical perturbation only. Using
\rho_{xt}=e^{t/\varepsilon}(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x})
, the transformed system becomes:

Transformed momentum equation:

v_{tt}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}v_{t}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}v-\bigl(e^{2t/\varepsilon}v_{x}^{3}\bigr)_{x}-\varepsilon\,v_{txx}+\delta\,v_{xxxx}\\
-\bigl(3U^{\prime\,2}v_{x}-3U^{\prime}\,e^{t/\varepsilon}v_{x}^{2}\bigr)_{x}\;=\;\gamma\,e^{-t/\varepsilon}\Theta_{x}.

(15)

Transformed thermal equation:

\Theta_{t}-\kappa\,\Theta_{xx}\;=\;\varepsilon\,e^{2t/\varepsilon}\!\left(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\right)^{\!2}-\gamma\theta_{0}\,e^{t/\varepsilon}\!\left(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\right)+2\varepsilon\,W\,e^{t/\varepsilon}\!\left(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\right)+S_{0}(x,t).

(16)

3
Main Theorem and Proof

Theorem 3.1

(Global existence)
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\phi>-\theta_{0}
.
Assume the wave-profile condition (
9
) holds, and assume
the coupling conditions

\gamma\varepsilon^{2}\;\leq\;4,\qquad\gamma\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa\varepsilon}{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma\;\leq\;1.

(17)

Assume further that the initial perturbation energy is sufficiently small:

\mathcal{E}(0)\;=\;E_{1}(0)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\phi\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;\delta_{1},

(18)

where
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta,\kappa,\gamma,\theta_{0},s,M_{U},\left\|U^{\prime\prime}\right\|)>0

is the smallness constant determined in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Then the thermoelastic Cauchy
problem (
12
)–(
13
) with initial
conditions (
4
)–(
5
) admits a unique global classical
solution
(u,\theta)
such that, for every
T>0
,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\;\in\;C\bigl([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)\cap C^{1}\bigl([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\bigr),

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\;\in\;C\bigl([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)\cap L^{2}\bigl([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{R})\bigr).

Moreover, under additional
L^{1}
and small-data assumptions on the
initial perturbation, the temperature perturbation
\Theta
decays
algebraically in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\to+\infty
at rate
(1+t)^{-1}
;
see Theorem
4.1
in Section
4
.

The proof proceeds via three lemmas providing a priori estimates at
successive regularity levels.

3.1
Energy Estimates

Notation.

Throughout,
C
and
C_{i}
denote uniform positive constants, independent of

t
, depending at most on the initial data,
\varepsilon,\delta,\kappa,\gamma,\theta_{0}
,
and
T
. We write
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|
for the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
norm, and

M_{U}=\max_{\mathbb{R}}\left|U^{\prime}\right|
.

We define the mechanical energy functional and the
growth rate bound

\displaystyle E_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\left[\frac{v^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{v_{t}^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}U^{\prime\,2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{e^{2t/\varepsilon}v_{x}^{4}}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}v_{xx}^{2}\right]dx,

(19)

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1+3M_{U}}{2\varepsilon}\,e^{2t/\varepsilon},

(20)

(note that
E_{2}
grows exponentially, so the Gronwall constant
C_{5}

in (
36
) below depends on
T
through

\exp\!\bigl(\int_{0}^{T}E_{2}\,ds\bigr)
; the separate Bernoulli argument in
Section
4
is needed precisely for this reason),
and the three coupled functionals

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;E_{1}(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2},

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\delta\left\|v_{xxx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2},

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;\left\|v_{xxt}\right\|^{2}+\delta\left\|v_{xxxx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta_{xx}\right\|^{2}.

(23)

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\|v\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{4}+\left\|v_{xx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}\,ds\;\leq\;C.

(24)

Proof.

Part 1: Mechanical energy estimate.

Multiply (
15
) by
v_{t}
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}
. The purely
mechanical terms are handled exactly as in
[
1
]

(cf. equations (15)–(17) therein), yielding

\frac{d}{dt}E_{1}(t)+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left\|v_{tx}\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;E_{2}(t)\,E_{1}(t)+\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\Theta_{x}\,v_{t}\,dx.

(25)

The coupling term on the right of (
25
) is estimated using
Young’s inequality
ab\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4}a^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}b^{2}
and the
bound
e^{-t/\varepsilon}\leq 1
:

\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\Theta_{x}\,v_{t}\,dx\;\leq\;\frac{\gamma\varepsilon}{4}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}.

(26)

The first term on the right of (
26
) is absorbed into the
dissipation
\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}
on the left of (
25
),
leaving a residual
\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon}-\tfrac{\gamma\varepsilon}{4}\bigr)\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}
,
valid when
\gamma\varepsilon^{2}\leq 4
(the first condition in (
17
)).
Thus:

\frac{d}{dt}E_{1}(t)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left\|v_{tx}\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;E_{2}(t)\,E_{1}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}.

(27)

Part 2: Thermal energy estimate.

Multiply (
16
) by
\Theta
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}
; integrating by
parts on the left gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}+\kappa\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}\;=\;I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

(28)

where the four terms on the right correspond to the four terms in (
16
).

Term
I_{1}
(viscous dissipation term).

Applying the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

\left\|\Theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{1/2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{1/2}

and Young’s inequality:

I_{1}\;=\;\varepsilon\,e^{2t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\bigr)^{\!2}\left|\Theta\right|\,dx\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa}{8}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+C_{1}\,e^{4T/\varepsilon}\Bigl(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}\Bigr)^{\!2}.

(29)

Term
I_{2}
(thermoelastic coupling term).

Using
e^{t/\varepsilon}\leq e^{T/\varepsilon}
,

\left\|\Theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{1/2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{1/2}
,
and Young’s inequality:

I_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa}{8}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+C_{2}\Bigl(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}\Bigr).

(30)

Term
I_{3}
(new bilinear term
2\varepsilon We^{t/\varepsilon}(v_{xt}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x})
).

Let
A=v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}
. Using
\left\|W\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=s\left\|U^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=:K_{W}<\infty
and Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\;=\;2\varepsilon\,e^{t/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}W\,A\,\Theta\,dx\;\leq\;2\varepsilon\,e^{T/\varepsilon}K_{W}\,\left\|A\right\|\,\left\|\Theta\right\|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa}{8}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+C_{3}\,e^{2T/\varepsilon}\,K_{W}^{2}\,\left\|A\right\|^{2}\,\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa}{8}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+C_{3}^{\prime}\,e^{2T/\varepsilon}\,K_{W}^{2}\,\mathcal{E}(t)^{2},

(31)

where we used
\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\leq 2\mathcal{E}
and

\left\|A\right\|^{2}\leq 2(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})\leq C\mathcal{E}

(via
\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}\leq CE_{1}\leq C\mathcal{E}
from the
3U^{\prime 2}v_{x}^{2}/2
term in
E_{1}
;
the term
\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}
is not contained in
\mathcal{E}
but is controlled
a posteriori by the a priori bound in Part 3 below).

Term
I_{4}
(new fixed source
S_{0}
).

Since
\left\|S_{0}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq K_{S_{0}}:=\varepsilon s^{2}\left\|U^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\gamma\theta_{0}s\left\|U^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\infty

is independent of
t
, by Cauchy–Schwarz and Young:

I_{4}\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{0}\,\Theta\,dx\;\leq\;K_{S_{0}}\,\left\|\Theta\right\|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}K_{S_{0}}^{2}\;\leq\;\mathcal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{2}K_{S_{0}}^{2}.

(32)

The term
\frac{1}{2}K_{S_{0}}^{2}
is a pure constant (independent of
t

and of the solution).

Substituting (
29
)–(
32
) into (
28
)
and absorbing the
\kappa/8
terms:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{3}^{\prime}e^{2T/\varepsilon}K_{W}^{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}+C_{4}\mathcal{E}+C_{5}^{\prime}\bigl(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}K_{S_{0}}^{2},

(33)

where the fourth power term from
I_{1}
and the
\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}
from
I_{2}
,
I_{3}

are treated in Part 3 below.

Part 3: Combining and Gronwall via a priori bound.

Add (
27
) and (
33
), and use

\tfrac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}

(second condition in (
17
)).

The terms
C_{1}e^{4T/\varepsilon}(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})^{2}

and
C_{5}^{\prime}(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2})
in (
33
) involve

\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}
, which is not contained in
\mathcal{E}
.We apply the
continuation argument
to bound these terms.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq M
for
t\in[0,T]
.
From (
27
), the viscous dissipation satisfies

\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\left\|v_{tx}\right\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{d}{dt}E_{1}+E_{2}E_{1}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}

pointwise, but integrating over
[0,t]
gives

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|v_{tx}\right\|^{2}\,ds\leq C(M,T)
. For the pointwise control
needed in the Gronwall ODE, we use the bound

\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}\leq(2/\varepsilon)\bigl(E_{2}(t)M+(\gamma/\varepsilon)\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+|\tfrac{d}{dt}E_{1}|\bigr)

to see that terms quadratic in
\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}
are
O(M^{2})
or
O(M)
.
Under the smallness condition (
18
) (so
M=C_{5}(\delta_{1})
is small),
the quartic contribution satisfies

C_{1}e^{4T/\varepsilon}\bigl(\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}\bigr)^{2}\;\leq\;C_{1}e^{4T/\varepsilon}\cdot C(M)\cdot\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;C_{4}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{E}(t),

(34)

where
C_{4}^{\prime}
depends on
M
but is finite for fixed
T
. Similarly

C_{5}^{\prime}\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}\leq C_{5}^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{E}(t)
. Combining all estimates:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;\bigl(E_{2}(t)+C_{4}\bigr)\,\mathcal{E}(t)+K,\qquad K\;:=\;\tfrac{1}{2}K_{S_{0}}^{2},

(35)

where
C_{4}
absorbs all the
\mathcal{E}
-linear terms.
Gronwall’s lemma applied to (
35
) gives

\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;\Bigl(\mathcal{E}(0)+K\!\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}(E_{2}+C_{4})}\,ds\Bigr)\exp\!\left(\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(E_{2}(s)+C_{4}\bigr)ds\right)\;\leq\;C_{5}.

(36)

Choosing
\delta_{1}
small enough that
C_{5}(\delta_{1})\leq M
closes the argument:

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{5}\leq M
for all
t\in[0,T]
, uniformly in
T
.
Integrating (
27
) and (
33
) over
[0,T]
and
using (
36
) yields the integral bounds on
\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}

and
\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{xxx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|v_{xxt}\right\|^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|\Theta_{xx}\right\|^{2}\,ds\;\leq\;C.

(37)

Proof.

Part 1: First derivative of the mechanical part.

[

The uniform a priori estimates of

The lower time-regularity of

Uniqueness.

We give a self-contained argument, independent of
[

,
13
,
9
]
,
by proving that any two solutions sharing the same initial data must coincide.
Let
(\rho^{(1)},\Theta^{(1)})
and
(\rho^{(2)},\Theta^{(2)})
be two solutions
of (
12
)–(
13
) in the regularity class of
Theorem
3.1
, with the same initial data

(\rho_{0},\phi)
. Set

w\;=\;\rho^{(1)}-\rho^{(2)},\qquad\Phi\;=\;\Theta^{(1)}-\Theta^{(2)}.

(52)

Then

satisfies

\displaystyle w_{tt}-(w_{x}-3U^{\prime 2}w_{x})_{x}+\delta\,w_{xxxx}-\varepsilon\,w_{xxt}

\displaystyle\;=\;\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{x}\bigr)_{x}+\gamma\,\Phi_{x},

(53)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}-\kappa\,\Phi_{xx}

\displaystyle\;=\;\mathcal{M},

(54)

with zero initial data

,

, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\;=\;(\rho^{(1)}_{x})^{3}-(\rho^{(2)}_{x})^{3}+3U^{\prime}[(\rho^{(1)}_{x})^{2}-(\rho^{(2)}_{x})^{2}],

(55)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon\bigl[(\rho^{(1)}_{xt})^{2}-(\rho^{(2)}_{xt})^{2}\bigr]-\gamma\theta_{0}\,w_{xt}+2\varepsilon\,W\,w_{xt}.

Note that the fixed source
S_{0}
cancels exactly in the difference, and the

2\varepsilon Ww_{xt}
to

Step 1: Energy functional for the difference.

We define

\Lambda(t)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|w_{x}\right\|^{2}(1-3M_{U}^{2})+\tfrac{\delta}{2}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Phi\right\|^{2}.

(57)

Note
\Lambda(t)\geq 0
since
3M_{U}^{2}<1
by the wave-profile
assumption (
9
).

Step 2: Mechanical energy inequality for the difference.

We multiply (
53
) by
w_{t}
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}
. The left-hand side
yields, after integration by parts:

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3M_{U}^{2})\left\|w_{x}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{\delta}{2}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|^{2}\right]+\varepsilon\left\|w_{xt}\right\|^{2}.

(58)

(The term from
\partial_{t}(3U^{\prime 2})=-6sU^{\prime}U^{\prime\prime}

C\left\|w_{x}\right\|^{2}\leq C\Lambda

The right-hand side produces two contributions.

Nonlinear term
\mathcal{N}

Using the factorisation

(a^{3}-b^{3})=(a-b)(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})
and

(a^{2}-b^{2})=(a-b)(a+b)
:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{N}_{x}\cdot w_{xt}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C\bigl(\left\|\rho^{(1)}_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\|\rho^{(2)}_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+M_{U}(\left\|\rho^{(1)}_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\rho^{(2)}_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}})\bigr)\left\|w_{x}\right\|\,\left\|w_{xt}\right\|

where

is uniformly bounded on

of Theorem

and the embedding

Coupling term
\gamma\Phi_{x}

\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{x}\,w_{t}\,dx\;\leq\;\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\left\|w_{xt}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\varepsilon}\left\|\Phi\right\|^{2},

(59)

after integrating by parts and applying Young’s inequality
(using
\Phi_{x}w_{t}=-\Phi w_{xt}+(w_{t}\Phi)_{x}

Combining and absorbing the

\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3M_{U}^{2})\left\|w_{x}\right\|^{2}+\tfrac{\delta}{2}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left\|w_{xt}\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{1}\bigl(\left\|w_{x}\right\|^{2}+\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Phi\right\|^{2}\bigr).

(60)

Step 3: Thermal energy inequality for the difference.

We multiply (
54
) by
\Phi
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}
:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\Phi\right\|^{2}+\kappa\left\|\Phi_{x}\right\|^{2}\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}\,\Phi\,dx.

(61)

We estimate

by bounding each of the three

\Phi

Viscous difference term:

Since
(\rho^{(1)}_{xt})^{2}-(\rho^{(2)}_{xt})^{2}=(\rho^{(1)}_{xt}+\rho^{(2)}_{xt})\,w_{xt}

\varepsilon\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho^{(1)}_{xt}+\rho^{(2)}_{xt})\,w_{xt}\,\Phi\,dx\right|\;\leq\;\varepsilon\left\|\rho^{(1)}_{xt}+\rho^{(2)}_{xt}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|w_{xt}\right\|\left\|\Phi\right\|,

\gamma\theta_{0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{xt}\,\Phi\,dx\right|\leq\gamma\theta_{0}\left\|w_{xt}\right\|\left\|\Phi\right\|

4
Decay Rates

In this section we prove that the temperature perturbation
\Theta

decays algebraically in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as
t\to+\infty
, so the material
returns to thermal equilibrium after a phase-transition event.
Physically, the latent heat released during a martensitic
transformation dissipates at a quantifiable rate.

We follow the spirit of Matsumura–Nishida. Since
\Theta
is coupled
to the mechanical perturbation through

\mathcal{E}(t)=E_{1}(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}
, we first prove
algebraic decay of
\mathcal{E}(t)
in the transformed variable

v=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\rho
; the thermal decay then follows. Under
smallness of the initial perturbation energy and
L^{1}
norms, the
nonlinear terms are absorbed into the dissipation, and we obtain a
Bernoulli-type differential inequality. The
L^{1}
norms of
v
are
uniformly bounded in
t
, so the interpolation inequality gives a
time-independent lower bound for the dissipation in terms of the energy.

Theorem 4.1

(Thermal equilibration and algebraic energy decay)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, assume additionally that

\rho_{0},\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(to control the long-wave behaviour needed for
the dissipation bound), and suppose the initial perturbation is
sufficiently small in the sense that

\mathcal{E}(0)+\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}\;\leq\;\delta_{0},

(67)

where
\delta_{0}\in(0,\delta_{1}]
depends only on
\varepsilon,\delta,\kappa,\gamma,\theta_{0}

(see (
82
) below). In particular
\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{1}
,
so (
67
) implies the smallness
condition (
18
) of Theorem
3.1
and the global
existence result applies. Then for all
t\geq 0
the
temperature perturbation decays algebraically:

\left\|\Theta(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{2C_{16}}{1+t}.

(68)

This bound is a consequence of the decay of the coupled energy
functional
\mathcal{E}(t)=E_{1}(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}
:

\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;\frac{C_{16}}{1+t},

(69)

which in turn implies

\left\|v(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{t}(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{xx}(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;C\,(1+t)^{-1},

(70)

where
v=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\rho
is the transformed mechanical perturbation.

Remark 4.2
.

The thermal decay (
68
) is the primary physical
conclusion: it states that the temperature field
\theta(x,t)
returns to
the reference temperature
\theta_{0}
at an algebraic rate, so the latent
heat released during a martensitic phase transition dissipates rather
than persisting. The energy decay (
69
) is the technical
vehicle that makes this possible, since
\Theta
and
v
are coupled
through
\mathcal{E}
.

The assumptions
\rho_{0},\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and (
67
) are
standard for decay results on
\mathbb{R}

(cf. Matsumura–Nishida
[
10
]
):
L^{1}
controls
the long-wave behaviour, while smallness allows the nonlinear terms
to be absorbed into the dissipation. Both are physically natural,
requiring the initial disturbance to be localised and of moderate
amplitude.

The rate
(1+t)^{-1}
improves upon the
(1+t)^{-1/4}
expected from
the
L^{2}
heat-kernel decay of
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
data, reflecting the
additional viscous dissipation and the time-independent lower bound
afforded by the exponential transformation. The decay of the

untransformed
mechanical perturbation
\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}

remains open, since
\rho=e^{t/\varepsilon}v
involves an unbounded
exponential factor; a different technique would be needed.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We divide the proof into three steps: we first establish a
time-independent lower bound on the dissipation, then use smallness
to absorb the nonlinear term, and finally close the decay via a Bernoulli
inequality.

Step 1: Lower bound.

We apply the interpolation inequality

\left\|f\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{int}}\,\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}}\,\left\|f_{x}\right\|,

(71)

(valid for
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, by
|f(x)|^{2}=2\int_{-\infty}^{x}f\,f_{x}\,dy
and Cauchy–Schwarz)
to the components of
\mathcal{E}
in the transformed variable
v
.
Since
v=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\rho
and
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by hypothesis,

\left\|v(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\;=\;e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\;\leq\;e^{-t/\varepsilon}\!\left(\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\rho_{t}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\,ds\right).

(72)

By the global
H^{3}
bounds from Theorem
3.1
and the
Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, the
right-hand side of (
12
) lies in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
uniformly in
s
,
so
\left\|\rho_{t}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{12}
for all
s\geq 0
.
Hence
\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+C_{12}\,t
,
and

\left\|v(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\;\leq\;e^{-t/\varepsilon}\bigl(\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+C_{12}\,t\bigr)\;\leq\;C_{13},

(73)

where the last inequality uses
\sup_{t\geq 0}\,e^{-t/\varepsilon}(A+Bt)=\max(A,A+B\varepsilon/e)<\infty
for any
A,B>0
.

Similarly,
\left\|v_{x}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{13}
and

\left\|v_{t}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{13}
by the same argument
applied to the spatial and temporal derivatives.

For the thermal perturbation, we establish
\left\|\Theta(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{13}^{\prime}

by a direct argument that avoids any circularity.
By the Duhamel formula applied to (
13
):

\Theta(\cdot,t)\;=\;G_{\kappa}(t)*\phi+\int_{0}^{t}G_{\kappa}(t-s)*S(\cdot,s)\,ds,

(74)

where
G_{\kappa}(t,x)=(4\pi\kappa t)^{-1/2}e^{-x^{2}/(4\kappa t)}
is the heat
kernel and
S=\varepsilon(\rho_{xt})^{2}-\gamma\theta_{0}\rho_{xt}
is the source.
Since
\left\|G_{\kappa}(t)*f\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}}
(heat semigroup
contraction in
L^{1}
):

\left\|\Theta(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\;\leq\;\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\!\left\|S(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\,ds.

(75)

It remains to bound
\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\,ds
uniformly in
t
.

First source term:

\left\|\varepsilon(\rho_{xt})^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}=\varepsilon\left\|\rho_{xt}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Since
\rho_{xt}=e^{t/\varepsilon}(v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x})
, we have

\left\|\rho_{xt}\right\|^{2}=e^{2t/\varepsilon}\left\|v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\right\|^{2}
.
By Lemma
3.2
,
\int_{0}^{T}\left\|v_{xt}\right\|^{2}\,ds\leq C_{5}
for all
T>0
.
Hence
\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon\left\|\rho_{xt}\right\|^{2}\,ds=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}e^{2s/\varepsilon}\left\|v_{xt}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}v_{x}\right\|^{2}\,ds
.
Since the integrand involves
e^{2s/\varepsilon}
, this integral is
not

uniformly bounded in
t
.

Second source term:

\left\|\gamma\theta_{0}\rho_{xt}\right\|_{L^{1}}
.
By the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\left\|f\right\|^{1/2}\left\|f_{x}\right\|^{1/2}
(valid for

f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
) and Hölder’s inequality in time:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!\left\|\rho_{xt}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\,ds

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\rho_{xt}\right\|^{1/2}\left\|\rho_{xxt}\right\|^{1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\rho_{xt}\right\|^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/4}\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\rho_{xxt}\right\|^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/4}\!\cdot t^{1/2},

which grows as
O(t^{1/2})
.

Thus the Duhamel bound (
75
)
does not directly yield a
uniform

L^{1}
bound on
\Theta
.
We instead obtain the needed control from the global
H^{2}
bound
of Lemma
3.4
. By the
Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on
\mathbb{R}
:

\left\|\Theta\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\;\leq\;C\,\left\|\Theta\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{1/2}\,\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{1/2}\;\leq\;C\,\left\|\Theta\right\|^{1/2}\,\left\|\Theta_{xx}\right\|^{1/2},

(76)

where we used
\left\|\Theta\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(\left\|\Theta\right\|+\left\|\Theta_{xx}\right\|)

and the equivalence of Sobolev norms on
\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\leq 2\mathcal{E}(t)\leq 2C_{5}
and

\left\|\Theta_{xx}\right\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{G}(t)\leq C_{10}
from
Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
:

\left\|\Theta(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\;\leq\;C\,C_{5}^{1/4}\,C_{10}^{1/4}\;=:\;C_{13}^{\prime}.

(77)

This bound is uniform in
t
, depends only on the parameters and
initial data (through
C_{5}
and
C_{10}
), and uses only the
a priori bounds from the three lemmas—no circularity.

Applying (
71
) to
\Theta
with the uniform
L^{1}

bound (
77
):

\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\leq C_{\mathrm{int}}\,C_{13}^{\prime}\,\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|
.
Together with the analogous bounds for the
v
-components
(where
L^{1}
norms are uniformly bounded by (
73
)):

\left\|v\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{xx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{14}\,\mathcal{D}(t)^{1/2},

(78)

where
C_{14}
depends on
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}
,
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}
,
and the parameters, but
not
on
t
. The key improvement over
working in
\rho
-variables is that the exponential decay

e^{-t/\varepsilon}
in
v=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\rho
eliminates the polynomial
growth that would otherwise arise in the
L^{1}
bounds.

For the energy
\mathcal{E}(t)=E_{1}(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}
,
we note that
E_{1}(t)
is controlled by
\left\|v\right\|^{2}
,
\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}
,

\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{2}
, and
\left\|v_{xx}\right\|^{2}
. The quartic term

e^{2t/\varepsilon}\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{4}/4
already appears inside
E_{1}

(cf. (
19
)), so no additional exponential factor is introduced.
By the Sobolev embedding
\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\|v_{xx}\right\|^{1/2}\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{1/2}
:

\frac{e^{2t/\varepsilon}\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{4}}{4}\;\leq\;C\,\left\|v_{xx}\right\|\,\left\|v_{x}\right\|^{3}\;\leq\;C\,\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}.

Under smallness
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\delta_{1}\leq 1
, we have
\mathcal{E}^{3/2}\leq\mathcal{E}
,
so all components of
\mathcal{E}
are controlled by (
78
).
Hence:

\mathcal{D}(t)\;\geq\;\frac{\mathcal{E}(t)^{2}}{C_{15}^{2}},

(79)

where
C_{15}
depends on
C_{14}
,
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}
, and
the parameters.

Step 2: The nonlinear term.

From the proof of Lemma
3.2
, the combined energy satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;-\,\mu\,\mathcal{D}(t)+C_{11}\,\mathcal{E}(t)^{2},

(80)

where
\mu=\min\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\bigr)>0
is the
combined dissipation rate and

\mathcal{D}(t)\;=\;\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}+\left\|v_{tx}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Theta_{x}\right\|^{2}

(81)

is the dissipation functional.
Substituting the lower bound (
79
) into the
nonlinear term:
C_{11}\mathcal{E}^{2}\leq C_{11}C_{15}^{2}\mathcal{D}
.
We choose the smallness parameter
\delta_{0}\in(0,\delta_{1}]
in (
67
)
small enough that the global bound
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{5}(\delta_{0})

from Lemma
3.2
satisfies
C_{5}\leq\delta_{1}
(ensuring
the lower bound (
79
) applies), and we impose

C_{11}\,C_{15}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\mu}{2},

(82)

which is achieved by taking
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}
,

\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\mathcal{E}(0)
sufficiently small (since

C_{15}
depends on the
L^{1}
norms of the initial data, and
these control the constant in (
79
)).
Under (
82
):

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;-\,\mu\,\mathcal{D}(t)+\frac{\mu}{2}\mathcal{D}(t)\;=\;-\,\frac{\mu}{2}\,\mathcal{D}(t).

(83)

Step 3: The decay estimate.

Substituting (
79
) into (
83
):

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;-\,\frac{\mu}{2C_{15}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(t)^{2}.

(84)

This is a Bernoulli inequality with no competing positive term.
Setting
\psi(t)=1/\mathcal{E}(t)
:

\frac{d}{dt}\psi(t)\;\geq\;\frac{\mu}{2C_{15}^{2}}\;=:\;\alpha>0.

(85)

Integrating:

\psi(t)\;\geq\;\psi(0)+\alpha\,t,

(86)

whence

\mathcal{E}(t)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\psi(0)+\alpha\,t}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{16}}{1+t}.

(87)

This gives
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{16}/(1+t)
, establishing (
69
).
Since
\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\leq 2\mathcal{E}(t)

(as
\mathcal{E}(t)=E_{1}(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}
),
bound (
68
) follows immediately.
The component bounds (
70
) follow from the definition of

E_{1}(t)
: since
E_{1}(t)\geq\left\|v\right\|^{2}/(2\varepsilon^{2})
,
E_{1}(t)\geq\left\|v_{t}\right\|^{2}/2
,
and
E_{1}(t)\geq(\delta/2)\left\|v_{xx}\right\|^{2}
, each squared norm is bounded by

C\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{16}C/(1+t)
.∎

Remark 4.3
.

The Bernoulli argument yields
\mathcal{E}(t)=O((1+t)^{-1})
,
which is optimal given (
79
) and implies the
thermal equilibration rate (
68
).
The improvement over the naive
(1+t)^{-1/2}
rate arises because
the uniform
L^{1}
bounds on
v=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\rho
yield a
time-independent constant in the lower bound (
79
).

5
Concluding Remarks

The main theorem shows that the strain
u_{x}
remains globally smooth,
so the thermoelastic phase-transition layers are smooth and of finite
thickness. Two related questions remain.

Remark 5.1
.

The decay in Section
4
applies to the temperature
perturbation
\Theta
and to the coupled energy
\mathcal{E}(t)
, but the decay
of
\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}
in the original mechanical variable remains
to be addressed.
Lemmas
3.2
–
3.4
bound
\rho
in
H^{3}
uniformly in
T
, so

\rho
does not grow; the question is whether a quantitative rate
holds. The exponential factor in
\rho=e^{t/\varepsilon}v
prevents a direct
transfer of the rate from
v
to
\rho
.

Remark 5.2
.

The smallness condition (
18
) is used in Lemma
3.2

to control the quartic and quadratic coupling terms. Whether it can be
removed on
\mathbb{R}
remains to be addressed. The bounded-domain results
of
[
12
,
17
]
do not need it, but they use
Poincaré’s inequality, which is not available here.
