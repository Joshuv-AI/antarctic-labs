Abstract
Quantum impurity models provide a paradigmatic setting for studying Kondo screening, boundary criticality, and impurity entropies. While these phenomena are well understood in unitary systems, their fate in non-Hermitian many-body settings remains largely unexplored. We study a 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathscr{PT} -symmetric quantum impurity model consisting of a unitary S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) 1 SU(2)_{1} Wess–Zumino–Witten bulk coupled to two impurity spins through complex-conjugate boundary Kondo interactions. Using an integrable lattice realization with 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathscr{PT} -symmetric boundary impurities, solved by the Bethe Ansatz and benchmarked against finite-temperature matrix-product-state calculations, we determine the impurity contribution to the free energy and entropy. In the Kondo-screened regime, where the spectrum remains entirely real and the impurities are screened by many-body Kondo clouds, we find that the impurity entropy decreases monotonically from ln ⁡ 4 \ln 4 in the ultraviolet to 0 0 in the infrared. This monotonic flow persists despite the nonunitary nature of the boundary interaction, which places the system beyond the standard assumptions of the g g -theorem.

A complementary field-theoretic characterization is provided by the boundary renormalization-group flow between distinct conformal boundary fixed points. In unitary systems, this flow is accompanied by a progressive reduction of the impurity degrees of freedom. The corresponding fixed-point degeneracies are encoded in the Affleck–Ludwig boundary

Recent works have suggested that generalized notions of boundary and defect entropy can remain meaningful in non-unitary critical systems whenever the spectrum relevant to the partition function remains real

These studies have revealed that non-Hermitian Kondo models can exhibit renormalization-group behavior with no analog in conventional impurity physics. In particular, Ref.

Such behavior has no counterpart in conventional unitary impurity systems and raises a fundamental question: is boundary unitarity actually necessary for impurity entropy to remain a meaningful and monotonic characterization of boundary renormalization-group flow?

Therefore, the Kondo regime provides a natural setting in which to ask whether the monotonic temperature dependence of the impurity entropy survives beyond unitarity. To address this question, we study an integrable
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric extension of the non-Hermitian Kondo model in which two boundary impurities are coupled through complex-conjugate Kondo interactions. Upon non-Abelian bosonization
29
, the spin sector is described by the unitary
SU(2)_{1}
Wess–Zumino–Witten theory, while the impurities appear as a pair of non-Hermitian defect lines related by
\mathscr{PT}
symmetry. The resulting continuum Hamiltonian is
H=H_{\rm WZW}+H_{\rm imp}
with

H_{\mathrm{imp}}=\lambda\left(\mathbf{J}_{L}+\mathbf{J}_{R}\right)_{x=0}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{L}+\lambda^{*}\left(\mathbf{J}_{L}+\mathbf{J}_{R}\right)_{x=L}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{R}.

(2)

Thus, non-unitarity enters through the insertion of a pair of complex-valued defect lines at the boundaries. The central question is whether the associated impurity entropy remains a meaningful thermodynamic characterization of the resulting boundary crossover despite the loss of unitarity. Holographic realizations of related
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric BCFTs with exactly marginal defect operators have recently been investigated in Ref.
19
. In contrast, the boundary Kondo interaction considered here is marginally relevant and generates a genuine boundary RG flow.

We investigate a lattice regularization of the continuum problem, realized through an integrable
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric spin chain. The microscopic Hamiltonian is

H=J\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j+1}+J_{\mathrm{imp}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}+J_{\mathrm{imp}}^{*}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{N}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R},

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R}
are the two impurity spins at the left and right ends of the chain. We parametrize the complex boundary coupling as

J_{\mathrm{imp}}=\frac{J}{1-\left(\beta+i\gamma\right)^{2}}.

The complex-conjugate boundary couplings render the Hamiltonian
\mathscr{PT}
symmetric, while
\gamma
and
\beta
control the degree of non-Hermiticity and the boundary phase structure, respectively.

The spectrum and phase diagram of this model were analyzed in Ref.

We focus on the regime
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
, which is continuously connected to the conventional antiferromagnetic Kondo phase. In this regime, no boundary-bound modes are present, the spectrum remains entirely real, and the low-energy excitations are described by conventional Bethe strings. For
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
, boundary-bound modes appear and the low-energy physics changes qualitatively

\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric deformation of the conventional Kondo problem, we restrict attention to the Kondo-screened phase throughout.

Using the exact Bethe Ansatz solution, we compute the impurity contribution to the free energy and the corresponding impurity entropy associated with the pair of
\mathscr{PT}
-conjugate impurities. We find that the impurity entropy flows from
\ln 4
in the ultraviolet, where the two spin-
\frac{1}{2}
impurities are effectively free, to
0
in the infrared, where both impurities are screened. Moreover, the flow is monotonic throughout the Kondo regime (see Fig.
3
). This is particularly noteworthy because non-Hermitian Kondo models are known to exhibit unconventional RG flows, including flow reversal
23
;
15
, while the Affleck–Ludwig
g
-theorem has only been established for unitary boundary conformal field theories.

The exact solution is obtained from the Bethe Ansatz equations

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu_{j}-\frac{i}{2}}{\mu_{j}+\frac{i}{2}}\right)^{2N}\prod_{\upsilon=\pm}\frac{\mu_{j}+\upsilon(\beta+i\gamma)-\frac{i}{2}}{\mu_{j}+\upsilon(\beta+i\gamma)+\frac{i}{2}}\frac{\mu_{j}+\upsilon(\beta-i\gamma)-\frac{i}{2}}{\mu_{j}+\upsilon(\beta-i\gamma)+\frac{i}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\prod_{l\neq j}^{M}\prod_{\upsilon=\pm}\frac{(\mu_{j}+\upsilon\mu_{l}-i)}{(\mu_{j}+\upsilon\mu_{l}+i)}.

(4)

The energy and momentum of an eigenstate are determined by the corresponding Bethe root configuration
\{\mu_{j},\,j=1,\ldots,M\}
. In particular, the energy takes the form

E=-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{2J}{\mu_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}+J(L-1)+\frac{2J(\gamma^{2}-\beta^{2}+1)}{\beta^{4}+2\beta^{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)+(\gamma^{2}+1)^{2}}.

(5)

In the thermodynamic limit
L\to\infty
, the Bethe roots organize into continuous distributions of strings and holes. In the Kondo phase (
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
), all eigenvalues remain real
13
. The corresponding finite-size Hamiltonian therefore admits a complete biorthogonal eigenbasis and a quasi-Hermitian description
21
;
22
, providing a natural framework for thermal equilibrium in the non-Hermitian setting
16
. We may therefore introduce biorthogonal left and right eigenstates,

H|R_{n}\rangle=E_{n}|R_{n}\rangle,\qquad\langle L_{n}|H=E_{n}\langle L_{n}|,

(6)

with

\langle L_{n}|R_{m}\rangle=\delta_{nm},\qquad\sum_{n}|R_{n}\rangle\langle L_{n}|=\mathbf{1}.

(7)

Using the completeness relation above, the basis-independent trace may be evaluated in the biorthogonal eigenbasis. Since all eigenvalues remain real in the
\mathscr{PT}
-unbroken phase, the thermal partition function is therefore well defined and can be written as

Z=\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-H/T}=\sum_{n}e^{-E_{n}/T}.

(8)

Introducing the densities
\sigma_{n}(\lambda)
and hole densities
\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda)
associated with
n
-strings, the thermodynamic Bethe equations take the form

\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda)=f_{n}^{\mathrm{str}}(\lambda)-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{nm}\sigma_{m}(\lambda).

(9)

The free energy is obtained from the Yang–Yang functional
31

F=E-hS^{z}-T\mathcal{S},

(10)

where
\mathcal{S}
is the Yang–Yang entropy. Minimizing the free energy with respect to the densities and introducing
\eta_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda)}{\sigma_{n}(\lambda)}
, yields the thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz equations

\ln\eta_{n}(\lambda)=-\frac{2\pi}{T}\frac{\delta_{n,1}}{\cosh(\pi\lambda)}+G\ln\!\left[1+\eta_{n+1}\right]+G\ln\!\left[1+\eta_{n-1}\right],

(11)

where

Gf(\lambda)=\int d\mu\,\frac{f(\mu)}{2\cosh\!\left[\pi(\lambda-\mu)\right]}.

(12)

The hierarchy is supplemented by the boundary conditions
\eta_{0}(\lambda)=0
, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{[n+1]\ln\left(1+\eta_{n}\right)-[n]\ln\left(1+\eta_{n+1}\right)\right\}=-\frac{h}{T},

(13)

where
h
denotes an external magnetic field.

For an open chain, the free energy contains an extensive
bulk contribution together with both impurity-independent
and impurity-dependent
O(1)
boundary terms. We define
the impurity free energy as

F_{\rm imp}(T)=F_{\rm total}(T)-F_{0}(T),

(14)

where
F_{0}
denotes the free energy of the corresponding
impurity-free system. This subtraction eliminates the
extensive bulk contribution and the impurity-independent
boundary terms associated with the exact

g
-function
25
;
10
, leaving only the
impurity contribution of interest. Notice that, as in the conventional Kondo problem, the TBA equations themselves are independent of the impurity couplings. The impurity parameters enter only through the
O(1)
boundary contribution to the free energy. Since the two boundary couplings are related by
\mathscr{PT}
conjugation, the physical impurity free energy is obtained from the combined contribution of the two boundaries,

F^{\mathrm{imp}}=F^{\mathrm{imp}}_{L}+F^{\mathrm{imp}}_{R}.

(15)

The evaluation of the impurity free energy is identical to that of the Hermitian boundary Kondo problem; we therefore omit the intermediate steps and refer the reader to the Supplementary Materials of Ref.
35
. For the present case of complex-conjugate boundary couplings, the resulting impurity free energy is

F_{\rm imp}=\frac{-T}{2}\sum_{\upsilon=\pm}\int d\lambda\,\frac{\cosh\left[\pi\left(\lambda+\upsilon\gamma\right)\right]\cos(\pi\beta)\ln\left(1+\eta_{1}(\lambda)\right)}{\cosh^{2}\left[\pi\left(\lambda+\upsilon\gamma\right)\right]-\sin^{2}(\pi\beta)}.

(16)

The impurity entropy is obtained from the standard thermodynamic relation

S_{\rm imp}=-\frac{\partial F_{\rm imp}}{\partial T}
.

The ultraviolet and infrared limits of the impurity entropy can be obtained analytically. In the high-temperature limit, the driving term in the TBA equations vanishes, and the hierarchy admits the constant solution
\eta_{n}=n(n+2)
. Substituting this solution into the impurity free energy yields

S_{\rm imp}(T\to\infty)=\ln 4,

(17)

corresponding to two free spin-
\frac{1}{2}
impurities.

In the opposite limit
T\to 0
, the asymptotic solution of the TBA equations gives

F_{\rm imp}=-\frac{T^{2}}{12}\cos(\pi\beta)\cosh(\pi\gamma)+O(T^{3}),

(18)

from which it follows that
S_{\rm imp}(T\to 0)=0
. Thus, despite the non-Hermitian boundary coupling, both impurities are completely screened in the infrared. Matching the low-temperature free energy to the universal local Fermi-liquid form
F_{\rm imp}=-\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6T_{K}}

11
;
3
,
we obtain the Kondo scale

T_{K}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\cos(\pi\beta)\cosh(\pi\gamma)}.

(19)

Note that increasing
\gamma
suppresses the Kondo scale exponentially,

T_{K}\sim e^{-\pi\gamma}
for
\gamma\gg 1
. The impurity entropy therefore interpolates between the ultraviolet value
\ln 4
and the infrared value
0
, with the crossover governed by
T_{K}
. The crucial remaining question is whether the impurity entropy remains monotonic throughout the crossover, despite the absence of the assumptions underlying the Affleck–Ludwig
g
-theorem. The full crossover between the ultraviolet and infrared fixed points is obtained by solving the TBA equations numerically. To benchmark the exact thermodynamic solution, we performed independent finite-temperature matrix-product-state calculations using the ITensor library

\rho\propto\mathbf{1}

N=100

N=102

In this work, we investigated impurity entropy in an integrable
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric non-Hermitian Kondo model. Using the exact Bethe Ansatz solution, supplemented by finite-temperature matrix-product-state calculations, we determined the impurity contribution to the free energy and entropy throughout the Kondo-screened phase. We found that the impurity entropy decreases monotonically from the ultraviolet value
\ln 4
to the infrared value
0
, corresponding to the complete screening of the two impurity spins.

The persistence of monotonicity is particularly striking because the boundary perturbation is non-Hermitian and lies outside the class of boundary RG flows for which the Affleck–Ludwig g-theorem has been established. Nevertheless, throughout the
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric Kondo phase, the impurity entropy continues to behave as a monotonic measure of impurity degrees of freedom. Furthermore, the low-temperature thermodynamics exhibits universal scaling governed by a single Kondo scale
T_{K}
, revealing a common infrared structure throughout the phase.

Our results demonstrate that key signatures of Kondo screening and boundary criticality persist well beyond the context of unitary quantum impurity systems. More importantly, they show that boundary unitarity is not essential for monotonic impurity entropy. Instead, our exact solution indicates that
\mathscr{PT}
symmetry together with an unbroken real spectrum is sufficient to preserve the irreversible thermodynamic flow between boundary fixed points. Since renormalization-group irreversibility has already been rigorously proven for broad classes of non-unitary but
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric quantum field theories through a generalization
5
of Zamolodchikov’s
c
-theorem
34
, our exact results strongly suggest that an analogous rigorous extension of the Affleck–Ludwig
g
-theorem
1
;
9
;
4
to
\mathscr{PT}
-symmetric boundary renormalization-group flows should also exist. Deriving such a theorem would place the monotonic impurity entropy observed here on the same rigorous footing as boundary entropy in unitary quantum field theory.
