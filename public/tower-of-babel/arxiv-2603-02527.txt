Unitary modules over the gap- p p Virasoro algebras
Chengkang Xu

Abstract
. For any irreducible Harish-Chandra module V V over the gap- p p Virasoro algebra, we determine the condition for V V to be unitary.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
17B10, 17B65, 17B68

1.
Introduction

Unitary modules for Lie algebras play significant roles in many areas of mathematics and physics,
such as statistical mechanics and the two-dimensional conformal quantum field theory.
In
[
4
]
, the authors classified irreducible unitary Harish-Chandra modules for the Virasoro algebra and the Virasoro superalgebras (both Ramond and Neveu-Schwarz cases).
These modules are the modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules satisfying certain conditions.
Unitary modules were also considered for many other infinite dimensional Lie algebras over the past decades,
such as the affine Kac-Moody algebras
[
2
,
3
]
,
Lie algebras of infinite matrices
[
11
,
12
]
,
the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
7
,
17
]
,
the
W
-infinity algebras
[
6
]
,
the
W
-algebra
W(2,2)
[
15
]
,
the Schrödinger-Virasoro algebra
[
16
]
,
and so on.

In this paper we study unitary modules over the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
which was first introduced in
[
13
]
.
The gap-
p
Virasoro algebra has a close relation to the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and the algebras of derivations over rational quantum tori.
It can be realized as the universal central extensions of some subalgebras of these two Lie algebras.
Moreover, when
p=2
,
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra introduced in
[
1
]
and named in
[
8
]
.
In
[
13
]
, irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
\mathfrak{g}
were classified.
Such modules are modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules.

Let us now briefly describe the organization of this paper.
In Section 2, we recall the notions of Harish-Chandra modules and unitary modules, and some related results about the Virasoro algebra and the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
In Section 3, we explicitly determine the irreducibility of the Verma modules, and the structures of irreducible highest weight modules for
\mathfrak{g}
.
Section 4 is dedicated to determine the conjugate-linear anti-involutions of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and Section 5 is to determine the conditions for irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
\mathfrak{g}
to be unitary.

Throughout this paper, we use the symbols
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{C}
to denote the sets of integers, non-negative integers, positive integers, real numbers, complex numbers respectively,
and for any subset
N
of
\mathbb{C}
we write
N^{\times}=N\setminus\{0\}
.
Denote by
S^{1}
the set of complex numbers of modulus one.
All vector spaces and algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
, the dual space of a vector space
V
is denoted by
V^{*}
, and the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is denoted by
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L})
.
We fix a positive integer
p>1
for this paper,
and for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote by
\overline{k}
the residue of
k
by
p
.
Finally, for
a\in\mathbb{C}
we denote by
\overline{a}
the conjugate of
a
,
and for any finite set
A
we denote by
|A|
the cardinal number of
A
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some related notations and known results about the Virasoro algebra and the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

The
gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
is a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a basis

\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)},C_{j}\,\mid\,n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}

and nontrivial relations

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{0}\delta_{m+n,0},

\displaystyle[I_{m}^{(i)},I_{n}^{(j)}]=\left(m+\frac{i}{p}\right)\delta_{i+j,p}\delta_{m+n+1,0}C_{\min\{i,p-i\}},\quad[L_{m},I_{n}^{(i)}]=-\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)I_{m+n}^{(i)},

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
, and

[\frac{p}{2}]
denotes the largest integer less than or equal to
\frac{p}{2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

To simplify notations, we write
C_{i}=C_{p-i}
for
[\frac{p}{2}]\leq i\leq p-1
in this paper.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra with a triangular decomposition in the sense of
[
10
]
,
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{-}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{+}
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is an abelian subalgebra.

Definition 2.3
.

A module
V
over
\mathcal{L}
is called a
weight module

if
V=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}V_{\lambda}
where
V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V\mid xv=\lambda(x)v\text{ for any }x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}\}
.
A weight
\mathcal{L}
-module
V
is called a
Harish-Chandra module
if
\dim V_{\lambda}<\infty
for any
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}
,
called a
module of intermediate series
if all
\dim V_{\lambda}\leq 1
,
and called a
highest
(resp.
lowest
)
weight module
of
highest
(resp.
lowest
)
weight

\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}

if
V
is generated by a
highest
(resp.
lowest
)
weight vector

v
such that
\mathcal{L}_{+}v=0
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{-}v=0
) and
xv=\varphi(x)v
for any
x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}
and
\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}
be the 1-dimensional module over
\mathcal{L}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{+}
defined by

xv_{\varphi}=\varphi(x)v_{\varphi},\ \mathcal{L}_{+}v_{\varphi}=0\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}.

Then induced
\mathcal{L}
-module

M_{\mathcal{L},\varphi}=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{+})}\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}

is called the
Verma module
of highest weight
\varphi
.
For any highest weight module
V
of highest weight
\varphi
with a highest weight vector
v
,
there is a natural
\mathcal{L}
-module epimorphism from
M_{\mathcal{L},\varphi}
to
V
defined by
v_{\varphi}\mapsto v
.

Set

\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{0},C_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq p-1\right\},

which is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Clearly,
\mathfrak{h}
exhausts all semisimple elements in
\mathfrak{g}
,
and the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, where

\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)}\mid n<0,1\leq i\leq p-1\right\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n+1},I_{n}^{(i)}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq p-1\right\}.

Set

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},C_{0}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{I_{n}^{(i)},C_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1\right\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{V}
is a Virasoro algebra,
\mathcal{H}
is a Heisenberg algebra and an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now we recall irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
and the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
which were classified in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
respectively.
They are modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules.

The Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
has a triangular decomposition
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{-}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{+}
, where

\mathcal{V}_{\pm}=\mathcal{V}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n}\mid\pm n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\},\quad\mathcal{V}_{0}=\mathcal{V}\cap\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{0},C_{0}\right\}.

Then we have the notion of Verma module
M_{\mathcal{V},\phi}
over
\mathcal{V}
of highest weight
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{*}
.
The following results are well known, and one may see
[
5
]
for reference.

Proposition 2.4
.

The Verma module
M_{\mathcal{V},\phi}
over
\mathcal{V}
is irreducible if and only if
\Phi_{h,c}(\alpha,\beta)=0
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
h=\phi(L_{0}),c=\phi(C_{0})
and

\Phi_{h,c}(\alpha,\beta)=\left(h+\frac{(\alpha^{2}-1)(c-13)}{24}+\frac{(\alpha\beta-1)}{2}\right)\left(h+\frac{(\beta^{2}-1)(c-13)}{24}+\frac{(\alpha\beta-1)}{2}\right)+\frac{(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})^{2}}{16}.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, there is a
\mathcal{V}
-module structure on the space
\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}v_{n}
with actions

L_{n}v_{k}=-(a+k+bn)v_{n+k},\quad C_{0}v_{k}=0\quad\text{for any }n,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The resulting
\mathcal{V}
-module of intermediate series is usually denoted by
V(a,b)
.
It is well known that
V(a,b)
is reducible if and only if
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
b\in\{0,1\}
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
F=(F_{i,j})
be a
(p-1)\times p
matrix with index
1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq p-1
satisfying that

F_{s,i}F_{r,\,\overline{i+s}}=F_{r,i}F_{s,\,\overline{i+r}}\quad\text{for any }1\leq r,s\leq p-1,0\leq i\leq p-1,

and

i+j\in\text{col}(F)\quad\text{if }j\in\text{col}(F)\text{ and }i\in\text{row}(F),

where
\text{col}(F)=\{j\mid F_{ij}\neq 0\text{ for some }i\}
and
\text{row}(F)=\{i\mid F_{ij}\neq 0\text{ for some }j\}
.
Then there is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
the space
\oplus_{j\in\text{col}(F),k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}v_{k+\frac{j}{p}}
with actions

L_{m}v_{k+\frac{j}{p}}=-\left(a+k+\frac{j}{p}+bm\right)v_{m+k+\frac{j}{p}},\quad I_{m}^{(i)}v_{k+\frac{j}{p}}=F_{i,j}v_{m+k+\frac{i+j}{p}},\quad C_{s}v_{k+\frac{j}{p}}=0

for
m,k\in\mathbb{Z},j\in\text{col}(F),1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq s\leq p-1
.
The resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module is a module of intermediate series and we denote it by
V(a,b,F)
in this paper.
For any
j\in\text{col}(F)
, denote

V_{(j)}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}v_{k+\frac{j}{p}},

which is a
\mathcal{V}
-module of intermediate series isomorphic to
V(a+\frac{j}{p},b)
,
and
V(a,b,F)=\bigoplus_{j\in\text{col}(F)}V_{(j)}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
13
, Proposition 4.2]

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V(a,b,F)
is reducible if and only if
|\text{col}(F)|=1,a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
b\in\{0,1\}
.

Next we recall some notions and results about unitary modules over
\mathcal{V}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra.
An
\mathcal{L}
-module
V
is called
unitary
if
V
admits a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that

\langle xu,v\rangle=\langle u,\theta(x)v\rangle\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathcal{L},u,v\in V,

where
\theta:\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}
is a
conjugate-linear anti-involution
on
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., a map satisfying that

\theta^{2}=\mathrm{identity},\ \theta(x+y)=\theta(x)+\theta(y),\ \theta(ax)=\overline{a}\theta(x),\ \theta([x,y])=[\theta(y),\theta(x)]

for any
a\in\mathbb{C},x,y\in\mathcal{L}
.

It is worthwhile to notice that unitary weight modules are completely reducible.
The following propositions are from
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Any conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathcal{V}
is one of the following types.

(i)
\theta_{\alpha}^{+}(L_{n})=\alpha^{n}L_{-n},\ \theta_{\alpha}^{+}(C_{0})=C_{0}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

(ii)
\theta_{\alpha}^{-}(L_{n})=-\alpha^{n}L_{n},\ \theta_{\alpha}^{-}(C_{0})=-C_{0}
with
\alpha\in S^{1}
the set of complex numbers of modulus one.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
V
be a nontrivial irreducible weight module over
\mathcal{V}
.

(1) If
V
is unitary for some conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta
of
\mathcal{V}
, then
\theta=\theta_{\alpha}^{+}
for some
\alpha>0
.

(2) If
V
is unitary for
\theta_{\alpha}^{+}
with
\alpha>0
, then
V
is unitary for
\theta_{1}^{+}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(1) The
\mathcal{V}
-module
V(a,b)
is unitary for
\theta_{1}^{+}
if and only if
a\in\mathbb{R},b\in\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}
.

(2) The irreducible highest weight module of highest weight
\phi
over
\mathcal{V}
is unitary for
\theta_{1}^{+}
if and only if
\phi(C_{0})\geq 1,\phi(L_{0})\geq 0
, or there exists an integer
m\geq 2
and
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

0\leq r<s<m,\quad\phi(C_{0})=1-\frac{6}{m(m+1)},\quad\phi(L_{0})=\frac{(mr+s)^{2}-1}{4m(m+1)}.

3.
Highest weight modules

In this section we determine the irreducibility of the Verma modules and structures of irreducible highest weight modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

Recall the Verma
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}
of highest weight
\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.
Denote

J=\{1\leq i\leq p-1\mid\varphi(C_{i})\neq 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad J^{C}=\{1\leq i\leq p-1\mid\varphi(C_{i})=0\}.

Since
C_{i}=C_{p-i}
, the set
J
is
symmetrical
in the sense that
p-i\in J
if
i\in J
. The set
J^{C}
is also symmetrical.
Then we have subalgebras

\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)},C_{0},C_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in J^{C}\right\}\text{ and }\mathcal{H}_{J}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{I_{n}^{(i)},C_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in J\right\}

of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{H}_{J}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}\ltimes\mathcal{H}_{J}=\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\mathcal{H}_{J}=\mathcal{H},\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}=\mathcal{V}
if
J^{C}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, they have triangular decompositions induced from
\mathfrak{g}
.
Define a linear function
\psi\in\left(\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}\right)^{*}
by

\psi(L_{0})=\varphi(L_{0})-\sum_{j\in J}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}};\quad\psi(C_{0})=\varphi(C_{0})-|J|;\quad\psi(C_{i})=0\quad\text{ for }i\in J^{C}.

(3.1)

Then we have the Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}
with highest weight
\psi
.
Clearly, if
J^{C}\neq\emptyset
,
then
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathcal{H}_{\{i,p-i\}}\right)v_{\psi}
generates a proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
,
where
i\in J^{C}
,

\mathcal{H}_{\{i,p-i\}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}I_{n}^{(i)}\oplus\mathbb{C}I_{n}^{(p-i)},

and
v_{\psi}
is a highest weight vector in
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
.
So
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
is reducible.
Denote by
\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}
the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{V}
.
Since any highest weight
\mathcal{V}
-module of highest weight
\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}
can be extended to a highest weight
\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}
-module of highest weight
\psi
by requiring that
I_{n}^{(i)}
and
C_{i}
act trivially for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i\in J^{C}
,
we see that the irreducible quotient of
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
is equivalent to the irreducible quotient of the Verma
\mathcal{V}
-module
M_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}
.

By demanding
\mathcal{H}_{J}M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}=0
, we turn
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
into a
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
and should denote the resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module by
\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
.

Set
\phi=\varphi\mid_{\mathcal{H}_{J}}
the restriction of
\varphi
to
\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{H}_{J}
.
We have the Verma module
M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}
of
\mathcal{H}_{J}
with highest weight
\phi
,
which one can easily prove is irreducible.
The following result is a special case of
[
14
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is an irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on the space
M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}
with actions given by

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}I_{n}^{(i)}\ &\text{if}\ i\in J,\\
0\ &\text{if}\ i\notin J,\end{cases}\ \ \ \ \quad C_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}\phi(C_{i})\ &\text{if}\ i\in J,\\
0\ &\text{if}\ i\notin J,\end{cases},\quad C_{0}\mapsto|J|,

(3.2)

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{2\phi(C_{j})}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}:I_{k}^{(j)}I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\right)+\delta_{n,0}\sum_{j\in I}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}},

(3.3)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
and the normal ordered product is defined by

:I_{k}^{(i)}I_{l}^{(p-i)}:=\begin{cases}I_{k}^{(i)}I_{l}^{(p-i)}\quad&\text{ if }k<l;\\
I_{l}^{(p-i)}I_{k}^{(i)}\quad&\text{ if }k\geq l.\end{cases}

We denote by
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
the resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module from Proposition
3.1
.
Moreover, we have a characterization of the Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

(1)
M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}\cong\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
if
J\neq\emptyset
.

(2) Any submodule of
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
is of the form
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{e}
,
where
W
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}}
-submodule of
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}
and
W^{e}
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-module obtained from
W
in the same fashion as
\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
.
Then the irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\varphi
is isomorphic to
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
if
J\neq\emptyset
,
and isomorphic to
\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
if
J=\emptyset
,
where
V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}
is the unique irreducible quotient of the Verma
\mathcal{V}
-module
M_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}

(i.e., the irreducible highest weight
\mathcal{V}
-module of highest weight
\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}
),
and
\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-module obtained from
V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}
in the same fashion as
\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
.

(3) The Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}
is irreducible if and only if
J=\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
and

\Phi(\alpha,\beta)\Phi(\beta,\alpha)+(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})^{2}=0\text{ for some }\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

where

\Phi(\alpha,\beta)=4\varphi(L_{0})-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{j(p-j)}{p^{2}}+\frac{(\alpha^{2}-1)(\varphi(C_{0})-p-12)}{6}+2(\alpha\beta-1).

Proof.

One can check that
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\left(M_{\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\psi}\right)^{e}
is a highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\varphi
.
Then (1) follows from the PBW theorem.
(2) follows from (1) and
[
14
, Proposition 3.5]
,
and (3) follows from (2) and Proposition
2.4
.
∎

4.
Conjugate-linear anti-involutions of
\mathfrak{g}

In this section we determine all conjugate-linear anti-involutions of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote

\mathfrak{c}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{C_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq p-1\right\}

the center of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}L_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{c}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\theta
be a conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

(1)
\theta(\mathfrak{c})=\mathfrak{c},\theta(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
and
\theta(L_{0})=\lambda L_{0}+C(L_{0})
, where
\lambda=\pm 1
and
C(L_{0})\in\mathfrak{c}
.

(2)
\theta(C_{i})=\lambda A_{0,i}A_{-1,p-i}C_{i}
and

\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})=\begin{cases}A_{-n-1,p-i}I_{-n-1}^{(p-i)}&\text{if }\lambda=1,\\
A_{n,i}I_{n}^{(i)}&\text{if }\lambda=-1,\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
and all
A_{j,k}
are nonzero complex numbers.
In particular,
\theta(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
x\in\mathfrak{c},y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
z=\theta(y)
.
Then
y=\theta^{2}(y)=\theta(z)
and
[\theta(x),y]=\theta[z,x]=0
, proving that
\theta(\mathfrak{c})=\mathfrak{c}
.
Since

[\theta(L_{0}),\theta(L_{n})]=\theta[L_{n},L_{0}]=n\theta(L_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad[\theta(L_{0}),\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})]=\theta[I_{n}^{(i)},L_{0}]=\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
, we see that
\theta(L_{0})\in\mathfrak{h}\setminus\mathfrak{c}
.
So
\theta(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
and we may write

\theta(L_{0})=\lambda L_{0}+C(L_{0})\quad\text{for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{C},C(L_{0})\in\mathfrak{c}.

From
L_{0}=\theta^{2}(L_{0})=\theta(\lambda L_{0}+C(L_{0}))=\overline{\lambda}\lambda L_{0}+\overline{\lambda}C(L_{0})+\theta(C(L_{0}))
we know that
\lambda\in S^{1}
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
, write (a finite sum)

\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}A_{k,j}I_{k}^{(j)}+C(I_{n}^{(i)}),\quad\text{where }C(I_{n}^{(i)})\in\mathfrak{c},A_{k,j}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

From the equation

\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})=\theta[I_{n}^{(i)},L_{0}]=[\theta(L_{0}),\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})]=-\lambda\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\left(k+\frac{j}{p}\right)A_{k,j}I_{k}^{(j)}

(4.2)

we see that
-\lambda\left(k+\frac{j}{p}\right)=n+\frac{i}{p}
for a fixed pair of
k,j
.
This implies that
\lambda
is a rational number and hence
\lambda=\pm 1
, proving (1).
Moreover, we have

\begin{cases}k=-n-1,j=p-i&\text{ if }\lambda=1,\\
k=n,j=i&\text{ if }\lambda=-1.\end{cases}

(2) The equation (
4.2
) also implies that
C(I_{n}^{(i)})=0
.
Then we get (
4.1
) from the above equation.
For any
1\leq i\leq p-1
, one can see from the following equation

\frac{i}{p}\theta(C_{i})=\theta[I_{0}^{(i)},I_{-1}^{(p-i)}]=[\theta(I_{-1}^{(p-i)}),\theta(I_{0}^{(i)})]=\lambda A_{0,i}A_{-1,p-i}\frac{i}{p}C_{i}

that
\theta(C_{i})=\lambda A_{0,i}A_{-1,p-i}C_{i}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Any conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathfrak{g}
is one of the following types.

(1)
\theta^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(L_{n})=\alpha^{n}L_{-n},\ \theta^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(I_{n}^{(i)})=\alpha^{n}\beta_{p-i}I_{-n-1}^{(p-i)},\ \theta^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(C_{0})=C_{0},\ \theta^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(C_{i})=\alpha^{-1}\beta_{i}\,\beta_{p-i}C_{i}

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\overline{\beta_{i}}\,\beta_{p-i}=\alpha
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
, and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p-1})
;

(2)
\theta^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}(L_{n})=-\alpha^{n}L_{n},\ \theta^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}(I_{n}^{(i)})=\alpha^{n}\beta_{i}I_{n}^{(i)},\ \theta^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}(C_{0})=-C_{0},\ \theta^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}(C_{i})=-\alpha^{-1}\beta_{i}\beta_{p-i}C_{i}

with
\alpha,\beta_{i}\in S^{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta
be a conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathfrak{g}
,
which fixes
\mathcal{H}
by Lemma
4.1
(2) and hence induces a conjugate-linear anti-involution, denoted by
\overline{\theta}
, on the quotient algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{H}
.
For an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\overline{x}
the image of
x
in
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{H}
in the following.
Since
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{V}
, we see from Proposition
2.7
that
\overline{\theta}
is one of the following two types.

(i)
\overline{\theta_{\alpha}^{+}}:\overline{L_{n}}\mapsto\alpha^{n}\overline{L_{-n}},\ \overline{C_{0}}\mapsto\overline{C_{0}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

(ii)
\overline{\theta_{\alpha}^{-}}:\overline{L_{n}}\mapsto-\alpha^{n}\overline{L_{n}},\ \overline{C_{0}}\mapsto-\overline{C_{0}}
for some
\alpha\in S^{1}
.

In the following we divide the proof into two cases according to the two types of
\overline{\theta}
.

Case 1
:
\overline{\theta}=\overline{\theta_{\alpha}^{+}}
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we may write (a finite sum)

\theta(L_{n})=\alpha^{n}L_{-n}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}B_{k,j}^{(n)}I_{k}^{(j)}+C(L_{n}),

where
B_{k,j}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{C},C(L_{n})\in\mathfrak{c}
.
In particular, comparing with Lemma
4.1
we see that
\lambda=1
and
\theta(L_{0})=L_{0}+C(L_{0})
.
Moreover, we have
\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})=A_{-n-1,p-i}I_{-n-1}^{(p-i)}
by (
4.1
).

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
we have

n\theta(L_{n})=[\theta(L_{0}),\theta(L_{n})]=[L_{0},\alpha^{n}L_{-n}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}B_{k,j}^{(n)}I_{k}^{(j)}]=n\alpha^{n}L_{-n}-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\left(k+\frac{j}{p}\right)B_{k,j}^{(n)}I_{k}^{(j)}.

This gives that
C(L_{n})=0
and
B_{k,j}^{(n)}=0
for all
k,j
.
So
\theta(L_{n})=\alpha^{n}L_{-n}
for
n\neq 0
.
From

\displaystyle\theta[L_{n},L_{-n}]=2n\theta(L_{0})+\frac{1}{12}(n^{3}-n)\theta(C_{0})=2nL_{0}+2nC(L_{0})+\frac{1}{12}(n^{3}-n)\theta(C_{0})

\displaystyle=[\theta(L_{-n}),\theta(L_{n})]=2nL_{0}+\frac{1}{12}(n^{3}-n)C_{0}

we see that
C(L_{0})=0
and
\theta(C_{0})=C_{0}
.
Therefore
\theta(L_{n})=\alpha^{n}L_{-n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Using (
4.1
) and expanding
\theta[L_{n},I_{0}^{(i)}]=[\theta(I_{0}^{(i)}),\theta(L_{n})]
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
,
we get
A_{-n-1,p-i}=\alpha^{n}A_{-1,p-i}
, or equivalently,

A_{n,i}=\alpha^{-n}A_{0,i}\quad\text{for any}\ n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1.

Set
\beta_{i}=\alpha A_{0,i}
for any
1\leq i\leq p-1
and set
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{p-1})
.
Then we have
\theta(I_{n}^{(i)})=\alpha^{n}\beta_{p-i}I_{-n-1}^{(p-i)}

and
\theta(C_{i})=\alpha^{-1}\beta_{i}\beta_{p-i}C_{i}
by Lemma
4.1
(2).
Moreover, from
I_{n}^{(i)}=\theta^{2}(I_{n}^{(i)})=\alpha^{-1}\overline{\beta_{p-i}}\,\beta_{i}I_{n}^{(i)}
we get
\overline{\beta_{p-i}}\,\beta_{i}=\alpha=\overline{\beta_{i}}\,\beta_{p-i}
.
This shows
\theta=\theta^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Case 2
:
\overline{\theta}=\overline{\theta_{\alpha}^{-}}
.
Similarly this case leads to
\theta=\theta^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

The subalgebras
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H},\mathfrak{g}_{J^{C}},\mathcal{H}_{J}
are stable under any conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
4.2
.
∎

5.
Unitary Harish-Chandra modules

In this section we determine the condition for any irreducible Harish-Chandra module over
\mathfrak{g}
to be unitary.
First we have the following

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
V
be a nontrivial irreducible weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module which is unitary for some conjugate-linear anti-involution of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
V
is unitary for some
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
where
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
such that
\overline{\beta_{i}}\,\beta_{p-i}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Proof.

Suppose
V
is unitary for a conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Consider
V
as a
\mathcal{V}
-module and
V
is unitary for
\theta|_{\mathcal{V}}
the restriction of
\theta
to
\mathcal{V}
.
We claim that
V
is nontrivial as a
\mathcal{V}
-module.
Otherwise, for any
v\in V,n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
,
it follows from
[L_{0},I_{n}^{(i)}]v=0
that
I_{n}^{(i)}v=0
.
Hence
C_{i}v=0
and
V
is a trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-module, a contradiction.

Since unitary weight
\mathcal{V}
-modules are completely reducible,

V
contains a nontrivial irreducible unitary
V
-submodule for
\theta|_{\mathcal{V}}
.
So we may assume
\theta|_{\mathcal{V}}=\theta_{1}^{+}
by Proposition
2.8
.
Then the statement follows from Proposition
4.2
.
∎

In the following we determine the condition for irreducible Harish-Chandra
\mathfrak{g}
-modules to be unitary for a fixed conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}
as in Proposition
5.1
.
We first do it for the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V(a,b,F)
of intermediate series.
Recall the set

\text{col}(F)=\{0\leq j\leq p-1\mid F_{ij}\neq 0\ \text{ for some }i\}.

Theorem 5.2
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V(a,b,F)
of intermediate series is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}

if and only if
a\in\mathbb{R},b\in\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{p-i}F_{p-i,\overline{i+j}}=\overline{F_{i,j}}
for any
j\in\text{col}(F),1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Proof.

If
a\in\mathbb{R},b\in\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{p-i}F_{p-i,\overline{i+j}}=\overline{F_{i,j}}
for any
j\in\text{col}(F),1\leq i\leq p-1
,
then it is easy to check that
V(a,b,F)
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
and
the positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined by

\langle v_{m+\frac{i}{p}},v_{n+\frac{j}{p}}\rangle=\delta_{m,n}\delta_{i,j}\quad\text{for }m,n\in\mathbb{Z},i,j\in\text{col}(F).

Conversely, suppose that
V(a,b,F)
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
and some positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Then all
V_{(j)}
’s are unitary
\mathcal{V}
-modules for
\left(\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}\right)|_{\mathcal{V}}=\theta_{1}^{+}
.
Therefore,
a\in\mathbb{R},b\in\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}
by Proposition
2.9
(1).
Moreover, for any
j\in\text{col}(F),1\leq i\leq p-1
,
normalize the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that
\langle v_{\frac{i+j}{p}},v_{\frac{i+j}{p}}\rangle=\langle v_{\frac{j}{p}},v_{\frac{j}{p}}\rangle=1

by replacing the basis elements
v_{\frac{j}{p}}
with a suitable multiple if necessary.
Then we have

\displaystyle\beta_{p-i}F_{p-i,\overline{i+j}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p-i}F_{p-i,\overline{i+j}}\langle v_{\frac{j}{p}},v_{\frac{j}{p}}\rangle=\langle\beta_{p-i}I_{-1}^{(p-i)}v_{\frac{i+j}{p}},v_{\frac{j}{p}}\rangle=\langle\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(I_{0}^{(i)})v_{\frac{i+j}{p}},v_{\frac{j}{p}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{\frac{i+j}{p}},I_{0}^{(i)}v_{\frac{j}{p}}\rangle=\overline{F_{i,j}}\,\langle v_{\frac{i+j}{p}},v_{\frac{i+j}{p}}\rangle=\overline{F_{i,j}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next we are to determine the condition for irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-modules to be unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
and some positive definite Hermitian form.

Recall the irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module with highest weight
\varphi
,
which by Theorem
3.2
is isomorphic to
V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}
if
J=\emptyset
,
and isomorphic to
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
if
J\neq\emptyset
.
The following lemma is easy to check.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
V,W
be two unitary module over a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
for some conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta
and positive definite Hermitian forms
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W}
separately.
Then the tensor product
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V\otimes W
is unitary for
\theta
and the positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined by

\langle v_{1}\otimes w_{1},v_{2}\otimes w_{2}\rangle=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{V}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{W}\quad\text{ for }v_{1},v_{2}\in V,w_{1},w_{2}\in W.

Notice that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
is a highest weight module with highest weight
\phi^{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\phi^{\mathfrak{g}}(L_{0})=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}},\quad\phi^{\mathfrak{g}}(C_{0})=|J|,\quad\phi^{\mathfrak{g}}(C_{i})=\begin{cases}\phi(C_{i})=\varphi(C_{i})&\text{if }i\in J;\\
0&\text{if }i\notin J.\end{cases}

Proposition 5.4
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
if and only if
\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
for all
i\in J
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
.
Let
v
be a nonzero highest weight vector in
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then for any
i\in J
, we have (noting that
\beta_{i}\,\overline{\beta_{p-i}}=1
)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{p}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\phi(C_{i})\langle v,v\rangle=\beta_{i}\langle[I_{0}^{(i)},I_{-1}^{(p-i)}]v,v\rangle=\beta_{i}\langle I_{0}^{(i)}I_{-1}^{(p-i)}v,v\rangle=\beta_{i}\langle v,\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(I_{-1}^{(p-i)})\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(I_{0}^{(i)})v\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}\,\overline{\beta_{i}\,\beta_{p-i}}\langle v,I_{0}^{(i)}I_{-1}^{(p-i)}v\rangle=\overline{\beta_{i}}\langle v,[I_{0}^{(i)},I_{-1}^{(p-i)}]v\rangle=\frac{i}{p}\overline{\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})}\langle v,v\rangle,

which implies that
\overline{\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})}={\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})}
.
So
\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

Conversely, suppose
\beta_{i}\phi(C_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
for all
i\in J
.
Notice that the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
has a decomposition

\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J}\cap\mathfrak{c})\oplus\left(\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}I_{-n}^{(i)}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})+\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})I_{n-1}^{(i)}\right).

Denote by
\rho:\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J}\cap\mathfrak{c})
the projection onto the first summand in the above decomposition.
Then the space
M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}
admits a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined by

\langle xv,yv\rangle v=\rho\left(\widetilde{\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}}(x)y\right)v,

where
x,y\in\mathcal{U}\left(\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{C}I_{-n}^{(i)}\right)

and
\widetilde{\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}}
is the anti-involution of
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
extended from

\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
by

\widetilde{\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})=\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(x_{k})\cdots\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(x_{1})\quad\text{ for any }x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{J}.

Then it is clear that the
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-module
M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}|_{\mathcal{H}_{J}}
.

Now for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times},u,w\in\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
,
we have (noting that
\beta_{i}\,\overline{\beta_{p-i}}=1
)

\displaystyle\langle L_{n}u,w\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{2\phi(C_{j})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle I_{k}^{(j)}I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}u,w\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{2\phi(C_{j})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle u,\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}\left(I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}\right)\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}\left(I_{k}^{(j)}\right)w\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\overline{\beta_{j}\beta_{p-j}}}{2\phi(C_{j})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle u,I_{-n+k}^{(j)}I_{-k-1}^{(p-j)}w\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{2\overline{\phi(C_{j})}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle u,I_{k}^{(j)}I_{-n-k-1}^{(p-j)}w\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,L_{-n}w\rangle=\langle u,\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(L_{n})w\rangle.

Since
L_{0}
acts semisimply on
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
, it is clear that
\langle L_{0}u,w\rangle=\langle u,L_{0}w\rangle
.
Moreover, for any
i\in J^{C},n\in\mathbb{Z},u,w\in\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
,
we have
\langle I_{n}^{(i)}u,w\rangle=0=\langle u,\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(I_{n}^{(i)})w\rangle.

So we have

\langle xu,w\rangle=\langle u,\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}(x)w\rangle\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathfrak{g},u,w\in\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}.

This shows that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\left(M_{\mathcal{H}_{J},\phi}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
if and only if
\psi(C_{0})\geq 1,\psi(L_{0})\geq 0
, or there exists an integer
m\geq 2
and
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

0\leq r<s<m,\quad\psi(C_{0})=1-\frac{6}{m(m+1)},\quad\psi(L_{0})=\frac{(mr+s)^{2}-1}{4m(m+1)}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}=0
, it is clear that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\left(V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{e}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
if and only if the
\mathcal{V}
-module
V_{\mathcal{V},\psi\mid_{\mathcal{V}}}
is unitary for
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}|_{\mathcal{V}}=\theta_{1}^{+}
.
Then it follows from Proposition
2.9
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

The irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\varphi
is unitary for the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
if and only if the following two statements stand.

(1)
\beta_{i}\varphi(C_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
for all
i\in J
;

(2)
\varphi(C_{0})\geq|J|+1,\varphi(L_{0})\geq\sum_{j\in J}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}}
, or there exists an integer
m\geq 2
and
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

0\leq r<s<m,\quad\varphi(C_{0})=|J|+1-\frac{6}{m(m+1)},\quad\varphi(L_{0})=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}}+\frac{(mr+s)^{2}-1}{4m(m+1)}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
5.3
, Proposition
5.4
and Proposition
5.5
.
∎

Denote by
\omega
the Chevalley involution of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\omega(L_{n})=-L_{-n},\ \omega(I_{n}^{(i)})=-I_{-n}^{(p-i)},\ \omega(C_{j})=-C_{j}\ \text{for any }n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq p-1.

Let
V
be an irreducible lowest weight module of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the following action

x\cdot w=\omega(x)w\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathfrak{g},w\in V,

makes
V
a highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
We denote the resulting module by
V^{*}
.
It is easy to check that
V
is unitary under some positive definite Hermitian form
<\cdot,\cdot>
and
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
if and only if

V^{*}
is unitary under
<\cdot,\cdot>
and
\theta_{1,\beta^{\prime}}^{+}
,
where
\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\beta_{p-1}^{\prime})
with
\beta_{i}^{\prime}=\beta_{p-i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Now we are in position to state the final theorem in this section,
which follows from Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
5.6
and the above argument.

Theorem 5.7
.

Any irreducible unitary Harish-Chandra module over
\mathfrak{g}
for the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\theta_{1,\beta}^{+}
is one of the following modules.

(1)
V(a,b,F)
with
a\in\mathbb{R}^{\times},b\in\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{i}F_{p-i,\overline{i+j}}=\overline{F_{i,j}}
for any
j\in\text{col}(F),1\leq i\leq p-1
;

(2) a highest weight module of highest weight
\varphi
satisfying the conditions in Theorem
5.6
;

(3) a lowest weight module of lowest weight
-\varphi
, where
\varphi
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
5.6
.
