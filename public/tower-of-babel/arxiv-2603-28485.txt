On Generalizations of Maiorana–McFarland and 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} Functions
Sezel Alkan, Nurdagül Anbar, Athina Avrantini, Erroxe Etxabarri-Alberdi, Tekgül Kalaycı, Beatrice Toesca

Abstract
We study generalizations of two classical primary constructions of Boolean bent functions, namely the Maiorana–McFarland ( M ​ M MM ) class and the (Desarguesian) partial spread ( 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} ) class. The construction of bent functions lying outside the completed M ​ M MM class has attracted considerable attention in recent years. In this direction, we construct families of generalized Maiorana–McFarland bent functions that are not equivalent to any function in the classical M ​ M MM or 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} classes, and hence lie outside their completed classes. As a second contribution, we investigate the decomposition of generalized 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} functions. We prove that when the degree is sufficiently small relative to the size of the underlying finite field, such functions do not, in general, admit a decomposition into bent or semibent functions. Consequently, they cannot be obtained from known secondary constructions based on concatenation. Finally, we present a secondary construction of Boolean bent functions arising from the concatenation of components of vectorial generalized 𝒫 ​ 𝒮 a ​ p \mathcal{PS}_{ap} functions. Our constructions and proofs rely on classical results concerning second-order derivatives of bent functions and their duals. In addition, we employ methods from the theory of algebraic curves and their function fields.



Keywords:
Boolean functions, Bent functions, Concatenation, Decomposition, Maiorana–McFarland functions, Partial spread functions, Algebraic curves, Function fields.

11T06, 94A60, 14H05.

Introduction

Let
p
be a prime and let
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
be an
n
-dimensional vector space
over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We fix a non-degenerate inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{n}
on
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
.

If
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is the vector space of
n
-tuples over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we take the usual dot product
\langle b,x\rangle_{n}=b\cdot x
.
If
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is the finite field of order
p^{n}
, we define
\langle b,x\rangle_{n}={\rm Tr}_{1}^{n}(bx)
,
where, for any divisor
k
of
n
,
{\rm Tr}_{k}^{n}
denotes the trace mapping from

\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
.
When
n=2m
, it is often convenient to identify

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
, in which case the
inner product is given by
\langle(u,v),(x,y)\rangle_{n}={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)
.

A function
f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is called a
p
-ary
function
; in the special case
p=2
, it is called a
Boolean function
.
The Walsh transform of
f
is the complex-valued function defined by

W_{f}(b)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}}\zeta_{p}^{\,f(x)-\langle b,x\rangle_{n}},\qquad b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)},

where
\zeta_{p}=e^{2\pi i/p}
and
i
is a primitive fourth root of unity.
The
extended Walsh spectrum
of
f
is the multiset

\{\,|W_{f}(b)|:b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\,\}
.

The function
f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is called
bent
if
|W_{f}(b)|=p^{n/2}
for all
b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
.
Equivalently,
f
is bent if and only if, for every nonzero vector

a\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
, its first-order derivative
D_{a}f(x)=f(x+a)-f(x)

is balanced, i.e., for each
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the equation

D_{a}f(x)=c
has exactly
p^{n-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
.

In the Boolean case
p=2
, the Walsh transform of a bent function
f

satisfies
W_{f}(b)=2^{n/2}(-1)^{f^{\ast}(b)}
,
where
f^{\ast}:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a Boolean function, called the

dual
of
f
, which is itself bent.
Since
W_{f}
is integer-valued in this case, Boolean bent functions exist only
when
n
is even.

In contrast, for odd
p
, bent functions

f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
exist for both even and odd values of
n
.
Their Walsh transform satisfies

W_{f}(b)=\begin{cases}\pm\zeta_{p}^{\,f^{\ast}(b)}\,p^{n/2},&\text{if $n$ is even, or if $n$ is odd and $p\equiv 1\mod{4}$},\\[4.30554pt]
\pm i\,\zeta_{p}^{\,f^{\ast}(b)}\,p^{n/2},&\text{if $n$ is odd and $p\equiv 3\mod{4}$},\end{cases}

where

f^{\ast}:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is again called the
dual
of

f
; see
[
19
]
.

A function
f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is called

s
-plateaued
if there exists an integer
0\leq s\leq n
such that
\lvert W_{f}(b)\rvert\in\{\,0,\,p^{(n+s)/2}\,\}
for all
b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
.
In particular,
f
is bent if and only if
s=0
.
Moreover, in the Boolean case
p=2
, since the Walsh transform
W_{f}
takes
integer values, the integers
n
and
s
must have the same parity.
A Boolean function is called
semibent
if
s=1
when
n
is odd, or
if
s=2
when
n
is even.

Let
F:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
be a function.
For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
, the associated
component
function
of
F
is defined by
F_{\alpha}(x)=\langle\alpha,F(x)\rangle_{k}
.
The function
F
is called
(vectorial) bent
if all its
component functions
F_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
, are
bent functions.
In this case, the set of component functions of
F
, together with the zero
function, forms a
k
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

of bent functions.

In terms of the Walsh transform, a function

F:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
is bent if, for every

\alpha\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
and every
b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
,

W_{F_{\alpha}}(b)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}}\zeta_{p}^{\,\langle a,F(x)\rangle_{k}-\langle b,x\rangle_{n}},\qquad\zeta_{p}=e^{2\pi i/p},

has absolute value
p^{n/2}
. Equivalently, a function
F:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
is bent if,
for every nonzero
a\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
, the derivative
D_{a}F(x)=F(x+a)-F(x)

is a balanced function from
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
to
\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
.

Extended-affine equivalence is a fundamental notion in the theory of
(vectorial) functions, as it characterizes transformations that
preserve important cryptographic properties, including the extended Walsh
spectrum and, in particular, bentness.

Two functions

F,G:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
are said to be

extended-affine equivalent (EA-equivalent)
if

G(x)=L_{1}\!\bigl(F(L_{2}(x)+a)\bigr)+L_{3}(x)+b,

where
L_{1}:\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
and

L_{2}:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
are linear permutations,

L_{3}:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
is a linear map, and

a\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
,
b\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
.
In the special case
k=1
, EA-equivalence reduces to the following relation
between functions
f,g:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
:

g(x)=f(L(x)+a)+\langle c,x\rangle_{n}+b,

where
L
is a linear permutation on
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
,

c\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
, and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

A class
\mathcal{C}
of
p
-ary, respectively Boolean, bent functions is
called
completed
if it is invariant under EA-. In other words,
\mathcal{C}
contains a function
f
if and only if
it contains every function EA-equivalent to
f
.
The
completion
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
, is
the smallest completed class containing
\mathcal{C}
.

Bent functions can be obtained via two main types of constructions:

primary
and
secondary
.
Primary constructions are direct algebraic methods that produce bent functions
from scratch, without relying on previously known examples.
In contrast, secondary constructions generate new bent functions from one or
more
known
bent (or related) functions by applying transformations or
combinations that preserve bentness. While many secondary constructions of bent functions are known (see

[
11
, Section 6.1.16]
), there are only two classical primary
constructions, namely the Maiorana–McFarland construction
[
22
]
and the
partial spread (Dillon) construction
[
14
]
.

It is well known that all bent functions in dimensions up to six belong to the
completed Maiorana–McFarland class. The first examples of bent
functions outside the
MM^{\#}
class were constructed by Dillon
[
14
]
,
who gave explicit examples in eight variables. It was later shown in
[
20
]
,
using computer search, that the
MM^{\#}
class constitutes only a very small
fraction of all bent functions. More precisely, the number of bent functions in
the
MM^{\#}
class is at most
2^{72}
, whereas there are approximately

2^{106}
bent functions in dimension eight.
This observation initiated an active line of research focused on the
construction of bent functions lying outside the
MM^{\#}
class; see, for instance, the recent papers

[
21
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
, as well as the survey paper

[
24
]
and the references therein.

While exclusion from the completed Desarguesian partial spread

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}^{\#}
class—a distinguished subclass of the partial spread
construction—can be detected using invariants such as the algebraic degree or
the
2
-rank (see
[
6
,
31
]
), membership in the
MM^{\#}
class is
characterized by a criterion due to Dillon
[
14
]
, formulated in terms
of second-order derivatives.

In this paper, although we present certain results in the more general setting
of an arbitrary prime
p
, our primary focus is on the construction and
classification of (vectorial) Boolean functions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly review two classical constructions:
Maiorana–McFarland and Desarguesian partial spread bent functions,
and their generalizations for arbitrary characteristic
p
.
In Section
3
, we present a construction of Boolean generalized Maiorana–McFarland functions that do not belong to the
MM^{\#}
class. In Section
4
, we study the decomposition of Boolean generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions and show that, in general, they do not arise from secondary constructions of bent or semibent functions via concatenation when their degree is sufficiently small relative to the size of the finite field on which they are defined. Finally, in Section
5
, we provide a secondary construction of Boolean bent functions motivated by the concatenation of the components of vectorial
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions.

2
Generalized Maiorana–McFarland and generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions

In this section, we briefly recall two fundamental primary constructions of

p
-ary (vectorial) bent functions: the
Maiorana–McFarland class, introduced independently by Maiorana (unpublished)
and McFarland
[
22
]
, and the Desarguesian partial spread class

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
, introduced by Dillon in his Ph.D. thesis
[
14
]
,
together with their generalizations.

Throughout, we use the bivariate representation
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
,
n=2m
,
and present the corresponding definitions in this setting.

Maiorana–McFarland (
MM
) class.

A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

is said to belong to the Maiorana–McFarland class if it is of the form

f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\bigl(x\,\pi(y)\bigr)+g(y),

where
\pi
is a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
and

g:\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is an arbitrary function.

In the vectorial case, a function
F:\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}

is called Maiorana–McFarland if it can be written as

F(x,y)=x\,\pi(y)+G(y),

where
G:\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
is an arbitrary function.

It is well known that
f
, and respectively
F
, is bent if and only if

\pi
is a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
.

The following characterization of Boolean bent functions, stated in a general form for the completed
MM
class, was given by Dillon.

Lemma 1
.

[
14
, Dillon’s criterion]

Let
n=2m
. A Boolean bent function
f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

belongs to the
MM^{\#}
class if and only if there exists an
m
-dimensional vector subspace

\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
such that, for all
a,b\in\mathcal{U}
, the second-order derivatives

D_{a}D_{b}f(x)=f(x)+f(x+a)+f(x+b)+f(x+a+b)

(1)

vanish identically.

A subspace
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
for which

D_{a}D_{b}f(x)=0
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{U}
and all

x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
is called an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace
of
f

(see
[
28
]
). We note that both the number of
\mathcal{M}
-subspaces of

f
and its maximal dimension, called the
linearity index

\operatorname{ind}(f)
of
f
, are invariant under EA-equivalence.
For a bent function
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the linearity
index satisfies
1\leq\operatorname{ind}(f)\leq n/2
;
see
[
24
, Proposition 5.1]
. Consequently, a bent function
f
on

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
belongs to the
MM^{\#}
class if and only if

\operatorname{ind}(f)=n/2
.

Generalized Maiorana–McFarland (
GMM
) class.

Let
m
and
k
be integers with
1\leq k\leq m
.
For each
z\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}
, let
f_{z}:\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

be a
k
-plateaued
p
-ary function, and define its Walsh support by

\operatorname{supp}(W_{f_{z}})=\{\,b\in\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(p)}:W_{f_{z}}(b)\neq 0\,\}.

Assume that the Walsh supports are pairwise disjoint, that is,

\operatorname{supp}(W_{f_{z}})\cap\operatorname{supp}(W_{f_{y}})=\emptyset\quad\text{for all }z,y\in\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)},\;z\neq y.

Then the function

f:\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(p)}\times\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p},\qquad f(x,z)=f_{z}(x),

(2)

is a
p
-ary bent function (see
[
13
]
for details).

Observe that, for fixed
y
, the Maiorana–McFarland function

f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\bigl(x\,\pi(y)\bigr)+g(y)

is affine in
x
, and hence corresponds to an
m
-plateaued
p
-ary function that is affine on each coset of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\{0\}
.
Note also that when
k=m
, the construction (
2
) reduces to the
Maiorana–McFarland class.
For this reason, functions defined by (
2
) are called

generalized Maiorana–McFarland functions
if they are affine on the cosets of a
k
-dimensional subspace.

A commonly used finite field representation of the
GMM
construction is the
following.
Consider the function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

defined by

\displaystyle f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yz),

(3)

where, for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, the function

f^{(z)}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is bent.
For fixed
z
, the function
f(\cdot,\cdot,z)
is a
k
-plateaued

p
-ary function on

\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, and
f
is affine on the cosets of
\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
.
Note that the corresponding Walsh
supports of
f^{(z)}
are pairwise disjoint, in accordance with the
GMM
construction.

In
[
1
, Theorem 3]
, the Walsh transform of
f
in (
3
) and the explicit form of its dual
f^{\ast}
are derived. In particular, the dual function
f^{\ast}
is again a
GMM
function, given by

f^{\ast}(x,y,z)=\bigl(f^{(y)}\bigr)^{\ast}(x)-{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yz),

and satisfies
f^{\ast\ast}=(f^{\ast})^{\ast}=f
.

Remark 1
.

A related notion of the generalized Maiorana–McFarland class was introduced
in
[
9
, Section 4.3]
as follows. Let

0\leq k\leq\tfrac{n}{2}-1
, and set
r=\tfrac{n}{2}-k
and

s=\tfrac{n}{2}+k
. A Boolean function
f_{\varphi,h}\colon\mathbb{V}_{r}^{(2)}\times\mathbb{V}_{s}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

of the form

f_{\varphi,h}(x,y)=\langle x,\pi(y)\rangle_{r}+h(y),\qquad x\in\mathbb{V}_{r}^{(2)},\;y\in\mathbb{V}_{s}^{(2)},

(4)

is said to be a generalized Maiorana–McFarland function, where

\pi\colon\mathbb{V}_{s}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{r}^{(2)}
and

h\colon\mathbb{V}_{s}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
are arbitrary functions;
see also
[
12
]
. Observe that, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
,
the function defined in (
3
) is affine in the variable
y
.
Consequently,
f
is equivalent to a function on
\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(2)}\times\mathbb{V}_{n+k}^{(2)}

of the form
f(x,y)=\langle x,\pi(y)\rangle_{k}+h(y)
,
where
\pi\colon\mathbb{V}_{n+k}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}^{(2)}
and

h\colon\mathbb{V}_{n+k}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Observe also that, when
k=0
, the above construction reduces to the classical Maiorana–McFarland class of bent functions, provided that
\pi
is a permutation, whereas for

k=\tfrac{n}{2}-1
the completed class defined by (
4
) contains all Boolean
functions on
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
.
There is an extensive research devoted to the classification of functions
of the form (
4
), in particular to the characterization of those
functions that are bent; see the survey paper
[
24
]
and the
references therein.

Desarguesian partial spread (
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
) class.

Let
n=2m
.
A
(complete) spread
of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
is a collection of

m
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspaces

U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{p^{m}}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}

such that
U_{i}\cap U_{j}=\{0\}
for all
0\leq i<j\leq p^{m}
.
Equivalently, every nonzero element of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
belongs to exactly one of
the sets
U_{j}^{\ast}=U_{j}\setminus\{0\}
.

It is well known that every function

f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

constructed as follows is a bent function. For every
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
the function
f
maps the elements of exactly
p^{m-1}
of the sets

U_{j}^{\ast}
,
1\leq j\leq p^{m}
, to the value
c
,
and
f
is constant on the subspace
U_{0}
.

Similarly, one obtains vectorial partial spread bent functions

F:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(p)}
.
For every
c\in\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(p)}
, the function
F
maps the elements of
exactly one of the sets
U_{j}^{\ast}
,
1\leq j\leq p^{m}
, to the value

c
, and
F
is constant on the subspace
U_{0}
.

A well-known example of a spread is the
Desarguesian spread
.
In the bivariate representation

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
,
it is given by

U=\{\,(0,y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\},\qquad U_{s}=\{\,(x,sx):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\,\},\quad s\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}.

Bent functions arising from the Desarguesian spread admit the explicit
representation

f(x,y)=P\!\left(y\,x^{p^{m}-2}\right),

(5)

where

P\colon\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

is a balanced function.

Analogously, vectorial bent functions

F\colon\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}

arising from the Desarguesian spread are represented as

F(x,y)=P\bigl(y\,x^{p^{m}-2}\bigr),

where

P\colon\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}

is a permutation.

Functions obtained from the Desarguesian partial spread construction are called

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions.
It has been shown that every (vectorial) bent function arising from a spread of

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
has algebraic degree
(p-1)n/2
;
see
[
14
]
for the case
p=2
and
[
8
]
for odd primes
p
.

Generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
class.

The construction of bent functions from spreads can be generalized via the
notion of
(normal) bent partitions

[
8
]
, defined as follows:

A partition of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
into an
(n/2)
-dimensional subspace
U
and
sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{K}
is called a

normal bent partition of depth
K
if every function

f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfying the following conditions is bent:

(i)

For each
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, exactly
K/p
of the sets

A_{j}
are contained in the preimage
f^{-1}(c)=\{x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}:f(x)=c\}
.

(ii)

The function
f
is constant on the subspace
U
.

In particular, if
U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{p^{m}}
is a spread of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(p)}
,
then the sets
U_{0},U_{1}^{\ast},\dots,U_{p^{m}}^{\ast}
, where

U_{i}^{\ast}=U_{i}\setminus\{0\}
, form a normal bent partition of depth

p^{m}
.

The first class of bent partitions that are not equivalent to those arising from spreads
was introduced in
[
8
]
for arbitrary prime
p
, and in
[
23
]
for
p=2
. Since then, several other primary
and secondary constructions of bent partitions have been developed; see,
for instance,
[
1
,
4
,
7
,
30
]
, as well as the survey paper

[
3
]
and the references therein.

Among these, the construction presented in
[
8
]
(and
[
23
]
) admits an explicit
representation and can be described as follows.

Let
m
,
k
, and
e
be integers such that
k\mid m
,

e\equiv p^{\ell}\mod{(p^{k}-1)}
, and

\gcd(p^{m}-1,e)=1
.
Let
\eta
denote the multiplicative inverse of
e
modulo
(p^{m}-1)
, that
is,
\eta e\equiv 1\mod{(p^{m}-1)}
.
For
s\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
, define the sets

U=\{\,(0,y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\,\},\qquad U_{s}=\{\,(x,sx^{e}):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\,\},\qquad U_{s}^{\ast}=U_{s}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

(6)

Similarly, define

V=\{\,(x,0):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\,\},\qquad V_{s}=\{\,(sx^{\eta},x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\,\},\qquad V_{s}^{\ast}=V_{s}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

(7)

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, define

\displaystyle A(\gamma)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\\
{\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}(s)=\gamma\end{subarray}}U_{s}^{\ast},\qquad B(\gamma)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\\
{\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}(s)=\gamma\end{subarray}}V_{s}^{\ast}.

(8)

Then the collections

\Omega_{1}=\{\,U,\,A(\gamma):\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\,\},\qquad\Omega_{2}=\{\,V,\,B(\gamma):\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\,\},

(9)

are bent partitions of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
of depth

p^{k}
. Moreover, similarly to the case of
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent
functions, the bent functions

f,g:\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

obtained from
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, respectively, admit explicit
representations given by

f(x,y)=P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(yx^{-e}\right)\right)+c_{0}\bigl(1-x^{p^{m}-1}\bigr),

and

g(x,y)=P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(xy^{-\eta}\right)\right)+c_{0}\bigl(1-y^{p^{m}-1}\bigr),

where
P:\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a balanced function and

c_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a constant. Similarly, the vectorial version of the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}

functions from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}

is defined using a permutation

P\colon\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
and
c_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
.

In the case
k=m
, the partitions
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}

are equivalent to the Desarguesian spread.
Accordingly, they are called
generalized Desarguesian spreads
, and the
bent functions obtained from them are referred to as

generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions
. For details, we refer to
[
3
,
8
]
.

In general, a generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{{ap}}
bent function does not
belong to the completed Maiorana–McFarland class. Moreover, there exist
generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{{ap}}
bent functions that lie neither in
the partial spread class nor in the completed Maiorana–McFarland class.
For further details, see
[
6
, Examples 5.1 and 5.2]
.

We remark that generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions were originally
presented without the additional terms

c_{0}\bigl(1-x^{p^{m}-1}\bigr)
and

c_{0}\bigl(1-y^{p^{m}-1}\bigr)
, respectively, as in the classical

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
construction. While any
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent
function is EA-equivalent to one of the form given in
Equation (
5
), these additional terms are nevertheless necessary in
order to obtain a complete set of inequivalent generalized

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions; see
[
3
, Example 1]
.

3
Construction of
GMM
functions outside the
MM^{\#}
class

We begin by examining the generalized Maiorana–McFarland construction given in
[
13
]

over finite fields.
Specifically, we consider functions

f:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
defined by

f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yz),

(10)

where, for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, the function

f^{(z)}:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
is bent.

To characterize functions lying outside the completed Maiorana–McFarland
class, we make use of Dillon’s criterion; see Lemma
1
.
In this context, it is essential to understand the behavior of second-order
derivatives under EA-equivalence.
It is shown in
[
2
]
that, for a Boolean function

f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the property of having constant
second-order derivatives is invariant under EA-equivalence. For the convenience
of the reader, we include the proof here.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a Boolean function, and let

a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be linearly independent. Let

L\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be a linear permutation, and let

c,d\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
and
e\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Then
D_{a}D_{b}f(x)
is identically equal to
0
(respectively, identically equal to
1
)
for all
x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
if and only if

D_{L^{-1}(a)}D_{L^{-1}(b)}\left(\ f\bigl(L(x)+c\bigr)+\langle d,x\rangle_{n}+e\ \right)

(11)

is also identically
0
(respectively, identically
1
) for all

x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
.

Proof.

Since any affine term
\langle d,x\rangle_{n}+e
has vanishing second-order
derivatives, we may assume without loss of generality that

g(x)=f(L(x)+c)
. By the linearity of
L
, it follows that

\displaystyle D_{L^{-1}(a)}D_{L^{-1}(b)}g(x)

\displaystyle\quad=g\bigl(x+L^{-1}(a)+L^{-1}(b)\bigr)+g\bigl(x+L^{-1}(a)\bigr)+g\bigl(x+L^{-1}(b)\bigr)+g(x)

\displaystyle\quad=f\bigl(L(x+L^{-1}(a)+L^{-1}(b))+c\bigr)+f\bigl(L(x+L^{-1}(a))+c\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+f\bigl(L(x+L^{-1}(b))+c\bigr)+f\bigl(L(x)+c\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad=f\bigl(L(x)+a+b+c\bigr)+f\bigl(L(x)+a+c\bigr)+f\bigl(L(x)+b+c\bigr)+f\bigl(L(x)+c\bigr).

Setting
y=L(x)+c
, we obtain

D_{L^{-1}(a)}D_{L^{-1}(b)}g(x)=f(y+a+b)+f(y+a)+f(y+b)+f(y)=D_{a}D_{b}f(y).

Since the map
x\mapsto L(x)+c
is a permutation of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
, the
assertion follows.
∎

Remark 2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a Boolean function, and let

L\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be a linear permutation. Then, by Lemma
2
,

\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
if and only if

L^{-1}(\mathcal{W})
is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f\circ L
.

We now analyze the second-order derivative of the function

f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
defined in Equation (
10
).
Recall that

f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yz).

Let

\nu_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})
and

\nu_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})

be two distinct nonzero elements of

\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
. Then

\displaystyle D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=f(x,y,z)+f(x+u_{1},y+v_{1},z+w_{1})+f(x+u_{2},y+v_{2},z+w_{2})

(12)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+f(x+u_{1}+u_{2},y+v_{1}+v_{2},z+w_{1}+w_{2})

\displaystyle=f^{(z)}(x)+f^{(z+w_{1})}(x+u_{1})+f^{(z+w_{2})}(x+u_{2})+f^{(z+w_{1}+w_{2})}(x+u_{1}+u_{2})+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl(w_{1}v_{2}+w_{2}v_{1}\bigr).

Remark 3
.

In the case
w_{1}=w_{2}=0
, Equation (
12
) yields

D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+f^{(z)}(x+u_{1})+f^{(z)}(x+u_{2})+f^{(z)}(x+u_{1}+u_{2}).

(13)

In particular,
D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=D_{u_{1}}D_{u_{2}}f^{(z)}(x)
,
and hence
D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)
is independent of
y
.
Consequently, if the functions
f^{(z)}
are
MM
functions
sharing a common
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of dimension
n/2
, then, by
Dillon’s criterion, the function
f(x,y,z)
is itself
MM
.

Indeed, it suffices to note that
f(x,y,z)
admits an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace
of dimension
k+n/2
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the common
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of the functions

f^{(z)}
, with
\dim\mathcal{V}=n/2
.
Then, by Equation (
13
), the set
\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\times\{0\}

forms an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
of dimension
k+n/2
, which
establishes the claim.

Remark
3
indicates that, in order to construct a
GMM
function from
MM
functions that does
not belong to the completed Maiorana–McFarland class, it is necessary to
combine
MM
functions that do not share a common
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
dimension
n/2
.

Motivated by this observation, we consider the construction of
GMM
functions
from
MM
functions that share only
trivial

\mathcal{M}
-subspaces, i.e.,
subspaces of dimension at most one.
Our approach begins with an
MM
function on
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
admitting a unique

\mathcal{M}
-subspace
\mathcal{W}
of dimension
n/2
, with the
additional property that every nontrivial
\mathcal{M}
-subspace is contained in

\mathcal{W}
.
By applying a suitably chosen linear permutation to this function and
embedding it into a higher-dimensional
GMM
framework, we obtain a
GMM
function
that admits only
\mathcal{M}
-subspaces of dimension strictly smaller than
those allowed for
MM
functions.

The following proposition formalizes this construction.

Proposition 3
.

Let
h
be an
MM
function on
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}

having a unique

\mathcal{M}
-subspace
\mathcal{W}
of dimension
n/2
, such that if
\mathcal{U}
is a nontrivial

\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
h
, then
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
be a complementary subspace of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
satisfying

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}=\mathcal{W}\oplus\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{W}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\{0\}
and
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}=\mathcal{W}+\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
), and let
L
be a linear permutation of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
with

{L}(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}})=\mathcal{W}
.

For each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
, define

\displaystyle f^{(z)}(x)=\begin{cases}h(x),&\text{if }z\in V,\\[6.0pt]
(h\circ L)(x),&\text{if }z\not\in V,\end{cases}

(14)

where
V=\bigl\{\,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}:{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(z)=0\,\bigr\}
.

With this choice of
f^{(z)}
in (
14
), if
n>2k+4
,
then the function
f:\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, defined by
f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl(yz\bigr)
, is not (equivalent to) an
MM
function.
Equivalently, if
\mathcal{U}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
, then

\dim(\mathcal{U})<n/2+k
.

Proof.

We recall that, by Remark
2
, the subspace

L^{-1}(\mathcal{W})=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of

h\circ L
of dimension
n/2
.
Moreover, by assumption,
\widetilde{W}
is the unique

\mathcal{M}
-subspace with the property that every nontrivial

\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
h\circ L
is contained in
\widetilde{W}
.
Equivalently, for distinct nonzero

{a},{b}\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
, the equality
D_{{a}}D_{{b}}\,h(L(x))=0
holds for all
x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}

if and only if
{a},{b}\in\widetilde{W}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
.
We derive an upper bound on the dimension
\dim(\mathcal{U})
of
\mathcal{U}
in two steps.

Step 1.

Define

\mathcal{U}_{V}=\bigl\{\,(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{U}:{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w)=0\,\bigr\}.

(15)

Let

\nu_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})
and
\nu_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})

be distinct nonzero elements of
\mathcal{U}_{V}
.
Since
V
is a subspace,
w_{1}+w_{2}\in V
.

Assume first that

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{1}v_{2}+w_{2}v_{1})=0
.
Then for any
z\in V
,
Equation (
12
) yields

\displaystyle D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=h(x)+h(x+u_{1})+h(x+u_{2})+h(x+u_{1}+u_{2})

\displaystyle=D_{u_{1}}D_{u_{2}}h(x).

Hence,

D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}

if and only if either

u_{1},u_{2}
are distinct nonzero elements of the

\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
h
, or
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\{(0,u),(u,0),(u,u):u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\}
.

Similarly, for
z\notin V
,
D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=D_{u_{1}}D_{u_{2}}\,h\circ L(x)
,
and vanishing occurs if and only if either

u_{1},u_{2}
belong to the
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of

h\circ L
, or
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\{(0,u),(u,0),(u,u):u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\}
.
Since
\mathcal{W}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\{0\}
,
we must have
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\{(0,u),(u,0),(u,u):u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\}
.

We define
\mathfrak{S}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{V}
to be a subset such that,
for any
(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i}),(u_{j},v_{j},w_{j})\in\mathfrak{S}
, one has
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\left(w_{i}v_{j}+w_{j}v_{i}\right)=0
. In other words,

\mathfrak{S}=\left\{\nu=(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{U}_{V}\;:\;{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\left(wv_{i}+w_{i}v\right)=0\ \text{for all }(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i})\in\mathfrak{S}\right\}.

Choose
\mathfrak{S}
of maximal dimension.
If
\mathfrak{S}
contains two elements with nonzero first components,
then these components must be the same.
Hence, for a fixed nonzero
u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
,

\mathfrak{S}
is contained in

\{0,u\}\times\left\{(v_{j},w_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}^{2}\;:\;{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{j})=0\ \text{and}\ {\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{j}v_{i}+w_{i}v_{j})=0\ \text{for all }i\right\},

which is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
.

Case (i):

If
\mathfrak{S}=\{(0,0,0)\}
, then
we claim that
\dim(\mathcal{U}_{V})\leq 2
.
Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
\mathcal{U}_{V}
contains
three linearly independent vectors

\nu_{i}=(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
.
Since
\mathfrak{S}=\{(0,0,0)\}
, we have
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl(w_{i}v_{j}+w_{j}v_{i}\bigr)=1
for all
i\neq j
.
By linear independence, the vectors

(u_{1}+u_{2},v_{1}+v_{2},w_{1}+w_{2})
and

(u_{3},v_{3},w_{3})

are distinct nonzero elements of
\mathcal{U}_{V}
.
Moreover, we have
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl((w_{1}+w_{2})v_{3}+w_{3}(v_{1}+v_{2})\bigr)=0
.
Hence,

(u_{1}+u_{2},v_{1}+v_{2},w_{1}+w_{2})
and

(u_{3},v_{3},w_{3})

both belong to
\mathfrak{S}
,
contradicting the assumption that

\mathfrak{S}=\{(0,0,0)\}
.

Case (ii):

Suppose that
\mathfrak{S}\neq\{(0,0,0)\}
. If

\mathfrak{S}=\mathcal{U}_{V}
,
then
\mathrm{dim}(\mathfrak{S})=\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{U}_{V})\leq 2k
.

In the case
\mathfrak{S}\not\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{V}
, choose

\nu_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})\in\mathcal{U}_{V}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
.
By maximality, there exists

\nu_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})\in\mathfrak{S}
with

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{1}v_{2}+w_{2}v_{1})=1
,
which forces
u_{2}=u
and
u_{1}\notin\{0,u\}
.
Otherwise,

h(x)+h(x+u_{1})+h(x+u_{2})+h\bigl(x+u_{1}+u_{2}\bigr)=0,

which would imply that, for any
z\in V
,
D_{\nu_{1}}D_{\nu_{2}}f(x,y,z)=1
.

Conversely, for any
\nu_{i}=(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i})\in\mathfrak{S}
with
u_{i}=u
,
we have
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl(w_{1}v_{i}+w_{i}v_{1}\bigr)=1
.
Indeed, if this were not the case, then

\{u,u_{1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{W}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
,
which contradicts our assumption that

\mathcal{W}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\{0\}
.

We claim that any
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
contains at most two vectors whose last components have trace zero and whose first components are linearly independent.
This implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{V}\subseteq\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\times\Bigl\{(v,w)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}:{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w)=0\Bigr\},

which is a subspace of dimension
2k+1
.
Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exist three vectors
\nu_{i}=(u_{i},v_{i},w_{i})\in\mathcal{U}_{V}
,
i=1,2,3,
with linearly independent first components satisfying
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl(w_{i}v_{j}+w_{j}v_{i}\bigr)=1
for all
i\neq j
.
Since
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}
is linearly independent, the vectors

(u_{1}+u_{2},v_{1}+v_{2},w_{1}+w_{2})
and

(u_{3},v_{3},w_{3})

are distinct nonzero elements of
\mathcal{U}_{V}
.
Moreover, we have
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}\!\bigl((w_{1}+w_{2})v_{3}+w_{3}(v_{1}+v_{2})\bigr)=0
.
By the argument above, this implies that

(u_{1}+u_{2},u_{3})\in\{(0,u),(u,0),(u,u):u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\}.

Since
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
and
u_{3}\neq 0
, we must have
u_{1}+u_{2}=u_{3}
,
which contradicts the linear independence of
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}
.

Thus, any
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
f
consisting of

(u,v,w)
with
w\in V
has dimension at most
2k+1
. In particular, the dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{V}
is at most
2k+1
.

Step 2:

We now consider the set
\bigl\{\,(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{U}:{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w)=1\,\bigr\}
.
Equivalently, for a fixed
(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})\in\mathcal{U}
with
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{1})=1
, we can consider

\bigl\{\,(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),\;(u_{1}+u,\;v_{1}+v,\;w_{1}+w)\,:\,(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{U},\,{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w)=1\,\bigr\},

as both sets span the same subspace.
Since
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{1})={\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w)=1
, we have
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(w_{1}+w)=0
.
In particular,

(u_{1}+u,v_{1}+v,w_{1}+w)\in\mathcal{U}_{V}
,
where
\mathcal{U}_{V}
is defined in Equation (
15
).
Hence,
\mathcal{U}_{V}
and
(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})
generate the whole space
\mathcal{U}
, which implies that

\dim(\mathcal{U})\leq 2k+2
. Then our assumption
n>2k+4
implies that
\dim(\mathcal{U})<\tfrac{n}{2}+k
,
which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

In Proposition
3
, in order to construct functions in the
GMM
class that do not belong to the completed Maiorana–McFarland class, we start from Maiorana–McFarland functions.
Specifically, for

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
with
n=2m
,
we consider the function

h:\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
defined by
h(x_{1},x_{2})={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\bigl(x_{1}\,\pi(x_{2})\bigr)
,
where
\pi
is a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.
For
a=(a_{1},a_{2}),b=(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
,

\displaystyle D_{a}D_{b}h(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl(x_{1}\,\pi(x_{2})+\,(x_{1}+a_{1})\,\pi(x_{2}+a_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\,(x_{1}+b_{1})\,\pi(x_{2}+b_{2})+\,(x_{1}+a_{1}+b_{1})\,\pi(x_{2}+a_{2}+b_{2})\Bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl(x_{1}\bigl(\pi(x_{2})+\pi(x_{2}+a_{2})+\pi(x_{2}+b_{2})+\pi(x_{2}+a_{2}+b_{2})\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\;{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl(a_{1}\,\pi(x_{2}+a_{2})+\;b_{1}\,\pi(x_{2}+b_{2})+\;(a_{1}+b_{1})\,\pi(x_{2}+a_{2}+b_{2})\Bigr).

(16)

Recall that
\mathcal{W}=\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\{0\}

is an
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
h
of dimension
m=n/2
, called the canonical
\mathcal{M}
-subspace.
Our goal is to determine examples of
\pi
ensuring that
h
has this unique
\mathcal{M}
-subspace
\mathcal{W}
of dimension
n/2
such that, if
\mathcal{U}
is a nontrivial
\mathcal{M}
-subspace of
h
, then necessarily
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
. Equivalently, for distinct nonzero
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
,

D_{a}D_{b}\,h(x_{1},x_{2})=0\quad\text{for all }(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\quad\iff\quad a,b\in\mathcal{W}.

From Equation (
3
), we see that
D_{a}D_{b}\,h(x_{1},x_{2})=0
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
if and only if, for every
x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}

Lemma 5
.

Let
m\geq 4
and let
c,d
be nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.
Then
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(d\left(\tfrac{1}{x}+\tfrac{1}{x+c}\right)\right)

does not vanish identically on

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\setminus\{0,c\}
.

Proof.

We show that
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(d\left(\tfrac{1}{x}+\tfrac{1}{x+c}\right)\right)
is not a constant function, i.e.,
there exist

x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\setminus\{0,c\}

such that
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(d\left(\tfrac{1}{x_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{x_{1}+c}\right)\right)=0
and
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(d\left(\tfrac{1}{x_{2}}+\tfrac{1}{x_{2}+c}\right)\right)=1
.
This will establish the claim.

Observe that
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(d\left(\tfrac{1}{x}+\tfrac{1}{x+c}\right)\right)
equals
0
(respectively
1
) for some
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\setminus\{0,c\}
if and only if the curve
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
defined by

\mathcal{X}_{\eta}:\qquad Z^{2}+Z=d\left(\tfrac{1}{X}+\tfrac{1}{X+c}\right)+\eta

has an affine
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational point for some
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
with
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=0
(respectively
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=1
).

Let
F_{\eta}
denote the function field of
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
, namely

F_{\eta}=\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x,z),\qquad z^{2}+z=d\left(\tfrac{1}{x}+\tfrac{1}{x+c}\right)+\eta.

Then
F_{\eta}
is an Artin–Schreier extension of

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
of degree
2
; see
[
29
, Proposition 3.7.8]
.
The only ramified places of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
in
F_{\eta}

are
(x=0)
and
(x=c)
(i.e., the zero of
x
and
x+c
), each with different exponent
2
.
Consequently,
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
is the full constant field of
F_{\eta}
.
By the Hurwitz genus formula (
[
29
, Theorem 3.4.13]
), we have
g(F_{\eta})=1
.
Hence, by the Hasse–Weil bound (
[
29
, Theorem 5.2.3]
), the number
N(F_{\eta})
of rational places of

F_{\eta}
satisfies

N(F_{\eta})\geq 2^{m}+1-2^{(m+2)/2}.

(19)

Recall that an Artin–Schreier curve of the form

Z^{2}+Z=f(X)/g(X)
has no affine singular points whenever

\gcd(f(X),g(X))=1
.
In particular, the curve
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
has no affine singularities.
Since the highest-degree term in its defining equation is
Z^{2}X^{2}
,
there are exactly two points at infinity, namely
(0:1:0)
and
(1:0:0)
.
These correspond to places lying above
(x=0)
,
(x=c)
, and
(x=\infty)
, which is the pole of
x
.
Because the places
(x=0)
and
(x=c)
are ramified in
F_{\eta}
,
there is a unique rational place lying above each of them.
Moreover, depending on whether
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=0
or

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=1
, the place
(x=\infty)
either splits into two
rational places or remains a single place of degree
2
.
Hence, there are at most four rational places corresponding to points at infinity.

Since
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
has no affine singular points, each affine

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational point corresponds to a unique rational place.
Therefore, by (
19
), the number
N
of affine rational points of

\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
satisfies

N\geq 2^{m}-2^{(m+2)/2}-3.

In particular, for
m\geq 4
, there exist affine

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational points on
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
for both

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=0
and
{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(\eta)=1
,
which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
m\geq 4
, and let
\pi
be the permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}

defined by
\pi(x)=x^{2^{m}-2}
. Then
\pi
satisfies
Property (P)

of Corollary
4
. That is,
\pi(x)

satisfies Equations (
17
)
and (
18
) for all

if and only if one of the
following conditions holds:

(a_{1},a_{2})=(0,0),\quad(b_{1},b_{2})=(0,0),\quad(a_{1},a_{2})=(b_{1},b_{2}),\quad\text{or}\quad a_{2}=b_{2}=0.

Proof.

The sufficiency of the conditions

(a_{1},a_{2})=(0,0)
,
(b_{1},b_{2})=(0,0)
,
(a_{1},a_{2})=(b_{1},b_{2})
, or
a_{2}=b_{2}=0

is straightforward. In each case,
\pi(x)
satisfies
Equations (
17
) and (

) for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.
Therefore, it remains to establish necessity.

For any nonzero

, we can write

\pi(x)=1/x
. Hence, for

x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\setminus\{0,a_{2},b_{2},a_{2}+b_{2}\}

Equation (

\displaystyle\pi(x)+\pi(x+a_{2})+\pi(x+b_{2})+\pi(x+a_{2}+b_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x+a_{2}}+\frac{1}{x+b_{2}}+\frac{1}{x+a_{2}+b_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2}b_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2})}{x(x+a_{2})(x+b_{2})(x+a_{2}+b_{2})}.

Consequently, if Equation (
17
) holds for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
,
then necessarily
a_{2}=0
or
b_{2}=0
, or
a_{2}=b_{2}
.

We next consider the case
a_{2}=b_{2}=c
for some nonzero

For

,

) becomes

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl(a_{1}\,\pi(x+a_{2})+b_{1}\,\pi(x+b_{2})+(a_{1}+b_{1})\,\pi(x+a_{2}+b_{2})\Bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl((a_{1}+b_{1})\bigl(\pi(x)+\pi(x+c)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Bigl((a_{1}+b_{1})\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{x}+\tfrac{1}{x+c}\Bigr)\Bigr).

(20)

By Lemma
5
, the above trace vanishes on

only if
a_{1}=b_{1}
, i.e.,
(a_{1},a_{2})=(b_{1},b_{2})
.

Finally, suppose that exactly one of
a_{2}
or
b_{2}
is zero.
A computation analogous to (
3
) shows that
(
18
) cannot hold for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}

unless, respectively,
a_{1}=0
or
b_{1}=0
.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Decomposition of the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be a subspace of dimension
n-2
, and
let
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be a complementary subspace such that
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{W}
.
Let
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a Boolean function.
For each
w_{i}\in\mathcal{W}
, define
f_{i}(x)=f(x+w_{i})
for
x\in\mathcal{S}
.
Equivalently,
f_{i}
is the restriction of
f
to the coset

w_{i}+\mathcal{S}
, viewed as a Boolean function on
\mathcal{S}
.

The
4
-decomposition of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{S}
is then defined as the
sequence
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})
,
where
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}\}=\mathcal{W}
. Throughout the paper, we simply refer to this as a decomposition (with
respect to
\mathcal{S}
).
If all the functions
f_{i}
are bent (respectively, semibent), then
f

is said to admit a bent (respectively, semibent) decomposition with
respect to
\mathcal{S}
.

We remark that, in the decomposition of a function
f
, the
functions
f_{i}
are pairwise EA-equivalent and hence have
the same extended Walsh spectrum. In particular, they are bent or semibent
simultaneously. Therefore, it suffices to consider the restriction of

f
to the subspace
\mathcal{S}
.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{S}
has codimension
2
, there exist linearly independent vectors

u,v\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
such that
\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
.
In other words,

\mathcal{S}=\{\,x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}:\langle u,x\rangle_{n}=\langle v,x\rangle_{n}=0\,\}.

An equivalent criterion for a bent function
f
to admit a bent
(respectively, semibent) decomposition, expressed in terms of the
second-order derivative of its dual function, is given in

[
10
, Theorem 7]
as follows.

Lemma 10
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a bent function with dual
f^{*}
.
Let
u,v\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
be linearly independent, and let
\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}

be the orthogonal complement of
\langle u,v\rangle
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

f
admits a bent decomposition with respect to
\mathcal{S}

if and only if
D_{u}D_{v}f^{*}=1
.

(ii)

f
admits a semibent decomposition with respect to
\mathcal{S}

if and only if
D_{u}D_{v}f^{*}=0
.

From Lemma
10
, it follows that
f
admits a semibent
decomposition if and only if its dual
f^{*}
possesses a nontrivial

\mathcal{M}
-subspace; equivalently, its linearity index satisfies

\mathrm{ind}(f^{*})>1
. In particular, any
MM
or
GMM
bent function
admits a semibent decomposition. In contrast to
MM
and
GMM
bent
functions, it appears that the majority of the
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent
functions admit neither a bent nor a semibent decomposition; for
details, we refer to
[
2
]
.

In this section, we investigate the decomposition of bent functions in the
generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
class.
As in
[
2
]
, our approach relies on curves defined over finite fields
and on estimates for their number of affine rational points.
However, due to the definition of generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
functions,
the analysis requires more involved technical computations.

Let
m
,
k
, and
e
be integers such that
k\mid m
,

e\equiv 2^{\ell}\mod{(2^{k}-1)}
, and

\gcd(2^{m}-1,e)=1
.
Let
\eta
denote the multiplicative inverse of
e
modulo
(2^{m}-1)
, that
is,
\eta e\equiv 1\mod{(2^{m}-1)}
.
In particular, we consider the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions defined by

g(x,y)=P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(x\,y^{-\eta}\right)\right),

(23)

where
P\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a balanced Boolean
function.

To apply Lemma
10

Lemma 11
.

Let
g\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

be the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function defined in
(
23
). Then the dual bent function
g^{\ast}
is given by

g^{\ast}(x,y)=P\!\left({\rm Tr}^{m}_{k}\!\left(y\,x^{-e}\right)^{2^{m-\ell}}\right)=P\!\left({\rm Tr}^{m}_{k}\!\left(\tilde{y}\,\tilde{x}^{-e}\right)\right),

where
\tilde{y}=y^{2^{m-\ell}}
and
\tilde{x}=x^{2^{m-\ell}}
.

Proof.

For
(u,v)\in\bigl(\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\bigr)\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, let
\chi_{u,v}
denote the character of

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
defined by

\chi_{u,v}(x,y)=(-1)^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}
.
Let
\Gamma_{2}=\{V,\mathcal{B}(\gamma)\;:\;\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\}

be the generalized Desarguesian spread defined in (
9
), where
V
and

\mathcal{B}(\gamma)
are as in (
7
) and (
8
), respectively.
We note that
g(x,y)=P(\gamma)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}(\gamma)
and

g(x,y)=P(0)
for all
(x,y)\in V
, i.e.,
g
is a bent function arising from the bent partition

\Gamma_{2}
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 10]
, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
we have

\chi_{u,v}\bigl(B(\gamma)\bigr)=\sum_{(x,y)\in B(\gamma)}(-1)^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}=\begin{cases}2^{m}-2^{m-k},&\text{if }u\neq 0\text{ and }\gamma^{2^{\ell}}={\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(vu^{-e}\right),\\[2.84526pt]
-\,2^{m-k},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\chi_{u,v}(V)=\sum_{(x,y)\in V}(-1)^{{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }u\neq 0,\\[2.84526pt]
2^{m},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We now compute the Walsh transform of
g
:

\displaystyle W_{g}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(-1)^{\,g(x,y)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(-1)^{\,P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(x\,y^{-\eta}\right)\right)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}}\sum_{(x,y)\in B(\gamma)}(-1)^{\,P(\gamma)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}+\sum_{(x,y)\in V}(-1)^{\,P(0)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ux+vy)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}}(-1)^{\,P(\gamma)}\chi_{u,v}\bigl(B(\gamma)\bigr)+(-1)^{\,P(0)}\chi_{u,v}(V).

Using the character values above together with the balancedness of

P\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we obtain

W_{g}(u,v)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{P(0)}\,2^{m},&\text{if }u=0,\\[2.84526pt]
(-1)^{P(\gamma)}\,2^{m},&\text{if }u\neq 0\text{, where }\gamma^{2^{\ell}}={\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(v\,u^{-e}\right).\end{cases}

This yields the claimed expression for the dual function, together with the identity
\gamma=\gamma^{2^{m}}={\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(v\,u^{-e}\right)^{\,2^{m-\ell}}={\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\!\left(v^{\,2^{m-\ell}}\,u^{-e{\,2^{m-\ell}}}\right)
.
∎

Note that the trace map

{\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}

and the function

P\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

are both balanced. Consequently, the composition

P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}(z)\right)

defines a balanced Boolean function from
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Hence, there exists a permutation
Q
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
such that
P\!\left({\rm Tr}_{k}^{m}(z)\right)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q(z)\right)
.
Therefore, in the remainder of this section, we consider the generalized

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions from

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}

of the form

\displaystyle f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left(x\,y^{-\eta}\right)\right).

(24)

We remark that, in this case, the dual function of
f
is given by

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left(\tilde{y}\,\tilde{x}^{-e}\right)\right),

(25)

where
\tilde{y}=y^{2^{m-\ell}}
and
\tilde{x}=x^{2^{m-\ell}}
.
Moreover, by Lemma
2
, we can without loss of generality suppose that
Q(0)=0
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
m>4
, and let
Q
be a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
of odd polynomial degree less than or equal to
\frac{2^{m/4}-1}{3e}
.
Let
u=(a,b)
and

be two linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
, and set
\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
.
Let
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the generalized

\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function defined in (
24
).
If
ad+bc\neq 0
, then
f
admits neither a bent nor a semibent decomposition on
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 6
.

Let

f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

be the function defined in (
24
), and suppose that it satisfies the
assumptions of Proposition
12
. Then, equivalently, we have:

(i)

The dual function
f^{\ast}
of
f
does not admit any

\mathcal{M}
-subspace

\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle
with
ad+bc\neq 0
.

(ii)

If
f
admits a bent or semibent decomposition on

\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle^{\perp}
, then necessarily

.
Equivalently,
(c,d)=\lambda(a,b)
for some

.

Before proving Proposition
12
, we require some preliminary results.
The first lemma is analogous to the one appearing in
[
2
]
; however,
we include its proof for completeness.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the
(n-2)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
defined by

{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(ax+by)=0\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(cx+dy)=0.

Assume that
ad+bc\neq 0
. Then the function
f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\bigl(Q(xy^{-\eta})\bigr)

admits a bent decomposition (respectively, a semibent decomposition) on
\mathcal{S}
if and only if
D_{(1,0)}D_{(0,1)}\widehat{f}(x,y)

is identically equal to
1
(respectively, identically equal to
0
) for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
, where

\widehat{f}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left(\bigl(b^{2^{m-\ell}}x+d^{2^{m-\ell}}y\bigr)\bigl(a^{2^{m-\ell}}x+c^{2^{m-\ell}}y\bigr)^{-e}\right)\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
10
, the function
f
admits a bent decomposition (respectively, a semibent decomposition) on
\mathcal{S}
if and only if the second-order derivative
D_{u}D_{v}f^{*}(x)

of the dual function
f^{*}(x,y)
given in (
25
)
is constantly equal to
1
(respectively, constantly equal to
0
), where
u=(a,b)
and
v=(c,d)
.

By Lemma
2
, this condition is equivalent to requiring that
D_{(1,0)}D_{(0,1)}f^{*}\bigl(L(x,y)\bigr)

is constantly equal to
1
(respectively,
0
), where
L
is a linear permutation of

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
satisfying
L(1,0)=(a,b)
and
L(0,1)=(c,d)
.

Define the linear map
L
by
L(x,y)=(ax^{2^{\ell}}+cy^{2^{\ell}},\;bx^{2^{\ell}}+dy^{2^{\ell}})
.
The map
L
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
if and only if
ad+bc\neq 0
. Under this assumption, we indeed have

L(1,0)=(a,b)
and
L(0,1)=(c,d)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle f^{*}\bigl(L(x,y)\bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left((bx^{2^{\ell}}+dy^{2^{\ell}})^{2^{m-\ell}}(ax^{2^{\ell}}+cy^{2^{\ell}})^{-2^{m-\ell}e}\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left(\bigl(b^{2^{m-\ell}}x+d^{2^{m-\ell}}y\bigr)\bigl(a^{2^{m-\ell}}x+c^{2^{m-\ell}}y\bigr)^{-e}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(x,y).

This yields the desired conclusion.
∎

By Lemma
13
, in order to prove Proposition
12
, it suffices to show that,
for
u=(1,0)
and
v=(0,1)
, the second-order derivative
D_{u}D_{v}\widehat{f}(x,y)

is nonconstant.
Since
a^{2^{m-\ell}}d^{2^{m-\ell}}+b^{2^{m-\ell}}c^{2^{m-\ell}}=(ad+bc)^{2^{m-\ell}}
,
the condition
ad+bc\neq 0
holds if and only if
a^{2^{m-\ell}}d^{2^{m-\ell}}+b^{2^{m-\ell}}c^{2^{m-\ell}}\neq 0
.
Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume
ad+bc\neq 0
and consider

\widetilde{f}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(Q\!\left((bx+dy)(ax+cy)^{-e}\right)\right).

More precisely, we compute

\displaystyle D_{u}D_{v}\widetilde{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(x+1,y+1)+\widetilde{f}(x+1,y)+\widetilde{f}(x,y+1)+\widetilde{f}(x,y)

(26)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\Big(Q\big((b(x+1)+d(y+1))(a(x+1)+c(y+1))^{-e}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+Q\big((b(x+1)+dy)(a(x+1)+cy)^{-e}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+Q\big((bx+d(y+1))(ax+c(y+1))^{-e}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+Q\big((bx+dy)(ax+cy)^{-e}\big)\Big).

The following lemma is frequently used to characterize the circumstances under which the second-order derivative of a function cannot be constant.

Lemma 14
.

For a positive integer
\ell\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
be the curve over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
defined by

Z^{2}+Z=P\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}X+\beta_{1}}{\kappa_{1}X+\upsilon_{1}}\right)+\cdots+P\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{\ell}X+\beta_{\ell}}{\kappa_{\ell}X+\upsilon_{\ell}}\right)+\vartheta,

(27)

where
P
is a polynomial of odd degree
t
.
Assume that

\alpha_{i}\upsilon_{i}+\beta_{i}\kappa_{i}\neq 0\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,\ell,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\exists j\text{ s.t. }\frac{\upsilon_{j}}{\kappa_{j}}\neq\frac{\upsilon_{i}}{\kappa_{i}}\ \text{for all }i\neq j.

Then the number
N_{\vartheta}
of affine
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational points

(x,z)\in\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
with
x\neq\upsilon_{i}/\kappa_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
, of
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}

satisfies

N_{\vartheta}\;\geq\;2^{m}-1-(\ell t+1)\ell t\,2^{\frac{m}{2}}-\ell(\ell t+2).

In particular, if
m\geq 4
and
\ell t\leq 2^{m/4}-1
, then
N_{\vartheta}>0
.

Proof.

Let
F_{\vartheta}=\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x,z)
be the function field of
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
.
We may regard
F_{\vartheta}
as an extension of the rational function field

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
defined by the equation
z^{2}+z=g_{\vartheta}(x)
,
where

g_{\vartheta}(x)=P\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}x+\beta_{1}}{\kappa_{1}x+\upsilon_{1}}\right)+\cdots+P\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{\ell}x+\beta_{\ell}}{\kappa_{\ell}x+\upsilon_{\ell}}\right)+\vartheta.

Note that
F_{\vartheta}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
is an Artin–Schreier extension of degree
2
;
see
[
29
, Proposition 3.7.8]
.
A place
R
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
ramifies in
F_{\vartheta}
only if the valuation

v_{R}(g_{\vartheta}(x))
is negative.
This can occur only when
R=(x=\upsilon_{i}/\kappa_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.
Moreover, by the strict triangle inequality (see
[
29
, Lemma 1.1.11]
),
for the place
R_{j}
corresponding to

x=\upsilon_{j}/\kappa_{j}
, where
\tfrac{\upsilon_{j}}{\kappa_{j}}\neq\tfrac{\upsilon_{i}}{\kappa_{i}}
for all
i\neq j
,
we obtain

v_{R_{j}}\bigl(g_{\vartheta}(x)\bigr)=-\deg(P)=-t.

Since
t
is odd, the place
R_{j}
is totally ramified in the extension

F_{\vartheta}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}(x)
.
Consequently,
F_{\vartheta}
is a function field with full constant field

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
is an absolutely irreducible curve defined
over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
; see
[
29
, Corollary 3.6.8]
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
has degree

\deg(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})\leq\ell t+2
.
Applying the Hasse-Weil bound (see
[
16
, Theorem 9.57]
), we obtain the following
estimate for the number
N(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational points of

\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
in the projective plane:

\displaystyle N(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{m}+1-(\deg(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})-1)(\deg(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})-2)\,2^{\frac{m}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{m}+1-(\ell t+1)\ell t\,2^{\frac{m}{2}}.

(28)

As the highest-degree term in the defining equation of

\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
is
X^{s}Z^{2}
with
s\leq\ell t
, the curve

\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
has at most two
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational points at infinity, namely
(0:1:0)
and
(1:0:0)
.
In order to ensure that the denominators in (
27
) do not vanish,
we must also exclude all points lying on the
\ell
lines defined by
\kappa_{i}X+\upsilon_{i}=0
.
By Bézout’s theorem, a line intersects
\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta}
in at most
\deg(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})
points.
Hence, by subtracting
2+\ell\deg(\mathcal{X}_{\vartheta})
from (
4
), we obtain the desired bound on the number
N_{\vartheta}
of affine
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-rational points.
Moreover, whenever
m\geq 4

and
\ell t\leq 2^{m/4}-1
, we obtain

(\ell t+1)\ell t\,2^{\frac{m}{2}}+\ell(\ell t+2)+1<2^{m},

and hence
N_{\vartheta}>0
.
∎

.

Therefore, the existence of such a point follows from
Lemma
14
.

\Box

We now give a complete analysis of the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function in the case
Q(x)=x
.
That is, we consider the bent function

f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2},\qquad f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(x\,y^{-\eta}\right).

Corollary 15
.

Let
m
,
k
, and
e
be positive integers such that

k\mid m,\qquad e\equiv 2^{\ell}\mod{(2^{k}-1)},\qquad\gcd(2^{m}-1,e)=1.

Let
\eta
denote the multiplicative inverse of
e
modulo
2^{m}-1
.
If
k\leq\tfrac{m}{4}-3
, then the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function
f(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(x\,y^{-\eta}\right)
on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}

satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The function
f
admits a semibent decomposition on

\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
, for linearly independent

u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
,
if and only if

u,v\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.

(ii)

The function
f
does not admit any bent decomposition.

Proof.

Let
u=(a,b)
and
v=(c,d)
be two linearly independent vectors in

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.
By Proposition
12
, the function
f
does not admit any semibent or bent
decomposition on
\mathcal{S}
whenever
ad+bc\neq 0
.
Therefore, we restrict our attention to the case
ad+bc=0
.
Recall that, since
u=(a,b)
and
v=(c,d)
are linearly independent vectors in

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
, the condition
ad+bc=0

is equivalent to
(c,d)=\lambda(a,b)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

By Lemma
10
, the function
f
admits a semibent (respectively, bent)
decomposition on
\mathcal{S}
if and only if its dual
f^{\ast}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\left(y^{2^{\ell}}\,x^{-2^{\ell}e}\right)

satisfies

D_{u}D_{v}f^{\ast}=0\quad\text{(respectively, }D_{u}D_{v}f^{\ast}=1\text{).}

Note that for the linear map
L(x,y)=(x^{2^{\ell}},y^{2^{\ell}})
we have

f^{\ast}(x,y)=\widetilde{f}(L(x,y))
, where

\widetilde{f}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(y\,x^{-e})
.
Hence, by Lemma
2
, it suffices to consider the second-order derivative of
\widetilde{f}
.

By replacing
c=\lambda a
and
d=\lambda b
,
the second-order derivative
D_{u}D_{v}\widetilde{f}(x,y)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle D_{u}D_{v}\widetilde{f}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Big(

\displaystyle(y+b(1+\lambda))\,(x+a(1+\lambda))^{-e}+(y+b)\,(x+a)^{-e}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(y+\lambda b)\,(x+\lambda a)^{-e}+y\,x^{-e}\Big).

We now proceed by a case-by-case analysis.

Case (i):

a=0
, and hence
b\neq 0
.

In this case, we obtain
D_{u}D_{v}\widetilde{f}=0
for all such choices of
u
and
v
.
Therefore,
\widetilde{f}
admits a semibent decomposition on
\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
.
In particular, for any linearly independent

u,v\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
,
the function
\widetilde{f}
admits a semibent decomposition on
\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
.
Since

L^{-1}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}})=\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}},

it follows from Lemma
2
that

f^{*}(x,y)
admits a semibent decomposition on

\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
for all linearly independent

u,v\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
.

Case (ii):

b=0
, and hence
a\neq 0
.

Then applying the changes of variables
x\mapsto ax
and
y\mapsto a^{e}y

and then setting
y=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Big((x+1+\lambda)^{-e}+(x+1)^{-e}+(x+\lambda)^{-e}+x^{-e}\Big).

(30)

Hence, if
D_{u}D_{v}f^{\ast}(x,y)
is a constant function,
then the function
g(x)
in (
30
) must also be constant,
which is impossible by Lemma
14
. Then, by Lemma
2
, the function
f^{*}
does not admit neither a bent or a semibent decomposition on
\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
for any linearly independent
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\{0\}
.

Case (iii):

ab\neq 0
.

Proceeding as before, we apply the change of variables
x\mapsto ax
and

y\mapsto by
, and then set
\alpha=ba^{-e}
and
y=0
. This yields

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}\!\Big(\alpha\Big((1+\lambda)\,(x+1+\lambda)^{-e}+(x+1)^{-e}+\lambda(x+\lambda)^{-e}\Big)\,\Big).

(31)

As in the previous cases, if
D_{u}D_{v}\widetilde{f}(x,y)
were a
constant function, then
g(x)
in (
31
) would also
have to be constant. This, however, is impossible by Lemma
14
. Then we similarly conclude that
f^{*}
admits neither a bent nor a semibent decomposition on
\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
for any such linearly independent elements
u,v
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 7
.

Note that the generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function

in

, and hence its dual

, is also an

to arbitrary permutation

whose degree is sufficiently small compared to the

, we obtain the following sufficient conditions under

bent function admits neither a

Theorem 2

Let

,
k
, and

Similarly, by Proposition
12

\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}

\widetilde{f}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{1}^{m}(Q(y\,x^{-e}))

Case
a=0
(and hence

Setting

5
Decomposition and concatenation of bent functions

Let
u,v
be two linearly independent elements of
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
, and let

\mathcal{S}=\langle u,v\rangle^{\perp}
.
Since
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{n}
is a nondegenerate inner product on

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
, we can write

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\langle u,v\rangle
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a Boolean function, and
let
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})
be its decomposition with respect to

\mathcal{S}
. After a suitable change of coordinates as described
above, we may identify
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{V}_{n-2}^{(2)}
and
u=(1,0)
,
v=(0,1)
.
Thus, we may regard
f
as a function on

\mathbb{V}_{n-2}^{(2)}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}
obtained by
concatenation of
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\colon\mathbb{V}_{n-2}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
That is, in this representation, the decomposition
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})

satisfies

f(x,y,z)=\begin{cases}f_{1}(x),&\text{if }(y,z)=(0,0),\\
f_{2}(x),&\text{if }(y,z)=(0,1),\\
f_{3}(x),&\text{if }(y,z)=(1,0),\\
f_{4}(x),&\text{if }(y,z)=(1,1),\end{cases}

where
x\in\mathbb{V}_{n-2}^{(2)}
and
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Equivalently,

f(x,y,z)=f_{1}(x)+yz\bigl(f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}+f_{4}\bigr)(x)+y\bigl(f_{1}+f_{3}\bigr)(x)+z\bigl(f_{1}+f_{2}\bigr)(x).

In other words,
f
is obtained by concatenation of the Boolean functions

f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}
.

We define the
4
-concatenation (or simply, concatenation) of arbitrary Boolean functions

f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
as the Boolean
function
f=f_{1}\,\|\,f_{2}\,\|\,f_{3}\,\|\,f_{4}
from

\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
given by

f(x,y,z)=f_{1}(x)+yz\bigl(f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}+f_{4}\bigr)(x)+y\bigl(f_{1}+f_{3}\bigr)(x)+z\bigl(f_{1}+f_{2}\bigr)(x),

where
x\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}
and
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

We remark that the concatenation method has been used efficiently for
the secondary construction of Boolean bent functions outside the
MM^{\#}
class. For instance, see

[
18
,
25
,
27
]
as well as the survey paper

[
24
]
and the references therein.
The necessary and sufficient condition for the concatenation of bent
functions to be bent is given as follows.

Lemma 16

(
[
17
, Theorem III.1]
)
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}
be four Boolean bent functions. Then the concatenation
f=f_{1}\,\|\,f_{2}\,\|\,f_{3}\,\|\,f_{4}

is bent if and only if
f_{1}^{*}+f_{2}^{*}+f_{3}^{*}+f_{4}^{*}=1
.

We recall that a
GMM
function
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

is defined by
f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+{\rm Tr}_{1}^{k}(yz)
,
where, for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
, the function

f^{(z)}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
is bent.
In the special case
k=1
, that is, for

f\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}

given by
f(x,y,z)=f^{(z)}(x)+yz
, we observe that

f(x,y,z)=f^{(0)}(x)
whenever
z=0
, whereas for
z=1
we have

f(x,y,z)=f^{(1)}(x)
if
y=0
and

f(x,y,z)=f^{(1)}(x)+1
if
y=1
.
Consequently, as noted in
[
2
]
,
f
can be expressed as the
concatenation
f=f^{(0)}\,\|\,f^{(1)}\,\|\,f^{(0)}\,\|\,\bigl(f^{(1)}+1\bigr)
.
In this case, the necessary and sufficient condition on the dual
functions in Lemma
16
is trivially satisfied.
More generally, as in the case
k=1
,
GMM
functions may be viewed as
concatenations of bent functions over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
.

In general, concatenations of bent functions (particularly the ones of the form
f=f^{(0)}\,\|\,f^{(1)}\,\|\,f^{(0)}\,\|\,\bigl(f^{(1)}+1\bigr)
) have been studied for constructing bent functions outside the
completed
MM
class. In the recent paper
[
2
]
, the
concatenation of vectorial
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions is
investigated. The motivation stems from the fact that the components of
a vectorial
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent function
F\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(2)}

satisfy the following property: for any nonzero

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(2)}
with
\alpha\neq\beta
, we have

\displaystyle(F_{\alpha})^{*}+(F_{\beta})^{*}=(F_{\alpha+\beta})^{*}.

(33)

The property in (
33
) holds for almost all bent functions arising from bent partitions; in particular, it applies to vectorial generalized
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions
F\colon\mathbb{V}_{n}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(2)}
. Owing to their explicit representation, analogous to that of
\mathcal{PS}_{ap}
bent functions, one can construct bent functions by concatenating the components of
F
as
f=F_{\alpha}\,\|\,F_{\beta}\,\|\,F_{\gamma}\,\|\,F_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}+1
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{V}_{m}^{(2)}
are nonzero elements such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma\neq 0
, as stated below. The proof proceeds by analogous arguments and is therefore omitted.

f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
