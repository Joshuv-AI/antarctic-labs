 What is the SLOPE Tool?
• State and Local Planning for Energy (SLOPE) is a free, easy-to-use data visualization platform developed by NLR researchers
 that supports state and local energy planning.

• The SLOPE Platform brings together potential and projection data organized by different jurisdictions (e.g., state, county) and
 topics:

 o Energy efficiency

 o Energy cost

 o Energy consumption

• The SLOPE Platform supports state and local governments by helping users explore opportunities, scenarios, and understand
 the impacts of energy-related actions.

 o Providing localized data

 o integrating interoperability across tools

 o Integrating information across sectors and technologies

 o Helping users explore opportunities and scenarios to guide strategy development.

 o Showing the potential impacts of energy-related actions to support informed choices.
 Source: NREL, 2024

 Scenario Planner

 Scenario Viewer Metrics

There are four available energy system metrics:

• Energy consumption (county and state level) can be used to identify the distribution of energy
  consumption across energy sectors and fuel types (non-electricity energy and electricity) for a
  selected jurisdiction.
 • Sectors with high energy consumption could be possible targets for energy efficiency
 opportunities, which you can explore using the High Energy Efficiency Demand Scenario.
 • You can also explore energy savings from electrifying vehicles and buildings using the High
 Level of Electrification Demand Scenario option.

• Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides (county and state level) are federally regulated air
  contaminants that have been shown to have serious impacts on human health and the environment.
 • Examining this energy metric can help communities compare how different energy strategies
 can result in air quality improvements.

                                                                                   National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   6

 Scenario Viewer Metrics (cont.)

• Carbon dioxide emissions (county and state level)
 • Exploring changes to this metric under various supply and demand scenario options can be
 used to understand and prioritize strategies for exhaust reduction across sectors.

• System costs (state level only) show the balance of investments and savings over time for energy
  system infrastructure, fuel, operations and maintenance, and equipment.
 • Communities can use this information to examine potential investments and discover savings
 opportunities.
 • The net system cost graph, capturing the sum of system expenditures and savings, is displayed
 per year, with investments shown as positive values and savings as negative values.
 • Users can also use this data to calculate cumulative costs and savings over a span of years.
 • Important note: not all investments or savings are always realized by the same stakeholders
 and will vary depending on business models.

 What Kinds of Questions can SLOPE Answer?

• What is the generation potential and cost of energy by technology across the counties in my state?
• What is the potential for bill savings from energy efficiency for low-income households in counties
 across my state?
• How many electric vehicles could be registered in each county in my state by 2030 in a high
 electrification scenario compared to a business-as-usual scenario?
• How much electricity and natural gas is consumed in my jurisdiction?
• How much energy could be produced if my jurisdiction fully developed residential and commercial
 rooftop solar, and how does that compare to utility scale solar potential?
• What is the energy bill savings potential of cost-effective efficiency measure for households in my
 county?
• What are reasonable goals and interim targets for vehicle electrification, energy generation, or
 building efficiency in my jurisdiction?
 Source: Corrigan and Henry, 2024

Case Studies

Grid Modernization and Transportation Planning in New
 Mexico
• Description: New Mexico is exploring:

• How local, distributed generation and energy efficiency programs can meet
 the state’s energy needs and minimize the need for new transmission
 infrastructure and out-of-state resources.

• Technologies to support energy affordability and differences between rural
 and urban areas

• How to ensure the electricity grid is prepared for transportation
 electrification.

• Technology: Solar and electric vehicles

• Impact:

• Residential solar rooftop potential alone can generate 49%–125% of
 counties’ residential electricity demand. Maps: NREL, 2025

• Rural counties can benefit from projects developed on rural parcels that
 bring in tax revenue and jobs.

• There may be as many as 193,000 electric vehicles by 2030 and perhaps
 more than 972,000 electric vehicles in New Mexico by 2050.

 Affordability Planning in Milwaukee, Wisconsin

• Description: The City and County of Milwaukee, Wisconsin, are using
 SLOPE to better understand:
• Their energy use and energy potential, focusing on which sectors can
 have the biggest impact on reducing costs and harmful air contaminants.
• How the benefits of an energy economy are realized by communities that
 experience energy affordability issues.
• What energy technologies are most cost effective over time.
• Technology: Wind
• Impact: SLOPE showed that:
• With a $20 incentive per MWh, Wisconsin’s residential sector can save an
 estimated additional 630 GWh in 2035, equating to an estimated $86
 million per year in additional cost savings based on 2020 electricity prices.
• SLOPE also projected that land-based, utility-scale wind to have the Graphs: NREL, 2025

 lowest levelized cost of energy from new generation facilities by 2030 in
 Milwaukee County.
