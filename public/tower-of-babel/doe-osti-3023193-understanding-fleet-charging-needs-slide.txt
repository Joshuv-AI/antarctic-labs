 Energy to Communities (E2C)
 Technical Assistance Opportunities
In-Depth Partnerships ~three years
Multiyear partnership made up of teams (local governments, community-based organizations, and electric utilities) that work
alongside national laboratory staff to apply robust modeling and analysis tools and simulate hardware in conditions that match
communities' unique contexts to evaluate potential scenarios and strategies before full technology deployment.
Supports nine communities currently.

Peer-Learning Cohorts ~six months
Six-months of regular convenings where cohorts of up to 15 participants exchange strategies and best practices, learn in a
collaborative environment, and workshop plans and strategies to overcome challenges relating to a similar energy
topic. Eligibility varies by cohort topic.
Supports about 100 communities per year.

Expert Match ~three months
Short-term, no-cost technical assistance for communities seeking to answer near-term energy questions. Eligible entities include
local governments, utilities, community-based organizations, schools, and nonprofits.
Supports about 200 communities per year. Applications are accepted all year on a rolling basis.

3: Identify What Type of EV Charging Is Needed

 3: Identify What Type of EV Charging Is Needed

 When calculating the EV charging power level needed to support
 fleet vehicles, consider the following:
 • Rarely will a vehicle need to charge from 0% to 100%.
 • For a vehicle relying on overnight charging, Level 1 or 2 charging is often sufficient.
 • For vehicles that need on-site midday charging, direct current (DC) fast charging may
 be needed.

 Networked EVSEs

A networked EVSE has built-in data management or metering 14

capabilities that track the energy use. This commonly provides the
ability to process payment transactions, control vehicle access, track
charging session data, and manage charging.

Best Practices for Identifying EVSE Needs

 Best Practices for Identifying EVSE Needs

 • Determine the required scale and
 type of EV charging stations before
 assessing feasibility and cost.
 • Ensure charging infrastructure is
 ready before ZEV delivery.
 • Design with optimal EV:EVSE ratios
 based on project area fleet needs.
 • Plan for future Level 2 and DC fast
 charging, including charging needs
 for medium- and heavy-duty
 vehicles.
