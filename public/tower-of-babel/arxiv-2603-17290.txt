State-dependent broadband X-ray timing reconfiguration in the changing-look AGN NGC 1566 2026 State-dependent broadband X-ray timing reconfiguration in the changing-look AGN NGC 1566 – 4
Y. Tao, J. Tang, X. Wei, X.-H. Zhang

Abstract
NGC 1566 has exhibited dramatic state changes in its X-ray spectrum, but the evolution of its broadband timing properties remains poorly constrained. We combine long-term Swift monitoring with high-time-resolution XMM-Newton observations to model the broadband X-ray power spectral density (PSD) in the dim and bright states. In the hard band, the PSD bend frequency shifts by about 1 dex between the two states, implying a substantially longer characteristic variability timescale in the bright state. The relative timing behaviour of the soft and hard bands also changes with state: in the dim state, the soft-band bend frequency is higher than the hard-band value by ∼ \sim 0.49 dex, whereas in the bright state the two become broadly consistent. The broadband variability evolution of NGC 1566 therefore involves not only an overall shift in characteristic timescale, but also a state-dependent change in the soft–hard timing relation, from a more stratified to a more tightly coupled configuration. Combined with previous spectral results, this supports a genuine reconfiguration of the inner radiative structure during the changing-look transition.



1
Introduction

Changing-look active galactic nuclei (CL AGNs) provide a valuable opportunity to probe rapid changes in the inner accretion flow across different radiative states. Multiwavelength observations increasingly suggest that, in many cases, their dramatic transitions are driven primarily by intrinsic accretion-flow changes rather than by line-of-sight obscuration alone
(
11
;
8
, e.g.)
. NGC 1566 is one of the best-studied examples, particularly following its extreme 2018 outburst
(
9
;
10
)
. Broadband X-ray studies showed that, near the peak of the outburst, the source displayed the spectral properties of a Seyfert 1 nucleus and that its behaviour was most likely associated with intrinsic accretion-flow evolution
(
10
)
. Later work further showed substantial changes in both the soft excess and the broadband continuum, together with systematic evolution in the disc, soft-excess, and coronal properties, supporting a reconfiguration of the inner radiative structure
(
3
;
14
)
.

In contrast, the broadband timing behaviour of NGC 1566 remains much less well constrained. The bend frequency
f_{\rm b}
in the power spectral density (PSD) defines a characteristic variability timescale,
t_{\rm b}=1/f_{\rm b}
, so a shift in
f_{\rm b}
implies a corresponding change in the dominant X-ray variability timescale
(
7
;
6
)
. Bend frequencies measured in different energy bands can also provide clues to how the variability-producing regions are coupled
(
1
;
15
, e.g.)
. This makes NGC 1566 an especially useful target for testing whether its changing-look transition is accompanied by a measurable evolution in characteristic X-ray timing behaviour.

Here we combine long-term
Swift
monitoring with high-time-resolution
XMM-Newton
observations to model the broadband X-ray PSD of NGC 1566 in its dim and bright states. We ask whether the bend frequency shifts significantly between states, and whether the soft–hard timing relation evolves as well. We show that the broadband PSD is reconfigured between the dim and bright states, with significant changes in both the bend frequency and its energy dependence.

2
Data and methods

2.1
Data reduction and state selection

We combine long-term
Swift
/XRT monitoring with high-time-resolution
XMM-Newton
observations to characterise the broadband X-ray variability of NGC 1566. Using the publicly available
Swift
/XRT 2–10 keV monitoring light-curve products retrieved from the online archive
(
2
)
, we define a dim-state interval spanning MJD 56185–57233 and a bright-state interval spanning MJD 58293–58449. These background-subtracted, exposure-corrected
Swift
light curves are used directly to constrain the low-frequency variability.

For the
XMM-Newton
data, we reduce the EPIC-pn observations falling within these two state intervals (ObsID 0763500201 for the dim state and ObsID 0800840201 for the bright state) using SAS (v22.1.0), following standard procedures including the generation of calibration files, event reconstruction, flare filtering based on the 10–12 keV background light curve, and event selection with
PATTERN <= 4
and
FLAG = 0
. We assess pile-up using
epatplot
and find it to be negligible in the bright-state observation. We then extract soft (0.3–2 keV) and hard (2–10 keV) light curves from a circular source region of radius 36 arcsec and a nearby source-free background region, and apply
epiclccorr
for background subtraction and instrumental corrections. The resulting
XMM-Newton
light curves, binned at 50 s, are used to constrain the high-frequency PSD. Together with the multi-year
Swift
monitoring, they provide complementary coverage of the low- and high-frequency variability for the joint broadband PSD modelling.

2.2
PSD modelling and joint constraints

We model the PSD with a bending power law, where
\alpha_{\rm low}
and
\alpha_{\rm high}
describe the low- and high-frequency slopes, and
f_{\rm b}
defines the bend frequency. The main parameters of interest are
f_{\rm b}
, corresponding to the characteristic timescale
t_{\rm b}=1/f_{\rm b}
, and
\alpha_{\rm high}
, which describes the suppression of short-timescale variability. For the irregularly sampled
Swift
light curves, we construct Lomb–Scargle periodograms
(
4
;
12
)
and quantify the low-frequency PSD constraint using a PSRESP-based forward-modelling approach that accounts for the sampling window, red-noise leak, and measurement noise
(
13
;
16
;
17
)
. For the continuous
XMM-Newton
light curves, we construct periodograms and evaluate the high-frequency constraint on the same
(f_{\rm b},\alpha_{\rm high})
grid. At each grid point, the PSD normalization and Poisson-noise level are treated as nuisance parameters and optimised by minimising the Whittle negative log-likelihood in periodogram space. In practice, we restrict the
XMM-Newton
fit to frequencies below an empirically determined upper limit at which the binned periodogram remains above the estimated Poisson-noise floor
(
18
)
. We then combine the
XMM-Newton
Whittle likelihood with the logarithm of the
Swift
PSRESP acceptance fraction on a common
(f_{\rm b},\alpha_{\rm high})
grid to define an empirical joint-constraint surface, focusing on the PSD shape parameters rather than on the absolute normalization. Here the
Swift
acceptance fraction is used as an empirical support term rather than as a formal Bayesian prior, and the resulting normalized joint weights are used only to rank solutions and to define weighted intervals. The Lomb–Scargle periodograms, PSRESP simulations, and joint grid calculations were carried out with custom Python scripts.

We do not impose a single low-frequency slope across all states and bands. Instead, we first use single-power-law fits to the
Swift
PSDs to guide the choice of
\alpha_{\rm low}
, and then perform the bending-model search. In the 2–10 keV band, both states favour stable solutions with
\alpha_{\rm low}=1
, which we adopt throughout the hard-band analysis. In the 0.3–2 keV band, the dim state favours a flatter low-frequency slope, and we therefore adopt
\alpha_{\rm low}=0.85
as the fiducial choice while retaining
\alpha_{\rm low}=1
as a control. The soft-band bright state is well described with
\alpha_{\rm low}=1
. The qualitative conclusion that the soft-band bend frequency is higher than the hard-band value in the dim state, and that the soft–hard bend-frequency separation becomes smaller in the bright state, remains unchanged when
\alpha_{\rm low}=1
is adopted as a control for the dim-state soft band.

To ensure that the inferred constraints are not driven by coarse grid sampling or by a single Monte Carlo realization, we further refine the parameter grid around the preferred solution and verify the stability of the inferred peak by increasing the simulation statistics and repeating the calculation with multiple random seeds. In what follows, we focus on the PSD shape, bend frequency, and high-frequency slope rather than on the absolute power normalization. Unless otherwise stated, all quoted parameter uncertainties correspond to the 16th–84th weighted percentile intervals derived from the normalized joint weights on the parameter grid. 3
Results

3.1
State dependence of the broadband hard-band PSD

Figure
3
presents the joint broadband PSD constraints for NGC 1566 in the dim and bright states in the 2–10 keV band. The two states show clearly different PSD shapes, with the most prominent difference being a shift in the bend frequency
f_{\rm b}
. For the dim state, we obtain

f_{\rm b}=8.32\times 10^{-6}\,[5.25\times 10^{-6},\,1.32\times 10^{-5}]~\mathrm{Hz},

whereas for the bright state we find

f_{\rm b}=8.87\times 10^{-7}\,[4.44\times 10^{-7},\,1.41\times 10^{-6}]~\mathrm{Hz}.

The two values differ by approximately 0.97 dex, implying a substantially longer characteristic variability timescale,
t_{\rm b}=1/f_{\rm b}
, in the bright state.

The difference is not limited to the bend frequency itself. The best-supported high-frequency slope changes from
\alpha_{\rm high}=2.05\,[1.85,\,2.15]
in the dim state to
\alpha_{\rm high}=1.59\,[1.50,\,1.60]
in the bright state, indicating that the high-frequency PSD is steeper in the dim state and comparatively flatter in the bright state. The state transition therefore affects not only the characteristic timescale but also the structure of the high-frequency variability.

3.2
State separation in parameter space

Figure
4
shows that the dim- and bright-state joint-constraint contours occupy distinct regions in the
(\log f_{\rm b},\alpha_{\rm high})
plane in both the hard and soft bands. In the 2–10 keV band, the separation is driven primarily by the offset in
\log f_{\rm b}
, together with a clear shift in the best-supported
\alpha_{\rm high}
. The soft band shows the same overall behaviour, although the dim-state joint constraint is more sensitive to the adopted low-frequency slope. Figure
4
therefore shows that the dim and bright states correspond to clearly separated broadband PSD solutions on the adopted joint-constraint grid.

3.3
State evolution of the energy dependence

In the soft X-ray band (0.3–2 keV), the two states likewise exhibit a pronounced change in bend frequency. For the dim state, we obtain

f_{\rm b}=2.57\times 10^{-5}\,[2.04\times 10^{-5},\,3.39\times 10^{-5}]~\mathrm{Hz},

while for the bright state we find

f_{\rm b}=4.55\times 10^{-7}\,[3.87\times 10^{-7},\,4.87\times 10^{-7}]~\mathrm{Hz}.

These values differ by approximately 1.75 dex, showing that the soft band also exhibits strong state dependence.

At the same time, the relationship between the soft and hard bands differs between the two states. In the dim state, the soft-band bend frequency is higher than that in the hard band by about 0.49 dex, with only limited overlap between the corresponding weighted intervals. In the bright state, the two become much more similar, with substantial overlap between their weighted intervals. The best-supported high-frequency slope in the soft band also changes between states, from
\alpha_{\rm high}=1.38\,[1.28,\,1.43]
in the dim state to
\alpha_{\rm high}=1.68\,[1.65,\,1.68]
in the bright state.

To quantify the state dependence of the soft–hard timing separation, we define

\Delta\log f_{\rm b}=\log f_{{\rm b,soft}}-\log f_{{\rm b,hard}}

and compare its joint-weight distributions between states. Under our empirical joint-weighting scheme, more than 99.9 per cent of the normalized joint weight supports a smaller soft–hard bend-frequency separation in the bright state than in the dim state, confirming that the apparent convergence in the bright state is robust within the adopted framework. The energy dependence of the variability is therefore itself state-dependent: the broadband PSD evolution of NGC 1566 involves not only an overall shift in characteristic timescale, but also a reconfiguration of the relative soft–hard timing behaviour.

4
Discussion and conclusions

The main result of this work is that the broadband X-ray variability of NGC 1566 evolves with state in both its characteristic timescale and its energy-dependent timing structure. In the dim state, the bend frequency in the soft band is clearly higher than that in the hard band, whereas in the bright state the two become much more similar, with the soft-band value slightly lower than the hard-band value. Quantitatively, under our empirical joint-weighting scheme, 99.99 per cent of the normalized joint weight supports a smaller soft–hard bend-frequency separation in the bright state than in the dim state. Within the framework of propagating fluctuations and coronal stratification
(
5
;
1
;
15
, e.g.)
, this is consistent with the interpretation that the regions dominating the soft and hard X-ray variability are more strongly separated in the dim state, while the bright state shows tighter timing coupling between the two bands.

The transition from the dim to the bright state is also accompanied by a shift of the bend frequency towards lower frequencies, implying a longer characteristic X-ray variability timescale in the bright state. This evolution is not a simple uniform translation of the PSD, but occurs together with a change in the relative soft–hard behaviour. The changing-look transition in NGC 1566 is therefore consistent with a broader reorganisation of the inner variability structure, in line with changing-state interpretations of CL AGN behaviour
(
8
, e.g.)
.

This timing picture is highly consistent with the existing spectral view of NGC 1566
(
9
;
10
;
3
;
14
)
. Previous broadband studies have shown that, near the peak of the 2018 outburst, the source displayed the characteristic X-ray spectrum of a Seyfert 1 nucleus and that its dramatic evolution was most plausibly driven by intrinsic accretion-flow changes rather than by simple obscuration or a tidal disruption event
(
10
)
. Subsequent work further showed that both the soft excess and the overall broadband continuum changed substantially across source states
(
3
)
, while multi-epoch analyses found systematic evolution in the disc, soft-excess, and coronal properties between the bright and dim states
(
14
)
. The broadband PSD results presented here provide an independent timing counterpart to this picture: the changing-look behaviour of NGC 1566 is not a simple rescaling of luminosity, but is accompanied by coupled evolution in characteristic variability timescale, high-frequency variability structure, and inter-band timing behaviour. NGC 1566 therefore provides a rare case in which state-dependent evolution of the inner radiative structure can be investigated consistently through both spectral and timing diagnostics.
