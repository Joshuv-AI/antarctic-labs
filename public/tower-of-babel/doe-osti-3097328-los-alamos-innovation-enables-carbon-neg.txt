Los Alamos Innovation Enables Carbon-Negative Infrastructure Through an AI Infrastructure
Partnership

Subheading: LANL-developed material licensed by Spiritus powers new collaboration to integrate
carbon management into data infrastructure

A material innovation first developed at Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) is now enabling a
major step forward in carbon management. Licensed by Spiritus, the technology is being deployed in a
$500 million partnership with Prometheus Hyperscale and Casper Carbon Capture to build one of the
largest carbon-negative digital infrastructure projects in the world.

Located in Casper, Wyoming, the initiative directly integrates permanent carbon removal with advanced
computing systems, illustrating how Los Alamos research continues to advance national goals in energy,
security, and innovation.

From Laboratory Discovery to Application
Researchers at Los Alamos developed a porous sorbent material capable of capturing carbon dioxide from
air through a low-energy process. That discovery became the foundation for Spiritus’ Carbon Orchard™
system, a modular, passive capture approach that mimics natural airflow and minimizes energy use.

The design arranges carbon capture modules in an orchard-like configuration to maximize exposure to air
while reducing operational costs. Combined with an advanced desorption process, the system
significantly improves the efficiency and scalability of direct air capture technologies.

Integrating Carbon Capture into AI Infrastructure
Spiritus’ technology will operate at the center of a new carbon-negative data campus developed by
Prometheus Hyperscale and Casper Carbon Capture. The project unites three key components:

 • Data Infrastructure: High-density compute halls for artificial intelligence workloads, designed
 by Prometheus Hyperscale using proprietary, non-consumptive cooling systems.
 • Energy Systems: Spiritus’ Orchard Power™ platform, providing up to 1.5 gigawatts of on-site
 firm power with integrated point-source and direct air capture for carbon-negative operations.
 • Carbon Sequestration: Casper Carbon Capture’s permitted wells in Natrona and Converse
 Counties for permanent CO₂ storage.

Together, these systems will demonstrate how carbon management can be embedded directly into
mission-critical energy and digital infrastructure. When fully operational, the project, known as Orchard
One, is expected to capture up to one megaton of carbon dioxide annually.

Advancing Science and Mission
This collaboration showcases how Los Alamos technologies transition from fundamental research to
commercial application through technology transfer and industry partnerships. It supports Department of
Energy and National Nuclear Security Administration objectives to advance energy security, carbon
management, and scientific innovation.

Spiritus maintains strong ties to northern New Mexico, where many of its engineers and scientists are
based. The company is also developing educational partnerships to expand technical training in carbon
management and strengthen the regional workforce.
