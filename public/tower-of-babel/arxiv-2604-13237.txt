Dynamical Casimir effect in the worldline formulation
C. D. Fosco

Abstract
We evaluate the effective action for the Dynamical Casimir Effect (DCE) for a real scalar field in d + 1 d+1 dimensions within the worldline formulation of quantum field theory. The scalar field is coupled to a spacetime-dependent mass term, which here plays the role of the moving medium and imposes imperfect boundary conditions on time-dependent surfaces. Expanding in powers of the departure of the geometry from a planar configuration, the worldline path integral factorizes into simpler, lower-dimensional ones. In the limit of a strong coupling to the surface, we recover the Dirichlet result and derive the systematic corrections in inverse powers of the coupling. Finally, we also apply the method to a two-surface configuration.



1
Introduction

The Dynamical Casimir Effect (DCE) refers to the production of particles from
the vacuum, due to a time dependence of the boundary conditions or even of
background fields
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
While traditionally approached via canonical or functional integral
quantization, the worldline formalism offers a powerful alternative.
This has indeed been the case for the static Casimir effect, where in the approach popularized by Gies et al.
[
4
]
the interaction with the medium is introduced through a background potential. This, in turn, allows for the
evaluation of the one-loop effective action in the context of the
worldline
[
5
]
.
Since the background potentials are assumed there to be time independent, the corresponding effective action is proportional to the Casimir energy. We show here how the worldline approach may also be useful for the analysis of
the DCE. In particular, it allows us to evaluate the effective action as a
measure of the amplitude for the creation of field quanta out of the vacuum.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
we define the system under consideration and introduce the notation and conventions. In Sect.
3
we apply the worldline formalism to the effective action, develop its perturbative expansion, and analyze its dependence on
\lambda
, the coupling to the moving medium. We also identify the general structure of the higher-order terms.

Sect.
4
extends the formalism to a two-surface
configuration. Finally, Sect.
5
presents our conclusions.

2
The model

We consider a real scalar field
\varphi(x)
in
D=d+1
dimensions,
coupled to dynamical media, in such a way that its Euclidean action
{\mathcal{S}}(\varphi,V)
may be written, after an integration by parts,
as follows:

{\mathcal{S}}(\varphi,V)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\int\mathrm{d}^{D}x\,\varphi(x)\big(-\partial^{2}+V\big)\,\varphi(x)\;,

(1)

where
x
has been used to denote the
d+1
spacetime coordinates.
We use the notation
x\equiv(x_{\parallel},x_{d})
,
x_{\parallel}\equiv(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1})
being the spacetime coordinates which
parametrize the world-volume of the dynamical medium. Space–time indices are
denoted by letters from the middle of the Greek
alphabet (
\mu,\nu,\ldots
), taking values
0,1,\ldots,d
, with
0

reserved for the imaginary time (natural units
are used:
\hbar\equiv 1
and
c\equiv 1
). The metric reduces to
the
(d+1)\times(d+1)
identity matrix, so no distinction is implied by
placing an index upstairs or downstairs. Unless stated otherwise, we also adopt
the Einstein summation convention over repeated indices in monomial expressions.

The role of the potential is straightforward: it suppresses the scalar field wherever it becomes large and positive, and in the strong-coupling limit it enforces Dirichlet boundary conditions. This occurs even though the form of the action places the system within the regime of linear response. Moreover, when more than one object is coupled to the vacuum field
\varphi
, the full potential
V
is obtained by adding the corresponding contributions from each object, as dictated by the assumed linear response of the media. The additivity of potentials does not, however, imply any linear superposition principle for the physical effects: energies, probabilities, and related observables are not obtained by summing the contributions of the individual parts of the media, but are instead nontrivial functionals of
V
.

The potential
V(x)
, for the case of a single surface
\Sigma
, is
assumed to have the form

V(x)\;=\;v\big[F(x)\big]\;\;,\;\;\;F(x)\,\equiv\,x_{d}-\psi(x_{\parallel})

(2)

where
\Sigma
is defined by
x_{d}=\psi(x_{\parallel})
, and
\psi
is
treated as a small perturbation around a flat configuration. On the
other hand,
v
is a real non-negative function of a single variable, highly
concentrated around
0
. In fact, the first example we consider
corresponds to a
\delta
-function (zero-width surface). We find it convenient,
at this point, to mention that the choice of
F
is of course not unique.
Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with
\nabla F\neq 0
on the level set
\Sigma=F^{-1}(0)
, which defines
\Sigma
.
By the coarea formula,

\delta(F(x))=\frac{1}{|\nabla F(x)|}\,\delta_{\Sigma}(x),

(3)

where
\delta_{\Sigma}
denotes the delta distribution intrinsic to
\Sigma
. It
follows that the combination
|\nabla F(x)|\,\delta(F(x))
defines a measure on

\Sigma
that is manifestly independent of the choice of defining function
F
.
For the Monge parametrization
F(x)=x_{d}-\psi(x_{\parallel})
, one has

|\nabla F|=\sqrt{1+(\nabla_{\parallel}\psi)^{2}}=1+O(\psi^{2})
,
so the discrepancy arising from the
|\nabla F|
factor enters only at
second order in derivatives of
\psi
. Moreover, as explained
in
[
7
]
, this factor contributes only to the real
(non-dissipative) part of the effective action and is therefore
irrelevant for the evaluation of pair-creation effects.

3
Effective action

The one-loop effective action
\Gamma(V)
is defined in terms of the
vacuum-to-vacuum Euclidean transition amplitude,
{\mathcal{Z}}
, via

e^{-\Gamma(V)}\,\equiv\,\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}(V)}{{\mathcal{Z}}(0)}\;,\;\;\;{\mathcal{Z}}(V)\,\equiv\,\int{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\,e^{-{\mathcal{S}}(\varphi,V)}\;,

(4)

where the
{\mathcal{Z}}(0)
factor has been introduced in order to subtract the
usual zero-point contribution of free space (namely, in the absence of any
object). Note, however, that we perform further subtractions to the effective
action, corresponding to terms that do not contribute to pair creation, which is the main focus of this work.

Since the functional integrals are Gaussian, we can safely apply the worldline
approach
[
4
,
5
]
, whereby we may write

\Gamma(V)=-\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int\mathrm{d}^{d+1}x\left[\langle e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau V(x(\tau))}\rangle_{x}-1\right]

(5)

where

V(x(\tau))\;\equiv\;v\!\big(x_{d}(\tau)-\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau))\big)\;,

(6)

and
\langle\dots\rangle_{x}
denotes a functional average over closed loops

x_{\mu}(\tau)
starting and ending at the same point,
x_{\mu}(0)=x_{\mu}(T)\equiv x
:

\langle\ldots\rangle_{x}\;\equiv\;\frac{\int^{x(T)=x}_{x(0)=x}{\mathcal{D}}x\ldots e^{-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\dot{x}_{\mu}(\tau)\dot{x}_{\mu}(\tau)}}{\int^{x(T)=x}_{x(0)=x}{\mathcal{D}}xe^{-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\dot{x}_{\mu}(\tau)\dot{x}_{\mu}(\tau)}}

(7)

Expression (
5
) for the effective action, when rotated back to
Minkowski spacetime in its dependence on
\psi
, will allow us to extract the
dissipative effects due to pair creation from the evaluation of its imaginary
part.

3.1
Perturbative Expansion

Let us expand the potential in powers of
\psi

V(x)\,\equiv\,v(x_{d}-\psi(x_{\parallel}))=v(x_{d})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}v^{(n)}(x_{d})\,\Big(\psi(x_{\parallel})\Big)^{n}\;,

(8)

and introduce it into the worldline, to get the respective expansion for the
effective action:

\Gamma(V)=\Gamma_{0}+\Gamma_{1}(\psi)+\Gamma_{2}(\psi)+\dots\;.

(9)

A key point is that, in the perturbative expansion above, all required worldline path integrals factorize into parallel and perpendicular averages. Let us display this explicitly for the first few terms.

At zeroth order one obtains

\Gamma_{0}\;=\;-\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int\mathrm{d}^{d+1}x\left[\langle e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,v(x_{d}(\tau))}\rangle_{x_{d}}-1\right]\;,

(10)

with

\langle\ldots\rangle_{x_{d}}\;\equiv\;\frac{\int^{x_{d}(T)=x_{d}}_{x_{d}(0)=x_{d}}{\mathcal{D}}x_{d}\ldots e^{-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\big(\dot{x}_{d}(\tau)\big)^{2}}}{\int^{x_{d}(T)=x_{d}}_{x_{d}(0)=x_{d}}{\mathcal{D}}x_{d}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\big(\dot{x}_{d}(\tau)\big)^{2}}}\;.

(11)

It is evident from (
10
) that this contribution to the effective action is divergent, since it is proportional to the spacetime volume swept by the surface over an infinite time interval. We therefore introduce a spacetime box with total evolution time
\mathcal{T}
and a
(d-1)
-dimensional “area”
L^{d-1}
for the flat surface. It is then natural to evaluate

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
, the vacuum energy per unit area, in the limit when the size
of the box tends to infinity:

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\equiv\Big[\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{\mathcal{T}\,L^{d-1}}\Big]_{\mathcal{T},\,L\to\infty}\;=\;-\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\left[\langle e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,v(x_{d}(\tau))}\rangle_{x_{d}}-1\right]\;,

(12)

which, depending on
d
and
v
, is usually divergent in the UV. On the other
hand, note that it does not contribute to the imaginary part of the effective
action.

We now consider the first-order term, which after standard manipulations may be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(\psi)\;=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big[\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\times\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\langle v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau))e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}v(x_{d}(\tau^{\prime}))}\rangle_{x_{d}}\Big]\;.

(13)

To tackle this term, it is convenient to deal first with the factor
involving a functional average of
\psi
. In terms of the Fourier transform of

\psi
,
\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\parallel})\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\,e^{-ik_{\parallel}\cdot x_{\parallel}}\psi(x_{\parallel})
, and
taking into account the fact that the worldline correlation function is:

\langle x_{\mu}(\tau_{1})x_{\nu}(\tau_{2})\rangle\;=\;\delta_{\mu\nu}\,\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\;,\;\;\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}{T}-|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|\;,

(14)

we find:

\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}=\widetilde{\psi}(0)=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\psi(x_{\parallel})\;.

(15)

This term may therefore be set to zero by a shift of origin, chosen so that the mean value of
\psi
vanishes. We assume that such a choice has been made, and therefore
\Gamma_{1}(\psi)=0
.

For the second-order term
\Gamma_{2}(\psi)
one finds a natural decomposition into two contributions,
\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{2,1}+\Gamma_{2,2}
, namely

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2,1}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\,\langle v^{\prime\prime}(x_{d}(\tau))\rangle_{x_{d}}\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\,\langle\big(\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau))\big)^{2}\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2,2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\,\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{1}))\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{2}))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\,\langle v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{1}))v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{2}))e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,v(x_{d}(\tau))}\rangle_{x_{d}}\;.

(16)

In both terms we need the average of two
\psi
insertions, one with coincident proper-time arguments. Using Fourier transforms once again, we obtain

\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{1}))\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{2}))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}\,=\,\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{\parallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\parallel})\big|^{2}e^{\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})k_{\parallel}^{2}}\;.

(17)

It then follows that
\Gamma_{2,1}
, independently of the detailed form of
v
, yields a term that cannot generate an imaginary part in the effective action; it may be interpreted as a mass term for
\psi
:

\Gamma_{2,1}(\psi)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\big(\psi(x_{\parallel})\big)^{2}\;,

(18)

with

m^{2}\,=\,\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\langle v^{\prime\prime}(x_{d}(\tau))\rangle_{x_{d}}\;.

(19)

For the evaluation of
\Gamma_{2,2}(\psi)
, we need to be more explicit about
the form of
v
. We first assume that
v(x_{d})=\lambda\delta(x_{d})
.
Inserting the explicit form of the worldline average into (
3.1
) and using the normalization of the free path integral, the relevant object may be written in terms of the propagator of a one-dimensional system:

\displaystyle I(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)\,\equiv\,\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\langle v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{1}))v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{2}))e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,v(x_{d}(\tau))}\rangle_{x_{d}}\;=\;(4\pi T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,

\displaystyle\times\int_{x_{d}(T)=x_{d}(0)}\mathcal{D}x_{d}\,v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{1}))v^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{2}))e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\big(\frac{1}{4}{\dot{x}_{d}}^{2}(\tau)+\lambda\delta(x_{d}(\tau))\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\;,

(20)

so that
\Gamma_{2,2}
becomes

\Gamma_{2,2}(\psi)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{\parallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\gamma(k_{\parallel})\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\parallel})\big|^{2}

(21)

with

\gamma(k_{\parallel})\,=\,-\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}I(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)\,e^{\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})k_{\parallel}^{2}}\;.

(22)

The value of
\gamma
at zero momentum corresponds to a redefinition of
m^{2}
. We shall use this fact to control the UV behavior of the integrals defining
\gamma
.

The path integral (
3.1
) may be written in terms of
K
, the
propagator in the presence of a
\delta
potential
[
6
]
, as follows:

\displaystyle I(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)\;=\;(4\pi T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{2}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}x_{d}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}y_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}z_{d}\;\big[K_{a}(x_{d},y_{d})\,\delta^{\prime}(y_{d})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\,K_{b}(y_{d},z_{d})\,\delta^{\prime}(z_{d})\;K_{c}(z_{d},x_{d})\big]

(23)

where

K_{a}\equiv K(T_{a};\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,)\,,\qquad K_{b}\equiv K(T_{b};\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,)\,,\qquad K_{c}\equiv K(T_{c};\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,)\,,

(24)

with proper-time intervals

T_{a}=T-\tau_{1}\,,\qquad T_{b}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\,,\qquad T_{c}=\tau_{2}\,,\qquad T_{a}+T_{c}=T-T_{b}\,

(25)

and

\displaystyle K(t;\,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\,e^{-(x-y)^{2}/(4t)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\exp\!\Bigl[\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\bigl(|x|+|y|\bigr)+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\,t\Bigr]\;\operatorname{erfc}\!\biggl[\frac{|x|+|y|+\lambda\,t}{2\sqrt{t}}\biggr],

(26)

where
t=\tau^{\prime\prime}-\tau^{\prime}
denotes the elapsed proper time.

Using
\int\!f(x)\,\delta^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-f^{\prime}(0)
twice in succession,
the three spatial integrals reduce to four terms involving the
middle kernel
K_{b}
and its derivatives evaluated at the origin:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=(4\pi T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{2}\!\int\!\mathrm{d}x_{d}\Bigl[\partial_{2}K_{a}(x_{d},0)\,\partial_{2}K_{b}(0,0)\,K_{c}(0,x_{d})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+K_{a}(x_{d},0)\,\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K_{b}(0,0)\,K_{c}(0,x_{d})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{2}K_{a}(x_{d},0)\,K_{b}(0,0)\,\partial_{1}K_{c}(0,x_{d})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+K_{a}(x_{d},0)\,\partial_{1}K_{b}(0,0)\,\partial_{1}K_{c}(0,x_{d})\Bigr].

(27)

where
\partial_{1},\partial_{2}
denote derivatives with respect to the first and
second spatial arguments.

Evaluating the derivatives of
K
at the origin using (
3.1
),
performing the
x_{d}
integral via the semigroup property of the heat kernel,
and combining the four terms, we find:

I(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)\;=\;\frac{\lambda^{2}\sqrt{T}}{2}\left[\frac{K(T-T_{b};\,0,0)}{T_{b}^{3/2}}\,+\,\frac{K(T_{b};\,0,0)}{(T-T_{b})^{3/2}}\right]

(28)

where
T_{b}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
. The expression is manifestly symmetric under

T_{b}\leftrightarrow T-T_{b}
.

Consider now the large-
\lambda
expansion of
I
. Introducing

S\equiv T-T_{b}
:

I(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{4\pi}\,(T_{b}\,S)^{3/2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\,(2n+1)!!\,2^{n}}{\lambda^{2n}}\left[\frac{1}{S^{n}}+\frac{1}{T_{b}^{n}}\right]\;

(29)

of which the Dirichlet limit (
\lambda\to\infty
) is:

I_{\infty}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};T)=\frac{\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\bigl[T_{b}(T-T_{b})\bigr]^{3/2}}\;.

(30)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{2\pi(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\frac{e^{[\frac{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}{T}-|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|]k_{\parallel}^{2}}}{[(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})(T-\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})]^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{2\pi(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\frac{e^{[\frac{\tau^{2}}{T}-|\tau|]k_{\parallel}^{2}}}{\tau^{3/2}(T-\tau)^{1/2}}\;.

(31)

Subtracting its value at
k_{\parallel}=0
:

\gamma_{D}(k_{\parallel})\equiv\gamma_{\infty}(k_{\parallel})-\gamma_{\infty}(0)\,=\,-\frac{1}{2\pi(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\frac{e^{[\frac{\tau^{2}}{T}-|\tau|]k_{\parallel}^{2}}-1}{\tau^{3/2}(T-\tau)^{1/2}}\;.

(32)

Under a change of variables:

\gamma_{D}(k_{\parallel})\,=\,-\frac{1}{2\pi(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}|k_{\parallel}|^{d+2}\,\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+4}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\frac{e^{T(\tau^{2}-\tau)}-1}{\tau^{3/2}(1-\tau)^{1/2}}\;.

(33)

The double integral can be evaluated in closed form.
Setting
v=\tau(1-\tau)
, the proper-time integral is performed
by dimensional regularization:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{(d+4)/2}}\;\big(e^{-v\,T}-1\big)\;=\;v^{(d+2)/2}\;\Gamma\!\Big(\!-\tfrac{d+2}{2}\Big)\;,

(34)

where the subtraction is automatically handled by the analytic
continuation in
d
. The remaining
\tau
-integral yields a Beta function:

\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\;\frac{\big[\tau(1-\tau)\big]^{(d+2)/2}}{\tau^{3/2}\,(1-\tau)^{1/2}}\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\;\tau^{(d-1)/2}\,(1-\tau)^{(d+1)/2}\;=\;\frac{\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{d+1}{2}\big)\,\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{d+3}{2}\big)}{\Gamma(d+2)}\;.

(35)

Combining, one obtains the closed-form expression

\gamma_{D}(k_{\parallel})\;=\;-\frac{|k_{\parallel}|^{d+2}}{2\pi\,(4\pi)^{d/2}}\;\frac{\Gamma\!\big(\!-\tfrac{d+2}{2}\big)\;\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{d+1}{2}\big)\;\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{d+3}{2}\big)}{\Gamma(d+2)}\;,

(36)

which agrees with the result obtained in
Refs.
[
7
,
8
]
, where
the Dirichlet limit was treated as a starting point.
For odd
d=2q+1
, all Gamma functions are finite and the coefficient
simplifies to
(-1)^{q+1}(q!)^{2}/[(2\pi)^{q+1}(2q+1)!(2q+3)!!]

[cf. eq. (
39
) with
n=0
].
The first few explicit values are:

\gamma_{D}\;=\;\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6\pi}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{3}&(d=1)\,,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle+\frac{1}{360\pi^{2}}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{5}&(d=3)\,,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{25200\pi^{3}}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{7}&(d=5)\,.\end{cases}

(37)

3.2
Large-
\lambda
corrections to
\gamma

Let us now derive the corrections to
\gamma_{D}
that arise from keeping
subleading terms in the
1/\lambda
expansion of
I
.
Substituting the asymptotic expansion (
29
) into the expression for

\gamma
, and performing the center-of-mass integration over the double
proper-time integral (which produces a factor of
T-T_{b}
), we find that the

n
-th order contribution is:

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(k_{\parallel})\,=\,

\displaystyle-\frac{(-1)^{n}\,(2n+1)!!\,2^{n-1}}{\lambda^{2n}\,2\pi\,(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}T_{b}\;e^{\Delta(T_{b})\,k_{\parallel}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1}{T_{b}^{3/2}\,(T-T_{b})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\,+\,\frac{1}{T_{b}^{n+\frac{3}{2}}\,(T-T_{b})^{1/2}}\right]\;.

(38)

The subtracted form
\gamma_{n}^{\rm sub}\equiv\gamma_{n}(k_{\parallel})-\gamma_{n}(0)
, after rescaling
T_{b}=uT
and
T\to T/k_{\parallel}^{2}
,
reads:

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{\rm sub}(k_{\parallel})\,=\,\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\,(2n+1)!!\,2^{n-1}}{\lambda^{2n}\,2\pi\,(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\,|k_{\parallel}|^{d+2n+2}\;{\mathcal{C}}_{n}(d)\;,

(39)

where

{\mathcal{C}}_{n}(d)\,=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2n+4}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\;\frac{e^{-u(1-u)\,T}-1}{u^{3/2}\,(1-u)^{1/2}}\left[\frac{1}{(1-u)^{n}}+\frac{1}{u^{n}}\right]\;.

(40)

The
T
integral is evaluated by dimensional regularization,

\int_{0}^{\infty}T^{-\alpha}\,e^{-vT}\,\mathrm{d}T=v^{\alpha-1}\,\Gamma(1-\alpha)
,
and the resulting
u
-integral factorizes into Beta functions. One obtains:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{n}(d)\;=\;\frac{\Gamma\!\big(\!-\tfrac{d+2n+2}{2}\big)}{\Gamma(d+n+2)}\left[\Gamma\!\Big(\tfrac{d+2n+1}{2}\Big)\,\Gamma\!\Big(\tfrac{d+3}{2}\Big)+\Gamma\!\Big(\tfrac{d+1}{2}\Big)\,\Gamma\!\Big(\tfrac{d+2n+3}{2}\Big)\right]\;.

(41)

The leading (Dirichlet,
n=0
) contribution recovers
\gamma_{D}
. For
d
odd,
the Gamma functions are finite for all
n
.
For instance, setting
d=2q+1
:

\gamma_{0}^{\rm sub}\,=\,\frac{(-1)^{q+1}(q!)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{q+1}(2q+1)!(2q+3)!!}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{2q+3}\;,

(42)

in agreement with Ref.
[
7
]
, noting that
\gamma
as defined
here is twice the coefficient appearing in that reference, since

\Gamma_{2,2}=\frac{1}{2}\int\gamma\,|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}
.
The first two corrections, for
d=1
, read:

\gamma_{1}^{\rm sub}\,=\,-\frac{1}{5\pi\,\lambda^{2}}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{5}\;,\qquad\gamma_{2}^{\rm sub}\,=\,-\frac{8}{21\pi\,\lambda^{4}}\;|k_{\parallel}|^{7}\;.

(43)

3.3
Exact form factor at finite coupling

In this subsection we evaluate (
22
) using (
28
),
obtaining the exact form factor
\gamma(k_{\parallel})
valid for arbitrary
coupling strength
\lambda
. Substituting the full propagator (
3.1
)
into (
28
) and then into (
22
), we arrive at one of
the central results of this work:

\displaystyle\gamma(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{(d+1)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\,e^{k_{\parallel}^{2}[\frac{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}{T}-(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})]}\sqrt{T}\times

(44)

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\big(\frac{1}{(T-T_{b})^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{b}^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{(T-T_{b})^{3/2}T_{b}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{4}\big(\frac{e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(T-T_{b})}}{T_{b}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{T-T_{b}}\;)+\frac{e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(T_{b})}}{(T-T_{b})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{T_{b}}\;)\big)\Big]\,

We decompose this expression as
\gamma(k_{\parallel})=\gamma_{1}(k_{\parallel})+\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})
, where
\gamma_{1}
collects the free-propagator contribution and
\gamma_{2}
contains the
\operatorname{erfc}
terms.

We begin with
\gamma_{1}(k_{\parallel})
. After integrating over
\tau_{1}
and renaming
T_{b}=\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{(d+1)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau(T-\tau)\,e^{k_{\parallel}^{2}[\frac{\tau^{2}}{T}-\tau]}

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\big(\frac{1}{(T-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{(T-\tau)^{3/2}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}}\big)\,,

which, after the change of variable
\tau=T\mu
, leaves us with

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\mu(1-\mu)\,e^{-Tk_{\parallel}^{2}\mu(1-\mu)}

(46)

\displaystyle\big(\frac{(1-\mu)^{1/2}}{\mu^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{(1-\mu)^{1/2}\mu^{1/2}}\big)

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}(k_{\parallel}^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{d}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d+2}{2}}\Gamma(d)}\,.

(47)

Equation (
46
) is well defined for all odd
d>1
, while for even
d
it must be understood through dimensional regularization, as expected.

For
\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})
one finds

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}}{8(4\pi)^{(d+1)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau(T-\tau)\,e^{k_{\parallel}^{2}[\frac{\tau^{2}}{T}-\tau]}

(48)

\displaystyle\big(\frac{e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(T-\tau)}}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{T-\tau}\;)+\frac{e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(\tau)}}{(T-\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{\tau}\;)\big)\,,

where we use
\operatorname{erfc}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}dx
, and we do the change of variables
\tau=\mu T
, and for the
\operatorname{erfc}
expressions we use
x=\sqrt{T}y
and
x=\sqrt{T}z
, leading to

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2(4\pi)^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\,T\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\mu\;e^{-k_{\parallel}^{2}T\mu(1-\mu)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg[\frac{T(1-\mu)}{T^{\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}T(1-\mu)}\sqrt{T}\int_{y_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}e^{-Ty^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}T\mu}\sqrt{T}\int_{z_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}e^{-Tz^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigg]\,,

(49)

where
y_{\text{min}}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{1-\mu}
and
z_{\text{min}}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{\mu}
.

Using the symmetry under
\mu\to(1-\mu)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2(4\pi)^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu^{3/2}}\;e^{-T\left[k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu(1-\mu)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(1-\mu)\right]}\int_{y_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;e^{-Ty^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}\Gamma(1-\frac{d}{2})}{2(4\pi)^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}\eta(k_{\parallel}^{2},\mu,\lambda)\,,

(50)

where we integrated
T
with analytic continuation, and we define

\displaystyle\eta(k_{\parallel}^{2},\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,[k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu(1-\mu)+y^{2}-y_{\text{min}}^{2}]^{\frac{d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,\big(k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu(1-\mu)+t\big)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\big(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(1-\mu)+t\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(51)

after the change of variables
t=y^{2}-y_{\text{min}}^{2}
.

The integral (
3.3
) is of the form covered by formula 3.197.1 of Ref.
[
9
]
:

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu-1}(x+a)^{-\mu}(x+b)^{-\rho}\,dx=a^{-\mu}\,b^{\nu-\rho}\,B(\nu,\mu-\nu+\rho)\;{}_{2}F_{1}\!\big[\mu,\nu;\mu+\rho;1-\tfrac{b}{a}\big]\,,

(52)

valid for
\mathrm{Re}\,\nu>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(\mu+\rho-\nu)>0
.

Identifying
x=t
,
a=A\equiv k_{\parallel}^{2}\,\mu(1-\mu)
,

b=B\equiv\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(1-\mu)
, with exponents

\nu=1
,
\mu=1-\tfrac{d}{2}
,
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}
,
the convergence condition
\mathrm{Re}(\mu+\rho-\nu)=\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2}>0

restricts us to
d<1
; for
d\geq 1
the result is defined by analytic continuation in
d
, as is standard in dimensional regularization.
With these identifications, and using the simplification

B(1,\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2})=\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2})/\Gamma(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2})=({\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2}})^{-1}
,
we obtain:

\eta(k_{\parallel}^{2},\mu,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{2(1-d)}\;\big[k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu(1-\mu)\big]^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\sqrt{1-\mu}\;\;{}_{2}F_{1}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{d}{2},\,1;\;\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2};\;1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu}\Big)\,.

(53)

We have verified this expression numerically against direct quadrature of
(
3.3
) for non-integer values of
d
and a range of parameters

k_{\parallel}^{2}
,
\mu
,
\lambda
, finding agreement to all significant digits.

An alternative representation, useful in the regime

\lambda\gg|k_{\parallel}|
, follows from Pfaff’s transformation

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=(1-z)^{-a}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,c-b;c;\tfrac{z}{z-1})
:

\eta=\frac{\lambda^{d-1}}{2^{d}\,(1-d)}\;\frac{(1-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu}\;\;{}_{2}F_{1}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{d}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2};\;\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2};\;1-\frac{4k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)\,.

(54)

In this form the argument of the hypergeometric function is

1-4k_{\parallel}^{2}\mu/\lambda^{2}
, which lies close to
1
when
\lambda
is large, making it well suited for expansions in that regime.

Substituting (
53
) into the expression for
\gamma_{2}
,
we arrive at

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{4}\,\Gamma(1-\frac{d}{2})}{8\,(4\pi)^{\frac{d+2}{2}}}\,(k_{\parallel}^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\mu\;\mu^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{5}{2}}\,(1-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\;{}_{2}F_{1}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{d}{2},\,1;\;\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2};\;1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4k_{\parallel}^{2}\,\mu}\Big)\,,

(55)

where we have used
\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2})/(1-d)=\tfrac{1}{4}

to simplify the overall prefactor.

The integral (
3.3
) requires a subtraction at

k_{\parallel}=0
to render it UV-finite, just as in the Dirichlet case. Such subtraction renders the sum
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
finite, canceling the
d=1
pole in (
46
) and the odd
d\geq 3
poles arising from the negative integer argument in
{}_{2}F_{1}
(the parameters satisfy
c=\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2}
,
which vanishes at
d=3
and becomes a negative integer for odd
d
greater than
3
).

For even values of
d
(or more precisely, for
d
treated as a continuous
parameter via dimensional regularization), both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are
individually well defined. In particular, for
d=2
, the hypergeometric function
reduces to a constant since
a=1-d/2=0
:

{}_{2}F_{1}(0,1;\tfrac{1}{2};z)=1
, and
\gamma_{2}
becomes a standard
Beta-function integral. Similarly, for
d=4
, the
{}_{2}F_{1}
terminates at
first order:
{}_{2}F_{1}(-1,1;-\tfrac{1}{2};z)=1+2z
, yielding a closed-form
expression for the
\mu
-integral.

For general
d
and arbitrary ratio
\zeta\equiv\lambda^{2}/(4k_{\parallel}^{2})
,
the
\mu
-integral in (
3.3
) is most efficiently
evaluated numerically. We have carried out this computation, obtaining the full

\lambda
-interpolation of the form factor
\gamma^{\rm sub}
between the
weak-coupling (
\lambda\to 0
) and Dirichlet (
\lambda\to\infty
) limits.
The results are displayed in Fig.
1
.

).

3.4
Higher-order terms

The third-order contribution to the effective action involves the term

\psi^{3}
from the Taylor expansion of
V
. After factorization, its
parallel part requires the worldline average of three
\psi
insertions:

\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\,\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{1}))\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{2}))\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{3}))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}\;.

(56)

After performing the
x_{\parallel}
integration and the Gaussian worldline
average, this evaluates to:

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;\widetilde{\psi}(k_{1})\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{2})\,\widetilde{\psi}(-k_{1}-k_{2})\;e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}\,\Delta(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})}\;.

(57)

However, the third-order terms in the expansion of
e^{-\int V}

involve an odd number of
v^{(n)}
insertions in the perpendicular
sector. For the
\delta
-potential case,
v(x_{d})=\lambda\,\delta(x_{d})
,
the perpendicular path integral is governed by a Hamiltonian that is
even under
x_{d}\to-x_{d}
. Therefore, the integrand for each perpendicular
average contains an odd power of
\delta^{\prime}(x_{d})
(which is odd under
reflection), rendering the integral zero. This is not, however, something which
is restricted to the
\delta
-potential case: we only need an even potential,
namely
v(-x_{d})=v(x_{d})
.

More explicitly, the three contributions at third order in
\psi
are:
(i) a single
v^{\prime\prime\prime}
insertion, (ii) a
v^{\prime\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}
cross term, and
(iii) a cubic
v^{\prime 3}
term. In all three cases, the total number of
derivatives of
v
acting on the perpendicular coordinate is odd.
Since
K(t;\,x,y)
depends on
|x|
and
|y|
(and is therefore even under

x_{d}\to-x_{d}
), and since the derivatives of odd order of the potential are odd
functions, each perpendicular integral vanishes. This argument extends to all
odd orders in
\psi
:

\Gamma_{2n+1}(\psi)\,=\,0\;,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots

(58)

for any even potential.

The fourth-order contribution
\Gamma_{4}(\psi)
receives five types of terms,
arising from the expansion of
\langle e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,V}\rangle

to fourth order in
\psi
.
We write them schematically as:

\Gamma_{4}\;=\;\Gamma_{4,1}+\Gamma_{4,2}+\Gamma_{4,3}+\Gamma_{4,4}+\Gamma_{4,5}\;,

(59)

where the subscripts indicate the partition of four
\psi
insertions into
groups associated with different
v^{(n)}
vertices:

(a)
\Gamma_{4,1}
: a single
v^{(4)}
insertion (
n=4
in the
Taylor expansion, one proper-time variable);

(b)
\Gamma_{4,2}
: a
v^{\prime\prime\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}
cross term (
n=3
and
n=1
,
two proper-time variables);

(c)
\Gamma_{4,3}
: a
(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}
term (
n=2
twice, two
proper-time variables);

(d)
\Gamma_{4,4}
: a
v^{\prime\prime}\cdot(v^{\prime})^{2}
term (
n=2
and two

n=1
insertions, three proper-time variables);

(e)
\Gamma_{4,5}
: a
(v^{\prime})^{4}
term (
n=1
four times, four
proper-time variables).

Each contribution factorizes into a parallel part (involving products of

\psi
in Fourier space) and a perpendicular part (a worldline average
of derivatives of
v
with the full
e^{-\int v}
insertion).

4
Two surfaces

In this section we analyze the case of two surfaces, one of them flat,
located at
x_{d}=a
(we leave the sign of
a
free), and one curved
surface located at
x_{d}=\psi(x_{\parallel})
. This setup leaves us
with
V(x)=\lambda_{1}\,\delta(x_{d}-\psi(x_{\parallel}))+\lambda_{2}\,\delta(x_{d}-a)
. The two-surface dynamical Casimir effect has been
studied in a functional-determinant framework for imperfect mirrors with
nonlocal couplings
[
10
,
11
]
; here we show how the
worldline approach naturally incorporates the second surface through
the two-
\delta
propagator.

Following the procedure of Sect.
3.1
, we focus on
the first term in the power expansion that can generate dissipative
effects, namely

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2,2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{4(4\pi)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg[\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x_{\parallel}\,\langle\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{1}))\psi(x_{\parallel}(\tau_{2}))\rangle_{x_{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\mathrm{d}x_{d}\,\langle\delta^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{1}))\delta^{\prime}(x_{d}(\tau_{2}))e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau\,V(x_{d}(\tau))}\rangle_{x_{d}}\Bigg]\,,

(60)

where
V(x_{d}(\tau))=\lambda_{1}\,\delta(x_{d})+\lambda_{2}\,\delta(x_{d}-a)
.

The difference between (
4
) and the
single-surface case (
3.1
) is captured entirely
by the difference between the Euclidean propagator for a particle in the
presence of one
\delta
-potential at the origin,
eq. (
3.1
), and the propagator
K^{(\delta_{2})}

corresponding to two
\delta
-potentials, one at the origin and one
at
x_{d}=a
. The latter may be represented via its resolvent as follows:

K^{(\delta_{2})}(t,x,y)=-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{2\pi}\,e^{-E\,t}\;G^{(\delta_{2})}(E,x,y)\;,

(61)

where, following
[
6
]
, the two-
\delta
Green function
is expressed in terms of the single-
\delta
one:

G^{(\delta_{2})}(E,x,y)=G^{(\delta_{1})}(E,x,y)-\frac{G^{(\delta_{1})}(E,x,a)\;G^{(\delta_{1})}(E,a,y)}{G^{(\delta_{1})}(E,a,a)-\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}}\;,

(62)

with

G^{(\delta_{1})}(E,x,y)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{-E}\,|x-y|}}{2\sqrt{-E}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\,e^{-\sqrt{-E}\,(|x|+|y|)}}{2\sqrt{-E}\bigl(2\sqrt{-E}-\lambda_{1}\bigr)}\;.

(63)

The inverse Laplace transform of
G^{(\delta_{1})}

via (
61
) reproduces the single-
\delta
kernel

K
of eq. (
3.1
) (with
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
).

We decompose the two-
\delta
propagator as

K^{(\delta_{2})}(t,x,y)\;=\;K(t,x,y)+\delta K(t,x,y)\;,

(64)

where the correction
\delta K
due to the second wall is

\displaystyle\delta K(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{2\pi}\,e^{-E\,t}\;\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2\sqrt{-E}\,(2\sqrt{-E}-\lambda_{1})}\;\frac{A(E,x)\,A(E,y)}{\mathcal{D}(E)}\;,

(65)

with

\displaystyle A(E,z)

\displaystyle=(2\sqrt{-E}-\lambda_{1})\,e^{-\sqrt{-E}\,|z-a|}+\lambda_{1}\,e^{-\sqrt{-E}\,(|z|+|a|)}\;,

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E)

\displaystyle=(2\sqrt{-E}-\lambda_{1})(2\sqrt{-E}-\lambda_{2})-\lambda_{1}\,\lambda_{2}\;e^{-2\sqrt{-E}\,|a|}\;.

(67)

Since the parallel sector of the worldline average is identical to the
single-surface case, the full result adopts the same quadratic form:

\Gamma_{2,2}(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{\parallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;\chi(k_{\parallel})\;|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\parallel})|^{2}\,,

(68)

with

\chi(k_{\parallel})=-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\;e^{k_{\parallel}^{2}\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}\;I^{(\delta_{2})}(T,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,.

(69)

Here
I^{(\delta_{2})}
is obtained by replacing

K\to K^{(\delta_{2})}
in the four-term structure of
eq. (
3.1
). The factor

(4\pi T)^{1/2}\lambda^{2}
that appears in the single-surface definition
of
I
(eq. (
3.1
)) is absorbed into the prefactor and
the power of
T
in eq. (
69
), exactly as in the passage
from eq. (
3.1
) to eq. (
22
). Keeping the
same definitions
T_{b}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
and
S=T-T_{b}
as before,
the result is:

\displaystyle I^{(\delta_{2})}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{b}(0,0)\;K^{(\delta_{2})}_{S}(0,0)\;+\;K^{(\delta_{2})}_{b}(0,0)\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{S}(0,0)

\displaystyle\;+\;\partial_{1}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{b}(0,0)\;\partial_{2}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{S}(0,0)\;+\;\partial_{2}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{b}(0,0)\;\partial_{1}K^{(\delta_{2})}_{S}(0,0)\;,

(70)

where
K^{(\delta_{2})}_{b}\equiv K^{(\delta_{2})}(T_{b};\,\cdot\,,\cdot)
and
similarly for
K^{(\delta_{2})}_{S}
.

Introducing (
64
) into (
4
), we write

I^{(\delta_{2})}=I^{(\delta_{1})}+\delta I
, where
I^{(\delta_{1})}
is
the single-surface contribution (setting
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
), and

\delta I
collects all terms involving at least one factor of
\delta K
.
Setting
\chi=\gamma+\delta\gamma
, we need only compute

\delta\gamma
:

\delta\gamma(k_{\parallel})=-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\;e^{k_{\parallel}^{2}\Delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}\;\delta I(T,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,.

(71)

We organize
\delta I=\delta I_{1}+\delta I_{2}
by the order in

\delta K
. The terms linear in
\delta K
are:

\displaystyle\delta I_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\delta K(T_{b};0,0)\;K(S;0,0)\;+\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K(T_{b};0,0)\;\delta K(S;0,0)

\displaystyle\;+\;\delta K(T_{b};0,0)\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K(S;0,0)\;+\;K(T_{b};0,0)\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\delta K(S;0,0)\,,

(72)

where we used the single-
\delta
identity

\partial_{i}K(t;\,0,0)=0
. The terms quadratic in
\delta K
are:

\displaystyle\delta I_{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\delta K(T_{b};0,0)\;\delta K(S;0,0)\;+\;\delta K(T_{b};0,0)\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\delta K(S;0,0)

\displaystyle\;+\;2\;\partial_{1}\delta K(T_{b};0,0)\;\partial_{1}\delta K(S;0,0)\;,

(73)

where the factor of
2
in the last term arises because
\delta K

is symmetric in its spatial arguments, so

\partial_{1}\delta K(t;\,0,0)=\partial_{2}\delta K(t;\,0,0)
, and the
two cross terms in (
4
) are equal.

To proceed, we need explicit expressions for
\delta K
and its
derivatives evaluated at
x=y=0
. The key simplification comes
from the function
A(E,z)
, which at
z=0
reduces to

A(E,0)\;=\;(2p-\lambda_{1})\,e^{-p|a|}+\lambda_{1}\,e^{-p|a|}\;=\;2p\,e^{-p|a|}\;,

(74)

where
p\equiv\sqrt{-E}
. For the derivative with respect to
z
,
we note that
\partial_{z}|z-a|\big|_{z=0}=-\operatorname{sgn}(a)
(well defined
for
a\neq 0
), while
\partial_{z}|z|\big|_{z=0}=0
in the
symmetric-derivative sense appropriate to
\int f\,\delta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x=-f^{\prime}(0)
. This gives

A^{\prime}(E,0)\;\equiv\;\partial_{z}A(E,z)\big|_{z=0}\;=\;p\,(2p-\lambda_{1})\,\operatorname{sgn}(a)\;e^{-p|a|}\;.

(75)

Since
\delta K(t;\,x,y)
factorizes as
A(E,x)\,A(E,y)
(times scalar
functions of
E
), the derivatives of
\delta K
at the origin are
determined by
A(E,0)
and
A^{\prime}(E,0)
alone. Substituting
(
74
)–(
75
) into the spectral
representation (
65
) and writing

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)\equiv\mathcal{D}(-p^{2})
, one obtains the three
independent building blocks:

\displaystyle\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{2\lambda_{2}\,p\;e^{-2p|a|}}{(2p-\lambda_{1})\,\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)}\;,

(76)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(a)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{\lambda_{2}\,p\;e^{-2p|a|}}{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)}\;,

(77)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{\lambda_{2}\,p\,(2p-\lambda_{1})\;e^{-2p|a|}}{2\,\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)}\;,

(78)

with

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)=(2p-\lambda_{1})(2p-\lambda_{2})-\lambda_{1}\,\lambda_{2}\;e^{-2p|a|}\;.

(79)

The integrals above are understood as spectral (inverse Laplace)
representations, valid up to possible bound-state contributions that
arise from the poles of
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}
. Such bound-state
terms contribute factors of the form
e^{-E_{b}t}
(with
E_{b}>0
), which
are exponentially localized in proper time and therefore do not affect the
UV (
T\to 0
) structure of
\delta\gamma
.

A useful identity is

\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,K(t;\,0,0)\;=\;\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}\,t^{3/2}}\;,

(80)

valid for all values of
\lambda_{1}
. This follows from the fact that,
at
x=y=0
, the symmetric derivative

\partial_{x}|x|\big|_{x=0}=0
kills the
\lambda
-dependent
correction in (
3.1
), leaving only the free-propagator
contribution.

Substituting the building blocks into
\delta I_{1}
and using
\partial_{i}K(t;\,0,0)=0
together with (
80
), the four terms
become

\displaystyle\delta I_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(T_{b})\;K(S)\;+\;\frac{\delta K(S)}{4\sqrt{\pi}\,T_{b}^{3/2}}\;+\;\frac{\delta K(T_{b})}{4\sqrt{\pi}\,S^{3/2}}\;+\;K(T_{b})\;\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(S)\;,

(81)

where we have suppressed the arguments
(0,0)
on all kernels.

For
\delta I_{2}
, which is quadratic in
\delta K
, all three terms
in (
4
) are retained as written. Since every factor of

\delta K
or its derivatives carries a suppression factor
e^{-2p|a|}

from the spectral integrals,
\delta I_{2}\sim e^{-4p|a|}
in the
large-
|a|
regime, while
\delta I_{1}\sim e^{-2p|a|}
. The leading
correction to
\delta\gamma
thus comes entirely from
\delta I_{1}
.

When
|a|
is large compared to the typical worldline scale

\sim 1/\sqrt{k_{\parallel}^{2}}
, the exponential factor
e^{-2p|a|}

in the spectral integrals suppresses
\delta K
and its derivatives. At
leading order, we expand
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}
by neglecting the
exponential term in (
79
):

\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(p)}\;\approx\;\frac{1}{(2p-\lambda_{1})(2p-\lambda_{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\,e^{-2p|a|}}{(2p-\lambda_{1})(2p-\lambda_{2})}\right]^{n}.

(82)

At leading (
n=0
) order, the mixed
derivative (
78
) simplifies by a cancellation of

(2p-\lambda_{1})
:

\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)\;\approx\;\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{\lambda_{2}\,p\;e^{-2p|a|}}{2\,(2p-\lambda_{2})}\;,

(83)

while
\delta K
and
\partial_{i}\delta K
retain both couplings:

\displaystyle\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{2\lambda_{2}\,p\;e^{-2p|a|}}{(2p-\lambda_{1})^{2}(2p-\lambda_{2})}\;,

(84)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(a)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{\lambda_{2}\,p\;e^{-2p|a|}}{(2p-\lambda_{1})(2p-\lambda_{2})}\;.

(85)

The correction to the form factor at this order is

\displaystyle\delta\gamma(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\mathrm{d}T_{b}\,(T-T_{b})\;e^{\Delta(T_{b})\,k_{\parallel}^{2}}\;\delta I_{1}(T_{b},\,T-T_{b})

\displaystyle\quad+\;O(e^{-4p|a|})\;,

(86)

where
\delta I_{1}
is given by (
81
) with the
leading-order spectral functions.

The physical content of (
4
) is that the second surface
modifies the dissipative form factor through an interference between
the single-wall propagator
K(t;\,0,0)
and the wall-to-wall correction

\delta K(t;\,0,0)
. This interference is exponentially suppressed
for large wall separations, reflecting the fact that the worldline must
traverse the distance
|a|
to “feel” the second surface, at a cost

\sim e^{-2p|a|}
in the spectral integral.

4.1
Dirichlet limit

In the limit
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\to\infty
at fixed
|a|
, the
building blocks exhibit a hierarchy:

\displaystyle K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\,t^{3/2}}\;,

\displaystyle\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\sim O(1/\lambda_{1}^{2})\;,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\sim O(1/\lambda_{1})\;,

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)

\displaystyle\to\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(t)\;,

(87)

where only the mixed derivative remains
O(1)
. Its limiting form is

\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(t)\;=\;-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;e^{-p^{2}t}\;\frac{p\,e^{-2p|a|}}{1-e^{-2p|a|}}\;.

(88)

The integrand admits a geometric-series expansion

\frac{p\,e^{-2p|a|}}{1-e^{-2p|a|}}\;=\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p\,e^{-2np|a|}\;,

(89)

interpretable as a sum over image charges at distances
2n|a|
. This is
the standard method-of-images result for the correction to the
Dirichlet propagator due to a second
wall
[
11
,
6
]
, providing an independent
verification of the two-surface formalism.

A further simplification occurs in
\delta I_{1}
: in the Dirichlet limit,

\delta K(t;\,0,0)
is proportional to

\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K(t;\,0,0)
with coefficient

4/\lambda_{1}^{2}
. Substituting into (
81
) and
factoring out
1/\lambda_{1}^{2}
, each of the four terms contributes with
the same
1/t^{3/2}
structure from either
K
or
\delta K
, yielding

\delta I_{1}^{\,\mathrm{Dir}}\;=\;\frac{2}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}\left[\frac{\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(T_{b})}{S^{3/2}}\;+\;\frac{\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(S)}{T_{b}^{3/2}}\right].

(90)

After multiplication by
\lambda_{1}^{2}
in the prefactor
of (
71
), the coupling cancels and the result is

\displaystyle\delta\gamma^{\,\mathrm{Dir}}(k_{\parallel})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{T^{(d+2)/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\mathrm{d}T_{b}\,(T-T_{b})\;e^{\Delta(T_{b})\,k_{\parallel}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\frac{\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(T_{b})}{S^{3/2}}+\frac{\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(S)}{T_{b}^{3/2}}\right].

(91)

We have verified numerically that the exact
\delta I_{1}
(with finite

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda
) converges to (
90
) as

\lambda\to\infty
, with the ratio approaching unity.

To obtain a more explicit expression, we substitute the image-charge
expansion (
89
) into (
4.1
) and rescale

T_{b}=u\,T
. The proper-time integral over
T
is then evaluated by
dimensional regularization (exactly as in the single-surface
derivation of Sect.
3.1
), with the
k_{\parallel}=0
subtraction handled automatically by analytic continuation. The result is

\delta\gamma^{\,\mathrm{Dir,sub}}(k_{\parallel})=\frac{\Gamma(-\tfrac{d}{2})}{2\pi^{3/2}(4\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;p\,e^{-2np|a|}\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}u\,(1-u)^{-1/2}\\
\times\Bigl[\bigl(p^{2}u\bigr)^{d/2}-\bigl(u(1-u)\,k_{\parallel}^{2}+p^{2}u\bigr)^{d/2}\Bigr]\\
+\frac{\Gamma(-\tfrac{d}{2})}{2\pi^{3/2}(4\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;p\,e^{-2np|a|}\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}u\,(1-u)\,u^{-3/2}\\
\times\Bigl[\bigl(p^{2}(1-u)\bigr)^{d/2}-\bigl(u(1-u)\,k_{\parallel}^{2}+p^{2}(1-u)\bigr)^{d/2}\Bigr].

(92)

For odd
d=2q+1
,
\Gamma(-d/2)
is finite and the
u
-integrals
can be evaluated in closed form. For
d=1
, both integrals reduce
to logarithms and inverse tangents, yielding:

\displaystyle\delta\gamma^{\,\mathrm{Dir,sub}}\big|_{d=1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}p\;p\,e^{-2np|a|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\frac{2|k_{\parallel}|}{p}\,\arctan\frac{|k_{\parallel}|}{p}-\log\!\left(1+\frac{k_{\parallel}^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\right].

(93)

The
p
-integral in (
4.1
) can be expressed in terms
of exponential integrals; in practice it is most efficiently
evaluated by direct quadrature. For
|k_{\parallel}|\ll 1/|a|
the
bracket behaves as
\sim k_{\parallel}^{2}/p^{2}
, and for

|k_{\parallel}|\gg 1/|a|
as

\sim\pi|k_{\parallel}|/p
, interpolating between a

k_{\parallel}^{2}
(non-dissipative) and a

|k_{\parallel}|^{3}
(dissipative) regime.
In particular, at large separation, the leading image (
n=1
) dominates
and the dissipative piece scales as

|k_{\parallel}|^{3}\,e^{-2|k_{\parallel}||a|}
,
exponentially suppressed relative to the single-surface result

\gamma_{D}=-|k_{\parallel}|^{3}/(6\pi)
. This, together with the
exact agreement of the single-surface Dirichlet limit
with the direct calculation of
Ref.
[
7
]
, provides an independent verification of the
two-surface formalism.

The Dirichlet-limit correction (
92
) has a
structure parallel to the single-surface result: comparing with the
Dirichlet form factor
\gamma_{D}
from (
36
), the factor

1/(4\sqrt{\pi}\,t^{3/2})
associated with the single-wall Dirichlet
propagator is replaced by
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\,\delta K^{\mathrm{Dir}}(t)
,
which encodes the effect of the second wall through the image-charge
sum (
89
). For large
|a|
,

\delta\gamma^{\,\mathrm{Dir}}
is exponentially suppressed, recovering
the single-surface Dirichlet result. In the opposite limit

|a|\to 0
, the image sum diverges and the perturbative expansion in

\delta K
breaks down, signaling the coalescence of the two surfaces.

5
Conclusions

We have developed a worldline approach to the Dynamical Casimir Effect for a
real scalar field coupled to a time-dependent surface modeled by a background
potential. The key advantage of this formulation is the natural factorization
of the worldline path integral into parallel and perpendicular sectors, which
reduces the computation of the effective action to one-dimensional quantum
mechanical problems.

For the case of a
\delta
-function potential of strength
\lambda
, we have
obtained an exact expression for the dissipative form factor

\gamma(k_{\parallel})
, valid for arbitrary coupling strength.
In the strong-coupling limit
\lambda\to\infty
, we recover the Dirichlet
result of Ref.
[
7
]
, and derive the systematic corrections in
inverse powers of
\lambda
: the
n
-th order contribution scales as

|k_{\parallel}|^{d+2n+2}/\lambda^{2n}
, with a closed-form coefficient

\mathcal{C}_{n}(d)
expressed through Gamma functions.

The full
\lambda
-dependent form factor has been obtained in closed form via
hypergeometric functions, by splitting
\gamma
into a free-propagator part

\gamma_{1}
and a
\delta
-potential correction
\gamma_{2}
. The latter is
expressed through a
{}_{2}F_{1}
function whose argument interpolates continuously
between the weak-coupling and Dirichlet regimes. We have verified the
cancellation of spurious poles between
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
at odd
spatial dimensions.

Using a parity argument based on the evenness of the perpendicular Hamiltonian
under
x_{d}\to-x_{d}
, we have shown that all odd-order contributions in

\psi
vanish identically for any even potential, and have classified the five
distinct types of terms arising at fourth order.

The formalism has been extended to a two-surface configuration, where the
second surface introduces corrections to the propagator that can be treated
perturbatively through the Green’s function of the two-
\delta

potential system. In the Dirichlet limit, the correction to the
form factor reduces to an image-charge sum, recovering the standard
method-of-images structure and connecting with the functional-determinant
results of Refs.
[
10
,
11
]
.

Several directions remain open. The explicit computation of the fourth-order term
\Gamma_{4}(\psi)
, together with the Bern-Kosower representation of the resulting amplitude, would provide a diagrammatic interpretation analogous to that of the string-inspired formalism and will be addressed elsewhere. Extensions to the electromagnetic field and to finite temperature are also natural next steps within this framework.
