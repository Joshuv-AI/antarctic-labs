Physiological and transcriptomic responses of sunflower to combined saline-alkali stress
Authors: Jiangna Zheng, Xinlong Gao, Haina Zhang, Dan Li, Suen Liu, Mengzhe Li, Junyi Geng, Baosheng Guo, Cunpeng Zhao, Kaihui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSunflower (Helianthus annuus L.), an important oilseed crop, is often used as a pioneer species for improving saline-alkali soils. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying sunflower seedling responses to combined saline-alkali stress remain unclear. This study aimed to elucidate the molecular basis of saline-alkali tolerance at the seedling stage by comparing physiological and transcriptomic responses between tolerant and sensitive sunflower hybrids.The saline-alkali tolerant hybrid K-27 and the sensitive hybrid K-7 were used as experimental materials. Root samples were collected at 0, 3, 12, 24, 48, and 96 h after exposure to combined saline-alkali stress (0.5% NaCl + Na2CO3, adjusted to pH 9.0). Physiological parameters, including antioxidant enzyme activities, osmolyte contents, ion concentrations, membrane damage levels, and cell wall components, were measured, followed by transcriptome sequencing analysis.ResultsPhenotypic analysis showed that the root length inhibition rate and fresh weight loss rate of K-27 were significantly lower than those of K-7, indicating stronger tolerance. Physiological analysis revealed that K-27 exhibited an inducible antioxidant enzyme response pattern. In addition, K-27 achieved osmotic adjustment through sustained proline accumulation (peaking at 12 h and remaining significantly higher than that of K-7 at 96 h) and exhibited higher basal levels of lignin and hemicellulose. Transcriptome analysis showed that the number of upregulated genes in K-27 was consistently higher than in K-7 at all time points, with 5,283 genes upregulated as early as 3 h after stress exposure. Venn analysis identified 44 core differentially expressed genes (cDEGs) shared between the two genotypes, which were mainly enriched in auxin biosynthesis regulation, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and glutathione metabolism. Among them, the benzoic acid carboxyl methyltransferase gene (BAMT) was continuously upregulated in K-27 but persistently downregulated in K-7. In addition, five other genes (encoding fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase, pectin methylesterase inhibitor, glutathione S-transferase, INPP5E, and HXXXD-type acyltransferase) exhibited significantly higher expression levels in K-27.Conclusion K-27 tolerates combined saline-alkali stress through coordinated multi-layered response mechanisms, including inducible antioxidant defense, maintenance of ion homeostasis, sustained osmotic adjustment, and activation of the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway. Candidate genes such as BAMT may provide potential targets for molecular breeding of saline-alkali tolerant sunflower, although their functions require further experimental validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-13039-5.

Introduction

Sunflower (Helianthus annuus L.) is one of the most important oilseed crops worldwide, with a high seed oil content of 40–50%. It also exhibits a certain level of salt tolerance and is often used as a pioneer crop for the improvement of saline-alkali soils [1–4]. The seedling stage is a critical period for sunflower growth and development and is also the stage most sensitive to environmental stress. The stress resistance mechanisms established during this stage directly affect subsequent growth and final yield [5]. However, existing studies on sunflower saline-alkali tolerance have mainly focused on single salt or alkali stress conditions. Although previous studies have established evaluation systems for salt tolerance in sunflower or improved salt tolerance through transgenic approaches [6, 7], and have analyzed alkaline tolerance networks from multi-omics perspectives [8], systematic investigations of molecular mechanisms underlying combined saline-alkali stress at the seedling stage remain limited, particularly studies involving dynamic monitoring across the full 0–96 h time course.

Previous studies have demonstrated that plant responses to combined stresses are unique and involve regulatory networks that are not activated under single stress conditions. Therefore, studies based on single-factor stress are insufficient to accurately reflect plant response mechanisms under natural environmental conditions [9]. Plants typically respond to combined saline-alkali stress through multi-level coordinated regulation, including physiological adjustments (ion homeostasis, osmotic regulation, and antioxidant defense) as well as signal transduction and transcriptional reprogramming at the molecular level [10]. The antioxidant enzyme system, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and catalase (CAT), plays a central role in scavenging stress-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) and maintaining redox homeostasis [11]. Meanwhile, maintaining a high K+/Na+ ratio and accumulating osmotic regulatory substances are important physiological bases for plant tolerance to saline-alkali stress. Although some stress resistance mechanisms of sunflower under single stress conditions have been reported, such as ion homeostasis regulation and identification of salt tolerance-related QTLs [12, 13], it remains unclear whether these mechanisms are still applicable under combined saline-alkali stress conditions, and systematic validation is still lacking.

In this study, the saline-alkali tolerant sunflower hybrid K-27 and the sensitive hybrid K-7 were used. Time-series root samples were collected from seedlings exposed to combined saline-alkali stress for 0–96 h. By integrating physiological measurements with RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis, the dynamic response characteristics of the two genotypes were systematically compared, and key candidate genes were validated by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). This study aimed to elucidate the physiological regulatory characteristics of sunflower seedlings under combined saline-alkali stress, reveal genotype-dependent transcriptional responses and their temporal patterns, and identify key saline-alkali tolerance-related genes and regulatory pathways, thereby providing a theoretical basis and genetic resources for molecular breeding of saline-alkali tolerance in sunflower.

Materials and methods

Sunflower materials and combined saline-alkali stress treatment

This study used the saline-alkali tolerant sunflower hybrid K-27 and the saline-alkali sensitive hybrid K-7 as experimental materials. Both hybrids are oilseed sunflower lines independently bred by the Institute of Cotton, Hebei Academy of Agriculture and Forestry Sciences, sharing a common paternal line (MFR1 × Xinkui No. 13) and derived from distinct maternal combinations. All seeds used in the experiments were uniformly preserved and provided by the institute’s sunflower germplasm repository. The difference in saline-alkali tolerance between the two hybrids was determined based on preliminary hydroponic screening of 31 sunflower hybrid accessions at the seedling stage (with NaCl gradients of 0.3%-0.6%, pH adjusted to 9.0 with Na2CO3), through comprehensive evaluation of growth phenotypes and physiological indicators.

Seeds were soaked in distilled water for 6 h and germinated in washed sand. After the seedlings developed two cotyledons, they were transferred to hydroponic culture boxes containing modified Hoagland nutrient solution. The solution was prepared using a 500× Hoagland concentrate solution (Beijing Coolaber Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China; Catalog No. NS10205) by diluting 5 mL of the stock in 10 L of distilled water, and was continuously aerated. Seedlings were grown until the two-leaf-one-heart stage, at which point the original nutrient solution was filtered to remove impurities. For the treatment group, NaCl was added to a final concentration of 0.5% (w/v), and the solution pH was adjusted to 9.0 using Na2CO3 to obtain the combined saline-alkali stress solution; the control group underwent the same filtration procedure without any further treatment.

For phenotypic analysis, seedlings were continuously subjected to the combined saline-alkali stress treatment for 14 days in a hydroponic culture room under the following conditions: light intensity of 350 µmol·m-2·s-1, a 13 h light/11 h dark photoperiod, a constant temperature of 24 °C, and relative humidity of 30%-40%. At the end of the treatment period, shoot height, root length, shoot fresh weight, and root fresh weight were measured, with six biological replicates per trait. The inhibition rates or loss rates of growth parameters were calculated relative to the corresponding genotype grown under normal conditions (distilled water) using the following formulas:

Determination of physiological and biochemical indices

Root and leaf samples were collected at 0, 3, 12, 24, 48, and 96 h after the initiation of combined saline-alkali stress treatment. Root samples were blotted with absorbent paper to remove surface moisture, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C for subsequent determination of ROS content, SOD activity, POD activity, CAT activity, MDA content, proline content, soluble sugar content, soluble protein content, and lignin, cellulose, and hemicellulose contents. Leaf samples were wiped clean and shredded for determination of photosynthetic pigment contents (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll, and carotenoids). All indicators were measured using corresponding commercial assay kits, with three biological replicates per time point, each consisting of an individual seedling. Detailed kit information is provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Gas exchange parameters were measured on fully expanded true leaves at the same time points using an LI-6400 portable photosynthesis system (LI-COR, USA) under the following conditions: light intensity of 500 µmol·m-2·s-1, CO2 concentration of 380–430 µmol·mol-1, and leaf temperature of 25 °C. The measured parameters included net photosynthetic rate (Pn), stomatal conductance (Gs), transpiration rate (Tr), and intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci).

Determination of ion content

Root samples used for ion content analysis were fresh plant material, which was homogenized and digested using the HNO3-HClO4 digestion method (GB 5009.268–2016) [14]. The contents of Na+, K+, and Ca2+ were determined using an inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES; Agilent 5110, USA), and results were expressed as mg per gram of fresh weight (mg·g-1 FW).

Transcriptome sequencing and differential expression analysis

Root samples for transcriptome analysis were collected at the same time points as those used for physiological and biochemical measurements, with three biological replicates per time point, each comprising pooled root samples from six seedlings. Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method (Invitrogen, USA). RNA concentration and purity were assessed with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, and RNA integrity was evaluated with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer; all samples met the quality requirement of RIN > 7.0. cDNA libraries were constructed as follows: 1 µg of total RNA was used for mRNA enrichment with oligo(dT) magnetic beads, followed by fragmentation at elevated temperature with divalent cations. First-strand cDNA was synthesized using random hexamer primers and reverse transcriptase, and second-strand cDNA was synthesized with DNA Polymerase I and RNase H. After purification, end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation, library fragments of approximately 350 bp were selected using AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter, USA), amplified by PCR, and assessed for quality on an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer; library concentration was determined with a Qubit fluorometer. Paired-end sequencing (2 × 150 bp) was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq PE150 platform, yielding no less than 6 Gb of clean reads per sample.

Raw reads in FASTQ format were processed with fastp (v0.23.2) [15] using the following criteria: (1) removal of adapter sequences; (2) removal of reads with > 10% N bases; (3) removal of reads with > 50% low-quality bases (Q ≤ 20); and (4) retention of trimmed reads ≥ 50 bp in length. Clean reads were aligned to the sunflower reference genome HanXRQr2.0-SUNRISE [16] using Kallisto (v0.46.1) [17], and gene expression levels were quantified as transcripts per million (TPM). Functional annotation of genes was performed with eggNOG-mapper (v2.1.12) [18] based on the eggNOG database, retrieving GO, KEGG, COG, and other functional classifications.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the DESeq2 package (v1.38.0) [19] with the criteria: |fold change| > 1.5 and Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted P-value (false discovery rate, FDR) < 0.05. GO enrichment analysis was performed with topGO (v2.50.0) [20], and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis with ClusterProfiler (v4.6.0) [21]; the significance threshold for both was FDR < 0.05. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed based on the STRING database (v12.0) [22] with a combined score cutoff > 0.4 and visualized using Cytoscape (v3.9.1) [23].

Definition and screening of core differentially expressed genes (cDEGs)

To identify core genes that consistently exert regulatory functions throughout the entire course of saline-alkali stress from large-scale transcriptomic data, this study proposes the analytical concept of core differentially expressed genes (cDEGs). The cDEGs screening workflow is as follows: (1) at each stress time point (3, 12, 24, 48, and 96 h) compared with the 0 h control, DEGs (|fold change| > 1.5, FDR < 0.05) and differentially enriched KEGG pathways (FDR < 0.05) were identified; (2) intersections across all five time points were taken to obtain genes consistently differentially expressed at all time points (shared DEGs) and KEGG pathways consistently significantly enriched at all time points (shared KEGG pathways); (3) the shared DEGs were mapped to the shared KEGG pathways-i.e., genes among the shared DEGs that could be annotated to the shared KEGG pathways were defined as cDEGs. The rationale for this strategy is that combined saline-alkali stress is a sustained process; the dual constraint of “gene + pathway” across all time points effectively excludes transiently responsive genes and focuses on key genes within core regulatory pathways that are persistently activated throughout the entire stress duration.

Quantitative real-time PCR validation

Six representative differentially expressed genes were selected for qRT-PCR validation: four were randomly chosen from the significantly differentially expressed genes, and two additional genes of particular interest involved in lignin metabolism-BAMT and CAD5-were included, yielding a total of six genes for qRT-PCR validation. Gene-specific primers were designed using Primer Premier 5.0 (Premier Biosoft, USA), and their specificity was verified by NCBI Primer-BLAST alignment against the sunflower reference genome to ensure unique target site matching. The sunflower 18 S rRNA gene was used as the internal reference [24], and all primer sequences are listed in Table S1. Primer specificity was further assessed by melting curve analysis and agarose gel electrophoresis. Reverse transcription was performed using the HiScript III RT SuperMix kit (Vazyme Biotech, R323). qRT-PCR was conducted on an Applied Biosystems QuantStudio 5 Real-Time PCR System using Taq SYBR Green qPCR Premix (Jiangsu Baisimei Biotechnology Co., Ltd., EG20117M). Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method. Three technical replicates were performed for each sample.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0 software. Phenotypic data were compared between genotypes using the independent-samples t-test. Time-series physiological and biochemical data were analyzed by one-way ANOVA, with post-hoc multiple comparisons performed using Duncan’s multiple range test. All data are presented as mean ± standard error (Mean ± SE), with statistical significance set at P < 0.05. The number of biological replicates for each indicator is indicated in the respective figure legends. Graphs were generated using R (v4.5.1) and GraphPad Prism 9.0.

Results

K-27 is a saline-alkali tolerant genotype, whereas K-7 is saline-alkali sensitive

To verify the differences in tolerance between the two sunflower hybrids under combined saline-alkali stress, hydroponically grown seedlings were treated with 0.5% NaCl (with the pH adjusted to 9.0 using Na2CO3) for 14 days, and their phenotypes and growth parameters were comparatively analyzed. The results showed that combined saline-alkali stress inhibited the growth of both hybrids; however, significant differences in stress responses were observed between K-27 and K-7 (Fig. 1A).

Under saline-alkali stress conditions, K-27 plants maintained relatively green leaves and a more intact root system. In contrast, K-7 plants exhibited pronounced leaf chlorosis and wilting, accompanied by severe root browning. No significant difference was observed in plant height inhibition rate between the two genotypes (P > 0.05). However, highly significant differences (P < 0.01) were detected in root length inhibition rate, shoot fresh weight loss rate, and root fresh weight loss rate. Specifically, the root length inhibition rate of K-27 was 15.82%, which was markedly lower than that of K-7 (46.05%). The shoot and root fresh weight loss rates of K-27 were 44.40% and 11.50%, respectively, substantially lower than those of K-7 (79.26% and 80.33%).

In summary, K-27 exhibited stronger root growth maintenance and biomass retention under combined saline-alkali stress and can therefore be regarded as a saline-alkali tolerant genotype. In contrast, K-7 displayed typical saline-alkali sensitivity, providing a suitable comparative basis for subsequent physiological and transcriptomic analyses.

K-27 exhibits enhanced saline-alkali tolerance through a stronger cell wall structural basis, stable redox regulation, and a more favorable K+/Na+ ratio

To systematically compare the physiological adaptive characteristics of K-27 and K-7 under combined saline-alkali stress, time-course analyses of root physiological and biochemical parameters, as well as leaf photosynthesis-related indices, were conducted at 0–96 h after treatment.

Combined saline-alkali stress significantly disturbed redox homeostasis in both genotypes, but their response patterns differed markedly. In K-7, ROS content rapidly increased at the early stage of stress (3 h) and reached a peak, whereas in K-27, the ROS peak was delayed until 24 h and remained significantly lower than that of K-7 during the early stress phase (Fig. 2A). Consistently, the activities of SOD and CAT in K-27 were significantly upregulated and peaked at 12 h, whereas K-7 did not exhibit a sustained induction pattern during the stress period (Fig. 2B, D). POD activity increased progressively with stress duration in both genotypes and reached maximum levels at 48 h (Fig. 2C). Membrane lipid peroxidation, indicated by MDA content, remained relatively low in K-27 during the mid-stress stage, while K-7 showed a significant increase at 48 h (Fig. 2E).

Ion content analysis revealed that Na+ content in roots of both genotypes continuously increased with stress duration and peaked at 96 h (Fig. 2F). In contrast, K-27 exhibited a clear advantage in K+ maintenance, with K+ content significantly recovering at 48 h and sustaining a higher K+/Na+ ratio during the later stress stage (Fig. 2G, H). Additionally, Ca2+ content in K-27 significantly increased at 96 h, whereas no similar change was observed in K-7 (Fig. 2I).

Regarding osmotic adjustment, proline content in K-27 peaked at 12 h and remained significantly higher than that in K-7 during the later stages of stress. In contrast, proline accumulation in K-7 showed only a transient increase at 3 h (Fig. 2J). Soluble sugar content peaked at 3 h in both genotypes, but K-27 displayed a higher peak value and overall level (Fig. 2K). Although soluble protein content showed relatively minor fluctuations overall, K-27 maintained significantly higher levels both at the basal state and during the later stages of stress compared with K-7 (Fig. 2L).

In terms of cell wall components, K-27 exhibited higher basal contents of lignin and hemicellulose. Cellulose content in both genotypes peaked at 12 h (Fig. 2M-O).

The Chl a/b ratio remained relatively stable in both genotypes throughout the stress period, indicating that the photosynthetic apparatus continued to function effectively despite the stress treatment (Fig. 2P). Total chlorophyll content showed a general decline at later stages in K-7, whereas it remained comparatively steady in K-27 (Fig. 2Q). Pn, Gs, and Tr significantly decreased in both genotypes following stress treatment, while Ci changed dynamically over time (Fig. 2R–U).

K-27 exhibits an earlier, stronger, and more sustained transcriptional response under combined saline-alkali stress

To elucidate the differences in transcriptional responses between K-27 and K-7 under combined saline-alkali stress, RNA-seq analysis was performed on root samples collected at 0, 3, 12, 24, 48, and 96 h from both sunflower hybrids. Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed clear separation among samples based on genotype and treatment time in the expression space, while biological replicates clustered closely together, indicating high reproducibility and reliability of the sequencing data (Fig. 3A). Differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis showed that as early as 3 h after stress treatment, 5,283 upregulated genes were detected in K-27, which was markedly higher than the 3,422 upregulated genes identified in K-7. Moreover, at all treatment time points in K-27, the number of upregulated genes exceeded that of downregulated genes, whereas this pattern was observed in K-7 only at 12 h (Fig. 3B). Overall, K-27 exhibited an earlier onset and a greater magnitude of transcriptional activation in response to combined saline-alkali stress.

cDEGs in K-27 support saline-alkali tolerance through coordinated oxidative defense, cell wall regulation, and metabolic pathways

To identify core genes in K-27 that exhibited sustained responses throughout the full time course of combined saline-alkali stress, genes and KEGG pathways showing significant differential expression at all treatment time points were selected. A total of 2,161 genes and 26 KEGG pathways were consistently differentially expressed across all time points (Fig. 4A, B). Among these, 461 genes were mapped to the 26 pathways and were defined as the cDEGs of K-27.

GO enrichment analysis (Fig. 4C) revealed that cDEGs were primarily enriched in biological processes such as “cellular oxidative detoxification” and “plant-type cell wall organization,” as well as molecular functions including “peroxidase activity” and “oxidoreductase activity.” At the cellular component level, “plant-type cell wall” was significantly enriched.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis indicated that “metabolic pathways” and “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites” served as key pathway nodes. Genes such as NADH dehydrogenase subunit J (ndhJ), NADH dehydrogenase subunit G (ndhG), Long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase 2 (LACS2), Fatty acid desaturase 2 (FAD2), and Phospholipase D alpha 1 (PLDα1) were directly associated with these pathways. Notably, PLDα1 was connected to multiple pathway nodes, including “metabolic pathways,” “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites,” and “glutathione metabolism.” The “MAPK signaling pathway-plant” was linked to ndhJ through WRKY transcription factor 22 (WRKY22). Network connectivity analysis identified ndhJ, PLDα1, and LACS2 as high-degree hub genes.

Expression clustering of cDEGs (Fig. 4E) revealed two distinct patterns: stress-activated and stress-repressed. Stress-activated genes in K-27 were rapidly upregulated during the early stress phase (3–12 h) and peaked in the mid-stress phase (24–48 h), whereas stress-repressed genes were rapidly downregulated at the onset of stress. Key gene expression patterns included: FAD2 (LOC110897314) reaching 369.23 TPM at 48 h in K-27, significantly higher than 31.73 TPM in K-7 at the same time point; PLDα1 peaking at 154.92 TPM at 3 h and maintaining relatively high expression thereafter; ndhJ and ndhG showing rapid downregulation from high basal expression to near-zero levels in K-27; and Cytochrome P450 707A2 (CYP707A2) displaying aberrantly high expression in K-7 at 96 h (84.92 TPM) compared with only 0.06 TPM in K-27.

Compared with K-7, K-27 maintains saline-alkali tolerance through a larger and more stable core gene network

Using the same selection criteria, cDEGs in K-7 were analyzed. A total of 1,171 genes and 18 KEGG pathways were consistently differentially expressed across all treatment time points (Fig. 5A, B), among which 200 genes were mapped to these pathways and defined as the cDEGs of K-7. Compared with K-27, K-7 exhibited substantially fewer consistently differentially expressed genes (1,171 vs. 2,161) and cDEGs (200 vs. 461).

GO enrichment analysis indicated that the cDEGs in K-7 were primarily enriched in hormone-related biological processes, including the biosynthesis and signaling pathways of auxin, ethylene, brassinosteroids, and jasmonic acid. At the molecular function level, terms such as “monooxygenase activity” and “nitrate transmembrane transporter activity” were significantly enriched (Fig. 5C).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis revealed that the core genes in K-7 were mainly distributed in modules associated with phenylpropanoid biosynthesis and secondary metabolite synthesis, with Cinnamyl Alcohol Dehydrogenase (CAD) and ent-Copalyl diphosphate synthase 1 (CPS1) participating in key pathway interactions (Fig. 5D). Expression pattern analysis showed that CAD in K-27 was induced at the early stage of stress, whereas its response in K-7 was comparatively delayed. Conversely, CPS1 was repressed in K-27 during early stress, while in K-7 it exhibited significant upregulation at 96 h (Fig. 5E).

BAMT is the only key node exhibiting opposite regulation among conserved core genes in sunflower under saline-alkali stress

To identify shared regulatory genes involved in sunflower responses to combined saline-alkali stress, a Venn analysis of cDEGs from K-27 and K-7 was performed (Fig. 6A). The analysis revealed 417 cDEGs specific to K-27, 156 cDEGs specific to K-7, and 44 cDEGs shared between the two genotypes.

GO enrichment analysis of the 44 shared cDEGs indicated that these genes were mainly enriched in hormone-related regulatory processes and antioxidant detoxification pathways, including auxin-centered hormone regulation, brassinosteroid-mediated signaling, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, glutathione metabolism, and toxin degradation pathways. Additionally, terms related to sucrose catabolism and disaccharide metabolism were significantly enriched (Fig. 6B).

Comparison of expression patterns showed that although both genotypes responded transcriptionally to saline-alkali stress, the magnitude and duration of upregulation in K-27 were generally higher than in K-7, while downregulated genes exhibited relatively weaker suppression. Among the 44 shared cDEGs, six genes displayed significant expression differences between the two genotypes, with the benzoic acid carboxyl methyltransferase gene (BAMT) showing the most pronounced genotype-specific expression pattern. Notably, BAMT was continuously upregulated in K-27 but persistently downregulated in K-7, making it the only gene among the 44 shared cDEGs that clearly exhibited opposite regulatory directions between the two genotypes, suggesting its potential involvement in the saline-alkali tolerance response of K-27, although its specific functional role remains to be experimentally validated.

The remaining five significantly different genes encode fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH), pectin methylesterase inhibitor (PMEI), glutathione S-transferase (GST), inositol polyphosphate 5-phosphatase (INPP5E), and HXXXD-type acyltransferase. These genes displayed consistent expression trends in both genotypes but were expressed at significantly higher levels in K-27.

Consistent expression of BAMT and other key genes further validates RNA-seq results

To verify the reliability of the RNA-seq data, six representative differentially expressed genes, including BAMT and cinnamyl alcohol dehydrogenase 5 (CAD5), were selected for qRT-PCR analysis (Fig. 7). The results showed that the expression trends detected by qRT-PCR were consistent with those observed in RNA-seq, with a significant correlation between the two methods (R2 > 0.85, P < 0.05), indicating high reliability of the transcriptome sequencing results.

Discussion

Coordinated responses of antioxidant defense, ion homeostasis, and osmotic adjustment

Combined saline-alkali stress simultaneously induces osmotic imbalance, ion toxicity, and oxidative stress, and the ability of plants to establish and maintain cellular homeostasis is critical for tolerance [10]. Our results indicate that the saline-alkali tolerant sunflower hybrid K-27 adopts a coordinated response strategy centered on homeostasis maintenance during stress, characterized by a “pre-adaptation-rapid response-sustained regulation” pattern, in contrast to the delayed and unstable regulatory responses observed in K-7.

At the redox level, SOD, POD, and CAT constitute the core cascade of enzymatic antioxidant defense: SOD converts superoxide anions (O2-) into H2O2, CAT disproportionates H2O2 into H2O and O2, while POD reduces H2O2 using electron donors and simultaneously participates in lignin biosynthesis [25]. The coordinated activation of these three enzymes in K-27 effectively controlled ROS accumulation, with the ROS peak delayed to 24 h (vs. 3 h in K-7) and MDA content maintained at a relatively low level during the mid-stress stage, indicating effective suppression of membrane lipid peroxidation [26]. Notably, K-27 does not rely on constitutively high antioxidant enzyme activity but rather exhibits a pronounced inducible antioxidant response-previous studies have suggested that inducible, rather than continuously high, antioxidant activation is more favorable for reducing metabolic costs and maintaining signaling sensitivity under prolonged stress [27]. In K-27, the enzymatic antioxidant system acts synergistically with glutathione-mediated non-enzymatic detoxification to maintain redox homeostasis during stress, consistent with its relatively low membrane lipid peroxidation levels [28, 29].

Regarding ion homeostasis, saline-alkali stress leads to excessive Na+ influx and K+ efflux, disrupting the activity of K+-dependent enzymes in the cytoplasm. K-27 maintains a relatively high K+/Na+ ratio during the later stages of stress, a physiological trait widely recognized as an important indicator of salt tolerance [30]. Concurrently, K-27 exhibits a recovery trend in Ca2+ levels during late stress; Ca2+, as a second messenger, can promote Na+ efflux through activation of the Salt Overly Sensitive (SOS) pathway (SOS3-SOS2-SOS1 cascade) [31]. Furthermore, differential expression of phospholipid signaling-related genes, such as PLDα1 and INPP5E, suggests a potential role for the membrane lipid-Ca2+ signaling network in maintaining homeostasis in K-27 [32–34]. PLDα1 reached its expression peak (154.92 TPM) as early as 3 h after stress in K-27, and the phosphatidic acid (PA) produced by PLDα1 catalysis serves as a key signaling molecule for activating downstream ion transporters; INPP5E was expressed at significantly higher levels in K-27 than in K-7 and may couple phospholipid signaling with Ca2+ homeostasis through regulation of phosphoinositide metabolism.

Regarding osmotic adjustment, K-27 exhibited a “delayed but sustained” proline accumulation pattern-peaking at 12 h and remaining significantly higher than that of K-7 at 96 h, whereas K-7 showed only a transient accumulation peak at 3 h. Soluble sugars and soluble proteins were also significantly higher in K-27, both at the basal level and during the later stages of stress, compared with K-7 [35]. Compared with the transient and fluctuating regulatory patterns observed in K-7, this multi-layered and coordinated homeostasis maintenance strategy is more effective in coping with the sustained pressure of combined saline-alkali stress.

Phenylpropanoid metabolism and cell wall remodeling

The phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway is a crucial branch of plant secondary metabolism, with its products playing central roles in reinforcing cell wall structure, mediating defense signaling, and facilitating metabolic reprogramming [36]. In this study, this pathway exhibited pronounced genotype-specific regulation in K-27 in response to combined saline-alkali stress.

Among the cDEGs shared between the two genotypes, BAMT displayed the most significant genotype-specific expression pattern: it was continuously upregulated in K-27 but persistently downregulated in K-7. BAMT encodes a member of the SABATH methyltransferase family that catalyzes the methylation of benzoic acid to produce methyl benzoate [37, 38]. Although this enzyme has primarily been reported in the context of floral scent biosynthesis, recent studies suggest that its products may act as signaling molecules involved in stress response regulation [39, 40]. This study reports for the first time the genotype-specific expression of BAMT in sunflower seedlings under saline-alkali stress, suggesting that it may play a novel role in abiotic stress defense. However, it must be clearly acknowledged that the current evidence for BAMT is limited to transcriptional-level expression analysis and lacks direct support from functional validation. Therefore, BAMT should be regarded as a candidate gene. Future studies should verify its function through transgenic or gene-editing approaches.

A good correspondence was observed between cell wall-related physiological data and gene expression. The basal lignin content of K-27 was approximately 1.5-fold that of K-7, but it showed a sharp decline at 12 h of stress, followed by recovery to a stable level from 24 to 96 h. This dynamic change is temporally consistent with the expression pattern of lignin biosynthesis genes-taking CAD5 (one of the most important rate-limiting enzymes in lignin biosynthesis in Arabidopsis [41, 42]) as an example, this gene was significantly upregulated at 3 h in both genotypes, but the induction magnitude in K-27 was substantially higher than that in K-7 (60.38 vs. 31.96 TPM), indicating that K-27 can more rapidly initiate lignin monomer synthesis [43]. The sharp decline in lignin at 12 h may reflect the reallocation of carbon metabolic resources during early stress: carbon skeletons are preferentially directed toward osmotic adjustment and antioxidant defense, with cell wall synthesis resuming once the adaptive mechanisms are established [44]. This strategy, combining high basal reserves with dynamic remodeling, may help maintain cell wall mechanical strength without excessive stiffening, thereby balancing defense and growth [45–47], and may be closely associated with the lower root length inhibition rate of K-27 (15.82% vs. 46.05% in K-7). Additionally, the high expression of genes associated with hemicellulose and pectin modification, such as PMEI, in K-27 inhibits pectin methylesterase activity to regulate the degree of pectin methylesterification in the cell wall, maintaining its mechanical properties and ion exchange capacity [48, 49], thereby further enhancing structural adaptability under saline-alkali stress.

Synergistic interactions and cross-talk among multi-level response mechanisms

The saline-alkali tolerance of K-27 does not depend on any single isolated pathway, but rather is achieved through the synergistic coordination of antioxidant defense, ion homeostasis, osmotic adjustment, and phenylpropanoid metabolism, with potential cross-regulatory relationships existing among multiple pathways.

A functional coupling exists between phenylpropanoid metabolism and the antioxidant system: products of phenylpropanoid metabolism (such as flavonoids and phenolic acids) themselves possess ROS-scavenging capacity and can serve as important components of the non-enzymatic antioxidant system, complementing the inducible enzymatic antioxidant system to collectively maintain redox homeostasis. Phospholipid signaling may serve as an upstream hub for ion homeostasis regulation-both PA produced by PLDα1 and phosphoinositide metabolites regulated by INPP5E are second messengers involved in intracellular Ca2+ signaling [50], consistent with the Ca2+ recovery and sustained K+/Na+ ratio observed in K-27 during late stress.

The differences in cDEGs functional enrichment between the two genotypes reveal fundamentally distinct regulatory strategies: the cDEGs of K-27 are primarily enriched in cell wall organization and oxidative detoxification, reflecting a tolerance strategy dominated by “structural defense”; in contrast, the cDEGs of K-7 are significantly enriched in hormone signaling pathways (auxin, ethylene, brassinosteroids, and jasmonic acid), potentially reflecting a passive “hormone-mediated stress response” pattern. The differential activation of phenylpropanoid metabolism may be a key factor driving the divergence of these two strategies, as this pathway not only provides precursors for cell wall reinforcement but also yields metabolic intermediates that can serve as signaling molecules participating in hormone regulation [51]. Despite the marked difference in tolerance between the two genotypes, GO enrichment of the 44 shared cDEGs indicates that they still share conserved response pathways, including glutathione metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and osmolyte metabolism (Fig. 6B), which may constitute a species-level basal defense framework for sunflower in response to saline-alkali stress.

Limitations and future perspectives

The cDEGs strategy employed in this study screened core genes through the dual constraint of “persistent differential expression across all time points + persistent pathway enrichment across all time points,” effectively excluding transiently responsive genes. However, the stringent criteria of this strategy may also omit genes that play critical regulatory roles only within specific temporal windows; therefore, it should be regarded as a screening tool rather than an exhaustive identification approach. From a temporal perspective, K-27 initiated a larger-scale transcriptional response as early as the initial stress phase (5,283 upregulated genes at 3 h vs. 3,422 in K-7) and maintained a larger and more functionally focused core gene network throughout the stress duration (cDEGs: 461 vs. 200), which corresponds well with its physiological phenotypes and collectively explains its ability to sustain higher tolerance over time.

This study also has several limitations. First, only two genotypes were used for comparison; the limited genetic background may not adequately represent the genetic diversity of sunflower germplasm resources, and future studies should validate the general applicability of the identified candidate genes in a broader panel of germplasm. Second, the functional inferences for key candidate genes, such as BAMT, CAD5, and PLDα1, are based on correlative evidence from transcriptional expression levels, and subsequent functional validation through transgenic overexpression or gene editing is required. Third, this study focused on the transcriptomic level; future integration of proteomic, metabolomic, and other multi-omics data would enable a more comprehensive dissection of the molecular regulatory network underlying saline-alkali tolerance.

The findings of this study provide multi-level insights for molecular breeding of saline-alkali tolerant sunflower. First, BAMT, as a candidate gene that is continuously upregulated in tolerant materials but persistently downregulated in sensitive genotypes, holds potential value as a molecular marker or functional gene for saline-alkali tolerance. In combination with the relatively high basal levels of lignin and hemicellulose, cell wall-related metabolic traits may serve as important selection criteria for screening saline-alkali tolerant germplasm. Second, the homeostasis maintenance strategy observed in K-27 highlights the importance of multi-mechanism coordination. Sustained osmotic adjustment capacity, a relatively high K+/Na+ ratio, and Ca2+-related signaling collectively underpin the physiological basis of saline-alkali tolerance. In breeding practice, selecting materials with BAMT inducible expression characteristics and high basal levels of lignin and hemicellulose may contribute to improved cell wall stability; simultaneously optimizing osmotic adjustment and ion homeostasis systems-by selecting lines with combined advantages in osmotic regulation and ion transport-may reduce stress damage and facilitate the development of saline-alkali tolerant cultivars. Despite the above limitations, this study provides a relatively systematic data foundation and candidate targets for understanding the physiological and molecular mechanisms of saline-alkali tolerance in sunflower.

Conclusion

K-27 is a sunflower hybrid with pronounced tolerance to saline-alkali stress. By integrating phenotypic, physiological-biochemical, and transcriptomic data, this study systematically elucidated its adaptive mechanisms under combined saline-alkali stress. The results indicate that K-27 effectively suppresses ROS accumulation through early inducible activation of antioxidant enzymes (SOD and CAT); maintains ion homeostasis via sustained accumulation of osmolytes and a relatively high K+/Na+ ratio; and relies on a high basal content of lignin and hemicellulose to provide stable cell wall support for the roots.

Transcriptome analysis revealed that K-27 initiates a faster and stronger transcriptional response at the early stage of stress, and identified 44 cDEGs shared between the two genotypes, primarily involved in hormone regulation, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and glutathione metabolism. Among them, BAMT was continuously upregulated in K-27 but persistently downregulated in K-7, representing the only candidate gene exhibiting opposite regulatory directions between the two genotypes, and its function awaits further validation.

These multi-layered and coordinated mechanisms collectively support K-27’s lower root growth inhibition and reduced biomass loss under saline-alkali stress. This study provides a systematic framework for understanding the physiological and molecular basis of saline-alkali tolerance in sunflower and offers potential targets for molecular breeding of tolerant cultivars.
