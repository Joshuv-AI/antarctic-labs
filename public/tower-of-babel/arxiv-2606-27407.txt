Observers, Symmetries, and the Hierarchy of Language Classes: A Theory of Computation Parameterized by the Observer
Fabio Francesco Gabriele Buono

Abstract
We introduce the observational hierarchy , a new axis of classification for formal languages, orthogonal to the Chomsky hierarchy. An observer is a function O : Σ ∗ → S O:\Sigma^{*}\to S that determines which information about the input is accessible to a computational system. The order-blind automaton, which perceives the input as a multiset of symbols rather than a sequence, constitutes the paradigmatic case. We prove that the class of languages recognisable by any machine equipped with such an observer coincides exactly with the permutation-closed languages. We then define a partial order on observers that induces a hierarchy of language classes parametrised not by the computational power of the machine, but by the structure of the observer. We prove that this hierarchy has the structure of a partial order with a diamond-shaped profile sub-lattice, comprising the length branch O ⊥ ≺ O len ≺ O prof ≺ O ⊤ O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{len}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top} and the parity branch O ⊥ ≺ O par ≺ O prof ≺ O ⊤ O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{par}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top} , with O len O_{\mathrm{len}} and O par O_{\mathrm{par}} incomparable, and an infinite subsequence branch O ⊥ ≺ O 1 ≺ O 2 ≺ ⋯ ≺ O ⊤ O_{\bot}\prec O_{1}\prec O_{2}\prec\cdots\prec O_{\top} , both converging to the complete observer. We prove that the observational hierarchy is strictly incomparable with the Chomsky hierarchy, and introduce the notion of observational complexity of a language. We further define observer-parametrised complexity classes 𝐏 O \mathbf{P}_{O} and 𝐍𝐏 O \mathbf{NP}_{O} , and show that computational hardness and structural blindness are two independent phenomena. In particular, 𝐏 O prof = 𝐍𝐏 O prof \mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}} holds as a structural collapse strictly inside 𝐏 \mathbf{P} .

Introduction

The classical theory of computational complexity classifies problems
according to the power of the machine required to solve them. Finite
automata, pushdown machines, Turing machines, and their deterministic and
nondeterministic variants
[
2
,
9
]
. In this framework,
the input is always a string, an ordered sequence of symbols, and full
access to that sequence is taken for granted.

This paper inverts that perspective. We ask:
which languages can a
computational system recognise when it does not have access to the complete
input, but only to a projection of it?
The power of the machine becomes
secondary; what matters is the structure of the
observer
, that is,
of the function that transforms the input into available information.

The motivating case is that of an observer that cannot distinguish the
temporal order of symbols, but perceives only their counts. Formally, such
an observer maps every string
x\in\Sigma^{*}
to its
profile

\mathrm{prof}(x)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, where
k=|\Sigma|
and the
i
-th component
counts the occurrences of symbol
a_{i}
. An automaton equipped in this way
does not distinguish
01
from
10
, nor
aab
from

aba
or
baa
.

).

(ii)

).

(iii)

and

incomparable, both below
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
) and an infinite

(Section
4
).

(iv)

Formal connection with Simon’s hierarchy and piecewise testable

(Section

).

(v)

Proof that the observational hierarchy is orthogonal to the

).

(vi)

2
The base model: the order-blind automaton

Let
\Sigma=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
be a finite alphabet.

Definition 2.1

(Profile)
.

The
profile
of a string
x\in\Sigma^{*}
is the function

\mathrm{prof}(x)=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}

where
k=|\Sigma|
and
c_{i}=|x|_{a_{i}}
is the number of
occurrences of
a_{i}
in
x
.

Definition 2.2

(Permutation-closed language)
.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is
permutation-closed
if

x\in L\;\Rightarrow\;\forall\,y\in\Sigma^{*}\ \text{with}\ \mathrm{prof}(y)=\mathrm{prof}(x),\quad y\in L.

Equivalently,
L
is permutation-closed if and only if there exists a
function
f:\mathbb{N}^{k}\to\{0,1\}
such that

L=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid f(\mathrm{prof}(x))=1\}
.

Definition 2.3

(Order-blind automaton)
.

An
order-blind automaton
is a quintuple

A=(Q,\,\Sigma,\,\delta,\,q_{0},\,F)

where
Q
is a finite set of states,
q_{0}\in Q
is the initial state,

F\subseteq Q
is the set of accepting states, and

\delta:Q\times\mathbb{N}^{k}\to Q

is the transition function. The
extended transition function
is

\hat{\delta}(q_{0},x)\coloneqq\delta(q_{0},\,\mathrm{prof}(x)),

(1)

and the
recognised language
is

L(A)=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid\hat{\delta}(q_{0},x)\in F\}.

(2)

Remark 2.4
.

Unlike a classical DFA, whose transition function has type

Q\times\Sigma\to Q
and processes the input symbol by symbol,
the order-blind automaton has transition function

\delta:Q\times\mathbb{N}^{k}\to Q
, which reads the entire profile in a
single step (equation (
1
)). This is intentional: the
model is designed to recognise any permutation-closed language, including
non-regular ones such as
\{x\mid|x|_{0}=|x|_{1}\}
.

Theorem 2.5

(Characterisation)
.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is recognisable by an order-blind
automaton if and only if it is permutation-closed.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
L=L(A)
for some order-blind automaton
A
. If

x\in L
and
\mathrm{prof}(y)=\mathrm{prof}(x)
, then

\hat{\delta}(q_{0},y)\overset{\eqref{eq:delta-hat}}{=}\delta(q_{0},\mathrm{prof}(y))=\delta(q_{0},\mathrm{prof}(x))\overset{\eqref{eq:delta-hat}}{=}\hat{\delta}(q_{0},x)\in F,

so
y\in L
.

(\Leftarrow)
Let
L
be permutation-closed, so there exists

f:\mathbb{N}^{k}\to\{0,1\}
with

L=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid f(\mathrm{prof}(x))=1\}
.
Construct
A
with
Q=\{q_{0},q_{\mathrm{acc}},q_{\mathrm{rej}}\}
,

F=\{q_{\mathrm{acc}}\}
, and

\delta(q_{0},v)=\begin{cases}q_{\mathrm{acc}}&\text{if }f(v)=1,\\
q_{\mathrm{rej}}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\hat{\delta}(q_{0},x)=\delta(q_{0},\mathrm{prof}(x))
by (
1
),
so
A
accepts
x
if and only if
f(\mathrm{prof}(x))=1
, giving
L(A)=L
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Over
\{0,1\}
:

•

L_{1}=\{x\mid|x|_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}\}
is
permutation-closed: recognisable.

•

L_{2}=\{x\mid|x|_{0}>|x|_{1}\}
is permutation-closed:
recognisable.

•

L_{3}=\{x\mid x\text{ starts with }1\}
is not
permutation-closed:
\mathrm{prof}(10)=\mathrm{prof}(01)=(1,1)
but

10\in L_{3}
and
01\notin L_{3}
. Not recognisable.

•

L_{4}=0^{*}1^{*}
is not permutation-closed:

\mathrm{prof}(01)=\mathrm{prof}(10)=(1,1)
but
01\in L_{4}
and

10\notin L_{4}
. Not recognisable.

Remark 2.7
.

Theorem
2.5
shows that the finiteness of
Q
is
not the binding constraint: it is the observer that determines the class.
The power of the machine cannot compensate for the weakness of the observer.
With an observer that maps everything to the same value, no machine, not
even a Turing machine, could recognise anything beyond

\emptyset
and
\Sigma^{*}
.

The transition function
\delta:Q\times\mathbb{N}^{k}\to Q
is not required
to be computable. The purpose of Theorem
2.5
is
to characterise
all
permutation-closed languages, which includes
non-recursive and non-r.e. languages
(Theorem
9.9
(ii)). Restricting
\delta
to computable
functions would yield a strictly smaller class. The model is therefore a

mathematical
characterisation rather than a computational one.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any machine
M
and any language
L\notin\{\emptyset,\Sigma^{*}\}
,
a system equipped with the trivial observer
O_{\bot}:x\mapsto\star

does not recognise
L
, regardless of the computational power of
M
.

Proof.

The trivial observer identifies all strings, the only

O_{\bot}
-decidable languages are
\emptyset
and
\Sigma^{*}
.
Any
L\notin\{\emptyset,\Sigma^{*}\}
contains some
x
and omits some

y
, yet
O_{\bot}(x)=O_{\bot}(y)=\star
, so no system receiving only

O_{\bot}(x)
can decide
L
correctly on both inputs.
∎

3
Theory of observers

Definition 3.1

(Observer)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite alphabet and
S
a set (the
observation
space
). An
observer
is a function
O:\Sigma^{*}\to S
.
A
computational system with observer
O
is a pair
(M,O)

where
M
is any computational model that receives
O(x)
as its entire
input in place of
x
. The language recognised by
(M,O)
is

L(M,O)=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid M\text{ accepts }O(x)\}.

Every observer
O
induces an equivalence relation on
\Sigma^{*}
:

x\sim_{O}y\;\iff\;O(x)=O(y).

Definition 3.2

(
O
-saturation)
.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is
O
-saturated
if

x\sim_{O}y\;\Rightarrow\;(x\in L\iff y\in L)

for all
x,y\in\Sigma^{*}
. Equivalently,
L
is a union of

\sim_{O}
-equivalence classes. When
S
is infinite we fix a canonical
injective encoding
\mathrm{enc}:S\to\Sigma^{*}
and interpret

\sim_{O}
as the relation induced on the encoded outputs

\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
.

Example 3.3

(
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated language)
.

The language
L=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}\mid|x|_{0}=|x|_{1}\}
depends only
on
\mathrm{prof}(x)=(|x|_{0},|x|_{1})
. If
O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x)=O_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)
then
|x|_{0}=|y|_{0}
and
|x|_{1}=|y|_{1}
, so

x\in L\iff y\in L
. Hence
L
is
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated.

Example 3.4

(Non-
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated language)
.

The language
L_{1}=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}\mid 01\sqsubseteq x\}
is not

O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated. The strings
01
and
10
satisfy

O_{\mathrm{prof}}(01)=O_{\mathrm{prof}}(10)=(1,1)
, yet
01\in L_{1}

(the subsequence
01
is present) and
10\notin L_{1}
(the only
length-
2
subsequence of
10
is
10
itself).

Definition 3.5

(Induced language class)
.

\mathcal{L}(O)\coloneqq\{L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid L\text{ is }O\text{-saturated (Definition\penalty\ \ref{def:saturation})}\}.

Convention 3.6

(Encoding for infinite observation spaces)
.

When
S
is infinite (e.g.
S=\mathbb{N}^{k}
for
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
, or

S=\mathcal{P}(\Sigma^{\leq k})
for
O_{k}
), we fix a canonical
injective binary encoding
\mathrm{enc}:S\to\{0,1\}^{*}
with explicit
separators. All machines receiving
O(x)
are standard Turing machines
operating on
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
. The cost of encoding and decoding is
at most polynomial in
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
and does not affect the
polynomial-time separations in Section
9
.

Definition 3.7

(Observational order)
.

Given two observers
O_{1}:\Sigma^{*}\to S_{1}
and

O_{2}:\Sigma^{*}\to S_{2}
, we say that
O_{1}
is
less powerful

than
O_{2}
, written
O_{1}\preceq O_{2}
, if there exists a function

f:S_{2}\to S_{1}
such that
O_{1}=f\circ O_{2}
. That is, every piece
of information
O_{1}
can deliver is derivable from what
O_{2}
delivers.

Proposition 3.8
.

The relation
\preceq
is a preorder on observers. It becomes a partial
order when quotienting by the equivalence

O_{1}\simeq O_{2}\iff O_{1}\preceq O_{2}\land O_{2}\preceq O_{1}
.

Proof.

Reflexivity
:
O\preceq O
via
f=\mathrm{id}
.

Transitivity
: if
O_{1}=f\circ O_{2}
and
O_{2}=g\circ O_{3}
,
then
O_{1}=(f\circ g)\circ O_{3}
.

Antisymmetry in the quotient
:
[O_{1}]\preceq[O_{2}]
and

[O_{2}]\preceq[O_{1}]
give
O_{1}\simeq O_{2}
, hence
[O_{1}]=[O_{2}]
.
∎

Convention 3.9
.

We identify observers up to
\simeq
throughout. In particular,

\mathcal{L}(O_{1})=\mathcal{L}(O_{2})
whenever
O_{1}\simeq O_{2}
: applying
Proposition
3.10
to both
O_{1}\preceq O_{2}
and

O_{2}\preceq O_{1}
gives equality.

Proposition 3.10

(Monotonicity)
.

O_{1}\preceq O_{2}\;\Rightarrow\;\mathcal{L}(O_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(O_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
L\in\mathcal{L}(O_{1})
, so
L
is
O_{1}
-saturated
(Definition
3.2
). If
O_{2}(x)=O_{2}(y)
, then

O_{1}(x)=f(O_{2}(x))=f(O_{2}(y))=O_{1}(y)
, so
x\sim_{O_{1}}y
.
By
O_{1}
-saturation of
L
,
x\in L\iff y\in L
.
Hence
L
is
O_{2}
-saturated, giving
L\in\mathcal{L}(O_{2})
.
∎

Definition 3.11

(Extremes of the order)
.

•

The
trivial observer

O_{\bot}:x\mapsto\star
(constant)
satisfies
O_{\bot}\preceq O
for every
O
.

\mathcal{L}(O_{\bot})=\{\emptyset,\Sigma^{*}\}
.

•

The
complete observer

O_{\top}:x\mapsto x
(identity)
satisfies
O\preceq O_{\top}
for every
O
.

\mathcal{L}(O_{\top})
is the class of all languages.

Remark 3.12

(Connection with information theory)
.

Proposition
3.10
has the form of the data processing
inequality. In the stochastic setting, in which observers map strings to
probability distributions
O:\Sigma^{*}\to\Delta(S)
, the order

\preceq
becomes
channel degradation
in the sense of
Blackwell
[
1
]
: a channel
P
is degraded with respect to

Q
if
P(\cdot\mid x)=\int K(\cdot\mid y)\,\mathrm{d}Q(y\mid x)

for some stochastic kernel
K
, the stochastic analogue of
O_{1}=f\circ O_{2}
. The deterministic observers of this paper are the limit in which
P

and
Q
are point masses; the general theory of
[
3
]
provides
the informational bridge. The stochastic case is an open direction
(Section
7
).

Having isolated
O
-saturation as the structural property induced by
observational equivalence (Definition
3.2
), we can now
develop the observational hierarchy and, in Section
9
,
the complexity theoretic framework built upon it.

4
The observational hierarchy

Definition 4.1

(Canonical observers)
.

Let
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
.

(i)

O_{\bot}(x)=\star
(trivial observer)

(ii)

O_{\mathrm{len}}(x)=|x|
(length only)

(iii)

O_{\mathrm{par}}(x)=(|x|_{0}\bmod 2,\,|x|_{1}\bmod 2)

(parities)

(iv)

O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x)=(|x|_{0},|x|_{1})
(full profile)

(v)

O_{k}(x)=\text{set of subsequences of }x\text{ of length}\leq k
(
k\geq 1
)

(vi)

O_{\top}(x)=x
(complete observer)

Remark 4.2
.

The restriction to
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
is without loss of generality for
all structural results in this section. The order relations among

O_{\bot}
,
O_{\mathrm{len}}
,
O_{\mathrm{par}}
,
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
,

O_{\top}
extend immediately to any finite alphabet

\Sigma=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
by replacing
(|x|_{0},|x|_{1})
with

(|x|_{a_{1}},\dots,|x|_{a_{k}})
; the separating witnesses carry over
with the same arguments.

Proposition 4.3

(Profile sub-lattice)
.

The following strict orderings hold:

\displaystyle O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{len}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}

(length branch)

\displaystyle O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{par}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}

(parity branch)

and
O_{\mathrm{len}}
and
O_{\mathrm{par}}
are incomparable:

O_{\mathrm{len}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{par}}
and

O_{\mathrm{par}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{len}}
.

Proof.

Length branch.

O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{len}}
.

Inclusion: via
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{\star\}
constant.
Strictness:
L=\{x\mid|x|\text{ even}\}
is in

\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{len}})\setminus\mathcal{L}(O_{\bot})
.

O_{\mathrm{len}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.

Inclusion: via
f(c_{0},c_{1})=c_{0}+c_{1}
, recovering
|x|
.
Strictness:
L=\{x\mid|x|_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}\}
satisfies

O_{\mathrm{len}}(01)=O_{\mathrm{len}}(11)=2
while
01\notin L

(
|01|_{1}=1
, odd) and
11\in L
(
|11|_{1}=2
, even), so

L\notin\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{len}})
.

O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}
.

Inclusion: via
f(x)=\mathrm{prof}(x)
.
Strictness:
L=0^{*}1^{*}
satisfies
\mathrm{prof}(01)=\mathrm{prof}(10)=(1,1)

while
01\in L
and
10\notin L

(Definition
3.2
), so
L\notin\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{prof}})
.

Parity branch.

O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{par}}
.

Inclusion: via the constant map. Strictness:
\{x\mid|x|_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}\}\in\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{par}})\setminus\mathcal{L}(O_{\bot})
.

O_{\mathrm{par}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.

Inclusion: via
f(c_{0},c_{1})=(c_{0}\bmod 2,c_{1}\bmod 2)
.
Strictness: let
L=\{x\mid|x|_{1}\geq 3\}
. The strings
011

and
01111
satisfy
O_{\mathrm{par}}(011)=(1,0)
(since

|011|_{0}=1
,
|011|_{1}=2
) and
O_{\mathrm{par}}(01111)=(1,0)

(since
|01111|_{0}=1
,
|01111|_{1}=4
), yet
011\notin L

(
|011|_{1}=2<3
) and
01111\in L
(
|01111|_{1}=4\geq 3
).
Hence
L
is not
O_{\mathrm{par}}
-saturated
(Definition
3.2
), so
L\notin\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{par}})
.

O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}
.

As in the length branch.

Incomparability
O_{\mathrm{len}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{par}}

and
O_{\mathrm{par}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{len}}
.

O_{\mathrm{par}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{len}}
.

O_{\mathrm{len}}(01)=O_{\mathrm{len}}(11)=2
, but

O_{\mathrm{par}}(01)=(1,1)\neq(0,0)=O_{\mathrm{par}}(11)
.
No
f
with
O_{\mathrm{par}}=f\circ O_{\mathrm{len}}
can exist.

O_{\mathrm{len}}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{par}}
.

For any
j\geq 0
,
O_{\mathrm{par}}(0^{2j})=O_{\mathrm{par}}(0^{2j+2})=(0,0)
, but

O_{\mathrm{len}}(0^{2j})=2j\neq 2j+2=O_{\mathrm{len}}(0^{2j+2})
. No such
f
exists.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Subsequence branch)
.

O_{\bot}\prec O_{1}\prec O_{2}\prec\cdots\prec O_{\top}
.

Proof.

O_{\bot}\prec O_{1}
.

O_{\bot}(0)=O_{\bot}(\varepsilon)=\star

while
O_{1}(0)\neq O_{1}(\varepsilon)
, so the inclusion
\mathcal{L}(O_{\bot})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(O_{1})
is strict.

O_{k}\prec O_{k+1}
for every
k\geq 1
.

The language
L_{k}=\{x\mid 0^{k+1}\sqsubseteq x\}
is in

\mathcal{L}(O_{k+1})
, since membership is determined directly by
O_{k+1}(x)
.
It is not in
\mathcal{L}(O_{k})
: every subsequence of
0^{k}
of length
\leq k

is some
0^{j}
with
j\leq k
, which is also a subsequence of
0^{k+1}
,
and vice versa, so
O_{k}(0^{k})=O_{k}(0^{k+1})
. Yet
0^{k}\notin L_{k}

and
0^{k+1}\in L_{k}
, so
L_{k}
is not
O_{k}
-saturated
(Definition
3.2
).

O_{k}\prec O_{\top}
for every finite
k
.

Inclusion via
f(x)=O_{k}(x)
. Strictness:
O_{k}
identifies
0^{n}1^{n}

with
0^{m}1^{m}
for all
n,m>k
, since both strings contain the same
set of subsequences of length
\leq k
. Hence

\{0^{n}1^{n}\mid n\geq 0\}\notin\mathcal{L}(O_{k})
for any finite
k
,
while
O_{\top}
recognises it.
∎

Proposition 4.5

(Cross-branch incomparability)
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
and
O_{k}
are incomparable.
Consequently,
O_{\mathrm{len}}
and
O_{\mathrm{par}}
are also
incomparable with every
O_{k}
.

Proof.

O_{k}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.

The strings
x=0^{k}1
and
y=10^{k}
satisfy

\mathrm{prof}(x)=\mathrm{prof}(y)=(k,1)
, so any
f
with

O_{k}=f\circ O_{\mathrm{prof}}
would require
O_{k}(x)=O_{k}(y)
.
But
0^{k}1
is a subsequence of
x
of length
k+1
and not of
y

(since in
y
the symbol
1
precedes all zeros), so

O_{k}(x)\neq O_{k}(y)
: no such
f
exists.

O_{\mathrm{prof}}\not\preceq O_{k}
.

O_{k}(0^{k+1})=O_{k}(0^{k+2})
(same subsequences of zeros of length

\leq k
), but
O_{\mathrm{prof}}(0^{k+1})=(k+1,0)\neq(k+2,0)=O_{\mathrm{prof}}(0^{k+2})
: no
f
with

O_{\mathrm{prof}}=f\circ O_{k}
exists.

O_{\mathrm{len}}
incomparable with every
O_{k}
.

O_{k}\not\preceq O_{\mathrm{len}}
:
O_{\mathrm{len}}(0)=O_{\mathrm{len}}(1)=1
but
O_{1}(0)\neq O_{1}(1)
.

O_{\mathrm{len}}\not\preceq O_{k}
:
O_{k}(0^{k+1})=O_{k}(0^{k+2})

but

Connection with Simon’s hierarchy

5.1
Piecewise testable languages

Definition 5.1

(Subsequence)
.

A string

of

)
if

can be obtained by deleting symbols from

.

Definition 5.2

(Simon’s preorder)
.

x\leq_{k}y\;\iff\;\forall\,u\in\Sigma^{\leq k},\;u\sqsubseteq x\Rightarrow u\sqsubseteq y.

The corresponding equivalence is

x\sim_{k}y\iff x\leq_{k}y\land y\leq_{k}x
.

Remark 5.3
.

The relation
\sim_{k}
coincides exactly with
\sim_{O_{k}}
: the family

\{O_{k}\}
is the computational realisation of Simon’s preorder.

Definition 5.4

(

.

if

.

5.2
Simon’s theorem

Theorem 5.5

(Simon, 1975
[
8
]
)
.

A regular language

is piecewise testable if and only if its
syntactic monoid is
\mathcal{J}
-trivial.

Corollary 5.6

(Observational reformulation)
.

A regular language
L
is in
\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{L}(O_{k})
if and only if its
syntactic monoid is
\mathcal{J}
-trivial.

Remark 5.7
.

Corollary

makes explicit that membership in the piecewise testable class is equivalent to

-decidability for some

, a fact that the algebraic characterisation leaves implicit.
For the algebraic theory of syntactic monoids and varieties, see

[

]
.

Theorem 5.8

(Separation of Simon’s hierarchy
[
8
]
)
.

\mathcal{L}(O_{k})\subsetneq\mathcal{L}(O_{k+1})

Proof.

The language

\mathcal{L}(O_{k+1})

(same subsequences of length

and

is not

-saturated

).
∎

with respect to Simon’s

\mathcal{L}(O_{\mathrm{prof}})

Let

,

Remark 6.1

(Parikh’s theorem)

states that for every

and

are

-profile-equivalent: no observer

can determine from

. This shows that

Theorem 6.2

(Incomparability)
.

\mathcal{L}_{\pi}
is strictly incomparable with
\mathbf{REG}
.

Proof.

\mathcal{L}_{\pi}\not\subseteq\mathbf{REG}
: the language

L=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}\mid|x|_{0}=|x|_{1}\}
is permutation-closed
(Example

) but not regular (pumping
lemma
[
9
]
).

\mathbf{REG}\not\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\pi}
:

is regular but not permutation-closed, since
\mathrm{prof}(01)=\mathrm{prof}(10)=(1,1)
while

01\in 0^{*}1^{*}
and
10\notin 0^{*}1^{*}
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

The observational hierarchy and the Chomsky hierarchy are independent
axes of language classification:

regular and
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated:

\{x\mid|x|_{1}\text{ even}\}

regular and not
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated:

non-regular and

-saturated:

\{x\mid|x|_{0}=|x|_{1}\}

-saturated:

(Universal incomparability)
.

For every observer

Observational complexity

Definition 7.1

(Observational complexity)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fixed family of observers. The

with respect to

when the minimum (in the partial order

minimum exists, the observational complexity is the antichain of minimal
observers in
\{O\in\mathcal{F}\mid L\in\mathcal{L}(O)\}
.

Proposition 7.2

(Examples)
.

In the family
\mathcal{F}=\{O_{\bot},O_{\mathrm{len}},O_{\mathrm{par}},O_{\mathrm{prof}},O_{1},O_{2},\dots,O_{\top}\}

(i)

\mathrm{OC}(\Sigma^{*})=\mathrm{OC}(\emptyset)=O_{\bot}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{OC}(\{x\mid|x|\text{ even}\})=O_{\mathrm{len}}

(iii)

\mathrm{OC}(\{x\mid|x|_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{2}\})=O_{\mathrm{par}}

(iv)

\mathrm{OC}(\{x\mid|x|_{0}=|x|_{1}\})=O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.

(v)

\mathrm{OC}(0^{*}1^{*})=O_{\top}
.

(Computational separation)

Since

with

but
O_{1}(x)=O_{1}(y)

Let

Then

-saturated (Definition

Under observer

∎

Corollary 7.5

(Structural limitation)
.

There exist languages that a Turing machine with observer

8
Physical interpretation

The framework admits a natural physical reading. In statistical mechanics, a macroscopic observer has access only to aggregate quantities
such as temperature, pressure, and mean density. Formally, such an
observer is a function
O_{\mathrm{macro}}:H\to\Gamma
where
H
is
the space of microscopic histories and
\Gamma
is the space of macrostates. Identifying
H=\Sigma^{*}
and
\Gamma=\mathbb{N}^{k}
, the
macroscopic observer coincides exactly with
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.

Corollary 8.1

(Observational limitation)
.

Let
O
be an observer with
O\preceq O_{\mathrm{prof}}
. Then
O

cannot distinguish any microscopic history
h
from any of its
permutations. Every physical property decidable by
O
is invariant
under permutation of events.

Proof.

If
O\preceq O_{\mathrm{prof}}
, then
O=f\circ O_{\mathrm{prof}}

for some
f
, so
O(x)=f(\mathrm{prof}(x))=f(\mathrm{prof}(y))=O(y)
whenever

\mathrm{prof}(x)=\mathrm{prof}(y)
, i.e. whenever
x
and
y
are permutations of
each other. Hence every
O
-saturated language
(Definition
3.2
) is permutation-closed.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

Corollary
8.1
is a restatement of
permutation-closure in physical language. The physical interpretation,
that thermodynamic irreversibility is an observational property of a
macroscopic observer structurally blind to microscopic order, is a
reading of the corollary, not a consequence derived from physical axioms.
A rigorous derivation of thermodynamic irreversibility from this framework
would require additional structure: a probability measure on microscopic
histories, a dynamics preserving the macroscopic observer, and an entropy
functional compatible with
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
.
These extensions are open directions (i) and (iii) in
Section
7
.

9
Observer-parametrised complexity classes

Classical complexity theory implicitly assumes the observer is always
O_{\top}
. The observational hierarchy makes this assumption explicit and
allows it to be relaxed.

Remark 9.1

(Computational status of observers)
.

An observer
O:\Sigma^{*}\to S
is a fixed total preprocessing map applied once to the input before any machine computation begins. It does
not answer queries, does not interact with the machine, and does not extend the computational model. All machines in this paper are standard
Turing machines over
\Sigma^{*}
; receiving
O(x)
means receiving

\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
on the input tape (Convention
3.6
).
The observer only reduces available information and never increases
computational power.

A language
L
that is not
O
-saturated (Definition
3.2
)
is not merely hard under observer
O
: it is not well-posed in the
observation space of
O
. If
x\sim_{O}y
but
x\in L
and
y\notin L
,
then any machine receiving only
O(x)
cannot decide correctly on both inputs, regardless of computational resources. This is
structural
blindness
, not computational hardness.

Lemma 9.2

(Simulation for computable observers)
.

Let
O:\Sigma^{*}\to S
be computable in time
s(|x|)
, where the time bound includes the steps required to write
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
on the
output tape, so that
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|=O(s(|x|))
. If a Turing
machine decides a language
L
on input
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
in time
p(|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|)
for some polynomial
p
, then there exists a
Turing machine deciding
L
on input
x
in time

q(|x|)=s(|x|)+p(O(s(|x|)))+O(1),

which is polynomial in
|x|
whenever
s
is polynomial. In particular,
\mathbf{P}_{O}\subseteq\mathbf{P}
for every observer computable in
polynomial time.

Proof sketch.

On input
x
, the simulating machine first computes
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
in time
s(|x|)
; the output length satisfies

|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|=O(s(|x|))
by the time bound on the
computation. It then runs the given machine on
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
,
which takes time
p(|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|)=p(O(s(|x|)))
. The total time
q(|x|)=s(|x|)+p(O(s(|x|)))+O(1)
is polynomial in
|x|

whenever
s
is polynomial. Full proof in Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 9.3

(On the boldness of this formalisation)
.

We are aware that Definition
9.4
below is a non-standard step: defining complexity classes parametrised by an observer opens a
two-dimensional landscape in which
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{NP}
is one
point. We offer it because the calculations compel us to. The
incomparability results of Section
4
, the orthogonality
with the Chomsky hierarchy in Section
6
, and the
collapse of
\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}
established below are all concrete, verifiable facts. They do not prove anything about the classical

\mathbf{P}
vs
\mathbf{NP}
question, and we do not claim they do. What they show is that the distinction between hardness and blindness is
real and measurable.

Definition 9.4

(
\mathbf{P}_{O}
and
\mathbf{NP}_{O}
)
.

Fix an observer
O:\Sigma^{*}\to S
.

•

\mathbf{P}_{O}
is the set of languages decidable in time
polynomial in
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
by a deterministic machine
receiving
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
as its entire input.

•

\mathbf{NP}_{O}
is the set of languages for which there exists
a nondeterministic machine that, receiving
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))

and a certificate of length polynomial in
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
,
verifies membership in time polynomial in
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
.

Remark 9.5
.

The complexity is measured with respect to
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
, the
size of the input actually received by the machine, not
|x|
. For
O=O_{\top}
these coincide. For
O=O_{\mathrm{prof}}
on a binary
alphabet,
\mathrm{enc}(O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x))
encodes the pair
(|x|_{0},|x|_{1})
in binary, giving

|\mathrm{enc}(O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x))|=O(\log|x|)
, strictly smaller than
|x|
.

The machine receives
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
as given and is not required
to compute
O(x)
from
x
. When reducing
\mathbf{P}_{O}
to
\mathbf{P}
,
however, one must construct a standard TM that first computes
O(x)
from

x
and then runs the given machine: this requires
O
to be computable,
as formalised in Lemma
9.2
.
All canonical observers of Definition
4.1
are
computable; in particular,
O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x)=(|x|_{0},|x|_{1})
is
computed by a single scan of
x
.

Proposition 9.6

(Boundary cases)
.

(i)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\top}}=\mathbf{P}
and

\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\top}}=\mathbf{NP}
.

(ii)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\bot}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\bot}}=\{\emptyset,\Sigma^{*}\}
.

(iii)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}
,
consisting exactly of the
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturated languages

for which

is computable in time polynomial in

|\mathrm{enc}(O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x))|
.

Proof.

(i)
O_{\top}(x)=x
, so a machine in
\mathbf{P}_{O_{\top}}
receives
x

directly and runs in time polynomial in
|x|
: this is
\mathbf{P}
.
The same for
\mathbf{NP}
.

(ii) The observation space of
O_{\bot}
is the singleton
\{\star\}
:
every input maps to the same value. A deterministic or nondeterministic
machine receiving always
\star
either accepts all strings or none.

(iii) A machine receiving
\mathrm{enc}(O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x))\in\{0,1\}^{*}
has a complete description of the symbol counts. Membership in
L
depends (by
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
-saturation) only on this vector.
The machine reads its entire input in time linear in
|\mathrm{enc}(O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x))|
and evaluates the membership
predicate. There is no combinatorial structure remaining to search over, the observer has performed the only aggregation possible, so
nondeterminism adds nothing. Hence
\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}
.
∎

Proposition 9.7

(Position of
\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}
)
.

(i)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}\subseteq\mathbf{P}
.

(ii)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}\subsetneq\mathbf{P}
.

(iii)

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}\subsetneq\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

(i)
By Lemma

O_{\mathrm{prof}}

applies and gives

Concretely: given

be a TM deciding

on input

in time

that on input

Remark 9.8

(Implications of the structural collapse)
.

Proposition

(a) Non-triviality.

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}
is a genuine intermediate class:
nonempty and strictly below
\mathbf{P}
.

(b) Separation of hardness from blindness.

The collapse

. The proof makes no assumption about

\mathbf{P}
vs
\mathbf{NP}
and implies nothing about it. The collapse

(c) Sublinear containment.

Since

O_{\mathrm{prof}}
receives a logarithmically
compressed summary. The total runtime of the simulation in Proposition

O(|x|)

cost of computing
O_{\mathrm{prof}}(x)
, while the decision itself takes
o(|x|)
. The collapse occurs strictly below

in
terms of the original input size.

(d) Conditional position in the
\mathbf{P}
vs
\mathbf{NP}

landscape.

Proposition
9.7

\mathbf{P}\neq\mathbf{NP}
(a hypothesis

Under this conditional hypothesis, every language a nondeterministic
verifier with observer

can certify is already
decidable in polynomial time by a deterministic machine with full input
access. The reason is not that nondeterminism loses its power, but that

O_{\mathrm{prof}}
cannot see the combinatorial structure that makes
problems hard in the first place.

10
Conclusion

We have introduced the observational hierarchy as a new axis of
classification for formal languages, independent of the Chomsky
hierarchy. The hierarchy has the structure of a partial order with a
diamond-shaped profile sub-lattice comprising the length branch

O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{len}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}

and the parity branch

O_{\bot}\prec O_{\mathrm{par}}\prec O_{\mathrm{prof}}\prec O_{\top}
,
with
O_{\mathrm{len}}
and
O_{\mathrm{par}}
incomparable at the
intermediate level, and an infinite subsequence branch

O_{\bot}\prec O_{1}\prec O_{2}\prec\cdots\prec O_{\top}
.

We have shown that computational limitations depend not only on the
power of the machine, but on the structure of the observer. There exist
languages that no Turing machine can recognise when equipped with a
sufficiently weak observer, but that a DFA recognises with a more
powerful observer. The power of the machine cannot compensate for the
weakness of the observer.

The framework extends to complexity theory through

\mathbf{P}_{O}
and
\mathbf{NP}_{O}
. The central distinction is between
computational hardness (which depends on the machine) and structural
blindness (which depends on what the observer has discarded). These are
two independent phenomena. In particular,

\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}=\mathbf{NP}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}

holds as a structural collapse strictly inside
\mathbf{P}

(Proposition
9.7
): it is not a resolution of the
classical
\mathbf{P}
vs
\mathbf{NP}
question, but evidence that
blindness and hardness are genuinely different phenomena.

Open directions.

(i)

Probabilistic observers
: stochastic channels

O:\Sigma^{*}\to\Delta(S)
, where
\preceq
becomes Blackwell
degradation (Section
3
sketches the connection).

(ii)

Composed observers
: categorical structure of observer
composition, its relation to forgetful functors, and the
connection between the product of two observers and their join in
the observational lattice.

(iii)

Observational complexity and information
: formalising the
connection between the observational complexity of a language and
the amount of information the observer must preserve, via the
data processing inequality.

(iv)

Intermediate observers
: algebraic characterisation of
classes induced by observers that combine subsequence and count
information, lying strictly between the profile sub-lattice and
the subsequence branch.

(v)

Profile sub-lattice completion
: characterisation of all
observers strictly between
O_{\bot}
and
O_{\mathrm{prof}}
,
incomparable with both
O_{\mathrm{len}}
and
O_{\mathrm{par}}
.

(vi)

Sublinear complexity
: whether
\mathbf{P}_{O_{\mathrm{prof}}}

coincides with a known sublinear complexity class, and how the
observational axis interacts with the fine structure of complexity
below
\mathbf{P}
(Remark
9.8
(c)).

Remark 10.1

Directions (ii) and (iii) are closely related: a categorical

Proof of Lemma
9.2

This appendix gives the full formal proof of the simulation lemma stated
and sketched in Section
9
.

Full proof of Lemma
9.2
.

Let
O:\Sigma^{*}\to S
be computable in time
s(|x|)
where
s
is a
function such that the computation halts after at most
s(|x|)
steps
and writes
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
on a dedicated output tape.
Since a Turing machine can write at most one symbol per step, the output
length satisfies
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|\leq s(|x|)
, hence

|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|=O(s(|x|))
.

Let
M
be a TM that decides
L
on input
\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
in time

p(|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|)
for some polynomial
p
. Construct the
simulating machine
M^{\prime}
as follows. On input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

1.

Run the computation of
O
on
x
to produce

y=\mathrm{enc}(O(x))
. This takes at most
s(|x|)
steps.

2.

Run
M
on
y
. This takes at most
p(|y|)
steps.

3.

Output whatever
M
outputs.

The total time for
M^{\prime}
on
x
is at most
s(|x|)+p(|y|)+O(1)
.
Since
|y|=|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|\leq s(|x|)
, we have

p(|y|)\leq p(s(|x|))
, giving total time

q(|x|)=s(|x|)+p(s(|x|))+O(1)
.
When
s
is a polynomial,
q
is also a polynomial in
|x|
, so

M^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}
witnesses
L\in\mathbf{P}
.

The conclusion
\mathbf{P}_{O}\subseteq\mathbf{P}
follows: every

L\in\mathbf{P}_{O}
has a TM
M
deciding it under
O
in polynomial
time in
|\mathrm{enc}(O(x))|
. The construction above produces
M^{\prime}

deciding
L
under
O_{\top}
in polynomial time in
|x|
.
∎
