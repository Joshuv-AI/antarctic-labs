On the consistency of the Domain of Dependence cut cell stabilization
Gunnar Birke \orcidID 0000-0002-2008-6679, Christian Engwer \orcidID 0000-0002-6041-8228, Jan Giesselmann \orcidID 0009-0008-0217-7244 and Sandra May \orcidID 0000-0001-9178-6595

Abstract
So called cartesian cut cell meshes provide efficient ways to generate meshes but do require tailored numerical methods to not suffer from stabilization issues, especially in the hyperbolic regime where the application of explicit time stepping schemes is common. In this scenario, due to potentially arbitrarily small cut cells, an infeasible restriction is imposed on the time step size. The Domain of Dependence (DoD) stabilization allows for a time step size based on the underlying Cartesian mesh. Being an extension of a discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method, one would expect similar accuracy properties as in the pure DG case. While numerical results do support this expectation, on the analytical level this has only been investigated thoroughly for k = 0 k=0 . Error analysis typically hinges on a consistency result. In this contribution we prove such a result for the DoD stabilization given an arbitrary polynomial degree and an exact solution of sufficient regularity. This in turn could open the way towards a more refined analysis of the method even in the high-order case.



1
Introduction

Meshing of complex geometries for numerical simulations poses a significant challenge. High-accuracy requirements on the requested mesh can impose serious costs when unstructured representations are used. Cartesian cut-cell meshes provide a similar level of accuracy by embedding objects of interest in a cartesian mesh and simply cutting them out, leading to a more efficient meshing procedure. However, this does not come for free, since the cutting process gives up most guaranties on mesh cell properties such as shape regularity and quasi-uniformity. Consequently, numerical methods on cut-cell meshes need to handle cells that are arbitrarily small or highly anisotropic in a special way. In the context of hyperbolic problems explicit time-stepping methods are often employed, where tiny cut-cells lead to prohibitively small time steps, rendering classical scheme infeasible. This is often called the
small cell problem
.

There are essentially two approaches to overcome the
small cell problem
. One option is to adjust the mesh and merge small cells with large neighbors. This is often called
cell merging
/
cell agglomeration
and is for example investigated in
cell-merging-1
;
cell-merging-2
;
cell-merging-3
;
cell-merging-4
.

The alternative is to develop schemes that allow a time step size based on the original cartesian mesh even in the presence of small cut cells. Over the years a variety of such schemes have emerged, often based on a finite volume (FV) or discontinuous Galerkin (DG) scheme that are extended by additional stabilization terms. Examples are the flux redistribution method
flux-redist-2
, the
h-box
method
h-box-1
;
h-box-2
, dimensional-split schemes
dim-split-1
;
dim-split-2
,
mixed implicit explicit
schemes
mixed-exp-imp
, an
active flux
method on cut cells
active-cut
, the
state redistribution
method
srd-1
;
srd-2
, the
Domain of Dependence (DoD)
stabilization
dod-1
;
dod-2
and
ghost-cell
methods in the hyperbolic context
ghost-cell-1
;
ghost-cell-2
.

Proving error estimates for these methods is delicate, one reason being the often very technical constructions due to missing geometric guaranties on the cut-cells. To our knowledge, in the hyperbolic case on cut-cell meshes such error analysis is mostly restricted to 1D, e.g.
h-box-1
;
dim-split-2
;
mixed-exp-imp-2
;
ghost-cell-1
;
ghost-cell-2
. In
dod-error-estimate
the authors were able to prove a two-dimensional result for the linear advection equation based on the DoD stabilization, although only for a first-order method. Unfortunately, the consistency result, underlying the final proof in
dod-error-estimate
does not allow a straightforward generalization to high-order discretizations.

Our goal in this work is not to prove a complete error estimate but rather to focus on a generalized consistency result, extending to high-order schemes, under the assumption of sufficient regularity.

The article is organized as follows: We will first state the (linear) PDEs that we are considering in this work and introduce some mesh related terms and the unstabilized DG scheme. Then we will define so called extension operators, one of the key building-blocks of the DoD stabilization,
on the discrete space and derive their extension onto the analytical solution space. Finally we give consistency results for two different versions of the DoD stabilization.

2.1
Base discretization

The final method is based on a classical DG discretization and is augmented with an additional stabilization.
We start with a rectangular domain
\widehat{\Omega}\supset\Omega
and a so-called background mesh
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
of
\widehat{\Omega}
, a structured Cartesian mesh
of mesh size
h
,
such that
\bigcup_{\widehat{E}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{h}}\overline{\widehat{E}}=\overline{\widehat{\Omega}}
.
Based on this we construct a cut-cell mesh on
\Omega
consisting of elements

\mathcal{M}_{h}=\{E=\widehat{E}\cap\Omega:\widehat{E}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\},

on which we define a discontinuous Galerkin space

\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\coloneqq\{v_{h}\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{m}\;\big|\;\forall E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}:(v_{h})_{E}\coloneqq(v_{h})_{|E}\in P^{r}(E)^{m}\}.

To formulate our numerical schemes on this space we will need additional notation. Let the set of internal and boundary faces be given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\gamma=\bar{E}_{1}\cap\bar{E}_{2}:|\gamma|>0,E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\gamma=\bar{E}\cap\partial\Omega:E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}\},\quad\Gamma_{h}\coloneqq\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}\cup\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h},

where
|\gamma|
denotes the 1D volume (length).
Given an element
E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
we denote its sets of
internal, boundary, and all faces by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}:\gamma\cap\partial E\neq\emptyset\},\quad\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}:\gamma\cap\partial E\neq\emptyset\},

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{h}(E)\coloneqq\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)\cup\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E).

We further define the index-set
\mathbb{I}(E)=\{1,\ldots,|\Gamma_{h}(E)|\}
, consecutively numbering all faces, such that any
i\in\mathbb{I}(E)
identifies exactly one face
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{h}(E)
. If
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)
is an internal face we denote by
E_{i}
the corresponding neighbor element. If
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)
and there is no physical neighbor at this face, we will abuse notation by still writing
E_{i}
and using this as an index variable later.

For each internal face
\gamma=\bar{E}_{1}\cap\bar{E}_{2}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
we fix a unique left-sided cell
E_{\gamma}^{L}\in\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
. The corresponding right-sided cell will be denoted by
E_{\gamma}^{R}
.
The unit normal vector
n_{\gamma}
on
\gamma
is uniquely defined to point from left to right, i.e.,
n_{\gamma}=n_{E_{\gamma}^{L}}|_{\gamma}
.

Given a face
\gamma=\bar{E}_{\gamma}^{L}\cap\bar{E}_{\gamma}^{R}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
and a function
w
defined on
E_{\gamma}^{L}\cup E_{\gamma}^{R}
such that both
w|_{E_{\gamma}^{L}}
and
w|_{E_{\gamma}^{R}}
have well-defined traces on
\gamma
, we define left and right values of
w
as

w_{\gamma}^{L}\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}_{\gamma}(w|_{E_{\gamma}^{L}}),\;w_{\gamma}^{R}\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}_{\gamma}(w|_{E_{\gamma}^{R}}),

and averages and jumps

\left\{\mskip-5.0mu\{{w}\right\}\mskip-5.0mu\}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(w^{L}_{\gamma}+w^{R}_{\gamma}),\quad\llbracket w\rrbracket_{\gamma}\coloneqq w^{L}_{\gamma}-w^{R}_{\gamma}.

In the DG discretization of the wave equation, the reflecting boundary condition
Eu=v\cdot n=0
in (
4
) is implemented weakly by reflecting state arguments of the numerical flux. Furthermore, the DoD stabilization requires a reflection of polynomial functions. We introduce different operators to handle both, abusing notation by using
\mathfrak{M}_{n}
to denote anyone of them.

Definition 1
(Mirroring operator)

Let a cell
E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
and a boundary face
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)
be given.
For a state
u=(p,v)^{T}
we define the state mirrored at
\gamma
component wise as

\mathfrak{M}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\begin{pmatrix}p\\
v\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}p\\
v-2(v\cdot n)n\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

Furthermore we denote the orthogonal projection of
x\in E
onto
\gamma
as
x^{\perp}
and

n
as the unit outer normal in
x^{\perp}
.
With this we define the generalized mirroring operator

\mathfrak{M}_{n}:\mathcal{P}^{r}(E)^{3}\to\mathcal{P}^{r}(E)^{3},\quad\bigg(x\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}p(x)\\
v(x)\end{pmatrix}\bigg)\mapsto\bigg(x\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}p(x)\\
v(x)-2(v(x^{\perp})\cdot n)n\end{pmatrix}\bigg),

(6)

which allows to evaluate
\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u)(x)~\forall x\in E
. Note that this definition coincides on
\gamma
with the definition of mirroring the state (
5
).

Let
u^{\mu},u^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be two states. We denote by
f^{S}_{n}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}[f_{n}(u^{\mu})+f_{n}(u^{\nu})]

the central flux and by
S_{n}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu})
a dissipative flux that is consistent, i. e.
S_{n}(u,u)=0
. For the linear advection equation (
3
) we will always pick
S_{n}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu})=\frac{|\beta\cdot n|}{2}(u^{\mu}-u^{\nu})
, leading to an upwind discretization. For the linear wave equation (
4
) we can, for example, pick
S_{n}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu})=\frac{c}{2}(u^{\mu}-u^{\nu})
.

Our base scheme reads: Find
u_{h}(t)\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
such that

\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle\partial_{t}u_{h}(t),w_{h}\rangle+a_{h}(u_{h}(t),w_{h})=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h},

(7)

where for the advection equation (
3
), with

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the euclidian innre product for state vectors,

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})=&-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}\int_{E}f(u_{h})\cdot\nabla w_{h}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}}\int_{\gamma}[f_{n}^{S}+S_{n}](u_{\gamma}^{L},u_{\gamma}^{R})\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\\
&+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}}\int_{\gamma}(\beta\cdot n)^{+}u_{h}w_{h},\end{split}

(8)

with
a^{+}=\frac{1}{2}(|a|-a)
beeing the positive part of a real number,
and for the wave equation (
4
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})=&-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}\int_{E}f(u_{h})\cdot\nabla w_{h}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}}\int_{\gamma}\langle[f_{n}^{S}+S_{n}](u_{\gamma}^{L},u_{\gamma}^{R}),\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\rangle\\
&+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}}\int_{\gamma}\langle[f_{n}^{S}+S_{n}](u_{h},\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u_{h})),w_{h}\rangle.\end{split}

(9)

Consistency of the base scheme is discussed, for example, in
error-estimate-2
and
error-estimate-1
.

3
Extension operators

One of the primary tools of the DoD method, introduced in
dod-1
, is the concept of extension operators:

Definition 2
(Extension operator)

Given a discrete function
w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
and a cell
{E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}
we introduce the extension operator

\mathcal{L}_{E}:\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to(P^{r}(\Omega))^{m},\quad w_{h}\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})

defined via
\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})(x)=w_{h}|_{E}(x),\;x\in E
, i.e., we select the polynomial components of
w_{h}
on
E
and extend them to the complete domain. This definition is well-posed since defining a polynomial function on an open domain is equivalent to defining it on the complete real space.

Definition 3
(Reflected extension operator)

Let
w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
be a discrete function,
E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
an element and
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}
a boundary face on which a reflecting wall boundary condition is applied.
We define the extension operator over this face
\gamma
as

\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\gamma}:\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to P^{r}(\Omega)^{3},\quad w_{h}\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\gamma}(w_{h})=\mathfrak{M}_{n_{\gamma}}(\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})).

(10)

From now on we will assume that the solution
u
of equation (
1
) lies in the space
V=H^{r+1}(\Omega)
where
r
is the polynomial degree of our discrete space
\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
. This regularity assumption will be crucial for our result. We further introduce the space

V^{\mathfrak{M}}=\{u\in V^{3}:\>\operatorname{tr}u-\mathfrak{M}_{n}(\operatorname{tr}u)=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}

(11)

which is the solution space to equation (
4
). The above application of the mirroring operator has to be understood pointwise, i. e.
\mathfrak{M}_{n}(\operatorname{tr}u)(x)=\mathfrak{M}_{n}(\operatorname{tr}u(x))
, see (
5
), and the equality in the sense of traces.

An important aspect of the proofs given in
error-estimate-1
and
error-estimate-2
is that the considered bilinear forms accept values in the space
V+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
in the first argument. This means that we
have to generalize the definition of the extension operator to this space
(or
V^{\mathfrak{M}}+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
in case of equation (
4
)). This
is achieved by mappings of the form

\displaystyle\text{id}+\mathcal{L}_{E}

\displaystyle:V+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to V+\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m},

(12a)

\displaystyle\text{id}+\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle:V^{\mathfrak{M}}+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to V^{\mathfrak{M}}+\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}

(12b)

where
\operatorname{id}
is the identity mapping on either
V
or
V^{\mathfrak{M}}
.
Existence and uniqueness of such mappings is given by the gluing Lemma of linear maps which we state here in the context of general function spaces:

Lemma 1

Let
X
and
T
be vector spaces,
U,W\subset X
subspaces,
f:U\to T
and
g:W\to T
be linear maps. There is a unique linear map

f+g:U+W\to T

such that
(f+g)_{|U}=f
and
(f+g)_{|W}=g
if and only if
f_{|U\cap W}=g_{|U\cap W}
, that is the two maps
f
and
g
agree on the intersection of
U
and
W
.

The other ingredient that we need is a connection between weak and strong differentiability of functions in our DG space. This statement can, for example, be found in
braess
:

Lemma 2

Let
k\geq 1
. A function
f:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
which is piecewise
C^{\infty}
is in
H^{k}(\Omega)
if and only if it is in
C^{k-1}(\bar{\Omega})
.

We can now show the following result.

Lemma 3

Let
r\geq 0
, then it holds

V\cap\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}=\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}.

(13)

Proof

Let
u\in V\cap\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
. Since
u\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
we know that
u
is defined on
\bar{\Omega}
and is piecewise
C^{\infty}
. Since
u\in V=H^{r+1}(\Omega)
we deduce from Lemma
2
that
u\in C^{r}(\Omega)
. Since
u
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
r
and has
r
continuous derivatives, we have
u\in\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}
.

Corollary 1

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
be a mesh cell. Then the mapping

\operatorname{id}+\mathcal{L}_{E}:V+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to V+\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}

(14)

is well-defined. If
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}
is a boundary face and
u
is a solution to equation (
4
), the mapping

\operatorname{id}+\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\gamma}:V^{\mathfrak{M}}+\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}\to V^{\mathfrak{M}}+\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}

(15)

is well-defined.

Proof

By Lemma
3
and the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{E}
is the identity on
\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}
the first statement holds true by Lemma
1
. For the second statement we first note that by Lemma
3

V^{\mathfrak{M}}\cap\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}=\{u\in\mathcal{P}^{r}(\Omega)^{m}:u-\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u)=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

Again we have that
\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\gamma}
is the identity on
V^{\mathfrak{M}}\cap\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
and we can apply Lemma
1
again to get the second statement.

4
Consistency of the Domain of Dependence stabilization

The DoD stabilization adds penalty terms on small cut cells to the base scheme (
7
). The general form is: Find
u_{h}(t)\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}
such that for all
w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}

\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{t}u_{h}(t),w_{h}\rangle+a_{h}(u_{h}(t),w_{h})+J_{h}(u_{h}(t),w_{h})=0.

(16)

Small cells that require stabilization will be collected in a subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{M}_{h}
. The (bilinear) form
J_{h}(u_{h}(t),w_{h})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{I}}J^{E}_{h}(u_{h}(t),w_{h})
is defined cell-wise. Concrete cell-stabilization terms will be introduced below. Assuming an already consistent base scheme it is enough to show that

J^{E}_{h}(u(t),w_{h})=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}

(17)

to prove consistency of the stabilized scheme.

4.1
Linear advection equation

To illustrate the application of Corollary
1
we first consider the DoD stabilization that was given in
dod-icosahom
for the linear advection equation (
3
). This formulation assumes that for any small cut cell
E
there is exactly one inflow face
E_{in}
and is given as:

Definition 4

Let
E\in\mathcal{I}
a small cut cell,
E_{\text{in}}
its only inflow face,
\eta_{E}\in[0,1]
.
The cell stabilization term for equation (
3
) is
J^{E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})=[J^{0,E}_{h}+J^{1,E}_{h}](u_{h},w_{h})
with

\displaystyle J^{0,E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=\eta_{E}\int_{\partial E}(\beta\cdot n)^{+}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{in}}(u_{h})-\mathcal{L}_{E}(u_{h}))\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket,

(18a)

\displaystyle J^{1,E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=\eta_{E}\int_{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{in}}(u_{h})-u_{h})\beta\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{L}_{E_{in}}(w_{h})-w_{h}).

(18b)

Proposition 1

Let
u
be the exact solution to equation (
3
). Then

J_{h}^{E}(u,w_{h})=0\quad\forall E\in\mathcal{I}\text{ and }\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}.

Proof

By Corollary
1
we know that
\mathcal{L}_{E_{in}}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{E}(u)=u
. Thus we have

J^{0,E}_{h}(u,w_{h})=\eta_{E}\int_{\partial E}(\beta\cdot n)^{+}(u-u)\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h},

and similarly for
J^{1,E}_{h}(u,w_{h})
.

4.2
Linear wave equation

The proof of consistency for the DoD stabilization for the wave equation, c.f.
dod-wave-eq
, will follow along the same lines. The formulation of the stabilization terms is more involved and we will recall the necessary definitions.

Definition 5
(Propagation forms)

Let
E\in\mathcal{I}
be a small cell and

\mathbb{I}(E)=\{1,\ldots,K\}
the set of face indices of
E
. We introduce a set of surface and volume propagation forms

\displaystyle p_{ij}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)

\displaystyle:(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\to\mathbb{R},\;i,j\in\mathbb{I}(E),i\neq j,

\displaystyle p_{V}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)

\displaystyle:(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle p_{V}^{E,*}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)

\displaystyle:(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\times(P^{r}(E))^{3}\to\mathbb{R}.

These forms are required to be symmetric in the first two arguments and linear in the third. They must further satisfy for all vector-valued polynomial functions
u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w\in\mathcal{P}^{r}(E)^{3}
the following properties:

1.

Let
i,j\in\mathbb{I}(E)
. Then it holds

p_{ij}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)+p_{ji}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)=p_{V}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)+p_{V}^{E,*}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w).

(19a)

2.

Let
j\in\mathbb{I}(E)
. Then it holds

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}(E),i\neq j}p_{ij}^{E}(u^{\mu},u^{\nu},w)=\int_{\gamma_{j}}\tfrac{1}{2}\langle f_{n}(u^{\mu})+f_{n}(u^{\nu}),w\rangle.

(19b)

In addition, the specific choice of propagation forms in
dod-wave-eq
satisfies the property

p_{V}^{E}(u,u,w)=\tfrac{2}{K(K-1)}\int_{E}f(u)\cdot\nabla w,

(20)

which will be necessary for consistency.

To formulate the cell stabilization we use the following notation: Let
E\in\mathcal{I}
be a small cut cell with
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{h}(E)
and
\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma_{h}(E)
being two of its faces,
i,j\in\mathbb{I}(E)
. Then we denote by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}&\quad\text{if }\mathcal{E}=E\text{ or }\mathcal{E}=E_{k},k\in\{i,j\}\text{ and }\gamma_{k}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}\\
\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{\gamma_{i}}&\quad\text{if }\mathcal{E}=E_{i}\text{ and }\gamma_{i}\not\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}\\
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{\gamma_{j}}&\quad\text{if }\mathcal{E}=E_{j}\text{ and }\gamma_{j}\not\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}\end{cases}

(21)

the extension operator between
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
that selects either of the previously defined extension operators based on whether we are extending from an internal or external boundary. We abuse notation here by writing
\mathcal{E}=E_{i}
even if
\gamma_{i}\not\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
for
i\in\mathbb{I}(E)
.

Definition 6
(Cell stabilization terms)

Let
E\in\mathcal{I}
be a small cut cell. The cell-wise stabilization term
J^{E}_{h}=J_{h}^{0,E}+J_{h}^{1,E}+J_{h}^{s,E}
is constructed from pairwise (for each pair of neighbors
i,j\in\mathbb{I}(E),i\neq j
) contributions as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}J^{0,E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{-}\>\eta_{E}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{I}(E)^{2},\,i<j}J^{0,E}_{h,ij}(u_{h},w_{h})\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}-\eta_{E}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle\tfrac{1}{2}[f_{n}(u^{L}_{\gamma})+f_{n}(u^{R}_{\gamma})],\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\rangle\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}-\eta_{E}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle\tfrac{1}{2}[f_{n}(u_{h})+f_{n}(\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u_{h}))],w_{h}\rangle,\end{split}

(22a)

\displaystyle J^{1,E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=\>\phantom{-}\>\eta_{E}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{I}(E)^{2},\,i<j}J^{1,E}_{h,ij}(u_{h},w_{h}),

(22b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J^{s,E}_{h}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{-}\>\eta_{E}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{I}(E)^{2},\,i<j}J^{s,E}_{ij}(u_{h},w_{h})\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}-\eta_{E}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle S_{n}(u^{L}_{\gamma},u^{R}_{\gamma}),\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\rangle\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}-\eta_{E}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle S_{n}(u_{h},\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u_{h})),w_{h}\rangle.\end{split}

(22c)

For the surface contributions
J_{h,ij}^{0,E}
of
J_{h}^{0,E}

we distinguish two cases: (1) two inner faces, i.e.
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
, and (2) one face being a reflecting boundary face.

(1)

For two inner faces
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
, associated with cut-cell neighbors
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
, the surface stabilization term between
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
is given by

\begin{split}J_{h,ij}^{0,E}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{+}\>p_{ij}^{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})-\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(w_{h}))\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+p_{ji}^{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})-\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}(w_{h})).\end{split}

(23)

(2)

If one of the two faces is a reflecting boundary face, the stabilization needs to be defined separately. W.l.o.g we assume that
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}
and
\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}
. The reflected surface stabilization term between
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
is then given by

\begin{split}J_{h,ij}^{0,E}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{+}\>p_{ij}^{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{\gamma_{i}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})-\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(w_{h}))\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+p_{ji}^{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{\gamma_{i}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})).\end{split}

(24)

To define the contribution
J_{h,ij}^{1,E}
we introduce a weighting factor
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}:=\{-1\text{ if }\mathcal{E}=E;\frac{1}{2}\text{ else}\}
. With this the volume stabilization is given as

\begin{split}J_{h,ij}^{1,E}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{+}\>\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}\big[p_{V}^{E}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(w_{h}))\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\>\phantom{+}\>\hphantom{\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}\big[]}-\tfrac{2}{K(K-1)}\int_{E}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(u_{h}))\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(w_{h})\big]\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+\>\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}p_{V}^{E,*}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(w_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(w_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(u_{h})).\end{split}

(25)

Finally the contribution
J^{s,E}_{ij}
to the dissipative stabilization is

\begin{split}J^{s,E}_{ij}(u_{h},w_{h})&=\>\phantom{+}\>\tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle S_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(u_{h})),\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(w_{h})\mathord{-}\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(w_{h}))\rangle\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+\tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma}\langle S_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(u_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(u_{h})),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(w_{h})\mathord{-}\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(w_{h}))\rangle.\end{split}

(26)

Proposition 2

Let
u
be the exact solution to equation (
4
). Then

J_{h}^{E}(u,w_{h})=[J^{0,E}_{h}+J^{1,E}_{h}+J^{s,E}_{h}](u,w_{h})=0\quad\forall E\in\mathcal{I}\text{ and }\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{r}_{h}.

Proof

For
u
being the exact solution, Corollary
1
implies that

\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{E}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\mathfrak{M}}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{\mathfrak{M}}(u)=u.

(27)

Furthermore
\mathfrak{M}_{n}(u)=u
at the boundary and
u^{L}_{\gamma}=u^{R}_{\gamma}
. With this (
22a
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\eta_{E}}J^{0,E}_{h}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle=\>\phantom{-}\>\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{I}(E)^{2},i<j}J^{0,E}_{h,ij}(u,w_{h})-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\langle f_{n}(u),\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\rangle

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\langle f_{n}(u),w_{h}\rangle.

We need to rewrite the first sum so that it matches the second and third.
Using
p^{E}_{ij}(u,u,\cdot)=p^{E}_{ji}(u,u,\cdot)

we rearrange terms inside the first sum to get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}(E),i<j}

\displaystyle J^{0,E}_{h,ij}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle=\>\phantom{+}\>\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}(E),i\neq j}p^{E}_{ij}(u,u,\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h})-\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}(w_{h}))

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}(E),i\neq j}p^{E}_{ij}(u,u,\mathcal{L}_{E}(w_{h}))

\displaystyle\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq: propagation forms consistency}}}{=}\>\phantom{+}\>\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{i}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\langle f_{n}(u),\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket\rangle+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}(E),\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma^{\text{b}}_{h}(E)}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\langle f_{n}(u),w_{h}\rangle.

Thus
J^{0,E}_{h}(u,w_{h})=0
. Let us now consider the volume contributions (
25
) that make up the complete volume term (
22b
). Using (
27
) we have

\begin{split}J_{h,ij}^{1,E}(u,w_{h})&=\>\phantom{+}\>\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}\big[p_{V}^{E}(u,u,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(w_{h}))-\tfrac{2}{K(K-1)}\int_{E}f(u)\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}^{ij}(w_{h})\big]\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+\>\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}p_{V}^{E,*}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(w_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(w_{h}),u)\\
&\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq: propagation forms volume consistency}}}{=}\>\phantom{+}\>\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\{E,E_{i},E_{j}\}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}p_{V}^{E,*}(\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}^{ij}(w_{h}),\mathcal{L}_{E_{j}}^{ij}(w_{h}),u)=0\end{split}

The last equation follows from the definition of the weighting factors
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}
and the linearity of
p^{E,*}_{V}
in the last argument. Now we can conclude
J_{h}^{1,E}(u,w_{h})=0
. Lastly we infer from (
27
) and the consistency of
S_{n}
that
J^{s,E}_{ij}(u,w_{h})=0
and
J^{s,E}(u,w_{h})=0
by a term-wise inspection.
