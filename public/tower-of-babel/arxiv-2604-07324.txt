The quantitative Beurling-Helson theorem
Tom Sanders

Abstract
. We show that for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 if ϕ : 𝕋 → 𝕋 \phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T} is continuous and ‖ exp ⁡ ( − 2 ​ π ​ i ​ z ​ ϕ ) ‖ A ⁡ ( 𝕋 ) = O | z | → ∞ ​ ( log 1 8 − ε ⁡ | z | ) \|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=O_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}(\log^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}|z|) then ϕ ⁡ ( x ) = w ​ x + t \phi(x)=wx+t for some w ∈ ℤ w\in\mathbb{Z} and t ∈ 𝕋 t\in\mathbb{T} .



1.
Introduction

Following Rudin
[
Rud90
]
, for
G
a compact Abelian group with Haar probability measure
\mu_{G}
and dual group
\Gamma
we define the Fourier transform of
f\in L_{1}(\mu_{G})
to be

\widehat{f}(\gamma):=\int{f(x)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{G}(x)}.

(1.1)

The space
A(G)
is the functions
f\in L_{1}(\mu_{G})
such that
\widehat{f}\in\ell_{1}(\Gamma)
and is a Banach algebra with the norm
\|f\|_{A(G)}:=\|\widehat{f}\|_{\ell_{1}(\Gamma)}
.

The Beurling-Helson theorem of the title is
[
BH53
, Theorem, p126]
in the special case of the circle group
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=O_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}(1).

Then
\phi(x)=wx+t
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
.

Kahane
[
Kah63
, p121]
conjectures that the weaker condition

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=o_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}(\log|z|)

would suffice for the same conclusion, and notes that if true this is the best possible result as nonlinear piecewise linear functions
\phi
have
\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=O_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}(\log|z|)
.

This last calculation of Kahane was strengthened by Lebedev in
[
Leb12b
]
, who also in a separate paper proved the first result towards Kahane’s conjecture:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Leb12a
, Theorem, p122]
)
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=o_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\log\log|z|}{\log\log\log|z|}\right)^{\frac{1}{12}}.

Then
\phi(x)=wx+t
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
.

Lebedev’s argument was refined by Konyagin and Shkredov:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
KS15
, Theorem 2, p110]
)
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=o_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\log|z|}{(\log\log|z|)^{6}}\right)^{\frac{1}{22}}.

Then
\phi(x)=wx+t
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
.

Our aim is to show the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and
\varepsilon>0
is such that

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=O_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}(\log^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}|z|).

Then
\phi(x)=wx+t
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
.

There is considerable further discussion of the problem in
[
Leb12a
]
.

Notation

We use big-
O
notation in the usual way in the paper, but also absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2},\dots>1
and
c_{1},c_{2},\dots\in(0,1)
. All of the absolute constants could be calculated. We give them names to help make clear the dependencies in some proofs, and in particular that those dependencies are not circular.

It will also be useful to generalise our notation for Haar measure on a compact Abelian group to any open
S\subset G
, by writing
\mu_{S}
for the measure
\mu_{G}
restricted to
S
and normalised to have total mass
1
.

Finally, we write
\mathbb{T}_{N}:=\{x/N+\mathbb{Z}:x\in\mathbb{Z}\}\leq\mathbb{T}
and equip it with Haar probability measure, and then
\mathbb{Z}_{N}:=\{x+N\mathbb{Z}:x\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a dual of
\mathbb{T}_{N}
with the usual pairing.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We pursue the same ideas as Lebedev
[
Leb12a
]
with the improvements of Konyagin and Shkredov
[
KS15
]
. Lebedev begins by considering

E:=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:\phi(x+y+z)-\phi(x+y)-\phi(x+z)+\phi(x)=0\},

which he shows is a large subset of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by an argument
[
Leb12a
, Lemma 2, p126]
which he attributes to Kahane. The hypothesis – that is the bound on
\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}
as a function of
z
– implies that a suitably smoothed version of

\Phi(x,y,z):=\exp(2\pi i(\phi(x+y+z)-\phi(x+y)-\phi(x+z)+\phi(x)))

is in
A(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.

Functions in
A(\mathbb{T}^{3})
are continuous in a quantitative sense: they have a set of positive measure over which they do not vary very much, and this measure has a lower bound depending only on the norm of the function. This can be used to show that
E
, which is a level set of
\Phi
, is almost the whole of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and then a limiting argument tells us that
E=\mathbb{T}^{3}
and this implies
\phi
has the required form.

The quantitative continuity of functions in
A(\mathbb{T}^{3})
is captured in a result of Green and Konyagin
[
GK09
]
recorded in
[
Leb12a
, (2), p124]
, and the final limiting argument is then on
[
Leb12a
, p129]
.

The final ingredient in Lebedev’s argument is a discrete approximation
[
Leb12a
, p124]
which allows him to take advantage of the formulation of Green and Konyagin’s results recorded in
[
GK09
]
. This approximation was considerably improved by Konyagin and Shkredov in
[
KS15
]
.

The main difference of the present paper is that instead of considering the set
E
, we consider the graph
\Gamma:=\{(x,\phi(x)):x\in\mathbb{T}\}
. The downside of this is that
\Gamma
is not large in the same way that
E
is, but we can recover the situation by using the standard additive combinatorial tools of Freiman’s Theorem and the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem. On the other hand, the upside is that the algebra norm of (a discretisation of)
\Gamma
is smaller than the corresponding norm when considering the set
E
. This saving leads to our improvement.

We turn now to recording the three main parts of our argument before giving the proof.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition
3.2
)
.

Suppose that
C\geq 1
and
c\in(0,1/2)
. Then there are constants
F(C,c),H(C,c)\geq 1
such that if
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous with

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}\leq C\log^{c}(3+|z|)\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then there is an integer
1\leq Q\leq\exp(F(C,c)\log^{\frac{2}{1-2c}}2N)
and
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{Q}
such that
\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{H(C,c)}{NQ}
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Proposition
4.1
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite Abelian group,
1_{A}\in A(G)
has
\|1_{A}\|_{A(G)}\leq M
for some
M\geq 1
, and
\eta\in(0,1/4]
. Then there is
V\leq G
with
\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{V}\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}\geq 1-\eta
and
|V|\geq\exp(-\hyperlink{const:partial}{C_{\ref{const:partial}}}M^{3}\eta^{-3}\log^{5}2M\eta^{-1})|A|
.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma
5.1
)
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and for all
\eta\in(0,1]
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
there is an integer
N\geq N_{0}
,
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
with graph
\Gamma:=\{(x,\phi^{*}(x)):x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\}
, and
W
such that

(i)

\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
;

(ii)

W
is a coset of a finite subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that
|\Gamma\triangle W|\leq 2\eta N
.

Then there is
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(x)=wx+t
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose that
c:=\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon>0
. From the hypothesis there is
C\geq 1
such that

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\log^{c}(3+|z|)\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.1)

Let

F:=F(C,c)\text{ and }H=H(C,c)

where these are the constants as in Proposition
3.2
.

\mathbb{T}_{N}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
and so
A(\mathbb{T}_{N})
and
A(\mathbb{T})
are well-defined subalgebras of
B(\mathbb{T}_{N})
and
B(\mathbb{T})
respectively, and the restriction map
B(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow B(\mathbb{T}_{N});f\mapsto f|_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
is a contractive surjection (this is essentially the proof of
[
Rud90
, Theorem 2.7.2, p53]
). Moreover, the indicator functions of closed subgroups have algebra norm
1
. Combining all these we get that

\displaystyle\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}

\displaystyle=\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{B(\mathbb{T}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)1_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}\|_{B(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{B(\mathbb{T})}\|1_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}\|_{B(\mathbb{T})}=\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}

(2.2)

for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Our aim now is to show that the hypothesis of Lemma
5.1
are satisfied. It is enough to establish these for all
\eta
sufficiently small so let
\eta\in(0,1/4]
. Since there are infinitely many primes we may let
N\geq N_{0}
be prime such that

\frac{H}{N}\leq\eta\text{ and }\exp(-\hyperlink{const:partial}{C_{\ref{const:partial}}}M^{3}\eta^{-3}\log^{5}2M\eta^{-1})>N^{-1}=\exp(-\log N)

(2.3)

where

Q_{0}:=\exp(F\log^{\frac{2}{1-2c}}2N)\text{ and }M:=C\log^{c}(3+Q_{0})+2\pi H.

(2.4)

This is possible since
c<\frac{1}{8}
, because this ensures
3\cdot c\cdot\frac{2}{1-2c}<1
.

Apply Proposition
3.2
to get
1\leq Q\leq Q_{0}
and
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{Q}
such that

\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq H/NQ\leq\eta\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

(2.5)

Let
\Gamma
be the graph of
\phi^{*}

i.e.
\Gamma=\{(x,\phi^{*}(x)):x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q}
, and for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}
write
s=\tilde{s}+Q\mathbb{Z}
for some
\tilde{s}\in\{1,\dots,Q\}
. Writing
f_{s}:=\exp(-2\pi i\widetilde{s}\phi)|_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
, we have from the first inequality in (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{1_{\Gamma}}(r,s)-\frac{1}{Q}\widehat{f_{s}}(r)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left|\frac{1}{NQ}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}{\exp(-2\pi ixr)\exp(-2\pi i\widetilde{s}\phi(x))\left(\exp(-2\pi i\widetilde{s}(\phi^{*}(x)-\phi(x)))-1\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{NQ}\cdot N\cdot 2\pi\cdot\sup{\{\widetilde{s}:s\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}\}}\cdot\frac{H}{NQ}=\frac{2\pi H}{NQ}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|1_{\Gamma}\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}}{\frac{1}{Q}\|f_{s}\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}}+2\pi H

\displaystyle\leq\sup{\{\|\exp(-2\pi\widetilde{s}\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}:s\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}\}}+2\pi H\leq M.

Apply Proposition
4.1
with
M
,
\eta
, the set
\Gamma
, and the group
\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q}
to get that there is
V\leq\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q}
such that
\|1_{\Gamma}\ast\mu_{V}\|_{\infty}\geq 1-\eta
and

|V|\geq\exp(-\hyperlink{const:partial}{C_{\ref{const:partial}}}M^{3}\eta^{-3}\log^{5}2M\eta^{-1})|\Gamma|>1

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q}
be such that
1_{\Gamma}\ast\mu_{V}(x_{0})\geq 1-\eta
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{T}_{N}\times\mathbb{T}_{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{N}
be the canonical projection. Since
\Gamma
is the graph of a function we know that
\pi
is injective on
\Gamma
. Moreover
|\Gamma\cap(x_{0}+V)|=1_{\Gamma}\ast\mu_{V}(x_{0})|V|
, and
|V|\geq 2
so

|\pi(x_{0}+V)|\geq|\pi(\Gamma\cap(x_{0}+V))|\geq(1-\eta)|V|>1.

Now
\pi(x_{0}+V)
is a coset of a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}_{N}
and
N
is prime, so
\pi(x_{0}+V)=\mathbb{T}_{N}
and hence
|V|=|x_{0}+V|\geq N
. In the other direction
\frac{|\Gamma|}{|V|}\geq 1-\eta
and hence
|V|\leq|\Gamma|/(1-\eta)<2N
. Since
V
has
\mathbb{T}_{N}
as a quotient and
N
is prime it follows by Lagrange’s theorem that
|V|=N
.

Finally, write
W:=x_{0}+V
so that

|\Gamma\triangle W|=|\Gamma|+|W|-2|\Gamma\cap(x_{0}+V)|\leq 2\eta|\Gamma|.

Between this and (
2.5
) the conditions of Lemma
5.1
are established and this gives the result.
∎

The
\eta
-dependence in Proposition
4.1
is not important to us, but the
M
-dependence determines the quality of the bound in Theorem
1.4
. If
G
is a cyclic group of prime order much larger than
\exp(M)
and
A
is an interval in
G
with
\|1_{A}\|_{A(G)}=M
then
|A|=\exp(\Omega(M))
, while we must have
|V|\leq 1
. It follows that in the lower bound the
M^{3}
-term cannot be improved to a smaller power than
M^{1}
. If a bound of that shape were known the remainder of the argument would give Theorem
1.4
with the
\frac{1}{8}
replaced by
\frac{1}{4}
.

3.
Dirichlet’s approximation theorem

The elements of
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
are cosets of
\mathbb{Z}
, and for
x\in\mathbb{T}
we write
\|x\|_{\mathbb{T}}
for
\min\{|t|:t\in x\}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Dirichlet’s approximation theorem)
.

Suppose that
A
is a set of size
d
,
\phi
maps into
\mathbb{T}
and has domain containing
A
, and
K\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then there is an integer
1\leq Q\leq K^{d}
and
\phi^{*}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{Q}
such that
\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{1}{KQ}
for all
x\in A
.

Lebedev
[
Leb12a
, p124]
used this in the case
A=\mathbb{T}_{N}
and
K=N
, where
Q
may be as large as
N^{N}
, and a key advance of Konyagin and Shkredov in
[
KS15
]
was to show that in the setup of interest to us, one can do much better.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
C\geq 1
and
c\in(0,1/2)
. Then there are constants
F(C,c),H(C,c)\geq 1
such that if
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous with

\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}\leq C\log^{c}(3+|z|)\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.1)

Then there is an integer
1\leq Q\leq\exp(F(C,c)\log^{\frac{2}{1-2c}}2N)
and
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{Q}
such that
\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\frac{H(C,c)}{NQ}
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
.

Our argument follows that of Konyagin and Shkredov. We introduce a little more smoothing to get the linear dependence on
Q
in the final estimate above. This is not needed by Konyagin and Shkredov (who made do with square-root dependence in the corresponding estimate
[
KS15
, (46), p120]
), but smoothing in this way is completely routine.

We also make the aesthetic choice to work with a version of dissociativity rather than the direct argument in
[
KS15
, Lemma 4, p112]
. This is both slightly weaker as we cannot use a result like
[
KS15
, Lemma 9, p113]
, which could lead to savings of a power of a double-logarithm; and is not as generally presented as
[
KS15
, Lemma 4, p112]
which is well-suited to use in other contexts.

For
G
an Abelian group,
a\in G^{d}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
write
\sigma\cdot a:=\sigma_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{d}a_{d}
. For
\phi:G\rightarrow H
, a map between groups, we write
\phi(a)
for the element of
H^{d}
defined by
\phi(a)_{i}:=\phi(a_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq d
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
and
a\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
is such that if
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
and
\sigma\cdot a=0
and
\|\sigma\cdot\phi(a)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta
, then
\sigma\equiv 0
. Then

d\leq\hypertarget{const:sksize}{\mbox{}}C_{1}\cdot(\log 2N)\cdot\sup_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}}{\|\exp(-2\pi iz\phi)\|_{A(\mathbb{T}_{N})}^{2}}.

Proof.

Define

F:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C};z\mapsto\begin{cases}\eta&\text{ if }z=0\\
\eta\left(\frac{\sin\pi\eta z}{\pi\eta z}\right)^{2}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
F(z)\geq 0
and we can compute that

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}{F(z)\exp(2\pi izx)}=1_{\left[-\frac{1}{2}\eta,\frac{1}{2}\eta\right]}\ast\mu_{\left[-\frac{1}{2}\eta,\frac{1}{2}\eta\right]}(\|x\|_{\mathbb{T}})\leq 1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(\|x\|_{\mathbb{T}}).

(3.2)

Using
|\sin\theta|\geq\frac{2}{\pi}|\theta|
whenever
|\theta|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, we also have

\tau:=\sum_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}}{F(z)}\geq\sum_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}}{\eta\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2}}\geq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}.

For
a\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
define

\lambda_{a}:\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow S^{1};(r,z)\mapsto\exp(2\pi i(ar+\phi(a)z)),

and define two probability measures
\widetilde{\mu}
and
\mu
on
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
with

\widetilde{\mu}(\{r,z\})=\frac{1}{N}F(z)\text{ and }\mu(\{(r,z)\})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\tau N}\cdot F(z)&\text{ if }|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, for
a,b\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
we have the orthogonality relations

\int{\lambda_{a}\overline{\lambda_{b}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }a=b\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.3)

From the hypothesis of the lemma, if
\sigma\cdot a=0
and
\|\sigma\cdot\phi(a)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta
for
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
, then
\sigma\equiv 0
, we conclude that
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
are distinct; write
A:=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
.

Let
k:=\lceil\log 2N\rceil
– we make this choice now to be clear about dependencies, but it arises from optimising (
3.5
) – and define a linear operator

T:\ell_{2}(A)\rightarrow L_{2k}(\mu);g\mapsto\sum_{a\in A}{g(a)\lambda_{a}}.

First we compute the norm of
T
. Suppose
g\in\ell_{2}(A)
. By convexity of
\exp(ty)
we have
\exp(ty)\leq\cosh t(1+y\tanh t)
whenever
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
-1\leq y\leq 1
, and so

\displaystyle\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\exp(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(a_{j})\lambda_{a_{j}}))}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-1}\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\exp(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(a_{j})\lambda_{a_{j}}))}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-1}\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\cosh|g(a_{j})|}\left(1+\frac{\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(a_{j})\lambda_{a_{j}})}{|g(a_{j})|}\tanh|g(a_{j})|\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\cosh|g(a_{j})|}\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{(1+\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\omega_{j}\lambda_{a_{j}})}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}

(3.4)

with the convention, and respectively notation, that

\frac{g(a_{j})}{|g(a_{j})|}\tanh|g(a_{j})|=0\text{ if }g(a_{j})=0;\text{ resp.\ }\omega_{j}:=\frac{g(a_{j})}{|g(a_{j})|}\tanh|g(a_{j})|.

Since
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z=\frac{1}{2}(z+\overline{z})
we have

\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{(1+\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\omega_{j}\lambda_{a_{j}})}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}=\int{\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}{\frac{1}{2^{\|\sigma\|_{\ell_{1}}}}\cdot\prod_{j:\sigma_{j}=1}{\omega_{j}}\cdot\overline{\prod_{j:\sigma_{j}=-1}{\omega_{j}}}\cdot\lambda_{\sigma\cdot a}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}}.

However, by (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\int{\lambda_{\sigma\cdot a}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}{\exp(2\pi i(\sigma\cdot a)r)}\cdot\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}{F(z)\exp(2\pi i(\sigma\cdot\phi(a))z)}

\displaystyle=1_{\{0\}}(\sigma\cdot a)1_{\left[-\frac{1}{2}\eta,\frac{1}{2}\eta\right]}\ast\mu_{\left[-\frac{1}{2}\eta,\frac{1}{2}\eta\right]}(\|\sigma\cdot\phi(a)\|_{\mathbb{T}})\leq 1_{\{0\}}(\sigma\cdot a)1_{[-\eta,\eta]}(\|\sigma\cdot\phi(a)\|_{\mathbb{T}}).

By the hypothesis of the lemma if
\sigma\cdot a=0
and
\|\sigma\cdot\phi(a)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta
for
\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
then
\sigma\equiv 0
, so we conclude that

\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{(1+\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\omega_{j}\lambda_{a_{j}})}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}}\leq 1.

Combining this with (
3.4
) we get

\int{\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\exp(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(a_{j})\lambda_{a_{j}}))}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq\tau^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\cosh|g(a_{j})|}\leq\tau^{-1}\exp(\|g\|_{\ell_{2}(A)}^{2}/2),

with the last inequality since

) that there is some
x_{0}\in G
such that
1_{S}\ast\widetilde{\nu}(x_{0})=\|1_{S}\ast\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}
, whence

1_{A}\ast\widetilde{\nu}(x_{0})=\int{1_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{x_{0}}(\nu)}\geq\int{1_{S}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{x_{0}}(\nu)}=\|1_{S}\ast\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}\geq\alpha_{M}.

If
|1_{A}\ast\mu(x_{0})|\leq\eta
, then since
\eta\leq\frac{1}{4}
we have from (
4.10
) that

\alpha_{M}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}\leq 1_{A}\ast\widetilde{\nu}(x_{0})(1-\eta)^{p}\leq 2^{-p}

which contradicts the choice of
p
, so by (
4.11
) we have
x_{0}\in T
.

From the definition of doubling dimension (
4.2
) and the inequality in (
4.1
) for covering numbers we have

|B_{\kappa}^{\prime}|\geq 2^{-\lceil\log_{2}\kappa^{-1}\rceil\dim^{*}B^{\prime}}|B_{1}^{\prime}|.

Inserting the upper bound on
\dim^{*}B^{\prime}
from (
4.6
), the lower bound on
|B^{\prime}_{1}|
in (
4.7
), the upper bound on
\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}(S;B_{1})
from (
4.4
) and finally the fact that
|S|\geq M^{-O(1)}|A|
we get

|B_{\kappa}^{\prime}|\geq\exp(-O(M^{3}\eta^{-3}\log^{5}2M\eta^{-1}))|A|.

Now
|V|\geq|B_{\kappa}^{\prime}|
and also

\int{1_{A}\ast\mu\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{x_{0}}\mu_{V}}\geq 1-\eta

since
x_{0}+V\subset T
, and hence
\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{V}\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}\geq 1-\eta
. The result is proved.
∎

5.
The limiting argument

In this final section we record a version of the limiting argument relevant to our circumstances.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is continuous and for all
\eta\in(0,1]
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
there is an integer
N\geq N_{0}
,
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
with graph
\Gamma:=\{(x,\phi^{*}(x)):x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\}
, and
W
such that

(i)

\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
;

(ii)

W
is a coset of a finite subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that
|\Gamma\triangle W|\leq\eta N
.

Then there is
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(x)=wx+t
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Define
\Phi
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by

\Phi(x,y,z):=\exp(2\pi i(\phi(x+y+z)-\phi(x+y)-\phi(x+z)+\phi(x))).

If
\Phi(x,y,z)=1
for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{T}
then the map
x\mapsto\phi(x)-\phi(0)
is a homomorphism. Since it is also continuous, it has the form
x\mapsto wx
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
Rud90
, p13]
), and the result is proved.

It follows that we may assume that
\Phi(x,y,z)\neq 0
for some
x,y,z\in\mathbb{T}
and hence, since
\Phi
is continuous, that

\kappa:=\int{|\Phi-1|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}}>0.

(5.1)

Again, by continuity of
\Phi
and the fact that
\mu_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
converges weakly to
\mu_{\mathbb{T}}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, there is
N_{0}
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
we have

\left|\int{|\Phi-1|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}}-\int{|\Phi-1|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}}}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\kappa.

(5.2)

Now, by the hypotheses of the lemma applied with
\eta:=\frac{1}{4}\min\{1,\frac{1}{2\hyperlink{const:5}{C_{\ref{const:5}}}},\frac{1}{\hyperlink{const:exp}{C_{\ref{const:exp}}}}\}\kappa>0
there is
N\geq N_{0}
and
\phi^{*}:\mathbb{T}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
with

\|\phi(x)-\phi^{*}(x)\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\eta\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

Define
\Phi^{*}
on
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}
by

\Phi^{*}(x,y,z):=\exp(2\pi i(\phi^{*}(x+y+z)-\phi^{*}(x+y)-\phi^{*}(x+z)+\phi^{*}(x))),

so that

|\Phi^{*}(x,y,z)-\Phi(x,y,z)|\leq\hypertarget{const:exp}{\mbox{}}C_{3}\max\{\|\phi^{*}(t)-\phi(t)\|_{\mathbb{T}}:t\in\{x,x+y,x+z,x+y+z\}\},

and hence

|\Phi^{*}(x,y,z)-\Phi(x,y,z)|\leq\hyperlink{const:exp}{C_{\ref{const:exp}}}\eta\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

(5.3)

The hypotheses also give
W
, a coset of a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, such that if
\Gamma
is the graph of
\phi^{*}
then
|\Gamma\triangle W|\leq 2\eta N
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between triples
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}
and triples
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with
\alpha,\alpha+\beta,\alpha+\gamma\in\Gamma
given by
\alpha:=(x,\phi^{*}(x))\in\Gamma
,
\alpha+\beta:=(x+y,\phi^{*}(x+y))\in\Gamma
and
\alpha+\gamma:=(x+z,\phi^{*}(x+z))\in\Gamma
. Moreover,
\Phi^{*}(x,y,z)\neq 1
if and only if
\alpha+\beta+\gamma\notin\Gamma
. Hence there are at most

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{1_{\Gamma^{c}}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=|\Gamma|^{3}-\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha)}

triples
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}
with
\Phi^{*}(x,y,z)\neq 1
. Now

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha+\gamma)1_{\Gamma}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}{1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha+\gamma)1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{\alpha}{\left(\sum_{\beta}{1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\beta)}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{|W\cap\Gamma-W\cap\Gamma|}\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta}{1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\alpha+\beta)1_{W\cap\Gamma}(\beta)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{|W\cap\Gamma|^{4}}{|W\cap\Gamma-W\cap\Gamma|}\geq\frac{|W\cap\Gamma|^{4}}{|W|}\geq\frac{(1-2\eta)^{4}}{1+2\eta}|\Gamma|^{3}\geq(1-\hypertarget{const:5}{\mbox{}}C_{4}\eta)|\Gamma|^{3}.

It follows that the number of triples
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}
such that
\Phi^{*}(x,y,z)\neq 1
is at most
\hyperlink{const:5}{C_{\ref{const:5}}}\eta N^{3}
. Hence, by (
5.3
),

\int{|\Phi-1|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}}}\leq\int{|\Phi^{*}-1|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}}}+\int{|\Phi^{*}-\Phi|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbb{T}_{N}^{3}}}\leq 2\hyperlink{const:5}{C_{\ref{const:5}}}\eta+\hyperlink{const:exp}{C_{\ref{const:exp}}}\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}\kappa,

which is a contradiction to (
5.2
) and (
5.1
).
∎
