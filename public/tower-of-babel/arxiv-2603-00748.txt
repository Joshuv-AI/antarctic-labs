Convergence of semilinear parabolic flows with general initial data
Daniel Restrepo

Abstract
. We analyze the long-time behavior of solutions to semilinear parabolic equations in the Euclidean space arising as gradient flows of an energy functional J J . We prove that, for general initial data –including those with non-compact support– the flow converges to a unique ground state of J J . The argument relies on a sharp stability estimate for almost critical points of J J , providing a flexible framework to prove convergence of gradient flows associated with constrained minimization problems in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . As an application we strengthen the convergence results of [ 8 , 11 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

This paper is concerned with the convergence as
t\to\infty
of non-negative solutions
u=u(t,x)
to the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-f(u)\quad\text{in}\,\,(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(t,x)\to 0,\hskip 42.67912pt\text{as $|x|\to\infty$},\\
u(.,0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Here
f
is a function that meets some standard growth and regularity conditions so that problem (
1.1
) can be recast as the
L^{2}
-gradient flow associated with the energy functional

(1.2)

J(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+F(v),

where
F(v)=\int_{0}^{v}f(s)ds
. In this case, one has that
J
is decreasing along solutions and, furthermore,

(1.3)

\frac{d}{dt}J(u(t,\cdot))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}

natural question is whether solutions of (
1.1
) converge, as
t\to\infty
, to a non-negative solution (ground state) of the associated stationary problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta\xi=f(\xi)\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\xi(x)\to 0,\quad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$}.\\
\end{cases}

A key difficulty in addressing this question in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
arises from the translation invariance of
J
, which implies lack of compactness of the set of stationary solutions and allows for bubbling phenomena along the flow. To illustrate this issue, assume that

\sup_{t>1}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,\qquad\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to\infty.

By local weak compactness in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exist times
t_{k}\to\infty
such that
u(t_{k},\cdot)
converges locally in
L^{2}
to some solution
\xi_{0}
of (
1.4
). However, this result is unsatisfactory: it only yields subsequential convergence and does not exclude loss of
L^{2}
–mass at infinity. Under suitable assumptions on
f
(see
[
16
,
12
,
11
]
), concentration–compactness theory refines this description. One obtains that
u_{k}
converges to a superposition of solutions
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
of (
1.4
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big\|u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\xi_{i}(\cdot-x_{k}^{i})\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|x^{i}_{k}-x^{j}_{k}|\to\infty\qquad\mbox{for $i\neq j$, $i,j=1,\cdots,M.$}

Statements like (
1.5
) are known in the literature as
bubbling phenomena
and reflects the lack of compactness of Palais–Smale sequences in certain variational problems in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For instance, in the context of functional inequalities, it is known that almost critical points to the Sobolev embedding in the Euclidean space are not necessarily close to Talenti bubbles (i.e., the class of functions that saturates Sobolev inequality) but to supperpositions of them, see
[
20
]
. In the parabolic setting, (
1.5
) arises by viewing
\partial_{t}u(t_{k})
as a vanishing perturbation of the elliptic equation
\nabla J(u)=\partial_{t}u
.

The decomposition (
1.5
) is particularly useful when
a priori
information on
u_{0}
prevents bubbling (e.g., compact support or energy below a threshold; see
[
12
,
11
,
4
]
). Whether bubbling may occur for general initial data was posed as an open problem in
[
12
]
. The following theorem provides a partial answer.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and

(1.6)

f(t)=a_{0}t-\sum_{l=1}^{L}a_{i}t^{p_{l}},

where
a_{i}\geq 0
,
a_{0}>0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{L}a_{i}>0
, and
1<r<p_{i}<\frac{n}{n-2}
. Let
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u_{0}\geq 0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
). Then,
u
is the unique solution to (
1.1
),
u(t,x)>0
for
t>0
, and either

(1.7)

\lim_{t\to\infty}J(u(t,\cdot))=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0

or

(1.8)

J(u(t,\cdot))-J(\xi)\leq Ce^{-\frac{t}{C}},\qquad\|u(t,\cdot)-\tau_{x^{*}}[\xi]\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq Ce^{-\frac{t}{C}},

\xi
is the unique radial positive solution of (
1.4
) and
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and where
C>0
depends on
n
,
u_{0}
, and
f
.

Remark 1.2
.

There is a substantial literature on the asymptotic behavior of solutions to semilinear heat equations in bounded domains; see
[
5
]
and references therein. Many of these techniques do not extend directly to unbounded domains because of the lack of compactness. In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, additional difficulties arise due to the translation invariance of the energy functional
J
.

A standard approach to regain compactness is to assume that
u_{0}
is compactly supported. Combined with the maximum principle or the moving planes method, this ensures uniform decay of solutions along the flow
[
12
,
8
,
5
,
11
,
14
]
. This assumption, together with the decomposition (
1.5
), reduces the problem to studying the asymptotic behavior around a single stationary solution. Under these conditions, uniqueness of the limiting profile and convergence rates follow, provided that the stationary problem (
1.4
) has a unique solution
\xi
(up to translations) and that the kernel of
D^{2}J(\xi)
is generated by
\{\partial_{i}\xi\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Similar results were obtained in
[
8
]
using different methods but still assuming compactly supported initial data.

Remarkably, under the minimal assumptions
f\in C^{1}
,
f(0)=0
, and
f^{\prime}(0)>0
, full convergence to a unique stationary profile was shown in
[
5
,
14
]
, again assuming compact support of the initial datum. These results do not require uniqueness or non-degeneracy of stationary solutions, but they do not provide rates of convergence, not even algebraic.

Remark 1.3
.

(On the structure of
f
)
Although Theorem
1.1
assumes the specific form (
1.6
), our methods apply to a broader class of reaction terms. In general, we work in the framework where the hypotheses
f\in C^{1,\beta}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty))
,
f(0)=0
, and
f^{\prime}(0)>0
are satisfied. Under these general assumptions, ground states are radially symmetric (up to translations) and have a precise asymptotic profile at infinity; see Proposition
2.1
.

In this setting, convergence of global solutions starting from compactly supported initial data was established in
[
5
]
(see Remark
1.2
). Our approach for non-compactly supported initial data is variational: convergence is proved by showing that the solution approaches a unique ground state minimizing a constrained variational problem. For our choice of
f
, this follows from Berestycki and Lions’ theory
[
2
]
, which covers a broader class of nonlinearities.

We also require: (i) uniqueness of bounded ground states up to translations (see Section
2
); and (ii) non-degeneracy of
D^{2}J
at ground states (see Proposition
2.3
). These two properties form the variational framework underpinning our methods.

Finally, we impose two additional conditions on
f
:

(H1)

tf^{\prime}(0)\geq f(t),\quad t\in[0,\max\xi],

where
\xi
is the unique radial ground state; and

(H2)

f\text{ is concave in }(0,\delta)\text{ for some }\delta>0.

These ensure it is energetically unfavorable for the flow to split into multiple solutions at infinity due to tail interactions (see Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
). Hypotheses (
H1
)–(
H2
) are satisfied by (
1.6
) and by
f(t)=t-t^{p}
,
p\in(1,(n+2)/(n-2))
, considered in
[
8
]
.
A generalized version of Theorem
1.1
for reaction terms satisfying these general hypotheses is given in Theorem
2.6
at the end of Section
2
.

Remark 1.4
.

(Convergence to a ground state as a threshold phenomena) Global solutions of (
1.1
) converging to a ground state are rare; they arise as threshold trajectories separating solutions that vanish at infinity from those that blow up in finite time. More precisely,
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that for any compactly supported
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there exists
\alpha(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that the solution with initial datum
\alpha u_{0}
satisfies:

•

vanishes as
t\to\infty
if
\alpha\in[0,\alpha(u_{0}))
;

•

converges to a ground state and satisfies (
1.8
) if
\alpha=\alpha(u_{0})
;

•

blows up in finite time if
\alpha>\alpha(u_{0})
.

Our results extend this trichotomy to initial data without compact support; the proof follows exactly as in
[
11
]
.

1.2.
Extension to volume constrained flows.

The strategy used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be adapted to establish convergence for gradient flows associated with volume-constrained variational problems in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, even when the resulting equation is non-autonomous due to the presence of Lagrange multipliers.

As a concrete example, consider the volume-constrained problem

(1.9)

\Psi(\varepsilon,m)=\inf\Bigg\{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(u)=m,\,u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\Bigg\},\qquad\varepsilon,m>0,

where the energy functional is the Allen–Cahn energy

(1.10)

\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W(u),\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Here
W
is a non-degenerate double-well potential,
W:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
, satisfying

(1.11)

W(0)=W(1)=0,\quad W>0\text{ on }(0,1),\quad W^{\prime\prime}(0),W^{\prime\prime}(1)>0,

and the volume potential is given by
V(t)=\big(\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{W}\,ds\big)^{n/(n-1)}
. Problem (
1.9
) provides a diffuse interface approximation to the Euclidean isoperimetric problem (when
\varepsilon\ll m^{1/n}
) and has been studied in detail in
[
17
]
. The particular choice of
V
is linked to the classical isoperimetric inequality; its key properties are discussed extensively in the introductions of
[
17
,
4
]
.

The gradient flow associated with (
1.9
) is given by the Cauchy problem

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u=2\Delta u-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(u)+\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]}{\varepsilon}V^{\prime}(u),\quad\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&u(0)=u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1]),\end{aligned}\right.

where the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u]
is defined by

\lambda_{\varepsilon}[v]:=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(v)+W^{\prime}(v)V^{\prime}(v)}{\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(V^{\prime}(v))^{2}}.

This choice ensures preservation of the volume constraint:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(u_{0}),\quad\forall t>0.

The flow (
1.12
) is the natural gradient flow associated with (
1.9
), in the sense that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(\cdot,t))=-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}.

From the perspective of classical parabolic theory, (
1.12
) exhibits several peculiar features. It is a non-autonomous semilinear PDE, where the non-autonomy stems from the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]
. Nevertheless, the underlying variational structure provides sufficient rigidity to establish a subsequential bubbling result analogous to (
1.5
). Moreover, when
u_{0}
is compactly supported, one can prove full convergence of solutions to minimizers of (
1.9
) for
m=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(u_{0})
and
\varepsilon
satisfying
\varepsilon<<m^{\frac{1}{n}}
; see
[
4
, Theorems 1.3–1.4]
.

Observing that, by (
1.11
), the nonlinearity

f(t,s):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(s)-\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]}{\varepsilon}V^{\prime}(s)

satisfies the expansion

f(t,s)=Cs+O(s^{1+\beta}),\quad C>0,

we see that it formally falls into the class of nonlinearities treated by Theorem
1.1
and is therefore amenable to the same proof strategy. More precisely, if
W\in C^{3}[0,1]
satisfies the standard non-degeneracy conditions together with

(1.13)

W^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq\frac{W^{\prime}(t)}{t},\quad\forall t\in[0,1],\qquad\text{and}\qquad W^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)<0,

which correspond to (
H1
) and (
H2
), then for
n\geq 2
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small (depending only on
\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})
,
m
,
W
, and
n
), one can prove full convergence of solutions of (
1.12
) to a unique minimizer of (
1.9
), without restrictions on the support of
u_{0}
. In this case, the convergence is exponential as in (
1.8
), with constants degenerating as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We do not pursue the proof of this statement here. Although the overall strategy parallels that of Theorem
1.1
, the presence of the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]
creates a structural discrepancy between the parabolic equation satisfied by
u
and the elliptic equation satisfied by the minimizers of (
1.10
). Addressing this mismatch requires several minor but technically involved modifications of the argument.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

Our argument is based on two main ideas:

(1)

Controlling the rate of decay of
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
as
t\to\infty
ensures strong convergence of the flow, thereby preventing bubbling.

(2)

Viewing the solution
u
to (
1.1
) as a nearly critical point of the energy functional
J
allows us to perform a quantitative stability analysis.

A crucial ingredient for the implementation of this strategy is the non-degeneracy of solutions to the stationary problem (
1.4
), including uniqueness up to translations. These properties are guaranteed by our choice of the nonlinearity
f
in Theorem
1.1
(see Section
2
).

The first point is a general principle for parabolic flows: (
1.8
) essentially follows from

(1.14)

J(u(t,\cdot))-MJ(\xi)\leq C\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

as established in Section
3.3
. The second point is a property of Palais–Smale sequences for elliptic problems. Writing (
1.1
) as
\nabla J(u)=\partial_{t}u
, the general concentration-compactness theory implies that as
\partial_{t}u\to 0
, the solution
u
approaches a superposition of stationary solutions in the sense of (
1.5
).

By performing a quantitative stability analysis of critical points of
J
, in the spirit of the theory of functional inequalities
[
13
,
1
,
10
]
, we can strengthen (
1.5
) to

(1.15)

\Big\|u(t,\cdot)-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha_{i}(t)\tau_{x_{i}(t)}[\xi]\Big\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for sufficiently large
t
, where
x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
are suitable parameters, and
\tau_{x_{i}(t)}[\xi]
1
1

1

We set
\tau_{x_{0}}[v](x)=v(x-x_{0})
for every
x,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
are translations of the unique positive solution
\xi
of (
1.4
). The linear estimate (
1.15
), combined with precise quantitative bounds on the errors introduced by these parameters, leads directly to (
1.14
) (see Section
3.2
).

There are several significant differences between our approach and previous stability analyses of critical points in functional inequalities. First, since
f^{\prime}(0)>0
and
f
is non-homogeneous, estimating the error terms in (
1.15
), namely

(1.16)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}|1-\alpha_{i}|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x_{i}(t)}[\xi]\tau_{x_{j}(t)}[\xi],

is more involved than in
[
13
,
1
,
10
]
, and requires new ideas, as detailed in Lemma
3.3
. Second, while quantitative stability for functional inequalities typically yields convergence rates of known unique limiting profiles
[
7
,
13
,
9
,
3
]
, in our setting both the uniqueness of the limit and the convergence rate are established simultaneously.

Finally, we remark that our approach can also be adapted to prove general linear stability estimates for critical points of
J
, i.e., for non-negative solutions
v
of
\nabla J(v)=h
with
h\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in the spirit of
[
13
,
1
,
10
]
.

2.
Background material

In this section, we recall some qualitative properties of ground state solutions to (
1.4
) relevant for our methods. As mentioned in the introduction, the general class of reaction terms considered in this paper are of the form
f(t)=m^{2}t+O(t^{p})
as
t\to 0^{+}
with
m>0
and
p>1
. We begin this section by pointing out that the classical results of
[
15
]
apply in this case, yielding radial symmetry and precise decay rates for solutions to (
1.4
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
,
n\geq 2
and let
f\in C_{loc}^{1,\beta}([0,\infty))
satisfying
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=m^{2}>0
. Assume that
v
is a
C^{2}
solution to

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta v=f(v)\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(x)\to 0,\quad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$}.\end{cases}

Then,
v=\tau_{x_{0}}[\xi]
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\xi
strictly radially decreasing. Moreover,

(2.2)

\frac{1}{C}\frac{e^{-|x|/m}}{\max\{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}},1\}}\leq\xi(x)\leq C\frac{e^{-|x|/m}}{\max\{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}},1\}}

and we have that given any
r_{0}>0
there exists
C(r_{0})
such that for
r>r_{0}

(2.3)

\frac{1}{C(r_{0})}\xi(x)\leq-\xi^{\prime}(|x|)\leq C(r_{0})\xi(x)\quad\mbox{ for $|x|\geq r$}.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem 2]
we have that
v=\tau_{x_{0}}[\xi]
for some
\xi
strictly radially decreasing with respect to some point
x_{0}
. Furthermore, the same result guarantees

(2.4)

\frac{1}{C}\frac{e^{-|x|/m}}{\max\{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}},1\}}\leq\xi(x)\leq C\frac{e^{-|x|/m}}{\max\{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}},1\}}.

Consider
w(r)=r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\xi(r)
-this is the so-called Emden-Fowler change of variables. A direct computation in spherical coordinates (see, e.g.,
[
17
, (3-36)]
) shows that

(2.5)

w^{\prime\prime}(r)=w\Big(\frac{a(a-1)}{r^{2}}+\frac{f(w)}{w}\Big),

where
a=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Since
w(r)\sim e^{-r/m}
in virtue of (
2.4
) and
f(w)=f^{\prime}(0)w+O(w^{\beta})
, we have that for
r_{0}
large enough (depending only on
f
and
n
, we have that

(2.6)

\frac{1}{C}w(r)\leq w^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq Cw(r)\quad\mbox{if $r\geq r_{0}$}.

So, by integration we also have that
-w^{\prime}(r)
is comparable with
e^{-\frac{r}{m}}
and since

-\xi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{-w^{\prime}(r)}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}+\frac{n-1}{2}\frac{w(r)}{r^{\frac{n+1}{2}}},

we readily deduce (
2.3
).
∎

Proposition 2.3

Let

Q(\varphi,\varphi):=D^{2}J(\xi)(\varphi,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\xi)\varphi^{2}.

(1)

Given any

Q(\xi^{\prime},\psi)=-(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\xi^{\prime}\psi}{|x|^{2}}dx,

(2)

(3)

There exists
C>0

(2.9)

We rewrite first

\Delta\xi^{\prime}-f(\xi)\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime\prime\prime}+\frac{(n-1)}{r}\xi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}(\xi)\xi^{\prime}=\frac{(n-1)}{r^{2}}\xi^{\prime},

\psi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\xi

Regarding part (2), in virtue of
[

have that the kernel of

\psi\,\xi_{1}^{\prime}
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Using the expansion

\alpha f(\xi_{1})-f(\alpha\xi_{1})=(1-\alpha)\big[f(\xi_{1})-f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}\big]+O(|1-\alpha|^{1+\beta}),

together with (
3.43
) and (
3.44
), we obtain

(3.69)

\displaystyle|1-\alpha|\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[f(\xi_{1})-f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}]\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[-2\Delta\rho+f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\rho]\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi\Big|+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho|^{1+\beta}|\xi_{1}^{\prime}|\psi_{1}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{1}|^{\beta}|\rho||\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi_{1}|

\displaystyle+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\omega_{1}|^{1+\beta}+\omega_{1}|1-\alpha|^{\beta})|\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi_{1}|

\displaystyle+C|1-\alpha|^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\rho||\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi_{1}|

\displaystyle+C|1-\alpha|^{1+\beta}+CI(t).

Testing
\Delta\xi=f(\xi)
against
\xi^{\prime}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[f(\xi_{1})-f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}]\xi_{1}^{\prime}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\xi_{1}\cdot\nabla\xi^{\prime}+f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}\xi^{\prime}=-D^{2}J(\xi_{1})(\xi^{\prime},\xi)<0

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.3
.
Hence, choosing the radius

(the radius of the ball where
\phi=1
) sufficiently large, we may ensure

(3.70)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[f(\xi_{1})-f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}]\xi_{1}\psi=-\kappa<0.

On the other hand, by exploiting (
3.8
) and integrating by parts we obtain

(3.71)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[-2\Delta\rho+f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\rho]\xi_{1}^{\prime}\psi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[2\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\xi_{1}^{\prime}+f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\rho\xi^{\prime}](1-\psi)\Big|+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi_{1}^{\prime}|\nabla\rho||\nabla\psi|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\xi^{\prime}\chi_{B_{r_{1}}^{c}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then, as a combination of (
3.69
), (
3.70
), and (
3.71
), we have deduced

(3.72)

|1-\alpha|\leq(E(t)+E(\gamma))(\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C|1-\alpha|^{1+\beta}+CI(t).

Since
|1-\alpha(t)|\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, the term

|1-\alpha|^{1+\beta}
can be absorbed into the left-hand side of (
3.72
) for
t
sufficiently large.
Combining this with (
3.40
), we may choose
T
sufficiently large and
\gamma
sufficiently small so that

(3.73)

|1-\alpha|\leq\frac{\delta}{M}\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Finally, since
\alpha
was an arbitrary coefficient
\alpha_{i}
, summing (
3.73
) over
i
yields (
3.41
).
∎

3.5.
Multibubble stability dichotomy:

We start this part generalizeing the stability estimate (

) for
M

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu>0
and let
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha_{i}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]
be a
M
-bubble such that

(3.74)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}|1-\alpha_{i}|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x^{i}}\big[\xi\big]\tau_{x^{j}}\big[\xi\big]\leq\nu.

Assume that

(3.75)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\partial_{k}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]\Big|\leq\mu\|h\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3.76)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big|D^{2}J(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])(h,\tau_{x^{i}}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big])\Big|\leq\mu\|h\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Then, there exist
\nu_{0},\mu_{0}>0
(independent of
h
) such that that if
\nu\in(0,\nu_{0})
and
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})

(3.77)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2|\nabla h|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\eta)h^{2}\geq\frac{1}{C}\|h\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Proof.

Preparations:
Let us start noticing that by an elementary projection argument it suffices to prove the case in which (
3.75
) and (
3.76
) holds with equality, i.e.,

(3.78)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\partial_{k}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]=0

and

(3.79)

D^{2}J(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])(h,\tau_{x^{i}}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big])=0

for
i=1,\cdots,M
and
k=1,\cdots,n
. Let us also introduce the notation

(3.80)

B(v,w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+f^{\prime}(\eta)vw.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, given
\gamma>0
to be determined, consider a radial bump function
0\leq\phi\leq 1
such that
\phi=\phi_{\gamma}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\phi\equiv 1
in
B_{r_{1}}
and with
|\nabla\phi|\leq\gamma
and satisfying (
3.46
) and (
3.47
). Set
\phi_{i}=\tau_{x^{i}}[\phi]
and let us decompose
h=\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}h_{i}
with
h_{i}=\phi_{i}h
for

and
h_{M+1}=h\psi
, with
\psi=1-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\phi_{i}
. Upon shrinking the value of
\nu
in (
3.74
), we can take the supports of
\phi_{i}
disjoint which implies, of course, that the supports of the
h_{i}
’s are also disjoint for
i=1,\cdots,M
. Thanks to this observation, we have that

(3.81)

B(h,h)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}B(h_{i},h_{i})+2\sum_{i=1}^{M}B(h_{i},h_{M+1})+B(h_{M+1},h_{M+1}).

Step 1:
We bound
B(h_{M+1},h_{M+1})
from below.

By homogeneity of (
3.77
) we may assume
\|h\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. Focusing on the last term of the right hand side of (
3.81
), we can exploit (
3.46
) and that
f^{\prime}(s)>0
for

to guarantee that
f^{\prime}(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{C}
in the support of

and therefore

(3.82)

B(h_{M+1},h_{M+1})\geq\frac{1}{C}\|h_{M+1}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Step 2:
We show that

(3.83)

\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}B(h_{i},h_{i})\geq\frac{1}{C}\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}\|h_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

Let us notice first that trivially for

B(h_{i},h_{i})\geq-\frac{1}{C_{0}}\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Additionally, let us notice that in order to prove (
3.83
), it suffices to show that for certain constant
\theta>0
, if, then

(3.85)

\mbox{if }\quad\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq\theta,\quad\mbox{then}\quad B(h_{i},h_{i})\geq\frac{1}{C}\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Indeed, if (
3.85
) holds and we take the set of indices

(possibly empty) for which

is arbitrarily small if

3.84
) and (
3.85
) that

(3.86)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}B(h_{i},h_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}B(h_{j},h_{j})+\sum_{j\in\{1,\cdots,M+1\}\setminus J}B(h_{j},h_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\Big(-\frac{C}{C_{0}}\sum_{j\in J}\|h_{j}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\sum_{j\in\{1,\cdots,M+1\}\setminus J}\|h_{j}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\Big).

On the other hand, since
\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}\|h_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=1
, then

-\frac{C}{C_{0}}\sum_{j\in J}\|h_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\sum_{j\in\{1,\cdots,M+1\}\setminus J}\|h_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq-\frac{CJ\theta}{C_{0}}+1-\theta J\geq\frac{1}{2},

if we pick
\theta
small enough in terms of

and

. This last inequality combined with (
3.86
) yields (
3.83
).

We are left to show (
3.85
). Let

such that

(3.87)

\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq\theta

holds. Let us exploit again (

to deduce

|f^{\prime}(\eta)-f^{\prime}(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])|\leq C\gamma^{\beta},\qquad\mbox{in supp ({$\phi_{i}$})}

Therefore,

(3.89)

B(h_{i},h_{i})\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2|\nabla h_{i}|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])h^{2}-C\gamma^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}^{2}.

Now, turning to the orthogonality conditions, notice that (
3.79
), (
3.47
), and (
3.87
) combined imply

(3.90)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}\partial_{k}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]\Big|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-\phi_{i}h\partial_{k}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]\Big|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\phi_{i})h\partial_{k}\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi]\Big|\leq C\gamma\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for

. Similarly, we can exploit (
3.79
) and combine it with (

\displaystyle\Big|D^{2}J(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])(h_{i},\tau_{x^{i}}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big])\Big|=\Big|D^{2}J(\tau_{x^{i}}[\xi])(h(1-\phi_{i}),\tau_{x^{i}}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big])\Big|\leq C\gamma\|h_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence, thanks to the stability inequality for one bubble combined with (
3.89
), we deduce the existence of

small enough (and therefore

) small enough, such that

which implies (
3.83
).

Step 4:
Conclusion.

Since
\|h\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, we have from the previous step and (
3.81
) that

(3.92)

\displaystyle B(h,h)\geq\frac{1}{C}+2\sum_{i=1}^{M}B(h_{i},h_{M+1}).

On the other hand, we notice that since
|\nabla\phi|\leq\gamma
,
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
are disjoint for
i\neq j
, and that
f^{\prime}(\eta)>0
in the support of
\psi

(3.93)

\displaystyle B(h_{i},h_{M+1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\phi_{i})h\nabla h\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}-\phi_{i}h\nabla h\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}-h^{2}|\nabla\phi_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\gamma C,

where we also used that

4.
Interaction estimates and related lemmata

The first lemma below exploits (
H2
) and is key to show that tail interaction is energetically unfavorable in Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
h(0)=0
and let
H(t)=\int_{0}^{t}h(s)ds
. Assume that
h
is concave in
(0,\delta)
for some
\delta>0
. Then, if
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
is a collection of non-negative numbers satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{M}x_{i}<\delta
, then

(4.1)

H\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{M}x_{i}\Big)-\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(x_{i})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}h(x_{i})x_{j}\leq 0.

Proof.

Reasoning by induction, let us first assume that
M=2
. Since
h
is concave and thus Lipschitz, we can apply the fundamental theorem of calculus twice to
H(x_{1}+x_{2})-H(x_{1})
and to
H(x_{2})
and exploiting
H(0)=h(0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle H(x_{1}+x_{2})-H(x_{1})-H(x_{2})-h(x_{1})x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[h(x_{1}+rx_{2})-h(x_{1})-h(rx_{2})]x_{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[h^{\prime}(x_{1}+rsx_{2})-h^{\prime}(srx_{2})]x_{2}^{2}\,t\,dsdr.

By concavity, we have
h^{\prime}(x_{1}+rsx_{2})\leq h^{\prime}(srx_{2})
a.e.
s,r\in(0,1)
, showing the base case.

For the inductive step, we easily conclude by rewriting the left hand side in (
4.1
) as

\displaystyle H\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{M}x_{i}\Big)-\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(x_{i})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}h(x_{i})x_{j}

\displaystyle=H\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{M}x_{i}\Big)-H(x_{1})-H\Big(\sum_{i=2}^{M}x_{i}\Big)-h(x_{1})\sum_{2\leq j\leq M\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M-1}}x_{j}

\displaystyle+H\Big(\sum_{i+2}^{M}x_{i}\Big)-\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(x_{i})-\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq M}h(x_{i})h(x_{j}),

and by noticing that the second line is non-positive by the base case, whilst the third line is non-positive in virtue of the inductive hypothesis. ∎

Lemma 4.2
.

[Decay and pairwise interaction of bubbles]
Let
n\geq 2
,
\beta\in(0,1]
, and let
f\in C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)>0
. Let
\xi
be a positive solution to

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta\xi=f(\xi)\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\xi(x)\to 0,\quad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$}.\end{cases}

Then,
\xi
is strictly radially decreasing with respect to some point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, if we assume that
\xi
is radial with respect to the origin, the following properties hold.

•

Let us consider the pairwise interaction function

g(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi.

There exists
R_{1}>0
such that for any
r_{0}>0
, there exists
C
depending on
r_{0}
,
f
and
n
such that

(4.3)

\frac{1}{C}\xi(x)\leq\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\tau_{x}\xi(y)\xi(y)dy\leq g(|x|)\leq C\xi(x)\quad\mbox{ for $|x|\geq\max\{2,r_{0}+R_{1}\}$.}

•

Given
\delta>0
, there exists
R(\delta)
such that if
r>R(\delta)

(4.4)

\int_{B_{r}^{c}\cup B_{r}(x)^{c}}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi+\tau_{x}[\xi]|\xi^{\prime}|+\tau_{x}[|\xi^{\prime}|]\xi\leq\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}>0
, by the decaying properties of
\xi
, there exists
C(r_{0})
such that
\xi\geq\frac{1}{C(r_{0})}\xi(0)
in
B_{r_{0}}
, and let
R_{1}>0
such that
f(\xi)\geq\frac{f^{\prime}(0)}{2}\xi
in
B_{R_{1}}^{c}
, so that

(4.5)

\Delta\xi\geq\frac{1}{C}\xi\mbox{ in $B_{r}^{c}$},

for
\geq R_{1}
. In particular, (
4.5
) implies that
\xi
is subharmonic in
B_{r}^{c}
. Together these properties imply, combined with the mean value inequality for subharmonic functions, that for
|x|\geq r_{0}+R_{1}

(4.6)

g(|x|)\geq\frac{1}{C(r_{0})}\xi(0)\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\tau_{x}\xi\geq\frac{1}{C(r_{0})}\xi(|x|),

proving the lower bound in (
4.3
).

Let us proceed to estimate
g(|x|)
. Since
\xi
is strictly radially deceasing, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle g(|x|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy+\int_{\{|y|\geq|x|\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy+\xi(|x|)\int_{\{|y|\geq|x|\}}\tau_{x}\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy+C\xi(|x|).

Additionally, by (
2.4
) we have that for
|x|>4

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\{|y|<1\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\{|y|<1\}}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{m}|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{e^{-\frac{|x|}{m}}}{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\int_{\{|y|<1\}}\frac{e^{\frac{|y|}{m}}\xi(y)}{|1-y/|x||^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\xi(x).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|<|x|\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy\leq\xi(|x|-1)\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|<|x|\}}\xi(y-x)dy\leq C\xi(|x|).

Combining the previous two estimates with (
4.7
) yields

(4.9)

g(|x|)\leq C\xi(|x|)+\int_{\{1\leq|y|\leq|x|-1\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy.

We claim that the second term on the right hand side of (
4.9
) decays faster than
\frac{\xi(r)}{r^{k}}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which would imply (
4.3
). Aiming to prove this claim, let us adopt the notation
y=(\tilde{y},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Since
g
is radially symmetric we can assume that
x=|x|e_{n}
(where
e_{n}
is the
n
-th canonical unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Let us notice that for
|y|<|x|
we have the elementary inequality

(4.10)

|y-e_{n}|x||+|y|\geq\frac{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}{2|y|}+|x|

Indeed, if we take
a=|y-e_{n}|x||
and
b=|y|-|x|
, we have that

\displaystyle(a+b)(a-b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y-e_{n}|x||^{2}-(|y|-|x|)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y_{n}-|x|)^{2}-y_{n}^{2}+2|y||x|-|x|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|x|(|y|-y_{n})

which implies

(4.11)

\displaystyle|y-e_{n}|x||+|y|-|x|=\frac{2|x|(|y|-y_{n})}{|y-e_{n}|x||+|x|-|y|}\geq|y|-|y_{n}|,

by noticing that
|y-e_{n}|x||+|x|-|y|\leq 2|x|
. On the other hand, from the fundamental theorem of calculus

|y|-|y_{n}|=\sqrt{|\tilde{y}|^{2}+|y_{n}|^{2}}-|y_{n}|^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}{2\sqrt{t|\tilde{y}|^{2}+|y_{n}|^{2}}}dt\geq\frac{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}{2|y|}

which combined with (
4.11
) yields (
4.10
). We now combine (
4.10
) with (
2.4
) and use spherical coordinates
y=r\theta
to deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\{1<|y|\leq|x|-1\}}\xi(y-|x|e_{n})\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\{1<|y|\leq|x|-1\}}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{m}(||x|e_{n}-y|+|y|)}}{(|(|x|-|y|)|y|)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-|x|/m}\int_{\{1<|y|\leq|x|-1\}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}{2|y|m}}}{(|(|x|-|y|)|y|)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-|x|/m}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{1}^{|x|-1}e^{-r\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}\Big(\frac{r}{|x|-r}\Big)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}drd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x|Ce^{-|x|/m}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{|x|}e^{-|x|\frac{s}{1+s}\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}\frac{s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(1+s)^{2}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

where in the last inequality we used the change of variables
s=\frac{r}{|x|-r}
(equivalently
r=|x|\frac{s}{1+s}
). We can bound this last integral as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{|x|}e^{-|x|\frac{s}{1+s}\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}\frac{s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(1+s)^{2}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-|x|s\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}e^{-|x|\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{4m}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\int_{1}^{|x|}s^{\frac{n-5}{2}}ds.

We claim that both

g_{1}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta),

and

g_{2}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}e^{-r\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{4m}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta),

decay faster than any polynomial. We will focus on
g_{1}
since the proof for
g_{2}
is completely analogous. We observe first that

(4.12)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}(s|\tilde{\theta}|^{2})^{k}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)=0,

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Notice first that if (
4.12
) holds for
k=0
, then holds for every
k
since
(s|\tilde{\theta}|^{2})^{k}\leq 1
. On the other hand, we have that for any
\delta>0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{|\tilde{\theta}|\leq\delta\}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{|\tilde{\theta}|\geq\delta\}}\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{|\tilde{\theta}|\geq\delta\}}\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{|\tilde{\theta}|\geq\delta\}}\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{-r\frac{\delta^{3}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta).

Thus,

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-rs\frac{|\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{2m}}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\leq C\delta,

implying (
4.12
) from the arbitrariness of
\delta
. Thus, L’Hôpital’s rule allows to deduce that
\lim_{k\to\infty}g_{1}(r)r^{k}\to 0
from (
4.12
). This finally implies that
g(|x|)\leq C\xi(x)
, i.e., the upper bound in (
4.3
).

We complete the proof by showing (
4.4
). Thanks to (
2.3
) it suffices to prove that given
\delta>0
, there exists
R(\delta)
such that if
r>R(\delta)

(4.13)

\int_{B_{r}^{c}\cup B_{r}(x)^{c}}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi\leq\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi.

Let
A_{r}(x)=B_{r}^{c}\cup B_{r}(x)^{c}
. Analogously to the decomposition (
4.7
), we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{r}(x)}\tau_{x}[\xi]\xi\leq\int_{\{|y|<|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy+\xi(|x|)\int_{\{|y|\geq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\tau_{x}\xi(y)dy.

We bound the second term on the right of (
4.14
) by noticing

(4.15)

\int_{\{|y|\geq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\tau_{x}\xi(y)dy\leq\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\xi,

which vanishes as
r\to\infty
. On the other hand, from the previous part of the proof, we have that

(4.16)

\int_{\{1<|y|<|x|-1\}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy\leq C_{k}\frac{\xi(|x|)}{|x|^{k}},

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. So, assuming
r>1
, it suffices to show that given
\delta>0
, there exists
R(\delta)
such that if
r\geq R(\delta)

(4.17)

\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|\leq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\tau_{x}\xi(y)\xi(y)dy\leq\delta\xi(|x|).

Proceeding as in (
4.8
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|\leq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\xi(y-x)\xi(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|\leq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\frac{e^{\frac{-|y|}{m}}\xi(y-x)}{|y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\xi(x)\int_{\{|x|-1<|y|\leq|x|\}\cap A_{r}}\xi(y-x)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\xi(x)\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\xi(y)dy

which, thanks again to the vanishing of
\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\xi(y)dy
as
r\to\infty
, concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
2.6
, there exists
T>0
sufficiently large such that for
t\geq T

(1)

(
3.2
) has a solution and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Gamma(t)=0
,

(2)

M
-bubbles solving (
3.2
) are
\nu(t)
-interacting with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\nu(t)=0
,

(3)

the system of equations (
3.3
) has a unique solution vector
(\alpha_{1}(t),\cdots,\alpha_{M}(t))
,

(4)

and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\alpha_{i}(t)=1
for
i=1,\cdots,M.

Proof.

Let us start proving (1). Arguing by contradiction, we can find a sequence of times
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that (
3.2
) is not attained. By Proposition
2.5
, we can extract a subsequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of simple
M
-bubbles
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{k}(x)=u(x,t_{k})
satisfies
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}-\theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
. Take
k
large enough such that
\|u_{k}-\theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. We claim that
\Gamma(t_{k})
admits a solution, leading to the desired contradiction. To verify the validity of this claim it suffices to show that if

\psi_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x^{i}_{j}}\big[\xi\big],

is a minimizing sequence for
\Gamma(t_{k})
, then the centers
\{x_{j}^{k}\}
must remain bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=1,\cdots,M
. If this were not the case, we would have that
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|u_{k}-\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
contradicting the fact that
\{\psi_{j}\}
is a minimizing sequence for
\Gamma(t_{k})
.
Arguing similarly, by contradiction, it follows that that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Gamma(t)=0
.

The proofs of (2), (3), and (4) follow by a very similar argument. We proceed to outline one by one.

In the case of (2), if we assume that
\theta(t_{k})
is a minimizer of
\Gamma(t_{k})
which are not
\nu
-interacting for some
\nu>0
for
t_{k}
sufficiently large, up to extracting a subsequence, in virtue of Proposition
2.5
we have that
\|u_{k}-\theta(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
with the centers of the
M
-bubble
\theta(t_{k})
drifting apart as
k\to\infty
. This, in virtue of Proposition
2.1
, we have that
\theta(t_{k})
is
\nu(t_{k})
-interacting with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\nu(t_{k})=0
.

In the case of (3) or (4), we assume the existence of a sequence of times where
(\alpha_{1}(t),\cdots,\alpha_{M}(t))
either (3) or (4) does not hold. In either case, we invoke Proposition
2.5
to guarantee that along such sequence, namely
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}-\theta(t_{k})\|_{W^{2,2}\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
. Hence, this property combined with Proposition
2.1
implies that the systems of equations (
3.3
) can be rewritten as

(4.18)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}(t_{k})Q_{ii}^{k}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}(t_{k})Q_{ij}^{k}=Q_{ii}^{k}+E_{k}\quad\mbox{for $i=1,\cdots,M$},

with
Q_{ij}^{k}=D^{2}J(\tau_{x^{i}(t_{k})}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big])(\tau_{x^{i}(t_{k})}\big[\xi^{\prime}\big],\tau_{x^{j}(t_{k})}\big[\xi\big])
and
E_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. By proposition
2.3

Q_{ii}^{k}=D^{2}J(\xi^{\prime})(\xi,\xi^{\prime})>0
, whilst
\lim_{k\to\infty}Q_{ij}^{k}=0
when
j\neq i
. Thus, (
4.18
) is a perturbation of a linear system whose coefficient matrix is an invertible diagonal matrix, which implies that
\alpha_{i}(t_{k})
is uniquely determined. From (
4.18
), it is also clear that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_{i}(t_{k})\to 1
, yielding the desired contradiction.
∎

Our last auxiliary result is a quantitative separation property. Let

P=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{M}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a collection of distinct points, and let
y\in P
be an extremal point of
P
, meaning that
y
cannot be written as a non-trivial convex combination of the elements of
P\setminus\{y\}
. Consider the set of directions

V_{y}=\left\{\frac{x_{i}-y}{|x_{i}-y|}\,\Bigg|\,x_{i}\in P\setminus\{y\}\right\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Since
y
is extremal for
P
, the set
V_{y}
is strictly contained in a hemisphere. Indeed, otherwise
0
would belong to the convex hull of
V_{y}
. Hence there would exist coefficients
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
, with
\sum_{x_{i}\neq y}\lambda_{i}=1
, such that

\sum_{x_{i}\neq y}\lambda_{i}\frac{x_{i}-y}{|x_{i}-y|}=0.

Rearranging terms gives

\frac{1}{S}\sum_{x_{i}\neq y}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{|x_{i}-y|}x_{i}=y,\qquad S=\sum_{x_{i}\neq y}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{|x_{i}-y|},

which expresses
y
as a non-trivial convex combination of the remaining points of
P
, contradicting its extremality.

The following lemma provides a quantitative strengthening of this geometric observation.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{M}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a collection of distinct points.
Then there exists a constant
D=D(M,n)>0
and a point
y\in P
for which one can find a unit vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
satisfying

(4.19)

(x_{i}-y)\cdot e\geq\frac{1}{D(M,n)}\,|x_{i}-y|,\qquad\text{for every }x_{i}\in P\setminus\{y\}.

Moreover, if the points of
P
satisfy the separation condition

|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq L\qquad\text{for all }i\neq j,

then there exists a constant
D_{2}=D_{2}(M,n)>0
such that

(4.20)

(x_{i}-z)\cdot e\geq\frac{1}{D_{2}(M,n)}\,|x_{i}-z|,\qquad\text{for every }x_{i}\in P\setminus\{y\},

whenever
z\in B_{L^{\prime}}(y)
, where
L^{\prime}=\frac{L}{2D(M,n)}
.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.19
) by induction on
M
. If
M=2
, the statement is immediate: taking

e=\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|},\qquad y=x_{1},

the inequality holds with
D=1
.

Assume now that the statement holds for any collection of
M-1
points.
Apply the inductive hypothesis to
P\setminus\{x_{M}\}
. Then there exist

y_{0}\in P\setminus\{x_{M}\}
and
e_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that

(4.21)

(x-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}\geq\frac{1}{D(M-1,n)}|x-y_{0}|,\qquad\text{for all }x\in P\setminus\{x_{M},y_{0}\}.

Set

K=\max\{4D(M-1,n),2\}.

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1:
Assume

(4.22)

\frac{|(x_{M}-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}|}{|x_{M}-y_{0}|}\geq\frac{1}{K}.

If
(x_{M}-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}>0
, then (
4.19
) holds with

y=y_{0}
,
e=e_{0}
, and
D(M,n)=K
.

If
(x_{M}-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}<0
, we set
y=x_{M}
and
e=e_{0}
.
From (
4.22
) we obtain

(y_{0}-x_{M})\cdot e_{0}\geq\frac{1}{K}|x_{M}-y_{0}|.

For
x\in P\setminus\{x_{M},y_{0}\}
, we deduce from (
4.21
) that

(x-x_{M})\cdot e_{0}=(x-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}+(y_{0}-x_{M})\cdot e_{0}\geq\frac{1}{D(M-1,n)}|x-y_{0}|+\frac{1}{K}|x_{M}-y_{0}|.

Using the triangle inequality and the definition of
K
, we deduce

(x-x_{M})\cdot e_{0}\geq\frac{1}{K}|x-x_{M}|.

Case 2:
Assume

(4.23)

\frac{|(x_{M}-y_{0})\cdot e_{0}|}{|x_{M}-y_{0}|}<\frac{1}{K}.

Let

e_{1}=\frac{x_{M}-y_{0}}{|x_{M}-y_{0}|},\qquad\alpha=\frac{2}{K},\qquad e=\frac{e_{0}+\alpha e_{1}}{|e_{0}+\alpha e_{1}|}.

Then
|e_{0}\cdot e_{1}|\leq 1/K
, and therefore

(4.24)

|e_{0}+\alpha e_{1}|^{2}\leq 1+\frac{8}{K^{2}}\leq 4.

A direct computation gives

(x_{M}-y_{0})\cdot e=|x_{M}-y_{0}|\frac{e_{0}\cdot e_{1}+\alpha}{|e_{0}+\alpha e_{1}|}\geq\frac{1}{2K}|x_{M}-y_{0}|.

If
x\in P\setminus\{x_{M},y_{0}\}
, writing

\nu_{x}=\frac{x-y_{0}}{|x-y_{0}|}
, the inductive hypothesis gives

\nu_{x}\cdot e_{0}\geq 1/D(M-1,n)
. Hence

(x-y_{0})\cdot e=|x-y_{0}|\frac{e_{0}\cdot\nu_{x}+\alpha e_{1}\cdot\nu_{x}}{|e_{0}+\alpha e_{1}|}\geq|x-y_{0}|\frac{1/D(M-1,n)-\alpha}{2}\geq\frac{1}{4D(M-1,n)}|x-y_{0}|.

This completes the proof of (
4.19
).

We now prove (
4.20
).
Let
z\in B_{L^{\prime}}(y)
and write
z=y+z_{0}
with
|z_{0}|\leq L^{\prime}
.
For
x\in P\setminus\{y\}
,

(x-z)\cdot e=(x-y)\cdot e-z_{0}\cdot e\geq\frac{1}{D(M,n)}|x-y|-|z_{0}|.

Since
|x-y|\geq L
and

L^{\prime}=\frac{L}{2D(M,n)}
, we obtain

(x-z)\cdot e\geq\frac{1}{2D(M,n)}|x-y|.

Finally,

|x-z|\leq|x-y|+|z-y|\leq|x-y|+L^{\prime}\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2D(M,n)}\Bigr)|x-y|,

which yields (
4.20
) with a suitable constant

D_{2}(M,n)>0
.
∎
