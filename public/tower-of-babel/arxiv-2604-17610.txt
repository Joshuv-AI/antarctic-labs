Parameter Estimation of the Gravitational-Wave Angular Power Spectrum in the Dirty-Map Space
Erik T. Floden, Alex E. Granados, Vuk Mandic

Abstract
We consider a search for the anisotropic stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) that decomposes the sky map into its spherical harmonics components in order to obtain estimators of the angular power spectrum. Such a search often requires the inversion of a Fisher information matrix which contains small singular values. Rather than dealing with biases induced by regularization methods used to facilitate this matrix inversion, we opt to avoid this inversion step entirely by working in the so-called “dirty map” space, and we introduce methodology for statistical model inference in this space. We apply our methodology to simulated model signals added to detector noise characterized by Advanced LIGO’s third observing run and consider angular power spectra for both the SGWB auto-correlation search as well as a cross-correlation search between the SGWB and electromagnetic tracers of matter structure in the universe. In both cases we are able to reliably recover simulated model parameters for sufficiently strong signals up to maximum order spherical harmonic modes of ℓ m ​ a ​ x = 10 \ell_{max}=10 . We find the limitations of our methodology to arise from the computational cost of testing complex models, the assumption of Gaussianity of the angular power spectrum, and a cosmic variance-like source of uncertainty which scales with the strength of the underlying signal.

II
Inference in the Clean Space

II.1
SGWB Angular Power Spectrum

When performing an auto-power search for the SGWB using the spherical harmonics decomposition of the sky, the resulting map of GW power on the sky is given by
48

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\ell m}=\sum_{f,t}\gamma_{\ell m}^{*}(f,t)\frac{H(f)}{P_{1}(f,t)P_{2}(f,t)}\hat{C}(f,t).

(1)

Here,
H(f)
is the frequency power spectrum of the SGWB model, taken to be a power-law,

\displaystyle H(f)=\left(\frac{f}{f_{\rm ref}}\right)^{\alpha-3},

(2)

where the spectral index
\alpha
takes on different values for different SGWB models and
f_{ref}
is some reference frequency, usually chosen to be 25 Hz
4
. Eq.
1
also includes the one-sided power spectra of two detectors
P_{1}(f,t),P_{2}(f,t)
, the overlap-reduction factor
\gamma_{\ell m}(f,t)
, and the cross-correlation spectra
\hat{C}(f,t)
. In these quantities we use
t
to denote time segments into which the data is divided, and we refer the reader to
48
for more details regarding these quantities. The map defined in Eq.
1
represents the GW power convolved with the response of the detectors. For this reason this map is referred to as the “dirty” map. The covariance matrix for the elements of the dirty map is referred to as the Fisher matrix and given as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=\sum_{f,t}\gamma_{\ell m}^{*}(f,t)\frac{H^{2}(f)}{P_{1}(f,t)P_{2}(f,t)}\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(f,t).

(3)

Previous searches for the SGWB in the spherical harmonics basis deconvolve the GW signal from the detector response. The resulting estimators of the elements of the “clean” map are given by

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\ell m}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\big(\Gamma_{\rm R}^{-1}\big)_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\hat{x}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}.

(4)

Note that obtaining estimators of the clean map requires inversion of the Fisher matrix. Gaps in the sensitivity of the detector network manifest as small singular values in the Fisher matrix. These small singular values of
\Gamma
result in large uncertainties in the estimators of the clean map elements. Regularization of
\Gamma
is often performed through the use of singular value decomposition (SVD) to reduce the uncertainty in the clean map estimators. The regularization method employed by the most recent searches involves reassigning the smallest one-third of the eigenvalues of
\Gamma
to infinity
2
;
3
;
4
. The subscript
\rm R
in Eq.
4
denotes that regularization was performed in calculating
\Gamma^{-1}
.

Finally the estimators of the clean map are used to calculate estimators of the angular power spectrum:

\displaystyle\hat{A_{\ell}}=\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}f_{\rm ref}^{3}}{3H_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{1+2\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[|\hat{a}_{\ell m}|^{2}-(\Gamma^{-1}_{R})_{\ell m,\ell m}\right],

(5)

where
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant. The second term in the sum in Eq.
5
is a bias correction term which comes from the definitions of the expectation value and variance (see Appendix
A
):

\displaystyle\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\hat{x}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\rangle\langle\hat{x}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle\approx\Gamma_{\ell m\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

(6)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\hat{a}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle\langle\hat{a}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle\approx\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}.

(7)

Since elements of the dirty map result from summing over many time segments of data, they are well-approximated as Gaussian-distributed random variables. The corresponding elements of the clean map are therefore also Gaussian-distributed. Note, therefore, that the angular power spectrum is a sum of squared Gaussian random variables and therefore not Gaussian. When
\ell
is large, the sum in Eq.
5
is performed over enough
\ell m
modes that the estimators
\hat{A_{\ell}}
may be approximated as Gaussian, but in the case of the SGWB search, the maximum spherical harmonics mode
\ell_{max}=4
is typically used for a background of compact binary coalescences.

With this limitation in mind, in this study we will approximate the angular power spectrum as Gaussian-distributed when developing a Bayesian likelihood analysis. Specifically, we use a Gaussian likelihood to compare our estimators of the angular power spectrum with model predictions
A_{l}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
parameterized by a set of parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
:

\ln{\mathcal{L}}\left(\hat{A}_{\ell}\mid A_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\propto-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{A}_{\ell}-A_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)(K^{A})^{-1}_{\ell,\ell}\left(\hat{A}_{\ell}-A_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)

(8)

where the covariance matrix
K^{A}
is given by
55
:

K^{A}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}f^{3}}{3H_{0}^{2}}\right)^{4}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\frac{|({\Gamma}_{R}^{-1})_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}+2Re[\hat{a}^{*}_{\ell m}({\Gamma}_{R}^{-1})_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}]}{(1+2\ell)(1+2\ell^{\prime})}.

(9)

Note that the above expression requires an inversion of the Fisher matrix
\Gamma
, which is often done through the use of SVD regularization. However, the bias introduced by this regularization propagates to the elements of
\hat{A}
and
K^{A}

54
. In order to avoid the effects of this bias on our estimators of angular power spectrum model parameters, we may choose to perform the parameter estimation in the dirty map space, where Fisher matrix inversion is no longer required.

II.2
SGWB-EM Angular Cross-Power Spectrum

Cross-correlations of the SGWB anisotropy with anisotropy measured in different EM tracers of structure of matter have the potential to aid in indirectly detecting the anisotropic SGWB
25
;
23
;
53
;
54
. Such EM tracers include galaxy counts surveys (GC), gravitational weak lensing, and the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)
25
;
54
. Each of the EM tracers are expected to be related to different astrophysical and cosmological processes that could give rise to the SGWB. The SGWB-EM cross-correlations, therefore, provide probes of what could give rise to anisotropy in SGWB, as well as of how the matter structure formed.

To estimate the SGWB-EM cross-correlation angular power spectrum in the clean space, we again start with equations Eq.
1
,
3
, and
4
to estimate the SGWB spherical harmonics decomposition (
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
’s). On the EM tracer side, the data often come from different surveys binned in a pixel basis on the sky. We then transform the EM data to the spherical harmonic basis and estimate EM tracer spherical harmonics coefficients, (
\hat{b}_{\ell m}
’s), following the process outlined in
30
. By default,
\hat{b}_{\ell m}
’s are already given in the clean space. The EM tracer power spectrum in the clean space,
\hat{B}_{\ell}
, is given by

\hat{B}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)r_{sky}^{2}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\lvert\hat{b}_{\ell m}\rvert^{2},

(10)

where
r_{sky}
is the fraction of the sky that was observed in the survey used for the EM tracer data. Together, the
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
’s and
\hat{b}_{\ell m}
’s combine to construct the SGWB-EM tracer angular cross-correlation power spectrum from the data in the clean space,
\hat{C}_{\ell}
, given by

\hat{C}_{\ell}=\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}f^{3}_{ref}}{3H^{2}_{0}}\right)\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)r_{sky}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(\hat{a}_{\ell m}\right)^{*}\hat{b}_{\ell m}.

(11)

As already noted, the
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
’s are multivariate Gaussian distributed with zero mean and covariance given by the regularized inverse Fisher matrix. Currently, the SGWB anisotropy signal-to-noise ratio is very low compared to that obtained in EM surveys. Consequently, the
\hat{b}_{\ell m}
’s contribute negligible noise to the
\hat{C}_{\ell}
’s. In this limit, we can view Eq.
11
as a linear transformation of the multi-variate Gaussian random variables
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
, resulting in multi-variate Gaussian-distributed estimators
\hat{C}_{\ell}
’s whose covariance matrix
K_{C}
can be derived from the inverse Fisher matrix and the
\hat{b}_{\ell m}
’s:

(K_{C})_{\ell,p}=\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}f^{3}_{ref}}{3H^{2}_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{sky}}\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)(2p+1)}\sum_{m,q}\left(\hat{b}_{\ell m}\right)^{*}\left(\Gamma_{R}^{-1}\Gamma\Gamma_{R}^{-1}\right)_{\ell m,pq}\hat{b}_{pq}.

(12)

We can then proceed to infer model parameters using Gaussian likelihood given by:

\ln{\mathcal{L}}\left(\hat{C}_{\ell}\mid C_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\propto-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{C}_{\ell}-C_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)(K_{C})^{-1}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left(\hat{C}_{\ell^{\prime}}-C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)

(13)

where
C_{\ell}^{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is a model cross-power spectrum in the clean space defined by some parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

III
Inference in the Dirty Space

III.1
SGWB Angular Power Spectrum in the Dirty Space

Theoretical models of the SGWB auto-power angular power spectrum make predictions in the clean space. That is, they are not convolved with any detector response function. Therefore, if we wish to perform a parameter estimation analysis for the angular power spectrum in the dirty map space using some theoretical model, we must convert that model to the dirty space. As shown in Eq.
4
, the Fisher matrix allows for transformations between the dirty and clean spaces. However, this transformation is done in terms of the individual map elements rather than the angular power spectrum itself.

As an example, let us assume a simple theoretical model for the clean-space angular power spectrum
A_{\ell}^{M}
as a function of
\ell
:

A_{\ell}^{M}(\theta)=\theta\ell.

(14)

Here,
\theta
is some proportionality constant which we wish to estimate, and the
M
superscript denotes that the power spectrum is a model. As we show in Appendix
B
, the corresponding model in the dirty map space can be written as

\displaystyle{X}^{M}_{\ell}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\theta)\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)\right).

(15)

To demonstrate inference in the dirty space, we perform simulations of both the signal and the noise in the dirty space and apply likelihood maximization. Specifically:

1.

We generate a set of noise-only dirty space elements
\hat{x}_{\ell m}
by drawing them from a multivariate Gaussian distribution with zero means and the Fisher matrix as the covariance matrix, as done in
18
.

2.

We next generate clean space model elements
a_{\ell m}^{M}(\theta_{0})
for a chosen value of the parameter
\theta_{0}
— we treat them as independent, Gaussian-distributed random variables. They are complex-valued, and for a given
\ell
we assume their real and imaginary components may be independently drawn from the Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{2}A^{M}_{\ell}(\theta_{0}))
. For the case when
m=0
, the corresponding
a_{\ell 0}^{M}
are purely real, and they may be drawn from
\mathcal{N}(0,A^{M}_{\ell}(\theta_{0}))
.

3.

We then convert the drawn clean model map into the dirty space by multiplying it with the Fisher matrix:

x_{\ell m}^{M}(\theta_{0})=\Gamma_{\ell m,pq}a_{pq}^{M}(\theta_{0}),

(16)

and we add this dirty signal sky-map to the noise-only map to produce a realistic simulated sky-map:

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}(\theta_{0})=\hat{x}_{\ell m}+x^{M}_{\ell m}(\theta_{0}).

(17)

The corresponding simulated angular power spectrum is (see Appendix
C
):

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+2\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[|\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}(\theta_{0})|^{2}-\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}\right].

4.

We next define the likelihood function to perform statistical inference. While
\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}
follow the generalized multivariate
\chi^{2}
distribution, we will approximate it here with a Gaussian, analogously to Eq.
8
:

\ln{\mathcal{L}}\left(\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}(\theta_{0})\mid X_{\ell}^{M}(\theta)\right)\propto-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}(\theta_{0})-X_{\ell}^{M}(\theta)\right)(K^{X}(\theta))^{-1}_{\ell,\ell}\left(\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}(\theta_{0})-X_{\ell}^{M}(\theta)\right).

(19)

5.

For each value of the parameter
\theta
, the dirty-space model spectrum is evaluated using Eq.
15
. The covariance matrix
(K^{X})
featured in Eq.
19
has two contributions. First, there is a contribution due to the detector noise encoded in the Fisher matrix. This contribution is analogous to Eq.
9
and is given by

K^{X}_{Fisher,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\frac{|({\Gamma})_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}+2Re[\hat{x}^{*}_{\ell m}{\Gamma}_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\hat{x}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}]}{(1+2\ell)(1+2\ell^{\prime})}.

(20)

Second, there is a contribution due to the random draw of the signal map elements
a^{M}_{\ell m}
in step 2, which introduces statistical fluctuations in the realization of the simulated signal. This contribution to the covariance may be calculated numerically, by repeating steps 2-3
10^{4}
times and drawing
10^{4}
realizations of
X_{\ell}^{M}
’s for the given value of
\theta
(see Appendix
B
). One can then manually calculate the covariance matrix from these samples, which we refer to as
K^{X}_{Draw}(\theta)
. The total covariance
K^{X}
is given as the sum:

K^{X}=K^{X}_{Fisher}+K^{X}_{Draw}(\theta).

(21)

Note that, as shown in Appendix
B
,
K^{X}_{Draw}(\theta)
depends on the model parameters and therefore must be calculated separately for each set of parameter values when evaluating the likelihood function.

III.2
SGWB-EM Angular Cross-Power Spectrum in the Dirty Space

To develop the inference formalism using the SGWB-EM tracer cross-correlation angular power spectrum in dirty space, we follow a similar procedure to the SGWB auto-power case. On the model side,
we again use a simple theoretical model for the clean-space SGWB-EM angular power spectrum
C_{\ell}^{M}
as a function of
\ell
:

C_{\ell}^{M}(\rho)=\rho\ell\sqrt{A_{\ell}^{M}B_{\ell}^{M}}.

(22)

Here,
A_{\ell}^{M}
’s represent the SGWB clean-space angular power spectrum, which we assume is known and can be calculated using Eq.
14
with
\theta=5\times 10^{-99}
. We also assume that the EM tracer angular power spectrum is known and given by
B_{\ell}^{M}=A_{\ell}^{M}/1000
. Finally,
\rho
is the correlation coefficient between SGWB and the EM tracer, and we treat it as a free parameter in our inference framework.

As we show in Appendix
D
, the corresponding dirty-space model can be written analytically as:

Z_{\ell}^{M}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}(\rho)\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(23)

Similarly to the auto-power case, we demonstrate the inference formalism using simulated data.
We follow a similar procedure to that outlined in Section
III.1
, with slight complications due to the inclusion of the EM tracer. We assume that the EM tracer clean map elements,
b_{\ell m}^{M}
, are complex-Gaussian distributed random variables with mean zero. Similarly to the auto-power case, for a given
\ell
we assume that the real and imaginary components of
a_{\ell m}^{M}
and
b_{\ell m}^{M}
can be drawn from a bi-variate Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\Sigma_{ab})
where
\Sigma_{ab}
is given by

\Sigma_{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}A_{\ell}^{M}&\frac{1}{2}C_{\ell}^{M}(\rho_{0})\\
\frac{1}{2}C_{\ell}^{M}(\rho_{0})&\frac{1}{2}B_{\ell}^{M}\end{pmatrix},

(24)

except for the
m=0
case which is purely real:

\Sigma_{ab}^{m=0}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\ell}^{M}&C_{\ell}^{M}(\rho_{0})\\
C_{\ell}^{M}(\rho_{0})&B_{\ell}^{M}\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

Here,
\rho_{0}
is the correlation coefficient chosen for the signal simulation.
The SGWB and EM tracer sky-maps drawn from this distribution are transformed into the dirty space, using Eq.
16
for the SGWB map elements and the following equation for the EM tracer map elements:

y_{\ell m}^{M}(\rho_{0})=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell^{\prime}=\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}b_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M}(\rho_{0})

(26)

The dirty-space simulated SGWB sky-map is then given by Eq.
17
, and we define the simulated SGWB-EM tracer angular cross-power spectrum in dirty space as:

\hat{Z}^{sim}_{\ell}(\rho_{0})=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}(\rho_{0})\right)^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}(\rho_{0})

(27)

For parameter estimation in the dirty space, the log-likelihood is analogous to Eq.
13
:

\ln{\mathcal{L}\left(\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}(\rho_{0})\mid Z_{\ell}^{M}(\rho)\right)}\propto-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}(\rho_{0})-Z_{\ell}^{M}(\rho)\right)\left(K^{Z}\right)^{-1}_{\ell,\ell}\left(\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}(\rho_{0})-Z_{\ell}^{M}(\rho)\right)

(28)

The covariance matrix
K^{Z}
has two contributions, just like in the SGWB-only case. First, there is a contribution due to the detectors’ noise propagated from the Fisher matrix as:

\displaystyle(K_{Fisher}^{Z})_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\frac{1}{2\ell^{\prime}+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\hat{y}_{\ell m}\right)^{*}\hat{y}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}-(-1)^{m}\Gamma_{\ell-m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right.

(29)

\displaystyle\left.+(-1)^{m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}-m^{\prime}}+(-1)^{m+m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell-m,\ell^{\prime}-m^{\prime}}\right].

Second, there is an additional uncertainty due to the random draw of the signal map elements
a_{\ell m}^{M}
and
b_{\ell m}^{M}
. This contribution is again calculated numerically by drawing SGWB and EM tracer map elements and calculating
Z_{\ell}^{M}
1,000 times (see Appendix
E.1
for more information on this calculation). We refer to this covariance as
K^{Z}_{draw}
, to give the total covariance as sum of the two sources of covariance:

K^{Z}(\rho)=K^{Z}_{Fisher}+K^{Z}_{draw}(\rho).

(30)

IV
Results

Here we apply the methodology described in the preceding sections and report the results of our parameter estimation scheme in the dirty map space for both the SGWB auto-power case as well as the cross-power case.

IV.1
SGWB Auto-Power Results

We carry out the procedure outlined in Sec.
III.1
. We add simulated model signal into noise-only maps and obtain dirty space angular power spectra which we then compare to the model using the likelihood defined in Eq.
19
. Noise maps are generated using the Fisher matrix
\Gamma
from Advanced LIGO’s third observing run (O3). The likelihood function is evaluated over a grid of values of the parameter
\theta
centered on the true injected value
\theta_{0}
. In a Bayesian formalism, this choice constitutes a uniform prior for our model parameter, and the posterior distribution of
\theta
is, therefore, equal to the likelihood function up to a normalization factor.

In Fig.
1
we show an example of a posterior distribution for the case where
\theta_{0}=2\times 10^{-98}
and
\ell_{max}=6
. The value of
\theta
for which the posterior is at its peak is recorded, and the process is repeated 1000 times, each with a different realization of the model signal added into a new realization of noise. The collection of 1000 recovered
\hat{\theta}
values are binned into a histogram shown in Fig.
2
. We choose to approximate this distribution of recovered
\hat{\theta}
values as Gaussian and apply a Gaussian fit. The mean of this fit
\mu_{\theta}
and its standard deviation
\sigma_{\theta}
then capture how well our inference formalism recovers the true model parameter
\theta_{0}
.

We plot
\mu_{\theta}
as a function of the injected parameter
\theta_{0}
in Fig.
3
. For a weak signal injection (low
\theta_{0}
), the simulated data are dominated by the detector noise, and our mean recovery deviates from the correct parameter value, obtaining estimators which fall below
\theta_{0}
. This bias at low
\theta_{0}
likely comes from the assumption of a Gaussian fit in Fig.
2

Note however that the 95

K^{X}_{Fisher}

K^{X}_{Draw}

While this contribution originates from our procedure of artificially adding signal into noise maps, there is an analogous source of variance which arises in real data analysis (without any signal simulations). This so-called cosmic variance results from only having a single realization of the SGWB and is proportional to the square of the angular power spectrum itself

;
13
;
45
;
44
;
20
.

V
Conclusion

SGWB auto-correlation searches for the anisotropic SGWB continue to be a promising avenue of research given their potential for informing our understanding of the distribution of GW sources both locally within our galaxy and also in the large-scale structure of the universe.
Cross-correlation of the SGWB with EM tracers of structure in the universe is a powerful method that simultaneously probes the sources or anisotropy in the SGWB and has the possibility of indirect detection of the anisotropic SGWB before direct detection methods.
Parameter estimation for these analyses are hindered by angular resolution limits stemming from inverting an ill-defined Fisher matrix.

We provided an alternative method of parameter estimation, for the auto-power and cross-power cases, that avoids inversion of the ill-defined Fisher matrix.
We achieved this by convolving the power spectra models with the GW detector response such that the analysis is performed in the dirty space.
We also demonstrated the effectiveness of this framework with simple models and signals injected into noise simulated using LIGO O3 sensitivity.

In the GW auto-power case, we have demonstrated our ability to perform parameter estimation for a simple model with a single parameter. As the strength of the injected signal increased, our parameter estimates increased in accuracy, but the width of our confidence intervals increased proportionally to signal strength. We tested our parameter estimation for spherical harmonic multipoles of
\ell_{max}=4
up to
\ell_{max}=10
and found that, while the precision of our parameter estimates increased at higher
\ell_{max}
, their accuracy slightly decreased.

For the cross-power case, using the correlation coefficient
\rho
as our free parameter, we were able to demonstrate that we can detect a correlation between SGWB and EM tracer sky map distributions down to a correlation of
\rho_{0}=0.4
for
\ell_{max}=6
with increasing sensitivity as
\ell_{max}
is increased.

There are some limitations to this framework for parameter estimation.
For both cases, we used simple models that we expect would be relatively easy to recover and interpret.
Increasing complexity by increasing
\ell_{max}
, including a more complicated model, or estimating more parameters proved to be computationally expensive under the current code framework.

Another limitation was the draw uncertainty, as it appeared to be the dominant source of uncertainty.
We posit this is a good representation of the cosmic variance, but it nevertheless limits the sensitivity of the analysis for both the auto-power and cross-power cases. As we increase the angular scale, this affect decreases.

In this study we have made the simplifying assumption that the SGWB auto-power angular spectrum is approximated as a Gaussian-distributed random variable, allowing us to define a simple likelihood function for our parameter estimation. While the assumption of Gaussianity is justified for high values of
\ell_{max}
(as is done for other contexts such as the cosmic microwave background
41
;
36
;
42
;
34
;
37
), it is less appropriate at the larger angular scales to which the SGWB search is limited
6
;
12
. It has been shown that the angular power spectrum is more accurately described by a generalized
\chi^{2}
distribution for which a closed-form likelihood does not exist, and distribution-free model validation methods have been developed to circumvent this issue
9
;
55
.

Future work in this area could include applying this framework to various proposed theoretical models. Such theoretical models for an anisotropic SGWB due to CBC events may be found in
26
;
22
for the auto-power case and
54
for the cross-power case.

An aspect we did not explore is the impact of shot noise to this framework for parameter estimation as is included for the cross-power case in
54
.
This is the contribution to variance which arises from the spatial and temporal discreteness of signal sources, and future work should include it in the covariance calculations.

For the cross-power case, future work should include the EM tracer sky maps for GC, weak lensing, and the CMB into the analysis. Further, more sophisticated models should be implemented in place of the simple toy model used in this study.

Appendix A
Dirty space SGWB auto-power angular power spectrum estimators

Consider the estimators of the clean map elements
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
, dirty map elements
\hat{x}_{\ell m}
, and Fisher matrix
\Gamma_{\ell m\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}
. From
48
we have, in the weak signal approximation,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\hat{x}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\rangle\langle\hat{x}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle\approx\Gamma_{\ell m\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

(31)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\hat{a}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle\langle\hat{a}^{*}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\rangle\approx\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

(32)

If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and
m=m^{\prime}
, then we have

\displaystyle\langle|\hat{x}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}\approx\Gamma_{\ell m\ell m}=var(\hat{x}_{\ell m})

(33)

\displaystyle\langle|\hat{a}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}\approx\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}=var(\hat{a}_{\ell m})

(34)

Now consider the (biased) estimators of the clean map angular power spectrum given by
48
:

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|\hat{a}_{\ell m}|^{2}.

(35)

Find the expectation value of both sides:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\langle\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|\hat{a}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle|\hat{a}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle.

(36)

Substitute from Eq.
34
:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}+\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}.

(38)

If the purpose of this estimator is to estimate the angular power spectrum of the SGWB sky realization in our universe, then we can assume
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle\neq 0
. Note that this is different from estimating the
true
SGWB angular power spectrum, of which our universe is only one realization—this is further discussed below. Therefore, we can define

\displaystyle{A}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2},

(39)

as the angular power spectrum in our universe, and we can rearrange Eq.
37
to give the result from
48
:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}+A_{\ell}.

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\hat{A}_{\ell}-\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m},

(41)

where the left side of Eq.
41
is written as
\hat{A}^{\prime}_{\ell}
to denote an unbiased estimator. Therefore
\langle\hat{A}^{\prime}_{\ell}\rangle=A_{\ell}
.

Note again that we relied on the fact that
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle\neq 0
. Namely, we are assuming
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle=a_{\ell m}
, where
a_{\ell m}
is the true value of the clean map element. Therefore
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle
is not an average over all possible realizations of the sky map, but rather an average over observations of our particular realization of the sky. In the former case, we would find
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle=0
, and in the latter case,
\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle=a_{\ell m}
.

We can repeat the above procedure in the dirty map space. By again assuming
\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\rangle=x_{\ell m}\neq 0
, where
x_{\ell m}
is the true value of the dirty map element, we’d find for the angular power spectrum:

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\hat{X}_{\ell}-\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\Gamma_{\ell m\ell m},

(42)

where

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|\hat{x}_{\ell m}|^{2}

(43)

and, equivalently to Eq.
39
,

\displaystyle{X}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle|\hat{x}_{\ell m}|\rangle^{2}.

(44)

Appendix B
Dirty space SGWB auto-power angular power spectrum model

Now let us consider the case in which we have a GW sky map resulting from a theoretical model
M
. This map may be in the dirty space,
x^{M}_{\ell m}
, or clean space,
a^{M}_{\ell m}
.

For a given element of the clean space model map
a^{M}_{\ell m}
, we draw the real and imaginary components from the same Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{2}A^{M}_{\ell})
(or
\mathcal{N}(0,A^{M}_{\ell})
when
m=0
). The variance of a given map element is therefore

\displaystyle var(a^{M}_{\ell m})=A^{M}_{\ell}=\langle|{a}^{M}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle-\langle{a}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}

(45)

If we follow the same procedure as before, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{A}^{RM}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(a^{M}_{\ell m})+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle{a}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}.

(46)

Here we use
A^{RM}_{\ell}
to denote the model angular power spectrum reconstructed from its constituent
a^{M}_{\ell m}
values. The distribution from which we are drawing the map elements explicitly has a mean of zero, so the expectation value in this context is referring to an average over realizations of the universe rather than an average over measurements of a single realization. Therefore we impose
\langle{a}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle=0
, leaving us with

\displaystyle\langle{A}^{RM}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(a^{M}_{\ell m})=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}A^{M}_{\ell}=A^{M}_{\ell},

(47)

\displaystyle\langle{A}^{RM}_{\ell}\rangle=A^{M}_{\ell}

(48)

which is the expected result. That is, if we draw many clean maps from a single angular power spectrum model, on average they should reconstruct the same values of
A^{M}_{\ell}
from which they were drawn.

We now apply this process to the dirty map space. First, note how we convert to the dirty map space:

\displaystyle x^{M}_{\ell m}=\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

(49)

The variance of each dirty map element would therefore be

\displaystyle var(x^{M}_{\ell m})=\langle|{x}^{M}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle-\langle{x}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}=var(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})

(50)

where

\displaystyle var(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=var\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}var(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})+2\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}<\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}Cov(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}},\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}})

(52)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}var(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})

(53)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}var(a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})

(54)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}.

(55)

Note that we assumed the covariance between different elements of the clean map is zero. We can then use the elements
x^{M}_{\ell m}
to construct the angular power spectrum in the dirty map space:

\displaystyle{X}^{M}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|{x}^{M}_{\ell m}|^{2}.

(56)

We then arrive at a result analogous to Eq.
46
by obtaining many sets of
x^{M}_{\ell m}
and using each set to construct
X^{M}_{\ell}
:

\displaystyle\langle{X}^{M}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(x^{M}_{\ell m})+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle{x}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}.

(57)

Note again that this expectation is taken over many realizations of the drawn map elements
a^{M}_{\ell m}
converted into the dirty map space. Since
\langle{a}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle=0
, we may also say that
\langle{x}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle=0
, so the second term on the right side of Eq.
57
goes to zero, and we may write:

\displaystyle\langle{X}^{M}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(x^{M}_{\ell m})

(58)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}

(59)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)\right)

(60)

One can use this result to convert a clean space angular power spectrum model directly into the dirty space.

Appendix C
Adding SGWB auto-power angular power spectrum model to detector noise in dirty space

Now if we add signal from a model
a^{M}_{\ell m}
into noise-only data
\hat{a}_{\ell m}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}=\hat{a}_{\ell m}+a^{M}_{\ell m}

(61)

\displaystyle var(\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m})=var(\hat{a}_{\ell m})+var(a^{M}_{\ell m})+2Cov(\hat{a}_{\ell m},a^{M}_{\ell m}).

(62)

We assume the noise and signal are independent of each other, so the covariance term drops out and we are left with

\displaystyle var(\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m})=\langle|\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}=\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}+A^{M}_{\ell}

(63)

where we have substituted using Eq.
34
and
45
.

Now define the quantity

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{sim}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}|^{2}.

(64)

The expected value can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle|\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle.

(65)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m})+\langle\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}

(66)

Since this estimator is meant to estimate the true angular power spectrum, and not just its realization in our universe, we can assume
\langle{a}^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle=0

\langle\hat{a}_{\ell m}\rangle=0

\langle\hat{a}^{sim}_{\ell m}\rangle=0

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\Gamma^{-1}_{\ell m\ell m}+A_{\ell}^{M}\right)

(70)

We also define the variance of these elements as

\displaystyle var(\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m})

\displaystyle=var(\hat{x}_{\ell m})+var(x^{M}_{\ell m})+2Cov(\hat{x}_{\ell m},x^{M}_{\ell m})

(71)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}+2Cov(\hat{x}_{\ell m},x_{\ell m}^{M})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}+2Cov(\hat{x}_{\ell m},\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right))

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}+2\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}Cov(\hat{x}_{\ell m},a^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}.

Estimators of the angular power spectrum are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{sim}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}|\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}|^{2}.

(72)

with expectation value

\displaystyle\langle\hat{X}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle|\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}|^{2}\rangle

(73)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}var(\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m})+\langle\hat{x}^{sim}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}.

Substituting from Eqs.
51
and
31
we may rewrite as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{X}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}A^{M}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}+x^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle^{2}\right)

(74)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left((\langle\hat{x}_{\ell m}\rangle+\langle x^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle)^{2}+\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}A_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}A_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\langle X^{M}_{\ell}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}

In the second line of Eq.
74
we assume
\langle x^{M}_{\ell m}\rangle=0
(again, averaging over many sky realizations), and in the fourth line we use the result from Eq.
58
. Our biased estimator can therefore be written as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{X}^{sim}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\langle X^{M}_{\ell}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle,

allowing us to write an expression for the unbiased estimators

\displaystyle(\hat{X}^{sim}_{\ell})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}-\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell m}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle,

where
\hat{X}_{\ell}^{sim}
is given in Eq.
72
.

Appendix D
Dirty space SGWB-EM angular cross-power spectrum model

Let us consider the case in which we have a GW sky map and EM tracer sky map resulting from a theoretical model M. This map may be in the dirty space,
x_{\ell m}^{M}
and
y_{\ell m}^{M}
, or the clean space,
a_{\ell m}^{M}
and
b_{\ell m}^{M}
, for the GW map and EM tracer maps respectively. We begin in the clean space.

For given elements of the clean space maps
a_{\ell m}^{M}
and
b_{\ell m^{{}^{\prime}}}^{M}
, we draw the real and imaginary components from a bi-variate Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\Sigma_{ab})
where
\Sigma_{ab}
is given by

\Sigma_{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}A_{\ell}^{M}&\frac{1}{2}C_{\ell}^{M}\\
\frac{1}{2}C_{\ell}^{M}&\frac{1}{2}B_{\ell}^{M}\end{pmatrix}

(77)

(or
2\Sigma_{ab}
when
m=0
).
The covariance of the given map elements is therefore

\mathrm{Cov}(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M})=C_{\ell}^{M}=\langle(a_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle-\langle(a_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}\rangle\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(78)

The model clean cross-power spectrum is given by

C^{M}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(a_{\ell m}^{M}\right)^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}

(79)

Find the expectation value of both sides:

\displaystyle\langle C_{\ell}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle\left(a_{\ell m}^{M}\right)^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(80)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\big[\langle(a_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}\rangle\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle+

(81)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\mathrm{Cov}(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M})\big]

We are assuming that
\langle a_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
as we are averaging over all possible realizations of the universe. This leaves us with

\displaystyle\langle C_{\ell}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}\left(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}C_{\ell}^{M}

(83)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\ell}^{M}}{2\ell+1}(2\ell+1)

(84)

\displaystyle\langle C_{\ell}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{\ell}^{M}

(85)

which is the expected result. That is, if we draw many clean maps from the power spectra models, on average they should reconstruct the same values of
C_{\ell}^{M}
from which they are drawn.

Applying this process to the dirty map space, we first note how to convert to the dirty space for EM tracers:

y_{\ell m}^{M}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}b_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M}

(86)

The covariance of given map elements would therefore be

\mathrm{Cov}\left(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M}\right)=\langle(x_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle-\langle(x_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}\rangle\langle y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(87)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M},\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}b_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{M}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}\left(\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M},\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}b_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{M}\right)

(89)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}\left(a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M},b_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{M}\right)\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}^{T}

(90)

and
\mathrm{Cov}\left(a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M},b_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}^{M}\right)
is only non-zero when
\ell^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime\prime}
and
m^{\prime}=m^{\prime\prime}
and
\Gamma^{T}_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}=\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime},\ell m}
so

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}\left(a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{M},b_{\ell^{\prime}m^{{}^{\prime}}}^{M}\right)\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(91)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(92)

We can write the SGWB-EM tracer cross-power in the dirty space:

\displaystyle Z_{\ell}^{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(x_{\ell m}^{M}\right)^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}

(93)

and following the same process as used before, we can arrive at an equation for the expectation value of
Z_{\ell}^{M}
:

\displaystyle\langle Z_{\ell}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M})+

(94)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\langle(x_{\ell m}^{M})^{*}\rangle\langle y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

Since
\langle a_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
and
\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
, we may also say that
\langle x_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
and
\langle y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
, so the second term of Eq.
94
goes to zero and we may write

\displaystyle\langle Z_{\ell}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M})

(95)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(96)

We used this result to convert a clean space angular cross-power spectrum model directly into the dirty space.

Appendix E
Adding SGWB-EM angular cross-power spectrum model to detector noise in dirty space

Now consider the case where we are injecting a signal from a model into noise-only GW data and assume that the EM tracer data has no associated noise. We have
\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim}
, given by Eq.
61
, and
b_{\ell m}^{M}
(since we are assuming that
b_{\ell m}
’s have no detector noise), yielding

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim},b_{\ell m}^{M})

\displaystyle=\langle(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle-\langle(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}\rangle\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(97)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{a}_{\ell m},b_{\ell m}^{M})+\mathrm{Cov}(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M})

(98)

We assume the noise and signal are independent of each other, so
\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{a}_{\ell m},b_{\ell m}^{M})=0
and
\langle b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle=0
. We are left with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim},b_{\ell m}^{M})

\displaystyle=\langle(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(99)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M})

(100)

\displaystyle=C_{\ell}^{M}

(101)

Now we have the clean space signal injected cross-power,

\hat{C}_{\ell}^{sim}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim}\right)^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}

(102)

and the expected value can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{C}_{\ell}^{sim}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\langle(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}b_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(103)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{a}_{\ell m}^{sim},b_{\ell m}^{M})

(104)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(a_{\ell m}^{M},b_{\ell m}^{M})

(105)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}C_{\ell}^{M}

(106)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{C}_{\ell}^{sim}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{\ell}^{M}

(107)

meeting our expectations.

Now, if we consider an equivalent scenario in the dirty space, the elements of the signal injected dirty space map,
x_{\ell m}^{sim}
and
y_{\ell m}^{M}
have covariance given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}\left(\hat{x}_{\ell m}^{sim},y_{\ell m}^{M}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\left(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M}\right)

(108)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(109)

\displaystyle=\langle(\hat{x}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(110)

The injected cross-power in the dirty space is defined as

\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\left(\hat{x}_{\ell m}^{sim}\right)^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}

(111)

with expectation value

\displaystyle\langle\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\langle(\hat{x}_{\ell m}^{sim})^{*}y_{\ell m}^{M}\rangle

(112)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{x}_{\ell m}^{sim},y_{\ell m}^{M})

(113)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Cov}(x_{\ell m}^{M},y_{\ell m}^{M})

(114)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(115)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{Z}_{\ell}^{sim}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell_{max}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\ell m,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}C_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\Gamma_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime},\ell m}

(116)

which, again, is similar to the expression that we get for the model in Eq.
96
. With enough data to average over, we expect to be able to recover the signal generated by the model, so this result is as expected, again without the bias correction term you see in the auto-power case.

E.1
Estimating
K_{draw}^{Z}

When injecting a signal into noise, similar to the auto-power case, we introduce additional uncertainty coming from the draw of the model map elements from the multivariate Gaussian distribution (Eq.
24
). We estimate this uncertainty by drawing the map elements in the clean space, transforming to the dirty space (Eq.
16
and
86
), and calculating the model cross-power (Eq.
93
). We
repeat this process
10^{4}
times to calculate the covariance matrix
K_{draw}^{Z}
with elements given by

\left(K_{draw}^{Z}\right)_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\langle Z_{\ell}^{M}Z_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\rangle-\langle Z_{\ell}^{M}\rangle\langle Z_{\ell^{\prime}}^{M}\rangle.

(117)
