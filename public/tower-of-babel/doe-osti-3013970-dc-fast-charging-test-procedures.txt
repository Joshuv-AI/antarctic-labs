Executive Summary
This report has been drafted as an initial effort to develop a standardized test procedure for DCFC
for LD EVs. The proposed test procedures are designed to be time-efficient, aligned with EV
discharge tests, and practical to implement, thereby minimizing the testing and computational
efforts required by OEMs and testing authorities.

This document builds upon SAE J2953-4 and ISO 12906:2024 EV charging procedures, while
also incorporating DCFC testing experience obtained through the NGP project conducted at
Argonne National Laboratory. The proposed draft seeks to harmonize the EV discharge
methodology defined in SAE J1634 or a similar standardized EV discharge procedure with EV
fast charging procedures, enabling their sequential execution. This integration is intended to
streamline EV testing, thereby reducing both test duration and effort. In addition, this draft includes
the requirements and specifications on measurement points for the EVSE and EV. These locations
are explicitly illustrated to ensure clarity regarding where measurements must be performed.

A secondary objective of this effort is to address the needs of the EV testing community. By
leveraging internationally recognized discharge and DCFC standards, this draft proposes a
consistent test procedure that produces standardized, repeatable fast charging performance testing
data based on measurable output rather than relying on vehicle-reported information. While
methodologies for DCFC performance metric definition and calculation may differ across regions,
the harmonized data acquisition framework proposed here is intended to assist comparability and
consistency worldwide. The recommendations provide an approach to capturing repeatable DCFC
performance characteristics measured at the vehicle battery. While the current measurements focus
on core vehicle parameters, we acknowledge the potential value of future research to include more
EVSE performance data.

It is noted that the definition and calculation of performance metrics are outside the scope of this
draft and may be incorporated in future revisions.

This draft procedure is structured as follows:

 • Definitions and explanations of terms
 • Testing instrumentation and measurements
 • Test conditions, pre- and post-conditions, integration into SAE J1634
 o Discharge Procedure - J1634
 o Relocation
 o Charge Procedure
 o Performance Metric Calculations (In future revisions)
 • Test procedure summary and off-nominal test scenarios

1 Terms and Definitions
• EV - Electric Vehicle
 A vehicle with one or more electric drives for vehicle propulsion. [Source: ISO 6469-3:2021]
• EVSE - EV supply equipment
 An equipment or combination of equipment that provides dedicated functions to supply
 electric energy from a fixed electrical installation or supply network to an electrically
 propelled vehicle for the purpose of charging.
• RESS - Rechargeable Energy Storage System
 A rechargeable system that stores energy for the delivery of electric energy for the electric
 drive. e.g. Battery, capacitor.
 In this draft, the EV battery pack is RESS under consideration.

• EV Predicted SOC - EV predicted state of charge
 The predicted residual capacity of the RESS available for discharge, as estimated by the EV
 BMS and displayed to the vehicle operator. The SOC is typically expressed as a fraction or
 percentage of the RESS’s total usable charge capacity.

• Pre-Conditioning/ Temperature Optimization Features
 All vehicle functions that positively impact the test results when activated either
 automatically or by the vehicle operator to bring the EV RESS temperature to the most
 favourable value for charging. (or discharging)

• DBE - Discharged Battery Energy
 Energy removed from the RESS during the discharge test procedures (SAE J1634 or similar)

 UBE – Usable Battery Energy
 The portion of the energy stored in the RESS that is available for use is referred to as the
 usable battery energy (UBE).

 1

 During the SAE J1634 procedures, the energy discharged by the RESS is utilized either to
 propel the EV or to discharge the battery using a battery cycler. Consequently, this document
 applies the definition of the DBE to determine the UBE of the EV battery.

• rUBE – Recharged Usable Battery Energy
 The recharged usable battery energy (rUBE) refers to the amount of energy supplied to the
 RESS during the charging process that is subsequently available for use during discharge.

 In practice, a portion of the recharged energy is inevitably lost as heat due to internal resistance
 and other system inefficiencies during charging as well as discharging. Accurately quantifying
 these losses requires detailed electrochemical and thermal modeling at the cell and system
 levels, an activity typically conducted by EV OEMs through extensive research and validation.

 It should be noted that, in accordance with SAE J1634, the UBE is determined during discharge
 procedures as the total energy delivered from the RESS to either propel the EV under test or
 to a battery cycler. In contrast, SAI/ISO 12906 defines rUBE as the total usable energy charged
 into the RESS during charging tests. To maintain clarity and consistency within this document,
 the previously defined concept of UBE is adopted. The term rUBE, although similar in nature,
 is not employed in this draft to avoid unnecessary complexity and reliance on OEM-specific
 battery modeling approaches.

• Soaking
 Establishing a targeted steady state condition by subjecting the electric vehicle (EV) to
 controlled environmental conditions, with particular emphasis on temperature conditioning. A
 soaking step may be incorporated into both fast-charging and discharging test procedures,
 depending on the specific test scenario.

2 EVSE Selection
EVSE selection must not constrain the vehicle's maximum DCFC performance in terms of
power, wattage, or amperage. Notably, charging adaptors may also degrade nominal
performance and are not recommended to achieve the best results, but are permissible, if
necessary, though it is important to document the adaptor used and to describe rated voltage and
amperage limits.

The subsequent sections define the instrumentation setup, specify the physical parameters to be
measured, identify the appropriate measurement points, and outline the recommended methods
for parameter acquisition on both the EV and EVSE.

3.1 Instrumentation
- All measurements shall be NIST-traceable or traceable to an equivalent national metrology
 institute in the country where the testing is performed.
- Equipment used for the measurements of the EVSE during an EV charge session includes
 wideband power analyzers, AC and DC meters, AC and DC voltage meters, and additional
 thermistors where necessary.
 o Wideband instruments (bandwidth of at least 10X that of the maximum fundamental
 frequency of interest) are required where pulsed power electronics are implemented
- Equipment must be chosen to meet the maximum rated current, voltage and power
 requirements of the EVSE and EV.
- Any watt-hour meter(s) using an integration technique shall have a maximum integration
 period of 0.05 seconds (20 Hz) so that short bursts of regeneration energy and current can be
 accommodated without causing integration errors.
- Measurement of EV data during charging sessions should adhere to requirements comparable
 to those specified for discharging sessions in SAE J1634, with the measurement equipment
 meeting the standards outlined for discharging procedures in the same document. In addition
 to these requirements, voltage measurements need to be captured during the DC fast charging
 session.
- It should be emphasized that EV-reported measurements via CAN bus, OBD-II, DoIP, or other
 in-vehicle communication protocols are not considered in this draft procedure, and their use is
 regarded as optional or redundant. Consequently, independent test instrumentation is
 mandatory to accurately and independently capture EV charging and discharging data in
 accordance with SAE J1634.
- The instrument-related data shall be documented for all tests. If the parameter does not apply
 to the test as performed (e.g., using on-board data source versus instrumented battery voltage
 sensor), the absence of the sensor and rationale should be documented.
 This documentation should include instrument type, make, serial number, calibration data, and
 any non-default settings.

3.3 Accuracy and Sampling Rates
3.3.1 Measurement Accuracy

For both AC and DC electrical measurements, the accuracy must be ±1% of the reading or 0.3%
of full scale, whichever is larger. This ensures sufficient precision across a wide range of
measurement values.

3.3.2 Sampling/Logging Rates

 • EVSE Instrumentation:
 The electrical and thermal signal measurement and data logging must occur at a rate of
 10 Hz or faster.

 • EV Instrumentation:
 The EV side signal measurement and data logging must occur at a rate of 10 Hz or faster.

4.1 Discharging for J1634
This method involves discharging the EV battery on a chassis dynamometer according to SAE
J1634. It enables controlled and repeatable test conditions by simulating driving cycles in a
laboratory environment.

A temperature soaking step may be incorporated before discharging test procedures, depending
on the specific test scenario.

 Figure 4. Illustration of Test Execution Steps: Soaking and J1634 Discharge Tests

4.1.1 EV Discharge and Charge Recommendations in SAE J1634

SAE J1634 defines four main EV discharge test procedures: Single-Cycle, Multi-Cycle, Short
Multi-Cycle (with battery cycler discharge), and Short Multi-Cycle Plus Steady-State. All test
procedures are aimed at consistently determining BEV energy consumption and range under
standardized, repeatable laboratory conditions.

 Figure 5.Chassis Dyno Test Setup Illustration
 Source: https://www.ksengineer.com/

 - Single Cycle Test (SCT)

 In SCT, the EV is fully charged and subjected to a single standard driving cycle, such as
 the Urban Dynamometer Driving Schedule (UDDS) or Highway Fuel Economy Driving
 Schedule (HFEDS). The prescribed drive cycle is executed continuously and repeatedly,
 recording total energy used and distance traveled until the vehicle is unable to complete
 the cycle due to battery depletion. The SCT is illustrated in the figure below.

 9

 Figure 6. Illustration of Single Cycle Test from SAE J1634

 - Multi Cycle Test (MCT)

 In the Multi-Cycle Test (MCT), the electric vehicle (EV) is driven through a repeated
 sequence of specified drive cycles, typically including the Urban Dynamometer Driving
 Schedule (UDDS) and the Highway Fuel Economy Driving Schedule (HFEDS) and a
 constant speed cycle. These cycles are performed in a predetermined order, continuously
 repeated until the battery is fully depleted. The MCT is illustrated in the figure below.

 Figure 7. Illustration of Multi Cycle Test from SAE J1634

 - Short Multi-Cycle Test (SMCT)

 SMTC test procedure aims to reduce the testing burden for high-capacity battery electric
 vehicles (BEVs) by combining a limited dynamometer test with direct battery discharge.
 In this method, a fully charged EV is first driven on a dynamometer through a standard
 drive cycle sequence executed once. Following this, the remaining battery energy is
 discharged using an external battery cycler (load), during which the usable energy is
 accurately measured. The total range is then estimated by summing the distance covered
 during the dynamometer phase with an equivalent distance calculated from the measured
 residual battery energy.

 10

 Figure 8. Illustration of Short Multi-Cycle Test from SAE J1634

 - Short Multi Cycle Test Plus Steady State (SMCT+)

 SMTC+ is an extension of the Simplified Multi-Test Cycle (SMTC) procedure,
 incorporating an additional constant-speed driving phase in place of direct battery
 discharge. After completing the standard multi-cycle driving phase, the vehicle is driven at
 a fixed, predetermined speed until the battery is fully depleted. This approach allows for a
 more vehicle-driven estimation of remaining range under controlled conditions.

 Figure 9. Illustration of Short Multi-Cycle Test Plus Steady State from SAE J1634

 • According to SAE J1634, all EV discharge test procedures must begin with the battery at
 or near a fully charged SOC, typically close to 100%, and continue until the EV battery is
 fully depleted or the vehicle can no longer maintain the prescribed speed, generally
 corresponding to the UBE and an SOC approaching 0%.

 • The total UBE capacity of the battery, corresponding to 100% state of UBE, is
 calculated as the total DBE measured during the SAE J1634 discharge procedures.

 • Following the J1634 discharge procedure, the RESS is charged back to 100% capacity
 using an AC Type-2 charging or following a charging process specified by the EV
 OEM, in accordance with SAE J1634 procedures.

4.1.2 Discharging to Achieve the DCFC Procedure Start Condition

Once the battery has been recharged to 100% capacity using AC Type-2 charging or a similar
charging method, it must be discharged to a particular battery energy level to initiate DCFC tests
prescribed in this draft. This discharge step is carried out according to the procedures described
below.

 Figure 11. Illustration of Test Execution Steps: Discharging to Achieve DCFC Start Condition

 1. Dynamometer Discharge

 Similar to the SAE J1634 discharge procedure, the EV battery energy may be depleted by
 operating the vehicle through one of the prescribed test cycles on a chassis dynamometer.
 It is recommended that OEM-provided battery pre-conditioning or temperature
 optimization features be activated during this phase to prepare the battery for the
 subsequent DCFC session. The discharge process shall be terminated once the desired

 12

 UBE value is reached, allowing immediate transition to DCFC. The target UBE value,
 along with its explanation, is provided at the end of this section.

 2. On-Road Discharge

 The EV battery may also be discharged through an on-road driving procedure. In this case,
 the vehicle should be operated along a predefined route and within a controlled speed
 range, in accordance with the guidelines specified in ISO 12906. It is strongly
 recommended to enable OEM pre-conditioning features, such as time-based, or
 geolocation-based, or temperature-optimization functions during this phase to ensure the
 battery is at an optimal temperature before charging. The vehicle’s BMS may
 autonomously initiate pre-conditioning based on geolocation or scheduled charging time
 to further optimize conditions for the upcoming DCFC session. The vehicle must be
 returned to the EVSE at the target UBE value, and charging shall be initiated immediately.
 The target UBE value, along with its explanation, is provided at the end of this section.

For vehicles that do not support geolocation-based or manual pre-conditioning, a standard chassis
dynamometer or on-road discharge shall be carried out. In such cases, the act of driving alone will
contribute to thermal preparation of the battery. As with other methods, the EV must be returned
to the EVSE at the specified UBE level, and DCFC shall commence without delay.

End-of-Discharge Criteria

The discharge phase must be terminated when the remaining UBE capacity reaches 10%±1% of
the 100% state of UBE capacity. This condition can be achieved as described below. The total
(100%) UBE shall be determined by measuring the total discharged energy (DBE) during the SAE
J1634 procedures. Care must be taken to perform this measurement at location P3, as illustrated in
Figure 1.

During the discharge phase outlined in section 3.1.2, a total of 90% of the full UBE capacity shall
be depleted, resulting in a final condition corresponding to 10%±1% of the total UBE. These steps
are illustrated graphically in Figure 8.

 13

 Figure 12: Steps to Achieve Starting Criteria for Charging Test Procedure

It should be noted that the discharge end condition/ DCFC test start condition is defined based on
the percentage of UBE rather than the SOC. The SOC displayed by the vehicle is typically non-
linear with respect to the actual energy remaining in the battery, and the available battery energy
(in kWh) is not directly proportional to SOC. Furthermore, SOC cannot be measured directly using
instrumentation; it requires detailed battery models and complex estimation algorithms. Since SOC
is only available via proprietary CAN bus or EV-side communication channel, relying on OEM-
supplied data is undesirable.

To maintain simplicity and consistency in the test procedures, this document uses UBE to define
the start criteria for the charging test. UBE provides a more uniform and quantifiable metric that
can be directly correlated with the RESS’s total capacity and the remaining energy content,
expressed as a percentage of the UBE.

4.2 Transition from Discharge (J1634) Test Setup to Charging Location

 Figure 13. Illustration of Test Execution Steps: Discharging to Achieve DCFC Start Condition

The vehicle must be brought to the DCFC EVSE at the target UBE value, and charging shall be
initiated immediately. This requirement ensures both a realistic representation of user fast charging

 14

behavior post-discharge and preservation of the thermal and electrochemical conditions
established during EV battery pre-conditioning. The following approaches are proposed:

 1. EVSE Located At/Near Chassis Dynamometer

 The most effective approach is the installation of a DCFC EVSE directly at or near the
 chassis dynamometer test station. This configuration enables immediate initiation of
 charging after the discharge session, eliminating the need for vehicle relocation. It ensures
 continuity of battery SOC and temperature, thereby preserving test integrity.

 2. Passive Vehicle Transport Via Tow or Trailer (per SAE J2953_4 and ISO 12906)

 If integrated EVSE is not available, the vehicle may be passively transported from the
 chassis dynamometer to the EVSE using a tow vehicle or trailer (e.g., crabwalk towing).
 Transport shall occur within 30 minutes of discharge completion to maintain the battery
 in an optimal thermal state for charging. This constraint reflects real-world conditions
 where charging typically follows shortly after vehicle operation and minimizes SOC or
 thermal drift.

The above relocation approaches apply to discharging the EV on a dynamometer tech bench. In
case of on-road discharge, the following approach must be followed.

 3. Controlled Self-Driving to EVSE Location

 The vehicle may be driven under its own power from the test route or track to the EVSE.
 To ensure the validity of the subsequent charging test, the UBE change during this transit
 shall not exceed 0.5%. This approach preserves alignment with typical user behavior while
 maintaining battery conditions representative of immediate post-driving charging events.

Each of these methods is intended to ensure consistency, data fidelity, and reproducibility by
preserving both SOC and thermal conditions during the transition from discharge to charging tests.

4.3 DCFC Charging Procedures

 Figure 14. Illustration of Test Execution Steps: DCFC Test Procedures

To evaluate the performance of DCFC, several key parameters shall be measured during a charging
event conducted under controlled conditions. The primary objectives of this charging test
procedure are to determine the following:
 • Charging durations (for UBE = 10% to UBE = 100%)
 • Charging voltages (V) and currents (A)
 • Maximum and Average Charging Power (kW)
 • Charging Efficiency (%)
 • Recharged Electrical Energy (kWh) and Recharged Driving Range (miles)

The charging test shall commence only after completion of the SAE J1634 discharge procedure,
the subsequent AC Type-2 or similar charging process, and a prescribed discharge to the target
UBE level, including, if required, relocation of the vehicle to the designated DCFC EVSE.

Vehicle Conditions During Charging:

To ensure consistency and accuracy, the following conditions must be strictly maintained
throughout the charging phase:
 • EV must be in park mode
 • Vehicle doors closed and EV unoccupied
 • Seat belts unbuckled
 • Key removed, but vehicle in charging-ready state
 • All EV Auxiliary loads (e.g., chillers, pumps, lights) controlled by the vehicle
 • Operator-switchable onboard equipment must be turned off

 16

 • No concurrent power transfer via other interfaces (e.g., solar panels, inductive charging,
 multiple inlets)
 • No limit on maximum EV battery power, current or SOC level

Environmental Requirements:

The EV must be charged in an environment that meets the following specifications:
 • Ambient temperature within ±3 °C of the defined test temperature
 • Minimum 1 meter clearance around the vehicle to facilitate airflow to radiators and
 condensers

 Figure 15. Clearance Space Around Vehicle (Source: ISO 12906)

EVSE Requirements:

 • The EVSE electrical ratings must be sufficient to meet the EV manufacturer’s specified
 charging power, voltage, current levels. The EVSE must be compatible with the HLC
 protocol supported by the EV.

 • Smart energy management and power curtailment features must be disabled to prevent
 artificial limitations on the charging power or current by the EVSE.

The charging session shall commence at the UBE values specified in the previous section and shall
proceed without interruption until the battery reaches 100% of its capacity or until charging is
automatically terminated by the EV BMS. No external intervention or environmental
modifications are permitted during the charging test procedure.

Specific measurement locations and instrumentation requirements are detailed in Section 2.4. All
relevant parameters described in Section 2 shall be recorded continuously for both the EV and the
EVSE throughout the entire test procedure. Additionally, the measurement time shall be extended
by at least 30 seconds beyond the actual charging duration. This extended window supports robust
post-processing and consistency in dataset standardization.

Following the charge session, the recorded data shall be used to calculate the key performance
metrics. The methodologies for the calculation of these metrics are not in the scope of this draft.

5 Test Execution Summary
The subsequent EV discharge and DCFC procedures described in this document are summarized
below.

 Figure 16. Illustration of Test Execution Steps Along with UBE During Each Step

 - To simulate real-world conditions for testing more accurately, an EV temperature soaking
 phase may be conducted before the discharging process. This phase ensures the battery is
 thermally conditioned to the target ambient temperature representative of a specific
 weather scenario.

 - The DC fast charging (DCFC) test procedures are preceded by the following steps:

 • Vehicle discharge to 0% UBE: Performed using simulated standardized drive cycles
 on a chassis dynamometer in accordance with SAE J1634 or equivalent discharge
 procedures.
 During this step, the total UBE capacity of the battery is calculated from DBE.

 • AC Level 2 charging or similar: Conducted to bring the battery back to a fully charged
 state.

 • Controlled discharge to establish the desired initial condition for DCFC testing:

 o Depletion of vehicle energy capacity using a prescribed test cycle on a chassis
 dynamometer test bench, or

 o On-road vehicle discharge.

 - Once the required starting condition for the DCFC procedure has been reached and thermal
 preconditioning of the EV battery has been achieved, the vehicle may be relocated, if

 18

 necessary, to the designated charging test site equipped with a DCFC EVSE. This
 relocation should be performed promptly to preserve any preconditioning effects, whether
 arising from the vehicle’s active thermal management features or from the prior discharge
 process. During this transition, care must be taken to maintain the achieved level of UBE,
 ensuring that it doesn’t fall below the established threshold. This precaution is essential to
 preserving the integrity of the test starting conditions.

 - The DCFC test begins immediately upon arrival at the EVSE. Throughout the charging
 session, vehicle and EVSE parameters are continuously recorded. These data form the basis
 for calculating key DCFC performance metrics.

 - To reflect real-world DCFC performance variability under different environmental
 conditions, the test procedures must be conducted at controlled ambient temperatures. The

 testing, which are selected to represent a range of climatic and EV battery conditions
 relevant to real-life EV operation and charging behavior.
