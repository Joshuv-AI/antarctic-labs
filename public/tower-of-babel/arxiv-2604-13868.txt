Fourier Dimension in Inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer Conjecture
Bo Tan, Qing-Long Zhou

Abstract
Let Q ⊆ ℕ Q\subseteq\mathbb{N} be a subset, and let ψ : ℕ → [ 0 , 1 2 ) \psi\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,\tfrac{1}{2}) , θ : ℕ → ℝ \theta\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R} be functions. Let { A q } \{A_{q}\} and { B q } \{B_{q}\} be sequences of integers such that gcd ⁡ ( A q , B q ) = 1 \gcd(A_{q},B_{q})=1 and B q > 0 B_{q}>0 for all q q . Define W Q ∗ ​ ( ψ , θ ) W_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta) to be the set of x ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] x\in[0,1] for which | x − p + θ ⁡ ( q ) q | < ψ ⁡ ( q ) q \left|x-\frac{p+\theta(q)}{q}\right|<\frac{\psi(q)}{q} holds for infinitely many ( p , q ) ∈ ℤ × Q (p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q with gcd ⁡ ( B q ​ p + A q , q ) = 1 \gcd(B_{q}p+A_{q},q)=1 . In this paper, we determine the Fourier dimension of W Q ∗ ​ ( ψ , θ ) W_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta) . Our result not only recovers the classical theorems of Kaufman and Bluhm (concerning the homogeneous case ψ ⁡ ( q ) = q − τ \psi(q)=q^{-\tau} with τ ≥ 1 \tau\geq 1 ) and the one-dimensional version of a result by Cai and Hambrook on the inhomogeneous approximable set, but also provides a complete inhomogeneous generalization. Moreover, it gives an affirmative answer to the coprime formulation of the Chen–Xiong conjecture.



1
Motivation
\colon
Metrical Diophantine approximation

A central theme in Diophantine approximation is to understand how well real numbers can be approximated by rationals. This topic lies at the heart of metric number theory, where one often studies the size—measured in terms of the Lebesgue measure, Hausdorff measure/dimension, Fourier dimension, etc.—of Diophantine sets satisfying prescribed approximation conditions. A fundamental problem in this context is, given an approximating function
\psi\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,1/2)
, to determine the size of the
\psi
-well approximable set for systems of linear forms:

W(n,m;\psi,\bm{\theta}):=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{nm}\;:\;|\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}-\bm{\theta}|<\psi(|\mathbf{q}|)\text{ for i.m. }(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}\right\},

where “infinitely many” is abbreviated as “i.m.”. Here,
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are regarded as row vectors, while
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{nm}
is interpreted as an
n\times m
matrix. Throughout this paper,
|\cdot|
denotes the maximum norm
\colon

|\mathbf{q}|=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|q_{j}|
. To set the stage, we briefly review the relevant metric results concerning
W(n,m;\psi,\bm{\theta})
, which will help motivate the present work.

Lebesgue Measure.

Khintchine–Groshev Theorem

[
21
,
41
]
established that for any function
\psi\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,\frac{1}{2})
that is monotonically decreasing, the Lebesgue measure of the set
W(n,m;\psi,\bm{\theta})
satisfies

\mathcal{L}\big(W(n,m;\psi,\bm{\theta})\big)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\psi(q)^{m}<\infty$},\\
\penalty\ &\\
1&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\psi(q)^{m}=\infty$}.\end{cases}

The non-monotonic case is more involved.
In the homogeneous setting
(\bm{\theta}=\bm{0})
, Beresnevich and Velani
[
4
]
removed the monotonicity condition when
nm\geq 2.
However, monotonicity is necessary in the case
(m,n)=(1,1),

as demonstrated by the counterexample of Duffin and Schaeffer
[
16
]
.
Further, consider the set

{W}^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,0):=\Big\{x\in[0,1]\colon|qx-p|<\psi(q)\text{ for i.m.}(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\gcd(p,q)=1\Big\}.

Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture
asserted that

\mathcal{L}\big(W^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,0)\big)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(q)}{q}\psi(q)<\infty$},\\
\penalty\ &\\
1&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(q)}{q}\psi(q)=\infty$},\end{cases}

where
\phi
is Euler’s totient function.
This conjecture animated a great deal of research until it was finally proved in a breakthrough by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
34
]
.

In the inhomogeneous case
(\bm{\theta}\neq\bm{0})
, Allen and Ramírez
[
1
]
removed the monotonicity condition for cases with
nm\geq 3
. For
(n,m)=(2,1)
, Hauke
[
25
]
showed that the monotonicity condition can be removed for any non-Liouville irrational inhomogeneous parameter
\theta
. Kim
[
33
]
further proved that this result extends to all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
(without the non-Liouville restriction) in the case
(n,m)=(2,1)
, and also holds for
(n,m)=(1,2)
with any rational parameter
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. The other cases for
(n,m)=(1,2)
are still wide open. Similarly to the homogeneous
case for
(n,m)=(1,1)
, counterexamples to the removal of monotonicity were given in
[
11
,
40
]
. Despite rumours of its falsity, the most direct analogue of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture remains formally open (see
[
40
]
): set

{W}^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,\theta):=\Big\{x\in[0,1]\colon|qx-p-\theta|<\psi(q)\text{ for i.m. }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\gcd(p,q)=1\Big\}.

Inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture
states that

\mathcal{L}\big(W^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,\theta)\big)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(q)}{q}\psi(q)<\infty$},\\
\penalty\ &\\
1&\text{if $\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(q)}{q}\psi(q)=\infty$}.\end{cases}

The so-called weak Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture (where the coprimality condition is dropped, so that the set
W
is used in place of
W^{\ast}
) also remains open; see
[
13
, Conjecture 1.22]
. This weak version was resolved for a wide class of non-monotonic functions in
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
. Results under extra divergence conditions were established by Yu
[
46
,
47
]
. For historical details, we refer to Tables 1 and 2 in
[
26

Hausdorff Dimension.

Jarník’s theorem
[
30
]
establishes that, under the monotonicity assumption on
\psi
, the Hausdorff dimension of
W(1,1;\psi,0)
is

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W(1,1;\psi,0)=\min\{t(\psi),1\},

where

t(\psi)=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}q\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{s}<\infty\right\}.

(In this paper, we do not need the definition of Hausdorff dimension; the interested reader may find further details in
[
18
]
.) An alternative proof of this result can be derived from Khintchine’s theorem by applying the mass transference principle due to Beresnevich and Velani
[
3
]
. For a general (not necessarily monotonic) function
\psi
, the Hausdorff dimension of the set
W(1,1;\psi,0)
was thoroughly investigated by Hinokuma and Shiga
[
29
]
. More recently, Yu
[
46
]
showed that

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W(1,1;\psi,0).

A straightforward covering argument yields
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W(1,1;\psi,\theta)\leq\min\{t(\psi),1\}
. Consequently, we obtain the following identities:

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W(1,1;\psi,\theta)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W^{\ast}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=\dim_{\mathrm{H}}W(1,1;\psi,0).

For details and a comprehensive overview of the Hausdorff dimension of
W(n,m;\psi,{\bm{\theta}})
in higher dimensions, see
[
45
,
5
]
and the references therein.

Fourier Dimension.
Before proceeding, we recall some definitions.
As usual, the Fourier transform of a non-atomic probability measure
\nu
is defined by

\hat{\nu}({\bm{t}}):=\int e^{-2\pi i\langle{\bm{t}},\mathbf{x}\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu(\mathbf{x})\qquad({\bm{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\langle{\bm{t}},\mathbf{x}\rangle=t_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+t_{d}x_{d}.

Loosely speaking, Fourier decay reflects the “smoothness" or “randomness" of a measure: it indicates that the measure is spread out in such a way that coherent long-range correlations are dampened. In harmonic analysis and geometric measure theory, this decay is intimately connected to the fractal and dimensional properties of the measure’s support. Indeed, a classical result of Frostman states that for
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\dim_{\rm H}E\geq\dim_{\rm F}E,

(1)

where
\dim_{\rm F}E
is the Fourier dimension of
E
, defined as

\dim_{\rm F}E=\sup\left\{s\in[0,d]:\exists\nu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\text{ such that }|\hat{\nu}({\bm{\xi}})|\ll_{s}(1+|{\bm{\xi}}|)^{-s/2},{\bm{\xi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}.

Here
\mathcal{P}(E)
denotes the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that give full measure to
E
. When we have equality in (
1
), the set
E
is called a Salem set.

A rich variety of random Salem sets is known to exist; we refer the interested reader to
[
36
]
for further details. Non-trivial deterministic fractal Salem or non-Salem sets are, however, hard to construct. This serves as one of the main motivations for the present paper. We survey the known results on Salem and non-Salem sets arising in Diophantine approximation, providing both a historical overview and a discussion of recent progress.

Remark 1.
Let us make the following remarks regarding Tables 1 and 2:

•

As summarized in Table 1, the
\tau
-well approximable set

\left\{x\in[0,1]:|qx-p|\leq q^{-\tau}\text{ for i.m. }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}\right\}

is Salem. However, the proofs by Kaufman and Bluhm of the lower bounds for the Fourier dimension of such sets essentially rely on considering the coprime setting, i.e., restricting to rational approximations
p/q
with
\gcd(p,q)=1
:

\left\{x\in[0,1]:|qx-p|\leq q^{-\tau}\text{ for i.m. }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\gcd(p,q)=1\right\}.

To the best of our knowledge, the Fourier dimension in the coprime setting remains unknown for general approximation functions. Our goal is to establish a complete characterization in the general setting.

•

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be a subset, and define the associated approximable set

W_{Q}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=\left\{x\in[0,1]:|qx-p-\theta|<\psi(q)\text{ for i.m. }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q\right\}.

Specially,
W_{\mathbb{N}}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=W(1,1;\psi,\theta).
Cai and Hambrook
[
8
, Theorem 1.4.1]
2
2

2

Cai and Hambrook studied the mutli-dimensional generalization of
W_{Q}(1,1;\psi,\theta)
proved that under the convergence condition
\sum_{q\in Q}\psi(q)<\infty
,

\dim_{\mathrm{F}}W_{Q}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=\min\{2s(Q,\psi),1\},

where

s(Q,\psi)=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sum_{q\in Q}\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{s}<\infty\right\}.

They further proposed the following conjecture: prove or disprove that

\text{if }\sum_{q\in Q}\psi(q)=\infty,\text{ then }\dim_{{\rm F}}W_{Q}(1,1;\psi,\theta)=1.

The authors
[
42
]
constructed a function
\psi
with

\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\psi(q)=\infty

but

\dim_{\rm F}W(1,1;\psi,0)=0,

providing a negative answer to this conjecture. Chen and Xiong recently established the complete result for the set
W(1,1;\psi,0)
: if

\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(q)\sup_{q\mid n}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}<\infty,

(2)

then

\dim_{\rm F}W(1,1;\psi,0)=\min\{2s(\psi),1\},

where

s(\psi)=\inf\left\{s\geq 0\colon\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{s}<\infty\right\}.

In the inhomogeneous case, they also proved that the dimension formula remains valid when
\theta
is rational, i.e.,
\theta=A/B
with
\gcd(A,B)=1
, provided that condition (
2
) holds.
Furthermore, they proposed the following conjecture:

\text{the dimension formula remains valid for }\theta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}.

While the conjecture remains unresolved, it motivates the investigation of related problems where additional arithmetic constraints are imposed, for instance, the coprime setting.

•

There also exist several natural and important open problems. For example
\colon

(1) The badly approximable set

\textbf{Bad}:=\left\{x\in[0,1]:\inf_{q\in\mathbb{N}}q\|qx\|>0\right\}

has null Lebesgue measure but full Hausdorff dimension, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the distance from a real number to the nearest integer. Currently, it is only known that
Bad
is of positive Fourier dimension
[
31
,
39
]
; whether this set is a Salem set remains an open problem.

(2) The Fourier dimension of
W(n,m;\psi,\bm{\theta})
established by Cai and Hambrook only provides isotropic estimates of Fourier decay for square targets (i.e., when the approximating functions are identical in all directions). What can be said about the Fourier dimension when the target is a rectangle (especially a very elongated one), whose Fourier transform exhibits significant directional decay? Precisely, consider the weighted
{\bf\Psi}
-well approximable set

\left\{{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{nm}\colon|{\bf qx}-{\bf p}-\bm{\theta}|<\psi_{j}(|{\bf q}|)\penalty\ (1\leq j\leq m)\text{ for i.m. }({\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}\right\},

where
\psi_{j}\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,\frac{1}{2})
for
1\leq j\leq m.
He
[
28
]
proved that if

\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}(|\mathbf{q}|)<\infty

and
s(\{\psi_{j}\})<1
, where

s(\{\psi_{j}\}):=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\left(\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\frac{\psi_{j}(|\mathbf{q}|)}{|\mathbf{q}|}\right)^{s}<\infty\right\},

then the set has Fourier dimension
2s(\{\psi_{j}\})
. However, whether this result holds in the general case remains unknown.

Applications.
There exists a profound connection between positive Fourier dimension and the existence of normal numbers in fractal sets, as discussed in the seminal work of Pollington
et al.

[
38
]
. Furthermore, constructing measures with prescribed Fourier decay on Diophantine sets has far-reaching implications. It can facilitate the development of the theory of multiplicative and simultaneous Diophantine approximation on fractals
[
37
,
14
,
43
]
, as well as twisted Diophantine approximation
[
27
,
15
]
. Consequently, Fourier dimension can be regarded as a critical bridge linking harmonic analysis and metric number theory.

Based on the above discussion and motivated by open problems in inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation with coprimality conditions, we study a concrete arithmetic framework that naturally incorporates a condition of Duffin–Schaeffer type.

Let
\{A_{q}\}
and
\{B_{q}\}
be sequences of integers such that
\gcd(A_{q},B_{q})=1
and
B_{q}>0
for all
q
. Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be a subset, let
\psi\colon Q\to[0,\tfrac{1}{2})
be a positive function, and let
\theta\colon Q\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary function.
Define
W_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)
to be the set of
x\in[0,1]
such that

\left|x-\frac{p+\theta(q)}{q}\right|<\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\text{ for i.m. }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q\text{ with }\gcd(B_{q}p+A_{q},q)=1.

Strictly speaking, the set
{W}_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)
depends on the sequences
\{A_{q},B_{q}\}
. Since these sequences are fixed throughout our study, we omit this dependence from the notation. Thanks to the limsup set structure of
W_{Q}^{*}(\psi,\theta)
, it follows from
[
2
, Proposition 1]
and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma that the Lebesgue measure of
W^{*}(\psi,\theta)
vanishes provided that

\sum_{q\in Q}\psi(q)\prod_{{p\mid q,\penalty\ p\nmid B_{q}}}\bigl(1-p^{-1}\bigr)<\infty,

(3)

where the product is over all prime numbers
p
that divide
q
and are coprime to
B_{q}
.
Our main result provides the Fourier dimension of this set.

Theorem 1
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{N}
, and let
\psi\colon Q\to[0,\frac{1}{2})
be a function satisfying the condition (
3
).
Then the Fourier dimension of
W_{Q}^{*}(\psi,\theta)
is

\dim_{{\rm F}}W_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)=\min\{2s(Q,\psi),1\}.

When
Q=\mathbb{N}
, we abbreviate
W_{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)
as
W^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)
. A direct corollary of Theorem
1
obtained by taking
Q=\mathbb{N}
reads as follows.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\psi\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,1/2)
be a function satisfying condition (
3
) with
Q
replaced by
\mathbb{N}
. Then

\dim_{\mathrm{F}}W^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)=\min\{2s(\psi),1\}.

Remark 2.
Although Corollary
2
is a special case of Theorem
1
, they are essentially equivalent. Indeed, assuming Corollary
2
holds, we prove Theorem
1
as follows.

We extend the function
\psi:Q\to[0,1/2)
to
\overline{\psi}:\mathbb{N}\to[0,1/2)
by setting

\overline{\psi}(q)=\begin{cases}\psi(q)&\text{if }q\in Q,\\[4.0pt]
0&\text{if }q\notin Q.\end{cases}

(The value of
\theta
on
\mathbb{N}\setminus Q
may be defined arbitrarily.) One then readily verifies that

W_{Q}^{\ast}(\psi,\theta)=W^{\ast}(\overline{\psi},\theta)\qquad\text{and}\qquad s(Q,\psi)=s(\overline{\psi}).

Remark 3.
Let us make more remarks regarding Theorem
1
\colon

•

In Theorem
1
, the Duffin–Schaeffer type condition
\gcd(A_{q}+pB_{q},q)=1
— where
A_{q}
and
B_{q}
are coprime integers — is a recurring theme in the literature, appearing in early works such as
[
41
]
and more recent contributions like
[
2
,
13
,
33
]
. In fact, the formulation of this condition is, to some extent, inspired by Dirichlet’s theorem: for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a pair
(A_{q},B_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
such that

|B_{q}\theta-A_{q}|<\frac{1}{q},\qquad 1\leq B_{q}\leq q,\qquad\gcd(A_{q},B_{q})=1.

Then
\left|x-({p+\theta})/{q}\right|
can be well approximated by
\left|x-({B_{q}p+A_{q}})/({B_{q}q})\right|
. Therefore, analogous to the homogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture, the restriction

\gcd(B_{q}p+A_{q},B_{q}q)=1,\text{ or equivalently, }\gcd(B_{q}p+A_{q},q)=1

is imposed.

•

The result
[
8
, Theorem 1.4.1]
of Cai and Hambrook does not apply directly to our setting for two main reasons. First, our set
W^{*}(\psi,\theta)
involves an additional coprimality constraint
\gcd(B_{q}p+A_{q},q)=1
and is governed by the different convergence condition (
3
) (if
Q=\mathbb{N}
, the condition (
3
) is weaker than the one required in
[
8
]
). Second, a natural approach to obtaining a lower bound for
\dim_{\mathrm{F}}W^{*}(\psi,\theta)
would be to select a suitable subset
Q\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
W_{Q}(1,1;\psi,\theta)\subseteq W^{*}(\psi,\theta)
. A concrete choice is to take
Q
as the set of all prime numbers. However, a direct comparison of the associated
s
-parameters reveals that
s(\psi)\geq s(Q,\psi)
. Consequently, the lower bound provided by the Cai and Hambrook theorem is strictly weaker than the desired lower bound
2s(\psi)
for
W^{*}(\psi,\theta)
. Hence, establishing the sharp Fourier dimension estimate in our setting calls for a nontrivial refinement of their method.

•

Putting
A_{q}=0
and
B_{q}=1
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we find that Theorem
1
not only recovers the classical results of Kaufman and Bluhm (which deal with the homogeneous case and
\psi(q)=q^{-\tau}
with
\tau\geq 1
) but also establishes a general inhomogeneous version. At the same time, Theorem
1
provides an affirmative answer to the coprime version of the Chen and Xiong conjecture.

•

In the special case where
A_{q}=1
and
B_{q}=q
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
, our result also recovers Theorem 1.4.1 of
[
8
]
in the one-dimensional setting.

•

Our method may conceivably be applicable to the study of Fourier dimension in the Duffin–Schaeffer setting for multiplicative and weighted Diophantine approximation, although we do not assert this with certainty.

To facilitate subsequent estimates, we prove the following auxiliary lemma giving a uniform upper bound for the general Ramanujan sum.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A,B
be coprime integers with
B>0.
Then for
q,k\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
\gcd(Bp+A,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q})\right|\ll\gcd(q,k)\cdot\log q,

where the implied constant in Vinogradov’s notation is independent of
A,B,q,k.

Proof.

Recall the well-known property of the Möbius function
\mu\colon

\sum_{l\mid d}\mu(l)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }d>1,\\
1&\text{if }d=1.\end{cases}

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
\gcd(Bp+A,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{q-1}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q})\cdot\sum_{l\mid\gcd(Bp+A,q)}\mu(l).

If
\gcd(l,B)\neq 1
, then since
\gcd(A,B)=1
, we have
l\nmid Bp+A
for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If, on the other hand,
\gcd(l,B)=1
, we can find an inverse
B^{(l)}\in\mathbb{N}
of
B
modulo
l
, i.e.,
B^{(l)}B\equiv 1\pmod{l}
. Consequently,

Bp+A\equiv 0\pmod{l}\quad\iff\quad p\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
\gcd(Bp+A,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\mid q}\mu(l)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
l\mid Bp+A\end{subarray}}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid q\\
\gcd(l,B)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(l)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
p\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}\end{subarray}}e(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q}).

For the inner sum, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
p\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}\end{subarray}}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q}\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{q/l-1}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p_{0}+lt}{q}\right)=e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)\sum_{t=0}^{q/l-1}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{t}{q/l}\right),

where
p_{0}
is the unique residue modulo
l
satisfying
p_{0}\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}
with
0\leq p_{0}<l
.
The inner geometric sum vanishes unless
(q/l)\mid k
, in which case it equals
q/l
. That is,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
p\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}\end{subarray}}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q}\right)=\begin{cases}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)\cdot\dfrac{q}{l}&\text{if }\dfrac{q}{l}\mid k,\\[6.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Inserting this into the outer sum over
l\mid q
and
\gcd(l,B)=1
yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid q\\
\gcd(l,B)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(l)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
p\equiv-B^{(l)}A\pmod{l}\end{subarray}}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid q,\;\frac{q}{l}\mid k\\
\gcd(l,B)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(l)\,e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p_{0}}{q}\right)\frac{q}{l}.

Taking absolute values, noting that
|\mu(l)|\leq 1
and
|e(x)|=1
, and then removing the restriction condition
\gcd(l,B)=1
on the summation index
l
, we obtain

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq q-1\\
\gcd(Bp+A,q)=1\end{subarray}}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p}{q}\right)\right|\leq\sum_{l\mid q,\;\frac{q}{l}\mid k}\frac{q}{l}.

If
l\mid q
and
(q/l)\mid k
, then
(q/l)\mid\gcd(q,k)
, and hence
q\mid l\gcd(k,q)
. Setting
t=l\gcd(k,q)/q
, we deduce that

\sum_{l\mid q,\;\frac{q}{l}\mid k}\frac{q}{l}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{q}\frac{\gcd(q,k)}{t}\ll\gcd(q,k)\cdot\log q,

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\varepsilon>0
we have

\nu(A_{q})\ll 2\psi(q)\prod_{{p\mid q,\penalty\ p\nmid B_{q}}}\bigl(1-p^{-1}\bigr)+\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{s-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Recall that

\nu(A_{q})=2\psi(q)\prod_{{p\mid q,\penalty\ p\nmid B_{q}}}\bigl(1-p^{-1}\bigr)+\sum_{p\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}_{A_{p,q}}(k)\overline{\widehat{\nu}(k)}.

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}_{A_{p,q}}(k)\overline{\widehat{\nu}(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{I}_{q}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}e\!\left(-k\cdot\frac{p+\theta(q)}{q}\right)\int_{B(0,\frac{\psi(q)}{q})}e(-kx)\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\overline{\widehat{\nu}(k)}

\displaystyle\ll\log q\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gcd(q,k)\cdot\frac{\bigl|\sin\bigl(2\pi k\cdot\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\bigr)\bigr|}{k^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\log q\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)\cdot\frac{\bigl|\sin\bigl(2\pi(tq+j)\cdot\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\bigr)\bigr|}{(tq+j)^{s+1}},

where the inequality follows from Lemma

Case 1:
t=0
.
Using the bound

\log q\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)\cdot\frac{\bigl|\sin\bigl(2\pi j\cdot\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\bigr)\bigr|}{j^{s+1}}\ll\log q\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)\cdot\frac{1}{j^{s}}\cdot\frac{\psi(q)}{q}.

Set

and write

\gcd(k,q/d)=1

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi(q)\log q}{q}\sum_{d\mid q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq q/d\\

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi(q)\log q}{q}\sum_{d\mid q}d^{1-s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq q/d\\

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\psi(q)\log q}{q}\sum_{d\mid q}d^{1-s}\cdot\left(\frac{q}{d}\right)^{1-s}

where

Case 2:

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\log q\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)\cdot\frac{\bigl|\sin\bigl(2\pi(tq+j)\cdot\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\bigr)\bigr|}{(tq+j)^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\log q\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)\cdot\left(\sum_{1\leq t\leq\frac{1}{\psi(q)}-1}\frac{(t+1)\psi(q)}{(tq)^{s+1}}+\sum_{t>\frac{1}{\psi(q)}-1}\frac{1}{(tq)^{s+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{s-\varepsilon},

where the last inequality follows from

that for all

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\gcd(q,j)=O(q^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

Having shown that both cases lead to the desired estimate, we conclude the proof.

∎

Finally, by Lemma

g_{M_{k}}
, where the sequence
(M_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is chosen recursively according to the stability lemma above.

Fix a function
h_{0}\in C_{c}^{K}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\int h_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
,
\operatorname{supp}(h_{0})=[0,1]
, and
h_{0}(x)>0
for
x\in[0,1]
.
Applying Lemma
2.7
iteratively, we choose

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=M_{1}(h_{0},2^{-1}),

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=M_{2}(h_{0}g_{M_{1}},2^{-2}),

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle=M_{k}(h_{0}g_{M_{1}}\cdots g_{M_{k-1}},2^{-k})\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

with
\{M_{k}:k\geq 1\}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
2M_{k}<M_{k+1}
for
k\geq 1
. We then define the measures

\mathrm{d}\nu_{0}=h_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}=h_{0}\,g_{M_{1}}\cdots g_{M_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

which satisfy

\bigl|\widehat{\nu}_{k}(\xi)-\widehat{\nu}_{k-1}(\xi)\bigr|\ll 2^{-k}\,|\xi|^{-(\eta-\varepsilon)}.

This estimate implies that the sequence
\{\widehat{\nu}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in the supremum norm.
Lévy’s continuity theorem
[
17
, Theorem 3.3.17]
then guarantees that
\{\widehat{\nu}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
converges weakly to a finite non-zero Borel measure
\nu
satisfying

\widehat{\nu}(\xi)=O\bigl(|\xi|^{-(\eta-\varepsilon)}\bigr).

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.5
, we have

\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\subseteq\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{supp}(g_{M_{k}})\subseteq W^{\ast}(\psi,\theta).

Normalizing
\nu
gives a probability measure. Then, by the definition of the Fourier dimension and the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, the theorem follows.
