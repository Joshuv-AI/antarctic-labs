Matrix Product States for Modulated Symmetries: SPT, LSM, and Beyond
Amogh Anakru, Sarvesh Srinivasan, Linhao Li, Zhen Bi

Abstract
Matrix product states (MPS) provide a powerful framework for characterizing one-dimensional symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases of matter and for formulating Lieb–Schultz–Mattis (LSM)-type constraints. Here we generalize the MPS formalism to translationally invariant systems with general modulated symmetries. We show that the standard symmetry “push-through” condition for conventional global symmetry must be revised to account for symmetry modulation, and we derive the appropriate generalized condition. Using this generalized push-through structure, we classify one-dimensional SPT phases with modulated symmetries and formulate LSM-type constraints within the same MPS-based framework.

Generalized symmetry push-through condition –
The ground state of a one-dimensional gapped, translation-invariant Hamiltonian with periodic boundary conditions can, in general, be represented by an injective MPS,

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{s_{1}\ldots s_{L}}\Tr(A^{s_1}\dots A^{s_L})\ket{s_1\ldots s_L},

(1)

where
A^{s}
are finite-dimensional square matrices, taken to be identical on every site.
Mathematically, injectivity demands that the transfer matrix
\mathbb{E}=\sum_{s}A^{s}\otimes(A^{s})^{*}
has a unique dominant eigenvalue separated by a spectral gap. This condition physically ensures that all correlation functions decay exponentially and the state is the non-degenerate ground state of a gapped local Hamiltonian.

To classify 1D SPT phases protected by global symmetries, it is sufficient to analyze how the symmetry acts on a single tensor. For injective MPS, the unitary transformation on the physical degrees of freedom can be “pushed through” the tensor and represented as unitaries acting on the virtual bonds. For unmodulated symmetries, it is sufficient to assume symmetry actions on two virtual legs are conjugate to each other
35
;
2

\omega\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
. This cocycle is invariant under symmetric finite-depth local unitary circuits and thus classifies 1D SPT phases

The MPS framework can also yields LSM-type constraints: if the physical degrees of freedom carry projective representations that are incompatible with symmetry push-through conditions for an injective MPS, then a symmetric short-range-entangled gapped phase is forbidden.

We now discuss the symmetry representations carried by the physical and virtual degrees of freedom. For SPT phases, we assume that the physical Hilbert space transforms linearly on each site, while the virtual bonds may carry projective representations characterized by a 2-cocycle
\omega_{j}
defined through
v_{j}(g)v_{j}(h)=\omega_{j}(g,h)\,v_{j}(gh)
. Eq. (
3
) then implies that, up to coboundaries, the cocycle is site-independent and satisfies

\omega(g,h)=\omega(\mathcal{T}(g),\mathcal{T}(h)).

(4)

We note that, by carefully tracking the phases in Eqs. (

For LSM-type constraints, we allow the physical degrees of freedom to transform projectively, with cocycle
\nu_{j}\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
defined by
U_{g,j}U_{h,j}=\nu_{j}(g,h)\,U_{gh,j}
. Translation invariance requires
\nu_{j}(g,h)=\nu_{j-1}(\mathcal{T}(g),\mathcal{T}(h))
. If a translation invariant injective MPS exists with these symmetry representations, then the physical and virtual projective data must satisfy

\begin{split}\omega_{j}(g,h)&=\nu_{j}(g,h)\,\omega_{j-1}(g,h),\\
\omega_{j}(g,h)&=\omega_{j-1}(\mathcal{T}(g),\mathcal{T}(h)),\end{split}

(5)

up to coboundaries. Conversely, if these conditions fail, then no injective MPS is compatible with the symmetry representations, yielding an LSM-type constraint.

These equations serve as the engine for the remainder of this work. It allows us to classify SPTs, and identify LSM constraints with modulated symmetries.

SPT classification with modulated symmetries.–
We now present two examples – (i) an exponential symmetry and (ii) two exponential symmetries with different exponents – to illustrate how to classify SPTs using Eq. (
4

We first consider an exponential symmetry

;

\mathcal{U}_{g}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}U_{g}^{\,b^{j-1}}

Assuming

has order
N

\varphi(N)

Applying the generalized push-through condition, we obtain

U_{g}\cdot A\doteq\,v_{g}^{\dagger}\,A\,v_{g}^{\,b}.

(6)

The SPT classification problem is to determining which projective representations can be carried by the virtual bond unitary
v_{g}
. Since
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{U}_{g}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{U}_{g}^{b}
, or equivalently
\mathcal{T}(g)=g^{b}

4

\omega(g,h)=\omega(g^{b},h^{b})=\omega(g,h)^{b^{2}},

(7)

Take

LSM theorems with modulated symmetries.–

To derive LSM-type theorems within the MPS framework, we ask whether a given assignment of on-site symmetry representations is compatible with the injective MPS conditions in Eq. (
5
). We illustrate this procedure with two examples: (i) two exponential symmetries and (ii) a non-Abelian symmetry group with an exponential structure.

In the first example, we consider a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
qudit chain. We introduce two exponential symmetries,

\mathcal{U}_{x}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}X_{j}^{a^{j-1}},\qquad\mathcal{U}_{z}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}Z_{j}^{\,nb^{j-1}},

(11)

where
a
and
b
are coprime to
N
. The corresponding local symmetry actions at site
j
furnish a projective representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, characterized by the coboundary-invariant phase

\nu_{j}(z,x)\nu_{j}(x,z)^{-1}=\exp\!\left[\frac{2\pi in(ab)^{j-1}}{N}\right]
,
since the on-site symmetry operators commute only up to this phase. Such site-dependent projective representation pattern is required to be consistent with the exponential symmetry and translation invariance.

Suppose there exists an injective MPS preserving these symmetries. The generalized push-through condition then implies

\begin{split}X^{a^{j-1}}\cdot A&\doteq(v_{x}^{\dagger})^{a^{j-1}}Av_{x}^{a^{j}},\\
Z^{nb^{j-1}}\cdot A&\doteq(v_{z}^{\dagger})^{b^{j-1}}Av_{z}^{b^{j}},\end{split}

(12)

where
v_{x}
and
v_{z}
act on the virtual indices. The corresponding virtual projective class is characterized by the coboundary-invariant phase
\omega_{j}(z,x)\omega_{j}(x,z)^{-1}=v_{z}^{b^{j}}v_{x}^{a^{j}}v_{z}^{-b^{j}}v_{x}^{-a^{j}}
. If we assume the starting point is
\omega_{0}(z,x)\omega_{0}(x,z)^{-1}=v_{z}v_{x}v_{z}^{-1}v_{x}^{-1}=e^{2\pi ik/N}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, one obtains for site
j
:
\omega_{j}(z,x)\omega_{j}(x,z)^{-1}=e^{2\pi ik(ab)^{j}/N}
. Eq. (
5
) requires
\omega_{j}(z,x)\omega_{j}(x,z)^{-1}=\omega_{j-1}(z,x)\omega_{j-1}(x,z)^{-1}\nu_{j}(z,x)\nu_{j}(x,z)^{-1}
, which gives
k[(ab)^{j}-(ab)^{j-1}]=n(ab)^{j-1}\pmod{N}
. Since
a
and
b
are coprime to
N
, this reduces to

k(ab-1)=n\pmod{N}.

(13)

This is the necessary condition for the existence of a symmetric injective MPS, in agreement with the group-cohomology result of Ref.
33
. Let
c=\gcd(ab-1,N)
. Then solutions exist iff
c\mid n
, in which case

k=\frac{n}{c}\Bigl(\frac{ab-1}{c}\Bigr)^{-1}_{N/c}+\tau\,\frac{N}{c},\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{c},

(14)

where
(\cdots)^{-1}_{N/c}
denotes the multiplicative inverse modulo
N/c
. For
n=0
, this reduces to the SPT classification discussed above.

If
c\nmid n
, no solution exists, and an LSM constraint follows. Even when
c\mid n
, an obstruction to a trivial phase may remain: if every allowed solution has

, the system is forced into a nontrivial SPT phase, yielding an SPT-LSM theorem

19

As an explicit example exhibiting an LSM constraint, consider arbitrary
N
with
a=b=-1
and
n=1

H=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\bigl(\cos\theta\,X_{i-1}X_{i}+\sin\theta\,Z_{i}Z_{i+1}\bigr)+\mathrm{h.c.},

(15)

which preserves the exponential symmetries
\mathcal{U}_{x}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}X_{j}^{(-1)^{j}}
and
\mathcal{U}_{z}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}Z_{j}^{(-1)^{j}}

1

The map transforms the exponential symmetries into two unmodulated symmetries, which together with translation give rise to a conventional LSM theorem, excluding a unique gapped ground state for the quantum torus chain. Thus, charge conjugation on odd sites directly relates the LSM constraint for exponential symmetries to the conventional LSM constraint for unmodulated symmetries.

In the second class, we consider a non-Abelian symmetry group for which only part of the symmetry is modulated. Specifically, we study a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}

\begin{split}X_{j}^{a^{j-1}}\cdot A&\doteq(v_{x}^{\dagger})^{a^{j-1}}Av_{x}^{a^{j}},\\

The smallest nontrivial case is

Conclusion and discussion –

In this work, we developed an MPS framework for one-dimensional systems with modulated symmetries. Our main result is a generalized symmetry push-through condition: for an injective translationally invariant MPS, a modulated symmetry acting on the physical leg is absorbed into site-dependent unitaries on the virtual bonds. This extends the standard MPS description of global symmetries to the modulated setting and can be applied to SPT classification and LSM-type constraints. We illustrated the framework with exponential symmetries, mixed charge-exponential symmetries, and a non-Abelian example with partially modulated dihedral symmetry, recovering known SPT classifications, constructing explicit symmetric MPS representatives, and deriving lattice models with the predicted LSM obstructions.

Several important directions remain open. On the conceptual side, it would be valuable to extend the present framework beyond injective MPS to non-injective tensors, which can describe symmetry-breaking phases and other interesting long-range-entangled states with modulated symmetries. It is also natural to ask how the virtual bond data identified here manifest at boundaries of a open chain, and whether modulated SPT phases show any interesting feature on an open chain with varying system sizes. On the symmetry side, extending the formalism to continuous
18
;
17
and more general non-Abelian modulated symmetries would substantially broaden its scope. Review of Matrix Product State Formalism

Here, we briefly review key aspects of matrix product states (MPS) that are essential for understanding the present work. We point the reader to Refs.

We focus on translationally invariant matrix product states generated by the three-legged tensor
A^{s}
depicted in Eq. (

A^{s}
is then a matrix in bond space (indicated by horizontal legs). A state

\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/MPS_Single.png}}}\ \implies\ \vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/MPS_periodic.png}}}

(20)

which represents the state

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{L}}\Tr(A^{s_1}\ldots A^{s_L})\ket{s_1\ldots s_L}.

(21)

We also define a

associated with the MPS tensor:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}=\ \vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/transfer_matrix.png}}},

(22)

where we work with a convention where the MPS tensor

with physical leg pointing upward is understood to be complex-conjugated. Multiple different tensors

can correspond to the same physical periodic state. One usually works with the left-canonical form defined by the following conditions:

\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/left_dominant.png}}}\ \,\,\,\,,\ \vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/right_dominant.png}}}

with these left and right eigenvectors of the transfer matrix being dominant eigenvectors. Any translationally invariant MPS can be transformed into this form without changing the physical state. The matrix

span the set of all matrices in bond space (these become matrices after fixing the indices

A final property is that of

. An injective translationally-invariant MPS admits a Hamiltonian whose unique gapped ground state is the MPS. The Hamiltonian is constructed as follows:

\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathbb{I}-\mathcal{P}_{j,\ldots,j+\ell}

\ell\geq\ell_{0}

Appendix B
Generalized push-through theorem

We consider a left-canonical, injective MPS tensor
A^{s}
which defines a translationally invariant periodic wavefunction
\ket{\psi}
on
L

\mathcal{U}_{g}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}U_{g,j}
. To obtain a well-defined thermodynamic limit, we further assume that for every positive integer
k

kL
(formed by concatenating
k

L
-site unit cells) is also an eigenstate of the
k
-fold periodic extension of the symmetry
\bigotimes^{k}\mathcal{U}_{g}
. This ensures that the modulated symmetry structure extends consistently to arbitrary large system size.

In this setting, we now prove the key result: on each site we have the symmetry push-through condition

\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/grand_pushthrough_image.png}}}

(26)

with
v_{j}
unitary (here and in what follows, we suppress the group label whenever no confusion can arise). Note that the convention of which bond is conjugated has changed from the main text. We note that if this condition is correct, it is trivial to see that the MPS is symmetric under the modulated symmetry.

B1
Proof strategy

For unmodulated symmetries, the conventional push-through rule is
U\cdot A=e^{i\phi}vAv^{\dagger}
. This is proven
[
35
,
2
]
by defining a structure known as a
U
-transfer matrix

, given by the tensor contraction

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U}=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{./images/u-transfer-matrix.png}}}.

(27)

The total symmetry operator is
\mathcal{U}=U^{\bigotimes L}
. One can show that the expectation
\absolutevalue{\bra{\psi}\mathcal{U}\ket{\psi}}=1
iff the
U
-transfer matrix has unit modulus eigenvalue. The consequence of this is then the push-through condition. When the on-site unitary is a dipole
[
20
]
or multipole symmetry
[

]

-transfer matrix which are forced to have a dominant eigenvalue on the unit circle, because the expectation

.

For general modulated symmetry, denoting the now site-dependent

-transfer matrix as
\mathbb{E}_{U_{(j)}}
, it is not clear at all that
\absolutevalue{\bra{\psi}\mathcal{U}\ket{\psi}}
is expressible as

. Thus, we need to demand a different condition. We note that one proof strategy for general modulated symmetries involves invoking a Fundamental Theorem for injective MPS
[

]
, which was used in Ref.
[

]
to prove the result. We will present a different proof that directly generalizes the strategies of
[

,
20
,
44
]
, and also allows for slight generalizations of the push-through result as stated in
[
53
]
.

\displaystyle=w_{n/2-1}\left\lvert\rho\right)\left(\mathbbm{1}\right\lvert v_{n+1}^{\dagger}+O(Ce^{-n/2\xi})+O(Dne^{-n/2\xi})

Finally, we can prove the claim that Eq. (

C2

In Eqs. (

C6
Exponential symmetry with charge symmetry: Faithful and Unfaithful Unitary Representations

In the main text, the SPT and SPT-LSM classification results we discuss are consequences of the purely algebraic properties of the modulated symmetry – specifically, the structure of the semidirect product

, which implies that the representation map from the modulated symmetry group to physical unitaries is not one-to-one. This unfaithfulness is present even when the on-site unitaries themselves are a faithful representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. The subgroup of the modulated group
(\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{L}
that is in fact represented faithfully can be written as
(\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{k})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{L}
with the automorphism
\mathcal{T}(g_{1},g_{2})=(g_{1}g_{2}^{b-1},g_{2}^{b})
. Using Eq.
4
, we find the constraint
\omega(g_{1},g_{2})^{b-1}=1
. Since
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{k},U(1))=\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{gcd}(N,k)}=\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, this constraint can only be satisfied for a nontrivial cocycle when
e^{2\pi ip(b-1)/k}=1
for some
p\neq 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,k
, i.e. if
\mathrm{gcd}(k,b-1)>1
. Note that for generic
b,N
, one can show that the symmetry in Eq.
76
realizes an on-site
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/\mathrm{gcd}(N,(b-1))}
symmetry.

Appendix D
LSM and SPT-LSM theorem

To discuss LSM-type obstructions, we suppose that the Hilbert space of each site carries a projective representation labeled by a cocycle
\nu(g,h)
.
Due to translation action,
\mathcal{T}U_{g,j}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}=U_{\mathcal{T}(g),j-1}
, we can obtain

\nu_{j}({g},{h})=\nu_{j-1}(\mathcal{T}({g}),\mathcal{T}({h}))

(81)

up to coboundaries.

With
[\nu]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
nontrivial, we will need to revisit the earlier results. It is still true that the bond unitaries form projective representations of
G
, but the cocycles
\omega_{j}(g,h)
are no longer translation invariant. A straightforward generalization of earlier arguments yields

{\omega_{j}(g,h)\omega_{j-1}^{-1}(g,h)}=\nu_{j}(g,h).

(82)

For translational invariance, we also need

\begin{split}v_{j}(g)&\doteq v_{j-1}(\mathcal{T}(g))\\
\implies\omega_{j}(g,h)&=\omega_{j-1}(\mathcal{T}(g),\mathcal{T}(h)).\end{split}

(83)

These two equations are up to coboundaries. These constraints on bond cocycles in terms of the on-site cocycles agree with the results from Ref.
[
33
]
. Using these constraints, one may systematically prove LSM theorems and ’SPT-LSM’ theorems.

D1
Abelian exponential symmetries

As an application, let us consider the general discrete Abelian group
\prod_{I}\mathbb{Z}_{N_{I}}
, where the generator
g_{I}
of each
\mathbb{Z}_{N_{I}}
is implemented by

\mathcal{U}_{I}=\bigotimes^{L}_{j=1}U^{a^{j-1}_{I}}_{I}

(84)

and
a_{I}
is coprime to
N_{I}
. On each site, we further suppose the symmetry operators satisfies a possible projective representation:

U_{I}U_{J}=\exp(\frac{2\pi i n_{IJ}}{N_{IJ}})U_{J}U_{I}

(85)

with
I<J
,
N_{IJ}=\text{gcd}(N_{I},N_{J})
and
n_{IJ}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N_{IJ}}
.

This gives rise to

\nu_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})\nu^{-1}_{j}(g_{J},g_{I})=\exp(\frac{2\pi i n_{IJ} (a_I a_J)^{j-1}}{N_{IJ}}).

(86)

Now suppose an injective MPS preserves this symmetry. The push-through condition then implies

U^{a^{j-1}_{I}}_{I}\cdot A\doteq(v^{\dagger}_{I})^{a^{j-1}_{I}}Av^{a^{j}_{I}}_{I}

(87)

where
v_{I}
acts on the virtual indices.

The projective representation on the virtual bond is captured by the phase
\omega_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})\omega^{-1}_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})=v^{a^{j}_{I}}_{I}v^{a^{j}_{J}}_{J}v^{-a^{j}_{I}}_{I}v^{-a^{j}_{J}}_{J}
with
I<J
, which is invariant under coboundary transformations. Writing
\omega_{0}(g_{I},g_{J})\omega^{-1}_{0}(g_{I},g_{J})=v_{I}v_{J}v^{-1}_{I}v^{-1}_{J}=\exp(2\pi i k_{IJ}/N_{IJ})
where
k_{IJ}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N_{IJ}}
, this yields
\omega_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})\omega^{-1}_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})=\exp( 2\pi i k_{IJ} (a_I a_J)^j/ N_{IJ})
.

Then according to Eq. (
82
), we have

\begin{split}\omega_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})\omega^{-1}_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})=\omega_{j-1}(g_{I},g_{J})\omega^{-1}_{j-1}(g_{I},g_{J})\nu_{j}(g_{I},g_{J})\nu^{-1}_{j}(g_{J},g_{I}),\end{split}

(88)

which gives

k_{IJ}[(a_{I}a_{J})^{j}-(a_{I}a_{J})^{j-1}]=n_{IJ}(a_{I}a_{J})^{j-1}\penalty\ (\text{mod}\penalty\ N_{IJ}).

(89)

As both
a_{I}
and
a_{J}
are coprime to
N_{IJ}
the above equation reduces to

k_{IJ}(a_{I}a_{J}-1)=n_{IJ}\penalty\ (\text{mod}\penalty\ N_{IJ}).

(90)

The solutions are given by

k_{IJ}=\begin{cases}\text{does not exist}&\text{if }c_{IJ}\text{ does not divides }n_{IJ}\\
\frac{n_{IJ}}{c_{IJ}}(\frac{a_{I}a_{J}-1}{c_{IJ}})^{-1}_{N_{IJ}/c_{IJ}}+\tau_{IJ}N_{IJ}/c_{IJ}&\text{if }c_{IJ}\text{ divides }n_{IJ},\end{cases}

(91)

where
c_{IJ}=\text{gcd}(a_{I}a_{J}-1,N_{IJ})
.
Here
(\cdots)^{-1}_{N_{IJ}/c_{IJ}}
denotes the multiplicative inverse modulo
N_{IJ}/c_{IJ}
and
\tau_{IJ}\in\mathbb{Z}_{c_{IJ}}
. Therefore, there is an LSM constraint for this class of exponential symmetry when
c_{IJ}
does not divides
n_{IJ}
.

Moreover, even when
c_{IJ}\mid n_{IJ}
, an obstruction to a trivial SPT phase may persist. If all allowed solutions
k_{IJ}
are nonzero modulo
N_{IJ}
, the system necessarily realizes a nontrivial SPT phase. This leads to an SPT-LSM theorem—an obstruction to a trivial SPT.

D2
Dipole symmetries

Another application is the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
dipole symmetry generated by

\mathcal{U}_{q}=\bigotimes^{L}_{j=1}U_{q,j},\penalty\ \mathcal{U}_{d}=\bigotimes^{L}_{j=1}U_{d,j},

(92)

which satisfies:

\mathcal{T}U_{d,j}\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}=U_{d,j-1}U_{q,j-1}.

(93)

We further assume the possible projective representation on each site:

U_{q,j}U_{d,j}=\exp(\frac{2\pi n i }{N})U_{d,j}U_{q,j}.

(94)

Now suppose an injective MPS preserves this symmetry. The push-through condition then implies

U_{q}\cdot A\doteq v^{\dagger}_{q}Av_{q},\quad U_{d}\cdot A\doteq v^{\dagger}_{d,j-1}Av_{d,j}.

(95)

Then the projective representation on the virtual bond is classified by the phase

\omega_{j}(q,d)\omega^{-1}_{j}(d,q)=v_{q}v_{d,j}v^{-1}_{q}v^{-1}_{d,j}

(96)

which is invariant under coboundary transformations.
Furthermore due to Eq. (
93
), we have

v_{d,j-1}v_{q}\doteq v_{d,j}.

(97)

This yields

\omega_{j}(q,d)\omega^{-1}_{j}(d,q)=\omega_{j-1}(q,d)\omega^{-1}_{j-1}(d,q),

(98)

which implies that the projective representation on the virtual bond is site-independent.
When
n\neq 0
, this equation is incompatible with the projective representation on each site in Eq. (
94
), which results in the LSM constraint.

A concrete example for the dipole LSM is a
N
-dimensional qudit chain with symmetry operators

\mathcal{U}_{q}=\bigotimes^{L}_{j=1}X_{j},\penalty\ \mathcal{U}_{d}=\bigotimes^{L}_{j=1}Z^{-n}_{j}X^{j}_{j}.

(99)

Here the
Z
and
X
are the on-site clock and shift operators. Under these symmetry actions, the qudit chain cannot be symmetric, gapped, and non-degenerate.
