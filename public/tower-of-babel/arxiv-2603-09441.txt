2.3.
Tate–Drinfeld modules

Here we recall the theory of Tate–Drinfeld modules, following the exposition of
[
Böc
, §2.2]
and
[
Hat2
, §4]
.

Let
x
be an indeterminate and let
A((x))
be the Laurent power series ring over
A
.
Let

\Lambda=\left\{\Phi_{a}^{C}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\ \middle|\ a\in A\right\}\subseteq A((x)).

Note that for any nonzero element
a\in A
, we have

\Phi^{C}_{a}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\in\frac{1}{x^{q^{\deg(a)}}}A[[x]]^{\times}.

Define

e_{\Lambda}(X)=X\prod_{\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\frac{X}{\alpha}\right)\in X+xX^{2}A[[x]][[X]].

Then
e_{\Lambda}(X)
is entire with respect to the
x
-adic topology on
A[[x]]
. In particular, for any element
\beta
of a finite extension

L/K((x))
, plugging in
X=\beta
defines an element
e_{\Lambda}(\beta)\in L
. Moreover, since
\Lambda
is stable under the action of

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
defined by
x\mapsto c^{-1}x
, each coefficient of
e_{\Lambda}(X)
lies in
A[[x^{q-1}]]
.

We denote by
e_{\Lambda}^{-1}(X)
the element of
A[[x^{q-1}]][[X]]
satisfying
e_{\Lambda}(e_{\Lambda}^{-1}(X))=e_{\Lambda}^{-1}(e_{\Lambda}(X))=X
.
For any
a\in A
, let

\Phi_{a}^{\Lambda}(X):=e_{\Lambda}(\Phi^{C}_{a}(e_{\Lambda}^{-1}(X)))\in A[[x^{q-1}]][[X]].

Then it is an element of
A[[x^{q-1}]][X]
having the following description. For
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
and an algebraic closure
K((x))^{\mathrm{alg}}
of the Laurent power series
field
K((x))
,
consider the subgroup

(\Phi_{a}^{C})^{-1}(\Lambda)=\{y\in K((x))^{\mathrm{alg}}\mid\Phi^{C}_{a}(y)\in\Lambda\}.

Let
\Sigma_{a}\subseteq(\Phi_{a}^{C})^{-1}(\Lambda)
be a complete set of representatives of

((\Phi^{C}_{a})^{-1}(\Lambda)/\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}.

Since
A
is a regular domain, the proof of
[
Böc
, Proposition 2.9]
is valid in this case and we have

\Phi_{a}^{\Lambda}(X)=aX\prod_{\beta\in\Sigma_{a}}\left(1-\frac{X}{e_{\Lambda}(\beta)}\right).

Write

\Phi^{\Lambda}_{t}=\theta+a_{1}\tau+a_{2}\tau^{2},\quad a_{i}\in A[[x^{q-1}]].

By
[
Hat2
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have

(2.1)

a_{1}\in 1+x^{q-1}A[[x^{q-1}]],\quad a_{2}\in x^{q-1}A[[x^{q-1}]]^{\times}.

Definition 2.7
.

The Tate–Drinfeld module
\mathrm{TD}(\Lambda)
is the Drinfeld module of rank two over
A((x))
with trivial underlying
invertible sheaf defined by

By (

), it is obtained by

over

with trivial underlying invertible sheaf

.

For any

-algebra

, let

be the base extensions along the natural maps
A((x))\to B((x))
and
A((x^{q-1}))\to B((x^{q-1}))
, respectively.

Remark 2.8
.

In
[
Hat2
]

3.1.
Biderivations and the de Rham module

In this subsection, we recall the theory of biderivations due to Gekeler
[
Gek2
, §3]
, following
[
PR
, §2]
.

Let
S
be a scheme over
A
. Let
E
be a Drinfeld module of rank two over
S
. Let
G=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
or
C
.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

An
(E,G)
-biderivation over
S
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear homomorphism

\delta:A\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{q},S}(E,G),\quad a\mapsto\delta_{a}

satisfying the following conditions.

•

\delta_{\lambda}=0
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

•

\delta_{ab}=\Phi_{a}^{G}\circ\delta_{b}+\delta_{a}\circ\Phi_{b}^{E}
for any
a,b\in A
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of
(E,G)
-biderivations over
S
is denoted by
\mathrm{Der}(E,G)
.

(2)

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of
(E,G)
-biderivations
\delta
such that the map
\delta_{t}^{*}:\omega_{G}\to\omega_{E}

is zero is denoted by
\mathrm{Der}_{0}(E,G)
.

(3)

An
(E,G)
-biderivation
\delta
is said to be inner if there exists an element
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{q},S}(E,G)
satisfying

\delta=\delta_{f}
, where
\delta_{f}
is the
(E,G)
-biderivation defined by

(\delta_{f})_{a}=f\circ\Phi_{a}^{E}-\Phi_{a}^{G}\circ f

for any
a\in A
. The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of inner
(E,G)
-biderivations over
S
is denoted by
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{in}}(E,G)
.

(4)

An inner
(E,G)
-biderivation
\delta_{f}
is said to be strictly inner if the map
f^{*}:\omega_{G}\to\omega_{E}
is zero. The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of
strictly inner
(E,G)
-biderivations is
denoted by
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{si}}(E,G)
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space
\mathrm{Der}(E,G)
admits right and left
A
-actions defined by

(\delta*c)_{a}=\delta_{a}\circ\Phi_{c}^{E},\quad(c*\delta)_{a}=\Phi_{c}^{G}\circ\delta_{a}

for any

.
Then the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of

\mathrm{Der}_{0}(E,G)\supseteq\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{in}}(E,G)\supseteq\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{si}}(E,G)

-actions, and two actions agree with each other on
the quotient
\mathrm{Der}(E,G)/\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{in}}(E,G)

PR
, p. 412]
.

Note that we have an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isomorphism

(3.1)

\mathrm{Der}(E,G)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{q},S}(E,G),\quad\delta\mapsto\delta_{t}.

We identify these
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces via this isomorphism. Moreover, writing
G=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{S}[Z])
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules

(3.2)

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes-q^{m}}_{E}\to\mathscr{H}\!\!\mathit{om}_{\mathbb{F}_{q},S}(E,G),\quad l\mapsto(Z\mapsto l).

Suppose that
S=\mathrm{Spec}(B)
for an
A
-algebra

.
When

, the linear action of

on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
gives structures of
B
-modules on

and its

-subspaces defined above, which are compatible with

-action
\delta\mapsto c*\delta
.

Definition 3.2
.

We call the

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\otimes-q}(S)\oplus\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\otimes-q^{2}}(S)

-modules

Let

\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}}
-linear map, consider the pull-back

\langle-,-\rangle_{B^{\Delta}_{R}}:=(T^{\triangledown})^{*}\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}.

Let
dX
and
\eta
be the elements of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), respectively. By (
4.9
) and
Corollary
5.7
, we have

(5.11)

\langle dX,\eta\rangle_{B^{\Delta}_{R}}=x^{2}l(x)a_{2}b_{h}^{-q}\in(\mathscr{B}_{R}^{\Delta})^{\times}.

Thus we obtain an extension as an
\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}}
-linear map by Lemma
4.3
. Moreover, since
X_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}
is an integral scheme,
any nonempty open subset
U

Y_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}

(5.12)

\mathcal{O}(U)\to\mathcal{O}(U\cap Y_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R})

Since
\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}

\overline{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}

\overline{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}=0

For the perfectness of
\overline{\langle-,-\rangle}_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}
, consider the map

\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}\to\mathscr{H}\!\!\mathit{om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}}}(\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{n})_{R}}),\quad\varphi\mapsto(\varphi^{\prime}\mapsto\overline{\langle\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}).

Then its pull-back by

is given by the
\mathscr{B}^{\Delta}_{R}
-linear map

(dX,\eta)\mapsto((dX)^{\vee},\eta^{\vee})\begin{pmatrix}0&-x^{2}l(x)a_{2}b_{h}^{-q}\\
x^{2}l(x)a_{2}b_{h}^{-q}&0\end{pmatrix},

4.3

Since we have proved that
\overline{\langle-,-\rangle}_{\mathrm{un}}^{\Delta}
