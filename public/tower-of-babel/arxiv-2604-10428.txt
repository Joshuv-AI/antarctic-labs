Worst-case Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd algorithm with average-case correct quantum Fourier transform
Changpeng Shao

Abstract
In [ Quantum 6, 872, 2022 ], Linden and de Wolf proposed a lightweight protocol for verifying the average-case correct behavior of the quantum Fourier transform (QFT). They proved that good average-case QFT performance suffices for good worst-case performance in several quantum tasks. Here we provide another application of this worst-case-to-average-case reduction, using a strengthened Linden–de Wolf protocol. We show that, across three distinct scenarios, the Harrow–Hassidim–Lloyd algorithm can be executed with provably good worst-case performance, assuming only that the QFT is correct on average.

Introduction

Quantum verification refers to the process of ensuring the correctness and reliability of quantum computers. Given the probabilistic nature of quantum systems, verification in quantum computing is a critical aspect of using quantum computers. A lot of efforts have been made on quantum verification in different scenarios, e.g., see
[
9
,
4
,
12
,
5
,
1
,
6
,
3
]
.

In quantum computing, the quantum Fourier transform (QFT) has emerged as a pivotal component in various quantum algorithms, such as Shor’s algorithm
[
16
]
and quantum phase estimation
[
8
]
.
Recently, in
[
10
]
, Linden and de Wolf proposed an efficient lightweight protocol for verifying the average-case correct behaviour of QFT. They also showed that one could achieve good worst-case performance for some key quantum tasks (e.g., phase estimation, period finding, and amplitude estimation) with a good average-case performance of the QFT. This is a very interesting finding since worst-case verification is usually exponentially hard, while average-case verification is relatively easy.
In this work, we aim to give a new application of this average-case correct QFT. We will focus on the implementation of the Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd (HHL) algorithm
[
7
]
for solving linear systems using average-case correct QFT.

The HHL algorithm is a nice application of phase estimation. It plays an important role in designing new quantum algorithms, especially in quantum machine learning, e.g., see
[
2
,
11
,
15
]
. In
[
10
]
, Linden and de Wolf showed that we can do phase estimation in the worst case with average-case correct QFT. So why it is still interesting here to consider the HHL algorithm? Indeed, what Linden and de Wolf have proved is the feasibility of outputting eigenvalues in phase estimation using average-case correct QFT. This is enough for many key tasks.
However, in the HHL algorithm, we need to output a quantum state that depends on the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the input matrix. A good average-case correct QFT in their sense may introduce some uncontrollable local phases to the final target state. This can make the final result far away from the desired state.

For example, let
C=DF_{N}^{-1}
, where
F_{N}^{-1}
is the inverse of QFT and
D
is an unknown diagonal unitary matrix. Then it is close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on average in the sense that
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C|\hat{k}\rangle,F_{N}^{-1}|\hat{k}\rangle)]\geq 1-\eta
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the fidelity and
\eta
is an error parameter.
Here
|\hat{k}\rangle=F_{N}|k\rangle
.
In the case that
C=DF_{N}^{-1}
, we have
\eta=0
. So this unitary will pass Linden-de Wolf’s protocol with success probability 1.
Suppose our input state is
\alpha_{1}|\widehat{k_{1}}\rangle\otimes|u_{1}\rangle+\alpha_{2}|\widehat{k_{2}}\rangle\otimes|u_{2}\rangle
. When we apply
C\otimes I
to it, we obtain
\alpha_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}|k_{1}\rangle\otimes|u_{1}\rangle+\alpha_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|k_{2}\rangle\otimes|u_{2}\rangle
for some unknown
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
. If the goal is to output
k_{1},k_{2}
, then we only need to measure the first register.
But if the goal is to output the state
\alpha_{1}|k_{1}\rangle|u_{1}\rangle+\alpha_{2}|k_{2}\rangle|u_{2}\rangle
up to certain error, then this
C
is not powerful enough unless
\theta_{1}\approx\theta_{2}
.
This phenomenon is very unfriendly for the HHL algorithm.
To resolve this problem, in this work, we will show how to strengthen Linden-de Wolf’s protocol so that any quantum channel that passes the strengthened protocol will allow us to run the HHL algorithm in the worst case with an average-case correct QFT.

We below state our main results in more detail. Let
C
be a quantum channel.
In
[
10
]
, Linden-de Wolf assumed that

{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[{\mathcal{F}}(C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),|k\rangle\langle k|)\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|)\,|k\rangle\right]\geq 1-\eta,

(1)

i.e.,
C
is close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on the Fourier basis.
To enhance the power of this quantum channel, in this work, we will assume that it satisfies

{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\left[\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle\right]\geq 1-\eta.

(2)

To better understand this channel, we suppose
C
is a unitary channel for simplicity, then the above implies that
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle k|C|\hat{k}\rangle|^{2}

\geq 1-\eta
, i.e., the normalised trace of
CF_{N}
is close to 1. As a unitary,
CF_{N}
will introduce a global phase up to certain errors.

To verify if
C
satisfies (
2
) or not in a lightweight way, we prove that if
C
is close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on the Fourier basis and close to
F_{N}
on the computational basis on average, then
C
will satisfy (
2
). The converse is also true. Combing this with Linden-de Wolf’s result, it suffices to give a lightweight protocol to test if
C
is close to
F_{N}
on the Fourier basis and close to
F_{N}
on the computational basis on average, which can be done by a similar protocol to Linden-de Wolf’s. Similar results hold for quantum channels
P
that are close to
F_{N}
on average in the sense of

{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\left[\langle\widehat{-k}|\,P(|k\rangle\langle l|)\,|\widehat{-l}\rangle\right]\geq 1-\eta.

(3)

Finally, with
C,P
satisfying (
2
) and (
3
), we prove that when
F_{N}^{-1}
is replaced by
C
and
F_{N}
is replaced by
P
in the HHL algorithm, we will obtain a state that is close to the desired state of the HHL algorithm with high fidelity on average. The rigorous statements are given in Theorems
10
and
13
. We also considered special cases when
C,P
are unitary channels under different assumptions. Due to the unitary property, the results are much more concise in comparison, see Theorems
14
,
15
,
17
and

18
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Some facts about fidelity and trace distance

The
fidelity
between two mixed quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
is defined as

{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma):=\left({\rm Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\sigma}\rho\sqrt{\sigma}}\,\right)^{2}.

(4)

For any two states
\rho
and
\sigma
, we have
0\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq 1
and
{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)=1
if and only if
\rho=\sigma
.
In particular, if
\sigma=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
is a pure state, then
{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=\langle\psi|\rho|\psi\rangle
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a quantum channel, then
{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\mathcal{E}(\sigma))
. The equality holds when
\mathcal{E}
is a unitary channel.

A closely related notion is
trace distance
.
It is defined as follows

{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^{\dagger}(\rho-\sigma)}.

(5)

The trace distance is a metric on the space of density matrices, i.e. it is non-negative, symmetric, and satisfies the triangle inequality.
Moreover,
let
\mathcal{E}
be a quantum channel, then
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\mathcal{E}(\sigma))\leq{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,\sigma)
, and equality holds when
\mathcal{E}
is a unitary channel.
The trace distance can be computed using the singular values of
\rho-\sigma
. More precisely, it equals the half of the sum of all singular values of
\rho-\sigma
.

The fidelity is related to the trace distance by the following inequalities:

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)}\leq{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)},

(6)

\displaystyle 1-{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\leq{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|).

(7)

Fidelity does not satisfy triangle inequality, however, according to (
6
), (
7
) we have the following inequality that will be used frequently in this work.
For more about properties of fidelity and trace distance, we refer to the book
[
18
]
.

Proposition 1
.

For any two density operators
\rho,\sigma
and any quantum state
|\psi\rangle
, we have

{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\geq 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,\sigma)}-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}(\sigma,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)}.

Proof.

From (
7
), we have
{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\geq 1-{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|).
By the triangle inequality of trace distance, we have that
{\mathcal{F}}(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\geq 1-{\mathcal{T}}(\rho,\sigma)-{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|).

Finally by (
6
), we obtain the claimed lower bound.
∎

Fact 2

(von Neumann’s trace inequality
[
13
]
)
.

For any
n\times n
operators
A,B
with singular values

\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}
and

\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\beta_{n}

respectively, we have
{\rm Tr}(AB)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}.

Particularly,
{\rm Tr}(AB)\leq\|A\|_{2}\|B\|_{1}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the operator norm and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the trace norm.

Fact 3

(Jensen’s inequality)
.

Let
X
be a random variable and
\varphi
be a convex function, then

\varphi({\mathbb{E}}[X])\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X)]
.

2.2
Sets of quantum channels that are close to the QFT on average

For any integer
N
, we denote by
[N]=\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
\omega_{N}=e^{2\pi i/N}
. The quantum Fourier transform (QFT) is defined as
F_{N}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j,k\in[N]}\omega_{N}^{jk}|j\rangle\langle k|
. In this work, we always assume that
N=2^{n}
for some integer
n>0
.
For any integer
k\in[N]
, we use
|\hat{k}\rangle
to denote the Fourier basis state, i.e.,

|\hat{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{l\in[N]}\omega_{N}^{kl}\,|l\rangle=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{n}\Big(|0\rangle+e^{2\pi ik/2^{l}}|1\rangle\Big).

Let

U_{k}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{2\pi ik/2^{l}}\end{pmatrix},

(8)

then
|\hat{k}\rangle=U_{k}|+\rangle^{n}
. It is easy to check that
U_{k}^{-1}=U_{-k}
.

In the following, we will define three different sets collecting all quantum channels that are close to the inverse of QFT in different senses. First, inspired by
[
10
]
,
for any
\eta\in[0,1]
, we define

\displaystyle S_{1}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{C\text{ is a quantum channel}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,|k\rangle\langle k|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-\eta\Big\}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{C\text{ is a quantum channel}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big[\langle k|\,C\circ F_{N}(|k\rangle\langle k|)\,|k\rangle\Big]\geq 1-\eta\Big\},

(10)

In the above,
C\circ F_{N}
refers to the composition of two channels, i.e.,
C\circ F_{N}(\rho)=C(F_{N}\rho F_{N}^{\dagger})
for any density operator
\rho
.
The set
S_{1}(\eta)
collects all channels
C
that are supposed to be close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on average on the Fourier basis. The following is a lightweight protocol for verifying this.

Testing Algorithm 1 (TA1).

[
10
, Theorem 1]

Choose
k\in[N]
uniformly at random and prepare
|\hat{k}\rangle
. Run
C
on
|\hat{k}\rangle
and measure the resulting state on the computational basis. Output 1 if the outcome is
k
, and output 0 otherwise.

It was proved in
[
10
, Theorem 1]
that to testing if
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
or not it suffices to run the above protocol
O(\eta^{-2}\log(1/\delta))
times, where
\delta
is the failure probability.
As explained in the introduction part,
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
is not stronger enough for us to run the HHL algorithm.
To overcome this, we define a new set as follows:

S_{3}(\eta)=\{C\text{ is a quantum channel}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle\geq 1-\eta\}.

(11)

To obtain some intuition about
S_{3}(\eta)
, we consider the case that
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
is a unitary channel.
We can check that the norm of the normalised trace of
CF_{N}
is close to 1, i.e.,
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle k|C|\hat{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq 1-\eta
. In this case, we will have
C\approx e^{i\theta}F_{N}^{-1}
for some
\theta
, which only introduces a global phase up to certain error.
We shall prove that
S_{3}(\eta)
is the right set for us to run the HHL algorithm. Before that, the first question we need to answer is how to verify if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
or not in a lightweight way. For this, we introduce another set containing all
C
that are close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on average on the computational basis:

S_{2}(\eta)=\Big\{C\text{ is a quantum channel}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|k\rangle\langle k|),\,|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-\eta\Big\}.

(12)

In the above and also in the following, by
-k
we always mean it is modulo
N
, i.e.,
|-k\rangle=|N-k\rangle
.

The following result states that to verify
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
it suffices to propose a lightweight protocol to verify if
C\in S_{2}(\eta)
or not. The proof of this theorem is given at the end of Section
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1})\cap S_{2}(\eta_{2})
, then
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})
.
Conversely, if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then
C\in S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
.

By Theorem
4
, if a testing algorithm indicates that
C\in S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
, then we know that
C\in S_{3}(2\eta)
. If
C\not\in S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
, we may not have

C\not\in S_{3}(2\eta)
. But what we can claim is
C\not\in S_{3}(\eta)
.
In practice, we can just focus on quantum channels in
S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
.
When
\eta
is small, this difference is negligible. So for us, it suffices to find a lightweight protocol to verify if
C\in S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
or not.
With
TA1
, we know how to test if
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
. Below, we propose a lightweight protocol for verifying if
C\in S_{2}(\eta)
or not.

Recall that
F_{N}^{-1}|k\rangle=|\widehat{-k}\rangle=U_{-k}|+\rangle^{n}=U_{-k}H^{\otimes n}|0\rangle^{n}
. Based on this we can propose the following protocol.

Testing Algorithm 2 (TA2).

Choose
k
uniformly at random. Run
H^{\otimes n}U_{k}C
on
|k\rangle
and measure the resulting state on the computational basis. Output 1 if the outcome is
|0\rangle^{n}
, and output 0 otherwise.

The intuition behind the above two testing algorithms: We shall suppose that
\eta=0
and
C
is a unitary channel for simplicity.
If
C
passes
TA2
, then we have
C=F_{N}^{-1}\Phi
where
\Phi
is diagonal and unitary. Note that if
C
also passes
TA1
, then in the special case that
\eta=0
we have
C=\Psi F^{-1}
where
\Psi
is diagonal and unitary. Thus, if
C
passes both
TA1
and
TA2
, we will have
C=F_{N}^{-1}
up to a global phase.

The correctness of
TA2
is similar to that of
TA1
. Similar to
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, we can estimate
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C(|k\rangle\langle k|),|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|)]
up to additive error
\varepsilon
, with success probability
1-\delta
, by running the above protocol
O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))
times.

3
Some facts about
S_{3}(\eta)

In this section, we present some basic properties of the set
S_{3}(\eta)
.
Let
C(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{\dagger}
be a Kraus representation of
C
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=I
, then
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
implies

1-\eta\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|A_{i}|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|A_{i}^{\dagger}|l\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\big|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|A_{i}|\hat{k}\rangle]\big|^{2}.

(13)

Particularly, when
r=1
, i.e., when
C(\rho)=U\rho U^{\dagger}
is a unitary channel for some unitary
U
, we have
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|U|k\rangle]|^{2}\geq 1-\eta
. The above also implies that

{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle
is always a nonnegtive real number.
The description (
13
) of
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
will be useful in the analysis of implementing the HHL algorithm using noised QFT.

As a quantum channel, an equivalent description is that there is a unitary
U
such that
C(\rho)={\rm Tr}_{2}[U(\rho\otimes|0^{m}\rangle\langle 0^{m}|)U^{\dagger}]
for some integer
m
. So if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then we have

\displaystyle 1-\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|{\rm Tr}_{2}[U(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|\otimes|0^{m}\rangle\langle 0^{m}|)U^{\dagger}]|l\rangle

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}(\langle k|\otimes I)U|\hat{k},0^{m}\rangle\langle\hat{l},0^{m}|U^{\dagger}(|l\rangle\otimes I)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}\Big[\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{k}(\langle k|\otimes I)U|\hat{k},0^{m}\rangle\Big)\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle\hat{k},0^{m}|U^{\dagger}(|k\rangle\otimes I)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\big|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle k,p|U|\hat{k},0^{m}\rangle\big|^{2}.

The above description of
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
will be useful in the proof of Theorem
4
.

The following result states that if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then the phases of

\{\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle:k,l\in[N]\}
are close to each other on average. This is a desired property when running the HHL algorithm. In the following result, we consider a general result.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that
x_{p}=r_{p}e^{i\theta_{p}}
for
p\in[M]
, where
r_{p}\in[0,1]
. If
|{\mathbb{E}}_{p}[x_{p}]|^{2}\geq 1-\eta
, then
|{\mathbb{E}}_{p}[e^{i\theta_{p}}]|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}_{p,q}[\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})]\geq 1-2\eta
.

Proof.

Denote

A_{+}=\{(p,q):\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})\geq 0\},A_{-}=\{(p,q):\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})<0\}
. Then

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}_{p}[x_{p}]|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{M}r_{p}r_{q}\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{+}}r_{p}r_{q}\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})-\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{-}}r_{p}r_{q}|\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})|.

Since
|{\mathbb{E}}_{p}[x_{p}]|^{2}\geq 1-\eta
, the above equality implies
|A_{-}|\leq M^{2}\eta
and

\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{+}}\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})\geq M^{2}(1-\eta).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{+}}\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})-\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{-}}|\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})|\geq M^{2}(1-\eta)-M^{2}\eta=M^{2}(1-2\eta).

In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{p,q}[\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})]=\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{+}}\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})-\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{(p,q)\in A_{-}}|\cos(\theta_{p}-\theta_{q})|\geq 1-2\eta.

This completes the proof.
∎

The next result states that if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then
C
is correct on any orthogonal basis on average. Combining Theorem
4
, this implies that if
C
is close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on average both on the Fourier basis and on the computational basis, then
C
is close to
F_{N}^{-1}
on any orthogonal basis on average.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\{|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in[N]}
be an orthogonal basis and
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then

{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle{\bf u}_{k}|\,C\circ F_{N}(|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{l}|)\,|{\bf u}_{l}\rangle\geq 1-\eta.

In particular, we have

S_{3}(\eta)=\{C\text{ is a quantum channel}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle\widehat{-k}|\,C(|k\rangle\langle l|)\,|\widehat{-l}\rangle\geq 1-\eta\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle=\sum_{l}u_{kl}|l\rangle
, then it is easy to compute that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle{\bf u}_{k}|\,C\circ F_{N}(|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{l}|)\,|{\bf u}_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q,r,s}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\bar{u}_{kp}u_{kq}]{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\bar{u}_{lr}u_{ls}]\langle p|\,C(|\hat{q}\rangle\langle\hat{r}|)\,|s\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,r}\langle p|\,C(|\hat{p}\rangle\langle\hat{r}|)\,|r\rangle.

Since
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, we have
\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,r}\langle p|\,C(|\hat{p}\rangle\langle\hat{r}|)\,|r\rangle\geq 1-\eta
.
In the above, we used the fact that
\{|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in[N]}
is an orthogonal basis so that
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\bar{u}_{kp}u_{kq}]=\delta^{p}_{q}
.
∎

Intuitively,
C\in S_{3}(\eta)\subseteq S_{1}(\eta)
implies that most diagonal entries of
C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)
have absolute values close to 1, so most non-diagonal entries should be small. With this intuition, we have the following result.

Proposition 7
.

Assume that
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
, then for any
k\neq 0
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle k+l|\,C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|k+l\rangle]\leq\eta
.

Proof.

As a quantum channel, we know that
C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)
is a density operator. So all diagonal entries are nonnegative real numbers and their sum is 1. Thus we have

\langle k+l|\,C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|k+l\rangle\leq 1-\langle l|\,C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle.

Since
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle l|\,C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle]\geq 1-\eta
.
This means

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle k+l|\,C(|\hat{l}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|k+l\rangle]\leq\eta
, which is as claimed.
∎

In the end, we aim to prove Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We first prove the first claim, i.e., if
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1})\cap S_{2}(\eta_{2})
, then
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})
.
We will use (
14
).
As a quantum channel, we assume that there is a unitary
U
such that

C(\rho)={\rm Tr}_{2}(U(\rho\otimes|0..0\rangle\langle 0..0|)U^{\dagger})
.
Assume that
U|\hat{k},0..0\rangle=\sum_{l,m}U^{k}_{l,m}|l,m\rangle
.
By assumption, we have
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1})\cap S_{2}(\eta_{2})
, so

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q,k}|U^{k}_{k,q}|^{2}\geq 1-\eta_{1},\quad\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{q,k}\left|\sum_{l,m}\omega^{k(m-l)}U^{l}_{m,q}\right|^{2}\geq 1-\eta_{2}.

(15)

For the second inequality, we can expand it as follows

\displaystyle 1-\eta_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{q,k}\left|\sum_{l}U^{l}_{l,q}+\sum_{l\neq m}\omega^{k(m-l)}U^{l}_{m,q}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{q,k}\left[\left|\sum_{l}U^{l}_{l,q}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{l\neq m}\omega^{k(m-l)}U^{l}_{m,q}\right|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{l}\overline{U}^{l}_{l,q}\right)\left(\sum_{l\neq m}\omega^{k(m-l)}U^{l}_{m,q}\right)\right].

The summation over
k
implies that the third term is 0. The first term is

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{q}\left|\sum_{l}U^{l}_{l,q}\right|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the second term satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{q,k}\left|\sum_{l}\omega^{-kl}\sum_{m\neq l}\omega^{km}U^{l}_{m,q}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{q,k}\sum_{l}\left|\sum_{m\neq l}\omega^{km}U^{l}_{m,q}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{q,k}\sum_{l}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\neq l}\omega^{k(m_{1}-m_{2})}U^{l}_{m_{1},q}\overline{U}^{l}_{m_{2},q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q}\sum_{l}\sum_{m\neq l}|U^{l}_{m,q}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l}\sum_{q}\left(\sum_{m}|U^{l}_{m,q}|^{2}-|U^{l}_{l,q}|^{2}\right).

Since
U
is a unitary,
\sum_{q}\sum_{m}|U^{l}_{m,q}|^{2}=1
, so the above equals

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l}\left(1-\sum_{q}|U^{l}_{l,q}|^{2}\right)=1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l}\sum_{q}|U^{l}_{l,q}|^{2}.

By the first inequality of (
15
), the above in upper bounded by
\eta_{1}
.
From the above calcultion, we finally obtain

{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|\,C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)\,|l\rangle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{q}\left|\sum_{l}U^{l}_{l,q}\right|^{2}\geq 1-\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}.

This means
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})
.

Next we prove that if
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
, then
C\in S_{1}(\eta)\cap S_{2}(\eta)
.
Similar to the computation of (
13
), we have that
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
if and only if

1-\eta\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle k|A_{i}|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|A_{i}^{\dagger}|k\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[|\langle k|A_{i}|\hat{k}\rangle|^{2}].

As a result, we have
S_{3}(\eta)\subseteq S_{1}(\eta)
.
About
S_{2}(\eta)
we have
C\in S_{2}(\eta)
if and only if

1-\eta\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle\widehat{-k}|A_{i}|k\rangle\langle k|A_{i}^{\dagger}|\widehat{-k}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[|\langle\widehat{-k}|A_{i}|k\rangle|^{2}].

Together with Proposition
6
, we have
S_{3}(\eta)\subseteq S_{2}(\eta)
.
∎

4

Proof.

This is followed by a similar argument to the proof of Theorem

.

The next result states that if we only have a quantum channel

Proposition 9

Assume that
C
is a quantum channel.

1.

Let

If

Consequently, if

, then

(1). Suppose

,
so

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C\circ R(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,|-k\rangle\langle-k|)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C(|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|),\,|-k\rangle\langle-k|)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,|k\rangle\langle k|)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(R\circ C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,|-k\rangle\langle-k|)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(R\circ C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,R(|k\rangle\langle k|))]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{F}}(C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),\,|k\rangle\langle k|)]

So

(2). First, we show that

1

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{3}(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),C^{2}(|k\rangle\langle k|)\Big)\Big]}-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{2}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{2}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|k\rangle\langle k|),|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|\Big)\Big]}-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]}.

{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{2}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-\sqrt{\eta_{1}}-\sqrt{\eta_{2}}.

(16)

Thus

{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{3}(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-\sqrt{\eta_{1}}-\sqrt{\sqrt{\eta_{1}}+\sqrt{\eta_{2}}}.

Next, we show that

. The proof is similar. By Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{3}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{3}(|k\rangle\langle k|),C(|-k\rangle\langle-k|)\Big)\Big]}-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|-k\rangle\langle-k|),|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|\Big)\Big]}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{2}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|-k\rangle\langle-k|\Big)\Big]}-\sqrt{1-{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C(|k\rangle\langle k|),|\widehat{-k}\rangle\langle\widehat{-k}|\Big)\Big]}.

By (

) and the assumption that

{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C^{3}(|k\rangle\langle k|),|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{k}|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-\sqrt{\eta_{2}}-\sqrt{\sqrt{\eta_{1}}+\sqrt{\eta_{2}}}.

The last claim now follows from Theorem
4
and Proposition

5
Worst-case HHL with average-case correct QFT

In this section, we analyze the performance of running the HHL algorithm using average-case correct QFT. A key step of the HHL algorithm is quantum phase estimation. So similar to
[
10
]
, we first focus on the perfect case where all the eigenvalues of the input matrix are represented by
n
bits precisely. We then extend the proofs and results to the general case.

5.1
Outline of the HHL algorithm

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
be a sparse Hermitian matrix,
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a vector. Suppose we have the ability to prepare the quantum state
|{\bf b}\rangle
efficiently. On a quantum computer, we can use the HHL algorithm to solve the linear system
A{\bf x}={\bf b}
. The output is a quantum state closing to
|A^{+}{\bf b}\rangle
, where
A^{+}
is the pseudoinverse. Moreover, using a similar idea to HHL, we can prepare the state
|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
for any efficiently computable function
f(x)
, and the HHL algorithm corresponds to
f(x)=1/x
. For completeness purposes, below we outline the procedure of preparing
|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
for a general
f(x)
.

Let the eigenvalue decomposition of
A
be
A=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\langle{\bf u}_{k}|
. Then there exist
\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|{\bf b}\rangle=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle
. To better understand the analysis, below we only state the algorithm in the perfect case by assuming that
\sigma_{k}=p_{k}/N
for some integer
p_{k}
, where
N=2^{n}
is determined by the accuracy. Namely,
\{\sigma_{k}:k\in[d]\}
are
n
-bit numbers exactly. Without loss of generality, we assume that
f(x)
is bounded by 1 in the interval
[-1,1]
. The following is a basic description of the HHL algorithm:

1.

Prepare

|\phi_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}|j\rangle|{\bf b}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|j\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle.

2.

Apply
\sum_{j}|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes e^{2\pi iAj}

|\phi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}e^{2\pi ij\sigma_{k}}|j\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\widehat{p_{k}}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle.

3.

Apply
F_{N}^{-1}\otimes I

|\phi_{3}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|p_{k}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle.

4.

Apply control rotations

|\phi_{4}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|p_{k}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right).

5.

Apply
F_{N}\otimes I

\displaystyle|\phi_{5}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\widehat{p_{k}}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}e^{2\pi ij\sigma_{k}}|j\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right).

6.

Apply
\sum_{j}|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes e^{-2\pi iAj}

\displaystyle|\phi_{6}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}|j\rangle\otimes\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right).

7.

Apply Hadamard gates to
|j\rangle

\displaystyle|\phi_{7}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes f(A)|{\bf b}\rangle\otimes|0\rangle+|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes\sqrt{1-f^{2}(A)}|{\bf b}\rangle\otimes|1\rangle.

The information of
f(A){\bf b}
is now included in the first term of
|\phi_{7}\rangle
. To obtain its quantum state, it suffices to measure the last qubit. We will obtain
|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
if the output of rhe measurement is 0.
We can also use amplitude amplification to increase the success probability, but that is a much less lightweight process that involves many applications of
|\phi_{7}\rangle
. Note that one application of the HHL algorithm is to estimate the expectation value
\langle{\bf x}|M|{\bf x}\rangle
for some operator
M
, where
|{\bf x}\rangle=|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
. We can also achieve this goal using
|\phi_{7}\rangle
. Namely, we compute
\langle\phi_{7}|(I^{n}\otimes M\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|)|\phi_{7}\rangle
. Because of the above reasons, in the following, we only focus on the analysis for
|\phi_{7}\rangle
.

5.2
Main result in the case when eigenvalues are represented by
n
-bit exactly

To introduce randomness, we shall use the same idea given in
[
10
]
by considering
A_{l}:=A+lI_{d}/N
for a random
l\in[N]
, which is chosen uniformly at random. The eigenvalues of
A_{l}
are
(p_{k}+l)/N
, where
k\in[d]
. The eigenvectors do not change. From
A_{l}
, we can also construct the state
|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
. It suffices to change
f(x)
to
f_{l}(x):=f(x-l/N)
. We denote the final state as
|\phi_{7,l}\rangle
. It is
|\phi_{7}\rangle
in the case where all eigenvalues are represented by
n
-bit. But generally, it may depend on
l
.

Given
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{3}(\eta_{2})

F_{N}^{-1}
with
C
and replacing
F_{N}
with
P
.
Since

are quantum channels, the algorithm should be implemented in the form of density operators.
Thus, we denote the final state as
\rho_{7,l}
.
When running HHL, we assume that all unitaries except
F_{N},F_{N}^{-1}
are perfect.
Our main result in the case when all eigenvalues of
A

n

Theorem 10
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}

|\phi_{7,l}\rangle=|0\rangle\otimes f(A)|{\bf b}\rangle+|0^{\bot}\rangle

1
1

1

Here we simplified the notation of
|\phi_{7,l}\rangle

and so equals
|\phi_{7}\rangle
.
Assume that

, and the eigenvalues of
A
are represented by
n
bits precisely. For any

\rho_{7,l}
be the resulting state by replacing

(18)

Proof.

Because of the introduction of
l
, we denote the states appeared in the HHL algorithm as
|\phi_{1,l}\rangle,\ldots,|\phi_{7,l}\rangle
.
Since all unitaries except

By assumption,

\sum_{i}A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=I

C(\rho)=\sum_{i}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{\dagger}

Thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C\otimes I(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{3,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{3,l}|\Big)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in[d]}|\beta_{k_{1}}|^{2}|\beta_{k_{2}}|^{2}\langle p_{k_{1}}+l|C\Big(|\widehat{p_{k_{1}}+l}\rangle\langle\widehat{p_{k_{2}}+l}|\Big)|p_{k_{2}}+l\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in[d]}|\beta_{k_{1}}|^{2}|\beta_{k_{2}}|^{2}\langle p_{k_{1}}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{p_{k_{1}}+l}\rangle\langle\widehat{p_{k_{2}}+l}|A_{i}^{\dagger}|p_{k_{2}}+l\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\left|\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\langle p_{k}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{p_{k}+l}\rangle\right|^{2}\right].

By Jensen’s inequality (see Fact
3
), it is bounded from below by

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\langle p_{k}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{p_{k}+l}\rangle\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle\right|^{2}.

By (
19
), it is greater than
1-\eta_{1}
.

Denote the control rotation that maps
|\phi_{3,l}\rangle
to
|\phi_{4,l}\rangle

R_{l}

Note that

|\phi_{4,l}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|p_{k}+l\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right)

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(R_{l}(C\otimes I)(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|\Big)\right]\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(C\otimes I(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{3,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{3,l}|\Big)\right]\geq 1-\eta_{1}.

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(P\otimes I(|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),\,|\phi_{5,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{5,l}|\Big)\right]\geq 1-\eta_{2}.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\Big((P\otimes I)R_{l}(C\otimes I)(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{5,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{5,l}|\Big)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}{\mathcal{F}}\Big(P\otimes I(|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),|\phi_{5,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{5,l}|\Big)}

\displaystyle-\,\sqrt{1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}{\mathcal{F}}\Big(P\otimes I(|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),(P\otimes I)R_{l}(C\otimes I)(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|)\Big)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}{\mathcal{F}}\Big(P\otimes I(|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),|\phi_{5,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{5,l}|\Big)}

\displaystyle-\,\sqrt{1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}{\mathcal{F}}\Big((|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),R_{l}(C\otimes I)(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|)\Big)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{\eta_{1}}-\sqrt{\eta_{2}}.

The claimed result follows from the above estimate since the unitaries in steps 6 and 7 are also perfect with no error by assumption and can only increase the fidelity.
∎

One application of the HHL algorithm is to compute the expectation value

\langle{\bf b}f(A)^{T}|M|f(A){\bf b}\rangle
for some operator
M
, or more generally
\langle\phi_{7}|M|\phi_{7}\rangle
in our notation. The above result suggests that on average, we can use
{\rm Tr}(M\rho_{7,l})
to approximate the desired value. We state this result as follows.

Theorem 11
.

Making the same assumption and notation as Theorem
10
, let
M
be any operator with
\|M\|_{2}\leq 1
, denote

X_{l}={\rm Tr}(M|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|),\widetilde{X}_{l}={\rm Tr}(M\rho_{7,l})
, then

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\widetilde{X}_{l}-X_{l}|]\leq 2(\eta_{1}^{1/4}+\eta_{2}^{1/4}).

Proof.

By von Neumann’s trace inequality (see Fact
2
), for any two operators
A,B
we have
{\rm Tr}(AB)\leq\|A\|_{2}\|B\|_{1}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the operator norm and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the trace norm. So
by (
6
) and Theorem
10

we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\widetilde{X}_{l}-X_{l}|]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|{\rm Tr}(M(\rho_{7,l}-|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|))|]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|M\|_{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\|\rho_{7,l}-|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|\|_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\|M\|_{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(\rho_{7,l},|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{7,l},|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|)]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\sqrt{\eta_{1}}+\sqrt{\eta_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\eta_{1}^{1/4}+\eta_{2}^{1/4}).

In the second equality, we used the definition of trace distance.
∎

5.3
Main result in the general case

In the general case, the eigenvalues of
A
may be specified by more than
n
bits. The analysis here will be a little complicated. Recall that
N=2^{n}
. For each
\sigma_{k}
, denote
p_{k}=\lfloor\sigma_{k}N\rfloor
, i.e.,
\sigma_{k}=p_{k}/N+\varepsilon
for some error
\varepsilon
. Denote

G_{k}=\{p_{k}-K+1,\ldots,p_{k}+K\},

(20)

which stands for the set of good candidates that can be used to approximate
\sigma_{k}
. Then
|G_{k}|=2K
.
Let

|\psi_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}e^{2\pi ij\sigma_{k}}|j\rangle=\sum_{g\in G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|\hat{g}\rangle+\sum_{g\notin G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|\hat{g}\rangle

(21)

for some
\alpha_{kg}\in\mathbb{C}
.
As shown in
[
10
, Proposition 2]
, for any constant
K>1
,
\sum_{g\notin G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}|^{2}\leq 2/(K-1)
. This is negligible when
K
is large enough.

Lemma 12
.

Let
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
be a quantum channel with Kraus representation
C(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{\dagger}
for any density operator
\rho
. Let
G\subseteq[N]
be a subset and

\displaystyle|\psi_{l}\rangle=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}|\widehat{g+l}\rangle+\sum_{g\notin G}\alpha_{g}|\widehat{g+l}\rangle,\quad|\varphi_{l}\rangle=\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}|{g+l}\rangle+\sum_{g\notin G}\alpha_{g}|{g+l}\rangle.

Assume that
\sum_{g\in G}|\alpha_{g}|^{2}=1-\delta
, then

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[\langle\varphi_{l}|A_{i}|\psi_{l}\rangle\Big]=(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\,\rangle\Big]+Err_{i},

for some error term
Err_{i}
. Moreover,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i}|^{2}\leq 2\eta|G|^{2}+18\delta.

Proof.

For convenience, we denote
|\psi_{l}\rangle=\sqrt{1-\delta}\,|\psi_{l1}\rangle+\sqrt{\delta}\,|\psi_{l2}\rangle,|\varphi_{l}\rangle=\sqrt{1-\delta}\,|\varphi_{l1}\rangle+\sqrt{\delta}\,|\varphi_{l2}\rangle
according to their decompositions. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[\langle\varphi_{l}|A_{i}|\psi_{l}\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[\langle\varphi_{l1}|A_{i}|\psi_{l1}\rangle\Big]+Err_{i1},

where
Err_{i1}=\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l1}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l2}\rangle+\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l2}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l1}\rangle+\delta{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l2}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l2}\rangle
.

We can compute that

\displaystyle(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[\langle\varphi_{l1}|A_{i}|\psi_{l1}\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle g_{1}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}|\alpha_{g}|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle+Err_{i2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle+Err_{i2},

where

Err_{i2}=\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2}\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle g_{1}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle.

The overall error is
Err_{i}:=Err_{i1}+Err_{i2}
.

From the Kraus representation of
C
, it is easy to check that for any states
|a_{l}\rangle,|b_{l}\rangle
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\langle a_{l}|A_{i}|b_{l}\rangle|^{2}\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle a_{l}|\,C(|b_{l}\rangle\langle b_{l}|)\,|a_{l}\rangle.

(22)

So we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Big(\delta(1-\delta)|\langle\psi_{l1}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l2}\rangle|^{2}+\delta(1-\delta)|\langle\psi_{l2}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l1}\rangle|^{2}+\delta^{2}|\langle\psi_{l2}|A_{i}|\varphi_{l2}\rangle|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\delta(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l1}|C(|\varphi_{l2}\rangle\langle\varphi_{l2}|)|\psi_{l1}\rangle+3\delta(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l2}|C(|\varphi_{l1}\rangle\langle\varphi_{l1}|)|\psi_{l2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\,3\delta^{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\psi_{l2}|C(|\varphi_{l2}\rangle\langle\varphi_{l2}|)|\psi_{l2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 6\delta(1-\delta)+3\delta^{2}\leq 9\delta.

And

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2}\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle g_{1}+l|A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2}\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}\langle g_{1}+l|\,A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2},g_{3}\neq g_{4}\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}\alpha_{g_{3}}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{4}}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\langle g_{1}+l|\,A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle\langle\widehat{g_{3}+l}|\,A_{i}^{\dagger}|g_{4}+l\rangle\right].

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the above is smaller than

\leq\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2},g_{3}\neq g_{4}\in G}|\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}\alpha_{g_{3}}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{4}}|\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\langle g_{1}+l|\,A_{i}|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle|^{2}\right]}\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\langle\widehat{g_{3}+l}|\,A_{i}^{\dagger}|g_{4}+l\rangle|^{2}\right]}.

By (
22
), it is

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2},g_{3}\neq g_{4}\in G}|\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}\alpha_{g_{3}}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{4}}|\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle g_{1}+l|\,C(|\widehat{g_{2}+l}\rangle\langle\widehat{g_{2}+l}|)\,|g_{1}+l\rangle}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\,\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle g_{4}+l|\,C(|\widehat{g_{3}+l}\rangle\langle\widehat{g_{3}+l}|)\,|g_{4}+l\rangle}.

By Proposition
7
, it is smaller than

\leq\eta\sum_{g_{1}\neq g_{2},g_{3}\neq g_{4}\in G}|\bar{\alpha}_{g_{1}}\alpha_{g_{2}}\alpha_{g_{3}}\bar{\alpha}_{g_{4}}|\leq\eta|G|^{2}(1-\delta)^{2}\leq\eta|G|^{2}.

Finally, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i}|^{2}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i1}|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i2}|^{2}\leq 2\eta|G|^{2}+18\delta.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 13
.

Using the same notation as Theorem
10
. Assume that
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{3}(\eta_{2})
.
Then for any integer
K\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(\rho_{7,l},\,|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|\Big)\Big]\geq 1-(\eta_{1}^{1/2}+\eta_{2}^{1/2})-2\sqrt{K}(\eta_{1}^{1/4}+\eta_{2}^{1/4})-\frac{4\sqrt{5}}{(K-1)^{1/4}}.

(23)

Proof.

As before, we introduce a random variable
l\in[N]
and denote

|\psi_{kl}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\in[N]}e^{2\pi ij(\sigma_{k}+l/N)}|j\rangle=\sum_{g\in G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|\widehat{g+l}\rangle+\sum_{g\notin G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|\widehat{g+l}\rangle,

where
G_{k}
is defined in (
20
).
In the general case, the state in step 2 of the HHL algorithm (see Subsection
5.1
) becomes

|\phi_{2}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle,

where
|\psi_{k}\rangle
is of the form (
21
).
When introducing a random variable
l
, we obtain

|\phi_{2,l}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\psi_{kl}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle.

By applying
C\in S_{3}(\eta_{1})
to it, we obtain a state that is close to

|\phi_{3,l}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\varphi_{kl}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle,

where

|\varphi_{kl}\rangle=\sum_{g\in G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|g+l\rangle+\sum_{g\notin G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}|g+l\rangle.

We now aim to estimate

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{F}}(C\otimes I(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{3,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{3,l}|)]
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
10
, we let
C(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{\dagger}
be the Kraus representation of
C
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\dagger}A_{i}=I
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{F}}(C\otimes I(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{3,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{3,l}|)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in[d]}|\beta_{k_{1}}|^{2}|\beta_{k_{2}}|^{2}\langle\varphi_{k_{1}l}|C\Big(|\psi_{k_{1}l}\rangle\langle\psi_{k_{2}l}|\Big)|\varphi_{k_{2}l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in[d]}|\beta_{k_{1}}|^{2}|\beta_{k_{2}}|^{2}\langle\varphi_{k_{1}l}|A_{i}|\psi_{k_{1}l}\rangle\langle\psi_{k_{2}l}|A_{i}^{\dagger}|\varphi_{k_{2}l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\left|\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\langle\varphi_{kl}|A_{i}|\psi_{kl}\rangle\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\langle\varphi_{kl}|A_{i}|\psi_{kl}\rangle\right]\right|^{2}.

By Lemma
12
, the above equals

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\Big((1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle+Err_{i}\Big)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle+Err_{i}\right|^{2}.

Expanding it, it is bounded from below by

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle]|^{2}-2(1-\delta)\sum_{i=1}^{r}|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle]||Err_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle]|^{2}-2(1-\delta)\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{r}|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle]|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Err_{i}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)^{2}(1-\eta_{1})-2\sqrt{2\eta_{1}|G|^{2}+18\delta}.

In the above, we used the fact
1-\eta_{1}\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|C(|\hat{k}\rangle\langle\hat{l}|)|l\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle l|A_{i}|\hat{l}\rangle|^{2}\leq 1
and Lemma
12
again. In summary, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{F}}(C\otimes I(|\phi_{2,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{2,l}|),|\phi_{3,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{3,l}|)]\geq(1-\delta)^{2}(1-\eta_{1})-2\sqrt{2\eta_{1}|G|^{2}+18\delta}=:E_{1}.

Similarly, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{F}}(P\otimes I(|\phi_{4,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{4,l}|),|\phi_{5,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{5,l}|)]\geq(1-\delta)^{2}(1-\eta_{2})-2\sqrt{2\eta_{2}|G|^{2}+18\delta}=:E_{2}.

The remaining part is similar to that of Theorem
10
based on Proposition
1
and Jensen’s inequality. Finally, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\Big[{\mathcal{F}}\Big(\rho_{7,l},|\phi_{7,l}\rangle\langle\phi_{7,l}|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-E_{1}}-\sqrt{1-E_{2}}.

Substituting
\delta=2/(K-1)
and
|G|=2K
into the above lower bound, and simplifying it leads to the claimed result.
∎

From the above theorem, to make the bound small, we should set
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\approx K^{-2}
.

6
Unitary channels

In this section, we consider two other situations that allow us to run the HHL algorithm in the worst case with a good average-case correct QFT. We will mainly focus on unitary channels because of some reasons explained below.

In the first case, we only use
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
with an extra assumption that we have the ability to use
C^{-1}
. Here we need to assume that
C
is an invertible quantum channel whose inverse is also a quantum channel, such as a unitary channel. A complete characterisation of such quantum channels in the general case is given in
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
. In the special case that
C
is a quantum channel from
L(\mathbb{C}^{p})
to
L(\mathbb{C}^{p})
whose inverse is also a quantum channel, then
C
must be a unitary channel. Here
L(\mathbb{C}^{p})
is the space of all linear maps from
\mathbb{C}^{p}
to
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. This is the case for us when studying the HHL algorithm.

In the second case, we assume that we only have
C\in S_{1}(\eta),P\in T_{2}(\eta)
and they are unitary channels. To ensure that the HHL algorithm still works, we will make an extra assumption that the average of the trace of
C\circ P
is close to 1.

6.1
What if we can use
C
and
C^{-1}
for
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
?

As discussed above, in this case,
C
can only be a unitary channel.
So the notation can be simplified. Note that

{\mathcal{F}}(|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle)=|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^{2}

and

{\mathcal{T}}(|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle)=\sqrt{1-|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^{2}}
,
so in terms of the trace distance, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{C\text{ is a unitary}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{T}}(C|\hat{k}\rangle,\,|k\rangle)^{2}]\leq\eta\Big\},

\displaystyle S_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{C\text{ is a unitary}:{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[{\mathcal{T}}(C|k\rangle,\,|\widehat{-k}\rangle)^{2}]\leq\eta\Big\},

\displaystyle S_{3}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{C\text{ is a unitary}:|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\langle k|C|\hat{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq 1-\eta\Big\}.

In the above, we used trace distance because it has the triangle inequality. This simplifies the analysis and more importantly makes the final results better.

As before, we first focus on the ideal case that all the eigenvalues are specified by
n
-bit exactly. Since now
C
is a unitary, there is no need to use the notation of density operators. Namely, we shall write
|\psi_{7,l}\rangle
rather than
\rho_{7,l}
as the output. The middle states will be denoted as
|\psi_{1,l}\rangle,\ldots,|\psi_{6,l}\rangle
. Recall that
|\phi_{1,l}\rangle,\ldots,|\phi_{7,l}\rangle
are the states in the HHL algorithm when the QFT and its inverse have no errors.

Theorem 14
.

Using the same notation as Theorem
10
. Assume that
C\in S_{1}(\eta)
, and we can use
C^{-1}
exactly. Furthermore, assume that the eigenvalues of
A
are represented by
n
bits precisely.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(|\phi_{7,l}\rangle,|\psi_{7,l}\rangle)^{2}]\leq 2\sqrt{2\eta}.

(24)

Proof.

We assume that
C|\hat{k}\rangle=\sum_{l}C_{k,l}|l\rangle
. In the HHL algorithm, if we replace
F_{N}^{-1}
with
C
and
F_{N}
with
C^{-1}
, then at step 5, we obtain

|\psi_{5,l}\rangle=|\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle+|G_{l}\rangle,

where

|\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|C_{p_{k}+l,p_{k}+l}|^{2}\,|p_{k}+l\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right),

and
|G_{l}\rangle
is a garbage state that is orthogonal to
|\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle
. So we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}\rangle|]\geq|{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle]|-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\||G_{l}\rangle\|].

Regarding the first term, we know that

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle]={\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}|C_{p_{k}+l,p_{k}+l}|^{2}\right]\geq 1-\eta.

Regarding the second term, we know that

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\||G_{l}\rangle\|]^{2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[\||G_{l}\rangle\|^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}_{l}[1-\||\psi_{5,l}^{\prime}\rangle\|^{2}]=1-\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|C_{p_{k}+l,p_{k}+l}|^{4}]\leq 1-(1-\eta)^{2}.

Therefore,

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}\rangle|^{2}]\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}\rangle|]^{2}\geq\left(1-\eta-\sqrt{1-(1-\eta)^{2}}\right)^{2}\geq 1-2\sqrt{2\eta}.

The last two steps of the HHL algorithm are assumed to be perfect with no error. So we obtain the claimed result.
∎

The general case can be analysed similarly.

About the second term, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\phi_{5,l,1}|\psi_{5,l,2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}\alpha_{kg}\bar{\alpha}_{kg^{\prime}}C_{g+l,g+l}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,g+l}\langle 0|R(g^{\prime})^{\dagger}R(g)|0\rangle\right]

Finally,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\phi_{5,l,1}|\psi_{5,l,3}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}\sum_{t:t\neq g+l}\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}C_{g+l,t}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,t}\langle 0|R(g^{\prime})^{\dagger}R(t-l)|0\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[d]}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{t:t\neq g+l}|C_{g+l,t}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,t}|\right].

In the above summation, if
g=g^{\prime}
, then

\sum_{g\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}|^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{t:t\neq g+l}|C_{g+l,t}|^{2}\right]=\sum_{g\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}|^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[1-|C_{g+l,g+l}|^{2}]\leq(1-\frac{2}{K-1})\eta.

If
g\neq g^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{t:t\neq g+l}|C_{g+l,t}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,t}|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[|C_{g+l,g^{\prime}+l}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,g^{\prime}+l}|\right]+\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{t:t\neq g+l,g^{\prime}+l}|C_{g+l,t}\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,t}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[|C_{g+l,g^{\prime}+l}|\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{g\neq g^{\prime}\in G_{k}}|\alpha_{kg}\alpha_{kg^{\prime}}|\,{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\left[\sum_{t:t\neq g+l,g^{\prime}+l}|C_{g+l,t}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{g^{\prime}+l,t}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|G_{k}|\sqrt{\eta}+|G_{k}|\eta=2K(\sqrt{\eta}+\eta).

Hence

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l,3}\rangle\leq 2K(\sqrt{\eta}+\eta)+(1-\frac{2}{K-1})\eta.

Combining the above estimations and recalling that
|Err|\leq\sqrt{2/(K-1)}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(|\phi_{7,l}\rangle,|\psi_{7,l}\rangle)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(|\phi_{5,l}\rangle,|\psi_{5,l}\rangle)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[|\langle\phi_{5,l}|\psi_{5,l}\rangle|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-\left((1-\frac{2}{K-1})(1-2\eta)-4K\sqrt{\eta}-2K\eta-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{K-1}}-\frac{2}{K-1}\right)^{2}.

Simplifying this leads to the claimed result.
∎

6.2
What if we only have
S_{1}(\eta)
and
T_{2}(\eta)
?

Usually,
S_{1}(\eta)
and
T_{2}(\eta)
are not powerful enough for us to make sure that the HHL algorithm still works. This can be seen easily by considering unitary channels and
\eta=0
. Now
C=\Phi F_{N}^{-1},P=F_{N}\Psi
for some diagonal unitaries
\Phi,\Psi
. Suppose that all eigenvalues are represented by
n
bits, we have

\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}|\widehat{p_{k}+l}\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle

at the second step of the HHL algorithm (see Subsection
5.1
).
If we apply
C
to the first register, we obtain

\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}e^{i\Phi_{k}}|p_{k}+l\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle.

We then do control rotations and apply
P
to the first register, the resulting state is

\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}e^{i(\Phi_{k}+\Psi_{k})}|p_{k}+l\rangle|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes\left(f(\sigma_{k})|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-f(\sigma_{k})^{2}}|1\rangle\right).

After the last two steps of the HHL algorithm listed in Subsection
5.1
, we finally obtain

\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}e^{i(\Phi_{k}+\Psi_{k})}f(\sigma_{k})|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle\otimes|0\rangle+|0\rangle^{\bot}.

As we can see, this does not include
f(A)|{\bf b}\rangle=\sum_{k\in[d]}\beta_{k}f(\sigma_{k})|{\bf u}_{k}\rangle
exactly because of the unknown phases
e^{i(\Phi_{k}+\Psi_{k})}
.
One natural extra assumption we can make to fix this is

|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\geq 1-\eta
. If this happens, then
\Phi=\Psi^{-1}
in the above situation when
\eta=0
. The following result confirms this for unitary channels.

Lemma 16
.

Assume that
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{2}(\eta_{2})
are unitary channels. If

|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\geq 1-\eta_{3},
then

|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle\hat{k}|P|k\rangle\langle k|C|\hat{k}\rangle]|\geq 1-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}).

Proof.

Assume that
P|k\rangle=\sum_{l}P_{kl}|\hat{l}\rangle
and
C|\hat{k}\rangle=\sum_{l}C_{kl}|l\rangle
. Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]={\mathbb{E}}_{k}[P_{kk}C_{kk}]+{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\sum_{l:l\neq k}P_{kl}C_{lk}\right].

Regarding the second term, since
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{2}(\eta_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\sum_{l:l\neq k}P_{kl}C_{lk}\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\sum_{l:l\neq k}|P_{kl}|^{2}+\sum_{l:l\neq k}|C_{lk}|^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[2-|P_{kk}|^{2}-|C_{kk}|^{2}\right]\leq\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}.

Thus

1-\eta_{3}\leq|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\leq|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[P_{kk}C_{kk}]|+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}.

This leads to the claimed result.
∎

With the above preliminaries, in the general case, the extra assumption we can make is
{\mathbb{E}}_{k,l}\langle k|\,C\circ P(|k\rangle\langle l|)\,|l\rangle\geq 1-\eta_{3}.
Note that this would imply
C\in S_{3}(\eta)
when
P=F_{N}
. For quantum channels, it may be not easy to find a lightweight protocol to verify this assumption.
Because of this, we now assume that
C,P
are unitary channels. In this case, the assumption
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\geq 1-\eta_{3}
made in Lemma
16
is easy to verify. The algorithm reads as follows: We randomly choose
k
, then run
C\circ P
on it and measure in the computational basis. Output 1 if the outcome is
k
, and output 0 otherwise.

Using a similar argument to the proof of Theorems
14
and
15
, and combining Lemma
16
, we obtain the following results.

Theorem 17
.

Using the same notation as Theorem
10
. Assume that
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{2}(\eta_{2})
and
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\geq 1-\eta_{3}
. Further, assume that the eigenvalues of
A
are represented by
n
bits precisely.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(|\phi_{7,l}\rangle,|\psi_{7,l}\rangle)^{2}]\leq 2\sqrt{2(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3})}.

(26)

Theorem 18
.

Using the same notation as Theorem
10
. Assume that
C\in S_{1}(\eta_{1}),P\in T_{2}(\eta_{2})
and
|{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\langle k|C\circ P|k\rangle]|\geq 1-\eta_{3}
.
Then for any integer
K\geq 1
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{l}[{\mathcal{T}}(|\phi_{7,l}\rangle,|\psi_{7,l}\rangle)^{2}]\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{K-1}}+8K\sqrt{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}}.

(27)

7
Conclusions

In this work, we provided another application of the worst-case-to-average-case reduction. Using a strengthened Linden-de Wolf’s protocol, we can also run the HHL algorithm for solving linear equations. As mentioned in
[
10
]
, it would be interesting to explore more applications of this idea. From this research, we feel that many quantum algorithms based on QFT can be implemented using this idea. For example, a similar idea to Linden-de Wolf has been used in
[
17
]
for the task of quantum tomography.
