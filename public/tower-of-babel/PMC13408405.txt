Olverembatinib plus immunotherapy versus olverembatinib plus allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation in adult patients with newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia
Authors: Chenying Li, Hongyan Tong, Huafeng Wang, Yanling Ren, Jiejing Qian, Xinping Zhou, Gaixiang Xu, Liangshun You, Liping Mao, Haitao Meng, Wenjuan Yu, Xingnong Ye, Min Yang, Jian Huang, De Zhou, Wanzhuo Xie, Jie Jin, Yinjun Lou
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Philadelphia chromosome–positive (Ph⁺) acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) accounts for approximately 20–30% of adult B-cell ALL cases [1]. Historically, Ph⁺ ALL has been associated with poor outcomes due to intrinsic chemoresistance. The integration of BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) into frontline induction regimens has dramatically improved complete remission (CR) rates to >95%. Allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) remains the standard consolidation strategy for patients in first CR [2].

However, allo-HSCT carries substantial risks of treatment-related morbidity and non-relapse mortality. Recent trials demonstrate that newer-generation TKIs (e.g., dasatinib, ponatinib) combined with immunotherapies such as blinatumomab or CD19-directed chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CART) therapy—can induce deep, durable molecular remissions in Ph⁺ ALL [3–7]. These approaches represent a paradigm shift toward chemotherapy-free approaches particularly for patients ineligible for or declining allo-HSCT. Nonetheless, comparative evidence on third-generation TKIs combined with immunotherapy versus allo-HSCT remains limited.

Olverembatinib is a new third-generation BCR::ABL1 TKI. It has demonstrated clinical efficacy and favorable safety in patients with chronic myeloid leukemia who are resistant to ponatinib or asciminib, or harbor the T315I mutation [8,9]. Recently, the efficacy and safety of olverembatinib-based regimens were reported in Ph⁺ ALL [10].

Our prior study demonstrated that dasatinib plus prednisone induction is feasible in adult Ph⁺ ALL and can yield durable remissions followed by allo-HSCT [11]. To further improve outcomes, olverembatinib has replaced dasatinib as the frontline TKI in our center since 2022. Additionally, immunotherapy (e.g., blinatumomab or CART) was recommended as an alternative consolidation option for allo-HSCT. In our initial cohort (n = 12), complete molecular response (CMR) was achieved in 6/12 patients (50%) at week 4 and in all 12 patients (100%) by week 8 [12].

Here, to further define the role of olverembatinib and optimize the post-remission strategies in de novo adult Ph⁺ ALL, we conducted a retrospective cohort study comparing two strategies: olverembatinib plus immunotherapy versus olverembatinib plus allo-HSCT.

All consecutive adult patients with newly diagnosed Ph⁺ ALL between January 2022 and November 2025 were screened for eligibility. Clinical and laboratory data were extracted from electronic medical records. BCR::ABL1 fusion transcript detection were performed using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of our institution (No. 2025B-IIT-1110). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patients received induction therapy with olverembatinib (40 mg was administered orally QOD in continuously) combined with steroids only or vindesine plus steroids or venetoclax plus steroids. Upon achievement of CR, allo-HSCT was recommended for eligible cases. Conditioning regimens were selected based on age, comorbidities, and donor type [13]. Alternatively, for patients ineligible for or declining allo-HSCT, immunotherapeutic strategies—including 4–6 cycles of blinatumomab or CD19-directed CART therapy—were offered as consolidation. Central nervous system (CNS) prophylaxis consisted of a minimum of 12 intrathecal injections (methotrexate, cytarabine and dexamethasone).

Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the time of treatment initiation until death. Event-free survival (EFS) was calculated from the time of treatment initiation to the earliest date of relapse or death from any cause. CMR is defined as BCR::ABL1 transcript levels below the limit of detection with a sensitivity of at least 10-4. Relapse was defined by recurrence of ≥5% lymphoblasts in the bone marrow aspirate or by the presence of extramedullary disease after achieving CR. Follow-up information was updated in March 2026. Time-to-event analyses were conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method and tested by log-rank analysis. The Fisher’s exact and Mann–Whitney were used to compare baseline variables. The WBC-based high-risk cutoff was determined using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic analysis [7]. Two-tailed P-values of < 0.05 were taken as being significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using the GraphPad Prism (Version 5.0, San Diego, CA) or R software (version 4.5.0).

A total of 132 consecutive adult patients with newly diagnosed Ph+ ALL who received olverembatinib-based induction were enrolled. The patient selection flowchart is shown in Suppl. Fig. S1. Two patients (1.5%) died of pneumonia-related complications; the remaining 130 patients (98.5%) achieved CR, among these, 45 (34.6%) achieved CMR after the 4-week induction regimen. Fourteen patients who did not proceed to either immunotherapy or allo-HSCT were excluded, yielding a final analytical cohort of 116 patients.

Patients were stratified into two cohorts based on the consolidation strategy actually administered: (1) the olverembatinib–allo-HSCT cohort (n = 73), in which 48 patients (65.8%) received haploidentical donor grafts, 18 (24.7%) received matched sibling donor grafts, and 7 (9.6%) received matched unrelated donor grafts; four patients who received 1–2 cycles of blinatumomab prior to allo-HSCT were included in this cohort; and (2) the olverembatinib–immunotherapy cohort (n = 43), comprising blinatumomab (n = 28, 65.1%) and CD19-directed CAR-T therapy (n = 15, 34.9%). All patients in the blinatumomab subgroup completed at least three cycles.

Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Median age at diagnosis was 46 years (range, 16–78 years); median presenting white blood cell (WBC) count was 36.0 × 10⁹/L (range, 0.7–518 × 10⁹/L). With a median follow-up of 21.4 months (range, 4.0–63.0 months) among surviving patients, the estimated 3-year OS and EFS rates were 83.0% (confidence interval [CI], 70.8–90.5%) and 77.8% (95% CI, 65.2–86.3%), respectively.

Patients in the transplant cohort were significantly younger (median age, 41 vs. 54 years; P < 0.001). The 3-month CMR rate was significantly higher in the immunotherapy cohort than in the transplant cohort (95.3% vs. 78.1%; P = 0.02). No other baseline demographic or clinical characteristics differed significantly between the two cohorts (Table 1).

When comparing the allo-HSCT and immunotherapy-based subgroups, the estimated 3-year EFS was 75.1% (95% CI: 60.3–85.1%) versus 91.4% (95% CI: 76.2–97.1%), respectively (P = 0.530; Fig. 1A). Similarly, the 3-year OS was 81.0% (95% CI: 66.6–89.6%) in the allo-HSCT group and 93.8% (95% CI: 77.3-98.4%) in the immunotherapy group (P = 0.502; Fig. 1B).

We further analyzed patterns of relapse and mortality according to treatment strategy. In the allo-HSCT subgroup (n = 73), isolated CNS relapse occurred in 4 patients (5.5%), and non-relapse mortality accounted for 9 deaths (12.3%). In the immunotherapy subgroup (n = 43), 1 patient died of infection during CMR; 1 developed isolated CNS relapse; and 1 progressed to both CNS and bone marrow relapse harboring the ABL1 Q252H mutation. Notably, the infection event and two relapsed cases were confined to the olverembatinib-blinatumomab cohort, whereas no relapse was observed in the olverembatinib-CART cohort (Suppl. Fig. S2).

We performed univariate analyses to identify baseline factors associated with EFS in the entire cohort, including age, sex, WBC count, hemoglobin level, platelet count, CMR status, and receipt of allo-HSCT or immunotherapy. Only WBC count at diagnosis was significantly associated with EFS (P = 0.001). WBC count > 84 × 10⁹/L was identified as the optimal threshold for stratifying patients into a higher-risk subgroup with inferior EFS (Suppl. Fig. S3).

To the best of our knowledge, this study is among the largest cohort of Ph+ ALL treated by olverembatinib based the frontline therapy. The current analysis demonstrates that patients receiving olverembatinib-immunotherapy achieved a remarkable 3-year EFS of 91.4% and OS of 93.8%. These results mirror ponatinib plus blinatumomab chemotherapy-free trials. In the phase II trial conducted at the MD Anderson Cancer Center, the ponatinib-blinatumomab regimen achieved a 3-year EFS of 78% and OS of 88%, no patients underwent allo-HSCT in the trial [7]. Similarly, the GIMEMA ALL2820 trial reported an 18-month EFS of 89.9%, and OS of 91.6% in patients receiving ponatinib-blinatumomab arm [14]. Thus, our findings reinforce the concept that the third-generation TKIs plus immune-targeted therapy “chemo-free” regimens could be considered as the frontline consolidative in newly diagnosed ph+ ALL. The present data also provide a rationale for incorporating olverembatinib-based regimens into frontline treatment algorithms, particularly where access to ponatinib is limited or toxicity concerns preclude its use.

Notably, this cohort represents one of the few contemporary comparisons between third-generation TKI–based immunotherapy and upfront allo-HSCT in de novo Ph⁺ ALL. No statistically significant difference was observed in 3-year EFS (91.4% vs. 75.1%; P = 0.530) or OS (93.8% vs. 81.0%; P = 0.502) between the two cohorts. However, early CMR rate was higher in the immunotherapy cohort compared to the allo-HSCT cohort. It indicated that patients without early molecular responders have a tendency to transplantation consolidation.

In the entire cohort, isolated CNS relapse occurred in 5 of 6 total relapses. This CNS-predominant relapse pattern recapitulates prior clinical observations and reinforces the critical need for intensified CNS-directed prophylaxis. Notably, no CNS relapse occurred among patients in the olverembatinib-CART cohort (n = 15), suggesting a potential protective role of CART cell therapy against CNS. Furthermore, consistent with the previous model, elevated presenting WBC count emerged as an independent predictor of inferior EFS [7]. The high-risk WBC threshold in our cohort was defined as > 84 × 10⁹/L.

This study has several inherent limitations. As a single-center, retrospective cohort analysis, treatment assignment was non-randomized— introducing potential confounding by indication and selection bias. The modest sample size and median follow-up of 21 months limit statistical power for detecting differences in late relapses or very long-term survival endpoints. Relevant toxicity profile and hospitalization metrics were not uniformly available.

In conclusion, while longer follow-up and prospective validation are warranted, our data support olverembatinib plus immunotherapy (blinatumomab/CART) as a chemotherapy-free frontline strategy—particularly for older adults or those unfit for intensive conditioning in patients with newly diagnosed Ph⁺ ALL.
