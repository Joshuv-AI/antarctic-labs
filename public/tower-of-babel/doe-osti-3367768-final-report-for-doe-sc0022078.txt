Final Report: Studies of key mechanisms and control strategies for
plasmas in micro/sub-micro scales
NS-DOE Partnership Grant DE-SC0022078
PI: John Verboncoeur, Michigan State University, 517-355-5133, johnv@msu.edu

Overview
In this project, we investigate microplasmas in the parameter regime from sub-micron to hundreds
of microns. These plasmas are often driven by thermionic or electric field emission electron sources.
The work includes microgap breakdown mode transition, microplasma scalability (scaling laws), and
control strategies for kinetic microplasmas with engineered surface morphologies. Previously, the
geometric effects, kinetic electrons, as well as the dynamics of the space charge effects in
microplasmas remained elusive, and their effects on the underlying fundamental plasma physics is
not well understood. In micro/sub-microgaps, when cathode electron emission becomes significant,
breakdown mode transitions from thermionic to field emission, and further to the space charge
limited regime, are still unanswered questions. We utilize multi-dimensional models, including
particle-in-cell (PIC), fluid and hybrid models with incorporated emission physics, to bridge the
knowledge gap. We use multi-dimensional models to understand the effects of the electrode
microstructures, space charge shielding, transport, as well as sheath dynamics and collision
processes in bulk microplasmas. Confidence in the models will be supported by rigorous verification
on known analytic solutions and validation using experimental results from the literature. Strategies
for controlling microplasma breakdown, mode transitions, scalability, and stability with engineered
surface morphologies will be explored.
The classical scaling laws for low- and high-density plasmas were both theoretically derived and
experimentally verified in distinct regimes. We explore scaling laws with modifications for
micro/sub-microscale plasmas from low to high density regimes under a broad range of conditions.
We will utilize our kinetic global model and PIC simulations to quantify deviations from the
classical scaling laws and further explore modified similarity relations for specific discharge
parameters, including nonlinear collisional and transport effects. This work will aid development of
universal scaling laws for plasmas from low to high ionization regimes, which is an enabling
capability for parameter estimation and device design in practical applications.

Topic A: Plasma breakdown near a dielectric surface
 A common source of microplasmas is breakdown near dielectric surfaces, including in dielectric barrier
discharges under many types of driving fields, and vacuum windows in the presence of microwaves.

Understanding the breakdown physics is a critical step for researchers to optimize/control the breakdown
process. In this part, we studied plasma breakdown physics near a dielectric surface, driven by an rf electric
field tangential to the dielectric surface. The process of plasma breakdown in this configuration occurs
within a microscale of around 0.2 mm away from the surface at the initial stage of plasma breakdown. The
main tools used are fully kinetic particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations and a theoretical model.

Figure 1. (a) the spatiotemporal normal electric field, Ex, vertical to the applied rf electric field; (b) the Ex
normalized to the applied electric field amplitude Erf0, at the position 𝑥 ≈ 12μm (black line) and the applied
transverse rf electric field waveform Ey/Erf0 ; (c) electron velocity distribution function at 𝑥 ≈ 12μm.
Progress Through 01-Jun-2022: We discovered novel physics that was not reported before in the plasma
breakdown field, i.e., the enhanced higher harmonic (HH) spectrum of the longitudinal physical quantities,
for example the normal electric field, at 10 GHz for a driving frequency of 1 GHz. The background pressure
is fixed at 0.2Torr. The higher harmonics start to appear at around the 6th rf period, when the discharge
transforms from a multipactor-dominant surface discharge to a space rf plasma discharge (see figure 1 (a)
and (b)). Meanwhile, the oscillating amplitude of normal electric field increases in time. The argon ions are
too heavy to response to the fast oscillations, and the HH normal electric field is attributed to the electron
kinetics. As a stream-like velocity distribution function of electron is found in kinetic particle-in-cell
simulations (see figure 1(c)), and we analyzed the origin of the stream-like shape velocity distribution
function of electrons via labeled the electrons based on their production sources, i.e, from the surface via
electron-surface multipacting process, or electron-neutral atom impact ionization event. It is found that the
secondary electrons from the surface inject into the bulk plasma, and the secondary electron shows a stream
behavior, showing a narrow peak in the velocity distribution function that guides us to model it as a cold
stream. Finally, we modelled the stream-plasma interaction system via treating the ionization electron and
secondary electron as fluids, resulting in a dispersion relationship that describes the longitude wave
propagation properties. The slowly varying electron fluid velocity and density is measured based on the
kinetic PIC simulation. The resulting oscillation frequency and growth rate of the fast oscillating field
amplitude in theory are in good agreement with the simulations. The good agreement between the theory
and simulations gives a strong understanding into the nature of the HH phenomenon.

Figure 2. The comparison between PIC simulations and theory for (a) the enhanced HH spectrum; (b) the
amplitude of the oscillating normal electric field.
  This work provides a strong understanding into the transit microscale plasma breakdown physics near a
dielectric surface. The HH generation appears in the early stage of plasma breakdown with electron-surface
multipaction process. Therefore, it may serve as a metric of plasma breakdown and the detection of HH
may provide a potential diagnostic method in experiments to investigate the transient plasma breakdown.
This work was published in Phys. Rev. Lett.
 The parametric studies of HH phenomenon in terms of background gas pressure and driving frequency
were also conducted. It is found that the HH disappears at gas pressure higher than around 0.8Torr, that is
because higher pressure gives higher ionization rate and faster plasma density growth, allowing little time
for the development of stream-plasma instability. The HH phenomenon is present for driving frequency
0.5GHz-2GHz. With increasing the driving frequency to 5 GHz and above, only the fundamental frequency
of the electric field is present as the plasma dynamics at driving frequency play a more important role. This
part of the work was published in Plasma Source Sci. Technol.
Progress through 01-Jun-2023: High power microwave sources are widely used in many fields, such as
space energy transmission, space-based satellite communication, fusion energy etc. The plasma breakdown
near the rf window of a microwave source is a key limiting factor for signal/power transmission.
Understanding the transport physics is a critical step for researchers to optimize/control the breakdown
process. Following up with the studies in 2021-2022, we further investigated the plasma breakdown physics
of higher harmonic (HH) generation published in Phys. Rev. Lett (see previous section) via fully kinetic
particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations and a theoretical model. We show the parametric dependence of HH
phenomenon in terms of background gas pressure and driving frequency as shown in figure 3.

Figure 3. Phase diagram of the presence/absence of high harmonics measured from the PIC simulations at
1 GHz. The blue boxes indicate the region in which HHs are observed, and red crosses represent the absence
of HHs. The dashed black line is the boundary.

 It is found that the HH disappears at gas pressure higher than around 0.8Torr, because higher pressure
gives a higher ionization rate and faster plasma density growth, allowing little time for the development of
a stream-plasma instability. This part of the work is published by Plasma Sources Sci. Technol. (2022) and
also shown in the International Online Plasma Seminar hosted by GEC (invited talk), the International

meeting (oral talk).
Topic B: Improvement of particle-in-cell code for high pressure/microscale argon discharges
  It is very challenging to understand microplasma characteristics via experiments due to the spatial micro-
scale, for example the probe diagnostic unavoidably perturbs a microplasma and optical diagnostics cannot
capture the spatial structure. Therefore, a high-fidelity simulation is very important tool in understanding
the fundamental properties of microplasmas and suggesting the control strategies. The conventional fluid
approach or fluid coupled with electron Monte Carlo collision modelling inevitably makes some
assumptions on the electron and/or ion velocity/energy distribution function, resulting in deviations of the
simulation results from ground truth. The PIC-based model is often the most accurate approach to describe
the charged particles. Meanwhile, in addition to the charged particle species (electron and ion), the excited
neutral atom or molecule can also play a role in the microplasma, which usually operates in high pressure
via multi-step ionization or Penning ionization, as well as surface processes. But the conventional direct
simulation Monte Carlo method for neutrals in PIC is very computationally expensive.
Progress Through 01-Jun-2022: In this part of the work, we upgraded the conventional PIC model to
incorporate a space and time-varying neutral fluid for discharges sustained within two parallel electrodes.
The PIC model is used to track charged particles and their collisions with neutral atoms and molecules, as
well as modeling space charge effects governed by the Poisson equation. The neutrals are described by
space and time-varying fluids considering the transport via diffusion and collision effects with a background
gas. The impact processes involving charged particle and neutrals, such as multi-step excitation and
ionization are also self-consistently considered via energy-dependent differential cross sections. Neutral-
neutral reaction process like Penning ionization and photon radiation processes are also considered. It turns
out those processes involving excited state neutrals are very important in intermediate to high pressure
typical of micro-discharges (above 1Torr). Studies of argon discharges were conducted. Initially, the
discharge pressure and electrode gap are chosen at intermediate pressure with gap around 2.5cm, for which
the simulation results from our new code can be compared to the available literature and even experimental
measurement. As an example, the contributions from different ionization sources are given in figure 4. One
can see that the Penning ionization and multi-step ionization are the most important ionization source over
the contribution of primary electron-ground state atom impact ionization, even though the former are most
frequently neglected in most discharge models. The detailed description regarding the code and strict
benchmarking work against well-established code, as well as the discharge characteristics at varied pressure
are presented in our recent publication in IEEE Trans. Plasma Sci. (DOI: 10.1109/TPS.2022.3174401).
 In order to validate the new PIC/fluid code, we also run this model at low pressure and macro-gap
discharge, where experimental diagnostics are available and recent experimental data are accessible in the
literature. After considering proper electron and neutral-surface processes, our model can give the plasma
density in very good agreement with experimental measurements. This work was presented at the Gaseous
Electronic Conference in Kyoto, Japan.

Figure 4. The ionization rates for an argon discharge driven by a current source 50A/m2, background gas
pressure 15Torr and 2.5cm electrode gap.

Progress Through 01-Jun-2023: A high-fidelity simulation is an indispensable tool in understanding the
fundamental properties of microplasmas and suggesting the control strategies as experimental measurement
is hard to capture the space structure of the microscale, for example the probe diagnostic unavoidably
perturbs a microplasma. In previous studies under the support of this project (2021-2022), we developed
Particle-in-Cell (PIC) simulations treating the neutral plasma species as a time- and space-varying fluid. By
this method, the plasma behavior, especially the important role of the metastable and resonance state
neutrals in the bulk region can be considered. In this year, we investigated the surface process, i.e., the ion,
electron and excited state neutral, as well as the resonance photons-induced secondary electron emission.
It is found that the surface process is essential for PIC simulations to get good agreement with the
experimental measurement as shown in figure 5. For the purpose of comparison with experiments, the
discharge conditions in simulations are set the same as the experiments [Schulenberg et al Plasma Sources
Sci. Technol. 30 105003]. This part of the work is published in Plasma Sources Sci. Technol. 32 (2023)
064001. We also showed this work at the 75th Annual Gaseous Electronics Conference in Kyoto, Japan

[M. C. Stewart, et al. 50th International Conference on Plasma Science, Santa Fe, New Mexico, USA,
May 21-25 (2023)] and [M.C. Stewart, 76th Annual Gaseous Electronics Conf., Ann Arbor, MI (2023)]
focused on convergence concerns in PIC codes, in which the plasma density would slowly grow without
bound and became worse with better particle statistics, quite the opposite of standard PIC numerical

heating theory. This was determined to be due to the PIC underestimate of short-range Coulomb collisons
within grid cells, and manifested as an accumulation of very cold particles which became trapped in low-
field regions. With lower resolution the physical Coulomb effect was replaced by numerical heating
which qualitatively compensated by providing an particle-noise based heating mechanism that acted
qualitatively similarly to the missing Coulomb collisions. It is hypothesized that adding a local sub-cell
Coulomb operator could mitigate the issue. That work is in progress.

Topic C: Global model studies for microscale plasmas
 In addition to the PIC/fluid model, we also conducted global model studies in micro-scale plasmas to
explore the importance of molecular ion Ar2+ and metastable molecule Ar2* in a wide range of pressure,
driving frequency and current for argon discharge sustained within two parallel plates. In the global model,
the ion density is assumed to be homogenous, and electrons oscillate as a uniform electron cloud. The
discharge is driven by a rf current source. The particle balance equation describing the production and loss
of charged particle and neutrals is solved in addition to the energy balance equation. The particle balance
equation considers the loss/production in the bulk plasma via reaction process and reactions on the surface
like the quenching process of excited states etc. The energy balance equation is based on Ohm’s law at high
pressure, where the electron-neutral momentum transfer frequency is much higher than the plasma and
driving frequencies.

Figure 11. (a) the density of electron, e, and ions, Ar+ and Ar2+, varies as a function of pressure; (b) the
neutral species densities of metastable (Ar*), excitation (Arr), and 4p level (Ar4p) state atom and
metastable molecules Ar2* versus pressure. Discharge conditions, driving frequency 0.5 GHz, gap d=0.5
mm, driving current amplitude J=50kA/m2
Progress Through 01-Jun-2022: It is found that the electron and ion Ar+ are dominant charged particles
at pressure of 0.1 atm and below. When the pressure is above 0.2 atm, then the ions Ar2+ is more
important than Ar+. Ar2* molecules becomes more important than excited state atoms when the pressure is
above 0.5 atm. Applying the similarity law and transforming the current discharge into a conventional
discharge with a macrogap of 5cm and pressure 0.76-7.6Torr by keeping the similarity parameter pd (p
the gas pressure and d the gap width) the same, we can see that the role of Ar2* is very different, leading
to a very different discharge at microscale compared to the well-known macro-gap conventional
discharges. We recently presented this conference paper at the 11th International Workshop on
Microplasmas (IWM-11) in North Carolina as an invited talk.
Progress Through 01-Jun-2023: We further investigated the microscale plasma sustained within two
parallel plates via extending the global model to incorporate the sheath multiplication process for the micro-
plasmas driven by a high current source. Based on the global model where the plasma species (charged
particle and neutral species) are assumed to be spatially homogenous, and electrons oscillate as a uniform
electron cloud, the corresponding production-loss particle and deposition-dissipation energy balance
equations are solved, we considered secondary electron emission on the surface and secondary electron
induced ionization impacts within the sheath region. The secondary electrons are treated as a separate
species and have a higher temperature due to the high sheath field acceleration. Based on the model, we
investigated the properties of micro-plasma applied in microwave resonator experiments and an example
of the results are as follows.

Figure 12. (a) the voltage amplitude across the electrode spacing; (b) the bulk electron temperature; (c)
electron and ion (Ar+ and Ar2+) density (d) the neutral species (Arm, Arr, Ar2*) density from the models with
and without sheath multiplication, labeled as “Sheath” and “NoSheath”, respectively. Discharge conditions:
driving frequency 0.5 GHz, gap d=1 mm at atmosphere pressure.

 As shown in figure 12(a), the voltage keeps increasing when the sheath multiplication is absent,
however, the voltage increases first and then decreases in the presence of the sheath model. This is
because the sheath multiplication process enhances the plasma density (see figure 12(c)) and the plasma is
more conductive, leading to a rapidly decreasing plasma impedance and finally a decreased voltage as a
function of driving current density. As the electron multiplication in the sheath plays an important role in
the plasma generation, the electrons in the bulk transfer their momentum to the neutrals thus the bulk
electron temperature decreases (see figure 12(b)) when the sheath model is considered. The dimer state is
dominant over the atomic excited state species in the whole current range if the sheath is excluded.
However, the dominant role of Ar2* is changed by the sheath multiplication, and atomic Arm and Arr are
more important at higher current. This study is presented as conference papers in the 75th Annual
Gaseous Electronics Conference (2022) in Japan and the 50th International Conference on Plasma
Science in New Mexico (2023).

Topic D: Discharge polarity and streamer breakdown criterion of ambient air in a non-uniform
electric field
Progress Through 01-Jun-2023: In this part, we studied the influence of discharge polarity on streamer
breakdown criterion of ambient air considering a non-uniform electric field. A needle-to-plate electrode
configuration is studied using a semi-analytic model and experimental measurements. A high voltage
(either positive or negative) is applied to a hollow needle with 0.51 mm outer diameter and 0.25 mm inner
diameter separated from a planar ground electrode by a gap distance of 0.1–1.4 cm. Breakdown voltages
are recorded for both positive and negative discharge polarities. Empirical relations between the critical
avalanche size for streamer breakdown and the gap distance are proposed. Using these empirical relations,
a semi-analytic model based on Meek’s criterion for streamer breakdown is developed to accurately
predict the measured breakdown voltages. It is found that for pd > 380 Torr cm (or d > 0.5 cm at one
atmosphere) streamer breakdown of ambient air occurs at a lower applied voltage for a positively biased
needle compared to that with a negatively biased needle, referred to as the polarity effect. For pd < 380
Torr cm breakdown is attained at a lower applied voltage with a negatively biased needle compared to
that with a positively biased needle, and the breakdown mode transits from the polarity effect to the so
called inverted polarity effect. This work is published in J. Phys. D: Appl. Phys. 56 (2022) 035204.
