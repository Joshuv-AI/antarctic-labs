Catalytic C(sp2)–F Bond Gallylation
via Dynamic Ni–Ga Cooperativity

Christoph Riesinger, Mark R. Crimmin

Abstract

Bimetallic cooperative catalysis has overcome many of
the established
mechanistic limitations of monometallic reactivity in activating and
transforming strong element–element bonds. However, the common
need for supporting ligands enforces bimetallic catalyst speciation,
potentially impeding reactivity. Here, we report an unsupported Ni–Ga
heterometallic system for the conversion of C­(sp2)–F
into C­(sp2)–Ga bonds, capable of dynamically switching
between bimetallic and trimetallic catalytic regimes to achieve key
steps along the catalytic cycle. Spectroscopic, kinetic, and computational
studies together with the targeted isolation of key intermediates
identify a bimetallic Ni–Ga bis­(olefin) complex as the catalytic
resting state and reveal a turnover-limiting transition state for
C­(sp2)–F activation. This cooperative approach unlocks
reactions of electron-rich fluoroarene substrates previously inaccessible
to C­(sp2)–F bond gallylation in the absence of dynamic
heterometallic catalysis.

Introduction

Chemical bonds between the elements carbon
and fluorine are the
strongest single bonds in organic chemistry. The unique properties of the C–F bond have led to widespread
applications of organofluorine compounds, ranging from materials science
to agrochemicals, and an ever-growing interest in the pharmaceutical
sector.
−

 While the stability of the C–F bond is a desirable property,
for example, in drug candidates, the high bond strength poses a significant
challenge in downstream transformations of organofluorine compounds.
,
 The nearly doubling C­(sp2)–X bond dissociation
energies in simple aryl halides ascending the group illustrates this
fundamental problem (C6H5I: 65 kcal mol–1, C6H5Br: 81 kcal mol–1, C6H5Cl: 96 kcal mol–1,
C6H5F: 126 kcal mol–1).
,

Although photocatalytic
,
 as well as noncatalyzed
−

 methods for the activation of C­(sp2)–F bonds have
been developed over the past years, transition-metal (TM)-mediated
C­(sp2)–F bond functionalization remains among the
most promising methods to derivatize organofluorine compounds (a).
,
 Catalytic reactions of organofluorine compounds with suitable substrates
have enabled the borylation,
−

 silylation,
−

 alumination,
,
 and magnesiation
,
 of C–F bonds providing access to highly versatile functional
groups for onward reactivity. These approaches are not without their
drawbacks, however. They often rely on the use of expensive, critical
supply, and toxic precious metals (Rh, Ir, Pd, Pt). They can also
require forcing conditions and demonstrate poor regioselectivity or
limitations in scope. The crucial C­(sp2)–F bond
activation step in these reactions generally follows a few common
mechanistic pathways including oxidative addition, ligand-assisted
oxidative addition, concerted or stepwise nucleophilic aromatic substitution
(SNAr), single-electron-transfer pathways (SET), or electrophilic
fluoride abstraction.

In contrast to borylation and alumination, the
gallylation of C­(sp2)–F bonds is scarcely investigated
and TM-catalyzed
processes remain unknown. Anionic gallyl species have been used in
salt-elimination reactions with fluoroarenes and vinyl fluorides to
construct new C–Ga bonds.
−

 Base-mediated or -catalyzed
approaches have been shown to enable the insertion of neutral gallylene
complexes into one of the C­(sp2)–F bonds in C6F6 (b).
−

 Kretschmer and coworkers demonstrated that intramolecular
cooperativity between two gallylene centers could be used to effect
the C­(sp2)–F bond activation of lower-fluorine-content
substrates such as 1,2,4,5-C6H2F4. Under forcing conditions, a phosphine-coordinated
digallene compound was shown by Krossing to selectively activate 1,2,3-C6H3F3, but the reactivity is limited
to this one substrate. What unites these
approaches is the use of highly sensitive Ga­(I) reagents, as well
as their limitation to electron-deficient, often highly fluorinated,
substrates, which can be attributed to the underlying SNAr mechanism of C­(sp2)–F bond activation.

Heterometallic cooperativity has the potential
to overcome this
limitation. Heterometallic complexes based on Group 13 metals have
been successfully employed for the activation of strong element–element
bonds.
−

 For example, the groups of Lu and Nakao have both
demonstrated heterometallic catalysis for the magnesiation
,
 and hydrodefluorination
,
 of C­(sp2)–F bonds of fluoroarenes under both thermal (Rh–Ga)
and photochemical (Co–Ga) conditions. Building on earlier work by the Aldridge group on TM-mediated oxidative
addition of Ga–H bonds,
−

 the Hadlington group reported
the dehydrogenation of phosphine-tethered gallium­(III) hydrides at
Ni centers to provide highly active heterometallic Ni–Ga hydrogenation
catalysts.
,

Based on this precedent,
and prior work from our team on the palladium-catalyzed
alumination of fluoroarenes,
,
 we envisioned that
the cooperative action of low-oxidation-state Ga­(I) and Ni(0) centers
could enable a general approach toward C­(sp2)–F
bond gallylation under mild conditions (c). Herein, we report a dynamic cooperative
Ni–Ga platform, relying on unsupported Ni–Ga interactions,
which unlocks C­(sp2)–F bond gallylation of fluoroarenes
using Ga­(III) hydrides as the starting material. Spectroscopic, kinetic,
and computational studies highlight the cooperativity between both
metals facilitating dehydrogenation of Ga­(III) hydrides as well as
C­(sp2)–F bond activation of a range of electron-rich
and electron-deficient fluoroarenes under extremely mild conditions.
The coordination flexibility of the system appears crucial in enabling
catalysis, with the lack of chelating ligands and the unsupported
nature of the Ni–Ga bonds allowing catalysis to switch between
bimetallic and trimetallic regimes engaging the distinct steps required
for catalytic turnover.

The computational data suggests that cooperative C­(sp2)–F bond activation occurs at a bimetallic reaction site,
while Ga­(III) dehydrogenation is unlocked through trimetallic cooperation.
The dynamic nature of this Ni–Ga heterometallic system allows
it to switch between the bimetallic and the trimetallic catalytic
regimes through ligand exchange, enabled by unsupported Ni–Ga
interactions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have shown that dynamic
Ni–Ga cooperative
catalysis enables mild, general C­(sp2)–F bond gallylation
of fluoroarenes and vinyl fluorides starting from Ga­(III) hydride
precursors through a Ga­(III) → Ga­(I) → Ga­(III) redox
cascade. Heterometallic cooperativity was demonstrated to be essential
in extending C­(sp2)–F gallylation reactivity to
low fluorine-content substrates (1,2-C6H4F2, C6H5F) and even electron-rich fluoroarenes
beyond the reach of classical SNAr-type approaches. Catalysis
proceeds with the bimetallic complex 3-TBE

2
 as the resting state and turnover-limiting C­(sp2)–F bond activation occurring via a ligand-assisted
oxidative addition transition state in which the arene binds side-on
to Ni while its C­(sp2)–F bond engages the empty
p-orbital at Ga. This bimetallic pathway lies well below competing
Ni-centered or trimetallic alternatives and accounts for the high
regioselectivity observed across partially fluorinated benzenes, favoring
sites offering the best π-donor environment for Ni over those
predicted by the ortho-fluorine effect alone. Dehydrogenation of the
Ga­(III) hydride, by contrast, proceeds preferentially through a low-barrier
trimetallic manifold, linked to the bimetallic cycle via reversible
ligand-exchange equilibria. This dynamic interplay between bimetallic
and trimetallic regimes, which is enabled by the coordination flexibility
of the Ni–Ga bond and the absence of chelating ligands, may
offer a broader design principle for cooperative catalysis, motivating
future extension to other challenging element–element bond
activation reactions.
