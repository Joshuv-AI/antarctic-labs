Decidability of Livelock Detection for Parameterized Self-Disabling Unidirectional Rings
Aly Farahat

Abstract
We prove that livelock detection is decidable in polynomial time for parameterized symmetric unidirectional rings of self-disabling processes with bounded domain ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} . Given a protocol T T , the algorithm decides whether a livelock exists for some ring size K ≥ 2 K\!\geq\!2 , running in O ⁡ ( | T | 3 ) O(|T|^{3}) time independent of K K . The algorithm computes ℒ ∗ \mathcal{L}^{*} —the greatest fixed point of a deflationary monotone operator on the finite set T T —and returns livelock iff ℒ ∗ ≠ ∅ \mathcal{L}^{*}\!\neq\!\emptyset . The livelock freedom argument rests on maximality: ℒ ∗ \mathcal{L}^{*} is the largest set of transitions that can simultaneously sustain a pseudolivelock at every process; its emptiness certifies freedom for all K K without any search over ring sizes. The work is grounded in the algebraic characterization of livelocks from Farahat ( Farahat 2012 ) , which establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for livelock existence but does not address decidability. We also handle the ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -asymmetric case in which one distinguished process P 0 P_{0} differs from the remaining K − 1 K\!-\!1 identical processes. Code and algebraic foundation are at https://github.com/cosmoparadox/mathematical-tools .

Introduction

Self-stabilizing distributed protocols recover from arbitrary transient
faults by converging to a legitimate state from any initial
configuration
(
Dijkstra 1974
)
. A key correctness property is

livelock freedom
: no infinite execution in which every process
fires indefinitely without stabilizing. For protocols on a unidirectional
ring of
K
processes, the verification question is whether livelock
freedom holds for all
K\!\geq\!2
—the
parameterized
livelock
freedom problem.

Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir
(
Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir 2013
;
Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir 2019
)
study
this problem for self-disabling deterministic protocols, establishing

\Pi^{0}_{1}
-completeness and a correct semi-algorithm for livelock

detection
. Building on the algebraic characterization of
Farahat
(
Farahat 2012
)
, where pseudolivelock and propagation are
structured computable relations on
T
, the present paper shows that
livelock
freedom
is decidable in
O(|T|^{3})
time via the
greatest fixed point
\mathcal{L}^{*}
of a single operator on
T
, independent of
ring size
K
.

Contribution.

We show livelock freedom reduces to checking
\mathcal{L}^{*}=\emptyset
, where

\mathcal{L}^{*}
is the greatest fixed point of a single deflationary operator on

T
. This runs in
O(|T|^{3})
time independent of
K
. It suffices that

one
process has no pseudolivelock to certify freedom for all
ring sizes simultaneously.

Foundation.

This paper builds on Farahat
(
Farahat 2012
)
(Ch. 6), which
characterizes livelocks via the
Propagation Law

H\!\circ\!E=E\!\circ\!H
and the
Circulation Law

H^{|E|}\cap E^{K}\!\neq\!\emptyset
. That work is existential and does
not address decidability. The present paper makes it computationally
effective. Code and the dissertation chapter are at

https://github.com/cosmoparadox/mathematical-tools
.

Setting and Definitions

Ring topology.

A
unidirectional ring
of size
K\!\geq\!2
consists of processes

P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{K-1}
with indices mod
K
. Each process
P_{r}
has a
state variable
x_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
for a fixed integer

m\!\geq\!2
. Process
P_{r}
reads its
predecessor value

x_{r-1}
and its
own value

x_{r}
, and may write a new value to

x_{r}
.

Definition 1

(Transition and protocol)
.

A
transition
is a triple
t=(v,w,w^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
where:

•

v=t.\mathit{pred}
: the predecessor value that enables
t
,

•

w=t.\mathit{own}
: the own value that enables
t
,

•

w^{\prime}=t.\mathit{written}
: the value written to
x_{r}
when
t
fires.

A
protocol
is a finite set
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
of transitions.
A transition
t=(v,w,w^{\prime})\in T
satisfies
w^{\prime}\neq w
(the written value
differs from the own value). It is
self-disabling
if no
transition in
T
has the form
(v,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})
for any
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
;
that is, no transition in
T
shares the same predecessor value
v

and has own value equal to the written value
w^{\prime}
of
t
. Intuitively,
after
t
fires and sets
x_{r}\!\leftarrow\!w^{\prime}
, process
P_{r}
cannot
fire again even if the predecessor value stays
v
, because no
transition is enabled with
(\mathit{pred},\mathit{own})=(v,w^{\prime})
.
Throughout this paper,
all transitions in
T
are assumed
self-disabling
.
A
protocol
is
self-disabling
if every transition in it
is self-disabling.

Definition 2

(Enabling and execution)
.

Transition
t=(v,w,w^{\prime})
is
enabled
at process
P_{r}
in global
state
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{K-1})
iff
x_{r-1}=v
and
x_{r}=w
.
Firing
t
sets
x_{r}\leftarrow w^{\prime}
and leaves all other variables
unchanged. An
execution
is an infinite sequence of
(process, transition) pairs, each pair enabled at the time of firing.
A
livelock
is an execution in which every process fires
infinitely often.

Definition 3

(
(l,q)
-ring topology)
.

A
(l,q)
-ring
of size
K=l+nq
(for integer
n\geq 1
)
is a unidirectional ring in which:

•

Processes
P_{0},\ldots,P_{l-1}
form a
prefix
of
l

distinct process types, each appearing exactly once.

•

Processes
P_{l},\ldots,P_{l+q-1}
form a
period
of
q

distinct process types, repeated
n
times to fill the ring.

A ring is
symmetric
(
l=0
,
q=1
) when all
K
processes run
the same protocol
T
; valid ring sizes are all
K\geq 2
.
A ring is
(1,1)
-asymmetric
(
l=1
,
q=1
) when
P_{0}
runs
protocol
T_{0}
and
P_{1},\ldots,P_{K-1}
all run
T_{\mathit{other}}
;
valid ring sizes are
K=1+n
for any
n\geq 1
.
This paper addresses the symmetric and
(1,1)
-asymmetric cases.
The general
(l,q)
-asymmetric case is left to a companion paper.

Definition 4

(Pseudolivelock graph
H(S)
and operator
\mathrm{PL}
)
.

For a set of transitions
S\subseteq T
, define the directed graph

H(S)
as follows:

•

Vertices
: the transitions in
S
.

•

Edges
: there is a directed edge
t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
in

H(S)
iff
t_{j}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{written}
, i.e., the value written by

t_{i}
equals the own value required to enable
t_{j}
.

The operator
\mathrm{PL}(S)
returns the set of transitions in
S
that lie on
at least one directed cycle in
H(S)
:

\mathrm{PL}(S)\;=\;\bigcup\,\{C\subseteq S:C\text{ is a non-trivial
strongly connected component of }H(S)\}.

Algorithmically,
\mathrm{PL}(S)
is computed by Tarjan’s SCC algorithm on

H(S)
, retaining only SCCs of size
\geq 2
and self-loops (edges

t\to t
). Complexity:
O(|S|^{2})
.

Definition 5

(Shadow of a pseudolivelock —
\mathrm{Shad}
)
.

Let
P\subseteq T
be a set of transitions with pseudolivelock graph

H(P)
. For each directed
H(P)
-edge
t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
(where

t_{j}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{written}
), define the
arc
of that edge as the
pair
(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred})
. Intuitively: when process
P_{r}

fires
t_{i}
and then
t_{j}
in succession, its predecessor value

x_{r-1}
must change from
t_{i}.\mathit{pred}
to
t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
between the
two firings. The transition in
P_{r-1}
responsible for this change
must have
\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{pred}
and
\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
. We call

(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred})
the
shadow
of the edge

t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
.

The
shadow set
of
P
collects all such required
(\mathit{own},\mathit{written})

pairs over every
H(P)
-edge:

\mathrm{Shad}(P)\;=\;\bigl\{\,(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\;t_{j}.\mathit{pred}):t_{i},t_{j}\in P,\;t_{j}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{written}\,\bigr\}.

A predecessor transition
t_{k}\in T_{r-1}
is a
shadow witness

for the edge
t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
iff
(t_{k}.\mathit{own},\,t_{k}.\mathit{written})=(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred})
,
i.e.,
t_{k}
has the form
(\,\cdot\,,\,t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred}\,)

in
T_{r-1}
. The shadow set
\mathrm{Shad}(P)
is exactly the set of

(\mathit{own},\mathit{written})
pairs that must be present among the predecessor
transitions to sustain equivariant backward propagation from
P_{r}
to

P_{r-1}
.

Definition 6

(Filter operator
\mathrm{Filter}
)
.

For a set
S\subseteq T
and a requirement set
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}
:

\mathrm{Filter}(S,\,R)\;=\;\{\,t\in S:(t.\mathit{own},\;t.\mathit{written})\in R\,\}.

\mathrm{Filter}(T_{r-1},\,\mathrm{Shad}(P))
is the
backward propagation step
:
it extracts from
T_{r-1}
every transition of the form

(\,\cdot\,,\,t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred}\,)
for some
H(P)
-edge

t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
. These are precisely the shadow witnesses in
T_{r-1}

that can sustain equivariant propagation backward from
P_{r}
to

P_{r-1}
. Any transition in
T_{r-1}
that is not a shadow witness
for any edge in
P
is removed, since it cannot participate in a
consistent backward propagation.

Remark 1

(Shadow witnesses are exactly the equivariance condition)
.

We explain why the existence of shadow witnesses in
T_{r-1}
is
equivalent to the equivariance condition

H_{r-1}\circ E\;=\;E\circ H_{r}
.

Fix an
H_{r}
-edge
(t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j})
in
L_{r}
and its shadow witness

t_{k}\in L_{r-1}
with
t_{k}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{pred}
and
t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
.
The arc construction sets
E[t_{k}][t_{j}]=1
. Now expand both sides:

Left side
(H_{r-1}\circ E)[t_{k}][t_{j}]=1

requires
\exists\,t_{s}
:
H_{r-1}[t_{k}][t_{s}]=1
and
E[t_{s}][t_{j}]=1
.
The first condition gives
t_{s}.\mathit{own}=t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
; the
second gives
t_{s}.\mathit{own}=t_{i^{\prime}}.\mathit{pred}
and
t_{s}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}

for some
H_{r}
-edge
(t_{i^{\prime}}\!\to\!t_{j})
. These are consistent:
t_{s}

is the
H_{r-1}
-successor of
t_{k}
with
t_{s}.\mathit{own}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
.

Right side
(E\circ H_{r})[t_{k}][t_{j}]=1

requires
\exists\,t_{s}
:
E[t_{k}][t_{s}]=1
and
H_{r}[t_{s}][t_{j}]=1
.
The first gives a
H_{r}
-edge
(t_{i}\!\to\!t_{s})
with
t_{k}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{pred}

and
t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{s}.\mathit{pred}
; the second gives
t_{j}.\mathit{own}=t_{s}.\mathit{written}
.

Why both sides agree:

The shared value is
t_{j}.\mathit{pred}=t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{s}.\mathit{own}
. It links the

H_{r-1}
-successor of
t_{k}
(whose
\mathit{own}
must equal
t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
) to
the
H_{r}
-successor of
t_{i}
(whose
\mathit{pred}
must equal
t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}
). The shadow condition
(t_{k}.\mathit{own},\,t_{k}.\mathit{written})=(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},\,t_{j}.\mathit{pred})
is therefore precisely the statement that the
arc
t_{k}\!\to\!t_{s}
in
H_{r-1}
mirrors the arc
t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j}
in

H_{r}
—which is exactly what
H_{r-1}\circ E=E\circ H_{r}
asserts.

Consequently,
\mathrm{Filter}(T_{r-1},\mathrm{Shad}(P_{r}))
retains precisely those
transitions of
T_{r-1}
that can participate in an equivariant
propagation from
P_{r}
to
P_{r-1}
.

Definition 7

(Fixed-point operator
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
and livelock kernel
\mathcal{L}^{*}
)
.

Design rationale.

Intuitively,
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
removes transitions that cannot participate in any
equivariant backward propagation and retains only those forming maximal
pseudolivelocks. By Remark
1
, one iteration implements a
backward equivariant propagation step: it (i) extracts the maximal
pseudolivelock
\mathrm{PL}(S)
, (ii) computes the shadow
\mathrm{Shad}(\mathrm{PL}(S))

encoding the equivariance requirement at the predecessor interface, and
(iii) filters
S
and re-extracts the maximal pseudolivelock.

Formally, define
\boldsymbol{\Phi}:2^{T}\to 2^{T}
by:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(S)\;=\;\underbrace{\mathrm{PL}\!\bigl(}_{\text{(iii) re-extract SCC}}\underbrace{\mathrm{Filter}(S,\;}_{\text{(ii) prune non-witnesses}}\underbrace{\mathrm{Shad}(\mathrm{PL}(S))}_{\text{(i) required pairs}}\bigr).

Starting from
\mathcal{L}^{(0)}=T
, iterate
\mathcal{L}^{(i+1)}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)})
.
The
livelock kernel
is the limit:

\mathcal{L}^{*}\;=\;\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}\;=\;\nu S.\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}(S),

the greatest fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
. We write
H^{(i)}=H(\mathcal{L}^{(i)})
.

Properties used throughout:

(D)

Deflationary:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(S)\subseteq S
,
because both
\mathrm{Filter}
and
\mathrm{PL}
only remove transitions.

(M)

Monotone:

S^{\prime}\!\subseteq\!S\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{\Phi}(S^{\prime})\!\subseteq\!\boldsymbol{\Phi}(S)
, because smaller
S
yields
smaller
\mathrm{PL}(S)
, hence smaller
\mathrm{Shad}(\mathrm{PL}(S))
, hence fewer
transitions survive the filter.

Definition 8

(Propagation

)
.

Given
\mathcal{L}^{*}
and the graph

, define the

propagation relation

(t_{i}\to t_{j})
in

t_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}

t_{k}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{pred}\quad\text{and}\quad t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}.

Set
E^{*}[t_{k}][t_{j}]=1
for each

such that
t_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
. Each transition

is a
predecessor enabler
: it is the transition that the
predecessor process
P_{r-1}
must fire to make the edge
(t_{i}\to t_{j})

propagate forward to
P_{r}
.

4
Correctness

The proof uses two results from Farahat as black boxes.

Lemma 1

(Farahat’s characterisation
(
Farahat 2012
, Ch. 6)
)
.

A livelock exists for some
K\!\geq\!2
if and only if there exist a
pseudolivelock
H
and propagation
E
on a non-empty support

\mathcal{L}\subseteq T
, satisfying:

(F1)

Every
t\in\mathcal{L}
lies on a directed cycle
in
H(\mathcal{L})
.

(F2)

E
is defined on all of
\mathcal{L}
: every

t_{k}\in\mathcal{L}
is the arc predecessor of some
H
-edge

(t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j})
in
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., participates in at
least one propagation arc. Here,
E
is strengthened by the equivariance condition with the predecessor’s interface.

(F3)

H\circ E=E\circ H
at every process
interface.

We call such
(H,E,\mathcal{L})
a
Farahat witness
.

Lemma 2

(Circulation Law
(
Farahat 2012
, Ch. 6)
)
.

Any Farahat witness yields a concrete ring size
K^{\prime}\geq 2
and a
periodic livelock execution on a ring of size
K^{\prime}
.

The theorem has two directions with different proof strategies.

Soundness
(
\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow
livelock): the algorithm
returns a non-empty fixed point
\mathcal{L}^{*}
on which it constructs a
pseudolivelock
H^{*}
at every process and a propagation
E^{*}

satisfying equivariance; Farahat’s theorem then gives the livelock.
This direction is straightforward: we simply verify (F1)–(F3) for the
objects
H^{*},E^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*}
built by the fixed point.

Completeness
(livelock
\Rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset
): if a
livelock exists, Farahat guarantees a witness
(H,E,\mathcal{L})
; we
show
\mathcal{L}
is a pre-fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, so by maximality of

\mathcal{L}^{*}
(Knaster-Tarski),
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{*}
and
\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset
.
This direction is harder: it requires showing that the equivariance
conditions enforced by
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
are exactly the ones satisfied by every
valid witness, so no witness support can escape the fixed point.

The following invariant is central to both directions.

Invariant 1

(Maximality of
H^{(i)}
)
.

At every iterate
i\geq 0
, every transition in
\mathcal{L}^{(i)}
lies on a
directed cycle in
H^{(i)}=H(\mathcal{L}^{(i)})
.

Proof of Invariant
1
.

By induction on
i
.

Base (
i=0
):

\mathcal{L}^{(1)}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(T)
.
Every element of
\mathcal{L}^{(1)}
is in
\mathrm{PL}(\cdot)
by definition, so lies on
a cycle in
H(\mathcal{L}^{(1)})=H^{(1)}
.

Step:
Assume the invariant at
i
, so
\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)})=\mathcal{L}^{(i)}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{(i+1)}=\mathrm{PL}(\mathrm{Filter}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)},\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L}^{(i)})))
; every element of

\mathcal{L}^{(i+1)}
lies on a cycle in
H^{(i+1)}
by definition of
\mathrm{PL}
. ∎∎

Lemma 3

(Completeness: every Farahat witness support is a pre-fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
)
.

Let
(H,E,\mathcal{L})
be a Farahat witness. Then

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathcal{L})
; hence
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

Proof.

We verify
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L})
and

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{Filter}(\mathcal{L},\mathrm{Shad}(\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L})))
.

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L})
:

By hypothesis (F1) of Lemma
1
,
H
is a pseudolivelock
on
\mathcal{L}
: every
t\in\mathcal{L}
lies on a directed cycle in

H=H(\mathcal{L})
. Hence
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L})
, and
in fact
\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}
.

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{Filter}(\mathcal{L},\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L}))
:

By hypothesis (F2),
E
maps
\mathcal{L}
into
\mathcal{L}
: for each

H
-edge
(t_{i}\!\to\!t_{j})
in
\mathcal{L}
, the arc predecessor

t_{k}\in\mathcal{L}
with
t_{k}.\mathit{own}=t_{i}.\mathit{pred}
and
t_{k}.\mathit{written}=t_{j}.\mathit{pred}

exists. Therefore
(t_{k}.\mathit{own},t_{k}.\mathit{written})=(t_{i}.\mathit{pred},t_{j}.\mathit{pred})\in\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L})
,
so
t_{k}\in\mathrm{Filter}(\mathcal{L},\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L}))
. If
t_{k}

did not participate in any propagation arc, then

would not be
defined at
t_{k}
, contradicting hypothesis (F2) that
(H,E,\mathcal{L})

is a valid Farahat witness. Hence every
t_{k}\in\mathcal{L}
is the arc
predecessor of some
H
-edge, satisfying

(t_{k}.\mathit{own},t_{k}.\mathit{written})\in\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L})
and giving

\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{Filter}(\mathcal{L},\mathrm{Shad}(\mathcal{L}))
.
(The operator
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
enforces this condition by construction, pruning any

5
Semantic Faithfulness of the Livelock Kernel

Theorem
6
established
\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset\iff

livelock via Farahat’s characterisation (Lemma
1
)
as a black box: the proof never reasons about executions directly.
We now provide an independent semantic proof of the same equivalence,
which additionally yields the bounded witness theorem
(Theorem
13
).
The argument proceeds through the execution semantics of
livelocks and establishes that every livelock—regardless of ring
size—reduces to simple cycles of bounded length residing in
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

5.1
Livelock executions and periodic walks

Remark 2

(Periodic structure of livelocks
(
Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir 2019
)
)
.

If a livelock exists on a ring of size
K
, it admits
a periodic execution in which each process repeatedly
traverses a closed walk in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
These walks are
equivariant
: the enabling walk for
each process’s closed walk is the closed walk of its
predecessor.

5.2
Completeness: livelock implies

\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset

We show that any livelock execution produces a nonempty
fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, and that the witness consists of
simple cycles.

Lemma 7

(Closed walks contain simple cycles)
.

Every closed walk in a directed graph contains a simple cycle.

Proof.

If any transition appears more than once in the walk, the
sub-walk between the two occurrences is a shorter closed walk.
Iterating yields a closed walk with no repeated transitions,
which is a simple cycle.
∎

Lemma 8

(Simple cycles induce equal-length closed walks)
.

Let
C
be a simple cycle of length
N
in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
Every enabling walk for
C
is a closed walk of length
N
.

Proof.

Each of the
N
edges of
C
contributes exactly one shadow
constraint, requiring exactly one witness transition.
The enabling walk therefore has exactly
N
entries.
Closure follows from the cyclicity of
C
:
the shadow of the last edge
(t_{N-1},t_{0})
requires

w_{N-1}.\mathit{written}=t_{0}.\mathit{pred}
,
while the shadow of the first edge
(t_{0},t_{1})
requires

w_{0}.\mathit{own}=t_{0}.\mathit{pred}
.
Hence
w_{N-1}.\mathit{written}=w_{0}.\mathit{own}
,
which is the H-edge condition
w_{N-1}\to w_{0}
,
closing the walk.
∎

Lemma 9

(Enabling walks are simple)
.

Let
C
be a simple cycle of length
N
in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})

at process
P_{r}
in a livelock on a ring of size
K
.
Every enabling walk for
C
is a simple cycle of the
same length
N
.

Proof.

Let
W
be an enabling walk for
C
at
P_{r-1}
.
By Lemma
8
,
W
is a closed walk
of length
N
.
Suppose
W
is not simple.
Then
W
decomposes into simple cycles
S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}

with
|S_{1}|+\cdots+|S_{p}|=N
and each
|S_{i}|<N
.

Each
S_{i}
is a valid simple cycle in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
Since
\mathcal{L}^{*}
is a fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, every simple
cycle in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
admits enabling walks at every
process.
Therefore
S_{i}
propagates independently through

P_{r-2},P_{r-3},\ldots
, producing a closed walk of
length
|S_{i}|
at each successive process.

The number of simple cycles of length
|S_{i}|

in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
is at most
\binom{|\mathcal{L}^{*}|}{|S_{i}|}
.
By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
m\geq 1
such
that after
mK
back-propagations, the image of
S_{i}

returns to
P_{r}
as a closed walk of length
|S_{i}|

whose transitions are drawn from
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

In the livelock execution,
P_{r}
executes
C
.
By Remark
2
, the enabling structure
at
P_{r}
is determined by
C
.
The image of
S_{i}
at
P_{r}
is therefore a closed walk
of length
|S_{i}|<N
that must be supported within
C
.

A simple cycle admits no proper closed sub-walk:
any closed sub-walk of length less than
N
would revisit
a transition in fewer than
N
steps,
contradicting the simplicity of
C
.
This is a contradiction.
Therefore
W
is simple.
∎

Lemma 10

(Livelock reduces to simple-cycle circulation)
.

If a livelock exists, then there exists a livelock supported
by a circulation of simple cycles, matched bijectively
(up to cyclic rotation) across processes.
All such cycles are contained in
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Remark
2
, a livelock admits periodic
equivariant closed walks in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
By Lemma
7
, each closed walk contains
a simple cycle.
By Lemma
9
, the enabling walk for
this simple cycle is itself a simple cycle of the same length.
The transitions of these simple cycles form a set closed under
shadow back-propagation and pseudolivelock completion;
by Lemma
3
, this set is a pre-fixed point
of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, hence contained in
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.
∎

5.3
Soundness:

\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset

implies livelock

Lemma 11

(Simple cycles in
\mathcal{L}^{*}
construct a livelock)
.

If
\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists a simple cycle
in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
that supports a livelock on any ring
whose size
K
satisfies the circulation law.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}^{*}=\nu\boldsymbol{\Phi}(T)
is a nonempty fixed point of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
,
every transition in
\mathcal{L}^{*}
lies on a cycle
in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
By Lemma
7
, this cycle contains a simple
cycle
C
.
Assign to each process
P_{r}
the cycle
C
, shifted by the
cyclic rotation determined by the enabling walk.
By Lemma
9
, the enabling walk at each
predecessor is a simple cycle of the same length, hence
a valid execution cycle.
Each process repeatedly fires its transitions in order,
producing a periodic execution that never terminates.
The circulation law ensures that the cyclic rotations are
consistent across all
K
processes.
∎

Main results

Theorem 12

(Semantic faithfulness)
.

For a self-disabling protocol with transition set
T
:

\mathcal{L}^{*}\neq\emptyset\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{a livelock execution exists for some~$K\geq 2$.}

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Lemma
10
.

(\Rightarrow)
Lemma
11
.
∎

Theorem 13

(Bounded witness)
.

If a livelock exists for a self-disabling protocol with
transition set
T
on state space
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then it admits
a witness consisting of a circulation of simple cycles
of length at most
|T|
, independent of the ring size
K
.

Proof.

By Lemma
10
, a livelock is supported by
simple cycles in
H^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{*})
.
A simple cycle visits each transition at most once, so its
length is at most
|\mathcal{L}^{*}|\leq|T|\leq m^{3}
.
The number of ring circulations required for the witness
to close is at most
\binom{|T|}{N}
for a cycle of
length
N
, by the pigeonhole argument in
Lemma
9
.
Therefore the witness is bounded and its size depends only
on
T
, not on
K
.
∎

Corollary 14

(Independent decidability)
.

Livelock existence for self-disabling protocols on
unidirectional rings is decidable.

Proof.

By Theorem
13
, every livelock is witnessed
by simple cycles of length at most
|T|
.
The set of such cycles is finite (at most

\sum_{N=2}^{|T|}\binom{|T|}{N}
candidates).
For each candidate, the enabling walk computation and
circulation law verification are finite procedures.
Livelock freedom holds if and only if no witness exists.
This decision procedure depends only on
|T|
, not on
K
.
∎

Remark 3

(Relationship to the polynomial-time algorithm)
.

Corollary
14
provides a decidability
proof that is independent of the fixed-point algorithm:
it relies solely on the bounded witness structure, not
on the computation of
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.
The naïve procedure implied by the corollary—enumerating
simple cycles and checking the circulation law—has
complexity exponential in
|T|
.
The
O(|T|^{3})
algorithm of the preceding section provides
a vastly more efficient decision procedure, but
Corollary
14
demonstrates that
decidability follows from the structure of the problem
alone, without reference to any particular algorithm.

Remark 4

(Why self-disabling bounds the witness)
.

The self-disabling property is the structural reason that
witness length is bounded.
In a self-disabling protocol, after firing
transition
(v,w,w^{\prime})
, no transition
(v,w^{\prime},{-})

exists in
T
; thus every continuation at the same process
requires a
different
predecessor value.
This forces the predecessor values along any simple cycle
to form a sequence of distinct consecutive pairs (shadows),
of which there are at most
m(m{-}1)
.
Every simple cycle therefore has length at most
m(m{-}1)
,
and the witness space is bounded.
Protocols that lack the self-disabling property can chain
transitions with identical predecessor values, admitting
unbounded local computations and hence unbounded witnesses.

Circulation Law

Remark 5
.

Lemma
2
(Farahat’s Circulation Law) is not an independent
condition: given a Farahat witness
(H^{*},E^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*})
, a valid
K^{\prime}

is computable in finite time because
H^{*}
is an SCC on the finite
set
\mathcal{L}^{*}
(so
\ord(H^{*})
is finite) and
E^{*}
is a binary
relation on the finite set
\mathcal{L}^{*}
, so repeated relational composition
is eventually periodic.

The minimum admissible
K
can be found by searching over pairs

(|E|,K)
with
(H^{*})^{|E|}\cap E^{K}\neq\emptyset
and
K\geq 2
,
using an
O(|\mathcal{L}^{*}|^{2})
matrix intersection check.

The relationship between the Circulation Law and the simple-cycle
structure of
\mathcal{L}^{*}
established in Section 5—specifically, the
bijective propagation of simple cycles across processes
(Lemma
9
)—suggests that valid ring sizes are
determined by the orbit structure of
E^{*}
on the simple cycles
of
H^{*}
.
A full characterisation of admissible
K
in terms of cycle orbits
and cyclic shifts is left to a subsequent paper.

The
(1,1)
-Asymmetric Case

By Definition
3
, the
(1,1)
-asymmetric ring has
P_{0}

running
T_{0}
and
P_{1},\ldots,P_{K-1}
running
T_{\mathit{other}}
,
with valid ring sizes
K=1+n
for any
n\geq 1
.

A single application of Algorithm 1 to
T_{\mathit{other}}
alone is

insufficient
: its fixed point
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathit{other}}
may contain
transitions whose shadow arcs are not supported by
T_{0}
. Declaring
livelock based on
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathit{other}}\neq\emptyset
alone would be
unsound. Similarly, ring closure back to
P_{0}
may shrink
L_{0}
, which
in turn would further constrain
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathit{other}}
; this process
must iterate.

Algorithm 2 performs a
joint fixed-point iteration
over the pair

(L_{0},L_{\mathit{other}})
:

1.

Initialise
L_{0}=\mathrm{PL}(T_{0})
,

L_{\mathit{other}}=\mathrm{PL}(T_{\mathit{other}})
.

2.

Constrain and re-stabilise
L_{\mathit{other}}
:
apply

\mathrm{Filter}(L_{\mathit{other}},\mathrm{Shad}(L_{0}))
to remove transitions
whose shadows
L_{0}
cannot support, then re-run Algorithm 1
warm-started from the result.

3.

Ring closure:

L_{0}^{\prime}=\mathrm{PL}(\mathrm{Filter}(T_{0},\mathrm{Shad}(L_{\mathit{other}})))
.

4.

If either set empties, return
free
.
If
L_{0}^{\prime}=L_{0}
(joint fixed point), return
livelock
.
Otherwise set
L_{0}\leftarrow L_{0}^{\prime}
and repeat.

Termination.

Both
L_{0}
and
L_{\mathit{other}}
are monotone decreasing across
iterations and bounded below by
\emptyset
. By Knaster-Tarski on the
finite joint lattice
(2^{T_{0}}\!\times\!2^{T_{\mathit{other}}},\subseteq)
,
the iteration terminates in at most
|T_{0}|
outer steps.

Complexity.

With warm-starting, the total work for all inner fixed points across
all outer iterations is
O(|T_{\mathit{other}}|^{3})
: each inner step
removes
\geq\!1
transition from
L_{\mathit{other}}
, so the total
number of inner steps is at most
|T_{\mathit{other}}|
, each costing

O(|T_{\mathit{other}}|^{2})
. Ring closures cost

|T_{0}|\times O(|T_{0}|^{2})=O(|T_{0}|^{3})
. Total:

O(|T_{\mathit{other}}|^{3}+|T_{0}|^{3})=O(|T|^{3})
, independent of
K
.

Correctness.

At the joint fixed point
(L_{0}^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathit{other}})
, equivariance
holds at all three ring interfaces:

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\mathit{other}}\circ E_{\mathit{cross}}

\displaystyle=E_{\mathit{cross}}\circ H_{0}^{*}

\displaystyle(P_{K-1}\to P_{0})

\displaystyle H_{0}^{*}\circ E_{0}

\displaystyle=E_{0}\circ H^{*}_{\mathit{other}}

\displaystyle(P_{0}\to P_{1})

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\mathit{other}}\circ E^{*}_{\mathit{other}}

\displaystyle=E^{*}_{\mathit{other}}\circ H^{*}_{\mathit{other}}

\displaystyle(P_{r}\to P_{r+1})

by the same Remark
1
argument applied at each interface.
Lemma
1
then gives a livelock.
Verified computationally for Dijkstra’s token ring at
m\in\{3,4,5\}

(
Farahat 2012
)

Related Work

Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir 2013
;
Klinkhamer and Ebnenasir 2019
establish
\Pi^{0}_{1}

In contrast, the present work establishes the boundedness of the livelock witnesses establishing a baseline for decidability. In addition, we enforce closure of recurrent supports under pseudolivelocks and propagation via a fixed-point construction. This contributes a polynomial decision procedure for livelock
freedom
, grounded in Farahat’s
algebraic framework
(

)

Suzuki 1988
proves undecidability of general temporal property
verification for rings of finite-state machines. Our result does not
contradict this: livelock detection is a specific structural property
admitting the
O(|T|^{3})
procedure of Theorem
6
for the stricter execution semantics of self-disabling processes.

Emerson and Namjoshi 1995
establish cutoff results for correctness properties

dependence on

entirely by computing
\mathcal{L}^{*}
from
T
.
