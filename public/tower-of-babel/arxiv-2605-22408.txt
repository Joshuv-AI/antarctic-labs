On the non-stationary Navier-Stokes type flows and reiterated homogenization
Lazarus Signing

Abstract
. We study the deterministic reiterated homogenization of the non-stationary Navier-Stokes type equations in fixed domains with periodically rapidly varying coefficients. One convergence theorem and a corrector result are proved, and we derive the macroscopic homogenized model.

)), we

that the limit
\mathbf{u}_{0}

solution to (

) belongs to

. Thus, the functions

Before we state for our corrector result, let us prove the following
proposition.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
4
are
satisfied. Then as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}+\nabla_{y}\mathbf{u}_{1}+\nabla_{z}\mathbf{u}_{2}\text{ reiteratively in }L^{2}\left(Q\right)\text{-strong }\Sigma\text{, }

i.e.,

\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial u_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\text{ reiteratively in }L^{2}\left(Q\right)\text{-strong }\Sigma\text{ }\left(1\leq j,k\leq N\right)\text{.}

Proof.

In view of (
3.14
) and Proposition
5
, it remains to prove
that, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

(3.39)

\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}\rightarrow\left\|\frac{\partial u_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)}\qquad\left(1\leq j,k\leq N\right)\text{.}

For this purpose let
\eta>0
. Using the density of
E_{0}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}^{1}
(see Lemma
1
), we fix
\mathbf{\Phi=}\left(\mathbf{\psi}_{0},\mathbf{\psi}_{1},\mathbf{\psi}_{2}\right)
as in (
3.7
) such that

(3.40)

\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{u}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt\leq\frac{\alpha\eta^{2}}{16}\text{,}

where
\alpha
is the constant in (
1.3
). Next, for any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
3.8
).

(i) We begin by proving that there is some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that

(3.41)

\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}\leq\frac{\eta}{2}\qquad\left(1\leq j,k\leq N\right)

for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}
. For this purpose we have

\int_{0}^{T}a^{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbf{f}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt

-2\int_{0}^{T}a^{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt+\int_{0}^{T}a^{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt\text{.}

By proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
4
, we deduce that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\int_{0}^{T}a^{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt\rightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbf{f}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{0}\left(t\right)\right)dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\prime}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{0}\left(t\right)\right)dt

-2\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{u}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt+\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt

=\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{u}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}\left(t\right)\right)dt-2\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{u}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt+\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right),\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt\text{.}

Hence, there is some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}a^{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right)dt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{a}_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{u}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right),\mathbf{u}\left(t\right)-\mathbf{\Phi}\left(t\right)\right)dt+\frac{3\alpha\eta^{2}}{16}

for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}
. But in view of (
1.3
)

3.40
), the preceding inequality leads to

\alpha\left\|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;H_{0}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)^{N}\right)}^{2}\leq\frac{\alpha\eta^{2}}{16}+\frac{3\alpha\eta^{2}}{16}=\frac{\alpha\eta^{2}}{4}\text{,}

for all

. Hence (
3.41
) follows
provided
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}
.

(ii) Now, let
1\leq j,k\leq N
be fixed freely. Thanks to (i), we have

\left|\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}-\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}\right|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}\qquad\left(0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}\right)\text{.}

On the other hand, by (

i.e.,

\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}+\nabla_{y}\mathbf{u}_{1}+\nabla_{z}\mathbf{u}_{2}-\nabla\mathbf{\psi}_{0}-\nabla_{y}\mathbf{\psi}_{1}-\nabla_{z}\mathbf{\psi}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)^{N^{2}}}\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\text{.}

This implies

\left|\left\|\frac{\partial u_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)}-\left\|\frac{\partial\psi_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)}\right|\leq\frac{\eta}{4}\text{.}

Moreover,

\frac{\partial\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial\psi_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\text{
reiteratively in }L^{2}\left(Q\right)\text{-strong }\Sigma

as

\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}\rightarrow\left\|\frac{\partial\psi_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial\psi_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)}

for all

\left|\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}-\left\|\frac{\partial u_{0}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}^{k}}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}^{k}}{\partial z_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q;L_{per}^{2}\left(Y\times\mathcal{T}\times Z\right)\right)}\right|\leq\left|\left\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}-\left\|\frac{\partial\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(Q\right)}\right|

Conclusion
. As a concluding remark, the time dependent
viscosities of the Navier-Stokes type flows have not been taken in account
in this study, in view of the lack of unicity of solutions to (
1.5
)-(
1.8
) and their estimates. It is not an easy task to prove the
unicity and estimates in
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
, when the
coefficients
a_{ij}
depend on the time variable. However, this
homogenization process remains valid provided that the estimates in
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
and the unicity are established.
