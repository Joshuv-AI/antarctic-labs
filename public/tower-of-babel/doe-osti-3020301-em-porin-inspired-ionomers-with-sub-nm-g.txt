The physiological functions of living systems heavily rely on biological ion channels, whose malfunction
can lead to disease. These channels enable selective and regulated transport of water, ions, or small
molecules across membranes. Analogously, ionomers used in energy conversion and storage technologies
govern ion transport within membrane separators and catalyst binder layers. This DOE Early CAREER
project aimed to achieve molecular-level control over ionic conductivity and ion permselectivity by
translating the functionalities of biological, gated ion channels into a novel class of ion-conducting
polymers (ionomers) incorporating macrocyclic calix[4]arene-based repeat units. The overarching goal
was to establish fundamental design principles and elucidate proton conduction mechanisms through
strategic design of macrocyclic calix[4]arene-containing ionomers, with close relevance to energy
conversion and storage devices, including proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs). The project
leveraged sub-nm sized macrocyclic pores to facilitate exceptionally fast ion transport (beneficial to
addressing sluggish ORR kinetics of PEMFC electrodes) and achieve functionalities of ionic diodes under
applied electrochemical fields, beneficial for selective transport/separation.
The objectives of this project were:
Research Objective 1. Exploring proton transport under confinement through the hierarchical synthesis
of macrocyclic calix[4]arene-based ionomers mimicking gated ion channels.
Research Objective 2. Investigating the role of ionomer chemical structure and cationic polyelectrolyte
doping on permselectivity of membranes.
Research Objective 3. Exploring molecular level self-assembly and nanostructure evolution with gradual
changes in the chemical structure of gated ionomers to gain control over ion conductivity and ion
permselectivity.

long-range ion conduction pathways.

Impact on Energy Technologies. As the demand for thinner electrochemical devices increases, a deeper
understanding of interfaces and improvement of interfacial performance is critical. The ion conductivity
and gating behavior we explored by designing calix-2 ionomers reveal the inclusion of porin-mimicking
macrocyclic repeat units within ionomer chemical structure as a novel ionomer design principle. Adopting
this principle can potentially guide the design of next-generation of ionomers for catalyst binders and
separators to effectively address the ion transport limitation of the ionomer-catalyst interface of electrodes
(critical for fuel cells and electrolyzers). At the same time, it can enable voltage-gating behavior to control
the direction of ion transport in membranes for devices requiring selective ion transport/separation.
