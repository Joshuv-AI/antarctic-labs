Convergence Rates for ℓ p \ell_{p} Norm Minimization in Convex Vector Optimization
Mohammed Alshahrani

Abstract
We analyze convergence rates of norm-minimization-based outer approximation algorithms for convex vector optimization when the scalarization uses an ℓ p \ell_{p} norm with p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . While the Euclidean case ( p = 2 p=2 ) achieves the optimal rate O ⁡ ( k 2 / ( 1 − q ) ) O(k^{2/(1-q)}) , the behavior under general ℓ p \ell_{p} norms has remained open. A direct approach via the modulus of smoothness yields only the weaker exponent min ⁡ ( p , 2 ) \min(p,2) , which degrades for 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 . We prove that the Hausdorff approximation error satisfies δ H ​ ( P k , A ) = O ⁡ ( k 2 / ( 1 − q ) ) \delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=O(k^{2/(1-q)}) for every p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) , where q q is the number of objectives and k k is the iteration count. The proof introduces a Euclidean intermediary technique that exploits the ambient inner product structure of ℝ q \mathbb{R}^{q} to obtain a quadratic bound on the hyperplane distance, bypassing the ℓ p \ell_{p} smoothness limitation; norm equivalence then converts this to any ℓ p \ell_{p} metric at the cost of only a dimension-dependent constant, not a loss of exponent. Numerical experiments confirm the p p -independent rate predicted by the theory.

1
Introduction

Outer approximation algorithms are a fundamental tool for solving convex vector optimization problems (CVOPs). Given a CVOP with
q
objectives, these algorithms iteratively construct a sequence of polyhedral outer approximations
\{P_{k}\}
to the upper image
\mathcal{P}
, refining the approximation by adding supporting halfspaces at each step. The efficiency of such algorithms depends critically on how quickly the Hausdorff approximation error
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)
decreases as the iteration count
k
grows, where
A
is a compact convex slice of
\mathcal{P}
.

The norm-minimization-based algorithm introduced by Ararat et al.
[
3
]
selects the next cut by solving a closest-point problem: given a vertex
v
of
P_{k}
, find
y^{v}=\arg\min_{y\in A}\|y-v\|
and use the gradient of the norm at the residual
z^{v}=y^{v}-v
as the cut normal. This approach is direction-free and produces cuts naturally adapted to the geometry of the upper image.

The convergence rate of this algorithm depends on the choice of norm
\|\cdot\|
used in the scalarization. For the Euclidean norm (
p=2
), Ararat et al.
[
2
]
proved the rate
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=O(k^{2/(1-q)})
, which matches the optimal rate for polyhedral approximation of smooth convex bodies
[
17
,
16
,
12
]
. The exponent
c=2
in this rate (meaning
\delta_{H}=O(k^{c/(1-q)})
) arises from the quadratic curvature of the Euclidean sphere and is central to the algorithm’s efficiency. In a companion paper
[
1
]
, we extended this result to all inner-product norms
\|\cdot\|_{M}
(where
M
is a symmetric positive definite matrix), showing that
c=2
for every such norm via an isometry argument.

A natural question, raised in
[
1
]
, is whether the exponent
c=2
persists when the scalarization uses a non-inner-product norm. The
\ell_{p}
norms with
p\neq 2
are the most natural candidates to investigate: they form a one-parameter family interpolating between
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{\infty}
, their geometry (moduli of convexity and smoothness) is precisely understood
[
8
,
18
,
4
,
22
]
, and they include both smoother-than-Euclidean (
p>2
) and rougher-than-Euclidean (
1<p<2
) cases.

A direct approach using the modulus of smoothness of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
yields the exponent
c(p)=s(p)=\min(p,2)
, which equals
2
for
p\geq 2
but degrades to
c(p)=p
for
1<p<2
. This suggests that the convergence rate might genuinely depend on
p
, with rougher norms paying a penalty. The main result of this paper shows that this is not the case:

Main result
(Theorem
11
).
For every
p\in(1,\infty)
, the norm-minimization algorithm using
\ell_{p}
scalarization satisfies
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=O(k^{2/(1-q)})
. The convergence rate exponent
c(p)=2
is universal across all
\ell_{p}
norms.

The exponent turns out to be determined not by the smoothness of the scalarization norm, but by the Euclidean curvature of the
deviation surface
—a geometric object built from support points and cut normals. We introduce a
Euclidean intermediary
technique that bounds the hyperplane distance via the squared Euclidean norm of the deviation vector difference, bypassing the
\ell_{p}
smoothness limitation; see Section
1.2
for details.

1.1
Related Work

The outer approximation approach to convex vector optimization originates with the work of Benson
[
5
]
for multi-objective linear programming, extended to the convex case by Ehrgott et al.
[
9
]
and Löhne et al.
[
25
,
23
]
. These methods typically use Pascoletti–Serafini scalarization, which requires a direction parameter. Ararat et al.
[
3
]
introduced the norm-minimization approach, which eliminates direction bias and admits a natural convergence analysis. Further developments include unbounded problems
[
29
]
, local upper bounds
[
10
]
, box-coverage methods
[
11
]
, and efficient parameter selection for Pascoletti–Serafini scalarization
[
21
]
.

The convergence rate framework for polyhedral approximation of convex bodies was developed by Kamenev
[
19
,
20
]
and Lotov et al.
[
26
]
. Their theory is built around the concept of
H(r,A)
-sequences of cutting: if each cut captures at least a fixed fraction
r
of the remaining error, then the approximation error decays as
O(k^{1/(1-q)})
. This general rate, with exponent
c=1
, holds for any norm and relies solely on packing estimates on the boundary of
A
. Improving the exponent to
c=2
requires geometric lemmas that quantify the relationship between hyperplane distance and the separation of support points. In the Euclidean setting, this was achieved by Lotov et al.
[
26
, Chapter 8]
and adapted to the CVOP context by Ararat et al.
[
2
, Section 7]
.

The optimal rate
O(n^{-2/(q-1)})
for approximating a smooth convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
by a polytope with
n
facets (or vertices) is a classical result in convex geometry, established by Gruber
[
15
,
17
,
16
,
14
]
, Glasauer and Gruber
[
12
]
, and Böröczky
[
6
]
; see Bronstein
[
7
]
and Schneider
[
27
, Chapter 2]
for surveys. The exponent
2/(q-1)
is universal—it depends only on the dimension and not on the specific norm used to measure the approximation error. Our result shows that the same universality holds in the algorithmic setting of norm-minimization-based CVOP.

The moduli of convexity and smoothness of
\ell_{p}
spaces were characterized by Clarkson
[
8
]
and Hanner
[
18
]
, with sharp constants established by Ball, Carlen, and Lieb
[
4
]
. These moduli govern the “power type” of the norm:
\ell_{p}
has smoothness power type
s(p)=\min(p,2)
and convexity power type
r(p)=\max(p,2)
. The Bregman divergence of
\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
is bounded by
\|x-y\|_{p}^{s(p)}
from above
[
30
,
28
]
, which is the source of the
\min(p,2)
exponent in the direct approach. Our Euclidean intermediary bypasses this limitation.

2.1
Convex Vector Optimization

We consider the convex vector optimization problem

\min\Gamma(x)\ \text{w.r.t. }\leq_{C}\quad\text{s.t. }x\in X,

(P)

where
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a closed, solid, pointed, polyhedral cone,
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a nonempty compact convex set, and
\Gamma:X\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is continuous and
C
-convex. The
upper image
of (
P
) is
P:=\Gamma(X)+C
.

Assumption 1

(Standing hypotheses)
.

Throughout this paper, we assume:

(a)

C\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a closed, solid, pointed, nontrivial, polyhedral cone.

(b)

X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a nonempty compact convex set with
\operatorname{int}X\neq\emptyset
.

(c)

\Gamma:X\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is continuous and
C
-convex.

Under these conditions,
P
is a closed convex set with
\operatorname{rec}(P)=C
. We work with a compact convex
slice

A
of the upper image (obtained by intersecting
P
with a compact slab), so that
A
has nonempty interior and finite diameter. For details on the algorithm and the slicing construction, see
[
3
,
2
]
.

2.2
\ell_{p}
Norms and Duality

The algorithm analyzed in this paper uses the
\ell_{p}
norm as the scalarization metric. We recall the basic definitions.

Definition 1

(
\ell_{p}
norm)
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, the
\ell_{p}
norm is defined as:

\|y\|_{p}:=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}|y_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}&\text{if }p\in[1,\infty),\\
\max_{i=1,\ldots,q}|y_{i}|&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

Definition 2

(Dual norm)
.

The dual norm of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is
\|\cdot\|_{p^{*}}
where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}=1
.

2.3
\ell_{p}
Geometry

We collect the key geometric properties of
\ell_{p}
norms that underlie the convergence analysis. Throughout, we fix
p\in(1,\infty)
and denote the conjugate exponent by
p^{*}:=p/(p-1)
, so that
1/p+1/p^{*}=1
. We write
S_{p}^{q-1}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:\|y\|_{p}=1\}
for the
\ell_{p}
unit sphere.

Definition 3

(Modulus of convexity)
.

The
modulus of convexity
of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
is

\delta_{p}(\varepsilon):=\inf\left\{1-\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}\right\|_{p}:\|x\|_{p}=\|y\|_{p}=1,\;\|x-y\|_{p}\geq\varepsilon\right\},\quad\varepsilon\in[0,2].

Definition 4

(Modulus of smoothness)
.

The
modulus of smoothness
of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
is

\rho_{p}(\tau):=\sup\left\{\frac{\|x+\tau y\|_{p}+\|x-\tau y\|_{p}}{2}-1:\|x\|_{p}=\|y\|_{p}=1\right\},\quad\tau\geq 0.

Proposition 1

(Sharp
\ell_{p}
moduli
[
18
,
4
]
)
.

For all
q\geq 2
, the
\ell_{p}
norms on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
satisfy the following sharp bounds.

(i)

There exists a constant
S_{p}>0
such that
\rho_{p}(\tau)\leq S_{p}\,\tau^{s(p)}
for all
\tau\geq 0
, where

s(p):=\min(p,2)=\begin{cases}p&\text{if }1<p\leq 2,\\
2&\text{if }p\geq 2.\end{cases}

(1)

Specifically:

•

For
1<p\leq 2
:
\rho_{p}(\tau)\leq\frac{1}{p}\,\tau^{p}

[
18
]
.

•

For
p\geq 2
:
\rho_{p}(\tau)\leq\frac{p-1}{2}\,\tau^{2}

[
4
]
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
K_{p}>0
such that
\delta_{p}(\varepsilon)\geq K_{p}\,\varepsilon^{r(p)}
for all
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
, where

r(p):=\max(p,2)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }1<p\leq 2,\\
p&\text{if }p\geq 2.\end{cases}

(2)

Specifically:

•

For
1<p\leq 2
:
\delta_{p}(\varepsilon)\geq\frac{p-1}{8}\,\varepsilon^{2}

[
18
,
8
]
.

•

For
p\geq 2
:
\delta_{p}(\varepsilon)\geq\frac{1}{p\cdot 2^{p}}\,\varepsilon^{p}

[
18
]
.

Remark 1

(Duality between smoothness and convexity)
.

The moduli of the dual pair
(\ell_{p},\ell_{p^{*}})
are linked: the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is
s(p)
-uniformly smooth if and only if
\|\cdot\|_{p^{*}}
is
r(p^{*})
-uniformly convex, with
r(p^{*})=s(p)^{*}:=s(p)/(s(p)-1)
. In particular:

•

\ell_{p}
(
p\geq 2
) is
2
-uniformly smooth
\Longleftrightarrow

\ell_{p^{*}}
(
p^{*}\leq 2
) is
2
-uniformly convex.

•

\ell_{p}
(
1<p\leq 2
) is
p
-uniformly smooth
\Longleftrightarrow

\ell_{p^{*}}
(
p^{*}\geq 2
) is
p^{*}
-uniformly convex.

Proposition 2

(
\ell_{p}
subdifferential and gradient)
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\setminus\{0\}
, the
\ell_{p}
norm is differentiable with gradient

\nabla\|z\|_{p}=\frac{1}{\|z\|_{p}^{p-1}}\left(|z_{1}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(z_{1}),\;\ldots,\;|z_{q}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(z_{q})\right).

(3)

Moreover,
\|\nabla\|z\|_{p}\|_{p^{*}}=1
for all
z\neq 0
, so the gradient lies on the dual unit sphere
S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
.

Proof.

Direct computation from the chain rule applied to
\|z\|_{p}=(\sum_{i}|z_{i}|^{p})^{1/p}
. The dual norm identity follows from Hölder’s inequality:

\|\nabla\|z\|_{p}\|_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{\|z\|_{p}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{q}|z_{i}|^{p}=1.\qed

Proposition 3

(Hanner’s inequalities
[
18
]
)
.

For
p\geq 2
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
:

\|x+y\|_{p}^{p}+\|x-y\|_{p}^{p}\geq\bigl(\|x\|_{p}+\|y\|_{p}\bigr)^{p}+\bigl|\|x\|_{p}-\|y\|_{p}\bigr|^{p}.

(4)

For
1<p\leq 2
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
:

\|x+y\|_{p}^{p}+\|x-y\|_{p}^{p}\leq\bigl(\|x\|_{p}+\|y\|_{p}\bigr)^{p}+\bigl|\|x\|_{p}-\|y\|_{p}\bigr|^{p}.

(5)

The reversed inequalities hold for the dual exponent
p^{*}
.

Remark 2

(Role of Hanner’s inequalities)
.

Hanner’s inequalities are the source of the sharp moduli bounds in Proposition
1
and are included here for completeness. A direct application of these moduli to bound the hyperplane distance yields the exponent
c(p)=\min(p,2)
(see Remark
4
). The Euclidean intermediary technique of Lemma
7
bypasses this route, achieving
c(p)=2
for all
p
.

2.4
H
-Sequences and Convergence Rates

We recall the convergence rate framework for polyhedral approximation of compact convex bodies, originally developed by Kamenev
[
19
]
and Lotov et al.
[
26
]
and adapted to the convex vector optimization setting by Ararat et al.
[
2
]
. We follow the notation and presentation of
[
2
, Section 6]
, which itself draws on
[
26
, Chapter 8]
.

Throughout, let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a compact convex set with
\operatorname{int}A\neq\emptyset
. For a direction
w\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
, define the
support function

h_{A}(w):=\max\{\langle w,y\rangle:y\in A\}
, the
supporting halfspace

H(w,A):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:\langle w,y\rangle\leq h_{A}(w)\}
, and the
supporting hyperplane

l(w,A):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:\langle w,y\rangle=h_{A}(w)\}
. For two compact convex sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
, the
Hausdorff distance
(with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
) is

\delta_{H}(A,B;\,\|\cdot\|_{p}):=\max\Bigl\{\max_{a\in A}\min_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_{p},\;\max_{b\in B}\min_{a\in A}\|a-b\|_{p}\Bigr\}.

When the norm is clear from context, we write
\delta_{H}(A,B)
.

We define the
inradius
of
A
(with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
) as

r(A):=\sup\{r>0:\exists\,c\in A\text{ such that }c+r\,\overline{B}_{p}\subseteq A\},

(6)

where
\overline{B}_{p}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:\|y\|_{p}\leq 1\}
is the closed
\ell_{p}
unit ball. The
circumradius

R(A):=\inf\{R>0:A\subseteq c+R\,\overline{B}_{p}\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\}
and the
asphericity

\omega(A):=R(A)/r(A)\geq 1
will also appear in our bounds.

The norm-minimization algorithm of
[
3
]
generates a sequence of outer approximating polytopes
\{P_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
with
P_{k}\supseteq A
for all
k
. At each iteration
k
, a
farthest vertex

v^{k}\in\ext(P_{k})
is selected—the one maximizing
\|z^{v}\|_{p}
over all vertices
v
of
P_{k}
, where
z^{v}:=y^{v}-v
and
y^{v}:=\arg\min_{y\in A}\|y-v\|_{p}
—and the new halfspace
H(w^{k},A)\supseteq A
is used to cut:
P_{k+1}:=P_{k}\cap H(w^{k},A)
, with
w^{k}:=\nabla\|z^{v^{k}}\|_{p}\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
.

The convergence theory rests on the following property of the generated sequence.

Definition 5

(
H(r,A)
-sequence of cutting
[
26
, Definition 8.3]
,
[
2
, Definition 6.2]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a nonempty compact convex set and let
(P_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of polytopes with
P_{0}\supseteq A
and
P_{0}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{I}H(\omega^{i},A)
for some
\omega^{1},\ldots,\omega^{I}\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
. We say that
(P_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is generated by a
cutting method
if, for every
k\geq 0
, it holds
P_{k}\supseteq A
and there exists a supporting halfspace
H_{k}=H(w^{k},A)
of
A
such that
P_{k+1}=P_{k}\cap H_{k}
. In this case,
(P_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is called an
H(r,A)
-sequence of cutting for a given constant
r>0
if, for every
k\geq 0
, it holds

\delta_{H}(P_{k},P_{k+1})\geq r\,\delta_{H}(P_{k},A).

(7)

The condition (
7
) states that each cut captures at least a fixed fraction of the remaining approximation error. This constant-fraction progress property underlies the asymptotic optimality of adaptive cutting schemes
[
26
, Section 5]
.

Proposition 4

(The algorithm generates an
H
-sequence
[
2
, Corollary 6.5]
)
.

Under Assumption
1
, the sequence of outer approximating polytopes
\{P_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
generated by the norm-minimization algorithm of
[
3
]
using
\|\cdot\|_{p}
scalarization (for any
p\in(1,\infty)
) is an
H(1,A)
-sequence of cutting. In particular, for every
k\geq 0
:

\delta_{H}(P_{k},P_{k+1})=\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=\|z^{v^{k}}\|_{p},

(8)

where
v^{k}
is the farthest vertex selected at iteration
k
.

Proof sketch.

The equality
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=\max_{v\in\ext(P_{k})}\|z^{v}\|_{p}
follows from the fact that
A\subseteq P_{k}
and the Hausdorff distance is attained at a vertex (see
[
3
, Lemma 5.3]
). Since the algorithm selects
v^{k}
as the farthest vertex, the cut
H_{k}=H(w^{k},A)
removes precisely the vertex that attains the Hausdorff distance, giving
\delta_{H}(P_{k},P_{k+1})=\|z^{v^{k}}\|_{p}=\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)
. In particular, the
H
-sequence condition (
7
) holds with
r=1
.
∎

We can now state the general convergence results.

Theorem 5

(Convergence of
H
-sequences
[
26
, Theorem 8.5]
,
[
2
, Theorem 6.6]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a nonempty compact convex set and let
(P_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be an
H(r,A)
-sequence of cutting for some
r>0
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=0.

Theorem 6

(General convergence rate
[
26
, Theorem 8.6]
,
[
2
, Theorem 6.9]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a nonempty compact convex set and
r>0
. Let
(P_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be an
H(r,A)
-sequence of cutting. Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
N\geq 0
such that for all
k\geq N
:

\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\lambda(r,A)\,k^{1/(1-q)},

(9)

where
\lambda(r,A)
depends on the topological properties of
A
and can be found in
[
19
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 3

(From exponent
1
to exponent
2
)
.

The general rate (
9
) gives the convergence exponent
c=1
, i.e.,
\delta_{H}=O(k^{1/(1-q)})
. This holds for
any
norm and relies solely on the
H
-sequence property (packing on the boundary of
A
). The improvement to
c=2
, i.e.,
\delta_{H}=O(k^{2/(1-q)})
, requires
geometric estimates
that quantify how the hyperplane distance
d=\langle w^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}\rangle
and the deviation vector separation
\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{p}
are related. In the Euclidean case, this was achieved by Ararat et al.
[
2
, Section 7]
using the quadratic curvature of the Euclidean sphere. In the present paper, we obtain the same improvement for all
\ell_{p}
norms via the Euclidean intermediary technique developed in Section
3
.

3
\ell_{p}
Geometric Lemmas

In this section we develop the geometric lemmas that replace the Euclidean-specific arguments in
[
2
, Section 7]
. The proof structure follows the same template—hyperplane distance bound, separation bound, strict convexity, and packing—but the constants and exponents now depend on the smoothness and convexity parameters of the
\ell_{p}
norm.

We adopt the following setting throughout this section. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a compact convex set with
\operatorname{int}A\neq\emptyset
, let
P_{k}\supseteq A
be the polyhedral outer approximation at iteration
k
, and let
\{z_{k}\}
and
\{w_{k}\}
be the sequences of optimal residuals and cut normals generated by the algorithm using
\|\cdot\|_{p}
scalarization. For a vertex
v
of
P_{k}
, we write
z^{v}:=y^{v}-v
where
y^{v}
is the closest point to
v
in
A
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, and
w^{v}:=\nabla\|z^{v}\|_{p}\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
is the corresponding cut normal. We denote by
\delta^{H}_{k}:=\delta_{H}(P_{k},A;\|\cdot\|_{p})
the Hausdorff approximation error and by
h_{k}:=\max_{v\in\ext(P_{k})}\|z^{v}\|_{p}
the maximal residual norm.

For a fixed parameter
\eta>0
(which will later be taken as the inradius
r(A)
of the target set), we define the
deviation vector
associated with a support point
y\in\operatorname{bd}A
and its normal
w\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
as

\alpha:=y-\eta\,w.

(10)

This construction, following
[
2
, Section 7]
and
[
26
, Chapter 8]
, encodes both the support point and the normal direction into a single vector whose separation properties govern the convergence rate. We also define the norm equivalence constant

N_{2,p}:=\begin{cases}q^{1/2-1/p}&\text{if }p\geq 2,\\
1&\text{if }1<p\leq 2,\end{cases}

(11)

which satisfies
\|x\|_{2}\leq N_{2,p}\,\|x\|_{p}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

Lemma 7

(Hyperplane distance bound)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\eta>0
. Let
y,y^{\prime}\in\operatorname{bd}A
be support points with associated cut normals
w=\nabla\|z\|_{p}
and
w^{\prime}=\nabla\|z^{\prime}\|_{p}
in
S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
, where
z=y-v
,
z^{\prime}=y^{\prime}-v^{\prime}
for vertices
v,v^{\prime}
of
P_{k}
. Let
\alpha=y-\eta w
and
\alpha^{\prime}=y^{\prime}-\eta w^{\prime}
be the corresponding deviation vectors (
10
). Then the hyperplane distance

d:=\langle w^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0

satisfies

d\leq C(p,q)\cdot\frac{\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{p}^{2}}{\eta},

(12)

where

C(p,q):=\frac{N_{2,p}^{2}}{2}=\begin{cases}q^{1-2/p}/2&\text{if }p\geq 2,\\[4.0pt]
1/2&\text{if }1<p\leq 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

We use the Euclidean inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
as an intermediary, bypassing the
\ell_{p}
smoothness limitation.

Step 1 (Euclidean expansion).

Since
\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}=(y-y^{\prime})+\eta(w^{\prime}-w)
, expanding the squared Euclidean norm gives

\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta\langle y-y^{\prime},w^{\prime}-w\rangle+\eta^{2}\|w^{\prime}-w\|_{2}^{2}.

(13)

Step 2 (Support conditions).

Decompose the cross-term:

\langle y-y^{\prime},w^{\prime}-w\rangle=\langle w^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}\rangle+\langle w,y^{\prime}-y\rangle=d+\langle w,y^{\prime}-y\rangle.

By the first-order optimality of the
\ell_{p}
norm-minimization subproblem,
y^{\prime}=\arg\min_{u\in A}\|u-v^{\prime}\|_{p}
implies
\langle w^{\prime},u-y^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0
for all
u\in A
; taking
u=y
gives
d=\langle w^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0
. Similarly,
y=\arg\min_{u\in A}\|u-v\|_{p}
implies
\langle w,y^{\prime}-y\rangle\geq 0
.

Since both terms are non-negative:

\langle y-y^{\prime},w^{\prime}-w\rangle=d+\langle w,y^{\prime}-y\rangle\geq d\geq 0.

(14)

Step 3 (Lower bound).

Substituting (
14
) into (
13
), every term on the right-hand side is non-negative:

\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 2\eta\,d.

Therefore

d\leq\frac{\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}.

(15)

Step 4 (Norm equivalence).

The standard inequality
\|x\|_{2}\leq N_{2,p}\,\|x\|_{p}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
(see (
11
)) gives
\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\leq N_{2,p}^{2}\,\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{p}^{2}
. Combining with (
15
):

d\leq\frac{N_{2,p}^{2}}{2\eta}\,\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{p}^{2}=C(p,q)\,\frac{\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{p}^{2}}{\eta}.\qed

Remark 4

(Comparison with the direct
\ell_{p}
approach)
.

A natural alternative is to bound
d
using the modulus of smoothness of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
directly, which yields
d\leq C_{1}(p)\cdot\|z-z^{\prime}\|_{p}^{s(p)}/\eta^{s(p)-1}
with
s(p)=\min(p,2)
—a weaker bound for
1<p<2
. The Euclidean intermediary argument avoids the
\ell_{p}
smoothness bottleneck entirely: the Pythagorean expansion is always quadratic in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
regardless of
p
, and the conversion to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
via norm equivalence costs only a dimension-dependent constant, not a loss of exponent.

Lemma 8

(Deviation vector separation)
.

Let the hypotheses of Lemma
7
hold. Define the hyperplane distances

d_{ij}:=\langle w_{j},y_{i}-y_{j}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad d_{ji}:=\langle w_{i},y_{j}-y_{i}\rangle\geq 0.

(i)

For any
h>0
with
d_{ij}\geq h
:

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\sqrt{\eta\,h},

(16)

where
C_{3}(p,q):=\sqrt{2}/N_{2,p}
.

(ii)

If
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\leq 0
(Euclidean inner product), then

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq C_{2}(p,q)\cdot\eta,

(17)

where
C_{2}(p,q):=\sqrt{2}\,c_{p,q}/N_{2,p}
with
c_{p,q}:=\min_{\|w\|_{p^{*}}=1}\|w\|_{2}>0
.

In particular,

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq\min\bigl\{C_{2}(p,q)\cdot\eta,\;C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\sqrt{\eta\,h}\bigr\}.

(18)

Proof.

The proof extracts lower bounds from the same Euclidean expansion used in Lemma
7
. Recall from (
13
) and (
14
):

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=\underbrace{\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|_{2}^{2}}_{\geq\,0}+\underbrace{2\eta\bigl(d_{ij}+\langle w_{i},y_{j}-y_{i}\rangle\bigr)}_{\geq\,2\eta\,d_{ij}\,\geq\,0}+\underbrace{\eta^{2}\|w_{i}-w_{j}\|_{2}^{2}}_{\geq\,0}.

(19)

Part (i).

From the middle term of (
19
):
\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 2\eta\,d_{ij}\geq 2\eta h
. By norm equivalence,
\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{2}/N_{2,p}\geq\sqrt{2\eta h}/N_{2,p}
.

Part (ii).

When
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\leq 0
:

\|w_{i}-w_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=\|w_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\|w_{j}\|_{2}^{2}-2\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\geq\|w_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\|w_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 2\,c_{p,q}^{2},

since
\|w\|_{2}\geq c_{p,q}
for all
w\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
. From the last term of (
19
):
\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{2}\geq\eta\|w_{i}-w_{j}\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,\eta\,c_{p,q}
, giving
\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq\sqrt{2}\,\eta\,c_{p,q}/N_{2,p}
.

The combined bound (
18
) follows by taking the minimum.
∎

Lemma 9

(Strict convexity of
\ell_{p}
)
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is strictly convex. Consequently, the norm-minimization scalarization subproblem

\min_{y\in A}\|y-v\|_{p}

has a unique optimal solution
y^{v}
for each
v\notin A
, and the cut normal
w^{v}=\nabla\|z^{v}\|_{p}
is uniquely determined. In particular, each iteration of the algorithm generates a new supporting halfspace, so

|Z_{k}|=J+1+k,

(20)

where
J+1
is the number of initial halfspaces defining
P_{0}
and
Z_{k}
denotes the set of distinct residual vectors up to iteration
k
.

Proof.

For
1<p<\infty
, the
\ell_{p}
norm is strictly convex: if
\|x\|_{p}=\|y\|_{p}=1
and
x\neq y
, then
\|(x+y)/2\|_{p}<1
. This is a consequence of the strict concavity of
t\mapsto t^{1/p}
for
p>1
combined with the triangle inequality, or equivalently follows from
\delta_{p}(\varepsilon)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
(Proposition
1
).

Strict convexity of the norm implies strict convexity of
z\mapsto\|z\|_{p}
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, which ensures uniqueness of the nearest point projection onto any closed convex set. Since the norm is also differentiable for
p\in(1,\infty)
(Proposition
2
), each optimal residual
z^{v}\neq 0
yields a unique cut normal
w^{v}=\nabla\|z^{v}\|_{p}
, and distinct vertices yield distinct cuts. The counting identity (
20
) follows as in
[
2
, Lemma 7.7]
.
∎

Lemma 10

(
\ell_{p}
packing number)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a compact convex set with
\operatorname{int}A\neq\emptyset
, contained in a ball of
\ell_{p}
-radius
R>0
. The maximum number of
\varepsilon
-separated points (in
\|\cdot\|_{p}
) on
\operatorname{bd}A
satisfies

N_{p}(\operatorname{bd}A,\varepsilon)\leq C_{4}(p,q)\cdot\left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)^{q-1},

(21)

where
C_{4}(p,q)>0
depends only on
p
and
q
.

Proof.

This is a standard volumetric packing argument. The exponent
q-1
is
dimensional
and does not depend on
p
—only the constant
C_{4}(p,q)
varies with the norm.

Consider the set of
\varepsilon
-separated points
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\subset\operatorname{bd}A
with
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{p}\geq\varepsilon
for
i\neq j
. The
\ell_{p}
-balls
B_{p}(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
are pairwise disjoint. Since each
x_{i}\in\operatorname{bd}A\subset B_{p}(0,R)
, each ball
B_{p}(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)\subset B_{p}(0,R+\varepsilon/2)
.

The
(q-1)
-dimensional content of
\operatorname{bd}A\cap B_{p}(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
is bounded below by the content of a
(q-1)
-dimensional
\ell_{p}
-ball cap. By convexity of
A
and the fact that
x_{i}\in\operatorname{bd}A
, a supporting hyperplane at
x_{i}
intersects
B_{p}(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
in a
(q-1)
-dimensional
\ell_{p}
-ball of radius
\varepsilon/2
. More precisely, a volumetric comparison gives

N\cdot V_{q-1,p}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{q-1}\leq\operatorname{Vol}_{q-1}\left(\operatorname{bd}(B_{p}(0,R+\varepsilon/2))\right)\leq C^{\prime}(p,q)\cdot R^{q-1},

where
V_{q-1,p}
is the volume of the
(q-1)
-dimensional unit
\ell_{p}
-ball. Rearranging gives

N\leq\frac{C^{\prime}(p,q)}{V_{q-1,p}}\cdot\left(\frac{2R}{\varepsilon}\right)^{q-1}=C_{4}(p,q)\cdot\left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)^{q-1}.

The norm equivalence
\|x\|_{2}/q^{1/2-1/p}\leq\|x\|_{p}\leq q^{1/p-1/2}\|x\|_{2}
ensures that
C_{4}(p,q)
is finite for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, though it may grow as
p\to 1^{+}
or
p\to\infty
due to the increasing asphericity of
\ell_{p}
balls.

The exponent
q-1
is universal across all norms
[
17
,
12
,
15
]
, since the equivalence of norms in finite dimensions implies that packing numbers differ only by a multiplicative constant.
∎

4
Main Results

We now state and prove the main result, which establishes that the convergence rate exponent is
c(p)=2
for every
\ell_{p}
norm with
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 11

(Universal
\ell_{p}
convergence rate)
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
be a compact convex slice of the upper image with
\operatorname{int}A\neq\emptyset
, contained in an
\ell_{p}
-ball of radius
R
. Let
\{P_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of outer approximations generated by the norm-minimization algorithm of
[
3
]
using
\|\cdot\|_{p}
scalarization. Then

\delta_{H}(P_{k},A;\,\|\cdot\|_{p})=O\bigl(k^{2/(1-q)}\bigr)\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

(22)

That is, the convergence rate exponent satisfies

c(p)=2\quad\text{for all }p\in(1,\infty).

(23)

More precisely, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
K\in\mathbb{N}
and a constant
\Lambda(p,q,R,A)>0
such that

\delta_{H}(P_{k},A;\,\|\cdot\|_{p})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\Lambda(p,q,R,A)\,k^{2/(1-q)}\quad\text{for all }k\geq K.

The constant
\Lambda
depends on
p
and
q
through the norm equivalence constant
N_{2,p}
(see (
11
)), but the exponent
2
is universal across all
\ell_{p}
norms.

Proof.

The proof follows the four-step strategy of
[
2
, Theorem 7.2]
, with Lemmas
7
and
8
providing the key estimates via the Euclidean intermediary.

Step 1: Residual counting.

By Lemma
9
, strict convexity of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for
p\in(1,\infty)
guarantees that each iteration produces a new, distinct support point and cut normal. After
k
iterations, the algorithm has generated
k+J+1
distinct support points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k+J+1}\in\operatorname{bd}A
with associated normals
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+J+1}\in S_{p^{*}}^{q-1}
, where
J+1
is the number of halfspaces defining
P_{0}
.

Step 2: Separation of deviation vectors.

Set
\eta:=r(A)>0
, the inradius of
A
(see (
6
)), which is positive since
\operatorname{int}A\neq\emptyset
. Define the deviation vectors
\alpha_{i}:=y_{i}-\eta w_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k+J+1
.

Let
h_{k}:=\delta_{H}(P_{k},A;\|\cdot\|_{p})
be the Hausdorff error at iteration
k
. By Proposition
4
, the algorithm generates an
H(1,A)
-sequence, so
\delta_{H}(P_{k},P_{k+1})=h_{k}=\|z^{v^{k}}\|_{p}
.

We claim that for all distinct indices
i,j\leq k+J+1
, the deviation vectors satisfy the separation bound

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq\min\bigl\{C_{2}(p,q)\cdot\eta,\;C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\sqrt{\eta\,h_{\max(i,j)-1}}\bigr\},

(24)

where
h_{m}:=\delta_{H}(P_{m},A;\|\cdot\|_{p})
. This follows by the same inductive argument as in
[
2
, Lemma 7.9]
, with our Lemma
8
providing the
\ell_{p}
estimates in place of their Euclidean counterparts: for each pair
(i,j)
with
i<j
, either the normals satisfy
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\leq 0
(giving Part (ii) of Lemma
8
), or
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle>0
and the hyperplane distance
d_{ij}=\langle w_{j},y_{i}-y_{j}\rangle\geq h_{j-1}
(giving Part (i)). The latter inequality uses the fact that the farthest vertex
v^{j-1}
satisfies
\langle w_{j},v^{j-1}-y_{j}\rangle\geq h_{j-1}
(since
v^{j-1}\notin H(w_{j},A)
when
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle>0
), combined with
y_{i}\in A
; see
[
2
, pp. 21–22]
for the detailed Euclidean argument, which transfers verbatim to the
\ell_{p}
setting because the Euclidean inner products in the separation conditions are norm-independent.

Since
\{h_{m}\}
is non-increasing and
\max(i,j)-1\leq k
for all indices under consideration, (
24
) implies

\|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\|_{p}\geq\varepsilon_{k}:=\min\bigl\{C_{2}(p,q)\cdot\eta,\;C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\sqrt{\eta\,h_{k}}\bigr\}.

(25)

For
k
large enough that
h_{k}
is sufficiently small, the second term dominates and we obtain
\varepsilon_{k}=C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\sqrt{\eta\,h_{k}}
.

Step 3: Packing bound.

The deviation vectors
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k+J+1}
are
\varepsilon_{k}
-separated in
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and lie in a bounded region (since
\|\alpha_{i}\|_{p}\leq\|y_{i}\|_{p}+\eta\leq R+\eta
). By Lemma
10
:

k+J+1\leq N_{p}(\varepsilon_{k})\leq C_{4}(p,q)\cdot\left(\frac{R+\eta}{\varepsilon_{k}}\right)^{q-1}.

(26)

Step 4: Assembly.

Substituting
\varepsilon_{k}=C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\eta^{1/2}\cdot h_{k}^{1/2}
into (
26
):

k\leq C_{4}(p,q)\left(\frac{R+\eta}{C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\eta^{1/2}\cdot h_{k}^{1/2}}\right)^{q-1}=C_{6}(p,q)\cdot h_{k}^{-(q-1)/2},

where
C_{6}(p,q):=C_{4}(p,q)\cdot\bigl((R+\eta)/(C_{3}(p,q)\cdot\eta^{1/2})\bigr)^{q-1}
absorbs all constants. Solving for
h_{k}
:

h_{k}\leq C_{6}(p,q)^{2/(q-1)}\cdot k^{-2/(q-1)}=\Lambda(p,q,R,A)\cdot k^{2/(1-q)},

which gives the stated rate
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)=O(k^{2/(1-q)})
with
c(p)=2
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.
∎

Remark 5

(Norm-independent exponent)
.

The convergence rate exponent
c(p)=2
is independent of
p
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
: the
\ell_{p}
norm-minimization algorithm matches the optimal Euclidean rate
O(k^{2/(1-q)})
regardless of the choice of
p
. Only the multiplicative constant
\Lambda(p,q,R,A)
varies.

Remark 6

(Role of norm equivalence)
.

While the exponent
c(p)=2
is universal, the constant
\Lambda(p,q,R,A)
depends on
p
and
q
through the norm equivalence constant
N_{2,p}=q^{1/2-1/p}
(for
p\geq 2
). Specifically,
\Lambda
grows as
q^{(q-1)(1-2/p)}
for large
p
, reflecting the increasing asphericity of
\ell_{p}
balls. For the Euclidean case
p=2
, we have
N_{2,2}=1
and
C(2,q)=1/2
, recovering the sharp constant of
[
2
]
.

Remark 7

(The Euclidean intermediary technique)
.

The universal exponent rests on the
Euclidean intermediary
argument in Lemma
7
: instead of working in the
\ell_{p}
metric (which gives the weaker exponent
s(p)=\min(p,2)
from the modulus of smoothness), we expand the squared Euclidean norm
\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}
and exploit the non-negativity of all terms. The Euclidean inner product structure of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
is available regardless of the scalarization norm, and the Pythagorean expansion is always quadratic. The conversion from
\|\cdot\|_{2}
to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
via norm equivalence incurs only a dimension-dependent constant, not a loss of exponent.

Remark 8

(Comparison with earlier approaches)
.

A direct analysis using the modulus of smoothness of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
yields
d\leq C_{1}(p)\cdot\|z-z^{\prime}\|_{p}^{s(p)}/\eta^{s(p)-1}
with
s(p)=\min(p,2)
(see Remark
4
), giving
c(p)=\min(p,2)
—a weaker result for
1<p<2
. An even earlier conjecture predicted
c(p)=\min(2,p,p/(p-1))
, mistakenly attributing a rate-limiting role to the dual norm’s convexity power type. In fact, neither the primal smoothness nor the dual convexity determines the exponent: the rate-limiting quantity is the Euclidean curvature of the deviation surface, which is always quadratic.

5
Algorithm and Implementation

We describe the
\ell_{p}
norm-minimization algorithm and discuss the computational aspects that arise when
p\neq 2
.

5.1
\ell_{p}
Scalarization Subproblem

The algorithm is based on the norm-minimizing scalarization introduced by Ararat et al.
[
3
]
. Given a vertex
v\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
of the current outer approximation
P_{k}
, the subproblem computes the distance from
v
to the compact slice
A=\mathcal{P}\cap S(\gamma)
of the upper image:

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mathcal{X},\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\|z\|_{p}

(P(
v
))

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\Gamma(x)-z-v\leq_{C}0,

\displaystyle\bar{w}^{\top}(v+z)\leq\gamma,

where
\bar{w}\in\operatorname{int}C^{+}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
define the slice
S(\gamma):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:\bar{w}^{\top}y\leq\gamma\}
. See
[
2
, Section 3]
for the construction of
\gamma
and the duality theory.

The optimal solution
(x^{v},z^{v})
of (
P(

v

)
) yields:

•

the
optimal residual

z^{v}
, whose norm
\|z^{v}\|_{p}=d(v,A;\,\|\cdot\|_{p})
is the
\ell_{p}
-distance from
v
to
A
;

•

the
support point

y^{v}:=v+z^{v}\in\operatorname{bd}A
;

•

the
cut normal

\tilde{w}^{v}:=w^{v}-\lambda^{v}\bar{w}
, where
(w^{v},\lambda^{v})
is an optimal dual solution. By Proposition
2
and the strong duality results of
[
2
, Section 3]
, when
z^{v}\neq 0
the cut normal satisfies
\|\tilde{w}^{v}\|_{p^{*}}=1
and is given by

\tilde{w}^{v}_{i}=\frac{|z^{v}_{i}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(z^{v}_{i})}{\|z^{v}\|_{p}^{p-1}},\quad i=1,\ldots,q.

(27)

The key computational distinction from the Euclidean case is the objective function of (
P(

v

)
):

•

For
p=2
:
\|z\|_{2}
is a second-order cone (SOC) objective, and (
P(

v

)
) reduces to a second-order cone program (SOCP) or, after squaring, a quadratic program. Standard QP/SOCP solvers apply directly.

•

For
p\neq 2
:
\|z\|_{p}
is convex but not SOC-representable in general. However, the
\ell_{p}
norm can be represented using a system of second-order and power cone constraints, which modern conic solvers (MOSEK, SCS) handle natively. Disciplined convex programming frameworks such as CVX
[
13
]
generate these representations automatically from the expression
\|z\|_{p}
.

5.2
The Algorithm

Algorithm
1
presents the
\ell_{p}
norm-minimization outer approximation procedure. The structure follows
[
2
, Algorithm 1]
, with
\|\cdot\|_{p}
replacing the Euclidean norm throughout.

\ell_{p}

Input:
CVOP
(\Gamma,\mathcal{X},C)
, norm exponent
p\in(1,\infty)
, tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, slice parameters

.

Initialize:

Compute initial halfspaces by solving weighted-sum problems
\text{WS}(\omega^{i})
for

Remark 9

(Validity of the algorithm for general
\ell_{p}
norms)
.

The foundational results of Ararat et al.
[
2
]
that underpin Algorithm
1
—strong duality of the scalarization
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]
, the supporting halfspace property
[
2
, Proposition 3.6]
, correctness
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
, finiteness
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
, the
H(1,A)
-sequence property
[
2
, Corollary 6.5]
, and the general convergence rate
O(k^{1/(1-q)})

[
2
, Corollary 6.10]
—are all established for an arbitrary norm. In particular, the proofs in
[
2
, Sections 3–6]
rely only on convexity of
\|\cdot\|
, Hölder’s inequality for the dual pair
(\|\cdot\|,\|\cdot\|_{*})
, and volumetric packing of norm balls, none of which is specific to the Euclidean case. Only the improved rate
O(k^{2/(1-q)})
in
[
2
, Section 7]
requires Assumption 7.1 therein (
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{2}
), which our Lemmas
7
–
10
and Theorem
11
replace for all
\ell_{p}
norms with
p\in(1,\infty)
. The additional properties needed for the improved rate—strict convexity (ensuring uniqueness of the projection and cut normal) and differentiability (yielding the gradient formula (
27
))—both hold for
\ell_{p}
norms with
1<p<\infty
.

6
Numerical Experiments

We present numerical experiments that confirm the
p
-independent convergence rate predicted by Theorem
11
. All experiments use Algorithm
1
with the full vertex enumeration strategy.

6.1
Test Problems

We test on three problems with known Pareto fronts, allowing precise evaluation of the Hausdorff approximation error.

The first test problem is Example 1 from
[
2
, Section 8]
:

\min\,\Gamma(x)=x\;\text{ w.r.t.\ }\leq_{\mathbb{R}^{q}_{+}}\quad\text{s.t. }\|x-e\|_{2}\leq 1,

(28)

where
e=(1,\ldots,1)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
. The Pareto front is a portion of the sphere
\|y-e\|_{2}=1
in the positive orthant. For
q=2
, this is a quarter-circle centered at
(1,1)
; for
q=3
, it is a spherical cap. The weighted-sum scalarization admits a closed-form solution
x^{*}=e-w/\|w\|_{2}
. We test with
q\in\{2,3\}
.

The second test problem is a rotated ellipse:

\min\,\Gamma(x)=x\;\text{ w.r.t.\ }\leq_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\quad\text{s.t. }\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}}{10}+\frac{(x_{1}+x_{2}-4)^{2}}{6}\leq 1.

(29)

The feasible set is an ellipse centered at
(2,2)
with semi-axes
\sqrt{10}
and
\sqrt{6}
, rotated
45\textdegree
from the coordinate axes. The non-isotropic curvature of the boundary provides a richer test case than Example 1, where the Pareto front has constant curvature. We test with
q=2
.

The third test problem is Example 2 from
[
2
, Section 8]
:

\min\,\Gamma(x)=\bigl(\|x-a_{1}\|_{2}^{2},\;\|x-a_{2}\|_{2}^{2},\;\|x-a_{3}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\top}\;\text{ w.r.t.\ }\leq_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}

(30)

subject to
x_{1}+2x_{2}\leq 10
,
0\leq x_{1}\leq 10
,
0\leq x_{2}\leq 4
, where
a_{1}=(1,1)^{\top}
,
a_{2}=(2,3)^{\top}
,
a_{3}=(4,2)^{\top}
. Unlike Example 1, the objective is nonlinear (sum of squared distances to three reference points) and the feasible set is a polytope rather than a ball. This provides a test case with
q=3
whose geometry is qualitatively different from Example 1.

6.2
Setup

The algorithm is implemented in MATLAB. The
\ell_{p}
subproblems (
P(

v

)
) are solved using CVX
[
13
]
, which accepts
\|\cdot\|_{p}
as a built-in atom and generates the appropriate conic representation automatically; for
p=2
this reduces to a standard SOCP, while for general
p
, CVX uses a combination of second-order and power cone constraints. Vertex enumeration (
\mathcal{H}
-to-
\mathcal{V}
conversion at line 4 of Algorithm
1
) is performed by the BENSOLVE Tools library
[
24
]
. The cut normal (
27
) is computed directly from the optimal residual
z^{v}
in
O(q)
operations, and the dual norm identity
\|\tilde{w}^{v}\|_{p^{*}}=1
(Proposition
2
) serves as a numerical check. Between iterations, subproblem solutions at non-cut vertices are cached and reused, reducing the per-iteration cost from
O(|V_{k}|)
subproblem solves to
O(|\text{new vertices}|)
.

All experiments were run on a desktop PC with an Intel Core i9-9900K CPU (3.60 GHz, 8 cores) and 32 GB RAM, running Windows 11.

For each test problem, we run Algorithm
1
with
p\in\{1.25,1.5,2,3,4,8\}
, covering both
p<2
(where the direct
\ell_{p}
approach would predict
c(p)=p<2
) and
p>2
(where the dual exponent
p^{*}<2
introduces further complications for naive approaches). The convergence tolerances are
\varepsilon=10^{-4}
for Example 1 with
q=2
,
\varepsilon=0.01
for Example 1 with
q=3
,
\varepsilon=10^{-3}
for the rotated ellipse, and
\varepsilon=0.05
for Example 2.

At each iteration
k
, we record the Hausdorff error
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A;\,\|\cdot\|_{p})
. To extract the empirical convergence rate exponent
\hat{c}(p)
, we fit the model

\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)\approx\Lambda\cdot k^{\hat{c}/(1-q)}

(31)

by linear regression in log-log coordinates (i.e.,
\log\delta_{H}
vs.
\log k
) on the active decay region, excluding the initial transient and the terminal plateau near
\varepsilon
. We apply a monotone envelope (
\delta_{H}(P_{k},A)\leftarrow\min_{j\leq k}\delta_{H}(P_{j},A)
) to smooth non-monotonicity caused by vertex enumeration artifacts.

6.4
Discussion

The experiments support the main theoretical result.

Across all four experimental configurations (Example 1 with
q=2
and
q=3
, the rotated ellipse with
q=2
, and Example 2 with
q=3
) and all six values of
p
, the fitted exponent
\hat{c}(p)
shows no systematic dependence on
p
. Within each configuration, the standard deviation of
\hat{c}
across
p
is at most
0.1
, and the spread
\hat{c}_{\max}-\hat{c}_{\min}
ranges from
0.09
(Example 2) to
0.32
(rotated ellipse). The earlier conjecture
c(p)=\min(p,p/(p-1))
, which predicts
c(2)=2
and
c(p)\to 1
as
p\to 1^{+}
or
p\to\infty
, is contradicted by the flat profile of
\hat{c}
across
p
.

The absolute value of
\hat{c}
depends on the number of objectives
q
. For
q=3
, the fitted exponents overestimate the theoretical
c=2
:
\hat{c}\approx 2.4
for Example 1 and
\hat{c}\approx 2.7
for Example 2. This overestimate is caused by an initial plateau of
3
–
5
iterations where the error barely decreases, which steepens the fitted slope. For
q=2
, the fitted exponents underestimate
c=2
:
\hat{c}\approx 1.55
for Example 1 and
\hat{c}\approx 1.6
for the rotated ellipse. This underestimate arises because the theoretical rate
O(k^{-2})
for
q=2
is faster, so the algorithm terminates in fewer iterations and the log-log fit captures transient behavior rather than the asymptotic slope. In both cases, the Euclidean run (
p=2
) shows the same bias, confirming that the deviation from
c=2
is a finite-sample effect, not a
p
-dependent one.

The convergence
constants
do depend on
p
: in Example 1 with
q=3
, the algorithm converges in
89
iterations for
p=1.25
but only
50
for
p=8
. This is consistent with the theoretical constant
\Lambda(p,q,R,A)
in Theorem
11
, which depends on
p
through
N_{2,p}
and the packing constant. The Euclidean case
p=2
achieves
N_{2,2}=1
and the smallest constants.

7
Conclusion

We have established that the convergence rate exponent of the norm-minimization-based outer approximation algorithm for convex vector optimization is
c(p)=2
for every
\ell_{p}
norm with
p\in(1,\infty)
, matching the optimal Euclidean rate
O(k^{2/(1-q)})
. This result resolves the question of how the choice of scalarization norm affects convergence speed within the
\ell_{p}
family: the exponent is entirely
p
-independent.

The proof relies on the
Euclidean intermediary technique
introduced in Lemma
7
. A direct analysis via the modulus of smoothness of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
yields only
c(p)=\min(p,2)
, which degrades for
1<p<2
. Instead, we expand the squared Euclidean norm of the deviation vector difference and exploit the non-negativity of all resulting terms under the support conditions. Converting to the
\ell_{p}
metric via norm equivalence costs only a dimension-dependent constant, preserving the quadratic exponent.

Together with
[
1
]
, which proves
c=2
for all inner-product norms
\|\cdot\|_{M}
, the present work shows that the rate exponent
2
is robust across two broad families of norms: inner-product norms (where the proof uses isometry invariance) and
\ell_{p}
norms (where the proof uses the Euclidean intermediary). In both cases, the ambient Euclidean structure of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
plays a decisive role, suggesting that the exponent
2
may be a universal feature of the algorithm rather than an artifact of any particular norm geometry.

Several directions remain open:

(i)

Does
c=2
hold for
every
strictly convex, smooth norm on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
? The Euclidean intermediary argument does not require any special property of the scalarization norm beyond strict convexity and differentiability; the
\ell_{p}
structure is used only in the packing estimates and the norm equivalence constant. An extension to arbitrary norms would settle this question.

(ii)

The
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{\infty}
norms lack strict convexity and smoothness, respectively, so the algorithm’s cut uniqueness (Lemma
9
) fails. Whether a modified algorithm can achieve algebraic convergence rates at these endpoints is unclear.

(iii)

While the exponent is
p
-independent, the constant
\Lambda(p,q,R,A)
grows with the norm equivalence factor
N_{2,p}
(Remark
6
). Our numerical experiments confirm that the Euclidean case
p=2
achieves the best constants. Determining the sharp dependence of
\Lambda
on
p
and
q
, and whether the norm equivalence bound is tight, remains open.

(iv)

Since
p
affects only the constants, one could in principle select or adapt the scalarization norm during the algorithm to minimize the approximation constant. How adaptive norm strategies interact with the H-sequence framework is an open question.
