On the volume of the elliptope and related metric polytopes
David Avis and Luc Devroye

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the relationships between the volumes of four convex bodies: the cut polytope, metric polytope, rooted metric polytope, and elliptope, defined on graphs with n n vertices. The cut polytope is contained in each of the other three, which, for optimization purposes, provide polynomial-time relaxations. It is therefore of interest to see how tight these relaxations are. Worst-case ratio bounds are well known, but these are limited to objective functions with non-negative coefficients. Volume ratios, pioneered by Jon Lee with several co-authors, give global bounds and are the subject of this paper. For the rooted metric polytope over the complete graph, we show that its volume is much greater than that of the elliptope. For the metric polytope, for small values of n n , we show that its volume is smaller than that of the elliptope; however, for large values, volume estimates suggest the converse is true. We also give exact formulae for the volume of the cut polytope for some families of sparse graphs.

In this paper, we investigate the relationships between four

The metric polytope, rooted metric polytope and elliptope all contain the cut polytope

The rooted metric polytope contains the metric polytope, but neither is contained in or
contains the elliptope.
The metric polytope and rooted metric polytope are linear relaxations defined by the triangle inequalities, all
of which are facets of the cut polytope. The elliptope provides a
semi-definite relaxation that contains all vertices of the cut polytope
as extreme points. Optimization over these three bodies can be performed
in polynomial time, whereas optimization is NP-hard in general for the cut polytope.
The goal of this paper is to compare how well the three relaxations
approximate the cut polytope in terms of their respective
volumes.

G=K_{2}
we see that
\mathcal{E}(K_{2})
is the
line segment
-1\leq x\leq 1
and hence
\mathcal{I}(K_{2})=\mathrm{Cut}(K_{2})
,
the line segment
0\leq x\leq 1

Therefore
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{I}(K_{2}))=1
. Using the product formula
(

) and following the argument of Proposition
4

we conclude that for any forest
F
,
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{I}(F))=1
.
For the cycle of length 3, using Table

, we observe that

\mathcal{I}(C_{3})=\mathcal{I}(K_{3})\approx 0.617

For a graph

which is a cactus or necklace with
all cycles of length 3, we are therefore able to compute

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{I}(G))
using the product formulae in Propositions

.
At present, we do not have a formula for the volume of the

and hence for the other graphs

Asymptotic volume of the elliptope
\mathcal{E}_{n}

In this section, we let
V_{n}=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{E}_{n})
.

V_{n}
was given as a recursive formula by Joe (2006,
[
16
]
)
follows:

V_{2}=2,V_{n}=V_{n-1}2^{(n-1)^{2}}(B(n/2,n/2))^{n-1},n\geq 3,

where
B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)
,

a,b>0
, is the beta function, and
\Gamma
denotes Euler’s gamma function.

The objective of this note is to derive precise asymptotics for
V_{n}
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 9
.

There exist finite constants
a
and
b
such that

a\leq\log(V_{n})-\log(v_{n})\leq b,

where

\displaystyle\log(v_{n})=

\displaystyle-\frac{n^{2}\log(n)}{4}+n^{2}\left(\frac{5}{8}+\frac{\log(\sqrt{2\pi})}{2}\right)-\frac{3n\log(n)}{4}-n\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\log(\sqrt{2\pi})}{2}\right)-\frac{\log(n)}{24}.

Proof.

Setting
L_{n}=\log(V_{n})
, we see that for
n\geq 3
,

L_{n}=\log(2)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(2)+2j\log(\Gamma((j+1)/2))-j\log(\Gamma(j+1))\right).

From Stirling’s series (see, e.g., Whittaker and Watson, 1963,
[
27
]
, and Alzer, 1997,
[
3
]
), we retain that
for a fixed integer
N\geq 1
,

\log(\Gamma(x))=(x-1/2)\log(x)-x+(1/2)\log(2\pi)+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)x^{2k-1}}+R_{N},

where

R_{N}=\theta\frac{B_{2N}}{2N(2N-1)x^{2N-1}},

\theta\in[0,1]
is an arbitrary constant,
and

B_{2k}
is the
2k
-th Bernoulli number. For our computations, we recall that
B_{0}=1
,
B_{2}=1/6
,
B_{4}=-1/30
, and
B_{6}=1/42
.
For example,

\log(\Gamma(x))=(x-1/2)\log(x)-x+(1/2)\log(2\pi)+\frac{1}{12x}-\frac{1}{360x^{3}}+\frac{\theta}{1260x^{5}}.

Using this representation and
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=\log(2)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(2)+j^{2}\log\left(\frac{j+1}{2}\right)-j(j+1)+j\log(2\pi)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(\frac{j}{3(j+1)}-\frac{2j}{45(j+1)^{3}}+\frac{\theta 16j}{315(j+1)^{5}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(-j(j+1/2)\log(j)+j^{2}-\frac{j}{2}\log(2\pi)-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{360j^{2}}-\frac{\theta}{1260j^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log(2)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j+\frac{j}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac{j}{3(j+1)}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(-\frac{2j}{45(j+1)^{3}}+\frac{\theta 16}{315(j+1)^{4}}-\frac{j}{2}\log(j)-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{360j^{2}}-\frac{\theta}{1260j^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log(2)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j+\frac{j}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac{1}{4}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(-\frac{1}{3(j+1)}-\frac{2}{45(j+1)^{2}}+\frac{2}{45(j+1)^{3}}+\frac{\theta 16}{315(j+1)^{4}}-\frac{j}{2}\log(j)+\frac{1}{360j^{2}}-\frac{\theta}{1260j^{4}}\right).

The last expression is not more than

\displaystyle\log 2+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(-\frac{j}{2}\log(j)+j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j+\frac{j}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{3(j+1)}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{45}(\zeta(2)-5/4))+O(1/n)+\frac{2}{45}(\zeta(3)-9/8)+\frac{16}{315}(\zeta(4)-17/16)+\frac{1}{360}(\zeta(2)-1)

\displaystyle\leq\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}j\log(j)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j)

\displaystyle+\frac{n(n-1)-2}{2}\log(\sqrt{2\pi})+\frac{n-2}{4}-\frac{H_{n}-3/2}{3}-\frac{2(\zeta(2)-5/4))}{45}+O(1/n)

\displaystyle+\frac{2(\zeta(3)-9/8)}{45}+\frac{16(\zeta(4)-17/16)}{315}+\frac{\zeta(2)-1}{360},

where
\zeta(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{s}}
is Riemann’s zeta function.
Similarly,
L_{n}
is at least

\displaystyle\log(2)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j+\frac{j}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{3(j+1)}-\frac{2}{45(j+1)^{2}}+\frac{2}{45(j+1)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(-\frac{j}{2}\log(j)+\frac{1}{360j^{2}}-\frac{1}{1260j^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\log(2)-\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\frac{j}{2}\log(j)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j\right)+\frac{n(n-1)-2}{2}\log(\sqrt{2\pi})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{n-2}{4}-\frac{H_{n}-3/2}{3}-\frac{2(\zeta(2)-5/4)}{45}+\frac{2(\zeta(3)-9/8)}{45}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\zeta(2)-1}{360}-\frac{\zeta(4)-1}{1260}-O(1/n^{2}).

The difference between the upper and lower bounds is a constant plus
O(1/n)
. We are left with the two summation signs in the bounds.
By Taylor’s series with remainder, and still letting
\theta\in(0,1)
be an
arbitrary constant, we note that

\displaystyle j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j=j^{2}\left(\frac{1}{j}-\frac{1}{2j^{2}}+\frac{1}{3j^{3}}\right)-\frac{1}{4(\theta+j)^{4}}=j-1/2+\frac{1}{3j}-\frac{j^{2}}{4(\theta+j)^{4}}.

Therefore,

\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(j^{2}\log(1+1/j)-j)

is not more than

\frac{n(n-1)-2}{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{H_{n-1}-1}{3},

and is at least

\frac{n(n-1)-2}{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{H_{n-1}-1}{3}-\frac{\zeta(2)-1}{4}.

The Barnes G-function

G(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(i)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}i!=\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}i^{n-1-i}

will be helpful. In particular,

\log(G(n+1))=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\log(j)=n\log(\Gamma(n))-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j\log(j).

We recall an asymptotic expansion of the Barnes G-function (
[
21
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
7

),

\log(G(n+1))=\frac{n^{2}}{4}+n\log(\Gamma(n+1))-\frac{n(n+1)\log(n)}{2}-\frac{\log(n)}{12}-\log(A)+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

where the constant
A
is the Glaisher–Kinkelin constant, and can be expressed as

\log(A)=\frac{1}{12}-\zeta^{\prime}(-1)=0.24875447\ldots.

Thus,

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j\log(j)=\frac{n(n+1)\log(n)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n\log(n)+\frac{\log(n)}{12}+\log(A)+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Collecting all this shows that
L_{n}
does not exceed a constant
C
plus
O(1/n)
plus

-\frac{n^{2}\log(n)}{4}+n^{2}\left(\frac{5}{8}+\frac{\log(\sqrt{2\pi})}{2}\right)-\frac{3n\log(n)}{4}-n\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\log(\sqrt{2\pi})}{2}\right)-\frac{\log(n)}{24},

where

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\log 2-\log(A)-\frac{5}{6}+\frac{9}{2}-\log(\sqrt{2\pi})

\displaystyle-\frac{2(\zeta(2)-5/4))}{45}+\frac{2(\zeta(3)-9/8)}{45}+\frac{16(\zeta(4)-17/16)}{315}+\frac{\zeta(2)-1}{360}.

As pointed out above,
L_{n}
is at least equal to the upper bound minus another
constant.
∎

Corollary 10
.

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{I}_{n})}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{RMet}_{n})}\ =\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}\log n}{4}+\Theta(n^{2})\right).

(17)

Proof.

From Theorem
9
, we have

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{I}_{n})=\exp(-n^{2}\log n/4+\Theta(n^{2})).

Using Stirling’s approximation, we have

\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{RMet}_{n+1})=\exp(-n\log n+\Theta(n)),

and thus,
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{RMet}_{n})=\exp(-n\log n+\Theta(n))
.
The result follows after taking ratios.
∎

6
Conclusion and open problems

In this paper, we use volume bounds to
give a measure of how well metric polyhedra approximate
the cut polytope compared to that provided by the elliptope.

Firstly in Section
3
, we derived exact formulae for

\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Cut}(G))
for various sparse graphs.
Since these graphs have no

K_{5}
minor this is also the same as
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Met}(G))
.

In Section
4
, we gave a tight bound
on the volume of the elliptope of the complete graph
K_{n}
.
Exact computation for small
n
showed the metric and
semimetric polytopes gave a much closer approximation
to the cut polytope than the elliptope does. However
volume estimation indicates that this relationship reverses with

n\geq 13
and for large
n
the elliptope appears to give a far
better approximation.

As a continuation of this work we give two open problems.

Open problem 1
.

Find formulae for
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{E}(C_{n}))
, where
C_{n}

is a cycle of length
n\geq 4
.

On the other hand, we have an exact formula and asymptotic bound on
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{E}_{n})

but no analytic bounds on the corresponding cut and metric polytopes.

Open problem 2
.

Prove bounds on
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Met}_{n})
and
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Cut}_{n})
.

Finally, we recall Lee and Skipper
[
20
]
posed the challenge
of finding a polynomial time algorithm for finding
\mathrm{vol}(\text{Cor}(G))

for graphs
G
with no
K_{4}
minor (series-parallel graphs).
Via the covariance, this is equivalent to computing
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Cut}(\nabla G))
.
These are a subset of graphs with no
K_{5}
minor and
so
\mathrm{Cut}(\nabla G)=\mathrm{Met}(\nabla G)
. An even bigger
challenge would be to find an algorithm for computing
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Cut}(G))

for any graph
G
with no
K_{5}
minor or show that it is #P-hard.
