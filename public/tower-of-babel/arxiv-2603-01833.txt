Abstract
. This paper investigates an inverse source problem for a multi-term time-fractional diffusion equation with Caputo derivatives. The source term is separable as f ⁡ ( x ) ​ g ​ ( t ) f(x)g(t) , with the unknown spatial component f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) reconstructed from an overdetermination condition at interior time t 0 ∈ ( 0 , T ] t_{0}\in(0,T] . The elliptic part is governed by a self-adjoint positive differential operator A ⁡ ( x , D ) A(x,D) of order m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . The solution features a spectral representation using the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function, for which we derive novel precise asymptotic expansions. These asymptotics provide a uniform lower bound for the solution’s characteristic denominator, enabling sufficient conditions for the existence of a classical solution. Uniqueness of the reconstructed source holds under natural assumptions on the data and g ⁡ ( t ) g(t) . Despite the problem’s ill-posedness, high-regularity classical solutions are achievable under suitable structural conditions.

Recall that the fractional derivatives
\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}
in the Caputo sense are defined as follows:

\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\rho_{j})}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial u(x,s)}{\partial s}\frac{ds}{(t-s)^{\rho_{j}}},\qquad 0<\rho_{j}<1,\;t>0,

provided that the right-hand side exists. Here
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function.

Most existing studies on inverse source problems for multi-term time-fractional diffusion equations focus on recovering time-dependent source terms and rely on specific spatial operators (classical Laplacian or its fractional powers). In contrast, the present paper investigates the inverse problem of identifying the spatially dependent source component
f(x)
in a separable source
f(x)g(t)
, using a single overdetermination condition at a fixed interior time
t_{0}\in(0,T]
.

We consider the classical solution of the following initial-boundary value inverse problem:

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}u(x,t)+A(x,D)u(x,t)=f(x)g(t),\quad x\in\Omega,\;0<t\leq T.

(1.1)

u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.2)

B_{j}u(x,t)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m_{j}}b_{\alpha,j}(x)D^{\alpha}u(x,t)=0,

(1.3)

0\leq m_{j}\leq m-1,\quad j=1,\dots,l,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\;0<t\leq T.

u(x,t_{0})=\Psi(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.4)

where
t_{0}
is a given fixed point of the segment
(0,T]
.
A(x,D)
is a positive, formally self-adjoint elliptic differential operator of even order
m=2l\geq 2
with smooth coefficients, the orders satisfy
0<\rho_{M}<\cdots<\rho_{1}<1
,
q_{j}>0
,
j=1,\dots.M
(with
q_{1}=1
without loss of generality), and all given functions are sufficiently smooth.

The main contribution is the construction of classical solutions via eigenfunction expansions, combined with a detailed asymptotic analysis of the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function–including precise expansions derived here for the first time. These asymptotics provide uniform lower bounds on the characteristic denominators, enabling rigorous proofs of existence and uniqueness for classical solutions of high regularity, despite the inherent ill-posedness of the inverse problem.

3.
Asymptotic behavior of the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function

In this section, we establish and justify the asymptotic behavior of the following multinomial Mittag–Leffler function:

E_{\rho^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{M}=k}\binom{k}{k_{1},\dots,k_{M}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{M}z_{j}^{k_{j}}}{\Gamma\left(\beta+\rho_{1}k_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{M}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{j})k_{j}\right)}.

(3.1)

\rho^{\prime}=(\rho_{1},\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{1}-\rho_{M}).

Asymptotic estimates for similar multi-index and multivariable Mittag-Leffler functions have been studied by several researchers. Examples include the following authors:

In paper
[
24
]
Virginia S. Kiryakova proved an asymptotic estimate for the multi-index Mittag–Leffler function.
The function is defined by

E_{(1/\rho_{i});(\mu_{i})}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma\!\left(\mu_{1}+\frac{k}{\rho_{1}}\right)\cdots\Gamma\!\left(\mu_{m}+\frac{k}{\rho_{m}}\right)}

(3.2)

where
m\geq 1
is an integer,
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}>0
, and

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m}
are arbitrary real numbers.
The following asymptotic estimate holds for the multi-index Mittag–Leffler function (
3.2
):

\left|E_{(1/\rho_{i});(\mu_{i})}(z)\right|\leq\exp\!\left((\sigma+\varepsilon)\,|z|^{\rho}\right),\qquad|z|\geq r_{0}(\varepsilon),

where

\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\rho_{m}},

and

\sigma=\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho}\right)^{\rho/\rho_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\rho_{m}}{\rho}\right)^{\rho/\rho_{m}}.

Here
r_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
is sufficiently large, that is,
the estimate holds for all
|z|\geq r_{0}(\varepsilon)
.

Furthermore, in
[
25
]
, Christian Lavault established the asymptotic behaviour of the two-variable Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y;\mu)=\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}y^{m}}{\Gamma(n\alpha+m\beta+\mu)},\quad(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha,\beta>0,\ \mu\in\mathbb{C}).

In this paper, we establish the asymptotic behavior of the Mittag-Leffler multinomial
function in form (
3.1
). We note that this behavior allows us to derive a uniform
lower bound for the spectral denominator appearing in the representation of the solution to the inverse problem. To our knowledge, this is the first time such an asymptotic estimate has been established. It should also be emphasized that this asymptotic estimate
is undoubtedly of independent interest.

The following theorem presents the asymptotic behavior for the multinomial Mittag–Leffler function in the form of (
3.1

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\beta>2\rho_{1}
and
1>\rho_{1}>\cdots>\rho_{M}>0
be given.
Assume that
\rho_{1}\pi/2<\mu<\rho_{1}\pi
,

\mu\leq|\arg(z_{1})|\leq\pi
,
|z_{1}|\to\infty

and there exists
K>0
such that
-K\leq z_{j}<0\;(j=2,\ldots,M)
. Then we have the following asymptotic formulas in which
p
is an arbitrary positive integer:

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta-\rho_{1})}-\frac{1}{z_{1}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta-2\rho_{1})}-\sum_{j=2}^{M}\frac{z_{j}}{\Gamma(\beta-\rho_{1}-\rho_{j})}\right)

-\sum_{k=3}^{p}\frac{C_{k}(z_{2},..,z_{M},\rho_{j},\beta)}{z_{1}^{k}}+O\left(|z_{1}|^{-p-1}\right).

Proof.

We have the following integral representation
[
22
]

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R,\theta)}\frac{\exp(s^{1/\rho_{1}})s^{(1-\beta)/\rho_{1}}}{s-z_{1}-\sum_{j=2}^{M}z_{j}s^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}}}\,ds.

(3.3)

where
\rho_{1}\pi/2<\theta<\mu

and
\gamma(R,\theta)
denotes the contour

\gamma(R,\theta):=\left\{s\in{C}\;;\;|s|=R,\;|\arg(s)|\leq\theta\right\}\;\cup\;\left\{s\in{C}\;;\;|s|>R,\;|\arg(s)|=\pm\theta\right\},

and ,

R>K+K\sum_{j=2}^{M}R^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}}.

In
[
22
]
,

is restricted to the interval
(0,2)

[
26
]

\beta>0

Now we introduce the following notation:

Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)=s-\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{M}z_{j}s^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}},

then, we have

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\,\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R,\theta)}\frac{\exp\!\left(s^{1/\rho_{1}}\right)\,s^{\frac{1-\beta}{\rho_{1}}}}{Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1}}\,ds.

For any

\frac{1}{1-a}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}a^{k-1}+\frac{a^{p}}{1-a}.

To prove this, we observe that

(1-a)\left(1+a+\cdots+a^{p-1}\right)=1-a^{p}.

Dividing both sides by

(for

Let us write the expression in the following form:

\frac{1}{Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1}}=-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}}}

=-\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{Q^{k-1}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{k}}+\frac{Q^{p}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{p}(Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1})}

=-\frac{1}{z_{1}}-\frac{s-\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{M}z_{j}s^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}}}{z_{1}^{2}}-\sum_{k=3}^{p}\frac{Q^{k-1}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{k}}+\frac{Q^{p}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{p}(Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1})}.

We substitute this into the integral,

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R,\theta)}\exp\!\left(s^{1/\rho_{1}}\right)\,s^{\frac{1-\beta}{\rho_{1}}}\times

\quad\times\left(-\frac{1}{z_{1}}-\frac{s-\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{M}z_{j}s^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}}}{z_{1}^{2}}-\sum_{k=3}^{p}\frac{Q^{k-1}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{k}}+\frac{Q^{p}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{p}(Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1})}\right)ds,

or

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R,\theta)}\exp\!\left(s^{1/\rho_{1}}\right)\,s^{\frac{1-\beta}{\rho_{1}}}ds

-\frac{1}{z^{2}_{1}}\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R,\theta)}\exp\!\left(s^{1/\rho_{1}}\right)\,s^{\frac{1-\beta}{\rho_{1}}}\left(s-\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{M}z_{j}s^{\rho_{j}/\rho_{1}}\right)ds

We introduce the following notation

|Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1}|\geq|z_{1}|\sin(\mu-\theta).

Therefore,

\left|\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}\pi i}\int_{|s|=R,\ |\arg s|\leq\theta}e^{s^{1/\rho_{1}}}s^{\frac{1-\beta}{\rho_{1}}}\frac{Q^{p}(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)}{z_{1}^{p}(Q(z_{2},..,z_{M},s)-z_{1})}\,ds\right|\leq\frac{C}{|z_{1}|^{p+1}},

where
C>0
is independent of
z_{1}
.

Then we have the following asymptotics,

E_{{\rho}^{\prime},\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{M})=-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta-\rho_{1})}-\frac{1}{z_{1}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta-2\rho_{1})}-\sum_{j=2}^{M}\frac{z_{j}}{\Gamma(\beta-\rho_{1}-\rho_{j})}\right)

-\sum_{k=3}^{p}\frac{C_{k}(z_{2},..,z_{M},\rho_{j},\beta)}{z_{1}^{k}}+O\left(|z_{1}|^{-p-1}\right).

This completes the proof. ∎

4.
Lower bound for an infinitesimal denominator

In the solution of the inverse problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4
), the denominator contains the term

b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}g(t_{0}-\xi)\,\xi^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},\ast)\,d\xi.

In this section, we derive a lower bound for this term. The estimate is obtained by taking into account whether the function
g(t)
preserves its sign or changes sign, together with the asymptotic behavior of the above multinomial Mittag–Leffler function.

Let us divide the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
into two groups
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
and
K_{\rho_{1}}
:

\mathbb{N}=K_{\rho_{1}}\cup K_{0,\rho_{1}},

where a number
k
is assigned to
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
if
b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=0
, and if
b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})\neq 0
, then the number is assigned to
K_{\rho_{1}}
.
Note that for some
t_{0}
the set
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
can be empty, in which case
K_{\rho_{1}}=\mathbb{N}
. For example, if
g(t)
is sign-preserving, then
K_{\rho_{1}}=\mathbb{N}
for all
t_{0}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\rho_{j}\in(0,1)
for
j=1,\dots,M
,
g(t)\in C[0,T]
and
g(t)\neq 0
for
t\in[0,T]
(that is,
g(t)
does not change sign). Then the following estimates hold for all
k
and
t_{0}
:

\frac{\widetilde{C}_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}\leq|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{1}}{\lambda_{k}},

(4.1)

where the constants
\widetilde{C}_{0}
and
\widetilde{C}_{1}>0
.

Proof.

By the Weierstrass theorem, there exists a constant
g_{0}>0
such that

|g(t)|\geq g_{0}
.
We apply the mean value theorem and Lemma
2.3
to obtain

|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|=\left|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\xi^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\,g(t_{0}-\xi)\,d\xi\right|

=|g(\eta_{k})|\,t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*),\quad\eta_{k}\in[0,t_{0}].

Applying the asymptotic estimate from Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})^{2}}\right).

Hence, for sufficiently large
k\geq k_{0}
, using the estimate

|g(t)|\geq g_{0},

one has

|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|=|g(\eta_{k})|t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\left(\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}\right)^{2}}\right)\right)\geq\frac{C_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}.

It remains to consider the finitely many indices
k<k_{0}
.
Since the function
g(t)
does not change sign, it follows that
k\in K_{\rho_{1}}
. Consequently, we have

b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})\neq 0
for all k.

For the finite set
\{k:k<k_{0}\}
,
the quantities

\lambda_{k}|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|

form a finite collection of positive numbers.
Therefore their minimum exists and is strictly positive:

\overline{C}=\min_{k<k_{0}}\lambda_{k}|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|>0.

Consequently,

|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|\geq\frac{\overline{C}}{\lambda_{k}},\qquad k<k_{0}.

We define

\widetilde{C}_{0}=\min\{C_{0},\overline{C}\}
. Then

|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}\quad\text{for all }k.

The upper estimate follows from Lemma
2.2
:

|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|\leq C\,|g(\eta_{k})|\frac{t_{0}^{\rho}}{1+\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho}}\leq C\,\max_{0\leq\eta_{k}\leq t_{0}}|g(\eta_{k})|\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{1}}{\lambda_{k}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\rho_{j}\in(0,1)
for
j=1,\dots,M
,
g(t)\in C^{1}[0,T]
and
g(0)\neq 0
(
g(t)
changes sign). Then there exist numbers
r_{0}>0
and
k_{0}
such that, for all
t_{0}\leq r_{0}
and
k\geq k_{0}
, the following estimates hold:

\frac{\widetilde{C}_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}\leq|b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})|\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{1}}{\lambda_{k}},

(4.2)

where the constants
\widetilde{C}_{0}
and
\widetilde{C}_{1}>0
depend on
r_{0}
and
k_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{j}\in(0,1)
. Using Lemma
2.3
we integrate by parts and then apply the mean value theorem. Then we have

b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}g(t_{0}-\xi)\,\xi^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\,d\xi

=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}g(t_{0}-\xi)\,d\big[\xi^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\big]

=g(t_{0}-\xi)\,\xi^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\Big|_{0}^{t_{0}}+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}g^{\prime}(t_{0}-\xi)\,\xi^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\,d\xi

=g(0)t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*)+g^{\prime}(\eta_{k})\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\xi^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\,d\xi,\quad\eta_{k}\in[0,t_{0}].

For the last integral, using Lemma
2.3

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\xi^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}(-\lambda_{k}\xi^{\rho_{1}},*)\,d\xi=t_{0}^{\rho_{1}+1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+2}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*).

Applying the Theorem
3.1
,

E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}_{0})^{2}}\right),

E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+2}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}_{0})^{2}}\right),

we obtain

b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\frac{g(0)}{\lambda_{k}}+\frac{g^{\prime}(\eta_{k})t_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}_{0})^{2}}\right).

If
g(0)\neq 0
, sufficiently small
t_{0}
and sufficiently large
k
we obtain the required lower estimate. This also implies the corresponding upper bound.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If the conditions of Lemma
4.2
are satisfied, then the estimate (
4.2
) holds for all
t_{0}\leq r_{0}
and
k\in K_{\rho_{1}}
.

It is not hard to see that, this statement follow from Lemma
4.1
.

Corollary 4.4
.

If the conditions of Lemma
4.2
are satisfied and
t_{0}
is sufficiently small, then the set
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
has a finite number of elements.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, for sufficiently small
t_{0}\leq r_{0}
and sufficiently large
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\frac{g(0)}{\lambda_{k}}+\frac{g^{\prime}(\eta_{k})t_{0}}{\lambda_{k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}_{0})^{2}}\right)\neq 0,

since
g(0)\neq 0
.

Therefore, the equality
b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=0
can only hold for finitely many indices
k<k_{0}
.
This implies that the set

K_{0,\rho_{1}}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}:b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=0\},

contains only finitely many elements.
∎

5.
Existence

According to the Fourier method, we seek the function
u(x,t)
as a formal series:

u(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}T_{k}(t)\,v_{k}(x),

(5.1)

here the functions
T_{k}(t)
are a solution of the Cauchy type problem

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}T_{k}(t)+\lambda_{k}T_{k}(t)=f_{k}g(t),

(5.2)

T_{k}(0)=\varphi_{k}.

(5.3)

Note that the unknowns in problem (
5.2
) are also the coefficients
f_{k}
. To determine these numbers, from (
1.4
) we obtain the additional condition

T_{k}(t_{0})=\Psi_{k}.

(5.4)

The solution of problem (
5.2
)–(
5.3
) has the form
[
11
]
,

T_{k}(t)=\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]+f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t).

(5.5)

Then we obtain the equation

T_{k}(t_{0})=\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}{t_{0}}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}{t_{0}}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]+f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\Psi_{k}.

Consequently,

f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=\Psi_{k}-\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right],

(5.6)

f_{k}=\frac{\Psi_{k}-\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}.

(5.7)

Thus, we have constructed the formal series

u(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x),

(5.8)

and

f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi_{k}v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}.

(5.9)

Obviously, the functions (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) are formal solutions. It only remains to justify the Fourier method.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\in D(\widehat{A}^{\tau+1})
and
\Psi\in D(\widehat{A}^{\tau+1})
, where

\tau>\frac{N}{2m}
, and
g(t)\in C[0,T]

.
Then there exists a classical solution
(u(x,t),f(x))
of the inverse problem (

)–(
1.4

5.8
) and (
5.9
).

Proof.

Let the assumptions of the theorem be satisfied. First, let us prove the absolute and uniform convergence of the series (
5.9
) for the function
f
. Set

f_{n}^{1}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Psi_{k}v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})},

f_{n}^{2}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}.

The condition of the theorem implies the convergence of the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\Psi_{k}|^{2}\leq C_{\Psi}<\infty.

For the function
\Psi
for some
\tau>\frac{N}{2m}
, and for the convergence of the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\leq C_{\varphi}<\infty,

for the function
\varphi
.

Then, applying Lemma
2.1
with
\alpha=0
and using the identity

\widehat{A}^{-\tau}v_{k}(x)=\lambda_{k}^{-\tau}v_{k}(x)
, we obtain

\|f_{n}^{1}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}=\left\|\widehat{A}^{-\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\Psi_{k}v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{C(\Omega)}\leq C\,\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\Psi_{k}v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.10)

By the orthonormality of the system
{v_{k}(x)}
, we have

\|f_{n}^{1}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\Psi_{k}}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right|^{2}.

Further, applying the Lemma
4.1
(lower bound), we obtain

\|f_{n}^{1}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\Psi_{k}|^{2}\leq C\cdot C_{\Psi}<\infty.

(5.11)

Using the same argument as above to the function
f_{n}^{2}(x)
, we arrive at the inequality

\|f_{n}^{2}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}=\left\|\widehat{A}^{-\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{C(\Omega)}

\leq C\,\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.12)

Since the system
{v_{k}(x)}
is orthonormal, it follows that

\|f_{n}^{2}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right|^{2}.

By Lemmas
2.2
,
2.4
and
4.1
(lower bound), we get

\|f_{n}^{2}(x)\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\leq C\cdot C_{\varphi}<\infty.

(5.13)

The estimates (
5.11
) and (
5.13
) imply the uniform convergence of the series (
5.9
) defining the function
f(x)
. On the other hand, the sums in (
5.10
) and (
5.12
) converge under any permutation of their
terms, because these terms are mutually orthogonal. This implies the absolute convergence of the
series (
5.9
).

Using the definition of the coefficients
f_{k}
, we study the following three sums:

u_{n}^{1}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x),

(5.14)

u_{n}^{2}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Psi_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})},

(5.15)

u_{n}^{3}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}.

(5.16)

Let
|\alpha|\leq m
. We need to show that each of these sums converges uniformly
and absolutely in the domain
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T]
after the operators
D^{\alpha}
and

\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}
are applied term by term to the sum.

By Lemma
2.1
, one has the estimate

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{1}\|_{C(\Omega)}=\left\|D^{\alpha}\widehat{A}^{-\tau-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)\right\|_{C(\Omega)}

\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]v_{k}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.17)

Using the orthonormality of the system
v_{k}(x)
, we obtain

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{1}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]\right|^{2}.

Apply Lemma
2.2
, to get

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{1}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\leq C\cdot C_{\varphi}<\infty.

Similarly, for
u_{n}^{2}(x,t)
, we have

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{2}\|_{C(\Omega)}=\left\|D^{\alpha}\widehat{A}^{-\tau-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\frac{\Psi_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{C(\Omega)}

\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\frac{\Psi_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.18)

By the orthonormality of the system
{v_{k}(x)}
, one has

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{2}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\frac{\Psi_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right|^{2}.

Let
g\in C[0,T]
and define

\|g\|=\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}|g(t)|.

Then,

\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}}\right)g(\xi)\,d\xi\right|

\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right|\,|g(\xi)|\,d\xi

\leq\|g\|\int_{0}^{t}\left|(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right|\,d\xi.

Consequently,

\left|\int_{0}^{t}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}},*\right)g(\xi)\,d\xi\right|^{2}

\leq\|g\|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left|(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}}\!\left(-\lambda_{k}(t-\xi)^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right|\,d\xi\right)^{2}.

Using Lemmas
2.2
,
2.3
, and
4.1
(lower bound), we obtain

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{2}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C||g||^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\Psi_{k}|^{2}\leq C\cdot||g||^{2}\cdot C_{\Psi}<\infty.

For
u_{n}^{3}(x,t)
, the following estimate holds:

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{3}\|_{C(\Omega)}=\left\|D^{\alpha}\widehat{A}^{-\tau-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{C(\Omega)}

\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)v_{k}(x)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.19)

By the orthonormality of the system
v_{k}(x)
, we get

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{3}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}\varphi_{k}\left[1-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}}E_{\rho^{\prime},\rho_{1}+1}\left(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho_{1}},*\right)\right]b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t)}{b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})}\right|^{2}.

Apply Lemmas
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
, and

(lower bound), together with the assumption that

\|D^{\alpha}u_{n}^{3}\|_{C(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C||g||^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(\tau+1)}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\leq C\cdot||g||^{2}\cdot C_{\varphi}<\infty.

This implies the uniform convergence of the series
(

)–(

, in the closed cylinder

.

)
converge under any permutation of their terms, because these terms are mutually orthogonal.
Therefore, the differentiated series
(

Now, it remains to verify the validity of the term-by-term application of the operator

\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}

It can be readily verified that

=-A(x,D)\,\widehat{A}^{-\tau-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau+1}T_{k}(t)v_{k}(x)+g(t)\widehat{A}^{-\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{\tau}f_{k}v_{k}(x).

The absolute and uniform convergence of the series on the right-hand side has already been proved above.

The fact that the functions (

) satisfy all conditions (

)–(

) of the inverse problem
is obvious.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\varphi\in D(\widehat{A}^{\tau+1})
and
\Psi\in D(\widehat{A}^{\tau+1})
, where

\tau>\frac{N}{2m}
, and
g(t)\in C^{1}[0,T]
,
g(0)\neq 0
.

1) If the set
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
is empty i.e.
b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})\neq 0
, for all
k
then there exists solution of the inverse problem (
1.1
)–(

6.
Uniqueness

Theorem 6.1
.

Let the functions
\varphi(x)
and
\Psi(x)
be continuous in the closed domain
\Omega
, and let
K_{0,\rho_{1}}
be empty.
Then the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) admits at most one classical solution
(u(x,t),f(x))
.

Proof.

Assume that, under the conditions of the theorem, there exist two pairs of solutions,

(u_{1}(x,t),f_{1}(x))
and
(u_{2}(x,t),f_{2}(x))
.
We prove that

u(x,t)=u_{1}(x,t)-u_{2}(x,t)\equiv 0,\qquad f(x)=f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)\equiv 0.

Since the problem under consideration is linear, the pair
(u(x,t),f(x))
satisfies the following problem:

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}u(x,t)+A(x,D)u(x,t)=f(x)g(t),\quad x\in\Omega,\;0<t\leq T,

(6.1)

u(x,0)=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

(6.2)

B_{j}u(x,t)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m_{j}}b_{\alpha,j}(x)D^{\alpha}u(x,t)=0,

(6.3)

0\leq m_{j}\leq m-1,\quad j=1,\dots,l,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\;0<t\leq T,

u(x,t_{0})=0,\quad x\in\Omega.

(6.4)

Let
v_{k}(x)
be an arbitrary eigenfunction of the spectral problem (
2.1
)–(
2.2
)
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
.
Consider the function

w_{k}(t)=\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)v_{k}(x)\,dx.

(6.5)

From (
6.1
), we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}w_{k}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}u(x,t)v_{k}(x)\,dx

=-\int_{\Omega}A(x,D)u(x,t)v_{k}(x)\,dx+g(t)\int_{\Omega}f(x)v_{k}(x)\,dx.

Using the definition of eigenfunctions, we see that

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}w_{k}(t)=-\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)A(x,D)v_{k}(x)\,dx+f_{k}g(t)=-\lambda_{k}w_{k}(t)+f_{k}g(t).

Consequently,
w_{k}(t)
satisfies the Cauchy-type problem

\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\partial_{t}^{\rho_{j}}w_{k}(t)+\lambda_{k}w_{k}(t)=f_{k}g(t),\qquad w_{k}(0)=0,

w_{k}(t_{0})=0.

(6.6)

By (
5.5
), the solution of this problem has the form

w_{k}(t)=f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t).

Using the additional condition (
6.6

w_{k}(t_{0})=f_{k}b_{k,\rho_{1}}(t_{0})=0.

Since

is empty, we have

Therefore,

for all

Hence,
w_{k}(t)\equiv 0
for all
k

By the completeness of the system of eigenfunctions

f(x)\equiv 0,\qquad u(x,t)\equiv 0.

This proves the uniqueness of the solution to the inverse problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4

∎

Remark 6.2

8.
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated an inverse source problem for a multi-term time-fractional diffusion equation with the Caputo derivatives. The source term was assumed in separable form
f(x)g(t)
, and the unknown spatial component was recovered from an additional over-determination condition imposed at an interior time.

The elliptic part of the equation is described by a general
self-adjoint positive operator
A(x,D)
of order
m\geq 2
. By means of the spectral decomposition associated with the operator, the inverse problem was reduced to a sequence of multi-term fractional ordinary differential equations. Their solutions were expressed in terms of multinomial Mittag–Leffler functions.

A detailed asymptotic analysis of the corresponding multinomial Mittag–Leffler functions was carried out. Sharp asymptotic estimates were established and used to derive uniform lower bounds for the spectral denominator appearing in the reconstruction formula. These estimates play a crucial role in ensuring well-posedness of the inverse problem in the non-degenerate case.

Under appropriate smoothness assumptions on the given data, sufficient conditions guaranteeing the existence of a classical solution were obtained. The uniform and absolute convergence of the associated Fourier series was rigorously justified. Furthermore, uniqueness was proved under the condition that the set of vanishing spectral denominators is empty. In the degenerate case, necessary and sufficient compatibility conditions were derived, and the structure of the non-uniqueness was completely characterized.

The abstract results were further specified for homogeneous elliptic operators with constant coefficients on the torus, where the domain of fractional powers of the operator was explicitly identified with Sobolev spaces. This example illustrates the applicability and structural clarity of the developed spectral approach.

The obtained results provide a comprehensive analytical framework for multi-term time-fractional inverse source problems and extend existing well-posedness theory to a broad class of elliptic operators.
