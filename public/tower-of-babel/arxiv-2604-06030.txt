C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} regularity for a class of singular/degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations with oblique boundary conditions
Sun-Sig Byun, Hongsoo Kim, SeungHyun Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish global C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} regularity for viscosity solutions to a class of singular and degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations subject to oblique boundary conditions. Our work extends the findings in [ 6 ] to a broader class of equations, notably encompassing the singular case.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider global regularity results for viscosity solutions to a class of fully nonlinear elliptic equations of the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Phi(x,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)&=f&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\beta\cdot Du&=g&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded
\displaystyle C^{1}
domain with boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
,
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a uniformly elliptic operator in the sense of
(A1):
and satisfies
\displaystyle F(0)=0
, and
\displaystyle\Phi:\Omega\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a continuous function that exhibits singular and degenerate behavior in the gradient, as described in
(A2):
. Here,
\displaystyle f\in C(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle g\in C^{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
, and
\displaystyle\beta\in C^{\alpha}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
, with
\displaystyle\beta
satisfying
(A3):
.

We begin by discussing condition
(A2):
. This condition was introduced in
[
1
]
, where the interior Hölder continuity of the gradient for viscosity solutions to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)=f\quad\text{ in }B_{1},

was established under the assumptions that
\displaystyle F
is a fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic operator and
\displaystyle f\in C(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
. We now present some examples satisfying
(A2):
as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p}
with
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=s(\Phi)=p
,
where
\displaystyle p>-1
.

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
with
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=\inf_{x\in\Omega}p(x)
and

\displaystyle s(\Phi)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}p(x)
,
where
\displaystyle p(\cdot)\in C(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}p(x)>-1
.

(3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}+a(x)t^{q(x)}
with
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left\{p(x),q(x)\right\}
and

\displaystyle s(\Phi)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left\{p(x),q(x)\right\}
,
where
\displaystyle p(\cdot),q(\cdot)\in C(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left\{p(x),q(x)\right\}>-1
and
\displaystyle 0\leq a(\cdot)\in C(\Omega)
.

(4)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=\phi(t)+a(x)\psi(t)
where
\displaystyle\phi
and
\displaystyle\psi
are suitable
\displaystyle N
-functions and
\displaystyle 0\leq a(\cdot)\in C(\Omega)
.

We next turn to boundary regularity results for singular and degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations. In
[
2
]
, the authors established global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for viscosity solutions to the Dirichlet problem associated with (
1.2
) on
\displaystyle C^{2}
domains. For the singular case corresponding to the power-type choice
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{\gamma}
with
\displaystyle-1<\gamma<0
,
[
12
]
proved global Lipschitz regularity under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions on
\displaystyle C^{2}
domains. For the degenerate case
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{\gamma}
with
\displaystyle\gamma>0
,
[
3
]
obtained global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity under nonhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions on
\displaystyle C^{2}
domains. For oblique boundary conditions, global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity was established in
[
6
]
for the same power-type choice
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{\gamma}
with
\displaystyle\gamma>0
on
\displaystyle C^{1}
domains. For further references, including those concerning uniformly elliptic equations, we refer the reader to
[
13
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
10
,
7
]
and the references therein.

Our goal in this paper is to establish global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for viscosity solutions to a general class of equations of the form (
1.1
), under nonhomogeneous oblique boundary conditions on
\displaystyle C^{1}
domains. The novelty of our result lies in treating both the degenerate case (
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geq 0
) and the singular case (
\displaystyle-1<i(\Phi)<0
) within a unified framework. In the degenerate regime, our result builds on
[
6
]
and applies to a broader class of nonlinearities beyond the power-type setting. In the singular regime, we improve upon the result of
[
12
]
, where global Lipschitz regularity was established via the Ishii–Lion’s method under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. We note that, in this singular regime, even the extension to nonhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions is not straightforward.

We now briefly explain the strategy used in the proof. To establish global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity, we show that a solution
\displaystyle u
can be approximated by a sequence of affine functions with an error of order
\displaystyle r^{k(1+\alpha)}
at each scale
\displaystyle r^{k}
(for some fixed
\displaystyle r\in(0,1)
) via an iterative argument. More precisely, we obtain a sequence of affine functions
\displaystyle\left\{l_{k}\right\}

satisfying

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert u-l_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\cap B_{r^{k}}\right)}\leq r^{k(1+\alpha)}\qquad\text{ for all }\;k\geq 0.

The case
\displaystyle k=1
follows from a compactness argument. By iterating this procedure—namely, by applying the compactness argument repeatedly to the scaled functions
\displaystyle v_{k}
defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle v_{k}(x):=\frac{(u-l_{k})(r^{k}x)}{r^{k(1+\alpha)}},

we obtain the estimate for any
\displaystyle k
. Note that
\displaystyle v_{k}
does not satisfy the original equation (
1.1
). Instead, it solves an equation of the form

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(|Dv_{k}-q|)F(D^{2}v_{k})=\tilde{f},

where
\displaystyle q=-\nabla l_{k}/r^{k\alpha}
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}
satisfies
(A2):
after an appropriate scaling (see Section
2.3
for details). Hence, to implement the compactness argument, we need an equicontinuity estimate for solutions to (
1.3
) that remains uniform in
\displaystyle q
.

In
[
9
]
, the authors introduced the sliding cusp method to establish Hölder estimates for uniformly elliptic equations that hold only where the gradient is large. In
[
6
]
, this result was extended to the oblique boundary setting. To address the difficulties arising from oblique boundary conditions, the authors combined the sliding paraboloid and cusp methods and exploited a modified scaling property adapted to the equation. As a consequence, they obtained Hölder estimates for uniformly elliptic equations that hold only where the gradient is far from some point on
\displaystyle C^{1}
domains.

By combining the above result with the structural condition
(A2):
on
\displaystyle\Phi
, we obtain the desired estimate in the degenerate case. In the singular case, after a suitable transformation (see Lemma
2.2
for more details), a term of the form
\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}
with
\displaystyle\gamma\in[0,1)
appears, which creates a major difficulty in the analysis. More specifically, (
1.3
) takes the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi\left(x,|Dv-q|\right)F(D^{2}v)=f|Dv-q|^{\gamma},

for an arbitrary
\displaystyle q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As mentioned before, we need to find a uniform analysis in
\displaystyle q
. However, as
\displaystyle|q|
becomes very large, the right-hand side may grow indefinitely, hindering the derivation of uniform estimates independent of
\displaystyle q
.
To circumvent this difficulty, we restrict our attention to the regime where
\displaystyle|q|^{\gamma}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded; in this case, the arguments from
[
8
,
6
]
remain applicable, without resorting to the Ishii-Lion’s method.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i},

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\Phi}(x,|D\widetilde{u}-q|)\widetilde{F}(D^{2}\widetilde{u})&=\widetilde{f}|D\widetilde{u}-q|^{\gamma}&&\text{in }\left(\frac{1}{s}\Omega\right)\cap B_{1},\\
\widetilde{\beta}\cdot D\widetilde{u}&=\widetilde{g}&&\text{on }\partial\left(\frac{1}{s}\Omega\right)\cap B_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{1}
and
\displaystyle D\varphi(0)=0
, we can choose
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
sufficiently small, depending only on
\displaystyle[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}
and the
\displaystyle C^{1}
modulus of
\displaystyle\varphi
, such that

|D\varphi(x)|\leq\epsilon\quad\text{for any }x\in\overline{B_{s}},

which yields
\displaystyle[\widetilde{\varphi}]_{C^{1}}\leq\epsilon
, and simultaneously,

(4.1)

[\widetilde{\beta}]_{C^{\alpha}}=s^{\alpha}[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1-r^{\overline{\alpha}}}{2\overline{C_{e}}}\epsilon.

Next, we define
\displaystyle K>1
by

K:=1+4\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left(\frac{4\overline{C_{e}}M^{2}}{(1-(1/2)^{\overline{\alpha}})\nu_{0}\epsilon}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{1/(1-\gamma+i(\Phi))}+\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon}+\frac{2}{\delta_{0}}\right)\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}}.

Noting that

(4.2)

\beta(0)\cdot\mathbf{n}(0)=\beta_{n}(0)\geq\delta_{0},

we clearly have
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(0)=0
and

\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{K}+\frac{\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{K\delta_{0}}\leq 1.

Furthermore, since
\displaystyle K/s\geq 1
,
(A2):
implies

\Phi\left(sx,\frac{K}{s}\right)\geq\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{K}{s}\right)^{i(\Phi)}\Phi(sx,1)\geq\frac{\nu_{0}}{M}\left(\frac{K}{s}\right)^{i(\Phi)}.

Using this bound, we deduce

\left\lVert\widetilde{f}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{Ms^{2-\gamma+i(\Phi)}}{\nu_{0}K^{1-\gamma+i(\Phi)}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{M}{\nu_{0}K^{1-\gamma+i(\Phi)}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1-(1/2)^{\overline{\alpha}}}{4\overline{C_{e}}M}\epsilon.

For the boundary data, we have
\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(0)=0
, and combining (
4.2
) and (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle[\widetilde{g}]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{\alpha+1}}{K}[g]_{C^{\alpha}}+\frac{s^{\alpha+1}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{K\delta_{0}}[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2\overline{C_{e}}\delta_{0}}\right)\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Finally, by (
4.2
), we have

|q|\leq\frac{\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{K\delta_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\overline{C_{e}}}{1-r^{\overline{\alpha}}}.

We now claim that there exists a sequence of linear functions
\displaystyle l_{k}(x)=b_{k}\cdot x
with
\displaystyle b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that for every integer
\displaystyle k\geq 0
:

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\left\lVert u-l_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{r^{k}}\right)}\leq r^{k(1+\overline{\alpha})},\\
\beta(0)\cdot b_{k}=0,\\
|b_{k}-b_{k+1}|\leq\overline{C_{e}}r^{k\overline{\alpha}}.\end{cases}

We proceed by induction. For
\displaystyle k=0
, the claim holds trivially by choosing
\displaystyle l_{0}=0
, since
\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
.
Suppose that the induction hypotheses (
4.3
) hold up to some integer
\displaystyle k\geq 0
. We consider the normalized function

v_{k}(x):=\frac{(u-l_{k})(r^{k}x)}{r^{k(1+\overline{\alpha})}}.

By the scaling introduced in Section
2.3
,
\displaystyle v_{k}
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Phi_{k}\left(x,\left|Dv_{k}-q_{k}\right|\right)F_{k}(D^{2}v_{k})&=f_{k}\left|Dv_{k}-q_{k}\right|^{\gamma}&&\text{in }\left(\frac{1}{r^{k}}\Omega\right)\cap B_{1},\\
\beta_{k}\cdot Dv_{k}&=g_{k}&&\text{on }\partial\left(\frac{1}{r^{k}}\Omega\right)\cap B_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

where the rescaled quantities are given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{\Phi(r^{k}x,r^{k\overline{\alpha}}t)}{\Phi(r^{k}x,r^{k\overline{\alpha}})},\quad\quad q_{k}:=\frac{q-b_{k}}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{k}(M)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=r^{k(1-\overline{\alpha})}F(r^{k(\overline{\alpha}-1)}M),\quad f_{k}(x):=\frac{r^{k(1-\overline{\alpha}(1-\gamma))}f(r^{k}x)}{\Phi(r^{k}x,r^{k\overline{\alpha}})},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\beta(r^{k}x),\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad g_{k}(x):=\frac{g(r^{k}x)-\beta(r^{k}x)\cdot b_{k}}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{\varphi(r^{k}x)}{r^{k}}\left(=\varphi_{\frac{1}{r^{k}}\Omega}\right).

In order to apply Proposition
3.1
, we now verify its hypotheses for
\displaystyle v_{k}
. We have
\displaystyle\left\lVert v_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
by the induction hypothesis. Since
\displaystyle r^{k\overline{\alpha}}\leq 1
,
(A2):
yields

\Phi(r^{k}x,r^{k\overline{\alpha}})\geq\frac{1}{M}(r^{k\overline{\alpha}})^{s(\Phi)}\Phi(r^{k}x,1)=\frac{1}{M}r^{k\overline{\alpha}s(\Phi)}.

Using this bound, we can estimate the source term as

(4.4)

\left\lVert f_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq Mr^{k(1-\overline{\alpha}(1+s(\Phi)-\gamma))}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Moreover, the induction hypothesis
\displaystyle|b_{j}-b_{j+1}|\leq\overline{C_{e}}r^{j\overline{\alpha}}
implies

(4.5)

|b_{k}|\leq\frac{\overline{C_{e}}}{1-r^{\overline{\alpha}}}.

Combining this with
\displaystyle|q|\leq\frac{\overline{C_{e}}}{1-r^{\overline{\alpha}}}
, we can bound
\displaystyle q_{k}
by

|q_{k}|=\frac{|q-b_{k}|}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}}\leq\frac{|q|+|b_{k}|}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}}\leq\frac{2\overline{C_{e}}}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}(1-r^{\overline{\alpha}})}.

Consequently, combining this bound with (
4.4
) gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1+|q_{k}|^{(\gamma-i(\Phi))_{+}})\left\lVert f_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{2\overline{C_{e}}}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}(1-r^{\overline{\alpha}})}\right)^{(\gamma-i(\Phi))_{+}}Mr^{k(1-\overline{\alpha}(1+s(\Phi)-\gamma))}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\overline{C_{e}}M}{1-(1/2)^{\overline{\alpha}}}r^{k(1-\overline{\alpha}(1+s(\Phi)-\gamma+(\gamma-i(\Phi))_{+}))}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{4\overline{C_{e}}M}{1-(1/2)^{\overline{\alpha}}}r^{k(1-\overline{\alpha}(1+s(\Phi)-\min(i(\Phi),\gamma)))}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\overline{C_{e}}M}{1-(1/2)^{\overline{\alpha}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\epsilon,

where we used the identity
\displaystyle 1+s(\Phi)-\gamma+(\gamma-i(\Phi))_{+}=1+s(\Phi)-\min(i(\Phi),\gamma)
, and the second-to-last inequality follows from our choice of
\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}
. Regarding the boundary data,
\displaystyle g(0)=0
and
\displaystyle\beta(0)\cdot b_{k}=0
together with (
4.5
) yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle|g_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{|g(r^{k}x)-g(0)-(\beta(r^{k}x)-\beta(0))\cdot b_{k}|}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{[g]_{C^{\alpha}}|r^{k}x|^{\alpha}+[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}|r^{k}x|^{\alpha}|b_{k}|}{r^{k\overline{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq[g]_{C^{\alpha}}+[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}\frac{\overline{C_{e}}}{1-r^{\overline{\alpha}}}\leq\epsilon.

It is also immediate that
\displaystyle[\beta_{k}]_{C^{\alpha}}\leq r^{k\alpha}[\beta]_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\epsilon
and
\displaystyle[\varphi_{k}]_{C^{1}}\leq[\varphi]_{C^{1}}\leq\epsilon
.

Thus, all hypotheses of Proposition
3.1
are satisfied for
\displaystyle v_{k}
. Hence, there exists a linear function
\displaystyle\tilde{l}(x)=\tilde{b}\cdot x
such that

\left\lVert v_{k}-\tilde{l}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{1}{r^{k}}\Omega\right)_{r}\right)}\leq r^{1+\overline{\alpha}},\quad\beta_{k}(0)\cdot\tilde{b}=\beta(0)\cdot\tilde{b}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad|\tilde{b}|\leq\overline{C_{e}}.

Setting

l_{k+1}(x):=l_{k}(x)+r^{k(1+\overline{\alpha})}\tilde{l}(x/r^{k}),

we verify that (
4.3
) holds for
\displaystyle k+1
. This completes the induction step, and the theorem follows.
∎
