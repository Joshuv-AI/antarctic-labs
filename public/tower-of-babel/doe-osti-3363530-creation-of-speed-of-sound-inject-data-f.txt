Summary
Technical Nuclear Forensics (TNF) exercises simulate a detonation of a nuclear device within the
United States, usually in a city. Speed-of-sound (SOS) phenomenology are the atmospheric
overpressure and ground shock mechanical motions that are observed at over-pressure (air-blast
and infrasound) and seismic sensors, respectively (Figure 1). Amplitudes of SOS data are related to
the explosive yield (Kinney and Graham, 1985; Koper et al., 2002; Bonner et al., 2013ab; Ford et al.,
2014, 2021; Templeton et al., 2018; Schnurr et al., 2020). Within the country, the United States
Prompt Diagnostics System (USPDS) includes a network of geophysical sensors to capture such
SOS signals.

Figure 1. Simulated surface emplacement explosion generating atmospheric overpressure and seismic
ground motion.

During the TNF exercise, the event data will be analyzed by the players who know nothing about the
technical details of the source (e.g. location, yield, explosive). Speciﬁcally, they will use the
Integrated Yield Determination Tool (IYDT) to estimate the yield and height-of-burst or depth-of-
burial (HOB/DOB). The IYDT allows the user to measure features on the overpressure and seismic
channels, evaluate the consistency of features and jointly estimate yield and HOB/DOB. For these
exercises, SOS data are simulated for the location, emplacement and yield of the device and
signals are propagated to the observing stations. This document describes the steps undertaken in
the simulation, validation, preparation and veriﬁcation of SOS signals for TNF exercises.

We start by broadly summarizing this activity as the following steps:

 • Simulation is the physics-based modeling of the excitation and propagation of SOS signals
 from the explosion to the sensors. This is the creation of SOS signals in physical MKS units

 (pressure in Pascals, seismic ground motion in velocity m/s or acceleration m/s/s) along
 with the metadata on the source and sensor locations. Timing of the signals must be
 correct assuming the source is at time “zero”.
 • Validation is the evaluation of the physical validity of the signals to reﬂect the location,
 emplacement and yield of the explosion. Validation is assessed through the interpretation
 of features measured from the signals with the physical models dependent on yield, height-
 of-burst/depth-of-burial, emplacement conditions, atmospheric conditions, seismic
 material properties, distance, etc… (e.g. Ford et al., 2014, 2020). Crucial to this step is
 analysis of the data with the Integrated Yield Determination Tool (IYDT).
 • Preparation is the treatment of SOS signal data to represent the digital data ﬂowing into the
 USPDS. This involves the time-series data in digital counts with absolute timing
 registration. Physical values of the overpressure and ground motion are obtained by the
 removal of the digitizer and sensor instrument response.
 • Veriﬁcation is the evaluation of the veracity (truthfulness) of the time-series. This includes
 correctness of the conversion from digital counts to physical overpressure or seismic
 motions as well as the timing of the signals relative to the origin time.

In the following, we provide details on the broad steps described above.

1. Simulation of SOS Signals
This step involves the computational modeling of SOS signals for the exercise-speciﬁc details.
Inputs:

 • event details:
 o location (geographic latitude and longitude);
 o height-of-burst (above ground surface) or depth-of-burial (below ground surface),
 hereafter referred to as HOB/DOB;
 o emplacement in surrounding urban structures;
 o device yield is nuclear equivalent kilograms of Trinitrotoluene (TnT)
 • sensor details for each receiver:
 o location (geographic latitude and longitude);
 o altitude/depth of sensor relative to the ground surface;
 o SEED (Standard for the Exchange of Earthquake Data) identiﬁcations (SEEDID) of
 channels to be used in the exercise: network.station.location.channel
 o Instrument response of each SEEDID to be simulated

Signals are simulated separately for overpressure and seismic ground motions.

Overpressure signals can be simulated with the SHAMRC hydrodynamics code (Needham, 2010).
This code uses physical models of the surface topography and building geometry to propagate the
air blast through and out of the urban environment. For ranges with signal overpressures of ~3,500
Pa or lower, signal propagation is performed with the Low Altitude Multiple Burst (LAMB) model
(Hikida and Needham, 1981) or SPECFEM2D-DG (Brissaud et al., 2017; Martire et at., 2021).
Atmosphere conditions are incorporated into the signal propagation model.

Seismic signals can be simulated using a Greens function approach. This method uses a three-
dimensional (3D) model describing the seismic wavespeeds, attenuation quality factors and
density of the domain and must include the source and receiver locations. In this approach the
source is placed at the normal scaled depth-of-burial (122 𝑚/𝑊 !/# , where W is the explosive yield
in kilotons). The motions are then scaled to the actual yield following the Muller-Murphy (1971)
explosion source model and the emplacement HOB/DOB for the geological material.

IYDT uses the impulse (area-under-the-curve) of the seismic ground velocity of the positive half-
cycle of the ﬁrst arriving P-wave. This corresponds to the peak displacement of the initial P-wave
and has been shown to be a robust feature relating seismic ground motion to yield (Ford et al.,
2014). In a similar fashion for overpressure, IYDT measures the impulse between zero crossings of
the ﬁrst positive pulse.

2. Validation
This step involves the evaluation of the validity of simulated SOS data from the previous step (the
time-series representing overpressure or ground motion in physical units relative to the origin time).
Validation is done ﬁrstly with simple methods but ultimately must be assessed with the IYDT
version that will be used in the exercise.

A simple evaluation can be checked by measuring the features used in the IYDT and comparing
these with model predictions for the event source parameters. This simple evaluation must be
done independent of the IYDT before the data are evaluated with the IYDT. Note that step 1 and this
simple evaluation must be repeated until the SOS signals are consistent with the known exercise
yield and HOB/DOB.

The most important evaluation of the validity of SOS inject data are the analyses with the IYDT. The
IYDT takes the SOS time-series from step 1 as SAC ﬁles. Importantly, the data must be in physical
MKS units and several SAC header variables must be set. This can be done with a simple python
program or jupyter notebook.

3. Preparation
Once the simulated SOS in physical units signals are acceptable they must be prepared for
injection into the operational system. Speciﬁcally, the noise-free simulated signals in physical
units must be:

 • time-shifted to reﬂect the actual detonation time of the device at ground zero and
 propagation to the sensor;
 • embedded into realistic noise based on the sensor location within or outside the urban
 environment providing 1 minute of noise pre-event and 4 minutes of signal post-event;
 • converted to digital counts using the associated instrument (sensor and digitizer) response;
 and
 • formatted to represent the data ﬂowing in the USPDS operational pipeline.

Inputs:

 • outputs of step 2

Outputs:

 • pipeline-formatted digital data as described above

4. Veriﬁcation
Finally, the pipeline-formatted data must be veriﬁed to have the proper formatting and produce the
simulated SOS time-series in physical units within the IYDT and thus should provide an acceptable
yield and HOB/DOB result. This step must be done within the operational environment where
USPDS data are ﬂowing. It must conﬁrm that the version of IYDT used in on the USPDS pipeline is
the same as that in step 2.
