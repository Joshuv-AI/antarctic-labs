Adiabatic Fast Passage Spin Manipulation Measurements in Solid Polarized Targets Journal: Nuclear Instruments and Methods in Physics Research A
M.F. Hossain, K. Nakano, N.G. Vismith, D. Keller

Abstract
Adiabatic fast passage (AFP) is an attractive technique for rapidly reversing polarization and manipulating spin populations in polarized solid targets. It enables frequent spin flips without requiring dynamic nuclear polarization (DNP) repolarization between reversals, which can take tens of minutes to several hours. We report AFP measurements for several target materials in a 5 T, 1 K DNP target system. The main advances are threefold: (i) new measurements of AFP efficiency in irradiated 15 NH 3 , irradiated 14 ND 3 , and butanol systems prepared either with TEMPO doping or by irradiation, summarized in a dedicated table; (ii) a joint manipulated-lineshape analysis, demonstrated here on irradiated deuterated butanol AFP spectra, that extracts the vector and tensor polarization components of a spin-1 system from AFP-manipulated NMR spectra, including non-Boltzmann “half-flip” states for which standard intensity-ratio methods fail; and (iii) an efficiency study of an irradiated 15 NH 3 sample showing a strong dependence of AFP efficiency on the initial polarization.



1
Introduction

Polarized solid targets are central tools for spin-dependent measurements in nuclear and high-energy physics. A recurring operational limitation is polarization reversal: reversal by DNP repolarization is reliable but slow—often requiring hours for some materials—during which beam conditions and detector acceptances may drift. AFP provides a rapid, coherent inversion mechanism by sweeping the RF frequency (or the holding field) through the NMR resonance so that the macroscopic magnetization follows an effective field in the rotating frame
[
Bloch1946Induction
,
Abragam1961
]
. The method is conceptually related to the “single-shot passage” and spin-temperature descriptions developed for solids
[
Goldman1968
,
Goldman1970
,
Provotorov1962
]
.

Historically, AFP performance has shown strong material dependence. Early studies in proton targets with relatively high paramagnetic doping found large losses that limited practical use
[
Parfenov1984
]
. In contrast, Hautle
et al.
demonstrated high AFP efficiencies for deuterons in deuterated alcohols (spin-1)
[
Hautle1992
,
Hautle1995
]
. These results highlighted that the relevant optimization for frozen-spin DNP targets is not only the classical adiabatic criterion but also the interplay of line width, relaxation in the rotating frame, and the coupling between nuclear spin reservoirs.

The present work extends AFP measurements and analysis in a modern 5 T/1 K polarized-target system
[
CrabbMeyer1997
]
. Our key contributions are:

3
AFP theory: from rotating-frame adiabaticity to solid-state limits

3.1
Rotating-frame description and Landau–Zener adiabaticity

We summarize AFP in the standard rotating-frame picture
[
Abragam1961
,
Bloch1946Induction
]
. For a spin system in a static field
B_{0}\hat{z}
, driven by a transverse RF field
B_{1}
near resonance, the rotating-wave approximation yields an effective Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rot}}(t)=-\hbar\Delta\omega(t)\,I_{z}-\hbar\gamma B_{1}\,I_{x},

(1)

where
\Delta\omega(t)=\omega(t)-\omega_{0}
is the instantaneous detuning and
\omega_{0}=\gamma B_{0}
. The dynamics can be visualized as precession about an effective field

\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)=\left(B_{1},\,0,\,\frac{\Delta\omega(t)}{\gamma}\right),

(2)

which sweeps from
+\hat{z}
to
-\hat{z}
as
\Delta\omega(t)
crosses zero. In the ideal adiabatic limit, the macroscopic magnetization follows
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, producing inversion.

For a linear sweep
\Delta\omega(t)=\alpha t
, the two-level crossing problem yields the Landau–Zener transition probability
[
Landau1932
,
Zener1932
]

P_{\rm diab}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi(\gamma B_{1})^{2}}{2|\alpha|}\right),

(3)

so that inversion approaches unity when
(\gamma B_{1})^{2}/|\alpha|\gg 1
. In practice for large solid targets, the spectrum is inhomogeneously broadened and the relevant condition must hold across the full line width and for the spatial distribution of
B_{1}
.

3.2
Spin-temperature (Provotorov) viewpoint in solids

In DNP target materials, nuclear spins form strongly interacting reservoirs and are coupled to paramagnetic centers. For broad lines, it is often useful to describe the nuclear system by spin temperatures (or inverse temperatures) associated with a Zeeman reservoir and a dipolar reservoir
[
Provotorov1962
,
Goldman1968
,
Goldman1970
]
. In this picture, RF irradiation and frequency sweeps exchange energy between reservoirs; the AFP efficiency is then limited by:

1.

Non-adiabaticity
(finite sweep rate or insufficient
B_{1}
),

2.

Relaxation in the rotating frame
(
T_{1\rho}
), and

3.

Spin diffusion and spectral diffusion
that redistribute populations across the broadened line.

Goldman
et al.
derived quantitative limits for “single-shot” passage in solids using the Provotorov equations
[
Goldman1968
]
. Parfenov and Prudkoglyad used this framework to explain the poor AFP efficiency observed for proton frozen-spin target materials with high paramagnetic impurity densities
[
Parfenov1984
]
.

A widely used engineering summary is the adiabaticity parameter

A\equiv\frac{\gamma B_{1}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}B/\mathrm{d}t}\quad\text{(equivalently }A\propto\frac{(\gamma B_{1})^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}t}\text{)},

(4)

and robust AFP typically requires
A\gg 1
across the line and across the sample
[
Hautle1995
]
.

3.3
Radiation damping, auto-oscillation, and superradiance

In large, highly polarized samples coupled to a high-Q resonant circuit, the nuclear magnetization can significantly drive the circuit. The resulting radiation damping can break the symmetry between AFP starting from
+P
and
-P

[
Bloom1957
,
Odehnal1973
]
. When the effective negative resistance produced by the spin system compensates circuit losses, auto-oscillation and superradiant emission can occur, leading to strongly asymmetric reversal efficiencies
[
Reichertz1994
]
. This phenomenon is particularly relevant for large
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
samples at 5 T/1 K, where the proton frequency is high and RF coils are often operated with high Q.

These effects motivate two practical choices in our measurements:
(i) using a small 1 g cup to reduce coupling and suppress superradiance during precision studies, and
(ii) explicitly studying the dependence of AFP efficiency on the initial polarization in a large 7 g
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
configuration (Sec.
).

3.4
Spin-1 (deuteron) AFP with quadrupolar splitting

For deuterons (
I=1
) the NMR spectrum is split by quadrupolar interactions, producing two overlapping
\Delta m=\pm 1
transitions. In polycrystalline materials this yields the Pake doublet
[
Pake1948
,
Dulya1997
,
Keller2020
]
. AFP in such a system is not a single isolated two-level crossing: inversion proceeds through sequential passages across the two transitions. As shown experimentally by Hautle
et al.
, complete inversion of the vector polarization requires a full frequency sweep and an additional half-sweep (“
1.5
sweeps”) under typical conditions
[
Hautle1992
]
.

This multilevel structure is also what enables tensor polarimetry: the populations
n_{+1},n_{0},n_{-1}
can be driven into non-Boltzmann distributions during partial sweeps, producing states with nontrivial
P
and
Q
that must be extracted from lineshape information rather than a single intensity ratio
[
Keller2020
]
.

4
Vector and tensor polarimetry in non-equilibrium AFP states

4.1
Definitions

For spin-1 nuclei, we define the vector polarization
P
and tensor polarization
Q
in terms of the Zeeman sublevel populations
[
Dulya1997
,
Keller2020
]

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=n_{+1}-n_{-1},

(5)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=n_{+1}+n_{-1}-2n_{0},

(6)

with
n_{+1}+n_{0}+n_{-1}=1
.

Inverting these relations gives the sublevel populations:

\displaystyle n_{+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{2}P+\frac{1}{6}Q,

(7)

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Q}{3},

(8)

\displaystyle n_{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{2}P+\frac{1}{6}Q.

(9)

The two absorption lines correspond to population differences:

\displaystyle I_{+}(\omega)

\displaystyle\propto(n_{+1}-n_{0})\,f_{+}(\omega),

(10)

\displaystyle I_{-}(\omega)

\displaystyle\propto(n_{0}-n_{-1})\,f_{-}(\omega),

(11)

where
f_{\pm}
are the (common) powder-pattern lineshape functions, broadened by instrumental response and inhomogeneity.

In thermal equilibrium (or in global spin-temperature equilibrium) the ratio of integrated line intensities constrains
P
and
Q

[
Keller2020
]
. During AFP, however, especially in half-flip or interrupted sweeps, this Boltzmann relation is generally violated, so a ratio-only analysis becomes invalid
[
Keller2020
,
Clement:2023eun
]
.

4.2
New analysis method

The central methodological advance of this work is a joint fit of the manipulated spin-1 lineshape that extracts
P
and
Q
from AFP-distorted spectra without assuming a single global Boltzmann distribution. The construction follows the differential-binning, rates-response, and spin-temperature-consistency principles of Ref.
[
Clement:2023eun
]
, extended here to the case of an AFP sweep.

For a background-subtracted spectrum, the model signal is written as

S(\omega)=A\left[S_{+}(\omega)+S_{-}(\omega)\right],

(12)

with

\displaystyle S_{+}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}a_{+,j}\,\Pi_{j}(\omega)\,f_{+}(\omega;\bm{\lambda}),

(13)

\displaystyle S_{-}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}a_{-,j}\,\Pi_{j}(\omega)\,f_{-}(\omega;\bm{\lambda}),

(14)

where
f_{\pm}
are the equilibrium Pake/Dulya component shapes,
\bm{\lambda}
denotes the shared line-center, quadrupole-splitting, and broadening parameters,
\Pi_{j}
is a piecewise binning of the frequency coordinate, and
a_{\pm,j}
are local transition amplitudes.

The amplitudes
a_{+,j}
and
a_{-,j}
are not treated as two unrelated fits. Instead, within each bin they are constrained by the local spin-temperature-consistency condition

\frac{a_{+,j}}{a_{-,j}}=r_{s}(R_{j},T_{s,j}),

(15)

where
r_{s}(R_{j},T_{s,j})
is the equilibrium asymmetry ratio at coordinate
R_{j}
for a local effective spin temperature
T_{s,j}

[
Clement:2023eun
]
. This allows the effective spin temperature to vary across the line while preserving a physically consistent relationship between the two overlapping transitions.

Hole burning and AFP distortion are introduced through a coupled response of mirrored bins. If a local depletion
\Delta a_{\pm,j}
is produced in one branch, the overlapping branch responds according to the rates-response relation

\Delta a_{\mp,j^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta a_{\pm,j},\qquad R_{j^{\prime}}\simeq-R_{j},

(16)

after convolution with a finite-width RF-response kernel that accounts for field inhomogeneity, circuit bandwidth, and the nonzero spectral width of the manipulation. For AFP data this kernel is allowed to depend on sweep direction and sweep rate so that the same framework describes both localized hole burning and sweep-history-dependent AFP distortion.

The complete model is fit to the digitized spectrum with weighted nonlinear least squares, using a constant point-by-point uncertainty determined from the RMSD of the signal tails as described in Sec.
7
. The fit returns a single manipulated spectrum with shared global line parameters and a coupled set of local response amplitudes. The fitted component areas

I^{\pm}=\int S_{\pm}(\omega)\,d\omega

are then converted to
P
and
Q
through Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), with statistical uncertainties propagated from the full covariance matrix of the joint fit.

4.3
Lineshape model for the deuteron spin system

The deuteron, a spin-1 nucleus, experiences both Zeeman and quadrupolar interactions in the presence of an external magnetic field. The magnetic sublevels
\ket{m}
(
m=+1,0,-1
) are split by the combined interactions. The corresponding energy levels can be expressed as
[
Abragam1961
,
Dulya1997
]
:

\displaystyle E_{m}

\displaystyle=-\hbar\omega_{d}m

(17)

\displaystyle+\hbar\omega_{q}\left[3\cos^{2}\theta-1+\eta\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2\phi\right]

\displaystyle\times(3m^{2}-2)

Here
\omega_{d}
and
\omega_{q}
are the deuteron Larmor and quadrupolar frequencies, respectively. The angle
\theta
is the polar angle between the molecular bond and the external magnetic field. The azimuthal angle
\phi
and the asymmetry parameter
\eta
characterize the orientation of the deuteron’s electric field gradient.

The observable
\Delta m=\pm 1
transitions occur at the frequencies

\displaystyle\omega_{+}

\displaystyle=\omega_{d}+3\omega_{q}(3\cos^{2}\theta-1),

(18)

\displaystyle\omega_{-}

\displaystyle=\omega_{d}-3\omega_{q}(3\cos^{2}\theta-1),

(19)

producing the characteristic Pake doublet
[
Pake1948
]
shown in Fig.
1
: two partially overlapping absorption lines
I_{+}(\omega)
and
I_{-}(\omega)
. The line
I_{+}
corresponds to the
m=+1\to m=0
transition at frequency
\omega_{+}
, while
I_{-}
corresponds to the
m=0\to m=-1
transition at frequency
\omega_{-}
. The two lines are symmetric about
\omega_{d}
but differ in intensity due to the distribution of crystallite orientations in the polycrystalline sample.

Figure 1
:
Example NMR lineshape of a spin-1 target with non-cubic symmetry, showing the two overlapping absorption lines
I_{+}
(red) and
I_{-}
(green).

Under thermal equilibrium conditions, the system satisfies Boltzmann statistics and the ratio of the absorption line intensities
r=I_{+}/I_{-}
uniquely determines both polarizations
[
Dulya1997
,
Keller2017
]
:

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2}+r+1},

(20)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}-2r+1}{r^{2}+r+1},

(21)

Experimentally, the polarizations can be extracted from the integrated absorption line areas
I^{\pm}=\int I_{\pm}(\omega)\,d\omega
:

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=C\,(I^{+}+I^{-}),

(22)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=C\,(I^{+}-I^{-}),

(23)

where
C
is a calibration constant determined under thermal-equilibrium conditions. These relations continue to hold for AFP-manipulated spectra provided that the component areas
I^{\pm}
are obtained from a physically constrained decomposition of the overlapping lines. By contrast, Eqs. (
20
)–(
21
) require global thermal equilibrium.

The analysis of AFP-manipulated NMR signals relies on three fundamental principles established for selective semi-saturating RF manipulation in spin-1 systems
[
Keller2017
,
Clement:2023eun
]
.
First,
differential binning
partitions the frequency domain into arbitrary bins so that each bin can be treated as a locally well-defined subset of the total population. Second, the
rates response
quantifies how RF irradiation in a given frequency region reduces the area of the affected absorption line while producing a compensating increase in the opposing line with one-half the magnitude, as derived in Sec. 4 of Ref.
[
Clement:2023eun
]
. Third,
spin-temperature consistency
requires that overlapping absorption lines at fixed frequency maintain a consistent intensity ratio
r_{s}(R)
corresponding to a unique
local
effective spin temperature, even when the system is driven out of global thermal equilibrium, as described in Sec. 5 of Ref.
[
Clement:2023eun
]
. Together, these principles enable decomposition and tracking of the evolving sublevel populations during AFP sweeps, and they motivate the fitting model introduced in Sec.
4.2
.

5
Spin-1 lineshape theory

The deuteron NMR lineshape in quadrupolar-broadened systems is derived in Abragam
[
Abragam1961
]
and is commonly represented in practice by the Dulya fit function
[
Dulya1997
]
, which accounts for both quadrupolar splitting and dipolar broadening effects. We define the dimensionless normalized frequency variable as

R=\frac{\nu-\nu_{d}}{3\nu_{Q}},

(24)

where
\nu
is the measured frequency,
\nu_{d}
is the central (Larmor) frequency of the deuteron, and
\nu_{Q}
is the quadrupolar frequency scale that determines the splitting of the two allowed transitions. In terms of
R
, the lineshape for each transition is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f(R,\epsilon)={}&\frac{1}{2\pi X}\Bigg[2\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(\arctan\!\left(\frac{Y^{2}-X^{2}}{2YX\sin(\alpha/2)}\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\\
&+\sin\!\left(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\right)\ln\!\left(\frac{Y^{2}+X^{2}+2YX\cos(\alpha/2)}{Y^{2}+X^{2}-2YX\cos(\alpha/2)}\right)\Bigg],\end{aligned}

(25)

where

\displaystyle X^{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+(1-\epsilon R-\eta\cos(2\phi))^{2}},

(26)

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\sqrt{3-\eta\cos(2\phi)},

(27)

\displaystyle\cos\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\epsilon R-\eta\cos(2\phi)}{X^{2}}.

(28)

Here,
\Delta
denotes the Lorentzian dipolar width,
\eta
is the
asymmetry parameter, and
\phi
is the azimuthal angle of the molecular axis
relative to the magnetic field. More details can be found in

[
Abragam1961
,
Cohen1957
]
. The parameter
\epsilon=\pm 1
specifies the peak orientation, with
\epsilon=+1
placing the tip to the right of
R=0
and
\epsilon=-1
placing it to the left.

The observed spectrum consists of the two overlapping transitions

I_{\pm}
, weighted by the population differences
(n_{+1}-n_{0})
and

(n_{0}-n_{-1})
defined via Eqs. (
7
)–(
9
).

In thermodynamic equilibrium, the intensity ratio
r=I_{+}/I_{-}

uniquely determines both polarizations via
Eqs. (
20
)–(
21
). The calibration
constant
C
is then obtained from a thermal-equilibrium measurement
using Eqs. (
22
)–(
23
)
[
Dulya1997
]
.
Once established,
C
is held fixed and combined with the joint manipulated-lineshape fit of Sec.
4.2
to extract
the vector and tensor polarizations during AFP sweeps.

6
Optimization

This section summarizes a practical procedure for selecting the RF amplitude and circuit bandwidth required for efficient deuteron AFP under the operating conditions listed in Table
1
. In the rotating frame, the magnetization follows the effective field

\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{eff}}=(B_{1},\,0,\,B_{0}-\omega/\gamma_{d})
, where
B_{1}
is the transverse RF field
amplitude and
\omega=2\pi f
is the instantaneous angular frequency.

Using the adiabaticity parameter
A
defined in
Eq. (
4
), and writing the sweep rate as

\mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}t=2\pi\,\Delta f/\tau
,
the transverse RF field required to achieve a given
adiabaticity is

B_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{A}{\gamma_{d}^{\,2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\mathrm{d}t}}.

(29)

This yields a target transverse field of roughly
2\text{--}5~\mathrm{G}
for representative adiabaticity values
A\sim 10\text{--}30
.

To translate this into a power scale, one may relate
B_{1}
to the RF coil current. For a short
solenoidal coil with turn density
n
, the sample-region field satisfies
B_{1}\approx\mu_{0}nI
.
The corresponding real power dissipated in the coil is

P_{\mathrm{coil}}=I_{\mathrm{rms}}^{2}R_{\mathrm{eff}}
, where
R_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the effective RF
resistance at
f_{0}
(including skin effect and series losses). For typical AFP coils at

\mathcal{O}(30~\mathrm{MHz})
, the required
B_{1}
corresponds to RF currents of order

I\sim\mathcal{O}(1~\mathrm{A})
and real coil powers of order
P_{\mathrm{coil}}\sim 1
–
5~\mathrm{W}
.
This is well below the maximum available amplifier power, so the operating point is set
empirically by increasing the drive until a clear efficiency plateau is reached while
maintaining acceptable reflected power and cryogenic heat load.

Finally, the resonant bandwidth must be sufficient to avoid underdriving the sweep endpoints.
For a tuned circuit with quality factor
Q
, the approximate bandwidth is

\Delta f_{\mathrm{BW}}\simeq f_{0}/Q
. A convenient design criterion is therefore

\Delta f_{\mathrm{BW}}\gtrsim\Delta f
, i.e.
Q\lesssim f_{0}/\Delta f

(Table
1
). In practice, the matching/tuning network is adjusted to (i) center
the resonance near
f_{0}
, (ii) achieve a moderate
Q
consistent with sweep coverage, and (iii)
minimize reflected power over the full sweep.

A proper impedance-matching network (e.g. series/parallel capacitors or an L-network) should be used to transform the coil+tank impedance to
50~\Omega
at
f_{0}
, thereby maximizing power transfer and minimizing reflections in the coaxial line.

Practical optimization strategy

Putting these elements together, an efficient AFP implementation under the present conditions proceeds as follows:

1.

Set the sweep parameters:

Choose the sweep width
\Delta f
and duration
\tau
based on the inhomogeneous linewidth and desired AFP speed. Here
\Delta f=400~\mathrm{kHz}
and
\tau=0.2~\mathrm{s}
fix
\mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}t
and hence the required
B_{1}
via the adiabaticity condition.

2.

Design the coil and tank:

Construct a coil that closely surrounds the deuterated butanol sample (to maximize filling factor) and choose the tuning and matching network such that:

(a)

The resonance is centered at
f_{0}=32.7~\mathrm{MHz}
.

(b)

The effective
Q
is of order
50
–
80
so that the bandwidth
\Delta f_{\text{BW}}\sim f_{0}/Q
comfortably covers the
400~\mathrm{kHz}
AFP sweep.

3.

Estimate and then measure
B_{1}
:

Using the coil geometry, estimate the
B_{1}
per ampere via
B_{1}\approx\mu_{0}nI
. From the adiabatic condition, target
B_{1}\sim 2
–
5~\mathrm{G}
, corresponding to
I\sim 0.7
–
1.2~\mathrm{A}
in the example geometry. From this and a measured or estimated
R_{\text{eff}}
, deduce that a few watts of RF power at the coil are sufficient.

4.

Use the amplifier conservatively:

Since the LZY-1X+ can deliver
\mathcal{O}(30~\mathrm{W})
and the AFP requires only
\mathcal{O}(1
–
5~\mathrm{W})
, reduce the drive from the signal generator (and/or insert fixed attenuators) such that the forward power at the coil, monitored via the directional coupler, lies in the desired range. Operating with large safety margin reduces RF heating of the sample and minimizes the risk of coil or cable damage.

5.

Fine-tune empirically:

With the
B_{1}
and power in the correct ballpark, optimize the AFP by:

(a)

Adjusting the sweep amplitude and center to fully cover the inhomogeneous resonance.

(b)

Slightly varying the RF amplitude and sweep rate to maximize polarization inversion efficiency while monitoring the temperature rise and cavity pressure (to ensure acceptable heat load at
1~\mathrm{K}
).

(c)

Checking reflected power at the coupler to confirm good impedance matching during the sweep.

In summary, for deuterated butanol at
1~\mathrm{K}
and
5~\mathrm{T}
with a
400~\mathrm{kHz}
sweep completed in
0.2~\mathrm{s}
around
32.7~\mathrm{MHz}
, the adiabatic condition implies a required transverse field of only a few gauss. For a realistic AFP coil, this corresponds to RF currents of order
1~\mathrm{A}
and real RF powers of order
1
–
5~\mathrm{W}
dissipated in the coil. The available
\sim 30~\mathrm{W}
from the amplifier therefore provides ample margin, and the optimization problem becomes one of carefully tuning
B_{1}
,
Q
, and matching to achieve robust adiabatic inversion with minimal heating and maximal efficiency.

7
Uncertainty Propagation

The statistical uncertainty assigned to each digitized point of an NMR spectrum is obtained from the noise level in the signal tails, where no absorption is present. Let
y_{i}
denote the background-subtracted NMR signal amplitude at frequency point
R_{i}
. Points with
|R|>4
are selected and a linear baseline
b(R)
is fitted to remove small offsets or slopes. The noise level is then defined as the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of the baseline-subtracted tail residuals,

\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{\rm tail}}\sum_{|R_{i}|>4}\left(y_{i}-b(R_{i})\right)^{2}}.

(30)

This uniform
\sigma
defines the statistical weight of each data point in the joint nonlinear least-squares fit of Sec.
4.2
. For the irradiated deuterated butanol sequence analyzed in Sec.
8.1
, the calibration constant
C
is determined from the final ten pre-AFP thermal-equilibrium spectra. Each spectrum
k
provides an independent estimate
C_{k}
. The weighted mean calibration constant is

C=\frac{\sum_{k}C_{k}/\sigma_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}1/\sigma_{k}^{2}},\qquad\sigma_{C}=\left(\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(31)

The resulting
C\pm\sigma_{C}
is applied to all spectra in the AFP sequence. For each AFP spectrum, the joint fit returns the best-fit parameter vector
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
and covariance matrix
V_{\theta}
. The integrated component areas
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
are smooth functions of
\bm{\theta}
, so their covariance matrix is obtained from the Jacobian

V_{I}=J_{I}\,V_{\theta}\,J_{I}^{\mathsf{T}},\qquad J_{I}\equiv\frac{\partial(I^{+},I^{-})}{\partial\bm{\theta}}.

(32)

Writing

V_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Var}(I^{+})&\mathrm{Cov}(I^{+},I^{-})\\
\mathrm{Cov}(I^{+},I^{-})&\mathrm{Var}(I^{-})\end{pmatrix},

(33)

and neglecting correlations between
C
and the AFP-fit parameters because
C
is obtained from separate calibration spectra, the propagated variances of
P
and
Q
are

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(P)

\displaystyle=(I^{+}+I^{-})^{2}\,\mathrm{Var}(C)

\displaystyle\quad+C^{2}\!\left[\mathrm{Var}(I^{+})+\mathrm{Var}(I^{-})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+2\,\mathrm{Cov}(I^{+},I^{-})\right],

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Q)

\displaystyle=(I^{+}-I^{-})^{2}\,\mathrm{Var}(C)

\displaystyle\quad+C^{2}\!\left[\mathrm{Var}(I^{+})+\mathrm{Var}(I^{-})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-2\,\mathrm{Cov}(I^{+},I^{-})\right].

(35)

The calibration term dominates at large signals, while the relative uncertainty in
Q
or
P
increases as the approach zero. The covariance term may be retained explicitly to extract both areas from a single constrained fit. However, to remove the off-diagonal elements of
V_{I}
,
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
are obtained from independent fits to the spectrum
[
Keller2026
]
, each targeting one transition component. Since the fits share no parameters, their covariance matrices are decoupled by construction and
\operatorname{Cov}(I^{+},I^{-})=0
exactly. The quality of these fits is therefore limited by the constraints from the burn and AFP conditions, making accurate prior knowledge of the manipulations essential to minimize bias. Fitting each intensity separately can reduce the statistical error in particular circumstances by may introduce bias for others, so this method is used on a case by case basis. Tests on simulated signals vs the fitted results are used to minimize systematic error in the method
[
Keller2020
,
Keller2026
]
.

8.1
Spin-1 lineshape fits

During an AFP sweep, the applied RF field can produce localized saturation at the swept frequencies, leading to
hole burning
in the overlapping spin-1 absorption lines. As established in the rates-response formalism (Sec. IV of Ref.
[
Clement:2023eun
]
), this results in a redistribution of spectral intensity as well as a net loss: depletion of one transition within a narrow frequency window is accompanied by a compensating enhancement of the opposing transition with
one-half
the depleted area at the mirrored coordinate
R\rightarrow-R
. For optimizing AFP efficiency, these effects are worth avoiding. However, here we use these highly manipulated lines to test our fitting technique. Additionally, the partial AFP pass state is also very useful for this application. In the analysis below, the fitted spectra in Fig.
2
are all taken from an irradiated deuterated butanol AFP sequence and are used as an illustrative example of AFP evolution with subsequent passes. The curves shown are joint manipulated-lineshape fits using the common Dulya/Pake kernel and the coupled-response constraints of Sec.
4.2
.

shows the deuteron line after DNP but still in initial Boltzmann equilibrium, from which the calibration constant
C
and baseline polarizations are established prior to any AFP frequency sweeps. Figure

shows the intermediate state in the same AFP sequence after a full low-to-high frequency sweep, where the local depletion/gain pattern expected from the rates response is evident. Figure
2(c)
shows the fully inverted state following the subsequent half-sweep. In all three cases, the joint manipulated-lineshape model describes the data adequately throughout the AFP sequence.

Pedestal (or any selected frequency domain) flipping with AFP represents another application of a highly manipulated line. This technique provides flexible control over tensor polarization by selectively targeting different regions of the NMR spectrum. It can be technically challenging to perform the AFP sweep in a small window without also seeing additional hole burning effects. (I^{+}-I^{-})
