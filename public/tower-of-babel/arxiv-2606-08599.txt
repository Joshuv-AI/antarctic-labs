Projective subvarieties of
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds and quasi-diagonals in products of elliptic curves

Abstract




Abstract.

We study complex subvarieties
in certain non-Kähler holomorphically symplectic
manifolds
X
, called the Bogomolov-Guan manifolds.
Let
E
be an elliptic curve,
L
an ample line bundle on

E
,
S\subset E^{2}
a complex curve, and
p_{1},p_{2}
the
corresponding projections of
S
to
E
. The curve
S

is called
a quasi-diagonal
if
p_{1}^{*}L\otimes p_{2}^{*}L^{-1}

is a torsion line bundle. We show that there
are at most countably many quasi-diagonals for any

(E,L)
. Using the quasi-diagonals,
we classify the projective subvarieties in the Bogomolov-Guan
manifold. The Bogomolov-Guan manifold
is equipped with a Lagrangian fibration

\pi:\;X\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
. We show that
an irreducible complex subvariety
Z\subset X

is Moishezon if and only if
\pi(Z)
is
a point or a certain complex curve which
is described in terms of quasi-diagonals.
This is used to prove that for a general
Bogomolov-Guan manifold, any projective
subvariety belongs to a fiber of
\pi
.

1
Introduction

1.1
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds

In an MPIM preprint
[
To
]
,
A. Todorov claimed that any compact,
simply connected holomorphically symplectic manifold with

h^{2,0}=1
is Kähler.
However, in
[
Gu1
,
Gu2
,
Gu3
]
, D. Guan constructed examples of
manifolds which are compact, simply connected, holomorphically symplectic
but non-Kähler. These examples, constructed from nilmanifolds, were later studied further and explained by F. Bogomolov (
[
Bo
]
), who used the Hilbert scheme of
the Kodaira surface. We call these complex manifolds
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds
or
BG-manifolds
,
and they are the central objects of study in this paper.

Although Bogomolov’s construction of the Bogomolov-Guan manifolds
is quite intricate (see Section
3.7
for the details)
a step-by-step analysis reveals that their geometry resembles, in many respects, that of irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds. In particular, these manifolds have even dimensions, and the first example of such a manifold occurs in dimension 4.

The first systematic study of the geometric properties of Bogomolov-Guan manifolds was carried out by Kurnosov and Verbitsky in
[
KV
]
.
They proved that the holomorphically symplectic deformations of
these manifolds are unobstructed, and demonstrated
that they admit an analogue of the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki
form.

Furthermore, it was shown in
[
BKKY
]
that the algebraic reduction
of a
2n
-dimensional Bogomolov-Guan manifold
BG
is a morphism

P\colon BG\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
to a projective space of half the dimension.
The generic fiber of
P
is an abelian variety, and the symplectic
form vanishes on the fibers; thus,
BG
admits a Lagrangian fibration.
The study of algebraic subvarieties in
[
BKKY
]
also revealed a
strong restriction: any subvariety of
BG
whose image under the
Lagrangian fibration
P
has dimension at least 2 cannot be projective.

1.2
Bogomolov-Guan precursors

Bogomolov’s construction of Bogomolov-Guan manifolds is long and complicated. Therefore, in this paper, we focus on their precursors: these are complex manifolds that are simpler to construct but closely related to the original ones.

As in the original construction of Bogomolov, we start from a
(primary) Kodaira surface
{\operatorname{Kod}}
(
3.4
),
which can be obtained as follows.
Let
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)
be the total space of all non-zero vectors
in an ample bundle over an elliptic curve
E
; it is a
principal
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fibration over
E
. Let
{\mathbb{Z}}
act on

\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)
by homothety, multiplying each vector
by a fixed complex number
\lambda
, with
|\lambda|>1
.
The Kodaira surface is defined as the quotient
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)/{\mathbb{Z}}
;
it is a holomorphic principal elliptic fibration over an elliptic curve.

The precursor is constructed as follows. We fix a Kodaira surface

{\operatorname{Kod}}
and consider its algebraic reduction
\pi\colon{\operatorname{Kod}}\to E
, where both the base
E
and the general fiber
E_{1}
are elliptic curves.
Let
\sigma\colon E^{n+1}\to E
be the morphism

\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n+1}
.
We denote by
D
the fiber of the composition

\sigma\circ\pi^{n+1}\colon{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}\to E
over a point
e\in E
.
The natural action of
E_{1}
on
{\operatorname{Kod}}
induces a diagonal action on
D
.
The quotient
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n}/E_{1}
is called
the unrestricted Bogomolov-Guan precursor
,
and
X:=D/E_{1}
is a
2n
-dimensional complex manifold which
we call a
restricted Bogomolov-Guan precursor
or
BG-precursor
.
We observe that the precursor depends on the Kodaira surface
{\operatorname{Kod}}

and on the integer
n
.

The Bogomolov-Guan precursor
X
inherits rich geometric properties.
The holomorphic symplectic form on the Kodaira surface
{\operatorname{Kod}}
induces
a holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega
on
X
. Furthermore, if we denote by
A
the
fiber of
\sigma\colon E^{n+1}\to E
over
e
, then
the precursor admits a natural map
P_{X}\colon X\to A\subset E^{n+1}
, which coincides with the
Lagrangian fibration on
X
with respect to
\Omega
. It is easy to observe that
A\cong E^{n}
.

The construction of the actual Bogomolov-Guan manifold

BG
is similar, but it replaces the
(n+1)
-fold product
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}

with the Hilbert scheme of
n+1
points on
{\operatorname{Kod}}
. In addition, after taking
the quotient by the diagonal action of
E_{1}
, one must pass to a specific
finite cover to resolve the singularities. For details, see Section

3.7
.

The Bogomolov-Guan manifold
BG
and its precursor
X
are related via the
action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
. Specifically,
\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
acts
naturally on
X
, and there exists a generically finite morphism

Q\colon BG\to X/\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
. This relationship is summarized by
the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{BG\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{P}
\scriptstyle{Q}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{P_{X}}
\scriptstyle{Q_{X}}
\textstyle{X/\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
\textstyle{E^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}

Here,
Q_{X}
denotes the quotient map (which is finite), while
Q
is generically finite.
The morphism
\alpha\colon E^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is defined in the following way:

\alpha\colon E^{n}\to E^{[n+1]};\ \ \ \ \ \alpha(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},-x_{1}-\dots-x_{n}\}.

The image of
\alpha
can be verified to be isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
In the following sections, we demonstrate that the subvarieties of
BG

contained in the fiber
P^{-1}(Z)
share the same geometric properties
as the subvarieties of
X
contained in

P_{X}^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}(Z))
for
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

By construction, the Bogomolov-Guan manifold
is equipped with a projective map to a finite quotient
of its precursor (
3.7
).

We are in a position to explain the starting point of the
research carried in this paper. It is not hard to see
that all positive-dimensional subvarieties of the Kodaira
surface are obtained from the fibers of the elliptic
fibration. Indeed, consider the Kodaira surface

{\operatorname{Kod}}=\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)/{\mathbb{Z}}
associated
with a line bundle
L
on an elliptic curve
E

(
3.4
), and
let
\pi:\;{\operatorname{Kod}}\to E
be the standard fibration.
Since the pullback of
L
to
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)
is trivial,
this line bundle is flat on
{\operatorname{Kod}}
, which implies that
c_{1}(\pi^{*}(L))=0
.
In other words, the pullback of the Kähler
form
\omega_{E}
on
E
to
{\operatorname{Kod}}
is exact. This implies
that no curve
S\subset{\operatorname{Kod}}
can be transversal
to fibers of
\pi
, indeed,
\int_{S}\pi^{*}\omega_{E}=0
,
which cannot happen if
TS
contains a vector which
is not tangent to the fibers of
\pi
.

This argument can be easily generalized, bringing
classification-type results for many other non-Kähler
complex manifolds
(
[
PUV
,
OV2
,
OVV
]
).

Unfortunately,
direct attempt to apply this method to BG-manifolds yields no result beyond what was already obtained in
[
BKKY
]
.
Consider the Lagrangian projection
\pi:\;X\to E^{n}
,
and let
V\subset H^{2}(E^{n})
be the space generated
by the Kähler classes of the individual elliptic curve
components in the product. Then
\pi^{*}:\;V\to H^{2}(X)

has a rank 1 kernel. Using the standard arguments
(
4.2
,
Step 1), this can be used to show that for any positive-dimensional
Kähler or Fujiki class C subvariety
Z\subset X
,
the restriction map
V\to H^{2}(Z)
has the same kernel
as
\pi^{*}:\;V\to H^{2}(X)
. Translated to geometry,
this implies that
\pi(Z)
is a curve, and its
projection to components of
E^{n+1}\supset E^{n}

has the same degree (
4.2
).
This result, originally proven in
[
BKKY
]
,
gives a complete characterization of Kähler and Fujiki
class C subvarieties in precursors, and, therefore, in
BG-manifolds.

To continue this work further, we focus on algebraic
(projective or Moishezon) subvarieties in BG-manifolds and
their precursors. In this case, we work with pullbacks
of non-torsion line bundles instead of pullbacks of the
Kähler forms. It is not hard to see
that a pullback of a non-torsion
line bundle under a dominant map

X\to Y
cannot become torsion,
if
X
and
Y
are Kähler or
Fujiki class C
(
2.5
).
This gives a more granular form of
Blanchard’s theorem (
3.1
)
on Kählerness of principal toric bundles.
As explained in
[
Hö
]
(see also
Subsection
3.1
),
holomorphic principal toric bundles on
M

are classified by
H^{1}(M,{\cal T})
, where
{\cal T}

is a sheaf of holomorphic maps to an
n
-dimensional
compact complex torus.
The coboundary map in the standard exact sequence (
3.1
)

0\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}H^{0,1}(X)^{n}\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})\to H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})\to...

produces a collection of classes in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})

called
the Chern classes
of the principal toric fibration.
Blanchard proved (
3.1
)
that the total space of a principal toric bundle
over a Kähler manifold is Kähler if and only
if its Chern classes are torsion. We prove that
it is projective if and only if the corresponding classes
in
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
are torsion
(
3.1
).
Applied to BG-precursors, this implies
the following classification of projective and Moishezon
subvarieties in a BG-precursor
\pi:\;X\to E^{n}
.

Theorem 1.1:

Let
\pi:\;X\to E^{n}
be a BG-precursor
associated with a curve
E
and an ample line bundle
L

as above. Denote by
L_{ij}
,
i,j=1,...,n+1

the bundle
p^{*}_{i}(L)\otimes p^{*}_{j}(L^{-1})
, where
p_{i}:\;E^{n+1}\to E

denotes the projection to the
i
-th factor, and
the base
E^{n}
of the BG-precursor is considered
as a subvariety in
E^{n+1}
.
Let
Z\subset X
be an irreducible subvariety of
positive dimension in a BG-precursor.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\pi(Z)
is a curve, and the pullback of

L_{ij}
to
\pi(Z)
is a torsion line bundle;

(ii)

Z\subset X
is projective.

Proof:
4.3
,

4.3
.

Remark 1.2:
This statement is true for restricted
precursors, as well as for the unrestricted ones.

This theorem naturally leads us to the main notion
of this paper — quasi-diagonals in a product of elliptic curves.

1.3
Quasi-diagonals in a product of elliptic curves

Fix an elliptic curve
E
and a line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}_{d}(E)
,
where
d\geqslant 1
and consider
an irreducible reduced curve
S
in the direct product
E^{n}

and denote by
p_{i}\colon S\to E
the projection to the
i
-th
component of the product. We say that
S
is a
quasi-diagonal
over
(E,L)

if the line bundle
p_{i}^{*}L\otimes p_{j}^{*}L^{-1}
is a torsion
element in
\operatorname{Pic}_{0}(S)
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n
.
If, moreover,
S
lies in the fiber of the map
\sigma\colon E^{n}\to E

such that
\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
, then
we say that
S
is a
restricted quasi-diagonal
.

As we stated in
1.2
a subvariety
Y\subset X
is projective if
and only if its projection
p(Y)\subset E^{n+1}
is either a point or a
restricted quasi-diagonal over
(E,L)
where
L
is a line bundle associated with
X
.

Quasi-diagonals in
E^{n}
are interesting geometric objects in their own right.
We construct several examples of such curves (see Section

2.2
) and establish some of their properties.
Our main result concerning quasi-diagonals is that they cannot appear
in continuous families: for a fixed line bundle
L
, the set of
quasi-diagonals in
E^{n}
over
(E,L)
is at most countable.

However, many questions on this subject remain open. One of the most intriguing
problems is how to construct new examples of quasi-diagonals and whether one
can classify them in any meaningful way. This problem is highly non-trivial
even for quasi-diagonals in
E^{2}
and a complete answer remains out of reach.
Note that in this case a quasi-diagonal
S\subset E^{2}
defines a self-correspondence
of
E
, suggesting the use of results from the theory of correspondences.

For instance, an important notion in the study of self-correspondences of curves
in
[
Bel
]
is an
irreducible complete set
: we say
that
Z\subset E
is such a set for a correspondence
S\subset E^{2}

if
p_{1}^{-1}(Z)=p_{2}^{-1}(Z)
. By
[
Bel
, Theorem 1]
, if
for a curve
S\subset E^{2}
there exist infinitely many irreducible
complete sets
Z\subset E
, then there exists a morphism
f\colon E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

such that
f\circ p_{1}=f\circ p_{2}\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
; that is,
S
is a
quasi-diagonal for the line bundle
L=f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
.
On the other hand, if the number of irreducible complete sets is
small, the self-correspondence exhibits trivial dynamics in view
of
[
Bel
, Theorem 2]
.

1.4
Quasi-diagonals and complex geometry of BG-precursor

Surprisingly, we were able to apply our argument
in the converse direction, deducing theorems about
quasi-diagonals from results on complex geometry
of Bogomolov-Guan precursors.

Let
X\to E^{n}
be a BG-precursor.
It is not hard to see that as well as the Kodaira surface,
the corresponding BG-precursor is
a nilmanifold (see Subsection

3.5
),
that is, a quotient of a nilpotent Lie group
by a cocompact, discrete subgroup acting by translations.
This nilpotent group is not a complex Lie group.
However, it carries an invariant complex structure,
obtained from a complex structure operator on
its Lie algebra. The integrability of this complex
structure is interpreted
as a Lie algebra condition
(
3.6
).

A Hermitian metric
\omega

on a complex manifold is called
pluriclosed
,
or
SKT
, if it satisfies
dd^{c}\omega=0
.

Special metrics on nilmanifolds are related
to 2-forms on their Lie algebra. If we identify the space
of invariant forms on a nilmanifold with the Grassmann
algebra of its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, de Rham differential
is induced from a map
\Lambda^{1}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\Lambda^{2}({\mathfrak{g}})

which is dual to the Lie bracket. Knowing an
explicit expression for the Lie bracket and
the complex structure, it is not hard to show
that a given 2-form is
dd^{c}
-closed.

Using this construction, we
obtain a pluriclosed form on any
BG-precursor manifold (
5.4
).

An argument based on Gromov’s compactness
(
[
V2
]
) implies then
that each component in the Barlet
moduli space of complex curves on a pluriclosed manifold

X
is compact (
5.4
).
This was used to show that all families of quasi-diagonals
on a product of elliptic curves are discrete; in particular, there
are countably many quasi-diagonals
(
5.5
).
An algebraic proof of this result is given in

1.5
.

In order to understand better the projective subvarieties in
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds and precursors, we study quasi-diagonals.
Our second result shows that quasi-diagonals never come in continuous
families.

Theorem 1.4:

Let
E
be an elliptic curve and
L
be an ample line bundle on
E
.
Then, for any integer
n\geqslant 2
,
the product

contains at most a countable set of

We provide two different proofs of this theorem
using distinct methods. In both proofs the first step is to reduce
to the case

The first proof in Section
2.5

uses purely algebro-geometric methods: we assume that there exists a family
of quasi-diagonals over
(E,L)
in

parametrized by a variety

consider the universal space

study the inverse image of the

to

On the one hand, the property of quasi-diagonals
implies that

equals a pull-back of a line bundle on

On the other hand, we show that the inverse image of the
line bundle

to

is supported on a multisection of

This leads us to a contradiction.

The second proof in Section

uses Campana’s coreduction theorem. This theorem
states that if
X
is a sufficiently good compact complex variety
then there exists a meromorphic map

which contracts
almost all Moishezon subvarieties in

. If

contains a
continuous family of quasi-diagonals then the variety

satisfies the coreduction theorem and two general points of

are connected by a chain of projective varieties similarly

to a point, which is impossible since
X

is not Moishezon (a Kodaira surface
{\operatorname{Kod}}
can be embedded in
X
).

demonstrates that quasi-diagonals are quite rare.

1.6
Structure of this paper

In Section
2
, we formally define
quasi-diagonals and restricted quasi-diagonals, provide
several explicit examples (including symmetric curves and
non-elliptic quasi-diagonals), and reduce the study of
quasi-diagonals in
E^{n}
to
E^{2}
. The section concludes
with the algebraic proof of
1.5
and states
several open questions.

In Section
3
, we review the
theory of principal toric and elliptic fibrations,
describe the construction of Kodaira surfaces as
nilmanifolds, and detail the construction of
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds and their precursors. Using the
theory of principal toric bundles, we then prove the
classification theorems for projective and Fujiki class C
subvarieties in the Bogomolov-Guan manifolds.

In Section
4
, we prove the
analogous classification result for subvarieties in the
Bogomolov-Guan precursor, completing the proof of

1.5
.

Section
5
provides a
complex-analytic perspective on our rigidity results. We
recall the definitions of Barlet spaces, Campana’s
coreduction map, and SKT (pluriclosed) metrics. We show
that the Bogomolov-Guan precursor admits an SKT
metric. Using Gromov’s compactness theorem for Barlet
spaces, we provide an independent geometric proof of

1.5
.

Finally, Appendix
A
presents a detailed analysis of the preimages of
general lines in a
4
-dimensional Bogomolov-Guan
manifold, demonstrating that they do not yield
quasi-diagonals in general and further illustrating the
scarcity of restricted quasi-diagonals.

2
Quasi-diagonals in a product of elliptic curves

2.1
Quasi-diagonals: definition and basic properties

Remark 2.1:
In this paper we assume that
an elliptic curve

has a fixed point and a group operation, and

a 1-dimensional complex torus
is
a 1-dimensional smooth complex curve of genus 1.
This distinction will be important in the sequel.

Definition 2.2:
The group of line bundles on a complex manifold
X
is denoted by
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
,
and is called
the Picard group
. When

we have

Each

Remark 2.3:
Let

on

coincides with the action induced by the action of

Definition 2.4:
Let

be an ample line bundle on an elliptic curve

Let

denote the projection to the

-th component.

is a curve

such that for all

Remark 2.5:
The degree of the bundle

is equal to

,

Therefore, the map

One of the aims of the present paper is the following
theorem.

Theorem 2.6:
There are at most countably many quasi-diagonals for any

Proof:
2.5

Remark 2.7:
The notions we discussed in this section so far are
“identity agnostic”: they are
defined for a 1-dimensional complex torus, without
fixing the unity. The notions which are identity agnostic

The definition of

Definition 2.8:

Remark 2.9:

Example 2.14:

Here we give an example of a quasi-diagonal which is not an elliptic curve.
Let

2.5
Families of quasi-diagonals

The main result of this subsection is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.22:

Let
E
be an elliptic curve and

an ample line bundle on

.
Then

contains at most countably many quasi-diagonals over

We prove this result at the end of this
subsection. Before,
we notice that

has an immediate corollary.

Corollary 2.23:

Let
E
be an elliptic curve.
Then the set of line bundles
L\in\operatorname{Pic}_{m}(E)
such that there exists a restricted quasi-diagonal in
E^{n}
for

is at most countable.

Proof. Step 1:
The group
E
acts on itself by
translations; we denote an automorphism
associated with

by

. Clearly, for any element

this action takes quasi-diagonals

over
(E,L)
to quasi-diagonals over
(E,\tau_{-x}^{*}L)
.

Step 2:

Fix an ample line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}_{m}(E)
over

Let

and let
{\mathfrak{Q}}_{z}(E,L)

denote the set of quasi-diagonals

over

which satisfy

\sigma:\;E^{n}\to E
is the

Clearly,
\tau_{x}({\mathfrak{Q}}_{z}(E,L))={\mathfrak{Q}}_{mx}(E,\tau_{-x}^{*}L)

Since

acts on

transitively, for each

there exists

such that

\tau_{-x}^{*}L^{\prime}=L
. Therefore, for any

over

the curve

is a quasi-diagonal over

(E,L)
, and satisfies

If the set of restricted quasi-diagonals
is non-empty for noncountably many

for non-countably many points

This implies that the set of quasi-diagonals over

Lemma 2.24:

Let

Denote by

the projection to the first component of the product.
Denote by
\pi\colon X\to T
the projection to the second component of the product.
Note that the image

cannot be 1-dimensional, because a 1-dimensional subvariety of

contains only finitely many irreducible components, whereas our family of quasi-diagonals is uncountable. Since

is irreducible, the map

Replace

by their resolutions of singularities if necessary.
For each

.
Finally, denote by

the composition
\pr_{i}\circ\alpha
.

By assumption there exists an open subset

such that
for any

the image of the map restriction
\alpha_{t}=\alpha|_{X_{t}}\colon X_{t}\to E^{2}

is a quasi-diagonal such that
\alpha_{t}^{*}(\pr_{1}^{*}{L})=\alpha_{t}^{*}(\pr_{2}^{*}{L})
.
This implies that the restriction of the line

P_{1}^{*}L\otimes P_{2}^{*}L^{-1}
to a general fiber

of

\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}
.
Thus, there exists a line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on

and

such that one has the following equality:

P_{1}^{*}({L})\otimes P_{2}^{*}({L}^{-1})=\pi^{*}\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(E_{1}-E_{2}).

(2.3)

Step 3:
There exist a point

and an integer

such that
{L}=\mathcal{O}_{E}(Me)
. Note that replacing

by

and replacing

such that the difference

is

we can assume
that the preimage of

under

and
P_{2}
is irreducible in

.
On the other hand, the equality (
2.3
) stays true.

Consider the surjective map onto the curve

P_{i}\colon X\to E
and denote by

the divisor
P_{i}^{-1}(e)
.
Condition (
2.3
) implies that there exist a

and integers

for

such that

is a proper subvariety in

and
the following equality holds in the free abelian

M(D_{1}-D_{2})-(f^{-1}(0)-f^{-1}(\infty))=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\xi_{i},

(2.4)

Step 4:
The divisor

is not linearly equivalent
to

since otherwise so is

and
2.5

In addition, the image

. Otherwise, there exists a point

implies that in this case

factors through

i.e.

Step 5:

Passing to a power of the bundle

3
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds

3.1
Principal toric and elliptic fibrations

Definition 3.1:
Let
M
be a complex manifold equipped with a free holomorphic action
of an elliptic curve
E
. Then
M
is called

a principal elliptic fibration
, over the base
M/E
.

Remark 3.2:

Let
\pi:\;M\to X
be a smooth holomorphic fibration
with fibers isomorphic to a given elliptic curve. Assume
that the monodromy of the corresponding Gauss-Manin local
system is trivial. Then the Fischer-Grauert theorem

[
FG
]

implies that
M
admits a structure of a principal
elliptic fibration over
X
.

Definition 3.3: A principal toric bundle
is a complex manifold
equipped with a free holomorphic action
of a compact complex torus.
Just like for elliptic fibrations,
a principal toric bundle
M
with an action
of
T
is holomorphically fibered over
M/T
.

Geometry and topology of principal toric bundles
were explored at great length in
[
Hö
]
.
Clearly, the set of isomorphism classes of
principal
T
-bundles over a complex manifold
X

is identified with
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
, where
{\mathbb{T}}
is the
sheaf whose group of sections
{\mathbb{T}}(U)
over an
open set
U\subset X
is the group of holomorphic
maps from
U
to
T
, considered as a complex Lie group.
Suppose that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T=n
, and let
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2n}=\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

be the corresponding lattice. To compute
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
,
we consider the following exact sequence of sheaves over
X
:

0\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{n}\to{\mathbb{T}}\to 0.

The corresponding long exact sequence gives

0\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}H^{0,1}(X)^{n}\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})\to H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})\to\dots

(3.1)

The
2n
classes in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}^{2n})
associated with
a given principal
T
-bundle are called
the Chern
classes
of this
T
-bundle.

Example 3.4:

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over a complex manifold
X
,
and
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)
the space of non-zero vectors in its total space,
considered as a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fibration over
X
.
Fix a complex number
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
|\lambda|>1
.
The group
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)
, transitively on
fibers. Consider the quotient
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L)/\langle\lambda\rangle
;
it is a principal elliptic bundle on
X
, with fibers
elliptic curves isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/\langle\lambda\rangle
.
It is easy to see that the corresponding Chern classes are
(0,c_{1}(L))
.

Theorem 3.5:

(Blanchard)

Let
\pi:\;M\to X
be a holomorphic principal toric fibration,
with
X
Kähler. Then
M
admits a Kähler structure
if and only if the Chern classes of
\pi
vanish in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

(equivalently, the Chern classes of
\pi
are torsion).

Proof:
[
Bl
]
.

In order to describe the properties of varieties which are going to arise
further, we need the following definition.

Definition 3.6:
A complex variety is called
Fujiki class C

if it is bimeromorphic to a Kähler manifold.
A complex variety is called
Moishezon

if it is bimeromorphic to a projective manifold.

Theorem 3.7:

Let
\pi:\;M\to X
,
X=M/T
be a holomorphic principal toric fibration,
with
X
and
T
projective. Assume that the Chern classes
of
\pi
are torsion. Then
M
is projective if and
only if the corresponding class in
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})

is torsion.

Proof:
If
M
is a projective variety then there exists a
multisection of
\pi
, that is,
an irreducible projective subvariety
Z\subset M

such that
\pi(Z)=X
and the map
\pi|_{Z}\colon Z\to X

is generically finite.

Consider the fiber product
M_{Z}=M\times_{X}Z
with the induced
principal toric fibration
\pi_{Z}\colon M_{Z}\to Z
. This fibration
admits a global section; thus, it is isomorphic to
a direct product
M_{Z}\cong Z\times T
where
T
is the fiber of

\pi
.

The class of the fibration
\pi_{Z}
vanishes and equals the image of
the class of the fibration
\pi
under the inverse image map
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})\to H^{1}(Z,{\mathbb{T}})
.
By (
3.1
), this
is possible only if the class of the fibration
\pi
is torsion.

Note that this proof works for a complex manifold
M
equipped
with a principal toric fibration: once
we have a multisection we observe that the inverse image of the fibration is a product.

In order to prove another implication, we need the following construction.
Recall that if
\zeta
is a cocycle in
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
and
M_{\zeta}
is a principal
toric bundle associated to
\zeta
over
X
then for any positive integer
k

there exists the following surjective unramified map:

\alpha\colon M_{\zeta}\to M_{k\zeta}.

One can define
\alpha
on local trivializations
U\times T
; we set
\alpha((u,t))=(u,k\cdot t)

and one can easily check that this map is compatible with the cocycle.

Now if
\pi\colon M\to X
is a principal toric fibration whose
c_{1}(\pi)
is torsion,
and corresponding class
\zeta\in H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
is torsion then by the above construction
there exists a surjective unramified map
\alpha\colon M\to X\times T
. By our assumption
the product
X\times T
is projective. Therefore, so is
M
since the inverse image of an ample
line bundle under
\alpha
is ample on
M
.

Finally, if
M
is a Moishezon variety then one can construct a subvariety
Y\subset M
such that
the map
\pi|_{Y}\colon Y\to X
is generically finite. Thus,
M\times_{X}Y
is isomorphic
to
Y\times T
. Therefore, the cocycle in
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{T}})
which defines the fibration
M
is torsion, following the same logic as in the first part of the proof.
We have shown that
M
is Moishezon if and only if
M
is projective.

3.2
Principal toric fibrations associated with
holomorphic line bundles

The constructions which are given in this subsection
are central in locally conformally Kähler geometry,

[
OV1
]
; we already used them when we
defined the Kodaira surface.

Definition 3.8:
Let
L_{1},...,L_{n}
be a collection of line bundles on a
complex manifold
M
,
and
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
complex numbers,
|\lambda_{i}|>1
.
Consider the fibered product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L_{i})}{\langle\lambda_{i}\rangle}
;
it is a principal toric fibration with a fiber

\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}{\langle\lambda_{i}\rangle}

When
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=\lambda
, we denote it
as
R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},...,L_{n})
.

Proposition 3.9:

Consider the projection

P:\;R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},...,L_{n}){\>\longrightarrow\>}M
.
Then
P^{*}(c_{1}(L_{i}))=0
.

Proof:
To prove that

P^{*}(c_{1}(L_{i}))=0
, it would suffice to show that

P^{*}L_{i}
has a nowhere vanishing section on
\prod_{M}S_{i}
.
This is clear because
for each
(t_{1},...,t_{n})\in\prod_{M}S_{i}
, the point

t_{i}
defines a unit vector in
P^{*}L_{i}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{(t_{1},...,t_{n})}}\right.}
.

Lemma 3.10:

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two line bundles on a smooth complex manifold
M
. If
F
is the elliptic curve
F={\mathbb{C}}^{*}/\langle\lambda\rangle
with the diagonal action on
R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},L_{2})
, then one has the following isomorphism:

R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},L_{2})/F\cong R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{-1}).

Proof:
The lemma is proved in
[
BKKY
, Lemma 6.4]
, we repeat the proof here since
it is easy and important.

We further denote by
\mathcal{L}
the line bundle
\mathcal{L}=L_{1}\otimes L_{2}^{-1}
. Let
\Phi
be the following map:

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L_{1})\times_{M}\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L_{2})\to\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(\mathcal{L});

\displaystyle\Phi(p,s_{1},s_{2})=(p,s_{1}\otimes s_{2}^{\vee}).

Here
p
is a point in
M
and
s_{i}
is an element in
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L_{i})
and
s_{2}^{\vee}
is the unique linear operator on
\operatorname{Tot}(L_{2})
such that
s_{2}^{\vee}(s_{2})=1
. One can check that
\Phi
extends to a surjective map
R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},L_{2})\to R_{\lambda}(M,\mathcal{L})
. Moreover, the map
\Phi
factors through the diagonal action of
F
and the induced map
R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},L_{2})/F\to R_{\lambda}(M,\mathcal{L})
is an isomorphism.

Corollary 3.11:

Let
L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}
be
n
line bundles on a smooth complex manifold
M
. If
F
is the elliptic curve
F={\mathbb{C}}^{*}/\langle\lambda\rangle
with the diagonal action on
R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n})
, then one has the following isomorphism:

R_{\lambda}(M,L_{1},\dots,L_{n})/F\cong R_{\lambda}(M,L_{2}\otimes L_{1}^{-1})\times_{M}\dots\times_{M}R_{\lambda}(M,L_{n}\otimes L_{1}^{-1}).

Proof:
See
[
BKKY
, Lemma 6.6]
.

3.3
Hilbert schemes

For definition, basic properties and other results on
Hilbert scheme of points on a complex surface, see
[
Nak
]
.

Definition 3.12:
A
Hilbert scheme of points
on a variety
M

is the Hilbert space of ideal sheaves
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{M}

with
F:=\mathcal{O}_{M}/I
supported in a finite subset of
M
; dimension of
H^{0}(F)

is called
length
. The Hilbert scheme of points
for length
n
is denoted by
M^{[n]}
.

Remark 3.13:
When
M
is a complex surface,
M^{[n]}

is a smooth resolution of the
n
-th symmetric power
of
M
(
[
Nak
]
).

Remark 3.14:
If the surface
M
is holomorphically symplectic,

M^{[n]}
is also holomorphically symplectic (this
observation, made in
[
Bea
]
, easily follows
from Serre duality).

Remark 3.15:
The Hilbert scheme of a K3 surface is a simply connected,
holomorphically symplectic manifold. The Hilbert scheme
of a torus
T
is not simply connected, but the fiber
of its Albanese map
T^{[n]}{\>\longrightarrow\>}T

has finite fundamental group. The universal cover
of this fiber is called
a generalized Kummer variety
.

This way one obtains the two main examples of simply connected
holomorphically symplectic projective manifolds. The third
series of examples, associated with the Hilbert scheme of Kodaira
surface, is non-projective and non-Kähler.

3.4
Kodaira surface

Definition 3.16:

Let
L
be a line bundle
on an elliptic curve
E
with the first Chern class

c_{1}(L)\neq 0
. Denote by
\tilde{S}
the corresponding
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-bundle
on
E
obtained by removing the zero section,

\tilde{S}=\operatorname{Tot}(L)\backslash 0
. Fix a complex
number
\lambda
with
|\lambda|>1
, and
let
h_{\lambda}:\;\tilde{S}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\tilde{S}

be the corresponding homothety of
\tilde{S}
.
The quotient
\tilde{S}/\langle h_{\lambda}\rangle

is called
a primary Kodaira surface
,
or
Kodaira-Thurston surface
, or

Kodaira surface
.

Remark 3.17:

The Kodaira surface is a principal elliptic fibration over
the elliptic curve
E
, with the fiber
identified with the elliptic curve
E_{L}:\;={\mathbb{C}}^{*}/\langle\lambda\rangle
.
Therefore, it is holomorphically symplectic.

Remark 3.18: The secondary Kodaira surfaces
are obtained from
the primary Kodaira surfaces as quotients by a free
holomorphic action of a finite group. These surfaces
are elliptically fibered over
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
; they are not
holomorphically symplectic.

Remark 3.19:

Though
{\operatorname{Kod}}
is not a projective surface, one still can define its
Hilbert scheme of points
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{[n]}
. Denote by
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}

the
n
-th symmetric power of
{\operatorname{Kod}}
i.e. the quotient of the product

{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n}
by the natural action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Note that the symmetric power
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}
is a singular variety.
By
[
CM
, Theorem 2.3.1]
there exists
the Douady–Barlet morphism
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{[n]}\to{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}
which resolves
the singularity of
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}
. Moreover, this morphism
is projective
[
CM
, Theorem 2.3.1]
.

By
3.4
the Kodaira surface
is a principal elliptic fibration with a fiber
E_{L}
.
Since the action of
E_{L}
on
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{[n]}
and
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}
is equivariant
with respect to the Douady–Barlet morphism, the induced morphism

{\operatorname{Kod}}^{[n]}/E_{L}\to{\operatorname{Kod}}^{(n)}/E_{L}
is also projective.

3.5
Nilmanifolds

For some proofs given at the end of this paper, we need to
do some explicit computations in the de Rham algebra of a power of
Kodaira surface. This is most easy to do if we interpret the Kodaira
surface as a nilmanifold
(
[
Ha
,
OV1
]
).
Here we introduce this notion
and some basic results, mostly due to A. Maltsev (
[
Mal
]
);
for proofs and more details, see
[
CG
]
.

Definition 3.20:
Let
M
be a compact manifold equipped
with a transitive action of a nilpotent Lie group.
Then
M
is called
a nilmanifold
.

Remark 3.21:
As shown by A. Maltsev, all nilmanifolds are obtained as quotient spaces,

M=G/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\subset G
is a discrete,
cocompact subgroup, acting by translations.

Theorem 3.22:
(Maltsev)

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and
G
its Lie group. Then
G
contains a discrete
subgroup
\Gamma
such that
G/\Gamma
is compact, and

\Gamma=e^{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}}
, where
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is a lattice
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Finally, all nilmanifolds are obtained this way.

Proof:
[
Mal
]
.

Remark 3.23:
Topologically, all simply connected
nilpotent Lie groups are diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and all nilmanifolds are
iterated circle fibrations.

3.6
Kodaira surface as a nilmanifold

Definition 3.24:

An integrable complex structure
on a real Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
is a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}

such that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}\oplus\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}}={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 3.25:
Any such decomposition defines a complex structure
I

on
{\mathfrak{g}}
by
I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}}}\right.}=\sqrt{-1}\>
and

I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{0,1}}}\right.}=-\sqrt{-1}\>
.
We extend this operator to a right-invariant almost
complex structure operator
{\cal I}
on
G
.
Integrability of
{\cal I}
is equivalent to
[T^{1,0}G,T^{1,0}G]\subset T^{1,0}G
,
which is equivalent to
[{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0},{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}]\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
.

Remark 3.26:
Right-invariant complex structures on a connected real Lie group
are in 1 to 1 correspondence with integrable
complex structures on its Lie algebra.

Definition 3.27:
Let
\Gamma\subset G
be a cocompact lattice in a nilpotent Lie group.

A complex nilmanifold
is a quotient

M:=G/\Gamma
equipped with a complex structure, in such a way
that
G
has a right-invariant complex structure,
and the projection
G{\>\longrightarrow\>}M
is holomorphic.

Let
G:={\mathbb{R}}\times G_{0}
, where
G_{0}
is the 3-dimensional
real Lie group of 3x3 upper triangular matrices.
This group contains many cocompact lattices, for example
\Gamma:={\mathbb{Z}}\times\Gamma_{0}
,
where
\Gamma_{0}
is the group of strictly upper triangular
integral matrices. The corresponding
Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is generated by
x,y,z,t

with the only non-zero commutator
[x,y]=z
.

Claim 3.28:
The Kodaira surface can be equivalently
defined as
M:=G/\Gamma
with the
complex structure defined by the subalgebra

Definition 4.1:

Proposition 4.2:
The orbits of the

-action on

the characteristic foliation
\ker\Omega

Proof:
Let
\sigma:\;E^{n}{\>\longrightarrow\>}E

(z_{1},...,z_{n})\to\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}
. Then

\ker d\sigma=TD
, where

is a 1-form obtained as a
derivative of
\sigma
, which is locally defined as a holomorphic
map to
{\mathbb{C}}
. Locally in
{\operatorname{Kod}}
, we choose
a coordinate system

such that

is the
coordinate on the base of

and

the coordinate on the fiber.
By construction, the symplectic form

4.3
Complex curves in the Bogomolov-Guan precursor

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.9:

Let
X={\operatorname{Kod}}^{n}/E_{1}
be an unrestricted Bogomolov-Guan precursor, and
let
P
be its projection to the base

.
Consider an irreducible subvariety

such that

is a curve. Then

Z
is projective if and only if
P(Z)
is a
quasi-diagonal.

Remark 4.10:

If
\dim P(Z)>1
,
Z
cannot be Fujiki class C or Moishezon
by
4.2
.
If
\dim P(Z)=0
, the variety
Z
is projective because all fibers of
P

are projective. This is why we care only about the case

\dim P(Z)=1
.

Proof of
4.3
.

Step 1:

For any curve
P(Z)
, its preimage in
X
is identified
with
R_{\lambda}(M,V_{1},...,V_{n})
by
4.2
.
Let
M
be a complex curve.
We are going to prove that
R_{\lambda}(M,V_{1},...,V_{n})

contains an irreducible complex curve not contained in the fiber
of the projection
P:\;R_{\lambda}(M,V_{1},...,V_{n})\to M

if and only if all line bundles
V_{i}
are torsion,
and this is equivalent to
R_{\lambda}(M,V_{1},...,V_{n})
being projective.

Step 2:

Since
R_{\lambda}(M,V_{1},...,V_{n})
is a fibered product
of
R_{\lambda}(M,V_{i})
, to prove that it is projective
it suffices to show that

R_{\lambda}(M,V)=\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(V)/\langle\lambda\rangle
is
projective if
V
is a torsion line bundle,
and contains no irreducible
complex curves, except those in the fibers of
P
, otherwise.

Step 3:
Consider the projection

P:\;R_{\lambda}(M,V)\to M
.
A multisection

of
P
is an irreducible subvariety
Z\subset R_{\lambda}(M,V)

such that
P:\;Z{\>\longrightarrow\>}M
is finite.
For any
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0},
consider a map

\Psi:\;R_{\lambda}(M,V)\times_{M}...\times_{M}R_{\lambda}(M,V){\>\longrightarrow\>}R_{\lambda}(M,V^{\otimes d})

associated with the product map

V\times V\times...\times V{\>\longrightarrow\>}V^{\otimes d}
.
If we apply this map to a multisection of degree
d
, we obtain a section.
Since
R_{\lambda}(M,V^{\otimes d})
is a principal elliptic fibration,
it is trivialized by any section, hence
R_{\lambda}(M,V^{\otimes d})=M\times E
.
Therefore, it is projective. Restricting
\Psi
to the diagonal,
we obtain a finite map from
R_{\lambda}(M,V)
to a projective manifold,
hence existence of multisections ensures that
R_{\lambda}(M,V)
is projective;
the converse is also true: any irreducible curve, not contained in the fiber,
gives a multisection.

Step 4:

If
V
is a torsion,
\Psi:\;R_{\lambda}(M,V){\>\longrightarrow\>}R_{\lambda}(M,V^{\otimes d})=M\times E

is finite, hence
R_{\lambda}(M,V)
is projective. Conversely, if
R_{\lambda}(M,V)
is projective,
it admits a multisection, which trivializes the elliptic
fibration

R_{\lambda}(M,V^{\otimes d}){\>\longrightarrow\>}M.

This completes the proof.

Remark 4.11:
4.3

says that projective submanifolds in
the unrestricted precursor are preimages
of quasi-diagonals.
Clearly, the projective submanifolds in
the restricted precursor are preimages
of the restricted quasi-diagonals.

It is possible to study the quasi-diagonals using the tools

in

]

(see also

]
). For the
rest of this paper, we mainly focus on this approach,
discovering interesting complex-geometric properties
of the Bogomolov-Guan precursor.

5.1
The Barlet spaces and the coreduction

We start by defining the Barlet space
and listing its basic properties. We follow

[
Mag
,
BM
,
CP
]
.

Definition 5.1: Barlet spaces
are spaces of cycles, that is,
closed complex analytic subvarieties
of a given dimension in a given complex manifold
with multiplicities (positive integers)
assigned to their irreducible components.
They are similar but distinct from the
Douady spaces
, which are
spaces of closed complex analytic subspaces (possibly with nilpotents
in the structure sheaf).

Definition 5.2:
Let
M
be a metric space. Recall that

the Hausdorff metric
on
the set
{\cal C}
of closed subsets of
M

is defined as follows:
d(X,Y)
is the infimum
of all
\varepsilon
such that
X
belongs to an

\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
Y
, and

Y
belongs to an
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
X
.
When
M
is compact, the corresponding topology
on
{\cal C}
is independent of the choice
of metric on
M
as long as the topology of
M

remains the same. It is called

the Hausdorff topology
.

Remark 5.3:
The Barlet space of cycles is complex analytic, with
the topology that is induced by the Hausdorff
topology on the set of all closed subvarieties

[
Mag
,
BM
]
.

Theorem 5.4:

(Bishop)

Let
(M,I)
be a compact complex manifold, and

\omega
a Hermitian form. Fix
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let

be the Barlet space
{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}(M)

of

-cycles

such that
\int_{Z}\omega^{k}\leqslant a
.
Then
Z_{a}
is compact.

Proof:
[
Mag
,
BM
]
.

5.2

In this subsection, we introduce the coreduction map,
due to F. Campana. We follow

[

,

]
.

Definition 5.5:

if there exists a line bundle

on

is ample on all fibers of

if there exists a
bimeromorphism
\mu:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}\tilde{X}

and a projective morphism
\tilde{f}:\;\tilde{X}{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y

such that
f=\tilde{f}\circ\mu
.

Theorem 5.6:

Let
f:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
be a surjective holomorphic map of
irreducible compact complex varieties with Moishezon fibers. Assume that all
components of the Barlet space of curves on

X
are compact, and there exists a subvariety
A\subset X

such that
f(A)=Y
and
f{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{A}}\right.}
is Moishezon.
Then
X
is Moishezon.

Proof:
[
C
]
.

This leads to the following theorem

Theorem 5.7:
Let
X
be a compact complex variety, such that all
components of the Barlet space of curves on

X
are compact. Then there exists a meromorphic map

f:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
with Moishezon fibers, called
the coreduction

of

, such that for a general point
x\in X

any Moishezon subvariety passing through

lies in the fiber of

.

Proof:
[
C
]
.

The coreduction construction is surveyed and presented in
[

, Theorem 3.11, Theorem 3.12]
.

5.3
Gauduchon metric and Gauduchon theorem

Definition 5.8:
Let
(M,I,\omega)
be a complex Hermitian
n
-manifold.
The Hermitian metric
\omega
is called
Gauduchon

if
dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})=0
.

The following theorem is one of the most fundamental
and important results in the theory of complex manifolds.
Additionally,
this is one of the very few known results which work for all compact complex
manifolds, and not for some special classes.

Theorem 5.9:
(Paul Gauduchon, 1977)

Let
(M,I,\omega)
be a compact complex Hermitian
n
-manifold.
Then there exists a unique (up to a constant factor)
positive smooth function
e^{f}
such that
e^{f}\omega
is
Gauduchon.

Proof:
[
Ga
]
, see also
[
OV1
, Chapter 23]
.

Example 5.10:

Let
M=G/\Gamma
be the Kodaira surface realized as
a nilmanifold (Subsection
3.6
), with
x,y,z,t
the generators of its Lie algebra,
and the only non-trivial commutator
[x,y]=z
,
and
a,b,c,d
the dual 1-forms. The de Rham differential
is a dual map to the commutator. Consider the complex
of left-invariant differential forms on
G
, identified
with
\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}
. Using the Cartan formula, we
can express de Rham differential on
\Lambda^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}

through the commutator in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. On
\Lambda^{1}{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
,
de Rham differential is the dual map to
[\cdot,\cdot]:\;\Lambda^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Therefore, the only non-trivial differential on

\Lambda^{1}{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is given by
dc=a\wedge b
. This
gives a formula for the left-invariant
Gauduchon metric:
\omega=a\wedge b+c\wedge d
.

5.4
SKT manifolds and Barlet spaces

Definition 5.11:
A Hermitian form
\omega
on a complex manifold is
SKT

(strong Kähler torsion) or
pluriclosed

if
dd^{c}\omega=0
.

The following theorem is a 1-dimensional version of
Bishop’s theorem (
5.1
);
however, it works for the space of
pseudo-holomorphic curves on almost complex manifolds.

Theorem 5.12:
(Gromov)

Let
M
be a compact Hermitian almost complex manifold,

{\mathfrak{X}}
the Barlet space of all complex curves on
M
, and

{\mathfrak{X}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Vol}}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}{\mathbb{R}}^{>0}
the volume
function. Then
{\operatorname{Vol}}
is
proper
(preimage of
a compact set is compact).

The following corollary was originally obtained in

[
V2
]
, see also
[
V1
]
.

Corollary 5.13:

Let
M
be a complex manifold, equipped with a pluriclosed
Hermitian form
\omega
, and
X
a component of the moduli of
complex curves. Then the function
{\operatorname{Vol}}:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{R}}^{>0}

is constant, and
X
is compact.

Proof:
Since
{\operatorname{Vol}}\geqslant 0
, the set

{\operatorname{Vol}}^{-1}(]-\infty,C])
is compact for all
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
the function
{\operatorname{Vol}}
has a minimum somewhere in
X
. However, a
pluriharmonic function which has a minimum is necessarily constant
by E. Hopf’s strong maximum principle (
[
GT
]
). Therefore,

{\operatorname{Vol}}
is constant:
{\operatorname{Vol}}=A
. Now,
compactness of
X={\operatorname{Vol}}^{-1}(A)
follows from Gromov’s theorem.

Later in this section,
we are going to prove that the Bogomolov-Guan precursor is
SKT, and apply
5.4
to its Barlet space.

Let
\omega=a\wedge b+c\wedge d
be the Gauduchon metric on
{\operatorname{Kod}}
,
written in terms of the frame associated with its Lie algebra
as above, and
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}
,
i=1,...,n+1

the corresponding 1-forms on
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}
,
obtained in the same way as in the nilmanifold
description of the Kodaira surface (Subsection

3.6
),

d(c_{i})=a_{i}\wedge b_{i},\ \ d(a_{i})=d(b_{i})=d(d_{i})=0.

Consider the
Hermitian form
\omega:=\sum_{i}a_{i}\wedge b_{i}+c_{i}\wedge d_{i}

on
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}
. Since
{\operatorname{Kod}}
is Gauduchon,
dd^{c}\omega=0
.

Remark 5.14:
Let
X_{1}:={\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}/E_{1}
, where
E_{1}
acts diagonally,
and
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}X_{1}
the corresponding
holomorphic projection. Denote by

\pi_{*}:\;\Lambda^{p,q}({\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}){\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(X_{1})

the pushforward, that is, the fiberwise integration of differential
forms.
Clearly,
\pi_{*}(\omega^{2})
is a Hermitian form on
X_{1}
.
Since
\pi_{*}
commutes with
d,d^{c}
,
to show that
dd^{c}\pi_{*}(\omega^{2})=0

it would suffice to show that the (2,2)-form

dd^{c}\pi_{*}(\omega^{2})=\pi_{*}(dd^{c}\omega\wedge\omega)+\pi_{*}(d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega)=\pi_{*}(d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega)

vanishes.

The following proposition immediately implies that
X_{1}
,
and hence the Bogomolov-Guan precursor
X\subset X_{1}
, is an SKT manifold.

Proposition 5.15:
Let
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}X_{1}
be the
holomorphic projection and
\omega
be the SKT Hermitian
form defined above. Then
\pi_{*}(d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega)=0
.

Proof:
For any invariant differential form, expressed as a
Grassmann polynomial of
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}
, the fiberwise
integration is given by the operator
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\iota_{z_{i}}\iota_{t_{i}}
,
where
\iota
denotes the convolution with a vector field, and

z_{i},t_{i}
the generators of the vector fields tangent
to the components of
{\operatorname{Kod}}^{n+1}
associated with the
frame
x,y,z,t\in T{\operatorname{Kod}}
dual to
a,b,c,d\in\Lambda^{1}{\operatorname{Kod}}.

Clearly,
d\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}\wedge b_{i}\wedge d_{i}
,
and
d^{c}\omega=-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}\wedge b_{i}\wedge c_{i}
,
hence
d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega=\sum_{i<j}a_{i}\wedge b_{i}\wedge d_{i}\wedge a_{j}\wedge b_{j}\wedge c_{j}
.
After contracting with the bivector

\sum_{i}z_{i}\wedge t_{i}
dual to
\sum_{i}c_{i}\wedge d_{i}
,
this form vanishes.

We have established the following theorem.

Theorem 5.16:

The Bogomolov-Guan precursor manifold (restricted and unrestricted)
admits an SKT metric.

5.5
Countability of the number of quasi-diagonals obtained from
the coreduction

In this subsection, we apply
4.3
,

5.4

and
5.4
to show that
the number of
(E,L)
-quasi-diagonals is countable
for any given
E
and
L
; an algebraic proof
of this result is given in

2.5
.

Theorem 5.17:

There are at most countably many quasi-diagonals for any given
(E,L)
.

Proof. Step 1:
Since quasi-diagonals in
E^{n}
are obtained from
quasi-diagonals in
E^{2}
(
2.3
),
it suffices to show that there are at most countably
many quasi-diagonals in
E^{2}
.
If the number of quasi-diagonals is uncountable,
there are continuous families of quasi-diagonals, which cover
E^{2}
.
We make this assumption and show that it leads to contradiction.

Step 2:

Denote by
B
a positive-dimensional
irreducible component of the Barlet space of
quasi-diagonals in
E^{2}
,
and let
C_{t}
,
t\in B
denote the quasi-diagonals.
Then
{\cal L}_{1}
and
{\cal L}_{2}
are torsion on each
C_{t}

and satisfy
{\cal L}_{1}^{\otimes d}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{t}}
for some fixed
d
and all
t
.
Let
P:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}E^{2}
be the unrestricted
Bogomolov-Guan precursor associated with
L
; by

3.2
, we have

X=R_{\lambda}(E^{2},{\cal L}_{1}\otimes{\cal L}_{2}^{-1})
.
Consider the degree
d^{2}
map

P_{d}:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}R_{\lambda}(E^{2},{\cal L}_{1}^{\otimes d},{\cal L}_{2}^{\otimes d})
.
By
4.2
,
P_{d}^{-1}(C_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{d}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}C_{t}

is a trivial
E_{1}^{2}
-fibration. Therefore, it admits a family
of sections parametrized by
E_{1}^{2}
. These sections
produce degree
d^{2}
multisections of

P:\;R_{\lambda}(E^{2},{\cal L}_{1},{\cal L}_{2}){\>\longrightarrow\>}E^{2}
,
passing through every point of
X=R_{\lambda}(E^{2},{\cal L}_{1},{\cal L}_{2})
.

Step 3:

Using the curves constructed in Step 2, we obtain
a collection of curves connecting any two points
in
X
. Using the coreduction theorem of Campana (
5.2
),
we obtain that
X
is Moishezon, contradicting

4.2
.

Corollary 5.18:
Let
X
be a restricted Bogomolov-Guan precursor associated
with a general
(E,L)
. Then all projective,
Moishezon subvarieties of

belong to the
fiber of the Lagrangian projection.

Proof:
By
4.3
, all
projective, Moishezon subvarieties in a restricted Bogomolov-Guan precursor
project to restricted
(E,L)
-quasi-diagonals in

.
By
2.5
,
for a general

there are no restricted
(E,L)
-quasi-diagonals.

Appendix A
Preimages of lines in 4-dimensional BG

In this section, we consider the Lagrangian fibration of a
4
-dimensional Bogomolov–Guan manifold
P\colon BG\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
One can ask whether a line on the Lagrangian base
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the image of a restricted quasi-diagonal on
E^{3}
for some line bundle

L
on
E
.
2.2
provides an example of this situation.

Lemma A.1:

Let
S\subset A=\{(x,y,z)\mid x+y+z=e\}\subset E^{3}
be
the curve defined in
2.2
.
Then
\alpha(S)
is a line on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof:
The group
\langle\zeta\rangle
acts diagonally on
E^{3}
.
This action preserves the divisor
A\subset E^{3}
and
commutes with the action of symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.
Thus,
\langle\zeta\rangle
acts on
A/\mathfrak{S}_{3}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
one can check that this action is faithful.

Consider the image of the curve
S\subset A
under
\alpha
.
One can observe that each point in
\alpha(S)
is fixed
under the action of
\langle\zeta\rangle
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Therefore,
\alpha(S)
is a pointwise fixed curve
under a non-trivial action of
\langle\zeta\rangle
.
Thus, it is a line.

The situation described in
2.2
is very specific.
We are going to check whether a preimage of a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
could be a smooth curve in
E^{3}

and whether one can find a line bundle on
E
such that this smooth curve is a quasi-diagonal.

We fix the notation: let
e\in E
be the neutral point and denote by

p_{i}\colon E^{3}\to E
the projection to the
i
-th component of the product.
Denote by
A\subset E^{3}
the divisor
A=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in E^{3}\mid x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=e\}
.
Denote by
\alpha\colon A\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
the symmetrization map. The ramification divisor of
\alpha

can be described in the following way.

Lemma A.2:

Consider the embedding by the linear system

\varphi=\varphi_{|\mathcal{O}_{E}(3e)|}\colon E\to\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}(3e)))^{\vee}\cong(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}.

The curve
\varphi(E)\subset(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
is projectively dual to the ramification divisor of
\alpha
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Moreover, for each point
(x,y,z)\in A
its image
\alpha((x,y,z))\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
corresponds to the line passing through points
\varphi(x)
,

\varphi(y)
and
\varphi(z)
in
(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
.

Proof:
[
BKKY
, Lemma 7.5]
.

Fix a line
\ell\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
and consider its preimage
S_{\ell}=\alpha^{-1}({\ell})
.
Then
S_{\ell}
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-invariant curve in
A
. Further, we deduce that
S_{\ell}
is
a smooth curve if
\ell
is general, see
A
. However, if
\ell
is tangent
to the ramification divisor of
\alpha
then its preimage is reducible.

Lemma A.3:

Let
{\ell}
be a line tangent to the ramification divisor of the map
\alpha
. Then there exists a point
x\in E
such that

S_{\ell}=\left(p_{1}^{-1}(x)\cup p_{2}^{-1}(x)\cup p_{3}^{-1}(x)\right)\cap A.

Proof:
This follows from the description of the ramification divisor of
\alpha
given in
A
.

Lemma A.4:
Each element in
the linear system
|\alpha^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))|
is the preimage of a line
\ell\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}

and a general element of
|\alpha^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))|
is smooth.

Proof:
Denote the line bundle
\alpha^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))
by
L
.
Denote by
F_{i}\in NS(A)
the class of fiber
p_{i}^{-1}(x)\cap A
.
On the surface
A
one has
F_{i}^{2}=0

and
F_{i}\cdot F_{j}=1
.

By
A
we observe that
c_{1}(L)=F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}
. Then by Riemann-Roch theorem we compute
(note that
A
is a torus, hence its Chern classes
vanish and don’t contribute to the Riemann-Roch-Hirzebruch formula):

h^{0}(A,L)-h^{1}(A,L)+h^{2}(A,L)=\chi(L)=\frac{1}{2}(F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3})^{2}=3.

Moreover, since for distinct
x\in E
the preimages of

\ell_{x}
don’t have common points,
the line bundle
L
has empty base locus.
Thus, it is ample and
H^{1}(A,L)=H^{2}(A,L)=0
by Kodaira
vanishing on
A
. This implies that
H^{0}(A,L)=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))
.
Thus, the result follows by Bertini theorem.

The next assertion is the main result of this section.
It is followed by several technical lemmas which are used in its proof.

Proposition A.5:

If
{\ell}
is a line on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
S_{\ell}
is smooth, then
S_{\ell}
is not a quasi-diagonal for any line bundle
L
on the curve
E
.

Proof:
By
A
for a general line
\ell
the curve
S_{\ell}
is smooth.
Assume that
y
is a point on
E

such that the curve
S_{\ell}
is a quasi-diagonal with
respect to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}([y])
.
Then, for certain
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>1}
, we have

\left(p_{1}^{*}L\otimes p_{2}^{*}L^{-1}\right)|_{S_{\ell}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S_{\ell}}
,
here
L=\mathcal{O}(n[y])
. Since replacing
n
by its multiple preserves
the properties of the quasi-diagonals, we further assume that
n\geqslant 3
.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3}
the following sheaves on
A
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}=p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(n[y]-[x])\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-n[y]-[x])\otimes p_{3}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-[x]);

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}=p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(n[y])\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-n[y]);

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}=\left(p_{1}^{*}L\otimes p_{2}^{*}(L^{-1})\right)|_{S_{\ell}}.

These sheaves fit the following exact sequence on the surface
A
:

0\to\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}_{2}\to\mathcal{F}_{3}\to 0.

(A.1)

If
S_{\ell}
is a quasi-diagonal then

\mathcal{F}_{3}=\mathcal{O}_{S_{\ell}}
; thus,
H^{0}(A,\mathcal{F}_{3})=\mathbb{C}

is smooth.
On the other hand, if

is not a quasi-diagonal
then

.
Thus, by

and
A

below in order to check whether

is a quasi-diagonal, it suffices
to check that the map

is injective.
Since this map is indeed injective by

, we conclude the result.

Lemma A.6:
Let
x
and
y
be two points on the elliptic curve
E
and let
n\geqslant 3
be a positive integer.
Then the cohomology groups of the line bundle
\mathcal{F}_{1}=p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(n[y]-[x])\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-n[y]-[x])\otimes p_{3}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-[x])
on
A

are as follows:

H^{i}(A,\mathcal{F}_{1})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&\text{ if $i=0$ or $2;$}\\
&{\mathbb{C}}^{n^{2}-3},&&\text{ if $i=1$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Proof:
Fix a point
a\in E
, consider the elliptic curve
C_{1}=p_{1}^{-1}(a)\cap A\subset A
and restrict

\mathcal{F}_{1}
to
C_{1}
. One can check that
\deg(\mathcal{F}_{1}|_{C_{1}})=-n-2
. Since the class of
C_{1}

is nef on
A
we deduce that
H^{0}(A,\mathcal{F}_{1})=0
.

Consider the curve
C_{2}=p_{2}^{-1}(a)\cap A\subset A
and restrict the dual line bundle

\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}
to
C_{2}
. One can check that
\deg(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}|_{C_{2}})=2-n<0
.
Since the class of the curve
C_{2}
is nef on

H^{2}(A,\mathcal{F}_{1})=H^{0}(A,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1})^{\vee}=0.

Now denote by
F_{i}\subset E^{3}
the preimage of a point
x\in E
under
the projection to the
i
-th component of the product.
The Neron–Severi group of

contains classes

One can check that

Since we already have

Lemma A.7:
Let

be a point on the elliptic curve

Lemma A.8:

The map
H^{1}(A,\mathcal{F}_{1})\to H^{1}(A,\mathcal{F}_{2})
