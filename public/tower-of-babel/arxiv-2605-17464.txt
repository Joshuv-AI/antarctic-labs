Abstract
This paper investigates the spectral structure, numerical dispersion, and observability of fully discrete approximations of the one-dimensional wave equation by P k P^{k} (local) discontinuous Galerkin methods. Characterizing the coupled space-time numerical dispersion reveals a trapping mechanism that forces the group velocities of both physical and spurious modes to vanish at selected frequencies. We then establish an exponential blow-up of order exp ⁡ ( h − ( 1 − ε ) ) \exp(h^{-(1-\varepsilon)}) for the observability constant under this trapping mechanism. To overcome this divergence for arbitrary k k , we propose a spectral filtering strategy to restore uniform observability. Theoretical analysis and numerical experiments indicate that higher-order methods may facilitate this recovery by preserving a larger genuine physical frequency band, thereby reducing filtering cost and observation time.

Introduction

1.1
Observability of the Wave Equation

We consider the Cauchy problem for the one-dimensional wave equation on the whole real line
\mathbb{R}

\begin{cases}u_{tt}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)=0,&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u^{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(x,0)=u^{1}(x),&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1)

Problem (
1
) admits plane waves whose spatial wavenumber
\xi
and temporal frequency
\tau
satisfy the dispersion relation
\tau(\xi)=\pm\xi
, yielding a constant unit group velocity. This uniform propagation is the physical mechanism underlying the observability of the continuous wave equation. By observability we mean the possibility of estimating the full energy of the solution from measurements performed only on part of the domain, or on part of the boundary, over a finite time interval. Physically, all wave components must reach the observation region, yielding the energy inequality behind controllability. To formalize this, we define the conserved total energy of (
1
)

\mathcal{E}(u(\cdot,t),u_{t}(\cdot,t)):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right).

(2)

For the continuous problem (
1
), classical observability holds when the observation domain is the complement of a compact set
[
32
]
, provided the observation time
T
is larger than the diameter of the unobserved domain. As detailed in
[
26
]
, for the domain
\Omega:=\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1]
and time
T>2
, any solution with initial data
(u^{0},u^{1})\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the observability inequality

\mathcal{E}(u^{0},u^{1})\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(u(\cdot,t),u_{t}(\cdot,t))\mathrm{d}t.

(3)

Here,
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(f^{0},f^{1})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|f^{0}(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f^{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)
is the energy in
\Omega
.

Although this observability property (
3
) is dual to exact controllability
[
34
]
, our primary focus is on its behavior when the continuous wave operator is fully discretized. From the numerical-analysis viewpoint, observability provides a stringent test of a scheme by revealing what goes wrong under discretization. A method may be stable and convergent for fixed final times while still producing high-frequency numerical waves that travel too slowly, or not
at all, on the observation time scale. Uniform observability detects precisely this
defect.

1.2
Motivation and Overview of Main Results

1.2.1
Motivation

Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods are widely used in wave simulations for their arbitrarily high-order accuracy and flexibility.
However, investigating observability in the fully discrete high-order setting is highly non-trivial. The primary challenge of high-order spatial discretizations is that the dispersion relation cannot be explicitly expressed. This lack of explicit algebraic formulas prevents the direct evaluation of wave velocities, rendering classical analytical tools inapplicable. Instead, the numerical dispersion is governed by a complex
(k+1)\times(k+1)
matrix symbol that couples
k+1
discrete modes, which explains why existing observability analyses predominantly restrict to low-order frameworks. Moreover, full discretization nonlinearly intertwines this spatial matrix with temporal stepping. This space-time coupling forces group velocities to vanish and trap high-frequency waves, a phenomenon we refer to as the trapping mechanism.

1.2.2
Overview of Main results

This work overcomes the aforementioned challenges inherent to high-order discretizations and full space-time coupling. Our main results range from abstract spectral properties to concrete observability limits, filtering mechanisms, and the potential advantages of higher-order schemes. We summarize these results below, with complete details provided in Section
3
:

1.

Fully Discrete Spectral Analysis.

To handle the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
symbol matrix, Theorem
3
establishes its precise spectral decomposition, separating the propagating dynamics from non-propagative anomalies.

2.

Using this spectral decomposition, we identify the trapping mechanism. Theorem
5
proves that space-time numerical dispersion forces the group velocities of both the physical and spurious modes to vanish at some frequencies. Furthermore, we characterize the specific conditions under which these group velocities are restored to non-zero values.

1.3
Methodology

We outline the core proofs, deferring full details to Section
5
. To bypass the algebraic complexities of the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
symbol, our framework builds on two key components:

Qualitative Spectral Analysis.
Rather than attempting to explicitly solve for the roots of the high-degree dispersion relations, we first establish the intrinsic structural properties of the symbol matrix, characterizing its Hermitian positive semi-definiteness and local even symmetries. Building upon this algebraic foundation, we leverage Kato’s Analytic Perturbation Theory
[
22
, Theorem 6.1]
to guarantee the real analyticity of the eigensystem. This combined approach provides the regularity necessary to differentiate the eigenvalues and perform asymptotic analysis on the trapping mechanism at critical frequencies.

Quantitative Non-stationary Phase Analysis.
To rigorously quantify the resulting observability loss, we construct poorly propagating numerical wave packets near the highest frequency using Gevrey class
G^{s}
cut-off functions. Governed by the aforementioned trapping mechanism, these high-frequency components remain localized outside the observation region. By performing a non-stationary phase analysis and tracking the asymptotic behavior of these highly oscillatory integrals, we bypass standard polynomial estimates to derive the exponential lower bound.

-LDG Scheme

We construct the LDG scheme by rewriting the second-order wave equation (

q=u_{x}

u_{tt}-q_{x}=0,\qquad q-u_{x}=0.

(4)

We multiply the system (

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h})_{tt}w\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{I_{j}}q_{h}w_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\widetilde{q_{h}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}w^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\widetilde{q_{h}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}w^{+}_{j-\frac{1}{2}},

The matrices

(\bm{K}_{-1}^{h})^{T}=\bm{K}_{+1}^{h},\quad(\bm{K}_{0}^{h})^{T}=\bm{K}_{0}^{h},\quad(\bm{M}^{h})^{T}=\bm{M}^{h}.

2.3
Discrete energy

Based on the scheme (
8

\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\|_{\mathbb{M}^{h}}^{2}+\frac{\|\bm{U}^{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}}{4}+\frac{\|\bm{U}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}}{4}-\frac{(\Delta t)^{2}}{4}\left\|\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}.

(9)

Here, the first term is the discrete kinetic energy, the middle two terms denote the averaged potential energy, and the final term acts as a time-discretization correction. The observation energy
\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}
is simply its restriction to
\Omega

With the energy defined in (
9

Lemma 2
.

For the fully discrete scheme (
8
), the energy (
9
) is conserved

\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{n-1},\qquad n\geq 1.

3.1
Spectral Structure

Our first main result
establishes the algebraic structure of the eigensystem (

Theorem 3
.

The eigenvalue problem (
12

\{\sigma_{j}(\xi),\bm{v}_{j}(\xi)\}_{j=1}^{k+1}
forming an

\blacksquare

A unique principal branch, denoted by
(\sigma_{ph}(\xi),\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi))

\sigma_{ph}(0)=0,\qquad\quad\bm{v}_{ph}(0)\propto\bm{e}_{0},\qquad\quad\sigma_{ph}^{\prime\prime}(0)=2,

(\sigma_{sp,i}(\xi),\bm{v}_{sp,i}(\xi))

\sigma_{sp,i}(0)>0,\qquad\ \ i=1,\dots,k.

\blacksquare

The eigenvalues satisfy

\sigma_{ph}(\xi)=\sigma_{ph}(-\xi),\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{sp,i}(\xi)=\sigma_{sp,i}(-\xi),\quad i=1,\dots,k.

Theorem
3
guarantees real non-negative eigenvalues, yielding well-defined temporal frequencies. This algebraic isolation at the origin naturally categorizes the discrete modes.

Definition 4
.

The discrete temporal frequency is defined as

\omega_{m}(\xi):=\mathrm{sign}(\xi)\frac{2}{\Delta t}\arcsin{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{m}(\xi)}\Delta t}{2}\right)},\quad m\in\{ph,sp,1,\dots,sp,k\}.

The pair

with

3.2
Trapping Mechanism

Based on the physical-spurious separation in Theorem
3
,
our second main result
characterizes the asymptotic behavior of these velocities at the long-wave limit and the highest-frequency limit of
\Pi_{h}

Theorem 5
.

The group velocities exhibit the following asymptotic behaviors.

\blacksquare

For the physical mode:

\lim_{\xi\to 0}v_{g}^{ph}(\xi):=\lim_{\xi\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega_{ph}(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}\xi}=1,\qquad\lim_{\xi\to\pm\frac{\pi}{h}}v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }(k,\Delta t)=(0,h),\\[2.0pt]

\blacksquare

For the spurious modes, provided

\lim_{\xi\to 0^{\pm}}v_{g}^{sp,i}(\xi):=\lim_{\xi\to 0^{\pm}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega_{sp,i}(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}\xi}=0,\qquad\lim_{\xi\to\pm\frac{\pi}{h}}v_{g}^{sp,i}(\xi)=0,\qquad i=1,\cdots,k.

Remark 6

By characterizing this trapping mechanism in Theorem

), with
C_{T}
growing exponentially as
h\to 0

C_{T}\geq C_{1,3}(k,T,s)\exp\left(\frac{C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)}{h^{1-\varepsilon}}\right),

where the positive constants
C_{1,3}(k,T,s)
and
C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)
are independent of h.

Remark 8

We note that the constant
C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)

\lambda

Remark 9
.

Assumption (

3.4
Filtering Recovery

By Theorem
5
, the physical group velocity satisfies
v_{g}^{ph}(0)=1
. The real analyticity of
\sigma_{ph}(\xi)
thus guarantees that it is positive in a neighborhood of the origin, as formalized in the following Lemma, where the proof is given in Subsection
5.4
.

Lemma 10
.

For the
P^{k}
-LDG scheme (
8
), there exists a constant
\eta_{k}\in(0,\pi)
, independent of
h
, such that the physical group velocity satisfies

v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)>0,\qquad\text{for all}\,\,|\xi|\leq\frac{\eta_{k}}{h}.

Motivated by Lemma
10
, we set
\delta_{k}=1-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\pi}
. The band
I_{\delta_{k}}=[\frac{-(1-\delta_{k})\pi}{h},\frac{(1-\delta_{k})\pi}{h}]
then lies within the region where
v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)>0
. Consequently, there exists a constant
v_{g}(\delta_{k})>0
such that
v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)\geq v_{g}(\delta_{k})
for all
\xi\in I_{\delta_{k}}
.

Our final main result
establishes a constructive recovery principle. The following Theorem achieves this by projecting the initial data onto the physical branch and restricting it to frequencies with positive group velocities.

Theorem 11
.

For
\delta_{k}=1-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\pi}\in(0,1)
, where
\eta_{k}
is given in Lemma
10
, there exists a positive constant
T^{*}(\delta_{k})
such that for any
T>T^{*}(\delta_{k})
, the scheme (
8
) with initial data restricted to the filtered discrete space
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}
is uniformly observable

\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\leq\frac{2}{T-T^{*}(\delta_{k})}\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n},

where
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\Delta t=T/N
. Here, the filtered discrete space
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}
is defined as

\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}=\left\{\bm{U}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{C}^{k+1})\ \Big|\ \,\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{\bm{U}}(\xi))\subset I_{\delta_{k}},\ \widehat{\bm{U}}(\xi)\in\text{span}\{\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)\}\right\}.

Moreover, the critical observation time is
T^{*}(\delta_{k})=C/v_{g}(\delta_{k})
, where the positive constant
C
is independent of
h
.

Remark 12
.

The constant
C
is explicitly given in Subsection
5.4
.

Remark 13
.

While the theoretical bound
\eta_{k}
ensures that the physical group velocity remains positive within

5.1
Proof of Theorem
3

For the sake of clarity, the proof of Theorem
3
is divided into the following sequence of Lemmas.

Lemma 14
.

The symbol matrix
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
is Hermitian positive semi-definite, and
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
and
\mathcal{K}^{h}(-\frac{\pi}{h})
are Hermitian positive definite matrices.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, a direct calculation verifies that
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
is Hermitian.
Since
\mathbb{K}^{h}
is positive semi-definite, for any sequence
\bm{U}=\{\bm{u}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{C}^{k+1})
, the global energy is non-negative

\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\bm{u}_{j}^{H}\left(\bm{K}_{-1}^{h}\bm{u}_{j-1}+\bm{K}_{0}^{h}\bm{u}_{j}+\bm{K}_{+1}^{h}\bm{u}_{j+1}\right)\geq 0.

(15)

Applying Parseval’s identity via the SDFT transforms the spatial summation (
15
) into the frequency domain

[-\frac{\pi}{h},\frac{\pi}{h}]

\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{h}}^{\frac{\pi}{h}}\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi)^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\geq 0.

(16)

Suppose, by contradiction, there exists
\xi_{0}\in[-\frac{\pi}{h},\frac{\pi}{h}]
and a non-zero vector
\bm{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{k+1}
such that

\bm{v}_{0}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi_{0})\bm{v}_{0}=-c<0
.
By the continuity of
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
, there exists a local neighborhood

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\xi_{0})=(\xi_{0}-\delta,\xi_{0}+\delta)
such that

\bm{v}_{0}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{0}\leq-\frac{c}{2}<0,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\xi_{0}).

We construct the sequence
\bm{U}
by defining its Fourier transform as
\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi)=\bm{v}_{0}\chi_{\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\xi_{0})}(\xi)
, where
\chi
denotes the indicator function. Substituting it into (
16
) yields

\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int_{\xi_{0}-\delta}^{\xi_{0}+\delta}\bm{v}_{0}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{0}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\frac{1}{2\pi h}\left(-\frac{c}{2}\right)(2\delta)<0.

Therefore,
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
must be positive semi-definite for all
\xi\in[-\frac{\pi}{h},\frac{\pi}{h}]
.

To prove the positive definiteness of
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
, we proceed by contradiction. Suppose there exists a non-zero vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{k+1}
such that
\bm{v}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})\bm{v}=0
. For any integer
N>0
, we define the sequence
\bm{U}^{N}=\{\bm{u}^{N}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{C}^{k+1})
by

\bm{u}^{N}_{j}=\begin{cases}\bm{v}(-1)^{j},&\text{if }|j|\leq N,\\
\mathbf{0},&\text{if }|j|>N.\end{cases}

Substituting
\bm{U}^{N}
into (
15
), we observe that for any
|j|\leq N-1
, the local evaluation matches
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
, which vanishes by our assumption

\bm{v}^{H}(-1)^{j}\left[\bm{K}_{-1}^{h}\bm{v}(-1)^{j-1}+\bm{K}_{0}^{h}\bm{v}(-1)^{j}+\bm{K}_{+1}^{h}\bm{v}(-1)^{j+1}\right]=\bm{v}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})\bm{v}=0.

As internal contributions vanish, the quadratic form (
15
) reduces to finite truncation boundary terms. Being independent of
N
and determined by
\bm{v}
and
\bm{K}_{\pm 1}^{h},\bm{K}_{0}^{h}
, the quadratic form (
15
) satisfies the bound
\|\bm{U}^{N}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}\leq C(\bm{v})
for all
N>0
. Thus,

\|\bm{U}^{N}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|q_{h}^{N}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}^{2}\leq C(\bm{v}).

In the internal region
|j|\leq N-1
, the truncated sequence
\bm{U}^{N}
identically coincides with
u_{h}^{*}(x)
generated by
\bm{u}_{j}^{*}=\bm{v}(-1)^{j}
. Let
q_{h}^{*}
be the auxiliary variable of this infinite sequence. We then have
\sum_{j=-N+1}^{N-1}\|q_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}^{2}\leq C(\bm{v}).

Because the sequence differs across adjacent cells only by a sign, which vanishes upon squaring, the local term
E_{0}=\|q_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}^{2}
is identical for all cells
j
. This yields
(2N-1)E_{0}\leq C(\bm{v})
. Letting
N\to\infty
, it holds that
E_{0}=0
. Consequently,
q_{h}^{*}=0
in each cell
I_{j}
, which yields

-\int_{I_{j}}u_{h}^{*}v_{x}\mathrm{d}x+\widetilde{u}_{h,j+\frac{1}{2}}v_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-\widetilde{u}_{h,j-\frac{1}{2}}v_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+}=0,\quad\forall v\in P^{k}(I_{j}).

Integrating the first term by parts, we obtain

\int_{I_{j}}(u_{h}^{*})_{x}v\mathrm{d}x+(\widetilde{u}_{h,j+\frac{1}{2}}-(u_{h}^{*})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-})v_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-(\widetilde{u}_{h,j-\frac{1}{2}}-(u_{h}^{*})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+})v_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{+}=0.

Choosing
\widetilde{u}_{h}=u_{h}^{-}
and setting
v(x)=(x-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}})(u_{h}^{*})_{x}\in P^{k}(I_{j})
, the equation reduces to

\int_{I_{j}}(x-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}})|(u_{h}^{*})_{x}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0
.
This implies
(u_{h}^{*})_{x}\equiv 0
, meaning
u_{h}^{*}
is a constant
c_{j}
within each
I_{j}
. Choosing
v\equiv 1
in the weak formulation yields

\widetilde{u}_{h,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\widetilde{u}_{h,j-\frac{1}{2}}=(u_{h}^{*})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-(u_{h}^{*})_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{-}=c_{j}-c_{j-1}=0.

Consequently,
\bm{v}(-1)^{j}=c
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, forcing
\bm{v}=\mathbf{0}
. This contradiction proves
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
is positive definite.

Noting that
\overline{\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)}=\mathcal{K}^{h}(-\xi)
, the matrix
\mathcal{K}^{h}(-\frac{\pi}{h})
is the conjugate of
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
. As both are Hermitian, the positive definiteness extends to
\xi=-\frac{\pi}{h}
.

Lemma
14
guarantees
k+1
non-negative real eigenvalues for problem (
12
), whose regularity we now examine to facilitate subsequent group velocity differentiation.

Lemma 15
.

For the system (
12
), there exists a complete set of real analytic eigenpairs
\{\sigma_{j}(\xi),\bm{v}_{j}(\xi)\}_{j=1}^{k+1}
forming an
\bm{M}^{h}
-orthonormal basis, satisfying

\bm{v}_{i}(\xi)^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{j}(\xi)=\delta_{ij}.

Proof.

Introducing
\bm{v}(\xi)=(\bm{M}^{h})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{u}(\xi)
converts (
12
) into the standard eigenvalue problem
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{h}(\xi)\bm{u}(\xi)=\sigma(\xi)\bm{u}(\xi)
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{h}(\xi)=(\bm{M}^{h})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)(\bm{M}^{h})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Since
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
comprises finite linear combinations of
e^{\sqrt{-1}j\xi h}
, the matrix
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{h}(\xi)
remains a real analytic Hermitian family.

Applying Kato’s analytic perturbation theory
[
22
, Theorem 6.1]
,this one-parameter family possesses real analytic orthonormal eigenpairs
\{\sigma_{j}(\xi),\bm{u}_{j}(\xi)\}_{j=1}^{k+1}
, resolving any eigenvalue crossings. Transforming back yields the
\bm{M}^{h}
-orthonormality and preserves the real analyticity of
\bm{v}_{j}(\xi)
.

Having established the real analyticity of these eigenmodes, we now proceed to identify the physical and spurious branches.

Lemma 16
.

There exists a unique physical branch
(\sigma_{ph}(\xi),\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi))
satisfying

\sigma_{ph}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{v}_{ph}(0)\propto\bm{e}_{0}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{T}.

The remaining
k
spurious branches
\{(\sigma_{sp,i}(\xi),\bm{v}_{sp,i}(\xi))\}_{i=1}^{k}
satisfy
\sigma_{sp,i}(0)>0
.

Proof.

At
\xi=0
, the matrix

\mathcal{K}^{h}(0)=\bm{K}^{h}_{0}+\bm{K}^{h}_{-1}+\bm{K}^{h}_{+1}
.
The consistency of the LDG scheme ensures that row sums vanish, yielding
\mathcal{K}^{h}(0)\bm{e}_{0}=\bm{0}
.
Thus, the system admits a zero eigenvalue
\sigma(0)=0
with eigenvector
\bm{v}(0)\propto\bm{e}_{0}
. We denote this specific eigenpair as
\{\sigma_{ph},\bm{v}_{ph}\}
.

To determine the algebraic multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue
\sigma_{ph}(0)=0
, we examine the quadratic form associated with the stiffness operator. A straightforward calculation reveals that

\bm{v}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(0)\bm{v}=\int_{I_{j}}|q_{h}(\bm{v})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{k+1}.

The equality holds if and only if
\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(I_{j})}=0
, which implies that
u_{h}
is a constant. Therefore,
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{K}^{h}(0))=\operatorname{span}\{\bm{e}_{0}\}
.
Given that
\mathcal{K}^{h}(0)
is Hermitian positive semi-definite and
\bm{M}^{h}
is positive definite, the zero eigenvalue
\sigma_{ph}(0)
has algebraic multiplicity 1. It follows that the remaining
k
spurious eigenvalues satisfy
\sigma_{sp,i}(0)>0
.

The following Lemma establishes the symmetry of the eigenvalues, which is fundamental for the subsequent asymptotic analysis.

Lemma 17
.

The analytic eigenvalues of the system (
12
) are even functions with respect to
\xi
, satisfying
\sigma_{m}(\xi)=\sigma_{m}(-\xi),\,m=ph,sp_{1},\cdots,sp_{k}
.

Proof.

Using the symmetry
\mathcal{K}^{h}(-\xi)=\overline{\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)}
, we can derive

\displaystyle 0=\det(\overline{\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)}-\sigma(-\xi)\bm{M}^{h})=\overline{\det(\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)-\sigma(-\xi)\bm{M}^{h})}=\det(\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)-\sigma(-\xi)\bm{M}^{h}).

Thus, the value
\sigma(-\xi)
is also a root of the characteristic equation at
\xi
. This logic applies to all eigenvalues, meaning the set of eigenvalues at
-\xi
is the same as the set of eigenvalues at
\xi
. By the uniqueness of the eigenvalues, we conclude

\sigma_{ph}(\xi)=\sigma_{ph}(-\xi),\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{sp,i}(\xi)=\sigma_{sp,i}(-\xi),\quad i=1,\dots,k.

This completes the proof.

We are now well-positioned to verify the low-frequency consistency of the continuous wave operator under our fully discrete framework (
8
).

Lemma 18
.

The physical temporal frequency satisfies
\lim\limits_{\xi\to 0}\omega_{ph}=\xi
.

Proof.

We consider its Taylor expansion at
\xi=0

\sigma_{ph}(\xi)=\sigma^{(2)}\xi^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{ph}^{(4)}(\eta)}{24}\xi^{4},\qquad\text{where }\sigma^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{ph}^{\prime\prime}(0),

for some intermediate
\eta
between
0
and
\xi
.

Let the eigenvector be normalized such that
\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)=1
, which implies

\sigma_{ph}(\xi)=\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}(\xi)\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)
.
Taking the first-order derivative with respect to
\xi
yields

\sigma_{ph}^{\prime}(\xi)=\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}+\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime}+(\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime})^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}.

(17)

Utilizing the Hermitian property of
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
, the last two terms of equation (
17
) simplify to
\sigma_{ph}(\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime}+(\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime})^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph})
. Differentiating the normalization condition
\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}=1
gives
\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime}+(\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime})^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}=0
, thus confirming the result
\sigma_{ph}^{\prime}(\xi)=\bm{v}^{H}_{ph}(\xi)\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)
.
Differentiating once more with respect to
\xi

\sigma_{ph}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}+\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime}+(\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime})^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}.

At
\xi=0
, we have
\bm{v}_{ph}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\bm{e}_{0}
. Straightforward calculations using the LDG scheme yield that
\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(0)\bm{e}_{0}=\bm{0}
.
Consequently,
\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime}=(\bm{v}_{ph}^{\prime})^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}=0
.
Substituting this into the second derivative, we arrive at

\sigma_{ph}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{h}\bm{e}_{0}^{H}\mathcal{K}^{h\prime\prime}(0)\bm{e}_{0}.

Evaluating the second derivative of
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)
at
\xi=0
yields
\mathcal{K}^{h\prime\prime}(0)=-h^{2}(\bm{K}_{-1}+\bm{K}_{+1})
.
Utilizing the consistency properties of the local matrices, we have
\bm{e}_{0}^{T}(\bm{K}_{-1}+\bm{K}_{+1})\bm{e}_{0}=-2/h
. Therefore,
\sigma_{ph}^{\prime\prime}(0)=h^{-1}(-h^{2})(-2/h)=2
.
Recalling
\omega_{ph}(\xi)=\mathrm{sign}(\xi)\frac{2}{\Delta t}\arcsin{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{ph}(\xi)}\Delta t}{2}\right)}
, we have

\lim_{\xi\to 0}\omega_{ph}=\lim_{\xi\to 0}\mathrm{sign}(\xi)\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{ph}^{(4)}(\eta)}{24}\xi^{4}}=\xi.

This completes the proof.

Theorem
3
follows directly from the preceding Lemmas.

\sigma_{sp,j}^{\prime}(\xi)\to 0

\lim_{\xi\to 0^{\pm}}v_{g}^{sp,j}(\xi)=0.

Case 2:

4-\sigma_{sp,j}(0)(\Delta t)^{2}=0
. Using the Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{4-\sigma_{sp,j}(\xi)(\Delta t)^{2}}=\Delta t\,\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{sp,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\xi^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{sp,j}^{(4)}(\eta_{1})}{24}\xi^{4}}.

Substituting (
19
) into the expression for group velocity (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle v_{g}^{sp,j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{sp,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\xi+\frac{\sigma_{sp,j}^{(4)}(\eta_{2})}{6}\xi^{3}}{\Delta t\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{sp,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\xi^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{sp,j}^{(4)}(\eta_{1})}{24}\xi^{4}}\sqrt{\sigma_{sp,j}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{sp,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\xi^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{sp,j}^{(4)}(\eta_{1})}{24}\xi^{4}}}.

This indicates that as
\xi\to 0^{+}
, the group velocity approaches a non-zero constant. A similar argument applies for
\xi\to 0^{-}
.

Next, we analyze the limits at
\pm\frac{\pi}{h}
. Due to symmetry, we restrict our analysis to
\xi>0
. By Lemma
14
,
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\frac{\pi}{h})
is positive definite, yielding positive eigenvalues at this boundary. For any mode, the group velocity reads

v_{g}^{m}(\xi)=\frac{\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)}{\sqrt{4-\sigma_{m}(\xi)(\Delta t)^{2}}\sqrt{\sigma_{m}(\xi)}},\quad m\in\{ph,sp,1,\dots,sp,k\}.

(20)

We first prove that
\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})=0
. Using the even symmetry
\sigma_{m}(\xi)=\sigma_{m}(-\xi)
and periodicity
\sigma_{m}(\xi)=\sigma_{m}(\xi+\frac{2\pi}{h})
, their derivatives satisfy
\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)=-\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(-\xi)
and
\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)=\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\xi+\frac{2\pi}{h})
. Chaining these relations at the boundary yields

\sigma_{m}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\pi}{h}\right)=\sigma_{m}^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\pi}{h}+\frac{2\pi}{h}\right)=\sigma_{m}^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\pi}{h}\right)=-\sigma_{m}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\pi}{h}\right),

which implies
\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})=0
. We then distinguish two cases for the denominator term.

Case 1:

4-\sigma_{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})(\Delta t)^{2}>0
. Since
\sigma_{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})>0
, the denominator is bounded away from zero at the boundary. Thus, the vanishing numerator implies
v_{g}^{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})=0
.

Case 2:

4-\sigma_{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})(\Delta t)^{2}=0
. We employ a Taylor expansion around the boundary by setting
\rho=\xi-\frac{\pi}{h}
. The expansions are

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})\rho+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{(4)}(\eta_{3})}{6}\rho^{3},\quad\sigma_{m}(\xi)=\sigma_{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})}{2}\rho^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{m}^{(4)}(\eta_{4})}{24}\rho^{4}.

Substituting these into (
20
), the limit simplifies to

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to(\frac{\pi}{h})^{-}}v^{m}_{g}(\xi)=\frac{-\sigma_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})}{\Delta t\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})}\sqrt{\sigma_{m}(\frac{\pi}{h})}}\neq 0.

Finally, for the physical mode with
k=0
and
\Delta t=h
, the explicit eigenvalue
\sigma(\xi)=\frac{4}{h^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\xi h}{2}\right)
yields
\sigma(\frac{\pi}{h})=\frac{4}{h^{2}}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})=-2
. Thus, we have

\lim_{\xi\to(\frac{\pi}{h})^{-}}v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)=\frac{-\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})}{h\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\frac{\pi}{h})}\sqrt{\sigma(\frac{\pi}{h})}}=\frac{-(-2)}{h\sqrt{\frac{4}{h^{2}}}}=\frac{2}{h(\frac{2}{h})}=1.

Due to symmetry, the corresponding limits as
\xi\to-\frac{\pi}{h}
follow identically.

Proof of Theorem

Before proving our main Theorem, we first derive the bound for the total energy of the initial data given by (

Lemma 19
.

For the discrete initial data
(\bm{U}^{0},\bm{U}^{1})

and

Proof.

By Parseval’s identity, the energy (

\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi h}\int_{\Pi_{h}}\left(\left\langle\bm{M}^{h}\frac{\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1}-\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}}{\Delta t},\frac{\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1}-\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}}{\Delta t}\right\rangle+\text{Re}\left\langle\mathcal{K}^{h}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1},\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}\right\rangle\right)(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

Using

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0}_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi h}\int_{\Pi_{h}}\left(\langle\bm{M}^{h}\frac{\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1}-\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}}{\Delta t},\frac{\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1}-\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}}{\Delta t}\rangle+\text{Re}\langle\mathcal{K}^{h}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{1},\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}\rangle\right)(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

(21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi h}\int_{\Pi_{h}}\sigma_{ph}(\xi)\left[1+\cos(\omega_{ph}(\xi)\Delta t)\right]\langle\bm{M}^{h}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0},\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}\rangle(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

Under Assumption
3.1
, there exist positive constants
C^{1}_{s}
and
C^{2}_{s}
such that

C^{1}_{s}\geq 1+\cos(\omega_{ph}(\xi)\Delta t)\geq C^{2}_{s},\quad\forall\xi\in\Omega_{\rho}=[\xi_{c}-\rho,\xi_{c}+\rho]\subset\Pi_{h}.

(22)

Next, we evaluate the inner product for the initial data

\displaystyle\langle\bm{M}^{h}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0},\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}\rangle(\xi)=h^{\gamma}|r(\xi)|^{2}|\chi_{\rho}(\xi)|^{2}\langle\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi),\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)\rangle=\frac{h^{1+\gamma}}{\sigma_{ph}(\xi)}|\chi_{\rho}(\xi)|^{2}.

(23)

Substituting (

) and (
23
) into (

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}_{s}}{4\pi}h^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\left|\psi\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_{c}}{\rho}\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\leq\frac{C^{1}_{s}}{4\pi}h^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\left|\psi\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_{c}}{\rho}\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Finally, performing the change of variables

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}_{s}}{4\pi}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}([-1,1])}^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\leq\frac{C^{1}_{s}}{4\pi}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}([-1,1])}^{2}.

Setting
C_{1}=\frac{C^{2}_{s}}{4\pi}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}([-1,1])}^{2}
and
C_{2}=\frac{C^{1}_{s}}{4\pi}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}([-1,1])}^{2}
establishes the desired constants, which are positive and independent of

We now establish the exponential decay of the observed energy. For clarity, the proof is divided into two sequential Lemmas.

Lemma 20
.

For any integer
p\geq 1
, the solution
\bm{u}_{j}^{n}
satisfies

\|\bm{u}_{j}^{n}\|\leq\frac{\rho}{\pi}h^{-\gamma/2}\widetilde{M}(p!)^{s}\left(\frac{\beta}{\rho|x_{j}|}\right)^{p},

(24)

where the positive constants
\widetilde{M}(k,s)
and
\beta(k,\lambda,s)
are independent of
h,p
, and
x_{j}
.

Proof.

The proof is organized into the following four steps:

Step 1: Temporal evolution of the solution.
Due to the modal purity of the initial data (
14
) and the linearity of the time-stepping scheme, the temporal evolution of the wave packet is given by

\widehat{\bm{U}}^{n}(\xi)=e^{-\sqrt{-1}\omega_{ph}(\xi)t_{n}}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{0}(\xi),\quad\forall n\geq 0.

(25)

Using the Inverse SDFT, the solution in the physical space at
I_{j}
and time
t_{n}
is

\bm{u}_{j}^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/h}^{\pi/h}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{n}(\xi)e^{\sqrt{-1}\xi x_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\mathcal{A}(\xi)e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\mathcal{A}(\xi)=h^{-\gamma/2}r(\xi)\chi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)
and
\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)=x_{j}\xi-\omega_{ph}(\xi)t_{n}
.

Step 2: Non-stationary phase setup.

We analyze the derivative
\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)=x_{j}-v^{ph}_{g}(\xi)t_{n}
.
The wave packet is concentrated near the critical frequency
\frac{\pi}{h}
, where the group velocity vanishes. By continuity, for sufficiently small
h
,
|v^{ph}_{g}(\xi)|
remains arbitrarily small in
\Omega_{\rho}
. Thus, for any
t_{n}\in[0,T]

|\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)|\geq|x_{j}|-|v^{ph}_{g}(\xi)|T\geq|x_{j}|-\delta_{1}>\frac{|x_{j}|}{2}>\frac{1}{2}>0.

We define the differential operator
\mathcal{L}
and its adjoint
\mathcal{L}^{*}
acting on a function
f
as

\mathcal{L}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\partial_{\xi}f,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{L}^{*}f=-\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\right).

Observe that
\mathcal{L}
acts as the inverse of the derivative on the oscillatory term

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\partial_{\xi}\left(e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}=e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}.

Substituting this identity into the integral and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\mathcal{A}(\xi)e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=-\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}(\xi)}{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\right)e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

(26)

Iterating this
p
times yields the integral representation involving the adjoint operator

\bm{u}_{j}^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}}e^{\sqrt{-1}\Phi_{j}^{n}(\xi)}(\mathcal{L}^{*})^{p}[\mathcal{A}(\xi)]\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

(27)

Step 3: Bounding the
p
-th power of the adjoint operator.
The integrand is bounded by

\|(\mathcal{L}^{*})^{p}[\mathcal{A}]\|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{p}D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)\cdot\left\|\partial_{\xi}^{m}\mathcal{A}\right\|,

(28)

where the coefficients
D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)
(defined below) are positive constants arising from the adjoint operator, and we define
g_{j,n}=\frac{1}{\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{j}^{n}}
.

To prove (
28
), we omit the unimodular factor
\sqrt{-1}
and define the operator
L_{0}=I
,
L_{p+1}u=\partial_{\xi}(g_{j,n}L_{p}u)
, with the expansion
L_{p}=\sum_{m=0}^{p}c_{p,m}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}^{m}
.
Analogously, we expand the operator
R_{p}u=\partial_{\xi}^{p}(g_{j,n}^{p}u)
as
R_{p}=\sum_{m=0}^{p}D_{p,m}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}^{m}
.
Let
D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)
be the structural majorant associated with
D_{p,m}(\xi)
. It is constructed by expanding
D_{p,m}
into a sum of monomial terms via the Leibniz rule, and replacing every derivative factor
\partial_{\xi}^{i}g_{j,n}
with its absolute value
\left|\partial_{\xi}^{i}g_{j,n}\right|
.

Then we prove (
28
) by induction on
p
. For any derivative order
r\geq 0
, we claim

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{r}c_{p,m}(\xi)\right|\leq\partial_{\xi}^{r}D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi),

(29)

where the derivative on the
D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)
is formal.

The base case
p=1
holds with
c_{1,0}=g_{j,n}^{\prime}
,
c_{1,1}=g_{j,n}
, and
D_{1,i}^{*}=|c_{1,i}|
. For the inductive step
p\to p+1
, the coefficient relations are

(i) For
c_{p+1,m}
:

Applying the definition
L_{p+1}[\mathcal{A}]=\partial_{\xi}(g_{j,n}L_{p}[\mathcal{A}])
, we have

L_{p+1}[\mathcal{A}]=\sum_{m=0}^{p}\left[(g_{j,n}c_{p,m})^{\prime}\mathcal{A}^{(m)}+g_{j,n}c_{p,m}\mathcal{A}^{(m+1)}\right].

Collecting the terms for
\mathcal{A}^{(m)}
, we obtain the relation for the coefficients

c_{p+1,m}=g_{j,n}c_{p,m-1}+g_{j,n}^{\prime}c_{p,m}+g_{j,n}c_{p,m}^{\prime}.

(30)

(ii) For
D_{p+1,m}
:

By definition,
D_{p+1,m}=\binom{p+1}{m}\partial_{\xi}^{p+1-m}(g_{j,n}^{p+1})
. Writing
g_{j,n}^{p+1}=g_{j,n}\cdot g_{j,n}^{p}
and applying the Leibniz rule, we obtain

D_{p+1,m}=\binom{p+1}{m}\left[g_{j,n}(g_{j,n}^{p})^{(p+1-m)}+(p+1-m)g_{j,n}^{\prime}(g_{j,n}^{p})^{(p-m)}+\mathcal{R}\right].

Using identity
\binom{p+1}{m}=\binom{p}{m-1}+\binom{p}{m}
and
(p+1-m)\binom{p+1}{m}=(p+1)\binom{p}{m}
, we can reconstruct the lower-order terms

\displaystyle D_{p+1,m}=\;

\displaystyle g_{j,n}\left[\binom{p}{m-1}(g_{j,n}^{p})^{(p-m+1)}+\binom{p}{m}(g_{j,n}^{p})^{(p-m+1)}\right]

\displaystyle+(p+1)g_{j,n}^{\prime}\left[\binom{p}{m}(g_{j,n}^{p})^{(p-m)}\right]+\binom{p+1}{m}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle g_{j,n}D_{p,m-1}+g_{j,n}D_{p,m}^{\prime}+(p+1)g_{j,n}^{\prime}D_{p,m}+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}.

(iii) For
r
-th derivatives:

To prove the induction step for any
r\geq 0
, we apply the generalized Leibniz rule to the relation (
30
)

c_{p+1,m}^{(r)}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}\left[g_{j,n}^{(i)}c_{p,m-1}^{(r-i)}+g_{j,n}^{(i+1)}c_{p,m}^{(r-i)}+g_{j,n}^{(i)}c_{p,m}^{(r-i+1)}\right].

Taking the absolute value and applying the triangle inequality, we have

|c_{p+1,m}^{(r)}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}\left[|g_{j,n}^{(i)}||c_{p,m-1}^{(r-i)}|+|g_{j,n}^{(i+1)}||c_{p,m}^{(r-i)}|+|g_{j,n}^{(i)}||c_{p,m}^{(r-i+1)}|\right].

(31)

Similarly, applying the formal
r
-th derivative to
D_{p+1,m}^{*}
, and subsequently discarding the non-negative remainder terms, we obtain

\displaystyle(D_{p+1,m}^{*})^{(r)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}\left[|g_{j,n}^{(i)}|(D_{p,m-1}^{*})^{(r-i)}+(p+1)|g_{j,n}^{(i+1)}|(D_{p,m}^{*})^{(r-i)}+|g_{j,n}^{(i)}|(D_{p,m}^{*})^{(r-i+1)}\right].

By the induction hypothesis (
29
), we have
|c_{p,\cdot}^{(s)}|\leq(D_{p,\cdot}^{*})^{(s)}
for all
s\geq 0
. Substituting this into the absolute value bound (
31
) establishes (
29
).

Step 4: Cauchy and Gevrey estimates.

To bound
D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)
, we introduce the variable
\eta=\xi h
. Writing
\sigma_{ph}(\xi)=h^{-2}\widetilde{\sigma}_{ph}(\eta)
yields the scalable dispersion relation
\omega_{ph}(\xi)=h^{-1}\widetilde{\omega}(\eta)
, where
\widetilde{\omega}(\eta)=\mathrm{sign}(\eta)\frac{2}{\lambda}\arcsin(\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{\widetilde{\sigma}_{ph}(\eta)})
. Under Assumption
3.1
,
\widetilde{\omega}(\eta)
is real analytic with a convergence radius
r_{0}>0
, which depends on
k
and
\lambda
but is independent of
h
. Recalling
x_{j}=jh
and
t_{n}=n\Delta t=n\lambda h
, the physical group velocity satisfies
v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)=\omega_{ph}^{\prime}(\xi)=\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(\eta)
. Thus, we have

g_{j,n}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}(jh-\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(\eta)n\lambda h)}=\frac{1}{h}\widetilde{g}_{j,n}(\eta),\quad\text{where}\quad\widetilde{g}_{j,n}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}(j-\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(\eta)n\lambda)}.

Because
\widetilde{g}_{j,n}
extends holomorphically to
D_{r_{0}}
with the bound
\sup_{z\in D_{r_{0}}}|\widetilde{g}_{j,n}(z)|\leq(c|j|)^{-1}
, applying the chain rule and Cauchy estimates, we obtain

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{i}g_{j,n}(\xi)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{h}\cdot h^{i}\cdot\partial_{\eta}^{i}\widetilde{g}_{j,n}(\eta)\right|\leq h^{i-1}\left(i!\cdot r_{0}^{-i}\frac{1}{c|j|}\right)=i!\cdot\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{-i}\left(\frac{1}{c|x_{j}|}\right).

To handle
D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)
, we introduce
M(z)=M_{0}(1-\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{-1}z)^{-1}
, where
M_{0}=(c|x_{j}|)^{-1}
, which satisfies
M^{(i)}(0)=i!\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{-i}M_{0}\geq|g_{j,n}^{(i)}(\xi)|
.
By definition,
D_{p,m}^{*}
is bounded by the derivative of
M(z)^{p}
at
z=0

D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)\!\leq\!\binom{p}{m}\partial_{z}^{p-m}\big(M(z)^{p}\big)\big|_{z=0}\!\leq\!\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-m-1)!}{(p-1)!}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{m-p}\!\left(\frac{1}{c|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}.

(32)

To estimate
\partial_{\xi}^{m}\mathcal{A}
, scaling
\mathcal{S}(\xi)=r(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)=h\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(\eta)
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
is analytic with convergence radius
R>0
, allows us to apply the chain rule and Cauchy estimates

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{\xi}^{i}\mathcal{S}(\xi)\right\|=\left\|h^{i+1}\cdot\partial_{\eta}^{i}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(\eta)\right\|\leq h^{i}\left(C_{S}i!\frac{1}{R^{i}}\right)=C_{S}i!\left(\frac{h}{R}\right)^{i},

(33)

where
R
and
C_{S}
are positive constants depending on
k
.
Since
\chi_{\rho}\in G^{s}
(
s>1
), there exist constants
C_{\chi},B_{\chi}>0
depending only on
s
such that for all
i\geq 0
, we have the Gevrey estimate

|\partial_{\xi}^{i}\chi_{\rho}(\xi)|\leq C_{\chi}(i!)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{B_{\chi}\rho}\right)^{i}.

(34)

We now derive the bound for the
m
-th derivative of
\mathcal{A}(\xi)
. Applying the Leibniz Rule to the product
\mathcal{S}(\xi)\chi_{\rho}(\xi)
, we have

\partial_{\xi}^{m}\mathcal{A}(\xi)=h^{-\gamma/2}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{i}\mathcal{S}(\xi)\right)\left(\partial_{\xi}^{m-i}\chi_{\rho}(\xi)\right).

(35)

Substituting (
33
) and (
34
) into (
35
), we obtain

\|\partial_{\xi}^{m}\mathcal{A}(\xi)\|\leq h^{-\gamma/2}M_{1}(m!)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{B_{\chi}\rho}\right)^{m}.

(36)

Here, we defined
M_{1}=2C_{S}C_{\chi}
by assuming
h
is small enough such that
\frac{hB_{\chi}\rho}{R}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Substituting these bounds (
36
) and (
32
) into the estimation (
28
), we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{L}^{*})^{p}[\mathcal{A}(\xi)]\|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{p}D_{p,m}^{*}(\xi)\left\|\partial_{\xi}^{m}\mathcal{A}(\xi)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{p}\left[\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-m-1)!}{(p-1)!}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{m-p}\left(\frac{1}{c|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}\right]\left[h^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}M_{1}(m!)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{B_{\chi}\rho}\right)^{m}\right].

(37)

Utilizing
\binom{p}{m}\frac{1}{(p-1)!}=\frac{p}{m!(p-m)!}
, we bound the combinatorial factors as

\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-m-1)!}{(p-1)!}(m!)^{s}=p\frac{(2p-m-1)!}{(p-m)!}(m!)^{s-1}\leq p\frac{(2p)!}{p!}(p!)^{s-1}=p\,4^{p}(p!)^{s}.

Substituting this bound and
M_{1}=2C_{S}C_{\chi}
back into the summation (
5.3
), we get

\|(\mathcal{L}^{*})^{p}[\mathcal{A}(\xi)]\|\leq h^{-\gamma/2}(2C_{S}C_{\chi})p\,4^{p}(p!)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{c|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}\sum_{m=0}^{p}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{h}\right)^{-(p-m)}\left(\frac{1}{B_{\chi}\rho}\right)^{m}.

Defining
\nu=\max(r_{0}^{-1},B_{\chi}^{-1})
, the condition
\rho\geq 1
bounds the remaining summation by
(p+1)(\nu/\rho)^{p}
. Absorbing this polynomial growth via
p(p+1)\leq C_{\tilde{\delta}}(1+\tilde{\delta})^{p}
for any
\tilde{\delta}>0
, and setting
\beta=4(1+\widetilde{\delta})\nu/c
along with
\widetilde{M}=2C_{S}C_{\chi}C_{\widetilde{\delta}}
, yields

\|(\mathcal{L}^{*})^{p}[\mathcal{A}(\xi)]\|\leq h^{-\gamma/2}\widetilde{M}(p!)^{s}\left(\frac{\beta}{\rho|x_{j}|}\right)^{p},

where the positive constants
\widetilde{M}(k,s)
and
\beta(k,\lambda,s)
are independent of
h,p
, and
x_{j}
. Noting that the integration length is
|\Omega_{\rho}|=2\rho
, we obtain the bound

\|\bm{u}_{j}^{n}\|\leq\frac{\rho}{\pi}h^{-\gamma/2}\widetilde{M}(p!)^{s}\left(\frac{\beta}{\rho|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}.

Lemma 21
.

For any fixed
T>0
and any arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, the observed energy satisfies

\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}\leq C_{3}(k,s,T)\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)}{h^{1-\varepsilon}}\right),

where the positive constants
C_{3}(k,s,T)
and
C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)
are independent of
h
.

Proof.

Let
p=\lfloor(\rho/(e^{s}\beta\delta))^{1/s}\rfloor
, where
\delta>1
. Substituting this
p
into the estimate (
38
) and using Stirling’s approximation
(p!)^{s}\leq Cp^{s/2}(p^{s}/e^{s})^{p}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{j}^{n}\|\leq\frac{\rho}{\pi}h^{-\gamma/2}\widetilde{M}Cp^{s/2}\left(\frac{p^{s}}{e^{s}}\right)^{p}\left(\frac{\beta}{\rho|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}\leq\widetilde{C}h^{-3\gamma/2}p^{s/2}\left(\frac{p^{s}\beta}{e^{s}\rho|x_{j}|}\right)^{p}.

Using the upper bound
p^{s}\leq\frac{\rho}{e^{s}\beta\delta}
, the base of the geometric term simplifies

\frac{p^{s}\cdot\beta}{e^{s}\rho|x_{j}|}\leq\frac{\rho}{e^{s}\beta\delta}\cdot\frac{\beta}{e^{s}\rho|x_{j}|}=\frac{1}{e^{2s}\delta|x_{j}|}.

Defining
\rho_{1}=e^{2s}\delta>2
ensures spatial decay, and for small
h
, we have
p^{s/2}\leq\rho_{1}^{p/2}
. Substituting
\rho=h^{-\gamma}
and absorbing the algebraic term
h^{-3\gamma/2}
into the exponential, we obtain

\|\bm{u}_{j}^{n}\|\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}\exp\left(\frac{\ln\rho_{1}}{2}\right)}{|x_{j}|^{p}}h^{\frac{-3\gamma}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{1/s}\ln\rho_{1}}{2(e^{s}\beta\delta)^{1/s}}\right)\leq C|x_{j}|^{-p}\exp\left(-\frac{c}{2}\,h^{-\gamma/s}\right).

(39)

We estimate the total discrete energy restricted to the observation region

\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\|_{\mathbb{M}^{h},\Omega}^{2}+\frac{\|\bm{U}^{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h},\Omega}^{2}}{4}+\frac{\|\bm{U}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h},\Omega}^{2}}{4}-\frac{(\Delta t)^{2}}{4}\left\|\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h},\Omega}^{2}.

By omitting the non-positive term, employing
\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{K}^{h}}^{2}\leq Ch^{-2}\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{M}^{h}}^{2}
, and utilizing
\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathbb{M}^{h},\Omega}^{2}\leq Ch\sum_{x_{j}\in\Omega}\|\bm{u}_{j}\|^{2}
, combined with the CFL condition, we can bound the discrete energy by the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the coefficient vectors

\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}\leq C_{k}\,h^{-1}\sum_{x_{j}\in\Omega}(\|\bm{u}^{n+1}_{j}\|^{2}+\|\bm{u}^{n}_{j}\|^{2}).

(40)

Computing the summation over the observation region

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{j}\in\Omega}(\|\bm{u}^{n+1}_{j}\|^{2}+\|\bm{u}^{n}_{j}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{2}\exp\left(-c\,h^{-\gamma/s}\right)\sum_{j:|jh|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|jh|^{2p}}.

(41)

Bounding the discrete summation by its integral counterpart for
p\geq 1
yields

\sum_{|jh|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|jh|^{2p}}\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{1^{2p}}+\int_{1/h}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(xh)^{2p}}dx\right)=2\left(1+\frac{1}{h(2p-1)}\right).

(42)

For sufficiently small
h
, the summation is bounded by
4/h
.

Substituting those bounds (
41
) and (
42
) back into (
40
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}\leq C_{inv}h^{-1}\cdot\left(2C^{2}\exp\left(-c\,h^{-\gamma/s}\right)\frac{4}{h}\right)=\widetilde{C}h^{-2}\exp\left(-c\,h^{-\gamma/s}\right).

Summing over the discrete time steps
n=0,\dots,N-1
, we obtain the estimate for the total energy in the observation region

\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}\leq T\widetilde{C}\,\exp\left(-\frac{c}{2}\,h^{-\gamma/s}\right):=C_{3}(k,s,T)\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}(k,\lambda,s)}{h^{\gamma/s}}\right).

Since
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
s>1
are chosen freely, for any small
\varepsilon>0
, setting
s
sufficiently close to
1
and
\gamma=s(1-\varepsilon)
yields
\gamma/s=1-\varepsilon
, completing the proof.

With Lemma
19
and Lemma
21
now established, the proof of Theorem
7
follows naturally and is therefore omitted.

5.4
Proof of Theorem
11

Before proving Theorem
11
, we first give the proof of Lemma
10
.

Proof.

Following Step 4 of Lemma
21
,the scaling
\eta=\xi h
gives
v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)=\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(\eta)
. Since
\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(0)=1
, continuity guarantees a radius
\eta_{k}>0
such that
\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}(\eta)>0
for
|\eta|\leq\eta_{k}
, which implies
v_{g}^{ph}(\xi)>0
for all
|\xi|\leq\eta_{k}/h
.

Having established that the physical group velocity possesses a positive interval, we now proceed to prove the Theorem
11
.

Proof.

Lemma
2
ensures energy conservation, allowing the accumulated energy over
N\Delta t
to be split into the observation domain
\Omega
and the blind zone
I=[-1,1]

N\Delta t\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}=\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,\Omega}^{n}+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}.

(43)

For
\delta_{k}=1-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\pi}\in(0,1)
, we define the filtered discrete space
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}
as

\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}=\left\{\bm{U}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{C}^{k+1})\ \Big|\ \operatorname{supp}(\widehat{\bm{U}}(\xi))\subset I_{\delta_{k}},\ \widehat{\bm{U}}(\xi)\in\text{span}\{\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)\}\right\},

where
I_{\delta_{k}}=\big[-\frac{(1-\delta_{k})\pi}{h},\frac{(1-\delta_{k})\pi}{h}\big]
is the filtered frequency band.

To bound
\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}
independently of
h
, we introduce a cutoff
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\Phi\geq 1
on
[-1,1]
and
\operatorname{supp}(\Phi)\subset[-2,2]
. Sampling
\varphi_{j}=\Phi(jh)
assembles the block-diagonal matrix
\bm{W}_{\varphi}=\operatorname{diag}(\varphi_{j}\bm{I}_{k+1})
, bounding
\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}
by the globally weighted form

\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\bm{W}_{\varphi}\mathbb{M}^{h}\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t},\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\langle\bm{W}_{\varphi}\mathbb{K}^{h}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n}\rangle,

(44)

For any constant-coefficient operator
\mathbb{A}^{h}
, it diagonalizes in the frequency domain as a symbol matrix
A(\xi)
. Substituting the inverse SDFT (
10
) into (
44
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\bm{W}_{\varphi}\mathbb{A}^{h}\bm{U},\bm{V}\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\iint_{(\Pi_{h})^{2}}\widehat{\bm{V}}^{H}(\eta)A(\xi)\widehat{\bm{U}}(\xi)\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(jh)e^{\sqrt{-1}jh(\xi-\eta)}\right]\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta.\end{aligned}

(45)

Step 1: Bi-directional Wave Decomposition.

Decomposing the frequency evolution in
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\delta_{k}}
into right- and left-going physical modes, the dynamics satisfy

\widehat{\bm{U}}^{n}(\xi)=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{+}^{n}(\xi)+\widehat{\bm{U}}_{-}^{n}(\xi),\quad\text{where}\quad\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\pm}^{n}(\xi)=e^{\mp\sqrt{-1}\omega_{ph}(\xi)t_{n}}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\pm}^{0}(\xi).

(46)

Substituting this decomposition into the discrete time accumulation of
\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}
, the weighted bilinear form expands into four integral components

\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}\leq\mathcal{I}_{++}+\mathcal{I}_{--}+\mathcal{I}_{+-}+\mathcal{I}_{-+}.

(47)

Here,
\mathcal{I}_{\pm\pm}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\pm\mp}
represent the co- and counter-propagating terms, respectively.

Step 2: Estimates for the terms
\mathcal{I}_{++}
and
\mathcal{I}_{--}
.

We first consider the estimation of
\mathcal{I}{++}
, and note that the estimation for
\mathcal{I}{--}
follows similarly.
To evaluate the bilinear form of
\mathcal{E}_{h,I}^{n}
, we define
\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)
as

\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{+}^{0}(\eta))^{H}\Big[\overline{\delta_{t}(\eta)}\bm{M}^{h}\delta_{t}(\xi)+e^{-\sqrt{-1}\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi)}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\Big]\widehat{\bm{U}}_{+}^{0}(\xi),

(48)

where
\delta_{t}(\xi)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{-1}\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi)}-1}{\Delta t}
. Under the physical projection
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{+}^{0}(\xi)=\alpha_{+}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)
, the
\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)
factorizes as

\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)=\overline{\alpha_{+}(\eta)}\alpha_{+}(\xi)\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\eta),

where

\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}(\eta)\Big[\overline{\delta_{t}(\eta)}\bm{M}^{h}\delta_{t}(\xi)+e^{-\sqrt{-1}\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi)}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\Big]\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi).

(49)

Due to the smoothness of the eigenvectors and the symbol matrices over
(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}
, the scalar kernel
|\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\eta)|\leq C_{Q}
.
Evaluating
\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)
at the diagonal
\xi=\eta
recovers the single-point physical energy density

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi):=\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{S}(\xi,\xi)\big)=|\alpha_{+}(\xi)|^{2}\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi)\big).

We then prove that
\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi))
admits a positive lower bound
E_{1}>0
over
I_{\delta_{k}}
. Utilizing the discrete dispersion relation
\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\bm{v}_{ph}=|\delta_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}
and substituting it into (
49
), we obtain

\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}|\delta_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\left(1+e^{-\sqrt{-1}\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi)}\right)\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}.

(50)

Taking the real part yields
\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi)\big)=\frac{1}{2}|\delta_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\left[1+\cos(\Delta t\omega_{ph})\right]\left(\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}\right)
.
Since
\bm{M}^{h}
is positive definite,
\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}\bm{M}^{h}\bm{v}_{ph}>0
. For propagating physical waves, the temporal difference satisfies
|\delta_{t}(\xi)|^{2}>0
. Furthermore, under the stability condition
\max_{\xi}{\sigma_{ph}(\xi)}<\frac{4}{(\Delta t)^{2}}
, the phase factor satisfies
1+\cos(\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi))>0
.

The pointwise positivity
\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi))>0
, combined with its continuity and the compactness of
I_{\delta_{k}}
, guarantees a positive minimum
E_{1}=\min_{\xi\in I_{\delta_{k}}}\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal{Q}_{++}(\xi,\xi))>0
.
By this lower bound,
|\alpha_{+}(\xi)|\leq\sqrt{|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)|/E_{1}}
. Extracting these gives

|\mathcal{S}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{E_{1}}\sqrt{|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)|}\sqrt{|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\eta)|}.

(51)

Summing the weighted inner product over
n=0,\dots,N-1
with step
\Delta t
yields

\mathcal{I}_{++}\!\!\leq\!\!\frac{hC_{Q}}{4\pi^{2}hE_{1}}\!\!\iint_{(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}}\!|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(jh)e^{\sqrt{-1}jh(\xi-\eta)}||K(\xi,\eta)|\!\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)}\!\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\eta)}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta.

(52)

where
K(\xi,\eta)=\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-\sqrt{-1}t_{n}(\omega_{ph}(\xi)-\omega_{ph}(\eta))}
.
To estimate this integral, we partition
(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}
into
D=\{(\xi,\eta):|\xi-\eta|<\varepsilon\}\cap(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}
and
D^{c}=(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}\setminus D
.

Given the
\varepsilon
-gap bound
|K(\xi,\eta)|\leq C_{K}
on
D^{c}
, we establish the super-algebraic decay of
\widehat{\Phi}(\zeta)=h\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(jh)e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}
for
\zeta=\eta-\xi
. Applying discrete summation by parts via
e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}-e^{-\sqrt{-1}(j-1)h\zeta}}{1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta}}
provides the first-order difference

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}(\zeta)=\frac{h}{1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{h}\Phi(jh)e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}.

Repeating this summation by parts
m
times consecutively, we obtain

\widehat{\Phi}(\zeta)=\frac{h}{(1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta})^{m}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{h}^{m}\Phi(jh)e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}.

Within
I_{\delta_{k}}
, the frequency difference
\zeta=\eta-\xi
spans up to
|\zeta|\leq 2(1-\delta_{k})\pi/h
. To bound the
(1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta})^{m}
, we use the following estimate

|1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta}|\geq\frac{2h}{\pi}\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|.

Spectral truncation separates the frequency difference from the aliasing pole
2\pi/h
by a
2\delta_{k}\pi/h
gap, bounding the wrapped distance in
D^{c}
from below by
\min(\varepsilon,2\delta_{k}\pi/h)>0
. This establishes

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Phi}(\zeta)|

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|h\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{h}^{m}\Phi(jh)e^{-\sqrt{-1}jh\zeta}\right|}{|1-e^{\sqrt{-1}h\zeta}|^{m}}\leq\frac{h\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\Delta_{h}^{m}\Phi(jh)\right|}{\left(\frac{2h}{\pi}\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|\right)^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}_{m}h^{m}}{\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{m}h^{m}(\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|)^{m}}=\frac{C_{m}}{(\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|)^{m}},\quad\forall m\geq 2,

where
C_{m}:=\widetilde{C}_{m}(\pi/2)^{m}
is h-independent constant.
Substituting these bounds and using Young’s inequality, the integral over
D^{c}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{++}^{D^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{Q}C_{K}C_{m}}{2\pi hE_{1}}\iint_{D^{c}}\frac{1}{\left(\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|\right)^{m}}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\eta)}{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta.

(53)

Symmetry reduces the integral to
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)
, where partitioning into low- and high-frequency regions produces the bound

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\eta\in I_{\delta_{k}},|\zeta|\geq\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left(\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\zeta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}\right|\right)^{m}}\mathrm{d}\eta&\leq 2\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi/h}\frac{1}{s^{m}}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{2\delta_{k}\pi/h}^{\pi/h}\frac{1}{s^{m}}\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{2}{m-1}\left[\varepsilon^{1-m}+\left(\frac{h}{2\delta_{k}\pi}\right)^{m-1}\right].\end{aligned}

For any
h\leq h_{0}
, we define

C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}:=\frac{2}{m-1}\left[\varepsilon^{1-m}+\left(\frac{h_{0}}{2\delta_{k}\pi}\right)^{m-1}\right]
.
Extracting this explicit constant
C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
, the double integral collapses into the Parseval identity

\mathcal{I}_{++}^{D^{c}}\leq\left(\frac{C_{Q}C_{K}C_{m}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}{2E_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int_{I_{\delta_{k}}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi=C_{3}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\mathcal{E}_{h,+}^{0}.

With
|\omega_{ph}(\xi)-\omega_{ph}(\eta)|\geq v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi-\eta|
guaranteed by the Mean Value Theorem on
D
, bounding
|K(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi-\eta|}
enables Young’s inequality to bound the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{++}^{D}\leq\left(\frac{C_{Q}C_{\varphi}}{2E_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int_{I_{\delta_{k}}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)\left(\int_{|\eta-\xi|<\varepsilon}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi-\eta|}\right)\mathrm{d}\eta\right)\mathrm{d}\xi,

(54)

where
C_{\varphi}=\|\widehat{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Integrating the singularity yields

\displaystyle\int_{|\eta-\xi|<\varepsilon}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\eta-\xi|}\right)\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left[1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right].

(55)

Therefore, substituting (
55
) into (
54
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{++}^{D}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left[1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right]\mathcal{E}_{h,+}^{0}.

By the symmetry of the wave equation, the left-going branch
\mathcal{I}_{--}
satisfies the identical upper bound. So we can obtain the following estimation

\mathcal{I}_{++}+\mathcal{I}_{--}\leq\left(C_{3}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}+\frac{C_{2}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left[1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right]\right)\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

(56)

Step 3: Estimates for the terms
\mathcal{I}_{+-}
and
\mathcal{I}_{-+}
.

For
\mathcal{I}_{+-}
, the oddness of
\omega_{ph}
and the Mean Value Theorem guarantee
|\omega_{ph}(\xi)+\omega_{ph}(\eta)|\geq v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi+\eta|
, bounding the discrete temporal kernel
L_{N}(\xi,\eta)=\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-\sqrt{-1}t_{n}(\omega_{ph}(\xi)+\omega_{ph}(\eta))}
by

|L_{N}(\xi,\eta)|=\left|\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-\sqrt{-1}t_{n}(\omega_{ph}(\xi)+\omega_{ph}(\eta))}\right|\leq\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi+\eta|}\right).

To evaluate
\mathcal{I}_{+-}
, we partition
(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}
into three mutually disjoint regions

D_{1}=\{|\xi+\eta|<\varepsilon\}\cap(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2},\,D_{2}=(\{|\xi-\eta|<\varepsilon\}\setminus D_{1})\cap(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}\,\text{and}\,D_{3}=(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}\setminus(D_{1}\cup D_{2}).

For
(\xi,\eta)\in D_{3}
, both
|\xi+\eta|\geq\varepsilon
and
|\xi-\eta|\geq\varepsilon
hold. The temporal kernel is bounded by
C_{K}:=\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})\varepsilon}
. For the
\Phi
, we recall the super-algebraic decay bound derived via discrete summation by parts. Since the inequality
|\xi-\eta|\geq\varepsilon
holds, we have

|\widehat{\Phi}(\xi-\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{m}}{\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi-\eta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}|^{m}}.

As in Step 2,
\mathcal{S}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)
factorizes into right- and left-going scalar amplitudes

|\mathcal{S}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)|=|\alpha_{+}(\xi)||\alpha_{-}(\eta)||\mathcal{Q}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)|,

where

\mathcal{Q}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\bm{v}_{ph}^{H}(\eta)\Big[\delta_{t}(\eta)\bm{M}^{h}\delta_{t}(\xi)+e^{-\sqrt{-1}\Delta t\omega_{ph}(\xi)}\mathcal{K}^{h}(\xi)\Big]\bm{v}_{ph}(\xi)
.

Recalling the positive lower bound
E_{1}>0
established in the Step 2, we deduce the inequalities
|\alpha_{+}(\xi)|\leq\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)/E_{1}}
and
|\alpha_{-}(\eta)|\leq\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)/E_{1}}
.
Since the
\mathcal{Q}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)
is continuous over the compact truncated domain
(I_{\delta_{k}})^{2}
, it is bounded by
|\mathcal{Q}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)|\leq C_{Q}
. Applying Young’s inequality yields the bound

|\mathcal{S}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{E_{1}}\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)}\sqrt{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)}\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{2E_{1}}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\Big).

Substituting the bounds into the integral
\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{3}}
, we obtain

|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{3}}|\!\leq\!\frac{C_{K}C_{Q}}{4E_{1}\pi h}\iint_{D_{3}}\frac{C_{m}}{\min_{k}|\xi-\eta-\frac{2k\pi}{h}|^{m}}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\Big)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta\!\leq\!C_{3}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

(57)

For
(\xi,\eta)\in D_{1}
,
\Phi
satisfies
|\widehat{\Phi}(\xi-\eta)|\leq C_{\varphi}
, and applying Young’s inequality yields
|\mathcal{S}_{+-}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{Q}}{2E_{1}}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\big)
.
Substituting these into the integral, we evaluate the contributions of the right-going and left-going energies symmetrically.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{1}}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\varphi}}{2\pi h}\iint_{D_{1}}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi+\eta|}\right)\frac{C_{Q}}{2E_{1}}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\Big)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{Q}C_{\varphi}}{4\pi hE_{1}}\left(\int_{I_{\delta_{k}}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)\left[\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|s|}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]\mathrm{d}\xi\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{Q}C_{\varphi}}{4\pi hE_{1}}\left(\int_{I_{\delta_{k}}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\left[\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|s|}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]\mathrm{d}\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{Q}C_{\varphi}}{4\pi hE_{1}}\!\left[\int_{0}^{\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}}T\mathrm{d}s\!+\!\!\int_{\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}}^{\varepsilon}\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})s}\mathrm{d}s\right]\!\!\left(\int_{I_{\delta_{k}}}\!\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\zeta)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\zeta)\Big)\mathrm{d}\zeta\!\right).

(58)

Substituting
s=\xi+\eta
, the integral of the positive, even, decreasing function
f(s)=\min(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|s|})
over any
2\varepsilon
-interval is maximized at the origin. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\!\frac{C_{Q}C_{\varphi}}{4\pi hE_{1}}\iint_{|\xi-\eta|<\varepsilon}\min\left(T,\frac{C_{1}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})|\xi+\eta|}\right)\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,+}(\xi)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h,-}(\eta)\Big)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\!\frac{1}{2}\frac{C_{2}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left[1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right]\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

(59)

Summing the contributions from the three mutually disjoint regions (
57
), (
58
) and (
5.4
), the total energy induced by the term
\mathcal{I}_{+-}
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{+-}|\leq|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{1}}|+|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{2}}|+|\mathcal{I}_{+-}^{D_{3}}|\leq 2\left[C_{3}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}+\frac{C_{2}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right)\right]\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

The conjugate symmetries
\widehat{\Phi}(\eta-\xi)=\overline{\widehat{\Phi}(\xi-\eta)}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{-+}(\xi,\eta)=\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{+-}(\eta,\xi)}
ensure
\mathcal{I}_{-+}=\overline{\mathcal{I}_{+-}}
. This identical absolute magnitude bounds the cumulative counter-propagating energy by

|\mathcal{I}_{+-}|+|\mathcal{I}_{-+}|\leq 4\left[C_{3}C_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}+\frac{C_{2}}{v_{g}(\delta_{k})}\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon v_{g}(\delta_{k})T}{C_{1}}\right)\right)\right]\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}.

(60)

Step 4: Obtain the Uniform Observability Inequality.

Substituting (
56

6
Conclusions

This paper identifies a structural obstruction to uniform observability for fully discrete high-order
P^{k}
-LDG scheme of the 1-D wave equation. The key mechanism is the coupled
space-time dispersion generated by the fully discrete symbol, which contains one physical branch and
k
spurious branches. This coupling forces the group velocities of relevant modes to vanish at critical frequencies, trapping high-frequency wave packets away from the observation region. Consequently, the observability constant grows at least exponentially as
h\to 0
,
revealing a severe instability of the unfiltered fully discrete dynamics for long-time wave propagation and control.
As a constructive remedy, uniform observability is recovered by projecting onto the physical branch and retaining only frequencies where the group velocity remains positive. Numerical experiments support this mechanism and suggest that higher-order LDG schemes preserve a larger usable physical frequency band, reducing the effective filtering cost and observability time.

While this work employs spectral filtering, bi-grid techniques provide an alternative physical-space regularization mechanism to restore uniform observability. This strategy, pioneered in
[
18
]
and further developed in
[
20
]
, evolves the discrete dynamics on a fine mesh while restricting the initial data to a coarser grid, bypassing explicit frequency-domain truncation. Both approaches suppress discrete modes with vanishing group velocities via distinct projection operators. Extending the bi-grid framework to fully discrete high-order schemes, alongside generalizations to multidimensional domains, variable coefficients, and nonuniform meshes, constitutes a natural direction for future research.
