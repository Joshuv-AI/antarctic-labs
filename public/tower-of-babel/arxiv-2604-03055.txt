Regularization operators for identifying the unknown source in the time-fractional convection-diffusion-reaction equation
Guillermo Federico Umbricht a,∗, Diana Rubio b

Abstract
This article presents a mathematical study of the problem of identifying a time-dependent source term in transport processes described by a time-fractional parabolic equation, based on noisy time-dependent measurements taken at an arbitrary position. The problem is analytically solved using Fourier techniques, and it is shown that the solution is unstable. To address this instability, three one-parameter families of regularization operators are proposed, each designed to counteract the factors responsible for the instability of the inverse operator. Additionally, a new rule for selecting the regularization parameter is introduced, and an error bound is derived for each estimate. Numerical examples with varying characteristics are provided to illustrate the advantages of the proposed strategies.

2
Source identification

In this section, we formally present the inverse problem under study, provide an analytical expression for its solution, and demonstrate that the problem is ill-posed.

2.1
Presentation of the problem

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we aim to determine the source term in the following linear time-fractional parabolic equation on an unbounded domain.

\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\omega\,u_{xx}(x,t)-\beta\,u_{x}(x,t)-\nu\,u(x,t)+f(t),\qquad x>0,\,\,\,t>0,

(1)

where the time-fractional derivative
\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha

(0<\alpha\leq 1)
defined by
[
69
]

\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{\partial u(x,s)}{\partial s}\dfrac{ds}{(t-s)^{\alpha}},&\quad 0<\alpha<1,\\
\dfrac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t},&\quad\alpha=1,\end{cases}

(2)

with
\Gamma
being the Gamma function defined by:

\Gamma(z)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt.

(3)

Without loss of generality, we assume that the initial and boundary conditions are zero, i.e.,

\begin{cases}u(x,t)=0,&\quad x\geq 0,\,\,\,t=0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&\quad x=0,\,\,\,t\geq 0,\end{cases}

(4)

additionally, we assumed that

u(x,t)\textit{ is bounded in }[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty).

(5)

The determination of the source term
f(t)
in (
1
)-(
3
), under the conditions (
4
)-(
5
), is performed using experimental or simulated noisy data, at a specific position
x_{0}
.

u(x_{0},t)=y(t),\qquad y_{\delta}(t)=y(t)+\textit{noise},\,\,\,t\geq 0,

(6)

where
y_{\delta}
represents the noisy data or measurements, and
\delta
denotes the noise in the data. It is also assumed that this noise is bounded, that is,

||y(t)-y_{\delta}(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta,\qquad 0<\delta<\delta_{M},\\

(7)

wich
\delta_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the maximum noise level tolerated. In practice,
\delta_{M}
is determined based on measurement, instrumentation and calibration errors.

2.2
Problem solution

The source estimation problem is solved using the Fourier transform, which is included here for the sake of completeness.

Definition 1

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the Fourier transform
[
70
]
is defined by

\widehat{g}(\xi):=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{e^{-i\xi t}\,g(t)\,dt},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\widehat{g}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the Fourier antitransform is defined by

g(t):=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{e^{i\xi t}\,\widehat{g}(\xi)\,d\xi},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Note 1

The Fourier transform on
L^{2}
is defined as the unitary extension of the Fourier transform from
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
to the whole space
L^{2}
. It preserves the
L^{2}
-norm and is a linear, bounded, bijective, and unitary operator.

By using Definition
1
, we can find the analytical solution to the problem of interest given by (
1
)-(
7
). The solution is presented in the following result.

Theorem 1
(Source identification)

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
and the parameters
\omega,\beta,\nu,x_{0},\delta,\delta_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\delta<\delta_{M}
. Consider functions
u(x,\cdot),f(\cdot),y(\cdot),y_{\delta}(\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
||y-y_{\delta}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta
and
y_{\delta}(t)=y(t)+\textit{noise}
satisfying the parabolic problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\omega\,u_{xx}(x,t)-\beta\,u_{x}(x,t)-\nu\,u(x,t)+f(t),&\quad x>0,\,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&\quad x\geq 0,\,\,\,t=0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&\quad x=0,\,\,\,t\geq 0,\\
u(x,t)\big|_{x\rightarrow\infty}\text{bounded},&\quad t\geq 0\\
u(x,t)=y(t),&\quad x=x_{0},\,\,\,t\geq 0,\end{cases}

(8)

Then, the expression for the source in the Fourier variable is given by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\Lambda(\xi)\widehat{y}(\xi),

where

\Lambda(\xi)=\dfrac{z(\xi)}{1-e^{-h(\xi)\,x_{0}}},\qquad h(\xi)=\dfrac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\,\omega\,z(\xi)}}{2\,\omega},\qquad z(\xi)=\nu+(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}.

Proof 1

In the proof of Theorem
1
, the Fourier transform defined in
1
is applied to the time variable of the system described by (
8
), using the fact that
\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}\,\widehat{u}(x,\xi)

[
4
]
.

\begin{cases}(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}\,\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=\omega\,\widehat{u}_{xx}(x,\xi)-\beta\,\widehat{u}_{x}(x,\xi)-\nu\,\widehat{u}(x,\xi)+\widehat{f}(\xi),&\,x>0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=0,&\,x=0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)\big|_{x\rightarrow\infty}\textit{bounded},&\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=\widehat{y}(\xi),&\,x=x_{0},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(9)

where
\xi
is the Fourier variable. Equivalently, the identification problem given by (
8
) can be reformulated in the frequency domain using (
9
), as follows:

\begin{cases}\omega\,\widehat{u}_{xx}(x,\xi)-\beta\,\widehat{u}_{x}(x,\xi)-z(\xi)\,\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=-\widehat{f}(\xi),&\,x>0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=0,&\,x=0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)\big|_{x\rightarrow\infty}\textit{bounded},&\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=\widehat{y}(\xi),&\,x=x_{0},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(10)

where
z(\xi)
is defined by the following expression:

z(\xi)=\nu+(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}.

(11)

The system described by (
10
) is represented by a second-order non-homogeneous differential equation with an initial condition. The analytical solution to this equation is given by,

\widehat{u}(x,\xi)=\dfrac{1-e^{-h(\xi)\,x}}{z(\xi)}\widehat{f}(\xi),

(12)

where

h(\xi)=\dfrac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\,\omega\,z(\xi)}}{2\,\omega}.

(13)

Since
\widehat{u}(x_{0},\xi)=\widehat{y}(\xi)
, evaluating (
12
) at
x=x_{0}
produces an expression for the source in the frequency domain,

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\Lambda(\xi)\widehat{y}(\xi),

(14)

where
\Lambda
denotes the inverse operator defined by:

\Lambda(\xi)=\dfrac{z(\xi)}{1-e^{-h(\xi)\,x_{0}}}.

(15)

which concludes the proof.

Remark 1

For the particular case
x_{0}=1
,
\omega=1
and
\beta=\nu=0
, it follows that
z(\xi)=(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}
,
h(\xi)=(i\,\xi)^{\alpha/2}
and therefore
\Lambda(\xi)=\dfrac{(i\,\xi)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-(i\,\xi)^{\alpha/2}}}
. This result coincides with the findings in
[
62
,
64
]
, where the estimation of the unknown source in the time-fractional diffusion equation is studied.

2.3
Ill-posed problem

We now demonstrate that the problem of identifying the time-dependent source in a time-fractional parabolic equation from noisy measurements is ill-posed in the sense of Hadamard
[
31
]
, as the solution does not depend continuously on the data. This ill-posedness arises because the inverse operator
\Lambda
(defined in (
15
)) is unbounded, as will be shown below.

Theorem 2
(The problem is ill-posed)

Under the assumptions given in (
1
)-(
7
), the identification problem (
8
) presented in Theorem
1
is an ill-posed problem in the sense of Hadamard, as the solutions do not vary continuously with respect to the data.

Proof 2

We denote
\widehat{f_{\delta}}(\xi)=\Lambda(\xi)\widehat{y_{\delta}}(\xi)
. It is evident that

\|\widehat{f}(\xi)-\widehat{f_{\delta}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\Lambda(\xi)\widehat{y}(\xi)-\Lambda(\xi)\widehat{y_{\delta}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\Lambda(\xi)(\widehat{y}(\xi)-\widehat{y_{\delta}}(\xi))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(16)

on the other hand, by using (
11
), (
13
), and (
15
), we find that,

\begin{split}|\Lambda(\xi)|&=\left|\dfrac{z(\xi)}{1-e^{-h(\xi)\,x_{0}}}\right|=\left|\dfrac{(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu}{1-e^{-\left(\frac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega[(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu]}}{2\omega}\right)\,x_{0}}}\right|=\dfrac{\left|(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu\right|}{\left|1-e^{-\left(\frac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega[(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu]}}{2\omega}\right)\,x_{0}}\right|}\\
&\geq\dfrac{\left|(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu\right|}{1+e^{-\Re\left(\frac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega[(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu]}}{2\omega}\,x_{0}\right)}}=\dfrac{\left|(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu\right|}{1+e^{-\Re\left(\frac{-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega(i\xi)^{\alpha}}}{2\omega}\,x_{0}\right)}},\end{split}

(17)

where

(i\xi)^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\left(\cos(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2})+i\sin(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2})\right),\qquad\xi\geq 0,\\
\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\left(\cos(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2})-i\sin(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2})\right),\qquad\xi<0.\end{cases}

(18)

By substituting (
18
) into (
17
), we find that
\Lambda(\xi)
is unbounded when
|\xi|
tends to infinity. As shown in (
16
), this unboundedness amplifies the error in measurements at high frequencies, potentially resulting in a significant estimation error
\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{f_{\delta}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
, even for very small observation or measurement errors. In other words, the solution to the identification problem (
8
) does not vary continuously with respect to the data (see
[
32
]
).

Not only can it be shown that the operator
\Lambda
is unbounded, but its asymptotic behavior at infinity can also be characterized, as established in the following result.

Proposition 1

The inverse operator
\Lambda
defined in (
15
) satisfies

\dfrac{\left|(i\xi)^{\alpha}+\nu\right|}{1+e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\Re\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega(i\xi)^{\alpha}}\right)}}\leq\left|\Lambda(\xi)\right|\leq\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu}\right)}}.

(19)

Proof 3

The lower bound was established in Equation (
17
) of Theorem
2
. In what follows, the corresponding upper bound is derived. Lemma
4
, applied to the absolute value of (
15
) is used to obtain,

\begin{split}&\left|\Lambda(\xi)\right|=\dfrac{\left|\nu+(i\xi)^{\alpha}\right|}{\left|1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega[\nu+(i\xi)^{\alpha}]}\right)}\right|}\\
&\leq\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{\left|1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega[\nu+\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\pm i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\right)]}\right)}\right|}\\
&=\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{\left|1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\pm 4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)}\right)}\right|}\\
&\leq\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\Re\left(\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\pm 4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)}\right)\right)}}\\
&\leq\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\Re\left(\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\pm 4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\right)}\right)}}\\
&=\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu+4\omega\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)}\right)}}\\
&=\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\left(\nu+\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\,\pi}{2}\right)\right)}\right)}}.\end{split}

Therefore, it follows that

\left|\Lambda(\xi)\right|\leq\dfrac{\nu+|\xi|^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\frac{x_{0}}{2\omega}\left(-\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\omega\nu}\right)}},

(20)

which completes the proof.

3
Regularization operators

When an inverse problem is ill-posed, a regularization method is typically employed to stabilize the solution. In this section, we propose three regularization operators for comparative purposes. We demonstrate the existence of regularization parameters that lead to three convergent methods and address basic theoretical issues related to regularization operators. Readers unfamiliar with this topic may find additional information in
[
32
,
33
]
.

3.1
Regularization solutions

To stabilize the ill-posed problem, we utilize regularization operators.

Definition 2

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Hilbert spaces, and let
T:\mathbb{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbb{X}
a linear unbounded operator. A regularization strategy for
T
is defined as a family of linear bounded operators that satisfies

R_{\mu}:\mathbb{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbb{X},\quad\mu>0,\quad/\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}R_{\mu}y=Ty,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{Y}.

Since the inverse operator (
15
) has a functional form similar to that obtained for the parabolic problem studied in
[
58
]
, we propose using the same three uniparametric families of linear operators, i.e.,

R_{\mu}^{i}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
i=1,2,3
; such that

(R_{\mu}^{1}\,\widehat{y})(\xi):=\dfrac{\Lambda(\xi)}{1+\mu^{2}\,\xi^{2}}\,\widehat{y}(\xi),\qquad(R_{\mu}^{2}\,\widehat{y})(\xi):=\dfrac{\Lambda(\xi)}{1+\mu^{2}\,\xi^{4}}\,\widehat{y}(\xi),\qquad(R_{\mu}^{3}\,\widehat{y})(\xi):=\dfrac{\Lambda(\xi)}{e^{\mu^{2}\,\xi^{2}/4}}\,\widehat{y}(\xi),

(21)

where
\Lambda(\xi)
is defined in (
15
),
R_{\mu}^{i}
with
i=1,2,3
represents regularization strategies for
\Lambda(\xi)
, and
\mu
is the regularization parameter.

Note 2

Note that the denominators in the expressions (
21
), were introduced to ensure that the resulting linear operators
R_{\mu}^{i}
(for
i=1,2,3
) stabilize the solution for the source.

Theorem 3
(Convergent regularization operators)

Consider the source identification problem (
8
).
Let
u(x,\cdot),f(\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy the following system

\begin{cases}\partial_{0^{+}}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\omega\,u_{xx}(x,t)-\beta\,u_{x}(x,t)-\nu\,u(x,t)+f(t),&\quad x>0,\,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&\quad x\geq 0,\,\,\,t=0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&\quad x=0,\,\,\,t\geq 0,\\
u(x,t)\big|_{x\rightarrow\infty}\text{bounded},&\quad t\geq 0.\end{cases}

(22)

and let
\{R_{\mu}^{i}\}
with
i=1,2,3
denote the families of operators defined in (
21
).
Then, for all
y(t)=u(x_{0},t)
there exists an a priori parameter choice rule for
\mu>0
such that the pairs
(R_{\mu}^{i},\mu)
for
i=1,2,3
constitute convergent regularization methods for the identification problem (
22
).

Proof 4

The operators
\{R_{\mu}^{i}\}
with
i=1,2,3
defined in (
21
) are continuous for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and are bounded, that is,

\begin{split}0\leq\lim_{|\xi|\to\infty}\left|R_{\mu}^{i}\right|<\infty,\qquad i=1,2,3.\end{split}

Therefore, for each parameter
\mu>0
, the operators
R_{\mu}^{i}
with
i=1,2,3
are linear, continuous and satisfy the following pointwise convergence:

\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}(R_{\mu}^{i}\,\widehat{y})(\xi)=(\Lambda\,\widehat{y})(\xi),\qquad i=1,2,3,

where
\widehat{y}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then
R_{\mu}^{i}
with
i=1,2,3
are regularization strategies for
\Lambda
. Therefore, according to Proposition 3.4 in
[
32
]
, there exist
a priori
parameter choice rules
\mu
such that
(R_{\mu}^{i},\mu)
with
i=1,2,3
constitute convergent regularization methods (see Definition 3.1 in
[
32
]
) for solving (

). Theorem
4
will provide a rule of choice for the regularization parameter.

The regularized solution of the inverse source identification problem in frequency space, is given by

\widehat{f}_{\delta,\mu}^{i}(\xi)=(R_{\mu}^{i}\,\widehat{y}_{\delta})(\xi),\qquad i=1,2,3.

(23)

3.2
Error Analysis

In this section, we will derive a bound for the error associated with each estimation.

3.2.1
Auxiliary results

To analyze the behavior of the proposed regularizations, we first introduce some results that will be used later to obtain an error bound between the source
f(t)

and each estimate
f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}(t).

Lemma 1

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. If
0<\mu<1
the following inequalities hold

\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{1+\rho^{2}\mu^{2}}<\dfrac{n_{1}}{\mu^{2}},\qquad\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{1+\rho^{4}\mu^{2}}<\dfrac{n_{2}}{\mu^{2}},\qquad\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{e^{\rho^{2}\mu^{2}/4}}<\dfrac{n_{3}}{\mu^{2}},

where
n_{1}=n_{1}(\alpha)
,
n_{2}=n_{2}(\alpha)
and
n_{3}=n_{3}(\alpha)
, are

n_{1}=\dfrac{2-\alpha}{2}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}\right)^{\alpha/2},\,n_{2}=\dfrac{4-\alpha}{4}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}\right)^{\alpha/4},\,n_{3}=\dfrac{(2\alpha)^{\alpha/2}}{e^{\alpha/2}}.

(24)

Proof 5

Let us defined:
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\theta_{1}(\rho)=\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{1+\rho^{2}\mu^{2}},\qquad\theta_{2}(\rho)=\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{1+\rho^{4}\mu^{2}},\qquad\theta_{3}(\rho)=\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha}}{e^{\rho^{2}\mu^{2}/4}}.

The derivatives of the functions
\theta_{i}

i=1,2,3
are obtained analytically

\begin{split}&\theta_{1}^{\prime}(\rho)=\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha-1}\left[\alpha+\rho^{2}\mu^{2}(\alpha-2)\right]}{(1+\rho^{2}\mu^{2})^{2}},\,\\
&\theta_{2}^{\prime}(\rho)=\dfrac{\rho^{\alpha-1}\left[\alpha+\rho^{4}\mu^{2}(\alpha-4)\right]}{(1+\rho^{4}\mu^{2})^{2}},\,\\
&\theta_{3}^{\prime}(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha-1}e^{-\rho^{2}\mu^{2}/4}\left(\alpha-\dfrac{\rho^{2}\mu^{2}}{2}\right).\end{split}

Then, each of the functions
\theta_{i}
, for
i=1,2,3
, has a unique critical point given by:

3.2.2
Analytical bound of error

It is now possible to obtain a bound for the error estimate.

Definition 3

The norm in the Sobolev space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, for
p>0
, is defined as:

\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R})}:=\left(\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{|\widehat{f}|^{2}\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{p}\,d\xi}\right)^{1/2}.

(46)

In the literature on the stabilization of ill-posed problems, specifically in works concerning the estimation of unknown sources, different approaches to define an a priori regularization parameter selection rules can be found, which utilize a bound for the norm of the source (
46
). Since the source is unknown, its norm is also unknown, necessitating the assumption of a value for the bound that may not be accurate. Typically,
C=1
is used (see, for example,
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
), which in some way limits the range of possible sources to be determined. To address this issue, this article proposes an a priori regularization parameter selection rule based solely on the noise level in the data. The regularization parameter is normalized with respect to the maximum tolerated noise. This selection rule allows for the derivation of an analytical expression for the error bound of each estimation, as demonstrated in the following result.

Theorem 4
(Analytical bound for the estimation error)

Consider the inverse problem of determining the source
f(t)
in (
8
). Let
f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}(t)
, with
i=1,2,3
, be the regularization solutions. It is assumed that there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
bounding the norm of
f
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for some
0<p<\infty
(
46
).
Letting

\mu^{2}=\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{2/p+2}<1,

(47)

there exist constants
K_{i}\,(i=1,2,3)
independent of
\delta
such that,

\|f-f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq K_{i}\,\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{2/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p-2/p+2}\right\},\qquad i=1,2,3.

Proof 8

\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi)-R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\,\widehat{y}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\left(1-\frac{R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)}{\Lambda(\xi)}\right)\frac{(1+\xi^{2})^{p/2}}{(1+\xi^{2})^{p/2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

rearranging

\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi)-R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\,\widehat{y}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+\xi^{2})^{-p/2}\left(1-\frac{R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)}{\Lambda(\xi)}\right)\right|\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi)(1+\xi^{2})^{p/2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

By using the definition of the norm in the Sobolev space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
given by the expression (
46
), it results,

\left\|\widehat{f}(\xi)-R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\,\widehat{y}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+\xi^{2})^{-p/2}\left(1-\frac{R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)}{\Lambda(\xi)}\right)\right|\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

(48)

By the triangle inequality, we have

\left\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left\|\widehat{f}-R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\,\widehat{y}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\,\widehat{y}(\xi)-\widehat{f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(49)

From the inequalities (
48
)-(
49

23
) in frequency space, we obtain,

\left\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+\xi^{2})^{-p/2}\left(1-\frac{R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)}{\Lambda(\xi)}\right)\right|\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R})}+\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\right|\left\|\widehat{y}-\widehat{y}_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(50)

Parseval’s identity
[
70

is used in (
50

C
bounds
f
in the norm
H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and the assumption that the error in the data is bounded
(\left\|y-y_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\widehat{y}-\widehat{y}_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta)
. Then,

\left\|f-f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+\xi^{2})^{-p/2}\left(1-\frac{R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)}{\Lambda(\xi)}\right)\right|+\delta\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\left|R_{\mu}^{i}(\xi)\right|.

(51)

By virtue of Lemmas
2

3

\left\|f-f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<C\,\max\left\{\mu^{2},\mu^{p},\mu^{p-2}\right\}+\frac{\delta}{\mu^{2}}\,M_{i},

(52)

where

25

\mu^{2}=\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{2/p+2}
(

\left\|f-f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<C\,\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{2/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p-2/p+2}\right\}+\delta_{M}\,M_{i}\,\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p/p+2},

equivalently

\left\|f-f_{\delta,\mu}^{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<K_{i}\,\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{2/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p/p+2},\left(\dfrac{\delta}{\delta_{M}}\right)^{p-2/p+2}\right\},

4.2
Discussion of results

The numerical examples demonstrate that the three regularization operators presented in this paper effectively address the instability issues in the source estimation solution. Each of the three
operators studied smooths the inverse operator, resulting in a stable recovery.
It is noteworthy that this stability is maintained regardless of the general characteristics of the function being determined, as demonstrated by the satisfactory results achieved even for the discontinuous source. Additionally, the three regularization strategies show to be robust, as the error committed in the estimation decreases as the error in the data decreases.

While no significant differences are observed between the performance of these, the operator
R^{1}_{\mu}
yielded the best results, allowing for the estimation of the source with the lowest relative error at the different disturbance levels considered.

5
Conclusions

This paper addresses a specific case of the inverse source identification problem, focusing on estimating the time-dependent source term in a time-fractional linear parabolic equation using noisy time-dependent data. An analytical solution to the estimation problem is provided, demonstrating that the problem is ill-posed due to the instability of the solution.

To counteract this instability, three families of regularization operators are defined, specifically designed to counterbalance the inestability factor of the inverse operator. Additionally, a rule for selecting the regularization parameters is proposed, based on the noise level in the data and the degree of smoothness of the source to be identified. It is shown that this parameter selection rule leads to stable methods. A bound for the estimation errors is derived, which is optimal.

Two numerical examples of source recovery from different Hilbert spaces are presented. In both cases, the regularization approaches exhibit good performance. A comparison is also carried out between the sources reconstructed using the inverse operator and those obtained through the proposed regularization operators, showing that the latter provide accurate estimations of the considered sources. Moreover, the results suggest that the smoother the high-frequency filtering, the better the performance. This is an interesting aspect that deserves further analysis.
