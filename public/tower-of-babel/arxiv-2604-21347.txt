Abstract
. We study properties of A α p A^{p}_{\alpha} spaces in the Dirichlet range, recently defined by Brevig, Kulikov, Seip and Zlotnikov as the set of all holomorphic functions on the unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} such that ∫ 𝔻 | f ⁡ ( z ) | p − 2 ​ | f ′ ​ ( z ) | 2 ​ ( 1 − | z | 2 ) α ​ 𝑑 A ​ ( z ) < ∞ , \int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty, when 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 and p > 0 p>0 . We answer in the negative two questions posed by Brevig et al. by showing that, if p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 and p > 1 2 p>\frac{1}{2} , A α p A^{p}_{\alpha} is not a vector space and that the norm is in general not increasing in p p . This is achieved by means of an equivalent description for A α p A^{p}_{\alpha} which is given in terms of the Poisson integral of the boundary function of its inhabitants. 1.
Introduction

Let
p>0
be a positive exponent and
\alpha>1
. For these values, the standard weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is defined as the set of all
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{{D}})
such that the quantity

\|f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}:=(\alpha-1)\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z),

where
dA(z)
denotes the normalized Lebesgue area measure of
\mathbb{{D}}
, is finite. It is a well-known fact that, for every
p
and every
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{{D}})
,

\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\|f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\,dt

and thus it makes sense to understand the Hardy space
H^{p}
as
A^{p}_{1}
. These spaces are very classical and have been extensively studied (see for instance the monographs
[
9
,
8
,
13
,
14
]
) but they have recently attracted a lot of attention because of the contractive embeddings between them and the preservation of conformal invariance on these chains of contractive inclusions. Namely, Kulikov showed in
[
15
]
that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{\beta,q}\leq\|f\|_{\alpha,p}

for all
f\in A^{p}_{\alpha}
as long as
p<q
,
1\leq\alpha<\beta
and
\frac{\alpha}{p}=\frac{\beta}{q}
. Furthermore, he also showed that equality in (
1.1
) is attained if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the function

k_{a}(z):=\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}},\quad a\in\mathbb{{D}}.

In other words,
f
must be a multiple of the image of the constant function
1
by the weighted composition operator

(1.2)

T_{a}(g)=T_{a}^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}(g):=\big(\varphi_{a}^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\cdot(g\circ\varphi_{a}),

where
\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}
. Using a change of variables, it can be checked that
\|T_{a}\|
is uniformly bounded (actually, constant) with respect to
a
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
and therefore we say that the
index of conformal invariance
of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is equal to
\frac{\alpha}{p}
(see
[
3
]
). If
p=2
, then
A^{2}_{\alpha}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space and the function
k_{a}
is precisely the kernel at the point
a\in\mathbb{{D}}
.
The second named author of this manuscript
[
16
]
used the results of
[
15
]
to prove that, when
p>2
,

(1.3)

\|f\|_{1,p}\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{c_{2/p}(n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
c_{\alpha}(n)=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)n!}
,
\Gamma
being the Gamma function, and
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence of Taylor coefficients of
f
. It should be noted that the right-hand side of (
1.3
) is finite if and only if
f^{\prime}\in A^{2}_{2/p}
. In other words, if
f
belongs to the Besov (or Dirichlet-type) space
B^{2}_{2/p}
, which is the unique Hilbert space that is conformally invariant with index
\frac{1}{p}
(again, see
[
3
]
).

Since
M_{p}^{p}(r,f):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\,dt
is a non-decreasing function with respect to
r
, one may use integration by parts to show that

(1.4)

\|f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}=|f(0)|^{p}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\dfrac{d}{dr}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\right)(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-1}\,dr,\quad\alpha\geq 1.

Observe that
(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-1}
is also integrable in the range
0<\alpha<1
. This extends the class
A^{p}_{\alpha}
to the range
\alpha\in(0,1)
using (
1.4
) as an alternative expression for
\|f\|_{\alpha,p}
. As was shown in
[
6
]
,
A^{p}_{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
enjoys some of the properties that hold in the Hardy or weighted Bergman case. They showed that the operators defined in (
1.2
) are isometries on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
and that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for every
0<p<q
and
0<\alpha<\beta
with
\frac{\alpha}{p}=\frac{\beta}{q}
. In particular, this also provides an alternative proof for inequality (
1.3
) (which corresponds to the case
p=2
and
\beta=1
). However, we must emphasize that
\|f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}
cannot be expressed as a
L^{1}
norm of
|f|^{p}
(or
|f^{\prime}|^{p}
) with respect to a finite measure on
\overline{\mathbb{{D}}}
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The case
p=2
(that is, the Hilbert case) is the unique exception of this fact. This will be of crucial importance for this work.

An application of Hardy-Stein identity,

\dfrac{d}{dr}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)=\dfrac{p^{2}}{2}\int_{r\mathbb{{D}}}|f(z)|^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dA(z),

shows that

(1.5)

\|f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}\simeq\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}|f(z)|^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|)^{\alpha}\,dA(z),

with equivalence constants depending only on
p
and
\alpha
.
This provides yet another equivalent description of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha>0
. Note that in the range
p=2
and
0<\alpha<1
one recovers the classical Dirichlet-type Hilbert spaces on the unit disc. Moreover, (
1.5
) shows that if
f
does not vanish in
\mathbb{{D}}
then

(1.6)

f\in A^{p}_{\alpha}\iff f^{\frac{p}{2}}\in A^{2}_{\alpha}.

The main goal of this work is to deepen our understanding of the class
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. The following two questions were posed in
[
6
]
:

Question 1.1

(Problem 1,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
p\neq 2
. Is
A^{p}_{\alpha}
a vector space?

Question 1.2

(Problem 2,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
f
be an analytic function on the unit disc and
0<\alpha<1
. Is
p\mapsto\|f\|_{\alpha,p}
increasing?

Hölder’s inequality yields that both questions above have positive answers when
\alpha\geq 1
(that is, when
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is either a Hardy or a weighted Bergman space). We show that Question
1.2
is quite far from having a positive answer in the range
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, since in this range
A^{p}_{\alpha}\not\subset A^{q}_{\alpha}
when
p>q>0
(see Corollary
3.3
). Actually, a direct argument in the whole range
0<\alpha<1
yields that Question
1.2
has a negative answer. Consider
e_{n}(z)=z^{n}
for each integer
n\geq 1
. We have

\|e_{n}\|_{\alpha,p}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\big(npr^{np-1}\big)(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-1}\,dr\right)^{1/p}=\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma\big(\frac{np}{2}+1\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{np}{2}+\alpha\big)}\right)^{1/p}.

Recall that
\Gamma
is a log-convex (and therefore convex) function in
(0,+\infty)
. In particular, it can be checked that it is decreasing in
(0,1]
and increasing in
[2,\infty)
. Thus, we may choose
n>\frac{4}{p}
such that

\Gamma(\alpha)>\Gamma(1)=1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Gamma\left(\frac{np}{2}+1\right)\geq\Gamma\left(\frac{np}{2}+\alpha\right),

so
\|e_{n}\|_{\alpha,p}>1
but
\lim_{q\to\infty}\|e_{n}\|_{\alpha,q}=1
. Hence, these quantities cannot be increasing with respect to the exponent
p
.

We show that also Question
1.1
has a negative answer, at least for all
p>\frac{1}{2}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
p\neq 2
. If
\alpha+p>\frac{1}{2}
, there exist two functions
f
and
g
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
such that
f+g
is not in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Note that
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,p}
is not a proper norm when
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p>\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right\}
. Nevertheless, we will refer to it as a norm of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
even when this class is not a linear space.

Our main tool in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is an equivalent norm for the space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
in the range
0<\alpha<1
, which is of its own interest. Note that
A^{p}_{\alpha}\subset H^{p}
if
\alpha<1
, hence every function
f
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
has boundary values at almost every point of
\mathbb{{T}}:=\partial\mathbb{{D}}
. For an integrable function
g
on
\mathbb{{T}}
, let

P_{z}(g):=\int_{\mathbb{{T}}}g(\xi)\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-\xi|^{2}}~\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}

denote its Poisson integral.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p>0
and
0<\alpha<1
. A function
f
in
H^{p}
is in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if

\rho_{\alpha,p}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\left(P_{z}(|f|^{p})-|f(z)|^{p}\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)<\infty.

This result has to be understood as an analogue of equivalent norms for the Besov spaces deduced by Böe
[
5
]
and Dyakonov
[
10
]
.

Theorem
1.4
is used throughout this note to show that
A^{p}_{\alpha}
spaces enjoy interesting properties even in the Dirichlet range, despite Theorem
1.3
. Suppose that
0<\alpha<1
. Since
A^{p}_{\alpha}\subset H^{p}
, any
f
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
has an inner-outer factorization
f=\Theta h
.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.4
, one recovers the property that if
f
is in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
and
\Theta
is an inner factor that divides
f
, then
f/\Theta
is also in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
(
[
6
, Theorem 1.8]
). Moreover, by using the inner-outer factorization in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
and the equivalent norm from Theorem
1.4
, we provide a more exhaustive version of (
1.6
) which does not require that
f
is non-vanishing:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
p>0
, and
f=\Theta h
a function in
H^{p}
,
\Theta
and
h
being its inner and outer factors, respectively. Then
f
belongs to
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if
\Theta h^{\frac{p}{2}}
is in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
. More generally,
f\in A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if
\Theta h^{\frac{p}{q}}\in A^{q}_{\alpha}
, where
q
is another positive exponent.

Note that, by setting
h=1
in Theorem
1.5
, we can conclude that the membership of an inner function in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
does not depend on
p
. Recall that all inner functions belong to
A^{2}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 1
, but this is not the case in the Dirichlet range. For instance, the atomic singular inner function belongs to
A^{2}_{\alpha}
if and only if
\alpha>1/2
,
[
17
]
. Moreover, Theorem
1.5
and (
1.6
) yield that a singular inner function
S_{\mu}
is in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if any of its powers is.

Theorem
1.5
has consequences for the study of weak products of Dirichlet-type spaces, a description of which is, to this day, missing (see
[
2
, Question 4.7]
). Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of functions on a domain, its weak product
\mathcal{H}\odot\mathcal{H}
is the vector space of those functions that can be written as

(1.7)

h=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}g_{i},\qquad f_{i},g_{i}\in\mathcal{H}.

The infimum of all values of
\sum_{i}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
among all the representations of
h
in (
1.7
) is a norm on
\mathcal{H}\odot\mathcal{H}
. It is well known that
H^{2}\odot H^{2}=H^{1}
. Moreover, the fact that any function in
H^{1}
is the product of two
H^{2}
functions says that
N
can be chosen to be equal to
1
in (
1.7
) for all
h
in
H^{1}
. This has been recently proved to be true for all complete Pick spaces, as a consequence of the column-row property,
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
. As a corollary of Theorem
1.5
, we show that
A^{1}_{\alpha}
is contained in the weak product of
A^{2}_{\alpha}
, thus providing a concrete function theoretical sufficient condition for the membership of a function in the weak product of a Dirichlet-type space:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Then any
f
in
A^{1}_{\alpha}
is the product of two functions in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
.

Note that Theorem
1.3
implies that the inclusion
A^{1}_{\alpha}\subset A^{2}_{\alpha}\odot A^{2}_{\alpha}
must be proper.

This manuscript is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
and its consequences to the inner-outer factorization for
A^{p}_{\alpha}
functions in the Dirichlet range, by proving Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
. As a by-product of our arguments, we show that for
0<\alpha<1
any function in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is the quotient of two bounded functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, where the denominator is outer (see Theorem
2.2
). It is worth mentioning that this is already known for
p=2
,
[
1
]
, since for
0<\alpha\leq 1
the space
A^{2}_{\alpha}
has the complete Pick property. In fact, in that case the denominator can be even chosen to be cyclic in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
, and both the numerator and the denominator are multipliers of
A^{2}_{\alpha}
.

Section
3
uses the properties of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
functions from Section
2
in order to prove Theorem
1.3
. This is achieved by adapting some techniques of Brown and Shields from
[
7
]
in order to study the membership of a concrete class of functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, see Proposition
3.1
.

Finally, Section
4
provides a proof, alternative to the one in
[
6
]
, that the index of conformal invariance of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is
\frac{\alpha}{p}
also for
0<\alpha<1
. Our proof uses Carleson measures for Dirichlet-type spaces and it can be extended to determine the index of conformal invariance of Besov spaces, which were recently found in
[
3
]
, see Remark
4.7
.

2.
An Equivalent Norm on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
in the Dirichlet range

Theorem
1.4
is a consequence of sub-harmonicity. If
u
is sub-harmonic and
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{{D}}})
, Green’s formula yields

(2.1)

P_{z}(u)-u(z)=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}G_{z}(w)\Delta u(w)\,dA(w),

where

G_{z}(w)\coloneqq\log\left|\frac{1-\overline{z}w}{w-z}\right|,\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{{D}}

is the Green function of the unit disc with pole at
w
. Recall that for any analytic function
f
, then
|f|^{p}
is sub-harmonic for all
p>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We apply (
2.1
) with
u=|f|^{p}
. Since
\Delta(|f|^{p})=p^{2}|f^{\prime}|^{2}|f|^{p-2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\left(P_{z}(|f|^{p})-|f(z)|^{p}\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}G_{z}(w)\Delta(|f|^{p})(w)\,dA(w)\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)\\
\simeq&\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}|f(w)|^{p-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}G_{w}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)\right)dA(w).\end{split}

We are therefore left to show that

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}G_{w}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)\simeq(1-|w|)^{\alpha}.

Using a conformal change of variables, the left side of (
2.2
) can be rewritten as

\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{|z|}\right)(1-|\varphi_{w}(z)|^{2})^{\alpha}\dfrac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}=(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{|z|}\right)\dfrac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2\alpha}}dA(z).

Now using that
r\mapsto\log\big(\tfrac{1}{r}\big)/(1-r^{2})
is a decreasing function in
(0,1)
, Chebyshev’s inequality (see
[
11
]
) yields that the quantities

\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\log\left(\frac{1}{|z|}\right)\,dA(z)

and

\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\,dA(z)

are comparable for every
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{{D}})
. Therefore, we are left to observe that the functions

f_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{\alpha}}

have bounded norms in
A^{2}_{\alpha+1}
, uniformly in
w
, if
0<\alpha<1
. This holds because of the fact that
f_{1}\in A^{2}_{\alpha+1}
if and only if
\alpha<\frac{\alpha+1}{2}
. That is, when
\alpha<1
.
∎

For all
p>0
and
\alpha<1
, any function
f
in
A^{p}_{a}
is also in
H^{p}
. In particular, any such
f
can be written as
f=\Theta h
, where
\Theta
is inner and
h
is outer.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
p>0
. Given any function
f
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
and any inner function
\Theta
, the product
\Theta f
belongs to
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}(1-|\Theta(z)|)|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
1.4
and the fact that

P_{z}(|\Theta f|^{p})-|\Theta(z)f(z)|^{p}=P_{z}(|f|^{p})-|f(z)|^{p}+(1-|\Theta(z)|^{p})|f(z)|^{p}

for all
z
in
\mathbb{{D}}
.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
:

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

If
\Theta
is constant then
f
does not vanish, and the results follows directly from (
1.6
).

If
f=\Theta h
is in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, then
h
is in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
thanks to Theorem
1.4
. Therefore
h^{\frac{p}{2}}
is in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
, by (
1.6
). Corollary
2.1
yields that
\Theta h^{\frac{p}{2}}
is in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
. The same argument yields the other direction of the claim.
∎

This yields Corollary
1.6
:

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Let
f=\Theta h
be in
A^{1}_{\alpha}
. By Theorem
1.5
,
\Theta\sqrt{h}
is in
A^{2}_{\alpha}
, and so is
\sqrt{h}
by Theorem
1.4
. Since
f=(\Theta\sqrt{h})\sqrt{h}
, the claim follows.
∎

Finally, we use the inner-outer factorization of a function
f
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
to show that any such function is the quotient of two bounded functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. To see this, note that, given an outer function
h
, one can obtain two truncated outer functions as follows:

h_{\mathrm{min}}(z):=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{{T}}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\log\min\{1,|h(\xi)|\}\,d\xi\right\}

and

h_{\mathrm{max}}(z):=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{{T}}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\log\max\{1,|h(\xi)|\}\,d\xi\right\}.

In particular,
h_{\mathrm{min}}
is bounded by
1
,
h_{\mathrm{max}}
is bounded below by
1
and
h=h_{\mathrm{min}}h_{\mathrm{max}}
. This yields that any function
f=\Theta h
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
can be written as

(2.3)

f(z)=\frac{\Theta(z)h_{\mathrm{min}}(z)}{h^{-1}_{\mathrm{max}}(z)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{{D}},

where both the numerator and the denominator in (
2.3
) are bounded.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
p>0
,
0<\alpha<1
and let
f=\Theta h
be a function in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. Then
\Theta h_{\mathrm{min}}
,
h_{\mathrm{max}}
and
h^{-1}_{\mathrm{max}}
are in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. In particular, any function
f
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
can be written as the quotient of two bounded functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, where the denominator is outer.

Proof.

For
p=2
, this is proved in
[
5
, Thm. 3.3, 3.4]
. For other values of
p
, it follows from the case
p=2
, from Theorem
1.5
and the fact that

h_{\mathrm{min}}^{\frac{p}{2}}=\left(h_{\mathrm{min}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad h_{\mathrm{max}}^{\frac{p}{2}}=\left(h_{\mathrm{max}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

∎

3.
Membership of a concrete class of functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}

We adapt a construction from
[
7
, Prop. 16]
to weighted Dirichlet spaces. We include the proof for completeness, since we will need a more quantitative statement (see Remark
3.4
below).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\geq 0
,
b\in\mathbb{{R}}
and
0<\alpha<1
. Consider the function

f_{a,b}(z):=(1-z)^{b}\exp\left(-a\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right).

Then:

(i)

f_{a,b}
belongs to
A_{\alpha}^{2}
if
b>\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
.

(ii)

If
b>-a
and
b\leq\frac{1}{2}-\alpha
, then
f
does not belong to
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We start with
(i)
. Note that

f_{a,b}^{\prime}(z)=-\big(b(1-z)+2a\big)(1-z)^{b-2}\exp\left(-a\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right).

Therefore,

\|f_{a,b}\|_{\alpha,2}^{2}\lesssim(a+|b|)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|1-z|^{2(b-2)}\bigg|\exp\bigg(-a\frac{1+z}{1-z}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z).

Mapping the disk
\mathbb{{D}}
to the open right half-plane
\mathbb{H}
with the change of variable

w=\frac{1+z}{1-z}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\phi(w)=\frac{w-1}{w+1}=z

and writing
w=u+iv
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{a,b}\|_{\alpha,2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(a+|b|)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}}|1-\phi(w)|^{2(b-2)}|e^{-aw}|^{2}(1-|\phi(w)|^{2})^{\alpha}|\phi^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\,dudv

\displaystyle\simeq(a+|b|)^{2}4^{b}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{\alpha}e^{-2au}\Bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\big(|1+w|^{2}\big)^{-b-\alpha}dv\Bigg)du.

Using a standard change of variables, we can check that

Remark 3.4
.

Using a change of variables and the monotone convergence theorem, we can show that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}j^{1+\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{u^{\alpha}}{(u+1)^{2(jb+\delta+\alpha)-1}}e^{-2jau}du

Proposition 4.1

Proof.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\alpha,p>0
. The weighted composition operators defined by

T_{a}f(z):=\left(\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}f(\varphi_{a}(z)),\quad z\in\mathbb{{D}},

satisfy

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{{D}}}\|T_{a}\|_{\mathcal{B}(A^{p}_{\alpha})}<\infty

if and only if the measures
\{\mu^{\alpha,2}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{{D}}}
(following (
4.2
)) are Carleson for
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, uniformly in
a
.

Proof.

Recall that

\|T_{a}f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}\simeq\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|T_{a}f(z)\big|^{p-2}\big|(T_{a}f)^{\prime}(z)\big|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}~dA(z).

Since

(T_{a}f)^{\prime}=\dfrac{\alpha}{p}\big(\varphi_{a}^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}-1}\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}f\big(\varphi_{a})+\big(\varphi_{a}^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}+1}f^{\prime}(\varphi_{a})

we have that

|I_{1}-I_{2}|\lesssim\|T_{a}f\|_{\alpha,p}^{p}\lesssim I_{1}+I_{2},

where:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(\varphi_{a}(z))\big|^{p-2}\big|f^{\prime}(\varphi_{a}(z))\big|^{2}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\big|^{\alpha+2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}~dA(z),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(\varphi_{a}(z))\big|^{p}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\big|^{\alpha-2}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)\big|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}~dA(z).

Thanks to conformal invariance, the integral
I_{1}
is comparable to
\|f\|^{p}_{\alpha,p}
, independently on
a
:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(z)\big|^{p-2}\big|f^{\prime}(z)\big|^{2}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(\varphi_{a}(z))\big|^{\alpha+2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{\alpha+2}~\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(z)\big|^{p-2}\big|f^{\prime}(z)\big|^{2}\left(\frac{1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2}}{|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)|}\right)^{\alpha+2}~\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(z)\big|^{p-2}\big|f^{\prime}(z)\big|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}~dA(z)

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|^{p}_{\alpha,p},

since

\frac{1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2}}{|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}=1-|z|^{2}.

Hence we are left to show that
I_{2}
is controlled by
\|f\|^{p}_{\alpha,p}
, uniformly in
a
.

By taking a derivative of the identity

\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(\varphi_{a}(z))=\frac{1}{\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)}=-\frac{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2}}{1-|a|^{2}}

we obtain

\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{a}(z))=-\frac{\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)^{3}}=-2\overline{a}\frac{(1-\overline{a}z)^{3}}{(1-|a|^{2})^{2}}.

Hence using the conformal invariance of the hyperbolic measure we have that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(\varphi_{a}(z))\big|^{p}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\big|^{\alpha-2}\big|\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(z)\big|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}~\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}.

\displaystyle\simeq|a|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(z)\big|^{p}\left(\frac{1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2}}{|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)|}\right)^{\alpha+2}~\frac{dA(z)}{\left|1-\overline{a}z\right|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=|a|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{{D}}}\big|f(z)\big|^{p}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}~dA(z)

which is controlled by
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{{D}},d\mu^{\alpha,2}_{a}\right)}^{p},
uniformly in
a
if and only if
\{\mu_{a}^{\alpha,2}\}_{a\in\mathbb{{D}}}
are uniformly Carleson measures for
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.
∎
