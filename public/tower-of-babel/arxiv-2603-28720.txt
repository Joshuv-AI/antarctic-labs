Categorical Time-Reversal Symmetries
Rui Wen, Sakura Schäfer-Nameki

Abstract
The classification of phases using categorical symmetries has greatly expanded the landscape of gapped and gapless phases. So far, however, these developments have largely been restricted to phases with unitary (higher-)categorical symmetries over ℂ \mathbb{C} . In this work, we incorporate anti-unitary symmetries, such as time-reversal symmetry ℤ 2 T \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{T} , and show that the relevant physical structures are naturally described by fusion categories over ℝ \mathbb{R} . A class of real fusion categories, which we call Galois-real fusion categories , provides the correct categorical model for anti-unitary symmetries. A simple example is the time-reversal symmetry ℤ 2 T \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{T} itself. We discuss the basic structures of real fusion categories and present a range of examples, including the group-theoretical categories ( G T ) ω (G^{T})^{\omega} and 𝖱𝖾𝗉 ⁡ ( G T ) \mathsf{Rep}(G^{T}) associated to anti-linear groups G T G^{T} , as well as non-invertible time-reversal symmetries described by a real analogue of Tambara–Yamagami fusion categories. We then classify gapped phases enriched with anti-linear symmetries in terms of module categories over Galois-real fusion categories. We furthermore apply the categorical formulation to prove dualities (i.e. gauge or Morita equivalences) of anti-linear symmetries generated by gauging subgroups. Complementing this, we also develop a Symmetry Topological Field Theory (SymTFT) framework, in which Galois-real fusion categories arise as boundary conditions of a ℤ 2 T \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{T} -enriched SymTFT. Morita equivalent anti-linear symmetries are shown to arise as different boundaries of the same ℤ 2 T \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{T} -enriched SymTFT.

Two types of Real Fusion Categories.

Unlike (unitary) fusion categories that are defined over

a local Hamiltonian, a defect is a subspace of the total lattice Hilbert space that looks like the ground state space of
H
near spatial infinity. In other words a defect is the ground state space of a Hamiltonian that only differs from

in a local region. When symmetry is involved, a symmetric defect is the ground state space of a symmetric Hamiltonian that differs from
H
in a local region. In this language, the trivial defect is the ground state space of
H
itself. The physical reason behind this definition is that in the IR limit only the ground state space is “visible” for a gapped system.

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}

The other phase is an SSB-phase for

We prove the following theorem:

Theorem II.2
.

The category of symmetric defects in a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SSB phase is
\mathsf{Vec}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}}:=\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathsf{Vec})

First let us again motivate this from the spin-chain. In the spin system it is obtained by considering
J=1
, then ground state space of the system is

\Omega=\left\{z|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle+w|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle\,;\ z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\,,

(II.16)

where
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle
are the eigenstates of
Z
with eigenvalues
\pm 1

\mathsf{D}_{k}:=\prod_{j>k}X_{j}\,,

(II.17)

which acts on the ground state space as

\mathsf{D}_{k}:\hskip 10.00002pt\left\{\begin{aligned} \penalty\ &|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle\mapsto|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle\\
&|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle\mapsto|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow_{k}\uparrow_{k+1}\cdots\uparrow\rangle\end{aligned}\right.\,.

(II.18)

This must be a non-trivial defect, since any local operator cannot change the behavior of a ground state near spatial infinity. The location
k
of the defect is irrelevant for the topological sector of the defect, so we will call this defect
\mathsf{D}
. Fusion of defects is defined by concatenation. The fusion
\mathsf{D}\otimes\mathsf{D}
is therefore represented by the space spanned by the states with two domain walls:

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\otimes\mathsf{D}

\displaystyle\simeq\left\{z|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{j}\downarrow_{j+1}\cdots\downarrow_{k}\uparrow_{k+1}\cdots\uparrow\rangle\right.

\displaystyle+w|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow_{j}\uparrow_{j+1}\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\uparrow\rangle\left|\,z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}\,.

(II.19)

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric local operator
\prod_{j<l<k+1}X_{l}
kills the pair of domain walls. We conclude the fusion rule is
\mathsf{D}\otimes\mathsf{D}\simeq\mathbf{1}
.

To fully determine the structure of the category of symmetric defects we need to do more work.
Next we consider the endomorphisms of the trivial defect
\Omega
. Assume that there is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric local operator
\mathcal{O}
that acts on the ground state space as a general linear transformation

(z,w)\mapsto(\alpha z+\beta w,\gamma z+\eta w)\,,

(II.20)

where
(z,w)
is short hand for
z|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle+w|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle
. Local operators cannot change the behavior of states near spatial infinity, hence
\beta=\gamma=0
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha z,\eta w)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(z,w)=U_{T}\mathcal{O}U_{T}(z,w)

\displaystyle=U_{T}\mathcal{O}(w^{*},z^{*})=U_{T}(\alpha w^{*},\eta z^{*})=(\eta^{*}z,\alpha^{*}w).

(II.21)

We see that
\eta=\alpha^{*}
. Therefore the only allowed maps of defects are

(z,w)\mapsto(\alpha z,\alpha^{*}w),\penalty\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}\,.

(II.22)

We conclude that in the category of symmetric defects in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SSB phase we have

\text{End}(\mathbf{1})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}\,.

(II.23)

One might conclude that the category of symmetric defects is the (complex) fusion category
\mathsf{Vec}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}
, with the non-trivial simple object being the domain wall defect
\mathsf{D}
. However, this is not the case and a subtle but crucial difference will be revealed by further analysis. Let us consider a domain wall
\mathsf{D}_{k}
at site

realizing the endomorphism
(z,w)\mapsto(\alpha z,\alpha^{*}w)
at site

. On the

\mathcal{O}_{j}
acts as
\alpha\mathrm{id}_{\uparrow}

, we notice the crucial relation

\mathcal{O}_{j}^{\alpha}\mathsf{D}_{k}=\mathsf{D}_{k}\mathcal{O}_{l}^{\alpha^{*}}(Z_{j}Z_{l})

(II.24)

The local operator

is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric hence belongs to the trivial superselection sector. Therefore up to

-symmetric local operators we have the relation

\mathcal{O}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathsf{D}=\mathsf{D}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\alpha^{*}}\,,

(II.25)

which says that a local scalar

is complex conjugated when it passes through the defect
\mathsf{D}
. This is expected: the defect
\mathsf{D}
is a

Another way to look at this is the following. The defect
\mathsf{D}_{k}
is the same as
\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathsf{D}_{k}

\mathsf{D}
by putting the operator

to the left of the domain wall

(
j<k
). This leads to an endomorphism:

\mathsf{D}_{k}\to\mathsf{D}_{k}:\penalty\ \hskip 20.00003pt\begin{aligned} {}&|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{j}|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle=\alpha|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle\\

(II.26)

Similarly, the defect
\mathsf{D}_{k}
is the same as
\mathsf{D}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{1}
, therefore putting any endomorphism of
\mathbf{1}
to the right of
\mathsf{D}_{k}
also gives an endomorphism of
\mathsf{D}_{k}

\mathcal{O}_{j}

\mathsf{D}_{k}

\mathsf{D}_{k}\to\mathsf{D}_{k}:\hskip 10.00002pt\begin{aligned} {}&|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{j}|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle=\alpha^{*}|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow_{k}\downarrow_{k+1}\cdots\downarrow\rangle\\

(II.27)

We see that the left and right actions on

-SSB phase has the following properties.

•

\mathbf{1},\mathsf{D}
.

•

The fusion rule is
\mathsf{D}\otimes\mathsf{D}=\mathbf{1}

•

The object
\mathbf{1}
satisfies

z\otimes\mathsf{D}=\mathsf{D}\otimes z^{*},\penalty\ z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Although

II.3
\mathbb{H}
is for Haldane

We have discussed the symmetry and charge categories associated with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
symmetry. Similar to the case with unitary group
G
symmetries, if there are two SPTs protected by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
, then the category of symmetric defects is
\mathsf{Rep}({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
in either SPT.
Hence the category of bulk symmetric defects cannot distinguish different SPTs.
There are generally two approaches to detecting SPT phases. One is via boundary/edge measurements, the other is via bulk string order parameters. The latter approach naturally leads to the SymTFT paradigm by realizing that the string order parameters are naturally anyons of a
(2+1)
d topological order, that end on the physical boundary but do not end on the symmetry one.

In this section we consider such approaches to the non-trivial
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SPT called the Haldane chain. The physical properties of the Haldane chain, including its boundary modes, are well-known in physics. Our focus here is on the categorical characterization of the Haldane chain. We will discuss two aspects of the Haldane chain from a categorical perspective. First, we show that the category of boundary conditions in the Haldane chain is
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{H}}
, where
\mathbb{H}
is the quaternion algebra. Second, we will show that the Haldane chain does not have a string order parameter, consequently it cannot be described by anyon condensation in a
(2+1)
d SymTFT as for SPTs with unitary
G
-symmetry. Finally, we will provide a module category approach that provides a complete characterization of
(1+1)
d phases with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetry.

II.3.1
The Haldane Chain and its Edge-Modes

The Haldane phase with one boundary can be modeled by the following Hamiltonian:

H_{0}=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2}

(II.29)

with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetry generated as in (
II.3
) by

U_{T}=K\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}X_{i}=KU\,.

(II.30)

The ground state space of
H_{0}
, denoted as
\Omega
, is two dimensional. One checks that the operators

\mathcal{X}:=X_{0}Z_{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{Z}:=Z_{0}

(II.31)

commute with the Hamiltonian, hence they act on the ground state space as a pair of Pauli operators. We will write
|\uparrow\rangle_{0},|\downarrow\rangle_{0}
for the basis of
\Omega
that are eigenstates of
\mathcal{Z}
. Since we do not have explicit ground state wavefunction, we will work out the action of
U_{T}
on the ground state space algebraically. Notice that
U_{T}\mathcal{X}U_{T}=-\mathcal{X}
and
U_{T}\mathcal{Z}U_{T}=-\mathcal{Z}
. Assume
U_{T}|\uparrow\rangle_{0}=e^{i\theta}|\downarrow\rangle_{0}
, then

U_{T}|\downarrow\rangle_{0}=U_{T}\mathcal{X}|\uparrow\rangle_{0}=-e^{i\theta}|\uparrow\rangle_{0}

(II.32)

Next we consider endomorphisms of the boundary condition
\Omega
. Similar to the case with defects, a morphism of boundary conditions is a linear map between ground state spaces that can be realized by symmetric local operators. Assume there is a local operator
\mathcal{O}
such that

\mathcal{O}|\uparrow\rangle_{0}=\alpha|\uparrow\rangle_{0}+\beta|\downarrow\rangle_{0},\penalty\ \mathcal{O}|\downarrow\rangle_{0}=\gamma|\uparrow\rangle_{0}+\delta|\downarrow\rangle_{0}.

(II.33)

Using
U_{T}\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}U_{T}
and the action of
U_{T}
on the ground state space, we obtain that
\delta=\alpha^{*},\penalty\ \gamma=-\beta^{*}
. Hence in the
\mathcal{Z}
-basis the most general form of an endomorphism is

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
-\beta^{*}&\alpha^{*}\end{pmatrix}

(II.34)

Such matrices form an
\mathbb{R}
-linear algebra, with an
\mathbb{R}
-linear basis given by
\mathbf{1},i\mathcal{X},i\mathcal{Y},i\mathcal{Z}
(
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
are Pauli matrices). Checking their algebraic relations, we see that the endomorphism algebra of the boundary
\Omega
is exactly the quaternion algebra
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\langle 1,i,j,k\rangle/\langle i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=ijk=-1\rangle
. More generally, we observe that the restriction of
U_{T}
on the ground state space satisfies
U_{T}^{2}=-1
. This is true not only for the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
but for any boundary condition: assume there is a Hamiltonian with one boundary that differs from
H_{0}
only near the boundary. Then there exists some
k>0
such that
Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2}=1
on the ground state space for any
i\geq k
. Therefore restricting to the ground state space we can replace
U_{T}
by

U_{T}\prod_{i\geq k}(Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2})=X_{0}X_{1}\cdots X_{k}(Z_{k}Z_{k+1})

(II.35)

which is supported near the boundary and squares to
-1
. Hence no matter what the Hamiltonian looks like near the boundary, the ground state space will be acted by an anti-unitary operator that squares to
-1
. To formalize the situation let us introduce the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}]^{\eta}
, which as a linear space is
\{z+wU_{T}|z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
, and satisfies
zU_{T}=U_{T}z^{*},\penalty\ U_{T}^{2}=-1
. Then the above discussion shows any boundary condition leads to a ground state space that is a representation of the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}]^{\eta}
. In fact

{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}]^{\eta}\simeq\mathbb{H}

(II.36)

follows by identifying

i\leadsto i,\ U_{T}\leadsto j,\ iU_{T}\leadsto k\,.

(II.37)

We conclude that the category of boundary conditions of the Haldane chain is exactly
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{H}}
the category of
\mathbb{H}
-modules. The specific boundary condition
H_{0}
can be thought of as the minimal boundary condition that corresponds to the simple object
\mathbb{H}\in\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{H}}
.

II.3.2
Lack of string orders

For an SPT protected by a unitary
G
-symmetry, it is well-known that string order parameters can be used to detect the SPT phase. Analyzing the mathematical structure of string order parameters is in fact one path towards the SymTFT paradigm. We now consider the situation with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SPT. We will give arguments that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SPTs do not have string order parameters.

A string order parameter should be a truncation of the symmetry generator on finite intervals (or half-infinite chain). This means the string order acts trivially on operators away from the interval and acts the same way as the symmetry generator on operators deep inside the interval. However, since complex conjugation is involved in the generator
U_{T}
, there is no sensible truncation of
U_{T}
to finite intervals. This is because complex conjugation always acts non-trivially everywhere. We may write down
(U_{T})_{jk}:=K\prod_{j<l<k}X_{l}
, but
(U_{T})_{jk}
is
not
supported on the interval
[j,k]
. For example
(U_{T})_{jk}(Y_{n})(U_{T})_{jk}=-Y_{n},\penalty\ \forall n\notin[j,k]
.

A mathematical argument is given by computing the Drinfeld center of the symmetry category
\mathsf{Vec}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}}
. For a unitary
G
-symmetry, the collection of all local and string order parameters form the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{G})
. We will argue later (
Section

V.2
) that this is still true for anti-linear symmetries. However, it is known that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}})=\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{R}}
is trivial. It will be more illuminating to look at the structure of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{G^{T}})
. For
\mathsf{Vec}_{G}
, we know that an object in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{G})
is labeled by a flux sector and a charge sector. For
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{G^{T}})
this is still true, but the flux sector is strictly in
G_{0}<G^{T}
, the unitary subgroup. This reflects the physical fact that any anti-unitary symmetry does not have a string order, due to the non-local nature of complex conjugation, see also
Pollmann:2012xdn
.

III

One major goal of this paper is to establish a categorical framework for
(1+1)

It turns out there are two types of real fusion categories that behave quite differently. We will define two types of real fusion categories as follows:

A fusion category

In the following we will discuss in detail the two types of real fusion categories. We will also discuss the roles of these real fusion categories in describing anti-linear symmetries. Examples will be discussed in the next section.

III.1
\mathbb{R}
-Real Fusion Categories

We now specialize to the case of a real fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with

(III.1)

i.e. the
\mathbb{R}
-real

\mathbb{R}
. By the classification of finite-dimensional division algebras over
\mathbb{R}
, this forces

(III.2)

Physical interpretation.

An
\mathbb{R}
-real fusion category naturally appears as the category of symmetric defects in a system with unbroken time-reversal symmetry (such as
\mathsf{Rep}({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric phases). Given an
\mathbb{R}
-real fusion category
\mathcal{C}
of symmetric defects, we can “forget” time reversal by extending scalars from
\mathbb{R}
to
{\mathbb{C}}

\mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{C}}\;:=\;\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{R}}}\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{C}}\,,

(III.3)

which is now a fusion category over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Here

is the category of finite dimensional real vector spaces, and

-real fusion category

can be recovered by remembering the time-reversal action on the complex fusion category

•

If a simple object

is real, then it comes from a simple object

that is invariant under time-reversal and

. In other words it is a time-reversal singlet.

•

If a simple

and time-reversal exchanges

•

If a simple
X\in\mathcal{C}

that is invariant under time-reversal and
T^{2}|_{X}=-1
. In other words it is a Kramers doublet.

An
\mathbb{R}
-real fusion category is rigid, hence all objects have duals. It is useful to introduce an indicator that is defined for a simple

The multiplicity of the unit on the RHS is determined by the type of the object

Physical Interpretation.

A Galois-real fusion category can arise in two situations. First it can be the category of symmetric defects in a time-reversal broken system, as we have seen in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SSB example.
The reason that time-reversal breaking leads to Galois-real fusion category can be understood as follows. Since time-reversal is broken we have two conjugate vacua, and local operators take the form
(z\,\text{id}_{1},w\,\text{id}_{2})
where
\text{id}_{1/2}

(z,w)\mapsto(w^{*},z^{*})

(z,z^{*})
which still form a copy of
{\mathbb{C}}

\mathcal{C}_{1}
consists of defects within each vacuum, and
\mathcal{C}_{T}
consists of domain walls between the two vacua. Compare with the time-reversal unbroken case: there is only one vacuum and the symmetric local operators are

\mathbb{R}
-real.

Second, a

We reject this interpretation as complex numbers should always be a symmetry of any quantum system. Allowing an

III.3

The theory of module categories over real fusion categories will be crucial to the categorical formulation and classification of gapped phases. The basic structure of the theory of module categories does not depend on the ground field. In fact, module category can be defined for any monoidal category, and we direct the reader to

In this paper by a module category we always mean a finite semi-simple one, meaning that the underlying category of the module category is finite semi-simple. The key structural results that we will use about module categories are the following, which was proven in

Next we consider the

The following is a direct generalization of

The category of symmetric defects in a

where

Example IV.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}^{T}
be the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
and the generator

is anti-linear.
Thus we have

\displaystyle s:\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}^{T}

\displaystyle\to\ {\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}

(IV.10)

\displaystyle T^{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\ T^{i\,\text{mod}\,2}\,.

Then

\ker(s)={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\{1,U\}

IV.9
). The trivial representation contributes

such that

. This leaves us two possibilities: either

and

is of real type, or

and

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
as
(z,w),\penalty\ z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}
, define
T(z,w)=(-\overline{w},\overline{z})
, then

and this realizes the non-trivial quaternionic representation of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}^{T}
. We conclude that there is only one non-trivial simple object in
\mathsf{Rep}({\mathbb{Z}}_{4}^{T})
, which we denote by
Q
. According to the formula (
III.5
), the tensor product

contains four copies of
\mathbf{1}
. Dimension counting shows it contains nothing more. Hence we have the following fusion rule.

\displaystyle Q\otimes Q\simeq\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}=4\cdot\mathbf{1}.

(IV.11)

An interesting feature about

is an abelian group, its representation category

IV.2.1
Examples:
\mathsf{Rep}(A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})

Let

be an abelian group and
A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
the direct product with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
. We now compute the structure of

\mathsf{Rep}(A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})\,.

(IV.12)

Let
R=(V,\rho)\in\mathsf{Rep}(A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
, since
T\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
commutes with elements of

, we can choose a basis for

so that

and

(V,\rho|_{A})
is a representation of

that is invariant under a complex conjugation on

is a representation of

\gamma^{2}\neq 1

•

•

It is direct to verify that the dimension formula (

) is satisfied: an object

\gamma^{-1}

F_{\gamma}\otimes F_{\gamma^{\prime}}=F_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}},\penalty\ F_{\gamma}\otimes E_{\gamma^{\prime}}=E_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}

(IV.13)

Fusion rules for

can be obtained using fusion rules of

and is generically non-invertible. We do not provide a general formula here since it is not very illuminating.

Remark IV.1
.

Let
G_{0}:=\ker s<G^{T}
be the subgroup of linear elements. It is always possible to describe
\mathsf{Rep}(G^{T})
in terms of
\mathsf{Rep}(G_{0})
together with a time-reversal action on
\mathsf{Rep}(G_{0})
. By a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-action on a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
we mean an

tensor equivalence

, together with equivalence of tensor functors
\mathcal{F}_{T}\circ\mathcal{F}_{T}\simeq\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
. This philosophy is known as Galois descent in mathematics. For

\mathsf{Rep}(A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
is the same as
\mathsf{Rep}(A)\simeq\widehat{A}
together with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-action on
\widehat{A}
. Here the action is simple to describe:
T:\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}
. To recover
\mathsf{Rep}(A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
, we take the (homotopy)
fixed points
of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-action. This is exactly what we did earlier: we took combinations of characters of
A
that are invariant under the action
T:\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}
.

Example IV.4
.

Let us consider a concrete example. Setting

\displaystyle=2\mathbf{1}\oplus 2F

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}\otimes F_{\gamma^{2}}\simeq F_{\gamma^{2}}\otimes E_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=E_{\gamma}

(IV.14)

We note that the

Remark IV.2
.

We discussed earlier how

Example IV.5
.

Let us consider a concrete example, let

Example IV.6

We now consider the group

IV.2.3

\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})

We discuss more the structure of real fusion categories of the form

\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})\,.

(IV.20)

According to Galois descend,
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
can be understood as
\mathsf{Rep}(G)
equipped with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-action. Here the action is straightfoward:

where
R^{*}
is the complex conjugate of

. If

is not invariant under

, meaning that
R^{*}
is not equivalent to

, then the invariant combination is

, which descends to a complex simple object in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
. On the other hand if

, then

descends to either a real object or a quaternion object in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
. Which case is true can be determined by the Frobenius–Schur indicator, defined as

\displaystyle\nu(R):=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{2})\in\{-1,0,1\}

(IV.21)

\chi(g):=\Tr(\rho(g))
is the character of the representation
(\rho,V)
. A standard result of representation theory is that the fate of a simple

in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
is completely determined by its Frobenius–Schur indicator.

•

If
\nu(R)=1
, then
R\simeq R^{*}
and
R
is well-defined over
\mathbb{R}
. This means we can choose a basis such that

are real matrices. Then
R
becomes a real object in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
.

•

If
\nu(R)=0
, then
R\neq R^{*}
. The sum
R\oplus R^{*}
is a complex object in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
.

•

If
\nu(R)=-1
, then
R\simeq R^{*}
but
R
is not well-defined over
\mathbb{R}
. This means there is no basis in which
R
is written as real matrices. In this case although
R\simeq R^{*}
, to make a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-invariant object we still need to make a direct sum

, which is a quaternion simple in
\mathsf{Rep}(G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T})
.

Let us check the validity of the dimension formula (
IV.9
). A simple

with
\nu(R)=1
contributes
2\frac{d_{R}^{2}}{1}=2d_{R}^{2}
; a simple

with
\nu(R)=0
gives rise to the complex simple

, which contributes

; a simple

with
\nu(R)=-1
gives rise to the quaternion simple

, which contributes
2\frac{(2d_{R})^{2}}{4}=2d_{R}^{2}
. In total the sum is

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Rep}(G),\nu(R)=1}2d_{R}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu(R)=0}4d_{R}^{2}+\sum_{\nu(R)=-1}2d_{R}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{R}d_{R}^{2}=2|G|=|G\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}|

(IV.22)

where the
\frac{1}{2}
in front of the second term is due to over-counting since
R\oplus R^{*}=R^{*}\oplus R
.

V.2
Order Parameters and Drinfeld Center

For an internal categorical symmetry

, the SymTFT is given by the Drinfeld center

. For instance, when

, and a dyonic anyon

corresponds to a string order parameter with flux sector

and charge decoration

As we have discussed for time-reversal in
Section

, a pure time-reversal symmetry does not have a string order that can detect the Haldane phase. This is not special to time-reversal: any anti-linear symmetry generator does not have a string order parameter. We will argue that given this observation, the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{T})
is still the correct category of all local and string order parameters of an anti-linear symmetry
\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{T}
. Drinfeld center is a notion that can be defined for any monoidal category, hence well-defined for real fusion categories, and in particular for Galois-real fusion categories. For a categorical anti-linear symmetry
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{T}
, a simple object in

is again given by a pair

, where

and

is the half-braid. However, as shown in

, the object

is in fact strictly in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, otherwise there is no consistent half-braid

. This is because, assuming a half-braid

does exists, then it gives natural isomorphism

, which identifies the left and right actions by scalars, implying

must be a linear(Galois-trivial) object
x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. This precisely matches with the expectation that an anti-linear symmetry generator does not have a string order parameter: assume a string order parameter does exist, then it can be used to identify the action by scalars from two sides of the string order:
z
z
x\in\mathcal{C}

which implies
x
is a linear symmetry generator. Hence the mathematical machinery of Drinfeld center surprisingly still captures the correct physical picture.

It is illuminating to consider the structure of

for a general (non-anomalous) group-like symmetry

. A simple object in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{G^{T}})

contains a copy of

-real fusion category.

Here we are not assuming any SymTFT interpretation of the Drinfeld center, simply viewing it as the category of 1D order parameters associated with

. The SymTFT interpretation will be discussed in

V.3
Duality and Gauging

One advantage of the categorical formulation of symmetries and phases is that it allows for straightfoward computation of duality and gauging. By that we mean that there is a well-developed Morita theory for (complex) fusion categories that capture exactly the physical operation of (generalized) gauging
Bhardwaj:2017xup
.

V.3.1
Gauging between Symmetry Categories

Recall that for two complex fusion categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
, we say they are Morita equivalent if there is some algebra
A\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathsf{Bimod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
are equivalent as fusion categories. In this case we say
\mathcal{D}
is obtained by condensing
A
in
\mathcal{C}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
are Morita equivalent if there is a
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{D}\simeq\text{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M})
. If
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
are symmetry categories for two internal generalized symmetries, then being Morita equivalent has the consequence that the 2-categories of gapped phases with symmetry
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
:
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{C}),\penalty\ \mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{D})
are equivalent. This means we can relabel phases enriched with symmetry
\mathcal{C}
as phases enriched with symmetry
\mathcal{D}
in such a way that does not affect any physical observables.

The Morita theory works well over
\mathbb{R}
as well, see our discussion in
Section

III.3
. Hence we say two anti-linear symmetry categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
are dual to each other if they are Morita equivalent as real fusion categories. This means there exists some algebra
A\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathsf{Bimod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
. Notice that since both
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
are Galois-real, the algebra
A
is necessarily in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
according to
Theorem

III.4
. This is the mathematical manifestation of the fact that one cannot literally gauge time-reversal, or any anti-linear symmetry generator. The gauging always happens inside the linear part
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Then if
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
are two dual anti-linear symmetries, the 2-category of gapped phases:
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{C}),\penalty\ \mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{D})
are equivalent.

V.3.2
Gauging between Gapped Phases

Although the symmetry categories are always Galois-real, the category of symmetric defects in a gapped phase with symmetry
\mathcal{C}
can be either Galois-real or
\mathbb{R}
-real. For instance, with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetry, the category of symmetric defects is
\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathbb{R}}
in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SPT phases. Given an anti-linear symmetry

, the category of symmetric defects in a gapped phase defined by a module category

over
\mathcal{C}
is
\text{End}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{C})

is Morita equivalent to

VI
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-Enriched Topological Orders and Their Boundaries

The SymTFT has been a powerful means of classification of phases, gapped and gapless, for categorical symmetries. In this section we discuss how such a SymTFT approach needs to be modified in the presence of time-reversal symmetry to consider
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetry-enriched SymTFTs instead.

In
Section

VI.1
we argue that there is a fundamental obstruction for gauging time-reversal, or any anti-linear symmetry, within the framework of standard quantum theory. This will motivate the use of a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-enriched SymTFT for anti-linear symmetries as in
Pace:2025hpb

Then in
Section

(2+1)

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-enriched non-chiral topological orders (SETs), including a complete bulk-boundary relation.

VI.1
T-Enrichment versus Gauging

For a
(d+1)
D internal finite
G
-symmetry, the standard SymTFT paradigm identifies the SymTFT as a
(d+2)
D
G
-gauge theory. Following this principle one tries to define the SymTFT for an anti-linear symmetry, e.g. the time-reversal
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}

(d+2)
D “
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-gauge theory”. However, there is a fundamental obstruction for gauging time-reversal.
Coupling a field theory to time-reversal background is really putting the theory on an unoriented manifold
M
, whose first Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{1}(M)
can be thought of as the time-reversal background field. However, unlike the background gauge field for an internal symmetry, the time-reversal background field
w_{1}(M)
is determined by the underlying manifold
M
and we are not allowed to vary it. Hence the only meaningful way of dynamically gauging time-reversal is to sum over spacetime topologies, which forces us into the realm of quantum gravity. Since we would like to stay in the realm of (topological) quantum field theory, we will not take this approach for the SymTFT for
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
, or any anti-linear symmetry. Instead, we argue that it suffices to take an unoriented TFT, or in condensed matter language, a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-enriched topological order, as our SymTFT for an anti-linear symmetry. We will show that for a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-enriched non-chiral topological order, its
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SSB boundaries are described by Galois-real fusion categories, hence give rise to anti-linear symmetry categories.

\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{A}
to
A
.

We start with a given boundary condition

D_{T}
surface. This means

must map all fluxes to charges. Since
D_{T}
has order 2, this automatically implies it maps all charges back to fluxes.

Now assume the

-action on simple anyons of

(VII.15)

The quiche take the following form

\mathfrak{B}^{\text{sym}}
\mathfrak{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{\mathsf{A}})^{\rho}
\mathfrak{Z}(\mathsf{Vec}_{A}^{*})^{\rho}

\mathsf{Vec}_{A}^{*}

VIII
Conclusions and Outlook

We have put forward a theory for categorical symmetries including time-reversal action
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
, and made a case that the natural mathematical habitat for such symmetries is real fusion categories. In fact, symmetry categories with time-reversal have to be so-called Galois-real fusion categories, as opposed to
\mathbb{R}
-real ones (where the endomorphisms of the identity form a real vector space). Generalized gauging (or Morita equivalence between categories) in the world of real fusion categories does not always follow the intuition that we may have formed working with complex fusion categories, and we provided some key examples to illustrate this. E.g. the symmetries
A\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
and
A\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
are gauge related and even the representation category of an abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{T}
can be non-invertible.
We furthermore set up a
T
-enriched SymTFT framework for such real fusion categories, which in particular gives an elegant way to derive many of the Morita equivalence relations.

Although the general theory we put forward is applicable in any dimensions, our examples focus on
(1+1)
d real fusion categories, and finite many simple objects. It would be interesting to extend our analysis to a full study of the phases using the SymTFT and to symmetries in higher dimensions, including continuous symmetries, such as

{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}^{T},U(1)\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T},U(1)\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
that play a role in topological insulator/superconductors.
One challenge for developing the SymTFT sandwich construction for gapped phases is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetry breaking on the symmetry boundary. This could be avoided by considering instead
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-symmetric (non-SSB) boundaries as in
Pace:2025hpb
, which may lead to a complete sandwich construction for gapped phases with symmetries of the form
\mathcal{C}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
, up to pure
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SPTs. Alternatively, one could interpret the compactification of the sandwich with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}
-SSB boundary not as the gapped phase itself but a doubled version where the two vacua are interpreted as the system itself and its complex conjugate.

In higher dimensions, we believe the generalization of our categorical formulation of symmetries and gapped phases is conceptually straightforward. The main challenge comes from the mathematical side, where the theory of fusion higher categories over non-algebraically-closed fields are not well-developed – see
D_coppet_2025
for a discussion of fusion 2-categories over non-algebraically-closed fields. We do mention here that the
(2+1)
d time-reversal anomaly
\omega\in H^{4}({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T},U(1)_{T})
can be described by the Galois-real fusion 2-category
2\mathsf{Vec}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{T}}^{\omega}

D_coppet_2025
, and generalizing it to
2\mathsf{Vec}_{G^{T}}^{\omega}
seems straightforward. This suggests that Galois-real fusion higher categories are still the correct language for describing generalized anti-linear symmetries in higher dimensions.

.

(A.1)

Proof.

Define a left
