2. ALTHOUGH THE LTP CAMPS WERE OSTENSIBLY DESIGNED TO ASSIST
PATIENTS IN OVERCOMING THEIR DEPENDENCE ON ALCOHOL THROUGH WORK
THERAPY, THEY WERE REALLY A MEANS OF OBTAINING THOUSANDS OF LABORERS
AT LITTLE OR NO COST TO THE GOVERNMENT. MEDICAL TREATMENT FOR
ALCOHOL DEPENDENCE WAS ALMOST NON-EXISTENT. ON A TYPICAL DAY, A
PATIENT WOULD ARISE EARLY, HAVE A SIMPLE BREAKFAST, AND THEN BE GIVEN
MEDICATION WHICH CONSISTED OF ANTABUS OR A FEW VITAMIN C TABLETS.

THE PATIENT WOULD THEN WORK FOR 10 HOURS ON CONSTRUCTION PROJECTS OR
OTHER TYPES OF SIMPLE HEAVY LABOR. AFTER A PLAIN DINNER, THE
PATIENTS WERE SUBJECTED TO POLITICAL INDOCTRINATION BEFORE THEY
RETIRED FOR THE NIGHT. THE COST TO FEED ONE PERSON FOR ONE DAY AT
THE CAMP WAS ABOUT 75 KOPEKS.
