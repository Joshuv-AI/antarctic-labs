Monochromatic k k in a row
Kuo-Han Ku

Abstract
We study a variant of the k k -in-a-row game in which players alternatively claim positions until a k k -in-a-row is created among all claimed positions. This leads to the constraint near k k -in-a-row avoiding on configurations and the associated problem of determining their extremal densities of such configurations on a given board B B . We investigate this problem on two types of boards: the grid ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} and hypercubes [ k ] d [k]^{d} . For the grid ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} , we establish nearly tight bounds on the maximum density D ⁡ ( k , ℤ 2 ) D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) , showing that D ⁡ ( k , ℤ 2 ) = 1 − 2 k D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})=1-\frac{2}{k} whenever 3 ∤ k 3\nmid k , and determine both D ⁡ ( 3 , ℤ 2 ) D(3,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) and d ⁡ ( 3 , ℤ 2 ) d(3,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) exactly. We also bound the minimum density d ⁡ ( k , ℤ 2 ) d(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) up to a gap of ( 8 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ k − 1 (8+o(1))k^{-1} . For hypercubes [ k ] d [k]^{d} , we derive asymptotic bounds on D ⁡ ( k , [ k ] d ) D(k,[k]^{d}) up to order k − 2 k^{-2} and obtain the exact value of d ⁡ ( k , [ k ] d ) d(k,[k]^{d}) . Our results contrast with the classical no- ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -in-line problem, a similar problem imposing different constraint, where the trivial upper bound is conjectured to be attainable.

Introduction

Five-in-a-row
, also known as “Gomoku”, is a strategy board game played on a
15\times 15
grid. Two players take turns placing stones of their respective color on the board. The winner is the first to form a consecutive line of five or more stones of their color. Variants of five-in-a-row have been extensively studied, particularly in positional game theory. Using computer-assisted analysis, Allis
[
1

Now imagine playing Five-in-a-row, but with two rules changed. First, all players place stones of the same color. Second, a player wins by occupying the last empty position of a five-in-a-row. We call this variant
Monochromatic Five-in-a-row

Under these new rules, the nature of the game changes completely. Rather than attacking and blocking the opponent, players must avoid creating a five-in-a-row with exactly one empty position, which we call a

\varepsilon>0

such that for all

\displaystyle\delta(\overline{S_{1,i}})>D-\varepsilon\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \delta(\overline{S_{0,i}})<d+\varepsilon.

For

and

, we have

\displaystyle\delta(M_{i})=\frac{1}{2^{i}}\delta(M_{i-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\delta\left(\overline{S_{\alpha_{j},\phi(i,j)}}\right)\in\sum_{j\in[i]:\alpha_{j}=1}\frac{D}{2^{j}}+\sum_{j\in[i]:\alpha_{j}=0}\frac{d}{2^{j}}\pm 2\varepsilon.

By subtraction and division, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[i]:\alpha_{j}=1}\frac{D}{2^{j}}+\sum_{j\in[i]:\alpha_{j}=0}\frac{d}{2^{j}}=(D-d)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2^{j}}\right)+d-\frac{d}{2^{i}}.

Since

approximates

with error at most

\displaystyle\delta(M_{i})\in(D-d)\alpha^{\prime}+d\pm 4\varepsilon=\alpha\pm 4\varepsilon.

Let

be the subset corresponding to

. Then

as

. By the same argument as in the proof of Proposition

,

avoids near

-lines since all danger lines are removed. Although

need not be saturated, we can extend it to a saturated configuration

. The additional points

must be contained in the empty boundary regions between blocks. A direct count shows that, as

and hence

∎

3.1
Nearly Tight Bounds on

We split the proof into two parts: the upper bound and the lower bound. For the upper bound, we introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

For

By this lemma, for every

\displaystyle D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}D(k,[k\ell]^{2})\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}.

Proof of Lemma
3.2

Let

Dividing both sides by

, we get

\displaystyle D(k,B)=\frac{|S|}{|B|}\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}+\frac{2|R|}{k|B|}.

∎

As all possible values of

), and derive exact values of

(Theorem

4.1
Bounds on
D(k,[k]^{d})

The upper bound is simple. As
[k]^{d}
can be partitioned into a number of
k
-lines, by Lemma
3.2
, we have
D(k,[k]^{d})\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}
. The lower bound follows the spirit of proof of Lemma
3.3
. However, instead of removing two zero sets from the board, we first partition the board into hypercubes, then remove a zero set from each part.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there are constants
C=C(d)>0
and
N=N(d)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle D(k,[k]^{d})\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{C}{k^{2}}

holds for
k\geq N
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\ell=\ell(k,d)
be the largest integer not greater than
\frac{k}{2}
such that it is coprime with
i
for every
i\in[d]
. Clearly, the difference btween
\frac{k}{2}
and
\ell
is not greater than
d!
. Like the
proof of Lemma
3.3
, for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

\displaystyle Z(r):=\{\ \vec{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ :\ \langle(1,1,\cdots,1),\vec{x}\rangle=r\pmod{\ell}\}.

We claim that, for every
\ell
-line
L=x+[0,\ell-1]\vec{d}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\vec{d}\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
, we have
|L\cap Z(1)|=1
. Recall that, as in the
proof of Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle|L\cap Z(r)|=1\ \ \text{ if and only if}\ \ \gcd(\langle(1,1,\cdots,1),\vec{d}\rangle,\ell)=1.

Note that, for
\vec{d}\in\{0,1\}^{t}\backslash\{0\}
, we have
\langle(1,1,\cdots,1),\vec{d}\rangle=\lVert\vec{d}\rVert_{1}\in[d]
. Then, as
\ell
is coprime with
[d]
, the claim is proved.
Set
B=B(\ell,d):=[0,\ell-1]^{d}\backslash Z(1)
. In the following, we will use projections and reflections of
B
as the building blocks to form a configuration
S^{\star}=S^{\star}(k,d)\in\mathcal{C}_{k}([k]^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{\star}|}{k^{d}}\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{4d!}{(k-2d!)k}.

With such configuration, let
C(d):=8d!
and
N(d):=4d!
, then we derive

\displaystyle D(k,[k]^{d})\geq\frac{|S^{\star}|}{k^{d}}\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{4d!}{(k-2d!)k}\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{C}{k^{2}}

for

, closing the proof. ∎

Building up the configuration

We start with defining the core

. Also, we define its

and

. For each point

\phi(x)
on it. The map

\displaystyle\phi:X

\displaystyle\to\left\{2^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to 2^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\left[Y\mapsto R_{\text{rev}(x)}(Y)\cap E_{\text{int}(x)}\right],

where
\text{rev}(x):=x^{-1}(\ell)\subseteq[d]
and
\text{int}(x):=x^{-1}([\ell+1,k-\ell])\subseteq[d]
are the set of reversing and interior indices respectively;
R_{I}
negates the
i
-th coordinate for each
i\in I
, and
E_{J}
is the subspace of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the
j
-th coordinate zero for each
j\in J
. Define

\displaystyle S^{\star}:=\bigsqcup\limits_{x\in X}(x+\phi(x)(B))\subseteq[k]^{d}.

Before we verify that

is a configuration, we first lower bound the density of this subset. Given
x\in X
, if

,
\phi(x)(B)
is a single point and
|\text{int}(x)|=d
. If
x\in\partial X
, the ground space
\phi(x)([0,\ell-1]^{d})
can be partitioned into
\ell^{d-1-|\text{int}(x)|}

\ell
-lines of direction in

. By
the claim above
, we know
|L\cap\phi(x)(B)|=\ell-1
for every

(2)

\displaystyle d(x):=\frac{|\phi(x)(B)|}{|\phi(x)([0,\ell-1]^{d})|}\geq\frac{(\ell-1)\ell^{d-1-|\text{int}(x)|}}{\ell^{d-|\text{int}(x)|}}=1-\frac{1}{\ell}.

As the ground spaces

form a partition of

can be regarded as the weighted-average of

by setting the weight of

to be

. Then (

\frac{|S^{\star}|}{k^{d}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{\ell}
. Hence, by
\ell>\frac{k}{2}-d!
, we obtain the desired lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{\star}|}{k^{d}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{\ell}=1-\frac{2\ell}{\ell k}-\frac{k-2\ell}{\ell k}\geq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{4d!}{(k-2d!)k}.

Claim 4.2
.

For every

In particular,

. Given a

, let

be such points in Claim

. Following

, we can see that

misses two points on

and the other from

Proof of Claim
4.2
.

Say
L=v+[0,k-1]\vec{d}
, where
\vec{d\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}
. Define
x,y\in[k]^{d}
by setting

\displaystyle x_{i}:=\begin{cases}\ell&\text{ if $d_{i}=1$ or ($d_{i}=0$ and $v_{i}\leq\ell$)},\\
k-\ell+1&\text{ if $d_{i}=-1$ or ($d_{i}=0$ and $v_{i}\geq k-\ell+1$)},\\
v_{i}&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle y_{i}:=\begin{cases}k-\ell+1&\text{ if $d_{i}=1$ or ($d_{i}=0$ and $v_{i}\leq\ell$)},\\
\ell&\text{ if $d_{i}=-1$ or ($d_{i}=0$ and $v_{i}\geq k-\ell+1$)},\\
v_{i}&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

for every
i\in[d]
. We now show that the
v+[0,\ell-1]\vec{d}
is contained in
x+\phi(x)([0,\ell-1]^{d})
. We show the containment holds coordinatewisely. Recall that
\phi(x)([0,\ell-1]^{d})
is a hyper-rectangle, hence there are
a_{i}<b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(x)([0,\ell-1]^{d})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[a_{i},b_{i}]
. Denote
v_{t}:=v+t\vec{d}
. for each
t\in[0,\ell-1]
. Consider three cases:

(d_{i}=1)

In this case, we have
x_{i}=\ell
and hence
\phi(x)
negates the
i
-th coordinate. Thus

[a_{i},b_{i}]=\ell-[0,\ell-1]=[1,\ell].

Noting that
(v_{t})_{i}=t+1
in this case, we have
(v_{t})_{i}\in[a_{i},b_{i}]
for each
t\in[0,\ell-1]
.

(d_{i}=-1)

Similar to the first case, as
x_{i}=k-\ell+1
,
\phi(x)
does nothing on the
i
-th coordinate. Hence

[a_{i},b_{i}]=(k-\ell+1)+[0,\ell-1]=[k-\ell+1,k].

Observing that
(v_{t})_{i}=k-t
, then we get
(v_{t})_{i}\in[a_{i},b_{i}]
for each
t\in[0,\ell-1]
.

(d_{i}=0)

By the discussion above, we already have

\displaystyle[a_{i},b_{i}]=\begin{cases}[1,\ell]&\text{ if }\ v_{i}\leq\ell,\\
[k-\ell+1,k]&\text{ if }\ v_{i}\geq k-\ell+1.\end{cases}

For the case
\ell<v_{i}<k-\ell+1
,
\phi(x)
eliminates all but points with the

-th coordinate zero. Therefore

\displaystyle[a_{i},b_{i}]=v_{i}+[0,0]=[v_{i},v_{i}].

In either case,
v_{i}\in[a_{i},b_{i}]
. Noting that
(v_{t})_{i}=v_{i}
for all

, then the result follows.

Due to the definition of
x
, we have
R_{\text{rev}(x)}^{-1}(\vec{d})=R_{\text{rev}(x)}(\vec{d})\subseteq\{-1,0\}^{d}
. Then, by changing the starting point of the

, it can be regarded as a
\ell
-line with direction in
\{0,1\}^{d}
. One can show that

is contained in

and it is a desired
\ell
-line in a similar way.
∎

4.2
Determining
d(k,[k]^{d})

In this section, we will provide the exact value of
d(k,[k]^{d})
. For the upper bound, we consider a smaller hypercube
S:=[2,k-1]^{d}
. Clearly,
S
contains no near
k
-line since it excludes two endpoints from every
k
-line. It remains to show that
S
is saturated. Given a point
x\in[k]^{d}\backslash S
. Consider the
k
-line
L:=x+[0,k-1]\vec{d}_{x}
, where the direction
\vec{d}
is defined by

\displaystyle\vec{d}_{x}:=\sum_{i:x_{i}=1}e_{i}-\sum_{i:x_{i}=k}e_{i}.

We have
(S\cup\{x\})\cap L=x+[0,k-2]\vec{d}_{x}
, which means
S\cup\{x\}
contains a near
k
-line. Therefore

\displaystyle d(k,[k]^{d})\leq\frac{|S|}{k^{d}}=\frac{(k-2)^{d}}{k^{d}}=\left(1-\frac{2}{k}\right)^{d}.

On the other hand, let
S_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}([k]^{d})
be a saturated configuration reaching minimum density. Define
X:=S\backslash S_{0}
and
Y:=S_{0}\backslash S
. Obviously, we have
|Y|\leq|X|
. We now claim there is an injection
f:X\to Y
. If so, then we have
|X|\leq|Y|
and hence
|S_{0}|=|S|=(k-2)^{d}
, which finishes the proof. For every
x\in X
, by the fact that
x\not\in S_{0}
and
S_{0}
is saturated, there is a
k
-line
L=L(x)
such that
|L\backslash S_{0}|=\{x,y\}
. Consider two cases:

(i)

Suppose
y\not\in S
, there is only one point
z
in
L\cap Y=(L\backslash S)\cap S_{0}
. Set
f(x):=z
.

(ii)

Suppose
y\in S
, there are two points
z
and
w
in
L\cap Y
. WLOG, let
z
be the one that
y
is not between
x
and
z
. Then we set
f(x):=z
.

To verify the injectivity, given
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
. If
L(x_{1})=L(x_{2})
, let
z
and
w
be that in (ii) when considering
x_{1}
, then we have

f(x_{1})=z\neq w=f(x_{2}).

If
L(x_{1})\neq L(x_{2})
, following Lemma
4.3
, we have
(L_{1}\backslash S)\cap(L_{2}\backslash S)=\emptyset
. Then we have
f(x_{1})\neq f(x_{2})
due to the facts
f(x_{i})\in L(x_{i})\backslash S
for
i=1,2
. ∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
x\in[k]^{d}\backslash S
, the
k
-line
L=x+[0,k-1]\vec{d}_{x}
is the only
k
-line such that
x\in L
and
L\cap S\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in[k]^{d}\backslash S
. Let
L^{\prime}=y+[0,k-1]\vec{d}
be a
k
-line in
[k]^{d}
such that
x=y+a\vec{d}
. Suppose
x
is not an endpoint of
L^{\prime}
, i.e.,
a\neq 0
and
a\neq k-1
. Since
x\in[k]^{d}\backslash S
, there is an index
i_{0}\in[d]
such that
x_{i_{0}}\in\{1,k\}
. We note that
d_{i_{0}}=0
. Otherwise, one of

\{y_{i_{0}},y_{i_{0}}+a,y_{i_{0}}+(k-1)\}\subseteq\{1,k\}\ \ \ \text{ or }\ \ \ \{y_{i_{0}},y_{i_{0}}-a,y_{i_{0}}-(k-1)\}\subseteq\{1,k\}

would hold, which is impossible. Hence
z_{i_{0}}=x_{i_{0}}\not\in[2,k-1]
for every
z\in L^{\prime}
, and thus
L^{\prime}\cap S=\emptyset
.

It follows that if
L^{\prime}\cap S\neq\emptyset
, then
x
must be an endpoint of
L^{\prime}
, and we may write
L^{\prime}=x+[0,k-1]\vec{d^{\prime}}
. We now determine
\vec{d^{\prime}}
coordinate-wisely. For each
i\in[d]
, consider three cases:

1.

If
x_{i}=1
, then
d^{\prime}_{i}\neq 1
implies either
L^{\prime}\cap S=\emptyset
or
x+\vec{d^{\prime}}\not\in[k]^{d}
. Hence
d^{\prime}_{i}=1
.

2.

If
x_{i}=k
, a symmetric argument shows that
d^{\prime}_{i}=-1
.

3.

If
x_{i}\in[2,k-1]
, then
d^{\prime}_{i}\neq 0
would imply
(x+(k-1)\vec{d^{\prime}})_{i}\not\in[k]
, which contradicts that
L^{\prime}\subseteq[k]^{d}
. Hence
d_{i}=0
.

Therefore, we obtain

\vec{d^{\prime}}=\sum_{i:x_{i}=1}e_{i}-\sum_{i:x_{i}=k}e_{i}=\vec{d}_{x},

and hence
L^{\prime}=L
.
∎

5.1.1
The Grid

From Lemma

and the proof of Theorem
3.8
, the inequalities

\displaystyle D(k,(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2})\leq D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}

. Note that, when

is not a multiple of

in the proof of Lemma

avoids not only
k

but also

-lines in

by the construction, which leads to

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2}{k}=\frac{|S_{k}|}{k^{2}}\leq D(k,(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2})\leq D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}

and forces the middle two terms,

and

. On the other hand, it is easy to see that

. Further, by exhaustive searching, we have

Conjecture 5.1
.

For every
k\in 3\mathbb{N}
,
D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})<1-\frac{2}{k}
.

Conjecture 5.2
.

For every

,
D(k,(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2})<D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
.

Suppose the Conjecture

is true, then the inequality

\displaystyle D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})\geq D(k,(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2})

forces the bound

to hold for

. Conversely, according to Theorem
3.8
, if

, then we must have

\displaystyle D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})\leq 1-\frac{2}{k}-\frac{1}{2k^{2}-k}<1-\frac{2}{k}

for the same

. Hence, for each

, the following are equivalent.

\bullet

D(k,\mathbb{Z}^{2})<1-\frac{2}{k}
.

\bullet

D(k,(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2})<1-\frac{2}{k}
.

\bullet

There is no subset

satisfying
|L\cap A|=2
for every axis-parallel or diagonal line
L
in
(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.

The third statement characterizes when a configuration has density
1-\frac{2}{k}
, where the subset

should be interpreted as its complement. Since this formulation avoids explicit reference to configurations, we restate Conjecture

accordingly.

Conjecture 5.3
.

(Restatement of Conjecture

)
For every
k\in 3\mathbb{N}
, there is no subset

satisfying

for every axis-parallel or diagonal line

.

W_{i}:=([k]\backslash\{i,(k+1)-i\})^{d}.

Given a
k
-line
L=v+[0,k-1]d
in
[k]^{d}
, the two points,
v+(i-1)d
and
v+(k-i)d
, are not contained in
W_{i}
. Hence
W_{i}
misses at least two points from every
k
-line, and therefore is near
k
-line avoiding. On the other hand, given a point
x\in[k]^{d}\backslash W_{i}
, consider the
k
-line

L=v+[0,k-1]\vec{d},

where
v:=x-(i-1)\vec{d}
and
\vec{d}:=\sum_{j:x_{j}=i}e_{j}-\sum_{j:x_{j}=(k+1-i)}e_{j}
. It is clear that
x=v+(i-1)\vec{d}
and
\overline{x}=v+(k-i)\vec{d}
are the only points in
L
not contained in
W_{i}
. Hence we have
|L\backslash W_{i}|=2
, and therefore
W_{i}
is saturated.

It appears that these are the only configurations reaching minimum density when
d\geq 2
. Indeed, this is true for
k=3
and all
d\geq 2
, and also for
k=4
and
d=2,3
. This motivates the following question.

Question 5.7
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, are
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in[\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor]}
the only saturated configurations, in
\mathcal{S}_{k}([k]^{d})
, reaching minimum density?

5.2
Extensions

Winner Determination

For a fixed board
B
, the monochromatic
k
-in-a-row game is a finite perfect-information game, and hence the outcome is determined: it is either a first-player win (
FP
), a second-player win (
SP
), or a draw. In this setting, draws occur if and only if the family of
k
-lines is empty, which is a degenerate case that we typically exclude, so the game is either
FP
or
SP
.

Unlike many positional games, the strategy-stealing argument does not apply here, as the additional move available to the first player may be disadvantageous; in particular, the first player cannot always guarantee a win. A natural problem is to determine the winning player for a given board
B
, or more ambitiously, to describe an explicit winning strategy.

For instance, the outcome on
[k]^{d}
is completely characterized: it is
FP
when
k
is odd and
SP
when
k
is even. This result comes from a pairing strategy for the winning player. See pages 46-48 in
[
8
]
for the construction.

Biased Game

One natural extension is the
biased
(p:q)
game
, in which the first and second players claim
p
and
q
points per move, respectively. The cases
p\geq k
or
q\geq k
are trivial, so we restrict to
\max\{p,q\}<k
.

A heuristic suggests that the player with the larger bias should have an advantage, and this is indeed the case when the board is sufficiently large. Let Alice and Bob denote the players with biases
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, and suppose
\alpha>\beta
. Consider the quantity
N
, defined as the minimum number of points needed to complete a
k
-line from the current state. Alice’s strategy is based on the value of
N
: If
N\leq\alpha
, Alice wins without doubt. If not, Alice claims
\alpha
points such that
N
is decreased by
1
. (This can be done once the board size is large enough.) In this setting, Bob cannot win; otherwise, one would obtain a contradiction
\beta\geq N-1\geq\alpha
before the very last move. Consequently, the most interesting case is the balanced biased
(p:p)
game with
1<p<k
, where the advantage of larger bias disappears.

t\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}

2

s
-Saturated Configurations

We can extract the concept of saturated configuration and quantize it. Given a
k
-board
B
. For every
s\in[k]
, we define the
s
-configurations

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k,s}(B):=\{S\subseteq B:|L\backslash S|\geq s\ \forall\text{ $k$-line $L$}\}.

Then we accordingly define the
saturated
s
-configurations
,
\mathcal{S}_{k,s}(B)
. Further, if
B
is finite, we also define
s
-density spectrum
,
\mathcal{D}_{k,s}(B)
, and
extremal
s
-densities
,
D_{s}(k,B)
and
d_{s}(k,B)
. Notably, the original problem is corresponding to the case
s=2
.

Several results can be extended to this setting. For instance, by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we can obtain

\displaystyle D_{s}(k,B)\leq 1-\frac{s}{k}+\frac{s|R|}{k|B|}

for every
s\in[k]
, where
R
is the complement of union of some disjoint
k
-lines. And, by adapting the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle d_{s}(k,B)\geq\frac{k-s}{sN(B)+(k-s)},

where
N(B):=\max_{x\in B}|\{\text{$k$-lines containing $x$}\}|
. See pages 54-58 in
[
8
]
for detailed proofs. However, bounds obtained from specific constructions still require further discussion and modification.

Extremal Board Problem

The original monochromatic
k
in a row problem asks how large or small the density of a saturated configuration on a given board can be.A related question is the following: for a fixed board size
n
, which boards maximize or minimize the extremal densities? To formalize this question, we restrict attention to
n
-subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For every
B\in\binom{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{n}
, we regard
B
as a board by defining

\mathcal{L}(B):=\{L:L\text{ is a $k$-line on $\mathbb{Z}^{2}$},\ L\subseteq B\}.

Then the question is to determine the terms

\displaystyle D_{\min}(k,n):=\min_{B\in\binom{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{n}}D(k,B)\ \ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \ d_{\max}(k,n):=\max_{B\in\binom{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{n}}d(k,B).

However, without additional assumptions determining
d_{\max}(k,n)
becomes trivial. Indeed, if a point
x\in B
is not contained in any
k
-line of
B
, then
x
must belong to every saturated configuration. In fact, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find a
n
-subset containing no
k
-line, it implies
d_{\max}(k,n)=1
. Therefore, it is natural to consider subsets that every point is contained in some
k
-lines.
