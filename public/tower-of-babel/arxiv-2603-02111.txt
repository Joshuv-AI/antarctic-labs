Horizontal Kakeya maximal operators in finite Heisenberg groups: Exact exponents and applications
Thang Pham, Andrea Pinamonti, Dung The Tran, Boqing Xue

Abstract
We study Kakeya maximal operators associated with horizontal lines in finite Heisenberg groups ℍ n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) . For the operator parameterized only by projective horizontal directions, we show that projection to 𝔽 q 2 ​ n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n} reduces the problem to the affine finite field Kakeya maximal operator, and we determine the exact ℓ u → ℓ v \ell^{u}\to\ell^{v} growth exponent for all n n and all 1 ≤ u , v ≤ ∞ 1\leq u,v\leq\infty . We then introduce a refined-direction operator that also records the central slope of a horizontal line. In ℍ 1 ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) , we prove the sharp ℓ 2 → ℓ 2 \ell^{2}\to\ell^{2} estimate ‖ ℳ ℍ 1 rd ​ F ‖ ℓ 2 ​ ( 𝒟 1 ) ≲ q 1 / 2 ​ ‖ F ‖ ℓ 2 ​ ( ℍ 1 ​ ( 𝔽 q ) ) , \|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}^{\mathrm{rd}}F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\lesssim q^{1/2}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}, deduce the exact mixed-norm exponent formula, and obtain lower bounds for horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya sets with prescribed refined directions. The argument is purely Fourier-analytic and does not use the polynomial method. An outlook toward a new approach to the affine Kakeya problem in 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{3} will be discussed in this paper.

Introduction

The classical Kakeya needle problem asks for the smallest planar region in which a unit line
segment can be rotated through all directions. While the original formulation is geometric, a major modern development is the study of
Kakeya (Besicovitch) sets
:
subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contain a unit line segment in every direction. Besicovitch
[
2
]

showed that such sets can have Lebesgue measure zero, revealing that
containing all
directions
is compatible with extreme measure-theoretic thinness.

Despite this, Kakeya sets are widely expected to be large in a finer sense. The

Kakeya set conjecture
predicts that any Kakeya set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
must have
Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension
n
. This conjecture is a central organizing problem because
it quantifies the limits of how efficiently long thin objects in many directions can overlap,
and it is intertwined with major questions in harmonic analysis.

The Kakeya maximal operator.

A convenient analytic formulation uses tubes at scale
\delta\in(0,1)
. Let
T
denote the

\delta
-neighbourhood of a unit line segment (so
T
has length
1
and cross-sectional
radius
\delta
). For
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
define the
Kakeya maximal operator

K_{\delta}f(e)\ :=\ \sup_{T\parallel e}\ \frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}|f(\mathbf{x})|\,d\mathbf{x},

(1)

where the supremum runs over all such tubes
T
with axis parallel to
e
.
The (scale-invariant)
Kakeya maximal operator conjecture
asserts that

\|K_{\delta}f\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\ \lesssim_{\varepsilon,n}\ \delta^{-\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }\varepsilon>0,

(2)

and is closely related (indeed essentially equivalent, after discretization) to quantitative
overlap bounds for direction-separated tube families. Such bounds imply sharp lower estimates
for the Minkowski/Hausdorff size of Kakeya sets.

In dimension
n=2
, Córdoba
[
3
]
proved sharp
L^{2}
-type estimates for
K_{\delta}
. In higher dimensions, Wolff’s seminal work
[
27
]

obtained improved
L^{p}
bounds for Kakeya-type maximal operators, yielding strong lower bounds
for the possible dimension of Kakeya sets and setting the stage for much subsequent progress. Kakeya sets also arise as obstructions in Fourier analysis; for instance, Fefferman’s counterexample to the ball multiplier problem is historically linked to Besicovitch-type configurations
[
14
]
. Recent progress has clarified parts of the Euclidean picture, especially in three dimensions: Katz and Zahl
[
23
]
obtained an improved lower bound
\dim_{H}(E)\geq\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon_{0}
for Besicovitch sets in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and Wang and Zahl
[
26
]
proved the three-dimensional Kakeya conjecture.

The finite field model (abelian).

A useful companion viewpoint replaces Euclidean space by a vector space
over a finite field. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the field with
q
elements.
A set
K\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is a
finite field Kakeya set
if it
contains a full affine line in every direction: for every
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, there exists

\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that

\{\mathbf{y}+s\mathbf{v}:s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\subset K.

The finite field analogue of the Kakeya conjecture asks whether such a set must have size comparable to the whole space, i.e.
|K|\geq c_{n}q^{n}
with

c_{n}
independent of
q
. Dvir’s breakthrough resolution of this problem introduced the polynomial method into Kakeya theory
[
9
]
.
Building on this method, Ellenberg, Oberlin, and Tao
[
10
]
proved sharp Kakeya-type maximal estimates associated to algebraic varieties over finite
fields, highlighting how algebraic structure may replace multiscale harmonic analysis.

While highly effective, the polynomial method relies fundamentally on algebraic vanishing. A natural complementary objective is to develop Fourier-analytic and geometric approaches that do not rely on algebraic vanishing, and therefore, extend to settings lacking strong algebraic structure.

In this paper, we pursue this program in the model setting of the finite Heisenberg group.

Kakeya sets and maximal operators in the Heisenberg group.

In the sub-Riemannian geometry of the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{1}
, a natural
analogue of the Kakeya problem asks how small a set can be if it contains
horizontal

unit line segments in every horizontal direction. Studying Kakeya phenomena in the Heisenberg group provides a natural test case for understanding which aspects of classical Kakeya behavior depend on Euclidean structure and which persist in a nonabelian setting with non-isotropic dilations. The interaction between the group law, anisotropic dilations, and the horizontal distribution produces genuinely different geometric and analytic phenomena, making the Heisenberg group a particularly rich environment in which to probe the limits of Euclidean Kakeya theory. Here the geometry is that determined by the canonical generating directions (the first layer of the stratification), and size is measured in terms of Hausdorff dimension with respect to a homogeneous metric such as the Koranyi metric.

In this direction, Liu
[
24
]
introduced a notion of Heisenberg Kakeya set and proved the sharp lower
bound
\dim_{\mathbb{H}}(E)\geq 3
for Kakeya sets in
\mathbb{H}_{1}
, with sharpness obtained
(under this definition) by the horizontal plane. A complementary analytic
viewpoint is provided by Kakeya-type maximal operators: Venieri
[
28
]
showed that, in analogy with
the Euclidean theory, suitable
L^{p}
bounds for a Kakeya maximal operator imply lower bounds
for the Heisenberg Hausdorff dimension of (bounded) Besicovitch/Kakeya sets.
More recently, Fässler, Pinamonti, and Wald
[
13
]
defined Heisenberg Kakeya maximal operators
M_{\delta}

by averaging over
\delta
-neighbourhoods of horizontal unit segments (Koranyi tubes) and
proved an essentially optimal estimate in
\mathbb{H}_{1}
, recovering Liu’s sharp dimension
bound as an application. Related techniques appear in
[
12
,
22
,
29
]
.

These developments motivate a finite field Heisenberg Kakeya problem adapted to horizontal geometry. Our aim is to develop a discrete counterpart of Heisenberg maximal-operator theory.

The finite field Heisenberg maximal operator.

Let
q
be a prime power. We identify the
n
th Heisenberg group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

equipped with the group law

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\Bigl(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{\prime},\,t+t^{\prime}+\bigl(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\bigr)\Bigr),

where
\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
denotes the standard dot product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
A
horizontal direction
is an element
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
represented by a nonzero vector
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
up to scaling. For

\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and

\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\neq\mathbf{0}
,
we define the horizontal line through

\mathbf{p}
in direction
[\mathbf{v}]
as the right coset

L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}:=\{\,\mathbf{p}\cdot(s\mathbf{a},s\mathbf{b},0):s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\,\}.

A direct computation gives the parametrization

L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}=\Bigl\{\bigl(\mathbf{x}_{0}+s\mathbf{a},\ \mathbf{y}_{0}+s\mathbf{b},\ t_{0}+(\mathbf{x}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{y}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{a})s\bigr):\ s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\Bigr\},

(3)

and hence
|L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}|=q
.
The term
(\mathbf{x}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{y}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{a})\,s
is the

Heisenberg twist
: it depends on the basepoint
(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0})

and couples it to the direction
[\mathbf{v}]
via the underlying symplectic form.
This twist is what distinguishes horizontal Kakeya from the standard affine Kakeya
problem on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n+1}
.

We now define two maximal operators associated with horizontal lines.
They differ in how much directional information they retain, and the gap between them drives the main results of this paper.

Throughout this paper, our maximal operators are defined using sums along lines
(not averages). The exponent formulas below reflect this definition.

To avoid notational conflict, we consistently use
[\mathbf{v}]
for projective directions and the letters

u,v
for exponents.

For
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the associated maximal operator

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F([\mathbf{v}]):=\max_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\ \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}}|F(\mathbf{x})|\qquad([\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})).

(4)

This operator remembers only the spatial direction
[\mathbf{v}]=[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}]
, in particular, it forgets the slope of the line in the central variable.

To capture the full Heisenberg geometry, we introduce a finer notion.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
L=L_{p,[\mathbf{v}]}
be a horizontal line in
\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where

\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
satisfies

(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\neq(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
.
We define the
t
-slope
of
L
by

c(L)\ :=\ \mathbf{x}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{y}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

where
\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0},t_{0})
is an arbitrary point of
L
, and
\cdot
denotes the standard dot product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.

The quantity
c(L)
is well-defined: if
\mathbf{p}
is replaced by another point
\mathbf{p}\cdot(s\mathbf{a},s\mathbf{b},0)\in L
, then we have the same slope.

Definition 1.2
.

Define the refined direction set

\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\bigl\{[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c]\in\mathbb{P}^{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):\ (\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\neq(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})\bigr\}.

For a horizontal line
L=L_{\mathbf{p},[(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})]}
, its refined direction is

\mathrm{Dir}(L):=[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c(L)]\in\mathcal{D}_{n}.

Note that
\mathcal{D}_{n}=\mathbb{P}^{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{[0:\cdots:0:1]\}
, i.e. the projective space with the single vertical direction removed.
Moreover, for every
[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c]\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, there exists a horizontal line
L\subset\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with

\mathrm{Dir}(L)=[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c]
. The set
\mathcal{D}_{n}
fibers over
\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
via the
projection
[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c]\mapsto[\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}]
,
with each fiber having
q
elements.

Definition 1.3
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is called a full-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya set if for every

\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
there exists a horizontal line
L\subset E
with
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\delta
.

Although
\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n+1}
coincide as sets, the preceding notion is
not
the same as the usual affine Kakeya
property in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n+1}
: in the Heisenberg setting one prescribes refined directions of horizontal lines, whereas affine Kakeya prescribes
ambient directions of arbitrary affine lines. See Examples
11.1
and
11.2
.

For
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the full refined-direction horizontal maximal operator

(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F)(\omega):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\ \mathrm{horizontal}\\
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\mathbf{p}\in L}|F(\mathbf{p})|,\qquad\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{n}.

(5)

In particular, the refined-direction operator discriminates among
horizontal lines that share the same spatial direction but have different

t
-slopes, so

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F([\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}])=\max\limits_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F([\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{b}:c]).

We now make the following observation. The projection
\pi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

maps each horizontal line
L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}
bijectively onto an affine line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
with the same projective direction
[\mathbf{v}]
.
By aggregating
F
along the
t
-fibers one obtains a pointwise domination

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F\leq M_{2n}G
,
where
M_{2n}
is the affine (abelian) Kakeya maximal operator on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and
G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\|F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\cdot)\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
.

For a finite set
X
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we use
\ell^{p}(X)
to denote the normed vector space consisting of complex-valued functions on
X
, equipped with the standard
\ell^{p}
-norm based on the counting measure.
Direct computations reveal that
sharp upper bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}
follow from existing abelian input. Note that this reduction is not reversible (Remark
2.4
), since the projection forgets the
t
-twist. In particular, there are functions
F
for which the projected abelian maximal operator is of size
q
in every direction, whereas
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F
remains uniformly
O(1)
. We therefore view the sharp exponent formulas for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}

as a reference estimate: they quantify what can be proved when one insists on the projective direction parameter alone, and they motivate the
refined-direction framework.

For
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, let
A_{n}(u,v)
be the smallest non-negative real number such that there
exists
C_{n,u,v}>0
with

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{n,u,v}\,q^{A_{n}(u,v)}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

(6)

for all
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
.

In the first theorem, we prove the exact value of
A_{1}(u,v)
.

Theorem 1.4

(Exact exponent for
n=1
)
.

For all
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A_{1}(u,v)=\max\Bigl(\frac{1}{v},\ 1-\frac{1}{u},\ 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\Bigr).

(7)

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is self-contained and is based on the projection-reduction framework as observed above. The core step is the
TT^{*}
argument yielding the

\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
bound (Lemma
2.5
)

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2q}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Interpolating this
\ell^{2}
estimate with the endpoint
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{\infty}
bounds gives the full range.

This
\ell^{2}
estimate serves as a key building block
in the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.6
below:
the zero-frequency contribution of the refined-direction operator reduces, after
Fourier decomposition in the central variable, to precisely this planar Kakeya estimate.
Each ingredient is developed with the broader Fourier-analytic program in mind.
When
n>1
, we obtain the following extension by replacing the planar
\ell^{2}
input (Lemma
2.5
) by Theorem
7.2
due to Ellenberg, Oberlin, and Tao in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Exact exponent for general
n
)
.

For all
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A_{n}(u,v)=\max\Bigl(\frac{2n-1}{v},\ 1-\frac{1}{u},\ 1+\frac{2n-1}{v}-\frac{2n}{u}\Bigr).

(8)

Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
capture what can be extracted from the projective direction parameter
[\mathbf{v}]
alone. To access the genuinely Heisenberg content of the problem, we now turn to the refined-direction operator.

Since
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
may be identified with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
, the results of Ellenberg, Oberlin, and Tao

[
10
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.4]
imply the bounds

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\lesssim q^{2/3}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))},\qquad\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\lesssim q\,\|F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}.

The
\ell^{3}\to\ell^{3}
estimate is optimal (see Example
11.3
). By contrast, the
\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
bound is not sharp. In the next theorem, we obtain the optimal exponent. The proof is purely Fourier-analytic and does not use polynomial vanishing.

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists an absolute constant
C
such that for
all
F:\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
,

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\leq C\,q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}.

Moreover, the exponent
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
is sharp.

The proof begins by linearizing the maximal operator and decomposing in the central Fourier variable.
The zero-frequency term reduces, after projection to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, to the planar Kakeya maximal estimate.
The nonzero frequencies are controlled by Plancherel and character orthogonality, which reduce the key
\ell^{2}
bound
to a counting problem for an explicit quadratic map.

For
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, let
A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)
be the smallest non-negative real number such that there exists

C_{u,v}>0
with

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\leq C_{u,v}\,q^{A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\qquad\text{for all }F:\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}.

(9)

Combining the sharp
\ell^{2}
estimate with endpoint bounds and interpolation, we determine the exact mixed-norm exponent formula.

Theorem 1.7
.

For all
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)=\max\Bigl(\frac{1}{v},\ 1-\frac{1}{u},\ \frac{2}{v}-\frac{1}{u},\ 1+\frac{2}{v}-\frac{3}{u}\Bigr).

(10)

The upper bounds in Theorem
1.7
are obtained by interpolating the sharp
\ell^{2}
estimate of Theorem
1.6
with endpoint bounds (
\ell^{1}\to\ell^{1}
,
\ell^{1}\to\ell^{\infty}
,
\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{\infty}
) and
\ell^{p}
embeddings on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. For sharpness, each of the four terms in (
10
) is forced by an explicit test function: a point mass, the indicator of a single horizontal line, the indicator of two non-parallel affine lines lifted to
t=0
, and the constant function
F\equiv 1
, respectively.

To extend Theorem
1.7
to higher rank
n\geq 2
, a natural first step is to establish the analogue of Theorem
1.6
, namely, an estimate of the form

\ell^{2}\bigl(\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\bigr)\longrightarrow\ell^{2n}(\mathcal{D}_{n}).

A straightforward adaptation of the
n=1
Fourier/Plancherel strategy yields a bound on the scale of
q^{\frac{n}{2}}
, rather than the smaller scale
q^{\frac{2n-1}{2n}}
suggested by the point-mass obstruction. Closing this gap therefore appears to require genuinely new input. We plan to address the case
n\geq 2
in a forthcoming arXiv revision.

One of the main motivations for developing the refined-direction framework is its connection
to the affine Kakeya problem.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
be an affine Kakeya set, identified with a subset
of
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
One may decompose the non-vertical direction set

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\Omega_{1}\sqcup\Omega_{2}
,
where
\Omega_{1}
consists of those refined directions realized by a horizontal line contained in
E
, and
\Omega_{2}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{1}
.
Theorem
1.8
immediately gives

|E|\geq C^{-2}\,q\,|\Omega_{1}|
. In particular,
|\Omega_{1}|\gtrsim q^{2}
already forces
|E|\gtrsim q^{3}
. It therefore suffices to handle the case
|\Omega_{2}|\gtrsim q^{2}
.
For each
\omega\in\Omega_{2}
, the Kakeya line realizing direction
\omega
is not horizontal: its basepoint is forced away from a prescribed affine locus
in the
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
-plane.
We show (Section
11.2
) that each such line carries a well-defined non-horizontality parameter
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, and lines with a fixed value of
\mu
can be straightened into horizontal lines by an explicit mapping, reducing each
\mu
-slice to a horizontal Kakeya configuration controlled by Theorem
1.6
.
The remaining task is to exploit the interaction between different
\mu
-slices, where the basepoint-avoidance constraints provide additional
structure not visible in the usual affine formulation.
We regard this program as a concrete route toward a new,
Fourier-analytic proof of the affine Kakeya theorem in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
.

We now discuss some applications. In the first consequence, we obtain quantitative lower
bounds for horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya sets, both for the full and restricted families of directions.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{D}_{1}
, and let
E\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Let
m
be an integer with
1\leq m\leq q
.
Assume that for every
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists a horizontal line

L\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|E\cap L|\geq m.

For
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, we have

|E|\ \gtrsim_{u,v}m^{u}\,|\Omega|^{u/v}\,q^{-u\,A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)}.

(11)

In particular, for
(u,v)=(2,2)
, one has

|E|\ \gtrsim\frac{m^{2}\,|\Omega|}{q}.

(12)

Hence, any full-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya set in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is of size at least
\gtrsim q^{3}
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

M_{E}(\omega):=(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{E})(\omega)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\ \mathrm{horizontal}\\

Theorem 1.9
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notations and Basics for
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Let
q
be a prime power and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field. The group
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is identified with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
with the group law

(x,y,t)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\bigl(x+x^{\prime},\,y+y^{\prime},\,t+t^{\prime}+(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime})\bigr).

For any
\mathbf{p}=(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\mathbf{v}=(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, the horizontal line

L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}=\{\,\mathbf{p}\cdot(sa,sb,0):\ s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\,\}=\bigl\{(x_{0}+sa,\ y_{0}+sb,\ t_{0}+s(x_{0}b-y_{0}a)):\ s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\bigr\}

has refined direction
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=[a:b:(x_{0}b-y_{0}a)]\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{[0:0:1]\}
. Given
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, the map
(x,y)\mapsto(xb-ya)
is a nontrivial linear functional and hence is surjective onto
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of all horizontal lines in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. For
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, or
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, denote
\mathcal{L}([\mathbf{v}])
,
\mathcal{L}(\omega)
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p})
the set of all lines in
\mathcal{L}
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
, with refined direction
\omega
, or through point
\mathbf{p}
, respectively. Note that

|\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q^{3},\quad|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q+1,\quad|\mathcal{D}_{1}|=q^{2}+q,\quad|L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}|=q.

It follows that

|\mathcal{L}|=\frac{q^{3}\cdot(q+1)}{q}=q^{2}(q+1).

Given
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, one has

|\mathcal{L}([v])|=\frac{q^{2}(q+1)}{q+1}=q^{2}.

Given
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, one has

|\mathcal{L}(\omega)|=\frac{q^{2}(q+1)}{q^{2}+q}=q.

Given
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, one has

|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p})|=\frac{q^{2}(q+1)}{q^{2}}=q+1.

Moreover, there is a natural one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p})
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. So each line in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p})
has different directions and different refined directions.

Basic lemmas in analysis

The first lemma collects Propositions 6.11 and 6.12 of
[

]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set with
|X|=N
and let
1\leq r\leq s\leq\infty
. Then for every
g:X\to\mathbb{C}
, one has

\|g\|_{\ell^{s}(X)}\leq\|g\|_{\ell^{r}(X)},

and

\|g\|_{\ell^{r}(X)}\leq N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}}\,\|g\|_{\ell^{s}(X)}.

The second lemma is an interpolation, whose proof can be found in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be finite sets, and let

T:\ell^{p_{0}}(X)\cap\ell^{p_{1}}(X)\longrightarrow\ell^{r_{0}}(Y)\cap\ell^{r_{1}}(Y)

be a linear operator.
Let
1\leq p_{0},p_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
.
Assume that the following estimates hold for all functions
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
:

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{r_{0}}(Y)}\leq C_{0}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(X)},\qquad\|Tf\|_{\ell^{r_{1}}(Y)}\leq C_{1}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{p_{1}}(X)}.

Then, for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, we have

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{r}(Y)}\leq C_{0}^{1-\theta}C_{1}^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(X)},

where the exponents
p
and
r
are defined by

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\qquad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}.

2.3
Planar domination lemma

For
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, define

\mathcal{M}_{2}f([\mathbf{v}]):=\max_{\ell\parallel[\mathbf{v}]}\ \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell}|f(\mathbf{x})|,\qquad[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

(13)

where the maximum is taken over all affine lines
\ell\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq u\leq\infty
and

F:\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
be given. For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, define

G(x,y):=\Bigl(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|F(x,y,t)|^{u}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{u}}

when
1\leq u<\infty
, and

G(x,y):=\max\limits_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|F(x,y,t)|

when
u=\infty
. Then

\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}=\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))},

(14)

and

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F([\mathbf{v}])\leq\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}])

(15)

for all
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

The identity (
14
) follows by expanding both sides:

\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}^{u}=\sum_{x,y}\sum_{t}|F(x,y,t)|^{u}=\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}^{u}.

Fix
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and choose a representative
\mathbf{v}=(a,b)\neq(0,0)
. Fix
\mathbf{p}=(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
and set
L=L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
be the projection defined by
\pi(x,y,t)=(x,y)
.
By (
3
), the projected set
\pi(L)
equals the affine line

\ell=\{(x_{0}+as,\ y_{0}+bs):s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},

which has direction
[\mathbf{v}]
. Since
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
, the map
s\mapsto(x_{0}+as,y_{0}+bs)
is a bijection from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
onto
\ell
.
Therefore, for each
(x,y)\in\ell
, there is a unique
t=t(x,y)
such that
(x,y,t)\in L
. Hence

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in L}|F(\mathbf{z})|=\sum_{(x,y)\in\ell}|F(x,y,t(x,y))|.

For each fixed
(x,y)
, one has

|F(x,y,t(x,y))|\leq\Bigl(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|F(x,y,t)|^{u}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{u}}=G(x,y).

Summing over
(x,y)\in\ell
implies

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in L}|F(\mathbf{z})|\leq\sum_{(x,y)\in\ell}G(x,y)\leq\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}]).

Taking the maximum over
\mathbf{p}
gives (
15
). The case
u=\infty
is identical.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

It is worth noting that the projection domination
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\leq\mathcal{M}_{2}G
is not reversible. To see this, fix a nonsquare
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and define

\phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{q},\qquad\phi(x,y):=x^{2}+\eta y^{2}.

Let

E_{\phi}:=\{(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):\ t=\phi(x,y)\}.

A direct computation shows that for all directions
[\mathbf{v}]
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}])=q
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F([\mathbf{v}])\leq 2
.

2.4
Planar Kakeya
\ell^{2}
bound

Lemma 2.5
.

For every function
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, one has

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{f}:=|f|
.
For every affine line
\ell\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, one has

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell}|f(\mathbf{x})|=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell}\tilde{f}(\mathbf{x}).

Taking the maximum over all affine lines
\ell
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
implies

\mathcal{M}_{2}f([\mathbf{v}])=\mathcal{M}_{2}\tilde{f}([\mathbf{v}])\qquad\text{for all }[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Also,

\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Therefore, it is enough to prove the stated inequality for
\tilde{f}
. Without loss of generality, we assume at the beginning of the proof that
f\geq 0
.

For each direction
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we choose an affine line

\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
. For this fixed family
\{\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\}_{[\mathbf{v}]}
, define the linear operator

T:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
by

Th([\mathbf{v}]):=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}h(\mathbf{x}).

It is enough to prove that the estimate

\|T\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2q},

(16)

holds uniformly over all such line families. Here
\|T\|_{X\to Y}:=\sup\{\|Th\|_{Y}:\ \|h\|_{X}=1\}
denotes the operator norm.

To see how this implies the conclusion, for a given function
f
, and each
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we choose an affine line

\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
such that

\mathcal{M}_{2}f([\mathbf{v}])=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}f(\mathbf{x}).

Let
T
be the operator associated with this family.
Since the bound
\|T\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2q}
is uniform over all line families, it applies to this choice.
Therefore,
Tf=\mathcal{M}_{2}f
, and

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=\|Tf\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2q}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

We now prove the estimate (
16
).
We compute the adjoint
T^{\ast}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})
.
For
g:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, one has

T^{\ast}g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}g([\mathbf{v}])\,\mathbf{1}_{\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}(\mathbf{x}).

Therefore, for each
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,

\displaystyle(TT^{\ast}g)([\mathbf{v}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}T^{*}g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{x\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}\ \sum_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}g([\mathbf{v}^{\prime}])\,\mathbf{1}_{\ell_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}g([\mathbf{v}^{\prime}])\,|\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\cap\ell_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]}|.

If
[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]=[\mathbf{v}]
, then
|\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\cap\ell_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]}|=|\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}|=q
.
If
[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\neq[\mathbf{v}]
, then
\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}
and
\ell_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]}
have distinct directions, hence they meet in exactly one point in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, so
|\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\cap\ell_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]}|=1
.
Thus

(TT^{\ast}g)([\mathbf{v}])=q\,g([\mathbf{v}])+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\neq[\mathbf{v}]\end{subarray}}g([\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]).

(17)

Let
J:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
be the rank–one operator

Jg([\mathbf{v}]):=\sum_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}g([\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]).

Then (
17
) may be rewritten as

TT^{\ast}=(q-1)I+J.

We now determine the eigenvalues of
J
.
Let
\mathbf{1}
denote the constant function
\mathbf{1}([\mathbf{v}]):=1
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Then

J\mathbf{1}([\mathbf{v}])=\sum_{[\mathbf{v}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}1=|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q+1,

so
\mathbf{1}
is an eigenvector of
J
with eigenvalue
q+1
.

Next, consider the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}
in

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
, namely

\Bigl(\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}\Bigr)^{\perp}=\Bigl\{g:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}:\ \langle g,\mathbf{1}\rangle=0\Bigr\}.

Since the inner product is
\langle g,h\rangle=\sum\limits_{[\mathbf{v}]}g([\mathbf{v}])\overline{h([\mathbf{v}])}
, the condition

\langle g,\mathbf{1}\rangle=0
is equivalent to

\sum_{[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}g([\mathbf{v}])=0.

For such
g
, one has
Jg\equiv 0
, hence every
g\in(\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}\})^{\perp}
is an eigenvector of
J
with eigenvalue
0
.

Therefore, the spectrum of
J
consists of the eigenvalue
q+1
on
\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}
and the eigenvalue
0
on its orthogonal complement.
It follows that the spectrum of
TT^{*}=(q-1)I+J
consists of exactly two eigenvalues

(q-1)+(q+1)=2q\qquad\text{and}\qquad(q-1)+0=q-1.

Hence,

\|TT^{\ast}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=2q.

Consequently,

\|T\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}^{2}=\|TT^{\ast}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=2q,

so
\|T\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q})}\leq\sqrt{2q}
.
This implies

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=\|Tf\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2q}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

)

3.1
Planar endpoint bounds

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
G:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

\displaystyle\leq(q+1)\,\|G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})},

(18)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})},

(19)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

\displaystyle\leq q\,\|G\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

(20)

Proof.

For (
18
), note that for every direction class
[\mathbf{v}]
, one has

\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}])\leq\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,2}}G(\mathbf{z})=\|G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Summing over all
[\mathbf{v}]
immediately implies (
18
). For (
20
), observe that each affine line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,2}
contains exactly
q
points, and hence

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\ell}G(\mathbf{z})\leq q\,\|G\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}\qquad\text{for every line }\ell\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}.

Taking the maximum over all lines
\ell
gives (
20
). Finally, (
19
) follows directly from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3.2
A planar
\ell^{u}\to\ell^{u}
theorem

Theorem 3.2
.

For every
1\leq u\leq\infty
and for all
G:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
, we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\tau(u)}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}

where

\tau(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{u},&1\leq u\leq 2,\\[3.99994pt]
\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{u},&2\leq u\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
G:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be given.
For each direction
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, choose an affine line

\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with direction
[\mathbf{v}]
such that

\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}])=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}G(\mathbf{x}).

Define the linear operator
T
on functions
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
by

(Tf)([\mathbf{v}]):=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}f(\mathbf{x}),\qquad[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Then

(TG)([\mathbf{v}])=\mathcal{M}_{2}G([\mathbf{v}])\qquad\text{for all }[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

(21)

Therefore,

\|\mathcal{M}_{2}G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=\|TG\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}.

(22)

We next record three endpoint bounds for
T
.

The
\ell^{1}
bound.

For every
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}f(\mathbf{x})\Bigr|\leq\sum_{[\mathbf{v}]}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}|f(\mathbf{x})|\leq(q+1)\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}|f(\mathbf{x})|

\displaystyle=(q+1)\,\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

(23)

The
\ell^{\infty}
bound.

For every
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
and every
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,

|(Tf)([\mathbf{v}])|=\Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}f(\mathbf{x})\Bigr|\leq\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{[\mathbf{v}]}}|f(\mathbf{x})|\leq q\,\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})},

since each affine line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
has exactly
q
points.
Taking the maximum over
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
implies

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq q\,\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

(24)

The
\ell^{2}
bound.

The operator
T
is obtained by selecting one affine line in each direction,
so the proof of Lemma
2.5
applies verbatim to
T
.
Therefore, for every
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

(25)

We now interpolate the bounds to obtain the desired exponents.

Case 1:
1\leq u\leq 2
.

Choose
\theta\in[0,1]
so that

\frac{1}{u}=(1-\theta)\cdot 1+\theta\cdot\frac{1}{2}.

Then
\theta=2\bigl(1-\frac{1}{u}\bigr)
.
Apply Lemma
2.2
to the linear operator
T
,
using the endpoint estimates (
3.2
) and (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\bigl(q+1\bigr)^{1-\theta}\,\bigl(\sqrt{2}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}\leq 2^{1-\frac{1}{u}}\,q^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}\leq\sqrt{2}q^{\frac{1}{u}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}

(26)

where we have used
1-\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac{1}{u}
and
1\leq u\leq 2
in the last inequality.
From here, we apply (
26
) with
f=G
and use (
22
).

Case 2:
2\leq u\leq\infty
.

Choose
\theta\in[0,1]
so that

\frac{1}{u}=(1-\theta)\cdot\frac{1}{2}+\theta\cdot 0.

Then
\theta=1-\frac{2}{u}
.
Apply Lemma
2.2
to the linear operator
T
,
using the endpoint estimates (
25
) and (
24
), we obtain

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\bigl(\sqrt{2}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)^{1-\theta}\,(q)^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\theta}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Since
\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\theta=1-\frac{1}{u}
, this implies

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{1-\frac{1}{u}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})},

(27)

which implies

\|TG\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{1-\frac{1}{u}}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Combining the two cases gives

\|TG\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\sqrt{2}\,q^{\tau(u)}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2})}.

Together with (
22
), this proves the theorem.
∎

3.3
Diagonal bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
1\leq u\leq\infty
and for all

5.1
The case
A_{1}(u,v)\geq 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has
A_{1}(u,v)\geq 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}
.

Proof.

Let

S:=\bigcup_{[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}L_{\mathbf{0},[\mathbf{v}]},\qquad F:=\mathbf{1}_{S}.

Distinct horizontal lines through
\mathbf{0}
intersect only at
\mathbf{0}
.
Since each such line has
q
points and there are
q+1
directions, we obtain

|S|=1+(q+1)(q-1)=q^{2}.

Therefore,
\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=|S|^{\frac{1}{u}}=q^{\frac{2}{u}}
.

For every
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the line
L_{\mathbf{0},[\mathbf{v}]}
is contained in
S
, hence

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F([\mathbf{v}])\geq\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in L_{\mathbf{0},[\mathbf{v}]}}\mathbf{1}_{S}(\mathbf{x})=q.

Consequently,

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\geq\Bigl(\sum_{[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}q^{v}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{v}}=q\,(q+1)^{\frac{1}{v}}\geq q^{1+\frac{1}{v}}.

Therefore,
q^{A_{1}(u,v)}\gtrsim q^{1+\frac{1}{v}}/q^{\frac{2}{u}}
, and hence
A_{1}(u,v)\geq 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For every

Then

be the direction of

Then

Therefore,
\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\geq q
for every

Thus

∎

6
Proof of Theorem
1.4
–
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

It follows from Lemmas
5.3
,
5.2
, and
5.1
that

A_{1}(u,v)\geq\max\Bigl(\frac{1}{v},\ 1-\frac{1}{u},\ 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\Bigr).

It remains to prove the matching upper bound. We distinguish four regions.

(i) The region:
1\leq v\leq u\leq 2
.

By Lemma
4.4
one has
A_{1}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{v}
.
In this region
\frac{1}{u}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{v}\geq\frac{1}{u}
, hence
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\leq\frac{1}{v}
and
1-\frac{1}{u}\leq\frac{1}{v}
.
Therefore,
\max(\frac{1}{v},1-\frac{1}{u},1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u})=\frac{1}{v}
, so the upper bound is sharp.

(ii) The region:
2\leq u\leq v\leq\infty
.

By Lemma
4.2
, one has
A_{1}(u,v)\leq 1-\frac{1}{u}
.
In this region
\frac{1}{u}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{v}\leq\frac{1}{u}
, hence
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\leq 1-\frac{1}{u}
and
\frac{1}{v}\leq 1-\frac{1}{u}
.
Therefore, the maximum equals
1-\frac{1}{u}
, so the upper bound is sharp.

(iii) The region:
2\leq u\leq\infty
and
1\leq v\leq u
.

By Lemma
4.1
, one has
A_{1}(u,v)\leq 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}
.
Since
\frac{1}{u}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\geq\frac{1}{v}
and
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\geq 1-\frac{1}{u}
.
Therefore, the maximum equals
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}
, so the upper bound is sharp.

(iv) The region:
1\leq u\leq 2
and
u\leq v\leq\infty
.
We have
A_{1}(u,v)\leq\max(\frac{1}{v},1-\frac{1}{u})
. Indeed, if
v\geq\frac{u}{u-1}
, apply Lemma
4.3
. If
u\leq v\leq\frac{u}{u-1}
, apply Lemma
4.5
.

In this region
\frac{1}{u}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{v}\leq\frac{1}{u}
, hence
1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\leq 1-\frac{1}{u}
.
Therefore,
\max(\frac{1}{v},1-\frac{1}{u},1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u})=\max(\frac{1}{v},1-\frac{1}{u})
, so the upper bound matches the claimed maximum.

Since these four regions cover all
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, we conclude that

A_{1}(u,v)=\max\Bigl(\frac{1}{v},\ 1-\frac{1}{u},\ 1+\frac{1}{v}-\frac{2}{u}\Bigr).

This completes the proof.
∎

7
Proof of Theorem
1.5
–
\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

The argument is the same as that of
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The proof for
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is self-contained. In higher dimensions, the only new input is the diagonal estimate in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
due to Ellenberg, Oberlin, and Tao in
[
10
]
, which is an extension of Lemma
2.5
. For completeness, we provide a sketch in this section.

Note that

\bigl|\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\bigr|=\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q-1},

(33)

and hence

q^{2n-1}\leq\bigl|\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\bigr|\leq 2q^{2n-1}.

(34)

We begin by extending the projection and domination principle of Lemma

2.3
to the higher-dimensional setting.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
1\leq u\leq\infty
and let
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
.
Define

G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\Bigl(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)|^{u}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{u}}\quad\text{for }1\leq u<\infty,\qquad G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\max_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}|F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)|\quad\text{for }u=\infty.

Then

\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})}=\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

and

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F([\mathbf{v}])\leq\mathcal{M}_{2n}G([\mathbf{v}])\quad\text{for all }[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

where
\mathcal{M}_{2n}
denotes the standard Kakeya maximal operator on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
.

Proof.

The identity of norms follows by expanding both sides.
For the domination, fix
[\mathbf{v}]
and choose a representative
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\neq 0
.
Let
\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and set
L=L_{\mathbf{p},[\mathbf{v}]}
.
The projection
\pi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
maps
L
bijectively to the affine line

\ell=\{(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0})+s(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}):s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}.

Therefore,

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in L}|F(\mathbf{x})|=\sum_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\ell}|F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}))|\leq\sum_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\ell}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

where
t(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is the unique
t
such that
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)\in L
.
Taking the maximum over
p
gives
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F([\mathbf{v}])\leq\mathcal{M}_{2n}G([\mathbf{v}])
.
∎

With the domination lemma in hand, it remains to bound
\mathcal{M}_{2n}G
in

\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
. This requires a sharp diagonal estimate
for the standard Kakeya maximal operator on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, which is provided
by the following result of Ellenberg, Oberlin, and Tao in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 7.2

(Theorem 1.3,
[
10
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{d}
be the Kakeya maximal operator from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
into
\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{M}_{d}f(\omega)=\max_{\ell\parallel\omega}\ \sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\ell}|f(\mathbf{z})|\qquad(\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{d-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})),

where the maximum is over all affine lines
\ell\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with direction
\omega
.
Then

\|\mathcal{M}_{d}f\|_{\ell^{d}(\mathbb{P}^{d-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{d}\,q^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{d}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d})},

where
C_{d}
depends only on
d
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
.
For every
1\leq u\leq\infty
and every
F:\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
one has

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{n,u}\,q^{\tau_{2n}(u)}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))},

where

\tau_{2n}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{2n-1}{u},&1\leq u\leq 2n,\\[4.0pt]
1-\frac{1}{u},&2n\leq u\leq\infty,\end{cases}

and

C_{n,u}:=\begin{cases}2^{\,1-\theta(u)}\,C_{2n}^{\,\theta(u)},&1\leq u\leq 2n,\\[3.99994pt]
C_{2n}^{\,\frac{2n}{u}},&2n\leq u\leq\infty,\end{cases}\qquad\theta(u):=\frac{2n}{2n-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{u}\Bigr).

Proof.

Let
G
be the function on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
associated with
F
as in Lemma
7.1
. By that lemma, we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}.

Assume first that
1\leq u\leq 2n
.
By Theorem
7.2
, one has

\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{2n}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{2n}\,q^{\frac{2n-1}{2n}}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})}.

For each direction
[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
the maximal operator satisfies

\mathcal{M}_{2n}G([\mathbf{v}])\leq\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}}|G(\mathbf{x})|.

Summing over all directions implies

\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq\bigl|\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\bigr|\,\|G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})}\leq 2q^{2n-1}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})}.

Interpolating between the trivial
\ell^{1}
bound and the sharp
\ell^{2n}
estimate of Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao via Riesz–Thorin, we obtain for every
1\leq u\leq 2n
,

\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{n,u}\,q^{\frac{2n-1}{u}}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})},

where
C_{n,u}=2^{\,1-\theta(u)}\,C_{2n}^{\,\theta(u)}
with
\theta(u):=\frac{2n}{2n-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{u}\Bigr)
.

Assume now that
2n\leq u\leq\infty
.
The trivial bound
\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq q\|G\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{2n}_{q})}
and interpolation with the case
u=2n
provide

\|\mathcal{M}_{2n}G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{n,u}\,q^{1-\frac{1}{u}}\,\|G\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n})},

where
C_{n,u}=C_{2n}^{\,\frac{2n}{u}}
.

Therefore,
\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}\leq C_{n,u}q^{1-\frac{1}{u}}\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}
.
∎

The remaining arguments mirror those in Section
4
, with
|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q+1
replaced by

|\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
.

Lemma 7.4

Lemma 7.5
.

Let

Let

We record two endpoint bounds for

to

which implies the claim.
∎

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\geq\frac{1}{v}
.
∎

9.4
The case
A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ 1-\frac{1}{u}

Lemma 9.6
.

For every
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ 1-\frac{1}{u}.

Proof.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be a fixed horizontal line and set
F:=\mathbf{1}_{L}
.
Then
\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=|L|^{\frac{1}{u}}=q^{\frac{1}{u}}
.
Let
\omega_{0}:=\mathrm{Dir}(L)
.
Since the maximum in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}
ranges over all horizontal lines
of refined direction
\omega_{0}
, we have

(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F)(\omega_{0})\ \geq\ \sum_{\mathbf{p}\in L}\mathbf{1}_{L}(\mathbf{p})\ =\ q.

Hence,
\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\geq q
for every
v
.
Therefore
q^{A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)}\gtrsim q/q^{\frac{1}{u}}
, and the claim follows.
∎

9.5
The case
A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ \frac{2}{v}-\frac{1}{u}

Lemma 9.7
.

For every
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ \frac{2}{v}-\frac{1}{u}.

Proof.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
be two non-parallel affine lines and set

B:=\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}
. Then
|B|=2q-1\sim q
.
Define

E:=\{(x,y,0)\in\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):(x,y)\in B\},\qquad F:=\mathbf{1}_{E}.

Then
\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=|E|^{\frac{1}{u}}\sim q^{\frac{1}{u}}
.

We claim that
(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F)(\omega)\geq 1
for every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
Fix
\omega=[a:b:c]\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, and let
\ell_{\omega}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
be the corresponding affine line.
If
\ell_{\omega}
is not parallel to
\ell_{1}
, then
\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{1}\neq\emptyset
; otherwise
\ell_{\omega}
intersects
\ell_{2}
since
\ell_{2}
is not parallel to
\ell_{1}
.
In either case we may choose
(x_{\omega},y_{\omega})\in\ell_{\omega}\cap B
, so
(x_{\omega},y_{\omega},0)\in E
.
By (
37
), the horizontal line
L_{\omega}:=L_{(x_{\omega},y_{\omega},0),[a:b]}
has refined direction
\omega
.
Hence

(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F)(\omega)\ \geq\ \sum_{\mathbf{p}\in L_{\omega}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathbf{p})\ \geq\ 1.

Therefore,

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\ \geq\ |\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{v}}\ \sim\ q^{\frac{2}{v}},

using
|\mathcal{D}_{1}|=q^{2}+q
.

This forces
q^{A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)}\gtrsim q^{\frac{2}{v}}/q^{\frac{1}{u}}
, and hence

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\geq\frac{2}{v}-\frac{1}{u}
.
∎

9.6
The case
A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ 1+\frac{2}{v}-\frac{3}{u}

Lemma 9.8
.

For every
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
, one has

A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)\ \geq\ 1+\frac{2}{v}-\frac{3}{u}.

Proof.

Let
F\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then

\|F\|_{\ell^{u}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=|\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|^{\frac{1}{u}}=q^{\frac{3}{u}}
.
For each
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, choose any horizontal line
L_{\omega}\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
.
Then
\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in L_{\omega}}|F(\mathbf{p})|=|L_{\omega}|=q
, so

(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F)(\omega)=q
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
Hence

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{v}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}=q\,|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{v}}\sim q\cdot q^{\frac{2}{v}}=q^{1+\frac{2}{v}}.

Therefore
q^{A^{\mathrm{rd}}_{1}(u,v)}\gtrsim q^{1+\frac{2}{v}}/q^{\frac{3}{u}}
, giving the claim.

∎

For every

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemmas

–
9.8
.
∎

11
Discussions and examples

11.1
Examples distinguishing affine Kakeya and refined-direction horizontal Kakeya

In this subsection, we record examples showing that the usual affine Kakeya in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
and the full
refined-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are
incomparable: neither condition implies the other.

We use the standard coordinate identification of sets

\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3},\qquad\mathbf{p}=(x,y,t).

Recall that the set of refined directions is

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{[a:b:c]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):(a,b)\neq(0,0)\}.

If
L=L_{\mathbf{p},[(a,b)]}
is a horizontal line (with horizontal direction
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
),
we write

\mathrm{Dir}(L)=[a:b:c(L)]\in\mathcal{D}_{1},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c(L):=x_{0}b-y_{0}a}\quad\text{for }\mathbf{p}=(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in L.

As discussed at the beginning of Section
8
, every
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
admits a unique
normal form

\omega=[1:m:\gamma]\quad(m,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}),\qquad\text{or}\qquad\omega=[0:1:\gamma]\quad(\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}),

and the family of horizontal lines with refined direction
\omega
is precisely

\displaystyle L_{\omega,\tau}

\displaystyle=\{(x,\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}mx-\gamma},\ \tau+\gamma x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\omega=[1:m:\gamma],

\displaystyle L_{\omega,\tau}

\displaystyle=\{({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma},\ y,\ \tau+\gamma y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\omega=[0:1:\gamma],

where
\tau\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
parametrizes the
q
distinct horizontal lines of refined direction
\omega
.

Example 11.1

(Affine Kakeya but not full refined-direction horizontal Kakeya)
.

Fix
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
Write
\omega_{0}
in normal form as
\omega_{0}=[1:m_{0}:\gamma_{0}]
or
\omega_{0}=[0:1:\gamma_{0}]
.

Define a subset
S_{\omega_{0}}\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by

S_{\omega_{0}}:=\begin{cases}\{(0,\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\gamma_{0}},t):t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},&\text{if }\omega_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[1:m_{0}:\gamma_{0}]},\\[1.99997pt]
\{({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{0}},0,t):t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},&\text{if }\omega_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0:1:\gamma_{0}]}.\end{cases}

Let
E:=\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus S_{\omega_{0}}
. Then, viewed as a subset of

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
, the set
E
is an affine Kakeya set, but
E
is not a full refined-direction
horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya set in the sense of Definition
1.3
.

Proof.

We first verify that
E
is not full refined-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya. Consider the
family
\{L_{\omega_{0},\tau}\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}
of all horizontal lines with refined direction

\omega_{0}
.
If
\omega_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[1:m_{0}:\gamma_{0}]}
, then for every
\tau\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
one has

(0,\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\gamma_{0}},\tau)\in L_{\omega_{0},\tau}\cap S_{\omega_{0}}.

If
\omega_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0:1:\gamma_{0}]}
, then for every
\tau\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
one has

({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{0}},0,\tau)\in L_{\omega_{0},\tau}\cap S_{\omega_{0}}.

In either case, every horizontal line
L
with
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega_{0}
meets
S_{\omega_{0}}
, and
hence no such line can be contained in
E
. Therefore,
E
fails the defining condition in
Definition
1.3
at the refined direction
\omega_{0}
.

We next show that
E
is affine Kakeya in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. Fix an ambient direction

[\mathbf{v}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and choose a representative
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
The affine lines in direction
[\mathbf{v}]
are the cosets of the 1-dimensional subspace

\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle
, and these
q^{2}
lines form a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
.
Since
|S_{\omega_{0}}|=q
, at most
q
of these
q^{2}
lines meet
S_{\omega_{0}}
(each point lies on
exactly one line of direction
[\mathbf{v}]
). Hence, there exists an affine line
\ell
of ambient
direction
[\mathbf{v}]
such that
\ell\cap S_{\omega_{0}}=\varnothing
, and therefore
\ell\subset E
.
As
[\mathbf{v}]
was arbitrary,
E
contains an affine line in every ambient direction, i.e.
E
is
an affine Kakeya set.
∎

Example 11.2

(Full refined-direction horizontal Kakeya but not affine Kakeya)
.

Assume
q
is odd and
q>3
.
For each
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
in normal form, define a horizontal line
L_{\omega}
by

L_{[1:m:\gamma]}:=\{(x,\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}mx-\gamma},\ m^{2}+\gamma x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},\qquad L_{[0:1:\gamma]}:=\{({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma},\ y,\ \gamma y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

Let

E:=\bigcup_{\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}L_{\omega}\ \subset\ \mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Then,
E
is a full refined-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya set (Definition
1.3
),
but
E
is not an affine Kakeya set in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
E
is full refined-direction horizontal Heisenberg Kakeya.

We now show that
E
is not affine Kakeya by proving that
E
contains no vertical affine line, i.e. no affine line of direction
[0:0:1]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Fix
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}

and consider the vertical fiber

V_{(x_{0},y_{0})}:=\{(x_{0},y_{0},t):t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

It suffices to show that
V_{(x_{0},y_{0})}\nsubseteq E
for every
(x_{0},y_{0})
.

Define

T(x_{0},y_{0}):=\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\ (x_{0},y_{0},t)\in E\}.

We bound
|T(x_{0},y_{0})|
. First consider the contribution from directions of the form
\omega=[1:m:\gamma]
.
For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the point
(x_{0},y_{0},t)
can lie on
L_{[1:m:\gamma]}
only if

y_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}mx_{0}-\gamma}
, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma=mx_{0}-y_{0}}
. For this value of
\gamma
, the point of

L_{[1:m:\gamma]}
with
x=x_{0}
has

t=m^{2}+\gamma x_{0}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m^{2}+(mx_{0}-y_{0})x_{0}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m^{2}+x_{0}^{2}m-x_{0}y_{0}}.

Hence, the set of
t
-values arising from all such
\omega
is contained in

A(x_{0},y_{0}):=\left\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m^{2}+x_{0}^{2}m-x_{0}y_{0}}:\ m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

Since
q
is odd, completing the square shows that
A(x_{0},y_{0})
is a translate of the set of squares
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and therefore

|A(x_{0},y_{0})|\leq\frac{q+1}{2}.

Next consider directions of the form
\omega=[0:1:\gamma]
. For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
there is a
unique
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma=x_{0}}
such that
L_{[0:1:\gamma]}
has first coordinate equal to
x_{0}
. Intersecting

L_{[0:1:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x_{0}}]}=\big\{\big(x_{0},\ y,\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x_{0}y}\big):y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\big\}

with
y=y_{0}
yields at most one additional value
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t=x_{0}y_{0}}
.

Consequently,

|T(x_{0},y_{0})|\leq|A(x_{0},y_{0})|+1\leq\frac{q+1}{2}+1=\frac{q+3}{2}<q\qquad\text{since }q>3.

Thus,
V_{(x_{0},y_{0})}\nsubseteq E
for all
(x_{0},y_{0})
, so
E
contains no vertical affine line.
In particular,
E
does not contain an affine line in the ambient direction
[0:0:1]
, and hence
E

is not an affine Kakeya set in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
.
∎

The preceding examples show that, although
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
coincide
as sets, the affine Kakeya property in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
and the full refined-direction horizontal
Heisenberg Kakeya property in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are incomparable.

Example 11.3

(Sharpness of the
\ell^{3}\to\ell^{3}
bound)
.

Let
F\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, i.e.
F=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
.
Then

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}=q\,|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{1/3}\sim q^{5/3},\qquad\|F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=|\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|^{1/3}=q.

Consequently,

\frac{\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}}{\|F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}}=|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}}=(q^{2}+q)^{\frac{1}{3}}\geq q^{\frac{2}{3}}.

In particular, any estimate of the form

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}\leq C\,q^{\alpha}\,\|F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}

(valid uniformly in
q
) forces
\alpha\geq\frac{2}{3}
. Hence, the exponent
q^{2/3}
in the

\ell^{3}\to\ell^{3}
bound is sharp up to absolute constants.

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
. As above, there exists a horizontal line
L
with
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega
,
and
|L|=q
. Since
F\equiv 1
, it follows that

(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F)(\omega)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\ \mathrm{horizontal}\\
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in L}|F(\mathbf{p})|=q.

Thus,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\equiv q
on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, and therefore

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rd}}_{\mathbb{H}_{1}}F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathcal{D}_{1})}=\Bigl(\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}q^{3}\Bigr)^{1/3}=q\,|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{1/3}.

Also
|\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q^{3}
, so
\|F\|_{\ell^{3}(\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))}=q
.

Finally,
|\mathcal{D}_{1}|=q^{2}+q
, hence
|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{1/3}=(q^{2}+q)^{1/3}\geq q^{2/3}
, which implies the stated sharpness.
∎

11.2
An outlook toward a new approach of the affine Kakeya problem in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
be an affine Kakeya set, and identify
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
with the underlying set of
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Write
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{[0:0:1]\}
for the set of non-vertical directions, and decompose

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\Omega_{1}\sqcup\Omega_{2}
as follows:
\Omega_{1}
consists of those
\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
for which
E
contains a horizontal line

L\subset\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\mathrm{Dir}(L)=\omega
, and
\Omega_{2}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{1}
.

Theorem
1.8
immediately controls the
\Omega_{1}
contribution. More precisely,

|E|\ \geq\ C^{-2}\,q\,|\Omega_{1}|.

In particular,
|\Omega_{1}|\gtrsim q^{2}
already forces
|E|\gtrsim q^{3}
.
Since
|\mathcal{D}_{1}|=q^{2}+q
, at least one of
\Omega_{1}
or
\Omega_{2}
has size
\gtrsim q^{2}
; hence, for the purpose of proving a lower bound of
order
q^{3}
, it suffices to treat the complementary range

|\Omega_{2}|\ \gtrsim\ q^{2}.

(59)

In this sense, the affine Kakeya problem in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
is reduced to understanding an
\Omega_{2}
-affine Kakeya configuration, in which a large
proportion of directions is realized only by non-horizontal lines in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

The advantage of this viewpoint is that the
\Omega_{2}
lines satisfy a concrete structural constraint.
Fix
\omega=[a:b:c]\in\Omega_{2}
with
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, and let

\ell_{\omega}=\{(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})+s(a,b,c):s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\subset E

be a Kakeya line of ambient direction
\omega
contained in
E
.
By definition of
\Omega_{2}
, the line
\ell_{\omega}
is
not
horizontal in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Equivalently, its basepoint fails the horizontality relation

bx_{0}-ay_{0}\ \neq\ c.

Thus, the Kakeya line in direction
[a:b:c]
is forced to choose its basepoint away from the affine line

\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}:\ bx-ay=c\}
in the
(x,y)
plane. We refer to this as a
basepoint-avoidance
constraint.

To quantify this failure of horizontality in a way that is intrinsic to the affine line (independent of the chosen point on the line and independent of scaling of
(a,b,c)
), we define a scalar parameter
\mu(\ell)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
using the normal-form coordinates on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
Write the direction of
\ell
in the unique normal form

\omega=[1:m:\gamma]\quad(m,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}),\qquad\text{or}\qquad\omega=[0:1:\gamma]\quad(\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}).

If
\ell
has direction
\omega=[1:m:\gamma]
, pick any point
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\ell
, and set

\mu(\ell)\;:=\;\gamma-(mx_{0}-y_{0})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

If
\ell
has direction
\omega=[0:1:\gamma]
, pick any point
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\ell
, and set

\mu(\ell)\;:=\;\gamma-x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

A direct check shows that
\mu(\ell)
does not depend on the choice of
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\ell
.
Moreover,
\mu(\ell)=0
if and only if
\ell
is horizontal in
\mathbb{H}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In particular, for
\omega\in\Omega_{2}
we necessarily have
\mu(\ell_{\omega})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

Now fix a choice of the Kakeya line
\ell_{\omega}\subset E
for each
\omega\in\Omega_{2}
, and define

k(\omega):=\mu(\ell_{\omega})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\qquad\Lambda_{k}:=\{\omega\in\Omega_{2}:\ k(\omega)=k\}.

Then,
\Omega_{2}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\Lambda_{k}
is a partition.

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, lines with
\mu(\ell)=k
can be
straightened
into horizontal lines by an explicit mapping:
in the chart
\omega=[1:m:\gamma]
, one uses
(x,y,t)\mapsto(x,y,t-kx)
, while in the chart
\omega=[0:1:\gamma]
, one uses
(x,y,t)\mapsto(x,y,t-ky)
.
Thus, each slice
\Lambda_{k}
may be viewed (after such a mapping) as contributing a horizontal Kakeya configuration, to which Theorem
1.6
applies.
In particular, if
|\Lambda_{k}|\gtrsim q^{2}
for some
k
, then this immediately forces
|E|\gtrsim q^{3}
.

In the remaining range, where
|\Omega_{2}|\gtrsim q^{2}
, but each individual slice
|\Lambda_{k}|
