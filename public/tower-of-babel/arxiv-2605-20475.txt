Abstract
. A unitary perfect number is a positive integer n n satisfying σ ∗ ​ ( n ) = 2 ​ n \sigma^{*}(n)=2n . Only five examples are known. The Three-Filter Closure Certificate

For an impostor kernel
K
and a representative
a
in its seed
congruence class, define the candidate
(K,a)
. We exhibit a rigorous
obstruction for each candidate via one of three filters.

3.1.
Filter Z: Zsigmondy / Higgs exponent

By Zsigmondy’s theorem
[
5
]
, every primitive prime divisor

r
of
p^{e}+1
(with
e>1
) satisfies
r\equiv 1\pmod{2e}
. For

r
to be 3-Higgs, every prime factor of
2e
must be 3-Higgs and
occur in
2e
with exponent at most 3. Applied to the seed exponent

a
itself, certain values of

are immediately incompatible.

3.2.
Filter N: Seed-divisor non-3-Higgs witness

If
m\mid a
and
a/m
is odd, then
2^{m}+1\mid 2^{a}+1
. If

2^{m}+1
contains a non-3-Higgs prime factor, then any UPN with

2^{a}\,\|\,n
would inherit a non-3-Higgs prime divisor, a
contradiction. We precompute (full or partial) factorizations of

2^{m}+1
for
m\leq M
and reject
(K,a)
whenever any factored
proper divisor exposes a non-3-Higgs prime.

3.3.
Filter O: 2-adic budget overshoot

Initialize
targets
to
K\cup(\text{factor}(2^{m}+1)\text{ for cached }m\mid a,a/m\text{ odd})
. Iterate: for each

p^{e}\in
targets, factor
p^{e}+1
and accumulate the resulting odd
needs; if any prime’s incoming valuation exceeds its current target,
raise the target. At each step, compute

v_{2}:=\sum_{p^{e}\in\mathrm{targets}}v_{2}(p^{e}+1).

Lemma 2
.

The lower-bound closure is monotone in the seed. If
v_{2}>a+1
at
any step using a subset

of
\mathrm{factor}(2^{a}+1)
, the same
overshoot survives when more primes from
\mathrm{factor}(2^{a}+1)

2^{a}\,\|\,n
and odd-component
structure refining

Proof.

Adding a prime

A UPN with

The Set

Let

and
H_{\mathrm{even}}:=H\cap 2\mathbb{Z}

Proposition 5

(Structural lemma)
.

Suppose
m=2k\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
with
k
odd and
k\geq 1
. Then

(1)

Every prime factor of
k
is a 3-Higgs prime.

(2)

For every prime
q\mid k
,
v_{q}(k)\leq 3
.

(3)

For every odd divisor
d\mid k
,
2d\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
.

The first two conditions say

is
Higgs-cubefree
: a product
of 3-Higgs primes each with multiplicity at most
3
. The third
condition is a multiplicative closure: writing

S=\{k\text{ odd}:2k\in H_{\mathrm{even}}\}
,
S
is closed
under taking odd divisors.

Proof.

By Zsigmondy’s theorem applied to
\{2^{n}+1\}_{n\geq 1}
, the number

2^{2k}+1
has a primitive prime divisor
r
for every
k\geq 1
odd
(the only Zsigmondy exception for the sequence
2^{n}+1
is

,
which does not arise since
2k
is even). The order of
2
modulo
r

is exactly
4k
, so
r\equiv 1\pmod{4k}
and
r-1=4k\cdot s

for some positive integer

.

By hypothesis
r
is 3-Higgs, so every prime
q\mid r-1
is
3-Higgs and
v_{q}(r-1)\leq 3
.

(1) For each prime
q\mid k
,
q\mid 4k\mid r-1
, so
q
is
3-Higgs.

(2) For each odd prime
q\mid k
,

v_{q}(r-1)=v_{q}(4k)+v_{q}(s)=v_{q}(k)+v_{q}(s)\geq v_{q}(k)
. Combined

v_{q}(r-1)\leq 3
, we get

. (The prime

does not

(3) If

, then

, so every prime divisor of

and hence 3-Higgs. So

.
∎

(Fermat-prime obstruction)
.

H_{\mathrm{even}}\subseteq\{m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\}

Proof.

Write
m=2^{j}\cdot\ell
with
\ell

then

, which gives the conclusion.

Fix

. The order of

Now

With

Therefore: for any

5.1.
Ford’s theorem and thinness

The structural lemma (Proposition
5
) confines

H_{\mathrm{even}}
to the doubled image of a much more restrictive
arithmetic object: the multiplicative semigroup

\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}\;:=\;\left\{\,n\geq 1\;:\;\begin{array}[]{l}\text{every prime factor of }n\text{ lies in }\mathcal{P}_{3},\\
\text{and each such prime has exponent at most }3\end{array}\right\}.

where
\mathcal{P}_{3}
denotes the 3-Higgs primes (OEIS A057447). The
defining recursive closure of
\mathcal{P}_{3}
,

p\in\mathcal{P}_{3}\iff q\mid(p-1)\Rightarrow q\in\mathcal{P}_{3},

makes
\mathcal{P}_{3}
a downward-closed set of primes in the sense of
Ford
[
6
]
. Since the smallest omitted prime is
p_{0}=17

(as
17-1=2^{4}
violates the
v_{2}\leq 3
exponent bound), Ford’s
theorem applies unconditionally and yields

(2)

\Pi_{3}(X):=\#\{p\leq X:p\in\mathcal{P}_{3}\}\;\ll\;\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1+1/(p_{0}-1)}}\;=\;\frac{X}{(\log X)^{17/16}},

together with
\Pi_{3}(X)\ll X^{1-\delta}
for some absolute

\delta>0
, and the absolute convergence

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{3}}1/p<\infty
.

An entirely analogous argument applies to
H_{\mathrm{odd}}:=H\setminus H_{\mathrm{even}}
(the odd part of
H
). For
m
odd in

H
with
m>3
, the Zsigmondy theorem yields a primitive prime
divisor
r
of
2^{2m}-1
with
r\mid 2^{m}+1
; this
r
has

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=2m
and so
2m\mid r-1
, giving the
same recursive constraint on the prime factors of
m
themselves.
The exceptional small values
m=1

There exists an absolute constant

5.3.
The remaining analytic obstacle

Scale obstruction: thinness alone is insufficient.

For each
m=2k\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
, a primitive prime divisor

r
of
2^{2k}+1
must satisfy

r\equiv 1\pmod{4k},\qquad\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=4k,\qquad(r-1)/(4k)\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}.

A naive counting argument from Theorem
22
fails badly
at the relevant scale. Even with the unconditional bound

\Pi_{3}(x)\ll x^{1-\delta}
, evaluating at the primitive-divisor
height
x=2^{2k}
gives

\Pi_{3}\bigl(2^{2k}\bigr)\;\ll\;2^{2(1-\delta)k},

which is still
exponential
in
k
. Even after restricting to
the residue class
r\equiv 1\pmod{4k}
(heuristic factor

1/\varphi(4k)
), the envelope remains exponential. So Ford’s
power-saving thinness, while genuinely strong as a global statement,
is on the wrong scale to force the candidate set of 3-Higgs primitive
divisors empty for large
k
. Likewise, a hypothetical bound such
as
\Pi_{3}(x)\ll x/(\log x)^{1+\varepsilon}
would only give

\Pi_{3}(2^{2k})\ll 2^{2k}/k^{1+\varepsilon}
, still exponential.
Density arguments by themselves cannot close
Conjecture
7
.

The precise bottleneck: log-mass, not reciprocal-mass.

The mismatch above can be made fully quantitative, and identifies
exactly the inequality that closes the conjecture. By the cyclotomic
mass identity

\log\bigl(2^{2p}+1\bigr)\;=\;\log 5+\log\Phi_{4p}(2)\;\sim\;2p\log 2,

the prime divisors
r\mid 2^{2p}+1
must collectively carry total
logarithmic mass
\sum\nu_{r}(2^{2p}+1)\log r\sim 2p\log 2
. By
Hong’s valuation bound
[
14
]
, the non-primitive part contributes
at most
O(\log(4p))
to this mass; consequently, in any scenario

2p\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
, the
primitive
divisors of

\Phi_{4p}(2)
must supply primitive log-mass
\gg p
. On the other
hand, Ford’s thinness combined with the residue-class restriction
gives a
reciprocal-mass
bound only:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\text{ prime, }r\equiv 1\pmod{4p}\\
(r-1)/(4p)\,\in\,\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r}\;\ll\;\frac{1}{p}.

A set of admissible primes can have reciprocal-mass
O(1/p)
and yet
total logarithmic mass enormously larger than
p
(for example, a
single admissible prime near
2^{2p}+1
has reciprocal mass

\sim 2^{-2p}
but log-mass
\sim 2p\log 2
). So the gap between what
Ford-type thinness controls and what would yield finiteness is
exponential, of size
2^{2p}/p
. This is the exact reason the
unconditional thinness theorem (Theorem
22
), while
sharp on its own terms, falls short of finiteness.

The precise missing theorem.

The genuine analytic target is a sharper statement combining
recursive semigroup friability with an exact multiplicative-order
condition:

Conjecture 24

(Hybrid semigroup-friable shifted-prime conjecture)
.

For all sufficiently large
k\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}
, no prime

r
satisfies both

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=4k\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{r-1}{4k}\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}.

Conjecture
24
would close
Conjecture
7
immediately, since the primitive
divisor produced by Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier
[
5
]
for
k\geq 4

would have to violate at least one of the two clauses, hence fail
the 3-Higgs test.

A strictly weaker conjecture, sharper in form because it directly
addresses the log-mass gap identified above, is the following:

Conjecture 25

(Semigroup log-mass conjecture)
.

There exists an absolute
\delta>0
such that for all sufficiently
large odd primes
p\in\mathcal{P}_{3}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\text{ prime},\,r\leq 2^{2p}+1\\
r\equiv 1\pmod{4p}\\
(r-1)/(4p)\,\in\,\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}\end{subarray}}\log r\;\leq\;(2\log 2-\delta)\,p.

Conjecture
25
would close
Conjecture
7
: in any scenario

2p\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
, every primitive divisor of
\Phi_{4p}(2)

contributes to the admissible-prime sum, so the LHS must be at least
the primitive log-mass
2p\log 2-O(\log p)
, contradicting the
upper bound for
p
large.

Caution: GRH and Artin/Hooley density are not the right
target.

It is tempting to expect that effective Chebotarev or
GRH-conditional primitive-root density theorems—e.g., Hooley’s
conditional Artin estimate or its Kummer-extension refinements—will
supply the needed bound. They will not, for a structural reason worth
making explicit. Artin/Hooley-style theorems count varying primes
q

in which a fixed integer
a
has order
q-1
; for fixed
p
, however,
the set
\{r:\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=4p\}
is finite—it is
exactly the prime support of the single integer
\Phi_{4p}(2)
. Thus
the relevant object is not a density of primes in a progression at
all; it is the set of prime divisors of a fixed cyclotomic value.
GRH/Artin density is the wrong scale of object. A finiteness proof
must control the divisors of
\Phi_{4p}(2)
individually (a
divisor-transference statement), not via a density estimate on

{1\bmod 4p}
.

The sharper conditional reduction.

The actually decisive conditional statement, identified by Hong’s
exact valuation framework
[
14
]
, is the following pair. Let

A(x):=\#\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}\cap[1,x]\bigr),\qquad Z(p):=\#\Bigl\{r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2):(r-1)/(4p)\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}\Bigr\}.

Conjecture 26

(Divisor-transference for
\Phi_{4p}(2)
)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for every sufficiently large

p\in\mathcal{P}_{3}
,

Z(p)\;\leq\;C\cdot\frac{A(2^{2p}/(4p))}{p}.

Conjecture 27

(Sublogarithmic semigroup growth)
.

A(x)=o(\log x)
as
x\to\infty
.

Theorem 28

(A strong sufficient criterion)
.

Conjectures
26
and
27

together imply
|H_{\mathrm{even}}|<\infty
.

Proof.

Combining the two hypotheses:
Z(p)\leq C\cdot A(2^{2p}/(4p))/p
;
with
x=2^{2p}/(4p)
one has
\log x\asymp p
, so by
Conjecture
27

A(x)=o(\log x)=o(p)
, whence

Z(p)\to 0
. Since
Z(p)
is a non-negative integer,
Z(p)=0
for
all
p
sufficiently large. Combined with the prime-case reduction
(Theorem
8
), this gives finiteness.
∎

Caveat: the sublogarithmic growth hypothesis is very strong.

Theorem
28
is logically valid as a sufficient
condition, but Conjecture
27
is dramatically
stronger than current evidence suggests for
\mathcal{P}_{3}
. The
following elementary proposition makes this precise.

Proposition 29
.

Let
P=\{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots\}
be an infinite set of primes, and let

\mathcal{S}(P):=\Bigl\{\,\prod_{j}p_{j}^{e_{j}}:\;0\leq e_{j}\leq 3\,\Bigr\},\qquad A_{P}(x):=\#\bigl(\mathcal{S}(P)\cap[1,x]\bigr).

If
A_{P}(x)=o(\log x)
, then
\#\{p\in P:p\leq x\}=O(\log\log x)
.

Proof.

Let
x_{t}:=\prod_{j\leq t}p_{j}
. Every squarefree subproduct of

p_{1},\dots,p_{t}
lies in
\mathcal{S}(P)
and is at most
x_{t}
, so

A_{P}(x_{t})\geq 2^{t}
. The hypothesis
A_{P}(x)=o(\log x)
then gives

2^{t}\leq A_{P}(x_{t})=o(\log x_{t})=o\!\bigl(\sum_{j\leq t}\log p_{j}\bigr)
.
Since
\sum_{j\leq t}\log p_{j}\leq t\log p_{t}
, this forces

\log p_{t}\geq 2^{t}/t
for all sufficiently large
t
, i.e.,
p_{t}

grows at least roughly doubly exponentially in
t
. Inverting,

\#\{p\in P:p\leq x\}=O(\log\log x)
.
∎

Applying Proposition

to

:
if
\mathcal{P}_{3}
is infinite (as Ford
[
6
]
conjectures for
recursively closed prime sets generally, and as our own enumeration
of
\mathcal{P}_{3}
through

empirically supports, fitting

), then

would force

\Pi_{3}(x)=O(\log\log x)
, contradicting the empirical growth.
Hence Conjecture

implicitly requires either
that

is itself
finite
(an even bolder
conjecture than

finite) or some combinatorial
pathology that makes the bounded-exponent semigroup much sparser
than the underlying prime set. Neither is plausible.
Therefore Theorem

should be read as a

very strong sufficient criterion
, not the most plausible route
to an unconditional proof.

The mathematically more credible analytic targets are divisor-level

The actual missing theorem.

We emphasize that Conjecture
30
is a

divisor-level equidistribution
statement about the prime
divisors of a single fixed integer
\Phi_{4p}(2)
. It is not a
consequence of any standard Chebotarev theorem, including effective
Chebotarev under GRH (Lagarias–Odlyzko
[
11
]
). The reason is
structural: Chebotarev theorems control varying primes across a
range subject to a Frobenius condition; here the prime support of

\Phi_{4p}(2)
is a finite set, not a range. The needed object is a

divisor-randomness
or transference-type theorem inspired by
recent work of Ford on the Poisson approximation of prime divisors
of shifted primes, transplanted from random shifted primes to prime
divisors of fixed cyclotomic values. Such a transplant does not
currently exist in the literature; identifying it as the missing
input is, in our view, the precise analytic content of the conjecture.

The summary is:

•

Theorem
28
(semigroup-growth route)
is logically valid but its hypothesis is too strong to be the
realistic endpoint.

•

Conjecture
30
(divisor mod-

equidistribution) is much closer to the real obstruction and
matches the empirical evidence directly.

•

Conjecture
25
(divisor log-mass bound) sits
between the two and is the cleanest formulation tied to the
cyclotomic identity.

Why the existing literature does not yet apply.

The shifted-prime smoothness literature (Baker–Harman, Banks et al.,
Liu–Wu–Xi, Lamzouri, Banks–Friedlander–Pomerance–Shparlinski,

cf.

[
10
]
) controls primes

with

(or

) friable over an
initial segment
of small primes,
typically with Dickman-type asymptotics. The semigroup

is qualitatively different: it is defined
recursively through prime chains rather than by a size cutoff, and
carries exponent caps. On top of that, the primitive-divisor
requirement gives a Chebotarev-type
exact-order
constraint

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=4k
, not merely the congruence

r\equiv 1\pmod{4k}
. No combination of these three ingredients
(recursive semigroup, exponent cap, exact order) appears together in
the published literature.

For

with

prime (the form of all

further force. Any final closure of those candidates would need new
structure beyond the basic order divisibility.

– materially below the
universal trivial upper bound and well below

A finiteness heuristic using the product structure.

Despite the scale obstruction, a refined heuristic using the
multiplicative product structure of

does predict
finiteness. Suppose
m=2p
with
p
an odd Higgs prime and

\omega:=\omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))

2^{2p}+1=5\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}r_{i}^{e_{i}}
with primitive divisors
r_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4p}

2p\log 2\;=\;\log 5+\sum_{i}e_{i}\log r_{i}.

Modelling each
r_{i}
as a “random” prime in the residue class

1\pmod{4p}
at scale

, the probability of being 3-Higgs is
heuristically

\Pr[r_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{3}]\;\sim\;\frac{\Pi_{3}(r_{i})}{\pi(r_{i};4p,1)}\;\sim\;\varphi(4p)\log(r_{i})\cdot r_{i}^{-\eta},

with
\eta
the empirical exponent (
1-0.62\approx 0.38
) observed in our own enumeration of
\mathcal{P}_{3}
through
2^{44}
; this is computational evidence, not derived from Ford’s upper-bound theorem. Assuming
multiplicative independence across the
\omega
primitive divisors,
the joint probability that
all

are 3-Higgs is

\Pr[\text{all }r_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{3}]\;\sim\;(\varphi(4p))^{\omega}\prod_{i}(\log r_{i})\cdot\biggl(\prod_{i}r_{i}\biggr)^{-\eta}\;\leq\;(2p)^{\omega+\omega\log\log(2^{2p})}\cdot 2^{-2p\eta}.

For
\omega\ll p/\log p
(a generic upper bound) and large

,
the exponential factor
2^{-2p\eta}
dominates. Summing over primes

gives a heuristically finite total expected count of
m=2p\in H_{\mathrm{even}}
. Crucially this is on the
right
scale (it
predicts finiteness, not just thinness), but two ingredients prevent
turning it into a proof:

(i)

the “random” model for primitive cyclotomic primes is
not validated — primes dividing
\Phi_{4p}(2)
may be subject to
correlations beyond the residue-class condition;

(ii)

joint independence of the 3-Higgs property across the

\omega
primes is unproven.

Both ingredients are special cases of Conjecture

which would supply the missing equidistribution input.

A conditional finiteness theorem.

We isolate one precise way the heuristic of the previous paragraph
becomes rigorous, identifying a minimal extra input.

Theorem 31

(Conditional finiteness of
H_{\mathrm{even}}
)
.

Suppose

(H1)

(
Effective Chebotarev for prime
divisors of cyclotomic values.
) There exists an effective constant

C>0
such that for every odd prime
p
with

\omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))\geq C\log p
, at least one prime divisor

r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2)
satisfies
r\equiv 1\pmod{16}
.

(H2)

(
Effective
\omega
-growth.
)
There exists an effective threshold
p_{0}
such that for every prime

p\geq p_{0}
we have
\omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))\geq C\log p
,
where
C
is the constant from
(H1)
.

Then
H_{\mathrm{even}}
is finite. Explicitly,

H_{\mathrm{even}}\;\subseteq\;\{2,6,10,18,26,30,46,62,82,122\}\;\cup\;\{m=2p:p\text{ prime},\ p<p_{0},\ m\in\mathrm{Open}\},

where
\mathrm{Open}
denotes the partial-cofactor candidate set
identified by Theorems

9
–
17
.

Proof.

For any odd prime
p\geq p_{0}
,
\omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))\geq C\log p

by (H2); then (H1) supplies a prime
r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2)
with

r\equiv 1\pmod{16}
. Such
r
has
v_{2}(r-1)\geq 4>3
, so

r\notin\mathcal{P}_{3}
. Since
r\mid 2^{2p}+1
, this forces

m=2p\notin H_{\mathrm{even}}
. For primes
p<p_{0}
, the structural
lemma (Proposition
5
) combined with
Theorems
9
–
18
confines

H_{\mathrm{even}}\cap[2,2p_{0}]
to the finite union shown.
The complement
H_{\mathrm{even}}\cap(2p_{0},\infty)=\varnothing

by the previous step, so
H_{\mathrm{even}}
is finite.
∎

Status of the hypotheses.
(H1) is a quantitative
form of the equidistribution
heuristic
for primitive prime
divisors of cyclotomic values
\Phi_{n}(2)
. The natural expectation is
that, for
n=4p
, the primes
r\mid\Phi_{n}(2)
should be
asymptotically equidistributed among the residue classes

\bmod\,16
that are compatible with the constraint
r\equiv 1\pmod{4p}
, so a
\tfrac{1}{4}
-proportion would satisfy

r\equiv 1\pmod{16}
. (H1) demands only that this expectation
eventually produces
at least one
such prime.

We emphasize that (H1) is
not
a known consequence of any
standard effective Chebotarev theorem, including effective
Chebotarev under GRH (Lagarias–Odlyzko
[
11
]
). Such theorems
control the distribution of
varying
primes
q\leq x
in
Frobenius classes of a fixed Galois extension; they do not, on their
own, govern the distribution
within
the prime-divisor set of
a single cyclotomic value
\Phi_{4p}(2)
. This scale mismatch is
precisely the obstacle discussed in

The analytic positioning
, above. A divisor-level
transference theorem of the form “the set
\{r:r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2)\}
inherits equidistribution properties from the
ambient set
\{q\leq 2^{2p}:q\equiv 1\pmod{4p}\}
” is what
(H1) really asks for. We do not know such a theorem; the closest
literature is divisor-level work in the Stewart–Hong tradition
(see
[
13
,
14
]
), but a quantitative result of the
specific form needed for (H1) appears to be absent. (H2) is an

\omega
-growth bound related in spirit to Stewart’s program on
greatest prime factors of Lucas–Lehmer sequences

[
12
,
13
]
;
we do not claim it as a current
theorem.
A primary-source effective lower bound of the form

\omega(\Phi_{n}(2))\gg\log n
is, to our knowledge, not in the
literature; the cited Stewart papers establish largest-prime-factor
bounds rather than
\omega
-bounds, so (H2) is best regarded as a
conjectural target that the manuscript’s analytic discussion
isolates rather than a near-result.
Both hypotheses are strictly weaker than
Conjecture
24
, but each is a
conjectural

analytic input. The unconditional version of
Theorem
31
remains open and is the
natural next research goal.

A proof of Conjecture
7
(equivalently
Conjecture
24

(filter
O

survivors, would close the impostor branch of the unitary perfect
number conjecture within
\mathcal{B}

Final assessment.

The full Subbarao–Warren conjecture is not proved here. What is
proved is a clean reduction of the impostor branch (within the
bounded box
\mathcal{B}
at
\max_{a}=10000
) to a single specific
analytic question (Conjecture
7
, equivalently
Conjecture
24
), together with two rigorous structural
reductions that further sharpen the target:

•

Theorem
8
(Prime-case reduction):

|H_{\mathrm{even}}|<\infty
iff
|H_{\mathrm{even}}^{\mathrm{prime}}|<\infty
, with explicit bound
|H_{\mathrm{even}}|\leq 4^{|H_{\mathrm{even}}^{\mathrm{prime}}|}
. So the full conjecture
hinges on showing that only finitely many
primes

p
have all
factors of
2^{2p}+1
in
\mathcal{P}_{3}
.

•

Theorem
31
(Conditional
finiteness): under (H1), a divisor-level
\bmod\,16
equidistribution
hypothesis on the prime factors of
\Phi_{4p}(2)
, plus (H2), an
effective lower bound
\omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))\gg\log p
,

H_{\mathrm{even}}
is finite with an explicit candidate set.
We stress that (H1) is
not
a known consequence of GRH or of
standard effective Chebotarev theorems—those control varying
primes in Frobenius classes, whereas (H1) is a divisor-level
statement about a single fixed cyclotomic value. (H2) is the
natural target of Stewart’s program on radicals of Lehmer
sequences
[
12
,
13
]

What we cannot do unconditionally is bridge the scale
gap between Ford’s thinness bound (
\ll X^{1-\eta}
for the entire

Where the remaining theorem must live.

To make the analytic positioning fully precise: Ford’s theorem proves
that the 3-Higgs prime set
\mathcal{P}_{3}
is power-saving thin once
it omits a prime, and this suffices for our unconditional thinness
theorem for
H_{\mathrm{even}}
. However, thinness is not finiteness.
On the remaining prime branch
m=2p
, the problem is
not
to
count primes in the progression
1\bmod 4p
globally, but to exclude
admissible prime divisors of the
single fixed
cyclotomic value

\Phi_{4p}(2)
. Existing friability theorems for shifted primes
concern initial-segment smoothness of
p-1
; what is missing here is
a
divisor-level
theorem controlling the logarithmic mass of
prime divisors
r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2)
for which

(r-1)/(4p)\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}
. Of the conjectures we
formulate, the divisor log-mass bound (Conjecture
25
)
and the divisor mod-
16
equidistribution
(Conjecture

) are the most plausible long-range
analytic targets; the semigroup-counting route
(Conjecture
27
) is logically valid but, by
Proposition
29
, requires
\mathcal{P}_{3}

itself to be implausibly sparse.
