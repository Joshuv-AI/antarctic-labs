Preservation Theorems in Semiring Semantics
Sophie Brinke, Anuj Dawar, Erich Grädel, Benedikt Pago

Abstract
We study the status of classical model-theoretic preservation theorems such as the Łoś-Tarski theorem and the homomorphism preservation theorem in the context of semiring semantics. Semiring semantics has its origins in the provenance analysis of database queries but has been extended to a systematic way of evaluating logical statements to values in a commutative semiring. Depending on the underlying semiring, this allows us to track descriptions of the atomic facts that are responsible for the truth of a statement or practical information about the evaluation such as costs or confidence. The systematic development of semiring semantics for first-order logic and other logical systems raises the question to what extent classical model-theoretic results can be generalised to this setting and how such results depend on the underlying semiring. The definitions of semantic properties such as preservation under extensions, substructures, or homomorphisms naturally generalise to the setting of semiring semantics. However, the status of the corresponding preservation theorem strongly depends on the algebraic properties of the particular semirings. We prove that these preservation theorems do indeed hold for all lattice semirings (a quite large class, encompassing practically relevant semirings and in particular all min-max semirings). The proofs combine adaptations of the classical compactness and amalgamation methods with specific reduction methods for logical entailment that have been developed in semiring semantics. On the other side, variants of the existential preservation theorem fail for many other semirings, including the tropical semiring, the Viterbi semiring, the Łukasiewicz semiring, and the natural semirings ℕ \mathbb{N} and ℕ ∞ \mathbb{N}^{\infty} . Surprisingly, the existential preservation theorem does hold for finite interpretations in a number of semirings, including the three-element min-max semiring, which extends the Boolean by just a single additional truth value. Thus, the situation for these semirings is in sharp contrast to the Boolean case, where the Łoś-Tarski theorem holds in general, but not in the finite.

Introduction

Preservation theorems.
In model theory, preservation theorems
typically state that the first-order sentences satisfying a specific semantic property
are precisely those that are equivalent to sentences of a particular syntactic
form. Well-known examples include the Łoś-Tarski theorem saying that
a first-order sentence is preserved under extensions of its models if, and only if,
it is equivalent to an existential sentence, and the homomorphism preservation theorem which states that the first-order sentences preserved under homomorphisms are precisely those that are equivalent to existential-positive sentences (unions of conjunctive queries).
Preservation theorems matter because they tell us which syntactic forms capture which structural invariance properties, and this drives applications in databases,
descriptive complexity, the simplification of axiomatisations, quantifier elimination, and the study of expressive power of fragments of first-order logic.

The prominence of preservation theorems in classical model theory has motivated persistent investigations of their status in other model-theoretic contexts, in particular in finite model theory and, more recently, also in many-valued logics. In finite model theory, it has been known for a long time that most of the classical preservation theorems fail when restricted to finite structures. In particular this concerns the Łoś-Tarski theorem for which Tait
[
27
]
and Gurevich
[
21
]
provide examples of first-order sentences whose finite models are closed under extensions but which are not equivalent, over finite structures, to any existential sentence. This failure has been strengthened by Dawar and Sankaran
[
10
]
who construct, for every
n
, sentences whose finite models are closed under extensions, but which are not equivalent in the finite to any
\Sigma_{n}
-sentence.
Among the few positive results on preservation theorems in finite model theory, the most significant one, due to Rossman
[
26
]
,
establishes that the homomorphism preservation theorem does indeed hold on finite structures. There are many further results on preservation theorems in finite model theory, see
[
25
]

for a survey and
[
22
]
for a more recent treatment.

In many-valued logics, Badia et al.
[
3
]
generalised the Łoś-Tarski theorem and also the
Chang-Łoś-Suszko preservation theorem for
\Pi_{2}
-sentences under unions of chains to the
specific setting of fixed finite MTL-chains (linearly ordered algebraic models of monoidal t-norm–based logic).
Dellunde and Vidal
[
11
]
established a variant of the homomorphism preservation theorem
in the same setting and Carr
[
9
]
provided a variant of this for finite many-valued structures, based on an extension of Rossman’s proof.

Semiring semantics.
In this paper, we extend the study of preservation theorems to the setting of semiring semantics. This approach,
which has its origins in the provenance analysis of database queries
[
19
]
, is based on the idea of evaluating logical statements not just to true or false, but more generally to values in some commutative semiring
\mathcal{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
. Atomic facts are annotated by values from
S
which are then propagated through a
database query or a logical formula, keeping track whether information is used alternatively or jointly.
Depending on the chosen semiring, provenance valuations give practical information about a statement, for instance concerning the
confidence
that we may
have in its truth, the
cost
of its evaluation, the required
clearance level
for the access to not freely available data,
the
number of successful evaluation strategies
, and so on. Beyond such provenance evaluations in specific
application semirings
,
more precise information is obtained by evaluations in
provenance semirings

of polynomials or formal power series, which permit us to
track
which
atomic facts are used (and how often) to compute the answer to the query. See
[
20
,
13
]
for surveys on semiring provenance in databases.

The detailed information on a logical statement, as provided by semiring provenance, is of interest
not just for databases, but also in many other areas of logic in computer science.
There is thus ample motivation to extend the approach of semiring provenance beyond database queries to a general semiring semantics
for logical systems, in particular for full first-order logic.
Further, semiring valuations have also been successfully applied to the strategy analysis for various forms of finite and infinite games
[
17
,
15
,
23
]
.
This poses a number of mathematical challenges that have been addressed in recent research, and it
raises the question to what extent classical logical methods and results also hold in this more general context.
Research on such questions has included the study of elementary equivalence and the axiomatisability of finite semiring interpretations
[
16
]
,
of the equivalence of the relational calculus with relational algebra (Codd’s Theorem)
[
4
]
,
0-1 laws
[
14
]
, Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games
[
8
]
, the locality theorems by Hanf and Gaifman
[
5
]
, compactness
[
6
]
,
and the interplay between local consistency and global consistency for relations over semirings
[
1
,
2
]
.
The study of preservation theorems in semiring semantics extends this research to a further important model-theoretic topic.

From the study of model-theoretic methods and results in the setting of semiring semantics, the following general picture has emerged.

•

For most of the central model-theoretic notions (such as elementary equivalence, homomorphisms, evaluation and comparison games, locality, compactness, random structures etc.), the
definitions

naturally generalise to the semiring setting. In some cases, the generalisations require that semirings with specific algebraic properties are considered (for instance absorptive semirings, or semirings with infinitary operations).

•

Sometimes, variants of such definitions which are clearly equivalent in the classical Boolean case can become different, non-equivalent notions in semiring semantics. An important example is compactness, which can be formulated either in terms of satisfiability or in terms of entailment, and in the classical setting either one of the formulations of the compactness theorem obviously implies the other. In semiring semantics this is not the case. There, compactness in terms of entailment is a stronger notion than compactness in terms of satisfiability, and there are important semirings, such as the tropical semiring where the two notions can be separated
[
6
]
.

•

While the
questions
about the status of classical logical theorems in semiring semantics arise very naturally, their
answers
(and the proofs of these) become much more complicated and often strongly depend on algebraic properties of the underlying semirings. Moreover, they often require the development of new mathematical methods.

Our contributions.

For preservation properties in semiring semantics,
we make similar observations.
The notions of preservation under extensions, substructures,
or homomorphisms generalise very naturally to semiring semantics,
and so do the straightforward directions (from syntactic forms to semantic properties)
of the preservation theorems that we consider: Existential sentences are preserved under extensions, universal sentences are preserved downwards and existential-positive sentences are preserved under homomorphisms. We study the question for which semirings also the ‘difficult’ direction (from semantic properties to syntactic forms) of the preservation theorems holds, both in the case of semiring interpretations over arbitrary domains and those just over finite domains.
More precisely, our questions concerning, say, the Łoś-Tarski theorem can be stated as follows:
Fix a semiring
\mathcal{S}
. Is it the case that for every first-order sentence
\psi
which is preserved under extensions of (finite)
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations there exists a purely existential sentence
which is equivalent to
\psi
under all (finite)
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations? Can we identify algebraic notions that imply positive or negative answers to this question?

The questions for universal preservation and preservation under
homomorphism are analogous.
Notice that the classical duality between existential and universal preservation breaks down. While the classical Łoś-Tarski theorem can be equivalently formulated in terms of
existential or universal preservation, these are separate
issues in semiring semantics.
We also note that preservation theorems for semirings
neither entail the classical Boolean variants, nor are they entailed by them. This is because on the semantic side, preservation properties for a semiring
\mathcal{S}
are stronger than their Boolean analogues,
but on the syntactic side also
\mathcal{S}
-equivalence is a stronger notion than classical logical equivalence.

Our main results can be summarised as follows:

•

The preservation theorems for extensions, subinterpretations and homomorphisms are indeed true for
all
lattice semirings

(S,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
. These are semirings induced by
a completely distributive complete lattice
(S,\leq)
(i.e. a partial order closed under suprema and infima in which infima distributive over suprema), with suprema and infima as semiring operations;
they encompass in particular all min-max semirings, such as the fuzzy semiring
\mathbb{F}=([0,1],\max,\min,0,1)
, and the
security semiring
for reasoning about access control
[
12
]
.
The proofs combine adaptations of the classical compactness and amalgamation methods to the specific three-element min-max semiring
\mathcal{S}_{3}
with a reduction method from
[
6
]
,
reducing entailments for arbitrary lattice semirings to
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-entailments.

•

Variants of the existential preservation theorem fail
for many other semirings, including the tropical semiring, the Viterbi semiring, the Łukasiewicz semiring and the natural semirings
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
.

•

Surprisingly, the existential preservation theorem does, however, hold for
finite
interpretations in the Viterbi semiring, the tropical semiring, the Łukasiewicz semiring, and every lattice semiring with at least three elements.
Thus the situation for these semirings is in sharp contrast to the
classical Boolean case, where the Łoś-Tarski theorem holds in general, but not in the finite. To prove this result, we develop a novel technique that allows us to translate evaluation strategies for sentences between models of different finite cardinalities (
\cref
lem-relationship-between-polynomials).

In particular, the latter result raises the intriguing question whether the failure of extension preservation in the finite is perhaps merely a sporadic phenomenon that is unique to the Boolean semiring.
Thus, our study provides the foundation for a new line of future research:
For which other (classes of) semirings does the extension preservation theorem survive in the finite?

Semiring Semantics for First-order Logic

We briefly describe the foundations of semiring semantics for first-order logic and refer to
[
18
]
for further details.

Definition 2.1
.

A
commutative monoid
is an algebraic structure
(M,+,0)
where
+
is a binary commutative and associative operation with
0\in M
as its neutral element.
A
commutative semiring
is a structure
\mathcal{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
with
0\neq 1
such that
(S,+,0)
and
(S,\cdot,1)
are commutative monoids,
\cdot
distributes over
+
, and
0\cdot s=s\cdot 0=0
.

In the following, we assume that all semirings are commutative and
naturally ordered
, that is,
s\leq t\Leftrightarrow\exists r(s+r=t)
defines a partial order. Addition and multiplication are monotone with respect to the natural order.
A semiring is
absorptive
if
s+st=s
for all
s,t\in S
, which is equivalent
to the property that multiplication is decreasing with respect to the natural order. Absorptive semirings are particularly important in semiring semantics, because they preserve, to some extent, the common logical dualities.
Addition in absorptive semirings is
idempotent
(i.e.,
s+s=s
for each
s\in S
), so sums coincide with suprema.
Beyond the Boolean semiring
\mathbb{B}=(\{0,1\},\vee,\wedge,0,1)
, there are
many other absorptive semirings that provide useful information about the evaluation of a formula.

•

A totally ordered set
(S,\leq)
with least element
s
and greatest element
t
induces the
min-max semiring

(S,\max,\min,s,t)
.

•

A more general class is the class of
lattice semirings

(S,\sqcup,\sqcap,s,t)
induced by
a bounded distributive lattice
(S,\leq)
. In fact, every absorptive semiring with idempotent multiplication is a lattice semiring.

•

The
tropical semiring

\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}^{\infty}_{+},\min,+,\infty,0)
is used for cost analysis.

•

To reason about confidence, we may use the
Viterbi semiring

\mathbb{V}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\max,\cdot,0,1)
or the
Łukasiewicz semiring

\mathbb{L}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\max,\odot,0,1)
where
s\odot t\coloneqq\max(s+t-1,0)
.

•

The semiring
\mathbb{D}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\min,\oplus,1,0)
with
s\oplus t\coloneqq\min(s+t,1)
models levels of doubt.

•

The most important non-absorptive semirings are the natural semiring
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,0,1)
and its extension to

\mathbb{N}^{\infty}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

•

Provenance semirings
allow us to track the atomic facts that are responsible for the truth of a formula. In the non-absorptive case, the most general
provenance semirings are
\mathbb{N}[X]
, the semirings of polynomials over a finite set
X
of indeterminates and coefficients from
\mathbb{N}
. In the absorptive setting
the important examples are the semirings
\mathbb{S}(X)
and
\mathbb{S}^{\infty}(X)
of (generalised) absorptive polynomials.
See
[
18
,
24
]
for more details.

To define semiring semantics also for infinite universes, and to extend it to valuations of infinite collections
\Phi
of first-order sentences,
the semirings need to be equipped with infinitary summation and product operators. Algebraic foundations, mathematical properties
and provenance valuations of such
infinitary semirings
have been studied in
[
7
]
. We will not go into details here and tacitly assume that infinitary sums and products are available and have the desired associativity and commutativity properties. In the specific semirings that we consider here, the definitions of these operations are straightforward.

To evaluate first-order formulae in semirings, classical structures are generalised to semiring interpretations that map the atomic facts and their negations to semiring values. While
0
represents falsity, every non-zero element provides an annotation of truth.

Definition 2.2

(Semiring interpretations)
.

Let
A
be a (finite or infinite) universe, let
\tau
be a relational vocabulary, and let
\mathcal{S}
be a semiring. We denote by
\Lit_{A}(\tau)
the set of
\tau
-literals of the form
R\bar{a}
or
\lnot R\bar{a}
that are instantiated with tuples from
A
. Analogously,
\operatorname{Atoms}_{A}(\tau)
denotes the set of
\tau
-atoms
R\bar{a}
over
A
.
An
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
(for
A
and
\tau
) is a function
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
.
We say that
\pi
is

model-defining
if for every literal
L\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
precisely one of the values
\pi(L)
and
\pi(\neg L)
is 0.
Every model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation defines a
\tau
-model
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}
where
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}\models L
if
\pi(L)\neq 0
for
L\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
.

We only consider model-defining interpretations in this paper.
Semiring semantics lifts each
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation for a universe
A
and vocabulary
\tau
to a mapping
\FO_{A}(\tau)\to\mathcal{S}
where
\FO_{A}(\tau)
is the set of first-order formulae instantiated with elements from
A
.
We assume that all formulae are written in negation normal form. This has emerged as the standard approach for dealing with negation in semiring semantics, and is explained in detail in
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Semiring Semantics)
.

For every
\varphi\in\FO_{A}(\tau)
in negation normal form, the semiring valuation
\pi\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is lifted beyond the literals inductively, by

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\psi\vee\vartheta\rrbracket

\displaystyle\coloneqq\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket+\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket,\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\forall x\psi(x)\rrbracket

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{a\in A}\pi\llbracket\psi(a)\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\psi\wedge\vartheta\rrbracket

\displaystyle\coloneqq\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\cdot\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket,\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\exists x\psi(x)\rrbracket

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{a\in A}\pi\llbracket\psi(a)\rrbracket.

Equality atoms are interpreted by their Boolean truth value, that is,
\pi\llbracket a=a\rrbracket\coloneqq 1
and
\pi\llbracket a=b\rrbracket\coloneqq 0
for
a\neq b
(and analogously for inequalities). For finite or infinite sets
\Phi\subseteq\FO_{A}(\tau)
we set
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\coloneqq\prod_{\varphi\in\Phi}\pi\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

Definition 2.4

(Entailment)
.

Let
\Phi,\Psi\subseteq\FO
be sets of first-order sentences and let
\mathcal{S}
be an (absorptive) semiring. We write

[(1)]

1.

\Phi\models_{\mathcal{S}}\Psi
(
\Phi

\mathcal{S}
-entails
\Psi
) if
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\leq\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
for every model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
, and

2.

\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\Psi
(
\Phi
is
\mathcal{S}
-equivalent to
\Psi
) if
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket
for every model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
.

If
\Phi
or
\Psi
is a singleton, we replace them with the corresponding formula in these notations.

Notions of preservation in semiring semantics

We next discuss how semantic preservation properties (upwards, downwards, and under homomorphisms) present themselves in the context of semiring semantics, and why they are implied by
specific syntactic forms.

Definition 2.5

(Homomorphisms and Subinterpretations)
.

•

A
homomorphism
between
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
is a mapping
g\colon A\to B
such that for any
R\bar{a}\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{A}(\tau)
it holds that
\sum_{\bar{a}^{\prime}:g(\bar{a}^{\prime})=g(\bar{a})}\pi_{A}(R\bar{a}^{\prime})\leq\pi_{B}(Rg(\bar{a}))
(note that we only take into account the positive literals here).
Further,
g
is a
strong homomorphism
if
\pi_{A}(L\bar{a})=\pi_{B}(Lg(\bar{a}))
for all
L\bar{a}\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
. If
g
, in addition, is injective, we call it an
embedding
and write
g\colon\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
. Note that for idempotent semirings (where addition is the supremum with respect to the natural order), the inequality above reduces to
\pi_{A}(R\bar{a})\leq\pi_{B}(Rg(\bar{a}))
for each
R\bar{a}\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{A}(\tau)
.

•

\pi_{A}
is a
subinterpretation
of
\pi_{B}
(and
\pi_{B}
is an
extension
of
\pi_{A}
), denoted
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
, if
A\subseteq B
and
\pi_{A}(L\bar{a})=\pi_{B}(L\bar{a})
holds for all literals
L\bar{a}\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
.

•

\pi_{A}
is an
elementary subinterpretation
of
\pi_{B}
(and
\pi_{B}
is an
elementary extension
of
\pi_{A}
), denoted
\pi_{A}\preceq\pi_{B}
, if
A\subseteq B
and
\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi(\bar{a})\rrbracket=\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi(\bar{a})\rrbracket
holds for all
\varphi(\bar{a})\in\FO_{A}(\tau)
.

As an example, consider the
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation
\pi_{A}
with universe
A=\{a\}
and vocabulary
\tau=\{R\}
, where
R
is unary, where we have
\pi_{A}(Ra)=1/2
and
\pi_{A}(\lnot Ra)=0
. Extending
A
by some element
b
and putting
\pi_{B}(Rb)=1/4
while
\pi_{B}(\lnot Rb)=0
yields a
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation
\pi_{B}
, which is an non-elementary extension of
\pi_{A}
, as
\pi_{A}\llbracket\forall xRx\rrbracket=1/2\neq 1/8=\pi_{B}\llbracket\forall xRx\rrbracket
. Further,
g\colon a\mapsto a,b\mapsto a
is a homomorphism from
\pi_{B}
to
\pi_{A}
, but not a strong one.

Classical preservation properties have the form that
sentences which are true in a specific structure remain true
when we move to another structure, related to the first one
as an extension, a substructure, or by a homomorphism.
In the context of semiring interpretations we evaluate
formulae not just by true or false, but in a general semiring. The property that the formula remains true
is replaced by the stronger relationship that the formula evaluates to a greater or equal value (w.r.t. to the natural order on the semiring) when we move from one interpretation to a new one.

Definition 2.6

(Preservation)
.

•

A sentence
\psi\in\FO(\tau)
is
preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{S}
if for all model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi_{A},\pi_{B}
with a homomorphism
g\colon A\to B
, we have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
.

•

A sentence
\varphi\in\FO(\tau)
is
preserved under subinterpretations in
\mathcal{S}
if for all model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
, we have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\geq\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

•

Analogously,
\varphi\in\FO(\tau)
is
preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}
if
\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\leq\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is true for all model-defining
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
.

We observe that the directions from the syntactic form to the semantic preservation properties of the classical preservation theorems
also hold in this context.
Recall that

•

\Sigma_{1}^{+}
denotes the set of
existential-positive
sentences
\exists\bar{x}\varphi(\bar{x})
where
\varphi(\bar{x})
is quantifier- and negation-free,

•

\Sigma_{1}
denotes the set of
existential
sentences
\exists\bar{x}\varphi(\bar{x})
where
\varphi(\bar{x})
is quantifier-free, and

•

\Pi_{1}
is the set of
universal
sentences
\forall\bar{x}\varphi(\bar{x})
where
\varphi(\bar{x})
is quantifier-free.

At this point, it becomes clear why our definition of homomorphism involves summation over the preimages of tuples. This accounts for the possibility that multiple tuples in the left-hand side interpretation
\pi_{A}
are collapsed onto the same tuple in
\pi_{B}
. Our definition ensures that existential-positive sentences are indeed preserved under homomorphisms. For example, consider the sentence
\exists x\exists yExy
. Now take for example
\pi_{A}
to be a bipartite graph with edge weights from
\mathcal{S}
, and
\pi_{B}
an
\mathcal{S}
-weighted graph that is just a single edge.
Since every bipartite graph maps homomorphically to a single edge,
\pi_{A}
should map homomorphically to
\pi_{B}
. But we have to insist that the value of the single edge in
\pi_{B}
is at least the sum of its preimages, or else, we would not have
\pi_{A}\llbracket\exists x\exists yExy\rrbracket\leq\pi_{B}\llbracket\exists x\exists yExy\rrbracket
. Moreover, in defining homomorphisms we use the natural order rather than insisting on equalities, so that edges are preserved while non-edges need not be.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\psi
is a positive Boolean combination of (positive) existential sentences, there is some
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
(
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
) such that
\psi\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\varphi
for all additively idempotent semirings
\mathcal{S}
. If
\psi
is a positive Boolean combination of universal sentences, there is some
\varphi\in\Pi_{1}
such that
\psi\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\varphi
for all lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a semiring and
\exists\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x}),\exists\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\in\FO_{A}(\tau)
where
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
are disjoint tuples of variables.
By (1) commutativity, associativity, and idempotence of addition, and (2) distributivity, we have the logical equivalences

\exists\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x})\circ\exists\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists\bar{x}\exists\bar{y}(\vartheta(\bar{x})\circ\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y}))

for
\circ\in\{\vee,\wedge\}

in any semiring
\mathcal{S}
since for any
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
of universe
A
,

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\exists\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x})\vee\exists\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta(\bar{a})\rrbracket+\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta^{*}(\bar{a})\rrbracket

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\sum_{\bar{b}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta(\bar{a})\rrbracket+\pi\llbracket\vartheta^{*}(\bar{b})\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\exists\bar{x}\exists\bar{y}(\vartheta(\bar{x})\vee\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y}))\rrbracket\text{ and }

\displaystyle\pi\llbracket\exists\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x})\wedge\exists\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta(\bar{a})\rrbracket\cdot\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta^{*}(\bar{a})\rrbracket

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}\sum_{\bar{a}\subseteq A}\sum_{\bar{b}\subseteq A}\pi\llbracket\vartheta(\bar{a})\rrbracket\cdot\pi\llbracket\vartheta^{*}(\bar{b})\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\exists\bar{x}\exists\bar{y}(\vartheta(\bar{x})\wedge\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y}))\rrbracket.

Hence, the first claim follows by induction.
In order to prove the second statement in an analogous way, we do not just need commutativity, associativity, and idempotence of multiplication, but also the dual distributivity law
\prod_{i\in I}s_{i}+\prod_{j\in J}t_{j}=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in J}(s_{i}+t_{j})
, where
(s_{i})_{i\in I},(t_{j})_{j\in J}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, which only holds in lattice semirings.
In any lattice semiring
\mathcal{L}
, however, we have the logical equivalences

\forall\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x})\vee\forall\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\exists\bar{x}\forall\bar{y}(\vartheta(\bar{x})\vee\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y}))\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\forall\bar{x}\vartheta(\bar{x})\wedge\exists\bar{y}\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\forall\bar{x}\forall\bar{y}(\vartheta(\bar{x})\wedge\vartheta^{*}(\bar{y})),

which inductively imply the second claim.
∎

By monotonicity of the semiring operations and the fact that addition is increasing with respect to the natural order, the implication from syntax to semantics follows readily by induction for
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
and
\Sigma_{1}
. We only need to take some care in the case
\Pi_{1}
, where we need multiplication to be decreasing. This is true exactly for absorptive semirings.

Lemma 2.8
.

[(1)]

1.

\Sigma_{1}^{+}
-sentences are preserved under homomorphisms in every semiring.

2.

\Sigma_{1}
-sentences are preserved under extensions in every semiring.

3.

\Pi_{1}
-sentences are preserved under subinterpretations in every absorptive semiring.

3
Failure of Extension Preservation in General Semirings

While the easy direction of the classical preservation theorems from syntax to semantics survives in semiring semantics, there are cases where the converse direction fails.
We present two examples where sentences or sets of sentences
are preserved under extensions but cannot be written without the use of universal quantifiers.
The key observation underlying both counterexamples is that a formula of the form
\forall y\varphi
, where
y
does not appear in
\varphi
, is logically equivalent to
\varphi
in the multiplicative idempotent case, but not in general. For example, this no longer holds when we evaluate in the natural numbers. In order to ensure well-defined semantics also on infinite universes, we expand the natural semiring
(\mathbb{N},+,\,\cdot\,,0,1)
to
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and first consider the semiring
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
.
The universal quantifier in a formula
\forall y\varphi
has the effect of just raising the value of the inner formula
\varphi
to the
n
-th power if
n
is the size of the universe, and otherwise to the infinite power.
However, one can see that expressing powers that grow with the size of the universe is not possible in
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
without the use of a universal quantifier.

Lemma 3.1
.

The sentence
\psi=\exists x\forall yRx
is preserved under extensions in
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
but not
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
-equivalent to a
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence.

Proof.

For
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
we have that

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{A}(Ra)^{|A|}\leq\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{A}(Ra)^{|B|}+\sum_{b\in B\setminus A}\pi_{B}(Rb)^{|B|}=\sum_{b\in B}\pi_{B}(Rb)^{|B|}=\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket

because addition and exponentiation are increasing. Hence,
\psi
is preserved under extensions in
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
. It remains to show that
\psi
is not
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
-equivalent to a
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence. For
n\in\omega
, let
\pi_{n}
be the
\mathbb{N}[\{x\}]
-interpretation with universe
A_{n}\coloneqq\{a_{i}\mid i\in[n]\}
and valuations
\pi_{n}(Ra_{i})=x
and
\pi_{n}(\lnot Ra_{i})=0
for all
a_{i}\in A_{n}
. For
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
, the valuation
\pi_{n}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is a polynomial whose degree is bounded by the length of
\varphi
(and does not depend on
n
). However,
\pi_{n}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=n\cdot x^{n}
. So there must be some
n,m\in\omega
such that for the homomorphism
h_{m}\colon\mathbb{N}[\{x\}]\to\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
induced by
h_{m}(x)=m
we have that
(h_{m}\circ\pi_{n})\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=h_{m}(\pi_{n}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket)>h_{m}(\pi_{n}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)=(h_{m}\circ\pi_{n})\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. Hence,
\psi\not\equiv_{\mathbb{N}^{\infty}}\varphi
.
∎

Our next counterexamples are the Viterbi semiring
\mathbb{V}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\max,\cdot,0,1)
and the semiring of doubt
\mathbb{D}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\min,\oplus,1,0)
with
s\oplus t=\min(s+t,1)
. Of course these results
translate to the tropical semiring
\mathbb{T}
and to the Łukasiewicz semiring
\mathbb{L}
, since
\mathbb{T}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}
, and
\mathbb{L}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{D}
.
Note that, unlike for
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
, multiplication is now decreasing rather than increasing, causing extension preservation of the sentence
\exists x\forall yRx
to fail in these semirings. Consider for example the
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation
\pi_{A}
of universe
\{a\}
with
\pi_{A}(Ra)=1/2
and its extension
\pi_{B}
over the universe
\{a,b\}
with
\pi_{B}(Rb)=1/2
, where we have
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=1/2>1/4=\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
.
On
infinite

\mathbb{V}
-interpretations, however, the universal quantifier
\forall y
raises the value of
Rx
already to its infinite power (i.e. the infimum of the finite powers), and the maximum over these values is preserved under extensions.
Thus, we construct an axiom system which forces its models to be infinite and establish that extension preservation fails for a
set
of sentences, or for semiring interpretations over infinite universes.
The key argument for proving that no set of existential sentences has the same semantics is that infinite suprema and powers do not commute in
\mathbb{V}
. The argument for
\mathbb{D}
is analogous.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\mathcal{S}\in\{\mathbb{V},\mathbb{D}\}
,
the set
\Phi\coloneqq\{\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{n}\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}\neq x_{j}\mid n\in\omega\}\cup\{\exists x\forall yRx\}
is preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}
, but is not
\mathcal{S}
-equivalent to a set of
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences.

Proof.

Consider first the case
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{V}
.
Let
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
be
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations. If
A
is finite, we have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=0\leq\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. So let

(and thus

, as
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
) be infinite. Then

\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\bigsqcup_{a\in A}\prod_{a^{\prime}\in A}\pi_{A}(Ra)=\bigsqcup_{a\in A}\pi_{A}(Ra)^{\infty}=\bigsqcup_{a\in A}\pi_{B}(Ra)^{\infty}\leq\bigsqcup_{b\in B}\pi_{B}(Rb)^{\infty}=\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket.

Hence,
\Phi
is preserved under extensions in
\mathbb{V}
.

Fix some chain
(r_{i})_{i\in\omega}\subseteq[0,1)_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\bigsqcup_{i\in\omega}r_{i}=1
. Let
\pi_{A}
be the
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation over universe
A=\{a_{i}\mid i\in\omega\}
where
\pi_{A}(Ra_{i})=r_{i}
, and let
\pi_{B}
be the extension of
\pi_{A}
with universe

with
\pi_{B}(Rb)=1
. We have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=0\neq 1=\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. We show that
\Phi
cannot be
\mathbb{V}
-equivalent to a set of
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences by proving that for every
\varphi=\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{k}\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\Sigma_{1}
with
\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=1
, we also have
\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=1
. So fix such a
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
. Let

\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and consider a tuple
\bar{b}\in B^{k}
.

Since
\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi(\bar{b})\rrbracket
is a product of the valuations of literals
R(\bar{b})
and
\lnot R(\bar{b})
, we can replace each occurrence of the unique element

in

by some sufficiently large

we get

.
Since
\varepsilon>0
and
\bar{b}\in B^{k}
were arbitrary, this shows that

\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\bigsqcup_{\bar{a}\in A^{k}}\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi(\bar{a})\rrbracket=\bigsqcup_{\bar{b}\in B^{k}}\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi(\bar{b})\rrbracket=1.

This finishes the case that
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{V}
. If
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{D}
, then the reasoning is analogous, with the difference that in
\mathbb{D}
, the natural order is reversed: To be clear, let
\leq_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the standard order on the interval
[0,1]_{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
\leq_{\mathbb{D}}
denote the order on
\mathbb{D}
induced by the semiring addition operation, which is
\min
in this case. Then

if and only if
b\leq_{\mathbb{D}}a
.
Let
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
be
\mathbb{D}
-interpretations. We can again assume that both
A
and
B
are infinite. Let

be the function that maps

to
0
and every other element of
\mathbb{D}
to
1
.
Also in
\mathbb{D}
,

is preserved under extensions:

\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\bigsqcap_{a\in A}\bigoplus_{a^{\prime}\in A}\pi_{A}(Ra)=\bigsqcap_{a\in A}t_{>0}(\pi_{A}(Ra))\leq_{\mathbb{D}}\bigsqcap_{a\in B}t_{>0}(\pi_{B}(Ra)).

The inequality is due to the fact that
B\supseteq A
, so the right-hand side can only be less than the left-hand side with respect to
\leq_{\mathbb{R}}
.
To show that
\Phi
is not
\mathbb{D}
-equivalent to a set of
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences, we use a dual construction to the previous one for
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
. That is, we fix a chain
(r_{i})_{i\in\omega}\subseteq(0,1]_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\bigsqcap_{i\in\omega}r_{i}=0
. Let
\pi_{A}
be the
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation over universe
A=\{a_{i}\mid i\in\omega\}
where
\pi_{A}(Ra_{i})=r_{i}
, and let
\pi_{B}
be the extension of
\pi_{A}
by an additional element
b
with
\pi_{B}(Rb)=0
. We have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1\neq 0=\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
.
It remains to argue that for every
\varphi=\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{k}\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\Sigma_{1}

\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=0
also implies

. This follows analogously as before by considering an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and
\bar{b}\in B^{k}
, and observing that by replacing each occurrence of the special element
b
in
\bar{b}
with a sufficiently small

, we obtain a tuple
\bar{a}
such that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi(\bar{a})\rrbracket\leq_{\mathbb{R}}\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi(\bar{b})\rrbracket+\varepsilon
. Hence, taking the infimum over all
\bar{a}\in A^{k}
, we conclude that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=0
and so,
\Phi
cannot be
\mathbb{D}
-equivalent to a set of
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences.
∎

4
Preservation Theorems in Lattice Semirings

Whether we consider single sentences or sets of them, the extension preservation theorem does not generalise to all semirings.
This raises the question in which semirings it does survive.
We show that both variants hold for the important and fairly general class of
lattice semirings
, induced by a completely distributive complete lattice (i.e. a partial order closed under suprema and infima in which infima distributive over suprema), with supremum and infimum as semiring operations. Also the homomorphism and subinterpretation preservation theorems generalise to the lattice setting.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice semiring.
A sentence
\psi\in\FO
is preserved under homomorphisms/extensions/subinterpretations in
\mathcal{L}
if, and only if, there is some
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
/
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
/
\varphi\in\Pi_{1}
such that
\psi\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\varphi
.

The classical proofs of most model-theoretic preservation theorems
rely on compactness and amalgamation. Compactness has recently been studied in the context of semiring semantics in
[
6
]
and positive results have been established, in particular, for the lattice case.
However, by itself, compactness alone does not suffice to
extend the proofs of preservation theorems to lattice semirings.

The extension preservation theorem under Boolean semantics, for example, is proved by showing that a set
\Phi\subseteq\FO
which is preserved under extensions is logically equivalent to the set
\Phi_{\exists}\coloneqq\{\psi\in\Sigma_{1}\mid\Phi\models\psi\}
of
\Sigma_{1}
-consequences of
\Phi
. By applying compactness, this yields a single equivalent
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence in the case where
\Phi
is a singleton. While it is an easy observation that
\Phi\models\Phi_{\exists}
, a more involved model-theoretic argument is needed to show the converse entailment
\Phi_{\exists}\models\Phi
. One proves that for every model
{\mathfrak{A}}\models\Phi_{\exists}
there is a suitable
{\mathfrak{B}}\models\Phi
that can be
amalgamated
into a structure
\mathfrak{C}\succeq\mathfrak{A}
that is also an extension of
{\mathfrak{B}}
. This yields
\mathfrak{C}\models\Phi
by extension preservation of
\Phi
. Since
\mathfrak{C}
is an elementary extension of
\mathfrak{A}
, this implies
{\mathfrak{A}}\models\Phi
. To ensure that amalgamation is possible,
{\mathfrak{B}}
has to be chosen such that every
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence that holds in
{\mathfrak{B}}
also holds in
{\mathfrak{A}}
, or, in other words, such that every finite substructure of
{\mathfrak{B}}
is a substructure of
{\mathfrak{A}}
, too.

This relationship to shared finite subinterpretations no longer holds if we move from the Boolean semiring to lattices. It even fails for min-max semirings over
4
elements where finite interpretations cannot be axiomatised up to isomorphism
[
16
]
.
The intuitive reason for this is that the semiring elements do not occur in the logic’s syntax.
The
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence
\psi=\exists x(Px\wedge\lnot Qx)
, for example, describes a substructure of size
1
in the Boolean case, while its evaluation in a lattice generally does not reflect the existence of a particular subinterpretation. Since existential quantifiers are evaluated to a supremum, it might not even be possible to attribute the valuation of
\psi
to a single instantiation of
x
.
Even if we fix some instantiation
a
, by commutativity, the evaluation of
Pa\wedge\lnot Qa
does not give insights into the contribution of the single literals.
So, in a sense,
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences seem weaker in the lattice context, and it is a priori unclear whether
\Phi_{\exists}
still entails
\Phi
in the presence of extension preservation.
This mismatch between
\Sigma_{1}
-sentences and the notions of subinterpretations and extensions in semiring semantics is what makes it a challenge to show that the extension preservation theorem still holds. We achieve this by combining arguments used in the classical proof with new methods specifically developed for semiring semantics.

4.1
Proof Methods from Semiring Semantics

The proofs of the three preservation theorems for
all
lattice semirings rely on a reduction to the case where we evaluate in
one fixed
such semiring. Here we make use of the three-element lattice
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, where an element denoted
\varepsilon
is sandwiched in between
0
and
1
, that is,
\mathcal{S}_{3}=(\{0,\varepsilon,1\},\max,\min,0,1)
.

Reduction to
\mathcal{S}_{3}

As
\mathcal{S}_{3}
embeds into any lattice semiring except for the Boolean, it can be thought of as the simplest lattice semiring beyond the Boolean.
As a consequence, both preservation properties and logical equivalences in

are a special case of the same property in any lattice

. In order to also transfer counterexamples to these properties from lattice semirings

back to

6
]

\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. Let
\Phi_{0}\coloneqq\{\varphi\in\Phi\mid h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)=h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)\}\neq\varnothing
. Suppose that for each
\varphi\in\Phi_{0}
there is some
X_{\varphi}
such that
\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\bigsqcap X_{\varphi}
and
h(x)>h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)
for each
x\in X
. Then
\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\bigsqcap\bigcup_{\varphi\in\Phi_{0}}X_{\varphi}\cup\{\pi_{B}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\mid\varphi\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{0}\}
, so
h
could not weakly
\leq
-separate
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
from
\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. Hence, there is some
\varphi\in\Phi
to which we can apply
\cref
thm-weak-fun for
t\coloneqq h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)
and we get
(h\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\leq(h\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\leq h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)
. Overall, we have shown that
(h\circ\pi_{A})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\geq h(\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)>h(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)\geq(h\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
be lattice semirings and
\Phi\subseteq\FO
be a set of sentences.
If
\Phi
is preserved under homomorphisms/extensions/subinterpretations in
\mathcal{L}
, then
\Phi
must also be preserved under homomorphisms/extensions/subinterpretations in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We only treat extension preservation, homomorphism and subinterpretation preservation are proved analogously.
First, we argue that extension preservation in
\mathcal{L}
implies extension preservation in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
must contain at least three elements, there is an embedding
e\colon\mathcal{S}_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
. For
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
, we have
e\circ\pi_{A}\subseteq e\circ\pi_{B}
. By the Fundamental Property (
\cref
fundamental-property), extension preservation in
\mathcal{L}
implies

(e\circ\pi_{A})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=e(\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)\leq e(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)=(e\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket.

Hence,
\Phi
must be preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, too.
Now suppose that
\Phi
was not extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, so that there are
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
-interpretations
\pi_{A}\subseteq\pi_{B}
such that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\not\leq\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. Now extend
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
by an additional element
0^{*}
such that
0^{*}\leq s
for all
s\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Let
\pi_{A}^{*},\pi_{B}^{*}
be
\mathcal{L}^{*}
-interpretations where we set
\pi_{A}^{*}(L)=0^{*}
whenever
\pi_{A}(L)=0
and
\pi_{A}^{*}(L)=\pi_{A}(L)
otherwise.
\pi_{B}^{*}
arises from
\pi_{B}
in the same way. Now let
h^{*}\colon\mathcal{L}^{*}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
map every element to itself except for
0^{*}
, which is mapped to
0
. We have
\pi_{A}=h^{*}\circ\pi_{A}^{*}
and
\pi_{B}=h^{*}\circ\pi_{B}^{*}
, so we cannot have that
\pi_{A}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\leq\pi_{B}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
as this would imply
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=(h^{*}\circ\pi_{A}^{*})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=h^{*}(\pi_{A}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)\leq h^{*}(\pi_{B}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)=(h^{*}\circ\pi_{B}^{*})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. Now
\mathcal{L}^{*}
does not have divisors of
0^{*}
, which means that we can apply
\cref
lem:ex-weakly-sep-horm and obtain a homomorphism
h\colon\mathcal{L}^{*}\to\mathcal{S}_{3}
weakly
\leq
-separating
\pi_{A}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
from
\pi_{B}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. By
\cref
lem:counterexample-pres-SThree,
(h\circ\pi_{A}^{*})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket>(h\circ\pi_{B}^{*})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
, so
\Phi
could not be preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, a contradiction. Hence,
\Phi
must be extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.
∎

Note that this reduction only works for
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, but not for the Boolean semiring itself. While the sentence
\psi=\forall x(Rx\vee\lnot Rx)
, for example, as a tautology, is clearly extension preserved in the classical sense, this no longer holds as soon as the lattice contains a third truth value
0<s<1
. Extending an
\mathcal{L}
-interpretation over a single element
a
whose valuation with respect to
R
is
1
by a second element
b
such that
Rb
is evaluated to
s
decreases the valuation of
\psi
.
Applying the method from
[
6
]
another time yields that not just the preservation properties but also all logical equivalences are invariant under the specific lattice one considers.

Proposition 4.7
.

For all
\Phi,\Psi\subseteq\FO
, the following are equivalent:

[(1)]

1.

\Phi\models_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
for all lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
;

2.

\Phi\models_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
for some lattice semiring
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
;

3.

\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket=1
for all
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations
\pi
with
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
.

Proof.

The implication
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
is clear. We prove
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
and
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, both via contraposition. First consider
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, so let
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
be such that
\Phi\models_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
. We want to prove that
\Phi\models_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\Psi
. Suppose the contrary. Then there was some
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
-interpretation
\pi
such that
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\not\leq\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket
. Transform
\pi
into an
\mathcal{L}^{*}
-interpretation
\pi^{*}
such that
s:=\pi^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\not\leq\pi^{*}\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket=:t
in the same way as in
\cref
prop:preservation-in-all-lattices. Because
\mathcal{L}^{*}
does not have
0
-divisors, we can fix a homomorphism weakly
\leq
-separating
s
from
t
according to
\cref
lem:ex-weakly-sep-horm and obtain
(h\circ\pi)\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket>(h\circ\pi)\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket
, so we would have that
\Phi\not\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Psi
. But
\mathcal{S}_{3}
embeds into
\mathcal{L}
, a contradiction (the argument is the same as in the proof of
\cref
prop:preservation-in-all-lattices).
Now we prove
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
and choose
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{S}_{3}
in (2). We only have to show that
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\varepsilon
implies
\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket\geq\varepsilon
for all
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations
\pi
. To this end, consider the homomorphism
h_{\geq\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{S}_{3}\to\mathcal{S}_{3}
with
h_{\geq\varepsilon}(s)=1
if
s\geq\varepsilon
and
h_{\geq\varepsilon}(0)=0
. By the fundamental property (
\cref
fundamental-property), we have that
(h_{\geq\varepsilon}\circ\pi)\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=h_{\geq\varepsilon}(\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)=1
, implying
(h_{\geq\varepsilon}\circ\pi)\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket=1
by assumption, and hence
\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket\geq\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

For all
\Phi,\Psi\subseteq\FO
, the following are equivalent:

[(1)]

1.

\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
for all lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
;

2.

\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
for some lattice semiring
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
;

3.

\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
if, and only if,
\pi\llbracket\Psi\rrbracket=1
for all
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations
\pi
.

Similar to the preservation properties, observe that there are logical equivalences that hold in the Boolean, not in any other lattice. The reason for this is that some Boolean tautologies can also only evaluate to
1
in a lattice as we interpret (in)equalities by their Boolean truth value, while others may take values different from
1
.
As an example, we have
\exists x(x=x)\not\equiv_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\exists x(Rx\vee\lnot Rx)
.

Combining
\cref
prop:preservation-in-all-lattices and
\cref
cor:equivalence-from-S3-to-any-lattice justifies that is suffices to show that one lattice semiring
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
satisfies one of the preservation theorems in order to prove it for all lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
: If
\psi
has some preservation property w.r.t
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, say, it is extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, then it must also be extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}
, and the
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence
\mathcal{L}
-equivalent to
\psi
must also be
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
-equivalent to
\psi
. We will prove that the extension, subinterpretation, and homomorphism preservation theorems generalise to
\mathcal{S}_{3}
.
An essential part of the proof will be the third statement of
\cref
cor:equivalence-from-S3-to-any-lattice, which intuitively says that the
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-semantics of a sentence is uniquely determined by its
1
-valuations. This is not to be confused with its classical Boolean semantics, because it is still evaluated on
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations that may also annotate literals with
\varepsilon
. However, this insight will yield some relationship between
\Sigma_{1}
and finite subinterpretations. Consider the sentence
\psi=\exists x(Px\wedge\lnot Qx)
again. While a valuation of some arbitrary element
s
does not generally ensure the existence of a particular subinterpretation, a valuation of
1
does: because
1
is maximal in any lattice semiring,
\psi
is evaluated to
1
by a
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation if, and only if, it contains the Boolean substructure classically described by
\psi
.

The semiring setting admits different notions of compactness. Restricting ourselves to the case

4.2
Homomorphism Preservation

The first preservation theorem we prove is for homomorphisms.
We first consider the case
\mathcal{S}_{3}
and generalise it to arbitrary lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}
.
More precisely, we prove that every set of sentences
\Phi
which is preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
is
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-equivalent to some
\Psi\subseteq\Sigma_{1}^{+}
.
As in Boolean semantics, we show that
\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Phi_{\exists^{+}}
, where
\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\coloneqq\{\psi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}\mid\Phi\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\psi\}
.
It holds that
\Phi\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Phi_{\exists^{+}}
because for any model-defining
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation
\pi
,
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
is a lower bound of
\{\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\mid\psi\in\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\}
, which implies
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\leq\bigsqcap\{\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\mid\psi\in\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\}=\pi\llbracket\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\rrbracket
.
It remains to show that if
\Phi
is preserved under homomorphisms, the converse entailment
\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Phi
holds as well. To this end, it suffices to consider valuations of
1
only, as justified by the
fundamental property
, which states that semiring semantics commutes with homomorphisms, and the following lemma.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\pi_{A}
be an
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation such that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=1
for all
\psi\in\Phi_{\exists^{+}}
. Then there is an
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation
\pi_{B}
such that

[(1)]

1.

\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
, and

2.

\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
for all
\psi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
with
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi\coloneqq\{\psi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}\mid\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon\}
and
suppose towards a contradiction that the set
\{(\varphi,\{1\})\mid\varphi\in\Phi\}\cup\{(\psi,\{0,\varepsilon\})\mid\psi\in\Psi\}
was unsatisfiable. By compactness (
\cref
thm:compactnessFiniteAbsorptive), there must already be a finite unsatisfiable subset. Let
\Psi_{0}\subseteq\Psi
be such that
\{(\varphi,{1})\mid\varphi\in\Phi\}\cup\{(\psi,\{0,\varepsilon\})\mid\psi\in\Psi_{0}\}
is unsatisfiable. That means that every model-defining
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation
\pi
with
\pi\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
also satisfies
\pi\llbracket\bigvee\Psi_{0}\rrbracket=1
. By
\cref
lem:entailment-from-S3-to-any-lattice, this implies
\Phi\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\bigvee\Psi_{0}
and since
\bigvee\Psi_{0}\equiv_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\psi
for some
\psi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
(by
\cref
lem:closure-under-and-or),
\bigvee\Psi_{0}
is logically equivalent to a formula in
\Phi_{\exists^{+}}
. By assumption,
\pi_{A}\llbracket\bigvee\Psi_{0}\rrbracket=1
, but we defined
\Psi
such that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
for all
\psi\in\Psi
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.13

(Homomorphism Amalgamation)
.

Let
\pi_{A},\pi_{B}
be
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretations such that
\pi_{B}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
for all
\psi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
with
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
. Then there is some
\pi_{C}\succeq\pi_{A}
and a mapping
f\colon B\to C
such that
\pi_{C}(Lf(\bar{b}))=1
for all
L\bar{b}\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{B}(\tau)
with
\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=1
.

Proof.

Suppose that such a
\pi_{C}
does not exist, i.e., that
\{(\varphi,\{\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\})\mid\varphi\in\FO_{A}(\tau)\}\cup\{(\psi,\{1\})\mid\psi\in\Psi\}
, where
\Psi\coloneqq\{L\bar{b}\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{B}(\tau)\mid\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=1\}
, is unsatisfiable. Let
\Psi_{0}\subseteq\Psi
be finite such that
\{(\varphi,\{\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\})\mid\varphi\in\FO\}\cup\{(\psi,\{1\})\mid\psi\in\Psi_{0}\}
is unsatisfiable; this exists by
\cref
thm:compactnessFiniteAbsorptive. Consider the set
\Psi_{0}^{*}
that is obtained from
\Psi_{0}
by replacing each constant
b_{i}\in B
with a variable
x_{i}
. Since
\Psi_{0}
is finite, only a fixed number
k
of variables is needed.
Suppose that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{k}\bigwedge\Psi_{0}^{*}\rrbracket=1
. Then there would be
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in A
such that
\pi_{A}(L\bar{a})=1
for all
L(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\Psi_{0}^{*}
, and the expansion of
\pi_{A}
where we interpret each constant
b_{i}\in B
occurring in
\Psi_{0}
with
a_{i}
would be a model of
\{(\varphi,\{\pi_{A}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\})\mid\varphi\in\FO\}\cup\{(\psi,\{1\})\mid\psi\in\Psi_{0}\}
.
This is a contradiction, so we must have that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{k}\bigwedge\Psi_{0}^{*}\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
.
By assumption, this implies
\pi_{B}\llbracket\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{k}\bigwedge\Psi_{0}^{*}\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon
, but this contradicts the definition of
\Psi
.
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

If
\Phi
is preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, then
\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\rrbracket=1
, i.e.,
\pi_{A}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=1
for all
\psi\in\Phi_{\exists^{+}}
. By
\cref
lem:entailment-from-S3-to-any-lattice, it suffices to prove that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
. Let
\pi_{B}
be as in
\cref
lem-interpretation-for-amalgamation and
\pi_{C}
be the corresponding amalgamated
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation according to
\cref
homomorphism-amalgamation. We know that
\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
and want to argue that
\pi_{C}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
holds as well. However, there need not be a homomorphism from
\pi_{B}
to
\pi_{C}
(only a mapping that preserves valuations of
1
), which is why we take a detour via another
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation
\pi_{B}^{*}
(over the same universe
B
) that still evaluates
\Phi
to
1
but admits a homomorphism into
\pi_{C}
.

Consider the homomorphism
h_{\geq 1}\colon\mathcal{S}_{3}\to\mathcal{S}_{3}
where
h_{\geq 1}(1)=1
and
h_{\geq 1}(0)=h_{\geq 1}(\varepsilon)=0
. By construction,
\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
, which implies
(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=h_{\geq 1}(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)=1
. Since valuations of
\varepsilon
do not occur in
(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})
, the mapping
f
we get from
\cref
homomorphism-amalgamation must be a homomorphism from
(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})
to
\pi_{C}
. However,
(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})
does not have to be model-defining and we might have that both a literal and its complement are evaluated to
0
. Hence, we construct yet another
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-interpretation
\pi_{B}^{*}
, also on the universe
B
, by inserting the missing valuations (we simply copy them from
\pi_{C}
), which ensures that
f
is still a homomorphism into
\pi_{C}
.

More precisely,
\pi_{B}^{*}
is defined as follows: For
L\bar{b}\in\Lit_{B}(\tau)
we set
\pi_{B}^{*}(L\bar{b})\coloneqq\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})
if
\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=1
or
\pi_{B}(\lnot L\bar{b})=1
.
1
1

1

\lnot L\bar{b}
refers to the dual literal, i.e. we identify
\lnot\lnot L\bar{b}
with
L\bar{b}
Otherwise, we set
\pi_{B}^{*}(L\bar{b})\coloneqq\pi_{C}(Lf(\bar{b}))
. Note that
\pi_{B}^{*}
is model-defining; in the first case this is inherited from
\pi_{B}
, and in the second case this is inherited from
\pi_{C}
. Moreover,
f
is a homomorphism from
\pi_{B}^{*}
to
\pi_{C}
: Consider
L\bar{b}\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{B}(\tau)
. We only need to consider the case where
\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=1
or
\pi_{B}(\neg L\bar{b})=1
; the rest is by definition of
\pi_{B}^{*}
. So suppose that
\pi_{B}^{*}(L\bar{b})=\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=1
. Then
\pi_{C}(Lf(\bar{b}))=1
follows because
f
preserves
1
-valuations of atoms in
\pi_{B}
. If
\pi_{B}(\lnot L\bar{b})=1
, then
\pi_{B}^{*}(L\bar{b})=\pi_{B}(L\bar{b})=0
, and
\pi_{C}(Lf(\bar{b}))
cannot be smaller than this. So
f
is indeed a homomorphism from
\pi_{B}^{*}
to
\pi_{C}
.

Now note that
\pi_{B}^{*}(L\bar{b})\geq(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})(L\bar{b})
for all
L\bar{b}\in\Lit_{B}(\tau)
. By monotonicity of
\max
and
\min
, this inductively yields
\pi_{B}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\geq(h_{\geq 1}\circ\pi_{B})\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=h_{\geq 1}(\pi_{B}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket)=1
. Since
\Phi
is preserved under homomorphisms by assumption, we have that
\pi_{C}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\geq\pi_{B}^{*}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
. With
\pi_{C}\succeq\pi_{A}
, it follows that
\pi_{A}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\pi_{C}\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=1
. Hence, we can overall conclude that
\Phi_{\exists^{+}}\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\Phi
.
∎

Using compactness via entailment, we can extract a single
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence from
\Phi_{\exists}
in the case where
\Phi
is a singleton, and generalise the homomorphism preservation theorem from
\mathcal{S}_{3}
to arbitrary lattice semirings
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice semiring.
If
\Phi\in\FO
is preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{L}
, then
\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\Psi
for some
\Psi\subseteq\Sigma_{1}^{+}
. In the special case where
\Phi=\{\varphi\}
is a singleton, there is a single
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
-sentence
\mathcal{L}
-equivalent to
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\subseteq\FO
be preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{L}
. By
\cref
lem:entailment-from-S3-to-any-lattice,
\Phi
must be preserved under homomorphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
.
By
\cref
thm:homomorphismPreservationMainDirection, we know that

4.3
The Łos-Tarski Theorem

We now move on from homomorphism preservation to preservation under extensions and substructures. That is, we prove a generalisation of the classical Łos-Tarski Theorem for Boolean semirings to lattice semirings. While in the Boolean setting, extension preservation and preservation under substructures are exactly dual to each other (and are thus covered by a single proof), this is no longer the case in semiring semantics. For a sentence preserved under subinterpretations in
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, it is a priori not clear if its negation is preserved under extensions, as in the Boolean case. Therefore, we have to prove both preservation theorems individually.

As before, we first consider the case
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, and later generalise the theorems to arbitrary lattice semirings. For a set of sentences

and
\Phi_{\forall}\coloneqq\{\psi\in\Pi_{1}\mid\Phi\models_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}\psi\}
.

5
Extension Preservation in the Finite

The classical Łos–Tarski theorem is known to fail in finite model theory, when both preservation and equivalence are restricted to finite structures.
However, unlike for other model-theoretic properties, this does not immediately imply the failure of the theorem for other semirings, as sentences that are preserved under extensions in the Boolean sense may not be preserved in other semirings as well.
Therefore, we study the question of whether there are semirings in which the extension preservation theorem remains true when restricted to finite universes.
It turns out that in semiring semantics, the situation is much more diverse than in the Boolean world. In some cases, e.g. if we consider the natural semiring, we can easily adapt the counterexample used to disprove the extension preservation theorem in the general setting.

Theorem 5.1
.

There is a sentence
\psi
which is extension preserved on finite
\mathbb{N}
-interpretations, but, on finite
\mathbb{N}
-interpretations, it is not equivalent to any
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
.

For other semirings, this is different. Despite the failure in the Boolean, the extension preservation theorem holds in the finite for the Viterbi and the Łukasiewicz semiring (and thus the tropical semiring and the semiring of doubt), and every lattice semiring.
In this section, we discuss the key concepts and main proof ideas, and refer to
\cref
sec:extension-preservation-finite-proof and
\cref
sec:FinExtPresLattices for a formal definition of all notions and proofs.

5.1
The Viterbi and Łukasiewicz Semiring

Recall the counterexample from
\cref
sec:counterexamples. It relied on the fact that on infinite
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations, the sentence
\psi=\exists x\forall yRx
is preserved under extensions.
If we restrict ourselves to finite
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations, this is no longer true as the universal quantifier generates a power to the size of the universe.
Consider for example the
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation
\pi_{1}
of universe
\{a\}
with
\pi_{1}(Ra)=1/2
and its extension
\pi_{2}
over the universe
\{a,b\}
with
\pi_{2}(Rb)=1/2
, where we have
\pi_{1}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=1/2>1/4=\pi_{2}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
. The reason why
\psi
is not extension-preserved is that multiplication is strictly decreasing in
\mathbb{V}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

We use this property to show that universal quantifiers always make it impossible for a sentence to be extension-preserved in the finite, except when: Either the universal quantifier is
trivial
and always evaluates to
1
(such as in
\forall x(x=x)
) and we can replace it with
\top
, or it does not actually contribute anything to the semantics of the formulae. As an example for the latter case, consider the sentence
\exists xRx\vee\forall xRx
. While addition is increasing, multiplication is decreasing in
\mathbb{V}
, so we have
\pi\llbracket\exists xRx\rrbracket\geq\pi\llbracket\forall xRx\rrbracket
for all
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations
\pi
, and we can replace the universal subformula with
\bot
.
Based on this idea, we can show that the extension preservation theorem holds not just for
\mathbb{V}
in the finite, but also for
\mathbb{T}\cong\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{L}\cong\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\{\mathbb{V},\mathbb{T},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{D}\}
. A sentence
\psi\in\FO
is preserved under extensions on finite
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations if, and only if, there is some
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
which, on finite
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations, is equivalent to
\psi
.

The implication from syntax to semantics is covered by
\cref
lem:syntax-to-semantics. It remains to prove the converse. Consider the case
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{V}
.
The main steps of the proof are as follows.

[(1)]

1.

Reduce the problem of equivalence to a
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence to sufficiently large
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations.

2.

Track the contribution of subformulae to the semantics of a formula by means of so-called evaluation strategies – these are equivalent to strategies of the Verifier in the classical model checking game for FO.

3.

Show that for extension preserved sentences and large
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations, evaluation strategies that use a subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
are always dominated by ones that avoid universal quantifiers.

4.

Remove all strategies using a subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
and rewrite the formula without universal quantifiers.

Equivalence on large
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations.
Given an extension-preserved sentence
\psi
, our goal is to construct an equivalent existential sentence. The first step is to argue that it suffices to find an existential sentence
\varphi
which is equivalent on
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations that have at least a certain cardinality. By replacing each universal quantifier of
\psi
with an
n

\varphi
on
\mathbb{V}
-interpretations of fixed size
n
. Using extension preservation of
\psi
and the fact that addition is the maximum with respect to the natural order,

\mathbb{T}

\mathbb{V}

Strategies and their valuation.
The semiring semantics of a sentence can be defined via the notion of

. We make this formal later on. Intuitively, an evaluation strategy
\mathcal{T}
for a sentence
\psi
and an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation

is given by specifying for each subformula
\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}
of
\psi
, whether we would like to evaluate
\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
, and for each instantiated subformula
\exists x\varphi(x,\bar{a})

with an element of the universe.
Then the value of the strategy
\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket
is the product over all
\pi\llbracket L\bar{a}\rrbracket
such that

is an instantiated literal in
\psi
that can be reached in the classical FO

. Essentially, these are all literals in subformulae of the disjuncts chosen by
\mathcal{T}
with all variable instantiations consistent with the existential ones chosen by
\mathcal{T}

(Sum-of-Strategies
[

For every semiring
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}

\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\sum\{\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket\mid\mathcal{T}\text{ is an evaluation strategy for $\psi$ and $\pi$}\}

Because addition is the maximum operation in
\mathbb{V}

for
\pi

\psi

if it does not use any subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)

\pi

\pi
and
\psi
.

Existence of existential optimal strategies.
To prove this claim, we consider the contraposition. Starting from a strategy
\mathcal{T}
that is optimal for a
\mathbb{V}
-interpretation
\pi
which uses a subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
, we aim to disprove extension preservation of
\psi
.
Essentially, we make use of the fact that multiplication is strictly decreasing on
\mathbb{V}\setminus\{0,1\}
to construct a strategy
\mathcal{T}^{*}
for a subinterpretation
\pi^{*}
of
\pi
such that
\pi^{*}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket>\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket
. By
\cref

\psi
is not extension-preserved because
\pi^{*}\subseteq\pi
, but
\pi^{*}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket>\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
. Let
A
denote the universe of
\pi
and
A^{*}\subset A
the universe of
\pi^{*}
.
The strategy
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is constructed from
\mathcal{T}
by restricting it to instantiations of

with elements of
A^{*}
, but there are some technical intricacies to this: The strategy
\mathcal{T}
may choose to instantiate existentially quantified variables with elements in
A\setminus A^{*}
, so we have to show that we can find alternative choices in
A^{*}
without changing the valuation of the strategy too much. We refer to this construction of
\mathcal{T}^{*}
from
\mathcal{T}
as

, and this is the key new technique that we develop for this proof.

Omitting universal quantifiers.
The final argument is to show that, given that we now know an optimal existential strategy for
\psi

\pi
, we can syntactically remove universal subformulae from
\psi
. More precisely, we replace every subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
with
\top
if it always evaluates to
1
, and otherwise with
\bot
.

5.2
Lattice Semirings

The central ingredient to the proof of
\cref
thm:viterbiFinExtPres is the existence of existential optimal strategies in the presence of finite extension preservation. Note that this argument really relied on the specific algebraic properties of the Viterbi and Łukasiewicz semiring and does not apply to the Boolean. In fact, it does not hold for any lattice semiring
\mathcal{L}
, in which multiplication is idempotent. It is not just that the existence of any optimal strategy is not guaranteed in the case where
\mathcal{L}
is not linearly ordered, even for min-max semirings it may be that all optimal strategies are non-existential. The sentence
\psi=\forall y\exists zRz\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}\exists zRz
, for example, is finite extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}
. However, it clearly does not admit
any
existential strategy, so, in particular, not an optimal one.

Surprisingly, by combining the techniques from
\cref
sec:lattices and
\cref
section:finiteExtPresIdea, we can prove that the finite extension preservation theorem also holds for every lattice semiring apart from the Boolean.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\not\cong\mathbb{B}
be a lattice semiring.
A sentence
\psi\in\FO
is finite extension preserved in
\mathcal{L}
if, and only if, on finite
\mathcal{L}
-interpretations, it is equivalent to an existential sentence.

To prove
\cref
thm:extension-preservation-lattice, we reuse the reduction method from
\cref
sec:lattices and restrict ourselves to the case where we evaluate in the
fuzzy semiring

\mathbb{F}=([0,1],\max,\min,0,1)
.
The structure of the proof is similar to the one from
\cref
section:finiteExtPresIdea: The first two steps are just the same. Due to its linear order, the notion of optimal strategies also applies to
\mathbb{F}
. The existence of existential optimal strategies in step 3, however, has to be replaced by a weaker statement. Intuitively, the reason why finite extension preservation in
\mathbb{F}
might still hold in the presence of universal quantifiers is that strategies might use subformulae
\forall y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
, however, without actually relying on a literal that contains the universally quantified variable
y
. In the example above, it is easy to rewrite
\psi
as an existential sentence: Because no strategy uses a literal containing
y
, we can simply delete the universal quantifier. A more complicated case occurs when strategies use a literal that contains
y
for some instantiations, but not for all of them, which might happen if we consider
\psi=\forall y(\exists zRz\vee\exists z(Rz\wedge Qy))
, for example.

Therefore, we consider
almost existential
strategies, where whenever a subformula
\forall y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
is used, at least one substrategy for some
\varphi(\bar{a},b)

does not
use any relational literal that contains
b
, and
the main claim we prove is that finite extension preservation in
\mathbb{F}
guarantees the existence of almost existential strategy optimal for sufficiently large
\mathbb{F}
-interpretations.
The proof requires a strategy translation lemma similar to the one for
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{L}
.
Knowing just the existence of almost, but not necessarily fully, existential optimal strategies, we cannot replace all universal subformulae by
\bot
right away as done in the Viterbi and Łukasiewicz case. Instead, in step 4 of the proof, we will first manipulate a given extension preserved sentence by basic logical equivalences to make sure that universal subformulae only occur at places where they do not admit almost existential strategies. This will allow us in the end to argue that such universal subformulae must be redundant and can be removed from the formula without changing its semantics. In the example above, we first transform
\psi={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\forall y(}\exists zRz\vee\exists z(Rz\wedge Qy){\color[rgb]{0,0,1})}
into
\exists zRz\vee{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\forall y}\exists z(Rz\wedge Qy)
, which is
\mathbb{F}
-equivalent by continuity, and then argue that
\psi\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}^{\omega}\exists zRz\vee\bot\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}\exists zRz
as
\forall y\exists z(Rz\wedge Qy))
can never be used in an almost existential strategy.

6
Proof of the Extension Preservation Theorem in the Finite for the Viterbi and Łukasiewicz Semiring

In this section, we provide the proof details of
\cref
thm:viterbiFinExtPres. Recall that we aim to show the following: Given a sentence
\psi\in\FO
that is preserved under extensions on
finite

\mathcal{S}
-interpretations, for
\mathcal{S}\in\{\mathbb{V},\mathbb{T},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{D}\}
, we can eliminate all universal quantifiers in it. That is,
\psi
is equivalent to a
\Sigma_{1}
-sentence on finite
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations.

6.1
Reducing the Problem to Large Universes

First, we want to justify that it suffices to construct a
\Sigma_{1}
-formula that is equivalent to
\psi
on sufficiently large
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations, as we can always hardcode the semantics of universal quantifiers on interpretations of a fixed size.

Definition 6.1
.

For a semiring
\mathcal{S}
and sentences
\varphi,\vartheta
, we write
\varphi\equiv^{n}_{\mathcal{S}}\vartheta
if
\pi\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket
for all
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations with universe of size
n
. Similarly, we write
\varphi\equiv^{\leq n}_{\mathcal{S}}\vartheta
(and
\varphi\equiv^{\geq n}_{\mathcal{S}}\vartheta
) is the above is satisfied by each
finite

\mathcal{S}
-interpretation with universe of size at most (at least)
n
. If
\pi\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket
for all
finite

\mathcal{S}
-interpretations, we write
\varphi\equiv^{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\vartheta
.

For a sentence
\psi\in\FO(\tau)
and
n\geq 1
, let
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a tuple of new variables, not occurring in
\psi
, and let
\psi_{n}\coloneqq\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{n}(\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}\neq x_{j}\wedge\psi_{n}^{*}(\bar{x}))
where
\psi_{n}^{*}(\bar{x})
is defined inductively by replacing each subformula
\exists y\varphi
by
\bigvee_{i\in[n]}(\varphi[y/x_{i}])_{n}^{*}(\bar{x})

and each subformula
\forall y\varphi
by
\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}(\varphi[y/x_{i}])_{n}^{*}(\bar{x})
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a semiring,
\pi
be an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation and
\psi\in\FO
. Then
\pi\llbracket\psi_{n}\rrbracket=\sum\{\pi^{*}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\mid\pi^{*}\subseteq\pi\text{ of size }n\}
. In particular,
\psi\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\psi_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation and
\bar{a}
be a tuple of distinct elements of its universe. By induction, it follows
that
\pi\llbracket\psi^{*}_{n}(\bar{a})\rrbracket=\pi^{*}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
where
\pi^{*}
is the subinterpretation of
\pi
which is induced by
\bar{a}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be additively idempotent and
\psi\in\FO(\tau)
be preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}
. Then
\psi\equiv^{\leq n}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}\psi_{i}
.

Proof.

We know that in additively idempotent semirings, addition is the same as supremum.
Let
\pi
be a
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation whose universe has size at most
n
. By
\cref
lem-subint-size-n, we have

\pi\llbracket\bigvee_{i\leq n}\psi_{i}\rrbracket=\bigsqcup\{\pi^{*}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\mid\pi^{*}\subseteq\pi\}\overset{*}{=}\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket,

where
(*)
is due to preservation under extensions.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be additively idempotent.
If
\psi
is preserved under extensions in
\mathcal{S}
and there is some
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
and
n\in\omega
such that
\varphi\equiv^{\geq n}_{\mathcal{S}}\psi
, then there must be some
\varphi^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}
such that
\varphi^{*}\equiv^{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\psi
.

Proof.

We claim that
\psi\equiv\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{n}(\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}\neq x_{j}\wedge\varphi)\vee\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}\psi_{i}=:\vartheta
. Let
\pi
be an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation of cardinality less than
n
. Then
\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}\psi_{i}\rrbracket\overset{*}{=}\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
, where
(*)
is by
\cref
lem-subint-size-at-most-n. If
\pi
is of size at least
n
, then

\pi\llbracket\vartheta\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\sqcup\pi\llbracket\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}\psi_{i}\rrbracket\overset{(1)}{=}\bigsqcup\{\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\}\cup\{\pi^{*}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\mid\pi^{*}\subseteq\pi\text{ of size at most }n\}\overset{(2)}{=}\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket,

where
(1)
is by
\cref
lem-subint-size-n and
(2)
follows from preservation under extensions.
Since
\vartheta
does not contain a universal quantifier, we can apply
\cref
lem:closure-under-and-or and obtain
\vartheta\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}^{\omega}\varphi^{*}
for some
\varphi^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}
.
∎

Hence, our goal is to construct for a given sentence
\psi
which is preserved under extensions on finite
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations some
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
such that
\psi\equiv^{\geq n}_{\mathcal{S}}\varphi
for some
n\in\omega
.

6.2
Tracking Redundancies via Evaluation Strategies

In order to avoid having to consider different equality types of instantiations, we use the variant
\FO^{\neq}
of
\FO
where quantifiers only range over instantiations that do not occur in the instantiations of the free variables. More precisely, formulae from
\FO^{\neq}
only contain relational atoms, but no equality atoms, and rather than the usual quantifiers, they may contain quantifiers
\exists^{\neq},\forall^{\neq}
with the semantics

\pi\llbracket\exists^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)\rrbracket\coloneqq\sum_{b\in A\setminus\bar{a}}\pi\llbracket\varphi(\bar{a},b)\rrbracket\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\pi\llbracket\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)\rrbracket\coloneqq\prod_{b\in A\setminus\bar{a}}\pi\llbracket\varphi(\bar{a},b)\rrbracket.

This way, we only have to consider tuples of instantiations that are pairwise distinct. Throughout this section we implicitly assume this when we write
\bar{a}\subseteq A
. By
\Sigma_{1}^{\neq}
we denote the existential fragment of
\FO^{\neq}
. According to the following lemma,
\FO
and
\FO^{\neq}
(as well as
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{1}^{\neq}
) have the same expressive power, which is why we can assume that all formulae considered in the following are in
\FO^{\neq}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For every
\varphi\in\FO
(
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
) there is some
\varphi^{*}\in\FO^{\neq}
(
\varphi^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}^{\neq}
) such that
\varphi\equiv\varphi^{*}
and vice versa.

Proof.

Inductively translate
\varphi\in\FO
into
\varphi^{*}\in\FO^{\neq}
as follows.

•

(x_{i}=x_{i})^{*}\coloneqq\top
and
(x_{i}=x_{j})\coloneqq\bot
for
i\neq j
. Analogously for inequalities.

•

(L\bar{x})^{*}\coloneqq L\bar{x}
for relational atoms
L\bar{x}
.

•

(\psi\circ\vartheta)^{*}\coloneqq\psi^{*}\circ\vartheta^{*}
for
\circ\in\{\vee,\wedge\}
.

•

(\exists y\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y))^{*}\coloneqq\bigvee_{i\in[n]}\psi^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{i})\vee\exists^{\neq}y\psi^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y)
. Analogously for universal quantifiers.

Since neither of the translation steps introduces new universal quantifiers, we have
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}
if, and only if,
\varphi^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}^{\neq}
.
Conversely, we can translate
\varphi\in\FO^{\neq}
into
\varphi^{*}\in\FO
according to:

•

(L\bar{x})^{*}\coloneqq L\bar{x}
for relational atoms
L\bar{x}
.

•

(\psi\circ\vartheta)^{*}\coloneqq\psi^{*}\circ\vartheta^{*}
for
\circ\in\{\vee,\wedge\}
.

•

(\exists^{\neq}y\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y))^{*}\coloneqq\exists y(\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}y\neq x_{i}\wedge\psi^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y))
. Analogously for universal quantifiers.

As before we have
\varphi\in\Sigma_{1}^{\neq}
if, and only if,
\varphi^{*}\in\Sigma_{1}
.
∎

To formally define what it means for a subformula
\varphi(\bar{x})
not to contribute to the semantics of a sentence
\psi
, we make use of evaluation strategies in the model checking game and track whether or not they use
\varphi(\bar{x})
.
The
model checking game graph for
\psi
and
n\geq\qr(\psi)
is a labelled tree
C_{n}(\psi)=(V,E,\lambda)
where each node
v
is labelled by a subformula
\lambda(v)=\varphi(\bar{a})
of
\psi
which is instantiated with elements from
[n]
. We inductively define
C_{n}(\varphi(\bar{a}))
for
\varphi(\bar{a})\in\FO^{\neq}_{[n]}(\tau)
and
n\geq\qr(\psi)
in negation normal form as follows. In any case, the root of
C_{n}(\varphi(\bar{a}))
is labelled
\varphi(\bar{a})
. If
\varphi(\bar{a})
is a literal or a Boolean constant,
C_{n}(\varphi(\bar{a}))
consists of a single node. For
\varphi(\bar{a})=\varphi_{0}(\bar{a})\star\varphi_{1}(\bar{a})
where
\star\in\{\vee,\wedge\}
, in
C_{n}(\varphi(\bar{a}))
we append
C_{n}(\varphi_{i}(\bar{a}))
for
i\in\{0,1\}
as subtrees to the root. Finally, if
\varphi(\bar{a})
has the form
Qx\vartheta(\bar{a},x)
where
Q\in\{\exists,\forall\}
we now append the trees
C_{n}(\vartheta(\bar{a},b))
for each
b\in[n]\setminus\bar{a}
to the root.

Definition 6.6
.

An
evaluation strategy
(or just
strategy
)

\mathcal{T}=(V,E,\lambda)
for
\psi
and
n\geq\qr(\psi)
is a subtree of
C_{n}(\psi)
where each node
labelled with
\varphi_{0}(\bar{a})\vee\varphi_{1}(\bar{a})
or
\exists x\vartheta(\bar{a},x)
has exactly one successor and each node labelled with
\varphi_{0}(\bar{a})\wedge\varphi_{1}(\bar{a})
or
\forall x\vartheta(\bar{a},x)
contains all successors in
C_{n}(\psi)
. We write
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)
and define the valuation
\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket
in an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
of universe
[n]
as the products of all valuations
\pi(L(\bar{a}))
of the leaves in
\mathcal{T}
.

In every linearly ordered semiring
\mathcal{S}
such as
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{L}
, a
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation induces a preorder
\preceq_{\pi}
on
C_{n}(\psi)
, where we put
\mathcal{T}\preceq_{\pi}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
whenever
\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket\leq\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\rrbracket
. Such a preorder corresponds to a linear order of equivalence classes.
According to the following theorem, the evaluation of a formula in a semiring where addition corresponds to the maximum always coincides with the valuation of any maximal strategy with respect to this order. We refer to such strategies as
optimal
.

Theorem 6.7

(Sum-of-Strategies
[
18
]
)
.

For every
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
with universe
[n]
and every instantiated formula
\psi(\bar{a})\in\FO^{\neq}_{[n]}
it holds that

\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\sum\{\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket\mid\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)\}
.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)
be a strategy. We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
optimal
for an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
if
\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket
.
A subformula
\varphi(\bar{x})
of
\psi
is
used
in
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{T}
contains a node
v
where
\lambda(v)=\varphi(\bar{a})
for some
\bar{a}\subseteq[n]
(recall that the entries of such a tuple must be pairwise distinct). If
\mathcal{T}
does not use any subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
, it is referred to as
existential
. Otherwise, we call it
non-existential
.

Our goal is to prove that, in the presence of finite extension preservation, non-existential strategies will always be dominated by existential ones.
The only exception we will have to exclude is when a subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
is
trivial
in the following sense.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a semiring and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
is
trivial for
n
(w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}
) if for all
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi
of universe
A
with exactly
n
elements and all
\bar{a}\subseteq A
, it holds that
\pi\llbracket\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)\rrbracket=1
.
We say that
\varphi(\bar{x})
is
trivial
(w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}
) if there is some
n_{0}
such that
\varphi(\bar{x})
is trivial for each
n\geq n_{0}
.

For example, the sentence
\forall^{\neq}x\exists^{\neq}y(\top\vee Rx)\equiv^{\geq 2}_{\mathcal{S}}\top
is trivial, but not trivial for
n=1
, while
\exists^{\neq}x(Rx\vee\lnot Rx)
is not trivial for any
n
.
By definition, trivial subformulae
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
of a sentence
\psi
can be replaced by
\top
without changing its semantics on sufficiently large
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations.
Now the idea is that if we replace a subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
in
\psi
with
\bot
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the strategies for
\psi
that do not use
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
and the
\bot
-free strategies of the resulting sentence
\vartheta
.
So if we are sure that for any
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation, there is at least one optimal strategy which does not use
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
, this replacement will not change the semantics of
\psi
.
Hence, the main claim we will prove in
\cref
thm:only-redundant-subform is that once occurrences of trivial subformulae
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
are eliminated, all remaining non-trivial subformulae
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
no longer contribute anything to the semantics of a finite extension preserved sentence.
Formally, we say such universal subformulae are
redundant
:

Definition 6.10

(Redundancy)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a semiring and
\psi\in\FO^{\neq}
be a sentence. We say that
\forall
is redundant in
\psi
(w.r.t
\mathcal{S}
) if there is some
n_{0}
such that for every
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
of size
n\geq n_{0}
, there is an existential strategy that is optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
. An
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi

disproves redundancy of
\forall
in
\psi
(or
\pi
is a
counterexample to redundancy of
\forall
in
\psi
) if every strategy optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
is non-existential.

As an example,
\forall
is redundant in both
\forall^{\neq}xRx\vee\exists^{\neq}xRx
and
\forall^{\neq}xRx\vee\exists^{\neq}x\exists^{\neq}yRx
, while it is not in
\forall^{\neq}xRx\wedge\exists^{\neq}xRx
.
Note that if
\forall
is not redundant in
\psi
, then there are arbitrarily large counterexamples to redundancies of
\forall
in
\psi
. We omit the semiring
\mathcal{S}
in these definitions whenever it is clear from the context.

When we want to reason about trivial formulae and redundancies, we do not need to track the semantics of a formula on just one fixed
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation, but argue about all of them at the same time. This is why we evaluate strategies not just on
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations but make use of interpretations over semirings of polynomials.
The variables we use to annotate the literals serve as placeholders and due to universality, valuations in such polynomial semirings represent the valuations in all
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations.
Because we only consider absorptive semirings in this section, we make use of absorptive polynomials for this purpose, and to reflect the fact that for complementary literals, one of them has value
0
in model-defining interpretations, we take a quotient based on a duality between variables. See
[
18
]
for more details.

Definition 6.11
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}^{+}\cup X_{n}^{-}
where
X_{n}^{+}\coloneqq\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n]}(\tau)\}
and
X_{n}^{-}\coloneqq\{x_{\lnot\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n]}(\tau)\}
.
The free absorptive semiring
\mathbb{S}(X_{n})
consists of
0,1
, and all antichains of monomials with respect to the absorption
order
\succeq
, which is also the natural order of
\mathbb{S}(X_{n})
. A monomial
m_{1}

absorbs

m_{2}
, denoted
m_{1}\succeq m_{2}
, if it has smaller exponents, i.e.
m_{2}=m\cdot m_{1}
for some monomial
m
.
The semiring
\mathbb{S}(X_{n}^{+},X_{n}^{-})
is the quotient of
\mathbb{S}(X_{n}^{+}\cup X_{n}^{-})
based on the congruence generated by
x_{\alpha}\cdot x_{\lnot\alpha}=0
for
\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n]}
.

We say that a variable assignment
f\colon X_{n}\to\mathcal{S}
is
consistent
if
f(x_{\alpha})\cdot f(x_{\lnot\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n]}(\tau)
. It is
model-defining
if for all
\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n]}
we have
f(x)=0
for exactly one
x\in\{x_{\alpha},x_{\lnot\alpha}\}
. Note that model-defining assignments are in particular consistent, while the converse only holds if
\mathcal{S}
does not contain divisors of
0
, so fails for the semirings
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{D}
, for example.

Proposition 6.12

(Universal Property
[
18
]
)
.

For any absorptive semiring
\mathcal{S}
and consistent
f\colon X_{n}\to\mathcal{S}
there exists a unique semiring homomorphism
h\colon\mathbb{S}(X_{n}^{+},X_{n}^{-})\to\mathcal{S}
such that
h(x)=f(x)
for all
x\in X_{n}
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the
\mathbb{S}(X_{n}^{+},X_{n}^{-})
-interpretation
\pi_{n}
over universe
[n]
where
\pi_{n}(\alpha)=x_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\operatorname{Lit}_{[n]}(\tau)
. Each such
\pi_{n}
evaluates a strategy
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)
to a monomial that describes which atomic facts are used in
\mathcal{T}
and how often.
If we now want to argue that for
all

\mathcal{S}
-interpretations of universe
[n]
a strategy
\mathcal{T}
yields a valuation which is at most the valuation of a strategy
\mathcal{T}^{*}
, we can compare the monomials
m\coloneqq\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket
and
m^{*}\coloneqq\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket
and prove that
m\preceq m^{*}
(recall that this means that each variable’s exponent in
m^{*}
is always at most its exponent in
m
).
This is because every
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
over universe
[n]
corresponds to a model-defining variable assignment
f\colon X_{n}\to\mathcal{S}
and hence a homomorphism
h\colon\mathbb{S}(X_{n}^{+},X_{n}^{-})\to\mathcal{S}
. By the fundamental property (
\cref
fundamental-property) we have
(h\circ\pi_{n})\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket=h(m)\leq h(m^{*})=(h\circ\pi_{n})\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket
as homomorphisms preserve the natural order. Because multiplication is strictly decreasing on
\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0,1\}
, we can argue analogously that
\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket<\pi\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket
for each
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
with
1\not\in\img(\pi)
whenever
m\prec m^{*}
.

6.3
Strategy Translation

Recall that our goal is to show that
\forall
must be redundant in every finite extension preserved sentence where
\forall
only occurs non-trivially. To prove this claim, we suppose that
\forall
was not redundant in a finite extension preserved sentence
\psi
. Starting from a non-existential strategy
\mathcal{T}
optimal for an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
,
we aim to infer a contradiction by disproving finite extension preservation of
\psi
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a strategy optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
and
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
be a subformula used in
\mathcal{T}
.
Now the idea is to remove some element
a
from the universe of
\pi
and construct a strategy

6.4
Excluding
1
-valuations

The final argument that is still missing to invoke
\cref
lem:counterexample-if-irredundant and finally infer that finite extension preservation implies redundancy of
\forall
is that we can make sure to not only remove substrategies that are evaluated to
1
during the strategy translation. As mentioned previously, we can assume without loss of generality that every subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
in
\psi
is non-trivial. However, recall that this only means that
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)

can
take a valuation different from
1
on arbitrarily large
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations. A priori, it is neither clear whether this can happen for
all
universe sizes, in particular those for which there is a counterexample to redundancy, nor does this immediately ensure that
no

\mathcal{S}
-interpretation of a particular size evaluates
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
itself or a strategy for
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
for
any
instantiation
\bar{a}
to
1
. We justify in
\cref
lem:non-trivial-eventually-never-1 that for
\mathcal{S}
-interpretations
\pi
that have at least a certain cardinality, strategies for non-trivial formulae can actually only be evaluated to
1
if
1
occurs as the valuation of a literal in
\pi
. In
\cref
lem:one-does-not-occur-in-pi, we then argue that we can translate every
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation into another one over the same universe, in which
1
-valuations no longer occur, while keeping at least one optimal strategy.

In order to prove
\cref
lem:non-trivial-eventually-never-1, we reuse the strategy translation lemma and apply the following auxiliary lemma, which allows us to express triviality in terms of the polynomial interpretations
\pi_{n}
and to prove that triviality never depends on a particular instantiation of the free variables.

Proof of the Extension Preservation Theorem in the Finite for Lattice Semirings

We proceed similarly as in

Unlike in
\cref

\mathcal{S}_{3}

\mathbb{F}=([0,1],\max,\min,0,1)

So our first goal is to prove the following.

Theorem 7.1
.

A sentence
\psi\in\FO^{\neq}
is preserved under extensions of finite
\mathbb{F}
-interpretations if, and only if, on finite
\mathbb{F}
-interpretations, it is equivalent to an existential sentence.

Its linear order allows us to apply the proof framework based optimal strategies from
\cref
sec:extension-preservation-finite-proof to
\mathbb{F}

Recall from
\cref

\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{L}
sentences such as

may be extension preserved in
\mathbb{F}
although they do not admit any existential strategy.
Hence, instead of distinguishing purely existential and non-existential strategies as in
\cref
sec:extension-preservation-finite-proof, we will now consider

[(1)]

1.

strategies where whenever a subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)

2.

The crucial insight will be that, in case (1), we can assume that the optimal strategy uses the universally quantified variable only in a constant number of substrategies of

This is why we will refer to strategies from (1) as

7.1

We fix some notation first. For a labelled tree
\mathcal{T}=(V,E,\lambda)

\mathcal{T}

For a strategy

\mathcal{T}
, as some instantiations might not actually appear inside a literal (recall
\forall^{\neq}y\exists^{\neq}xRx

Finally, for

[(1)]

The goal of this section is to prove that for extension preserved sentences and (sufficiently large) finite
\mathbb{F}
-interpretation there will always be an almost existential optimal strategy. The structure of the proof will roughly be as follows. Suppose there is a strategy relying on
\forall
that

\pi^{*}

The most technical part is the strategy translation. The strategy

\pi

\mathcal{T}^{*}

\pi^{*}
that contains fewer witnesses. More precisely, the number of witnesses can be bounded by a constant and will no longer depend on the size of the universe. This allows us to find a suitable substitute for

\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}_{m}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)
, where the latter inclusion is by induction.
It remains to verify (1)–(3). Fix some
w\in vE_{m}^{\prime}
.

[(1)]

1.

We can upper-bound
|A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(w))|
by the number of inner nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(w)
. If we truncate the tree
\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(w)
at all universal nodes, we get a tree of depth at most
|\psi|
which has branching degree at most
2

2^{|\psi|}
inner nodes. For each leaf
t
of this truncated tree, we may have a substrategy
\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(t)
of a formula
\forall^{\neq}z\vartheta(\bar{a},z)

c_{m}+1

c_{m}
elements as instantiations of existential quantifiers. Hence,
|A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(w))|<2^{|\psi|}+2^{|\psi|}\cdot(c_{m}+1)\cdot c_{m}\leq c_{m+1}
.

2.

Similarly, the number of leaves in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}(w)
provides an upper bound on

. The tree obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}(w)

By construction, only the subtrees
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}(w_{i_{j}})

.

Proceed analogously with all other

with

and
\qr_{\forall}(\varphi(\bar{a},y))=m+1
to finally obtain

Lemma 7.5

(Translation of almost existential strategies)
.

Let
\psi\in\FO^{\neq}
and
r\coloneqq\qr(\psi)
.
There is some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all almost existential strategy
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n+r}(\psi)
with
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)\subseteq X_{n}
, there is some almost existential
\mathcal{T}^{*}\in C_{n}(\psi)
such that
\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)
.

Proof.

Apply
\cref
lem:translation-almost-existential-1 to the sentence
\forall^{\neq}y\psi
and choose
n_{0}\coloneqq c_{r+1}+r
. Now let
n\geq n_{0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\psi}\in C_{n+r}(\psi)
be almost existential with
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{\psi}\rrbracket)\subseteq X_{n}
, and
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n+r}(\forall^{\neq}y\psi)
coincide with
\mathcal{T}^{\psi}
for every instantiation of
y
. Transform
\mathcal{T}
into
\mathcal{T}_{r+1}
according to
\cref
lem:translation-almost-existential-1. We have
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}_{r+1}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)=\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{\psi}\rrbracket)
, so it suffices to provide
\mathcal{T}^{*}\in C_{n}(\psi)
with
\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}_{r+1}\rrbracket)
. We have
|A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{r+1})|\leq c_{r+1}\leq n-r
, so there must be
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[n+r]
such that
i_{j}\not\in A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{r+1})
for
j\leq r
.
Now fix some substrategy
\mathcal{T}_{r+1}^{\psi}
for
\psi
(and an arbitrary instantiation of
y
) and consider the strategy
\mathcal{T}^{*}
, which arises from
\mathcal{T}_{r+1}^{\psi}[n+1\leftrightarrow i_{1},\dots,n+r\leftrightarrow i_{r}]

by deleting all subtrees
\mathcal{T}(w_{j})
where
w_{j}
is successor of a universal node and
\lambda_{r}(w_{j})=\varphi(\bar{a},n+j)
for some
\varphi(\bar{x},y)\in\FO^{\neq}
and
\bar{a}\subseteq[n+r]
. Because
i_{j}\not\in A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{r+1})
,
n+j
cannot occur in
\mathcal{T}^{*}
anymore, so indeed,
\mathcal{T}^{*}\in C_{n}(\psi)
. Further,
\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)
because
n+j
did not occur in any leaf of
\mathcal{T}_{r+1}
as
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}_{r+1}\rrbracket)\subseteq X_{n}
, and since
i_{j}\not\in A_{\exists}(\mathcal{T}_{r+1})
,
i_{j}
can only occur in a leaf of
\mathcal{T}_{r+1}
which is on a path containing
v,w
with
\lambda_{r}(v)=\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{a},y)
and
\lambda_{r}(w)=\varphi(\bar{a},i_{j})
, so this leaf cannot occur in
\mathcal{T}^{*}
. Hence, neither
n+j
nor
i_{j}
occur in a leaf of
\mathcal{T}^{*}
, which ensures
\supp(\pi_{n}\llbracket\mathcal{T}^{*}\rrbracket)\subseteq\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)
.
∎

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\psi\in\FO^{\neq}
be finite extension preserved in
\mathbb{F}
such that
\psi\not\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}^{\geq m}\bot
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
\mathbb{F}
-interpretations
\pi
with universe
[n]
, there is an almost existential strategy optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
.

Proof.

Because
\psi
is finite extension preserved in
\mathbb{F}
and
\psi\not\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}^{\geq m}\bot
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there must be some
m_{0}
such that
\psi\not\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}\bot
for all
m\geq m_{0}
.
Let
n_{0}\geq m_{0}
be large enough to apply
\cref
lem:translation-almost-existential.

Now suppose that there was an
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation
\pi
with universe
[n]
where
n\geq n_{0}
such that all strategies optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
rely on
\forall
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq 0
: In the case
\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=0
every
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)
is optimal for
\pi
and
\psi
, so there cannot be an almost existential
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n}(\psi)
at all and we can replace
\pi
with some
\mathcal{S}
-interpretation that does not evaluate
\psi
to
0
. This is possible as we have chosen
n
to be large enough to ensure
\psi\not\equiv_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}\bot
.

Hence, we can fix some
0<\varepsilon<h_{n}(\pi_{n}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket)
and define an extension
\pi_{\text{ext}}
of
\pi
, which has universe
[n+r]
. We set
\pi_{\text{ext}}(\alpha)=\varepsilon
and
\pi_{\text{ext}}(\alpha)=0
for each
\alpha\in\operatorname{Atoms}_{[n+r]\setminus[n]}(\tau)
.
Now let
\mathcal{T}\in C_{n+r}(\psi)
be optimal for
\pi_{\text{ext}}
.
If
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)\not\subseteq X_{n}
, we would have a contradiction to finite extension preservation of
\psi
as
\pi_{\text{ext}}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\pi_{\text{ext}}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket\leq\varepsilon<\pi\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
.
So suppose that
\supp(\pi_{n+r}\llbracket\mathcal{T}\rrbracket)\subseteq X_{n}
. In particular, this excludes the case that
\mathcal{T}
relies on
\forall
because for every node
v
of
\mathcal{T}
with

such that

7.2
Rewriting the Formula

We want to apply
\cref
cor:strategies-relying-on-forall-dont-matter to eliminate subformulae
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
of
\psi
and eventually obtain an
\mathbb{F}
-equivalent existential sentence. Unlike in the Viterbi case, where we could prove the existence of existential optimal strategies, causing all universal subformulae to be redundant, an additional argument is needed in the case where all optimal strategies are only almost existential and still use a subformula
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)

\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)
first, and by translating
\forall^{\neq}y\varphi(\bar{x},y)

\mathbb{F}
-equivalent formula where the universal quantifier only admits strategies relying on
\forall

Lemma 7.7
.

is

We only consider the case

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a lattice semiring.
Every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})\in\FO^{\neq}
that does not contain a universal quantifier is
\mathcal{S}
-equivalent to a formula
\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\bigvee_{i}\vartheta_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
, where each
\vartheta_{i}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
is a conjunction of literals.

Proof.

Using distributivity, the claim readily follows by structural induction on
\varphi(\bar{x})
. Nothing remains to be shown in the cases where
\varphi(\bar{x})
is a literal (we permit the quantifier prefix to be empty) or of the form
\exists^{\neq}y\chi(\bar{x},y)
.

So let
\varphi(\bar{x})=(\psi\circ\vartheta)(\bar{x})
where
\circ\in\{\vee,\wedge\}
. By IH, there are formulae
\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\bigvee_{i}\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
and
\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigvee_{j}\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{u})
such that
\psi(\bar{x})\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\bigvee_{i}\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
and
\vartheta(\bar{x})\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigvee_{j}\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{u})
.
Rename variables if needed to make sure that the entries of
\bar{z}
and
\bar{u}
are pairwise distinct.
In the case
\circ=\vee
, we get
\varphi(\bar{x})\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigl(\bigvee_{i}\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\vee\bigvee\{\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\mid\bar{v}\subseteq\bar{u}\cup\bar{z}\text{ pairwise distinct, }j\}\bigr)
. Note that because we now nest the existential quantifiers, we have to take into account instantiations of
\bar{z}
for
\vartheta_{j}^{*}
too.
If
\circ=\wedge
, distributivity yields

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigl(\bigvee_{i}\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\wedge\bigvee\{\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\mid\bar{v}\subseteq\bar{u}\cup\bar{z}\text{ pairwise distinct, }j\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigl(\bigvee\{(\bigvee_{i}\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z}))\wedge\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\mid\bar{v}\subseteq\bar{u}\cup\bar{z}\text{ pairwise distinct, }j\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{S}}\exists^{\neq}\bar{z}\exists^{\neq}\bar{u}\bigl(\bigvee\{\psi_{i}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\wedge\vartheta_{j}^{*}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\mid\bar{v}\subseteq\bar{u}\cup\bar{z}\text{ pairwise distinct, }j,i\}\bigr).\qed

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we have provided a framework for studying the interplay between syntactic forms and semantic properties of first-order formulae evaluated on semirings. As
semiring semantics refines the classical Boolean semantics,
it provides more fine-grained model-theoretic notions. Preservation properties, for example, naturally generalise to this context based on natural order of the semirings.
Relating them to specific syntactic forms allow us to answer questions such as: How can we syntactically characterise those queries for which we only gain confidence, or reduce costs,
if we extend our data?

As a first result, we have seen that preservation theorems do not always generalise to the semiring setting as, for instance, the extension preservation theorem fails for the tropical semiring, the Viterbi semiring, the Łukasiewicz semiring, and the natural semirings
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}^{\infty}
.
However, on the fairly broad class of lattice semirings we manage to recover the classical preservation theorems for homomorphisms, extensions and subinterpretations.
The proof combines adaptations of classical methods from model theory, based on compactness and amalgamation, with a reduction technique specifically designed for semiring semantics. Results with a somewhat similar flavour have been
studied in the context of certain many-valued logics
[
3
,
9
]
. However, our framework
differs from these studies in multiple ways: Our notions of preservation and logical equivalence are much more fine-grained
as they do not just distinguish valuations of
1
from those taking different values. Moreover, in our setting, elements of the algebraic structure in which we evaluate the formulae do not appear as constants in the logic (which has several relevant consequences as existential sentences can no longer describe the finite subinterpretations of a given interpretation), and we obtained positive results for arbitrary lattice semirings rather than just the finite linearly ordered ones.

Surprisingly, the extension preservation theorem, which fails in finite model theory under Boolean semantics, does hold for a number of semirings including the tropical semiring, the Viterbi semiring, the Łukasiewicz semiring, and every lattice semiring, which is the second main contribution of this paper.

The results presented here suggest two main directions for future research: towards other semirings, and other preservation theorems (both in the finite and general case). More specifically, the approach employed for lattices might be applicable to other preservation theorems, for example concerning chain preservation.
Moreover we believe that the curious phenomenon of extension preservation in the finite deserves further investigation.
We suspect that a similar proof technique could be used to establish extension preservation, as well as further preservation theorems such as subinterpretation preservation, in the finite also other semirings.
For all we know, it is possible that the Boolean lattice is the
only one
which does not satisfy extension and substructure preservation in the finite. Exploring this question further would shed a new light on preservation theorems in finite model theory.
