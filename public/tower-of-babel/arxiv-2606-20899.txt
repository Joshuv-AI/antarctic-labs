Finite-Window Recursive Audit Chains for Navier–Stokes Generated Packages
Runlong Yu

Abstract
We develop a finite-window recursive audit framework for Navier–Stokes-generated packages. On a fixed window, the underlying anti-phantom certificate asserts that a baseline-visible package is either detected locally or charged to an explicit residual ledger. The first component of the framework is a broad one-step admissibility criterion for pressure-admissible finite-energy Navier–Stokes-generated audit packages under an explicit ledger of synchronization, localization, projection, harmonic-tail, chart, clean-gap, gate/slack, and detector mismatches. The second component is a finite-chain propagation theorem: once one-step admissibility supplies a finite renormalized chain of packages and a static finite-window audit certificate is available at each scale, variable-coefficient error recursion and weighted summation yield a recursive finite-window anti-phantom lower bound. We also give compact/effective pressure projection criteria, reduced quotient chart visibility, clean detector gaps, a pressure–flux–energy matrix kernel condition, a smooth reduced NS-generated verification class, and a conditional Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg compatible defect-extraction criterion.

1.1
Main contributions

The main contributions are as follows.

(i)

It defines broad NS-generated finite-window audit packages and the renormalized one-step ledger.

(ii)

It proves fixed-window PDE modules: pressure/source preservation, energy/flux localization, and localized pressure recomputation.

(iii)

(iv)

It proves finite-chain recursive audit propagation by variable-coefficient error recursion and weighted summation.

(v)

1.2
Scope of the results

None of the theorems proves infinite-chain propagation, scale-uniform regularity, singularity exclusion, or the Navier–Stokes Clay problem. The broad one-step theorem is conditional on every named mismatch being dominated by the corresponding ledger component. The reduced chart and clean-gap mechanisms are finite-dimensional or compact-quotient inputs unless separately verified. The CKN branch is a finite-window defect-extraction statement under an audit-to-CKN domination hypothesis; it is not a regularity proof.

1.3
Logical status of the main assertions

For clarity, we separate the assertions into three classes. First, the fixed-window PDE estimates in
Section

5
are proved directly from pressure admissibility, the local energy inequality, scaling, Holder’s inequality, and Calderon–Zygmund estimates. Second, the synchronization, gate/slack, detector-comparison, coefficient-update, and recursive summation statements are finite-dimensional or algebraic consequences of explicitly stated hypotheses. Third, compact chart visibility, clean detector gaps, PFE kernel-freeness, scale-uniform propagation, and the audit-to-CKN comparison are structural inputs unless a cited or separate theorem verifies them in a concrete class. Thus each theorem should be read with its displayed hypotheses; no hidden claim is made that these structural hypotheses follow automatically for arbitrary suitable weak solutions.

5
PDE-Generated Modules

5.1
Pressure/source preservation

Proposition 5.1

(Pressure/source preservation)
.

Let
D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\mathrm{NS}}
be generated by pressure-admissible data. Then

U_{k}=u_{k}\in L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3},\qquad F_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\in X_{\mathrm{src}},

P_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\in Y_{\mathrm{prs}},\qquad P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}\in Y_{\mathrm{harm}}.

Moreover
P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}
is harmonic in the interior region on which

\eta=1
, modulo the chosen pressure normalization.

Proof.

Since
u_{k}\in L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3}
,

u_{k}\otimes u_{k}\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1})^{3\times 3},\qquad\left\lVert u_{k}\otimes u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\leq\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}.

The cutoff
\eta
is smooth and bounded, hence

F_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}=\eta\,u_{k}\otimes u_{k}\in X_{\mathrm{src}}.

By the fixed-window Calderon–Zygmund estimate (
2.1
),

\left\lVert P_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}=\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}F_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\left\lVert F_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{X_{\mathrm{src}}},

so
P_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\in Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
.

The global active pressure lift

\widetilde{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}=R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{act}})
is locally

L^{3/2}
on
B_{3/4}
by the same Calderon–Zygmund bound applied before
restriction. Since
p_{k}\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1})
, the difference

P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}=(p_{k}-\widetilde{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{act}})\big|_{B_{3/4}}

belongs to
Y_{\mathrm{harm}}
.

It remains only to identify the interior equation. With the convention used
in
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}
,

-\Delta R_{i}R_{j}F_{ij}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}F_{ij}

in distributions. Therefore

-\Delta\widetilde{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(\eta u_{k,i}u_{k,j}).

On every smaller interior ball
B_{\rho}\Subset B_{3/4}
, the cutoff satisfies

\eta=1
, so

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(\eta u_{k,i}u_{k,j})=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u_{k,i}u_{k,j})

there. Pressure admissibility gives

-\Delta p_{k}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u_{k,i}u_{k,j}),

and hence

-\Delta\bigl(p_{k}-\widetilde{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{act}}\bigr)=0

in distributions on
B_{\rho}
, for almost every time. Since
\rho<3/4

is arbitrary,
P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}
is spatially harmonic on the interior region
where
\eta=1
, modulo the allowed time-dependent pressure normalization.
∎

Definition 5.3

(Energy/flux leakage)
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathsf{Leak}_{\phi}^{\mathrm{EF}}(u,p)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{2}\bigl(|\partial_{t}\phi|+|\Delta\phi|\bigr)

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{1}}\bigl(|u|^{3}+2|p||u|\bigr)|\nabla\phi|+2\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi.

Let

\Omega_{\chi}:=\operatorname{supp}\chi.

The pulled-back one-step energy/flux leakage in scale-
k
normalized
variables is

\displaystyle\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}:=

\displaystyle\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda\Omega_{\chi}}\bigl(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle+\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\bigl(|u_{k}|^{2}+|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\bigr).

Proposition 5.4

(Energy/flux localization)
.

Assume
D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\mathrm{NS}}
is generated by finite-energy data satisfying the
local energy inequality on the normalized window. Then the localization terms
created by restriction and rescaling to the next scale are charged by

\mathsf{Leak}_{\phi}^{\mathrm{EF}}(u_{k+1},p_{k+1})\leq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}.

Consequently the localization component is admissible whenever

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{EF}}\geq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}.

Proof.

The local energy inequality is the admissibility mechanism that makes the
energy/flux channel a legitimate localization channel. The estimate displayed
here is the fixed-window scaling bound for the terms in that channel.

Use the one-step rescaling

u_{k+1}(Y,S)=\lambda u_{k}(\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}S),\qquad p_{k+1}(Y,S)=\lambda^{2}p_{k}(\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}S).

With
y=\lambda Y
,
s=\lambda^{2}S
, one has

dY\,dS=\lambda^{-5}\,dy\,ds.

The energy term with the time derivative satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{k+1}|^{2}|\partial_{S}\phi|\,dY\,dS

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-3}\left\lVert\partial_{S}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda\Omega_{\chi}}|u_{k}|^{2}\,dy\,ds.

The energy term with the spatial Laplacian satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{k+1}|^{2}|\Delta_{Y}\phi|\,dY\,dS

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-3}\left\lVert\Delta\chi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}|u_{k}|^{2}\,dy\,ds.

The cubic flux term obeys

\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{k+1}|^{3}|\nabla_{Y}\phi|\,dY\,dS\leq\lambda^{-2}\left\lVert\nabla\chi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}|u_{k}|^{3}\,dy\,ds,

and the pressure flux term obeys

\int_{Q_{1}}|p_{k+1}|\,|u_{k+1}|\,|\nabla_{Y}\phi|\,dY\,dS\leq\lambda^{-2}\left\lVert\nabla\chi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}|p_{k}|\,|u_{k}|\,dy\,ds.

Finally,

\nabla_{Y}u_{k+1}(Y,S)=\lambda^{2}(\nabla_{y}u_{k})(\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}S),

so

\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla_{Y}u_{k+1}|^{2}\phi\,dY\,dS\leq\lambda^{-1}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda\Omega_{\chi}}|\nabla_{y}u_{k}|^{2}\,dy\,ds.

Combining the five estimates and absorbing the fixed powers of
\lambda

and cutoff norms into
C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}
gives the claimed bound.
∎

5.3
Pressure recomputation and commutator leakage

The commutator estimate uses only the separated-kernel part of the Calderon–Zygmund theory for the double Riesz transform.

Proposition 5.6

(Localized pressure recomputation)
.

For pressure-admissible data in
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\mathrm{NS}}
, the localized
Calderon–Zygmund commutator estimate is

\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{k,i}u_{k,j})\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\eta}\left\lVert u_{k}\otimes u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(A_{\eta}))}.

Under a finite amplitude bound
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\leq M_{U}
,

\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{k,i}u_{k,j})\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\eta}M_{U}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-1,0)\times A_{\eta})}.

Proof.

Let
T_{ij}=R_{i}R_{j}
and
f_{ij}=u_{k,i}u_{k,j}
. We use the convention

[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}:=\eta\,T_{ij}f_{ij}-T_{ij}(\eta f_{ij}).

On the pressure observation ball
B_{1/2}
,
\eta=1
. Hence

[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}=T_{ij}\bigl((1-\eta)f_{ij}\bigr)\qquad\text{on }B_{1/2}.

The function
(1-\eta)f_{ij}
is supported in
A_{\eta}
. Since

\operatorname{dist}(B_{1/2},A_{\eta})>0
, the kernel
K_{ij}
of
T_{ij}

is smooth and bounded on
B_{1/2}\times A_{\eta}
. Therefore, for almost
every time
s
,

|[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}(x,s)|\leq C_{\eta}\int_{A_{\eta}}|f(y,s)|\,dy,\qquad x\in B_{1/2}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})
-norm and using the finite measure of

A_{\eta}
gives

\left\lVert[\eta,T_{ij}]f_{ij}(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{\eta}\left\lVert f(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(A_{\eta})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}
-norm in time yields the claimed
Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
estimate.

For
f=u_{k}\otimes u_{k}
,

\left\lVert u_{k}\otimes u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0)\times A_{\eta})}=\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-1,0)\times A_{\eta})}^{2}.

If
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\leq M_{U}
, then

\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-1,0)\times A_{\eta})}^{2}\leq M_{U}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-1,0)\times A_{\eta})},

which gives the finite-amplitude form.
∎

Definition 6.5

denote the selected harmonic
pressure-tail observation. Choose finite-rank maps

P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}},\qquad H_{M,k+1}^{\mathrm{harm}}:Y_{\mathrm{harm}}\to Y_{\mathrm{harm}}.

No orthogonality is assumed unless it is specified as part of a later reduced
model. Define the one-step projection and harmonic tail errors by

\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k}:=\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})\mathsf{P}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}},

and

\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k}:=\left\lVert(I-H_{M,k+1}^{\mathrm{harm}})\mathsf{H}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{harm}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{harm}}}.

Proposition 6.6

(Projection and harmonic ledger insertion)
.

Under the finite-window datum in
Definition

6.5
, if

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\geq\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}\geq\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k},

then the pressure-projection and harmonic-tail entries of the one-step
admissibility checklist are verified.

Proof.

The pressure-projection entry asks that the portion of the selected clean
pressure observation outside the chosen finite-rank pressure coordinate be
charged to the projection ledger. By
Definition

6.5
, that portion
has size
\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k}
. The harmonic-tail entry is identical with

\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k}
for the chosen harmonic finite-rank map. The two
displayed inequalities therefore assign both tails to their respective
ledger components.
∎

Assumption 6.7

(Projection and harmonic ledger assignment)
.

The clean pressure projection and harmonic-tail mismatches are interpreted
through
Definition

6.5
and satisfy

\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k}\leq\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}},\qquad\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k}\leq\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}.

Compactness of a selected pressure image, effective finite-rank projection, or
explicit harmonic truncation estimates may verify these inequalities in
particular settings. The statement records only the finite-window ledger
convention.

Remark 6.8

(Status of projection and harmonic tails)
.

Definitions

6.5
and
6.6
do not prove convergence
as
N,M\to\infty
, compactness of a pressure image, summability of tail
increments, or scale-uniform approximation. They provide the finite-window
bookkeeping convention used by the one-step theorem.

6.3
Gate/slack channel

Definition 6.9

(Gate/slack ledger datum)
.

At the next scale let
\mathsf{G}_{k+1}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{gate},k+1}
be the
selected gate/slack coordinate in a finite-dimensional normed space, and let

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate},k+1}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{gate},k+1}

be the admissible closed gate set. Define the gate/slack violation by

\mathrm{GateErr}_{k}:=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{gate},k+1}}\bigl(\mathsf{G}_{k+1},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gate},k+1}\bigr).

For scalar inequality gates this is the same convention as measuring the
positive part of the violated slack vector, after choosing the corresponding
finite-dimensional norm.

Proposition 6.10

(Gate/slack ledger insertion)
.

Under the finite-window datum in
Definition

6.9
, if

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gate}}\geq\mathrm{GateErr}_{k},

then the gate/slack entry of the one-step admissibility checklist is verified.

Proof.

The gate/slack entry asks that the next-scale gate coordinate lie in the
admissible gate set up to a charged violation. By

Definition

6.9
, this violation is exactly

\mathrm{GateErr}_{k}
. If
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gate}}
dominates it, the full
gate/slack mismatch is assigned to the gate component of the ledger.
∎

Remark 6.11

(Status of gate/slack bookkeeping)
.

Definitions

6.9
and
6.10
do not prove that the
gate violation is small, summable, or generated by the Navier–Stokes
equations. They only fix the finite-window convention used by the one-step
ledger.

6.4
Reduced detector comparison for PFE clean audit channels

This subsection realizes the detector channel in a concrete reduced
finite-dimensional model. The comparison is between a local reduced detector
and the pressure–flux–energy clean detector after applying the reduced clean
chart.

Definition 6.12

(Reduced PFE detector-comparison datum)
.

Fix finite-dimensional normed spaces

X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\qquad Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}.

Let

\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}:X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be the reduced clean chart. Let

T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}:X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}

be the reduced local detector/tax map and the reduced clean PFE detector/tax
map. Finally, let

J_{\Lambda,k}:Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}

be the observation transport map. For
D\in X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and

d\in Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, define

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D):=\left\lVert T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}},\qquad M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(d):=\left\lVert T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}d\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}}.

The reduced detector commutator is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{det},k}(D):=T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}(D)-J_{\Lambda,k}T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D).

Definition 6.13

(Detector residual domination)
.

The concrete detector-commutator residual is

\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{det.red},k}(D):=\left\lVert\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{det},k}(D)\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}}.

If a broader reduced residual functional
\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}

is used, the detector-comparison convention requires constants

C_{\mathrm{res},k}\geq 0
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k}\geq 0
such that

\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{det.red},k}(D)\leq C_{\mathrm{res},k}\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}(D)+\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k}.

With the concrete choice

\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}=\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{det.red},k}
,
one may take
C_{\mathrm{res},k}=1
and

\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k}=0
.

Assumption 6.14

(Observation transport lower bound)
.

There are constants
c_{J,k}>0
and
\Delta_{J,k}\geq 0
such that, for all

D\in X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\left\lVert T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}}\geq c_{J,k}\left\lVert J_{\Lambda,k}T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}}-\Delta_{J,k}.

This is a reduced finite-dimensional observability or transport convention.

Theorem 6.15

(Reduced detector comparison for PFE clean audit channels)
.

Assume the reduced detector-comparison datum in

Definition

6.12
, the residual domination in

Definition

6.13
, and the observation transport lower
bound in
6.14
. Then for every

D\in X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D)\geq c_{J,k}M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\bigl(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}D\bigr)-c_{J,k}C_{\mathrm{res},k}\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}(D)-c_{J,k}\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k}-\Delta_{J,k}.

Proof.

By definition of the detector commutator,

T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}(D)=J_{\Lambda,k}T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D)+\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{det},k}(D).

Hence

\left\lVert J_{\Lambda,k}T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D)\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}}\geq M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}D)-\left\lVert\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{det},k}(D)\right\rVert_{Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}}.

Applying the observation transport lower bound and then the reduced residual
domination gives

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D)\geq c_{J,k}M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}D)-c_{J,k}\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{det.red},k}(D)-\Delta_{J,k}

and therefore the displayed estimate.
∎

Corollary 6.16

(Normalized reduced detector comparison)
.

In the normalized observation case

c_{J,k}=1,\qquad\Delta_{J,k}=0,

the comparison becomes

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D)\geq M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\bigl(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}D\bigr)-C_{\mathrm{dc},k}\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}(D)-\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k},

with
C_{\mathrm{dc},k}=C_{\mathrm{res},k}
.

Proof.

This is
Theorem

6.15
with

c_{J,k}=1
,
\Delta_{J,k}=0
, and

C_{\mathrm{dc},k}=C_{\mathrm{res},k}
.
∎

Corollary 6.17

(Detector ledger insertion for reduced PFE channels)
.

Let
D_{k}^{N,\mathrm{loc}}\in X_{N,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
be the reduced local coordinate of the
synchronized package
D_{k}-\zeta_{k}
. Define

\mathrm{DetErr}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:=c_{J,k}C_{\mathrm{res},k}\operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{red},k}(D_{k}^{N,\mathrm{loc}})+c_{J,k}\Delta_{\mathrm{det},k}+\Delta_{J,k}.

If

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{det}}\geq\mathrm{DetErr}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}},

then the reduced PFE detector-comparison entry of the one-step admissibility
checklist is verified:

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{loc}}(D_{k}^{N,\mathrm{loc}})\geq c_{J,k}M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\bigl(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}D_{k}^{N,\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)-\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{det}}.

Proof.

Substitute
D=D_{k}^{N,\mathrm{loc}}
in

Theorem

6.15
. The definition of

\mathrm{DetErr}_{k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
collects exactly the residual,
commutator, and observation-transport losses. If

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{det}}
dominates this quantity, the displayed detector
comparison follows.
∎

Remark 6.18

(Scope of the reduced detector comparison)
.

Theorems

6.15
and
6.17

are reduced finite-dimensional detector-comparison statements. They do not
prove detector comparison for the full infinite-dimensional Navier–Stokes
package geometry. They also do not prove scale-uniformity, singularity
extraction, Navier–Stokes regularity, or any Clay-level conclusion.

7
Finite-Window One-Step Admissibility Criteria

The one-step admissibility assumption in
4.5
is the first
recursive structural input. This section records a checkable finite-window
criterion for it and verifies the pressure/source part for
Navier–Stokes-generated packages. The result is intentionally local: it
does not prove detector stability, chart kernel-freeness, gate/slack closure,
or scale-uniformity.

7.1
Coordinate admissibility budgets

Use the normalized spaces

X_{u}:=L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3},\qquad X_{\mathrm{src}}:=L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1}))^{3\times 3},

Y_{\mathrm{prs}}:=L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})),\qquad Y_{\mathrm{harm}}:=L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{3/4})).

Definition 7.1

(Coordinate admissibility checklist)
.

At scale
k+1
, a recomputed package
D_{k+1}
satisfies the coordinate
admissibility checklist with excess vector

\mathbf{e}_{k+1}=(e_{u},e_{\mathrm{src}},e_{\mathrm{prs}},e_{\mathrm{harm}},e_{\mathrm{sync}},e_{\mathrm{loc}},e_{\mathrm{gate}},e_{\mathrm{det}},e_{\mathrm{chart}},e_{\mathrm{gap}})

if the following hold:

(a)

U_{k+1}\in X_{u}
, the selected source coordinate

S_{k+1}\in X_{\mathrm{src}}
,

P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{act}}\in Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
, and

P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{harm}}\in Y_{\mathrm{harm}}
;

(b)

the representative mismatch, localization leakage, gate/slack
mismatch, detector mismatch, chart error, and clean-gap error are bounded
by the corresponding entries of
\mathbf{e}_{k+1}
;

(c)

under the energy/flux convention used here, the
localization entry is
e_{\mathrm{loc}}=e_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{EF}}
and is
verified by the update mechanism in
Theorem

7.15
;

(d)

the pressure projection and harmonic truncation errors obey

\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k+1}\leq e_{\mathrm{prs}}+e_{\mathrm{src}},\qquad\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k+1}\leq e_{\mathrm{harm}}.

In a componentwise ledger these errors are later charged by requiring

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\geq\mathrm{ProjErr}_{k+1}
and

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}\geq\mathrm{HarmErr}_{k+1}
.

The total checklist excess is

|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}|_{1}:=e_{u}+e_{\mathrm{src}}+e_{\mathrm{prs}}+e_{\mathrm{harm}}+e_{\mathrm{sync}}+e_{\mathrm{loc}}+e_{\mathrm{gate}}+e_{\mathrm{det}}+e_{\mathrm{chart}}+e_{\mathrm{gap}}.

Theorem 7.2

(Coordinate-budget one-step admissibility)
.

Let
D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and let
D_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(D_{k})
be
obtained by restriction, rescaling, synchronization, and recomputation. If

D_{k+1}
satisfies the coordinate admissibility checklist with excess

\mathbf{e}_{k+1}
, with the localization entry supplied by the
energy/flux update of
Theorem

7.15
. Assume that the componentwise
ledger dominates the corresponding checklist errors, namely

\begin{gathered}\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync}}\geq e_{\mathrm{sync}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{EF}}\geq e_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gate}}\geq e_{\mathrm{gate}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{det}}\geq e_{\mathrm{det}},\\
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}\geq e_{\mathrm{chart}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gap}}\geq e_{\mathrm{gap}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\geq e_{\mathrm{prs}}+e_{\mathrm{src}},\qquad\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{harm}}\geq e_{\mathrm{harm}}.\end{gathered}

Equivalently, under a total-budget convention, it is enough to choose

\Delta_{k}\geq C_{\mathrm{adm}}|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}|_{1}

for a fixed finite-window bookkeeping constant
C_{\mathrm{adm}}\geq 1
and a
fixed allocation of the total budget to the named components. Then

D_{k+1}
belongs to the increment-enlarged admissible class

\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\Delta_{k})
. In particular, if all checklist excesses
vanish, then
D_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k+1}
in the exact finite-window
sense.

Proof.

The admissible class
\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\Delta_{k})
is defined by the same
coordinate membership requirements as
\mathcal{A}_{k+1}
, with each
non-exact channel allowed an error bounded by its assigned component of the
ledger in
Definition

4.2
. The checklist gives the required
coordinate membership in
X_{u}
,
X_{\mathrm{src}}
,
Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
, and

Y_{\mathrm{harm}}
, and assigns every synchronization, localization, gate/slack,
detector, chart, projection, harmonic, and clean-gap defect to one of the
named components. The displayed componentwise inequalities dominate those
errors by the corresponding ledger entries. Under the coarser total-budget
convention, the fixed allocation and the bound

\Delta_{k}\geq C_{\mathrm{adm}}|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}|_{1}
provide the same component
bounds after increasing
C_{\mathrm{adm}}
if necessary. This is precisely
membership in
\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\Delta_{k})
. If

\mathbf{e}_{k+1}=0
, no enlargement is used.
∎

7.2
A reduced synchronization model

The synchronization increment
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync}}
measures the failure of
the representative chosen after renormalization to equal the renormalized
representative chosen before renormalization. In full package geometry this
may be a genuine structural input. In a reduced finite-dimensional quotient
model, however, the drift has a simple algebraic form.

Definition 7.3

(Reduced synchronization datum)
.

A reduced synchronization datum consists of finite-dimensional normed spaces

\mathfrak{X}_{k}
, gauge subspaces
\mathfrak{Z}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{X}_{k}
,
closed complements
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
, and projections

\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{k},\qquad\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{H}_{k},\qquad\mathfrak{X}_{k}=\mathfrak{Z}_{k}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{k}.

The synchronized representative is

\zeta_{k}(D):=\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}D,\qquad D-\zeta_{k}(D)=\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D.

For a reduced one-step map
R_{k}^{N}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}
, the
synchronization drift is

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync},N}(D):=\left\lVert\zeta_{k+1}(R_{k}^{N}D)-R_{k}^{N}\zeta_{k}(D)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}}.

Theorem 7.4

(Finite-dimensional synchronization drift)
.

Let
R_{k}^{N}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}
be linear and gauge
compatible:

R_{k}^{N}\mathfrak{Z}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{Z}_{k+1}.

Then

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync},N}(D)\leq\left\lVert\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}}\,\left\lVert D-\zeta_{k}(D)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{k}}.

If, in addition,

R_{k}^{N}\mathfrak{H}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{H}_{k+1},

then
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync},N}(D)=0
for every
D
.

Proof.

Decompose
D=\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}D+\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D
. Since

\zeta_{k+1}(R_{k}^{N}D)=\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}D
, we have

\zeta_{k+1}(R_{k}^{N}D)-R_{k}^{N}\zeta_{k}(D)=\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D+\bigl(\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}D-R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}D\bigr).

The second term vanishes because
R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{Z}}D\in\mathfrak{Z}_{k+1}
and
\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}
is the identity on

\mathfrak{Z}_{k+1}
. Thus

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync},N}(D)=\left\lVert\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}},

The stated bound follows from

\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D=D-\zeta_{k}(D).

If
R_{k}^{N}\mathfrak{H}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{H}_{k+1}
, then

\Pi_{k+1}^{\mathfrak{Z}}R_{k}^{N}\Pi_{k}^{\mathfrak{H}}D=0,

so the drift vanishes.
∎

Corollary 7.5

(Synchronization increment insertion)
.

In a reduced finite-dimensional chain satisfying the hypotheses of

Theorem

7.4
, the synchronization component of the one-step ledger
may be chosen as

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync}}\geq\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync},N}(D).

If the reduced map preserves both gauge and complement subspaces, then the
model synchronization contribution is zero.

Proof.

The first assertion is the definition of assigning the computed drift to the
ledger component
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{sync}}
. The second assertion follows from
the last part of
Theorem

7.4
.
∎

7.3
Gate and slack update model

The gate/slack component of the one-step ledger is another channel where a
finite-window algebraic update can be written without new PDE estimates. Let

\mathfrak{A}
be the finite set of active gate channels. For

a\in\mathfrak{A}
, write
B_{a,k}\geq 0
for the used budget,

\tau_{a,k}\geq 0
for the threshold, and
s_{a,k}\geq 0
for the slack.
The gate violation and slack identity mismatch are

V_{a,k}:=(B_{a,k}-\tau_{a,k})_{+},\qquad\Sigma_{a,k}:=\left\lvert B_{a,k}+s_{a,k}-\tau_{a,k}\right\rvert.

Theorem 7.6

(Gate/slack one-step update)
.

Assume that for each active gate channel
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, the
renormalized budgets and thresholds satisfy

B_{a,k+1}\leq\alpha_{a,k}B_{a,k}+\beta_{a,k}\mathfrak{q}_{k}+r_{a,k},

and

\tau_{a,k+1}\geq\alpha_{a,k}\tau_{a,k}-q_{a,k},

Here
\mathfrak{q}_{k}\geq 0
is the non-gate disturbance supplied by the other
one-step channels. The constants

\alpha_{a,k},\beta_{a,k},r_{a,k},q_{a,k}
are nonnegative. Then

V_{a,k+1}\leq\alpha_{a,k}V_{a,k}+\beta_{a,k}\mathfrak{q}_{k}+r_{a,k}+q_{a,k}.

If the next slack is chosen canonically by

s_{a,k+1}^{\mathrm{can}}:=(\tau_{a,k+1}-B_{a,k+1})_{+},

then

\left\lvert B_{a,k+1}+s_{a,k+1}^{\mathrm{can}}-\tau_{a,k+1}\right\rvert=V_{a,k+1}.

If exact slack identity is desired instead, the signed canonical choice

s_{a,k+1}^{\mathrm{sgn}}:=\tau_{a,k+1}-B_{a,k+1}

gives

B_{a,k+1}+s_{a,k+1}^{\mathrm{sgn}}-\tau_{a,k+1}=0,

but may fail to be nonnegative when the gate is violated.

Proof.

Subtract the threshold lower bound from the budget upper bound:

B_{a,k+1}-\tau_{a,k+1}\leq\alpha_{a,k}(B_{a,k}-\tau_{a,k})+\beta_{a,k}\mathfrak{q}_{k}+r_{a,k}+q_{a,k}.

Taking positive parts and using monotonicity and subadditivity of

(\cdot)_{+}
gives

(B_{a,k+1}-\tau_{a,k+1})_{+}\leq\alpha_{a,k}(B_{a,k}-\tau_{a,k})_{+}+\beta_{a,k}\mathfrak{q}_{k}+r_{a,k}+q_{a,k}.

This is the claimed violation update. For the nonnegative canonical slack,
if
\tau_{a,k+1}\geq B_{a,k+1}
, then the displayed mismatch is zero; if

B_{a,k+1}>\tau_{a,k+1}
, then the canonical slack is zero and the
mismatch is exactly
B_{a,k+1}-\tau_{a,k+1}=V_{a,k+1}
. The signed slack
identity is immediate from the definition.
∎

Corollary 7.7

(Gate increment insertion)
.

Let

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gate}}\geq\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{A}}\left(\beta_{a,k}\mathfrak{q}_{k}+r_{a,k}+q_{a,k}\right)+\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{A}}\alpha_{a,k}V_{a,k}.

Then all next-scale gate violations generated by

Theorem

7.6
are charged to the gate component of the one-step
ledger. If a transported noncanonical slack
\widetilde{s}_{a,k+1}
is
used, add

\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{A}}\left\lvert\widetilde{s}_{a,k+1}-s_{a,k+1}^{\mathrm{can}}\right\rvert

to
\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gate}}
.

Proof.

Summing the estimate in
Theorem

7.6
over the finite channel set

\mathfrak{A}
gives the first assertion. For a transported slack,

\left\lvert B+\widetilde{s}-\tau\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert B+s^{\mathrm{can}}-\tau\right\rvert+\left\lvert\widetilde{s}-s^{\mathrm{can}}\right\rvert,

so the additional displayed term controls the noncanonical slack mismatch.
∎

7.4
Reduced detector stability

The detector update in (
9.4
) is model-dependent in the full
package setting. In a reduced finite-dimensional model it follows from a
standard commutator estimate between the detector map and the one-step
renormalization map.

Definition 7.8

(Detector commutation defect)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}_{k}
be a reduced package space and let

T_{k}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{k}

be the combined detector/tax-channel map into a finite-dimensional normed
space
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
. Define

M_{k}(D):=\left\lVert T_{k}D\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}.

Let
R_{k}^{N}:\mathfrak{X}_{k}\to\mathfrak{X}_{k+1}
be the reduced one-step map
and let
L_{k}:\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}
transport detector
coordinates. The detector commutation defect is

\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D):=T_{k+1}R_{k}^{N}D-L_{k}T_{k}D.

Theorem 7.9

(Reduced detector-stability estimate)
.

In the reduced detector model of
Definition

7.8
,

M_{k+1}(R_{k}^{N}D)\leq\left\lVert L_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}}M_{k}(D)+\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}}.

Consequently, if

\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}}\leq B_{k}E_{k}+C_{k}\Delta_{k}+\epsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{det}},

then

M_{k+1}(R_{k}^{N}D)\leq A_{k}M_{k}(D)+B_{k}E_{k}+C_{k}\Delta_{k}+\epsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{det}},

with

A_{k}:=\left\lVert L_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D)
,

T_{k+1}R_{k}^{N}D=L_{k}T_{k}D+\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D).

Taking the
\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}
-norm and using the triangle inequality gives

The first term is bounded by

\left\lVert L_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{k+1}}\left\lVert T_{k}D\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}=A_{k}M_{k}(D).

The asserted detector update follows after inserting the assumed bound on

\mathfrak{C}_{k}(D)
.

7.5
NS-generated pressure/source coordinate preservation

Theorem 7.11

(Pressure/source preservation)
.

Let
(u,p)
be pressure-admissible Navier–Stokes data on

Q_{r}(z_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})

with

u\in L^{3}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))^{3},\qquad p\in L^{3/2}(Q_{r}(z_{0})),

and

-\Delta p=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u_{i}u_{j})

in distributions, modulo time-dependent constants. Define the normalized
fields on
Q_{1}
by

u_{r}(y,s)=ru(x_{0}+ry,t_{0}+r^{2}s),\qquad p_{r}(y,s)=r^{2}p(x_{0}+ry,t_{0}+r^{2}s).

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
satisfy
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{3/4}
, and
set

F_{r,ij}^{\mathrm{act}}:=\eta u_{r,i}u_{r,j},\qquad p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}:=R_{i}R_{j}(F_{r,ij}^{\mathrm{act}}),\qquad p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}:=p_{r}-p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}.

Then

u_{r}\in X_{u},\qquad F_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\in X_{\mathrm{src}},\qquad p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\in Y_{\mathrm{prs}},\qquad p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}\in Y_{\mathrm{harm}}.

Moreover,
p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}
is harmonic on
B_{3/4}
for almost every time.

Proof.

The change of variables gives

\left\lVert u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{3}=r^{-2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{3},\qquad\left\lVert p_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}^{3/2}=r^{-2}\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{3/2},

so
u_{r}\in L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3}
and
p_{r}\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1})
. Hence

F_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}=\eta u_{r}\otimes u_{r}\in X_{\mathrm{src}},\qquad\left\lVert F_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{X_{\mathrm{src}}}\leq\left\lVert u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}.

By the fixed-window Calderon–Zygmund estimate,

\left\lVert p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\left\lVert F_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{X_{\mathrm{src}}}.

The same global Calderon–Zygmund bound gives

\left\lVert p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{3/4}))}\leq C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\left\lVert F_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{X_{\mathrm{src}}}.

The pressure-admissibility identity is invariant under Navier–Stokes
scaling:

-\Delta_{y}p_{r}=\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}(u_{r,i}u_{r,j})

in distributions. Also,

-\Delta_{y}p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}=\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}(\eta u_{r,i}u_{r,j})

in the same distributional convention for the double Riesz transform. Since

\eta=1
on
B_{3/4}
, the difference
p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}=p_{r}-p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}

satisfies

-\Delta_{y}p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}=0

on
B_{3/4}
for almost every time. Finally,

\left\lVert p_{r}^{\mathrm{harm}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{harm}}}\leq\left\lVert p_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{3/4}))}+\left\lVert p_{r}^{\mathrm{act}}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{3/4}))},

and the right-hand side is finite.
∎

7.6
Energy/flux localization update under renormalization

This subsection fixes the localization convention used in the recursive
ledger. It records the energy/flux leakage created by one restriction and
Navier–Stokes rescaling step. The statement is deliberately a bookkeeping
estimate: it assigns the next-scale localization coordinate to the one-step
ledger, but it does not assert smallness or summability.

Let

u_{k+1}(Y,S)=\lambda u_{k}(\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}S),\qquad p_{k+1}(Y,S)=\lambda^{2}p_{k}(\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}S),

where
0<\lambda<1
. Fix

0\leq\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}),\qquad\chi\equiv 1\text{ on }B_{3/4},

and
0\leq\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1,0])
. Set

\phi(Y,S):=\theta(S)\chi(Y),\qquad A_{\chi}:=\operatorname{supp}\nabla\chi\cup\operatorname{supp}\Delta\chi,\qquad A_{\lambda,\chi}:=\lambda A_{\chi}.

Definition 7.13

(Energy/flux localization leakage)
.

For finite-energy pressure-admissible data on
Q_{1}
, define

\displaystyle\mathsf{Leak}_{\phi}^{\mathrm{EF}}(u,p):=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{2}\left(\left\lvert\partial_{t}\phi\right\rvert+\left\lvert\Delta\phi\right\rvert\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{1}}\left(|u|^{3}+2|p||u|\right)\left\lvert\nabla\phi\right\rvert\,dx\,dt+2\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi\,dx\,dt.

The spatial-shell part is

\mathsf{Leak}_{\mathrm{shell}}^{\mathrm{EF}}(u,p):=\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{A_{\chi}}\left(|u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{3}+|p||u|\right)\,dx\,dt.

The full pulled-back one-step energy/flux leakage is

\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}\left(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dy\,ds+\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\left(|u_{k}|^{2}+|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right)\,dy\,ds.

Its shell contribution is

\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF,shell}}:=\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\left(|u_{k}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}+|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right)\,dy\,ds.

Remark 7.14
.

The full pulled-back leakage includes the core
L^{2}
and dissipation
contributions because the terms

\partial_{t}\phi
and
\phi|\nabla u|^{2}
are not supported only on the
spatial transition shell. The shell functional records the part generated
by the spatial cutoff. Using the shell-only quantity for the full local
energy expression requires an additional convention that the time-cutoff and
core dissipation terms have already been charged to a separate energy
budget.

Theorem 7.15

(Energy/flux localization update under one-step renormalization)
.

Let
D_{k}
be an NS-generated scale-
k
package generated by data
satisfying, in normalized scale-
k
variables,

u_{k}\in L^{\infty}((-1,0);L^{2}(B_{1}))^{3},\qquad\nabla u_{k}\in L^{2}(Q_{1})^{3\times 3},

u_{k}\in L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3},\qquad p_{k}\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1}).

Let
D_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(D_{k})
be obtained by restriction to

(-\lambda^{2},0)\times B_{\lambda}
, Navier–Stokes rescaling to
Q_{1}
,
and recomputation of the next-scale package. Then

\mathsf{Leak}_{\phi}^{\mathrm{EF}}(u_{k+1},p_{k+1})\leq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\,\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}.

Consequently, if

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{EF}}\geq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\,\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}},

then the energy/flux localization component of the scale-
(k+1)

coordinate admissibility checklist is charged to the one-step ledger.

Proof.

Use the change of variables

y=\lambda Y,\qquad s=\lambda^{2}S,\qquad dY\,dS=\lambda^{-5}\,dy\,ds.

The rescaled fields satisfy

|u_{k+1}(Y,S)|=\lambda|u_{k}(y,s)|,\qquad|p_{k+1}(Y,S)|=\lambda^{2}|p_{k}(y,s)|,

and

|\nabla_{Y}u_{k+1}(Y,S)|=\lambda^{2}|\nabla_{y}u_{k}(y,s)|.

The constants

\|\theta\|_{C^{1}},\qquad\|\chi\|_{C^{2}}

control
\left\lvert\partial_{S}\phi\right\rvert
,
\left\lvert\nabla_{Y}\phi\right\rvert
, and

\left\lvert\Delta_{Y}\phi\right\rvert
. The terms containing
\nabla_{Y}\chi
or

\Delta_{Y}\chi
are supported in
(-\lambda^{2},0)\times\lambda A_{\chi}

after pullback. The time-cutoff energy term and the dissipation term are
supported in
(-\lambda^{2},0)\times\lambda B_{1}
.

Therefore

\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{k+1}|^{2}\left(\left\lvert\partial_{S}\phi\right\rvert+\left\lvert\Delta_{Y}\phi\right\rvert\right)\,dY\,dS\leq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}|u_{k}|^{2}\,dy\,ds,

\int_{Q_{1}}\left(|u_{k+1}|^{3}+2|p_{k+1}||u_{k+1}|\right)\left\lvert\nabla_{Y}\phi\right\rvert\,dY\,dS\leq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}\left(|u_{k}|^{3}+|p_{k}||u_{k}|\right)\,dy\,ds,

and

2\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla_{Y}u_{k+1}|^{2}\phi\,dY\,dS\leq C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda B_{1}}|\nabla_{y}u_{k}|^{2}\,dy\,ds.

The constants absorb the fixed powers of
\lambda
, including

\lambda^{-3}
,
\lambda^{-2}
, and
\lambda^{-1}
. Adding the three
estimates gives the claimed bound. The final assertion is the definition of
the localization component of the ledger.
∎

Corollary 7.16

(Finite-amplitude localization bookkeeping)
.

If
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\leq M_{U}
, then

\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}|p_{k}||u_{k}|\,dy\,ds\leq\left\lVert p_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-\lambda^{2},0)\times\lambda A_{\chi})}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-\lambda^{2},0)\times\lambda A_{\chi})}

and

\int_{-\lambda^{2}}^{0}\int_{\lambda A_{\chi}}|u_{k}|^{3}\,dy\,ds=\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-\lambda^{2},0)\times\lambda A_{\chi})}^{3}.

Thus the nonlinear flux and pressure-flux parts of

\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}
are charged to the existing finite-window
velocity and pressure budgets. No smallness is implied without additional
smallness assumptions.

Proof.

The pressure-flux estimate is Holder’s inequality with exponents
3/2

and
3
. The cubic identity is the definition of the
L^{3}
-norm. The
finite-amplitude hypothesis records that the global velocity factor is
finite when such a factor is needed elsewhere in the ledger; it does not
turn the displayed quantities into small errors.
∎

Corollary 7.17

(Localization increment insertion)
.

In the energy/flux convention, set

e_{\mathrm{loc},k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}:=C_{\lambda,\chi,\theta}\mathsf{Leak}_{k\to k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}}.

If

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{EF}}\geq e_{\mathrm{loc},k+1}^{\mathrm{EF}},

then the localization entry in the scale-
(k+1)
checklist is verified.
Consequently, if the pressure/source coordinates are preserved and the
synchronization, gate/slack, detector, chart, clean-gap, projection, harmonic,
and energy/flux localization entries are bounded by their assigned ledger
terms, then

D_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\Delta_{k}).

Proof.

The first assertion is exactly
Theorem

7.15
with the
localization entry named as a checklist excess. The final assertion is

Theorem

7.2
applied with this localization entry and the other
entries assigned to the remaining components of
\Delta_{k}
.
∎

7.15
is a finite-window localization bookkeeping result.

7.7
Coefficient update and finite-window positivity

The one-step audit theorem uses the coefficient update

c_{k+1}\geq c_{k}-\eta_{k}.

At the finite-window level this update is an algebraic consequence of how the
clean gap, chart visibility, and residual-loss coefficient change from one
renormalized window to the next.

Definition 7.19

(Scale coefficient drift)
.

Write

\mu_{k}:=\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}},\qquad\lambda_{k}:=\lambda_{G,k},\qquad\ell_{k}:=\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\mathrm{res}},

and

c_{k}:=\mu_{k}\lambda_{k}-\ell_{k}.

The coefficient drift from scale
k
to
k+1
is controlled by
nonnegative numbers

\delta_{\mu,k},\qquad\delta_{\lambda,k},\qquad\delta_{\ell,k},

if

\mu_{k+1}\geq(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})_{+},\qquad\lambda_{k+1}\geq(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k})_{+},\qquad\ell_{k+1}\leq\ell_{k}+\delta_{\ell,k}.

Define the induced coefficient-loss allowance

\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}:=\mu_{k}\lambda_{k}-(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})_{+}(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k})_{+}+\delta_{\ell,k}.

Theorem 7.20

(Finite-window coefficient update)
.

Assume the coefficient drift bounds in
Definition

7.19
. Then

c_{k+1}\geq c_{k}-\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}.

Consequently, the abstract update rule (
9.3
) holds whenever

\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}.

Proof.

By the drift assumptions,

c_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\lambda_{k+1}-\ell_{k+1}\geq(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})_{+}(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k})_{+}-\ell_{k}-\delta_{\ell,k}.

Since
c_{k}=\mu_{k}\lambda_{k}-\ell_{k}
, the right-hand side equals

c_{k}-\left[\mu_{k}\lambda_{k}-(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})_{+}(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k})_{+}+\delta_{\ell,k}\right]=c_{k}-\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}.

This proves the estimate. The final assertion follows by choosing the
update-rule loss
\eta_{k}
at least as large as the displayed coefficient
loss.
∎

Corollary 7.21

(Linear coefficient-loss bound)
.

If

0\leq\delta_{\mu,k}\leq\mu_{k},\qquad 0\leq\delta_{\lambda,k}\leq\lambda_{k},

then

\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}\leq\mu_{k}\delta_{\lambda,k}+\lambda_{k}\delta_{\mu,k}+\delta_{\ell,k}.

If, in addition,
\mu_{k}\leq\overline{\mu}_{k}
and

\lambda_{k}\leq\overline{\lambda}_{k}
, then the simpler bound

\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}\leq\overline{\mu}_{k}\delta_{\lambda,k}+\overline{\lambda}_{k}\delta_{\mu,k}+\delta_{\ell,k}

also holds.

Proof.

Under the displayed size assumptions,

(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})_{+}(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k})_{+}=(\mu_{k}-\delta_{\mu,k})(\lambda_{k}-\delta_{\lambda,k}).

Therefore

\eta_{k}^{\mathrm{coeff}}=\mu_{k}\delta_{\lambda,k}+\lambda_{k}\delta_{\mu,k}-\delta_{\mu,k}\delta_{\lambda,k}+\delta_{\ell,k}\leq\mu_{k}\delta_{\lambda,k}+\lambda_{k}\delta_{\mu,k}+\delta_{\ell,k}.

The upper-bound formulation follows immediately.
∎

Corollary 7.22

(Finite-chain positivity persistence)
.

If

c_{0}>\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\eta_{j}^{\mathrm{coeff}},

then

c_{k}\geq c_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j}^{\mathrm{coeff}}>0\qquad\text{for }0\leq k\leq K.

In particular,

c_{K}^{\min}:=\min_{0\leq k\leq K}c_{k}\geq c_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\eta_{j}^{\mathrm{coeff}}.

Proof.

Iterating
Theorem

7.20
gives

c_{k}\geq c_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j}^{\mathrm{coeff}}.

The assumed strict inequality makes the right-hand side positive for every

k\leq K
. Taking the minimum over
k
gives the displayed lower bound for

c_{K}^{\min}
.
∎

Remark 7.23
.

Theorem

7.20
is purely finite-window algebra. It does not prove
that
\mu_{k}
,
\lambda_{k}
, or
\ell_{k}
are scale-uniform. It only
identifies the exact coefficient loss that must be included in the recursive
ledger when these quantities drift from one scale to the next.

10
Finite-Chain Recursive Anti-Phantom Theorem

10.1
Error recursion algebra

Lemma 10.1

(Variable-coefficient error recursion)
.

Suppose

E_{k+1}\leq a_{k}E_{k}+b_{k}\Delta_{k},\qquad k=0,\dots,K-1.

With the convention that an empty product equals
1
, one has

E_{K}\leq\left(\prod_{i=0}^{K-1}a_{i}\right)E_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}b_{j}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{K-1}a_{i}\right)\Delta_{j}.

Proof.

For
K=1
the claim is the assumed recursion. Assume it is true for
K
.
Then

E_{K+1}\leq a_{K}E_{K}+b_{K}\Delta_{K}.

Substituting the induction hypothesis for
E_{K}
gives

E_{K+1}\leq\left(\prod_{i=0}^{K}a_{i}\right)E_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}b_{j}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{K}a_{i}\right)\Delta_{j}+b_{K}\Delta_{K},

which is the desired formula at
K+1
.
∎

Corollary 10.2

(Constant-coefficient recursion)
.

If
E_{k+1}\leq aE_{k}+b\Delta_{k}
, then

E_{K}\leq a^{K}E_{0}+b\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}a^{K-1-j}\Delta_{j}.

10.2
Weighted chain lower bound

Theorem 10.3

(Finite-chain recursive anti-phantom theorem)
.

Let
D_{0},\dots,D_{K}
be a finite renormalized audit chain and let

w_{k}\geq 0
. Assume each scale carries a valid certificate

M_{k}\geq c_{k}\delta_{k}-E_{k}.

Set

c_{K}^{\min}:=\min_{0\leq k\leq K}c_{k}

and

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\mathrm{rec}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}E_{k}.

Then

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}M_{k}\geq c_{K}^{\min}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\delta_{k}-\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\mathrm{rec}}.

(10.1)

Moreover, the terms
E_{k}
are controlled by
Lemma

10.1
on
each prefix of the chain.

Proof.

Multiplying
M_{k}\geq c_{k}\delta_{k}-E_{k}
by
w_{k}\geq 0
and summing gives

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}M_{k}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}c_{k}\delta_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}E_{k}.

Since
c_{k}\geq c_{K}^{\min}
, this is (
10.1
). The
claimed control of
E_{k}
follows from
Lemma

10.1
applied with
terminal index
k
.
∎

Corollary 10.4

(Recursive anti-phantom alternative)
.

Under the hypotheses of
Theorem

10.3
, at least one of the
following alternatives holds:

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}M_{k}\geq\frac{c_{K}^{\min}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\delta_{k},

or

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\mathrm{rec}}\geq\frac{c_{K}^{\min}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\delta_{k}.

Proof.

If

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\mathrm{rec}}\leq\frac{c_{K}^{\min}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\delta_{k},

then (
10.1
) gives the first alternative. If this
inequality fails, the second alternative holds.
∎

Recursive Error Regimes

Consider the constant-coefficient recursion

E_{k+1}\leq aE_{k}+b\Delta_{k}.

Proposition 11.1

(Contractive regime)
.

If
0\leq a<1
and
\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{*}
for all
k
, then

E_{K}\leq a^{K}E_{0}+\frac{b}{1-a}\Delta_{*}.

Proof.

Use
Corollary

10.2
and the geometric-series bound

\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}a^{K-1-j}\leq\frac{1}{1-a}.

∎

Proposition 11.2

(Neutral regime)
.

If
a=1
, then

E_{K}\leq E_{0}+b\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{j}.

Thus finite-chain propagation remains useful whenever the accumulated
increments are controlled on the considered chain.

Proof.

This is
Corollary

10.2
with
a=1
.
∎

Proposition 11.3

(Expanding regime)
.

If
a>1
, then

E_{K}\leq a^{K}E_{0}+b\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}a^{K-1-j}\Delta_{j}.

Finite
K
estimates still hold, but scale-uniform propagation requires
additional decay or cancellation in
E_{0}
and the increments

\Delta_{j}
.

Proof.

This is again
Corollary

10.2
. The final statement is an
interpretation of the exponential weights
a^{K-1-j}
.
∎

12
Compact, Reduced, and PFE Structural Inputs

This section verifies finite-window structural inputs for selected compact,
smooth, reduced, or effectively projected package classes. No result in
this section is asserted for all suitable weak Navier–Stokes solutions.

12.1
Pressure projection compactness and effective projection

Let

X_{\mathrm{src}}:=L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1}))^{3\times 3},\qquad Y_{\mathrm{prs}}:=L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})).

The clean pressure map is

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}F:=R_{i}R_{j}(F_{ij})\big|_{B_{1/2}},

with sources extended by zero outside
B_{1}
. We assume the fixed
Calderon–Zygmund estimate

\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}F\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{X_{\mathrm{src}}}.

Definition 12.1

(Selected source and pressure image)
.

At scale
k
, define

\mathcal{F}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}:=\{F_{D_{k}-\zeta_{k}}^{\mathrm{cl}}:D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\mathrm{NS}}\}\subset X_{\mathrm{src}},

and

\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}:=\{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}F:F\in\mathcal{F}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}\}\subset Y_{\mathrm{prs}}.

For finite-rank clean pressure projections
P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
, set

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}:=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}}\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}.

Theorem 12.2

(Compact pressure image gives uniform projection tails)
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}\Subset Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
,

P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}g\to g\quad\text{in }Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\text{ for every }g\in Y_{\mathrm{prs}},

and

C_{P}:=\sup_{N}\left\lVert P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}<\infty.

Then

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\to 0.

Proof.

Let
K_{k}=\overline{\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}}
, compact in
Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose a finite

\varepsilon/(3(1+C_{P}))
-net
g_{1},\dots,g_{J}
for
K_{k}
. For each
center, strong convergence gives

\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{j}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\to 0.

For
N
large, all these quantities are at most
\varepsilon/3
. Given

g\in K_{k}
, choose
g_{j}
with

\left\lVert g-g_{j}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3(1+C_{P})}.

Then

\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq(1+C_{P})\left\lVert g-g_{j}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}+\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{j}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}.

Taking the supremum over
g\in\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}
proves the claim.
∎

Theorem 12.3

(Source compactness implies pressure-image compactness)
.

If
\mathcal{F}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}\Subset X_{\mathrm{src}}
, then

\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}\Subset Y_{\mathrm{prs}},

and hence
\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\to 0
.

Proof.

The map
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{prs}}:X_{\mathrm{src}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}
is bounded linear, hence
continuous. Continuous images of compact sets are compact. The last claim
follows from
Theorem

12.2
.
∎

Proposition 12.4

(Compactness criteria)
.

Each of the following hypotheses implies

\mathcal{F}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}\Subset X_{\mathrm{src}}
.

(a)

Smooth finite-window class: the selected sources are generated by a
family of smooth Navier–Stokes data with a uniform
C^{m}
or
H^{s}

bound strong enough to compactly embed into
L^{3/2}
on the fixed
window.

(b)

Sobolev compactness: for some
s>0
,

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{W^{s,3/2}(Q_{1})}<\infty.

(c)

Kolmogorov–Riesz compactness: the family is uniformly bounded in

L^{3/2}
, supported in the fixed window, and uniformly translation
continuous in
L^{3/2}
after zero extension.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from Arzela–Ascoli or Rellich–Kondrachov, depending on
whether a
C^{m}
or
H^{s}
formulation is used. Part (b) is the
Rellich–Kondrachov compact embedding

W^{s,3/2}(Q_{1})\Subset L^{3/2}(Q_{1})

on a bounded domain, applied componentwise. Part (c) is the
Kolmogorov–Riesz compactness theorem on a bounded domain after zero
extension.
∎

Proposition 12.5

(Effective projection replacement)
.

For recursive audit estimates, compactness may be replaced by the direct
assumption

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq\varepsilon_{k,N},\qquad\varepsilon_{k,N}\to 0.

For a finite weighted chain,

\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq\varepsilon_{K,N}^{\mathrm{chain}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{K}w_{k}\varepsilon_{k,N}.

Proof.

The statement is immediate from the definition of the effective projection
bound and summation with nonnegative weights.
∎

Definition 12.6

(Projection-tail transport data)
.

Let

T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}:Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}

be a bounded pressure-coordinate transport map from scale
k
to scale

k+1
. The transported pressure-image defect is a number

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}\geq 0
such that for every

g_{k+1}\in\mathcal{G}_{k+1,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}

there exists
g_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}
satisfying

\left\lVert g_{k+1}-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}.

For finite-rank projections
P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and
P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, define
the projection-transport commutator size

\omega_{k,N}^{\mathrm{proj}}:=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}}\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}.

Theorem 12.7

(Projection-tail drift under pressure-image transport)
.

Assume the projection-tail transport data in
Definition

12.6
. Set

C_{P,k+1}:=\left\lVert P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}},\qquad C_{T,k}:=\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}\to Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}.

Then

\Delta_{k+1,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq(1+C_{P,k+1})\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}+C_{T,k}\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}+\omega_{k,N}^{\mathrm{proj}}.

Proof.

Fix
g_{k+1}\in\mathcal{G}_{k+1,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}
. Choose

g_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}
with

\left\lVert g_{k+1}-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}.

Then

\displaystyle(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})(g_{k+1}-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k}.

For the first term,

\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})(g_{k+1}-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq(1+C_{P,k+1})\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}.

For the second term, add and subtract

T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{k}
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lVert(I-P_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}g_{k}-T_{k}^{\mathrm{prs}}(I-P_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,k}\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}+\omega_{k,N}^{\mathrm{proj}}.

Taking the supremum over
g_{k+1}\in\mathcal{G}_{k+1,0}^{\mathrm{NS}}
gives the
claim.
∎

Corollary 12.8

(Projection increment insertion)
.

If the projection component of the one-step ledger satisfies

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\geq(1+C_{P,k+1})\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}+C_{T,k}\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}+\omega_{k,N}^{\mathrm{proj}},

then the scale-
(k+1)
uniform projection-tail contribution is charged to
the one-step ledger. In particular, under the effective projection bound

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq\varepsilon_{k,N},

it is enough to require

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\geq(1+C_{P,k+1})\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{img}}+C_{T,k}\varepsilon_{k,N}+\omega_{k,N}^{\mathrm{proj}}.

Proof.

This is
Theorem

12.7
with the right-hand side assigned to the
projection component of
\Delta_{k}
. The effective projection version
follows by substituting

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},N}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq\varepsilon_{k,N}
.
∎

12.2
Chart visibility for reduced NS-generated packages

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{red},\mathrm{NS}}
be a reduced finite-window
NS-generated class with coordinates
x_{k}(D_{k})\in X_{N,k}
. Let

G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{int}}\subset X_{N,k},\qquad G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be the local and clean gauge subspaces, and let

\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}:X_{N,k}\to Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be the reduced clean chart. Define

V_{k}(D_{k}):=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}\bigl(\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}(D_{k}-\zeta_{k}),G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\bigr).

Theorem 12.9

(Compact quotient chart visibility)
.

Assume:

(a)

the reduced baseline unit quotient section

S_{k,0}^{\mathrm{red}}:=\{D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{red},\mathrm{NS}}:\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}})=1\}

is compact modulo the admissible local gauge;

(b)

V_{k}
is lower semicontinuous;

(c)

chart kernel-freeness holds:

V_{k}(D_{k})=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{k}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}};

(d)

quotient homogeneity holds for
V_{k}
and
\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}
.

Then

\lambda_{G,k}:=\inf_{D_{k}\in S_{k,0}^{\mathrm{red}}}V_{k}(D_{k})>0,

and

V_{k}(D_{k})\geq\lambda_{G,k}\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}})

for all reduced packages.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{G,k}=0
, compactness and lower semicontinuity give

D_{*}\in S_{k,0}^{\mathrm{red}}
with
V_{k}(D_{*})=0
. Kernel-freeness then
gives
D_{*}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}}
, contradicting

\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}(D_{*},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}})=1
. For an arbitrary package
with positive baseline distance, normalize it to the unit quotient section
and use homogeneity. The zero-distance case is trivial.
∎

Corollary 12.10

(Additive-error chart visibility)
.

Suppose an ideal chart distance
\widetilde{V}_{k}
satisfies

\widetilde{V}_{k}(D_{k})\geq\lambda_{G,k}\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}}),

and the realized chart distance satisfies

V_{k}(D_{k})+\delta_{G,k}\geq\widetilde{V}_{k}(D_{k}).

Then

V_{k}(D_{k})\geq\lambda_{G,k}\operatorname{dist}_{0,k}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}})-\delta_{G,k}.

Proof.

Subtract
\delta_{G,k}
from the ideal lower bound.
∎

Definition 12.11

(Reduced chart transport defect)
.

Let

T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be a bounded clean chart-coordinate transport map. In the conservative
reduced convention used here, assume exact clean-gauge transport:

T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset G_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

For a one-step package
D_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(D_{k})
, define

y_{k}(D_{k}):=\Theta_{\Lambda,k}^{N}(D_{k}-\zeta_{k}),\qquad y_{k+1}(D_{k+1}):=\Theta_{\Lambda,k+1}^{N}(D_{k+1}-\zeta_{k+1}),

and define the reduced chart transport defect

\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k}):=y_{k+1}(D_{k+1})-T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}y_{k}(D_{k}).

Theorem 12.12

(Reduced chart-distance drift)
.

Assume the reduced chart transport convention in

Definition

12.11
. Then

V_{k+1}(D_{k+1})\leq\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}\,V_{k}(D_{k})+\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}.

Proof.

Let
g_{k}\in G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Since

T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}g_{k}\in G_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}},

we have

\displaystyle V_{k+1}(D_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(y_{k+1}(D_{k+1}),G_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert y_{k+1}(D_{k+1})-T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}+\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}(y_{k}(D_{k})-g_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}+\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}\left\lVert y_{k}(D_{k})-g_{k}\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}}.

Taking the infimum over
g_{k}\in G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
gives the claim.
∎

Corollary 12.13

(Chart increment insertion)
.

If the chart component of the one-step ledger satisfies

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}\geq\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k})\right\rVert_{Y_{N,k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}},

then the reduced chart-transport mismatch is charged to the chart component
of the one-step increment. If the next-scale admissibility criterion also
requires the full chart distance
V_{k+1}(D_{k+1})
, it is enough to use

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}\geq\left\lVert T_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert\,V_{k}(D_{k})+\left\lVert\mathfrak{C}_{k}^{\mathrm{chart}}(D_{k})\right\rVert.

Proof.

The first statement is only the assignment of the chart commutation defect to
the chart ledger. The second statement follows from

Theorem

12.12
, with the operator norm and target-space norm
understood as in that theorem.
∎

12.3
Clean gap for a genuine detector class

A clean package at scale
k
is written as

D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}=(P_{k}^{\mathrm{act},\mathrm{cl}},P_{k}^{\mathrm{harm},\mathrm{cl}},S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},G_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}).

Define a clean detector

\displaystyle M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{O}}+\beta_{\mathrm{prs}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{prs}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\beta_{\mathrm{harm}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{harm}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{\mathrm{gate}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\beta_{\mathrm{det}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}),

with all active weights positive.

Definition 12.14

(Clean kernel)
.

The clean detector kernel is

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:=\{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0\}.

The clean kernel-free condition is

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Theorem 12.15

(Clean compact quotient gap)
.

Assume:

(a)

the clean class is stable under quotient normalization;

(b)

the clean unit quotient section

S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:=\{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=1\}

is compact;

(c)

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}
is lower semicontinuous and positively
homogeneous;

(d)

clean kernel-freeness holds.

Then

\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}:=\inf_{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}}M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})>0,

and

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})\geq\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}).

Proof.

If the infimum were zero, compactness and lower semicontinuity would give a
minimizer
D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
with

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0.

Thus
D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, so by kernel-freeness

D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. This contradicts

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=1
. Homogeneity extends the
unit-section estimate to all clean packages.
∎

Corollary 12.16

(Additive-error clean gap)
.

Let
\widetilde{M}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}
satisfy the exact clean gap with
constant
\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}
, and assume

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\Delta_{\mathrm{gap},k}\geq\widetilde{M}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}).

Then

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})\geq\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})-\Delta_{\mathrm{gap},k}.

Proof.

Combine the ideal clean gap with the comparison between realized and ideal
detectors.
∎

Proposition 12.17

(Zero-set and matrix criteria)
.

If simultaneous vanishing of

O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\quad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{prs}}^{\mathrm{cl}},\quad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{harm}}^{\mathrm{cl}},\quad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{\mathrm{cl}},\quad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{cl}}

implies
D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, then the clean detector is
kernel-free. In a reduced finite-dimensional clean detector class with
linear detector/tax map
T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and clean gauge subspace

G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, this condition is equivalent to

\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

when all clean gauge directions are detector-invisible.

Proof.

Because all detector terms are nonnegative and all active weights are
positive, vanishing of
M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})
is equivalent to
vanishing of all active clean channels. The zero-set implication therefore
gives kernel-freeness. In finite dimensions, the common zero set is the
kernel of the detector/tax map. If gauge directions are invisible, then

G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}},

and kernel-freeness is precisely the reverse inclusion.
∎

12.4
Clean gap for a genuine pressure–flux–energy detector

The preceding compact quotient theorem is an abstract clean-gap criterion. We
now record a concrete reduced realization. The purpose is not to prove
kernel-freeness in the full clean package space, but to give a finite-window

observation coordinates.

Definition 12.18

(PFE clean package)
.

At scale
k
, a reduced pressure–flux–energy clean package is

Here
p_{k}^{\mathrm{act},\mathrm{cl}}
is the clean Calderon–Zygmund pressure
coordinate,
p_{k}^{\mathrm{harm},\mathrm{cl}}
is the clean harmonic pressure coordinate,

F_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is the clean source coordinate,
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}

is the clean flux coordinate,
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{en}}
is the clean
energy/dissipation coordinate,
G_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is the clean gate/slack
coordinate, and
O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is the selected clean observation. The clean
gauge subspace is denoted by

G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}},

and the clean distance is
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})
.

Definition 12.19

(Pressure–flux–energy clean detector)
.

Fix finite-rank pressure and harmonic selectors

\Pi_{J,k}^{\mathrm{prs}},\qquad\Pi_{M,k}^{\mathrm{harm}},

and fixed finite-dimensional norms on the flux, energy, and gate spaces. The
pressure–flux–energy detector is

\displaystyle M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{O}}+\beta_{\mathrm{prs}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{prs}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\beta_{\mathrm{harm}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{harm}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{\mathrm{flux}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{flux}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\beta_{\mathrm{en}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{en}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\beta_{\mathrm{gate}}\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}),

where every active weight is positive and

\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{prs}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}):=\left\lVert(I-\Pi_{J,k}^{\mathrm{prs}})p_{k}^{\mathrm{act},\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}},\qquad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{harm}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}):=\left\lVert(I-\Pi_{M,k}^{\mathrm{harm}})p_{k}^{\mathrm{harm},\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{harm}}},

\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{flux}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}):=\left\lVert T_{\mathrm{flux}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{flux}}},\qquad\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{en}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}):=\left\lVert T_{\mathrm{en}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{en}}},

and

\mathsf{Tax}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}):=\left\lVert T_{\mathrm{gate}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{gate}}}.

Here
T_{\mathrm{flux}}
,
T_{\mathrm{en}}
, and
T_{\mathrm{gate}}
are the
fixed reduced flux, energy, and gate-channel maps. If the gate ledger is
implemented through positive gate violation coordinates,
T_{\mathrm{gate}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is
the chosen reduced coordinate whose vanishing is equivalent to vanishing of
that gate violation on the selected finite-dimensional model.
This is a normalized finite-dimensional detector. It uses genuine clean
pressure, flux, energy, and gate coordinates, but it is not asserted to be
kernel-free outside the selected reduced class.

Definition 12.20

(PFE detector matrix and kernel)
.

Let

T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}

be the combined finite-dimensional detector/tax map

T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\bigl(O_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}),T_{\mathrm{prs}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},T_{\mathrm{harm}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},T_{\mathrm{flux}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},T_{\mathrm{en}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},T_{\mathrm{gate}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\bigr),

where the components are the linear maps whose norms define the observation,
pressure, harmonic, flux, energy, and gate channels in

Definition

12.19
. The PFE zero set is

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:=\{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0\}.

The finite-dimensional PFE kernel condition is

\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Equivalently, the induced map on the quotient

\overline{T}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}/G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to Z_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}

is injective. This condition is a matrix rank condition in the chosen
reduced coordinates.

Theorem 12.21

(Pressure–flux–energy zero-set rigidity)
.

Assume all PFE detector channels are nonnegative, all active weights are
positive, and

\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Then

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Equivalently,

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\subset G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Proof.

Since every summand in
M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
is nonnegative and
every active weight is positive,
M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0

forces every active channel to vanish. Thus

T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}=0.

The matrix kernel condition gives

D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}},

which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 12.22

(Reduced status of the PFE kernel condition)
.

This is a reduced detector realization, not a full Navier–Stokes clean-gap
theorem. The condition

\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

is a finite-dimensional matrix condition in the selected clean coordinates.
It is not derived from the Navier–Stokes equations alone and is not asserted
for the full infinite-dimensional clean package geometry.

Theorem 12.23

(PFE compact quotient clean gap)
.

Let

\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}\subset Y_{N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be a reduced clean package class. Assume:

(a)

\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}
is stable under quotient
normalization;

(b)

the clean unit quotient section

S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}:=\{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}:\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=1\}

is compact;

(c)

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
is lower semicontinuous and
positively homogeneous;

(d)

the PFE zero-set rigidity theorem
Theorem

12.21

holds.

Then

\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}:=\inf_{D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}}M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})>0,

and for every
D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}
,

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}})\geq\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}).

Proof.

If the infimum were zero, compactness and lower semicontinuity would give a
minimizer
D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in S_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{PFE}}
with

M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}(D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}})=0.

By
Theorem

12.21
,
D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}}\in G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, contradicting

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(D_{*}^{\mathrm{cl}},G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}})=1
. Thus the infimum is positive on
the unit quotient section. Positive homogeneity extends the bound to
arbitrary nonzero clean packages, and the zero package case is immediate.
∎

Corollary 12.24

(Additive-error PFE clean gap)
.

Let
\widetilde{M}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}
satisfy the exact gap in

Theorem

12.23
with constant

When the PFE detector is the chosen clean detector, we set

\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}:=\mu_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}.

Proof.

Combine the exact PFE gap for
\widetilde{M}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}

with the realized/ideal detector comparison. The final convention is only a
renaming of the clean gap constant when the PFE detector supplies the clean

∎

Theorem 12.25

(Genuine clean gap on smooth reduced NS-generated packages)
.

Let

For each scale

is a realized detector satisfying the

12.5
Localized Calderon–Zygmund commutator input

This subsection records the separated-support Calderon–Zygmund input used in the pressure ledger; the underlying singular-integral estimates are standard.

Let
f=u_{i}u_{j}
and let
T=R_{i}R_{j}
. Choose

\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
with
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{3/4}
. For

x\in B_{1/2}
,

[\eta,T]f(x):=\eta(x)Tf(x)-T(\eta f)(x)=T((1-\eta)f)(x).

The source
(1-\eta)f
is supported in the annular region where

\eta\neq 1
, separated from
B_{1/2}
.

Theorem 12.26

(Localized Calderon–Zygmund commutator)
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}]f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{\eta}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4})},

with the evident interpretation when the support of
1-\eta
is contained
in a slightly larger annulus. In particular,

\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{i}u_{j})\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\eta}\left\lVert u\otimes u\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}))}.

If
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\leq M_{U}
, then

\left\lVert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}](u_{i}u_{j})\right\rVert_{Y_{\mathrm{prs}}}\leq C_{\eta}M_{U}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}((-1,0);L^{3}(B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}))}.

Proof.

The kernel of
R_{i}R_{j}
is a Calderon–Zygmund kernel
K_{ij}(x-y)
. If

x\in B_{1/2}
and
y\in\operatorname{supp}(1-\eta)\subset B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}
, then

\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq c_{\eta}>0
. Thus
K_{ij}(x-y)
is bounded and smooth on
the relevant product set. Hence

\left\lvert[\eta,R_{i}R_{j}]f(x)\right\rvert\leq C_{\eta}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}}\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert\,dy.

This gives the
L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})
bound by
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}}
, and the
same separated-kernel operator is bounded from
L^{p}
on the annulus to

L^{p}(B_{1/2})
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
, for instance by Schur’s
test. Applying this at almost every time with
p=3/2
and integrating in
time gives the space-time estimate. The final inequality follows from
Holders inequality:

\left\lVert u\otimes u\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(A)}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}(A)}\leq M_{U}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}(A)}.

∎

Corollary 12.27

(Conservative pressure-tail visibility input)
.

For pressure-admissible local data with
u\in L^{3}(Q_{1})^{3}
, the localized
pressure recomputation error on
B_{1/2}
is bounded by the annular
velocity leakage:

\mathsf{Err}_{k}^{\mathrm{proj}}\lesssim\left\lVert u\otimes u\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}((-1,0);L^{3/2}(B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}))},

and under finite amplitude by
CM_{U}\mathsf{Leak}_{\mathrm{ann}}(u)
.

Proof.

This is the
p=3/2
space-time estimate in
Theorem

12.26
, with the
right side named as the annular leakage contribution.
∎

13
Smooth Reduced NS-Generated Verification Class

The preceding section proves individual finite-window input criteria. This
section packages several of them into a single selected class generated by
smooth Navier–Stokes data and then reduced by finite-dimensional quotient and
projection conventions. The point is modest but important: the recursive
audit framework is not purely formal. Some of its structural inputs hold on a
nonempty finite-window PDE-generated class. No assertion is made for all
suitable weak solutions.

13.1
The smooth reduced class

Definition 13.1

(Smooth reduced NS-generated finite-window class)
.

Fix
0<\lambda<1
,
r_{k}=\lambda^{k}r_{0}
,
k=0,\dots,K
, a reduction
dimension
N
, a smoothness bound
M
, and an integer
m
large enough
for the compact embeddings used below. The class

\mathcal{A}_{K,N,M}^{\mathrm{sm,red}}

consists of finite chains

D_{0}(u,p)\to D_{1}(u,p)\to\cdots\to D_{K}(u,p)

generated by smooth local Navier–Stokes data
(u,p)
on
Q_{r_{0}}(z_{0})
,
with normalized fields

u_{k}(y,s)=r_{k}u(x_{0}+r_{k}y,t_{0}+r_{k}^{2}s),\qquad p_{k}(y,s)=r_{k}^{2}p(x_{0}+r_{k}y,t_{0}+r_{k}^{2}s),

and satisfying the following finite-window conditions.

(a)

Uniform smooth bound:

\sup_{0\leq k\leq K}\left(\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{C^{m}(\overline{Q_{1}})}+\left\lVert p_{k}\right\rVert_{C^{m-1}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\right)\leq M.

Equivalently, one may use Sobolev bounds

\sup_{0\leq k\leq K}\left(\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(Q_{1})}+\left\lVert p_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{s-1}(Q_{1})}\right)\leq M

with
s
large enough for the same compactness conclusions.

(b)

Pressure admissibility at each scale:

-\Delta p_{k}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u_{k,i}u_{k,j})

in distributions on
B_{1}
, modulo time-dependent constants.

(c)

Fixed cutoff convention: choose

\eta,\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}),\qquad\eta\equiv\chi\equiv 1\text{ on }B_{3/4},

and

A_{\chi}:=\operatorname{supp}\nabla\chi\cup\operatorname{supp}\Delta\chi.

(d)

Reduced coordinates: fixed finite-dimensional reduction maps

\Pi_{N,k}D_{k}=x_{k}(D_{k})\in X_{N,k}

are used for the clean, source, chart, and detector coordinates.

(e)

Synchronized quotient section:

X_{N,k}=G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{int}}\oplus H_{N,k},

and
\zeta_{k}(D_{k})
denotes the
G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{int}}
-component. The
synchronized representative is
D_{k}-\zeta_{k}(D_{k})
.

(f)

Reduced quotient compactness and chart kernel-freeness: the reduced
baseline unit section is compact modulo
G_{N,k}^{\mathrm{int}}
, the reduced
chart
V_{k}
is lower semicontinuous and homogeneous, and

V_{k}(D_{k})=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{k}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}}

on the reduced class.

(g)

Clean detector compactness and kernel-freeness: the clean unit
quotient section is compact,
M_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{comp}}
is lower
semicontinuous and positively homogeneous, and

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

In a reduced finite-dimensional detector model this may be checked by

\ker\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

for the clean detector/tax-channel matrix
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.
In the pressure–flux–energy realization below, this condition is replaced
by the concrete matrix condition

\ker T_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{PFE}}=G_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

(h)

Projection convention: the selected clean source family is either
compact in
X_{\mathrm{src}}
by the smooth bound, or the effective
projection estimate

\Delta_{k,\mathrm{proj},J}^{\mathrm{unif}}\leq\varepsilon_{k,J},\qquad\varepsilon_{k,J}\to 0

is imposed for the chosen pressure projection rank
J
.

Remark 13.2

(Slice notation)
.

The object
\mathcal{A}_{K,N,M}^{\mathrm{sm,red}}
is a class of finite chains. In
the statements below, a scale-
k
package
D_{k}
is always understood as
the
k
-th slice of some chain

D_{0}\to\cdots\to D_{K}\in\mathcal{A}_{K,N,M}^{\mathrm{sm,red}}.

This convention avoids treating the chain class itself as a single-scale
package class.

Remark 13.3

(Nonemptiness)
.

The class is nonempty for suitable choices of
M
and of the reduced
coordinate spaces. For example, smooth local Navier–Stokes solutions with
bounded
C^{m}
-norm generate finite-window packages in the coordinate part of
the class. The zero solution is always a smooth example when the selected
quotient data are compatible, and nonzero constant velocity fields with
constant pressure give nonzero smooth examples in compatible reduced models.
Nonzero smooth local solutions with nonzero strain may be used to generate
nontrivial pressure/source coordinates, provided the selected reduced model
admits them.
This nonemptiness statement says nothing about singular or merely suitable
weak solutions.

Selected input verification

Proposition 13.7

16
Defect-to-CKN Smallness and Baseline Audit Defect Extraction

This section adds the finite-window bridge from CKN-compatible quotient closeness to Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg smallness, using the standard epsilon-regularity endpoint and quantitative regularity perspective
[
5
,
7
,
15
,
19
,
21
]
. It is a conditional
defect-extraction branch: the hard comparison between the audit baseline
distance and the CKN-compatible distance is kept as an explicit hypothesis.
The section does not prove Navier–Stokes regularity, singularity exclusion,
or detector/ledger cost unsustainability.

16.1
Normalized CKN quantity

Fix
0<\vartheta<1
, for instance
\vartheta=1/2
, and write

Q_{\vartheta}:=B_{\vartheta}\times(-\vartheta^{2},0).

For
f\in L^{1}(B_{\vartheta})
, set

(f)_{B_{\vartheta}}:=\frac{1}{|B_{\vartheta}|}\int_{B_{\vartheta}}f(y)\,dy.

For normalized pressure-admissible fields
(u_{k},p_{k})
on
Q_{1}
, define

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta):=\int_{-\vartheta^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\vartheta}}|u_{k}|^{3}\,dy\,ds+\int_{-\vartheta^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\vartheta}}|p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}\,dy\,ds.

The Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg epsilon-regularity theorem
[
5
]
is used only as a
known endpoint: if
\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}
, then the
corresponding physical point is regular on a smaller cylinder. This endpoint
is not reproved here.

Remark 16.1

(Fixed-window normalization)
.

Because
\vartheta
is fixed throughout this branch, the usual scale-invariant
CKN quantity with a prefactor
\vartheta^{-2}
is equivalent to the displayed

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)
after changing the numerical epsilon. Thus

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}
in this section denotes the fixed-window
threshold corresponding to the chosen
\vartheta
.

16.2
CKN-compatible clean class and distance

Definition 16.2

(CKN-compatible clean class)
.

The class
\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
is a selected regular/clean
target class inside the scale-
k
package space. Each

\zeta\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
has associated normalized fields

(u_{\zeta},p_{\zeta})
on
Q_{1}
, with pressure defined modulo
time-dependent constants, and satisfies

\Phi_{\zeta}(\vartheta):=\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|u_{\zeta}|^{3}+\int_{-\vartheta^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{\vartheta}}|p_{\zeta}-(p_{\zeta})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{4}.

This class is not identified with the full audit admissible class unless a
separate theorem proves that the full audit admissible class implies CKN
smallness.

Definition 16.3

(CKN-compatible baseline distance)
.

The auxiliary CKN baseline distance from
D_{k}
to

\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
is

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}:=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{CKN}}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}),

where the right-hand side is defined by

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{CKN}}(D_{k},\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}):=\inf_{\zeta\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}}\left[\left\lVert u_{k}-u_{\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})}+\inf_{a=a(s)}\left\lVert p_{k}-p_{\zeta}-a(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})}\right].

The infimum over
a(s)
accounts for the pressure gauge by time-dependent
constants.

Assumption 16.4

(CKN-compatible audit domination)
.

The audit baseline distance dominates the CKN-compatible distance up to an
explicit pressure-gauge comparison error:

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}\leq C_{\mathrm{CKN/aud}}\,\delta_{k}+\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gauge}}.

Throughout this section we use Convention A. If the audit baseline metric
is defined to include the
L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})
velocity component and the
mean-free
L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})
pressure component, the domination is built
into the metric. If the audit distance is weaker, this comparison is a
separate structural input.

16.3
A sufficient metric condition for audit-to-CKN domination

The previous assumption is the main interface between the audit quotient
geometry and the CKN epsilon-regularity scale. The following proposition gives
a finite-window sufficient condition for it. It is useful when the audit
distance has an explicit field-control component, or when a reduced
representative can be cleaned into the CKN-small class with a quantified
gauge error.

Definition 16.5

(CKN field distance)
.

For two normalized field pairs
(u,p)
and
(v,q)
on
Q_{1}
, define

d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u,p),(v,q)\bigr):=\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})}+\inf_{a=a(s)}\left\lVert p-q-a(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})}.

Assumption 16.6

(Field control by the audit baseline norm)
.

Each audit representative
\zeta\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}}
used in this
comparison has associated fields
(u_{\zeta},p_{\zeta})
on
Q_{1}
, modulo
time-dependent pressure constants, and there is a finite-window constant

C_{\mathrm{fld}}
such that

d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u_{k},p_{k}),(u_{\zeta},p_{\zeta})\bigr)\leq C_{\mathrm{fld}}\lvert D_{k}-\zeta\rvert_{0,k}

for every such representative.

Assumption 16.7

(CKN cleaning of near-minimizing audit representatives)
.

For every
\eta>0
there exists

\zeta_{\eta}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{int}}
with

\lvert D_{k}-\zeta_{\eta}\rvert_{0,k}\leq\delta_{k}+\eta

and a CKN-clean representative

\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
such that

d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u_{\zeta_{\eta}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}}),(u_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}})\bigr)\leq\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gauge}}+\eta.

Proposition 16.8

(Metric realization of the CKN-compatible domination)
.

Assume
16.6
and
16.7
. Then

16.4
holds with

C_{\mathrm{CKN/aud}}=C_{\mathrm{fld}}.

Proof.

Fix
\eta>0
, and choose
\zeta_{\eta}
and

\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
as in
16.7
. By the
definition of
\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
,

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}\leq d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u_{k},p_{k}),(u_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}})\bigr).

The triangle inequality for
d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}
gives

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}\leq d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u_{k},p_{k}),(u_{\zeta_{\eta}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}})\bigr)+d_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{fld}}\bigl((u_{\zeta_{\eta}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}}),(u_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}},p_{\zeta_{\eta}^{\mathrm{CKN}}})\bigr).

For the pressure component, this triangle inequality is taken in the quotient
by time-dependent constants: if two differences are represented using gauges

a_{1}(s)
and
a_{2}(s)
, then their sum is represented using the admissible
gauge
a_{1}(s)+a_{2}(s)
, and taking infima gives the displayed estimate.
Using
16.6
and
16.7
, this is bounded by

C_{\mathrm{fld}}(\delta_{k}+\eta)+\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gauge}}+\eta.

Letting
\eta\downarrow 0
yields

\delta_{k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}\leq C_{\mathrm{fld}}\delta_{k}+\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{gauge}},

which is
16.4
with

C_{\mathrm{CKN/aud}}=C_{\mathrm{fld}}
.
∎

(Built-in CKN-compatible audit metric)
.

Suppose the audit clean target for this branch is already the

and the audit baseline norm controls the CKN field distance as in

Thus

and

In this case take

in

16.4
Quotient closeness implies CKN smallness

Lemma 16.11

(CKN smallness from CKN-compatible quotient closeness)
.

Assume there exists
\zeta\in\Gamma_{\Lambda,k}^{\mathrm{CKN}}
such that

\left\lVert u_{k}-u_{\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})}+\inf_{a=a(s)}\left\lVert p_{k}-p_{\zeta}-a(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})}\leq\tau.

Then there is a constant
C_{\vartheta}
such that

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)\leq 2\Phi_{\zeta}(\vartheta)+C_{\vartheta}(\tau^{3}+\tau^{3/2}).

In particular, since

\Phi_{\zeta}(\vartheta)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}/4
, one also has the
coarser bound

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)\leq C_{\vartheta}\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{4}+\tau^{3}+\tau^{3/2}\right].

Consequently there exists
\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}>0
such that

\tau\leq\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Proof.

Fix
\eta_{0}>0
. Since the pressure distance contains an infimum over
time-dependent constants, choose
a(s)
such that

\left\lVert u_{k}-u_{\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})}+\left\lVert p_{k}-p_{\zeta}-a(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})}\leq\tau+\eta_{0}.

For every
r>1
and every
\alpha>0
,

|A+B|^{r}\leq(1+\alpha)|A|^{r}+C_{r,\alpha}|B|^{r}.

Using this with
r=3
,
A=u_{\zeta}
, and

B=u_{k}-u_{\zeta}
, and choosing
\alpha\leq 1
, gives

\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|u_{k}|^{3}\leq 2\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|u_{\zeta}|^{3}+C\left\lVert u_{k}-u_{\zeta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}(Q_{\vartheta})}^{3}.

For pressure, write

q:=p_{k}-p_{\zeta}-a(s).

Since spatial mean subtraction removes the time-dependent pressure constant,

p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)=p_{\zeta}-(p_{\zeta})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)+q-(q)_{B_{\vartheta}}(s).

Applying the same perturbative inequality with
r=3/2
yields

\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|p_{k}-(p_{k})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}\leq 2\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|p_{\zeta}-(p_{\zeta})_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}+C\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|q-(q)_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}.

The mean-subtraction map is bounded on
L^{3/2}(B_{\vartheta})
, uniformly for
the fixed ball
B_{\vartheta}
, so

\int_{Q_{\vartheta}}|q-(q)_{B_{\vartheta}}(s)|^{3/2}\leq C_{\vartheta}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\vartheta})}^{3/2}.

Combining the velocity and pressure estimates and using the choice of

a(s)
gives

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)\leq 2\Phi_{\zeta}(\vartheta)+C_{\vartheta}\bigl((\tau+\eta_{0})^{3}+(\tau+\eta_{0})^{3/2}\bigr).

Letting
\eta_{0}\downarrow 0
proves the first estimate. The second estimate
follows from
\Phi_{\zeta}(\vartheta)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}/4
.

Choose
\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}>0
so small that

C_{\vartheta}\left(\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}^{3}+\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}^{3/2}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{2}.

Then the first estimate gives

\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)<\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{2}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}},\qquad\tau\leq\tau_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

∎

For every

whose

in its quantitative form gives

At each scale, the finite-window coefficient has the schematic form

where

is a clean compact quotient gap,

The final theorem deliberately stops at finite-window and finite-chain statements. The following items remain outside the scope of the results.

Remark 18.1

(Final positioning)
.

The one-step admissibility part supplies the left edge of the recursive audit framework: it explains how a single NS-generated package can remain admissible after restriction, rescaling, synchronization, and recomputation when every mismatch is charged. The recursive part supplies the finite-chain middle mechanism: once admissible packages and static certificates are available at each scale, the detector lower bound propagates through a weighted finite chain. Any infinite-chain or regularity-facing conclusion requires additional uniform hypotheses not proved here.
