 ABSTRACT

This final report documents the results of an evaluation of the U content in J-swipes for the International
Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). J-swipes are manufactured with inked numbers (1–30) and U
backgrounds that are considered consistent across different lots and manufacturing times. These
characteristics make them ideal for use by IAEA inspectors for pre-inspection check samples to ensure
inspectors are not cross-contaminating sites. However, recent work has found elevated U concentrations
in the inked numbers compared with the J-swipe material. To determine whether the inked numbers
correlate with the U backgrounds, triplicate U content measurements were made on Swipes 1, 2, 11, 22,
and 28. Triplicates of each swipe were processed through the standard chemistry used by the Oak Ridge
National Laboratory Network of Analytical Laboratories team and analyzed for U content by isotope
dilution via multicollector inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry. Finally, samples were made by
mass consisting of only ink areas and only cotton swipe material to determine the trace element
differences between the cotton and the ink.

 1. INTRODUCTION

J-swipes are well characterized: the natural U background of the swipes is extremely low in comparison
to the typical 10 cm × 10 cm cotton environmental swipe used by the International Atomic Energy
Agency (IAEA) during inspections. As such, the IAEA uses cotton J-swipes for multiple applications;
however, one of the most important applications of the J-swipe is a pre-inspection check (PIC) swipe
before inspectors enter a facility. The PIC provides IAEA with defensibility that the inspectors were
uncontaminated and that all results found during an inspection were from the facility under inspection and
not from the inspectors. Because the IAEA is interested in very small amounts of elements of interest
(including U), ensuring the ability to reliably detect small amounts of U contamination during analysis of
PIC samples is very important.

The J-swipes are manufactured with numbers (1–30) inked/printed directly on the center of the J-swipe.
Recent work by Bradley et. al. showed that elevated levels of U were present in the ink on J-swipes
compared with the background cotton material during microextraction. This result was further confirmed
by laser ablation inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), as shown in Figure 1 [1]. The
goal of this work is to further investigate the U content and whether the inked number correlates with the
U background.

 Figure 1. Laser ablation ICP-MS mapping of 238U of J-swipe 26 from Bradley et. al., 2024 [1].

 2. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS

Chemical processing required to isolate and purify U from the J-swipe was performed using Optima grade
HNO3, H2O2, and HF that were purchased from Fisher Scientific. ASTM Type I (18.2 MΩ∙cm) water,
generated using a Thermo Scientific Barnstead GenPure xCAD Plus water purification system, was used
to dilute the acids and prepare all other reagents. Prepacked 1 mL columns of TEVA and UTEVA resin
(50–100 μm particle size) were purchased from Eichrom Industries Inc. Certified reference materials for
U were purchased from the Institute for Reference Materials and Measurements (IRMM‐74/01 for U),
now the Joint Research Center of the European Commission or Nuclear Forensic Reference Material
Program 233U spike. J-swipes were used as received from Technol Corporation. All labware was acid-
leached in 6 mol·L−1 HCl, 8 mol·L−1 HNO3, and 18.2 MΩ∙cm H2O before use according to established
procedures [2].

Trace elements were determined using a Thermo Scientific Element II (Bremen, Germany) high-
resolution inductivity coupled plasma mass spectrometry (HR-ICP-MS) instrument. The samples were
introduced using a 50 μL/min integrated PFA nebulizer into an ESI Apex Micro. Images of the inked area
were collected using an AxisPro FC from MicroSupport. The inked areas were analyzed using ImageJ, an
open-source image processing software. Microscopy images were first imported into ImageJ, converted to
grayscale (8 b) images, and then the Threshold tool was used to identify the pixels of interest—
specifically, those containing the ink. The pixels of interest were then measured using a user-set scale
(obtained from scale bars on the microscope image), and areas were obtained for the highlighted regions.
To compare the percent of inked pixels to un-inked pixels per swipe, total areas of the circular part of the
swipe were also calculated using ImageJ.

J-swipe samples were prepared by ashing swipes in a Thermcraft tube furnace equipped with a Eurotherm
2404 temperature controller at 600°C for 12 h, per established procedures [3-5]. The resulting ash residue
was digested in 3.6 mol∙L−1 HNO3–2.5 mol∙L−1 HF (11 mL) for 48 h at 110°C, followed by 30%
H2O2 (1 mL) and 4 mol∙L−1 HNO3 (2 mL) for 48 h at 50°C with dry-down steps between reagent
additions. Samples were brought up in 1 mL 4 mol∙L−1 HNO3 before being spiked with approximately
200 pg of 233U. Samples were heated at 60°C, dried down, and brought up in 2 mL 3 mol∙L−1 HNO3
before separation. Separation was done as previously described [4-6]. Briefly, the sample was loaded onto
preconditioned, stacked, 1 mL TEVA/UTEVA cartridges on a vacuum box system. The resin was rinsed,
the columns were separated and rinsed again, and the U was eluted from the UTEVA cartridge and dried
down. Any column organics were treated with 50 µL of 1:1 8 mol∙L−1 HNO3:H2O2 (30%), heated at 60°C
for 30 m, dried down, and brought up in 1.5 mL of 2% HNO3 for analysis by MC-ICP-MS.

Spiked samples were analyzed using a Thermo Scientific Neptune Plus MC-ICP-MS instrument in the
Chemical and Isotopic Mass Spectrometry Group at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. The measurements
were made using a self-aspirating, 50 µL∙min−1 PFA nebulizer run through an Apex Ω desolvating system
equipped with a Jet sample and X skimmer cone combination. The spiked samples were analyzed using a
method consisting of 233U, 235U, and 238U measured using 1011 Ω resistance amplifiers set on cups L2,
center, and H2. Two standards were analyzed during this session: IRMM-74/01 used to correct for
instrumental fractionation and IRMM-74/02 run as quality controls (QCs). Samples and QCs were
preceded by a 2% HNO3 blank and were bracketed by the IRMM-74/01 standard every two samples or
QCs. The solutions were analyzed for 120 s with 8 s integrations, for a total of 15 cycles. The data are
pulled from the instrument and reduced offline, correcting for blank contributions and instrumental
fractionation using a direct comparison approach to IRMM-74/01.

3.2 SWIPE ASHING

During the ashing of the swipes, batches were made in the following way, using one process blank and
Swipes 1, 2, 11, 22, 28, and 30 (Figure 5). One replicate of each sample was ashed in the batch, and three
batches were ashed in total, to provide triplicate replicates of each swipe and the process blank. When the
furnace tubes were removed from the tube furnace, the inked number did not appear to have been fully
ashed and was separated from the swipe material, as shown in Figure 6. However, upon addition of
4 mol∙L−1 HNO3, no visible solids remained. After complete digestion, like the screening method used for
IAEA samples, replicate measurements of Swipe 30 were analyzed by HR-ICP-MS for trace elements.
The remaining five swipe samples and process blanks were spiked and separated for U assay by isotope
dilution mass spectrometry (IDMS).

 Figure 5. Image of a batch of J-swipes in furnace tubes before ashing.

 Figure 6. Swipe 28 after ashing.

Finally, a blank swipe was weighed, and samples were created by mass to simulate the cotton and ink
samples, as shown in Figure 7. The cotton portion was created by combining blank cotton portions of
swipes to the same mass as a full swipe. Inked samples were created by combining the ink portion from
25 swipes and from 46 swipes along with some of the remaining cotton from the swipe to create a sample
with the same mass as the blank swipe. These samples were also ashed, digested, and processed the same
as the triplicate swipes.

3.3 TRACE ELEMENT ANALYSIS

Typically, a 1% screen is run on all IAEA samples to determine trace element content to ensure adequate
separation procedures are followed to remove any isobaric or molecular interferences. Because J-swipes
are known to have a very small background, Swipe 30 was ashed exclusively to examine the trace
elements present in the swipe. After complete digestion, the swipes were dissolved in 1.5 mL of 2%
HNO3 and submitted for analysis by HR-ICP-MS for trace elements. Specifically, the following elements
were examined due to their potential interference with typical IAEA measurements: Zr, Mo, Ru, Pd, Cd,
Ta, W, Os, Pt, Au, Hg, Tl, Pb, Bi, Th, Np, U, Pu, and Am. Table 1 lists the total amount of each element
that was measured at >1 ng total in the sample. These five elements are commonly found in different inks.

3.5 COTTON AND INK SAMPLES

After ashing and digestion of the cotton and ink samples that were made by mass from the inked area and
uninked portions of multiple J-swipes, the samples were analyzed by HR-ICP-MS by external calibration.
The samples were prepared in duplicate, Sample 1 was not separated whereas Sample 2 was separated.
The unseparated samples showed significant amounts of Zr and Pb, which were present in both the cotton
and ink samples; Mo, W, and Bi, which are commonly found in ink, were present in the ink samples in
much greater quantities than the cotton sample, as shown in Figure 9. As expected, the separated samples
showed the removal of all trace elements.

 4. CONCLUSION

This study examined the small amounts of U observed in J-swipe blanks and found 0.132 –0.159 ng U,
with an average of 0.144 ng, per swipe. Although variation was observed in the total amount of U, it was
not well correlated with the swipe numbers because the inked numbers have different sizes, depending on
the number. In general, the ink corresponds to approximately 10% of the total U content for the full
swipe. However, the ink corresponds to significantly elevated interferences (Mo, W, Pb) that are present
in the ink and not the cotton swipe. Because W and Pb have molecular and polyatomic interferences with
U and Pu measurements, removing the ink from the J-swipes has the potential to improve the ultratrace
detection of U and Pu in PIC samples.
