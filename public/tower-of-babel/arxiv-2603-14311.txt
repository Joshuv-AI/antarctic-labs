Non-Resonant Boundary Time Crystals from Quantum Synchronization Breakdown
Jun Wang, Shu Yang, Zeqing Wang, Ran Qi, Haiping Hu, Weidong Li, Jianwen Jie

Abstract
Quantum synchronization (QS) in dissipative systems is often inferred from smooth phase locking, leaving open whether its breakdown constitutes a genuine nonequilibrium transition. Here we introduce a Liouvillian framework that classifies driven–dissipative dynamics by the structure of the undriven dissipative background and show that QS breaks down via a Hopf-type dynamical phase transition into a boundary time crystal (BTC). The character of this transition is determined by the background attractor: systems with a self-sustained oscillator (SSO) support robust non-resonant BTCs, whereas those with a polar fixed point (PFP) sustain BTCs only at resonance and lose them under detuning. We identify sharp dynamical and spectral signatures of the QS–BTC transition and thereby establish, within U(1)-symmetric collective-spin Lindbladians driven by a single coherent tone, a background-based allowed/forbidden criterion that unifies QS, its breakdown, and time-crystalline order within a single Liouvillian framework.

Main Results—

QS breakdown is a Hopf-type DPT into a BTC within U(1)-symmetric collective-spin Lindbladians driven by a single coherent tone.

A unified understanding follows by rewriting Eqs. (

The resulting rapidly oscillating but biased thrust acquires a finite time average,

As a result, the resonant BTC melts to a stationary fixed point or a phase-locked QS state.
On a PFP background, the divergent amplification near the stable pole renders the dynamics akin to a heavily damped pendulum,
such that the phase-locked fixed point remains robust over a wide detuning range.
By contrast, on an SSO background the bounded phase-selecting torque yields a

Conclusion.—
We have shown that QS breakdown in driven-dissipative collective-spin systems constitutes a genuine Hopf-type dynamical phase transition in the thermodynamic limit, rather than a smooth crossover. By classifying Liouvillian dynamics according to the structure of their undriven background attractors, we identify a structural principle: non-resonant BTCs emerge only from self-sustained oscillator (SSO) backgrounds and are generically excluded for polar fixed point (PFP) backgrounds. Persistent collective oscillations thus reflect a property of the underlying Liouvillian background structure rather than a model-dependent feature.

The Hamiltonian part is straightforward:

\displaystyle i[\hat{H},\hat{S}_{+}]

\displaystyle=i\Delta[\hat{S}_{z},\hat{S}_{+}]-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}[\hat{S}_{x},\hat{S}_{+}]

\displaystyle=i\Delta\hat{S}_{+}-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}[\hat{S}_{-},\hat{S}_{+}]=i\Delta\hat{S}_{+}+\frac{i\epsilon}{2}\hat{S}_{z}.

(S6)

For the linear-gain channel
\hat{L}_{+}
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\hat{L}_{+}^{\dagger}[\hat{S}_{+},\hat{L}_{+}]+[\hat{L}_{+}^{\dagger},\hat{S}_{+}]\hat{L}_{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{S}\Big(\hat{S}_{-}[\hat{S}_{+},\hat{S}_{+}]+[\hat{S}_{-},\hat{S}_{+}]\hat{S}_{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{S}\Big(0-2\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\Big)=-\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{S}\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}.

(S7)

For the nonlinear-loss channel
\hat{L}_{-}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{-}/S^{3}}\,\hat{S}_{-}\hat{S}_{z}
(so that
\hat{L}_{-}^{\dagger}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{-}/S^{3}}\,\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}
),
a direct but lengthy commutator algebra yields an exact operator identity of the form

\frac{1}{2}\Big(\hat{L}_{-}^{\dagger}[\hat{S}_{+},\hat{L}_{-}]+[\hat{L}_{-}^{\dagger},\hat{S}_{+}]\hat{L}_{-}\Big)=\frac{\Gamma_{-}}{2S^{3}}\Big(2\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}^{2}+2\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}-\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}\Big),

(S8)

where we have used Eq. (
S5
) repeatedly to reorder products.

Putting Eqs. (
S6
)–(
S8
) together gives

\dot{\hat{S}}_{+}=i\Delta\hat{S}_{+}+\frac{i\epsilon}{2}\hat{S}_{z}-\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{S}\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}+\frac{\Gamma_{-}}{2S^{3}}\Big(2\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}^{2}+2\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}-\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}\Big).

(S9)

Similarly, for
\hat{X}=\hat{S}_{z}
one finds

\dot{\hat{S}}_{z}=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{S}_{y}+\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{S}\hat{S}_{-}\hat{S}_{+}-\frac{\Gamma_{-}}{S^{3}}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}\hat{S}_{z}^{2},

(S10)

where the Hamiltonian part gives
i[\hat{H},\hat{S}_{z}]=-(\epsilon/2)\hat{S}_{y}
and the two dissipators follow from Eq. (
S2
).

Step 2: mean-field closure and large-
S
scaling.

We introduce the normalized magnetization components

m_{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\langle\hat{S}_{\alpha}\rangle}{S}\qquad(\alpha=x,y,z),\qquad m_{\pm}\equiv\frac{\langle\hat{S}_{\pm}\rangle}{S}=m_{x}\pm im_{y}.

(S11)

In the thermodynamic (large-
S
) limit we apply a standard mean-field factorization for products of collective operators,

\langle\hat{A}\hat{B}\rangle\approx\langle\hat{A}\rangle\langle\hat{B}\rangle,\qquad\langle\hat{A}\hat{B}\hat{C}\rangle\approx\langle\hat{A}\rangle\langle\hat{B}\rangle\langle\hat{C}\rangle,

(S12)

and, crucially, we keep only the leading contributions in
S
.
This matters for the nonlinear-loss term in Eq. (
S9
):

•

\langle\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}^{2}\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(S^{4})
, hence it survives after the prefactor
\Gamma_{-}/S^{3}
and the final division by
S
.

•

\langle\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{z}\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(S^{3})
and

\langle\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(S^{3})
, hence they contribute only
\mathcal{O}(1/S)
to
\dot{m}_{+}
and are negligible as
S\to\infty
.

Therefore, at leading order,

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{+}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{S}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{S}_{+}\rangle\simeq i\Delta m_{+}+\frac{i\epsilon}{2}m_{z}-\Gamma_{+}m_{z}m_{+}+\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{3}m_{+}.

(S13)

In the same spirit, from Eq. (
S10
) we use
\langle\hat{S}_{-}\hat{S}_{+}\rangle/S^{2}\simeq m_{-}m_{+}=m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2}
and

\langle\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}\hat{S}_{z}^{2}\rangle/S^{4}\simeq(m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2})m_{z}^{2}
to obtain

\frac{dm_{z}}{dt}\simeq-\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{y}+\Gamma_{+}(m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2})-\Gamma_{-}(m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2})m_{z}^{2}.

(S14)

Finally, using the Bloch-sphere constraint (proven below)
m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2}=1-m_{z}^{2}
in the large-
S
mean-field dynamics,
Eq. (
S14
) becomes

\frac{dm_{z}}{dt}=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{y}+(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})(1-m_{z}^{2}).

(S15)

Step 3: equations for
m_{x},m_{y},m_{z}
.

Taking real and imaginary parts of Eq. (
S13
) (recall
m_{+}=m_{x}+im_{y}
) gives the closed nonlinear mean-field equations

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{x}

\displaystyle=-\Delta m_{y}-(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})m_{x}m_{z},

(S16)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{y}

\displaystyle=\Delta m_{x}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{z}-(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})m_{y}m_{z},

(S17)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{y}+(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})(1-m_{z}^{2}).

(S18)

These are precisely Eqs. (2) in the main text (with
\Gamma_{\pm}\equiv\Gamma_{\pm}
there, if one uses
\Gamma
notation).

I.2
Dynamical equation for the spin-length

The mean-field flow preserves the Bloch-sphere constraint.
Defining
\vec{m}=(m_{x},m_{y},m_{z})
, we compute

\frac{d}{dt}|\vec{m}|^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}(m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2}+m_{z}^{2})=2m_{x}\dot{m}_{x}+2m_{y}\dot{m}_{y}+2m_{z}\dot{m}_{z}.

(S19)

Substituting Eqs. (
S16
)–(
S18
) and simplifying, the Hamiltonian terms cancel identically, and we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}|\vec{m}|^{2}=2m_{z}\big(|\vec{m}|^{2}-1\big)\big(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2}\big).

(S20)

Hence, if
|\vec{m}(0)|^{2}=1
, then Eq. (
S20
) implies
|\vec{m}(t)|^{2}=1
for all times, which provides a convenient numerical consistency check when integrating the mean-field equations.

II
Resonant threshold from mean-field linear stability

Here we derive the critical driving strength
\epsilon_{c}
under resonant driving,

\Delta=\omega_{0}-\omega_{d}=0
, for the mean-field equations (main text, Eq. (2)):

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{x}

\displaystyle=-(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})m_{x}m_{z},

(S21)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{z}-(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})m_{y}m_{z},

(S22)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{y}+(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2})(1-m_{z}^{2}).

(S23)

For
\Delta=0
, the manifold
m_{x}=0
is invariant since
\dot{m}_{x}\propto m_{x}
.
Therefore the stability/threshold can be obtained from the reduced
(m_{y},m_{z})
dynamics.

Stationary (QS) solution.

Let
(m_{y}^{\ast},m_{z}^{\ast})
denote a stationary point with
m_{z}^{\ast}\neq 0
.
From
\dot{m}_{y}=0
we obtain

0=\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{z}^{\ast}-(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2})m_{y}^{\ast}m_{z}^{\ast}\quad\Rightarrow\quad m_{y}^{\ast}=\frac{\epsilon}{2\left(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2}\right)}.

(S24)

Substituting Eq. (
S24
) into
\dot{m}_{z}=0
yields a single self-consistency condition for
m_{z}^{\ast}
:

\left(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1-(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2}\right)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}.

(S25)

Thus, a real stationary (QS) solution exists only if the right-hand side does not exceed the maximum of the left-hand side over
(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2}\in[0,1]
.

Resonant critical drive for Fig. 3 parameters.

For Fig. 3 we use
\Gamma_{+}=1
and
\Gamma_{-}=2
(and
\Delta=0
).
Writing
x\equiv(m_{z}^{\ast})^{2}\in[0,1]
, Eq. (
S25
) becomes

(1-2x)^{2}(1-x)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}.

(S26)

Define
g(x)=(1-2x)^{2}(1-x)
. Its derivative is

g^{\prime}(x)=-(2x-1)(6x-5),

(S27)

so the only interior extrema are at
x=1/2
and
x=5/6
, where
g(1/2)=0
and
g(5/6)=2/27
.
On the boundaries,
g(0)=1
and
g(1)=0
.
Therefore the global maximum is
g_{\max}=1
attained at
x=0
.
Equation (
S26
) admits a real stationary solution only if

\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\leq g_{\max}=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{c}=2.

(S28)

Hence, for
\Gamma_{+}=1
the mean-field stability threshold is

\epsilon_{c}=2.0,

(S29)

consistent with the linear-stability analysis reported in Ref. [54] and with the finite-size scaling extracted in Fig. 3.

Interpretation.

For
\epsilon<\epsilon_{c}
, Eqs. (
S22
)–(
S23
) admit a stationary solution

(m_{y}^{\ast},m_{z}^{\ast})
satisfying Eq. (
S25
);
this fixed point is the mean-field attractor corresponding to the QS phase.
When
\epsilon>\epsilon_{c}
, the stationary solution ceases to exist, and the dynamics is necessarily attracted to a time-periodic mean-field limit cycle, corresponding to the BTC phase in the thermodynamic limit.

III
Physical picture from the angular equations: pendulum versus gyroscope

III.1
Derivation of the angular equations on the Bloch sphere

The mean-field dynamics in the rotating frame is conveniently expressed in terms of the normalized magnetization

\bm{m}=(m_{x},m_{y},m_{z})
on the Bloch sphere,
|\bm{m}|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}m_{\alpha}^{2}=1
.
Starting from Eq. (2) in the main text,

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{x}

\displaystyle=-\Delta m_{y}-\bigl(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2}\bigr)m_{x}m_{z},

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{y}

\displaystyle=\Delta m_{x}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{z}-\bigl(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2}\bigr)m_{y}m_{z},

(S30)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{y}+\bigl(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}m_{z}^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-m_{z}^{2}\bigr),

we introduce spherical coordinates on the Bloch sphere,

m_{x}=\sin\theta\cos\phi,\qquad m_{y}=\sin\theta\sin\phi,\qquad m_{z}=\cos\theta,

(S31)

with
\theta\in[0,\pi]
the polar (latitude) angle and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
the azimuthal phase.

Latitude equation.

From
m_{z}=\cos\theta
one has
\dot{m}_{z}=-\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}
.
Using
1-m_{z}^{2}=\sin^{2}\theta
and
m_{y}=\sin\theta\sin\phi
in the
m_{z}
equation yields

\dot{\theta}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sin\phi-\sin\theta\bigl(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}\cos^{2}\theta\bigr),

(S32)

valid away from the poles (
\sin\theta\neq 0
).

Azimuth equation and exact cancellation of dissipation.

A standard identity gives
m_{x}\dot{m}_{y}-m_{y}\dot{m}_{x}=\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{\phi}
.
Applying Eqs. (
S30
) one finds

m_{x}\dot{m}_{y}-m_{y}\dot{m}_{x}=\Delta(m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2})+\frac{\epsilon}{2}m_{x}m_{z},

(S33)

where the dissipative terms cancel exactly.
With
m_{x}^{2}+m_{y}^{2}=\sin^{2}\theta
and
m_{x}m_{z}=\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi
, this yields

\dot{\phi}=\Delta+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cot\theta\,\cos\phi,

(S34)

again for
\sin\theta\neq 0
.
Equations (
S32
) and (
S34
) are the angular form discussed in the physics picture section in the main text.

III.2
Meaning of each term and the unified physical picture

Equations (
S32
) and (
S34
) expose a transparent control logic by separating latitude (amplitude-like) and azimuth (phase-like) dynamics while keeping their feedback explicit.

Dissipation: nonlinear restoring force and the background attractor.

The dissipative part of Eq. (
S32
),

-\sin\theta(\Gamma_{+}-\Gamma_{-}\cos^{2}\theta)
,
acts as a nonlinear latitudinal restoring force.
In the undriven limit (
\epsilon=0
), it generates either polar fixed points (PFPs) at the north/south poles or a finite-tilt self-sustained oscillator (SSO) background satisfying

\cos\theta_{0}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{+}/\Gamma_{-}}
for
\Gamma_{+}<\Gamma_{-}
.
In this sense, dissipation selects the
background
on which the drive and detuning act.

Drive: phase-selecting torque with latitude amplification.

The coherent drive enters Eq. (
S32
) as a latitudinal thrust
(\epsilon/2)\sin\phi
,
and enters Eq. (
S34
) as a phase-selecting torque
(\epsilon/2)\cot\theta\cos\phi
.
The prefactor
\cot\theta
provides a strong
latitude amplification
:
it diverges near a PFP (
\theta\to 0
or
\pi
) but remains bounded on an SSO (
\theta\simeq\theta_{0}
).
This is the geometric reason why the same drive has qualitatively different effects on different backgrounds.

Resonant drive: from phase pinning to phase self-trapping and resonant BTC.

On resonance (
\Delta=0
), the azimuthal motion is activated solely by the drive torque.
For weak driving, the phase-selecting term tends to favor
\cos\phi\simeq 0
, i.e.,
\phi
near
\pi/2
(mod
\pi
),
which maximizes the thrust
(\epsilon/2)\sin\phi
in Eq. (
S32
).
This allows the thrust to balance the dissipative restoring force and stabilize a nontrivial dynamical state.

At stronger drive, this simple balance generically breaks down:
because the torque in Eq. (
S34
) depends on
\cot\theta
,
the coupled
(\theta,\phi)
dynamics generates an
asymmetric effective phase potential
in which
\phi
becomes
self-trapped
(bounded oscillations around a biased phase).
The thrust
(\epsilon/2)\sin\phi(t)
then becomes rapidly oscillating but biased, acquiring a finite time average.
This nonzero averaged thrust supports a stable limit cycle and yields a
resonant BTC
for both backgrounds considered.

Detuning: constant precession torque and the fate of phase self-trapping.

Detuning adds the constant drift
\Delta
in Eq. (
S34
), i.e., a uniform precession torque in the rotating frame.
Over long timescales this drift tends to make
\phi
explore
[0,2\pi)
, thereby washing out
\phi
–
\theta
correlations and suppressing the effective latitudinal thrust.
In this sense, detuning destroys phase self-trapping and causes the resonant BTC to
melt
into a stationary attractor (a fixed point / phase-locked QS state).

Pendulum versus gyroscope: why the outcome depends on the background.

On a PFP background, the divergent amplification
\cot\theta
near the stable pole makes the response akin to a heavily damped pendulum:
the phase-selecting torque is extremely strong close to the pole, and the phase-locked fixed point remains robust over a wide detuning range.

By contrast, on an SSO background the amplification is bounded (
\cot\theta_{0}<\infty
) and the undriven phase is intrinsically free.
The same detuning term
\Delta
can therefore destabilize the locked phase and induce sustained azimuthal motion.
Through the thrust term in Eq. (
S32
), this azimuthal motion establishes a new self-consistent dynamical balance in
\theta
,
giving rise to a
non-resonant BTC
.
This angular-equation picture is fully consistent with the structural distinction between PFP and SSO backgrounds emphasized in the main text.
