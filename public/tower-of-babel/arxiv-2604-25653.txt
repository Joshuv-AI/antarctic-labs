On numerical semigroups with embedding dimension four
Kazimierz Chomicz

Abstract
We develop a geometric procedure for finding the Apéry set of any numerical semigroup with embedding dimension four. We use this method to find the Frobenius numbers, genera, Betti elements, minimal presentations and catenary degrees of numerical semigroups generated by four consecutive squares and four consecutive triangular numbers.

A subset of
\mathbb{N}
that contains 0, is closed under addition and is missing only finitely many positive integers is called a
numerical semigroup
. The Frobenius problem, also known as the Coin problem or the Chicken McNugget problem, concerns finding the largest integer not in a numerical semigroup. This integer is called the
Frobenius number
and is denoted
F(S)
, for the numerical semigroup
S
.

For a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
, we let
\langle A\rangle
denote the set of all linear combinations of elements in
A
with nonnegative coefficients. It is well known that
\langle A\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(A)=1
(for instance, see
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
). We define the integer e
(S)
, called the
embedding dimension
, as the cardinality of the unique set of minimal generators of a numerical semigroup
S
.

An explicit formula for
F(S)
is well known in the case of a numerical semigroup with two generators (i.e. e
(S)=2
):

F(\langle a,b\rangle)=ab-a-b,

obtained by Sylvester in 1883 (see
[
33
]
). However, for e
(S)\geq 3
, the Frobenius problem remains open. Indeed, Curtis showed in
[
9
]
that it is impossible to find a polynomial formula (that is, a finite set of polynomials) to compute the Frobenius number if e
(S)=3
. Moreover, Ramírez-Alfonsín
[
24
]
proved that the question is NP-hard. Despite this, for the case of three generators, a lot of useful results were proved, with Johnson
[
16
]
, and Brauer and Shockley
[
6
]
being among the fundamental ones (for more information we refer the reader to
[
25
]
).

Many formulas for special types of numerical semigroups have also been established, such as those generated by arithmetic
[
7
,
26
]
or almost arithmetic sequences
[
20
,
32
]
, geometric sequences
[
23
]
, generated by three consecutive squares or cubes
[
11
,
19
]
, by three Fibonacci numbers
[
21
]
, by squares of three consecutive Fibonacci numbers
[
27
]
, by triangular and tetrahedral numbers
[
28
]
, and for many others.

Numerical semigroups generated by infinite sequences were also considered, including infinite sequences of squares
[
22
]
, of other
k
th powers
[
4
]
, and infinite quadratic sequences
[
14
]
. See also the references in
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
.

In this paper, we will be interested in finding the Frobenius numbers of numerical semigroups generated by four consecutive squares and four consecutive triangular numbers. The corresponding problems for three generators have been solved in
[
11
,
19
]
and
[
28
]
.

In Section
2
, we propose a method for finding the Apéry set of any numerical semigroup with embedding dimension four, from which the Frobenius number can be obtained. This is the main result of this paper. There, we also give a result about the uniqueness of
L
-shapes, which is related to the paper
[
1
]
.

In Section
3
, we build on the results of the previous section to find the catenary degree. It plays a crucial role in factorization theory, and recently in
[
12
]
, it was shown that there is no polynomial formula for the catenary degree, which is valid for all numerical semigroups (similarly to the Frobenius number
[
9
]
). In the process of finding the catenary degree, we will find the set of Betti elements and a minimal presentation.
For more information about this topic, we refer the reader to
[
5
, Chapter 6]
.

In Sections
4
and
5
, mostly as a showcase, we apply the tools developed throughout the previous sections to numerical semigroups generated by four consecutive squares and four consecutive triangular numbers. Similar results for a different family of numerical semigroups with embedding dimension four were obtained in
[
28
]
. In particular, the authors studied the Frobenius numbers, Apéry sets, Betti elements, and minimal presentations of numerical semigroups generated by four consecutive tetrahedral numbers. However, the proofs rely heavily on the property
-
defined there
-
of being telescopic, and cannot be applied for most numerical semigroups. Specifically, they cannot be applied for four consecutive triangular numbers, which is possible using our methods.

We finish the paper with remarks which may stimulate further research.

Geometric procedure

2.1
Preliminaries

For a numerical semigroup
S
and an element
a\in S
, we define the
Apéry set
of
S
with respect to
a
as

\text{Ap}(S,a)=\{s\in S\mid s-a\notin S\}

(see
[
3
]
). We have
\#\text{Ap}(S,a)=a
, because the set contains exactly one element from each residue class modulo
a
. This set is of interest to us because from the Apéry set one can obtain several semigroup invariants. Specifically,
F(S)=\max\{\text{Ap}(S,a)\}-a
, and
G(S)=\frac{1}{a}\left(\sum_{s\in\text{Ap}(S,a)}s\right)-\frac{a-1}{2}
, where
G(S)
is the cardinality of the set
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
and is called the
genus
(see
[
32
]
).

A geometric procedure for calculating the Frobenius number for numerical semigroups with embedding dimension three has been well established (see
[
6
]
). For a numerical semigroup
\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
with
\gcd(d_{i},d_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
, it involves finding the smallest positive integers
a_{11},a_{22},a_{33}
such that:

\displaystyle a_{11}d_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{12}d_{2}+a_{13}d_{3},

\displaystyle a_{22}d_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{21}d_{1}+a_{23}d_{3},

\displaystyle a_{33}d_{3}

\displaystyle=a_{31}d_{1}+a_{32}d_{2},

for some nonnegative integers
a_{12},a_{13},a_{21},a_{23},a_{31},a_{32}
.
The above equations are called the
minimal relations
of
\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
. The Frobenius number is then given by the formula

F(\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle)=\max\{(a_{22}-1)d_{2}+(a_{13}-1)d_{3},(a_{12}-1)d_{2}+(a_{33}-1)d_{3}\}-d_{1}.

(1)

The geometric nature of the above identity can be seen in the following sketch of a proof.

Consider a collection of unit squares in the first quadrant, placed against the
x
- and
y
-axes.
We label the square, whose vertex closest to the origin has the coordinates
(i,j)
, with the value
id_{2}+jd_{3}
. We then find the points
(a_{22},0)
,
(0,a_{33})
,
(a_{12},a_{13})
, and remove the regions
\{x>a_{22},y>0\}
,
\{x>0,y>a_{33}\}
,
\{x>a_{12},y>a_{13}\}
(see Figure

).

(also, see

Killingbergtrø, in
[
18
]
, considered a possible generalization of the above procedure to numerical semigroups with embedding dimension four. Among other results, he computed the Frobenius number of a randomly chosen numerical semigroup
\langle 103,133,165,228\rangle
, using a geometric procedure, to find the Apéry set with respect to 103. His method for a semigroup
\langle d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
involves constructing a three-dimensional figure (Figure
2
), consisting of a collection of unit cubes in the first octant, placed against the
xy
-,
yz
-, and
zx
-

(i,j,k)
, is labeled with the value
id_{1}+jd_{2}+kd_{3}
. Similarly to the method for three generators, we then find some special set of points and for each of them having coordinates
(a,b,c)
, we remove the region
\{x>a,y>b,z>c\}
. We do this for the set of labels of the resulting figure to end up being the Apéry set with respect to
d_{0}
. We will often refer to this set as simply the Apéry set for short.

The set of points used by Killingbergtrø (and its counterpart for other numerical semigroups) is in general not sufficient to produce the Apéry set. We propose a modified approach that works for any numerical semigroup with embedding dimension four.

2.2
Procedure

Let
\langle d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup with embedding dimension four.

Consider unit cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Each unit cube
[i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]\times[k,k+1]\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
has natural coordinates
(i,j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
and is labeled with the value
id_{1}+jd_{2}+kd_{3}
. We will refer to the collection of all such labeled cubes, as
the initial collection of cubes
. We denote the unit cube
[i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]\times[k,k+1]
by
[[i,j,k]]
.

The remainder of this section develops a method for finding the Apéry set of
\langle d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
with respect to
d_{0}
. Theorem
2.2
provides the fundamental result that makes this possible, although it is not directly practical on its own and the idea is not new (see
[
17
]
). Propositions
2.5
and
2.8
are the key results that make our method innovative and useful in practice. We also include further results
in Propositions
2.9
and
2.10
.

Define the minimal relation of
d_{0}
:

a_{00}d_{0}=a_{01}d_{1}+a_{02}d_{2}+a_{03}d_{3},

(2)

where
a_{00}
is the smallest positive integer such that there exist
a_{01},a_{02},a_{03}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying (
2
). Furthermore, for every triple
(a_{01},a_{02},a_{03})\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, such that (
2
) is satisfied, we construct the point

P_{0}=(a_{01},a_{02},a_{03}).

(3)

All such points satisfy that
xd_{1}+yd_{2}+zd_{3}
is a positive multiple of
d_{0}

-
here, and for the rest of this section,
x,y,z
will always denote the
x
-,
y
- and
z
- coordinates of a point in question.

Next, define the remaining minimal relations of
\langle d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
:

\displaystyle a_{11}d_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{10}d_{0}+a_{12}d_{2}+a_{13}d_{3},

\displaystyle a_{22}d_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{20}d_{0}+a_{21}d_{1}+a_{23}d_{3},

\displaystyle a_{33}d_{3}

\displaystyle=a_{30}d_{0}+a_{31}d_{1}+a_{32}d_{2},

where
a_{11},a_{22},a_{33}
are the smallest positive integers such that the above is true for some nonnegative integers
a_{ij}
. Using the above coefficients, we construct the points

P_{1}=(a_{11},-a_{12},-a_{13}),\ P_{2}=(-a_{21},a_{22},-a_{23}),\ P_{3}=(-a_{31},-a_{32},a_{33}),

(4)

each of which satisfies that
xd_{1}+yd_{2}+zd_{3}
is a nonnegative multiple of
d_{0}
.

Additionally, we also define the relations:

\displaystyle b_{11}d_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{10}d_{0}+b_{12}d_{2}+b_{13}d_{3},

\displaystyle b_{22}d_{2}

\displaystyle=b_{20}d_{0}+b_{21}d_{1}+b_{23}d_{3},

\displaystyle b_{33}d_{3}

\displaystyle=b_{30}d_{0}+b_{31}d_{1}+b_{32}d_{2},

where
b_{11},b_{22},b_{33}
are the smallest positive integers such that the above is true for some nonnegative integers
b_{ij}
, and specifically with
b_{10},b_{20},b_{30}
being positive. We call these the
d_{0}
-positive minimal relations
of
\langle d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
. Using the above coefficients, we construct the points

P^{+}_{1}=(b_{11},-b_{12},-b_{13}),\ P^{+}_{2}=(-b_{21},b_{22},-b_{23}),\ P^{+}_{3}=(-b_{31},-b_{32},b_{33}),

(5)

each of which satisfies that
xd_{1}+yd_{2}+zd_{3}
is a positive multiple of
d_{0}
.

Note that in the minimal relations (resp.
d_{0}
-positive minimal relations)
a_{ii}
(resp.
b_{ii}
) is unique, however
a_{ij}
(resp.
b_{ij}
) for
i\neq j
is not necessarily unique.

From this point on, for a point
P=(a,b,c)
, we will call the region
\{x>a,y>b,z>c\}
,
the region associated to

P
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Deleting from the initial collection of cubes the region associated to a point
P=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, satisfying
\mu_{1}d_{1}+\mu_{2}d_{2}+\mu_{3}d_{3}=\mu d_{0}
for some positive integer
\mu
, does not remove any cube labeled with an element of the Apéry set with respect to
d_{0}
.

Proof.

Note that the Apéry set with respect to
d_{0}
is the set of the smallest positive integers from each residue class modulo
d_{0}
that are in the numerical semigroup
\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
. Suppose, to the contrary, that for some residue
r\in\{0,1,\dots,d_{0}-1\}
, a cube labeled with the smallest number
\lambda d_{0}+r
(
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
) that is in
\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
, has been deleted. Let
[[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}]]
be the deleted cube, which was labeled with
\lambda d_{0}+r
. Since it is deleted by the region associated to
P
, we have
\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq\mu_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}\geq\mu_{3}
. We get

(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})d_{1}+(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})d_{2}+(\lambda_{3}-\mu_{3})d_{3}=(\lambda-\mu)d_{0}+r,

(6)

where all coefficients on the left-hand side are nonnegative. This means that the value (
6
), is congruent to
r
modulo
d_{0}
, is nonnegative, is in
\langle d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\rangle
, and is strictly less than
\lambda d_{0}+r
, which contradicts the minimality of
\lambda d_{0}+r
.
∎

.

Delete from the initial collection of cubes the regions associated to all points of the form (
3
) and the regions associated to the points (
5
). Additionally, for all identities of the form

\lambda_{0}d_{0}+\lambda_{1}d_{1}=\lambda_{2}d_{2}+\lambda_{3}d_{3},

with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{0}>0
,
\lambda_{2}<b_{22}
, and
\lambda_{3}<b_{33}
, remove the region associated to the point
(-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
. For all identities of the form

\lambda_{0}d_{0}+\lambda_{2}d_{2}=\lambda_{1}d_{1}+\lambda_{3}d_{3},

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{0}>0
,
\lambda_{1}<b_{11}
, and
\lambda_{3}<b_{33}
, remove the region associated to the point
(\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
. Finally, for all identities of the form

\lambda_{0}d_{0}+\lambda_{3}d_{3}=\lambda_{1}d_{1}+\lambda_{2}d_{2},

with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{0}>0
,
\lambda_{1}<b_{11}
, and
\lambda_{2}<b_{22}
, remove the region associated to the point
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3})
.
Call the remaining collection of cubes the figure

R
.

Proof.

The condition
\lambda_{i}<b_{ii}
ensures that we consider only the identities that matter
-
the points (
5
) already delete the regions
\{x>b_{11}\}
,
\{y>b_{22}\}
,
\{z>b_{33}\}

ensures that every cube labeled with an element of the Apéry set is in
R
. We need to prove that only the cubes labeled with elements of the Apéry set are in
R
. Suppose, to the contrary, that for some remainder
r\in\{0,1,\dots,d_{0}-1\}
,
\lambda d_{0}+r
and
\lambda^{\prime}d_{0}+r
, with
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
, are labels in

. Now for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

\lambda d_{0}+r=\lambda_{1}d_{1}+\lambda_{2}d_{2}+\lambda_{3}d_{3},

(7)

\lambda^{\prime}d_{0}+r=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}d_{1}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}d_{2}+\lambda_{3}^{\prime}d_{3},

(8)

with

Subtracting (

(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)d_{0}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})d_{1}+(\lambda_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda_{2})d_{2}+(\lambda_{3}^{\prime}-\lambda_{3})d_{3},

(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)d_{0}+(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime})d_{2}+(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{3}^{\prime})d_{3}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})d_{1}.

(10)

Since all the above coefficients are nonnegative,

If

, there is nothing to prove. Assume
a_{11}<b_{11}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be as in Theorem
2.2
. Delete from
R
the regions
\{x>a_{11}\}
,
\{y>a_{22}\}
, and
\{z>a_{33}\}
. Then, in the resulting collection, each element of the Apéry set labels at most one cube.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\lambda_{3}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\lambda_{i},\lambda^{\prime}_{i}<a_{ii}
for
i=1,2,3
, we have

\lambda_{1}d_{1}+\lambda_{2}d_{2}+\lambda_{3}d_{3}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}d_{1}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}d_{2}+\lambda_{3}^{\prime}d_{3}

(15)

\implies(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})d_{1}+(\lambda_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda_{2})d_{2}+(\lambda_{3}^{\prime}-\lambda_{3})d_{3}=0.

(16)

If coefficients in (
16
) are zero, we have nothing to prove. Therefore, either one or two coefficients in (
16
) are positive. Regardless, without loss of generality, we can assume that

(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})d_{1}=(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime})d_{2}+(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{3}^{\prime})d_{3},

(17)

where all the coefficients in the above equation are nonnegative. However,
(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})
is positive, because otherwise all the coefficients would be zero. This implies

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
a_{ii}=b_{ii}
for at least two distinct indices

Define an

as a subset of the initial collection of cubes such that:

•

all its labels are elements of the Apéry set,

•

every element of the Apéry set labels exactly one cube in it,

•

In particular, the collection from Proposition

and is established in the literature.

It turns out that when the conditions of Proposition

are met, we can employ the strategy of essentially guessing the Apéry set. We already mentioned in the introduction that a general polynomial formula for the Frobenius number does not exist. When we seek the Frobenius number of a special family of numerical semigroups, for which we suspect the existence of a polynomial formula, we also expect that the corresponding Apéry set
-
and also the figure
R

-
will also exhibit a certain regularity. To test these conjectures, we can examine numerical examples with the aid of an algebraic calculator, thus clarifying the behavior of the family in question.

If a family of numerical semigroups is well-behaved enough, the minimal relations and the identities from Theorem

should have a form that we should be able to predict.

If the conditions of Proposition

are satisfied, it tells us

.

Remark 2.6

If the conditions of Proposition

Suppose that

By the region associated to a cube, we mean the region associated to the vertex of the cube closest to the origin.

3
Catenary degree

In this section, we will be interested in describing the set of Betti elements and the catenary degree using the Apéry set, following the procedure developed in Section
2
. We start with some preliminary definitions and then proceed to the main results. To apply those results in Sections
4
and
5
, Proposition
2.9
will be essential. We do not have a version of the aforementioned Proposition for numerical semigroups that do not satisfy the assumption of Proposition
2.5
. As a consequence, in this Section we will consider only numerical semigroups fulfilling those assumptions; see Problem
6.3
.

Results closely related to those of this section were obtained by Bresinsky in
[
8
]
, and in fact, his approach applies to all numerical semigroups of embedding dimension four. Our method, however, provides a different perspective; it explicitly relates the Betti elements and minimal presentations to the Apéry set, a connection that is absent from

. Consequently, Bresinsky’s framework does not yield the results that we establish in Sections

and

3.1
Preliminaries

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup minimally generated by
\{d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}
. The
factorization morphism
of
S
is

\varphi:\mathbb{N}^{k+1}\to S,\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi(z_{0},\dots,z_{k})=z_{0}d_{0}+\dots+z_{k}d_{k}.

The
set of factorizations
of an element
s\in S
is

\varphi^{-1}(s)=\{(z_{0},\dots,z_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}\mid z_{0}d_{0}+\dots+z_{k}d_{k}=s\}.

If
z=(z_{0},\dots,z_{k})\in\varphi^{-1}(s)
, then the
length
of
z
is
|z|=z_{0}+\dots+z_{k}
. The
support
of
z
is defined by

\text{supp}(z)=\{i\in\{0,\dots,k\}\mid z_{i}>0\}.

For
z=(z_{0},\dots,z_{k}),z^{\prime}=(z_{0}^{\prime},\dots,z_{k}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
we set

\gcd(z,z^{\prime})=(\min\{z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}\},\dots,\min\{z_{k},z_{k}^{\prime}\}).

The
distance
between
z
and
z^{\prime}
is defined by

\text{d}(z,z^{\prime})=\max\{|z-\gcd(z,z^{\prime})|,|z^{\prime}-\gcd(z,z^{\prime})|\}.

Given
s\in S
and
z,z^{\prime}\in\varphi^{-1}(s)
, an
N
-chain
of factorizations from
z
to
z^{\prime}
is a sequence
z_{1},\dots,z_{p}\in\varphi^{-1}(s)
such that
z_{1}=z
,
z_{p}=z^{\prime}
, and d
(z_{i},z_{i+1})\leq N
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
. The
catenary degree
of
s
,
\textup{c}(s)
, is the minimal
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that for any two factorizations
z,z^{\prime}\in\varphi^{-1}(s)
, there is an
N
-chain from
z
to
z^{\prime}
. The catenary degree of
S
is defined by

\textup{c}(S)=\sup\{\textup{c}(s)\mid s\in S\}.

Two elements
z
and
z^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
are
\mathcal{R}
-related if there exists a finite sequence
z=z_{1},\dots,z_{p}=z^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
such that
\text{supp}(z_{i})\cap\text{supp}(z_{i+1})
is nonempty for all
i\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
. We write
z\,\mathcal{R}\,z^{\prime}
in this case. We will refer to equivalence classes of
\varphi^{-1}(s)
under the relation
\mathcal{R}
as the
\mathcal{R}
-classes.
We say that
s\in S
is a
Betti element
if there exist two elements of
\varphi^{-1}(s)
that are not
\mathcal{R}
-related (i.e. it has at least two different
\mathcal{R}
-classes). The set of all Betti elements of
S
is denoted
\text{Betti}(S)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 15]
Let
S
be a numerical semigroup. Then

\textup{c}(S)=\max\{\textup{c}(b)\mid b\in\textup{Betti}(S)\}.

We define the
kernel congruence
of
\varphi
as
\sigma=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{k+1}\mid\varphi(a)=\varphi(b)\}
. It is well known that
S
is isomorphic to the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{k+1}/\sigma
.
A
presentation
for
S
is a subset
\rho
of
\sigma
such that
\sigma
is the least congruence (with respect to set inclusion) containing
\rho
. A

is a presentation that is minimal with respect to set inclusion.
It turns out that the set of Betti elements is also helpful in finding minimal presentations.

We will describe a process to find all minimal presentations of
S
. Let
\mathcal{R}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{R}_{l}
be the different
\mathcal{R}
-classes of
\varphi^{-1}(b)
for some

. We choose
v_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{i}
for each

Corollary 5.1
.

The Frobenius number of the numerical semigroup generated by the four consecutive triangular numbers
\binom{n+1}{2},\binom{n+2}{2},\binom{n+3}{2},\binom{n+4}{2}
is as follows:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{3}n^{3}+\frac{11}{6}n^{2}+\frac{5}{2}n-1&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 0\pmod{6},\ n\geq 6,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n^{3}+\frac{5}{3}n^{2}+\frac{10}{3}n+1&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 2\pmod{6},\ n\geq 2,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n^{3}+\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+\frac{13}{6}n&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 4\pmod{6},\ n\geq 4,\\
&\frac{1}{2}n^{3}+\frac{3}{2}n^{2}-3&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 1\pmod{6},\ n\geq 1,\\
&\frac{1}{2}n^{3}+2n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-4&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 3\pmod{6},\ n\geq 3,\\
&\frac{1}{2}n^{3}+2n^{2}+\frac{5}{2}n&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 5\pmod{6},\ n\geq 5.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

.

The genus of the numerical semigroup generated by the four consecutive triangular numbers
\binom{n+1}{2},\binom{n+2}{2},\binom{n+3}{2},\binom{n+4}{2}

&\frac{25}{144}n^{3}+n^{2}+\frac{17}{12}n+\frac{7}{9}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 2\pmod{6},\ n\geq 2,\\
&\frac{25}{144}n^{3}+n^{2}+\frac{13}{12}n-\frac{4}{9}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 4\pmod{6},\ n\geq 4,\\
&\frac{37}{144}n^{3}+\frac{43}{48}n^{2}+\frac{1}{48}n-\frac{169}{144}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 1\pmod{6},\ n\geq 1,\\
&\frac{37}{144}n^{3}+\frac{49}{48}n^{2}+\frac{3}{16}n-\frac{27}{16}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 3\pmod{6},\ n\geq 3,\\
&\frac{37}{144}n^{3}+\frac{49}{48}n^{2}+\frac{49}{48}n+\frac{37}{144}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 5\pmod{6},\ n\geq 5.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Corollary 5.3
.

The catenary degree of the numerical semigroup generated by the four consecutive triangular numbers
\binom{n+1}{2},\binom{n+2}{2},\binom{n+3}{2},\binom{n+4}{2}
is as follows:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{2}{3}n+1&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 0\pmod{6},\ n\geq 6,\\
&\frac{2}{3}n+\frac{5}{3}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 2\pmod{6},\ n\geq 8,\\
&\frac{2}{3}n+\frac{7}{3}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 4\pmod{6},\ n\geq 4,\\
&n+2&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 1\pmod{2},\ n\geq 3.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Moreover:
\textup{c}\left(\langle\binom{3}{2},\binom{4}{2},\binom{5}{2},\binom{6}{2}\rangle\right)=10
.

Corollary 5.4
.

The cardinality of the minimal presentation of the numerical semigroup generated by the four consecutive triangular numbers
\binom{n+1}{2},\binom{n+2}{2},\binom{n+3}{2},\binom{n+4}{2}
is as follows:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{3}n+3&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 0\pmod{6},\ n\geq 12,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{10}{3}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 2\pmod{6},\ n\geq 8,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{11}{3}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 4\pmod{6},\ n\geq 10,\\
&\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{9}{2}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 1\pmod{6},\ n\geq 7,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n+3&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 3\pmod{6},\ n\geq 9,\\
&\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{7}{3}&&\text{if}\ \ n\equiv 5\pmod{6},\ n\geq 11.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Moreover:
\#\rho\left(\langle\binom{3}{2},\binom{4}{2},\binom{5}{2},\binom{6}{2}\rangle\right)=1
,
\#\rho\left(\langle\binom{4}{2},\binom{6}{2},\binom{7}{2},\binom{8}{2}\rangle\right)=1
,
\#\rho\left(\langle\binom{5}{2},\binom{6}{2},\binom{7}{2},\binom{8}{2}\rangle\right)=4
,
\#\rho\left(\langle\binom{6}{2},\binom{7}{2},\binom{8}{2},\binom{9}{2}\rangle\right)=2
,
\#\rho\left(\langle\binom{7}{2},\binom{8}{2},\binom{9}{2},\binom{10}{2}\rangle\right)=4
.

6
Closing remarks

In
[
22
]
, Moscariello demonstrated that the Frobenius number of numerical semigroups generated by the infinite sequences
n^{2},(n+1)^{2},(n+2)^{2},\dots
is asymptotically
O(n^{2})
. This result was used in
[
4
]
to show that the embedding dimension of such semigroups is asymptotically
O(n)
. In
[
19
]
, the Frobenius number of the numerical semigroup generated by
n^{2},(n+1)^{2},(n+2)^{2}
was determined, and it turned out to be given by a finite set of degree-three polynomials in
n
. This matches the results of this paper on four consecutive squares, as in this case we also obtain polynomials of degree three in
n
. Since for infinite sequences of squares the answer is asymptotically
O(n^{2})
, the question arises whether there exists a positive integer
k
such that
F(\langle n^{2},(n+1)^{2},\dots,(n+k)^{2}\rangle)
is found by a finite set of quadratic polynomials in
n
. Using the following lower bound on the Frobenius number, proved in
[
18
]
(see also
[
25
, Chapter 3]
), the answer is negative.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
be positive integers such that
\gcd(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=1
. Then

F(\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle)\geq((m-1)!a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{m})^{\frac{1}{m-1}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}.

Substituting into Theorem
6.1
, we obtain

F(\langle n^{2},(n+1)^{2},\dots,(n+k)^{2}\rangle)\geq(k!(n(n+1)\cdots(n+k))^{2})^{\frac{1}{k}}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}(n+i)^{2}\geq(k!(n^{k+1})^{2})^{\frac{1}{k}}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}(n+i)^{2}.

Hence, for
n
large enough, the right-hand side is
\geq C\cdot(n^{2})^{\frac{k+1}{k}}
for some positive constant
C
, which shows that the Frobenius number cannot be a quadratic polynomial in
n
.

In view of the results of Sections
2
and
3
, we propose the following:

Problem 6.2
.

Apply the methods from Sections
2
and
3
to other families of numerical semigroups with embedding dimension four.

, we restricted ourselves to work under the hypotheses of Proposition
2.5

4

to all numerical semigroups with embedding dimension four. By Lemma
2.7
