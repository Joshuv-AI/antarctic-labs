Tirzepatide Efficacy and Tolerability According to Early Weight Response: A Post Hoc Analysis of the SURMOUNT‐1 and SURMOUNT‐2 Trials
Authors: Alexander Kokkinos, Tina Thethi, Clare J Lee, Lisa M Neff, Adam Stefanski, Dachuang Cao, Angel Rodriguez, Amy Bartee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsObesity affects over 800 million people globally and is associated with complications including cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes (T2D). Early response to weight loss interventions may help optimize achievement of individual treatment goals. Tirzepatide has demonstrated significant weight reduction and improvement in cardiometabolic risk parameters (CRPs) in the SURMOUNT (SM)‐1 (NCT04184622) and SM‐2 (NCT04657003) trials. We aimed to assess weight reduction, CRPs, tolerability and hypoglycaemia according to early body weight response to tirzepatide in these studies.Materials and MethodsSM‐1 and SM‐2 were phase 3, multicentre, randomized, placebo‐controlled, double‐blind trials evaluating the safety and efficacy of tirzepatide in individuals with obesity or overweight, without (SM‐1) or with T2D (SM‐2). Post hoc exploratory analyses included tirzepatide‐treated participants from SM‐1 (n = 1775) and SM‐2 (n = 609), categorized as early responders (ERs; ≥ 5% weight reduction) or nonearly responders (non‐ERs; < 5% weight reduction) at Week 8. Outcomes were assessed using the efficacy estimand with logistic regression and mixed models for repeated measures.ResultsAcross both studies (N = 2384), ERs were more likely to be female, White and to have lower baseline HbA1c levels. Clinically meaningful weight reduction and improvements in CRPs, including HbA1c, were observed from baseline to Week 72, with significantly greater improvements in ERs compared with non‐ERs. The pattern, severity and time course of gastrointestinal events were similar between groups.ConclusionsIn this post hoc analysis of the SURMOUNT‐1 and SURMOUNT‐2 trials, tirzepatide‐treated participants with obesity or overweight in both ER and non‐ER groups achieved clinically meaningful weight reduction and improvements in CRPs at Week 72. However, ERs experienced greater weight reduction and improvements in CRP than non‐ERs, with a comparable gastrointestinal tolerability profile.Trial RegistrationSURMOUNT‐1: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04184622 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04184622). SURMOUNT‐2: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04657003 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04657003)Keywords: GIP, GLP‐1, obesity therapy, weight management

Introduction

Obesity is a chronic disease affecting over 800 million people worldwide [1] associated with various health complications, including cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes (T2D) [2]. Weight reduction may lead to many health benefits, including decreased risk of cardiovascular disease. Tirzepatide is a once‐weekly glucose‐dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor and glucagon‐like peptide‐1 (GLP‐1) receptor agonist [3] approved for the treatment of obesity, T2D [4] and obstructive sleep apnoea in multiple geographies [5].

Previous data from the 72‐week SURMOUNT‐1 and ‐2 (SM‐1 and SM‐2) trials have demonstrated significant body weight reduction [3, 6] and improvement in multiple cardiometabolic risk parameters with tirzepatide compared with placebo [7]. Gastrointestinal (GI) adverse events (AEs) are the most frequently observed AEs, typically mild to moderate in severity and decreased over time [3, 6].

The response to interventions for obesity may be clinically useful in meeting individual treatment goals. There is evidence that an earlier response to interventions for obesity, together with dietary interventions and physical activity counselling, resulted in increased weight loss and improvement in blood pressure and circulating lipid levels [6, 8, 9].

Registrational trials of pharmacologic interventions typically employ a standardized dose escalation that does not adjust for individual participants' weight response. This exploratory analysis assessed the association between early body weight reduction in SM‐1 and SM‐2 participants with obesity or overweight and changes in body weight and cardiometabolic risk parameters after 72 weeks of tirzepatide treatment. Additionally, descriptive analyses of GI tolerability and incidence of hypoglycaemia were performed.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This was a post hoc analysis of data from the SM‐1 and SM‐2 phase 3 clinical trials in participants with obesity or overweight. Full clinical trial protocols for both studies are included as Supporting Information. Eligibility criteria for these studies have previously been published [3, 6] (Figures S1 and S2). Of note, in SM‐1, participants without T2D and with a body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m2 or ≥ 27 kg/m2 and at least one weight‐related complication (e.g., hypertension or dyslipidemia) were included [6]. In SM‐2, participants with T2D, a BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2 and glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) of 7%–10% under stable antihyperglycemic therapy for ≥ 3 months before screening were included [6]. Tirzepatide 2.5 mg (or matching placebo) was initiated once a week, and dose escalated every 4 weeks until the randomized dose of 5, 10 or 15 mg in SM‐1 and 10 or 15 mg in SM‐2 was achieved and continued until Week 72.

The protocols were approved by local institutional review boards, and the trials were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the Council of International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All participants provided written informed consent.

Outcomes

To assess whether early body weight response was associated with varying degrees of body weight reduction, cardiometabolic risk parameters, tolerability and safety at Week 72, the following primary endpoints were analysed: percent change in body weight from baseline to Week 72 and proportion of participants who achieved weight reduction targets (≥ 5%, ≥ 10%, ≥ 15%, ≥ 20%, ≥ 25% and ≥ 30%) at Week 72. Additional outcomes included change from baseline to Week 72 in waist circumference, BMI, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), HbA1c and serum lipids. The percentage of participants experiencing level 2 or 3 (< 54 mg/dL) hypoglycaemia was also assessed (SM‐2). Incidence and severity of nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea (NVD), constipation and decreased appetite were summarized throughout the study.

Data from SM‐1 and SM‐2 tirzepatide‐treated patients were categorized into two groups for the purpose of this analysis: early responders (≥ 5% weight reduction at Week 8) and nonearly responders (< 5% weight reduction at Week 8). Body weight reduction ≥ 5% was used, as it is considered to have a clinically meaningful effect on improving metabolic health parameters such as SBP, DBP, lipids and HbA1c [3, 6]. Week 8 of the study was used for categorization, as this time point represents a standardized treatment exposure at which all participants (from both SM‐1 and SM‐2) had been receiving tirzepatide 5 mg for 4 weeks and were scheduled for dose escalation to randomized dose [6].

Statistical Analysis

Analyses were conducted on tirzepatide‐treated participants using the efficacy analysis set, defined as randomized participants taking at least one dose of tirzepatide (excluding off‐treatment data). Only participants with non‐missing baseline and Week 8 weight data were analysed in this study. Mixed model for repeated measures was used to analyse percent change and change from randomization to 72 weeks in the continuous efficacy measures (e.g., body weight, lipids), with treatment group, visit, treatment‐by‐visit interaction and stratification factors (country, sex, prediabetes for SM‐1/type of antihyperglycemic medication for SM‐2) as fixed effects and baseline value of the response variable as a covariate. Lipids were analysed using log transformation. Logistic regression was used to compare the proportion of participants achieving body weight reduction targets (e.g., ≥ 5%) between the two groups at 72 weeks, with a covariate of baseline weight and factors of treatment group and stratification factors. Incidence of AEs was summarized using frequency and percentage. All analyses were post hoc and not adjusted for multiplicity, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS version 9.4 or above.

Results

Participants

Of the tirzepatide‐treated participants in SM‐1 and SM‐2 trials, data from 2384 participants were analysed. In SM‐1, females accounted for 67.5% of participants, with a mean age of 44.8 ± 12.3 years and were mostly White (70.3%). In SM‐2, participants were more evenly split across sexes (50.4% female vs. 49.6% male), with a mean age of 53.9 ± 10.6 years and were mostly White (74.4%). Early responders accounted for 56.6% (n = 1350) of analysed participants (SM‐1: 62.1%, n = 1103; SM‐2: 40.6%, n = 247). Baseline and demographic characteristics by early versus nonearly response for both studies are described in Table 1. In both studies, participants in the early responder group were more likely to be female and White and have lower HbA1c, while differences in weight and BMI between groups were only apparent in SM‐1. Regarding baseline use of oral antihyperglycemic medication in the SM‐2 population, 54% of participants were receiving 1 medication and 39% were receiving 2 or more such medications [6]. Overall, there were fewer participants on more than one antihyperglycemic medication at baseline in the early responder group (28.3% vs. 44.2% of nonearly responders).

Weight Reduction by Early Response

In both studies, early responders achieved significantly greater percent body weight reduction from baseline to Week 72 than nonearly responders. In SM‐1, the percent change in body weight from baseline to 8 weeks was −7.9% ± 0.1% in early responders and −3.1% ± 0.1% in nonearly responders (p < 0.001). Similarly, in SM‐2, the percent change in body weight from baseline to 8 weeks was −7.1% ± 0.1% in early responders and −2.5% ± 0.1% in nonearly responders (p < 0.001). The curves continued to diverge (Figure 1A,B), and significant differences were maintained throughout the observation period for both studies. At Week 72, weight reduction in SM‐1 was −23.3% ± 0.3% in early responders and −14.6% ± 0.4% in nonearly responders (p < 0.001). Similarly, in SM‐2, the mean body weight reduction was −20.0% ± 0.5% in early responders versus −10.8% ± 0.5% in nonearly responders (p < 0.001; Figure 1A,B).

When change in body weight over time was analysed by dose, significant reductions from baseline were seen with all tirzepatide doses, regardless of early response. However, the reduction in body weight at Week 72 for each dose was greater in early responders than in nonearly responders, consistently across both studies (Figure 1C,D). In SM‐1, early responders in the 5, 10 and 15 mg dose arms had a body weight reduction from baseline of −18.9% ± 0.5%, −25.3% ± 0.5% and −25.9% ± 0.5% versus −10.6% ± 0.6%, −14.8% ± 0.6% and −18.1% ± 0.5% in nonearly responders, respectively. In SM‐2, early responders in the 10 mg dose arm had a reduction in body weight from baseline of −18.6% ± 0.8% versus −10.4% ± 0.5% in nonearly responders. In the 15 mg dose arm, early responders had a reduction in body weight from baseline of −21.2% ± 0.8% versus −11.2% ± 0.6% in nonearly responders.

Furthermore, more participants in the early responder group achieved body weight reduction targets at Week 72 compared with nonearly responders in both studies. The difference was more evident at higher body weight reduction targets (≥ 15% and above; Figure 1E,F).

In both studies, early responders achieved greater mean change in BMI from baseline than nonearly responders at 72 weeks (SM‐1: −8.7 ± 0.1 kg/m2 vs. −5.7 ± 0.2 kg/m2; SM‐2: −7.2 ± 0.2 kg/m2 vs. −3.9 ± 0.2 kg/m2; p < 0.001 for both studies). Early responders also achieved greater mean reduction in waist circumference from baseline to Week 72 than nonearly responders (SM‐1: −20.6 ± 0.3 cm vs. −13.7 ± 0.4 cm; SM‐2: −16.8 ± 0.6 cm vs. −9.5 ± 0.5 cm; p < 0.001 for both studies).

Similarly, in SM‐2, significantly greater improvements in cardiometabolic risk parameters were also seen from baseline to Week 72 in early versus nonearly responders for SBP (−8.4 ± 0.8 vs. −6.2 ± 0.7 mmHg, p < 0.05), DBP (−3.7 ± 0.5 vs. −2.0 ± 0.4 mmHg, p < 0.05), TGs (−34.5% ± 1.8% vs. −23.9% ± 1.8%, p < 0.001), HDL‐C (13.4% ± 1.3% vs. 4.8% ± 1.0%, p < 0.001) and ALT (−38.7% ± 1.8% vs. −28.4% ± 1.8%, p < 0.001). Some of these parameters, which were also available at Week 8, showed meaningful improvements at this early time point (Table S1).

Of note, in SM‐2, early responders achieved greater reduction in HbA1c from baseline to Week 72 compared with nonearly responders (−2.5% ± 0.06% vs. −2.0% ± 0.05%, p < 0.001), with more participants achieving normoglycemia (HbA1c < 5.7%; 69.7% vs. 41.2%). In the SM‐1 population without T2D, early responders achieved a reduction in HbA1c of −0.5% ± 0.01% (p < 0.001), while nonearly responders a achieved HbA1c reduction of −0.4% ± 0.01% (p < 0.001).

Likewise, in SM‐2, early responders had a mean reduction in fasting serum glucose of −59.3 ± 2.0 mg/dL (p < 0.001) while nonearly responders had a mean reduction of −46.3 ± 1.6 mg/dL (p < 0.001). In SM‐1, the reduction in fasting serum glucose was smaller (−10.3 ± 0.3 mg/dL for early responders vs. −7.7 ± 0.5 mg/dL for nonearly responders; p < 0.001).

Most participants in both groups did not report any AEs. Regardless of early body weight response, the majority of NVD events reported in tirzepatide‐treated subjects in both SM‐1 and SM‐2 were mild to moderate in severity, occurring more frequently in the dose escalation phase, with lower incidence in the later phases (Figure S5). There were no reports of hypoglycaemia during the screening period among participants in SM‐2. Occurrence of hypoglycaemia (< 54 mg/dL) during the first 8 weeks was reported in 3 (1.2%) of early responders and 3 (0.8%) of nonearly responders. From baseline to Week 72, there were 12 (4.9%) cases of hypoglycaemia in early responders and 14 (3.9%) in nonearly responders. For reference, in the SURMOUNT‐2 primary publication [6], hypoglycaemia (< 54 mg/dL) was reported by 4% participants in the tirzepatide 10 mg group and 5% in the tirzepatide 15 mg group. More participants (9%–12%) treated with sulfonylureas at baseline reported hypoglycaemia (< 54 mg/dL) with tirzepatide treatment than those who were not taking sulfonylureas (2%–3%).

Discussion

This is the first analysis of the efficacy and tolerability of tirzepatide by early body weight response using SURMOUNT clinical program data. Overall, most tirzepatide‐treated participants had a clinically significant weight reduction of ≥ 10%, and meaningful improvements in cardiometabolic risk parameters at the end of the 72‐week treatment period, regardless of the degree of early body weight response. However, early responders showed greater improvements in weight‐related measures and cardiometabolic risk parameters at Week 72, observed in participants with or without T2D. Similarly, the proportion of participants achieving weight reduction thresholds of > 5%, > 10% and > 15% by Week 72 were higher among early responders than among nonearly responders. Prior studies with GLP‐1 receptor agonists or bupropion/naltrexone used later timepoints to define the early response and were mainly focused on the identification of subjects that would still show weight benefit from treatment past that timepoint, so that if the initial response was not deemed clinically meaningful, unnecessary treatment discontinuations could be avoided [10, 11, 12]. While our observations are generally consistent with those studies, the earlier timepoint of 8 weeks used for the assessment of early response together with the magnitude of the differences between groups at the end of the observation period (Week 72) make these results relevant for clinical practice.

In the Look AHEAD study, which assessed the impact of an intensive lifestyle intervention in adults with T2D, initial weight loss at 4 and 8 weeks was associated with weight changes in the longer term [13, 14].

In our analysis, a greater 72‐week degree of body weight reduction was seen for all tirzepatide doses in the early responder group compared with that in the nonearly responder group. This finding has potential clinical implications, as clinicians may approach dose escalation differently in patients who show an early, substantial response in the first few weeks of treatment. Conversely, for those who do not exhibit substantial weight loss in the first 8 weeks, dose escalation up to the most appropriate dose for that individual should be considered with continued clinical monitoring. In any case, gastrointestinal tolerability may influence the dose escalation in real life, which may differ from the more controlled or standardized clinical trial setting.

In our analysis, early responders had lower HbA1c and BMI at baseline and were more likely to be female and White. This is consistent with previous findings from SURPASS studies [15] but may partly reflect the type of participants in these trials. The observation that female participants are more likely to achieve greater weight loss than male participants has been seen in other studies [16, 17, 18] and may be explained by physiological factors such as differences in body fat distributions between sexes and proportionally greater fat mass typically observed in females [18, 19].

According to available evidence, the magnitude of weight reduction with incretin‐based medications in people with overweight or obesity and T2D is generally lower than that in people without T2D [20]. We found that the average 20% body weight reduction in early responders in SM‐2 (10 and 15 mg doses) is comparable to other studies with non‐T2D participants. Unlike SM‐1, SM‐2 had an early responder group that was smaller than the nonearly responder group, and we cannot rule out the possibility that average body weight reduction may have been influenced by the size of groups and not only by the potential physiologic impact of T2D on weight reduction. More evidence is needed to clearly identify participants who are more likely to respond earlier and better, using analyses such as additional predictor analysis.

Regarding glycaemic control in participants with T2D, the proportion of participants achieving HbA1c targets at Week 72 is shown in Figure S6. HbA1c plots over time showed that a reduction in HbA1c occurred early in both groups, with clear separation of curves already apparent at the first time point (Week 12; Figure S7) between early and nonearly responders for body weight. This is consistent with previous observations in SURPASS studies [15] and suggests that early improvement in glycaemic control is not driven solely by weight reduction but also by an increase in insulin secretion and a decrease in insulin resistance [21]. Notably, nonearly responders also benefited from clinically meaningful reductions in HbA1c at Week 72.

As these studies had randomized dose assignments, with study participants allocated to the lowest doses in both trials of either 5 mg or 10 mg that were maintained for the duration of the trial, some individuals may not have reached their potential for body weight loss at 72 weeks. Nevertheless, early responders in both trials showed greater weight reduction even on maintained lower doses, highlighting the value of the early observations in the clinical course of patients' weight loss journey and assessment of appropriate dose.

As expected, tirzepatide‐treated participants in SM‐1 and SM‐2 showed improvements in several cardiometabolic risk parameters, although changes seemed to be more pronounced in the early responder group in both studies. This is not unexpected, as some of these parameters, such as blood pressure and lipid profile, show greater improvements with greater body weight reduction [22], but it highlights the broader benefits associated with greater body weight reduction.

Generally, GI AEs were reported more frequently during the first 8 weeks, were mild or moderate in severity and decreased over time, regardless of category of response or T2D status, in line with prior evidence on tirzepatide showing that GI AEs were reported more frequently in the first few weeks of treatment [23, 24]. Of the GI AEs reported, nausea was the most commonly reported across groups and studies [23]. Although the incidence of reported NVD events during the first 8 weeks appears to be higher for early responders, it is important to highlight that most participants did not report any GI issues or AEs related to decreased appetite, regardless of the degree of early response. This would indicate that the presence of GI AEs may be used to manage dose escalation decisions to meet patients' weight or cardiometabolic goals, but should not be used to ‘predict’ or assess the degree of treatment response. This analysis is consistent with other analyses of incretin‐based medications, indicating that GI AEs are minimally predictive of greater weight reduction [23, 24].

There are several strengths of this analysis. The scope of the study is broad, including a large number of participants from two obesity studies with and without T2D, a follow‐up duration of 72 weeks and an adequate number of participants in the early and nonearly responders subgroups. All participants received healthy lifestyle counselling, with recommendations for daily 500‐cal deficit and 150 min of physical activity per week per guidelines [25]. This approach, combined with the longer study duration, may have contributed to the magnitude of weight reduction observed and aligns with similar findings in the SURPASS program.

This analysis has several limitations. This is a post hoc analysis not powered to detect differences in treatment effect between early and nonearly responders. Given the post hoc nature of the study, multiplicity adjustment was not performed for statistical comparisons, thus the results should be interpreted as hypothesis‐generating. Only participants on treatment and with non‐missing baseline and Week 8 weight data were analysed, and therefore, data on discontinuation of study treatment by early response category are not available. As in many studies, the sample size for non‐White race groups or certain ethnicities was limited, so findings cannot be extrapolated to all populations. Although treatment adherence appears similar between early and nonearly responders (data not shown), differences in diet and exercise, income or education may have influenced our results. Finally, systematic scales were not used to measure event severity in the SURMOUNT program. Considering the subjective nature of GI AE reporting, site and country variability cannot be excluded. Also, gastrointestinal tolerability and adverse event reporting may be different in real‐life settings versus clinical trials, and this should be considered for the appropriate management of treatment in each patient.

In conclusion, participants with obesity or overweight treated with tirzepatide in SM‐1 and SM‐2 achieved significant weight reduction and improvement in cardiometabolic risk parameters at Week 72 compared with baseline, regardless of early weight response. Furthermore, earlier body weight response was associated with greater improvements in weight‐related measures and cardiometabolic risk parameters at Week 72. In both early and nonearly responders, the overall incidence of GI events was generally comparable between groups and typically decreased over time, suggesting no meaningful differences in GI tolerability of tirzepatide based on early responder status. These results suggest that early assessment of weight loss with tirzepatide may help clinicians manage treatment to achieve individual therapeutic goals.
