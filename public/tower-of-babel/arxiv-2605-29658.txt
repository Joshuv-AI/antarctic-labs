Introduction

Let
m\geq n\geq 2
. The
Zarankiewicz number

z(m,n)
is the maximum number of edges in an
m\times n
bipartite graph that contains no complete bipartite subgraph
K_{2,2}
(equivalently, no cycle
C_{4}
). This classical extremal problem goes back to Zarankiewicz
[
12
]
; a general upper bound was proved by Kövári, Sös and Turán
[
5
]
. Exact values are known only in special cases.

A basic extremal family is obtained from complete graphs. Let
V(K_{q+1})=\{0,1,\dots,q\}
. The incidence graph of
K_{q+1}
has left vertex set
\binom{V(K_{q+1})}{2}
and right vertex set
V(K_{q+1})
. It contains one edge for each incidence of a graph edge with one of its endpoints. This graph is
C_{4}
-free and has
\binom{q+1}{2}
left vertices,
q+1
right vertices, and
q(q+1)
edges. It is classical that

z\!\left(\binom{q+1}{2},q+1\right)=q(q+1),

see Reiman
[
10
]
and Guy
[
4
]
. In particular,

\displaystyle z(6,4)

\displaystyle=12,\qquad z(10,5)=20,\qquad z(15,6)=30,

\displaystyle z(21,7)

\displaystyle=42,\qquad z(28,8)=56.

Qi, Cui, and Xu
[
8
]
introduced the
augmented Zarankiewicz number

z_{A}(m,n)
and the
limited augmented Zarankiewicz number

z_{L}(m,n)
. Both parameters arise from the study of SOS ranks of biquadratic forms. The 1-edge subgraph is denoted by

G_{1}=(S,T,E_{1}).

For
z_{A}(m,n)
, the graph
G_{1}
may be any extremal
C_{4}
-free bipartite graph. For
z_{L}(m,n)
, one requires
|E_{1}|=z(m,n)
. In both cases one allows the addition of 2-edges, subject to constraints forbidding generalized
C_{4}
-configurations. They proved

\operatorname{BSR}(m,n)\geq z_{A}(m,n)\geq z_{L}(m,n)\geq z(m,n).

Thus every lower bound for
z_{L}
yields a lower bound for
\operatorname{BSR}(m,n)
.

A companion paper
[
9
]
established a general lower bound by means of a
K_{4t}
block construction. If
m=\binom{4t}{2}
and
n=4t
, then

z_{L}(m,n)\geq 2\binom{4t}{2}+4t^{2}-2t.

Consequently,

\frac{z_{L}-z}{z}\geq\frac{4t^{2}}{16t^{2}-4t}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{4},

so the gap is asymptotically at least
25\%
of the classical Zarankiewicz number.

In this paper we focus on the incidence-graph family arising from complete graphs. This family is highly structured, the 1-edge subgraph is unique up to isomorphism, and the admissibility conditions admit a finite optimization formulation. Our goal is to determine or bound

z_{L}\!\left(\binom{q+1}{2},q+1\right)

for small values of
q
within this family.

Main contributions

The main contributions are as follows.

1.

We formulate the search for admissible 2-edges as a direct 0–1 integer linear program with occupancy variables and explicit linear encodings of
(S)
,
(C2)
, and
(C3)
on a prescribed candidate family.

2.

For the lower-bound cases, we also describe a constructive search procedure based on randomized greedy insertion, local delete-and-repair improvement, and final exact admissibility verification.

3.

We obtain the exact values

z_{L}(6,4)=14,\qquad z_{L}(10,5)=26.

4.

We obtain the lower bounds

z_{L}(15,6)\geq 43,\qquad z_{L}(21,7)\geq 64,\qquad z_{L}(28,8)\geq 88.

These bounds come from directly verified admissible families with
|E_{2}|=13
,
|E_{2}|=22
, and
|E_{2}|=32
, respectively.

5.

We compare these values and bounds with the
K_{4t}
construction and observe that the resulting gap ratios already exceed the asymptotic
25\%
benchmark in the small cases considered here.

Outline

Section 2 recalls the incidence-graph model, the source-paper notions of degenerate and nondegenerate 2-edges, and the admissibility conditions. Section 3 presents the direct ILP framework used for the exact computations at
q=3,4
, and then gives a pseudocode-style constructive-search algorithm used in the lower-bound cases
q=5,6,7
. Section 4 states the small-parameter results. Section 5 records a lifting comparison for
(21,7)
. Section 6 compares the resulting ratios with the
K_{4t}
construction, and Section 7 concludes with a brief list of open problems.

2
The limited augmented Zarankiewicz number for the incidence graph of
K_{q+1}

Fix an integer
q\geq 2
. Let

V=\{0,1,\dots,q\},\qquad S=\binom{V}{2},\qquad T=V.

Define

E_{1}=\{(e,v)\in S\times T:v\in e\}.

Then

G_{1}=(S,T,E_{1})

is the incidence graph of
K_{q+1}
. It has

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle=\binom{q+1}{2},\qquad|T|=q+1,

\displaystyle|E_{1}|

\displaystyle=2\binom{q+1}{2}=q(q+1)=z\!\left(\binom{q+1}{2},q+1\right).

The following proposition shows that, for these parameters, the extremal
C_{4}
-free bipartite graph is unique up to isomorphism. Consequently, the choice of the 1-edge subgraph is unambiguous in the incidence-graph family under study.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
n=q+1
and
m=\binom{n}{2}
. If
H=(X,Y,F)
is a
C_{4}
-free bipartite graph with

|X|=m,\qquad|Y|=n,\qquad|F|=n(n-1),

then
H
is isomorphic to the incidence graph of
K_{n}
.

Proof.

For each
x\in X
, let
d(x)
denote its degree. Because
H
is
C_{4}
-free, any two vertices of
Y
have at most one common neighbor in
X
. Hence

\sum_{x\in X}\binom{d(x)}{2}\leq\binom{n}{2}=m.

On the other hand, for every integer
d\geq 0
,

\binom{d}{2}\geq d-1,

with equality if and only if
d\in\{1,2\}
. Therefore

m\geq\sum_{x\in X}\binom{d(x)}{2}\geq\sum_{x\in X}(d(x)-1)=\sum_{x\in X}d(x)-|X|.

Now
\sum_{x\in X}d(x)=|F|=n(n-1)
, while
|X|=m=\binom{n}{2}=n(n-1)/2
. Thus

\sum_{x\in X}d(x)-|X|=n(n-1)-\binom{n}{2}=m.

So equality holds throughout. In particular,
\binom{d(x)}{2}=d(x)-1
for every
x\in X
, hence
d(x)\in\{1,2\}
for all
x
. Since the average degree on
X
is

\frac{|F|}{|X|}=\frac{n(n-1)}{\binom{n}{2}}=2,

every vertex of
X
must actually have degree exactly
2
.

Each vertex
x\in X
therefore determines an unordered pair of neighbors in
Y
. Two distinct vertices of
X
cannot determine the same pair, for otherwise those two vertices together with those two vertices of
Y
would form a
C_{4}
. Hence the map
x\mapsto N(x)
is an injection from
X
into
\binom{Y}{2}
. But both sets have size
m=\binom{n}{2}
, so this injection is a bijection. Thus every two-element subset of
Y
occurs exactly once as the neighborhood of a vertex of
X
. This is precisely the incidence graph of
K_{n}
.
∎

An
augmented bipartite graph
has the form

G=(S,T,E_{1}\cup E_{2}),

where
E_{2}
is a set of
2-edges
. A 2-edge is written as

(i_{1}i_{2},i_{3};i_{4}i_{5},i_{6}),

where
\{i_{1},i_{2}\},\{i_{4},i_{5}\}\in S
and
i_{3},i_{6}\in T
. Its two halves are the cells

(\{i_{1},i_{2}\},i_{3}),\qquad(\{i_{4},i_{5}\},i_{6}).

Following
[
8
]
, a 2-edge is called
nondegenerate
if

\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\neq\{i_{4},i_{5}\},\qquad i_{3}\neq i_{6}.

It is called
row-degenerate
if
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}=\{i_{4},i_{5}\}
and
i_{3}\neq i_{6}
, and
column-degenerate
if
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\neq\{i_{4},i_{5}\}
and
i_{3}=i_{6}
. Both degenerate and nondegenerate 2-edges are allowed in the general definition of
z_{L}
. The case
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}=\{i_{4},i_{5}\}
and
i_{3}=i_{6}
is excluded by the simplicity condition
(S)
.

Admissibility conditions

We use the following three conditions.

(S)

No 2-edge shares a cell with a 1-edge or with another 2-edge.

(C2)

For a
nondegenerate
2-edge
(i_{1}i_{2},i_{3};i_{4}i_{5},i_{6})
, the two opposite cells

(\{i_{1},i_{2}\},i_{6}),\qquad(\{i_{4},i_{5}\},i_{3})

cannot both be occupied.

(C3)

There do not exist a 2-edge
(i_{1}i_{2},i_{3};i_{4}i_{5},i_{6})
and an occupied cell
(x,y)
, with

x\notin\bigl\{\{i_{1},i_{2}\},\{i_{4},i_{5}\}\bigr\},\qquad y\notin\{i_{3},i_{6}\},

such that the five cells

(x,y),\ (x,i_{3}),\ (x,i_{6}),\ (\{i_{1},i_{2}\},y),\ (\{i_{4},i_{5}\},y)

are all occupied. If the 2-edge is nondegenerate, then these five cells are additionally required to be pairwise distinct.

Definition 2.2
.

For
m=\binom{q+1}{2}
and
n=q+1
, consider the incidence-graph family associated with
K_{q+1}
. The limited augmented Zarankiewicz number in this family is the maximum value of

|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|

over all augmented bipartite graphs
G=(S,T,E_{1}\cup E_{2})
satisfying
(S)
,
(C2)
, and
(C3)
, with
G_{1}
fixed as the incidence graph of
K_{q+1}
.

By
[
8
]
, every admissible construction yields a lower bound for
\operatorname{BSR}(m,n)
:

\operatorname{BSR}(m,n)\geq z_{L}(m,n)\geq z(m,n).

3
Direct ILP, constructive search, and exact verification

For
q=3
and
q=4
, we solve the problem exactly on the full candidate family by means of a direct 0–1 ILP whose variables record both the selected 2-edges and the occupied available cells. For
q=5
,
q=6
, and
q=7
, we report explicit admissible families whose validity has been checked directly against
(S)
,
(C2)
, and
(C3)
. Throughout this section, the terms degenerate and nondegenerate are understood in the sense of
[
8
]
: a 2-edge is nondegenerate precisely when its two halves lie in different rows and different columns.

Available cells and candidate families

For each row
r\in S=\binom{V}{2}
and each column
c\in T=V
, the cell
(r,c)
is already occupied by a 1-edge if and only if
c\in r
. Hence the set of available cells is

A_{q}:=\{(r,c)\in S\times T:c\notin r\}.

Since every row
r\in S
excludes exactly two columns, we have

|A_{q}|=\binom{q+1}{2}(q-1).

A 2-edge is determined by an unordered pair of distinct available cells. Accordingly, the full candidate family is

\mathcal{E}_{q}:=\bigl\{\{a,b\}:a,b\in A_{q},\ a\neq b\bigr\}.

For a candidate
e=\{a,b\}\in\mathcal{E}_{q}
, write

a=(r_{1},c_{1}),\qquad b=(r_{2},c_{2}).

Then
e
is nondegenerate if and only if
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
and
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
. It is row-degenerate if
r_{1}=r_{2}
and
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
, and column-degenerate if
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
and
c_{1}=c_{2}
.

For
q=3
and
q=4
, we use the full candidate family in the exact ILP. For
q=5
, the full candidate family has size

|\mathcal{E}_{5}|=\binom{60}{2}=1770,

while its nondegenerate subfamily has size

|\mathcal{E}_{5}^{\mathrm{nd}}|=1410.

For
q=6
and
q=7
, we do not enumerate the full candidate families in this paper; instead we present directly verified admissible examples produced by the constructive search described below.

Direct 0–1 ILP formulation

Fix a candidate family
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{q}
. We now write the exact optimization model in a standard form that can be implemented directly.

Index sets and auxiliary notation.

For each candidate
e\in\mathcal{E}
, write

e=\{(r_{1}(e),c_{1}(e)),(r_{2}(e),c_{2}(e))\},

where
(r_{1}(e),c_{1}(e))
and
(r_{2}(e),c_{2}(e))
are its two halves. Let

\mathcal{N}:=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:r_{1}(e)\neq r_{2}(e),\ c_{1}(e)\neq c_{2}(e)\}

be the set of nondegenerate candidates.

For each available cell
a\in A_{q}
and each candidate
e\in\mathcal{E}
, define the incidence coefficient

M_{a,e}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }a\text{ is one of the two halves of }e,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For every cell
(r,c)\in S\times T
, define the occupancy symbol

\bar{o}_{r,c}:=\begin{cases}1,&c\in r,\\
o_{(r,c)},&(r,c)\in A_{q}.\end{cases}

Thus
\bar{o}_{r,c}
is a constant on 1-edge cells and a binary variable on available cells.

For each candidate
e\in\mathcal{E}
, define the admissible witness set

\mathcal{W}(e):=\Bigl\{(x,y):x\in S\setminus\{r_{1}(e),r_{2}(e)\},\ y\in T\setminus\{c_{1}(e),c_{2}(e)\}\Bigr\},

with the additional restriction that, when
e\in\mathcal{N}
, the five cells

(x,y),\ (x,c_{1}(e)),\ (x,c_{2}(e)),\ (r_{1}(e),y),\ (r_{2}(e),y)

must be pairwise distinct.

Decision variables.

For each candidate
e\in\mathcal{E}
, let

x_{e}\in\{0,1\}

indicate whether
e
is selected. For each available cell
a\in A_{q}
, let

o_{a}\in\{0,1\}

indicate whether
a
is occupied by a selected 2-edge.

Optimization model.

We solve

\min-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}x_{e}

subject to

o_{a}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}M_{a,e}x_{e}=0,\qquad a\in A_{q},

(ILP-S)

x_{e}+\bar{o}_{r_{1}(e),c_{2}(e)}+\bar{o}_{r_{2}(e),c_{1}(e)}\leq 2,\qquad e\in\mathcal{N},

(ILP-C2)

and

x_{e}+\bar{o}_{x,y}+\bar{o}_{x,c_{1}(e)}+\bar{o}_{x,c_{2}(e)}+\bar{o}_{r_{1}(e),y}+\bar{o}_{r_{2}(e),y}\leq 5,\qquad e\in\mathcal{E},\ (x,y)\in\mathcal{W}(e),

(ILP-C3)

together with

x_{e}\in\{0,1\}\quad(e\in\mathcal{E}),\qquad o_{a}\in\{0,1\}\quad(a\in A_{q}).

This formulation is a
binary linear integer program
. Indeed, the objective function is linear in the variables
x_{e}
. Constraint
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{S})
is linear because each equation involves only a binary occupancy variable
o_{a}
and a linear sum of candidate-selection variables
x_{e}
. Constraint
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{C2})
is also linear, because for every cell
(r,c)\in S\times T
the symbol
\bar{o}_{r,c}
is not a product term: it equals the constant
1
when
(r,c)\in E_{1}
, and it equals the binary variable
o_{(r,c)}
when
(r,c)\in A_{q}
. The same observation shows that
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{C3})
is linear as well. Therefore the entire model can be implemented directly in standard MILP solvers without any linearization step.

Constraint
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{S})
is exactly the simplicity condition
(S)
: each available cell can be used by at most one selected 2-edge, and the variable
o_{a}
records whether that cell is used. Constraint
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{C2})
is the direct linear encoding of condition
(C2)
for nondegenerate 2-edges. Constraint
(\mathrm{ILP}\text{-}\mathrm{C3})
is the direct linear encoding of condition
(C3)
, with the distinctness requirement imposed through the definition of
\mathcal{W}(e)
whenever
e
is nondegenerate.

If the optimal objective value of the above minimization model is
\zeta(\mathcal{E})
, then the maximum number of selected 2-edges in the chosen candidate family is

|E_{2}|_{\max}(\mathcal{E})=-\zeta(\mathcal{E}).

Hence, when
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{q}
is the full candidate family,

z_{L}\!\left(\binom{q+1}{2},q+1\right)=q(q+1)-\zeta(\mathcal{E}_{q}).

If
\mathcal{E}
is only a restricted subfamily, then every feasible solution yields the lower bound

z_{L}\!\left(\binom{q+1}{2},q+1\right)\geq q(q+1)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}x_{e}.

This is exactly the model implemented, in equivalent programmatic form, by the checked Python and MATLAB codes.

5
A lifting comparison for
(21,7)

We next record the lifting argument used to obtain the bound for
(21,7)
.

Theorem 5.1

(Lifting bound,
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose an admissible configuration for the incidence graph of
K_{q+1}
contains
t
2-edges. Then there exists an admissible configuration for the incidence graph of
K_{q+2}
containing at least

t+\left\lfloor\frac{q}{2}\right\rfloor

2-edges.

Applying this theorem with
q=5
and
t=13
gives a configuration for
K_{7}
with at least

13+\left\lfloor\frac{5}{2}\right\rfloor=15

2-edges. Since the incidence graph of
K_{7}
has
42
1-edges, we obtain

z_{L}(21,7)\geq 42+15=57.

This lifting bound is weaker than the directly verified bound
z_{L}(21,7)\geq 64
obtained in Section 4, but it remains a useful general construction principle.

For comparison, lifting the exact
q=4
value gives only

z_{L}(15,6)\geq 30+\left(6+\left\lfloor\frac{4}{2}\right\rfloor\right)=38,

which is weaker than the bound
z_{L}(15,6)\geq 43
coming from the verified
q=5
family.

7
Conclusion

For the incidence-graph family of complete graphs, we have formulated the limited augmented Zarankiewicz problem through a direct 0–1 ILP together with explicit occupancy variables and linear encodings of
(S)
,
(C2)
, and
(C3)
, and we have complemented this exact framework with a constructive search procedure and exact final admissibility verification for the lower-bound cases. This yields exact values for
(6,4)
and
(10,5)
, as well as lower bounds for
(15,6)
,
(21,7)
, and
(28,8)
:

z_{L}(6,4)=14,\qquad z_{L}(10,5)=26,\qquad z_{L}(15,6)\geq 43,

z_{L}(21,7)\geq 64,\qquad z_{L}(28,8)\geq 88.

The larger-case bounds are supported by directly verified admissible families under the definitions of
[
8
]
. These results improve the corresponding classical Zarankiewicz numbers and thus strengthen the available lower bounds for
\operatorname{BSR}(m,n)
in this setting.

We conclude with the following three open problems.

1.

Determine the exact values of
z_{L}(15,6)
,
z_{L}(21,7)
, and
z_{L}(28,8)
in the incidence-graph family.

2.

Determine more precisely the role of degenerate 2-edges, in the row/column sense of
[
8
]
, in optimal constructions.

3.

Compare the incidence-graph family with other extremal
C_{4}
-free families, and determine whether the asymptotic gap ratio can exceed
1/4
.
