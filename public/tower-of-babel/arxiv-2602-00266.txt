Complete Identification of Deep ReLU Neural Networks by Many-Valued Logic
Yani Zhang, Helmut Bölcskei

Abstract




Abstract
Deep ReLU neural networks admit nontrivial functional symmetries: vastly different architectures and parameters (weights and biases) can realize the same function. We address the complete identification problem—given a function
f
, deriving the architecture and parameters of
all
feedforward ReLU networks giving rise to
f
. We translate ReLU networks into Łukasiewicz logic formulae,
and effect
functional equivalent network transformations through algebraic rewrites governed by the logic axioms.
A compositional norm form is proposed to facilitate the mapping from Łukasiewicz logic formulae back to ReLU networks. Using Chang’s completeness theorem, we show that for every functional equivalence class, all ReLU networks in that class are connected by a finite set of symmetries corresponding to the finite set of axioms of Łukasiewicz logic.
This idea is reminiscent of Shannon’s seminal work on switching circuit design, where the circuits are translated into Boolean formulae, and synthesis is effected by algebraic rewriting governed by Boolean logic axioms.

Introduction

0.1.1
Background and existing work

Deep learning has achieved unprecedented success
in various applications,
and is being deployed in production models
of numerous fields. The success of deep learning largely depends on, among other factors, the
expressive power of deep neural networks in realizing a vast class of functions
(see, e.g.,
[
11
,
20
,
1
]
).
Behind this expressive power is the flexible architecture and parameter space of neural networks.
The flexibility, nevertheless,
also leads to nonuniqueness in neural network realization of functions:
There exist networks with different architectures, or the same architecture but different parameters,
that realize the same function. In this paper, we address the nonuniqueness in neural network realization of functions,
focusing on the complete identification of neural networks with the ReLU nonlinearity

\rho:x\mapsto\max(0,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
from their realized functions.
Specifically, we are interested
in systematically
characterizing how the architecture and parameters of functionally equivalent ReLU networks

We view a ReLU network as a computational graph
with weighted edges, input, output, and possibly also hidden nodes,

The directed edges represent the flow of data; each non-input node

A symmetry of ReLU networks is an equality

The nonuniqueness in ReLU network realization of functions

All networks with the fixed architecture realizing

In this case,

In the general case with no restriction on the architecture,

The complete identification of general ReLU networks

We also remark that the complete identification of neural networks with the

We hasten to add that
we are concerned with the fundamental nonuniqueness in ReLU network realization of functions without

Practical learning algorithms work

Several methods

Symmetries that lead to

Neither do we consider the construction of ReLU networks
to realize given functions; relevant work can be found in e.g.,
[

Instead, we start from a general ReLU network and

0.1.3
The idea: many-valued logic

The main idea of this paper is to
apply Łukasiewicz infinite-valued logic (in short, Łukasiewicz logic)
to analyze the nonuniqueness of
ReLU networks.
With truth values taken from the real interval
[0,1]
,
it is a generalization of the
\{0,1\}
-valued Boolean logic.
On the question of ReLU network identification,
as we shall see in detail in later sections, Łukasiewicz logic
offers a new suite of language and tools.
The high-level idea is as follows.
We express the input-output maps of ReLU networks
by Łukasiewicz logic formulae,
and carry out the modification of ReLU networks
through syntactic manipulation of the associated formulae; the manipulation is further guided by
the logic axioms.
Before getting into the details, we first review the relevant concepts.

As mentioned above,
Łukasiewicz logic is a generalization of Boolean logic.

We call a hidden ReLU node

\langle\hskip-1.70709pt\langle v\rangle\hskip-1.70709pt\rangle(x)>0

A ReLU network can be arbitrarily manipulated by
pumping it with dead ReLU nodes that are always inactive on the whole domain.
Similarly,
if
a hidden ReLU node
is always active on the domain, its activation function reduces to the identity function
and the node
can be easily absorbed into its previous or next layer.
But such manipulation is meaningless.
Likewise, by the equality

\rho(x)=\underbrace{\frac{1}{m}\rho(x)+\cdots+\frac{1}{m}\rho(x)}_{m\text{ times }},m\in{\mathbb{N}},m\geq 2,

a ReLU network can be vaculously manipulated by splitting a ReLU neuron into
m
copies in the same layer
with the same local map. To avoid such meangless nonuniqueness phenomena,
we require the following non-degeneracy condition.

Definition 10
.

Consider a ReLU network
with the node set
V
,
the set of input nodes
V^{(0)}
with
V^{(0)}=d_{0}
, and the output node
{v_{\text{out}}}
. We call it non-degenerate on the domain
[0,1]^{d_{0}}

if it satisfies

•

for every
v\in V\backslash(V^{(0)}\cup\{{v_{\text{out}}}\})
, there exists
x,x^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{d_{0}}
satisfying
\langle\hskip-1.70709pt\langle v\rangle\hskip-1.70709pt\rangle(x)>0
and
\langle\hskip-1.70709pt\langle v\rangle\hskip-1.70709pt\rangle(x^{\prime})\leq 0
.

•

for
{v_{\text{out}}}
, not all weights in the set
\{w_{{v_{\text{out}}},v}:v\in\mathrm{par}(v)\}
are zero.

•

there do not exist two nodes at the same level that have the same local map.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we always consider non-degenerate ReLU networks throughout the paper.

Two other

have been proposed in the literature.

We now briefly review these existing normal forms and compare them with the new normal form we propose above.

Mundici
[
24
]
proposed a normal form whose minterms are formulae associated with Schauder hats, which are functions of pyramidal
shape supported on unions of simplices. Formulae in this normal form are built up by concatenating

Di Nola and Lettieri
[

\oplus

Our normal form differs from the existing ones most prominently in the way the minterms are connected.
In

to form a normal formula.
In contrast, all other normal forms mentioned above build normal formulae by way of concatenation.

Finally, we remark that the existing normal forms
are proposed for certain utilitarian purposes. Disjunctive and conjunctive normal forms are useful for
automated theorem proving,
Di Nola and Lettieri’s normal form is proposed to study the length of Łukasiewicz logic formulae,
and Mundic’s normal form is proposed as a consequence of a constructive proof of the McNaughton theorem.
Similarly, we propose the
substitution-based normal form above in the context of ReLU network identification.
The further development of this normal form and the systematic study of its computational complexity and conversion with other normal forms
are outside the scope of this paper; we leave it to future work.
