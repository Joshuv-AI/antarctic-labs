Zenon Jan Jabłoński, Il Bong Jung, Paweł Pietrzycki and Jan Stochel

Abstract
. We investigate the n n th root problem for bounded operators on a Hilbert space within the class of conditionally positive definite (CPD) operators determined by the Lévy–Khintchine formula. The class contains subnormal operators, complete hypercontractions of order 2 2 , and 3 3 -isometries. Our main result shows that if T T is a CPD operator such that T n T^{n} is subnormal (resp., quasinormal, normal, or a 3 3 -isometry), then T T belongs to the corresponding class. This establishes the invariance of these classes under taking n n th roots within the CPD class and extends several earlier results in operator theory. Furthermore, we provide characterizations of quasinormal and normal operators in terms of their CPD property and the structure of the representing triplet. Finally, we show that the classes of CPD and normaloid operators are distinct by means of both theoretical arguments and explicit examples.

Preliminaries

In this paper, we use the following notation. The fields of real and
complex numbers are denoted by
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
,
respectively. The symbols
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{N}
, and

\mathbb{R}_{+}
stand for the sets of nonnegative integers, positive
integers, and nonnegative real numbers, respectively. Given a set

\varDelta\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, we write
\varDelta^{*}=\{\bar{z}\colon z\in\varDelta\}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{B}(\varOmega)
the

\sigma
-algebra of all Borel subsets of a Hausdorff topological
space
\varOmega
.

A sequence
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is said to be
positive definite
(
PD
for brevity) if

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{k}\gamma_{i+j}\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geq 0

(2.1)

for all finite sequences
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. The sequence
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is said to be
conditionally
positive definite
(
CPD
) if (
2.1
) holds for all finite
sequences
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}=0
. Trivially, every PD sequence is CPD.

A sequence
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is
said to be a
Stieltjes moment sequence
if there exists
a
positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such
that

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}t^{n}\,d\mu(t),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(2.2)

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
(
2.2
) is called a
representing measure
of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. The
Stieltjes theorem states that
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is a Stieltjes moment
sequence if and only if the sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
and

\{\gamma_{n+1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are PD (see

[
7
, Theorem 6.2.5]
). If
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is a Stieltjes moment
sequence which has a unique representing measure, then we say that

\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is
determinate
. It is well known that if a Stieltjes
moment sequence has a representing measure with compact support, then
it is determinate. The reader is referred to
[
7
]
for the
foundations of the theory of moment problems.

The
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on a (complex)
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The identity operator
on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
I
. Let
\mathscr{A}
be a
\sigma
-algebra of
subsets of a set
\varOmega
, and let
F\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

be a
semispectral measure
, that is,
\langle F(\cdot)f,f\rangle
is a positive measure for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Denote by

L^{1}(F)
the vector space of all
\mathscr{A}
-measurable functions

f\colon\varOmega\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\int_{\varOmega}|f(x)|\,\langle F(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x)h,h\rangle<\infty\quad\text{for all }h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then, for every
f\in L^{1}(F)
, there exists a unique operator

\int_{\varOmega}f\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that (see, e.g.,
[
43
, Appendix]
)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\int_{\varOmega}f\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F\,h,h\Big\rangle=\int_{\varOmega}f(x)\,\langle F(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x)h,h\rangle,\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

In this paper, we do not assume that a semispectral measure
F
on

\mathscr{A}
is normalized, that is,
F(\varOmega)=I
. Observe that if

F
is a
spectral measure
, that is, a normalized semispectral
measure such that

is an orthogonal projection for

\varDelta\in\mathscr{A}
, then
\int_{\varOmega}f\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F

coincides with the usual spectral integral. If
F
is the spectral
measure of a normal operator
T
, then we write

f(T)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F
for a Borel function
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
; the map

is called the
Stone–von Neumann functional calculus. We refer the reader to

[
45
,
8
,
36
]
for the basic facts of spectral theory.

An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
subnormal
if there
exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and a normal operator

N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
that extends

of a subnormal operator

is the orthogonal projection of

onto
\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle T^{*m}T^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\bar{z}^{m}z^{n}\,G(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}z),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Hence, applying the measure transport theorem (cf.

\displaystyle T^{*n}T^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{n}\,G\circ\phi^{-1}(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(2.3)

where

(see
[
27
]

Following

We will show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{NMD}\not\subseteq\mathcal{CPD}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{CPD}\not\subseteq\mathcal{NMD},

(6.1)

We begin by proving, using a purely theoretical argument, that the
first relation listed in (

) holds when the class

is replaced by any scalable class of Hilbert space
operators that properly contains the class of subnormal operators.
Recall that a class

for every

[

]
for more examples). On the other hand, the

-isometric,

[

[

[

Proposition 6.1
.

Let

be a scalable class of Hilbert space operators
that is not contained in the class of subnormal operators. Then

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that

. Let

. Then

for all

\alpha T\in\mathcal{CPD}
for all

. By

[
24

, this implies that

is subnormal.
Hence,
\mathcal{C}
is contained in the class of subnormal

∎
