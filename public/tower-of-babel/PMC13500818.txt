Active volume scaling of gold vapor laser oscillating in ultraviolet and deep ultraviolet spectral ranges
Authors: Ivan Kirilov Kostadinov, Krassimir Angelov Temelkov, Lidia Todorova Popova, Stefka Ivanova Slaveeva, Georgi Petkov Yankov, Borislav Lubomirov Ivanov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUsing six laser tubes with various diameters, lengths, and volumes of the active zone, an investigation on an ultraviolet gold vapor laser is undertaken. We achieve a maximal average output power of 1.07 W at 312.2 nm wavelength. We also obtain a specific average laser power of 56.3 mW.cm− 3, which is more than 6 times higher than the value described so far. Polarized high-beam-quality laser radiation is generated for the first time through an unstable cavity equipped with a Glan prism. In addition, the sum frequency mixing of the fundamental 312.2-nm and 627.8-nm output yields laser oscillation at a new spectral 208.5-nm line. This laser source constitutes a cheaper alternative of excimer and other UV lasers for materials surface modification and photochemistry applications.Keywords: Metal vapor lasers, Gold vapor laser, Master oscillator, High-beam-quality laser radiation, Sum-frequency mixing.Subject terms: Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Metal vapor lasers operating on the atomic self-terminating transitions of different chemical elements, such as copper (Cu), gold (Au), strontium (Sr), lead (Pb), iron (Fe), etc., are still very promising, because of the possibility to deliver significant average output power at relatively high efficiency in any spectral range, namely from ultraviolet (UV) to mid infrared ones. Even the average laser powers of 550 W and 10 kW have been obtained with single-tube copper vapor laser (CVL) and master socillator – power amplifiers (MOPAs) system2, respectively, are still the highest ones published to date for the visible spectral range. Precise microprocessing through laser ablation requires laser radiation in the deep ultraviolet (DUV) and ultraviolet (UV) spectral diapasons, since the size of the processed area after focusing depends linearly on the laser radiation wavelength. Except that these lasers achieve the necessarily high resolution, they have sufficiently high photon energy to induce some desired phenomena in different media, such as material and its surface modification, image recording, fluorescence, etc. Well-developed DUV and UV laser sources, such as excimer lasers and frequency-converted solid state lasers, prevail in those spectral ranges. Unfortunately, the gold vapor laser (GVL) is the only laser source that operates in the UV spectral range on the atomic self-terminating transition. The GVLs are less researched and developed in comparison with the CVLs and their various versions, due to the considerably higher energy of the upper laser levels in GVL than the ones in CVL, which requires higher electron temperature for their population, and severe technical difficulties related to the extremely high operating temperature for both atomic Au 627.8- and 312.2-nm lines. The highest average laser power of 20 W at the 627.8-nm line has been delivered by a laser tube with an active volume of 2375.8 cm3 (55 mm inside diameter and 100 cm length) at a pulse repetition frequency (prf) of 5.5 kHz and an average electrical power of 15.7 kW1. These results correspond to a specific average output powert (SAOP) of 0.008 W.cm− 3 and a wall-plug efficiency of 0.13%. Utilizing kinetically enhanced method for excitation, which has considerably improved the output parameters of CVLs, a discharge tube with an active volume of 231.9 cm3 (22 mm inside diameter and 61 cm length) has produced at the 627.8-nm line an average laser power of 11.6 W at a prf of 27.5 kHz and an average electrical power of 3.9 kW3. The corresponding SAOP of 0.050 W.cm− 3 and the wall-plug efficiency of 0.30% are the highest SAOP and efficiency reported till now for the GVLs.

The upper laser level for the UV atomic Au line is at even higher energy than the one of the atomic Au 627.8-nm laser line, while the lower metastable level is so close to the ground state that its thermal population is always too great to allow a population inversion to be achieved. Output at the UV Au line has been obsereved under extreme and contradictory gas-discharge conditions, such as rapid application of very high voltages to the laser tube (higher than 25 kV), unusually low neon (Ne) buffer-gas pressure (lower than 10 Torr) and low prf (lower than 10 kHz). Investigating two discharge tubes, namely the first laser tube have been with an active volume of 18.4 cm3 (6 mm inside diameter and 65 cm length), while the second discharge tube have had an active volume of 140.7 cm3 (16-mm inside diameter and 70-cm length), average output powers of 110 mW and 1.20 W, respectively, have been declared4 at the 312.2-nm line, as the optimal neon pressure and prf have been 10 Torr and 9.1 kHz, respectively. The second average laser power is still the record-high value published to date. A special high-power high-voltage (HV) power supply with three hydrogen thyratron TGI 1000/25 connected in parallel has been used4, in order to decrease the risetime of the excitation pulse. By decreasing the buffer-gas pressure and prf to less than 15 Torr and 9 kHz, respectively, weak laser oscillation at the 312.2-nm line has been also observed3, but the average output power has been insufficient to be accurately measured.

In this paper, using a single-thyratron high-power HV power supply based on Blumlein excitation scheme with pulse compression circuits, an active volume scaling of an UV GVL is carried out studying six laser tubes with different diameters, lengths and volumes of the active zone. The highest average laser power of 1.07 W at the atomic Au 312-nm line is delivered by a discharge tube with an active volume of 49.5 cm3 (10-mm inside diameter and 63-cm length), which is only 0.13 W lower than the record-high value of 1.20 W measured so far4. The highest SAOP of 56.3 mW.cm− 3, which is more than 6 times higher than the SAOP of 8.5 mW.cm− 34, is produced through a laser tube delivering average laser power of 304 mW with an active volume of 5.4 cm3 (a 4-mm bore and an active length of 43 cm). Placing this second discharge tube (5.4-cm3 active volume) in a negative-branch unstable laser cavity with magnification M = 20, which incorporates a Glan Prism (GP) for linear polarization of the laser beam, polarized high-beam-quality output is obtained for the first time. Focusing the laser beam into a 5-mm-long nonlinear crystal made of β-Barium Borate (BBO) crystal, DUV laser oscillation is obtained for the first time at a new spectral line with a wavelength of 208.5 nm via Sum-Frequency Mixing (SFM) of the two atomic Au 312.2- and 627.8-nm lines. Experimental setup

The active volume scaling is made by investigation of six laser tubes with different diameter, length and volume of the active zone. The construction of the fifth of the sixth discharge tubes is plotted in Fig. 1a, while the sixth laser tube has special design depicted in Fig. 1b. The features of the discharge tubes are summarized in Table 2. The active zone of the laser tube LT2 is confined by sixteen Al2O3 ceramic inserts, which have inside and outside diameters of 4 mm and 7 mm, respectively, and are coaxially mounted in a BeO ceramic tube with an inside diameter of 7 mm and a length of 50 cm. The Au pieces, which are not larger than 2 mm, are placed between the Al2O3 ceramic inserts. Hence, the length of the active zone is 43 cm, i.e. the active volume is 5.4 cm3. Compact thermal powder insulation of ZrO2 or Al2O3 between the main ceramic insert (carrier tube insert in the case of LT2) and the basic tube made of fused quartz is used. Cylindrical or porous Cu electrode are utilized. Silica windows with double-sided anti-reflection coating are sealed to the ends of each tube studied.

The laser tubes are studied with a stable cavity, which consists of a concave high-reflectivity mirror with a radius of curvature of 12 m and an uncoated plane-parallel CaF2 plate as an output coupler with calculated reflectivity of 6.9%. The optical cavity length is as short as the tube length allows.

The investigated discharge tubes are excited via a single-hydrogen-thyratron (EEV CX 1835) high-power HV power supply based on a command charged symmetrical Blumlein (CCSB) scheme with magnetic assisting inductor, magnetic pulse compression circuits and peaking capacitor. The average output power is measured through a calorimetric power–energy meter Scientech Inc. Vector S310 with a sensitivity range from 200 nm to 10 μm.

For the nonlinear frequency conversion experiments, the laser tube LT2 is placed in a negative-branch confocal unstable cavity with magnification M = 20, which consists of two concave mirrors with radii of curvature of 2 m and 10 cm, respectively, and a GP for vertical linear polarization of the laser beam. The high-beam-quality polarized output is extracted using a 45o scraper mirror with a 500-µm central hole placed at the common focal point of the two cavity mirrors. Laser beam is focused with a concave mirror with a focusing distance of 25 cm into a BBO nonlinear crystal and recollimated with a lens, which has a focusing distance of 15 cm is made of CaF2. Though the BBO crystal is cut at an angle that corresponds to the phase-matching angle for the Second Harmonic Generation (SHG) of Nd: YAG laser at 532 nm, it has sufficient aperture to be tuned for the SHG of 627.8 nm and SFM of 312.2 nm and 627.8 nm.The studied nonlinear crystal is placed on a rotary table for precise optimization and measurement of the angle of incidence. The rotary table itself has adjustable height (along the Z axis) and is also mounted on a X-Y optical stage. Thus, the focusing spot position is three-dimensionally controlled with four degrees of freedom.

Results and discussion

A stable laser operation is obtained at the atomic Au 312- and 627.8-nm lines in a nanosecond pulsed longitudinal discharge in neon (Ne) buffer gas and hydrogen (H2) – Ne buffer-gas mixture with each of the discharge tubes studied. The ratio of 0.05 between the partial pressures of H2 and Ne is kept constant, when H2–Ne buffer-gas mixture is used. It is well known that small amount of H2 to the Ne buffer gas improves considerably the output characteristics of the lasers oscillating on the self-terminating transitions.

Тhe highest average laser power is achieved with the laser tube LT5 (49.5-cm3 active volume). The dependence of the average output power on the H2–Ne buffer-gas mixture pressure is shown in Fig. 2 for the discharge tube LT5 with all the relevant input parameters listed on the graph. The average laser power monotonically increases with the reduction in the H2–Ne buffer-gas mixture pressure as visible from Fig. 2.

The discharge tube LT2 is sealed-off at partial pressures of 2 Torr and 38 Torr for H2 and Ne respectively and is placed in a negative-branch confocal unstable laser cavity with magnification 20 equipped with a GP for vertical linear polarization of the laser beam. UV polarized high-beam-quality laser radiation is produced for the first time with an average laser power of 190 mW.

Also using average output power of 200 mW at the atomic Au 627.8-nm laser line and focusing the laser beam into the BBO nonlinear crystal at an angle of incidence of 40o, a weak laser oscillation is obtained for the first time at a new spectral line with a wavelength of 208.5 nm by SFM. Unfortunately, the average laser power is insufficient to be accurately measured with a power meter, i.e. it is less than 1 mW. Though the nonlinear crystal at such an angle of incidence acts as a prism, i.e. the overlapping of the laser beams at the two fundamental wavelengths is very small, the laser oscillation at the 208.5-nm line still exists even with a decrease in the average output powers at the 627.8-nm and 312.2-nm laser lines down to 8 mW and 1.5 mW, respectively.

Conclusions

The active volume scaling of the ultraviolet GVL allows us to obtain not just the first measurable average laser power in almost five decades, but to achieve an average output power of 1.07 W at the atomic Au 312-nm line, which is even comparable to the highest value of 1.20 W reported in 1978. The SAOPs of 56.3 mW.cm− 3 and 177 mW.cm− 3 are more than 6 and 3 times higher than the SAOPs obtained so far at the 312.2- and 627.8-nm laser lines. UV polarized high-beam-quality laser beam is produced for the first time via a negative-branch confocal unstable cavity equipped with a Glan prism for linear polarization of the laser beam. Laser oscillation is obtained for the first time at a new spectral line with a wavelength of 208.5 nm by SFM of the two atomic Au 312.2- and 627.8-nm lines.
