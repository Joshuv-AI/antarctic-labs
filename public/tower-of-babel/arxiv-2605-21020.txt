Microwave Linear Analog Computer (MiLAC)-Aided MIMO Radar Sensing: Transmit Beamforming Design and DoA Estimation
Ziang Liu, Zheyu Wu, and Bruno Clerckx

Abstract
Multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) radar has waveform diversity and large spatial degrees of freedom (DoFs), making it attractive for high-resolution sensing. Scaling MIMO radar to massive arrays can further improve sensing performance, but it also increases hardware cost, power consumption, and digital processing complexity. The microwave linear analog computer (MiLAC) can tackle these challenges by moving linear operations from the digital domain to the analog domain. MiLAC has shown promising benefits for communications in recent studies and this paper identifies its potential for radar sensing. Specifically, we consider both MiLAC-aided transmit beamforming and receiver-side two-dimensional discrete Fourier transform (2D-DFT)-based direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation. For transmit beamforming, we formulate a weighted Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) minimization problem under lossless and reciprocal MiLAC constraints and propose a penalty dual decomposition (PDD)-based iterative algorithm to address the non-convex problem. We further prove that MiLAC-aided and fully-digital beamforming achieve the same CRB. For receiver processing, we show that the 2D DFT can be implemented by a lossless reciprocal MiLAC, which enables analog-domain DoA estimation without digital optimization. Numerical results confirm the theoretical finding and show that the MiLAC-aided approach achieves the same CRB and DoA estimation performance as the fully-digital benchmark. Meanwhile, hardware cost and power consumption are reduced because only low-resolution DACs are required at the transmitter, while RF chains and ADCs are eliminated at the receiver. Moreover, performing the 2D DFT in the analog domain eliminates all digital DFT operations for DoA estimation.

However, these results focus on communication systems. The use of MiLAC for MIMO radar sensing, especially under practical lossless and reciprocal constraints, remains unexplored. Recent prototype-level evidence shows that hybrid couplers and phase shifters can realize linear transforms,

In radar systems, scaling to massive arrays increases not only hardware cost and power consumption due to high-resolution DACs and RF chains, but also computational complexity due to large-dimensional DFT operations for DoA estimation. To address these challenges, this paper investigates MiLAC-aided MIMO radar for the first time from both the transmitter and receiver sides. Under lossless and reciprocal constraints, we show that MiLAC-aided radar sensing has the following benefits.

1) MiLAC achieves the same sensing performance as fully-digital beamforming
, since the set of achievable transmit covariance matrices under MiLAC constraints is identical to that of fully-digital beamforming, and the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) depends on the beamforming matrix only through the transmit covariance (See Section
III-B
). In addition, the 2D DFT-based DoA estimation can be exactly implemented by a lossless reciprocal MiLAC (See Section
IV
).

2) MiLAC reduces hardware cost and power consumption
, because MiLAC enables fully analog beamforming and thus the RF chains can directly carry radar-modulated signals with lower-resolution DACs/ADCs.

3) MiLAC requires no per-symbol digital processing
, as the transmit beamforming in MIMO radar is entirely implemented in the analog domain.

4) DFT-based DoA estimation can be achieved with minimum computational complexity
, because the DFT can be exactly implemented by a lossless reciprocal MiLAC without any offline optimization.

5) Ultra-low latency especially in massive MIMO radar
due to that the DFT is performed in the analog domain. This is particularly beneficial for real-time detection and tracking applications such as automotive radar.
These advantages make MiLAC especially appealing for massive array and real-time radar applications.

Contributions and Overview of Results.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1.

This is the first work to investigate MiLAC-aided MIMO radar sensing under practical lossless and reciprocal constraints. Specifically, we formulate transmit beamforming for MiLAC-aided MIMO radar as a weighted CRB minimization problem for multi-target 2D angle estimation, under both the total transmit power constraint and the lossless reciprocal constraints imposed by the MiLAC impedance network. On the receiver side, we show that the 2D DFT can be implemented by a lossless reciprocal MiLAC, which enables analog-domain DoA estimation without offline digital optimization.

2.

We prove that MiLAC-aided and fully-digital beamforming achieve the same optimal CRB. The key insight is that the CRB depends on the beamforming matrix only through the transmit covariance, and we show that the set of achievable transmit covariance matrices is identical under both architectures.

3.

We develop a penalty dual decomposition (PDD)-based iterative algorithm
[
33
]
to solve the non-convex transmit beamforming problem. The algorithm alternately updates the beamforming matrix, power allocation vector, and auxiliary variable until convergence.

4.

Numerical results confirm that the proposed MiLAC-aided beamforming design achieves the same CRB as fully-digital beamforming and can probe the beams at the target angles. On the receiver side, MiLAC-aided 2D-DFT DoA estimation achieves the same performance as fully-digital DFT-based DoA estimation. Furthermore, the receiver-side computational complexity of 2D-DFT-based DoA estimation is significantly reduced by MiLAC, especially for large arrays. These gains in sensing performance are obtained with lower hardware cost and computational complexity compared to fully-digital approaches.

5.

Overall, under lossless and reciprocal constraints, we show that 1) MiLAC achieves the same sensing performance as fully-digital beamforming, 2) MiLAC reduces hardware cost and power consumption, 3) MiLAC requires no per-symbol digital processing, 4) DFT-based DoA estimation can be achieved with minimum computational complexity, and 5) Ultra-low latency especially in massive MIMO radar.

\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Digital}}=\{\mathbf{W}\mid\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}\leqslant P_{T}\}
, which also imposes the same total transmit power constraint as the MiLAC-aided beamforming. This ensures a fair comparison between MiLAC-aided and fully-digital beamforming under the same transmit power budget.

Since each parameter
\xi_{i}
belongs to a specific target
k
, and only
\mathbf{A}_{k}
depends on the parameters of target
k
, the derivatives of
\bm{\mu}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}
w.r.t. target
k
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\vartheta_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\vartheta_{k}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r,k}\,\dot{\mathbf{A}}_{\vartheta_{k}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S},

(19a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\varphi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\varphi_{k}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{r,k}\,\dot{\mathbf{A}}_{\varphi_{k}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S},

(19b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\bm{\alpha}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\bm{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\jmath\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\otimes\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S},

(19c)

where
\dot{\mathbf{A}}_{\vartheta_{k}}\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}_{k}}{\partial\vartheta_{k}}
and
\dot{\mathbf{A}}_{\varphi_{k}}\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}_{k}}{\partial\varphi_{k}}
denote the derivatives of the per-target round-trip steering matrix w.r.t. the elevation and azimuth angles, respectively. Their explicit expressions are provided in Appendix A.

For estimating
\bm{\xi}
from
L
observations, the FIM has the block structure
[
14
]

\mathbf{J}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}&\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}\\
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}^{\top}&\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}\end{array}\right].

(20)

The submatrices
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 2K}
,
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 2K}
, and
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 2K}
are assembled from the contributions of all target pairs. Each scalar entry of the FIM is given by

[\mathbf{J}]_{i,j}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\mathfrak{R}\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}[\ell]^{H}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}[\ell]}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right\},\quad i,j\in\{1,\dots,4K\}.

(21)

Substituting the derivatives (
19
), which share the common structure
\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\xi_{i}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}
, into (
21
) and using
\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{H}\}=L\mathbf{I}
yields

[\mathbf{J}]_{i,j}=\frac{2L}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\mathfrak{R}\left\{\operatorname{tr}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}^{H}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\mathbf{R}_{x}\right)\right\},

(22)

where
\mathbf{R}_{x}\triangleq\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
is the transmit covariance matrix. This shows that the FIM, and hence the CRB, depends on
\mathbf{W}
only through
\mathbf{R}_{x}
.

III-B

Equivalence of MiLAC-aided and Digital Beamforming in Terms of CRB

The following theorem shows that, despite the restricted beamforming flexibility of lossless and reciprocal MiLAC, the MiLAC-aided and fully-digital designs achieve the same optimal CRB.

Theorem 1
.

Under the assumption
M=N
to achieve full DoFs, let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}\triangleq\{\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}\mid\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}\}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}}\triangleq\{\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}\mid\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Digital}}\}
denote the sets of transmit covariance matrices achievable by MiLAC-aided and fully-digital beamforming, respectively. Then

\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}}=\{\mathbf{R}_{x}\succeq\mathbf{0}\mid\tr(\mathbf{R}_x)\leqslant P_{T}\}.

(23)

Consequently, MiLAC-aided and fully-digital transmit beamforming designs achieve the same optimal CRB value.

Proof.

We prove the two inclusions separately.

(\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}})
: Since every MiLAC-achievable beamforming matrix is also digitally achievable,
i.e.,

\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Digital}}

[
41
]
, any transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
realized by some
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}
is also realized by the same
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Digital}}
.

(\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}})
: Let
\mathbf{R}_{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}}
be an arbitrary digitally achievable transmit covariance. By definition, there exists some
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Digital}}
with
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}{\mathbf{W}^{\prime}}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
. To show
\mathbf{R}_{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}
, it suffices to find
one

\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}
satisfying
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
. Consider the eigendecomposition
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^{H}
, where
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is unitary and
\bm{\Lambda}=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})\succeq\mathbf{0}
. Construct
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and set
\mathbf{p}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})^{\top}
. Then: (i)
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
; (ii)
\mathbf{W}^{H}\mathbf{W}=\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{H}\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{\Lambda}=\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})
, so
\mathbf{W}^{H}\mathbf{W}\preceq\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})
; (iii)
\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}=\tr(\bLam)=\tr(\mathbf{R}_x)\leqslant P_{T}
. Hence
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}
by (
13
), confirming
\mathbf{R}_{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}
.

Combining both inclusions yields
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{MiLAC}}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{Digital}}
. Since the FIM
\mathbf{J}
in (
20
) is a function of
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
only, the CRB
\tr(\mathbf{J}^{-1})
is identical for any two beamforming matrices sharing the same
\mathbf{R}_{x}
. The equality of the covariance sets therefore implies that MiLAC-aided and fully-digital transmit beamforming designs achieve the same optimal CRB value.

\blacksquare

Remark 1
.

Theorem
1
stands in contrast to the communication setting in
[
41
]
, where lossless and reciprocal MiLAC-aided beamforming leads to a sum-rate loss compared to fully-digital beamforming at high SNR. The difference arises because the CRB depends on
\mathbf{W}
only through
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
, whereas the multiuser sum-rate depends on the individual columns of
\mathbf{W}
via the per-user SINR. The spectral-norm constraint
\|\mathbf{F}\|_{2}\leqslant 1
imposed by lossless and reciprocal MiLAC restricts the column correlations of
\mathbf{W}
, which excludes full-power matrices with non-orthogonal columns
[
41
]
. This restriction affects the sum-rate, which is sensitive to the column-level structure, but does not affect the CRB, which depends only on the aggregate spatial covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}
.

III-C

MiLAC Transmit Beamforming Design

We aim to design the beamforming matrix
\mathbf{W}
to minimize the total CRB across all target parameters and thus improve the sensing performance, subject to the MiLAC lossless and reciprocal constraints in (
13
). This yields the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}1:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\tr\{\bm{\Xi}\}

(24a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{H}\mathbf{W}\preceq\text{diag}(\mathbf{p}),

(24b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}\leqslant P_{T}.

(24c)

Directly minimizing
\tr\{\bm{\Xi}\}=\tr\{\mathbf{J}^{-1}\}
is non-convex due to the matrix inverse.
Following
[
17
]
, we introduce auxiliary variables
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}
and reformulate the problem using the Schur complement. Specifically, the condition
t_{k}\geqslant[\mathbf{J}^{-1}]_{k,k}
is equivalently expressed as the linear matrix inequality (LMI) in (
25
), where
\mathbf{e}_{k}
denotes the
k
th column of the
4K
-dimensional identity matrix. Since the FIM
\mathbf{J}
in (
20
) is a linear function of the transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}\triangleq\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
, the resulting formulation is a semidefinite program (SDP) w.r.t.
\mathbf{R}_{x}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}2:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{4K}\mu_{k}t_{k}

(25a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{J}&\mathbf{e}_{k}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}&t_{k}\end{array}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},\,k=1,\cdots,4K

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{W}\\
\mathbf{W}^{H}&\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})\end{matrix}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},

(25d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}\leqslant P_{T}.

(25e)

The LMI constraint (
25d
) is transformed from the original quadratic constraint
\mathbf{W}^{H}\mathbf{W}\preceq\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})
by Schur complement.

The difficulty for solving problem
\mathcal{P}2
is that the FIM depends on
\mathbf{W}
through the transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H}
, whereas the MiLAC constraint involves the Gram matrix
\mathbf{W}^{H}\mathbf{W}
. This creates a non-trivial coupling between the two sides of
\mathbf{W}
. To decouple them, we introduce an auxiliary copy
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{W}
so that the covariance constraint
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}
is separated from the MiLAC constraint on
\mathbf{W}
. This leads to the following reformulation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}3:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{4K}\mu_{k}t_{k}

(26a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{J}&\mathbf{e}_{k}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}&t_{k}\end{array}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},\,k=1,\cdots,4K

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{W}\\
\mathbf{W}^{H}&\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})\end{matrix}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},

(26d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}\leqslant P_{T},

(26e)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{X},

(26f)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}.

(26g)

Problem
\mathcal{P}3
involves two coupled constraints: the equality
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{X}
in (
26f
) and the quadratic relation
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
in (
26g
). To address this problem, the penalty dual decomposition (PDD) method
[
33
]
is applied to solve (
26
) iteratively. Specifically, the PDD method introduces the augmented Lagrangian function by dualizing the equality constraint (
26f
) with a Lagrange multiplier
\mathbf{\Gamma}
and adding a quadratic penalty term with penalty parameter
\rho>0
. The resulting problem is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}4:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{4K}\mu_{k}t_{k}+\frac{1}{2\rho}\|\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{X}+\rho\mathbf{\Gamma}\|^{2}

(27a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{J}&\mathbf{e}_{k}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}&t_{k}\end{array}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},\,k=1,\cdots,4K

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{W}\\
\mathbf{W}^{H}&\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})\end{matrix}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},

(27d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}\leqslant P_{T},

(27e)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}.

(27f)

The PDD consists of an inner loop that solves the augmented Lagrangian problem and an outer loop that updates the dual variable and penalty parameter. In the inner loop, the problem is solved by alternating optimization over three variable blocks:
\{\mathbf{R}_{x},\{t_{k}\}\}
,
\{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{p}\}
, and
\mathbf{X}
. When updating each block, other variables are fixed and the designed variable is optimized by solving a convex sub-problem. In the outer loop, the dual variable
\mathbf{\Gamma}
is updated by a gradient ascent step, and the penalty parameter
\rho
is updated by a geometric decay rule. This alternating optimization continues until convergence. The details of the inner loop block update and outer loop update are explained below.

III-C
1
Inner Loop Block Update

The details of the inner loop block update are as follows.

Update
\mathbf{R}_{x},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}
.

With
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{X}
fixed, the penalty term in (
27a
) is constant, and thus the sub-problem is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}5:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{x},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{4K}\mu_{k}t_{k}

(28a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{R}_{x},\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{4K}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{J}&\mathbf{e}_{k}\\
\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}&t_{k}\end{array}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},\,k=1,\cdots,4K

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{x}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(28d)

which is a SDP since the FIM
\mathbf{J}
is a linear function of
\mathbf{R}_{x}
, and can be solved efficiently.

Update
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{W}
.

With
\mathbf{R}_{x}
,
\{t_{k}\}
, and
\mathbf{X}
fixed, only the augmented Lagrangian penalty term depends on
\mathbf{W}
. The sub-problem w.r.t.
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{p}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}6:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{W}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\|\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{X}+\rho\mathbf{\Gamma}\|^{2}

(29a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{W}\\
\mathbf{W}^{H}&\text{diag}(\mathbf{p})\end{matrix}\right]\succeq\mathbf{0},

(29b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{p}\leqslant P_{T}.

(29c)

This is a also SDP and can be solved efficiently.

Update
\mathbf{X}
.

With
\mathbf{R}_{x}
,
\mathbf{W}
, and
\mathbf{p}
fixed, the sub-problem w.r.t
\mathbf{X}
aims to minimize the penalty term subject to the covariance constraint:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}7:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{X}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\|\mathbf{X}-(\rho\mathbf{\Gamma}+\mathbf{W})\|^{2}

(30a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{X}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}.

(30b)

To solve the sub-problem, we first define
\bm{\Delta}\triangleq\rho\mathbf{\Gamma}+\mathbf{W}
. Subsequently, we expand the objective function, which has the following form

\tr(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{X}^H)-2\mathfrak{R}(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^H \mathbf{X}))+\tr(\boldsymbol{\Delta} \boldsymbol{\Delta}^H).

(31)

Since the constraint fixes
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
, both
\tr(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{X}^H)=\tr(\mathbf{R}_x)
and
\tr(\boldsymbol{\Delta} \boldsymbol{\Delta}^H)
are constant. The problem therefore reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}8:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{X}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\mathfrak{R}(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^H \mathbf{X}))

(32a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{X}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}.

(32b)

Substituting
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{R}_{x}^{1/2}\mathbf{Q}
with
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^{H}=\mathbf{I}
, which automatically satisfies the constraint
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{H}=\mathbf{R}_{x}
, the problem becomes the unitary Procrustes problem
[
32
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}9:~

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\mathfrak{R}(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Psi}^H \mathbf{Q})),

(33a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{Q}}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^{H}=\mathbf{I},

(33b)

where
\bm{\Psi}\triangleq\mathbf{R}_{x}^{1/2}\bm{\Delta}
. Let
\bm{\Psi}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{H}
be its singular value decomposition (SVD). The closed-form solution is
[
32
]

\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{H}.

(34)

The original variable
\mathbf{X}
is then recovered as

\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{R}_{x}^{1/2}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{R}_{x}^{1/2}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{H}.

(35)

Receiver-Side MiLAC Design for DoA Estimation

IV-A

The target angles are estimated by applying the 2D DFT to the received signal in (
14

(40)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}12:~

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed a MiLAC-aided MIMO radar sensing approach that implements both transmit beamforming and receiver-side 2D-DFT-based DoA estimation. On the transmit side, we have formulated the beamforming design as a weighted CRB minimization problem subject to the total transmit power constraint and the lossless and reciprocal constraints imposed by the MiLAC, and developed a PDD-based iterative algorithm to solve the resulting non-convex problem. We have further proved that MiLAC-aided and fully-digital beamforming achieve the same optimal CRB, because the CRB depends on the beamforming matrix only through the transmit covariance and the set of achievable transmit covariance matrices is identical under both architectures. On the receiver side, we have shown that the 2D DFT can be exactly implemented by a lossless reciprocal MiLAC in closed form, enabling analog-domain DoA estimation. Numerical results have confirmed the theoretical finding and demonstrated that MiLAC-aided DoA estimation achieves MSE performance the same as the fully-digital benchmark, while significantly reducing hardware cost and computational complexity. These results confirm that MiLAC is a promising hardware architecture for MIMO radar sensing, which reduces hardware cost and power consumption due to employing low-resolution DACs at the transmitter and eliminating RF chains and ADCs at the receiver, as well as eliminates all digital DFT operations for DoA estimation.

Future work will explore designing MiLAC to support high-resolution DoA estimation algorithms such as MUSIC.

Appendix A: Derivation of The Fisher Information Matrices

In this appendix, we derive the angle-dependent derivatives of the round-trip steering matrix used in the FIM construction. Recall that
\mathbf{A}(\psi_{x},\psi_{y})=\mathbf{b}(\psi_{x},\psi_{y})\mathbf{a}^{\top}(\psi_{x},\psi_{y})\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
. Since
\mathbf{A}
depends on the electrical angles
\psi_{x}
and
\psi_{y}
, and these electrical angles are in turn functions of the physical angles
\vartheta
and
\varphi
, the chain rule gives

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\vartheta}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}\frac{\partial\psi_{x}}{\partial\vartheta}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}}\frac{\partial\psi_{y}}{\partial\vartheta},

(48)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\varphi}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}\frac{\partial\psi_{x}}{\partial\varphi}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}}\frac{\partial\psi_{y}}{\partial\varphi}.

(49)

We therefore first derive
\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}
and
\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}}
. Using the product rule, we obtain

\dot{\mathbf{A}}_{\psi_{x}}\left(\psi_{x},\psi_{y}\right)=\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{b}}{\partial\psi_{x}}\mathbf{a}^{\top}+\mathbf{b}\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}^{\top}}{\partial\psi_{x}}.

(50)

To simplify the derivation, define
\mathbf{D}_{x}=\text{diag}(0,1,2,\dots,N_{x}-1)
and
\mathbf{D}_{y}=\text{diag}(0,1,2,\dots,N_{y}-1)
. The derivatives of the one-dimensional steering vectors are then given by

\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x})}{\partial\psi_{x}}=\jmath\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x}).

(51)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y})}{\partial\psi_{y}}=\jmath\mathbf{D}_{y}\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y})

(52)

Substituting these expressions into the Kronecker-product steering-vector model yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}}{\partial\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y})\otimes\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x})}{\partial\psi_{x}}\right)=\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y})\otimes(\jmath\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x}))

(53)

\displaystyle=\jmath(\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{x})\mathbf{a}(\psi_{x},\psi_{y})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}}{\partial\psi_{y}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y})}{\partial\psi_{y}}\right)\otimes\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x})=(\jmath\mathbf{D}_{y}\mathbf{a}_{y}(\psi_{y}))\otimes\mathbf{a}_{x}(\psi_{x})

(54)

\displaystyle=\jmath(\mathbf{D}_{y}\otimes\mathbf{I})\mathbf{a}(\psi_{x},\psi_{y})

For notational convenience, define
\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}=\jmath(\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{x})
and
\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{y}}=\jmath(\mathbf{D}_{y}\otimes\mathbf{I})
. Then

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}=(\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a}^{\top}+\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}\mathbf{a})^{\top}.

(55)

Since
\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}^{\top}=\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}
, this expression simplifies to

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}=\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{x}}.

(56)

Applying the same argument to the
y
-direction gives

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}}=(\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{y}}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{a}^{\top}+\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{y}}\mathbf{a})^{\top}=\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{y}}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}_{\psi_{y}}.

(57)

We next relate the electrical-angle derivatives to the physical-angle derivatives. From (
1
), we have

\frac{\partial\psi_{x}}{\partial\vartheta}=\kappa d_{x}\cos(\vartheta)\cos(\varphi),\quad\frac{\partial\psi_{y}}{\partial\vartheta}=\kappa d_{y}\cos(\vartheta)\sin(\varphi),

(58)

\frac{\partial\psi_{x}}{\partial\varphi}=-\kappa d_{x}\sin(\vartheta)\sin(\varphi),\quad\frac{\partial\psi_{y}}{\partial\varphi}=\kappa d_{y}\sin(\vartheta)\cos(\varphi).

(59)

Substituting these derivatives into the chain-rule expressions at the beginning of the appendix yields

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\vartheta}=\left[\kappa d_{x}\cos(\vartheta)\cos(\varphi)\right]\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}+\left[\kappa d_{y}\cos(\vartheta)\sin(\varphi)\right]\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}},

(60)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\varphi}=\left[-\kappa d_{x}\sin(\vartheta)\sin(\varphi)\right]\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{x}}+\left[\kappa d_{y}\sin(\vartheta)\cos(\varphi)\right]\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\psi_{y}}.

(61)
