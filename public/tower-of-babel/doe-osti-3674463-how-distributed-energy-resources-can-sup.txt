 Options to Improve Resiliency

• Grid Improvements – strengthening the grid in areas where damage is possible or
  expected (hardening, undergrounding, etc).
• Generation Disbursement – power generation that is more likely to provide
  service when needed.
• Resiliency Hubs – defined locations designed to provide key services to a
  community members during normal times but also during disruptions and recovery,
  typically includes back-up energy services.
• Grid Isolation – isolating from the main grid in times of need (microgrids).
• Critical Energy Service Reductions – reducing need for resilience, move critical
  services to more secure locations, don’t build in vulnerable locations.
• Energy Service Alternatives - transitioning to more resilient methods to meet end
  needs – alternative lighting, locally sourced heating (wood), improved building
  design.

Additional Assessment Tools/Frameworks

• Electromagnetic Transient (EMT) models / Digital Twins (PSCAD,
  OpenDSS, and others): Allows detailed contingency modeling of electric
  power systems at any scale, addressing fault conditions and line rating.
• Microgrid Design Toolkit (MDT): Microgrid decision support software for
  microgrid designers.
• Resilient Node Cluster Analysis Tool (ReNCAT): Looks at nodal microgrids
  to understand value of potential portfolios of microgrids within a community or
  set of communities that maximize residents’ access to critical services.
• Direct Support: Multiple DOE Laboratories have expertise to support
  addressing specific resiliency questions through frameworks they have
  developed. This includes primarily NLR, Sandia, and Idaho National
  Laboratories.

Examples of Utility Engagement

Tri-State G&T Technical Assistance Focus

• Peak Assessment: Determines when member and Tri-State peaks coincide,
  directly impacting capacity value, infrastructure utilization, and system-wide cost
  savings from distributed wind-plus-storage.
• Charge/Discharge Strategy: Defines how storage is operated to reduce peaks,
  support reliability, and capture value, while avoiding unintended creation of new
  billable peaks or suboptimal dispatch outcomes.
• Dispatch and Control Hierarchy: Establishes how competing objectives (peak
  reduction, market signals, reliability, and operational constraints) are prioritized to
  ensure consistent, system-optimal operation.
• Charging Costs and Auxiliary Loads: Accounts for efficiency losses and thermal
  management energy needs that reduce net system benefit and materially affect
  realized project economics.
• Cost Allocation Structures: Evaluates how assigning charging and operating
  costs between members and Tri-State influences dispatch behavior and system
  value.
