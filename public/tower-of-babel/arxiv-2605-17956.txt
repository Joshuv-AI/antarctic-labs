Entropy additivity from exponential decay of correlations: a coarse-grained operator approach
Bob Osano

Abstract
Thermodynamic extensivity is commonly introduced as a postulate — the homogeneity of degree one of thermodynamic potentials. We provide a constructive derivation of this property from microscopic conditions on the pair potential, without assuming it. Working with the one- and two-particle reduced densities of the N N -body canonical Gibbs state, we introduce a combined coarse-graining operator 𝒞 \mathcal{C} on single-particle phase space ℳ = Λ × ℝ 3 \mathcal{M}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{3} , producing dimensionless mesoscopic probabilities { V i × Π α } \{V_{i}\times\Pi_{\alpha}\} over spatial–momentum cells. Under three conditions on the pair potential — stability, temperedness, and exponential cluster decomposition with correlation length ξ \xi . We show using the Ursell cluster expansion that the coarse-grained entropy satisfies S CG = ∑ i S i + O ( | Λ | ℓ d e − ℓ / ξ ) , S_{\mathrm{CG}}=\sum_{i}S_{i}+O\!\left(\frac{\left|\Lambda\right|}{\ell^{d}}\,e^{-\ell/\xi}\right), where ℓ ≫ ξ \ell\gg\xi is the cell diameter. The correction is exponentially suppressed per cell, making entropy additive and recovering the thermodynamic limit of Ruelle and Fisher in explicit operator language. For systems with long-range interactions, where temperedness fails, the correction does not vanish, and non-additivity is quantified through inter-cell mutual information. We further show that spatial averaging does not commute with nonlinear thermodynamic functionals such as the entropy density — a thermodynamic analogue of the cosmological averaging problem — and we derive the generalised Euler relation with explicit surface corrections.

Introduction

The distinction between intensive and extensive thermodynamic variables occupies a central position in classical thermodynamics, yet it is not primitive. We argue that the distinction emerges from the interplay of three structural ingredients: the existence of a thermodynamic limit, the short-range character of microscopic interactions, and the decay of correlations. Standard treatments
[

constructive

\mathcal{C}
acting on the single-particle phase space
\mathcal{M}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
of an
N
-body canonical ensemble, building on the one-particle reduced density
f^{(1)}
and the Ursell cluster expansion for the reduced
s
-particle densities. Within this framework, we demonstrate that extensivity is equivalent to the statistical factorisation of

across spatial cells, and that the coarse-grained entropy is additive up to a correction that is exponentially small in
\ell/\xi
(the cell diameter in units of the correlation length). It follows that when the pair potential violates temperedness, and correlations do not decay exponentially, this factorisation fails, and the entropy is genuinely non-additive, with the departure quantified by inter-cell mutual information.

2.1
Two Levels of the Extensivity Question

We distinguish two logically independent levels:

(i)

Phenomenological level.

A state function

is

X(\lambda S,\,\lambda V,\,\lambda N)=\lambda\,X(S,V,N)\quad\text{for all }\lambda>0.

(1)

Equivalently,

is homogeneous of degree one in
(S,V,N)
. At this level,
extensivity is defined by postulate
[
1
,
2
]
. We note that
S,V
, and
N
retain their conventional interpretations as entropy, volume, and particle number, respectively.

(ii)

Structural level.

The present paper derives (
1
) from microscopic conditions on the pair

–
2.5

appealing to (
1

2.2
The Classical N-Body System

To investigate the subject of the previous, let us consider
N
identical classical particles of mass
m
in a bounded domain

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
) with pairwise interaction potential

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We can express the total Hamiltonian for this system as follows

H_{N}(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left|p_{k}\right|^{2}}{2m}+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\phi(q_{k}-q_{l}),\qquad\gamma=(q_{1},\dots,q_{N},p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\in\Gamma_{N},

(2)

The phase-space coordinates appearing in
2
are defined as follows:

•

q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

are the position and momentum coordinates of the
i
-th
particle, respectively, for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
Together, they define the full
6N
-dimensional phase-space
point

\gamma\;=\;(q_{1},\ldots,q_{N},\,p_{1},\ldots,p_{N})\;\in\;\Gamma_{N}\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}^{6N}.

•

p_{k}
is the momentum coordinate of the
k
-th particle,

k=1,\ldots,N
, used in the indicator decomposition

\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}\times\Omega_{\alpha}}(\gamma_{k})

when determining the cell
V_{i}\times\Omega_{\alpha}
to
which particle
k
is assigned.

•

\Gamma_{N}=\Lambda^{N}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
is the
2dN
-dimensional phase space.

We can then specify the Gibbs measure at inverse temperature
\beta=1/(k_{\mathrm{B}}T)
as follows:

\varrho_{N}(\gamma)=Z_{N}^{-1}\,e^{-\beta H_{N}(\gamma)},\qquad Z_{N}=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}e^{-\beta H_{N}(\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

(3)

2.3
Reduced Densities

Definition 2.1

(Reduced
s
-particle density)
.

For
1\leq s\leq N
, the
reduced
s
-particle density
is

f^{(s)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s}):=\frac{N!}{(N-s)!}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\varrho_{N}(\gamma)\,\prod_{k=s+1}^{N}\,\mathrm{d}z_{k},

(4)

where
z_{k}=(q_{k},p_{k})\in\mathcal{M}:=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the single-particle phase-space
coordinate. In particular,
f^{(s)}\geq 0
and

\int_{\mathcal{M}^{s}}f^{(s)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})\,\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\,\mathrm{d}z_{s}=\frac{N!}{(N-s)!}.

(5)

For
s=1
:
\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=N
.
For
s=2
:
\int_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}f^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}=N(N-1)
.

The construction of reduced densities via marginalisation of the Gibbs measure follows the standard framework of
[
37
,
3
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The domain of each reduced density is the
single-particle phase space

\mathcal{M}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
not
the
N
-body phase space
\Gamma_{N}
. This is the
fundamental distinction from the formulations in phase-space coarse-graining
approaches
[
20
]
: the reduced densities live on a fixed low-dimensional

, making them the natural objects on which to define spatial

2.4
Ursell Cluster Decomposition

To handle the interconnected cells, we need an Ursell function. In particular, we need the Ursell decomposition to factorise the
s
-particle reduced density into connected
(irreducible) parts
[
41
,
28
,
27
]
. For example, for
s=2
, we characterise the factorisation as follows

f^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})=f^{(1)}(z_{1})\,f^{(1)}(z_{2})+u^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2}),

(6)

where the
pair Ursell function

u^{(2)}
satisfies
\int_{\mathcal{M}}u^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}=0

for all
z_{1}
. Eq.
6
ensures that the pair Ursell function
u^{(2)}
carries only the genuinely two-body connected correlation, with no contribution from the single-particle marginals already accounted for in
f^{(1)}(z_{1})f^{(1)}(z_{2})
. Without this condition, the factorised part and the connected correction would overlap, double-counting the one-body contribution to the two-body density. The same orthogonality condition generalises to all orders
s\geq 2
.
For general
s
, the
s
-body Ursell function
u^{(s)}
is defined recursively
through the cumulant expansion of
f^{(s)}
(see, e.g.,
[
27
,
28
]
); it encodes purely
s
-body connected
correlations and satisfies

\int_{\mathcal{M}}u^{(s)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})\,\mathrm{d}z_{s}=0\quad\text{for all }z_{1},\dots,z_{s-1}.

(7)

We can now stipulate conditions for the interaction potential. We will define these at this stage and leave the related theorems for later.

2.5
Microscopic Conditions on the Pair Potential

Definition 2.3

(Stability)
.

The potential
\phi
is
stable
if there exists
B\geq 0
such that

\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\phi(q_{k}-q_{l})\geq-BN\qquad\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall\,(q_{1},\dots,q_{N})\in\Lambda^{N}.

(8)

Definition 2.4

(Temperedness)
.

The potential
\phi
is
tempered
if there exist
R_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such
that

\left|\phi(r)\right|\leq\left|r\right|^{-(d+\varepsilon)}\qquad\text{for all }\left|r\right|>R_{0}.

(9)

Definition 2.5

(Exponential cluster decomposition)
.

The Gibbs state (
3
) satisfies
exponential cluster decomposition
with
correlation length
\xi>0
if the integrated pair Ursell function obeys

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\hat{u^{(2)}}(x,y)\right|\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\,e^{-\left|x\right|/\xi}\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10)

where
\hat{u^{(2)}}(x,y):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left|u^{(2)}((x,p),(y,q))\right|\,\mathrm{d}p\,\,\mathrm{d}q

is the momentum-integrated modulus of
u^{(2)}
, and similarly for all
s
-body Ursell
functions:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(s-1)}}\left|\hat{u^{(s)}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})\right|\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\cdots\,\mathrm{d}x_{s}\leq C^{s}\,e^{-\left|x_{1}\right|/\xi}\qquad\forall\,s\geq 2.

(11)

Remark 2.6
.

Stability and temperedness are sufficient for the existence of the thermodynamic limit
[
3
,
4
]
; exponential cluster decomposition
(
10
)–(
11
) holds in the high-temperature or low-density regime via the Mayer cluster expansion
[
3
,
7
,
9
]
, and more generally whenever the system is in a unique Gibbs phase
[
10
,
32
,
33
]
: the Dobrushin–Shlosman criterion
[
32
]
provides an explicit sufficient condition for uniqueness that directly implies the bound (
10
) with a computable
\xi
. The hypotheses of the main theorem are therefore satisfied throughout the one-phase region of the phase diagram.

2.6
The Thermodynamic Limit

Theorem 2.7

(Ruelle–Fisher thermodynamic limit
[
3
,
4
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be stable and tempered. For any van Hove sequence of domains
\Lambda_{n}\nearrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
N_{n}/\left|\Lambda_{n}\right|\to\rho_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, the specific free energy

f(\beta,\rho_{0}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{\beta N_{n}}\ln Z_{N_{n}}(\Lambda_{n},\beta)

(12)

exists, is finite, is independent of boundary conditions, and is a concave function of

(\rho_{0},-\beta)
. Consequently,

F(N,V,T)=N\,f(T,N/V)+O(\left|\partial\Lambda\right|)
,
so
F
is homogeneous of degree one in
(N,V)
in the thermodynamic limit, and
the entropy
S=-\partial F/\partial T
satisfies

S=N\,s(T,\rho_{0})+O(\left|\partial\Lambda\right|)

for a well-defined specific entropy
s(T,\rho_{0})
.

We state this theorem without proof; the proof may be found in the cited references.

3
The Combined Coarse-Graining Operator

3.1
Partition of Single-Particle Phase Space

We work entirely on the
single-particle phase space

\mathcal{M}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Partition
\Lambda
into disjoint spatial cells
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
of volume

\left|V_{i}\right|=\ell^{d}
, and partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into momentum cells
\{\Pi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}

of volume
\left|\Pi_{\alpha}\right|=:\nu_{\alpha}
. The product cells

C_{i,\alpha}:=V_{i}\times\Pi_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{M}

(13)

partition
\mathcal{M}
. Throughout, the spatial cell diameter
\ell
satisfies the scale
separation condition

\xi\ll\ell\ll L:=\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda),

(14)

which ensures that each spatial cell contains many particles (since
\ell^{d}\rho_{0}\gg 1
)
while remaining small on macroscopic scales (so spatially varying fields are resolved).
The condition
\ell\gg\xi
is used explicitly in the proof of

proposition

4.1
to justify the exponential decay of inter-cell correlations
before the next cell boundary is reached.

3.2
The Combined Coarse-Graining Operator

Definition 3.1

(Combined coarse-graining operator)
.

The
combined coarse-graining operator

\mathcal{C}
maps the one-particle reduced density

f^{(1)}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M})
to a piecewise-constant function:

(\mathcal{C}f^{(1)})(z):=\sum_{i,\alpha}\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}\,\mathbf{1}_{C_{i,\alpha}}(z),\qquad\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}:=\frac{1}{\left|C_{i,\alpha}\right|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}f^{(1)}(z^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}.

(15)

This operator is a projection (
\mathcal{C}^{2}=\mathcal{C}
) and a contraction on
L^{1}(\left\|\mathcal{C}f^{(1)}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\left\|f^{(1)}\right\|_{L^{1}}=N)
. Its constituent operators

(\mathcal{C}_{x}g)(x,p):=\sum_{i}\!\left(\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}g(x^{\prime},p)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right)\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}(x),\quad(\mathcal{C}_{p}g)(x,p):=\sum_{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}}\int_{\Pi_{\alpha}}g(x,p^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\Pi_{\alpha}}(p),

(16)

satisfy
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{x}\circ\mathcal{C}_{p}=\mathcal{C}_{p}\circ\mathcal{C}_{x}
by Fubini’s theorem (which tells us that for measurable functions, on a product of measure space, if the integral of the absolute value is finite, then the order of integration does not matter), since the
x
and
p
integrations act on independent variables
[
26
]
. However, as we show next, this commutativity
of the
linear
operator breaks down as soon as one applies it to
nonlinear

thermodynamic functionals.

3.3
Non-Commutativity of Nonlinear Thermodynamic Functionals

For linear observables
A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M})
, Fubini’s theorem gives

\mathcal{C}_{x}(\mathcal{C}_{p}A)=\mathcal{C}_{p}(\mathcal{C}_{x}A)
. The situation is fundamentally different for nonlinear
thermodynamic functionals such as the entropy density
\eta(\rho):=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\rho\ln\rho
or
the free-energy density
\psi(\rho)
. Define the spatial average of a functional
\Psi

of
f^{(1)}
over cell
V_{i}
as

\langle\Psi[f^{(1)}]\rangle_{i}:=\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}\Psi[f^{(1)}(x,\cdot)]\,\mathrm{d}x.

(17)

The ordering problem is then whether

\Psi[\langle f^{(1)}\rangle_{i}]=\langle\Psi[f^{(1)}]\rangle_{i}
,
i.e., whether spatial averaging commutes with the nonlinear operation
\Psi
.

Proposition 3.2

(Non-commutativity of spatial averaging with nonlinear functionals)
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly convex. Define

\bar{\rho}_{i}(p)=\left|V_{i}\right|^{-1}\int_{V_{i}}f^{(1)}(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}x
(the spatially averaged density
at momentum
p
, i.e., the coarse-grained density in
V_{i}
). Then

\Psi\!\left[\bar{\rho}_{i}(p)\right]\leq\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}\Psi[f^{(1)}(x,p)]\,\mathrm{d}x,

(18)

with equality if and only if
x\mapsto f^{(1)}(x,p)
is constant on
V_{i}
.
In particular, let
\rho>0
, for the entropy functional
\Psi(\rho)=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\rho\ln\rho
(concave), the inequality reverses:

-k_{\mathrm{B}}\bar{\rho}_{i}(p)\ln\bar{\rho}_{i}(p)\geq\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}(-k_{\mathrm{B}}f^{(1)}(x,p)\ln f^{(1)}(x,p))\,\mathrm{d}x,

(19)

i.e., the entropy of the spatially averaged density exceeds the spatial average of the
local entropy density.

Proof.

Direct application of Jensen’s inequality: since
\Psi
is convex and the Lebesgue
measure on
V_{i}
is a probability measure when normalised by
\left|V_{i}\right|
,

\Psi\!\left(\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}f^{(1)}(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq\frac{1}{\left|V_{i}\right|}\int_{V_{i}}\Psi(f^{(1)}(x,p))\,\mathrm{d}x.

Equality holds iff
f^{(1)}(\cdot,p)
is constant on
V_{i}
. For
\Psi(\rho)=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\rho\ln\rho
, the function is concave, so the inequality reverses.∎

Remark 3.3

(Connection to the Buchert–Ostermann problem)
.

Inequality (
19
) is a special case of the Buchert–Ostermann commutation rule
[
14
,
15
]
: for any nonlinear scalar functional

\mathcal{Q}
of the metric or matter fields, the spatial average
\langle\mathcal{Q}\rangle
differs from
\mathcal{Q}(\langle\text{fields}\rangle)
by a
backreaction
term.
In the thermodynamic context, this backreaction is exactly the difference between the fine-grained and coarse-grained entropies.
proposition

3.2
therefore places the
Buchert problem in the broader setting of any coarse-grained statistical mechanics.

3.4
Mesoscopic Probabilities and Coarse-Grained Entropy

Definition 3.4

(Mesoscopic probability)
.

The
mesoscopic probability
associated with cell
C_{i,\alpha}
is the
dimensionless quantity

\pi_{i,\alpha}:=\frac{\left|C_{i,\alpha}\right|}{N}\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}=\frac{1}{N}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}f^{(1)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(20)

Lemma 3.5

(Normalisation)
.

The mesoscopic probabilities satisfy
\pi_{i,\alpha}\geq 0
and

\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}=1
.

Proof.

Non-negativity is immediate from
f^{(1)}\geq 0
. For the sum:

\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}=N^{-1}\sum_{i,\alpha}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}f^{(1)}\,\mathrm{d}z=N^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}\,\mathrm{d}z=N^{-1}\cdot N=1

.
∎

Definition 3.6

(Coarse-grained entropy)
.

The
coarse-grained Boltzmann entropy
is the Shannon entropy of the mesoscopic
probability distribution
[
43
,
44
]
:

S_{\mathrm{CG}}[f^{(1)}]:=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}\ln\pi_{i,\alpha}.

(21)

This is well-defined (dimensionless logarithm) and satisfies
S_{\mathrm{CG}}\geq 0
, with equality only if all mass is concentrated on a single cell.

Remark 3.7
.

By Jensen’s inequality applied to
-t\ln t
(concave), the coarse-grained entropy
(
21
) is always at least as large as the Gibbs fine-grained entropy

S_{\mathrm{fine}}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}\ln(f^{(1)}/N)\,\,\mathrm{d}z/N,

reflecting the loss of
information through coarse-graining
[
43
,
44
]
.

3.5
Two-Cell Joint Probability and Factorisation

Definition 3.8

(Two-cell joint probability)
.

The
joint mesoscopic probability
for a particle to occupy cell
C_{i,\alpha}

while a
second, distinct
particle occupies cell
C_{j,\beta}
is defined via
the two-particle reduced density:

\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}:=\frac{\left|C_{i,\alpha}\right|\,\left|C_{j,\beta}\right|}{N(N-1)}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}\int_{C_{j,\beta}}f^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2},\qquad i\neq j.

(22)

This is well-defined because
f^{(2)}
describes
pairs
of distinct particles; the
factor
N(N-1)
in the denominator ensures

\sum_{(i\neq j),\alpha,\beta}\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}=1.

Remark 3.9
.

Definition
3.8
uses
f^{(2)}
defined on
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}
(two distinct
single-particle phase-space points),
not
on
\Gamma_{N}
.

Extensivity, Factorisation, and the Euler Relation

4.1
Main Proposition

Proposition 4.1

(Extensivity as emergent factorisation)
.

Let
\phi
be stable and tempered (
definitions

2.3
and
2.4
) and let the
canonical Gibbs state (
3
) satisfy exponential cluster decomposition
(
definition

2.5
) with correlation length
\xi
. Let
\ell
satisfy the scale
separation condition (
14
).

(i)

Pair factorisation.

For non-adjacent cells (
i\neq j
, with
\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|\gg\xi
where
x_{i},x_{j}
are
cell centres), the joint probability (
22
) satisfies

\left|\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}-\pi_{i,\alpha}\,\pi_{j,\beta}\right|\leq\frac{C}{N}\,e^{-\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|/\xi}.

(23)

(ii)

Entropy additivity.

The coarse-grained entropy satisfies

S_{\mathrm{CG}}=\sum_{i}S_{i}-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}}I_{s}(i_{1},\dots,i_{s}),

(24)

where
S_{i}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}\ln\pi_{i,\alpha}
is the marginal entropy
of cell
V_{i}
, and
I_{s}
is the
s
-cell multi-information. Under exponential
cluster decomposition (
11
), the total correction obeys

\left|{S_{\mathrm{CG}}-\sum_{i}S_{i}}\right|\leq k_{\mathrm{B}}\,\frac{C_{1}\left|\Lambda\right|}{\ell^{d}}\,e^{-\ell/\xi},

(25)

where
C_{1}>0
depends on
d
,
\xi
, and the constants in
definition

2.5
but
not on
\left|\Lambda\right|
or
\ell
.

(iii)

Converse.

If
\phi
is long-range with
\left|\phi(r)\right|\sim r^{-s_{0}}
for

4.4
Breakdown of Extensivity for Long-Range Interactions

Corollary 4.6

(Non-additivity for non-tempered potentials)
.

If the pair potential violates temperedness, with
\left|\phi(r)\right|\sim r^{-s_{0}}
for

s_{0}\leq d
, then:

\liminf_{\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|\to\infty}I(i,j)>0,

(37)

the series in (

S_{\mathrm{CG}}\neq\sum_{i}S_{i}.

(38)

The departure from extensivity is quantified exactly by

\Delta S:=S_{\mathrm{CG}}-\sum_{i}S_{i}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}}I_{s},

which is negative (entropy is sub-additive relative to the sum of marginals) whenever
inter-cell correlations are positive.

Proof.

For
s_{0}\leq d
, the Ursell function
\hat{u}^{(2)}(x,y)
decays only as

\left|x-y\right|^{-s_{0}}

[
3
]
, a consequence of the failure of the cluster property when correlations are long-range
[
39
,
40
]
. Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|x-y\right|^{-s_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}y

diverges for
s_{0}\leq d
, the bound (
10
) fails for any
\xi<\infty
.
Consequently,
\left|\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}-\pi_{i,\alpha}\pi_{j,\beta}\right|\sim\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{-s_{0}}
does not tend to zero, and
I(i,j)\geq c\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{-s_{0}}

for some

. The series

\sum_{i<j}I(i,j)\geq c\,M\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{d-1}k^{-s_{0}\ell^{s_{0}}}

diverges for
s_{0}\leq d
, so the entropy correction does not vanish.
∎

4.6

The thermodynamic limit (

and

,

therefore earn the
label “extensive” as a

4.7
The Generalised Euler Relation

Theorem 4.8

(Generalised Euler relation)
.

Under stability and temperedness, for a finite domain
\Lambda

\left|\partial\Lambda\right|<\infty

U=TS-PV+\mu N+E_{\partial},

where

,

,

, and

Proof.

In the thermodynamic limit,
U
is homogeneous of degree one in
(S,V,N)
by

2.7
(convexity of

in

is established in
[

3
]

.

For a finite system,

2.7
gives

\lambda

5
Numerical Illustrations

The propositions and lemmas of the preceding sections establish the
theoretical framework analytically. We now provide a set of numerical
illustrations that make each result concrete, verify the predicted
scalings computationally, and allow direct comparison with standard
Gibbs statistics. The computations are performed for a one-dimensional
classical gas (
d=1
) in natural units
k_{\mathrm{B}}=m=1
, a setting in
which all quantities can be evaluated exactly or to high numerical
precision without approximation beyond the discretisation of phase space.

5.1
Physical Model

We consider
N
classical particles of unit mass confined to a one-dimensional domain
\Lambda=[-L,L]
of length
2L
, with pairwise
interaction potential
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. The single-particle phase space is
M=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}
, and the one-particle reduced density takes the factorised form

f^{(1)}(x,p)\;=\;\varrho(x)\,f_{\mathrm{MB}}(p;T),\qquad f_{\mathrm{MB}}(p;T)\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi T}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{p^{2}}{2T}\right),

(40)

where
f_{\mathrm{MB}}(p;T)
is the Maxwell–Boltzmann momentum distribution and

\varrho(x)
is the spatial number density. The factorisation
of (
40
) holds exactly for the ideal gas and to leading
order in the interaction strength for the weakly interacting models
considered below. Three distinct interaction regimes are studied,
corresponding to the three physically relevant cases identified in the
main theorems.

Model I: Ideal gas (
\phi\equiv 0
)

The pair potential vanishes identically. The
N
-body Gibbs
measure factorises exactly:

\varrho_{N}(\gamma)\;=\;\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\varrho_{0}\,f_{\mathrm{MB}}(p_{k};T)}{N},

with
\varrho(x)=\varrho_{0}=N/(2L)
uniform. The pair Ursell
function satisfies
u^{(2)}\equiv 0
exactly, the joint mesoscopic
probability factorises as
\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}=\pi_{i,\alpha}\,\pi_{j,\beta}

for all
i\neq j
, and the mutual information
I(i,j)=0
for all cell
pairs. This model serves as the exact reference against which the
mesoscopic framework is calibrated: all deviations from standard Gibbs
statistics are purely numerical artefacts of the finite cell partition,
not physical effects.

Model II: Short-range interacting gas

A weak repulsive interaction is introduced via a Yukawa-type pair
potential. The momentum-integrated modulus of the pair Ursell function
is modelled as

\hat{u}^{(2)}(x,y)\;:=\;A_{\mathrm{SR}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{|x-y|}{\xi}\right),

(41)

where
A_{\mathrm{SR}}>0
is the correlation amplitude and
\xi>0

is the correlation length. This satisfies Definition 2.5 with
exponential decay rate
\xi^{-1}
, placing the model firmly in the
regime covered by Proposition 4.1: temperedness holds, and the
correction to entropy additivity is exponentially suppressed as

O(|\Lambda|\,\ell^{-1}\,e^{-\ell/\xi})
for cells of diameter

\ell\gg\xi
.

Model III: Long-range interacting gas

The pair Ursell function decays algebraically:

\hat{u}^{(2)}(x,y)\;:=\;\frac{A_{\mathrm{LR}}}{(1+|x-y|)^{s_{0}}},\qquad s_{0}\;<\;d\;=\;1,

(42)

with
s_{0}=0.5
and
A_{\mathrm{LR}}>0
. Since
s_{0}<d
, the
temperedness condition (Definition 2.4) fails:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{u}^{(2)}(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}y
diverges. By
Corollary 4.6, correlations between cells decay only as

|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-s_{0}}
, the mutual information series

\sum_{i<j}I(i,j)
diverges, and the entropy is genuinely
non-additive. This model illustrates the breakdown of extensivity
identified in Proposition 4.1(iii).

5.2
Numerical Implementation

Single-particle phase space
M=[-L,L]\times[-P_{\max},P_{\max}]

is partitioned into a uniform grid of
M_{x}\times M_{p}
rectangular cells
of dimensions
\Delta x=2L/M_{x}
and
\Delta p=2P_{\max}/M_{p}
,
with

chosen sufficiently large that the momentum truncation
error is negligible (
P_{\max}=6\sqrt{T}
ensures less than
10^{-8}

relative error in the Maxwell–Boltzmann normalisation). The mesoscopic
probabilities (Definition 3.4) are evaluated as

\pi_{i,\alpha}\;\approx\;\frac{\Delta x\,\Delta p}{N}\,f^{(1)}(x_{i},p_{\alpha}),\qquad x_{i}=-L+(i-\tfrac{1}{2})\Delta x,\quad p_{\alpha}=-P_{\max}+(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})\Delta p,

(43)

where
x_{i}
and
p_{\alpha}
are cell-centre coordinates. The resulting
array is renormalised to correct for momentum truncation:

\pi_{i,\alpha}\leftarrow\pi_{i,\alpha}/\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}
, ensuring exact
normalisation
\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}=1
in all cases.

The coarse-grained entropy (Definition 3.6) is computed as

S_{\mathrm{CG}}\;=\;-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,\alpha\\
\pi_{i,\alpha}>0\end{subarray}}\pi_{i,\alpha}\ln\pi_{i,\alpha},

(44)

and the Gibbs fine-grained entropy (the reference standard) is evaluated
by two-dimensional numerical quadrature on a fine grid of
2000\times 2000
points:

S_{\mathrm{Gibbs}}\;=\;-k_{\mathrm{B}}\int_{M}f^{(1)}(z)\ln\!\left(\frac{f^{(1)}(z)}{N}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(45)

For the ideal gas,
S_{\mathrm{Gibbs}}
is also computed analytically
(the one-dimensional Sackur–Tetrode analogue) and used as an
independent check on the numerical accuracy of (
45
).

The mutual information between spatial cells
i
and

is evaluated
from the Ursell correction to the joint probability
(Lemma 4.2, Eq. (26)–(28)):

I(i,j)\;\approx\;\frac{1}{2}\left[\hat{u}^{(2)}(x_{i},x_{j})\,\frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{N^{2}}\right]^{2},

(46)

which is the leading-order approximation valid for small correlation
amplitude

.

The Jensen correction of Proposition 3.2 is computed cell by cell
on a refined sub-grid of

spatial cell:

\Delta_{J}\;=\;\sum_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\eta\!\left(\bar{\varrho}_{i}(p)\right)-\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{V_{i}}\eta\!\left(f^{(1)}(x,p)\right)\mathrm{d}x\right]\mathrm{d}p,\qquad\eta(\varrho)=k_{\mathrm{B}}\varrho\ln\varrho,

(47)

Discussion

Summary of the framework.

The operator we defined,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{x}\circ\mathcal{C}_{p}
, coarse-grains the one-particle reduced density on single-particle phase space
\mathcal{M}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By working on
\mathcal{M}
rather than on the
2dN
-dimensional
N
-body phase space
\Gamma_{N}
, the joint mesoscopic probabilities (Definition
3.8
) are well-defined via the reduced two-particle density
f^{(2)}
—a function on
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}
and the ill-definedness that arises when one tries to integrate a single
N
-body density over two disjoint phase-space cells simultaneously is avoided.

Separation of two distinct corrections.

Our propositions
proposition

4.1
and
theorem

4.8
distinguish between two physically independent sources of deviation from strict extensivity:

(a)

Bulk correlation correction
:
O(\left|\Lambda\right|e^{-\ell/\xi})
. This arises
from inter-cell statistical dependence in the
bulk
and is exponentially
suppressed for
\ell\gg\xi
.

(b)

Surface correction
:
O(\left|\partial\Lambda\right|)
. This arises from the
breaking of translation invariance at the boundary, and is present even for
non-interacting systems.

Standard textbook treatments conflate these two corrections under a single
O(\left|\partial\Lambda\right|)
term, which is incorrect in general.

Long-range interactions.

For gravitational systems (e.g. self-gravitating gas clusters
[
3
]
) or unscreened Coulomb plasmas,
\phi(r)\sim r^{-1}
in
d=3
and temperedness fails(for a survey of such systems see
[
42
,
27
]
).
corollary

4.6
shows that
I(i,j)
decays only as
\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{-1}
, whose sum over all pairs diverges, and
S_{\mathrm{CG}}\neq\sum_{i}S_{i}
. This is consistent with the known non-extensivity of self-gravitating systems studied in the Tsallis framework
[
11
,
12
]
.

Non-commutativity and the Buchert problem.

proposition

3.2
demonstrates that the spatial average of a nonlinear thermodynamic functional differs from the functional of the spatial average by a Jensen correction term.
In the context of general relativity, this hints at the origin of the Buchert backreaction
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
: spatial averaging of the Einstein equations produces extra terms (backreaction) because the equations are nonlinear in the metric.
proposition

3.2
is the flat-space, non-relativistic analogue of this statement, providing a thermodynamic counterpart to the cosmological averaging problem.

Scope and limitations.

The results are established for classical systems in equilibrium. Extensions to quantum
systems, non-equilibrium steady states, or relativistic kinematics would require
corresponding generalisations of the Ursell expansion, and are not addressed here. The
cluster decomposition condition (
definition

2.5
) restricts the results to the
single-phase region; at phase transitions the correlation length
\xi
diverges and the
bounds weaken.

7
Conclusion

We have provided a constructive, operator-based derivation of extensivity in classical
statistical mechanics. The main contributions are:

(i)

Consistent framework.
By working with reduced
s
-particle densities
on single-particle phase space
\mathcal{M}
rather than the full
N
-body phase space, all
joint mesoscopic probabilities are well-defined, dimensionless, and properly
normalised.

(ii)

proof of entropy additivity at higher orders.

proposition

4.1
proves that the coarse-grained entropy satisfies

S_{\mathrm{CG}}=\sum_{i}S_{i}+O(\left|\Lambda\right|e^{-\ell/\xi}/\ell^{d})
by bounding the
entire multi-information series using the Ursell cluster expansion, including all

s
-body terms.

(iii)

Non-commutativity of nonlinear functionals.

proposition

3.2
demonstrates via Jensen’s inequality that spatial averaging does not
commute with nonlinear thermodynamic functionals such as the entropy density. This is
the structural source of non-extensivity and connects to the Buchert–Ostermann
commutation problem in relativistic cosmology.

(iv)

Quantified non-extensivity.
For systems with long-range interactions,

corollary

4.6
identifies inter-cell mutual information as the precise measure
of departure from extensivity, providing a bridge between thermodynamics and
information theory.

(v)

Generalised Euler relation.

theorem

4.8
derives the standard Euler
relation
U=TS-PV+\mu N
as a corollary of the thermodynamic limit, and gives the
explicit surface correction
E_{\partial}=O(\left|\partial\Lambda\right|)
for finite systems.

These results are consistent with the classical work of Ruelle
[
3
]
, Fisher
[
4
]
, and Lebowitz–Penrose
[
7
]
, and with the
Gibbs-measure framework of Georgii
[
9
]
. The contribution of the present paper is an explicit operator formalism that makes the factorisation mechanism transparent,
quantifies all correction terms, and connects thermodynamic extensivity to the broader problem of spatial averaging in nonlinear theories. We reserve the question of perturbation theory, in particular higher order perturbation theory in the cosmological context
[
25
,
23
,
24
]
, for future work.

Appendix B
The Overall Narrative

These plots together tell a single coherent story.

1.

The coarse-grained framework is
consistent with standard Gibbs statistics
in the fine-graining limit, with a controlled

O(M^{-2})
convergence rate (Plot 3).

2.

The mesoscopic probabilities
\pi_{i,\alpha}
(Plot 2) are the natural objects for a reduced description, inheriting the structure of

f^{(1)}(x,p)
(Plot 1) while discarding sub-cell fluctuations.

3.

Entropy is additive—and thermodynamics is extensive—if and only if inter-cell mutual information decays fast enough (Plots 4–5).
Short-range interactions guarantee exponential decay; long-range interactions produce algebraic decay and permanent non-additivity.

4.

The failure of extensivity for long-range interactions is not merely a correction: it is a qualitatively different regime visible
as a rising
S/N
curve in Plot 6.

5.

Spatial averaging does not commute with nonlinear thermodynamic functionals (Plot 7). This is a real, quantifiable effect connected
to the cosmological backreaction problem.

6.

Surface corrections to the Euler relation vanish relative to
bulk quantities in the thermodynamic limit (Plot 8), and they are
physically distinct from bulk correlation corrections.

The overall conclusion is that
extensivity is not a postulate
: it is a derived consequence of microscopic stability,
temperedness, and the exponential decay of correlations. When any of these conditions fails, the entropy is non-additive, and the degree of
non-additivity is quantified by the inter-cell mutual information—a bridge between classical thermodynamics and information
theory made explicit by the coarse-graining framework of this paper.
