Itô integral for a two-sided Lévy process
Raluca M. Balan, Jaime Garza

Abstract
In this article, we construct an Itô integral with respect to a two-sided finite-variance Lévy process { L ⁡ ( x ) } x ∈ ℝ \{L(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}} , without a Gaussian component. Using Rosenthal inequality for discrete-time martingales, we give an estimate for the p p -th moment of this integral, for any even integer p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . Then, using Poisson-Malliavin calculus, we show that the Itô integral is an extension of the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral with respect to the compensated Poisson random measure associated to the Lévy process.

Primary 60G51 ; Secondary 60G60, 60H07

Keywords:
Lévy process, Lévy white noise, Poisson random measure, Itô integral, Poisson-Malliavin calculus

1
Introduction

Let
N
be Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}
of intensity
\mathfrak{m}(dx,dz)=dx\nu(dz)
,
defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, where
\nu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}_{0}
which satisfies:

m_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}z^{2}\nu(dz)<\infty.

The space
\mathbb{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\verb 2\2\{0\}
is endowed with the distance
d(x,y)=|x^{-1}-y^{-1}|
. Let
\widehat{N}(F)=N(F)-\mathfrak{m}(F)
be the compensated version of
N
.

The goal of this short note is to construct an Itô integral for a two-sided Lévy process
\{L(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
with the Lévy-Itô decomposition:

L(x)={\rm sgn}(x)\left(b|x|+\int_{A_{x}\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}z\widehat{N}(dy,dz)+\int_{A_{x}\times\{|z|>1\}}zN(dy,dz)\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

and to discuss two of its properties: (i) a moment estimate; (ii) its relation with the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral.
Here
A_{x}=[0,x]
if
x\geq 0
and
A_{x}=[x,0]
if
x<0
.
We assume that

b=-\int_{\{|z|>1\}}z\nu(dz),

so that
L(x)
is centered and has the representation:

L(x)={\rm sgn}(x)\int_{A_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}z\widehat{N}(dy,dz).

While there are many references dedicated to stochastic analysis for Lévy processes (e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
), this specific construction and properties cannot be found in the literature. One can define a stochastic integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
for any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0};\mathfrak{m})
, and this integral (called
I_{1}(f)
in Section
4
below) is an isometry. Then, for any deterministic
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)L(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}\varphi(x)z\widehat{N}(dx,dz).

The difficulty is that this integral is
not
a martingale, since
N
has no time component.

To address this problem, we consider a more general process:

L(A)=\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}z\widehat{N}(dx,dz)\quad\mbox{for all $A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})$},

(1)

where
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
is the class of bounded Borel sets of
\mathbb{R}
.

We say that
L=\{L(A);A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})\}
is a
(time-independent) Lévy white noise
. This process has the following properties:

(i)
L(\emptyset)=0
and
L(\{x\})=0
a.s. for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)
\mathbb{E}[L(A)]=0
and
\mathbb{E}|L(A)|^{2}=m_{2}|A|
,
where
|A|
is the Lebesgue measure of
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
;

(iii)
L(A_{1}),\ldots,L(A_{k})
are independent, for any disjoint sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
;

(iv) for any
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,
L(A)
has characteristic function:

\mathbb{E}(e^{i\theta L(A)})=\exp\left\{|A|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{i\theta z}-1-i\theta z\big)\nu(dz)\right\}\quad\mbox{for any}\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

Letting
L(x):=L([0,x])
if
x\geq 0
and
L(x):=-L([x,0])
if
x<0
, we see that:

L((x,y])=L(y)-L(x)\quad\mbox{for any}\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R},x<y.

Then
\{L(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
is a Lévy process, since it has stationary and independent increments, and
L(0)=0
a.s. Moreover,
\mathbb{E}[L(x)]=0
and
\mathbb{E}|L(x)|^{2}=m_{2}|x|
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the construction of the Itô integral with respect to
L
. In Section
3
, we prove the following moment estimate, which may be well-known but we could not locate it in the literature:

\mathbb{E}|L(x)|^{p}\leq C_{p}^{*}\left\{(m_{2}|x|)^{p/2}+m_{p}|x|\right\}\quad\mbox{where}\quad m_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz).

Then, we extend this inequality to random integrands.
In Section
4
, we prove that the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral is an extension of the Itô integral.

2
Itô integral

In this section, we give the construction of the integral with respect to the Lévy white noise
L
. This is similar to an Itô integral, except that it is defined on
\mathbb{R}
, instead of
[0,\infty)
.

We let
(\mathcal{F}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
be the filtration induced by
N
, given by:

\mathcal{F}_{x}=\sigma\big\{N\big([a,b]\times\Gamma\big);-\infty<a<b\leq x,\,\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})\big\}\vee\mathcal{N},

where
\mathcal{N}
is the class of
\mathbb{P}
-negligible sets.

A process
\Phi=\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
is called
simple
if it is of the form

\Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]}(x)

(3)

where
-\infty<b_{0}<b_{1}<\ldots<b_{n}<\infty
and
Y_{i}
is a bounded
\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}
-measurable for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the class of simple processes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\sigma(\mathcal{E})
be the predictable
\sigma
-field on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. A process
\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
is called
(spatially) predictable
if the map
(\omega,x)\mapsto\Phi(\omega,x)
is
\mathcal{P}
-measurable.

For a simple process
X
of form (
3
) and
K>0
, we define the stochastic integral with respect to
L
on
[-K,K]
by

I_{K}(X):=\int_{-K}^{K}X(x)L(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}L\big((b_{i-1},b_{i}]\cap[-K,K]\big).

This integral has the following properties:

(i)

\mathbb{E}[I_{K}(X)]=0
and
\mathbb{E}|I_{K}(X)|^{2}=m_{2}\big[X\big]_{K,2}^{2}
for any
X\in\mathcal{E}
, where

\big[X\big]_{K,2}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\int_{-K}^{K}|X(x)|^{2}dx.

(ii)

I_{K}(aX+bY)=aI_{K}(X)+bI_{K}(Y)
for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{E}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
;

(iii)

I_{K}(X)=I_{K^{\prime}}\big(X1_{[-K,K]}\big)
for any
X\in\mathcal{E}
and
K^{\prime}\geq K>0
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be the set of predictable processes
X
such that
[X]_{K,2}<\infty
for all
K>0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
X\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
, then there exists a sequence
(X_{m})_{m\geq 1}
of simple processes such that

\big[X_{m}-X\big]_{K,2}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, for any
K>0
.

Proof.

If
X
is predictable and bounded, we use a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma 2.4 in Chapter 3 of
[
7
]
,
replacing
[0,T]
by
[-K,K]
. In the general case, we proceed as in the proof of Proposition 2.6 in Chapter 3,
ibid.
We omit the details.
∎

If
X\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
and
(X_{m})_{m\geq 1}
is the sequence from Lemma
2.2
, then
\{I_{K}(X_{m})\}_{m\geq 1}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We denote its limit by
I_{K}(X)
. The limit does not depend on the sequence
(X_{m})_{m\geq 1}
. Properties
(i)
-
(iii)
above continue to hold for processes in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

To extend the integral to the entire space, we let
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
be the set of predictable processes
X
such that

\big[X\big]_{2}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|X(x)|^{2}dx<\infty.

If
X\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
then
\{I_{K}(X)\}_{K>0}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, since for any
K^{\prime}>K
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|I_{K^{\prime}}(X)-I_{K}(X)|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big|I_{K^{\prime}}(X)-I_{K^{\prime}}\big(X1_{[-K,K]}\big)\big|^{2}=m_{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{\{K<|x|\leq K^{\prime}\}}|X(x)|^{2}dx\to 0,

as
K,K^{\prime}\to\infty
. We denote
I(X):=\lim_{K\to\infty}I_{K}(X)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We will use the notation:

I(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}X(x)L(dx).

Then
\mathbb{E}[I(X)]=0
and

\mathbb{E}|I(X)|^{2}=m_{2}\big[X\big]_{2}^{2}
.

3
Moment estimate

In this section, we prove a moment estimate for the stochastic integral with respect to the Lévy white noise
L
. Since the noise does not have a time component, we cannot use Rosenthal’s inequality for continuous martingales, as in
[
2
,
3
]
. Instead, we develop a moment inequality for
L(\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then for the stochastic integral of a simple process (using Rosenthal’s inequality for discrete martingales), and finally for the stochastic integral of a general process (by approximation with simple processes).

For any
p\geq 1
, we
we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
the norm in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, and

m_{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz).

We recall that for any integer
m\geq 2
, the
m
-th moment of a random variable
X
is given by the
cumulant formula
:

\mathbb{E}[X^{m}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|},

where
\mathcal{P}_{m}
is the set of all partitions of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
, “
B\in\pi
” means that
B
is one of the sets (or “blocks”) of partition
\pi
,
|B|
is the size of
B
, and
\kappa_{n}
is the
n
-th cumulant
of
X
:

\kappa_{n}=(-i)^{n}H^{(n)}(0)\quad\mbox{where}\quad H(\theta)=\log\mathbb{E}[e^{i\theta X}],\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

If we let
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)
and
\mu_{n}^{\prime}=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^{n}]
for any integer
n\geq 1
, then

\kappa_{1}=\mu,\quad\kappa_{2}=\mu_{2}^{\prime},\quad\kappa_{3}=\mu_{3}^{\prime},\quad\kappa_{4}=\mu_{4}^{\prime}-3\mu_{3}^{\prime}.

Note that if
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
, then
\kappa_{1}=0
and hence,

\mathbb{E}[X^{m}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{*}}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|},

(4)

where
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{*}
is the set of all partitions
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
which contain blocks
B
with
|B|\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an even integer such that
m_{p}<\infty
. For any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathbb{E}|L(\varphi)|^{p}\leq C_{p}^{*}\left\{\left(m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p/2}+m_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx\right\},

(5)

where
C_{p}^{*}={\rm card}(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{*})
.
In particular, for any set
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathbb{E}|L(A)|^{p}\leq C_{p}^{*}\left\{\big(m_{2}|A|\big)^{p/2}+m_{p}|A|\right\}.

(6)

Proof.

By (
2
) and Taylor formula,

H(\theta):=\log\mathbb{E}[e^{i\theta L(A)}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(e^{i\theta\varphi(x)z}-1-i\theta\varphi(x)z\big)dx\nu(dz)=\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{\big(i\theta\big)^{n}}{n!}\widetilde{m}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{n}dx,

where
\widetilde{m}_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}z^{n}\nu(dz)
. Hence,
H^{(n)}(0)=i^{n}\widetilde{m}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{n}dx
, and the
n
-th cumulant of
L(\varphi)
is

\kappa_{n}=\widetilde{m}_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{n}dx\quad\mbox{for any $n\geq 2$}.

Since
p
is even, by the cumulant formula (
4
),

0\leq\mathbb{E}|L(\varphi)|^{p}=\mathbb{E}[L(\varphi)^{p}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{*}}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|},

(7)

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{*}
be arbitrary. Assume that
\pi
has blocks
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
with
|B_{i}|=b_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}=p
and
b_{i}\geq 2
. It follows that

0\leq\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\kappa_{b_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{m}_{b_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{b_{i}}dx\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{b_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{b_{i}}dx.

Next, we provide an estimate for
m_{b_{i}}
, based on
m_{2}
and
m_{p}
.
Any
r\in\{3,\ldots,p-1\}
can be written as
r=p\theta+2(1-\theta)
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. By Hölder’s inequality,

m_{r}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(|z|^{p}\big)^{\theta}\big(|z|^{2}\big)^{1-\theta}\nu(dz)\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz)\Big)^{\theta}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}|z|^{2}\nu(dz)\Big)^{1-\theta}=m_{p}^{\theta}m_{2}^{1-\theta}.

This inequality holds trivially for
r=2
(when
\theta=0
) and
r=p
(when
\theta=1
). Hence

m_{r}\leq m_{p}^{\theta}m_{2}^{1-\theta}\quad\mbox{for any $r\in\{2,\ldots,p\}$, with $\theta=\frac{r-2}{p-2}$.}

Similarly, for any
r\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
, letting
\theta=\frac{r-2}{p-2}
, we have:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{r}dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1-\theta}.

Using this estimate, we obtain that

\displaystyle\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(m_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{b_{i}-2}{p-2}}\left(m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p-b_{i}}{p-2}}

\displaystyle=\Big[\Big(m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]^{\frac{2k-2}{p-2}}\cdot\big(m_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx\big)^{\frac{p-2k}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k-2}{p-2}\Big(m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{p/2}+\frac{p-2k}{p-2}m_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{p/2}+m_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}dx,

(8)

using the inequality
x^{a}y^{1-a}\leq ax+(1-a)y
for any
a\in[0,1]
and
x,y>0
.

Relation (
6
) follows from (
7
) and (
8
).
∎

Next, we will extend this moment inequality to simple processes. For this, we use
Rosenthal’s inequality
for discrete-time martingales, which we recall below. Let
(S_{k})_{k=0,1,\ldots,n}
be a martingale with respect to a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k=0,1,\ldots,n}
, such that
S_{0}=0
. Let
X_{i}=S_{i}-S_{i-1}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. If
p\geq 2
is such that
\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p}<\infty
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, then

\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{k\leq n}|S_{k}|^{p}\big]\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}^{p}\left\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\Big)^{p/2}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p}\right\},

(9)

where
\mathcal{B}_{p}>0
is a constant depending on
p
; see
[
5
,
9
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an even integer such that
m_{p}<\infty
.
For any
X\in\mathcal{E}
,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}X(x)L(dx)\right|^{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|X(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p/2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|X(x)|^{p}dx\right\},

where
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}=2\mathcal{B}_{p}C_{p}^{*}(m_{2}^{p/2}\vee m_{p})
, and
\mathcal{B}_{p},C_{p}^{*}
are the constants from (
9
), respectively (
6
).

Proof.

Let
X
be a simple process of form (
3
). Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}X(x)L(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}L(A_{i})\quad\mbox{where}\quad A_{i}=(b_{i-1},b_{i}].

Let
S_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}L(A_{i})
for
k=1,\ldots,n
and
S_{0}=0
. Then
(S_{k})_{k=0,\ldots,n}
is a zero-mean martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{b_{k}})_{k=0,\ldots,n}
:
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}L(A_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}]=Y_{i}\mathbb{E}[L(A_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}]=Y_{i}\mathbb{E}[L(A_{i})]=0,

since
L(A_{i})
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}
. Similarly,
\mathbb{E}\big[Y_{i}^{2}L(A_{i})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}\big]=Y_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}|L(A_{i})|^{2}=Y_{i}^{2}m_{2}|A_{i}|
.
Then, using Rosenthal’s inequality (
9
), followed by estimate (
6
), we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|S_{n}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}^{p}\left\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[Y_{i}^{2}L(A_{i})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}\big]\Big)^{p/2}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big|Y_{i}L(A_{i})\big|^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{p}^{p}\left\{m_{2}^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y_{i}|^{2}|A_{i}|\Big)^{p/2}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}|^{p}\,\mathbb{E}|L(A_{i})|^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}^{p}\left\{m_{2}^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y_{i}|^{2}|A_{i}|\Big)^{p/2}\Big]+\mathcal{C}_{p}^{*}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}|^{p}\big[(m_{2}|A_{i}|)^{p/2}+m_{p}|A_{i}|\big]\right\}.

Using the fact that
\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y_{i}|^{p}|A_{i}|^{p/2}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y_{i}|^{2}|A_{i}|\big)^{p/2}\big]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|S_{n}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}\left\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y_{i}|^{2}|A_{i}|\Big)^{p/2}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}|^{p}|A_{i}|\right\}.

On the other hand, since the sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are disjoint,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|X(x)|^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{2}|A_{i}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}|X(x)|^{p}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{p}|A_{i}|.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Finally, we extend the moment inequality to the stochastic integral of a general process.
From Lemma
3.2
, we infer that for any even integer
p\geq 2
such that
m_{p}<\infty
,

\|I(X)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X\big]_{p}\quad\mbox{for any $X\in\mathcal{E}$},

where

\displaystyle\big[X\big]_{p}

\displaystyle:=\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{*}
be the set of predictable processes
X
such that
\big[X\big]_{p}<\infty
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
, since
\|X\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H)}\leq\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}
with
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{L}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is the set of predictable processes
X
such that
\big[X\big]_{K,p}<\infty
for all
K>0
, and

\big[X\big]_{K,p}:=\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([-K,K]))}+\|X\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times[-K,K])}.

The next result provides an extension of Lemma
3.2
to processes in
\mathcal{L}_{p}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an even integer such that
m_{p}<\infty
. If
X\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
, then

\|I_{K}(X)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X\big]_{K,p}\quad\mbox{for any}\ K>0.

(10)

Proof.

Similarly to Lemma
2.2
, it can be proved that there exists a sequence
(X_{m})_{m\geq 1}
of simple processes such that
\big[X_{m}-X\big]_{K,p}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, for any
K>0
.

Fix
K>0
. Since
\big[\cdot\big]_{K,2}\leq\big[\cdot\big]_{K,p}
, it follows that

\big[X_{m}-X\big]_{K,2}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Hence
I_{K}(X_{m})\to I_{K}(X)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
. On the other hand,
\{I_{K}(X_{m})\}_{m\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, since by Lemma
3.2
,

\|I_{K}(X_{m})-I_{K}(X_{n})\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X_{n}-X_{m}\big]_{K,p}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n,m\to\infty$}.

Therefore,
\{I_{K}(X_{m})\}_{m\geq 1}
converges in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Its limit must be
I_{K}(X)
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\|I_{K}(X_{m})\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X_{m}\big]_{K,p}\quad\mbox{for any}\ m\geq 1.

The conclusion follows letting
m\to\infty
.
∎

Finally, we provide an extension of this result to the stochastic integral on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an even integer such that
m_{p}<\infty
. For any
X\in\mathcal{L}_{p}^{*}
,

\|I(X)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X\big]_{p}.

(11)

Proof.

Note that
\{I_{K}(X)\}_{K>0}
is Cauchy in
L^{p}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\|I_{K^{\prime}}(X)-I_{K}(X)\|_{p}=\|I_{K^{\prime}}(X)-I_{K^{\prime}}(X1_{[-K,K]})\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\big[X-X1_{[-K,K]}\big]_{K^{\prime},p}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{C}_{p}\left\{\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\Big(\int_{\{K<|x|\leq K^{\prime}\}}|X(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{p/2}\right]\right\}^{1/p}+\left\{\mathbb{E}\int_{\{K<|x|\leq K^{\prime}\}}|X(x)|^{p}dx\right\}^{1/p}\right\}\to 0,

as
K,K^{\prime}\to\infty
and
K^{\prime}>K
. Therefore
\{I_{K}(X)\}_{K>0}
converges in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as
K\to\infty
. Its limit must be
I(X)
(since
X\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
). The conclusion follows letting
K\to\infty
in (
10
).
∎

As an application of Theorem
3.4
, we obtain the following result for a mixed Lebesgue-stochastic convolution, which contains
ds
and
L(dy)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an even integer such that
m_{p}<\infty
. Let
\Phi=\{\Phi(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
be a random field such that
(\omega,x,t)\mapsto\Phi(\omega,t,x)
is
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-measurable, and

\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}|\Phi(t,x)|^{p}<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $T>0$}.

Let
(t,x)\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{t}(x)
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\nu_{T}:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{G}_{t}^{p}(x)|dxdt<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $T>0$}.

(12)

Then, for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}(x-y)\Phi(s,y)dsL(dy)\right|^{p}\leq B_{t,p}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)+\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{p}(x-y)\big)\mathbb{E}|\Phi(s,y)|^{p}dyds,

where

B_{t,p}^{p}=2^{p-1}\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}\big(t^{p/2}\nu_{t}^{p/2-1}+t^{p-1}\big),

(13)

and
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is the constant from (
11
).

Proof.

Let
\Psi(y)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}(x-y)\Phi(s,y)ds

By Fubini theorem,
\Psi
is predictable. By Hölder’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(y)|^{p}dy\leq t^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{p}(x-y)|\mathbb{E}|\Phi(s,y)|^{p}dyds<\infty.

Hence,
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}_{p}^{*}
.
By Theorem
3.4
,

\mathbb{E}|I(\Psi)|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}\Big(\|\Psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})}^{p}\Big)=:2^{p-1}\mathcal{C}_{p}^{p}(T_{1}+T_{2}).

We estimate separately the two terms. First note that,

\|\Psi(y)\|_{p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{G}_{t-s}(x-y)|\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}ds

and hence, by Hölder’s inequality,

\|\Psi(y)\|_{p}^{2}\leq t\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}^{2}ds,\quad\|\Psi(y)\|_{p}^{p}\leq t^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{p}(x-y)|\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}^{p}ds.

By Minkowski’s inequality, and Hölder’s inequality with respect to the measure
\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)dsdy
on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}
(whose total measure is
\nu_{t}
),
we obtain that:

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi^{2}(y)dy\right\|_{p/2}^{p/2}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\Psi(y)^{2}\|_{p/2}dy\right)^{p/2}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\Psi(y)\|_{p}^{2}dy\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{p/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}^{2}dyds\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{p/2}\nu_{t}^{p/2-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}^{p}dyds.

For
T_{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\Psi(y)\|_{p}^{p}dy\leq t^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}_{t-s}^{p}(x-y)\|\Phi(s,y)\|_{p}^{p}dyds.

∎

4
Relation with Hitsuda-Skorohod integral

In this section, we show that the Itô integral coincides with the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral for predictable integrands.

We begin by recalling some basic material about Malliavin calculus with respect to the compensated Poisson random measure
\widehat{N}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
, where

({\bf Z},\mathcal{Z})=\big(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0},\ \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{0})\big).

\bullet

Chaos Expansion.

Any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
which is
\mathcal{F}^{N}
-measurable has the
Poisson-chaos expansion
:

F=\mathbb{E}(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}),\quad\mbox{for some $f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}$}.

(14)

This series is orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Here
I_{n}
is the multiple integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}
is the set of symmetric functions in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.
For any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
,

\mathbb{E}[I_{n}(f)]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}|I_{n}(f)|^{2}=n!\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

where
\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of
f
. Moreover,
I_{n}(f)=I_{n}(\widetilde{f})
for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.

\bullet

Malliavin Derivative.

For any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with chaos expansion (
14
), we define the
Malliavin derivative
of
F
by:

D_{\xi}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,\xi)\big),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\xi\in{\bf Z},

provided that

\mathbb{E}\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}nn!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

In this case, we write
F\in{\rm dom}(D)
.

\bullet

Hitsuda-Skorohod integral.

Let
\delta:{\rm dom}(\delta)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the adjoint of
D
, where
{\rm dom}(\delta)
is the set of
V\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
for which there exists a constant
C=C_{V}>0
depending on
V
, such that

\big|\mathbb{E}\langle DF,V\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq C\|F\|_{2}\quad\mbox{for any $F\in{\rm dom}(D)$}.

We say that
\delta(V)
is

the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral
of
V
with respect to
\widehat{N}
, and we write

\delta(V)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}V(x,z)\widehat{N}(\delta x,\delta z).

By duality, for any
V\in{\rm dom}(\delta)
,

\mathbb{E}\langle DF,V\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbb{E}[F\delta(V)]\quad\mbox{for any $F\in{\rm dom}(D)$}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
be arbitrary. Then, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}[I_{k}(h)|\mathcal{F}_{y}]=I_{k}(h^{y}),

where
h^{y}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{k},z_{k})=h(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{k},z_{k})1_{(-\infty,y]^{k}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{k}
be the set of functions of the form

h(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k})=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}1_{F_{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times F_{i_{k}}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}),\quad\mbox{with}\quad\xi_{i}=(x_{i},z_{i})\in{\bf Z},

(15)

where
\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}=0
if
i_{j}=i_{\ell}
for some
j\not=\ell
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\in\mathcal{Z}
are disjoint of the form
F_{i}=A_{i}\times B_{i}
with
A_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and
B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})
. Thus, the sum in (
15
) is based only on
distinct
indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
.
Since
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
,
it is enough to prove the result for
h\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
. If
h
is of form (
15
), then

I_{k}(h)=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{N}(F_{i_{j}}),

and

\mathbb{E}[I_{k}(h)|\mathcal{F}_{y}]=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{N}(F_{i_{j}})\big|\mathcal{F}_{y}\Big].

For any
i=1,\ldots,m
, we write

\widehat{N}(F_{i})=\widehat{N}(A_{i}\times B_{i})=\widehat{N}\big((A_{i}\cap(-\infty,y])\times B_{i}\big)+\widehat{N}\big((A_{i}\cap(y,\infty))\times B_{i}\big)=:X_{i}+Y_{i}.

Note that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
are
\mathcal{F}_{y}
-measurable, while
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
are independent of
\mathcal{F}_{y}
. Moreover,
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
are independent, since the sets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
are disjoint.

Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be distinct. Then

\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{N}(F_{i_{j}})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(X_{i_{j}}+Y_{i_{j}}\big)=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\prod_{j\in J}X_{i_{j}}\prod_{j\in J^{c}}Y_{i_{j}},

and hence,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{N}(F_{i_{j}})\big|\mathcal{F}_{y}\Big]=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\prod_{j\in J}X_{i_{j}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j\in J^{c}}Y_{i_{j}}\Big].

If
J^{c}
is non-empty,
\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j\in J^{c}}Y_{i_{j}}\Big]=\prod_{j\in J^{c}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{i_{j}}]=0
since
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
are distinct and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
are independent. Therefore, the only term which contributes to the sum above if the one corresponding to
J=\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}[I_{k}(h)|\mathcal{F}_{y}]=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{N}\big((A_{i_{j}}\cap(-\infty,y])\times B_{i_{j}}\big).

On the other hand,
h^{y}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{k},z_{k})=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}1_{A_{i_{j}}\cap(-\infty,y]}(x_{j})1_{B_{i_{j}}}(z_{j})
,
and hence,

I_{k}(h^{y})=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\widehat{N}\big((A_{i_{j}}\cap(-\infty,y])\times B_{i_{j}}\big).

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
F\in{\rm dom}(D)
is such that
F
is
\mathcal{F}_{y}
-measurable for some
y\in\mathbb{R}
, then
D_{x,z}F=0
for any
x>y
and
z\in\mathbb{R}_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume that
F
has the chaos expansion (
14
). By Lemma
4.1
,

F=\mathbb{E}[F|\mathcal{F}_{y}]=\mathbb{E}(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[I_{n}(f_{n})|\mathcal{F}_{y}]=\mathbb{E}(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}^{y}).

Then, for any
x>y
and
z\in\mathbb{R}_{0}
,
D_{x,z}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}\big(f_{n}^{y}(\cdot,x,z)\big)=0
, since

f_{n}^{y}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},z_{n-1},x,z)=f_{n}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},z_{n-1},x,z)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}1_{(-\infty,y]}(x_{j})1_{(-\infty,y]}(x)=0.

∎

The following result gives the relation between the Hitsuda-Skorohod integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
and the Itô integral with respect to
L
, which originates from definition (
1
) of
L
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
be arbitrary. Define
V_{\Phi}(x,z)=\Phi(x)z
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}_{0}
. Then
V_{\Phi}\in{\rm dom}(\delta)
and
\delta(V_{\Phi})=I(\Phi)
.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of Theorem 10.2.7 of
[
10
]
.

Step 1.
Assume that
\Phi
is a simple process of form (
3
) where
Y_{i}\in{\rm dom}(D)
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
u_{i}(x,z)=z1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]}(x)
. Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. By Proposition 10.2.6 of
[
10
]
,
Y_{i}u_{i}\in{\rm dom}(\delta)
and

\delta(Y_{i}u_{i})=y_{i}\delta(u_{i})-\langle DY_{i},u_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\delta(u_{i}DY_{i}).

Since
Y_{i}
is
\mathcal{F}_{b_{i-1}}
-measurable,
D_{x,z}Y_{i}=0
for all
x>b_{i-1}
(by Lemma
4.2
). Hence,

D_{x,z}Y_{i}z1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]}(x)=0\quad\mbox{for all $(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}$.}

It follows that
\langle DY_{i},u_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0
and
u_{i}DY_{i}=0
. Therefore,

\delta(Y_{i}u_{i})=Y_{i}\delta(u_{i})=Y_{i}\int_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}z\widehat{N}(dx,dz)=Y_{i}L\big((b_{i-1},b_{i}]\big)=I(Y_{i}1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]}).

Note that
V_{\Phi}(x,z)=z\Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}u_{i}(x,z)
. Hence,
V_{\Phi}\in{\rm dom}(\delta)
and

\delta(V_{\Phi})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta(Y_{i}u_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}L\big((b_{i-1},b_{i}]\big)=I(\Phi).

Step 2.
Assume that
\Phi\in\mathcal{E}
is of form (
3
). Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
u_{i}
be as in Step 1. Since
{\rm dom}(D)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence
\{Y_{i}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset{\rm dom}(D)
such that
\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{k}-Y_{i}|^{2}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.
By
Step 1
, for any
k\geq 1
,
Y_{i}^{k}u_{i}\in{\rm dom}(\delta)
and

\delta(Y_{i}^{k}u_{i})=I(Y_{i}^{k}1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]}).

(16)

Note that
Y_{i}^{k}u_{i}\to Y_{i}u_{i}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
as
k\to\infty
, since

\mathbb{E}\|Y_{i}^{k}u_{i}-Y_{i}u_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}|Y_{i}^{k}-Y_{i}|^{2}u_{i}^{2}(x,z)dx\nu(dz)=m_{2}(b_{i}-b_{i-1})\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{k}-Y_{i}|^{2}.

Since
\delta
is a closed operator,
\delta(Y_{i}^{k}u_{i})\to\delta(Y_{i}u_{i})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
k\to\infty
.
Letting
k\to\infty
in (
16
), we obtain that
\delta(Y_{i}u_{i})=I(Y_{i}1_{(b_{i-1},b_{i}]})
. The conclusion follows.

Step 3.
Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
be arbitrary. By the construction of the integral, there exists a sequence
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{E}
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi_{n}(x)-\Phi(x)|^{2}dx\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

By
Step 2
,
\delta(V_{\Phi_{n}})=I(\Phi_{n})
for any
n\geq 1
. The conclusion follows letting
n\to\infty
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, using the fact that
\delta
is a closed operator and
V_{\Phi_{n}}\to V_{\Phi}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
.

∎
