Strategic Non-Shareability of Quantum Correlations
Fumin Wang

Abstract
Correlations distributed by a mediator are usually valued for the coordination they enable between authorized agents, but in adversarial settings a more decisive property is whether the same coordination can be inherited by an outside colluder without disturbing the authorized marginal. Classical shared randomness is freely copyable, so a hidden seed coordinating two agents can be duplicated for a third; entanglement is constrained by monogamy, which can forbid such lossless extensions in strongly nonlocal regimes. We turn this asymmetry into an operational resource for private-information games. For a fixed authorized behavior P 12 P_{12} , we define its collusive shadow as the set of relabelled behaviors a colluder can reproduce in any admissible tripartite extension preserving P 12 P_{12} , and we identify strategic non-shareability with the distance from this shadow. We prove that, on finite alphabets, the game-optimized anti-collusion capacity equals the total-variation distance to the shadow; a fixed game provides a task-specific separating witness, while optimization over relabelled games recovers the full distance. Classical hidden-variable mediators have zero capacity in this slice. We complement these results with two operational tools: a Hoeffding-based finite-data certification protocol that turns observed Bell scores into confidence-bounded anti-collusion certificates, and a level-2 NPA semidefinite relaxation that extends certified upper envelopes to tilted Bell inequalities. These results recast entanglement monogamy as a measurable shareability deficit for quantum-mediated strategic networks.

, underscoring the transition from theory to practical quantum networks

Beyond cryptography, quantum correlations also serve as mediators in strategic games with private information, where they can enlarge the set of achievable correlated equilibria beyond classical limits

;
Cleve2004
. Classical mediators rely on shared randomness, which is freely copyable and therefore inherently vulnerable to collusion: every authorized correlation can be duplicated for a colluder without loss. Quantum mediators, by contrast, are constrained by the no-cloning theorem and entanglement monogamy

;
Werner1989
. These physical restrictions suggest that quantum coordination carries a built-in defense against unauthorized extension. Yet, a rigorous operational framework that quantifies this defense—and certifies it from observed data—has been missing.

Certification of quantum correlations relies on semidefinite programming relaxations

;

;

;

;

;
ArnonFriedman2018
and randomness extraction

We close this gap by shifting attention from correlation

to correlation
extendibility
. In adversarial settings, the decisive question is not how well a mediator coordinates authorized agents, but whether the same coordination can be inherited by a colluder without disturbing the authorized marginal. We call the obstruction to such inheritance
strategic non-shareability
: the inability of a useful authorized correlation to be reproduced in any admissible collusive extension. The central geometric object is the
collusive shadow
—the set of all relabelled behaviors that a colluder can cast with an authorized party while preserving the observed marginal (Fig.
1
). Distance from this shadow measures the operational shareability deficit and directly bounds the optimal anti-collusion advantage.

Our contributions are fivefold. First, we formalize the collusive shadow for classical, no-signalling, and quantum extension classes, including fixed-dimensional and closed quantum sets. Second, we prove that—under standard finite-alphabet compactness assumptions—the game-optimized anti-collusion capacity equals the total-variation distance from the collusive shadow (Theorem
2
). Third, we solve the Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) score-certified frontier exactly,

and identify the Bell local bound

as the sharp onset of certifiable strategic non-shareability (Theorem
6

1/(2\sqrt{2})
at Tsirelson’s bound is attained by maximally entangled states via exact monogamy (Theorem

2.5

3

These results recast entanglement monogamy not merely as a static structural property, but as a dynamic, game-weighted resource for strategic networks. They bridge quantum foundations—Bell nonlocality, monogamy, and device-independent certification—with the operational needs of quantum-mediated games and quantum-internet architectures.

Results

2.1
Strategic non-shareability of mediated correlations

We consider three agents. Players 1 and 2 form the authorized pair, and player 3 is a potential colluder. Each player
i
receives a private type
t_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
and produces an action
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
. After receiving types, players cannot communicate. A mediator supplies local recommendations, which may be classical or quantum.

A classical mediator is a local hidden-variable device,

P_{12}(x_{1},x_{2}|t_{1},t_{2})=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)p_{1}(x_{1}|t_{1},\lambda)p_{2}(x_{2}|t_{2},\lambda),

(1)

and a quantum mediator uses a bipartite state and local POVMs,

P_{12}(x_{1},x_{2}|t_{1},t_{2})=\Tr\!\left[\rho_{12}\left(E^{(1)}_{t_{1},x_{1}}\otimes E^{(2)}_{t_{2},x_{2}}\right)\right].

(2)

The mediator is required to be no-signalling: for every subset
S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
,

P(x_{S}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=P(x_{S}|t_{S}).

(3)

This condition rules out explanations based on hidden communication.

A collusive extension is a tripartite distribution satisfying

\sum_{x_{3}}P_{123}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=P_{12}(x_{1},x_{2}|t_{1},t_{2}).

(4)

The physical question is whether such an extension can preserve the authorized marginal while also giving the colluder a high score with player 1.

For an authorized behavior
P_{12}
, we define the
collusive extension set
under resource class

\mathsf{R}\in\{\mathsf{C},\mathsf{NS},\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin},\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d},\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl},\mathsf{Q}_{\rm co}\}

as

{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\left\{P_{123}\in\mathsf{R}:\sum_{x_{3}}P_{123}(\cdot,\cdot,x_{3}|\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)=P_{12}\right\},

(5)

where
\mathsf{C}
denotes classical hidden-variable extensions,
\mathsf{NS}
general no-signalling extensions,
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
finite-dimensional quantum extensions,
\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d}
fixed-dimensional quantum extensions with local Hilbert-space dimension bounded by
d
,
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}:=\overline{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}
the closure of the finite-dimensional quantum set, and
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm co}
the commuting-operator model associated with the NPA limit. Unless closure issues are relevant, we use “quantum extension” informally; the precise closed classes are specified in theorems that require compactness.

Definition 1

(Collusive shadow)
.

Let
\Pi_{13\to 12}
denote the relabelling map that identifies player 3’s type and action alphabets with player 2’s. The
collusive shadow
of an authorized behavior
P_{12}
under resource class
\mathsf{R}
is

\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\left\{\Pi_{13\to 12}P_{13}:P_{123}\in{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})\right\},

(6)

where
P_{13}(x_{1},x_{3}|t_{1},t_{3})=\sum_{x_{2}}P_{123}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
and no-signalling ensures this marginal does not depend on
t_{2}
.

The collusive shadow is the set of all authorized-looking behaviors that a colluder can cast with player 1 without disturbing the observed authorized marginal
P_{12}
. A mediator is strategically non-shareable when its own authorized behavior lies outside, or far from, this shadow.

.

2.2
Anti-collusion capacity equals distance to the collusive shadow

The operational cost of collusion is measured by three quantities. The
collusive vulnerability

V_{13}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})
is the largest collusive score achievable by an adversary constrained only by preserving the authorized marginal
P_{12}
:

V_{13}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=\sup_{P_{123}\in{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}C_{13}(P_{123})=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\langle h,Q\rangle,

where
h
is the collusive scoring kernel. The
anti-collusion power
is the positive part of the authorized–collusive gap,

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\bigl[A_{12}(P_{12})-V_{13}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})\bigr]_{+},

which is positive exactly when the authorized score exceeds every admissible collusive score. The
strategic non-shareability distance

\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}d_{\rm TV}(P_{12},Q)

is the minimum total-variation distance between the authorized behavior and any behavior the colluder can reproduce with player 1; it is a game-independent measure of non-shareability. Finally, optimizing over all normalized relabelled scoring rules
0\leq h\leq 1
gives the
anti-collusion capacity

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\bigl[\langle h,P_{12}\rangle-\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\langle h,Q\rangle\bigr]_{+}.

For a fixed authorized behavior, we write

U^{\rm wc}_{\lambda,\mathsf{R}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=A_{12}(P_{12})-\lambda V^{\mathsf{R}}_{13}(P_{12};\mathcal{G}),

with
U^{\rm wc}_{1}
denoting the case
\lambda=1
when the resource class is clear.

These quantities are linked by the following exact equality.

Theorem 2

(Anti-collusion capacity equals distance from the collusive shadow)
.

Let
P_{12}
be an authorized behavior with finite input and output alphabets. Let

\mathsf{R}\in\{\mathsf{C},\mathsf{NS},\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d},\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}\}

be an extension class for which
{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})
is non-empty, compact and convex. Then

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}d_{\rm TV}(P_{12},Q).

(7)

Thus a fixed game provides a separating hyperplane that witnesses non-shareability, whereas the game-optimized capacity equals the full operational distance from the collusive shadow. The proof uses Sion’s minimax theorem and the variational characterization of total-variation distance (see Methods).

For the unclosed finite-dimensional quantum class
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
, the equality should be interpreted after passing to its closure
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}=\overline{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}
, or by restricting to a fixed local dimension
\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d}
. This distinction is not needed for the CHSH score-certified result, which follows directly from the Toner–Verstraete inequality.

2.3
Classical copied seeds have zero anti-collusion power

Classical hidden-variable mediators are freely shareable because the hidden seed can be copied. Therefore, whenever the collusive test is a relabelled copy of the authorized test, the colluder can inherit the authorized coordination.

Theorem 3

(Classical free shareability)
.

Every classical mediator implemented by shared randomness admits a lossless extension to any number of additional players. In particular, if
P_{12}
has the representation in Eq. (
1
), then for any response rule
p_{3}(x_{3}|t_{3},\lambda)
,

P_{123}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)p_{1}(x_{1}|t_{1},\lambda)p_{2}(x_{2}|t_{2},\lambda)p_{3}(x_{3}|t_{3},\lambda)

(8)

is a valid extension preserving
P_{12}
.

Proposition 4

(Zero anti-collusion power for copied classical seeds in relabelled tests)
.

For every classical hidden-variable mediator and every collusive score that is a relabelled copy of the authorized score, the copied-seed extension satisfies
C_{13}=A_{12}
. Hence

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{C}}(P_{12})=0.

(9)

Equivalently, the worst-case anti-collusion payoff at
\lambda=1
satisfies
U_{1}^{\rm wc,classical}(P_{12})\leq 0
. Moreover,

\sup_{P_{12}\in\mathrm{C}}U_{1}^{\rm wc}(P_{12})=0.

(10)

Classical copied seeds are free resources because they allow the colluder to reproduce the authorized score whenever the collusive test is a relabelled version of the authorized test. Their correlations admit extensions that reproduce the authorized score, leaving no shareability deficit.

2.4
Exact CHSH-certified anti-collusion frontier

The general framework reduces anti-collusion to a geometric question: how far is the authorized behavior from its collusive shadow? We now solve this question analytically in the CHSH score slice.

For the CHSH expression, let
S_{12}
and
S_{13}
denote signed Bell correlator scores for the pairs
(1,2)
and
(1,3)
using the same two observables on player 1’s side, normalized so that an uncorrelated strategy has
S=0
, the local bound is
S\leq 2
, and the Tsirelson value is
S=2\sqrt{2}
. The associated CHSH winning probability is
\omega=1/2+S/8
for the fixed CHSH orientation. Quantum theory obeys the Toner–Verstraete monogamy inequality

S_{12}^{2}+S_{13}^{2}\leq 8.

(11)

Theorem 5

(CHSH shareability frontier)
.

In any tripartite quantum strategy with binary CHSH measurements for player 1 against players 2 and 3,

S_{12}^{2}+S_{13}^{2}\leq 8.

(12)

Equivalently, if
S_{12}\geq 2\sqrt{2}-\delta
, then
|S_{13}|\leq\sqrt{4\sqrt{2}\,\delta-\delta^{2}}\leq 2^{5/4}\sqrt{\delta}
for
0\leq\delta\leq 2\sqrt{2}
, where the looser upper bound
2^{5/4}\sqrt{\delta}
becomes tight as
\delta\to 0
(i.e., near Tsirelson’s bound).

Theorem 6

(Exact score-certified CHSH anti-collusion power)
.

Let a tripartite quantum strategy produce an authorized CHSH score
S_{12}=s
, with
0\leq s\leq 2\sqrt{2}
, and let the collusive CHSH score
S_{13}
be defined using the same two observables of player 1. The score-certified anti-collusion power is

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)=\left[\frac{s-\sqrt{8-s^{2}}}{8}\right]_{+}.

(13)

In particular,
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)>0
if and only if
s>2
, and
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(2\sqrt{2})=1/(2\sqrt{2})
.

This formula is the projection of the general collusive-shadow distance onto the CHSH scoring direction. It is a one-dimensional shadow of the full strategic non-shareability distance. This equality is exact as a score-certified CHSH quantity because the Toner–Verstraete boundary is tight. For a fixed microscopic implementation, the actual state-dependent anti-collusion power may be larger;
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)
is the value certified from the observed authorized score alone.

Bell nonlocality is precisely the onset of certifiable strategic non-shareability in this CHSH-mediated private-information game. Below the local bound, the authorized CHSH score can be matched by a collusive score in the certified slice; above the local bound, monogamy creates a strictly positive shareability deficit.

Certification protocol.

The score-certified formula yields a direct operational protocol:

1.

Estimate the authorized CHSH score
S_{12}
.

2.

Use the monogamy frontier:
S_{13}^{\max}\leq\sqrt{8-S_{12}^{2}}
.

3.

Convert to a collusive winning-probability bound:

\omega_{13}^{\max}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{8-S_{12}^{2}}}{8}
.

4.

Report the certified anti-collusion power:

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(S_{12})=[(S_{12}-\sqrt{8-S_{12}^{2}})/8]_{+}
.

This protocol does not require direct access to the colluder. It certifies an upper bound on any collusive CHSH score compatible with quantum theory and the observed authorized score. The certified frontier and the classical–quantum separation are illustrated in Fig.
2
.

We encode the shareability deficit into a three-player private-information game. Players 1 and 2 receive CHSH-type private bits
t_{1},t_{2}\in\{0,1\}
and produce binary actions
x_{1},x_{2}
. The authorized score is
A_{12}=\mathbb{P}[x_{1}\oplus x_{2}=t_{1}t_{2}]
. Player 3 receives a private bit
t_{3}
in an analogous collusive test with player 1, and the collusive leakage score is
C_{13}=\mathbb{P}[x_{1}\oplus x_{3}=t_{1}t_{3}]
. The anti-collusion objective is
U_{\lambda}=A_{12}-\lambda C_{13}
.

Theorem 7

(Optimal payoff separation)
.

For the CHSH private-information game at
\lambda=1
,

\max_{P\in\mathsf{C}}U_{1}^{\rm wc}(P)=0,

(14)

where
\mathsf{C}
is the copied-seed classical class, while

\max_{P\in\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}U_{1}^{\rm wc}(P)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}},

(15)

where
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
denotes the usual finite-dimensional quantum strategy class. Because the maximum is attained in
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
itself, no closure to
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}
is needed for this statement.

The maximum in Equation (14) is attained: any classical mediator with the trivial uniform-output strategy gives
A_{12}=V^{\mathsf{C}}_{13}=1/2
, so
U^{\rm wc}_{1}=0
. The maximum cannot exceed zero because, for every classical mediator
P_{12}\in\mathsf{C}
representable as in Eq. (1), the copied-seed extension of Proposition
4
gives a valid tripartite extension with
C_{13}=A_{12}
under any relabelled scoring rule, hence
V^{\mathsf{C}}_{13}(P_{12})\geq A_{12}(P_{12})
and
U^{\rm wc}_{1}(P_{12})\leq 0
. See Supplementary Theorem
13
for full details.

The theorem does not assert that all possible coalitions are impossible. It states that, under the physically natural copied-seed extension available to classical hidden-variable mediators, the authorized score can be inherited by a colluder, whereas a maximally entangled authorized link admits no such extension.

2.5
Finite-data certification of strategic non-shareability

Finite-data certification protocol.

The deterministic certification protocol of Section
2.4
assumes an exact authorized CHSH score. In an experiment, this score is estimated from finite data. In the simplest i.i.d. setting with uniformly random CHSH settings, one estimates each correlator

E_{xy}=\mathbb{E}[ab\mid x,y],\qquad a,b\in\{\pm 1\},\quad x,y\in\{0,1\},

(16)

from the
N_{xy}
trials with setting pair
(x,y)
. Since
ab\in[-1,1]
, Hoeffding’s inequality gives

\Pr\left(|E_{xy}-\widehat{E}_{xy}|\geq r\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{N_{xy}r^{2}}{2}\right).

(17)

A union bound over the four setting pairs implies that, with probability at least
1-\alpha
,

|E_{xy}-\widehat{E}_{xy}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\log(8/\alpha)}{N_{xy}}}

(18)

for all
(x,y)
. Writing
N_{\min}=\min_{x,y}N_{xy}
and

\widehat{S}_{12}=\widehat{E}_{00}+\widehat{E}_{01}+\widehat{E}_{10}-\widehat{E}_{11},

we obtain

S_{12}\geq S_{\rm LCB}=\widehat{S}_{12}-4\sqrt{\frac{2\log(8/\alpha)}{N_{\min}}}

(19)

with confidence at least
1-\alpha
. Before applying the CHSH certification map, we clip the lower confidence bound to the physical interval:

S_{\rm cert}=\min\{2\sqrt{2},\max\{0,S_{\rm LCB}\}\}.

(20)

The certified finite-data anti-collusion bound is

\Gamma_{\rm LCB}=\left[\frac{S_{\rm cert}-\sqrt{8-S_{\rm cert}^{2}}}{8}\right]_{+}.

(21)

For example, if an experiment obtains
\widehat{S}_{12}=2.65
with
N_{\min}=2.5\times 10^{4}
samples per setting and confidence
99\%
(
\alpha=0.01
), the corrected Hoeffding radius is approximately
0.0925
, giving
S_{\rm LCB}\approx 2.56
and
\Gamma_{\rm LCB}\approx 0.169
.

Statistical model assumptions.

The Hoeffding bound used here assumes that trials are independent and identically distributed and that measurement settings are drawn from a uniform distribution independently of the device’s internal state. It does not assume any model of the internal state or measurements. The i.i.d. assumption is standard in the prepare-and-measure literature but is stronger than the memory-tolerant assumptions used in modern device-independent security proofs based on entropy accumulation
Dupuis2020
;
ArnonFriedman2018
. Extending the present certification to the fully memory-tolerant regime is a natural next step; the monotone certification map
s\mapsto\Gamma^{+}_{\rm CHSH}(s)
is compatible with any concentration tool that yields a lower confidence bound on
S_{12}
, including martingale-based bounds.

Noise-induced transition into strategic non-shareability.

The anti-collusion power persists under moderate noise, but with a sharp threshold. Consider the Werner-noisy authorized state

\rho_{12}(\eta)=\eta\outerproduct{\Phi_2}{\Phi_2}+(1-\eta)\frac{I}{4}.

(22)

The authorized CHSH score is
S_{12}(\eta)=2\sqrt{2}\,\eta
. The score-certified CHSH anti-collusion value inferred from the Werner visibility is

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{\rm score}(\eta)\equiv\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(S_{12}=2\sqrt{2}\,\eta)=\left[\frac{\eta-\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}{2\sqrt{2}}\right]_{+},

(23)

which is positive exactly when
\eta>1/\sqrt{2}
. This is a certified lower bound on the state-dependent anti-collusion power of the specific Werner implementation. It is not claimed to be the exact state-dependent value unless a saturating tripartite extension is constructed.

The threshold
\eta=1/\sqrt{2}
is the transition from shareable coordination to certified strategic non-shareability. The visibility threshold for positive anti-collusion power coincides with the CHSH nonlocality threshold. Werner noise gives a score-certified lower bound and a sharp onset threshold; it does not imply exact state-dependent saturation for every Werner state unless a saturating tripartite extension is explicitly constructed.

2.6
NPA upper envelopes beyond CHSH

The shareability-deficit framework is not tied to the exact CHSH circle. For general games, the collusive vulnerability

V_{13}^{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=\sup_{P_{123}\in{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}}(P_{12})}C_{13}(P_{123})

(24)

is a quantum correlation optimization problem. It can be relaxed by standard semidefinite hierarchies for quantum correlations, such as the NPA hierarchy.

SDP relaxation for collusive vulnerability.

Let
\Gamma
be the moment matrix of the NPA hierarchy at level
k
. The relaxed collusive vulnerability is

V_{13}^{{\rm NPA},k}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=\max_{\Gamma\succeq 0}\langle G_{13},\Gamma\rangle

(25)

subject to NPA moment constraints, the observed authorized constraints, and no-signalling consistency. The NPA hierarchy gives a sequence of outer approximations to the quantum or commuting-operator correlation set. At each finite level, the exact optimum of the NPA relaxation is an upper bound on the corresponding closed quantum or commuting-operator value. A numerical value is used as a certificate only when solver diagnostics verify dual consistency, feasibility and residual tolerances. Under the standard convergence assumptions of the hierarchy, these bounds converge to the corresponding closed quantum or commuting-operator value. This suggests a practical route to estimating anti-collusion power beyond analytically solvable CHSH-type frontiers.

Tilted CHSH NPA upper envelopes.

To test whether the framework extends beyond the symmetric CHSH circle, we consider the standard tilted-CHSH family

I_{\alpha}=\alpha\langle A_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle-\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle,

V_{13}^{{\rm NPA},2}(s;\alpha)=\max I_{\alpha}^{13}

(27)

subject to the moment constraints and the authorized score constraint
I_{\alpha}^{12}\geq s
.

The NPA level-2 moment matrix is indexed by words of length at most 2 drawn from
\{I,A_{0},A_{1},B_{0},B_{1},C_{0},C_{1}\}
, with projective substitutions
A_{x}^{2}=B_{y}^{2}=C_{z}^{2}=I
and commutativity of operators belonging to different parties. At level 1 the relaxation is too loose because the Alice–Bob and Alice–Charlie subsystems are only weakly coupled through Alice’s single-party marginals; level 2 couples them through the shared second-order moments and yields a useful bound.

At finite NPA level, the relaxation gives an outer approximation to the quantum extension set. A numerical value is treated as a numerically certified NPA upper bound under the stated residual criteria only when the SDP solution passes dual-feasibility and residual checks. In Fig.

Resource-theoretic viewpoint.

The present results suggest a resource-theoretic viewpoint of strategic non-shareability. Freely shareable correlations, such as copied classical seeds in relabelled collusive tests, form the free objects. Non-extendible correlations become resources because they generate positive anti-collusion power. The CHSH result provides the first exactly solvable instance of this resource viewpoint.

The framework is closely related to
k
-extendibility, but with a task-dependent direction. Standard
k
-extendibility asks whether a bipartite state or behavior can be symmetrically extended to additional parties. Anti-collusion power instead asks how well a specific collusive score can be achieved over all extensions preserving the authorized marginal. It is therefore a game-weighted, operational version of non-extendibility.

3
Discussion

The central principle of this work is that anti-collusion is a property of extension geometry. A mediator is strategically vulnerable when its useful authorized correlation can be extended to a colluder without loss. It is strategically protected when the relevant authorized coordination has a positive shareability deficit.

We have introduced strategic non-shareability as a measurable physical property of mediated correlations, formalized through the collusive shadow and the operational distance from it. The general theorem connecting anti-collusion capacity to total-variation distance from the collusive shadow shows that game-optimized anti-collusion capacity equals the operational distance; fixed games provide witnesses of this distance. The CHSH case is solved exactly and yields a sharp onset at the Bell local bound, with a finite-data certification protocol from observed Bell scores. The SDP/NPA construction provides a systematic route to extending the framework beyond analytically solvable frontiers. When the resulting upper bounds are numerically certified, they can witness anti-collusion regions for non-CHSH Bell scores such as tilted CHSH.

The exact CHSH frontier and the tilted-CHSH NPA computations play different roles. The CHSH result is an analytic equality in a score-certified slice. The tilted-CHSH computation is a finite-level semidefinite outer approximation. It provides numerically certified NPA upper bounds only at verified grid points under the stated residual criteria. Without matching lower-bound strategies, the resulting curves should not be interpreted as exact tilted-CHSH monogamy frontiers. Their role is to demonstrate a reproducible route for extending anti-collusion certification beyond analytically solvable cases.

The present framework separates three levels of certification. The distance theorem is a general finite-alphabet statement for compact closed extension classes, including classical, no-signalling, fixed-dimensional quantum, and closed quantum behavior sets. The CHSH result is an exact score-certified quantum frontier following from Toner-Verstraete monogamy. The tilted-CHSH results are NPA level-2 upper envelopes on collusive vulnerability; they are treated as numerically certified upper bounds only at grid points with verified dual feasibility, primal-dual gap and residuals below the stated tolerances. This separation is important: exact analytic frontiers, finite-data certificates, and semidefinite relaxations play different roles in quantifying strategic non-shareability.

The present framework is closely related to, but distinct from, the standard theory of
k
-extendibility and quantum marginal compatibility. Standard
k
-extendibility asks whether a bipartite state or behavior admits a symmetric extension to additional copies of one party. The collusive shadow considered here is asymmetric and operationally weighted. The authorized marginal
P_{12}
is held fixed, while the colluder’s attainable behavior is obtained by projecting every admissible extension through the relabelled marginal map
P_{123}\mapsto\Pi_{13\to 12}P_{13}
. The central quantity is therefore not merely the feasibility of an extension, but the image of the extension set under this collusive marginal map and the largest game-dependent score achievable over that image. In this sense, collusive vulnerability is a game-weighted extension optimization, and anti-collusion power is an operational form of non-extendibility.

Semidefinite hierarchies such as the Doherty–Parrilo–Spedalieri hierarchy for symmetric extensions
DPS2002
;
DPS2004
and the NPA hierarchy for quantum correlations
NPA2007
;
NPA2008
provide natural relaxation tools for these extension and marginal-compatibility problems. Our use of NPA is therefore not intended as a new hierarchy. Rather, it is an operational deployment of existing extension relaxations to bound collusive vulnerability in mediated games. The additional structure introduced here is the strategic layer: a relabelled collusive shadow, a score functional on that shadow, and a game-optimized capacity that reduces to the total-variation distance from the authorized behavior to the shadow.

Relation to no-cloning.

The physical intuition behind strategic non-shareability is rooted in the same quantum feature that underlies the no-cloning theorem
Wootters1982
: quantum information cannot be freely duplicated. However, the operational question addressed here is distinct in three respects.
First, no-cloning is a structural impossibility statement about maps between Hilbert spaces, whereas the present framework is a quantitative resource theory: anti-collusion power takes values in
[0,1/(2\sqrt{2})]
for the CHSH score slice and varies continuously with the authorized score.
Second, the relevant physical obstruction is not no-cloning itself but its many-body descendant, the monogamy of entanglement
Coffman2000
and its Bell-correlation form
Toner2006
. A colluder in our framework does not attempt to clone the authorized state; the colluder is already a party in an admissible tripartite extension and asks how much correlation can be carried into that extension.
Third, no-cloning protects information identifiability, while the present framework protects strategic advantage: the certified quantity is the worst-case score gap in a game, not a fidelity. This makes shareability deficit directly measurable from observed Bell scores using finite-data concentration, whereas no-cloning is a theoretical impossibility that cannot itself be witnessed in this way.
Together, these distinctions place the shareability-deficit framework within the operational, game-theoretic side of the no-cloning
\to
monogamy hierarchy: it inherits the same physical asymmetry between classical and quantum resources, but turns it into a measurable adversarial figure of merit.

The present results do not imply universal security against all coalitions. The exact analytic formula relies on the CHSH monogamy frontier and on collusive scores sharing the same observables of player 1. Other Bell inequalities, multipartite resources, and coalition structures may have different extension geometry. The contribution is therefore not a universal security theorem, but a framework for quantifying when a mediated correlation is non-shareable in a strategically useful way.

Future work should close the gap between NPA upper bounds and explicit lower-bound strategies for non-CHSH inequalities, develop fully memory-tolerant device-independent confidence analyses, and identify monotones under natural shareability-preserving free operations. Experimentally, the relevant task is not simply to violate a Bell inequality, but to certify a positive shareability deficit from observed authorized correlations. Candidate platforms include photonic polarization qubits, trapped ions, superconducting qubits, or nitrogen-vacancy based networks.

4.1
Classical copied-seed extension

The proof of Theorem
3
generalizes immediately from one colluder to
m
colluders by assigning response rules
p_{j}(x_{j}|t_{j},\lambda)
for
j=3,\ldots,m+2
and summing over the added outputs. For every classical hidden-variable mediator in the copied-seed threat model, the colluder can reproduce the authorized score in any relabelled test, giving zero anti-collusion power (Proposition

4.2
Distance theorem

Supplementary Note
7
gives the full proof of Theorem
2
. The proof uses: finite-alphabet compactness of the behavior sets; convexity of the collusive shadow; Sion’s minimax theorem; and the variational characterization of total-variation distance. The basic properties needed for the framework are: (i) freely shareable correlations have zero anti-collusion power; (ii) positive anti-collusion power witnesses task-relevant non-shareability; and (iii) collusive vulnerability is concave under unflagged mixing.

4.3
CHSH monogamy frontier

Let
\mathcal{B}_{12}
and
\mathcal{B}_{13}
be the two CHSH operators sharing player 1’s observables. The standard Toner–Verstraete monogamy inequality
S_{12}^{2}+S_{13}^{2}\leq 8
bounds the squared expectation values. Supplementary Note
10
states the operator form, derives the tightness construction, and gives the exact certified anti-collusion power formula of Theorem
6
. Theorem
7
uses exact monogamy of a pure authorized marginal: if
\rho_{12}=\Tr_{3}\rho_{123}
is pure, then
\rho_{123}=\rho_{12}\otimes\rho_{3}
. Because the maximally entangled Bell state
\ket{\Phi_2}
has maximally mixed local marginal
\Tr_{B}\outerproduct{\Phi_2}{\Phi_2}=I/2
, Alice’s outcome distribution is uniform for any local observable. After the factorization
\rho_{123}=\outerproduct{\Phi_2}{\Phi_2}_{12}\otimes\rho_{3}
, the colluder’s system is product with Alice’s, so any collusive response rule gives
C_{13}=1/2
regardless of the colluder’s strategy.

4.4
Finite-data estimator

We estimate the CHSH score either by estimating four correlators separately or by using a single-trial unbiased estimator
Z_{i}=4(-1)^{xy}ab
. The main-text confidence interval uses the correlator-wise estimator with a union bound; the Supplement gives both variants. Supplementary Note
14

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)
on
[0,2\sqrt{2}]

4.5
NPA relaxation and diagnostics

The tilted-CHSH collusive vulnerability is bounded by an NPA level-2 semidefinite relaxation implemented in CVXPY. For three parties with binary
\pm 1
observables
A_{x},B_{y},C_{z}
and projective-measurement substitutions
A_{x}^{2}=B_{y}^{2}=C_{z}^{2}=I
, the level-2 moment matrix is
22\times 22
and indexed by words of length at most 2 drawn from
\{I,A_{0},A_{1},B_{0},B_{1},C_{0},C_{1}\}
. Different-party operators commute. The relaxed problem is

V_{13}^{{\rm NPA},2}(s;\alpha)=\max_{\Gamma\succeq 0}\;I_{\alpha}^{13}

subject to
\Gamma\succeq 0
, normalization
\Gamma_{\emptyset,\emptyset}=1
, algebraic substitutions, and the authorized-score constraint
I_{\alpha}^{12}\geq s
.

The SDP was implemented in CVXPY 1.8.2 and solved using SCS 3.2.7 with tolerance parameters (
eps_abs
=1e-9,
eps_rel
=1e-9,
max_iters
=100000). The authorized-score grid used 60 threshold values for each tilt parameter. A grid point is labelled certified only when the solver status is “optimal”, the dual objective is available, the primal-dual gap is below
10^{-6}
, the maximum affine residual is below
10^{-5}
, and the minimum eigenvalue of the moment matrix is no smaller than
-10^{-7}
. Points failing these tests are shown only as numerical indications and are not used as certified upper bounds. The numerical implementation uses a real symmetrized relaxation in which non-Hermitian same-party products are represented by their real symmetrized moments. This is an outer relaxation of the full complex Hermitian NPA problem and is used only for numerical upper-envelope certification. At level 1 the relaxation is too loose to yield a useful collusive bound because the Alice–Bob and Alice–Charlie subsystems are only weakly coupled through Alice’s single-party marginals; level 2 couples them through the shared second-order moments and yields a useful bound.

\sum_{x_{j}}p_{j}(x_{j}|t_{j},\lambda)=1

7
Anti-collusion capacity as distance from the collusive shadow

Supplementary Theorem 3

(Distance theorem)
.

Let
P_{12}
be an authorized behavior with finite input-output alphabets. Let

\mathsf{R}\in\{\mathsf{C},\mathsf{NS},\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d},\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}\}

be an admissible extension class, where
\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d}
denotes quantum behaviors realizable with local Hilbert-space dimensions bounded by
d
and
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}=\overline{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}
denotes the closure of the finite-dimensional quantum set. Then

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}d_{\rm TV}(P_{12},Q).

(31)

Proof.

Step 1: Finite-dimensional behavior space.
Because the input and output alphabets are finite, all conditional behaviors live in a finite-dimensional real vector space. The set of all no-signalling behaviors is a compact polytope. The classical set is also a compact polytope. The fixed-dimensional set
\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d}
is compact because the sets of density operators and POVM effects in fixed finite dimension are compact and the Born rule is continuous. The closed set
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}=\overline{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}
is compact as a closed subset of the finite-dimensional probability simplex.

Step 2: Collusive shadow compactness.
The collusive shadow

\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\{\Pi_{13\to 12}P_{13}:P_{123}\in{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})\}

is the image of
{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})
under a linear map. Hence it is compact and convex whenever
{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})
is compact and convex.

Step 3: Capacity definition.
For relabelled scoring rules
0\leq h\leq 1
,

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\left[\langle h,P_{12}\rangle-\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\langle h,Q\rangle\right]_{+}.

Since
h=0
gives value zero, the positive part can be absorbed into the supremum:

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\langle h,P_{12}-Q\rangle.

Step 4: Sion minimax.
The set of predicates
0\leq h\leq 1
is compact and convex. The collusive shadow is compact and convex. The payoff
(h,Q)\mapsto\langle h,P_{12}-Q\rangle
is bilinear, hence both convex and concave in the relevant variables. By Sion’s minimax theorem,

\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\langle h,P_{12}-Q\rangle=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\langle h,P_{12}-Q\rangle.

Step 5: TV variational characterization.
For fixed
Q
, the variational characterization of total variation gives

\sup_{0\leq h\leq 1}\langle h,P_{12}-Q\rangle=d_{\rm TV}(P_{12},Q).

Therefore,

\mathcal{C}_{\rm AC}^{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{R}}(P_{12})}d_{\rm TV}(P_{12},Q).

∎

Supplementary Remark 4
.

If
\mathsf{R}
is the unclosed finite-dimensional quantum set
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
with unbounded dimension, the theorem applies after passing to its closure
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}
. For fixed dimension
d
, it applies directly to
\mathsf{Q}_{\leq d}
. The CHSH score-certified result used in the main text does not depend on this subtlety because it follows directly from the Toner-Verstraete inequality.

Supplementary Proposition 5

(Pure marginal factorization)
.

Let
\rho_{ABC}

Supplementary Proposition 10

(Tightness of the monogamy frontier)
.

For every angle
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
there exists a three-qubit pure state and a choice of binary observables for each player such that
S_{12}=2\sqrt{2}\sin\theta
and
S_{13}=2\sqrt{2}\cos\theta
. Consequently every point on the quarter-circle
S_{12}^{2}+S_{13}^{2}=8
with
S_{12},S_{13}\geq 0
is attained.

Proof.

Define
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\cos\theta|110\rangle+\sin\theta|101\rangle+|011\rangle)
. Let
A_{0}=\sigma_{x}
,
A_{1}=\sigma_{y}
, and
B_{0}=C_{0}=(\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y})/\sqrt{2}
,
B_{1}=C_{1}=(\sigma_{x}-\sigma_{y})/\sqrt{2}
. A direct evaluation of the expectation values yields
\langle\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}\otimes I\rangle=\sin\theta
and
\langle\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{y}\otimes I\rangle=0
(the cross terms cancel by orthogonality). Hence
\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sin\theta+0)=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}

\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}
,
\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}
, and
\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle=-\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}
. Summing gives
S_{12}=2\sqrt{2}\sin\theta
; the computation for
S_{13}
is symmetric under the

S_{12}^{2}+S_{13}^{2}=8
is attained as
\theta

∎

Supplementary Remark 11

(Scope of Bell monogamy)
.

The CHSH monogamy relation and its tightness should not be read as a universal statement about all Bell inequalities. Outside the Toner–Verstraete family, general multi-party Bell monogamy remains open. The operational anti-collusion guarantee is therefore tied specifically to self-testing inequalities such as CHSH, whose maximal violation pins down the authorized state.

Certified CHSH anti-collusion power

Supplementary Proposition 12

(Exact certified anti-collusion power)
.

Let a tripartite quantum strategy produce an authorized CHSH score
S_{12}=s
,
0\leq s\leq 2\sqrt{2}
, with the collusive CHSH score
S_{13}
using the same two observables of player 1. Then

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)=\left[\frac{s-\sqrt{8-s^{2}}}{8}\right]_{+}.

(35)

In particular,
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)>0\iff s>2
, and
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(2\sqrt{2})=1/(2\sqrt{2})
.

Proof.

From Theorem
9
, any collusive extension satisfies
S_{13}\leq\sqrt{8-s^{2}}
. Converting to winning probabilities gives
\omega_{12}(s)-\omega_{13}^{\max}(s)=(s-\sqrt{8-s^{2}})/8
. The positive part yields the formula. Positivity requires
s>2
.
∎

12
Minimal game separation

Supplementary Theorem 13

(Supremum-level payoff separation for
\lambda=1
)
.

For any classical hidden-variable mediator in the copied-seed threat model,
V_{13}^{\rm classical}(P_{12})\geq A_{12}(P_{12})
, and hence
U_{1}^{\rm wc,classical}(P_{12})\leq 0
. Moreover,
\sup_{P_{12}\in\mathrm{C}}U_{1}^{\rm wc}(P_{12})=0
.

For any quantum strategy, the colluder can output a uniformly random bit, so
V_{13}^{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}\geq 1/2
. Tsirelson’s bound gives
A_{12}\leq 1/2+1/(2\sqrt{2})
. Therefore
U_{1}^{\rm wc}\leq 1/(2\sqrt{2})
. A maximally entangled CHSH strategy attains this value:
A_{12}=\cos^{2}(\pi/8)
and, by Proposition
5
,
V_{13}^{\rm quantum}=1/2
. Thus
\sup_{P\in\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}U_{1}^{\rm wc}(P)=1/(2\sqrt{2})
. Here
\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}
denotes the usual finite-dimensional quantum strategy class; no closure issue is involved in the attainable maximally entangled CHSH strategy.

Proof.

The classical statement follows from Corollary
2
. For the quantum upper bound, note that a uniformly random output by player 3 yields
C_{13}=1/2
regardless of player 1’s strategy, so
V_{13}^{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}\geq 1/2
for every quantum mediator. Tsirelson’s bound
S_{12}\leq 2\sqrt{2}
implies
A_{12}=\omega_{12}\leq 1/2+1/(2\sqrt{2})
. Hence
U_{1}^{\rm wc}=A_{12}-V_{13}^{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm fin}}\leq 1/(2\sqrt{2})
. The maximally entangled strategy achieves equality because it saturates Tsirelson’s bound and, by exact monogamy, every extension factorizes. For the maximally entangled CHSH strategy, Alice’s two optimal observables have zero expectation, so Alice’s output is unbiased for both inputs. After factorization, the colluder’s output is independent of this unbiased bit. Hence every collusive response rule gives
C_{13}=1/2
, and therefore
V_{13}^{\rm quantum}=1/2
.
∎

13
Noise-induced transition of anti-collusion power

Supplementary Proposition 14

(Werner-noise anti-collusion power)
.

Let players 1 and 2 share the Werner-noisy authorized state
\rho_{12}(\eta)=\eta\outerproduct{\Phi_2}{\Phi_2}+(1-\eta)\frac{\mathbb{I}_{4}}{4}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and use the CHSH measurements optimal for
\outerproduct{\Phi_2}{\Phi_2}
. Then
A_{12}(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta}{2\sqrt{2}}
. By CHSH monogamy, the maximal collusive winning probability for any tripartite extension is bounded by
C_{13}^{\max}(\eta)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}{2\sqrt{2}}
. Therefore the score-certified anti-collusion power satisfies

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{\rm score}(\eta)\equiv\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(2\sqrt{2}\,\eta)=\left[\frac{\eta-\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}{2\sqrt{2}}\right]_{+}.

(36)

This is a score-certified lower bound on any implementation with observed Werner visibility
\eta
. It is not claimed to be the exact state-dependent anti-collusion power except at
\eta=1
.

Proof.

The Werner state scales the CHSH correlator by
\eta
, giving
S_{12}(\eta)=2\sqrt{2}\,\eta
and
A_{12}(\eta)=1/2+\eta/(2\sqrt{2})
. By CHSH monogamy,
S_{13}^{2}\leq 8-S_{12}(\eta)^{2}=8(1-\eta^{2})
, so
C_{13}^{\max}(\eta)\leq 1/2+\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}/(2\sqrt{2})
. Subtracting gives the certified lower bound. Positivity requires
\eta>1/\sqrt{2}
.
∎

Supplementary Remark 15
.

This is a score-certified lower bound, not an exact state-dependent saturation for every
\eta
. The bound is tight in the
\eta=1
limit because exact monogamy forces the colluder to be uncorrelated with player 1, but for intermediate
\eta
the exact state-dependent anti-collusion power may be larger.

14
Finite-data certification

14.1
Correlator-wise estimator (main-text protocol)

Supplementary Proposition 16

(Finite-data lower confidence bound)
.

Assume
N
independent CHSH trials with uniformly random settings. For each setting pair
(x,y)\in\{0,1\}^{2}
let
N_{xy}
be the number of trials and define

\widehat{E}_{xy}=\frac{1}{N_{xy}}\sum_{i:x_{i}=x,y_{i}=y}a_{i}b_{i},\qquad a_{i},b_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}.

Then, with probability at least
1-\alpha
,

S_{12}\geq S_{\rm LCB}=\widehat{S}_{12}-4\sqrt{\frac{2\log(8/\alpha)}{N_{\min}}},

(37)

where

\widehat{S}_{12}=\widehat{E}_{00}+\widehat{E}_{01}+\widehat{E}_{10}-\widehat{E}_{11},\qquad N_{\min}=\min_{x,y}N_{xy}.

Consequently,

\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(S_{12})\geq\Gamma_{\rm LCB}=\left[\frac{S_{\rm cert}-\sqrt{8-S_{\rm cert}^{2}}}{8}\right]_{+},

(38)

where

S_{\rm cert}=\min\{2\sqrt{2},\max\{0,S_{\rm LCB}\}\}.

Proof.

For each fixed setting pair
(x,y)
, the random variables
a_{i}b_{i}
lie in
[-1,1]
. Hoeffding’s inequality gives

\Pr\left(|E_{xy}-\widehat{E}_{xy}|\geq r\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{N_{xy}r^{2}}{2}\right).

Setting this probability to
\alpha/4
gives

r_{xy}=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(8/\alpha)}{N_{xy}}}.

A union bound over four setting pairs gives joint confidence at least
1-\alpha
. Hence

|S_{12}-\widehat{S}_{12}|\leq\sum_{x,y}|E_{xy}-\widehat{E}_{xy}|\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{2\log(8/\alpha)}{N_{\min}}}.

This proves the lower confidence bound. The monotonicity of
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}
on
[0,2\sqrt{2}]
(Lemma
17
) gives the final inequality after clipping
S_{\rm LCB}
to the physical interval.
∎

Supplementary Lemma 17

(Monotonicity of
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}
)
.

The certified anti-collusion power
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)=[(s-\sqrt{8-s^{2}})/8]_{+}
is monotone increasing on
[0,2\sqrt{2}]
.

Proof.

For
s\leq 2
the function is identically zero, hence monotone. For
s>2
, differentiate:

\frac{d}{ds}\frac{s-\sqrt{8-s^{2}}}{8}=\frac{1}{8}\left(1+\frac{s}{\sqrt{8-s^{2}}}\right)>0.

Thus
\Gamma_{\rm CHSH}^{+}(s)
is strictly increasing on
[2,2\sqrt{2}]
, and therefore monotone on the entire interval.
∎

14.2
Single-trial unbiased estimator (alternative)

An alternative is to use the single-trial unbiased estimator

Z_{i}=4(-1)^{x_{i}y_{i}}a_{i}b_{i}\in[-4,4],

which satisfies
\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}]=S_{12}
. With
\widehat{S}_{12}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}Z_{i}
, Hoeffding’s inequality for range
[-4,4]
gives

\Pr[\widehat{S}_{12}-S_{12}\geq r]\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{2Nr^{2}}{64}\right)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{Nr^{2}}{32}\right).

Solving
\exp(-Nr^2/32)=\alpha
yields
r=4\sqrt{2\log(1/\alpha)/N}
. This bound is looser than the correlator-wise bound for typical setting distributions but requires no union bound.

Supplementary Remark 18

(Empirical Bernstein alternative)
.

If the CHSH settings are randomized adaptively, a martingale bound such as the empirical Bernstein inequality can replace Hoeffding’s bound and yield tighter confidence intervals when the variance is small. The qualitative conclusion—finite-data certification of anti-collusion power—remains unchanged.

No-signalling and private information

Supplementary Proposition 19

(Full no-signalling mediator)
.

Let a tripartite quantum mediator be specified by a state
\rho_{123}
and local POVMs
\{E^{(i)}_{t_{i},x_{i}}\}_{x_{i}}
with
\sum_{x_{i}}E^{(i)}_{t_{i},x_{i}}=\mathbb{I}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
. The induced distribution

P(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\Tr\!\left[\rho_{123}\left(E^{(1)}_{t_{1},x_{1}}\otimes E^{(2)}_{t_{2},x_{2}}\otimes E^{(3)}_{t_{3},x_{3}}\right)\right]

(39)

is fully no-signalling: for every subset
S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
,
P(x_{S}|t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=P(x_{S}|t_{S})
.

Proof.

For any subset
S
, sum over
\{x_{j}:j\notin S\}
and use completeness of the POVMs. The resulting expression depends only on
\{t_{i}:i\in S\}
.
∎

16
NPA relaxation for collusive vulnerability

16.1
General NPA formulation

For a general game, the quantum collusive vulnerability

V_{13}^{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=\sup_{P_{123}\in{\rm Ext}_{\mathsf{Q}_{\rm cl}}(P_{12})}C_{13}(P_{123})

(40)

is a quantum correlation optimization. The NPA hierarchy provides a sequence of semidefinite relaxations.

At level
k
, one introduces a moment matrix
\Gamma
indexed by strings of measurement operators of length at most
k
. The matrix entries correspond to expectations of products of the local POVM elements
E^{(1)}_{a|x}
,
E^{(2)}_{b|y}
,
E^{(3)}_{c|z}
. The relaxed problem is

V_{13}^{{\rm NPA},k}(P_{12};\mathcal{G})=\max_{\Gamma\succeq 0}\;\langle G_{13},\Gamma\rangle

(41)

subject to normalization
\Gamma\succeq 0
and
\Gamma_{\emptyset,\emptyset}=1
, no-signalling consistency, and commutativity of operators belonging to different parties.

Authorized behavior constraints are imposed as linear constraints on moment entries. In the projector formulation,

P_{12}(a,b|x,y)=\langle E^{(1)}_{a|x}E^{(2)}_{b|y}\rangle,

and this expectation is identified with the corresponding moment entry. In the binary-observable formulation used for tilted CHSH, the relevant constraints and objectives are linear functions of moments such as
\langle A_{x}B_{y}\rangle
,
\langle A_{x}C_{z}\rangle
,
\langle A_{x}\rangle
. For the tilted-CHSH upper envelope we impose the authorized-score constraint
I_{\alpha}^{12}(\Gamma)\geq s
directly as a linear constraint on these moment entries.

The NPA hierarchy gives a sequence of outer approximations to the quantum or commuting-operator correlation set. At each finite level, the exact optimum of the finite-level SDP is an upper bound; a numerical solver value is treated as a certificate only when the dual objective, primal-dual gap, affine residual and PSD residual satisfy the stated diagnostic thresholds. Under the standard convergence assumptions of the hierarchy, these bounds converge to the corresponding closed quantum or commuting-operator value.

16.2
Reduced length-2 tilted-CHSH relaxation

For the tilted-CHSH numerical frontier in Figure
4
of the main text, we use the standard tilted-CHSH expressions

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{12}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle A_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}B_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{1}B_{0}\rangle-\langle A_{1}B_{1}\rangle,

(42)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{13}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle A_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}C_{0}\rangle+\langle A_{0}C_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{1}C_{0}\rangle-\langle A_{1}C_{1}\rangle,

(43)

with the same observables
A_{0},A_{1}
for player 1 in both expressions. The classical bound is
I_{\alpha}^{\rm L}\leq 2+\alpha
and the quantum maximum is
I_{\alpha}^{\rm Q}=\sqrt{8+2\alpha^{2}}
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
. For each fixed
\alpha
and authorized score
s
, we solve

\displaystyle V_{13}^{{\rm NPA},2}(s;\alpha)=\max_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{13}(\Gamma)

(44)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\Gamma\succeq 0,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\emptyset,\emptyset}=1,

\displaystyle A_{x}^{2}=B_{y}^{2}=C_{z}^{2}=I,

\displaystyle[A_{x},B_{y}]=[A_{x},C_{z}]=[B_{y},C_{z}]=0,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{12}(\Gamma)\geq s.

This gives an upper envelope: the largest collusive tilted-CHSH score allowed by the relaxation among all strategies whose authorized tilted-CHSH score is at least
s
.

For the numerical tilted-CHSH relaxation we use the reduced length-2 word set

\mathcal{W}_{2}=\bigl\{I,\;A_{0},A_{1},B_{0},B_{1},C_{0},C_{1},\;A_{0}A_{1},B_{0}B_{1},C_{0}C_{1},\;A_{0}B_{0},A_{0}B_{1},A_{1}B_{0},A_{1}B_{1},\\
A_{0}C_{0},A_{0}C_{1},A_{1}C_{0},A_{1}C_{1},\;B_{0}C_{0},B_{0}C_{1},B_{1}C_{0},B_{1}C_{1}\bigr\}.

This gives a
22\times 22
moment matrix. We refer to this reduced length-2 word relaxation as “level 2” in this manuscript.

Different-party observables commute and are reordered into a canonical party order. Same-party observables are not assumed to commute. In particular,
A_{0}A_{1}
and
A_{1}A_{0}
are adjoints of one another and are not identified by commutation. The code handles adjoint words when constructing entries
\Gamma_{u,v}=\langle u^{\dagger}v\rangle
.

The code does not globally sort all operators. It only reorders operators belonging to different parties. This prevents imposing false commutation relations such as
A_{0}A_{1}=A_{1}A_{0}
.

The numerical implementation uses a real symmetrized relaxation in which non-Hermitian same-party products are represented by their real symmetrized moments. This is an outer relaxation of the full complex Hermitian NPA problem and is used only for numerical upper-envelope certification. It does not impose same-party commutativity.

At level 1 the relaxation is too loose because the Alice–Bob and Alice–Charlie subsystems are only weakly coupled through Alice’s single-party marginals; level 2 couples them through the shared second-order moments and yields a useful bound. The optimization is performed with the SCS solver on a uniform grid of
60
authorized-score values per tilt parameter.

16.3
Solver diagnostics and certificate criteria

A grid point is labelled certified if:

1.

the solver status is
optimal
;

2.

a dual objective is available;

3.

the primal-dual gap is below
\epsilon_{\rm gap}=10^{-6}
;

4.

the maximum affine residual is below
\epsilon_{\rm aff}=10^{-5}
;

5.

the minimum eigenvalue of the moment matrix is no smaller than
-\epsilon_{\rm psd}=10^{-7}
.

Points failing these tests are reported as numerical indications only and are not used as certified upper bounds.

The certificate flag refers to the numerical SDP solve, not to exactness of the tilted-CHSH frontier. Even certified NPA values are finite-level outer bounds; they do not establish tightness unless matched by explicit strategies.

The SDP was solved using SCS 3.2.7 through CVXPY 1.8.2 with tolerances
eps_abs
=1e-9,
eps_rel
=1e-9,
max_iters
=100000. SCS was used for exploratory scans. Points are labelled certified only when independently verified by dual-feasibility and residual checks. Otherwise they are shown as numerical indications.

16.4
\alpha=0
CHSH sanity check

As a sanity check, we set
\alpha=0
, where tilted CHSH reduces to standard CHSH. The analytic monogamy frontier is

I_{13}^{\max}(s)=\sqrt{8-s^{2}}

for
0\leq s\leq 2\sqrt{2}
. We ran the NPA level-2 upper envelope on a grid of 60 points and compared with the analytic curve. On certified grid points, the maximum absolute deviation is
5.5\times 10^{-4}
and the mean absolute deviation is
2.7\times 10^{-5}
. The single non-certified point is the Tsirelson endpoint
s=2\sqrt{2}
, where SCS returns
optimal_inaccurate
. This provides a numerical validation of the moment-matrix construction and score extraction in the CHSH limit. It does not by itself establish tightness of the tilted-CHSH envelopes for
\alpha>0
.
