Color–Speed Separation for Mixed Random Operators in Multispeed Stochastic Klein–Gordon Systems
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
We study a two-component fractional stochastic Klein–Gordon system on 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} , driven by independent space-time white noises and with distinct high-frequency propagation speeds. The main object is the mixed paracontrolled random operator generated by the cross interaction u 1 ​ u 2 u_{1}u_{2} . We prove an operator-level color–speed separation principle for its direct Gaussian contraction channels: cross-color contractions vanish by independence, while surviving same-color contractions are forced by the coupling graph to have different Duhamel and noise speeds. In the strongly low–high region this gives a speed-gap denominator and yields finite deterministic Volterra multipliers. We construct the baseline stochastic lift, prove first Picard smoothing, analyze the resonant cubic colored Wick symbols, and prove pathwise convergence of the mixed random-operator package for α > 3 / 4 \alpha>3/4 . The genuinely delicate operator estimates are concentrated in the ultra-low input window; once the input frequency leaves this window, the positive Sobolev regularity of the full input gives a direct dyadic gain and the middle-frequency complement closes deterministically. The limiting enhanced data contain centered fluctuations and finite deterministic Volterra contractions; no divergent operator-level counterterm is introduced in the mixed channel. The final deterministic result is conditional: assuming the analytic Strichartz package stated in the paper, the enhanced data define a local solution map for α > 12 / 13 \alpha>12/13 .



Keywords:
Stochastic PDEs; paracontrolled distributions; multispeed Klein–Gordon equations; random operators; color–speed separation; colored Wick contractions.

Introduction

1.1
Singular dispersive SPDEs and random operators

Singular stochastic PDEs driven by space-time white noise require an enhanced formulation: the linear stochastic convolution is typically a distribution, and nonlinear expressions built from it must be interpreted through stochastic lifts together with a deterministic solution map from enhanced data to solutions. In the parabolic setting this philosophy appears in the Da Prato–Debussche decomposition, regularity structures, and paracontrolled distributions
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

For dispersive and hyperbolic equations, heat-type smoothing is absent. In dimension three, the stochastic analysis is intertwined with the oscillatory geometry of the linear propagator. The paracontrolled random-operator construction of Gubinelli, Koch, and Oh
[
4
]
is the main methodological reference point: it organizes random operators into deterministic contractions and centered fluctuations, and treats the scalar same-speed pure-difference channel by a precise Fourier symmetrization. The present paper keeps this random-operator viewpoint, but the mechanism studied here is different. The cross coupling
u_{1}u_{2}
produces a two-color, two-speed algebra in which direct contractions are selected by the coupling graph itself.

The purpose of the paper is to isolate that structural selection. The four mixed blocks split into two families: the blocks with the same Duhamel and noise speed are cross-color and therefore centered by independence; the blocks with a same-color contraction are forced to have different Duhamel and noise speeds. Hence the delicate same-color same-speed direct contraction does not occur in this mixed operator package. The surviving same-color contractions have a genuine high-frequency speed gap, and the corresponding Volterra denominator is obtained directly from that gap. The only part of the mixed operator that has to be treated as a rough random operator is the extremely low-input, strong low–high core. The middle-frequency complement has a different nature: the input already carries positive Sobolev regularity, and the dyadic gain from that regularity is large enough to close the estimates deterministically.

1.2
The multispeed model

We consider the coupled system

L_{1}u_{1}=u_{1}u_{2}+\xi_{1},\qquad L_{2}u_{2}=u_{1}u_{2}+\xi_{2},\qquad L_{i}=\partial_{t}^{2}+\omega_{i}(D)^{2},

(1.1)

on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are independent real-valued space-time white noises and

\omega_{i}(n)=\bigl(1+c_{i}^{2}|n|^{2\alpha}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1,\qquad c_{i}>0,\qquad c_{1}\neq c_{2}.

(1.2)

The positivity of
c_{i}
is assumed so that
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
is exactly the high-frequency speed condition; equivalently one may write
|c_{1}|\neq|c_{2}|
.

The two colors should be read as two components of a coupled field or medium, each driven by its own microscopic fluctuation. Different propagation speeds are natural in multi-component wave models: they may represent different branches of a coupled medium, different characteristic velocities of interacting fields, or distinct wave families exchanging energy. Coupled Klein–Gordon systems have been used as models for energy exchange in two-component media, and the mathematical literature on different-speed Klein–Gordon systems shows that the separation of speeds changes the resonance geometry in a substantive way

Independent noises are also a natural first model for multi-source random forcing: different components are often driven by unrelated microscopic fluctuations, or by fluctuations that have been diagonalized at the covariance level. When the noise covariance and the leading speed matrix are simultaneously diagonal, the present color–speed bookkeeping is exactly the stochastic analogue of working in the component basis adapted to the propagation speeds.

The deterministic background comes from multispeed Klein–Gordon systems, where different propagation speeds alter the phase geometry in low–high interactions. This goes back to the normal-form viewpoint of Shatah
[

]
and to later analyses of coupled Klein–Gordon systems and different-speed resonance structures
[

. The elementary deterministic fact behind the stochastic mechanism is the following low–high phase comparison.

Fix
0<\theta<1
, assume
|\ell|\sim N\gg 1
, and let the low frequency satisfy
|q|\leq N^{\theta}
. Since

\omega_{i}(n)=c_{i}|n|^{\alpha}+O(|n|^{-\alpha})\qquad(|n|\to\infty),

(1.3)

and

|\ell+q|^{\alpha}=|\ell|^{\alpha}+O(N^{\alpha-1}|q|)=|\ell|^{\alpha}+O(N^{\alpha-1+\theta}),

(1.4)

one obtains, for
i\neq j
,

\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)=(c_{i}-c_{j})|\ell|^{\alpha}+O(N^{\alpha-1+\theta})+O(N^{-\alpha}).

(1.5)

The error is
o(N^{\alpha})
, while the leading coefficient
c_{i}-c_{j}
is nonzero. Thus the minus phase is uniformly of size
N^{\alpha}
in the strong low–high region:

|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\gtrsim N^{\alpha},\qquad i\neq j.

(1.6)

This estimate is the source of the extra Volterra denominator in the same-color contraction estimate.

The same-speed comparison is different. If the two speeds coincide, the leading
c_{i}|\ell|^{\alpha}
terms cancel and Taylor expansion gives

\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{i}(\ell)=c_{i}\alpha|\ell|^{\alpha-2}\ell\cdot q+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2})+O(N^{-\alpha-1}|q|).

(1.7)

Consequently

|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{i}(\ell)|\lesssim N^{\alpha-1}|q|+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2})+O(N^{-\alpha-1}|q|)\lesssim N^{\alpha-1+\theta},

(1.8)

which is
o(N^{\alpha})
for every
\theta<1
and may even vanish to first order, for instance when
q=0
or when
q
is nearly tangent to the sphere
|\ell|=N
. Thus the same-speed pure-difference branch does not provide a uniform high-frequency denominator of size
N^{-\alpha}
, and it is exactly the kind of branch for which parity-based symmetrization is a sharp and well adapted strategy. The multispeed
u_{1}u_{2}
coupling considered here is useful because the same-color contractions that survive in the mixed operators are never same-speed contractions.

1.4
Colored Wick contraction channels

There is a second, more probabilistic, way to state the mechanism. The stochastic expansion is not merely a list of formal products. It is a colored Wick expansion in the Gaussian Hilbert space generated by the two independent noises. The algebraic rule is the standard product formula for multiple Wiener integrals in an orthogonal direct sum of Gaussian Hilbert spaces; see, for example,
[
6
,
7
]
. What is used here is the resulting channel bookkeeping in the specific two-color, two-speed kernel. Thus every possible contraction must obey two rules at the same time:

(i)

a contraction can occur only between variables of the same color;

(ii)

after a same-color contraction, the remaining unpaired variables determine the chaos order and the color of the surviving stochastic object.

For example, in the resonant cubic block

V_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}=I_{2}(\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2})\circ\Psi_{1},

the product contains two color-
1
Gaussian variables and one color-
2
Gaussian variable. The color-
2
variable is independent of both color-
1
variables, so it cannot contract with them. The two color-
1
variables are not independent; their pairing produces a first-chaos term proportional to the remaining color-
2
stochastic convolution. Schematically,

\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2}\Psi_{1}=:\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2}\Psi_{1}:+\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{1}\Psi_{1}]\,\Psi_{2}.

This is not a deterministic Wick constant. It is a cut-out stochastic contraction, and its kernel still contains the oscillatory Klein–Gordon phases. Appendix
C
proves this decomposition at the Hilbert-kernel level and shows that the resulting first-chaos Volterra term is smoother than the centered third-chaos component.

This colored contraction-channel viewpoint is one of the useful structural features of the model. The novelty is not the Wick product formula itself, but its precise interaction with the coupling graph and the two-speed phase geometry. In the present cubic block the contraction lowers the chaos order without eliminating randomness: it leaves a first-chaos Volterra term in the remaining color. This suggests a useful bookkeeping principle for other multi-component Gaussian SPDEs: the interaction graph identifies which same-color pairings survive, while the dispersive phase geometry decides whether the surviving lower-chaos kernels are finite, divergent, or require further renormalization.

1.5
The color–speed separation principle

The preceding discussion can be summarized as an operator-level contraction principle.

Color–speed separation principle.

Consider two independently driven color components and the mixed random operators

T^{i;j,k}(w)=I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})\circ\Psi_{k}

in the strong low–high region
|q|\leq N^{\theta}
,
0<\theta<1
, with high frequency
|\ell|\sim N
. Their direct Gaussian contractions obey the following dichotomy:

\begin{array}[]{ll}j\neq k:&\text{the contraction is cross-color and vanishes by independence},\\[5.69054pt]
j=k,\ i\neq j:&\text{same-color contraction with different Duhamel and noise speeds}.\end{array}

In the second case the low–high phase has the speed gap

|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\gtrsim N^{\alpha},

and a Volterra integration by parts yields the denominator
N^{-\alpha}
. In the mixed algebra generated by the cross interaction
u_{1}u_{2}
, the configuration
i=j=k
does not occur among the direct contraction channels.

The point is algebraic as much as analytic. The coupling graph splits the four mixed blocks into same-speed cross-color blocks and same-color different-speed blocks. Thus the same-color same-speed pure-difference channel is not estimated away; it is absent from this operator package. This is the structural advantage of the model. The direct same-color contractions that remain are finite speed-separated Volterra multipliers, and the singular part of the random-operator theorem is concentrated in the extremely low input window.

This provides a framework complementary to the scalar same-speed theory. In the scalar case, the pure-difference phase is genuinely delicate and its treatment relies on the Fourier geometry of the phase, including symmetrization and Taylor-remainder bookkeeping. In the present cross-coupled multispeed model, the algebraic channel selection removes that branch from the mixed operator family. The denominator is supplied directly by

\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)\sim(c_{i}-c_{j})|\ell|^{\alpha},

in the surviving same-color channels.

The operator estimates still contain restrictions from Hilbert–Schmidt shell counting, the very-low input window, and output Sobolev weights; for instance the strong low–high theorem requires

s_{2}<4\alpha-3-\frac{3}{2}\theta-\varepsilon,\qquad 3-4\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)<0.

These are the summability conditions for the mixed random-operator package. They are not the same as the later deterministic Strichartz closure threshold.

First, the paper proves an unconditional mixed random-operator mechanism. In the four blocks

u_{1}u_{2}

1.7
Relation with scalar same-speed random operators

The comparison with the scalar same-speed theory is included to locate the mechanism. The random-operator construction of Gubinelli–Koch–Oh
[
4
]
is the closest methodological reference point: it organizes paracontrolled random operators into deterministic contractions and centered fluctuations, and treats the scalar same-speed pure-difference branch by a geometry-sensitive Fourier symmetrization.

The present paper identifies a different contraction architecture. In the mixed two-color, two-speed algebra generated by
u_{1}u_{2}
, the blocks with the same Duhamel and noise speed are cross-color and therefore centered, while the blocks with a same-color contraction have different speeds. Consequently the same-color same-speed branch handled in the scalar theory is not present in this mixed operator family. The surviving same-color contractions are controlled by a genuine low–high speed gap and become finite deterministic Volterra multipliers.

Thus the two mechanisms are naturally complementary. The scalar theory develops the correct cancellation for the same-speed pure-difference phase. The present work shows that in a cross-coupled multispeed system the interaction graph can select channels in which that phase is absent and the Volterra denominator comes directly from speed separation. This structural selection is the new point used throughout the mixed random-operator theorem.

Model, truncations, and main results

2.1
Equation and cutoff system

Define the Duhamel operators

I_{i}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\omega_{i}(D))}{\omega_{i}(D)}f(s)\,ds.

(2.1)

The stochastic enhanced objects are constructed from the zero-data stochastic convolutions. This convention is only a normalization of the random lift, not a restriction on deterministic Cauchy data. The cutoff equation may be read with prescribed deterministic Cauchy data,

L_{1}u_{1,N}=u_{1,N}u_{2,N}+\pi_{N}\xi_{1},\qquad L_{2}u_{2,N}=u_{1,N}u_{2,N}+\pi_{N}\xi_{2},

(2.2)

with the deterministic initial data propagated by the linear Klein–Gordon semigroup in the deterministic fixed point. In the conservative formulation of Section
7
, the affine semigroup term is placed in the regular component of the mild map. Thus the random lift remains zero-data, and no deterministic initial datum is added to the stochastic enhanced package. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, cutoff limits are taken with respect to this fixed Fourier projector family
\pi_{N}
; all canonical convergence statements below refer to this cutoff family. No counterterm is inserted in (
2.2
). The point of the paper is to show that the random-operator contractions generated by the paracontrolled expansion admit a finite deterministic-contraction/centered-fluctuation decomposition, with no divergent operator-level subtraction in the limiting equation.

2.2
Paraproduct and resonant-product notation

We use one fixed smooth Littlewood–Paley decomposition throughout the paper. With
\Delta_{N}
denoting a dyadic block and
S_{<N}
a low-frequency cutoff, the Bony products are written as

f<g:=\sum_{N}S_{<N/8}f\,\Delta_{N}g,\qquad f>g:=g<f,\qquad f\circ g:=\sum_{N\sim M}\Delta_{N}f\,\Delta_{M}g.

(2.3)

Thus
<
is the low–high paraproduct,
>
is the high–low paraproduct, and
\circ
is the resonant high–high product. The symbol
\circ
is never used for composition. When one factor is a stochastic distribution, such as in
V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}
or
I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})\circ\Psi_{k}
, the resonant product is interpreted through the finite-cutoff enhanced objects constructed below. The Fourier multiplier
\chi^{\circ}
appearing later is the smooth cutoff implementing the support condition
N\sim M
in the resonant product; it is part of the fixed Bony convention and should not be confused with an additional stochastic operation.

Remark 2.7

(Status and initial-data convention)
.

The unconditional stochastic part of the paper is Theorem
2.1
, Proposition
2.2
, Theorem
2.3
, and Proposition
2.4
. The deterministic linear input used in the conservative fixed point is supplied by Assumption
7.2
. The stochastic enhanced objects are zero-data objects, as in the standard construction of stochastic convolutions. Deterministic Cauchy data enter through the homogeneous semigroup in the mild fixed point, as in the usual mild formulation for semilinear wave or Klein–Gordon equations. They are not treated as part of the stochastic enhanced data. The present conservative theorem does not optimize deterministic initial-data regularity; it assumes enough regularity for the semigroup term to belong to the regular component used below.

Lemma 5.1

(Coefficient-level dyadic chaos criterion)
.

Let
Z
be a spatially homogeneous random distribution on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
whose dyadic block
P_{N}Z(t)
belongs to a fixed Wiener chaos of order at most
m
. Suppose that, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and
|n|\sim N
,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T}N^{p},

(5.1)

and that for some
0<\eta\leq 1
,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}(n,t)-\widehat{Z}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}\lesssim_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}N^{p+2\alpha\eta}.

(5.2)

Then for every
\kappa>0
and every
\gamma<\eta
there is an almost surely finite random constant
C_{\omega}
such that

\|P_{N}Z\|_{C^{\gamma}([0,T];L^{\infty}_{x})}\leq C_{\omega}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha\eta+\kappa}.

(5.3)

In particular, after choosing
\eta>0
sufficiently small,
Z\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
for every

s<-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{p}{2}.

(5.4)

The same statement applies to dyadic cutoff tails.

Proof.

For fixed
x
, the variance of
P_{N}Z(t,x)
is bounded by the sum of the coefficient variances on the shell, hence by
N^{3+p}
. Nelson hypercontractivity on a fixed Wiener chaos upgrades this to all finite
L^{r}(\Omega)
norms with the same power of
N
, up to constants depending on
r
and the chaos order. Applying the same estimate to a finite spatial net and to finitely many derivatives gives the dyadic
L^{\infty}_{x}
bound with an arbitrary
N^{\kappa}
loss. The time estimate follows from (
5.2
) in the same way, with the usual fractional KG cost
N^{\alpha\eta}
, and Kolmogorov plus Borel–Cantelli over dyadic
N
yields the almost sure estimate. Summability of
N^{s}\|P_{N}Z\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}
gives (
5.4
). Tail estimates are identical because at least one Fourier variable is restricted to the removed dyadic tail.
∎

be the low input frequency. We choose a smooth strong low–high cutoff
\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)
supported where
|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}
, and a resonant cutoff
\chi^{\circ}(a,b)
supported where
|a|\sim|b|
. The dyadic kernel of the strong region is then

A_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)=\sum_{\ell+r=n-q}\rho_{N}(\ell)\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)\chi^{\circ}(q+\ell,r)K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)\,\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\,\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r,t),

(6.4)

where

K_{i}(\tau,m)=\frac{\sin(\tau\omega_{i}(m))}{\omega_{i}(m)}.

Thus

\widehat{T_{N}^{i;j,k}w}(n,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{q}A_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)\widehat{w}(q,s)\,ds.

(6.5)

If
j=k
, define the deterministic diagonal contraction

D_{N}^{i;j}(q;t,s)=\sum_{\ell}\rho_{N}(\ell)\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)\chi^{\circ}(q+\ell,-\ell)K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)\sigma_{j}(\ell;s,t),

(6.6)

where

\sigma_{j}(\ell;s,t):=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(-\ell,t)\bigr].

(6.7)

The centered fluctuation kernel is then

B_{N}^{i;j,j}(n,q;t,s)=A_{N}^{i;j,j}(n,q;t,s)-\mathbf{1}_{\{n=q\}}D_{N}^{i;j}(q;t,s),

(6.8)

while for
j\neq k
we simply set
B_{N}^{i;j,k}=A_{N}^{i;j,k}
.

6.3
Centered fluctuation theorem

This subsection proves the pathwise convergence of the centered fluctuation kernels in the strong low–high region. We give the details because the estimate is the point at which the shell count

N^{6-8\alpha+2s_{2}+3\theta}

becomes an almost sure operator bound rather than only a fixed-time second moment estimate.

We measure the dyadic kernels in the Hilbert space

\|B_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n,q}\langle n\rangle^{2(s_{2}-\alpha)}\bigl|\widetilde{B}_{N}(t)(n,q,s)\bigr|^{2}ds,

(6.9)

where

\widetilde{B}_{N}(t)(n,q,s)=\mathbf{1}_{0\leq s\leq t}B_{N}(n,q;t,s).

(6.10)

The Hilbert–Schmidt step is

\|B_{N}w(t)\|_{H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\leq\|B_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}\,\|w\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}_{x})}\leq T^{1/2}\|B_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}\,\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}.

(6.11)

Thus it is enough to prove dyadic summability of the random Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

Lemma 6.1

(Elementary KG kernel and covariance bounds)
.

Let
|m|\sim N\geq 2
and
0<\eta\leq 1/2
. Then, uniformly for
0\leq u,v,t,t^{\prime}\leq T
,

\displaystyle|K_{i}(t,m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\alpha},

(6.12)

\displaystyle|K_{i}(t,m)-K_{i}(t^{\prime},m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}N^{-\alpha+\alpha\eta},

(6.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(m,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N^{-2\alpha},

(6.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(m,t)-\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(m,t^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}N^{-2\alpha+2\alpha\eta},

(6.15)

\displaystyle|\sigma_{i}(m;u,v)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N^{-2\alpha},

(6.16)

\displaystyle|\sigma_{i}(m;u,v)-\sigma_{i}(m;u,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}|v-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}N^{-2\alpha+\alpha\eta}.

(6.17)

Proof.

The bound (
6.12
) follows from
|\sin x|\leq 1
and
\omega_{i}(m)\sim N^{\alpha}
. For the increment, use

\left|\frac{\sin(t\omega)-\sin(t^{\prime}\omega)}{\omega}\right|\leq\min\{|t-t^{\prime}|,2\omega^{-1}\}\leq|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}\omega^{-1+\eta}.

This gives (
6.13
). The stochastic convolution estimates follow from Itô isometry. For example,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(m,t)|^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}|K_{i}(t-r,m)|^{2}dr\lesssim_{T}N^{-2\alpha}.

Writing the time increment as the sum of the common interval with an incremented kernel and the boundary interval
[t\wedge t^{\prime},t\vee t^{\prime}]
, one obtains (
6.15
); the boundary term is bounded by
|t-t^{\prime}|N^{-2\alpha}
and is absorbed because
\eta\leq 1/2
. The covariance estimates follow from Cauchy–Schwarz:

|\sigma_{i}(m;u,v)|\leq\bigl(\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(m,u)|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{i}(-m,v)|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2},

and similarly with one factor replaced by a time increment.
∎

Lemma 6.2

(Weighted output counting)
.

Let
\beta>-3
. Then, uniformly in dyadic
N
and
0<\theta<1
,

\sum_{|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}}\sum_{|n-q|\lesssim N}\langle n\rangle^{\beta}\lesssim N^{3\theta}N^{3+\beta}.

(6.18)

In particular, in the applications below
\beta=2(s_{2}-\alpha)>-3
.

Proof.

For each fixed
q
, translation by
q
does not change the order of the sum:

\sum_{|n-q|\lesssim N}\langle n\rangle^{\beta}\lesssim N^{3+\beta},

because
\beta>-3
. There are
O(N^{3\theta})
choices of
q
. This proves (
6.18
). Notice that this is a weighted lattice-sum estimate, not a pointwise replacement of
\langle n\rangle^{\beta}
by
N^{\beta}
. This distinction matters when
\beta<0
and high–high cancellation can produce low output.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Centered covariance bound)
.

On the strong low–high support, for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}|B_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)|^{2}\lesssim_{T}N^{3-6\alpha}.

(6.19)

Moreover, for every
0<\eta\leq 1/2
and
0\leq s\leq t\wedge t^{\prime}\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}|B_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)-B_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t^{\prime},s)|^{2}\lesssim_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}N^{3-6\alpha+2\eta\alpha}.

(6.20)

Proof.

Set

r_{\ell}=n-q-\ell,\qquad c_{\ell}(t,s;n,q)=\rho_{N}(\ell)\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)\chi^{\circ}(q+\ell,r_{\ell})K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell).

On the support of the cutoffs,

|q|\lesssim N^{\theta},\qquad|\ell|\sim N,\qquad|q+\ell|\sim|r_{\ell}|\sim N,\qquad|n-q|\lesssim N.

Thus
|c_{\ell}|\lesssim N^{-\alpha}
. For fixed
(n,q)
let

S_{N}(n,q)=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\rho_{N}(\ell)\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)\chi^{\circ}(q+\ell,n-q-\ell)\neq 0\}.

Then
\#S_{N}(n,q)\lesssim N^{3}
. We use the real-valued Fourier covariance convention

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(m,u)\widehat{\Psi}_{b}(m^{\prime},v)]=\mathbf{1}_{a=b}\mathbf{1}_{m+m^{\prime}=0}\sigma_{a}(m;u,v).

(6.21)

The zero mode and the finite exceptional modes are absorbed into the implicit constants.

Cross-color case.
If
j\neq k
, then
B_{N}^{i;j,k}=A_{N}^{i;j,k}
and the kernel is centered. Wick’s formula and independence give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|B_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in S_{N}(n,q)}c_{\ell}c_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell^{\prime},s)]\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r_{\ell},t)\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r_{\ell^{\prime}},t)].

The two Kronecker constraints leave only one free high-frequency variable. Using (
6.16
),

\mathbb{E}|B_{N}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\ell\in S_{N}(n,q)}N^{-2\alpha}N^{-2\alpha}N^{-2\alpha}\lesssim N^{3-6\alpha}.

Same-color case.
If
j=k
, then

A_{N}^{i;j,j}=\sum_{\ell\in S_{N}(n,q)}c_{\ell}(t,s;n,q)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(r_{\ell},t).

Its mean is nonzero only when
\ell+r_{\ell}=0
, equivalently
n=q
, and this is exactly the diagonal term subtracted in (
6.8
). Hence

B_{N}^{i;j,j}=A_{N}^{i;j,j}-\mathbb{E}A_{N}^{i;j,j}

is a centered second-chaos variable. For Gaussian variables
X_{\ell},Y_{\ell}
one has

\mathbb{E}[(X_{\ell}Y_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}X_{\ell}Y_{\ell})(X_{\ell^{\prime}}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}X_{\ell^{\prime}}Y_{\ell^{\prime}})]=\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}X_{\ell^{\prime}}]\mathbb{E}[Y_{\ell}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\mathbb{E}[X_{\ell}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}]\mathbb{E}[Y_{\ell}X_{\ell^{\prime}}].

Applying this with
X_{\ell}=\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)
and
Y_{\ell}=\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(r_{\ell},t)
gives two pairings. In each nonzero pairing, once
(n,q)
is fixed there is at most one free high-frequency variable. Each covariance has size
O(N^{-2\alpha})
and
|c_{\ell}c_{\ell^{\prime}}|\lesssim N^{-2\alpha}
, so

\mathbb{E}|B_{N}^{i;j,j}(n,q;t,s)|^{2}\lesssim N^{3-6\alpha}.

The important point is that one must apply the centered Wick formula before estimating. Estimating the deterministic subtraction separately by absolute values would introduce a spurious two-variable count.

Time increment.
For the stated pointwise increment we assume
s\leq t\wedge t^{\prime}
. Write, for instance,

A_{N}(t)-A_{N}(t^{\prime})=\sum_{\ell}(c_{\ell}(t,s)-c_{\ell}(t^{\prime},s))\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r_{\ell},t)+\sum_{\ell}c_{\ell}(t^{\prime},s)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)(\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r_{\ell},t)-\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r_{\ell},t^{\prime})).

The first term uses (
6.13
) in place of one kernel bound; the second uses (
6.15
) in place of one terminal covariance. The same Wick calculation as above gives the extra factor
|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}N^{2\eta\alpha}
. In the same-color case, do not estimate the subtraction separately. Instead use

B_{N}(t)-B_{N}(t^{\prime})=(A_{N}(t)-A_{N}(t^{\prime}))-\mathbb{E}[A_{N}(t)-A_{N}(t^{\prime})],

which is again a centered second-chaos variable. The centered Wick formula leaves one high-frequency variable and one increment factor. This proves (
6.20
).
∎

Proposition 6.4

(Hilbert–Schmidt moments and increments)
.

Let
X_{N}(t)=\widetilde{B}_{N}(t)
be the fixed-time Hilbert-valued kernel in (
6.9
), and set

a=6-8\alpha+2s_{2}+3\theta.

Then

\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{2}\lesssim_{T}N^{a},

(6.22)

and for every
0<\eta\leq 1/2
,

\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)-X_{N}(t^{\prime})\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{2}\lesssim_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}N^{a+2\alpha\eta}.

(6.23)

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
,

\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{n,q}\langle n\rangle^{2(s_{2}-\alpha)}\mathbb{E}|B_{N}(n,q;t,s)|^{2}\,ds.

The strong low–high support gives
|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}
and
|n-q|\lesssim N
. Applying Lemma
6.2
with
\beta=2(s_{2}-\alpha)
gives

\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{2}\lesssim_{T}N^{3-6\alpha}\,N^{3\theta}N^{3+2(s_{2}-\alpha)}=N^{a}.

For the increment, assume
t^{\prime}>t
. Then

X_{N}(t^{\prime})-X_{N}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{0\leq s\leq t}(B_{N}(t^{\prime},s)-B_{N}(t,s))+\mathbf{1}_{t<s\leq t^{\prime}}B_{N}(t^{\prime},s)=:Y_{1}+Y_{2}.

The term
Y_{1}
is controlled by (
6.20
) and the same weighted count. The boundary strip
Y_{2}
has
s
-measure
|t^{\prime}-t|
and is controlled by the fixed-time estimate (
6.19
); since
\eta\leq 1/2
, this is bounded by
|t^{\prime}-t|^{2\eta}N^{a+2\alpha\eta}
after increasing the constant. This proves (
6.23
).
∎

Lemma 6.5

(Hilbert-valued hypercontractivity)
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space and let
F
be an
H
-valued random variable whose scalar coordinates belong to a finite Wiener chaos of order at most two. Then, for every
p\geq 2
,

\big(\mathbb{E}\|F\|_{H}^{p}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim(p-1)\big(\mathbb{E}\|F\|_{H}^{2}\big)^{1/2}.

(6.24)

The same estimate holds for differences.

Proof.

This is the Hilbert-valued Nelson–Gross estimate. For finite-dimensional projections it follows from scalar hypercontractivity applied to all linear functionals; then one passes to the Hilbert norm by the standard vector-valued argument and removes the projection by monotone convergence. In the present application
X_{N}(t)
is finite Fourier cutoff and belongs to the second Wiener chaos after centering.
∎

Proposition 6.6

(Time supremum for the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel)
.

Fix
0<\eta\leq 1/2
, take
p>1/\eta
, and choose
\gamma
with
1/p<\gamma<\eta
. Then

\Big(\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p,\eta,T}N^{a/2+\alpha\eta}.

(6.25)

Consequently,

\big(\mathbb{E}\|B_{N}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}^{p}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p,\eta,T}N^{a/2+\alpha\eta}.

(6.26)

Proof.

Proposition
6.4
and Lemma
6.5
give

\|X_{N}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{H}_{N})}\lesssim_{p}N^{a/2},

and

\|X_{N}(t)-X_{N}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{H}_{N})}\lesssim_{p}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}N^{a/2+\alpha\eta}.

Using the fractional Sobolev embedding

W^{\gamma,p}([0,T];\mathfrak{H}_{N})\hookrightarrow C([0,T];\mathfrak{H}_{N}),\qquad\gamma p>1,

we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq T}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X_{N}(t)-X_{N}(t^{\prime})\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{N}}^{p}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{1+\gamma p}}\,dt\,dt^{\prime}.

The double integral is finite because
(\eta-\gamma)p>0
. This proves (
6.25
). The operator estimate follows from (
6.11
).
∎

Theorem 6.7

(Pathwise centered fluctuations)
.

Assume

a:=6-8\alpha+2s_{2}+3\theta<0.

(6.27)

Then the centered dyadic fluctuation series

B^{i;j,k}:=\sum_{N\ {\rm dyadic}}B_{N}^{i;j,k}

(6.28)

converges almost surely in

\mathcal{L}\bigl(E_{T},C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}\bigr).

(6.29)

More precisely, for every sufficiently small
\kappa>0
there is an almost surely finite random constant
C_{\omega}
such that

\|B_{N}^{i;j,k}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\leq C_{\omega}N^{a/2+\kappa}

(6.30)

for all dyadic
N
.

Proof.

Choose
0<\kappa<-a/2
. Pick
0<\eta\leq 1/2
so small that
\alpha\eta<\kappa/2
, and then choose
p>1/\eta
large. Proposition
6.6
gives

\big(\mathbb{E}\|B_{N}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}^{p}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p,\eta,T}N^{a/2+\alpha\eta}.

By Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\|B_{N}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}>N^{a/2+\kappa}\Big)\lesssim_{p,\eta,T}N^{-p(\kappa-\alpha\eta)}.

For dyadic
N=2^{m}
this probability is summable in
m
if
p
is chosen large. Borel–Cantelli gives the dyadic estimate (
6.30
) for all large dyadic
N
, and finitely many small shells are absorbed into
C_{\omega}
. Since
a/2+\kappa<0
, the dyadic series is absolutely summable in the Banach space
\mathcal{L}(E_{T},C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha})
.
∎

6.4
Same-color deterministic contractions

This subsection proves the deterministic part of the same-color blocks at the level of operator norm. This is the point where the color–speed separation mechanism enters most directly. The same-color expectation is not discarded; it converges to a finite deterministic Volterra multiplier. The proof below separates the covariance remainder, the leading covariance, the Volterra integration by parts, and the low-frequency cost coming from the input time derivative.

Recall that

\sigma_{j}(\ell;s,t)=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(-\ell,t)]=\frac{s}{2\omega_{j}(\ell)^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell))+\frac{\sin((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell))}{4\omega_{j}(\ell)^{3}}-\frac{\sin((t+s)\omega_{j}(\ell))}{4\omega_{j}(\ell)^{3}}.

(6.31)

We write

\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j,0}+\sigma_{j,r},\qquad\sigma_{j,0}(\ell;s,t):=\frac{s}{2\omega_{j}(\ell)^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell)).

(6.32)

If
|\ell|\sim N
, then

|\sigma_{j,r}(\ell;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}N^{-3\alpha},\qquad|\sigma_{j,0}(\ell;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}N^{-2\alpha}.

(6.33)

Thus the remainder part already has one extra factor
N^{-\alpha}
and may be estimated absolutely. The leading part has only
N^{-2\alpha}
and must use the different-speed Volterra phase.

Multiplying the leading covariance with the output Duhamel kernel produces the phases

\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)=\omega_{i}(q+\ell)\pm\omega_{j}(\ell).

(6.34)

Indeed,

\frac{\sin((t-s)\omega_{i}(q+\ell))}{\omega_{i}(q+\ell)}\cos((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell))=\frac{1}{2\omega_{i}(q+\ell)}\sum_{\pm}\sin((t-s)\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)).

Lemma 6.8

(Low–high speed gap and same-speed contrast)
.

Fix
0<\theta<1
. There exist constants
N_{0}
and
c_{*}>0
such that, whenever
N\geq N_{0}
,
|\ell|\sim N
, and
|q|\leq N^{\theta}
, the following estimates hold.

If
i\neq j
, then

|\omega_{i}(q+\ell)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\geq c_{*}N^{\alpha},

(6.35)

and

\omega_{i}(q+\ell)+\omega_{j}(\ell)\geq c_{*}N^{\alpha}.

(6.36)

More precisely, after fixing the dyadic annulus convention, the difference branch satisfies a two-sided estimate

c_{*}N^{\alpha}\leq|\omega_{i}(q+\ell)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\leq C_{*}N^{\alpha}.

(6.37)

In contrast, in the same-speed branch one only has the pure-difference expansion

\omega_{i}(q+\ell)-\omega_{i}(\ell)=c_{i}\alpha|\ell|^{\alpha-2}\ell\cdot q+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2})+O(N^{-\alpha-1}|q|),

(6.38)

and hence

|\omega_{i}(q+\ell)-\omega_{i}(\ell)|\lesssim N^{\alpha-1}|q|+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2})+O(N^{-\alpha-1}|q|)\lesssim N^{\alpha-1+\theta}.

(6.39)

Thus the same-speed difference is smaller than
N^{\alpha}
in the strong low–high regime and has no uniform lower bound of order
N^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

The precise dyadic-annulus statement, including the dependence of the constants on the annulus convention and on
|c_{i}-c_{j}|
, is proved in Appendix
A
; see Proposition
A.3
. The plus branch follows from positivity and the high-frequency lower bounds for the two speeds. The same-speed expansion and the estimate (
6.39
) are proved in Proposition
A.4
. The key point is that the different-speed difference has the non-vanishing main term
(c_{i}-c_{j})|\ell|^{\alpha}
, whereas this term cancels when the speeds are the same.
∎

Remark 6.9

(Where the extra denominator enters)
.

Before using oscillation, the leading same-color shell has size

N^{3}\cdot N^{-\alpha}\cdot N^{-2\alpha}=N^{3-3\alpha},

where the three factors are the high-frequency shell count, the output Duhamel kernel, and the leading covariance. Lemma
6.8
gives
|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}|\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
in the same-color mixed channels, and one Volterra integration by parts gives an additional
N^{-\alpha}
. Hence the leading shell becomes

N^{3}\cdot N^{-\alpha}\cdot N^{-2\alpha}\cdot N^{-\alpha}=N^{3-4\alpha}.

In the same-speed pure-difference branch there is no comparable uniform denominator; the phase may be as small as
O(N^{\alpha-1}|q|)
and can vanish at
q=0
. This is the precise low–high geometry behind the color–speed separation principle.

The next lemma is the exact form of the Volterra estimate used below. It is written for the input space

E_{T}=C_{T}L^{2}\cap C_{T}^{1}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon},\qquad\|w\|_{E_{T}}:=\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

Lemma 6.10

(Volterra multiplier on the low output window)
.

Let
|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}
and let
|\Phi(q)|\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
. For a Fourier mode
w_{q}(t)=\widehat{w}(q,t)
define

I_{q}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}sw_{q}(s)e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi(q)}\,ds.

Then

\sup_{t\leq T}|I_{q}(t)|\lesssim_{T}N^{-\alpha}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|w_{q}(s)|+\sup_{s\leq T}|\partial_{t}w_{q}(s)|\right).

(6.40)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}}\langle q\rangle^{2(s_{2}-\alpha)}\sup_{t\leq T}|I_{q}(t)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(6.41)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{T}N^{-\alpha}\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+N^{-\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

(6.42)

The same estimate holds with sine or cosine phases in place of the complex exponential.

Proof.

For fixed
q
and
\Phi=\Phi(q)
, integrate by parts:

\displaystyle I_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}sw_{q}(s)e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{tw_{q}(t)}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(w_{q}(s)+s\partial_{s}w_{q}(s)\bigr)e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}\,ds.

There is no lower endpoint boundary term because of the factor
s
in the leading covariance. Since
|\Phi|^{-1}\lesssim N^{-\alpha}
, this proves (
6.40
).

We then take the weighted
\ell^{2}_{q}
norm on the low window. The
w
term is controlled by
\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}
because the parameter range (
2.10
) implies
s_{2}<\alpha
; indeed
s_{2}<4\alpha-3\leq\alpha
for
\alpha\leq 1
, and the losses make the inequality strict. For the time derivative, use

\langle q\rangle^{s_{2}-\alpha}=\langle q\rangle^{s_{2}+\varepsilon}\langle q\rangle^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}\lesssim N^{\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\langle q\rangle^{-\alpha-\varepsilon},\qquad|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}.

This gives (
6.41
).
∎

(Same-color deterministic contraction)

Assume (

\|\mathcal{D}_{N}^{i;j}w\|_{C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\lesssim_{T}N^{3-4\alpha}\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+N^{3-4\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

Consequently

\mathcal{D}^{i;j}:=\sum_{N\ {\rm dyadic}}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{i;j}

converges in
\mathcal{L}(E_{T},C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha})
. Its limit is a finite deterministic Volterra multiplier retained in the enhanced random-operator package.

Proof.

We split the covariance according to (
6.32
). Let
\mathcal{D}_{N,r}^{i;j}
be the part generated by
\sigma_{j,r}
. On the strong low–high support,

|q|\lesssim N^{\theta},\qquad|\ell|\sim N,\qquad|q+\ell|\sim N,

and therefore

|K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)|\lesssim N^{-\alpha},\qquad|\sigma_{j,r}(\ell;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}N^{-3\alpha}.

For each fixed
q
there are
O(N^{3})
admissible high frequencies
\ell
. Hence

|D_{N,r}^{i;j}(q;t,s)|\lesssim_{T}N^{3}N^{-\alpha}N^{-3\alpha}=N^{3-4\alpha}.

(6.46)

Since
s_{2}-\alpha<0
and the contraction is diagonal in the output variable
q
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{N,r}^{i;j}w\|_{C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N^{3-4\alpha}\left(\sum_{|q|\lesssim N^{\theta}}\langle q\rangle^{2(s_{2}-\alpha)}\sup_{s\leq T}|\widehat{w}(q,s)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(6.47)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}N^{3-4\alpha}\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}.

(6.48)

Thus all lower-order covariance terms already have the desired summable shell size.

It remains to estimate the leading part
\mathcal{D}_{N,0}^{i;j}
. Inserting
\sigma_{j,0}
gives

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{N,0}^{i;j}w)_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\rho_{N}(\ell)\chi_{<}^{\theta}(q,\ell)\chi^{\circ}(q+\ell,-\ell)

(6.49)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)\frac{s}{2\omega_{j}(\ell)^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell))\widehat{w}(q,s)\,ds.

(6.50)

By the product-to-sum identity, each summand is a finite sum of sign branches with phases
\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)
. Lemma
6.8
gives

|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)|\gtrsim N^{\alpha}

for all such branches when
i\neq j
. Apart from the Volterra integral, the static amplitude is

\frac{1}{\omega_{i}(q+\ell)\omega_{j}(\ell)^{2}}\lesssim N^{-3\alpha}.

(6.51)

Applying Lemma
6.10
to the amplitude
s\widehat{w}(q,s)
yields, after summing the
O(N^{3})
admissible
\ell
’s,

\|\mathcal{D}_{N,0}^{i;j}w\|_{C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\lesssim_{T}N^{3}N^{-3\alpha}\left(N^{-\alpha}\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+N^{-\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}}\right).

(6.52)

This is exactly

\|\mathcal{D}_{N,0}^{i;j}w\|_{C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\lesssim_{T}N^{3-4\alpha}\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+N^{3-4\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\alpha-\varepsilon}}.

Combining this with (
6.47
) proves (
6.45
).

Finally, the dyadic convergence follows from the assumptions

3-4\alpha<0,\qquad 3-4\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)<0.

For
M_{2}>M_{1}
,

\left\|\sum_{M_{1}<N\leq M_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{i;j}\right\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}\lesssim_{T}\sum_{M_{1}<N\leq M_{2}}\left(N^{3-4\alpha}+N^{3-4\alpha+\theta(s_{2}+\varepsilon)}\right),

and the right hand side tends to zero as
M_{1}\to\infty
. Finitely many low-frequency shells are finite-dimensional Volterra operators and are absorbed into the constant.
∎

6.5
The full operator and the positive-input complement

The estimates above construct the genuinely critical strong low–high core. This is the only part of the mixed operator that has to be controlled on the rough energy input space
E_{T}
. We now explain how the rest of the mixed operator is incorporated. The point is not to introduce new random symbols, nor to repeat the rough random-operator argument in every dyadic region. Away from the ultra-low input window the full input has positive Sobolev regularity, and the corresponding
Q^{-\sigma}
gain closes the middle-frequency and nonresonant terms.

Define

E_{T}^{\sigma}:=E_{T}\cap C_{T}H^{\sigma},\qquad\|w\|_{E_{T}^{\sigma}}:=\|w\|_{E_{T}}+\|w\|_{C_{T}H^{\sigma}}.

(6.53)

At the cutoff level we write

T_{N}^{i;j,k}=\mathcal{T}_{N,\mathrm{crit}}^{i;j,k}+\mathcal{T}_{N,\mathrm{comp}}^{i;j,k},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{N,\mathrm{crit}}^{i;j,k}=\mathbf{1}_{j=k}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{i;j}+\mathcal{B}_{N,\mathrm{sLH}}^{i;j,k},

(6.54)

where the first term is supported on
|q|\leq N^{\theta}
. The complementary operator is simply the remaining dyadic part of the same cutoff operator. We do not split it into additional enhanced components. Same-color deterministic diagonals that occur outside the strong window are included in this complementary operator; they are finite Volterra pieces and are controlled together with the other complementary low–high terms. This convention keeps the enhanced data aligned with the real difficulty: only the strong low–high core is a rough-input random operator.

The critical estimate already proved is

\|\mathcal{B}^{i;j,k}_{\mathrm{sLH}}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}+\mathbf{1}_{j=k}\|\mathcal{D}^{i;j}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}<\infty.

(6.55)

The full operator theorem is the following concise form of Proposition
2.4
.

Theorem 6.12

(Full mixed operators: critical core and positive-input completion)
.

Assume (
2.10
) and (
2.13
). Then the limits of (
6.54
) exist almost surely and

\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{crit}}^{i;j,k}\|_{E_{T}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}+\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{i;j,k}\|_{E_{T}^{\sigma}\to C_{T}H^{s_{2}-\alpha}}<\infty.

(6.56)

Cross-color blocks have no deterministic contraction. Same-color blocks have finite speed-separated Volterra contractions and centered fluctuations. No divergent random-operator counterterm is subtracted; the finite Volterra multipliers are retained in the limiting enhanced data.

6.6
Positive-input completion of the complementary operator

We now prove the complementary estimate used in Theorem
6.12
. This part is not another rough-input random-operator theorem. It is the dyadic completion away from the extremely low input window, and its basic gain is the positive Sobolev factor

\|P_{Q}w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\leq Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{C_{T}H^{\sigma}}.

(6.57)

Here
N
denotes the stochastic high scale,

the input scale, and

the output scale. We use fixed smooth Littlewood–Paley and resonant cutoffs and write schematically

T^{i;j,k}_{N,Q,M}w:=P_{M}\Big(I_{i}\big((P_{Q}w)<P_{N}\Psi_{j}\big)\circ P_{N}\Psi_{k}\Big),

The dyadic stochastic bounds used below are consequences of Lemma

\|P_{N}\Psi_{i}\|_{C_{T}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\omega}N^{3/2-\alpha+},\qquad\|P_{N}\Psi_{i}\|_{C_{T}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim_{\omega}N^{3/2-\alpha+}.

(6.60)

The estimates below are deliberately crude. Their role is only to show that once

Lemma 6.13

(Output-comparable complement)
.

Assume

and

N

Proof.

The input gain gives

∎

Lemma 6.14

(High–high low-output complement)
.

In the complementary high–high low-output region

7
Conditional deterministic solution map from enhanced data

This section is deliberately conditional. The stochastic lift, colored Wick symbols, and mixed random-operator package have already been constructed in the preceding sections. Here we prove only the deterministic map from these enhanced data to a paracontrolled solution on
[0,T]
, assuming the analytic Strichartz input stated below. No result in this section is used to prove the random-operator theorem.

The stochastic enhanced objects are zero-data objects. Deterministic Cauchy data enter through the homogeneous Klein–Gordon semigroup in the mild formulation. They are not added to the stochastic enhanced data.

We use the Duhamel operator
I_{i}
already defined in (
2.1
). The homogeneous semigroup is

S_{i}(t)(f,g)=\cos(t\omega_{i}(D))f+\frac{\sin(t\omega_{i}(D))}{\omega_{i}(D)}g.

(7.1)

In the conservative theorem we prescribe

(y_{i,0},y_{i,1})\in H^{s_{2}}\times H^{s_{2}-\alpha},\qquad i=1,2,

(7.2)

and place these data in the affine part of the
Y
component:

X_{i}(0)=\partial_{t}X_{i}(0)=0,\qquad(Y_{i}(0),\partial_{t}Y_{i}(0))=(y_{i,0},y_{i,1}).

(7.3)

Thus the full input is always

W_{i}=V_{i}+X_{i}+Y_{i}.

(7.4)

The affine term
S_{i}(t)(y_{i,0},y_{i,1})
sits outside the Duhamel integral in the fixed-point map. The theorem is deliberately conservative in the deterministic data: it assumes the semigroup term belongs to the regular
Y_{T}^{s_{2}}
component, and it does not introduce a separate initial-data stochastic symbol.

7.1
Analytic Strichartz input and literature status

The deterministic closure below uses only local-in-time Strichartz estimates for the diagonal propagators associated with

L_{i}=\partial_{t}^{2}+\omega_{i}(D)^{2},\qquad\omega_{i}(n)=(1+c_{i}^{2}|n|^{2\alpha})^{1/2}.

The stochastic estimates of the preceding sections are independent of this subsection. The present input is used only to close the deterministic map from enhanced data. We record the input as an assumption in the exact form needed below; the comments on Dinh’s fractional-wave estimates and the compact wave/Klein–Gordon estimates indicate standard sources for this assumption, but the deterministic theorem is stated conditional on the package as written.

We use one of the following two parameter choices.

Case F: the subunit fractional case.

If

, we choose a fractional-wave admissible non-endpoint pair

(7.5)

(7.6)

For the

(p_{Y},q_{Y})=(4,4)
and

\kappa_{Y}=\frac{3-\alpha}{4}.

(7.7)

This is the compact fractional-wave regime covered by Dinh’s fractional-wave Strichartz estimates
[
16
]
. In dimension three the derivative exponent is exactly

\kappa_{\alpha}(p,q)=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{p},

for fractional-admissible pairs

in the subunit range

. The near-

choice is used only to close the deterministic

Case W: the classical endpoint.

If

In either case the function spaces are

\displaystyle\|X\|_{X_{T}^{s_{1}}}

Lemma 7.1

(Dyadic transfer to the massive speed multipliers)
.

For each fixed
c_{i}>0
, the local dyadic Strichartz estimates for the fractional wave propagator with phase
c_{i}|D|^{\alpha}
transfer to the propagators
e^{\pm it\omega_{i}(D)}
, with the same derivative exponents. The constants may depend on
c_{i}
and on the compact time interval, but not on the speed gap
|c_{1}-c_{2}|^{-1}
.

Proof.

On a high-frequency shell
|n|\sim N\gg 1

\omega_{i}(n)=c_{i}|n|^{\alpha}+O(N^{-\alpha}).

After rescaling the shell to unit size, the derivatives of the error symbol obey the corresponding order-zero dyadic bounds. Thus

e^{it\omega_{i}(D)}P_{N}=e^{itc_{i}|D|^{\alpha}}A_{i,N}(t,D),\qquad A_{i,N}(t,n)=e^{it(\omega_{i}(n)-c_{i}|n|^{\alpha})}P_{N}(n),

where
A_{i,N}(t,D)
is a uniformly bounded order-zero Fourier multiplier on every fixed local time interval. The constant
c_{i}
only rescales the principal phase and does not change the derivative exponent. The finitely many low-frequency modes are finite dimensional and are absorbed into the energy estimate. Hence the Strichartz exponents are unchanged. The constants may depend on
c_{i}
, but not on
|c_{1}-c_{2}|^{-1}
; the latter enters only in the stochastic speed-gap estimates.
∎

8
Conclusion and outlook

The paper proves a color–speed separation principle for the mixed random operators arising in a two-component multispeed fractional stochastic Klein–Gordon system. The conclusion is an operator-level contraction statement. The four mixed blocks are arranged by the coupling graph into two families: same-speed blocks are cross-color and hence centered by independence, while same-color blocks are different-speed and therefore carry a genuine phase gap. Thus the same-color same-speed contraction is not estimated away; it is absent from this mixed operator algebra.

This places the result next to the scalar same-speed random-operator theory rather than inside it. The scalar pure-difference channel requires a geometry-sensitive symmetrization; the mixed channel considered here has a different contraction graph, and the denominator comes instead from

\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)\sim(c_{i}-c_{j})|\ell|^{\alpha},\qquad i\neq j.

The shell count

N^{3}\cdot N^{-\alpha}\cdot N^{-2\alpha}\cdot N^{-\alpha}=N^{3-4\alpha}

identifies
\alpha=3/4
as the natural critical exponent of the mixed random-operator algebra.

The resonant cubic analysis gives a corresponding colored Wick contraction-channel decomposition. The Gaussian algebra used here is standard: it is the multiple-Wiener-integral product formula applied in the orthogonal sum of the two noise Hilbert spaces. The contribution is the model-specific channel identification and the phase estimate for the resulting lower-chaos kernel. In the block
V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}
, the two same-color Gaussian variables are non-independent and therefore produce an explicit first-chaos contraction, whereas the opposite-color variable is independent and remains unpaired. This gives the exact decomposition

V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}=\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}+C_{i},

where
\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}
is centered third chaos and
C_{i}
is a centered first-chaos Volterra term. The proof is not an independence estimate for
V_{3-i}
and
\Psi_{i}
; it is a Hilbert-kernel Wick projection in a colored Gaussian space.

The conditional deterministic theorem uses the full-input split: on
[0,T]
the low–high input is
W_{i}=V_{i}+X_{i}+Y_{i}
, where the deterministic Cauchy data are carried by the homogeneous semigroup inside
Y_{i}
. In this formulation the only rough random-operator input is the strong low–high core; the middle-frequency complement is paid for by positive regularity of the full input. The first Picard smoothing places
V_{i}
in the random-operator input space and gives positive regularity; the resonant cubic products
V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}
are controlled by the refined Wick analysis; and
V_{1}V_{2}
is then an ordinary product in the conservative range
\alpha>12/13
. The required stochastic pieces are supplied by the first Picard and cubic estimates; the remaining analytic input is the compact fractional-wave/Klein–Gordon Strichartz package specified in Section
7
. In the subunit fractional case the deterministic space places
X
in a near-endpoint fractional-admissible pair
(p_{X},q_{X})\approx(4,3)
; at
\alpha=1
the fixed wave-admissible pair of Section
7
is used.

The same bookkeeping is relevant for multi-source systems. In an
M
-component model, the interaction graph determines which same-color contractions can occur, while the leading speed matrix determines whether those contractions are same-speed or speed-separated. Physically motivated coupled systems often have separate fluctuation sources and different characteristic speeds; if the covariance matrix and the leading speed matrix are simultaneously diagonalizable, the present proof applies after choosing the corresponding color basis. If they are not simultaneously diagonalizable, cross-color contractions reappear and one should combine the color–speed argument with a matrix-valued resonance analysis.

A primitive formulation remains a possible route toward lower full-SPDE thresholds. The present conservative proof already uses first Picard smoothing and refined cubic estimates. A deeper lift, in which one constructs Duhamel primitives of the remaining stochastic forcing before closing the deterministic map, would require additional multilinear phase estimates and could potentially bring the conditional local theory closer to the operator-level threshold
\alpha>3/4
.

Appendix A
A rigorous proof of the low–high phase gap

This appendix records the exact estimate behind Lemma
6.8
. The purpose is to make explicit that the phase lower bound used in the same-color deterministic contraction is a genuinely high-frequency, strong low–high, different-speed statement.

We fix constants

0<a_{0}<a_{1}<\infty,\qquad A\geq 1,\qquad 0<\theta<1,

and interpret the dyadic notation
|\ell|\sim N
,
|q|\leq N^{\theta}
in the precise form

a_{0}N\leq|\ell|\leq a_{1}N,\qquad|q|\leq AN^{\theta}.

(A.1)

All constants in this appendix may depend on
a_{0},a_{1},A,\theta,\alpha,c_{i},c_{j}
, but not on
N,\ell,q
.

Lemma A.1

(High-frequency expansion)
.

Let
c>0
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and

\omega_{c}(x)=(1+c^{2}|x|^{2\alpha})^{1/2}.

For
|x|\geq 1
,

\omega_{c}(x)=c|x|^{\alpha}+R_{c}(x),\qquad|R_{c}(x)|\leq c^{-1}|x|^{-\alpha}.

(A.2)

Proof.

The identity

\omega_{c}(x)-c|x|^{\alpha}=\frac{\omega_{c}(x)^{2}-c^{2}|x|^{2\alpha}}{\omega_{c}(x)+c|x|^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{\omega_{c}(x)+c|x|^{\alpha}}

gives
0\leq\omega_{c}(x)-c|x|^{\alpha}\leq c^{-1}|x|^{-\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Low-frequency perturbation of the radius)
.

Assume (
A.1
). For all sufficiently large
N
,

|\ell+tq|\geq\frac{a_{0}}{2}N,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,

(A.3)

and

\bigl||\ell+q|^{\alpha}-|\ell|^{\alpha}\bigr|\leq CN^{\alpha-1}|q|\leq CAN^{\alpha-1+\theta}=o(N^{\alpha}).

(A.4)

Proof.

Since
\theta<1
, for large
N
one has
AN^{\theta}\leq a_{0}N/2
. Hence

|\ell+tq|\geq|\ell|-|q|\geq a_{0}N-AN^{\theta}\geq\frac{a_{0}}{2}N.

On this segment,
x\mapsto|x|^{\alpha}
is smooth and

|\nabla|x|^{\alpha}|=\alpha|x|^{\alpha-1}\leq CN^{\alpha-1}.

The mean-value theorem gives (
A.4
).
∎

Proposition A.3

(Different-speed low–high phase gap)
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
c_{i},c_{j}>0
, and
c_{i}\neq c_{j}
. If (
A.1
) holds, then there are
N_{0}\geq 1
and constants
c_{*},C_{*}>0
such that, for all
N\geq N_{0}
,

c_{*}N^{\alpha}\leq|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\leq C_{*}N^{\alpha}.

(A.5)

Moreover,

\omega_{i}(\ell+q)+\omega_{j}(\ell)\geq c_{*}N^{\alpha}.

(A.6)

The lower-bound constant degenerates as
|c_{i}-c_{j}|\to 0
. Finitely many shells
N<N_{0}
are finite-dimensional and are absorbed into the low-frequency constants of the operator estimates.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)

\displaystyle=c_{i}|\ell+q|^{\alpha}-c_{j}|\ell|^{\alpha}+O(N^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=(c_{i}-c_{j})|\ell|^{\alpha}+c_{i}\bigl(|\ell+q|^{\alpha}-|\ell|^{\alpha}\bigr)+O(N^{-\alpha}).

(A.7)

Therefore

|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\geq|c_{i}-c_{j}|\,|\ell|^{\alpha}-CN^{\alpha-1+\theta}-CN^{-\alpha}.

Using
|\ell|\geq a_{0}N
gives

|\omega_{i}(\ell+q)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\geq|c_{i}-c_{j}|a_{0}^{\alpha}N^{\alpha}-CN^{\alpha-1+\theta}-CN^{-\alpha}.

Since

N^{\alpha-1+\theta}=N^{\alpha}N^{-(1-\theta)}=o(N^{\alpha}),\qquad N^{-\alpha}=N^{\alpha}N^{-2\alpha}=o(N^{\alpha}),

we may choose
N_{0}
so large that the last two terms are at most

\frac{1}{2}|c_{i}-c_{j}|a_{0}^{\alpha}N^{\alpha}.

This yields the lower bound in (
A.5
). The upper bound follows from
|\ell+q|\lesssim N
,
|\ell|\lesssim N
, and the high-frequency expansion. Finally, (
A.6
) follows from positivity of the speeds and
|\ell+q|\sim|\ell|\sim N
for
N
large.
∎

Proposition A.4

(Same-speed pure difference)
.

Let
\omega=\omega_{c}
with
c>0
. Under the same dyadic and low-frequency assumptions,

|\omega(\ell+q)-\omega(\ell)|\leq CN^{\alpha-1}|q|\leq CN^{\alpha-1+\theta}=o(N^{\alpha}).

(A.8)

More precisely,

\omega(\ell+q)-\omega(\ell)=\nabla\omega(\ell)\cdot q+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2}),\qquad|\nabla\omega(\ell)|\lesssim N^{\alpha-1}.

(A.9)

Equivalently, using the high-frequency expansion,

\omega(\ell+q)-\omega(\ell)=c\alpha|\ell|^{\alpha-2}\ell\cdot q+O(N^{\alpha-2}|q|^{2})+O(N^{-\alpha-1}|q|).

(A.10)

Proof.

Differentiating

\omega_{c}(x)=(1+c^{2}|x|^{2\alpha})^{1/2}

gives

\nabla\omega_{c}(x)=\frac{\alpha c^{2}|x|^{2\alpha-2}x}{(1+c^{2}|x|^{2\alpha})^{1/2}}.

On the segment
\ell+tq
, Lemma
A.2
gives
|\ell+tq|\sim N
, so

|\nabla\omega_{c}(\ell+tq)|\lesssim N^{\alpha-1}.

The mean-value theorem yields (
A.8
). A direct computation also gives
|\nabla^{2}\omega_{c}(x)|\lesssim N^{\alpha-2}
for
|x|\sim N
; Taylor’s theorem gives (
A.9
). Formula (
A.10
) follows by differentiating the high-frequency expansion (
A.2
). In particular, for
q=0
the same-speed phase is exactly zero, and for nearly tangential
q
the leading linear term may be much smaller than
N^{\alpha-1}|q|
.
∎

Remark A.5

(Plus branch and the Volterra denominator)
.

In the deterministic same-color contraction, the covariance leading term and the output Duhamel kernel generate

\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)=\omega_{i}(\ell+q)\pm\omega_{j}(\ell).

For
i\neq j
, Proposition
A.3
gives

|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)|\gtrsim N^{\alpha}

in the strong low–high region. Therefore one Volterra integration by parts supplies

|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}(q,\ell)|^{-1}\lesssim N^{-\alpha}.

This is exactly the last factor in the contraction count

\underbrace{N^{3}}_{\text{high shell count}}\,\underbrace{N^{-\alpha}}_{\text{Duhamel kernel}}\,\underbrace{N^{-2\alpha}}_{\text{same-color covariance}}\,\underbrace{N^{-\alpha}}_{\text{speed-gap Volterra denominator}}=N^{3-4\alpha}.

For the same-speed pure-difference branch, Proposition
A.4
shows that the minus phase has no comparable uniform lower bound; it can even vanish when
q=0
.

Remark A.6

(Scope of the estimate)
.

The estimate above has three restrictions. First, the high-frequency speeds must be distinct; since
\omega_{i}
contains
c_{i}^{2}
, the condition is
|c_{i}|\neq|c_{j}|
unless one assumes
c_{i},c_{j}>0
. Second, the proof uses the strong low–high condition
|q|/|\ell|\to 0
. In balanced-output or full resonant source regions with
|q|\sim N
, the relation
c_{i}|\ell+q|^{\alpha}\approx c_{j}|\ell|^{\alpha}
can occur, so the uniform lower bound is no longer automatic. Third, the constant

degenerates as
c_{i}\to c_{j}
. These restrictions are exactly those used in the same-color random-operator contraction. Balanced cubic source sectors cannot be controlled by this pointwise low–high lemma; in Appendix
C
they are instead treated by a phase-layer count for the exact factor
\min\{1,|\Omega|^{-1}\}
.

Appendix B
First Picard smoothing from the three-frequency phase geometry

This appendix proves the first Picard smoothing estimate used in the full-input formulation. The object is

V_{i}=I_{i}(\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2}),\qquad I_{i}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(t-s,D)f(s)\,ds,

where

K_{a}(t,n)=\frac{\sin(t\omega_{a}(n))}{\omega_{a}(n)},\qquad\omega_{a}(n)=\bigl(1+c_{a}^{2}|n|^{2\alpha}\bigr)^{1/2}.

Throughout this appendix

\frac{3}{4}<\alpha\leq 1,\qquad c_{1},c_{2}>0,\qquad c_{1}\neq c_{2}.

The two noises are independent. Hence
\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2}
is already the cross Wick product; no scalar Wick constant is subtracted at this level.

We use the Japanese bracket
\langle n\rangle=(1+|n|^{2})^{1/2}
. All dyadic variables are at least one. Constants may depend on
T,\alpha,c_{1},c_{2}
, the fixed smooth cutoffs and the dyadic convention, but never on the Fourier cutoff. The notation
A\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B
allows a loss by an arbitrary power
N^{\varepsilon}
in the relevant dyadic scale. Finitely many low shells are always absorbed into the constants.

B.1
Statement of the closed estimate

Let
\pi_{\Lambda}
be the Fourier cutoff to
|n|\leq\Lambda
, and define

\widehat{\Psi}_{a,\Lambda}(m,t)=\mathbf{1}_{|m|\leq\Lambda}\int_{0}^{t}K_{a}(t-s,m)\,d\beta^{a}_{m}(s),\qquad V_{i,\Lambda}=I_{i}(\Psi_{1,\Lambda}\Psi_{2,\Lambda}).

The cutoff is used only to justify algebraic manipulations. The estimates below are uniform in
\Lambda
.

Theorem B.1

(First Picard smoothing)
.

For every
T<\infty
, every
\varepsilon>0
, and
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon},

(B.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\partial_{t}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(B.2)

Moreover, for sufficiently small
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\alpha)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)-\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{3-7\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon},

(B.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\partial_{t}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)-\partial_{t}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(B.4)

The cutoff approximations
V_{i,\Lambda}
converge almost surely and canonically in
C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
for every

s<\frac{7\alpha}{2}-3,

and
\partial_{t}V_{i,\Lambda}
converge almost surely and canonically in
C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
for every

s<\frac{5\alpha}{2}-3.

Consequently

V_{i}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}-3-}),\qquad\partial_{t}V_{i}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{\frac{5\alpha}{2}-3-}).

B.2
A coefficient-to-Besov path criterion

The following criterion is used only in fixed finite chaoses. It is stated with the precise powers needed below.

Lemma B.2

(Coefficient-to-Besov path criterion)
.

Let
Z_{\Lambda}
be spatially homogeneous random distributions on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, uniformly belonging to a finite sum of homogeneous Wiener chaoses of order at most
m
. Suppose that, for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
, some
0<\eta\leq 1/2
, and every small
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\Lambda}\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{p+\varepsilon},

(B.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Lambda}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(n,t)-\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{p+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(B.6)

Then for every
s<-3/2-p/2
, after reducing
\eta
and
\varepsilon
if necessary, there is an almost surely finite random constant
C_{\omega}
, independent of
\Lambda
, such that for all dyadic
N
,

\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}\|_{C([0,T];L^{\infty}_{x})}\leq C_{\omega}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\kappa}

for every
\kappa>0
. In particular,
Z_{\Lambda}
is uniformly bounded in
C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s})
.

If the same estimates hold for cutoff differences, with the corresponding dyadic coefficient majorants tending to zero in the weighted summation

\sum_{N}N^{2s+3}\sup_{|n|\sim N}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{(Z_{\Lambda^{\prime}}-Z_{\Lambda})}(n,t)|^{2},

and similarly for increments, then
Z_{\Lambda}
is almost surely Cauchy in
C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s})
.

Proof.

We give the random-block argument explicitly, since the conclusion is not a deterministic Sobolev embedding applied to a random
L^{2}_{x}
bound. Fix a dyadic shell
|n|\sim N
. Spatial homogeneity gives diagonal covariance for a dyadic block; equivalently,

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(n,t)\overline{\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(m,t)}]=0\qquad\text{unless }n=m.

Therefore, for each fixed
(t,x)
,

\mathbb{E}|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t,x)|^{2}=\sum_{|n|\sim N}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim N^{3+p+\varepsilon}.

Since
P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t,x)
belongs to a fixed finite sum of Wiener chaoses, Nelson hypercontractivity gives, for every finite
r\geq 2
,

\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t,x)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{r,m,\varepsilon}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\varepsilon},\qquad x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{N}
be an
N^{-A}
-mesh of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, with
A
fixed large. Since

\#\mathcal{G}_{N}\lesssim N^{3A}
, the
L^{r}
-bound for the maximum over the mesh gives

\Big\|\max_{x\in\mathcal{G}_{N}}|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t,x)|\Big\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{r,m,A,\varepsilon}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\varepsilon+3A/r}.

Choosing
r
large makes
3A/r
an arbitrary small dyadic loss. To pass from the mesh to the full torus, use the Bernstein inequality

\|\nabla P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim N\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

If
A>2
, then the oscillation on each mesh cell is bounded by

N^{1-A}\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
, and this term is absorbed into the left hand side. Thus

\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}(t)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{\infty}_{x})}\lesssim_{r,m,\varepsilon,\kappa}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\kappa}

for every
\kappa>0
, after taking
r
large and
\varepsilon
small.

The same argument applied to
P_{N}(Z_{\Lambda}(t)-Z_{\Lambda}(t^{\prime}))
, using (
B.6
), gives

\|P_{N}(Z_{\Lambda}(t)-Z_{\Lambda}(t^{\prime}))\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{\infty}_{x})}\lesssim_{r,m,\varepsilon,\eta,\kappa}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha\eta+\kappa}.

Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion in time, or equivalently the fractional Sobolev embedding in time after choosing
r
large, yields

\|P_{N}Z_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{\infty}_{x}))}\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\kappa}.

Markov’s inequality and Borel–Cantelli over dyadic
N
give the almost sure dyadic bound with a random constant independent of
\Lambda
. The Besov summability condition is

\sum_{N}N^{s}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{2}+\kappa}<\infty,

which holds whenever
s<-3/2-p/2
, after choosing
\kappa>0
sufficiently small. The Cauchy statement follows by applying the same argument to cutoff differences and using the assumed vanishing deterministic tail majorants.
∎

B.3
Stochastic Fubini and the three propagators

For
a=0,1
, set

K_{i}^{(0)}(t,n)=K_{i}(t,n),\qquad K_{i}^{(1)}(t,n)=\cos(t\omega_{i}(n)).

Thus
\partial_{t}^{0}V_{i}=V_{i}
and
\partial_{t}^{1}V_{i}=\partial_{t}V_{i}
.

At finite cutoff,

\widehat{V}_{i,\Lambda}(n,t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(t-s,n)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{\Psi}_{1,\Lambda}(k,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{2,\Lambda}(n-k,s)\,ds.

Since the colors are independent, for each
k
,

\widehat{\Psi}_{1,\Lambda}(k,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{2,\Lambda}(n-k,s)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{s}K_{1}(s-u,k)K_{2}(s-v,n-k)\,d\beta^{1}_{k}(u)d\beta^{2}_{n-k}(v).

This identity is understood as an identity in the colored double Wiener integral. Hence

\partial_{t}^{a}\widehat{V}_{i,\Lambda}(n,t)=\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{i,a}(n,k;t,u,v)\,d\beta^{1}_{k}(u)d\beta^{2}_{n-k}(v),

(B.7)

where

\mathcal{K}_{i,a}(n,k;t,u,v)=\int_{u\vee v}^{t}K_{i}^{(a)}(t-s,n)K_{1}(s-u,k)K_{2}(s-v,n-k)\,ds.

(B.8)

For
a=1
, there is no boundary term in differentiating the outer Duhamel integral, because
K_{i}(0,n)=0
.

All identities in this subsection are first understood at finite Fourier cutoff, where the sums are finite and stochastic Fubini is purely finite-dimensional. For orientation, the
a=0
representation is already justified by the absolute square-integrability estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{K}_{i,0}(n,k;t,u,v)|^{2}\,du\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{T}\langle n\rangle^{-2\alpha}\sum_{k}\langle k\rangle^{-2\alpha}\langle n-k\rangle^{-2\alpha}<\infty

for
\alpha>3/4
. For
a=1
, the outer denominator is absent, and we do not use an a priori absolute convergence argument. Instead, (
B.7
) is a finite-cutoff identity; Lemmas
B.10
–
B.12
give uniform coefficient estimates and cutoff Cauchy convergence, which pass the representation to the limiting process.

B.4
Oscillatory kernel bounds

For
\sigma=(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}
, define the three-frequency phase

\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)=\sigma_{0}\omega_{i}(n)+\sigma_{1}\omega_{1}(k)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{2}(n-k).

(B.9)

Put

N_{0}=\langle n\rangle,\qquad N_{1}=\langle k\rangle,\qquad N_{2}=\langle n-k\rangle,\qquad N_{\max}=\max(N_{0},N_{1},N_{2}).

Lemma B.3

(Static oscillatory kernel)
.

For
a=0,1
, uniformly in
0\leq u,v\leq t\leq T
,

|\mathcal{K}_{i,a}(n,k;t,u,v)|\lesssim_{T}\frac{N_{0}^{a\alpha}}{N_{0}^{\alpha}N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}^{\alpha}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}}\min\{1,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|^{-1}\}.

(B.10)

Proof.

Expand each sine and cosine in (
B.8
) into exponentials. The factors depending on
t,u,v
have modulus one. The denominators are
N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}^{\alpha}
, and the outer denominator contributes
N_{0}^{-\alpha}
for
a=0
and no denominator for
a=1
, which is exactly the prefactor
N_{0}^{a\alpha}/N_{0}^{\alpha}
. Each term contains an integral of the form

\int_{u\vee v}^{t}e^{is\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)}\,ds,

whose absolute value is bounded by
C_{T}\min\{1,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|^{-1}\}
. This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma B.4

(Kernel time increments)
.

Let
0<\eta\leq 1/2
. If
0\leq u,v\leq t\wedge t^{\prime}\leq T
, then for
a=0,1
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{i,a}(n,k;t,u,v)-\mathcal{K}_{i,a}(n,k;t^{\prime},u,v)|

(B.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\eta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}\frac{N_{0}^{a\alpha}N_{\max}^{\alpha\eta}}{N_{0}^{\alpha}N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}^{\alpha}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}}\min\{1,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|^{-1}\}.

Proof.

Assume
t^{\prime}>t
; the other case is identical. In the sign expansion used in Lemma
B.3
, the dependence on
t
occurs through endpoint exponentials and the upper limit of the
s
-integral. Endpoint factors obey

|e^{it\omega}-e^{it^{\prime}\omega}|\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}\omega^{\eta}\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}N_{\max}^{\alpha\eta}.

For the change of the integration interval,

\left|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}e^{is\Phi}\,ds\right|\leq\min\{|t-t^{\prime}|,|\Phi|^{-1}\}\leq|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}\min\{1,|\Phi|^{-1}\}^{1-\eta}.

Since
|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|\lesssim N_{\max}^{\alpha}
,

\min\{1,|\Phi|^{-1}\}^{1-\eta}\leq N_{\max}^{\alpha\eta}\min\{1,|\Phi|^{-1}\}.

Multiplying by the same denominator factors as in Lemma
B.3
proves (
B.11
).
∎

B.5
Lattice phase-layer estimates

This subsection is the only geometric input. We begin by isolating the lattice-thickening step used in the layer counts.

Lemma B.5

(Unit-cube thickening of phase layers)
.

Let
\Phi(n,x)
be a continuous sign branch of the phase (
B.9
), with the lattice variable
k
replaced by
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be one of the dyadic annular regions used below. If
L\geq 1
, then

\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\ k\in\mathcal{A},\ |\Phi(n,k)|\leq L\}\lesssim\bigl|\{x\in\mathcal{A}^{+1}:\ |\Phi(n,x)|\leq CL\}\bigr|,

where
\mathcal{A}^{+1}
is the unit-cube thickening of
\mathcal{A}
. The constant
C
depends only on the fixed dyadic conventions and on the speeds.

Proof.

Cover each lattice point by its unit cube. If
x
belongs to the unit cube centered at
k
, then

\bigl||x|-|k|\bigr|+\bigl||n-x|-|n-k|\bigr|\lesssim 1.

For
3/4<\alpha\leq 1
, the functions

\omega_{a}(\xi)=\bigl(1+c_{a}^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}\bigr)^{1/2}

are globally Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Hence
|\Phi(n,x)-\Phi(n,k)|\lesssim 1
. Since the modulation parameter is dyadic and
L\geq 1
, the layer
|\Phi|\leq L
is enlarged only to
|\Phi|\leq CL
. The thickened annuli contribute the boundary terms, such as
M^{2}
or
R^{2}
, which are explicitly retained in the estimates below.
∎

We also use the two-center coordinate formula. If
d=|n|>0
,
\rho=|x|
, and
\lambda=|n-x|
, then on the region
|\rho-\lambda|\leq d\leq\rho+\lambda
,

dx=2\pi\frac{\rho\lambda}{d}\,d\rho\,d\lambda.

(B.12)

This is the standard change of variables obtained by writing
x
in spherical coordinates around the axis generated by
n
. If
d=0
or if one of the dyadic scales is bounded, we do not use (
B.12
); those finite-dimensional low shells are absorbed into the constants or into the low-output high-high summation.

Lemma B.6

(Balanced phase layers)
.

Assume
|n|\sim|k|\sim|n-k|\sim M\gg 1
. For every sign branch
\sigma
and every dyadic
1\leq L\lesssim M^{\alpha}
,

\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:|n|\sim|k|\sim|n-k|\sim M,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|\leq L\}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{\varepsilon}(M^{2}+M^{3-\alpha}L).

(B.13)

Proof.

Use
d=|n|
,
\rho=|k|
, and
\lambda=|n-k|
. In the balanced region,
d\sim\rho\sim\lambda\sim M
, so the density
\rho\lambda/d
in (
B.12
) is
O(M)
. For fixed
\lambda
, the phase is

\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}=\sigma_{0}\omega_{i}(d)+\sigma_{1}\omega_{1}(\rho)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{2}(\lambda),

and

|\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|=|\omega_{1}^{\prime}(\rho)|\sim M^{\alpha-1}.

Thus the admissible
\rho
-length inside
|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|\leq L
is

O(LM^{1-\alpha}+1).

The
\lambda
-interval has length
O(M)
, and the density contributes
O(M)
. The thickened volume is therefore

O\bigl(M\cdot M\cdot(LM^{1-\alpha}+1)\bigr)=O(M^{3-\alpha}L+M^{2}).

Lemma
B.5
and dyadic boundary losses are absorbed into
M^{\varepsilon}
, proving (
B.13
).
∎

Lemma B.7

(High-low phase layers)
.

Assume
|n|\sim|k|\sim M
and
|n-k|\sim R
, where
1\leq R\ll M
. For every sign branch
\sigma
and every dyadic
1\leq L\lesssim R^{\alpha}
,

\#\{k:|n|\sim|k|\sim M,|n-k|\sim R,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|\leq L\}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}(R^{2}+R^{3-\alpha}L).

(B.14)

The same estimate holds with the roles of
k
and
n-k
exchanged.

Proof.

Write
q=n-k
,
p=|q|
, and
h=|n-q|=|k|
. In two-center coordinates with centers
0
and
n
, the phase is

\sigma_{0}\omega_{i}(|n|)+\sigma_{1}\omega_{1}(h)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{2}(p).

For fixed
h
,

|\partial_{p}\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|=|\omega_{2}^{\prime}(p)|\sim R^{\alpha-1}.

Hence the
p
-length of the layer
|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|\leq L
is
O(LR^{1-\alpha}+1)
. Since
|h-|n||\leq p\lesssim R
, the effective
h
-interval has length
O(R)
. The density
hp/|n|
is
O(R)
. Thus the thickened volume is

O\bigl(R\cdot R\cdot(LR^{1-\alpha}+1)\bigr)=O(R^{3-\alpha}L+R^{2}).

Lemma
B.5
gives (
B.14
). The exchanged case is identical, with
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
interchanged.
∎

Lemma B.8

(Low-output high-high speed gap)
.

There exist constants
C_{0}\gg 1
and
c_{*}>0
, depending only on the dyadic convention,
\alpha
, and
c_{1},c_{2}
, such that if

|k|\sim|n-k|\sim M,\qquad M\geq C_{0}\langle n\rangle,

then for every sign branch
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}
,

|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|\geq c_{*}M^{\alpha}.

(B.15)

The constant
c_{*}
degenerates as
|c_{1}-c_{2}|\to 0
, as expected.

Proof.

The output term satisfies
\omega_{i}(n)\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{\alpha}\leq C_{0}^{-\alpha}M^{\alpha}
. If the two high-frequency signs agree, then

|\sigma_{1}\omega_{1}(k)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{2}(n-k)|=\omega_{1}(k)+\omega_{2}(n-k)\gtrsim M^{\alpha},

and the small output term cannot cancel this after choosing
C_{0}
large.

If the high-frequency signs disagree, use the high-frequency expansion

\omega_{a}(x)=c_{a}|x|^{\alpha}+O(|x|^{-\alpha}).

Since

\bigl||k|-|n-k|\bigr|\leq|n|\leq C_{0}^{-1}M,

we have

|n-k|^{\alpha}=|k|^{\alpha}+O(C_{0}^{-1}M^{\alpha}).

Therefore

\omega_{1}(k)-\omega_{2}(n-k)=(c_{1}-c_{2})|k|^{\alpha}+O(C_{0}^{-1}M^{\alpha})+O(M^{-\alpha}).

Because
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
, choosing
C_{0}
large and then absorbing finitely many small shells gives a lower bound
\gtrsim M^{\alpha}
. Again the output term is too small to cancel the high-frequency contribution. This proves (
B.15
).
∎

B.6
The deterministic phase sums

For
a=0,1
and
0\leq\eta\leq 1/2
, define

S_{i}^{(a,\eta)}(n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{N_{0}^{2a\alpha}N_{\max}^{2\alpha\eta}}{N_{0}^{2\alpha}N_{1}^{2\alpha}N_{2}^{2\alpha}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}}\min\{1,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}(n,k)|^{-1}\}\right)^{2}.

(B.16)

We write
S_{i}^{(a)}=S_{i}^{(a,0)}
.

Proposition B.9

(Phase-sum estimate)
.

Choose
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
so small that

3-6\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon<0.

(B.17)

Then, for
a=0,1
,

S_{i}^{(a,\eta)}(n)\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\eta}\langle n\rangle^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(B.18)

In particular,

S_{i}^{(0)}(n)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon},\qquad S_{i}^{(1)}(n)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(B.19)

Proof.

Since
(\sum_{\sigma}a_{\sigma})^{2}\leq 8\sum_{\sigma}a_{\sigma}^{2}
, it is enough to estimate one sign branch. Decompose dyadically according to
N_{1}\sim\langle k\rangle
and
N_{2}\sim\langle n-k\rangle
, and keep
N_{0}=\langle n\rangle
fixed. The relation
n=k+(n-k)
implies that the two largest of
N_{0},N_{1},N_{2}
are comparable. Low finite shells are absorbed into constants.

Balanced region.
Suppose
N_{0}\sim N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim M
. By Lemma
B.6
, dyadic modulation layers
|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|\sim L
,
1\leq L\lesssim M^{\alpha}
, give

\displaystyle M^{2a\alpha}M^{2\alpha\eta}M^{-6\alpha}\sum_{1\leq L\lesssim M^{\alpha}}L^{-2}M^{\varepsilon}(M^{2}+M^{3-\alpha}L)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta-6\alpha}M^{3-\alpha+\varepsilon}=M^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

The contribution of the
M^{2}
term is smaller for
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

High-low region.
Suppose
N_{0}\sim N_{1}\sim M
and
N_{2}\sim R\ll M
. The case
N_{0}\sim N_{2}\sim M
,
N_{1}\sim R
, is the same. For
1\leq L\lesssim R^{\alpha}
, Lemma
B.7
gives

\displaystyle M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta}M^{-4\alpha}R^{-2\alpha}\sum_{1\leq L\lesssim R^{\alpha}}L^{-2}R^{\varepsilon}(R^{2}+R^{3-\alpha}L)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta-4\alpha}R^{3-3\alpha+\varepsilon}.

For the larger modulations
R^{\alpha}<L\lesssim M^{\alpha}
, the trivial count
O(R^{3})
gives

M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta-4\alpha}R^{-2\alpha}R^{3}\sum_{L>R^{\alpha}}L^{-2}\lesssim M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta-4\alpha}R^{3-4\alpha},

which is no worse. Summing dyadically in
1\leq R\leq M
yields

\sum_{R\leq M}M^{2a\alpha+2\alpha\eta-4\alpha}R^{3-3\alpha+\varepsilon}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

At
\alpha=1
, the endpoint logarithm is absorbed into
M^{\varepsilon}
.

Low-output high-high region.
Suppose
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim M
and
M\geq C_{0}N_{0}
. Lemma
B.8
gives
\min\{1,|\Phi_{i}^{\sigma}|^{-1}\}\lesssim M^{-\alpha}
. The contribution of a single
M
-shell is therefore

N_{0}^{-2\alpha+2a\alpha}M^{-4\alpha}M^{2\alpha\eta}M^{3}M^{-2\alpha}=N_{0}^{-2\alpha+2a\alpha}M^{3-6\alpha+2\alpha\eta}.

By (
B.17
), the dyadic sum over
M\geq C_{0}N_{0}
converges and is bounded by

N_{0}^{-2\alpha+2a\alpha}N_{0}^{3-6\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}=N_{0}^{3-(8-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon},

which is stronger than (
B.18
). The remaining range
M\lesssim N_{0}
belongs to the balanced or high-low alternatives. This proves the proposition.
∎

B.7
Fixed-time and time-increment estimates

Lemma B.10

(Fixed-time coefficient bounds)
.

For
a=0,1
,

\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\partial_{t}^{a}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(B.20)

Proof.

At finite cutoff, apply the double Ito isometry to (
B.7
). Lemma
B.3
gives

\mathbb{E}|\partial_{t}^{a}\widehat{V}_{i,\Lambda}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T}S_{i}^{(a)}(n),

uniformly in
\Lambda
. Proposition
B.9
with
\eta=0
gives (
B.20
), uniformly in the cutoff. We keep the statement at finite cutoff until the tail estimate of Lemma
B.12
is proved; that lemma gives the Cauchy convergence and identifies the limiting coefficient.
∎

Lemma B.11

(Time-increment coefficient bounds)
.

For sufficiently small
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
and
a=0,1
,

\mathbb{E}|\partial_{t}^{a}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t)-\partial_{t}^{a}\widehat{V}_{i}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(B.21)

Proof.

Assume
t^{\prime}<t
; the other case is symmetric. In the double stochastic representation, split the square
[0,t]^{2}
into the common square
[0,t^{\prime}]^{2}
and the boundary strip
[0,t]^{2}\setminus[0,t^{\prime}]^{2}
.

On the common square, the difference of kernels is controlled by Lemma
B.4
. The double Ito isometry gives

\text{common-square contribution}\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}S_{i}^{(a,\eta)}(n).

On the boundary strip, whose measure is
O_{T}(|t-t^{\prime}|)
, use the static kernel bound from Lemma
B.3
. Since
0<\eta\leq 1/2
and
|t-t^{\prime}|\leq T
, this contribution is bounded by

C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\eta}S_{i}^{(a,\eta)}(n),

because
N_{\max}^{2\alpha\eta}\geq 1
. Proposition
B.9
proves (
B.21
).
∎

Taking
a=0
and
a=1
in Lemmas
B.10
and
B.11
gives (
B.1
)–(
B.4
).

B.8
Cutoff tails and canonical convergence

Let
S_{i,>\Lambda}^{(a,\eta)}(n)
be the phase sum (
B.16
) restricted to

\max\{|k|,|n-k|\}>\Lambda.

This is the deterministic majorant for the coefficient variance of cutoff differences. The next lemma proves the weighted tail summability needed in Lemma
B.2
.

Lemma B.12

(Tail phase sums)
.

Fix
a=0,1
, choose
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
satisfying (
B.17
), and let

s<\frac{(7-2a)\alpha}{2}-3.

Then, after reducing
\eta
and the harmless losses if necessary,

\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\sum_{N_{0}\ {\rm dyadic}}N_{0}^{2s+3}\sup_{\langle n\rangle\sim N_{0}}S_{i,>\Lambda}^{(a,\eta)}(n)=0.

(B.22)

Proof.

For each fixed dyadic output shell
\langle n\rangle\sim N_{0}
, there are finitely many output frequencies
n
, and for each of them the restricted sum
S_{i,>\Lambda}^{(a,\eta)}(n)
decreases to zero as
\Lambda\to\infty
. Hence the shell supremum tends to zero for fixed
N_{0}
.

It remains to dominate the series in (
B.22
). The proof of Proposition
B.9
gives the uniform majorant

S_{i,>\Lambda}^{(a,\eta)}(n)\leq S_{i}^{(a,\eta)}(n)\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

The low-output high-high part has an even more explicit tail bound:

S_{i,>\Lambda}^{(a,\eta)}(n)\lesssim N_{0}^{-2\alpha+2a\alpha}\sum_{M\geq\max(C_{0}N_{0},\Lambda)}M^{3-6\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon},

which tends to zero and is dominated by the full low-output estimate because of (
B.17
). The balanced and high-low tails are handled identically by starting the corresponding dyadic sums at the removed shell. Finally,

\sum_{N_{0}}N_{0}^{2s+3}N_{0}^{3-(7-2a)\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}<\infty

whenever
s<((7-2a)\alpha)/2-3
and
\eta,\varepsilon
are chosen sufficiently small. Dominated convergence proves (
B.22
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
B.1
.

The coefficient bounds (
B.1
)–(
B.4
) were proved in Lemmas
B.10
and
B.11
. Applying Lemma
B.2
with

p_{0}=3-7\alpha+\varepsilon

gives

V_{i}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s})\qquad\text{for every }\quad s<-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{p_{0}}{2}=\frac{7\alpha}{2}-3-.

Applying the same lemma with

p_{1}=3-5\alpha+\varepsilon

gives

\partial_{t}V_{i}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s})\qquad\text{for every }\quad s<-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{p_{1}}{2}=\frac{5\alpha}{2}-3-.

The Cauchy convergence of
V_{i,\Lambda}
and
\partial_{t}V_{i,\Lambda}
follows from Lemma
B.12
and the cutoff-difference form of Lemma
B.2
. The convergence is canonical in the sense that any two smooth cutoff sequences whose symbols tend to one on fixed frequencies have differences controlled by the same tail condition
\max\{|k|,|n-k|\}>\Lambda
.

At finite cutoff,

V_{i,\Lambda}(t)-V_{i,\Lambda}(0)=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}V_{i,\Lambda}(s)\,ds.

Both sides converge uniformly in time in slightly lower spatial regularity. Therefore the limiting process has the asserted time derivative. This proves the theorem.
∎

Remark B.13

(Source of the gain)
.

The static estimate for
I_{i}(\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2})
uses only the outer Duhamel denominator and gives
V_{i}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{3\alpha-3-}
. The estimate above keeps the three propagators simultaneously. The phase-layer bound for

\sigma_{0}\omega_{i}(n)+\sigma_{1}\omega_{1}(k)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{2}(n-k)

changes the fixed-time coefficient power from
3-6\alpha
to
3-7\alpha
, which is exactly an additional
\alpha/2-
derivative in Besov regularity. The speed separation
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
is used only in the low-output high-high sector; balanced and high-low sectors are controlled by one-frequency phase-layer counting.

Appendix C
The resonant cubic symbols: colored Wick bookkeeping and phase estimates

This appendix gives a closed construction of the resonant cubic symbols

\Gamma_{i}=V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i},\qquad i=1,2,

including the centered third homogeneous chaos, the cut-out first-chaos contraction, the time-increment estimates, and the Fourier-cutoff Cauchy convergence. The point of the proof is twofold.

First, the Wick contraction bookkeeping is colored. In the block

V_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}=I_{2}(\Psi_{1}\Psi_{2})\circ\Psi_{1},

there are two color-1 Gaussian variables and one color-2 Gaussian variable. The color-2 variable cannot contract with either color-1 variable. The unique possible contraction pairs the two color-1 variables and leaves a color-2 first-chaos stochastic convolution. Thus the lower-chaos term is not a scalar counterterm.

Second, the contraction produces an infinite sum over the contracted high frequency. The formal multiplier

M_{21}(n;t,s)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\chi^{\circ}(n-r,r)K_{2}(t-s,n-r)\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)

is not the right object to define first. Its leading dyadic shell has size

M^{3}\cdot M^{-\alpha}\cdot M^{-2\alpha}=M^{3-3\alpha},

which is not summable for
\alpha\leq 1
. The correct object is the integrated Volterra kernel obtained after inserting the remaining stochastic convolution. Its single-shell bound is

\sup_{0\leq u\leq t\leq T}|K_{21,M}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon},

and this is summable precisely because
\alpha>3/4
. The cutoff-tail convergence of this integrated kernel is stated and proved explicitly below.

Throughout, the constants may depend on
T
, the smooth Littlewood-Paley cutoffs, the resonant cutoff
\chi^{\circ}
,
\alpha
, and the speeds
c_{1},c_{2}
, but not on the dyadic frequencies or on the Fourier cutoff parameter. Finitely many zero or low modes are finite-dimensional and are absorbed into the constants. We write

\omega_{i}(n)=\bigl(1+c_{i}^{2}|n|^{2\alpha}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad K_{i}(\tau,n)=\frac{\sin(\tau\omega_{i}(n))}{\omega_{i}(n)}.

The resonant cutoff
\chi^{\circ}(m,r)
is supported where
\langle m\rangle\sim\langle r\rangle
. All Besov–Hölder spaces are on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. The stochastic convolutions are constructed with zero deterministic data.

The only input from Appendix B is the first Picard estimate and its cutoff-tail version: for every
\varepsilon>0
, small
\eta>0
, and
j=1,2
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{j}(m,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle m\rangle^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon},

(C.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{j}(m,t+h)-\widehat{V}_{j}(m,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|h|^{2\eta}\langle m\rangle^{3-7\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(C.2)

The linear stochastic convolution satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(r,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\langle r\rangle^{-2\alpha},

(C.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(r,t+h)-\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(r,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\eta}|h|^{2\eta}\langle r\rangle^{-2\alpha+2\alpha\eta}.

(C.4)

We shall repeatedly use the following coefficient criterion, which is the same dyadic hypercontractive criterion used in the main text.

Lemma C.1

(Coefficient-to-path-space criterion)
.

Let
Z
be a spatially homogeneous random distribution whose Fourier coefficients belong to a fixed finite sum of Wiener chaoses of bounded order. Suppose that, for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
, every
\varepsilon>0
, and some small
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{p+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}(n,t+h)-\widehat{Z}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|h|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{p+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

Then
Z\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s})
almost surely for every

s<-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{p}{2}.

The same statement holds for cutoff tails: if the coefficient bounds above are replaced by bounds for the difference of two cutoffs, with a dyadic majorant summable in the corresponding Besov exponent and with pointwise convergence of each coefficient, then the cutoffs are Cauchy in
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
.

Proof.

For a fixed dyadic shell
|n|\sim N
, the variance of
P_{N}Z(t,x)
is bounded by

\sum_{|n|\sim N}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim N^{3+p+\varepsilon}.

Nelson hypercontractivity on a fixed finite chaos upgrades this to every finite
L^{q}(\Omega)
norm with the same power of
N
, up to constants depending on
q
. Applying the estimate to a spatial net, using Bernstein on the shell, and then using Kolmogorov continuity in time gives, for every small
\kappa>0
,

\|P_{N}Z\|_{C_{T}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq C_{\omega}N^{3/2+p/2+\kappa}

for all dyadic
N
, almost surely. Summability of
N^{s}\|P_{N}Z\|_{C_{T}L_{x}^{\infty}}
gives the asserted range of
s
. The cutoff-tail version is the same argument applied to the cutoff difference, followed by dominated convergence over dyadic shells.
∎

Theorem C.2

(Resonant cubic symbols)
.

Assume
3/4<\alpha\leq 1
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, and
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
. For each
i=1,2
, the finite Fourier cutoff approximations of

\Gamma_{i}=V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}

converge canonically in the sense below and admit the Wick decomposition

\Gamma_{i}=\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}+C_{i},

where
\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}
is a centered third homogeneous chaos and
C_{i}
is a centered first-chaos Volterra term. Moreover,

\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2}-},\qquad C_{i}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{5\alpha-\frac{9}{2}-}.

Consequently,

\Gamma_{i}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2}-}.

The first-chaos contraction is smoother than the third-chaos component by
\alpha/2-
, and it is not a deterministic counterterm.

The rest of the appendix proves Theorem
C.2
. We write the proof for
\Gamma_{1}=V_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}
; the proof for
\Gamma_{2}=V_{1}\circ\Psi_{2}
is obtained by exchanging the colors.

C.1
Finite cutoff Wick decomposition in the colored Gaussian space

Let
\pi_{\Lambda}
denote a smooth Fourier cutoff to
|n|\leq\Lambda
. Define

\Psi_{i}^{\Lambda}=\pi_{\Lambda}\Psi_{i},\qquad V_{2}^{\Lambda}=I_{2}(\Psi_{1}^{\Lambda}\Psi_{2}^{\Lambda}),

and

\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{\Lambda}(n,t)=\sum_{m+r=n}\chi^{\circ}(m,r)\widehat{V}_{2}^{\Lambda}(m,t)\widehat{\Psi}_{1}^{\Lambda}(r,t).

Expanding
V_{2}^{\Lambda}
,

\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{\Lambda}(n,t)=\sum_{a+b+r=n}^{\Lambda}\chi^{\circ}(a+b,r)\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s,a+b)\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(a,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(b,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(r,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}s,

(C.5)

where the superscript
\Lambda
on the sum indicates that the three stochastic frequencies occurring in the cutoff approximation are restricted according to the chosen cutoff. The exact shape of the smooth cutoff is immaterial for the estimates below.

At finite cutoff, let
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\Lambda}
be the real Gaussian Hilbert spaces generated by the two Fourier Brownian families up to frequency
\Lambda
on
[0,T]
. The full Gaussian space is the orthogonal direct sum

\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\Lambda}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\Lambda}.

The orthogonality is exactly color independence. Multiple Wiener integrals are symmetrized only inside variables of the same color.

Lemma C.3

(The unique colored contraction channel)
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\Lambda}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\Lambda}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\Lambda}
. Then

I_{(1,1)}(F)I_{(1,0)}(g)=I_{(2,1)}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_{1}(F\otimes g)\bigr)+I_{(0,1)}(F\otimes_{1}g),

(C.6)

where
\operatorname{Sym}_{1}
symmetrizes the two color-1 slots and
F\otimes_{1}g\in\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\Lambda}
is the contraction in the color-1 slot. There is no contraction between the color-2 slot of
F
and
g
.

Proof.

It suffices to check finite-rank tensors. Write

F=\sum_{a,b}F_{a,b}e_{a}^{(1)}\otimes e_{b}^{(2)},\qquad g=\sum_{r}g_{r}e_{r}^{(1)},

with orthonormal bases in the two color Hilbert spaces. The only nontrivial Gaussian product is

W_{1}(e_{a}^{(1)})W_{1}(e_{r}^{(1)})=:W_{1}(e_{a}^{(1)})W_{1}(e_{r}^{(1)}):+\langle e_{a}^{(1)},e_{r}^{(1)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}.

Multiplication by the independent color-2 coordinate
W_{2}(e_{b}^{(2)})
gives exactly (
C.6
). Completion gives the statement for general finite-cutoff kernels.
∎

In the real Fourier convention, the same-color covariance has the form

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(k,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{b}(k^{\prime},t)]=\mathbf{1}_{a=b}\mathbf{1}_{k+k^{\prime}=0}\sigma_{a}(k;s,t),

(C.7)

where

\sigma_{a}(k;s,t)=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(k,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(-k,t)].

(C.8)

Thus the product in (
C.5
) has only one possible contraction:
\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(a,s)
contracts with
\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(r,t)
, and this forces
a+r=0
. Therefore

\Gamma_{1}^{\Lambda}=\Gamma_{1}^{(3),\Lambda}+C_{1}^{\Lambda}.

(C.9)

The third chaos is

\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3),\Lambda}(n,t)=\sum_{a+b+r=n}^{\Lambda}\chi^{\circ}(a+b,r)\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s,a+b):\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(a,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(b,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(r,t):\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

(C.10)

The contraction is

\widehat{C}_{1}^{\Lambda}(n,t)=\mathbf{1}_{|n|\leq\Lambda}\int_{0}^{t}M_{21}^{\Lambda}(n;t,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(n,s)\,\,\mathrm{d}s,

(C.11)

where

M_{21}^{\Lambda}(n;t,s)=\sum_{r}^{\Lambda}\chi^{\circ}(n-r,r)K_{2}(t-s,n-r)\sigma_{1}(r;s,t).

(C.12)

The notation
\sum_{r}^{\Lambda}
means that only the contracted frequencies allowed by the finite cutoff are included.

Remark C.4

(The formal multiplier is not the limiting definition)
.

For
|r|\sim|n-r|\sim M
, the leading part of
M_{21}^{\Lambda}
has shell size

M^{3}\cdot M^{-\alpha}\cdot M^{-2\alpha}=M^{3-3\alpha}.

This is not summable when
\alpha\leq 1
. Hence the limiting contraction must not be defined as a pointwise absolutely convergent infinite sum in (
C.12
). The object that converges is the integrated kernel obtained after inserting the remaining color-2 stochastic convolution. This is constructed in Subsection C.3 below.

C.2
The centered third homogeneous chaos

The third-chaos estimate is a kernel-norm estimate in the colored Wiener space. One should not argue that
V_{2}
and
\Psi_{1}
are independent; they are not, because
V_{2}
contains a color-1 factor.

Lemma C.5

(Third-chaos kernel norm)
.

For every finite cutoff and every fixed
(n,t)
,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3),\Lambda}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{m+r=n}|\chi^{\circ}(m,r)|^{2}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{2}^{\Lambda}(m,t)|^{2}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{1}^{\Lambda}(r,t)|^{2}.

(C.13)

The same estimate holds for differences of two finite cutoffs, with one factor replaced by the corresponding cutoff difference.

Proof.

For each
m
, write
\widehat{V}_{2}^{\Lambda}(m,t)=I_{(1,1)}(F_{m}^{\Lambda}(t))
. Then

\|F_{m}^{\Lambda}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}_{2}^{\Lambda}(m,t)|^{2}.

Similarly
\widehat{\Psi}_{1}^{\Lambda}(r,t)=I_{(1,0)}(g_{r}^{\Lambda}(t))
, with

\|g_{r}^{\Lambda}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{1}^{\Lambda}(r,t)|^{2}.

By Lemma
C.3
, the third-chaos projection of the product is

I_{(2,1)}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_{1}(F_{m}^{\Lambda}(t)\otimes g_{r}^{\Lambda}(t))\bigr).

The output
n
kernel is the sum over
m+r=n
of these symmetrized kernels multiplied by
\chi^{\circ}(m,r)
.

Before symmetrization, the frequency part is of the form

H(a,r,b)=\chi^{\circ}(a+b,r)F_{m;a,b}^{\Lambda}(t)g_{r}^{\Lambda}(t),\qquad m=a+b,

with
a+b+r=n
. Symmetrization of the two color-1 slots gives

\widetilde{H}(a,r,b)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(H(a,r,b)+H(r,a,b)\bigr).

Thus
|\widetilde{H}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|H(a,r,b)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|H(r,a,b)|^{2}
, and the second contribution is reduced to the first by exchanging the dummy variables
a
and
r
. Summing over all Fourier and time variables gives (
C.13
). For cutoff differences, write the difference of kernels and apply the same argument term by term.
∎

Lemma C.6

(Third-chaos coefficient estimate)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3)}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{6-9\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.14)

Consequently
\Gamma_{1}^{(3)}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2}-}
, after time increments and cutoff convergence are included.

Proof.

By Lemma
C.5
, (
C.1
), and (
C.3
),

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3)}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\sum_{m+r=n}|\chi^{\circ}(m,r)|^{2}\langle m\rangle^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon}\langle r\rangle^{-2\alpha}.

On the resonant support
\langle m\rangle\sim\langle r\rangle\sim M
, and necessarily
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
. The dyadic shell contribution is

M^{3}\cdot M^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon}\cdot M^{-2\alpha}=M^{6-9\alpha+\varepsilon}.

Since
6-9\alpha<0
for
\alpha>3/4
, summing over dyadic
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
gives (
C.14
). The Besov exponent follows from Lemma
C.1
once the next lemma supplies time increments.
∎

Lemma C.7

(Third-chaos time increments)
.

For sufficiently small
0<\eta<1
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3)}(n,t+h)-\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}^{(3)}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|h|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{6-9\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(C.15)

Proof.

Using the colored kernel representation from Lemma
C.5
, the difference is estimated by placing the time increment either on the first Picard kernel or on the external linear stochastic convolution. Equations (
C.2
) and (
C.4
) give, on the resonant shell
\langle m\rangle\sim\langle r\rangle\sim M
,

\displaystyle M^{3}\Bigl(M^{3-7\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}M^{-2\alpha}+M^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon}M^{-2\alpha+2\alpha\eta}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim M^{6-9\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

Choose
\eta
and
\varepsilon
so that the exponent is negative. Summing over
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
proves (
C.15
).
∎

Lemma C.8

(Third-chaos cutoff Cauchy convergence)
.

For every

s<\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2},

the finite cutoff third-chaos approximations
\Gamma_{1}^{(3),\Lambda}
are Cauchy in
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
almost surely.

Proof.

Compare two cutoffs
\Lambda_{2}>\Lambda_{1}=L
. The difference of the third-chaos kernels is a finite sum of terms in which either the external color-1 factor
\Psi_{1}(r)
is in the tail or the first Picard kernel
V_{2}(m)
is in the tail.

For the external tail, the resonant shell contribution starts at
M\gtrsim\max\{\langle n\rangle,L\}
, hence it is bounded by

\sum_{M\gtrsim\max\{\langle n\rangle,L\}}M^{6-9\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.16)

This tends to zero for each fixed
n
, and it is dominated by the full summable majorant used in Lemma
C.6
.

For the internal tail, Appendix B gives Cauchy convergence of the first Picard kernels and the tail estimate dominated by
\langle m\rangle^{3-7\alpha+\varepsilon}
. Therefore the same resonant convolution as above is controlled by the majorant
M^{6-9\alpha+\varepsilon}
, and it tends to zero for each fixed
n
by dominated convergence in the internal Picard variables.

The increment estimates have the same tail structure with the harmless factor
M^{2\alpha\eta}
. Taking
\eta
small, Lemma
C.1
yields almost sure Cauchy convergence in every
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
with
s<9\alpha/2-9/2
.
∎

C.3
The first-chaos contraction as an integrated Volterra kernel

We now give the closed construction of the lower-chaos term. At finite cutoff, insert the remaining stochastic convolution

\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(n,s)=\int_{0}^{s}K_{2}(s-u,n)\,\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{2,n}(u)

into (
C.11
). Stochastic Fubini is immediate at finite cutoff and will be justified in the limit by the estimates below. Thus

\widehat{C}_{1}^{\Lambda}(n,t)=\mathbf{1}_{|n|\leq\Lambda}\int_{0}^{t}K_{21}^{\Lambda}(n;t,u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{2,n}(u),

(C.17)

where

K_{21}^{\Lambda}(n;t,u)=\int_{u}^{t}\sum_{r}^{\Lambda}\chi^{\circ}(n-r,r)K_{2}(t-s,n-r)\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)K_{2}(s-u,n)\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

(C.18)

The limiting contraction
C_{1}
will be defined by the limit of these integrated kernels.

The covariance admits the exact formula

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}K_{1}(s-\tau,r)K_{1}(t-\tau,-r)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2\omega_{1}(r)^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{1}(r))+\frac{\sin((t-s)\omega_{1}(r))}{4\omega_{1}(r)^{3}}-\frac{\sin((t+s)\omega_{1}(r))}{4\omega_{1}(r)^{3}}.

(C.19)

We write

\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)=\sigma_{1,0}(r;s,t)+R_{1}(r;s,t),\qquad\sigma_{1,0}(r;s,t)=\frac{s}{2\omega_{1}(r)^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{1}(r)).

(C.20)

On
|r|\sim M
,

|\sigma_{1,0}(r;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}M^{-2\alpha},\qquad|R_{1}(r;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}M^{-3\alpha}.

(C.21)

The phase estimate below is the only place in Appendix C where the speed separation
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
is used. It controls the low-output high-high part. The balanced part is controlled by a one-frequency phase-layer count.

Lemma C.9

(One-frequency phase-layer estimate)
.

Let

R_{M}(n)=\{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:|r|\sim M,\ |n-r|\sim M\},\qquad M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle.

For a sign vector
\sigma=(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}
, set

\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)=\sigma_{0}\omega_{2}(n)+\sigma_{1}\omega_{2}(n-r)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{1}(r).

(C.22)

Then, for every
0\leq\delta<1
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{r\in R_{M}(n)}\min\{1,|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|^{-1}\}^{1-\delta}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\delta}M^{3-\alpha(1-\delta)+\varepsilon}.

(C.23)

In particular,

\sum_{r\in R_{M}(n)}\min\{1,|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|^{-1}\}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{3-\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.24)

Proof.

There are two regimes.

First assume
M\geq C_{0}\langle n\rangle
, with
C_{0}
sufficiently large depending only on the speeds and the cutoff convention. If the two high-frequency signs in

\sigma_{1}\omega_{2}(n-r)+\sigma_{2}\omega_{1}(r)

are equal, the high-frequency contribution has size
\gtrsim M^{\alpha}
. If they are opposite, then

\omega_{2}(n-r)-\omega_{1}(r)=(c_{2}-c_{1})|r|^{\alpha}+O(M^{\alpha-1}\langle n\rangle)+O(M^{-\alpha}),

and the leading term is nonzero because
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
. Increasing
C_{0}
makes the error lower order. The output term
\omega_{2}(n)\lesssim C_{0}^{-\alpha}M^{\alpha}
cannot cancel the high-frequency contribution. Hence
|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|\gtrsim M^{\alpha}
, and the contribution is bounded by

\#R_{M}(n)M^{-\alpha(1-\delta)}\lesssim M^{3-\alpha(1-\delta)}.

It remains to treat the balanced regime
M\sim\langle n\rangle
. Write

\rho=|r|,\lambda=|n-r|,d=|n|.

In two-center coordinates the continuous volume element is

\,\mathrm{d}x=2\pi\frac{\rho\lambda}{d}\,\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(C.25)

On the balanced region the density is
O(M)
. Fixing
\rho
, the phase as a function of
\lambda
has derivative

\partial_{\lambda}\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)=\sigma_{1}\omega_{2}^{\prime}(\lambda),\qquad|\omega_{2}^{\prime}(\lambda)|\sim M^{\alpha-1}.

Therefore the layer
|\Omega_{\sigma}|\leq L
, for dyadic
1\leq L\lesssim M^{\alpha}
, has
\lambda
-length
O(LM^{1-\alpha}+1)
. The
\rho
-interval has length
O(M)
, and the two-center density contributes
O(M)
. After the usual unit-cube thickening, the lattice layer estimate is

\#\{r\in R_{M}(n):|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|\leq L\}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{\varepsilon}(M^{2}+M^{3-\alpha}L).

(C.26)

Decomposing into dyadic modulation layers gives

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in R_{M}(n)}\min\{1,|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|^{-1}\}^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\sum_{1\leq L\lesssim M^{\alpha}}L^{-(1-\delta)}M^{\varepsilon}(M^{2}+M^{3-\alpha}L)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\delta}M^{3-\alpha(1-\delta)+\varepsilon}.

The term generated by
M^{2}
is at most
M^{2+\alpha\delta+\varepsilon}
, and this is bounded by
M^{3-\alpha(1-\delta)+\varepsilon}
because
\alpha\leq 1
. This proves the lemma.
∎

For dyadic
M
, let
K_{21,M}
denote the part of (
C.18
) with
|r|\sim|n-r|\sim M
, after replacing the finite cutoff by the corresponding dyadic projector. The resonant cutoff enforces
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
.

Lemma C.10

(Single-shell integrated contraction kernel)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{0\leq u\leq t\leq T}|K_{21,M}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon},\qquad M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle.

(C.27)

Proof.

The covariance remainder in (
C.20
) is estimated absolutely. Using
|K_{2}(t-s,n-r)|\lesssim M^{-\alpha}
,
|R_{1}(r;s,t)|\lesssim M^{-3\alpha}
,
|K_{2}(s-u,n)|\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}
, and
\#R_{M}(n)\lesssim M^{3}
, its contribution is

\lesssim_{T}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3}M^{-\alpha}M^{-3\alpha}=\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha}.

For the leading covariance, expand the three oscillatory factors

K_{2}(t-s,n-r),\qquad\cos((t-s)\omega_{1}(r)),\qquad K_{2}(s-u,n).

For each sign branch the
s
-dependent phase is one of the
\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)
in (
C.22
), up to harmless sign changes. Endpoint factors depending on
(t,u)
have modulus one. The static amplitude for a fixed
r
is

\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{-\alpha}M^{-2\alpha}=\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{-3\alpha}.

Moreover,

\left|\int_{u}^{t}se^{\mathrm{i}s\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)}\,\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\lesssim_{T}\min\{1,|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|^{-1}\}.

Summing in
r
and applying (
C.24
) yields

|K_{21,M}^{\mathrm{lead}}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{-3\alpha}M^{3-\alpha+\varepsilon}=\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon}.

The result follows.
∎

Lemma C.11

(Convergence of the integrated kernel)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

\sup_{0\leq u\leq t\leq T}|K_{21}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.28)

More precisely, for every dyadic lower cutoff
L\geq 1
, the internal contraction tail

K_{21,>L}(n;t,u)=\sum_{M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle,\ M>L}K_{21,M}(n;t,u)

satisfies

\sup_{u,t}|K_{21,>L}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}\sum_{M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle,\ M>L}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.29)

In particular, for each fixed
n
,
K_{21,>L}(n;t,u)\to 0
uniformly in
(u,t)
as
L\to\infty
.

Proof.

The bound (
C.29
) is the direct summation of (
C.27
). Since
3-4\alpha<0
, the full sum over dyadic
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
gives

\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}\sum_{M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon}\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}\langle n\rangle^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon}=\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+\varepsilon},

provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen so small that
3-4\alpha+\varepsilon<0
. The same negative exponent gives the fixed-mode tail convergence.
∎

Proposition C.12

(First-chaos coefficient bound)
.

The limiting first-chaos contraction defined by

\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{21}(n;t,u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{2,n}(u)

(C.30)

satisfies, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.31)

Consequently, after time increments and cutoff convergence are included,
C_{1}\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{5\alpha-\frac{9}{2}-}
.

Proof.

By Itô isometry and (
C.28
),

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t)|^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}|K_{21}(n;t,u)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\lesssim_{T}\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+\varepsilon}.

The Besov exponent follows from Lemma
C.1
once the time-increment estimate in Lemma
C.14
and the cutoff Cauchy estimate in Lemma
C.15
have been proved.
∎

Lemma C.13

(Single-shell time increment for the integrated contraction kernel)
.

Let
0<\eta<1/2
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, after decreasing
\eta
if necessary,

\sup_{u\leq t\leq T}|K_{21,M}(n;t+h,u)-K_{21,M}(n;t,u)|\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|h|^{\eta}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha+\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}

(C.32)

whenever both kernels are evaluated on their common domain. The same estimate holds for the change of the upper endpoint of the
s
-integral.

Proof.

The difference is decomposed into: a time increment of the outer kernel
K_{2}(t-s,n-r)
, a time increment of the covariance
\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)
, changes of endpoint phase factors in the sign expansion, and the change of the upper integration endpoint.

For the kernels and covariances we use the fractional increment bounds

|K_{a}(\tau+h,m)-K_{a}(\tau,m)|\lesssim|h|^{\eta}\langle m\rangle^{-\alpha+\alpha\eta},

and, from (
C.3
),

|\sigma_{1}(r;s,t+h)-\sigma_{1}(r;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}|h|^{\eta}M^{-2\alpha+\alpha\eta}

after retaining the leading covariance, with better bounds for the remainder. These terms repeat the proof of Lemma
C.10
with the additional factor
|h|^{\eta}M^{\alpha\eta}
, giving the right side of (
C.32
).

For the change of the upper endpoint, each sign branch contains an interval integral of the form

\int_{t}^{t+h}se^{\mathrm{i}s\Omega}\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

The interpolation estimate

\left|\int_{t}^{t+h}se^{\mathrm{i}s\Omega}\,\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\lesssim_{T}|h|^{\eta}\min\{1,|\Omega|^{-1}\}^{1-\eta}

combined with the
\delta=\eta
version of Lemma
C.9
gives

\displaystyle\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{-3\alpha}|h|^{\eta}\sum_{r\in R_{M}(n)}\min\{1,|\Omega_{\sigma}(n,r)|^{-1}\}^{1-\eta}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|h|^{\eta}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{-3\alpha}M^{3-\alpha(1-\eta)+\varepsilon}=|h|^{\eta}\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}M^{3-4\alpha+\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

This proves the endpoint part as well.
∎

Lemma C.14

(First-chaos time increments)
.

For sufficiently small
0<\eta<1/2
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t+h)-\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,\eta}|h|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(C.33)

Proof.

Choose
\eta
and
\varepsilon
so small that
3-4\alpha+\alpha\eta+\varepsilon<0
. Summing (
C.32
) over dyadic
M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle
gives

\sup_{u}|K_{21}(n;t+h,u)-K_{21}(n;t,u)|\lesssim|h|^{\eta}\langle n\rangle^{3-5\alpha+\alpha\eta+\varepsilon}.

(C.34)

Assume
h>0
. From (
C.30
),

\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t+h)-\widehat{C}_{1}(n,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl[K_{21}(n;t+h,u)-K_{21}(n;t,u)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{2,n}(u)+\int_{t}^{t+h}K_{21}(n;t+h,u)\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{2,n}(u).

The first integral is controlled by Itô isometry and (
C.34
). The boundary integral is bounded by

|h|\sup_{u}|K_{21}(n;t+h,u)|^{2}\lesssim|h|\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+\varepsilon}\lesssim|h|^{2\eta}\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+2\alpha\eta+\varepsilon},

since
0<\eta<1/2
,
|h|\leq 1
, and
\langle n\rangle^{2\alpha\eta}\geq 1
. This proves (
C.33
). The case
h<0
is identical.
∎

It remains to make the cutoff-tail convergence of the infinite contraction explicit. The following lemma is the closed form of the missing tail estimate.

Lemma C.15

(Cauchy convergence of the infinite contraction tails)
.

Let
C_{1}^{\Lambda}
be the finite cutoff contraction in (
C.17
). For every

s<5\alpha-\frac{9}{2},

the family
C_{1}^{\Lambda}
is Cauchy in
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
almost surely.

Proof.

We separate the two possible tails.

Internal contraction tail.
Let
L
be a dyadic lower cutoff and let
C_{1,>L}
denote the part of the integrated kernel in which the contracted shell satisfies
M>L
. By Itô isometry and (
C.29
),

\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1,>L}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim_{T}\langle n\rangle^{-2\alpha}\left(\sum_{M\gtrsim\langle n\rangle,\ M>L}M^{3-4\alpha+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}.

(C.35)

For fixed
n
, this tends to zero as
L\to\infty
. We also need a summable dyadic majorant. The full bound is

\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1,>L}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim\langle n\rangle^{6-10\alpha+\varepsilon}.

(C.36)

This is precisely the majorant associated with the regularity
5\alpha-9/2-
.

To see uniform dyadic decay more explicitly, split the output dyadic shells into
N\leq L
and
N>L
. If
\langle n\rangle\sim N\leq L
, then (
C.35
) gives

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{1,>L}(n,t)|^{2}\lesssim N^{-2\alpha}L^{2(3-4\alpha+\varepsilon)}.

For
s=5\alpha-9/2-\kappa
, the contribution of these shells to the dyadic coefficient criterion is bounded, up to harmless losses, by

L^{6-8\alpha+}\sum_{N\leq L}N^{2s+3-2\alpha+}\lesssim L^{-2\kappa+}\to 0

if the plus losses are chosen smaller than
\kappa
. If
N>L
, the full majorant
N^{6-10\alpha+}
gives the tail

\sum_{N>L}N^{2s+3}N^{6-10\alpha+}\to 0

for the same range of
s
. The time-increment tail is identical, with the harmless additional factor
M^{2\alpha\eta}
and with
\eta
chosen small.

External remaining-color tail.
The finite cutoff also removes the remaining color-2 Brownian mode when
|n|>\Lambda
. This is merely an output-frequency tail. The bound (
C.31
), together with the increment estimate (
C.33
), shows that the dyadic output tail tends to zero in every
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{s}
with
s<5\alpha-9/2
.

Combining the internal and output tails proves that
(C_{1}^{\Lambda})_{\Lambda}
is Cauchy in the stated space by Lemma
C.1
.
∎

Conclusion and source bookkeeping consequence

The construction of

The contraction component is constructed as the integrated first-chaos Volterra term (

Remark C.16

(No divergent operator-level counterterm in the cubic block)
.

The term
C_{i}
is lower chaos, but it remains random and centered. The contraction removes the two same-color Gaussian variables and leaves the opposite-color stochastic convolution. Thus the cubic resonant symbol has the form

V_{3-i}\circ\Psi_{i}=\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}+C_{i},

with no scalar deterministic subtraction. The apparent infinite sum of contractions is finite only after the remaining stochastic convolution is inserted and the resulting Volterra kernel is summed; the summability comes from the phase-layer factor
M^{-\alpha}
, yielding the shell
M^{3-4\alpha+}
.

Remark C.17

(Regularity bottleneck)
.

The first-chaos contraction is smoother than the centered third chaos:

\left(5\alpha-\frac{9}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2}\right)=\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Therefore the resonant cubic source regularity is dictated by
\Gamma_{i}^{(3)}
, not by
C_{i}
. In the conservative source norm used in the main text, one requires

s_{2}-\alpha<\frac{9\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad s_{2}<\frac{11\alpha}{2}-\frac{9}{2},

up to the usual arbitrarily small losses.
