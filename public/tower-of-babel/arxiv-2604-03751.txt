Virtual element approximation of eigenvalue problems: is the stabilization of the right hand side necessary?
Daniele Boffi, Francesca Gardini, Lucia Gastaldi

Abstract
. The VEM approximation of eigenvalue problems usually involves the appropriate tuning of stabilization parameters, unless self-stabilizing or stabilization-free VEM are used. In this paper we prove that for elliptic self-adjoint eigenvalue problems the stabilization of the mass matrix is not necessary when lower order standard VEM spaces are adopted. Numerical evidence shows that also for higher order schemes the same result is true on various mesh sequences.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
65N30, 65N25

1.
Introduction

This paper deals with the virtual element approximation of eigenvalue
problems associated with partial differential equations. A typical variational
formulation for an eigenvalue problem seeks eigenvalues
\lambda

and non vanishing eigenfunctions
u
in a suitable functional space
V
such
that

a(u,v)=\lambda b(u,v)\qquad\forall v\in V.

In this exploratory work we consider an eigenvalue problem associated with a
self-adjoint elliptic operator. At the continuous level in most cases the
bilinear forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b(\cdot,\cdot)
are symmetric and
coercive. Standard conforming Galerkin methods lead to a matrix problem of the
form

\mathsf{A}\mathsf{x}=\lambda\mathsf{B}\mathsf{x}

with
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
symmetric and positive definite.

Several discretization schemes require suitable stabilizations of the bilinear
forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b(\cdot,\cdot)
. The virtual element method (VEM)
is one of those and we refer to
[
7
]
for a throughout discussion
of the risks originating from the modification of the bilinear forms. When the
matrices
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
contain parametric terms, spurious eigensolutions
are added to the spectrum and may pollute the results if the stabilization
parameters are not chosen appropriately.
The use of standard VEM spaces seems to require such stabilization: the
bilinear forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b(\cdot,\cdot)
are usually evaluated
using projection operators on polynomial spaces, ensuring consistency but
leading to unstable methods where the involved matrices
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}

could even become singular if not properly stabilized.
A look at the relevant analysis presented in
[
7
]
shows that a
naive idea that the stabilization parameters should be
large enough
is
prone to subtle drawbacks, more evident for
\mathsf{B}
than for
\mathsf{A}
.
Actually, increasing the stabilization parameter for the bilinear form

a(\cdot,\cdot)
has the effect of shifting the spurious eigenvalues to the
higher part of the spectrum, while increasing the stabilization parameter for
the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
may move the spurious eigenvalues towards
the lower part of the spectrum, thus polluting the typical window of interest
when eigenvalues close to the fundamental mode are sought.

The first papers dealing with VEM approximation of eigenvalue
problems
[
15
,
14
,
20
]
show the convergence of the
numerical scheme, as the meshsize
h
tends to zero, when the stabilizing
parameters are fixed. As explained above, it became apparent
soon
[
7
,
8
]
that asymptotic a priori estimates with a
prededermined choice of the parameters are not enough to guarantee that the
method is effective in practice. Already in
[
15
]
it can be seen that
spurious eigenvalues might appear with a wrong choice of the parameters.

In this context, several research directions are emerging, aiming at
identifying VEM schemes that are not affected by stabilization issues when
applied to eigenvalue problems. For instance, stabilization free or
self-stabilizing methods have been intensively studied during the last years
for the approximation of the source
problem
[
6
,
5
,
16
]
. This
could be a way of avoiding the tuning of parameters, even if questions remain
open about the optimal choice of the projection degree and the increased
polynomial degree of the projections may lead to a higher assembly time and to
a worse condition number of the involved matrices
[
13
]
. Research in
this direction are presented, for instance,
in
[
19
,
10
,
17
,
18
]
. In other cases, it has
been shown that schemes can work even if the right hand side matrix
\mathsf{B}
is
not stabilized
[
2
]
.

In any case, it is timely and interesting to discuss whether the stabilization
is needed when standard VEM spaces are used for the approximation of
eigenvalue problems. This is the aim of this paper and we focus on the
stabilization of the right hand side matrix
\mathsf{B}
that, as explained above,
is more critical than the stabilization of
\mathsf{A}
.

In Section
2
we describe the eigenvalue problem we are dealing
with, and we introduce our notation. We discuss the Descloux–Nassif–Rappaz
theory for the convergence analysis of non compact operators
[
11
]
. It
is not so uncommon to use this theory when non-conforming approximations of
compact operators are present. Moreover, the standard
analysis
[
9
]
cannot always be used in case of VEMs due to the
difficulty of defining the discrete solution operator for all functions in

L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The developed analysis holds in general and is
applied to VEM discretizations in Sections
3
and
4
.
Section
5
is the core of our contribution where we show that
for lower order degrees (
k=1,2
) the discrete eigensolutions converge even
when the right hand side matrix
\mathsf{B}
is not stabilized. Finally, a series
of numerical tests is reported in Section
6
for several choices of
mesh sequences. It can be appreciated that the method can converge also when
the matrix
\mathsf{B}
has a non trivial kernel. Moreover, it can be seen that the
convergence holds also beyond the theoretical results when higher order
schemes are used.

2.
Abstract setting

Let us consider two Hilbert spaces
V
and
H
with
V\subset H
, with dense and continuous embedding, endowed with norms
\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H}
, respectively. We introduce two symmetric and continuous bilinear forms
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
and
b:H\times H\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following assumptions:

•

there exists a positive constant
\alpha
such that for all
v\in V

(1)

a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_{V}^{2};

•

for all
u\in H
with
u\neq 0

(2)

b(u,u)>0.

We consider the following eigenvalue problem.

Problem 1
.

Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
u\in V
with
u\neq 0
satisfying

a(u,v)=\lambda b(u,v)\quad\forall v\in V.

The associated source problem reads as follows.

Problem 2
.

Given
f\in H
find
u\in V
such that

a(u,v)=b(f,v)\quad\forall v\in V.

Thanks to the coercivity and continuity assumptions, the Lax–Milgram lemma implies that Problem
2
is well-posed in the sense that there exists a unique solution
u\in V
such that

\|u\|_{V}\leq C\|f\|_{H}.

We denote by
V_{0}
be the space containing the solutions of Problem
2

for all
f\in H
, so that we have also the regularity estimate

(3)

\|u\|_{V_{0}}\leq C\|f\|_{H}.

The analysis of variationally posed eigenvalue problems typically relies on
the definition of the solution operator
T:V\to V
such that, for all
f\in V
,
Tf=u
with
u
solution of Problem
2
, that is

a(Tf,v)=b(f,v)\quad\forall v\in V.

We observe that
T
is self-adjoint. We have that
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of
T
if there exists
u\in V
with
u\neq 0
such that
Tu=\mu u
.
We assume that
T:V\to V
is a compact operator. Hence, the spectrum of
T
contains
0
and
a countable set of strictly positive eigenvalues
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(counted with their multiplicities) having only
0
as possible accumulation
point. If the range of
T
is not finite dimensional then the eigenvalues can
be sorted as a decreasing sequence converging to
0
as
n
goes to infinity.
It is well-known that these positive eigenvalues are the reciprocal of the
eigenvalues of Problem
1
, that is
\mu_{n}=1/\lambda_{n}
and

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\cdots,

and that the eigenspaces are the same.
Moreover, the corresponding eigenfunctions
u_{n}
are orthogonal with respect
to both the forms
a
and
b
(this is automatic for simple eigenvalues, while
it can be enforced in case of multiple ones), and we set that
b(u_{n},u_{n})=1
.

For the discretization of Problem
1
, let
V_{h}
be a finite dimensional subspace of
V
, and let us consider
discrete bilinear forms
a_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and
b_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
. We assume that
a_{h}
and
b_{h}
are continuous with respect to the
norms
\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H}
, respectively, and that there exists a positive constant
\underline{\alpha}
independent of
h
such that

(4)

a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\underline{\alpha}\|v_{h}\|_{V}^{2}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

With the above definitions, the discrete counterpart of Problem
1
reads as follows.

Problem 3
.

Find
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
u_{h}\in V_{h}
with
u_{h}\neq 0
satisfying

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Contrary to the continuous case, we do not require that
b_{h}
is strictly
positive (see assumption in
2
) but only non negative.
Therefore,
there might exist elements
w_{h}
in
V_{h}
such that
b_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}
. We call the set of such
w_{h}
’s the kernel of
b_{h}
and denote it as follows:

(5)

\mathbb{K}_{b,h}=\{w_{h}\in V_{h}:b_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\}.

The generalized algebraic eigenvalue system associated to Problem
3
is

(6)

\mathsf{A}\mathsf{x}=\mathsf{\lambda}\mathsf{B}\mathsf{x},

with
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
matrices of dimension
N_{h}:=\dim(V_{h})
, and
\mathsf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
. The matrix
\mathsf{B}
may be not full rank,
due to the fact that
b_{h}
can have a non trivial kernel
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
. In such case

\mathsf{\lambda}=\infty
is an eigenvalue of (
6
)
with multiplicity equal to the dimension of
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
. In our analysis, we are
going to avoid dealing with such infinite eigenvalues by restricting our
problem to a suitable subspace of
V_{h}
which does not contain elements of

\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
.

Similarly to the continuous case, we consider the associated discrete source problem and discrete solution operator.

Problem 4
.

Given
f_{h}\in V_{h}
, find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Thanks to the coercivity assumption (
4
), there exists a
unique solution to Problem
4
. We observe that if

f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
, then the right hand side
b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})
vanishes for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, so
that the solution
u_{h}
is zero.

The discrete solution operator
\widetilde{T}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
, is then defined for all

f_{h}\in V_{h}
by
\widetilde{T}_{h}f_{h}=u_{h}
, with
u_{h}
the unique solution to Problem
4
, that is

(7)

a_{h}(\widetilde{T}_{h}f_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

The eigenvalues
\widetilde{\mu}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
of
\widetilde{T}_{h}
satisfy that there exists

\widetilde{u}_{h}\in V_{h}
, with
\widetilde{u}_{h}\neq 0
, such that

\widetilde{T}_{h}\widetilde{u}_{h}=\widetilde{\mu}_{h}\widetilde{u}_{h}
. Since
V_{h}
is of
dimension
N_{h}
, we have exactly
N_{h}
eigenvalues of

\widetilde{T}_{h}
. We can have that
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}\neq\{0\}
, so that
\widetilde{T}_{h}
admits the null eigenvalue with multiplicity equal to
\dim(\mathbb{K}_{b,h})
and
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
is the associated eigenspace. If
\widetilde{\mu}_{h}\neq 0
, then
\widetilde{u}_{h}
does not belong to
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
and

\left(\lambda_{h},\widetilde{u}_{h}\right)
with
\lambda_{h}=\dfrac{1}{\widetilde{\mu}_{h}}
is an eigensolution of Problem
3
,
that is

(8)

a_{h}(\widetilde{u}_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{h}(\widetilde{u}_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Taking
v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
in (
8
), we obtain that

a_{h}(\widetilde{u}_{h},v_{h})=0
, therefore
\widetilde{u}_{h}
is orthogonal
to
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
with respect to the scalar product induced by
a_{h}
. We denote by
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
such space, with the following definition

(9)

\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}=\{w_{h}\in V_{h}:a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}\}.

We characterize the action of
\widetilde{T}_{h}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

The operator
\widetilde{T}_{h}
maps the space
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
into itself.

Proof.

Let us consider
f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
, then by definition of
\widetilde{T}_{h}
, see (
7
), we
have that for all
v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}

a_{h}(\widetilde{T}_{h}f_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})=0,

hence due to (
9
)
\widetilde{T}_{h}f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
.
∎

The restriction of
\widetilde{T}_{h}
to the space
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
is denoted by
T_{h}:\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}\to\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
and is defined as:

(10)

\forall f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}\text{ find }T_{h}f_{h}\in V_{h}:\quad a_{h}(T_{h}f_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}.

The eigenmodes of the operator
T_{h}
are such that
T_{h}u_{h}=\mu_{h}u_{h}
. Hence

T_{h}
admits exactly
N_{h}-\dim(\mathbb{K}_{b,h})
strictly positive eigenvalues
\mu_{h}
.
The pair
(\lambda_{h},u_{h})
with
\lambda_{h}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{h}}
is an eigenmode of the following variational
discrete eigenvalue problem.

Problem 5
.

Find
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
u_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
with
u_{h}\neq 0
satisfying

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}.

In order to discuss the convergence of the discrete eigenmodes of
Problem
5
to those of Problem
1
,
we use the abstract theory of
[
11
]
. In particular, we consider
the following two properties:

P1:

\displaystyle\sup_{f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}}\dfrac{\|(T-T_{h})f_{h}\|_{V}}{\|f_{h}\|_{V}}

tends to
0
as
h
goes to
0
;

P2:

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{V}
tends to
0
as
h
goes
to
0
for all
u\in V_{0}
, where
V_{0}
is the space containing the solutions of
Problem
2
.

The following theorem was proved in
[
11
]
:

Theorem 2
.

Let us assume that properties
\mathbf{P1}
and
\mathbf{P2}
are satisfied, then the
eigenvalues of Problem
5
converge to those of
Problem
1
in the sense that for any
\lambda
of multiplicity

m
solution of Problem
1
there exist exactly
m
discrete
eigenvalues solution of Problem
5
converging to it as
h\to 0
.

Remark 1
.

An analogous theorem holds also for the convergence of the eigenspaces. As
usual, in case of a multiple eigenvalue
\lambda
, the discrete eigenspaces
associated with all the eigenvalues converging to
\lambda
should be
considered.

Remark 2
.

In this paper we address the issue of the convergence (and absence of spurious
modes) without investigating the rate of convergence. Actually, once the
convergence is assured, estimating the rate can be done, for instance, using
the tools of
[
12
]
and is usually an easier task.

3.
Property P1 for general VEM approximation of elliptic eigenvalue
problem

As an example for the situation illustrated in the above section, we consider
the Virtual Element Method (VEM) for approximating an elliptic
partial differential equation. In
this case the bilinear forms
a_{h}
and
b_{h}
have to be chosen carefully and
stabilization techniques are usually introduced in order that the
matrices in (
6
) are positive definite.
However, the stabilization procedure introduces parameters in the discrete
formulation of the problem and optimal choices of them could be a difficult
task, as it has been pointed out in
[
7
]
. Actually the main
troubles arise in connection with the choice of the stabilization parameter of

b_{h}
, as it can be seen in
[
15
, Fig. 7]
. Therefore here we
discuss the VEM discretization of the Poisson equation avoiding the
stabilization of
b_{h}
.

A typical virtual element method relies on a polygonal decomposition of the
domain.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open connected polygonal domain and let us
introduce a family of decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Omega
into non overlapping
polygons
E
of an arbitrary number of edges satisfying the following standard
assumptions. We denote by
h_{e}
the length of the edge
e
for

e\subset\partial E
, by
h_{E}
the diameter of
E
and by
h=\max\{h_{E},\ E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
the mesh size.
We assume that there exists a constant
\delta_{1}>0
independent of
h
such that

h\leq\delta_{1}h_{e}
for all
e\subset\partial E
with
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Each
E
is star shaped with respect to a ball with radius
\rho_{E}
, and
there exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that
\rho_{E}\geq\delta_{2}h_{E}
. The notation

\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
stands for the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
on the
element
E
, while
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
stands for the space of piecewise polynomials
of degree at most
k
on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

In order to deal with the virtual element discretization we need that the
Hilbert spaces
H
and
V
contain functions defined on
\Omega
and enjoy
some local properties as follows. For
any subset
D
of
\Omega
, both
H
and
V
can be restricted to
D
.
We denote such restriction
H(D)
and
V(D)
, and the corresponding
norms
\|\cdot\|_{H(D)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V(D)}
, respectively. Similarly, the
bilinear forms
a
and
b
can be restricted to any subdomain
D\subset\Omega
.

Following
[
3
]
, we introduce the basic properties of the virtual
element discretization for a general elliptic problem (see
Problem
2
).

We consider a finite dimensional subspace
V_{h}\subset V
constructed in such a
way that its restriction
V_{h}(E)
to each element
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
contains
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)

with
k\geq 1
.
Then, we introduce the continuous bilinear forms
a_{h}
and
b_{h}
from

V_{h}\times V_{h}
to
\mathbb{R}
which are continuous in
V
and in
H
, respectively.
Moreover they satisfy the following local properties

(11)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a^{E}_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})\quad\forall v_{h},w_{h}\in V_{h}

\displaystyle b_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}b^{E}_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})\quad\forall v_{h},w_{h}\in V_{h}.

The local bilinear form
a^{E}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is assumed to be consistent and
stable, that is:

(12)

a^{E}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=a^{E}(u_{h},v_{h})

whenever
u_{h}
or
v_{h}
belongs to
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
, and

(13)

\underline{C}a^{E}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq a^{E}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\overline{C}a^{E}(v_{h},v_{h})

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}(E)
.

From the stability property we have that
a_{h}
is coercive on
V
since it
inherits this property from the continuous bilinear form
a
. This together
with the continuity of
b_{h}
implies that there exists a unique solution of the
discrete source Problem
4
with

\|u_{h}\|_{V}\leq C\|f\|_{H}.

The following approximation properties of the VEM space are useful for showing
the convergence of the discrete solution to the continuous one. We recall that

V_{0}
is the subspace of
V
containing the solutions of
Problem
2
for
f\in H
. We denote by
V_{0}(D)
the
subspace of the restrictions of the elements in
V_{0}
to any
D\subset\Omega

endowed with norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{0}(D)}
.

Assumption 1
.

There exists
\omega_{1}(h)
tending to
0
as
h\to 0
, such that for
w\in V_{0}

there exists a piecewise polynomial element
w_{\pi}
with
w_{\pi}|_{E}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
satisfying

\|w-w_{\pi}\|_{V(E)}\leq\omega_{1}(h)\|w\|_{V_{0}(E)}

for all
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Assumption 2
.

There exists
\omega_{2}(h)
tending to
0
as
h\to 0
, such that for
w\in V_{0}

one can find an interpolant
w^{I}\in V_{h}\subset V
satisfying

\|w-w^{I}\|_{V(E)}\leq\omega_{2}(h)\|w\|_{V_{0}(E)}

for all
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

The next assumption takes care of the consistency error that we might introduce
using the bilinear form
b_{h}
instead of
b
.

Assumption 3
.

There exists
\omega_{3}(h)
tending to
0
as
h\to 0
, such that for any

f\in V

\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(f,v_{h})-b_{h}(f,v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{V}}\leq\omega_{3}(h)\|f\|_{V}.

With the above abstract setting we are now in the
position of proving property
\mathbf{P1}
for
T
and
T_{h}
defined in
Section
2
.

Proposition 3
.

If Assumptions
1
–
3
hold true, then
there exists
\omega(h)
tending to
0
as
h\to 0
, such that for all

f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
it holds

\|(T-T_{h})f_{h}\|_{V}\leq\omega(h)\|f_{h}\|_{V}.

Proof.

Given
f_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
, let us consider
u=Tf_{h}
and
u_{h}=T_{h}f_{h}
which are the solution of the
following equations

(14)

\displaystyle a(u,v)=b(f_{h},v)\qquad\forall v\in V

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}

Let
u^{I}\in V_{h}
be the interpolant of
u
defined in Assumption
2
,
then we can use the ellipticity of
a_{h}
and write

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}(u_{h}-u^{I},u_{h}-u^{I})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h}-u^{I})-a_{h}(u^{I},u_{h}-u^{I})

\displaystyle=b_{h}(f_{h},u_{h}-u^{I})-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a^{E}_{h}(u^{I},u_{h}-u^{I}).

We evaluate the last term introducing the piecewise
\mathbb{P}_{k}
approximation

u_{\pi}
of
u
(see Assumption
1
) and taking into account the
consistency of
a_{h}
, see (
12
)

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a^{E}_{h}(u^{I},u_{h}-u^{I})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(a^{E}_{h}(u^{I}-u_{\pi},u_{h}-u^{I})+a^{E}(u_{\pi},u_{h}-u^{I})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u^{I}-u\|_{V}+\|u-u_{\pi}\|_{h})\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}

\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(a^{E}(u_{\pi}-u,u_{h}-u^{I})+a^{E}(u,u_{h}-u^{I})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u^{I}-u\|_{V}+\|u-u_{\pi}\|_{h})\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}+a(u,u_{h}-u^{I}),

where

\|\cdot\|_{h}=\left(\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\cdot\|_{V(E)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.
We put together the last two relations, using (
14
) and we
get

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u^{I}-u\|_{V}+\|u-u_{\pi}\|_{h})\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}

\displaystyle+b_{h}(f_{h},u_{h}-u^{I})-b(f_{h},u_{h}-u^{I}).

Due to Assumptions
1
and
2
, the first term on the right
hand side is bounded by
(\omega_{1}(h)+\omega_{2}(h))\|u\|_{V_{0}}
. To
evaluate the difference between
b_{h}
and
b
we use Assumption
3

as follows

b_{h}(f_{h},u_{h}-u^{I})-b(f_{h},u_{h}-u^{I})\leq\omega_{3}(h)\|f_{h}\|_{V}\|u_{h}-u^{I}\|_{V}

where we used that
f_{h}\in V_{h}\subset V
.

Hence we have obtained the desired estimate with

\omega(h)=\omega_{1}(h)+\omega_{2}(h)+\omega_{3}(h).

∎

4.
Virtual elements in practice

We consider an eigenvalue problem associated to a second order elliptic
diffusion-reaction operator. Without losing generality we restrict ourselves to
the eigenvalue problem associated with the Laplace operator: find

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
u\neq 0
satisfying

(15)

(\nabla u,\nabla v)=\lambda(u,v)\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Problem (
15
) fits into the abstract framework presented in
Section
2
with
V=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
a(u,v)=(\nabla u,\nabla v)
and

b(u,v)=(u,v)
. The solution operator
T:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined as follows:
given
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
Tf=u
where
u
is the solution of the equation:

(16)

a(u,v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Since, for polygonal domains, the solution of (
16
)
belongs to
H^{1+s}(\Omega)
for some
s>1/2
we have that

V_{0}\subset H^{1+s}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the following bound holds

(17)

\|u\|_{1+s}\leq C\|f\|_{0}.

Since
H^{1+s}(\Omega)
is compactly
embedded into
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
the solution operator
T
results to be compact.

4.1.
Virtual element method

We consider a decomposition of
\Omega
into polygons as described at the
beginning of the previous section.
In each polygon
E
, we define a local virtual space for
k\geq 1
as follows,

(18)

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}(E)=

\displaystyle\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(E):v_{h}\in C^{0}(\partial E),\ v_{h}|_{e}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(e)\ \forall e\in\partial E,

\displaystyle\Delta v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(E),\ ({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}-v_{h},q)_{E}=0\ \forall q\in\mathcal{M}^{k,k-1}(E)\}.

Here
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}
is the projection operator from
H^{1}(E)
to
\mathbb{P}^{k}(E)\subset V_{h}^{k}(E)
such that

(19)

\displaystyle a^{E}({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v,q)=a^{E}(v,q)\quad\forall q\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(E),

\displaystyle({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v,1)_{E}=(v,1)_{E},\text{ for }k\geq 2

\displaystyle({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v,1)_{\partial E}=(v,1)_{\partial E},\text{ for }k=1,

with
a^{E}(v,w)=(\nabla v,\nabla w)_{E}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}^{k}(E)
is
the subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{k}(E)
containing monomials of degree
k
.
We refer to
[
1
]
for the detailed construction of the local virtual
element space and the definition of the degrees of freedom.

Remark 3
.

By definition, the local virtual element space contains not only polynomials of
degree
k
, but also
virtual
unknown functions.
Using the degrees of freedom, we have that
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}
is computable
for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(E)
, so that we can evaluate
a^{E}({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}u_{h},{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h})

for all
u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(E)
, but the associated algebraic matrix could be
singular since we can have
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}=0
for some
v_{h}\neq 0
.

The usual stabilization of
a^{E}
involves a
suitable bilinear form
S^{E}
with the following property: there exist positive
constants
\underline{C}
and
\overline{C}
satisfying

(20)

\underline{C}a^{E}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq S^{E}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\overline{C}a^{E}(v_{h},v_{h})

for all
v_{h}
belonging to the kernel of
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}
.

Hence, we use the following discrete version of the bilinear form

a^{E}

(21)

a^{E}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=a^{E}({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}u_{h},{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h})+S^{E}((u_{h}-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}u_{h},v_{h}-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}).

We observe that if either
u_{h}
or
v_{h}
in the above equations belong to

\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
then the local discrete bilinear form
a^{E}_{h}
satifies both the
consistency and stability assumptions, see (
12
)
and (
13
), respectively.

Analogous considerations hold for the bilinear form
b^{E}
.
Setting
b^{E}(v,w)=(v,w)_{E}
, the projection operator

\Pi^{0}_{k}:L^{2}(E)\to\mathbb{P}^{k}(E)\subset V_{h}^{k}(E)
is used as follows

(22)

b^{E}(\Pi^{0}_{k}v,q)=b^{E}(v,q)\quad\forall q\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(E).

We have that
\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}
is computable for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(E)
using the degrees
of freedom.
Then, the discrete version of
b^{E}
is

(23)

b^{E}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=b^{E}(\Pi^{0}_{k}u_{h},\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h})\quad\forall u_{h},\,v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}(E).

Notice that, differently than the bilinear form
a^{E}_{h}
, we do not add a
stabilization term to
b^{E}_{h}
.

The global VEM space is then given by:

V_{h}^{k}=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):v|_{E}\in V_{h}^{k}(E)\ \forall E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\\

In the following, with abuse of
notation, we denote by
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}
also the global projector operator
so that, for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
,
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
coincides
with the local projector when restricted to an element. Similarly, for

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we denote by
\Pi^{0}_{k}f
the piecewise
L^{2}
-projector onto

\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Therefore, the bilinear form
b_{h}
is well defined also in

L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Given
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
, its projection
\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}
could vanish on each element
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
and we define the kernel of
b_{h}
as follows

(24)

\mathbb{K}_{b,h}=\{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}:b_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}\}=\{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}:\Pi^{0}_{k}w_{h}=0\}.

We recall basic results for the VEM approximation which show that
Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied,
see
[
3
,
4
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Under the assumptions on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, for
w\in H^{1+s}(E)
with
0<s\leq k

there exists an element
w_{\pi}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
satisfying

\|w-w_{\pi}\|_{0,E}+h_{E}|w-w_{\pi}|_{1,E}\leq Ch_{E}^{s+1}|w|_{s+1,E}

for a suitable constant
C>0
independent of
h_{E}
.

Using the degrees of freedom defined above, one can define an interpolant

w^{I}\in V_{h}^{k}
for all
w\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
with
0<s\leq k
such that the
following approximation property holds true.

Lemma 5
.

Under the assumptions on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, there exists a constant
C>0

independent of
h
such that for
w\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
with
0<s\leq k
,
there exists an interpolant
w^{I}\in V_{h}^{k}
satisfying

\|w-w^{I}\|_{0,E}+h_{E}|w-w^{I}|_{1,E}\leq Ch_{E}^{s+1}|w|_{s+1,E}\quad\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

4.2.
Discrete eigenvalue problem and solution operator

With the definitions given in the previous section, the discrete counterpart of
the eigenvalue problem (
15
) reads: find
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that there exists
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
with
u_{h}\neq 0
satisfying

(25)

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

We associate to the eigenvalue problem (
25
), the discrete source
problem as follows: given
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
such that

(26)

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f,v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

It is well-known that problem (
26
) admits a unique solution,
see
[
3
,
4
]
.

Lemma 6
.

For all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
there exists a unique solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
of
problem (
26
) with the following a priori estimate

\|u_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq C\|f\|_{0,\Omega}.

In the following lemma we report the error estimate for the VEM approximation
of the source problem, provided by
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
be the solutions to problems (
16
)
and (
26
), respectively.
There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
such that

|u-u_{h}|_{1,\Omega}\leq C\left(|u-u^{I}|_{1,\Omega}+|u-u_{\pi}|_{h,\Omega}+\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}}\frac{b(f,v_{h})-b_{h}(f,v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\right),

where
|\cdot|_{h,\Omega}
stands for the broken
H^{1}
-seminorm.

The discrete problem (
26
) fits into the framework of
Section
2
. Indeed,
a_{h}
is coercive and continuous and
b_{h}
is
continuous. In addition, the bilinear form
b_{h}
might not be positive due to
its construction which makes use of the projector
\Pi^{0}_{k}
. Since we
are not adding any stabilization term, the kernel
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}

(see (
5
)) might not be
reduced to the
0
element, as we shall show in the numerical results section.

Let us recall the solution operator associated to (
26
). In view
of the solution of the eigenvalue problem, following the discussion of
Section
2
, we consider
f_{h}
in

\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}=\{w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}:a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=0\ \forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}\},

the orthogonal complement of
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
with respect to
a_{h}
, see (
9
).
Then
T_{h}:\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}\to V_{h}^{k}
maps elements of
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
to the solution of the following
equation:

(27)

a_{h}(T_{h}f_{h},v_{h})=b_{h}(f_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

As observed in Lemma
1
,
T_{h}:\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}\to\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
.

It remains to show that properties
\mathbf{P1}
and
\mathbf{P2}
hold true
for the VEM approximation of the eigensolutions of problem (
15
).
In the following section, we shall investigate the validity of these two
properties depending on the degree of the virtual element spaces and on the
type of meshes.

5.
Convergence analysis

In this section, we prove properties
\mathbf{P1}
and
\mathbf{P2}
for
T

and
T_{h}
defined, respectively, in Subsection
4
and
in (
27
). In order to show that
\mathbf{P1}
holds true,
we apply Proposition
3
. Since Assumptions
1

and
2
have been already checked, it remains to show that
Assumption
3
is verified.

Lemma 8
.

For any
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we have

\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(f,v_{h})-b_{h}(f,v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\leq Ch\|f\|_{1}.

Proof.

By definition of the bilinear forms
b_{h}
and
b
, we have

\displaystyle b_{h}(f,v_{h})-b(f,v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}b^{E}(\Pi^{0}_{k}f-f,v_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\Pi^{0}_{k}f-f\|_{0,E}\|v_{h}\|_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\|f\|_{1}\|v_{h}\|_{1},

where we used the continuity of
b^{E}
and that
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

We collect the previous results in the following statement.

Theorem 9
.

For any mesh satisfying the assumptions described at the beginning of
Section
3
, property
\mathbf{P1}
holds true.

The proof of property
\mathbf{P2}
depends on the number of the edges of the
polygons of the mesh and on the degree of polynomials in the VEM space
V_{h}^{k}
.
In the following proposition, we show that property
\mathbf{P2}
holds true for
polygons of any number of edges and for
k=1
and
k=2
.

Proposition 10
.

Let us consider
k=1
and
k=2
, then for any mesh satisfying the
assumptions described at the beginning of Section
3
, property

\mathbf{P2}
holds true.

Proof.

Our proof is based on the property that
{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}=\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}
for
k=1,2
, as
it is observed in
[
1
]
. In order to show this fact,
we recall the definition of the local virtual space
V_{h}^{k}(E)
as
in (
18
).

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}(E)=

\displaystyle\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(E):v_{h}\in C^{0}(\partial E),\ v_{h}|_{e}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(e)\ \forall e\in\partial E,

\displaystyle\Delta v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(E),\ ({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h}-v_{h},q)_{E}=0\ \forall q\in\mathcal{M}^{k,k-1}(E)\}.

Therefore, for
k=1
, the definition of the local space implies that

({\Pi_{1}^{\nabla}}v_{h}-v_{h},q)_{E}=0\quad\forall q\in\mathcal{M}^{1,0}(E),

which corresponds to the definition of
\Pi^{0}_{1}
as the
L^{2}
-projection from

L^{2}(E)
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}(E)
. On the other hand, for
k=2
the enhanced condition
reads

({\Pi_{2}^{\nabla}}v_{h}-v_{h},q)_{E}=0\quad\forall q\in\mathcal{M}^{2,1}(E),

moreover from (
19
) we also have
({\Pi_{2}^{\nabla}}v_{h}-v_{h},1)_{E}=0
. These
conditions ensure that
{\Pi_{2}^{\nabla}}
satisfies the same moment constraints as
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

To prove property
\mathbf{P2}
, for any
v\in V_{0}
we will find an element

\bar{v}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}
such that
\|v-\bar{v}\|_{1}
tends to zero as
h\to 0
. We
remind that (
17
) ensures that
V_{0}
,
the space of the solution of the source equation (
16
), is contained in
H^{1+s}(\Omega)
.

Let
v\in V_{0}\subset H^{1+s}(\Omega)
, we can take
\bar{v}
as the
interpolant
v^{I}\in V_{h}^{k}
as defined in Lemma
5
.
We decompose
v^{I}
into the sum
v^{I}=v^{0}+v^{\perp}
with
v^{0}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
and
\ v^{\perp}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}

defined as the following projections:

(28)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v^{0},w)=a_{h}(v^{I},w)\quad

\displaystyle\forall w\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}

\displaystyle a_{h}(v^{\perp},w)=a_{h}(v^{I},w)\quad

\displaystyle\forall w\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}^{\perp}.

Since
v^{0}\in\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
, then
\Pi^{0}_{k}v^{0}=0
(see (
24
)) and also

{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v^{0}=0
for
k=1,2
. Hence we have

(29)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v^{0},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(a^{E}({\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v^{0},{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v_{h})+S^{E}((I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{0},(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v_{h})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}S^{E}(v^{0},(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v_{h}).

We now show that
\|v-v^{\perp}\|_{1}
tends to zero as
h\to 0
. By triangular
inequality we have that

(30)

\|v-v^{\perp}\|_{1}\leq\|v-v^{I}\|_{1}+\|v^{0}\|_{1},

hence, thanks to Lemma
5
, it remains to show that
\|v^{0}\|_{1}
tends to zero.
Using the ellipticity of
a_{h}
(
13
), the decomposition of

v^{I}
(
28
),
and (
29
), and the properties of the stabilization bilinear
form (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}\|v^{0}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}(v^{0},v^{0})=a_{h}(v^{I},v^{0})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}S^{E}((I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I},v^{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}S^{E}((I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I},(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I})^{1/2}S^{E}(v^{0},v^{0})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I}\|_{1,E}\|v^{0}\|_{1,E}.

We estimate the term
\|(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I}\|_{1,E}
, taking into account that

{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}
is the elliptic projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(E)
,
see (
19
). Therefore, for
v\in H^{1+s}(E)
, it holds that
\|v-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v\|_{1,E}\leq Ch^{s}\|v\|_{1+s,E}
.
Hence, using Lemma
5
, we can write

\displaystyle\|(I-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}})v^{I}\|_{1,E}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{I}-v\|_{1,E}+\|v-{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}v\|_{1,E}+\|{\Pi_{k}^{\nabla}}(v-v^{I})\|_{1,E}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\|v\|_{1+s,E}.

Summing on
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain that

\|v^{0}\|_{1}\leq Ch^{s}\|v\|_{1+s},

which, together with (
30
), implies property
\mathbf{P2}
.

∎

We conclude by considering a less standard mesh, called
dyadic
and labeled by
\mathcal{D}
, obtained by dividing the unit square into
N^{2}
squares. Each square is considered as a
polygon with 8 edges by adding the midpont to each edge. Therefore the number of vertices is
(3N+1)(N+1)
. The mesh corresponding to

N=4
is depicted in Figure
2
, where we highlighted the vertices and the edges.

Coarsest dyadic mesh

21
displays the dimensions of
\mathbb{K}_{b,h}
and of
V_{h}

N
ranging from
4
to
64
,
and of the degree
k
from
1
to
4
. In this case we have that the kernel is
not trivial for all the degrees of the polynomials contained in the VEM space,
however we have no spurious eigenvalues. This is predicted by our theory for
the cases
k=1,2
, while for

we have only numerical evidence. Moreover,

-

close to machine precision.
