The “Ticking Time Bomb” Within
Authors: Fawaz Al-Mufti
Published: 2026

About the Artist

Dr Fawaz Al-Mufti is a triple board-certified physician-artist whose work explores the uncertainty, fragility, and resilience inherent in neurologic disease, as well as the social and cultural dynamics that shape the human experience. Drawing from his experiences at the bedside in the angiography suite, and in humanitarian and conflict settings, he transforms lived experiences into visual narratives of vulnerability, rupture, recovery, and healing.

About the Technique

Using India ink on Bristol board, the artwork aptly exemplifies the complexity and intricacies of the cerebral vasculature. The human head is deconstructed into a lattice of light and color through a mosaic lens, reflecting both the intricate architecture of the cerebrovascular system and the internal struggle that follows diagnosis.

Artist Perspective

This piece represents a convergence of cerebrovascular anatomy, pathology, and perception. It explores the tension between perception and reality in pathology, where subtle nuances may carry uncertain risks yet are often framed in language that exacerbates fear. With the intent of conveying urgency, patients are often told they harbor a “ticking time bomb,” a phrase I try to avoid at all costs, as it can profoundly shape perception, transform uncertainty into perceived inevitability, and risk into fear. It may trigger a progression of DABDA: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and ultimately acceptance. Like the cadence of tachycardia, DABDA. DABDA. DABDA. A relentless pulse, reverberating through the uncertainty that follows diagnosis. The mosaics within the aneurysm evoke flow, fragility, and the delicate thresholds preceding rupture.

At its core, this work reflects the burden of uncertainty. To treat is to accept procedural risk; not to treat is to accept the unknowns of natural history. These are not abstract probabilities but deeply personal decisions, often made under the weight of language that may overstate certainty or danger. For neurointerventionalists, this uncertainty is central to our practice. We are tasked not only with identifying and managing risk, but with communicating it responsibly.

This piece invites reflection on the importance of measured, transparent counseling, grounded in evidence, individualized risk, and trust, so that patients are empowered, not alarmed, as they navigate complex cerebrovascular disease. Ultimately, it challenges us to reconsider not only how we treat disease, but how we frame it. Between rupture and nonrupture, certainty and ambiguity, intervention and observation, lies a space where communication becomes as critical as technique, and where the words we choose may shape outcomes as profoundly as the procedures we perform.
