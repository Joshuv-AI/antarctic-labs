Distribution of NetB toxin in chickens infected with Clostridium Perfringens and cytotoxicity study of NetB toxin
Authors: Xin-yue Kong, Ya-nan Song, Hai-rong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAvian necrotic enteritis (NE) causes significant economic losses to the global poultry industry. Clostridium perfringens NetB toxin is a key virulence factor causing necrotizing enteritis in chickens, but the receptor of the toxin remains unknown, and the target organ of the toxin and its distribution in chickens are still unclear. To explore the distribution pattern and pathogenic mechanism of NetB toxin in the host, firstly in the study, by using a monoclonal antibody against NetB toxin, a specific immunohistochemical detection method and a double antibody sandwich method (DAS-ELISA) was established to dynamically monitor the distribution of NetB toxin in chicks after infection of Clostridium perfringens type G. The immunohistochemical results showed that NetB toxin was detected in the heart, lungs, spleen, kidneys, liver and small intestine 3 days after bacterial infection, the positive signals in the jejunal villi and lamina propria were the strongest. Toxins still could be detected in the liver and small intestine on the sixth day after infection. By the 14th day, only a small amount of toxin was detected in the intestinal tract. The DAS-ELISA test indicated that the concentration of NetB toxin in the serum was 10.37 ± 1.42 ng/mL 6 h after infection, and then decreased over time. Among the intestinal content samples, the toxin concentration in the jejunum contents was the highest, reaching 38.91 ± 2.36 ng/mL. Toxins in the contents of duodenal, ileum, jejunum and cecum could be detected 3 to 14 days after infection, but gradually decreased over time. Based on the above, this study conducted a treat test on chicken embryo hepatocytes using soluble NetB toxin expressed by Escherichia coli. The results indicated that the recombinant NetB toxin had a toxic effect on primary chicken embryo hepatocytes, leading to the release of lactate dehydrogenase, a decrease in cell activity, and destruction of cell morphology. The results of this study indicate that Clostridium perfringens NetB toxin not only causes pathogenic effects in the intestinal tract, but also is distributed in important organs such as the liver, spleen and kidney, and may cause pathogenic effects on them.Keywords: Clostridium perfringens, NetB toxin, Immunohistochemistry, Antigen distribution, Cytotoxicity

Introduction

Clostridium perfringens is a conditional pathogenic bacterium that can produce various exotoxins or enzymes, leading to multiple diseases in humans and animals. Avian necrotic enteritis (NE) is a disease caused by Clostridium perfringens that primarily affects the intestinal tract of chickens, characterized by intestinal hemorrhage, ulcers, and necrosis (Fathima et al., 2022). This disease is widespread globally and causes significant economic losses to the poultry industry. According to relevant statistics, the global poultry industry suffers approximately $6 billion in losses annually due to this disease (To et al., 2017; Wade et al., 2015).

In 2008, NetB (Necrotic Enteritis B-like, NetB) toxin was discovered from a strain of Clostridium perfringens isolated from chickens with necrotic enteritis (Keyburn et al., 2008). In recent years, increasing research has demonstrated that the NetB toxin is a key virulence factor causing avian necrotic enteritis (Keyburn et al., 2010; Chalmers et al., 2008; Lee et al., 2021; Shojadoost et al., 2012). Clostridium perfringens strains secreting the NetB toxin are designated as type G Clostridium perfringens (Rood et al., 2018). Studies have shown that the NetB toxin is a member of the β-pore-forming toxin family, and its pathogenic action requires binding to receptors on host cells (Chalmers et al., 2008; Lee et al., 2021). However, the receptor for the NetB toxin remains unclear, the target organs and distribution of the toxin are not well defined (Lee et al., 2021), and the receptor and distribution of the toxin are closely related to its pathogenic mechanism.

The presence of the NetB toxin were detected in chicken serum samples, suggesting that the toxin enters the bloodstream from the intestine and may cause damage to organs beyond the gut (Lee et al., 2021). Compared to simply detecting toxin concentration in blood, immunohistochemical detection of the toxin offers unique and irreplaceable advantages. Combined with samples from different time points, immunohistochemistry can dynamically display the process of how the toxin is absorbed, transported, distributed to target organs, and ultimately cleared after entering the body, much like "surveillance footage." Furthermore, by observing the proportion of cells with positive signals and the staining intensity, the load of the toxin in tissues can be assessed. To better understand the role and mechanism of the NetB toxin in the pathogenesis of chicken necrotic enteritis, this study aimed to establish an immunohistochemistry method, combing with a double-antibody sandwich ELISA method, to detect the dynamic distribution pattern of the NetB toxin in different organs and tissues of infected chickens, additionally, the toxic effect of the NetB toxin on primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes was evaluated through cell experiments in vitro.

Materials and methods

Main experimental materials and experimental animals

Clostridium perfringens type A standard strain NCTC 528, Type G Clostridium perfringens strain 7a9-2, Escherichia coli strain BL21 transformed with the recombinant expression plasmid pGEX-6p-1-NetB, Hybridoma cells secreting monoclonal antibodies against NetB toxin (1E2), and Rabbit polyclonal antibodies against NetB toxin were prepared and preserved by the Environmental Microbiology Laboratory of Shandong Agricultural University.

E. coli strain BL21 transformed with the recombinant expression plasmid pGEX-6p-1-NetB were prepared as follows: The NetB gene sequence optimized for E. coli expression was synthesized and cloned into the prokaryotic expression vector pGEX-6p-1, using homologous recombination. Proving by restriction enzyme digestion and sequencing verification, the pGEX-NetB recombinant plasmid was successfully constructed and transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3) competent cell. The recombinant NetB protein was successfully expressed by the transformed E. coli BL21 (Ren, 2023).

Hybridoma cells secreting monoclonal antibodies against NetB toxin (1E2) were prepared as follows: The recombinant NetB protein expressed by the transformed E. coli BL21 were purified by using Ni-chelate resin and used to immunize BALB/c mice; after three immunizations, when the titers of antibody in the mouse serum were all higher than 1:102,800, spleen cells from mice with high titers were fused with myeloma cell. The resulting hybridoma cells underwent four rounds of subcloning, yielding a hybridoma cell line (1E2) with high specificity and good reactogenicity to NetB toxin of Clostridium perfringens (Ren, 2023).

Rabbit polyclonal antibodies against the NetB fusion protein were prepared as follows: The expressed recombinant NetB protein was purified and used to immunize New Zealand White rabbits for three immunizations, yielding immunoserum (titer higher than 1:204,800). High-titer polyclonal antibodies of rabbits were obtained and purified by affinity chromatography (Ren, 2023) .

Chicken coccidiosis tetravalent live vaccine was purchased from Guangdong Zhengdian Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; SPF female BALB/c mice (6-7 weeks old) were purchased from Jinan Pengyue Experimental Animal Breeding Co., Ltd.; 1-day-old black chickens were purchased from Taian Yurong Poultry Industry. Fluid Thioglycollate Medium (FTG) and Blood Agar Base No. 2 Medium were purchased from Qingdao Haibo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) assay kit was purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and CCK-8 kit was purchased from Shanghai Saint-Bio Co.

Preparation of NetB toxin monoclonal antibody and establishment of double-antibody sandwich ELISA method

Preparation of monoclonal antibodies against NetB toxin

The NetB toxin hybridoma cells (1E2) preserved in the laboratory were resuscitated and injected intraperitoneally into mice. Ascites was collected, and the supernatant was taken. The ascites was then purified using Protein A/G magnetic beads. The specificity of the monoclonal antibody was identified by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and WB. Finally, the antibody titer was detected by indirect ELISA.

Purification of recombinant NetB toxin protein

The bacterial culture of E. coli strain BL21 transformed with the recombinant expression plasmid pGEX-6p-1-NetB preserved in the laboratory was spread on LB solid medium (Amp+). After incubation at 37 °C for 14 h, single colonies were picked and inoculated into LB liquid medium. When the OD600nm value reached 0.4-0.6, IPTG was added to a final concentration of 0.6 mM, and expression was induced at 16 °C for 12 h. After induction, the bacterial solution was centrifuged at 5200 rpm for 15 min, the supernatant was discarded, and the bacterial pellet was collected and ultrasonically disrupted. The solution was centrifuged at 4 °C (10000 rpm, 45 min), and the supernatant was collected. The supernatant after centrifugation was purified using GST magnetic beads. After purification, the protein concentration was detected using the Kangwei BCA protein quantification kit.

Criteria of Double-Antibody Sandwich ELISA and Establishment of Standard Curve

The double-Antibody Sandwich ELISA was done as follows: The mouse monoclonal antibody against the NetB toxin was diluted at 1:800 with 0.1 M carbonate buffer (pH 9.6) and used to coat the plates overnight at 4 °C; after washed for three times with PBST, blocked overnight at 4 °C with 5% skim milk in PBST and washed for three times with PBST; the samples were added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h; after washed for three times with PBST; rabbit polyclonal antibodies against the NetB fusion protein was diluted at 1:1,600 with PBS, then added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h; after washed for three times with PBST; HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG diluted at 1:10,000 with PBS was added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h; after washed for three times with PBS; TMB substrate was added and the color was developed at 37 °C in the dark for 15 min; 2 M H₂SO₄ was added to stop the reaction, and the OD₄₅₀ₙₘ value was read .

The double-antibody sandwich ELISA method was used to detect 25 negative samples (medium supernatant), with positive controls set. The OD450nm values were measured, and the average value (X) and standard deviation (SD) of the negative samples were calculated. X + 2SD and X + 3SD were used as the critical values for negative and positive results, respectively.

The OD450nm value of the recombinant NetB toxin was then measured. The toxin was serially diluted two-fold horizontally starting from 1500 ng/mL, with two replicates for each dilution, and negative and blank controls were set. A curve was plotted with the concentration of recombinant NetB toxin as the abscissa and OD450nm as the ordinate. Based on this curve, the optimal linear range was selected. Using the logarithm of the concentration as the abscissa and the OD450nm value as the ordinate, a standard curve was plotted.

To further evaluate the reliability of this method, repeatability tests were performed. This standard curve was plotted based on a single representative experiment, with two replicate wells for each concentration. Its reliability has been verified through independent repeatability tests.

Infection experiment of Clostridium perfringens

Infection procedure

Sixty 1-day-old black chickens were selected and evenly divided into two groups of 30 each: control group and infection group. Both groups were vaccinated according to the routine immunization program, specifically: inactivated vaccine of Newcastle disease and avian influenza, Newcastle disease-infectious bronchitis combination vaccine and infectious bursal disease vaccine was used at 7, 9 and 14 days old. At 16 days old, each chicken in the infection group was orally administered 1 mL of a chicken coccidial oocyst suspension at a concentration of 22,000 sporocysts/ml, while the control group received an equal volume of sterile physiological saline. From 18 to 22 days old, each chicken in the infection group was orally administered 2 mL of Type G Clostridium perfringens bacterial suspension at a concentration of approximately 1 × 10^8 CFU/ml (Lee KW, 2021), twice daily (morning and evening). The control group was orally administered an equal volume of sterile FTG medium. The entire experimental period was 37 days.

Sample collection

At 6 h, 1, 2, 3, 6 and 14 days post-challenge, serum samples were randomly collected from 5 chickens of each group and stored at −20 °C for toxin detection. At 3, 6 and 14 days post-infection, six chickens were randomly selected from each group and exsanguinated via the jugular vein, serum samples were collected and stored, simultaneously, heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, duodenum, ileum, and jejunum tissues were collected and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for pathological and immunohistochemical examination. Additionally, the contents of the duodenum, ileum, and jejunum were collected, mixed with sterile PBS at a 1:1 mass ratio, left standing at 4 °C for 2 h, and centrifuged at 10,000 r/min for 15 min. The supernatant was taken as the sample for toxin content detection.

Intestinal lesion scoring in infected chicks

At 3, 6, and 14 days post-challenge, six chickens from each group were randomly selected for dissection, and the lesions of small intestine were observed. Intestinal lesion scoring referred to the method of Shojadoost et al. (Shojadoost et al., 2012), examining the entire intestine for the degree of lesions and providing an overall score. The scoring criteria used a 0-6 point system: 0 points: no obvious lesions; 1 point: intestinal wall thinning and fragility; 2 points: 1-5 necrotic foci; 3 points: 6-15 necrotic foci; 4 points: 16 or more necrotic foci; 5 points: patchy necrosis 2-3 cm in length; 6 points: large areas of diffuse necrosis.

Histopathological examination of infected chicks and establishment of immunohistochemistry method

Collection of pathological materials and histopathological examination

Six chickens were dissected at 3, 6, and 14 days post-challenge. Liver, spleen, kidney, and various segments of the small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) were taken and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution. Sections were prepared, stained with HE, and pathological changes in various tissues were observed.

Establishment and optimization of immunohistochemistry method

The following parameters were selectively optimized to establish the optimal immunohistochemical reaction conditions: antigen retrieval method (high-pressure retrieval, microwave retrieval, trypsin retrieval), antigen retrieval time (4 min - 10 min), peroxidase blocking time (30 min - 90 min), optimal primary antibody dilution (1:100 - 1:500), primary antibody incubation conditions (37 °C for 1, 1.5, and 2 h; 4 °C overnight incubation followed by 37 °C rewarming for 1 h), secondary antibody incubation time (20 min - 90 min), and enhanced HRP-DAB chromogenic time (1 min - 5 min). The immunohistochemical staining results were judged based on the specificity of staining, intensity of background staining, and the presence, quantity, and type of brown-yellow staining in specific cells.

Jejunum tissue from chickens artificially infected with Type G Clostridium perfringens and coccidia causing necrotic enteritis was used as a positive control; the same tissues from chickens injected with sterile saline served as negative controls. Simultaneously, non-immune mouse serum and PBS were used instead of the primary antibody for immunohistochemical staining to evaluate the specificity of the method, while other steps remained unchanged.

Detection of NetB toxin distribution in infected chicks

The collected intestinal content and serum samples were subjected to double-antibody sandwich ELISA to detect NetB toxin concentration. Intestinal contents and serum from the control group served as negative controls.

The collected heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, duodenum, jejunum, and ileum tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, sectioned, and then subjected to immunohistochemistry to detect the dynamic distribution of NetB toxin in different tissues at different time points.

Effect of recombinant NetB toxin on primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes

Primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes were isolated using the tissue digestion method. After isolation, the primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes were treated with 0, 75, 150, 300, and 600 µg/mL recombinant NetB toxin protein for 24 h. Cell morphology was observed under a microscope, cell relative viability was detected using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), and the released amount of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) was measured to assess the degree of cell damage.

This experiment was repeated three times, and the data were preliminarily organized using Excel 2020. Statistical analysis was performed using the independent samples t-test and one-way ANOVA in SPSS 21.0, with Duncan’s multiple range test for comparisons, and data visualization was conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The double-antibody sandwich ELISA method was used to detect 25 negative samples. The average value was 0.15, and the standard deviation was 0.02. The calculation of the negative and positive critical values was the same as described in the section "Criteria of Double-Antibody Sandwich ELISA and Establishment of Standard Curve", i.e., OD450 nm > 0.21 was considered a positive result, and OD450 nm < 0.19 was considered a negative result.

As shown in Fig. 3A, the recombinant NetB toxin protein showed a good linear relationship in the range of 5.86 ng/mL to 375 ng/mL, including 7 standard points. Based on these 7 standard points, a linear regression was performed to create the standard curve (Fig. 3B). The regression equation was y = 0.0696x + 0.1225, R² = 0.9975. The minimum detection concentration was 3.52 ng/mL (Fig. 3).

Histopathological observation of infected chicks

Histopathological examination was performed on the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, duodenum, ileum, and jejunum after infection. The results showed that in the infection group, the heart were infiltrated by a small amount of red blood cell and inflammatory cell; in the liver tissue, hepatocytes were loosely arranged or separated, hepatic sinusoids were dilated, hepatocyte nuclei were blurred, some nuclei were condensed, fragmented, or dissolved and disappeared, and the portal area, interlobular veins, and hepatic sinusoids were congested; in the spleen, a small number of lymphocytes were missing in the red and white pulp, and a small amount of inflammatory cell was observed with in the red pulp; the lungs were congested with widened interstitium; in the kidneys, the peritubular space was widened, the interstitium was congested and hemorrhagic, and some glomerular capillaries were congested; in the duodenum, the villous mucosal epithelial cells were shed; in the ileum, local mucosal epithelial cells were necrotic and shed, intestinal villi became thick and short, with a small amount of inflammatory cell infiltration in the lamina propria; in the jejunum, intestinal villi were disordered, local epithelium was necrotic and shed, with a small amount of inflammatory cell infiltration in the lamina propria. No significant pathological changes were observed in the various organs of the control group. The above results indicate that multiple tissues and organs of the infected chickens suffered varying degrees of pathological damage (Fig. 4).

Dynamic distribution of NetB toxin in chick organ tissues

The localization and distribution of NetB toxin in tissues and organs showed that positive signals could be detected in the heart, liver, lungs, kidneys, and various segments of the small intestine at 3 days post-infection. The distribution was mainly localized in the cytoplasm. The positive signals showed a progressive attenuation trend with prolonged infection time. The specific manifestations were as follows:

In the immunohistochemical sections of the heart, weak positive signals were only observed in the cytoplasm of myocardial cells at 3 days post-infection. In the liver, positive signals were mainly observed in the hepatic sinusoids and the cytoplasm of degenerated and necrotic hepatocytes at 3 days post-infection, a small number of positive signals can also be observed in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes from 6 days post-infection, In the spleen, diffuse positive signals were seen in the red and white pulp only at 3 days post-infection. In the lungs, positive signals were mainly located in the flattened epithelial cells, cuboidal epithelial cells lining the bronchi, the flat and cuboidal epithelial cells lining the pulmonary atrium only at 3 days post-infection. In the kidneys, positive signals were mainly distributed in the cytoplasm of renal tubular epithelial cells only at 3 days post-infection. In the intestinal tissues, strong positive signals were observed in the epithelial cells and lamina propria of the jejunum at 3 days post-infection, with weaker positive signals in the duodenum and ileum. At 6 and 14 days post-infection, although the positive signals gradually decreased, they could still be detected in the jejunum, ileum and duodenum (Fig. 5).

Pathogenicity of Recombinant NetB Toxin in Reducing Primary Chicken Embryonic Hepatocytes

Pathogenicity of recombinant NetB toxin to the viability and cell membrane integrity of primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes

To evaluate the cytotoxicity of NetB toxin to hepatocytes, primary chicken embryonic cells were treated with different concentrations (0, 75, 150, 300, and 600 µg/mL) of recombinant NetB toxin protein for 24 h. Cell survival rate was detected using the CCK-8 method, and the cytotoxicity of NetB toxin was further verified using the LDH cytotoxicity detection kit. As shown in Fig. 7A, the results of CCK-8 cell survival rate showed that recombinant NetB toxin significantly inhibited hepatocyte survival. Starting from 150 µg/mL, cell viability decreased significantly (P < 0.05), and the inhibitory effect showed a dose-dependent manner with increasing toxin concentration. LDH is a cytosolic enzyme present only in the cytoplasm. When cells undergo pyroptosis or necrosis, the cell membrane structure is damaged, and LDH is released from the cytoplasm into the cell culture supernatant. By measuring the LDH activity in the medium, the degree of cell damage can be quantitatively analyzed. As shown in Fig. 7B, compared with the blank group and the empty vector control group, 150 µg/mL recombinant NetB toxin significantly increased LDH release (P < 0.05), and this increase also showed concentration dependence (Fig. 7).

Effect of recombinant NetB toxin on the morphology of primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes

Cell morphological damage was observed using a microscope. The results are shown in Fig. 8 A-D. Hepatocytes in the blank group and the GST empty vector control group showed irregularly sized epithelial-like cells aggregated in island-like clusters. In contrast, hepatocytes treated with recombinant NetB toxin protein for 24 h were deformed, with unclear cell structures. Discussion

The gut-liver axis is an important link between the gut microbiota and the liver, reflecting the close functional connection and bidirectional communication between the two organs. When intestinal barrier integrity is compromised, leading to increased permeability, intestinal pathogens and their toxins can be translocated to the liver via the portal circulation. The exposure of liver to these toxic factors and gut bacteria ultimately triggers inflammatory injury (Konturek et al., 2018; Løvland et al., 1999). This study detected NetB toxin both in collected intestinal content and serum samples using the double-antibody sandwich ELISA method, consistent with the results of Lee et al. who detected NetB toxin in serum samples (Lee et al., 2021) , validating the view of Moran et al. that toxin derived from the intestinal lumen can penetrate the intestinal barrier and enter the systemic circulation via the portal vein (Moran, 2014).

Quantitative analysis of toxin in intestinal contents from various segments at different stages post-infection (3 d ∼ 14 d) using the double-antibody sandwich ELISA method showed that NetB toxin was positive at all sampling time points, and its content showed a significant time-dependent decreasing trend (P < 0.05). This change may be closely related to the intervention of the host immune system and the initiation of intestinal mucosal repair processes in the later stages of necrotic enteritis (> 7 d) (Alizadeh et al., 2021). In the early stage of infection, the host immune system is not fully activated, and intestinal mucosal damage is more severe, providing conditions for toxin production and accumulation. Therefore, in the early stage of infection, the toxin content in the contents of all intestinal segments is high, especially in the jejunum.

Compared with conventional pathogen detection techniques, immunohistochemistry can precisely locate the spatial distribution characteristics of pathogen-related antigens in target organs (Chen et al., 2024;Hassan et al., 2025).When Clostridium perfringens invades the animal body, intestinal colonization is the first step of the infection process (Zhu et al., 2024), and the intestinal lamina propria is the main site for the diffusion of Clostridium perfringens toxins to adjacent cells. The immunohistochemical detection in this study showed that NetB toxin positive signals could be detected in the duodenum, jejunum, and ileum at 3, 6, and 14 days post-challenge, and the cell positivity rate was highest in the villi and lamina propria of the jejunum. Therefore, the jejunum may be the primary target organ for NetB toxin. As the main site for digestion and absorption in the intestine, the jejunum has a better anaerobic environment than the stomach and duodenum, making it easier for bacteria to colonize, reproduce, and produce toxins. The absorbed toxins can enter the body via the enterohepatic circulation, reach the blood circulation system, and subsequently access various tissues and organs throughout the body (Pabst et al., 2023).

The immunohistochemical results of this study verified this speculation: on the third day after challenge with Type G Clostridium perfringens, NetB toxin positive signals could be detected in the heart, liver, lung, kidney, duodenum, ileum, and jejunum tissues. The widest distribution of the toxin in the body was showed at this time point, and also was the period when clinical symptoms were most obvious in chicks infected with Clostridium perfringens. As the infection time progressed, at 6 and 14 days post-infection, positive signals could not be detected in the heart, spleen, lung, and kidney tissues. This phenomenon may be because: after bacterial invasion, the chick's immune system is stimulated to produce corresponding antibodies, which can effectively inhibit further bacterial proliferation and toxin secretion in the chicks (He et al., 2023).

Furthermore, at the same time point, the distribution intensity of the positive signal varies significantly among different organs. Among them, the positive signal of NetB toxin is the strongest in the ileum tissue. Besides being influenced by the concentration differences of the toxin in different organs, this may also be closely related to the distribution of receptors in the tissues and organs. The distribution of exogenous toxins in the body is uneven, and their distribution in various organs mainly depends on the binding rate to plasma or tissue proteins, membrane permeability, and local blood flow. In the initial distribution phase, organs with rich blood flow have higher toxin concentrations, but as the time prolongs, toxin distribution is influenced by the rate of membrane diffusion and tissue affinity, potentially leading to redistribution within the body (Deng et al., 2018).

When bacteria invade the animal body, they multiply and produce various virulence factors. These toxins enter the bloodstream and disseminate to important parenchymal organs such as the liver and kidneys, causing a series of acute injuries (Takehara et al., 2021). Relevant reports indicate that avian necrotic enteritis can be accompanied by liver damage and lesions, and liver lesions can serve as a diagnostic reference indicator for avian necrotic enteritis (Løvland et al., 1999). The results of histopathological examination in this study showed: besides intestinal lesions, pathological changes also occurred in the liver, spleen, kidney, heart, and lungs of the challenged chickens. Immunohistochemical detection showed: NetB toxin positive signals existed in organs such as the liver, spleen, kidney. However, antigen distribution in tissues does not directly equate to pathogenic effects, and lesions may also be caused by other virulence factors of Clostridium perfringens.

NetB toxin can cause cell rupture by forming transmembrane pores (Yan et al., 2013). Studies have shown that NetB toxin exhibits significant cytotoxicity to chicken leghorn male hepatoma (LMH) cell line and HeLa cells (Keyburn et al., 2008; Wang et al., 2016). However, it has also been found that NetB toxin does not cause cytopathic effects in chicken embryonic fibroblast DF1 cells or chicken macrophage HD11 cells (Keyburn et al., 2008), and African green monkey kidney cells (Vero cells) are also not susceptible to it (Islam et al., 2021). Whether visceral cells such as hepatocytes have toxin receptors and whether the toxin is pathogenic to hepatocytes remain unclear. Therefore, this study systematically evaluated the impact of NetB toxin on hepatocytes by culturing primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes in vitro to simulate the in vivo environment.

Changes in cell morphology are an intuitive manifestation of cell damage caused by NetB toxin. After infecting primary chicken embryonic hepatocytes with recombinant NetB toxin protein for 24 h, the cells showed phenomena such as round-like changes, clustering into groups, loose structure and fragmentation, suggesting that NetB toxin interferes with the normal structure and function of the cytoskeleton, causing internal structural disorder and leading to changes in cell morphology (Seo et al., 2022).

LDH is a stable cytoplasmic enzyme existing in all cells. When cells are damaged by intracellular or external pressure, LDH will be rapidly released into the extracellular environment. The increase of this release amount is an important index to judge cell pyroptosis, necrosis and other damage forms (Kumar et al., 2018). It was found that the release of LDH increased in a dose-dependent manner after NetB toxin was infected with primary hepatocytes of chicken embryos. When the concentration of recombinant protein was 150 μ g/ml, the content of LDH increased significantly (P < 0.05). On the other hand, CCK-8 showed that recombinant NetB toxin could inhibit the survival rate of hepatocytes, and the cell viability decreased significantly from 150 μ g/ml (P < 0.05), and the inhibition was dose-dependent with the increase of toxin concentration. The results of the two methods showed that when the concentration of NetB toxin reached a certain level, it could significantly cause cell membrane damage of primary chicken embryo hepatocytes, and further trigger cascade reaction in cells, resulting in cell viability decline and necrosis, which was consistent with the morphological changes of cell.

However, we also found that the concentration of NetB toxin detected by double antibody sandwich ELISA in serum and intestinal contents is low, which is far from the concentration of recombinant NetB toxin which caused significant difference in chicken embryo hepatotoxicity test in vitro. On the one hand, the sensitivity of chicken embryo hepatocytes and chicken hepatocytes may be quite different, and although endotoxin was removed; on the other hand, the expressed toxin might also be different from natural toxin in pathogenicity, Therefore, there remains uncertainty regarding the pathogenicity of NetB toxins to the liver in vivo.
