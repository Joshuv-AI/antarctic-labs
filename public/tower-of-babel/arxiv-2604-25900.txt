Abstract
. We prove a sharp stable 2 2 -systolic inequality for complex projective space under the scalar curvature lower bound of the normalized Fubini–Study metric. 1.
Introduction

Lower scalar curvature bounds often lead to rigidity and extremality results that
single out canonical model geometries. Classical examples include the Geroch
conjecture
[
21
,
8
]
for flat tori, the positive mass theorem for Euclidean
[
20
,
26
]
and hyperbolic
space
[
25
,
3
]
, and Llarull’s theorem
[
17
]
for the round sphere.

In this paper we study the relationship between lower scalar curvature bounds and the stable 2-systole.
Stable systoles are homological systolic invariants in which one measures the asymptotic size of nontrivial homology classes. More
precisely, if
h\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{torsion}
, its stable norm is the asymptotic minimal area per multiple of
h
:

\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\inf\{\Area(T):T\text{ is an integral }2\text{-cycle representing }kh\}.

The stable 2-systole is then defined by

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g):=\min\{\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}:0\neq h\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{torsion}\}.

This invariant is particularly well suited to scalar curvature questions in
degree two. On the one hand, it records the smallest stabilized area of a
nontrivial two-dimensional homology class. On the other hand, by the
Federer–Gromov duality theorem, the stable norm on homology is dual to the
comass norm on cohomology.
Since the curvature term in the Lichnerowicz formula for the Dirac operator twisted with a line bundle is controlled by the comass of a
degree-two curvature form, this duality turns an analytic scalar curvature estimate into a stable systolic inequality.
This point of view was introduced by Gromov
[
10
, Section 5.9]
, who proved a sharp systolic inequality with scalar curvature on 3-manifolds.
More recently, these ideas have been developed further by Orikasa
[
19
]
and Stryker
[
23
]
.

The main result of this paper is a sharp upper bound for the stable 2-systole of complex projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, together with a rigidity statement for the equality case.
Let
J_{0}
be the standard complex structure on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
We normalize the Fubini–Study metric
g_{\textup{FS}}
on
(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
by

\int_{\ell}\omega_{\textup{FS}}=\pi

for every projective line
\ell\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. With this normalization,

\scal_{g_{\textup{FS}}}=4n(n+1),

and every projective line has area
\pi
.
Our main theorem establishes extremality and rigidity for the stable 2-systole of Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with scalar curvature bounded from below by
4n(n+1)
.
This characterizes the Fubini–Study metric as the unique Riemannian metric, up to biholomorphism, with constant scalar curvature
4n(n+1)
and stable 2-systole
\pi
, in the precise sense specified by the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
such that
\scal_{g}\geq 4n(n+1)
.
Then

\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Moreover, if equality holds, then there exist a complex structure
J
on
M
and
a biholomorphism
B\colon(M,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
such that

g=B^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

Remark 1.1
.

Stryker
[
23
, Theorem 1.6]
recently obtained an upper bound for the stable 2-systole of
\mathbb{CP}^{2q+1}
with scalar curvature bounded from below by a positive constant.
These bounds are not sharp.
For example, on
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
with
\scal_{g}\geq 48=\scal_{g_{\textup{FS}}}
, Stryker’s explicit estimate
[
23
, Theorem 1.8]
gives
\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(\mathbb{CP}^{3},g)\leq 5\pi
.
In contrast, Theorem
A
gives the sharp upper bound for the stable
2
-systole of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
in every complex dimension and shows that equality occurs only for the Fubini–Study metric, up to biholomorphism.

Remark 1.2
.

In real dimension four, the case
M\cong\mathbb{CP}^{2}
also follows from LeBrun’s perturbed Seiberg–Witten scalar curvature estimate
[
16
, Theorem 1]
.
Indeed, applying this estimate to the
g
-harmonic representative of the positive generator
h\in\HZ^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{2};\mathbb{Z})
, and using stable
norm–comass duality, gives

\scal_{g}\geq 24\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(\mathbb{CP}^{2},g)\leq\pi.

The equality case follows from LeBrun’s rigidity statement
[
16
, Theorem 2]
, together with uniqueness of Kähler–Einstein metrics on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Suppose that
g
is a Kähler metric on the standard complex manifold
(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
with
[\omega_{g}]=\lambda h
, where
h=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1))
.
Then the Kähler form
\omega_{g}
calibrates projective lines and the stable 2-systole
\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g)
coincides with the homological 2-systole

\sys_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g):=\inf\bigl\{\Area_{g}(\Sigma)\bigr\},

where
\Sigma
ranges over all integral
2
-cycles with
0\neq[\Sigma]\in\HZ_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
.
More precisely, every projective line
\ell\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is area minimizing in its real homology class and

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g)=\sys_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g)=\Area_{g}(\ell)=\int_{\ell}\omega_{g}=\lambda.

Thus, for Kähler metrics on
(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
, Theorem
A
recovers the sharp homological 2-systole estimate

\scal_{g}\geq 4n(n+1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sys_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g)\leq\pi,

with equality only if
g
is the Fubini–Study metric, up to a holomorphic automorphism of
(\mathbb{CP}^{n};J_{0})
.
In this Kähler setting the inequality itself also follows directly from the Chern–Weil formula for the total scalar curvature; the point of Theorem
A
is that no Kähler assumption is imposed on
g
.
For
n=2
, this recovers Sha’s sharp 2-systole upper bound and rigidity result for Kähler metrics on

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
; see
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Thus Theorem
A
shows that, among all Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with scalar curvature bounded below by that of the normalized Fubini–Study metric, the stable
2
-systole is maximized precisely by the Fubini–Study metric.
In this sense, Theorem
A
is a scalar curvature rigidity theorem for complex projective space, in the same spirit as the rigidity results mentioned above.

We also prove an odd-dimensional analogue:

Theorem B
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
such that
\scal_{g}\geq 4n(n+1)
.
Then

\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Moreover, if equality holds, then the universal cover of
(M,g)
is isometric to

(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g_{\textup{FS}})\times\mathbb{R},

where
g_{\textup{FS}}
is normalized as above.

Remark 1.4
.

When
n=1
, Theorem
B
recovers
[
10
, Section 5.9]
.

Let us briefly indicate the mechanism behind these results.
Underlying Theorem
A
is the following general comass comparison result which may be of independent interest.

Theorem C
.

Let
M^{2m}
be an even-dimensional closed connected oriented manifold and let
c\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
such that
c\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\mod 2
and

\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle\neq 0.

Let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
denote the image of
c
under the natural map
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
Then for every Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, we have

\min_{M}\scal_{g}\leq 4m\pi\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}.

Moreover, if equality holds and
c_{\mathbb{R}}\neq 0
, then
(M,g)
is Kähler–Einstein with Kähler form
\omega
satisfying

[\omega]=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}

in
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

We also have an odd-dimensional analogue of Theorem
C
, see
Theorem

3.4
, which is needed to prove Theorem
B
.

Remark 1.5
.

This rigidity result is closely related to results of
[
18
]
and
[
7
, Theorem 1.9]
, from which one would already expect that
M
must locally be a product of a Kähler manifold with a Ricci-flat manifold.
The reason why there is no Ricci-flat factor in our case is that our formulation of the inequality in terms of the comass together with
c_{\mathbb{R}}\neq 0
forces equality in our sharp pointwise Clifford-algebra estimate (
Lemma

3.1
), which yields a stronger rigidity conclusion.
An additional technical issue that we have to overcome in our case is that the form minimizing the comass in a given cohomology class is a priori not necessarily smooth.

The relevant
\Spin^{c}
index condition in Theorem
C
and
Theorem

3.4
produces a harmonic spinor: directly in even dimensions and by spectral flow in odd dimensions.
The
\Spin^{c}
Lichnerowicz formula then relates the scalar curvature to the curvature of the determinant line bundle.
A sharp pointwise Clifford-algebra estimate (
Lemma

3.1
) bounds this curvature term in terms of the comass of the underlying degree-two cohomology class.
Finally, stable norm–comass duality (
Corollary

4.2
) converts the resulting scalar curvature estimate into the stable systolic inequalities above.

The equality case is governed by the equality case in the Clifford estimate.
In even dimensions, this forces the relevant two-form to be a parallel Kähler form and the metric to be Kähler–Einstein.
In odd dimensions, the same argument produces a parallel two-form with one-dimensional kernel, which yields the isometric splitting of the universal cover.
For
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
, the final rigidity statements follow from the Hirzebruch–Kodaira–Yau and Kobayashi–Ochiai characterizations of projective space, together with the uniqueness of Kähler–Einstein metrics on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we recall the background material on the comass norm and
\Spin^{c}
structures.

Section

3
contains the main abstract estimates: we prove sharp scalar curvature–comass bounds for
\Spin^{c}
manifolds, together with the corresponding rigidity statements in the equality case.
In
Section

4
we turn to geometric applications.
After recalling the stable norm–comass duality, we apply the results of
Section

3
to prove Theorems
A
and
B
.

2.
Notation and conventions

Throughout,
M
denotes a closed oriented manifold and
g
a Riemannian metric on

M
. Since only degree two enters the sequel, we formulate the notions of mass,
comass, and stable norm in that degree.

2.1.
Comass

For a real
2
-form
\omega\in\Dforms^{2}(M)
and
p\in M
, define the
pointwise comass of
\omega
at
p
by

\lvert\omega\rvert_{\ast,p}:=\sup\bigl\{\lvert\omega_{p}(v)\rvert:v\in\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}_{p}M\text{ simple and }\lVert v\rVert=1\bigr\}.

Its global comass is

\lVert\omega\rVert_{\ast}:=\sup_{p\in M}\lvert\omega\rvert_{\ast,p}.

Equivalently, for every
p\in M
and every simple
v\in\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}_{p}M
,

\lvert\omega_{p}(v)\rvert\leq\lvert\omega\rvert_{\ast,p}\,\lVert v\rVert.

For
\phi\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
, its comass norm is

\lVert\phi\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}:=\inf\bigl\{\lVert\omega\rVert_{\ast}:\omega\in\Dforms^{2}(M),\ \mathrm{d}\omega=0,\ [\omega]=\phi\bigr\}.

This defines a norm on
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
. In particular, for every

\phi\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
smooth closed real
2
-form
\omega
such that

[\omega]=\phi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lVert\omega\rVert_{\ast}\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}+\varepsilon.

2.2.
\Spin^{c}
structures and Dirac operators

If
c\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, we write
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
for its image under the
natural map
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
Suppose now that
M=M^{2m}
is even-dimensional. A
\Spin^{c}
structure on
M
comes with a complex spinor bundle

S=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}

and a Hermitian determinant line bundle
L\to M
satisfying

\chernclass_{1}(L)\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\mod 2.

Given a unitary connection
A
on
L
, we write

\mathcal{D}_{A}^{+}\colon\Ct^{\infty}(M,S^{+})\to\Ct^{\infty}(M,S^{-}),\qquad\mathcal{D}_{A}\colon\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)\to\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)

for the associated chiral and full
\Spin^{c}
Dirac operators. Our curvature convention is that
\LineCurv_{A}
is an
\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
-valued
2
-form; thus we may write

\LineCurv_{A}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta

for a real closed
2
-form
\eta
, and then
[\eta]=\chernclass_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem
[
15
, Theorem D.15]
gives

(2.1)

\ind(\mathcal{D}_{A}^{+})=\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{\chernclass_{1}(L)/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle,

where
\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\in\HZ^{4*}(M;\mathbb{Q})
is the
\widehat{\mathrm{A}}
-class of
\mathrm{T}M
; see
[
15
, Chapter III,§11]
.
Moreover, the
\Spin^{c}
Lichnerowicz formula
[
15
, Theorem D.12]
reads

(2.2)

\mathcal{D}_{A}^{2}=\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}+\frac{\scal_{g}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\clm(\LineCurv_{A}).

3.
A general comass comparison result for scalar curvature

We start with a linear algebra lemma which relates the comass of a form to the corresponding curvature term in the Lichnerowicz formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean vector space, let
E
be a Hermitian
\mathrm{Cl}(V)
-module with Clifford multiplication
\clm
, and set
m:=\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
Then for every
\eta\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
,

\lvert\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq m\,\lvert\eta\rvert_{\ast}.

Moreover, if
C>0
,
\lvert\eta\rvert_{\ast}\leq C
, and
0\neq\psi\in E
satisfy
\langle\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle=-mC\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}
, and if
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
is defined by

\omega(v,w):=-\frac{\langle\mathrm{i}\,\clm(v\wedge w)\psi,\psi\rangle}{\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}}\qquad\text{for }v,w\in V,

then
\eta=C\,\omega
. Equivalently, there exists an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
V
such that
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{2j-1}\wedge e^{2j}
and
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(e_{2j-1})\clm(e_{2j})\psi=-\psi
for every
j=1,\dots,m
. In particular,
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\psi=-mC\,\psi
.

Proof.

Choose an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
V
such that

\eta=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\,e^{2j-1}\wedge e^{2j}

for suitable
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Set
C_{0}:=\lvert\eta\rvert_{\ast}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert
and
A_{j}:=\mathrm{i}\,\clm(e_{2j-1})\clm(e_{2j})
.
Then
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}A_{j}
.
For each
j
, the operator
A_{j}
is self-adjoint and satisfies
A_{j}^{2}=\id
, hence has operator norm
1
.
Therefore

\lvert\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\leq m\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert=mC_{0}.

Now assume that
C>0
,
\lvert\eta\rvert_{\ast}\leq C
, and that
0\neq\psi\in E
satisfies

\langle\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle=-mC\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}.

Then

-mC\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\,\langle A_{j}\psi,\psi\rangle\geq-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}\geq-mC\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}.

Hence equality holds throughout.
In particular,
\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert=C
for every
j
, and
\lambda_{j}\,\langle A_{j}\psi,\psi\rangle=-\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\,\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}
for every
j=1,\dots,m
.
If
\varepsilon_{j}:=\lambda_{j}/C\in\{\pm 1\}
, then
\langle\varepsilon_{j}A_{j}\psi,\psi\rangle=-\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}
.
Since
\varepsilon_{j}A_{j}
is self-adjoint and has operator norm
1
, this forces
\varepsilon_{j}A_{j}\psi=-\psi
.
After replacing
e_{2j}
by
-e_{2j}
whenever
\varepsilon_{j}=-1
, we may therefore assume that
\lambda_{j}=C
and
A_{j}\psi=-\psi
for every
j
.
Define
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
by
\omega(v,w):=-\lvert\psi\rvert^{-2}\langle\mathrm{i}\,\clm(v\wedge w)\psi,\psi\rangle
.
Then
\omega(e_{2j-1},e_{2j})=-\lvert\psi\rvert^{-2}\langle A_{j}\psi,\psi\rangle=1
for every
j
.
Finally, let
a<b
be a pair not equal to any of the pairs
(2j-1,2j)
, and set
B:=\mathrm{i}\,\clm(e_{a})\clm(e_{b})
.
Then
B
anticommutes with some
A_{j}
, namely one for which
\{a,b\}
meets
\{2j-1,2j\}
in exactly one index.
Using that
A_{j}
is self-adjoint and
A_{j}\psi=-\psi
, we obtain
\langle B\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle A_{j}B\psi,A_{j}\psi\rangle=-\langle B\psi,\psi\rangle
. Therefore
\omega(e_{a},e_{b})=0
.
Hence
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{2j-1}\wedge e^{2j}
, so
\eta=C\,\omega
, and
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}CA_{j}\psi=-mC\,\psi
. ∎

3.1.
Even-dimensional case

Next, we prove Theorem
C
, which we restate for convenience.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M^{2m}
be an even-dimensional closed oriented connected manifold and let
c\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
such that
c\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\mod 2
and

\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle\neq 0.

Let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
denote the image of
c
under the natural map
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
Then for every Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, we have

\min_{M}\scal_{g}\leq 4m\pi\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}.

Moreover, if equality holds and
c_{\mathbb{R}}\neq 0
, then
(M,g)
is Kähler–Einstein with Kähler form
\omega
satisfying

[\omega]=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}

in
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Fix a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, and set
\sigma:=\min_{M}\scal_{g}
.
We will show that
\sigma\leq 4m\pi\,\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}
.

Since
c\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\mod 2
, there exists a
\Spin^{c}
structure on
M
whose determinant line bundle
L
satisfies
\chernclass_{1}(L)=c
.
Let
S=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
be the associated complex spinor bundle.
Let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
denote the image of
c
under the natural map
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By the definition of
\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}
, there exists a smooth closed real
2
-form
\eta
with

[\eta]=c_{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\lVert\eta\rVert_{\ast}\leq\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}+\varepsilon.

Choose a unitary connection
A
on
L
with curvature

\LineCurv_{A}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta.

Indeed, if
A_{0}
is any unitary connection on
L
, then
\frac{\LineCurv_{A_{0}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}
is a closed representative of
\chernclass_{1}(L)=c
, so
\eta-\frac{\LineCurv_{A_{0}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{d}\alpha
for some real
1
-form
\alpha
, and
A:=A_{0}+2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\alpha
has the required curvature.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{A}^{+}:\Ct^{\infty}(M,S^{+})\to\Ct^{\infty}(M,S^{-})
be the chiral
\Spin^{c}
Dirac operator, and let
\mathcal{D}_{A}:\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)\to\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)
be the full Dirac operator.
By the Atiyah–Singer index theorem,

\ind(\mathcal{D}_{A}^{+})=\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{\chernclass_{1}(L)/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle=\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle\neq 0.

Hence
\ker(\mathcal{D}_{A})\neq 0
.
Choose

0\neq\psi\in\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathcal{D}_{A}\psi=0.

The
\Spin^{c}
Lichnerowicz formula gives

\mathcal{D}_{A}^{2}=\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}+\frac{\scal_{g}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\,\clm(\LineCurv_{A}).

Integrating by parts yields

0=\lVert\nabla_{A}\psi\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{M}\left(\frac{\scal_{g}}{4}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\clm(\LineCurv_{A})\psi,\psi\rangle\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}_{g}.

Since
\LineCurv_{A}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\,\clm(\LineCurv_{A})=\pi\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta)
.
By
Lemma

3.1
, applied to
V=\mathrm{T}_{p}M
and
E=S_{p}
, at each point
p\in M
,

\bigcap_{p<\infty}\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
with

\LineCurv_{A_{\infty}}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{\infty},

and a nonzero spinor
\psi_{\infty}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(S)
such that

\lVert\eta_{\infty}\rVert_{\ast}\leq C\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{A_{\infty}}\psi_{\infty}=0.

Moreover,
\lvert\psi_{\infty}\rvert
is constant and nonzero, and

\scal_{g}\equiv 4m\pi C,\qquad\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta_{\infty})\psi_{\infty}=-mC\,\psi_{\infty}

almost everywhere.

Proof.

Fix a smooth unitary connection
A_{0}
on
L
with
\LineCurv_{A_{0}}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{0}
for some
\eta_{0}\in\Dforms^{2}(M)
.
Choose
\varepsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
.
For each
j

2
-form
\eta_{j}
with
[\eta_{j}]=c_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lVert\eta_{j}\rVert_{\ast}\leq C+\varepsilon_{j}
.
Define
A_{j}
from
\eta_{j}
exactly as in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
, so that
\LineCurv_{A_{j}}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{j}
.

Fix
u\colon M\to\mathrm{S}^{1}
representing
\xi
, and define
\alpha
as above.
For
t\in[0,1]
, set

A_{j}^{t}:=A_{j}+4\pi\mathrm{i}\,t\,\alpha.

Write
A_{j}^{\bullet}:=(A_{j}^{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
and
\mathcal{D}_{A_{j}^{\bullet}}:=(\mathcal{D}_{A_{j}^{t}})_{t\in[0,1]}
.
As before,

\operatorname{sf}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{A_{j}^{\bullet}}\bigr)=-\langle[M],\xi\smile\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle\neq 0,

so there exist
t_{j}\in[0,1]
and
\psi_{j}\in\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)
such that

\mathcal{D}_{A_{j}^{t_{j}}}\psi_{j}=0,\qquad\lVert\psi_{j}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=1.

Set
B_{j}:=A_{j}^{t_{j}}
.
The Lichnerowicz estimate from the first part gives

0\geq\lVert\nabla_{B_{j}}\psi_{j}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sigma}{4}-\pi m\,\lVert\eta_{j}\rVert_{\ast}\right)\lVert\psi_{j}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\sigma=4m\pi C
and
\lVert\eta_{j}\rVert_{\ast}\leq C+\varepsilon_{j}
, it follows that
\lVert\nabla_{B_{j}}\psi_{j}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\pi m\,\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
.

As in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
, the bound
\lVert\eta_{j}\rVert_{\ast}\leq C+\varepsilon_{j}
yields uniform
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
-bounds on
A_{j}-A_{0}
for every
p<\infty
.
Since

B_{j}-A_{0}=(A_{j}-A_{0})+4\pi\mathrm{i}\,t_{j}\,\alpha

and
(t_{j})\subset[0,1]
, the sequence
(B_{j}-A_{0})
is also uniformly bounded in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
for every
p<\infty
.
Applying exactly the same compactness argument as in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
to the sequence
(B_{j},\psi_{j})
, and choosing
p>2m+1
, we then obtain, after passing to a subsequence, a connection
A_{\infty}
on
L
, a closed real
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

2
-form
\eta_{\infty}
, and a nonzero spinor
\psi_{\infty}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(S)
such that

\LineCurv_{A_{\infty}}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{\infty},\qquad\lVert\eta_{\infty}\rVert_{\ast}\leq C,\qquad\nabla_{A_{\infty}}\psi_{\infty}=0.

Moreover, the same equality-case argument as in
Lemma

3.3
implies that
\lvert\psi_{\infty}\rvert
is constant and nonzero, that
\scal_{g}\equiv 4m\pi C
, and that
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta_{\infty})\psi_{\infty}=-mC\,\psi_{\infty}
almost everywhere. ∎

By
Lemma

3.5
, there exist a unitary connection
A_{\infty}
on
L
, a closed real
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

2
-form
\eta_{\infty}
, and a nonzero spinor
\psi_{\infty}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(S)
such that
\nabla_{A_{\infty}}\psi_{\infty}=0
and
\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\eta_{\infty})\psi_{\infty}=-mC\,\psi_{\infty}
almost everywhere.
After rescaling
\psi_{\infty}
, we may assume
\lvert\psi_{\infty}\rvert\equiv 1
.

Define a real
2
-form
\omega
by

\omega(X,Y):=-\langle\mathrm{i}\,\clm(X\wedge Y)\psi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty}\rangle.

Since Clifford multiplication is parallel and
\nabla_{A_{\infty}}\psi_{\infty}=0
, we have
\nabla\omega=0
distributionally, hence
\omega
is smooth and parallel.

For almost every
p\in M
, apply the rigidity statement in
Lemma

3.1
to
V=\mathrm{T}_{p}M
,
E=S_{p}
,
\eta=(\eta_{\infty})_{p}
, and
\psi=\psi_{\infty}(p)
.
This yields an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{2m+1}
of
\mathrm{T}_{p}M
such that

(\eta_{\infty})_{p}=C\,\omega_{p}=C\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{2j-1}\wedge e^{2j}

and, with
B_{j}:=\mathrm{i}\,\clm(e_{2j-1})\clm(e_{2j})
,

B_{j}\psi_{\infty}(p)=-\psi_{\infty}(p)\qquad\text{for every }j=1,\dots,m.

Hence

\ker(\omega_{p})=\mathbb{R}e_{2m+1}.

Since
\omega
is parallel,
\ker(\omega)\subset\mathrm{T}M
is a parallel
1
-dimensional distribution.

Since
\omega
is smooth and
\eta_{\infty}=C\,\omega
almost everywhere, the form
\eta_{\infty}
is smooth.
Exactly as in the even-dimensional equality case, elliptic regularity then implies that
A_{\infty}
is smooth, and standard regularity implies that
\psi_{\infty}
is smooth.
Hence all identities above hold everywhere on
M
.
Exactly as in the even-dimensional equality case we obtain
[\eta_{\infty}]=c_{\mathbb{R}}
in
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
Since
\eta_{\infty}=C\,\omega
, we have

[\omega]=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{C}=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}

in
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\pi\colon(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g})\to(M,g)
be the universal cover.
Since
M
is closed,
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g})
is complete and simply connected.
Pull back
A_{\infty}
,
\eta_{\infty}
,
\omega
, and
\psi_{\infty}
to
\widetilde{M}
, and keep the same notation.
The form
\omega
is parallel with
1
-dimensional kernel, so by the de Rham splitting theorem there is an isometric splitting

(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g})\cong(N,g_{N})\times\mathbb{R}.

Moreover,
\omega
restricts to a parallel nondegenerate
2
-form
\omega_{N}
on
N
.
Let
J\in\End(\mathrm{T}N)
be defined by

g_{N}(JX,Y)=\omega_{N}(X,Y).

By construction,
\omega=\proj_{N}^{*}\omega_{N}
under this splitting.
The pointwise normal form above shows that
J^{2}=-\id
, so
J
is a parallel complex structure and
(N,g_{N},J)
is Kähler.

Finally, since
\psi_{\infty}
is parallel, the curvature identity
[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
gives

0=\frac{1}{2}\,\clm(\Ric(X))\psi_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2}\,\clm(\ins_{X}\LineCurv_{A_{\infty}})\psi_{\infty}

for every vector field
X
.
Since
\LineCurv_{A_{\infty}}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{\infty}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,C\,\omega
, this becomes

0=\frac{1}{2}\,\clm(\Ric(X))\psi_{\infty}-\pi\mathrm{i}\,\clm(\ins_{X}\eta_{\infty})\psi_{\infty}.

If
X\in\ker(\omega)
, then
\ins_{X}\eta_{\infty}=0
, hence
\clm(\Ric(X))\psi_{\infty}=0
and thus
\Ric(X)=0
.
If
X\perp\ker(\omega)
, then
\ins_{X}\omega=(JX)^{\flat}
and
\clm(JX)\psi_{\infty}=-\mathrm{i}\,\clm(X)\psi_{\infty}
as in the even-dimensional case, so
\clm(\ins_{X}\eta_{\infty})\psi_{\infty}=-\mathrm{i}\,C\,\clm(X)\psi_{\infty}
and therefore
\Ric(X)=2\pi C\,X
.
In particular,
(N,g_{N})
is Einstein.
Since it is Kähler, it is Kähler–Einstein, and the claimed splitting of
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g})
follows. ∎

4.
Geometric Applications

4.1.
Stable norm, duality and calibrations

Recall from
Section

2.1
that
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\ast}
denotes the comass of a
2
-form and

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}
the induced norm on
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
We now recall the dual homological notion that will be used in the applications.

A
2
-current on
M
is a continuous linear functional

T:\Dforms^{2}(M)\to\mathbb{R},

where
\Dforms^{2}(M)
is equipped with its standard
\Ct^{\infty}
-topology.
Its boundary is the
1
-current
\bd T
defined by

(\bd T)(\alpha):=T(\mathrm{d}\alpha),\qquad\alpha\in\Dforms^{1}(M).

A
2
-current is a cycle if
\bd T=0
. The mass of a
2
-current
T
is

M(T):=\sup\{\lvert T(\omega)\rvert:\omega\in\Dforms^{2}(M),\ \lVert\omega\rVert_{\ast}\leq 1\}.

Let
Z_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
denote the vector space of
2
-cycles of finite mass and
let
B_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})\subset Z_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
be the subspace of boundaries.
Via the standard identification

\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})\cong Z_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})/B_{2}(M;\mathbb{R}),

the stable norm of
h\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
is

\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}:=\inf\{M(T):T\in Z_{2}(M;\mathbb{R}),\ [T]=h\}.

If
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{torsion}\neq 0
, the stable
2
-systole is

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g):=\min\{\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}:0\neq h\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{torsion}\}.

The following classical theorem states the duality between the stable norm on homology and the comass norm on cohomology; see Federer
[
5
, Theorem 4.10]
and
Gromov
[
9
, Proposition 4.35]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
h\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
,

\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}=\sup\bigl\{\langle\varphi,h\rangle:\varphi\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R}),\ \lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}\leq 1\bigr\}.

When
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
has rank one, this duality takes a particularly nice form.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose that
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{torsion}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Let
a
be a generator, and
let
x\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
be the dual generator, that is,
\langle x,a\rangle=1
. Then

\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=\frac{1}{\lVert a\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}}.

Proof.

Since
\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
is one-dimensional, every class
\varphi\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
can be
written as
\varphi=tx_{\mathbb{R}}
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\langle\varphi,a\rangle=t\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=\lvert t\rvert\,\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}.

By
Theorem

4.1
,

\lVert a\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}=\sup\bigl\{t:t\in\mathbb{R},\ \lvert t\rvert\,\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}\leq 1\bigr\}=\frac{1}{\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}.\qed

A closed
2
-form
\omega\in\Dforms^{2}(M)
with
\lVert\omega\rVert_{\ast}\leq 1
is called a calibration.
If
T\in Z_{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
and
\omega
is a calibration such that
T(\omega)=M(T)
, then
T
is mass minimizing in its real homology class. Indeed,

M(T)=T(\omega)=\langle[\omega],[T]\rangle\leq\lVert[T]\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}\leq M(T),

so equality holds throughout.

In particular, if
\Sigma\subset M
is a compact oriented closed surface calibrated
by
\omega
, i.e.

\omega|_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma},

then its integration current
T_{\Sigma}
satisfies

M(T_{\Sigma})=\Area_{g}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}\omega,

and hence

\lVert[\Sigma]\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}=\Area_{g}(\Sigma).

On a Kähler manifold
(M,g,J)
, Wirtinger’s inequality implies that the Kähler form has comass
1
, and every complex
curve with its complex orientation is calibrated by it; see
[
11
, Example I, p. 59]
.
For the calibration formalism and the mass-minimizing property of calibrated currents, we refer the reader to the foundational paper by Harvey and Lawson
[
11
]
.

4.2.
Complex projective spaces

Let
J_{0}
denote the standard complex structure on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(-1)
the tautological line bundle and by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1)
its dual bundle.
More generally, we consider powers
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(k)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1)^{\otimes k}
.
Let

h:=\chernclass_{1}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1)\bigr)\in\HZ^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z}).

Then
\HZ^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
h
. If

\ell\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
is a projective line, endowed with its complex
orientation, then its homology class

a:=[\ell]\in\HZ_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})

generates
\HZ_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, and the pairing satisfies

\langle h,a\rangle=\int_{\ell}h=1.

Equivalently,
h
is the Poincaré dual of the class of a hyperplane

\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
Moreover,
(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
is Fano of index
n+1
, since

K^{-1}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(n+1),

where
K^{-1}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}
denotes the anticanonical bundle, that is, the dual of the canonical bundle
K_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}=\Lambda^{n,0}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.

Let
g_{\textup{FS}}
be the Fubini–Study metric on
(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})
, normalized by

\int_{\ell}\omega_{\textup{FS}}=\pi

for a projective line
\ell\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. Then

[\omega_{\textup{FS}}]=\pi h.

Moreover,
g_{\textup{FS}}
is Kähler–Einstein with

\Ric_{g_{\textup{FS}}}=2(n+1)\,g_{\textup{FS}},\qquad\scal_{g_{\textup{FS}}}=4n(n+1).

Next, we prove Theorem
A
which we restate below for convenience.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
such that

\scal_{g}\geq 4n(n+1).

Then

\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Moreover, if equality holds, then there exist a complex structure
J
on
M
and
a biholomorphism

B\colon(M,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})

such that

g=B^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

Proof.

Fix a diffeomorphism
f\colon M\to\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and endow
M
with the pullback orientation.
Let

b:=f_{*}^{-1}a\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}),\qquad x:=f^{*}h\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}).

Then
\langle x,b\rangle=\langle h,a\rangle=1
.
Every nonzero class in
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is of the form
kb
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
By homogeneity,

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)=\lVert b\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}.

Set

c:=(n+1)x\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}),

and let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
be its image in real cohomology.
As
f
is a diffeomorphism,
c\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\pmod{2}
and
\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)=f^{*}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})
.
Moreover,

\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)=f^{*}\!\left(\mathrm{e}^{\chernclass_{1}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})\right)=f^{*}\!\operatorname{Td}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})

from which

\langle[M],\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle=\langle[\mathbb{CP}^{n}],\operatorname{Td}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})\rangle=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}})=1.

Thus the hypotheses of
Theorem

3.2
are satisfied for the class
c
.
Applying
Theorem

3.2
with
m=n
and using
Corollary

4.2
, we obtain

4n(n+1)\leq\min_{M}\scal_{g}\leq 4n\pi\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=4n\pi(n+1)\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=\frac{4n(n+1)\pi}{\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)}.

Hence

\sys_{2}^{\textup{st}}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Assume now that
\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)=\pi
.
Then equality holds throughout the previous chain of inequalities, so equality holds in
Theorem

3.2
.
It follows that there exists a complex structure
J
on
M
such that
(M,g,J)
is Kähler–Einstein with Einstein constant
2(n+1)
, and its Kähler form
\omega
satisfies

[\omega]=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}=\frac{(n+1)x_{\mathbb{R}}}{(n+1)\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}=\pi x_{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, by the Hirzebruch–Kodaira–Yau theorem (see
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
originally proved in
[
12
,
27
]
), there is a biholomorphism

F\colon(M,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0}).

If we set

\widetilde{\omega}:=(F^{-1})^{*}\omega,\qquad\widetilde{g}:=(F^{-1})^{*}g,

then
\widetilde{g}
is a Kähler–Einstein metric on the standard complex manifold
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. Let
\widetilde{\rho}
denote its Ricci form.
Since
F
is a biholomorphism,

\widetilde{\rho}=(F^{-1})^{*}\rho=2(n+1)\widetilde{\omega}.

Using Chern–Weil theory for
\mathrm{T}^{1,0}\mathbb{CP}^{n}
(see
[
13
, §4.4]
), together with
[
13
, Prop. 4.A.11]
and the previous identity, we conclude

(4.1)

(n+1)h=\chernclass_{1}(\mathrm{T}^{1,0}\mathbb{CP}^{n})=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}[\tr_{\mathbb{C}}(F_{\nabla})]=\frac{[\tilde{\rho}]}{2\pi}=\frac{(n+1)[\tilde{\omega}]}{\pi}.

Hence

[\widetilde{\omega}]=\pi h=[\omega_{\textup{FS}}].

Thus
\widetilde{g}
is a Kähler–Einstein metric on the standard complex manifold

\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, and its Kähler form
\widetilde{\omega}
lies in the same Kähler
class as
\omega_{\textup{FS}}
. In order to apply the Bando–Mabuchi uniqueness theorem
[
1
, Theorem A]
,
we rescale and set

\widehat{\omega}:=2(n+1)\widetilde{\omega},\qquad\widehat{\omega}_{\textup{FS}}:=2(n+1)\omega_{\textup{FS}}.

Since constant rescaling does not change the Ricci form, both

\widehat{\omega}
and
\widehat{\omega}_{\textup{FS}}
are Kähler–Einstein and satisfy

[\widehat{\omega}]=[\widehat{\omega}_{\textup{FS}}]=2\pi\chernclass_{1}(\mathbb{CP}^{n}).

By the Bando–Mabuchi uniqueness theorem, there exists a holomorphic automorphism
\Phi\in\Aut(\mathbb{CP}^{n})
such that
\widehat{\omega}=\Phi^{*}\widehat{\omega}_{\textup{FS}}
.
Equivalently,

\widetilde{\omega}=\Phi^{*}\omega_{\textup{FS}},\qquad\widetilde{g}=\Phi^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

Therefore

g=F^{*}\widetilde{g}=F^{*}(\Phi^{*}g_{\textup{FS}})=(\Phi\circ F)^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

Setting

B:=\Phi\circ F\colon(M,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})

completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The constant
\pi
in
Theorem

4.3
is sharp. Indeed, for the normalized Fubini–Study metric
g_{\textup{FS}}
, the Kähler form
\omega_{\textup{FS}}
is a calibration and calibrates every projective line
\ell\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, see
[
11
, Example 1, page 59]
.
Hence
\ell
is mass minimizing in its real homology class and therefore

\lVert a\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}=\Area_{g_{\textup{FS}}}(\ell)=\int_{\ell}\omega_{\textup{FS}}=\pi.

4.3.
Extremality and rigidity in the odd-dimensional case

We now prove Theorem
B
, which we restate below for convenience.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
.
Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
such that

\scal_{g}\geq 4n(n+1).

Then

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Moreover, if equality holds, then the universal cover of
(M,g)
is isometric to

(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g_{\textup{FS}})\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Fix a diffeomorphism
f\colon M\to\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
and endow
M
with the pullback orientation.
Let
p_{1},p_{2}
denote the two projections from
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
onto
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, respectively.
Retain the notation
a\in\HZ_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
and
h\in\HZ^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
from
Section

4.2
, where
a=[\ell]
is the class of a projective line and
h=\chernclass_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1))
.
Choose
s_{0}\in\mathrm{S}^{1}
and set

b:=f^{-1}_{*}[\ell\times\{s_{0}\}]\in\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}),\qquad x:=f^{*}p_{1}^{*}h\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}),

and

\xi:=f^{*}p_{2}^{*}\theta\in\HZ^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}),

where
\theta\in\HZ^{1}(\mathrm{S}^{1};\mathbb{Z})
denotes the positive generator. Then

\langle x,b\rangle=1.

Since
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, every nonzero class in
\HZ_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is of the form

kb
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. By homogeneity,

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)=\lVert b\rVert_{\mathrm{st}}.

Set

c:=(n+1)x\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z}),

and let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in\HZ^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
be its image in real cohomology. Since
f
is a
diffeomorphism,

c\equiv w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)\pmod{2}.

Moreover,

\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)=f^{*}\bigl(p_{1}^{*}\operatorname{Td}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})\bigr),

and hence

\displaystyle\langle[M],\xi\smile\mathrm{e}^{c/2}\widehat{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{T}M)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}],p_{2}^{*}\theta\smile p_{1}^{*}\operatorname{Td}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[\mathbb{CP}^{n}],\operatorname{Td}(\mathrm{T}\mathbb{CP}^{n})\rangle=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}})=1.

Thus the hypotheses of
Theorem

3.4
are satisfied for the classes
c
and
\xi
.

Applying
Theorem

3.4
with
m=n
and using
Corollary

4.2
, we obtain

4n(n+1)\leq\min_{M}\scal_{g}\leq 4n\pi\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=4n\pi(n+1)\lVert x_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}=\frac{4n(n+1)\pi}{\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)}.

Hence

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)\leq\pi.

Assume now that
\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(M,g)=\pi
.
Then equality holds throughout the previous chain of inequalities, so equality holds in
Theorem

3.4
.
It follows that there exists a parallel
2
-form
\omega
on
M
such that

[\omega]=\frac{c_{\mathbb{R}}}{\lVert c_{\mathbb{R}}\rVert_{\mathrm{comass}}}=\pi x_{\mathbb{R}},

and that the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
is isometric to

(N,g_{N})\times\mathbb{R},

where
N
is a Kähler–Einstein manifold of real dimension
2n
.
If
\widetilde{\omega}
denotes the lift of
\omega
to
\widetilde{M}
, and
\omega_{N}
the Kähler form of
N
, then

\widetilde{\omega}=\proj_{N}^{*}\omega_{N}.

Let
q:\widetilde{M}\to M
be the universal covering.
Since
\widetilde{M}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, the manifold
N
is closed and homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
Hence,
\HZ^{2}(\widetilde{M};\mathbb{Z})\cong\HZ^{2}(N;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore, there exists a unique class
h_{N}\in\HZ^{2}(N;\mathbb{Z})
such that

q^{*}x=\proj_{N}^{*}h_{N}.

Since
[\widetilde{\omega}]=\pi q^{*}x
, we obtain

[\omega_{N}]=\pi h_{N}.

Furthermore, the scalar curvature of the product
(N,g_{N})\times\mathbb{R}
is pulled back from
N
, and the covering map
q:(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})\to(M,g)
is a local isometry.
Therefore,

\proj_{N}^{*}\scal_{g_{N}}=\scal_{\widetilde{g}}=q^{*}\scal_{g}\equiv 4n(n+1).

Thus,

\scal_{g_{N}}\equiv 4n(n+1).

Since
g_{N}
is Kähler–Einstein of complex dimension
n
, its Ricci form

\rho_{N}
satisfies

\rho_{N}=2(n+1)\omega_{N}.

Therefore

\chernclass_{1}(N)=\frac{[\rho_{N}]}{2\pi}=(n+1)\frac{[\omega_{N}]}{\pi}=(n+1)h_{N}.

Thus
N
is Fano of index
n+1
.
By the theorem of Kobayashi–Ochiai
[
4
, Theorem A]
(or originally
[
14
]
), there exists a biholomorphism

F:(N,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0}).

If we set

\widetilde{\omega}_{N}:=(F^{-1})^{*}\omega_{N},\qquad\widetilde{g}_{N}:=(F^{-1})^{*}g_{N},

then
\widetilde{g}_{N}
is a Kähler–Einstein metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and

[\widetilde{\omega}_{N}]=\pi h=[\omega_{\textup{FS}}].

Arguing exactly as in the proof of
Theorem

4.3
and applying the Bando–Mabuchi uniqueness theorem
[
1
, Theorem A]
, we conclude that there exists a biholomorphism

B:(N,J)\to(\mathbb{CP}^{n},J_{0})

such that

g_{N}=B^{*}g_{\textup{FS}}.

Hence

(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})\cong(\mathbb{CP}^{n},g_{\textup{FS}})\times\mathbb{R}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The constant
\pi
in
Theorem

4.5
is sharp.
Indeed, for the product metric
g_{\textup{FS}}+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)^{2}
on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
, the closed
2
-form
\proj_{1}^{*}\omega_{\textup{FS}}
is a calibration and calibrates every projective line
\ell\times\{s\}
.
Hence

\sys^{\textup{st}}_{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathrm{S}^{1},g_{\textup{FS}}+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)^{2})=\pi.
