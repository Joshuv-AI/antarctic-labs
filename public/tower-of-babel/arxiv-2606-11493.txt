Mean field games with terminal state constraints
Luciano Campi, Luca Di Persio, Viktorya Vardanyan

Abstract
We study a mean field game (MFG) of state and control with state dynamics described by stochastic differential equations driven by both idiosyncratic and common noise, and subject to the constraint that the terminal state variable belongs to a nonempty, convex, closed set. The mean-field interaction enters both the dynamics and the costs through state and control. Inspired by the work of El Karoui, Peng and Quenez [ KarouiPeng ] and Ji and Zhou [ JiZhou ] , we formulate an equivalent auxiliary MFG problem and derive the stochastic maximum principle for an auxiliary optimization problem under fixed flows. By means of a suitable forward-backward stochastic differential equation (FBSDE) of conditional McKean-Vlasov type, we establish a characterization of MFG solutions via such a system. Moreover, we prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the FBSDE system in the specific case where the running cost is zero and the state coefficients are linear. Additionally, we obtain some uniqueness results à la Lasry-Lions and we apply our findings to MFGs of optimal investment with a quadratic relative performance criterion.



Keywords:
Mean field games, state constraints, stochastic maximum principle, Ekeland’s variational principle, optimal investment.

Introduction

Mean field games (MFGs) were introduced by Lasry and Lions in
[
LasryLions
]
and, independently, by Huang, Malhamé, and Caines in
[
Huang
]
. MFGs are limiting models of large population symmetric stochastic differential games with mean field type interaction as the number of players
N
goes to infinity. Moreover, in these games, it is assumed that the influence of each player on the overall system becomes negligible as
N
increases.

In this paper, we study MFGs characterized by both idiosyncratic and common noise, with a constrained terminal state. Common noise affects all players simultaneously, introducing correlated uncertainty into the system. This is in contrast to idiosyncratic noise, which involves each player independently (see, e.g.,
[
CarmonaDelarueI
]
,
[
CarmonaDelarueII
]
,
[
CarmonaDelarueP
]
).

The dynamics of the private states of player
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
in the
N
-player game are given by

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} dX_{t}^{i}=b(t,X_{t}^{i},\alpha_{t}^{i},\beta_{t}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N})dt&+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{i},\alpha_{t}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N})dW_{t}^{i}+\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t}^{i},\beta_{t}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N})dW_{t}^{0}\end{aligned}\\
X_{0}^{i}=a,\ X_{T}^{i}\in\mathscr{D}\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha_{t}^{i}
,
\beta_{t}^{i}
are the controls of player
i
at time
t
, and
\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
denotes the empirical distribution of the state-control triples:

\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{(X_{t}^{j},\alpha_{t}^{j},\beta_{t}^{j})}.

W^{0},W^{1},\ldots,W^{N}
are
N+1
independent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions.
W^{0}
is said to be the common source of noise and
(\mathscr{F}^{0}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is the filtration generated by
W^{0}
satisfying the usual conditions of completeness and right-continuity.
\mathscr{D}
is a nonempty closed convex subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Each player chooses a control to minimize the following expected cost functional

J^{i}(\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{N},\beta^{1},\ldots,\beta^{N})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\bar{\ell}\left(t,X_{t}^{i},\alpha_{t}^{i},\beta_{t}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N}\right)dt+\phi\left(X_{T}^{i},\bar{\mu}_{T}^{X,N}\right)\right],

for given functions
\bar{\ell}
and
\phi
. In this
N

\bar{\mu}_{t}^{X,N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}}.

As

, we expect the empirical distributions

\mathscr{L}(X_{t},\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0})

1.

(Optimality condition) For a given flow
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, adapted to the common noise filtration, one solves the following optimization problem

\inf_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathscr{A},\,X_{T}\in\mathscr{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\bar{\ell}(t,X_{t},\alpha_{t},\beta_{t},\mu_{t})dt+\phi(X_{T},\mu_{T}^{X})\right],

(1)

where

denotes the flow of first marginals of

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\alpha_{t},\beta_{t},\mu_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t},\alpha_{t},\mu_{t})dW_{t}+\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t},\beta_{t},\mu_{t})dW_{t}^{0},

\displaystyle X_{0}=a.

\mathscr{A}
denotes the set of all admissible controls, which will be rigorously defined in the next section.

2.

(Fixed point or consistency condition)
Find a stochastic flow
(\hat{\mu}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

\hat{\mu}_{t}=\mathscr{L}(\hat{X}_{t},\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t}|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0}),\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T],

In the case of deterministic dynamics, MFGs with state constraints are studied by Cannarsa, Capuani, and Cardaliaguet in
[
CanCap
]
,
[
CanCar
]
, and
[
Cannarsa
]
. They solve the problem via a system of partial differential equations (PDEs) associated with the state-constrained MFG.
Under fairly general assumptions in
[
CanCap
]
, the existence of MFG equilibria is shown. In the same paper, the uniqueness of the solution is also derived under an adapted version of the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition. In
[
CanCar
]
, they address the regularity of solutions and obtain solutions that are more regular. In
[
Cannarsa
]
, the authors obtain a more useful regularity property of generalized solutions, which is a global semiconcavity property.
Porretta and Ricciardi have analysed state-constrained MFGs of second order in
[
Porretta
]
. They consider a model in which the individual dynamics are affected by additive idiosyncratic noise. The equilibrium is characterized by a solution to a second-order MFG system, in which the value function diverges at the boundary and the players’ density is smooth near it. Graber and Mayorga in
[
GM
]
extend the results of
[
CanCap
]
to the case of an MFG of controls, where the controls are velocities.

In
[
DaudinII
]
Daudin addresses the convergence problem of mean-field control theory with state constraints and non-degenerate idiosyncratic noise. The pre-limit problem involves a large number of interacting particles subject to symmetric, almost-sure constraints. In contrast, in the limit, the constraint acts on the law of a typical particle. The limit problem is an optimal control problem of the Fokker-Planck equation under state constraints on the space of probability measures, which has been studied by Daudin in
[
DaudinI
]
.

To address the terminal state-constrained problem
(
1
), we use the approach introduced by El Karoui, Peng, and Quenez in
[
KarouiPeng
]
. They use the backward approach, proving a version of the stochastic maximum principle by taking the terminal wealth as control. Ji and Zhou, in
[
JiZhou
]
, use the backward formulation and Ekeland’s variational principle
[
Ekeland
]
to derive the stochastic maximum principle (SMP). Peng in
[
Peng
]
proves the SMP for stochastic optimal control problems with an endpoint constraint on the state variable using Ekeland’s variational principle. Buckdahn, Djehiche, and Li in
[
BuckdahnLi1
]
,
[
BuckdahnLi2
]
study the optimal control for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) of mean-field type and obtain a Peng-type SMP.

Inspired by the approach developed in those articles, we formulate an auxiliary optimization problem by converting the terminal state constraint into a control constraint. This transformation allows for a more tractable analysis and solution approach. In the auxiliary problem, the initial condition of the original problem becomes a constraint. Using Ekeland’s variational principle, we derive a SMP that characterizes the optimal terminal state. Specifically, the transversality condition provides a necessary condition for the optimal terminal state. A similar transversality condition can be observed in the book by Yong and Zhou
[
YongZhou
, Chap. 3, Sec. 6]
, where they establish the SMP for optimal control problems with constrained cost functionals. Necessary and sufficient conditions for the SMP are obtained, leading to a suitable FBSDE system of conditional McKean-Vlasov type.
We study the well-posedness of such a system in the one-dimensional linear case. Finally, to illustrate the resolution method developed in this paper, we provide a financial application to an optimal investment problem for a representative risk-neutral agent with a quadratic relative performance criterion in two scenarios with and without common noise.

The paper’s structure is as follows: Section 2 introduces the problem setting and establishes the equivalence between the original MFG with terminal state constraints and the auxiliary formulation. In Section 3, we derive the SMP (both necessary and sufficient conditions), establish a verification theorem, discuss the uniqueness of MFG solutions, and prove the existence of the solution to the FBSDE for a specific linear case. In Section 4, we present the financial applications with some numerics and economic interpretations. Finally, the appendix contains the proofs of the necessary and sufficient conditions, as well as statements and proofs of several intermediate results.

(a,q,z)
gives the terminal value
X_{T}
(by solving the SDE), while
X_{T}
gives the triple
(a,q,z)
(by solving the BSDE).

Since
\xi
now is the control variable, the state constraint becomes a control constraint in (
7
). The backward formulation makes the problem easier to solve but in addition we will have the original initial condition as a constraint. We can now provide the definition of the auxiliary MFG problem.

Definition 2.3
.

A solution to the
auxiliary
MFG with terminal state constraint is any pair
(\hat{\xi},\hat{\mu})
, where
\hat{\xi}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
,
\hat{\mu}
is an
(\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0})
-adapted process with values in
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}M_{2}(\hat{\mu}_{t})^{2}dt\right]<\infty
, and the following two properties are satisfied:

1.

(Optimality condition) Given
\hat{\mu}
, the random variable
\hat{\xi}
solves the following optimal control problem

\inf_{\xi\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ell(t,X_{t},q_{t},z_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})dt+\phi(\xi,\hat{\mu}^{X}_{T})\right],

(8)

where
(X,q,z)\in\mathscr{S}^{2}_{n}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}
solves

-dX_{t}=f(t,X_{t},q_{t},z_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})dt-q_{t}dW_{t}-z_{t}dW_{t}^{0},\hskip 9.24994ptX_{T}=\xi;

(9)

2.

(Consistency condition) For all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\hat{\mu}_{t}=\mathscr{L}(\hat{X}_{t},\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t}|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0}),\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T],

where
\hat{X}
denotes the state with terminal condition
\hat{X}_{T}=\hat{\xi}
and

(\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t})=(\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma}_{0})(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{q}_{t},\hat{z}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}).

(10)

The following result establishes a bijective correspondence between the original MFG with terminal state constraint and the auxiliary formulation. This equivalence allows us to solve the constrained problem by analyzing the auxiliary MFG in the subsequent sections.

Proposition 2.4
.

Under assumptions (A1)-(A5), a triple
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\mu})
is a solution of the MFG with terminal state constraint (Definition
2.1
) if and only if the pair
(\hat{\xi},\hat{\mu})
is a solution of the auxiliary MFG as in Definition
2.3
.

Proof.

The proof consists of two directions:

Direction 1: Original
\implies
auxiliary.

Let
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\mu})
be a solution of the original MFG. We verify the conditions of Definition
2.3
:

Feasibility (
a
-feasibility):

By the optimality condition of the forward problem, the optimal state process
\hat{X}
satisfies the initial condition
\hat{X}_{0}=a
and the terminal constraint
\hat{X}_{T}\in\mathscr{D}
almost surely. By setting
\hat{\xi}=\hat{X}_{T}
, we clearly have
\hat{\xi}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
.
Let
\hat{q},\hat{z}
be defined by

\hat{q}_{t}=\sigma(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994pt\hat{z}_{t}=\sigma_{0}(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}).

Due to the equivalence between SDE (
2
) and BSDE (
6
) under assumption (A5), the triplet
(\hat{X},\hat{q},\hat{z})
solves the BSDE (
6
) with terminal condition
\hat{\xi}
. Since
\hat{X}_{0}=a
, the variable
\hat{\xi}
belongs to the set of
a
-feasible random variables
L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
.

Optimality condition:

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\hat{\xi}
is not optimal for the auxiliary problem (
7
). Then there exists a random variable
\xi^{\prime}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
such that

J(\xi^{\prime};\hat{\mu})<J(\hat{\xi};\hat{\mu}).

(11)

Let
(X^{\prime},q^{\prime},z^{\prime})
be the solution to the BSDE (
6
) associated with
\xi^{\prime}
. Since
\xi^{\prime}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
, we have
X^{\prime}_{0}=a
. We define the corresponding forward controls using the inverse mappings:

\alpha^{\prime}_{t}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,X^{\prime}_{t},q^{\prime}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994pt\beta^{\prime}_{t}=\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,X^{\prime}_{t},z^{\prime}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}).

(12)

We must ensure
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in\mathscr{A}
. Under assumptions (A1), (A2), and (A5), the inverse mappings
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
are Lipschitz continuous in
(x,q)
and
(x,z)
, respectively.

We prove the claimed Lipschitz continuity of
\tilde{\sigma}
only, as the same property for
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
can be shown in the same way. We recall first that, under (A1), (A2), (A5), the mapping
\alpha\mapsto\sigma(t,x,\alpha,\mu)
is bijective. We define its inverse as
\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q,\mu)=\alpha
. To prove this inverse is Lipschitz in
(x,q)
uniformly in
t
, we show that Lipschitz continuity holds separately in
x
and
q
uniformly in the other variable as well as in
t
. Joint Lipschitz continuity in
(x,q)
follows easily.
We start with checking the Lipschitz continuity in
x
.
Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and fix
q
. Let
\alpha_{1}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{1},q,\mu)
and
\alpha_{2}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{2},q,\mu)
. Then
\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{1},\mu)=\sigma(t,x_{2},\alpha_{2},\mu)=q
. Thus

\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{1},\mu)-\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{2},\mu)=\sigma(t,x_{2},\alpha_{2},\mu)-\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{2},\mu).

(13)

Since the first derivatives of
\sigma
are bounded (Assumption (A2)),
\sigma
is Lipschitz in
x
with some constant
L>0
uniform in the other variables. Therefore, (
13
) leads to

k_{1}|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|\leq|\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{1},\mu)-\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{2},\mu)|=|\sigma(t,x_{2},\alpha_{2},\mu)-\sigma(t,x_{1},\alpha_{2},\mu)|\leq L|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Applying this and (A5) yield

k_{1}|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|\leq L|x_{1}-x_{2}|\implies|\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{1},q,\mu)-\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{2},q,\mu)|\leq\frac{L}{k_{1}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Now, we turn to the Lipschitz continuity in
q
. Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Let
\alpha_{1}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q_{1},\mu)
and
\alpha_{2}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q_{2},\mu)
. By (A5), we have

|q_{1}-q_{2}|=|\sigma(t,x,\alpha_{1},\mu)-\sigma(t,x,\alpha_{2},\mu)|\geq k_{1}|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|,

which entails

|\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q_{1},\mu)-\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q_{2},\mu)|\leq\frac{1}{k_{1}}|q_{1}-q_{2}|.

Notice that
k_{1}
is a fixed positive constant from assumption (A5). Therefore, the Lipschitz constants
L/k_{1}
and
1/k_{1}
above are uniform in, respectively,
(t,q,\mu)
and
(t,x,\mu)
. Consequently,

|\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q,\mu)-\tilde{\sigma}(t,x^{\prime},q^{\prime},\mu)|\leq\frac{L}{k_{1}}|x-x^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{k_{1}}|q-q^{\prime}|,

and similarly for
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
.
We must ensure
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in\mathscr{A}
. We have just shown that, under assumptions (A2) and (A5), the inverse mappings
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
are Lipschitz continuous in
(x,q,z)
uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. To establish the linear growth of the inverse controls
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
in (
12
), we use first the Lipschitz continuity of
\tilde{\sigma}
in
(x,q)
shown above. For any
(x^{\prime},q^{\prime},\mu)
, we have:

|\tilde{\sigma}(t,x^{\prime},q^{\prime},\mu)|\leq|\tilde{\sigma}(t,0,0,\mu)|+\frac{L}{k_{1}}|x^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{k_{1}}|q^{\prime}|.

(14)

Let
\alpha_{0}=\tilde{\sigma}(t,0,0,\mu)
. By the definition of inverse function, it holds that
\sigma(t,0,\alpha_{0},\mu)=0
. We can now bound
\alpha_{0}
by comparing it to the origin
\alpha=0
. Using Assumption (A5), we obtain:

k_{1}|\alpha_{0}-0|\leq|\sigma(t,0,\alpha_{0},\mu)-\sigma(t,0,0,\mu)|.

Substituting
\sigma(t,0,\alpha_{0},\mu)=0
, the inequality simplifies to

|\alpha_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{k_{1}}|\sigma(t,0,0,\mu)|
.
Applying the growth condition at the origin from Assumption (A4), i.e.,
|\sigma(t,0,0,\mu)|\leq C(1+M_{2}(\mu))
, yields

|\tilde{\sigma}(t,0,0,\mu)|\leq\frac{C}{k_{1}}(1+M_{2}(\mu)).

Finally, substituting this back into (
14
), we conclude that the control
\alpha^{\prime}
satisfies

|\alpha^{\prime}_{t}|\leq\bar{C}(1+M_{2}(\hat{\mu}_{t})+|X_{t}^{\prime}|+|q_{t}^{\prime}|),

where
\bar{C}
is a constant independent of

. Identical arguments lead to the same bound for
\beta_{t}^{\prime}=\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,X^{\prime}_{t},z_{t}^{\prime},\hat{\mu}_{t})
.
Since the BSDE (
6
) solution
(X^{\prime},q^{\prime},z^{\prime})
belongs to
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{n}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}
and
\hat{\mu}
is an
(\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0})
-adapted process with values in
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}M_{2}(\hat{\mu}_{t})^{2}dt\right]<\infty
, the linear growth just established ensures that:

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(|\alpha^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}+|\beta^{\prime}_{t}|^{2})dt\right]\leq\bar{C}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(1+M_{2}(\mu_{t})^{2}+|X^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}+|q^{\prime}_{t}|^{2}+|z^{\prime}_{t}|^{2})dt\right]<\infty.

Furthermore, the continuity of
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
ensures that the progressive measurability of
(X^{\prime},q^{\prime},z^{\prime})
is preserved. Thus, we deduce
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in\mathscr{A}
.

By construction, the state associated to such pair satisfies
X^{\prime}_{0}=a
and
X^{\prime}_{T}=\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
. By the definition of the costs
\ell
and
\phi
, we have

J(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\hat{\mu})=J(\xi^{\prime};\hat{\mu})\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptJ(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta};\hat{\mu})=J(\hat{\xi};\hat{\mu}).

Substituting these into (
11
), we obtain
J(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\hat{\mu})<J(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta};\hat{\mu})
, which contradicts the optimality of
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})
in the original MFG problem (
4
). Thus,
\hat{\xi}
must be optimal for the auxiliary problem.

Consistency condition:

The consistency condition for the forward problem is

\hat{\mu}_{t}=\mathscr{L}(\hat{X}_{t},\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t}|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{0}).

Since the processes
(\hat{X},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})
and the flow
\hat{\mu}
are identical in both formulations via the bijective mappings, the consistency condition in Definition
2.3
is satisfied.
Therefore,
(\hat{\xi},\hat{\mu})
is a solution for the auxiliary MFG.

Direction 2: Auxiliary
\implies
original.

Let
(\hat{\xi},\hat{\mu})
be a solution of the auxiliary MFG with corresponding state
(\hat{X},\hat{q},\hat{z})
as in Definition
2.3
. Going back to the original problem, we have
J(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})=J(\hat{\xi})
for

(\hat{\alpha}_{t},\hat{\beta}_{t})=(\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma}_{0})(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{q}_{t},\hat{z}_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T].

Under assumptions (A1), (A2), (A4) and (A5), the inverse mappings
(\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma}_{0})
are Lipschitz continuous in
(x,q,z)
uniformly in
t
, which implies a linear growth condition (see the proof of Direction 1). Since the BSDE solution
(\hat{X},\hat{q},\hat{z})
belongs to
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{n}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}
, it follows that
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})\in\mathscr{A}
. With this admissibility established, we have
J(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})\leq J(\alpha,\beta)
for any admissible control pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathscr{A}
satisfying

a.s. Now, let

\xi=X_{T}^{\alpha,\beta},\hskip 9.24994ptq_{t}=\sigma(t,X_{t},\alpha_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994ptz_{t}=\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t},\beta_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t}).

Under the growth assumptions (A2) and (A4), and since
(X,\alpha,\beta)
belongs to
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{n}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}
, it follows that
(q,z)\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{n\times d}
.
Consequently, we have
\xi\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
. Therefore, the optimality condition in Definition
2.1
is satisfied. The consistency condition therein is also trivially fulfilled and we can conclude that
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\mu})
is a solution for the MFG with terminal constraint.
∎

\mu

3.2
On the uniqueness of the MFG solution

In this section we prove some uniqueness results for the auxiliary MFG under some further assumptions. Using the bijective correspondence between solutions of the auxiliary problem and those of the original problem, we deduce uniqueness of the original MFG solution. The working assumptions are the following:

(C1)

The coefficients
b,\sigma,\sigma_{0}
do not depend on the measure argument.

(C2)

The coefficients
b,\sigma,\sigma_{0}
are affine in the state and control variables, i.e.,

b(t,x,\alpha,\beta)=b_{0}(t)+B_{x}(t)x+B_{\alpha}(t)\alpha+B_{\beta}(t)\beta,

\sigma(t,x,\alpha)=\sigma_{1}(t)+S_{x}(t)x+S_{\alpha}(t)\alpha,

and

\sigma_{0}(t,x,\beta)=\sigma_{2}(t)+S_{x}^{0}(t)x+S_{\beta}^{0}(t)\beta,

for all
(t,x,\alpha,\beta)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, where the coefficients

(b_{0},B_{x},B_{\alpha},B_{\beta},\sigma_{1},S_{x},S_{\alpha},\sigma_{2},S_{x}^{0},S_{\beta}^{0})

are bounded measurable functions on
[0,T]
. Moreover, for every
t\in[0,T]
, the matrices
S_{\alpha}(t)
,
S_{\beta}^{0}(t)
are invertible and the matrix-valued functions

t\mapsto S_{\alpha}(t)^{-1},t\mapsto S_{\beta}^{0}(t)^{-1}

are bounded and measurable on
[0,T]
.

(C3)

The running cost
\bar{\ell}
has a separated structure of the form:

\bar{\ell}(t,x,\alpha,\beta,\mu)=\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,x,\mu)+\bar{\ell}_{1}(t,x,\alpha,\beta),

where
\bar{\ell}
is convex in
(x,\alpha,\beta)
for all
(t,\mu)
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that the following growth conditions hold:

|\bar{\ell}(t,x,\alpha,\beta,\mu)|\leq C_{0}\left(1+|x|+|\alpha|+|\beta|+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2},

|\phi(x,\mu)|\leq C_{0}\left(1+|x|+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2},

for all
(t,x,\alpha,\beta,\mu)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(C4)

\phi
is

\lambda
-convex in
x
, uniformly in
\mu
, i.e.

\phi(x,\mu)-\phi(x^{\prime},\mu)\geq\phi_{x}(x^{\prime},\mu)(x-x^{\prime})+\lambda|x-x^{\prime}|^{2},\hskip 18.49988ptx,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some constant
\lambda>0
.

(C5)

The functions
\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
and
\phi
are monotone in the sense of Lasry and Lions (see the following Definition
3.3
).

Definition 3.3
.

A function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}

is said to be
monotone
(in the sense of Lasry and Lions), if for all
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function
x\mapsto\psi(x,\mu)
is at most of quadratic growth uniformly in
\mu
, and for all
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\psi(x,\mu)-\psi(x,\mu^{\prime})\right)\,d(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(x)\geq 0.

Under the assumptions established above, we derive several structural properties of the coefficients associated with the auxiliary MFG problem, which will be useful to prove the uniqueness results.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under assumptions
(C1)–(C3)
, the driver
f
and the running cost
\ell
, are defined by

f(t,x,q,z)\equiv-b\big(t,x,\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q),\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)\big),

\ell(t,x,q,z,\mu)\equiv\bar{\ell}\big(t,x,\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q),\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z),\mu\big),

for all
(t,x,q,z,\mu)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(i)

The driver
f
is affine in
(x,q,z)
and is independent of the measure argument
\mu
.

(ii)

The running cost
\ell
is convex in
(x,q,z)
.

(iii)

Setting
\ell_{0}(t,x,\mu)\equiv\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,x,\mu)
and
\ell_{1}(t,x,q,z)\equiv\bar{\ell}_{1}\big(t,x,\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q),\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)\big)

has the separated structure

\ell(t,x,q,z,\mu)=\ell_{0}(t,x,\mu)+\ell_{1}(t,x,q,z).

(iv)

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that the following quadratic growth condition holds globally:

|\ell(t,x,q,z,\mu)|\leq C\left(1+|x|+|q|+|z|+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2}.

(v)

The function
\ell_{0}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
inherits the Lasry–Lions monotonicity property from
\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
.

Proof.

We prove each item using the structural assumptions provided.

(i)–(iii).
By assumption (C2), the inverse control functions
\alpha=\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q)
and
\beta=\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)
are explicitly given by the affine relations:

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q)

\displaystyle=S_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}\big(q-\sigma_{1}(t)-S_{x}(t)x\big),

(19)

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)

\displaystyle=S_{\beta}^{0}(t)^{-1}\big(z-\sigma_{2}(t)-S_{x}^{0}(t)x\big).

Substituting (
19
) into the linear expression for
b
from (C2) yields:

f(t,x,q,z)=-b_{0}(t)-B_{x}(t)x-B_{\alpha}(t)\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q)-B_{\beta}(t)\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z).

Since
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
are affine in
(x,q)
and
(x,z)
respectively,
f
is affine in
(x,q,z)
. Moreover, by assumption (C1), the coefficients

\mu
, which guarantees that the driver

does not depend on
\mu
.

Similarly, substituting (
19

\bar{\ell}

\ell(t,x,q,z,\mu)=\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,x,\mu)+\bar{\ell}_{1}\big(t,x,\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q),\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)\big).

Defining
\ell_{0}\equiv\bar{\ell}_{0}
and
\ell_{1}(t,x,q,z)\equiv\bar{\ell}_{1}(t,x,\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q),\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z))
, we immediately obtain the desired separated structure.

(ii)
Let
(x_{1},q_{1},z_{1}),(x_{2},q_{2},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, and let
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Since the mappings (
19
) are affine, we have:

\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{\lambda},q_{\lambda})=\lambda\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{1},q_{1})+(1-\lambda)\tilde{\sigma}(t,x_{2},q_{2})\equiv\lambda\alpha_{1}+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{2},

\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x_{\lambda},z_{\lambda})=\lambda\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x_{1},z_{1})+(1-\lambda)\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x_{2},z_{2})\equiv\lambda\beta_{1}+(1-\lambda)\beta_{2},

where we set
x_{\lambda}=\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}
,
q_{\lambda}=\lambda q_{1}+(1-\lambda)q_{2}
, and
z_{\lambda}=\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)z_{2}
. Exploiting convexity of
\bar{\ell}
in
(x,\alpha,\beta)
from (C3), we deduce:

\displaystyle\ell(t,x_{\lambda},q_{\lambda},z_{\lambda},\mu)

\displaystyle=\bar{\ell}\big(t,\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2},\lambda\alpha_{1}+(1-\lambda)\alpha_{2},\lambda\beta_{1}+(1-\lambda)\beta_{2},\mu\big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\bar{\ell}(t,x_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\mu)+(1-\lambda)\bar{\ell}(t,x_{2},\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},\mu)

\displaystyle=\lambda\ell(t,x_{1},q_{1},z_{1},\mu)+(1-\lambda)\ell(t,x_{2},q_{2},z_{2},\mu),

which shows that
\ell
is convex in
(x,q,z)
.

(iv)
Since the time-dependent coefficients in (C2) and the inverse matrices
S_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}
,
S_{\beta}^{0}(t)^{-1}
are bounded and measurable in

, there exists a uniform constant
K>0
such that

, and similarly for the
\sigma_{0}
coefficients. Applying the triangle inequality to (

|\tilde{\sigma}(t,x,q)|\leq K\big(|q|+K|x|+K\big)\leq K_{1}\big(1+|x|+|q|\big),

|\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(t,x,z)|\leq K\big(|z|+K|x|+K\big)\leq K_{1}\big(1+|x|+|z|\big),

for a sufficiently large constant

from (C3) gives:

\displaystyle|\ell(t,x,q,z,\mu)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(1+|x|+K_{1}(1+|x|+|q|)+K_{1}(1+|x|+|z|)+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left((1+2K_{1})+(1+2K_{1})|x|+K_{1}|q|+K_{1}|z|+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+|x|+|q|+|z|+M_{2}(\mu)\right)^{2},

for some constant

(v)
This property is obvious since
\ell_{0}(t,x,\mu)\equiv\bar{\ell}_{0}(t,x,\mu)
for all

Now we establish uniqueness of the optimal control for the auxiliary MFG problem. This result will play a key role in the analysis and it will also be used in the applications of Section

Proposition 3.5
.

Let assumptions (C1)-(C4) hold. Then the optimal control problem (
8
) admits at most one minimizer in
L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
.

Proof.

Let
\xi^{1},\xi^{2}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
be two optimal minimizers. We prove that

\xi^{1}=\xi^{2}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}.

Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
and define

\xi^{\theta}:=\theta\xi^{1}+(1-\theta)\xi^{2}.

Since
\mathscr{D}
is convex, under assumptions (C1)–(C2)
f
is affine (for the proof see Lemma
3.4
) and
\xi^{1},\xi^{2}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
, we first show that
\xi^{\theta}\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathscr{D})
. Consequently,
L_{a}^{2}(\mathscr{D})
is convex.

Let
(X^{i},q^{i},z^{i})
be the solution of (
6
) corresponding to

\xi^{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Since
\xi^{1},\xi^{2}
are
a
-feasible, we have

X_{0}^{1}=X_{0}^{2}=a
. Define

X_{t}^{\theta}:=\theta X_{t}^{1}+(1-\theta)X_{t}^{2},\hskip 18.49988ptq_{t}^{\theta}:=\theta q_{t}^{1}+(1-\theta)q_{t}^{2},\hskip 18.49988ptz_{t}^{\theta}:=\theta z_{t}^{1}+(1-\theta)z_{t}^{2}.

Using the affine structure of
f
, we obtain

\theta f(t,X_{t}^{1},q_{t}^{1},z_{t}^{1})+(1-\theta)f(t,X_{t}^{2},q_{t}^{2},z_{t}^{2})=f(t,X_{t}^{\theta},q_{t}^{\theta},z_{t}^{\theta}).

Therefore, by multiplying the BSDEs for
(X^{1},q^{1},z^{1})
and

(X^{2},q^{2},z^{2})
by
\theta
and
1-\theta
, respectively, and adding them, we see that
(X^{\theta},q^{\theta},z^{\theta})
is a unique solution to (
6
) with terminal condition
\xi^{\theta}
. Indeed,

X_{T}^{\theta}=\theta X_{T}^{1}+(1-\theta)X_{T}^{2}=\theta\xi^{1}+(1-\theta)\xi^{2}=\xi^{\theta}.

In particular,

X_{0}^{\theta}=\theta X_{0}^{1}+(1-\theta)X_{0}^{2}=\theta a+(1-\theta)a=a.

Hence
\xi^{\theta}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
. Thus
L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
is convex.
We now prove convexity of the cost functional. Under assumption (C3)

\ell
is convex in
(x,q,z)
(for the proof see Lemma
3.4
),

\displaystyle\ell(t,X_{t}^{\theta},q_{t}^{\theta},z_{t}^{\theta},\mu_{t})\leq\theta\ell(t,X_{t}^{1},q_{t}^{1},z_{t}^{1},\mu_{t})+(1-\theta)\ell(t,X_{t}^{2},q_{t}^{2},z_{t}^{2},\mu_{t}).

Integrating over
[0,T]
and taking expectations gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ell(t,X_{t}^{\theta},q_{t}^{\theta},z_{t}^{\theta},\mu_{t})\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq\theta\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ell(t,X_{t}^{1},q_{t}^{1},z_{t}^{1},\mu_{t})\,dt\right]

(20)

\displaystyle+(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ell(t,X_{t}^{2},q_{t}^{2},z_{t}^{2},\mu_{t})\,dt\right].

On the other hand, by assumption (C4) since
\phi
is
\lambda
-convex in
x
, we have

\phi(\xi^{\theta},\mu_{T}^{X})\leq\theta\phi(\xi^{1},\mu_{T}^{X})+(1-\theta)\phi(\xi^{2},\mu_{T}^{X})-\lambda\theta(1-\theta)|\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}|^{2}.

Taking expectations, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi^{\theta},\mu_{T}^{X})]\leq\theta\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi^{1},\mu_{T}^{X})]+(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi^{2},\mu_{T}^{X})]-\lambda\theta(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}|^{2}\right].

(21)

Adding the two inequalities (
20
) and (
21

J(\xi^{\theta})\leq\theta J(\xi^{1})+(1-\theta)J(\xi^{2})-\lambda\theta(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}|^{2}\right].

Since
\xi^{1}
and
\xi^{2}
are both minimizers,

J(\xi^{1})=J(\xi^{2})=\inf_{\xi\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})}J(\xi):=\inf J.

Moreover, since
\xi^{\theta}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathscr{D})
, we also have

J(\xi^{\theta})\geq\inf J
.
Therefore,

\inf J\leq J(\xi^{\theta})\leq\inf J-\lambda\theta(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}|^{2}\right].

Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi^{1}-\xi^{2}|^{2}\right]=0
.
Consequently,
\xi^{1}=\xi^{2}
a.s., and the problem admits at most one minimizer in
L_{a}^{2}(\mathscr{D})
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

We observe that in the applications of Section 4, the terminal cost functional

A direct consequence of the previous two propositions is the following corollary, establishing a uniqueness result for the original MFG with constraint.

Corollary 3.9
.

Under the assumptions
(C1)–(C5)
there exists at most one solution
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\mu})
to the original MFG with terminal state constraint (Definition
2.1
).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
, there is a bijective correspondence between solutions
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},\hat{\mu})

(\hat{\xi},\hat{\mu})

(based on the Lipschitz continuity and linear growth of the inverse maps

3.3
Existence of solutions for the FBSDE system in the linear case

We consider the FBSDE system (
18
) with
\ell=0
, where the coefficients are linear and independent of the measure variable, except for
\phi
, which depends on the measure through its conditional average. Since in the FBSDE system (
18
) we have
h_{0}>0
, we may normalize the adjoint equation and the terminal variational inequality by dividing by
h_{0}
. Hence, without loss of generality, we take
h_{0}=1
. We will prove existence and uniqueness of solutions of the FBSDE system in the one-dimensional case, where
\mathscr{D}=[c,\infty)
, for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
, together with the condition

m_{T}=\mathbb{E}(X_{T}\mid\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0}).

(25)

More specifically, to establish the existence and uniqueness result we need some more technical assumptions:

(A6)

The drift function
f
is linear in
(x,q,z)
, i.e.,

f(t,x,q,z)=b_{0}(t)x+qc_{0}(t)+z\tilde{c}_{0}(t),\hskip 9.24994pt(t,x,q,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(b_{0},c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0}):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

are bounded measurable functions.

(A7)

We assume that the function
\phi_{x}(x,m)
is linear in both arguments and satisfies

\phi_{x}(x,m)=k_{0}+k_{1}x+k_{2}m,\hskip 9.24994pt(x,m)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

for some constants
k_{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, satisfying
k_{1}>0
,
k_{2}\leq 0
, and
k_{1}+k_{2}>0
.

Under these assumptions, the mean field FBSDE system (
18
) reduces to the following:

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=-[b_{0}(t)X_{t}+q_{t}c_{0}(t)^{\top}+z_{t}\tilde{c}_{0}(t)^{\top}]dt+q_{t}dW_{t}+z_{t}dW_{t}^{0}\\
X_{0}=a,\hskip 9.24994ptX_{T}=\xi\\
dY_{t}=b_{0}(t)Y_{t}dt+c_{0}(t)Y_{t}dW_{t}+\tilde{c}_{0}(t)Y_{t}dW_{t}^{0}\\
Y_{0}=h_{1}\\
\big(Y_{T}+\phi_{x}(\xi,m_{T})\big)\big(\xi^{\prime}-\xi\big)\geq 0\ a.s.,\ \forall\xi^{\prime}\in L^{2}([c,\infty)).\end{cases}

(26)

We will be using the notation

\mathscr{E}_{t}(c,c^{\prime}):=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}(c(s)dW_{s}+c^{\prime}(s)dW^{0}_{s})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(|c(s)|^{2}+|c^{\prime}(s)|^{2})ds\right)

for the Doléans-Dade exponential of a Wiener integral with respect to the
2d
-dimensional Brownian motion
(W,W^{0})
, where
(c,c^{\prime}):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
are measurable and bounded functions.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
a>c\ e^{\int_{0}^{T}b_{0}(t)\,dt}
. Under Assumptions (A1)-(A7), the FBSDE system (
26
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

The adjoint SDE can be solved for
t\in[0,T]
explicitly as

Y_{t}=h_{1}e^{\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(s)\,ds}\mathscr{E}_{t}\left(c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0}\right).

(27)

Moreover, since
b_{0},c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0}
are bounded, the stochastic exponential
\mathscr{E}(c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0})
and its reciprocal have finite moments of every order. By Doob’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}|h_{1}|^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathscr{E}_{T}(c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0})^{p}\right]<\infty,

for some constant
C_{p}>0
, so that
Y\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
for all
p\geq 2
. In particular,
Y^{(1)},1/Y^{(1)}\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
for all
p\geq 2
.

Defining the set
\Omega_{0}=\{\omega\in\Omega\ |\ \xi(\omega)=c\}
, the condition

\big(Y_{T}+\phi_{x}(\xi,\mu)\big)\big(\xi^{\prime}-\xi\big)\geq 0,\text{a.s.},\ \forall\xi^{\prime}\in L^{2}([c,\infty))

can be written as

Y_{T}+\phi_{x}(\xi,m_{T})=0\ \text{a.s. on}\ \Omega_{0}^{c},

(28)

Y_{T}+\phi_{x}(c,m_{T})\geq 0\ \text{a.s. on}\ \Omega_{0}.

(29)

From (
28
) and (
29
) we obtain

\xi=\left(\dfrac{-Y_{T}-\phi_{x}(c,m_{T})}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}+c.

(30)

We prove existence and uniqueness by separating the problem into two steps. First, for a fixed
h_{1}
, we solve for the conditional mean
m_{T}
. Second, we determine
h_{1}
from the initial constraint

X_{0}=a
.
Let us introduce the following convenient notation, stressing the dependence on
h_{1}
:

Y_{t}^{(h_{1})}:=Y_{t}=h_{1}e^{\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(s)ds}\mathscr{E}_{t}\left(c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0}\right),

so that
Y_{t}^{(h_{1})}=h_{1}Y_{t}^{(1)}
, where
Y_{t}^{(1)}
does not depend on
h_{1}
. Moreover, for any process
Z\neq 0
a.s., and any time instants

Z_{s,t}:=Z_{t}/Z_{s}

Y^{(h_{1})}_{t}=h_{1}Y_{t}^{(1)}=h_{1}Y_{s}^{(1)}Y_{s,t}^{(1)},

(31)

where

is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{s}

Y_{s}^{(1)}
.
Therefore, under Assumption (A7) the representation (
30

\xi=c+\left(\frac{-Y_{T}^{(h_{1})}-k_{0}-k_{1}c-k_{2}m_{T}}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}.

(32)

Hence, for fixed
h_{1}

must satisfy

(33)

For fixed
h_{1}
, define the map

\Gamma_{h_{1}}:L^{2}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})

\Gamma_{h_{1}}(m):=c+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{-Y_{T}^{(h_{1})}-k_{0}-k_{1}c-k_{2}m}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}\;\middle|\;\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0}\right].

(34)

We show that
\Gamma_{h_{1}}
is a contraction. Let
m,m^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})
. Since the map
x\mapsto x^{+}
is
1
-Lipschitz, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma_{h_{1}}(m)-\Gamma_{h_{1}}(m^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\frac{-Y_{T}^{(h_{1})}-k_{0}-k_{1}c-k_{2}m}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}-\left(\frac{-Y_{T}^{(h_{1})}-k_{0}-k_{1}c-k_{2}m^{\prime}}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|k_{2}|}{k_{1}}\|m-m^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}.

Under assumption (A7),
k_{1}>0
,
k_{2}\leq 0
and
k_{1}+k_{2}>0

|k_{2}|<k_{1},
and so

\frac{|k_{2}|}{k_{1}}<1

Thus,
\Gamma_{h_{1}}

L^{2}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})

h_{1}

\xi^{(h_{1})}:=c+\left(\frac{-Y_{T}^{(h_{1})}-k_{0}-k_{1}c-k_{2}m_{T}^{(h_{1})}}{k_{1}}\right)^{+}.

from the initial condition

.

It remains to construct processes

26
) and to verify that
(X,q,z)\in\mathscr{S}^{2}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{1\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{2}_{1\times d}
. We proceed directly from the terminal variable. The fixed-point argument for
m_{T}^{(h_{1})}
works in
L^{p}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})
, for every
p\geq 2
, with the same contraction constant
|k_{2}|/k_{1}<1
. Since
Y_{T}^{(1)}
has moments of all orders, it follows that
m_{T}^{(h_{1})}\in L^{p}(\mathscr{F}_{T}^{0})
,
\xi^{(h_{1})}\in L^{p}(\mathscr{F}_{T})
, and
\xi^{(h_{1})}Y_{T}^{(1)}\in L^{p}(\mathscr{F}_{T})
, for every
p\geq 2
. Define

M_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\big[\xi^{(h_{1})}Y_{T}^{(1)}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\big],\hskip 18.49988ptt\in[0,T].

By Doob’s inequality,
M\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
for every
p\geq 2
. Set

X_{t}:=\frac{M_{t}}{Y_{t}^{(1)}},\hskip 18.49988ptt\in[0,T].

Since
1/Y^{(1)}\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
for every
p\geq 2
, Hölder’s inequality gives
X\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
for every
p\geq 2
. Moreover,
X_{T}=\xi^{(h_{1})}
and, by the definition of the zero of
G
,

X_{0}=\mathbb{E}\big[\xi^{(h_{1})}Y_{T}^{(1)}\big]=a.

By the martingale representation theorem, there exist predictable processes
(\widetilde{q},\widetilde{z})
such that

M_{t}=a+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{q}_{s}\,dW_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{z}_{s}\,dW_{s}^{0},\hskip 18.49988ptt\in[0,T].

The Burkholder-Davis-Gundy and Doob inequalities yield
(\widetilde{q},\widetilde{z})\in\mathscr{H}^{p}_{1\times d}\times\mathscr{H}^{p}_{1\times d}
, for every
p\geq 2
. Applying Itô’s formula to
X_{t}=M_{t}/Y_{t}^{(1)}
, and using

dY_{t}^{(1)}=b_{0}(t)Y_{t}^{(1)}dt+c_{0}(t)Y_{t}^{(1)}dW_{t}+\widetilde{c}_{0}(t)Y_{t}^{(1)}dW_{t}^{0},

we obtain

dX_{t}=\big(-b_{0}(t)X_{t}-q_{t}c_{0}(t)^{\top}-z_{t}\widetilde{c}_{0}(t)^{\top}\big)dt+q_{t}\,dW_{t}+z_{t}\,dW_{t}^{0},

where

q_{t}=\frac{\widetilde{q}_{t}}{Y_{t}^{(1)}}-X_{t}c_{0}(t),\hskip 18.49988ptz_{t}=\frac{\widetilde{z}_{t}}{Y_{t}^{(1)}}-X_{t}\widetilde{c}_{0}(t).

Since
c_{0}
and
\widetilde{c}_{0}
are bounded, while
X\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
,
1/Y^{(1)}\in\mathscr{S}^{p}
, and
(\widetilde{q},\widetilde{z})\in\mathscr{H}^{p}\times\mathscr{H}^{p}
for sufficiently large

, Hölder’s inequality gives
q,z\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{1\times d}
. Thus
(X,q,z)
solves the first equation in (
26
) and belongs to the required space.

4
Application to optimal investment for risk-neutral agents with quadratic relative performance criterion

We consider a MFG of optimal investment for risk-neutral investors with quadratic relative performance criterion and a terminal wealth constraint. Specifically, the investors require their terminal wealth to stay above a certain fixed constant threshold. This variant of the optimal investment problem received a lot of attention in the past mathematical finance literature; we refer especially to the papers by Cox and Huang
[
CoxHuang
]
, El Karoui et al.
[
ElK-J-L
]
, Korn
[
Korn
]
and Teplá
[
Tepla
]
, to cite just a few. The idea is that the representative investor fears big losses, hence she incorporates the tolerated minimum wealth in her criterion. For the sake of simplicity, here we assume that the minimum level is constant and applies only at maturity. Moreover, in order to match our general setting, we consider investors who are willing to maximize their expected terminal wealth and, at the same time, compare their performance to their competitors. The latter is described by some quadratic relative performance criterion appearing in their objective.
Optimal investment problems under relative performance criteria have been studied rather extensively in the literature, i.e., by Espinosa, Touzi in
[
EspinosaTouzi
]
and by Lacker, Soret, Zariphopoulou in
[
LackerS
]
,
[
LackerZ
]
in the MFG setting.

We start by describing the
N
-player optimal investment game. Then, we will pass to MFG for
N\to\infty
, which we will set up in a more rigorous way. We assume that the interest rate is zero, so that the riskless asset is identically equal to one, and that each agent
i=1,\ldots,N
invests in a risky asset, whose price at time
t
is denoted
S_{t}^{i}
and follows the dynamics

\frac{dS^{i}_{t}}{S_{t}^{i}}=bdt+\sigma dW^{i}+\sigma^{0}dW_{t}^{0},\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T],

where
W^{0},W^{1},\ldots,W^{N}
are
N+1
independent Brownian motions,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and both
\sigma,\sigma^{0}
are nonnegative constants with
\sigma+\sigma^{0}>0
. The information of the agents is modelled by the filtration generated by all these Brownian motions and satisfying the usual conditions.
Each agent
i
trades using a self-financing strategy
(\alpha_{t}^{i})_{t\in[0,T]}
, which represents the proportion of wealth invested in the risky asset. Therefore, agent
i
’s wealth, denoted
X_{t}^{i}
, evolves as

dX_{t}^{i}=\alpha_{t}^{i}X_{t}^{i}(bdt+\sigma dW_{t}^{i}+\sigma^{0}dW_{t}^{0}),\ X_{0}^{i}=a,

where
a>0
is the initial wealth.

Each agent aims at maximizing her expected terminal wealth while considering her performance relative to the average terminal wealth of all investors. Hence, given the terminal wealth of all agent
i
’s competitors
(X_{T}^{j})_{j\neq i}
, her optimization problem is

\sup_{\alpha^{i}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T}^{i}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(X_{T}^{i}-\dfrac{\theta}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}^{N}X_{T}^{j}\right)^{2}\right],

under the constraint
X_{T}^{i}\geq c
, for a given threshold
0<c<a
; where
\gamma>0
is the agent’s risk aversion coefficient and
\theta\in[0,1)
models her relative performance concern.

Our goal is to solve the corresponding MFG using the methods developed in the previous sections with
\mathscr{D}=[c,+\infty)
. Therefore, we will consider only the two cases
\sigma^{0}=0
(no common noise) and
\sigma=0
(pure common noise). The first case corresponds to a situation where each investor is specialized in a specific asset, while in the second one all agents are investing in the same asset. In both cases below,
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
will denote the filtration generated by two independent real-valued Brownian motions
W^{0},W
defined in a common complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
. The filtration is assumed to be completed with all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets, so that the usual conditions are fulfilled.
In the intermediate situation where both

\sigma^{0}
and
\sigma
are not zero, the method developed in the paper can be used if we allow for different controls in the noise terms, which is not a natural assumption in this application.

The case with no common noise.

In this case, i.e.,
\sigma^{0}=0
, the corresponding MFG is given by the following two-step procedure:

1.

Fix a constant
m_{T}\in\mathbb{R}
and solve the following maximization problem

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T}-\frac{\gamma}{2}(X_{T}-\theta m_{T})^{2}\right],

subject to

dX_{t}=\alpha_{t}X_{t}(bdt+\sigma dW_{t}),\hskip 9.24994ptX_{0}=a,\hskip 9.24994ptX_{T}\geq c,

(37)

where
\mathscr{A}
is the set of all admissible investment strategies, i.e., all
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-progressively measurable processes
\alpha
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha|^{2}dt\right]<+\infty
.

2.

Find a fixed point
\hat{m}_{T}=\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}_{T}]
, where
\hat{X}
is the state associated to the optimal control
\hat{\alpha}
obtained in 1.

Before moving to the resolution of the MFG, we need to fix some preliminary notation. Let
\rho:=b/\sigma
denote the market price of risk, let
Y^{(1)}:=\mathscr{E}(-\rho,0)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\hat{h}_{1},\hat{m}_{T})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
be the unique solution of the coupled system

\begin{cases}m_{T}=c+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}\right]\\
G(h_{1}):=c-a+\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{T}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}\right]=0,\\
\end{cases}

(38)

and let

\hat{g}(t,y):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[yY^{(1)}_{t,T}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta\hat{m}_{T}-\frac{\hat{h}_{1}}{\gamma}yY^{(1)}_{t,T}\right)^{+}\right],\hskip 9.24994pt(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.

The unique MFG solution is given by the couple
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{m}_{T})
where

\hat{\alpha}_{t}=-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\dfrac{Y^{(1)}_{t}\partial_{y}\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})-\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})}{cY^{(1)}_{t}+\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})},\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T).

Proof.

First, we reformulate the control problem in terms of the amount
\pi_{t}
invested
in the risky asset at time
t
, i.e., by letting

\pi_{t}:=\alpha_{t}X_{t},\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T],

the wealth dynamics (
37
) becomes

dX_{t}=\pi_{t}(b\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}),\hskip 18.49988ptX_{0}=a.

In this reformulated problem the diffusion coefficient
\pi\sigma
satisfies assumption (A4), so that we can use the approach developed in the previous sections and set

q_{t}:=\sigma\pi_{t},\hskip 18.49988pt\rho:=\frac{b}{\sigma},

yielding

dX_{t}=q_{t}(\rho\,dt+dW_{t}),\hskip 18.49988ptX_{0}=a.

After solving the reformulated problem, the original control will be recovered via

\alpha_{t}=\frac{\pi_{t}}{X_{t}}=\frac{q_{t}}{\sigma X_{t}},

whenever
X_{t}>0
, which we will verify to be true at the equilibrium. Using Theorem
3.2
, we are reduced to solving the following FBSDE system:

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=q_{t}(\rho dt+dW_{t})\\
X_{0}=a,\hskip 9.24994ptX_{T}=\xi\\
dY_{t}=-\rho Y_{t}dW_{t}\\
Y_{0}=h_{1}\\
\big(Y_{T}-1+\gamma(\xi-\theta m_{T})\big)\big(\xi^{\prime}-\xi\big)\geq 0\ \text{a.s.},\ \forall\xi^{\prime}\in L^{2}([c,+\infty)).\end{cases}

(39)

Applying the same computations as in the first part of Theorem
3.10
(see also Remark
3.12
) with the parameters

c_{0}(t)=-\rho,\hskip 9.24994ptb_{0}(t)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{c}_{0}(t)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptk_{0}=-1,\hskip 9.24994ptk_{1}=\gamma,\hskip 9.24994ptk_{2}=-\gamma\theta,

we have that the terminal condition of system (
39

\xi=c+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}.

(40)

By the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.10
, we deduce that there exists a unique pair
(\hat{h}_{1},\hat{m}_{T})
which solves the following coupled system

\displaystyle m_{T}

\displaystyle=c+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}\right],

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=c+\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{T}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}\right].

Since
XY^{(1)}
is a martingale, we have

X_{t}=\frac{1}{Y_{t}^{(1)}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T}Y_{T}^{(1)}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right].

Using the terminal condition

X_{T}=c+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+},

we obtain

X_{t}=c+\frac{1}{Y_{t}^{(1)}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{T}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta m_{T}-\frac{h_{1}}{\gamma}Y_{T}^{(1)}\right)^{+}\;\middle|\;\mathscr{F}_{t}\right].

We notice that, since
Y^{(1)}_{T}=Y^{(1)}_{t}Y^{(1)}_{t,T}
, where
Y^{(1)}_{t}
and
Y^{(1)}_{t,T}
are independent, we can express
X

X_{t}=c+\frac{1}{Y^{(1)}_{t}}\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t}),

where

\hat{g}(t,y):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[yY^{(1)}_{t,T}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-c+\theta\hat{m}_{T}-\frac{\hat{h}_{1}}{\gamma}yY^{(1)}_{t,T}\right)^{+}\right].

Observe that
Y^{(1)}_{t,T}
follows a lognormal distribution with a smooth density on
(0,\infty)
. Hence,
\hat{g}(t,\cdot)
is a lognormal convolution of a payoff with at most quadratic growth, so
\hat{g}\in C^{1,2}([0,T)\times(0,\infty))
. Since
Y^{(1)}>0
, we can safely apply Itô’s formula to
X_{t}=c+\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})/Y^{(1)}_{t}

dX_{t}=\left(\partial_{t}\kappa(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}(Y_{t}^{(1)})^{2}\,\partial_{yy}\kappa(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})\right)dt-\rho Y^{(1)}_{t}\,\partial_{y}\kappa(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})dW_{t},\hskip 9.24994ptt<T,

q_{t}=-\rho Y_{t}^{(1)}\partial_{y}\kappa(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})=-\rho\left(\partial_{y}\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})-\frac{1}{Y^{(1)}_{t}}\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})\right),\hskip 9.24994ptt<T.

is well defined, and recalling that

q_{t}=\alpha_{t}X_{t}\sigma
, we obtain the equilibrium strategy

\hat{\alpha}_{t}=-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\dfrac{Y^{(1)}_{t}\partial_{y}\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})-\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})}{cY^{(1)}_{t}+\hat{g}(t,Y^{(1)}_{t})},\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,T).

It remains to prove uniqueness. For notational convenience, we write

instead of

. Fix

. By Proposition
3.5
and Remark

,
\xi^{m}\in L^{2}_{a}([c,+\infty))
given by (

As observed in Remark
3.8
, the terminal cost functional is not monotone in the sense of Lasry-Lions. Therefore, we establish uniqueness via the following contraction argument.
Define

\Gamma(m):=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{m}].

We show that
\Gamma
is a contraction.
Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and set

\xi_{1}:=\xi^{m_{1}},\xi_{2}:=\xi^{m_{2}}
.
Since
L^{2}_{a}([c,+\infty))
is convex, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\xi_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)\xi_{1}+\varepsilon\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{a}([c,+\infty)).

Moreover, since
\xi_{1}
minimizes
J_{m_{1}}
over
L^{2}_{a}([c,+\infty))
,

J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})\leq J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{\varepsilon}).

On the other hand, by the
\lambda

J_{m_{1}}
,

J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\leq(1-\varepsilon)J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})+\varepsilon J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{2})-\lambda\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right].

Combining these last two inequalities gives

J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})\leq(1-\varepsilon)J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})+\varepsilon J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{2})-\lambda\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right].

Rearranging and dividing by
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{2})-J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})\geq\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right].

Repeating the same argument with
m_{2}
, minimizer
\xi_{2}
, and competitor
\xi_{1}
, yields

J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{1})-J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{2})\geq\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right].

Adding the last two inequalities gives

2\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right]\leq J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{2})-J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})+J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{1})-J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{2}).

(42)

Since

J_{m}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}\left[-\xi+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\xi-\theta m)^{2}\right],

the right-hand side in (

\displaystyle J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{2})-J_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1})+J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{1})-J_{m_{2}}(\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[(\xi_{2}-\theta m_{1})^{2}-(\xi_{1}-\theta m_{1})^{2}+(\xi_{1}-\theta m_{2})^{2}-(\xi_{2}-\theta m_{2})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\gamma\theta(m_{1}-m_{2})\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}].

Therefore,

\displaystyle 2\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\theta(m_{1}-m_{2})\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\theta|m_{1}-m_{2}|\left|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}]\right|.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\left|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}]\right|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}\right]
.
Hence, if
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}]\neq 0

\Gamma

\mathbb{R}
. Therefore the consistency condition

2.4

(\hat{\alpha},\hat{m}_{T})
.

The case with pure common noise.

We conclude by considering the case with common noise only, i.e., all agents trade in the same risky asset. In this case

\tau_{N}=\inf\Big\{t\in[0,T]:|X_{t}|+|\hat{X}_{t}|+|q_{t}|+|\hat{q}_{t}|+|z_{t}|+|\hat{z}_{t}|+|Y_{t}|+|M_{2}(\mu_{t})|\geq N\Big\}\wedge T.
