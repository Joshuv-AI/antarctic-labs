Association of the Fibrosis-4 index with thyroid nodule prevalence in women: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Ruixia Liu, Mengmeng Zheng, Tianrong Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe relationship between liver-related metabolic markers and thyroid nodules (TNs) has attracted increasing interest. However, whether the Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index is independently associated with TN prevalence in women, particularly after accounting for the age embedded in the score, remains unclear. We investigated the association between FIB-4 and prevalent TNs in a female health-check cohort and assessed its robustness in age-stratified and age-adjusted analyses.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional analysis of 695 women from a health examination cohort at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. TNs were defined as ultrasound-detected thyroid nodules. Multivariable logistic regression estimated odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for prevalent TNs according to standardized FIB-4 levels. Prespecified age-stratified analyses were performed for women aged ≤ 60 years and > 60 years. Within each age stratum, an additional model adjusted for continuous age. Age-adjusted within-stratum results were considered the main basis for interpretation.ResultsIn the full female cohort, the association between FIB-4 and prevalent TNs was attenuated after additional adjustment for continuous age (OR 1.104, 95% CI 0.822–1.484; P = 0.511). In age-adjusted within-stratum analyses, FIB-4 was not significantly associated with prevalent TNs in either women aged ≤ 60 years (OR 1.211, 95% CI 0.837–1.751; P = 0.310) or women aged > 60 years (OR 0.567, 95% CI 0.289–1.113; P = 0.099). The corresponding base model estimates before additional continuous-age adjustment were OR 1.564 (95% CI 1.086–2.253; P = 0.016) and OR 0.576 (95% CI 0.278–1.193; P = 0.138), respectively. The interaction between FIB-4 and age group was not statistically significant (P for interaction = 0.239).ConclusionsAfter additional adjustment for continuous age within age strata, the association between FIB-4 and prevalent thyroid nodules in women was attenuated and no longer statistically significant. Background

The incidental detection of thyroid nodules (TNs) has become a common finding in modern endocrine practice, largely because of the widespread use of high-resolution ultrasonography [1]. Although most TNs are benign, their high prevalence creates a substantial clinical and surveillance burden, especially in adult women [2, 3]. Epidemiological studies have consistently shown that TNs are more common in women and are associated with several metabolic abnormalities, including obesity, insulin resistance, and dyslipidemia [4–6].

These observations have renewed interest in the metabolic interplay between the thyroid and the liver. The liver is central to thyroid hormone metabolism and transport, while thyroid dysfunction may also influence hepatic metabolic homeostasis [7–9]. In parallel, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly termed nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, has been linked to thyroid nodules in population-based studies [10, 11]. Together, these findings suggest that liver-related metabolic abnormalities may coexist with, or contribute to, thyroid structural changes.

However, MASLD spans a broad clinical spectrum, ranging from simple steatosis to fibrosis. Liver fibrosis is generally considered a more advanced and prognostically important stage of liver injury, with stronger links to adverse hepatic and extrahepatic outcomes [12]. The Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index is a simple noninvasive score derived from age, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and platelet count, and has been widely used as a surrogate marker of fibrosis risk in chronic liver disease and metabolic liver disease settings [13, 14]. More recently, higher noninvasive fibrosis scores have also been linked to extrahepatic malignancies and, in selected metabolic cohorts, to thyroid nodule malignancy [15, 16]. Nevertheless, whether the FIB-4 index is associated with the much more common outcome of ultrasound-detected TN prevalence in generally asymptomatic women remains unclear.

Age substantially complicates this question. It is both an explicit component of the FIB-4 formula and one of the strongest determinants of TN prevalence. In addition, women experience marked metabolic and hormonal changes across midlife and later life. As a result, analyses across a broad age range may obscure or distort observed associations, whereas age-stratified analyses alone may still leave important residual age-related variation within strata. Prior work has also shown that age can affect the interpretation of noninvasive fibrosis scores, including FIB-4 [17].

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the association between the FIB-4 index and thyroid nodule prevalence in a cohort of Chinese women undergoing routine health examinations. We performed prespecified age-stratified analyses and, importantly, further examined whether any observed association was robust to additional adjustment for continuous age within each age stratum.

Methods

Study design and population

We conducted a cross-sectional study based on retrospectively collected data from 4,947 individuals who attended the Health Management Center of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University for routine health check-ups between April 2025 and May 2025. After excluding duplicate records, individuals aged < 18 years, individuals with a history of thyroidectomy, those with known chronic liver disease, and those with missing key data, a final analytic cohort of 1,222 participants (527 men and 695 women) was established (Fig. 1). Known chronic liver disease was identified from the available medical history records, including documented chronic viral hepatitis, alcohol-related liver disease, cirrhosis, and other chronic liver disorders. The primary analysis focused on women, who were further stratified by age (≤ 60 years vs. >60 years). The 60-year cutoff was selected a priori as a pragmatic threshold to reflect later-life hormonal and metabolic transition in women, rather than as a direct proxy for menopausal status.

The final analytic cohort comprised 1,222 participants, including 527 men (thyroid nodules: 279; no nodules: 248) and 695 women, who were further stratified by age (≤ 60 years: n = 636 [thyroid nodules: 426; no nodules: 210]; >60 years: n = 59 [thyroid nodules: 49; no nodules: 10]). Thyroid nodule status was determined by screening ultrasound. Derived indices (FIB-4, NLR, and PLR) were set to missing if any component variable was missing. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. All procedures were performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments.

Data collection and variable definitions

Demographic information, medical history, anthropometric measurements, and blood pressure values were collected during routine health examinations. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height squared (m²). Hypertension was defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg, diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg, or self-reported physician-diagnosed hypertension.

Fasting venous blood samples were obtained and analyzed in the hospital central laboratory. Biochemical indices included fasting plasma glucose (FPG), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), uric acid (UA), ALT, AST, and creatinine. Thyroid function tests, including thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), total triiodothyronine (T3), and total thyroxine (T4), were also measured. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the CKD-EPI equation. Using complete blood count parameters, we derived the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR). The FIB-4 index was calculated as: age (years) × AST (U/L) / [platelet count (10⁹/L) × √ALT (U/L)] [13].

Thyroid nodules were evaluated by high-resolution color Doppler ultrasonography (7.5–10 MHz transducer) performed as part of the routine examination workflow by experienced physicians in ultrasonography. A TN was defined as one or more discrete focal lesions within the thyroid parenchyma that were distinguishable from surrounding tissue on ultrasonography. As this was a health-check-based cross-sectional study, cytological or histopathological confirmation was unavailable for most participants; therefore, TNs were analyzed as an ultrasound-defined presence/absence outcome without differentiation between benign and malignant nodules.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (Version 20.0). Continuous variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), and categorical variables as number (percentage). Group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test or chi-square test, as appropriate.

The initial multivariable logistic regression model examined the association between standardized FIB-4 and prevalent TNs in women. To facilitate comparison, FIB-4 was standardized, and results are presented per 1 standard deviation (SD) increase. This base model was adjusted for BMI, FPG, TG, HDL-C, hypertension, eGFR, UA, PLR, and TSH, based on clinical relevance and data availability. Effect estimates are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

To evaluate whether the association differed by age group, an interaction term between FIB-4 and age group (≤ 60 vs. >60 years) was tested in the overall female cohort. In addition, because age is an explicit component of the FIB-4 formula and is also associated with TN prevalence, we performed age-adjusted analyses within each age stratum by additionally entering continuous age into the corresponding multivariable model. Thus, for each age stratum, two models were examined: Model 1, the base multivariable model; and Model 2, the base model plus continuous age. The age-adjusted within-stratum results from Model 2 were considered the main basis for interpretation.

Analyses were performed using complete-case data for the variables included in each model. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study population

The participant selection process is shown in Fig. 1. The final analytic cohort included 1,222 participants, of whom 695 (57.9%) were women. The overall prevalence of TNs was higher in women than in men (68.3% vs. 52.9%). The female cohort was further stratified into 636 women aged ≤ 60 years and 59 women aged > 60 years.

Baseline characteristics in women

Baseline characteristics of the female analytic cohort are presented in Table 2. Among 695 women, 475 had ultrasound-detected TNs and 220 did not. Women with TNs were significantly older than those without TNs [median 40 (33, 54) vs. 35 (30, 41) years; P < 0.001]. They also had higher BMI, higher blood pressure levels, and a higher prevalence of hypertension. In addition, the TN group had higher TG, LDL-C, AST, and FIB-4 levels, as well as lower eGFR, leukocyte counts, and lymphocyte counts. These unadjusted baseline differences, particularly in age and cardiometabolic variables, supported the use of multivariable models and additional continuous-age adjustment in subsequent analyses.

Base model estimates and age-adjusted comparison

In the full female cohort, FIB-4 showed a modest association with prevalent TNs in the base multivariable model (OR 1.364, 95% CI 1.005–1.853; P = 0.047). When continuous age was added to this full-cohort model, the estimate was attenuated and was no longer statistically significant (OR 1.104, 95% CI 0.822–1.484; P = 0.511) (Table 3, Panel A).

For comparison, the age-stratified base model estimates were OR 1.564 (95% CI 1.086–2.253; P = 0.016) in women aged ≤ 60 years and OR 0.576 (95% CI 0.278–1.193; P = 0.138) in women aged > 60 years (Table 3, Panel B). However, the interaction between FIB-4 and age group was not statistically significant (P for interaction = 0.239). Therefore, the difference in nominal statistical significance between the two age strata was not interpreted as evidence that an association was present in younger women but absent in older women. The main age-adjusted within-stratum results are reported in the next section.

Age-adjusted within-stratum analyses

Because age is an explicit component of the FIB-4 formula and may still influence the association within each age stratum, we constructed age-adjusted within-stratum models by adding continuous age to the corresponding age-stratified base model, while keeping all other covariates unchanged. These age-adjusted within-stratum analyses were considered the main basis for interpretation (Table 3, Panel C). After this additional adjustment, FIB-4 was no longer significantly associated with prevalent TNs in either women aged ≤ 60 years (OR 1.211, 95% CI 0.837–1.751; P = 0.310) or women aged > 60 years (OR 0.567, 95% CI 0.289–1.113; P = 0.099). In women aged ≤ 60 years, continuous age remained significantly associated with prevalent TNs (OR 1.044, 95% CI 1.007–1.081; P = 0.019), whereas in women aged > 60 years the estimate for age was not statistically significant (OR 1.318, 95% CI 0.873–1.992; P = 0.189).

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study of women undergoing routine health examinations, the initial association between FIB-4 and prevalent ultrasound-detected thyroid nodules was attenuated after continuous age was additionally accounted for. In the age-adjusted within-stratum analyses, FIB-4 was no longer significantly associated with TN prevalence in either age group. Moreover, the apparent difference in nominal statistical significance observed in the base age-stratified models was not supported by a statistically significant interaction between FIB-4 and age group. Therefore, the base-model difference between age strata should not be interpreted as evidence of true effect modification by age, and the present data do not support a robust or definitive age-specific association between FIB-4 and TN prevalence.

This issue has important methodological implications. FIB-4 was originally developed as a noninvasive fibrosis score composed of age, AST, ALT, and platelet count [13]. At the same time, TN prevalence increases with age and is particularly common in women, and metabolic abnormalities are also linked to nodule burden in female populations [1, 5, 6]. In such a setting, an apparent association between FIB-4 and TNs may partly arise because the exposure and the outcome share a strong age-related structure. Although age stratification can reduce broad life-stage heterogeneity, substantial age variation still remains within the ≤ 60-year subgroup. The attenuation of the FIB-4 estimate after further adjustment for continuous age suggests that the initially observed association may not represent a fully independent thyroid-related effect of FIB-4. Rather, in a female health-check population, FIB-4 may function more as an age-related composite marker than as an independent indicator for thyroid nodules. This interpretation is also consistent with previous work showing that age materially influences the performance and clinical meaning of noninvasive fibrosis scores, including FIB-4 [17].

The 60-year cutoff was selected a priori as a pragmatic threshold to reflect later-life hormonal and metabolic transition in women rather than as a direct proxy for menopausal status. Importantly, the present findings indicate that age dichotomization alone was insufficient for interpretation, because even within the ≤ 60-year stratum, additional adjustment for continuous age materially changed the association between FIB-4 and TN prevalence. This reinforces that age stratification may reduce broad heterogeneity but cannot fully eliminate residual age-related confounding when the exposure itself embeds age.

This perspective also helps position the present findings within the broader literature on metabolic–thyroid interactions. Prior studies have linked metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance-related traits, and metabolic dysfunction to TN prevalence [4, 5, 10, 11]. In parallel, the concept of a liver–thyroid axis has been proposed to describe shared endocrine and metabolic pathways between these two organs [7–9, 18]. However, our results do not provide strong support for interpreting FIB-4 as an independent fibrosis-driven determinant of TN prevalence. Instead, they suggest that when FIB-4 is examined in a generally asymptomatic health-check population, part of its apparent relationship with TNs may reflect the fact that both FIB-4 and TN prevalence are structured by age and overlapping metabolic characteristics. In other words, the present data support caution in interpreting FIB-4 as more than a composite metabolic-age marker in this context.

Current clinical guidance on metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease recommends fibrosis risk stratification using non-invasive tools such as FIB-4 within broader cardiometabolic-risk assessment pathways, followed when appropriate by imaging-based assessment [19, 20]. Against this background, the present findings suggest that in endocrine or health-check settings, an elevated FIB-4 in women with TNs should not be interpreted as a thyroid-specific or nodule-predictive signal. Rather, it may be more appropriately viewed as a prompt to consider standard liver-risk assessment according to current MASLD care pathways. In this sense, the translational value of our study lies less in predicting TNs and more in clarifying how FIB-4 should—and should not—be interpreted when thyroid nodules and hepatometabolic risk coexist.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, a central limitation of this study is the structural dependence between FIB-4 and age. Because age is a direct mathematical component of the FIB-4 formula and is also a major determinant of TN prevalence, FIB-4 and age are not independent variables in this context. This introduces collinearity and limits the extent to which an age-independent effect of FIB-4 can be meaningfully estimated or interpreted. Although we performed additional age-adjusted analyses within each age stratum, the present study cannot determine whether any genuine fibrosis-related signal persists once the age component embedded in FIB-4 is removed. We also did not disentangle the individual non-age components of FIB-4, including AST, ALT, and platelet count, or corroborate the findings using an age-independent fibrosis index such as the AST-to-platelet ratio index. Second, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Third, FIB-4 is a surrogate score rather than a direct fibrosis measure, and direct fibrosis assessment was unavailable. Fourth, although participants with known chronic liver disease were excluded, smoking, alcohol consumption, dietary iodine intake, thyroid autoimmunity, family history, and medication use were not systematically available in the original health-check dataset, leaving the possibility of residual confounding. Fifth, the > 60-year subgroup was relatively small, limiting statistical precision and reducing the ability to assess true effect modification by age, an issue that is well recognized in subgroup analyses [21]. Sixth, thyroid ultrasonography is operator-dependent, and inter-observer variability could not be fully assessed in this retrospective dataset. Finally, this was a single-center health-check cohort, which may limit generalizability.

Despite these limitations, the study also has strengths. It used a clearly defined health-check cohort, included a relatively large female sample, incorporated multiple metabolic covariates, and directly addressed the central interpretive issue raised by the structure of FIB-4 itself. Rather than simply reporting a positive or negative association, the present analysis shows that the apparent relationship between FIB-4 and TN prevalence is strongly influenced by age adjustment. In that sense, the study provides a more cautious framework for interpreting composite biomarkers that embed age when the outcome under investigation is itself age-dependent.

Conclusion

In summary, after additional adjustment for continuous age within each age stratum, FIB-4 was not independently associated with prevalent thyroid nodules in women. These findings suggest that the apparent relationship between FIB-4 and TN prevalence in this health-check population is strongly influenced by age handling and should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Future studies should evaluate liver-related markers that do not embed age, or direct fibrosis assessments, when examining thyroid outcomes.
