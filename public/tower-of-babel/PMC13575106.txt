ABSTRACT
AbstractDissipative dynamics across physical systems exhibit organizing structural boundaries. The dimensionless damping ratio defines a stability architecture in which the critical threshold χ =1 marks a second-order non-Hermitian Exceptional Point (EP2). Within spatially flat ΛCDM, an exact algebraic identity is demonstrated linking the onset of cosmic acceleration to critical damping of structure growth: . This identity is a structural reformulation of the standard Friedmann and growth equations, not a new physical law; its scientific value lies in recasting a known kinematic transition as a stability-phase transition and in generating the falsifiable prediction that departures from flat ΛCDM produce a measurable, nonzero offset between the q=0 and transitions (identically zero under flat ΛCDM, generically nonzero in extensions). A substrate inheritance relation is then proposed as a leading-order projection approximation, whereby emergent modes acquire effective parameters from substrate precursors. Within this framework, the observed particle distribution is consistent with stability organization: long-lived matter occupies localized stability basins at , while short-lived resonances occupy a secondary –1 band whose sub-cluster evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive. Introduction

Boundary structure in dissipative dynamics

The goal of this work is not to propose new microphysical laws but to identify structural commonalities in the mathematical form of dissipative dynamics across domains. The organizing object is a single dimensionless ratio whose qualitative role is identical in every system admitting an effective second-order response.

Many physical theories are structured around boundary conditions and limiting principles. The speed of light c limits information propagation, and Planck’s constant ℏ bounds measurement precision. The dimensionless ratio

Among dimensionless combinations of γ and ω, χ uniquely controls the sign of the quadratic discriminant and therefore the qualitative class of the system’s eigenvalues. It is the minimal invariant parameter governing stability classification: Δ > 0 iff χ > 1 (overdamped); Δ = 0 iff χ = 1 (critically damped); Δ < 0 iff χ < 1 (underdamped). Any other dimensionless combination of γ and ω can be written as a function of χ alone. Across all domains discussed in this manuscript, χ is defined as the ratio of effective damping to twice the characteristic response frequency; domain-specific mappings (e.g., , for particle physics) are interpretations of this common definition rather than distinct definitions.

At χ = 1, the quadratic response has a repeated root; in defective non-Hermitian realizations the effective generator coalesces and the impulse response transitions to

defining a second-order non-Hermitian exceptional point (EP2)1–4. The term “exceptional point” is used throughout in the restricted algebraic sense of discriminant coalescence in the quadratic response kernel; specific non-Hermitian realizations require that the relevant reduced generator possess the corresponding defective structure.

Quadratic eigenproblems for linear response arise across classical dissipative modes, retarded propagators in open quantum field theory, and cosmological growth dynamics5–8. Markovian open-system generators of Lindblad type can realize the same EP2 structure in suitable reduced response channels or effective mode equations (Supplementary Section 1)9,10, but the EP condition is asserted here at the level of the shared quadratic response structure.

Physical interpretation and scope

Definitions

The term stability architecture denotes the partitioning of effective-mode parameter space by the critical-damping boundary χ =1 into underdamped (χ < 1, oscillatory decay), critically damped (χ = 1, algebraic-exponential kernel Eq. (2)), and overdamped (χ > 1, monotone exponential decay) sectors. The term structural separatrix denotes the codimension-one surface χ = 1 in the plane at which eigenvalues coalesce and response changes mathematical class from oscillatory to monotone decay.

Cross-domain scope

The correspondences drawn across domains are structural, arising from shared mathematical form in the effective second-order response, rather than analogical in the sense of assuming physical equivalence between systems. The framework applies only to systems admitting a dominant-mode reduction to a second-order effective equation; strongly multimodal, chaotic, or intrinsically non-Markovian systems do not in general admit such a reduction without additional coarse-graining.

Utility

The utility of the framework lies in converting qualitative regime distinctions into a single measurable parameter, enabling cross-system comparison and identification of structural transitions not explicit in the original formulations of each domain. The Δ z offset in cosmology, the spectral doublet merger in circuit QED, and the viscous-dissipation shift in neutron stars are all consequences of this single-parameter classification.

Epistemic classification

The results span three epistemic levels kept distinct throughout. (1) Derived results: the cosmological identity (exact within flat ΛCDM), the EP2 discriminant structure (exact for the specified second-order response class), and the perturbative RG stability estimate for weakly coupled theory. (2) Structural analogies supported by formal consistency: the substrate inheritance relation (a structural hypothesis motivated by consistency across reduced descriptions, not derived from QFT), the particle stability classification, and the information-efficiency interpretation for specified channel models. (3) Speculative extensions requiring future calculation: the QCD substrate χ estimate, the gauge-coupling stability classification, and quantitative cross-domain structural claims. Structural interpretation

The exceptional-point boundary at χ =1 is not interpreted as a dynamical attractor. Rather, it serves as a structural separatrix where impulse response transitions from oscillatory to monotone decay. The framework classifies near-critical operation as a structurally significant operating point, with departures incurring complementary penalties: χ < 1 produces persistent ringing that degrades signal fidelity under finite bandwidth, while χ > 1 produces slow relaxation that suppresses information throughput for fixed dissipation.

Conclusion

Limitations

The following limitations are stated explicitly.

Flat ΛCDM dependence

The identity is exact only for spatially flat geometry, pressureless clustering matter, general relativity, and w = -1. Generic extensions produce a nonzero Δ z, although specially tuned models could preserve Δ z = 0 accidentally.

Linear response regime

Systems requiring strongly nonlinear, multi-resonance, or non-Markovian descriptions may not admit a meaningful single-χ characterization.

Phenomenological coarse-graining

The particle mapping compresses full decay amplitudes, branching ratios, spin structure, and interference effects into two numbers.

Perturbative RG scope

Estimates apply to weakly coupled theory. Non-perturbative regimes and the top-sector Yukawa ( per e-fold) are not covered.

Information-efficiency retraction

Full numerical integration of for both output-power and kinetic entropy proxies shows no interior maximum near χ = 1 for any tested bandwidth configuration; extrema occur at scan boundaries rather than near the critical-damping point (Supplementary Section 2.4). The original claim is retracted for these proxy definitions. Lemma S2.1 (conditional localization) is retained but does not assert existence of the maximum for the proxies tested.

Substrate inheritance

The inheritance relation is a structural hypothesis motivated by consistency in reduced descriptions; it is not derived from first principles.

Summary

The framework is evaluated not by retrospective reinterpretation but by forward falsifiability: each domain discussed yields a concrete test whose failure would directly challenge the proposed structural correspondence.

A single dimensionless ratio, , provides a common stability classification language across quantum, field-theoretic, and cosmological dynamics. The critical boundary χ = 1 marks a non-Hermitian EP2 that coincides exactly with the onset of cosmic acceleration via the algebraic identity within flat ΛCDM. Substrate inheritance constrains the admissible parameter space for emergent modes in the leading-order projection approximation, and the observed particle distribution is consistent with stability-based classification. The results range from algebraically exact (“Cosmic crossing: ”) through structurally motivated (“Substrate inheritance as a coarse-grained structural relation”) to explicitly speculative (“Particle and substrate classification”). The principal falsifiable tests are: (i) measurement of Δ z between the q = 0 and transitions (identically zero in flat ΛCDM and generically nonzero in extensions); (ii) spectral coalescence at χ = 1 in circuit QED platforms, the most near-term accessible test; (iii) near-critical scalar-channel width in finite-temperature lattice QCD near the confinement crossover; and (iv) systematic measurement of in gravitational-wave observations, with the viscous-dissipation channel as the falsifiable target. The framework is therefore offered as a structurally grounded classification with defined scope and testable consequences.
