m m -sectorial discrete Laplacians and recurrence of complex-weighted graphs
Anna Muranova

Abstract
. We consider complex-weighted graphs, whose edge weights belong to a sector in the complex plane. We show that the corresponding Dirichlet Laplacian is m m -sectorial, and, hence, generates a contractive holomorphic C 0 C_{0} -semigroup. Further, it is shown that every sectorial complex-weighted graph can be extended to an electrical network, where by electrical networks we mean graphs, whose edge weights are holomorphic functions, arising from physical admittances. This result allows us to establish convergence results for the infinite complex-weighted graphs, e.g. convergence of solutions of Dirichlet problems and convergence of complex-valued capacities on a finite exhaustion. Finally, we define a recurrence for complex-weighted graphs, and, using the convergence results, give its characterizations in terms of functional spaces, capacity, Green’s function, resolvents of the Dirichlet Laplacian and properties of the Neumann Laplacian.



Keywords:
weighted graph, sectorial form,
m
-sectorial operator, discrete Laplacian, Dirichlet Laplacian, recurrence,
C_{0}
-semigroup, holomorphic semigroup, resolvent, Green’s function, admittance, electrical network.

1.
Introduction

The discrete Laplacian on graphs is a classical topic. Firstly it was considered on combinatorial graphs, and than extended to weighted graphs with positive weights. For further details we refer reader to monographs
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
. The Laplacian is known to be closely related to random walks, Markov chains, flows and electrical networks, see
[
6
,
11
,
17
,
18
]
.

It is known in physics, that an electrical network, connected to an AC power source, can be modeled using a graph, whose edge weights are rational functions of special structure. More precisely,
the electrical network, can be represented as a graph, whose each edge
\{x,y\}
is endowed with a complex-valued rational function

a^{(s)}(x,y)=\dfrac{s}{L_{xy}s^{2}+R_{xy}s+D_{xy}},

where
R_{xy}\geq 0
is the
resistance of this edge,
L_{xy}\geq 0
is the inductance,
D_{xy}\geq 0
is the inverse
capacitance and at least one of
R_{xy},L_{xy},D_{xy}
is not equal
0
. Usually, one take the domain of this function to be the complex right half-plane
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}s>0
,
and call the function
admittance
(see
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
). This model gives rise to a family of graphs and, consequently, Laplace operators
\mathcal{L}^{(s)}
on the set of functions
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, where
X
is a finite or countable set of vertices of a graph, see
[
14
,
15
]
. Moreover, in
[
16
]
it is proven, that recurrence and transience is well-defined for each family, i.e. it does not depend on
s
.

In this paper we consider complex-weighted graphs, whose edge weight
b:X\times X\to\mathbb{C}
maps to a sector, i.e.

|\operatorname{Im}b(x,y)|\leq c\cdot\operatorname{Re}b(x,y),

for some
c\geq 0
and any edge
\{x,y\}
.
We prove that the corresponding Dirichlet Laplacian is an
m
-sectorial operator, and, hence, by Lumer-Phillips theorem, generates contractive holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup.

The main result of this paper is Theorem
4.3
which states, that for each complex-weighted graph, whose edge weights belong to a sector, there exist an electrical network and
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}s_{0}>0
such that
b(x,y)=a^{(s_{0})}(x,y)
for all edges
\{x,y\}
, i.e. any complex-weighted graph can be “extended” to an electrical network. This result can be considered as a generalization of the classical correspondence between real-weighted graphs and electrical networks with resistors, see, e.g. monograph
[
6
]
for more details.

Further, we introduce a recurrence for the complex-weighted graphs. Theorem
4.3
allows us to introduce a capacity of infinite complex-weighted graph, and characterize the recurrence of complex-weighted graphs in terms the capacity, Theorem
5.8
. Then we introduce a Green’s function on the complex-weighted graphs and, using holomorphicity of capacity and Theorem
4.3
, we show, that the Green’s function is well-defined and also characterizes recurrence. Finally, we show that the Neumann Laplacian on the complex-weighted graphs is also
m
-sectorial and relate its properties to recurrence. In fact, Theorem
4.3
allows us to use uniform convergence of holomorphic functions for complex-weighted graphs instead of monotone convergence, used for real-weighted graphs and not applicable for complex values.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains preliminaries and basic results on sectorial complex-weighted graphs. Section
3
elaborates on finite approximations of the infinite complex-weighted graphs. Section
4
explains a relation between the complex-weighted graphs and the electrical networks. Section
5
is dedicated to the definition of recurrence for complex-weighted graphs and its characterizations in terms of capacity, Green’s function, resolvents of the Dirichlet Laplacian and properties of the Neumann Laplacian.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Complex-weighted graphs and formal Laplacian

Definition 2.1
.

A
graph (with a measure)
is a triple
(X,E,m)
, where
X
is an at most countable set,

E\subset\{\{x,y\}\in X\times X\;\mid\;x\neq y\}

is a given subset of unordered pairs of elements of
X
, and
m:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The elements of the set
X
are called
vertices
, the elements of
E
are called
edges
, and
m
is the
measure
.

For any
x,y\in X
we write
x\sim y
if
\{x,y\}\in E
. A graph is called
locally finite
if the set

\{y\;\mid\;y\sim x\}
is finite for all
x\in X
. A
path
between vertices
x,y\in X
is a finite sequence
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, of vertices such that

x=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim x_{2}\sim\dots\sim x_{n}=y.

A set
K\subseteq X
is called
connected
, if there exists a path between any two vertices of it, and so we call the graph
connected
if
X
is connected.

Assumption 2.2
.

In this paper we assume that all graphs
(X,E,m)
are locally finite and connected.

Let us denote

\mathbb{H}_{r}:=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\;\mid\;\operatorname{Re}s>0\}.

Definition 2.3
.

A
complex-weighted graph
is a graph
(X,E,m)
, whose each edge is equipped with the weight
b:E\to\mathbb{H}_{r}
, satisfying the following property: there exists
c\geq 0
such that

(1)

\left|\operatorname{Im}b(x,y)\right|\leq c\cdot\operatorname{Re}b(x,y).

We call this property
sectoriality
and
c
is a
sectoriality constant
.

We assume
b(x,y)\equiv 0
if there is no edge between
x
and
y
. Hence, any complex-weighted graph is uniquely determined by the triple
(X,b,m)
, and we will refer to this triple as the complex-weighted graph.

Assumption 2.4
.

In this paper the complex weightsof a graph always satisfy a sectoriality (
1
), i.e. all the weights of the graph belong to a sector of the complex plane.

The sectoriality leads to many useful features of the graph and its energy form, the most basic of which are discussed in this section below. Moreover, the corresponding Dirichlet Laplacian is
m
-sectorial and generates a contractive holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup, see Section
2.3
.

Let
X
be an at most countable set and
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. We denote by
C(X)
the set of all complex-valued functions on
X
.

The
formal Laplacian
is acting on any function
f\in C(X)
via

\mathcal{L}_{b,m}f(x):=\dfrac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in X}(f(x)-f(y))b(x,y)

We will omit subscripts
b
and(or)
m
when they are clear from the context.

2.2.
Energy form and formal sesquilinear form

Let
\ell^{2}(X,m)
be the set of functions

\{f\in C(X)\;\mid\;\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}m(x)<\infty\big\},

with a scalar product

(f\mid g)=\sum_{x\in X}f(x)\overline{g(x)}m(x).

Obviously,
\ell^{2}(X,m)
is a Hilbert space.

Further, let us define a subspace
\mathcal{D}(X)\subset C(X)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(X)

\displaystyle=\Big\{f\in C(X)\;\mid\;\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}|b(x,y)|<\infty\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{f\in C(X)\;\mid\;\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}b(x,y)\begin{subarray}{c}\mbox{ converges for some (all) }\\
\mbox{ order of summations}\end{subarray}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{f\in C(X)\;\mid\;\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{Re}b(x,y)<\infty\Big\},

where the equalities follow from the sectoriality (
1
).

The
(formal) energy
of a function is defined for any
f\in\mathcal{D}(X)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{b}(f)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}b(x,y).

We will omit the subscript
b
, if it is clear from the context.

Note that

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{Re}b(x,y)\geq 0,

and

\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{Q}(f)=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{2}\operatorname{Im}b(x,y).

Further, the following lemma follows immediately from the sectoriality (
1
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. The form
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{b}
with the domain
\mathcal{D}(X)
is sectorial, i.e. it satisfies

|\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{Q}(f)|\leq c\cdot\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f),

for some
c\geq 0
for any
f\in\mathcal{D}(X)
.

The next several lemmas are almost immediate corollaries of the definition of the form
\mathcal{Q}
and are presented here for further references.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. For any
f\in C(X)
the following are equivalent:

•

f\in\mathcal{D}(X)
,

•

the both functions
\operatorname{Im}f,\operatorname{Re}f\in\mathcal{D}(X)
.

Moreover, if any of the equivalent conditions is satisfied, the following holds:

(2)

\mathcal{Q}(f)=\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Re}f)+\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Im}f).

Proof.

The statement follows from the sectoriality (
1
), the fact that

|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}=|\operatorname{Re}f(x)-\operatorname{Re}f(y)|^{2}+|\operatorname{Im}f(x)-\operatorname{Im}f(y)|^{2}.

and the definition of
\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

The corresponding (formal) sesquilinear form is defined for any
f,g\in\mathcal{D}(X)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}{(f(x)-f(y))}\overline{(g(x)-g(y))}b(x,y)

The following inequality, which is a consequence of the to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, is known for sectorial forms:

(3)

|\mathcal{Q}(f,g)|\leq(1+c)\left(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let us denote by
C_{c}(X)
the subset of
C(X)
of all complex-valued functions on
X
with finite support.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. Then
C_{c}(X)
is dense in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Let
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a finite exhaustion of
(X,b,m)
, i.e.
K_{n}\subset X
is finite and connected,
K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}=X
. Let
f_{n}:=f\mid_{K_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\|f-f_{n}\|^{2}=\sum_{x\in X\setminus K_{n}}|f(x)|^{2}m(x)\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Theorem 2.8

(Green’s formula)
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. Then

(a)

For all
f\in~\mathcal{D}(X)
and
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
the following holds

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}f(x)\phi(x)m(x)=\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}\phi(x)f(x)m(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(f(x)-f(y)).

(b)

For all
f\in\mathcal{D}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
and
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
the following holds

\mathcal{Q}(\phi,f)=(\mathcal{L}\phi\mid f).

If, in addition,
\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
, then

\mathcal{Q}(f,\phi)=(\mathcal{L}f\mid\phi).

Proof.

Due to the locally finitness of graph all the sums are finite. The equalities follow by direct computations, see e.g
[
15
, Lemma 7]
.
∎

2.3.
Dirichlet Laplacian and corresponding form on infinite graphs

In this section we introduce the Dirichlet Laplacian on a complex-weighted graph. The approach is similar to the one for the real-weighted Dirichlet Laplacian, see
[
13
]
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be an infinite graph and
\mathcal{Q}
be its energy form. We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{0}
the subspace of functions
f\in C(X)
for which
there exists a sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
with
\phi_{n}\to f
pointwise and
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We say that
the sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
approximates
f
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
is equivalent to
\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
due to the sectoriality of
\mathcal{Q}
, Lemma
2.5
.

Obviously,
C_{c}(X)\subset\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Moreover,

Lemma 2.10
.

For any complex-weighted graph
(X,b,m)
the following inclusion of functional spaces holds:
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Then there exist
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
with
\phi_{n}\to f
pointwise and
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{N})<\varepsilon
, i.e

\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X\setminus B_{1}(\supp\phi_{N})}|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\operatorname{Re}b(x,y)\leq\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{N})<\varepsilon,

where

B_{1}(\supp\phi_{N})=\{x\in X\;\mid\;\mbox{there exists }y\in\supp\phi_{N}\mbox{ such that }x\sim y\}.

Hence,
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{D}
.
∎

The norm, associated to the form
\mathcal{Q}
, is defined for any
f\in\mathcal{D}
as

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}}=(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)+\|f\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Theorem 2.11
.

For any graph
(X,b,m)
the following holds

\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)=\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}.

Before we prove the theorem, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f\in C(X)
and
H\in\Big\{\mathcal{D}_{0},\ell^{2}(X,m),\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}\Big\}
. Then

f\in H
if and only if
\operatorname{Re}f,\operatorname{Im}f\in H
.

Proof.

•

Let
H=\mathcal{D}_{0}
.
Let
(\phi_{n})_{n}\in C_{c}(X)
approximates
f
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Firstly note, that

\phi_{n}\to f
pointwise implies
\operatorname{Re}\phi_{n}\to\operatorname{Re}f
and
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{n}\to\operatorname{Im}f
.
Secondly, by Lemma
2.6
(
2
) we have

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Re}f-\operatorname{Re}\phi_{n})+\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Im}f-\operatorname{Im}\phi_{n}),

and, since all summands are positive and
\mathcal{Q}
is sectorial, we get

\operatorname{Re}f,\operatorname{Im}f\in~D_{0}
. Moreover, they can be approximated in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
by real-valued functions. The other direction follows from the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is a vector space.

•

Let
H=\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Then we obtain

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}m(x)=\sum_{x\in X}(|\operatorname{Re}f(x)|^{2}+|\operatorname{Im}f(x)|^{2})m(x)

from where the statement follows.

•

Let
H=\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
.
By Lemma
2.6
(
2
) and definition of
\ell^{2}
-norm we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})+\|f-\phi_{n}\|^{2}=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Re}f-\operatorname{Re}\phi_{n})+\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{Im}f-\operatorname{Im}\phi_{n})

\displaystyle+\|\operatorname{Re}f-\operatorname{Re}\phi_{n}\|^{2}+\|\operatorname{Im}f-\operatorname{Im}\phi_{n}\|^{2},

from where the statement follows due to the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}
and positivity of all summands.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.11
.

Let
f\in\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
. Then there exists a sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
such that

\|f-\phi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{Q}}=(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})+\|f-\phi_{n}\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Therefore,
\phi_{n}\to f
in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
and, hence,
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
and
\phi_{n}\to f
pointwise. Moreover,
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f-\phi_{n})\to 0
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. From all the above follows
\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
.

Let
f\in D_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Then, by Lemma
2.12
we obtain that
\operatorname{Re}f,\operatorname{Im}f\in D_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
and can be approximated in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
by real-valued functions. Hence, since
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(\cdot)=\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\cdot)
one can apply the reasoning for positive forms on real-weighted graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
, see
[
13
, proof of Theorem 1.19]
, to show that
\operatorname{Re}f,\operatorname{Im}f\in\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
which implies
f\in\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
by Lemma
2.12
. Therefore,
\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)\subset\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
and the proof is finished.

∎

We define the form
Q^{(D)}
as a restriction of the form
\mathcal{Q}
to
\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
. We call
Q^{(D)}
the
form corresponding to the Dirichlet Laplacian
.

Corollary 2.13
.

The form
Q^{(D)}
with the domain
D(Q^{(D)})=\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
is a densely defined closed sectorial form in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
.

Proof.

The sectoriality follows from Lemma
2.5
. The density is clear, since
C_{c}(X)\subset D(Q^{(D)})
is dense in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Finally, the form is closed due to Theorem
2.11
.
∎

By the general theory of sectorial forms and operators
[
12
]
, the form
Q^{(D)}
defines a unique
m
-sectorial operator
L^{(D)}
with a domain
D(L^{(D)})\subset D(Q^{(D)})
, see
[
12
, p. 322, Theorem 2.1]
. We call the operator
L^{(D)}
the
Dirichlet Laplacian
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. Let
L^{(D)}
be its Dirichlet Laplacian. Then

L^{(D)}f(x)=\mathcal{L}f(x),

for all
f\in D(L^{(D)})
, for any
x\in X
.

Proof.

By the definition of an operator, associated with a form, for any
f\in D(L^{(D)}),
and
\phi\in C(X)\subset D(Q^{(D)})
we have

(L^{(D)}f\;\mid\;\phi)=Q^{(D)}(f,\phi)=\mathcal{Q}(f,\phi)=\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}f(x)\overline{\phi(x)}m(x),

where the last equality is due to the Green’s formula. Now taking
\phi:=1_{x}/m
the statement follows.
∎

By Lumer-Phillips theorem for generation of holomorphic semigroups (see e. g.
[
12
, p. 492, Theorem 1.24]
) we obtain that
-L^{(D)}
generates a contractive holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup
T(t)
of angle
\theta=\pi/2-\arg c
, where
c
is the sectoriality constant (
1
). Then by integral representation of the resolvent (see, e.g.
[
7
, p. 55 Theorem 1.10]
) the following holds

(L^{(D)}+\alpha)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}T(t)dt,

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
. We will elaborate more on this equality in the next sections.

3.
Finite approximations of complex-weighted graphs

Let
(X,b,m)
be an infinite complex-weighted graph and let
K\subset X
be a finite connected subset, fixed for this section. Let us denote

C_{c}(K)=\{f\in C_{c}(X)\;\mid\;\supp f\subset K\},

and

C(K)=\{f:K\to\mathbb{C}\}.

We denote by
\ell^{2}(K,m_{K})
a Hilbert space
of functions
\{f\in C(K)\}
with the scalar product

(f\mid g)_{K}=\sum_{x\in K}f(x)\overline{g(x)}m(x).

Let us define a sesquilinear form
\widehat{Q}_{K}
on
\ell^{2}(K,m_{k})
by

\widehat{Q}_{K}(f,g)=\mathcal{Q}(i_{K}f,i_{K}g),

where
i_{K}:C(K)\to C_{c}(K)
is an extension of
f\in C(K)
to
X
by setting
i_{K}f
be identically zero outside
K
. It follows from sectoriality of
\mathcal{Q}
, Lemma
2.5
, that
\widehat{Q}_{K}
is a sectorial form. We denote a sectorial operator, associated to
\widehat{Q}_{K}
, by
\widehat{L}_{K}
and call it the
(Dirichlet) Laplacian on
K
. We denote by
D(\widehat{L}_{K})
the domain of
\widehat{L}_{K}
. The operator
\widehat{L}_{K}
is
m
-sectorial and, due too the finitness of
K
,
D(\widehat{L}_{K})=\ell^{2}(K,m_{K})
(see, e.g.
[
12
, p. 322, Theorem 2.1]
).

Following the same outline as in the proof of Lemma
2.14
we can show, that

\widehat{L}_{K}f(x)=(\mathcal{L}\circ i_{K})(f)(x),

for all
f\in\ell^{2}(K,m_{K})
and any
x\in K
.

Now we are in the position to show that the operators
\widehat{L}_{K}+\alpha
are invertible for all
\alpha\geq 0
(Theorem
3.2
). We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph,
K\subset X
is finite connected,
x\in K
. Let
\alpha\geq 0
. Then the problem

(4)

\begin{cases}v_{\alpha}(x)=r,\\
(\mathcal{L}+\alpha)v_{\alpha}=0\mbox{ on }K\setminus\{x\},\\
v_{\alpha}=0\mbox{ on }X\setminus K,\end{cases}

has a unique solution
v_{\alpha}\in C(X)
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,

Theorem 3.3

4.
Electrical networks

4.1.
Definitions and main properties

It is known, see, e.g.
[
2
,
8
,
15
]
that an electrical network, connected to an AC source, can be represented as a graph, whose edges are equipped with complex weights, depending on a parameter. The following formal definition was introduced in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

An
electrical network
is a graph
(X,E,m)
, whose each edge is equipped with an
admittance
,
i.e. a function

a^{(s)}(x,y)=\dfrac{s}{L_{xy}s^{2}+R_{xy}s+D_{xy}},

where
L_{xy},R_{xy},D_{xy}\geq 0
and at least one of them is not zero.

We assume
a^{(s)}(x,y)\equiv 0
if there is no edge between
x
and
y
. Then
a^{(s)}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
for any
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
and any electrical network is uniquely determined by the triple
(X,a^{(s)},m)
to which we will refer as an electrical network.

Theorem 4.2
.

The electrical network
(X,a^{(s)},m)
defines a complex-weighted graph with the secoriality constant

c=\dfrac{|\operatorname{Im}s|}{\operatorname{Re}s},

for any
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
.

Proof.

By
[
14
, Lemma 4]
we have

\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x,y)}{|a^{(s)}(x,y)|}\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}s}{|s|},

which can be rewritten as

{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}a^{(s)}(x,y))}{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}s+\operatorname{Im}^{2}s)}\geq{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}s)}{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}a^{(s)}(x,y)+\operatorname{Im}^{2}a^{(s)}(x,y))},

which is equivalent to

{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}a^{(s)}(x,y))}{(\operatorname{Im}^{2}s)}\geq{(\operatorname{Re}^{2}s)}{(\operatorname{Im}^{2}a^{(s)}(x,y))},

from where immediately follows that the weight
a^{(s)}(x,y)
satisfies sectoriality (
1
) with

c=\dfrac{|\operatorname{Im}s|}{\operatorname{Re}s}.

∎

Therefore, we the formal Laplacian
\mathcal{L}^{(s)}
and sesquilinear form
\mathcal{Q}^{(s)}
can be introduced for electrical networks. We will omit superscript
(s)
when it is clear from the context.

4.2.
Complex-weighted graph “extension” to an electrical network

The real-weighted graphs are known to be related to electrical networks with resistors, see, e.g.
[
6
]
. In this section we show, that any complex-weighted graph (satisfying sectoriality property (
1
)) can be “extended” to an electrical network. Being an interesting result on its own, this also allows us to prove convergence results in Section
5
. The following theorem can be considered as the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph,
c
be its sectoriality constant (
1
).
Then for any
s_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
with
\dfrac{|\operatorname{Im}s_{0}|}{\operatorname{Re}s_{0}}\geq c
, there exist an electrical network
(X,a^{(s)},m)
such that

a^{(s_{0})}(x,y)=b(x,y)

for any
x,y\in X
.

Proof.

We need to prove that the equation

\dfrac{s_{0}}{L_{xy}s_{0}^{2}+R_{xy}s_{0}+D_{xy}}=b(x,y).

has a solution in terms of
L_{xy},R_{xy},D_{xy}
, where all the variables are positive, for any
b(x,y)\neq 0
, satisfying sectoriality (
1
). Then the corresponding electrical network will be defined by

a^{(s)}(x,y):=\dfrac{s}{L_{xy}s^{2}+R_{xy}s+D_{xy}},

for all
x,y\in X
with
b(x,y)\neq 0
and
a^{(s)}(x,y):=0
otherwise.

Note, that if
\operatorname{Im}b(x,y)=0
, then
L_{xy}=D_{xy}=0
and
R_{xy}=1/b(x,y)
is the needed solution. So assume
\operatorname{Im}b(x,y)\neq 0
. Let us denote
b:=b(x,y)
, omit subscripts
{xy}
for the proof and solve:

\dfrac{s_{0}}{Ls_{0}^{2}+Rs_{0}+D}=b.

Assume, the denominator is not zero (which will hold if the solution will be non-negative and positive for at least one of the variables
L,R,D
). Without loss of generality we can assume that
\operatorname{Re}s_{0}=1
(otherwise scale
L,R,D
). Hence, let us write
s_{0}=1+\omega i
, where
|\omega|\geq c
. Then we should solve

b(L(1+i\omega)^{2}+R(1+i\omega)+D)={1+i\omega},

which we rewrite as

(14)

L(1+i\omega)^{2}+R(1+i\omega)+D=\dfrac{{(1+i\omega)}\overline{b}}{|b|^{2}}.

Let
b_{1}=\operatorname{Re}b
and
b_{2}=\operatorname{Im}b
. By assumption
0<|b_{2}|\leq c\cdot b_{1}
. Then the equation (
14
), being split into real and imaginary part, is equivalent to:

\begin{cases}(1-\omega^{2})L+R+D=\dfrac{b_{1}+\omega b_{2}}{|b|^{2}},\\
2\omega L+\omega R=\dfrac{b_{1}\omega-b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}.\end{cases}

Since there are
2
equations and
3
variables, let us assume that
D=D_{0}
is fixed (we will choose it later) and find a solution for
(L_{0},R_{0})
, using the Cramer’s rule. Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1-\omega^{2}&1\\
2\omega&\omega\end{pmatrix},A_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{b_{1}+\omega b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}-D_{0}&1\\
\dfrac{b_{1}\omega-b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}&\omega\end{pmatrix},

and

A_{R}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\omega^{2}&\dfrac{b_{1}+\omega b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}-D_{0}\\
2\omega&\dfrac{b_{1}\omega-b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence we get

\det A=-\omega^{3}-\omega
and
\sgn(\det A)=-\sgn\omega
. Further,

(15)

\det A_{L}=\dfrac{(1+\omega^{2})b_{2}}{|b|^{2}}-\omega D_{0},

\det A_{R}=\dfrac{-(1+\omega^{2})(b_{1}\omega+b_{2})}{|b|^{2}}+2\omega D_{0}.

Since
L=\dfrac{\det A_{L}}{\det A}
and
R=\dfrac{\det A_{R}}{\det A}
should be non-negative, we should require
\sgn(\det A_{L}),\sgn(\det A_{R})\in\{0,-\sgn\omega\}
, i.e. we should choose
D_{0}
such that

(16)

\dfrac{(1+\omega^{2})b_{2}}{\omega|b|^{2}}\leq D_{0}\leq\dfrac{(1+\omega^{2})(b_{1}\omega+b_{2})}{2\omega|b|^{2}}.

Firstly note, that
|b_{1}\omega|=|b_{1}||\omega|\geq b_{1}c\geq|b_{2}|
, while
\sgn b_{1}\omega=\sgn\omega
(since
b_{1}\geq c|b_{2}|>0
), and, hence,
\sgn(b_{1}\omega+b_{2})=\sgn\omega
or
\sgn(b_{1}\omega+b_{2})=0
, i.e. the right hand side of the inequality (
16
) is non-negative. If
\sgn b_{2}=-\sgn\omega
, then the left hand side is negative and there exists a needed
D_{0}\geq 0
(note that if
D_{0}=0
, then
L_{0}\neq 0
since
\det A_{L}\neq 0
due to (
15
) and the assumption
b_{2}\neq 0
). So assume
\sgn b_{2}=\sgn\omega
. In this case since
b_{1}>0
, we obtain

\dfrac{b_{1}\omega+b_{2}}{\omega}=\dfrac{|b_{1}\omega+b_{2}|}{|\omega|}=\dfrac{|b_{1}||\omega|+|b_{2}|}{|\omega|}\geq\dfrac{|b_{1}|\cdot c+|b_{2}|}{|\omega|}\geq\dfrac{2|b_{2}|}{|\omega|}=\dfrac{2b_{2}}{\omega},

from where immediately follows that right-hand side of (
16
) is greater than or equal to the left-hand side and, hence, there exists
D_{0}>0
, satisfying (
16
), since
b_{2}\neq 0
.

∎

5.
Characterization of recurrence

5.1.
Concept of recurrence

A concept of recurrence has a very important meaning in probability theory, optimal transport and theory of electrical networks. The meaning of recurrence is the existence of a non-zero flow on a graph. For more details we refer reader to
[
13
, Chapter 6]
for analytic point of view and to
[
17
,
18
]
for the probabilistic interpretation. Moreover, there is a branch of equivalent definitions of recurrence for real-weighted graphs, see
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
, which gives the most complete description of equivalent definitions.
We also point out that in
[
16
]
the concept of recurrence for electrical networks (in the sense of Definition
4.1
) is considered with its applications to classical reversible random walks.

In this section we introduce recurrence for complex-weighted graphs and present some equivalent characterizations for it, following the real case, see
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
. In particular, we characterize recurrence in terms of capacity, Green’s function, resolvents of the Dirichlet Laplacian, and properties of the Neumann Laplacian. Although the results are the same as for real graphs, we should point out that methods of proofs are very different and heavily based on the fact that every complex-weighted graph is an electrical network, i.e. Theorem
4.3
, while in the case of real-weighted graphs proofs are usually based on monotone convergence theorem.

We firstly remind the reader how the concept of recurrence looks for a real-weighted graph, i.e. a complex-weighted graph
(X,\mathfrak{b},m)
with
\mathfrak{b}:V\to~\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
.

Definition 5.1
.

The real-weighted graph
(X,\mathfrak{b},m)
is called
recurrent
, if

(17)

\inf\;\{\;\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\phi)\;|\;\phi(x)=1,\phi\in C_{c}(X)\;\}=0,

for some (all)
x\in X
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is the corresponding formal energy.
Otherwise, the graph is called
transient
.

Note that classically one considers functions
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
, taking values in
\mathbb{R}
, in the definition of recurrence for real-weighted graphs (see, e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 6.1(vii), Definition 6.2]
), but the inverse triangle inequality implies

\big|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big|\geq\big||\phi(x)|-|\phi(y)|\big|,

for any
x,y\in X
and any
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
, and, hence, the classical definition is equivalent to (
17
).

We start with the following theorem, which immediately states an equivalence of several definitions of recurrence for complex-weighted graph, see Definition
5.3
below.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph and
\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{Q}_{b}
be a formal energy on it. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\inf\;\{\;|\mathcal{Q}(\phi)|\;|\;\phi(x)=1,\phi\in C_{c}(X)\;\}=0,
for some (all)
x\in X
,

(ii)

\inf\;\{\;\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\phi)\;|\;\phi(x)=1,\phi\in C_{c}(X)\;\}=0,
for some (all)
x\in X
,

(iii)

\inf\;\{\;\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\phi)\;|\;\phi(x)=1,\phi\in C_{c}(X)\}=0,
for some (all)
x\in X
.

Proof.

We will prove the equivalences for a fixed
x\in X
. Then the equivalence of “some” and “all” will follow from (iii). Indeed, (iii) for “some”
x\in X
defines a recurrence on the real-weighted graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
, and for real-weighted graphs the equivalence of “some” and “all” is known, see, e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 6.1 (vii)]
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from the sectoriality, Lemma
2.5
.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) since

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}b(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}\operatorname{Re}b(x,y)=\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\phi),

for any
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
.

∎

Definition 5.3
.

A complex-weighted graph
(X,b,m)
is called
recurrent
if any of the equivalent conditions of Theorem
5.2
is satisfied. Otherwise, the graph is called
transient
.

Note, that for the real-weighted graph Definition
5.3
coincides with the classical definition, described in the beginning of this section. Moreover, Theorem
5.2
immediately implies

Corollary 5.4
.

A graph
(X,b,m)
is recurrent if and only if the graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
is recurrent.

The following theorem gives one more basic characterization of recurrence.

Theorem 5.5
.

A graph
(X,b,m)
is recurrent if and only if
1\in\mathcal{D}_{0}.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
” Let the graph
(X,b,m)
be recurrent. Then the graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
is recurrent by Corollary
5.4
. Hence, by
[
13
, Theorem 6.1(i)]
, there exists a sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
with
\phi_{n}\to f
pointwise and

\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(1-\phi_{n})=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}|\phi_{n}(x)-\phi_{n}(y)|^{2}\operatorname{Re}b(x,y)\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence,
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(1-\phi_{n})=\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(1-\phi_{n})\to 0
and
1\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
.

“
\Leftarrow
”
Let
1\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Then there exists
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}(X)
such that
Q(1-\phi_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,

\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(1-|\phi_{n}|)\leq Q_{\operatorname{Re}b}(1-\phi_{n})=\operatorname{Re}Q(1-\phi_{n})\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty,

and the graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
is recurrent by
[
13
, Theorem 6.1(i)]
, which by Corollary
5.4
implies that
(X,b,m)
is recurrent.
∎

5.2.
Capacity

To define the capacity of an infinite graph we start with finite approximations and follow the approach from
[
9
]
and
[
14
]
for networks.

Let
(X,b,m)
be an infinite complex-weighted graph,
K\subset X
be finite and connected,
x\in K
. Then the
effective capacity of
K
at
x
is defined as

\operatorname{cap}_{K}(x)=\mathcal{L}u(x)m(x),

where
u
is the solution of the
Dirichlet problem:

\begin{cases}u(x)=1,\\
\mathcal{L}u(x)=0\mbox{ on }K\setminus\{x\},\\
u=0\mbox{ on }X\setminus K,\end{cases}

which is unique due to Lemma
3.1
.
Note that although
m
appears in the definition of effective capacity of
K
at
x
, indeed it does not depend on
m
since it cancels with the
m
appearing in the definition of the Laplacian. Moreover, by Green’s formula we obtain:

\operatorname{cap}_{K}(x)=Q(u).

We remind that a sequence of finite connected subsets
(K_{n})_{\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
is a finite exhaustion of
(X,b,m)
if
K_{n}\subset X
,
K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
X=\cup_{\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}K_{n}
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be an infinite complex-weighted graph,
x\in X
be a fixed vertex,
(K_{n})
be a finite exhaustion of the graph such that
x\in K_{0}
. Then the limit

\operatorname{cap}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{cap}_{K_{n}}(x)

is called an
(effective) capacity
of
x
.

Theorem 5.7
.

The effective capacity is well-defined.
i.e. the limit exists and does not depend on the exhaustion.

Proof.

In
[
14
, Theorem 6(a)]
it is proven, that the limit of effective capacities exists in the case of electrical networks and is a holomorphic function on
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
(see Definition
4.1
), for the ball exhaustion
(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
,
B_{n}(x)=\{y\in X\mid\dist(x,y)<n\}
, where by
\dist(x,y)
one means the lenght of the shortest path between vertices
x,y\in X
. Using the same outline of proof, one can show that the limit exists for any exhaustion
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
. Moreover, since the capacity in this case is a holomorphic function on the parameter
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
and the limits coincide for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
due to the classical theory of real-weighted graph, we immediately get, that the effective capacity of electrical network does not depend on the exhaustion, i.e. the capacity is well-defined for the electrical networks.

Finally, due to Theorem
4.3
, the capacity is well-defined for any complex-weighted graph, since the uniform limit (see again
[
14
, Theorem 6(a)]
) on any compact subset of
\mathbb{H}_{r}
implies pointwise limit.
∎

In the case of real-weighted graphs, the solution of the Dirichlet problem on any finite
K
minimizes the energy among all the functions, supported on
K
. This fact almost immediately implies, that recurrence is equivalent to a zero capacity for some (all) vertices of an infinite graph. In the complex-weighted case the minimization property for the solution of the Dirichlet problem fails. In the next theorem we show, that the recurrence is still equivalent to the zero capacity.

Theorem 5.8
.

A graph
(X,b,m)
is recurrent if and only if
\operatorname{cap}(x)=0
for some (all)
x\in X
.

Proof.

Due to Definition
5.3
of recurrence it is enough to prove that
\operatorname{cap}(x)=~0
is equivalent to

(18)

\inf\;\{\;|\mathcal{Q}(\phi)|\;|\;\phi(x)=1,\phi\in C_{c}(X)\;\}=0,

for a fixed
x\in X
. Then the statement for all
x\in X
will follow from Theorem
5.2
.

Let
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
be a finite exhaustion of
(X,b,m)
,
x\in K_{0}
.
Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the solutions of the Dirichlet problems

\begin{cases}u_{n}(x)=1,\\
\mathcal{L}_{b}u_{n}(y)=0,\;y\neq x,y\in K_{n},\\
u_{n}(y)=0,\;y\in X\setminus K_{n}.\end{cases}

•

Let
\operatorname{cap}(x)=0
. Then
\mathcal{Q}(u_{n})=\operatorname{cap}_{K_{n}}(x)\to 0,n\to\infty
, i.e. (
18
) holds.

•

Let now (
18
) holds. Let us consider the Dirichlet problems on
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
, i.e.

\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)=1,\\
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Re}b}\widetilde{u}_{n}(a)=0,\;y\neq x,y\in K_{n},\\
\widetilde{u}_{n}(y)=0,\;y\in X\setminus K_{n}.\end{cases}

Note, that
\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
, due to the classical theory of real-weighted graphs (see
[
13
, Theorem 6.1(xi.b)]
), since
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
is recurrent by Corollary
5.4
. Hence

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(\widetilde{u}_{n})=\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Further, since all functions are finitely supported, we get by Green’s formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n},\widetilde{u}_{n})=(\mathcal{L}_{b}u_{n}\;|\;\widetilde{u}_{n})=\mathcal{L}_{b}u_{n}(x)m(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{b}u_{n}(x)u_{n}(x)m(x)=(\mathcal{L}_{b}u_{n}\;|\;u_{n})=\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n}).

Using (
3
), we get

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})\leq|\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})|=|\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n},\widetilde{u}_{n})|\leq(1+c)(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n}))^{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(\widetilde{u}_{n}))^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
c
is the sectoriality constant (
1
) for
(X,b,m)
.
Therefore,

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})\leq(1+c)^{2}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(\widetilde{u}_{n})=(1+c)^{2}\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

and
|\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})|\leq(1+c)\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, where the last inequality is due to the sectoriality of
\mathcal{Q}
.

∎

Remark 5.9
.

Since
\widetilde{u}_{n}
minimizes the energy on all the real-valued functions, supported on
K_{n}
for the graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
and
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(|u_{n}|)\leq\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})
by convexity of absolute value, we get

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\leq\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(|u_{n}|)\leq\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(u_{n})\leq(1+c)^{2}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}_{b}(\widetilde{u}_{n}).

5.3.
Green’s function

Now we define Green’s function for complex-weighted graph. The definition requires some substantial work and is based on Theorem
4.3
and estimate (
6
). Moreover, further in this section we show, that the relation of the Green’s function to recurrence is the same as in the case of real-weighted graphs, i.e. the Green’s function is equal to infinity if and only if the graph is recurrent, see Corollary
5.12
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph,
x,y\in X
. We define the
Green’s function

G:X\times X\to\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
by

G(x,y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}T(t)1_{x}(y)dt,

where
T(t)
is the contractive holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup, generated by the Dirichlet Laplacian
L^{(D)}
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph,
x,y\in X
. The Green’s function is well-defined, i.e. the corresponding limit exists in
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Proof.

By resolvent equality and Theorem
3.4
we have:

(19)

G(x,y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}(L^{(D)}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}({L_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y),

where
L_{n}:=L_{K_{n}},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
are the Dirichlet Laplacians with respect to
K_{n}
(see (
13
)), for some finite exhaustion
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
of
(X,b,m)
. Without loss of generality we can assume
x\in K_{0}
. It is clear by (
13
), that

(L_{n}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}(\widehat{L}_{n}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y),\quad y\in K_{n}\\
0,\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\widehat{L}_{n}
is the Laplacian on
\ell^{2}(K_{n},m_{K_{n}})
. Hence, the quantity
({L_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}
is well-defined for any
x,y\in X
. Moreover, for a fixed
y\in X
there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
y\in K_{N}
and, hence,

(20)

(L_{n}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=(\widehat{L}_{n}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)\mbox{ for all }n>N.

Therefore we need to prove the existence of the limit

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}({L_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}({\widehat{L}_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y).

By the proof of Theorem
3.2
we have

(21)

(\widehat{L}_{n}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\dfrac{v_{\alpha,n}(y)}{(\mathcal{L}v_{\alpha,n}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n})(x)}\mbox{ for all }\alpha>0,n\in\mathbb{N},

where
v_{\alpha,n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, are the solutions of the following Dirichlet problems:

(22)

\begin{cases}v_{\alpha,n}(x)=1,\\
(\mathcal{L}+\alpha)v_{\alpha,n}=0\mbox{ on }K_{n}\setminus\{x\},\\
v_{\alpha,n}=0\mbox{ on }X\setminus K_{n}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
4.3
there exist an electrical network
(X,a^{(s)},m)
such that for
s_{0}=1+ic
(i.e
\operatorname{Im}s_{0}/\operatorname{Re}s_{0}=c
), where
c
is the sectoriality constant of the graph
(X,b,m)
, the following holds

a^{(s_{0})}(x,y)=b(x,y),

for all
x,y\in X
. Let us extend the Dirichlet problem (
22
) to the electrical network
(X,a^{(s)},m)
, i.e. we have

\begin{cases}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x)=1,\\
(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}+\alpha)v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}=0\mbox{ on }K_{n}\setminus\{x\},\\
v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}=0\mbox{ on }X\setminus K_{n}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
3.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\big(\mathcal{L}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\mathcal{Q}\big(v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)\Big|+\alpha\|v_{\alpha,n}\|^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\dfrac{|\operatorname{Im}s|^{2}}{(\operatorname{Re}{s})^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in X:\\
y\sim x\end{subarray}}\big|a^{(s)}(x,y)\big|+\alpha\cdot m(x)\right),

where we have used Theorem
4.2
.
By simple calculations, see
[
14
, proof of Corollary 2]
, we have:

(24)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in X:\\
y\sim x\end{subarray}}a^{(s)}(x,y)\leq\frac{1+|s|^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in X:\\
y\sim x\end{subarray}}\left(R_{xy}+L_{xy}+D_{xy}\right)^{-1},\mbox{ for any }x,y\in X.

Combining this with (
5.3
) we get

(25)

\Big|\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)\Big|\leq\dfrac{|s|^{2}}{(\operatorname{Re}s)^{2}}\left(\frac{1+|s|^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}s}C_{1}+C_{2}\right),

where
C_{1},C_{2}
do not depend on
s
and
n
. Hence, the function
\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)
is uniformly bounded for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
in any domain

\{\operatorname{Re}s\geq\varepsilon,|s|\leq C\},\quad\varepsilon,C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Hence, by Montel’s theorem (see, e.g.
[
5
, p. 153]
), the sequence

\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

has a normally converging subsequence. Since the limit of the sequence is known to exist for any
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
(in this case we get a real-weighted graph), and the holomorphic function is uniquely determined by its values on the real line, we get that there exist the limit

(26)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x),

for any
\alpha\geq 0
, and this limit is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}_{r}
.
Further, since (
25
) implies uniform boundness of
\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)
for any
\alpha<1
, we obtain by the same line of arguments, that there exist a holomorphic limit

(27)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x).

Since the limit (
26
) for
\alpha=0
and the limit (
27
) coincide on the real positive half-line (
[
13
, Theorem 6.26]
) we have:

(28)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}+\alpha v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{0,n}^{(s)}\big)(x).

Moreover, since by Green’s formula

\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s)}v_{0,n}^{(s)}\big)(x)=\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(v^{(s)}_{0,n})}{m(x)}>0,

for all
n>N
, for any
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
, by Hurwitz theorem (see, e.g.
[
5
, p. 152, 2.6 Corollary]
) the limit in (
28
) either has no zeros on the right half-plane or is identically zero. Note that due to Theorem
5.8
, the latest case is equivalent to the recurrence of the graph
(X,b,m)
, where
b=a^{(s)}
for (some) all
s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
.

Now we apply a similar argument to show the convergence of
v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)
, where
y\in X
is fixed, i.e. it is enough to prove the uniform boundness of
v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)
for compact subsets of
\mathbb{H}_{r}
. Let
x=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim\dots\sim x_{k}=y
be a path between
x
and
y
. By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
5.3
) and (
24
) we get

(29)

\displaystyle\big|

\displaystyle v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)-1\big|^{2}=\big|v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)-v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big|v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x_{i})-v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x_{i-1})\big|\,\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big|v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x_{i})-v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(x_{i-1})\big|^{2}\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\mathcal{Q}\big(v^{(s)}_{\alpha,n}\big)\Big|\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\dfrac{|\operatorname{Im}s|^{2}}{(\operatorname{Re}{s})^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in X:\\
y\sim x\end{subarray}}\big|a^{(s)}(x,y)\big|+\alpha\cdot m(x)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{|s|^{2}}{(\operatorname{Re}s)^{2}}\left(\frac{1+|s|^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}s}C_{1}+C_{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\right),

where
C_{1},C_{2}
does not depend on
s
. Let us estimate the last term, starting from an estimate of
\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(z,w),z,w\in X,s\in\mathbb{H}_{r}
. By Definition
4.1
of an electrical network we have

\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}(z,w)=\dfrac{s}{L_{zw}s^{2}+R_{zw}s+D_{zw}}.

We estimate, using
|s|^{2}\geq|s|\operatorname{Re}s
for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
in the second line:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}a^{(s)}

\displaystyle(z,w)=\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{s(L_{zw}\overline{s}^{2}+R_{zw}\overline{s}+D_{zw})}{|L_{zw}s^{2}+R_{zw}s+D_{zw}|^{2}}\geq\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{s(L_{zw}\overline{s}^{2}+R_{zw}\overline{s}+D_{zw})}{(L_{zw}|s|^{2}+R_{zw}|s|+D_{zw})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\dfrac{L_{zw}|s|^{2}\overline{s}+R_{zw}|s|^{2}+D_{zw}s}{(L_{zw}|s|^{2}+R_{zw}|s|+D_{zw})^{2}}\geq(\operatorname{Re}s)\dfrac{L_{zw}|s|^{2}+R_{zw}|s|+D_{zw}}{(L_{zw}|s|^{2}+R_{zw}|s|+D_{zw})^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{Re}s)\dfrac{1}{L_{zw}|s|^{2}+R_{zw}|s|+D_{zw}}\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}s}{\max\{1,|s|,|s|^{2}\}}\dfrac{1}{L_{zw}+R_{zw}+D_{zw}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}s}{\max\{1,|s|^{2}\}}\dfrac{1}{L_{zw}+R_{zw}+D_{zw}}\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{Re}s}{1+|s|^{2}}\dfrac{1}{L_{zw}+R_{zw}+D_{zw}}.

since either
|s|<1
or
|s|\leq|s|^{2}
.
Combining this with (
29
) we obtain:

\big|v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)-1\big|^{2}\leq\dfrac{|s|^{2}(1+|s|^{2})}{(\operatorname{Re}s)^{3}}\left(\frac{1+|s|^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}s}C_{1}+C_{2}\right)C_{3},

where
C_{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(L_{x_{i-1}x_{i}}+R_{x_{i-1}x_{i}}+D_{x_{i-1}x_{i}})
,
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
do not depend on
s
,
n
and any
\alpha<1
. Hence,
v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)
is uniformly bounded for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
in any domain

\{\operatorname{Re}s\geq\varepsilon,|s|\leq C\},\quad\varepsilon,C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Therefore, again by Montel’s theorem and existence of the limit on the real half-line, we conclude
the existence and the equality of the limits

(30)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{\alpha,n}^{(s)}(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{0,n}^{(s)}(y).

Moreover, note that in the case
\Big|\mathcal{Q}\big(v^{(s)}_{0,n}\big)\Big|\to 0
(i.e in the case of recurrence), we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{0,n}^{(s)}(y)=1
by the fourth line of (
29
).

Finally, (
21
), (
28
) and (
30
) imply

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}({\widehat{L}_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}({\widehat{L}_{n}})^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{v_{0,n}^{(s_{0})}(y)}{\big(\mathcal{L}^{(s_{0})}v_{0,n}^{(s_{0})}\big)(x)},

and the last limit is finite in the case of transient graph and is
1/0=\infty
in the case of recurrent graph. Due to (
19
) and (
20
) the theorem is proven.
∎

The proof above immediately implies the following characterization of recurrence, which is known for the real-weighted graphs, where it can be proven by monotone convergence argument (see
[
13
, Chapter 6.4]
).

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. Then for its Green’s function holds

G(x,y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}(L^{(D)}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\to 0,\\
\alpha>0\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\to\infty}({L_{n}}+\alpha)^{-1}1_{x}(y).

Moreover, the graph is recurrent if and only if
G(x,y)=\infty
for some (all)
x,y\in X
.

5.4.
Neumann Laplacian

The aim of this section is to introduce the Neumann Laplacian on infinite complex-weighted graphs, and show that it coincides with the Dirichlet Laplacian for all measures if and only if the graph is recurrent. This result is known for real-weighted graphs and we use the same line of arguments, see
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a complex-weighted graph. We define the form
Q^{(N)}
as a restriction of the formal form
\mathcal{Q}
to
\mathcal{D}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
. We will call
Q^{(N)}
the
form corresponding to the Neumann Laplacian
.

Lemma 5.13
.

The form
Q^{(N)}
with the domain
D(Q^{(N)})=\mathcal{D}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
is a densely defined closed sectorial form in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
.

Proof.

The sectoriality follows from Lemma
2.5
. The density is clear, since
C_{c}(X)\subset\mathcal{D}
is dense in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
. To prove the closedness, we use the fact that the lower semi-continuity implies closedness (see
[
13
, Theorem B.9, proof (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)]
). Since
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)=\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}(f)
, using lower-semicontinuity of the form
\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Re}b}
on the real-weighted graph
(X,\operatorname{Re},b,m)
(see
[
13
, Proposition 1.3]
for the details) and Lemma
2.6
(2), we obtain that the form
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{b}
is lower semi-continuos, i.e.

(31)

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f)\leq\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{Q}(f_{n})

for any
f,f_{n}\in\mathcal{D},n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{n}(x)\to f(x),n\to\infty
pointwise for all
x\in X
. Since
Q^{(N)}
is a restriction of
\mathcal{Q}
and convergence in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
implies pointwise convergence, we get from (
31
) that

\operatorname{Re}Q^{(N)}(f)\leq\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Re}Q^{(N)}(f_{n})

for any
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
f
in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Therefore,
Q^{(N)}
with the domain
D(Q^{(N)})=\mathcal{D}\cap\ell^{2}(X,m)
is also lower semi-continuous, and, hence, closed.
∎

By the general theory of sectorial forms and operators, see e.g.
[
12
]
, the form
Q^{(N)}
defines an
m
-sectorial operator, which we call the
Neumann Laplacian
and denote by
L^{(N)}
, with a domain
D(L^{(N)})\subset D(Q^{(N)})
, see
[
12
, p. 322, Theorem 2.1]
.
Following the same outline as in the proof of Lemma
2.14
we can show, that
L^{(N)}f(x)=\mathcal{L}f(x)
for all
f\in D(L^{(N)})
and any
x\in X
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
(X,b,m)
be a graph. Then
D\big(Q^{(D)}\big)=D\big(Q^{(N)}\big)
if and only if

D(L^{(D)})=\{f\in D(Q^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\}.

Proof.

By the definition of the associated operator,
L^{(D)}
maps to
\ell^{2}(X,m)
, Hence, since its action coincides with the action of the formal Lapalcian, the inclusion

D(L^{(D)})\subset\{f\in D(Q^{(D)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\},

always holds. This immediately implies the inclusion

D(L^{(D)})\subset\{f\in D(Q^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\},

since
D(Q^{(D)})\subset D(Q^{(N)})
by the definitions of the domains.

•

Let
D\big(Q^{(D)}\big)=D\big(Q^{(N)}\big)
. By the above we need to prove

\{f\in D(Q^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\}\subset D(L^{(D)}).

Let
f\in D(Q^{(N)})=D(Q^{(D)})
. Then by Greens’ formula we have

Q^{(D)}(f,\phi)=(\mathcal{L}f\;\mid\;\phi),

for all
\phi\in C_{c}(X)
. As
D(Q^{(D)})=\overline{C_{c}(X)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Q}}}
by Theorem
2.11
, we conclude

Q^{(D)}(f,g)=(\mathcal{L}f\;\mid\;g),

for all
g\in D(Q^{(D)})
. Hence,
f\in D(L^{(D)})
.

•

Let
D(L^{(D)})=\{f\in D(Q^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\}.
By the definition of the associated operator,
L^{(N)}
maps to
\ell^{2}(X,m)
. Hence, since its action coincides with the action of the formal Lapalcian we conclude

D(L^{(N)})\subset\{f\in D(Q^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)\}=D(L^{(D)}).

As both operators are restrictions of
\mathcal{L}
and
m
-sectorial, we conclude
D(L^{(N)})=D(L^{(D)}).
Hence, the associated forms also coincide.

∎

Theorem 5.15
.

A complex-weighted graph
(X,b,m)
is recurrent if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold.

(i)

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{0}
.

(ii)

for all measures
\mu:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
the domains
D\big(Q_{\mu}^{(D)}\big)
and
D\big(Q_{\mu}^{(N)}\big)
of the forms on graph
(X,b,\mu)
, coincide.

(iii)

for all measures
\mu:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
:

D(L_{\mu}^{(D)})=\{f\in D(Q_{\mu}^{(N)})\;\mid\;\mathcal{L}f\in\ell^{2}(X,\mu)\},

where
L_{\mu}^{(D)}
is the Dirichlet Laplacian and
Q_{\mu}^{(N)}
is a form, corresponding to the Neumann Laplacian on the graph
(X,b,\mu)
.

Proof.

Recurrence
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
(X,b,m)
be recurrent. By Corollary
5.4
the graph
(X,\operatorname{Re}b,m)
is also recurrent. Hence, any real-valued function from
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is in
\mathcal{D}
(see
[
13
, Theorem 6.1 (i.a)]
). Now (i) follows from Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
2.6
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) since
D\big(Q_{\mu}^{(D)}\big)=\mathcal{D}_{0}\cap\ell^{2}(X,\mu)
and
D\big(Q_{\mu}^{(D)}\big)=\mathcal{D}\cap\ell^{2}(X,\mu)
by the definitions of the corresponding forms.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
Recurrence
. Taking a finite measure
\mu
(i.e.
\mu(X)<\infty
), we get
1\in\ell^{2}(X,\mu)
. Hence,
1\in D(Q_{\mu}^{(N)})=D(Q_{\mu}^{(D)})
, from where follows
1\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
and recurrence by Theorem
5.5
.

Finally, (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) due to Lemma
5.14
.

∎
