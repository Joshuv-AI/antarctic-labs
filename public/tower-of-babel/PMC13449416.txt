Rescuing a refractory Leiomyosarcoma: ALK-DCTN1 fusion as a gateway to long-term control with Ceritinib
Authors: Francis Yew Fu Tieng, Sri Kisha Subramaniam, Po Lin Ooi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUterine leiomyosarcoma (uLMS) is an aggressive malignancy with limited advanced-stage treatments. Actionable molecular alterations like ALK fusions are extremely rare but present critical opportunities for targeted therapy.Case PresentationA 59-year-old woman with stage IV uLMS, initially misdiagnosed as uterine fibroids, underwent a hysterectomy in 2008. Recurrence was confirmed in 2011. Over the following decade, she developed progressive pelvic disease requiring multiple debulking surgeries, arterial embolizations, and pembrolizumab, which was stopped due to progression and adrenal insufficiency. Her course was complicated by obstructive uropathy and bowel perforation. Next-generation sequencing identified an ALK-DCTN1 fusion. She was commenced on the ALK inhibitor ceritinib at 450 mg daily, later reduced to 300 mg due to transaminitis. Treatment yielded significant tumour regression and durable partial remission. A May 2025 PET-CT showed no FDG-avid disease and no evidence of new local recurrence or distant metastasis, consistent with a metabolic complete response, although stable residual pelvic lesions persisted.ConclusionThis case underscores the importance of comprehensive molecular profiling in advanced uLMS. It demonstrates that targeting rare ALK rearrangements with ceritinib can achieve sustained disease control after conventional therapies have failed or were poorly tolerated.

Introduction

Uterine leiomyosarcoma is an aggressive mesenchymal malignancy representing 1% to 2% of all uterine neoplasms (Dunphy and Sheridan, 2021; Alodaini, 2024). Characterized by high recurrence rates and rapid hematogenous dissemination, advanced uLMS carries a dismal prognosis and responds poorly to conventional cytotoxic anthracycline-based regimens (Khamaiseh et al., 2025). Furthermore, efforts to utilize immunotherapy have yielded disappointing results; the landmark SARC028 Phase II trial reported a 0% objective response rate to single-agent pembrolizumab within its leiomyosarcoma cohort, establishing that these tumours are immunologically “cold” and highly resistant to standard immune checkpoint blockade (Tawbi et al., 2017).

This therapeutic impasse has driven a paradigm shift toward comprehensive genomic profiling to uncover rare, actionable oncogenic drivers. While uLMS is characterized by a complex genomic landscape dominated by tumour suppressor gene alterations (such as TP53 and RB1), next-generation sequencing has identified rare but actionable ALK gene fusions in approximately 1–2% of cases (Alodaini, 2024; Choi et al., 2021; Davis et al., 2019). These chromosomal rearrangements cause ligand-independent, constitutive activation of the ALK receptor tyrosine kinase, driving downstream oncogenic proliferation and offering a distinct therapeutic vulnerability (Voena et al., 2025; Holla et al., 2017).

The clinical availability of successive generations of ALK-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)—such as crizotinib, alectinib, and lorlatinib—now provides an avenue for durable, low-toxicity molecular matching (Lin et al., 2017). In this report, we present a patient with advanced uLMS harboring an actionable ALK rearrangement. By bypassing empirical cytotoxic chemotherapies and ineffective immunotherapies, this case demonstrates how early genomic intervention and targeted TKI therapy can fundamentally subvert the aggressive trajectory of a traditionally treatment-resistant malignancy.

Case presentation

A 53-year-old woman (at the time of diagnosis of malignancy) with a remote history of pulmonary tuberculosis in her early 20s (having completed six months of anti-tuberculous therapy), presented with pelvic pain since 2010. She was initially diagnosed with uterine fibroids and underwent trans-abdominal hysterectomy (without oophorectomy) in the same year.

Following hysterectomy, she developed recurrent pelvic pain. A CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis performed during subsequent evaluation demonstrated recurrent tumour in the lower abdominal mesentery and left anterior abdominal wall. A Gastrografin® enema showed extrinsic compression of the sigmoid colon. She subsequently underwent removal of the mass and salpingo-oophorectomy. Histopathological examination confirmed uLMS. Over the following years, the mass recurred only locally in the pelvis, requiring further debulking surgery of the pelvic mass in 2011. In 2013, the mass continues to progress, and she underwent super-selective embolization of the mass due to persistent pelvis pain. She reported marked symptomatic improvement, and imaging showed tumour shrinkage with a clinically stable course for approximately six years. No systemic therapy was given throughout this period.

In 2019, surveillance imaging revealed an interval increase in tumour size, and embolization of feeding arteries was repeated. Despite this, the pelvic mass continued to progress and causing pain despite analgesics. In 2020, she underwent further debulking surgery followed by initiation of pembrolizumab in another private hospital, administered every 3 weekly for eight months. This was a shared decision made with patient and family as data was scarce for use of pembrolizumab for sarcoma. However, serial ultrasound scans demonstrated disease progression, with tumour dimensions increasing from approximately 8.6 × 8.1 × 6 cm to 10 × 10 × 9 cm (Fig. 1) with no evidence distant metastases. The patient subsequently developed adrenal insufficiency, attributed to prior pembrolizumab therapy, which manifesting as episodes of hypoglycaemia. A further attempt at embolization in 2020 was unsuccessful and was complicated by a sealed bowel perforation, necessitating laparotomy, peritoneal washout, and small bowel resection with primary anastomosis. She was enrolled into a comprehensive genetic profiling clinical trial in March 2021. Molecular analysis identified an ALK-DCTN1 fusion, a rare but therapeutically relevant rearrangement in uLMS, suggesting potential sensitivity to ALK-directed TKI therapy. Considering this finding, she was commenced on ceritinib 450 mg once daily as this was the ALK inhibitor that patient has access to. Shortly after initiation, she developed Grade 2 transaminitis, and the dose was reduced to 300 mg once daily, which was subsequently well tolerated. Renal function showed grade 1 rise in serum creatinine after initiation of ceritinib but remained stable with close monitoring.

Following commencement of ceritinib, serial imaging demonstrated significant reduction in tumour size, with residual pelvic lesions that decreased and subsequently stabilised. She reported marked improvement in pelvic pain and performance status, no longer requiring high-dose opioids. She continued ceritinib 300 mg once daily with regular clinical and radiological follow-up.

Since 2021, she has remained in remission, with clinical review and abdominal ultrasonography every one to two months, and cross-sectional imaging approximately every two months. The most recent PET-CT in 2025 has shown no evidence of active disease (Fig. 2). Clinically, she remains in radiological remission for more than four years after initiation of ceritinib, with no pain reported and good quality of life. Discussion

Uterine leiomyosarcoma is a rare, highly aggressive mesenchymal malignancy that accounts for roughly 40–50% of uterine sarcomas and approximately 1–2% of all uterine cancers (Dunphy and Sheridan, 2021; Alodaini, 2024). Despite therapeutic advances, clinical outcomes for advanced, unresectable, or recurrent uLMS remain poor, with durable responses to conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy achieved in only a small minority of patients (Arend et al., 2018). Large-scale multi-omics analyses consistently demonstrate that uLMS is characterized by a highly complex genome dominated by copy-number alterations and mutations in core tumour suppressor pathways (TP53, RB1, ATRX) rather than canonical, targetable oncogenic drivers (Alodaini, 2024; Choi et al., 2021). However, a small subset of uLMS cases (∼2.4%) harbor kinase gene fusions, including ALK rearrangements, which may represent actionable therapeutic vulnerabilities (Davis et al., 2019).

Prior to identifying the ALK rearrangement via comprehensive genomic profiling, the patient underwent multiple local therapies, including debulking surgeries and arterial embolization, which provided only temporary symptomatic and disease control. Similarly, treatment with pembrolizumab failed to halt tumour progression. This lack of efficacy was consistent with findings from the landmark SARC028 Phase II trial, which reported an objective response rate of 0% among patients with leiomyosarcoma treated with single-agent pembrolizumab, highlighting the limited activity of immune checkpoint blockade in this histological subtype (Tawbi et al., 2017). Compounding the lack of efficacy, the patient suffered immune-related adrenal insufficiency, presenting with severe hypoglycemic episodes that required long-term, permanent hydrocortisone replacement under endocrine supervision. This underscored an urgent need for an oncogene-directed strategy, which was successfully achieved only after enrolling the patient in a molecular profiling clinical trial.

Since ALK inhibitors are not explicitly FDA-approved for soft tissue sarcomas or uLMS, their use in this setting remains off-label and requires consideration of available clinical evidence, tumour biology, and individual patient factors (Denu et al., 2024; Testa et al., 2021; Mavroeidis et al., 2024). The development of ALK inhibitors has progressed through three generations, with each generation designed to improve potency, overcome acquired resistance mechanisms, and enhance central nervous system (CNS) activity (Lin et al., 2017; Shreenivas et al., 2023). The first-generation inhibitor crizotinib established the clinical efficacy of ALK inhibition and demonstrated activity in ALK-rearranged malignancies, including inflammatory myofibroblastic tumours. However, its clinical utility is limited by acquired resistance mechanisms and relatively poor CNS penetration compared with newer-generation ALK inhibitors (Costa et al., 2011).

Second-generation ALK inhibitors, including ceritinib, alectinib, and brigatinib, were developed to provide greater ALK inhibitory potency and activity against several resistance mutations that emerge during crizotinib treatment. These agents also demonstrate improved CNS activity, although their pharmacological profiles and toxicity patterns differ. Ceritinib, the ALK inhibitor selected in our patient, is a potent ATP-competitive ALK inhibitor that prevents ALK kinase activation and downstream signalling pathways involved in tumour proliferation and survival. Although alectinib and brigatinib are currently favoured in many treatment settings due to favourable efficacy and tolerability profiles, ceritinib remains a clinically active ALK inhibitor with demonstrated activity in ALK-rearranged tumours, including rare non-classical ALK fusion-positive malignancies (Friboulet et al., 2014).

The third-generation inhibitor lorlatinib was subsequently developed to address additional resistance mechanisms and possesses broad activity against multiple ALK resistance mutations, including certain compound mutations. It also demonstrates excellent CNS penetration, although its use requires monitoring for characteristic adverse effects such as hyperlipidaemia and neuropsychiatric toxicity (Bauer et al., 2020).

Following the identification of the ALK–DCTN1 fusion in March 2021, our patient was commenced on ceritinib 450 mg once daily. The clinical management of this targeted approach required vigilant monitoring; after developing an initial grade 2 transaminitis and a grade 1 rise in serum creatinine, a proactive dose reduction to 300 mg once daily resolved the toxicity while preserving therapeutic efficacy. This dose adjustment allowed for long-term compliance and sustained oncogenic suppression.

Most importantly, this case underscores the critical role of molecular profiling in identifying targetable alterations in tumours that have become refractory to conventional therapies. Comprehensive genomic testing revealed an ALK-DCTN1 fusion, a rare but actionable rearrangement that is more commonly described in non-small cell lung cancer and certain lymphomas. The presence of this fusion provided a strong biological rationale for initiating ALK-directed therapy in this patient's uLMS. Treatment with ceritinib led to substantial reduction in tumour burden and durable partial remission lasting more than four years to date, with manageable toxicity following dose reduction for transaminitis.

From a uterine perspective, ALK alterations are rare but clinically significant. A recent narrative review of uterine mesenchymal tumours, summarising data from the large genomic registry AACR GENIE, reported that histology-agnostic, FDA-actionable alterations—including NTRK fusions, mismatch-repair defects, BRAF V600E mutations and RET fusions—were present in only 2.7% of uterine sarcomas, whereas an additional 3.8% harboured potentially targetable lesions such as BRCA1/2 mutations, FGFR fusions and ALK fusions (Alodaini, 2024). Within this cohort, ALK rearrangements were detected in a small number of cases spanning LMS, undifferentiated uterine sarcoma, adenosarcoma, and smooth muscle tumours of uncertain malignant potential, underscoring the very low but clinically relevant prevalence of ALK fusions across uterine mesenchymal histology.

Published clinical experience with ALK-rearranged uLMS and related uterine mesenchymal tumours, though limited, supports the use of ALK inhibitors in this setting. Kyi et al. described four patients with uterine mesenchymal tumours harboring ALK fusions, including cases classified as LMS and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour, all of whom received ALK-targeted therapy (crizotinib or second-generation ALK TKIs) (Kyi et al., 2021; Carballo et al., 2022). In this small series, ALK inhibition led to radiological responses or durable disease stabilisation, illustrating that ALK-rearranged uterine mesenchymal tumours can be clinically ALK-dependent.

A particularly relevant example is the case reported by Testa et al., describing a 63-year-old woman with metastatic uLMS carrying an FN1–ALK fusion (Testa et al., 2021). Following progression on multiple lines of chemotherapy and limited benefit from crizotinib, the patient achieved a prolonged response of approximately 16 months with sequential second-generation ALK inhibitors, alectinib and lorlatinib. This case represents one of the first well-documented examples of uLMS with an ALK fusion demonstrating sustained clinical benefit from ALK-directed therapy, supporting the potential applicability of ALK inhibition strategies established in ALK-rearranged non-small cell lung cancer to selected rare sarcoma subtypes.

The sustained disease control observed in this patient indicates that ALK inhibition may represent a valuable therapeutic option for selected patients with ALK-rearranged uLMS, particularly those with heavily pre-treated, unresectable, or metastatic disease, and limited tolerance for further cytotoxic chemotherapy. This case also underscores the importance of multidisciplinary care, highlighting the need for close collaboration among gynaecologic oncology, interventional radiology, medical oncology, endocrinology, and palliative care teams throughout a prolonged and complex disease course.

Conclusion

In this patient with long-standing stage IV uLMS, comprehensive molecular profiling identified an ALK-DCTN1 fusion that enabled targeted treatment with ceritinib, resulting in significant and durable tumour regression after multiple prior locoregional and systemic therapies had failed or caused substantial toxicity. The case underscores the value of routine genomic profiling in advanced or recurrent uLMS and supports the use of ALK-directed therapy as a potentially meaningful treatment option for appropriately selected patients.

Patient perspective

The patient reported that prior to starting ceritinib, she experienced severe pelvic pain, frequent hospitalisations, and considerable anxiety related to disease progression and treatment complications. Since initiating ceritinib, she describes her pain as much better controlled, with the ability to resume many of her daily household activities. Although she remains cautious about her diet and attends regular follow-up appointments, she feels that the targeted therapy has given her “a second chance”, with substantially improved quality of life compared to earlier in her illness. Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report and accompanying images.
