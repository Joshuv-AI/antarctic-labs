Structure-aware acoustic scene classification: a feature decoupling framework using HPSS and asymmetric convolutions
Authors: Weijie Liu, Yanyan Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAddressing the computational efficiency and cross-device generalization challenges faced by acoustic scene classification in resource-constrained environments, this study proposes a structure-aware dual-stream feature disentanglement framework based on harmonic-percussive source separation and asymmetric convolutions. The framework achieves structured decomposition of acoustic signals through HPSS techniques, performs independent modeling targeting temporal-frequency dimension differentiated characteristics through cascaded asymmetric convolution kernels, and realizes adaptive feature fusion through a dual attention mechanism. Systematic validation on the DCASE 2020 and TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2022 datasets demonstrates that the proposed method significantly reduces parameter count and inference time while maintaining competitive classification accuracy, exhibiting superior robustness compared to Transformer-based methods in cross-device scenarios and consistent generalization capability under reduced input duration conditions. This study provides a solution balancing performance and efficiency for acoustic scene classification deployment on edge devices and mobile platforms, with the proposed structure-aware feature disentanglement concept offering a new perspective for enhancing the interpretability of deep learning models through leveraging physical prior knowledge in the audio signal processing domain.Keywords: Acoustic scene classification, Harmonic-percussive source separation, Feature disentanglement, Asymmetric convolution, Cross-device generalizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Acoustic scene classification is an essential component of environmental audio understanding that plays an important role in intelligent surveillance systems, robotic auditory perception, smart city development, etc. The rapid development of the DCASE challenge has forced the field to address the challenges of cross-device generalization and limited computing resources1. The application of acoustic scene classification on mobile devices requires the model to have strong robustness under limited memory and computing capacity2, while the difference in acoustic characteristics between devices makes it difficult to apply models trained on single devices to other devices3,4. The application of lightweight architecture has been shown to have potential in simplifying the architecture of acoustic scene classification models5,6, while the application of feature disentanglement methods in cross-city and cross-device scenarios has shown the significance of structured modeling methods7; however, how to improve the robustness of cross-device acoustic scene classification while maintaining classification accuracy is an urgent problem to be solved.

However, deep learning technologies offer diversified solutions for acoustic scene classification, which still suffer from the problem of balancing parameter efficiency with performance. Audio Spectrogram Transformer has shown superiority in global modeling, which can effectively extract long-range temporal-frequency relations8. Meanwhile, self-supervised pre-training strategies can improve the generalization capacity of the model with limited labeled data9. The multi-scale spectrogram modeling can effectively extract fine-grained textures and coarse-grained structures by using multi-scale patch embedding mechanisms10. In addition, dynamic token pruning and attention sparsification can significantly reduce the computational overhead with good performance11. Convolutional neural networks can adaptively highlight discriminative temporal-frequency regions by using temporal-frequency attention mechanisms12 . The combination of graph neural networks with pre-trained models can introduce structured representation methods13. The temporal-frequency domain joint scanning mechanisms can achieve the decoupling of spatial exploration and feature aggregation14. Meanwhile, adaptive temporal resolution learning can enable the model to adjust the windows according to the dynamic characteristics of each scene15. The Patchout mechanism has been introduced to reduce the computational cost of audio Transformers by stochastically removing input patches during training while maintaining classification performance16, and iterative audio pre-training with acoustic tokenizers has demonstrated effective transfer learning capability for downstream audio classification tasks17. However, although these methods show good performance in single-device scenarios, the characteristics of large parameter counts and high computational overhead limit their applications in real-world scenarios.

The intrinsic dual nature of harmonic and percussive components within audio signals creates a unique perspective for feature engineering, based on the inherent presence of both sustained tonal and transient impulsive components within acoustic environments. Harmonic percussive source separation (HPSS) enables an effective separation process based on the continuity of tonal components within the frequency axis and the locality of impulsive components within the time axis18, while phase-aware convex optimization techniques improve temporal frequency consistency constraints during the separation process19. However, existing HPSS approaches are largely geared towards music signal processing, while their suitability for environmental acoustic scene classification tasks remains largely untapped, particularly with regards to the absence of thorough analysis on the contribution of different acoustic components towards scene classification.

Lightweight network architecture design aims to achieve a balance between parameter efficiency and representation ability through architectural innovations, with asymmetric convolution being the main technical solution. Asymmetric convolution techniques rely on using different kernel configurations for different dimensions to exploit the anisotropic properties of audio signals in both the time domain and frequency domain to maintain the ability to capture patterns along each dimension while reducing the number of parameters20. Depthwise separable convolution significantly reduces multiply accumulate operations by decomposing regular convolution into depthwise convolution and pointwise convolution21, while diverse branch blocks are employed to extract multi-receptive field features within a single convolutional layer22. The combination of asymmetric convolution with depthwise separable convolution has been shown to achieve better parameter efficiency for agricultural pest classification tasks23, while lightweight network architectures designed for audio classification have been shown to achieve model compression using knowledge distillation and channel pruning techniques24. Analytical studies on various model compression techniques have shown the trade-offs between classification accuracy and speed for quantization, pruning, and low rank decomposition-based techniques for audio classification tasks25, while hardware-based neural architecture search techniques have been shown to incorporate latency and energy constraints into the search objectives for neural architecture search26. The synergy of multi-scale dilated convolution with depthwise separable convolution has been shown to achieve outstanding performance for sound event detection tasks27, while lightweight transformers have been applied to underwater acoustic target recognition tasks to highlight the computational benefits of depthwise separable convolution28. Asymmetric multi-task networks based on Kolmogorov-Arnold networks have introduced a new paradigm for multi-task knowledge sharing29.

Data augmentation and transfer learning methods have been significant contributors to the improvement of the models’ robustness. The data augmentation methods for the spectrogram simulate the partial occlusion effect, which is characteristic of real-world environments. The random masking approach has been useful in overcoming the issue of overfitting30. Global embeddings, which have been pre-trained on large bioacoustic datasets, have provided universal features that have been useful for transfer learning across domains31. The application of end-to-end residual networks has provided the ability for fast adaptation to medical audio screening applications32. The use of mobile audio Transformers for the estimation of the acoustic parameters of a room environment, as part of the blind room acoustic parameter estimation, has provided the ability for the application of self-attention models for unsupervised scene understanding33. The systematic review of the visual representation of the temporal-frequency domain and the features of textures has provided a comprehensive landscape of the techniques used for audio applications34.

It is worth noting that the challenges of time–frequency feature extraction and robust classification under complex conditions extend beyond acoustic scene classification to related signal processing domains, where analogous architectural innovations have demonstrated significant effectiveness. Time–frequency aware hierarchical feature optimization has been applied to SAR jamming recognition through structured multi-scale feature extraction35, and semantic graph reasoning over multi-domain fusion has demonstrated the benefits of integrating structural representations from heterogeneous feature domains for radar jamming recognition36. In the domain of automatic modulation recognition, multi-level feature distribution learning has addressed the open-set recognition challenge through hierarchical feature space modeling37, while local–global feature space constraint networks have achieved robust open-set performance by jointly capturing fine-grained and holistic feature representations38. The scalable subspace approach with similarity completion has further advanced the class incremental recognition paradigm through efficient feature space expansion mechanisms39. These developments in related signal classification tasks underscore the universal importance of structured feature extraction and adaptive classification strategies across time–frequency analysis domains, providing additional motivation for the structure-aware approach adopted in the current investigation.

However, despite the achievements of existing studies on acoustic scene classification from various aspects, there are still three major gaps in existing studies that need to be addressed: Deep learning-based methods typically regard audio spectrograms as general images for processing, ignoring the harmonic-percussive dual structure characteristics of audio signals, which leads to the lack of specificity and explainability of the extracted features. The advantage of transformer-based methods in global modeling capability cannot be fully utilized due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention mechanisms and the large number of model parameters, which makes it difficult to meet the requirements of edge deployment with strict real-time constraints. The enhancement of cross-device generalization capability is typically realized by increasing model capacity or using complicated domain adaptation mechanisms, which contradicts the goal of designing lightweight models and is not practical in real-world applications.

It is important to note that while existing dual-stream architectures for acoustic scene classification have explored parallel feature extraction from different input representations, these approaches typically employ symmetric convolutional kernels across both streams and rely on simple concatenation for feature fusion without leveraging the physical structural priors inherent in audio signals. The HPSS technique, although well-established in music information retrieval, has not been systematically integrated into a deep learning-based dual-stream framework for acoustic scene classification with component-aware asymmetric convolution and attention-based disentanglement fusion, leaving the potential of combining structured signal decomposition with differentiated deep feature learning largely unexplored.

In this study, a structure-aware dual-stream feature disentanglement framework is proposed, which can be divided into three parts: audio signals can be divided into two parts through harmonic-percussive source separation; asymmetric convolutional neural networks can be designed for differential modeling of the temporal-frequency characteristics of different audio components; and dual-stream features can be adaptively combined through disentanglement fusion mechanisms. Systematic experiments on the DCASE 2020 and TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2022 benchmark datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in both single-device and cross-device scenarios as well as under reduced input duration conditions.

Methodology

Overall framework design

The acoustic scene classification task aims to map input audio signals to a predefined set of scene categories . The proposed structure-aware dual-stream feature disentanglement framework achieves targeted feature extraction by fully exploiting the harmonic-percussive dual structure characteristics of audio signals, with the core innovation lying in combining structured signal decomposition with deep neural network representation learning to capture complementary discriminative information from different acoustic components through a dual-stream parallel processing mechanism.

The framework adopts Mel-spectrogram as the input representation, converting raw audio into a time–frequency representation through short-time Fourier transform, where 128 denotes the number of Mel filter banks and represents the number of time frames. The harmonic-percussive source separation module decomposes the time–frequency representation into harmonic component and percussive component , preserving sustained tonal features and transient impulsive features respectively. The dual-stream asymmetric convolutional neural network performs independent feature extraction on the two complementary components, where the harmonic stream and percussive stream adopt identical network structures but maintain independent parameters, enabling each stream to optimize convolution kernel configurations tailored to the temporal-frequency characteristics of its corresponding acoustic component. The feature disentanglement and fusion module adaptively integrates dual-stream features through a dual attention mechanism, modeling the contribution of different feature channels in the channel dimension and locating discriminative regions on the time–frequency plane in the spatial dimension. HPSS-based dual-stream feature extraction

The harmonic percussive source separation techniques are based on prior knowledge of the audio signals within the time–frequency domain. For instance, the harmonic components are represented by horizontal bands along the frequency axis, while the percussive components are represented by vertical bands along the temporal axis. In this investigation, the median filtering-based harmonic percussive source separation technique has been employed to separate the structural components of the signals within the spectrogram domain using median filters of size 17 along the frequency axis and the temporal axis, respectively. The process of extracting the harmonic components preserves the energy components that are continuously distributed along the frequency axis, while the process of extracting the percussive components preserves the energy components that are locally concentrated along the temporal axis.

The separated harmonic and percussive components are converted into Mel-spectrogram representations as inputs to the dual-stream network. This harmonic component shows strong discriminative ability for acoustic scenes where there are musical background sounds and/or mechanical steady-state noise. Conversely, the percussive component of the signal focuses on the temporal localization and intensity characteristics of impulsive information, showing stronger discriminative ability for acoustic scenes with sudden acoustic events. This component-level distinction provides structured input for the subsequent differentiated modeling of the dual-stream network, where the varying proportions of harmonic and percussive components of different acoustic scenes allow the dual-stream features to show contrasting discriminative ability. To demonstrate the effectiveness of HPSS-based separation for different acoustic environments, Fig. 2 presents the HPSS-based separation for three acoustic scenes chosen from the DCASE 2020 dataset.

In a street traffic scene, there are significant levels of percussive energy across the entire frequency range, corresponding to braking, horns, and accelerating sounds. These patterns substantiate the dual role of discriminative features and provide a physical basis for architectural design.

Asymmetric convolutional neural network

The design of asymmetric convolutional neural networks incorporates the specific physical semantics of audio spectrograms along the temporal and frequency axes. Along the frequency axis, where the specific physical semantics of the structure of acoustic signals are represented, there are strong correlations between adjacent frequency components due to formant and harmonic effects. Along the temporal axis, where the specific physical semantics of the temporal evolution of acoustic events are represented, the correlations between adjacent time frames are affected by the duration of the events. The asymmetric convolution module developed in the current study employs a cascaded structure of 3 × 5 and 5 × 3 convolutional kernels. This structure extends the receptive fields along the frequency and temporal axes, respectively. The 3 × 5 convolution, with its extended frequency-axis receptive field spanning five adjacent Mel bins, is specifically designed to capture cross-frequency correlations arising from harmonic overtone series where fundamental frequencies and their integer multiples occupy predictable spectral positions, while the 5 × 3 convolution, with its extended temporal receptive field spanning five consecutive frames, captures the onset-sustain-decay dynamics of acoustic events that unfold over multiple time steps. This cascaded configuration ensures that each convolution operates along the dimension where the strongest structural correlations exist for its corresponding acoustic component, rather than applying a uniform square kernel that allocates equal modeling capacity to both dimensions regardless of the underlying signal characteristics, a design principle that is empirically validated by the ablation results in Table 1 where the asymmetric 3 × 5 and 5 × 3 configuration outperforms both the 3 × 3 and 5 × 5 symmetric alternatives.

The harmonic and percussive streams of the dual-stream network architecture make use of the same network structure while performing independent parameter optimization. Each stream is formed of a series of four blocks of asymmetric convolution. Each of the blocks consists of a cascaded structure of 3 × 5 convolution followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation, and then 5 × 3 convolution followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation. The feature map channel count progressively increases from an initial 64 channels to 512 channels across network depth, with global average pooling layers compressing spatial dimension feature maps into vector representations . The main advantage of the dual-stream network is that it can learn convolutional filters based on frequency continuity features as well as temporal transient features through independent parameter training of the harmonic and percussive streams, respectively, leading to improved discriminative feature learning compared to other network architectures.

Feature disentanglement and fusion

The feature disentanglement and fusion module achieves adaptive feature integration through a dual attention mechanism. The channel attention mechanism captures the statistical distribution of feature maps through global average pooling and global max pooling, generating channel dimension attention weights through a shared multilayer perceptron, with this mechanism highlighting feature channels with high contribution to scene discrimination while suppressing redundant channel influence, drawing inspiration from Gaussian-weighted self-attention in speech enhancement tasks40. The spatial attention mechanism focuses on locally salient areas in the time–frequency domain of the feature map. Spatial-dimensional attention weights are learned using 7 × 7 convolutional layers after applying pooling in the channel dimension. This helps to focus more on discriminative time–frequency areas, which is analogous to dense convolutional neural networks that incorporate self-attention mechanisms for handling long-range dependencies in time-domain speech enhancement41. The application of channel and time–frequency attention has been shown to result in significant performance improvements in monaural speech enhancement42, with recent advances further demonstrating the effectiveness of triple attention mechanisms combined with temporal convolutional architectures for real-time speech enhancement43, complex dual-path multi-axial transformers with frequency prompting for joint spectral-temporal modeling44, and a comprehensive survey consolidating the evolution of deep learning-based speech enhancement techniques45, collectively supporting the extension of attention-based feature integration to acoustic scene classification in the proposed framework.

Feature disentanglement mechanisms have been proven to improve the performance of models in different domains. This is evident in the application of decoupling methods in deep neural networks for multi-agent collaborative inference, demonstrating the importance of decoupling in achieving efficient feature representation in models46. The application of robustly optimized deep feature decoupling networks in fatty liver detection also achieves more reliable diagnostic results through the decoupling of features from influencing factors in the detection process47. The decoupling of feature extraction and classification layers in neural network calibration also improves the quality of confidence estimation in the model48. The success of deep learning decoupling in improving the interpretability of image recognition also underscores the importance of decoupling in machine learning models in general49.

The classifier adopts a two-layer fully connected network configuration with an intermediate layer dimension of 256 and Dropout regularization (dropout rate = 0.5), with the output layer mapping to category probability distributions through Softmax. Training employs the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 0.001), applying SpecAugment data augmentation strategies with random masking in the time–frequency domain (time mask width 40 frames, frequency mask width 8 bins, 2 masks each) to enhance model robustness. The entire framework achieves joint optimization through end-to-end backpropagation, enabling the HPSS separation, dual-stream feature extraction, and disentanglement fusion modules to collaboratively learn optimal parameter configurations oriented toward scene classification tasks.

Results

Experimental setup

This study conducts systematic experimental validation using the TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2020 Mobile dataset (https://zenodo.org/record/3819968)50. The dataset comprises 10 urban acoustic scene categories, encompassing Airport, Bus, Metro, Metro station, Park, Public square, Shopping mall, Street pedestrian, Street traffic, and Tram, with the training set containing 14,400 ten-second audio clips (1440 per class) and the test set containing 4185 audio clips. A distinctive feature of this dataset lies in its utilization of three different recording devices (Device A as the primary device, Devices B and C for cross-device generalization testing) with audio sampled at 44.1 kHz, with this multi-device configuration providing rigorous testing conditions for evaluating the practical deployment robustness of models.

Model performance is quantitatively assessed through three evaluation metrics, where classification accuracy reflects overall scene recognition capability, macro-average F1 score measures the balanced performance of the model across different categories, and Log Loss serves as the DCASE official evaluation metric to reflect the quality of predicted probability distributions. To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, nine representative baseline methods are selected for comparison, including three classical methods comprising the DCASE 2020 official Baseline (based on standard CNN architecture), ResNet-18 (deep residual network), and VGGish (Google pre-trained audio classification model), along with six recent advanced methods including Audio Spectrogram Transformer, EfficientAT, Self-Supervised AST, Temporal-Frequency Attention CNN, PaSST, and BEATs, with these baseline methods covering different technical approaches such as convolutional neural networks, standard and lightweight Transformer architectures, and attention mechanisms. The experimental implementation is based on the PyTorch 2.0 framework, employing the Adam optimizer (learning rate 0.001, weight decay 1e-4), with batch size set to 32 and training conducted for 100 epochs with early stopping strategy to prevent overfitting. Data augmentation strategies include SpecAugment (time mask width 40 frames, frequency mask width 8 bins, 2 masks each) and MixUp to enhance model robustness, with all experiments performed on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU to ensure computational efficiency and result reproducibility.

Ablation study

In order to evaluate each of these components of the structure-aware two-stream feature decoupling framework, this study proposes three groups of ablation experiments to verify the effectiveness of HPSS separation, asymmetric convolution, and feature decoupling mechanisms, respectively. The rationale for conducting ablation experiments to verify the effectiveness of HPSS separation is based on the fundamental hypothesis of this study, that is, the discriminative ability of both harmony and percussion features for different acoustic scenes is complementary. This group of ablation experiments is used to evaluate the necessity of adopting two-stream architecture, and four different configurations are used to compare each other, that is, one-stream architecture based on raw audio, one-stream architecture based on harmony features, one-stream architecture based on percussion features, and two-stream architecture based on both harmony and percussion features. The rationale for conducting ablation experiments to evaluate asymmetric convolution is to explore the benefits of different modeling strategies for different components within the time–frequency domain. This group of ablation experiments compares the effectiveness of different configurations of convolutional kernels, that is, conventional 3 × 3 symmetric convolution, large 5 × 5 symmetric convolution, and asymmetric 3 × 5 and 5 × 3 cascaded convolution, and seeks to evaluate the trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness of asymmetric convolution while fixing other components of the architecture. The rationale for conducting ablation experiments to evaluate feature decoupling is to quantify the contribution of adopting an attention mechanism for feature integration. Four different strategies, that is, simple splicing, channel attention, space attention, and dual attention, are used to compare each other, and all ablation experiments are conducted on device A to keep all factors unchanged, and Table 1 summarizes all ablation study results.

The ablation results presented in Table 1 quantify the individual contributions of the three core components constituting the proposed framework. The HPSS dual-stream architecture demonstrates the most substantial performance gain of 2.7 percentage points over the single-stream baseline using original audio, with Log Loss reduced from 1.06 to 0.93, while the individual harmonic stream at 68.2% and percussive stream at 67.5% both fall below the original audio baseline of 69.4%, confirming that neither component alone captures the full discriminative information and that their complementary fusion is essential for recovering and exceeding the baseline performance. The asymmetric convolution configuration contributes an additional improvement of 1.7 percentage points over the standard 3 × 3 symmetric convolution and 1.2 percentage points over the 5 × 5 symmetric alternative, with the F1-score correspondingly increasing from 68.9% and 69.4% to 70.6%. The dual attention disentanglement mechanism yields a gain of 1.8 percentage points over simple concatenation, with channel attention alone at 71.2% and spatial attention alone at 70.8% each contributing partially, while their combination achieves 72.1% with a Log Loss of 0.93 that surpasses both individual variants.

The quantified contributions reveal a hierarchical importance ordering where HPSS-based structured decomposition provides the foundational performance gain, asymmetric convolution refines the feature extraction efficiency along differentiated time–frequency dimensions, and the dual attention mechanism further optimizes the feature space through synergistic channel-spatial interactions where channel attention selectively emphasizes discriminative feature channels and spatial attention focuses on salient time–frequency regions that distinguish acoustically similar scenes.

Cross-device generalization performance

The capability of cross-device generalization acts as a vital measure for evaluating the robustness of acoustic scene classification systems. The rationale for using this experimental methodology is rooted in the highly challenging requirements set by the DCASE challenge, which calls for high performance in model transfer from one recording device to another. The fourth configuration represents a mixed training scenario, which trains on three devices and tests on each individual device, thereby evaluating the performance of the model with respect to joint training on multiple devices. Discussion

The proposed framework of structure-aware dual-stream feature disentanglement has been shown to attain an accuracy of 72.1%, which is an increase of 6.8 percentage points over the DCASE 2020 Baseline of 65.3%. The relative contributions of the different components of the framework have been made evident through ablation studies, where the HPSS dual-stream architecture has been seen to contribute to an increase of 2.7 percentage points over the baseline, with asymmetric convolution and dual attention disentanglement showing significant contributions to the performance of the framework. The effectiveness of different strategies of feature disentanglement has been evident in different domains as well. For example, in the context of domain adaptation research, iterative resampling decoupling has been used to attain domain transfer with class imbalance52, while in the context of domain generalization networks, robustness under unknown conditions has been attained53, with the proposed framework being able to attain more interpretable component-level disentanglement with the help of acoustic physical priors. Decoupling techniques applied to long-tail classification have similarly demonstrated the effectiveness of separating representation learning from classifier training to mitigate class imbalance54 , further corroborating the general principle that structured decomposition of the learning process yields performance benefits across diverse task configurations.

In the horizontal comparison using advanced techniques, the unique benefits of the proposed method are clearly illustrated through the efficiency-performance trade-off. For instance, the proposed method has an accuracy of 72.1%, slightly lower than that of the Audio Spectrogram Transformer at 73.1%, but with significantly fewer parameters at 12.4 million compared to 86 million, and an inference time of 24.1 ms instead of 68.3 ms. For cross-device scenarios, the proposed method has lower performance degradation of 5.4% and 6.1% when transferring from Device A to Device B and Device C, respectively, compared to Audio Spectrogram Transformer at 7.0% and 7.8%, and ResNet-18 at 6.5% and 7.2%, PaSST at 6.9% and 7.6%, and BEATs at 6.7% and 7.5%. The lower degradation is attributed to the effectiveness of the proposed method in capturing recording device-agnostic acoustic structural characteristics using the HPSS-based spectrogram separation technique.

A deeper theoretical examination of this robustness advantage reveals that the spectral distortions introduced by different recording devices predominantly manifest as broadband frequency response variations, encompassing microphone sensitivity differences, analog-to-digital converter nonlinearities, and device-specific noise floor characteristics, all of which alter the overall spectral envelope of the recorded signal in a manner that is approximately multiplicative in the frequency domain. The median filtering operation employed in the HPSS process is largely robust to such multiplicative spectral distortions, as it extracts structural patterns based on the relative continuity or locality of spectral energy distribution rather than on absolute energy magnitudes, meaning that the harmonic and percussive components retain their fundamental structural characteristics even when the underlying spectral envelope is systematically shifted by device-specific transfer functions. The harmonic component, which captures sustained tonal patterns such as engine noise and ventilation hum, reflects intrinsic inter-frequency relationships that are governed by the physical properties of the sound sources rather than by the recording hardware, while the percussive component, which captures transient impulsive events such as footsteps and braking sounds, encodes temporal onset and offset dynamics that are determined by the acoustic event characteristics rather than by the device frequency response. This decomposition therefore functions as an implicit domain normalization mechanism that projects the device-dependent raw spectrogram into a pair of structurally regularized representations where the inter-device distributional discrepancy is reduced, enabling the dual-stream network to learn classification-relevant features from a feature space with lower domain shift. The independent parameter optimization within the dual-stream architecture further amplifies this effect, as each stream develops specialized convolutional filters attuned to the higher-order structural invariances along the frequency and temporal axes respectively, rather than to the device-sensitive absolute spectral characteristics that degrade the generalization capacity of methods operating on the undecomposed spectrogram.

The combined spectrogram representations with deep convolutional neural networks have been proven to be effective in feature extraction for audio spoof detection tasks55,56, while the optimized features have shown higher discriminability using multi-level iterative engineering in deepfake audio detection tasks57,58. The asymmetric convolution operation helps to improve the efficiency of the model through the differentiation characteristics of spectrograms along the temporal-frequency dimensions. The two-stage architecture has shown its effectiveness in achieving modularity flexibility for soundscape classification tasks59, while the proposed method has shown its effectiveness in achieving end-to-end optimization for joint learning of both separation and classification.

The analysis of the confusion matrix shows that the discriminability of the proposed method differs for different scene categories. It is worth noting that the proposed method achieves high accuracy, 79.0%, 76.0%, and 77.0%, for Airport, Park, and Tram, respectively, while bidirectional confusion of 12.0% occurs between Metro and Metro Station, and confusion from 9.0 to 11.0% between Street pedestrian and Street traffic. The confusion occurs due to the nature of the acoustic components of similar scenes. Moreover, the proposed method demonstrates the dependence of the quality of Harmonic/Percussive/Source Separation (HPSS) and the selection of the window size of the median filter, which affects the quality of the separation depending on the acoustic environment. The ability of the proposed method to process extremely short audio segments requires further research.

Conclusion

This study aims to address the efficiency of computations and the challenges of cross-device generalizations in acoustic scene classification under resource-constrained environments through the proposal of a novel framework called “structure-aware dual-stream feature disentanglement,” which incorporates harmonic percussive source separation (HPSS) and asymmetric convolutions. The proposed framework performs structured signal decomposition using HPSS separation methods, models each stream separately to focus on the temporal-frequency differentiated characteristics of the acoustic signal using cascaded asymmetric convolutional neural networks with 3 × 5 and 5 × 3 filters, and finally performs adaptive fusion of features with the aid of the dual attention mechanism. The effectiveness of the proposed framework was evaluated through rigorous experiments on the DCASE 2020 dataset, achieving an accuracy of 72.1%, Log Loss of 0.93, which represents an improvement of 6.8 percentage points over the DCASE 2020 Baseline. The proposed framework has 12.4 M parameters and an inference time of 24.1 ms, which is much less compared to other methods like the Transformer architecture. In terms of cross-device environments, the proposed framework showed performance degradations of 5.4 and 6.1 percentage points when the model was transferred from Device A to Devices B and C, respectively, which are better compared to the Audio Spectrogram Transformer and ResNet-18 models.

The study presented in this manuscript offers a solution that strikes a balance between performance and efficiency, specifically for acoustic scene classification on edge devices and mobile devices. The proposed solution also offers a structure-aware feature disentanglement concept, which can provide a new avenue for enhancing the interpretability of deep learning-based models by using prior knowledge from the physical world, specifically in the audio signal processing domain. The generalizability of the proposed framework has been validated on both the DCASE 2020 and TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2022 datasets, and extending this evaluation to acoustic scene classification benchmarks with different category taxonomies remains a valuable direction for future investigation. The adaptive tuning of HPSS parameters, particularly the median filter size that governs the trade-off between harmonic and percussive separation quality, represents a promising avenue for improving the effectiveness of structural decomposition across diverse acoustic environments. The integration of model compression techniques such as knowledge distillation from the dual-stream teacher network to a single-stream student network and post-training quantization for integer-arithmetic deployment can further enhance real-time inference performance on highly resource-constrained edge platforms.
