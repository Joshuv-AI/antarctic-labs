The Metallic Fuels Irradiation and Physics Database (FIPD) is an organized collection of legacy
metallic fuel U(Pu)Zr data, measurements, and reactor conditions from the Experimental Breeder
Reactor II (EBR-II) and the Fast Flux Test Facility (FFTF). The database provides a wealth of
information and is easily accessed and utilized by the U.S. nuclear industry and the Nuclear
Regulatory Commission (NRC). The database contains three categories of data. The first category
covers fuel pin fabrication specifications, including fuel slug diameter and length, cladding
diameter, smeared density, etc. The second consists of operational conditions, which include axial
distributions of power, temperature, fluence, burnup, isotopic density, etc. The third category
contains post-irradiation examination (PIE) results, largely collected at facilities now part of the
Idaho National Laboratory (INL), formerly part of Argonne National Laboratory -West (ANL-W)
and at the Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility (AGHCF) at Argonne National Laboratory, comprising
fission gas release and chemistry analysis, profilometry measurements, neutron radiography data,
etc. All these data have been organized and preserved in FIPD.

In addition to the FIPD, which stores steady-state irradiation data, several other key databases
support the evaluation of metallic fuel performance. These include: (1) the Out-of-Pile Transient
Database (OPTD), containing records of out-of-pile furnace transient tests conducted on metallic
fuel samples at the Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility (AGHCF) at Argonne National Laboratory;
and (2) the TREAT Experimental Relational Database (TREXR), containing data and records from
hundreds of experiments for various fast reactor fuels, including metallic and other fuel forms,
conducted on nuclear reactor fuels, including metallic and other fuel forms, at the Transient
Reactor Test (TREAT) facility since 1960. Together, these databases form a comprehensive
knowledge base essential for the qualification of metallic fuel.

In order to manage the large amount of data and associated documents and to qualify the data, a
Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP) was established for the legacy metallic fuel data
following the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Nuclear Quality Assurance
(NQA-1) 2008/2009 Addenda Standard. Metallic fuel data qualification is being performed
following the QAPP. The plan has been accepted by the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission and
can be referenced in licensing applications for nuclear power plants. With the plan, a significant
amount of metallic fuel data has been qualified and provided to the U.S. nuclear industry to support
licensing activities.

 1. Metallic Fuels Irradiation and Physics Database (FIPD)

The Metallic Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD) [1-5] is a structured repository of
legacy U(Pu)Zr metallic fuel data, containing experimental measurements, reactor operating
conditions, and other documents relevant to reactor and fuel performance. These records
primarily originate from the Experimental Breeder Reactor II (EBR-II) and the Fast Flux Test
Facility (FFTF). During the Integral Fast Reactor (IFR) program, numerous significant metallic
fuel experiments were conducted in these two fast reactors and includes data from experiments
designed to qualify metallic driver fuel for use in these reactors. The resulting data were
systematically collected, organized, and archived within the FIPD. While the majority of the data
were produced during the IFR program [6], the database also includes post-irradiation
examination (PIE) data generated after the program's conclusion [7, 8], covering EBR-II and
FFTF-irradiated fuel pins.

The database is organized into three main categories of data:
 1. Fuel Pin Fabrication Data – This includes detailed parameters such as fuel compositions,
 cladding type, U-235 enrichment and isotope information, fuel slug diameter and length,
 cladding dimensions, smeared density, and related manufacturing data. Both nominal
 design specifications and measured fabrication data are included.
 2. Operational Conditions – This category captures in-reactor performance data, including
 axial distributions of power, temperature, neutron fluence, burnup, and isotopic densities,
 determined using Argonne's suite of physics and thermal hydraulics codes. These codes,
 such as REBUS [9], EBRFLOW [10], RCT [11], and SUPERENERGY-II [12, 13], have
 historically been applied to EBR-II simulations.
 3. Post-Irradiation Examination (PIE) Results – These data were primarily collected at
 facilities now part of the Idaho National Laboratory (INL), including the former Argonne
 National Laboratory-West (ANL-W), as well as the Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility
 (AGHCF) at Argonne National Laboratory. This category includes measurements and
 analyses such as fission gas release, fuel and cladding chemistry, profilometry, and neutron
 radiography.

FIPD includes a comprehensive library of documents related to various fuel pins and
experiments. This collection includes original (raw) post-irradiation examination (PIE) data,
design specifications, safety analyses, and operational reports. The EBR-II fuel pin data stored in
the FIPD represents a wide range of materials and operating conditions that address the needs of
the nuclear industry [14, 15] and other stakeholders such as national laboratories and universities
for research and development purposes.

The database includes EBR-II fuel pin compositions such as U-xPu-10Zr (with x ranging from 0
to 28 wt%) and U-xZr alloys (with x ranging from 0 to 10 wt%), paired with different cladding
materials: 316SS, D9, and HT9. Smeared density values range from 58% to 90%, with plenum-
to-fuel volume ratios between 1 and 3. Fuel diameters range from 0.168 to 0.313 inches (0.427–
0.795 cm). Operating conditions span peak linear powers of 10 to 19 kW/ft (33–62 kW/m), peak
burnup levels from 0.5 to 20 at%, and peak cladding temperatures from 490 to 660 °C.

FIPD also includes full-length metallic fuel pin PIE data from the FFTF IFR-1 experiment [16,
17] and the MFF series experiments [18, 19], along with the corresponding irradiation conditions
[1]. In addition, a new section titled "Reactor-Level Documents" has been added to the FIPD.
This section provides comprehensive resources related to EBR-II operations, fuel fabrication
processes, and quality assurance documentation. Unlike the original data library, these materials
pertain to reactor-level activities and are not tied to specific fuel pins, subassemblies, or
experiments.

 2. Out-of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD)

The Out-of-Pile Transient Database (OPTD) is a structured archive that contains records of a
series of out-of-pile furnace transient tests [20, 21] performed on metallic fuel samples at the
Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility (AGHCF) at Argonne National Laboratory. These tests were
conducted using two types of furnace systems: the Whole Pin Furnace (WPF) [22] for thermally
testing full irradiated fuel pins, and the Fuel Behavior Test Apparatus (FBTA) for thermally
testing irradiated fuel pin segments [22-24].

Before testing, the samples underwent pre-test examinations to characterize their as-irradiated
fuel and cladding structures. Each test began by installing a sample in the furnace and ramping
up the temperature slowly to a pre-determined value. The transient sequence then commenced,
increasing the temperature at a controlled rate to a designated peak target. The sample was held
at this peak temperature for a specified duration or until failure occurred (in WPF tests). The test
concluded with a furnace scram—either after the full hold time elapsed or if pin failure was
detected via a pressure spike—and the sample was allowed to cool through convective heat loss.
Following each test, data were recorded and post-test examinations were conducted.

The FBTA was used to test over 140 irradiated fuel pin segments, mainly extracted from EBR-II
fuel pins (and one from FFTF), at constant temperatures between 625 and 850 °C. Test durations
ranged from a few minutes up to 36 hours, with most lasting about one hour. The tested
segments included binary and ternary fuels (U-xPu-10Zr, x = 0, 19, & 26) with HT9, D9, or 316
stainless steel cladding. Peak burnups ranged from 2.3 to 17 at%. Some tests also examined
ternary fuels with varying zirconium content (U-19Pu-yZr, y = 6, 10, & 14). The FBTA results
helped define the time- and temperature-dependence of eutectic cladding penetration and
established rough threshold temperatures for fuel liquefaction.

In contrast, WPF tests tested intact EBR-II irradiated fuel pins to study the combined effects of
fission gas pressure and fuel-cladding chemical interactions. Seven WPF tests were conducted to
identify failure modes, determine safety margins and failure thresholds, and investigate fuel
motion within the pin [21].

OPTD includes comprehensive records (test summaries, experimenter notes, and post-test
metallographic examinations) related to the design, planning, and execution of each test.
Reference [21]. provides an overview of the out-of-pile furnace tests, detailing the tested fuel
types, experimental conditions, test objectives, and key findings.

 3. TREAT Experimental Relational Database (TREXR)

The TREAT Experimental Relational Database (TREXR) is a structured archive that contains
records of nuclear fuel experiments conducted at the Transient Reactor Test (TREAT) facility
from 1960 to 1994. These experiments were designed to study the behavior of nuclear fuel
samples under extreme conditions resembling those encountered during reactor accidents. The
TREAT reactor was built and operated to replicate such challenging transient scenarios [25].

Within the TREXR database, particular attention is given to seven M-series tests (M1 through
M7) conducted between 1985 and 1987. These experiments focused on investigating in-pin fuel
motion in advanced sodium-bonded metallic fuel designs for fast reactors under severe transient
overpower conditions. Tests M1 through M4 examined irradiated 316 stainless steel-clad,
sodium-bonded U-5Fs fuel. Test M1 used fuel with a burnup of 3.5 at%, while tests M2 through
M4 used fuel with burnups ranging from 0.3 to 7.9 at%. Tests M5 through M7 evaluated five
D9-clad U-19Pu-10Zr fuel pins with burnups ranging from 0 to 9.8 at% and one HT9-clad U-
10Zr fuel pin with a burnup of 2.9 at%. All fuel pins had been previously irradiated in EBR-II.

In Test M1, steady-state power was applied without flowing coolant [25]. In contrast, Tests M2
through M7 employed an 8-second exponential power ramp starting from approximately nominal
fast reactor power, with the power terminated at or shortly after incipient cladding failure. These
six tests utilized flowing sodium coolant. Key objectives included evaluating the effects of fuel
pin plenum pressure, retained fission gas, and sodium logging within the fuel.

The TREXR database includes documentation on test design and operation as well as measured
instrumentation data for several experiments. Users can view and save reports and records, plot
instrument data in real time, and download datasets. A summary report covering selected
historical TREAT experiments is also available [25].

 4. Data Qualification
The legacy metallic fuel data qualification activities started in 2015 with support from the U.S.
Department of Energy (DOE) Advanced Reactor Technologies (ART) licensing area, followed by
support from Microreactor Program from 2018-2023 and the Fast Reactor Program (FRP) from
2023 to present, with additional support from NE-4 Advanced Fuels Campaign (AFC) in FY25.
The essential document for the data qualification activities is the Quality Assurance Program Plan
(QAPP) [26], which adheres to the ASME NQA-1 2008/2009 Addenda standard. The QAPP acts
as the principal policy document, defining the approach to validate quality for the SFR legacy
metallic fuel data [27]. The document was developed and submitted to the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory
Commission (NRC) for review in 2019. The Quality Assurance and Vendor Inspection Branch at
the NRC has conducted a thorough evaluation and confirmed that the ANL's QAPP meets the QA
specifications outlined in Appendix B of Title 10 in the Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR)
Part 50 [28] in 2020.

Among the methods outlined in the QAPP, the peer review approach has been selected for the
independent evaluation of data. This method evaluates whether the originally applied QA
methodology is acceptable, whether confidence in the data acquisition or developmental results
is justified, and whether the data have been used in similar applications [27]. One or more

 4

subject matter experts (SMEs), with specialization in fuels and materials, are assigned to perform
the data review. Their assessment is conducted according to the criteria defined in the NQA-1
standards, focusing on the data's acceptability.

The data quality assurance (QA) process, carried out by the SME(s), begins with identifying the
specific data to be qualified. The SME(s) then obtain the original (raw) data from the database.
These data may be available in various formats, including scanned hard copies (PDFs) or digital
files. After acquiring the data, the SME(s) compile all relevant documentation associated with
the experiment and/or measurement practices to support the data qualification process. This
supporting material may include data specifications, operating manuals and procedures,
calibration records, technical reports, published articles, laboratory memos, and similar records.

Next, the SME(s) review the collected raw data alongside the supporting documents, using the
data specification as a guide [29-34], to assess the data’s accuracy, relevance, and completeness.
During this review, the SME(s) document their findings in a Data Qualification Report (DQR),
noting any issues encountered, such as inconsistencies, omissions, or inaccuracies [15]. They
may also define the valid use range for the data, particularly when only part of the dataset meets
the qualification standards described in the report [5, 14, 15, 35].

The DQR is then subjected to the review and approval process outlined in the QA plan to ensure
the conclusions are accurate, relevant, and well-structured. Revisions, updates, or clarifications
may be suggested to improve the final version of the report. After incorporating any review
feedback, the finalized report is submitted to the official documents and records repository [5].

In addition to the established data qualification process, selected qualified data packages undergo
an external third-party peer review to provide an added layer of scrutiny. This additional step
enhances the overall quality, objectivity, and reliability of the data by enabling a more
independent and thorough evaluation. It also helps identify any potential gaps or areas for
improvement that may have been overlooked during the initial review. Incorporating this
external review reflects a strong commitment to maintaining high standards of accuracy and
rigor throughout the data qualification process.

Qualified metallic fuel data are available for direct download from the FIPD and OPTD
databases. For access to the complete qualified data packages, users may submit a formal request
to the ART FRP Database team [36].

Through the implementation of the QAPP, extensive data qualification efforts have been carried
out. In 2021, following the QAPP’s acceptance, the NRC conducted an audit of its implementation.
The audit concluded that the QAPP, as applied, provides adequate quality program controls for
key legacy data intended for use by applicants [37]. To date, more than 150 data packages have
been qualified—some containing data for up to 61 fuel pins, including post-irradiation examination
(PIE), operating conditions, or fuel fabrication details. These qualified data packages have been
delivered to the U.S. nuclear industry to support licensing activities. Notable examples include:
(1) Oklo’s 2018 licensing submittal, supported through the DOE NE GAIN voucher; and (2)
TerraPower’s Natrium project, ongoing since 2011, supported through the DOE ARDP program.

 5. Summary

This paper presents an overview of several structured databases developed to preserve and
organize legacy metallic fuel data for fast reactor applications, supporting ongoing research,
development, and licensing activities. The FIPD serves as a comprehensive archive of U(Pu)Zr
fuel data, including fuel fabrication details, reactor operating conditions, and PIE results from the
EBR-II and the FFTF. It includes a wide range of fuel compositions, cladding types, and
irradiation conditions, along with supporting reactor-level documentation.

The OPTD complements the FIPD by capturing results from furnace-based thermal transient
tests conducted at the AGHCF on irradiated fuel samples from EBR-II. These tests explored
failure mechanisms and fuel-cladding chemical interactions under elevated temperatures.

The TREXR database documents experimental campaigns at the TREAT facility designed to
simulate reactor transient scenarios. This includes the M-series tests, which investigated fuel
behavior under severe overpower conditions using irradiated metallic fuel pins.

To ensure the usability of this legacy data for modern reactor design and licensing, a formal data
qualification process was established under the NRC accepted QAPP. This effort has resulted in
the qualification of over 150 data packages, many of which have supported licensing applications
for advanced reactor developers such as Oklo and TerraPower.
