Monodromy groups of polynomials of composition length 2 2
Angelot Behajaina, Joachim König, Danny Neftin

Abstract
. We study the monodromy groups of compositions of two indecomposable polynomials. In particular, we show that such monodromy groups either fulfill a certain “largeness” property, or are in an explicit list of exceptions. Such largeness results are crucial for dealing with compositions of more than two polynomials, and consequently are expected to have a wide range of applications to problems concerning the arithmetic of polynomials. Concretely, our main result is a key ingredient in the solution of a long-standing open problem due to Davenport, Lewis and Schinzel, achieved in the companion paper [ 3 ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
. The
(arithmetic) monodromy group
of
f\in k[X]\setminus k
over
k
, denoted by
{\rm Mon}_{k}(f)
(or simply
\Mon(f)
when there is no risk of confusion), is the Galois group of
f(X)-t
over
k(t)
, viewed as a permutation group acting on the generic fiber
f^{-1}(t)\subset\overline{k(t)}
. The
geometric monodromy group
of
f
is the normal subgroup
{\rm Mon}_{\overline{k}}(f)\trianglelefteq\Mon_{k}(f)
.

The classification of monodromy groups of indecomposable polynomials over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Q}
, along with their possible ramification types, was carried out by Müller in
[
13
]
. The next step is to address the following:

Problem 1.1
.

Classify the monodromy groups of polynomials of length
2
over
\mathbb{C}
and over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Recall that the
(composition) length
of
f\in k[X]\setminus k
is the number of factors in a decomposition of
f
as a composition of indecomposable polynomials.
1
1

1

By Ritt’s theory (see
[
18
]
), this number is independent of the chosen decomposition.

We say that
f,g\in k[X]
are
linearly equivalent
(over
\overline{k}
) and write
f\sim g
, if there exist linear polynomials
\mu,\nu\in\overline{k}[X]
such that
g=\mu\circ f\circ\nu
. Of course, linearly equivalent polynomials have the same geometric monodromy group.
Note that an upper bound for the monodromy group of a composition
f=g\circ h
is given by (the imprimitive wreath product)
\Mon(h)\wr\Mon(g)=\Mon(h)^{\deg(g)}\rtimes\Mon(g)
. In case of equality, one in particular has that
\ker(\Mon(g\circ h)\to\Mon(g))=\Mon(h)^{\deg(g)}
is maximal possible. It turns out that, for many applications (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
for applications in arithmetic dynamics), a slight weakening of this property is sufficient: namely, for
g,h\in k[X]
of degree
>1
with
h
indecomposable, we say that
g\circ h
has a
large kernel
, if either

\soc(\Mon_{k}(h))^{\deg(g)}\leq\ker(\Mon_{k}(g\circ h)\to\Mon_{k}(g)),

or

\soc(\Mon_{k}(h))\,\,\textrm{is cyclic and}\,\,\soc(\Mon_{k}(h))^{\deg(g)-1}\leq\ker(\Mon_{k}(g\circ h)\to\Mon_{k}(g)).

Recall finally that the
Chebyshev polynomial

T_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
of degree
n\geq 1
is uniquely determined by the identity
T_{n}(X+1/X)=X^{n}+1/X^{n}
. Chebyshev polynomials and monomials
X^{n}
are at the other end of “large kernel”, fulfilling
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(T_{n})=D_{n}
(
n\geq 3
) and
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(X^{n})=C_{n}
, and thus
\ker(\Mon_{\overline{k}}(T_{n}\circ T_{m})\to\Mon_{\overline{k}}(T_{n}))=\ker(\Mon_{\overline{k}}(X^{n}\circ X^{m})\to\Mon_{\overline{k}}(X^{n}))=C_{m}
. A more diverse source for violation of the large kernel property (cf. Proposition
3.13
) are pairs
f,g
admitting
Ritt move
; see Section
2
for a definition.

Our main result states in a precise way that, for length-
2
polynomials, the above examples are “almost” the only ones violating the large kernel property. Namely,

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g,h\in k[X]
be indecomposable polynomials of degree
>1
.
Then
g\circ h
has a large kernel unless one of the following cases holds:

1.

Over
\overline{k}
, one has
g\circ h\sim T_{p^{2}}
or
g\circ h\sim X^{p^{2}}
for some prime
p
;

2.

g\circ h
has a Ritt move;

3.

\Mon(g\circ h)
is one of the groups in Table
1
. In particular, one of the following holds:

a.

h\sim X^{2}
and
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(g)\in\{S_{4},{PGL}_{2}(5),{PSL}_{3}(2),{PGL}_{2}(7),\\
{P\Gamma L}_{2}(9),{M}_{11},{PSL}_{3}(3),{PSL}_{4}(2),{P\Gamma L}_{3}(4),{M}_{23},{\rm PSL}_{5}(2)\}
;

b.

h\sim X^{3}
and
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(g)\in\{A_{5},{PGL}_{2}(7),{PSL}_{2}(11),{PSL}_{3}(3)\}
;

c.

h\sim T_{3}
and
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(g)=PSL_{3}(3)
.

In particular, if
\Mon(g),\Mon(h)
are both solvable, then
g\circ h
either has large kernel or is linearly equivalent to a monomial or a Chebyshev polynomial, or one of only two other cases holds:

•

\Mon_{\overline{k}}(g\circ h)=C_{3}\times S_{4}\leq S_{12}
, with a Ritt move;

•

\Mon_{\overline{k}}(g\circ h)=GL_{2}(3)\leq S_{8}
, without a Ritt move.

The techniques used to prove this theorem are diverse and include permutation group theory, representation theory and linear algebra, as well as some topological ideas related to braid group action and configuration spaces of Galois covers. Some parts are also based on computer calculations in Magma
[
4
]
, mainly using the database of transitive groups. Code for the nontrivial Magma verifications is contained in an ancillary file.

Applications of Theorem
1.2
go far beyond the consideration of only compositions of
two
indecomposables. We demonstrate this with sample results on composition of arbitrarily many indecomposables in Section
6
. In
[
3
]
, the authors apply (the restriction to solvable groups of) the theorem to solve a long-standing open problem due to Davenport, Lewis and Schinzel
[
8
]
on the reducibility behavior of
f(X)-g(Y)
, with
arbitrary
polynomials
f,g
.
Further applications are expected in the field of arithmetic dynamics, notably the study of dynamical Galois groups, i.e., the groups
G_{f,\alpha}:=\varprojlim_{n}\textrm{Gal}(f^{n}(X)-\alpha/k)
, where
f^{n}
denotes the
n
-th iterate of the polynomial
f\in k[X]
(and
\alpha\in k
). To illustrate this, we give a sample application in Section
6.2
. More comprehensive applications will be developed in follow-up work.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we establish preliminary results needed for the sequel.

3.1.
Setup

Given two permutation groups
U\leq{\rm Sym}(m)
and
V\leq{\rm Sym}(n)
, the
wreath product

U\wr V
is the semidirect product
U^{n}\rtimes V
, where
V
permutes the
n
copies of
U
.
For any
n\geq 2
and
m\geq 2
, the
augmentation subgroup
of
C_{m}^{n}
is

{\rm Aug}(C_{m}^{n})=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in C_{m}^{n}\mid a_{1}\cdots a_{n}=1\}.

For a group
G
, an integer
d
and an element
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in G^{d}
, let
\supp(x):=\{i\in\{1,\dots,d\}\mid x_{i}\neq 1\}
denote the
support
of
x
.

Given groups
G_{1},G_{2},H
, and morphisms
\phi_{1}:G_{1}\to H,\phi_{2}:G_{2}\to H
, the
fiber product
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
over
H
is the subgroup of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
given by

G_{1}\times_{H}G_{2}:=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in G_{1}\times G_{2}\mid\phi_{1}(g_{1})=\phi_{2}(g_{2})\}.

The
affine general linear group of degree
1
over a field
F
, denoted by
{AGL}_{1}(F)
, is the semidirect product
F\rtimes F^{\times}
, where
F^{\times}
acts by multiplication on
F
; this group acts naturally on
F
, where
F
acts on itself by translation. An indecomposable polynomial
f\in k[X]
is called an
{AGL}_{1}
-polynomial
if
p=\deg(f)
is prime and
{\rm Mon}(f)
is solvable; equivalently,
{\rm Mon}_{\overline{k}}(f)
is either cyclic or dihedral, that is, a permutation subgroup of
{AGL}_{1}(p)
.

A
function field

F
over
k
is a finite extension of
k(t)
, where
t
is transcendental over
k
. Denote by
g_{F}
its
genus
. Let
F_{1}/F
be an extension of function fields over
k
. For a place
Q
of
F_{1}
lying above a place

of
F
, write

for the ramification index (cf.
[

) of

. Let

be the places of
F_{1}
lying above a place

of
F
. The multiset

E_{F_{1}/F}(P):=[\,e(Q_{1}\mid P),\dots,e(Q_{r}\mid P)\,]

is called the
ramification type
of

in

. The place

is called a

.
Letting

is finite.
The multiset

\{\,E_{F_{1}/F}(P):P\in S\,\}

is called the

of

.

Assume now that
F=k(t)
and that the Galois closure of

has Galois group

, viewed as a transitive group of degree

. Write

. For each

(corresponding to relabeling of the set

) and

(see Section

), and satisfies
\pi_{P_{1}}\cdots\pi_{P_{u}}=1
.
The
Riemann–Hurwitz formula
gives:

(1)

2(n-1+g(F_{1}))=\sum_{Q\,\,\text{place of }F_{1}}\left(e(Q\mid Q\cap k(t))-1\right)=\sum_{P\in S}\textrm{ind}(\pi_{P}),

where the

\textrm{ind}(\pi)

is defined as

minus the number of orbits of
\langle\pi\rangle
.

We say that

, for indecomposable

, is a

such that

, but there is no linear polynomial

with

. It is known due to Ritt’s theorems (
[
16
]
; see
[
18
]

for

\gcd(\deg(g),\deg(h))=1
and there exist indecomposable polynomials

such that

\deg(u)=\deg(g),\,\,\deg(v)=\deg(h),\,\,g\circ h=v\circ u,

and moreover, after possibly interchanging
(g,h)
and
(v,u)
, there exist linear polynomials

3.5.
Topological ideas: coalescing of branch points and braid group action

A useful idea to lower-bound the monodromy group of a polynomial

with
r\geq 4
branch points and branch cycles
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
(with
\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{r}=1
) is the following observation: assume that

is the inertia group generator at infinity, i.e., is a full cycle in
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(f)

\langle\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-1}\rangle\supseteq\langle\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2}\rangle

\Mon_{\overline{k}}(f)
, and the right side is still a transitive subgroup of
\Mon_{\overline{k}}(f)
(due to the presence of the full cycle
\pi_{1}
); the group
\langle\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2}\rangle
is thus the monodromy group of a polynomial
6
6

6

That the tuple
(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2},\pi_{r-1}\pi_{r})
indeed yields the monodromy group of a
polynomial
can be seen easily from the fact that the full cycle
\pi_{1}
is contained, together with the fact that the genus given by the tuple
(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2},\pi_{r-1}\pi_{r})
is upper bounded by the one given by
(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2},\pi_{r-1},\pi_{r})
(which however is zero); the latter fact can easily seen combinatorially from the Riemann-Hurwitz genus formula (
1
), or from the topological interpretation via deformation of covers.
with branch cycles
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r-2},\pi_{r-1}\pi_{r}
, which topologically corresponds to a deformation of a family of covers
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, letting the
r-1
th and
r
-th branch point coalesce (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).

Note furthermore that the branch cycles of a polynomial map

r
branch points are only uniquely defined up to relabelling of the points

(i.e., conjugation in
S_{d}
) and action of the

. The latter is defined as the group generated by braids

fulfilling the relations

\beta_{i}\beta_{j}=\beta_{j}\beta_{i},\text{ for all }i<j-1,

\beta_{i}\beta_{i+1}\beta_{i}=\beta_{i+1}\beta_{i}\beta_{i+1},\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq r-2,\text{ and }

\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{r-1}\beta_{r-1}\cdots\beta_{1}=1;

and acting on

(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r})^{\beta_{i}}:=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{i-1},\pi_{i}\pi_{i+1}\pi_{i}^{-1},\pi_{i},\dots,\pi_{r}).

See
[
11

In our applications, applying the braid group action can be useful for polynomials

). It is well-known (and of course easy to verify computationally for the case of degree

(1,2,3,4),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4)

The following theorem shows that arbitrarily long compositions of polynomials linearly equivalent to Chebyshev polynomials always have large kernel in the absence of Ritt moves.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let

be polynomials of odd prime degrees

,

, with

.

b)

.

Proof.

By induction over

. Obviously it suffices to prove the assertion for algebraically closed fields

. The case

. Assume that the assertion holds for

-part of

. In particular,

embeds into

, where

.

By Theorem

, we have
{\rm Aug}(C_{2}^{p_{r-1}}).D_{p_{r-1}}\leq H\leq C_{2}\wr D_{p_{r}}
. We next claim that the image
\tilde{H}
of the blocks kernel

under projection to any of the (

) blocks still contains

. For this, note that

is a normal subgroup of

and contains an element of order

contains the normal closure a

-Sylow group of

. Since

has no element of order

, all elements in

, whence

, showing the claim.

We will now choose a setup allowing us to invoke Lemma
3.9
. Let
\widehat{\sigma}
be a generator of a cyclic transitive subgroup in

(e.g., an inertia group generator at infinity), and

in

. Set

. Then

(for

), and

is still the blocks kernel in

. Moreover, there is a natural map

whose image equals the kernel

contains

is of course a direct sum of faithful irreducible modules, since the only nontrivial normal subgroup

acts faithfully on each nondiagonal submodule of

,

acts nontrivially on the heart. Thus, if
d\geq 3
and
\Gamma/\Delta
contains a subgroup subgroup
C_{3}^{d-1}
, Assumption iii) clearly holds as well (namely, with
s=2
elements of order
3
, each supported on only two components), yielding the conclusion
\Delta\supseteq({\rm Aug}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{4}))^{d}
.

A situation still not falling into the scope of Lemma
B.1
is
H\leq C_{p}\wr C_{p}
(
p
prime), since the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[C_{p}]
permutation module is indecomposable with submodules of every dimension
0\leq d\leq p
. To obtain a reasonably simple criterion here, we restrict to a particular special case of Assumption iii).

Lemma B.3
.

Let
H=W.U
, where

W
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[U]
-module.
Let
G\leq H\wr S_{d}

be such that
\pi:G\to S_{d}
maps onto a transitive subgroup of
S_{d}
, and let
\Gamma=\ker(\pi)=G\cap H^{d}
. Furthermore, let
\rho:\Gamma\to U^{d}
be the natural projection and
\Delta=\ker(\rho)=\Gamma\cap W^{d}
. Assume all of the following:

i)

The image of
\Gamma

under projection to one component contains
H^{\prime}:=W.U^{\prime}
for some subgroup
U^{\prime}\leq U
such that
W
is an indecomposable
\mathbb{F}_{p}[U^{\prime}]
-module.

ii)

\Gamma/\Delta\supseteq U^{\prime d}
.

Set
W_{0}:=W
and
W_{i}:=[W_{i-1},H^{\prime}]
for
i\geq 1
, where
[W_{i-1},H^{\prime}]
denotes the module generated by commutators.
Then the following hold:

1)

\Delta\supseteq W_{2}^{d}
,

2)

If additionally
W_{1}\subsetneq W
is the unique maximal
\mathbb{F}_{p}[U^{\prime}]
-submodule of
W
, then either
W_{1}=W_{2}
or

\Delta/W_{2}^{d}
contains an element of support size
2
in
(W_{1}/W_{2})^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in U^{\prime}
. Due to Assumption ii), there exists
x,y\in\Gamma
with
\rho(x)=(u,1,\dots,1)
and
\rho(y)=(v,1,\dots,1)
. I.e.,
x=(\tilde{u},w_{2},\dots,w_{d})
for some
\tilde{u}\in H
mapping to
u
under the projection
H\to U
, and for some
w_{2},\dots,w_{d}\in W
; and analogously for
y
.
Moreover, for every
w\in W
, there exists
z\in\Gamma
with first component
z_{1}=w
, due to Assumption i). Then the commutator
[z,x]
is an element of
\Delta
with first coordinate
[w,\tilde{u}]
. Thus, the commutator
[[z,x],y]\in\Delta
is supported only on the first component, with entry
[[w,\tilde{u}],\tilde{v}]
. As
u,v
run through all of
U^{\prime}
and
w
through all
W
, these elements generate all of
W_{2}
, so that
\Delta\supseteq W_{2}^{d}
, showing 1). Next, consider the quotient module
\tilde{\Delta}:=\Delta/W_{2}^{d}
.
We distinguish two cases, depending on the component entries of the element
x
above.

Case 1
: For all
x
as above, it holds that
w_{j}\in W_{1}
for all
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
. Then all but the first component entry of
[z,x]
are in fact in
W_{2}
. On the other hand, as soon as
W_{1}\subsetneq W
, we may pick
z
such that its first component entry
w
is in
W\setminus W_{1}
. The additional assumption of
W_{1}
being the unique maximal submodule of
W
implies that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[U^{\prime}]
-submodule generated by
w
is all of
W
. If the module
[w,H]
generated by all commutators
[w,h]
,
h\in H^{\prime}
is contained in
W_{2}
, then
W_{1}=[W,H^{\prime}]=[w,H^{\prime}]=W_{2}
. We may thus assume that there exists
\tilde{u}\in H^{\prime}
with
[w,\tilde{u}]\notin W_{2}
,
so that for this choice of
w
and
\tilde{u}
, the element
[z,x]
maps to an element of
\tilde{\Delta}
supported exactly on the first component, trivially implying 2).

Case 2
: There exists
x
as above and
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
with
w_{j}\notin W_{1}
.
As in Case 1), we may assume that there exists
h\in H^{\prime}
with
[w_{j},h]\notin W_{2}
. By Assumption ii), we may pick
\tilde{x}\in\Gamma
with
\supp(\rho(\tilde{x}))=\{j\}
and whose
j
-th component
\tilde{x}_{j}
has the same image as
h
under projection
H\to U
.
Then
[\tilde{x},x]
is supported at most on the first and the
j
-th component, and additionally the
j
-th component entry equals
[w_{j},h]\notin W_{2}
. This again implies 2).
∎

Remark B.4
.

The commutator approach used in the above proof shows that in fact
W_{2}^{d}\subseteq[\Gamma,\Gamma]\cap\Delta
, and even more precisely that
W_{2}^{d}\subseteq[\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{0}]\cap\Delta
with
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
the preimage of
U^{\prime d}\subseteq\Gamma/\Delta
.

Example B.5
.

For
W=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p}
the permutation module under the cyclic group
U^{\prime}=C_{p}
(i.e.,
H^{\prime}=C_{p}\wr C_{p}
),
W_{j}
is the unique submodule of codimension
j
in
W
; in particular, one has
W_{1}=\textrm{Aug}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p})
and
W_{1}/W_{2}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In the case where
\pi(G)\leq S_{d}
additionally acts primitively (e.g., when
d
is a prime), an element of support size
2
necessarily generates a submodule of codimension
1
in
(W_{1}/W_{2})^{d}
. Lemma
B.3
thus asserts that in this case
\Delta
contains a submodule of codimension
\leq 1
of
(\textrm{Aug}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p}))^{d}
, and due to Remark
B.4
, this submodule is even contained in
\Delta\cap[\Gamma_{p},\Gamma_{p}]
with a
p
-Sylow subgroup
\Gamma_{p}
of
\Gamma
.
