New directions in the study of prime ideals in rational, nilpotent Iwasawa algebras
Adam Jones, William Woods

Abstract
Let G G be a nilpotent p p -valuable (compact p p -adic Lie) group. There is an ongoing investigation into the prime ideals of its completed group algebra ( Iwasawa algebra ) ℤ p ​ [ [ G ] ] \mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]] , and there remains an open conjecture that they can all be proved to have a canonical standard form. We very this conjecture for several new classes of nilpotent groups, including those corresponding to the positive subalgebra of almost all classical and exceptional types, curiously excluding those of type C C . † † 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 22E35, 22E27, 20C07, 16W70, 16W80, 17B22

Introduction

Fix a prime
p
. and let
G
be a
p
-valuable group in the sense of
[
10

p
(if

(if

Let

be a commutative pseudocompact ring. The

(also known to number theorists as the

) of

over

R[[G]]=\varprojlim_{U}R[G/U],

where

is the ordinary group algebra of the (finite, discrete) group

with coefficients in

, as

runs over the set of open normal subgroups of
G
.

Now fix a finite extension of fields
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, uniformiser
\pi
, and residue field
k=\mathcal{O}/\pi
. There is an ongoing investigation into the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}G:=\mathcal{O}[[G]]
, especially when
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, beginning with the paper
[
1
]
, and advanced in several subsequent articles, including
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
.

We say that a prime ideal
P
of
\mathcal{O}G
is

if we can find a closed normal subgroup
H
of
G
such that
G^{*}:=G/H
is
p
-valuable and the image of

under the surjection
\mathcal{O}G\to\mathcal{O}G^{*}
is centrally generated. We say that
P
is

if
P\cap\mathcal{O}U
is standard for some open subgroup
U
of
G

[
9
, Definition 1.1]
.

The main conjecture in the study of prime ideals in Iwasawa algebras (see e.g.
[
9
, Conjecture 1.2]
) roughly states that all prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}G
are virtually standard. This is now known for large classes of prime ideals in various groups, but in general it remains open.

To simplify the conjecture, fix a prime ideal
P
of
\mathcal{O}G
and set

P^{\dagger}=\ker(G\to\mathcal{O}G^{\times}\to(\mathcal{O}G/P)^{\times}),

a closed normal subgroup of
G
: then
P
contains the augmentation ideal
\varepsilon=(P^{\dagger}-1)\mathcal{O}G
, so we can pass to the quotient

P^{*}=P/\varepsilon\subseteq\mathcal{O}G/\varepsilon\cong\mathcal{O}G^{*},

where
G^{*}=G/P^{\dagger}
. Then
P^{*}
is a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}G^{*}
, and now we have
(P^{*})^{\dagger}=1
, so to prove
P
is standard, we only need to prove that
P^{*}
is centrally generated.

Note:
The resulting group
G^{*}
may no longer be
p
-valuable, as it may no longer be torsion-free. However, there is a finite normal subgroup
F^{*}\leq G^{*}
such that the quotient
G^{*}/F^{*}

is

p
-valuable, and
P^{*}\cap\mathcal{O}F^{*}
is the intersection of a
G

\mathcal{O}F^{*}\cong\mathcal{O}[F^{*}]
, the (ordinary) group algebra of
F^{*}
. Hence it is considered relatively harmless to assume that

.

We say that a prime ideal
P
of
\mathcal{O}G
is

if
P^{\dagger}=1
. Then setting

as the centre of

Conjecture A.
If
P
is a faithful prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}G
, then
P=(P\cap\mathcal{O}Z)\mathcal{O}G
.

There are two natural cases to consider here.

1.

If
\pi\in P
, we may quotient out by
(\pi)
and pass to

. This version of Conjecture A was asked as

2.

If

is semisimple of type
A
or

[
12

[
11
, Theorem 2]
, or for primitive ideals when
G

[
9

(Warning:

In this paper, we restrict to case 2, about which much less is known. We will also assume that

is a nilpotent group, and we want to build on the results from
[

and try to prove Conjecture A for all prime ideals, rather than just primitive ones.

In §

of

Theorem.

[

Setting

P=(P\cap KA)KG

Of course, if

Moreover, the construction of

In this note, we will define a smaller subgroup

Theorem 1.

Needless to say, how much of an improvement this result is depends entirely on how much smaller than

the subgroup

However, in §

, we will explore a number of examples of groups

for which we can prove that

is a proper subgroup of

, and in several of them we even have that

Theorem 2.
(Corollary

is nilpotent and Lie
(G)
is the positive root subalgebra of a split-semisimple

-Lie algebra, containing no component of type

of

It is curious that this approach fails when

The following facts about group commutators are well known, and can be easily checked directly: we include them only for ease of reference later.

Lemma 2.1

Let

(i)

(ab,c)={}^{a}(b,c)\cdot(a,c)

2.1
p
-valuable groups

Definition 2.2
.

[
10
, Ch. III, Définition 2.1.2]

A
p
-valuation

\omega
on a group
G
is a function
\omega:G\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
such that, for all
g,h\in G
,

(i)

\omega(gh^{-1})\geq\min\{\omega(g),\omega(h)\}
,

(ii)

\omega((g,h))\geq\omega(g)+\omega(h)
,

(iii)

\omega(g^{p})=\omega(g)+1
,

(iv)

\omega(g)>(p-1)^{-1}
, and

(v)

\omega(g)=\infty\Leftrightarrow g=1
.

Moreover, we say that the
p
-valuation
\omega
is
saturated
if for all
g\in G
such that
\omega(g)>\frac{1}{p-1}+1
, there exists
h\in G
with
g=h^{p}
.

Note:
If
G
carries a
p
-valuation then it is torsionfree, and if
g^{p}=h^{p}
for any
g,h\in G
then
g=h
. In particular, if
\omega
is saturated and
\omega(g)>\frac{1}{p-1}+1
, then there is a
unique
element

such that
h^{p}=g
, we write
h=g^{p^{-1}}
.

Defined in this way, a

-valuation is a kind of group filtration. More precisely, we isolate the following properties from
[
10
]
.

Properties 2.3
.

Let

be a group and
\omega

-valuation on

1.

Given any

2.

Define the

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(G)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{gr}_{\nu}(G)

[

There is also an

There is also a notion of

Note:
If

then for any

\overset{N}{\underset{n=1}{\prod}}g^{\lambda_{n}p^{n}}

2.2
Lazard’s equivalence of categories

Let
G
be a
p
-valuable group, with
p
-valuation
\omega
, and let
\Lambda_{G}:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}G
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Iwasawa algebra of
G
. Then
\Lambda_{G}
carries a natural topology inherited from
\omega
. Specifically, we know using
[
10
, III.2.3.6]
that there exists a separated ring filtration
w
on the group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G]
such that

•

w
restricts to the
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

•

w(g-1)>1
for all
g\in G
,

•

the associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}_{w}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G]
is isomorphic to the enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}_{\omega}(G)
, and

•

\Lambda_{G}
arises as the topological completion of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G]
with respect to
w
.

Therefore, we can of course extend
w
to
\Lambda_{G}
. Moreover,
w
extends further to the rational Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda_{G}
, where we define
w(p^{-m}\otimes r):=w(r)-m
. However,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
is no longer complete with respect to
w
, so we can pass to the completion
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
with respect to
w
.

Now, inside
\Lambda_{G}
,
w(g-1)>1
, so
w((g-1)^{n})\geq n\geq v_{p}(n)=w(n)
, so it follows that the sequence
\left(\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(g-1)^{n}\right)
converges to 0 in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
, and hence the logarithm series
\log(g):=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(g-1)^{n}
converges in
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}
.

Now, if
\omega
is a saturated
p
-valuation, we define the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie lattice of
G
to be the set

\log(G):=\{\log(g):g\in G\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}

Theorem 2.8
.

[
10
, IV.3.2.3]

For any
p
-saturable group
G
,
\log(G)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie subalgebra of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}
of finite rank, equal to the rank of
G
. Moreover, for any
g,h\in G
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

•

\lambda\log(g)=\log(g^{\lambda})

•

\log(g)+\log(h)=\log\left(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}{\left(g^{p^{n}}h^{p^{n}}\right)^{p^{-n}}}\right)

•

[\log(g),\log(h)]=\log\left(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}{\left(g^{p^{n}},h^{p^{n}}\right)^{p^{-2n}}}\right)

In fact, the functor
G\mapsto\log(G)
defines an equivalence (and in fact isomorphism) of categories between the category of
p
-saturable groups and a certain category of free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras known as
saturable Lie algebras

[
10
, IV.3.2.6]
. This is known as
Lazard’s equivalence of categories
.

Example 2.9
.

If
G
is a uniform pro-
p
group, then if we let
\mathcal{L}:=\log(G)
, then
\mathcal{L}
is a
powerful
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra
, i.e.
[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}]\subseteq p\mathcal{L}
.

Moreover, the logarithm functor restricts to an equivalence of categories between the category of uniform pro-
p
groups and the category of powerful
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras of finite rank.

Lazard’s equivalence will prove useful when looking for examples of
p
-valuable groups, since it is practically much easier to start with a powerful
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra and pass to its image under the inverse functor
\exp
rather than look for examples directly.

Finally, we define the
Lie algebra
of a
p
-valuable group

to be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{G}=Lie(G):=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\log(Sat(G))

Unfortunately, the functor

is no kind of equivalence of categories, and there are plenty of examples of non-isomorphic groups

such that
\mathfrak{g}_{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{H}
(for example, if

is any open subgroup of
G
). But note that whenever

is
p
-saturable,
\log(G)
is a full rank
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie lattice in
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
.

Remark 2.10
.

If
G
is not

-saturable, then
\log(G)\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}
need not be closed under addition or the Lie bracket. Still, we can embed
\log(G)
as a subset of
\log(Sat(G))

Lemma 2.11

For any

Let
G

(or

Lemma 2.12
.

There exist mutually inverse bijections

\textstyle{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{prime ideals of }\mathcal{O}G\text{ containing }p\\
\end{array}\right\}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\textstyle{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{prime ideals of }KG\\
\end{array}\right\}}}

where
\Phi(Q):=K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Q
and
\Psi(P):=P\cap\mathcal{O}G
. Moreover,
\Phi(Q)^{\dagger}=Q^{\dagger}
, and
P
is controlled by a closed, normal subgroup
H
of
G
if and only if
\Phi(P)
is controlled by
H
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that every two-sided ideal
I
of
KG
can be written as
K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}J
, where
J=I\cap\mathcal{O}G
, and that
I
is prime if and only if
J
is prime.

On the other hand, if
Q
is a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}G
not containing
p
, then let
P:=K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Q
. It is clear that
Q\subseteq P\cap\mathcal{O}G
, and for all
r\in P\cap\mathcal{O}G
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p^{n}r\in Q
. But
p
is central in
\mathcal{O}G
, so since
p\notin Q
and
Q
is prime, it follows that
r\in Q
. Therefore
P\cap\mathcal{O}G=Q
, so it follows that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are well defined, mutually inverse bijections.

Moreover, for all group elements
g\in G
, and all prime ideals
P
of
KG
, since
g-1\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\subseteq\mathcal{O}G
, it follows that
g-1\in P
if and only if
g-1\in P\cap\mathcal{O}G=\Psi(P)
, so
P^{\dagger}=\Psi(P)^{\dagger}
. Since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are mutually inverse, it follows immediately that
\Phi(Q)^{\dagger}=Q^{\dagger}
for all prime ideals
Q
of
\mathcal{O}G
not containing
p
.

Now, if
H
controls a prime ideal
Q
of
\mathcal{O}G
with
p\notin Q
, then for any
r\in\Phi(Q)
, we know that
p^{n}r\in Q
for some

, so we can write

for some

,

. Thus

, and since

, it follows that

.

Finally, let
P
be a prime ideal of
KG
, and it remains to prove that if
H

Definition 3.2

4
Examples

Throughout this section, we will fix
\mathfrak{g}
a finite dimensional, nilpotent,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra. We want to calculate
B(\mathfrak{g})
for various examples.

4.1
Positive root bases

To first outline a possible method to calculating
B(\mathfrak{g})
, we want to focus on a particular class of examples.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a basis
\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
for
\mathfrak{g}
, we say that this is a
positive root basis
if for every pair
i,j\in I
, there exists
k:=k(i,j)\in I
such that
[x_{i},x_{j}]\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}x_{k}
.

Note:
If
[x_{i},x_{j}]=0
, we may take
k(i,j)
to be any element of
I
. For convenience, we will usually take
k(i,j)=i
in this case. If
[x_{i},x_{j}]\neq 0
then
k(i,j)
is uniquely defined.

Example 4.2
.

The key examples of nilpotent Lie algebras carrying positive root bases come from Lie theory. Recall, if
\mathfrak{s}
is a split semisimple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then the standard Cartan decomposition gives us

\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\underset{\alpha\in\Phi}{\bigoplus}\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}

where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra,
\Phi\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is the root system of
\mathfrak{s}
, and
\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
is the one dimensional
\alpha
-eigenspace of the action of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{s}
. Note that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
,

[\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha},s_{\mathfrak{\beta}}]=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha+\beta}&\alpha+\beta\in\Phi\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Decompose
\Phi=\Phi^{+}\sqcup\Phi^{-}
, where
\Phi^{+}
(resp.
\Phi^{-}
) consist of the positive (resp. negative) roots in
\Phi
with respect to some ordering, and let
\mathfrak{n}^{+}:=\underset{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\bigoplus}\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\mathfrak{n}^{-}:=\underset{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}{\bigoplus}\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
). Then
\mathfrak{n}^{+},\mathfrak{n}^{-}
are nilpotent subalgebras of
\mathfrak{s}
, and we may write
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{n}^{+}
, and for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
let
x_{\alpha}
be a generator of the one dimensional space
\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
. Then
\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
is a positive root basis for
\mathfrak{g}
(hence the name), where the element
k(\alpha,\beta)
from Definition
4.1
is defined by

k(\alpha,\beta)=\begin{cases}\alpha+\beta&\alpha+\beta\in\Phi\\
\alpha&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The following technical result provides a method of calculating
B(\mathfrak{g})
whenever
\mathfrak{g}
has a positive root basis.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
be a positive root basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, and suppose
B(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\text{ Span}\{x_{j}:j\in J\}
for some subset
J\subseteq I
. Suppose further that

•

there exists
i\in I
such that
[x_{i},[x_{i},x_{j}]]=0
for all
j\in J
.

•

There exists a subset
\varnothing\neq S\subseteq J
, maximal with respect to the property that
[x_{i},x_{m}]\neq 0
for all
m\in S
.

•

k(i,m)\neq k(i,m^{\prime})
if
m,m^{\prime}\in S
and
m\neq m^{\prime}
.

Then there exists a proper subset
T
of
J
such that
B(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\text{ Span}\{x_{j}:j\in T\}
.

Proof.

For each
m\in S
, write
[x_{i},x_{m}]=\mu_{i,m}x_{k(i,m)}
, with
\mu_{i,m}\neq 0
. Since
[x_{i},[x_{i},x_{j}]]=0
for all
j\in J
and
B(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\text{ Span}\{x_{j}:j\in J\}
, we know that
[x_{i},[x_{i},B(\mathfrak{g})]]=0
, so
[x_{i},B(\mathfrak{g})]=0
by (

∗
⁣
∗

‣
3.3
).

Moreover, by maximality of
S
,
[x_{i},x_{j}]=0
for all
j\in J
with
j\notin S
. So if
x\in B(\mathfrak{g})
and
x=\underset{j\in J}{\sum}\lambda_{j}x_{j}
, then

0=[x_{i},x]=\underset{j\in J}{\sum}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},x_{j}]=\underset{m\in S}{\sum}\lambda_{m}[x_{i},x_{m}]=\underset{m\in S}{\sum}\lambda_{m}\mu_{i,m}x_{k(i,m)}

so since
k(i,m)\neq k(i,m^{\prime})
if
m\neq m^{\prime}
, we see using linear independence that
\lambda_{m}\mu_{i,m}=0
for all
m\in S
and hence
\lambda_{m}=0
. It follows that if
T:=J\backslash S
then
x=\underset{j\in T}{\sum}\lambda_{j}x_{j}
. Since this is true for all
x\in B(\mathfrak{g})
, we deduce that
B(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\text{ Span}\{x_{j}:j\in T\}
, and since
S
is non-empty by assumption,
T
is a proper subset of
J
.∎

Of course, the difficulty in utilising this result lies in finding the element
i\in I
and the subset
S\subseteq J
, and this issue must be dealt with separately for each example.

4.2
Positive root subalgebras

Again, let
\mathfrak{s}
be a split semisimple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
, root system
\Phi
and decomposition
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{n}^{+}

\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
be the associated positive root basis for
\mathfrak{g}

Now, recall that the positive system
\Phi^{+}
contains a unique highest root
\theta
(with respect to the Bruhat ordering), and the centre of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}
is the one dimensional eigenspace
\mathfrak{s}_{\theta}
. The following technical results allow us to apply Proposition
4.3
to root bases of this form.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
\Phi
is an irreducible root system, not of type
C
, and
\Psi\subseteq\Phi^{+}
is any subset with
\theta\in\Psi
but
\Psi\neq\{\theta\}
. Then there exists
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that
2\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi
for all
\beta\in\Psi
, but
\alpha+\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
for
some

\gamma\in\Psi
.

Proof.

As we will show, for many irreducible root systems
\Phi
, we can choose a positive system
\Phi^{+}
such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
,
2\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi
. More generally, it would suffice to prove that for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,
\beta\in\Psi
,
2\alpha+\beta\notin\Psi
, because in this case, since
\theta
is the unique highest root, it follows from
[
13

that for all
\gamma\in\Psi
with
\gamma\neq\theta
, there exists
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha+\gamma\in\Phi
. Since we are assuming
\Psi\neq\{\theta\}
this completes the proof.

However, there are examples where there do exist pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
of positive roots such that
2\alpha+\beta\in\Phi
, so we will need to treat these cases separately. We will examine and eliminate each irreducible root system in turn, and we begin each example by realising each positive system as a set of real vectors. In all the examples below,
\{e_{i}:i=1,\dots,n\}
represents the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

1.

Type
A_{n}
: We take
\Phi
to be the set of all integer vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of length
\sqrt{2}
whose entries sum to 0. We can take
\Phi^{+}=\{e_{i}-e_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}
.

For every
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, writing
\alpha=e_{i}-e_{j}

Type

Remark 4.6

To assess how much of an improvement the method of using

Lemma 4.10

Suppose

So clearly the set

Moreover, for all

. In fact, if
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
and

\alpha=e_{i}-e_{j}
for some

, so for any

if and only if

or

or
[\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\beta}]=s_{\gamma}
for some

\mathfrak{h}=\underset{\alpha\in A}{\bigoplus}\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
is an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is an abelian ideal containing
\mathfrak{h}
. Then for any element

is a generator of

and since

, and hence

for all

. It follows that

and thus
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{h}
, so
\mathfrak{h}
is a maximal abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

So to prove that
\mathfrak{h}=B(\mathfrak{g})
, it remains to show that
\mathfrak{h}
satisfies (

⁣
∗

‣
3.3

We may assume that the

\displaystyle 0=[x,[x,x_{2e_{k}}]]

Corollary 4.11
.

If
G
is a
p
-valuable group and

is a positive nilpotent subalgebra of a split simple algebraic Lie algebra of type

\binom{n+1}{2}
, while

has rank 1.

Proof.

We know
\mathfrak{g}_{A(G)}
is an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
B(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}_{B(\mathfrak{g})}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{A(G)}
. But
\mathfrak{g}_{B(\mathfrak{g})}
is a maximal abelian ideal of dimension

.

But

has rank 1.∎

So the

, we know that it produces solid results in all other types. The only question remaining is, could a result such as this could be proved using the original

The short answer to this is no, because there are examples of groups

For example, if

4.4
Obstacles to a complete proof

While the methods outlined in this paper allow us to establish our Conjecture A for a wide range of new examples, they are still not sufficient to prove it in full generality, because there are still many examples of nilpotent groups
G
such that
B(G)\neq Z(G)
, as demonstrated by Corollary
4.11
, and also the following result.

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose
G
contains a closed, isolated, abelian normal subgroup
H
such that
G/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. If
G
has nilpotency class at least 3 then
B(G)=A(G)=H
and
B(G)\neq Z(G)
.

Proof.

We will show that
H
satisfies (

∗

‣
3.2
), and thus
H\subseteq B(G)\subseteq A(G)
by definition. So since
H
and
A(G)
are both closed and isolated by Lemma
3.8
, it follows that
A(G)=H
or
A(G)=G
. But
G
is nilpotent, so
A(G)\neq G
by Lemma
3.3
, thus
A(G)=B(G)=H
.

Suppose
g\in G
and
(g,(g,H))=1
. If
g\in H
then
(g,H)=1
since
H
is abelian, so assume for contradiction that
g\notin H
. Then since
G/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we know that
g
and
H
generate an open normal subgroup
U
of
G
. Moreover, since
(g,H)\subseteq H
, and
g
and
H
both commute with
(g,H)
, it follows that
(U,(g,H))=1
, i.e.
(g,H)
is central in
U
.

But
(g,g)=1
so this means that
(g,U)
is central in
U
by Lemma
2.1
, and hence
g\in Z_{2}(U)
. But
Z_{2}(U)\subseteq Z_{2}(G)
by the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 4.9]
. So
g\in Z_{2}(G)
, and since
(H,H)=1
this means that
(U,H)\subseteq Z(G)
, so
H\subseteq Z_{2}(U)\subseteq Z_{2}(G)
, and hence
U\subseteq Z_{2}(G)
. This means that
Z_{2}(G)
is an open normal subgroup of
G
, so since it is isolated this implies that
Z_{2}(G)=G
, contradicting the assumption that
G
has nilpotency class at least 3.

So
B(G)=A(G)=H
, and

since
G
is non-abelian and

has rank 1.∎

To complete a proof of Conjecture A for nilpotent groups in general, it would be enough to establish that it holds for groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

We will conclude with some further examples, not arising from Lie theory and not covered by the previous cases. The following result suggests how we can try to employ an inductive strategy in the cases where

or

are too large, in particular circumventing the issue that

whenever
