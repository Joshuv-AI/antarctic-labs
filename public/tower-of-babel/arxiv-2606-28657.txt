Riesz means of quadratic class numbers
Olivia Beckwith, Tushar Karmakar

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for a weighted Riesz mean of Hurwitz class numbers and real quadratic class numbers. To do this, we introduce L L -functions for weight 1 2 \frac{1}{2} sesquiharmonic Maass forms of moderate growth and prove a formula for the Riesz means of the corresponding generalized mock modular forms, generalizing a recent result of the first author with Diamantis, Gupta, Rolen, and Thalagoda for mock modular forms. We then apply this formula to a sesquiharmonic Maass form that was first introduced by Duke, Imamoḡlu, and Tóth.



1.
Introduction

The study of the growth of class numbers of quadratic number fields dates back to Gauss’ conjectures. In the case of negative discriminants, Gauss Conjectures’ were resolved throughout the twentieth century in several celebrated works
[
Gol85
,
GZ
,
Hee52
,
Hei34
,
Sie35
,
Sta67
]
, while for positive discriminants, Gauss’ conjectures remain open.

The richness of the study of class numbers owes in part to the connections between class numbers and other important objects in analytic number theory, especially
L
-functions and modular forms. The works of Heilbronn
[
Hei34
]
and Siegel
[
Sie35
]
relied on Dirichlet’s class number formula for the value of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-series at 1. The connections to modular forms go back to Gauss, who related class numbers to coefficients of the third power of the Jacobi theta function. More precisely, these coefficients are given in terms of Hurwitz class numbers
H(n)
, which are defined in terms of binary quadratic forms of negative discriminant and have been studied extensively in the literature: their recursive relations such as the Kronecker-Hurwitz relations
[
Coh75
]
, traces of Hecke operators acting spaces of modular forms
[
Mer16
]
, divisibility properties
[
Bec17
,
Bec20
,
AC55
,
Ono99
,
BRR24
]
, elliptic curves
[
Sch87
]
, and mathematical physics
[
CM23
]
.

Many of these works rely the mock modularity of the generating function of
H(n)
, known due to work by Zagier
[
Zag
]
. Unlike the usual modular forms, mock modular forms lack the usual modular symmetry, but instead have nonholomorphic modular completions called harmonic Maass forms, which consist of a sum of a holomorphic form and a nonholomorphic generating series involving the incomplete gamma function. The study of such functions can be traced back to back to Ramanujan’s seminal discovery of mock theta functions, but was developed in its modern formulation in the works of Zwegers
[
Zwe
]
and Bruinier and Funke
[
Bru
]
. An overview of the theory of harmonic Maass forms and their applications in number theory, partition statistics, representation theory, and various areas of mathematical physics can be found in
[
Bri
]
.

Positive discriminant analogues
\mathrm{Tr_{d}(1)}
of Hurwitz class numbers were introduced by Duke, Imamoḡlu and Tóth
[
DIT11
]
and shown to be coefficients of the holomorphic part of a nonholomorphic modular form belonging to the so-called class of sesquiharmonic Maass forms. These functions have since appeared in the setting of the smallest parts function in partition theory
[
Bri08
]
, non-critical values of modular
L
-functions
[
BDR13
]
, and theta lifts
[
BFI15
]
. While the Fourier expansions of harmonic Maass forms involving two infinite generating series (a holomorphic series and a generating series involving the incomplete gamma function), the Fourier series for sesquiharmonic Maass forms include a third generating series involving an additional special function.

In this paper, we focus on the class of weight
\frac{1}{2}
sesquiharmonic Maass forms of moderate growth (see the next section for precise definitions). This class includes the function
\hat{Z}(\tau)
defined in
[
DIT11
]
and the higher level generalizations introduced in
[
BM
]
. They were used in
[
ABS25
]
to give a half-integral weight Eisenstein analogue of a result of
[
MO16
]
on shifted convolution sums. Functions in this class have Fourier expansions of the form

\displaystyle F(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left(d_{F}(0)+d_{F}(1)\log v+d_{F}(2)y^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>0\end{subarray}}{c_{F}(n)q^{n}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<0\end{subarray}}{b_{F}(n)\beta(-4nv)q^{n}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}{a_{F}(n)\alpha(4nv)q^{n}},

(1.1)

where throughout we use the standard notation
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
,
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, and for
y>0
, the special functions
\alpha(y)
and
\beta(y)
are given by

\displaystyle\alpha{(y)}:=\frac{\sqrt{y}}{4\pi}{\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}e^{-{\pi}yt}\log(1+t)dt},\quad\beta{(y)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{{\pi}y}^{\infty}{t^{1/2-1}e^{-t}}dt.

(1.2)

A central feature of the classical theory of holomorphic modular forms is the associated
L
-series, whose analytic continuation and functional equation and (for Hecke eigenforms) Euler products are useful in studying the Fourier coefficients and related sums. Nonholomorphic generating series pose an obstruction to these methods. The problem of defining
L
-functions for harmonic Maass forms has been studied recently by
[
SS22
]
, who restricted to the class of harmonic Maass forms of moderate growth, and Diamantis, Lee, Raji, and Rolen
[
DLRR23
]
, who employed test functions to deal with both the nonholomorphic obstruction and the exponential growth of harmonic Maass forms. Here we extend the method of Shankadhar and Singh and define
L
-functions for sesquiharmonic Maass forms of weight
\frac{1}{2}
with moderate growth. With
F
given as in (

\Lambda_{N}(F,s)
associated to

as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}(F,s)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\left[\Gamma(s)L^{+}(F,s)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}W_{\frac{1}{2}}(s)L^{-}(F,s)+Z(s)L^{*}(F,s)\right],

We show that these series converge absolutely for sufficiently large

Let

We use these

.

For

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\left(1-\frac{n}{x}\right)^{\rho}

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Tr_{n}(1)}+\frac{H(n)((x/n)-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2n}(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,\frac{1}{2},\rho+\frac{3}{2};\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)\right)

where

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;z):=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(c+s)}z^{s}.

2.
Modular Forms Background

The Jacobi theta function is defined by

\displaystyle\theta(\tau):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}

where we use the notation

\displaystyle q:=e(\tau)=e^{2\pi i\tau},\ \ \ \tau\in\mathbb{H},\ \ \ \operatorname{Re}(\tau)=u,\ \ \operatorname{Im}(\tau)=v.

The multiplier system for the theta function is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{\theta}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\right):=(c\tau+d)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\frac{\theta(\gamma\tau)}{\theta(\tau)}=\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{-1},

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{d}=\begin{cases}1&d\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
i&d\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

For any
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we define the
|_{k}\gamma
operator acting on functions
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
by

f|_{k}\gamma(z)=(cz+d)^{-k}f(\gamma z).

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, this defines a group action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\{f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}\}
.

We let
\Gamma_{0}(N)
denote the usual congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, consisting of matrices in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
whose lower left entry is divisible by
N
. Given a Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
N
and any
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
\chi(\gamma)
denotes
\chi
evaluated at the lower right entry of
\gamma
.

We have the
weight
k
hyperbolic Laplace operator
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}:=-v^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)+ikv\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right),

and this operator decomposes as

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}=-\xi_{2-k}\xi_{k},

(2.1)

where
\xi_{k}
acts on functions

\xi_{k}(f)(\tau)=2iy^{k}\overline{\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\tau}}}.

In particular, holomorphic functions are automatically annihilated by

.
Further, this operator intertwines with the slash operator in “dual weights”

Lemma 3.3
.

Z(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and it has an analytic continuation to
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\alpha\left(\frac{2x}{\pi}\right)e^{-x}
. Using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we find that
f(x)
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
.

Now note that as
x\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{t^{-1/2}e^{-2xt}\log(1+t)}dt=x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2u}\log(1+\frac{u}{x})du=\mathcal{O}(x^{-\frac{3}{2}}).

It follows that
f(x)=O(x^{-1})
as
x\rightarrow 0
.
On the other hand,
f(x)
tends to
0
as
x\to\infty
. Therefore by using Proposition 3.1.22 of
[
CS17
]
,
we deduce that
Z(s)
converges absolutely
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and is holomorphic in this region.

For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
, we define
Z(s)
by the formula in Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle Z(s):=\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{8\pi^{3}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-1/2}\log(1+t)}{(2t+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}dt.

To see that the function on the right hand side is holomorphic, note that

\displaystyle Z(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{8\pi^{3}}}\sum_{N\geq 0}\int_{N}^{N+1}\frac{t^{-1/2}\log(1+t)}{(2t+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}dt

Since
F_{N}(s)=\int_{N}^{N+1}\frac{t^{-1/2}\log(1+t)}{(2t+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}dt
is holomorphic for each
N
on
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
, and the series
f(s):=\sum_{N\geq 0}F_{N}(s)
converges uniformly on any compact subset in the region
\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}(s)>0\}
,
f(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
,

Z(s)=\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2x+1}}\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{dx}{1+x}

where
\mathbf{B}_{z}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{z}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt
is the incomplete beta function.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\log(1+t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dx}{1+x}

Then as an application of Fubini’s theorem, we can write
Z(s)
as follows

\displaystyle Z(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{8{\pi}^{3}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-1/2}}{(2t+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dx}{1+x}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{8{\pi}^{3}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-1/2}}{(2t+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}dt\right)\frac{dx}{1+x}

A change of variables allows us to rewrite the inner integral in terms of the incomplete beta function, proving the result.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha,\rho,r
be positive real numbers with
\rho>\frac{1}{2}
. Assume that on the line
R(s)=\alpha,L(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)n^{-s}
is absolutely convergent. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq r}c(n)h_{\rho}(n,r)=\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)L(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}r^{s}ds

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle h_{\rho}(n,r)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{r-n}{r}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{1}\log\left(1+\frac{(t-1)(n-r)}{2n}\right)t^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt.

Proof.

Here using Lemma
3.7
and Stirling’s formula
[
DLMF
, (5.11.9)]
, we deduce that the integral
\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}r^{s}ds
is absolutely convergent for
\rho>-\frac{1}{2}
and with this and the absolute convergence of
L(s)
, we can interchange the summation and integration as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)L(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}r^{s}ds=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}\left(\frac{r}{n}\right)^{s}ds

(3.4)

Let
y=\frac{r}{n}
with
y>1
. Then using Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}y^{s}ds=\int_{(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{du}{1+u}\right)\frac{y^{s}ds}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}

We will use Fubini’s theorem to swap the order of summation. To justify this, first note that we have the bound

|\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\right)|\leq\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right)<\infty

for
\alpha>0
.
So we compute

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}|\cdot|\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{y^{s}}{1+u}|duds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+iT+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+\alpha+iT)}|\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{y^{\alpha}}{1+u}dudT

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}|dT\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{du}{1+u}

Stirling’s formula
[
DLMF
, (5.11.9)]
ensures the convergence of the first integral for
\rho>\frac{1}{2}
, and the convergence of the second integral follows from the estimate
\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right)=O(u^{-1})
as
u\to\infty
.
Now applying Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}y^{s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{2u+1}}\left(s,\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{y^{s}}{1+u}duds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+2u}}t^{-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}(ty)^{s}ds\right)dt\frac{du}{1+u}

Now it is known from Section 7.3, eq. 20 of
[
BEMO54
]
that for
\gamma,a>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\gamma)}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+a)}y^{-s}ds=\frac{(1-y)^{a-1}}{\Gamma(a)}\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}(y)

where
\mathbf{1}_{A}
is the indicator function for a set A.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\rho+s)}(ty)^{s}ds=\int_{(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(v)}{\Gamma(v+\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{-(v-\frac{1}{2})}dv

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(1-\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)

for
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho>-\frac{1}{2}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}y^{s}ds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+2u}}t^{-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)dt\right)\frac{du}{1+u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{y}}^{\frac{1}{1+2u}}t^{-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}dt\right)\frac{du}{1+u}

Now by using the change of variables

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{y}}^{\frac{1}{1+2u}}t^{-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{ty}\right)^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}dt=\left(\frac{y-1}{y}\right)^{\rho}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{y-1-2u}{y-1}}\left(\rho+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}y^{s}ds=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{y-1}{y}\right)^{\rho}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{y-1-2u}{y-1}}\left(\rho+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{du}{1+u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{y-1}{y}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{B}_{\frac{y-1-2u}{y-1}}\left(\rho+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{du}{1+u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{y-1}{y}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{2u}{y-1}}t^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{dtdu}{1+u}

Here since the right hand side is absolutely convergent for

, we can swap the two integrals and get the following,

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}y^{s}ds=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{y-1}{y}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{(t-1)(1-y)}{2}}\frac{du}{1+u}\right)t^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt

Now substituting

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{Z(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}\left(\frac{r}{n}\right)^{s}ds=\frac{1}{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(\rho+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{r-n}{r}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{1}\log\left(1+\frac{(t-1)(n-r)}{2n}\right)t^{\rho-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt
