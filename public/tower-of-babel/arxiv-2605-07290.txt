A revisit via slicing method on a quadratic semilinear wave equation in two space dimensions
Masakazu Kato, Hiroyuki Takamura, Kyouhei Wakasa

Abstract
In this paper, we are focusing on proofs of a blow-up result for a quadratic semilinear wave equation in two space dimensions. There is a logarithmic loss in estimating the lifespan of a classical solution if the 0th moment of the initial speed does not vanish. This result is already known with almost sharp constants. But in order to have a direct application to the numerical analysis, we show a simple proof by iteration argument for a point-wise estimate of the solution with a slicing technique. Such a research direction can be found in the case of critical nonlinearity [ 26 ] .

2
Theorem and its proof by slicing method

The purpose of this paper is to reprove the following theorem
by iteration argument with slicing technique.

Theorem 2.1
(
[
15
,
24
]
)

Let
n=2
and
p=2
.
Assume that
f\in C_{0}^{3}({\bf R}^{2})
and
g\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R}^{2})
satisfy
\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}g(x)dx>0
.
Then, there is a positive constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f,g)
such that
a classical solution of (
1.1
) cannot exist as far as
T
satisfies

T>Ca({\varepsilon})\quad{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

where
a
is defined in (
1.7
) and
C
is a positive constant independent of
{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.1

This theorem is weaker version of Lindblad
[
15
]
as stated in Introduction.
The proof in Takamura
[
24
]
is due to a combination
with the improved Kato’s lemma on ordinary differential inequalities and
a point-wise estimate of a solution of free wave equation.
We note that the conclusion shows that

T({\varepsilon})\leq Ca({\varepsilon})

with the same
{\varepsilon}
and
C
.

From now on, we may assume that

\mbox{supp}\ (f,g)\subset\{x\in{\bf R}^{2}\ :\ |x|\leq k\},\ k\geq 1

(2.1)

without loss of generality.
It is well-known that (
2.1
) implies that

\mbox{supp}\ u\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}^{2}\times[0,T)\ :\ |x|\leq t+k\}.

(2.2)

In order to prepare all the tools for the proof,
we introduce the equivalent integral equation to a classical solution of (
1.1
)
with
n=2
and
p=2
,

u={\varepsilon}u^{0}+L(u^{2})\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}^{2}\times[0,T),

(2.3)

where
u^{0}=u^{0}(x,t)
is a solution of free wave with the initial data
(f,g)

and
L
is Duhamel’s term,

L(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{|x-y|\leq t-s}\frac{v(y,s)}{\sqrt{(t-s)^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}dy.

For a function
u=u(x,t)
, define a spherical mean of
u
at
x=0
with radius
r>0
defined by

\widetilde{u}(r,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\omega|=1}u(r\omega,t)dS_{\omega}.

First, let us employ the following lemma which will be a frame of the iteration.

Lemma 2.1
(
[
1
]
)

Let
n=2
and
p=2
.
Assume that
(f,g)\in C_{0}^{3}({\bf R}^{2})\times C_{0}^{2}({\bf R}^{2})
satisfies (
2.1
).
Then, a classical solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq{\varepsilon}\widetilde{u^{0}}(r,t)+\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{r}}\iint_{T_{r,t}}\sqrt{\lambda}\widetilde{u}(\lambda,\tau)^{2}d\lambda d\tau\quad\mbox{for}\ k\leq t-r\leq r,

(2.4)

where a domain
T_{r,t}
is defined by

T_{r,t}:=\{(\lambda,\tau)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:t-r\leq\lambda,\ \tau+\lambda\leq t+r,\ k\leq\tau-\lambda\leq t-r\}.

Proof.
See (2.12) in Agemi
[
1
]
.
(
2.4
) includes
{\varepsilon}\widetilde{u^{0}}
, but it is trivial in view of (
2.3
).

\Box

As the first step of the iteration, we need the following estimate for solution of free wave equation.

Lemma 2.2
(
[
7
,
15
,
9
]
)

Let
n=2
. Assume that
(f,g)\in C_{0}^{3}({\bf R}^{2})\times C_{0}^{2}({\bf R}^{2})
satisfies (
2.1
).
Then, there exist positive constants
D_{1},D_{2}
independent of
{\varepsilon}
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\left|u^{0}(x,t)-\frac{D_{1}}{\sqrt{t+|x|+2k}\sqrt{t-|x|+2k}}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}g(x)dx\right|\\
\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{2}}{\sqrt{t+|x|+2k}(t-|x|+2k)^{3/2}}\end{array}

(2.5)

in
\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}^{2}\times[0,\infty)\ :\ |x|\leq t+k\}
.

Remark 2.2

This lemma firstly appeared in Hörmander
[
7
]
in a partial space-time domain.
Later, Lindblad
[
15
]
refined it.
Finally, Imai, Kato, Takamura and Wakasa
[
9
]
extended it in the full domain.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.
(
2.5
) doesn’t appear in
[
9
]
explicitly,
especially in the characteristic domain denoted by
D_{\rm ext}
.
But in view of its proof, it is easy to see that (
2.5
) is established also in
D_{\rm ext}
.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.1
by slicing the blow-up set.

We note that (
2.2
) implies that

\mbox{supp}\ \widetilde{u}\subset\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)\times[0,T)\ :\ r\leq t+k\}.

We shall make the first step of the iteration.
It follows from Lemma
2.2
and the assumption
\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}g(x)dx>0

that there exist positive constants
B=B(f,g)
and
M=M(f,g)(\geq 2k)

independent of
{\varepsilon}
such that

\widetilde{u^{0}}(r,t)\geq\frac{B}{\sqrt{t+r}\sqrt{t-r}}\quad\mbox{for}\ M\leq t-r\leq r.

(2.6)

Introducing characteristic variables by

\alpha:=\tau+\lambda,\ \beta:=\tau-\lambda

into the right-hand side in (
2.4
) and making use of
t+r\geq 3(t-r)
, we have that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{D}{\sqrt{t+r}}\int_{l_{j}M}^{t-r}d\beta\int_{2(t-r)+\beta}^{3(t-r)}\sqrt{\alpha-\beta}\widetilde{u}(\lambda,\tau)^{2}d\alpha\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{j},

(2.7)

where
D:=(4\sqrt{2}\pi)^{-1}>0
and “sliced” domains
\Sigma_{j}\ (j\in{\bf N}\cup\{0\})
are defined by

\Sigma_{j}:=\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)\times[0,T)\ :\ l_{j}M\leq t-r\leq r\}

with a “slicer”

l_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{1}{2^{i}}.

We note that

\Sigma_{j+1}\subset\Sigma_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1\leq l_{j}<2

hold for
j\in{\bf N}\cup\{0\}
.

First we shall make the first step of the iteration.
Making use of a trivial inequality
\widetilde{u}\geq{\varepsilon}\widetilde{u^{0}}

and substituting
\widetilde{u}
in the right hand side of (
2.7
) with
j=0
by right hand side of (
2.6
),
we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widetilde{u}(r,t)&\displaystyle\geq\frac{DB^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\sqrt{2(t-r)}}{\sqrt{t+r}}\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}\frac{1}{\beta}d\beta\int_{2(t-r)+\beta}^{3(t-r)}\frac{1}{\alpha}d\alpha\\
&\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}DB^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}}{3\sqrt{t+r}\sqrt{t-r}}\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}\frac{t-r-\beta}{\beta}d\beta\end{array}

in
\Sigma_{0}
.
Then, the
\beta
-integral in the last line above can be estimated as follows.
The integration by parts yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}\frac{t-r-\beta}{\beta}d\beta&\displaystyle=\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{0}M}\right)^{\prime}d\beta\\
&\displaystyle=\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}\log\frac{\beta}{l_{0}M}d\beta.\end{array}

Hence it follows from
\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{0}
and

\frac{l_{0}}{l_{1}}(t-r)\geq l_{0}M\quad\mbox{for}\ (r,t)\in\Sigma_{1}

that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{l_{0}M}^{t-r}\frac{t-r-\beta}{\beta}d\beta&\displaystyle\geq\int_{l_{0}(t-r)/l_{1}}^{t-r}\log\frac{\beta}{l_{0}M}d\beta\\
&\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{l_{0}}{l_{1}}\right)(t-r)\log\frac{t-r}{l_{1}M}\end{array}

in
\Sigma_{1}
which yields that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{t+r}}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{1}M}\right)^{1/2}\log\frac{t-r}{l_{1}M}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{1},

(2.8)

where

C_{1}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}DB^{2}}{3^{2}}{\varepsilon}^{2}>0.

This estimate shows the essence of the slicing method.

Now, we assume an estimate

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{C_{j}}{\sqrt{t+r}}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j}M}\right)^{a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j}M}\right)^{b_{j}}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{j},

(2.9)

where all the positive constants
a_{j},b_{j},C_{j}
are defined later.
But in view of (
2.8
), this estimate is true for
j=1
with

a_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\quad b_{1}=1,\quad C_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}DB^{2}}{3^{2}}{\varepsilon}^{2}.

(2.10)

Substituting
\widetilde{u}
in the right hand side of (
2.7
) with
the right hand side of (
2.9
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widetilde{u}(r,t)&\displaystyle\geq\frac{DC_{j}^{2}\sqrt{2(t-r)}}{\sqrt{t+r}}\int_{l_{j}M}^{t-r}\left(\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}d\beta\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}\int_{2(t-r)+\beta}^{3(t-r)}\frac{1}{\alpha}d\alpha\\
&\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}DC_{j}^{2}}{3\sqrt{t+r}\sqrt{t-r}}\int_{l_{j}M}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)\left(\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}d\beta\end{array}

in
\Sigma_{j}
.
Hence it follows from
\Sigma_{j+1}\subset\Sigma_{j}
and

\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}(t-r)\geq l_{j}M\quad\mbox{for}\ (r,t)\in\Sigma_{j+1}

that the
\beta
-integral in the last line above can be estimated in
\Sigma_{j+1}

by slicing method again as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{l_{j}M}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)\left(\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}d\beta\\
\displaystyle\geq\int_{l_{j}(t-r)/l_{j+1}}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)\left(\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{\beta}{l_{j}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}d\beta\\
\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}\int_{l_{j}(t-r)/l_{j+1}}^{t-r}(t-r-\beta)d\beta\\
\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}(t-r)^{2}\\
\displaystyle\geq\frac{(t-r)^{2}}{2^{2j+5}}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2a_{j}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}\end{array}

because of

1-\frac{l_{j}}{l_{j+1}}=\frac{1}{2^{j+1}l_{j+1}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{j+2}}.

Summing up, we obtain that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}DC_{j}^{2}}{2^{2j+5}\cdot 3\sqrt{t+r}}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2a_{j}+\frac{3}{2}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2b_{j}}

(2.11)

in
\Sigma_{j+1}
.

Now we are in a position to define sequences
\{a_{j}\},\{b_{j}\}
by

a_{j+1}=2a_{j}+\frac{3}{2},\quad b_{j+1}=2b_{j},

which can be solved by (
2.10
) as

a_{j}=2^{j}-\frac{3}{2},\quad b_{j}=2^{j-1}\quad(j\in{\bf N}).

Hence it follows from (
2.11
) that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}DC_{j}^{2}}{2^{2j+5}\cdot 3\sqrt{t+r}}\left(\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2^{j+1}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\log\frac{t-r}{l_{j+1}M}\right)^{2^{j}}

in
\Sigma_{j+1}
.
So we have to set

C_{j+1}=\frac{E}{4^{j}}C_{j}^{2},

where

E:=\min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{2}D}{2^{5}\cdot 3}\right\}>0.

This can be solved by (
2.10
) as

C_{j+1}=\frac{E^{1+2+\cdots+2^{j-1}}C_{1}^{2^{j}}}{4^{j+2(j-1)+\cdots+2^{j-1}}},\quad C_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}DB^{2}}{3^{2}}{\varepsilon}^{2}.

We note that

j+2(j-1)+\cdots+2^{j-1}=2^{j-1}\left(\frac{j}{2^{j-1}}+\frac{j-1}{2^{j-2}}+\cdots+1\right)

and there is a positive constant
S
such that

\frac{j}{2^{j-1}}+\frac{j-1}{2^{j-2}}+\cdots+1\nearrow S\quad(j\rightarrow\infty)

by d’Alembert criterion.

Therefore we obtain that

\widetilde{u}(r,t)\geq\frac{E^{-1}}{\sqrt{t+r}}\left(\frac{t-r}{2M}\right)^{-3/2}\exp\left(2^{j}\log I(r,t)\right)\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{\infty},

where we set

\Sigma_{\infty}:=\{(r,t)\in(0,\infty)\times[0,T)\ :\ 2M\leq t-r\leq r\}

and

I(r,t):=\frac{EC_{1}}{4^{S/2}}\left(\frac{t-r}{2M}\right)^{2}\log\frac{t-r}{2M}.

If there is a point
(r_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{\infty}
such that
I(r_{0},t_{0})>1
,
then
\widetilde{u}
as well as
u
cannot be a classical solution of (
1.1
) by letting
j\rightarrow\infty
.
We easily find that one of its sufficient condition on such a
(r_{0},t_{0})
is

I(r_{0},t_{0})=B_{1}t_{0}^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\log\frac{t_{0}}{4M}>1

by setting
t_{0}=2r_{0}
and
\displaystyle B_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}DB^{2}E}{4^{S/2+2}\cdot 3^{2}M^{2}}
.

We set

\displaystyle t_{0}=B_{2}^{-1}a({\varepsilon})\quad\mbox{and}\quad a({\varepsilon}_{0})=(8M)^{2},

where
B_{2}=\min\{1,(B_{1}/4)^{1/2}\}
. From (
1.7
), we see that
a({\varepsilon})
is monotonously decreasing in
{\varepsilon}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0+0}a({\varepsilon})=\infty
. For
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{0}\geq(8M)^{2}\quad\mbox{i.e.}\quad\frac{1}{2}\log t_{0}\geq\log 8M.

It follows that

\displaystyle I(t_{0}/2,t_{0})

\displaystyle\geq B_{1}t_{0}^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\log\frac{t_{0}+1}{8M}

\displaystyle\geq 2B_{2}^{2}t_{0}^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\log(t_{0}+1)

\displaystyle\geq 2a({\varepsilon})^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\log\left(a({\varepsilon})+1\right)=2.

Therefore
u
cannot exist as far as
T
satisfies

T>B_{2}^{-1}a({\varepsilon})\quad\mbox{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

\Box

Remark 2.3

In view of the proof of the first step of the iteration,
there will be no logarithmic term in (
2.8
) if we have an estimate such as

\widetilde{u^{0}}(r,t)\geq\frac{B}{\sqrt{t+r}(t-r)^{3/2}}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Sigma_{0}

when

\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}g(x)dx=0

instead of (
2.6
).
This estimate provides us the upper bound in the second line of (
1.6
)
with a rough constant.
