Coexact completion of profinite Heyting algebras and uniform interpolation
Lingyuan Ye

Abstract
. This paper shows that the sheaf representation of finitely presented Heyting algebras given in 12 is, from an algebraic perspective, equivalent to the construction of profinite completion . We show that the dual category of profinite Heyting algebras is an infinitary extensive regular category, and its ex/reg-completion is exactly the sheaf topos constructed in loc. cit. , which we refer to as the K-topos. We show how certain properties of uniform interpolation can be generalised to the context of arbitrary profinite Heyting algebras, and are consequences of the internal logic of the K-topos. Along the way we also establish various topos-theoretic properties of the K-topos. Keywords. uniform interpolation, intuitionistic logic, Heyting algebra, profinite completion, exact completion. MSC2020. 06D20, 03C40, 03B20.

2.
The K-topos as sheaves on Kripke frames

Let
\mathbf{Pos}_{\mathrm{f.}}
be the category of finite posets. It is well-known that there is a duality between finite distributive lattices and finite posets,

\operatorname{Spec}:\mathbf{DL}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Pos}_{\mathrm{f.}}:\mathcal{U},

where
\operatorname{Spec}D=\mathbf{DL}(D,2)
is the poset of models of a finite distributive lattice, and
\mathcal{U}P
takes a finite poset
P
to the distributive lattice of upward closed subsets of
P
.

Each finite distributive lattice is in fact a complete lattice, and distributivity implies it will be a Heyting algebra (also a co-Heyting algebra). In this case, it is well-known that a map between finite poset
f:P\to Q
dually corresponds to a Heyting algebra morphism iff it is
open

p\in P
and
fp\leq q
in
Q

p\leq p^{\prime}
that
fp^{\prime}=q
. We let
\mathbf{Kp}
be the category of finite posets and open maps between them. This way, we have a duality

\operatorname{Spec}:\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Kp}:\mathcal{U},

where
\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}
is the category of finite Heyting algebras.

We thus view
\mathbf{Kp}
as the category of Kripke frames. The topos
\mathcal{K}

\mathbf{Kp}
for some suitable topology. To construct this topology, we first establish some categorical properties of

J
-covering, but we have shown in
Lemma

2.4
that
J
is stable under pullbacks, thus
S
is universally effectively epic. Hence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3).
∎

Henceforth, we will denote the sheaf topos
\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{Kp},J)
as
\mathcal{K}
, and refer to it as the
K-topos
. Since
J
is the canonical topology, the Yoneda embedding factors through
\mathcal{K}
and we have a fully faithful embedding
\text{\char 72\relax}:\mathbf{Kp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
. However, we often omit explicitly mentioning
ℍ
and identify
P\in\mathbf{Kp}
as the representable functor in
\mathcal{K}
.

Remark 2.6

(Sheaves and left exact functors)
.

By
Proposition

2.5
, if a functor
\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
preserves finite limits then it will be a sheaf. However, we will see that not all sheaves are of this form. In fact,
Section

4
will show that these sheaves exactly corresponds to profinite Heyting algebras (cf.
Remark

4.4
).

Remark 2.7

(Logical interpretation of sheaves in the K-topos)
.

A sheaf
X
in
\mathcal{K}
assigns a set
X(P)
to each Kripke frame
P\in\mathbf{Kp}
. Intuitively, this allows one to build representation for (finitely presented) Heyting algebras because we can present a Heyting algebra
A
as its
collection of models
on finite Kripke frames. Concretely, we can associate a sheaf
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A
to the Heyting algebra
A
, where
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A(P)
encodes the set of all Kripke models of
A
on the Kripke frame
P
, i.e. the set of evaluations on
P
that validates
A
. We will come back to this construction in
Section

4
.

3.
Some topos theoretic properties of the K-topos

In this section we establish some topos-theoretic properties of the K-topos
\mathcal{K}
. Many of them will have application to profinite Heyting algebras and uniform interpolation later, when we understand better the relationship between these subjects and the K-topos.

3.1.
Supercompact generation

\mathcal{K}
is
supercompactly generated
in the sense of
27
. An object
e
in a topos
\mathcal{E}
is
supercompact
if every jointly epic family of maps
\{\,e_{i}\to e\,\}_{i\in I}
contains an actual epimorphism. And
\mathcal{E}
is supercompactly generated if it has a generating family of supercompact objects.

To show
\mathcal{K}
is supercompactly generated, we consider another site presentation of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
be the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Kp}
consisting of
rooted
finite posets, i.e. those posets containing a minimum. We may look at the site
(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J)
where
J
is the topology on
\mathbf{Kp}
restricted to
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
. These two sites generate the same sheaf topos:

Lemma 3.1
.

The inclusions
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Kp}
induces equivalent sheaf topoi,

\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{Kp},J)\simeq\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J).

Proof.

By the comparison lemma (cf.
(
17
, C2.2)
), it suffices to show every object in
\mathbf{Kp}
admits a covering family of objects from
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
. For any finite poset
P
, the family of open embeddings
\{\,\operatorname{\uparrow}p\hookrightarrow P\,\}_{p\in P}
is a
J
-covering, and the domains
\operatorname{\uparrow}p
are all rooted.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

\mathcal{K}
is supercompactly generated.

Proof.

Firstly, notice that if
P
is a rooted poset, then a sieve
S
is a
J
-covering sieve on
P
iff it contains a surjection: the sieve being jointly epic implies the root of
P
lies in the image of some
f:Q\to P
, and
f
being open implies it must be a surjection. Thus, the site
(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J)
is a principle site in the sense of
(
27
, Def. 2.1.6)
, and the representables from
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
will all be supercompact in
\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J)\simeq\mathcal{K}
, which form a generating family.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If we identify
\mathcal{K}
as a subtopos of the presheaf topos
[\mathbf{Kp}_{*}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
, then the inclusion
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow[\mathbf{Kp}_{*}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
preserves arbitrary unions of subobjects.

Proof.

This follows from
(
27
, Cor. 2.1.8)
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

\mathcal{K}
is completely distributive in the sense of
5
, i.e. the subobject lattice of any sheaf in
\mathcal{K}
is a completely distributive lattice.

Proof.

This is simply due to the fact that both intersections and unions of subobjects are computed pointwise over
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
, thus they are completely distributive.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Subobject lattices in the K-topos)
.

In fact one can say more about subobject lattices in
\mathcal{K}
. Since
\mathcal{K}
is supercompactly generated, subobject lattices in
\mathcal{K}
will also be supercompactly generated as frames. By
(
6
, Thm 4.2)
, they will be lattices of upward closed subsets of certain posets.
Section

4
will give a characterisation of which posets arise this way.

3.2.
Subobject classifier

The subobject classifier
\Omega
in
\mathcal{K}
will be crucial for applications to uniform interpolation which will be discussed in
Section

5
. Thus, here we provide an explicit description of
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The subobject classifier
\Omega
of
\mathcal{K}
as a sheaf on
\mathbf{Kp}
is given by

\Omega(P)\cong\mathcal{U}P.

Proof.

It is well-known that
\Omega(P)
can be described as the set of
J
-closed sieves on
P
(cf. the proof of
(
17
, Lem. A.2.1.10)
). We show that every
J
-closed sieve
S
on
P
is generated by some open inclusion
Q\hookrightarrow P
in
\mathbf{Kp}
. Given such an
S
, consider the union of images of maps in
S
and denote it as the inclusion
i:Q\hookrightarrow P
. By construction,
i^{*}(S)
is jointly surjective on
Q
, thus is a
J
-covering. By
J
-closedness,
i\in S
, thus since every map in
S
factors through
i
,
S
is generated by
i
. This shows that
J
-closed sieves on
P
can be identified with upward closed subsets of
P
, hence
\Omega(P)\cong\mathcal{U}P
.
∎

As an easy consequence, we observe immediately that
\mathcal{K}
is 2-valued. Let
\Sigma=\{\,0<1\,\}
be the Sierpiński poset.

Corollary 3.7
.

\mathcal{K}
is two-valued, i.e.
\Omega
has exactly two global points.

Proof.

We can directly compute the global sections of
\Omega
via
Lemma

3.6
,

\mathcal{K}(1,\Omega)\cong\Omega(1)\cong\mathcal{U}1\cong\Sigma.

Thus it is two valued.
∎

Equivalently, this is saying that
\mathcal{K}
is a
hyperconnected topos
(cf.
(
17
, A4.6)
) We will meet different presentations of the subobject classifier in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we will establish more properties about the subobject classifier, and see how it connects to properties of uniform interpolation.

3.3.
The K-topos as a gros topos

\mathcal{K}
also has a
cohesive
structure à la Lawvere. In the terminology of
23
, it is a
pre-cohesive topos
with
sufficient cohesion
. Concretely, we have the following family of adjoints between
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathbf{Set}
:

Proposition 3.8
.

\mathcal{K}
is a connected, punctually locally connected, and local topos (cf.
18
). This means that there is an adjoint quadruple between
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathbf{Set}
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Set}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

Here

takes the global points of sheaves. The functors

are fully faithful and the left adjoint

of

Proof.

We use the site characterisation provided in

. Notice that

. For any covering sieve

\mathbf{Kp}_{*}

(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J)

\mathcal{K}

∎

Remark 3.9

(Concrete description of the adjoint quadruple)
.

It is also straightforward to provide a direct computation of the functors arising above. For the left adjoint

\Pi F\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{P\in\mathbf{Kp}_{*}}F(P).

. For

.

\mathcal{K}
is furthermore a “sufficiently cohesive”, or a “gros topos”, in the sense of

.

.

, i.e. for all

,

.

From

. This holds because we have

4.
Nerve construction over the K-topos

The goal of this section is to explain the relation between the sheaf representation of Heyting algebras as sheaves in the K-topos and their profinite completion. As mentioned in
Section

1
, we will follow a categorical approach by describing the
nerve
construction over the K-topos.

The K-topos
\mathcal{K}\simeq\mathbf{Sh}(\mathbf{Kp},J)
as sheaf topos over the canonical topology on
\mathbf{Kp}
is in particular a cocompletion of
\mathbf{Kp}
, though not the free one. Via left Kan extension, suitable functors
\mathbf{Kp}\to\mathcal{E}
will induce nerve adjunctions between
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{K}
.

Definition 4.1

(Representably continuous and left exact functors)
.

A functor
F:\mathbf{Kp}\to\mathcal{E}
is
representably
J
-continuous
, if for any
e\in\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}(F-,e):\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
is a
J
-sheaf on
\mathbf{Kp}
. If
\mathcal{E}(F-,e)
additionally preserves all finite limits, then we say it is
representably left exact
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
F:\mathbf{Kp}\to\mathcal{E}
be a representably
J
-continuous functor into a cocomplete category. Then there is an induced adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Kp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{K}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
ℍ
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where
L
is obtained by left Kan extension
L\cong\mathsf{lan}_{\text{\char 72\relax}}F
, and
N
is the nerve
Ne\cong\mathcal{E}(F-,e)
.

Proof.

By assumption
N
is well-defined since each
Ne
is a
J
-sheaf for any
e\in\mathcal{E}
. Since
L\cong\mathsf{lan}_{\text{\char 72\relax}}F
is a left Kan extension into a cocomplete category, it is computed pointwise,

LF\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{P\in\mathbf{Kp}}F(P).

This way, for any
F\in\mathcal{K}
and
e\in\mathcal{E}
we have the following natural isomorphisms,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(LF,e)

\displaystyle\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{x\in F(P)}\mathcal{E}(FP,e)

\displaystyle\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{x\in F(P)}\mathcal{K}(P,Ne)

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{K}(F,Ne)

The first and third isomorphisms holds by the colimit formula of
LF
, and the second isomorphism holds by Yoneda. Thus, we have an adjunction
L\dashv N
.
∎

Notice that since
L
is the left Kan extension along the fully faithful embedding
ℍ
, we in fact have
L\text{\char 72\relax}\cong F
. The remaining of this section proceeds to discuss various important examples of the nerve construction over
\mathcal{K}
.

4.1.
Nerve for the dual category of profinite Heyting algebras

Let
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})
be the category of profinite Heyting algebras, viz. the pro-completion of the category of finite Heyting algebras
\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}
. By
(
16
, Ch. VI.2.10)
,
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})
also has a concrete description as the category of internal Heyting algebras in the category of Stone spaces. As mentioned in
Section

1
, the dual category
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
is a reflective subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
, and we describe this reflective adjunction as a special case of the nerve adjunction given in
Proposition

4.2
.

We first mention a more concrete description of the dual category of profinite Heyting algebras. Notice that by construction we have the following equivalences,

\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Ind}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}})\simeq\mathbf{Ind}(\mathbf{Kp}).

In fact, it is not hard to directly compute the ind-completion of
\mathbf{Kp}
.
3
provides a concrete description of
\mathbf{Ind}(\mathbf{Kp})
as the category of
image finite posets
and open maps between them. A poset
X
is image finite if for all
x\in X
, the set
\operatorname{\uparrow}x
is finite. We will write
\mathbf{IKp}
for the category of image finite posets with bounded morphisms between them. In this concrete description, the duality between profinite Heyting algebras and image finite posets are given by

\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}:\mathcal{J}.

Here
\mathcal{U}
takes an image finite poset to its lattice of upward closed sets, while
\mathcal{J}
takes a profinite Heyting algebra to the dual poset of completely join-irreducible elements.

Since
\mathbf{Kp}
has finite colimits, the ind-completion
\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Ind}(\mathbf{Kp})
is locally finitely presentable, thus in particular cocomplete, and the inclusion
\mathbf{Kp}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{IKp}
preserves finite colimits. Thus, for any
X\in\mathbf{IKp}
the functor
\mathbf{IKp}(-,X):\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
preserves finite limits, which means the inclusion
\mathbf{Kp}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{IKp}
is representably left exact. By
Proposition

4.2
, we get an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{IKp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left|-\right|}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

As promised, we show this adjunction is
reflective
:

Proposition 4.3
.

The nerve adjunction between
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{IKp}
and
\mathcal{K}
is reflective.

Proof.

For this proof let
N:\mathbf{IKp}\to\mathcal{K}
be the right adjoint. To show the nerve adjunction is reflective it suffices to show
N
is fully faithful. To this end, let
X,Y\in\mathbf{IKp}
. By construction,

\mathcal{K}(N(X),N(Y))\cong\int_{P\in\mathbf{Kp}}\mathbf{Set}(\mathbf{IKp}(P,X),\mathbf{IKp}(P,Y)).

On the other hand, since
\mathbf{Kp}
has finite colimits, we also have the following equivalences

\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Ind}(\mathbf{Kp})\simeq\mathbf{Lex}(\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}),

where as a full subcategory of
[\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
, an object
X\in\mathbf{IKp}
is exactly identified as the functor
P\mapsto\mathbf{IKp}(P,X)
. This implies that

\int_{P\in\mathbf{Kp}}\mathbf{Set}(\mathbf{IKp}(P,X),\mathbf{IKp}(P,Y))\cong\mathbf{Lex}(\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set})(X,Y)\cong\mathbf{IKp}(X,Y),

thus
N
is fully faithful.
∎

Remark 4.4

(Image finite posets as left exact sheaves)
.

By the proof of
Proposition

4.3
, the inclusion
\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
is the unique factorisation of two reflective subcategories of the presheaf category
[\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{IKp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]}}

where
\mathbf{IKp},\mathcal{K}
corresponds to left exact and
J
-continuous functors out of
\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}}
, respectively. From now on we will identify
\mathbf{IKp}
as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
. The following are equivalent for a sheaf
F
in
\mathcal{K}
:

•

F\cong NX
for an essentially unique
X\in\mathbf{IKp}
;

•

F:\mathbf{Kp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
preserves finite limits;

•

The category of elements
\int\!\!F
is cofiltered.

Remark 4.5

(The nerve of profinite Heyting algebras)
.

Via the dual equivalence

\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}:\mathcal{J},

for any profinite Heyting algebra
A
its nerve in
\mathcal{K}
is given by

N\mathcal{J}A(P)\cong\mathbf{IKp}(P,\mathcal{J}A)\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})(A,\mathcal{U}P).

Example 4.6

(Subobject classifier as a profinite Heyting algebra or an image finite poset)
.

Let

be the free Heyting algebra on one generator. Concretely, it is the

). It is well-known that

). By

, its nerve is given by

The final isomorphism is calculated in

3.6
, where

is the subobject classifier in
\mathcal{K}
. This shows that

belongs to the reflective subcategory
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
. Under the equivalence
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{IKp}
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}

.

4.2.
Sheaf representation of Heyting algebras as a nerve

As mentioned in
Section

1
,
12
constructed a sheaf representation functor of Heyting algebras,

\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}:\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{K},

which takes any Heyting algebra
A
to the following sheaf

\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A(P)=\mathbf{HA}(A,\mathcal{U}P).

In other words,
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A(P)
is exactly the set of Kripke models of
A
over the finite Kripke frame
P
. We first notice that this is a special case of the nerve construction:

Lemma 4.12
.

The functor
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}:\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{K}
described above is the nerve induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{Kp}\simeq\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

Notice that
\mathbf{HA}
is complete, thus
\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
is cocomplete. Furthermore, for any Heyting algebra
A
, the functor

\mathbf{HA}(A,-):\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}\to\mathbf{Set}

preserves finite limits, since representable functors preserve any limits and finite limits in
\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}
are computed as in
\mathbf{HA}
. Under the equivalence
\mathbf{Kp}\simeq\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}}
, this shows the inclusion

\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{Kp}\simeq\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}

is representably left exact. By construction, its nerve takes
P\in\mathbf{Kp}
to the set

\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathcal{U}P,A)\cong\mathbf{HA}(A,\mathcal{U}P)\cong\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A(P).

This shows
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}
is indeed the nerve functor for the inclusion
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{Kp}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
.
∎

Since
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{Kp}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
is in fact representably left exact,
Proposition

4.11
shows that this nerve functor factors through
\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
, and the following is straightforward:

Theorem 4.13
.

The sheaf representation functor
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}:\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{K}
factors through the reflective subcategory
\mathbf{IKp}
. Under the equivalence
\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
, this is exactly the dual of the profinite completion functor

\widehat{-}:\mathbf{HA}\to\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}}).

In other words, the following diagram commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{IKp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{J}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

Proof.

We first show that the left adjoint of
\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}:\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{IKp}
is
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{IKp}\to\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
, which takes the upward closed subsets of an image finite poset. For any Heyting algebra

and any

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{IKp}(X,\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}A)

\displaystyle\cong\int_{P\in\mathbf{Kp}}\mathbf{Set}(X(P),\mathbf{HA}(A,\mathcal{U}P))

3.6
; the final one holds since subobjects of

in
\mathbf{IKp}
are exactly given by its upward closed subsets. This implies

is indeed the left adjoint of

Now under the equivalence
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.p.}})^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{IKp}
, this implies the induced left adjoint is simply the inclusion
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Notice that the profinite completion functor is left adjoint to the forgetful functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{HA}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

By the uniqueness of adjoints, under the equivalence

must thus be equivalent to the dual of the profinite completion.
∎

Remark 4.14

(Sheaf representation as profinite completion)
.

Now we can complete our promise in
Section

1
. In
12
, one studies a Heyting algebra
A
by mapping it into the subobject lattice of its sheaf representation,

By

, this map is exactly the counit of the adjunction
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{IKp}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{HA}^{\mathrm{op}}:\operatorname{\overline{Spec}}
. Furthermore, under the equivalence
\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
, this counit is equivalently the unit for the adjunction
\widehat{-}:\mathbf{HA}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})
, i.e. the map is given by the profinite completion of the Heyting algebra

,

If

is finitely presented, then by the finite model property of IPC, this map will indeed be an embedding. Thus, by the result in

, the uniform interpolation can be indeed obtained via restricting the adjoints between profinite Heyting algebras.

Remark 4.15

(Bellissima’s representation of Heyting algebras)
.

In fact, the nerve functor

also agrees with Bellissima’s representation of finitely generated free Heyting algebras given in

, the

constructed a poset, in fact an image finite poset, where

5.1.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi(x)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

. Thus,

holds.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

For any profinite Heyting algebra

. For the claim on finitely presented Heyting algebras, recall that the uniform interpolation is the restriction of the corresponding adjoints between their profinite completion. In particular, for any finitely presented Heyting algebra

, we have

5.2.
The subobject classifier is internally irreducible

Next, we will establish that the relevant right adjoints of maps between profinite Heyting algebras in fact preserves finite joins. As we will see, this is a consequence of
\Omega
being
internally irreducible
in
\mathcal{K}
. However, unlike the case of injectivity which holds for
\Omega
in a general topos, this is a property special to
\mathcal{K}
. To show this we first need some preliminary results on Heyting algebras.

Definition 5.5

(Local Heyting algebra)
.

A Heyting algebra
A
is
local
if it is non-trivial, and for any
a,b\in A
,
a\vee b=1
iff
a=1
or
b=1
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
A
be a Heyting algebra, and suppose the canonical inclusion
A\rightarrowtail A[\mathsf{x}]
into the free Heyting algebra over
A
generated by one object has a right adjoint
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}:A[\mathsf{x}]\to A
. In this case if
A
is local, then so is
A[\mathsf{x}]
.

Proof.

We apply a glueing argument. Since
A\rightarrowtail A[\mathsf{x}]
is an inclusion,
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}
is a retract, i.e.
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}a=a
for
a\in A
. Thus will identify
A
as a subalgebra of
A[\mathsf{x}]
. Now consider the following comma square,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{A[\mathsf{x}]}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A[\mathsf{x}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}}

Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}
is a right adjoint, it preserves all finite meets. Thus the comma is the lattice-theoretic construction of Artin glueing (cf.
29
). This way,
\widetilde{A[\mathsf{x}]}
is again a Heyting algebra and
r
is a Heyting algebra morphism. Elements in in
\widetilde{A[\mathsf{x}]}
are simply pairs
(\varphi,a)
such that
a\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}\varphi
. The maps

are simply projections. The meets and joins in
\widetilde{A[\mathsf{x}]}
are pointwise, while the implication is given by

(\varphi,a)\to(\psi,b)=(\varphi\to\psi,(a\to b)\wedge\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}(\varphi\to\psi)).

This way, the map

is a Heyting algebra morphism

, thus by the universal property of

, the element

induces a section

of

. Since this is a section, for any

we also write

.

Now we proceed to show

is local. Evidently

in
A[\mathsf{x}]
if
A
is non-trivial. Suppose
\varphi\vee\psi=1
. This way,

s(\varphi\vee\psi)=s\varphi\vee s\psi=1.

But since
A
is local, it follows that
s\varphi=1
or
s\psi=1
. Without loss of generality say
s\varphi=1
, then by construction we have

s\varphi=1\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}\varphi\Rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}\varphi=1.

In particular,
1\leq\varphi
in
A[\mathsf{x}]
, thus
\varphi=1
.
∎

Remark 5.7

(Preservation of locality by polynomial algebras)
.

Given
Proposition

5.6
, the existence of the right adjoint
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}:A[\mathsf{x}]\to A
is indeed desirable. If
A
is finite, then the existence of this right adjoint is automatic, since it can be defined by a finite join. In general, such right adjoint indeed exists for
all
Heyting algebras, and is a consequence of uniform interpolation (cf.
(
26
, Cor. 15)
). However, we make this assumption explicit in
Proposition

5.6
because this result will later be used in the proof of
Proposition

5.8
for finite Heyting algebras, and we do not want the latter to depend on the proof of uniform interpolation for IPC.

Proposition 5.8
.

\Omega
in
\mathcal{K}
is internally irreducible:
\Omega
is inhabited, and

\mathcal{K}\models\forall\varphi,\psi\!\in\!\mathcal{P}(\Omega).\ \varphi\cup\psi=\Omega\to\varphi=\Omega\vee\psi=\Omega.

Proof.

\Omega
being inhabited is evident, since it has a global point. For irreducibility under binary union, by the Kripke-Joyal semantics (cf.
21
), it suffices to show for any
P\in\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
and any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)(P)
, we have

\varphi\vee\psi=1\Rightarrow\varphi=1\vee\psi=1.

In other words, we need to show
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)(P)
is a local Heyting algebra for any
P\in\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
. Notice that we have the following isomorphisms,

\mathcal{P}(\Omega)(P)\cong\mathcal{K}(P\times\Omega,\Omega)\cong\mathcal{U}(P\times\Omega).

By the duality
\mathcal{U}:\mathbf{IKp}\simeq\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}
, we have

\mathcal{U}(P\times\Omega)\cong\mathcal{U}P\widehat{\oplus}\mathcal{U}\Omega\cong\mathcal{U}P\widehat{\oplus}H[\mathsf{x}]\cong\mathcal{U}P[\widehat{\mathsf{x}}]\cong\widehat{\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]}.

Now notice that
\mathcal{U}P
is local since
P\in\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
is rooted.
\mathcal{U}P
is a finite Heyting algebra thus the right adjoint
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}:\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]\to\mathcal{U}P
exists. Thus by
Proposition

5.6
, the polynomial algebra
\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]
is also local. Let us write
\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]
as a quotient of a finitely generated free Heyting algebra. Since
\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]
is finitely presented, there exists some
n
and
\varphi\in H[n]
that

H[n]\twoheadrightarrow H[n]/\varphi\cong\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}].

Since
\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]
is local,
\varphi
must be join-irreducible in
H[n]
. By
(
8
, Cor. 7.2)
, this implies
\varphi\in\widehat{H[n]}
is also join-irreducible. Observe that
\widehat{\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]}
is also the following quotient,

\widehat{\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]}\cong\widehat{H[n]/\varphi}\cong\widehat{H[n]}/\varphi.

This way, it follows that
\widehat{\mathcal{U}P[\mathsf{x}]}\cong\mathcal{P}(\Omega)(P)
is indeed local.
∎

Remark 5.9

(An alternative way to show irreducibility)
.

In the proof of
Proposition

5.8
we cite a result in
8
, that essentially allows us to show for a finitely presented Heyting algebra
A
, if
A
is local then so is its profinite completion
\widehat{A}
. However, we can also show the result in
Proposition

5.8
via a direct translation using Kripke-Joyal semantics to a statement about finite posets.

Corollary 5.10
.

For any profinite Heyting algebra
A
, the right adjoint
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}:A[\widehat{\mathsf{x}}]\to A
to the canonical inclusion
A\rightarrowtail A[\widehat{\mathsf{x}}]
preserves finite joins. In particular, the right uniform interpolation for finitely presented Heyting algebras preserves finite joins.

Proof.

We first show for any object
X\in\mathcal{K}
, it holds internally in
\mathcal{K}
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\models

\displaystyle\,\forall\varphi,\psi\!\in\!\mathcal{P}(X\times\Omega).\ \forall x\!\in\!X.\

\displaystyle\,\forall p\!\in\!\Omega.\ (\varphi(x,p)\vee\psi(x,p))\to(\forall p\!\in\!\Omega.\ \varphi(x,p)\vee\forall p\!\in\!\Omega.\ \psi(x,p)).

We reason constructively in the internal logic of
\mathcal{K}
. Suppose we have
\varphi,\psi\subseteq X\times\Omega
and
x\in X
. Consider the following subsets,

U=\{\,p:\Omega\mid\varphi(x,p)\,\},\quad V=\{\,p:\Omega\mid\psi(x,p)\,\}.

Now by assumption
U\cup V=\Omega
. By
Proposition

5.8
,
U=\Omega
or
V=\Omega
, thus the desired claim holds. Again by
Lemma

5.1
, the externalisation of this internal property for
X=\mathcal{J}A
exactly shows
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}:A[\widehat{\mathsf{x}}]\to A
preserves finite joins. The claim on uniform interpolation follows from a similar argument as in
Corollary

5.4
.
∎

6.
Subtopoi of the K-topos

In this section we digress a bit from our main investigation and classify all subtopoi of
\mathcal{K}
. Our strategy is to calculate explicitly those maps

in
\mathcal{K}
that constitutes a Lawvere-Tierney topology. As a start, we can easily classify all endo-morphisms of
\Omega

Lemma 6.1
.

\mathcal{K}(\Omega,\Omega)\cong H[\mathsf{x}]

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{K}}\cong\mathcal{K}(-,\Omega):\mathcal{K}\to\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f.}})^{\mathrm{op}}

\Omega\cong\mathcal{J}H[\mathsf{x}]

∎

Thus, our goal is to characterise those elements in

.

Remark 6.6

(Perfectness and scatteredness)
.

In an unpublished note of Johnstone, a notion of
perfect topos
is defined (a related notion is discussed in
24
as well). Perfectness is in some sense a complementary notion to
scatteredness
introduced in
11
. Recall from
loc. cit.
that a topos is scattered iff it has a Boolean dense open subtopos. One may define a topos
\mathcal{E}
to be perfect iff
its only Boolean open subtopos is degenerate
. In particular, if a topos is both perfect and scattered then it is degenerate. As a corollary of
Theorem

6.5
,
\mathcal{K}
will be perfect
: itself is not Boolean, and its only non-trivial subtopos
\mathbf{Set}
, though being Boolean, is not open by
Corollary

3.7
that
\mathcal{K}
is two-valued.

7.
The K-topos as an exact completion

In this last section we will show one of the main results in this paper, which is that the
\mathcal{K}
topos is the ex/reg-completion of the dual category of profinite Heyting algebras. Here the ex/reg-completion is the universal embedding of a regular category into an exact category (cf.
7
). For convenience, in this section we will mainly work with the dual category
\mathbf{IKp}
. We will first show that the inclusion
\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
identifies it as a reflective subcategory closed under small coproducts and image factorisation. In particular,
\mathbf{IKp}
will be an infinitary extensive regular category, and the structures are created by the inclusion
\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
. We will then show that the additional objects in
\mathcal{K}
can all be written as effective quotients in
\mathbf{IKp}
, thus delivering the main result.

We first show
\mathbf{IKp}
is closed under coproducts:

Lemma 7.1
.

\mathbf{IKp}
is closed under small coproducts in
\mathcal{K}
, hence it is infinitary extensive.

Proof.

Here we use the site presentation
(\mathbf{Kp}_{*},J)
of
\mathcal{K}
. Suppose we have a family
\{\,X_{i}\,\}_{i\in I}
in
\mathbf{IKp}
. Let
\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
be their disjoint union. It is easy to see that this is their coproduct in
\mathbf{IKp}
. Now for any
P\in\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
,

\mathbf{IKp}(P,\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i})\cong\coprod_{i\in I}\mathbf{IKp}(P,X_{i}),

since
P
by definition is rooted, thus any map
P\to\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
factors through a unique
X_{i}
. By
Corollary

3.3
, coproducts in
\mathcal{K}
are computed pointwise on
\mathbf{Kp}_{*}
, thus this shows the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
is exactly the coproduct
\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
in
\mathcal{K}
, hence
\mathbf{IKp}
is closed under coproducts in
\mathcal{K}
.
∎

For image factorisation, we first show that epis in
\mathbf{IKp}
and
\mathcal{K}
agree:

.

, which means locally we can find sections of

. Let

, we may factor

. This way we obtain the following diagram,

. Now given

in
\mathbf{IKp}
, let

be the set-theoretic image of

. Since

.

also agrees with that in

.

\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}

is an accessible reflective subcategory of

.

Proof.

By

, as an ind-completion

, filtered colimits in

.

, its coequaliser in
\mathbf{IKp}
is

. This means

. This can already be seen in the fact that the Yoneda embedding

.

Remark 7.5

(Intuition of ex/reg-completion)
.

To help the reader build intuition on ex/reg-completion, we here mention another instance. Let
\mathbf{Stone}
and
\mathbf{KHaus}
be the category of Stone spaces and compact Hausdorff spaces, respectively. The canonical inclusion
\mathbf{Stone}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{KHaus}
is again an ex/reg-completion (cf.
22
). Though
\mathbf{Stone}
is cocomplete, not all equivalence relations have effective quotients. For instance, the closed interval
[0,1]
admits a close surjection from the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}\twoheadrightarrow[0,1]
, whose kernel pair
R\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{N}}
will be an equivalence relation in
\mathbf{Stone}
. However, the coequaliser of
R\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbf{Stone}
is the singleton space
1
, since
[0,1]
is connected. It implies that this equivalence relation in
\mathbf{Stone}
is not effective. By adding effective quotients to all equivalence relations in
\mathbf{Stone}
, one obtains all compact Hausdorff spaces.

Similarly, though
\mathbf{IKp}
is cocomplete, not all equivalence relations in
\mathbf{IKp}
has effective quotients. By adding these missing quotients to
\mathbf{IKp}
, one obtains the K-topos
\mathcal{K}
.

Theorem 7.6
.

The inclusion
\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
identifies
\mathcal{K}
as the ex/reg-completion of
\mathbf{IKp}
.

Proof.

We have shown that the inclusion
\mathbf{IKp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
is a fully faithful regular functor. According to the characterisation of ex/reg-completion in
7
, it suffices to show that every object in
\mathcal{K}
is the effective quotient of an equivalence relation in
\mathbf{IKp}
. In fact, it suffices to show for any
F\in\mathcal{K}

from

, since by

4.7

\mathbf{IKp}
is closed under subobjects, and the kernel
R\rightarrowtail X\times X
of

as a subobject of the product will belong to
\mathbf{IKp}
, thus
F
becomes the effective quotients of the equivalence relation
R\rightrightarrows X
in
\mathbf{IKp}
.

Now let

, it lifts to the domain by construction,
