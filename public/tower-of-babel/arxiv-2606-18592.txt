Global bounded solutions for a class of generalized Hillen-Painter models near Couette flow in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Yubo Chen, Wendong Wang, Guoxu Yang, Yi Zhang

Abstract
. We investigate the global well-posedness of a class of generalized Hillen–Painter systems—specifically, supercritical volume-filling chemotaxis models—in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} under the influence of Couette flow. It is well established that, in the absence of fluid flow, solutions to this system may develop finite-time singularities (blow-up) for arbitrary initial cell mass. It is proved that the introduction of a Couette flow with sufficiently large amplitude guarantees the global existence of solutions for all initial masses. By employing a novel frequency decomposition technique, we successfully remove the mass threshold limitation presented in previous studies on the domain 𝕋 × ℝ \mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R} (Wang et al., Commun. Contemp. Math.), thereby establishing global regularity in the whole space without any smallness assumptions.



Keywords:
Hillen-Painter system; chemotaxis system; stability; Couette flow; blow-up suppression

Mathematics Subject Classification:
35B44; 35Q92; 92C17

1.
Introduction

Consider the following Patlak–Keller–Segel (PKS) system coupled with the Navier–Stokes (NS) equations in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}n+v\cdot\nabla n=\nabla\cdot\left(D(n)\nabla n\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(S(n)\nabla c\right),\\
-\Delta c=n-\alpha c,\\
\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\Delta v+n\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot v=0,\end{array}\right.

where
n
denotes the microorganism density,
c
the chemoattractant density and
v
the velocity of the fluid. Moreover,
P
is the scalar pressure,
\alpha\geq 0
and
\phi
is a given potential function accounting the effects of external forces. For simplicity, assume that
\phi(x,y)=y
.
Moreover,
D(n)
represents self-diffusion and
S(n)
denotes chemotactic sensitivity, which originate from a series of papers of Hillen-Painter (for example, see
[
18
,
26
]
) by incorporation of a population-sensing (or “quorum-sensing”) mechanism, where they assumed that

(1.2)

D(n)=Q(n)-nQ^{\prime}(n),\quad S(n)=nQ(n),

and
Q(n)
states that the probability of a jump into a site decreases linearly with the cell density at that site.

If
v=0
,
\phi=0
,
D\equiv 1
and
S\equiv id
, the system (
1.1
) is reduced to the classical Patlak–Keller–Segel system, and the origin of the PKS model is a classic narrative of interdisciplinary science derived by Patlak
[
27
]
and Keller–Segel
[
20
]
.
For the initial mass
M:=\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{1}}<8\pi
, Blanchet–Dolbeault–Perthame
[
4
]
proved the existence of bounded solutions; when
M=8\pi
, Blanchet–Carrillo–Masmoudi
[
3
]
used the free energy functional to prove that infinite-time aggregation occurs; when
M>8\pi
, the solution blows up with delta Dirac formation, and the relevant proof was provided in
[
30
,
31
]
. Wei
[
36
]
unified and extended these results to the critical case, proving that a global mild solution exists if and only if
M\leq 8\pi
. For the related progress and higher-dimensional settings, we refer the reader to
[
25
,
5
,
8
]
and the references therein.

For
v=0
, the general rates
D
and
S
of diffusion and cross-diffusion essentially exhibit asymptotic behavior of the form

(1.3)

D(n)\simeq n^{p},\quad S(n)\simeq n^{q},\quad n\simeq\infty,

where
p\in\mathbb{R},q\in\mathbb{R}
.
For the system of the form (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, whether blow-up occurs depends on a critical exponent. It was established in
[
28
]
by Sugiyama-Kunii that the critical threshold is
p=q-\frac{2}{N}
: global weak solutions exist when
p>q-\frac{2}{N}
, whereas blow-up may occur for
p\leq q-\frac{2}{N}
with sufficiently large initial data. The supercritical case
1<p<q-\frac{2}{N}
is analyzed in
[
24
]
by Luckhaus-Sugiyama, where the solution behaves like “the Barenblatt solution” asymptotically as
t\rightarrow\infty
. In bounded domains, Winkler–Djie
[
37
]
established a similar critical threshold: the solutions are globally bounded when
p>q-\frac{2}{N}
, and finite-time blow-up occurs when
p<q-\frac{2}{N}
. In
[
15
]
, Ding-Winkler investigated the asymptotic behavior of the radial solution as
n\rightarrow\infty
and showed that finite-time blow-up occurs if

(1.4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S(\xi)}{D(\xi)\xi^{\frac{2}{N}}}>0.

For more references on this topic, we refer to Tao–Winkler
[
29
]
and the references therein.

As shown in
[
16
]
, chemotaxis typically occurs in fluid environments and is subject to fluid advection. We focus on whether the mixing effect of fluid flow can suppress finite-time blow-up. First, substantial progress has been made in using fluid flows to suppress chemotactic blow-up via fluid advection. Bedrossian–He
[
2
]
studied the suppression of blow-up by strong shear flows in
\mathbb{T}^{d}(d=2\;\rm or\;3)
: in the 3D case, when
M<8\pi
, a sufficiently strong shear flow can suppress solution blow-up; however, for
M>8\pi
, finite-time blow-up solutions still exist. Kiselev–Xu
[
21
]
provided a blow-up example and demonstrated that blow-up can be suppressed by stationary relaxation-enhancing flows and Yao–Zlatos flows in
\mathbb{T}^{d}(d=2\;\rm or\;3)
. He
[
17
]
studied the suppression of blow-up by using a large strictly monotone shear flow on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. Deng–Shi–Wang
[
13
]
considered the blow-up suppression in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by Green’s method.
Second, for the 2D PKS system coupled with the fluid equations, Zeng–Zhang–Zi
[
38
]
studied the PKS–NS system near Couette flow in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. Li–Xiang–Xu
[
22
]
suppressed the blow-up for the PKS–NS system via the Poiseuille flow in the same domain. Hu–Kiselev
[
19
]
suppressed blow-up in a PKS–coupled Stokes–Boussinesq system by using strong buoyancy effects. In 2D whole space, Chen–Wang–Yang
[
6
]
investigated the suppression of blow-up of solutions to the PKS(–NS) system by a large Couette flow, where they established a precise relationship between the amplitude
A
of the Couette flow and the initial data. Moreover, for the suppression of blow-up for the 3D PKS–NS system via Couette flow, we refer to the following works: Cui–Wang–Wang
[
12
]
and Cui–Wang–Wang–Wei–Yang
[
9
]
in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}
; Cui–Wang–Wang
[
11
]
in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
; Cui–Wang–Wang–Wei
[
10
]
in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and the references therein.

Motivated by the above progress, a natural question is whether the shear flow can suppress the blow-up of the supercritical system (
1.1
) when
Q(n)
behaves as in (
1.2
). By assuming that the chemotactic
response is switched off at high cell densities, Hillen-Painter in
[
18
]
showed that the response to high population
densities prevents overcrowding, and they proved local and global existence in time of
classical solutions when
Q(n)=1-n
.
For
Q(n)=n+1
, Wang–Wang–Zhang
[
32
]
proved that a sufficiently large-amplitude shear flow can suppress blow-up of solutions in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, while this result holds for
M<\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}
. However, the sharp critical mass threshold is still unknown. Next we investigate this issue and focus on the system (
1.1
) with a general form of
Q(n)=\kappa n+\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and we call it
a generalized Hillen-Painter model
.
Introduce the Couette flow, which generates horizontal advection and induces vertical diffusion. It is easy to verify that
(\tilde{n},\tilde{c},\tilde{v})=(0,0,U)
with the Couette flow
U=(Ay,0)
is a stationary solution to the system (
1.1
). Denote the perturbation
u(t,x,y)=v(t,x,y)-U(y)
, then
(n,c,u)
satisfies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}n+Ay\partial_{x}n+u\cdot\nabla n-\Delta n=-\nabla\cdot\left[n\left(\kappa n+\mu\right)\nabla c\right],\\
-\Delta c=n-\alpha c,\\
\partial_{t}u+Ay\partial_{x}u+\binom{Au_{2}}{0}+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p=\binom{0}{n},\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
\left.(n,\,u)\right|_{t=0}=(n_{\mathrm{in}},\,u_{\mathrm{in}}),\end{array}\right.

where
\kappa,\mu
are constants. Define

\omega=\partial_{y}u_{1}-\partial_{x}u_{2},\quad u=\nabla^{\perp}\Phi=\left(\partial_{y}\Phi,-\partial_{x}\Phi\right),

then

\Delta\Phi=\omega

and
\omega
satisfies

\partial_{t}\omega+Ay\partial_{x}\omega-\Delta\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=-\partial_{x}n.

After the time rescaling
t\mapsto A^{-1}t
, one rewrites the system (
1.5
) by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}n+y\partial_{x}n-\frac{1}{A}\Delta n=-\frac{1}{A}(\nabla\cdot\left[n\left(\kappa n+\mu\right)\nabla c\right]+u\cdot\nabla n),\\
-\Delta c=n-\alpha c,\\
\partial_{t}\omega+y\partial_{x}\omega-\frac{1}{A}\Delta\omega=-\frac{1}{A}\left(\partial_{x}n+u\cdot\nabla\omega\right),\\
u=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega,\\
\left.(n,\,u)\right|_{t=0}=(n_{\mathrm{in}},\,u_{\mathrm{in}}).\end{array}\right.

1.1.
Main result

To state the main theorem, we first introduce some notation. Denote
D_{x}:=-i\partial_{x}
, understood as a Fourier multiplier operator. All norms involving
D_{x}
are defined on the Fourier side and
\langle\cdot\rangle:=\sqrt{1+(\cdot)^{2}}
. For
t\geq 0
and certain smooth enough
f
, define the anisotropic norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}:=\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}},

and the space-time norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{a}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}f\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A}\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{1}{3}}f\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\partial_{x}\nabla\Delta^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}<m
, assume that the initial data
u_{\rm in}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
0\leq n_{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and
\omega_{\mathrm{in}}=\operatorname{curl}u_{\mathrm{in}}
satisfying
\|\omega_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{1/3}n_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}<\infty.

There exists a positive constant
C^{*}
depending on
\epsilon,m,\kappa,\mu
and
\alpha

such that if

A>C^{*}\left(\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\chi_{|\kappa|+|\mu|>0}+M^{2}\chi_{|\mu|>0}+\|\omega_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}^{2}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}n_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}^{2}+1\right)^{3+12\chi_{|\kappa|>0}+6\chi_{|\mu|>0}-3\chi_{|\kappa||\mu|>0}},

where
\chi
denotes the characteristic function, the solutions to (
1.6
) are global in time satisfying

\displaystyle\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}\leq C\left(\|\omega_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}n_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}+1\right),

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{6}+M^{6}+\|\omega_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}^{6}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}n_{\rm in}\|_{Y_{m,\epsilon}}^{6}+1\right)

for all
t\geq 0
, where
0<a<\frac{1}{16(1+2\pi)}
and
C
depends on
\epsilon,m,\kappa,\mu
and
\alpha
. Additionally,
\epsilon>\frac{1}{3}
is required when
\alpha=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

It is worth noting that the supercritical volume-filling chemotaxis system (
\kappa>0
) can exhibit blow-up for arbitrarily initial mass in the absence of fluid flow (as established in Theorem 1.2
[
34
]
). Here we investigated a generalized Hillen–Painter type model coupled with the Navier–Stokes equations under the influence of a strong Couette flow. For
\kappa=\mu=\alpha=1
, Wang–Wang–Zhang
[
32
]
had proven that a sufficiently large Couette flow can suppress blow-up of (
1.6
) in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
M<\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}
is necessary. In fact, the zero mode
n_{0}
defined by

P_{0}n=n_{0}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}n(t,x,y)dx
satisfying

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\dfrac{2}{A}\|\partial_{y}n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\dfrac{2}{3A}\int_{\mathbb{R}}n_{0}^{4}dy+\cdots

with the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|n_{0}\|_{L^{4}}\leq C_{*}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{y}n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

implies that
\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
must be small (for full details, see Lemma 3.6 in
[
32
]
), which seems necessary.
In contrast, our result in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
removes this restriction on the initial mass
M
. It seems surprising that the critical mass threshold may depend on the different domains.

Remark 1.3
.

To handle horizontal convolutions in nonlinear terms, we adopt the frequency decomposition strategy from
[
23
]
. Specifically, we partition the integration domain into three regions (for example, see (
3.12
)) based on the interactions among
k
,
l
, and
k-l
:

•

Near-resonant region
(
\frac{1}{2}|k-l|\leq|k|\leq 2|k-l|
): here
|k|\sim|k-l|
, and
|l|
represents the lowest frequency;

•

High-low interaction
(
|k|>2|k-l|
): corresponds to
|k|\sim|l|
, with
|k-l|
being the lowest frequency;

•

Low-high interaction
(
2|k|<|k-l|
): corresponds to
|k-l|\sim|l|
, with
|k|
being the lowest frequency.

This decomposition facilitates precise estimates by exploiting the relative scales of the frequencies in each region. The essential point is that, in the whole-space setting, the horizontal
low-frequency singularity is compensated by the weighted factor

\langle D_{x}^{-1}\rangle^{\epsilon}
and by a trichotomy of horizontal
frequency interactions. Consequently, one can avoid the separate
zero-mode analysis which, in the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
,
leads to a mass restriction.

Remark 1.4
.

Define the space
Y_{\epsilon}:=\big\{f:\|\langle D_{x}^{-1}\rangle^{\epsilon}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}<\infty\big\}
with a small parameter
\epsilon>0
. Compared to
L_{x}^{2}
, the space
Y_{\epsilon}
requires additional regularity in the
x
-direction, specifically imposing a stricter constraint on the low-frequency Fourier modes. Assume the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle|\hat{f}(k)|\sim|k|^{s}\quad\text{as}\quad|k|\to 0,

inclusion in
Y_{\epsilon}
requires
s>\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}
. This condition is strictly stronger than the standard
L^{2}
-integrability threshold
s>-\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 1.5
.

In Theorem
1.1
,
\epsilon
needs an additional lower bound
{1}/{3}
when
\alpha=0
, which allows us to gain additional integrability

\||k|^{-\frac{5}{6}}\langle k\rangle^{-m}\langle\frac{1}{k}\rangle^{-\epsilon}\|_{L_{k}^{2}}<\infty

(for example, see (3.7) in
[
6
]
) and thereby formally control
\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}}
with the help of
\|n\|_{L^{2}}
.

Remark 1.6
.

As seen in Theorem
1.1
, the initial
n
requires
1/3
more regularity in
x
compared to
\omega
. This is a consequence of the derivative transfer
|D_{x}|^{1/3}
in (
3.30
), analogous to the handling of the buoyancy term in
[
7
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and proofs of the main theorem

2.1.
Construction of the multipliers

For
k,\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
A>0
, denote that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(k,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\arctan\left(A^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{-\frac{1}{3}}\operatorname{sgn}(k)\xi\right)+\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}(k,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\arctan\left(\frac{\xi}{k}\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}.

Then, these two self-adjoint Fourier multipliers satisfy

(2.1)

1\leq\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{1}+\mathcal{M}_{2}+1\leq 1+2\pi.

The enhanced dissipation multiplier
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and the inviscid damping multiplier
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are inspired by
[
1
,
14
,
35
]
. On the Fourier side, these multipliers act as ‘ghost weights’—bounded weights that provide additional dissipation properties. A crucial feature of these multipliers is the following identity:

(2.2)

2\Re\big(\left(\partial_{t}+y\partial_{x}\right)f\mid\mathcal{M}_{i}f\big)=\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}k\partial_{\xi}\mathcal{M}_{i}(k,\xi)|\hat{f}|^{2}dkd\xi,

which holds for any sufficiently smooth function
f=f(t,x,y)
on
(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any smooth enough function
f=f(t,x,y)
on
(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and Fourier multiplier
\mathcal{M}
defined in (
2.1
), it holds that

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}k\partial_{\xi}\mathcal{M}(k,\xi)|\hat{f}|^{2}dkd\xi\geq\frac{1}{4A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\|\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{1}{3}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2A}\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}\nabla\Delta^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Direct calculation shows that

k\partial_{\xi}\mathcal{M}_{1}(k,\xi)=\frac{A^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}}{1+A^{-\frac{2}{3}}|k|^{-\frac{2}{3}}|\xi|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4A^{\frac{1}{3}}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}-\frac{1}{2A}|\xi|^{2}

and

k\partial_{\xi}\mathcal{M}_{2}(k,\xi)=\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+\xi^{2}},

which imply (
2.3
).
∎

\displaystyle I_{nl2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\kappa p(2p-1)}{(2p+1)A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n^{2p+1}\Delta cdxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{2\kappa p(2p-1)}{(2p+1)A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n^{2p+2}dxdy-\frac{2\kappa p(2p-1)\alpha}{(2p+1)A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n^{2p+1}cdxdy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2|\kappa|p(2p-1)}{(2p+1)A}\left(\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}+\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2p-1}{2pA}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(2p-1)(p-1)}{pA}\left(\frac{p}{2p+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(p-1)(2p-1)}{p(p+1)A}\left[\frac{p(p+1)}{2p+1}\right]^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}.

Adding (
3.23
)–(
3.25
) together, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{A}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}p}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}p^{3}}{A}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{C\mu^{4}p^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{4}}^{4},

which, along with the Nash inequality

-\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-C\dfrac{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}}{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}},

yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C}{A}\frac{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}}{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}p}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}+\frac{C|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}p^{3}}{A}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}+\frac{C\mu^{4}p^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{A\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}\big(\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}p\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}-|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}p^{3}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\quad-\mu^{4}p^{4}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\big)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{A\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}\big(\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-2}}-|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{3(p-1)}{p-2}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-2}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\quad-\mu^{4}p^{4}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\big).

Hence, proceeding by contradiction, we conclude that

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\|n^{p}_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{1}}^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{3(p-1)}{p-2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{1}}^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-2}}+\mu^{4}p^{4}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle=C\bigg(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2p}}^{2p}+|\kappa|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+|\kappa|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}p^{\frac{3(p-1)}{p-2}}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p-2}}+\mu^{4}p^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{2p}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\|{n_{\mathrm{in}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p-2}+p^{4}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p-2}}+\frac{p-2}{p-1}p^{\frac{4(p-1)}{p-2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p-2}}+1\right)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\max\left\{\left(1+\|{n_{\mathrm{in}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{2p-2},p^{8}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p-2}}\right\},

where we used that
\frac{p}{p-2}\leq 2
and
\frac{2p}{p-2}\leq 3
.

Step III:
L^{\infty}L^{\infty}
estimate of n.
Denote
B:=C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)
and

D:=1+\|{n_{\mathrm{in}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
Recalling
p=2^{j}
with
j\geq 2
, by iteration we infer from (
3.26
) that

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq B\cdot 2^{8j}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}

\displaystyle\leq B\cdot 2^{8j}\left(B\cdot 2^{8(j-1)}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j-1}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}

\displaystyle=B^{1+\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}\cdot 2^{8j+8(j-1)\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j-1}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}

\displaystyle\leq B^{1+\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}\cdot 2^{8j+8(j-1)\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}}\left(B\cdot 2^{8(j-2)}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j-2}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j-1}-2}{2^{j-2}-2}}\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}

\displaystyle=B^{1+\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}+\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}\cdot 2^{8j+8(j-1)\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}+8(j-2){\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j-2}}dxdy\right)^{{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{j}-2}{2^{j-1}-2}}\cdot\frac{2^{j-1}-2}{2^{j-2}-2}}

\displaystyle\quad...

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq B^{(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=3}^{j+1}\frac{1}{2^{i}-2}}2^{8(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=3}^{j+1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i}-2}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{2}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{2}-2}},

where we assumed the maximum was attained by the iterative term. Otherwise, let
j\geq j_{0}(j)\geq 3
be the first step where the maximum is determined by the first term, i.e.,

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq B^{(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=j_{0}+1}^{j+1}\frac{1}{2^{i}-2}}2^{8(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=j_{0}+1}^{j+1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i}-2}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|n|^{2^{j_{0}}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j_{0}}-2}}

\displaystyle\leq B^{(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=j_{0}+1}^{j+1}\frac{1}{2^{i}-2}}2^{8(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=j_{0}+1}^{j+1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i}-2}}\left(BD^{2\cdot 2^{j_{0}-1}-2}\right)^{\frac{2^{j+1}-2}{2^{j_{0}}-2}}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq B^{(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=3}^{j+1}\frac{1}{2^{i}-2}}2^{8(2^{j+1}-2)\Sigma_{i=3}^{j+1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i}-2}}D^{2^{j+1}-2}.

In this case, one can find that the bound is already established, and the subsequent iteration steps are unnecessary.

Direct calculations show

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}-2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}\frac{i-1}{2^{i}-2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{i}{2^{i}}\leq 2.

Then, raising both sides of (
3.27
) and (
3.28
) to the power of
\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}
, and letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{16}B\max\{\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{2},D\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{2}+\|{n_{\mathrm{in}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right),

which, combined with (
3.20
) and (
3.22
), completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
The energy estimate of
\omega
.

In this subsection, we give the energy estimate of
\omega
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
0<\epsilon<1/2<m
,
0<a<\frac{1}{16(1+2\pi)}
and
0\leq t\leq T
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
2.2
to
\eqref{eq:main}_{3}
, we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\Re\left(\partial_{x}n\left\lvert\,\mathcal{M}e^{2aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{2m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{2\epsilon}\omega\right.\right)\right|dt.

Moreover, inspired by the handling of the buoyancy term
\partial_{x}\theta
in
[
7
]
, we infer that

(3.30)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\Re\left(\partial_{x}n\left\lvert\,\mathcal{M}e^{2aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{2m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{2\epsilon}\omega\right.\right)\right|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}|D_{x}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\omega\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\|e^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left|D_{x}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}|D_{x}|^{\frac{2}{3}}n\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}.

Combining (
3.30
) with (
3.29
), the proof is complete.
∎

3.4.
Closing the energy

With the a priori estimates for
n
and
\omega
at hand, we can let
A
sufficiently large so that the energy can be closed as follows.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

By (
2.7
), Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
, we get for
0<t\leq T
that

\displaystyle E(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{A^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left(\frac{C}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega\|_{X_{a}}+\frac{C|\mu|}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}+\frac{C|\kappa|}{A^{\frac{1}{6}}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

(3.31)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left[\frac{(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{(2K)^{2}}{A^{\frac{2}{3}}}+\frac{(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{|\mu|(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\frac{|\kappa|(2K)^{4}}{A^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right].

Choose
\bar{A}_{1}=\bar{A}_{1}(K,\mu,\kappa):=C\left(|\kappa|^{6}K^{24}+|\mu|^{3}K^{9}+K^{6}\right)
large enough such that if
A>\bar{A}_{1}
, it holds that

(3.32)

\displaystyle C\left[\frac{(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{(2K)^{2}}{A^{\frac{2}{3}}}+\frac{(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{|\mu|(2K)^{3}}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\frac{|\kappa|(2K)^{4}}{A^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right]\leq 1.

Now we denote

K:=\sqrt{C\left(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m+\frac{1}{3}}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+1}.

Then, by Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
2.4
, (
3.31
) and (
3.32
), we have for
\alpha>0
that

\displaystyle E_{\infty}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(|\kappa|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}+|\mu|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\right)\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+1\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{X_{a}}^{3}+\alpha^{-1}\mu^{4}\|n\|_{X_{a}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(|\kappa|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}+|\mu|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\right)\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+1\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}K^{3}+\mu^{4}K^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\left(\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+K^{2}+\frac{|\kappa|K_{\infty}^{2}+|\mu|K_{\infty}}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}K^{2}+1\right),

and for
\alpha=0
that

\displaystyle E_{\infty}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{6}}}\left(|\kappa|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}+|\mu|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+1\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}\|{n}\|_{X_{a}}^{3}+\mu^{4}\|n\|_{X_{a}}^{4}+\mu^{4}M^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{6}}}\left(|\kappa|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}+|\mu|\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{m}\langle\frac{1}{D_{x}}\rangle^{\epsilon}n\|_{X_{a}}^{2}+1\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\kappa|^{3}K^{3}+\mu^{4}K^{4}+\mu^{4}M^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\left[\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+K^{2}+\left(\frac{|\kappa|K_{\infty}^{2}+|\mu|K_{\infty}}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}K^{2}+1\right].

\bar{A}_{2}=\bar{A}_{2}(K_{\infty},\mu,\kappa):=C\left(|\kappa|^{3}K_{\infty}^{6}+|\mu|^{3}K_{\infty}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{|\kappa|K_{\infty}^{2}+|\mu|K_{\infty}}{A^{\frac{1}{3}}}\leq 1.

\displaystyle K_{\infty}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C\left(|\kappa|^{3}K^{3}+\mu^{4}K^{4}+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\left(\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+K^{2}+1\right),\,\text{if}\,\,\alpha>0;\\
