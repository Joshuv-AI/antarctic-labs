Independence relations in the Solovay model II 2020 AMS subject classification 03E25, 22F05.
Jindřich Zapletal

Abstract
I provide a novel axiomatization of the Solovay model and a purely geometric treatment of the theory of balanced forcing.



1
Introduction

In the previous instalment
[
14
]
, I provided a “geometric” axiomatization of the Solovay model using an independence relation
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
reminiscent of stable or simple theories in model theory. Its purpose lies in the ability to analyze the theory of the model without constantly referring back to its construction. In the current paper, I develop the theory of balanced forcing under the geometric axiomatization, which allows a detailed study of many generic extensions of the Solovay model. This methodological change turns out to be a great simplification and generalization of the treatment in
[
6
]
and its sequels such as

[
13
,
11
,
10
,
9
]
. The basic terminology of these papers is retained, even though the new definitions are more general and stated in the choiceless context.

As for the architecture of the paper, in Section
2
I provide a new definition of balanced forcing, and prove basic theorems for it. Section
3
provides basic forcing examples. As in the case of proper forcing in ZFC, the class of balanced forcings needs to be stratified in order to obtain flexibility instrumental for most independence results. The stratification described in this paper uses independence relations which are variations and weakenings of the master independence relation
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
of the Solovay model. Section
4
defines the concept of an independence relation and derives its main features. The subsequent sections go through a list of independence relations which are interesting in their own right and which also appear in slight disguise in preceding work. Each of these sections defines an independence relation
\perp
and its associated class of
\perp
-balanced posets, provides examples of
\perp
-balanced forcings, provides methods to create
\perp
-independent tuples often connected with continuous actions of Polish groups, and finally includes a typical preservation theorem for the class of
\perp
-balanced forcings with an associated consistency result. The treatment should not be viewed as definitive, as many other preservation theorems can readily be derived.

The base theory of this paper is the geometric axiomatization of the Solovay model as exposed in
[
14
]
. It includes ZF+DC plus definability, inaccessibility, and Baire property axioms and critically, the existence of a master independence relation
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
with several properties. The axiomatization together with its basic consequences is recorded in the appendix Section
9
for reference purposes. The appendix also explains the details of parlance regarding Cohen-generic points of Polish spaces.

The notation follows the set theoretic standard of
[
4
]
and the parlance of the previous installment
[
14
]
. In particular, if
x
is a set of ordinals then
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
denotes the class of all sets definable from

\mathrm{HOD}_{x}

\mathrm{OD}_{x}

O

O
is closed under arbitrary intersections.

2
Balanced forcing: basics

This section provides a definition of the class of balanced forcings under the geometric axiomatization.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\langle P,\leq\rangle
be a poset.

1.

Let
x
be a set of ordinals such that
\langle P,\leq\rangle\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. A nonempty open set
O\subset P
is
balanced over
x
if it is in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
and whenever
y_{0},y_{1}
are sets of ordinals and
p_{0},p_{1}\in O
are conditions such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
and
p_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
p_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
then
p_{0},p_{1}
have a common lower bound in
P
;

2.

the poset
P
is
balanced
if there is a set
x
of ordinals such that for every set
y
of ordinals such that
x\in\mathrm{HOD}_{y}
and every condition
p\in P\cap\mathrm{HOD}_{y}
there is a set
O\subset P
balanced over
y
which consists of conditions stronger than
p
;

3.

the poset
P
is
cofinally balanced
if for every set
x
of ordinals and every condition
p\in P
there is a set
y
of ordinals such that
x,p\in\mathrm{HOD}_{y}
and a set
O\subset P
balanced over
y
which consists of conditions stronger than
p
.

For brevity of exposition, this paper only presents posets which are balanced. Many natural cofinally balanced, not balanced posets are used in
[
6
]
; existence or nonexistence of balanced sets over a set
x
of ordinals may depend on the internal theory of
\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
. The following proposition and corollary record a list of basic properties of balanced sets.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\langle P,\leq\rangle
be a poset and
x
a set of ordinals such that
\langle P,\leq\rangle\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Let
O\subset P
be a nonempty open set in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

O
is balanced over
x
;

2.

whenever
y_{0},y_{1}
are sets of ordinals such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
holds, and
O^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
O^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
are nonempty open subsets of
O
, then
O^{\prime}_{0}\cap O^{\prime}_{1}\neq 0
.

Proof.

The implication (2)
\to
(1) is immediate by considering the open sets
O^{\prime}_{0}=\{q\in P\colon q\leq p_{0}\}
and
O^{\prime}_{1}=\{q\in P\colon q\leq p_{1}\}
. For (1)
\to
(2), suppose that
O
is balanced over
x
and
y_{0},y_{1},O^{\prime}_{0},O^{\prime}_{1}
are as in (2). Use the extension property of
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
to find a set
z_{0}
of ordinals such that
z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{xy_{0}}}y_{1}
and there is an element
p_{0}\in O^{\prime}_{0}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}z_{0}}
. By the transitivity property of
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
,
y_{0}z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
holds. Use the extension property of
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
to find a set
z_{1}
of ordinals such that
z_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{xy_{1}}}y_{0}z_{0}
and there is an element
p_{1}\in O^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}z_{1}}
. By the transitivity property of
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
,
y_{0}z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}z_{1}
holds. By the balance assumption on
O
, the conditions
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
have a common lower bound, which is the desired element of
O^{\prime}_{0}\cap O^{\prime}_{1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\langle P,\leq\rangle
be a poset and
x
a set of ordinals such that
\langle P,\leq\rangle\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Let
O\subset P
be a balanced set over
x
.

1.

any nonempty open subset of
O
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is dense in
O
and balanced over
x
;

2.

the set
\{p\in P\colon O
is dense below
p\}
is balanced over
x
;

3.

every condition in
O
decides every statement of the
P
-forcing language with parameters in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, and all conditions in
O
decide a given statement in the same way.

Any two regular open subsets of
P
which are balanced over
x
are either disjoint or equal.

Proof.

For (1), suppose
O_{0}\subset O
is a nonempty open set in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Its balance is clear from the definitions. For the density, consider
O_{1}=\{p\in O\colon
for every
q\leq p
,
q\notin O_{0}\}
. This is an open subset of
O
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
; if it is nonempty then by Proposition
2.2
, the intersection
O_{0}\cap O_{1}
must be nonempty, which is absurd. Thus,
O_{1}
is empty and
O_{0}
must be dense in
O
. For (2), note that the set
\{p\in P\colon O
is dense below
p\}
inherits the criterion in Proposition
2.2
(2) from
O
. For (3), if
\phi
is a statement of the
P
-forcing language with parameters in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
then the sets
O_{0}=\{p\in O\colon p\Vdash\phi\}
and
O_{1}=\{p\in O\colon p\Vdash\lnot\phi\}
are open and in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. By (1), if they are both nonempty, then they must have a nonempty intersection, which is absurd. Thus, one of these sets is empty, and the other is then equal to
O
.

For the last sentence, if
O_{0}\subset P
and
O_{1}\subset P
are balanced over
x
and regular open, then their intersection is either empty or dense in both
O_{0}
and
O_{1}
by item (1). If the latter option occurs, the regularity of
O_{0}
and
O_{1}
implies that
O_{0}\cap O_{1}=O_{0}=O_{1}
.
∎

Corollary
2.3
(2) shows that every balanced set is a subset of a regular open balanced set; the last sentence then opens the possibility of classifying regular open sets which are balanced over
x
. This is a task performed in many places in
[
6
]
.

Among the many general features of balanced forcing extensions proved in
[
6
]
, I will prove only one here.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a cofinally balanced poset. In the
P
-extension, every well-ordered sequence of elements of the ground model belongs itself to the ground model.

In particular, cofinally balanced forcing does not add any new sets of ordinals. While this behavior is impossible under the Axiom of Choice, in the choiceless context it is in fact quite common
[
3
,
2
]
.

Proof.

Let
p\in P
be a condition and let
\tau
be a name such that
p\Vdash\tau
is a function from an ordinal
\alpha
to the ground model. Use the cofinal balance to find a set
x
of ordinals such that
P,p,\tau\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, and there is an open set
O\subset P
consisting of conditions below
p
balanced over
x
. It will be enough to show that for each
\beta\in\alpha
there is an element
u\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that all conditions in
O
force
\tau(\check{\beta})=\check{u}
.

To see this, use the extension property to find sets
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
of ordinals, conditions
q_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
q_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
in
O
and elements
u_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
u_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
such that
q_{0}\Vdash\tau(\check{\beta})=\check{u}_{0}
and
q_{1}\Vdash\tau(\check{\beta})=\check{u}_{1}
. By the balance assumption on
O
, the conditions
q_{0},q_{1}
are compatible, so
u_{0}=u_{1}
must hold. Thus,
u_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
and the latter intersection is equal to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
by Fact
9.2
(3). All conditions in
O
then decide the statement
\tau(\check{\beta})=\check{u}_{0}
in the same way, and as
q_{0}
decides it in the affirmative, so must all conditions in
O
.
∎

3
Balanced forcing: examples

In this section, I produce several basic examples of (cofinally) balanced forcings with classification of their balanced sets. These examples are all present in
[
6
]
; however, the arguments are much more natural in the new axiomatic setting.

Example 3.1
.

Let
P
be the poset of all infinite subsets of
\omega
ordered by modulo finite inclusion. The poset is designed to add a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\omega
.

1.

The poset
P
is balanced;

2.

the regular open sets balanced over
x
are classified by nonprincipal ultrafilters on
\omega
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
.

Proof.

Start with (2). Let
x
be a set of ordinals. For a nonprincipal ultrafilter
u\in\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
on
\omega
write
O_{u}=\{q\in P\colon q
diagonalizes
u\}
. I will show that

•

each
O_{u}
is regular open and balanced over
x
;

•

every regular open set balanced over
x
is of the form
O_{p}
for some nonprincipal ultrafilter
p\in\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
.

For the first item note that the set
O_{u}
is nonempty since the ultrafilter
u
contains only countably many subsets of
\omega
. In addition,
O_{u}\subset P
is clearly open, regular, and in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. To prove the balance of the set
O_{u}
, suppose that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
are sets of ordinals and
q_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
q_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
are conditions in the set
O_{u}
. If
q_{0}\cap q_{1}
is infinite, then it is the required common lower bound of
q_{0},q_{1}
. So, suppose towards a contradiction that the two sets have finite intersection; shrinking them if necessary, I may assume that they are actioally disjoint. By Fact
9.2
(4), there is a set
a\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that
a\subset\omega
,
q_{0}\subseteq a
and
q_{1}\cap a=0
. Now, consider the question whether
a\in u
or not. If the answer is affirmative,
q_{1}
should not be disjoint from it; if the answer is negative, then the complement of the set
a
is in
u
and
q_{0}
should not be disjoint from it. In both cases, a contradiction is reached.

For the second item, if
O\subset P
is a regular open set balanced over
x
, in view of Corollary
2.3
and the regularity assumption, it will be enough to find an ultrafilter
u\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that
O\cap O_{u}\neq 0
. To do that, use the extension axiom to find sets
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
of ordinals containing conditions
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
in
O
respectively such that for every set
a\subset\omega
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, either
q_{0}\leq a
or
q_{0}\leq\omega\setminus a
, and the same for subscript
1
. Since
q_{0},q_{1}
have a common lower bound by the balanced assumption on
O
, the sets
\{a\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}\colon q_{0}\leq a\}
and
\{a\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}\colon q_{1}\leq a\}
must be equal. Their common value
u
belongs to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
by Fact
9.2
(3), and by the choice of
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
it is an ultrafilter in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Both conditions
q_{0},q_{1}
then witness the fact that
O\cap O_{u}\neq 0
.

(1) now immediately follows as whenever
x
is a set of ordinals, in
\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
the axiom of choice holds and every infinite subset of
\omega
is contained in some nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\omega
.
∎

Example 3.2
.

Let
V
be a vector space over a well-orderable field
C
. Let
P
be the poset of well-orderable linearly independent subsets of
V
, ordered by reverse extension. The poset is designed to add a Hamel basis of
V
over
C
.

1.

The poset
P
is balanced;

2.

if
x
is a set of ordinals such that
C,V\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, then the regular open subsets of
P
balanced over
x
are classified by maximal linearly independent subsets of
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
.

Proof.

To argue for (2), let
x
be a set of ordinals such that
V,C\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Note that
C\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds by Fact
9.2
(2) and
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is again a vector space over
C
. For each inclusion-maximal linearly independent set
p\subset V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
define
O_{p}=\{q\in P\colon q\leq p\}
. Then

•

for each inclusion-maximal linearly independent subset
p\subset V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, the set
O_{p}
is regular open and balanced over
x
;

•

every regular open set balanced over
x
is of this form.

To prove the first item, the regularity of
O_{p}
is left to the reader. Suppose that
y_{0},y_{1}
are sets of ordinals such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
. Suppose that
q_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
q_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
are elements of the open set
O
; I must prove that they have a common lower bound, meaning that their union is a linearly independent set. Suppose towards a contradiction that this fails, and there is a nontrivial linear combination
b
of elements of
q_{0}\cup q_{1}
giving zero. Use Fact
9.2
(2) to conclude that
q_{0}\subset\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
q_{1}\subset\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
. Reorganizing the combination
b
, one can obtain an equality
b_{0}=b_{1}
, where
b_{0}
contains only terms of
b
in
\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
b_{1}
contains only terms in
\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
. Then the common value of
b_{0}
and
b_{1}
belongs to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
by the initial independence assumption on
y_{0},y_{1}
and Fact
9.2
(3). However, that common value is already expressed as a linear combination of elements of
p
. The linear independence of each of
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
then shows that the linear combinations
b_{0}
and
b_{1}
are the same, and the initial combination
b
must have been trivial as desired.

To prove the second item, suppose that
O\subset P
is a regular open set balanced over
x
. It will be enough to find a basis
p\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
of
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that
O\cap O_{p}\neq 0
, as by Corollary
2.3
it then follows that
O=O_{p}
. Just use the extension property of
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
to find sets of ordinals
y_{0},y_{1}
and conditions
q_{0},q_{1}\in O
such that the linear hull of both
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
contains
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
,
q_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
,
q_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
, and
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
. By the balance of the set
O
,
q_{0},q_{1}
are compatible. Thus, for each
v\in V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, the linear combination
b_{0}
of elements of
q_{0}
which yields
v
must be the same as the linear combination
b_{1}
on the
q_{1}
side. Since
b_{0}=b_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
, Fact
9.2
(3) shows that these linear combinations belong to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, so must use only elements of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. It follows that the linear hull of
q_{0}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}=q_{1}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is equal to
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. By Fact
9.2
(3) again, the set
p=q_{0}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}=q_{1}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
belongs to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The conditions
q_{0},q_{1}
both show that
O\cap O_{p}\neq 0
, completing the proof of the second item.

Now, (2) of the proposition follows. To prove (1), suppose that
x
is a set of ordinals such that
V,C\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, and
p\in P\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is a condition. By Fact
9.2
(2),
p\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds. The canonical well-ordering of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
provides a way of extending
p
to a linearly independent subset of
V\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
which is iclusion-maximal and in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The above work then provides a set balanced over
x
consisting of conditions stronger than
p
.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Let
A
be an arbitrary set. Let
P
be the poset of all linear orderings on well-orderable subsets of
A
, ordered by reverse inclusion. The poset is designed to add a linear ordering of the set
A
.

1.

the poset
P
is balanced;

2.

for every set
x
of ordinals such that
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, the regular open sets balanced over
x
are classified by linear orderings on
A\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
which belong to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
.

Proof.

First, argue for (2). Let
x
be a set of ordinals such that
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. For each linear ordering
p\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
on
A\cap\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, let
O_{p}=\{q\in P\colon p\subseteq q\}
. Then

•

for every linear ordering
p\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, the set
O_{p}\subset P
is regular open and balanced over
x

4

Many consistency proofs in
[

13

\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}

Definition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a number. A class
d+1
-ary relation
\perp
on sets of ordinals is an
independence relation
if it satisfies the following properties, for every set
x
of ordinals such that
\perp
is definable from
x
.

1.

(nontriviality) if
\bar{z}
is the
d
-tuple all of whose entries are equal to
x
then
\perp_{x}\bar{z}
;

2.

(monotonicity) suppose that
i\in d
is an index,
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
are
d
-tuples which agree on all entries except possibly at
i
, and
\perp_{x}\bar{y}
holds. If
\bar{z}(i)\in\mathrm{OD}_{x\bar{y}(i)}
then
\perp_{x}\bar{z}
holds;

3.

(transitivity) suppose that
i\in d
is an index,
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
are
d
-tuples which agree on all entries except possibly at
i
, and
\perp_{x}\bar{y}
holds. If
\bar{z}(i)\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x\bar{y}(i)}}\bar{y}\restriction d\setminus\{i\}
then
\perp_{x}\bar{z}
.

In addition, the relation may or may not have the following features:

(4)

(symmetry) if
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
are
d
-tuples which are permutations of each other, then
\perp_{x}\bar{y}
is equivalent to
\perp_{x}\bar{z}
;

(5)

(strong transitivity) suppose that
i\in d
is an index,
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
are
d
-tuples which agree on all entries except possibly at
i
, and
\perp_{x}\bar{y}
holds. If
\perp_{x\bar{y}(i)}\bar{z}
then
\perp_{x}\bar{z}
.

In case of a
2+1
-ary relation, the notation will be
y_{0}\perp_{x}y_{1}
.

In the common case of

-ary symmetric relations, transitivity implies that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
implies

. On the other hand, if
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
implies
y_{0}\perp_{x}y_{1}
, then strong transitivity implies transitivity. These observations are left to the kind reader.

The following is the requisite strengthening of the notion of balance.

7.2
\perp^{L}
-independent generic extensions

The main point of the current section is learning to distinguish between different Artinian semilattices. In order to do that, recall a well-known notion of rank of Artinian distributive lattice. Suppose that
\langle L,\land,\lor\rangle
is a distributive Artinian lattice. Call an element
u\in L

irreducible
if for any
v_{0},v_{1}\in L
, if
u\leq v_{0}\lor v_{1}
then
u\leq v_{0}
or
u\leq v_{1}
holds. Every element
u\in L
can be written in a unique way as
u=\bigwedge a
where
a
is a finite set of irreducible elements. One consequence of the uniqueness is that if
x
is a set of ordinals such that
u\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, then
a\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
and by Fact
9.2
(2)
a\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The
rank
of an Artinian lattice is the maximal length of strictly decreasing sequence of irreducible elements if such exists. It is well-known that the lattice of algebraic subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has rank
n
.

Now let
L
be a distributive Artinian lattice of rank at most
n
. I want to produce a pair of
\perp^{L}
-independent generic extensions. For the sake of brevity and coherence I consider a group-theoretic situation only.

Definition 7.4
.

Let
G
be a Polish group and
C\subset G
a nonempty closed set. Say that
C
has
duplication index

\geq n
in
G
if for all nonempty open sets
U\subset G
and
O_{i}\subset C
for
i\in n
(the latter in the inherited topology on
C
), the set
\{\langle g+\bar{c}(i)\colon i\in n\rangle\colon g\in U
and
\bar{c}\in\prod_{i}O_{i}\}\subset G^{n}
has nonempty interior.

Example 7.5
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

has duplication index
\geq n
in
G
.

Proof.

It is enough to verify the statement for open neighborhoods
U
of the origin. Shrinking

and
O_{i}
if necessary I may assume that
U
is a ball around the origin, any tuple
\bar{c}\in\prod_{i}O_{i}
is linearly independent, and in addition for any sequence
\bar{d}\in 4U^{n}
, the tuple
\bar{c}+\bar{d}
is linearly independent. Then it follows that the map

from

is one-to-one.

To see this, choose a point

and an element

such that for each

\bar{c}(i)+g\in O_{i}
holds, and work to show that

. The assumption means that for every

Thus, the map

with addition and multiplication. The rest of the argument follows easily from well-known results in real algebraic geometry
[
8
]
. The domain of

has dimension

, so its range has the same (tame-topological) dimension. The range is then a definable subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}
of full dimension, which means that it contains a nonempty open subset.
∎
