Joint Exclusivity
Nawaf Mohammed

Abstract
We introduce joint exclusivity (JE), a form of extremal negative dependence that extends the classical notion of mutual exclusivity. The JE structure is analytically tractable and is defined by the exclusion of the interior of the non-negative orthant. We establish a sharp necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a JE random vector with prescribed marginals, namely ∑ i ∈ N F ¯ i ​ ( 0 ) ≤ n − 1 \sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq n-1 . We propose a canonical construction that distributes probability mass on lower-dimensional faces of the support, while allowing flexible copula specifications within each face. The framework is further extended to a generalized class (G-JE) via marginal distortion functions. Finally, we identify a correspondence between the support structures of JE and joint mixability, revealing a structural link between the two concepts. Key words and phrases : counter-monotonicity; joint exclusivity; generalized joint exclusivity; mutual exclusivity; joint mixability

Introduction

Dependence modelling fundamentally relies on selecting structures that adequately capture the phenomenon of interest. Of particular importance are those of extremal nature
(
8
;
4
;
6
;
2
)
. In the bivariate setting, the positive extreme is
co-monotonicity

(
5
)
, which corresponds to the case where the components move in the same direction. Formally,
X_{12}=(X_{1},X_{2})
is co-monotonic if

X_{12}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\bigl(h_{1}(Z),\,h_{2}(Z)\bigr),

for some random variable
Z
and functions
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
that are both non-decreasing (or both non-increasing). This structure is characterised by the Fréchet upper bound

F_{12}(x_{1},x_{2})=\min\!\left\{F_{1}(x_{1}),\,F_{2}(x_{2})\right\},

where
F_{12}
denotes the joint cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
X_{12}
and
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are the marginal distributions. It represents the strongest form of positive dependence, in the sense that an increase in one component almost surely does not decrease the other.

At the opposite extreme lies
counter-monotonicity

(
7
)
, where an increase in one component almost surely does not increase the other. Formally,
X_{12}
is counter-monotonic if

X_{12}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\bigl(h_{1}(Z),\,h_{2}(Z)\bigr),

where
h_{1}
is non-decreasing and
h_{2}
is non-increasing (or vice versa), for some random variable
Z
. Its joint distribution is given by the Fréchet lower bound

F_{12}(x_{1},x_{2})=\max\!\left\{F_{1}(x_{1})+F_{2}(x_{2})-1,\;0\right\}.

In the bivariate case, both Fréchet bounds define valid joint CDFs for arbitrary marginals,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, and therefore the two extremal structures are uniquely defined.

These two notions, however, behave differently in higher dimensions. Let
{N}=\{1,\dots,n\}
denote the index set. Co-monotonicity extends naturally for
X_{{N}}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
: the representation

X_{{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\bigl(h_{1}(Z),\,\dots,\,h_{n}(Z)\bigr),

where all
h_{i}
are non-decreasing (or all non-increasing), remains well-defined for any
n\geq 2
. Moreover, the Fréchet upper bound

F_{{N}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\min_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(x_{i})

is a valid joint distribution for arbitrary marginals and any
n\geq 2
.

In contrast, counter-monotonicity does not admit a canonical multivariate extension. For
n\geq 3
, the stochastic representation is no longer unique, as opposing monotonicity cannot be consistently imposed across more than two components. Correspondingly, the Fréchet lower bound

F_{{N}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\max\!\left\{\sum_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(x_{i})-(n-1),\;0\right\}

fails, in general, to define a valid joint distribution when
n\geq 3
. As a result, several extensions of counter-monotonicity have been proposed, each tailored to specific contexts.

Among the strongest of these is
mutual exclusivity
(ME)
(
7
;
3
)
. Let
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}
denote the decumulative distribution function (DDF), then a non-negative random vector
X_{{N}}
is said to be ME if

\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{ij}(0,0)=0,\qquad\text{for all }i<j,\,\,i,j\in{N},

(1.1)

that is, no two components are simultaneously positive. An ME random vector with prescribed marginals
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
exists if and only if

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq 1.

(1.2)

Whenever (
1.2
) holds, the Fréchet lower bound is a valid joint distribution and coincides with that of the ME random vector. However, condition (
1.2
) is rather restrictive, which limits the practical applicability of ME.

Alternative notions of counter-monotonicity have been introduced to overcome this limitation, including joint mixability
(
12
)
,
d
-counter-monotonicity
(
9
)
, and
\Sigma
-counter-monotonicity; see
11
for a unified treatment. These approaches typically impose that a given function of the components is almost surely constant. Although they allow for greater flexibility in the choice of marginals than ME, they are often difficult to construct explicitly or characterise completely for a given set of marginals. This stands in contrast to ME, whose existence is completely determined by condition (
1.2
) and its construction is exactly the Fréchet lower bound. This motivates the following question:

does there exist a notion of counter-monotonicity that is more flexible than ME, yet remains easy to construct and admits a complete and tractable characterisation analogous to (
1.2
)?

A natural answer emerges by relaxing the pairwise requirement in (
1.1
). Instead of prohibiting any pair of components from being simultaneously positive, one may only require that all
n
components are never simultaneously positive. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A non-negative random vector
X_{{N}}
is said to be jointly exclusive (JE) if

\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{N}}(0,\dots,0)=0,

(1.3)

that is, its support is contained in
[0,\infty)^{n}\setminus(0,\infty)^{n}
.

Figure 1
:
Comparison of the supports of the ME and the JE random vectors for
n=3
.

When
n=2
, conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) coincide, and thus ME and JE are equivalent. For
n\geq 3
, ME implies JE, but not conversely, making JE a strictly more flexible notion as illustrated in Figure
1
. As we demonstrate in this paper, JE additionally admits a canonical construction and a complete characterisation of the admissible marginals, thereby providing an affirmative answer to the question above.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section
2
introduces the notation and presents basic properties of JE. Section
3
develops the main construction methods and characterisation results. Further remarks on the reflected and translated variants of JE as well as its connection to joint mixability are discussed in Section
4
. Section
5
concludes.

2
Preliminaries

From the index set
{N}=\{1,\dots,n\}
define
{M}=\{{I}\subset{N}\,:\,2\leq|{I}|\leq n-1\}
and for
i\in{N}
define the collection
{M}_{i}=\{{I}\in{M}\,:\,i\in{I}\}
. We primarily consider non-negative random vectors
X_{{N}}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
, with sub-vectors denoted by
X_{{I}}=(X_{i},\,i\in{I})
for
{I}\in{M}
. Similarly, we write
x_{{N}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{{I}}=(x_{i},\,i\in{I})
for their deterministic counterparts. We use
F
,
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}
,
f
, and
\varphi
to denote the CDF, DDF, density function, and characteristic function, respectively, with subscripts indicating the corresponding vector or sub-vector. For example,
F_{{N}}
and
F_{{I}}
denote the CDFs of
X_{{N}}
and
X_{{I}}
.

By Definition
1.1
and as illustrated in Figure
1
, the support of a JE random vector is singular, lying in an
(n-1)
-dimensional subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently, its joint distribution is fully determined by its lower-dimensional margins, as formalized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

A random vector
X_{{N}}
is JE if and only if its CDF and characteristic function satisfy

F_{{N}}(x_{{N}})=\begin{cases}1-\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})+\sum\limits_{{I}\in{M}}(-1)^{|{I}|}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{I}}(x_{{I}}),&x_{{N}}\in[0,\infty)^{n},\\
\hskip 21.33955pt=(-1)^{n-1}\left(1-\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(x_{i})+\sum\limits_{{I}\in{M}}(-1)^{|{I}|}\,F_{{I}}(x_{{I}})\right),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(2.1)

and

\varphi_{{N}}(t_{{N}})=(-1)^{n-1}\left(1-\sum_{i\in{N}}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(-1)^{|{I}|}\,\varphi_{{I}}(t_{{I}})\right),\quad t_{{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.2)

Proof.

The first representation of the CDF follows directly from the inclusion–exclusion principle applied to
F_{{N}}(x_{{N}})
, noting that the highest-order term
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{N}}(x_{{N}})
vanishes under the JE property. The second representation is obtained by applying inclusion–exclusion again to the lower-dimensional terms
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{I}}(x_{{I}})
.

For the characteristic function, observe that

\prod_{i\in{N}}\left(\exp\left\{\mathrm{i}\,t_{i}X_{i}\right\}-1\right)=0

almost surely due to JE. Taking expectations and expanding the product yields the stated expression after rearranging terms.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The representation in (
2.1
) highlights the structural difference between the JE and the ME dependence. In particular, the higher-order terms

\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(-1)^{|{I}|}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{I}}(x_{{I}})

(2.3)

capture interactions beyond the one-dimensional marginals. In the ME case, each term
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{I}}(x_{{I}})
is zero, so the sum in (
2.3
) vanishes. Consequently, the CDF reduces to

F_{{N}}(x_{{N}})=\begin{cases}1-\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})=\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(x_{i})-(n-1),&x_{{N}}\in[0,\infty)^{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which coincides with the Fréchet lower bound. Under condition (
1.2
), this defines a valid CDF for the ME random vector
X_{{N}}
.

3
Main Results

We begin by establishing the fundamental characterisation of JE random vectors in terms of their marginal distributions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
be given marginal distributions. There exists a JE random vector
X_{{N}}
with these marginals if and only if

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq n-1.

(3.1)

Proof.

Necessity.

Suppose that
X_{{N}}
is JE with marginals
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
. By definition
1.1
, we have

\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{N}}(0,\dots,0)=0.

Applying the Fréchet lower bound yields

\max\!\left\{\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-(n-1),\,0\right\}\leq\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{N}}(0,\dots,0)=0,

which implies
\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq n-1
.

Sufficiency.

Assume that condition (
3.1
) holds. We construct a JE random vector
X_{{N}}
supported on
[0,\infty)^{n}\setminus(0,\infty)^{n}
by assigning probability masses to its disjoint components.

Step 1: Higher-dimensional faces.
For each
{I}\in{M}
, define

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>x_{i},\,i\in{I};\,X_{j}=0,\,j\in{N}\setminus{I}\right)=p_{{I}}\,C_{{I}}\!\left(\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})}{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)},\,i\in{I}\right),

(3.2)

for
x_{{I}}\in[0,\infty)^{|{I}|}
, where
p_{{I}}\in[0,1]
denotes the total probability assigned to the face

\{x_{{N}}\in[0,\infty)^{n}\setminus(0,\infty)^{n}:\;x_{i}>0\text{ for }i\in{I},\;x_{j}=0\text{ for }j\in{N}\setminus{I}\},

and
C_{{I}}
are arbitrary copulas
(
10
)
. No consistency between copulas of different dimensions is required i.e. even if
{I}_{1}\subset{I}_{2}
then
C_{{I}_{1}}
is not necessarily a marginal copula of
C_{{I}_{2}}
.

Step 2: Axes.
To ensure the correct marginals, for each
i\in{N}
we define

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>x_{i};\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})-\left(\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}\right)\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})}{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)},

(3.3)

for
x_{i}\in[0,\infty)
.

Step 3: Origin.
Finally, the probability mass at the origin is determined by normalization:

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}=0,\,i\in{N}\right)=1-\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(|{I}|-1)p_{{I}}.

(3.4)

The construction yields a valid distribution provided that all assigned masses define proper probabilities. Since (
3.2
) is valid by construction, it remains to ensure that (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) are well-defined.

The non-negativity and monotonicity of (
3.3
) are equivalent to

\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}\leq\,\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\qquad\text{for all }i\in{N},

(3.5)

while (
3.4
) is non-negative if and only if

\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(|{I}|-1)p_{{I}}\geq\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1.

(3.6)

Thus, the problem reduces to showing that there exist coefficients
p_{{I}}\geq 0
satisfying (
3.5
)–(
3.6
).

Step 4: Feasibility via linear programming.
Consider the linear program

\displaystyle\text{maximize }\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(|{I}|-1)p_{{I}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }p_{{I}}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}\leq\,\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\quad i\in{N}.

Its dual problem is

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\,r_{i},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }r_{i}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{i\in{I}}r_{i}\geq|{I}|-1,\quad{I}\in{M}.

By strong duality, the maximum of (
3.7
) equals the minimum of (
3.8
). The feasible region of the dual is a polytope, and the minimum is attained at its extreme points. It can be verified that the only relevant vertices are

\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1},\dots,\frac{n-2}{n-1}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad(1,\dots,1,0),\ (1,\dots,0,1),\ \dots,\ (0,1,\dots,1).

Evaluating the objective function at these points yields

\min\left\{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\;\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\max_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\right\}.

(3.9)

Therefore,

\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(|{I}|-1)p_{{I}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\;\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\max_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\right\}.

Combining this with (
3.6
), existence is ensured if and only if

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1\leq\min\left\{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\;\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\max_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\right\}.

(3.10)

If the minimum is attained by the second term, (
3.10
) reduces to

\max_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq 1,

which always holds. If it is attained by the first term, then (
3.10
) becomes

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq n-1,

which is precisely condition (
3.1
).

Hence, under (
3.1
), there exist non-negative coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfying (
3.5
)–(
3.6
), and the construction above defines a valid JE random vector with marginals
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
.
∎

The necessary and sufficient condition (
3.1
) for JE is significantly less restrictive than its counterpart (
1.2
) for ME. This difference becomes increasingly pronounced as the dimension
n
grows.

Indeed, for JE the condition (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\sum_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(0)\geq 1,

whereas for ME one requires

\sum_{i\in{N}}F_{i}(0)\geq n-1.

Consequently, JE allows for substantially greater flexibility in the marginal distributions. In particular, for non-degenerate marginals, it suffices that as few as two of them have atoms at zero, while the remaining
n-2
may be fully supported on
(0,\infty)
. In contrast, under ME each marginal must necessarily possess an atom at zero.

To further investigate the structural properties of JE, we adopt the construction introduced in the proof of Theorem
3.1
as a canonical model.

Definition 3.2
.

X_{{N}}
is called a canonical JE random vector if its distribution is specified by (
3.2
)–(
3.4
), for some coefficients
p_{{I}}\in[0,1]
,
{I}\in{M}
, satisfying (
3.5
)–(
3.6
).

For fixed marginals,
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
, if condition (
3.1
) holds, then Theorem
3.1
ensures the existence of a canonical JE random vector. Moreover, its CDF admits the following explicit form as a corollary of Proposition
2.1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

A random vector
X_{{N}}
is a canonical JE random vector if and only if its CDF is given by

F_{{N}}(x_{{N}})=\begin{cases}1-\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})+\sum\limits_{{I}\in{M}}(-1)^{|{I}|}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{J}\in{M}\\
{I}\subseteq{J}\end{subarray}}p_{{J}}\,C_{{J}}\!\left(\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.43405ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})}{\,\overline{\kern-1.43405ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)},\,i\in{I};\,1,\,j\in{J}\setminus{I}\right),&x_{{N}}\in[0,\infty)^{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.11)

For canonical JE random vectors, the coefficients
p_{{I}}
determine how probability mass is allocated across the different faces of the support, and hence indirectly along the axes and at the origin. Their feasible values are governed entirely by the marginal quantities
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{1}(0),\dots,\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{n}(0)
. In contrast, the copulas
C_{{I}}
control how the mass
p_{{I}}
is distributed within each face. These copulas can be chosen independently across different
{I}
and are not constrained by the marginals.

The following remark highlights several instructive choices of the parameters
p_{{I}}
and their implications for the resulting dependence structure.

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

If
p_{{I}}=0
for some
{I}\in{M}
, then the corresponding face is excluded from the support. Moreover, by the lower Fréchet bound applied to (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sum_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)+\sum_{j\in{N}\setminus{I}}(1-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0))-(n-1),\,0\right\}\leq p_{{I}}=0,

which implies

\sum_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{j\in{N}\setminus{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)\leq n-1-|{N}\setminus{I}|.

At the extreme, if
p_{{I}}=0
for all
{I}\in{M}
, then all mass is concentrated on the axes and the origin, recovering the ME structure. In this case, (
3.6
) reduces to

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\leq 1,

which is the ME condition (
1.2
).

(2)

If
p_{{I}}=1
for some
{I}\in{M}
, then all mass is concentrated on the corresponding
{I}
-th face, and necessarily
p_{{J}}=0
for all
{J}\in{M}\setminus\{{I}\}
. From the Fréchet upper bound of (
3.2
),

1=p_{{I}}\leq\min\left\{\min_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\;\min_{j\in{N}\setminus{I}}(1-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0))\right\},

which implies
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)=1
for all
i\in{I}
and
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)=0
for all
j\in{N}\setminus{I}
.

(3)

The mass on the
i
-th axis is determined by (
3.3
). If no mass is assigned to that axis, then (
3.5
) must hold with equality:

\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0).

If all axes carry no mass, this yields a system of
n
linear equations in
|{M}|=2^{n}-n-2
unknowns. For
n\geq 4
, the system is under-determined and admits infinitely many solutions. When
n=3
, the system is exactly determined, leading to the explicit solutions for all
I\in{M}

p_{{I}}=\max\left\{\frac{\sum_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)}{2},\,0\right\},\qquad j\in{N}\setminus{I}.

(4)

The mass at the origin is governed by (
3.4
). Provided that (
3.5
) holds, assigning zero mass at the origin requires

\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(|{I}|-1)p_{{I}}=\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1,

which corresponds to equality in (
3.6
).

(5)

If
\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)=n-1
, meaning that equality is achieved in condition (
3.1
), then by summing the equations in (
3.3
) over all
i\in{N}
and setting each
x_{i}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}|I|p_{{I}}=n-1.

Furthermore, since
\sum_{{I}\in{M}}|I|p_{{I}}\leq(n-1)\sum_{{I}\in{M}}p_{{I}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}|I|p_{{I}}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)

\displaystyle-(n-2)\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)

\displaystyle+(n-1)\sum_{{I}\in{M}}p_{{I}}.

Applying the relation
\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}p_{{I}}=1-\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}=0,\,i\in{N}\right)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)+\sum_{{I}\in{M}}|I|p_{{I}}

\displaystyle\leq n-1-(n-1)\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}=0,\,i\in{N}\right)

\displaystyle-(n-2)\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right),

which simplifies to

\displaystyle n-1\leq n-1-(n-1)\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}=0,\,i\in{N}\right)-(n-2)\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right).

This implies that each axis probability,
\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>0;\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)
, as well as the origin probability,
\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}=0,\,i\in{N}\right)
, must be zero. Consequently, the JE structure concentrates its mass entirely on the faces, such that
\sum_{I\in{M}}p_{{I}}=1
.

Once the marginals are fixed, the admissible choices of the coefficients
p_{{I}}
are precisely those satisfying conditions (
3.5
)–(
3.6
). These coefficients exhibit a particularly tractable structure in the trivariate case (
n=3
).

To elucidate this, consider the linear relations in (
3.5
)–(
3.6
). In general, the
n+1
corresponding sums of the coefficients
p_{{I}}
are linearly independent. For
n=3
, however, the sum in (
3.6
) is equal to one half of the aggregate of the three sums in (
3.5
). Hence, one constraint becomes redundant, and the system reduces to three independent linear constraints for the three unknowns (see also Remark
3.4
-(3)). This yields a uniquely determined affine structure of admissible solutions.

In particular, parametrized by the same scalar, all coefficients
p_{{I}}
can be expressed as linear interpolations between their extremal values implied by (
3.5
)–(
3.6
). Consequently, the feasible set collapses from a three-dimensional region to a one-dimensional family, which substantially simplifies both the analysis and the implementation. The following proposition makes this structure explicit.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
n=3
and suppose that condition (
3.1
) holds. Define, for each
{I}\in{M}
,

p_{{I}}=\lambda\,\min_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)+(1-\lambda)\,\max\left\{\sum_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1,\,0\right\}.

(3.12)

Then there exists
\lambda^{*}\in[0,1]
such that the coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfy (
3.5
)–(
3.6
).

Proof.

Assume that (
3.1
) holds and define

U_{{I}}=\min_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),\qquad L_{{I}}=\max\left\{\sum_{i\in{I}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1,\,0\right\}.

Substituting (
3.12
) into (
3.5
)–(
3.6
), and using the fact that
\lambda\in[0,1]
, yields the constraint

\max\left\{\frac{\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})},\,0\right\}\leq\lambda\leq\min_{i\in{N}}\left\{\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\right\}.

(3.13)

Moreover, since
\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}U_{{I}}\geq\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
for at least two indices
i\in{N}
, the upper bound is at most
1
. The inequality
U_{{I}}\geq L_{{I}}
follows directly from the Fréchet bounds.

We now show that the interval in (
3.13
) is non-empty. For each
i\in{N}
, consider

\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}.

First, note that the numerator can only take one of the following values:
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
,
1-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)
for all
j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}
, or
2-\sum_{j\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)
. Each of these quantities is non-negative; in particular, the non-negativity of the last expression follows from condition (
3.1
). Hence,

\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\geq 0.

Next, since
\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}=\sum_{I\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}+L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}
, and using the inequality
\sum_{j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)-1-L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}\leq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}+\sum_{j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)-1-L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{j\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}.

Combining the above bounds yields

\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\geq\max\left\{\frac{\sum_{j\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{j}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})},\,0\right\},

for all
i\in{N}
. Therefore, the lower bound in (
3.13
) does not exceed the upper bound, and the interval is non-empty. Consequently, there exists
\lambda^{*}\in[0,1]
such that the coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfy (
3.5
)–(
3.6
).
∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
n=3
, so that
{M}=\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}
. Suppose
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})=(1/2)(1-x_{i})
for
x_{i}\in[0,1]
,
i\in{N}
. Then
\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)=3/2
, so condition (
3.1
) is satisfied.

Using the method in Proposition
3.5
, the coefficients are given by

p_{{I}}=\frac{\lambda}{2},\qquad\lambda\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\,\frac{1}{2}\right].

At
\lambda=1/3
, the mass at the origin vanishes (see Remark
3.4
-(4)), whereas at
\lambda=1/2
the axes carry no mass (see Remark
3.4
-(3)). Intermediate values of
\lambda
distribute mass across both components.

Now choose the copulas

C_{12}(u_{1},u_{2})=u_{1}u_{2},\quad C_{13}(u_{1},u_{3})=\min\{u_{1},u_{3}\},\quad C_{23}(u_{2},u_{3})=\max\{u_{2}+u_{3}-1,0\}.

Thus, each face exhibits a distinct dependence structure: independence, co-monotonicity, and counter-monotonicity, respectively.

By Corollary
3.3
, the resulting CDF is

F_{{N}}(x_{{N}})=\begin{cases}1-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i\in{N}}(1-x_{i})+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}(1-x_{1})(1-x_{2})\\
\hskip 28.45274pt+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\min\{1-x_{1},1-x_{3}\}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\max\{1-x_{2}-x_{3},0\},&x_{{N}}\in[0,1]^{3},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The support of
X_{{N}}
is illustrated in Figure
2
.

Figure 2
:
Support of the canonical JE random vector
X_{{N}}
with copulas
C_{12}
,
C_{13}
, and
C_{23}
.

The corresponding Pearson correlations are

\rho_{12}=\frac{6\lambda-3}{5},\quad\rho_{13}=\frac{8\lambda-3}{5},\quad\rho_{23}=\frac{4\lambda-3}{5}.

For example,

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}:\qquad\rho_{12}=-\frac{1}{5},\quad\rho_{13}=-\frac{1}{15},\quad\rho_{23}=-\frac{1}{3},

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}:\qquad\rho_{12}=-\frac{1}{10},\quad\rho_{13}=\frac{1}{15},\quad\rho_{23}=-\frac{4}{15},

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}:\qquad\rho_{12}=0,\quad\rho_{13}=\frac{1}{5},\quad\rho_{23}=-\frac{1}{5}.

As
\lambda
increases, the correlations tend to increase, reflecting the diminishing influence of mass concentrated on the axes (which induces strong negative dependence). At
\lambda=1/2
, the dependence is entirely governed by the copulas on the faces.

This example highlights the flexibility of the canonical JE framework. Although the global structure enforces a form of counter-monotonicity, the model accommodates a wide range of local dependence behaviors through the joint specification of the copulas
C_{{I}}
and their coefficients
p_{{I}}
.

Although the canonical JE covers a wide range of modelling choices, its support on the faces is partly governed by the fixed marginals. The copulas
C_{{I}}
may be chosen freely, but the induced joint distribution still inherits the range of the marginals through
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})/\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
. To accommodate a broader class of dependence structures on the faces, we introduce a new construction, which we call the
generalized JE
or G-JE, that affords greater control over the probability mass assigned to each face.

Definition 3.7
.

For each
i\in{N}
, let
G_{i}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a non-decreasing, left-continuous function satisfying
G_{i}(u)=0
for all
u\in[0,a_{i}]
and
G_{i}(u)=1
for all
u\in[b_{i},1]
, where
0\leq a_{i}<b_{i}\leq\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
. A random vector
X_{{N}}
is said to be G-JE if its face probabilities are given, analogously to (
3.2
)–(
3.4
), by: for each
{I}\in{M}
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>x_{i},\,i\in{I};\,X_{j}=0,\,j\in{N}\setminus{I}\right)=p_{{I}}\,C_{{I}}\!\left(G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})\right),\,i\in{I}\right),\quad x_{{I}}\in[0,\infty)^{|{I}|};

(3.14)

for all
i\in{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{i}>x_{i};\,X_{j}=0,\ j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}\right)=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})-\left(\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}\right)G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})\right),\quad x_{i}\in[0,\infty);

(3.15)

and the probability at the origin is as in (
3.4
). The coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfy condition (
3.6
) as well as

\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}\leq G_{i}^{*},\qquad\text{for all }i\in{N},

(3.16)

where

G_{i}^{*}=\inf_{0\leq x_{i}<y_{i}}\,\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(y_{i})}{G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})\right)-G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(y_{i})\right)}.

(3.17)

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
are given marginal distributions. Then a G-JE random vector
X_{{N}}
with these marginals exists if and only if

\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1\leq\min\left\{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*},\;\sum_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*}-\max_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*}\right\}.

(3.18)

Proof.

The proof parallels the sufficiency argument of Theorem
3.1
.

Since each
G_{i}
is non-decreasing and left-continuous with the prescribed boundary conditions, the compositions
G_{i}\circ\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
are well-defined DDFs. By construction, the face probabilities in (
3.14
) are well-defined; (
3.15
) is derived so as to recover the prescribed marginals; and the probability at the origin follows by normalization. The non-increasing requirement on (
3.15
) yields condition (
3.16
) with the constants defined in (
3.17
), while non-negativity of the origin probability is equivalent to (
3.6
). Finally, applying the same linear programming machinery as in (
3.7
)–(
3.8
), with
G_{i}^{*}
in place of
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
, gives the bound

\min\left\{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*},\;\sum_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*}-\max_{i\in{N}}\,G_{i}^{*}\right\},

so existence is guaranteed if and only if (
3.18
) holds.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

(1)

The G-JE is strictly more flexible than the canonical JE: the constants
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
restrict the support on each face to a sub-range, while the functions
G_{i}
introduce distortions within that range, all without disrupting the overall JE structure. The price paid is a more stringent existence condition (
3.18
) compared to (
3.1
). Indeed, evaluating
G_{i}^{*}
at
x_{i}=0
and
y_{i}\to\infty
gives

0\leq G_{i}^{*}\leq\frac{\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(\infty)}{G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)\right)-G_{i}\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(\infty)\right)}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0),

so
G_{i}^{*}\leq\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
always, making (
3.16
) more restrictive than (
3.5
) and the admissible set for the parameters
p_{{I}}
correspondingly smaller. When
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
attains the values
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
, this bound sharpens to

0\leq G_{i}^{*}\leq\frac{b_{i}-a_{i}}{G_{i}\left(b_{i}\right)-G_{i}\left(a_{i}\right)}=b_{i}-a_{i}\leq\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0).

(2)

If
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
is continuous at some point
x_{i}^{*}
while
G_{i}\circ\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
has a jump there, then along any sequence
x_{i}^{(k)}\nearrow x_{i}^{*}
we have
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i}^{(k)})-\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i}^{*})\to 0
while
G_{i}\!\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i}^{(k)})\right)-G_{i}\!\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i}^{*})\right)\to c>0
, forcing
G_{i}^{*}=0
. Condition (
3.16
) then requires

p_{{I}}=0\quad\text{for all }{I}\in{M}_{i}.

This is to be expected: if
p_{{I}}>0
for some
{I}\in{M}_{i}
, then the mass at
x_{i}^{*}
on the
{I}
-th face would force a jump in
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
at that point, contradicting the assumed continuity. Thus, to avoid degeneracy, wherever
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
is continuous,
G_{i}\circ\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
must be continuous as well.

(3)

If
G_{i}\circ\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
is constant on some sub-interval while
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
is strictly increasing there, the ratio in (
3.17
) diverges. This causes no difficulty, as the infimum simply disregards such pairs.

(4)

The CDF of a G-JE random vector
X_{{N}}
takes the same form as the canonical expression (
3.11
), with each argument
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})/\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
of the copula
C_{{J}}
replaced by
G_{i}\!\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})\right)
.

(5)

The result in Remark
3.4
-(1) carries over to G-JE without modification. Item (2) of that remark holds with the additional constraint
G_{i}^{*}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)=1
for all
i\in{I}
. Analogously to Remark
3.4
-(3), no mass is assigned to the
i
-th axis whenever

\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}p_{{I}}=G_{i}^{*},

and in this case we necessarily have
G_{i}\!\left(\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})\right)=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(x_{i})/\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
, thus
G_{i}^{*}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
. For
n=3
, if all axes carry zero probability, then each
p_{{I}}
can be written explicitly and uniquely. Item (4) of that remark holds without modification while the implication in item (5) does not generally extend to G-JE random vectors.

Analogously to Proposition
3.5
, any trivariate G-JE random vector admits a convenient one-parameter linear interpolation representation for the coefficients
p_{{I}}
, as stated below.

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose condition (
3.18
) holds for a trivariate set of marginals. Define, for each
{I}\in{M}
,

p_{{I}}=\lambda\,\min_{i\in{I}}G_{i}^{*}+(1-\lambda)\,\max\left\{\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-G_{j}^{*}-1,\,0\right\},\quad j\in{N}\setminus{I}.

(3.19)

Then there exists
\lambda^{*}\in[0,1]
such that the coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfy both (
3.16
) and (
3.6
).

Proof.

The proof mirrors that of Proposition
3.5
. Assume (
3.18
) holds and set

U_{{I}}=\min_{i\in{I}}G_{i}^{*},\qquad L_{{I}}=\max\left\{\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-G_{j}^{*}-1,\,0\right\},\quad j\in{N}\setminus{I}.

Substituting (
3.19
) into (
3.16
) and (
3.6
), and using
\lambda\in[0,1]
, yields the constraint

\max\left\{\frac{\sum_{i\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})},\,0\right\}\leq\lambda\leq\min_{i\in{N}}\left\{\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\right\}.

(3.20)

Since
\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}U_{{I}}\geq G_{i}^{*}
for at least two indices
i\in{N}
, the upper bound does not exceed
1
. Moreover, (
3.18
) ensures
U_{{I}}\geq L_{{I}}
for all
{I}\in{M}
, so the denominators are positive.

It remains to show that the interval in (
3.20
) is non-empty. Fix
i\in{N}
. The numerator of the
i
-th term in the upper bound equals one of the following quantities:
G_{i}^{*}
;
G_{i}^{*}+G_{j}^{*}-\bigl(\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-1\bigr)
for
j\in{N}\setminus\{i\}
; or
\sum_{k\in{N}}G_{k}^{*}-2\bigl(\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-1\bigr)
. All three are non-negative by condition (
3.18
), so

\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\geq 0.

Furthermore, using
\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}=\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}+L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}
together with
\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-G_{i}^{*}-1-L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}\leq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}+\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-G_{i}^{*}-1-L_{{N}\setminus\{i\}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}.

Combining both bounds gives

\frac{G_{i}^{*}-\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}_{i}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})}\geq\max\left\{\frac{\sum_{k\in{N}}\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{k}(0)-1-\sum_{{I}\in{M}}L_{{I}}}{\sum_{{I}\in{M}}(U_{{I}}-L_{{I}})},\,0\right\},

so the interval (
3.20
) is non-empty, and there exists
\lambda^{*}\in[0,1]
such that the coefficients
p_{{I}}
satisfy both (
3.16
) and (
3.6
).
∎

We close this section with illustrative examples of the functions
G_{i}
and the rich G-JE structures they generate.

Example 3.11
.

(1)

Consider the linear functions
G_{i}(u)=\alpha_{i}u+\beta_{i}
, with
\alpha_{i}>0
and
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, defined on
[a_{i},b_{i}]
and extended by
G_{i}(u)=0
for
u\in[0,a_{i}]
and
G_{i}(u)=1
for
u\in[b_{i},1]
. The boundary conditions
\alpha_{i}a_{i}+\beta_{i}=0
and
\alpha_{i}b_{i}+\beta_{i}=1
uniquely determine
\alpha_{i}=1/(b_{i}-a_{i})
and
\beta_{i}=-a_{i}/(b_{i}-a_{i})
, giving

G_{i}(u)=\frac{u-a_{i}}{b_{i}-a_{i}},\quad u\in[a_{i},b_{i}].

Setting
a_{i}=0
and
b_{i}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
recovers the canonical JE, since then
G_{i}^{*}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
.

Now take
a_{i}>0
and
b_{i}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)
, and assume that each
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}
attains at least one value in
[a_{i},\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0))
. Then
G_{i}^{*}=\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{i}(0)-a_{i}
, and condition (
3.18
) becomes

4
Further Discussion

The preceding sections provided a thorough study of the JE structure: we fully characterized its existence, examined its distributional properties, and derived a tractable canonical form, which was subsequently extended to the G-JE via the distortion functions
G_{i}
on the marginals.

The primary variant of JE considered throughout this work is
JE from below
, defined by the condition
\,\overline{\kern-1.75003ptF\kern 0.0pt}_{{N}}(0,\dots,0)=0
for non-negative random vectors
X_{{N}}
. In a manner analogous to ME, the
JE from above
counterpart is defined by the condition
\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}<0,\,i\in{N})=0
for non-positive random vectors
Y_{{N}}
.

By symmetry,
Y_{{N}}
is JE from above if and only if
X_{{N}}:=-Y_{{N}}
is JE from below. Consequently, with appropriate adjustments for reflection, all results established in Sections
2
and
3
extend to the ”from above” case. Specifically, condition (
3.1
) is reformulated as:

\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}<0)\leq n-1.

Furthermore, any JE from below random vector
X_{{N}}
is closed under component-wise translation. Specifically, for constants
l_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, the translated vector
Y_{i}=X_{i}+l_{i}
remains JE, where condition (
3.1
) takes the form:

\sum_{i\in{N}}\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}>l_{i})\leq n-1.

An identical translation argument applies to the JE from above case.

As noted in the introduction, JE is not the only higher-dimensional counter-monotonic structure. Another prominent concept is
joint mixability
(JM). A random vector
Y_{{N}}
is JM if
\sum_{i\in{N}}Y_{i}=c
almost surely for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
(taken without loss of generality to be
0
). Although these two notions may appear unrelated, they are intimately connected through the geometry of their supports. Both supports are singular and contained in
(n-1)
-dimensional non-increasing subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The support of JM lies in the hyperplane
\{y_{{N}}:\sum_{i\in{N}}y_{i}=0\}
, while that of JE lies in
[0,\infty)^{n}\setminus(0,\infty)^{n}
. It is therefore natural to ask whether a bijection exists between the two supports.

Indeed, define
W_{\mathrm{JE}}=\{x_{{N}}\in[0,\infty)^{n}\setminus(0,\infty)^{n}\}
and
W_{\mathrm{JM}}=\{y_{{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i\in{N}}y_{i}=0\}
, and let
\Phi:W_{\mathrm{JE}}\to W_{\mathrm{JM}}
be given by

\Phi(x_{{N}})=\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in{N}}x_{j},\;i\in{N}\right),\quad\text{with inverse}\quad\Phi^{-1}(y_{{N}})=\left(y_{i}-\min_{j\in{N}}y_{j},\;i\in{N}\right).

To verify bijectivity, take
x_{{N}}\in W_{\mathrm{JE}}
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(x_{{N}}))

\displaystyle=\left(\left(x_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in{N}}x_{j}\right)-\min_{k\in{N}}\left(x_{k}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in{N}}x_{j}\right),\;i\in{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x_{i}-\min_{k\in{N}}x_{k},\;i\in{N}\right)=x_{{N}},

where the last equality uses
\min_{k\in{N}}x_{k}=0
. Conversely, for
y_{{N}}\in W_{\mathrm{JM}}
:

\displaystyle\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(y_{{N}}))

\displaystyle=\left(\left(y_{i}-\min_{j\in{N}}y_{j}\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in{N}}\left(y_{k}-\min_{j\in{N}}y_{j}\right),\;i\in{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(y_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in{N}}y_{k},\;i\in{N}\right)=y_{{N}},

where the last equality uses
\sum_{k\in{N}}y_{k}=0
.

Consequently, if
X_{{N}}
is JE then
Y_{{N}}:=(X_{i}-\,\overline{\kern-1.25ptX\kern 0.0pt},\;i\in{N})
is JM, and conversely, if
Y_{{N}}
is JM then
X_{{N}}:=(Y_{i}-\min_{j\in{N}}Y_{j},\;i\in{N})
is JE. Figure
4
illustrates the two sets
W_{\mathrm{JE}}
and
W_{\mathrm{JM}}
for
n=3
.

5
Conclusions

This paper establishes joint exclusivity (JE) as a rigorous and analytically tractable framework for modeling extremal negative dependence in arbitrary dimensions. By relaxing the pairwise constraints inherent in mutual exclusivity, JE resolves key structural rigidities while preserving a geometrically transparent support. We have provided a complete characterization of the existence of JE random vectors, showing that the admissibility condition is substantially less restrictive than that of mutual exclusivity.

Our canonical construction separates global marginal constraints from local dependence, enabling the latter to be specified flexibly through copulas on lower-dimensional faces. This flexibility is further enhanced by the generalized JE (G-JE) extension via marginal distortion functions, accommodating a broader class of dependence structures. Moreover, by establishing a geometric bijection between the supports of JE and joint mixability, we uncover a unifying structural link between two fundamental paradigms of extremal dependence.

These results, together with the inherent tractability of the JE framework, suggest promising directions for future research, including applications to optimal transport, risk aggregation, and the statistical inference of negatively dependent systems.
