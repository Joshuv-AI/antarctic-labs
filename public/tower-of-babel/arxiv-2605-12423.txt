Measurements of transverse-momentum dependent effects in semi-inclusive DIS at Compass
Jan Matousek on behalf of the COMPASS Collaboration

Abstract
Compass experiment at CERN had a wide physics programme. Its cornerstone were studies of hadron production in deep inelastic scattering (DIS), which can be interpreted in the transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) factorisation framework, allowing to access the distributions of polarisation and transverse momentum of quarks within the nucleon in the language of TMD PDFs, and the hadronisation in terms of TMD fragmentation functions. The data collected with a liquid hydrogen target in 2016–2017 will soon bring new information on the transverse momentum and may allow for the first time to extract the transverse polarisation of quarks within an unpolarised nucleon described by the Boer–Mulders function. The unique data collected with a transversely polarised deuteron target have already improved the knowledge of the d-quark transversity (transverse counterpart of the helicity PDF), reducing the uncertainties by a factor of 2.5 at large Bjorken x x , and are yet to yield a number of interesting results.

Hadron production in DIS

The cross section for a semi-inclusive measurement of hadron production in DIS off a transversely polarised target
\upmu{\rm N}^{\uparrow}\rightarrow\upmu^{\prime}{\rm hX}
is in terms of the usual variables
x
,
y
and
Q^{2}
,
the fraction
z
of the energy transferred to the hadron, its transverse momentum
P_{\rm T}
, and the azimuthal angles
\phi_{\rm h}
of the hadron momentum and
\phi_{\rm S}
of the target polarisation in the gamma–nucleon system

]
:

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{\rm T}\bigl[A_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})}\sin(\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})+\varepsilon A_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}+\phi_{\rm S})}\sin(\phi_{\rm h}+\phi_{\rm S})+\varepsilon A_{\rm UT}^{\sin(3\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})}\sin(3\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\sqrt{2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}A_{\rm LT}^{\cos(2\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})}\cos(2\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})\bigr]\biggr\},

(1)

where
\gamma=2Mx/Q
,
M

\varepsilon=\bigl(1-y-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigr)\big/\bigl(1-y+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}y^{2}\bigr)
,

is the longitudinal beam polarisation,

is the transverse target nucleon polarisation,

x
,
z

P_{\rm T}
and
Q^{2}

The asymmetries – the amplitudes of the azimuthal modulations in Eq. (
2

A_{\rm UU}^{\cos\phi_{\rm h}}=F_{\rm UU}^{\cos\phi_{\rm h}}/(F_{\rm UU,T}+\varepsilon F_{\rm UU,L})
.
If the transverse momentum is small,
P_{\rm T}\ll zQ
, each structure function can be interpreted in the transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) factorisation framework as a convolution of a TMD PDF
f(x,k_{\rm T}^{2},Q^{2})
and fragmentation function (FF)
D(z,P_{\kern-1.22911pt\perp}^{2},Q^{2})
, which depend on the intrinsic quark transverse momentum within the nucleon
k_{\rm T}
and the transverse momentum of the hadron
P_{\kern-1.22911pt\perp}
obtained in hadronisation, weighted with a function
w(\bm{k_{\rm T}}\cdot\bm{P_{\rm T}},\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}}\cdot\bm{P_{\rm T}},\bm{k_{\rm T}}\cdot\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}})
,

\mathcal{C}[wfD]=x\sum_{q}e^{2}_{q}\int{\rm d}{{}^{2}\bm{k_{\rm T}}}\,{\rm d}{{}^{2}\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}}}\,\delta^{(2)}(z\bm{k_{\rm T}}+\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}}-\bm{P_{\rm T}})w\,f^{q}\,D^{q\to h}.

(2)

Measurements of the amplitudes of the azimuthal modulations in Eq. (
2
) give access to a variety of TMD PDFs, describing correlations between the nucleon and quark polarisation and transverse momentum
[
7
]
.
Up to the order
1/Q
(sub-leading twist) and denoting ‘…’ terms vanishing in a Wandzura–Wilczek-type approximation (neglecting terms related to qgq correlators
[
8
]
), we have:

\displaystyle F_{\rm UU,T}=\mathcal{C}\left[f_{1}D_{1}\right],\quad F_{\rm UU,L}=0,\quad F_{\rm UU}^{\cos 2\phi_{\rm h}}=\mathcal{C}\left[\frac{2(\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{k_{\rm T}})(\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}})-\bm{k_{\rm T}}\cdot\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}}}{MM_{\rm h}}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1}^{\perp}\right],

\displaystyle F_{\rm LU}^{\sin\phi_{\rm h}}=\frac{2M}{Q}\mathcal{C}\left[...\right],\qquad F_{\rm UU}^{\cos\phi_{\rm h}}=\frac{2M}{Q}\mathcal{C}\left[-\frac{\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{k_{\rm T}}}{M}f_{1}D_{1}-\frac{(\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}})k_{\rm T}^{2}}{M^{2}M_{\rm h}}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1}^{\perp}+...\right],

\displaystyle F_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})}=\mathcal{C}\left[-\frac{\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{P_{\kern-1.20744pt\perp}}}{M}f_{\rm 1T}^{\perp}D_{1}\right],\qquad F_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}+\phi_{S})}=\mathcal{C}\left[-\frac{\hat{\bm{h}}\cdot\bm{k_{\rm T}}}{M_{h}}h_{1}H_{1}^{\perp}\right],

(3)

where we used a unit vector
\hat{\bm{h}}=\bm{P_{\rm T}}/P_{\rm T}
and denoted
M_{\rm h}
the mass of the produced hadron.
The structure function
F_{\rm UU,T}
gives access to the TMD PDF of unpolarised quarks within an unpolarised nucleon
f_{1}
and the TMD FF
D_{1}
, describing the hadronisation of an unpolarised quark.

In an unpolarised nucleon, quarks may be transversely polarised with respect to their transverse momentum, as described by the Boer–Mulders TMD PDF
h_{1}^{\perp}

[
9
]
. This would lead to
A_{\rm UU}^{\cos 2\phi_{\rm h}}
asymmetry due to the azimuthal-angle-dependent hadronisation of transversely polarised quarks described by the Collins TMD FF
H_{1}^{\perp}

[
10
]
.
The
h_{1}^{\perp}
also contributes to the asymmetry
A_{\rm UU}^{\cos\phi_{\rm h}}
,
which is expected to receive the dominant contribution from a kinematic effect arising from a non-zero
\langle k_{T}\rangle
predicted by Cahn
[
11
]
.
A_{\rm LU}^{\sin\phi_{\rm h}}
arises from purely sub-leading twist effects (qgq correlators).

In a transversely polarised nucleon, the asymmetry
A_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}-\phi_{\rm S})}
is induced by the Sivers TMD PDF
f_{\rm 1T}^{\perp}
, which describes the correlation between the quark transverse momentum and the nucleon transverse spin
[
12
]
,
while the asymmetry
A_{\rm UT}^{\sin(\phi_{\rm h}+\phi_{\rm S})}
arises from the transversity
h_{1}
(the transverse counterpart of the helicity PDF
g_{1}
) coupled to the Collins function
H_{1}^{\perp}

Measurements of these structure functions (or asymmetries) in DIS, combined with results from the Drell–Yan process and semi-inclusive hadron production in e
+
e
-
annihilation, allow phenomenologists to extract the TMD PDFs and FFs, which become benchmarks for lattice QCD calculations and models, and play a role in the interpretation of other measurements.

Although for
\upmu{\rm N}\rightarrow\upmu^{\prime}{\rm hX}
at
Q^{2}>1~({\rm GeV}/c)^{2}
DIS is the leading process, there is an important background: the decay of diffractively produced vector mesons V,

\upmu{\rm N}\to\upmu^{\prime}{\rm N^{\prime}V}
,
{\rm V\to h^{+}h^{-}}

The production of
\uprho

\uppi^{+}\uppi^{-}
, has the largest cross section, while
\upphi\to{\rm K^{+}K^{-}}
is important if the production of kaons is studied.
The contamination of the DIS hadron sample is small in most of the phase space, but can reach 50% at large
z
and low
P_{\rm T}
and
Q^{2}
.
In addition, the hadrons exhibit large azimuthal modulations

and
A_{\rm UU}^{\cos 2\phi_{\rm h}}

13

The TMD framework does not account for the radiative effects of QED.
In particular, a real photon emission not only distorts the reconstructed

,
P_{\rm T}
and
\phi_{\rm h}
, calling for radiative corrections

Compass

Conclusions and outlook

Deep inelastic scattering (DIS) is a powerful tool for investigating the transverse-momentum- and transverse-spin-dependent nucleon structure.
Intense work is ongoing on the datasets collected in 2016–2017 with the liquid hydrogen target and in 2022 with the transversely polarised deuteron (
6
LiD) target by the
Compass
Collaboration at CERN.
Both are about to yield interesting results. The hydrogen one because of advances in analysis methods such as the treatment of the background from diffractive vector mesons and radiative corrections, and the other one because of the absolute uniqueness of polarised deuteron data.
