A flower-shaped dual-band four port MIMO antenna with novel DGS for 5G mmWave applications

Mohammed Bendaoued, Anouar Es-saleh, Soufian Lakrit, Sudipta Das, Abdelhamid Bou-El-Harmel, Manish Varun Yadav, Boddapati Traka Phani Madhav, Heba G Mohamed, Daosen Zhai

Abstract

A novel quad-port multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antenna with enhanced isolation is proposed by incorporating a specifically designed hybrid defected ground structure (DGS) for dual band 5G mmWave wireless applications. This suggested MIMO antenna system features a distinctive DGS, consisting of a central circular slot combined with four surrounding trapezoidal cut-outs at the base of the ground plane for enhanced isolation and comprises four identical elliptical monopole elements. Initially, a four-element MIMO system is developed to achieve optimal performance. Subsequently, the conFigd hybrid DGS is included in the proposed structure to improve the isolation by effectively analyzing the impact of alterations in the various geometrical parameters of the DGS. The designed MIMO radiator resonates at 26.73/38.29 GHz and operates from 26.39 to 27.04 GHz and from 38.11 to 38.47 GHz, achieving gains of 4.80 dBi and 7.61 dBi at 26.73 GHz and 38.29 GHz, respectively, maintaining a compact size of 21 mm × 21 mm (1.849 λ × 1.849 λ). The designed four-port MIMO radiator demonstrates exceptional performance in terms of various impedance, radiation and diversity metrics. The design and simulation studies are conducted using the CST simulation tool. Finally, the intended antenna has been prototyped and experimentally tested, confirming the correctness of the simulation analysis. The proposed antenna partially operates within 5G NR mmWave n258/n260 bands and is suitable for 5G Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) applications.

1. Introduction

Managing multiple antennas in the user equipment’s transmitter and/or reception is more complicated than operating a single, well-isolated antenna system. The 5G, or fifth-generation wireless communication networks, are dispersed widely and utilize technology that follows the 4G. The characteristics of MIMO antenna systems include reduced latency, increased data throughput, and improved isolation between antenna parts []. To achieve the unprecedented reliability, capacity, and speed that characterize the fifth generation of wireless communication, MIMO antennas are essential for 5G technology []. In 5G applications, the four-port MIMO antenna system is an essential component among other MIMO design configurations. By using four transmitting and receiving antennas, this setup allows 5G networks to improve their capacity and coverage with increased spatial diversity and multiplexing [–]. Four-port MIMO antennas can enhance five-generation wireless communication performance in several ways, including enhanced data rates, larger network capacity, and improved dependability [,]. The four-port MIMO antennas improve wireless performance by utilizing multiple transmission paths to provide faster data throughput, reliable signal connection, and better network coverage in complex propagation environments. These antennas also help minimize co and adjacent channel interference, which help for better utilization of spectrum contention in congested network [–]. Recent literature introduces innovative antenna configurations like dual-operating four-port MIMO systems to fulfill 5G demands for high speed, low latency, and reliability. This setup transmits and receives multiple data streams simultaneously, boosting transfer speed and network efficiency [].

The performance of antennas can be enhanced by incorporating novel DGS configurations. DGS is a unique electromagnetic structure that helps mitigate interference, improve radiation characteristics, and reduce backward radiation. Specifically, it is critical and challenging to minimize interference and optimize signal quality in 5G applications, which makes DGS a valuable addition to the four-port MIMO antenna []. This innovative antenna configuration and DGS integration contribute to the successful implementation of 5G technology, particularly in scenarios where high-frequency bands are used to achieve ultra-fast data rates and support massive device connectivity. The inclusion of a novel defected ground structure (DGS) with MIMO antennas is a significant design step in optimizing their performance. The strategic design of DGS involves manipulating the electromagnetic properties of the ground plane beneath an antenna. By introducing defects or variations in the ground plane, DGS can achieve various objectives such as reducing interference, improving bandwidth, radiation patterns, radiation efficiency of an antenna. The combination of MIMO technology and DGS holds an enormous promise for 5G uses. It enables the reduction of co-channel interference and enhances the antenna’s capabilities in terms of signal quality, connectivity, and coverage, which are essential factors in realizing the potential of 5G networks for many different uses, including augmented reality, autonomous vehicle, and the Internet of Things [,].

MIMO antennas with DGS in the ground plane offer several advantages in 5G and other wireless applications:

- Improved Isolation or Reduced Mutual Coupling: DGS can help to minimize mutual coupling between radiating elements in a MIMO system, thus, enhancing isolation between channels and reducing interference.
- Enhanced Radiation Patterns: The use of DGS can shape the radiation patterns of MIMO antennas, allowing for better coverage and directionality as needed in different scenarios.
- Bandwidth Improvement: The implementation of DGS can extend the bandwidth of the antenna, enabling support for multiple frequency bands and diverse communication requirements.
- Interference Reduction: The DGS-based antenna is capable to mitigate co-channel interference, improving signal quality in dense network environments. These characteristics make MIMO antennas with DGS in the ground plane a valuable choice for 5G applications, as they enable better performance, coverage, and network efficiency [,].

The next-generation 5G networks require dual-band mmWave MIMO antennas to counteract severe path loss and compact form-factor constraints. By concurrently operating across dual operating frequency bands within the mmWave spectrum, these systems enable extreme data rates and enhance spatial multiplexing, within constrained physical footprint of mobile devices. In this context, researchers have adopted several MIMO antenna design methodologies [–] to support dual band mmWave 5G communications.

The stated objective of this research is to develop a miniature-sized four elements-based dual band MIMO antenna with an overall improved performance. A novel hybrid DGS has been incorporated into the proposed structure to enhance isolation and facilitate miniaturization. A detailed investigation has been conducted on the impact of this hybrid DGS on the performance improvement of the suggested four-port MIMO radiator system. The proposed antenna shows considerable performance in terms of its impedance, radiation, and diversity metrics across the entire dual operating bands. The proposed four-port MIMO antenna achieves impedance bandwidths of about 0.65 GHz (26.39–27.04 GHz) and 0.36 GHz (38.11–38.47 GHz), resonating at 26.73 GHz and 38.29 GHz. The reflection coefficient drops to −60.94 and −16.80 dB at 26.73 and 38.29 GHz, respectively. The gain is 4.80 dBi at 26.73 GHz and 7.61 dBi at 38.29 GHz; the maximum radiation efficiencies are about 97.61% and 95.45% for the first and second band, respectively. The suggested antenna shows an exceptional diversity performance with ECC ≤ 0.00028, DG ≈10 dB, TARC ≤−15.42 dB, CCL < 0.1, MEG <−3 dB, and Multiplexing efficiency > 91.1%.

The novel contribution of this study is the development of a four-port MIMO antenna that combines four identical orthogonally arranged flower-shaped elliptical patch elements with a specifically engineered and systematically optimized hybrid defected ground structure (DGS) to achieve enhanced isolation across two distinct mmWave operating bands. The proposed DGS configuration is uniquely designed by a central circular slot combined with four surrounding trapezoidal slots to effectively improve isolation between the radiating patches while preserving high gain and radiation efficiency performance. Furthermore, it offers a favorable combination of dual-resonant band functionality, high isolation, size miniaturization, reliable radiative performance as well as excellent diversity metrics, ensuring improved signal reliability and maximized data throughput. This integrated design approach distinguishes the proposed antenna from previously reported MIMO structures, making it a potential choice for dual band 5G mmWave communication applications.

The following point summarizes the novel aspects of the suggested work:

- Compact size: The four identical elliptical monopole elements, transformed into flower shaped with a novel hybrid defected ground plane occupies an area of only 21 mm × 21 mm (1.849 λ × 1.849 λ).
- Hybrid DGS configuration: A distinctive DGS, consisting of circular and trapezoidal shapes are strategically incorporated at the ground plane’s base to minimize mutual coupling, improving overall isolation. This helps minimize the electromagnetic interference, improving overall system reliability.
- Dual-Resonance and stable radiation performance: The designed four port MIMO radiator operates from 26.39–27.04 GHz and 38.11–38.47 GHz, partially covering the spectrums of 5G NR n258/n260 bands, yielding stable gains of 4.80 and 7.61 dBi at 26.73 and 38.29 GHz, respectively along with radiation efficiencies of 97.61% and 95.45%, respectively.
- Optimized metrics: Exceptionally low ECC, high DG, competitive CCL, MEG, TARC and desired multiplexing efficiency confirm its superior MIMO diversity performance.
- Performance validation and applicability: All the analyzed results obtained by using CST MWS tool are validated through measurements of the realized antenna prototype. The measurement results confirm its dual band utilization. The proposed antenna partially operates within 5G NR mmWave n258/n260 bands and is suitable for 5G Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) applications.

3. Two-element MIMO antenna: Design perspectives and analysis of structures

Despite providing dual-band resonance with adequate impedance matching, this single-element design restricts operations to single-input-single-output (SISO), which limits data throughput and makes the system susceptible to multipath fading. Consequently, it is inadequate for high-data-rate 5G applications. In contrast, a two-element MIMO antenna deploys two independent radiators to handle multiple data streams simultaneously, effectively doubling capacity and dramatically improving signal reliability. The two-port setup provides a primary foundation for MIMO systems, enabling researchers to quantify mutual coupling, signal correlation, and overarching radiation behavior between antenna elements. Therefore, a two-element MIMO system is designed using two identical unit patch elements. displays four different configurations of the designed two-element MIMO antennas, which are developed by positioning the single-element antenna in various orientations.

These configurations are evaluated based on their reflection coefficients (S11) and isolation characteristics to identify the most suitable design. shows the S11 results: The two parallel elements configuration with a partial ground plane achieves S11 of −18 dB at 26.24 GHz along with an IBW of 0.46 GHz (26.02–26.48 GHz). For the second band, S11 is about −22.43 dB at 37.82 GHz, offering IBW of 0.35 GHz (37.65–38.0 GHz). The two perpendicular elements (case 2) also performs well, showing −18 dB at 26.2 GHz (bandwidth 0.47 GHz) and −16 dB at 37.7 GHz (bandwidth 0.29 GHz). In terms of isolation, both two perpendicular patch elements (case 1 and case 2) configurations demonstrate acceptable values below −15 dB, which is the typical threshold for MIMO systems. In conclusion, the configuration with two perpendicular patch elements (case 1) shows best impedance matching and isolation performance. Therefore, the configuration of two perpendicular patch elements (case 1) is selected as the proposed structure, due to its balanced performance in terms of reflection, isolation, and miniaturization.

4. Proposed four-element configuration of the MIMO antenna

This section investigates the four-element configuration of the MIMO antenna. The four-elemental radiating arrangement yields significantly higher data throughput, vastly improved signal reliability, and stronger link stability—especially intended for modern 5G networks. The configuration of the suggested MIMO radiator, a detailed analysis of the selection of intended ground plane geometry, and the impacts of various defected ground plane (DGS) design parameters are analyzed and discussed in this section.

4.1. Configuration of the antenna

The total dimension of this compact MIMO antenna is 21 × 21 mm2. The proposed four-port MIMO antenna has an electrical size of 1.849 λ × 1.849 λ. 4.2. Selection of ground plane with defected structure

To get optimal performance, the MIMO antenna is initially designed with four elements with ground planes. Secondly, a 15 × 15 mm2 square is used to connect the ground plane, and for greater isolation and miniaturization, the ground plane is made to be defected, disturbing its surface currents flow. Next, to optimize the performance in terms of isolation and compactness, several configurations of defected ground structures (DGS) are explored.

- Step 1–Connected ground plane (cross-shaped connection) [}:

The modelling is initiated by connecting the four ground planes with a complete conductor base to form a continuous ground, as shown in . Compared to the baseline of four isolated ground planes [], the connected ground configuration reduces mutual coupling; this improvement is apparent when comparing the S-parameter plots for the connected and isolated cases [ vs ].

- Step 2 – Circular-slot DGS []:

A circular slot (radius RDGS = 2 mm) is introduced into the baseline ground arrangement to form a circular-slot DGS []. The S-parameters for this case are shown in . While the circular slot modifies the resonance locations and the impedance behaviour, it does not improve isolation relative to the connected ground configuration; in fact, the plots in show increased coupling in some bands compared with . This step is therefore important as it reveals the effect of a central circular perturbation on resonance tuning and coupling, even though it alone does not give the desired isolation improvement.

- Step 3–Trapezoidal-slot DGS [

Finally, four trapezoidal slots (dimensions hQ = 2 mm, WQ = 5.5 mm, HQ = 2.5 mm) are placed symmetrically at each side of the baseline ground to form a trapezoidal-slot DGS. The S-parameters are presented in . This modification is evaluated because it offers a different spectral perturbation than the circular slot; the results show how slot shape and placement influence both resonance alignment and inter-element coupling, guiding the choice of the final ground geometry.

- Final Step – Merged (hybrid) DGS Design []:

Ultimately, the circular and trapezoidal slot designs are combined to form a hybrid DGS structure. This final configuration delivered the best performance, showing in excellent isolation above 19.32 dB and strong dual-band operation at both 26.73 GHz and 38.29 GHz.

This systematic exploration confirmed that while some DGS configurations degraded performance, the optimized merged design achieved significant improvements in bandwidth, isolation, and overall MIMO efficiency.

4.3. Effect of radius of DGS circle (RDGS)

When the radius of the DGS circle (RDGS) changes from 1.6 mm to 2.3 mm, and shows that the lower resonant frequency is affected significantly, with little impact on the higher band resonance frequency. However, prominent variations in both the reflection and isolation parameters are clearly noted due to changes in RDGS. The best reflection coefficient and hence best impedance matching is obtained for the proposed dimension of RDGS = 2 mm, dropping to −60.94 dB at 26.73 GHz and −16.80 dB at 38.29 GHz, respectively. The operating bandwidths are 0.65 GHz (26.39–27.04 GHz) and 0.36 GHz (38.11–38.47 GHz) at 26.73/38.29 GHz, respectively. This indicates that the radius of circular-shaped DGS, RDGS = 2 mm, is utilized in the proposed design. In addition, the suggested design with RDGS = 2 mm ensures an isolation of greater than 19.32 dB among the various radiating elements. At 26.73 GHz, a gain of 4.80 dBi and 97.61% radiation efficiency, and at 38.29 GHz, 7.61 dBi gain and 95.45% radiation efficiency are recorded. The proposed RDGS produces excellent results and improves isolation, as compared to other tested dimensions.

4.4. Effect of the short base (hQ) of DGS trapezoidal

There is a noticeable downward shift in the lower resonant frequency along with significant improvement in reflection coefficient due to an increment (from 1 mm to 2 mm) in the dimensions of the short base (hQ) of the DGS. Similarly, the isolation among patch elements varies due to changes in hQ parameter. According to and , the reflection coefficient drops to −60.94 dB at 26.73 GHz and −16.80 dB at 38.29 GHz, respectively, when the short base (hQ) of the DGS trapezoidal is proposed to be 2 mm, and the impedance bandwidth is around 0.65 GHz (26.39–27.04 GHz) and 0.36 GHz (38.11–38.47 GHz) at 26.73/38.29 GHz, respectively. It exhibits a gain of 4.80/7.61 dBi at 26.73/38.29 GHz, respectively. The corresponding radiation efficiencies are 97.61 and 95.45%, respectively at 26.73 and 38.29 GHz. Although the proposed radiator with other values of hQ provides improved isolation, the proposed hQ of 2 mm yields the optimal balance of impedance matching, gain, radiation efficiency, and isolation.

4.5. Effect of the horizontal base (WQ) of the DGS trapezoidal

The resonant response undergoes a notable change in operational bandwidth and impedance matching when the WQ is changed from WQ = 4.5 mm to WQ = 6 mm. Although the designed antenna shows improved isolation for WQ = 4.5 mm, other crucial parameters like reflection coefficient, gain, radiation efficiency, operating bandwidth deteriorate along with frequency shifts for this case. Further increment of WQ to 6 mm also leads to unsatisfactory results, as summarized in . When the proposed horizontal base (WQ) of the DGS trapezoidal is 5.5 mm, the reflection coefficient improves to −60.94 and −16.80 dB, respectively at 26.73 and 38.29 GHz [ and ]. At 26.73 GHz, the impedance bandwidth is around 0.65 GHz (26.39 to 27.04 GHz), and at 38.29 GHz, it is about 0.36 GHz (38.11 to 38.47 GHz). The analyzed results show a gain of 4.80 dBi along with 97.61% radiation efficiency at 26.73 GHz and at 38.29 GHz, these values correspond to 7.61 dBi and 95.45%, respectively. Hence, the antenna with proposed WQ of 5.5 mm provides an overall balanced performance in comparison to the other two values of WQ.

To understand the physical insight behind the dual-band operation and isolation enhancement, the surface current distributions at 26.73 GHz and 38.29 GHz for different antenna ports are analyzed in –. At 26.73 GHz and 38.29 GHz, the antenna elements exhibit maximum surface current amplitudes of 260 A/m and 1092 A/m, respectively. These values are derived from the surface current distribution, which includes a corresponding color scale. In general, the concentration of the current distributions is observed along the margins of the patches and in the ground plane slots. On the surface of the radiating patch, current distributions are found to be larger in the circular and trapezoidal DGS slots. This indicates that the slots have improved the bandwidth of the antenna by facilitating the transmission of electromagnetic waves. Specifically, at 26.73 GHz , the surface current flows uniformly along the entire length of the patch, signifying that the fundamental mode governs the lower band. Conversely, at 38.29 GHz, intense current concentration shifts towards the slot edges and curvilinear discontinuities, generating a secondary shorter effective path that yields the upper resonant band. Furthermore, the decoupling mechanism introduced by the trapezoidal-shaped based defected ground structure (DGS) is apparent in minimizing mutual coupling among orthogonally placed antenna elements. When an individual port is active, the DGS behaves as a high-impedance band-stop wall. It traps and perturbs the ground plane currents, preventing them from coupling into adjacent elements. This causes the unexcited patches to exhibit minimum current intensity, which directly supports the highly suppressed mutual coupling observed in the S-parameter characteristics.

7. Conclusion

The use of quadri-port MIMO antennas in 5G networks is essential to meet the growing demand for faster data rates, lower latency, and the ability to connect a larger number of devices. The recommended MIMO antenna is designed using a modular approach, starting from a single elliptical patch element, two-element MIMO, and extending to a four-element MIMO configuration to provide improved diversity performance. A novel hybrid defected ground structure (DGS) is introduced to enhance isolation and minimize coupling, while maintaining compact dimensions of 21 × 21 mm2. The proposed model shows dual-band resonance with a gain of 7.22 dBi at upper resonant frequency, and maximum radiation efficiency of 97.61% at 26.73 GHz. Furthermore, the presented four-port radiator demonstrates exceptional diversity performance, proving its efficacy in mitigating multipath fading and ensuring reliable coverage. These experimental findings demonstrate the proposed design to be a promising option for next-generation 5G mmWave applications.
