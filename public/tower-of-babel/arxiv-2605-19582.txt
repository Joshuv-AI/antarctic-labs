The inhomogeneous Khintchine Theorem in dimension two
Demi Allen, Manuel Hauke–Treuer, Felipe A. Ramírez

Abstract
We prove that the inhomogeneous variant of Khintchine’s Theorem holds in dimension 2 2 without any monotonicity assumption. This resolves the last remaining case in the metric theory of inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation: while the monotonicity assumption is known to be unnecessary in dimensions m ¾ 3 m\geqslant 3 and necessary in dimension m = 1 m=1 , the two-dimensional case has remained open. It also settles the final outstanding case of a Khintchine–Groshev-type theorem for the approximation of systems of linear forms, confirming a conjecture of the first and third authors. Our results bring the inhomogeneous theory of metric Diophantine approximation into alignment with its homogeneous counterpart.

Introduction and Statement of Results

The problem addressed in this article concerns the simultaneous
approximation of two real numbers
\alpha_{1}

\alpha_{2}

(u_{1}+{}_{1})/q

To a function

Theorem 1.1

Let

Hausdorff measure statements

It is a matter of ongoing interest to establish metric dichotomies for
measures other than Lebesgue. The foundational result in this
direction is Jarnik’s theorem
[
26
]
, which is the analogue of
Khintchine’s theorem for Hausdorff measures.

Briefly, to a function
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
with

f(x)\to 0\,(x\to 0)
, one associates a measure
\mathcal{H}^{f}
, called the
Hausdorff
f
-measure, that is able to “see” sets on scales
determined by
f
. Often, a set of zero Lebesgue measure is best
understood through the use of an appropriate Hausdorff measure. For
example, the standard middle-thirds Cantor set, whose Lebesgue measure
is zero, is seen by the Hausdorff
f
-measure associated to the
function
f(x)=x^{s}
where
s=\log 2/\log 3
. (See
[
16
]
.)

In 2006, Beresnevich and Velani established the Mass Transference
Principle
[
9
]
, a result that relates the Lebesgue measures
of limsup sets of balls to the Hausdorff measures of limsup sets of
suitably dilated balls. Using this, they showed that Khintchine’s
theorem actually implies Jarnik’s theorem. The Mass Transference
Principle has since become a standard tool for deducing metric
dichotomies for Hausdorff measures from corresponding Lebesgue-measure
statements, and many variants of it have appeared in the
literature. (See
[
5
]
for an overview.) One such
variant is
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, a version of the Mass
Transference Principle for systems of linear forms. By applying it, we
deduce the following theorems from the general univariate and
multivariate statements above. The contributions coming
from ?THM?
are the cases where
m=2
.

Theorem

(Hausdorff Measure Multivariate Khintchine–Groshev
Theorem)
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
, and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
arbitrary. Let

f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
g:r\to g(r):=r^{-m(n-1)}f(r)
be
dimension functions such that
r^{-nm}f(r)
is monotonic. Then
for any function
\psi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{f}\left(W_{n,m}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\psi)\right)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{m}g\left\lparen\frac{\psi(\mathbf{q})}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen<\infty,\\[8.61108pt]
\mathcal{H}^{f}\left([0,1]^{mn}\right)&\textrm{if }\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{m}g\left\lparen\frac{\psi(\mathbf{q})}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{H}^{f}
denotes the Hausdorff
f
-measure.

Theorem

(Hausdorff Measure Univariate Khintchine–Groshev Theorem)
.

Let
mn\geqslant 2
, and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
arbitrary. Let

f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and

be

is monotonic. Then for

Heuristics and a model problem

In this section, we explain the obstacles to proving
Theorem
and others like it, how those obstacles
were overcome in preceding works that solved the corresponding problem
in higher
n
-by-
m
-dimensions, and why the methods of those
preceding works fail in the
(n,m)=(1,2)
case. We also present a
model problem that highlights the key ideas of this manuscript and
serves to give an overview of the steps in the proof
of ?THM?
.

Most of the discussion takes place in the simultaneous
setting (that is, where
n=1
), where the goal is to simultaneously
approximate the components of a vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Quasi-independence on average.

In all these Khintchine-type questions, one asks about the measure of

\limsup_{q\to\infty}E_{q}
where
{E_{q}:=}E_{q}(\psi)
is a union of
small hypercubes. The first Borel–Cantelli lemma proves the
convergence part of the metric dichotomy immediately, and one is left
with the task of showing that the limsup set has full measure under
the divergence assumption. If we had perfect stochastic independence
of the set system
(E_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
, then the second
Borel–Cantelli Lemma would immediately provide us with the
result. However, the system does not satisfy this strong independence
assumption, and the second Borel–Cantelli lemma is in practice
useless. Instead, there are refined versions of it that guarantee
positive measure of
\limsup_{q\to\infty}E_{q}
under weaker
independence assumptions on
(E_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
. One commonly-used such
refinement—the one we use here—guarantees positive measure as long
as we can show the second moment bound

\sum_{q\neq r\leqslant Q}\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})\leqslant C\left(\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\lambda(E_{q})\right)^{2}

(2)

for infinitely many
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, where
C>0
is an absolute
constant
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
. In applications to Khintchine-type
problems, additional equidistributive properties of the sets
E_{q}

allow one to strengthen the conclusion from positive measure to full
measure. Therefore, most of the effort goes toward
establishing (
2
),
quasi-independence on average
.

Overlap estimates.

The above mentioned unions of hypercubes are

\displaystyle E_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in[0,1]^{m}:\lvert q\boldsymbol{\alpha}-\mathbf{u}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\rvert<\psi(q)\textrm{ for some }\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\right\}

(3)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}B\left\lparen\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q},\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right\rparen\cap[0,1]^{m}

where
B(\mathbf{x},r)
denotes the
\lvert\cdot\rvert
-ball (a hypercube)
centered at
\mathbf{x}
having radius
r
.

In order to establish (
2

sufficiently sharp upper bounds for
\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})
, usually
known as
overlap estimates
. An elementary calculation (see
e.g.
[
20
, Chapter 3]

\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})\ll\left(\min\left\{\frac{\psi(q)}{q},\frac{\psi(r)}{r}\right\}\right)^{m}\cdot\#\underbrace{\left\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}_{r}^{m}:\left|\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{r}\right|<\frac{X}{qr}\right\}}_{C(q,r)},

(4)

where
X:=X(q,r)=\max\left\{2r\psi(q),2q\psi(r)\right\}
and

\mathbb{Z}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}/(q\mathbb{Z})
. The set
C(q,r)
consists of points

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}

(2X)^{m}
,
which serves as a crude estimate of
\#C(q,r)
with an error that is

O(\gcd(q,r)^{m})

X\geqslant\gcd(q,r)

\#C(q,r)\ll X^{m}

\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})\ll\lambda(E_{q})\lambda(E_{r})

Once again, this is too much to hope for in practice.
Accepting that

\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})\ll\lambda(E_{q})\lambda(E_{r})+\left(\gcd(q,r)\right)^{m}\left(\min\left\{\frac{\psi(q)}{q},\frac{\psi(r)}{r}\right\}\right)^{m},

(8)

where

S_{q}:=\{\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{2}:\gcd(u_{1},u_{2},q)=1\}
.
A routine computation shows that
\#S_{q}\gg q^{2}
and therefore

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\gg\lambda(E_{q})
. (By contrast, when
m=1
the
corresponding surgery leads to a factor of
\varphi(q)/q
in the new
measure. The monotonicity assumption in Khintchine’s theorem ensures
that this new factor will not affect the divergence of the measure
sum, and the difficulty of the Duffin–Schaeffer Conjecture lay in
contending with this new factor for general functions
\psi
.)

The same reasoning that led to (
4
) now gives

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})\ll\left(\min\left\{\frac{\psi(q)}{q},\frac{\psi(r)}{r}\right\}\right)^{2}\cdot\#\left\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}:\left|\frac{\mathbf{u}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}}{r}\right|<\frac{X}{qr}\right\}.

(9)

In the formerly problematic cases where
X(q,r)<\gcd(q,r)
, we now have

\left\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}:\left|\frac{\mathbf{u}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}}{r}\right|<\frac{X}{qr}\right\}=\left\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}:\frac{\mathbf{u}}{q}=\frac{\mathbf{v}}{r}\right\},

which, by the primitivity assumptions
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}
,
is empty when
q\neq r
. Therefore, the previous issue vanishes and
one can deduce (
2
) for
(E_{q}^{\prime})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
.

This approach has been generalized to allow for a
rational

inhomogeneous parameter
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\mathbf{A}/B=(A_{1}/B,A_{2}/B)
(see e.g.
[
8
,
29
]
). Here, one replaces the set

S_{q}
in the definition of
E_{q}^{\prime}
with the set

\{\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{2}:\gcd(u_{1}B+A_{1},u_{2}B+A_{2},Bq)=1\},

(10)

and the basic overlap estimates become

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})\ll\left(\min\left\{\frac{\psi(q)}{q},\frac{\psi(r)}{r}\right\}\right)^{2}\cdot\#\left\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}:\left|\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{r}\right|<\frac{X}{qr}\right\}.

(11)

The primitivity condition in (
10
) now forbids
solutions
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
to

\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{r}=0,

(12)

so that

\left\lvert\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{r}\right\rvert\geqslant\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{qrB},\quad(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}.

(13)

This separation is used to bound the number of solutions counted
in (
11
), leading to

\displaystyle\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ll\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})\mathbb{1}_{\left[2D\geqslant 1/B\right]}

(14)

\displaystyle\ll B^{2}\cdot\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime}).

Since
B
is fixed, it can be absorbed into the implied constant
and (
2
) follows for
E_{q}^{\prime}
.

Clearly, this approach would have to be modified in order for it to
apply in the case of irrational
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. One natural modification is
to let the primitivity condition in (
10
) depend on a
sequence
\{\mathbf{A}_{k}/B_{k}\}_{k}
of increasingly good rational
approximations to
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. Such primitivity conditions were applied
by Schmidt in
[
40
]
, and also more recently by Chow–Technau
in
[
12
]
, where they coined the term “shift-reduced fractions”.
Reproducing the above discussion in this setting leads to

\ll B_{k}^{2}\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})
. Since
B_{k}
grows unboundedly,
the implicit constant does not absorb it, and (
2
) does not
immediately follow. Therefore, the strategy requires other
ingredients.

A recent paper of Kim
[
30
]
introduces one such ingredient in
the form of an extra assumption on
\psi
. Kim uses a shift-reduction
strategy to prove (
1
) under the added assumption
that
\psi(q)=O(q^{-\delta})
for some
\delta>0
. The effect of
this assumption is to introduce an upper bound on the quantity
X/qr

which, when combined with his choice of shift-reduction, causes

C(q,r)
in (
11
) to be empty when

\gcd(q,r)>q^{1-{\varepsilon}}
, for a suitably chosen
{\varepsilon}>0
. Meanwhile,
the GCD sum (
6
) is bounded
\ll q^{2}
when restricted by
the condition
\gcd(q,r)\leqslant q^{1-{\varepsilon}}
, and (
2
) follows.

The proof of ?THM?
also involves a
shift-reduction scheme, but before introducing it, let us describe a
more delicate view of the obstacle arising from small values of

X(q,r)
. This view, when combined with our shift-reductions, will
give crucial refinements of (
14
).

A more delicate view.

Recall that (
5
) comes from regarding
the second term in the estimate

\#C(q,r)\ll X^{2}+\gcd(q,r)^{2}

as unavoidable. It is more accurate to say that it is not

always
avoidable, and we profit from remembering that it only
arises from solutions
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
to

\left|\frac{\mathbf{u}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{r}\right|<\frac{X}{qr}

(15)

when
X
is much smaller than
\gcd(q,r)
. Such solutions are rare,
and it is possible that for some
q,r
, there are none. Taking this possibility into account, a straightforward calculation
shows

\lambda(E_{q}\cap E_{r})\ll\lambda(E_{q})\lambda(E_{r})+\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q})\lambda(E_{r})\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\rVert<D\right]},

(16)

where
D(q,r):=X(q,r)/\gcd(q,r)
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes the
distance to the nearest integer
vector with respect to the maximum norm.

Dropping the indicator variable reproduces (
5
),
which we know is too crude for our purposes in dimension
m=2
. The
desired advantage from including the indicator comes from the
equidistribution of
(n\boldsymbol{\gamma})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
modulo one. A
natural hope is that
\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\pmod{1}
sees that
equidistribution and that the indicator takes a value
\ll D^{2}
on
average. Note however that
D:=D(q,r)
depends on
\psi(q)
and

\psi(r)
and may therefore behave erratically. Imposing a regularity
condition on
\psi
(such as monotonic decrease or an
Erdős–Vaaler-type condition as in Yu
[
44
]
) allows indeed to take advantage of equidistribution. But a general
treatment requires us to allow for the possibility that the behavior
of
\psi
prevents the direct use of equidistribution.

It turns out that the most decisive obstruction in non-monotonic
approximations comes from the possibility that the
support

of
\psi
might be quite sparse. The recent paper
[
11
]
formalizes this by producing
Duffin–Schaeffer-type counterexamples that, unlike the classical
examples, are monotonic on their support and in fact can have
prescribed values on their support. This suggests that the

regularity
of
\psi
on its support is more of a technical
burden, and we are thus motivated to study the following model
problem which is free from that burden by design.

A model problem.

We make the simplifying assumption that

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma,\gamma)
for some irrational
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
Assume a large dyadic range
[T,2T]
to be given, and let

\mathcal{M}=\supp\psi\cap[T,2T]
with

\lvert\mathcal{M}\rvert=M
, where
M
is a quantity that does not
depend on
T
. Now for
q\in\mathcal{M}
, suppose

\psi(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\quad\textrm{so that}\quad\sum_{T\leqslant q\leqslant 2T}\psi(q)^{2}=1.

The second term in (
16
) now takes the form

\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q})\lambda(E_{r})\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\rVert<D\right]}=\frac{1}{D^{2}M^{2}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<D\right]},

and the goal is to control it on average over the pairs

(q,r)\in[T,2T]
. Note that only the pairs with
D(q,r)<1
present
any concern. We aim to show that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}\subseteq{[T,2T]}\\
|\mathcal{M}|=M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{2j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\neq r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[{\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert}<2^{-j}\right]}\ll 1,

(17)

with an implicit constant that neither depends on
T
nor on
M
.

We make the following crucial observation: If
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}
,
then
\max\{\frac{q}{\gcd(q,r)},\frac{r}{\gcd(q,r)}\}\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}
,
and consequently
\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}
. Note that
this is a bound in
M
, and not in
T
, which is key to having any
chance of proving (
17
). We now rewrite

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\neq r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[{\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert}<2^{-j}\right]}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\\
{\lVert h\gamma\rVert<2^{-j}}\end{subarray}}f_{j}(h)

where

f_{j}(h):=\#\left\{(q,r)\in\mathcal{M}:D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j},\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}=h\right\}.

For fixed
q\in\mathcal{M}
,
there are at most
2^{-j}\sqrt{M}
choices for
e:=\frac{r}{\gcd(q,r)}
,
and for fixed
e
, there is at most one
r
solving the system

\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}=h,\quad\frac{r}{\gcd(q,r)}=e.

Thus we obtain
f_{j}(h)\ll M^{3/2}2^{-j}
, which gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\\
\lVert h\gamma\rVert\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}f_{j}(h)\ll M^{3/2}2^{-j}\underbrace{\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\lVert h\gamma\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}}_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}.

It is vital to find a good estimate of the cardinality
\mathcal{B}_{j}

of the Bohr set. Note that by simply dropping the condition

\lVert h\gamma\rVert<2^{-j}
, we obtain the Bohr set estimate

\mathcal{B}_{j}\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}
for
j\ll\log M
and
\mathcal{B}_{j}=0

for
j\gg\log M
. Plugging this bound into the
left-hand side of (
17
), gives

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}\subseteq[T,2T]\\
|\mathcal{M}|=M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{2j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}\ll\sum_{j\ll\log M}1,

which fails to prove (
17
).

Evidently, we must
win back a factor
\mathcal{B}_{j}\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}g(j)
with

\sum_{j}g(j)\ll 1
by making consequential use of the condition

\lVert h\gamma\rVert<2^{-j}
. This can be achieved if
\gamma

has finite Diophantine exponent
\tau<\infty
, meaning
that
\lVert h\gamma\rVert\gg h^{-\tau}
for all
h\geqslant 1
. In this
case, the members of the Bohr set are pairwise separated by

\gg 2^{j/\tau}
and we get

\mathcal{B}_{j}\ll(2^{-j}\sqrt{M})2^{-j/\tau}
, leading to

g(j):=2^{-j/\tau}
. This solves the model problem
for non-Liouville
\gamma
, and suggests a strategy for proving
Theorem
for non-Liouville parameters

\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

With a more careful analysis, one can use this same strategy to
solve the model problem for parameters that are “tamely
Liouville,” for example in the sense that

\lVert h\gamma\rVert\gg\exp(-\sqrt{h})
. Note however that
for wildly Liouville
\gamma
, or in the extremal case rational
numbers, the crude bound
\mathcal{B}_{j}\ll 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}
is all one
can hope for.

On the other hand, we have already described a strategy that
works for rational parameters, namely, to impose the primitivity
condition (
10
) and thereby avoid the issues
that arise from small
X(q,r)
. It turns out that the idea for
irrational parameters of adapting (
10
) to a
sequence of increasingly good rational approximations becomes
useful when combined with the more delicate
estimate (
16
).

Solution to the model problem using shift reductions.

Continuing with the model problem, we define

A/B:=A(M)/B(M)
to be a rational number such that

\left\lvert\gamma-\frac{A}{B}\right\rvert<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}},

(18)

with
B
minimal. Since
\psi(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
,
the triangle inequality shows that every solution

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
to (
15
) is also a solution to

\left\lvert\frac{\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{A}/B}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{A}/B}{r}\right\rvert<\frac{2X}{qr},

(19)

where
\mathbf{A}=(A,A)
.

We now define
S_{q}
as in (
10
). For the same reasons that lead to (
14
), we see that (
19
) can only have solutions

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}
if
2D(q,r)\geqslant 1/B
. Incorporating
this into the reasoning that led to (
14
) and (
16
) gives
the refined estimate (see Lemma
)

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})\ll\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<D\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[2D\geqslant 1/B\right]},

(20)

where the sets
E_{q}^{\prime}
are defined by (
8
), now with
the currently relevant, shift-reduced definition of
S_{q}
. This
produces a refined version of the model
problem (
17
), namely, we must show

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}\subseteq[T,2T]\\
|\mathcal{M}|=M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{2j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[2^{j-1}\leqslant B(M)\right]}\ll 1.

(21)

Importantly, the implied constant must be absolute, and is in
particular not allowed to depend on
B
, since
B=B(M)
grows with

M
. By following the arguments from above, one sees that
establishing (
21
) reduces to showing

\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\end{subarray}}2^{j}\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\gamma\right\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}\ll 1.

(22)

By (
18
) and the triangle inequality, it suffices to show

\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\end{subarray}}2^{j}\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\ll 1.

(23)

The Bohr set sum is now associated to a
rational
rotation, and
estimating it is a matter of understanding the distribution of
rational rotations (compare Lemma
).

If
2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\geqslant B
, then

\{h(A/B):1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\}
is a full rotation
orbit, and we have

\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\ll(2^{-j+1}B+1)\frac{2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}{B}\ll 2^{-2j}\sqrt{M},

recalling that in (
23
) we only consider
j
for
which
2^{j-1}\leqslant B
.
It follows that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\\
2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\geqslant B\end{subarray}}2^{j}\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}M}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\end{subarray}}2^{-j}\ll 1.

On the other hand, if
2^{-j}\sqrt{M}<B
, then

\{h(A/B):1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}\}
is a partial orbit omitting
0
. We
have

\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant B-1}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\ll B2^{-j+1}.

This estimate provides us with

\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\\
2^{-j}\sqrt{M}<B\end{subarray}}2^{j}\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\left\lparen\frac{A}{B}\right\rparen\right\rVert<2^{-j+1}\right]}\ll\frac{B}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j-1}\leqslant B\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{B\log B}{\sqrt{M}}.

If there were a fixed parameter
0<\sigma<1
and a guarantee
that
B\leqslant M^{\sigma/2}
, then this estimate would lead
to (
23
), as desired. Fixing such a
parameter, we are left only with the case where
B>M^{\sigma/2}
.

In this case, we modify the shift-reduction in a way that can be
seen as one of the main technical innovations in this article.
Rather than shift-reducing with respect to
\mathbf{A}/B
, we shift-reduce
with respect to
\mathbf{a}/b
, where
1\leqslant b<B
and

b\left|\gamma-\frac{a}{b}\right|=\lVert b\gamma\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{B}<M^{-\sigma/2}

(24)

with
\mathbf{a}=(a,a)
. The rational number
a/b
is guaranteed by
Dirichlet’s theorem to exist. We now have no solutions
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})

to (
12
) (having replaced
(\mathbf{A},B)
with
(\mathbf{a},b)
),
which implies (
13
) (having made the same
replacement). Meanwhile, the triangle inequality shows that any
solution
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in S_{q}\times S_{r}
to (
15
) is also a solution
to

\left\lvert\frac{\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{a}/b}{q}-\frac{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{a}/b}{r}\right\rvert<\frac{X}{qr}+\left\lvert\frac{(q-r)\lVert b\gamma\rVert}{qrb}\right\rvert

which, when combined with (
13
) (with
\mathbf{a},b
instead
of
\mathbf{A},B
), gives

\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{qrb}\leqslant\frac{X}{qr}+\left\lvert\frac{(q-r)\lVert b\gamma\rVert}{qrb}\right\rvert.

Multiplying by
qrb/\gcd(q,r)
gives

1\leqslant Db+\left\lvert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\right\rvert\lVert b\gamma\rVert\leqslant Db+D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert.

Incorporating this into the reasoning
that led to (
20
) gives

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})\ll\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<D\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[1\leqslant Db+D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert\right]},

(25)

which will lead to a newly refined model problem.

Splitting into the cases
D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{2}

and
D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert>\frac{1}{2}
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}_{\left[1\leqslant Db+D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert\right]}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{1}_{\left[D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert\leqslant 1/2\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[2D\geqslant 1/b\right]}+\mathbb{1}_{\left[D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert>1/2\right]},

and fixing a small parameter
0<\rho<\sigma
, we find

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{1}_{\left[2b>D^{-\rho}\right]}+\mathbb{1}_{\left[D\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert>1/2\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[2b\leqslant D^{-\rho}\right]}.

As before, we stratify over dyadic values of
D
to arrive at the
model problem we need to solve when
B>M^{\sigma/2}
. It is

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}\subseteq[T,2T]\\
|\mathcal{M}|=M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{M^{2}}\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2b>2^{\rho j}\end{subarray}}2^{2j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2b\leqslant 2^{\rho j}\\
2^{j}<\sqrt{M}\lVert b\gamma\rVert\end{subarray}}2^{2j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,r\in\mathcal{M}\\
D(q,r)\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert\right]<2^{-j}}\right\rparen\ll 1.

(26)

Again, the treatment of this problem requires suitable
estimates on

\sum_{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert h\gamma\right\rVert<2^{-j}\right]}.

Depending on the case, we employ different bounds for the Bohr sets in
the discussion (compare ?THM?
): Decomposing
h=mb+s
and using (
24
),
we note that

\#\{1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}\sqrt{M}:\lVert h\gamma\rVert\leqslant 2^{-j}\}\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}{b}}\sum_{1\leqslant s\leqslant b}\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert s\left\lparen\frac{a}{b}\right\rparen+mb\gamma\right\rVert\leqslant(2^{-j}+M^{-\sigma/2})\right]}.

(27)

In the case
2b>2^{\rho j}
, we bound this further from above by

\begin{split}\frac{2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}{b}\underbrace{\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\#\{1\leqslant s\leqslant b:\left\lVert s\left\lparen\frac{a}{b}\right\rparen+\beta\right\rVert\leqslant(2^{-j}+M^{-\sigma/2})\}}_{\ll b(2^{-j}+M^{-\sigma/2})+1}\ll 2^{-2j}\sqrt{M}+2^{-j}M^{(1-\sigma)/2}+\frac{2^{-j}\sqrt{M}}{b}.\end{split}

This yields that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j}\leqslant\sqrt{M}\\
2b>2^{\rho j}\end{subarray}}2^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant h\leqslant 2^{-j}M\\
\lVert h\gamma\rVert\leqslant 2^{-j}\end{subarray}}1\ll 1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
2^{j}\leqslant\sqrt{M}\end{subarray}}M^{-\sigma/2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
b>2^{\rho j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{b}\ll 1+\frac{\log M}{M^{\sigma/2}}+\frac{\log b}{b}\ll 1,

bounding the first sum in (
26

In summary of the solution of the model problem.

The treatment of the model problem can be summarized as follows. Once
we have shift-reduced, we obtain the overlap estimate

\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime}\cap E_{r}^{\prime})\ll\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{D^{2}}\lambda(E_{q}^{\prime})\lambda(E_{r}^{\prime})\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<D\right]}\mathbb{1}_{\left[2D\geqslant 1/B\right]},

which holds regardless of

If
\gamma

\mathbb{1}_{\left[\left\lVert\frac{q-r}{\gcd(q,r)}\gamma\right\rVert<D\right]}

would lead to

this would not work for irrational
\gamma

If
\gamma

q\gamma\pmod{1}
,
as described. In fact, this strategy is viable even without
shift-reductions. But it does not work for Liouville
\gamma
, even
with the shift-reductions.

For Liouville
\gamma
, we cannot drop either of the indicators, but
instead must use both simultaneously. Each indicator has a different
regime where—through the fine-tuning of the shift-reduction
scheme—it unfolds its full power. The different regimes cover the
full range of possibilities, and, in particular, this strategy works
equally well for non-Liouville
\gamma
as it does for Liouville

\gamma

\gamma
has Diophantine type

M^{\frac{1}{2}\left\lparen\frac{1}{1+\tau}\right\rparen}\ll B\ll M^{\frac{1}{2}\left\lparen\frac{\tau}{1+\tau}\right\rparen}.

(29)

If we select

with respect to

of the choice of
\sigma

Beyond the model problem.

Of course, the model problem is a much simplified version of the main

The model problem’s assumption that

We end this section with some remarks on the
