Calcaneal locking intramedullary nailing versus extensile lateral plating for displaced intra‑articular calcaneal fractures: a propensity‑weighted cohort study focusing on perioperative soft‑tissue burden and early recovery trajectories
Authors: Liang Liu, Dong Wang, Qidong Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDisplaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures (DIACFs) are among the most challenging hindfoot injuries and are often associated with persistent disability, reduced quality of life, and treatment-related complications [1, 2]. Although extensile lateral open reduction and plate fixation can restore calcaneal anatomy, it is accompanied by substantial soft-tissue morbidity [3–6]. Locking intramedullary nailing (IMN) is less invasive, yet comparative studies often emphasize overall complication rates and long-term functional scores, with limited quantification of perioperative soft-tissue management burden and early recovery trajectories [9, 10].MethodsWe performed a retrospective cohort study comparing locking IMN with extensile lateral plating (ORIF) in adults with DIACFs. The primary outcomes were (1) a Soft‑Tissue Burden Index (STBI) incorporating drain utilization, postoperative drainage volume, wound leakage duration, dressing changes, aspiration for hematoma/seroma, and soft‑tissue reintervention; and (2) early recovery trajectories using repeated pain scores (VAS) and patient‑reported outcomes (Foot Function Index, FFI) at prespecified time points (2, 6, and 12 weeks; 6 and 12 months). Confounding was addressed using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) [13–15, 17].ResultsNinety patients were included (IMN n = 28; ORIF n = 62). After IPTW, baseline covariates were balanced. IMN demonstrated a lower STBI than ORIF (weighted mean 1.3 ± 1.1 vs. 3.5 ± 1.4; p < 0.001), driven by reduced drain utilization (3/28 [11%] vs. 59/62 [95%]; p < 0.001) and fewer dressing changes within 14 days (2.2 ± 1.5 vs. 6.4 ± 3.1; p < 0.001). Early recovery favored IMN, with lower VAS at 6 weeks (2.5 ± 1.2 vs. 3.7 ± 1.4; p < 0.001) and 12 weeks (1.7 ± 1.0 vs. 2.4 ± 1.2; p = 0.020), and better FFI at 6 weeks (52.3 ± 14.7 vs. 63.1 ± 15.4; p < 0.001) and 12 weeks (34.4 ± 12.3 vs. 43.2 ± 13.1; p = 0.003). Time to full weight bearing was shorter in IMN (10.7 ± 2.3 vs. 12.9 ± 2.6 weeks; p = 0.004). Twelve‑month functional outcomes were similar (FFI 16.8 ± 8.6 vs. 18.2 ± 9.4; p = 0.520). Wound‑related complications were lower in IMN (1/28 [4%] vs. 12/62 [19%]; p = 0.048).ConclusionsLocking IMN showed lower perioperative soft‑tissue burden and faster early recovery compared with extensile lateral plating, while maintaining similar mid‑term outcomes.

Introduction

Displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures (DIACFs) remain challenging injuries with long-term consequences for hindfoot biomechanics, pain, work capacity, and quality of life [1, 2]. Contemporary reviews have emphasized that these fractures often lead to persistent disability and substantial socioeconomic burden, while the overall management of calcaneal fractures remains controversial because improved radiographic restoration does not invariably translate into superior patient-reported outcomes and surgery itself carries important risks [1, 2].

Soft-tissue complications are central to this controversy. The extensile lateral approach provides broad visualization for posterior facet reduction and restoration of calcaneal morphology, but it requires wide flap elevation and creates dead space, predisposing patients to wound edge necrosis, infection, and delayed wound healing [1–4]. Recent comparative literature has continued to show that extensile approaches are associated with higher skin complication rates and adverse joint sequelae than less invasive approaches, while fracture morphology and complexity also influence postoperative prognosis [4–8].

Locking intramedullary nailing (IMN) has emerged as a less invasive alternative that may mitigate soft-tissue morbidity while maintaining stable fixation [9, 10]. However, most comparative studies still emphasize broad complication rates or long-term functional scores rather than the day-to-day perioperative burden imposed by drainage, wound leakage, dressing changes, aspiration procedures, and early recovery [9, 10]. The present study therefore compared locking IMN with extensile lateral plating for DIACFs, focusing on (1) a composite Soft-Tissue Burden Index (STBI) and (2) early recovery trajectories. We hypothesized that IMN would reduce perioperative soft-tissue burden and accelerate early recovery while maintaining comparable radiographic restoration and mid-term outcomes.

Methods

Study design and setting

We conducted a retrospective cohort study at The Second Hospital of Shanxi Medical University between January 1, 2021 and December 31, 2023. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional review board.

Participants

Inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) displaced intra‑articular calcaneal fracture confirmed by CT and classified as Sanders type II or III; (3) surgical treatment using either locking IMN or extensile lateral plating; and (4) minimum follow‑up of 12 weeks. Exclusion criteria: Sanders type IV fractures, severe pre‑existing ipsilateral hindfoot disease, inadequate records for key outcomes, and patients unable to follow standardized rehabilitation due to severe polytrauma.

Fracture classification and imaging

Fractures were classified using the Sanders classification on preoperative CT scans. Two independent surgeons assigned the Sanders type; disagreements were resolved by consensus. Only Sanders II–III fractures were included in the comparative analyses.

Surgical techniques

All procedures were performed by the same orthopedic trauma team, with attending surgeons experienced in both techniques. IMN group: reduction through minimally invasive windows and nail insertion according to the manufacturer’s technique (e.g., C‑Nail system), with locking screws as required. ORIF group: extensile lateral approach with direct reduction of the posterior facet and fixation using a lateral calcaneal plate. Perioperative antibiotics, tourniquet usage, and analgesia followed institutional protocols.

Drain and soft‑tissue care protocol

ORIF cases routinely received a closed-suction drain, removed at 24–48 h per protocol [11]. IMN cases did not routinely receive a drain; drains were used only if intraoperative oozing or dead space was considered substantial. Dressing changes were recorded during hospitalization and for the first 14 postoperative days.

Rehabilitation

All patients followed a standardized rehabilitation pathway: ankle and subtalar range-of-motion exercises beginning after wound stabilization, non-weight bearing for the initial phase, then progressive partial weight bearing (PWB) and full weight bearing (FWB) guided by clinical and radiographic signs of healing [12]. Time to PWB and FWB were recorded in weeks.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes were: (1) the Soft‑Tissue Burden Index (STBI), a prespecified composite score designed to quantify perioperative soft‑tissue management burden; and (2) early recovery trajectories assessed by repeated pain scores (VAS) and patient‑reported outcomes (Foot Function Index, FFI) at prespecified time points.

STBI was calculated by summing six prespecified components related to postoperative soft-tissue management: drain utilization, 0–48 h drain output, wound leakage duration, dressing changes, hematoma/seroma aspiration, and soft-tissue reintervention. The scoring rules, thresholds, assigned points, and assessment windows are summarized in Table 1. The total STBI ranged from 0 to 9, with higher scores indicating greater soft-tissue burden. All STBI components were also reported separately. Components and scoring rules of the soft-tissue burden index

FFI was assessed using the original 23-item Foot Function Index, comprising pain, disability, and activity limitation subscales. Scores were normalized to a 0–100 scale, with lower scores indicating better function.

Secondary outcomes included operative time, length of stay (LOS), radiographic parameters, complication rates, reoperations, and subtalar arthrodesis.

Statistical analysis

To reduce confounding, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) based on propensity scores was applied [13–15]. The propensity model included age, sex, BMI, smoking, diabetes, time to surgery, Sanders type, and surgeon experience. Balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD), targeting SMD < 0.10. Weighted linear regression and weighted logistic regression were used for continuous and binary outcomes, respectively. Recovery trajectories across follow-up time points were evaluated using IPTW-weighted mixed-effects models, with fixed effects for treatment group (IMN vs. ORIF), time, and the group × time interaction, and a random intercept for each patient to account for within-patient correlation. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Secondary outcomes

Operative time was shorter in IMN (78.0 ± 18.0 min) compared with ORIF (105.0 ± 22.0 min; p < 0.001). Length of stay was also shorter (6.1 ± 1.8 vs. 8.0 ± 2.4 days; p < 0.001). Fluoroscopy time was longer in IMN (1.8 ± 0.7 vs. 1.2 ± 0.5 min; p = 0.010). Radiographic restoration was comparable: postoperative Böhler angle was 25.1 ± 6.0 (IMN) versus 24.4 ± 5.8 (ORIF; p = 0.620), with similar maintenance at last follow‑up (23.2 ± 5.9 vs. 22.8 ± 6.1; p = 0.810).

Complications and reoperations

Wound‑related complications were lower in IMN (1/28, 4%) than ORIF (12/62, 19%). Hardware irritation occurred in 3 IMN and 4 ORIF cases. One ORIF patient developed deep infection requiring debridement and prolonged antibiotics. At 12 months, symptomatic subtalar arthritis was documented in 2 IMN and 3 ORIF cases; subtalar arthrodesis was performed in 0 IMN and 1 ORIF case. Discussion

This study emphasizes perioperative soft-tissue management burden and early recovery trajectories when comparing locking intramedullary nailing (IMN) with extensile lateral plating for DIACFs. IMN was associated with a substantially lower STBI, reflecting reduced need for drains, fewer dressing changes, and fewer soft-tissue interventions. Early pain relief and functional recovery within 12 weeks were also faster after IMN, while 12-month patient-reported outcomes and radiographic maintenance were similar between groups. The present findings suggest that the principal advantage of IMN lies in the perioperative and early rehabilitation phases, whereas mid-term functional outcomes appear to converge over time.

These findings should be interpreted in the broader context of calcaneal fracture care. DIACFs are frequently followed by long-term pain, disability, and reduced quality of life, and the optimal management strategy remains debated because anatomical restoration must be balanced against the risk of treatment-related complications [1, 2]. In this setting, perioperative soft-tissue burden is not a trivial secondary issue; it is closely linked to patient comfort, nursing workload, outpatient resource use, and the pace of rehabilitation.

Our results are directionally consistent with the contemporary literature emphasizing the soft-tissue price of extensile exposures. Meta-analyses comparing extensile lateral and less invasive approaches have consistently reported fewer wound problems with limited-incision techniques [5, 6, 16], and a recent randomized trial found higher rates of skin complications and subtalar osteoarthritis after the extensile lateral approach than after the sinus tarsi approach [4]. Modern reviews of DIACFs likewise note that wound-healing problems remain a major limitation of extensile lateral fixation and that tissue-sparing strategies may achieve comparable restoration with lower soft-tissue morbidity [1, 2]. Although our comparison involved IMN rather than sinus tarsi fixation, the same soft-tissue-preserving principle likely explains the lower STBI and faster early recovery observed in the IMN group. Fracture morphology and structural complexity may also influence prognosis, which further supports individualized surgical decision-making in this patient population [7, 8].

An additional contribution of the present study is the use of a composite Soft-Tissue Burden Index rather than reliance on binary wound complications alone. Measures such as drain output, leakage duration, dressing workload, and aspiration procedures capture clinically relevant postoperative burden that is often overlooked in comparative calcaneal fracture studies. This perspective may be particularly useful when evaluating treatment pathways in which radiographic restoration, soft-tissue management, and rehabilitation efficiency must all be considered together.

The interpretation of the STBI should also consider the protocol-driven difference in drain use between the two treatment pathways. In our institution, closed-suction drainage was routinely used after extensile lateral plating because of the larger exposure, wider flap elevation, and potential dead space, whereas drains were not routinely used after IMN. Therefore, the drain-related components of the STBI may partly reflect protocol-driven perioperative management rather than postoperative soft-tissue morbidity alone. Nevertheless, this difference also represents a practical component of perioperative soft-tissue management associated with the extensile lateral approach. To improve transparency, all STBI components were reported separately, allowing drain-related and non-drain-related parameters to be considered alongside the total STBI.

This study has several limitations, including its retrospective design, susceptibility to selection bias, possible learning-curve effects, and incomplete CT-based assessment of articular reduction. In addition, the STBI was a prespecified, study-specific composite measure and has not been externally validated. Although it was designed to capture clinically relevant aspects of postoperative soft-tissue management, its thresholds and weighting scheme require validation in independent cohorts before broader application. The total STBI may also be influenced by protocol-driven drain use, because closed-suction drainage was routinely used after extensile lateral plating but not after IMN. Therefore, the drain-related components should be interpreted together with the separately reported non-drain-related parameters. The sample size was modest, and residual confounding remains possible despite IPTW adjustment [13–15]. Prospective multicenter studies with standardized rehabilitation, more detailed postoperative imaging, and formal health-economic analyses would strengthen causal inference and help define which patient subgroups benefit most from minimally invasive fixation [9, 10, 12].

Conclusion

In this study, locking IMN was associated with lower perioperative soft-tissue burden and faster early recovery than extensile lateral plating, while maintaining similar radiographic and mid-term outcomes. These findings support the value of tissue-sparing fixation strategies when managing DIACFs.
