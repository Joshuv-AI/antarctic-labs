Andreas Holmsen, Attila Jung, Balázs Keszegh, Dániel G. Simon, Gábor Tardos

Abstract
Graphs whose maximum clique size exceeds half of the total number of vertices satisfy a classical property: the family of their maximum sized cliques can be pierced by a single vertex. This result dates back to Hajnal [ 4 ] . Motivated by this theorem, Jung, Keszegh, Pálvölgyi, and Yuditsky recently conjectured that an analogous result should hold for hypergraphs of larger uniformity, with an appropriate constant replacing the threshold 1 2 \tfrac{1}{2} . In this paper we refute this conjecture in a strong form. We show that for any constant c < 1 c<1 and integers k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , there exist k k -uniform hypergraphs G G whose maximum clique size exceeds c ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | c\,|V(G)| , yet the family of maximum size cliques of G G cannot be pierced by t t vertices. This demonstrates that no universal constant threshold guarantees bounded piercing number for maximum cliques in uniform hypergraphs. We discuss further questions concerning the relationship between clique size and piercing maximum cliques in hypergraphs, and introduce a geometric variant of the problem using Helly’s Theorem.

Introduction

For a uniform hypergraph
G

For a collection

Theorem 1

(Hajnal
[
4
]
)

Let

be a graph and

|\cap\mathcal{Q}|\geq 2\omega(G)-|\cup\mathcal{Q}|

Corollary 2
.

If
G
is a graph with
\omega(G)>\frac{1}{2}v(G)
, then the maximum size cliques of

can be hit with a single vertex.

Jung, Keszegh, Pálvölgyi, and Yuditsky conjectured that something similar should hold for hypergraphs.

Conjecture 3

(Jung, Keszegh, Pálvölgyi, Yuditsky
[
6
]
)

For all

The complement of a

Theorem 4
.

Let

k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
such that
\omega(\mathcal{H})\geq cv(\mathcal{H})
and its maximum size cliques cannot be pierced by

.

We investigate further the relationship between

. Let

be the minimal function, such that if a

-uniform hypergraph

Theorem 5

Let

be integers. There are constants

\frac{1}{C\log k}\left(\frac{tn}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq f_{k,t}(n)\leq c2^{2t}\frac{n}{\log n}.

We prove Theorem

Piercing maximum size cliques by one vertex in
3
-uniform hypergraphs.

In this section we show that there exists a sequence
\mathcal{H}_{m}
of
3
-uniform hypergraphs with
\frac{\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}\to 1

\eta(\mathcal{H})>1

3
-uniform hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}_{m}
for all

\mathcal{H}_{m}

C_{i}
.

The vertex set of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
is the following: let

, and take pairwise disjoint sets
A_{J}

\binom{m-2}{s-1}

. This way we have that the sets

are cliques of equal size and that
\cap_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}=\emptyset
. The specific sizes of the sets

are chosen so that the sets

A_{J}
are an extremal solution of the inequality in Case 1 of the proof of Claim
9

Apart from the sets

being maximum cliques of

\frac{\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}=\frac{|C_{i}|}{|\cup C_{i}|}\to 1

. For this condition,

with any

Claim 6
.

If
c\in(0,1)
,
m\to\infty
, and
s\sim cm

\frac{|C_{1}|}{|\cup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}|}\to 1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{1}|}{|\cup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{m-1}{s-1}+\binom{m-1}{m-2}\binom{m-2}{s-1}}{\binom{m}{s}+\binom{m}{m-1}\binom{m-2}{s-1}}=\frac{\frac{s}{m}\binom{m}{s}+\frac{s(m-s)}{m}\binom{m}{s}}{\binom{m}{s}+\frac{s(m-s)}{m-1}\binom{m}{s}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{s}{m}+\frac{s(m-s)}{m}}{1+\frac{s(m-s)}{m-1}}\sim\frac{c+c(1-c)m}{1+c(1-c)m}\to 1.

∎

We call a family

Claim 7
.

A subset
K

3

K\subset\cup_{J\in\mathcal{F}}A_{J}

3

-intersecting
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[m]}{s}\cup\binom{[m]}{m-1}

Proof.

K
is a clique if and only if every set of three vertices of

is contained in one of the sets

, in notation
\binom{K}{3}\subset\cup_{i=1}^{m}\binom{C_{i}}{3}
. Thus, for any
A_{J_{1}},A_{J_{2}},A_{J_{3}}
with
A_{J_{1}}\cap K\neq\emptyset
,
A_{J_{2}}\cap K\neq\emptyset
,
A_{J_{3}}\cap K\neq\emptyset
, there has to be a shared index

.
∎

We use the following strengthening of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem.

Theorem 8

[

]
)

If

Claim 9

If

Proof.

Any maximal clique

3
-wise

Case 1:
If there is no

\displaystyle|K_{\mathcal{F}}|

\displaystyle=\binom{m-1}{s-1}+\binom{m-1}{m-2}\binom{m-2}{s-1}=|C_{i}|

Observation 10
.

If Theorem
4
is true for

Proof.

If we have a
3
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with
\omega(\mathcal{H})>cv(\mathcal{H})
and
\eta(\mathcal{H})>t
, then for any
3\leq k\leq\omega
, the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{k}
of the

The proof for
t
requires a bit more work. For the construction we need a variant of the lexicographic product of hypergraphs where certain ”mixed” hyperedges are also allowed.

Definition
.

Given two
k
-uniform hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, let
V(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=V(\mathcal{H}_{1})\times V(\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Let
p_{i}:(v_{1},v_{2})\mapsto v_{i}
be the projection to the
i
th coordinate and let

E^{\prime}=\left\{\{x\}\times e_{2}:x\in V(\mathcal{H}_{1}),e_{2}\in E(\mathcal{H}_{2})\right\},

E^{\prime\prime}=\left\{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H}_{1})\times V(\mathcal{H}_{2})}{k}:|p_{1}(e)|\geq 2\text{ and }\exists e_{1}\in E(\mathcal{H}_{1})\text{ with }p_{1}(e)\subseteq e_{1}\right\},

and

E(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=E^{\prime}\cup E^{\prime\prime}.

The only difference with the original lexicographic product is the condition
|p_{1}(e)|\geq 2
instead of
|p_{1}(e)|\geq|e|
(which would in turn imply
p_{1}(e)=e_{1}
) in the definition of
E^{\prime\prime}
. This is crucial for the following claim to hold.

Claim 11
.

We have

1.

v(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=v(\mathcal{H}_{1})v(\mathcal{H}_{2})

2.

\omega(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=\omega(\mathcal{H}_{1})\omega(\mathcal{H}_{2})

3.

\eta(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=\eta(\mathcal{H}_{1})\eta(\mathcal{H}_{2})

Proof.

We have
v(\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2})=|V(\mathcal{H}_{1})\times V(\mathcal{H}_{2})|=v(\mathcal{H}_{1})v(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

Clearly, a set

C
is a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2}
, if and only if
p_{1}(C)
is a subset of a clique
in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
C_{x}:=\{y|(x,y)\in C\}
is a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
for
every
x\in p_{1}(C)
. So
C
is a maximum size clique if and
only if
p_{1}(C)
is a maximum size clique and so is
C_{x}

for every
x\in p_{1}(C)
.

Thus
S
is a hitting set of maximum cliques in
\mathcal{H}_{1}\circ\mathcal{H}_{2}
if and only if
\{x|S_{x}\text{ is a
hitting set in }\mathcal{H}_{2}\}
is a hitting set in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, and for that we need at least
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{1})\eta(\mathcal{H}_{2})
vertices.
∎

4
On a possible weakening of Conjecture
3

We consider a weaker form of Conjecture
3
. Let
f_{k,t}
be the minimal function, such that if a
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
has
v(\mathcal{H})-\omega(\mathcal{H})\leq f_{k,t}(v(\mathcal{H}))
, then
\eta(\mathcal{H})\leq t
. The construction from Section
2
provides an upper bound on
f_{3,1}
as follows.

Claim 12
.

The previously constructed sequence
\mathcal{H}_{m}
of
3
-uniform hypergraphs is such that
v(\mathcal{H}_{m})-\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})=O\left(\frac{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{\log v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}\right)
and
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{m})>1
. Hence,

f_{3,1}(n)=O\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right).

Proof of Claim
12
.

Suppose
c\leq\frac{1}{3}
and
s=cm
is an integer. Then

\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\binom{m-1}{s-1}+\binom{m-1}{m-2}\binom{m-2}{s-1}=\frac{s}{m}\binom{m}{s}+\frac{s(m-s)}{m}\binom{m}{s}

and

v(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\binom{m}{s}+\binom{m}{m-1}\binom{m-2}{s-1}=\binom{m}{s}+\frac{s(m-s)}{m-1}\binom{m}{s}.

Thus we have
v(\mathcal{H}_{m})\sim c(1-c)m\binom{m}{s}
and
v(\mathcal{H}_{m})-\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})\sim(1-c^{2})\binom{m}{s}
. As we have
\log v(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\Theta(m)
, the inequality
v(\mathcal{H}_{m})-\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})=O\left(\frac{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{\log v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}\right)
follows.
∎

Corollary 13
.

For any integers
k\geq 3,t\geq 1
, the following upper bound holds:

f_{k,t}(n)=O\left(\frac{2^{2t}n}{\log n}\right).

Proof.

We consider the induction steps for increasing the values of
k
and
t
in our construction. Increasing
k
does not change the maximum clique size in the construction, and also does not decrease
t
, hence we get the same upper bound for
f_{k,t}(n)
as for
f_{3,t}(n)
for all
k\geq 3,t\geq 1
.

Now consider our method of increasing
t
. In the base step, we start with a construction of
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{1})>1
, so it is at least two.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{i+1}=\mathcal{H}_{i}\circ\mathcal{H}_{1}
for all
i\geq 1
integer.
Then
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{i})\geq 2^{i}
for all
i
integers by Claim
11
. From here onward, let
v_{i}=v(\mathcal{H}_{i}),\omega_{i}=\omega(\mathcal{H}_{i})
, and let
s_{i}
be such that
\omega_{i}\geq v_{i}-s_{i}
. We can pick
s_{1}=\frac{cv_{1}}{\log v_{1}}
by Claim
12
and let
c_{1}=c
. Now we prove by induction that
s_{i}\leq\frac{c_{i}v_{i}}{\log v_{i}}
for certain
c_{i}
. Via Claim
11
, we have
v_{i+1}=v_{i}^{2}
and
w_{i+1}=w_{i}^{2}=(v_{i}-s_{i})^{2}=v_{i+1}-\frac{4c_{i}v_{i+1}}{\log v_{i+1}}+\frac{4c_{i}^{2}v_{i+1}}{\log^{2}v_{i+1}}
. Thus
s_{i+1}=\frac{4c_{i}v_{i+1}}{\log v_{i+1}}-\frac{4c_{i}^{2}v_{i+1}}{\log^{2}v_{i+1}}\leq\frac{4c_{i}v_{i+1}}{\log v_{i+1}}
. Hence, we can set
c_{i+1}=4c_{i}
and thus
s_{i}\leq\frac{2^{2i-2}cv_{i}}{\log v_{i}}
for all
i\geq 1
.

Thus, if we require
\eta(\mathcal{H})=t
, then for an upper bound on
f_{k,t}(n)
we can take the graphs to be of form
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\lfloor\log_{2}(t)\rfloor+1}
deduced from some starting
k
-uniform construction
\mathcal{H}_{1}
avoiding a single piercing vertex of the maximum cliques. By the previous calculations, these kinds of hypergraphs have
\eta(\mathcal{H})>t
, and
v(\mathcal{H})-\omega(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{2^{2t}cv(\mathcal{H})}{\log v(\mathcal{H})}
.

Thus we can conclude that
f_{k,t}(n)=O\left(\frac{2^{2t}n}{\log n}\right)

∎

We also provide a lower bound on the best possible
f_{k,t}(v(\mathcal{H}))
. To prove it, we need to define sunflowers.

Definition
.

A sunflower with
s
petals is a family of
s
hyperedges, where each pair of edges intersect in the same (possibly empty) set of vertices.
Sun(s,k)
denotes the minimum integer, such that for any set of that many hyperedges of size
k
, some
s
of them forms a sunflower with
s
petals.

Erdős and Rado conjectured that
Sun(s,k)\leq c(s)^{k}

[
3
]
. The current best bound is due to Bell, Chueluecha, and Warnke:

Theorem 14

(Bell, Chueluecha, Warnke
[
2
]
)
.

There is a constant
C\geq 4
such that
Sun(s,k)\leq(Cs\log k)^{k}
for all integers
s,k\geq 2
.

We are now well-equipped to prove our lower bound on
f_{k,t}(v(\mathcal{H}))
.

Theorem 15
.

Let
k\geq 3,t\geq 1
be integers. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with clique number
\omega(\mathcal{H})
. There is a constant
C\geq 4
, such that if
v(\mathcal{H})-\omega(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{1}{C\log k}\left(\frac{tn}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}-1
, then the maximal cliques of
\mathcal{H}
can be pierced with
t
vertices, so
\eta(\mathcal{H})\leq t
. Hence,

f_{k,t}(n)>\frac{1}{C\log k}\left(\frac{tn}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}-1.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices, with clique number
\omega(\mathcal{H})=n-s
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
be the
k
-uniform complement hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
.

The hyperedges of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
cannot be pierced by
s-1
vertices, as otherwise the other
n-s+1
vertices would form a clique in
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathcal{H}}
be a minimal subhypergraph on the same vertex set, such that its hyperedges cannot be pierced by
s-1
vertices. This means that if we remove any hyperedge from
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, the remaining hyperedges can be pierced by
s-1
vertices.

Observation
.

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
does not contain a sunflower
with
s+1
petals.

Proof of the observation.

Suppose for a contradiction that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains a sunflower with
s+1
petals. By minimality, if we erase one of the hyperedges of the sunflower, we can pierce the rest of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
by
s-1
vertices. These
s-1
vertices can only pierce the remaining
s
hyperedges of the sunflower, if there is at least one vertex in its center. But that vertex pierces the removed hyperedge as well, so these
s-1
vertices pierce the whole
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Hence,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains at most
Sun(s+1,k)
many hyperedges. Now we can apply Theorem
14
: There is a universal constant
C\geq 4
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains at most
(C(s+1)\log k)^{k}
hyperedges.

Observation
.

If
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains a vertex
x
that is contained by at most
t-1
hyperedges, then
x
together with one vertex from each of these hyperedges pierce all maximal cliques in
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof of observation.

Suppose a maximal clique of
\mathcal{H}
is not pierced by these vertices, then these vertices form a subset of the
s
vertices not in the maximum clique. By definition of a maximal clique of
\mathcal{H}
, these
s
vertices pierce
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. But if we remove the vertex
x
, the
s-1
vertices still pierce
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, since we picked one vertex from each hyperedge containing
x
. This is a contradiction.
∎

We finish the proof using pigeonhole principle. If
tn>k(C(s+1)\log k)^{k}
, then there must be a vertex
x
pierced by at most
t-1
hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
By the previous observation, we can then pick at most
t
vertices piercing all maximum cliques of
\mathcal{H}
.
After rearranging we get that if

s<\frac{1}{C\log k}\left(\frac{tn}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}-1,

then all the maximal cliques of
\mathcal{H}
can be pierced by a set of at most
t
vertices.
∎

For

)
.

For any integer

.

For

,

Proof.

Use the same method as before, but conclude with the improved upper-bound of Theorem

. So by pigeonhole principle,

implies the existence of a piercing family of size at most

. From that, one can easily conclude that

\mathcal{H}_{d}
be a
d+1
uniform intersection hypergraph of
n
convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the intersection hypergraph, the convex sets correspond to the vertices of the graph, and
d+1
vertices form a hyperedge, if and only if the corresponding
d+1
convex sets share a common point.

Question 1
.

Is it true that for every dimension
d
there exists a
c_{d}<1
such that if
\mathcal{H}_{d}
is a
d+1
uniform intersection hypergraph of convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})\geq c_{d}v(\mathcal{H}_{d})
, then
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{d})=1
?

Note that via Helly’s theorem, the maximal cliques of a
d+1
uniform intersection hypergraph of convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be represented by points contained by all convex sets corresponding to the vertices of the maximal clique.

The case when
\mathcal{H}_{d}
consists of the
d+1
facets of a
d
-dimensional simplex, shows that
c_{d}\geq\frac{d}{d+1}
. On the plane, we can improve on that with the following construction.

Claim 18
.

There exist
3
-uniform intersection hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}_{m}
of planar convex sets such that
\frac{\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}\to 3/4
as
m
goes to infinity, and the maximum size cliques do not have a common intersection.

Proof.

Take a regular
m
-gon and let the convex sets be all the convex hulls of
(m-1)
-tuples of vertices of the
m
-gon and the convex hulls of consecutive
\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil
-tuples of vertices. This construction has
\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})=m+\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil-1
sized maximum cliques, and
v(\mathcal{H}_{m})=2m
convex sets, with a ratio
\frac{\omega(\mathcal{H}_{m})}{v(\mathcal{H}_{m})}=\frac{m+\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil-1}{2m}\to\frac{3}{4}
as
m
approaches infinity.
∎

The following result of Kalai helps to prove some positive results.

Theorem 19

(Kalai
[
5
]
)
.

If
\omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})\geq n-s
, then there are at most
\binom{d+s}{d}
maximum size cliques.

The complement of a matching shows that there is no analogous result for general hypergraphs. Using the above result of Kalai, we could prove the following dependence between the clique number and
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{d})
.

Corollary 20
.

Let
t
be a positive integer. If
\omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})\geq n-s
and
s\leq O_{d,t}(n^{\frac{t}{t+d}})
, then
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{d})\leq t
.

Proof.

Suppose we have a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{d}
with
\omega({\mathcal{H}})\geq n-s
and
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{d})>t
. We will bound
s
.

Since
\eta(\mathcal{H}_{d})>t
, for any
t
vertices of the hypergraph there must be a maximum sized clique that is disjoint from it. By Theorem
19
, the hypergraph has at most
\binom{d+s}{d}
maximum size cliques. Each clique is disjoint from at most
\binom{s}{t}

t
-sets of vertices. There are
\binom{n}{t}

t
-sets of vertices, hence we must have

\binom{d+s}{d}\binom{s}{t}\geq\binom{n}{t}
. Using the well-known bounds
(\frac{a}{b})^{b}\leq\binom{a}{b}\leq(\frac{ea}{b})^{b}
on binomial coefficients, after a small computation this implies
s\geq\Omega(n^{\frac{t}{t+d}})
.
∎

This result is analogous to the lower bound for
f_{k,t}(n)
established in the previous section.
While the general Theorem
15
requires the more restrictive condition

s=O_{d,t}(n^{\frac{1}{d+1}})
for intersection hypergraphs,
Corollary
20
permits
s=O_{d,t}(n^{\frac{t}{d+t}})
.
For
t\geq 2
, this represents a polynomial improvement in
the required condition for this special case.
