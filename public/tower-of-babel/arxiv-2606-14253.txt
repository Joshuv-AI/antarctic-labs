Quantum codes and optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs via the MP construction
Meng Cao and Kun Zhou

Abstract
The matrix-product (MP) construction method combines several classical codes of the same length with a defining matrix to form a new classical code known as an MP code. This approach provides an effective approach for constructing longer classical codes and various kinds of quantum codes. Quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -locally recoverable codes (LRCs) are a new kind of quantum codes introduced in [ 12 ] , with significant potential for quantum data storage, where the optimal construction of pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs is a fundamental and compelling topic. In this paper, we employ MP codes whose defining matrices are τ \tau -optimal defining ( τ \tau -OD) matrices to construct new quantum codes and quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs. Specifically, we report the following results: • We establish a unified τ \tau -monomial decomposition theorem for invertible self-adjoint matrices over finite fields of arbitrary characteristic, which generalizes the result in [ 9 ] where the characteristic was required to be odd. Based on this theorem, we prove the existence of τ \tau -OD matrices over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} for any characteristic and demonstrate that there exist several new infinite families of τ \tau -OD matrices over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} of characteristic 2 2 . As an application of MP codes involving τ \tau -OD matrices, we construct several infinite families of quantum codes with flexible parameters. Within this framework, we present 222 222 record-breaking quantum codes that surpass the best-known records maintained in Grassl’s database [ 18 ] . • We propose two effective schemes for constructing optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs via MP codes. Accordingly, we construct four new infinite families of optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs with flexible parameters. Notably, we report an interesting phenomenon by exhibiting 30 30 optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs derived from our framework; that is, there exist quantum codes that are not only optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs but also, according to Grassl’s database [ 18 ] , best-known, optimal, or record-breaking quantum codes. To the best of our knowledge, the new discovery that quantum codes are simultaneously optimal pure quantum ( r , δ ) (r,\delta) -LRCs and record-breaking quantum codes has not been previously reported in the literature.

Introduction

Unlike classical bits, which can be easily replicated and are relatively robust, quantum states are inherently fragile and highly susceptible to environmental disturbances. Furthermore, the no-cloning theorem prohibits the perfect copying of arbitrary quantum states, rendering naive classical redundancy techniques inapplicable in the quantum setting. To overcome these limitations, quantum information is encoded into a larger Hilbert space, enabling error detection and correction without collapsing the logical state. Consequently, quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) have become indispensable for mitigating decoherence and noise in quantum information processing.

The theory of QECCs originated with Shor’s pioneering work
[
33
]
, which introduced the
[[9,1,3]]
code, the first scheme capable of correcting arbitrary single-qubit errors. In
[
34
]
, Steane introduced the
[[7,1,3]]
code, representing another major milestone.
In
[
16
]
, Gottesman developed the stabilizer formalism, which established a systematic framework for quantum code construction and laid the foundation for much of modern quantum error correction.
Currently, the majority of studied quantum codes fall within the framework of stabilizer codes, primarily due to their profound links to classical codes.
Prominent examples include the CSS construction
[
4
,
35
]
and the Hermitian construction
[
1
]
, both of which enable the efficient derivation of QECCs from suitable classical codes.

Recently, Galindo, Hernando, Martín-Cruz, and Matsumoto in
[
12

introduced
quantum
(r,\delta)
-locally recoverable codes (LRCs)

These codes represent a novel class of quantum codes with significant potential for quantum data storage.
A quantum

-LRC can correct
\delta-1

r+\delta-1
qudits.
In the same work, they established a fundamental connection between Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing codes and quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs.
Their work effectively explored general quantum

-LRCs and optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)

In

II
Preliminaries

An
[n,k,d]_{q}
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
.
By the
Singleton bound
, the minimum distance satisfies
d\leq n-k+1
.
A code achieving equality, i.e.,
d=n-k+1
, is called a
maximum distance separable (MDS) code
.

The
Euclidean dual code

\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{E}}}
and the
Hermitian dual code

\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{H}}}
of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{E}}}=\Big\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}:\ \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{\mathrm{E}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=0\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ \mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{C}\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{H}}}=\Big\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}:\ \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}^{q}=0\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ \mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{C}\Big\}.

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
Euclidean self-orthogonal
if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{E}}}
,
and
Hermitian self-orthogonal
if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{H}}}
.
Conversely,
\mathcal{C}
is called
Euclidean dual-containing
if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{E}}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
,
and
Hermitian dual-containing
if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\mathrm{H}}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.

II-D

Quantum Codes and Quantum
(r,\delta)
-Locally Recoverable Codes (LRCs)

A
q
-ary quantum code
[[n,k,d]]_{q}
is defined as a
q^{k}
-dimensional subspace of the complex Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{q^{n}}
, where
d
is the minimum distance. It is capable of detecting up to
d-1

\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor
quantum errors, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function. The parameters of an
[[n,k,d]]_{q}
quantum code satisfy the
quantum Singleton bound
, i.e.,
2d\leq n-k+2
.
Codes that attain this bound, i.e.,
2d=n-k+2
, are referred to as
quantum MDS codes
.

One of the most effective methods for constructing quantum codes is the
Hermitian construction
(see
[
1
, Corollary 1]
), which yields quantum codes from Hermitian dual-containing codes.

.

(
[
1

)
Let
\mathcal{C}

[[n,2k-n,d(\mathcal{Q})\geq d]]_{q}
, where

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d,&\mathrm{if}\ \mathcal{C}^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}=\mathcal{C},\\
\mathrm{wt}(\mathcal{C}\backslash\mathcal{C}^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}),&\mathrm{if}\ \mathcal{C}^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}.\end{cases}

Here,

.
The quantum code

is called

(
[
12
, Definition 33]
)
Following the notation of Lemma
8
, a pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC
Q^{\prime}(\mathcal{C})
with parameters
[[n,k,d(\mathcal{C})]]_{q}
is called an

optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC
if

\displaystyle k+2d(\mathcal{C})+2\bigg(\bigg\lceil\frac{n+k}{2r}\bigg\rceil-1\bigg)(\delta-1)=n+2.

(4)

Remark 1
.

Due to the condition
k=2\dim(\mathcal{C})-n
in Lemma
8
, one can easily verify that Eq. (
4
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{C})=n-\dim(\mathcal{C})+1-\bigg(\bigg\lceil\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{r}\bigg\rceil-1\bigg)(\delta-1).

This means that
\mathcal{C}
is an optimal classical
(r,\delta)
-LRC. Consequently, within the framework of Lemma
8
and Definition
1
,
a Hermitian (resp. Euclidean) dual-containing classical
(r,\delta)
-LRC is optimal if and only if its induced pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRC is optimal.

III
Unified
\tau
-Monomial Decomposition Theorem and New
\tau
-OD Matrices

In this section, we establish a unified
\tau
-monomial decomposition theorem for invertible self-adjoint matrices over finite fields of arbitrary characteristic.
Based on our decomposition theorem, we prove the existence of
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for any characteristic and demonstrate that there exist several new infinite families of
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of characteristic
2
.

III-A

Notation and Concepts

We need to use the following notation and concepts:

•

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\sigma^{2}={\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, where
{\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
denotes the identity
automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

•

For any matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we define
A^{\sigma}=(\sigma(a_{i,j}))
and
A^{\star}=(A^{\top})^{\sigma}
, where
A^{\top}
denotes the

transpose
of
A
. A matrix
A
is said to be
self-adjoint
if
A^{\star}=A
. For any row vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, we define
\mathbf{a}^{\sigma}=(\sigma(a_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(a_{k}))
and
\mathbf{a}^{\star}=(\sigma(a_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(a_{k}))^{\top}
.

•

Let
{\rm Sym}_{k}
denote the
symmetric group
of degree
k
, which consists of all permutations of the set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
A permutation
\tau\in{\rm Sym}_{k}
is called the
identity permutation
if
\tau(i)=i
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, and we denote it by
\mathbbm{1}_{k}
.
For any
1\leq a<b\leq k
, we denote by
(a,b)
a
2-cycle
in
{\rm Sym}_{k}
.
Specifically, if
\tau=(a,b)\in{\rm Sym}_{k}
, then
\tau(a)=b
,
\tau(b)=a
and
\tau(i)=i
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}\backslash\{a,b\}
.

•

Let
I_{s}
denote the
s\times s
identity matrix. Let
O_{s\times t}
(resp.
O_{t\times s}
) denote the
s\times t
(resp.
t\times s
) zero matrix.

•

Let
\mathbf{0}_{1\times s}
(resp.
\mathbf{0}_{s\times 1}
) denote the
1\times s
(resp.
s\times 1
) zero vector.

•

A matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
is called a
\tau
-monomial matrix
if we can write
A=DP_{\tau}
, where
D\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
is
an invertible diagonal matrix and
P_{\tau}
is a permutation matrix with respect to the permutation
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
such that the
\tau(i)
-th row of
P_{\tau}
corresponds to the
i
-th row of the identity matrix
I_{k}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

•

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
.
If
a_{i,j}=0
for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
(resp.
1\leq j<i\leq k
), then
A
is called a
lower triangular
(resp. an
upper triangular
)
matrix
.
If a lower triangular (resp. an upper triangular) matrix
A
satisfies
a_{i,i}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, then it is called a
unit lower triangular

(resp. a
unit upper triangular
)
matrix
.

III-B

Unified
\tau
-Monomial Decomposition Theorem

We first consider the finite field extension
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}
, where
q
is a prime power and
a
is a positive integer.
The
trace function

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{F}}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{F}}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}x^{q^{i}},\;x\in\mathbb{E}.

Proposition 3
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 2.25]
)
Suppose
\mathbb{E}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{E}
. Then,
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{F}}(\alpha)=0
if and only if there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{E}
such that
\alpha=\beta^{q}-\beta
.

If we consider finite fields of characteristic
2
, the following result can be readily obtained.

Lemma 9
.

Let
q=2^{a}
, where
a
is a positive integer. Then, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\alpha=\beta^{q}+\beta
.

Proof:

Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has characteristic
2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=0.

Applying Proposition
3
and again using the fact that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has characteristic
2
, we conclude that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\alpha=\beta^{q}+\beta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, let us introduce the concept of the
\tau
-monomial decomposition for invertible self-adjoint matrices.

Definition 2
.

(
\tau
-Monomial Decomposition)

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
\sigma^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. We say that
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
admits a
\tau
-monomial decomposition
if for any invertible self-adjoint matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
, there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
and a
\tau
-monomial matrix
DP_{\tau}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
such that
LAL^{\star}=DP_{\tau}
.

Proposition 4
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1]
)
For a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of odd characteristic and an automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
\sigma^{2}={\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
,
there exists a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
.

The following lemma is useful for establishing the main result of this subsection, namely Theorem
1
.

Lemma 10
.

If
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has characteristic
2
and
\sigma={\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, then the
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
does not exist.

Proof:

Consider the following
2\times 2
invertible self-adjoint matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
:

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}.

Any
2\times 2
unit lower triangular matrix
L
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has the form

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\alpha&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
2
and
\sigma={\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, a direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle LAL^{\star}=A,

which is not a
\tau
-monomial matrix for any
\tau\in{\rm Sym}_{2}
. Therefore, the
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to

(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
does not exist.
∎

In the following, we establish a unified
\tau
-monomial decomposition theorem, which completely characterizes the existence of the
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
for finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of odd characteristic or of characteristic
2
.

Theorem 1
.

(Unified
\tau
-Monomial Decomposition Theorem)

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
\sigma^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Then, there exists a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
if and only if one of following conditions holds:

(1)

q
is odd;

(2)

q=2^{2e}
for some positive integer
e
, and
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma(x)=x^{2^{e}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof:

By Proposition
4
, it suffices to consider the theorem in the case where
q
is even.
Suppose that there exists a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
, where
q
is even.
By Lemma
10
, we have
q=2^{2e}
for some positive integer
e
, and
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma(x)=x^{2^{e}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Conversely, assume that
q=2^{2e}
for some positive integer
e
, and
\sigma(x)=x^{2^{e}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We aim to show that there exists a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
. We proceed by induction on the size
k\times k
of the invertible self-adjoint matrix over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The result holds trivially for
k=1
. Assume the conclusion holds for any invertible self-adjoint matrix in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
with
k<n
.
We shall show that it remains true for
k=n
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
be an arbitrary invertible self-adjoint matrix.
In what follows, we consider the cases
a_{1,1}\neq 0
and
a_{1,1}=0
, respectively.

Case I
:

When
a_{1,1}\neq 0
, the matrix
A
can be partitioned as

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\mathbf{b}\\
\mathbf{b}^{\star}&A_{2,2}\end{pmatrix},

where
a_{1,1}=a_{1,1}^{\sqrt{q}}
and
A_{2,2}=A_{2,2}^{\star}
. By setting

\displaystyle L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-1)}\\
\frac{1}{a_{1,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}&I_{n-1}\end{pmatrix},

we derive

\displaystyle L_{1}AL_{1}^{\star}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-1)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-1)\times 1}&A_{2,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\end{pmatrix}.

The inductive hypothesis implies that there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L_{2}
and a
\tau_{1}
-monomial matrix
B_{1}
for some
\tau_{1}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n-1}
such that

\displaystyle L_{2}\left(A_{2,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\right)L_{2}^{\star}=B_{1}.

Taking the unit lower triangular matrix

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-1)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-1)\times 1}&L_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}{L}_{1},

we derive

\displaystyle LAL^{\star}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-1)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-1)\times 1}&B_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix},

which is a
\tau
-monomial matrix for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}
.

Case II
:

When
a_{1,1}=0
, we assume that
s
is the smallest positive integer such that
a_{1,s}\neq 0
.
As
a_{i,j}=a_{j,i}^{\sqrt{q}}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, the matrix
A
can be written as

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&a_{1,s}&\mathbf{b}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&A_{1,1}&\mathbf{c}^{\star}&A_{1,2}\\
a_{s,1}&\mathbf{c}&a_{s,s}&\mathbf{d}\\
\mathbf{b}^{\star}&A_{2,1}&\mathbf{d}^{\star}&A_{2,2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

As
a_{s,s}=a_{s,s}^{\sqrt{q}}
, it follows from Lemma
9
that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
a_{s,s}=\beta^{\sqrt{q}}+\beta
.
We define a unit lower triangular matrix as follows:

\displaystyle L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{c}^{\star}&I_{s-2}&\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&O_{(s-2)\times(n-s)}\\
\frac{\beta}{a_{1,s}}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\frac{a_{s,s}}{a_{s,1}a_{1,s}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}+\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{d}^{\star}&O_{(n-s)\times(s-2)}&\frac{1}{a_{s,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}&I_{n-s}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Then, through direct computation and substituting
a_{s,s}=\beta^{\sqrt{q}}+\beta
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{1}AL_{1}^{\star}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&a_{1,s}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&A_{1,1}&\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&A_{1,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{c}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\\
a_{s,1}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-s)\times 1}&A_{2,1}+\frac{1}{a_{s,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{c}&\mathbf{0}_{(n-s)\times 1}&A_{2,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{d}^{\star}\mathbf{b}+\frac{1}{a_{s,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{d}+\frac{a_{s,s}}{a_{s,1}a_{1,s}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

Let

\displaystyle A_{n-2}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,1}&A_{1,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{c}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\\
A_{2,1}+\frac{1}{a_{s,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{c}&A_{2,2}+\frac{1}{a_{1,s}}\mathbf{d}^{\star}\mathbf{b}+\frac{1}{a_{s,1}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{d}+\frac{a_{s,s}}{a_{s,1}a_{1,s}}\mathbf{b}^{\star}\mathbf{b}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

By the inductive hypothesis, there exists an
(n-2)\times(n-2)
unit lower triangular matrix

\displaystyle L_{n-2}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1,1}&O_{(s-2)\times(n-s)}\\
L_{2,1}&L_{2,2}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(7)

and a
\tau_{1}
-monomial matrix
B_{1}
for some
\tau_{1}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n-2}
such that

\displaystyle L_{n-2}A_{n-2}L_{n-2}^{\star}=B_{1}.

(8)

Finally, we define an
n\times n
unit lower triangular matrix

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&L_{1,1}&\mathbf{0}_{(s-2)\times 1}&O_{(s-2)\times(n-s)}\\
0&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(s-2)}&1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times(n-s)}\\
\mathbf{0}_{(n-s)\times 1}&L_{2,1}&\mathbf{0}_{(n-s)\times 1}&L_{2,2}\\
\end{pmatrix}L_{1}.

(9)

Applying Eqs. (
5
)-(
9
), it is straightforward to verify that
LAL^{\star}
is a self-adjoint
\tau
-monomial matrix for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}
.

In summary, for both cases, there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L
such that
LAL^{\star}
is a
\tau
-monomial matrix for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}
. This confirms the existence of a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2
.

For any positive integer
k\leq q^{2}
, there exists a
k\times k

\tau
-OD matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
.

Proof:

By Proposition
1
and Theorem
1
, we deduce the existence of a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},\sigma)
, regardless of whether
q
is odd or even.

Note that there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
and a
\tau
-monomial matrix
DP_{\tau}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
such that
LNN^{{\dagger}}L^{{\dagger}}=DP_{\tau}
. Moreover, it follows from Proposition
2
that
LN
is an NSC matrix. Consequently,
LN
is a
\tau
-OD matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
, which confirms the statement.
∎

III-C

Existence of Several New Infinite Families of
\tau
-OD Matrices

Let
A
be a
k\times k
matrix. Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
and
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
be two sets of
s
pairwise distinct positive integers in
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.
We denote by

\displaystyle\left|\begin{pmatrix}u_{1},&u_{2},&\cdots,&u_{s}\\
v_{1},&v_{2},&\cdots,&v_{s}\end{pmatrix}_{A}\right|

the determinant of the submatrix of
A
formed by the
u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,u_{s}^{\prime}
-th rows and the
v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{s}^{\prime}
-th columns, where
1\leq u_{1}^{\prime}<u_{2}^{\prime}<\ldots<u_{s}^{\prime}\leq k
and
1\leq v_{1}^{\prime}<v_{2}^{\prime}<\ldots<v_{s}^{\prime}\leq k
, such that
\{u_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{s}=\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
and
\{v_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{s}=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
.

The following result corresponds to the finite field case of
[
9
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
be an invertible matrix. If there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
and a unit upper triangular matrix
U\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
such that
LAU
is a
\tau
-monomial matrix for some
\tau\in{\rm Sym}_{k}
, then
\tau
satisfies:

\displaystyle\tau(1)={\rm min}\left\{1\leq s\leq k:a_{1,s}\neq 0\right\},

and for all
2\leq i\leq k
,

\displaystyle\tau(i)={\rm min}\left\{1\leq s\leq k:s\notin\{\tau(1),\ldots,\tau(i-1)\},\ \left|\begin{pmatrix}1,&\cdots,&i-1,&i\\
\tau(1),&\cdots,&\tau(i-1),&s\end{pmatrix}_{A}\right|\neq 0\right\}.

Building upon Theorem
1
and Lemma
11
, the following theorem establishes a unified approach for constructing
\tau
-OD matrices over finite fields of arbitrary characteristic, extending the scope of
[
9
, Theorem 4]
from odd characteristic to all characteristics.

Theorem 3
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
be an NSC matrix and
\tau\in{\rm Sym}_{k}
. Then, there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
such that
LN
is a
\tau
-OD matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if

\displaystyle\tau(1)={\rm min}\left\{1\leq s\leq k:n_{1,s}\neq 0\right\},

(10)

where
n_{1,s}
is the
(1,s)
-entry of
NN^{{\dagger}}
, and for all
2\leq i\leq k
,

\displaystyle\tau(i)={\rm min}\left\{1\leq s\leq k:s\notin\{\tau(1),\ldots,\tau(i-1)\},\ \left|\begin{pmatrix}1,&\cdots,&i-1,&i\\
\tau(1),&\cdots,&\tau(i-1),&s\end{pmatrix}_{NN^{{\dagger}}}\right|\neq 0\right\}.

(11)

Proof:

“
\Longrightarrow
”: Suppose there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
such that
LN
is a
\tau
-OD matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then,
LN(LN)^{{\dagger}}
is a
\tau
-monomial matrix.
Setting
A=NN^{{\dagger}}
and
U=L^{{\dagger}}
in Lemma
11
, it follows that Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) hold.

“
\Longleftarrow
”: Conversely, suppose
\tau
satisfies Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
).
By Theorem
1
, there exists a
\tau
-monomial decomposition with respect to
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},\sigma)
, regardless of whether
q
is odd or even.
Since
NN^{{\dagger}}
is an invertible self-adjoint matrix, there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{k\times k}
such that

LNN^{{\dagger}}L^{{\dagger}}
is a
\tau^{\prime}
-monomial matrix for some
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
. By Proposition
2
and Lemma
11
,
\tau^{\prime}=\tau
, and therefore
LN
is a
\tau
-OD matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Therefore, we complete the proof.
∎

Now, by applying Theorem
3
, we prove the existence of a new infinite family of
\tau
-OD matrices over finite fields of characteristic
2
in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
q=2^{e}>2
be an even prime power. Suppose
(k-1)\mid(q^{2}-1)
with
k\geq 3
. Let
i+qt_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{k-1}
, where
1\leq t_{i}\leq k-2
for
1\leq i\leq k-2
. Then, there exists an infinite family of
k\times k

\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, where

\displaystyle\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\cdots&k-1&k\\
1&t_{1}+1&t_{2}+1&\cdots&t_{k-2}+1&k\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

Proof:

We define the following
k\times k
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
:

\displaystyle N=\begin{pmatrix}g&1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
0&1&g^{m}&g^{2m}&\cdots&g^{(k-2)m}\\
0&1&g^{2m}&g^{4m}&\cdots&g^{2(k-2)m}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&1&g^{(k-2)m}&g^{2(k-2)m}&\cdots&g^{(k-2)^{2}m}\\
0&1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
g
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
m=\frac{q^{2}-1}{k-1}
. One can easily verify that
N
is an NSC matrix. To compute
NN^{{\dagger}}
, let
\mathbf{n}_{i}
denote the
i
-th row of
N
for
1\leq i\leq k
. The following properties regarding the Hermitian inner products of these rows are then obtained.

1)

Clearly,
\langle\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{1}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=g^{q+1}+1
and

\langle\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=\langle\mathbf{n}_{k},\mathbf{n}_{1}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=\langle\mathbf{n}_{k},\mathbf{n}_{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=1
.

2)

When
2\leq i\leq k-1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{i}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=\langle\mathbf{n}_{k},\mathbf{n}_{i}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=0.

The fact

implies that for

\langle\mathbf{n}_{i},\mathbf{n}_{1}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=\langle\mathbf{n}_{i},\mathbf{n}_{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=0

3)

When

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{n}_{i},\mathbf{n}_{j}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{k-2}g^{sm[i-1+q(j-1)]}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}k-1,&\mbox{if }j=t_{i-1}+1,\\
0,&\mbox{if }j\neq t_{i-1}+1.\end{cases}

It follows that

\displaystyle NN^{{\dagger}}=(g^{q+1}+1)E_{1,1}+(k-1)\left(E_{1,k}+E_{k,1}+E_{k,k}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}E_{i,t_{i-1}+1}\right).

Here

denotes the matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
, while all other entries are
0
.

By Theorem
1
, there exists a unit lower triangular matrix
L
such that
LNN^{{\dagger}}L^{{\dagger}}
is a
\tau
-monomial matrix
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for some
\tau\in{\rm Sym}_{k}
. Hence,
LN
is a
\tau
-OD matrix. Applying Theorem
3
, we derive
\tau(1)=1
,
\tau(k)=k
, and

\tau(i)=t_{i-1}+1
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
. This result is consistent with Eq. (
12
), which completes the proof.
∎

V
Optimal Pure Quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs

In this section, we propose two effective schemes for constructing optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs from MP codes.
Based on these schemes, we explicitly construct four new infinite families of optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs with flexible parameters.
In addition, we report an interesting phenomenon regarding optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs derived from our framework.
To the best of our knowledge, the new discovery that quantum codes are simultaneously optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs and record-breaking quantum codes has not been previously reported in the literature.

Lemma 12
.

Let
(n-1)\mid(q^{2}-1)
and
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\mid n
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be a primitive
(n-1)
-th root of unity.
Denote
\mathbf{a}=(0,1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-2})
and
\mathbf{1}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
as two row vectors of length
n
.
Then,
\mathrm{GRS}_{k_{2}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}\subseteq\mathrm{GRS}_{k_{1}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})
if and only if the following relation holds in
\mathbb{Z}_{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\{q,2q,\ldots,(n-k_{2}-1)q\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k_{1}-1\}.

Proof:

Let
\mathbf{u}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{j}
be the
i
-th row of
G_{n-k}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})
and the
j
-th row of
G_{k}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})
, respectively, where
1\leq i\leq n-k
and
1\leq j\leq k
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{GRS}_{n-k}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})\subseteq\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})^{\bot_{\mathrm{E}}}=\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}.

Then,

\mathrm{GRS}_{n-k}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})=\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}
. Hence,

\mathrm{GRS}_{k_{2}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})^{\bot_{\mathrm{H}}}\subseteq\mathrm{GRS}_{k_{1}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})
if and only if

\mathrm{GRS}_{n-k_{2}}(\mathbf{a}^{q},\mathbf{1})\subseteq\mathrm{GRS}_{k_{1}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{1})
. Equivalently, the following relation holds in
\mathbb{Z}_{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\{q,2q,\ldots,(n-k_{2}-1)q\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k_{1}-1\},

which completes the proof.
∎

VI
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we established a unified
\tau
-monomial decomposition theorem for invertible self-adjoint matrices over finite fields of arbitrary characteristic.
We proved the existence of
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for any characteristic and demonstrated that there exist several new infinite families of
\tau
-OD matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of characteristic
2
. As an application, we constructed several infinite families of quantum codes and presented
222
record-breaking quantum codes that surpass the best-known records maintained in Grassl’s database
[
18
]
.

Based on Theorems
10
and
11
, we constructed four new infinite families of optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs with flexible parameters in Theorems
12
-
15
. In addition, we reported an interesting phenomenon: there exist quantum codes that are not only optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs but also, according to Grassl’s database
[
18
]
, best-known, optimal, or record-breaking quantum codes.
To the best of our knowledge, the new discovery that quantum codes are simultaneously optimal pure quantum
(r,\delta)
-LRCs and record-breaking quantum codes has not been previously reported in the literature.
