The impact of new-quality agricultural productivity on green total factor productivity in agriculture
Authors: Yanxiao Yin, Xuanguo Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe evolution in the quality of production factors offers the opportunity to improve extensive agricultural production and advance green agricultural development. This study analyses panel data from 30 Chinese provinces to measure the levels of new-quality agricultural productivity (NQAP) and agricultural green total factor productivity (AGTFP). The study then analyses their spatial evolution characteristics and examines the impact of NQAP on AGTFP alongside the underlying mechanisms. Findings indicate that NQAP is unevenly distributed, and is higher in the east and lower in the west, and higher in the south and lower in the north. The AGTFP displays a “high-high, low-low” spatial distribution. NQAP significantly enhances AGTFP, and the promotional effect is regionally heterogeneous. Regionally, the ranking of NQAP’s contribution to improved AGTFP is highest in the western region, followed in descending order by the eastern region, central region, and balanced production-consumption regions, major grain-producing regions, and major grain-consuming regions. NQAP enhances AGTFP by improving green efficiency change (GEC); NQAP’s overall impact on green technological change (GTC) is not significant but has distinct threshold characteristics. The government agricultural support intensity (GOVS)and government environmental concern (GOVC) can amplify the effectiveness of NQAP. These findings provide quantitative evidence and theoretical support for developing NQAP and enhancing AGTFP.Keywords: New-quality agricultural productivity, Agricultural green total factor productivity, Green efficiency change, Green technological change, Text analysis methodSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

Achieving high-quality agricultural development and building China into an agricultural power are key tasks in the modernization of Chinese agriculture in the new era. As resource and environmental constraints continue to intensify, the traditional agricultural growth model, characterized by high input, high resource consumption, and high emissions, has become increasingly unsustainable. Agricultural development therefore urgently needs to shift from factor-input-driven growth to growth driven by efficiency improvement, technological progress, and green transformation1,2. Against this backdrop, agricultural green total factor productivity (AGTFP) has become a crucial indicator for measuring high-quality agricultural development. AGTFP not only reflects the efficiency of agricultural inputs and outputs, but also incorporates resource consumption and environmental pollution into the productivity evaluation system, thereby providing a more comprehensive measure of the quality of agricultural development, the level of green transformation, and the capacity for sustainable growth3. Consequently, how to effectively enhance AGTFP has become a critical theoretical and practical issue in advancing green agricultural development and agricultural modernization.

New-quality agricultural productivity (NQAP) is a systemic form of agricultural productivity driven primarily by technological innovation. It emphasizes the synergistic optimization of laborers, production inputs, labor objects, and their combinations, as well as the systematic transformation of production methods4. Unlike traditional agricultural productivity, which relies primarily on the quantitative expansion of land, labor, and capital, and unlike isolated agricultural technological progress or digital agriculture, which focus solely on technological improvements, NQAP is characterized by the integration of multiple factors, systematic innovation in production methods, and the creation of green value5. Unlike agricultural total factor productivity, which is mainly an outcome-oriented measure of production efficiency, NQAP emphasizes the driving process of technological innovation, factor reallocation, production-mode transformation, and green value creation. In practice, it integrates digital agricultural technologies, smart equipment, green and low-carbon technologies, modern agricultural organizational models, and efficient factor allocation, thereby providing a new source of momentum for enhancing AGTFP.

Although new-quality productive forces have become a key topic in studies on high-quality development, and relevant research has highlighted their significant role in manufacturing6, industrial restructuring7, and high-quality regional economic development8, most existing findings have focused on new-quality productive forces in general or on non-agricultural sectors. Limited attention has been paid to their effects on green productivity in agriculture. In particular, can NQAP significantly enhance AGTFP? Is this impact mainly achieved through improvements in green efficiency or through green technological progress? Does the external policy environment further influence the relationship between the two? Answering these questions is of great significance for developing NQAP and advancing green agricultural development.

Research has systematically defined the meaning of “new quality” productive forces from diverse perspectives, including the three factors of production (Laborers, means of labor, objects of labor)9, input-output relationships10, and innovation-driven production systems11. These have been measured across provincial12, municipal13, and county14 levels. Despite different research perspectives and measurement methods, researchers have reached consensus on the innovation-driven nature, optimized factor allocation, and environmentally friendly characteristics of new-quality productive forces15. Studies have also explored the strategic value of new-quality productive forces, indicating its positive role in driving industrial structure transformation16 and inclusive growth17. Given China’s current socio-economic development context, new-quality productive forces is recognized as a vital instrument for advancing China’s modernization process18 and achieving high-quality development19.

As research in this area deepens, some studies have begun to apply the concept of “new-quality productivity” to the context of agricultural development, exploring how it can drive the green transformation of agriculture. Some scholars have sought to analyze the macro-level pathways through which new-quality productivity promotes green agricultural development; these studies view new-quality productivity as the fundamental driving power behind agricultural greening20. Meso-level studies have outlined pathways by which new-quality productivity facilitates the green transformation of agricultural industrial chains, arguing that these forces are a crucial lever for the sustainable development of these chains21. Micro-level studies have examined the impact of new-quality productivity on farmers’ green production practices, concluding that new-quality productivity can mitigate the problem of excessive chemical inputs22.

As a concrete manifestation of new-quality productive forces in the agricultural sector, NQAP has gradually emerged as a key theoretical framework for interpreting high-quality development in agriculture and rural areas. Relevant research primarily focuses on three aspects: First, from the perspective of efficiency enhancement, NQAP is regarded as a vital driving force for improving agricultural production efficiency23, grain yield per unit area24, and total factor productivity25. Its development supports food security and high-quality agricultural development through technological innovation, the application of smart equipment, the improvement of workers’ skills, and the optimal allocation of production factors. Second, from the perspective of equity and synergy, NQAP is linked to issues such as farmers’ income growth26, common prosperity27, and urban-rural integration28. Studies argue that by optimizing the flow of factors between urban and rural areas, and enhancing the value-added capacity of rural industries, NQAP can drive farmers’ income growth and the coordinated development of agriculture and rural areas. Third, from the perspective of green sustainability, NQAP can promote agricultural carbon emission reductions29, and improve the green utilization efficiency of arable land30, and advance green and low-carbon agricultural development through green technological innovation, resource conservation, and the transition to low-carbon production methods.

Although existing research has gradually expanded from a narrow focus on efficiency improvement to broader dimensions such as equity, synergy, and green sustainability, studies on the effects of NQAP from a green perspective remain mainly concentrated on agricultural carbon emissions and carbon reduction31–33. Insufficient attention has been paid to multiple types of environmental pollution in agricultural production, such as chemical fertilizers, pesticides, agricultural films, and agricultural wastewater. Moreover, the green performance of NQAP has not yet been fully examined from a comprehensive perspective that integrates production efficiency, resource consumption, and environmental pollution.

Against this backdrop, AGTFP provides a more comprehensive analytical perspective for evaluating the green performance of NQAP. AGTFP incorporates resource and environmental factors into the measurement system of agricultural production efficiency, taking both production benefits and environmental benefits into account, and is therefore more consistent with the requirements of green and sustainable agricultural development3. Against this background, research on the relationship between NQAP and AGTFP has gradually emerged. Given the consistency between their connotations, Gong and Yuan34 incorporated the two into the same analytical framework and pointed out that the improvement of AGTFP is an inevitable outcome of the development of NQAP. Liu35 further identified the mismatches in development speed, development requirements, and development models faced by NQAP in enhancing AGTFP, and elaborated on its pathways for improving AGTFP. However, existing studies remain largely theoretical, focusing mainly on conceptual clarification and pathway optimization. As a result, they are unable to fully reveal the actual relationship between NQAP and AGTFP or accurately assess the magnitude of this effect.

To address these shortcomings, Guo and Han36 empirically examined the relationship between NQAP and AGTFP, pointing out that industrial structure upgrading and industrial agglomeration are key pathways through which NQAP generates green growth effects. However, existing research on the mechanism of action has primarily focused on external transmission channels, such as agricultural industrial restructuring and spatial agglomeration. It has not fully considered the intrinsic dual attributes of AGTFP—namely, green efficiency change (GEC) and green technological change (GTC)—thereby making it difficult to reveal the internal transmission pathways through which NQAP affects AGTFP. At the same time, the cultivation and realization of NQAP are highly policy-dependent; policy support, institutional arrangements, and green regulations directly influence technological innovation, factor mobility, and the process of green transformation. Existing research rarely incorporates the external policy environment into its analytical framework and has not fully addressed whether policy support or institutional arrangements moderate the green performance of NQAP.

Despite these advances, three gaps remain in the existing literature. First, in terms of research content, although previous studies have begun to link NQAP with green and sustainable agricultural development, the green effects of the NQAP have not yet been fully explored from the perspective of AGTFP. Second, in terms of research methods, due to the complex conceptual framework of NQAP and the difficulty of quantifying it, existing studies have primarily relied on theoretical analysis, with relatively limited empirical testing. This limitation makes it difficult to accurately measure the extent to which NQAP influences AGTFP. Third, in terms of research perspectives, existing analyses of the mechanisms through which NQAP influences AGTFP have primarily focused on external drivers in the agricultural industrial environment, while paying insufficient attention to the internal drivers inherent in AGTFP itself. At the same time, few studies have incorporated government behavior or policy conditions into their analytical frameworks. The internal transmission channels and external moderating mechanisms through which NQAP affects AGTFP remain inadequately elucidated.

Building on these research gaps, this paper uses panel data from 30 provincial-level regions in China from 2013 to 2023 to examine the spatial distribution characteristics of NQAP and AGTFP across Chinese provinces. It then tests the impact of NQAP on AGTFP and further reveals the mechanisms through which NQAP affects AGTFP, specifically through green technical efficiency and green technological progress.

This paper makes three marginal contributions. First, in terms of research content, it takes green sustainability as its starting point and the improvement of AGTFP as its ultimate goal, incorporating NQAP and AGTFP into a unified analytical framework. This enriches the application of NQAP in the field of green agricultural production. Second, in terms of methodology, this paper combines theoretical analysis with empirical testing. It employs ArcGIS visualization analysis, panel two-way fixed-effects model, double machine learning model, mediation model, moderating model, GMM model, dynamic panel model, and IV-2SLS model. By integrating diverse datasets, including provincial panel data, text data, and map-based big data, this study investigates the impact of NQAP on AGTFP and its underlying mechanisms, thereby enhancing the reliability and explanatory power of the research conclusions. Third, in terms of research perspective, this paper constructs an analytical framework that combines internal drivers with external moderators. Specifically, this study considers the transmission effects of GEC and GTC within the agricultural sector, as well as the moderating effects of the external policy environment, including government support for agriculture and government attention to environmental issues. In doing so, it provides a more comprehensive explanation of the mechanisms through which NQAP affects AGTFP and offers theoretical and practical support for agricultural green transformation, industrial upgrading, and policy formulation.

Theoretical analysis

NQAP’s approach to enhancing AGTFP appears in three specific dimensions: technological transformation, digital and intelligent empowerment, and factor substitution (Fig. 2). From a technological transformation perspective, NQAP emphasizes science and technology-driven approaches, knowledge aggregation, and innovation. NQAP addresses agricultural development challenges through scaled-up, mechanized production models. These models are a crucial pathway for achieving green agricultural transformation and increasing AGTFP. From the perspective of digital and intelligent empowerment, these are vital components of NQAP. Applying such technologies achieves precision fertilization and real-time monitoring, preventing wasted agricultural inputs and enhancing their utilization efficiency37. This drives the transformation of agricultural production factors towards higher efficiency and lower pollution, enhancing AGTFP. With regard to factor substitution, NQAP development generates substitution effects on traditional agricultural production factors. This facilitates improved allocation of land, labor, capital, and technological factors in production, and promotes greener factor allocation and more efficient agricultural production. These enhance AGTFP.

AGTFP organically combines GEC with GTC. As such, this paper posits that the NQAP influences AGTFP through the two pathways of GEC and GTC.

For GEC, the effects of NQAP appear in three main dimensions: production optimization, factor allocation, and innovation development. At the production optimization level, NQAP leverages its digital and intelligent advantages to promptly capture agricultural production-related information, increase the precision of technology application, and elevate agricultural GEC. For factor allocation, NQAP permeates agricultural production, enabling factor reorganization and aggregation. This reduces the misallocation of production factors in agricultural production, thereby enhancing agricultural GEC38. For innovation development, advancing NQAP fosters new business models and forms of agricultural production, which have distinct efficiency-enhancing characteristics. For instance, new agricultural organizations can effectively address land fragmentation, facilitate scaled production, and increase GEC.

For GTC, the core of NQAP lies in technological innovation, which aligns with the objectives of green technological advancement. The role of NQAP in advancing green technology appears across three stages: research and development, application, and dissemination. First, in green technology R&D, NQAP drives the establishment of agricultural production data platforms. This helps agribusinesses and agricultural research institutions swiftly and accurately access relevant production data. This enhances the targeted and timely nature of GTC39. Second, in the application of green technologies, NQAP coordinates across production, consumption, and distribution sectors. This achieves parallel development throughout pre-production, production and post-production phases, and drives the application of green technologies throughout all links of agricultural production. This advances GTC40. Finally, concerning green technology dissemination, NQAP fosters interconnected agricultural production by establishing an integrated extension system linking government, enterprises, research institutions, and agricultural producers. This system leverages demonstration effects to reduce dissemination barriers and enhance producers’ acceptance and adoption of green technologies through extension services and environmental advocacy. This drives GTC.

Dependent variable

This study adopts the super-efficient SBM-GML index to measure the dependent variable AGTFP, incorporating non-desirable outputs43. Input variables include: total crop sown area; number of persons employed in agriculture; total agricultural machinery power; effectively irrigated area; pesticide use; agricultural plastic film use; and fertilizer application (converted to pure nutrient equivalent). Expected output is measured as the actual total output value of agriculture (deflated using 2000 as the base year). Unexpected output is defined as agricultural non-point source pollution generated during agricultural production, measured using the total chemical oxygen demand (COD), total nitrogen (TN), and total phosphorus (TP)44.

Spatial evolution characteristics of NQAP and AGTFP

This study focuses on the indices of NQAP and AGTFP from 2013, 2016, 2019, and 2021. Jenks’ natural breaks method is used to classify the index values and analyze their spatial distribution characteristics. Figure 3 reveals a pronounced spatial imbalance in NQAP across China. With the eastern coastal regions serving as the main growth poles, the NQAP generally exhibits higher values in the east and south and lower values in the west and north. This spatial pattern may be explained by the following factors. First, the eastern coastal regions have a high concentration of agricultural science and technology resources and related industries, which facilitates the accumulation and transformation of innovation resources related to NQAP. In contrast, the western regions are constrained by relatively weak economic foundations, insufficient agricultural technological innovation, and limited capital investment, which may explain they’re relatively lagging NQAP development. Meanwhile, southern regions are endowed with abundant natural resources and have gradually developed new agricultural production models, such as ecological circular agriculture and facility agriculture, thereby promoting the development of NQAP. By contrast, northern regions face more severe constraints in terms of soil and water resources, which restrict the formation and diffusion of NQAP.

Endogeneity testing

To mitigate potential endogeneity problems, this paper further employs an instrumental variable approach to address potential endogeneity. The validity of the instrumental variable depends on two conditions: relevance and exogeneity. Given the dynamic accumulation characteristics of NQAP, this paper uses their one-period lagged value as an instrumental variable and applies both GMM and IV-2SLS models for estimation. In terms of relevance, path dependence theory suggests that the development of economic systems is neither entirely random nor instantaneous, but is continuously shaped by existing technological foundations, institutional arrangements, resource allocation patterns, and historical development trajectories. NQAP exhibit significant path dependence and dynamic accumulation. Their formation is jointly driven by agricultural technological innovation, digital infrastructure construction, the upgrading of agricultural machinery and equipment, the diffusion of green technologies, human capital accumulation, and the cultivation of new agricultural business entities. These factors are characterized by strong continuity and persistence. As a result, the previous-period level of NQAP may affect the current-period level through technological stock, infrastructure foundations, business-entity capabilities, and factor-allocation inertia, thereby satisfying the relevance condition of the instrumental variable. In terms of exogeneity, the one-period lagged variable is predetermined relative to current agricultural green total factor productivity, and current changes in AGTFP cannot reversely affect the level of NQAP formed in the previous period. Thus, the use of the lagged variable helps alleviate reverse-causality concerns and provides a reasonable basis for the exogeneity of the instrumental variable36.

However, when a model’s error term shows serial autocorrelation, lagged instrumental variables may become associated with the error term through serial correlation. This would not fully resolve any endogeneity problems. As such, this study selects the interaction term between terrain undulation and a dummy variable for time as an instrumental variable for endogeneity testing. There are two reasons for selecting terrain undulation as the instrumental variable. First, according to natural endowment theory, terrain undulation is a long-term natural geographical condition that imposes inherent constraints on agricultural production. Agricultural production depends heavily on natural endowments such as land, climate, topography, and water resources, while terrain conditions affect the continuity of farmland, the feasibility of mechanized farming, the cost of infrastructure construction, and the accessibility of modern agricultural services. Plain regions, with flat terrain and concentrated farmland, are more conducive to agricultural mechanization, large-scale operation, the deployment of digital agricultural equipment, and the diffusion of green technologies. In contrast, mountainous and hilly regions, characterized by fragmented terrain and steep slopes, tend to increase the costs of mechanized operations, agricultural input transportation, technology extension, and socialized agricultural services, thereby constraining the development of NQAP. Thus, terrain undulation is closely related to NQAP and satisfies the relevance condition of the instrumental variable. Second, from the perspective of economic geography, natural geographical conditions such as topography, elevation, slope, and rivers are generally regarded as fundamental determinants of the spatial distribution of economic activities and are unlikely to be affected by short-term changes in economic variables. Terrain undulation is inherent, stable, and irreversible; current changes in agricultural green total factor productivity cannot alter a region’s terrain undulation. This provides a reasonable basis for the exogeneity of the instrumental variable49. Substitution of core explanatory variable

Since the core explanatory variable in this study, New-Quality Agricultural Productivity (NQAP), is measured through an indicator system using the entropy-weighted TOPSIS method, potential measurement bias may arise from the selection and weighting of indicators. To further test the robustness of the results, this study drew on the methodology of Jin and Yang52 and employs text analysis to re-measure the NQAP index based on provincial government work reports from 2013 to 2023. First, based on official data from the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China and relevant literature, a seed word set consisting of 11 terms related to NQAP was identified. Second, considering that a single concept or entity may be expressed through multiple similar terms, this study applies the Word2Vec neural network model and deep learning techniques to expand the seed word set. To ensure measurement accuracy, only terms with a similarity score greater than 0.85 to the seed words were retained, while terms unrelated to the research topic were excluded. This process ultimately generated a keyword set consisting of 30 NQAP-related terms (Fig. 5). Finally, using Python 3.8, this study extracted these keywords from annual provincial government work reports and calculated the frequency of NQAP-related terms for each province. To avoid the loss of samples with zero-word frequency due to logarithmic transformation and to mitigate the influence of high-frequency samples on the estimation results, ln (word frequency + 1) was used as a proxy variable for NQAP. As shown in column (3) of Table 5, after replacing the core explanatory variable, NQAP still exerts a significant positive effect on AGTFP, thereby confirming the robustness of the regression results.

Heterogeneity analysis based on grain production region

Variations in resource endowments and agricultural production foundations across different grain-producing regions may influence the effectiveness of NQAP. As such, this study includes a heterogeneity analysis, categorizing regions into major grain-consuming regions, major grain-producing areas, and balanced production-consumption areas. Table 6, Column (2) indicates that the regression coefficient for NQAP in major grain-consuming regions is not statistically significant. Major grain-consuming regions mainly encompass large urban centers and regions with limited natural resource endowments for agriculture. NQAP development does not mitigate natural constraints, such as climate and soil conditions. This leads to the non-significant regression coefficient.

The NQAP regression coefficient for major grain-producing regions passes the significance test, but is small. This may be due to the challenges in further increasing agricultural output within grain-producing regions. These areas bear heavy burdens in agricultural production, focusing primarily on cultivating grain with a limited variety of agricultural products and small-scale cash crops. The sale of major agricultural products yields modest returns, with most agricultural profits flowing into the main grain-consuming regions. This inequitable distribution of benefits undermines production incentives in major grain-producing regions, slowing progress in achieving agricultural efficiency and improving quality54.

The balanced production-consumption regions have the highest NQAP regression coefficient at 0.2172. This reflects two factors. First, compared to the more developed grain-producing regions, these balanced regions have considerable room for agricultural improvement. Second, compared to grain-consuming regions, they have specific agricultural production conditions and resources, enabling the NQAP to have a pronounced effect in enhancing AGTFP.

Discussion

This paper examines the influence of NQAP on AGTFP by describing the characteristics and the spatial evolution of NQAP and AGTFP. The spatial distribution of NQAP has uneven characteristics, with a progressive diffusion pattern centered on the eastern coastal regions. The NQAP distribution pattern is characterized by higher levels in the east and west, and lower levels in the south and north. This aligns with the distribution pattern of new-quality productive forces56 and indicates that NQAP is a pivotal component and lever for developing these forces. The AGTFP shows spatial unevenness, characterized by dual-core development driven by Shandong and Henan. This pattern reflects extensive aggregation coupled with localized dispersion. This outcome is consistent with Zhu et al.57. There are regional disparities in agricultural resource utilization efficiencies and ecological conservation; these results enrich the spatial economic theoretical framework in environmental economics.

This paper is grounded in agriculture, which is the foundational sector of China’s economy, and adopts a green and sustainable perspective. By integrating NQAP with AGTFP into a unified analytical framework, this paper demonstrates the positive impact of NQAP on AGTFP. This finding contrasts with previous studies exploring the economic growth and efficiency-enhancing effects of NQAP58,59. As such, it validates the inherently green and sustainable nature of NQAP60. This has implications for broader productivity transformation and upgrading, and provides a theoretical framework for examining how technological innovation and shifts in productivity development models influence industrial green production.

This study also explores the intrinsic drivers of AGTFP, revealing the key mechanisms through which NQAP influences GEC and GTC. Building upon the mediation effect model, it examines the pathways through which NQAP exerts its influence. Considering the phased characteristics of its effects17, this study finds that GEC represents the primary pathway through which NQAP operates. GTC is an additional pathway, but with distinct threshold characteristics. This conclusion aligns with the technological catch-up theory, which posits that economies enhance AGTFP through efficiency gains during their early developmental stages. Genuine technological progress typically becomes pronounced after reaching a specific level of development61. This paper extends the technological catch-up theory to examine NQAP’s impact on AGTFP, clarifying the heterogeneous effects of NQAP on GEC and GTC.

This study reveals the complex and differentiated impact of NQAP on AGTFP, based on geographical location and production area classification62,63. With respect to location, the western region benefits the most from the NQAP’s enhancement effect on the AGTFP. This is driven by the Western Development Strategy. In contrast, in the central and western regions, accelerated industrialization and urbanization lead to crowding-out effects that lower the NQAP’s efficacy. With respect to production area heterogeneity, major grain-consuming regions, where agricultural output largely relies on external supplies, have negligible NQAP-driven AGTFP enhancement. major grain-producing regions, which are characterized by long-standing grain production fatigue and inequitable benefit distribution, have markedly constrained NQAP-driven AGTFP improvement. In contrast, balanced production-sales regions show the optimal NQAP-driven AGTFP enhancement effect.

Finally, building upon the theory of agricultural priority development, this study integrates the fragility of agricultural production with the critical importance of governmental support. The study incorporates governmental governance into the analytical framework for green agricultural production, thereby demonstrating that increased governmental support for agriculture and heightened governmental environmental awareness provide a favorable external policy environment in which NQAP influences AGTFP. This amplifies the efficacy of NQAP. This paper constructs an environment where market-driven influences can coexist with external governmental guidance and reveals how the NQAP influences the scenario-dependent characteristics of the AGTFP.

Conclusions and policy implications

Using panel data from 30 Chinese provinces spanning 2013–2023, this study examines the spatial distribution patterns of NQAP and AGTFP. It then evaluates the impact and mechanisms of NQAP forces on AGTFP through multiple dimensions, including direct effects, indirect effects, and heterogeneity. Key study findings are as follows. (1) Both NQAP and AGTFP have significant spatial non-equilibrium characteristics. (2) NQAP is an effective way to enhance AGTFP, but has a heterogeneous effect based on geographical location and production area. (3) The influence of NQAP on AGTFP primarily operates by enhancing GEC. There are distinct thresholds and phased characteristics with respect to the impact on GTC. The impact of NQAP on AGTFP can be strengthened by increasing both governmental support for agriculture and governmental environmental concern.

Based on these findings, this study proposes the following recommendations:

First, NQAP should be cultivated to enhance AGTFP. The spatial distribution of NQAP and AGTFP is imbalanced, due primarily to disparities in technological development levels and natural resource endowments. Western and northern regions should continuously increase investment in agricultural research and actively promote modern agricultural development models. They should use technological innovation and knowledge aggregation to increase and coordinate the development of NQAP and AGTFP. Eastern and southern regions should deepen the demonstration and spillover effects of NQAP and AGTFP, strengthening the driving force for agricultural green transformation.

Second, it is important to continuously refine strategies for enhancing NQAP and AGTFP. Guiding the application of NQAP in agricultural production should be emphasized by promoting the integration of digital-intelligent factors with traditional agricultural inputs to advance NQAP and AGTFP. Vigilance is needed to protect against the loss of production factors due to siphoning and polarization effects; this requires a combination of the “visible hand” and the “invisible hand” to build an effective market and a capable government. It is also vital to refine mechanisms for compensating competing interests between grain-producing regions to achieve shared benefits. This should enhance the profitability of agricultural production in major grain-producing regions, thereby stimulating the vitality of green agricultural production.

Third, the intermediary effects of enhancing green efficiency change and advancing green technology change need to be strengthened. This involves expanding the application of agricultural mechanization, digitalization, and intelligent equipment to leverage their factor integration and substitution effects. This would help alleviate non-rational resource allocations and increase the efficiency of green agricultural technologies. It is also important to guide the application of NQAP to promote green and sustainable development. This includes strengthening the application of modern technologies, resolving bottlenecks in agricultural technological innovation, and promoting the development of green agricultural technologies.

This study’s findings provide value for the field, but like all studies, it has limitations. First, due to data availability, this paper uses provincial-level data to examine the macro-level relationship between NQAP and AGTFP, but does not fully investigate the role of micro-level entities in green agricultural production. Future research should comprehensively explore the impacts and operational mechanisms by which NQAP affects AGTFP, by integrating subjective input from individual farmers and new agricultural business entities in agricultural modernization and green production. Second, the relationship between NQAP and AGTFP may have complex dynamic evolution characteristics and spatial spillover effects. Future studies should apply dynamic models and spatial econometric models to investigate their long-term effects and spillover impacts. Third, NQAP is characterized by comprehensiveness, systemic complexity, and multidimensionality, making it difficult to fully capture its essence with a single indicator. Although this paper constructs an indicator system to measure NQAP in a relatively comprehensive manner and employs text analysis methods for robustness testing, certain elements, such as indicator selection and keyword construction, may still involve a degree of subjectivity. Future research should further develop more objective, standardized, and multidimensional measurement methods based on the essential attributes and core characteristics of NQAP, thereby improving the scientific rigor and robustness of the measurement results. Despite these limitations, this study expands the research field of agricultural modernization and greening by examining the impact and underlying mechanisms of NQAP on AGTFP.
