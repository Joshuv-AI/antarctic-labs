Differentially Private Obfuscation of Power Grid Dynamics
Shengyang Wu, Vladimir Dvorkin

Abstract
Dynamic models of power systems are critical for analyzing grid response to disturbances and blackouts, but the release of real-world dynamic models is hindered by privacy and cybersecurity concerns, as such models carry sensitive information about transmission, generation, and load parameters. We develop an algorithm for synthesizing dynamic grid models from real-world power grids balancing two objectives: the privacy of the source grid, quantitatively measured using the notion of differential privacy, and the fidelity of the synthesized model. The algorithm applies privacy-preserving noise to obfuscate the original grid parameters, but then optimizes the perturbed parameters to ensure that the resulting model dynamics are statistically consistent with those observed in the source grid. Application to the frequency dynamics of the IEEE 30-bus system reveals the inherent privacy-fidelity trade-off: stricter privacy requirements degrade modeling fidelity, yet optimization significantly improves the quality of the synthesized models.

III-A

Background on Differential Privacy

Given a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
stacking the parameters of model (
1
), the DP goal is to produce its synthetic counterpart
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
that yields similar frequency dynamics yet does not disclose the original parameters up to prescribed privacy guarantees. Formally, releasing
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
must make
\mathbf{x}
indistinguishable from an adjacent vector
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
in the following sense.

Definition 1

(Adjacency)
.

Two vectors
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
\alpha-
adjacent, for some
\alpha>0
, if
\exists i\in\{1,\dots,n
}, such that
x_{j}=x_{j}^{\prime},\forall j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\backslash i
, and
|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}|\leqslant\alpha
. That is, they are different in one item by at most
\alpha
.
\triangleleft

DP guarantees that an adversary cannot distinguish between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
when observing
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
, even when it knows all but one element of
\mathbf{x}
, up to a privacy loss parameter
\varepsilon>0
. The vector-Laplace mechanism is commonly used to establish this guarantee
[
10
]
. Let
\text{Lap}(s)^{n}
denote an
n-
dimensional zero-mean Laplace random variable with scale parameter
s
. Then, the DP synthetic release takes the form
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}+\text{Lap}(\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon})^{n}
, i.e., the original vector is perturbed by a calibrated Laplace noise. This satisfies the following definition of
\varepsilon-
DP.

Definition 2

(
\varepsilon-
DP)
.

The Laplace mechanism is called
\varepsilon-
DP if, for any outcome
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
in the range of the mechanism, and any two
\alpha-
adjacent vectors
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
, the ratio of probabilities of observing the same outcome is bounded as

\displaystyle\left|\log\left(\frac{\text{Pr}[\mathbf{x}{{}^{\prime}}+\text{Lap}(\alpha/\varepsilon)^{n}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}]}{\text{Pr}[\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+\text{Lap}(\alpha/\varepsilon)^{n}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}]}\right)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon,

(18)

i.e., the log-likelihood ratio associated with any outcome
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
under two
\alpha
-adjacent vectors is uniformly bounded by
\varepsilon
.

\triangleleft

A smaller privacy loss
\varepsilon
makes the outcomes on adjacent vectors more indistinguishable, thus stronger protecting their difference. Stronger guarantees, however, come at the cost of larger noise, as the Laplace scale
\alpha/\varepsilon
grows as
\varepsilon
decreases.

We next present two important properties of DP.

Theorem 3

(Sequential composition
[
14
]
)
.

A series of
k

\varepsilon_{i}-
DP releases of
\mathbf{x}
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}-
DP.

Theorem 4

(Post-processing immunity
[
14
]
)
.

If
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
satisfies
\varepsilon
-DP, then
g\circ\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
, where
g
is any data-independent post-processing of the query answer, also satisfies
\varepsilon
-DP.

The former bounds the privacy loss across multiple computations on the original data, and the latter states that any data-independent transformation of the DP release answer preserves the privacy guarantee. The two are important building blocks.

DP Release of Frequency Dynamics Models

We seek to synthesize a DP counterpart of (

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\gamma}^{0}\dot{\bm{\omega}}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{m}+\text{Lap}\left(\frac{\alpha_{m}}{\varepsilon/3}\right)^{\!\!|\gamma|\!\!}\!\!,\;\tilde{\mathbf{d}}^{0}=\mathbf{d}+\text{Lap}\left(\frac{\alpha_{d}}{\varepsilon/3}\right)^{\!\!|\gamma|}\!\!\!\!\!\!

2.

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{|\mathcal{E}|}}\left[\mathbf{k}+\text{Lap}\left(\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\varepsilon/3}\right)^{|\mathcal{E}|}\right]

The second step is the DP Kron reduction. Since the perturbation is applied directly to the edge weights prior to forming the Laplacian, the resulting
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}

The release of the perturbed model (

-DP guarantee per the following result.

(

-DP of frequency dynamics model)

-DP release of the frequency dynamics model for
\alpha_{k}
-adjacent power flow sensitivity,
\alpha_{m}
-adjacent inertia, and
\alpha_{d}
-adjacent damping vectors.
\triangleleft

Proof.

Per the Laplace mechanism (Definition

) and sequential composition (Theorem

\tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon

). To ensure modeling fidelity, we propose to post-process synthetic inertia, damping and network parameters while preserving the privacy guarantee of Theorem
5
. That is, we seek the smallest adjustment of
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}^{0},\tilde{\mathbf{d}}^{0}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{0}
that replicates the frequency dynamics of the source model. We make the following assumption.

Assumption 1

(Public knowledge)
.

The frequency trajectories
\hat{\bm{\omega}}(0),\dots,\hat{\bm{\omega}}(T)
of the source model are publicly observable.

This assumption is reasonable, as frequency trajectories under severe transients are already publicly released, e.g., after the 2025 Iberian Peninsula blackout
[
15
]
. By post-processing immunity (Theorem
4
), fitting the DP model to such public trajectories does not affect the DP guarantee of Theorem
5
.

The proposed post-processing optimization takes the form:

\displaystyle\underset{{\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{m}},\tilde{\mathbf{d}},\bm{\omega},\bm{\delta},\mathbf{p}}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\left\|\bm{\omega}(t)-\hat{\bm{\omega}}(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\rho\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\mathbf{k}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{\text{r}}^{0}\\
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}-\tilde{\mathbf{m}}^{0}\\
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}-\tilde{\mathbf{d}}^{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}

(21a)

subject to

\displaystyle\text{diag}[\tilde{\mathbf{m}}]

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\omega}}(t)=\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}\text{diag}[\tilde{\mathbf{k}}]\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}^{\top}\bm{\delta}(t)-\text{diag}[\tilde{\mathbf{d}}]\bm{\omega}(t)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\dot{\bm{\omega}}(t)=}\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;+\mathbf{p}(t)-\bm{\ell}_{\gamma}-\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{ac}}^{0}\bm{\ell}_{\beta},

(21b)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\delta}}(t)=\bm{\omega}(t),\;\!\dot{\mathbf{p}}(t)=\!-\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{p}(t)+\mathbf{R}\bm{\omega}(t)),

(21c)

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}(0)=0,\;\bm{\delta}(0)=0,\;\mathbf{p}(0)=0.

(21d)

which jointly optimizes the model parameters
\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{m}},\tilde{\mathbf{d}}
and the frequency states
\bm{\omega},\bm{\delta},\mathbf{p}
coupled through the ODE constraints (
21b
)–(
21d
). The objective minimizes the distance between the public
\hat{\bm{\omega}}
and resulting
\bm{\omega}
frequency trajectories over a period
T
, improving modeling fidelity, with a regularization term weighted by
\rho>0
anchoring the solution to the Laplace perturbation (
20
). Constraint (
21b
) replicates the swing equation (
19
), where
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}
are diagonalized to form the inertia and damping matrices, and the Kron-reduced Laplacian
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{red}}=\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}\text{diag}[\tilde{\mathbf{k}}]\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}^{\top}
is reconstructed from the optimized edge weights
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}
and the node-edge incidence matrix
\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}
of the reduced network. For simplicity, the accompanying matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{ac}}
is fixed to its DP value
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{ac}}^{0}
from Step 2 in (
20
) and is not post-processed; accordingly,
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}
in (
21
) refers exclusively to the edge weights of
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{red}}
, with
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{\text{r}}^{0}
from
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{\text{red}}^{0}=\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}\text{diag}[\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{\text{r}}^{0}]\mathbf{C}_{\text{r}}^{\top}
serving as the regularization reference.

The ODE-constrained problem (
21
) is not amenable to off-the-shelf solvers. We propose a gradient-based solution via the adjoint method
[
5
]
. Let
\bm{\theta}=(\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{m}},\tilde{\mathbf{d}})
collect the model parameters and
\mathbf{x}=(\bm{\omega},\bm{\delta},\mathbf{p})
the system states. Problem (
21
) is written compactly as:

\displaystyle\underset{{\bm{\theta},\mathbf{x}}}{\text{minimize}}\;

\displaystyle J(\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\left\|\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}(t)\!-\hat{\bm{\omega}}(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\rho\|\bm{\theta}-\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}\|_{2}^{2}

subject to

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=f(\mathbf{x}(t),\bm{\theta}),\quad\mathbf{x}(0)=\mathbf{0},

(22)

where
\mathbf{H}
extracts the frequency states from
\mathbf{x}
. The proposed algorithm initializes
\bm{\theta}
from the Laplace perturbation outcome
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}
in (
20
) and updates it via gradient descent:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}\leftarrow\bm{\theta}-\eta\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J(\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta}),

(23)

where
\eta>0
is the step size (learning rate). By the chain rule, the total gradient
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J
decomposes as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\left(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}l\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}+\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l\right)\mathrm{d}t

(24)

where
l(\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta})=\|\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}(t)-\hat{\bm{\omega}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\rho}{T}\|\bm{\theta}-\tilde{\bm{\theta}}^{0}\|_{2}^{2}
. The term
\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l
is straightforward to compute. The difficulty lies in
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}
, which captures how the states respond to parameter changes through the ODE constraint in (
22
). Differentiating (
22
) with respect to
\bm{\theta}
yields the sensitivity ODE:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{t}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}=\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}+\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f,\quad\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(0)=\mathbf{0},

(25)

which must be integrated forward in time for each component of
\bm{\theta}
. Since
|\bm{\theta}|
grows with network size, this is computationally intractable at scale. The adjoint method resolves this by replacing the
|\bm{\theta}|
sensitivity ODEs with a single adjoint ODE of the same dimension as
\mathbf{x}
, from which the integral term in (
24
) is recovered without computing
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}
explicitly.

To derive the total derivative via the adjoint method, we formulation the Lagrangian function of (
22
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\big[l(\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta})-\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\left(\dot{\mathbf{x}}-f(\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta})\right)\big]\mathrm{d}t\;-\bm{\mu}^{\top}\mathbf{x}(0),

(26)

where
\bm{\lambda}
is the time-dependent vector of Lagrange multipliers (also termed
adjoint variables
) associated with the ODE, and
\bm{\mu}
is the time-invariant multiplier associated with the initial condition. Since constraints of (
22
) are equalities, we have
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J
, so the total derivative is expressed from (
26
) as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\left[\partial_{\mathbf{x}}l\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}+\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l-\bm{\lambda}^{\top}(\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\dot{\mathbf{x}}-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f\,\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}-\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f)\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\bm{\mu}^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\left[\partial_{\mathbf{x}}l\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}+\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l+\bm{\lambda}^{\top}(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}+\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f)\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\bm{\mu}^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(0)-\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(t)\Big|_{0}^{T}+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\big[\dot{\bm{\lambda}}^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\theta}\mathbf{x}\big]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\big[(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}l\!+\!\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f\!+\!\dot{\bm{\lambda}})^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}\big]\mathrm{dt}\!+\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\big[\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l\!+\!\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f\big]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\bm{\lambda}(T)^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(T)+(\bm{\lambda}(0)-\bm{\mu})^{\top}\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}(0),

(27)

which still contains the gradient
\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}\mathbf{x}
difficult to compute. We eliminate it using the following adjoint equations
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{x}}l+\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f+\dot{\bm{\lambda}}=\mathbf{0},

(28a)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(T)=\mathbf{0},\quad\bm{\lambda}(0)=\bm{\mu},

(28b)

where (
28a
) is an ODE for the adjoint variable
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(t)
,
integrated backward in time from
t=T
to
t=0
with terminal condition

\boldsymbol{\lambda}(T)=\mathbf{0}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(0)

is the multiplier on the initial state constraint. Plugging (
28
) into (
27
) simplifies the gradient as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\bm{\theta}}J=\int_{0}^{T}\big[\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l+\bm{\lambda}^{\top}\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f\big]\mathrm{d}t,

(29)

where
\partial_{\bm{\theta}}l
and
\partial_{\bm{\theta}}f
are straightforward partial derivatives of known functions, and
\bm{\lambda}(t)
is obtained by solving the adjoint ODE (
28a
) backward in time. The total cost of computing the gradient is one forward ODE solve for
\mathbf{x}(t)
and one backward ODE solve for
\bm{\lambda}(t)
, regardless of the dimension of
\bm{\theta}
.

DP Release of Grid Dynamics Model

The algorithm for DP release of the frequency dynamics model is summarized in Alg.

V
Conclusion

We develop a principled framework for synthesizing power grid dynamic models from real-world systems with formal DP guarantees on the source grid parameters. Applied to grid frequency dynamics, the framework eliminates the risk of reverse engineering network parameters from Kron-reduced networks via calibrated perturbation of the source admittances. An ODE-constrained post-processing optimization then recovers modeling fidelity lost to DP noise while preserving the privacy guarantee, yielding synthetic models that are both privacy-preserving and useful for downstream analysis. In doing so, this work extends DP and optimization-based post-processing from steady-state grid optimization data to power grid dynamics, protecting the dynamical model itself while preserving its frequency response under load transients.

Experiments on the IEEE 30-bus system confirm the privacy-fidelity trade-off: stricter privacy requirements increase frequency trajectory deviation, yet post-processing consistently recovers fidelity across all privacy levels. The parameter distributions reveal interpretable impacts of post-processing optimization: lines and generators critical for frequency dynamics undergo larger post-processing adjustments, while less influential components retain wider variability.

Future work will focus on testing the framework in applications to synchronous generator, PSS, AVR, and inverter models, and to linear dynamical systems more broadly.
