A model of anisotropic branched optimal transport
Martina Bellettini & Andrea Marchese

Abstract
We propose a new anisotropic optimal transport model based on the theory of currents, where the anisotropic cost function splits as the product of a factor depending only on the spatial direction and a factor depending only on the multiplicity of the current. We prove that the planar transport problem admits a minimizer. In arbitrary dimension, we show that a minimizer exists provided that the ambient space endowed with the anisotropic norm is hypermetric.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to prove the existence of minimizers for an anisotropic branched transport problem. Branched transport problems model situations in which transporting mass together along a common route is cheaper than transporting it separately. Classical examples come from communication networks, irrigation patterns, the cardiovascular or nervous system, the veins of a leaf and the branches or roots of a tree. In these models, the cost depends on the whole transport network, rather than only on the distance between source and target. For this reason, the relevant competitors are not maps or plans, but one-dimensional objects representing the union of the trajectories of the moving particles, with their orientation, carrying a multiplicity which represents the intensity of the flow.

The formulation in terms of rectifiable currents was introduced by Xia in
[
34
]
, generalizing Gilbert’s discrete model, where the source and target are atomic measures connected by a weighted oriented graph (see
[
19
]
). A Lagrangian formulation was introduced in
[
23
]
; the equivalence between the main formulations is discussed in the monograph
[
3
]
, see also
[
30
]
. In both formulations, the cost is designed to favor the aggregation of mass along common paths, and this feature is typically encoded through a suitable function of the multiplicity.

Several aspects of branched transport and related models have been studied in the literature. Elementary and structural properties of optimal irrigation patterns are investigated in
[
17
,
2
]
, while interior and boundary regularity results are established in
[
35
,
36
,
27
,
6
]
; examples exhibiting highly nontrivial branching behavior can be found in
[
26
,
20
]
. Stability, well-posedness and uniqueness issues are addressed in
[
4
,
13
,
14
,
10
]
. Alternative formulations and variational viewpoints are developed in
[

,
8
,

]
. Related extensions and variants include multi-material transport problems
[

,
24
]
, transport problems with capacity constraints
[

]
, and shape-optimization-type questions
[

,

]
. Finally, further recent directions concern convex relaxations and formulas for the

-mass
[

]
and dimensional estimates
[

.

In several applications it is natural to allow the cost to depend not only on the multiplicity, but also on the orientation of the moving particles, because some spatial directions might be preferable to others. This motivates the anisotropic model considered here.

Let us describe the framework more precisely. Let
\mu^{-},\mu^{+}
be positive finite measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mu^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mu^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We want to transport the

\mu^{-}

\mu^{+}
by means of a

, namely a rectifiable
1
-current

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

We consider the class of competitors

\mathcal{C}\vcentcolon=\{R\in\mathbf{R}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\partial R=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}\}\text{.}

Recall that a rectifiable
1
-current
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be written as
R=\llbracket E,\tau,\theta\rrbracket
, where

is a
1
-rectifiable set,

\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

.

The cost functional considered in this paper is the anisotropic
H
-mass. We first introduce it for general rectifiable
k

Definition 1.1
.

We call

a branching function if it is even, lower semicontinuous, subadditive and

.

Definition 1.2
.

A

-anisotropy in

is a continuous function
\sigma_{k}:{\rm G}(k,n)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}

and

E
and
F
.

Continuous strictly positive functions on

are known in the literature as

(see for example
[
31
]
).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be a
k
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any nonzero simple
k
-vector

, we set
\hat{v}\vcentcolon=\frac{v}{\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}}
and we define

\lVert v\rVert_{\sigma_{k}}\vcentcolon=\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}\sigma_{k}(L_{\hat{v}})\text{,}

where
L_{w}
denotes the vector subspace spanned by the
k
-vector

. We call
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sigma_{k}}
a
k
-anisotropic norm.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
be a
1
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since every
1
-vector is simple, Definition
1.3
induces a positively

-homogeneous function on

We say that

is

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be an anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
H

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)\vcentcolon=\int_{E}{H(\theta(x))\lVert\tau(x)\rVert_{\sigma_{k}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}(x)\text{.}

The subadditivity of

Our main goal is to study the

problem, abbreviated as (ABOT):

\inf\{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R):R\in\mathcal{C}\}\text{.}

(
ABOT
)

We also consider the sequential lower semicontinuous envelope of
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}
on
\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(see Section

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be a
k
-anisotropy on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the sequential lower semicontinuous envelope of
{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}}

\Phi_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)\vcentcolon=\inf\biggl\{\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(P_{i}):P_{i}\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathbb{F}(R-P_{i})\to 0\biggr\}\qquad\forall R\in\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{.}

(1.1)

We refer to Definition
2.5
for the notion of integral geometric representation of a
k
-anisotropy. Our first main result identifies
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}
with its relaxation.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be a
k
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)=\Phi_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)\text{.}

In particular,
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}
is sequentially flat lower semicontinuous on
\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The hypothesis that

is a branching function is necessary for the functional

to be well-defined and lower semicontinuous on

; for the details, see
[
11
]
. Our second main result concerns the existence of solutions to the problem (

.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
\sigma_{1}
be a convex
1
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H
be a branching function that is non-decreasing on

\inf\{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R):R\in\mathcal{C}\}<+\infty.

Then:

1.

If

, problem (

2.

If

If

Definition 2.2

Let

Remark 2.4

The quantity

Definition 2.8

A metric space

is said to be hypermetric if for any

2.

3
Integral geometric representations

3.1
General facts

Throughout this section, we fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
0<k\leq n-1
. The relevant case for the optimal transport problem is
k=1
; for completeness, we state the results for general
k
when possible.

Proposition 3.1
.

A
k
-anisotropy
\sigma_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
admits an integral geometric representation via a positive measure
\varphi_{n-k}
on
{\rm G}(n-k,n)
if and only if for every linear
k
-subspace
E\in{\rm G}(k,n)
and every Borel set
M\subset E
it holds

\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)\sigma_{k}(E)=\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}{\int_{L}{\mathcal{H}^{0}(p_{L}^{-1}(y)\cap M)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}(y)}\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\text{.}

(3.1)

Proof.

If
\sigma_{k}
admits an integral geometric representation, then by Remark
2.6
and the area formula applied to the linear map
p_{L}|_{E}:E\to L
, see equation 8.2 of
[
32
]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)\sigma_{k}(E)&=\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}J(E,L)\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\\
&=\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}\mathcal{H}^{k}(p_{L}(M))\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\\
&=\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}{\int_{L}{\mathcal{H}^{0}(p_{L}^{-1}(y)\cap M)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}(y)}\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\text{.}\end{split}

Conversely, if we assume that (
3.1
) holds, then by the same computation above, after dividing by
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)
for some Borel set
M\subset E
with
0<\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)<+\infty
, we obtain

\sigma_{k}(E)=\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}J(E,L)\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L),

and therefore
\sigma_{k}
admits an integral geometric representation via
\varphi_{n-k}
.
∎

3.2
The planar case

Remark 3.2
.

By Definition

, if
\sigma_{1}
is convex then
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We set

C_{\sigma_{1}}\vcentcolon=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lVert u\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}\leq 1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
\sigma_{1}
is convex, then
C_{\sigma_{1}}
is convex, compact, centrally symmetric and
0\in{\rm Int}C_{\sigma_{1}}
. Conversely, any convex, compact, centrally symmetric set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in{\rm Int}C
identifies and is identified by a norm having
C
as unit ball.

In this section, we show that any
1
-anisotropic norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits an integral geometric representation. This result is well-known. For the reader’s convenience, we provide a self-contained constructive proof based on approximation by centrally symmetric polygons.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex, compact, centrally symmetric set with
0\in{\rm Int}C
. Let us first consider the case
C=P\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
P
a polygon.
Let
2N\geq 4
be the number of edges of
P
. We fix any vertex of
P
and we call
v_{1}
the vector identified by such vertex. Moving counterclockwise from
v_{1}
, we name the vertices
v_{2},\dots,v_{2N}
.
For
i=1,\dots,2N
, we call
F_{i}
the counterclockwise edge of
P
with first extreme point
v_{i}
and we call
w_{i}:=Rv_{i}
, where
R
is the counterclockwise rotation of
90
degrees.

Remark 3.3
.

1.

By central symmetry of
P
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}

v_{i+N}=-v_{i},\quad w_{i+N}=-w_{i}.

2.

With this choice of
w_{1},\dots,w_{2N}
we have

w_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{i,x}\\
w_{i,y}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-v_{i,y}\\
v_{i,x}\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

3.

If the edge
F_{i}
is not parallel to any axis, it lies in a line of equation
y=m_{i}x+q_{i}
for some nonzero
m_{i},q_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

i\in\{1,\dots,2N\}.
We have that by symmetry of
P

m_{i+N}=m_{i},\quad q_{i+N}=-q_{i}

for any
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

Moreover, a vector
u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{x}\\
u_{y}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
belongs to the line containing the edge
F_{i}
if and only if

u_{y}=m_{i}u_{x}+q_{i}\quad\iff\quad a_{i}u_{x}+b_{i}u_{y}=1,

where
a_{i}=-\frac{m_{i}}{q_{i}},b_{i}=\frac{1}{q_{i}}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a centrally symmetric, convex polygon.
Then there exist positive coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
depending on
P
such that:

1.

for any vector
u\in\partial P
it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lvert\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\rvert=1,

(3.2)

2.

if
B_{r}(0)\subset P
for some
r\in(0,+\infty),
then there exists a constant
M=M(r)
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert\leq M.

Proof.

Up to a rotation, we can assume that

•

none of the vertices of
P
lies on the coordinate axes,

•

none of the edges of
P
is parallel to the coordinate axes.

For each
i=1,\dots,2N
, let

n_{i}\vcentcolon=(a_{i},b_{i}),

so that the line containing
F_{i}
is given by

\langle{n_{i}},{u}\rangle=1.

By Remark
3.3
, for
i=1,\dots,N
we have

n_{i+N}=-n_{i}
.

We first prove point
1
. For
k=1,\dots,N
, define
\lambda_{k}>0
by

2\lambda_{k}w_{k}=n_{k}-n_{k-1},\qquad\text{with }n_{0}\vcentcolon=-n_{N}.

(3.3)

This is well defined, because both
n_{k}
and
n_{k-1}
satisfy

\langle{n_{k}},{v_{k}}\rangle=\langle{n_{k-1}},{v_{k}}\rangle=1,

hence

\langle{n_{k}-n_{k-1}},{v_{k}}\rangle=0.

Therefore
n_{k}-n_{k-1}
is orthogonal to
v_{k}
, so it is a scalar multiple of

w_{k}=Rv_{k}
.
Since the outward normals rotate monotonically along the boundary of a convex polygon, this scalar is positive; hence
\lambda_{k}>0
.

Summing (
3.3
), for every
k=1,\dots,N
we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}w_{i}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}\lambda_{i}w_{i}=n_{k}.

(3.4)

Indeed,

2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}w_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-n_{i-1})=n_{k}+n_{N},

while

2\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}\lambda_{i}w_{i}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}(n_{i}-n_{i-1})=n_{N}-n_{k},

and subtracting gives (
3.4
).

Now let
u\in F_{k}
with
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Since the vertices are ordered counterclockwise and
u
lies on the edge joining
v_{k}
to
v_{k+1}
, we have

\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for }i\leq k,\qquad\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\leq 0\quad\text{for }i\geq k+1.

Hence, by (
3.4
),

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lvert\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\rvert=\langle{u},{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}w_{i}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}\lambda_{i}w_{i}}\rangle=\langle{u},{n_{k}}\rangle=1.

If instead
u\in F_{k}
with
k\in\{N+1,\dots,2N\}
, then
-u\in F_{k-N}
and therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lvert\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\rvert=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lvert\langle{-u},{w_{i}}\rangle\rvert=1.

This proves (
3.2
).

We now prove point (2). From (
3.3
) and
\lVert w_{i}\rVert=\lVert v_{i}\rVert
we get

2\lambda_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert=\lVert n_{i}-n_{i-1}\rVert,

hence

\lambda_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\lvert a_{i}-a_{i-1}\rvert+\lvert b_{i}-b_{i-1}\rvert\bigr),\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

where again
n_{0}=-n_{N}
.

Assume now that
B_{r}(0)\subset P
. Since each line
\langle{n_{i}},{u}\rangle=1
supports
P
, its distance from the origin is at least
r
and therefore

\lVert n_{i}\rVert\leq\frac{1}{r}\qquad\text{for every }i.

In particular,

\lvert a_{i}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{r},\qquad\lvert b_{i}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{r}.

For
i=1,\dots,N
, the normals
n_{i}
move counterclockwise. After splitting them into at most four sectors, each coordinate is monotone on each sector. Therefore the total variations of the sequences
(a_{i})_{i}
and
(b_{i})_{i}
are bounded by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lvert a_{i}-a_{i-1}\rvert\leq\frac{8}{r},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lvert b_{i}-b_{i-1}\rvert\leq\frac{8}{r}.

Summing the previous estimate on
\lambda_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert
, we conclude

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\lvert a_{i}-a_{i-1}\rvert+\lvert b_{i}-b_{i-1}\rvert\bigr)\leq\frac{8}{r}.

Hence the thesis holds with
M(r)=8/r
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Denoting by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{P}
the norm whose unit ball is
P
, we have proven that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\lVert u\rVert_{P}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\lvert\langle{u},{w_{i}}\rangle\rvert\text{.}

If
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is any compact, convex, centrally symmetric set with
0\in{\rm Int}C
, we can prove an integral geometric formula for
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C}
, the norm induced by
C
, by approximation via polygons.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact, centrally symmetric and convex set with
0\in{\rm Int}C
. Then
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C}
admits an integral geometric representation, i.e. there exists a measure
\mu_{C}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\lVert u\rVert_{C}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{\lvert\langle{u},{\omega}\rangle\rvert}\,d\mu_{C}(\omega)\text{.}

Proof.

We claim that there exists a nested sequence
\{P_{k}\}_{k}
of centrally symmetric, convex polygons in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that:

1.

C\subset P_{k}
for every
k
,

2.

The Hausdorff distance
d_{\mathcal{H}}(P_{k},C)\longrightarrow 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

To prove such claim consider for every
k\geq 2
the points
Q^{k}_{1},\dots,Q^{k}_{2^{k}}
, where each
Q^{k}_{j}
is obtained intersecting
\partial C
with the halfline

\left\{\left(t\cos\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{k}}2\pi\right),t\sin\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{k}}2\pi\right)\right):t\in[0,+\infty)\right\}\text{.}

For every
j=1,\ldots 2^{k-1}
, consider a half-space
H^{k}_{j}
such that

•

if
Q^{k}_{j}=Q^{k-1}_{i}
for some
i
, then
H^{k}_{j}=H^{k-1}_{i}
,

•

H^{k}_{j}\supset C
for every
j
,

•

\partial H^{k}_{j}\ni Q^{k}_{j}
for every
j
.

For
j=2^{k-1}+1,\ldots,2^{k}
, set
H^{k}_{j}\vcentcolon=-H^{k}_{j-2^{k-1}}
.
Finally, let
{P}_{k}
be the convex polygon

{P}_{k}\vcentcolon=\bigcap_{j=1}^{2^{k}}H^{k}_{j}\text{.}

By construction, property
1
holds and
{P}_{k}
is centrally symmetric and convex. We prove that also property
2
holds. Denote

D:=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}P_{k}\text{.}

Assume by contradiction that
2
does not hold. Since the
P_{k}
’s are nested and contain
C
, we have that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(P_{k},C)\xrightarrow{k\to+\infty}0
if and only if
D\equiv C
, hence we assume that there exists a point
p\in D\setminus C
. Denote by
z
the point
z:={\rm conv}\{0,p\}\cap\partial C
. We have that

•

For every
k\geq 2
and for every
j=1,\dots 2^{k}
, the point
Q^{k}_{j}\in\partial D
: indeed,
Q^{k}_{j}\in C
by construction and therefore
Q^{k}_{j}\in D
, but
Q^{k}_{j}\in\partial P_{k}
and hence
Q^{k}_{j}\not\in{\rm Int}D
;

•

By construction, the set
\mathcal{Q}\vcentcolon=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{j=1,\ldots,2^{k}}\{Q^{k}_{j}\}
is dense in
\partial C
.

Hence,
z\in\partial C=\overline{\mathcal{Q}}\subset\partial D
.
Since
D
is closed and convex and
z\in\partial D
, there exists a supporting closed half-space
H
such that

D\subset H
and
z\in\partial H
.
Moreover,
0\in{\rm Int}C\subset{\rm Int}D
, hence
0\in{\rm Int}H
.
Since
z\in{\rm conv}\{0,p\}
and
z\in\partial H
, while
0\in{\rm Int}H
, it follows that

p\notin H
.
This contradicts the fact that
p\in D\subset H
.

Now we associate to any polygon
P_{k}
the measure
\mu_{k}\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\lambda_{i}^{k}\lVert v_{i}^{k}\rVert\delta_{\omega_{i}^{k}}
supported on
\mathbb{S}^{1},
where
\omega_{i}^{k}\vcentcolon=\frac{w_{i}^{k}}{\lVert w_{i}^{k}\rVert}
and
\lambda_{i}^{k},

v_{i}^{k},

w_{i}^{k}
are determined as in Proposition
3.4
.
Since
0\in{\rm Int}C
, there exists
r>0
such that
B_{r}(0)\subset C\subset P_{k}
for every
k
. Therefore, by Proposition
3.4
, the measures
\mu_{k}
have uniformly bounded total variation:

\mu_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\lambda_{i}^{k}\lVert v_{i}^{k}\rVert\leq M(r).

Hence, there exists a measure
\mu_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that

\mu_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu_{\infty}

up to subsequence. We claim that
\mu_{C}:=\mu_{\infty}
satisfies the thesis. Indeed, since
d_{\mathcal{H}}(P_{k},C)\longrightarrow 0
as
k\to+\infty,
we have that

\lVert u\rVert_{P_{k}}\longrightarrow\lVert u\rVert_{C}\qquad\text{uniformly in $u\in\mathbb{S}^{1}$, as }k\to+\infty,

and, for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{\lvert\langle{u},{\omega}\rangle\rvert}\,d\mu_{k}(\omega)\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{\lvert\langle{u},{\omega}\rangle\rvert}\,d\mu_{\infty}(\omega)\text{,}

by the weak
*
convergence of the measures
\mu_{k}.

Since by Proposition
3.4

\lVert u\rVert_{P_{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\lambda_{i}^{k}\lVert v_{i}^{k}\rVert\lvert\langle{u},{\omega_{i}^{k}}\rangle\rvert=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{\lvert\langle{u},{\omega}\rangle\rvert}\,d\mu_{k}(\omega)\text{,}

the thesis follows.
∎

We stress that this argument is genuinely two-dimensional: it relies on the planar identification of a direction with its orthogonal one. For this reason, the proof does not extend directly to polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where the unit cube already provides a counterexample, see Example
2.9
, point
3
).

4
Lower semicontinuity and relaxation

4.1
Proof of
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}\leq\Phi_{H,\sigma_{k}}
on
\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

We claim that, if
\sigma_{k}
admits an integral geometric representation, then
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}
is sequentially flat lower semicontinuous on
\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The proof is similar to the one presented in
[
11
]
; we show the main steps for the sake of completeness. If
R=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\llbracket\{x_{i}\},1,\theta_{i}\rrbracket\in\mathbf{R}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
if
i\neq j
, we define the
H
-mass of
R
as

\mathbb{M}_{H}(R)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(\theta_{i})\text{.}

Let
R\in\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
\{P_{j}\}_{j}\subset\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence such that
\mathbb{F}(R-P_{j})\longrightarrow 0
as
j\to+\infty
. Since
\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}
is a finite measure, from
[
11
, Prop. 2.6]
it follows that, up to a not relabeled subsequence,
\mathbb{F}(\langle{R},{p_{L}},{y}\rangle-\langle{P_{j}},{p_{L}},{y}\rangle)\longrightarrow 0
for
\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{k}
-a.e.
(L,y)\in{\rm G}(k,n)\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. By Lemma
3.8
and using that
\mathbb{M}_{H}
is sequentially flat lower semicontinuous on

we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)&=\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}{\int_{L}{\mathbb{M}_{H}(\langle{R},{p_{L}},{y}\rangle)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}(y)}\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\\

Therefore, using Fatou’s lemma, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R)&\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{{\rm G}(k,n)}{\int_{L}{\mathbb{M}_{H}(\langle{P_{j}},{p_{L}},{y}\rangle)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}(y)}\,d\varphi_{n-k}^{\perp}(L)\\

Passing to the infimum over the set of sequences

we get
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}\leq\Phi_{H,\sigma_{k}}
on
\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

∎

5
Existence of minimizers

We begin with a few preliminary results that allow us to reduce problem (
ABOT
) to a locally flat compact class of competitors.

Remark 5.1
.

The class
\mathcal{C}
of competitors for problem (
ABOT
) is nonempty.
This follows from the standard approximation procedure used in branched transport; see for instance
[
34
, Prop. 3.1]
. More precisely, one approximates
\mu^{-}
and
\mu^{+}
by sequences of atomic measures

(\mu_{h}^{-})_{h}
and
(\mu_{h}^{+})_{h}
with equal total mass, constructs a polyhedral current
P_{1}

with boundary
\mu_{1}^{+}-\mu_{1}^{-}
and then adds correction currents joining, for every
h\geq 1

\mu_{h}^{-}
to
\mu_{h+1}^{-}
and
\mu_{h}^{+}
to
\mu_{h+1}^{+}
. In this way one obtains a summable series of polyhedral currents, whose sum is a rectifiable current

R\in\mathbf{R}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\partial R=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}.

In general, however, the value of problem (
ABOT
) may be
+\infty
. Hence, in the existence theorem below, one has to assume

\inf\{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R):R\in\mathcal{C}\}<+\infty.

5.1
Compactness of minimizing sequences

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be a
k
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H
be a branching function non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let
R\in\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
C\in\mathbf{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a nontrivial cyclic subcurrent of
R
. Then

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R-C)\leq\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R).

Proof.

Write

R=\llbracket E,\tau,\theta\rrbracket.

Since
C
is a rectifiable subcurrent of
R
, by the standard characterization of subcurrents of rectifiable currents there exists a Borel function
\lambda:E\to[0,1]
such that

C=\llbracket E,\tau,\lambda\theta\rrbracket.

Therefore

R-C=\llbracket E,\tau,(1-\lambda)\theta\rrbracket.

Since
H
is even and non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

H((1-\lambda(x))|\theta(x)|)\leq H(|\theta(x)|)\qquad\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{k}$-a.e. }x\in E.

Multiplying by
\lVert\tau(x)\rVert_{\sigma_{k}}
and integrating on
E
, we obtain

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R-C)\leq\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{k}}(R).

∎

Remark 5.3
.

1.

Setting

\mathcal{AC}_{1}\vcentcolon=\{R\in\mathbf{R}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\partial R=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-},\ R\text{ acyclic}\}\text{,}

by Proposition 3.8 of
[

1
-current

contains a cycle

such that

Hence, for every
R\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists a cyclic subcurrent

of

R-C\in\mathcal{AC}_{1}
and

\partial(R-C)=\partial R=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}.

Applying Lemma

\inf_{R\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R)=\inf_{R\in\mathcal{AC}_{1}}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R)\text{.}

(5.1)

2.

By Proposition 3.6 of
[
12

, for every

\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\mathbb{M}(\mu^{+})=\mathbb{M}(\mu^{-})
.

Since we will rely on the finiteness of

\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{H(y)}{y}=+\infty\text{.}

(5.2)

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
be a
1
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H
be a branching function, non-decreasing on

Proof.

If

we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{M}(R)&=\int_{E\cap\{\theta\neq 0\}}{\lvert\theta(x)\rvert}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)=\int_{E\cap\{\theta\neq 0\}}{\frac{\lvert\theta(x)\rvert}{H(\lvert\theta(x)\rvert)}H(\lvert\theta(x)\rvert)\frac{\lVert\tau(x)\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}}{\lVert\tau(x)\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\\
&\leq\left(\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lVert\tau\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\sup_{0<y\leq\mathbb{M}(\mu^{+})}\frac{y}{H(y)}\right)\int_{E\cap\{\theta\neq 0\}}{H(\theta(x))\lVert\tau(x)\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\\

where in the second inequality we used Remark

, the finiteness of the supremum

is upper semicontinuous on an interval

and

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

\sup_{i}\bigl(\mathbb{M}(T_{i})+\mathbb{M}(\partial T_{i})\bigr)<+\infty.

Then, there exists a current

such that, up to a non-relabeled subsequence,

\mathbb{F}\bigl((T_{i}-T)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0\qquad\text{for a.e. }R>0.

Proof.

Set

C:=\sup_{i}\bigl(\mathbb{M}(T_{i})+\mathbb{M}(\partial T_{i})\bigr)<+\infty.

By the compactness theorem for normal currents, up to a subsequence we may assume

T_{i}\rightharpoonup T\qquad\text{weakly-$*$ as currents},

for some
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, if we define the finite Radon measures

\mu_{i}:=\|T_{i}\|+\|\partial T_{i}\|,

then, up to a further subsequence, we may also assume

\mu_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu\qquad\text{weakly-* as Radon measures}

for some finite Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let

u(x):=|x|
. Since
u
is
1
-Lipschitz, for each
i
the slice

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{M}\bigl(\langle T_{i},u,R\rangle\bigr)\,dR\leq\mathbb{M}(T_{i})\leq C.

Moreover Fatou’s lemma yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{M}\bigl(\langle T_{i},u,R\rangle\bigr)\,dR\leq C,

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{M}\bigl(\langle T_{i},u,R\rangle\bigr)<+\infty\qquad\text{for a.e. }R>0.

Let

be the set of radii

\mu(\partial B_{R})=0

2.

\|T\|(\partial B_{R})=0
,

\liminf_{i}\mathbb{M}(\langle T_{i},u,R\rangle)<+\infty

, the set

T_{i}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\rightharpoonup T\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\qquad\text{weakly-$*$ as currents}.

(5.3)

For a.e.

Since flat convergence implies weak convergence and the whole sequence

T_{i}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}
converges weakly to
T\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}
, necessarily

S=T\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}.

Thus every subsequence of
(T_{i}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R})
admits a further subsequence converging in flat norm to the same limit
T\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}
. It follows that the whole sequence converges, that is,

\mathbb{F}\bigl((T_{i}-T)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0.

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
be a
1
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H
be a branching function satisfying condition (
5.2
) and non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Assume that

\inf\{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R):R\in\mathcal{C}\}<+\infty.

Then every minimizing sequence for problem (
ABOT
) is relatively compact with respect to the local flat topology in
\mathbf{N}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence for (
ABOT
). Since by assumption
\inf_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}<+\infty
, we can suppose without loss of generality that there exists
c>0
such that
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k})<c
for every
k
. As a consequence of (
5.1
) we can assume
R_{k}\in\mathcal{AC}_{1}
for every
k
.

By Lemma
5.4
, we have
\mathbb{M}(R_{k})\leq C\,c
for every
k
. Since
\partial R_{k}=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
such that

\mathbb{M}(R_{k})+\mathbb{M}(\partial R_{k})\leq\widetilde{C}\qquad\forall k.

We may therefore apply Lemma
5.5
to the sequence
(R_{k})_{k}
. It follows that, up to a subsequence, there exists
T\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\mathbb{F}\bigl((R_{k}-T)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0\qquad\text{for a.e. }R>0.

In particular,
(R_{k})_{k}
is relatively compact for the local flat topology.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
be a
1
-anisotropy in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H
be a branching function, non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, satisfying (
5.2
). Assume that

\inf\{\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R):R\in\mathcal{C}\}<+\infty.

Then any minimizing sequence
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of problem (
ABOT
) admits a subsequence converging in the local flat topology to an element of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence for problem (
ABOT
). By (
5.1
), we may assume without loss of generality that
R_{k}\in\mathcal{AC}_{1}
for every
k
.

By Lemma
5.6
, there exist
R_{\infty}\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a subsequence, not relabeled, such that

\mathbb{F}\bigl((R_{k}-R_{\infty})\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0\qquad\text{for a.e. }R>0.

In the proof of Lemma
5.5
one also obtains, up to subsequences, the weak-
*
convergence
R_{k}\rightharpoonup R_{\infty}
as currents in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; hence

\partial R_{\infty}=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}

by continuity of the boundary operator with respect to weak convergence.

Since
\{R_{k}\}_{k}
is minimizing, there exists
c>0
such that
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k})<c
for every
k
. Writing
R_{k}=\llbracket E_{k},\tau_{k},\theta_{k}\rrbracket
, we then have

\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lVert\tau\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}\int_{E_{k}}H(\theta_{k}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\leq\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k})<c\qquad\forall k\text{.}

(5.4)

Therefore

\mathbb{M}_{H}(R_{k})\vcentcolon=\int_{E_{k}}H(\theta_{k}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\leq C(\sigma_{1})c<+\infty\text{,}

uniformly in
k
. Fix now a radius
R>0
for which
\mathbb{F}\bigl((R_{k}-R_{\infty})\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0
. Since
\mathbb{M}_{H}(R_{k}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R})\leq\mathbb{M}_{H}(R_{k})
, Proposition 2.8 of
[
11
]
applied on
B_{R}
yields that
R_{\infty}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}
is rectifiable. Choosing an increasing sequence of such radii
R_{j}\to+\infty
, we conclude that
R_{\infty}
is itself rectifiable, namely
R_{\infty}\in\mathbf{R}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Hence
R_{\infty}\in\mathcal{C}
and the convergence is local flat by construction.
∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
1.8

Now we can conclude the proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
\{R_{k}\}_{k}
be a minimizing sequence for problem (
ABOT
). By (
5.1
), we may assume
R_{k}\in\mathcal{AC}_{1}
for every
k
. By Lemma
5.7
, up to a subsequence there exists
R_{\infty}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathbb{F}\bigl((R_{k}-R_{\infty})\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R}\bigr)\to 0\qquad\text{for a.e. }R>0.

Choose an increasing sequence of radii
R_{j}\to+\infty
such that the previous convergence holds for every
j
and, in addition,
\|R_{\infty}\|(\partial B_{R_{j}})=0
. For each fixed
j
, the currents
R_{k}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R_{j}}
and
R_{\infty}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R_{j}}
have compact support in
\overline{B_{R_{j}}}
, so the convergence is flat on that ball. If either
n=2
, or
n\geq 3
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sigma_{1}})
is hypermetric, Proposition
3.7
ensures that
\sigma_{1}
admits an integral geometric representation. Hence, by Theorem
1.7
, for every
j
we have

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{\infty}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R_{j}})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B_{R_{j}})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k}).

Letting
j\to+\infty
and using monotone convergence in the definition of
\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}
, we obtain

\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{\infty})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R_{k})=\inf_{R\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{M}_{H,\sigma_{1}}(R).

Therefore
R_{\infty}
is a minimizer. This proves both points of the theorem.
∎
