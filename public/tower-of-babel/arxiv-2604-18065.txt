Morita equivalence for quantum graphs Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 16D90, 46L07, 47L25, 81P45 Thanks: Key words and phrases: Quantum graphs, Morita equivalence, ternary rings of operators, operator systems, graph parameters.
Alexandros Chatzinikolaou, Gage Hoefer, Nikolaos Koutsonikos-Kouloumpis, Ioannis Apollon Paraskevas

Abstract
. We introduce an operator-algebraic framework for Morita equivalence of quantum graphs based on Δ \Delta -equivalence of operator systems introduced by Eleftherakis, Kakariadis and Todorov. Adopting the perspective of Weaver, we view quantum graphs as quantum relations, that is, operator systems endowed with a bimodule structure over the commutant of a von Neumann algebra. Within this framework, we show that two irreducibly acting quantum graphs are Morita equivalent if and only if they are both full pullbacks of a common quantum graph. This extends a result of Eleftherakis, Kakariadis and Todorov for graph operator systems to the quantum graph setting. In passing we construct a true-twin reduction analogue for an irreducibly acting quantum graph. We further characterise the case where we have simultaneous TRO-equivalence of the quantum graphs and their associated algebras, thus giving a second, stronger notion of Morita equivalence. In the special case of noncommutative graphs, corresponding to the zero-error quantum communication setting, the two notions coincide and we obtain a characterisation in terms of strong co-homomorphisms of noncommutative graphs. Finally, we show that connectivity, the independence number, Shannon capacity, quantum complexity and subcomplexity, Haemers bound, and the Lovász number are invariant under Morita equivalence.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Quantum graphs as quantum relations

Quantum graphs have attracted significant attention in recent years due to their deep connections with operator algebras, quantum information theory, quantum groups, and noncommutative geometry. They were first introduced by Duan, Severini and Winter
[
15
]
in the study of noncommutative confusability graphs of quantum channels, where they were modeled as operator systems
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
. Around the same time, Weaver
[
46
]
introduced the notion of quantum graphs as quantum relations, and subsequently Musto, Reutter and Verdon
[
34
]
developed a categorical framework via quantum adjacency operators. These approaches were later shown to be equivalent; see
[
12
,
44
]
.

Classically, a graph can be viewed as a relation
E\subseteq V\times V
that is symmetric (undirected) and reflexive (with loops at every vertex). The noncommutative analogue of a relation is given by a bimodule over a maximal abelian selfadjoint algebra (MASA). This correspondence originates in the work of Erdos, Katavolos and Shulman
[
24
]
and in the work of Shulman and Turowska
[
42
]
. More precisely, given standard measure spaces
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
, every subset
\kappa\subseteq X\times Y
determines a subspace
{\mathcal{M}}(\kappa)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}(X,\mu),L^{2}(Y,\nu))
consisting of operators supported on
\kappa
, and this yields an
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
-
L^{\infty}(Y,\nu)
-bimodule. Conversely, every such MASA-bimodule arises in this way (see
[
24
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
). In finite dimensions, this correspondence becomes particularly concrete: a relation
\kappa\subseteq[n]\times[n]
corresponds to the subspace

{\mathcal{M}}(\kappa)=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{i,j}:(i,j)\in\kappa\}\subseteq M_{n},

which is a
D_{n}
-bimodule, and every
D_{n}
-bimodule is of this form
[
39
, Proposition 1.1]
.

In this work, we adopt Weaver’s perspective
[
46
,
47
]
, where they defined a
quantum
relation on a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
to be a weak*-closed operator space
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that is an
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-bimodule. A
quantum graph
is then a symmetric (
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}^{*}
) and reflexive (
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
) quantum relation; equivalently, an operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that is a bimodule over
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. This framework recovers classical and noncommutative examples. When
{\mathcal{A}}=D_{n}
, every quantum graph arises from a classical graph
{\mathcal{G}}
as a graph operator system
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{i,j}:i\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}j\}.

When
{\mathcal{A}}=M_{n}
, a quantum graph is simply an operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
, corresponding to the noncommutative graphs associated with quantum channels as shown in
[
14
, Lemma 2]
. We note that in this work we will only deal with quantum graphs acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

1.2.
Morita equivalence

The study of Morita equivalence in the context of noncommutative analysis was introduced by Rieffel
[
40
]
in the 1970’s, who reformulated classical Morita theory for rings in the setting of C*- and W*-algebras. Rieffel’s approach proved to be highly effective, as many fundamental results of Morita theory
[
3
]
extend to these categories. Morita equivalence, realized as an equivalence between categories of C*-modules (and W*-modules respectively) is strictly weaker than
*
-isomorphisms and under suitable assumptions it coincides with stable isomorphisms
[
8
]
(i.e., the stabilizations obtained by tensoring with the compact operators are
*
-isomorphic).

The development of operator space theory paved the way for new applications of Morita theory. Blecher, Muhly and Paulsen
[
6
]
defined Morita equivalence of operator algebras, extending Morita equivalence of C*-algebras. Two operator algebras
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}
are said to be Morita equivalent when there exist appropriate bimodules
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}
over
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
such that

{\mathcal{A}}\cong{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{N}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{B}}\cong{\mathcal{N}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{M}}.

Following the same direction, Blecher and Kashyap
[
4
]
defined weak Morita equivalence for dual operator algebras.

During that time, significant attention was drawn to ternary rings of operators (TROs) that is, operator spaces
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
satisfying
{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
. Such structures arise naturally in the context of Morita equivalence of C*-algebras, as well as in the study of normalisers of reflexive algebras
[
27
]
.
Using these objects, Eleftherakis introduced TRO-equivalence
[
17
]
. A key advantage of TRO-equivalence is that it captures the selfadjoint structure, to the extent that it is present in an operator algebra. Building on this framework, Eleftherakis introduced
\Delta
-equivalence
[
16
]
, a Morita type equivalence that generalizes Rieffel’s theory, and which coincides with stable isomorphism in the case of dual operator algebras
[
22
]
, as well as for operator algebras
[
18
]
, under the additional assumption of the existence of a
\sigma
-unit.

Moreover, the nature of
\Delta
-equivalence allowed for further extensions of the theory to operator spaces and operator systems. This was first carried out by Eleftherakis, Paulsen, and Todorov
[
23
]
, who established its connection with stable isomorphism for dual operator spaces. In that work, it was shown that
\Delta
-equivalence is well suited to capturing the multiplicative structure carried by operator spaces, as reflected in their multiplier algebras
[
23
, Theorem 2.13]
.

Eleftherakis and Kakariadis
[
19
]
extended these results to operator spaces by proving that
\Delta
-equivalent operator spaces have TRO-equivalent TRO-envelopes
[
19
, Theorem 5.10]
, and in the unital case TRO-equivalent C*-envelopes
[
19
, Theorem 4.11]
. In
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
, it is shown that stable isomorphism implies
\Delta
-equivalence. Moreover, under additional natural assumptions such as (approximate) unitality
[
19
, Corollary 4.7]
or separability
[
19
, Corollary 4.8]
, the two notions coincide.
Subsequently, Eleftherakis, Kakariadis, and Todorov
[
20
]
introduced
\Delta
-equivalence for operator systems and showed that
\Delta
-equivalent operator systems admit TRO-equivalent representations inside their C*-envelopes. These representations further restrict to a TRO-equivalence between their corresponding multiplier algebras
[
20
, Proposition 3.9]
. Furthermore, they proved that, in this setting,
\Delta
-equivalence coincides with stable isomorphism.

In addition, Eleftherakis, Kakariadis, and Todorov
[
21
]
provide a characterisation of Morita equivalence in the operator system category via a tensorial decomposition, where the analytic structure is induced by symmetrisation. This result establishes an operator system analogue of the factorisation Morita theorem in other categories.
Finally, in recent work of the third-named author
[
30
]
,
\Delta
-equivalence and stable isomorphism are shown to be equivalent in the setting of dual operator systems, i.e., operator systems that are also dual operator spaces.

1.3.
Morita equivalence for quantum graphs

Our goal is to develop a notion of Morita equivalence for quantum graphs and to identify the structural features that are preserved under this equivalence. Links between Morita theory and quantum graph isomorphisms were established by Musto, Reutter and Verdon
[
35
]
. In particular, it was shown that graphs quantum isomorphic to a given graph are in bijective correspondence with Morita equivalence classes of certain Frobenius algebras in the category of finite-dimensional representations of the quantum automorphism algebra of that graph. Our motivation comes from
[
20
, Corollary 7.6]
, where Eleftherakis, Kakariadis and Todorov proved that for graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
;

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
;

(iii)

{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are full pullbacks of isomorphic graphs.

A graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is said to be a
pullback
of
{\mathcal{H}}
if there exists a map

f\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{H}})\quad\text{such that}\quad x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}y\iff f(x)\simeq_{{\mathcal{H}}}f(y).

Such a map is called a
pullback
. If moreover,
f
is surjective
it is called a
full
pullback.

We show that the property of being full pullbacks of isomorphic graphs corresponds precisely to being clique blow-ups of isomorphic graphs or equivalently having an isomorphism between the skeletons of the graphs (see Proposition
2.6
and Remark
2.7
). We define the
skeleton
of a graph
{\mathcal{G}}
to be the graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
obtained by identifying vertices that are true twins (vertices which have the same closed neighborhoods), and declaring two resulting vertices to be adjacent whenever the original vertices are adjacent. Any graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is a full pullback of its skeleton
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
. In addition, we show that the multiplier algebra of a graph operator system admits a canonical decomposition into a direct sum of matrix blocks where each block corresponds to a true-twin equivalence class (see Proposition
2.4
).

The main purpose of this work is to extend the result of
[
20
]
to arbitrary quantum graphs.
Our first objective is to quantize the notion of the pullback between graphs (see Definition
3.6
and Proposition
3.10
). The natural candidate for a
pullback between quantum graphs

({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
and
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
turns out to be a unital
*
-homomorphism
\theta\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
with a Kraus representation

\theta(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}xv_{i}\quad\text{such that}\quad v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i}{\mathcal{S}}v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}.

A
full pullback
is a pullback that is moreover a faithful
*
-homomorphism. In the case that such a
\theta
exists we say that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a (full) pullback of
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
.
The first condition can be thought of— in the classical case— as preserving adjacency-or-equality, while the second condition can be thought of as reflecting adjacency-or-equality. The fullness condition corresponds to a surjective pullback function in the classical situation. This notion naturally connects with homomorphisms of quantum graphs, as introduced by Stahlke
[
43
]
in the setting of trace-free noncommutative graphs, and with the recent work of
[
29
]
.

Pullbacks and pushforwards were defined in
[
46
]
in the more general context of quantum relations. Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, and let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital completely positive map with a Kraus representation

\varphi(b)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}bv_{i}\text{ for all }b\in{\mathcal{B}}.

The
pushforward of
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
along
\varphi
, denoted by

{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
, is the
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
-operator bimodule generated by

\{v_{i}sv_{j}^{*}:s\in{\mathcal{S}}\text{ and }i,j=1,\dots,r\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K),

and the
pullback of
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
along
\varphi
, denoted by

{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
, is the
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-operator bimodule generated by

\{v_{i}^{*}tv_{j}:t\in{\mathcal{T}}\text{ and }i,j=1,\dots,r\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H).

In
[
12
]
and in
[
46
]
it is proved that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
are independent of the Kraus representation.
In Theorem
3.9
we prove that if
\theta
is a
*
-homomorphism, then
\theta
is a pullback if and only if
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
, and moreover
\theta
is a full pullback if and only if

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
and
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\theta}
.

Our second objective is to construct the
skeleton of an irreducibly acting quantum graph
as a noncommutative analogue of the skeleton of a graph. We isolate this construction in Subsection
3.2
as it may be of independent interest, in the hope of contributing to the broader development of noncommutative combinatorics.
This construction retrieves the true-twin reduction of a classical graph (see Proposition
3.14
). In particular, the decomposition of the multiplier algebra isolates multiplicity spaces corresponding to such “true-twin vertices”, and the operator system factors accordingly. The resulting quantum graph
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
plays the role of the skeleton of
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
, while the original quantum graph is recovered from it via a canonical amplification along these multiplicity spaces. In particular, as in the classical case
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a full pullback of
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
.

Theorem A.
(
[
20
, Theorem 7.5]
, Theorem
3.15
)

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting irreducibly on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{T}}
.

(iii)

There exists a quantum graph
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
acting on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space such that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
are full pullbacks of
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
.

(iv)

There exists a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L)
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
L
and unital
*
-homomorphisms
\theta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
and
\varrho\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
such that

{\mathcal{T}}^{\rightarrow\varrho}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\theta},\quad{\mathcal{T}}=({\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\theta})^{\leftarrow\varrho}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{S}}=({\mathcal{T}}^{\rightarrow\varrho})^{\leftarrow\theta}.

The irreducibly acting assumption allows us to view the C*-envelope of
{\mathcal{S}}
, and hence also the multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
, concretely acting on the same Hilbert space as
{\mathcal{S}}
, something that plays a central part in our proof. The equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)] was shown in
[
20
, Theorem 7.5]
.
The equivalence [(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)] follows from Theorem
3.9
where we connect full pullback maps in our sense and the pushforwards and pullbacks of
[
46
]
. The implication [(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)] relies on Proposition
2.1
, and the implication [(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)] relies on the skeleton of a quantum graph.

We then proceed to investigate a second, stronger notion of Morita equivalence for quantum graphs via simultaneous TRO-equivalence of the operator systems and their associated algebras. Let
H,K
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. For C*-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
and a unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
with Kraus operators
v_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H,K),i\in[r]
, we define

{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}:=\operatorname{span}\{{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}v_{i}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}:i\in[r]\}.

In
[
29
, Proposition 6.1]
it is proved that
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}
does not depend on the choice of Kraus operators
v_{i}
for
i\in[r]
.

Theorem B.
(Theorem
3.19
)

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces

H
and
K
, respectively. The following are equivalent:

(i)

There is a non-degenerate TRO
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
which is also a
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
-
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-bimodule implementing the TRO-equivalences

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{B}}.

(ii)

There exists a faithful unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
such that

{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},\quad{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}},\quad{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}.

In the special case of graph operator systems, this stronger notion of Morita equivalence gives graph isomorphism (see Remark
3.25
). For noncommutative graphs the two notions of Morita equivalence coincide and the equivalence admits a particularly concrete formulation in terms of completely positive maps. In this setting, TRO-equivalence can be characterised entirely through pullback and pushforward operations associated to a single map.

Theorem C.
(Corollary
3.21
)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq M_{m}
be noncommutative graphs.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
.

(ii)

There exists a unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon M_{m}\to M_{n}
such that

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}.

A main topic in the study of noncommutative graphs is how to extend classical graph parameters to the operator-algebraic setting, see for example
[
7
,
15
,
25
,
31
]
. Several basic invariants, including the independence number, clique number, chromatic number, and different capacity-type quantities, have been extended to noncommutative graphs. These noncommutative versions stay closely related to the classical ones while also allowing truly quantum features such as entanglement and nonlocality. It is therefore important to understand how these parameters behave under natural equivalence relations, especially under Morita equivalence, in order to build a strong and flexible theory of quantum graphs.

In Remark
4.2
we show that the chromatic number and the clique number of a noncommutative graph are not invariant under TRO-equivalence.
However, Theorem C makes it possible to compare several noncommutative graph parameters directly under TRO-equivalence.

Theorem D.
(Theorem
4.3
)

The parameters
\alpha,c_{0},\vartheta,\widehat{\vartheta},{\mathfrak{H}},\beta
and
\gamma
are invariant under TRO-equivalence of noncommutative graphs. In particular, they are invariant under
\Delta
-equivalence of irreducibly acting noncommutative graphs.

1.4.
Contents

The structure of the manuscript is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the necessary background on operator systems, noncommutative graphs, and Morita-type equivalences, including
\Delta
-equivalence and TRO-equivalence. We also revisit
\Delta
-equivalence of graph operator systems in terms of the true-twin equivalence relation and clique blow-ups. In Section
3
, we introduce our framework for quantum graphs, establish the basic properties of pullback and pushforward constructions. The main part of the section is devoted to the development of Morita type-equivalences for quantum graphs, culminating in Theorem
3.15
and Theorem
3.19
, which characterises these equivalences in several ways. We also present a true-twin reduction construction of irreducibly acting quantum graphs. This construction plays a central role in the proof Theorem
3.15
and it may be interesting in its own merit. We specialize to noncommutative graphs and obtain a concrete description in terms of completely positive maps. Finally, in Section
4
, we study invariance results for various noncommutative graph parameters under Morita equivalence.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Operator systems and TROs

For an excellent introduction to the theory of operator spaces and operator systems, the reader is addressed to
[
5
,
37
]
.
In this work, all inner products are assumed to be linear on the second variable. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we write
[n]
for the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. We write
M_{n}
for the
n\times n
matrices with complex entries and
D_{n}
for the diagonals. Furthermore, we use
\varepsilon_{ij}
to denote the
(i,j)
-th matrix unit in
M_{n}
, for
i,j\in[n]
. We use
\perp
to denote orthogonality with respect to the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product on
M_{n}
defined by

\left\langle x,y\right\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(x^{*}y)\text{ for all }x,y\in M_{n}.

Let
H
and
K
be Hilbert spaces.
For
\xi,\eta
in
H
, the rank one projection
\xi\eta^{*}\colon H\to H
acts as
\xi\eta^{*}(\zeta)=\left\langle\eta,\zeta\right\rangle\xi
. We write
{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
for the space of all bounded linear operators from
H
into
K
, and set
{\mathcal{B}}(H)={\mathcal{B}}(H,H)
. We write
I_{H}
or just
I
for the identity operator on
H
, when the context is clear. If
{\mathcal{X}}
is a set of operators in
{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
we set

[{\mathcal{X}}H]:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x\xi:\;x\in{\mathcal{X}},\xi\in H\}.

For a set
{\mathcal{X}}
of operators inside
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
we will write
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
for their commutant, i.e.,

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}:=\{a\in{\mathcal{B}}(H):ab=ba\text{ for all }b\in{\mathcal{X}}\}.

An
operator space

{\mathcal{X}}
is a norm-closed subspace of
{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
. An
operator system
is an operator space
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with
I_{H}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}={\mathcal{S}}
.
An operator space
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
is called a
TRO
if it satisfies

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}.

It follows directly from the definition that for a TRO
{\mathcal{M}}
the norm-closed subspaces
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
are C*-algebras. We say that an operator space
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
is
non-degenerate
(resp.
acts non-degenerately
) if
I_{H}\in[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}]
and
I_{K}\in[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}]
(resp.
[{\mathcal{X}}H]=K
and
[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}K]=H
).

Let
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}}
be operator spaces. For a linear map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-th
amplification

\phi^{(n)}\colon M_{n}({\mathcal{X}})\to M_{n}({\mathcal{Y}})
is defined by

\phi^{(n)}((x_{ij})_{i,j}):=(\phi(x_{ij}))_{i,j}.

We say that
\phi
is
completely bounded
if

\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\phi^{(n)}\|<\infty.

It is called
completely contractive
if
\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\leq 1
, and
completely isometric
if
\phi^{(n)}
is an isometry for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now let
{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{T}}
be operator systems.
We say that
\phi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is
unital
if
\phi(1_{\mathcal{S}})=1_{\mathcal{T}}
, and
positive
if
\phi({\mathcal{S}}_{+})\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{+}
. The map
\phi
is called
completely positive
if each
\phi^{(n)}
is positive for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
\phi
is a
complete order isomorphism
if it is a bijection whose inverse is completely positive, and a
complete order embedding
if it is a complete order isomorphism onto its range.

For an operator system
{\mathcal{S}}
, a
C*-envelope
of
{\mathcal{S}}
is a pair
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}),\iota)
consisting of a C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})
and a unital completely isometric map

\iota\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})

such that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\iota({\mathcal{S}}))=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})
and the following universal property holds:
whenever
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a unital complete isometry, there exists a unique surjective
*
-homomorphism

\pi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\varphi({\mathcal{S}}))\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})\text{ such that }\pi\circ\varphi=\iota.

Such a pair exists, and it is unique up to
*
-isomorphism. The existence of the C*-envelope of an operator system was established by Hamana
[
26
]
through the existence of the injective envelope.
An independent proof for unital operator algebras was established by Dritschel and McCullough
[
13
]
through the existence of maximal dilations. In particular, they show that the C*-envelope is the C*-algebra generated by a unital completely isometric maximal representation.
A simplified proof by Arveson
[
1
]
yields the same result for operator systems.

An operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)

acts irreducibly
on
H
if there is no non-trivial closed subspace
L\subseteq H

reducing
{\mathcal{S}}
such that the restriction map

{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(L);s\mapsto s|_{L}

is completely isometric. For an irreducibly acting operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
there exist positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}

with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n
such that

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M_{n_{i}}.

This is shown by Arveson in
[
2
]
. For a simplified proof see also
[
20
, Proposition 7.1]
.

We recall the following facts and definitions from
[
20
]
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be operator systems.
We say that
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are
TRO-equivalent (via
{\mathcal{M}}
)
, and write

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}},

if there exists a non-degenerate TRO
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
such that

{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}.

It is clear that in this case
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]
-bimodule and
{\mathcal{T}}
is an
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]
-bimodule.
Our operator systems are assumed to be norm-closed and therefore as shown in
[
20
, Proposition 3.3]
if
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are TRO-equivalent via
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
, then the non-degeneracy of
{\mathcal{M}}
implies

[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}]={\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]={\mathcal{T}}.

We say that two operator systems
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are called
\Delta
-equivalent
,
and write
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{T}}
if there exist Hilbert spaces
H
and
K

and unital complete order embeddings

\varphi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)

such that

\varphi({\mathcal{S}})\sim_{\rm TRO}\psi({\mathcal{T}}).

For background on multiplier algebras of operator spaces, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
.
A
left multiplier
of an operator space
{\mathcal{X}}
is a map
u\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
for which there exist a Hilbert space
H
, a complete isometry
i\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and an operator
c\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
such that

i(u(b))=ci(b)\text{ for all }b\in{\mathcal{X}}.

The
multiplier norm
of
u
is defined as the
\inf{\|c\|}
over all such triples
(i,c,H)
, and the set of all left multipliers of
{\mathcal{X}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}})
. Analogous definitions hold for right multipliers
{\mathcal{M}}_{r}({\mathcal{X}})
.

The
left adjointable multiplier algebra
of
{\mathcal{X}}
is

{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}}):={\mathcal{M}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}})\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}})^{*},

and it consists of all
u\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
such that there exists a triple
(i,c,H)
as before with

i(u(b))=ci(b)\text{ for all }b\in{\mathcal{X}}\quad\text{and}\quad c^{*}i({\mathcal{X}})\subseteq i({\mathcal{X}}).

In case that
{\mathcal{S}}
is an operator system, we have

{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{S}})\cong\{a\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}):a{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\},

and therefore

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{S}})

\displaystyle\cong\{a\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}):a{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},a^{*}{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\}

\displaystyle=\{a,b\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}):a{\mathcal{S}}b\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\}

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}):{\mathcal{S}}a\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}a^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{r}({\mathcal{S}}).

In particular, both
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{S}})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{r}({\mathcal{S}})
can be viewed as an operator subsystem of
{\mathcal{S}}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
, and carrying the structure of a C*-subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})
.

In the sequel we will use this finite-dimensional modification of
[
20
, Proposition 3.4]
. Although the proof is similar we include the details for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be two operator systems acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Suppose that there exists a non-degenerately acting operator space
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
such that

{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}.

Set
{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}})
. Then
{\mathcal{M}}:=[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}]
is a non-degenerate TRO implementing
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.
.

Since
{\mathcal{X}}
acts non-degenerately, we have
[{\mathcal{X}}H]=K
and
[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}K]=H
.
Hence the C*-algebras
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*})
act non-degenerately,
and by finite-dimensionality they must be unital. We first note that

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}]={\mathcal{M}},

so
{\mathcal{M}}
is a TRO.

We show that
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]={\mathcal{A}}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*})
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is unital, we have
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
,
and hence
[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]
.
As
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]
is a C*-algebra, it follows that

{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}})\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}].

Conversely, for
x,y\in{\mathcal{X}}
and
a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
we have

(xa)^{*}(yb)=a^{*}x^{*}yb\in{\mathcal{A}},

since
x^{*}y\in[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
.
Thus
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
, and so
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]={\mathcal{A}}
.
A similar argument gives
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*})
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}
is non-degenerate.

Now we show that
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}
-bimodule. Set
{\mathcal{P}}:=[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}]
.
Since
I_{K}\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
, we obtain

{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
.
Moreover,

{\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}){\mathcal{X}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}.

By induction,
{\mathcal{P}}^{n}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}

for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
we conclude that

{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},

and by unitality of
{\mathcal{A}}
we also have
{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
.
Finally, we have

{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},

and similarly

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}.

Thus
{\mathcal{M}}
is a non-degenerate TRO implementing

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

The next result follows from the arguments in the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 3.9]
; we include a proof as it plays a central role in the sequel— see also
[
19
, Theorem 5.10]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be two operator systems acting irreducibly on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. If
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
then we can pick a non-degenerate TRO
{\mathcal{N}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
implementing
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
such that
[{\mathcal{N}}^{*}{\mathcal{N}}]={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
[{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}]={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
be a non-degenerate TRO such that

[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}]={\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]={\mathcal{T}}.

Note that by the TRO-property
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are bimodules over
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]
and
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]
, respectively. As both
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are irreducibly acting we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{T}})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K).

Hence by definition of the multiplier algebra we obtain

[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}.

Fix
t\in{\mathcal{T}}
. Then
t=\sum_{i}c_{i}^{\prime}s_{i}c_{i}^{*}
for
c_{i},c_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
s_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}
. For any
d,d^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
we have

d^{\prime}ad^{*}t=\sum_{i}d^{\prime}ad^{*}c_{i}^{\prime}s_{i}c_{i}^{*}\in[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{T}},

and thus
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}
by the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}
. Similarly, we have
[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
. In particular, we have equalities since

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}.

Set
{\mathcal{N}}:=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}]
. Then

[{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}]=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}]=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}]=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}]={\mathcal{T}},

and similarly
[{\mathcal{N}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{N}}]={\mathcal{S}}
. Moreover,

[{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}]=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}]={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{N}}^{*}{\mathcal{N}}]=[{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}]={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}.

Thus
{\mathcal{N}}
is the required non-degenerate TRO implementing a TRO-equivalence between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

We will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{\beta}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
be a TRO such that

[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]=M_{\alpha}\otimes I_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]=M_{\beta}\otimes I_{n}.

Then we have
{\mathcal{M}}=M_{\beta,\alpha}\otimes u
for some unitary
u\in M_{n}
.

Proof.
.

Set
{\mathcal{A}}:=[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}]
and
{\mathcal{B}}:=[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]
. By
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a
*
-isomorphism
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
such that

(2.1)

{\mathcal{M}}=\{x\in M_{\beta,\alpha}\otimes M_{n}:xa^{\prime}=\pi(a^{\prime})x\text{ for all }a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\}.

Observe that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}=I_{\alpha}\otimes M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=I_{\beta}\otimes M_{n}
and thus
\pi
induces a
*
-automorphism of
M_{n}
. In particular, we have

\pi(I_{\alpha}\otimes a)=I_{\beta}\otimes(uau^{*})\text{ for all }a\in M_{n},

for some unitary
u\in M_{n}
.

We will prove that
{\mathcal{M}}=M_{\beta,\alpha}\otimes u
. Note that the inclusion
M_{\beta,\alpha}\otimes u\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
is immediate. Hence is remains to show the reverse inclusion.
To that end, fix
x=\sum_{i,j}\varepsilon_{ij}\otimes x_{ij}\in{\mathcal{M}}
. Then (
2.1
) implies that

x(I_{\alpha}\otimes a)=\pi(I_{\alpha}\otimes a)x=(I_{\beta}\otimes uau^{*})x\text{ for all }a\in M_{n}.

By expanding the above equality we obtain

\sum_{i,j}\varepsilon_{ij}\otimes(x_{ij}a)=\sum_{i,j}\varepsilon_{ij}\otimes(uau^{*}x_{ij})\text{ for all }a\in M_{n},

which implies that

x_{ij}a=uau^{*}x_{ij}\text{ for all }i,j\text{ and }a\in M_{n}.

We thus obtain that
u^{*}x_{ij}\in M_{n}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}I_{n}
for all
i,j
. We conclude that
x\in M_{\beta,\alpha}\otimes u
and the proof is complete.
∎

2.2.
Noncommutative graphs and graph operator systems

Let
H
and
K
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. A
quantum channel
is a map
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}(K)
which is completely positive and trace-preserving. By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
we have that for a quantum channel
\Phi
there exist operators
v_{i}\colon H\rightarrow K
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(c)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}cv_{i}^{*},\text{ for all }c\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}v_{i}=I_{H}.

The equation (
2.2
) is called a
Kraus representation of
\Phi
, and the operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
are the
Kraus operators
of the representation. We note that the second equality in (
2.2
) is equivalent with the trace-preservation of the map
\Phi
. The subspace

{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}:=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}^{*}v_{j}:\;i,j\in[r]\}

is called the
noncommutative graph of

\Phi
; by trace-preservation, this is an operator system in
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
We also set

{\mathcal{X}}_{\Phi}:=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}:\;i\in[r]\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)

as the
Kraus space for

\Phi
. It can be shown (see
[
45
, Corollary 2.23]
) that both
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{\Phi}
are independent of the choice of the Kraus representation for
\Phi
.
In fact, by similar arguments, for a general (not necessarily completely positive) trace-preserving linear map
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}(K)
there exist operators
v_{i},w_{i}:H\rightarrow K
for
i=1,\ldots,r
such that

\Phi(c)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}cw_{i}^{*}\text{ for all }c\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)\text{ and }\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}w_{i}^{*}v_{i}=I_{H}.

For such a linear map, we write

\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}_{\Phi}:=\operatorname{span}\{w_{i}^{*}v_{j}:\;i,j\in[r]\}.

The distinction between
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}_{\Phi}
will be made clear in context.
As in the discussion directly preceding
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
, we set

{\mathfrak{C}}({\mathcal{S}}):=\{\Phi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(H)\rightarrow M_{k}:\text{ where }\Phi\text{ is a quantum channel with }{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Finally, for a quantum channel
\Phi
as above we let
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{B}(K)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the dual of
\Phi
; this is a unital, completely positive map which in finite-dimensions has Kraus representation

\Phi^{*}(d)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}dv_{i},\text{ for all }d\in\mathcal{B}(K),

when
\Phi
has a Kraus representation as in (
2.2
).

We now give some connections with classical graph theory. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V({\mathcal{G}}),E({\mathcal{G}}))
be a graph. We say a graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is
simple
if there are no loops at any vertices in
{\mathcal{G}}
. For
x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}})
, we write
x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}y
whenever
x\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}}y
or
x=y
. Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. When convenient, we implicitly identify the vertex set
V({\mathcal{G}})
with
[n]
where
|V({\mathcal{G}})|=n
.
The
graph operator system
of
{\mathcal{G}}
is defined as

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}:=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{xy}:x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}y\}\subseteq M_{n}.

For a graph
{\mathcal{G}}
and a vertex
x\in V({\mathcal{G}})
we set

{\mathcal{N}}(x):=\{y\in V({\mathcal{G}}):x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}y\},

and call this set the
closed neighborhood
of
x
.
Two vertices
x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}})
are called
true twins
if
{\mathcal{N}}(x)={\mathcal{N}}(y)
.
We write
x\approx y
for two vertices that are true twins, and it follows that
\approx
defines an equivalence relation on
V({\mathcal{G}})
. We denote by
[x]
the true-twin equivalence class of
x
. The
skeleton
of
{\mathcal{G}}
is the graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
obtained by replacing the vertices of
{\mathcal{G}}
with their true-twin equivalence classes and where
[x]
is adjacent to
[y]
in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
whenever
x
is adjacent to
y
in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a graph on
n
vertices. In
[
36
, Theorem 3.2]
it is proven that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}})
is the C*-algebra generated by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}
inside
M_{n}
. In particular, the multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
can be realised inside
M_{n}
as well.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then

(2.3)

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}M_{n_{j}}\text{ for }n_{j},k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n,

where each
M_{n_{j}}
corresponds to the true-twin equivalence class
C_{j}
of size
n_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,k
.

Moreover, any irreducibly acting operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
whose multiplier algebra admits a decomposition as in (
2.3
) is necessarily a graph operator system.

Proof.
.

We begin by proving that

\varepsilon_{xy}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\approx y.

Fix
x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}})
such that
x\approx y
and let
\varepsilon_{wz}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. By definition of
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, we have
z\in{\mathcal{N}}(w)
.
To show that
\varepsilon_{xy}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
, it suffices to verify that

\varepsilon_{xy}\varepsilon_{wz}
and
\varepsilon_{wz}\varepsilon_{xy}
are both in
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
.
We prove only that
\varepsilon_{xy}\varepsilon_{wz}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, since the argument to show that
\varepsilon_{wz}\varepsilon_{xy}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
is analogous.
Indeed, it follows that

\varepsilon_{xy}\varepsilon_{wz}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{xz},&\text{if }y=w,\\
0,&\text{if }y\neq w,\end{cases}

so it suffices to consider the case
y=w
.
In this case, we obtain
z\in{\mathcal{N}}(w)\equiv{\mathcal{N}}(y)={\mathcal{N}}(x)
, and hence

\varepsilon_{xz}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, as required.

Conversely, suppose that
\varepsilon_{xy}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}}
for some
x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}})
,
and let
w\in{\mathcal{N}}(y)
.
Then
\varepsilon_{yw}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, and therefore

\varepsilon_{xw}=\varepsilon_{xy}\varepsilon_{yw}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}.

This implies that
w\in{\mathcal{N}}(x)
, showing that
{\mathcal{N}}(y)\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}(x)
.
By symmetry, we conclude that
{\mathcal{N}}(x)={\mathcal{N}}(y)
, and thus
x\approx y
.

Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
be the true-twin equivalence classes of
{\mathcal{G}}
, and set

p_{j}:=\sum_{x\in C_{j}}\varepsilon_{xx}\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,k.

By definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
we have
\varepsilon_{xx}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
for every
x\in V({\mathcal{G}})
. In particular, we have that each
p_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
, and moreover these projections are mutually orthogonal and satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}=I
.
From the previous step, we have

\varepsilon_{xy}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}
if and only if
x\approx y
,
so

p_{j}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}p_{j}=\mathrm{span}\{\varepsilon_{xy}:x,y\in C_{j}\}.

The family
\{\varepsilon_{xy}:x,y\in C_{j}\}
forms a full system of
matrix units on
\ell^{2}(C_{j})
, hence

p_{j}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}p_{j}\cong M_{n_{j}},\qquad n_{j}=|C_{j}|.

If
j\neq\ell
, then
x\not\approx y
for
x\in C_{j}
,
y\in C_{\ell}
,
so
p_{j}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}p_{\ell}=\{0\}
. Therefore,

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}}p_{j}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}M_{n_{j}},

as required.

Now let

be an irreducibly acting operator system and assume
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{k}M_{n_{j}}
is a block-diagonal algebra in the standard basis.
Since

we have that

contains all diagonal matrix units

for some graph

on

∎

While the following result is standard, we include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a graph on
n
vertices. The graph operator system

is irreducibly acting.

Proof.
.

There exist

and

such that

and

see for example the proof of

. Suppose that

is reducing for

(and hence also for

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V({\mathcal{G}}),E({\mathcal{G}}))
and
{\mathcal{H}}=(V({\mathcal{H}}),E({\mathcal{H}}))
be graphs. A
graph homomorphism
from
{\mathcal{G}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
is a map

satisfying

x\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}}y\quad\Rightarrow\quad f(x)\sim_{{\mathcal{H}}}f(y)\text{ for all }x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}}).

If moreover

is bijective and
f^{-1}
is a graph homomorphism

will be called
graph isomorphism
and we will say that the graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are

.

A

is a map
f\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{H}})
satisfying

x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}y\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f(x)\simeq_{{\mathcal{H}}}f(y)\text{ for all }x,y\in V({\mathcal{G}}).

If moreover
f
is surjective we say that
f
is a
full
pullback. In this case we say that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a (full) pullback of
{\mathcal{H}}
.

In

, Corollary 7.6]
it is shown that for graphs

and
{\mathcal{H}}
, the statements
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, and

and
{\mathcal{H}}
being full pullbacks of isomorphic graphs are all equivalent. In the following proposition we prove that these statements are also equivalent to
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
and

being isomorphic.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be graphs. The following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
are isomorphic.

(ii)

{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are full pullbacks of isomorphic graphs.

Proof.
.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]. Define

f_{\mathcal{G}}\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{G}}^{\circ})\text{ by }f_{\mathcal{G}}(x)=[x]\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{H}}\colon V({\mathcal{H}})\to V({\mathcal{H}}^{\circ})\text{ by }f_{\mathcal{H}}(z)=[z].

Note that by definition of the skeletons

x\simeq_{\mathcal{G}}y\Leftrightarrow f_{\mathcal{G}}(x)\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}}f_{\mathcal{G}}(y)\quad\text{and}\quad w\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}z\Leftrightarrow f_{\mathcal{H}}(w)\simeq_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}}f_{\mathcal{G}}(z),

and therefore
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are full pullbacks of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
, respectively.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)]. Suppose that
{\mathcal{K}}
is a graph and let

be a full pullback map. Consider the skeleton
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
; we will show that

induces a graph isomorphism from
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
to
{\mathcal{K}}^{\circ}
.

First of all, define the map

by

. To see that

is well-defined and injective note that
[x]\neq[y]
if and only if there exists a

such that
x\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}z
while
y\not\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}z
(or vice versa).
As

such that

f(x)\simeq_{{\mathcal{K}}}w
while
f(y)\not\simeq_{{\mathcal{K}}}w
(or vice versa), which is equivalent to
[f(x)]\neq[f(y)]
.
Moreover,

is clearly surjective, as

is surjective.

Finally, if

then
x\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}}y
and there exists a

such that
x\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}}z
but
z\nsim_{{\mathcal{G}}}y
(or vice versa). This means that
f(x)\simeq_{{\mathcal{K}}}f(z)
and
f(z)\not\simeq_{{\mathcal{K}}}f(y)
which implies

[x]\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}}[y]
if and only if
[f(x)]\sim_{{\mathcal{K}}^{\circ}}[f(y)]

is a graph isomorphism. Hence if

and

are full pullback maps we conclude that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}\cong{\mathcal{K}}^{\circ}\cong{\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
, as required.
∎

3.
Morita equivalence for quantum graphs

In this section, we pursue the following main objectives. First, we introduce the notions of a co-homomorphism and an associated pullback map in the setting of quantum graphs, and examine their relationship to the pullback constructions defined in
[
47
]
and
[
20
]
. Second, we construct the skeleton of an irreducibly acting quantum graph and show that it extends the notion of a skeleton of a graph. Third,
we extend
[
20
, Corollary 7.6]
from the classical graph setting to the broader framework of irreducibly acting quantum graphs. Finally, we characterise a simultaneous TRO-equivalence of quantum graphs and their associated algebras and apply it in the setting of noncommutative graphs.

3.1.
Co-homomorphisms and pullbacks of quantum graphs

We begin by recalling the notion of a quantum graph, as introduced in
[
46
]
. Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
a von Neumann algebra, i.e.,
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. A
quantum graph
on
{\mathcal{A}}
is an operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
that is a bimodule over
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, that is,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
. For brevity, if
{\mathcal{S}}
,
{\mathcal{A}}
and
H
are as above we will often say that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a quantum graph acting on
H
. We may assume without loss of generality that for a quantum graph
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is unital and
1_{\mathcal{S}}=1_{\mathcal{A}}=I_{H}
. We will say that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is
acting irreducibly
on
H
if
{\mathcal{S}}
is acting irreducibly on
H
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then the corresponding graph operator system
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\subseteq M_{n}
is a quantum graph on
{\mathcal{B}}=D_{n}
. In particular, by
[
39
, Proposition 1.1]
we have that an operator subsystem
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
that is a bimodule over
D_{n}
, gives rise to a classical graph in the sense that there exists a graph
{\mathcal{G}}
on
n
vertices such that
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
.

Example 3.2
.

Any operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
H
is trivially a bimodule over
\mathbb{C}I_{H}
, and hence it defines a quantum graph on
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{B}}(H)
. Such quantum graphs will be called
noncommutative graphs
. In
[
14
, Lemma 2]
it is shown that every operator system
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
arises as the noncommutative graph of a quantum channel, so this terminology is consistent.

We recall the definitions of pushforwards and pullbacks of quantum graphs, as given in
[
47
]
. Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, respectively.
Let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital completely positive map with Kraus form

\varphi(b)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}bv_{i},\qquad v_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H,K).

(i)

The
pushforward of
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
along
\varphi
, denoted by

{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
, is the
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
-operator bimodule generated by

\{v_{i}sv_{j}^{*}:s\in{\mathcal{S}}\text{ and }i,j=1,\dots,r\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K).

(ii)

The
pullback of
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
along
\varphi
, denoted by

{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
, is the
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-operator bimodule generated by

\{v_{i}^{*}tv_{j}:t\in{\mathcal{T}}\text{ and }i,j=1,\dots,r\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H).

Remark 3.3
.

It is shown in
[
47
]
(see also
[
12
]
) that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
are independent of the Kraus representation of
\varphi
. Moreover, in
[
12
, Theorem 7.5]
it is shown that the weak*-closure of the linear span of the set

\{v_{i}^{*}tv_{j}:t\in{\mathcal{T}}\text{ and }i,j\in[r]\}

is automatically an
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-bimodule and hence in our finite-dimensional setting we have

{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}^{*}tv_{j}:t\in{\mathcal{T}}\text{ and }i,j=1,\dots,r\}.

The following definition of a co-homomorphism is the quantum graph analogue of the one given in
[

Definition 3.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces

and

, respectively.
A

(from

to

\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}

with Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}

\varphi(b)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}bv_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\text{ for all }i,j=1,\dots,r.

Remark 3.5
.

The notion of a co-homomorphism

coincides with that of a noncommutative graph homomorphism as introduced by Stahlke in
[

. In
[

the theory is formulated in terms of orthogonal complements of operator systems rather than the operator systems themselves. Since our approach focuses on operator systems, it is more natural to work directly with the present formulation. In the classical setting, this correspondence recovers the usual notion of graph homomorphism. Indeed one may verify that by
[

, for graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
and

if and only if there exists a co-homomorphism from

where by

and

, respectively.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces

and
K
, respectively, and let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be a completely positive map. We may assume that
\varphi
admits some Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
(for example, use Arveson’s Extension Theorem to extend

\varphi(b)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}bv_{i}\text{ for all }b\in{\mathcal{B}}.

Define

that is, the

.
By

for

.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, respectively.

(i)

A
strong co-homomorphism
from
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
to
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
with Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
such that

v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i}\,{\mathcal{S}}\,v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}\text{ for all }i,j=1,\dots,r.

(ii)

A
pullback
from
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
to
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a unital
*
-homomorphism
\theta:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
that is a strong co-homomorphism.

If in addition,
\varphi
is faithful (resp.
\theta
)
we say that
\varphi
is a
full

\theta
is a full pullback).
If
\theta
is a (full) pullback we say that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})

Clearly for a unital completely positive map

\text{pullback}\Rightarrow\text{ strong co-homomorphism}\Rightarrow\text{co-homomorphism}.

We note that if
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}

to
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
, then using the bimodule structures of

and

{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}.

On the other hand, if

and
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
then by

we may pick Kraus operators

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, respectively. Let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be unital completely positive with Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
that span
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}
and set
v=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}
. Then, the following hold:

(i)

The
{\mathcal{B}}
-support projection
p_{{\mathcal{B}}}(v)
is the projection onto
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]
. In particular,

[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K\quad\text{if and only if}\quad p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=I_{K};

(ii)

The map
\varphi
is faithful if and only if
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K
;

Proof.
.

(i) We need to show that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=q
where
q
is the projection on
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]
. We start by showing that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)\leq q
. It is evident that
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]
is invariant for
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
and hence it is reducing. Consequently, we obtain
q\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathcal{B}}
. It remains to show that
\operatorname{ran}(v)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(q)
and then by the definition of
p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)
we will get
p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)\leq q
. But this follows from the fact that
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]\supseteq\operatorname{ran}(v)
as both
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
are unital. We now show that
q\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)
. Note that we have

(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))(\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}v_{i}^{*})(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))=0,

and therefore we obtain that

(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))v_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\in[r],

and

p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)v_{i}=v_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[r].

In particular,
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))
as required.

(ii) Suppose that
\varphi
is faithful. We have

(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))v_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\in[r]

and thus

\varphi((I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))^{*}(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)))=\varphi((I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)))=\sum_{i}v_{i}^{*}(I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v))v_{i}=0.

Faithfulness of
\varphi
implies that
I_{K}-p_{\mathcal{B}}(v)=0
.

Conversely, assume that
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K
. For
x^{*}x\in\ker\varphi
we have

0=\varphi(x^{*}x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}^{*}x^{*}xv_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(xv_{i})^{*}(xv_{i}),

which implies that
xv_{i}=0
for all
i\in[r]
. Consequently
xv_{i}\eta=0
for all
i\in[r]
and all
\eta\in H
. Since
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K
we obtain that
x\xi=0
for all
\xi\in K
implying that
x=0
. Thus
\varphi
is faithful.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Let
\theta\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital

-homomorphism between finite dimensional von Neumann algebras with

and note that

Since
\theta
is multiplicative, the range of

is invariant for

\pi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to M_{r}({\mathcal{B}});\pi(b)=b\otimes I_{r}.

Hence, we get

VV^{*}\in\pi({\mathcal{B}})^{\prime}={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\otimes M_{r}.

and thus

v_{i}v_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\text{ for all }i,j\in[r].

We now provide the connection between co-homomorphism and pullback maps with the pushforwards and pullbacks defined in
[

.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be quantum graphs acting on Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, respectively. Let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}

be a unital completely positive map.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
and
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
;

(ii)

\varphi
is a full strong co-homomorphism.

Moreover, if
\theta\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a
*
-homomorphism then
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
if and only if
\theta
is a pullback.

Proof.
.

Choose Kraus operators such that
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}:i\in[r]\}
.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)].
Assume
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
and
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
. The inclusion
v_{i}^{*}\,{\mathcal{T}}\,v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
for all

follows directly from the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
and hence
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
. On the other hand, we have
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}={\mathcal{T}}
and thus
\varphi
is a strong co-homomorphism. To see that
\varphi
is full note that
I_{K}\in{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
hence

I_{K}=\sum_{\ell}b_{\ell}v_{i(\ell)}s_{\ell}v_{j(\ell)}^{*}b^{\prime}_{\ell}\quad\text{for some }b_{\ell},b^{\prime}_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\text{ and }s_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Assume
\xi\in K
, then

\xi=I_{K}\xi=\sum_{\ell}b_{\ell}v_{i(\ell)}s_{\ell}v_{j(\ell)}^{*}b_{\ell}^{\prime}\xi\in[{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H].

Thus
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K
and by Lemma
3.7
we obtain that
\varphi
is faithful.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)].
Assume that
v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
and

v_{i}{\mathcal{S}}v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
for all
i,j
.
The first inclusion together with the fact that
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-bimodule implies
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
.
Conversely, let
s
be in
{\mathcal{S}}
. Since
\sum_{i}v_{i}^{*}v_{i}=I_{H}
, we have

s=(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{*}v_{i})s(\sum_{j}v_{j}^{*}v_{j})=\sum_{i,j}v_{i}^{*}(v_{i}sv_{j}^{*})v_{j}.

By assumption,
v_{i}sv_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{T}}
, and hence each summand lies in
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
.
Therefore
s\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
and so
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}
, as required.

Assume that moreover
\varphi
is full, that is
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}H]=K
. Since
v_{i}{\mathcal{S}}v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
is a
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
-bimodule we have
{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
.
Using that
\varphi
is unital, we can see that
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}K]=H
, that is,
{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}
acts non-degenerately. Now set
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}=\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi})
and
{\mathcal{M}}=[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}]
. Note that
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}
is unital,
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}
-bimodule and
{\mathcal{M}}
is non-degenerate (see the proof of Proposition
2.1
). In particular,
[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]
is unital and

{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}.

Thus

{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}]\subseteq[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varphi}^{*}]\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi},

concluding that
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}
.

We now prove the last statement, let
\theta
be a unital
*
-homomorphism and pick Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
such that
{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}:i\in[r]\}
. The fact that if
\theta
is a pullback implies that
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
is already shown in the first paragraph of the proof of implication [(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)]. For the converse assume
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
.
The inclusion
v_{i}^{*}\,{\mathcal{T}}\,v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
for all
i,j\in[r]
follows directly from the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
.
For the reverse inclusion, fix
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Since
H
is finite-dimensional, by
[
12
, Theorem 7.5]
we may write

s=\sum_{k,\ell}v_{k}^{*}\,t_{k\ell}\,v_{\ell}\qquad\text{with }t_{k\ell}\in{\mathcal{T}}.

Then for fixed
i,j
we obtain

v_{i}sv_{j}^{*}=\sum_{k,\ell}(v_{i}v_{k}^{*})\,t_{k\ell}\,(v_{\ell}v_{j}^{*}).

Since
{\mathcal{T}}
is a
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
-bimodule and
v_{i}v_{k}^{*},v_{\ell}v_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
for every
i,j,k,\ell\in[r]
(see Remark
3.8
) we obtain that
each term of the sum above lies in
{\mathcal{T}}
and hence
v_{i}sv_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{T}}
, as required.
∎

The following proposition shows that pullbacks of quantum graphs coincide in the classical case with pullbacks of graphs in the sense of
[
20
]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be graphs.

(i)

If
f\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{H}})
is a pullback then the map
\theta_{f}\colon D_{V({\mathcal{H}})}\to D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}
defined by

\theta_{f}(\sum_{w\in V({\mathcal{H}})}\lambda_{w}\varepsilon_{ww})=\sum_{i\in V({\mathcal{G}})}\lambda_{f(i)}\varepsilon_{ii}

is a unital
*
-homomorphism that satisfies
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}}
.

(ii)

Conversely, let
\theta\colon D_{V({\mathcal{H}})}\to D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}
be a unital
*
-homomorphism such that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
.
Then there exists a unique pullback
f\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{H}})
such that
\theta=\theta_{f}
.

In addition,
f
is surjective if and only if
\theta_{f}
is injective if and only if
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta_{f}}\,\mathbb{C}^{V({\mathcal{G}})}]=\mathbb{C}^{V({\mathcal{H}})}
.

Proof.
.

(i) The fact that
\theta_{f}
is a unital
*
-homomorphism follows directly from its definition.
For each
i\in V({\mathcal{G}})
set

v_{i}:=\varepsilon_{f(i)i}\in M_{V({\mathcal{H}}),V({\mathcal{G}})},

and note that for every
w,z\in V({\mathcal{H}})
we have

v_{i}^{*}\varepsilon_{wz}v_{j}=\varepsilon_{i,f(i)}\,\varepsilon_{wz}\,\varepsilon_{f(j),j}=\delta_{f(i),w}\delta_{z,f(j)}\,\varepsilon_{ij}.

In particular,

\sum_{i\in V({\mathcal{G}})}v_{i}^{*}\varepsilon_{ww}v_{i}=\sum_{i\in V({\mathcal{G}})}\delta_{f(i),w}\varepsilon_{ii}=\theta_{f}(\varepsilon_{ww}),

and thus
\theta_{f}
admits the Kraus representation

\theta_{f}(d)=\sum_{i\in V({\mathcal{G}})}v_{i}^{*}dv_{i}\quad\text{for all }d\in D_{V({\mathcal{H}})}.

By definition,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}}
is the
D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}
-bimodule generated by

\{v_{i}^{*}tv_{j}:i,j\in V({\mathcal{G}}),t\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}\}.

Since
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}
is spanned by
\{\varepsilon_{wz}:w\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}z\}
, the computation above yields

(3.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}^{*}\varepsilon_{wz}v_{j}:i,j\in V({\mathcal{G}}),w\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}z\}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:f(i)\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}f(j)\}.

Hence

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:i\simeq_{\mathcal{G}}j\}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:f(i)\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}f(j)\}={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}},

since
f
is a pullback.

(ii) Let
\theta\colon D_{V({\mathcal{H}})}\to D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}
be a unital
*
-homomorphism.
The projections

\{\theta(\varepsilon_{ww})\}_{w\in V({\mathcal{H}})}\subseteq D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}

are pairwise orthogonal and sum to
I_{V({\mathcal{G}})}
, so there exists a unique partition

V({\mathcal{G}})=\bigsqcup_{w\in V({\mathcal{H}})}F_{w}\text{ such that }\theta(\varepsilon_{ww})=\sum_{i\in F_{w}}\varepsilon_{ii}.

Define
f\colon V({\mathcal{G}})\to V({\mathcal{H}})
by
f(i)=w
if and only if
i\in F_{w}
.
Then
\theta=\theta_{f}
, and uniqueness of
f
follows from the definition of
\theta_{f}
.

Assume
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta}
. As noted in
[
12
]
, the pullback
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}}
is independent of the Kraus representation, so we may compute it using the Kraus family
\{v_{i}:=\varepsilon_{f(i),i}\}_{i\in V({\mathcal{G}})}
. By (
3.1
),

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta}={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\leftarrow\theta_{f}}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:f(i)\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}f(j)\}.

Thus

\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:i\simeq_{\mathcal{G}}j\}=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon_{ij}:f(i)\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}f(j)\}.

Linear independence of the matrix units gives

i\simeq_{\mathcal{G}}j\iff f(i)\simeq_{\mathcal{H}}f(j)\quad\forall\,i,j\in V({\mathcal{G}}),

so
f
is a pullback.

Finally,
f
is surjective if and only if each
w\in V({\mathcal{H}})
occurs as
f(i)
for some
i
, which is equivalent to injectivity of
\theta_{f}
. The equivalence with the fullness condition
[{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta_{f}}\,\mathbb{C}^{V({\mathcal{G}})}]=\mathbb{C}^{V({\mathcal{H}})}
follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Example 3.11
.

The previous proposition shows that classical full pullbacks are precisely those implemented by unital
*
-homomorphisms. This cannot, in general, be extended to faithful unital completely positive maps.

Indeed: let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the empty graph on three vertices and
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}
the complete graph on three vertices. Thus

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}=D_{3},\qquad{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{3}}=M_{3}.

Define a unital completely positive map

\varphi\colon D_{V({\mathcal{G}})}\to D_{V({\mathcal{K}}_{3})}

by

\varphi(\varepsilon_{aa})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{11}+\varepsilon_{33}),\qquad\varphi(\varepsilon_{bb})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{11}+\varepsilon_{22}),\qquad\varphi(\varepsilon_{cc})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{22}+\varepsilon_{33}).

Then
\varphi
is faithful and admits a Kraus representation

\varphi(d)=\sum_{k}v_{k}^{*}dv_{k}

with Kraus operators

\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{a,1},\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{b,1},\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{b,2},\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{c,2},\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{a,3},\;\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{c,3}.

A direct computation shows that

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}=M_{3}={\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{3}}.

However, there is no surjective pullback map
f\colon V({\mathcal{K}}_{3})\to V({\mathcal{G}})
.
Indeed, the pullback condition

i\simeq_{{\mathcal{K}}_{3}}j\iff f(i)\simeq_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(j)

reduces here to
f(i)=f(j)
for all
i,j
, so every pullback map must be constant.
Hence the only pullback maps are the three constant maps onto
a
,
b
, or
c
,
none of which is surjective.

3.2.
A true-twin construction for quantum graphs

In this subsection we will describe a construction that will be particularly useful in the main theorem. This can be thought of as the quantum analogue of the true-twin reduction in classical graphs in which we obtain the skeleton of an irreducibly acting quantum graph.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
be a quantum graph acting irreducibly on a Hilbert space
H
. Since
{\mathcal{S}}
acts irreducibly, we may view
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
concretely as a unital C*-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
. Hence there exist positive integers

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad n_{1},\dots,n_{k}

such that, up to unitary equivalence,

H=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H).

Write

H_{i}:=\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}\text{ for all }i\in[k].

Let
p_{i}
denote the corresponding minimal central projections of
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
.
We will frequently use the identification

p_{i}{\mathcal{B}}(H)p_{j}\cong{\mathcal{B}}(H_{j},H_{i})\cong M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes M_{n_{i},n_{j}},

thus we view

p_{i}=I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H_{i})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)\text{ for all }i\in[k].

For
i,j\in[k]
set

{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}:=p_{i}{\mathcal{S}}p_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H_{j},H_{i}).

Note that
{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
since
p_{i},p_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
for all
i,j\in[k]

and
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
-bimodule. Moreover, we have

(M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}})\;{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}\;(M_{\alpha_{j}}\otimes I_{n_{j}})\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

Indeed, if

a\in M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}},\quad b\in M_{\alpha_{j}}\otimes I_{n_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad x\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij},

then write
x=p_{i}sp_{j}
, for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and hence

axb=p_{i}(ap_{i})s(p_{j}b)p_{j}\in p_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}p_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

Fix
i,j\in[k]
and let
\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{rs}
denote the
(r,s)
-matrix unit of

M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}
.
Let
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{j}}
be of norm one and consider the rank one operator
\xi\eta^{*}\in M_{\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}}
.
We set

L_{\xi\eta^{*}}\colon M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes M_{n_{i},n_{j}}\to M_{n_{i},n_{j}}

to be the left slice map associated with the rank one operator
\xi\eta^{*}\in M_{\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}}
, that is,

L_{\xi\eta^{*}}(a\otimes b)=\operatorname{Tr}((\xi\eta^{*})a)b\text{ for }a\otimes b\in M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes M_{n_{i},n_{j}}.

We define

{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}:=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij})\subseteq M_{n_{i},n_{j}}.

Lemma 3.12
.

For each
i,j\in[k]
, let
{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
be defined via the slice construction described above. Then the following hold:

(i)

The space
{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
is independent of the choice of rank one operator used in its definition.

(ii)

We have the decomposition

{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}=M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}.

Proof.
.

(i) To see why
{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
is independent of the choice of rank one operators, we will show that for any rank one operator
\xi\eta^{*}\in M_{\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}}
where
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{j}}
are of norm one, we have

L_{\xi\eta^{*}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij})=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij}).

Indeed, choose unitaries
u^{i}\in M_{\alpha_{i}}
and
u^{j}\in M_{\alpha_{j}}
with

u^{i}(e_{1})=\eta\quad\text{and}\quad u^{j}(e_{1})=\xi.

Then, simple calculations show that

L_{\xi\eta^{*}}(x)=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}((u^{i}\otimes I)^{*}x(u^{j}\otimes I))\text{ for all }x\in M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes M_{n_{i},n_{j}}.

Since
{\mathcal{S}}_{i,j}
is invariant under left and right multiplication with
M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}}
and
M_{\alpha_{j}}\otimes I_{n_{j}}
, respectively, and
u^{i},u^{j}
are unitaries we conclude that

L_{\xi\eta^{*}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij})=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}(((u^{i})^{*}\otimes I_{n_{i}}){\mathcal{S}}_{ij}(u^{j}\otimes I_{n_{j}}))=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij}).

(ii)
For fixed
k,\ell
and
x=\sum_{k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}\otimes x_{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}
we have

(\varepsilon^{(i,i)}_{1k}\otimes I_{n_{i}})x(\varepsilon^{(j,j)}_{\ell 1}\otimes I_{n_{j}})=\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{11}\otimes x_{k\ell}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

Applying
L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}
yields

x_{k\ell}=L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}(\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{11}\otimes x_{k\ell})\in{\mathcal{R}}_{ij},

and hence

{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}\subseteq M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}.

Conversely, if
\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{k\ell}\otimes y\in M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
choose
x\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}
with
L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}(x)=y
.
Then

\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{11}\otimes y=\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{11}\otimes L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(j,i)}}(x)=(\varepsilon^{(i,i)}_{11}\otimes I_{n_{i}})x(\varepsilon^{(j,j)}_{11}\otimes I_{n_{j}})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

In particular,

\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{k\ell}\otimes y=(\varepsilon^{(i,i)}_{k1}\otimes I_{n_{i}})(\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{11}\otimes y)(\varepsilon^{(j,j)}_{1\ell}\otimes I_{n_{j}})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

We conclude that

{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}=M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
, as required.
∎

With the notation above, set

L:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M_{n_{i}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L),\qquad{\mathcal{R}}:=\bigoplus_{i,j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L).

We will call
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
the
skeleton
of the quantum graph
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
.

Proposition 3.13
.

The skeleton
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
of an irreducibly acting quantum graph
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a quantum graph and
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a full pullback of
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
.

Proof.
.

By Lemma
3.12
, we have

{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}=M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}\qquad\text{for all }i,j\in[k].

We first show that
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
is a quantum graph.
We have that
{\mathcal{R}}
is a selfadjoint subspace since
({\mathcal{S}}_{ij})^{*}={\mathcal{S}}_{ji}
, and it
contains
I_{L}
as

L_{\varepsilon_{11}^{(i,i)}}(I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}})=I_{n_{i}}.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{R}}
is trivially a bimodule over
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}I_{n_{i}}
and therefore

({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
is a quantum graph. We now construct the pullbacks. Define
\theta\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\prime}
by

\theta(\oplus_{i=1}^{k}x_{i})=\oplus_{i=1}^{k}(I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes x_{i}),

and since
{\mathcal{S}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
-bimodule, by the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
we obtain
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
and hence
\theta({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
.
This is a unital
*
-homomorphism since it is the direct sum of the canonical
amplifications
x_{i}\mapsto I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
on each block.

We will now obtain a Kraus representation for
\theta
. For each
i\in[k]
and
r\in[\alpha_{i}]
define the operators
v_{i,r}:H\to L
by

v_{i,r}:\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\zeta_{i}\\
\vdots\\
\zeta_{k}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
(e_{r}^{*}\otimes I_{n_{i}})(\zeta_{i})\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}

with zeros in all but the
i
-th coordinate and
e_{r}^{*}(\xi)=\langle e_{r},\xi\rangle
for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}
. Thus
v_{i,r}\colon H\to L
vanishes off
H_{i}
and

v_{i,r}(\sum_{s}e_{s}\otimes\xi_{s})=\xi_{r}\quad\text{for}\quad\sum_{s}e_{s}\otimes\xi_{s}\in H_{i}.

In addition, we have

v_{i,r}^{*}:\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{i}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{k}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
e_{r}\otimes\xi_{i}\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Note that

\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}v_{i,r}^{*}v_{i,r}=p_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}v_{i,r}v_{i,r}^{*}=\alpha_{i}I_{L_{i}},

and therefore

\sum_{i,r}v_{i,r}^{*}v_{i,r}=I_{H}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i,r}v_{i,r}v_{i,r}^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}I_{L_{i}}.

Fix
x=\oplus_{i}x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L)
.
For

\zeta=(\zeta_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\in H\text{ with }\zeta_{j}=\sum_{s}e_{s}\otimes\xi_{s}^{(j)}\in H_{i},

we have

(v_{i,r}^{*}xv_{i,r})(\zeta)=v_{i,r}^{*}x_{i}\xi_{r}^{(i)}=e_{r}\otimes x_{i}\xi_{r}^{(i)},

and hence

\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}(v_{i,r}^{*}xv_{i,r})(\zeta)=\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}e_{r}\otimes x_{i}\xi_{r}^{(i)}=(I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes x_{i})\zeta.

Summing over all
i\in[k]
yields the Kraus form

\theta(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}v_{i,r}^{*}\,x\,v_{i,r}.

To show the pullback relations, fix
i,j\in[k]
and let
y
be
{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
. We claim that

v_{i,r}^{*}\,y\,v_{j,r^{\prime}}=(\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{rr^{\prime}}\otimes y),

under the usual identification of
{\mathcal{B}}(H_{j},H_{i})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
To verify this, it suffices to evaluate both sides on elementary tensors
e_{s}\otimes\eta\in H_{j}

where
1\leq s\leq\alpha_{j}
and
\eta\in L_{j}
. Indeed, we have

v_{i,r}^{*}\,y\,v_{j,r^{\prime}}(e_{s}\otimes\eta)=v_{i,r}^{*}(\delta_{r^{\prime}s}\,y\eta)=\delta_{r^{\prime}s}\,(e_{r}\otimes y\eta),

and

(\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{rr^{\prime}}\otimes y)(e_{s}\otimes\eta)=(\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{rr^{\prime}}e_{s})\otimes y\eta=\delta_{r^{\prime}s}\,e_{r}\otimes y\eta.

Since
y\in{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}
, we obtain

v_{i,r}^{*}\,y\,v_{j,r^{\prime}}=\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{rr^{\prime}}\otimes y\in M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}\otimes{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}={\mathcal{S}}_{ij},

showing that

v_{i,r}^{*}{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}v_{j,r^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}.

Conversely, if
x\in{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}
write

x=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\alpha_{i}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\alpha_{j}}\varepsilon^{(i,j)}_{\mu\nu}\otimes y_{\mu\nu}\text{ for }y_{\mu\nu}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}.

Then for
\eta\in L_{j}
we obtain

v_{i,r}xv_{j,r^{\prime}}^{*}(\eta)=v_{i,r}(x(e_{r^{\prime}}\otimes\eta))=v_{i,r}(\sum_{\mu=1}^{\alpha_{i}}e_{\mu}\otimes y_{\mu r^{\prime}}\eta)=y_{rr^{\prime}}\eta,

so

v_{i,r}xv_{j,r^{\prime}}^{*}=y_{rr^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}.

Therefore, we have

v_{i,r}^{*}\,{\mathcal{R}}\,v_{j,r^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i,r}\,{\mathcal{S}}\,v_{j,r^{\prime}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{R}},

and
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a pullback of
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
.
Finally
\theta
is clearly injective and hence it is a full pullback.
∎

The following proposition shows that the skeleton of a graph operator system is the graph operator system of the skeleton of the graph, showing that the construction of the skeleton of a quantum graph is indeed a true-twin reduction analogue in the quantum graph setting.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a graph on
n
vertices and let
k
be the number of vertices of its skeleton
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
. Then the skeleton of the quantum graph
({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}},D_{n})
is the quantum graph
({\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}},D_{k})
.

Proof.
.

By Proposition
2.5
we have that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}
is irreducibly acting. Let
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
be the skeleton of
({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}},D_{n})
. By Proposition
2.4
and the construction of
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
we obtain

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M_{\alpha_{i}},\quad L=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{C}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{C}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}M_{1}\cong D_{k}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L),

where the number of blocks
k=V({\mathcal{G}}^{\circ})
is exactly the number of true-twin equivalence classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\alpha_{i}=|C_{i}|
for all
i\in[k]
. Moreover, we have

p_{i}=I_{\alpha_{i}}=\sum_{x\in C_{i}}\varepsilon_{xx}\text{ for all }i\in[k],

and since vertices within a true-twin equivalence class have identical neighborhoods

({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}})_{ij}=p_{i}{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}p_{j}=\begin{cases}M_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}&\text{if }C_{i}\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}}C_{j},\\
(0)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, we get

{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}=L_{\varepsilon^{(j,i)}_{11}}({\mathcal{S}}_{ij})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\text{if }C_{i}\sim_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}}C_{j},\\
(0)&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and therefore

{\mathcal{R}}=\bigoplus_{i,j}{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(L)\cong M_{k},

is precisely the operator system spanned by the matrix units
\varepsilon_{ij}

corresponding to adjacency in the skeleton graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}
.
Thus
{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}^{\circ}}
.
∎

3.3.
TRO-equivalence via pullbacks

In this subsection we state and prove one of the main result of this paper. We also present examples of TRO-equivalent quantum graphs and demonstrate how our result extends
[

, Corollary 7.6]
from the classical graph setting to the quantum graph framework.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be two quantum graphs acting irreducibly on Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
, respectively. The following are equivalent:

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{T}}
.

(iii)

There exists a quantum graph
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{C}})
so that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})

{\mathcal{R}}

(iv)

.

[(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)]. It follows from
[

, Theorem 7.5]
.

[(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)]. It follows from Theorem

[(iii)

implementing the full pullbacks of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
with
{\mathcal{R}}
, respectively. We may choose Kraus operators such that

{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}:i=1,\ldots,r\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,L)\text{ and }{\mathcal{X}}_{\varrho}=\operatorname{span}\{w_{j}:\;j=1,\ldots,s\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K,L).

[{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta}H]=L=[{\mathcal{X}}_{\varrho}K].

showing that

acts non-degenerately.
We have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}={\mathcal{X}}_{\varrho}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\theta}^{*}{\mathcal{X}}_{\varrho}

A similar argument shows that

Applying Proposition
2.1
yields that
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
, as required.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)].
Suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
are TRO-equivalent. Since both
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
act irreducibly, by Proposition
2.2
we may assume that

{\mathcal{S}}=[{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}}]\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{T}}=[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}],

via a non-degenerate TRO

satisfying

By
[
17

we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\prime}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{\prime},

and therefore up to unitary equivalence we may write

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(M_{\beta_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K),

with

H=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}})\quad\text{and}\quad K=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{\beta_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}).

Let
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{S}}},{\mathcal{C}})
and
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
be the skeletons of the quantum graphs
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})

({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{S}}},{\mathcal{C}})
and
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
are quantum graphs on the same algebra due to the decompositions of

and
K
. By Proposition
3.13
we have that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
are full pullbacks of
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{S}}},{\mathcal{C}})
and
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
via the maps
\theta^{\prime}
and
\varrho
, respectively. We will prove that
{\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
and
{\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}
are unitarily equivalent through a unitary conjugation that preserves
{\mathcal{C}}

({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and

are full pullbacks of
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
.

p_{i}
and
q_{i}
be the minimal central projections corresponding to the above block decompositions. Since the
*
-isomorphism

implemented by
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
preserves minimal central projections, and hence after permuting blocks we may assume that

q_{i}x=xp_{i}\text{ for all }x\in{\mathcal{M}}\text{ and }i=1,\dots,k.

Therefore, we obtain

{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}=q_{i}{\mathcal{T}}q_{j}=q_{i}[{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}]q_{j}=[q_{i}{\mathcal{M}}p_{i}{\mathcal{S}}p_{j}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}q_{j}]=[(q_{i}{\mathcal{M}}p_{i}){\mathcal{S}}_{ij}(p_{j}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}q_{j})].

[(q_{i}{\mathcal{M}}p_{i})^{*}(q_{i}{\mathcal{M}}p_{i})]=[p_{i}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}p_{i}]=[p_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}p_{i}]=M_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes I_{n_{i}},

({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{S}}},{\mathcal{C}})

({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
through the map

\operatorname{ad}{u^{*}}\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}};x\mapsto u^{*}xu.

We conclude that
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a full pullback of
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
through the map

and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
is a full pullback of
({\mathcal{R}}^{{\mathcal{T}}},{\mathcal{C}})
through the map
\varrho

3.4.

.

({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and

and

Remark 3.20

\Delta
-equivalent but not TRO-equivalent. Indeed, if this was the case, we would have that
\mathbb{C}I_{n}={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\cong{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=M_{m}
which is not possible for
n,m>1
.

While the previous remark shows that in general the two notions of Morita equivalence are distinct, they coincide in the case of noncommutative graphs. In
[
20
, Proposition 7.2]
it is shown that TRO-equivalence between two noncommutative graphs
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq M_{m}
is equivalent to the existence of a non-degenerate subspace
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq M_{m,n}
such that
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
. As shown in the next corollary the operator space
{\mathcal{X}}
may be assumed to be merely non-degenerately acting. In particular, TRO-equivalence implies the existence of a strong co-homomorphism between
{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq M_{m}
be noncommutative graphs.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
;

(ii)

There exists a non-degenerately acting subspace
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq M_{m,n}
such that

{\mathcal{X}}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}};

(iii)

There exists a (faithful) unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon M_{m}\to M_{n}
such that

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\leftarrow\varphi}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}^{\rightarrow\varphi}.

Proof.
.

The equivalence of items (i) and (iii) follows from Theorem
3.19
and Theorem
3.9
while the equivalence of items (i) and (ii) follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We now see some examples in this case.

Example 3.22
.

Consider

{\mathcal{S}}=\operatorname{span}\{I_{2},E_{12},E_{21}\}\subseteq M_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}=M_{2},

and the faithful unital
*
-representation

\theta\colon M_{2}\to M_{4},\qquad\theta(x)=x\oplus x=v_{1}^{*}xv_{1}+v_{2}^{*}xv_{2},

where

v_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{2}&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad v_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Then

{\mathcal{T}}:={\mathcal{S}}^{\leftarrow\theta}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{i}^{*}sv_{j}:s\in{\mathcal{S}},i,j=1,2\}={\mathcal{S}}\otimes M_{2}.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{T}}
is a quantum graph on

\theta(M_{2})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}:x\in M_{2}\right\}\subseteq M_{4}.

Hence, by Corollary
3.21

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
. In fact, amplifications of
{\mathcal{S}}
are the only quantum graphs on
M_{m}
that are TRO-equivalent to
{\mathcal{S}}
(see
[
20
, Example 3.11]
).

Example 3.23
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}(H)={\mathcal{B}}(K)=M_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then if
\Phi,\Psi:M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
are
*
-automorphisms of
M_{n}
(i.e., they are describing closed quantum dynamical systems on
M_{n}
), we have
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{\Psi}
. Indeed, as every
*
-automorphism of
M_{n}
is inner, the Kraus decomposition for both are given by unitary operators
u,\widetilde{u}\in M_{n}
with
\Phi(a)=uau^{*}
and
\Psi(a)=\widetilde{u}a\widetilde{u}^{*}
, so

{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}=\operatorname{span}\{u^{*}u\}=\mathbb{C}I_{n}=\operatorname{span}\{\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{u}\}={\mathcal{S}}_{\Psi},

with TRO-equivalence following trivially.

Example 3.24
.

A
depolarizing channel

\Phi_{\lambda}:M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
is a quantum channel of the form

\Phi_{\lambda}(\rho)=(1-\lambda)\rho+\frac{\lambda}{n}I_{n}\text{ for some }0\leq\lambda\leq 1+\frac{1}{n^{2}-1},

and it is
completely depolarizing
if
\lambda=1
. Let
\Phi\colon M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
and
\Psi\colon M_{m}\rightarrow M_{m}
be completely depolarizing channels on their respective systems.
Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{\Psi}
.

Indeed, as any two states are confusable the confusability graphs for
\Phi
and
\Psi
are easily seen to be
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}=M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Psi}=M_{m}
and hence
{\mathcal{M}}=M_{m,n}
is a non-degenerate TRO implementing TRO-equivalence between
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Psi}
.

In fact, if
{\mathcal{N}}:[m]\rightarrow[k]
is any classical channel for which
{\mathcal{N}}(j|i)\neq 0
for all
i\in[m]
and
j\in[k]
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{N}}}
for any completely depolarizing channel
\Phi
. This follows from the previous argument, in conjunction with the fact that the Kraus operators for
{\mathcal{N}}
(considered as a quantum channel) are
\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}(j|i)}\epsilon_{ji}
, with all non-zero for every choice of
(i,j)\in[m]\times[k]
.

Finally, we end this section by showing that this stronger notion of Morita equivalence, in the sense of Theorem

, collapses into isomorphism in the case of graph operator systems.

Remark 3.25
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be graphs on
n
and

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{G}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{H}}

4.
Noncommutative graph parameters

In this section, we specialize to the case of noncommutative graphs, i.e., quantum graphs on
M_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and investigate how TRO-equivalence of quantum graphs interacts with various noncommutative graph parameters. The following definitions are taken from
[
7
,
15
,
38
]
; we indicate the source when appropriate.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with
\dim H=k
and let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be an operator system. Following
[
7
,
15
]
, a set of mutually orthogonal unit vectors

\{\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n}\subseteq H

is called
{\mathcal{S}}
-
independent
if

\xi_{i}\xi_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},\qquad i\neq j,

and
{\mathcal{S}}
-
clique
if

\xi_{i}\xi_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}},\qquad i\neq j.

We recall several numerical parameters associated with
{\mathcal{S}}
, which are noncommutative analogues of classical graph parameters.
The
independence number
of
{\mathcal{S}}
is

\alpha({\mathcal{S}}):=\max\Big\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq H\text{ forming an ${\mathcal{S}}$-independent set}\Big\}.

The
clique number
of
{\mathcal{S}}
is

\omega({\mathcal{S}}):=\max\Big\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq H\text{ forming an ${\mathcal{S}}$-clique}\Big\}.

The fact that
\alpha
is a quantization of the classical independence number was shown in
[
15
]
, and that
\omega
is a quantisation of the classical clique number was shown
[
7
, Corollary 3.9]
.
The
minimal chromatic number
of
{\mathcal{S}}
, denoted
\chi_{0}({\mathcal{S}})
, is the least
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that there exist
a basis
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of
H
and a partition

\{1,\dots,k\}=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{n},

for which

\xi_{i}\xi_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},\text{ when }i\neq j,\;i,j\in S_{\ell},\;\ell=1,\dots,n.

In
[
28
, Theorem 14]
(see also
[
38
]
) it is shown that this is quantization of the classical chromatic number.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that the definition of the minimal chromatic number

is a slightly modified version of the chromatic number as defined in

be the noncommutative graph of

. Following

finite-dimensional, is the noncommutative graph for a quantum channel
\Phi
. Thus, we define the Shannon capacity

.

In the discussion following
[

\displaystyle\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{S}})^{-1}=\sup\{\inf\{\|\Phi(\rho)\|:\rho\text{ a state of }{\mathcal{B}}(H)\}:\Phi\in{\mathfrak{C}}({\mathcal{S}})\},

Following

Remark 4.2
.

It is not true that
\chi_{0}
and
\omega
are invariant under TRO-equivalence. This can be shown in the setting of graph operator systems. Indeed, consider two complete graphs
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{m}
with

. Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}}=M_{n}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{m}}=M_{m}
are TRO-equivalent via

and

\omega({\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})=\chi_{0}({\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}})=n\neq m=\chi_{0}({\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{m}})=\omega({\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{m}}).

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

The parameters
\alpha,c_{0},\vartheta,\widehat{\vartheta},{\mathfrak{H}},\beta
and
\gamma
are invariant under TRO-equivalence of noncommutative graphs.

Proof.
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be irreducibly acting noncommutative graphs on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
H,K
and suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
.
By Corollary
3.21
we may pick a unital completely positive map
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(K)\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with Kraus operators
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
that satisfy

v_{i}{\mathcal{S}}v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\text{ for all }i,j\in[r].

Consider the column isometry
V:=(v_{1},\dots,v_{r})
.

First, we show that
\alpha({\mathcal{S}})=\alpha({\mathcal{T}})
. Let
\{\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
be an
{\mathcal{S}}
-independent set. As
V
is a column isometry, we claim that
\{V\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
is an
M_{r}({\mathcal{T}})
independent set. Indeed, it follows that
\{V\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
is an orthonormal set, and by taking orthogonal complements

(4.1)

\displaystyle v_{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{\perp}v_{j}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}^{\perp}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},

for all
i,j\in[r]
. As
\{\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
is an
{\mathcal{S}}
-independent set, if
\ell\neq p
, then
\xi_{\ell}\xi_{p}^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\perp}
. By (
4.1
), we then have that
v_{i}\xi_{\ell}\xi_{p}^{*}v_{j}^{*}\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\perp}
for
\ell\neq p
. Thus,
\{V\xi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
is an
M_{r}({\mathcal{T}})
-independent set. This implies
\alpha({\mathcal{S}})\leq\alpha(M_{r}({\mathcal{T}}))
. Finally, using
[
31
, Remark IV.6]
, we may argue
\alpha(M_{r}({\mathcal{T}}))=\alpha({\mathcal{T}})
, showing
\alpha({\mathcal{S}})\leq\alpha({\mathcal{T}})
. A symmetric argument shows that
\alpha({\mathcal{T}})\leq\alpha({\mathcal{S}})
, and thus
\alpha({\mathcal{S}})=\alpha({\mathcal{T}})
as claimed.

We now prove that
c_{0}({\mathcal{S}})=c_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
. We rely on the (readily verified) fact that if
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}
, then
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes n}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{T}}^{\otimes n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the previous result, we have that
\alpha({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes n})=\alpha({\mathcal{T}}^{\otimes n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

c_{0}({\mathcal{S}})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\alpha({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes n})}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{\alpha({\mathcal{T}}^{\otimes n})}=c_{0}({\mathcal{T}}).

Next we prove that
\beta({\mathcal{S}})=\beta({\mathcal{T}})
and
\gamma({\mathcal{S}})=\gamma({\mathcal{T}})
.
Assume that
\beta({\mathcal{T}})=n
and let
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(K)\rightarrow M_{n}
be a quantum channel in
{\mathfrak{C}}({\mathcal{T}})
with Kraus decomposition

\Phi(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}aw_{i}^{*}\text{ for all }a\in{\mathcal{B}}(K).

The composition
\Phi\circ\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{B}}(H)\rightarrow M_{n}
with Kraus decomposition

(\Phi\circ\varphi^{*})(a)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}w_{i}v_{j}av_{j}^{*}w_{i}^{*},\text{ for all }a\in{\mathcal{B}}(H),

is another quantum channel. Furthermore, we claim
\Phi\circ\varphi^{*}\in{\mathfrak{C}}({\mathcal{S}})
. Indeed, as
\Phi\in\mathfrak{C}({\mathcal{T}})
, we have that
w_{j}^{*}w_{i}\in{\mathcal{T}}
for each
i,j\in[\ell]
. Therefore,

{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi\circ\varphi^{*}}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{p}^{*}w_{j}^{*}w_{i}v_{q}:\;i,j\in[\ell],p,q\in[r]\}\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{v_{p}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{q}:\;p,q\in[r]\}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}},

by the inclusion relations on
\varphi
. Therefore,
\Phi\circ\varphi^{*}\in{\mathfrak{C}}({\mathcal{S}})
as claimed, and so
\beta({\mathcal{S}})\leq\beta({\mathcal{T}})
. Under TRO-equivalence, we may use a symmetric argument to conclude
\beta({\mathcal{S}})=\beta({\mathcal{T}})
. The argument for
\gamma
is analogous.

Finally, we show that
\vartheta({\mathcal{S}})=\vartheta({\mathcal{T}})
,

\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{S}})=\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{T}})
and
{\mathfrak{H}}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathfrak{H}}({\mathcal{T}})
.
The dual map

\varphi^{*}\colon{\mathcal{B}}(H)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K);\qquad\varphi^{*}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}av_{i}^{*}\text{ for all }a\in{\mathcal{B}}(K),

is completely positive and trace-preserving and its Kraus operators satisfy
v_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{T}}v_{j}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
for all
i,j\in[r]
. In
[
7
, Proposition 6.1]
it is shown that the quantities
\vartheta
and
\widehat{\vartheta}
are monotone under such maps. Hence,
\vartheta({\mathcal{T}})\leq\vartheta({\mathcal{S}})
and
\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{T}})\leq\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{S}})
.
Since TRO-equivalence is symmetric, the same argument in the opposite direction yields
\vartheta({\mathcal{S}})=\vartheta({\mathcal{T}})
and

\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{S}})=\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{T}})
. An analogous argument and
[
25
, Proposition 12]
yield
{\mathfrak{H}}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathfrak{H}}({\mathcal{T}})
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.6

{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{B}}(H)

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{B}}(H)
, then
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{B}}(H,K)
is a non-degenerate TRO that implements TRO-equivalence between
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and
{\mathcal{B}}(K)
. Assume now that
{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\rm TRO}{\mathcal{B}}(K)
. For any quantum channel
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{B}}(K)\rightarrow M_{d}
we have
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Phi}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K)
, and thus
\Phi\in\mathfrak{C}({\mathcal{B}}(K))
. Using
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
we get
\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{B}}(K))=1
and hence Theorem
4.3
yields that
\widehat{\vartheta}({\mathcal{S}})=1
. Finally, by
[
7
, Proposition 6.7]
we have
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{B}}(H)
, as required.
∎

We finish the section with an application to connectedness in the quantum graph setting. Connectedness for quantum graphs was introduced in
[
9
]
in the noncommutative graph case and in
[
33
]
in the quantum adjacency setting. The definition that we use comes from the work of
[
11
]
(see Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5 therein) in which the different models were unified.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
be a quantum graph acting on a Hilbert space
H
. We say that the quantum graph

({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is
connected
if

\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{B}}(H).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
be two irreducibly acting quantum graphs. Assume that

{\mathcal{S}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{T}}.

Then
({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
is connected if and only if
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{B}})
is connected.

Proof.
.

By
[
20
, Proposition 7.5]
, we have
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})\sim_{\rm TRO}\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{T}})
. Hence, by Corollary
4.6
we obtain that
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{B}}(H)
if and only if
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{T}})={\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
∎

In particular, for graph operator systems we have the following.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be graphs and suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sim_{\Delta}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Then
{\mathcal{G}}
is connected if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}
is connected.

Proof.
.

It follows from
[
9
, Corollary 3.4]
that
{\mathcal{G}}
is connected if and only if
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
is connected.
The result now follows from Proposition
4.7
.
∎
