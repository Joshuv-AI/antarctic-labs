Mapping the Audiology and Speech Language Pathology (ASLP) curricula to NEP 2020: a document analysis of the RCI revised syllabus (2024)
Authors: Krupa Venkatraman, Prakash Boominathan, Radhakrishnan Chella Perumal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 calls for a multidisciplinary, flexible and outcome-oriented approach to education in India. Responding to this pivotal policy framework, the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI) has come up with a revised syllabus (2024) for Audiology and Speech- Language Pathology courses. This investigation was a systematic mapping of the newly propagated undergraduate and postgraduate syllabi of B. ASLP, MSc (SLP), and MSc (Aud) against the central directions of the NEP 2020 namely, goals of holistic integration, structural flexibility and digital pedagogy.MethodA qualitative structure of document analysis was used as set out by Bowen, in the analysis of the three syllabi for RCI 2024. Through a deductive strategy, the contents were coded into three domains: philosophical orientation, structural features and pedagogical strategies.Alignment in each domain was evaluated through the Theory of Change (ToC) framework to examine how elements of the curriculum could lead to long term transformations in professional training.ResultsThe analysis showed strengths especially in the structural dimensions such as the introduction of a modular credit system and the explicit articulation of competencies. However, there remain major gaps in terms of explicit pedagogic strategies, multilingual provision and interdisciplinary linkages. Although the structural framework is consistent with national policy objectives, the operational pathways of digital integration and learner autonomy are not adequately outlined.ConclusionThe RCI syllabi exhibit promising structural congruence with the NEP 2020. However, pedagogical innovation and incorporating philosophical foundations are limited.Recommendations include incorporating graduate attributes into learning outcomes, operationalising the Academic Bank of Credit (ABC) and formalising digital teaching strategies.This study represents a first step in the reflexive process of the translation of education policy into clinical curriculum practice.

The 2030 agenda for Sustainable Development Growth (SDG-4) commits the United Nations to “ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all” [1]. The vision of the agenda emphasises holistic development, learner centred approaches and outcome-based education. The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 aims to overcome the issue of fragmentation in the sphere of higher education by raising the Gross Enrolment Ratio from 26.3 per cent to 50 per cent by 2035. The policy envisions the transformation of higher education institutions (HEIs) into large multidisciplinary establishments which include at least 3,000 students, which will help create dynamic scholarly communities. HEIs are classified into 3 broad categories: Research-intensive Universities, Teaching-intensive Universities and Autonomous Degree-Granting Colleges (ACs). The NEP further calls for the abolition of affiliated colleges along with setting up of Multidisciplinary Education and Research Universities (MERUs) [2].

In this global pledge for equitable quality education, NEP 2020 is one of the most ambitious national reforms in education, and has implications for professional health education governed by Statutory Bodies like the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI). The study falls within a growing literature on health professions education research that uses systematic qualitative methods to analyse the extent and quality of curriculum policy implementation, thereby creating empirical evidence of the “how” and “how well” of the process of curriculum policy implementation in a regulated clinical training environment.

Curricular reform is a departure from strict disciplinary boundaries and instead encourages critical thinking, problem-solving and experiential learning. The policy includes a flexible degree structure, Academic Bank of Credit and a criterion-based grading structure. It makes excellent research a pre-requisite of excellent education and establishes the National Research Foundation (NRF) to fund and stimulate excellent, peer-reviewed research in all fields. Faculty are empowered with autonomy in their teaching and pedagogical approaches, and a merit-based framework for tenure, promotion and remuneration will be established.

The regulatory architecture of higher education is such that certain functions, such as regulation, accreditation, funding and academic standard setting, are undertaken by independent bodies who work under the umbrella of a single institution, as outlined previously. The policy’s focus is on equitable access to quality education and places a great emphasis on socio-economic disadvantaged groups (SEDGs) and persons with disabilities (PWD). The implications of the NEP 2020 are wide-ranging across disciplines and its integration into professional health education, including its integration into the medical and allied health sciences, is especially relevant in the context of the imperative to prepare efficient and adaptable professionals to meet the rapidly changing healthcare landscape [3].

June 1993, amended in 2000, the RCI is the primary governing body which monitors quality and accountability of rehabilitation and professional training in India. Its mandate includes four areas: (1) Regulation and Monitoring-the quality of services provided to persons with disabilities; (2) Syllabi Standardisation-the standardisation of all rehabilitation and special education courses; (3) Registration-the compilation of a Central Rehabilitation Register of all individuals who are qualified to provide services in the sector; and (4) Enforcement-the imposition of penalties on individuals for providing services without the necessary training. Through its mandate, the RCI is recognised as the statutory authority setting professional standards for key rehabilitation courses, including Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology.

Assuming the role of steward of standard-setting, the RCI continuously refines training curricula to keep contemporary quality. An examination of the RCI’s programme statements, including that of the Bachelor of Audiology and Speech - Language Pathology (B. ASLP), shows that there is an intention to produce professionals with basic competencies, which start with an understanding of foundational concepts in speech, language, communication, hearing and disability, skills to screen and to assess and diagnose various disorders across the life span; and practical skills in managing rehabilitation programmes, including counselling to patients and families. The RCI serves as the top body that ensures quality and accountability in the provision of rehabilitation services and professional training. One of it’s principal mandates is syllabi standardisation. This mandate places the RCI as the sole authority in setting the pedagogical and professional standards for Audiology and Speech - Language Pathology courses. The process of standardisation is rigorous to ensure that personnel are competent and qualified both in clinical practice and academic research within all RCI co-ordinated training programmes.

Consequently, the RCI updated the syllabi for the B. ASLP, MSc in SLP and MSc in Audiology in the year 2024 in line with the NEP 2020. This revision was made through an appeal to the principles of academic flexibility, holistic learning, and learner autonomy. Students are gradually exposed to the vision of the NEP, although the level of enjoyment of its benefits varies with different factors including the implementation of essential features of the NEP, such as academic flexibility, holism and multidisciplinary learning, and learner autonomy. New curricula contain varying mixtures of “major” and “minor” courses in accordance with the University Grants Commission (UGC) National Credit Framework (NCrF). Within the NCrF, major indicates a primary area of study with a higher credit load than mandatory courses, while a minor is an optional area with lesser credits leading to academic breadth. While these definitions offer flexibility and expose students in various disciplines, the practical applicability of these definitions within RCI syllabi is yet to be fully understood. For example, the way that voluntary optional courses and compulsory electives are placed in their present courses of study may follow or not follow the intent of the NCrF. Additionally, the specific mechanisms by which students can complete programmes and transfer credits are yet to be clarified. Consequently, the fit of these newly modified syllabi to the precepts of the NEP is also an open question, especially in medical, rehabilitation and allied health professional education.

The RCI uses the term ‘syllabus’ (plural: ‘syllabi’) to denote a detailed programme-level curriculum document and not an individual course outline by an instructor. Every RCI syllabus document contains: (a) programme objectives and graduate outcomes for the entire degree programme; (b) the complete course list (including credit allocation) for the entire degree programme; (c) content outlines (course-by-course) for each course; (d) practical and clinical training requirements; and (e) assessment modalities (theory examinations, clinical assessments, dissertation requirements). These documents are prescribed by RCI and must be followed equally by all the recognised training institutes in India, which is equivalent to a ‘national curriculum framework’ or ‘programme curriculum map’” as it is called in the international world.

In the studies of how NEP implementation occurred in traditional or liberal education disciplines, the emphasis has traditionally been on curricular openness and credit-based mobility [4]. However, ASLP has some special issues that require consideration. Chief among these is the focus on clinical proficiency, along with rigorous government regulations and accreditation requirements, which may impede curricular flexibility. Therefore, a detailed analysis of the NEP 2020 framework based on the RCI’s updated syllabi is important.

Research into the implementation of the NEP in Indian medical, rehabilitationand health professions education is still scarce. Existing literature is largely in the form of commentaries and institution-level responses to issues such as credit transfer, the need for continuous professional development across disciplines and the integration of e-learning with traditional classroom instruction [5, 6]. Nonetheless, there are still very few discipline-specific studies. In the field of ASLP education, there is a notable absence of scholarly assessment for curriculum congruence with national reforms and the research gap is substantial. This is especially concerning in light of the fact that communication and language sciences are situated at the intersection of education and public health and the need for evidence based and globally recognised training in ASLP [7].

The present study, therefore, attempts to qualitatively evaluate the revamped curricula for the B. ASLP, MSc (SLP) and MSc (Aud) programmes in the light of NEP 2020 using the Theory of Change (ToC) framework as an organising tool. Theory of Change (ToC) is a planning and evaluation tool that visualises the ‘why’ chain of how inputs and activities are expected to create intended outcomes [8]. ToC is a way of thinking that makes it possible to identify assumptions and enabling conditions (the mediating processes) necessary for change to occur, whereas, linear logic models do not do this. In the process of curriculum reform, ToC enables analysts to shift from asking themselves if a policy has been executed (structural compliance) to if the conditions for meaningful execution exist (operational readiness). In this study, ToC is used as an analytical scaffold, namely, deductive coding is structured into inputs (NEP policy requirements), mediating processes (curricular structures and pedagogies), and outcomes (professional attributes of graduates), and as a visual synthesis tool (Fig. 2). Three core domains were selected for analysis: philosophical values (e.g. empathy, environmental ethics, cultural sensitivity), structural changes in the syllabus (flexibility of curricular design) and pedagogical strategies.

The choice to base the analysis of RCI syllabi on these three domains, is based on the need to discuss the NEP’s mandate for systemic and profound transformation in specialised professional education. The inclusion of philosophical orientation permits to assess the alignment with the overarching vision of the policy, going beyond the cognitive competences, also including the ethical grounding and the holism of the future professionals. Structural features are focused on the flexibility architecture of the policy, including looking for mechanisms for mandatory implementation through e.g. multidisciplinary combinations, credit systems, a move away from rigid silos - thus setting out the new academic architecture. Pedagogical strategies target the major educational processes, alluding to the necessary shift from rote learning to permanent and competency-based assessment and technology-enhanced teaching techniques by experience that are the mainstay of the construction and validation of the professional competence. With the combination of these interrelated lenses, the research will have a holistic analysis of the goals of the policy, the structural processes of policy implementation, and the real working environment in the classroom.

The RCI syllabi have been updated to include various assessment approaches to measure theoretical knowledge and/or clinical skills. This study offers evidence to support the next cycle of revising NEP 2020; it also offers evidence that will help programme directors enhance assessment practice, covering aspects of NEP 2020 that include both formative and portfolio-based assessments and technology-enhanced assessments. The 2024 RCI syllabus is a conscious and significant effort to include structural provisions of NEP 2020 like credit-based design, major and minor course frameworks and aligning with UGC National Credit Framework (NCrF). The study focuses on one of the core questions: whether these structural measures have been fully implemented, including the implementation of the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) and the inter-institutional flexibility.An early draft of the work was delivered on the First International Conference on Accreditation, Ranking and Quality (ARQ) in Higher Education held by the Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education and Research (SRIHER) in Chennai in June 2025. The global conference, dedicated to the change of curriculum and policy alignment in higher education, could represent productive feedback which significantly improved this manuscript [9].

The educational valuation of curriculum alignment in professional health teaching is significant. The fidelity of the programme-level syllabus to the philosophy, structure and pedagogical goals of national policy is important in order to produce graduates who are clinically competent, socially responsive, self-directed and prepared for the rapidly changing healthcare environment. This study is driven by the need to articulate these educational stakes in a systematic manner by using an alignment analysis, which will serve as evidence to inform the ongoing evolution of the ASLP education in India for the RCI, the programme directors and otherregulatory bodies (National Council of Allied Health Professionals, National Medical Commission, etc.). This study attempts to resolve a significant gap in the literature by providing a qualitative and theory-based study to determine how the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 corresponds to the updated syllabi of Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology (ASLP) training programmes. However, by focusing on the context of regulatory body and a critical analysis of important structural and pedagogical elements, the current study fulfills the growing literature on health education reforms in India and offers a framework that can be implemented to similar reviews in other related health fields.

We expected to see more alignment in structural than in philosophical and pedagogical domains, based on the pattern of structural responsiveness that preceded philosophical and pedagogical change in similar regulatory curriculum reform efforts [10–13].

Study design

In this study, the qualitative document analysis (QDA) was used to assess the suitability of the 2024 Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI) syllabi on Bachelor of Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology (B. ASLP), MSc (Audiology), and MSc (Speech-Language Pathology) as per the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020. The reason why QDA was selected is its ability to interpret policy-oriented textual data systematically within an interpretivist paradigm, which focuses on contextual and socio-political aspects.

Ethical considerations

This QDA represents an initial step within a larger multi-institutional study investigating the implementation of the revised RCI syllabus.

Clinical trial number: not applicable.

Data sources

The main sources of data included NEP 2020 policy document and RCI syllabi of the B. ASLP, MSc (Audiology), and MSc (Speech-Language Pathology) and were supported with the UGC National Credit Framework (NCrF) and the RCI NEP alignment presentation [2, 14–16].

Analytical framework: bowen’s document analysis

The five-step model presented by Bowen [17] was used to conduct the analysis by a deductive approach to focus on objectives of higher education aligned to NEP 2020 for higher education (Fig. 1).

This analysis started by carrying out a preliminary skimming of all the documents in order to develop the familiarity with the structural nature of the documents and their areas of focus, and then the process of reading the documents meticulously and coming up with central themes, sections that make up the document and the objectives that are being articulated.

Deductive coding was thus used to fit the material into three domains that reflected the National Education Policy (NEP): (1) philosophical orientation (e.g., equity, ethics), (2) structural characteristics (e.g., modularity, Academic Bank of Credits) and (3) pedagogical strategies (e.g., digital tools, formative assessments). The interpretative part of the investigation focused on analysing the extent to which the 2024 syllabi reflect the NEP 2020 purpose, based on the outlined domains, coded. Deductive Domains to be evaluated in QDA: Philosophical Orientation: the existence or the lack of values such as empathy, equity, professional ethics, cultural sensitivity, learner autonomy and environmental awareness as part of curriculum goals, learning outcomes, and assessment standards; explicit articulation of such underlying values in the RCI syllabi (e.g., is empathy mentioned among graduate attributes or ethical reasoning in exams compulsory? ). Structural Features: adoption of modular curriculum design, credit-based system (majors/minors), and allusions to the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC); flexibility in terms of entry and exit points, credit systems based on choice, multidisciplinary and holistic delivery, and combination of theory-clinical models. Analysis of the alignment with the UGC/NCrF standards of structure and flexibility and multidisciplinary pathways as required by NEP. Pedagogical practices: integration (or lack of it) of digital learning systems (SWAYAM, DIKSHA), instruction based on simulations, and instruction based on technology use; focus on assessment modes such as summative (examinations) and formative (continuous evaluation, portfolios, clinical logs) and the application of learner centred pedagogies such as enquiry based learning, reflective practice and innovative instruction. The codes were rated on explicitness, depth of amalgamation, and consistency between undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) syllabi, where alignment was rated as strong (concepts are explicitly stated, operationalised and repeated across levels), emerging concept (concepts are mentioned but not properly embedded within outcomes or assessments) and partial concept (concepts are not present or tokenistic).

Lastly, the synthesis of findings included internal triangulation and author consensus, according to the best practices recommended by Bowen [17], to generate actionable findings basing on a Theory of Change framework that coded NEP 2020 outcomes (Fig. 2).

Analytical procedure and trustworthiness

The three-level rubric was pre-established using the explicit language of NEP 2020 and the criteria set up in Bowen’s document analysis framework [17] for the assignment of an alignment rating. Strong Alignment refers to the explicit naming, operation and consistent use of an NEP construct in all three syllabi, with the addition of assessment criteria. Partial Alignment means that one or two syllabi contain the construct but there are no measurable outcomes or assessment links. Emerging Alignment is when the construct is referenced in a non-explicit or rhetorical way only, with no structured embedding in the course objectives, learning outcomes or assessments.

Primary coding was done by both KV (Assistant Professor, Speech-Language Pathology, SRFASLP) and PB (Professor and Principal, SRFASLP) with both being doctoral degree holders with active clinical and academic experience in ASLP. Coded outputs were independently audited by RCP (Professor & Head, Speech-Language Pathology, SRFASLP). All three authors are registered rehabilitation practitioners with more than 15 years (KV) and 25 years (PB and RCP) of experience, who made their content-level decisions in accordance with the RCI, which guarantees content-level validity of coding decisions.

Triangulation was done using three methods: (i) cross-coder comparison, wherein each section of the syllabus was coded independently by KV and PB before comparison of coding of the assignments; (ii) cross-document triangulation, comparisons of coding across all three syllabi (B. ASLP, MSc (Aud), MSc (SLP); (iii) cross-referencing with the literature, wherein the findings of the coding were compared with the existing literature on NEP implementation in the Indian health professions education.

If there was a difference between the two codes then the item was identified and discussed at a structured consensus meeting between the two codes, and an agreement reached.

RCP was called in as an arbitrator in less than 10% of the coded units. Only when all three authors agreed on the rating of final alignment was it included. The study was supported by coded excerpts which can be obtained from the corresponding author upon request.

Results

The alignment study of the 2024 RCI syllabi for Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology programs with NEP 2020 yielded different results across three domains: philosophical orientation, structural features, and pedagogical strategies. The ideas from the NEP 2020 were compared with the RCI syllabi. A thematic matrix with three domains was created to show where the two documents were similar and where they differed. These interpretations were confirmed through internal consensus among the authors and were supported by the literature on NEP implementation in professional education.

Philosophical orientation: emerging alignment

For example, the BASLP syllabus states under Programme Objectives: ‘To develop professional ethics and values in students’ However, there is no specific learning outcome, assessment rubric or graduate attribute descriptor that directly equates with this aspiration. Philosophical alignment is not a feature on any of the three syllabi, as the terms ‘cultural competence’, ‘social accountability’ and ‘health equity’ are not present. The National Education Policy 2020 has philosophical underpinnings that emphasize on learner autonomy, universal education, compassion to others, peaceful coexistence with nature and holistic development. The RCI syllabi show emerging alignment by incorporating professional ethics, community orientation and role-based clinical practice.

Structural features: strong alignment

The syllabus structure decisively and straightforwardly corroborates the NEP’s intention of academic flexibility and modularity. The syllabi of both UG and PG programmes are based on a modular credit system, offer combinations of major and minor subjects, and connect theory with practice. Moreover, the ABC is mentioned in all syllabi, which places the learning and credit transfer concepts very close to multi-entry and multi-exit options, lifelong learning, and credit portability. Nonetheless, the documents still do not disclose the exact mechanisms for making the ABC operational, including credit transfer procedures, interoperability between institutions and formal recognition frameworks. However, the implication is that the curriculum achieves strong alignment through its modular design and formal references to NEP structures, but it still falls short of full implementation. This is similar to the broader sectoral challenges identified in recent health-education reports [10]. However, because modular design and credit planning are uniformly applied across programs, the domain can still be classified as strongly aligned.

Pedagogical strategies: partial alignment

The RCI syllabi maintain a high level of clinical relevance pedagogically, based on requisite supervised practicum, fieldwork and dissertation elements. However, the methodologies of teaching and learning are still largely based on the traditional paradigms. The digital transformation of the NEP is a very relevant strength, but it is not yet implemented through digital learning options like SWAYAM and DIKSHA, or even through simulation-based learning platforms. Assessment models are mostly summative, which include final examinations, clinical posting assessments and research dissertation defenses. Formative assessment, ongoing assessment, and portfolio-based systems are introduced/implemented at the lowest level and on a nascent level. This sparsity highlights a sharp contrast to the NEP-imagined change to adaptatory, competency-based assessment and learner-focused pedagogies. Notably, the lack of reflective practice, self-assessment, and the lack of digital literacy opportunities are amongst some of the gaps that require change via innovation. A comparative study of NEP-congruent curricular programs in allied health areas, as reported by Singh and Pal [11] and Dass [18] indicated key tools, to wit, integrated e-portfolios, simulation modules, and formative assessment rubrics, which are effective in alleviating the described gap. As a result, despite the level of conformity in the pedagogical strategies, there is still a significant area of pedagogical innovation in the future.

The NEP 2020 mandates and the 2024 RCI syllabi were analyzed using a thematic matrix to introduce the coherence between both syllabi. The application of the QDA framework developed by Bowen in the coding conducted in each syllabus focused on the occurrence, applicability, and explicitness of NEP-consistent constructs in three meta-domains, which were Philosophical Orientation, Structural Features, and Pedagogical Strategies. Discussion

Thematic matrix from Table 1 above illustrates alignment ratings for the three domains. These findings are interpreted in the context of the Theory of Change framework, starting with each domain, and then with the impact of those findings on policy-practice coherence in ASLP education. The differential preparedness with respect to the various attributes of alignment between the 2024 RCI syllabi and NEP 2020 points to the fact that structural responsiveness has outpaced pedagogical transformation and philosophical embedding. This uneven progress epitomizes what may be described as a “policy lag,” or prioritization of regulatory compliance within structural arrangements, with cultural and pedagogical adaptation lagging within professional education. Such differentiation is not unique to the RCI, or the allied health sector in India, but has been documented in several efforts at health curriculum reform around the world.

As Singh and Pal [11] point out, this widens the dilemma of balancing the regulatory mandates with the day-to-day realities of academia. While creditably the RCI began credit-based modular restructuring in congruence with the NEP vision, alignment across values and in instructional innovation are still in their infancy revealing deeper systemic tensions in regulated health professions education.

Philosophical orientation

The NEP calls for holistic development of the graduates by equity, empathy, critical reasoning and respect for values embedded in the Indian Constitution. By contrast, the RCI syllabi address “ethics and professionalism” only within discrete course modules, without framing these as foundational, assessable graduate attributes. For example, in the 2024 B. ASLP syllabus “ethical practice is mentioned, but there are no corresponding outcome measures around measuring empathy or cultural sensitivity.” As Dass [18] indicates, it is due to the absence of such values in rubrics or portfolio-based assessments that they often leave them rhetorical. This omission is all the more salient, given that one of the rapidly developing consensuses in current frameworks for health education includes health equity, anti-racism and social responsibility as a core curricular anchor points [19, 20]. Lester [21] suggested that professional competence should be built not only through the transmission of skills but by promoting reflective, ethical reasoning in the context of changing social situations. The ToC framework of Clark and Taplin [8] underscores that value translation requires more than policy inputs; it calls for deliberate process steps-such as explicit phrasing of learning outcomes, targeted faculty development, and systematic reflective audits-that embed these values throughout the curriculum. The NEP’s mandate to “create engaged, productive, and contributing citizens” [2] is yet to be translated into the curriculum of the RCI which continues to focus on creating functional clinicians.

Structural features

Structurally, the syllabi represent great advances in semester- and modular-type courses with specific credit loads. This coherence in structure is very much in line with what the NEP recommends regarding curriculum design that is based on credit and the Academic Bank of Credit (ABC) model. However, implementation has been symbolic; the RCI syllabi make references to the ABC but have no mechanism for credit portability, lateral movement and inter-university equivalence. While the explicit mention of the Academic Bank of Credit (ABC) in the syllabi aligns with current UGC guidance, practical details, such as mechanisms for credit transfer or recognition of prior learning, are absent. This observation is evident in comparative studies of NEP adoption in the health sciences [10], where symbolic references to progressive structures often precede the establishment of robust operating procedures and faculty readiness.

Another aspect of the syllabus is that the ‘major’ and ‘minor’ courses have been identified, however, the real essence of its application as ‘major or minor’ course leading to completion of programme and options of higher studies remains vague and unclear. Typically, ‘major’ courses must cover a central area of study, and because of this, must cover the majority of courses in the same subject. To graduate, students also have to have other required classes that support the major classes and fulfill the general curriculum requirements. Additionally, the possibility of choosing electives, which are courses that can be chosen according to the interests of the students, gives a complete freedom to diversify. A ‘minor’ is typically an optional secondary area of focus that requires fewer courses than a ‘major’. Students may choose a minor to complement their major or to explore a different passion. If one does not choose a minor, they can still take classes in that subject without completing all the minor requirements to earn the degree. A compulsory elective (however, not clearly defined in this version of the syllabus) stated in the curriculum again defies the essence of the application of ‘elective’ courses to the graduate degree. These disjunctions between policy and execution echo broader critiques that the NEP’s modular promises remain bureaucratically unrealised in clinical education contexts.

In principle, the adoption of ‘Major’ and ‘Minor’ classifications is in keeping with the UGC National Credit Framework (NCRF) wherein the former is the primary disciplinary aspect and the latter is the route for interdisciplinary diversification. However, the operational mechanics of these streams with respect to the RCI context is undefined. The syllabus currently does not specify the protocols for credit distribution, elective choice and the criteria for the award of a Minor degree and the practical application of these flexible pathways remains unclear. It is, therefore, very important to differentiate between architectural compliance and operational readiness. While the syllabus succeeds in strong alignment in the structural design (the ‘Inputs’), the Theory of Change analysis shows that the mediating conditions, in this case, of clear guidelines for implementation and institutional capacity, are the critical bottleneck preventing immediate transformation. This finding is reminiscent of the recent analysis of NEP implementation in pharmacy and general higher education where lack of coherence between policy design and ground realities, specifically regional infrastructure gaps and faculty readiness have been cited as a major obstacle to reform [12, 13].

Most people across the world are coming to appreciate the use of ToC in curriculum reform [8], because it forces stakeholders to articulate assumptions, search and remove bottlenecks, and create a feedback loop that is iterative.

ToC points out that despite carefully planned structural input, even in the case of lacking deliberate strategies to favor philosophical and pedagogical change, the case may not have an impactful change. In this way, ToC can streamline the clarification of what is missing along with the design of the further intervention, including faculty workshops, policy audits, or digital integration pilots that are likely to increase the fidelity and depth of NEP implementation in RCI-regulated programmes.

Actionable recommendations and identified roadblocks

The following specific impediments and suggestions are identified for boosting alignment and impact.

Roadblock 1: Lack of operationalisation of ABC: The RCI should develop an addendum that mentions credit transfer procedures, and recognition of prior learning criteria and inter-institutional equivalence mechanisms that are compatible with the UGC's Academic Bank of Credits portal. Road Block 2: Pedagogical inertia: Faculty development workshops are recommended which involve SWAYAM/DIKSHA content teams, so as to create digital literacy in ASLP faculty before mandating the integration of digital tools in the syllabus. Roadblock 3: The RCI needs to establish a Graduate Attribute Framework for ASLP (akin to what is done in medicine and nursing) that aligns attributes (such as empathy, cultural competence and social accountability) with measurable programme learning outcomes. Roadblock 4: Phased introduction of portfolio-based clinical logs and structured reflective journals to help transition towards a reliance on formative assessment, consistent with models that are currently used throughout the world in RCI recognised programmes.

Conclusion

The policy-implementation lag is recognized in the literature, but this study contributes with several specific findings which go beyond documenting its occurrence. Firstly, it generates the first alignment profile of the ASLP with its domains which will set an empirical benchmark on which future revisions of RCI syllabus can be measured. Second, the use of the Theory of Change framework provides opportunities for specific actionable information about which mediating conditions are missing, not simply that implementation is lacking. Third, the analytical framework (QDA + ToC) is generalisable: any allied health regulatory body interested in analysing its curriculum in the context of evaluation of curriculum against NEP 2020 or any other national policy change can adapt this methodology. Fourth, the findings have direct implications for the next revision cycle of the RCI, programme directors who are adapting the RCI, reviewers/inspectors who are evaluating programme quality based on national standards, and RCI authors.In order to achieve the high levels of congruence between the RCI syllabi and the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, such a shift as a change into the compliant mode instead of a transformation is necessitated. Curriculum reform, in the Theory of Change paradigm, is essentially iterative, requiring cycles of critical reflection, hypothesis testing, and feedback. To attain deep-seated congruence the syllabi would have to incorporate core graduate attributes in form of empathy, ethics, and autonomy in clear learning outcomes and assessment measures. The Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) should be realized by institutions with well-defined operational structures that provide an easy entry exit model.

An intrinsic limitation of document analysis as a method is that it reflects the prescribed curriculum (RCI’s on paper), not the enacted curriculum, which is the actual pedagogical practice, faculty behaviours, and institutional resources that shape student experiences. Since the variation between what the syllabus specifies and what is actually offered by individual institutions is quite large, especially in terms of clinical placement capacity, faculty qualification and infrastructure, there may be significant variation between what is offered and what is prescribed in the syllabus. The document level analysis should be supported by institutional surveys, faculty interviews and direct observation of the classroom to assess the actual implementation of the prescribed curriculum.

The integration of digital technologies, including simulation tools, SWAYAM products, and multimodal formative assessment, is also crucial to the promotion of pedagogical innovation. Finally, the effectiveness of such reform is not only based on reported alignment of syllabus but faculty willingness and institutional dedication to turn vision of the policy into practical action.
