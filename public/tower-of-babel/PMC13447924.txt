Cyber resilience across domains: an in-depth exploration of cybersecurity practices and paradigms from the home environment to Operational Technology (OT)
Authors: T Sakthivel, K Balasubramanian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe intersection of smart home Internet of Things (IoT) devices, enterprise Information Technology (IT) infrastructures and Operational Technology (OT) systems has greatly expanded the cyberattack surface, opening up smart environments to advanced persistent threats (APTs), ransomware, insider attacks and OT protocol exploits. Current cyber security solutions are largely domain specific, and do not have a unified approach for cross domain threat correlation, adaptive response orchestration and proactive cyber resilience. In this regard, this study introduces a novel framework called Cross-Domain Cyber Resilience Framework (CD-CRF) that combines multi-domain telemetry fusion, behavioral anomaly detection, intelligent threat deduction, probabilistic risk scoring, and adaptive response orchestration under a single architecture. The framework utilizes signature independent behavioral analytics and cross domain threat correlation with identification of complex multi-stage attacks in heterogeneous Home, IT and OT environments. Experimental assessment was performed with a set of heterogenous cybersecurity data sets, including intrusion detection logs, traces of ransomware activities, IoT botnet traffic, SCADA/ICS telemetry, authentication data and communication data for malware. The proposed CD-CRF successfully reduced the false positive rate to 1.8%, and attained a detection accuracy of 98.6%, threat prediction rate of 94.8% and a cross-domain correlation efficiency of 97.1%. Moreover, it had a response time of 120ms, about 50% faster mitigation than traditional methods, and statistically significantly outperformed GCSM and CAT and TinyML models (p < 0.05). The results show that CD-CRF improves cyber situational awareness, it increases the ability to detect threats early, it decreases operational risks, and it offers scalable, adaptive cyber resilience solution to converged IT–OT environments.Keywords: Cyber resilience, Cross-domain security, Operational technology security, Behavioral anomaly detection, Advanced persistent threats, Proactive cyber defenseSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction: the era of cyber convergence

Driven by the rapid development of digital technologies, modern cyber-physical ecosystems have transformed and integrated smart homes, enterprise IT infrastructures, and OT landscapes into connected digital spaces1. In fact, the Internet of Things (IoT) devices are already at home, and can be used for lighting, HVAC, surveillance cameras and smart appliances automation2. The growing trend of distributed cloud platforms, virtualization systems and remote access systems is enabling business organizations to achieve greater operational efficiency3. It is clear that the autonomous operation of industrial systems is controlled by the OTEs in all the sectors of the power and energy, manufacturing, transportation, and utility sectors4. Although this digital transformation has brought many advantages in terms of scalability, automation and real-time monitoring, it’s also creating more of a cyberattack surface and systemic vulnerabilities5.

Cross-domain cyber resilience

A Cyber Resilience is more than just a Cyber Security; it is the capacity to proactively manage, resist, react and recover from cyber incidents and continue operations6. Resilience needs to be pushed in multi-domain systems with various systems of differing priorities and constraints7. Besides the convenience and connectivity that is provided by home networks, enterprise IT systems are focused on data integrity and data confidentiality, and OT infrastructures are focused on availability and safety8—which is a significant challenge for architectural and operational coordination in protection9.

Convergence of home, IT, and OT ecosystems

The days of traditional systems, networks and devices are coming to an end with the rise of cloud integration, the Internet of Things and the remote connection models of home, IT and OT all becoming one10. Weakened home IoT devices can be a gateway into enterprise networks, and IT system failures can propagate to OT systems, impacting physical operations11. The outcome of this intersection is complex interdependencies that can mean weaknesses in one aspect can be transmitted to other aspects. Therefore, single security measures cannot deal with multi-domain propagation of threats12.

Escalation of advanced cross-domain threats

Today the cyber adversary uses sophisticated tools such as APTs, ransomware, supply chain attacks and zero-day exploits13. Usually these attacks are multi-stage, stealthy, intrusions, which cannot be detected by traditional signature based detection techniques14. As attackers are able to traverse between the IT and OT networks, operational and financial risks are also heightened15. Cyberattacks are becoming more automated and sophisticated, and thus requires more intelligent and adaptive defence mechanism16.

Need for unified deductive and preventive defense

However, even if there are recent developments in the field of IoT security, enterprise defense, and industrial cybersecurity, most of the existing solutions are still disjointed and ineffective17. Unified threat detection, accurate identification, and proactive prevention mechanisms are urgently needed that can detect behavioral anomalies among the different domains18. Proposes a CD-CRF that combines multi-layer behavioral analytics, intelligent threat deduction and orchestration of adaptive responses19. The solution under discussion enhances situational awareness, reduces false alarm rate, and offers a single solution for home, IT and OT interconnected ecosystems to be more proactive when it comes to cyber resilience20.

Contributions

While substantial progress has been made in IoT security, enterprise cybersecurity, and OT protection, most of the solutions proposed so far, such as the Global Cyber Security Model (GCSM), TinyML-based frameworks, and Cybersecurity Awareness and Training (CAT), lack a unified cross-domain cyber resilience. GCSM is mainly concerned with organization awareness and managerial governance, CAT with cybersecurity education and workforce preparedness and TinyML-based methods with lightweight anomaly detection with limited contextual visibility. Such methods are not equipped with built-in multi-domain telemetry fusion, intelligent cross-domain threat correlation, adaptive response orchestration, and inter-connected Home IoT, Enterprise IT, and OT protection capabilities. Therefore, the current study introduces a novel Cross-Domain Cyber Resilience Framework (CD-CRF) consisting of four components: behavioral anomaly detection, probabilistic risk assessment, intelligent threat deduction, and adaptive response orchestration, all integrated into a single architecture that also includes a continuous learning component. One of the biggest innovations of CD-CRF is its ability to conduct signature-independent behavioral analytics and real-time threat correlation across disparate domains, detecting and mitigating multi-stage attacks, advanced persistent threats, ransomware, insider threats and OT-specific exploits. CD-CRF provides proactive defense, automated containment, cross-domain situational awareness with a scalable structure, which substantially enhances detection accuracy, decrease false positive and response time and increase the operational resilience of the enterprise’s cybersecurity beyond the ability of the current cybersecurity model (Table 1).

Unlike the typical TinyML-based approaches which are optimised to detect threats in a lightweight fashion and from limited contextual visibility, the proposed framework is designed to perform unified multi-domain telemetry fusion and coordinated threat inference across heterogeneous environments. Current cybersecurity frameworks are primarily designed for different cyber environments, including IoT, Healthcare, Enterprise IT, and OT, with little support for a unified, cross-domain approach to protection. However, while frameworks such as GCSM and CAT are mostly concerned with the awareness and organizational aspects, TinyML and AI-driven models are more targeted at lightweight anomaly detection in a specific domain. Proposed cross-domain cyber resilience framework (CD-CRF)

The proposed CD-CRF endows one security architecture capable of stitching together the home security, enterprise IT security and OT security into a unified security environment. The framework will be structured as a multi-layered architecture with four modules related to telemetry fusion, behavioural intelligence, threat deduction, precision identification, proactive response and continuous learning.

Behavioral Intelligence involves the analysis of historical and real-time patterns of user, device, application, network traffic, or industrial processes to create dynamic baseline profiles and determine deviations from them that can be used to detect malicious activity. Intelligent Threat Deduction refers to the ability to identify potential cyber threat, based on the correlation of multi-domain telemetry data, anomaly indicators, contextual information, and probabilistic risk assessment, thereby uncovering hidden relationships in attacks and multi-stage intrusion campaigns that might not be caught by signature-based means. Precision Attack Identification is the ability to accurately identify and validate detected anomalies through risk-scoring, behavioral signatures, threat intelligence and contextual verification technologies to differentiate between true threats and false alarms. Together, they provide a proactive approach to cyber defense by converting raw telemetry data into actionable security intelligence, boosting accuracy in detection and minimizing false-positive alerts, thus helping to build greater cross-domain cyber resilience in Home IoT, Enterprise IT and Operational Technology (OT).

CD-CRF architecture.

The proposed CD-CRF has been designed with implementation oriented modular components which will help to deploy it in Home, IT and OT environments. The framework leverages standard communication protocols like MQTT, Modbus, TCP/IP, and REST based APIs to collect logs, network flows, authentication records, IoT traffic and OT sensor data using distributed telemetry collectors. Collected data is normalized in a centralized normalization engine and sent to the behavioral analytics and threat deduction modules that are deployed on edge–cloud infrastructure. A machine learning anomaly detection and probabilistic risk-scoring models are combined with automated response orchestration for firewall updates, IoT isolation, network segmentation and privilege control. The modular architecture allows to achieve interoperability, scalability and real-time coordinated defense across the cyber-physical infrastructures composed of heterogeneous infrastructures, which enhances the practical feasibility and deployability of the proposed framework.

Multi-domain data acquisition and normalization

This layer gathers heterogeneous data from IoT devices, routers, servers, cloud platforms, firewalls, PLCs, SCADA systems, and industrial sensors. Available data sources include system logs, NetFlow logs, command sequences, authentication logs, and process telemetry. These datasets have different structures and protocols, so a normalization mechanism unifies the attributes, timestamps, and metadata into a single schema. To ensure cross-domain analytics compatibility, noise filtering, feature extraction, and encoding methods are used. A single data foundation enables accurate correlation of Home, IT, and OT ecosystems.

Behavioral modeling and baselining

The framework forms dynamic behavior baselines for users, devices, applications, and industrial processes. The algorithms of statistical and machine learning analyze past activity to determine the usual mode of operation. Profiles are also constantly updated based on acceptable differences in usage and workload. In home networks, the model monitors the frequency and access behavior of IoT communication; in IT systems, it monitors login and log-out patterns; and in OT systems, it monitors control command and sensor timing sequences. These thresholds serve as a reference point for identifying abnormalities that indicate a cyber threat.

Intelligent threat deduction engine

Multi-layer anomaly detection and cross domain event correlation are performed by the Intelligent Threat Deduction Engine. It fuses signature-free analytics with behavioral deviation scoring to uncover stealthy and multi-stage attacks. They are the targets on which the engine can progress the attack. It detects lateral movement, privilege escalation, and coordinated intrusion campaigns by analyzing the temporal and contextual relationships that can span home networks and OT infrastructures.

Precision attack identification module

Once anomalies are detected, the Precision Attack Identification Module detects and authenticates those anomalies with risk-scores and pattern recognition. The module filters out false positives and real intrusions by using contextual verification and integration with threat intelligence. Attack attribution systems analyze IoCs and behavioral signatures, as well as identify ransomware, APTs, insider misuse and zero-day attacks. An accuracy-based approach that reduces false alarms and maintains high detection rates.

Preventive layer and response to adaptation layer

When a threat is confirmed, the framework triggers automated containment and mitigation plans. These include device isolation, dynamic firewall updates, granted privileges, and network segmentation. Fail-safe mechanisms are employed in OT environments to ensure operational stability during incident response. Mitigation strategies employed by adaptive policy orchestration are selected based on risk severity and system criticality. Such a proactive approach reduces response time and eliminates cross-domain failures.

Cross-domain orchestration and continuous learning

The orchestration layer provides coordinated defense alignment of ecosystems in the home, IT, and OT. It coordinates alerts, policies, and mitigation measures to ensure situational awareness. An ongoing learning module adds threat intelligence feeds and feedback of past incidents to train detection models. This is a cyclical process of enhancing long-term resilience to cyberattacks, preparing the system to counter new and evolving attack vectors.

Cross-Domain Cyber Resilience Framework (CD-CRF).

The suggested CD-CRF algorithm 1 synthesizes multi-domain data (Home, IT, and OT) and transforms them into a single feature space. It forms behavioral baselines and employs multi-head attention to form inter-domain correlations and anomalies. Anomaly magnitude and sigmoid activation are used to calculate a risk score. The system also categorizes threats based on an established threshold and enables proactive response measures to foster adaptive cyber resilience.

Machine learning configuration and training procedure

The proposed CD-CRF uses a cross-domain threat identification technique that combines the hybrid machine learning approach of behavioral modeling, anomaly detection, and probabilistic risk scoring. The behavioral modeling is implemented with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, which is capable of learning temporal relationships in authentication logs, IoT communication logs, network traffic, sensor readings, and command sequences in the OT. The LSTM architecture has two hidden layers with 128 and 64 neurons, respectively, as well as followed by a fully connected dense layer and sigmoid activation function for anomaly probability estimation. A dropout rate of 0.3 is put in between hidden layers, which is used to prevent overfitting.

The framework calculates the deviation from behavioral norms as anomaly scores, which are calculated by comparing the current telemetry vector with adaptive behavioral baseline profiles, for anomaly detection. Normalized Euclidean distance and contextual threat indicators are used to calculate the anomaly score. Anomaly magnitude, domain criticality weights and threat-intelligence information are then combined to create a single risk value per event, which forms the basis for probabilistic risk scoring.

It has been implemented using TensorFlow and trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001. Binary cross-entropy was used as the loss function for anomaly classification. The number of training epochs used in the model was 100 with early stopping to avoid overfitting, and the batch size was 64. The hyperparameters were optimized by grid-search over the validation accuracy and false-positive rate. The learning rate, contextual threat coefficient, and threat-intelligence integration coefficient were optimized at α = 0.05, β = 0.6, and γ = 0.4, respectively.

The data set was split into 80% training and 20% testing data, and 5-fold cross-validation was utilized to enhance the model performance in terms of generalizability and robustness. Feature normalization, categorical feature encoding with one hot encoding and numerical feature encoding with min-max scaling were used to optimize the performance. The overall complexity of the training process for the framework is of the order of (O(n \times m \times e)), where (n) is the number of training samples, (m) is the number of extracted features and (e) is the number of training epochs. This setup provides efficient learning with scalability for large-scale Home IoT, Enterprise IT, and Operational Technology (OT) deployments.

Results and discussion

The high rate of merging between home IoT, enterprise IT, and OT infrastructures has increased the cyber-attack area, exposing them to sophisticated cross-domain and persistent threats. The conventional domain-specific defenses are not coordinated with intelligence. Thus, an integrated, deduction-based cyber resilience model is necessary to ensure proactive identification, prevention, and coordinated cross-domain security.

Dataset: The Real-CyberSecurity-Datasets repository is very relevant to the study of cybersecurity and testing models. It offers publicly available data on intrusion detection, malware traffic, botnets, IoT attacks, and Industrial Control System (ICS) security. Such heterogeneous data sets can be used to train and validate cross-domain models such as CD-CRF, particularly to assess detection rates, anomaly relationships, and performance of cross-domain Resilience in IT and OT systems [36].

The experiments used heterogeneous cybersecurity datasets from the Real-CyberSecurity-Datasets repository, such as intrusion detection logs, ransomware traces, IoT botnet traffic, SCADA/ICS telemetry, authentication data, firewall logs and malware communication patterns. The data gathered included around 120,000 telemetry points representing both benign and malicious traffic like Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), insider threats, ransomware and attacks against the OT protocol. The data was preprocessed before training, which involved removing duplicates, synchronizing the timestamps, filtering out noise, one-hot encoding categorical features into numerical representations, and normalizing the numeric features using min-max scaling between 0 and 1. Optimized feature vectors were created using behavioral and temporal characteristics like login frequency, packet-flow statistics, command-sequence behavior, session duration and sensor activity. Data was partitioned into an 80:20 training-test split, with stratified sampling to assure a balanced distribution of attacks, and five-fold cross validation was used to enhance the generalization of the models and prevent overfitting. Wireshark and CICFlowMeter were employed for traffic analysis and telemetry extraction, respectively, while Python 3.11, TensorFlow, and Scikit-learn, NumPy, and Pandas libraries were used for implementing the framework. Testing was done on an Intel Core i7 processor and 16 GB of RAM with Ubuntu 22.04 operating system. The performance of CD-CRF was assessed by detection accuracy, false positive rate, response time, detection latency, threat prediction rate, cross-domain correlation efficiency, energy efficiency and resilience recovery time.

Cybersecurity datasets were collected and then balanced using stratified sampling to ensure that the training and testing sets of data were proportionate in terms of benign traffic, ransomware, Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), insider threats, IoT botnets and OT protocol attacks. Data Sets Balancing, Hyperparameter Configuration, Validation Strategy, and Implementation Feasibility: The data sets collected were balanced using stratified sampling to ensure that training and testing subsets contained a proportional amount of benign traffic, ransomware, Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), insider threats, IoT botnets, and OT protocol attacks. The final dataset was the result of a large number of telemetry instances—about 120,000, with a relatively even class distribution and with classification bias minimized. The hyperparameters used were systematically tuned by a grid-search optimization process. The values of the three parameters, namely adaptive learning rate, contextual threat weighting coefficient, and threat-intelligence integration parameter, were varied between 0.01 and 0.30, 0.1 and 1.0, and 0.1 and 1.0 respectively. The optimal configuration was chosen based on the highest validation accuracy and lowest false-positive rate. The 5-fold cross validation technique was used to avoid over fitting and to enhance the generalization capability with maintaining the same attack-class distribution in each fold along with the 80:20 ratio for the training and testing set. To support feature processing and threat correlation, the proposed CD-CRF framework has a high complexity of (O(n\logn)), where (n) corresponds to the number of telemetry events. Moreover, the framework was built with deployment simplicity in mind and supported by the commonly used communication protocols: MQTT, Modbus, TCP/IP, and REST APIs, making it easy to integrate with current Home IoT, Enterprise IT, and OT systems. The modular structure enables distributed deployment (edge-cloud), real-time telemetry processing, scaling threat analysis and automated response orchestration, providing evidence that the proposed framework is feasible in real-world cyber-physical environments and critical infrastructure systems.

The reported results are averaged over the 10 independent runs performed for each experiment performed under the same conditions. The stability and consistency of the framework across repeated experiments was measured by standard deviation (σ). Moreover, 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for the key evaluation parameters under discussion—such as detection accuracy, false positive rate, response time, detection latency, and threat prediction rate—to quantify and establish the confidence and robustness of the results. The proposed CD-CRF measured with a set of experimental runs, gave a detection accuracy of 98.6% ± 0.42, a false positive rate of 1.8%±0.21 and a response time of 120 ± 6.3 ms, which has low variance throughout experimental runs. Also, statistical significance testing was carried out by using paired t-tests for CD-CRF and baseline models of GCSM, CAT and TinyML. The p values obtained were all less than 0.05 for all the major metrics, indicating that the performance improvements were not due to random variations.

The framework extends to the heterogeneous Home IoT, Enterprise IT, and Operational Technology (OT) worlds using a variety of communication protocols, data formats, and security policies, which mandate interoperability and inter-standardized telemetry integration. With a large-scale deployment, the continuous and simultaneous collection and processing of telemetry data from several domains can result in large storage, network bandwidth and processing latencies. Moreover, there is computational overhead in behavioral modeling, anomaly detection, probabilistic risk scoring and cross domain threat correlation as features are continually extracted, models are continually updated, and inferences are continually made in real-time. These processes offer significant improvements in detection accuracy and situational awareness, but could impact on the performance of resource-limited IoT devices and high-throughput industrial applications. The proposed framework aims to address these issues by introducing a distributed edge–cloud system in which lightweight data preprocessing and filtering are undertaken at the edge and heavy analytics and threat deduction tasks are handled at the centralized server or cloud platform. This deployment model will help limit local resource use while providing the ability to see threats in real time and respond to them at the same time. The framework is therefore highly practicable for application in smart homes, enterprise systems, industrial control systems, and critical infrastructure networks, and will be optimized in the future with the goal of making it more efficient in terms of real-time operation, more scalable, and more economical with respect to computational complexity.

The proposed CD-CRF is an excellent contribution in the field of cross domain cyber resilience and will help to improve detection accuracy and false positive reduction, response time and predictive defence. The relative analysis with GCSM, CAT and TinyML demonstrates that CD-CRF is a scalable, accurate and proactive solution for cybersecurity by achieving the best performance in eight of the principal parameters.

Conclusion

The proposed Cross-Domain Cyber Resilience Framework (CD-CRF) offers an integrated and extensible cybersecurity framework for Home IoT, Enterprise IT and Operational Technology (OT) domains. The framework brings together multi-domain telemetry fusion, behavioral anomaly detection, intelligent threat deduction, probabilistic threat assessment, and orchestration of adaptive response, all of which significantly increase detection accuracy, decrease false positives, decrease response latency, and increase the resilience to sophisticated cyber threats. At a practical level, the proposed framework can facilitate the cybersecurity work for smart home networks, industrial automation systems, critical infrastructures, manufacturing plants, energy systems or enterprise networks and provide for coordinated threat detection and mitigation in heterogeneous environments. Nevertheless, there are some constraints that remain. The framework has been tested primarily with readily available cybersecurity datasets, and its applicability to real-world cyber-physical systems might not be representative of their complexity, scale and dynamics. Moreover, the use of behavioral analysis and cross-domain correlation engines is adding computation overhead, which can pose a problem in scarce resources setting. Future research will concentrate on federated learning techniques for privacy-preserving distributed threat intelligence sharing, ExplaiNable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) for enhancing transparency and interpretability of security decisions, and lightweight edge-based optimization strategies for large-scale deployments. Moreover, real-time implementation and validation of the CD-CRF framework in different industry control systems (ICS), smart manufacturing systems, critical infrastructure networks and Operational Technology (OT) environments will be explored, to evaluate the scalability, robustness, and practical effectiveness under actual cyber threats.
