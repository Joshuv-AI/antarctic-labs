LATAM-AKID registry: an international multicentre, observational study of acute kidney injury requiring dialysis in Latin America
Authors: Aqeeb Ur Rehman, Natalia Nombera-Aznaran, Stefania Renzi, Lama Ghazi, Ashita J Tolwani, Alejandra Molano Trivino, Thiago Reis, Olynka Vega-Vega, Lilia Rizo-Topete, Rolando Claure-Del Granado, Javier A Neyra

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute kidney injury requiring dialysis (AKI-D) is a major contributor to morbidity and mortality worldwide, with the highest burden observed in low- and lower-middle-income countries (LLMICs). In Latin America (LATAM), a region composed largely of LLMICs, the lack of region-specific data leads to variable clinical practices and inconsistent health policies for AKI, which remain major barriers to optimal kidney care. The LATAM-AKID registry aims to fill this gap and systematically characterise the epidemiology, processes of care and outcomes of patients with AKI-D across representative countries of LATAM.Methods and analysisLATAM-AKID is an international, multicentre, retrospective cohort study that will enrol consecutive critically ill adults with Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes stage 3 AKI requiring dialysis. Different hospitals across 10 Latin American countries will contribute data through a standardised REDCap platform hosted at the University of Alabama at Birmingham. Deidentified data will be collected at baseline, intensive care unit (ICU) admission and renal replacement therapy (RRT) initiation, with follow-up for up to 7 days to capture ICU- and RRT-related clinical data. Study outcomes will be assessed at ICU and hospital discharge and at 90-day follow-up. The primary outcome is in-hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes include ICU mortality, 90-day mortality, ICU and hospital lengths of stay, status of renal recovery and RRT dependence at hospital discharge and at 90-day follow-up, and RRT processes and complications related to anticoagulation, catheter dysfunction, infection and circuit interruptions. Data will be analysed using multivariable logistic and Cox regression models, with planned subgroup analyses by RRT modality (continuous vs intermittent), patient-level illness severity, hospital characteristics and country gross national income categories.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the institutional review board of the University of Alabama at Birmingham (IRB-300013633). Local ethics committee approvals will also be obtained at all participating sites. Results from this work will be disseminated by publications in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at national and international scientific meetings.Trial registration numberThis study has been registered with clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07262320).

Background and rationale

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common syndrome with consequential morbidity and mortality and increased costs to healthcare systems.1 2 Approximately one in five adults worldwide experience AKI during hospital admission3 and this incidence appears as high as 30%–40% in low- and lower-middle income countries (LLMICs).4 Evidence from low-resource regions further highlights substantial preventable burden of AKI, driven primarily by structural and logistical constraints.5 6 In this context, Latin America (LATAM), a region composed primarily of LLMICs, reportedly has one of the highest rates of AKI worldwide.3 Further, evidence suggests that AKI-associated mortality is inversely related to the country’s gross national income and health expenditure.3 To that end, the International Society of Nephrology (ISN) 0by25 Global Snapshot demonstrated that patients with AKI in South America and the Caribbean had over four times higher mortality compared with North America, thereby comprising the highest AKI-related mortality risk observed worldwide.7 8 The reasons for this epidemiological observation are likely multifactorial, including suboptimal AKI diagnosis and early management, deficient infrastructure to provide renal replacement therapy (RRT), inadequate and inconsistent protocols, gaps in health policy, and absence of systematic epidemiological data collection to inform practice and policy.5911

Despite the high burden and worse outcomes of AKI in LATAM, critical knowledge gaps persist, particularly in the subset of patients with AKI requiring dialysis (AKI-D), regarding epidemiology, access to RRT, processes of care, and short- and long-term outcomes. A recent survey by Sociedad Latinoamericana de Nefrología e Hipertensión (SLANH) provided an overview of the availability of RRT modalities to support patients with AKI but lacked granular patient, process and outcomes data.12 A detailed understanding of the current epidemiology of AKI-D in LATAM is essential to guide clinical practice, inform health policy, enable health advocacy, and establish a foundation for longitudinal epidemiological surveillance, clinical phenotyping and future interventional research.13 In this context, the LATAM-AKID registry aims to provide a comprehensive, real-world epidemiological study of AKI-D across diverse and representative healthcare systems in the LATAM region.

Methods

Study design and objectives

This is an international, multicentre, retrospective cohort study of critically ill adult patients with AKI undergoing any form of RRT in LATAM hospitals participating in the study. The principal aim of the LATAM-AKID study is to establish a registry of patients with AKI-D to identify clinical characteristics, risk factors, processes of care and outcomes that can lay the foundation for future interventional and implementation research and promote regional improvement in healthcare systems caring for these patients. The specific aims of this registry are to:

Establish a comprehensive and contemporary database containing clinical, laboratory, treatment, process and outcome data of critically ill adult patients with AKI-D in LATAM.

Ethics and dissemination

This research project will be conducted in accordance with the protocol, the Declaration of Helsinki, the principles of Good Clinical Practice, the Human Research Act, the Human Research Ordinance, as well as other locally relevant regulations. Ethical approval has been obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at the University of Alabama at Birmingham (UAB, IRB-300013633), and additional local IRB approvals will be sought from participating institutions across LATAM. Data transfer agreements (DTA) will also be sought, where applicable, based on local regulations. Informed consent has been waived, as the study poses minimal risk to participants and will involve only anonymised, retrospective data without direct contact with patients. This waiver enables inclusion of consecutive patients with AKI-D hospitalised at participating institutions, reducing the potential for non-reporting bias and enhancing both sample size and the generalisability of results. Ongoing study oversight will be provided by the LATAM-AKID steering committee, which is comprised of the following authors: LG, AJT, AMT, TR, OV-V, LR-T, RC-DG, with data integrity monitoring provided by scholars and statisticians at UAB.

The findings from the LATAM-AKID registry will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences and publications in peer-reviewed journals. Results will also be shared with participating sites and healthcare system leaders across LATAM to support clinical and administrative decision-making, guide resource allocation and inform regional health policy.

Eligibility criteria

The study population will consist of consecutively admitted critically ill adults (≥18 years of age) hospitalised in the intensive care unit (ICU) with Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes AKI stage 3 requiring any type of RRT. Only the first ICU admission during index hospitalisation will be considered for inclusion. Eligible patients must have RRT initiated no earlier than 3 days prior to ICU admission and no later than 7 days after ICU admission. Patients will be excluded if they are transferred from another hospital with ongoing RRT, have RRT exposure shorter than 2 days for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) and peritoneal dialysis (PD) or fewer than two haemodialysis (HD) or sustained low-efficiency dialysis (SLED) sessions (to ensure completeness of data and to support the primary aim of characterising RRT processes in LATAM), have pre-existing end-stage renal disease on maintenance dialysis, have a history of kidney transplantation, or have a previous or new diagnosis of glomerulonephritis during index hospitalisation. A summary of eligibility criteria and enrolment procedures is available in figure 1. Site participation will be determined by a structured feasibility evaluation which consists of: (1) availability of EHR or structured paper-based documentation sufficient to support retrospective data abstraction; (2) availability of personnel (such as investigators, research coordinators, research scholars or clinical fellows) capable of performing study data abstraction; (3) capability to complete ethics review and DTA within the anticipated study timeline and (4) sufficient patient volume and RRT delivery to support recruitment targets. For sites with known limitations in EHR infrastructure, eligibility will be contingent on the availability of reliable paper-based documentation and the engagement of investigators to facilitate chart review-based data entry.

Data collection

Sites of data collection

Initial recruitment will focus on at least 15 sites with broad representation from multiple LATAM countries (figure 2). In subsequent phases, these centres may act as regional hubs and enrol additional satellite sites to expand coverage and enhance the diversity of the patient population in the registry.

Geographic representation of LATAM-AKID participating sites.

Method of data collection

Participating sites will use non-probability consecutive sampling, identifying 50–100 eligible ICU patients through electronic health record (EHR) review or detailed patient chart review, subject to site capabilities. Each site will enter data into a REDCap database hosted by UAB. In accordance with the registry protocol, data will be anonymised locally, and REDCap will generate a unique deidentified patient ID. Only researchers from the participating institutions will hold the key linking deidentified data to participants’ identifiers and will enter into agreements prohibiting the release of protected health information to the UAB research team. The REDCap project will use role-restricted access, multifactor authentication and encrypted data transfer and storage. Research teams at each site will receive standardised REDCap training prior to activation to ensure consistent data entry practices across all sites.

Collected data

Data will be collected retrospectively at various time points, including at enrolment (at the time of meeting eligibility criteria after RRT initiation), at ICU admission, daily during ICU stay and while on RRT for 7 days, at ICU and hospital discharge, and at 90-day follow-up.

Patient demographics, socioeconomic status and baseline clinical characteristics will be recorded at the time of enrolment (table 1). Initial data collection will also include ICU admission data and RRT initiation data. ICU-related variables will be collected once daily for seven consecutive days or until death or discharge (whichever occurs first). RRT-related data will also be collected longitudinally for seven consecutive days or until death or RRT termination (whichever occurs first) (table 2). Additional data will be obtained at ICU and hospital discharge, with follow-up truncated at 90 days from the date of enrolment (table 3). Interventions

As an observational retrospective study, no interventions or additional laboratory testing will be conducted as part of the LATAM-AKID registry. Routinely documented laboratory and clinical data as part of clinical care will be obtained as per protocol whenever available. No biospecimen collection or storage will occur as part of the study.

Data quality monitoring

Data quality will be ensured through both automated and manual oversight procedures. REDCap data fields will include built-in validation rules such as range checks, branching logic and required fields to minimise entry errors and missing data. A detailed data dictionary and protocol manual accompany the REDCap instrument, providing explicit definitions for each variable to all staff at participating sites. Each site will also be required to maintain a screening log, which will periodically be cross-referenced with data entries on REDCap to verify completeness and accuracy. This screening log will be compiled from each site at the conclusion of the study. Researchers at UAB will perform periodic data reviews, generate data quality reports and issue queries to sites for clarification or correction of inconsistent or missing data. All changes to records will be logged within the REDCap audit trail to preserve data integrity. In addition to staff training at the time of site activation, recurrent data issues will be addressed through retraining of staff and communication with site investigators. These iterative procedures collectively constitute a multilayer data quality assurance framework appropriate for a registry study in resource-variable settings.

The primary outcome is in-hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes include ICU mortality, 90-day follow-up mortality, ICU and hospital lengths of stay, status of renal function recovery and RRT dependence at hospital discharge and at 90-day follow-up, and RRT-related processes and complications. Sample size

Due to the observational nature of the LATAM-AKID registry, a formal sample size calculation was not performed. However, based on data from prior LATAM AKI registries, such as Epidemiology of Acute Kidney Injury in Latin America (EPILAT-IRA)14 and the ISN 0by25 Global Snapshot,7 we anticipate an in-hospital mortality rate of approximately 40%–55% in critically ill adult patients with AKI-D in LATAM. Therefore, recruitment is targeted at 1000 patients over the course of 18 months, yielding about 500 primary outcome events. This event rate will support multivariable logistic regression models with up to 50 predictor variables (applying the widely used guideline of approximately 10 events per variable (EPV)), which is sufficient for all prespecified multivariable models. Similarly, for time-to-event analyses using Cox proportional hazards regression, the estimated primary event rate will support models of comparable complexity. The prespecified recruitment target reflects a pragmatic assessment of feasibility across participating sites, with each site contributing 50–100 consecutively admitted ICU patients with AKI-D. Overall, this sample size provides adequate statistical power to address the primary and key secondary objectives of the registry while remaining achievable within the operational constraints of LATAM.

Handling of missing data

Based on known capabilities of the projected study sites and prior experience with similar multinational registries in LATAM (eg, EPILAT-IRA14), we anticipate high data completeness for demographics and clinical and laboratory parameters (>90%), moderate completeness for daily ICU variables (75%–90% for the first 3 days), and lower completeness for daily RRT prescription details at sites with less structured documentation (estimated 60%–75%). These projections have been incorporated into the revised protocol. Sites are required to complete a minimum of 50 consecutive patients, and data completeness will be tracked continuously by the data coordinating centre through periodic data quality reports generated through REDCap.

Patterns of missingness for all key variables will be systematically characterised at the conclusion of data collection. The proportion of missing values will be reported per variable and per site, and the likely mechanism of missingness will be classified as missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR) or missing not at random (MNAR), based on both empirical assessment (eg, Little’s MCAR test) and clinical reasoning. For example, missing creatinine values at 90-day follow-up are likely MAR conditional on length of hospital stay, while missing RRT prescription details at sites with limited EHR infrastructure may have a systematic (MNAR) component.

For variables meeting the MCAR/MAR assumptions, multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) will be employed. The number of imputed datasets will be determined by the proportion of missing data (typically 20–50 datasets), and imputation models will include all variables used in the analytical models as well as auxiliary variables known to predict missingness. Results will be pooled using Rubin’s rules. For variables with suspected MNAR status, sensitivity analyses using pattern-mixture models or delta-adjustment approaches will be conducted to assess the robustness of findings to departures from the MAR assumption. Complete-case analyses will be reported alongside imputed analyses to allow transparent assessment of the potential impact of missing data on study conclusions.

Certain daily ICU and RRT variables collected for up to seven consecutive days may have higher rates of missingness at later time points, particularly at sites with limited EHR infrastructure. The completeness of daily data will be reported separately from baseline and discharge data, and longitudinal imputation methods appropriate for repeated-measures data will be applied when needed.

Statistical analysis

Data will be presented as summary statistics, including mean or median with appropriate measures of variability (SD or IQR) for continuous data. Data will be compared using standard statistical methods as appropriate. Associations between patient characteristics (eg, demographics, socioeconomic status and acuity of illness) and RRT-process characteristics (eg, modality, anticoagulation, etc) and study outcomes will be evaluated using univariable and multivariable logistic and Cox proportional hazards regression models. Model specifications will be informed by clinical and epidemiologic considerations, and the number of covariates included will be constrained by available outcome events to avoid model overfitting. Candidate variables for multivariable models will be selected a priori based on clinical and epidemiological rationale, targeting an EPV ratio of ≥10. Variable reduction will be guided by clinical prioritisation rather than stepwise selection to minimise overfitting. For exploratory analyses, penalised regression approaches (eg, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) will be considered as a supplementary strategy. The covariates for the primary multivariable model will be prespecified, and any deviations from the prespecified analytical plan will be transparently reported.

Prespecified subgroup analyses will be conducted based on RRT modality (CRRT, SLED, HD or PD), illness severity at enrolment (eg, Charlson Comorbidity Score, Sequential Organ Failure Assessment Score), country gross income level category, hospital characteristics (eg, number of ICU beds, academic vs non-academic), type of healthcare systems (public, private and mixed settings), and hospital location (urban vs suburban). Data may also be used to address additional research questions, as appropriate and as permitted by the available dataset.

Exploratory analyses will include site- and country-level variability. To account for the multicentre and multinational nature of this registry, multilevel (mixed-effects) models will be considered when clustering at site or country level meaningfully affects variance estimates. The decision to include site or country as a random effect will be determined a priori based on intraclass correlation coefficients. For the primary outcome and key secondary outcomes, measures of between-site heterogeneity (eg, I² statistic) will be reported, and site-level variability will be explored.

For outcomes in which death precludes observation of RRT dependence, a hierarchical composite endpoint using the WIN ratio may be explored to account for the competing risk and to preserve the clinical priority of mortality over recovery. RRT independence and longitudinal creatinine trajectories will be examined to provide additional clinical context for renal recovery, particularly in the setting of competing mortality risk. Sensitivity analyses may be performed to assess the robustness of alternative definitions of baseline creatinine. A two-tailed p value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant, recognising that subgroup and exploratory analyses are hypothesis-generating. All analyses will be performed using R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Data availability

Data from this study will be made available for future collaborative research. Requests should be submitted to the principal investigator and will be reviewed in accordance with applicable institutional policies and local data governance requirements.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study.

Discussion

The LATAM-AKID registry is a contemporary, international, multicentre study that aims to systematically characterise the epidemiology, processes of care and outcomes of patients with AKI-D receiving care in representative countries of LATAM. Building on prior global and LATAM-focused efforts such as the ISN 0by25 Global Snapshot, the SLANH AKI survey and the EPILAT-IRA study,7 12 14 this registry addresses the urgent need for contemporary and region-specific data related to AKI-D epidemiology, access to RRT, processes of care and relevant patient outcomes. However, the potential impact of this registry extends beyond epidemiological research only. LATAM-AKID seeks to establish a collaborative and sustainable network that can support future interventional and implementation research initiatives addressing specific needs and challenges of LATAM.5 By capturing real-world practice variability of RRT processes such as modality, anticoagulation and timing of RRT initiation and de-escalation, the registry will explore how system-level resources and constraints affect AKI-D outcomes in LATAM. By doing so, this registry can inform health-systems planning, guide resource allocation, strengthen workforce development and enable effective advocacy while also serving as a scalable framework for other low-resource regions in building efficient AKI-D data infrastructure.

This registry has several strengths. First, it integrates data from diverse clinical settings, especially related to types of healthcare systems (public, private, academic and community settings), hospital locations (urban and suburban) and variable country gross income levels (high, low to middle and low-income), which will enhance the generalisability of the findings and capture heterogeneity in clinical practice across different countries and healthcare systems, and therefore ensuring that the registry reflects real-world practice patterns across LATAM. Second, this registry will capture data about RRT processes (eg, RRT modality, prescription information, anticoagulation, etc) that are largely absent from literature originating from LATAM.15 16 Third, this registry will also collect data on system-level determinants of AKI-D care delivery, such as barriers to offering the RRT modality of choice, delays in RRT initiation and transitions across RRT modalities. These elements remain insufficiently described in prior LATAM studies and will allow for a more comprehensive understanding of inequities and inefficiencies in care delivery and care models, which are essential for advocacy and the development of context-appropriate interventions. Fourth, this registry will characterise AKI-D aetiologies specific to LATAM (eg, intoxication, envenomation and endemic infections) that are insufficiently reported in literature.17 Fifth, this registry will also enable comparisons of patient characteristics, RRT processes and outcomes across distinct hospitals in different LATAM countries. Lastly, this registry will collect data up to 90 days postenrolment to evaluate kidney function recovery and RRT dependence and therefore characterise AKI to chronic kidney disease transition in this susceptible LATAM population.1820

Despite the aforementioned strengths, the retrospective and observational design of this registry presents some limitations. First, although selection bias will be minimised by enrolling consecutive ICU patients with AKI-D, some eligible patients may be missed during retrospective identification due to inconsistent EHR systems and documentation practices across different study sites. Second, the retrospective design of this registry is susceptible to missingness of relevant data parameters. Therefore, imputation methods will be applied as needed. Third, because participating centres are predominantly tertiary hospitals in LATAM, the study sample may over-represent urban and higher resource settings, which may underestimate the true burden of AKI-D in LATAM, particularly in low-resource environments. Fourth, there may be significant heterogeneity in local hospital protocols and models of AKI-D care delivery, especially for timing and indications of RRT initiation, RRT modality and anticoagulation strategies, which may limit comparability across different study sites. Lastly, the requirement for a minimum duration of RRT exposure (≥2 days for CRRT/PD or ≥2 sessions for HD/SLED) is intended to exclude patients who experience very early death or rapid renal recovery. This threshold was selected to ensure adequate exposure to RRT for meaningful characterisation of treatment processes and complications. However, it may also lead to underestimation of early in-hospital mortality and over-representation of patients with more prolonged acute illness course. Despite these limitations, the LATAM-AKID registry represents an important step forward in addressing the gaps in epidemiological AKI-D data and research infrastructure in LATAM. This registry is expected to contribute to a better understanding of current epidemiology and AKI-D processes of care in LATAM and lay the foundation for research, advocacy and innovation in improving the care for AKI-D patients in LATAM.
