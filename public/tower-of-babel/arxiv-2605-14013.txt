Linear representations of manifolds
Rongbiao Thomas Wang, Lek-Heng Lim, Ke Ye

Abstract
. A finite-dimensional linear representation of a group or an algebra may be regarded as a map into a space of matrices, endowing abstract elements with coordinates, and encoding algebraic operations as matrix products. With this in mind, we define a linear representation of a 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -manifold ℳ \mathcal{M} as a map into a space of matrices, representing points as matrices and the 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -action as matrix products. We show that this generalizes group representations to any 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -manifold that may not have a group structure, with homogeneous spaces 𝖦 / 𝖧 \mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H} an important special case; and in this case it also generalizes Cartan embeddings of symmetric spaces to more general 𝖦 / 𝖧 \mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H} . To demonstrate the utility of such manifold representations, we use them to provide effective bounds for Mostow–Palais 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -equivariant embeddings of 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -manifolds into 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -modules 𝕍 \mathbb{V} . Unlike Whitney and Nash embeddings, Mostow–Palais embeddings have no known effective bounds; before our work, it was only known that dim 𝕍 < ∞ \dim\mathbb{V}<\infty if 𝖦 \mathsf{G} is compact. We will give explicit values for dim 𝕍 \dim\mathbb{V} and show that our bounds are sharp. Furthermore, our method is constructive, giving explicit expressions for these minimal-dimensional Mostow–Palais embeddings.



1.
Introduction

It is easiest to capture the spirit of what we call “manifold representation” through analogy: At their most basic level, assuming finite dimension and working over
\mathbb{R}
, a group representation is a map
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, an algebra representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and a manifold representation
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

\mathsf{G}
is a (Lie) group,
\mathfrak{g}
a (Lie) algebra,
\mathcal{M}
a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold,
\mathbb{U}
a
\mathsf{G}
-module, and

the

matrices. The maps

\varepsilon
should respect the inherent algebraic structure, so
\pi
is a group homomorphism,
\rho
an algebra homomorphism, and
\varepsilon
an equivariant map.

Studies of

-equivariant maps of
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds into
\mathsf{G}
-modules are not new. Mostow
[
30
]
and Palais
[
32

-equivariant immersion
[
4
]
and

[

are also common in topology. What is new here is that our
\mathsf{G}
-module is a space of matrices, i.e.,

is the analogue of a
linear
representation in the context of group and algebra representations.

At first glance, it may appear that there is hardly any difference. As Euclidean spaces, the space of matrices
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is no different from
\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
, but this is only because we limited ourselves to metric and vector space structures. Matrices are endowed with vastly richer properties — we may multiply or decompose them; impose orthogonality or symmetry on them; define determinant, norm, rank, or eigen/singular values and vectors; among a myriad of yet other features. We have used this to great effect in our earlier works, resolving conjectures
[
27
]
, demonstrating computational intractability
[
20
]
, calculating complicated geometric objects
[
21
]
, finding novel generators
[
25
]
, among other things — all through embedding manifolds as submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
in an equivariant manner.

Our notion of a linear representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is consistent with
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant maps of manifolds in differential geometry and linear representations of groups in representation theory. In fact, it extends the latter: Every group representation is a manifold representation with
\mathcal{M}=\mathsf{G}
. It is also consistent with Cartan immersions of symmetric spaces, which lies in the intersection of differential geometry and representation theory.

1.2.
Why different

Unlike Mostow–Palais embeddings, we do not need to assume compactness of
\mathsf{G}
for our classification of faithful linear representations in
1
.

The classification in
1
cannot be derived from any known classifications of, say, symmetric spaces (a la Cartan) or finite-type quivers (a la Gabriel). Indeed, most of the manifolds in Table
1
are not even symmetric spaces. While quivers are implicit when we consider classical groups of types
\mathrm{A}
,
\mathrm{B}
,
\mathrm{C}
, and
\mathrm{D}
, they are clearly not all of finite type, and include tame and wild ones as well. The symmetric spaces in Table
1
are Gelfand pairs
[
11
]
but our classification list bears little relation with these: Except in some degenerate cases, the Stiefel manifolds in our list are not Gelfand pairs; on the other hand, oriented Grassmannians are Gelfand pairs but do not appear on our list.

As previously mentioned, any
\mathsf{G}
-manifold with a transitive
\mathsf{G}
-action is diffeomorphic to a homogeneous space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
. This invites comparisons with induced representations
[
28
,
29
]
and isotropy representations
[
16
, Section II.3]
. For the former, the critical difference is that while our linear representation of a manifold
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
trivially yields a linear representation of the group
\mathsf{G}
on
\mathbb{V}
, an induced linear representation of
\mathsf{G}
is obtained from a linear representation of
\mathsf{H}
. For the latter, our linear representation of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
represents the homogeneous space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
as a submanifold of matrices whereas an isotropy representation represents the subgroup
\mathsf{H}
on the tangent space at the identity of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
; there is no relation between these two kinds of representation in general.

Given that
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
is an
affine
space, our faithful linear representations are distinct from Chevalley’s construction
[
17
, Section 11.2]
which, for a linear algebraic group
\mathsf{G}
, embeds a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold into
projective

\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{V})
for some
\mathsf{G}
-module
\mathbb{V}
.

The upshot is that despite the abundance of adjacent topics, the linear representations of
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds in this article likely constitute a hitherto unexplored subject.

has dimension

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{j-1}\kappa_{s}}{j-i}+1\biggr)&\text{if }\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\\
\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{j-1}\kappa_{s}}{j-i}+1\biggr)\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq m}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{m}\kappa_{s}+\sum_{t=j}^{m-1}\kappa_{t}}{2m+1-i-j}+1\biggr)&\text{if }\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2m+1}(\mathbb{C}),\\
\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{j-1}\kappa_{s}}{j-i}+1\biggr)\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{m}\kappa_{s}+\sum_{t=j}^{m-1}\kappa_{t}}{2m-i-j}+1\biggr)&\text{if }\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2m}(\mathbb{C}),\\
\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{j-1}\kappa_{s}}{j-i}+1\biggr)\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\biggl(\frac{\sum_{s=i}^{n}\kappa_{s}+\sum_{t=j}^{n}\kappa_{t}}{2n+2-i-j}+1\biggr)&\text{if }\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).\end{cases}

For a complex simple Lie group
\mathsf{G}
, an irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-module will mean an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module where
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of
\mathsf{G}
.

3.
Linear representations of
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds

We establish some basic definitions and results about our main object of study, a linear representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
. The notion has its roots in differential geometry. In differential geometric lingo
[
19
,
24
]
, if
\mathcal{N}
is another
\mathsf{G}
-manifold, then a differentiable map
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant if
\varepsilon(g\cdot x)=g\cdot\varepsilon(x)
for all
g\in\mathsf{G}
,
x\in\mathcal{M}
. If, moreover,
\varepsilon
is an embedding, then
\mathcal{M}
is called a
\mathsf{G}
-submanifold
of
\mathcal{N}
. In the special case where the manifold is linear, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{V}
, a
\mathsf{G}
-module, the
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant embedding
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
is called a
Mostow–Palais embedding
.

What we call a linear representation imposes further restrictions on the nature of
\mathbb{V}
and its
\mathsf{G}
-action, bringing these differential geometric notions closer to group representations:

Definition 3.1

(Manifold representation)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
. A
linear representation
of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is an equivariant differentiable map
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
where
\mathbb{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and
\mathsf{G}
acts on
\mathbb{V}
via matrix multiplication.

All representations in this article, whether of manifolds, groups, or algebras, will be linear representations, i.e., taking values in
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
. Henceforth we will drop the word “linear” except in section headings to serve as a reminder.

The requirement that
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
in Definition
3.1
is strictly speaking unnecessary. For example, for any
\mathsf{G}
that has an
n
-dimensional group representation
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, we may let
\pi(\mathsf{G})
play the role of
\mathsf{G}
above. Nevertheless for convenience we will assume that
\mathsf{G}
is a Lie subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
throughout. The action via matrix multiplication
\mathsf{G}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
in the definition could be left or right matrix multiplication, equivalence, congruence, or similarity:

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}(A,X)\mapsto AX,\quad(A,X)\mapsto XA^{-1},\quad(A_{1},A_{2},X)\mapsto A_{1}XA_{2}^{-1},\\
(A,X)\mapsto AXA^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\quad(A,X)\mapsto AXA^{-1},\end{gathered}

but we leave open the possibility of yet other unusual actions like bicongruence
(A_{1},A_{2},X)\mapsto A_{1}XA_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
(which is the same as equivalence for the groups we consider in this article). The products above are all matrix multiplications so these actions would not be well-defined without the assumptions that
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathbb{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, which will be implicit whenever we speak of a representation of a manifold.

The definition of a representation of
\mathsf{G}
-manifold in Definition
3.1
is consistent with both
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant map in differential geometry and representation of a group in representation theory. In fact, a manifold representation may be regarded as an extension of a group representation to
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds that may not be groups:

Proposition 3.2

(Every group representation is a manifold representation)
.

Let
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
be a representation of a Lie group
\mathsf{G}
in the sense of group representation. If we regard
\pi
as a map
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, then it is also a representation of
\mathsf{G}
in the sense of manifold representation.

Proof.

Here
\mathsf{G}
acts on
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\mathsf{G}
via left multiplication and on
\mathbb{V}\coloneqq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
via
\pi
, i.e., for all
g\in\mathsf{G}
and
X\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,
g\cdot X=\pi(g)X
. Clearly
\pi
is
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant as
\pi(g\cdot x)=\pi(gx)=\pi(g)\pi(x)=g\cdot\pi(x)
for any
g\in\mathsf{G}
,
x\in\mathcal{M}
.
∎

The use of group representation lingo in Definition
3.1
is suggestive, immediately leading to notions of equivalence, faithfulness, and irreducibility for representations; minimality for faithful ones; alongside common constructions of new representations from old ones. While these are commonplace in group (and algebra) representations, to the best of our knowledge, they are absent from differential geometry.

Definition 3.3

(Equivalence)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
be
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds. Two representations
\varepsilon_{1}:\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}:\mathcal{M}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{2}
are
equivalent
, denoted
\varepsilon_{1}\cong\varepsilon_{2}
, if there exist a diffeomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{M}_{1}\cong\mathcal{M}_{2}
and a
\mathsf{G}
-module isomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{V}_{1}\cong\mathbb{V}_{2}
such that
\psi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varphi
and

\varphi(g\cdot x)=g\cdot\varphi(x),\quad\psi(g\cdot v)=g\cdot\psi(v),\quad

for any
g\in\mathsf{G}
,
x\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
, and
v\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
.

The notions of faithfulness and minimality are particularly important for us as a main goal of this article is to classify
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds that have faithful representations and explicitly find all minimal dimensional ones, thereby arriving at sharp effective bounds for their Mostow–Palais embeddings.

Definition 3.4

(Faithfulness and minimality)
.

A representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
is
faithful
if it is a
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant embedding. A faithful representation is
minimal
if there is no faithful representation
\varepsilon^{\prime}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{V}^{\prime}<\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{V}
.

Any faithful representation is clearly a Mostow–Palais embedding; it will follow from our classification result that the converse is false.

The notions of equivalence and faithfulness in Definitions
3.3
and
3.4
are also consistent with those same notions in group representation theory:

Proposition 3.5
.

(1)

If
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
is faithful in the sense of group representation, then, when regarded as a map
\pi:\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, it is faithful in the sense of manifold representation.

(2)

If
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:\mathsf{G}\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
are equivalent in the sense of group representation, then, when regarded as maps
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, they are equivalent in the sense of manifold representation.

Proof.

If
\pi
is a faithful Lie group representation, then it is a
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant embedding of
\mathsf{G}
into
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
. If
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are equivalent Lie group representations, then there exists
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
such that for all
g\in\mathsf{G}
,
\pi_{2}(g)=A\pi_{1}(g)A^{-1}
. We verify that
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
by taking
\varphi=\operatorname{id}
, the identity map on
\mathsf{G}
, and

\psi:\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times n},\quad X\mapsto AXA^{-1}

in Definition
3.3
. For all
g\in\mathsf{G}
and
X\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,

\pi_{2}(g)\psi(X)=(A\pi_{1}(g)A^{-1})(AXA^{-1})=A\pi_{1}(g)XA^{-1}=\psi(\pi_{1}(g)X)

and
\psi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\pi_{1}(g)=A\pi_{1}(g)A^{-1}=\pi_{2}(g)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varphi
.
∎

Standard constructions of new group representations from old ones extend to manifold representations almost verbatim, as Definitions
3.6
and
3.7
show.

Definition 3.6

(Direct sum and tensor product)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{2}
be two representations of
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
. The
direct sum

\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\varepsilon_{2}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
is defined by
\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\varepsilon_{2}(x)=\varepsilon_{1}(x)\oplus\varepsilon_{2}(x)
. The
tensor product

\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\varepsilon_{2}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}
is defined by
\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\varepsilon_{2}(x)=\varepsilon_{1}(x)\otimes\varepsilon_{2}(x)
.

The equivalence of representations is not necessarily preserved by direct sums (see Example
4.6
) or tensor products.

Given a manifold representation
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold, we may limit it in three ways: to
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, to
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
, or to
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{V}
; each gives a meaningful manifold representation.

Definition 3.7

(Restricted, restrained, sub-, and complementary representations)
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
be a representation. If
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathsf{G}
-submanifold, then
\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{N}}
is the
restricted representation
of
\mathcal{N}
. If
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
is a closed subgroup, then
\varepsilon
is
restrained representation
of
\mathcal{M}
viewed as a
\mathsf{H}
-manifold. If
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{V}
is a
\mathsf{G}
-submodule, then
\varepsilon^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon
is the
subrepresentation
of
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
. If
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
has a complementary subspace
\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime}
, that is also a
\mathsf{G}
-submodule, then
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=p^{\prime\prime}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon
is the
complementary representation
of
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Here
p^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime\prime}
denote the orthogonal projections onto
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime}
respectively.

The notions of irreducibility and indecomposability in group representations readily extend to manifold representations.

Definition 3.8

(Irreducibility and indecomposability)
.

A representation
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
is
irreducible
if
\mathbb{V}
is irreducible as a
\mathsf{G}
-module; it is
indecomposable
if
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\varepsilon_{2}
implies that either
\varepsilon_{1}
or
\varepsilon_{2}
is the trivial representation
\mathcal{M}\to\{0\}
.

Decompositions into indecomposables for arbitrary
\mathsf{G}
and irreducibles for reductive
\mathsf{G}
likewise extend to manifold representations.

Proposition 3.9

(Complete decomposability and reducibility)
.

Every representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
can be written as
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\varepsilon_{k}
, where each
\varepsilon_{j}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}
is an indecomposable representation of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{V}_{k}
is a unique decomposition up to isomorphism. If, furthermore,
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is reductive, then
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}
can be chosen to be irreducible.

Proof.

By the Krull–Schmidt theorem
[
2
, Theorem 12.9]
, the finite-dimensional
\mathsf{G}
-module
\mathbb{V}
admits an indecomposable decomposition
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{V}_{k}
that is unique up to isomorphism. We set
\varepsilon_{j}\coloneqq p_{j}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon
where
p_{j}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}
is the projection,
j=1,\dots,k
. Suppose
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime}\oplus\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime\prime}
where
\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime\prime}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
with
\mathbb{V}_{j}=\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
. As
\mathbb{V}_{j}
is indecomposable, either
\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime}
or
\mathbb{V}_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\{0\}
, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime}
or
\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is trivial. So
\varepsilon_{j}
is indecomposable. For linearly reductive
\mathsf{G}
, the decomposition of
\mathbb{V}
may be chosen to be irreducible
[
33
, Section 7.3]
and in which case
\varepsilon_{j}
is irreducible.
∎

A representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold may be decomposed in yet another sense, namely, via the
\mathsf{G}
-orbits in
\mathcal{M}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote its orbit and stabilizer under the action of
\mathsf{G}
by

\mathsf{G}\cdot x\coloneqq\{g\cdot x\in\mathcal{M}:g\in\mathsf{G}\},\quad\mathsf{G}_{x}\coloneqq\{g\in\mathsf{G}:g\cdot x=x\}.

The following corollary of Proposition
3.9
shows that a representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold is completely determined by its restrictions to orbits.

Corollary 3.10

(Characterization by orbits)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold and
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
any nonzero differentiable map. Then
\varepsilon
is a representation of
\mathcal{M}
if and only if for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
, the restriction
\varepsilon|_{\mathsf{G}\cdot x}:\mathsf{G}\cdot x\to\mathbb{V}
is a representation of the orbit
\mathsf{G}\cdot x
. Furthermore,
\varepsilon
is irreducible if and only if
\varepsilon|_{\mathsf{G}\cdot x}
is irreducible for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

When discussing a representation
\varepsilon:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{V}
, there are two
\mathsf{G}
-actions involved, one on the
\mathsf{G}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
, and another on the
\mathsf{G}
-module
\mathbb{V}
. To avoid ambiguity, and since the
\mathsf{G}
-action on
\mathcal{M}
is transitive, in the remainder of this article we will assume
\mathcal{M}=\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
with
\mathsf{H}
a closed subgroup of
\mathsf{G}
. Henceforth, whenever we speak of a
\mathsf{G}
-action,
\mathsf{G}
-orbit, or
\mathsf{G}
-stabilizer, it will exclusively refer that of the
\mathsf{G}
-module
\mathbb{V}
.

4.
Linear representations of homogeneous spaces

By the discussion at the end of the last section, we limit our attention to
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds that are homogeneous spaces of the form
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
. Corollary
3.10
ensures that representations respect this quotient structure. It turns out that we may further narrow our focus to the following kind of homogeneous spaces:

Definition 4.1

(Irreducibly faithfully representable spaces)
.

We say that
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
is
irreducibly faithfully representable
if, for any closed subgroups
\mathsf{H}_{1}
,
\mathsf{H}_{2}
with

\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{1}\subseteq\mathsf{G},\quad\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{2}\subseteq\mathsf{G},

and
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{1}
,
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{2}
both admit faithful representations, then
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\mathsf{H}_{2}
implies that
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}
or
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{2}
.

Irreducibly faithfully representable homogeneous spaces admit an alternative characterization.

Proposition 4.2

(Irreducibly faithfully representability)
.

A homogeneous space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
is irreducibly faithfully representable if and only if for any faithful representation of the form
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}):\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{V}_{k}
, where
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}
are representations of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
, there exists a faithful
\varepsilon_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots k\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
be irreducibly faithfully representable. If
\varepsilon
is faithful, then there are closed subgroups
\mathsf{H}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{H}_{k}
containing
\mathsf{H}
, such that each
\varepsilon_{j}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}
factors through some faithful representation
\eta_{j}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{j}\to\mathbb{V}_{j}
, and
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathsf{H}_{k}
. By definition,
\varepsilon_{j}
must be faithful for some
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Conversely, let
\mathsf{H}_{1},\mathsf{H}_{2}
be two closed subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
such that
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\mathsf{H}_{2}
and that
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}
admits a faithful representation
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
,
i=1,2
.
These induce a faithful representation
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}):\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{G}/(\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\mathsf{H}_{2})\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
. By assumption, either
\varepsilon_{1}
or
\varepsilon_{2}
must be faithful, so either
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}
or
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{2}
.
∎

4.1.
Mostow–Palais embeddings of homogeneous spaces

While we are ultimately interested in faithful representations, the results in this section hold true more generally for Mostow–Palais embeddings and we will state them as such. In other words, here we study
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant embeddings
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
with no further assumption on
\mathbb{V}
other than that it is a
\mathsf{G}
-module. Our first observation is that any Mostow–Palais embedding of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
in
\mathbb{V}
must be a
\mathsf{G}
-orbit:

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a Mostow–Palais embedding
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
if and only if there exists
v\in\mathbb{V}
such that
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{G}_{v}
. In this case, we must have
\varepsilon(\mathsf{H})=v
.

We next show that Mostow–Palais embeddings respect direct sums.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
\mathbb{V}_{2}
be
\mathsf{G}
-modules. A map
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
is a Mostow–Palais embedding if and only if
\varepsilon=\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}
for some Mostow–Palais embeddings
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, where
\mathsf{H}_{1},\mathsf{H}_{2}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
are closed subgroups such that
\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\mathsf{H}_{2}=\mathsf{H}
, and
\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
is
\varepsilon_{i}
composed with the quotient map
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Let
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
and
g\in\mathsf{G}
. Then

(4.1)

\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}=\mathsf{G}_{v_{1}}\cap\mathsf{G}_{v_{2}}.

Let
\varepsilon=\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}
as in the statement of the lemma. By Lemma
4.3
, there exist
v_{1}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
v_{2}\in\mathbb{V}_{2}
such that
\mathsf{H}_{1}=\mathsf{G}_{v_{1}}
and
\mathsf{H}_{2}=\mathsf{G}_{v_{2}}
. So
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}
by (
4.1
). Applying Lemma
4.3
again, we conclude that
\varepsilon
is a Mostow–Palais embedding as
\varepsilon(\mathsf{H})=(\varepsilon_{1}(\mathsf{H}_{1}),\varepsilon_{2}(\mathsf{H}_{2}))=(v_{1},v_{2})
.

For the converse, let
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
be a Mostow–Palais embedding. By Lemma
4.3
, there exist
v_{1}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
v_{2}\in\mathbb{V}_{2}
such that
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}
. Let
\mathsf{H}_{1}=\mathsf{G}_{v_{1}}
and
\mathsf{H}_{2}=\mathsf{G}_{v_{2}}
. Then
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\mathsf{H}_{2}
by (
4.1
). Applying Lemma
4.3
again, we obtain Mostow–Palais embeddings
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
,
i=1,2
. By construction, we have
\varepsilon(\mathsf{H})=(v_{1},v_{2})=(\varepsilon_{1}(\mathsf{H}_{1}),\varepsilon_{2}(\mathsf{H}_{2}))
. Hence
\varepsilon=\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}
.
∎

By repeatedly applying Lemma
4.4
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a linearly reductive Lie group and let
\mathbb{V}
be a
\mathsf{G}
-module. A map
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
is a Mostow–Palais embedding if and only if
\varepsilon=\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{k}
where

(1)

\mathsf{H}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{H}_{k}
are closed subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
and
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathsf{H}_{k}
;

(2)

\mathbb{V}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{V}_{k}
are irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-modules and
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{V}_{k}
;

(3)

\varepsilon_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
is a Mostow–Palais embedding,
i=1,\dots,k
;

(4)

\overline{\varepsilon}_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
is
\varepsilon_{i}
composed with the quotient map
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
.

We highlight a potential pitfall here: Suppose
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}_{i}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}^{\prime}_{i}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}_{i}
are Mostow–Palais embeddings with
\varepsilon_{i}\cong\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}
,
i=1,2
. It is possible that
\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}\not\cong\overline{\varepsilon}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}^{\prime}
. In other words, for
(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{V}_{2}
satisfying

(4.2)

\mathsf{G}_{u_{1}}\cong\mathsf{G}_{v_{1}},\quad\mathsf{G}_{u_{2}}\cong\mathsf{G}_{v_{2}},

it is not necessarily true that
\mathsf{G}_{(u_{1},u_{2})}\cong\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}
. We provide an example below

Example 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{G}=\SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})
act on
\mathbb{U}_{1}=\mathbb{U}_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
by left multiplication. Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\mathsf{G}_{e_{1}}=\biggl\{\begin{bmatrix}1\\
&Q\end{bmatrix}:Q\in\SO_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\biggr\}\cong\SO_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathsf{G}_{e_{n}}=\biggl\{\begin{bmatrix}Q\\
&1\end{bmatrix}:Q\in\SO_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\biggr\}.

So
(e_{1},e_{1})
and
(e_{1},e_{n})
satisfy (
4.2
). However, a direct calculation shows that

\mathsf{G}_{(e_{1},e_{1})}=\biggl\{\begin{bmatrix}1\\
&Q\end{bmatrix}:Q\in\SO_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\biggr\}\cong\SO_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\not\cong\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1\\
&U\\
&&1\end{bmatrix}:U\in\SO_{n-2}(\mathbb{R})\right\}=\mathsf{G}_{(e_{1},e_{n})}.

In Section
4.2
, we will see that (
4.2
) is equivalent to
\mathsf{G}_{v_{1}}=A\mathsf{G}_{u_{1}}A^{-1}
,
\mathsf{G}_{v_{2}}=C\mathsf{G}_{u_{2}}C^{-1}
for some
A,C\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
. In which case, since

\mathsf{G}_{(u_{1},u_{2})}=\mathsf{G}_{u_{1}}\cap\mathsf{G}_{u_{2}},\quad\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}=A\mathsf{G}_{u_{1}}A^{-1}\cap C\mathsf{G}_{u_{2}}C^{-1},

there is no guarantee that
\mathsf{G}_{(u_{1},u_{2})}\cong\mathsf{G}_{(v_{1},v_{2})}
unless
A=C
.

Incidentally, Example
4.6
also shows that the homogeneous space
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SO_{n-2}(\mathbb{R})
is not irreducibly faithfully representable and that equivalence of representations is not preserved under direct sum.

Lastly, we show that if
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real form of a complex
\mathsf{G}
, then Mostow–Palais embeddings of
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-homogeneous spaces into complex
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-modules are determined by those of
\mathsf{G}
-homogeneous spaces into
\mathsf{G}
-modules, important for our classification in Section
5
.

Lemma 4.7

(Mostow–Palais embeddings of real forms)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real form of a simple complex Lie group
\mathsf{G}
. If
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{W}
is a Mostow–Palais embedding into a complex
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-module
\mathbb{W}
, then
\mathbb{W}
is a
\mathsf{G}
-module and there exist a closed subgroup
\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
such that
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
and a Mostow–Palais embedding
\varepsilon^{\prime}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{W}
such that
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\iota
where
\iota:\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}^{\prime}
,
g\mathsf{H}\mapsto g\mathsf{H}^{\prime}
, is the natural inclusion. Moreover, if
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{U}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
for some real
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-module
\mathbb{U}
, then there exist
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{U}
such that
\mathsf{H}=(\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}})_{v_{1}+iv_{2}}=(\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}})_{v_{1}}\cap(\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}})_{v_{2}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
is simple,
\mathbb{W}
is a completely reducible
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-module. As irreducible complex
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
-modules are exactly irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-modules,
\mathbb{W}
, when viewed as a
\mathsf{G}
-module, is a direct sum of complex irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-modules. By Lemma
4.3
, there exists
v\in\mathbb{W}
such that
\mathsf{H}=(\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}})_{v}
and
\varepsilon(g\mathsf{H})=g\cdot v
. Let
\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathsf{G}_{v}
. Then
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Applying Lemma
4.3
to the action of
\mathsf{G}
on
v
yields

\varepsilon^{\prime}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{W}\quad g\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\mapsto g\cdot v

as a Mostow–Palais embedding. It remains check that for any
g\mathsf{H}\in\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathsf{H}
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\bigl(\iota(g\mathsf{H})\bigr)=\varepsilon^{\prime}(g\mathsf{H}^{\prime})=g\cdot v=\varepsilon(g\mathsf{H})
.
The case of
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{U}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
follows from Lemma
4.3
and Proposition
4.5
.
∎

4.2.
Stabilizers of
\mathsf{G}
-actions

The homogeneous spaces of interest to us would all take the form
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
where
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a Lie subgroup and
\mathsf{H}
is a stabilizer of one of three standard actions: equivalence, congruence, or similarity. For any
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
-module
\mathbb{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and
X\in\mathbb{V}
, we have

(4.3)

\mathsf{G}_{X}=\{g\in\mathsf{G}:g\cdot X=X\}=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}\cap\mathsf{G}.

We next show that the set of homogeneous spaces
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
that has a faithful representation is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of possible stabilizers
\mathsf{G}_{X}
,
X\in\mathbb{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, again important for our classification in Section
5
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Define an equivalence relation
\mathsf{H}_{1}\sim\mathsf{H}_{2}
if
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{1}\cong\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{2}
and set

\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{G})\coloneqq\left\{\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{G}:\mathsf{H}\text{ closed in }\mathsf{G}\text{ and }\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\text{ is a }\mathsf{G}\text{-submanifold of }\mathbb{V}\right\}/\sim.

Define another equivalence relation
v_{1}\sim v_{2}
if
\mathsf{G}\cdot v_{1}\cong\mathsf{G}\cdot v_{2}
and set
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{V})\coloneqq\mathbb{V}/\sim
. Then for any Mostow–Palais embedding
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{V}
, the map

\varphi:\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{G})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{V}),\quad\varphi([\mathsf{H}])=[\varepsilon(\mathsf{H})]

is well-defined and bijective. In particular,
\varphi
is independent of the choice of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Suppose
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is another Mostow–Palais embedding of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
into
\mathbb{V}
. Let
v\coloneqq\varepsilon(\mathsf{H})
and
v^{\prime}\coloneqq\varepsilon^{\prime}(\mathsf{H})
. Then
\mathsf{G}_{v}=\mathsf{G}_{v^{\prime}}=\mathsf{H}
and so
\mathsf{G}\cdot v\cong\mathsf{G}\cdot v^{\prime}\cong\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
. Thus
\varphi
does not depend on the choice of
\varepsilon
.
The surjectivity of
\varphi
follows from Lemma
4.3
. For injectivity, suppose
\varphi([\mathsf{H}_{1}])=\varphi([\mathsf{H}_{2}])=[v]
. Then there are Mostow–Palais embeddings
\varepsilon_{1}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{1}\to\mathbb{V}
and
\varepsilon_{2}:\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}
such that

\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{1}\cong\mathsf{G}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}(\mathsf{H}_{1})\cong\mathsf{G}\cdot v\cong\mathsf{G}\cdot\varepsilon_{2}(\mathsf{H}_{2})\cong\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}_{2},

implying
[\mathsf{H}_{1}]=[\mathsf{H}_{2}]
.
∎

We will reduce our classification tasks in Sections
6
–
8
to the case of simple groups. Thus equivalence reduces to left and right multiplications; and as the latter is dual to the former, it suffices to determine the stabilizer for left multiplication. So for our purpose, it suffices to determine the stabilizer
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}
under left multiplication, congruence and similarity. Surprisingly, they cannot be easily found in standard references; we will describe these stabilizers below for our later use.

Let
\mathsf{G}_{1}\subseteq\GL_{p}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathsf{G}_{2}\subseteq\GL_{n-p}(\mathbb{F})
be closed subgroups. We define the subgroup

(4.4)

\P(\mathsf{G}_{1},\mathsf{G}_{2})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&C\\
0&A_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}):A_{1}\in\mathsf{G}_{1},\;A_{2}\in\mathsf{G}_{2},\;C\in\mathbb{F}^{p\times(n-p)}\right\}.

For a trivial group
G_{1}=\{I_{r}\}
, we will write
\P(I_{r},\mathsf{G}_{2})
, omitting the braces.

Lemma 4.9

(Stabilizers of left multiplication)
.

Let
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
act on
\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
,
k\leq n
, by left multiplication

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times k},\quad(A,X)\mapsto AX.

For any
X\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
with
\rank(X)=r
, the stabilizer
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}=Q\P\bigl(I_{r},\GL_{n-r}(\mathbb{F})\bigr)Q^{-1}
for some
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
Q\in\Un_{n}
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Over
\mathbb{R}
, by the QR decomposition, there exist
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
S\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(k-r)}
, a permutation matrix
\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, and an upper triangular matrix
R\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{R})
, such that

X=Q\begin{bmatrix}R&S\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Pi^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}.

Given
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we partition
Q^{-1}AQ\eqqcolon C=\begin{bmatrix}C_{11}&C_{12}\\
C_{21}&C_{22}\end{bmatrix}
into blocks
C_{11}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
,
C_{12}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(n-r)}
,
C_{21}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-r)\times r}
, and
C_{22}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-r)\times(n-r)}
, from which it is clear that
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{X}
if and only if
C_{11}=I_{r}
and
C_{21}=0
. The same argument holds over
\mathbb{C}
with
\Un_{n}
in place of
\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Congruence defines an action on both the space of skew-symmetric matrices
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
and that of symmetric matrices
\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
. We characterize the respective stabilizers in the next two lemmas.

Lemma 4.10

(Skew-symmetric stabilizers of congruence)
.

Let
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
act on
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
by congruence:

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\to\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n}),\quad(A,X)\mapsto AXA^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}.

For any
X\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
with
\rank(X)=2r
, the stabilizer
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}=Q\P\bigl(\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{F};\Omega),\GL_{n-2r}(\mathbb{F})\bigr)Q^{-1}
for some
\Omega\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{2r})\cap\GL_{2r}(\mathbb{F})
, and
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
Q\in\Un_{n}
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

For a positive integer
m
, write

\Omega_{2m}\coloneqq\diag\biggl(\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},\dots,\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}}_{m\text{~times}}\biggr)\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{2m}).

By
[
36
]
and
[
41
]
,
X
has the canonical form

X=Q\diag(\Omega,0_{n-r})Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\quad\Omega=\diag(\lambda_{1}\Omega_{2n_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{s}\Omega_{2n_{s}}),

with
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
satisfying
2n_{1}+\dots+2n_{s}=2r
. Given
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, we partition
Q^{-1}AQ\eqqcolon C=\begin{bmatrix}C_{11}&C_{12}\\
C_{21}&C_{22}\end{bmatrix}
into blocks
C_{11}\in\mathbb{R}^{2r\times 2r}
,
C_{12}\in\mathbb{R}^{2r\times(n-2r)}
,
C_{21}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-2r)\times 2r}
, and
C_{22}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-2r)\times(n-2r)}
, from which it is clear that
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}
if and only if
C_{11}\Omega C_{11}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}=\Omega
and
C_{21}=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.11

(Symmetric stabilizers of congruence)
.

Let
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
act on
\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
by congruence:

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\to\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n}),\quad(A,X)\mapsto AXA^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}.

For any
X\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
with
\rank(X)=r
, the stabilizer
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}=Q\P\bigl(\O_{r}(\mathbb{F},B),\GL_{n-r}(\mathbb{F})\bigr)Q^{-1}
for some
B\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{r})\cap\GL_{r}(\mathbb{F})
, and
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
Q\in\Un_{n}
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Over
\mathbb{R}
, by the spectral decomposition for real symmetric matrices,

X=Q\diag(\lambda_{1}I_{n_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{m}I_{m},0_{n-r})Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},

where
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{m}
, and
(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
satisfying
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=r
. The required characterization of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{X}
follows from the same argument in the proof of Lemma
4.10
. Over
\mathbb{C}
, the Takagi decomposition for complex symmetric matrices
[
35
]
yields the required result with
Q\in\Un_{n}
.
∎

For positive integers
k,l,k_{1},\dots,k_{p}
, we let
\UT(k,l,\mathbb{F})
denote the space of
k\times l
upper triangular Toeplitz matrices over
\mathbb{F}
, and
\BUT(2k,2l,\mathbb{F})
the space of
k\times l
block upper triangular Toeplitz matrices over
\mathbb{F}
with blocks of the form
\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. In addition, we define

\displaystyle\UT_{k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}(\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigl\{(T_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{p}\in\GL_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}}(\mathbb{F}):T_{ij}\in\UT(k_{i},k_{j},\mathbb{F}),\;i,j=1,\dots,p\bigr\},

\displaystyle\BUT_{2k_{1},\dots,2k_{p}}(\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigl\{(T_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{p}\in\GL_{2k_{1}+\dots+2k_{p}}(\mathbb{F}):T_{ij}\in\BUT(2k_{i},2k_{j},\mathbb{F}),\;i,j=1,\dots,p\bigr\},

and we drop the parenthetical
\mathbb{F}
when the choice of field is clear.
It is easy to see that both
\UT_{k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}
and
\BUT_{2k_{1},\dots,2k_{p}}
are Lie subgroups. The next lemma follows immediately from
[
12
, Theorems 9.1.1 and 12.4.2]
.

Lemma 4.12

(Stabilizers of similarity)
.

Let
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
act on
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
by similarity:

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times n},\quad(A,X)\mapsto AXA^{-1}.

For any
X\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, the stabilizer

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})_{X}=A\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{p}\UT_{n_{i,1},\dots,n_{i,s_{i}}}\times\prod_{j=1}^{q}\BUT_{m_{j,1},\dots,m_{j,t_{j}}}\Bigr)A^{-1},

for some
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, positive integers
n_{i,1},\dots,n_{i,s_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,p
, and
m_{j,1},\dots,m_{j,t_{j}}
,
j=1,\dots,q
, satisfying

n=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n_{i,1}+\dots+n_{i,s_{i}})+\sum_{j=1}^{q}(m_{j,1}+\dots+m_{j,t_{j}}).

Moreover, when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, we have
m_{j,1}=\dots=m_{j,t_{j}}=0
for all
j=1,\dots,q
.

5.
Reduction of the main classification problem

In this brief section, we outline the general arguments used to obtain our classification of
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds that have a faithful representation, i.e., what we promised in item
1
of Section
1.1
. We consider all
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds that have transitive
\mathsf{G}
-actions by

(5.1)

\mathsf{G}\in\{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{F}),\SO_{n}(\mathbb{F}),\Sp_{n}(\mathbb{F}),\SU_{n},\SO_{p,q},\Sp_{n}:\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}\}.

The main effort for this classification will be carried out over the next three sections on a case-by-case basis: Section
6
for classical groups of type A, Section
7

for type C. From a high-level perspective, each section follows the same approach below.

. In Sections

,
7.1
, and
8.1
, we first list all possible irreducible complex

-modules, then determine all possible

, obtaining a classification.

Real representations of
\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathsf{H}
for real forms

By Lemma

, it suffices to classify faithful representations
\varepsilon:\mathsf{G}_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

Lemma 5.1

(Dimension inequality for irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-modules)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be

,

6.1.
Faithful linear representations of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa_{n-1},\dots,\kappa_{1}}=\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n-1}}.

Lemma 6.1

(Low dimension
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module)
.

Let
n\geq 9
. A complex irreducible
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module
\mathbb{W}
has dimension at most
n^{2}
if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
or
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{W}\cong\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
, it suffices to prove
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\leq n^{2}
if and only if
\kappa
is one of the following
(n-1)
-tuples:

\displaystyle(0,\dots,0),\quad(1,0,\dots,0),\quad(0,\dots,0,1),\quad(0,1,0,\dots,0),

\displaystyle(0,\dots,0,1,0),\quad(2,0,\dots,0),\quad(0,\dots,0,2),\quad(1,0,\dots,0,1).

If
\kappa
is one of these
(n-1)
-tuples, then
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\leq n^{2}
by a direct calculation using Proposition
2.1
. To show that
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}>n^{2}
, we split into three cases:

(1)

If
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},0,\dots,0,\kappa_{n-1})
where
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{n-1}\geq 1
and
\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{n-1}\geq 3
, then by applying Lemma
5.1
and (
6.1
) recursively, we get

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\geq\dim\mathbb{W}_{2,0,\dots,0,1}=\frac{n(n-1)(n+2)}{2}>n^{2}.

(2)

If
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},0,\dots,\kappa_{n-1})
where either
\kappa_{1}\geq 3
and
\kappa_{n-1}=0
, or
\kappa_{1}=0
and
\kappa_{n-1}\geq 3
, then Lemma
5.1
and (
6.1
) yield

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\geq\dim\mathbb{W}_{3,0,\dots,0}=\binom{n+2}{3}>n^{2}.

(3)

If
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n-1})
where either
\kappa_{i}\geq 1
for some
3\leq i\leq n-3
, or
\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{n-2}\geq 2
and
\kappa_{i}=0
for all
3\leq i\leq n-3
, then Lemma
5.1
gives

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\geq\begin{cases}\dim\mathbb{W}_{e_{i}}=\binom{n}{i}\quad&\text{if $\kappa_{i}\geq 1$ for some $3\leq i\leq n-3$},\\
\dim\mathbb{W}_{2e_{2}}=\frac{n^{2}(n+1)(n-1)}{12}\quad&\text{if $\kappa_{3}=\dots=\kappa_{n-3}=0$ and $\kappa_{2}\geq 2$},\\
\dim\mathbb{W}_{2e_{n-2}}=\frac{n^{2}(n+1)(n-1)}{12}\quad&\text{if $\kappa_{3}=\dots=\kappa_{n-3}=0$ and $\kappa_{n-2}\geq 2$},\\
\dim\mathbb{W}_{e_{2}+e_{n-2}}=\frac{n^{2}(n+1)(n-3)}{4}\quad&\text{if $\kappa_{3}=\dots=\kappa_{n-3}=0$ and $\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{n-2}=1$}.\end{cases}

Since
n\geq 9
, we have
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}>n^{2}
. ∎

Combining Lemma
6.1
with the general results of Section
3
, we obtain the following, where the notation
\P(\mathsf{G}_{1},\mathsf{G}_{2})
is as in (
4.4
).

Theorem 6.2

(Classification of faithful representations of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-spaces)
.

Let
n\geq 9
. Let
\mathsf{H}\subsetneq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a closed subgroup and
\mathbb{W}
be a complex
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module of dimension at most
n^{2}
. The homogeneous space
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathsf{H}
admits a faithful representation in
\mathbb{W}
if and only if there are integers
0\leq b\leq n
;
0\leq c\leq 2
;
0\leq d,e\leq 1
such that
\bigl(\frac{c+d}{2}+e-1\bigr)n^{2}+\bigl(b-\frac{c}{2}+\frac{d}{2}\bigr)n-e\leq 0
and

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}^{n\times b}\oplus\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus c}\oplus\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus d}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus e},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{c}\mathsf{H}_{2,i}\biggr)\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}\mathsf{H}_{3,j}\biggr)\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{k=1}^{e}\mathsf{H}_{4,k}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=Q\P\bigl(I_{r},\SL_{n-r}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)Q^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{2,i}

\displaystyle=U_{i}\P\bigl(\Sp_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{C};\Omega_{i}),\SL_{n-2r_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)U_{i}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{3,j}

\displaystyle=V_{j}\S\Bigl(\P\bigl(\O_{s_{j}}(\mathbb{C};B_{j}),\GL_{n-s_{j}}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\Bigr)V_{j}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{4,k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\S\Bigl(\prod_{u=1}^{p}\UT_{n_{k,u,1},\dots,n_{k,u,t_{u}}}\Bigr)A_{k}^{-1},

for some integers
0\leq r\leq b
;
0\leq r_{i}\leq n/2
;
0\leq s_{j}\leq n
;
1\leq n_{k,u,1},\dots,n_{k,u,t_{u}}\leq n
such that

\sum_{u=1}^{p}(n_{k,u,1}+\dots+n_{k,u,t_{u}})=n
, and matrices
Q,U_{i},V_{j}\in\Un_{n}
,
\Omega_{i}\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{i}})\cap\GL_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
,
B_{j}\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{r_{i}})\cap\GL_{r_{j}}(\mathbb{R})
,
A_{k}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, with
1\leq i\leq c
,
1\leq j\leq d
, and
1\leq k\leq e
.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{W}
and
\mathsf{H}
satisfy (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) respectively, then Proposition
4.5
and (
4.3
) collectively imply that
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathsf{H}
has a faithful representation in
\mathbb{W}
. For the converse, we apply Proposition
4.5
and Lemma
6.1
to conclude that
\mathbb{W}
must take the form in (
6.2
). The constraints on
b,c,d,e
follows from the assumption that
\dim\mathbb{W}\leq n^{2}
. Proposition
4.5
also shows that
\mathsf{H}
is the intersection of stabilizers of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{n\times b}
(via left multiplication),
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
(via congruence), and
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(via the adjoint action, which reduces to similarity). Thus (
6.3
) follows from Lemmas
4.9
–
4.12
and (
4.3
).
∎

for some real irreducible

.

Corollary 6.3

(Low dimension
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})

.

Let

. A real irreducible
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-module
\mathbb{U}_{\kappa}
has dimension at most

if and only if it is isomorphic to

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, or
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

Applying the same arguments in the proof of Theorem
6.2
verbatim, we arrive at the required classification.

Proposition 6.4

(Classification of faithful representations of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-spaces)
.

Let
n\geq 9
. Let
\mathsf{H}\subsetneq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a closed subgroup and
\mathbb{U}
be a real
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-module of dimension at most
n^{2}
. The homogeneous space
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathsf{H}
admits a faithful representation in
\mathbb{U}
if and only if there are integers
0\leq b\leq n
;
0\leq c\leq 2
;
0\leq d,e\leq 1
such that
\bigl(\frac{c+d}{2}+e-1\bigr)n^{2}+\bigl(b-\frac{c}{2}+\frac{d}{2}\bigr)n-e\leq 0
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}^{n\times b}\oplus\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\oplus c}\oplus\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\oplus d}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{\oplus e},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{c}\mathsf{H}_{2,i}\biggr)\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}\mathsf{H}_{3,j}\biggr)\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{k=1}^{e}\mathsf{H}_{4,k}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=Q\P\bigl(I_{r},\SL_{n-r}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)Q^{\ast},\qquad\mathsf{H}_{2,i}=U_{i}\P\bigl(\Sp_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{R};\Omega_{i}),\SL_{n-2r_{i}}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)U_{i}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{3,j}

\displaystyle=V_{j}\S\Bigl(\P\bigl(\O_{s_{j}}(\mathbb{R};B_{j}),\GL_{n-s_{j}}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)\Bigr)V_{j}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{4,k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\S\Bigl(\prod_{u=1}^{p}\UT_{n_{k,u,1},\dots,n_{k,u,t_{u}}}\times\prod_{v=1}^{q}\BUT_{m_{k,v,1},\dots,m_{k,v,l_{v}}}\Bigr)A_{k}^{-1},

for some integers
0\leq r\leq b
;
0\leq r_{i}\leq n/2

0\leq s_{j}\leq n

such that
\sum_{u=1}^{p}(n_{k,u,1}+\dots+n_{k,u,t_{u}})+\sum_{v=1}^{q}(m_{k,v,1}+\dots+m_{k,v,l_{v}})=n
and matrices

,
\Omega_{i}\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{i}})\cap\GL_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
,
B_{j}\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{r_{i}})\cap\GL_{r_{j}}(\mathbb{R})
,
A_{k}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
with
1\leq i\leq c
,
1\leq j\leq d
, and
1\leq k\leq e
.

6.3.
Faithful linear representations of
\SU_{n}
-spaces

Unlike the case of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, only some irreducible complex
\SU_{n}
-modules can be obtained from the real ones. We reproduce
[
10
, Proposition 26.24]
for easy reference:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}
be an irreducible complex representation of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
. There exists an irreducible real
\SU_{n}
-module
\mathbb{U}
such that
\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}=\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbb{C}
if and only if
\kappa_{i}=\kappa_{n-i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n-1
and one of the following holds:

(1)

n\equiv 1\pmod{2}
,

(2)

n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,

(3)

n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
\kappa_{n/2}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

In particular, if
\mathbb{W}\leq n^{2}
and
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbb{C}
for some real
\SU_{n}
-module
\mathbb{U}
, then it follows from Lemmas
6.1
and
6.5
that
\mathbb{U}
is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathfrak{su}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.6

(Classification of faithful representations of
\SU_{n}
-spaces)
.

Let
n\geq 9
. Let
\mathsf{H}\subsetneq\SU_{n}
be a closed subgroup and
\mathbb{U}
be a real
\SU_{n}
-module such that
\dim\mathbb{U}\leq n^{2}
. Then
\SU_{n}/\mathsf{H}
admits a faithful representation in
\mathbb{U}
if and only if

\mathbb{U}\cong\mathfrak{su}_{n},\quad\mathsf{H}=Q\S(\Un_{k_{1}}\times\dots\times\Un_{k_{d}})Q^{\ast},

where
Q\in\SU_{n}
,
1\leq k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\leq n
, and
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}=n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.5
,
\mathbb{U}
is either a direct sum of trivial modules or the module of adjoint representation. If
\mathbb{U}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\oplus b}
for some
b\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
\SU_{n}/\mathsf{H}
is a single point since
\SU_{n}
acts trivially on
\mathbb{U}
. In this case
\mathsf{H}=\SU_{n}
. If
\mathbb{U}\cong\mathfrak{su}_{n}
, then by the spectral theorem, we may write any
X\in\mathfrak{su}_{n}
as
X=iQ\diag(\lambda_{1}I_{k_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{d}I_{k_{d}})Q^{\ast}
, where
Q\in\SU_{n}
,
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}=n
, and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
are distinct with
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}=0
. A direct calculation shows that
(\SU_{n})_{X}=Q\S(\Un_{k_{1}}\times\dots\times\Un_{k_{d}})Q^{\ast}
. This together with Lemma
4.3
completes the proof.
∎

7.
Faithful linear representations of Type
\mathrm{B}
and
\mathrm{D}
spaces

In this section we will find all homogeneous spaces
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
that have faithful representations, where
\mathsf{G}=\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
or its real form
\SO_{p,n-p}
,
p=0,1,\dots,n
.

7.1.
Faithful linear representations of
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-spaces

We have the following analogue of Lemma
6.1
for
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 7.1

(Low dimension
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-modules)
.

Let
n\geq 19
. Let
\mathbb{W}
be an irreducible complex
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module. The dimension of
\mathbb{W}
is at most
n^{2}
if and only if
\mathbb{W}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, or
\Sym_{\circ}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{W}
is irreducible,
\mathbb{W}\cong\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}
for some
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
,
m\coloneqq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. It suffices to show that
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\leq n^{2}
if and only if

(1)

n=2m
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
is one of the following
m
-tuples:

(0,\dots,0),\;(1,0,\dots,0),\;(0,\dots,0,1,0,0),\;(0,1,0,\dots,0),\;(0,\dots,0,1,0,0,0),\;(2,0,\dots,0);

(2)

n=2m+1
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
is one of the following
m
-tuples:

(0,\dots,0),\;(1,0,\dots,0),\;(0,\dots,0,1,0),\;(0,1,0,\dots,0),\;(0,\dots,0,1,0,0),\;(2,0,\dots,0).

By Proposition
2.1
, we verify that
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\leq n^{2}
when
\kappa
is one of the
m
-tuples above. It remains to show the converse. Let
\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\leq n^{2}
. We first show that
\kappa_{m}=0
. If
\kappa_{m}\geq 1
, then Lemma
5.1
together with the assumption that
n\geq 19
leads to a contradiction:

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\geq\dim\mathbb{W}_{0,\dots,0,1}=2^{m}>n^{2}.

Next we observe that
\kappa_{m-1}=0
when
n=2m
; otherwise Lemma
5.1
gives

\dim\mathbb{W}_{\kappa}\geq\dim\mathbb{W}_{0,\dots,0,1,0}=2^{m-1}>n^{2}.

Thus we obtain

\kappa=\begin{cases}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m-2},0,0)&\text{if }n=2m,\\
(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m-1},0)&\text{if }n=2m+1.\end{cases}

The rest of the proof is similar to that of Lemma
6.1
, using Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 7.2

(Classification of faithful representations of
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-spaces)
.

Let
n\geq 19
. Let
\mathsf{H}\subsetneq\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a closed subgroup and
\mathbb{W}
be a complex
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module of dimension at most
n^{2}
. The homogeneous space
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathsf{H}
admits a faithful representation in
\mathbb{W}
if and only if there exist integers
0\leq b\leq n
,
0\leq c,d\leq 2
satisfying
(\frac{c+d}{2}-1)n^{2}+(b+\frac{d-c}{2})n-d\leq 0
and

\mathbb{W}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n\times b}\oplus\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus c}\oplus\Sym_{\circ}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus d},\quad\mathsf{H}=\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})\cap\mathsf{H}_{1}\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{c}\mathsf{H}_{2,i}\biggr)\cap\biggl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}\mathsf{H}_{3,j}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=Q\P\bigl(I_{r},\SL_{n-r}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)Q^{\ast},\quad\mathsf{H}_{2,i}=U_{i}\P\bigl(\Sp_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{C};\Omega_{i}),\SL_{n-2r_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)U_{i}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{3,j}

\displaystyle=V_{j}\P\bigl(\O_{s_{j}}(\mathbb{C};B_{j}),\GL_{n-s_{j}}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)V_{j}^{\ast},

for some integers
0\leq r\leq b
,
0\leq r_{i}\leq n/2
,
0\leq s_{j}\leq n
and matrices
Q,U_{i},V_{j}\in\Un_{n}
,
\Omega_{i}\in\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{i}})\cap\GL_{2r_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
,
B_{j}\in\S^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s_{j}})\cap\GL_{s_{j}}(\mathbb{R})
with
1\leq i\leq c
and
1\leq j\leq d
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
and Lemma
7.1
,
\mathbb{W}
and
\mathsf{H}
take the forms above. The constraints on the integers
b
,
c
, and
d
follow from
\dim\mathbb{W}\leq n^{2}
. To determine
\mathsf{H}_{1},\mathsf{H}_{2,i}
and
\mathsf{H}_{3,j}
, we compute the stabilizers of matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times b}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
\Sym_{\circ}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
under left multiplication and congruence by
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, using Lemmas
4.9
–
4.11
.
∎

7.2.
Faithful linear representations of
\SO_{p,n-p}
-spaces

A complex module
\mathbb{W}
of a real Lie group
\mathsf{G}
is
real
if
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbb{C}
for some real module
\mathbb{U}
, and is
quaternionic
if it is contained in a quaternionic module
[
10
, Section 26.3]
.

Lemma 7.3

(Low dimension
\SO_{p,n-p}
-module)
.

Let
n\geq 19
and
0\leq p\leq n
. Let
\mathbb{U}
be an irreducible real
\SO_{p,n-p}
-module. The dimension of
\mathbb{U}
is at most
n^{2}
if and only if
\mathbb{U}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};I_{p,n-p})
, or
\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{R}^{n};I_{p,n-p})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{U}_{0}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n},\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p}),\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p})\}
. We first verify that

\mathbb{W}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{U}_{0}\otimes\mathbb{C}\in\{\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}^{n},\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\},

showing that
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are real in the sense of the comment preceding this lemma. This is clearly the case if
\mathbb{U}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
\mathbb{U}_{0}=\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p})
, consider the complex linear map

\varphi:\mathbb{W}_{0}=\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p})\oplus i\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p})\to\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\quad\varphi(X_{1},iX_{2})=I_{p,n-p}(X_{1}+iX_{2}).

Since
(X_{1}+iX_{2})^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}I_{p,n-p}=-I_{p,n-p}(X_{1}+iX_{2})
,
\varphi
is well-defined. A direct calculation verifies that
\varphi
is bijective and
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant. Thus
\mathbb{W}_{0}\cong\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. The proof for
\mathbb{U}_{0}=\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{R}^{n},I_{p,n-p})
is nearly identical and thus omitted.

Next, let
\mathbb{U}
be an irreducible real
\SO_{p,n-p}
-module of dimension at most
n^{2}
. By Lemma
7.1
,
\mathbb{W}\coloneqq\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. By Schur’s Lemma, the algebra
\End_{\SO_{p,n-p}}(\mathbb{U})
consisting of
\SO_{p,n-p}
-equivariant endomorphisms of
\mathbb{U}
is a division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore
\End_{\SO_{p,n-p}}(\mathbb{U})\cong\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
, or
\mathbb{H}
, and, accordingly,
\mathbb{W}
is either irreducible, splits into the direct sum of two conjugate modules, or splits into the direct sum of two isomorphic modules. Since
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are all self-conjugate modules, we rule out
\End_{\SO_{p,n-p}}(\mathbb{U})\cong\mathbb{C}
. So
\End_{\SO_{p,n-p}}(\mathbb{U})\cong\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{H}
and

\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{C}^{\oplus a}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus b}\oplus\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus c}\oplus\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\oplus d},

where either
a+b+c+d=1
or one of
a,b,c,d
is
2
and the others are
0
. Suppose the latter holds. Then
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{W}_{1}^{\oplus 2}
for some
\mathbb{W}_{1}\in\{\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}^{n},\Alt^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\Sym^{2}_{\circ}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\}
, implying that
\End_{\SO_{p,n-p}}(\mathbb{U})\cong\mathbb{H}
. But this in turn implies that
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is quaternionic, contradicting the fact that the modules
\mathbb{C}
,

10.
Conclusion

The four contributions in this work are, in order of increasing specificity, (i) proposing a general theory of representations of
\mathsf{G}
-manifolds (Section
3
); (ii) specializing to the representations of homogeneous spaces
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
with emphasis on faithful ones (Section
4
); (iii) giving a near-complete classification of faithfully representable
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
when
\mathsf{G}
is a simple classical group (Sections
5
–
8
); and (iv) describing these faithful representations explicitly and proving their minimality (Section
9
).

As we mentioned in Section
1
, the focus on faithful manifold representations here is natural, because faithful group and algebra representations also took precedence in the history of these subjects: Group representation theory grew out of Dedekind and Frobenius’s exchanges about the group determinant
[
22
, p. 366]
, and underlying it is the regular representation, a faithful representation. Likewise, Lie’s original study of Lie algebras as infinitesimal transformations is, in modern lingo, a faithful representation on the space of smooth functions
[
15
, p. 252]
.

Just as a faithful representation of a group encodes group operation as matrix multiplication and a faithful representation of an algebra encodes Lie bracket as matrix commutator, a faithful representation of a
\mathsf{G}
-manifold encodes the group action as a matrix product. In applications and computations, having a matrix representation of an abstract object can be enormously beneficial, allowing for abstract operations to be concretely performed via standard numerical linear algebra. Take manifold optimization
[
9
]
for example, the list of candidate manifolds in this vastly popular subject is unfortunately quite limited, essentially one has just four choices for
\mathcal{M}

[
40
, p. 622]
. But with the work in this article, the list has now been expanded to not just the
25
manifolds in Tables
5
but infinitely many possibilities with Theorem
9.1
.

From a purely mathematical angle, the results in Section
9
show that faithful representations can shed new light on old geometry problems: We obtained the best possible effective bounds for the dimensions of Mostow–Palais embeddings in Table
6
. Prior to these results, it was only known that these dimensions are finite; there were no effective bounds.

Future work

The are several immediate avenues to extend our study of faithfulness: (a) when
\mathsf{G}
is one of the omitted simple classical groups
\SO^{*}_{2n}
,
\SU_{p,q}
,
\Sp_{p,q}
,
\SU^{*}_{2n}
, or an exceptional group; (b) when
\mathsf{G}
is semisimple or reductive; (c) when the
\mathsf{G}
-action on
\mathcal{M}
is nontransitive. Nevertheless, we view the study of faithful representations as a beginning. Such representations are
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant
embeddings
into a space of matrices. So we might for instance study
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant
immersions
or
submersions
in the context of manifold representation.
