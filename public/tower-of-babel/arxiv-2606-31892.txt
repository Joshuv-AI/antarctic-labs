Better Understanding, Understanding Better
Yu Wei

Abstract
“Any fool can know; the point is to understand.” A well-known remark often attributed to Einstein captures a widely shared intuition: understanding is more than merely knowing. Yet epistemic logic has paid relatively little attention to understanding, despite its central role in contemporary epistemology, philosophy of science, and recent debates about AI. A recurring theme in the philosophical literature is that, unlike knowledge, understanding comes in degrees: one may understand something more or less well, and one’s understanding may be better than another’s. We introduce a comparative epistemic logic of understanding with graded modalities 𝖴 i τ \mathsf{U}_{i}^{\tau} and a comparative connective ( i ≻ j ) ​ φ (i\succ j)\varphi for “ i i understands why φ \varphi better than j j ”. Semantically, we enrich multi-agent epistemic models with agent-indexed graded explanation structures and a justification-style term algebra. This yields a unified framework for representing minimal, ordinary, more demanding, and ideal understanding, together with comparisons between agents with respect to the same formula at issue. We distinguish a finitary bounded-level calculus from an infinitary full-language companion system. We establish soundness and strong completeness, and show that each fixed finite-level fragment is decidable.

Modern epistemic logic has rich tools for knowledge and belief, but much less machinery for these two features taken together: degree-sensitive understanding and comparative understanding.

This work is best read as a synthesis of epistemic, comparative, and

At its base, the framework remains an epistemic logic in the strict technical sense:

1.2
Framework and Contributions

We propose a comparative epistemic logic of understanding with two operator families:

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\tau}\varphi
for level-indexed understanding-why and
(i\succ j)\varphi
for comparative understanding with respect to a formula. At the conceptual level, we take finite indices
\tau
to range over
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
. This already lets the language capture the spectrum emphasized in

[
21
]
: minimal understanding
<
everyday understanding

<
typical scientist’s understanding
<
ideal understanding. Schematically, these may be represented by

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\varphi,\mathsf{U}_{i}^{2}\varphi,\mathsf{U}_{i}^{m}\varphi,\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\varphi
.
Level
1
is intended to model a minimal explanatory foothold
(a necessary but not yet typical notion of understanding),
while level
2
is intended to capture ordinary or everyday explanatory understanding
in a higher-order sense
[
22
,
32
]
.
Some finite level
m>2
can then be used to represent typical scientific understanding.
Higher levels correspond to explanations that are more systematically organized
and more deeply integrated into scientific understanding.
This level-sensitive hierarchy fits Khalifa’s spectrum picture, in which ideal understanding
is a limit notion
[
21
]
. Formally, we capture that limit by requiring every finite level:

\bigwedge_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi
.

The numerical levels are a formal idealization. Finite indices on the understanding operators represent an ordered family of explanatory standards
fixed in a model; a larger index marks a more demanding standard for the same formula. Depending on the application, such standards may concern specificity, systematic integration, or counterfactual robustness. The logic does not decide which substantive features make one standard stronger than another in every
application; it only provides a framework for representing such standards once fixed. Meeting a stronger standard entails meeting weaker standards for the same formula, not possessing every particular explanation that might satisfy a weaker
standard.

Our main technical contributions are fourfold. First, we give a graded semantics for level-indexed and comparative understanding, with comparisons always relative to the formula at issue. Second, for each fixed finite understanding-level domain, we introduce a finitary calculus and establish soundness, strong completeness, and decidability. Third, for the full language, we move to an infinitary extension of the finitary calculus, reflecting the ideal-understanding level, and prove soundness and strong completeness. Fourth, we isolate the boundary between the bounded and full languages: in each fixed finite-level fragment the comparative connective is eliminable, while in the full language it is not; moreover, the full language is non-compact, so no sound finitary proof system can be strongly complete for it.

The rest follows this architecture.

2
introduces syntax and semantics.
Section
3
presents the calculi and soundness.
Section
4
proves bounded and full completeness and gives a
bounded decidability result.
Section
5
concludes.

Remark 2.4
.

(i\succ j)\varphi
becomes eliminable in the comparative-free fragment of

That is, for every model

Proposition 2.5

In
\mathbf{CELU}

(i\succ j)\varphi

Proof.

It suffices to show non-eliminability for one instance, say

Suppose

than all those indices.
Take two pointed models

with the same valuation, in particular with

-formula is false

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix distinct agents
i\neq j
and an atom

. Let

\mathbf{CELU}

For any finite

Definition 3.1

(Finitary calculus)
.

\displaystyle(\mathtt{TAUT})

\displaystyle(\mathtt{DISTK})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{K}_{i}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\to\mathsf{K}_{i}\psi)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{T})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\to\varphi

\displaystyle(\mathtt{4})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\to\mathsf{K}_{i}\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi

\displaystyle(\mathtt{5})

\displaystyle\quad\neg\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\to\mathsf{K}_{i}\neg\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi

\displaystyle(\mathtt{DISTU})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\to\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi)

\displaystyle\quad(n\in L)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{DEG})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\to\mathsf{U}_{i}^{m}\varphi

\displaystyle\quad(n,m\in L,\ n>m)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{UYK})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\to\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi

\displaystyle\quad(n\in L)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{4}^{*})

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\to\mathsf{K}_{i}\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi

\displaystyle\quad(n\in L)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{KYU})

\displaystyle(\mathtt{CMPn})

\displaystyle(\mathtt{CMP}1)

\displaystyle\quad(i\succ j)\varphi\rightarrow\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\varphi\wedge\mathsf{K}_{j}\varphi

\displaystyle(\mathtt{CMP}\kappa)

\displaystyle\quad(i\succ j)\varphi\rightarrow\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{\kappa}\varphi.

The inference rules are:

(\mathtt{MP})

Modus Ponens

(\mathtt{N})

\vdash\varphi/\ \vdash\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi

(\mathtt{NE})

\varphi\in\Lambda/\ \vdash\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\varphi

.

Definition 3.2

(Full calculus)
.

The system
\mathsf{SCU}
over
\mathbf{CELU}
is the
\omega
-companion of
\mathsf{SCU}_{\kappa}
:
it has the same finitary schemas/rules as Definition

with all finite level indices ranging over

, except that the finite-language upper-bound axiom

is replaced by

(\mathtt{CMP}\omega)\quad(i\succ j)\varphi\rightarrow\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{\omega}\varphi,

and it additionally includes the following axiom schema:

(\mathtt{DEG}_{\omega}^{n})\quad\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\varphi\rightarrow\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\ \ (n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}),

and the following infinitary rule schema:

(\omega\mathrm{I})\quad\frac{\{\chi\to\mathsf{K}_{j_{1}}(\theta_{1}\to\cdots\mathsf{K}_{j_{m}}(\theta_{m}\to\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi)\cdots)\mid n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\}}{\chi\to\mathsf{K}_{j_{1}}(\theta_{1}\to\cdots\mathsf{K}_{j_{m}}(\theta_{m}\to\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\varphi)\cdots)}.

Here
m\geqslant 0
,
j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\in I
, and

\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}\in\mathbf{CELU}
. If

, the rule is read without

-operators, i.e.,

If

, the rule includes the instance

\{\chi\to\mathsf{K}_{j}\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\mid n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\}/(\chi\to\mathsf{K}_{j}\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\varphi)

That is, under the same assumption
\chi
, if
j
knows that
i
has
understanding of
\varphi
at every finite level,
then
j
also knows that
i
has ideal understanding of
\varphi
.
Thus
m>0
makes the same limit step available
inside epistemic implication contexts.

Since
\omega
-introduction is an infinitary rule with countably many premises,
we represent derivability of system
\mathsf{SCU}
by well-founded proof trees, following the treatment of infinitary formal proofs in

[

]
.

Definition 3.3

(
\mathsf{SCU}
-derivability)
.

A
proof tree from
\Sigma
is a well-founded tree whose nodes are labeled by formulas.
Each node is either an assumption leaf (label in
\Sigma
), an axiom leaf
(instance of a
\mathsf{SCU}
-axiom), or is obtained by one of the following rules:

•

(\mathtt{MP})
: from children labeled
\psi\to\chi
and
\psi
, conclude
\chi
;

•

(\mathtt{NE})
: conclude
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\psi
for
\psi\in\Lambda
;

•

(\mathtt{N})
: from one
closed
child labeled
\psi
, conclude
\mathsf{K}_{i}\psi
;

•

(\omega\mathrm{I})
: for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, from children
labeled

\chi\to\mathsf{K}_{j_{1}}(\theta_{1}\to\cdots\mathsf{K}_{j_{m}}(\theta_{m}\to\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi)\cdots)
,
one for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, conclude the corresponding formula with

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi
in place of
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi
;

where “closed” means that the corresponding subtree has no assumption leaves.
We write
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\varphi
iff there exists such a proof tree with root label
\varphi
.
A set
\Sigma
is
\mathsf{SCU}
-consistent
iff
\Sigma\nvdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\bot
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\psi
and
\Sigma\cup\{\psi\}\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\chi
, then

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\chi
.

Proof.

Graft a fresh copy of a proof tree of
\psi
from
\Sigma
onto each assumption leaf
labeled
\psi
in a proof tree of
\chi
from
\Sigma\cup\{\psi\}
, leaving all other nodes unchanged and preserving the original rule applications. The result is a proof tree from
\Sigma
. The only point requiring checking is
the requirement in
(\mathtt{N})
that its premise child be closed: this is preserved because a closed child subtree contains no assumption leaves, so no grafting takes place inside it. Well-foundedness is also preserved: any descending chain either stays in the original tree or eventually enters a single grafted copy, both of which are well-founded.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\Sigma\nvdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\varphi
, then

\Sigma\cup\{\neg\varphi\}
is
\mathsf{SCU}
-consistent.

Proof.

Suppose that

\Sigma\cup\{\neg\varphi\}\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\bot
. By induction on proof trees, we first obtain:
if
\Sigma\cup\{\alpha\}\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\beta
, then

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\alpha\to\beta
.
The finitary cases are standard. For
an
\omega
-introduction instance, write its premises as
\chi\to B_{n}

(n\in\mathbb{N}^{+})
and its conclusion as
\chi\to B_{\omega}
, where

B_{\omega}
is obtained from
B_{n}
by replacing the displayed

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi
with
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi
. By the induction hypothesis,

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\alpha\to(\chi\to B_{n})
for all
n
. Hence

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}(\alpha\wedge\chi)\to B_{n}
for all
n
, so

(\omega\mathrm{I})
gives

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}(\alpha\wedge\chi)\to B_{\omega}
, and therefore

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\alpha\to(\chi\to B_{\omega})
. Then we get
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\neg\varphi\to\bot
, therefore
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\varphi
, contradicting the hypothesis. So
\Sigma\cup\{\neg\varphi\}
is consistent.
∎

4
Completeness and Bounded Decidability

This section proves strong completeness for the bounded finitary and full infinitary
calculi, and also establishes bounded decidability. We therefore use two
canonical-model constructions: a bounded one, following the witness-based strategy of

[
36
,
32
]
but adapted to graded witnesses, agent-indexed
explanation stores, and comparative formulas, and a full-language construction based on maximal consistent sets closed under the infinitary rule.

We first construct the bounded canonical model for
\mathsf{SCU}_{\kappa}
. Let
\Omega
be the set of all maximal
\mathsf{SCU}_{\kappa}
-consistent sets of

\mathbf{CELU}^{\kappa}
-formulas.

Definition 4.1
.

The canonical model for
\mathsf{SCU}_{\kappa}
is

\mathcal{M}^{c}=(W^{c},\{R_{i}^{c}\mid i\in I\},V^{c},E^{c},\mathrm{gr}^{c},\{\mathcal{E}_{i}^{c}\mid i\in I\})
, where:

•

is generated by the BNF:

t::=c\mid\varphi^{n}\mid(t\cdot t)\mid(t+t)\mid!t
,
where

,

•

The canonical grade map
\mathrm{gr}^{c}:E^{c}\to\mathbb{N}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathrm{gr}^{c}(c)&=&1,\\
\mathrm{gr}^{c}(\varphi^{n})&=&n,\\
\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t\cdot s)&=&\min\{\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t),\mathrm{gr}^{c}(s)\},\\[2.84526pt]
\mathrm{gr}^{c}(!t)&=&\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t)=0,\\
2,&\text{if }\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t)>0,\end{cases}\\[8.53581pt]
\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t+s)&=&\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\min\{\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t),\mathrm{gr}^{c}(s)\}=0,\\
\min\{\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t),\mathrm{gr}^{c}(s)\}-1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

For each

.

•

is the set of all triples
\langle\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle

such that
\Gamma\in\Omega
and:

–

\vec{f}=\{f_{i}^{n}\mid i\in I,\ n\in\{1,\dots,\kappa\}\}

f_{i}^{n}:\{\varphi\mid\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\in\Gamma\}\to\{t\in E^{c}\mid\mathrm{gr}^{c}(t)=n\}

–

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{i}\mid i\in I\}

is the
agent-
i
explanation store

satisfying the following base and admissibility conditions relative to

\Gamma
and
\vec{f}
:

\begin{array}[]{@{}lp{0.78\linewidth}@{}}\textit{(Base${}_{i}$)}&$\{\langle c,\varphi\rangle\mid\varphi\in\Lambda\}\subseteq F_{i}$ and
$\{\langle f_{i}^{n}(\varphi),\varphi\rangle\mid\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\in\Gamma\}\subseteq F_{i}$;\\[2.84526pt]
\textit{(App${}_{i}$)}&if $\langle t,\varphi\to\psi\rangle,\langle s,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$ then $\langle t\cdot s,\psi\rangle\in F_{i}$;\\[2.84526pt]
\textit{(Sum${}_{i}$)}&if $\langle t,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$ or $\langle s,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$ then $\langle t+s,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$;\\[2.84526pt]
\textit{(Int${}_{i}$)}&if $\langle t,\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$, then $\langle!t,\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}$.\end{array}

•

\langle\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle R_{i}^{c}\langle\Delta,\mathcal{G},\vec{g}\rangle
iff
\{\varphi\mid\mathsf{K}_{i}\varphi\in\Gamma\}\subseteq\Delta\
and
\forall n\in\{1,\dots,\kappa\}\ (f_{i}^{n}=g_{i}^{n})
.

•

For each
i\in I
,
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{c}:E^{c}\times\mathbf{CELU}^{\kappa}\to 2^{W^{c}}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{i}^{c}(t,\varphi):=\{\langle\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle\in W^{c}\mid\langle t,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}\}
.

•

V^{c}(p):=\{\langle\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle\in W^{c}\mid p\in\Gamma\}
.

In this construction, each canonical world

packages exact-grade witnesses for the
\mathsf{U}
-formulas in
\Gamma
.
For each

f_{i}^{n}
selects a term

Each

i
), (Sum
i

The specific clauses in the canonical grading

still satisfies the original semantic constraints and, at the same time, supports the later
technical arguments.

Definition 4.2

, and assume

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\varphi\notin\Gamma
and
\langle t,\varphi\rangle\in F_{i}
for some

t\in E_{n}^{c}
.
There exists
w^{\prime}=\langle\Delta,\mathcal{G},\vec{g}\rangle\in W_{\infty}^{c}
such that

wR_{i}^{c}w^{\prime}
and
\langle t,\varphi\rangle\notin G_{i}
.

Proof.

It is the same as Lemma
4.8
, with

\mathbb{N}^{+}
in place of
\{1,\dots,\kappa\}
.
\omega
-introduction is not used.
∎

Lemma 4.16

(Truth Lemma for
\mathbf{CELU}
)
.

For
\varphi\in\mathbf{CELU}
and

w=\langle\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle\in W_{\infty}^{c}
:

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\varphi
iff
\varphi\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Induction on
\varphi
.
The Boolean cases are standard. The
\mathsf{K}_{i}
case uses
Lemma
4.14
. The finite-level
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}
case is the same as in
Lemma
4.9
, using Lemma
4.15
for the
refutation direction.

For
\varphi=\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi
.

•

If
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi\in\Gamma
, then by
(\mathtt{DEG}_{\omega}^{n})
,

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi\in\Gamma
for every
n\geqslant 1
. By IH,

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi

for every
n\geqslant 1
, hence

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi
.

•

If
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi
,
then by semantics

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi

for all
n\geqslant 1
, so by the finite-level
\mathsf{U}
-case

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{n}\psi\in\Gamma
for all
n\geqslant 1
.
By closure under the basic
\omega
-introduction instance,

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{\omega}\psi\in\Gamma
.

For
\varphi=(i\succ j)\psi
.
The case
i=j
is the same as in Lemma
4.9
. Assume
i\neq j
.

•

Assume
(i\succ j)\psi\in\Gamma
.
By
(\mathtt{CMP}1)
and
(\mathtt{CMP}\omega)
,

\mathsf{K}_{j}\psi,\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\psi,\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{\omega}\psi\in\Gamma
; hence

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\mathsf{K}_{j}\psi
by the preceding
\mathsf{K}
-case.
Let
S_{j}:=\{n\geqslant 1\mid\mathsf{U}_{j}^{n}\psi\in\Gamma\}
.
By
(\mathtt{DEG})
,
S_{j}
is downward closed and
S_{j}\neq\mathbb{N}^{+}
; otherwise the basic

\omega
-introduction instance would contradict

\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{\omega}\psi\in\Gamma
.
If
S_{j}=\emptyset
, then
\deg_{j}(w,\psi)=0
, while

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\psi\in\Gamma
gives
\deg_{i}(w,\psi)\geqslant 1
by the finite-level
\mathsf{U}
-case.
If
S_{j}\neq\emptyset
, let
m+1
be the least not in
S_{j}
.
Then
m\geqslant 1
and
S_{j}=\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Hence
\mathsf{U}_{j}^{m}\psi,\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{m+1}\psi\in\Gamma
and, by
(\mathtt{CMPn})
,

\mathsf{U}_{i}^{m+1}\psi\in\Gamma
.
By the finite-level
\mathsf{U}
-case,

\deg_{j}(w,\psi)=m
and
\deg_{i}(w,\psi)\geqslant m+1
.
In either case,
\deg_{i}(w,\psi)>\deg_{j}(w,\psi)
, so

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash(i\succ j)\psi
.

•

Assume

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash(i\succ j)\psi
.
Then
\mathsf{K}_{j}\psi\in\Gamma
by the
\mathsf{K}
-case. The degree inequality
also gives
\deg_{j}(w,\psi)\neq\omega
.
If
\deg_{j}(w,\psi)=0
, then

\deg_{i}(w,\psi)\geqslant 1
and
\mathsf{U}_{j}^{1}\psi
is false. By the finite-level
\mathsf{U}
-case,
\mathsf{U}_{i}^{1}\psi,\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{1}\psi\in\Gamma
; hence

(i\succ j)\psi\in\Gamma
by
(\mathtt{UYC}0)
.
If
\deg_{j}(w,\psi)=m\geqslant 1
, then

\deg_{i}(w,\psi)\geqslant m+1
. By the finite-level
\mathsf{U}
-case,

\mathsf{U}_{j}^{m}\psi,\neg\mathsf{U}_{j}^{m+1}\psi,\mathsf{U}_{i}^{m+1}\psi\in\Gamma
; then
(\mathtt{UYC})
yields

(i\succ j)\psi\in\Gamma
.

∎

Theorem 4.17

(Strong completeness for
\mathsf{SCU}
)
.

For all
\Sigma\cup\{\varphi\}\subseteq\mathbf{CELU}
,

\Sigma\models\varphi
implies
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\varphi
.

Proof.

Assume
\Sigma\nvdash_{\mathsf{SCU}}\varphi
.
By Lemmas
3.5
and
4.12
, choose

\Gamma^{\infty}\in\Omega_{\infty}
with

\Sigma\cup\{\neg\varphi\}\subseteq\Gamma^{\infty}
.
By Lemma
4.13
, choose
\mathcal{F},\vec{f}

such that
w:=\langle\Gamma^{\infty},\mathcal{F},\vec{f}\rangle\in W_{\infty}^{c}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c}
be the corresponding canonical model over
\Omega_{\infty}
.
By Lemma
4.16
,

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\Sigma
and
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{c},w\vDash\neg\varphi
.
Thus
\Sigma\not\models\varphi
.
∎

5
Conclusion

We have developed a comparative epistemic logic of understanding. On the semantic side, the framework is based on agent-indexed graded explanations;
on the proof-theoretic side, it separates a bounded finitary layer from a full
infinitary layer. In this way, the logic captures understanding in degrees and
comparative understanding with respect to the same formula at issue, while also supporting
strong completeness for the intended calculi and decidability for each fixed
finite-level fragment.

The framework also suggests several natural directions for further work.
On the technical side, it would be worth studying richer proof-theoretic
presentations for the full language. On the dynamic side, one may ask how graded and comparative understanding behaves
under information change, communication, or learning. More broadly, the present
logic invites closer comparison both with other comparative epistemic frameworks
and with neighboring formal accounts of explanation and understanding.
