𝔾 m {\mathbb{G}}_{m} -cohomology of p p -adic Stein spaces
Sally Gilles, Damien Junger

Abstract
. We compute the étale 𝔾 m {\mathbb{G}}_{m} -cohomology of some p p -adic rigid analytic Stein spaces. The computation is done by considering the filtration induced by the subgroup of principal units U = 1 + 𝔪 ​ 𝒪 + U=1+\mathfrak{m}\mathscr{O}^{+} of 𝔾 m {\mathbb{G}}_{m} . We then determine the U U -cohomology via methods from p p -adic Hodge theory (passage to the pro-étale site, comparison theorems with p p -adic cohomologies), while the 𝔾 m / U {\mathbb{G}}_{m}/U -cohomology is obtained using Kummer exact sequences. In particular, our formula applies to the case of Drinfeld upper-half space.



1.
Introduction

Our aim in this work is to describe the cohomology of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, the étale sheaf of invertible functions, for some classical rigid spaces and more precisely smooth Stein spaces over a complete algebraically closed field
C/{\bf Q}_{p}
(to avoid the contribution of the base fields and other technical descent). One of the main interests of these groups comes from the fact that they admit geometric interpretations, more particularly in degrees
0
,
1
and
2
. For example, if a space
X
is the complement of a normal crossing divisor
F
on a proper space
\bar{X}
(i.e.
X=\bar{X}\setminus F
), the group
\hhh^{0}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
should reflect some aspects of the geometry of its compactification
\bar{X}
. On the other hand, the first degree is the Picard group which classifies some locally free modules of rank
1
over the structure sheaf
{\mathscr{O}}
, whereas the second degree classifies, at least in the algebraic case, some Azumaya algebras and Severi-Brauer varieties and these groups constitute deep invariants of the studied space.

Those cohomology groups should also carry some motivic aspects of the space in question. For example, we have as many
\ell
-adic cohomology groups as prime integers
\ell
, and they all live over different fields with different structures and topologies. The sheaf
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on the other hand only admits a structure of
{\bf Z}
-module but it can be related to all of these groups at the same time by the Kummer exact sequence suggesting the hope to exhibit a common
{\bf Z}
-structure.

A lot is known for the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-cohomology for algebraic spaces, but the situation is rather mysterious on the analytic case, except in degree
0
or
1
, where they could be compared to the cohomology on the analytic site (see for example for more detail on this strategy
[
28
,
18
]
). However, many phenomena that can only be seen on the étale site vanish in higher degrees and new methods are needed (for example, in most of the examples treated in previous cited works, the analytic cohomology of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
will vanish in degree higher than
2
whereas étale cohomology will still exhibit interesting representations).

Let us say here a few words about the algebraic case from which we will draw our inspiration for the strategy of this paper. Here the key result is the following observation of Grothendieck.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
13
, Proposition 1.4]

For a regular noetherian scheme
X/C
, the groups
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
are torsion in degree
i\geq 2
.

This allows, thanks to the Kummer exact sequence, to completely control the cohomology of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
by the
\ell
-adic cohomology for all prime
\ell
. More precisely if the groups
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\bf Z}/\ell^{n}{\bf Z}(-1))
are all free over
{\bf Z}/\ell^{n}{\bf Z}
of rank
\alpha_{i}
independent of the prime
\ell
and the integer
n
, then we would expect an identity of the form

\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\simeq\bigoplus_{\ell}({\bf Q}_{\ell}/{\bf Z}_{\ell})^{\alpha_{i}}\simeq({\bf Q}/{\bf Z})^{\alpha_{i}}.

This is best illustrated in the classical formula for a point
X=\operatorname{Spec}(K)
(more precisely when
K/{\bf Q}_{p}
is a finite extension)

{\rm Br}(K)\simeq{\bf Q}/{\bf Z}

proved via Hilbert’s Theorem 90 and the calculation of the
\ell
-adic cohomology of
K
(even if this particular example has preceded historically and suggested this strategy).

The idea is to exhibit a presentation of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

0\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\iota_{\eta,*}K(\eta)\to{\rm Div}\to 0

where each
\iota_{\eta,*}K(\eta)
and
{\rm Div}
can be related to pushforwards of sheaves on points of
X
. But the cohomology of these sheaves are known to be torsion on non-zero degrees by classical results on cohomology of profinite groups. Unfortunately, this strategy completely fails for analytic spaces where we could not have such a simple description of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
in terms of points.

Even worse, we can exhibit smooth spaces for which these groups are not torsion. This is mainly due to the fact that the category of torsion groups is not stable under infinite limit in the category of groups. In particular, when we are interested in a space
X=\bigcup_{n}X_{n}
admitting a presentation by increasing affinoid open
X_{n}
(typically a Stein space), then the group
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
should admit as a quotient
\varprojlim_{n}\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
, which could very well be not torsion even when all the groups
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
are themselves torsion. It will be indeed the case for our main example, the Drinfeld symmetric space. However in the examples we consider, all the desired cohomology groups are torsion for every opens
X_{n}
making the obstruction still controlled and manageable.

Our hope is to exhibit a sufficiently large class of smooth quasicompact spaces for which those cohomology groups are indeed torsion. The first intermediate result of this paper actually builds on this intuition. To properly state it, we introduce the usual Stein covering of the Drinfeld space
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}=\bigcup_{n}U_{n}
of dimension
d\geq 1
over
C
(see for example
[
23
, §1]
).

Proposition 1.2
.

(Corollary
5.3
)
The closed balls
{\bf B}(r)
, the closed annuli
{\bf A}(r,s)
and the opens
U_{n}
have torsion
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-cohomology in degree
i\geq 2
.

We say a few words about the arguments. The sheaf
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
admits, as a subgroup, the group of principal units
U:=1+{\mathscr{O}}^{++}\subset{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
which consists of the elements of the form
1+f
with
f
topologically nilpotent. Denoting by
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}/U
the quotient, both groups
U
and
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}
have intimate relations with coherent groups thanks to the following
\log
and
\exp
exact sequences

\displaystyle 1\to\mu_{p^{\infty}}\to U\xrightarrow{{\rm log}}{\mathscr{O}}\to 1,

\displaystyle 1\to{\mathscr{O}}\xrightarrow{{\rm exp}}{\mathscr{O}}^{\times}[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to{\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}}^{\times}\to 1.

The long exact sequence associated to
1\to U\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}\to 1
allows to focus on studying the torsion of both groups
U
and
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}
. The first one is automatically torsion when the space is quasi-compact and has no coherent cohomology thanks to the
\log
exact sequence, and we are reduced to study
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}
. For the moment, we can only show the desired torsion property for specific examples, namely the balls, the annuli and other spaces built around these two such as the opens
U_{n}
. On the other hand,
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}
is related to the invertible functions on the special fiber when there exists a nice enough model and our hope is that in the general case (at least the semi-stable one), this question could be treated by reduction to the algebraic case. This is somewhat close to what we did for the balls and the annuli. Actually, we have shown this intermediate property.

Proposition 1.3
.

(Lemma
5.2
)
If
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times})
is torsion then so are
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X\times{\bf B}(r),\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times})
and
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X\times{\bf A}(r,s),\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times})
for any smooth
X
.

The argument presented here actually reveals some remarkable phenomenon as the group
U
only exhibits the
p^{\infty}
-torsion of
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
, whereas
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times}
is related to the prime-to-
p
torsion (see Lemma
2.4
and Remark
2.6
). Thanks to this intermediate result, we can determine the cohomology of each open affinoid
X_{n}
in the Stein covering of
X
by the Kummer exact sequence, and then apply a limit argument to completely describe it as an extension of
\varprojlim_{n}\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
by
\rrr^{1}\varprojlim_{n}\hhh^{i-1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
by the usual formalism of the derived limit. Thanks to this strategy, we are able to describe
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
for some classical Stein spaces and obtain the main result of our paper.

.

We have, for the affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
of dimension
d\geq 1
over
C

\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}({\mathbb{A}}^{d},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\simeq\Omega^{i-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{d})(-i+1)/{\rm Ker}(d),

and for

and

such that the graded pieces are:

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{0}{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,C}^{d})

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega^{i-1}({\mathbb{G}}^{d}_{m,C})(-i+1)/{\rm Ker}(d),\ \ \ {\rm gr}^{1}{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,C}^{d})\simeq(\wedge^{i}{\bf Q}^{d}_{p}/\wedge^{i}{\bf Z}^{d}_{p})(-i+1),

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{2}{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,C}^{d})\simeq\bigoplus_{\ell\neq p}(\wedge^{i}{\bf Q}^{d}_{\ell}/\wedge^{i}{\bf Z}^{d}_{\ell})(-i+1);

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{0}{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{H}}^{d})

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega^{i-1}({\mathbb{H}}^{d})(-i+1)/{\rm Ker}(d),\ \ \ {\rm gr}^{1}{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t},{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{H}}^{d})\simeq({\rm Sp}_{i}({\bf Q}_{p})^{*}/{\rm Sp}_{i}({\bf Z}_{p})^{*})(-i+1),

where the sum is completed for some well-chosen topology (see §

Remark 1.5
.

•

We can actually construct a filtration
{\mathscr{F}}^{(i)}(X)
of
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
for any smooth Stein space
X/C
.

•

For the affine space, the first and second graded pieces
{\mathscr{F}}^{(i)}({\mathbb{A}}^{d})
are trivial and all three descriptions above are actually analogous.

•

The graded pieces of this filtration are not explicit for any smooth Stein space, but we give general conditions for which we can achieve the calculation. All the examples

,
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{H}}
above satisfy these hypotheses.

•

Fix
(X,(X_{n})_{n}))
a smooth Stein space together with a fixed Stein covering and let us put specify the three main conditions metnioned in the previous point (the rest of hypotheses are there to avoid some technicalities and pathologies in the limit argument), namely we ask for the groups
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
to be torsion, for the groups
\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X_{n},{\bf Z}/\ell^{k}{\bf Z})
with

to be flat over

and for the injectivity of the maps

for all

, where

denotes the

-twisted Hyodo-Kato cohomology from Colmez-Dospinescu-Nizioł (see Section

•

We can also construct a similar filtration for the pro-étale cohomology and calculate its graded pieces under the same general conditions.

Let us explain how to construct such a filtration
{\mathscr{F}}^{(i)}(X)
for a general smooth Stein space
X
. By dévissage of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
by the principal units
U
, we have a filtration with subobject

{\mathscr{F}}_{1}^{(i)}(X):={\rm Im}(\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,U)\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m}))

and quotient

{\rm gr}^{2}{\mathscr{F}}^{(i)}(X):={\rm Im}(\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times})).

Note that by the conclusion of Proposition
1.3
(together with extra technical conditions that we will not mention in this introduction, see for example Corollary
2.7
for more details), we have instead identifications
{\mathscr{F}}_{1}^{(i)}(X)\simeq\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,U)
and
{\rm gr}^{2}{\mathscr{F}}^{(i)}(X)\simeq\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,\overline{{\mathscr{O}}}^{\times})
. Now we explain how to give a subfiltration of the term
{\mathscr{F}}_{1}^{(i)}(X)
by considering the pro-étale site. More precisely we have a natural map of topoi
\nu:X_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}\to X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}
which gives rise to compatible Leray spectral sequences

\displaystyle E_{2}^{i,j}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})=\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathrm{R}}^{j}\nu_{*}{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\Rightarrow\hhh^{i+j}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

\displaystyle E_{2}^{i,j}(U)=\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathrm{R}}^{j}\nu_{*}U)\Rightarrow\hhh^{i+j}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,U).

Thanks to
\log
and
\exp
methods and Hodge-Tate isomorphism most of the terms of those spectral sequences vanish by vanishing of the coherent cohomology of

, we can construct (compatible) morphisms:

\displaystyle{\rm HTlog}_{i}:\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to\Omega^{i}(X)(-i)

\displaystyle{\rm HTlog}_{U,i}:\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,U)\to\Omega^{i}(X)(-i)

such that the degeneracy of those spectral sequences yields (compatible) long exact sequences

\displaystyle\dots\xrightarrow{{\rm HTlog}_{i-1}}\Omega^{i-1}(X)(-i+1)\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathscr{O}}^{\times})\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathscr{O}}^{\times})\xrightarrow{{\rm HTlog}_{i}}\Omega^{i}(X)(-i)\to\cdots,

\displaystyle\cdots\xrightarrow{{\rm HTlog}_{U,i-1}}\Omega^{i-1}(X)(-i+1)\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X,U)\to\hhh^{i}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,U)\xrightarrow{{\rm HTlog}_{U,i}}\Omega^{i}(X)(-i)\to\cdots

Proposition 5.8

For

is torsion for all

.

The reasoning to prove the above proposition is similar to the one from
[
18
, Section 5.2]
. Let us first prove the following general result:

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
X
be a rigid analytic space and let
{\mathscr{U}}=\{U_{i},i\in I\}
be a family of open sets in
X
. Assume that
\hhh^{\bullet}(-)
is a cohomology theory that satisfies Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence. Then,

(1)

If for all
k\geq 1
and all finite subset
J\subset I
the groups
\hhh^{k}(\bigcap_{j\in J}U_{j})
are torsion, then for all
k\geq|J|
, the cohomology groups of finite unions
\hhh^{k}(\bigcup_{j\in J}U_{j})
are torsion.

(2)

If for all
k\geq|J|
and all finite subset
J\subset I
the groups
\hhh^{k}(\bigcup_{j\in J}U_{j})
are torsion, then the cohomology groups of all the finite intersections
\hhh^{k}(\bigcap_{j\in J}U_{j})
are torsion.

Proof.

We can assume
I=\{1,\dots,n\}
. Both points will be proved by induction on
n
. Let us first prove (1). If
n=1
, this is trivial. Take
n\geq 2
and let us assume that the result of (1) is true for
I=\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. It suffices to prove that the cohomology of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
is torsion in all degree
\geq 1
. By hypothesis, we have an exact sequence:

\hhh^{k-1}(U^{(n-1)}\cap U_{n})\to\hhh^{k}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i})\to\hhh^{k}(U^{(n-1)})\oplus\hhh^{k}(U_{n}),

where
U^{(n-1)}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}U_{i}
. Since we have
U^{(n-1)}\cap U_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}V_{i}
with
V_{i}:=U_{i}\cap U_{n}
, its cohomology groups are torsion (by induction hypothesis). The same is true for
\hhh^{k}(U^{(n-1)})
and
\hhh^{k}(U_{n})
. This proves that
\hhh^{k}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i})
is torsion, as wanted.

To prove the second point, we can assume
I=\{1,\dots,n\}
and we are reduced to prove that the cohomology of
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
is torsion in all degree
\geq 1
. Setting
V_{i}:=U_{i}\cap U_{n}
, we see that it is enough to show that the cohomology of
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}V_{i}
is torsion. By induction, it suffices to show that
\hhh^{k}(\bigcup_{j\in J}V_{j})
is torsion for any finite subset
J\subset\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
k\geq|J|
. Let us write
U^{J}:=\bigcup_{j\in J}U_{j}
and
V^{J}:=\bigcup_{j\in J}V_{j}=U^{J}\cap U_{n}
. We have an exact sequence:

\hhh^{k}(U^{J})\oplus\hhh^{k}(U_{n})\to\hhh^{k}(V^{J})\to\hhh^{k+1}(U^{J}\cup U_{n}).

But by hypothesis, we have that the groups
\hhh^{k}(U^{J})
,
\hhh^{k}(U_{n})
and
\hhh^{k+1}(U^{J}\cup U_{n})
are all torsion. We deduce that
\hhh^{k}(V^{J})
is torsion.
∎

Proposition

follows then from the second point of Lemma
5.9
combined with the following result:

.

Let

be a subset of

This will also follow from Lemma

a subset of
{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
and write

(see also

and an admissible covering
