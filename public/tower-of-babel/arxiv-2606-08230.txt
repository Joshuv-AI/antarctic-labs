T-matrix analysis of pion-proton femtoscopy
Liang Zhang ID, Tianhao Shao, Song Zhang ID, Kai-Jia Sun ID, Yu-Gang Ma ID

Abstract
The observed shift of the Δ ⁡ ( 1232 ) \Delta(1232) resonance peak in π \pi - p p femtoscopic correlations challenges the conventional Breit-Wigner description of resonances in femtoscopy. We revisit the Koonin-Pratt framework by formulating femtoscopy in the momentum-space representation and employing the T-matrix approach to disentangle on-shell and off-shell contributions. By employing a Friedrichs-Lee model constrained by low-energy scattering data, we demonstrate that the finite spatial extent of the emission source induces sensitivity to off-shell dynamics, which leads to a peak shift accompanied by a dip on the high-momentum side of the peak. The resulting correlation strength, however, does not fully reproduce the measured amplitude, and a high-momentum side dip is not observed in experiments. The remaining discrepancies may be attributed to the structural complexity of the emission source beyond a simple spherical Gaussian approximation.

II
Femtoscopic correlations in momentum space

The KP formula
19
;
27
can be expressed in Dirac notation,

\displaystyle C({\boldsymbol{p}})=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}\langle\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}|{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle{\boldsymbol{k}}|\hat{S}|{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\rangle\langle{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle+

(1)

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}|R\rangle\langle R|\hat{S}|R\rangle\langle R|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle.

where
\hat{S}
represents the normalized emission source,

|R\rangle
denotes the bare resonance state, which couples to the continuum
|{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
,
and
|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle
is the scattering state satisfying the outgoing boundary condition.
The emission source consists of two components.
The dominant contribution considered in the present work arises from directly produced particle pairs, described by the continuum source kernel
\langle{\boldsymbol{k}}|\hat{S}|{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\rangle
.
For completeness, we also include a possible explicit resonance source contribution
\langle R|\hat{S}|R\rangle
,
whose detailed treatment is deferred to the Appendix. Although the emission source for target pair
\langle{\boldsymbol{r}}|\hat{S}|{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}\rangle
is conventionally approximated as diagonal in coordinate space, its finite spatial extent necessitates an intrinsic non-diagonal structure in the momentum-space representation. This non-diagonal structure reflects the ability of the source kernel to couple different momentum states. As will be shown below, such off-shell contributions play an essential role in understanding the experimentally observed peak displacement.

To study the internal dynamics of resonance states, the scattering wave function
\langle{\boldsymbol{k}}|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle
needs to be directly constructed from the T-matrix via the Lippmann-Schwinger equation ignoring the Coulomb interaction.

\langle{\boldsymbol{k}}|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle=\delta^{(3)}({\boldsymbol{k}}-{\boldsymbol{p}})+G^{(+)}({\boldsymbol{k}};E)T({\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{p}};E),

(2)

where
G^{(+)}({\boldsymbol{k}};E)=2\mu/(p^{2}-k^{2}+i\epsilon)
is the continuum propagator, with
E
and
p=|{\boldsymbol{p}}|
being the eigenenergy and momentum of
|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}^{(+)}\rangle
.
In the energy range considered here, only a single resonance contributes, and the T-matrix can be generally expressed as

T({\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{p}};E)=\frac{g({\boldsymbol{k}})g^{*}({\boldsymbol{p}})}{E-E_{0}-\Sigma_{0}(E)+i\epsilon}.

(3)

The pole in the complex-energy plane and the resonance peak position on the real axis are determined by the real bare mass
E_{0}
and the complex self-energy
\Sigma_{0}(E)
. Unitarity requires that the self-energy satisfy
\operatorname{Im}\Sigma_{0}(E)=-\pi\rho(E)|g(E)|^{2}
, where
\rho(E)
is the density of states at energy
E
.

To reveal the internal dynamics of the resonance, we substitute the scattering wave function (Eq. (
2
)) into the KP formula. The correlation function
C(p)
naturally decomposes into three components,

\displaystyle C(p)=

\displaystyle 1+2\operatorname{Re}\langle{\boldsymbol{p}}|\hat{S}GT|{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle+\langle{\boldsymbol{p}}|T^{\dagger}G^{\dagger}\hat{S}GT|{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+C_{\text{int}}(p)+C_{\text{scat}}(p),

in which the
C_{\text{int}}
term represents the interference between the incident wave and the scattered wave, and
C_{\text{scat}}
accounts for the pure scattering contribution to the femtoscopic correlation.
The central object in both terms is the continuum propagator
G^{(+)}({\boldsymbol{k}};E_{p})
.
By applying the Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem, we perform a “spectral surgery” on the propagator into a principal value part and a pole contribution,

\frac{2\mu}{p^{2}-k^{2}\pm i\epsilon}=\text{P.V.}\frac{2\mu}{p^{2}-k^{2}}\mp i2\mu\pi\delta(p^{2}-k^{2}).

(5)

After isolating the on-shell contribution associated with the
\delta
function in the propagator, the remaining principal-value integral probes momentum configurations away from the pole at
k=p
. In this sense, it encodes the off-shell dynamics weighted by the source function.
This decomposition serves as a diagnostic tool to disentangle different contributions to the correlation function. The full correlation function does not depend on this decomposition.

The on-shell parts of
C_{\text{int}}(p)
and
C_{\text{scat}}(p)
both exhibit Breit-Wigner (BW) profiles. Crucially, the two contributions appear with opposite signs,

\displaystyle C^{\text{(on-shell)}}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle(p)

(6)

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(E)^{2}/2}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}(S\mathcal{P}_{J_{R}})({\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{p}}),

\displaystyle C^{\text{(on-shell)}}_{\text{scat}}

\displaystyle(p)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}_{J_{R}}S\mathcal{P}_{J_{R}})({\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{p}}).

Here
\mathcal{P}_{J_{R}}
denotes the projector onto the total spin-
J_{R}
resonance state,
with the resonance mass and width defined as

M_{R}(E)=E_{0}+\mathrm{Re}[\Sigma_{0}(E)]
and

\Gamma(E)=-2\,\mathrm{Im}[\Sigma_{0}(E)]
, respectively.
Accordingly,
C_{\text{int}}(p)
produces a dip structure,
while the resonant peak originates from the two-body scattering contribution
C_{\text{scat}}(p)
in Eq. (
7
).
In the present framework, however, the total on-shell contribution leads to a dip structure, while the resulting peak in the correlation function emerges predominantly from off-shell dynamics encoded in the principal-value integral of the propagator.

In contrast, the off-shell contribution of
C_{\text{int}}
, from the principal value integration, introduces a dispersive structure,

\displaystyle C^{\text{(off-shell)}}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle(p)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{R}(E)-E}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{F}_{\text{int}}(E,S)+

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(E)/2}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{F}_{\text{int}}(E,S),

where
\mathcal{F}_{\text{int}}(E,S)
is a smooth response factor that integrates the source geometry and the T-matrix vertices for the interference term. There is a dispersive term which is positive for
E<M_{R}
and negative for
E>M_{R}
(Fig.
1
). The second term is neglected in the numerical calculations. See Appendix
B
for a discussion of the response factor.

The off-shell parts of
C_{\text{scat}}
also provide a contribution from a skewed BW peak which is strongly influenced by a source response factor
\mathcal{F}_{\text{scat}}(E,S)
,

\displaystyle C^{\text{(off-shell)}}_{\text{scat}}

\displaystyle(p)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(E)/[2\pi\rho(E)]}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma(E)^{2}/4}\times\mathcal{F}_{\text{scat}}(E,S).

Here
\rho(E)
denotes the density of states at the scattering energy
E
.
Due to the strong cancellation between the on-shell part of
C_{\text{scat}}(p)
and
C_{\text{int}}(p)
, the off-shell contribution of
C_{\text{scat}}(p)
becomes dominant (Fig.
1
). This results in the characteristic peak shift toward lower energies, which is a reflection of off-shell dynamics.

III
The resonance model

The characteristics of unstable states are elegantly delineated by different models sharing a similar conceptual framework
33
. The most paradigmatic one is the Friedrichs-Lee (FL) model
14
;
24
, which provides a non-perturbative approach to describe the coupling between a discrete state and a continuum. In our calculation, we adopt a non-relativistic approximation. For the
\pi-N
system, relativistic effects become significant for
p>100
MeV/c, but this does not alter the qualitative discussion on the shape of the correlation function. In this framework, the
\Delta
resonance is no longer a physical particle, but emerges as a dressed state from the coupling between a bare discrete state
|\Delta,M\rangle
and the
\pi
-nucleon scattering continuum
|k,s\rangle
, where
M
and
s
denote the spin projection components of the
\Delta
resonance and the nucleon, respectively.

Neglecting the subdominant background
\pi^{+}
-
p
interaction
9
;
17
and the Coulomb interaction, the system is governed by the non-relativistic approximate Hamiltonian
H=H_{0}+V
, where

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}E_{k}|{\boldsymbol{k}},s\rangle\langle{\boldsymbol{k}},s|+\sum_{M}E_{\Delta 0}|\Delta,M\rangle\langle\Delta,M|,

(10)

\displaystyle V=\sum_{M,s}\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(g_{M,s}({\boldsymbol{k}})|\Delta,M\rangle\langle{\boldsymbol{k}},s|+\text{h.c.}\right).

Here,
E_{k}=m_{\pi}+m_{p}+k^{2}/2\mu
is the non-relativistic kinetic energy and
E_{\Delta 0}
represents the bare mass of the
\Delta
. The coupling
g_{M,s}({\boldsymbol{k}})
is parameterized using a dipole form factor
f({\boldsymbol{k}})
to regularize the UV behavior,

g_{M,s}({\boldsymbol{k}})=\frac{g_{0}}{m_{\pi}}kf(k)\mathcal{P}^{33}_{M,s}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}),\quad f(k)=\left(1+k^{2}/\Lambda^{2}\right)^{-2}.

(11)

The term
\mathcal{P}^{33}_{M,s}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}})
ensures the interaction is projected onto the
P_{33}
partial wave. The cutoff
\Lambda
and the coupling strength
g_{0}
serve as the model’s fundamental parameters to be constrained by vacuum scattering data.

The full T-matrix can be solved analytically in closed form. The resulting transition amplitude for the
P_{33}
channel is:

T_{3/2}(k^{\prime},k;E)=\frac{\left(\frac{g_{0}}{m_{\pi}}\right)^{2}k^{\prime}kf(k)f(k^{\prime})}{E-E_{\Delta 0}-\Sigma_{0}(E)+i\epsilon}

(12)

The complex self-energy
\Sigma_{0}(E)=\int q^{2}dq\frac{|g_{0}/m_{\pi}qf(q)|^{2}}{E-E_{q}+i\epsilon}
is the key to the model. It shifts the bare mass
E_{\Delta 0}
to the physical pole in the complex energy plane. In this formulation, the resonance is introduced as a pole in the transition matrix. The behavior of the T-matrix on the complex plane is entirely determined by three parameters: the resonance bare mass
E_{\Delta 0}
, the coupling strength
g_{0}
, and the cutoff
\Lambda
.

The parameters, governing the complex-energy pole and the real-axis resonance behavior, are extracted by fitting the cross section of
\pi^{+}-p\rightarrow\Delta^{++}

9
;
17
.
According to the optical theorem together with the partial wave expansion
17
, the cross section can be expressed solely in terms of the
P_{33}
phase shift,

\sigma(\pi^{+}-p\rightarrow\Delta^{++})=\frac{8\pi}{k^{2}}\sin^{2}(\delta_{P_{33}}).

(13)

Here,
k
corresponds to the center-of-mass momentum and
\delta_{P_{33}}
is the phase shift of the
P_{33}
channel, which can be directly obtained from the T-matrix.
We fit to the background-subtracted cross section rather than the total cross section. This choice is natural in the current framework: the model contains only the resonant
P_{33}
channel and does not include non-resonant background contributions.
The fitting procedure (Fig.
2
) yields the optimal parameters
E_{\Delta 0}=1327.69~\mathrm{MeV}
,
g_{0}=0.026~\mathrm{MeV}^{-1/2}
, and
\Lambda=458.80~\mathrm{MeV}
. These values successfully place the resonance pole at
\sqrt{s_{\text{pole}}}=1211.65-i\ 98.88/2~\mathrm{MeV}
as well as the resonance peak position at
E_{\text{peak}}=1231.19~\mathrm{MeV}
, which is in agreement with the vacuum
\Delta(1232)
properties recorded by the Particle Data Group
26
. The consistency ensures that our T-matrix faithfully captures the analytic structure of the
\Delta
resonance. Consequently, this framework provides physically consistent analysis for the off-shell dynamics in femtoscopic correlations.

To validate the quantitative performance of our framework, we solve the corresponding Schrödinger equation in coordinate space, accounting for both Coulomb and background interactions
7
. Using Gaussian sources with
R\simeq 0.8\,\text{to}\,2.5
fm, which are consistent with the radii extracted from experimental fits in different
m_{T}
regions in Ref.
7
, we compare our calculations with experimental data in Fig.
3
. While the theoretical calculations capture the widths and positions of the experimental peaks in different
m_{T}
regions, they underestimate the peak amplitudes and predict high-momentum side dips absent in the experimental data. In the comparison, we add a strength parameter
\lambda
, which does not affect the shape of the correlation function, to make the amplitudes comparable to the data.

C_{\text{tuned}}(p)=1+\lambda(C(p)-1).

(14)

These residual discrepancies may stem from medium effects, collective motion, or more complex background dynamics not captured in the vacuum scattering sector.
Nevertheless, establishing such a baseline from the resonance dynamics is a prerequisite for understanding the final-stage evolution of high-energy collisions via femtoscopy.

IV
Summary

The ALICE Collaboration has observed a shift of the
\Delta(1232)
resonance peak in both
\pi^{+}-p
and
\pi^{+}-d
femtoscopic correlations
7
;
5
. We revisited resonance contributions from scattering dynamics and found that the off-shell dynamic contributes to the phenomenon.
We generalized the T-matrix formulation to the Koonin-Pratt framework. A spectral decomposition analysis was applied to the correlation function for the first time, allowing the on-shell and off-shell components of the interference and scattering contributions to be isolated. The finite source induces non-diagonal contributions in momentum space and naturally generates sensitivity to off-shell scattering dynamics in resonance femtoscopy.

We found that on-shell contributions provide a dip structure,
while off-shell components become important due to finite source size and generate a dispersive structure. We numerically computed the correlation functions using the FL model constrained by
\pi^{+}-p
scattering.
A peak on the left side of the resonance mass is produced, accompanied by a dip on the right side.
Compared to the experimental data, our calculation does not fully reproduce the peak amplitude, and the predicted high-momentum side dip is absent in the experimental data. These discrepancies may stem from non-trivial characteristics in high-energy collisions, and call for further investigation of the underlying source structure.

Appendix A
Direct resonance source contribution

In the KP formula (Eq. (
1
)), the contribution of resonances directly produced by the source is explicitly given by

C_{\Delta}({\boldsymbol{p}})=\langle\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}|R\rangle\langle R|\hat{S}|R\rangle\langle R|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle,

(15)

where the scattering state
|\psi^{(+)}_{\boldsymbol{p}}\rangle
is obtained as the solution to the Lippmann-Schwinger (LS) equation.
The coupling between the resonance and the continuum state can be generally expressed as

T_{R,{\boldsymbol{p}}}(E)=g({\boldsymbol{p}})\frac{E-E_{0}}{E-E_{0}-\Sigma_{0}(E)+i\epsilon}.

(16)

Here,
E
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}
are the on‑shell energy and the corresponding relative momentum of the
\pi
‑
p
pair.
Consequently, the direct resonance term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle C_{\Delta}({\boldsymbol{p}})\propto\frac{\Gamma(E)/(2\mu\pi k)}{[E-M_{R}(E)]^{2}+\Gamma^{2}(E)/4}\langle R|\hat{S}|R\rangle.

(17)

This expression exhibits an asymmetric Breit-Wigner-like structure that is skewed toward the low-momentum region.

Appendix B
Expression of the response factors

The response factor in Eq. (
8
) is expressed as

\mathcal{F}_{\text{int}}(E,S)=-\text{P.V.}\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}\,S({\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{k}})\mathcal{P}_{J_{R}}\frac{g({\boldsymbol{k}})g^{*}({\boldsymbol{p}})}{E-k^{2}/2\mu}.

(18)

The factor in Eq. (
9

\mathcal{F}_{\text{scat}}(E,S)=\text{P.V.}\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}_{J_{R}}S({\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime})\mathcal{P}_{J_{R}}\frac{g^{*}({\boldsymbol{k}})g({\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime})}{E-k^{2}/2\mu}.

(19)

In the numerical calculations based on Gaussian source and FL model, these two factors are positive real-valued functions.
