On the Independence Number of the Prime-Coprime Graph of a Finite Group
Ravi Ranjan and Shubh Narayan Singh Surbhi Kumari and Shidra Jamil

Abstract
. The prime-coprime graph Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) of a finite group G G is the simple graph with vertex set G G , where two distinct elements are adjacent whenever the greatest common divisor of their orders is either 1 1 or a prime. We characterize all finite groups G G for which Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) is a split graph. We establish a general lower bound for the independence number of Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) of an arbitrary finite group G G . Moreover, we explicitly compute the independence number of Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) for several distinguished families of finite groups, including cyclic, dihedral, dicyclic, and semidihedral groups.

Introduction

Graphs arising from finite groups have been extensively studied over the past two decades; see the survey in
[
3
]
. These graphs highlight the deep interplay between group theory and graph theory,
allowing group-theoretic properties to be examined through graph invariants. Among recent constructions, the concept of prime–coprime graphs, introduced in 2021, has attracted considerable attention; see
[
1
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

Let
G
be a finite group. The
prime-coprime graph
of
G
, denoted by
\Theta(G)
, is the simple graph with vertex set
G
, where two distinct vertices are adjacent whenever the greatest common divisor of their orders is either
1
or a prime
[
1
]
. This graph is also referred as the
coprime order graph
in the literature; see
[
8
,
11
]
. Adhikari and Banerjee
[
1
]
characterized the completeness and Eulerian properties of
\Theta(G)
, and determined its domination number, diameter, and girth. For specific groups
G
, they further studied Hamiltonicity, planarity, vertex connectivity, and the signless Laplacian spectrum of
\Theta(G)
. Hao et al.
[
8
]
classified finite groups
G
for which
\Theta(G)
is planar, and determined its vertex connectivity for finite cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups. Li et al. proved that for any fixed integer
k\geq 1
, there are only finitely many finite groups
G
such that
\Theta(G)
has (non)orientable genus
k

[
11
]
. In
[
14
]
, the first two authors studied the Hamiltonicity and clique number of
\Theta(G)
for finite cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups.

The determination of the independence number of a graph is generally NP-hard. This graph invariant has been investigated for certain graphs associated with finite groups; see
[
4
,
7
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
. In this paper, we focus on the independence number of prime–coprime graphs of finite groups. The subsequent sections of this paper are organized as follows. Section 2 provides preliminary definitions and notation. In Section 3, we characterize all finite groups
G
for which
\Theta(G)
is a split graph. Furthermore, we establish a general lower bound for the independence number of
\Theta(G)
of an arbitrary finite group
G
. Moreover, we explicitly compute the independence number of
\Theta(G)
for the following four distinguished families of finite groups:

\displaystyle\text{Cyclic groups }\,\,\Z_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle a\,\colon a^{n}=1\rangle,\quad n\geq 1;

\displaystyle\text{Dihedral groups }\,\,D_{2n}

\displaystyle=\langle a,b\,\colon a^{n}=1,\,b^{2}=1,\,bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle,\quad n\geq 3;

\displaystyle\text{Dicyclic groups }\,\,Q_{4n}

\displaystyle=\langle a,b\,\colon a^{2n}=1,\,b^{4}=1,\,bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle,\quad n\geq 3;

\displaystyle\text{Semidihedral groups }\,\,SD_{8n}

\displaystyle=\langle a,b\,\colon a^{4n}=1,\,b^{2}=1,\,bab^{-1}=a^{2n-1}\rangle,\quad n\geq 3.

Preliminaries and Notation

The cardinality of a set

We denote by

. A

3.
Main Results

In this section, we characterize all finite groups
G
for which
\Theta(G)
is a split graph. Furthermore, we establish a general lower bound for the independence number
\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)
of an arbitrary finite group
G
. Moreover, we explicitly compute the independence number
\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)
for the four distinguished families of finite groups, namely cyclic, dihedral, dicyclic, and semidihedral groups.

Our first main result is the following theorem, which characterizes all finite groups
G
for which
\Theta(G)
is a split graph.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group. Then
\Theta(G)
is a split graph if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

G\setminus P(G)=\varnothing
.

(ii)

G\setminus P(G)
consists exclusively of elements of order
p^{k}
, where
p
is a fixed prime and
k\geq 2
.

(iii)

G\setminus P(G)
consists exclusively of elements of order
pq
, where
p
and
q
are fixed distinct primes.

Proof
.

Assume that
\Theta(G)
is a split graph. If
\Theta(G)
is a complete graph, then
P(G)=G
by Theorem
2.1
, and hence the condition (i) holds. Suppose that
\Theta(G)
is not complete. By Theorem
2.1
, it follows that
G\setminus P(G)\neq\varnothing
. Choose
g\in G\setminus P(G)
. Note that
|g|
is composite. We claim
\omega(|g|)\leq 2
. Suppose to the contrary that
\omega(|g|)\geq 3
. Then there exist distinct primes
p_{i},p_{j},p_{\ell}
dividing
|g|
. Therefore
G
contains at least
\varphi(p_{i}p_{j})
elements of order
p_{i}p_{j}
, and at least
\varphi(p_{j}p_{\ell})
elements of order
p_{j}p_{\ell}
. Observe that
\varphi(p_{i}p_{j}),\varphi(p_{j}p_{\ell})\geq 2
, and choose
u,v,x,y\in G
with
|u|=|v|=p_{i}p_{j}
and
|x|=|y|=p_{j}p_{\ell}
.
Then the subset
\{u,v,x,y\}
induces a subgraph of
\Theta(G)
isomorphic to
2K_{2}
. This contradicts the assumption that
\Theta(G)
is split. Hence
\omega(|g|)\leq 2
. Condition (ii) holds immediately if
\omega(|g|)=1
. Finally, suppose that
\omega(|g|)=2
. We claim that
|g|=pq
for distinct primes
p,q
. Suppose to the contrary that
|g|=p^{a}q^{b}
with
a+b\geq 3
. Without loss of generality, assume that
a\geq 2
. Then
G
contains at least
\phi(p^{a})
elements of order
p^{a}
, and at least
\phi(pq)
elements of order
pq
. Observe that
\phi(p^{a}),\phi(pq)\geq 2
, and choose
u,v,x,y\in G
with
|u|=|v|=p^{a}
and
|x|=|y|=pq
. Then the subset
\{u,v,x,y\}
induces a subgraph of
\Theta(G)
isomorphic to
2K_{2}
. This contradicts the assumption that
\Theta(G)
is split. Hence
|g|=pq
for distinct primes
p,q
, and thus the condition (iii) holds.

Conversely, assume that one of the three conditions holds. If
G\setminus P(G)=\varnothing
, then by Theorem
2.1
, the graph
\Theta(G)
is complete. Therefore
\Theta(G)
is split.

Suppose
G\setminus P(G)\neq\varnothing
. We consider the two distinct cases separately.

Case 1:
Suppose that every element of
G\setminus P(G)
has order
p^{k}
for some fixed prime
p
and
k\geq 2
. Then any two distinct
g,h\in G\setminus P(G)
are non-adjacent in
\Theta(G)
, since
(|g|,|h|)
is composite. Therefore
G\setminus P(G)
is an independent set of
\Theta(G)
.

Case 2:
Suppose that every element of
G\setminus P(G)
has order
pq
for fixed distinct primes
p,q
. Then any two distinct
g,h\in G\setminus P(G)
are non-adjacent in
\Theta(G)
, since
(|g|,|h|)
is composite.
Therefore
G\setminus P(G)
is an independent set of
\Theta(G)
.

In either case,
G\setminus P(G)
is an independent set of
\Theta(G)
. By Remark
2.2
,
P(G)
is a clique of
\Theta(G)
. Hence
\Theta(G)
is split. ∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group. If
\Theta(G)
is a split graph, then
\Theta(G)
is a complete split graph.

Proof
.

If
G\setminus P(G)=\varnothing
,
then by Theorem
2.1
,
\Theta(G)
is complete. Therefore
\Theta(G)
is complete split.

Suppose
G\setminus P(G)\neq\varnothing
. Since
\Theta(G)
is split, Theorem
3.1
provides the split partition
\bigl(P(G),\,G\setminus P(G)\bigr)
of
\Theta(G)
. By Remark
2.2
,
P(G)
is a clique and every element of
P(G)

is dominating in
\Theta(G)
. Hence
\Theta(G)
is complete split.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group. If
\Theta(G)
is a split graph, then

\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $G\setminus P(G)=\varnothing$},\\
|G|-|P(G)|&\text{if $G\setminus P(G)\neq\varnothing$}.\end{cases}

Proof
.

If
G\setminus P(G)=\varnothing
, then by Theorem
2.1
, the graph
\Theta(G)
is complete. Therefore
\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)=1
.

Suppose
G\setminus P(G)\neq\varnothing
. Since
\Theta(G)
is split, Theorem
3.1
provides the split partition
\bigl(P(G),\,G\setminus P(G)\bigr)
of
\Theta(G)
. By Corollary
3.2
,
\Theta(G)
is complete split, whence
G\setminus P(G)
forms the maximum independent set. Therefore
\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)=|G\setminus P(G)|=|G|-|P(G)|
.
∎

In the remainder of the paper, we assume that
n
is a composite integer and that
G
denotes a group of order
n
. For each
n
, we define

SP(n)=\bigl\{d\in\N\ \colon d\text{ is a semiprime divisor of }n\bigr\}.

Furthermore, for each
d\in SP(n)
, we define

I_{d}(G)=\bigl\{g\in G\ \colon d\text{ divides }|g|\bigr\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
d\in SP(n)
such that
I_{d}(G)\neq\varnothing
. Then
I_{d}(G)
forms a maximal independent set of
\Theta(G)
.

Proof
.

To show first that
I_{d}(G)
is an independent set of
\Theta(G)
, let
g,h\in I_{d}(G)
with
g\neq h
. Then, by definition of
I_{d}(G)
, the semiprime
d
divides both
|g|
and
|h|
. Therefore
d
divides
(|g|,|h|)
, and so
(|g|,|h|)
is composite. Consequently, no edge of
\Theta(G)
joins
g
and
h
, and hence
I_{d}(G)
is an independent set of
\Theta(G)
.

Suppose to the contrary that
I_{d}(G)
is not maximal. Then there exists
x\in G\setminus I_{d}(G)
such that
I_{d}(G)\cup\{x\}
is independent in
\Theta(G)
. By definition of
I_{d}(G)
, we have
d\nmid|x|
. Therefore, for any
g\in I_{d}(G)
with
|g|=d
, we have
(|x|,|g|)\in\{1\}\cup\pi(G)
. Hence
x
is adjacent to such
g\in I_{d}(G)
in
\Theta(G)
.
This contradicts the assumption that
I_{d}(G)\cup\{x\}
is independent. Hence
I_{d}(G)
is maximal.
∎

Lemma
3.4
immediately provides the following proposition, which establishes a general lower bound for the independence number
\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)
for any group
G
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
. Then

\alpha\bigl(\Theta(G)\bigr)\geq\max\bigl\{|I_{d}(G)|\colon d\in SP(n)\bigr\}.

In order to render the lower bound in Proposition
3.5
explicit for cyclic groups case, it is first necessary to determine
|I_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|
for each
d\in SP(n)
. The following two results establish this computation.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
d\in SP(n)
. Then

|I_{d}(\Z_{n})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid\ell\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(\ell).

Proof
.

Recall that
I_{d}(\Z_{n})=\bigl\{g\in\Z_{n}\colon d\text{ divides }|g|\bigr\}
.
Let
g\in I_{d}(\Z_{n})
with
|g|=\ell
. Then
d\mid\ell
and
\ell\mid n
. By Theorem
2.3
, the group
\Z_{n}
contains exactly
\varphi(\ell)
elements of order
\ell
. Consequently, the elements of
I_{d}(\Z_{n})
are exactly those of order
\ell
with
d\mid\ell
and
\ell\mid n
. Hence, summing
\varphi(\ell)
over all such divisors
\ell
of
n
with
d\mid\ell
provides

|I_{d}(\Z_{n})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid\ell\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(\ell)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{k_{t}}
be the canonical prime factorization of
n
, and
d\in SP(n)
. Then

|I_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|=\begin{cases}(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-p_{i})\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}},&\text{if $d=p_{i}^{2}$},\\[5.0pt]
(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)(p_{j}^{k_{j}}-1)\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}},&\text{if $d=p_{i}p_{j}$ with $i\neq j$}.\end{cases}

Proof
.

By Lemma
3.6
, we have
|I_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid\ell\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(\ell)
. Let
\ell\in\N
such that
d\mid\ell
and
\ell\mid n
. Then
\ell
can be uniquely expressed as
\ell=d_{1}d_{2}
, where
d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}p_{j}^{k_{j}}
,
d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}
, and
(d_{1},d_{2})=1
(cf.
[
2
, p. 108, Lemma]
). Furthermore, we have
\varphi(\ell)=\varphi(d_{1}d_{2})=\varphi(d_{1})\varphi(d_{2})
, since
\varphi
is multiplicative. Since
d=p_{i}p_{j}
for some
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, we consider separately the two cases:

Case 1:

i=j
. Then
d=p_{i}^{2}
. Since
d\mid d_{1}d_{2}
, the exponent of
p_{i}
in
d_{1}
must be at least
2
. It follows that
d_{1}=p_{i}^{s}
, where
2\leq s\leq k_{i}
. Therefore
\sum_{d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}}\varphi(d_{1})=\sum_{s=2}^{k_{i}}\varphi(p_{i}^{s})=p_{i}^{k_{i}}-p_{i}
. Recall that
\varphi(\ell)=\varphi(d_{1})\varphi(d_{2})
. Therefore,

\sum_{\ell\mid n}\varphi(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}\\
d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}\end{subarray}}\varphi(d_{1})\varphi(d_{2})=\biggl(\sum_{d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}}\varphi(d_{1})\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}}\varphi(d_{2})\biggr).

By
[
2
, Theorem 7.6]
, we have
\sum_{d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}}\varphi(d_{2})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}
. It follows that

\sum_{\ell\mid n}\varphi(\ell)=(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-p_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

|I_{d}(\Z_{n})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid\ell\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(\ell)=(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-p_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}.

Case 2:

i\neq j
. Then
d=p_{i}p_{j}
, where
p_{i},p_{j}
are distinct. Since
d\mid d_{1}d_{2}
, the exponent of both
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
in
d_{1}
must be at least
1
. It follows that
d_{1}=p_{i}^{s}p_{j}^{m}
, where
1\leq s\leq k_{i}
and
1\leq m\leq k_{j}
. Therefore, since
\varphi(p_{i}^{s}p_{j}^{m})=\varphi(p_{i}^{s})\varphi(p_{j}^{m})
, we obtain

\sum_{d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}p_{j}^{k_{j}}}\varphi(d_{1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{i}^{s}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}\\
p_{j}^{m}\mid p_{j}^{k_{j}}\end{subarray}}\varphi(p_{i}^{s})\varphi(p_{j}^{m})=\biggl(\sum_{s=1}^{k_{i}}\varphi(p_{i}^{s})\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{m=1}^{k_{j}}\varphi(p_{j}^{m})\biggr)=(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)(p_{j}^{k_{j}}-1).

Recall that
\varphi(\ell)=\varphi(d_{1})\varphi(d_{2})
. Thus,

\sum_{\ell\mid n}\varphi(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}p_{j}^{k_{j}}\\
d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}\end{subarray}}\varphi(d_{1})\varphi(d_{2})=\left(\sum_{d_{1}\mid p_{i}^{k_{i}}p_{j}^{k_{j}}}\varphi(d_{1})\right)\Biggl(\sum_{d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}}\varphi(d_{2})\Biggr).

By
[
2
, Theorem 7.6]
, we have

\sum_{d_{2}\mid\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}}\varphi(d_{2})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}.

It follows that

\sum_{\ell\mid n}\varphi(\ell)=(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)(p_{j}^{k_{j}}-1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

|I_{d}(\Z_{n})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid\ell\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\varphi(\ell)=(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)(p_{j}^{k_{j}}-1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}.

∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{k_{t}}
be the canonical prime factorization of
n
. Then

\alpha\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\bigr)\;\geq\;\max\Biggl\{\,(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-p_{i})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}},\;(p_{i}^{k_{i}}-1)(p_{j}^{k_{j}}-1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}^{k_{r}}\Biggr\},

where the maximum is taken over all indices
i
with
k_{i}\geq 2
and all pairs
i\neq j
.

Moreover, if
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{t}=1
, then

\alpha\!\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\bigr)\geq(p_{t-1}-1)(p_{t}-1)\prod_{r=1}^{t-2}p_{r}.

Proof
.

The first inequality follows immediately from Propositions
3.5
and
3.7
. Next, assume
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{t}=1
. Then

\alpha\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\bigr)\;\geq\;\max_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\biggl\{\,(p_{i}-1)(p_{j}-1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\
r\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{t}p_{r}\biggr\}=n\cdot\max_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\left\{\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)\right\}.

Since
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{t}
, we have
1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}<1-\frac{1}{p_{2}}<\cdots<1-\frac{1}{p_{t}}
. Therefore,

\max_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\left\{\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)\right\}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{t-1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{t}}\right).

Hence,

\alpha\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\bigr)\geq n\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{t-1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{t}}\right)=(p_{t-1}-1)(p_{t}-1)\prod_{r=1}^{t-2}p_{r}.

∎
