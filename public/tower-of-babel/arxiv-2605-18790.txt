Matter one-loop logarithms and homogeneous TTNC scale response of Lifshitz black branes
Yingnan Xu, Shuangshuang Chu

Abstract
We compute the logarithmic one-loop matter contribution to the thermodynamics and homogeneous TTNC scale response of a four-dimensional Lifshitz black brane. The background is the neutral planar member of the analytic Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton Lifshitz black-brane family, while the quantum fields are treated as probes: a real scalar with arbitrary mass and nonminimal curvature coupling, a four-component Dirac spinor, and an Abelian Maxwell field with its Faddeev–Popov ghosts. For each spin sector the logarithmic coefficient separates into a smooth radial heat-kernel contribution, governed by 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} , and a horizon-localized conical contribution, governed by 𝒲 h \mathcal{W}_{h} . The smooth coefficient controls the source-induced homogeneous projection of the TTNC Weyl Ward identity, whereas the thermodynamic logarithmic entropy is controlled by 𝒞 therm = 𝒞 1 + z ​ L 2 ​ 𝒲 h \mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}=\mathcal{C}_{1}+zL^{2}\mathcal{W}_{h} . We give closed expressions for 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} , 𝒲 h \mathcal{W}_{h} , and 𝒞 therm \mathcal{C}_{\rm therm} for the scalar, Dirac, and Maxwell probe sectors, identify the gauge-field contact contribution in the conical entropy, and verify that the smooth source response vanishes in the relativistic planar z = 1 z=1 limit while the standard horizon heat-kernel entropy coefficient remains.

The main point of the calculation is that the logarithmic sector naturally

first is a smooth radial logarithm in the integrated bulk heat-kernel

\mathcal{C}_{\rm sm}\equiv\mathcal{C}_{1}

\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}\equiv zL^{2}\mathcal{W}_{h}

\mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}=\mathcal{C}_{\rm sm}+\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}=\mathcal{C}_{1}+zL^{2}\mathcal{W}_{h}.

projection is governed by
\mathcal{C}_{1}

governed by the combined coefficient
\mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}

therefore provides a sharp diagnostic of how ultraviolet source
renormalization and horizon replica physics enter Lifshitz black-brane
thermodynamics.

The resulting coefficients give a compact spin-dependent characterization of
matter one-loop effects on the Lifshitz saddle. They also provide useful
checks on the relativistic planar limit: for

coefficient vanishes in the flat-boundary source sector, while the conical
horizon coefficient remains and reproduces the standard local heat-kernel
entropy contribution. In this sense the calculation supplies both a
consistency test of the Lifshitz/TTNC renormalization structure and a
reference point for the more involved dynamical EMD one-loop problem.

2
The Lifshitz black-brane background

We work in four bulk dimensions and take the boundary theory to have two spatial directions. As a concrete exact saddle we use the neutral planar member of the Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton Lifshitz black-brane family of Tarrio and Vandoren
24
. The bulk theory has the schematic form

I_{\rm EMD}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{4}}\int\differential^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\mathcal{R}-2\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{I}e^{\lambda_{I}\varphi}F_{I}^{2}\right].

(2)

Only the metric is needed below, since all quantum fields considered in this paper are probes. In Lorentzian signature the neutral planar black brane is

\differential s^{2}=L^{2}\left[-r^{2z}f(r)\differential t^{2}+r^{2}(\differential x^{2}+\differential y^{2})+\frac{\differential r^{2}}{r^{2}f(r)}\right],\qquad f(r)=1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{z+2}.

(3)

The Euclidean metric is obtained by
t=-i\tau
,

\differential s_{E}^{2}=L^{2}\left[r^{2z}f(r)\differential\tau^{2}+r^{2}(\differential x^{2}+\differential y^{2})+\frac{\differential r^{2}}{r^{2}f(r)}\right].

(4)

Regularity of the Euclidean cigar at
r=r_{h}
fixes the inverse temperature,

T=\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{z+2}{4\pi}r_{h}^{z}.

(5)

The horizon area density and classical entropy density are

\frac{A_{h}}{V_{2}}=L^{2}r_{h}^{2},\qquad s_{0}=\frac{L^{2}r_{h}^{2}}{4G_{4}}.

(6)

Near the boundary, the induced Lifshitz geometry is naturally described in terms of torsional Newton–Cartan data
10
;
11
. For the homogeneous black brane these sources reduce to flat TTNC data,

\tau=\differential t,\qquad h_{ij}\differential x^{i}\differential x^{j}=\differential x^{2}+\differential y^{2},\qquad\tau\wedge\differential\tau=0.

(7)

The corresponding anisotropic Weyl transformation acts as

\tau_{\mu}\to e^{z\sigma}\tau_{\mu},\qquad h_{\mu\nu}\to e^{2\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}.

(8)

For a homogeneous equilibrium state in
2+1
boundary dimensions the thermal projection of the Lifshitz/TTNC Weyl Ward identity takes the form

z\varepsilon-2p=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TTNC}}.

(9)

Classically, for flat boundary sources,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TTNC}}=0
and therefore
z\varepsilon_{0}=2p_{0}
.

3
Probe matter determinants and heat-kernel coefficients

We now define the probe one-loop determinants. The fields considered here do not represent the coupled graviton–gauge–dilaton fluctuation system of the background EMD theory. Rather, they are probe matter sectors on the fixed geometry (
4
). This distinction is important for the Maxwell field: the vector determinant below is an Abelian probe determinant and includes its Faddeev–Popov ghosts, but it is not the determinant of the background-supporting EMD gauge fluctuations mixed with the metric and dilaton.

3.1
Real scalar

The scalar action is

I_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}\int\differential^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\left[g^{MN}\partial_{M}\chi\partial_{N}\chi+m_{\chi}^{2}\chi^{2}+\xi\mathcal{R}\chi^{2}\right].

(10)

After integrating by parts,

I_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}\int\differential^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\chi\Delta_{\chi}\chi,\qquad\Delta_{\chi}=-\nabla^{2}+m_{\chi}^{2}+\xi\mathcal{R}.

(11)

The one-loop effective action is

\Gamma_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}\log\det\frac{\Delta_{\chi}}{\mu^{2}}.

(12)

The relevant four-dimensional heat-kernel expansion is

\Tr e^{-s\Delta}\sim\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{2}}\int\differential^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\left[a_{0}+a_{2}s+a_{4}s^{2}+\cdots\right].

(13)

For the scalar operator (
11
), and dropping total derivatives, the coefficient is
27

a_{4}^{(\chi)}=\frac{1}{180}\left(\mathcal{R}_{MNRS}\mathcal{R}^{MNRS}-\mathcal{R}_{MN}\mathcal{R}^{MN}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{\xi}^{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}+\nu_{\xi}m_{\chi}^{2}\mathcal{R}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\chi}^{4},

(14)

where

\nu_{\xi}\equiv\xi-\frac{1}{6}.

(15)

3.2
Dirac spinor

For one four-component Euclidean Dirac spinor,

\Gamma_{\psi}=-\log\det(\slashed\nabla+m_\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\log\det\frac{\Delta_{1/2}}{\mu^{2}},\qquad\Delta_{1/2}=-\nabla^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{R}+m_{\psi}^{2}.

(16)

The minus sign is the Grassmann sign. Including this sign in the local density, the relevant coefficient is

\mathfrak{a}_{4}^{(\psi)}=\frac{7}{360}\mathcal{R}_{MNRS}\mathcal{R}^{MNRS}+\frac{1}{45}\mathcal{R}_{MN}\mathcal{R}^{MN}-\frac{1}{72}\mathcal{R}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}m_{\psi}^{2}\mathcal{R}-2m_{\psi}^{4}.

(17)

The notation
\mathfrak{a}_{4}
indicates that the statistics sign has already been included.

3.3
Maxwell vector

For one Abelian Maxwell probe in Feynman gauge,

\Gamma_{A}=\frac{1}{2}\log\det\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\mu^{2}}-\log\det\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\mu^{2}},

(18)

where

(\Delta_{1})_{M}{}^{N}=-\delta_{M}{}^{N}\nabla^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{M}{}^{N},\qquad\Delta_{0}=-\nabla^{2}.

(19)

The second determinant is the complex Faddeev–Popov ghost determinant. The signed gauge-plus-ghost heat-kernel density is

\mathfrak{a}_{4}^{(A)}=-\frac{13}{180}\mathcal{R}_{MNRS}\mathcal{R}^{MNRS}+\frac{88}{180}\mathcal{R}_{MN}\mathcal{R}^{MN}-\frac{25}{180}\mathcal{R}^{2}.

(20)

The conical entropy extracted from this gauge-fixed determinant contains the standard spin-one contact contribution at the horizon. This is the appropriate local coefficient for the gauge-fixed one-loop gravitational entropy of the probe Maxwell field

Logarithmic holographic renormalization and Weyl variation

We regulate the radial coordinate at
r=\Lambda
. For any probe sector, the smooth logarithmic part of the regulated one-loop functional follows from (

\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{reg}}^{\rm sm,(s)}\supset\frac{\beta V_{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(s)}r_{h}^{z+2}\log\frac{\Lambda}{r_{h}}.

The logarithmic counterterm that removes the cutoff dependence is

\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{ct}}^{\rm sm,(s)}=-\frac{\log(\Lambda/\mu)}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}}\differential^{3}x\,N\sqrt{h}\,\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{TTNC},\rm sm}^{(s)}.

For the homogeneous black brane,

\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}}\differential^{3}x\,N\sqrt{h}\,\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{TTNC},\rm sm}^{(s)}=\beta V_{2}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(s)}r_{h}^{z+2}.

Combining (

\boxed{f_{1,\log}^{\rm sm,(s)}=-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(s)}}{16\pi^{2}}r_{h}^{z+2}\log\frac{r_{h}}{\mu}}.

The horizon contribution is obtained from the replica variation of the local effective action. It is useful to represent it thermodynamically by a logarithmic free-energy term whose entropy derivative reproduces (

\boxed{f_{1,\log}^{\Sigma,(s)}=-\frac{zL^{2}\mathcal{W}_{h}^{(s)}}{16\pi^{2}}r_{h}^{z+2}\log\frac{r_{h}}{\mu}}.

Thus the total logarithmic thermal free-energy density is

\boxed{f_{1,\log}^{\rm therm,(s)}=-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}^{(s)}}{16\pi^{2}}r_{h}^{z+2}\log\frac{r_{h}}{\mu}}.

Let

Discussion

We have computed the logarithmic one-loop matter sector of a four-dimensional Lifshitz black brane for scalar, Dirac, and Maxwell probe fields. The result separates two local heat-kernel effects with distinct geometric origins. The smooth bulk determinant produces the radial logarithm governed by
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, which gives the homogeneous source-induced TTNC projection in (
58
). The replicated geometry produces the horizon-localized conical entropy governed by
\mathcal{W}_{h}
. The thermodynamic combination
\mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}=\mathcal{C}_{1}+zL^{2}\mathcal{W}_{h}
controls the total logarithmic thermal entropy in (
56
).

The spin-dependent extension makes the geometric split more transparent. The scalar coefficient depends on the nonminimal coupling through
\nu_{\xi}
, the Dirac coefficient contains the fermionic statistics sign, and the Maxwell coefficient contains the gauge-fixed vector contribution together with the complex ghost determinant. In the Maxwell case, the conical entropy includes the standard spin-one contact term at the horizon. These results are summarized in table
1
and combine linearly for a general probe matter content as in (
42
)–(
44
).

The relativistic planar limit provides a useful check. At
z=1
, all smooth radial coefficients vanish, so the flat-boundary source-induced projection vanishes for the probe sectors considered here. The remaining logarithmic coefficient is the local conical heat-kernel entropy coefficient for the planar
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}
black brane. For a massless conformally coupled scalar, a massless Dirac spinor, and a Maxwell vector, the corresponding
\mathcal{C}_{\rm therm}
values are
1/30
,
1/5
, and
2/5
, respectively.

The present calculation establishes a matter-probe benchmark for quantum
logarithmic effects in the Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton Lifshitz black-brane
background. By isolating the scalar, spinor, and vector contributions in a
fixed geometry, it provides a controlled set of local coefficients against
which the full EMD one-loop determinant can be compared. The complete
dynamical calculation, involving the coupled graviton–gauge–dilaton
fluctuation operator together with gauge fixing, ghost determinants, possible
zero modes, and operator mixing, is a natural next step. The coefficients
obtained here give useful input for that program. Further extensions include
non-flat TTNC sources, where the full invariant expansion in
(
72
) can be extracted, and finite non-local
determinants, for which radial determinant methods

19
;
14
provide a direct continuation beyond the
logarithmic sector.

Appendix A

The local logarithmic part of a one-loop effective action may be written in the form

\Gamma_{1,\log}^{\mathrm{loc}}=-\frac{\log(r_h/\mu)}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\mathfrak{a}_{4}.

(74)

Only the dependence of this local term on the conical opening angle is needed for the logarithmic entropy. Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}

around the horizon two-surface
\Sigma

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=n\int_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{R}+4\pi(1-n)\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{\gamma},

(75)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{R}^{2}

\displaystyle=n\int_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{R}^{2}+8\pi(1-n)\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{\gamma}\,\mathcal{R},

\displaystyle=n\int_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{R}_{MN}\mathcal{R}^{MN}+4\pi(1-n)\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{\gamma}\,\mathcal{R}_{\perp},
