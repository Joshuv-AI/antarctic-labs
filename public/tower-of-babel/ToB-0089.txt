# TOB-0089 — PICATRIX
## Maslamah al-Majrīṭī (Arabic) | c.943–944 CE | Medieval Islamic Occultism

---

## 📖 OVERVIEW

**Full Title:** Picatrix — The Latin Version of Ghâyat al-Ḥakîm ("The Aim of the Wise")
**Author:** Arabic original by Maslamah al-Majrīṭī (10th–11th c. CE); Latin translation of the Arabic version (13th c.)
**Date:** Arabic original c.943–944 CE; Latin translation c.1250s
**Pages:** ~600
**Language:** Latin (this edition) / Arabic (original: Ghâyat al-Ḥakîm)
**Category:** Grimoire / Astrological Magic / Medieval Islamic Occultism

---

## 📚 CONTENTS

### Book I — Foundations

- The nature of celestial influences
- The planets and their properties
- Fixed stars and their powers
- The four elements

### Book II — Talismanic Magic

- Making astrological talismans
- Lunar mansions (the 28 lunar stations)
- Images and figures for various purposes
- Consecrations and timing

### Book III — Spirit Operations

- Summoning and controlling spirits
- The nature of spirits and angels
- Demons and their uses
- Rituals for spirit communication

### Book IV — Practical Magic

- Love magic
- Healing and medicine
- Gaining wealth and power
- Magical operations for specific goals

---

## 📌 HISTORICAL NOTES

**Importance:** Picatrix is one of the most influential occult texts in history. It was the primary conduit through which Arabic astrological and magical knowledge entered European Renaissance thought.

**Influence:** Used by Marsilio Ficino, Pico della Mirandola, Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, and many other Renaissance magicians and philosophers. It shaped the entire Western ceremonial magic tradition.

**Lunar Mansions:** The 28 lunar mansions (manzils) described in Picatrix are a unique feature not found in most Western grimoires — they come directly from Arabic astronomical/astrological tradition.
