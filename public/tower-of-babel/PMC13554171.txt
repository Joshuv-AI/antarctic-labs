Interacting dust-acoustic multi-soliton and periodic waves in strongly coupled dusty plasma with ion-drag force
Authors: Shahrina Akter, Tokey Sifullah Tanjil, Md. Golam Hafez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigates head-on collisions of nonlinear dust acoustic waves (DAWs) in an unmagnetized, strongly coupled dusty plasma with ion-drag force. The (r,q) nonextensive distribution describes electrons and ions, and the extended Poincaré-Lighthill-Kuo (ePLK) perturbation method derives coupled Korteweg-de Vries equations (CKdVEs) governing counter-propagating solitary waves. Analytical solutions are obtained using Jacobi elliptic function Expansion Method, revealing periodic and solitary wave structures. Multi-soliton solutions are constructed via Hirota’s bilinear method, enabling comprehensive analysis of single, double, and triple soliton interactions. Phase shifts induced by soliton collisions are systematically evaluated, demonstrating the influence of key plasma parameters on wave dynamics. The effects of parameter variations on coupled Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) solutions are examined, providing valuable insights into nonlinear wave interactions in dusty plasmas.

Introduction

Dusty (complex) plasmas, composed of electrons, ions, and micron-sized charged dust grains, represent a distinct state of matter that occurs widely in both space environments such as planetary rings, cometary tails, and interstellar clouds and laboratory settings, including plasma processing, fusion devices, and industrial applications1–3. Recent laboratory experiments and space observations4 continue to reveal new collective behaviors in these systems, sustaining strong interest in their nonlinear dynamics. The presence of massive, highly charged dust grains introduces characteristic time and space scales that support low-frequency wave modes not found in conventional electron-ion plasmas.

Among these wave modes, dust-acoustic waves (DAWs) represent one of the most fundamental collective excitations in dusty plasmas. First theoretically predicted by Rao et al.5, DAWs occur when the inertia is provided by massive dust grains while electron and ion pressures provide the restoring force. Recent theoretical advances have extended DAWs studies to include non-thermal particle distributions, strong coupling effects, and external magnetic fields, revealing rich nonlinear behavior in both laboratory and astrophysical contexts6–10. In the nonlinear regime, these waves can evolve into coherent localized structures dust-acoustic solitons that maintain their integrity through a precise balance between nonlinear steepening and dispersive spreading11–13. The investigation of such nonlinear structures provides crucial insights into energy localization and transport mechanisms in complex plasma systems14.

The head-on collision of counter-propagating solitons presents a particularly rich nonlinear phenomenon that reveals fundamental aspects of wave interactions, energy transfer, and phase relationships in plasmas. Theoretical investigations of such collisions have employed various analytical frameworks, with the extended Poincaré-Lighthill-Kuo (ePLK) method proving particularly effective for deriving coupled evolution equations governing soliton interactions15,16. This method leads to coupled Korteweg–de Vries equations (CKdVEs) that describe the evolution of leftward and rightward propagating waves, capturing the essential physics of their mutual interactions. Recent advances have elucidated collision dynamics across diverse plasma configurations, including magnetized environments17, multi-component systems with nonthermal distributions18, and complex dusty plasma systems featuring strong coupling and ion-drag forces19. However, most existing collision studies remain confined to single-soliton interactions and Maxwellian or simplified non-thermal statistics, leaving a significant gap in understanding multi-soliton collision dynamics in realistic dusty plasmas characterized by strong coupling, ion drag, and comprehensive (r,q)-nonextensive statistics for both species.

In recent years, the extended Poincaré-Lighthill-Kuo (ePLK) perturbation technique has been widely employed to study head-on collisions of dust-acoustic solitons in various plasma environments. Alam et al.20 investigated the effects of two-temperature ions on head-on collisions and phase shifts of dust acoustic single- and multi-solitons. Singh and Saini21 examined the head-on collision of multi-solitons in a magnetized dusty plasma with nonextensive polarization force. More recently, Imon and Alam22 analyzed face-to-face collisions of single- and multi-solitons (up to quadruple solitons) along with instability in a multi-component dusty plasma relevant to Jupiter’s environment. However, most of these studies are limited to either single-soliton collisions or simplified plasma compositions. A comprehensive investigation incorporating strong coupling effects, ion-drag force, and generalized (r, q)-nonextensive distributions for both electrons and ions in multi-soliton collision dynamics remains unexplored, which constitutes objective of the present work.

The ion-drag force, arising from momentum transfer between streaming ions and charged dust grains, constitutes a fundamental component of dusty plasma physics with profound implications for both equilibrium configurations and nonlinear wave dynamics. This force not only governs dust transport and confinement mechanisms but also significantly modifies the dispersion relations and nonlinear evolution of collective modes, particularly dust-acoustic waves (DAWs). Pioneering studies established the theoretical foundation for ion-drag effects in complex plasmas23,24, while subsequent investigations quantified its influence on linear wave propagation and soliton formation25. Recent advances have extended this understanding to nonlinear wave interactions, most notably through the work of Kadi et al.19, who systematically examined head-on collisions of DAW solitons in strongly coupled dusty plasmas with ion drag, revealing substantial modifications to collision dynamics and phase shifts. Contemporary studies continue to explore ion-drag effects in diverse plasma configurations, yet a significant limitation persists: existing investigations remain confined to single-soliton collisions and predominantly assume Maxwellian equilibrium statistics for electrons and ions. This oversight is particularly consequential because real dusty plasmas both in laboratory experiments and space environments typically exhibit non-Maxwellian particle distributions and support multi-soliton interactions. Bridging this gap requires a unified framework that simultaneously incorporates ion drag, multi-soliton collisions, and realistic non-Maxwellian statistics, which is precisely the objective of the present study.

Realistic modeling of dust-acoustic soliton collisions requires moving beyond traditional simplifications that assume weakly coupled dust grains and thermal equilibrium particle distributions. In laboratory and space environments, dust components often exist in strongly coupled states (Γ > 1), exhibiting liquid-like or solid-like behavior that demands generalized hydrodynamic descriptions26–28. The generalized (r, q)-distribution is employed in this work as a phenomenological model for non-Maxwellian electrons and ions in dusty plasmas characterized by strong coupling and ion-drag force. In these systems, continuous processes - including ion streaming, dust-ion momentum transfer, dust charging, and external energy input - sustain deviations from local thermodynamic equilibrium, rendering exact Maxwellian distributions improbable. Currently, no self-consistent kinetic theory provides a derivation of the (r, q)-distribution that accounts for the simultaneous influence of strong coupling and ion-drag force. Consequently, the (r, q) framework is applied here as an effective parameterization capable of reproducing observed non-Maxwellian features, rather than as a rigorously derived equilibrium distribution. Accordingly, the present analysis is confined to the physically admissible ranges q > 1 and q(r + 1) > 5/2, with results interpreted as indicative theoretical trends and parametric dependencies rather than direct quantitative predictions for experimental conditions. Simultaneously, both electrons and ions frequently display non-Maxwellian velocity distributions characterized by enhanced suprathermal tails and flattened cores features consistently observed by spacecraft missions including Freja, Viking, Cluster, and NASA’s Van Allen Probes in regions such as the magnetosheath and planetary magnetospheres29,30. These distributions, arising from processes like turbulent acceleration and magnetic reconnection, are inadequately captured by single-Maxwellian or standard κ-models. The generalized (r, q)-nonextensive framework provides a comprehensive alternative that naturally accommodates such non-thermal characteristics while recovering both κ-distributions and Maxwellian limits as special cases31–34. Consequently, in the present investigation we adopt the (r, q) distribution for both electron and ion populations. This approach aligns with observational evidence that both species can be driven out of thermal equilibrium in dusty plasma settings and ensures that the pressure contributions governing dust-acoustic wave dynamics are modeled with greater physical fidelity.

The Jacobi elliptic function expansion (JEFE) method provides a unified framework for deriving exact periodic and solitary wave solutions to nonlinear evolution equations (NLEEs), smoothly connecting cnoidal waves (0< m < 1) and solitary waves (m → 1) through the functions cn, sn, and dn. The integrability and rich soliton dynamics of KdV-type systems, including stochastic perturbations and non-autonomous Gardner KP extensions, have been extensively studied via Lax pairs, Darboux transformations, and bilinear methods40,41. For multi-soliton interactions, Hirota’s bilinear method offers the most robust analytical framework for constructing exact N-soliton solutions, enabling detailed analysis of collision dynamics, phase shifts, and soliton integrity42,43. Recent plasma studies have applied this method to electron-acoustic wave collisions44, dust-ion acoustic multi-soliton interactions, and ion-acoustic soliton dynamics in planetary ionospheres45. These works highlight the necessity of multi-soliton analyses to understand collective nonlinear energy transfer. In this work, we employ the JEFE method to obtain exact cnoidal, snoidal, and dnoidal solutions for the coupled KdV equations (CKdVEs), while also integrating Hirota’s method to systematically derive single, double, and triple soliton solutions, together revealing how plasma parameters govern the periodic-to-solitary transition and multi-soliton collision dynamics in our strongly coupled dusty plasma model with nonextensive electrons and ion drag.

This paper presents a comprehensive theoretical investigation of head-on collisions between dust-acoustic multi-solitons in an unmagnetized, strongly coupled dusty plasma with ion-drag force and (r,q)-nonextensive distributed electrons and ions. We consider micron-sized dust particles with typical laboratory parameters: radius \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 4\ \mu$$\end{document}m and density \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 1.51\ \text {g/cm}^3$$\end{document}. Our work extends the single-soliton collision analysis of Kadi et al.19 to multi-soliton scenarios while self-consistently incorporating strong coupling effects and generalized statistics for both plasma species. The principal novelties of this study include: (i) the first analysis of multi-soliton (single, double, and triple) head-on collisions in a dusty plasma model that simultaneously accounts for strong coupling, ion drag, and (r,q)-nonextensive statistics; (ii) the derivation of exact analytical solutions using complementary mathematical techniques JEFE for periodic and solitary waves, and Hirota’s bilinear method for explicit multi-soliton solutions; and (iii) a detailed parametric study that elucidates how ion drag, coupling strength, and distribution parameters collectively govern soliton characteristics and phase shifts during collisions. The CKdVEs are employed because the plasma consists of two distinct ion species (positive and negative) interacting via the electrostatic field. This coupling enables two distinct collision scenarios: overtaking collisions, where faster solitons catch up and pass slower ones propagating in the same direction, and head-on collisions, where solitons approach from opposite directions. The CKdV model uniquely captures the energy exchange, constructive interference, and elastic phase shifts characteristic of both collision types, especially the interaction of not only counter-propagation single solitons but also among multi-solitons, which a single KdV equation cannot describe.

The electrons and ions follow a non-Maxwellian (r, q) velocity distribution, while the dust component is treated within a generalized hydrodynamic framework appropriate for strongly coupled regimes. The model, which builds upon and extends the formulation of Kadi et al.19, describes the dust dynamics through continuity, momentum, and Poisson equations.

This polynomial representation is a standard and well-established procedure in the literature for deriving evolution equations for small-amplitude electrostatic waves in multi-component plasmas. The same coefficient formulas apply to both species, with the appropriate species parameters used in each case. The novelty of the present model lies in the combination of the following elements within a single analytical framework: (i) the generalized hydrodynamic treatment of strongly coupled dust, (ii) the inclusion of a nonlinear ion-drag force, and (iii) the description of electrons and ions with a dual-parameter (r, q) nonextensive distribution. While individual components have precedents in the literature, their synthesis and the subsequent analysis of head-on solitary wave collisions via the ePLK method constitute the original contribution of this work.

Equations (1)–(3), together with the density expansions (5) and (6), form a closed set describing nonlinear dust-acoustic waves in a strongly coupled, nonextensive dusty plasma with ion drag. In the next section we employ the extended Poincaré–Lighthill–Kuo method to derive from them a pair of CKdVEs governing head-on collisions.

Derivation of coupled Korteweg–de Vries equations (CKdVEs)

The ePLK method is a powerful perturbation technique for analyzing wave interactions in nonlinear systems. It extends standard reductive perturbation theory by introducing multiple spatial and temporal scales, allowing the derivation of coupled evolution equations for counter-propagating waves. In our application, the ePLK method yields two CKdVEs that describe the nonlinear evolution of leftward and rightward propagating dust-acoustic waves, capturing their mutual interactions during head-on collisions. That is why we apply the ePLK method to analyze head-on collisions of nonlinear dust acoustic waves governed by equations (1)–(3)15,51. The KdV description derived via the ePLK method remains valid within specific parameter bounds. Colliding multi-soliton solution and phase shifts

This section presents analytical solutions for single, double, and triple solitons associated with head-on collisions of nonlinear dust acoustic waves in a dusty plasma, governed by CKdVEs that are determined from (1), (2), and (3). To determine these solution, one must be needed to implement the bilinear method42,55. Because, Hirota’s bilinear method is an analytical technique for constructing exact multi-soliton solutions to integrable NLEEs. Through a bilinear transformation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi = \frac{\partial ^2}{\partial \xi ^2} \ln f$$\end{document}, the nonlinear CKdVEs are converted into a bilinear form that admits exact N-soliton solutions. Hirota’s method can capture both overtaking collisions (solitons traveling in the same direction with different speeds) and head-on collisions (solitons traveling in opposite directions). While our ePLK-derived CKdVEs specifically models head-on collisions, Hirota’s method provides the analytical framework to obtain exact solutions for these interactions. Results and discussion

Physical model and methodological overview

This study investigates head-on collisions of dust-acoustic multi-solitons in a strongly coupled, unmagnetized dusty plasma composed of electrons, ions, and micron-sized negatively charged dust grains. The governing equations (1)–(3) follow a generalized hydrodynamic framework for strongly coupled regimes26,27 and include an ion-drag force term19. Both electrons and ions are described by (r, q)-nonextensive velocity distributions, a choice motivated by observational evidence from spacecraft missions (Freja, Viking, Cluster, Van Allen Probes)30 showing non-Maxwellian features with suprathermal tails in space plasmas29,30 and confirmed in laboratory dusty plasmas where charging currents drive species out of thermal equilibrium56,57. The (r, q) framework captures both suprathermal tails (parameter q) and flattened cores (parameter r), recovering Maxwellian (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q\rightarrow \infty$$\end{document}, r=0) and κ-distributions (r=0) as special cases. Phase velocity and parametric trends

The phase velocity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda = \sqrt{(H_1\gamma ' + R)/H_1}$$\end{document} reflects the balance between electrostatic restoring forces, strong-coupling compressibility, ion-drag momentum transfer, and non-Maxwellian statistics. Figure 1 shows how λ varies with the plasma parameters r, q, μ, and ν. Figure 1a plots λ against the spectral index r and normalized ion density μ for fixed q = 3.5. Increasing r raises λ because stronger high-energy tails in the (r,q) distribution enhance the pressure support (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_1$$\end{document} increases), accelerating the waves. Conversely, larger μ adds inertial loading, reducing λ. Figure 1b shows λ versus the entropic index q and normalized electron density ν at fixed r = 0.01. Higher q (closer to Maxwellian) increases λ by reducing superthermality and strengthening the pressure gradient, whereas higher ν enhances electron screening and weakens the restoring force, lowering λ. A consistent trend is the elevation of λ when ion drag is active (R > 1) compared to the drag-free case (R = 1). The drag force provides additional momentum transfer from streaming ions, quantified by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R = 1 + \frac{a\omega _{pi}T_e}{b\nu _{in}T_i}$$\end{document}, which directly boosts the phase speed. This enhancement persists across the entire parameter space, underscoring ion drag’s role in modifying linear wave propagation. A quantitative comparison of λ with and without drag is shown later in Fig. 10b. These trends also indicate how the phase velocity would behave if only ions were strongly nonextensive while electrons remained near-Maxwellian–a scenario that would raise λ similarly to increasing r in Fig. 1a, but with reduced electron-screening effects.

Higher \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_e$$\end{document} weakens the restoring force, producing shallower, broader waves, while higher \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_i$$\end{document} intensifies nonlinearity via the compressibility factor γ ', leading to deeper, narrower structures. The regular oscillations arise because dispersive effects dominate over nonlinearity for m < 1, enabling stable periodic propagation. These cnoidal wave solutions are modulationally stable within the KdV framework for the parameter ranges considered. As exact solutions of the CKdVEs, their stability can be assessed through standard modulational stability analysis. For our system with coefficients A, B, C, and D that incorporate strong coupling effects via γ ', small perturbations to the cnoidal wave solution satisfy a linearized equation. The condition for modulational stability requires positive dispersion (B > 0) and well-behaved coefficients across the parameter space. Our analysis confirms B > 0 for all Γ in 1< Γ < 200 and for the admissible (r, q) ranges (q > 1, q(r+1) > 5/2), ensuring stability. This analytical stability is consistent with the preserved waveform structure observed in the exact solutions shown in Fig. 4. The smooth transition from periodic waves (m < 1) to solitary waves (m → 1) demonstrates the method’s ability to capture the full nonlinear wave spectrum. The significance of comparing double and triple soliton cases lies in their distinct collision dynamics during the interactions among them. A double soliton (two bound solitons) colliding head-on with another counter-propagating double soliton results in four simultaneous pairwise collisions, producing a localized interference pattern with enhanced amplitude. Remarkably, both double and triple solitons emerge from these violent head-on collisions preserving their original shapes, speeds, and internal bound structures, demonstrating the elastic nature of multi-soliton interactions. From a physical standpoint, these studies are crucial because space and laboratory plasmas frequently host multiple coherent structures (e.g., soliton trains, electron holes, or envelope pulses) that interact head-on, influencing wave turbulence, energy cascades, and nonlinear saturation mechanisms.

Validity limits and model extensions

The CKdVEs derived via the ePLK method remain valid under specific parameter constraints. The perturbation parameter must satisfy \varepsilon < 0.2, with numerical validation showing increasing discrepancies for \varepsilon > 0.348. Our analysis employs \varepsilon = 0.1 throughout. The long-wavelength assumption \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k\lambda _D \ll 1$$\end{document} is inherent to the perturbation scheme. For coupling parameters, the generalized hydrodynamic framework applies for 1< Γ < 200. For Γ > 170 (approaching crystallization) or Γ > 200, viscoelastic effects dominate, requiring more comprehensive treatments26,27. These validity limits define the physical scope of our analytical results. Future work could extend the present analysis to larger amplitudes via higher-order KdV equations, to stronger coupling regimes using viscoelastic models, or to include explicit dust-neutral damping to study inelastic collision effects.

Summary and conclusions

This study has systematically investigated head-on collisions of nonlinear dust-acoustic multi-solitons and periodic waves in an unmagnetized, strongly coupled dusty plasma with ion-drag force and (r, q)-nonextensive distributions for electrons and ions. Using the extended Poincaré-Lighthill-Kuo (ePLK) perturbation technique, coupled Korteweg-de Vries equations (CKdVEs) were derived. Exact periodic and solitary wave solutions were obtained via the Jacobi elliptic function expansion method, while Hirota’s bilinear method was employed to construct single, double, and triple soliton solutions along with their collision dynamics and phase shifts.

It is found that the ion-drag force significantly enhances the phase velocity and amplitude of the solitons. The spectral index r and entropic index q play crucial roles in modifying soliton width, amplitude, and the magnitude of phase shifts after collision. The phase shift increases with the number of solitons, being larger in the triple soliton case compared to the double soliton case. All interactions remain elastic, preserving soliton identities after collision.

This work extends earlier single-soliton studies by incorporating strong coupling, ion-drag force, and comprehensive (r, q)-nonextensive statistics simultaneously in multi-soliton collision scenarios. The obtained results provide valuable theoretical understanding of nonlinear wave interactions in realistic dusty plasma environments.

The present analysis is based on the small-amplitude reductive perturbation approach and treats the (r, q) distribution phenomenologically. Future extensions may include higher-order perturbation methods, viscoelastic effects at stronger coupling, or dissipative terms to study inelastic collisions and wave damping.
