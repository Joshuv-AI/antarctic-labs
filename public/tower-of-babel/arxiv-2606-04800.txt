Extension of Lohwater – Pommerenke’s Theorem for strongly-normal Maps
Gopal Datt, Rahul Gogoi

Abstract
. We introduce strong normality for holomorphic curves and logharmonic mappings, extending classical normality concepts. We establish an extension of the rescaling characterization due to Lohwater and Pommerenke for not strongly-normal maps . In addition, we also study the Bloch mappings , little-Bloch mappings and prove Zalcman – Pang type rescaling results for them. The framework is further extended to strongly φ \varphi -normal mappings , yielding a unified treatment across these settings.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The notion of normality in complex analysis originates from the study of convergence phenomena of
holomorphic functions. Beginning with the foundational work of Weierstrass and leading to
Montel’s theory of normal families, the idea of compactness through boundedness and equicontinuity
became central to function theory. In the 1934, Yosida
[
31
]
first shifted this
perspective to individual meromorphic functions, a viewpoint later enhanced by Noshiro
[
25
]
, which was formalized by Lehto and
Virtanen
[
23
]
in 1957, who introduced the modern concept of normal meromorphic functions via conformal invariance and the spherical
derivative. Noshiro
[
25
]
provided a characterization of normal functions in terms of the spherical derivative. He showed that a
function
f
meromorphic in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
is
normal
if and only if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})f^{\#}(z)<\infty,

where
\displaystyle f^{\#}(z)=\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}
is the spherical derivative.

Later, Eremenko
[
18
]
widened the scope of normality beyond meromorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
to holomorphic curves from

\mathbb{D}
into complex projective spaces (
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
), thereby placing the theory within a broader geometric framework. Namely, a holomorphic curve
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is
normal
if and only if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\|f^{\prime}(z)\|_{FS}<\infty,

where
\|f^{\prime}(z)\|_{FS}
is the Fubini – Study derivative which will be discussed in the preliminaries section.

Building upon this foundation, further refinements of the concept have been introduced. In particular, Chen and Gauthier
[
12
]
proposed the
notion of
strongly-normal
functions, requiring the sharper condition

(1-|z|^{2})f^{\#}(z)\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D},

where
\partial\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
.

Following this, we now introduce the strong normality of holomorphic curves, which
is inspired by the definition of strongly-normal functions given by Chen and Gauthier
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A holomorphic curve
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is
strongly-normal
if

(1-|z|^{2})\|f^{\prime}(z)\|_{FS}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D}.

Recently, in 2019, Arbeláez et al.
[
6
]
introduced the concept of a normal function in the context of harmonic mappings. Subsequently, in
2020, Jiang
[
20
]
extended this idea by defining a notion of normal function for logharmonic
mappings. These definitions closely follow those introduced by Noshiro and Eremenko, with the spherical derivative adapted to the corresponding setting.

To extend this notion beyond these, we briefly recall that a
\mathbb{C}
-valued mapping
f
in
\mathbb{D}

is harmonic if there exist holomorphic functions
g
,
h
in
\mathbb{D}
where
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)},\ z\in\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, a
\mathbb{C}
-valued
mapping
f
defined in
\mathbb{D}
is logharmonic if it satisfies the nonlinear elliptic partial differential equation

\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}=a_{f}(z)\,\left(\frac{f_{z}}{f}\right){\overline{f}},

(i)

where the function
a_{f}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is analytic and satisfies
|a_{f}(z)|<1,\ \text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

The above equation describes a class of mappings that generalizes analytic functions by allowing a
controlled dependence on the conjugate variable. In particular, when
a_{f}\equiv 0
,
the equation reduces to
f_{\bar{z}}=0
and hence
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.

If
f
is a non-vanishing logharmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f
admits the representation

f(z)=h(z)\,\overline{g(z)},\ z\in\mathbb{D}

(ii)

where
h
and
g
are holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

If
f
is a non-constant logharmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and vanishes only at the origin
z=0
, then
f
can be written as

f(z)=z^{m_{0}}|z|^{2\beta\,m_{0}}h(z)\overline{g(z)},\ z\in\mathbb{D}

(iii)

where
m_{0}
is non-negative integer which represents the order of the zero at the origin,
\Re(\beta)>-\tfrac{1}{2}
,

h
and
g
are holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
,
g(0)=1
and
h(0)\neq 0
.
It follows that
f(0)\neq 0
if and only if
m_{0}=0
, in which case the representation reduces to
(
ii
). Moreover, if
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and vanishes at the origin, then
necessarily
m_{0}=1
and thus
f
assumes the form

f(z)=z\,|z|^{2\beta}h(z)\overline{g(z)}.

Chen and Gauthier
[
12
]
also introduced the notion of strong normality for harmonic mappings using an
analogous formulation. We now proceed to define strong normality in logharmonic mappings.

Definition 1.2
.

A logharmonic mapping
f=z|z|^{2\beta}h\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
strongly-normal
if

(1-|z|^{2})f^{\diamondsuit}(z)\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D},

where

f^{\diamondsuit}(z)=\dfrac{|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\overline{z}}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}.

It is interesting to note that the notion of a
\varphi
-normal function
was introduced by
Aulaskari and Rättyä
[
8
]
, which defines a class,
significantly larger than the class of normal functions, in
\mathbb{D}
. Recently, Bohra et al.

[
10
]
introduced the analogue of
\varphi
-normal function

for
\mathbb{C}
-valued
harmonic mappings
. Note that the term
\varphi
here denotes a
\mathbb{R}
-valued
function whose growth is greater than that of growth of
1/(1-r^{2})
where
0\leq r<1
and it admits a regularity condition close to
1
.

Recall that an increasing function
\varphi:[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
is smoothly increasing if

\varphi(r)(1-r)\to\infty\ \text{ as }\ r\to 1

and

R_{a}(z):=\frac{\varphi(|a+(z/\varphi(|a|))|)}{\varphi(|a|)}\to 1

as
|a|\to 1
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
. With
these preparations, we are now in a position to
define
\varphi
-normal logharmonic mappings
.

Definition 1.3
.

A logharmonic mapping
f=z|z|^{2\beta}h\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is

\varphi
-
normal
if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\diamondsuit}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}<\infty.

In a similar way, we introduce
strongly
\varphi
-normal harmonic mappings
and
strongly
\varphi
-normal logharmonic mappings
.

Definition 1.4
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
strongly

\varphi
-
normal
if

\frac{f^{\star}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D},

where

f^{\star}(z)=\dfrac{|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}.

Definition 1.5
.

A logharmonic mapping
f=z|z|^{2\beta}h\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
strongly

\varphi
-
normal
if

\frac{f^{\diamondsuit}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D}.

Let us now focus on another class of functions named
Bloch functions
. The concept of Bloch
functions was first studied in a systematic manner by Seidel and Walsh
[
28
]
and was subsequently developed further by
several authors, including Pommerenke
[
27
]
, Yamashita
[
30
]
and Colonna
[
13
]
. Colonna stated that a holomorphic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is
Bloch
if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

In a similar way, Colonna formalized the definition in the harmonic setting
and introduced the class of
Bloch harmonic mappings

[
14
]
. We also recall the notion of
little-Bloch functions
, introduced by Anderson et al. in
[
5
]
,
which serves as a counterpart to strong normality by capturing vanishing boundary
behavior. That is, a holomorphic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is
little-Bloch
if

(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\to 0,\ \text{as}\ z\to\partial\mathbb{D}.

Motivated by this
perspective, Aljunaid and Colonna
[
4
]
introduced the notion of
little-Bloch harmonic mappings
and Chang et al.

[
11
]
gave us the notion of
Bloch logharmonic mappings
and
little-Bloch logharmonic mappings
.

We begin by recalling foundational rescaling principles for normality in various settings. In
1973, Lohwater and Pommerenke
[
24
, Theorem 1]
established a theorem that characterizes non-normal meromorphic functions
in terms of their behavior under suitable rescalings near
\partial\mathbb{D}
. A cornerstone in
this direction is the Lohwater – Pommerenke theorem for holomorphic curves in projective space, established by Hahn.

Result 1.6

(
[
21
, Theorem 4]
)
.

A holomorphic curve
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is normal if and only if there does not
exist any sequence
\{z_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
, a sequence
\{\rho_{\nu}\}\subset(0,\infty),\ \rho_{\nu}\searrow 0

and
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
such that
g_{\nu}(\zeta)=f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)
converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant
holomorphic curve
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}{(\mathbb{C})}
.

Motivated by this rescaling paradigm, one may ask how such phenomena behave under stronger notions of
normality. In this direction, Aulaskari and Wulan developed an analogue tailored to
strongly-normal meromorphic functions
,
incorporating a quantitative restriction on the scaling.

Result 1.7

(
[
7
, Theorem 1]
)
.

A function
f
meromorphic in
\mathbb{D}
is strongly-normal function
if and only if there does not exist a constant
R>0
; a sequence
\{z_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
with
|z_{\nu}|\to 1
;
a sequence
\{\rho_{\nu}\}\subset(0,\infty)
where
\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{1-|z_{\nu}|}<\frac{1}{2R}
such that
\{f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)\}
converges uniformly on every
compact subset to a non-constant meromorphic function in
|\zeta|<R
.

Bohra et al. introduced an analogous framework for
\varphi
-normal harmonic mappings
, thereby
broadening the scope of the theory beyond normal harmonic mappings.

Result 1.8

(
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

A non-constant mapping
f
harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
is not
\varphi
-normal if and only if there exist sequences
\{z_{\nu}\}
,
\{\rho_{\nu}\}
where
\rho_{\nu}>0
as
\nu\to\infty
such that

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}f\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)=g(\zeta)

uniformly on compact subsets in
\mathbb{C}
, where
g
is a non-constant harmonic mapping.

Guided by the above results, we now formulate a partial Zalcman – Pang type generalization of Result
1.7
, beginning with meromorphic functions.

Theorem 1.9
.

If a function
f
meromorphic in
\mathbb{D}

; a sequence

The complex

(1)

The
Fubini – Study distance
between any two points in

is defined as

d_{FS}(p,q)=\frac{\sum_{k,l=0}^{n}|p_{k}q_{l}-p_{l}q_{k}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n}|p_{k}|^{2}}\,\sqrt{\sum_{l=0}^{n}|q_{l}|^{2}}},

and

\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

(2)

The
Fubini – Study norm
of
f
at
z
is defined as

\|f(z)\|_{FS}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f_{k}(z)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(3)

The
Fubini – Study derivative
of
f
at
z
is defined as

\|f^{\prime}(z)\|_{FS}=\frac{\left(\sum_{0\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq n}\left|f_{l_{1}}^{\prime}(z)f_{l_{2}}(z)-f_{l_{1}}(z)f_{l_{2}}^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}.

The lemma serves as a fundamental rescaling criterion for non-normality for a family of holomorphic

\{z_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and of real numbers
\{\rho_{\nu}\}\subset(0,\infty)
with

\rho_{\nu}\searrow 0
as
\nu\to\infty
such that the functions
g(\zeta)=\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)
converges
locally uniformly to a non-constant harmonic mapping
g(\zeta)
where
g^{\star}(\zeta)\leq g^{\star}(0)=1
, where
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)
.

The above result naturally extends to the logharmonic framework, where a similar rescaling principle characterizes non-normality.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

A non-constant mapping
f
logharmonic in
\mathbb{D}
is non-normal if and only if there exist sequence of points
\{z_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and of real
numbers
\{\rho_{\nu}\}\subset(0,\infty)
with
\rho_{\nu}\searrow 0
as
\nu\to\infty
such that the functions
g(\zeta)=\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)
converges
locally uniformly to a non-constant logharmonic mapping
g(\zeta)
whre
g^{\diamondsuit}(\zeta)\leq g^{\diamondsuit}(0)=1
, where
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)
.

It is worth noting that, in the above result due to Ahamed and Mandal, the converse direction is not
correctly established. For the purposes of this article, we therefore restrict our attention to the
forward implication. In the same way, throughout our subsequent results, we will consider only with the forward direction of Lemmas
2.3
and

2.4
and confine ourselves to the range
\alpha\geq 0
.

We now proceed to a corresponding characterization in the setting of
\varphi
-normal logharmonic mappings.

Lemma 2.5
.

A non-constant mapping
f
logharmonic in
\mathbb{D}
is not
\varphi
-normal if and only if there exist
sequences
\{z_{\nu}\}
,
\{\rho_{\nu}\}
where
\rho_{\nu}>0
as
\nu\to\infty
such that

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}f\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)=g(\zeta)

uniformly on every compact subset in
\mathbb{C}
, where
g
is a non-constant logharmonic mapping.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows mutatis mutandis from the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
established by Bohra et al.∎

Proof of the Main Results

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Suppose
f
is not strongly-normal. Then there exist a positive constant
c
and a sequence
\{\tilde{z}_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}

with
\tilde{z}_{\nu}\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
such that

(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|^{2})f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq c\quad\text{for }\ \nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(
1.9
.1)

If
f
is not a normal function, the result follows from Lemma
2.2
. So we assume that
f
is normal function and assume that

|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|>1/2
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
s_{\nu}=2|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|/(1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)
and
s_{\nu}^{\prime}=|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|/(2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

1/3<s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}<1
and
s_{\nu}\to 1,\ s_{\nu}^{\prime}\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
and there exist a constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{4}>0\quad\text{for }\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(
1.9
.2)

Also we have

s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime}=\frac{2|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}-\frac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}=\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{(1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)(2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}

and

1-s_{\nu}=\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}.

Thus

\displaystyle 3(1-s_{\nu})\geq\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\geq\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{(1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)(2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}=s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime}.

(
1.9
.2.1)

Define

F_{\nu}(t,z):=\dfrac{\left(1-\frac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}t^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z)|^{2})f^{\#}(z)}{1+\left(1-\frac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{\alpha}t^{2\alpha}|f(z)|^{2}},

where
0<t\leq 1
and
s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z|<s_{\nu}
. Evidently, the functions
F_{\nu}(t,z)
are continuous
in
(0,1]\times\{s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z|<s_{\nu}\}
. Also, since
\alpha\geq 0
and
f
is meromorphic, it follows that

\lim_{t\to 0}F_{\nu}(t,z)=0.

(
1.9
.3)

We claim that

F_{\nu}(t,z)\geq t^{1+\alpha}\left(1-\tfrac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}f^{\#}(z).

(
1.9
.4)

As
\alpha\geq 0
, then using

\left(1-\tfrac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{\alpha}t^{2\alpha}\leq 1,

we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(t,z)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(1-\frac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}t^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z)|^{2})f^{\#}(z)}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=t^{1+\alpha}\left(1-\tfrac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}f^{\#}(z).

From (
.2
) and (
.4
), we have

F_{\nu}(1,\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq c_{0}\quad\text{for }\nu\in\mathbb{N},

where
c_{0}>0
.
for sufficiently large
\nu
,
F_{\nu}(1,\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq c_{1}
, where
0<c_{1}<c_{0}
and hence

\sup_{s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z|<s_{\nu}}F_{\nu}(1,z)\geq F_{\nu}(1,\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq c_{1}.

Also, from (
.3
), it follows that

\sup_{s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z|<s_{\nu}}F_{\nu}(t,z)<c_{1}\quad\text{for sufficiently small }t.

Thus, by the intermediate value theorem, there exist
t_{\nu}\in(0,1)
and
z_{\nu}

with
s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}
such that

\sup_{s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z|<s_{\nu}}F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z)=F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z_{\nu})=c_{1}.

(
1.9
.5)

From (
.4
) and (
.5
), we get

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z_{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},\tilde{z}_{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq t_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}f^{\#}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=t_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}F_{\nu}(1,\tilde{z}_{\nu}).

Since
F_{\nu}(1,\tilde{z}_{\nu})\geq c_{0}
, it follows that as
\nu\to\infty
,
t_{\nu}\leq c_{1}/c_{0}
. Put

\rho_{\nu}:=\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}t_{\nu}.

From (
.2.1
), we get

1-|z_{\nu}|>1-s_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{3}(s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}(|z_{\nu}|-s_{\nu}^{\prime}),\ \ \nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{1-|z_{\nu}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{(s_{\nu}-|z_{\nu}|)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{3}(|z_{\nu}|-s_{\nu}^{\prime})\right)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{\nu}}{s_{\nu}^{1/2}|z_{\nu}|^{1/2}}

\displaystyle<\frac{12\sqrt{3}c_{1}}{c_{0}}:=\frac{1}{2R}\quad\text{for }\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(
1.9
.6)

Thus the function

g_{\nu}(\zeta)=\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)

is defined for
|\zeta|<R
.
Carrying out the computation, we obtain

g_{\nu}^{\#}(\zeta)=\frac{\rho_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2})f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)}{1+\rho_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}.

(
1.9
.7)

In particular,

\displaystyle g_{\nu}^{\#}(0)=

\displaystyle~\frac{\rho_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu})|^{2})f^{\#}(z_{\nu})}{1+\rho_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\dfrac{\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{(1+\alpha)/2}t_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu})|^{2})f^{\#}(z_{\nu})}{1+\left(1-\tfrac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{\alpha}t_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z_{\nu})=c_{1}.

(
1.9
.8)

We now need to show that the sequence
\{g_{\nu}(\zeta)\}
is normal.

Since
\left[\left(1-\frac{|z_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}\right]/\left[\left(1-\frac{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}\right)^{1/2}\right]\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
, there exist
\epsilon_{\nu}>0
with
\epsilon_{\nu}\to 0
as
\nu\to\infty
such that

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{\nu})\left(1-\tfrac{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}\right)^{1/2}t_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\nu}\leq(1+\epsilon_{\nu})\left(1-\tfrac{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}\right)^{1/2}t_{\nu}

(
1.9
.9)

Take
\Upsilon_{\nu}=\left(1-\tfrac{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\tfrac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta|}\right)^{1/2}t_{\nu}
.
Therefore, from (
.5
), (
.7
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle g^{\#}_{n}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{\nu})^{1+\alpha}\Upsilon_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|)f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)}{1+(1-\epsilon_{\nu})^{2\alpha}\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\dfrac{(1+\epsilon_{\nu})^{1+\alpha}\Upsilon_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|)f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)}{1+\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ ~~\left[\dfrac{1+\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}{1+(1-\epsilon_{\nu})^{2\alpha}\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon_{\nu})^{1+\alpha}F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z_{\nu})\left[\dfrac{1+\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}{1+(1-\epsilon_{\nu})^{2\alpha}\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon_{\nu})^{1+\alpha}F_{\nu}(t_{\nu},z_{\nu})\left[1+(1-(1-\epsilon_{\nu})^{2\alpha})\Upsilon_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\to c_{1}\ \text{ as }\ \nu\to\infty.

(
1.9
.10)

Hence, by Marty’s criterion
[
3
, p. 226, Theorem 17]
,
\{g_{\nu}\}
is a normal family
in
|\zeta|<R
and therefore converges locally
uniformly in
|\zeta|<R
to a meromorphic mapping
g
. From (
.8
) and (
.10
),

g^{\#}(\zeta)\leq g^{\#}(0)=c_{1}\neq 0,

so
g
is non-constant.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Suppose that

F_{\nu}(\zeta)=\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta)

converges locally uniformly to
F(\zeta)
in
|\zeta|<R
, where
F(\zeta)
is a non-constant holomorphic function and
|z_{\nu}|\to 1
with

\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{1-|z_{\nu}|}<\frac{1}{2R}\quad\text{for all }\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Choose
\zeta_{0}
,
|\zeta_{0}|<R
, such that
F^{\#}(\zeta_{0})>0
.
The proof will be completed by contradiction.
As

\displaystyle F_{\nu}^{\#}(\zeta_{0})=

\displaystyle~\frac{\rho_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2})f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})}{1+\rho_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\rho_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})\left(\frac{1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}}{1+\rho_{\nu}^{2\alpha}|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\rho_{\nu}^{1+\alpha}f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})\left(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha+1}\left(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})|^{2}\right)}{1-|z_{\nu}|-\rho_{\nu}|\zeta_{0}|}\left(1-|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}\right)f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{R}\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}\left(1+|f(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})|^{2}\right)\left(1-|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}\right)f^{\#}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{R}\rho_{\nu}^{\alpha}\left(1-|z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0}|^{2}\right)|f^{\prime}(z_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\zeta_{0})|.

As
f
is little-Bloch function, so
F_{\nu}^{\#}(\zeta_{0})\to 0
. So
F^{\#}(\zeta_{0})=0
and so
F
is
constant which is a contradiction. Thus
f
is not little-Bloch function.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.19
.

Suppose
f
is not a strongly
\varphi
-normal harmonic mapping, then there exist
c>0
, a sequence

\{\tilde{z}_{\nu}\}
of points in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
and

\frac{f^{\star}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})}{\varphi(|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}\geq c,\ \text{ for }\ \nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(
1.19
.1)

If
f
is not
\varphi
-normal, then the result follows from the Lohwater - Pommerenke Theorem for
\varphi
-normal harmonic mappings i.e., Result

1.8
. So we assume that
f
is
\varphi
-normal and say that
|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|>1/2
, for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. We define

\displaystyle s_{\nu}=\frac{2|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\ \ \text{and}\ \ s_{\nu}^{\prime}=\frac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}.

Then
1/3<s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}<1
and
s_{\nu}\to 1
,
s_{\nu}^{\prime}\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
.
We have,

s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime}=\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{(1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)(2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}

and

1-s_{\nu}=\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}.

This gives us,

3(1-s_{\nu})\geq\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\geq\frac{3|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}{(1+|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)(2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}=s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime}.

(
1.19
.2)

Take
\{z_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\nu}=&\max_{s_{\nu}^{\prime}\leq|z|\leq s_{\nu}}\left(1-\frac{|z|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z|}\right)^{1/2}\frac{f^{\star}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}\\
=&\left(1-\frac{|z_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}\frac{f^{\star}(z_{\nu})}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}.\end{split}

(
1.19
.3)

Since
s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}
, by (
.1
) we have,

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\nu}&\geq\left(1-\frac{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}\frac{f^{\star}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})}{\varphi(|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}\\
&=\left(\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}{2-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}\frac{f^{\star}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})}{\varphi(|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)\frac{f^{\star}(\tilde{z}_{\nu})}{\varphi(|\tilde{z}_{\nu}|)}\geq\frac{c}{4}>0.\end{split}

(
1.19
.4)

Therefore
s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}
,
|z_{\nu}|\to 1
as
\nu\to\infty
and by (
.3
) and (
.4
),
\displaystyle\frac{f^{\star}(z_{\nu})}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\to\infty
, as
\nu\to\infty
. Take

\rho_{\nu}=\frac{1}{M_{\nu}}\left(1-\frac{|z_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}{f^{\star}(z_{\nu})}.

Then
\rho_{\nu}\to 0
. From

1-|z_{\nu}|>1-s_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{3}(s_{\nu}-s_{\nu}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}(|z_{\nu}|-s_{\nu}^{\prime}).

(
1.19
.5)

Since
1-|z_{\nu}|>s_{\nu}-|z_{\nu}|
and
1/3<s_{\nu}^{\prime}<|z_{\nu}|<s_{\nu}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{1-|z_{\nu}|}&=\frac{1}{(1-|z_{\nu}|)M_{\nu}}\left(1-\frac{|z_{\nu}|}{s_{\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{\nu}^{\prime}}{|z_{\nu}|}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{(s_{\nu}-|z_{\nu}|)^{1/2}(|z_{\nu}|-s_{\nu}^{\prime})^{1/2}}{M_{\nu}(s_{\nu}-|z_{\nu}|)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{3}(|z_{\nu}|-s_{\nu}^{\prime})\right)^{1/2}s_{\nu}^{1/2}|z_{\nu}|^{1/2}}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{M_{\nu}s_{\nu}^{1/2}|z_{\nu}|^{1/2}}\leq\frac{12\sqrt{3}}{c}:=\frac{1}{2R}.\end{split}

(
1.19
.6)

The harmonic mappings
g_{\nu}(\zeta)=f\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)
are defined for
|\zeta|<R
.
Then
g_{\nu}^{\star}(0)=\left(\dfrac{\rho_{\nu}}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)f^{\star}(z_{\nu})=1
. To
show that the sequence
\{g_{\nu}(\zeta)\}
is normal. Let us consider

\displaystyle g_{\nu}^{\#}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}f^{\#}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\rho_{\nu}\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\dfrac{f^{\#}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)}{\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\left(\rho_{\nu}\dfrac{f^{\#}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)}{\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}\right)\to 1.

Thus
\{g_{\nu}(\zeta)\}
is normal in
|\zeta|<R
and has a convergent subsequence which converges to a
harmonic mapping
g
in
|\zeta|<R
with
g^{\#}(0)=1\neq 0
. So
g
is non-constant.

Conversely, suppose that
g_{\nu}(\zeta)=f\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)
converges locally
uniformly to
g(\zeta)
in
|\zeta|<R
, where
g
is non-constant and
\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{1-|z_{\nu}|}<\frac{1}{2R}
. Now,
g_{\nu}(\zeta)=f_{\nu}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)
are defined for

\displaystyle|\zeta|<R
.

\displaystyle g_{\nu}^{\star}(\zeta)=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}f^{\star}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\rho_{\nu}\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\dfrac{f^{\star}\left(z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right)}{\varphi\left(\left|z_{\nu}+\frac{\rho_{\nu}\zeta}{\varphi(|z_{\nu}|)}\right|\right)}\to 0\ \text{ as }\ \nu\to\infty.

As
f
is strongly
\varphi
-normal, so
|z|\to\partial\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{f^{\star}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}\to 0.

So
\{g_{\nu}\}
is strongly
\varphi
-normal, thus
g_{\nu}^{\star}(\zeta)\to 0
, where
g
is constant, which is a contradiction.
∎
