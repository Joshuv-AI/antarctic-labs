Pseudo-Euclidean Novikov Superalgebras: Structure and Properties Thanks: S. Bo. was supported by the grant NYUAD-065
Saïd Benayadi, Sofiane Bouarroudj, Hamza El Ouali

Abstract
. A pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is a Novikov superalgebra endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form ⟨ , ⟩ \langle\;,\;\rangle such that all left multiplication operators are ⟨ , ⟩ \langle\;,\;\rangle -antisymmetric. In this case, the associated Lie superalgebra ( 𝒜 − , ⟨ , ⟩ ) ({\mathcal{A}}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle) is a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra. In this paper, we investigate the structure of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras. In particular, we introduce a distinguished subclass, called Milnor superalgebras , and prove that any pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra whose two-sided ideal is non-degenerate belongs to this class. We provide a method for constructing pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras. We also introduce a double extension procedure for pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras and show that every such superalgebra with a degenerate two-sided ideal can be obtained via this method. Furthermore, we establish that any pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra is either a Milnor superalgebra or can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions starting from a Milnor superalgebra. As an application, we provide a complete classification of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras of total dimension at most four.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie (super)algebras

A pseudo-Riemannian Lie group is a Lie group
G
endowed with a left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mu
. Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
, equipped with the bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle=\mu_{e}
, is called a pseudo-Riemannian (or pseudo-Euclidean) Lie algebra. It is well known that there exists a unique torsion-free connection compatible with the metric, called the
Levi-Civita connection
. This connection induces a bilinear product on
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
Levi-Civita product
, defined by the Koszul formula:

2\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle[u,v],w\rangle-\langle[v,w],u\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle,\qquad\text{ for all }\,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

This product satisfies the identities

u\bullet v-v\bullet u=[u,v],\qquad\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle+\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

In the framework of pseudo-Euclidean non-associative (super)algebras, and in particular Leibniz (super)algebras, this construction has been extended in
[
7
]
. See also
[
11
,
12
]
for the antisymmetric case.

The curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection at the identity element
e
is given by

\mathcal{R}(u,v):={\mathrm{L}}_{[u,v]}-[{\mathrm{L}}_{u},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}],

where
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
denotes the left multiplication operator defined by

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}(v)=u\bullet v,
for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

A pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
(G,\mu)
is said to be
flat
if its curvature
\mathcal{R}
vanishes identically. In this case, the associated Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is called a
flat pseudo-Riemannian
(
or pseudo-Euclidean
)
Lie algebra
. The vanishing of the curvature is equivalent to the fact that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left-symmetric algebra. Consequently, a flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra can be viewed as a left-symmetric algebra endowed with a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form for which all left multiplication operators are antisymmetric with respect to this form.

In the case where the metric is Riemannian (or Euclidean), that is, positive definite (or negative definite), J. Milnor
[
33
]
proved that a Euclidean Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
decomposes orthogonally as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u},

where
\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal,
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra, and
\mathrm{ad}_{u}
is
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-antisymmetric, for all
u\in\mathfrak{b}
.

In recent years, several works have been devoted to the study of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras with arbitrary signatures. In the Lorentzian case, that is, for signature
(1,n-1)
, it was shown in
[
19
]
that any flat Lorentzian Lie algebra is necessarily solvable. In
[
3
]
, the authors introduced a construction method, called the
double extension
, for flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras, which is an analogue of the double extension procedure for quadratic Lie algebras developed by A. Medina and P. Revoy
[
32
]
. They proved that flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras can be obtained via this method and that they are at most
3
-step nilpotent. Furthermore, M. Guédiri
[
23
]
classified
2
-step nilpotent flat Lorentzian Lie algebras and showed that such algebras are trivial extensions of the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
, where the restriction of the metric to
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
being Lorentzian, and
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
has a degenerate center. Later, M. Ait Ben Haddou, M. Boucetta and H. Lebzioui
[
1
]
further investigated flat Lorentzian Lie algebras. They proved that flat Lorentzian Lie algebras with degenerate center can be obtained by the double extension method. In addition, it was shown in
[
15
]
that non-unimodular flat Lorentzian Lie algebras can also be constructed using this approach. A complete classification of flat Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras was later obtained in
[
6
]
.

For signature
(2,n-2)
, M. Boucetta and H. Lebzioui
[
16
]
studied flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras and showed that every nilpotent flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra of this signature arises as a double extension of a nilpotent flat Lorentzian Lie algebra.
Moreover, in
[
28
]
, the author proved that any unimodular flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra of signature
(2,n-2)
with degenerate center arises as a double extension of a unimodular flat Lorentzian Lie algebra.

More recently, in
[
7
]
, the authors introduced the notion of the Levi-Civita product associated with pseudo-Euclidean non-associative (super)algebras, in particular left Leibniz (super)algebras, together with a corresponding notion of curvature. They investigated flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz (super)algebras and developed a double extension procedure in this setting. In particular, they proved that any flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz (super)algebra can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions starting from a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie (super)algebra. Consequently, the classification of flat pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz (super)algebras reduces to the classification of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie (super)algebras.

1.2.
Novikov (super)algebras

Novikov algebras constitute an important subclass of left-symmetric algebras. A Novikov algebra is a left-symmetric algebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
satisfying

(u\bullet v)\bullet w=(u\bullet w)\bullet v,\quad\text{ for all $u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.$}

It is well known that the commutator

[u,v]=u\bullet v-v\bullet u,
for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},
defines a Lie algebra structure on
{\mathcal{A}}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
.

In
[
29
]
, the author introduced the notion of a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, that is, a Novikov algebra endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form such that all left multiplication operators are
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-antisymmetric. It follows that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebra if and only if
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra and

[{\mathrm{R}}_{u},{\mathrm{R}}_{v}]=0,
for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},

where
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
is the right multiplication operator, and
\bullet
coincides with the Levi-Civita product associated with
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
.

In
[
29
]
, the author also studied pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras in the Lorentzian case. In particular, using Milnor’s theorem, it was shown that the Levi-Civita product of a flat Euclidean Lie algebra is Novikov. Moreover, it was proved that a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
such that the derived ideal
[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}]
is non-degenerate satisfies the orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{A}}^{-}=[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}]^{\perp}\oplus[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}],

where both components are abelian,
[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}]
is non-degenerate, and
\mathrm{ad}_{u}
is
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-antisymmetric for all
u\in[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}]^{\perp}
. In particular, such algebras are
2
-step solvable. In the degenerate case, it was shown that these algebras can be obtained via the double extension process from flat Euclidean Lie algebras.

In
[
14
]
, the authors generalized these results to arbitrary signature. They proved that pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras are
2
-step solvable and established analogous structural decompositions when the derived ideal is non-degenerate. Furthermore, they introduced a double extension by blocks for pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras and showed that, in the degenerate case, such algebras are obtained by a double extension starting from pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras such that the derived ideal
[{\mathcal{A}}^{-},{\mathcal{A}}^{-}]
is non-degenerate .

In a related direction, H. Lebzioui
[
27
]
studied the class
\mathcal{C}
of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
such that the derived ideal
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is contained in the left annihilator of the Levi-Civita product. In fact, Novikov algebras are subclasses of
\mathcal{C}
. This condition implies, in particular, that these Lie algebras are
2
-step solvable. Moreover, this is equivalent to
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
being a pseudo-Euclidean left-symmetric
{\mathrm{L}}
-algebra, where
{\mathcal{A}}
is the underlying vector space of
\mathfrak{g}
. It was shown that every Lorentzian algebra in
\mathcal{C}
with degenerate derived ideal can be obtained by a flat double extension. In the nilpotent case, the derived ideal is necessarily degenerate, and the algebra is obtained by successive flat double extensions starting from an abelian Euclidean Lie algebra. In the non-degenerate case, a complete characterization was obtained for any signature. The study of this class was further developed in
[
8
]
, where the authors provided an inductive description of all elements of
\mathcal{C}
in arbitrary signature by introducing double extensions by algebras of dimension
1
and
2
. They also showed that pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras form a subclass of pseudo-Euclidean left-symmetric
{\mathrm{L}}
-algebras. In addition, in
[
9
]
, the authors investigated Lie-admissible algebras whose product is a biderivation of the associated Lie algebra, and studied in particular pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras satisfying this condition.

Zelmanov
[
37
]
studied Novikov algebras endowed with a positive definite symmetric bilinear form for which the right multiplications are symmetric. He proved that such algebras are necessarily associative and provided their classification. The Lorentzian case was later investigated by Guediri
[
22
]
, who obtained a complete classification in that setting.

In
[
4
,
34
]
, the authors studied Novikov superalgebras
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
(see the definition of Novikov superalgebras in the Background section) endowed with an even nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
which is invariant, that is,

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle,\quad\text{ for all }u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}.

Such structures are called
symmetric Novikov superalgebras
or
quadratic Novikov superalgebras
. They showed that such superalgebras are associative satisfying an additional condition, and that the associated Lie superalgebra
(\mathcal{A}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic
2
-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra. Several examples are also provided. Moreover, the authors in
[
4
]
introduced the notion of double extensions in order to obtain inductive descriptions of symmetric Novikov superalgebras.

In this paper, we study Novikov superalgebras endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form for which left multiplications are antisymmetric.

1.3.
On the results

Section
2
collects the preliminary material required for our work. For completeness and to establish notation, we include a review of key concepts, some of which may be familiar to specialists. We introduce the notions of
{\mathrm{L}}
- and
{\mathrm{R}}
-superalgebras. If both conditions are satisfied, we define the notion of an
{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}
-superalgebra. We also show that any
{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}
-superalgebra is Lie admissible. Moreover, we show that the corresponding Lie superalgebra is
2
-step solvable, see Proposition
2.3.2.  Proposition
.

In section
3
, we introduce the concept of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra, see Definition
3.1.1.  Definition
. These are Novikov superalgebras endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
such that all left multiplication operators are
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-antisymmetric. We show that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra if and only if
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra with the property that

[{\mathrm{R}}_{u},{\mathrm{R}}_{v}]=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},

where
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
denotes the right multiplication operator. In this case, the product
\bullet
coincides with the Levi-Civita product associated with
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
. This equivalence allows us to reduce the study of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras to that of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebras together with commuting right multiplication operators.

First, we establish a key structural result (see Lemma
3.1.4.  Lemma
): if
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is a superalgebra endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form such that all left multiplication operators are antisymmetric and the right multiplication operators commute, then

(u\bullet v)\bullet w=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\,u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.

We then prove that a pseudo-Euclidean superalgebra is Novikov if and only if it is an
{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}
-superalgebra if and only if it is a left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebras (see Propositions
3.1.5.  Proposition
).

Furthermore, by applying Engel’s theorem, we show that a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is nilpotent if and only if all left multiplication operators
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
are nilpotent for every
u\in{\mathcal{A}}
, which is also equivalent to the nilpotency of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
(see Definition
3.1.9.  Definition
and Proposition
3.1.7.  Proposition
).

While studying the structure theory of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras, we are led to introduce a distinguished subclass, called
Milnor
superalgebras (see Definition
3.1.9.  Definition
and Proposition
3.1.10.  Proposition
). In particular, we prove that any pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra whose two-sided ideal is non-degenerate is necessarily a Milnor superalgebra (see Theorem
3.1.11.  Theorem
). Furthermore, we show that if
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra such that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate, then
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
is nilpotent if and only if the product
\bullet
is trivial, see Corollary
3.1.12.  Corollary
.

We introduce the notion of representation of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras and define the associated product. More precisely, given such a superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, we define a new bilinear product
\star
on
{\mathcal{A}}
by means of the relation

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\star w\rangle,
for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}},
and we show that this product endows
{\mathcal{A}}
with a structure of a
2
-step nilpotent Lie superalgebra. Furthermore, we establish a characterization of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras in terms of bimodule isomorphisms. More precisely, we prove that the existence of a suitable bilinear form is equivalent to the isomorphism between certain naturally associated
{\mathcal{A}}
-bimodules (see Theorem
3.2.15.  Theorem
). Finally, motivated by this representation and inspired by the
T^{*}
-extension for quadratic algebras introduced in
[
10
]
, we provide a method for constructing pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras (see Theorems
3.2.19.  Theorem
and
3.2.23.  Theorem
).

In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of
double extension
for pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras, see Theorem
4.4.4.  Theorem
, Theorem
4.4.9.  Theorem
, Theorem
4.4.12.  Theorem
, and Theorem
4.4.16.  Theorem
. This construction is inspired by the classical double extension, originally introduced by Medina and Revoy
[
32
]
for quadratic Lie algebras.

In section
5
, we show that every pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra whose two-sided ideal is degenerate can be obtained by a double extension (see Theorems
5.2.  Theorem
and
5.5.  Theorem
).
More precisely, every non-trivial even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean nilpotent Novikov superalgebra can be obtained by a finite sequence of double extensions of even (resp. odd) pseudo-Euclidean nilpotent Novikov superalgebras, starting from the trivial superalgebra (see Corollary
5.3.  Corollary
and Corollary
5.6.  Corollary
).

Finally, we prove that any pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra is either a Milnor superalgebra or can be obtained by a sequence of double extensions from a Milnor superalgebra.

Section
6
is devoted to the classification of all pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras of total dimension at most four. The classification is carried out over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

2.
Backgrounds

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero. The group of integers modulo
2
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a superspace defined over
{\mathbb{K}}
. The parity of a homogeneous element
v\in V_{\bar{i}}
is denoted by
|v|:=\bar{i}
. The element
v
is called
even
if
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and
odd
if
v\in V_{\bar{1}}
. The
superdimension
of
V
is
\mathrm{sdim}(V)=a+b\epsilon
, where
\epsilon^{2}=1
, and
a=\mathrm{dim}(V_{\bar{0}})
,
b=\mathrm{dim}(V_{\bar{1}})
. Usually,
\mathrm{sdim}(V)
is shorthanded as
a\mid b
. The total dimension of
V
, which is
a+b
, is denoted by
\mathrm{dim}(V)
.

Throughout the text, all elements are supposed to be homogeneous unless otherwise stated.

A linear map
\varphi:V\rightarrow W
between superspace is called
even
if
\varphi(V_{\bar{i}})\subset W_{\bar{i}}
and
odd
if
\varphi(V_{\bar{i}})\subset W_{\overline{i}+\overline{1}}
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a vector superspace. We denote by
\Pi
the
change of parity functor

\Pi:V\mapsto\Pi(V)
, where
\Pi(V)
is another copy of
V
such that
\Pi(V)_{\bar{0}}:=V_{\bar{1}};~~\Pi(V)_{\bar{1}}:=V_{\bar{0}}
. Elements of
\Pi(V)
shall be denoted by
\Pi(v),\text{ for all }v\in V
. In fact,
|\Pi(v)|=|v|+{\bar{1}}
for every homogeneous
v\in V
.

Let
U
and
V
be two vector superspaces. Given a morphism
G:U\to V
, we define the morphism

G^{\Pi}:\Pi(U)\to\Pi(V)

defined by
G^{\Pi}(\Pi(u))=\Pi(G(u))
. It is straightforward to verify that this construction makes the assignment

U\mapsto\Pi(U)
into a functor satisfying
\Pi^{2}=\mathrm{id}
.

Moreover, given a morphism
G:U\to V
, one can construct morphisms
\Pi(G):U\to\Pi(V),
defined by
\Pi(G)(u)=\Pi(G(u)),
and

G\Pi:\Pi(U)\to V,
defined by
(G\Pi)(\Pi(u))=G(u).

Clearly, we have the factorization
G^{\Pi}=\Pi(G\Pi)
, see

[
31
]
.

Let
V
be a vector superspace. For homogeneous elements
x,y\in V
, we denote by
x^{*},y^{*}\in V^{*}
their duals . For every
x^{*}\otimes y^{*}\in V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
and
u\otimes v\in V\otimes V
, we define the pairing

\langle x^{*}\otimes y^{*},u\otimes v\rangle:=(-1)^{|y||u|}x^{*}(u)\,y^{*}(v).

The symmetric product (graded symmetrisation) is given by

x^{*}\odot y^{*}=x^{*}\otimes y^{*}+(-1)^{|x||y|}\,y^{*}\otimes x^{*}.

2.1.
Bilinear forms on a superspace

Let
V
and
W
be two superspaces. Let
\beta\in\text{Bil}(V,W)
be a homogeneous bilinear form. Recall that the Gram matrix
B=(B_{ij})
associated with
\beta
is given by the following formula:

B_{ij}=(-1)^{|\beta||v_{i}|}\beta(v_{i},w_{j})\text{~~for the basis vectors $v_{i}\in V$ and $w_{j}\in W$.}

This definition allows us to identify a bilinear form of
\mathrm{Bil}(V,W)
with an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(V,W^{*})
.
Consider the
upsetting
of bilinear forms

u:\text{Bil}(V,W)\rightarrow\text{Bil}(W,V)
given by the formula

u(\beta)(w,v)=(-1)^{|v||w|}\beta(v,w)\text{~~for any $v\in V$ and $w\in W$.}

In terms of the Gram matrix
B
of
\beta
, we have

u(B)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R^{t}&(-1)^{|B|}T^{t}\\
(-1)^{|B|}S^{t}&-U^{t}\end{array}\right),\text{ for $B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
T&U\end{array}\right)$}.

Suppose now that
V=W
. Following
[
30
]
, we say that:

(i)

the form

\beta
is
symmetric
if and only if
u(B)=B
;

(ii)

the form
\beta
is
anti-symmetric
if and only if
u(B)=-B
.

(iii)

the form
\beta
is non-degenerate if
u\in V
satisfies
\beta(v,u)=0,
for any
v\in V
, then
u=0
,

(iv)

the form
\beta
is even if
\beta(V_{\bar{0}},V_{\bar{1}})=\beta(V_{\bar{1}},V_{\bar{0}})=\{0\}
,

(v)

the form
\beta
is odd if
\beta(V_{\alpha},V_{\alpha})=\{0\}
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},

From now on, we will denote a bilinear form with
\langle\;,\;\rangle
instead.

A
pseudo-Euclidean
vector superspace
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a vector superspace
V
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form. It is called
Euclidean
if the form
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is definite-positive.

Let
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean vector superspace. Let
f
be a homogeneous endomorphism of
V
of parity
\alpha\in\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
.

(i)

The adjoint of
f
with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
, denoted by
f^{*}
, is the unique homogeneous endomorphism of parity
\alpha
satisfying

\langle f(u),v\rangle=(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f^{*}(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

(ii)

The endomorphism
f
is said to be:

–

Symmetric (or self-adjoint) with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
if
f^{*}=f
, i.e.,

\langle f(u),v\rangle=(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

–

Anti-symmetric (or anti-self-adjoint) with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
if
f^{*}=-f
, i.e.,

\langle f(u),v\rangle=-(-1)^{\alpha|u|}\langle u,f(v)\rangle,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V.

Instead of saying that
f
is symmetric (resp. anti-symmetric) with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
, we adopt the terminology that
f
is
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-symmetric (resp.
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-anti-symmetric).

Lemma
.

Let
(V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a finite-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean vector superspace.

(i)

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is even, then
\dim V_{\bar{1}}
is even.

(ii)

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is odd, then
\dim V_{\bar{0}}=\dim V_{\bar{1}}
. Moreover, the total dimension of
V
is even.

Proof.

(i) The result follows from the fact that the restriction
\langle\;,\;\rangle|_{V_{\bar{1}}}
is
non-degenerate and anti-symmetric. It is well known that, in the non-super setting, such a bilinear form can exist only on vector spaces of even dimension.

(ii) Let
0\neq u\in V_{\bar{1}}
. Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is odd and non-degenerate, there exists
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
such that
\langle u,v\rangle=1
. Set
E:=\operatorname{Span}\{u,v\}
.
We claim
E^{\perp}\cap E=\{0\}
. Indeed, let
w\in E^{\perp}\cap E
.
Put
w=\alpha u+\beta v
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
. We have

0=\langle u,w\rangle=\langle u,\alpha u+\beta v\rangle=\beta,

and similarly,

0=\langle v,w\rangle=\langle v,\alpha u+\beta v\rangle=\alpha.

Thus
w=0
, proving the claim.

Now, let
x\in V
. We can write

x=\bigl(x-\langle x,u\rangle v-\langle x,v\rangle u\bigr)+\bigl(\langle x,u\rangle v+\langle x,v\rangle u\bigr)\in E^{\perp}\oplus E.

Hence,

V=E\oplus E^{\perp},

and therefore

\operatorname{sdim}(V)=\operatorname{sdim}(E^{\perp})+(1|1).

The restriction
\langle\;,\;\rangle|_{E^{\perp}}
is again odd and non-degenerate, so the same argument
can be applied inductively to
E^{\perp}
. Since
V
is finite-dimensional, the result
follows. ∎

2.3.
Novikov superalgebras

A superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
over a field
\mathbb{K}
consists of a superspace
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{1}}

{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha+\beta},\text{ for }\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}

For every

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}

{\mathrm{R}}_{u}

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}(v):=u\bullet v\text{ and }{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}(v):=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,\text{ for all $u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}$}.

Let us define the following normalizers

N_{\ell}({\mathcal{A}},\bullet):=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{A}},\;\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u}=0\right\},\;N_{r}({\mathcal{A}},\bullet):=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{A}},\;\mathrm{R}_{u}^{\bullet}=0\right\},\;\text{and}

N({\mathcal{A}},\bullet):=N_{\ell}({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)\cap N_{r}({\mathcal{A}},\bullet).

We refer to these as the
left normalizer

right normalizer
, and (two-sided)
normalizer

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a non-associative superalgebra. On the underlying vector superspace
{\mathcal{A}}

define the corresponding commutator

[u,v]_{\bullet}:=u\bullet v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,\;\text{ for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}.

The superalgebra

{\mathcal{A}}^{-}

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)

{\mathcal{A}}^{-}

Similarly, on the underlying vector superspace
\mathcal{A}

[u,v]_{\bullet}^{+}:=u\bullet v+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,\;\text{ for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}.

The superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;]_{\bullet}^{+})

{\mathcal{A}}^{+}

A superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is called left-symmetric (resp. right-symmetric) if and only if

(u,v,w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\,(v,u,w),\quad(\text{resp. }(w,u,v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\,(w,v,u)),\text{ for all $u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}$,}

(u,v,w):=(u\bullet v)\bullet w-u\bullet(v\bullet w)
is the associator. It is well-known that such superalgebras are Lie-admissible.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left-symmetric (resp. right-symmetric) superalgebra. Then the left (resp. right) multiplication operators satisfy the identity:

(1)

\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{[u,v]_{\bullet}}=\left[\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{v}\right]\quad(\text{resp. }\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{[u,v]_{\bullet}}=-\left[\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{R}^{\bullet}_{v}\right]),\quad\text{for all $u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

It is obvious that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra if and only if
\left(\mathcal{A},\bullet_{\text{opp }}\right)
is a right-symmetric superalgebra where
u\bullet_{\text{opp }}v:=-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u

and the following identity is satisfied:

(w\bullet u)\bullet v=(-1)^{|u||v|}(w\bullet v)\bullet u,\quad\text{for all $u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a superalgebra. Then

is a Novikov superalgebra if and only if

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{[u,v]_{\bullet}}=\left[\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{u},\mathrm{L}^{\bullet}_{v}\right],\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v}=(-1)^{|u||v|}{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u},\quad\text{for all $u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

According to the relations (

Remark
.

The vanishing of the curvature of
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is equivalent to the fact that

\mathfrak{g}
, endowed with its Levi-Civita product, is a left-symmetric superalgebra.
Thus, a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra can be viewed as a left-symmetric
superalgebra equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form with respect to which
the left multiplications are
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-anti-symmetric.

Indeed, assume that
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a flat Lie superalgebra and let
\bullet

denote its Levi-Civita product. The vanishing of the curvature of

(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
implies that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra.
Moreover, the Levi-Civita product satisfies

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=0,\text{ for all $u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}$,}

which means that the left multiplications are

\langle\;,\;\rangle
-anti-symmetric. Hence
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form with respect to which the left
multiplications are
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-antisymmetric.

Conversely, assume that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
such that

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle+(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=0,\qquad\text{ for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is well known that
\mathfrak{g}^{-}
is a Lie superalgebra with Lie bracket

[u,v]_{\bullet}=u\bullet v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u.

Moreover, the above identity shows that
\bullet
satisfies the defining
property of the Levi-Civita product. By uniqueness,
\bullet
coincides with the Levi-Civita product associated with

(\mathfrak{g}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
. Consequently,
(\mathfrak{g}^{-},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a flat pseudo-Euclidean
Lie superalgebra.

The main goal of this paper is to introduce the notion of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras and to investigate their structures.

The problem we study can be approached in two equivalent ways.

Approach 1.

(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\langle\,,\,\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Lie superalgebra and let

On the other hand, for the Lie superalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{[u,v]}={\mathrm{L}}_{[u,v]}-{\mathrm{R}}_{[u,v]}=-{\mathrm{R}}_{[u,v]}.

Therefore,
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
is
2
-step nilpotent if and only if
{\mathrm{R}}_{[u,v]}=0
,
which is equivalent to
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
being right-symmetric.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.
Then
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent if and only if the associated Lie superalgebra

\mathcal{A}^{-}
is nilpotent.
Moreover,
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent if and only if the left multiplication operator
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}
is nilpotent, for any
u\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent.
Then it is clear that the Lie superalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{-}
is also nilpotent.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{A}^{-}
is nilpotent.
Since
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is a Novikov superalgebra, Eq (
9
) implies that
(u\bullet v)\bullet w=0
for all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}
. It follows that

{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet}=0,\;{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}=0,\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal{A}.

Hence, in order to prove that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent, it is sufficient to show that
the left multiplication operators are nilpotent, that is,

{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{2}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}=0,\quad\text{for all }u_{i}\in\mathcal{A},

for
n
large enough.

Recall that the adjoint operator is given by

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}={\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}.

A direct computation yields

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{v}=({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet})\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet})={\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet},

and

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{v}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{w}={\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{w}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{\bullet}.

By induction, for all
n\geq 1
and for all
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, we obtain

(10)

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{n}}={\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}-{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n-1}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}.

Since
\mathcal{A}^{-}
is nilpotent, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{n_{0}}}=0,\quad\text{for all }u_{i}\in\mathcal{A}.

Using (
10
), we deduce that

{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n_{0}}}^{\bullet}={\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{u_{n_{0}}}^{\bullet}.

It follows, for all
u_{n_{0}+1}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n_{0}}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{u_{n_{0}+1}}^{\bullet}={\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{u_{n_{0}}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{u_{n_{0}+1}}^{\bullet}=0.

From (
10
), we obtain that

(11)

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{n_{0}}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u_{n_{0}+1}}={\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n_{0}+1}}-{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u_{n_{0}+1}}.

Thus, we deduce that

{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{1}}^{\bullet}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u_{n}}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u_{n_{0}+1}}=0,\text{ for all $u_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}$}

which shows that
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent.

Finally, if
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}
is nilpotent for all
u\in\mathcal{A}
, then (
10
) implies that

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
is nilpotent for all
u\in\mathcal{A}
.
By Engel’s theorem, the Lie superalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{-}
is nilpotent, and therefore

(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
is nilpotent as well.
The converse implication is obvious.
∎

We now introduce an important class of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras. In
[
33
, Theorem 1.5]
, Milnor showed that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of a flat Riemannian (or Euclidean) Lie group decomposes as a semidirect product of an abelian ideal
\mathfrak{u}
and an abelian subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
. Furthermore, for any
u\in\mathcal{B}
, the adjoint map
\operatorname{ad}_{u}
is antisymmetric.

With respect to this decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathcal{B}
, the
Levi-Civita product
\bullet
satisfies

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }u\in\mathfrak{u},\\[5.69054pt]
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u},&\text{if }u\in\mathcal{B}.\end{cases}

Here
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}={\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}
, for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
. This is
equivalent to the fact that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{\bot}
, with

\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0,\ \text{for all }u\in\mathfrak{u}.

We enlarge the context and introduce the following definition.

Definition
.

A Milnor (super)algebra is a pseudo-Euclidean (super)algebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
such that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is Lie-admissible superalgebra and there exists a
nondegenerate
two-sided ideal
I
of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
such that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\subset I
and
{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0
, for any
u\in I
.

The following proposition justifies this terminology and demonstrates that Milnor (super)algebras form a subclass of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov (super)algebras.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Milnor superalgebra by means of a graded ideal
I
. Then:

(i)

Both
I
and
I^{\perp}
are trivial subsuperalgebras of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
. Thus
I
is an abelian graded ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
and
I^{\perp}
is an abelian Lie subsuperalgebra.

(ii)

{\mathcal{A}}=I\oplus I^{\perp}
and, for any

,

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}\quad u\in I,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}&\mbox{if}\quad u\in I^{\perp}.\end{cases}

(iii)

The center
Z({\mathcal{A}}^{-})
of
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
satisfies
Z({\mathcal{A}}^{-})=\{u\in{\mathcal{A}},\,{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0\}
.

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.

Proof.

Part (i). It is a consequence of the fact that
{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0
, for any
u\in I
, and the fact that
I^{\perp}\subset({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp}
which implies
{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0
, for any

, by virtue of (
12

Part (ii). The only thing to point out is that

and so, for any
u\in I^{\perp}
,
{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}=0
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}={\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}
.

Part (iii). Let
v=v_{1}+v_{2}\in Z({\mathcal{A}}^{-})
where

v_{1}\in I
and
v_{2}\in I^{\perp}
. Since both
I
and
I^{\perp}
are abelian Lie subsuperalgebras, we deduce that

From

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v_{1}}={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v_{1}}
and
{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}
.

Moreover, since

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}=0
.

Therefore, we obtain

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v}={\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v_{1}}+{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}=0,\qquad{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v}={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v_{1}}+{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{v_{2}}=0.

is pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra is an immediate consequence of
(ii)
and the fact that
[\;,\;]_{\bullet}
is a Lie bracket. Indeed, since

3.2.
Representations of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras

Here, we show, as a consequence of the results obtained so far, that every pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra naturally carries the structure of a left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra. We then introduce the notion of representation of a left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra and define the associated product related to pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras. Furthermore, we establish a characterization of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras in terms of bimodule isomorphisms. More precisely, we show that the existence of a suitable bilinear form is equivalent to the isomorphism between certain naturally associated
{\mathcal{A}}
-bimodules. Motivated by this representation, we construct a new class of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras, analogous to the
T^{*}
-extension for quadratic algebras introduced in
[

.

in

(u+x)\bullet(v+y)=u\bullet_{\mathcal{B}}v+l(u)(y)+r(v)(x)+x\circ y,\;\text{for all $u,v\in\mathcal{B}$ and $x,y\in V$,}

and

\langle\;,\;\rangle=\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}+\langle\;,\;\rangle_{V},

where the product on
V
is defined by

\langle x\circ y,u\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}=\langle y,r(u)(x)\rangle_{V},\text{ for all $u\in\mathcal{B}$ and $x,y\in V$.}

Assume that the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\langle x\circ t,\,y\circ z\rangle_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}}

\displaystyle=-\langle y\circ t,\,x\circ z\rangle_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}},\;\;\langle r(u)(x),\,r(v)(y)\rangle_{V}=-\langle r(u)(y),\,r(v)(x)\rangle_{V},

\displaystyle l(x\circ y)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad(x\circ y)\bullet_{\mathcal{B}}u=0,

for all
x,y,z,t\in V
and
u\in\mathcal{B}
.

Then
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is an even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.

The above construction is analogous to that introduced for even quadratic Lie superalgebras (see, for instance,
[
26
]
). Indeed, in the Lie case, the invariance of the bilinear form is expressed by

\langle[u,v],w\rangle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,[u,w]\rangle.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean left-symmetric superalgebra. Consider the bilinear map
\star:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
defined by

(16)

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\star w\rangle,\quad\text{ for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proposition
.

The product
\star
defined in Eq.
16
satisfies the following identities:

(a)

u\star v=-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star u
,
\text{ for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
,

(b)

(u\bullet v)\star v-u\star(v\star w)=u\bullet(v\star w)-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star(u\bullet w),

\text{ for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}

Moreover, if
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra, then the product
\star
defined in Eq.
3.2.8.  Proposition
satisfies:

(c)

u\star(v\star w)=0
,
\text{ for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
,

(d)

u\bullet(v\star w)=0
,
\text{ for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
,

(e)

(u\bullet v)\star w=-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star(u\bullet w),\text{ for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

Let
u,v,w,z\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(a)

We have

\displaystyle\langle u,v\star w\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=-(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u\bullet w,v\rangle=-(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u,w\star v\rangle.

Thus, the product
\star
is antisymmetric.

(b)

Since
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is left-symmetric, then we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\langle(u\bullet v)\bullet w-(-1)^{|u||v|}(v\bullet u)\bullet w-u\bullet(v\bullet w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet(u\bullet w),z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,v\star(w\star z)\rangle+\langle u,v\bullet(w\star z)\rangle-\langle u,(v\bullet w)\star z\rangle-(-1)^{|v||w|}\langle u,w\star(v\bullet z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,v\star(w\star z)+v\bullet(w\star z)-(v\bullet w)\star z-(-1)^{|v||w|}w\star(v\bullet z)\rangle

Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is nondegenerate, then

v\star(w\star z)+v\bullet(w\star z)=(v\bullet w)\star z+(-1)^{|v||w|}w\star(v\bullet z).

Now assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.

(c)

According to Prosposition
3.1.5.  Proposition
, we have

\displaystyle 0\overset{\eqref{kly0}}{=}\langle(u\bullet v)\bullet w,z\rangle=\langle u\bullet v,w\star z\rangle=\langle u,v\star(w\star z)\rangle.

Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle

v\star(w\star z)=0

(d)

According to Prosposition

\displaystyle 0\overset{\eqref{kly0}}{=}\langle(u\bullet v)\bullet w,z\rangle=\langle u\bullet v,w\star z\rangle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet(w\star z)\rangle.

Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is nondegenerate, then
u\bullet(w\star z)=0
.

(e)

This part follows from (b), (c) and (d).

Remark
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra. As mentioned previously,
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is not necessarily nilpotent; indeed, there exist non-nilpotent examples (see Remark
4.4.5.  Remark
). Consequently, the associated Lie superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},[\;,\;]_{\bullet})
is not necessarily nilpotent.

On the other hand, the Lie superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\star)
is always
2
-step nilpotent. Therefore,
{\mathcal{A}}
carries two Lie superalgebra structures: one induced by the commutator
[\;,\;]_{\bullet}
and the other one is given by
\star
.

These two structures coincide, that is,
\star=[\;,\;]_{\bullet}
, if and only if
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is an anticommutative superalgebra.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\cdot)
be a superalgebra. For a linear map
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathrm{End}(V)
, we define a linear map
\phi^{*}:{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathrm{End}(V^{*})
by

\phi^{*}(u)(f)(x):=-(-1)^{|f|(|\phi|+|u|)}f(\phi(u)(x))\quad\text{ for all }u\in{\mathcal{A}},\;f\in V^{*},\;x\in V.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra, then the pair
\big(-({\mathrm{L}}^{\star})^{*},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})^{*}\big)

defines a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is a Novikov superalgebra. Let
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
. By the Novikov identities (c), (d), and (e) of Proposition
3.2.8.  Proposition
, we obtain

({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet})^{*}(f)=-(-1)^{|f||v|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet})=(-1)^{|f||v|+|f||u|+|u||v|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}.

Using again the left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra identities, this yields

({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet})^{*}=(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}.

Moreover,

({\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet})^{*}(f)=(-1)^{|f|(|u|+|v|)}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\bullet}=0,

by the left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra condition.

Similarly, we have

({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star})^{*}(f)=(-1)^{|f||v|+|f||u|+|u||v|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=0,

by identity
(c)
, and

({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet})^{*}(f)=(-1)^{|f||v|+|f||u|+|u||v|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\bullet}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=0,

by identity
(d)
. Moreover, we have

-({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}\circ({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star})^{*}(f)=-(-1)^{|f||v|+|f||u|+|u||v|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}=(-1)^{|f||v|+|f||u|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\star}=-({\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{\star})^{*}(f),

by identity
(e)
.

Therefore,
\big(-({\mathrm{L}}^{\star})^{*},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})^{*}\big)
defines a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
.

Proposition
.

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}
and
{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}

{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}

\star

16
. Then there exists an isomorphism
\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{*}

\Phi({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}(a))=(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(\Phi(a))\;\text{and}\;\Phi({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}(a))=-(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}(\Phi(a)),\text{ for all $u,a\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{*}

\Phi(a):=\langle a,\cdot\rangle,
for all,

\langle\;,\;\rangle

\Phi

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a Novikov superalgebra, and let
{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}
and
{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}
denote the left and right multiplication operators, respectively. Then there exists a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

\langle\;,\;\rangle:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}

such that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra if and only if there exists an isomorphism

\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{*}

satisfying

\Phi({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}(a))=(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(\Phi(a))\;\text{and}\;\Phi({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}(a))=-(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}(\Phi(a)),

where the product
\star
is antisymmetric.

Proof.

Assume first that
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra. Then the result follows directly from Proposition
3.2.14.  Proposition
.

Conversely, suppose that there exists an isomorphism
\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{*}
such that

\Phi({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}(a))=(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(\Phi(a))\;\text{and}\;\Phi({\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{u}(a))=-(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}(\Phi(a)),\text{ for all $u,a\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

Consider the bilinear form

T:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
defined by
T(u,v):=\Phi(u)(v),

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
\Phi
is an isomorphism,
T
is a non-degenerate bilinear form.

Moreover, for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\displaystyle T(u\bullet v,w)

\displaystyle=\Phi(u\bullet v)(w)=\Phi({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}(v))(w)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u})^{*}(\Phi(v))(w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\Phi(v)({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{u}(w))=-(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u\bullet w)

Thus, we get that

(17)

T(u\bullet v,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u\bullet w).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle T(v\bullet u,w)

\displaystyle=\Phi(v\bullet u)(w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\Phi({\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\bullet}(v))(w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|+|\Phi||u|}({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u})^{*}(\Phi(v))(w)

\displaystyle=\Phi(v)({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}(w))=T(v,u\star w)=-(-1)^{|u||w|}T(v,w\star u)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||w|}T(v\bullet w,u)\overset{\eqref{s1}}{=}(-1)^{(|u|+|v|)|w|}T(w,v\bullet u)

Thus, we obtain

(18)

T(u\bullet v,w)=(-1)^{(|u|+|v|)|w|}T(w,u\bullet v).

Now, we introduce the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of the bilinear form
T
. Define the bilinear forms
T_{s},T_{a}:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{K}
by

T_{s}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T(u,v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u)\Big),\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{a}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T(u,v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u)\Big),

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Clearly,
T=T_{s}+T_{a}
.

Since
T
satisfies (
17
), for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{s}(u\bullet v,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T(u\bullet v,w)+(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}T(w,u\bullet v)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-(-1)^{|u||v|}T(v,u\bullet w)-(-1)^{|w||v|}T(u\bullet w,v)\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{|u||v|}\Big(T(v,u\bullet w)+(-1)^{|v|(|u|+|w|)}T(u\bullet w,v)\Big)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}T_{s}(v,u\bullet w).

It follows that
T_{s}(u\bullet v,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}T_{s}(v,u\bullet w)
.

Moreover, since
T
is satisfies (
18
), for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{a}(u\bullet v,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T(u\bullet v,w)-(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}T(w,u\bullet v)\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{s2}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\Big((-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}T(w,u\bullet v)-T(u\bullet v,w)\Big)

Therefore,

T_{a}(u\bullet v,w)=-T_{a}(u\bullet v,w),\text{
which implies }T_{a}(u\bullet v,w)=0.

We define the two spaces:

N:=\{u\in{\mathcal{A}}\mid T_{a}(u,v)=0\ \text{for all }v\in{\mathcal{A}}\}\text{ and }W:=\{u\in{\mathcal{A}}\mid T_{s}(u,v)=0\ \text{for all }v\in{\mathcal{A}}\}.

By the previous argument, we have
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}={\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq N
, and consequently
N
is a two-sided ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
T
is non-degenerate, we have
N\cap W=\{0\}
. Hence, there exists a vector subsuperspace
V\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
such that
{\mathcal{A}}=W\oplus V,
and clearly
N\subseteq V
. Moreover, since
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\subseteq N\subseteq V
, it follows that
V
is a two-sided ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
and that
T_{s}|_{V\times V}
is non-degenerate.

Consider a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

H:W\times W\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}.
We define a bilinear form
\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{K}
by

\widetilde{H}|_{W\times W}=H,
and
\widetilde{H}(V,{\mathcal{A}})=\widetilde{H}({\mathcal{A}},V)=0.

Clearly,
\widetilde{H}
is symmetric. Now, define the bilinear form

\langle\;,\;\rangle:=T_{s}+\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}.

Since
T_{s}
and
\widetilde{H}
are symmetric, it follows that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is symmetric. We will show that it is actually non-degenerate.

Let
u=w+v\in{\mathcal{A}}
, with
w\in W
and
v\in V
, and assume that

\langle u,z\rangle=0,
for all
z\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Then

\widetilde{H}(w,z)+T_{s}(v,z)=0.

If we choose
z\in W
, then
T_{s}(v,z)=0
, hence
\widetilde{H}(w,z)=0
, and thus
H(w,z)=0
. Since
H
is non-degenerate, it follows that
w=0
.

If we choose
z\in V
, then
\widetilde{H}(w,z)=0
, and therefore
T_{s}(v,z)=0
. Since
T_{s}|_{V\times V}
is non-degenerate, it follows that
v=0
.

Thus
u=0
, and consequently
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is non-degenerate.

Finally, since
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\subseteq V
,
\widetilde{H}(V,{\mathcal{A}})=\widetilde{H}({\mathcal{A}},V)=0
, and
T_{s}
satisfie (
17
), we obtain, for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
,

\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=T_{s}(u\bullet v,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}T_{s}(v,u\bullet w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle.

Therefore,
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.
∎

{\mathrm{L}}

{\mathrm{L}}

\langle(u+f)\ast(v+g),w+h\rangle_{c}=\langle u+f,(v+g)\rhd(w+h)\rangle_{c},

(v+g)\rhd(w+h)=v\star w-g\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{\bullet}+(-1)^{|v||w|}h\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet}.\quad\text{ for all }u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}},\,f,g,h\in{\mathcal{A}}^{*}

3.2.8.  Proposition

\big(-({\mathrm{L}}^{\star})^{*},({\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet})^{*}\big)

{\mathcal{A}}

{\mathcal{A}}^{*}

\displaystyle\langle(u+f)\ast(v+g),w+h\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u\bullet v+({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(g)-(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star})^{*}(f),\,w+h\right\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}g(u\bullet w)+f(v\star w)+(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}h(u\bullet v)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\Big(g(u\bullet w)+(-1)^{(|u|+|w|)|v|}f(w\star v)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)+|u||v|}h(u\bullet v)\Big)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\Big(g(u\bullet w)+(-1)^{(|u|+|w|)|v|}f\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{w}(v)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)+|u||v|}h\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\Big(g(u\bullet w)-(-1)^{(|u|+|w|)|v|+|u||w|}({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{w})^{*}(f)(v)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(h)(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\left\langle v+g,u\bullet w+({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(g)-(-1)^{|u||w|}({\mathrm{L}}_{w}^{\star})^{*}(f),\,w+h\right\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\left\langle v+g,(u+g)\ast(w+h)\right\rangle_{c}.

Therefore,
({\mathcal{A}},\ast,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{c})
is an even pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle u+f,(v+g)\rhd(w+h)\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle(u+f)\ast(v+g),w+h\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u\bullet v+({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\bullet})^{*}(g)-(-1)^{|u||v|}({\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{\star})^{*}(f),\,w+h\right\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|u|(|v|+|w|)}g\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{w}(u)+f(v\star w)

\displaystyle\;+(-1)^{|w|(|u|+|v|)+|u||v|}h\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet}(u)

\displaystyle=\langle u+f,v\star w-g\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{\bullet}+(-1)^{|v||w|}h\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet}\rangle_{c}.

Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{c}
is nondegenerate, then

(v+g)\rhd(w+h)=v\star w-g\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{\bullet}+(-1)^{|v||w|}h\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{v}^{\bullet}.

Thus, which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Double extensions of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of
double extension
for pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras. It is inspired by the concept of double extension, originally introduced by Medina and Revoy in
[
32
]
for quadratic Lie algebras.

4.1.
Central extension of left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebras

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra, let
\h:=\K e
be a one-dimensional vector superspace, and let
\mu:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\K
be a homogeneous bilinear map of a given parity.
We define a new product
\diamond
on the vector space

\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}:={\mathcal{A}}\oplus\K e

as follows:

(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e):=u\bullet v+\mu(u,v)e,\qquad\text{ for all }u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},\;\alpha,\beta\in\K.

Thus,
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}},\diamond)
is a left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra, if and only if the following condition holds

(19)

\mu(u\bullet v,w)=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu(u,v\bullet w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\mu(v,u\bullet w),\quad\text{for all $u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}$.}

In this case,
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}},\diamond)
is called the
central extension
of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
by means of
\mu
.

4.2.
Almost semi-direct product of left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebras

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra. Let
\mathcal{V}:=\K d
be a one-dimensional vector superspace, let
D:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}},\;\xi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}},
be homogeneous endomorphisms of parity
|d|
, and let
b_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}
and a scalar
\lambda\in\K
.
On the vector superspace

\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}:=\K d\oplus{\mathcal{A}},

we define a new product
\bar{\bullet}
as follows: (here
\lambda=0
if
d
is odd)

d\bar{\bullet}d:=\lambda d+b_{0},\qquad d\bar{\bullet}u:=D(u),\qquad u\bar{\bullet}d:=\xi(u),\qquad u\bar{\bullet}v:=u\bullet v,

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Then
(\overline{{\mathcal{A}}},\bar{\bullet})
is a left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra if and only if the following conditions are satisfied (for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
):

(20)

\displaystyle\xi(u\bullet v)=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\xi(u)\bullet v=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle D(u)\bullet v=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle u\bullet\xi(v)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet\xi(u),

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{b_{0}}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle D\circ\xi(u)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|u||d|}u\bullet b_{0},

\displaystyle D(u\bullet v)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|u||d|}u\bullet D(v),

\displaystyle\lambda=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\xi(b_{0})=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle(1-(-1)^{|d|})D(b_{0})=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\xi\circ D=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle(1-(-1)^{|d|})D^{2}=

\displaystyle 0.

Definition
.

If
(D,\xi,b_{0})
satisfies the compatibility conditions (

then the left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra
(\overline{{\mathcal{A}}},\bar{\bullet})
is called the

almost semi-direct product
of the left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)

by the one-dimensional vector superspace
\K d
by means of

In this case, the 5-tuple

-superalgebra

4.4.
Double extensions of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of
double extension
of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras.

4.4.1.
Even double extensions of even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras.

Let
(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\mathcal{B}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathcal{B}})
be an even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra.
Let
V=\K d
be a one-dimensional vector superspace and
V^{*}=\K e
denote its dual.

In order to introduce the process of double extension by
V
, we consider two even linear maps
\xi,D:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B},
such that
D
is
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}
-antisymmetric, and an even element
b_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{\bar{0}}

\xi
to

\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi}:\mathcal{B}\oplus V^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\oplus V^{*},

as follows:

(21)

\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{D}(u+\alpha e):=D(u)-\langle b_{0},u\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\,e,\quad\widetilde{\xi}(u+\alpha e):=\xi(u),\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{B}$ and $\alpha\in\K$.}\end{array}

Lemma
.

The superspace
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{B}\oplus V^{*}
equipped with the product

(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e):=u\bullet v-\langle\xi(u),v\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\,e,\qquad\text{for all }\,u,v\in\mathcal{B},\;\alpha,\beta\in\K,

is a left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra if and only if

(22)

\xi(u\bullet v)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad u\bullet\xi(v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet\xi(u),\quad\text{for all $u,v\in\mathcal{B}$.}

Proof.

(\mathcal{H},\diamond)
is left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra if and only if the bilinear map

\mu(u,v):=-\langle\xi(u),v\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

satisfies Eq. (
19
), which is equivalent to Eq. (
22
).
∎

Lemma
.

(V,\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{B}\oplus V^{*},\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi},b_{0})
is a context of almost semi-direct product
of left-Leibniz
{\mathrm{L}}
-superalgebra if and only if, for all
u,v\in\mathcal{B}
, the following conditions hold:

(23)

\begin{cases}\xi(u\bullet v)=\xi(u)\bullet v=D(u)\bullet v=0,\\
u\bullet\xi(v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet\xi(u),\quad D(u\bullet v)=u\bullet D(v),\\[5.69054pt]
D\circ\xi={\mathrm{R}}_{b_{0}}^{\bullet},\quad\xi^{2}=\xi\circ D={\mathrm{L}}^{\bullet}_{b_{0}}=0,\quad b_{0}\in\ker{\xi},\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{B}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.
A straightforward computation shows that the following equivalences hold:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}\big((u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e)\big)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\xi(u\bullet v)=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\xi(u)\bullet v=0\;\text{ and $\xi^{2}=0$},

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow D(u)\bullet v=0,\quad\text{$\xi\circ D=0.$}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{2}(u+\alpha e)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\text{$\xi^{2}=0,$}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}\circ\widetilde{D}(u+\alpha e)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\text{ and }\text{$\xi\circ D=0.$}

Moreover, the equation

X\diamond\widetilde{\xi}(Y)=(-1)^{|X||Y|}Y\diamond\widetilde{\xi}(X),\quad\text{ for all }X,Y\in\h

is equivalent to

u\bullet\xi(v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet\xi(u).

Further

\widetilde{D}((u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e))=(u+\alpha e)\diamond\widetilde{D}(v+\beta e)

is equivalent to

D(u\bullet v)=u\bullet D(v),\quad\mbox{and}\quad D\circ\xi={\mathrm{R}}^{\bullet}_{b_{0}}.

In addition, the equation
\widetilde{D}\circ\widetilde{\xi}(X)=(-1)^{|X|}X\bullet b_{0}
for all
X\in\h

is equivalent to

D\circ\xi(u)=(-1)^{|u|}u\bullet b_{0}.

Finally, the last identities are equivalent to the conditions

{\mathrm{L}}_{b_{0}}^{\bullet}=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi(b_{0})=0.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is degenerate.
Then
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

(D,\xi,b_{0})

an odd double extension of an even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra
(\mathcal{B},\bullet_{\mathcal{B}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathcal{B}})

(D,\xi,b_{0},c_{0})

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\cap({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\bot}\neq 0

The case where

{\mathrm{L}}

\mu

19

6.
Classification of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras of low
dimensions

In this section, we provide a classification of pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras of dimension at most
4. The classification follows as a consequence of Theorems
3.1.11.  Theorem
,
5.2.  Theorem
, and
5.5.  Theorem
.

In order to complete the classification, we analyze separately the cases determined by the degeneracy of the product space
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
.

In the case of Novikov algebras, a classification up to dimension 3 was offered in
[
14
]
in the case where
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate. Here we complete the classification in the case where
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is degenerate. We cover the super case as well.

The classification of
nilpotent
Novikov algebras up to dimension 4 has been carried out in
[
25
]
using different techniques.

6.1.
The case where
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate

We have proved in Theorem
3.1.11.  Theorem
that any pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
for which
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate is of Milnor type. We have also shown in Corollary
3.1.12.  Corollary
that such
{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
is nilpotent if and only if
\bullet
is trivial.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebra of dimension
at most
3
such that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate. We have:

(1)

If
\dim{\mathcal{A}}=2

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isomorphic to

({\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{1},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, the even pseudo-Euclidean trivial superalgebra.

(2)

If
\dim{\mathcal{A}}=3
, then
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isomorphic to one of the following even pseudo-Euclidean Novikov superalgebras:

(i)

({\mathcal{A}}^{3}_{1},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, the trivial superalgebra, i.e,.
u\bullet v=0
, for
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}^{3}_{1}.

(ii)

({\mathcal{A}}^{3}_{2},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, where the product and the form are given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},f_{1}\}
by

f_{1}\bullet e_{1}=\lambda e_{2},\qquad f_{1}\bullet e_{2}=-\epsilon\lambda e_{1},\quad\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\epsilon}=e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+\epsilon e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+f_{1}^{*}\otimes f_{1}^{*},

\lambda\neq 0
and
\epsilon=\mp 1
.

(iii)

({\mathcal{A}}^{3}_{3},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, where the product and the form are given in the basis
\{f_{1}\mid e_{1},e_{2}\}
by

f_{1}\bullet e_{1}=\lambda e_{1},\qquad f_{1}\bullet e_{2}=-\lambda e_{2},\quad\langle\;,\;\rangle=-e_{1}^{*}\odot e_{2}^{*}+f_{1}^{*}\otimes f_{1}^{*}.

(iv)

({\mathcal{A}}^{3}_{4},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
, where the product and the form are given in the basis
\{f_{1}\mid e_{1},e_{2}\}
by

f_{1}\bullet e_{1}=\lambda e_{2},\qquad f_{1}\bullet e_{2}=e_{1},\quad\quad\langle\;,\;\rangle=-e_{1}^{*}\odot e_{2}^{*}+f_{1}^{*}\otimes f_{1}^{*}.

where
\lambda\neq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
is non-degenerate. Then

{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\oplus({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp}.

First, observe
that
{\mathcal{A}}\neq{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
by
Proposition
3.1.5.  Proposition
. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp}

Eq.
12
, the Novikov superalgebra

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
is trivial.

The case where
\dim({\mathcal{A}})=2
.
Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\neq 0
, in this case

\dim({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})=\dim(({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp})=1.

By Lemma
2.2.  Lemma
, both subspaces
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}
and
({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\bot}
are even.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}=\K e_{1}
and
({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp}=\K f_{1}
such that
\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle=\epsilon
where
\epsilon=\mp 1
.
By Theorem
3.1.10.  Proposition
, there exists
\alpha\in\K
such that

f_{1}\bullet e_{1}=\alpha e_{1}
.
However,

\epsilon\alpha=\langle f_{1}\bullet e_{1},e_{1}\rangle=-\langle e_{1},f_{1}\bullet e_{1}\rangle=-\epsilon\alpha=0,

which contradicts
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\neq\{0\}

The case where
\dim({\mathcal{A}})=3

We distinguish two subcases: either

\dim({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})=2

\dim(({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp})=1,

\dim({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})=1

Subcase 1:
Let us assume that
\dim({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})=2

\dim(({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp})=1

By Lemma
2.2.  Lemma
, either
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}

({\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}})^{\perp}

(i) Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}\bullet{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2}\mid 0\}
