Prognostic value of tertiary lymphoid structures in locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy: a retrospective study
Authors: Tiantian Xue, Yuhao Wu, Qihan Zi, Tiancui Zi, Shengguai Gao, Jie Mao, Yizhou Peng, Jiaping Chen, Xi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis retrospective study analyzed 73 patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, aiming to investigate the impact of the multiple heterogeneity of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) on treatment efficacy and prognosis. The results demonstrated that intratumoral TLS presence correlated with superior radiological and pathological responses, whereas peritumoral TLS expression was associated with poorer outcomes. Regarding spatial heterogeneity, the subgroup characterized by high intratumoral TLS abundance combined with absent peritumoral TLS exhibited the most favorable progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Furthermore, a higher degree of TLS maturation was significantly associated with improved survival benefits. Multivariate analysis confirmed that body mass index (BMI) served as an independent protective factor for both PFS and OS, while pre-treatment TNM stage emerged as an independent risk factor for PFS. These findings suggest that TLS holds promise as a potential predictive biomarker for the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04448-4.Keywords: Tertiary lymphoid structures, Esophageal cancer, Squamous cell carcinoma, Retrospective study

Introduction

Epidemiology and current treatment landscape of esophageal cance

Esophageal cancer (EC) is a common malignancy worldwide, characterized by its high incidence and poor prognosis. China is a high-incidence region for this disease. According to statistics from the National Cancer Center of China, there were approximately 220,000 new cases of esophageal cancer in China in 2022, ranking as the fourth most common newly diagnosed gastrointestinal cancer. The disease and its complications led to about 180,000 deaths in the same year, making it the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality among gastrointestinal malignancies. In China, esophageal cancer is predominantly squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), accounting for over 90% of all cases [1, 2].

Although curative-intent surgical resection remains the primary therapeutic option for esophageal cancer at present [3], However, due to its insidious onset and the lack of obvious early symptoms and signs, the vast majority of patients are already diagnosed with advanced or locally advanced EC. For this patient population, the efficacy of surgery alone is limited by a high post-operative recurrence rate and a significant incidence of distant metastasis. In recent years, the development of neoadjuvant treatment modalities has brought new hope to these patients. Notably, neoadjuvant immuno-chemotherapy (NICT) has demonstrated promising results in several clinical trials [6, 7, 8, 9, 10],This approach has significantly improved the R0 resection rate and has gained recognition in guidelines issued by both the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) [4, 5], Therefore, neoadjuvant immuno-chemotherapy (NICT) has become the preferred treatment for locally advanced esophageal cancer (EC).

Tertiary Lymphoid Structures (TLS).

The concept of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) was first proposed by Aloisi and colleagues. They are defined as organized aggregates of lymphocytes that form around PNAd⁺ high endothelial venules in chronic inflammatory sites, and are also referred to as ectopic/tertiary lymphoid structures or tertiary lymphoid organs [11]. Subsequently, in 2014, Dieu-Nosjean and colleagues further refined the histological definition of TLS [12]. TLS are ectopic, lymph node-like structures that develop in non-lymphoid organs under the persistent stimulation of chronic inflammation. They represent a process of lymphoid neogenesis, which occurs when non-lymphoid tissues are chronically exposed to inflammatory signals mediated by chemokines and cytokines [13].

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a critical factor in tumorigenesis, progression, and metastasis. Research by Peske JD et al. indicates that TLS serve as an important biomarker reflecting the state of the TME [14], Within the TME, TLS represent a state of organized immune infiltration and provide a critical site for both cellular and humoral immunity [29]. TLS can be classified into two categories: immature TLS (iTLS) and mature TLS (mTLS) (Fig. 1) [15, 16]. Serving as the core of antitumor immunity within the tumor microenvironment, TLS are capable of forming spontaneously or being induced by various factors such as chemokines, cancer vaccines, chemotherapeutic agents, or immunotherapeutic agents [17–28].

To date, the biological characteristics of TLS have been extensively investigated in various solid malignancies [16, 32, 33, 34] (such as in hepatocellular carcinoma, soft tissue sarcoma, gallbladder cancer, and breast cancer), yet their specific biological effects are not uniformly favorable [30, 31]. The prognostic impact of TLS on patients primarily depends on three factors: TLS abundance, the spatial relationship between TLS and the tumor, and TLS maturity. These factors can be summarized as multiple dimensions of heterogeneity, namely spatial, morphological, and compositional heterogeneity [43, 44]. However, existing research has predominantly focused on the role of TLS in prognostic assessment or in the context of surgery alone. Systematic investigation into their dynamic changes and predictive value in locally advanced ESCC patients undergoing the emerging treatment modality of neoadjuvant immuno-chemotherapy (NICT) remains lacking. This study aims to address this gap by comprehensively analyzing the impact of multiple dimensions of TLS heterogeneity on treatment response and prognosis following NICT.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

In this retrospective study, we collected clinicopathological data from patients who underwent radical surgical resection in the Department of Thoracic Surgery at Yunnan Cancer Hospital between January 2022 and December 2024. The inclusion criteria were: pathologically confirmed esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and receipt of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy prior to surgery. Exclusion criteria comprised: history of other malignancies, lost to follow-up, or incomplete clinical data. Ultimately, 73 patients were enrolled in this study, with a median follow-up duration of 303 days.

Clinical information

Clinical data collected included: patient demographics (sex, age, ethnicity, occupation); etiological/risk factors (comorbidities, smoking history, alcohol consumption history); details of immunotherapy administration; pre- and post-treatment TNM stage; tumor regression grade (TRG); lymph node response based on imaging; tumor response based on imaging; overall survival (OS); progression-free survival (PFS); intratumoral TLS expression (TLS-T); peritumoral TLS expression (TLS-P); and maturity of intratumoral TLS (TLS-T maturity).

Treatment response evaluation

Pathological Evaluation: For each patient, pathological and TLS assessment was performed on 8 to 12 consecutive tissue sections. All pathological slides were independently reviewed by two experienced pathologists. Pathological treatment response was evaluated using the Mandard tumor regression grade (TRG) system (Supplementary Table 1). The description of TRG 2 (rare residual cancer cells) in the Mandard-system was supplemented with the Ryan TRG 1 definition, which specifies residual cancer as single cells or small clusters. The detailed grading criteria are as follows: TRG 1, no residual cancer cells, replaced by extensive fibrosis; TRG 2, residual cancer as single cells or small clusters within fibrosis; TRG 3, fibrosis predominates over residual cancer; TRG 4, residual cancer predominates over fibrosis; TRG 5, no evidence of tumor regression. For analytical purposes, TRG grades 1–3 were categorized as pathological responders (TRG-e, excellent response), and grades 4–5 as poor responders (TRG-b, poor response).

Radiological Evaluation: For each patient, imaging data were retrieved from the Hospital Information System (HIS). The images were independently reviewed and evaluated by two experienced radiologists. Radiological treatment response was assessed using a radiological tumor regression grading system, which was developed based on the WHO criteria and RECIST 1.1 guidelines (Supplementary Table 2) [35]The evaluation criteria are as follows: Complete Response (CR): Disappearance of all target lesions (measurable lesions) and non-target lesions (lesions not suitable for precise measurement but evaluable for follow-up); reduction in the short axis of all pathological lymph nodes to < 10 mm; normalization of tumor markers; and no new lesions; Partial Response (PR): At least a 30% decrease in the sum of diameters (longest diameter) of target lesions, taking as reference the baseline sum; with no progression of non-target lesions; and no new lesions; Stable Disease (SD): Tumor shrinkage or growth insufficient to meet the criteria for either PR or Progressive Disease, falling between the two; with no progression of non-target lesions; and no new lesions; Progressive Disease (PD): At least a 20% increase in the sum of diameters of target lesions, taking as reference the smallest sum recorded since treatment started, with an absolute increase of at least 5 mm; or unequivocal progression of non-target lesions; or the appearance of new lesions.

Survival Endpoints: Overall Survival (OS): Defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the date of death from any cause. Progression-Free Survival (PFS): Defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the date of documented tumor progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Follow-up Protocol: All surviving patients were followed for a minimum of 12 months. For patients who died during the study period, follow-up was censored at the date of death.

Evaluation of TLS heterogeneity: composition, morphology, and spatial distribution

TLS Assessment: TLS in each patient were evaluated through systematic examination of histological slides. On hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained sections, TLS were identified as well-circumscribed, rounded aggregates of lymphocytes. For each patient, TLS identified across all examined sections were assessed, counted, and the results were averaged. Spatial heterogeneity was evaluated according to the TLS scoring criteria (Supplementary Table 3) [36].

Spatial Heterogeneity Scoring: Spatial heterogeneity was assessed by scoring intratumoral TLS (TLS-T) and peritumoral TLS (TLS-P) separately according to the following criteria: Score 0: No TLS present in the intratumoral or peritumoral region; Score 1: Presence of 1–2 TLS in the intratumoral region OR distribution of peritumoral TLS covering < 50% of the assessed area; Score 2: Presence of at least 3 TLS in the intratumoral region without confluence, OR peritumoral TLS distribution covering > 50% of the assessed area; Score 3: Presence of at least 3 confluent TLS in the intratumoral region, OR abundant peritumoral TLS.

Compositional Heterogeneity Definition: For compositional heterogeneity, samples with a TLS-T or TLS-P score of 0 were classified as TLS-Tn or TLS-Pn (negative), respectively. Samples with a score ≥ 1 were classified as TLS-Tp or TLS-Pp (positive), respectively. For analytical purposes, a TLS score ≥ 2 was defined as high TLS expression (TLS-h), and a score ≤ 1 was defined as low TLS expression (TLS-l) [34].

Morphological Heterogeneity: Morphological heterogeneity was evaluated by assessing the maturity of intratumoral TLS (TLS-T) according to a TLS maturity grading system based on hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained sections (Supplementary Table 4) [37], Specifically: Early TLS (E): formation of a dense lymphocyte aggregate; Primary follicle-like TLS (PLF): presence of a follicular dendritic cell (FDC) network but without a germinal center (GC) reaction indicated by a B-cell cluster; Secondary follicle-like TLS (SFL): an active GC reaction present on the basis of PLF features.

Statistical methods

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables are presented as mean±standard deviation, while categorical variables are expressed as frequency and percentage. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared with the log-rank test. Univariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate potential prognostic factors for OS and PFS, including age, sex, and TLS biological features. Variables identified as significant (P < 0.05) in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate Cox regression model to identify independent prognostic factors. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Analysis of TLS Heterogeneity and Treatment Response.

This study performed correlation analyses and multivariate logistic regression to evaluate the association between TLS compositional and spatial heterogeneity and the clinicopathological response profiles of patients.

Radiographic Response:

The presence of intratumoral TLS (TLS-Tp) was indicative of superior radiographic tumor regression. Multivariate logistic regression confirmed that TLS-Tp correlated with enhanced tumor shrinkage (Odds Ratio [OR] = 0.197, 95% Confidence Interval [CI]: 0.056–0.698, P = 0.012). Conversely, peritumoral TLS (TLS-Pp) was associated with poorer nodal response (OR = 3.500, 95% CI: 1.019–12.017, P = 0.047). Neither intratumoral nor peritumoral TLS expression significantly impacted the downstaging of TNM stage (P > 0.050).

Analysis of the association between TLS heterogeneity and survival outcomes

Following the preliminary characterization of the distinct roles of TLS-T and TLS-P in treatment response, this study extended its analysis to survival outcomes, specifically investigating their impact on Progression-Free Survival (PFS) and Overall Survival (OS).

Kaplan-Meier survival curves with Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) testing demonstrated significant prognostic associations: Intratumoral TLS (TLS-T): Expression status was significantly associated with both PFS (χ² 18.962, P < 0.001; Fig. 2) and OS (χ² 24.435, P < 0.001). Peritumoral TLS (TLS-P): Expression status also showed statistically significant associations with PFS (χ² 5.654, P 0.017; Fig. 3) and OS (χ² 8.233, P 0.004).

Analysis of combined TLS heterogeneity groups in relation to treatment response

Based on the aforementioned findings that TLS-T and TLS-P exert distinct influences on treatment response and survival, patients were stratified into four groups according to the abundance of TLS-T and the expression status of TLS-P: TlPn, ThPn, TlPp, and ThPp. Subsequent chi-square test analysis revealed statistically significant differences among these four groups in terms of tumor stage change (χ² 20.676, P 0.002), radiological tumor response (χ² 20.313, P 0.002), radiological lymph node response (χ²=19.182, P 0.004), and TRG score (χ² 32.565, P < 0.001).

Based on the Kaplan-Meier method with Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) testing, significant prognostic differences were observed among the TP stratification groups for both PFS (χ² 30.061, P < 0.001) and OS (χ² 32.540, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4).

Progression-Free Survival (PFS):

Pairwise comparisons revealed that the “ThPn” group exhibited significantly superior PFS rates compared to the “TlPn,” “TlPp,” and “ThPp” groups (all P < 0.05). Additionally, the “ThPp” group showed significantly higher PFS rates than the “TlPp” group (P < 0.050). Cox regression analysis further quantified these risks: compared to the “ThPn” group, the hazard ratio (HR) for disease progression was approximately 3-fold higher in the “ThPp” group (HR 3.017, 95% CI: 1.050 ~ 8.666, P 0.040) and approximately 3.8-fold higher in the “TlPn” group (HR 3.831, 95% CI: 1.717 ~ 8.544, P 0.001). No statistically significant differences in PFS were found among the remaining pairwise comparisons (P > 0.050).

Overall Survival (OS):

Similarly, pairwise analysis showed that the “ThPn” group had significantly higher OS rates than the “TlPn,” “TlPp,” and “ThPp” groups (all P < 0.05). Compared to the “ThPn” reference group, the risk of death in the “TlPn” group was increased by approximately 5.2-fold (HR = 5.205, 95% CI: 2.177–12.447, P < 0.001). No significant differences in OS were detected among the other groups (P > 0.050).

Impact of TLS morphological heterogeneity on survival outcomes

Previous research by Ding GY et al. on TLS morphological heterogeneity has demonstrated that TLS with higher maturity differ from those with lower maturity in the context of cancer therapy. In line with this, the present study applied the TLS maturity grading system proposed by these investigators (Supplementary Table 3) [30], Based on the TLS maturity grading system, TLS morphological heterogeneity was categorized into the following groups: secondary follicle-like TLS (SFL)-predominant, primary follicle-like TLS (PLF)-predominant, early TLS (E)-predominant, mixed TLS (MIX), and TLS negative (Tn).

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with Log-rank testing was performed to evaluate the prognostic value of TLS-T maturity. Significant differences were observed for both PFS (χ² 22.690, P < 0.001) and OS (χ² 26.869, P < 0.001) across the TLS-T maturity subtypes (Fig. 5).

Progression-Free Survival (PFS):

Pairwise comparisons demonstrated that the Secondary Lymphoid Follicle (SLF) subtype derived the most pronounced PFS benefit. Compared to the reference group (presumably immature TLS), the hazard ratios (HR) were: SLF (HR 0.056, 95% CI: 0.007 ~ 0.430, P 0.006), Primary Follicle (PF) (HR 0.247, 95% CI: 0.110 ~ 0.555, P 0.001), Early-stage (E) (HR 0.275, 95% CI: 0.118 ~ 0.641, P 0.003), and Mixed (MIX) (HR 0.056, 95% CI: 0.007 ~ 0.430, P 0.006). A clear trend was observed wherein higher TLS-T maturity correlated with superior PFS outcomes.

Overall Survival (OS):

Similarly, the SLF subtype exhibited the most significant OS benefit. The hazard ratios for OS were: SLF (HR 0.054, 95% CI: 0.007 ~ 0.428, P 0.006), PF (HR 0.179, 95% CI: 0.075 ~ 0.426, P < 0.001), E (HR 0.210, 95% CI: 0.084–0.526, P 0.001), and MIX (HR 0.271, 95% CI: 0.122 ~ 0.601, P 0.001). Consistent with the PFS findings, a positive correlation between TLS-T maturity and OS survival was evident.

Discussion

Our findings indicate that TLS expression shows no significant association with patient demographics. Specifically, factors such as gender, age, ethnicity, and history of underlying conditions did not influence TLS expression. Consequently, propensity score matching to adjust for these baseline characteristics was not required in this study. Interestingly, in our study, males constituted the overwhelming majority. We attribute this phenomenon to the fact that Yunnan Province, as a multi-ethnic region, sees many ethnic groups frequently consuming alcohol to celebrate festivals, where alcohol abuse is predominantly observed among males.

Currently, immunohistochemistry (IHC) or immunofluorescence (IF) staining is considered the gold standard for studying tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), as it allows detailed assessment of lymphocyte subsets (e.g., CD20⁺ B cells, CD3⁺ T cells) and follicular dendritic cells (CD21⁺/CD23⁺) within TLS. However, as a retrospective clinical study, this research aims to conduct preliminary exploration and validation using routinely archived hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained slides. This approach offers higher accessibility and generalizability in translational clinical research, facilitating subsequent validation in larger or multicenter cohorts. Moreover, in some key clinical studies [30, 31, 34, 37, 39], similarly, H&E staining has been used to identify and grade the characteristics of TLS, with studies indicating an association between TLS and prognosis. The success of these studies demonstrates that H&E staining can be reliable under certain conditions. Furthermore, this study does not focus on the cellular composition of TLS but rather investigates the preliminary clinical significance of spatial and morphological heterogeneity in TLS. H&E staining is sufficient to assess basic TLS features, such as spatial heterogeneity, compositional heterogeneity, and fundamental maturity levels.

In the analysis of treatment outcomes, TLS composition and spatial heterogeneity were grouped into two categories: TLS-T and TLS-P. When analyzing the TLS-T group, this study found that compared to TLS-Tn, the TLS-Tp group achieved better tumor radiological response. Conversely, TLS-Pp showed inferior lymph node efficacy compared to TLS-Pn. These findings confirm that TLS-Tp acts as a protective factor in treatment response, with its expression associated with therapeutic remission, whereas TLS-Pp serves as a risk factor linked to poor treatment outcomes. However, neither group exhibited differences in tumor TNM staging changes. This result is consistent with previous studies on TLS in various malignancies, including gallbladder cancer and breast cancer [16, 32, 33, 34].

However, the role of TLS in peritumoral areas (TLS-P) appears contrary to that in intratumoral regions (TLS-T), which we have discussed and attributed to potential factors such as structural immaturity, dysfunction of TLS, and infiltration of suppressive immune cells. Studies by Cabrita R et al. [47]and Helmink BA et al. [48]in melanoma have shown that although TLS is generally associated with favorable prognosis, the B-cell receptor repertoires and functional states of TLS vary by location. Based on this, we postulate that the B-cell receptor repertoire and functional status in peritumoral TLS may differ from those in intratumoral TLS, and a dysfunctional B-cell repertoire may fail to mount effective anti-tumor responses. Additionally, Ding GY et al. [49]reported in hepatocellular carcinoma that immunosuppressive cells—such as regulatory T cells (Tregs), myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), or exhausted T cells—infiltrate peritumoral TLS, thereby suppressing the immunostimulatory function of TLS and turning it into an “immune-privileged” or “functionally impaired” niche.

Subsequently, this study investigated the morphological heterogeneity of TLS and classified them into three groups—SLF, PLF, and E—based on the criterion that the proportion of highest maturity was ≥ 50%. Additionally, a MIX group (with each maturity proportion < 50%) and a Tn group were included for analysis of maturity and survival outcomes. In the K-M survival curves, the SLF and Tn groups were positioned at the top and bottom ends, respectively, while the PLF, MIX, and E groups ranked sequentially downward. Inter-group comparisons revealed that the SLF group achieved the greatest benefit in both PFS and OS, and overall, higher TLS maturity was associated with better PFS and OS outcomes. The correlation between the maturity of TLS and PFS and OS has been demonstrated in the study by Dong Liyuan et al. [16] and the conclusions of this study are consistent with it.

Cox regression analysis was performed on factors affecting survival. Univariate Cox regression analysis of PFS revealed that BMI (P = 0.038 < 0.050), SLF (P = 0.023 < 0.050), PLF (P = 0.032 < 0.050), TLS-T morphological heterogeneity (P = 0.002 < 0.050), TLS-T (P = 0.004 < 0.050), and TLS-P (P = 0.028 < 0.050) had statistically significant impacts on PFS outcomes. However, after multivariate Cox regression analysis of these factors, BMI and pre-immunotherapy TNM staging demonstrated significant statistical effects on PFS outcomes. BMI (P = 0.023, HR = 0.361; 95% CI 0.150 ~ 0.869) emerged as an independent protective factor influencing PFS prognosis, while pre-immunotherapy TNM staging (P = 0.023, HR = 2.419; 95% CI 1.127 ~ 5.190) was identified as an independent risk factor for PFS prognosis. Univariate regression analysis of OS showed that BMI (P = 0.016 < 0.050), SLF (P = 0.043 < 0.050), TLS-T morphological heterogeneity (P < 0.050), TLS-T (P < 0.050), and TLS-P (P = 0.036 < 0.050) had statistically significant impacts on OS outcomes. Upon inclusion in multivariate Cox regression analysis, BMI (P = 0.022, HR = 0.443; 95% CI 0.220 ~ 0.890) demonstrated significant statistical effects on OS outcomes, confirming BMI as an independent protective factor influencing patient OS. In this retrospective analysis, the study primarily focused on TLS pathological features and clinical indicators. Although BMI was identified as an independent protective factor for OS, the study did not systematically collect and analyze other nutritional markers (e.g., albumin) or systemic inflammatory markers (e.g., neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio NLR, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio PLR, systemic immune-inflammation index SII). These markers may correlate with BMI and collectively influence patient prognosis. However, BMI itself is a comprehensive nutritional status indicator. In chronic wasting diseases such as esophageal cancer, higher BMI typically reflects better nutritional reserves and less cancer cachexia, consistent with previous research findings [45, 46]. Therefore, although it was not possible to adjust for all potential confounding factors, BMI, as an easily accessible clinical indicator, still has significant clinical implications for independent prognostic value. In subsequent studies, comprehensive nutritional and inflammatory profiles can be included to construct appropriate scoring systems, which can more accurately assess the impact of the patient’s overall condition on the efficacy and prognosis of immunotherapy. As for the TNM staging before immunotherapy, its effect on PFS is already a well-recognized fact.

The multiple dimensions of TLS heterogeneity represent a significant factor influencing tumor treatment efficacy, a finding that has been demonstrated across various malignancies such as breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and melanoma. In the present study, we specifically explored the roles of TLS spatial, morphological, and compositional heterogeneity in patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Our results indicate that distinct patterns across these three dimensions differentially affect both therapeutic response and survival outcomes.However, it is important to recognize that these three dimensions of TLS heterogeneity are not static and immutable throughout the treatment course. Each dimension may undergo therapy-induced changes as treatment progresses, a dynamic phenomenon that has been validated across multiple treatment modalities [16, 38–42]. Therefore, performing targeted assessments of TLS heterogeneity at multiple longitudinal time points throughout a patient’s treatment course could inform and potentially tailor therapeutic strategies, which may lead to improved clinical outcomes. Furthermore, in our investigation of TLS morphological heterogeneity, we observed descending survival gradients for both PFS and OS as TLS maturity decreased. This pattern suggests that higher TLS maturity grades may confer enhanced antitumor immunity.

However, this study also has certain limitations, including the inherent bias of retrospective studies, a relatively small sample size, being a single-center study, short follow-up time, and the lack of continuous observation of patients’ TLS throughout the overall treatment process. Although this study examined each patient’s pathological slices and used a quantity-based approach to approximate the whole, there may still be insufficient observation. In addition, regarding TLS observation, this study only used H&E-stained slices, which may lead to differences in TLS observation. Future multi-center prospective studies are expected; multiple time points for TLS detection should be adopted; and under H&E staining conditions, the study should be further improved by combining various immunofluorescence staining techniques.

Conclusions

The biological characteristic of TLS, namely triple heterogeneity, affects the therapeutic effect of tumors in different ways, and during the treatment process it can show a dual impact. The results of this study suggest that TLS may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker for local advanced ECSS after NICT treatment, but further verification is still needed through large-sample, multicenter, and long-term follow-up studies.

When a certain treatment has a positive effect on the triple heterogeneity of TLS, it can enhance the body’s anti-tumor treatment efficacy; conversely, when it has a negative effect on TLS triple heterogeneity, it can reduce the body’s anti-tumor treatment efficacy. Therefore, its role as a biomarker is not limited to describing the body’s anti-tumor effects, but can also serve as a marker for evaluating the effectiveness of treatment methods. Imagine that after using TLS to evaluate a treatment, when selecting a combined therapy plan for patients, the TLS evaluation score can be used to visually choose between different combinations of plans. This not only shortens the observation period for the efficacy of combined therapy plans but also provides a basis for selecting different treatment options for patients.
