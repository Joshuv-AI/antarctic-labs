 Crosscutting Machine Learning at the Frontier of Particle Physics
 Jason Brodsky
                                          24-ERD-002

Project Overview
 The objective of this project was to enhance data analysis for particle physics experiments
using cutting-edge techniques in machine learning (ML).
 ML is thoroughly established as an important tool in the design and analysis of particle
physics experiments. However, the bulk of research into ML applications for particle physics
focuses on classifiers of particle interactions or regressors to measure particle properties such
as energy. In this project, we explored techniques that could expand the role ML plays in
particle physics experiments. We targeted techniques that would be broadly applicable across a
range of particle physics experiments, with an eye towards the even broader sphere of
applications in other mission areas at LLNL.
 Towards that end, we developed two techniques, semi-supervised denoising and
simulation-based inference, and demonstrated their applicability on two topics at the frontier
of particle physics: the neutrinoless double beta decay detector nEXO and beyond-the-
Standard-Model Higgs physics at the Large Hadron Collider.

Mission Impact
 This project advanced the field of ML for particle physics with a valuable publication
describing our denoising methods (Parker et al. 2026). This work was also presented at the
premier conference on ML for neutrino physics as (Parker 2026). A publication on our
simulation-based inference work for Higgs physics is forthcoming.
 This project intentionally chose techniques to develop that could see wide application in
mission areas at LLNL. Techniques developed in this LDRD contributed to six proposals to the
2026 Genesis Mission Phase I call, two of which were funded. In addition, we expect these
techniques to be useful across the national security mission space of the lab and have initiated
collaboration building in those application areas.
