Glucodependence in Cardiorenometabolic Disease: Naming the Problem
Authors: Salvador Lopez Giacoman
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

I read with great interest the review by Kugathasan et al.1 “Novel Therapeutic Strategies for Patients With CKD and Diabetes Mellitus”. The synthesis of glucagon-like peptide-1–based therapies, dual and triple incretin modulators, and other emerging agents for the cardiorenometabolic syndrome is highly relevant for nephrologists.

I would like to propose that your article could be strengthened by explicitly integrating a behavioral framework centered on compulsive eating behaviors related to ultraprocessed, sugar-rich foods.2,3 In clinical practice, many patients with chronic kidney disease and diabetes struggle to sustain dietary change not only because of lack of knowledge or motivation, but also because they face an addiction-like pattern of intake that is reinforced by the current food environment and family habits.4

We propose the following concept of glucodependence as a descriptive clinical construct to capture this pattern: compulsive, recurrent consumption of sugar-rich and ultraprocessed foods, characterized by intense cravings, loss of control, and persistence despite adverse metabolic and renal consequences. Newer glucagon-like peptide-1–based and dual/triple incretin therapies appear to improve outcomes partly by attenuating this compulsive drive, suggesting that neurobehavioral mechanisms are central to the cardiorenometabolic syndrome. However, pharmacologic therapy alone is unlikely to produce sustained change unless patients recognize that they are facing an addiction-like behavior and understand how their environment perpetuates it. As in other substance-use disorders, the first therapeutic step is acknowledging the problem.2

Integrating structured behavioral modification, problem recognition, and environmental changes alongside pharmacologic innovation may be essential to address the structural problem of ultraprocessed food dominance. Including this dimension would strengthen the clinical relevance of your article for nephrologists and improve long-term adherence in patients with chronic kidney disease and diabetes.
