MPINeuralODE: Multiple-Initial-Condition Physics-Informed Neural ODEs for Globally Consistent Dynamical System Learning
Lake Yang, Antonio Malpica-Morales, Frank Ioannis Papadakis Wood, Serafim Kalliadasis

Abstract
Neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs) often fit training trajectories while generalizing poorly to unseen initial conditions and long horizons. We propose MPINeuralODE, which combines a soft physics-informed residual with a Multiple-Initial-Condition (MIC) multiple-shooting curriculum whose ingredients are structurally complementary: the physics term anchors the vector-field magnitude on the support that MIC enlarges. We evaluate along three axes — out-of-sample error, long-horizon stability, and Hamiltonian drift — that together expose whether the learned dynamics have recovered the true vector field. On Lotka–Volterra, MPINeuralODE achieves the lowest out-of-sample and long-horizon MSE among data-driven methods (a 26 % 26\% reduction over the baseline Neural ODE) while essentially matching the PINN ablation on Hamiltonian drift. An anonymous reproduction artifact is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/MPINeuralODE/ .

Introduction

Dynamical systems describe how state variables evolve under internal
interactions and external forcing, from predator–prey ecology

(
18
)
to chemical kinetics and geophysical
fluid flow. A persistent tension in data-driven modeling is the
tradeoff between mechanistic interpretability and empirical
flexibility. Classical mechanistic models

(
25
)
provide identifiable rate constants but
depend on assumptions — well-mixed populations, linear functional
responses, absence of unmodeled dynamics — that are routinely
violated in practice. Purely data-driven approaches

(
28
;
4
;
19
)
offer
representational capacity but sacrifice interpretability and often
generalize poorly outside the training distribution.

Neural ODEs
(
6
)
parameterize the instantaneous vector
field with a neural network and obtain trajectories by numerical
integration. The formulation handles irregularly-sampled data

(
24
)
, produces smooth solver-controlled
trajectories, and supports adjoint training. Yet our experiments
confirm earlier observations

(
16
;
29
)
that
pure Neural ODEs can learn vector fields valid only near training
trajectories.
Figure

1
illustrates this: on the
Lotka–Volterra system, the baseline Neural ODE fits training
trajectories well, but on held-out initial conditions it produces
artificial spirals where the true dynamics admit conservative closed
orbits.

(b)

:

Accurate in-sample fit
(a)
coexists with structurally
broken out-of-sample behavior
(b)
: the learned vector field
is valid only in the training corridor and produces qualitatively
wrong dynamics elsewhere. The closed-orbit topology of the true
system is replaced by artificial spirals.

This paper investigates two complementary remedies and their
combination. Soft
physics-informed regularization

(
22
;
23
)
steers the learned vector
field toward physically plausible behavior at sampled collocation
points.
Multiple-Initial-Condition multiple-shooting

(
3
;
26
)
broadens phase-space
coverage and enforces continuity of the learned flow across
segments. A central methodological observation is that a single
scalar metric — such as validation MSE — does not reveal whether
a learned model has recovered the true dynamics or merely fit
observed trajectories. We evaluate every method on three
complementary axes:

1.

Out-of-sample (OOS) prediction error
— accuracy on
held-out initial conditions in the training distribution.

2.

Long-horizon stability
— accumulated error over
multiple oscillation periods, exposing compounding error.

3.

Drift in the conserved Hamiltonian

H(x,y)
—
a trajectory-independent measure of whether the learned
dynamics respect the geometric structure of phase space.

A model that performs well on all three has captured something close
to the true vector field; a model that excels only on OOS error may
still drift over long horizons or dissipate invariants.

Contributions.

(1) We propose MPINeuralODE, a Neural ODE framework whose physics
residual and MIC continuity penalty are mutually compensating on the
visited support of phase space rather than a weighted sum of
independent objectives.
(2) We argue that single-scalar trajectory error is insufficient for
evaluating learned dynamical systems and propose a three-axis report
that exposes failure modes a scalar MSE hides.
(3) On Lotka–Volterra, MPINeuralODE achieves the lowest OOS and
long-horizon error among purely data-driven methods while
essentially matching the PINN ablation on Hamiltonian drift. No
axis is worse than any single ingredient alone. In the typical
practitioner regime where exact equations are unavailable, this
makes it the most practical surrogate among the methods we
evaluated.
(4) We release MPINeuralODE as an installable Python package shipping
ready-to-use
LotkaVolterra
,
Lorenz63
, and

FitzHughNagumo
subclasses.

Related Work

Neural ODEs and variants.
Neural ODEs
(
6
)

parameterize a continuous-time vector field with a neural network.
Latent ODEs
(
24
)
, Augmented Neural ODEs

(
8
)
, and Neural Controlled Differential

(

address architectural limitations

Physics-informed losses.
Physics-Informed Neural Networks

(

23

enforce soft constraints

(

The direct multiple-shooting method
originated in numerical optimal control
(
3
)
to
overcome the phase-mismatch pathology of single-shooting on
long-horizon oscillatory systems.
26
first
applied this idea to neural differential equations, showing it can
rescue the “flattened-out” trajectories single-shooting produces
on oscillatory time series. Our MIC mechanism is a multiple-shooting
curriculum in that lineage, augmented with a freshly-sampled
initial-condition distribution each epoch and combined with a
PINN-style residual that these works do not address.

Structure-preserving networks.
Hamiltonian Neural Networks

(
11
)
, related Hamiltonian-system
identification
(
2
)
, and symplectic networks

(
13
)
build conservation laws into the architecture
itself; this approach is orthogonal to and potentially combinable
with ours.

Mechanistic-neural hybrids.
Universal Differential
Equations
(
21
)
embed a mechanistic model with a
neural residual, achieving strong performance when mechanistic
knowledge is complete; we use this construction as an
oracle
reference
. Data-driven discovery methods

(
4
;
5
)
excel when the true
model lies in a known function library but struggle when the
functional form is unknown — the setting MPINeuralODE targets.

Method

3.1
Lotka–Volterra Benchmark

The Lotka–Volterra (LV) system

(
17
;
27
)

\frac{dx}{dt}=\alpha x-\beta xy,\quad\frac{dy}{dt}=\delta xy-\gamma y

(1)

provides a mathematically tractable benchmark with three useful
properties. First, it exhibits neutrally-stable closed orbits with
no inherent error dissipation: the coexistence equilibrium at

(\gamma/\delta,\,\alpha/\beta)
has purely imaginary eigenvalues,
indicating a center
(
25
)
. Any artificial
damping or drift therefore accumulates rather than decays. Second,
families of orbits at different amplitudes share the same phase
plane, requiring the learner to generalize across orbits. Third,
the system admits the conserved quantity
(
18
)

H(x,y)=\delta\,x-\gamma\ln x+\beta\,y-\alpha\ln y,

(2)

constant along true trajectories. Drift in
H(t)
along a learned
trajectory is therefore a trajectory-independent diagnostic of how
well the model respects the geometric structure of phase space —
any deviation from a constant is unambiguously a model error and
does not depend on which initial condition we picked. We adopt

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=(1.5,1.0,3.0,1.0)
, giving
period
T\approx 5.13
and interior equilibrium
(3.0,1.5)
.

Data generation.
Ground-truth trajectories are integrated
with adaptive Dormand–Prince
(
7
)
at

\textrm{rtol}=\textrm{atol}=10^{-8}
over
t\in[0,30]
with

\Delta t=0.1
, yielding
301
time points per trajectory (
\sim 5.85
cycles). Initial conditions are drawn from a mixed
distribution: half uniform on
[0.1,10.0]^{2}
(typical regime) and
half log-uniform on
[10^{-3},10.0]^{2}
(edge regime near the
extinction axes). This mixed scheme avoids pathological behavior
near coordinate axes.

3.3
Neural ODE Baseline

The Neural ODE
(
6
)
parameterizes the vector field
as
d\mathbf{z}/dt=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})
and obtains trajectories
by solving the corresponding initial value problem. Training
minimizes trajectory MSE

\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{z}_{\text{pred}}(t_{i})-\mathbf{z}_{\text{true}}(t_{i})\|_{2}^{2}

(3)

via the adjoint method
(
20
;
6
)

with Adam optimization
(
15
)
. The vector-field
network has three tanh hidden layers of
128
units each (the
Phase-1 winner) with Xavier-normal initialization

(
9
)
. Integration uses adaptive
Dormand–Prince
(
7
;
12
)
; gradient
norms are clipped at
10
to prevent pathological spikes from the
adjoint pass through the solver
(
10
)
. A schematic of how this baseline
extends to the full MPINeuralODE training step is shown in

Figure

3
.

3.4
Intermediate Extensions: PINN and MIC

We evaluate each ingredient of MPINeuralODE in isolation under the
Phase-1 configuration of
Section

3.2
, so observed differences
isolate the algorithmic contribution.

LV_PINN.

Augments the data loss with a soft physics
term that penalizes deviation of the learned vector field from the
known
f_{\text{LV}}
, evaluated symmetrically on states the model
visits and corresponding ground-truth states. Because the residual
is only
applied
where samples exist, its effect is
concentrated in whatever corridor of phase space the integrated
rollouts actually traverse during training. Mechanistically, the
residual directly shapes
f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})
to approximate the
true
f_{\text{LV}}(\mathbf{z})
pointwise, so orbits inherit the
physical structure of the true system — closed orbits, correct
period, conservation of
H
— wherever the trajectory visits.
Outside that visited corridor,
f_{\theta}
remains free to drift.

LV_MIC.

Draws a larger batch of
m>n
initial
conditions per epoch from the mixed sampler of
Section

3.1
,
then splits each trajectory into
K=4
segments and applies the
direct multiple-shooting method
(
3
)
along the
time axis. Segment
k>0
starts from the detached predicted
endpoint of segment
k-1
; a continuity penalty pulls the predicted
endpoint of segment
k-1
toward the
true
state at the next
segment’s boundary, which is the soft-penalty form of the
multiple-shooting continuity constraint advocated by

26
for neural differential equations.
Mechanistically, the broader IC sampler exposes the model to many
orbit families and the continuity penalty enforces that learned
trajectories behave like an autonomous flow whose segments
concatenate at ground truth. This is what keeps trajectories inside
the positive orthant (the IC sampler covers it densely) and
preserves the closed-orbit
topology
. What MIC alone does

not
provide is an inductive bias on the absolute magnitude
of
f_{\theta}
: orbits can still rotate at the wrong rate, producing
the mild spiraling visible in
Figure

2
(b).

(a)

LV_PINN: drift suppressed in the training corridor, but outer orbits remain under-constrained.

(b)

LV_MIC: orbital topology preserved across both inner and outer trajectories, but vector-field magnitude drifts.

3.5
Universal Differential Equation Oracle

We implement a Universal Differential Equation

(
21
)
as an
oracle reference
. In its
general form the UDE combines a mechanistic backbone with a neural
residual,

d\mathbf{z}/dt=f_{\text{phys}}(\mathbf{z})+f_{\text{NN}}(\mathbf{z}).

In our setting
f_{\text{phys}}
is taken to be
exactly
the
Lotka–Volterra functional form, with only the four scalar rate
constants
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)
to be learned; the
neural residual is dropped because with the correct functional form
supplied it is identically zero. The remaining four-parameter
problem is well-posed under a collocation loss (matching

f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})\approx f_{\text{LV}}(\mathbf{z})
pointwise on
ground-truth states) and recovers the true parameters to
\sim 10^{-6}

relative error.

Parameter-count asymmetry.
LV_Structured therefore has
only
4
learnable parameters, against
\sim 67
k for the neural
methods. This is not a fair-comparison issue: the structured model

contains the true LV mechanism (the underlying differential
equations) a priori
, so it carries a substantial learning advantage
over methods that must learn the entire vector field from data. The
purpose of including LV_Structured is to provide a
ceiling

on what is achievable when accurate mechanistic knowledge is fully
available, not a competing data-driven method. A real practitioner
does not know the functional form a priori; what MPINeuralODE claims is
that without that knowledge it can still substantially close the
qualitative gap on the three evaluation axes.

3.6
MPINeuralODE

The full method combines the Neural ODE baseline with both the
physics residual and the MIC multiple-shooting curriculum. The
per-epoch loss is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}+\lambda_{\text{phys}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}+\lambda_{\text{cont}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cont}}+\lambda_{\text{reg}}\,\|\theta\|_{1},

(4)

with the symmetric physics residual

\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathrm{Pred}}\!\|\mathbf{r}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})\|_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathrm{True}}\!\|\mathbf{r}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\!,

(5)

where
\mathbf{r}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})-f_{\text{LV}}(\mathbf{z})
. The two expectations in

Equation

5
use
uniform sampling
:
\mathbf{z}\sim\mathrm{Pred}
samples uniformly from the states

\{\hat{\mathbf{z}}(t_{s})\}
visited along the predicted trajectory
at the training time grid
\{t_{s}\}
, and
\mathbf{z}\sim\mathrm{True}
samples uniformly from the corresponding ground-truth
states
\{\mathbf{z}_{\text{true}}(t_{s})\}
at the same time points.
Together they form the collocation set
\mathcal{C}
used in

Algorithm

1
. Default weights are
\lambda_{\text{phys}}=10
,

\lambda_{\text{cont}}=1
,
\lambda_{\text{reg}}=10^{-5}
.

Why symmetric.
When the learned vector field is initially
far from truth, a one-sided expectation over predicted states alone
would leave large untouched regions of true-trajectory support,
vice versa. The symmetric form anchors
f_{\theta}
both where the
trajectory currently goes and where it should go.

Why PINN and MIC are complementary.

The two ingredients
appear superficially redundant but are mutually compensating.

Physics-only is local
:
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}
is a
Monte-Carlo estimator averaged over the support of the collocation
distribution; with a single sampled IC batch per epoch, that support
is whatever narrow corridor the integrated trajectories happen to
traverse. Outside the corridor the residual is unobservable to the
optimizer and
f_{\theta}
remains free to drift, which is exactly
what
Figure

2
(a) shows.
MIC-only is unanchored
:
the multiple-IC sampler with continuity penalty enlarges the visited
support, but the continuity penalty constrains only piecewise
consistency of the flow, not the absolute magnitude of
f_{\theta}
.

The combination is a closure relation
: MIC enlarges the
support over which
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}
is meaningfully
evaluated, and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}
supplies the absolute
anchor on
f_{\theta}
that continuity does not provide. Each
ingredient’s weakness is the regime where the other is strong.

trading a small amount of trajectory MSE for a vector field closer
to truth. A model that minimized in-sample MSE alone would
necessarily lose this trade.

The structured ceiling is qualitative.
LV_Structured sits
six orders of magnitude below the neural methods on trajectory error
and three orders below on Hamiltonian drift. The qualitative gap
that MPINeuralODE closes is not the trajectory-error gap (which the
oracle’s mechanistic prior makes essentially unbridgeable for purely
data-driven methods) but the
phase-space-structure
gap:
MPINeuralODE recovers closed-orbit topology and bounded Hamiltonian
drift, while LV_NN spirals and dissipates.

4.5
Summary: Why Three Axes Matter

Putting the three axes together:

•

Physics regularization (LV_NN
\to
LV_PINN) reduces OOS MSE
by
\sim 19\%
and Hamiltonian drift by
\sim 13\%
. It is the
largest single-ingredient contribution.

•

MIC multiple-shooting (LV_NN
\to
LV_MIC) reduces OOS MSE
by
\sim 22\%
, slightly better than PINN-only on this axis, but
matches PINN on Hamiltonian drift only approximately. It contributes to the breadth of phase-space coverage rather than direct
constraint on
f_{\theta}
.

•

MPINeuralODE achieves OOS MSE
5.3\%
better than MIC-only and

8.7\%
better than PINN-only, while essentially tying PINN on
Hamiltonian drift. No axis is worse than either ingredient alone.

The methodological takeaway is that
validation MSE alone
understates the value of combining these ingredients
: the largest
qualitative gains — closed-orbit topology, stable long-horizon
integration, bounded conservation drift — are visible only when
all three axes are reported together. For applications in which
the learned model must extrapolate to new initial conditions,
integrate over many cycles, or preserve known invariants, the case
for combining physics-informed regularization with multiple-IC
training is much stronger than any single metric admits.

4.6
Discussion: When to Use Which Method

The three-axis report enables concrete advice on when each method in our comparison is the right choice.

LV_NN
(vanilla Neural ODE) wins only on the narrow
in-sample trajectory-error axis, and only because it concentrates a
fixed capacity on a single typical-regime distribution. Use it when
the deployment distribution is identical to the training
distribution and short-horizon prediction is the only objective —
e.g. smoothing observed trajectories in a controlled experimental
setting. Do not use it when the model will be asked to extrapolate
to new initial conditions or integrate over many oscillation cycles:
the spirals in
Figure

1
(b) are not a freak
training failure but a generic consequence of having no inductive
bias outside the visited corridor.

LV_PINN
is the right choice when the dominant evaluation
metric is conservation-of-invariants and the deployment distribution
sits near the training corridor. Its physics residual is the
single largest contributor to Hamiltonian fidelity on our
benchmark, and at
0.940
relative drift it is statistically
indistinguishable from the combined MPINeuralODE model. The
qualification is that “near the training corridor” is a strong
condition: the residual is a Monte-Carlo estimator and its support
is the same narrow set of states the integrated rollouts visit, so
the conservation guarantee does not extend to far-out-of-distribution
initial conditions.

LV_MIC
is the right choice when the dominant evaluation
metric is broad phase-space coverage and partial physics knowledge
is unavailable. Its multiple-shooting curriculum is what gives the
learned vector field the breadth that the physics residual then
sharpens; with
\lambda_{\text{phys}}=0
the method still recovers
the closed-orbit topology of
Figure

2
(b), trading
some Hamiltonian fidelity for the ability to operate without an
assumed
f_{\text{phys}}
.

MPINeuralODE
is the right choice when the deployment regime is
unknown in advance and the model must extrapolate. It is the only
method in our comparison that places at or near the best on all
three axes simultaneously, with no axis worse than any single
ingredient alone, and is the configuration we recommend as the
default starting point for practitioners.

LV_Structured
can be applied only when accurate
mechanistic equations are available, and only their parameters must
be calibrated. The implicit precondition — that the functional form of

f_{\text{phys}}
is known a priori — is what most
settings violate and motivates our practical solution.

5
Conclusion

We introduced MPINeuralODE, a Neural ODE framework that combines a soft
physics-informed residual with a Multiple-Initial-Condition
multiple-shooting curriculum. The case for combining the two is
structural rather than additive: each ingredient’s blind spot is
exactly the regime the other constrains. On Lotka–Volterra,
MPINeuralODE achieves the lowest OOS and long-horizon error among
purely data-driven methods while essentially matching the PINN
ablation on Hamiltonian drift, and is the most consistently strong
method across the three axes simultaneously. In the typical
practitioner regime — partial mechanistic knowledge, limited data
coverage, and a desire for long-horizon extrapolation — this makes
MPINeuralODE the most practical surrogate among the methods we
evaluated.

Generality beyond Lotka–Volterra.
The construction is
system-agnostic: any dynamical system for which an approximate

f_{\text{phys}}
can be written fits into the same training and
evaluation pipeline, with a conserved quantity required only for
the Hamiltonian drift axis.

The framework applies to systems satisfying four conditions:
(i)
autonomous
or weakly time-driven, so the Neural-ODE
adjoint is well-posed; (ii)
continuous in state
, so the
adaptive integrator does not encounter switching events;
(iii)
smooth enough for adaptive Runge–Kutta integration
,
so stiff regions do not exhaust the time budget; and (iv)

at least partial mechanistic knowledge
expressible as

f_{\text{phys}}
. When no partial physics is available the method
still applies with
\lambda_{\text{phys}}=0
(MIC-only), though
the strongest guarantees require both ingredients. Systems outside
this scope might include those with discrete switching and strongly forced
or controlled dynamics.

Impact Statement

This paper advances scientific machine learning by improving the
ability of Neural ODE models to learn dynamical systems that
generalize across initial conditions, remain stable over long
prediction horizons, and respect known conservation laws. Direct
applications include ecological forecasting, chemical kinetics
modeling, epidemic spread modeling, and any setting where partial
physical knowledge is combined with observational time-series data.
The method encodes physical structure as a soft constraint that the
learner can override when data contradicts the assumed physics; this
is a feature for scientific exploration but a risk in
safety-critical applications, where mis-specified physics could
silently bias forecasts. Practitioners deploying MPINeuralODE in such
settings should report the three evaluation axes proposed here —
out-of-sample accuracy, long-horizon stability, and conservation-law
fidelity — rather than a single trajectory-error scalar, so that
silent geometric failures of the learned vector field are visible
to auditors. There are many other potential societal consequences
of work in this area, none of which we feel must be specifically
highlighted here.

Appendix F
In-Sample Performance and the Regularization Tradeoff

Table

2
in the main text reports that LV_NN
has the lowest in-sample MSE while MPINeuralODE has the highest among
the four neural methods. At first glance this is counterintuitive:
MPINeuralODE is the most accurate method on every other axis. The
pattern reflects a deliberate regularization tradeoff and is not a
sign of a failed training run.

Capacity allocation.
LV_NN trains on
32
initial
conditions per epoch, drawn uniformly from the typical regime

[0.1,10.0]^{2}
. The model concentrates its capacity on this narrow
distribution. LV_MIC and MPINeuralODE train on
128
ICs per epoch
with the mixed sampler that also covers the log-uniform edge regime

[10^{-3},10.0]^{2}
, spreading the same capacity across a broader
phase-space support.

Physics constraint.
LV_PINN and MPINeuralODE are pulled by

\lambda_{\text{phys}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}
toward the true

f_{\text{LV}}
pointwise. When the bare data fit and the physics
constraint disagree on a particular trajectory, the model trades a
small amount of trajectory MSE for a vector field closer to truth.
This is the regularization-vs-fit tradeoff, visible as the in-sample
MSE gap between LV_NN (
1.31
) and LV_PINN (
2.68
).

Long-horizon integration of in-sample ICs.
The
“in-sample” metric integrates each held-out IC over the full

t\in[0,30]
horizon (
\sim 5.85
oscillation cycles). Any
small vector-field error compounds over five cycles, so an
in-sample MSE of
3
corresponds to an RMSE of
\sim 1.7
in
population space. All four neural methods produce visually
plausible trajectories on the in-sample plots; the differences are
most visible in the late-time phase relative to ground truth.

A reader interested in raw in-sample fit on a narrow training
distribution should use LV_NN. A reader who wants a model that
generalizes, stays stable over long horizons, and respects known
conservation laws should use MPINeuralODE. The three-axis report
exposes this choice transparently in a way that a single
trajectory-MSE scalar would not.
