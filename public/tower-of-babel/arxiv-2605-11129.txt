Thin surface subgroups of non-uniform arithmetic lattices in SO + ⁡ ( n , 1 ) \operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)
Sara Edelman-Muñoz, Michael Zshornack

Abstract
. We show that the fundamental groups of all non-compact, arithmetic, hyperbolic, n n -manifolds for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 contain thin surface subgroups. As a consequence of the proof of this theorem we also show that the fundamental groups of the doubles of cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic n n -manifolds embed as GFERF subgroups of SO + ⁡ ( n + 1 , 1 ) \operatorname{SO}^{+}(n+1,1) .



1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 3
and
\Gamma\leqslant\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)
, be a lattice. A
surface subgroup
of
\Gamma
is a subgroup isomorphic to the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
. Understanding the existence and geometric features of surface subgroups of
\Gamma
has been of interest to topologists and geometers for many years. This is, in part, due to the relationship between such subgroups and incompressible surfaces in the ambient hyperbolic manifold. When such a subgroup exists, it is always infinite-index in
\Gamma
and can often be made Zariski-dense in
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)
. When
\Gamma
is arithmetic, such a subgroup is said to be
thin
in the sense of
[
24
]
.

Thin subgroups of arithmetic groups have attracted independent interest in recent years within number theory and combinatorics as these subgroups share a number of useful properties with the arithmetic group they live in. For instance, such subgroups satisfy forms of
superstrong approximation
: they surject onto almost all congruence quotients of the lattice and their induced Cayley graphs form expander families (see
[
13
]
). Despite the rich emerging theory behind the properties of thin groups, it is not particularly well understood how such examples arise. Though thin
free
subgroups are abundant in many contexts, (c.f.,
[
12
]
), methods from hyperbolic geometry have proven especially useful in constructing examples of varying isomorphism type. In
[
5
]
, the authors draw attention to the general lack of non-free examples and raise the question of which isomorphism types of groups can arise as a thin subgroup of an arithmetic group.

Due to the interest in constructing thin groups of non-free isomorphism type and the relationship between surface subgroups of lattices and incompressible surfaces in locally symmetric spaces, we focus on constructing Zariski-dense surface subgroups of fundamental groups of hyperbolic manifolds. In dimension
3
, such examples are known: all lattices in
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
contain thin surface subgroups. For uniform lattices, this follows from
[
17
]
while for non-uniform lattices, it follows from
[
10
]
. In higher dimensions, the existence of thin surface subgroups is known for uniform lattices in
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)
for odd
n
by
[
15
]
and for even
n
by recent work in
[
18
]
. What remains open is the case of non-uniform lattices when
n>3
. Presently, there is only example of a commensurability class of non-uniform lattices in each dimension known to contain a thin surface subgroup
[
11
]
. Notably, these lattices are all arithmetic. In this paper, we extend these results by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-uniform, arithmetic lattice in
\textnormal{SO}^{+}(n,1)
, with
n\geq 4
. Then
\Gamma
has a thin surface subgroup.

The proof of Theorem
1
is an induction on the dimension
n
, beginning with
n=4
. We first find a subgroup of
\Gamma\leqslant\operatorname{SO}^{+}(4,1)
isomorphic to the fundamental group of a cusped, arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-orbifold. Because this
3
-orbifold virtually fibers, it has a finite cover,
M
, with an embedded punctured subsurface
\Sigma
. We define the
folded double
of
M
,
FM
, a graph of spaces whose fundamental group contains the fundamental group of the double of
\Sigma
,
D\Sigma
. We then describe how
FM
maps into the
4
-orbifold
\mathbb{H}^{4}/\Gamma
and show that the induced map
\pi_{1}(FM)\to\Gamma
is faithful and its image Zariski-dense. Since
\pi_{1}(D\Sigma)
is embedded in
\pi_{1}(FM)
, the result is a thin surface subgroup of
\Gamma
. Finally, we generalize to higher dimensions.

In the interest of the questions in
[
5
]
on constructing
any
non-free examples of thin subgroups of
\Gamma
as above, we note that the proof additionally produces thin subgroups of
\Gamma
isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(FM)
.

As a further application of the methods of Theorem
1
, we also prove a subgroup separability result about the groups considered. Recall that a discrete group of isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is said to be
GFERF
if it is subgroup separable on its geometrically finite subgroups. We use the folded double construction of Theorem
1
and results of
[
6
]
to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic
n
-orbifold and let
DM
be the double of
M
over its cusps. Then
\pi_{1}(DM)
embeds discretely into
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n+1,1)
and its image is GFERF.

Note that the property of a group being GFERF is weaker than being
LERF
, i.e., being subgroup separable on all finitely generated subgroups. Nonetheless, there has been interest in both properties because of their use in lifting immersed incompressible submanifolds to embedded ones in a finite cover. More intrinsic versions of this theorem, not reliant on embeddings into
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)
are also discussed at the end of §
5
.

In the course of the proof of Theorem
2
, we also show that LERF is too restrictive a property for these groups. Though the fundamental groups of hyperbolic
3
-manifolds are LERF by
[
2
,
27
]
, the fundamental groups of higher dimensional arithmetic hyperbolic manifolds are not by
[
26
,
7
]
. Since, also by
[
26
]
, the fundamental groups of doubles of non-compact, arithmetic, hyperbolic
3
-manifolds over their cusps are not LERF, showing these groups are GFERF is significant. The starting point for Theorem
2
is the work of Bergeron–Haglund–Wise in
[
6
]
showing that fundamental groups of arithmetic hyperbolic manifolds of simplest type are GFERF. From there, we generalize the methods of
[
20
]
, where it was shown that the double of the figure-
8
knot complement over its cusps is GFERF, extending this construction to the doubles of all non-compact arithmetic hyperbolic
n
-manifolds.

2.
Non-uniform Arithmetic Subgroups of
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)

We first discuss arithmetic groups defined by quadratic forms to motivate how to find subgroups isomorphic to fundamental groups of cusped
3
-manifolds. Recall that if
X
is a non-uniform hyperbolic
n
-orbifold, its fundamental group,
\Gamma<\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n,1)

and admits a non-compact fundamental domain of finite-volume. If, in addition,

is arithmetic, then

\operatorname{SO}^{+}(q,\mathbb{Z})

*Proposition 6.4.2). Recall that
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(q,\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{g\,|\,g\in\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z}),\ g^{T}A_{q}g=A_{q}\}

Specializing to the case where

, the starting point of our induction, we leverage the fact that we have explicit representatives for quadratic forms in each commensurability class of non-uniform, arithmetic lattices. First, we fix some notation.

Definition 1

For integers

From

[

where

Lemma 3

Proof.

We will show that for each projective equivalence class there is some

3.
Doubles of Fibered 3-Manifolds in 4-Manifolds and Orbifolds

The representation we constructed in Section

5.
GFERF Subgroups

We say that a group of isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
GFERF
, or
geometrically finite locally extended residually finite
if it is subgroup separable on all its geometrically finite subgroups. This is a slightly weaker condition than being separable on all finitely generated subgroups, or being LERF. In this section, we prove Theorem
2
, restated here.

Theorem 12
.

Let
M
be a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic
n
-orbifold and let
DM
be the double of
M
over its cusps. Then
\pi_{1}(DM)
embeds discretely into
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(n+1,1)
and its image is GFERF.

Proof.

Subgroup separability is a property that can be passed easily to subgroups. That is to say that if we have groups
G,H,K
such that
H<K<G
, if
G
is
H
-subgroup separable then
K
is
H
-subgroup separable. This fact follows from the definition of subgroup separability [See
[
1
]
for details]. Additionally, if
G,H
, and
K
act by isometries on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the choice of supergroup that contains
H
has no effect on its fundamental domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Hence, if
H
is geometrically finite as a subgroup of
K
then it is geometrically finite as a subgroup of
G
. Therefore, subgroups of GFERF groups are themselves GFERF.

The fundamental groups of cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic, orbifolds are GFERF
[
6
]
. Thus, if there is a faithful representation of
\pi_{1}(DM)
in
\pi_{1}(X)
, where

is a cusped arithmetic hyperbolic
(n+1)
-orbifold then
\pi_{1}(DM)
is GFERF. Therefore, the fundamental groups of the doubles of the fibered 3-manifolds that we considered in Section

are GFERF. We now extend this result to the doubles of all cusped arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds and orbifolds, as well as higher dimensional manifolds and orbifolds.

We proceed by using the folded double, as in Section

. Though we initially defined

for finite-volume, cusped, hyperbolic 3-manifolds, the construction pictured in Figure

. So any faithful representation of

Appendix A
Computations of Hasse-Minkowski Invariants

In this appendix we compute the Hasse-Minkowski invariants of all diagonal quadratic forms with signature
(4,1)
and coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. The definition of Hasse-Minkowski invariants relies on the Hilbert Symbol:

Definition 5
.

The
Hilbert Symbol
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of two non-zero elements
a
and
b
is a function
(a,b)_{p}
that characterizes the quadratic form
q=\langle a,b,-1\rangle
. It is defined:

(a,b)_{p}=\begin{cases}1&q\textrm{\ is isotropic}\\
-1&q\textrm{\ is anisotropic}\end{cases}

Definition 6
.

Let
q=\langle a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle
be a non-degenerate, diagonal quadratic over the local field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The
Hasse-Minkowski invariant

c_{p}(q)
is the product of the Hilbert symbols of the coefficients. It can be computed with the formula

c_{p}(q)=\prod_{i<j}(a_{i},a_{j})_{p}

Note that as the name suggests Hasse-Minkowski invariants are invariant within projective equivalence classes of quadratic forms, so we only need to compute these values for representative elements of each equivalence class. Hence, we can restrict to the family of forms from
[
23
]
used in Section
2
.

For the computations in this section, we find it convenient to use the following formula for Hilbert symbols
[
25
]
. Let
a=p^{\alpha}*u
,
b=p^{\beta}*v
, and let
\epsilon(u)\equiv\frac{1}{2}(u-1)\mod 2
and
\omega(u)\equiv\frac{1}{8}(u^{2}-1)\mod 2
. Then

(2)

(a,b)_{p}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\epsilon(u)\epsilon(v)+\alpha\omega(v)+\beta\omega(u)}&p=2\\
(-1)^{\alpha\beta\epsilon(p)}\cdot\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)^{\beta}\cdot\left(\frac{v}{p}\right)^{\alpha}&p\neq 2\\
\end{cases}

where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\cdot}\right)
is the Legendre symbol. We will also use the following properties
[
25
]
:

(1)

(a,1)_{p}=1

(2)

(a,-a)_{p}=1

(3)

(a,b)_{p}=(b,a)_{p}

(4)

(ab,c)_{p}=(a,c)_{p}(b,c)_{p}
.

By definition, if
S\equiv 1\mod 4
then
c_{p}(q)=(-1,aS)_{p}(aS,a)_{p}(-1,a)_{p}
. From the properties listed above, we get
c_{p}(q)=(S,-a)_{p}(-1,a)_{p}
. Similarly, when
S\equiv-1\mod 4
we know that
c_{p}(q)=(aS,-a)_{p}=(S,-a)_{p}
. Immediately this gives us the following Hasse-Minkowski invariants:

c_{p}(q)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\tfrac{-a-1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{S-1}{2}}&p=2\\
(S,-a)_{p}&p=a,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv 1\mod 4\\
(-1)\cdot(S,-a)_{p}&p=a,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv-1\mod 4\\
(S,-a)_{p}&p|S\\
1&else\\
\end{cases}

Then the only value left to compute is
(S,-a)_{p}
. Applying Equation
2
we see that when
p
divides
S
, this is
\left(\frac{-a}{p}\right)
, which is
-1
by assumption. When
p=a
, we have
(S,-a)_{a}=\left(\frac{S}{a}\right)
. We wish to turn this into a product of
\left(\frac{-a}{p_{i}}\right)
for
p_{i}
dividing
S
, so we will use the following properties of the Legendre symbol
[
14
]
:

(1)

Definition:
\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)=(-1)^{\tfrac{p-1}{2}}

(2)

Multiplicativity:
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\left(\frac{b}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{ab}{p}\right)

(3)

Quadratic reciprocity:
\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=(-1)^{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{b-1}{2}}}
for
a
and
b
distinct odd primes

Beginning with quadratic reciprocity, we see that:

\begin{array}[]{*{3}{rcl}l}\left(\frac{p_{i}}{a}\right)\left(\frac{a}{p_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{p_{i}}\right)&=&(-1)^{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{b-1}{2}}}\left(\frac{-1}{p_{i}}\right)\\
\left(\frac{p_{i}}{a}\right)\left(\frac{-a}{p_{i}}\right)&=&(-1)^{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}}}(-1)^{\frac{p_{i}-1}{2}}\\
\left(\frac{p_{i}}{a}\right)&=&(-1)^{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}}+\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}+1}\\
\left(\frac{S}{a}\right)&=&\prod_{p_{i}|S}(-1)^{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}}+\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}+1}\\
\left(\frac{S}{a}\right)&=&(-1)^{\sum_{p_{i}|S}({\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}}+\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}+1)}\\
\end{array}

We want to compute the parity of the sum above. Thus, our computation relies on
a\mod 4
and
p_{i}\mod 4
. We can use our formulation of
S
to determine these values. Note that when
p_{i}\equiv 1\mod 4
the term
{{\tfrac{a-1}{2}}\cdot{\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}}+\tfrac{p_{i}-1}{2}+1}
is odd. Similarly, when
p_{i}\equiv-1\equiv a\mod 4
this term is odd. However, when
p_{i}\equiv-1\equiv-a\mod 4
it is even. Now, let
S_{0}
be the set of
p_{i}
such that
p_{i}\equiv 1\mod 4
and
S_{1}
be the set of
p_{i}
such that
p_{i}\equiv-1\mod 4
. The parity of the sum is exactly the parity of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}\cdot\frac{a-1}{2}
.

If
S\equiv 1\mod 4
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}
is even. So the parity of the sum in question is exactly the parity of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}
. Furthermore, by our initial assumptions,
a\equiv(-1)^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}}\equiv(-1)^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}}\mod 4
, so it is also the parity of
\frac{a-1}{2}
. Hence, we have that
\left(\frac{S}{a}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{a-1}{2}}
.

Conversely, if
S\equiv-1\mod 4
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}
is odd. In this case by our assumptions on
a
we have that
a\equiv(-1)^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}+1}\equiv(-1)^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}}\mod 4
. Hence,
{\frac{a-1}{2}}\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{1}\right\rvert}
is even if and only if
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{0}\right\rvert}
is even. Then the sum of these two terms is always even, and
\left(\frac{S}{a}\right)=1
. This gives us the Hasse-Minkowski invariants in terms of
S\mod 4
and
a\mod 4
:

c_{p}(q)=\begin{cases}1&p=2,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv 1\mod 4\\
-1&p=2,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv-1\mod 4,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv 1\mod 4\\
1&p=2,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv-1\mod 4,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv-1\mod 4\\
1&p=a,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv 1\mod 4,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv 1\mod 4\\
-1&p=a,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv 1\mod 4,\hskip 4.0pta\equiv-1\mod 4\\
1&p=a,\hskip 4.0ptS\equiv-1\mod 4\\
-1&p|S\\
1&else\\
\end{cases}
