A hybrid intelligent model that performs product evaluation via semantic mining and optimized decision processing
Authors: Dezheng Wu, Xueqian Jiang, Yawen Hu, Kexiang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The current product evaluation systems, constrained by fixed indicators and subjective weighting, struggle to adapt to dynamic market demands. This study proposes a data-driven framework integrating latent Dirichlet allocation, fuzzy analytic hierarchy process, and particle swarm optimization. User reviews and product parameters are collected from e-commerce platforms, with latent Dirichlet allocation extracting latent themes to build a multi-dimensional indicator system. The fuzzy analytic hierarchy process quantifies qualitative indicators to form an initial judgment matrix, while particle swarm optimization performs global optimization and consistency correction to minimize expert bias. An empirical case on smartwatches verifies the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed framework, demonstrating its ability to capture user-centered demand characteristics and produce comprehensive evaluation results. The proposed evaluation system effectively combines demand insights with market adaptability, offering a robust theoretical and methodological foundation for product design optimization and differentiated strategy formulation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55759-4.

Introduction

The smart wearable device industry, particularly the smartwatch market, is experiencing sustained and rapid growth. As user scenarios and lifestyles diversify, consumer demands for smartwatches have become increasingly multifaceted. These demands extend beyond core functional requirements such as health monitoring accuracy, communication capabilities, and battery life to encompass subjective preferences like interactive experience, ecosystem compatibility, and emotional design. These needs exhibit distinct dynamism and show significant variation across different user groups.

Recent advancements in hybrid decision models across various domains demonstrate potential for addressing similar complexities. For instance, the application of spherical fuzzy SWARA-WASPAS in prioritizing low-carbon policies1 and the ESG evaluation framework based on fuzzy Delphi and cluster analysis showcase robust approaches for handling uncertainty and conflicting criteria2. These studies provide a solid theoretical foundation for fuzzy judgment and optimization techniques. However, their application in the dynamic evaluation of smart wearable products incorporating real-time user feedback remains to be explored.

Current research on smart wearable devices, particularly smartwatches, primarily focuses on two directions: first, exploring consumers’ purchase intentions and usage attitudes toward smartwatches3–5; second, examining technological advancements in smartwatches, such as sensor technology, interactive features, or algorithmic performance6–8. However, systematic design evaluation research remains relatively scarce for consumer electronics like smartwatches—products characterized by multidimensionality, strong interactivity, and rapid iteration.

Although some scholars have attempted to incorporate statistical methods into product design evaluation, such as Yonaha9 who developed a design support tool using product databases and design feature metrics. Baghirov and Zhang,10 explored the relationship between aesthetic design and product attitude through mediating and moderating effect analyses. Wei and Shi11 employed FMEA-TOPSIS to construct a perceived evaluation system for intelligent product service failures. Sun et al.,12 proposed an image fuzzy set method integrating TOPSIS-GRP to enhance the accuracy of conceptual design evaluation. However, most of the aforementioned studies have failed to transcend the limitations of static metric systems. Their evaluation dimensions are often predefined, unable to automatically capture emerging themes from vast volumes of user reviews. Simultaneously, these approaches exhibit significant shortcomings in integrating objective parameters with subjective textual feedback, rendering them ill-equipped to address the multimodal data evaluation challenges posed by complex products like smartwatches.

To address these challenges, this study employs an LDA–FAHP–PSO integrated evaluation model for smartwatch design assessment, aiming to resolve key issues in this field such as dynamic shifts in user needs and difficulties in multimodal data fusion. As a comprehensive wearable device integrating health monitoring, communication, and entertainment, smartwatch evaluation encompasses not only hardware performance but also subjective factors like interaction experience, ecosystem compatibility, and emotional design. Traditional methods struggle to provide a comprehensive characterization. Based on this, this study constructs a comprehensive evaluation system spanning from data-driven indicator generation to weight optimization: First, LDA is employed to automatically extract latent themes from user reviews, establishing a multidimensional indicator system aligned with genuine needs. Subsequently, FAHP integrates experts’ fuzzy judgments on qualitative indicators. Finally, PSO is introduced to perform global consistency optimization on the judgment matrix, thereby significantly reducing subjective bias and enhancing the robustness of weight allocation.

Although previous research13 has applied this model to the evaluation of walking aids for the elderly and validated its universality. However, the application of this study within the smartwatch context highlights the model’s distinctive value: firstly, the smartwatch user base is broader and more diverse in its requirements, evolving rapidly with technological iterations. LDA’s capability for dynamic theme identification enables it to effectively capture multi-dimensional concerns such as health monitoring, battery performance, and system ecosystem. Secondly, this study further constructs a comprehensive closed-loop system encompassing ‘theme extraction—fuzzy judgement—consistency optimisation’, incorporating additional elements such as robustness validation and sensitivity analysis to enhance the transparency and reproducibility of this composite methodology. Finally, this study also demonstrates a comprehensive end-to-end evaluation process, encompassing dynamic indicator generation, weight optimisation, fuzzy comprehensive evaluation, and multi-source validation mechanisms. This approach differs from the more theory-framework-oriented presentation adopted by13. By focusing on smartwatch application scenarios and refining validation mechanisms, this study not only achieved effective identification of user requirements and quantitative assessment of key design factors, but also demonstrated the advantages of the LDA–FAHP–PSO evaluation model in addressing complex, dynamic product evaluation tasks. It provides an actionable theoretical foundation and practical pathway for smartwatch product iteration and design innovation.

Methods

Latent dirichlet allocation (LDA)

The topic model is a three-layer Bayesian model consisting of “document-topic-word,” which ultimately reveals the text information in the form of “document-topic” distribution and “topic-vocabulary” distribution. As a bag-of-words model, LDA ignores the order in which words appear in a document. Additionally, the LDA model assumes that each document contains multiple hidden topic information, with each document following a multinomial distribution of topics, and each topic following a multinomial distribution of vocabulary.

In recent years, an increasing number of studies have sought to integrate user-generated content (UGC) with multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methodologies for product evaluation and decision support. Some research employs sentiment analysis or topic extraction techniques to identify user concerns from online reviews. For instance, Huang et al.,14 utilised LDA to discern technological topic evolution trends. Xue,15 applied LDA topic mining to circular economy product research. Within consumer product contexts, Parker et al.,16 and Tian et al.,17 demonstrated how topic modelling can extract latent user demand structures from large-scale textual data. However, most of these studies employ text mining merely as a descriptive tool or as supplementary input for simple ranking methods, without directly embedding textual themes into a systematic framework for determining MCDM weights. More importantly, existing text-assisted MCDM methods typically rely on predefined lists of metrics or simplistic sentiment scores, making it difficult to dynamically reflect shifts in user demand trends. Most frameworks lack mechanisms to address inconsistencies in expert judgements and, more critically, lack a unified evaluation system that integrates textual insights with structured product parameters. Consequently, these approaches struggle to meet the evaluation demands of complex products like smart wearables within multimodal, high-dimensional, and rapidly evolving data environments.

Therefore, the LDA topic model, as a mature and reliable probabilistic statistical tool, is particularly suitable for topic identification and feature mining in large-scale text. The principle and process of text recognition using the LDA topic model are illustrated in Fig. 1. However, in product design research, there are still shortcomings in constructing a systematic mechanism for identifying product attributes from the user’s perspective. This study employs smartwatches as a case study, applying the LDA model to extract latent themes from user-generated content. This process establishes a systematic evaluation framework, providing both data support and methodological foundations for product refinement and user experience optimisation.

At its heart, LDA operates on the principle that how often a keyword pops up in a document can be explained by a joint probability distribution. This distribution is swayed both by how likely that word is to show up in a particular topic and how likely that topic is to be present in the document itself. Think of a dataset of shopping reviews: if we let M stand for the total number of reviews, W for the complete vocabulary of keywords, and T for the number of underlying topics, then φt helps us pin down the distribution of keywords within each topic, while θm gives us a sense of the topic mix in any given review. When we zoom in on the individual words, we can say that ωm, n represents the nth word within the mth comment. Think of τm, n as the topic tag slapped onto that specific word. With these labels in hand, we can build a model to understand how keywords and topics intertwine. The probability of seeing a particular combination can then be worked out using this model:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P\left( {\theta \left. {,\tau ,\omega } \right|\alpha ,\beta } \right) = p\left( {\left. \theta \right|\alpha } \right)\mathop \prod \limits_{n}^{{N_{m} }} p\left( {\left. {\tau _{n} } \right|\theta } \right)p\left( {\left. {\omega _{n} } \right|\tau _{n} ,\beta } \right)$$\end{document}

In this probabilistic model, the hyperparameters α and β are used to regulate the sparsity of the topic distribution and term distribution, respectively. Specifically, first, a Dirichlet distribution with α as the prior can be used to generate the corresponding topic distribution θm for each document. Then, each word in the document is assigned to a specific topic based on the probability structure of θm, thereby obtaining the corresponding topic label τm, n. Additionally, a Dirichlet distribution with β as the prior determines the word distribution φt within each topic, and based on this, specific terms in the document are sampled and generated, resulting in the final word ωm, n.

Particle swarm optimization (PSO)

In the mid-1990s, inspired by Heppner’s bird flock habitat model, Kenney and Eberhart proposed the particle swarm algorithm18. The concept behind the algorithm is this: initially, each particle’s location within the population is tossed into the decision vector space of the issue at random. Afterward, the particles take to the skies and navigate through the search space using a specific set of rules. This biomimetic algorithm, which uses particles to mimic the foraging behavior of birds, represents a solution to the optimization problem based on the coordinates of the particles’ positions. The fitness value of the particles determines the quality of the solutions they represent19. In algorithm design, Kenmey mimics the information-sharing behavior among bird individuals to establish rules for information exchange between particles. In each generation selection, all particles must calculate their fitness values based on the fitness function at each iteration step. By sorting the fitness values from the historical records of all particles, the global optimal position gBest is determined, and the individual optimal position pBest is determined based on the historical records of each particle. As can be seen, the PSO algorithm’s optimization process is similar to birds searching for food, both guided by the best individuals and their own best positions within the population20. The Particle Swarm Optimisation algorithm, as a quintessential swarm intelligence-inspired method, is widely applied to complex optimisation problems owing to its structural simplicity, high search efficiency, and robust global optimisation capabilities. Pham and Raahemi,19 note in their systematic review that PSO demonstrates robust performance when handling high-dimensional, multi-constraint, and non-linear optimisation tasks. Consequently, it is frequently employed for weight learning and consistency correction within multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM). Against this backdrop, PSO has been widely employed to optimise weight allocation in supplier selection within supply chain systems21,22. These studies essentially utilise PSO to address expert subjectivity and optimise consistency in decision matrices. This, of course, represents a key strength of PSO.

Meanwhile, the application boundaries of PSO continue to expand: in engineering optimisation, Mesquita and Gaspar20, Liu et al.,23, and Norouzi and Bazargan24 have respectively applied PSO to unmanned aerial vehicle path planning, water pollution simulation, and permafrost distribution prediction, thereby enhancing computational efficiency. In the field of image and signal processing, Li et al.,25 enhanced image restoration performance based on PSO. Regarding network and system optimisation, Guler,26 introduced PSO for spectrum scheduling in blockchain-enhanced optical networks, while Carstensen and Lin27 employed PSO for efficient utility set mining. Puma et al.,28 employed HT-PSO to improve convergence stability in power grid reconfiguration. The aforementioned results demonstrate the PSO’s capability in handling non-convex search spaces, complex constraints, and noisy data, further validating its theoretical reliability as a tool for optimising judgement matrix consistency and weight learning.

Building upon the aforementioned research foundations, this study incorporates the Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO) algorithm into the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP). By implementing parametric encoding of the fuzzy complementary judgement matrix, the PSO performs global search under consistency constraints. This approach mitigates expert subjectivity, thereby enhancing the accuracy and stability of weight allocation. Unlike traditional FAHP, which relies on manual calibration for consistency, the PSO-driven collaborative optimisation mechanism is better suited to high-dimensional scenarios involving multiple expert inputs. This constitutes the key technological foundation of the LDA–FAHP–PSO integrated evaluation framework.

Each particle has two attributes: velocity and position. Velocity represents the speed at which the particle moves, while position represents the direction of the particle’s movement. Optimizing the FAHP model

The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was proposed by American scholar Saaty29 in 1973. Its basic idea is to establish a hierarchical decision-making framework to convert qualitative judgments on complex issues into quantifiable analytical results. With its excellent systematicity, flexibility, and scalability, AHP has become one of the most widely used classic methods in multi-criteria complex decision-making. The key lies in constructing a consistent judgment matrix. However, traditional AHP is often limited by cognitive fuzziness when expressing the subjective preferences of decision makers. To address this shortcoming, Van Laarhoven and Pedrycz30 were the trailblazers who first brought to light the concept of Triangular Fuzzy Numbers (TFN), thereby crafting the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP). This innovative process has emerged as a powerful tool for grappling with uncertain information. Building on this foundation, subsequent studies have consistently sought to refine the methodology. Take, for instance, Chang,31, who introduced a novel weight calculation model grounded in the Extent Analysis Method (EAM), now known as geometric fuzzy AHP. On the other hand, achieves consistency testing through geometric mean closure operations on fuzzy numbers. Both of these operations require the design of complex fuzzy number multipliers. Therefore, considering the difficulty of combining PSO encoding in subsequent steps, this study will adopt the FAHP method based on fuzzy consistency matrices.

Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) has demonstrated significant application value in multiple fields. In the field of power safety, Sadeghi-Yarandi et al.,32 proposed the EISRI model, which is based on FAHP and establishes a three-dimensional quantitative assessment system for work risks covering individuals, the environment, and organizations. In urban transportation planning, Kramar and Sternad33 employed a triple integration method that combines NGT-FGs and FAHP to identify and prioritize sustainable transportation obstacles systematically. In the field of marine environmental management, Zhang et al.,34 combined FAHP with sensitivity analysis to provide a quantitative framework for prioritizing coastal and marine pollution sources, identifying chemical pollutants as the primary risk factors. At the same time, methodological innovations have driven the dynamic and intelligent development of FAHP. For example, Xie et al.,35 integrated AHP and FAHP to construct a quantitative assessment model for shield machine adaptability and verified its effectiveness through on-site construction. Mostafa et al.,36 combined FAHP with the Delphi method, significantly improving the accuracy of weighting economic criteria in forest management. Fan and Ma,37 proposed a framework for integrating FAHP with ecosystem service quantification, effectively coordinating the conflicting priorities of ecological protection and spatial development in the Shenyang metropolitan area. Despite significant progress, FAHP still has limitations, including a heavy reliance on expert judgment and a lack of dynamic adaptation mechanisms. Its static analysis characteristics are insufficient for dealing with the nonlinear evolution of complex systems. Therefore, this study combines FAHP with particle swarm optimization (PSO) to enhance its dynamic adaptability and computational stability by introducing intelligent optimization mechanisms, thereby constructing a more robust and objective product evaluation model.

As a typical representative of swarm intelligence optimization methods, the particle swarm optimization algorithm demonstrates significant advantages when dealing with high-dimensional, nonlinear optimization problems. Compared with genetic algorithms (GA). Particle Swarm Optimization, known for its ease of parameter configuration, rapid convergence, and robust global search skills, was ingeniously integrated into the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) in this research. We developed a novel FAHP-PSO cooperative optimization scheme. This approach tackles the consistency adjustment issue in fuzzy judgment matrices by framing it as a constrained, nonlinear optimization challenge. To begin with, a fitness metric was crafted to measure the level of consistency within the matrix. Next, we leverage an iterative search strategy via particle swarm optimization to nudge us closer to the optimal outcome bit by bit. This approach adeptly transforms the consistency adjustment of the fuzzy judgment matrix and the hierarchical weight assignment in the FAHP into manageable nonlinear optimization challenges. As a result, the model churns out a perfectly consistent fuzzy judgment matrix and delivers a precise ranking of all evaluation factors. This not only enhances the system’s stability but also elevates its scientific credibility.

Approach overview

This study conducts a systematic survey and data mining of the smartwatch market based on large-scale user-generated content (UGC) in the B2C e-commerce model. First, Python was used to crawl online reviews from multiple e-commerce platforms. After multi-level data cleaning and preprocessing, text segmentation and part-of-speech tagging were performed using Jieba, followed by topic modeling and visualization analysis using the pyLDAvis tool. This process identified the core concerns of consumers when purchasing smartwatches, such as health monitoring accuracy, battery life, and user experience. On this basis, a three-dimensional evaluation index system encompassing functional performance and human-computer interaction was constructed, drawing on relevant international standards, ergonomic parameters, professional literature, and Delphi expert opinions (see Fig. 3).

To further enhance the scientific rigor and dynamic adaptability of the evaluation system, this study proposes a FAHP–PSO collaborative optimization framework. In this framework, the FAHP employs λ-cut aggregation constraints to correct the consistency of the judgment matrix. At the same time, the PSO algorithm utilizes a global search mechanism with a dynamic inertia factor to efficiently optimize in high-dimensional spaces, thereby obtaining the optimal weight distribution. This enables cross-modal mapping between LDA semantic features and engineering parameters. The model solution results reveal the multi-level weight relationships of key design factors in smartwatches, providing reliable quantitative support for product optimization, market positioning, and differentiated competitive strategies.

Step-by-step structure of the whole checking mark system

In order to build the evaluation framework, this study uses a step-by-step, top-down approach and divides it into three clearly defined levels (Fig. 4). The first layer is the objective layer, which serves as the core of the entire system and contains only one element (denoted as P) to represent the overall objective of the comprehensive evaluation. The second layer is the criterion layer (B), which further elaborates on the objective layer, encompassing the key dimensions and primary standards that must be followed to achieve the overall objective. As a third-level system, the secondary standard layer connects the past and separates the core principles into more refined components and measurement indicators, thereby ensuring that the evaluation system is logically rigorous and structurally systematic.

11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${p_{ij}}=0.5\left( {1+b({\omega _i} - {\omega _j})} \right)$$\end{document}

However, when we put complex systems into practical applications, the judgment matrix is often difficult to perfect because the problems are inherently intertwined, and personal opinions inevitably vary. Therefore, FAHP generally only requires the matrix to have acceptable consistency. If the matrix P fails to meet the established criteria, it needs to be corrected. To this end, this study uses the Particle Swarm Optimization algorithm to evaluate and optimize the consistency of the fuzzy judgment matrix. The modified matrix is denoted Y. At this point, the weight vectors corresponding to each evaluation criterion are ωY, and they satisfy the following conditions:

12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$CI(Y) = \frac{1}{{n^{2} }}\mathop \sum \limits_{{i = 1}}^{n} \mathop \sum \limits_{{j = 1}}^{n} \left| {y_{{ij}} - 0.5(1 + b(\omega _{i} - \omega _{j} ))} \right|$$\end{document}

Among these, CI is the consistency indicator function, which measures the deviation between the matrix and perfect consistency. By leveraging the global search capabilities of PSO, the matrix can be iteratively optimized in high-dimensional space, gradually approaching the optimal solution. When the consistency indicator value is less than 0.1, the matrix is considered to have achieved an acceptable level of consistency; if the global minimum is reached, it indicates that the matrix has achieved perfect consistency.

Obtaining user needs based on text mining

The survey results indicate that smartwatch sales are mainly conducted through three channels: O2O, B2C, and B2B. The current market offers a wide range of smartwatch products that differ in design, functionality, and market positioning, reflecting the heterogeneous needs of different consumer groups.

Regarding online data collection, existing approaches generally fall into two categories: customised web crawlers and general-purpose scraping tools. To ensure flexibility, reproducibility, and scalability, this study employed a Python-based data collection and analysis framework. All online product and review data were collected exclusively from JD.com (Jingdong), one of the largest and most representative e-commerce platforms for consumer electronics in China. A search strategy centred on the core keyword “smartwatch” was adopted, and product popularity indicators provided by the platform (e.g., sales ranking and review volume) were used as screening criteria to identify representative products and capture mainstream market trends and user preferences.

The data collection process was conducted over the period from [2025.04] to [2025.06], ensuring temporal consistency of product information and user reviews. Prior to data collection, key attributes were explicitly defined, including product name, price, sales volume, number of reviews, manufacturer information, and delivery regions. A fixed crawl depth strategy was applied, extracting product information from the top ten pages of search results on JD.com, as ranked by the platform’s default relevance and recency mechanisms. This approach balances data representativeness with computational efficiency. Following data standardisation and export to Excel format, Python scripts were further deployed to crawl user-generated review texts associated with the selected smartwatch products.

After systematic data cleaning procedures—including the removal of duplicate entries, promotional content, and non-informative comments—and consistency validation, approximately 3,000 valid and high-quality user review records were retained for subsequent analysis. Owing to the platform’s privacy protection policies, detailed demographic attributes of reviewers (such as age, gender, or income level) were not publicly accessible, making explicit demographic stratification infeasible. Consequently, the analysis focused on experiential and functional content embedded in user reviews rather than demographic segmentation, a limitation that is acknowledged in this study.

Optimal topic selection and visualization in LDA modeling

After completing text cleaning and standardization, this study employed the topic coherence metric to determine the appropriate number of topics for the LDA model. Topic coherence measures the semantic consistency and interpretability of topic partitions, with higher values indicating more semantically concentrated and distinguishable topic structures. As shown in Fig. 6, when the number of topics increases from 3 to 5, the coherence value rises markedly, indicating a substantial improvement in topic quality. Although further increases in the number of topics (K = 6–10) lead to minor fluctuations and a gradual upward trend, the overall growth rate of coherence becomes noticeably flatter, with the maximum value observed at K = 10 (approximately 0.46).

It should be noted, however, that topic number selection in this study was not based on coherence alone. While larger K values may yield marginally higher coherence scores, an excessive number of topics tends to fragment semantically related content, resulting in redundant or overly fine-grained themes. Such semantic dispersion reduces interpretability and complicates the construction of a concise and stable evaluation hierarchy for subsequent multi-criteria decision-making analysis. Considering the trade-off between statistical coherence, semantic separability, and practical applicability to FAHP-based indicator construction, this study selected five topics as a balanced and methodologically appropriate solution. This choice preserves the core experiential structure of user evaluations while avoiding unnecessary analytical complexity, thereby better supporting the development and application of the smartwatch product evaluation indicator system.

Formulation and deployment of the fuzzy complementary matrix questionnaire

To ensure methodological rigor and robustness of the evaluation results, this study followed three principles in expert sample selection: multi-role coverage, professional experience stratification, and sample stability assessment. A total of 35 cross-disciplinary participants were invited to form the evaluation panel, including 8 smartwatch device experts, 6 health monitoring engineers, 9 industrial designers, 5 product managers, and 7 representatives of long-term smartwatch users. The adopted panel size is consistent with commonly recommended ranges in fuzzy AHP, fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (FCE), and MCDM studies (typically 20–40 experts), and was further supported by a convergence-oriented stability assessment. It should be noted that among the 35 panel members, seven participants were categorised as long-term smartwatch users. These individuals were not engaged as technical experts but rather as experience-oriented contributors, aiming to capture sustained user perceptions—perceptions that are often underestimated in purely expert-led evaluations. All user representatives met predefined inclusion criteria, including continuous smartwatch usage exceeding two years and experience with at least two distinct brands or models.

In the judgment aggregation process, no explicit a priori weighting was assigned based on expert type. Instead, all judgments were integrated using the FAHP framework with PSO-based consistency correction, which reduces the influence of inconsistent or extreme inputs during matrix optimization. Additional inter-group consistency checks confirmed that the inclusion of long-term users did not introduce systematic bias into the weight estimation results.

To examine the effect of expert sample size on weight estimation stability, a simulation-based convergence analysis was conducted by progressively increasing the number of experts and tracking the resulting variations in indicator membership degrees and weights. The analysis indicates that when the sample size approaches approximately 30 experts, the fluctuations in key indicator weights decrease markedly and enter a plateau region, with average relative changes falling below 2%. Further increases in the number of experts produce only marginal adjustments to the overall weighting structure. Based on this observed stabilization trend, a 35-member expert panel was adopted to balance diversity of perspectives with robustness of the aggregated weights.

To further assess the rationality of the expert composition, inter-group scoring consistency was examined using variance comparison and Kendall’s W coefficient. The results demonstrate a high level of agreement across different expert groups, with no evidence of systematic structural bias. Overall, the interdisciplinary panel of 35 experts provides sufficient stability, representativeness, and reliability for constructing the fuzzy complementary judgment matrix, while effectively mitigating the influence of individual subjectivity on the final evaluation outcomes.

The questionnaire comprised two sections. The first collected respondents’ background information to ensure data traceability and reliability, while the second focused on core smartwatch evaluation criteria. Participants were required to conduct pairwise comparisons and assign scores based on their professional knowledge and usage experience. To reduce respondent burden and improve data processing efficiency, experts were asked to complete only the upper triangular part of the fuzzy complementary judgment matrix, with the lower triangular elements automatically generated according to the complementary reciprocity rules.

Applying PSO for nonlinear optimization of evaluation weights

In the process of applying PSO for the consistency correction of fuzzy complementary judgment matrices, this study formulates the consistency improvement task as a nonlinear optimization problem defined over the 10 independent elements of the matrix. For a 5 × 5 fuzzy complementary matrix, the upper-triangular non-diagonal entries contain n(n-1)/2 = 10 free variables. Accordingly, the decision space of PSO is 10-dimensional. The lower-triangular entries are automatically generated by the reciprocal–complement relation rji = 1-rij, and the diagonal entries are fixed at 0.5, ensuring that the matrix structure remains valid throughout the optimization process.

Initialization and fitness evaluation: Each particle is represented as a 10-dimensional vector corresponding to the ten optimizable elements of the judgment matrix. Particle positions are initialized uniformly within the interval [0.1, 0.9], and velocities are drawn randomly from the range [-vmax, vmax]. After initialization, the fitness of each particle is computed using the consistency index CI (Y, w), which quantifies the level of inconsistency in the corresponding reconstructed matrix.

Iterative search and update: During optimization, particles iteratively update their positions and velocities according to the standard PSO update rules. If a particle’s current solution outperforms its historical best, the personal best is updated accordingly. Likewise, the global best is updated when a particle surpasses the best performance observed in the entire swarm. After each update, boundary repair and reciprocal-complement enforcement are applied to maintain structural validity of the judgment matrix.

Stopping criterion and weight derivation: The search terminates when the maximum number of iterations (500) is reached or when convergence criteria are satisfied. The consistency-corrected fuzzy complementary matrix obtained from PSO is then used as input to FAHP. The final weight vector of the evaluation indicators is derived using the geometric mean method, thereby enabling accurate quantification of the hierarchical criteria. Specifically, the decision variables for the optimization problem are defined as the upper triangular elements (excluding the diagonal) of the fuzzy complementary judgment matrix. For a 5th-order judgment matrix, the variable dimension D is 10. The particle swarm size is set to 100, a commonly used scale for solving such medium-dimensional problems, sufficient to maintain population diversity. The learning factors c1 and c2 both adopt the standard value of 2.0 to balance the particles’ reliance on their own experience and the group’s social information. The inertia weight ω employs a linear decay strategy, gradually decreasing from 0.9 to 0.4. This allows the algorithm to focus on global exploration during the early iterations and shift toward local refinement in the later stages. Additionally, velocity clamping (capping maximum velocity at 20% of the search space range) was implemented to control particle movement stride, preventing particles from skipping over optimal regions.

In the experimental implementation, the position of each particle was randomly initialized within the variable domain [0.1, 0.9]. To mitigate the randomness inherent in the optimization algorithm, we conducted 30 independent runs and selected the solution with the optimal fitness value (i.e., the lowest consistency index CI) as the final result.

To avoid ambiguity, it is clarified that the objective function minimized by PSO is the consistency index CI (Y, w) defined in section “Consistency analysis and PSO-based correction for fuzzy judgment matrices”, where the reconstructed consistent matrix Y\(w) is computed from the weight vector obtained via the geometric mean method. Because PSO optimizes the ten free upper-triangular elements rather than the weights directly, no normalization constraint such as ∑wi = 1 is imposed within the optimization loop; the weight vector produced by the geometric mean method naturally satisfies this condition. In addition to the maximum iteration limit of 500, a secondary convergence criterion was applied: optimization stops early if the improvement in the global best fitness falls below 10− 6 for 50 consecutive iterations. To ensure robustness against stochastic effects, the optimization procedure was repeated for 30 independent runs. Statistical descriptors (mean, standard deviation, and best-run performance) were recorded to assess the stability of the algorithm before selecting the best solution for the final FAHP weight computation.

When determining the weight distribution of the smartwatch design evaluation index system, this study used user evaluation data obtained from LDA topic modeling and further refined the five core dimensions (Performance & Hardware, Design & Comfort, Health & Fitness Tracking, Connectivity & Features, Durability & Value) into 30 specific evaluation indicators. Subsequently, the six high-frequency keywords within each theme are normalized to obtain the indicator layer weights. Finally, the comprehensive weight is calculated as “criterion layer weight × indicator layer weight” to reflect the relative importance of each indicator in the overall evaluation system. From the results, Health & Fitness Tracking (weight 0.240) has the highest proportion among the five core dimensions, indicating that users are most concerned about the health monitoring functions of smartwatches; followed by Performance & Hardware (both with a weight of 0.220), highlighting the important role of hardware performance and durability in user experience. In the indicator layer, metrics such as “Heart rate,” “Battery life,” and “Waterproof” rank highly in terms of composite weight, indicating their central role in user evaluations and demands.

Sensitivity of the FAHP–PSO weighting process to expert input

To assess the robustness of the FAHP-PSO weighting process against potential variations in expert judgment, we conducted a series of sensitivity analyses.

First, a leave-one-expert-out (LOEO) analysis was performed. For each evaluation indicator, one expert was removed in turn, and the fuzzy complementary judgment matrix was reconstructed using the remaining experts’ data. The entire FAHP-PSO pipeline was then rerun to generate a new weight vector. Across all LOEO trials, the maximum deviation observed for any indicator’s weight was below 4.3%, and the relative ranking of all indicators remained unchanged. This demonstrates that the weighting process is not overly dependent on the judgment of any single expert. The results of this analysis are summarized in Fig. 9.

Weighted bias bar chart.

As illustrated in Fig. 9, the maximum percentage change for each indicator’s weight is well below the 4.3% threshold (dashed line). The most sensitive indicator shows a deviation of only 3.8%, providing visual confirmation that the results are not unduly influenced by any individual expert, thereby demonstrating the robustness of the FAHP-PSO process.

Second, to evaluate robustness under minor judgment inconsistencies, random noise drawn from a uniform distribution U (-0.05, + 0.05) was added to each expert’s original pairwise comparison entry. The perturbed values were then re-normalized to preserve the fuzzy complementary constraints. This experiment was repeated 50 times. The resulting weight distributions showed low dispersion, with a mean standard deviation of 0.018, indicating strong resilience to small perturbations. The compact interquartile ranges (the boxes) and the stability of the median values (the central lines) indicate low variability. The presence of longer whiskers and a single outlier for B10 suggests that while the weight for this indicator can be more sensitive to specific perturbations, the overall impact remains within acceptable limits, as corroborated by the LOEO analysis in Fig. 9.

Robustness of LDA topic modeling

The robustness of the LDA topic modeling component was rigorously evaluated against variations in its key parameters and preprocessing choices.

First, the sensitivity to the number of topics (K) was assessed. LDA models were trained with different topic numbers K ∈ {8, 10, 12, 14}, and their topic coherence scores (Cv metric) were computed. As shown in Fig. 11, the coherence curve exhibits a clear maximum at K = 10, providing quantitative justification for the model configuration adopted in this study.

Fig. 11Parameter sensitivity of LDA topic models.

Parameter sensitivity of LDA topic models.

The distinct peak at K = 10 in Fig. 11 indicates that the topics extracted with this parameter are the most semantically interpretable and coherent. The subsequent decline in coherence for K > 10 suggests the emergence of redundant or overlapping topics. This graph provides data-driven validation for our parameter choice. Furthermore, the core semantic themes—such as battery life, health-monitoring reliability, UI/UX experience, and material quality—were consistently identified across all tested K values, with an average Jaccard similarity of 0.76 for the top-10 keywords per topic, indicating semantic stability.

Second, the impact of text preprocessing choices was tested using three configurations: (1) a baseline stop-word list; (2) an expanded stop-word list including review-specific filler terms; and (3) synonym merging (e.g., mapping “battery duration” to “battery life”). Across these configurations, topic coherence varied within a narrow range (0.486–0.512), and the dominant topics remained semantically stable. This confirms that the LDA-derived indicators are not overly sensitive to preprocessing variations.

Finally, the stability of the LDA model (with K = 10) was tested by training it with 10 different random seeds. The cosine similarity between the topic-word distributions from different runs was computed pairwise, yielding a mean similarity of 0.84. This indicates high stability and low sensitivity to the algorithm’s inherent randomness.

Across all three robustness dimensions examined—expert perturbation, LDA parameter sensitivity, and algorithmic randomness—the variations in the final indicator weights and product evaluation scores were minor. Crucially, no changes occurred in the ranking of the smartwatches. These results collectively demonstrate that the proposed LDA-FAHP-PSO evaluation system is stable and reliable, even under reasonable variations in expert inputs or topic modeling configurations.

In the comparative analysis of smartwatch case studies, W3 (Apple Watch Series 10) achieved the highest overall score among the evaluated products, establishing itself as the benchmark case for validating the assessment framework’s efficacy. The final standardised scores and ranking outcomes for W1 to W4 clearly illustrate each product’s relative performance across all evaluation dimensions.

W3 demonstrated a clear advantage in the highest-weighted dimension, “Health and Fitness Tracking” (weight = 0.240). Specifically, its heart rate monitoring (B31) and blood oxygen detection (B35) indicators received “extremely high” ratings from more than 40% of experts, which substantially contributed to its overall score. In addition, W3 performed consistently well in performance hardware (e.g., processing speed and display quality) and design craftsmanship, resulting in a balanced and robust multi-dimensional performance profile. From the user review perspective, health-related features were mentioned in approximately 65% of positive evaluations, indicating a qualitative consistency between the evaluation outcomes and user-perceived value.

By contrast, the remaining products exhibited strengths in specific dimensions but showed limitations in addressing multiple high-priority criteria simultaneously. W4 (Samsung Galaxy Watch7) achieved relatively high scores in health monitoring accuracy and connectivity; however, its lower performance in user experience and ecosystem integration constrained its overall ranking. W2 (Xiaomi Watch S4 Sport) performed well in cost-effectiveness and battery endurance, yet these indicators were assigned relatively low weights (0.0309 and 0.0370, respectively), resulting in a limited contribution to the composite score. W1 (Huawei WATCH GT5) showed a clear advantage in battery life but lagged behind in multidimensional integration, particularly in software ecosystem compatibility and interactive experience, which led to a comparatively lower overall score.

Taken together, the ranking results reflect the current value orientation of the smartwatch market, where health monitoring capabilities and user experience-related attributes play a dominant role in shaping overall product competitiveness. Although the alignment between the evaluation rankings and market popularity or user ratings is primarily discussed at a qualitative level due to the lack of directly comparable external numerical indicators, the observed consistency in rank order provides preliminary support for the validity of the proposed framework. Future research could further strengthen this validation by incorporating quantitative correlation analysis using standardized sales or rating datasets.

Discussion and outlook

Methodological contributions of the LDA-FAHP-PSO framework

This study employs the LDA topic model to effectively identify latent demand themes within user-generated content, overcoming the limitations of traditional methods that rely on expert experience and prior knowledge to define metrics. Leveraging big data semantic mining, this evaluation model dynamically tracks user preferences, broadens the research perspective on product evaluation, and provides reliable data support and theoretical foundations for innovative design. This facilitates the translation of evaluation theory into design practice.

At the methodological level, this study employs multiple mature techniques from various fields to propose a comprehensive evaluation model based on the synergistic operation of LDA, FAHP, and PSO. FAHP plays a pivotal role in integrating expert knowledge and addressing uncertainties within the evaluation process, while PSO compensates for potential consistency biases inherent in traditional subjective judgments through global search and parameter optimization. This integration not only enhances the scientific rigor and stability of indicator weight allocation but also enables more efficient solutions to complex multi-objective evaluation problems, forming a data-driven, intelligent evaluation model.

The theoretical contribution primarily lies in the cross-disciplinary integration and systematic innovation of methodologies. This study organically combines semantic data mining, fuzzy analytic hierarchy process, and swarm intelligence optimization to form a closed-loop pathway from requirement information extraction and weight determination to optimization solution. The LDA model reveals the latent thematic structure within textual data, providing a robust data foundation for indicator construction. The coupled application of FAHP and PSO enhances the system’s accuracy and robustness when confronting complex, multidimensional decision-making scenarios. This innovative integration not only deepens the research into product evaluation but also provides quantitative, systematic tools for designing and optimizing smart wearable products. It offers a viable solution for the industry’s transition from experience-driven to data-driven approaches.

Evaluation framework extensibility and cross-scenario value

This study successfully validated its predefined research objectives by establishing a product evaluation framework. Concurrently, it optimised the systematic assessment pathway for smart wearable devices: firstly, by translating product selection and evaluation processes into computable knowledge models; secondly, by constructing a comprehensive workflow encompassing requirement identification, solution screening, and design optimisation, underpinned by an experiment-data-driven validation mechanism.

The LDA-FAHP-PSO integrated evaluation system was validated in a B2C scenario in this study. By performing semantic mining on user-generated content from e-commerce platforms, the LDA model identifies latent demand themes. Combined with FAHP to construct a multidimensional decision matrix, the PSO algorithm achieves global optimization of indicator weights. This enables quantitative mapping and analysis of product attributes against user preferences. The evaluation model also demonstrates application potential in O2O and B2B contexts. In O2O scenarios, evaluations extend beyond online reviews to encompass multiple factors such as offline service experiences, store environments, and logistics delivery. This necessitates expansion in data integration and dynamic feedback mechanisms. At this stage, LDA remains effective in identifying latent needs within social media and review data, while FAHP and PSO can adjust weight distributions by incorporating offline metrics and real-time data to ensure results align with actual operational environments. In B2B scenarios, evaluations place greater emphasis on technical performance, cost-effectiveness, supply chain reliability, and strategic alignment. Unlike B2C user experience data, B2B data sources rely more heavily on standardized corporate procurement records and industry research reports. By expanding the metric system and adjusting algorithm inputs, LDA can still uncover shifts in industry demand. Meanwhile, FAHP and PSO enable optimized decision-making based on expert knowledge and structured data, making evaluation outcomes better aligned with the practical needs of corporate collaboration and long-term strategic planning.

In summary, the LDA-FAHP-PSO integrated evaluation system not only enables efficient consolidation and quantitative analysis of user feedback in B2C environments but also extends its applicability to O2O and B2B models through multi-source data fusion and dynamic optimization mechanisms. This framework provides a scientific, transferable support tool for product design, marketing promotion, and strategic decision-making across diverse sales models, demonstrating strong adaptability and practical value.

Limitations and future prospects

Although the LDA–FAHP–PSO integrated evaluation framework demonstrates strong innovation and adaptability in both methodology and application, several limitations remain. First, the empirical analysis focuses on a case study of smartwatches, whose sample size and scope are relatively limited. This may affect the model’s external validity when applied to larger-scale, multi-category product environments. Secondly, FAHP remains heavily reliant on expert input when constructing fuzzy judgment matrices. Even with consistency optimization via PSO, it cannot entirely eliminate the inherent subjectivity in expert selection bias and pairwise comparison judgments. Furthermore, traditional LDA exhibits limited topic extraction capabilities in scenarios involving short texts and semantic sparsity, potentially resulting in unclear topic boundaries. Finally, in large-scale or real-time evaluation scenarios, LDA topic modeling combined with multi-round PSO optimization incurs high computational costs, imposing certain limitations on system scalability and online application capabilities.

Future research may explore the deep integration of advanced machine learning models with ordinal multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) frameworks to enhance decision-making capabilities for complex systems. In recent years, relevant studies have demonstrated that deep learning exhibits significant advantages in handling high-dimensional feature interactions, temporal dependencies, and highly nonlinear relationships41,42. Consequently, integrating neural network architectures such as Transformers, GNNs, BiLSTMs, and convolutional self-attention into ordinal MCDM holds promise for enhancing automated feature extraction, criterion relationship modeling, and preference learning. This approach opens new possibilities for application in dynamic, multimodal decision-making scenarios. Furthermore, future research can expand the application scope by extending this evaluation framework to comprehensive assessment tasks involving multiple product types, diverse data sources across platforms, and cross-cultural contexts. This will systematically validate the model’s generalizability, adaptability, and robustness.
