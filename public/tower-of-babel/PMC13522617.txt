Enhanced multi-attribute decision-making method to analyze renewable energy sources for sustainable transportation by synergizing the cubic set with picture fuzzy hypersoft sets
Authors: Muhammad Sajid, Khuram Ali Khan, Atiqe Ur Rahman, Rostin Matendo Mabela
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDue to increasing environmental degradation, urban air pollution, and energy instability, the Government of Pakistan is focusing on transitioning to cleaner and more sustainable transportation options. While biofuels have often been seen as a partial substitute, their long-term environmental and economic sustainability is limited. Problems such as land use conflicts, food security concerns, and minimal carbon reduction highlight the need for a systematic shift to renewable energy sources like solar, wind, and hydropower. These alternatives offer greater sustainability, lower carbon footprints, and support Pakistan’s national climate goals and energy diversification strategies. This research introduces a new approach to decision-making on green energy options using cubic picture fuzzy hypersoft sets (CPFHSSs), which effectively manage the ambiguity and complexity involved in the evaluation process. The decision-making method applies OR and AND operations of the CPFHSSs to combine expert judgments across multiple criteria, considering both optimistic and pessimistic perspectives in the final evaluation. The methodology is demonstrated through a case study based on recent official guidance to identify and rank the most suitable renewable energy sources for sustainable transportation in Pakistan. Introduction

The global automobile industry has become a vital sector, significantly influencing both the economy and research and development efforts. The growing number of automobiles on the road has facilitated efficient and rapid transportation. However, the increasing reliance on internal combustion vehicles that operate on conventional, non-renewable fuels has raised concerns about their environmental impact and energy consumption1. Despite this, the rise in conventional vehicles powered by fossil fuels has significantly contributed to increased air pollution in urban areas, as shown by higher levels of pollutants like particulate matter, nitrogen oxides , carbon monoxide (CO), and sulphur dioxide , among others2. A European Union assessment indicates that the transportation sector is responsible for over 28% of total carbon dioxide emissions, with road transport accounting for over 70% of emissions in this sector3. Fossil fuels are the unfortunate source of the necessary energy, which contributes to climate change and greenhouse gas emissions two of the most significant and dangerous problems for environmental sustainability4,5. Therefore, there is a need to drastically cut back on fossil fuel consumption and greenhouse gas emissions in order to maintain environmental stability6. Pakistan is aiming to transition its transportation sector from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources to promote sustainable mobility. This transition involves the adoption of new energy vehicles powered by electricity, hydrogen, or biofuels. However, the decision-making process for renewable energy options is often challenging due to uncertainty and ambiguity. To address this, a new mathematical framework based on the CPFHSS has been introduced. This new decision-support model offers a more adaptable way to assess complicated situations, making it easier to make trustworthy decisions in sustainable transportation planning.

In 1965, Zadeh invented fuzzy sets (FSs)7 as a method for managing uncertainty in decision-making processes. The FSs ascertain an object’s degree of membership by mapping the set to the interval [0, 1]. Zadeh subsequently introduced interval-valued fuzzy sets (Iv-FSs)8, which serve as a generalisation of fuzzy sets. Atanassov introduced intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs)9, which extend fuzzy sets by incorporating membership and non-membership functions. He later proposed interval-valued IFSs10 to handle higher uncertainty. Picture fuzzy sets (PFSs)11,12 are an extension of intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs), comprising membership, abstention, and non-membership functions. They must reside within a closed unit interval. Cuong created interval-valued PFSs (Iv-PFSs)13, which describe membership, abstention, and non-membership degrees as closed subintervals of [0, 1]. Iv-PFSs provide a superior framework for the representation and management of fuzzy information in comparison to alternative techniques.

Jun et al.14 introduced the concepts of internal and external cubic sets. Additionally, refined definitions of the minimum and maximum between two interval numbers were presented in15. Ashraf et al.16 presented the concept of cubic PFS (CPFS), facilitating the concurrent storing of both definite and indeterminate picture fuzzy information. Abdullah and Ashraf17 devised aggregation algorithms to synthesise confusing information in CPFSs, tackling challenges related to petroleum circulation centre evaluations. Ashraf et al.18 introduced a cleaner production evaluation framework for gold mines utilising a distance measure approach with cubic picture fuzzy numbers. Tehreem et al.19 developed three categories of average aggregation operators derived from CPFSs.

The theory of soft sets (SS)20 has greatly advanced since it was founded by Molodtsov, addressing issues in FSs arising from inadequate parameterisation. SSs are parameterised subsets of the universal set, formulated using diverse relations, functions, and methodologies21–23. Maji et al. amalgamated SS theory with the FS framework to develop fuzzy soft sets (FSSs)24. Extensions of SSs encompass interval-valued fuzzy soft sets (25), intuitionistic fuzzy soft sets (26), picture fuzzy soft sets (27), and interval-valued picture fuzzy soft sets (28).

Smarandache introduced a novel approach to handling uncertainty by transforming the function into a multi-argument function. This extension led to the development of the hypersoft set (HSS)29 and its various hybrids, including fuzzy HSS30, interval-valued fuzzy HSS31, intuitionistic fuzzy HSS32, interval-valued intuitionistic fuzzy HSS33, picture fuzzy HSS34, and interval-valued picture fuzzy HSS35. Harl et al.36 recently published fresh recommended distances and similarity metrics customised for PFHSSs. Saeed and Harl37 introduced additional operations and features of PFHSS and GPFHSS, validated De Morgan s laws for PFHSS, and proposed an approach with an innovative expectation score function to efficiently rank alternatives in multi-attribute decision-making (MADM) situations.

Relevant literature

Kumar et al.38 examined the growing application of multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) techniques in energy planning, particularly for rural and remote areas. These methods help decision-makers address the complex interplay of technical, economic, environmental, and social factors by enabling the simultaneous evaluation of multiple, often competing, objectives. Buonocore et al.39 introduced country-level metrics to assess the impact of renewable energy and transportation developments on emissions and public health, revealing disparities in corporate progress toward the Sustainable Development Goals. Işık et al.40 proposed a hybrid model for evaluating Pakistani commercial bank performance using MADM. Alam et al.41 presented a fuzzy MADM framework for the evaluation of Pakistans flood preparedness. Similarly, Ramezanzade et al.42 proposed a hybrid decision-making framework for selecting the most appropriate renewable energy projects at the sub-national level, incorporating environmental, economic, technical, and social criteria. Saeed et al.43 used a dynamic fuzzy HSS-based method to assess the Renewable Energy systems that are currently in place and offer a decision support system for installing new ones based on various geographical, political, social, and natural factors for the best possible resource management. Butkiene and Streimikiene44 employ a MCDM methodology to evaluate sustainable energy development, which is crucial for attaining carbon neutrality, while taking into account both practical and socio-political dimensions of the energy transition in sectors such as power distribution, residential energy, and transportation. Recently, Wang et al.45 integrated“fuzzy analytical hierarchy process” and“technique for order of preference by similarity to ideal solution” to evaluate and select optimal renewable energy sources for Taiwan’s logistics industry. Reddy et al.3 conducted a comprehensive comparison of four sustainable vehicle technologies: biofuel powered vehicles, fuel cell vehicles, electric vehicles, and solar vehicles, evaluating their development, operational principles, and sustainability based on technical, environmental, economic, and policy factors.

Sajid et al.46 recently proposed a novel algorithmic MADM framework for solar panel selection, utilizing modified aggregations of cubic intuitionistic fuzzy hypersoft sets (HSSs). Subsequently, they applied multi-attribute decision-making methods under the single-valued neutrosophic cubic hypersoft set framework to evaluate motorcycle brands47. In another study, Sajid et al.48 employed improved cosine similarity measures for single-valued neutrosophic cubic hypersoft sets in a MADM technique aimed at sustainable supplier selection in the healthcare industry. Additionally, Ali and Yang49 analyzed renewable energy alternatives using circular bipolar complex intuitionistic fuzzy linguistic information, incorporating Frank power aggregation operators and the multi-attribute border approximation area comparison model.

As an extension of SS, HSS can get around restrictions by using the multi-attributes function and can be applied for MADM problems without limiting the attributes of the decision-makers. It provides a more precise model compared to traditional mathematical methods. To develop the CPFHSS, the two frameworks that were previously explained are combined. The offered structure is better than others found in the literature because it uses the flexibility of cubic picture fuzzy structures to address ambiguity and uncertainty in decision analysis while providing access to data parameters as sub-parametric values. Aggregation strategies: the OR-operation and the AND-operation

In MADM problems, complex and uncertain data such as evaluating renewable energy options for sustainable transportation, is involved. It is essential to combine individual opinions or expert evaluations in a meaningful and robust way. Since each decision-maker might view options differently based on various factors, we need a good way to combine these opinions into one clear decision-making framework. To address this, we introduce two aggregation strategies OR-operation and AND-operation on CPFHSSs. These operations help capture the optimistic and pessimistic perspectives of the evaluators, allowing for a more comprehensive and flexible analysis in uncertain environments.

Problem statement

In recent years, energy has become a crucial global resource. Many sectors worldwide face challenges such as energy shortages, inefficient resource utilization, and sustainability risks52. The transportation industry, in particular, is one of the most affected by these issues. As a vital link in the global business and social ecosystem, the transportation sector relies heavily on energy. Technological advancements have enabled the adoption of various methodologies to enhance energy supply within this industry . These option are electric vehicle battery and fuel-cell engine technologies in lieu of gasoline engines53. Simultaneously, these technologies serve as effective alternatives for efficient energy utilization and environmental sustainability. Nevertheless, these technological advancements cannot independently resolve the entire issue. In examining the energy usage procedures within the transportation sector, it becomes evident that the primary issues related to resources are associated with the energy generation components. For instance, while utilizing electric vehicles and battery technology to mitigate carbon emissions, it is imperative to investigate the source of the energy employed for charging the battery. Furthermore, while employing fuel-cell technology that is entirely hydrogen-based, it is imperative to ascertain the energy sources utilized in the hydrogen generation process54. As a result, compared to earlier times, the countries’ energy production resources and methods have become increasingly significant. The use of traditional fossil fuel resources as a base for energy creation is a major problem for the world because they are outdated, inefficient, and unsustainable, yet they have a large producing capacity in comparison to other options. One example of the region’s heavy reliance on coal for electricity generation is Asia Pacific, where coal-fired power plants provide about 56% of the region’s electricity52. Therefore, there are significant health and environmental dangers associated with air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions55. It’s also no secret that leaders of both wealthy and emerging nations have a responsibility to make decisions about their countries’ future, including formulating policies surrounding their energy infrastructures. However, it is important to acknowledge that pricing and costing factors can lead to hesitancy, particularly in underdeveloped nations56. The transportation sector’s high reliance on fossil fuels is a major contributor to economic instability, energy insecurity, and carbon emissions. For transportation, an effective and sustainable alternative energy source is required.

Case study and numerical example of renewable energy in sustainable transportation

This study focuses on modeling the decision-making process for new energy investments in transportation infrastructure in a developing nation reliant on fossil fuels. Using a Multi-Attribute Decision-Making (MADM) model, it evaluates green energy sources for sustainable transportation. Transitioning to renewable energy is vital for these countries, offering economic, environmental, and social benefits, enhancing energy security, and lowering transportation costs. It also reduces carbon emissions and air pollution, supporting public health and climate goals. Investments in clean energy are essential for economic growth, job creation, and innovation. However, developing countries like Nigeria, Pakistan, India, and Indonesia face challenges in adopting sustainable mobility due to inadequate green energy infrastructure, leading to significant environmental and energy security concerns. Energy system of these nations mostly depend on fossil fuel. For example, Nigeria boasts the largest economy and the most abundant oil resources on the African continent, leading to fluctuations in energy supply and pollution issues. It continues to be the biggest producer and consumer of petrol in West Africa. Nigeria is undergoing significant power sector reforms, including electrification plans. In Pakistan, the majority of main energy supply is derived from oil and natural gas. The nation’s primary renewable energy source is hydropower, however the shares of wind and solar photovoltaic are gradually increasing. India’s energy demand is rising quickly, which has significant ramifications for the world energy market. This increasing need is met by a variety of energy sources, with coal continuing to be the dominant source. As the Indian government meets its lofty renewable energy targets and lowers local air pollution, it remains committed to providing safe, affordable, and sustainable electricity. In recent years, Indonesia’s oil imports have grown significantly. This resource-rich country is the leading supplier of gas in Southeast Asia and the fourth-largest producer of coal worldwide. The Indonesian government has presented multifaceted ideas that include expanding the current infrastructure, encouraging off-grid solar and micro-hydro technologies, as well as isolated diesel generators and solar-diesel hybrids. As these countries strive to transition to more environmentally friendly energy sources, lowering their dependency on fossil fuels will be essential to achieving sustainable development goals. Conclusion

In this study, we introduced a new mathematical framework called the CPFHSS to address the confusion, uncertainty, and unclear aspects often encountered in MADM problems. The CPFHSS framework enhances traditional decision models by incorporating the advantages of cubic picture fuzzy sets and hypersoft set theory, making it particularly effective for complex evaluations, such as assessing renewable energy sources for sustainable transportation. To effectively aggregate expert opinions and evaluate various attributes, we developed and implemented two aggregation strategies: the OR-operation and the AND-operation on CPFHSSs. These operations offer flexible and comprehensive methods for evaluating alternatives in both optimistic and pessimistic decision-making environments. Building on this theoretical foundation, we designed a practical algorithm capable of solving MADM problems by utilizing the CPFHSS model along with the OR and AND operations. The algorithm systematically processes complex decision matrices and identifies the most suitable alternative based on clearly defined scoring rules. To validate the applicability and robustness of the proposed method, we applied the algorithm to a real-world case study in which the Pakistani government seeks to identify the most appropriate renewable energy source for sustainable transportation. The results highlighted the superiority and consistency of one alternative across both aggregation strategies, thus demonstrating the method s effectiveness in realistic policy-making scenarios. The flexibility and adaptability of the proposed framework make it applicable to a broad range of other decision-making domains such as supply chain management, healthcare systems, environmental planning, and financial investment analysis. Future research can explore further extensions of this model to include hybrid aggregation operators and dynamic decision-making environments.
