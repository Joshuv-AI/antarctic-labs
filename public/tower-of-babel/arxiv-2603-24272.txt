Abstract
Based on a novel method for producing antineutrons via J / ψ J/\psi decays, we report a study of n ¯ ​ p \bar{n}p inelastic scattering into final states containing kaons. The analysis uses ( 10087 ± 44 ) × 10 6 (10087\pm 44)\times 10^{6} J / ψ J/\psi events collected at the BESIII detector operating at the BEPCII storage ring. Antineutrons are produced via J / ψ → p ​ π − ​ n ¯ J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n} decays and tagged by the detected protons and pions, resulting in antineutron momenta ranging from 0 to 1174 Me ​ V / c {\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c} , while target protons are provided by the hydrogen in the beam-pipe material. The cross sections of the reactions n ¯ ​ p → K + ​ K − ​ π + \bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+} and n ¯ ​ p → K + ​ K − ​ π + ​ π 0 \bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0} are measured to be 0.53 − 0.12 + 0.15 ± 0.08 0.53^{+0.15}_{-0.12}\pm 0.08 mb and 1.09 − 0.30 + 0.36 ± 0.31 1.09^{+0.36}_{-0.30}\pm 0.31 mb respectively, where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second systematic. Due to limited statistics, the intermediate states in these processes are not investigated. The observation of clean antineutron-proton scattering events indicates the potential of this approach for future investigations of antineutron-proton interactions.



Scattering experiments are powerful tools for probing the microstructure of matter and the interactions among particles. They have been widely used since Rutherford aimed
\alpha
particles at a gold target in 1911
E.Rutherford
. As fundamental constituents of matter, nucleons and their antiparticles, antinucleons, have attracted extensive research interest. In particular, the development of antiproton beams at LEAR made it possible to conduct comprehensive studies of antiproton-proton (
\bar{p}p
) scattering
Amsler:2019ytk
, including cross section measurements, meson spectroscopy studies, and searches for bound
\bar{p}p
states, significantly advancing our understanding of nucleon-antinucleon interactions.

Unlike
\bar{p}p
scattering, antineutron-proton (
\bar{n}p
) scattering involves only the
I=1
state and is free from significant Coulomb interactions. These advantages make it an ideal laboratory for studying the strong interactions of the nucleon and antinucleon. Although antiproton-neutron (
\bar{p}n
) scattering shares these advantages, neutron targets are experimentally challenging due to the presence of surrounding protons. Moreover, due to the substantial difficulties in producing and controlling antineutron beams,
\bar{n}p
scattering experiments are much less than
\bar{p}p
experiments.
The traditional method for producing antineutron beams is via the charge exchange (CEX) reaction
\bar{p}p\rightarrow\bar{n}n
. This technique has been used in experiments such as BNL E-767 (with antineutron momentum between 100 and 500
{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}
)
BNL
and CERN OBELIX (with antineutron momentum between 50 and 400
{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}
)
OBELIX
. These antineutron beams have enabled important investigations, yielding intriguing results, particularly in strangeness production. For example, a notable violation of the OZI rule was observed in
\bar{n}p\rightarrow\phi\pi^{+}
at antineutron momentum below 300
{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}

OBELIX:1994hbn
, where
\phi
production greatly exceeded expectations. Additionally, a prominent di-kaon decay mode of the
f_{0}(1500)
was observed; this state is a candidate for having a large scalar glueball component.

Although previous experiments have significantly advanced our understanding of antinucleon-nucleon interactions, antineutron scattering data remain limited because of the low production rate of antineutron via the CEX reaction. Recently, a novel method for producing antineutrons and hyperons has been proposed
Yuan
;
Dai:2024myk
, in which the
J/\psi
meson serves as an efficient antineutron source. This approach takes advantage of the appreciable branching fractions of
J/\psi
decays to final states containing antineutrons and the high production cross section of
J/\psi
in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation. Antineutrons produced through
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
decays cover a broad momentum range up to 1174
{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}
, extending the range beyond previous experiments. Using this method, the BESIII Collaboration has measured the cross sections for
\bar{n}p
inelastic scattering into pionic states
PWA
, and has also reported studies of hyperon and antihyperon scattering with nucleons and nuclei
BESIII:2023clq
;
BESIII:2023trh
;
BESIII:2024geh
;
BESIII:2025bft
.

In this Letter, we report the study of
\bar{n}p
inelastic scattering into final states with kaons based on this novel method at the BESIII detector. The detector details are described in Ref.
BESIII
. Antineutrons are produced via
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
with a BF of 0.212(9)%, and tagged by the recoil of the detected
p\pi^{-}
particles, while the target protons originate from the beam pipe. The beam pipe consists of a gold layer (14.4
\mu
m), two beryllium layers (0.8 and 0.6 mm) and an oil layer (0.8 mm) sandwiched between the beryllium layers. The excellent particle identification and the reaction vertex reconstruction with a resolution of about 1 mm
Xu:2009zzg
, make the BESIII detector well-suited for studying
\bar{n}p
scattering involving kaon production. This analysis focuses on the scattering between antineutrons and protons (
{}^{1}\rm{H}
) in the oil layer. For simplicity, only the cross sections of the reactions
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
and
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
are measured.

The signal MC sample of

J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}

The signal event selection begins with the tagging of the antineutron from the decay
J/\psi\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{n}

\theta

|\cos\theta|<0.93

\bar{n}p
scattering, the distance of closest approach to the interaction point (IP) must be less than 10 cm along the
z

E

\mathcal{L}(h)~(h=p,K,\pi)
for each hadron
h

To better distinguish particles from
J/\psi

\bar{n}p
scattering, a vertex fit is performed on the
p\pi^{-}

\chi^{2}

p\pi^{-}

RM(p\pi^{-})

p_{p}

are the four momenta of the initial

system, the proton and the pion before kinematic fit, respectively.

After tagging the antineutron, we reconstruct
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}(\pi^{0})
among the remaining charged tracks and photon candidates. Charged tracks from
\bar{n}p
scattering are required to be within the polar angle range of
|\cos\theta|<0.93
, with no requirements applied on the distance of closest approach to the IP. After the PID requirement, events are selected if the remaining tracks are consistent with
K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
. Photon candidates are identified using isolated showers in the electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC). The deposited energy of each shower must be more than 25 MeV in the barrel region (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
) and more than 50 MeV in the end cap region (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
). To exclude showers that originate from charged tracks, the angle subtended by the EMC shower and the position of the closest charged track at the EMC must be greater than 10 degrees as measured from the IP. To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within [0, 700] ns.
Neutral pion candidates are reconstructed via
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
with
0.115{\mathrm{\ Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}/c^{2}}<M(\gamma\gamma)<0.150{\mathrm{\ Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}/c^{2}}
, and a one-constraint kinematic fit is applied to constrain the invariant mass of the photon pair to the nominal
\pi^{0}
mass
pdg
. If multiple
\pi^{0}
candidates are reconstructed, the one with the smallest
\chi^{2}
is selected. To suppress non-antineutron background, the
\chi^{2}
of the one-constraint kinematic fit on
p\pi^{-}
is required to be less than 11. A vertex fit is performed on the
K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
combination, with its
\chi^{2}
required to be less than 6; the resulting vertex position is taken as the
\bar{n}p
scattering point
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})
. Both
\chi^{2}
thresholds are optimized based on the figure of merit,
S/\sqrt{S+B}
for the
\bar{n}p\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
channel, where
S
is the number of events in the signal MC after all selections and
S+B
is determined from the inclusive MC sample.

In summary, we report the cross section measurements of the reactions
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
and
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
, utilizing a novel method of antineutron production at the BESIII detector. Antineutrons are produced by
J/\psi\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
with momentum from 0 to 1174
{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}
and the target protons are provided by the hydrogen of the cooling oil in the beam pipe. The cross sections for
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
and
\bar{n}p\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
are measured to be
0.53^{+0.15}_{-0.12}\pm 0.08
mb and
1.09^{+0.36}_{-0.30}\pm 0.31
mb respectively, where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second systematic. For reference, the cross section of the isospin-symmetric process
p\bar{p}\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
has been measured to be
0.347\pm 0.037
mb at
900{\mathrm{\ Me\kern-1.00006ptV}/c}

CrystalBarrel:2006mhj
. Due to the limited statistics, differential cross section measurements in individual momentum bins are not feasible, so a direct comparison cannot be made. Nevertheless, the cross section of
p\bar{p}\rightarrow K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
provides a useful benchmark for our measurements.
Although limited statistics prevent a detailed study of intermediate states, the observed pure
\bar{n}p
scattering samples offer promising opportunities for future hadron spectroscopy studies with larger data samples. Proposed future
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders in the tau-charm energy region, such as STCF and SCTF
stcf
;
sctf
