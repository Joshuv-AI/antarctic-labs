Curved DG Modules and Matrix Factorizations from Noncommutative Quadric Hypersurfaces
Peter Goetz

Abstract
. The category of noncommutative quadratic quadric hypersurfaces, 𝚀𝚞𝚊𝚍 ​ - ​ 𝚀𝙷𝚂 {\tt Quad}\text{-}{\tt QHS} , consists of pairs ( A , f ) (A,f) , where A A is a quadratic algebra and f ∈ A f\in A is a nonzero degree 2 2 element. We associate to such ( A , f ) (A,f) a pair ( A ¯ ! , f ! ) (\bar{A}^{!},f^{!}) , and show that this association makes 𝚀𝚞𝚊𝚍 ​ - ​ 𝚀𝙷𝚂 {\tt Quad}\text{-}{\tt QHS} into a category with duality. We construct a faithful functor from the category of graded modules over A ¯ ! \bar{A}^{!} to the homotopy category of curved DG modules over a canonical curved DG algebra ( A ⊗ A ¯ ! , d , f ⊗ f ! ) (A\otimes\bar{A}^{!},d,f\otimes f^{!}) . If A A satisfies the left strong rank condition and f ∈ A f\in A is not a right zero divisor, we show that the restriction of our functor to a natural full subcategory of the category of graded modules over A ¯ ! \bar{A}^{!} is valued in a stable category of noncommutative matrix factorizations of f f . When A A is Koszul of finite global dimension and f ∈ A f\in A is normal and regular, we prove that the even Clifford algebra, A ¯ ! [ ( f ! ) − 1 ] 0 \bar{A}^{!}[(f^{!})^{-1}]_{0} , is isomorphic to a canonical PBW-deformation of a Zhang twist of the 2 2 -Veronese subalgebra of the Koszul dual A ! A^{!} . Finally, we study several classes of Artin-Schelter regular algebras to illustrate our results.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The notion of a
matrix factorization
of an element in a commutative ring was introduced by Eisenbud,
[
9
]
. If
A
is a commutative ring and
f\in A
, a matrix factorization of
f
is a pair of maps
\phi:F\to G
,
\psi:G\to F
between free
A
-modules such that
\psi\phi=f\operatorname{id}_{F}
and
\phi\psi=f\operatorname{id}_{G}
. In the case when
A
is a regular local ring and
f\in A
,
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
shows that matrix factorizations are intimately related to periodic resolutions and maximal Cohen-Macaulay (MCM) modules over the hypersurface ring
A/(f)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. In
[
4
]
, Buchweitz-Eisenbud-Herzog considered the case of a commutative polynomial ring
A=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and a quadratic form
f\in A
. They proved,
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, there is an equivalence of categories from the category of graded MCM
A/(f)
-modules without free summands to the category of modules over the
even Clifford algebra

C_{0}(f)
. More generally, in
[
4
, Appendix]
Buchweitz explained that this equivalence is closely related to the BGG-correspondence associated to a pair of graded Gorenstein noetherian Koszul dual algebras
(A,A^{!})
, also see
[
5
, Theorem 4.4.1, Theorem C.1.3]
.

Many researchers have built upon the works
[
5
,
4
,
9
]
and extended these ideas to noncommutative settings. Of relevance to this paper, we were motivated by
[
6
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
]
. We summarize some of the main results in these works as follows. Let
A
be a connected graded noetherian
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, let
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}A
and
{\tt Tors}\text{-}A
denote the category of graded right
A
-modules and the category of graded torsion right
A
-modules, respectively. Then the
noncommutative projective scheme
associated to
A
is by definition the Serre quotient

{\tt Tails}\,A:=\dfrac{{\tt GrMod}\text{-}A}{{\tt Tors}\text{-}A}.

The
global dimension
of
{\tt Tails}\,A
is defined as

\gldim({\tt Tails}\,A):=\sup\big\{i:\Ext^{i}_{{\tt Tails}\,A}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\neq 0\text{ for some }\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in{\tt Tails}\,A\big\}.

The category
{\tt Tails}\,A
is
smooth
if it has finite global dimension.

Smith-Van den Bergh studied the case where
A
is a connected graded noetherian Gorenstein Koszul algebra of finite global dimension and
f\in A_{2}
is a
central regular
element. Letting
\bar{A}=A/\langle f\rangle
,
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
states an equivalence from a stable category of graded MCM modules over
\bar{A}
to the bounded derived category of
{\tt Tails}\,\bar{A}^{!}
. Furthermore, there is an equivalence
{\rm D}^{\rm b}({\tt Tails}\,\bar{A}^{!})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}({\tt mod}\text{-}C(A))
, where
C(A)
is a finite-dimensional algebra analogous to the even Clifford algebra, and moreover, if
C(A)
is semisimple, then
{\tt Tails}\,A
is smooth.

These results were extended by Mori-Ueyama to the case where
f\in A
is only required to be a
normal regular
element. In
[
14
]
they defined a category of
graded noncommutative matrix factorizations
and showed that if
A
is a noetherian Artin-Schelter regular algebra and
f\in A_{d}
is a normal regular element, then there is an equivalence from a stable category of noncommutative matrix factorizations of
f
over
A
to a stable category of graded MCM modules over
\bar{A}
,
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
. In subsequent work,
[
15
]
, they defined the even Clifford algebra
C(A)
when
f\in A_{2}
is normal and regular, and proved an equivalence from the stable category of graded MCM modules over
\bar{A}
to the bounded derived category of finitely generated modules over
C(A)
,
[
15
, Lemma 4.13]
. Furthermore, they proved that the algebra
C(A)
is semisimple if and only if the category
{\tt Tails}\,\bar{A}
is smooth. To give a more precise statement of this result, recall that Ueyama,
[
21
, Definition 2.2]
, defines an algebra
A
to be a
graded isolated singularity
if
{\tt Tails}\,A
is smooth.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
15
, Theorem 5.5]
)
.

Let
A
be a quadratic noetherian Artin-Schelter regular algebra of global dimension
n
and let
f\in A_{2}
be a normal regular element. Let
\bar{A}=A/\langle f\rangle
and let
C(A)=\bar{A}^{!}[(f^{!})^{-1}]_{0}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\bar{A}
is a graded isolated singularity.

(2)

\bar{A}
has finite Cohen-Macaulay representation type.

(3)

C(A)
is semisimple.

He-Ye,
[
11
]
, studied the even Clifford algebra
C(A)
in the case where
A
is a noetherian Koszul Artin-Schelter regular algebra and
f\in A_{2}
is a
central
regular element. They proved that
C(A)
is isomorphic to the degree
0
part of a certain
Clifford deformation
of the Koszul dual algebra
A^{!}
.

Before discussing the main contributions of this paper, we draw attention to two observations regarding the above results. First, notice that the domains of all of the above equivalence functors are either stable categories of MCMs or categories of noncommmutative matrix factorizations. If one desires to actually construct MCMs or noncommutative matrix factorizations one could, in theory, write down quasi-inverses of these functors, however, this does not seem to be so easy to do. Second, most of the above results, at a minimum, require
A
to be a Koszul algebra as input.

In this paper, we work in greater generality and consider any quadratic algebra
A
and any nonzero element
f\in A_{2}
. We refer to such a pair
(A,f)
as a
noncommutative quadratic quadric hypersurfaces
. We associate to such
(A,f)
a canonical curved differential graded algebra
T^{\bullet}
. One of our main results, see Theorem
1.3
below, constructs a canonical faithful functor from the graded module category over
\bar{A}^{!}
to the homotopy category of curved differential graded modules over the curved differential graded algebra
T^{\bullet}
. We show that the restriction of our functor to a natural full subcategory produces graded noncommutative matrix factorizations of
f
over
A
in the sense of
[
14
]
. We also generalize the above mentioned result in
[
11
]
regarding the even Clifford algebra. We show that when
A
is a noetherian Koszul algebra of finite global dimension and
f\in A_{2}
is normal and regular, then the even Clifford algebra is isomorphic to a canonical PBW-deformation of a Zhang twist of the
2
-Veronese subalgebra of the quadratic dual algebra
A^{!}
, see Theorem
5.9
.

\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{Z}
-graded,

If

and

for all

. We will also denote the right hand side of the last equation either by

of

2.2.
Associative algebras

In this paper, the term
algebra
refers to an associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, and
graded algebra
refers to a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, locally finite-dimensional, associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. A
connected graded algebra
is an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded algebra
A
such that
A_{0}=\mathbb{K}
. The
tensor algebra
on the vector space
V
is denoted by
T(V)
. We always consider
T(V)
as an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded algebra in the usual way; that is,
V
is considered as the degree
1
component of
T(V)
. If
A
is a graded algebra and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
d
-Veronese
of
A
is the subalgebra

A_{(d)}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{di}

of
A
. Let
{\tt GrAlg}
denote the category of graded algebras with degree-preserving algebra morphisms. We say that a graded algebra
A
is
one-generated
if the natural map
\pi_{A}:T(A_{1})\to A
is surjective. Let
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
denote the full subcategory of
{\tt GrAlg}
consisting of all one-generated algebras. For any morphism
\varphi:A\to B
in
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
, there is the induced morphism
\varphi:T(A_{1})\to T(B_{1})
, also in
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
. Moreover, it is clear that
\varphi(\ker\pi_{A})\subset\ker\pi_{B}
. In fact, the association
A\to T(A_{1})
yields a functor
T:{\tt GrAlg}^{1}\to{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
. For the purposes of this paper, there is no harm or loss in generality in assuming that the objects in
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
are explicitly presented in the form
T(V)/I
for some finite-dimensional vector space
V
and some ideal
I
of
T(V)
. Furthermore, a morphism
T(V)/I\to T(W)/J
in
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
determines, and is determined by, a morphism
T(V)\to T(W)
in
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
carrying
I
into
J
. An algebra
T(V)/I\in{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
is a
quadratic algebra
if the ideal
I\subset T(V)
is generated by its degree
2
component
I_{2}
. We let
{\tt Quad}
denote the full subcategory of
{\tt GrAlg}^{1}
consisting of all quadratic algebras. We will denote objects of
{\tt Quad}
as
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
, with the implication that
I_{A}
is a subspace of
V\otimes V
.

If
A
is a graded algebra and
f\in A
, then
f
is
normal
if
fA=Af
. The element
f\in A
is
regular
if
f
is neither a left nor a right zero-divisor in
A
. We will say that
f\in A
is
1-regular
if both left and right multiplication by
f
on
A_{1}
is injective. If
f
is both normal and regular, then the
normalizing automorphism of
f
is the algebra isomorphism
\varphi_{f}:A\to A
determined by
fa=\varphi_{f}(a)f
for all
a\in A
. When
A
is one-generated and
f\in A
is normal, regular and homogeneous, then the normalizing automorphism of
f
is graded and is completely determined by its restriction to
A_{1}
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. For any graded algebra automorphism
\zeta
of
A
, the
Zhang twist
of the algebra
A
by the automorphism
\zeta
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A^{\zeta}
given by
A^{\zeta}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces with multiplication given by

x\ast y=x\zeta^{n}(y)\quad\text{for all }\,x\in A_{n},\,y\in A.

We refer to
[
23
]
for more details.

2.3.
Modules

If
A
is an algebra, then
A\text{-}\tt{Mod}
denotes the category of left
A
-modules. The categories
A\text{-}\tt{mod}
and
A\text{-}\tt{fdim}
are the full subcategories of
A\text{-}\tt{Mod}
consisting of finitely generated modules, respectively, finite-dimensional modules. We use corresponding notation for right
A
-module categories:
{\tt{Mod}}\text{-}A
,
{\tt{mod}}\text{-}A
and
{\tt{fdim}}\text{-}A
. If
M\in A\text{-}\tt{Mod}
and
a\in A
, then
\lambda_{a}:M\to M
denotes left multiplication by
a
. Similarly, if
M
is a right
A
-module, then
\rho_{a}:M\to M
denotes right multiplication by
a\in A
. If
A
is noetherian (meaning left and right noetherian), then the
global dimension of
A
is denoted by
\gldim A
. Recall that
A
is said to satisfy the
left strong rank condition
if whenever
A^{m}\to A^{n}
is a monomorphism of finitely generated free left
A
-modules, then
m\leq n
; see
[
12
, Section 1.1]
. It is well known that every left noetherian algebra satisfies the left strong rank condition.

If
A
is a graded algebra, we denote by
A\text{-}{\tt GrMod}
the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded left
A
-modules, with degree-preserving morphisms. We have the following full subcategories of
A\text{-}{\tt GrMod}
:
A\text{-}\tt{grmod}
consists of all finitely generated graded modules, and
A\text{-}\tt{grfdim}
consists of all finite-dimensional graded modules. For categories of graded right
A
-modules we use corresponding notation:
{\tt{GrMod}}\text{-}A
,
{\tt{grmod}}\text{-}A
and
{\tt{grfdim}}\text{-}A
.

For
M\in A\text{-}{\tt GrMod}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
M(i)
the degree-shifted graded left
A
-module defined by
M(i)_{n}=M_{n+i}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
graded Hom functor
for graded left
A
-modules is given by

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A\text{-}{\tt GrMod}}(M,N(i)).

This functor is left exact, and its
i
-th right derived functor is denoted
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,N)
. The space
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,N)
inherits a grading from the graded Hom functor, and we denote the homogeneous degree
j
component by
\Ext^{i,j}_{A}(M,N)
. One may compute the space
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,N)
as the homology group
H^{i}(P_{\bullet},N)
where
(P_{\bullet},d_{\bullet})
is a graded free resolution of
M
. Thus

\Ext_{A}(M,N)=\bigoplus_{i}\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,N)=\bigoplus_{i,j}\Ext^{i,j}_{A}(M,N)

is bigraded.
When
A
is a connected graded algebra and
M=N=\mathbb{K}
is the trivial module, the vector space
E(A)=\Ext_{A}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{K})
admits the structure of a bigraded algebra via the Yoneda composition product, see
[
17
, p. 4]
for more details.
The algebra
E(A)
is called the
Yoneda algebra
of
A
.

2.4.
Quadratic and Koszul algebras

Let
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
be a quadratic algebra. Then
I_{A}^{\perp}
, the orthogonal complement of
I_{A}
, is a subspace of
V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
. The
quadratic dual
of

is the quadratic algebra
A^{!}:=T(V^{*})/\langle I_{A}^{\perp}\rangle
. If
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
and
B=T(W)/\langle I_{B}\rangle
, and
\varphi:A\to B
is a morphism in
{\tt Quad}
, then there is a natural morphism
\varphi^{!}:B^{!}\to A^{!}
in
{\tt Quad}
induced by the
\mathbb{K}
-linear dual map
\varphi^{*}:W^{*}\to V^{*}
, and of course
\varphi^{*}:T(W^{*})\to T(V^{*})
carries
\langle(I_{B})^{\perp}\rangle
into
\langle(I_{A})^{\perp}\rangle
.

Recall that a connected graded algebra
A
is
Koszul
if the trivial module
\mathbb{K}
admits a resolution in
A\text{-}{\tt GrMod}

\cdots\to P_{3}\to P_{2}\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to\mathbb{K}\to 0

such that each
P_{i}
is a graded free left
A
-module generated in degree
i
. It is immediate from the definition that every Koszul algebra is quadratic.
It is well known that
A
is Koszul if and only if the canonical homomorphism
A^{!}\to E(A)
is an algebra isomorphism.

2.5.
Curved algebras and modules

In this section we discuss the notions of curved differential graded algebras and modules. Following
[
17
]
, a
curved differential graded algebra
(
CDG-algebra
) is a triple
B^{\bullet}=(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})
consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
, an odd derivation of degree
1

d_{B}:B\to B,\quad d_{B}(xy)=d_{B}(x)y+(-1)^{|x|}xd_{B}(y)\quad\text{for all homogeneous }x,y\in B,

and an element

\theta_{B}\in B_{2}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ d_{B}(\theta_{B})=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ d_{B}^{2}(x)=[\theta_{B},x]=\theta_{B}x-x\theta_{B}\ \ \text{for all }x\in B.

The element
\theta_{B}
is called the
curvature element
. There is a simple way to produce examples of CDG-algebras.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
B=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and let
b\in B_{1}
. Define
d_{b}:B\to B
by
d_{b}(x)=bx-(-1)^{|x|}xb
for all homogeneous
x\in B
. Then the triple
(B,d_{b},b^{2})
is a CDG-algebra.

Proof.

Let
c=b^{2}
. It is routine to check that
d_{b}
is an odd derivation of degree
1
, and clearly
d_{b}(c)=0
. We will check that
d_{b}^{2}(x)=[c,x]
. First note that
d_{b}(b)=2b^{2}=2c
. For homogeneous
x\in B
we have

\displaystyle d_{b}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=d_{b}(bx-(-1)^{|x|}xb)

\displaystyle=d_{b}(bx)-(-1)^{|x|}d_{b}(xb)

\displaystyle=d_{b}(b)x-bd_{b}(x)-(-1)^{|x|}(d_{b}(x)b+(-1)^{|x|}d_{b}(b))

\displaystyle=2cx-b(bx-(-1)^{|x|}xb)-(-1)^{|x|}(bx-(-1)^{|x|}xb)b-2xc

\displaystyle=[c,x].

∎

A
morphism of CDG-algebras
is
(f,c):(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})\to(C,d_{C},\theta_{C})
such that
f:B\to C
is a graded algebra morphism and
c\in C_{1}
, satisfying

\displaystyle f(d_{B}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{C}(f(x))+\{c,f(x)\}

\displaystyle f(\theta_{B})

\displaystyle=\theta_{C}+d_{C}(c)+c^{2},

where
\{x,y\}=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
. We note that the construction in Proposition
2.1
can be made into a functor. If
f:B\to C
is a graded algebra morphism such that
f(b)=c
for
b\in B_{1}
, then
(f,0):(B,d_{b},b^{2})\to(C,d_{c},c^{2})
is easily checked to be a morphism of CDG-algebras. It is clear that this correspondence between pairs
(B,b)
and the CDG-algebra
(B,d_{b},b^{2})
determines a faithful covariant functor.

Now we define curved modules over a CDG-algebra. A
right curved differential graded module
(
right CDG-module
) over the CDG-algebra
(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})
is a pair
(N,d_{N})
consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
B
-module
N
and an odd derivation of degree
1
,
d_{N}:N\to N
:

d_{N}(nx)=d_{N}(n)x+(-1)^{|n|}nd_{B}(x)\quad\text{for all homogeneous }n\in N,x\in B,

such that
d^{2}_{N}(n)=-n\theta_{B}
for all
n\in N
.

The next result constructs CDG-modules over the CDG-algebras defined in Proposition
2.1
. The proof is straightforward and left to the reader.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, let
b\in B_{1}
, and consider the CDG-algebra
(B,d_{b},b^{2})
defined in Proposition
2.1
. Let
N\in{\tt GrMod}\text{-}B
, and define a map
d_{N,b}:N\to N
by
d_{N,b}(n)=(-1)^{|n|+1}nb
for all homogeneous
n\in N
. Then the pair
(N,d_{N,b})
is a right CDG-module over
(B,d_{b},b^{2})
.

Proof.

This is straightforward.
∎

Let
(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})
be a CDG-algebra. We now describe the
DG category of right CDG-modules over
B^{\bullet}=(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})
. This DG category will be notated as
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}B^{\bullet}
. A basic general reference for DG categories is
[
22
, Chapter 3]
. Let
(N_{1},d_{N_{1}})
and
(N_{2},d_{N_{2}})
be objects in
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}B^{\bullet}
. Since
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
B
-modules we may consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\tt GrMod}\text{-}B}(N_{1},N_{2}(i)).

Then it is well known that
\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})
is a DG vector space with degree
1
differential
d:\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{i}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{i+1}
given by

[d(\phi)](n_{1})=d_{N_{2}}\phi(n_{1})-(-1)^{i}\phi d_{N_{1}}(n_{1})\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{i},\ n_{1}\in N_{1}.

One can easily check that
d^{2}=0
.

We will denote the
homotopy category of right CDG modules over
(B,d_{B},\theta_{B})
by
{\tt{Ho}}({\tt{CDGMod}}\text{-}B^{\bullet})
. This is the category with the same objects as
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}B^{\bullet}
with morphism sets equal to
H^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2}))
. In more detail, morphisms in
{\tt{Ho}}({\tt{CDGMod}}\text{-}B^{\bullet})
are cochain maps of degree
0
,
\phi:N_{1}\to N_{2}
, up to coboundaries of
d
. We will also write

\displaystyle Z^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2}))=\ker(d\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{0}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{1}),

\displaystyle B^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2}))=\operatorname{im}(d\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{-1}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N_{1},N_{2})_{0}).

See
[
22
, Definition 3.4.6]
for more details.

3.
Categories of noncommutative quadratic and Koszul quadric hypersurfaces

Given a quadratic algebra
A
and
f\in A_{2}
, it is well known that there is a canonical quadratic element
f^{!}\in(A/\langle f\rangle)^{!}
, unique up to an element of
\mathbb{K}^{*}
, such that
(A/\langle f\rangle)^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle\cong A^{!}
, see
[
17
,
19
]
. In this section we make this correspondence more precise as a functorial construction. To do this, we introduce two categories and then prove that they are categories with duality.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
denote the category whose objects are pairs
(A,f)
, where
A
is a quadratic algebra and
0\neq f\in A_{2}
. By definition, a morphism in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
, say
(A,f)\to(B,g)
, is a morphism
\varphi:A\to B
in
{\tt GrAlg}
such that
\varphi(f)=g
. We call
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
the
category of noncommutative quadratic quadric hypersurfaces
.

Construction 3.2
.

We construct a contravariant functor

(\ )^{!}:{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}\to{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}

as follows.
First, we define
(\ )^{!}:{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}\to{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
on objects. Consider some pair
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
, where
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
and
0\neq f\in A_{2}
. Choose any
F\in V\otimes V
such that
f=F+I_{A}
, and consider the subspace
I_{A}+\mathbb{K}F
of
V\otimes V
. Note that this sum is direct and the subspace does not depend on the choice of
F
. We let
\bar{A}=T(V)/\langle I_{A}+\mathbb{K}F\rangle
, and define
I_{\bar{A}}=I_{A}+\mathbb{K}F
. The quadratic dual of
\bar{A}
is
\bar{A}^{!}=T(V^{*})/\langle(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}\rangle
. It is not hard to check that the map defined by

(3.1)

\psi_{(A,f)}:\bar{A}^{!}_{2}\to I_{\bar{A}}^{*},\quad[\psi_{(A,f)}(t+(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp})](r)=t(r)

is a vector space isomorphism. Define the linear functional
\phi_{(A,f)}\in I_{\bar{A}}^{*}
by

(3.2)

\phi_{(A,f)}(I_{A})=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \phi_{(A,f)}(F)=1,

and note that
\phi_{A,f}
does not depend on the choice of
F\in V\otimes V
made above. Let

f^{!}=\psi^{-1}_{(A,f)}(\phi_{(A,f)})\in\bar{A}^{!}_{2},

then one checks that
f^{!}
only depends on
f
, so the notation
f^{!}
is well-chosen. Noting that, indeed,
(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})\in{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
, we define

(A,f)^{!}=(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!}).

Now we define
(\ )^{!}:{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}\to{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
on morphisms. Let
(A,f)
,
(B,g)
be objects in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
with
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
,
0\neq f\in A_{2}
and
B=T(W)/\langle I_{B}\rangle
,
0\neq g\in B_{2}
. Then we may consider the pairs
(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
and
(\bar{B}^{!},g^{!})
. Suppose that we have some morphism
\varphi:(A,f)\to(B,g)
in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
. Since
\varphi:A\to B
maps
f
to
g
, it is clear that we have an induced morphism
\bar{\varphi}:\bar{A}\to\bar{B}
. So, taking quadratic duals, we have a morphism
\bar{\varphi}^{!}:\bar{B}^{!}\to\bar{A}^{!}
. Now we will check that
\bar{\varphi}^{!}
maps
g^{!}
to
f^{!}
. By assumption,
\varphi(f)=g
, so it follows that if we have chosen
F\in V\otimes V
and
G\in W\otimes W
such that
f=F+I_{A}
and
g=G+I_{B}
, then we have
\varphi(F)-G\in I_{B}
. Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{(\bar{B})^{!}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{(B,g)}}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\varphi}^{!}}
\textstyle{(I_{\bar{B}})^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{(\bar{A})^{!}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{(A,f)}}
\textstyle{(I_{\bar{A}})^{*}.}

The rightmost vertical map,
\rho:(I_{\bar{B}})^{*}\to(I_{\bar{A}})^{*}
, is the
\mathbb{K}
-linear dual of the map
\overline{\varphi}\otimes\overline{\varphi}:I_{\bar{A}}\to I_{\bar{B}}
. A straightforward unwinding of the definitions shows that this diagram is commutative. It is easy to check that
\rho(\phi_{(B,g)})=\phi_{(A,f)}
as linear functionals on
I_{\bar{A}}
, so using commutativity of the diagram we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{!}(g^{!})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{!}(\psi^{-1}_{(B,g)}(\phi_{(B,g)}))

\displaystyle=\psi^{-1}_{(A,f)}\rho(\phi_{(B,g)})

\displaystyle=\psi^{-1}_{(A,f)}(\phi_{(A,f)})

\displaystyle=f^{!}.

Hence we have the morphism
\varphi^{!}:(B,g)^{!}\to(A,f)^{!}
in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
.

It is obvious that the definition of
(\ )^{!}
on
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
respects identity maps, composition of morphisms, and reverses arrows, so

(\ )^{!}:{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}\to{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}

is a contravariant functor. This completes the construction.

Recall the notion of a
category with duality
, see
[
1
, Definition 1]
. This is a triple
({\tt C},\ast,\eta)
consisting of a category
\tt C
, a contravariant functor
\ast:{\tt C}\to{\tt C}
and a natural isomorphism
\eta:{\rm Id}_{\tt C}\to\ast\circ\ast
, such that for all objects
M\in{\tt C}
,

(\eta_{M})^{\ast}\circ\eta_{M^{\ast}}={\rm id}_{M^{\ast}}.

For example, consider the triple
({\tt FinVect},\ast,\eta)
, where
{\tt FinVect}
is the full subcategory of
{\tt Vect}
consisting of finite-dimensional vector spaces, the functor
*:{\tt FinVect}\to{\tt FinVect}
is given by
*(V)=V^{*}
, the usual
\mathbb{K}
-linear dual of
V
, and
\eta_{V}:V\to(V^{*})^{*}
is the natural map given by sending
v\in V
to the linear functional on
V^{*}
given by evaluation at
v
. Then it is easy to check that
({\tt FinVect},\ast,\eta)
is a category with duality.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\eta:{\rm Id}_{{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}}\to(\ )^{!}\circ(\ )^{!}
be the natural isomorphism whose component at
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
is induced by the natural isomorphism
A\to(A^{!})^{!}
. Then the triple
({\tt Quad\text{-}QHS},(\ )^{!},\eta)
is a category with duality.

Proof.

Let
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
be a quadratic algebra. Then

(A^{!})^{!}=T((V^{*})^{*})/\langle(I_{A}^{\perp})^{\perp}\rangle,

where
(I_{A}^{\perp})^{\perp}
is considered as a subspace of
(V^{*})^{*}\otimes(V^{*})^{*}
. It is well known that the algebra map
\eta_{A}:A\to(A^{!})^{!}
induced by the canonical map
V\to(V^{*})^{*}
is an isomorphism, and, moreover, defines a natural isomorphism
\eta:{\rm Id}_{\tt Quad}\to(\ )^{!}\circ(\ )^{!}
. Now consider some pair
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
. We will now prove that the isomorphism
\eta_{A}:A\to(A^{!})^{!}
in
{\tt Quad}
yields an isomorphism
\eta_{(A,f)}:(A,f)\to((A,f)^{!})^{!}
in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
. Recall that
(A,f)^{!}=(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
, and choose some
F^{!}\in V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
such that
f^{!}=F^{!}+(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}
. Then it is easy to see that we have
(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}+\mathbb{K}F^{!}=I_{A}^{\perp}
as subspaces of
V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
, hence

\overline{(\bar{A}^{!})}=T(V^{*})/\langle(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}+\mathbb{K}F^{!}\rangle=T(V^{*})/\langle I_{A}^{\perp}\rangle=A^{!}.

Therefore we see that
(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})^{!}=((A^{!})^{!},(f^{!})^{!})
. Now we will show that the morphism
\eta_{A}:A\to(A^{!})^{!}
sends
f
to
(f^{!})^{!}
. Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{A_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lambda}
\textstyle{(A^{!})^{!}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{(I_{A}^{\perp})^{*},}

where the maps are given by

\psi:(A^{!})^{!}_{2}\to(I_{A}^{\perp})^{*},\quad\psi(t+(I_{A}^{\perp})^{\perp})(r)=t(r)\text{ for all }t\in(V^{*})^{*}\otimes(V^{*})^{*}\text{ and }r\in I_{A}^{\perp};

and

\lambda:A_{2}\to(I_{A}^{\perp})^{*},\quad\lambda(X+I_{A})(r)=r(X)\text{ for all }X\in V\otimes V\text{ and }r\in I_{A}^{\perp}.

One can easily check that these maps are well-defined, that
\psi
is the vector space isomorphism defined above, and that the diagram commutes. Furthermore, we can see that
\lambda(f)=\phi_{(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})}
, where
\phi_{(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})}=\psi((f^{!})^{!})
is the functional defined above, so it follows that
\eta_{A}(f)=(f^{!})^{!}
. Thus, in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
we have the isomorphism
\eta_{(A,f)}:(A,f)\to((A,f)^{!})^{!}
, induced by
A\to(A^{!})^{!}
. It is clear that
\eta_{(A,f)}
is natural in
(A,f)
, whence we have a natural isomorphism of functors
\eta:{\rm Id}_{{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}}\to(\ )^{!}\circ(\ )^{!}
.

Finally, the required identity

(\eta_{(A,f)})^{!}\circ\eta_{(A,f)^{!}}={\rm id}_{(A,f)^{!}}

boils down to the fact that the analogous identity

(\eta_{V})^{*}\circ\eta_{V^{*}}={\rm id}_{V^{*}}

holds in the triple
({\tt FinVect},\ast,\eta)
, as discussed above.
∎

We now begin to narrow our focus to a certain full subcategory of
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
consisting of pairs of Koszul algebras and quadratic normal regular elements. This category is defined below, Definition
3.7
, and will be a main object of consideration in the following sections when we study matrix factorizations.

Given some
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
, we first consider the relationship between the conditions of normality and regularity for the elements
f\in A
and
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
. We have the following interesting result.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
19
, Lemma 1.3]
)
.

The element
f\in A
is normal if and only if the element
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
is 1-regular.

We make this more precise in the following.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
be a quadratic algebra. Suppose that
f\in A_{2}
is normal and
1
-regular, and let
\varphi:V\to V
be the unique invertible linear map such that
fv=\varphi(v)f
for all
v\in V
. Then the element
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}_{2}
is normal and
1
-regular. Moreover,
f^{!}\lambda=(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)f^{!}
for all
\lambda\in V^{*}
, where
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}
is the
\mathbb{K}
-linear dual of
\varphi^{-1}
.

Proof.

The statement that
f^{!}
is normal and
1
-regular is proved in
[
19
, Lemma 1.3]
. However, note that for the implication

f\text{ 1-regular }\implies f^{!}\text{ normal},

we can use our result above that
\eta_{(A,f)}:(A,f)\to((A^{!})^{!},(f^{!})^{!})
is an isomorphism in
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
.

We proceed to prove the moreover statement. Fix a choice of
F\in V\otimes V
such that
f=F+I_{A}
. Let
I_{\bar{A}}=I_{A}+\mathbb{K}F
and
\bar{A}=T(V)/\langle I_{\bar{A}}\rangle
. Fix a choice of
F^{!}\in V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
such that
f^{!}=F^{!}+(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}
. Let
\phi=\phi_{(A,f)}\in(I_{\bar{A}})^{*}
be as above.

We first claim that the map

\psi:=\phi\otimes 1-\varphi^{-1}\otimes\phi:I_{\bar{A}}\otimes V\cap V\otimes I_{\bar{A}}\to V

is the zero map. To see this, let
r\in I_{\bar{A}}\otimes V\cap V\otimes I_{\bar{A}}
and write

r=r_{1}+F\otimes v_{1}=r_{2}+v_{2}\otimes F

for some
r_{1}\in I_{A}\otimes V
,
r_{2}\in V\otimes I_{A}
;
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
. Then
\psi(r)=v_{1}-\varphi^{-1}(v_{2})
.

By assumption,

v_{2}\otimes F\equiv F\otimes\varphi^{-1}(v_{2})\pmod{V\otimes I_{A}+I_{A}\otimes V}.

Since
F\otimes v_{1}-v_{2}\otimes F=r_{2}-r_{1}\in V\otimes I_{A}+I_{A}\otimes V
, we have

F\otimes(v_{1}-\varphi^{-1}(v_{2}))\equiv F\otimes v_{1}-v_{2}\otimes F\equiv 0\pmod{V\otimes I_{A}+I_{A}\otimes V}.

So
f(v_{1}-\varphi^{-1}(v_{2}))=0
in
A
, and so
v_{1}-\varphi^{-1}(v_{2})=0
, whence the claim.

Now we claim that

F^{!}\otimes\lambda-(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)\otimes F^{!}\in(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}\otimes V^{*}+V^{*}\otimes(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda\in V^{*}.

To see this, since

[(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}\otimes V^{*}+V^{*}\otimes(I_{\bar{A}})^{\perp}]^{\perp}=I_{\bar{A}}\otimes V\cap V\otimes I_{\bar{A}},

it is equivalent to show that

r(F^{!}\otimes\lambda-(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)\otimes F^{!})=0\quad\text{ for all }r\in I_{\bar{A}}\otimes V\cap V\otimes I_{\bar{A}}.

Write
r=r_{1}+F\otimes v_{1}=r_{2}+v_{2}\otimes F
as above. Then

\displaystyle r(F^{!}\otimes\lambda-(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)\otimes F^{!})

\displaystyle=\lambda(v_{1})-[(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)](v_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda(v_{1}-\varphi^{-1}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\lambda(0)\quad\text{ by the first claim}

\displaystyle=0.

This proves the second claim, and we see that
f^{!}\lambda=(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(\lambda)f^{!}
in
\bar{A}^{!}
for all
\lambda\in V^{*}
, as desired.
∎

In the case where
A
is a Koszul algebra, we have the following stronger result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose
A=T(V)/\langle I_{A}\rangle
is Koszul and
f\in A_{2}
is normal and regular with normalizing automorphism
\varphi_{f}:A\to A
. Then
f^{!}\in(\bar{A})^{!}_{2}
is normal and regular. Furthermore, the normalizing automorphism
\varphi_{f^{!}}:\bar{A}^{!}\to\bar{A}^{!}
of
f^{!}
is induced by the linear map
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}:V^{*}\to V^{*}
.

Proof.

The statement that
f^{!}\in(\bar{A})^{!}_{2}
is normal and regular is proved in
[
19
, Corollary 1.4]
. The statement that the normalizing automorphism of
f^{!}\in(\bar{A})^{!}_{2}
is induced by
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}:V^{*}\to V^{*}
follows immediately from Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Motivated, in part, by the last result, we now define the promised full subcategory of
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
denote the full subcategory of the category
{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}
whose objects are pairs
(A,f)
, where
A
is a Koszul algebra and
f\in A_{2}
is a normal, regular element. We call
\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
the
category of noncommutative Koszul quadric hypersurfaces
.

Given some
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
, the well-known result that
A/\langle f\rangle
is Koszul, (
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
), implies that the algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
is also Koszul. Hence, using Proposition
3.6
, the contravariant functor

(\ )^{!}:{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}\to{\tt Quad\text{-}QHS}

restricts to a functor
(\ )^{!}:\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}\to\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
. The next result then follows immediately
from Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary 3.8
.

The triple
(\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS},(\ )^{!},\eta)
is a category with duality.

4.
Homotopy category of curved modules and noncommutative matrix factorizations

Suppose that
(A,f)
is a noncommutative quadratic quadric hypersurface. In this section we first construct a functor from the category of graded modules over the algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
to the homotopy category of CDG-modules over a certain CDG-algebra. More precisely, let
T=A\otimes\bar{A}^{!}
and
\theta_{T}=f\otimes f^{!}\in T
. We show that there is a canonical CDG-algebra
T^{\bullet}=(T,d_{T},\theta_{T})
. Then we construct and prove that there is a faithful functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
. We then impose some mild restrictions on the pair
(A,f)
and consider a certain full subcategory, denoted
{\tt P}(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
, of
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
. We then prove that the restriction of the functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
to
{\tt P}(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
is valued in the category of graded left noncommutative matrix factorizations of
f
, in the sense of
[
14
]
.

4.1.
The curved DG algebra associated to
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad}\text{-}{\tt QHS}

Suppose that
(A,f)
is a noncommutative quadratic quadric hypersurface. Consider the tensor product algebra
T=A\otimes\bar{A}^{!}
(so
A
and
\bar{A}^{!}
are commuting subalgebras), and endow

{\mathbb{N}}
-graded algebra structure via

,

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

Proof.

Let

\bar{A}^{!}

\psi_{(A,f)}:\bar{A}^{!}_{2}\to I_{\bar{A}}^{*}

\mu:A\otimes A

\bar{A}^{!}

\textstyle{V\otimes V\otimes V^{*}\otimes V^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{\bar{A}^{!}}}
\scriptstyle{\Omega}
\textstyle{A_{2}\otimes\bar{A}^{!}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V\otimes V,V\otimes V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

and

4.2.
The functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})

We continue to consider the CDG-algebra
T^{\bullet}=(T,d_{T},\theta_{T})
from Proposition
4.2
, where
T=A\otimes\bar{A}^{!}
, and the element
b\in T_{1}
, the map
d_{T}
and
\theta_{T}=f\otimes f^{!}
are as described above. We proceed to construct a functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
.

To define
{\mathcal{F}}
on objects, consider a graded module
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
in
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
. Define the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
{\mathcal{T}}(M)
by

{\mathcal{T}}(M)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{T}}(M)_{i}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A(i)\otimes M_{i}.

The reason for the somewhat strange shifted module
A(i)
will become apparent in Section
4.3
. There is an obvious right action of
T
on
{\mathcal{T}}(M)
and it is clear that this makes
{\mathcal{T}}(M)
into a graded right
T
-module. We define a degree
1
linear map
d_{{\mathcal{T}}(M)}:{\mathcal{T}}(M)\to{\mathcal{T}}(M)
by

d_{{\mathcal{T}}(M)}(a\otimes m)=(-1)^{|m|+1}(a\otimes m)b\quad\text{for all }a\in A,\text{ and homogeneous }m\in M.

Then by Proposition
2.2
, the pair
({\mathcal{T}}(M),d_{{\mathcal{T}}(M)})
is an object of the homotopy category
{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
and we set
{\mathcal{F}}(M)=({\mathcal{T}}(M),d_{{\mathcal{T}}(M)})
.

We wish to explain, from another point of view, that the definition of
d_{{\mathcal{T}}(M)}
is a natural one. Let
\rho_{i}^{M}:M_{i}\otimes V^{*}\to M_{i+1}
be the right action map, and recall the definition of the adjoint map
\Delta(p_{i}^{M}):M_{i}\to V\otimes M_{i+1}
, see subsection
2.1
. We define the map
\phi_{i}^{M}:\mathcal{T}(M)_{i}\to\mathcal{T}(M)_{i+1}
to be the composite

\mathcal{T}(M)_{i}=A(i)\otimes M_{i}\xrightarrow{{\rm id}\otimes\Delta(\rho_{i}^{M})}A(i)\otimes V\otimes M_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\mu_{A}\otimes{\rm id}}A(i+1)\otimes M_{i+1}=\mathcal{T}(M)_{i+1},

where
\mu_{A}:A\otimes V\to A
is multiplication in the algebra
A
. Using equation
2.1
it is easy to check that
\phi_{i}^{M}
is right multiplication by
b\in T_{1}
.

Now we turn to defining
{\mathcal{F}}
on morphisms. Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be in
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
, and suppose that
\psi:M\to M^{\prime}
is a morphism in
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
. We consider the graded map
\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi:{\mathcal{T}}(M)\to{\mathcal{T}}(M^{\prime})
whose
i
th component is
\operatorname{id}_{A(i)}\otimes\psi_{i}:{\mathcal{T}}(M)_{i}\to{\mathcal{T}}(M^{\prime})_{i}
. It is straightforward to check that
\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi\in Z^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{T}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}(M^{\prime})))
, and we define
{\mathcal{F}}(\psi)=[\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi]
to be the class of
\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi
in
H^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{T}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}(M^{\prime})))
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad}\text{-}{\tt QHS}
and let
T^{\bullet}=(T,d_{T},f\otimes f^{!})
be the CDG-algebra given in Proposition
4.2
. The construction above defines a faithful functor

{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}).

Proof.

It is clear that
{\mathcal{F}}
preserves identity morphisms and composition, so
{\mathcal{F}}
is a functor. To check that
{\mathcal{F}}
is faithful, note that both
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
and
{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear categories. Let
\psi:M\to M^{\prime}
be a morphism in
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
and suppose that
{\mathcal{F}}(\psi)=0
in
{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
. This means that there is some map
\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}_{T}({\mathcal{F}}(M),{\mathcal{F}}(M^{\prime}))_{-1}
such that
d\sigma=\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi
. So we have

d_{{\mathcal{F}}(M^{\prime})}\sigma+\sigma d_{{\mathcal{F}}(M)}=\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi.

Evaluating the left hand side of the last equation at
a\otimes m\in A(i)\otimes M_{i}
yields

(-1)^{i}\sigma(a\otimes m)b+\sigma((-1)^{i+1}(a\otimes m)b)=0,

where the last equality holds since
\sigma
preserves the right action of
T
on
{\mathcal{T}}(M)
. Therefore
\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi=0
and
\psi=0
follows since the functor
A\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\underline{\ \ }
is exact. Hence
{\mathcal{F}}
is faithful.
∎

The categories
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
and
{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
are both additive
\mathbb{K}
-linear categories, so the following result follows immediately from the fact that the functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
is faithful.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(A,f)\in{\tt Quad}\text{-}{\tt QHS}
and let
T^{\bullet}=(T,d_{T},f\otimes f^{!})
be the CDG-algebra given above. For

{\mathcal{F}}(M)=0
in
{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
if and only if

in
{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
.

Let
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}
be the full subcategory of
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}
whose objects are the right CDG-modules of the form
(N,d_{N,b})
(as described in Proposition
2.2
), where

is defined as above. Note that the functor
{\mathcal{F}}
from Theorem
4.3
has its values in the category
{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}
so we may consider
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}
. As we will now prove, this functor is left adjoint to a functor
{\mathcal{G}}:{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}\to{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
.

We now proceed to define
{\mathcal{G}}
. For
(N,d_{N,b})\in{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}

{\mathcal{G}}(N,d_{N,b})=\operatorname{Hom}_{T}(T,N)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\tt GrMod}\text{-}T}(T,N(i)).

Then

is endowed with the structure of a graded right

-module as follows. For

To check that

is an adjoint pair of functors, define maps

as in

\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}}({\mathcal{F}}(M),(N,d_{N,b}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\displaystyle[\tau(\phi)(m)](a\otimes a^{!})

\displaystyle=\phi(a\otimes ma^{!})\quad\text{for all }m\in M,a\in A,a^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}

\displaystyle=\psi(m)(a\otimes 1)\quad\text{for all }a\in A,m\in M.

Proposition 4.5
.

As defined above, the functors
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}
and
{\mathcal{G}}:{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}\to{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
form an adjoint pair
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})
of functors.

Question 4.6
.

We have checked that the functor
{\mathcal{G}}:{\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b}\to{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
also induces a functor
{\mathcal{G}}:{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}_{b})\to{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}
. It would interesting if the pair

can be upgraded to an equivalence of categories by passing to some kind of derived categories. Based on noncommutative algebraic geometry, one could try upgrading

to the bounded derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}({\tt Tails}\,\bar{A}^{!})
. It is less clear how one might go about “deriving” the category

since the differential,

, need not square to zero, hence the notion of a quasi-isomorphism of CDG-modules does not make sense. Positselski and Becker have proposed several candidates for derived categories of CDG-modules (or categories of matrix factorizations), see
[
2

18

. So we have the following question. Can the functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt GrMod}\text{-}\bar{A}^{!}\to{\tt Ho}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
be extended to a category equivalence

{\mathcal{F}}:{\rm D}^{\rm b}({\tt Tails}\,\bar{A}^{!})\to{\rm D}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet}),

where
{\rm D}({\tt CDGMod}\text{-}T^{\bullet})
is one of Positselski’s or Becker’s candidates?

If

5.
Noncommutative matrix factorizations from finite-dimensional representations

In this section we consider noncommutative Koszul quadric hypersurfaces and prove three main results. Associated to any
(A,f)\in{\tt Kos}\text{-}{\tt QHS}
, there are two important algebras
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
and
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
, referred to, respectively, as the
Clifford algebra
and the
even Clifford algebra
of the pair
(A,f)
. First, we prove that the construction of the Clifford algebra yields a contravariant functor
{\mathcal{C}l}:\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}\to\tt{GrAlg}
. Second, we use the results of Section 4 to prove that for certain pairs
(A,f)
, there is a faithful functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\tt fdim}\text{-}\mathcal{C}(A,f)\to{\underline{\tt NMF}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{A}(f)
. Finally, we prove that when
A
is a noetherian algebra of finite global dimension, then the even Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
is a PBW-deformation of a Zhang twist of the
2
-Veronese subalgebra of the quadratic dual algebra
A^{!}
.

5.1.
The Clifford algebra and even Clifford algebra associated to
(A,f)

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
. By Proposition
3.6
, the element
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
is normal and regular, so the set
\{(f^{!})^{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is a right Ore set in
\bar{A}^{!}
. Hence, we define the algebra

{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)=\bar{A}^{!}[(f^{!})^{-1}]

to be the associated right ring of fractions.

When the algebra
A
is commutative, the work
[
4
]
, seminal for the study of matrix factorizations on commutative quadrics, reveals important connections between
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
and classical Clifford algebras of quadratic forms. For this reason, we will call
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
the
Clifford algebra
of the pair
(A,f)
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
is naturally
\mathbb{Z}
-graded. For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
i
th graded component of
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
is given by

{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{i}=\big\{x(f^{!})^{-k}:k\in\mathbb{Z},\ x\in\bar{A}^{!}_{i+2k}\big\}.

In fact, by
[
15
, Lemma 4.4]
the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
is strongly graded.

Furthermore, if
(A,f)\to(B,g)
is a morphism in
\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
, then the morphism
(\bar{B}^{!},g^{!})\to(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
induces a canonical morphism
{\mathcal{C}l}(B,g)\to{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
in
\tt{GrAlg}
. It is then straightforward to prove the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

Using the construction above, we have defined a contravariant functor
{\mathcal{C}l}:\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}\to\tt{GrAlg}
.

The degree
0
subalgebra of
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
will be very important in the sequel.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(A,f)={\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{0}
be the degree
0
subalgebra of
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
. We note that
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
is often written as
C(A/\langle f\rangle)
by other authors,
[
14
,
15
,
20
]
. Due to the functorial emphasis of this paper, we use the notation
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
. We will refer to
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
as the
even Clifford algebra
of the pair
(A,f)
.

The following result gives conditions ensuring that the graded pieces of
{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)
are finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
, as well as determining their dimensions. The proof closely follows that of
[
20
, Lemma 5.1(3)]
, but because we need a little more generality we include it.

Proposition 5.4

(
[
20
, Lemma 5.1(3)]
,
[
15
, Lemma 4.7]
)
.

Let
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
with

a noetherian Koszul algebra of finite global dimension. Then for all

the following statements hold:

(1)

{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{i}
is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
,

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}A^{!}_{(2)}
,

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{1}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A^{!}_{2n+1}
, and

(4)

\rho_{f^{!}}\colon{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{i}\to{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{i+2}
is a vector space isomorphism.

Proof.

Statement (4) is obvious since the inverse of
\rho_{f^{!}}
is
\rho_{(f^{!})^{-1}}
. Now we consider (2) and (3). Since
A
is Koszul of finite global dimension the algebra
A^{!}
is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
. Recall that
A^{!}\cong\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle
. Since
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}_{2}
is regular we have an equality in terms of Hilbert series

H_{A^{!}}(t)=(1-t^{2})H_{\bar{A}^{!}}(t).

Since
A^{!}
is finite-dimensional it follows that
\bar{A}_{m+2}^{!}=\bar{A}^{!}_{m}f^{!}
for all

sufficiently large. Note that for all

it is clear that

so we have

Hence, for all

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}l}(A,f)_{1}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{A}^{!}_{2m+1}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A^{!}_{2n+1},

where the second equality follows from the Hilbert series equation. The proof of statement (2) for

Finally, statement (1) is immediate from (2), (3) and (4).

5.3.
The even Clifford algebra as a PBW-deformation

When the algebra
A
has finite global dimension, Proposition
5.4
(2) begs the question whether there is a more precise relationship between the algebras
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
and
A^{!}_{(2)}
. In the case where
f\in A_{2}
is a
central
element, such a relationship was uncovered in
[
11
, Section 5]
in the context of the notion of
Clifford deformations
. The Clifford deformations in
[
11
]
are special cases of more general notions, namely
nonhomogenenous quadratic algebras of PBW type
in the sense of
[
3
]
, and
PBW-deformations
in the sense of
[
7
]
.

Following
[
3
]
, let
V\in{\tt Vect}
be finite-dimensional and let
P
be a subspace of
\mathbb{K}\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes V)
. Then the
nonhomogenous quadratic algebra associated to the pair
(V,P)
is the algebra
Q(V,P)=T(V)/\langle P\rangle
. Consider the canonical filtration on the tensor algebra given by
F^{i}(T(V))=\oplus_{j\leq i}T(V)_{j}
. This induces a filtration on the algebra
Q(V,P)
and we let
{\rm gr\,}Q(V,P)
denote the associated graded algebra. Consider the canonical projection
\pi_{2}:T(V)\to V\otimes V
, and let
R=\pi_{2}(P)
. Then the
quadratic algebra associated to the pair
(V,P)
is the algebra
Q(V,R)=T(V)/\langle R\rangle
. It is clear that there is a natural epimorphism
Q(V,R)\to{\rm gr\,}Q(V,P)
; when this map is an isomomorphism, the algebra
Q(V,P)
is said to be of
PBW type
.
In the language of
[
7
]
, one refers to the algebra
Q(V,P)
as a
deformation
of the algebra
Q(V,R)
, and if the epimorphism
Q(V,R)\to{\rm gr\,}Q(V,P)
is an isomorphism, then
Q(V,P)
is a
PBW-deformation
of
Q(V,R)
.

We will now prove that when the algebra
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
, then it is a PBW-deformation of a Zhang twist by an automorphism,
\Phi
, of the
2
-Veronese subalgebra of the algebra
A^{!}
. This result will be useful in determining the finite-dimensional representations of
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
, and in combination with Theorem
5.7
will also help determine nontrivial noncommutative matrix factorizations of
f
. Additionally, it uncovers that the quadratic dual of the algebra
[A^{!}_{(2)}]^{\Phi}
is canonically endowed with the structure of a CDG-algebra,
[
17
, Chapter 4, Section 4]
. We intend to explore these ideas in future work.

Construction 5.8
.

Let
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
, and assume that
A
is noetherian of finite global dimension. By Proposition
3.6
we know that
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
is a normal regular element with normalizing automorphism,
\varphi_{f^{!}}:\bar{A}^{!}\to\bar{A}^{!}
. For notational convenience, let
\Phi=\varphi_{f^{!}}
. Since
\Phi
fixes
f^{!}
, it is clear that
\Phi(\langle f^{!}\rangle)=\langle f^{!}\rangle
, hence, abusing notation, we have the induced algebra automorphism
\Phi:\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle\to\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle
. Recalling that
\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle
is canonically isomorphic to
A^{!}
, we let
\Phi:A^{!}\to A^{!}
be the algebra automorphism corresponding to
\Phi:\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle\to\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle
after identifying
\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle\cong A^{!}
. The restriction of
\Phi:A^{!}\to A^{!}
to the
2
-Veronese subalgebra of
A^{!}
will be denoted by
\Phi:A^{!}_{(2)}\to A^{!}_{(2)}
.

Since
A^{!}
is Koszul, by
[
17
, Proposition 3.2.2(i)]
we know that
A^{!}_{(2)}
is also a Koszul algebra, in particular,
A^{!}_{(2)}
is a quadratic algebra. We choose a presentation for
A^{!}_{(2)}
as follows. Let
\{g_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
be a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis for the vector space
A^{!}_{2}
. Then the kernel of the canonical projection
T(A^{!}_{2})\to A^{!}_{(2)}
is generated as an ideal by finitely many elements of the form

r_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}\otimes g_{j},\quad\lambda_{i,j,k}\in\mathbb{K},\quad 1\leq k\leq p\quad\text{for some }p\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
\bar{A}^{!}/\langle f^{!}\rangle\cong A^{!}
, we may consider
\{g_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}\cup\{f^{!}\}
as a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis of
\bar{A}^{!}_{2}
. For any
1\leq k\leq p
, the equation
\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}g_{j}=0
in
A^{!}
can be lifted to
\bar{A}^{!}
yielding the equation

\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}g_{j}=b_{k}f^{!}\quad\text{for some unique }b_{k}\in\bar{A}^{!}_{2}.

The uniqueness of such
b_{k}
follows from the regularity of
f^{!}
. Then we may write

b_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi_{i,k}g_{i}+\theta_{k}f^{!}\quad\text{for some unique }\phi_{i,k},\,\theta_{k}\in\mathbb{K}.

Therefore in
\bar{A}^{!}
we have the equations

\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}g_{j}-\sum_{i}\phi_{i,k}g_{i}f^{!}-\theta_{k}(f^{!})^{2}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq p.

Right multiplying by
(f^{!})^{-2}
in the ring
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
we have

\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}g_{j}(f^{!})^{-2}-\sum_{i}\phi_{i,k}g_{i}(f^{!})^{-1}-\theta_{k}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq p.

Finally, noting that
g_{j}(f^{!})^{-1}=(f^{!})^{-1}\Phi(g_{j})
, in
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
we have

\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,g_{i}(f^{!})^{-1}\Phi(g_{j})(f^{!})^{-1}-\sum_{i}\phi_{i,k}g_{i}(f^{!})^{-1}-\theta_{k}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq p.

Let
\{G_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
be a set of formal indeterminates and let
W
denote the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space spanned by
\{G_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
. Then
W
is isomorphic to
A^{!}_{2}
via the map
G_{i}\mapsto g_{i}
and by abuse of notation we let
\Phi:W\to W
be the
\mathbb{K}
-linear isomorphism corresponding to
\Phi:A^{!}_{2}\to A^{!}_{2}
under this identification
W\cong A^{!}_{2}
. For each
1\leq k\leq p
define an element of
T(W)
by

R_{k}=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}\,G_{i}\otimes\Phi(G_{j})-\sum_{i}\phi_{i,k}G_{i}-\theta_{k}.

Let
P
be the subspace of
\mathbb{K}\oplus W\oplus(W\otimes W)
spanned by the set
\{R_{k}:1\leq k\leq p\}
and define a nonhomogeneous quadratic algebra by

Q(W,P)=T(W)/\langle P\rangle.

The above analysis shows that we can define an algebra homomorphism via

\delta:Q(W,P)\to{\mathcal{C}}(A,f),\quad\delta(G_{i})=g_{i}(f^{!})^{-1}\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq m.

Furthermore, since the
g_{i}(f^{!})^{-1}
generate
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
as an algebra, it is clear that
\delta
is surjective.

In summary, we have associated to any
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
, with
A
a noetherian algebra of finite global dimension, a nonhomogeneous quadratic algebra
Q(W,P)
and a surjective algebra homomorphism
\delta:Q(W,P)\to{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
. This completes the construction.

Now we have the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(A,f)\in\tt{Kos\text{-}QHS}
, and assume that
A
is noetherian of finite global dimension. Adopting the notation of Construction
5.8
, the following statements hold.

(1)

The algebra homomorphism
\delta:Q(W,P)\to{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is an isomorphism.

(2)

The algebra
Q(W,P)
is a PBW-deformation of the algebra
[A^{!}_{(2)}]^{\Phi}
.

Proof.

First, by Proposition
5.4
, the algebra
\mathcal{C}(A,f)
is finite-dimensional and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)=\dim_{k}A^{!}_{(2)}
.

As in Construction
5.8
, let
R\subset W\otimes W
be the subspace spanned by the set

\Big\{\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j,k}G_{i}\otimes\Phi(G_{j}):1\leq k\leq p\Big\}.

The quadratic algebra associated to the pair
(W,P)
is the algebra

Q(W,R)=T(W)/\langle R\rangle,

and this algebra is clearly isomorphic to the Zhang twist algebra
[A^{!}_{(2)}]^{\Phi}
.
Therefore we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,R)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}[A^{!}_{(2)}]^{\Phi}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}A^{!}_{(2)}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f),

where the second equality is due to the fact that Zhang twists preserve vector space dimension.

As discussed above, there is a natural filtration on the algebra
Q(W,P)
induced by the usual filtration on the tensor algebra
T(W)
. Let
{\rm gr\,}Q(W,P))
denote the associated graded algebra of
Q(W,P)
with respect to this filtration. As usual with filtered algebras, there is a vector space isomorphism
{\rm gr\,}Q(W,P)\cong Q(W,P)
.

Recalling the natural epimorphism
Q(W,R)\to{\rm gr\,}Q(W,P))
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,P)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,R),

and it follows that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,P)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
. The algebra morphism

\delta:Q(W,P)\to{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)

is surjective, so we also have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,P)\geq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
. Therefore

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,P)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,f),

and it follows that
\delta:Q(W,P)\to{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is an isomorphism.

Noting that we have proved that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,R)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}Q(W,P)
, it follows that the epimorphism
Q(W,R)\to{\rm gr\,}Q(W,P)
is an isomorphism, and hence, by definition,
Q(W,P)
is a PBW-deformation of the algebra
Q(W,R)\cong[A^{!}_{(2)}]^{\Phi}
.

∎

6.
Examples

In this final section we give some examples illustrating our main results. We assume that
\mathbb{K}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We will write matrix factorizations of
f\in A
as sequences of matrices over
A
. Such sequences are deternined by choosing bases as in the proof of Theorem
4.9
, and then maps of graded free left
A
-modules are given by matrices acting on the right on elements of free modules written as row vectors. Note that with this choice, some care must be taken: if
\phi
and
\psi
are endomorphisms of a graded free left
A
-module and represented by matrices
\Phi
and
\Psi
, respectively, then the composite mapping
\phi\,\psi
is represented by the matrix
\Psi\,\Phi
.

Example 6.1
.

We consider the
quantum plane
. Fix
q\in\mathbb{K}
,
q\neq 0
. Let

A=\mathbb{K}_{q}[x,y]=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y\rangle}{\langle yx-qxy\rangle}.

It is well known that
A
is a Koszul Artin-Schelter regular algebra of global dimension
2
.
We now consider two examples of quadratic normal regular elements of
A
, namely
f=x^{2}
and
f=xy
.

Case 1.
Let
f=x^{2}\in A
. Then
f
is normal with normalizing automorphism

\varphi_{f}(x)=x,\quad\varphi_{f}(y)=q^{-2}y.

The algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
is presented as

\bar{A}^{!}=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y\rangle}{\langle y^{2},yx+q^{-1}xy\rangle},

and
f^{!}=x^{2}\in\bar{A}^{!}
. So we may write the quadratic dual of
A
as

A^{!}=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y\rangle}{\langle x^{2},y^{2},yx+q^{-1}xy\rangle}.

Therefore we have

A^{!}_{(2)}=\mathbb{K}[xy]\cong\frac{\mathbb{K}[g]}{(g^{2})}.

Following Construction
5.8
we need to lift the equation
(xy)^{2}=0
in
A^{!}
to an equation in
\bar{A}^{!}
,

xyxy=bf^{!}=(\phi xy+\theta x^{2})x^{2}

for some unique
\phi,\theta\in\mathbb{K}
. It is easy to see that we must have
\phi=\theta=0
. Using Proposition
3.6
, or direct computation, we find that the normalizing automorphism of
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
is given by

\varphi_{f^{!}}(x)=x,\quad\varphi_{f^{!}}(y)=q^{2}y.

So we have
\varphi_{f^{!}}(xy)=q^{2}xy
and it follows that the space
P
from Construction
5.8
is spanned by
G\otimes\varphi_{f^{!}}(G)=q^{2}(G\otimes G)
.
Therefore we conclude by Theorem
5.9
(1) that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})\cong\mathbb{K}[G]/(G^{2})
. From this it is clear that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
is not semisimple, so by
[
15
, Theorem 5.5]
, the algebra
\bar{A}
is not a graded isolated singularity and does not have finite Cohen-Macaulay representation type. However, the algebra
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
does have finite representation type, with precisely two indecomposable (right) modules, namely the trivial module
\mathbb{K}
and the regular module
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
. Using Proposition
5.6
we now determine the matrix factorizations corresponding to these indecomposable modules.

If
N=\mathbb{K}
is the trivial right
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
-module, then the graded pieces of the right
\bar{A}^{!}
-module

\widehat{N}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})}{\mathcal{C}l}(A,x^{2})_{i}

are all one-dimensional. Following the proof of Theorem
4.9
we find that the left
A
-linear maps
\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}:{\mathcal{T}}(\widehat{N})_{i}\to{\mathcal{T}}(\widehat{N})_{i+1}
are given by right multiplication by
x\in A
.

If
N={\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
is the regular right
{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})
-module, then the graded pieces of the right
\bar{A}^{!}
-module

\widehat{N}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}(A,x^{2})}{\mathcal{C}l}(A,x^{2})_{i}

are all two-dimensional. Following the proof of Theorem
4.9
, the left
A
-linear maps
\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}:{\mathcal{T}}(\widehat{N})_{i}\to{\mathcal{T}}(\widehat{N})_{i+1}
are given by right multiplication by the following
2\times 2
matrices over
A
acting on
1\times 2
row vectors of
{\mathcal{T}}(\widehat{N})_{i}
. We have

\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}x&q^{i}y\\
0&-qx\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for }i\equiv 0(2),\quad\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}x&q^{i-1}y\\
0&-q^{-1}x\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for }i\equiv 1(2).

Using these matrices the reader can check that indeed
\phi_{i+1}^{\widehat{N}}\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}
is given by right multiplication by
x^{2}\in A
. For example, if
i\equiv 0(2)
, then

\phi_{i+1}^{\widehat{N}}\phi_{i}^{\widehat{N}}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}x&q^{i}y\\
0&-qx\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x&q^{i}y\\
0&-q^{-1}x\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{2}&-q^{i-1}(yx-qxy)\\
0&x^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{2}&0\\
0&x^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Case 2.
Next we consider
f=xy\in A
. Then
f
is normal with normalizing automorphism

\varphi_{f}(x)=qx,\quad\varphi_{f}(y)=q^{-1}y.

The algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
is presented as

\bar{A}^{!}=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y\rangle}{\langle x^{2},y^{2}\rangle},

and
f^{!}=yx+q^{-1}xy\in\bar{A}^{!}
. So we may write the quadratic dual of
A
as

A^{!}=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y\rangle}{\langle x^{2},y^{2},yx+q^{-1}xy\rangle}.

Therefore we have

A^{!}_{(2)}=\mathbb{K}[xy]\cong\frac{\mathbb{K}[g]}{(g^{2})}.

Following Construction
5.8
we need to lift the equation
(xy)^{2}=0
in
A^{!}
to an equation in
\bar{A}^{!}
,

xyxy=bf^{!}=(\phi xy+\theta(yx+q^{-1}xy))(yx+q^{-1}xy)

for some unique
\phi,\theta\in\mathbb{K}
. It is easy to see that we must have
\phi=q
,
\theta=0
. Using Proposition
3.6
, or direct computation, we find that the normalizing automorphism of
f^{!}\in\bar{A}^{!}
is given by

\varphi_{f^{!}}(x)=q^{-1}x,\quad\varphi_{f^{!}}(y)=qy.

So we have
\varphi_{f^{!}}(xy)=xy
and it follows that the space
P
from Construction
5.8
is spanned by
G\otimes G-qG
.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.9
(1), we have an isomorphism of algebras,
{\mathcal{C}}(A,xy)\cong\mathbb{K}[G]/(G^{2}-G)
. This makes it clear that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,xy)
is semisimple, so, by
[
15
, Theorem 5.5]
, the algebra
\bar{A}
is a graded isolated singularity and has finite Cohen-Macaulay representation type. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}}(A,xy)
has, up to isomorphism, precisely two one-dimensional simple right modules. Therefore, unsurprisingly, we find two rank 1 noncommutative matrix factorizations of
xy\in A
.

Example 6.2
.

We consider some three-dimensional
Sklyanin algebras
. Fix
c\in\mathbb{K}
,
c\neq 0
,
c^{3}\neq 1
. Let

A=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle}{\langle yz+zy+cx^{2},zx+xz+cy^{2},xy+yx+cz^{2}\rangle}.

This family of algebras was studied in
[
8
, Section 6]
, a good reference for some of the unverified details below. It is well known that
A
is a Koszul Artin-Schelter regular algebra of global dimension
3
. When
c^{3}\neq-8
, the point scheme of
A
is an elliptic curve in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. If
c^{3}=-8
, then the point scheme of
A
is the union of three distinct lines in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. We treat the cases
c\neq-2
and
c=-2
separately in what follows.

Let
f=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\in A
. Then it is easy to check that
f
is a central element. The algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
is presented as

\bar{A}^{!}=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle}{\langle[x,y],[x,z],[y,z],(y-x)(cz+y+x),(y-z)(cx+y+z)\rangle},

where
[r,s]=rs-sr
, and
f^{!}=-cxz+y^{2}\in\bar{A}^{!}
.

A
point module
for
\bar{A}^{!}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
\bar{A}^{!}
-module
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}M_{i}=1
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Up to isomorphism, such a module is determined by the action of
x,y,z\in\bar{A}^{!}_{1}
on
M_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We describe this action by giving a point
[\lambda:\mu:\nu]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, and specify that
x,y,z
act on a fixed basis of
M_{i}
by the scalars
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{K}
, respectively.

Case 1.
Assume that
c\neq-2
. Then the commutative algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
has precisely four point modules. They are represented by the following distinct points of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
,

[1:1:1],\quad[-(1+c):1:1],\quad[1:-(1+c):1],\quad[1:1:-(1+c)].

Let
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}
be the corresponding point modules. We note that the module
M_{i}
is an object of the category
{\tt P}(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
. Following the proof of Theorem
4.9
, we find four rank
1
factorizations of
f=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}
over the algebra
A
. These factorizations are

\displaystyle f=(x+y+z)((-c+1)^{-1}(x+y+z))

\displaystyle f=(-(1+c)x+y+z)((c^{2}+c+1)^{-1}(-(1+c)x+y+z))

\displaystyle f=(x-(1+c)y+z)((c^{2}+c+1)^{-1}(x-(1+c)y+z))

\displaystyle f=(x+y-(1+c)z)((c^{2}+c+1)^{-1}(x+y-(1+c)z)).

Case 2.
Now assume that
c=-2
. In this case,
\bar{A}^{!}
has only one point module
M
, represented by
[1:1:1]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. It follows that the four-dimensional commutative algebra
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
has only one simple module, so
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is not semisimple. As an application of Theorem
5.9
we will now outline a proof that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is isomorphic to the algebra
\mathbb{K}[x,y]/(x^{2},y^{2})
. Following Construction
5.8
and using the notation there, we find that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is the quotient of the commutative polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}]
by the ideal
I
generated by

\displaystyle 9g_{1}g_{2}-g_{1}-g_{2}+2g_{3}-1

\displaystyle 9g_{1}g_{3}-g_{1}+2g_{2}-g_{3}-1

\displaystyle 9g_{2}g_{3}+2g_{1}-g_{2}-g_{3}-1

\displaystyle 9g_{1}^{2}-10g_{1}-4g_{2}-4g_{3}+5

\displaystyle 9g_{2}^{2}-4g_{1}-10g_{2}-4g_{3}+5

\displaystyle 9g_{3}^{2}-4g_{1}-4g_{2}-10g_{3}+5.

Identify
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
with the algebra
\mathbb{K}[g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}]/I
. Fix a primitive cube root of unity,
\omega\in\mathbb{K}
, that is,
\omega^{3}=1
and
\omega\neq 1
. Define elements of
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
by

x=\omega^{2}g_{1}+\omega g_{2}+g_{3},\qquad y=\omega g_{1}+\omega^{2}g_{2}+g_{3}.

Then one checks that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is the
\mathbb{K}
-linear span of the linearly independent set
\{1,x,y,xy\}
, and that
x^{2}=y^{2}=0
. It follows that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)\cong\mathbb{K}[x,y]/(x^{2},y^{2})
. It is well known that this algebra has infinite representation type. Morover, one can check that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
, there is a family
M(n,\lambda)
of pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable modules given by declaring that
x
,
y
act respectively by

\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\begin{bmatrix}0&J_{n}(\lambda)\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},

where
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix and
J_{n}(\lambda)
is the
n\times n
Jordan block matrix with
\lambda
on the diagonal. We leave it to the interested reader to determine the matrix factorizations of
f
corresponding to these indecomposables via the functor
{\mathcal{F}}
in Theorem
5.7
.

Example 6.3
.

We consider a four-dimensional Artin-Schelter regular algebra recently studied in detail in
[
10
]
. Let

A=\frac{\mathbb{K}\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\rangle}{\langle x_{1}x_{2}-x_{3}^{2},x_{2}x_{1}-x_{4}^{2},x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}x_{4},x_{4}x_{1}-x_{3}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3}-x_{3}x_{1},x_{4}x_{2}-x_{1}x_{4}\rangle}.

As proved in
[
10
]
, the algebra
A
is a Koszul Artin-Schelter regular algebra of global dimension
4
. Furthermore, there are, up to scale, precisely four normal quadratic elements
f\in A
with distinct normalizing automorphisms, namely

x_{1}x_{4}+x_{3}x_{1},\quad x_{1}x_{4}-x_{3}x_{1},\quad x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{3},\quad x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{3}.

We consider the normal element
f=x_{1}x_{4}+x_{3}x_{1}\in A
in some detail. One checks that the algebra
\bar{A}^{!}
has precisely four point modules, which determine, via Theorem
4.9
, four rank
1
matrix factorizations of
f
. More interestingly, there is also a rank
2
noncommutative matrix factorization of
f
, which we now describe.

Define a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}M_{i}=2
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there is a graded right
\bar{A}^{!}
-module structure on
M
given as follows. Consider elements of the space
M_{i}
to be
2\times 1
column vectors and let
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\bar{A}^{!}
act on the left, respectively, by the following matrices

\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&i\end{bmatrix},\quad\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},

where
i\in\mathbb{K}
is a fixed primitive fourth root of unity. Then this makes
M
into a graded right
\bar{A}^{!}
-module, and moreover,
M\in{\tt P}(\bar{A}^{!},f^{!})
. In fact, one can also check that
M
is a critical module with respect to Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, so
M
is a fat point module of multiplicity
2
for
\bar{A}^{!}
. Following the proof of Theorem
4.9
, we find that the left
A
-linear maps
\phi_{i}^{M}:{\mathcal{T}}(M)_{i}\to{\mathcal{T}}(M)_{i+1}
are given by right multiplication by the following
2\times 2
matrices over
A
acting on
1\times 2
row vectors of
{\mathcal{T}}(M)_{i}
. We have

\phi_{0}^{M}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}ix_{4}&x_{2}\\
x_{1}&ix_{3}\end{bmatrix},\ \phi_{1}^{M}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}-ix_{2}&x_{4}\\
x_{3}&-ix_{1}\end{bmatrix},\ \phi_{2}^{M}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}ix_{3}&x_{1}\\
x_{2}&ix_{4}\end{bmatrix},\ \phi_{3}^{M}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}-ix_{1}&x_{3}\\
x_{4}&-ix_{2}\end{bmatrix}

and
\phi_{i}^{M}\equiv\phi_{i(4)}^{M}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using these matrices, the reader can check that indeed
\phi_{i+1}^{M}\phi_{i}^{M}
is given by right multiplication by
f\in A
. For example,

\phi_{1}^{M}\phi_{0}^{M}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}ix_{4}&x_{2}\\
x_{1}&ix_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-ix_{2}&x_{4}\\
x_{3}&-ix_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{4}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{3}&ix_{4}^{2}-ix_{2}x_{1}\\
-ix_{1}x_{2}+ix_{3}^{2}&x_{1}x_{4}+x_{3}x_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}f&0\\
0&f\end{bmatrix}.

The four point modules and the fat point module
M
for
\bar{A}^{!}
correspond to four one-dimensional simples and one two-dimensional simple for the algebra
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is eight-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
, one can conclude via
[
13
, Proposition 7.2(2)]
together with the assumption that
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed, that
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is semisimple and isomorphic to the algebra
\mathbb{K}^{\oplus 4}\oplus{\rm M}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.

A similar analysis for the other three quadratic normal elements in
f\in A
yields that, in all cases,
{\mathcal{C}}(A,f)
is semisimple and isomorphic to the algebra
\mathbb{K}^{\oplus 4}\oplus{\rm M}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
. Hence, for all quadratic normal elements of
A
, the algebra
\bar{A}
is a graded isolated singularity, in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 2.2]
. This answers the first query in
[
10
, Question 5.3.5]
in the affirmative.
