Estimation of the input-to-state stability gain functions from finite-dimensional approximations
Birgit Hillebrecht ⋆,† and Benjamin Unger ⋆,‡

Abstract
. Since the concept of input-to-state stability (ISS) was introduced, it has been extensively investigated for finite-dimensional control systems and has recently received attention for infinite-dimensional systems. While numerical techniques provide a bridge between these two worlds, a rigorous connection between the ISS of an infinite-dimensional system with an unbounded control operator and the properties of its finite-dimensional approximations has not yet been established. In this manuscript, we make a first step towards closing this gap by investigating numerical approximations of linear (boundary) control systems using semigroup theory. Specifically, we focus on linear boundary control systems where the autonomous evolution is governed by an analytic semigroup. For these systems, we show that ISS gains can be computed from approximations. We illustrate the applicability of these findings using a one-dimensional heat equation with Dirichlet boundary control for which reference ISS gains are known.



Keywords:
Control system, boundary control, finite-dimensional approximation, input-to-state stability, Trotter–Kato, spatial discretization, analytic semigroups

AMS subject classification:
37M99,93C05,93C20,93D20

1.
Introduction

Estimating system states in the presence of external inputs is a central problem in control theory, which motivated the concept of input-to-state stability (
ISS
)
[
27
]
.
ISS
has been particularly well studied for finite-dimensional systems
[
28
,
29
]
while the interest for infinite-dimensional systems has grown recently
[
15
,
14
,
22
,
25
,
16
]
. The numerical analysis link between the two settings has been explored for bounded control operators
[
1
]
, but remains unstudied for unbounded linear control operators. Hence, approximating infinite-dimensional
ISS
gain functions by means of finite-dimensional discretizations is still an open problem, in particular, when the control acts on the boundary only. This manuscript presents the first rigorous framework that connects numerical analysis and
ISS
theory for a specific class of parabolic equations with unbounded control operators.

In more detail, we consider abstract infinite-dimensional control systems of the form

(1.1)

\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t)&=\mathcal{A}x(t)+{\mathcal{B}}u(t),\\
x(0)&=x_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

with state
x(t)\in X
and input
u(t)\in U
. The state space
X
is a Banach space, the input space
U
is a normed vector space, the operator
\mathcal{A}\colon D(\mathcal{A})\subseteq X\longrightarrow X
generates an analytic semigroup, and the (generally unbounded) operator
\mathcal{B}\colon U\longrightarrow X
models how the input impacts the state; the precise degree of unboundedness of
\mathcal{B}
will be specified in the forthcoming
Section

2.2
. Systems with unbounded control operators
{\mathcal{B}}
naturally appear when transforming partial differential equations (
PDEs
) with boundary control, i.e., systems of the form (cf.
[
3
, eq. 10.1]
)

(1.2)

\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t)&=\mathfrak{A}x(t),\\
\mathfrak{D}x(t)&=u(t),\\
x(0)&=x_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathfrak{D}
is a suitable trace operator, into the form (
1.1
)
[
25
, Prop. 2.8, Part 4]
. In the following, we refer to (
1.2
) as a
boundary control system
.

In this work, we study
L^{\infty}
-
ISS
for both aforementioned systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Specifically, we seek to quantify functions
\beta
and
\gamma
in suitable function spaces (see the forthcoming
Section

2.2
for details), so-called
ISS
gains, such that the state
x(t)
satisfies the estimate

(1.3)

\|x(t)\|_{X}\leq\beta(\|x_{0}\|_{X},t)+\gamma(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)})

for all
t\geq 0
.

Our interest in quantifying suitable
ISS
gains is motivated by the posteriori error bound for physics-informed neural networks (
PINNs
) derived by the authors in
[
11
]
. In that setting, the input
u
is not a control variable but is induced by the mismatch between the
PINN
prediction and the prescribed boundary condition. This boundary error then has to be reflected in the overall prediction error of the
PINN
, which can be bounded by the
ISS
gain
\gamma
. Consequently, the practical use of the error estimator hinges on the availability of a valid gain function
\gamma
. At present, such gain functions are typically available only from rigorous analytical results for problems posed on simple geometries, such as intervals or rectangular domains, which renders the error bound inapplicable to many engineering problems. A first step toward overcoming this limitation was taken in
[
10
]
, where a computable bound resembling the
ISS
estimate (
1.3
) was introduced. However, this approach required the input
u
, i.e., the boundary error, to be differentiable with respect to time. This paper eliminates the differentiability requirement and reinstates the original
ISS
estimate, thereby broadening the applicability of the theory.

With this motivation in mind, we deliberately avoid relying on explicit knowledge of functional-analytic properties that are often difficult to obtain for problems defined on complicated geometries. These properties include, for example, the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{A}
or the extrapolated operator
\mathcal{A}_{-1}
. Instead, we focus on strategies to retrieve
\beta
and
\gamma
via finite-dimensional approximations of the form

(1.4)

\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{n}(t)&=\mathcal{A}_{n}x_{n}(t)+{\mathcal{B}}_{n}u(t),\\
x_{n}(0)&=\mathcal{P}_{n}x_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

with approximate state
x_{n}(t)\in X^{n}
evolves in a Banach space
X^{n}
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}\colon X\to X^{n}
maps an element
x\in X
into the discretized space
X^{n}
, and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
models the impact of the input
u
on the evolution of the state.

Our main contributions are the following:

(i)

We first focus on the estimation of
ISS
gains for (
1.1
) when the operator
{\mathcal{B}}\colon U\rightarrow X
is known and bounded into the fractional power space
X_{-1+\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We show in
Theorem

4.1
that under the assumptions stated in
2.18
, the
ISS
gains can be taken as suitable limits from the
ISS
gains of the finite-dimensional approximation (
1.4
).

(ii)

We then focus on the link between (
1.2
) and (
1.1
), i.e., to settings where
{\mathcal{B}}
is not explicitly available; cf.
2.19
. By introducing a pre-boundary closure discretization, we extend the results from
Theorem

3.7
to boundary control approximations in
Theorem

4.1
. We emphasize that for this step, we require the underlying Banach spaces to be complex.

Finally, in
Section

5
, we apply these results to the one-dimensional heat equation with Dirichlet boundary control and compare the gain functions determined via finite-dimensional approximations with the theoretical gain functions available in the literature.

Notation

We denote the space of bounded linear operators between Banach spaces X and Y by
\mathfrak{L}({X},{Y})
and write
\mathfrak{L}({X})\coloneqq\mathfrak{L}({X},{X})
if
Y=X
. For a linear operator
\mathcal{A}\colon D(\mathcal{A})\subseteq X\rightarrow X
we denote the range by
\range(\mathcal{A})
and the graph norm by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
. Further, the resolvent set and the spectrum are denoted by
\rho(\mathcal{A})
and
\sigma(\mathcal{A})
, respectively. The resolvent is written as
R(\lambda,\mathcal{A})\coloneqq(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A})^{-1}
. In case
\mathcal{A}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup (short:
C_{0}
-semigroup) of operators
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, we say that the semigroup is of type
(M,\omega)
if
\|\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{X}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
. Convergence in the strong and norm topology for operators for a sequence of operators
\mathcal{K}_{n}\in\mathfrak{L}({X},{Y})
are denoted by

\mathcal{K}_{n}\xrightarrow[\textsf{SOT}(X,Y)]{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{K}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\mathcal{K}_{n}\xrightarrow[\textsf{NT}(X,Y)]{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{K},

respectively. Further, given a normed space
X
, we denote the closure with respect to a norm
\|\cdot\|
by
\overline{X}^{\|\cdot\|}
.
The
j
th unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathbf{e}_{j}
, the dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
is not stated explicitly as it will be clear from the context.
We use
i
to denote the imaginary unit, and for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the argument is denoted by
\arg(z)
. With a slight misuse of notation, we use the symbol
\Gamma
to denote both an abstract path and the Gamma function
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t
.

Definition 2.2

(​​

)
.

A family of operators
-\mathcal{A}_{n}

Definition 2.3

(​​
[
4
, Def. II.4.5]
)
.

A semigroup of operators
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is called
analytic
of angle
\varsigma\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
if for a sector
S_{\varsigma}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\arg(z)|<\varsigma,\;z\neq 0\}
the following holds:

(i)

\mathcal{S}(0)=\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{S}(z_{1})\mathcal{S}(z_{2})=\mathcal{S}(z_{1}+z_{2})
for
z_{1},z_{2}\in S_{\varsigma}
,

(ii)

the map

is analytic in

, and

(iii)

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0,z\in S_{\varsigma-\epsilon}}\mathcal{S}(z)x=x
for
\epsilon\in(0,\varsigma)
and all

.

Sectorial operators and analytic semigroups have many useful properties. Amongst them, the following three will prove especially advantageous in the subsequent discussion. First, the image of the semigroup lies in the domain of the generator, i.e.,
\range(\mathcal{S}(t))\subseteq D(\mathcal{A})
for all
t>0

[
4
, Ch. II, Thm. 4.6, part (c)]
. Second, the product
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{S}(t)
is bounded
[
24
, Ch. 2, proof of Thm. 5.2]
by

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq\frac{M}{\pi\cos(\theta)}\frac{1}{t}

for
\theta\in(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\varsigma+\tfrac{\pi}{2})
, where
M
denotes the first parameter from the growth bound of the semigroup
(M,\omega)
. Third, a particularly useful feature of sectorial operators is that fractional powers can be defined and computed using path integrals. A suitable example of a path
\Gamma
is displayed in
Figure

1(b)
.

Definition 2.4

(​​
[
4
, Def. II.5.25, II.5.31]
)
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
negative fractional power
of a sectorial operator
-\mathcal{A}\in\textsf{Sect}(\varsigma)
is defined as

(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}s^{-\alpha}R({s},{-\mathcal{A}})\,\mathrm{d}s

for a suitable path
\Gamma
which runs in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{S_{\varsigma}}
from
\infty\mathrm{e}^{-i\delta}
to
\infty\mathrm{e}^{i\delta}
for
\delta\in(\varsigma,\pi)
.
The positive fractional power is then defined as the inverse

(-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha}\coloneqq((-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha})^{-1}

on the domain
D((-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha})=\range((-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha})
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the negative fractional power can also be represented as
[
24

(2.2)

(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\alpha}R(t,-\mathcal{A})\,\mathrm{d}t

or by using the generated semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}

[
24
, Ch. 2, eq. (6.9)]

(2.3)

(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\mathcal{S}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We remark that there are different representations of the fractional power in terms of the resolvent or the semigroup of operators;
a concise overview can be found in
[
9
]
.

For a sectorial operator
-\mathcal{A}

In the context of fractional power spaces, we use in

(​​

(​​

Given Banach spaces

(​​
[

.

2.2.
Control systems and input-to-state stability

Before heading to the concrete definition of the control systems with unbounded control operators, we recall the definition of the extrapolated space
X_{-1}
and the extrapolated semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. This provides a precise framework for handling unbounded operators.

Definition 2.9

(​​
[
4
, Def. II.5.4, Thm. II.5.5]
)
.

For a given Banach space
X
and a semigroup of operators
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, which is generated by
\mathcal{A}\colon D(\mathcal{A})\subseteq X\mapsto X
with
0\in\rho(\mathcal{A})
, we define the norm

\|x\|_{X_{-1}}\coloneqq\|\mathcal{A}^{-1}x\|_{X}.

The completion of X with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{X_{-1}}
is denoted by
X_{-1}
and called an
extrapolation space
. The continuous extensions of
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{A}
to
X_{-1}
are denoted by
\{\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{-1}
respectively.

Definition 2.10

(​​
[
25
, Def. 2.1, Ex. 2.4]
)
.

Consider a Banach space
X
, a normed space
U
, a closed, densely defined, linear operator
\mathcal{A}\colon D(\mathcal{A})\subseteq X\rightarrow X
that generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
X
, and an operator
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1}})
. The linear time-invariant system

(2.5)

\dot{x}(t)=\mathcal{A}x(t)+{\mathcal{B}}u(t)

is called a
control system
, denoted as
\Sigma(\mathcal{A},{\mathcal{B}};X,U)
, with state
x(t)\in X
and input
u(t)\in U
. If
\mathcal{A}
is analytic, then we call
\Sigma(\mathcal{A},{\mathcal{B}};X,U)
an

.

Since
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1}})
, it is not guaranteed that

(2.6)

x({t};{x_{0}},{u})=\mathcal{S}(t)x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s){\mathcal{B}}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

is an element of
X
, but rather
x({t};{x_{0}},{u})\in X_{-1}
. However, if (
2.6
) lies in
X
, it is called the
mild solution
of (
2.5
)
[
22
, Def. 4.7]
. The notion of
admissibility
formalizes the existence of the mild solution for a set of allowed inputs.

Definition 2.11

(​​
[
31
]
)
.

The control system
\Sigma(\mathcal{A},{\mathcal{B}};X,U)
is called
L^{\infty}
-admissible
, if

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s){\mathcal{B}}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\in X

for all
t>0
and
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;U)
.

We now turn our attention to boundary control systems.

Definition 2.12

(​​​
[
25
, Def. 2.5]
)
.

Given a Banach space
X
, a normed space

, a linear operator
\mathfrak{A}\colon D(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq X\rightarrow X
, and a boundary operator
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathfrak{L}({D(\mathfrak{A})},{U})

-semigroup

\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t,\cdot)&=\mathfrak{A}x(t,\cdot)\quad&\mathrm{in}\;\mathbb{T}\times\Omega,\\

and denoted with

As previously mentioned, control systems (

,

]

Lemma 2.13

(​​
[

]
,
[

, p. 93]

A boundary control system

Finally, we recall the definition of

for (boundary) control systems. This definition relies on the standard classes of comparison functions

, and
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
, c.f.
[

, given as

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\mu:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\mid\mu(0)=0,\mu\;\mathrm{cont.},\mathrm{strictly\;increasing}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\mu:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\mid\mu\;\mathrm{cont.},\mathrm{strictly\;decreasing},\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu(t)=0\right\},\,\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\mu\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\mid\mu(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{K}\text{ for all }t\geq 0;\;\mu(s,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}\text{ for all }s\geq 0\right\}.

Definition 2.14

(​​
[
29
]
)
.

An

-admissible control system

-

2.3.
Approximation of strongly continuous semigroups of operators

For the approximations (
1.4
), we adopt the framework from
[
23
,
18
]
with the following definitions and assumption.

Definition 2.15
.

Given a Banach space
X

Definition 2.17
.

Consider a Banach space
X
, a semigroup of operators
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
X
, an approximation sequence
(X^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
X

. The sequence of semigroups

is called

if for all

and

The analysis of

To solve

(i)

(M,-\omega)
of

and
D
such that

for

(see

3.1
),

).

To estimate the second factor, we

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the generator of an analytic semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
X
of type
(M,-\omega)
and angle
\varsigma
. Let
\varsigma^{\prime}\in(\varsigma,\pi)
and
D>0
such that

\|R({\lambda},{-\mathcal{A}})\|_{X}\leq\frac{D}{|\lambda|+1}\;\text{for}\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{S_{\varsigma^{\prime}}}

be given. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\varsigma)
define

\displaystyle K_{1}(\omega,M,\alpha)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\omega M}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega s}\,\mathrm{d}s,\,\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle K_{2}(\omega,M,\alpha,D,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{D}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\pi|\cos(\theta)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\alpha}(1+s)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then,

\|(-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq K_{1}(\omega,M,\alpha)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}+K_{2}(\omega,M,\alpha,D,\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}t^{-\alpha}.

Proof.

We apply a suitable adaptation of
[
24
, proof of Ch. 2, Thm. 5.2, eq. 5.13]
, i.e., defining
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}+\omega I
with associated semigroup
\hat{\mathcal{S}}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\mathcal{S}(t)
of type
(M,0)
to find

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

\displaystyle=\|(\hat{\mathcal{A}}-\omega I)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\hat{\mathcal{S}}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\|\hat{\mathcal{A}}\hat{\mathcal{S}}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}+\omega\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\|\hat{\mathcal{S}}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

(
2.1
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\frac{D}{\pi|\cos(\theta)|}t^{-1}+\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\omega M=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\left(\omega M+\frac{D}{\pi|\cos(\theta)|}t^{-1}\right)

and (using the alternative representation of the negative fractional power (
2.3
))

\displaystyle\|(-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

\displaystyle=\|(-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha-1}(-\mathcal{A})\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{A})\mathcal{S}(r+t)\,\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{S}(r+t)\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(t+r)}\left[\omega M+\frac{D}{\pi|\cos(\theta)|}(t+r)^{-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{\leq}\frac{\omega M\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega r}\,\mathrm{d}r+\frac{D\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\pi|\cos(\theta)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}(t+r)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega M\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega r}\,\mathrm{d}r+\frac{Dt^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\pi|\cos(\theta)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\alpha}(1+s)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

where
(\ast)
holds because
\mathrm{e}^{\omega r}\leq 1
for
r\geq 0
and
\omega<0
. This shows the claim.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In the literature, e.g., in

Lemma 3.3
.

Let the setting be as described in
2.18
and
\varsigma^{\prime}\in(\varsigma,\pi)
. Then,

\|R({\lambda},{-\mathcal{A}})\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq\frac{\hat{D}}{|\lambda|+1}\quad\text{with}\quad D=\mu_{\mathrm{e}}\mu_{\mathrm{p}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D_{n}

Proof.

Since by
(A3)
implies pointwise convergence of the resolvents in

and conclude that for

\displaystyle\|(|\lambda|+1)R({\lambda},{-\mathcal{A}})\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the setting be as described in
2.18
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the negative fractional powers converge in the norm topology

(-\mathcal{A}_{n})^{-\alpha}\xrightarrow[\textsf{NT}(X,X)]{n\rightarrow\infty}(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}.

Proof.

Starting from (
2.2
), we obtain for
x\in X
and
\hat{\Gamma}
according to
(A4)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left\|(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}-\mathcal{E}_{n}(-\mathcal{A}_{n})^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\hat{\Gamma}}\lambda^{-\alpha}\left\|R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A})-\mathcal{E}_{n}R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A}_{n})\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

with in which the integrand is bounded for
\lambda\in\hat{\Gamma}
and for all

by

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\alpha}\left\|R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A})-\mathcal{E}_{n}R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A}_{n})\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-\alpha}\biggl[\left\|R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A})\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}+\left\|\mathcal{E}_{n}R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A}_{n})\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\biggr]

is integrable from 0 to
\infty

imply

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|(-\mathcal{A})^{-\alpha}-\mathcal{E}_{n}(-\mathcal{A}_{n})^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\\
\overset{\text{DCT}}{\leq}\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\hat{\Gamma}}\lambda^{-\alpha}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A})-\mathcal{E}_{n}R(\lambda,-\mathcal{A}_{n})\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\overset{\mathrm{\ref{ass::convergence-semigroups}}}{=}0.

∎

Lemma 3.6

For a setup as described in

Using

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s){\mathcal{B}}u(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)({\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n})u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{X}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{X}.

Leveraging the analyticity of the semigroup and fractional power spaces, we bound the first term as

\displaystyle\quad\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)\left({\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\right)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)(-\mathcal{A})^{1-\alpha}(-\mathcal{A})^{-1+\alpha}\left({\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\right)u(s)\right\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)(-\mathcal{A})^{1-\alpha}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}({(D((-\mathcal{A})^{1-\alpha}),\|\cdot\|_{X})},{X})}\|(-\mathcal{A})^{-1+\alpha}\left({\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\right)u(s)\|_{X}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(K_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(t-s)}+K_{2}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(t-s)}\right)\|{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\|u(s)\|_{U}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)}\|{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\left[\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\overset{r\coloneqq t-s}{=}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)}\|{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\left[-K_{1}\int_{t}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega r}\,\mathrm{d}r-K_{2}\int_{t}^{0}r^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right]

\displaystyle\overset{z=\omega r}{=}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)}\|{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\left[\frac{K_{1}}{\omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega r}\bigg|_{t}^{0}-K_{2}\omega^{-\alpha}\int_{t}^{0}z^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-z}\,\mathrm{d}z\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)}\|{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\left[\frac{K_{1}}{\omega}+K_{2}\omega^{-\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)\right].

Similarily one yields for the second term

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{-1}(t-s)\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{X}\leq\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;U)}\|\mathcal{E}_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({U},{X_{-1+\alpha}})}\left[\frac{K_{1}}{\omega}+K_{2}\omega^{-\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)\right],

which shows the desired inequality.
∎

and

independent of

(B2)
and

, while
(i)
follows from

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{D}_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathfrak{A}_{n}\mathfrak{D}_{n,0})u\|_{X_{-1}}

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\text{\ref{ass:norm-convergence-resolvent-path}}}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{D}_{0}-\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathfrak{A}_{n}\mathfrak{D}_{n,0})u\|_{X}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\ref{ass:norm-convergence-resolvent-path}}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{D}_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}_{n}\mathfrak{D}_{n,0})u\|_{X}=0

due to assumption

.

To establish convergence of (

) to zero in the

-norm, we need to show boundedness of
\mathcal{E}_{n,-1}\mathcal{A}_{n,-1}\mathcal{P}_{n}
to
\mathcal{A}_{-1}
as operators in
\mathfrak{L}({X_{\alpha}},{X_{-1+\alpha}})

is already assumed in
(B5)
. Since we assumed that

, we employ the identification of the fractional power spaces with the complex interpolation spaces (cf.
[

, Thm. 11.6.1]
) to then show that there exists
C_{1}>0
independent of

such that

(4.12)

\|\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}-\mathcal{A}_{n,-1}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({[X,D(\mathcal{A})]_{\alpha}},{[X_{-1},X]_{\alpha}})}\leq C_{1}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Exactness of the interpolation, cf.
[

, then allows us bound the norm in the interpolated space for an operator

mapping from

to

by

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({[X,D(\mathcal{A})]_{\alpha}},{[X_{-1},X]_{\alpha}})}\leq\|\mathcal{F}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathfrak{L}({X},{X_{-1}})}\|\mathcal{F}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{L}({D(\mathcal{A})},{X})}.

Applying this to (

) yields that we need to show

(i)

that there exists

\|\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}-\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({D(\mathcal{A})},{X})}=\|\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\|_{\mathfrak{L}({X})}\leq C_{2},

(ii)

and that there exists

Remark 4.2
.

The construction of a right-inverse for the discrete boundary operator
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
is not spelled out in either the proof or the statement of the theorem. A convenient way to obtain
\mathfrak{D}_{n,0}
is to proceed in the following three steps:

(i)

Construct a discretized boundary control
u_{n}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{P}^{U}_{n}u(t)
with
\mathcal{P}^{U}_{n}\colon U\rightarrow U_{n}
being a suitable restriction operator.

(ii)

Construct a matrix representation of
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
and compute the right inverse
\mathfrak{D}^{U}_{n,0}
using standard linear algebra.

(iii)

Compute
\mathfrak{D}_{n,0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{D}^{U}_{n,0}\mathcal{P}^{U}_{n}
.

The proof of the previous theorem can be adapted to use
u_{n}\in U_{n}
and a suitably adapted convergence criterion

\|(\mathcal{E}_{n}\mathfrak{D}^{U}_{n,0}\mathcal{P}^{U}_{n}-\mathfrak{D}_{0})u\|_{X_{\alpha}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

6.
Discussion

We showed for an
L^{\infty}
-admissible analytic control system with an unbounded control operator how to obtain explicit
ISS
-gain estimates relying on the properties of a sequence of finite-dimensional approximations. The required assumptions consist of Trotter–Kato-type conditions together with additional convergence requirements for both the control operator and the generator’s resolvent. Moreover, we showed that when the unbounded control operator is the result of the Fattorini trick applied to a boundary control system, we can conclude the convergence of the control operators in the interior of the domain based on the convergence of a pre-boundary closure discretisation.

Before continuing to focus on limitations and future research directions in the context of
ISS
, we put the introduction of the pre-boundary closure discretization into a broader context. The pre-boundary closure discretization offers a first step towards a systematic approach of switching between the boundary trace formulation and the abstract Cauchy problem from a numerical analysis point of view. Both views have distinct advantages: the boundary trace formulation is well-suited for energy estimates, regularity arguments, and the explicit treatment of boundary data, while the abstract Cauchy problem setting is beneficial for operator-theoretic approaches, semigroup theory, and the design of robust controls. Hence, having a rigorous bridge between them might be advantageous also for other applications beyond
ISS
.

The Riccati operator theory provides an alternative way to obtain
ISS
gains for linear infinite-dimensional systems
[
19
]
. This approach requires solving an operator Riccati equation, which is in general a challenging task (cf.
[
3
, Ch. 9, Ex. 9.2.14]
). By contrast, the presented approach avoids using the Riccati equation and directly approximates the required parameters from the system semigroup and the control operator. A natural next step is to explore whether the discretisation framework for boundary control systems can be combined with the Riccati-based approximation results of
[
19
]
.

For future work, we envision broadening the applicability of our results to encompass more general boundary conditions, to address hyperbolic problems, and to include nonlinear or non-autonomous systems.
To elaborate the first point further, a systematic investigation of the assumptions (A*) and (B*) for Neumann or mixed boundary conditions is required. For conforming finite elements in 2D with Dirichlet boundaries, a norm-resolvent convergence result is available, for example, in
[
6
]
, which is missing or not easily accessible for applied mathematicians for other types of boundary conditions. Secondly, the approximation results can not be applied to hyperbolic problems due to the restriction to sectorial operators. However, specific hyperbolic problem groups might possess sufficient regularity properties to allow adapting the presented approach.
Finally, extensions in the direction of semilinear systems and in the direction of non-autonomous systems, for which an extension of the Fattorini trick is readily available
[
13
]
, are of interest for applications.
