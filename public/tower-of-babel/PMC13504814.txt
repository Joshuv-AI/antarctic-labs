Evaluation of fatty liver disease self-care applications: a systematic review and evaluation of apps using the mobile application rating scale (MARS)
Authors: Niloofar Mohammadzadeh, Amir Hossein Daeechini, Atefeh Paghe, Hossein Valizadeh Laktarashi, Erfan Esmaeeli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe use of mobile health applications (apps) has a significant impact on lifestyle changes, especially in chronic diseases such as fatty liver disease, and provides a good opportunity for self-care and lifestyle improvement. This study aimed to systematically evaluate the quality of self-care mobile applications for fatty liver patients based on the MARS rating features.MethodsUsing related keywords from Google Play and App Combo stores, applications related to fatty liver disease self-care were searched and included in the study. The quality of the applications was then evaluated by four experts using the MARS questionnaire.Results58 apps were identified and screened according to the exclusion criteria, and 10 applications were included in the study. The average MARS score across all included applications was 3.06 out of 5, with the majority of the apps achieving a minimum acceptability score of 3.0. The highest score was related to the functional dimension with an overall average of 4.2 and the lowest score was related to the subjective quality dimension with an overall average of 2.5.ConclusionsMobile applications in self-care for fatty liver disease in this study were evaluated at an acceptable level, and most of the applications provided comprehensive information about the disease and dietary recommendations. However, in providing some secondary features, such as providing a specific diet, communicating with a specialist, and reminders, they need to be improved, which can help to increase the effectiveness of the apps.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-05001-2.Keywords: Mobile applications, MARS tool, Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, Self-care

Background

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a prevalent etiology of chronic liver disease worldwide [1]. Improper and high-fat diets cause lipid accumulation, which is one of the leading causes of liver disease including NAFLD and alcoholic fatty liver disease (AFLD) [2, 3]. The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) report revealed that NAFLD is the fastest-growing factor in the burden of chronic liver diseases (CLD), including cirrhosis and liver cancer [4, 5].

Inadequate management of NAFLD can lead to challenges associated with liver illnesses, highlighting its significance due to the absence of any authorized pharmaceutical treatment for NAFLD [6]. Hence, current NAFLD management strategies focus on lifestyle changes, such as weight loss, a healthy diet, increasing exercise, and reducing comorbidities associated with metabolic syndrome [7–9]. Guidelines of World Health Organization (WHO) for self-care interventions published in 2022 define self-care as “the ability of persons and communities to maintain and promote health, prevent disease, and manage disease and disability on their own or with the help of health professionals” [10].

Currently, transformation in healthcare provision is impossible without progress in intelligent technologies and mobile phone applications [11]. Furthermore, the widespread onset of COVID-19 reveals the importance of mobile phones in helping patients and health professionals communicate more accurately [12]. The use of mobile phones has changed the accuracy and extent of healthcare conveyance in unfathomable ways [13]. Mobile health applications, in combination with the worldwide development of mobile phone technologies, are increasingly providing healthcare services and information [14].

Mobile health applications for clinical care have resulted in improved quality delivery, patient-reported outcomes, and self-management [15], indicating healthy life achievement [16]. Mobile applications have the potential to provide healthcare information beyond clinical environments and can offer personalized interventions to promote healthy behaviors and lifestyles. They are increasingly being used to deliver lifestyle modification interventions to patients with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [6]. Kwon et al. [17] in a randomized controlled trial study developed a personalized lifestyle coaching intervention using a mobile app, which included information on NAFLD management, self-monitoring of diet and physical activity, and coaching sessions based on patient records and SMS text messages.

Many mobile health applications can confuse users in choosing the right application and increase the probability of accessing low-quality applications [18]. Identifying features and rating the quality of mobile apps can help users choose the most suitable application [19, 20]. Evaluating mobile phone applications, including mobile health applications, is becoming essential because of the lack of laws and regulations regarding the development of smart mobile health user applications [21]. Stoyanov et al. developed the Mobile Application Rating Scale in 2015. This tool evaluates and ranks mobile applications based on qualitative, objective, and subjective criteria [22] and is currently the most used tool in this scope [23].

MARS is a standard tool for evaluating and investigating the quality of health applications [24], comprising 23 items across four qualitative dimensions: (engagement (5 items), functionality (4 items), esthetics (3 items), and information (7 items), as well as a subjective quality (4 items) dimension [25]. Additionally, there is an app-specific dimension, which consists of 6 items. The initial validation study of this tool demonstrated strong reliability for the subscales (α = 0.80 to 0.89) and the overall scale (α = 0.90), as well as good objectivity (subscales: Intra-class correlation (ICC) = 0.50 to 0.80; overall = 0.90) [26]. These findings have been confirmed in several studies that explored the essential aspects of the MARS tool’s translated versions [23, 27, 28]. The MARS questionnaire has been translated and validated in different languages, including Persian [29], Turkish [30], French [31], Arabic [32], Greek [18], Korean [33], Italian [34], Japanese [35], and German [23]. MARS has been used to evaluate smartphone applications in various fields, such as the management of inflammatory bowel disease [36], cardiac rehabilitation [37], epilepsy [38], HIV/AIDS [39], overweight control with a cognitive behavioral therapy approach [21], management of COVID-19 [40], rare disease patients [41], and food allergies [42].

To the best of our knowledge, no published articles have evaluated fatty liver self-care applications using the MARS questionnaire based on library reviews in the PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar databases. This study aimed to systematically evaluate the quality of self-care mobile applications for fatty liver patients available in Google Play and APK Combo Stores based on the MARS questionnaire.

Methods

This study utilized the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) checklist [43] as the framework for documenting the review process.

Search strategy

Smartphone-based applications were explored in January 2024 based on the terms and keywords, including “fatty liver,” “fatty liver disease,” “nonalcoholic fatty liver disease,” “NAFLD,” “fatty liver diet,” “m-Health,” “mobile app,” “evaluation,” and “self-care” available in the Google Play and APK Combo stores. The search was limited to apps published or updated for fatty liver until January 30, 2024, and apps were selected based on the title and description available in Google Play and APK Combo stores. Data from February 2024 to March 2024 were analyzed.

Inclusion and exclusion Criteria

The inclusion criteria for fatty liver-focused applications included being in English, free to download and install, related to fatty liver self-care, and available in the Google Play and APK Combo stores. The exclusion criteria included non-English language, lack of free download and installation, animal-specific development, games, and logging issues.

App selection and data extraction process

Initially, four researchers (AD, AP, HV, and EE) independently reviewed the application names and descriptions of all smartphone-based applications in Google Play and APK Combo stores, based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Differences in opinion were resolved through a review of the fifth expert in health information management (NM). All the experts possessed experience in application development and held master’s degrees or higher in medical informatics or health information technology. In the next step, the evaluators identified and installed the chosen applications on their mobile phones to conduct a more detailed investigation, recording all relevant information in a researcher-designed form aligned with the study’s objectives. This form includes essential information such as the application name, version, update date, app store, developer, category, age group, number of consumer reviews, user ratings, and file size.

Data analysis

All items was scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = inadequate; 2 = poor; 3 = acceptable; 4 = good; and 5 = excellent) using MARS. Moreover, N/A is considered valid for not applicable items [44]. Four experts answered the 23 questions of the MARS scale to assess the quality of the applications blindly. The app’s general information and the evaluators’ MARS tool results were imported into the SPSS software (version 16) for analysis. The authors then presented descriptive data for the analyzed applications and evaluation results of the MARS tool. Inter-rater reliability was assessed using Cohen’s kappa for dual data (yes/no) and the ICC for categorical data. A significance level of 0.01 was considered in this study. In this study, we conducted Spearman’s correlation analysis to explore the relationships between the quality element domains, as indicated by MARS. A significance level of 0.01 was considered statistically significant.

Synthesis of results

The study variables were used to perform a descriptive analysis, which involved calculating and presenting frequency and percentage parameters. The results are displayed as graphs and tables.

Results

Initially, the researchers searched the databases (Google Play and APK Combo) and found 58 apps. By screening the titles and descriptions of these apps, the researchers realized that 20 apps did not focus on fatty liver and its self-care, and were excluded. After removing duplicates, 34 patients remained in the study. During the review of applications based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 24 applications were removed due to non-English content (n = 9), not being free (n = 1), log-in requirements (n = 12), and apps developed for animals (n = 2). Finally, 34 applications were examined, of which 10 were included in the current study (Fig. 1).

The selected applications were classified based on the store category (health and fitness, food and beverage, lifestyle, and medical). The majority of applications (n = 6; 60%) were classified into the health and fitness category, whereas only two (n = 2; 20%) were classified into the medical category. Furthermore, all applications (100%) exhibited commercial dependencies. There were no applications associated with a university or research center, and seven of the applications (n = 7; 70%) required Internet access when using the application.

Quality evaluation of included apps by MARS

Engagement

The scores of the engagement domain were based on five criteria: entertainment, interest, customization, interaction, and targeting, as much as 2.62 an average (1.7–3.4). The liver health app received the highest score in this field (3.4 out of 5). The purpose of these applications is to assist patients with fatty liver in remembering to take medications, guiding the names of medications, advising on eating or avoiding certain foods, sharing information, accessing liver video blogs, and providing natural treatment recipes and ways to prevent fatty liver.

Functionality

The functionality domain was assessed using a 5-point Likert scale across its four subgroups, with an average score of 4.2, ranging from 3.5 to 4.75. Most apps performed well in functionality, and the “Fatty liver diet (Rikki Blakk)” application scored 4.75 out of 5. However, the “Liver Health” app had the weakest score (3.5/5) among the related applications.

Esthetics

Esthetics was another domain evaluated based on a 5-point Likert scale in its three subgroups, with an average score of 2.82 ranging from 1.83 to 4.2. The “Tips & diet for Fatty Liver Disease” application received the highest score due to better graphic and visual quality, and the “Fatty Liver Diet (James Scott)” application received the lowest score regarding esthetics.

Information

The authors assessed the quality of the app contents based on a review of related studies on fatty liver disease and its self-care. Furthermore, Table 2, which indicates the features provided by each app, was generated after reviewing the descriptions of the apps in this phase of evaluation with MARS. The information domain was evaluated based on a 5-point Likert scale in its seven subgroups in the range of 2.47–3.71 (average = 3.21). The “Tips & diet for Fatty Liver Disease” application scored the highest (3.71). This application contains tips about the causes, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, and recommendations for eating and avoiding certain foods in patients with fatty liver.

App subjective quality

The app’s subjective quality encompasses its utility, frequency of usage, cost, and level of satisfaction. The scores in this section ranged from 1.37 to 3.62, and the “Liver well” application obtained the highest score (3.62 out of 5). Four evaluators rated each application independently on a 5-point Likert scale based on the MARS rating features. Any disagreement regarding any of the ratings was resolved by a fourth researcher.

Discussion

This study aimed to systematically evaluate the quality of self-care mobile applications for fatty liver patients available on Google Play and APK Combo Stores based on MARS. This study revealed that two apps, named “The Tips & Diet for Fatty Liver Disease” and “Liver Health app,” scored the highest among the studied apps, with total scores of 3.48 and 3.43 out of 5, respectively. The MARS tool yielded average scores ranging from 2.37 to 3.48 when used to evaluate the quality of the studied applications. The MARS ratings indicate that seven out of ten applications met acceptable quality standards, and three were poor, based on a maximum of five points.

“Tips & diet for Fatty liver” application obtained the highest overall score, which could identify the disease, its symptoms, and signs, provide a general diet, provide a specific diet for each person (in the form of purchase), communicate with a physician, provide a daily or weekly meal plan (in the form of purchase), and yoga activities. The remarkable point was the closeness of the total scores of the applications, which revealed that most of the studied applications had a similar quality level.

In 2024, Ansari et al. [45] conducted a study to develop and evaluate a self-care mobile application for viral hepatitis patients. The quality of the app was evaluated using MARS. Results of this study indicated that the quality of the developed application related to four sections was at a good level (a mean score of ≥ 4 out of 5). Furthermore, the functionality obtained the highest score among others. The results of this study are consistent with ours.

Similar to most studies [21, 38, 39, 45] evaluating health applications using the MARS tool, the current research obtained the highest score in the functionality domain compared to other fields. The highest score in domain functionality indicates that this issue is essential for users when using applications, because low functionality causes the user to give up in the very early stages of using the application. The audience’s willingness to use applications can effectively increase the quality of the information provided. In the study by Gerner et al. [36], 14 mobile apps in the field of inflammatory bowel disease management were evaluated and reviewed using the MARS tool. Similar to our research, the functionality domain obtained the highest score, whereas the subjective quality domain obtained the lowest score among the MARS domains.

The studied applications received the lowest scores regarding the app’s subjective quality and engagement. The evaluators’ opinions about recommending the application to others, the value of paying for its use, and the frequency of its use determine the app’s subjective quality. The Engagement section pertains to entertainment, design and visual information. These items have a significant impact on user attraction and continuous use of the application, and users prefer practicality, attractiveness, and ease of use. In their study [46], Valizadeh and Aghebati developed and assessed a self-care smartphone application designed for patients with cirrhosis. The application was evaluated by five software technicians and 74 patients diagnosed with cirrhosis, using MARS and heuristics. The findings indicated that patients rated the developed application as having relatively high quality. However, the esthetics (68.60%) and engagement (71.36%) sections received the lowest scores, respectively. Consistent with these findings, the current study also reported low scores in the engagement and esthetics sections.

According to the MARS evaluation scores, the applications studied in the information quality section achieved an acceptable level, with a score of 3.21 out of 5. Because their users are patients, health applications require highly accurate and reliable information. Gould et al. [47] evaluated the quality of applications for the self-management of patients with gastroesophageal reflux and found that 61% of the evaluated applications had a poor overall app score. In addition, many apps are poor in terms of information quality and user engagement. Esmaeeli et al. [48] evaluated hemodialysis apps using the MARS questionnaire. The included apps scored 2.76 out of 5 in the information quality section. Additionally, the apps had a poor overall score, with a mean of 2.91 out of 5. In another study, Mohammadi Mogharab et al. [49] assessed mobile health apps for breast cancer management using the MARS questionnaire and the Feature-based Application Rating Method (FARM). The included apps obtained a mean score of 3.2 out of 5 in the information quality section. Furthermore, the apps achieved a score of 3 out of 5 as the MARS overall score, which shows that the total quality of the included apps was at an acceptable level.

Only two of the applications mentioned references for their content. However, it was impossible to determine whether the studied applications were valid. Applications were weak in providing functions such as alarms and communication with doctors, importing medicine, and providing a specific diet for each person. Each feature was provided sporadically in only one case of different applications, and the applications needed to be improved in the user interaction section.

A critical point in the applications related to fatty liver was the similarity of the content and name of approximately 50% of the applications. The developers of these applications should conduct a comprehensive review of the status of other applications and perform a strong need assessment to meet the needs of patients with fatty liver and the problems related to these applications.

Study limitations

Since the time of evaluation, some applications have undergone updates and changes in the highly volatile, unpredictable, and constantly changing mobile application market. Applications on the iOS operating system were not assessed due to the conduct of this study in Iran, sanctions against Iran, and limitations of filtering mobile applications. Some applications had log-in problems and could not be used for Iranian national codes and numbers; therefore, they were excluded from the study. Furthermore, the lack of an advanced search strategy in the Google Play store, particularly the App Combo store, made access difficult, as did the lack of a systematic search for all fatty liver disease self-care applications.

Conclusion

Since self-care, a healthy lifestyle, and a proper diet are necessary for patients with fatty liver disease, they can have a substantial impact on self-management and monitoring of chronic illnesses, including fatty liver disease. In this study, mobile applications for fatty liver disease self-care were evaluated at an average level using MARS. The MARS evaluation demonstrated that most applications have a suitable quality standard. The diversity in the quality of these applications and the failure of some applications to meet the overall quality ratings suggest the use of new approaches in the design and development of these applications. Furthermore, designers and developers of self-care applications should focus on interaction, beauty, and mental quality. Since users’ attitudes and knowledge regarding the use of self-care applications significantly affect their adherence to these tools, it is suggested that there should be effective collaboration between designers and developers, patients, and healthcare providers. Certainly, developing these applications in accordance with users’ needs will make them more user-friendly and practical.
