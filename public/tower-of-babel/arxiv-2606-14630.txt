Abstract
We adapt the recent theory for unique solvability of perturbed saddle-point problems in Banach spaces to the case of parameter-independent stability bounds. This also constitutes an extension to the Brezzi–Braess theory for parameter-robust stability of perturbed saddle-point problems from the Hilbert to the Banach setting. We apply the new abstract result to the mixed formulation of the advection-diffusion-reaction equation and tackle also its coupling with the Darcy–Forchheimer equations. The complete system is shown to be stable irrespective of the model parameters of permeability, Forchheimer coefficient, and reaction modulation. We discretise the problem with mixed finite element methods and show convergence in appropriately parameter-weighted norms. We also design operator-based preconditioners for the full system, utilising weighted norms that provide robustness with respect to some model parameters. We also present a few numerical examples that show the convergence and robustness of the proposed method in some regimes of practical interest. MSC (2010): 65N30, 65N12, 65N15. Keywords: Nonlinear flow in porous media, robust well-posedness in Banach spaces, mixed finite element methods, robust stability, fixed-point analysis, preconditioners, numerical experiments.

Introduction

This paper investigates the robust parameter stability of a system of the Darcy–Forchheimer equations coupled with the advection-diffusion-reaction (ADR) equation as presented in
[
31
]
.
Let

and solute concentration

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}+{\mathtt{F}|\boldsymbol{u}|\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{f}(\varphi)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\boldsymbol{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi-{\alpha^{-1}}\boldsymbol{u}\varphi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\varphi-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}

\displaystyle=m\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=\varphi

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}$},

(1.1e)

\displaystyle p=\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on $\Gamma_{p}$}.

(1.1f)

Here,
\kappa
is the hydraulic conductivity (permeability of the porous matrix divided by the fluid viscosity),

\boldsymbol{f}
is a forcing vector that depends on the concentration field which satisfies the following condition (cf.
[
31
, Assumption 2.1]

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{f}(\varphi)\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}+\alpha_{1}\|\varphi\|_{0,6,\Omega},

(1.2)

where

is a positive constant.
In addition,

is the Forchheimer coefficient,
\alpha>0
is the diffusivity coefficient,
\varrho_{0}>0
is the reaction coefficient and
m
is the concentration source/sink. A variety of discretisations with finite differences and finite element methods can be found in, e.g.
[
18
,
27
,
9
]
, or in
[
1
]
for the case of singular sources (see also
[
13
,
25
]

Main contribution.

Owing to the form of (
1.1
), and with the aim of preserving both robustness of the parameters–both at the continuous and discrete levels–the variational formulation of the problem is cast involving sums and intersections of reflexive Banach spaces. The main tool in the theoretical analysis of well-posedness (as well as error estimates) is a new abstract result for perturbed saddle-point problems in reflexive Banach spaces that stands an extension of
[
15
]
(to the parameter-robust context) as well as of
[
10
]
(to its non-Hilbert counterpart).
A more closely related reference that deals with the design of preconditioning for flow-transport couplings, is the recent work
[
12
]
addressing robust stability of a linearised model for coupled heat and Biot equations. The main differences here are that, due to the presence of the nonlinear terms and Banach spaces functional structure, we need to take into consideration sums of three non-Hilbert spaces and realise the action of duality maps that are non-standard when compared to the recent literature on operator preconditioning (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
12
,
26
]
). It should be noted that, in the context of ADR problems, the presence of the advective term renders the underlying saddle-point structure non-symmetric. The treatment of this term within the design of preconditioners remains challenging for both preconditioning strategies and iterative solvers.

The preconditioners we explore here are inspired by Schur complement and operator preconditioning frameworks for symmetric elliptic operators; however, their extension to the nonsymmetric and nonnormal setting lacks a firm theoretical basis
[
32
,
21
]
. Additionally, while the individual Darcy–Forchheimer and reaction-diffusion subproblems are uniformly bounded and inf-sup stable, robustness with respect to
\alpha
and
\mathtt{F}
is partially lost in the coupled system due to the fixed-point coupling of the nonlinear velocity and transport flux. This analytical loss of robustness does not manifest in our observed numerical convergence rates, suggesting it may be an artifact of sub-optimal scaling in the weighted norms or limitations inherent to the fixed-point framework.

Outline.

Note that, by the generalised Hölder’s inequality with 1/3+1/2+1/6=1, and the uniform positivity and boundedness of
\kappa,
the following estimate readily follows:

\displaystyle d_{2}^{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\varphi)\leq C^{c}_{\alpha}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\,|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}\,|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi},

(3.12)

where
C^{c}_{\alpha}:={{\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}}}(1+C_{\mathsf{emb}}\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

Invoking the bound of
d_{2}^{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
(cf. (
3.12
)), it follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)\in{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}\frac{\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi;\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}\geq\bigl[C_{c}-C^{c}_{\alpha}{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,3,\Omega}}\bigr]|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{W}\times\Psi.

Hence, under the assumption that for each
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}
such that
\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{{0,3,\Omega}}\leq\frac{C_{c}}{2C^{c}_{\alpha}}
, we arrive at

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)\in{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}\frac{\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi;\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}\geq\frac{C_{c}}{2}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}.

(3.13)

Therefore, the ADR system (cf. (
2.3c
)-(
2.3d
)) is well-posed.
Additionally, using (
3.11
) in (
3.13
), and using the Hölder’s inequality to see that
H(\psi)\leq\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\|\psi\|_{0,6,\Omega},
we obtain the continuous dependence on the data as follows:

\displaystyle|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}\leq C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega},

(3.14)

with
C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}:={2}{C_{c}}^{-1}(1+C_{\mathsf{emb}}\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}})
being independent of
\varrho_{0}
. This concludes the rest of the proof.

\square

3.2
Fixed-point analysis

Now, define the following map

\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}:\Psi\to\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q},\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\varphi}\mapsto\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\hat{\varphi})=(\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}(\hat{\varphi}),\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{2}(\hat{\varphi})):=(\boldsymbol{u},p),

where
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}
is the unique solution of the Darcy–Forchheimer equations for fixed
\hat{\varphi}\in\ \Psi
, as stated in Theorem
3.1
, and
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}:\Psi\to\mathbf{V}
,
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{DF}}:\Psi\to\mathrm{Q}.

Further, for a fixed
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V},
set the solution operator associated with the mixed ADR equations as

\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{W}\times\Psi,\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\mapsto\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})=(\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}),\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})):=(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi),

where
(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)
is the unique solution of the ADR equations, as stated in Theorem
3.3
, and
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{1}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{W},\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}:\mathbf{V}\to\Psi
. These maps are well-defined; the same holds for the following one

\mathcal{S}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V},\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\mapsto\mathcal{S}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}):=\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})).

(3.15)

Finding a fixed point
\boldsymbol{u}
of
\mathcal{S}
is therefore equivalent to find a solution to (
2.3
). We use the Banach contraction mapping theorem, and consider, for a generic
r>0
, the following closed ball

\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}:=\{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}:\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq r\},

and proceed next to show that
\mathcal{S}
maps it to itself and that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction. For
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}
, using Theorem
3.1
, (
3.2
), (
1.2
), and Theorem
3.3
shows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathbf{V}}=\|\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}))\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[\|\boldsymbol{f}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}))\|_{0,\Omega}\bigr]\leq{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}}+C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\alpha_{1}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\bigr].

(3.16)

Lemma 3.4
(Ball mapping property)

Under the small data assumption

{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}}+C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\alpha_{1}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\bigr]\leq r<\frac{1}{2},

(3.17)

we have that
\mathcal{S}\big(\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}\big)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}
.

Proof.

Given
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}
, a use of (
3.15
) with (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) yields

\|\mathcal{S}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\|_{\mathbf{V}}=\|\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}))\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq r<\frac{1}{2},

and hence,
\mathcal{S}\big(\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r}\big)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{\boldsymbol{0}}^{r},
which concludes the proof.

\square

Lemma 3.5
(Lipschitz-continuity)

There exists a positive constant
L_{\mathcal{S}}
such that

\|\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq L_{\mathcal{S}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\in\mathbf{V}.

(3.18)

Proof.

It is enough to show the Lipschitz-continuity of the maps
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}
, as in that case, using the Lipschitzness of the individual maps, one simply obtains the following

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}))-\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}))\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq L_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

with respective Lipschitz constants,
L_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
and
L_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}
(to be specified below) associated with the maps
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}
. We now proceed to show the Lipschitzness of
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}
.

Lipschitz-continuity of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\Psi
be two elements in
\Psi.
Fixing
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
results in two solutions
(\boldsymbol{u}_{1},p_{1}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{2},p_{2})\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}
corresponding to (
2.3a
)-(
2.3b
). Now, denote
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}(\varphi_{1}):=\boldsymbol{u}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{DF}}_{1}(\varphi_{2}):=\boldsymbol{u}_{2}.
Substituting
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2},q=p_{1}-p_{2}
into (
2.3a
), (
2.3b
), using (
1.2
), and an application of the uniformly strong monotonicity of
a_{1}(\bullet,\bullet)+d_{1}(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)
in the new equation obtained by subtracting (
2.3b
) from (
2.3a
) implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}({\varphi_{1}})-\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}({\varphi_{2}})\|_{\mathbf{V}}=\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq L_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\Psi},

(3.19)

where
L_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}:=\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{DF}}{\alpha_{1}}C^{2}_{\mathsf{emb}}
,
\alpha_{\mathsf{DF}}
being the strong monotonicity constant (see,
[
17
, Equation 3.8]
), and
\alpha_{1}
is the local Lipschitz continuity constant for the Darcy–Forchheimer system (see,
[
17
, Equation 3.7]
), which can be further bounded by
{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}},\alpha_{1},C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c},
and
\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}
(cf. (
3.2
) and (
1.2
)).

Lipschitz-continuity of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{2}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\in\mathbf{V}
be two elements in
\mathbf{V}.
Fixing
\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2}
in (
2.3c
),(
2.3d
) produces the solutions
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1},\varphi_{1}),(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2},\varphi_{2})\in\mathbf{W}\times\Psi
corresponding to (
2.3c
)-(
2.3d
). Let us denote

Subtracting (
2.3d
) from (
2.3c
), adding
\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\varphi_{2},\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\varphi_{1}
, and subtracting
2\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\varphi_{2}
, and using Theorem
3.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)\in\mathbf{W}\times\Psi\\
(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)\neq(\boldsymbol{0},0)\end{subarray}}\frac{{-\int_{\Omega}((\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\cdot\boldsymbol{w})\varphi_{2}+\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{w})\varphi}}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}.

Finally, using Hölder’s inequality and (
3.5
), we conclude the following

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)\in\mathbf{W}\times\Psi\\
(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)\neq(\boldsymbol{0},0)\end{subarray}}\frac{{\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}}{{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\boldsymbol{w},\psi)\in\mathbf{W}\times\Psi\\

\displaystyle|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\Psi}\leq{\frac{2C^{c}_{\alpha}}{C_{c}(C^{c}_{\alpha}-\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}})}}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\;\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

(3.20)

where the above inequality makes sense when

This concludes the Lipschitz-continuity
of
\mathcal{S}
by invoking the bounds from (
3.19
) and (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}))-\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\mathsf{DF}}(\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}))\|_{\mathbf{V}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{\mathsf{DF}}^{-1}\alpha_{1}\|\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\|_{\Psi}\leq\frac{2C^{c}_{\alpha}}{C_{c}(C^{c}_{\alpha}-1)}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\alpha_{\mathsf{DF}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

with the Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathcal{S}}:={\frac{2C^{c}_{\alpha}}{C_{c}(C^{c}_{\alpha}-\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}})}}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\alpha_{\mathsf{DF}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{1}.

This completes the rest of the proof.

\square

As a consequence of the previous analysis, we can state the following main result.

Theorem 3.6
(Well-posedness of the fully-coupled continuous problem)

Suppose that

\displaystyle{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}}+C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\alpha_{1}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\bigr]\leq r<\frac{1}{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and
that }\hskip 10.00002ptL_{\mathcal{S}}<1.

Then the coupled problem (
2.3
) has a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}\times\mathbf{W}\times\Psi
. Moreover, there holds

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|p\|_{\mathrm{Q}}+\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{\mathbf{W}}+\|\varphi\|_{\Psi}\leq C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}+

\displaystyle{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\max\biggl\{{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}}+C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\alpha_{1}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\bigr],

\displaystyle\bigl({C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{c,\boldsymbol{u}}}\bigl[{\|\boldsymbol{f}(0)\|_{0,\Omega}}+C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{c}\alpha_{1}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\bigr]\bigr)^{2}\biggr\},

where the constants do not depend on the model parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F},\varrho_{0}
.

Proof.

We first recall that Lemma
3.4
guaranties that
\mathcal{S}
maps
\mathrm{W}
to itself. Then, bearing in mind the Lipschitz-continuity of
\mathcal{S}:\mathrm{W}\to\mathrm{W}
given by Lemma
3.5
along with the fact that
L_{\mathcal{S}}<1
, a direct application of the Banach fixed-point theorem yields the existence of a unique fixed point
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{V}.
Hence, a unique solution of the system (
2.3
). In addition, the
a priori
estimate is a direct consequence of the continuous dependence on data provided by Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
.

\square

as well as in the Lipschitz constant

(cf. (

)).

(4.7a)

(4.7b)

(4.7d)

4.1
Robust discrete solvability

Consider the following system for a fixed
\hat{\varphi}_{h}\in\Psi_{h}

\displaystyle a_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+d_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b_{1,h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle=F^{\hat{\varphi}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(4.8a)

\displaystyle b_{1,h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h},

(4.8b)

and recall from
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
the following result.

Theorem 4.1
(Well-posedness of discrete Darcy–Forchheimer equations)

Assume that

\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\varphi}_{h})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega).
Then there exists a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
to (
4.7a
)-(
4.7b
). Furthermore, there is a positive constant
{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{\mathsf{d}}},
independent of the parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F},h
, such that

\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbf{V}\times\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\leq{C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{\mathsf{d}}}\max\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\varphi}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega},\|\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\varphi}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\bigr).

Additionally, there exists a positive constant
C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{\mathsf{d},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}},
independent of
\kappa,\mathtt{F},\boldsymbol{u}_{h},
and
h
, such that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq C^{\mathsf{DF}}_{\mathsf{d},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\,\|\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\varphi}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}.

(4.9)

We next prove the following lemma, which is crucial in deducing the discrete inf-sup stability of
b_{2,h}(\bullet,\bullet)
.

Lemma 4.2

There exists an operator
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{W}_{h}),\hskip 10.00002pt\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{W}_{h})}\ \text{is independent of }\alpha,\varrho_{0},h\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle{{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}_{\frac{6}{5}}}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}(\ell)],\psi_{h}\rangle=\langle\ell,\psi_{h}\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\ \ell\in\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\psi_{h}\in\Psi_{h}.

(4.10)

Proof.

First, construct
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}
in such a way that it satisfies the following properties

\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\Omega))^{\prime},(\mathbf{L}_{h}^{2}(\Omega)\bigr),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{L}_{h}^{6}(\Omega))^{\prime},\mathbf{H}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{\frac{6}{5}},\Omega)\bigr),

respectively, and then using the fact that (cf. (
1.3
))
\Psi_{h}^{\prime}=(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\Omega)+\mathrm{L}_{h}^{6}(\Omega))^{\prime}=(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\Omega))^{\prime}\cap(\mathrm{L}_{h}^{6}(\Omega))^{\prime},
we conclude that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{W}_{h})
.
The existence of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\Omega))^{\prime},\mathbf{L}_{h}^{2}(\Omega)\bigr)

follows directly from
[
17
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Next, we aim to construct
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{L}_{h}^{6}(\Omega))^{\prime},\mathbf{H}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{\frac{6}{5}},\Omega)\bigr)
.
Let
q_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{6}_{h}(\Omega),
and
\mathcal{O}
be a convex bounded domain containing
\overline{\Omega}.
Now, define

\displaystyle\ell:=\begin{cases}|q_{h}|^{4}\,q_{h},&\text{in }\Omega,\\[2.0pt]
0,&\text{in }\mathcal{O}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(4.11)

Note that,
\ell\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathcal{O}),
satisfying the following

\displaystyle\|\ell\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}=\||q_{h}|^{4}q_{h}\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}=\|q_{h}\|^{5}_{0,6,\Omega}.

(4.12)

It now follows from
[
23
, Corollary 1]
that, there exists a unique
z\in\mathrm{W}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{1,\frac{6}{5}}(\mathcal{O})\cap\mathrm{W}^{2,\frac{6}{5}}(\mathcal{O})
satisfying the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle\Delta z=\ell\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\Omega,\hskip 10.00002ptz=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}},\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla z\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma_{p},

(4.13)

and additionally, there exists a positive constant
C_{\mathsf{reg}}
(depending only on
d
and
\Omega
) such that
z
satisfies the following stability estimate (the last equality is due to (
4.12
))

\displaystyle\|z\|_{2,\frac{6}{5},\mathcal{O}}\leq C_{\mathsf{reg}}\|\ell\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}=C_{\mathsf{reg}}\|q_{h}\|^{5}_{0,6,\Omega}.

(4.14)

Next, set
\varsigma:=(\nabla z)|_{\Omega}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega),
and note from (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varsigma)

\displaystyle=\ell=|q_{h}|^{4}q_{h}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\Omega,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{1,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|z\|_{2,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\leq C_{\mathsf{reg}}\|q_{h}\|^{5}_{0,6,\Omega}.

(4.16)

In turn, let us now define
\varsigma_{h}:=\Pi^{k}_{h}(\varsigma)
(
\Pi_{h}^{k}
being the Raviart–Thomas projection operator satisfying
[
14
, Equation 5.38, Equation 5.39]
). Further, using the boundedness of the projection operator, (
4.16
), and finally the continuous injection of
\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)
into
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),
we obtain the following estimate (the absorbed constant being independent of
h
)

\displaystyle\|\varsigma_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}=\|\Pi_{h}^{k}(\varsigma)\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathsf{reg}}\|q_{h}\|^{5}_{0,6,\Omega}.

(4.17)

In addition, from
[
14
, Equation (5.35)]
and (
4.15
), we obtain the following commutativity of the divergence operator and the projection operator

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varsigma_{h})=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\Pi^{k}_{h}(\varsigma))=\Pi_{h}^{k}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varsigma))=\Pi_{h}^{k}(|q_{h}|^{4}q_{h}),

(4.18)

and further, using (
4.15
), and the boundedness of

it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varsigma_{h})\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\leq\|q_{h}\|^{5}_{0,6,\Omega}.

(4.19)

Then, it suffices to define

which further implies that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{L}_{h}^{6}(\Omega))^{\prime},\mathbf{H}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{\frac{6}{5}},\Omega)\bigr).

\square

We now consider the following discrete ADR system for a fixed
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle a_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})+b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})+d_{2}^{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{h},

(4.20a)

\displaystyle b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})-c_{2,h}(\varphi_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=H_{h}(\psi_{h})\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi_{h}\in\Psi_{h},

(4.20b)

and employ the discrete version of Theorem

to discuss its parameter-robust unique solvability.

Theorem 4.3
(Well-posedness of discrete advection-diffusion-reaction equations)

Assume that

m\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)
. Then, for a fixed
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},
there exist positive constants
C_{d},C^{d}_{\alpha}
(depending on
\alpha
), such that, whenever
\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{{0,3,\Omega}}\leq\frac{C_{d}}{2C^{d}_{\alpha}},
there exists a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}
to the problem (
4.20
).
Furthermore, there is a positive constant
C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{\mathsf{d}}
independent of
h,
and
\varrho_{0}
, such that

\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\leq C^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{\mathsf{d}}\|m\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}.

Proof.

The boundedness of
a_{2}(\bullet,\bullet),b_{2,h}(\bullet,\bullet),\,c_{2,h}(\bullet,\bullet),
and symmetry and positive definiteness of
a_{2}(\bullet,\bullet)
and
c_{2,h}(\bullet,\bullet),
and the inf-sup stability of
a_{2}(\bullet,\bullet)
in
\mathbf{Z}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{h}:b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})=0,\,\forall\psi_{h}\in\Psi_{h}\}
follow analogously as in Theorem
3.3
. Therefore, to complete the rest of the proof, it is enough to prove the discrete inf-sup stability of
b_{2,h}(\bullet,\bullet)
and the discrete Braess condition.

Discrete inf-sup condition.
From the existence of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}
defined in Lemma
4.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{h}}\!\!\frac{\langle{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h}\rangle}{\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\neq\ell\in\Psi_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle{{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}_{\frac{6}{5}}}\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}(\ell),\psi_{h}\rangle}{\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}(\ell)\|_{\mathbf{W}}}=\sup_{0\neq\ell\in\Psi_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle\ell,\psi_{h}\rangle}{\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}(\ell)\|_{\mathbf{W}}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\|^{-1}_{\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{W}_{h})}\sup_{0\neq\ell\in\Psi_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle\ell,\psi_{h}\rangle}{\|\ell\|_{\Psi_{h}^{\prime}}}=\tilde{C}_{b}\|\psi_{h}\|_{\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

where
\tilde{C}_{b}=\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{d}}\|^{-1}_{\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{W}_{h})},
is independent of
\alpha,\varrho_{0},h
, and hence the discrete stability of inf-sup is achieved.

Discrete Braess condition.
Setting
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}
,
\psi_{h}=0,
and exploiting
\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}=|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},0)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},
one obtains

\displaystyle\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}\frac{a_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})+b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\frac{\alpha^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},0)|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}=\frac{\alpha^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}.

(4.21)

Next, taking the particular values
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0},
and
\psi_{h}=\mathrm{sgn}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}|^{\frac{1}{5}}
, one obtains the following

\displaystyle(\mathrm{LHS})_{\eqref{eq:Braess.disc.1st}}\geq\frac{a_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{0})+b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\mathrm{sgn}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}|^{\frac{1}{5}})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{0},\mathrm{sgn}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h})|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}|^{\frac{1}{5}})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|^{\frac{6}{5}}_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}}{\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|^{\frac{1}{5}}_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}}=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}.

(4.22)

Now, let us combine (
4.21
) and (
4.22
) to obtain the following

\displaystyle\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}\frac{a_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})+b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\max\biggl({\frac{\alpha^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}},\frac{1}{2}\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}}\biggr).

(4.23)

Denoting
\gamma:=\frac{\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}\in[0,1)
, and using (
4.23
), we establish the following inequality

\displaystyle\max\biggl({\frac{\alpha^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}},\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}}\biggr)\geq\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}\biggl(\max_{t\in[0,1)}\bigl[\gamma^{2},\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}\bigr]\biggr).

On the other hand, if we denote
\gamma_{\mathsf{d}}:=\biggl(\max_{t\in[0,1)}\bigl[\gamma^{2},\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}\bigr]\biggr),
we can readily conclude that

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}\frac{a_{2}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})+b_{2,h}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\gamma_{\mathsf{d}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}.

An appeal to Theorem
2.1
now yields the well-posedness of the discrete reaction-diffusion system (
4.7c
) and (
4.7d
) without the advection part. Additionally, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\tau_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq C_{d}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

(4.24)

where
C_{d}
is the positive constant from
(
2.17
), independent of the model and discretisation parameters.

Finally, for the well-posedness of the uncoupled discrete ADR equations, for a fixed
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},
we consider another bilinear form
\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{ADR}}_{\mathsf{d}}:\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h}):=\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})+d_{2}^{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\varphi_{h}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}.

(4.25)

Thus, to show the well-posedness of (
4.7c
)-(
4.7d
), we now analyse the problem: Find
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})=H_{h}(\psi_{h}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi_{h}\in\Psi_{h}.

From the boundedness of

,

\displaystyle d_{2}^{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\varphi_{h})\leq C^{d}_{\alpha}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\,|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

(4.26)

where

, combined with (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), we can assert that

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})\in{\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},{\psi_{h}})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\bigl[C_{d}-C^{d}_{\alpha}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\bigr]|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

for all

Hence, under the assumption that

\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\leq\frac{C_{d}}{2C^{d}_{\alpha}}

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})\in{\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{d}}^{\mathsf{ADR}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h};\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})}{|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\psi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\geq\frac{C_{d}}{2}|\!|\!|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})|\!|\!|_{\mathbf{W}\times\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

(4.27)

Therefore, the discrete

) in (

Theorem 4.4
(Well-posedness of the fully-coupled discrete problem)

\displaystyle L^{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathcal{S}}<1
. Then, the coupled problem (
4.7
) has a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}\times\Psi_{h}

\displaystyle+{\bigl(\max(2C_{1},C_{2})h^{l}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l,\Omega}+\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l,3,\Omega})\bigr)^{2}},\,\text{and}

\displaystyle+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}|p_{2}-P_{h}p_{2}|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}|p_{3}-P_{h}p_{3}|_{1,\frac{3}{2},\mathcal{T}_{h}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}^{C\acute{e}a}\max\bigl(C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}\bigr)h^{l}\bigl(|p_{1}|_{l,\Omega}+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}|p_{2}|_{l+1,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}|p_{3}|_{l+1,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}\bigr),

where
C_{4}
is same as in
[
17
, Theorem 4.6]
.
Next, for the error estimate for flux, at first take
m=0
and
t=0
in (
4.40a
), and then,
m=0
and
t=\frac{6}{5}
in (
4.40b
), which lead to

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{1}C_{\mathsf{V}}h^{l}\,|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|_{l,\Omega},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\zeta})\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}\leq C_{2}C_{\mathsf{V}}h^{l}|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}|_{l,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}.

In turn, owing to Remark
4.1
, we derive the error bound on the flux as follows

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{W}}\leq C_{\mathsf{F}}\,\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\mathbf{W}_{h})\leq C_{\mathsf{F}}\max(C_{1},C_{2})h^{l}(\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{l,\Omega}+\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{0,\frac{6}{5},\Omega}).

Lastly, for the concentration we employ the decomposition
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2},
and the properties of the sum norm (
4.5
), and apply (
4.40c
) with
m=0
and
t=2
, and (
4.40d
) with
t=0
and
t=6,
summed over
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We combine that with the following trace inequality to control the jump term in the broken norms

h_{F}^{1-t}\|[\![\varphi_{j}-P_{h}\varphi_{j}]\!]\|^{t}_{0,t,F}=h_{F}^{1-t}\|[\![P_{h}\varphi_{j}]\!]\|^{t}_{0,t,F}\leq C_{5}h^{lt}|\varphi_{j}|^{t}_{l+1,r,\omega_{F}},

where
\omega_{F}
stands for the usual face patch. Therefore, we readily obtain the following bound

\displaystyle\|\varphi-P_{h}\varphi\|_{\widehat{\Psi}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{C\acute{e}a}(\|\varphi_{1}-P_{h}\varphi_{1}\|_{0,6,\Omega}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}|\varphi_{2}-P_{h}\varphi_{2}|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{C\acute{e}a}\max\bigl(C_{2},C_{3},C_{5}\bigr)h^{l}\bigl(|\varphi_{1}|_{l,6,\Omega}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}|\varphi_{2}|_{l+1,\Omega}\bigr),

where
C_{5}>0
is independent of all the parameters. This completes the proof.

\square

2.3
).

5
Operator preconditioning

This section discusses preconditioners for the monolithic coupled system arising from the weighted norms introduced in the previous sections which is robust with respect to all the model parameters expect for coefficient of diffusion.

First, for
0<s<\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{J}_{s}:\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)\to\bigl(\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega)\bigr)^{\prime}
the canonical duality map defined as

\langle{\mathcal{J}}_{s}(\theta_{1}),\theta_{2}\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}|\theta_{1}|^{s-2}\theta_{1}\theta_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega).

(5.1)

Similarly, mimicking the operator notation
-\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits
that induces the div-div bilinear form, we define
-\nabla\mathcal{J}_{s}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)
as

{\langle-\nabla\mathcal{J}_{s}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1}),\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2}\rangle:={\langle}\mathcal{J}_{s}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1}{),\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2}\rangle},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2}\ \text{such that}\ \mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{i}\in\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega).}

(5.2)

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})
the Gâteaux derivative of the nonlinear operator
d_{1}(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)
, i.e.,
|\boldsymbol{u}|\boldsymbol{u}
at the state
\boldsymbol{u}
in the direction of
\delta\boldsymbol{u}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{2}(\ast)
by the linearisation of
d_{2}^{\ast}(\bullet,\bullet)
around
\ast
. The linearised system corresponding to (
1.1
) takes the following form

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{f}(\hat{\varphi})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\nabla\varphi+{\alpha^{-1}}\mathcal{H}_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\varphi

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\varrho_{0}\varphi

\displaystyle=-m\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $\Omega$},

with the same boundary conditions as in (
1.1
).
In addition, consider the Newton–Raphson linearisation, which starting from an initial guess
\boldsymbol{u}^{0},p^{0},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{0},\varphi^{0}
, and iterating on
m=1,2,\ldots
until convergence, seeks velocity, pressure, flux, and concentration increments
(\delta\boldsymbol{u},\delta p,\delta\boldsymbol{\zeta},\delta\varphi)
in the space

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{V}}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}\times\underline{\mathbf{W}}\times\underline{\Psi}

\displaystyle:=\bigl[\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\{\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\}^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\bigl[\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1}(\Omega)+\{\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}\}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{W}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)\bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\bigl[(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{-1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\alpha)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{2}\cap\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{\frac{6}{5}},\Omega)\bigr]\times\bigl[(\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\alpha)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)+\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)\bigr],

(5.4)

such that the following holds in
\underline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime}\times\underline{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}\times\underline{\Psi}^{\prime}

\begin{pmatrix}[\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})]\mathcal{I}&\nabla&0&0\\
-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits&0&0&0\\
\alpha^{-1}\varphi^{m}&0&\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{I}&-\nabla+\alpha^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\\
0&0&\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits&-\varrho_{0}\mathcal{I}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\boldsymbol{u}\\
\delta p\\
\delta\boldsymbol{\zeta}\\
\delta\varphi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}F-\{\kappa^{-1}+|\boldsymbol{u}^{m}|\}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})-\nabla{p}^{m}\\
G+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits{\boldsymbol{u}}^{m}\\
-\alpha^{-1}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{m}+\nabla\varphi^{m}-\alpha^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\varphi^{m}\\
H-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{m}-\varrho_{0}\varphi^{m}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.5)

Here,
\mathcal{I}
denotes the identity operator at the continuous level, but for the discrete case it stands for the corresponding mass matrix (either for velocity or pressure or flux or concentration). Further, we note that, in weak form, the action of the operator
\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})\mathcal{I}:\underline{\mathbf{V}}\to\underline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}
reads

\langle\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})(\delta\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\mathtt{F}|\boldsymbol{u}^{m}|\delta\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}+\int_{\Omega}\mathtt{F}|\boldsymbol{u}^{m}|^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\delta\boldsymbol{u})(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}),

and

\langle\mathcal{H}_{2}(\boldsymbol{u}^{m})\boldsymbol{\tau},\varphi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau})\varphi.

Then, update the solution as
(\boldsymbol{u}^{m+1},p^{m+1},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{m+1},\varphi^{m+1})=(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}+\delta\boldsymbol{u},p^{m}+\delta p,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{m}+\delta\boldsymbol{\zeta},\varphi^{m}+\delta\varphi)
.
Next, we follow the approach recently advanced in
[
12
, Section 3]
to propose a robust block-diagonal preconditioner for (
5.5
).
We construct block-diagonal preconditioners for the Darcy–Forchheimer velocity and pressure blocks following

[
17
]
. On the other hand, and differently from
[
12
]
and
[
17
]
, we readily note that the sub-blocks associated with the ARD equations consist of a perturbed,
non-symmetric
saddle-point of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{-1}\mathbb{A}&\mathbb{B}^{*}+\alpha^{-1}\mathbb{D}\\
\mathbb{B}&-\varrho_{0}\mathbb{C}\end{pmatrix},

(5.6)

where we are abusing of notation to emphasise the parameter dependence of the different blocks.
Let us first concentrate on the discrete case since it simplifies the discussion as
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and so the Banach structure is not required. In this case,
\mathbb{A}:\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\to\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)^{\prime}
denotes the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
mass map in
\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
,
\mathbb{B}:\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{\prime}
is the divergence operator,
\mathbb{D}
is the
mixed
advection coupling operator (
\mathbb{D}\varphi\approx\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\varphi
), and
\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{\prime}
is the scalar mass map in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Therefore a typical Riesz map preconditioner, also based on a Schur complement approximation, is motivated by
[
8
, Sect. 3.1]
(see also
[
6
, Section 1]
for the symmetric, unperturbed case). The guiding principle is to scale the flux space with
\alpha^{-1}
, the concentration space with
\varrho_{0}
, and ensure that the Schur complement is spectrally equivalent to a properly weighted operator.
In this case, a block-diagonal preconditioner
reads as follows (using a complete Schur complement structure for the (2,2)-block and an incomplete one for the (1,1)-block)

\mathrm{diag}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})=\begin{pmatrix}(\alpha^{-1}\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}^{*}\mathbb{B})^{-1}&0\\
0&([1+\varrho_{0}]\,\mathbb{C})^{-1}+(\varrho_{0}\mathbb{C}+\alpha\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{*}+\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{D})^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

(5.7)

or, equivalent Schur complement structure for the (1,1)-block
(and note that it is also possible to derive block-triangular versions, see for e.g.,
[
30
]
).
For the the advection term the preconditioner part
\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{D}
is not symmetric and it corresponds to perturbation with respect to the diffusion term
\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{*}
if
\boldsymbol{u}^{m}
is bounded. However, for
\alpha\ll 1
, the advection term
\mathbb{D}
becomes dominant and the Schur complement changes from diffusion–reaction to transport–reaction and it can destroy the spectral equivalence. For a fully computable preconditioner associated with (
5.7
), we approximate the top diagonal block by
(\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{I}-\alpha^{-1}\nabla(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits))^{-1}
,
where
\mathcal{I}-\nabla(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)

is the Raviart–Thomas full matrix. Finally, inspired by (
5.7
), and considering another formulation inspired by the primal formulation, we propose the following preconditioners (
\mathcal{B}^{1,1}_{\mathsf{DFARD}},\mathcal{B}^{1,2}_{\mathsf{DFARD}},\mathcal{B}^{2,1}_{\mathsf{DFARD}},
and
\mathcal{B}^{2,2}_{\mathsf{DFARD}}
) for the coupled monolithic problem (
5.3
), where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{i,j}_{\mathsf{DFARD}}:=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{i}&0&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{Q}_{i}&0&0\\
0&0&\mathcal{R}_{j}&0\\
0&0&0&\mathcal{S}_{j}\end{pmatrix},

(5.8)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle:={\bigl([\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})]\,\mathcal{I}-[\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})]\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bigr)^{-1}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{Q}_{1}:=[\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})]^{-1}{\mathcal{I}}^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}

\displaystyle:={\bigl([\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})]\mathcal{I}-\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bigr)^{-1}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{Q}_{2}:={\mathcal{I}^{-1}+(-\kappa\Delta)^{-1}-\bigl([\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})]^{-1}\Delta_{\frac{3}{2}}\bigr)^{-1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle:=(\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\nabla\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits+\alpha^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\nabla\cdot)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{S}_{1}:={(1+\varrho_{0})\,\mathcal{I}^{-1}+(\varrho_{0}\mathcal{I}-\alpha\Delta)^{-1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}

\displaystyle:=(\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\nabla\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{S}_{2}:={(1+\varrho_{0})\,\mathcal{I}^{-1}+(\varrho_{0}\mathcal{I}-\alpha\Delta+\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\nabla)^{-1}}.

A conclusion from Table
A.3
is that whenever
\frac{|\hat{\mathbf{u}}|h}{\alpha}\ll 1
, both the condition numbers of the preconditioned system and the MINRES iteration counts remain well controlled. Outside this regime, however, the performance of the preconditioner deteriorates and becomes inconsistent.
Possible remedies include a better stabilisation for the advection term
\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\varphi
(pure upwind in general will not yield robust preconditioners for extreme regimes).
It is well established
[
21
]
that classical discretisations of convection-dominated systems give rise to highly nonnormal discrete operators. Such nonnormality is not adequately reflected by the eigenvalues of the preconditioned systems alone; instead, their behavior deteriorates markedly by increasing Péclet number and mesh refinement. As a consequence, Krylov subspace methods will struggle for this type of problems.

Another difficulty already mentioned in Section
5
is the nonsymmetric nature of the underlying discrete operators. Operator preconditioning and the usual Schur complement structures are not yet systematically developed. Here we have adopted a structure motivated by symmetric elliptic operators, which do not seem to provide definitive or robust conclusions.
See
[
32
]
for a detailed review of the challenges associated with preconditioning nonsymmetric systems.
