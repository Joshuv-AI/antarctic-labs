 ABSTRACT

This report documents the completion of the Advanced Reactor Technologies Level 3 Milestone (M3AT-
26OR0605054), “Provide status on microstructural studies of nuclear graphite,” due May 1, 2026. The
report summarizes recent progress in the development of the Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library,
including datasets that have been generated, curated, and submitted for publication, as part of the broader
effort to establish and integrate the library within the Nuclear Data Management and Analysis System
(NDMAS) platform.

The Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library is being developed to provide a curated repository of
microstructural datasets for graphite materials used in nuclear applications. This effort compiles
multiscale characterization data obtained from advanced imaging techniques, including x-ray computed
tomography, electron microscopy, and complementary methods used to investigate the internal structure
of nuclear graphite. The library aims to preserve and organize datasets from both historical and modern
graphite grades, enabling systematic analysis of key microstructural features such as porosity, filler
morphology, and structural anisotropy. A part of the library also focuses on datasets acquired from
specimens exposed to in-service conditions, including oxidation, neutron irradiation, and molten salt
impregnation. In addition, the library incorporates related carbon-based materials that can serve as
reference systems for improving the interpretation and understanding of nuclear graphite behavior.

To ensure long-term data accessibility, the microstructure library is being integrated into NDMAS, a data
repository maintained by Idaho National Laboratory. The integration process includes standardized data
formatting, metadata documentation, and future expansion of dataset collection. Beyond supporting
traditional characterization efforts, the library is intended to provide training datasets for artificial
intelligence (AI) and machine learning approaches aimed at automated microstructural analysis. The
resulting resource will support research, modeling, and industry efforts related to the development and
qualification of graphite materials for advanced nuclear reactor systems.

 1. OBJECTIVES OF THE LIBRARY OF MICROSTRUCTURES

This report documents the completion of the Advanced Reactor Technologies Level 3 Milestone (M3AT-
26OR0605054), “Provide status on microstructural studies of nuclear graphite,” due May 1, 2026. The
objective of this task is to summarize recent progress in microstructural characterization efforts and the
ongoing development of the Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library, including data curation, integration
into the Nuclear Data Management and Analysis System (NDMAS) platform, and dissemination of
results through technical reports and peer-reviewed publications. A greater emphasis during this reporting
period has been placed on the integration of the microstructure library with the NDMAS platform,
including data standardization, metadata development, and initial dataset organization.

 journal target: Journal of Nuclear Materials

Preserving and organizing microstructural datasets will provide substantial value to the US operators,
reactor developers, and researchers with limited prior experience in nuclear graphite by enabling direct
comparison of graphite grades and improved understanding of how microstructure influences material
behavior. A centralized library allows users to evaluate key features such as porosity, filler morphology,
binder distribution, and texture, as well as track the evolution of these features under various in-service
conditions.

The preservation of datasets from historical graphite grades is particularly valuable because these
materials serve as important reference systems. Many historical grades are associated with extensive
irradiation and performance databases, making them ideal benchmark materials for validating models and
comparing the expected behavior of newly developed grades. These legacy datasets provide critical
context for understanding dimensional change, property evolution, and failure mechanisms in current
reactor applications.

The library also supports comparison across candidate nuclear graphite grades, including both coarse- and
fine-grained materials, which often exhibit distinct mechanical and irradiation responses. For example,
fine-grained graphites can display different fracture behavior and crack evolution pathways compared
with coarser grades, requiring adapted mechanical testing methodologies to properly capture their
response. A microstructure-informed approach is therefore essential for understanding the origin of these
differences and for connecting graphite performance to the underlying pore structure and phase
morphology.

A dedicated portion of the library is focused on comparing nominally identical graphite grades
manufactured at different points in time. This comparison is intended to demonstrate that, even when
materials are produced by the same manufacturer under similar processing conditions, the resulting
microstructure can evolve due to changes in raw material sources, feedstock characteristics, or subtle
manufacturing variations; such changes may ultimately influence the performance of the final product.

A portion of the library focuses on datasets obtained from graphite exposed to oxidation and neutron
irradiation, areas where publicly available microstructural data remain limited. Expanding access to these
datasets is particularly important as reference data and for improving the understanding of degradation
mechanisms and supporting the interpretation of inspection data from reactor components. These datasets
can help reactor operators, designers, and model developers better understand how graphite
microstructure evolves as a function of irradiation dose, oxidation state, and service environment.

The Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library has several key objectives:

 Preserve and organize microstructural datasets obtained from nuclear graphite characterization
 campaigns, including legacy materials and modern reactor grades.
 Provide a standardized and curated dataset repository that can be accessed and analyzed through the
 NDMAS platform.

 2

 Enable advanced characterization workflows by providing reference datasets that support quantitative
 analysis of graphite microstructure.
 Support the development of AI and machine learning approaches for segmentation, feature
 recognition, super-resolution, and automated microstructural quantification.
 Facilitate collaboration with industry and research partners by providing reliable microstructural data
 to support graphite qualification, modeling, inspection, and reactor design.

Through these objectives, the library aims to serve as a centralized resource for the nuclear materials
community, bridging historical knowledge, modern characterization, AI-driven analysis, and industrial
application.

 2. STRUCTURE OF THE LIBRARY

The library datasets are organized using a hierarchical framework in which data are first grouped by
graphite grade, then by material condition (e.g., as-received, oxidized, neutron-irradiated, or infiltrated),
and finally by characterization method. A schematic of the structure of the library is given in Figure 1.

 Figure 1. Structure of the library of microstructures and potential uses of the data.

As part of the ongoing integration of the Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library with NDMAS, future
efforts will focus on developing a standardized material data sheet for each graphite grade, including
relevant material information, key references, and representative microscopy data. Within each branch of
the hierarchy, datasets are accompanied by a text file that describes the imaging parameters and other
relevant information. For materials that underwent oxidation, irradiation, or exposure to other conditions,
relevant condition-specific descriptors are included.

This hierarchical organization is particularly valuable for multimodal microstructural correlation because
it enables multiple datasets acquired from the same graphite grade and service condition to be linked
across techniques.

 3. INTEGRATION WITH NDMAS AND THE NUCLEAR RESEARCH DATA SYSTEM

To ensure long-term preservation and accessibility, the microstructure library is being integrated into the
NDMAS and the Nuclear Research Data System (NRDS) platforms managed by Idaho National
Laboratory. The integration process involves several steps:

 3

 1. Data formatting and standardization
 Microstructural datasets are converted into the original data format and standardized imaging
 formats.
 2. Metadata generation
 Metadata records are included to document the original file, acquisition parameters, and
 processing steps associated with each dataset.
 3. Data upload
 The datasets and metadata are uploaded to the NRDS infrastructure, where they are stored,
 and listed on the NDMAS website.
 4. Repository indexing and accessibility
 Once uploaded, the datasets become part of the NDMAS database, which allows the data to
 be searched and provides controlled access and future expansion of the library.

The microstructure library’s integration with NDMAS ensures that it benefits traceability, version control,
and long-term data storage.

 4. ROLE OF THE LIBRARY IN AI-DRIVEN MATERIALS CHARACTERIZATION

An important objective of the Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library is to support the development of
AI tools for microstructural characterization, particularly in the areas of super-resolution image
enhancement, automated segmentation, and AI microstructural data generation.

Microstructural datasets acquired through techniques such as x-ray computed tomography, scanning
electron microscopy, and focused ion beam–scanning electron microscopy tomography often involve
trade-offs between spatial resolution, field of view, and acquisition time. AI-based super-resolution
methods provide a promising approach to address these limitations by enhancing the effective resolution
of existing datasets. Using training data derived from high-resolution microstructural images, super-
resolution algorithms can reconstruct finer structural details in lower-resolution datasets, improving the
visualization and quantification of features such as small pores, microcracks, and filler particle
boundaries.

In addition to super-resolution, the microstructure library supports the development of AI segmentation
workflows. Accurate segmentation of graphite microstructures remains a challenging task due to the
complex contrast variations between binder, filler, and porosity phases. By providing curated and
annotated datasets, the library enables the training and validation of deep learning models that can
automatically identify and segment key microstructural features and help reactor designers assess
important parameters such as filler particle size or porosity content. These approaches can significantly
reduce operator bias and improve the reproducibility and efficiency of microstructural analysis.

Another activity related to the project is the generation of synthetic microstructures derived from scarce
datasets, such as irradiated or oxidized specimens. This capability will help expand microstructure-
informed models.

The availability of a structured dataset repository also facilitates the benchmarking and comparison of AI
algorithms across different imaging modalities and graphite grades. As the library continues to expand, it
will provide increasingly robust datasets for training AI models and improving automated characterization
tools. These developments are expected to enhance the extraction of quantitative microstructural
information and support advanced modeling efforts related to the behavior of nuclear graphite materials.

 5. SUMMARY

The Nuclear Graphite Microstructure Library represents an important step toward establishing a centralized,
curated repository of microstructural data for nuclear graphite materials. Through integration with the
NDMAS platform, the library ensures long-term data storage and accessibility.

By supporting advanced characterization methods along with emerging AI-driven analysis approaches, the
library will contribute to improved understanding of graphite behavior and provide valuable resources for
the development and qualification of materials for advanced nuclear reactors.
