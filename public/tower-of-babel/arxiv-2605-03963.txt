Constraining F-theory Model Building with QCD Axions
Keren Chen 1, Qinjian Lou 1, Yi-Nan Wang 1,2

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate axion physics in 4D F-theory MSSM models. We derive the axion coupling term with QCD gauge fields and the axion potential from a top-down perspective, from both IIB superstring and the dual M-theory picture. For the explicit geometric model, we employ the “quadrillion” landscape of 4D F-theory models with the exact Standard Model chiral spectrum, and study simple base threefolds such as ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} , ℙ 1 × ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2} , the generalized Hirzebruch threefold 𝔽 ~ 3 \tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{3} and ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} . We derive exclusion constraints on the Kähler moduli space of the base threefold from the CP violation angle, the Standard Model gauge coupling constants and the stretched Kähler cone condition. We find stringent constraints on the set of base divisors that should be rigid or rigidified by the inclusion of flux. For the allowed regions of the parameter space, we estimate the typical mass of detectable QCD axions to be around 10 − 9 10^{-9} eV, and the axion decay constant to be around f a ∼ 10 15 f_{a}\sim 10^{15} GeV.

II.1
QCD axion

In QCD, it is allowed to add a topological

L_{\theta}=\frac{\theta}{32\pi^{2}}F^{\mu\nu}_{a}\tilde{F}_{a\mu\nu}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a\partial^{\mu}a-\frac{m^{2}_{a,0}}{2}a^{2}-\frac{\partial^{\mu}a}{f}\sum_{F}\bar{\psi}_{c}c_{F}\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{F}

(7)

\displaystyle+c_{GG}\frac{\alpha_{3}}{4\pi}\frac{a}{f}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}^{\mu\nu,a}+c_{WW}\frac{\alpha_{2}}{4\pi}\frac{a}{f}W^{A}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{W}^{\mu\nu,A}

\displaystyle+c_{BB}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4\pi}\frac{a}{f}B_{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}\,.

II.3
RG flow of the gauge coupling constants

At one-loop level, the renormalization group equations for the gauge couplings
g_{i}
can be written as

\mu\frac{dg_{i}}{d\mu}=\frac{b_{i}}{16\pi^{2}}g_{i}^{3}\,,

(37)

where the coefficients
b_{i}
depend on the particle content of the theory. For convenient, we will discuss RG flow with the GUT normalization in this section, i.e.
g_{1}=\sqrt{5/3}g_{Y}
.

In the Standard Model (SM), using the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme, the one-loop beta function coefficients are

(b_{1},\,b_{2},\,b_{3})_{\text{SM}}=\left(\frac{41}{10},\,-\frac{19}{6},\,-7\right).

(38)

In the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM), the coefficients become

(b_{1},\,b_{2},\,b_{3})_{\text{MSSM}}=\left(\frac{33}{5},\,1,\,-3\right).

(39)

The difference arises from the enlarged particle spectrum in the MSSM, where each Standard Model particle is accompanied by a superpartner. As a result, additional fermionic (gaugino, Higgsino) and scalar (sfermion) degrees of freedom modify the running of the gauge couplings, generally reducing the magnitude of asymptotic freedom and enabling gauge coupling unification at a high energy scale.

The supersymmetry breaking scale is assumed to lie in the range

M_{\text{SUSY}}\sim 10^{4}\text{--}10^{11}\,\text{GeV}
in this paper.
In our setup, the bulk volume is taken to be
V_{b}=\mathcal{O}(10\text{--}10^{2})
in string units.
The Kaluza-Klein scale is therefore estimated as

M_{\mathrm{KK}}\sim\frac{M_{s}}{V_{b}^{1/6}}\,,

(40)

which typically gives
M_{\mathrm{KK}}\sim 10^{16}\,\text{GeV}
for a string scale close to the Planck scale.
Accordingly, the four-dimensional effective field theory description is expected to be valid up to energies

\mu\lesssim 10^{14}\,\text{GeV}
, below the Kaluza-Klein threshold.

We apply the model presented in
42
to calculate the RG flow of gauge coupling constants from the electroweak scale to
10^{14}\text{GeV}
.

We evolve the gauge couplings from the electroweak scale to
\mu\sim 10^{14}\,\text{GeV}
using the one-loop renormalization group equations

\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}(\mu)}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}(M_{Z})}-\frac{b_{i}^{\mathrm{SM}}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{M_{\text{SUSY}}}{M_{Z}}-\frac{b_{i}^{\mathrm{MSSM}}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\mu}{M_{\text{SUSY}}}\,.

(41)

Here

denotes the mass of the
Z

M_{Z}\simeq 91\,\text{GeV}\simeq 10^{11}\,\text{eV}.

(42)

We use it as the reference scale where the gauge couplings are experimentally measured.

We take

\displaystyle(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})_{\mathrm{SM}}=\left(\frac{41}{10},-\frac{19}{6},-7\right),

(43)

\displaystyle(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})_{\mathrm{MSSM}}=\left(\frac{33}{5},1,-3\right),

\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}(M_{Z})}\simeq 59,\quad\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}(M_{Z})}\simeq 29.6,\quad\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}(M_{Z})}\simeq 8.5\,.

(44)

Now evaluate the running for two representative values of the supersymmetry breaking scale.

IV
Results for different base geometries

In this section, we compare the constraints from
\theta_{\rm QCD}
and the gauge couplings on the Kähler moduli space for different base geometries. Our goal is not only to determine whether there exist points in the stretched Kähler cone satisfying

\theta_{\rm QCD}<10^{-10},

but also to understand whether the corresponding gauge couplings can be sufficiently large from the phenomenological point of view.

A basic difficulty is that the supersymmetry breaking scale is not known. Since the gauge couplings run logarithmically, a lower supersymmetry breaking scale generally leads to larger gauge couplings at the UV scale relevant to our geometric construction. In addition, if one allows extra pairs of chiral fermion, then the gauge couplings are also enhanced under the RG flow. Therefore, within our present analysis, we cannot assign a unique value to the gauge couplings at the compactification scale. Instead, what can be robustly extracted is a conservative lower bound on each gauge coupling, obtained in the minimal case without additional fermions. Any extra fermion or lower supersymmetry breaking scale would only increase the gauge couplings.

Motivated by this, we divide the models into three classes, denoted by “
Y
”, “
O
”, and “
N
”. A model is said to be of type “
Y
” if, for any supersymmetry breaking scale within the range we regard as physically plausible, one can find points in the allowed moduli space such that
\theta_{\rm QCD}<10^{-10}
and all three gauge couplings are simultaneously above their respective lower bounds. In this sense, the “
Y
” models are robustly viable against the uncertainty in the RG running, if no additional non-chiral matter fields are introduced. On the other hand, a model is said to be of type “
N
” if, for every point in the allowed moduli space satisfying
\theta_{\rm QCD}<10^{-10}
, at least one gauge coupling is below its corresponding lower bound. Such models are therefore robustly excluded in our setup. The remaining models are classified as type “
O
”: they are not definitively excluded, but their viability depends on details of the supersymmetry breaking scale. To make the classification more conservative, we slightly enlarge the parameter region assigned to the class “
O
”. The details of the classification criteria is shown in Appendix
A
, that are slightly shifted from the results in section
II.3
.

With this classification in hand, we now study the explicit base geometries
\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
, and
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in turn.

VII
conclusion

In this work, we analyzed the physical constraints on the 4D F-theory MSSM models. The first constraint comes from the lower bound of the UV value of gauge couplings (see Appendix
A
), setting an upper bound on the Kähler moduli of the base threefold
B_{3}
. The second constraint is from the upper bound of CP violation angle
\theta_{\rm QCD}
, which we derive from the scalar potential
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{UV}+\mathcal{V}_{IR}
of the axions
a_{i}
(axion-like-particles) from the integration of
C_{4}
gauge field over 4-cycles
D_{i}
on the base
B_{3}
. The
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
piece arise from the non-perturbative superpotential in 4D
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity, arised from the Euclidean D3-brane wrapping 4-cycles
D_{\alpha}
from the IIB perspective. Such 4-cycle should be either rigid or rigidified after the inclusion of flux, to have a non-zero contribution to
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
and to stabilize all the Kähler moduli of the base
B_{3}
.

More explicitly, we investigate the models in the ensemble of
42
for the example of bases
B_{3}=\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. With the constraints from
\alpha_{i}
,
\theta_{\rm QCD}
and the 1-stretched Kähler cone condition, we plot the allowed subregion of the Kähler moduli space of
B_{3}
. Here are the general findings:

1.

To have a strong physical constraint on the Kähler moduli space, we should assume that at least
h^{1,1}(B_{3})
linearly independent divisors on
B_{3}
, called
D_{\alpha}
are rigid or rigidified. This is also important to stabilize all the Kähler moduli. In constrast, if the divisors are all non-rigid, no
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
arises from the string theory perspective, and the IR axion mass solely arises from the QCD effect
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
. In such a case the experiment would not impose any constraint on the string geometry. More generally, we consider that the SU(3) gauge group lives on the divisor
D_{\text{SU(3)}}
. If
D_{\text{SU(3)}}
cannot be expressed as linear combination of
D_{\alpha}
. The
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
will not affect the
\theta_{QCD}
, so the
\theta_{QCD}
does not constrain the string geometry. But
\mathcal{V}_{UV}
may continue to influence the axions’ mass and produce ALPs.

2.

The constraint on the Kähler moduli space is insensitive to the Pfaffian coefficients
A_{D}
. As shown in Figs
2
-
6
, even if we vary the Pfaffian coefficients by 3 orders of magnitude, the exclusion curves only slightly differ.

3.

As for
f_{a}
, we estimate that the axion decay constant
f_{a}
is generally close to the string scale in the models used in this paper. For
m_{a}
, it may be subject to further UV corrections, and we generally allow our string-theoretic axions to locate in the right and bottom side of the QCD axion line in Fig
1
. We pinpoint the axion mass and axion decay constant that we acquire in the allowed region of moduli space in Fig.
10
. We can see the
f_{a}
deed close to the string scale, and the point is locate to the region that we described. Note that the axion masses in our result fit in the range of
18
for weakly coupled IIB setups.

An important assumption to obtain the non-zero value
\theta_{\rm QCD}
is the undetermined complex phase
\phi_{\alpha}
of the Pfaffian
A_{\alpha}
and the
\phi_{0}
of
W_{0}
in (
29
), which we take as an
\mathcal{O}(1)
random number in this work. If there are some mechanisms for these complex phases to align, we will obtain a smaller
\theta_{\rm QCD}
from the UV potential, and that would be subject to future investigations.

In this work we did not consider the GUT type models, i.e. realizing the Standard Model gauge group as a subgroup of a larger non-abelian gauge group on the base
B_{3}
, including the tuned
SU(5)
models or the non-Higgsable
E_{7}
models
80
;
79
;
81
, and the latter can be naturally realized on the largest part of the 4D F-theory landscape
106
;
104
. The common challenges of these models are to find the hypercharge flux which breaks the GUT gauge group down to the Standard Model gauge group, and a natural realization of doublet-triplet splitting. It is expected that for such F-theory GUT models, the physical constraints analyzed in this paper are less stringent, due to more freedom to tune the Kähler parameters, the gauge coupling and superpotentials.

In the future, an obviously important question is to explicitly solve the candidate
G_{4}
flux responsible for the rigidification of divisors
D_{\alpha}
. This is generally a hard problem, as the horizontal flux and remainder flux shall be included in the discussion. As shown in section
VI
, the typical order of magnitude for
h^{2,2}(Y_{4})
is around
\mathcal{O}(10^{3})
, which is much larger than the number of basis for the vertical flux. We will leave this problem to future investigations.

Of course, one can extend the discussion to
B_{3}
with a larger
h^{1,1}
in the ensemble of 4319 bases in
42
, and see if they are qualitatively different from the simple models in this paper.

Appendix A
Introduction to the data files

The public data files summarize the classification of models obtained by
scanning the stretched Kähler cone for the three bases

\displaystyle B_{3}=\mathbb{P}^{3}\,,\qquad B_{3}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\,,

(157)

\displaystyle B_{3}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}}^{3}\,,\quad B_{3}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\,.

For
\mathbb{P}^{3}
we use the basis
D_{1}=H
, so that
[i]
denotes
iH
.
For
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}^{3}
we use the divisor basis

D_{1}=S\,,\qquad D_{2}=H\,,

(158)

so that
[i,j]
denotes
iD_{1}+jD_{2}
. For

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
we use the basis
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
, and
[i,j,k]

denotes
iD_{1}+jD_{2}+kD_{3}
. The tables list the distinct choices of

(\mathcal{S}_{7},\mathcal{S}_{9})
used in the final scan.

For
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, the data files are one-dimensional tables with filenames

\texttt{p3\_s7=[i]\_s9=[j].txt}\,,

(159)

which indicate

\mathcal{S}_{7}=iH\,,\qquad\mathcal{S}_{9}=jH\,.

(160)

Each row lists one rigid divisor, the classification, the smallest value of

\theta_{\rm QCD}
found in the scan, the three inverse gauge couplings, and
the point counts used in the classification.

For the two bases with
h^{1,1}(B_{3})=2
, the data files are arranged in a
matrix format. Their filenames are

p1p2_s7=[i,j]_s9=[k,l].txt

(161)

for
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and

f3_s7=[i,j]_s9=[k,l].txt

(162)

for
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}^{3}
. The filename specifies

\mathcal{S}_{7}=iD_{1}+jD_{2}\,,\qquad\mathcal{S}_{9}=kD_{1}+lD_{2}\,.

(163)

The first lines inside each file repeat the geometry and the chosen

(\mathcal{S}_{7},\mathcal{S}_{9})
. The body of the file has the form

\texttt{row\_divisor}\ |\ \texttt{statuses\_against\_previous\_divisors}.

(164)

Each row is labelled by a divisor
D_{a}
. The entries in that row give the
classification of the model constructed from
D_{a}
and the divisors appearing
in the previous columns. Thus, for an entry in row
a
and column
b
, the
two rigid divisors are

D_{a}\,,\ D_{b}\,.

(165)

Only one ordering of each unordered pair is displayed. Redundant entries are
marked by
-
.

For
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, each model contains three rigid divisors, so
the data are written as grouped text tables rather than two-dimensional
matrices. The filenames are

\texttt{p111\_s7=[i,j,k]\_s9=[l,m,n].txt}\,,

(166)

which indicates

\mathcal{S}_{7}=iD_{1}+jD_{2}+kD_{3}\,,\ \mathcal{S}_{9}=lD_{1}+mD_{2}+nD_{3}.

(167)

Each row in such a file lists the three rigid divisors, the classification,
the smallest value of
\theta_{\rm QCD}
found in the scan, the three inverse
gauge couplings at the corresponding point, and the point counts used in the
classification.

The entries in the tables are denoted by

\texttt{Y}\,,\qquad\texttt{O}\,,\qquad\texttt{N}\,.

(168)

The label “
Y
” means that there exists a point
p
in the 1-stretched
Kähler cone such that

\theta_{\rm QCD}(p)<10^{-10},

(169)

and all three gauge couplings lie in the more restrictive range

\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}(p)\leq 23\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}(p)\leq 26\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}(p)\leq 30.

(170)

The label “
N
” means that no such “
Y
” point is found, and every
point satisfying
\theta_{\rm QCD}<10^{-10}

the conservative bounds of gauge coupling constants at high energy scales,

\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}(p)>37\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}(p)>41\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}(p)>40.

(171)

All remaining cases are labeled “
O
”.

(172)

\displaystyle b_{11}=\frac{3}{2}(-K_{B})-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{S}_{7}-\frac{1}{6}\mathcal{S}_{9}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha_{Y}}=4\,{\rm Vol}(b_{11})\,,

(173)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}=\frac{3}{5}\frac{1}{\alpha_{Y}}\,.
