Dirichlet Green’s functions with singular drifts at the boundary of convex domains.
Aritro Pathak

Abstract
In convex bounded domains, in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we establish interior pointwise upper bounds for the Dirichlet Green’s function of elliptic operators in the unit ball B ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) ⊂ ℝ n B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , whose principal part is the Laplacian and which include a drift term that diverges near the boundary like a negative power of the distance with exponent strictly less than 1 1 . This work extends an earlier result for operators with such drifts in the unit ball, and streamlines the proof in particular to adopt it to the question in convex domains.

3.3
Outline of subsequent argument

We outline how the argument of this article is modified, in comparison to the original argument for the unit ball. In
[
Pat25
]
, we consider the points of maximum and minimum for particular level sets. In the ”near field” case, this involved moving ”outwards” from the minimum point
z_{y}
of a given level set, by a given infinitesimal amount. We then consider the value of the Green’s function
G(z_{y}+h)
, and consider the minimum point
z_{y+dy}
of the level set
\{y:G(y)=G(z_{y}+h)\}
which by definition is at a distance at most
h
from the level set
\{y:G(y)=G(z_{y})\}
. Then we invoke the differential inequalities at each of the points
z_{y}
and
z_{y+dy}
.

In the far field case, we looked at point
z_{y}
of maximum of a given level set, and moved infinitesimally “inward” to a new level set and considered the maximum point
z_{y-dy}
of the new level set
\{y:G(y)=G(z_{y}-h)\}
.

In either of these cases, we have to deal with situations where radial values of these maximum or minimum points converge, which is then ruled out by invoking a-priori bounds on the Green’s function in domains of the form
A_{ij}
.

More crucially, the geometry of the unit ball is necessary in the argument of
[
Pat25
]
in the far field situation, to ensure that the bounds from the exponential factor coming from the drift is bounded. In case the geometry of the domain is not the unit ball, it might happen in the far field case that while we move infinitesimally inward towards the origin in successive infinitesimal steps, we remain arbitrarily close to the boundary for long times.

In this article, we consider one modification of the earlier method, that is able to extend the earlier result to convex domains, using a Minkowski interpolation between the original domain
K
and an interior ball
B_{1}
. By a careful modification of the existing argument of
[
Pat25
]
, one then considers each such domain
K_{y}=(1-y)K_{0}+yB_{1}
. One main departure from the previous argument, is that within
K\setminus B_{1}
, for one differential element
t_{y}
, the subsequent differential element might be significantly larger than the
t_{y}
, but remains controlled depending on the maximal ratio of the support functions across different directions for
K
. We are able to show that the gradient of the Green function, once with enough decay in
K\setminus B_{1}
, cannot then increase arbitrarily, which is sufficient for our purpose.

In the far field case within
K\setminus B_{1}
, one moves inward from
z_{y}
by some amount
t_{y}
. Instead of looking at the level set of the Green’s function
\{y:G(y)=G(z_{y}-t_{y})\}
, one instead looks at the maximum value of
G
on the unique boundary
\partial K_{y+dy}
on which
(z_{y}-t_{y})
lies. A careful modification of the original argument shows this also gives the expected decay of the Green’s function using the differential inequality. Moreover, one does not have to keep track of possible accumulations of the maximum points, since now the maximum points under consideration lie on successive
\partial K_{y+ndy}
, for integer
n\geq 0
.

More importantly, for the convex domain
K
considered here, this means these maximum points are isolated at controlled distances away from the boundary in a way that ensures that the radial integral of the drift converges. As a result, in the domain
K\setminus B_{1}
, one needs to consider the supporting hyperplanes of infinitely separated domains
K_{y}
and
K_{y+dy}
in given directions, and modify the differential argument to this setting.

It is also easy to see that the integral of the drift is bounded when one considers the far field effect; it is easily seen to be bounded by the integral of the upper bound of the form
\frac{M}{\delta(x)^{1-\beta}}
along the direction that has the
\hat{n}
so that
h_{K}(\hat{n})=\min_{S^{n-1}}h_{K}(y)
.

Thus, as in Equation (59) of
[
Pat25
]
, in Case (1), we need to show that one cannot have

\frac{|\{x:G_{i}(x,0)\geq 2\alpha_{i}^{*}\}|}{|B(0,\frac{1}{L})|}\not\to 0,\text{ as }i\to\infty.

For further progress on this and related questions, it is plausible that both the original method of
[
Pat25
]
as well as the modifications introduced in this manuscript, might prove to be useful in dealing with this question with more general non convex domains, and with poles of the Green function arbitrarily close to the boundary. One would plausibly study these methods in many other problems in partial differential equations involving potential terms and also the landscape function as considered in
[
DGM23
,
Pog24
]
in the setting of similarly singular potentials.

\frac{R}{2}\geq v\geq v_{1}
, we have
|G^{\prime}(s_{v})|\leq L
.

•

In this case, by an argument identical to ones used in the previous two cases, we get that
|G^{\prime}(s_{v})|\leq L_{1}
, and so,

\alpha_{i}^{*}=\Delta G\leq LD.

Now, consider the two sequences of minimum points
a_{i,k}|_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\bar{a}_{i,k}|_{k=1}^{\infty}
from
Lemmas

4
and
5
. Also, consider the associated set of maximum points to
(a_{i,k}|_{k=1}^{\infty})
, which we denoted as
(b_{i,k}|_{k=1}^{\infty})
.

Thus, identical to Lemma 7 of
[
Pat25
]
, we get the following result.

Lemma 6
.

For any
a_{i,k_{0}}\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}
(or any
\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
) with
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
minimum points of some spheres
\partial B_{v}
,

\displaystyle G(r_{1},0)-G(r_{2},0)\geq C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{r_{2}^{n-2}}\Big)

(81)

\displaystyle G(r_{1},0)-G(r_{2},0)>C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}}\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{r_{2}^{n-2}}\Big).

(82)

Specifically we choose
a_{i,k_{0}}<r_{1}=y_{i,\min}\leq r_{2}=\frac{1}{L}
, and get, as in
[
Pat25
]
, that,

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{i}^{*}-\frac{1}{C_{1}}\alpha_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle>G(y_{i,\min})-G(\frac{1}{L}s_{\min})

\displaystyle\geq C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{i,k}}a_{i,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}-L^{n-2}\right)

(30)

Further, we also have,

2\alpha_{i}^{*}-\frac{1}{C_{1}}\alpha_{i}^{*}>C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k}}\bar{a}_{i,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}-L^{n-2}\right)

(83)

As
y_{i,\min}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, we choose the
k_{0}
value appropriately so that
a_{i,k_{0}}<y_{i,\min}
,
\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}<y_{i,\min}
, and so we will have

\alpha_{i}^{*}\gtrsim C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}},\quad\alpha_{i}^{*}\gtrsim C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}}\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}

Here
C_{1}\geq 1
is a constant arising from the Harnack inequality.

Thus, we can compare Equations (30), (31), with Equations (28), (29), and we get

\frac{1}{2}\left(C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}+C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}}\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}\right)\\
\leq\alpha_{i}^{*}\leq\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{i,k}}b_{i,k}^{n-1}\left(L^{n-2}+\left(\frac{2}{R}\right)^{n-1}LD\right)+LD

(84)

So,

\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\left(\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}+\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}}\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\right)\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}\leq\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{i,k}}b_{i,k}^{n-1}\left(L^{n-2}+\left(\frac{2}{R}\right)^{n-1}LD\right)+LD

Now we will invoke
Lemmas

4
and
5
, to get the desired contradiction. From
Lemma

4
, we get that

\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}>\frac{1}{M_{0}^{\prime}}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{i,k}}b_{i,k}^{n-1},

\text{ and so as }y_{i,\min}\to 0
we get

\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{i,k_{0}}}a_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}>\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{i,k}}b_{i,k}^{n-1}\left(L^{n-2}+(\frac{2}{R})^{n-1}LD\right)

as
i\to\infty
.

Similarly, from
Lemma

5
, we get that for
i\to\infty
, we have

\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}}\bar{a}_{i,k_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{y_{i,\min}^{n-2}}>L_{1}D

Thus for
y_{i,\min}
small enough, depending on the parameters
L
(which in turn depends on
M
and
D
), we get a contradiction, and thus the Case (1) is proved.

5
Proof for Case 2

Now we complete the proof for Case 2.
This argument follows from the argument in
[
Pat25
]
with minor modifications, along with adopting the argument of Case (1) of this paper. In this case, we had defined
\tilde{\alpha}_{N}=\max_{S(0,R/2)}G(\cdot)
.

For each large enough integer
N
, we have
f(\alpha_{N})>Nf(\alpha_{2N})
for some
\alpha_{N}>\tilde{\alpha}_{N}
. Thus there exist boundary points
z_{N}\in\partial\Omega_{N},z_{2N}\in\partial\Omega_{2N}
with
z_{2N}
being the point at the minimum distance to
\partial\Omega_{2N}
from the origin, and
z_{N}
the point at the maximum distance to
\partial\Omega_{N}
from the origin, and by definition
z_{N}\in B(0,R/2)
.
As
N\to\infty
, we have
\frac{|z_{N}|}{|z_{2N}|}\to\infty
. Consider the value
\alpha^{*}=\max_{x\in S(0,|z_{N}|)}G(x)=G(z_{N},0)
.

Now as in the Case (1), we have sequences
\{a_{N,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{\bar{a}_{N,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{b_{N,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying the conditions of
Lemmas

4
and
5
, so that we have,

\alpha_{N}^{*}\leq C^{\prime}\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{N,k}}b_{N,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{|z_{N}|^{n-2}}+\left(\frac{2}{R}\right)^{n-1}\frac{1}{|z_{N}|}D\right)+L_{1}D

(85)

Further,

\alpha_{N}^{*}\gtrsim C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{a_{N,k}}a_{N,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{|z_{2N}|^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{|z_{N}|^{n-2}}\right)

(86)

\bar{\alpha}_{N}^{*}\gtrsim C\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{\bar{a}_{N,k}}\bar{a}_{N,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{|z_{2N}|^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{|z_{N}|^{n-2}}\right)

(87)

Thus, as
|z_{2N}|/|z_{N}|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, give us a contradiction. In the case where instead of
Eq.

85
, we have..

\alpha_{N}^{*}\leq\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|_{b_{N,k}}b_{N,k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{|z_{N}|^{n-2}}\right)+L_{1}D

or when
\alpha_{N}^{*}\leq L_{1}D
, the argument also adopts immediately and we have the result.
