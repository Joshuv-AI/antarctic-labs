Robustness of Starobinsky inflation in a minimal two-field scalar-tensor completion
Boris Latosh

Abstract
We study a minimal two-field scalar-tensor completion of Starobinsky inflation motivated by the one-loop effective action of scalar-tensor gravity. The model admits an exact Starobinsky branch, but the relevant question is whether nearby trajectories generate observable multifield effects. We show that a non-trivial class of initial conditions relaxes to an attractor-connected slow-roll branch continuously connected to the Starobinsky solution. We then solve the coupled adiabatic and entropy perturbation equations numerically. On the branch studied here, the entropy mode remains sufficiently suppressed that its sourcing of the curvature perturbation is negligible, while the tensor sector is unchanged. The inflationary observables therefore remain effectively Starobinsky-like, providing a robustness test of Starobinsky inflation against a minimal radiative scalar-tensor deformation.

Introduction

The Starobinsky model of inflation
[
52

However, it sharpens the motivation to test how robust the Starobinsky predictions are under theoretically motivated deformations and, in particular, to identify which classes of corrections can significantly modify its predictions and which leave them essentially unchanged.

One natural way to probe this question is to introduce quantum corrections to the classical action. Such corrections arise generically from radiative effects, matter couplings, or higher-curvature terms in the effective gravitational action (see, e.g.,

The Starobinsky model occupies a special place among inflationary scenarios because it captures the ghost-free scalar part of the local curvature-squared correction to Einstein gravity that is relevant for slow-roll inflation
[

In this paper, we do not attempt to analyse the full quadratic-gravity EFT. We focus on the ghost-free scalar sector whose behaviour is widely studied and well understood. Concretely, we retain the
R^{2}
completion and do not include the Ricci-tensor-squared operator. Thus, our use of the terms “minimal” and “universal” should be understood in this restricted sense: we study the minimal radiative scalar-tensor completion of Starobinsky inflation within the ghost-free scalar sector, rather than the most general curvature-squared extension of Einstein gravity.

Therefore, one can treat the Starobinsky inflation as a reasonable EFT approximation. It describes the influence of one-loop effects with the
R^{2}

PT

[

, their status is not universally recognised. Thus, we do not pursue them further.

We explore a way to deform the Starobinsky model within the same paradigm using operators generated by QFT at the one-loop level. We choose a model based on the following considerations. First and foremost, we focus on scalar-tensor gravity. Since the standard cosmological setup admits a uniform and isotropic background, we expect that only scalar degrees of freedom would be enough to describe the leading effects. Secondly, our goal is to isolate the universal gravitational radiative effects in the effective action, so we deliberately switch off non-gravitational interactions among the additional degrees of freedom and keep only their minimal coupling to gravity (see, e.g.,
[

To study slow-roll inflation in model (

Second, we show that the model embeds the standard Starobinsky inflation. If one sets the scalaron initial condition exactly as in the Starobinsky model and keeps
\chi

The relevant question is whether nearby initial data lead to an attractor-connected branch on which the additional scalar can leave observable multifield signatures. The main result of this paper can be summarised as follows. The effective action (
1

\chi

For initial conditions that are close to the Starobinsky initial conditions, the corresponding phase trajectories remain close to the Starobinsky attractor. The tensor sector receives no corrections at the linear level. At the same time, the scalar sector is genuinely two-field and must be analysed in the adiabatic-entropy basis. However, the entropy mode remains sufficiently suppressed that its sourcing of the curvature perturbation is negligible in the regime studied here. As a consequence, within the observational window analysed in this work, the model provides a nontrivial two-field radiative completion of Starobinsky inflation without generating an observable deviation from its predictions.

2
The origin of the model

Multiple publications considered various quantum-corrected versions of the Starobinsky model. In most of them, the corrections are introduced phenomenologically either at the level of an effective
f(R)
action or an Einstein-frame potential. Typical examples are models with added logarithmic terms such as
R^{2}\ln(R/\mu^{2})
, motivated by loop effects in curved space
[
8
,
23
]
. Another approach is to extend the curvature sector beyond
R^{2}
and to include higher powers of curvature
[
1
]
. Some models incorporate radiative corrections from couplings of the Starobinsky inflaton to additional matter fields via an RG-improved effective potential
[
17
]
. Another approach is to assume that a consistent gravitational EFT also contains the Weyl-squared term and to study its impact on inflationary observables
[
11
]
. Related analyses argue that small corrections can substantially shift predictions of Starobinsky-like attractor models into the ACT-preferred region
[
56
]
. All of these approaches are well motivated and can account for certain phenomenology of quantum effects, but they do not uniquely specify which operators must appear once a particular microscopic theory is fixed.

It is crucial to note that in most of the existing literature, the dominant quantum effects are assumed to come from
matter
loops. In these cases, radiative corrections are generated by extra fields coupled to the scalaron. Examples include Higgs-like sectors, Yukawa couplings, or other reheating-motivated interactions
[
8
,
23
,
17
]
. In this work, we take a complementary approach. We consider only corrections induced by the gravity itself. More precisely, we treat general relativity as an effective field theory applicable below the Planck scale. We already computed the one-loop effective action generated by graviton fluctuations in the minimal microscopic model specified below
[
34
,
3
]
. This approach allows for more rigorous testing because it is more restrictive. Once the microscopic content of a model is fixed, the structure of the one-loop operators is fixed as well. It is then clear which corrections are present and which are absent. In this sense, our setup isolates the gravitational radiative effects, rather than parametrising quantum corrections through additional matter sectors or RG-improved potentials.

Below, we follow the proposed methodology. We fix a minimal microscopic scalar-tensor theory and treat it as an effective field theory applicable below the Planck scale. We recall the computation of the one-loop effective action generated by graviton fluctuations. We do not introduce any phenomenological corrections or corrections due to additional matter degrees of freedom. So it remains transparent which operators appear at the one-loop level and keeps the setup minimal, since we deliberately neglect the non-gravitational interactions of a scalar field. In the remainder of this section, we briefly outline the main steps of the calculation and state the resulting effective action calculated in
[
34
,
3
]
that will be used in the inflationary analysis.

The starting point of
[
34
,
3
]
was the microscopic action

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\cfrac{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}{2}\,R-\cfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-\cfrac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{2}\,\chi^{2}\right].

(3)

Here
m_{\text{P}}
is the Planck mass and
\chi
is a single additional scalar field with mass
m_{\chi}
. We omit self-interactions such as
\chi^{4}
, and do not couple
\chi
to any other matter fields. Action (
3
) is the microscopic action, so one shall use it in the generating functional for quantum theory:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}[g]\,\mathcal{D}[\chi]\,\exp\Big[\penalty\ i\,\mathcal{A}[g,\chi]\penalty\ \Big].

(4)

To study quantum effects in this model, one uses the perturbative quantum gravity framework
[
13
,
18
,
54
,
53
,
6
,
7
]
. Gravitational degrees of freedom are small perturbations
h_{\mu\nu}
of the spacetime metric over a flat background
\eta_{\mu\nu}
, so the spacetime metric reads:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa\,h_{\mu\nu}.

(5)

Here
\kappa^{2}=32\pi G_{\text{N}}=4\,m_{\text{P}}^{-2}
is the gravitational coupling related to the Newton constant
G_{\text{N}}
. At the level of the generating functional, the perturbative expansion is a simple shift of the integration variables from
g_{\mu\nu}
to
h_{\mu\nu}
, so one can change the integration variable:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}[h]\,\mathcal{D}[\chi]\,\exp\Big[i\,\mathcal{A}[\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa\,h_{\mu\nu},\chi]\Big].

(6)

At the one-loop level, the local gravitational EFT generates curvature-squared operators. In the present work, however, we restrict the analysis to the ghost-free scalar sector relevant for the FLRW slow-roll branch. For this reason, we retain the
R^{2}
contribution and do not include the Ricci-tensor-squared operator, which belongs to the massive spin-
2
sector of quadratic gravity.

The one-loop effective action for (
3
) was derived in
[
34
,
3
]
. One introduces the generating function as a path integral defined with the microscopic action. In turn, the perturbation theory is built around an expansion of the spacetime metric in small perturbations
h_{\mu\nu}
. The one-loop effective action is computed as a sum of one-particle-irreducible (1PI) diagrams. To obtain the effective potential
V(\chi)
, one evaluates the momentum-independent part of the 1PI vertices (the part with external scalar momenta set to zero). This makes clear why derivative couplings, such as the Einstein-tensor kinetic term, do not contribute to
V(\chi)
.

The resulting one-loop effective action takes the following compact form
[
34
,
3
]

\displaystyle\Gamma=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\cfrac{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}{2}\left(R+\cfrac{1}{6\,m_{0}^{2}}\,R^{2}\right)-\cfrac{1}{2}\left(g^{\mu\nu}+\cfrac{4\,\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,G^{\mu\nu}\right)\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-V(\chi)\right],

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V(\chi)=&-m_{\chi}^{4}\,\cfrac{\ln 2}{64\,\pi^{2}}+\cfrac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{2}\left[1+\cfrac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\,\cfrac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,(1+\ln 4)\right]\chi^{2}\\
&+\cfrac{m_{\chi}^{4}}{128\,\pi^{2}}\left(1-8\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\sqrt{1-16\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}}\right)\ln\left[1-\sqrt{1-16\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}}\right]\\
&+\cfrac{m_{\chi}^{4}}{128\,\pi^{2}}\left(1-8\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}+\sqrt{1-16\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}}\right)\ln\left[1+\sqrt{1-16\,\cfrac{\chi^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}}\right].\end{split}

(8)

The
R^{2}
term represents the universal curvature-squared completion of the gravitational sector. The term
G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\nu}\chi
is universally generated at the loop level by the minimal coupling between gravity and the scalar. Despite involving a higher derivative, it belongs to the Horndeski gravity
[
24
,
31
]
, so the field equations remain of the second order. The potential
V(\chi)
is generated at one loop and is fixed uniquely by the microscopic theory. Although (
8
) contains square roots and logarithms, it defines a real and smooth function of
\chi
. It admits a regular expansion around
\chi=0
, with
V(0)=V^{\prime}(0)=0
and
V^{\prime\prime}(0)=m_{\chi}^{2}
.

The important consequence of the minimal microscopic action (
3
) is that some operators, often present in general scalar-tensor EFTs, are not generated. In particular, an
R\,\chi^{2}
term does not appear in our one-loop EFT. To generate such an operator, the model must admit additional microscopic couplings, for example, a
\chi^{3}
interaction, which we have explicitly omitted (see the discussion in
[
34
]
).

3
Model diagonalization

The model (
7
) describes the standard gravitational degrees of freedom and two scalar degrees of freedom

\displaystyle\Gamma=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\cfrac{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}{2}\left(R+\cfrac{1}{6\,m_{0}^{2}}\,R^{2}\right)-\cfrac{1}{2}\left(g^{\mu\nu}+\cfrac{4\,\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,G^{\mu\nu}\right)\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-V(\chi)\right].

(9)

To proceed, one should make these degrees of freedom explicit by introducing new variables with canonical kinetic terms. We use the existing terminology and refer to this as diagonalisation. The term diagonalisation originated in quantum field theory. There, models with non-canonical kinetic terms have energy operators with non-diagonal matrices. Bringing such a model to canonical form makes the kinetic energy operator matrix diagonal, which explains the term diagonalisation. In inflation theory, diagonalisation is crucial because it separates the dynamical degrees of freedom for which we define the initial conditions.

To diagonalise the effective action (
7
), we now apply the standard conformal mapping procedure used for metric
f(R)
gravity
[
15
,
51
,
43
,
42
]
. This involves first introducing an auxiliary field
\widetilde{\phi}
and rewriting the higher-curvature sector as follows:
Varying with respect to
\widetilde{\phi}
yields
f^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{\phi})(R-\widetilde{\phi})=0
, so for
f^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
this action is classically equivalent to the original one and enforces
\widetilde{\phi}=R
.

Further, we perform the conformal transformation from the original metric
g_{\mu\nu}
to the new metric
\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\widetilde{\phi})\,g_{\mu\nu}.

(10)

With this choice, the gravitational sector takes the form of the Einstein-Hilbert action. Finally, we introduce the canonically normalised scalaron field
\phi
by

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\cfrac{3}{2}}\,m_{\text{P}}\ln f^{\prime}(\widetilde{\phi}),

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\widetilde{\phi})

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right].

(11)

This relation will be used below to express all derivatives of the conformal factor in
terms of derivatives of the scalaron field
\phi
.

Since the conformal mapping of the original Starobinsky model has been well-discussed in several papers
[
55
,
37
,
19
,
21
,
15
]
, we will not repeat it here. Instead, we now turn to the transformation of the non-minimal kinetic coupling.

To transform the non-minimal derivative coupling, it is convenient to make the index structure explicit:

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\,G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\nu}\chi=\cfrac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{-g}\,\left(g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}+g^{\mu\beta}g^{\nu\alpha}-g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}\right)R_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_{\alpha}\chi\,\nabla_{\beta}\chi.

(12)

Since
\chi
is a scalar, its first covariant derivative coincides with the ordinary derivative (
\nabla_{\mu}\chi=\partial_{\mu}\chi
), so they are independent of the Levi-Civita connection. Moreover, in four spacetime dimensions, the combination

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\,\left(g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}+g^{\mu\beta}g^{\nu\alpha}-g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}\right)

(13)

is conformally invariant. The metric transforms as
g_{\mu\nu}\to\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
, so the volume factor transforms as
\sqrt{-g}\to\Omega^{4}\sqrt{-g}
, and
g^{\mu\nu}\to\Omega^{-2}g^{\mu\nu}
. Consequently, the given tensor remains invariant.

Therefore, the only non-trivial contribution comes from the transformation of the Ricci tensor. Introducing
\sigma\equiv\ln\Omega
, in four dimensions one has

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}\to R_{\mu\nu}-2\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\sigma-g_{\mu\nu}\square\sigma+2\nabla_{\mu}\sigma\nabla_{\nu}\sigma-2g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla\sigma)^{2}.

(14)

In our case,

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\cfrac{\phi}{\sqrt{6}\,m_{\text{P}}}\,,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\sigma

\displaystyle=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}\,m_{\text{P}}}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\,,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\sigma

\displaystyle=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}\,m_{\text{P}}}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,.

(15)

Substituting these relations into the expression above, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{-g}\,G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\chi\nabla_{\mu}\chi\to\sqrt{-g}\Bigg[&G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\chi\nabla_{\mu}\chi+\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{1}{m_{\text{P}}}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\chi+\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\chi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\right)\\
&+\cfrac{1}{6}\,\cfrac{1}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\left(2\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi+\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\right)\Bigg].\end{split}

(16)

This formula shows that the non-minimal kinetic coupling term remains in the action, but it introduces additional terms that describe kinetic couplings between the scalar degrees of freedom.

The diagonalised form of the effective action (
7
) reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma_{\text{diagonal}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg[&\cfrac{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}{2}\,R-\cfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\cfrac{3}{4}\,m_{0}^{2}\,m_{\text{P}}^{2}\left(\exp\left[-\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]-1\right)^{2}\\
&-\cfrac{1}{2}\,\exp\left[-\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-\exp\left[-2\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]V(\chi)\\
&-\cfrac{2\,\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-2\,\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{3}}\,\left(\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\chi+\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\chi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\right)\\
&-\cfrac{\beta}{3\,m_{\text{P}}^{4}}\,\left(2\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi+\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\right)\Bigg].\end{split}

(17)

Here,
\phi
is the scalaron, which is the scalar degree of freedom associated with the
R^{2}
term. The field
\chi
is the same scalar degree of freedom that is present in (
7
). In this parametrisation, the field
\chi
admits a term that contains both the non-minimal coupling to
\phi
and the canonical kinetic term:

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{2}\,\exp\left[-\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi=\cfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi-\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\cfrac{1}{m_{\text{P}}}\,\phi\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi+\mathcal{O}\left(\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right)^{2}\,.

(18)

Similarly, the conformal mapping introduces a coupling between the scalar field
\chi
potential and the scalaron.

Let us discuss features of the model (
17
) that are evident from the Lagrangian. The model extends Starobinsky inflation. Setting
\chi=0
restores the original Starobinsky model. Any initial conditions with
\chi(t=0)=0
and
\dot{\chi}(t=0)=0
keep
\chi
at zero, so the model matches Starobinsky inflation exactly.

The model deviates from Starobinsky inflation if one sets small, non-zero initial conditions for
\chi(t=0)
and
\dot{\chi}(t=0)
. The system’s phase trajectory then slightly diverges from the Starobinsky case. These corrections affect both the background solution and cosmological perturbations, so they can influence the observable power spectra.

Further, the non-minimal kinetic coupling terms are suppressed in the homogeneous slow-roll background on the Starobinsky-like branch. In a spatially flat cosmological background, these terms take the following form

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\cfrac{2\,\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi&=-6\,\cfrac{\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\,H^{2}\,\dot{\chi}^{2},\\
-2\,\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{3}}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\chi+\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\chi\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\right)&=-2\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{3}}\,\dot{\chi}\left(\dot{\phi}\,\ddot{\chi}+\dot{\chi}\,\ddot{\phi}\right),\\
-\cfrac{\beta}{3\,m_{\text{P}}^{4}}\left(2\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi+\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla^{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,\nabla^{\nu}\phi\right)&=-\cfrac{\beta}{m_{\text{P}}^{4}}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}\,\dot{\chi}^{2}.\end{split}

(19)

All of them are suppressed in the slow-roll regime. In multi-field inflation, one introduces several slow-roll parameters. These describe how slowly all the fields are changing (see the discussion in
[
33
]
):

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2\,m_{\text{P}}^{2}\,H^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\phi\chi}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\sqrt{\cfrac{1}{6}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]\cfrac{\dot{\phi}\,\dot{\chi}}{2\,m_{\text{P}}^{2}\,H^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\chi\chi}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}}\,\cfrac{\phi}{m_{\text{P}}}\right]\cfrac{\dot{\chi}^{2}}{2\,m_{\text{P}}^{2}\,H^{2}}\,.

(20)

Therefore, near the Starobinsky-like branch, the non-minimal kinetic coupling terms are suppressed by an additional power of slow-roll parameters and can be neglected.

Let us note that this conclusion is specific to the slow-roll scenario discussed in the present paper. It does not imply that the non-minimal derivative coupling is irrelevant in all inflationary regimes of the model (
7
). In kinetically driven scenarios, such as

-inflation and
G
-inflation, derivative operators can play a leading dynamical role
[

,

,

,

These features significantly simplify the analysis of inflation and cosmological perturbations in this model. We carry out this analysis in the next section.

Our goal is not to classify the full four-dimensional space of initial data

Attempts to negate the discussed suppression immediately spawn completely different inflationary scenarios. In order to negate the suppression in the kinetic term, one should choose the initial condition in such a way that
\chi

This means one will no longer be dealing with a modification of Starobinsky inflation, but with a completely different model. On the other hand, in order to negate the suppression of the potential, one has to increase its amplitude, which is also a fine-tuning of the model parameter. This reasoning shows that there is a universal generalisation of the Starobinsky inflation that follows the logic outlined in the Introduction. Any attempts to modify it differently require either performing fine-tuning or choosing a different motivation for modification.

5
Perturbations power spectrum

In the previous section, we showed that the background dynamics are well controlled for initial conditions chosen near the Starobinsky branch. Here, we study whether such scenarios can alter the dynamics of perturbations, leading to meaningful deviations from the Starobinsky case.

This question admits a sharp answer on the slow-roll branch continuously connected to the Starobinsky attractor. The perturbation analysis below shows a non-trivial robustness result: although the scalar sector is genuinely two-field at the level of the coupled Mukhanov–Sasaki system, no observable multifield correction is generated on the attractor branch studied in this paper. The tensor sector remains unchanged and does not deviate from the Starobinsky inflation. The scalar sector becomes effectively adiabatic, and entropy perturbations are strongly suppressed. Lastly, the curvature perturbations behave somewhat differently from the Starobinsky case, but the deviations are not strong enough to meaningfully change observable parameters. In that sense, the Starobinsky inflation remains protected from the new effects.

Tensor perturbations probe the gravitational sector directly. In linear order, the transverse traceless tensor mode is gauge invariant. It is an independent sector of cosmological perturbations
[
32
,
39
,
38
]
. The quadratic action determines the kinetic normalisation and propagation speed of the tensor modes and fixes the corresponding spectrum.

The tensor sector of multifield generalised

In particular, the extra scalar degree of freedom does not modify the tensor sector. Therefore, on the slow-roll attractor branch studied here, the model does not generate an independent tensor signature, and the prediction for the tensor-to-scalar ratio remains the Starobinsky one to the accuracy relevant for the present analysis.

\displaystyle=-(1+2\Phi)dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(1-2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

\displaystyle\phi(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\phi(t)+\delta\phi(t,\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\chi(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\chi(t)+\delta\chi(t,\mathbf{x}).

(48)

The linearised Klein–Gordon equations for
\delta\phi
and
\delta\chi
are coupled both to each other and to the metric perturbation
\Phi

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta\ddot{\phi}&+3H\,\delta\dot{\phi}+\left[\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+\mathcal{V}_{\phi\phi}-2b_{\phi}^{2}e^{2b}\dot{\chi}^{2}\right]\delta\phi+\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi}\,\delta\chi-2b_{\phi}e^{2b}\dot{\chi}\,\delta\dot{\chi}=4\dot{\phi}\,\dot{\Phi}-2\mathcal{V}_{\phi}\,\Phi,\\
\delta\ddot{\chi}&+\left(3H+2b_{\phi}\dot{\phi}\right)\delta\dot{\chi}+\left(\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+e^{-2b}\mathcal{V}_{\chi\chi}\right)\delta\chi+2b_{\phi}\dot{\chi}\,\delta\dot{\phi}+e^{-2b}\left(\mathcal{V}_{\chi\phi}-2b_{\phi}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}\right)\delta\phi=4\dot{\chi}\dot{\Phi}-2e^{-2b}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}\Phi.\end{split}

(49)

Field equations for the metric provide the following constraints

\displaystyle\begin{split}&3H(\dot{\Phi}+H\Phi)+\dot{H}\,\Phi+\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\,\Phi=-\cfrac{1}{2m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\left[\dot{\phi}\,\delta\dot{\phi}+e^{2b}\dot{\chi}\,\delta\dot{\chi}+b_{\phi}e^{2b}\dot{\chi}^{2}\,\delta\phi+\mathcal{V}_{\phi}\,\delta\phi+\mathcal{V}_{\chi}\,\delta\chi\right],\\
&\dot{\Phi}+H\Phi=\cfrac{1}{2m_{\text{P}}^{2}}\left[\dot{\phi}\,\delta\phi+e^{2b}\dot{\chi}\,\delta\chi\right].\end{split}

(50)

Perturbations
\delta\phi
,
\delta\chi
, and
\Phi
are gauge dependent, so we introduce the gauge-invariant Mukhanov–Sasaki variables:

\displaystyle Q_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\delta\phi+\cfrac{\dot{\phi}}{H}\,\Phi,

\displaystyle Q_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\delta\chi+\cfrac{\dot{\chi}}{H}\,\Phi.

(51)

These variables coincide with the field perturbations in the spatially flat gauge. In particular, they show that the perturbation problem is already genuinely two-field before any adiabatic-entropy rotation is performed. The corresponding field equations read

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ddot{Q}_{\phi}+3H\,\dot{Q}_{\phi}-2e^{2b}b_{\phi}\dot{\chi}\,\dot{Q}_{\chi}+\left(\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+C_{\phi\phi}\right)Q_{\phi}+C_{\phi\chi}Q_{\chi}=0,\\
&\ddot{Q}_{\chi}+\left(3H+2b_{\phi}\dot{\phi}\right)\dot{Q}_{\chi}+2b_{\phi}\dot{\chi}\,\dot{Q}_{\phi}+\left(\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+C_{\chi\chi}\right)Q_{\chi}+C_{\chi\phi}Q_{\phi}=0,\end{split}

(52)

where the background-dependent coefficients are

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{\phi\phi}=&-2e^{2b}b_{\phi}^{\,2}\dot{\chi}^{2}+\cfrac{3\dot{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{2b}\dot{\phi}^{2}\dot{\chi}^{2}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-\cfrac{\dot{\phi}^{4}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}+\cfrac{2\dot{\phi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+\mathcal{V}_{\phi\phi},\\
C_{\phi\chi}=&\cfrac{3e^{2b}\dot{\phi}\dot{\chi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{4b}\dot{\phi}\dot{\chi}^{3}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{2b}\dot{\phi}^{3}\dot{\chi}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}+\cfrac{\dot{\phi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+\cfrac{e^{2b}\dot{\chi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi},\\
C_{\chi\chi}=&\cfrac{3e^{2b}\dot{\chi}^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{4b}\dot{\chi}^{4}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{2b}\dot{\phi}^{2}\dot{\chi}^{2}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}+\cfrac{2\dot{\chi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+e^{-2b}\mathcal{V}_{\chi\chi},\\
C_{\chi\phi}=&\cfrac{3\dot{\phi}\dot{\chi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\cfrac{e^{2b}\dot{\phi}\dot{\chi}^{3}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-\cfrac{\dot{\phi}^{3}\dot{\chi}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-2e^{-2b}b_{\phi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi}+\cfrac{e^{-2b}\dot{\phi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+\cfrac{\dot{\chi}\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+e^{-2b}\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi}.\end{split}

(53)

Thus, already at the level of the Mukhanov–Sasaki equations, the two scalar modes remain non-trivially coupled through both the kinetic prefactor and the effective potential.

For the scalar analysis, it is more useful to decompose the Mukhanov–Sasaki variables into the instantaneous adiabatic and entropy directions in field space. Following
[
33
]
, we define

\displaystyle Q_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\,Q_{\phi}+\sin\theta\,e^{b}Q_{\chi},

\displaystyle\delta s

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta\,Q_{\phi}+\cos\theta\,e^{b}Q_{\chi},

(54)

with

\displaystyle\cos\theta

\displaystyle=\cfrac{\dot{\phi}}{\dot{\sigma}}\,,

\displaystyle\sin\theta

\displaystyle=\cfrac{e^{b}\dot{\chi}}{\dot{\sigma}}\,,

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\dot{\phi}^{2}+e^{2b}\dot{\chi}^{2}}\,.

(55)

The perturbation equations take the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ddot{Q}_{\sigma}+3H\dot{Q}_{\sigma}+\left(\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+C_{\sigma\sigma}\right)Q_{\sigma}+2\,\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}\,\dot{\delta s}+C_{\sigma s}\,\delta s=0,\\
&\ddot{\delta s}+3H\dot{\delta s}+\left(\cfrac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+C_{ss}\right)\delta s-2\,\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}\,\dot{Q}_{\sigma}+C_{s\sigma}\,Q_{\sigma}=0,\end{split}

(56)

so that the coupled system for
Q_{\sigma}
and
\delta s
directly encodes the transfer between adiabatic and entropy modes. This is the system that we solve numerically below.

The background-dependent coefficients are

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{\sigma\sigma}&=\mathcal{V}_{\sigma\sigma}-\left(\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}\right)^{2}+\cfrac{2\dot{\sigma}\,\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+\cfrac{3\dot{\sigma}^{2}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}}-\cfrac{\dot{\sigma}^{4}}{2m_{\text{P}}^{4}H^{2}}-b_{\phi}\left(s_{\theta}^{2}c_{\theta}\,\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}+(c_{\theta}^{2}+1)s_{\theta}\,\mathcal{V}_{s}\right),\\
C_{\sigma s}&=6H\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}+\cfrac{2\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}^{2}}+2\mathcal{V}_{\sigma s}+\cfrac{\dot{\sigma}\,\mathcal{V}_{s}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}+2b_{\phi}\left(s_{\theta}^{3}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}-c_{\theta}^{3}\mathcal{V}_{s}\right),\\
C_{ss}&=\mathcal{V}_{ss}-\left(\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}\right)^{2}+b_{\phi}(1+s_{\theta}^{2})c_{\theta}\,\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}+b_{\phi}c_{\theta}^{2}s_{\theta}\,\mathcal{V}_{s}-\dot{\sigma}^{2}\left(b_{\phi\phi}+b_{\phi}^{2}\right),\\
C_{s\sigma}&=-6H\cfrac{\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}-\cfrac{2\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}\mathcal{V}_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}^{2}}+\cfrac{\dot{\sigma}\,\mathcal{V}_{s}}{m_{\text{P}}^{2}H}\,.\end{split}

(57)

Here
s_{\theta}=\sin\theta
and
c_{\theta}=\cos\theta
, while the projected derivatives of the potential are defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}&=\cos\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi}+\sin\theta\,e^{-b}\mathcal{V}_{\chi},\\
\mathcal{V}_{s}&=-\sin\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi}+\cos\theta\,e^{-b}\mathcal{V}_{\chi},\\
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma\sigma}&=\cos^{2}\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\phi}+2e^{-b}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi}+e^{-2b}\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi\chi},\\
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma s}&=-\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\phi}+e^{-b}(\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta)\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi}+e^{-2b}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi\chi},\\
\mathcal{V}_{ss}&=\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\phi}-2e^{-b}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\phi\chi}+e^{-2b}\cos^{2}\theta\,\mathcal{V}_{\chi\chi}.\end{split}

(58)

Lastly, we define the gauge-invariant comoving curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}
and normalised entropy perturbation
\mathcal{S}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{H}{\dot{\sigma}}\,Q_{\sigma},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{H}{\dot{\sigma}}\,\delta s.

(59)

These are the variables from which we extract the scalar power spectrum and determine the spectral tilt
n_{s}
.

In a multifield system, the numerical setup must isolate the attractor basin relevant for the present claim rather than propagate arbitrary seed data all the way to the observable stage. For this reason, we use a short background pre-evolution as a physical filter: its role is to remove spurious dependence on the seed initial conditions before the perturbations are initialised, thereby restricting the analysis to the family of trajectories continuously connected to the Starobinsky branch. This step is especially important in the present two-field model, because transient bending of the background trajectory and temporary entropy sourcing could otherwise be interpreted as genuine multifield corrections, even though such effects lie outside the basin probed here by the relaxed seed data.

Our numerical procedure follows
[
33
]
. We first choose seed initial conditions for the background equations (
26
) near the Starobinsky branch so that inflation lasts for
68
e-foldings. We then evolve the background freely for the first
\Delta N=8
e-foldings, allowing the system to settle onto the attractor trajectory. After this pre-evolution, we recover the corresponding background values of
\phi
and
\chi
and use them as the initial conditions for the full numerical evolution. Only at that stage do we impose the Bunch–Davies initial conditions for the perturbations and solve (
24
) together with the coupled system (
56
). Repeating this procedure for two independent sets of initial perturbations allows us to reconstruct the spectra of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{S}
for the restricted family of trajectories analysed in this section.

For the perturbation equations (
56
), we use the standard Bunch-Davies initial conditions and solve equations separately for the real and imaginary parts of perturbations. This is equivalent to solving the complex system, but it is numerically more convenient. From the corresponding real and imaginary solutions, we reconstruct the curvature and entropy perturbations and define the power spectra through the absolute values of the resulting modes. Denoting
\mathcal{R}_{1}
,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
,
\mathcal{S}_{2}
the outputs of the two independent vacuum initial conditions, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(|\mathcal{R}_{1}|^{2}+|\mathcal{R}_{2}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(|\mathcal{S}_{1}|^{2}+|\mathcal{S}_{2}|^{2}\right).

(60)

In the numerical analysis, we adopt the benchmark choice

\displaystyle m_{\chi}=m,

(61)

where
m
is the scalaron mass parameter of the Starobinsky sector. This equality is not imposed by the effective action (
1
) and should therefore be understood as a phenomenologically motivated restriction of the parameter space rather than as a prediction of the model. The motivation for this choice is that, in Starobinsky inflation, the scalaron mass sets the inflationary scale and is of the same order as the Hubble parameter. Consequently, the condition
m_{\chi}\sim m
places the additional scalar in the regime
m_{\chi}\sim H
, in which multifield effects are expected to be least suppressed. In contrast, for
m_{\chi}\gg H
the additional field is expected to decouple to a good approximation, while for
m_{\chi}\ll H
the system moves towards a different light-isocurvature regime. In this sense, the choice
m_{\chi}=m
provides a natural benchmark for testing whether the minimal two-field completion considered here can generate observable deviations from the Starobinsky predictions. Lastly, we define the pivot wave that crosses the horizon when exactly
N=60
e-foldings remain before the end of inflation. Its wavelength
k_{*}
is fixed by the horizon-crossing relation

\displaystyle k_{*}=a_{*}H_{*},

(62)

where
a_{*}
and
H_{*}
are evaluated at the corresponding crossing time.

The numerical analysis results are as follows. We ran several simulations for different initial field values. In each run, we generated
500
points for spectra for spectra
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}(k)
. In each run, the wavelength changed from
k_{0}=a(0)H(0)
to
2\,k_{*}
.

In all runs, the curvature power spectra were always well approximated by the standard scale-free spectrum

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=A_{\mathcal{R}}\left(\cfrac{k}{k_{*}}\right)^{n_{s}-1},

(63)

from which we recover both the amplitude
A_{\mathcal{R}}
and the tilt
n_{s}
. Across all runs, the fitted curvature observables remain tightly clustered around
A_{\mathcal{R}}\simeq 1.6\times 10^{-11}
and
n_{s}\simeq 0.961
. The entropy amplitude stays at the level
A_{\mathcal{S}}\sim 10^{-16}
, i.e. roughly five orders of magnitude smaller, so it is suppressed too strongly to induce observable effects. Table
1
provides the more detailed data. The Wolfram Mathematica scripts used to generate the numerical data together with the generated data sets are published online
[
35
]
.

These results show that, on the slow-roll attractor branch considered here, the curvature perturbation sector remains essentially Starobinsky-like even though the underlying perturbation system is genuinely two-field. More precisely, for phase trajectories close to the Starobinsky inflation and which enter the same attractor, the scalar spectrum retains an almost scale-free form with nearly unchanged tilt, while the entropy contribution is too small to source any observable effects. The perturbation analysis, therefore, does not establish a general theorem for the full space of trajectories of the model, but it does show that the minimal two-field completion considered in this work is not sufficient, on the attractor-connected branch studied here, to produce a meaningful departure from Starobinsky phenomenology.

Discussion and conclusion

We analysed inflation in a minimal two-field modification of the Starobinsky model motivated by the one-loop effective action (
17
). Near the phenomenologically relevant slow-roll branch, the model does
not
produce an observable departure from Starobinsky inflation. This conclusion is nontrivial, since the theory contains two interacting scalar degrees of freedom and higher-derivative operators generated at the loop level. Because of this, it was reasonable to expect a large discrepancy between the model under discussion and Starobinsky inflation. Instead, the observable branch turns out to be remarkably rigid.

The result provides a new insight into the role of quantum effects during inflation, as the deformation considered here is not introduced phenomenologically at the level of an
ad hoc
Einstein-frame potential. The operator content is fixed by the microscopic scalar-tensor theory (
3
) and by its one-loop effective action (
1
). In this sense, the result appears to be model-independent. It shows that the
minimal
set of operators that are generated in every scalar-tensor model is itself too constrained to generate a meaningful shift of the inflationary observables near the slow-roll Starobinsky branch.

The dynamical origin of this result can be traced to two related mechanisms. First, along the Starobinsky-like slow-roll branch, non-minimal kinetic couplings such as
G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi
are additionally suppressed, reducing the system to the simpler two-field model (
22
). Second, in the Einstein frame, the kinetic and potential terms of the additional scalar
\chi
are multiplied by exponential prefactors, which strongly suppress its contribution at large positive
\phi
. Although the extra field remains dynamically present, its backreaction on the observable slow-roll evolution is minimal.

Our numerical analysis shows that this branch is not only present but also attractive. Seed trajectories chosen in a neighbourhood of the embedded Starobinsky solution quickly converge to a common relaxed slow-roll trajectory after a short pre-evolution stage. The observable inflationary regime is therefore largely insensitive to the details of the seed initial conditions, provided that they lie inside the attractor basin probed in this work. This point is conceptually important: the effective single-field-like behaviour observed in the spectra is not the consequence of a fine-tuned choice of initial data, but a property of the attractor-connected branch itself.

The perturbation analysis leads to the same conclusion. In the tensor sector, the quadratic action coincides with the standard one and the model remains indistinguishable from the Starobinsky model at linear order. In the scalar sector, the system is genuinely two-field at the level of the perturbation equations, so the absence of a meaningful multifield signal is not guaranteed. Nevertheless, for the family of relaxed trajectories considered here, the entropy mode remains too small to source an appreciable correction to the curvature perturbation during the observable stage of inflation.

Quantitatively, the curvature spectrum near the pivot scale is always well fitted by a power law with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle\simeq 1.6\times 10^{11}\,,

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle\simeq 0.961\,.

(64)

At the same time, the entropy amplitude is suppressed by approximately five orders of magnitude relative to the curvature amplitude. Therefore, although the formal perturbation system is multifield, the observable scalar spectrum remains effectively adiabatic and essentially Starobinsky-like on the attractor branch studied in this paper.

This allows one to sharpen the interpretation of the result. The present work does not merely show that one particular numerical setup happens to resemble Starobinsky inflation. Rather, it identifies a concrete class of loop-generated corrections that are strongly suppressed and protected by the attractor so they cannot develop significant deviation from Starobinsky phenomenology. In practical terms, this means a meaningful departure from the standard Starobinsky scenario within the EFT shall be organised otherwise. One must either move away from the attractor-connected branch, enter a regime in which the non-minimal kinetic couplings are large and the inflation no longer proceeds in the slow-roll regime, or modify the microscopic theory so that additional operators are generated in the effective action.

Let us especially highlight that the conclusion should not be overstated. We did not attempt an exhaustive scan of the full space of initial conditions or of the full parameter space of the effective theory. Our analysis was deliberately restricted to a
minimal
model that includes operators generated in (almost) all scalar-tensor gravity models. Further, we only considered phase trajectories close to the embedded Starobinsky scenario and to the corresponding slow-roll regime, in which the derivative couplings are subleading. The present paper, therefore, establishes a robustness result for this physically motivated sector of the model, but it does not exclude the existence of qualitatively different inflationary solutions elsewhere in phase space.

This also points to the most natural directions for future work. First, it would be useful to explore trajectories outside the Starobinsky attractor and to determine whether they can provide viable inflationary scenarios. Second, one should investigate regimes in which the non-minimal kinetic couplings are not slow-roll suppressed and may therefore affect the background dynamics and perturbations at leading order. In particular, it would be interesting to examine whether the full effective action (
17
) admits kinetically driven solutions of the
k
-inflation or
G
-inflation type
[
4
,
22
,
30
,
31
]
. Third, one may consider less minimal microscopic completions in which additional interactions generate new effective operators, thereby relaxing the rigidity found in the present work.
