The Cullis’ determinant as Pfaffian
Alexander Guterman, Andrey Yurkov

Abstract
The Cullis’ determinant is a generalization of the ordinary determinant for rectangular matrices. It is defined as the alternating sum of maximal minors of given matrix. In this paper we express the Cullis’ determinant of a matrix X X as the Pfaffian of the matrix obtained from X X by matrix multiplication and transposition. Relying on this result, we present an efficient polynomial-time algorithm for calculating the Cullis’ determinant of given matrix.



Keywords:
rectangular matrices, Cullis’ determinant, Pfaffian

1
Introduction

The Cullis’ determinant is a generalization of the determinant of square matrices for rectangular matrices. It was introduced by Cullis in 1913 in his monograph “Matrices and Determinoids” (see
[
3
]
) as follows:

Definition 1.1

(Cf.
[
3
, §3, p. 12 and §30, Theorem I.]
)
.

Let
n\geq k\geq 1
be integers,
X=(x_{i\,j})
be a
n\times k
matrix. Then the Cullis’ determinant
\det_{n\,k}(X)
of
X
is defined by the formula

\det_{n\,k}(X)=(-1)^{(1+\ldots+k)}\sum_{1\leq c_{1}<\ldots<c_{k}\leq n}(-1)^{c_{1}+\ldots+c_{k}}\begin{vmatrix}x_{c_{1}\,1}&\cdots&x_{c_{1}\,k}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{c_{k}\,1}&\cdots&x_{c_{k}\,k}\end{vmatrix}.

That is, the Cullis’ determinant of
X
is an alternating sum of basic minors of
X
. We also use the following notation for
\det_{n\,k}(X)
:

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\begin{vmatrix}X_{1\,1}&\cdots&X_{1\,k}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
X_{n\,1}&\cdots&X_{n\,k}\end{vmatrix}_{n\,k}.

When
n=k
,
\det_{n\,n}
is also denoted as
\det_{k}
and is clearly equal to the ordinary determinant of a square matrix.

For
k>n
the Cullis’ determinant of a
n\times k
matrix
X
is defined by
\det_{n\,k}(X)=\det_{k\,n}(X^{t})
(see
[
16
, Definition 1]
).

The Cullis’ determinant has many properties similar to those of the ordinary determinant (see
[
3
, §5, §27, §32]
,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
for detailed proofs).

1.

For a
n\times n
matrix
X
,
\det_{n\,n}(X)=\det(X).

2.

\det_{n\,k}(X)
is a linear function of columns of
X
.

3.

If a matrix
X
has two identical columns or one of its columns is a linear combination of other columns, then
\det_{n\,k}(X)
is equal to zero.

4.

Interchanging any two columns of
X
changes the sign of
\det_{n\,k}(X)
.

5.

Adding a linear combination of columns of
X
to another column of
X
does not change
\det_{n\,k}(X)
.

6.

\det_{n\,k}(X)
can be calculated using the Laplace expansion along a column of
X
. That is, if
1<k\leq n
, then for any
n\times k
matrix
X=(x_{i\,j})
the expansion of
\det_{n\,k}(X)
along the
j
-th column is given by

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+j}x_{i\,j}\det_{(n-1)\,(k-1)}\Bigl(X(i|j)\Bigr),

where
X(i|j)
denotes the
(n-1)\times(k-1)
matrix obtained from
X
by deleting the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column.

After the work of Cullis,
\det_{n\,k}
was studied by Radić in
[
16
,
18
,
17
,
19
]
, Makarewicz, Mozgawa, Pikuta and Szałkowski in
[
13
,
11
,
12
]
, Amiri, Fathy and Bayat in
[
1
]
, Nakagami and Yanay in
[
15
]
and by the authors in
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

While there exist polynomial-time algorithms for calculating the determinant of square matrix such as Gaussian elimination, every algorithm developed thus far for finding the value of the Cullis determinant of given matrix requires an exponential time. The main result of this paper allows to present a polynomial-time algorithm for computing the Cullis’ determinant. In order to formulate it we need to provide several definitions.

Definition 1.2
.

By
\Pi_{2m}\subseteq S_{2m}
we denote the set of all permutations
\pi
such that
\pi(1)<\pi(3)\ldots<\pi(2n-1)
and
\pi(2l-1)<\pi(2l)
for all
1\leq l\leq m
.

Definition 1.3

(
[
20
, Cf. p. 103]
)
.

Let
X
be a
2m\times 2m
skew-symmetric matrix. Then the Pfaffian
\pf(X)
of
X
is defined to be the function:

\pf(X)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi)\Pi_{l=1}^{m}x_{\pi(2l-1)\,\pi(2l)}.

The Pfaffian is a polynomial function of coefficients of antisymmetric matrix
A
which satisfies the equality
\pf^{2}(A)=\det(A)
. The proof of this equality and the further information on the Pfaffian could be found, example, in
[
2
, III.5]
. The properties of the Pfaffian which we use in our proofs are presented in Section
2
.

Definition 1.4
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
. Given a tuple
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
of natural numbers, the sign of
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is denoted by
\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
and is defined by

\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})=\begin{cases}0,&\exists 1\leq i<j\leq n\colon c_{i}=c_{j}\\
(-1)^{|\{(i,j)|1\leq i<j\leq n,c_{i}>c_{j}\}|},&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Definition 1.5
.

By
{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}=(d^{(n)}_{i\,j})
we denote the
n\times n
matrix defined by

d^{(n)}_{i\,j}=(-1)^{i+j}\sgn(i,j),\;1\leq i,j\leq n.

Using these definitions, the main result of our paper is formulated as follows:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n,k\geq 1
,
X=(x_{i\,j})
a matrix of size
n\times k
with entries from the field
{\mathbb{F}}
. If
n\geq k
, then the following equality in
{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1\,1},\ldots,x_{n\,k}]
holds

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+k}\pf\left(X^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X\right),&k\;\;\mbox{is even},\\[15.0pt]
(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+(k+1)}\pf\left((X^{\prime})^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X^{\prime}\right),&k\;\;\mbox{is odd},\;n\;\;\mbox{is even},\\[15.0pt]
(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+(k+1)}\pf\left((X^{\prime\prime})^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n+1)}X^{\prime\prime}\right),&k\;\;\mbox{is odd},\;n\;\;\mbox{is odd}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here
X^{\prime}
is a
n\times(k+1)
matrix defined by
X^{\prime}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1\,1}&\cdots&x_{1\,k}&1\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{n\,1}&\cdots&x_{n\,k}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
X^{\prime\prime}
is a
(n+1)\times(k+1)
matrix defined by
X^{\prime\prime}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1\,1}&\cdots&x_{1\,k}&1\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{n\,1}&\cdots&x_{n\,k}&1\\
0&\cdots&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. If
n<k
, then the equality (
1.1
) holds for
\det_{k\,n}(X^{t})
which is equal to
\det_{n\,k}(X)
by the definition.

To prove Theorem
1.6
, we begin with the case where the
n\times k
matrix
X
has even number of columns, i.e.,
k
is even. In this case, we establish that the coefficients of monomials of the form
x_{i_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{i_{k}\,k}
in the expansions of
\det_{n\,k}(X)
and
\pf\left(X^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X\right)
are equal for all
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\leq n
(Lemma
4.1
). For this, we prove that
\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})
is expressed as the Pfaffian of the
2m\times 2m
matrix
C=(c_{i\,j})
, where
c_{i\,j}=\sgn(c_{i},c_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2m
(Lemma
3.6
). The remaining cases in the equality (
1.1
) are carried out by applying the properties of the Cullis’ determinant (Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.5
).

The further text is organized as follows: in Section
2
we provide the necessary notation, definitions and preliminary facts regarding the Cullis’ determinant and the Pfaffian; in Section
3
we introduce the notion of the sign of a tuple of integers and prove its necessary properties; Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the main theorem, which is performed by splitting it into three lemmas; in Section
5
we discuss computational aspects of obtained result.

2
Notation, definitions, and preliminary facts

By
{\mathbb{F}}
we denote a field without any restrictions on its characteristic and cardinality.

We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
the set of all
n\times k
matrices with the entries from a certain field
{\mathbb{F}}.
Let
E_{i\,j}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}
denote the matrix unit, i.e. all elements of
E_{i\,j}
are equal to 0 except the element belonging to the intersection of the
i
-th row and
j
-th column which is equal to 1. By
x_{i\,j}
we denote the element of a matrix
X
lying on the intersection of its
i
-th row and
j
-th column. The notation
X_{i\,j}
is also used for this purpose if it is necessary.

For
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}(\mathbb{F})
by
A^{t}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,n}(\mathbb{F})
we denote a transpose of the matrix
A
, i.e.
A^{t}_{i\,j}=A_{j\,i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq n
.

We use the notation for submatrices following
[
14
]
. That is, given a matrix
A
, by
A[I|J]
we denote the
|I|\times|J|
submatrix of
A
lying on the intersection of rows with the indices from the set
I
and the columns with the indices from the set
J
. By
A(I|J)
we denote a submatrix of
A
derived from it by striking out from it the rows with indices belonging to
I
and the columns with the indices belonging to
J
. The absence of one of index set
I,J
means that the corresponding set is the empty set, e.g.,
A(I|)
denotes a matrix derived from
A
by striking out from it the rows with indices belonging to
I
. If one of
I,J
is given explicitly, then the curly brackets used in set notation are omitted, e.g., we write
A[1,2|3,4]
instead of
A[\{1,2\}|\{3,4\}]
. The notation with mixed brackets is also used, e.g.,
A(|1]
denotes the first column of the matrix
A
.

We use the bold font to denote vectors and lower indices to denote their coordinates. In the case if we need the series of vectors, we use the upper indices placed in braces. E.g., if
\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},
then
\mathbf{v}^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
\mathbf{v}_{1}=1
. If
\mathbf{u}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{u}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
, then
\mathbf{u}^{(1)}_{1}=1
and
\mathbf{u}^{(2)}_{1}=0
.

We also use the notation below which is introduced following
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

By
[n]
we denote the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a set and
k\geq 1
be an integer. By
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{k}
we denote the set of all injections from
[k]
to
X
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. By
S_{n}
we denote the set of all permutations of
[n]
, i.e.,
S_{n}=\mathcal{C}_{[n]}^{n}.

Definition 2.4
.

Given an injection
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{[n]}^{k}
we denote by
\sgn_{n\,k}(\sigma)
a number defined by

\sgn_{n\,k}(\sigma)=(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)}\cdot\sgn(\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(k)).

Lemma 2.5

(Cf.
[
15
, Theorem 13]
)
.

If
n\geq k
and
X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}(\mathbb{F})
, then

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{[n]}^{k}}\sgn_{n\,k}(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)\,1}x_{\sigma(2)\,2}\ldots x_{\sigma(k)\,k}.

The following properties of the Pfaffian are used throughout this paper.

Lemma 2.6

(Cf.
[
20
, Proposition 2.3(b)]
)
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2m\,2m}({\mathbb{F}})
and
A
is skew-symmetric. Then

\pf(BAB^{t})=\det(B)\pf(A).

Lemma 2.7

(Cf.
[
20
, Proposition 2.3(c)]
)
.

Let
G=(g_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2m\,2m}({\mathbb{F}})
be a skew-symmetric matrix defined by
g_{i\,j}=\sgn(i,j)
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2m
. Then
\pf(G)=1
.

Lemma 2.8

(Cf.
[
4
, Formula (D1) on p. 116]
)
.

If
X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2m\,2m}({\mathbb{F}})
and
1\leq j\leq 2m
, then

\pf(X)=\sum_{1\leq i<j}(-1)^{i+j-1}x_{i\,j}\pf\left(X(i,j|i,j)\right)+\sum_{j<i\leq 2m}(-1)^{i+j}x_{i\,j}\pf\left(X(i,j|i,j)\right).

3
The sign of a tuple of integers and its properties

In Section we defined the notion of the sign of a tuple (see Definition
1.4
). Here we provide and prove its necessary properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be a tuple of natural numbers. Then by
\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
we denote a matrix defined by

\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}\sgn(c_{1},c_{1})&\cdots&\sgn(c_{1},c_{n})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\sgn(c_{n},c_{1})&\cdots&\sgn(c_{n},c_{n})\end{pmatrix}

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
be a permutation. Then by
\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
we denote the permutation matrix corresponding to
\pi
which is defined by

\mathbf{P}^{\pi}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i\,(\pi i)}
.

The definition of the determinant of square matrix implies that
\det(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})=\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(n))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
be a permutation and
A=(a_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
be a matrix. Then

\mathbf{P}^{\pi}A(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\pi(1)\,\pi(1)}&\cdots&a_{\pi(1)\,\pi(n)}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{\pi(n)\,\pi(1)}&\cdots&a_{\pi(n)\,\pi(n)}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.1)

Proof.

Indeed, if
1\leq k,l\leq n
, then

(\mathbf{P}^{\pi}A(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t})_{k\,l}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}{p^{\pi}}_{k\,i}a_{i\,j}{(p^{\pi})}^{t}_{j\,l}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}p^{\pi}_{k\,i}a_{i\,j}p^{\pi}_{l\,j}.

(3.2)

Since the only nonzero element of the
i
-th row of
\mathbf{P}^{\pi}
lies in the
\pi(i)
-th column and is equal to
1
,

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}p^{\pi}_{k\,i}a_{i\,j}p^{\pi}_{l\,j}=p^{\pi}_{k\,\pi(k)}a_{\pi(k)\,\sigma(l)}p^{\pi}_{l\,\pi(l)}=a_{\pi(k)\,\pi(l)}.

(3.3)

By combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we obtain that

(\mathbf{P}^{\pi}A(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t})_{k\,l}=a_{\pi(k)\,\pi(l)}.

(3.4)

Since the equality (
3.4
) holds for all
1\leq k,l\leq n
, the equality (
3.1
) is established.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
be a permutation, and
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be a tuple of natural numbers. Then

\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\cdot\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t}=\SGN(c_{\pi(1)},\ldots,c_{\pi(n)})

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be a tuple of natural numbers,
\pi\in S_{n}
be a permutation. Then

\sgn(c_{\pi(1)},\ldots,c_{\pi(n)})=\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(n))\cdot\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}).

Proof.

Indeed, this equality clearly holds in the case if
\pi
is a transposition and consequently holds for all
\pi\in S_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2m}
be a tuple of natural numbers. Then

\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right)=\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m}).

(3.5)

Proof.

If there exist
1\leq i<j\leq 2m
such that
c_{i}=c_{j}
, then the equality (
3.5
) clearly holds.

Assume now that all
c_{i}
are pairwise distinct. Then there is
\pi\in S_{2m}
such that

c_{\pi(1)}<\ldots<c_{\pi(2m)}.

(3.6)

The equality (
3.6
) implies in particular that

\sgn(c_{\pi(i)},c_{\pi(j)})=\sgn(i,j)\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;1\leq i,j\leq 2m.

(3.7)

Hence,

\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t}\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Corollary}\penalty\ \ref{cor:PermMatOnSGNMat}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\SGN(c_{\pi(1)},\ldots,c_{\pi(2m)})\overset{\eqref{lem:PfOfSGNIsSgn:eq1}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}G,

(3.8)

where
G=(g_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2m\,2m}({\mathbb{F}})
is defined by
g_{i\,j}=\sgn(i,j).

On the one hand,

\pf\left(\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t}\right)\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:PfOfProdTrans}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\det(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right)\\
\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize see Definition\penalty\ \ref{def:PermMat}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right).

(3.9)

On the other hand,

\pf\left(\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})(\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{t}\right)\overset{\eqref{lem:PfOfSGNIsSgn:eq2}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\pf(G)\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:PfD}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}1.

(3.10)

By aligning (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) together we obtain that

\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\cdot\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right)=1.

(3.11)

In addition,

\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\cdot\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:PermChangesSign}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\sgn(c_{\pi(1)},\ldots,c_{\pi(2m)})\overset{\eqref{lem:PfOfSGNIsSgn:eq3}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}1

(3.12)

By aligning (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) together we conclude that

\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\cdot\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right)=\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\cdot\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m}).

(3.13)

Since
\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\neq 0
, the equality (
3.13
) implies (
3.5
).
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2m}
be a tuple of natural numbers. Then

\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\sgn(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)}).

(3.14)

Proof.

Using the definition of the Pfaffian, we obtain that

\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi)\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\sgn(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)})=\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right).

(3.15)

Then from Lemma
3.6
we obtain that

\pf\left(\SGN(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\right)=\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m}).

(3.16)

By aligning the equalities (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) together we obtain (
3.14
).
∎

4
Expressing
\det_{n\,k}
through the Pfaffian

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
in which the Cullis’ determinant is expressed through the Pfaffian. The proof is split into three lemmas in which we consider different
classes of tuples
(n,k)
: in Lemma
4.1
we assume that
k
is even, in Lemma
4.3
we consider the case when
k
is odd and
n
is even, and Lemma
4.5
is devoted to the case when
k
is odd and
n
is odd.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 2m
,
X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2m}({\mathbb{F}}).
Then

\det_{n\,2m}(X)=(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\pf\left(X^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X\right)

(4.1)

as elements of
{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1\,1},\ldots,x_{n\,2m}],
where
{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}=(d^{(n)}_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
is from Definition
1.5
.

Proof.

Let us first notice that monomials on the both sides of (
4.1
) have the form
x_{c_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{c_{2m}\,2m}
for some tuple
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\in\mathbb{N}^{2m}
. Let us show that the coefficients of these monomials are equal to each other.

Lemma
2.5
implies that the coefficient of
x_{c_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{c_{2m}\,2m}
on the left-hand side of (
4.1
) is equal to

(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(c_{i}-i)}\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m}).

(4.2)

To find the coefficient of
x_{c_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{c_{2m}\,2m}
on the right-hand side of (
4.1
), let us expand the right-hand side of (
4.1
) as follows

(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\pf\left(X^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X\right)=(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\\
\times\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\left(X^{t}{\mathbf{D}}^{(n)}X\right)_{\pi(2l-1)\,\pi(2l)}\\
=(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}x_{i\,\pi(2l-1)}d^{(n)}_{i\,j}x_{j\,\pi(2l)}\right)\\
\overset{{\scriptsize\mbox{Definition}\penalty\ \ref{def:Dmat}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\phantom{XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX}\\
\times\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(2m))\Pi_{l=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}\sgn(i,j)x_{i\,\pi(2l-1)}x_{j\,\pi(2l)}\right).

(4.3)

The monomials
x_{c_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{c_{2m}\,2m}
and
x_{i_{1}\,\pi(1)}x_{j_{1}\,\pi(2)}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{i_{1}\,\pi(2m-1)}x_{j_{1}\,\pi(2m)}
are equal if and only if
(i_{l},j_{l})=(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)})
for all
1\leq l\leq k
. Therefore, (
4.3
) implies that the coefficient of
x_{c_{1}\,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{c_{2m}\,2m}
on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is equal to

(-1)^{1+2+\ldots+2m}\left(\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi)(-1)^{c_{1}+\ldots+c_{2m}}\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\sgn(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)})\right)\\
=(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(c_{i}-i)}\left(\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi)\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\sgn(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)})\right).

(4.4)

From Lemma
3.7
we obtain that

\sgn(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{2m}}\sgn(\pi)\Pi_{l=1}^{m}\sgn(c_{\pi(2l-1)},c_{\pi(2l)}).

Hence, (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) are equal. Since these coefficients are equal for all tuples
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2m})\in\mathbb{N}^{2m}
, the equality (
4.1
) is established.
∎

5
Algorithm for computing
\det_{n\,k}(X)

In this section we show that Theorem
1.6
could be used to develop an algorithm for finding the value of the Cullis’ determinant that improves the definition-based algorithm.

Indeed, if we calculate
\det_{n\,k}(X)
directly by finding all the maximal minors of
X
, then it requires
\binom{n}{k}D(k)
arithmetical operations, where
D(k)
is the number of arithmetical operations required for finding the determinant of
k\times k
matrix. Assume now that
k\geq 4
is fixed. Then
\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n\cdot(n-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(n-(k+1))}{1\cdot\ldots\cdot k}
is a polynomial of degree at least
4
over
n
. If we use the Gaussian elimination for calculating maximal minors, then in this case the definition-based method requires
O(n^{4})\cdot D(3)=O(n^{4})
arithmetical operations. If
k
is not fixed and tends to infinity, then the asymptotics became worse.

Let us define the procedure
\mathbf{CULLISDET}(n,k,X)
which receives as input integers
n,k\geq 1
and a matrix
X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
and returns
\det_{n\,k}(X)
. This procedure calls the subprocedure
\mathbf{PF}
defined in
[
5
, p. 273]
which finds the value of the Pfaffian of given matrix with entries belonging to any integral domain.
\mathbf{PF}
receives as input an element
c
of the integral domain, a positive integer
n
and a skew-symmetric
2n\times 2n
matrix
X
whose principal minors of any order
r\not\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
are all known to be divisible by
c^{r-1}
, and returns
\pf(\Gamma)/c^{r-1}
. In particular,
\mathbf{PF}(1,n,\Gamma)
returns
\pf(\Gamma)
.

\mathbf{CULLISDET}
procedure

(
