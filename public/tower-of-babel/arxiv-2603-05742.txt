E ​ 𝒵 E\mathcal{Z} -boundaries, splittings over finite subgroups, and dense amalgams
Mateusz Kandybo, Jacek Świątkowski

Abstract
The dense amalgam is an operation (introduced in [ 13 ] ) which to any finite collection of metrizable compacta associates canonically some new highly disconnected compact metrisable space in which embedded copies of the initial spaces are appropriately uniformly and disjointly distributed. We show that in the very general framework of E ​ 𝒵 E\mathcal{Z} -boundaries (unifying many frameworks such as Gromov boundaries, CAT(0)-boundaries, systolic boundaries, etc.), any boundary Z Z of an infinitely ended group Γ \Gamma equipped with an appropriate splitting along finite subgroups has a form of the dense amalgam of the limit sets in Z Z of the factor subgroups of this splitting.

Introduction

It is a classical and widely studied problem, to relate algebraic features
of discrete groups with some corresponding topological properties of their
boundaries at infinity. In this paper we study such a relationship
for the property of admitting a nontrivial splitting over finite subgroups.

As it was shown in
[
13
]
, in many frameworks
(such as Gromov boundaries of hyperbolic groups, CAT(0) boundaries,
systolic boundaries, etc.) groups that split over finite subgroups admit
boundaries that have some particular form of so called
dense amalgam

(of the boundaries of the factors).
Dense amalgam is a topological operation (introduced in
[
13
]

and recalled in Subsection
2.3
below) which to any finite collection
of compact metrisable spaces associates some new (unique up to homeomorphism)
compact metrisable and highly disconnected space in which infinitely many copies of the initial spaces
appear as appropriately uniformly distributed subspaces (see Definition
2.3.13
).
The goal of this paper is the twofold extension of the aforementioned observation. First, we show that in the contexts as above

any
boundary (of the corresponding kind) of a group admitting
a splitting over finite subgroups has a form of the dense amalgam of some
spaces associated naturally to the factors. This is indeed an extension,
since a given group can have many pairwise non-homeomorphic boundaries
of a given kind, and we show that
all
of them have a form of a
dense amalgam. Second, we justify the just mentioned ‘‘dense amalgam’’
phenomenon in a unified framework of
so called
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundaries of groups (as recalled in Subsection
2.2
).
The latter framework encompasses and extends all the previously
mentioned frameworks.

The precise statement of our main result requires few more technical
and terminological explanations. First of all, we encode splittings of groups

\Gamma
over their finite subgroups by means of expressions of
these groups as the fundamental groups
\pi_{1}\mathcal{G}
of appropriate
graphs of groups
\mathcal{G}
. The vertex groups
G_{v}
of
\mathcal{G}

are then viewed naturally as subgroups of
\Gamma
, and they are the factors
of the considered splittings. See Subsection
2.1
for more detailed explanations, and in particular see Definition
2.1.7
for the explanation of the concept
of a non-elementary graph of groups.
Next, we refer to the notion of the limit set of a subgroup for a group
equipped with an
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary. The details of this concept
are recalled in Subsection
2.2
.
We denote by
V_{Y}
the vertex set of a graph
Y
.
Our main result then reads as follows.

Theorem A

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a non-elementary graph of groups (over a finite graph
Y
) with finite
edge groups, let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}\mathcal{G}
be its fundamental group,
and let
\left\{G_{v}:v\in V_{Y}\right\}
be the family of the vertex groups of
\mathcal{G}
, which we assume to be finitely generated
(and which we view as subgroups of
\Gamma
). Let
(\overline{E},Z)
be an

E\mathcal{Z}
-structure for
\Gamma
. Then the
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary

Z
of
\Gamma
(corresponding to this structure) is homeomorphic to
the dense amalgam of the family of the limit sets
\left\{\Lambda G_{v}:v\in V_{Y}\right\}

in
Z
, i.e.

Z\cong\damalgam_{v\in V_{Y}}\Lambda G_{v}.

By referring to Stallings’ theorem about ends of groups
and to various further basic properties of splittings of groups over finite subgroups,
Theorem A combined with few other observations from
this paper yields the following corollary (see the last part of Section
5

for a detailed justification).

Theorem B

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely presented group, and suppose that

\Gamma
admits an EZ-boundary
Z
. Then:

1.

\Gamma
is 1-ended iff
Z
is connected;

2.

\Gamma
is 2-ended iff
Z
is a doubleton;

3.

\Gamma
is infinitely ended iff
Z
is the dense amalgam of a family of connected spaces.

The paper is organized as follows.
We present an outline of the proof of Theorem A in Subsection
2.4

(after completing all preliminary informations in Subsections
2.1
-
2.3
).
The actual proof of Theorem A occupies Section
5
. Sections
3
and
4

contain some preparations for this proof. In particular, in Section
3
we
introduce tools related to various phenomena of separation in spaces
and groups. Section
4
contains some auxiliary analysis and classification of the

E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary points
for groups
\Gamma=\pi_{1}\mathcal{G}
as in the statement of Theorem A. Lastly, in Section
6
we state some final remarks and ask
3
questions regarding possible expansions of our results from Theorems A and B.

Preliminaries

2.1
Graphs of groups

In this subsection we recall some definitions and basic facts from Bass-Serre theory. The reader is encouraged to consult the book
[
12
]
for more complete exposition, or Section
4
of
[
13
]
, where this material is treated in the context related to the aims of this paper.

For any graph
Y
(we allow graphs to have loops and multiple edges) we denote the set of vertices of
Y
as
V_{Y}
, the set of all (unoriented) edges of
Y
as
\left|O\right|_{Y}
. To each edge
\left|y\right|\in\left|O\right|_{Y}
we associate two oppositely oriented edges underlied by
\left|y\right|
. Moreover we denote the set of all oriented edges of
Y
as
O_{Y}
. For any oriented edge
y\in O_{Y}
we denote its initial vertex as
\alpha(y)
, its final vertex as
\omega(y)
, the oppositely oriented edge as
\overline{y}
and the corresponding non-oriented edge as
\left|y\right|
.

Definition 2.1.1

(Paths and branches)

An
immersed path
in a graph
Y
(or shortly a
path
) is a (finite or infinite) sequence
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
of oriented edges such that each pair of consecutive edges satisfies
\omega(c_{i})=\alpha(c_{i+1})
and
c_{i+1}\neq\overline{c_{i}}
. The
length

l(c)
of a path
c
is the number of edges in this path. The
initial
vertex of a path, denoted
\alpha(c)
is the initial vertex of its first edge, i.e.
\alpha(c)\coloneqq\alpha(c_{1})
. In case of finite paths, the
final
vertex of a path
\omega(c)
is the final vertex of its last edge, i.e.
\omega(c)\coloneqq\omega(c_{l(c)})
. When
Y
is a tree, any infinite path in
Y
is called a
branch
.

Definition 2.1.2

(Graph of groups)

A
graph of groups

(\mathcal{G},Y)
is a graph
Y
together with a family of groups
\mathcal{G}
indexed by all vertices of
Y
and all oriented edges of
Y
together with a set of group monomorphisms described below. To differentiate between the family
\mathcal{G}
and groups being its elements we use a standard notation where
\mathcal{G}
denotes the whole family while
G_{v},G_{y}
are elements of that family indexed respectively by
v,y
(note the lack of the calligraphic font for groups). The group monomorphisms in the family
\mathcal{G}
are
i_{y}:G_{y}\to G_{\omega(y)}
and they are defined for every
y\in O_{Y}
; it is also assumed that
G_{y}=G_{\overline{y}}
.

In this paper we will always assume that the underlying graph
Y
of a graph of groups
\mathcal{G}
is finite, and that the vertex groups
G_{v}
are finitely generated, even though the next definitions in this subsection do not require these assumptions.

The following definition can be found in Chapter 5.1 of
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1.3

(Fundamental group of a graph of groups)

Fix a spanning tree
T
of
Y
. Define the
fundamental group

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
as the quotient of the free product
\displaystyle{F_{O_{Y}\setminus O_{T}}*\mathop{{{{*}}}}_{v\in V_{Y}}G_{v}}
by the normal subgroup generated by all relations of one of the following form:

i_{y}(g)^{-1}i_{\overline{y}}(g)=1\text{ where }y\in O_{T}\text{ and }g\in G_{y}

as well as, for
s_{y}
denoting the generator of
F_{O_{Y}\setminus O_{T}}
corresponding to an edge
y
,

s_{y}s_{\overline{y}}=1\text{ where }y\in O_{Y}\setminus O_{T},

i_{y}(g)^{-1}s_{y}^{-1}i_{\overline{y}}(g)s_{y}=1\text{ where }y\in O_{Y}\setminus O_{T},g\in G_{y}.

It was proven in Chapter 5.2 of
[
12
]
that each of the vertex groups
G_{v}

naturally embeds into
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
as a subgroup.
Using these facts, we will abuse notation treating all
G_{v}
as subgroups of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
. Furthermore, it was shown in Chapter 5.1 of
[
12
]
that any choice of a spanning tree
T
would yield an isomorphic
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
, therefore we refer to it as the fundamental group of
\mathcal{G}
.

The fundamental groups of graphs of groups provide a convenient way of describing groups obtained as iterated free products with amalgamation and HNN-extensions. The main advantage of using the graph of groups expression comes from existence of the so-called Bass-Serre tree – a structure on which the corresponding fundamental group acts in a manageable way. In our case, Bass-Serre trees will be useful for constructing appropriate separating sets in spaces and graphs appearing in later parts of the paper. We present the definition of a Bass-Serre tree below. More details and context concerning this definition can be found in Chapter 5.3 of
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1.4

(Bass-Serre tree)

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a graph of groups together with a fixed spanning tree
T
and a fixed orientation of edges in
Y
(i.e. a set
A
such that for each pair of edges
y,\overline{y}
exactly one of those edges belongs to
A
). We will denote the unique edge not in
A
from any pair
\left\{y,\overline{y}\right\}
as
\hat{y}
. Then, for each edge
y
we have
G_{y}=G_{\hat{y}}\hookrightarrow G_{\omega(\hat{y})}<\Gamma\coloneqq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
. With that we can treat all the edge groups of
(\mathcal{G},Y)
as subgroups of
\Gamma
. A
Bass-Serre tree
of a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},Y)
is a graph
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{X}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
whose sets of vertices and edges are respectively
V_{\widetilde{X}}=\bigsqcup_{v\in V_{Y}}\Gamma/G_{v}
and
O_{\widetilde{X}}=\bigsqcup_{y\in O_{Y}}\Gamma/G_{y}
.
It remains to define
\omega(\gamma G_{y})
and
\overline{\gamma G_{y}}
for each edge
\gamma G_{y}\in O_{\widetilde{X}}
, which is done as follows:

•

\overline{\gamma G_{y}}=\gamma G_{\overline{y}}
,

•

\omega(\gamma G_{y})=\begin{cases}\gamma s_{y}G_{\omega(y)}\text{, when }y\in A\setminus O_{T}\\
\gamma G_{\omega(y)}\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}
.

It is shown in a Chapter 5.3 of
[
12
]
that a graph constructed in such a way is a tree. Moreover
\widetilde{X}
is equipped with a natural action by
\Gamma
:

\gamma^{\prime}\cdot(\gamma G_{v})=\gamma^{\prime}\gamma G_{v}\text{ and }\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\gamma G_{y}=\gamma^{\prime}\gamma G_{y}.

Remark 2.1.5

Note that, with the definition as above, the coset
\gamma G_{y}
representing any edge in a Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
is contained in the union of cosets
\alpha(\gamma G_{y})\cup\omega(\gamma G_{y})
representing the endpoints of this edge. Indeed

\gamma G_{y}=\gamma G_{\hat{y}}\subseteq\gamma G_{\omega(\hat{y})}=\omega(\gamma G_{\hat{y}})\subseteq\alpha(\gamma G_{\hat{y}})\cup\omega(\gamma G_{\hat{y}})=\alpha(\gamma G_{y})\cup\omega(\gamma G_{y}).

The next two definitions aim to provide an appropriate context for the technical concept of a
non-elementary graph of groups
, that appears in the statement of our first main result (Theorem A of the introduction). The primary source for this concept is Subsection 4.1 of the paper
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.1.6

(Elementary collapse)

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a graph of groups and let
y\in O_{Y}
be an edge that is not a loop such that the map
i_{y}
is an isomorphism. An
elementary collapse
along the edge
y
is an operation returning a new graph of groups
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
, where
Y^{\prime}
is a graph obtained from
Y
by contracting the edge
y
to a point (denoted
v_{y}
) and by setting
G_{v_{y}}=G_{\alpha(y)}
and not changing all of the other groups and maps.

Note that elementary collapses do not change the fundamental group of a graph of groups.

Definition 2.1.7

(Non-elementary graph of groups)

We say that a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},Y)
is
simply elementary
if it satisfies one of the three conditions:

•

Y
consists of a single vertex without any edges;

•

Y
consists of two vertices connected by a unique non-oriented edge
\left|y\right|
and the images of both maps
i_{y},i_{\overline{y}}
are the subgroups of index 2
in the corresponding vertex groups;

•

Y
consists of a single vertex and a single non-oriented loop
\left|y\right|
and both of the maps
i_{y},i_{\overline{y}}
are isomorphisms.

A graph of groups is
non-elementary
if it can’t be reduced to a simply elementary graph via elementary collapses.

Some consequences of being a non-elementary graph of groups are provided in Lemma 4.1.6 in
[
13
]
.
Since we will need them in our arguments
for proving Theorem A, we restate this lemma below
for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.1.8

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a non-elementary graph of groups with all of the edge groups being finite. Let
\Gamma\coloneqq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
, and let
\widetilde{X}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}(\mathcal{G},Y,T)
be the Bass-Serre tree.

(i)

For every vertex
v\in V_{Y}
with infinite
G_{v}
, there exists an edge
y\in O_{Y}
adjacent to
v
and such that any edge of form
\left|\gamma G_{y}\right|
splits
\widetilde{X}
into two subtrees, each of which contains at least one vertex of form
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
for any vertex
v^{\prime}\in V_{Y}
and any
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
;

(ii)

If all of the vertex groups of
\mathcal{G}
are finite, then

\widetilde{X}
is an infinite locally finite tree and
there exists
v\in V_{Y}
such that for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
the vertex

\gamma G_{v}
splits
\widetilde{X}
into at least
3
infinite components.

2.2
\mathbf{E}\mathbfcal{Z}
-boundaries
and limit sets

We present the definition of an

E\mathcal{Z}
-structure and
an
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary, in a version appropriate for the purposes of
this paper (and slightly more general than the versions appearing in the literature,
see e.g.
[
7
,
8
]
).

Definition 2.2.9

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group.
The pair of spaces
(\overline{E},Z)
such that
Z\subseteq\overline{E}

is called an
E\mathcal{Z}
-
structure

for
\Gamma
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)
\overline{E}
is a compact metrizable space which is connected and locally connected;

(2)
Z
is a Z-set of
\overline{E}
, i.e. it is closed in
\overline{E}

and there is a deformation

h_{t}:\overline{E}\to\overline{E}
with
h_{0}=\hbox{id}
and

h_{t}(\overline{E})\cap Z=\emptyset
for
t>0
;

(3)
E\coloneqq\overline{E}\setminus Z
is equipped with a proper and cocompact action by
\Gamma
;

(4) for any compact
K\subseteq E
the family of
\Gamma
-translates
of
K
(under the above action) is
null
in
\overline{E}
(i.e. for any metric on
\overline{E}
compatible with its topology the diameters of the
\Gamma
-translates
of
K
converge to 0);

(5) the above action of
\Gamma
on
E
extends continuously
to an action on
\overline{E}
.

The set
Z
above is called an
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary
of
\Gamma
.

Remark 2.2.10

Note that, under the assumptions as above, the group
\Gamma
is automatically finitely generated, and it is finitely presented e.g. when
E
is additionally a locally finite simply connected CW-complex. The space
E
above is automatically locally compact, connected, and locally connected.

Note also that if
Z\subseteq\overline{E}
is a Z-set, then for any compact subset

K\subseteq E=\overline{E}\setminus Z
:

(1) there is a deformation
h_{t}:\overline{E}\to\overline{E}
with

h_{0}=\hbox{id}
,
h_{t}|_{K}=\hbox{id}_{K}
for
t\geq 0
, and with

h_{t}(\overline{E})\cap Z=\emptyset
for
t>0
;

(2) there is a deformation

h_{t}:\overline{E}\setminus K\to\overline{E}\setminus K
with
h_{0}=\hbox{id}

and with
h_{t}(\overline{E}\setminus K)\cap Z=\emptyset
for
t>0
;

(3) the inclusion
E\setminus K\to\overline{E}\setminus K

is a homotopy equivalence.

The concept of a
limit set
, to which we now turn, is well-known in group theory and used e.g. to study Kleinian groups. It is hard to pinpoint the original source of the definition of a limit sets. The definition we present below is based on Definition 2.9 of the paper
[
9
]
, however we state it in slightly more general fashion.

Definition 2.2.11

(Limit set)

Let
(\overline{E},Z)
be an
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure for
\Gamma
. The
limit set
of a subset
N\subseteq E
is the intersection
\overline{N}\cap Z
, where
\overline{N}
is the closure of
N
in
\overline{E}
. Such limit set will be denoted as
\Lambda N
. The
limit set of a subset
M\subseteq\Gamma
is defined as the limit set
\Lambda Me_{0}
of the set of translates
Me_{0}\coloneqq\{\gamma e_{0}:\gamma\in M\}

of a fixed base point
e_{0}\in E
. Note that even though this definition involves a base point
e_{0}
, the limit set itself does not depend on the choice of
e_{0}
. Indeed: for any two choices of points
e_{0},e_{0}^{\prime}
the diameters of the translates of the doubleton
\left\{e_{0},e_{0}^{\prime}\right\}
converge to
0
, thus for any sequence
m_{n}e_{0}
convergent to a point in
Z
the corresponding sequence
m_{n}e_{0}^{\prime}
converges to the same point. Furthermore one can observe that, for
M
being a subgroup, this definition agrees with the definition from
[
9
]
. We will abuse the notation and denote the limit set of
M\subseteq\Gamma
as
\Lambda M
.

The following useful observation seems to belong to folklore,
and we include its proof for completeness.
We refer the reader e.g. to Chapter 9.1 in
[
5
]

for an introduction concerning the concept of ends (and 1-endedness) of a group.

Lemma 2.2.12

Let
\Gamma
be a group, and let
(\overline{E},Z)
be an
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure for
\Gamma
. Suppose that
H<\Gamma
is a finitely generated
subgroup of
\Gamma
which is 1-ended.
Then the limit set
\Lambda H\subseteq Z
is connected.
In particular, if
\Gamma
is 1-ended then
Z=\Lambda\Gamma

is connected.

Proof.

We start with some auxiliary terminology and observation.
Given a metric space
(X,d_{X})
and a real number
\epsilon>0
,
an
\epsilon
-path
in
X
is any finite sequence

x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
such that
d_{X}(x_{i-1},x_{i})<\epsilon

for
i=1,\dots,n
.
We say that
X
is
\epsilon
-connected
if any two points of
X

can be joined by an
\epsilon
-path.
It is not hard to observe that a compact metric space is connected
iff it is
\epsilon
-connected for any
\epsilon>0
.
Thus, to show that
\Lambda H
is connected, it is enough to show that
it is
\epsilon
-connected for any
\epsilon>0
.

Fix any two points
z,z^{\prime}\in\Lambda H
, and any
\epsilon>0
.
We will construct an
\epsilon
-path joining
z
with
z^{\prime}
.
To do this, fix a finite symmetric generating set
S=S^{-1}
for
H
,
and a base point
e_{0}\in E
. Fix also a metric
d
in
\overline{E}
,
and a compact subset
K\subseteq E
large enough so that for any pair

(\gamma,\gamma s):\gamma\in\Gamma,s\in S

such that
\gamma e_{0}\notin K
and
\gamma se_{0}\notin K
we have

d(\gamma e_{0},\gamma se_{0})<\epsilon/3

(such a
K
exists due to condition (4) in Definition

2.2.9
).
Put
A\coloneqq\{\gamma\in H:\gamma e_{0}\in K\}
and note that
A

is a finite subset of
H
. Enlarge
A
to a new finite subset
A_{0}\subseteq H

so that the distance of any point
\gamma e_{0}:\gamma\in H\setminus A_{0}

from the limit set
\Lambda H
is smaller than
\epsilon/3
.
By 1-endedness of
H
, the complement of
A_{0}
in the Cayley graph
\hbox{Cay}(H,S)

has precisely one unbounded connected component, and by local finiteness of

\hbox{Cay}(H,S)
, the number of bounded components in this complement is finite.
We can thus fix an even bigger finite
subset
A_{0}^{\prime}\subseteq H
such that any two elements of
H\setminus A_{0}^{\prime}

can be connected
in the Cayley graph
\hbox{Cay}(H,S)
by a polygonal path disjoint with
A_{0}
.

Now, choose any
h,h^{\prime}\in H\setminus A_{0}^{\prime}
such that
d(he_{0},z)<\epsilon/3

and
d(h^{\prime}e_{0},z^{\prime})<\epsilon/3
.
Connect the vertices
h,h^{\prime}
of
\hbox{Cay}(H,S)
with a polygonal path

h=h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{n}=h^{\prime}
disjoint from
A_{0}
. Then for any
i=1,\dots,n

we have
h_{i}=h_{i-1}s_{i}
for some
s_{i}\in S
.
Since this path omits
A_{0}
, and hence also omits
A
, the points

h_{i}e_{0}
are not contained in
K
, and thus we have

d(h_{i-1}e_{0},h_{i}e_{0})=d(h_{i-1}e_{0},h_{i-1}s_{i}e_{0})<\epsilon/3

for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Moreover, by the choice of
A_{0}
,
for any
i=1,\dots,n-1
there is a point
z_{i}\in\Lambda H
such that

d(z_{i},h_{i}e_{0})<\epsilon/3
.
By referring to the triangle inequality, we get that
the sequence
z,z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1},z^{\prime}
is an
\epsilon
-path in

\Lambda H
, as required. This finishes the proof.

2.3
Dense amalgams

Below we present a definition of a dense amalgam which was firstly described in the second author’s paper
[
13
]
. The definition of the dense amalgam can be found in the introduction of
[
13
]
and the proof of its existence and uniqueness can be found in Sections
1
and
2
of the same paper.

Definition 2.3.13

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be non-empty compact metrisable spaces.
The dense amalgam
of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, denoted
\damalgam(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, is the unique up to homeomorphism compact metrisable space which satisfies the following.
There exists a countable family
\mathcal{W}
of subspaces of
\damalgam(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, which splits as
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\mathcal{W}_{n}
, such that:

(a1)

\mathcal{W}
consists of pairwise disjoint subspaces
and each of the subfamilies
\mathcal{W}_{i}
consists of homeomorphic copies of
X_{i}
, respectively;

(a2)

the family
\mathcal{W}
is null (in the same sense as in Definition
2.2.9
)

(a3)

each
W\in\mathcal{W}
is a boundary subset of

3
Separation

In this section we study the concept of separating sets. Intuitively, a subset of a space is separating if it splits the space into two or more components. This definition however does not work for discrete spaces, such as finitely generated groups. Thus we formulate an auxiliary property called
R
-separation. In Subsection
3.1
we analyse how
the property of
R
-separation in groups interplays with ordinary separation
by appropriate subsets in the corresponding
E\mathcal{Z}
structures.
In Subsection
3.2
we develop a method of creating
R
-separating sets inside a group, which correspond to subgroups, along which
the group splits algebraically. In Subsection
3.3
we show a number of basic properties of compact separating sets in
E\mathcal{Z}
-structures. Finally, in the last Subsection
3.4
we prove a key lemma (Lemma
3.4.18
) that will allow us to easily find well-behaved compact separating sets inside
E\mathcal{Z}
-structures for groups splitting
over finite subgroups.

3.1
\mathbf{R}
-separation

Definition 3.1.1

(Separating set)

Let
X
be a connected topological space and let
x_{0},x_{1}\in X
. The set
I\subseteq X

separates

x_{0}
from
x_{1}
if
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are contained in different connected components of
X\setminus I
.

The main idea of separation in spaces that are not necessarily connected is obtained by ‘‘coarsifying’’ the definition of separation. This is done by replacing connected components in a previous definition by
R
-path connected components (in a sense of
R
-paths described in Definition I.8.27 of
[
2
]
and already mentioned in slightly different context in the proof of Lemma
2.2.12
). This idea is employed in a definition below.

Definition 3.1.2

(
\mathbf{R}
-separating set)

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
R>0
be a real number. An
R
-path
in
X
is a finite sequence
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
of elements of
X
such that
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leqslant R
for all
i\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
. We say that a set
I\subseteq X

R
-separates

x_{0}
from
x_{1}
if
d(x_{0},I)\geqslant R
,
d(x_{1},I)\geqslant R
and
x_{0},x_{1}
lie in two different
R
-path connected components of
X\setminus I
, i.e. there does not exist an
R
-path
x_{0}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime}
in
X\setminus I
such that
x_{0}^{\prime}=x_{0},x_{n}^{\prime}=x_{1}
.

The notions of separation and
R
-separation can be related in many ways. The following lemma, describing the way we can modify separating sets to be
R
-separating, will be useful later in this paper in the context of Cayley graphs.

Lemma 3.1.3

Let
X
be a geodesic metric space and let
x_{0},x_{1}
be two points in
X
such that some set
I
is separating
x_{0}
from
x_{1}
. If
d(x_{0},I)\geqslant\frac{3R}{2}
and
d(x_{1},I)\geqslant\frac{3R}{2}
, then the metric neighbourhood
N_{R/2}(I)
is
R
-separating
x_{0}
from
x_{1}
.

Proof.

First we can observe that
d(x_{0},N_{R/2}(I))\geqslant d(x_{0},I)-\frac{R}{2}\geqslant R
and
d(x_{1},N_{R/2}(I))\geqslant d(x_{1},I)-\frac{R}{2}\geqslant R
, thus it is enough show that
x_{0},x_{1}
lie in different
R
-path connected components.

Now suppose on the contrary that
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
lie in the same
R
-path connected components
X\setminus N_{R/2}(I)
, let
x_{0}^{\prime}=x_{0},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime}=x_{1}
be an
R
-path in
X\setminus N_{R/2}(I)
. Now let
\eta_{i}:[0,1]\to X
be a geodesic between
x_{i}^{\prime}
and
x_{i+1}^{\prime}
. Then we can define a path
\eta:[0,n]\to X
by setting
\eta(t)=\eta_{\lfloor t\rfloor}(t-\lfloor t\rfloor)
for all
t\in[0,n)
and putting
\eta(n)=x_{1}
. Of course
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
lie in different connected components of
X\setminus I
, therefore they have to lie in different path connected components of
X\setminus I
. In particular
\eta
is a continuous function thus we have
\eta(t)\in I
for some
t\in[i,i+1]
so in particular
\eta_{i}(t-i)\in I
. Since
\eta_{i}
is a geodesic we have

d(x_{i}^{\prime},\eta_{i}(t-i))+d(\eta_{i}(t-i),x_{i+1}^{\prime})=d(x_{i}^{\prime},x_{i+1}^{\prime})\leqslant R

and therefore we we have either
d(x_{i}^{\prime},\eta_{i}(t-i))\leqslant\frac{R}{2}
or
d(x_{i+1}^{\prime},\eta_{i}(t-i))\leqslant\frac{R}{2}
. This means that
x_{i}\in N_{R/2}(I)
or
x_{i+1}\in N_{R/2}(I)
. This contradiction ends the proof.
∎

In this subsection we make some preparatory observations concerning

-structures for

More precisely, we relate separation properties of edges in the corresponding

in the corresponding

Lemma 3.4.18

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a graph of groups with finite edge groups,
let
\Gamma
be its fundamental group, and let
\widetilde{X}
be the corresponding

(\overline{E},Z)
be an

-structure for
\Gamma
, and

containing

and a constant

Let
\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}}
be any edge of
\widetilde{X}
, and let

\widetilde{X}_{0},\widetilde{X}_{1}

the interior of
{\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}}}
from
\widetilde{X}

Finally, define the subsets

\gamma^{\prime\prime}K

with

\delta^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}\setminus N_{R_{0}}(\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}})

By cocompactness and properness of the group action, we know that there exists
a compact subset

\Gamma L=E
and

\diam(P)

from all of the points
\delta^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}\setminus N_{3\diam(P)/2}(\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}})

N_{\diam(P)/2}(\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}})
is also
\diam(P)

4
Points in
\mathbf{Z}

The standing assumption for this section is that
\mathcal{G}

is a graph of groups with finite edge groups,

\Gamma=\pi_{1}\mathcal{G}
is its fundamental group,
and
(\overline{E},Z)
is an
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure for
\Gamma
.
We proceed to study the topological structure of the set
Z
.
In Subsection
4.1

we describe two kinds of points
in
Z
, which we call
branch points
and
vertex points
(see Definitions
4.1.6
and
4.1.8
, respectively), and we show that there are no other points in
Z
. In Subsection
4.2
we study the properties of aforementioned points both as limits of points from
E
and by stating their topological properties inside the space
Z
. In Subsection
4.3
we use the previous two sections in order to describe the space
Z
in case when all of the vertex groups
G_{v}
are finite (so that there are no vertex points in
Z
). In that case the studied group
\Gamma
is finite, virtually
\mathbb{Z}
or virtually free,
and we get that the topological shape of
Z
is uniquely determined in each of these three cases (i.e.
Z
is empty,
a doubleton or it is homeomorphic to the Cantor space, respectively). Even though these latter observations seem to belong to the ‘‘mathematical folklore’’, we have decided to include the analysis of those cases for the sake of completeness of this paper.

4.1
Classification of points in
\mathbf{Z}

Let
\widetilde{X}
be the Bass-Serre tree of
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 4.1.1

Let
\varphi\colon O_{\widetilde{X}}\to\Gamma
be a function such that
\varphi(\gamma G_{y})\in\gamma G_{y}
.

Even though the function
\varphi
is not uniquely determined, it is quite well-behaved, due to finiteness of the edge groups in
(\mathcal{G},Y)
. The basic properties of
\varphi
are described in the next two facts, for which we omit the straightforward proofs.

Fact 4.1.2

There exists a constant
D
such that for any two functions
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
as above, and for any edge
\gamma G_{y}
of
\widetilde{X}
, we have
d_{S}(\varphi(\gamma G_{y}),\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma G_{y}))\leqslant D
.

Fact 4.1.3

The function
\varphi
as above is uniformly finite-to-one.

Using the function
\varphi
, to each branch in
\widetilde{X}
we can associate a sequence of points in
E
. The following lemma shows, that such sequence is always convergent to a point in
Z
.

Lemma 4.1.4

Fix a base point
e_{0}\in E
. For any branch
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
in
\widetilde{X}
, the sequence of points
\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
does converge to a point
z\in Z
.

Proof.

Put
e_{n}=\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
and
c_{n}=\gamma_{n}^{\prime}G_{y_{n}}
. Of course, since
\overline{E}
is compact, we can choose a convergent subsequence
e_{n_{k}}
. By properness of the action of
\Gamma
on
E
,
the limit
z=\lim_{k\to\infty}e_{k}
is a point of
Z
. Now let us take a compact set
K\subseteq E
as in the assertion of Lemma
3.4.18
and a positive integer
R
as in the assertion of Fact
3.4.20
. We will show that a sequences
\gamma_{k}=\varphi(c_{n_{k}})
and
e_{n}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
3.3.16
.

For any given
l\in\mathbb{N}
let
k_{l}
be a natural number such that
n_{k_{l}}>l+R
. Of course such chosen sequence of natural numbers
n_{k_{l}}
converges to infinity. It remains to show that a compact set
\gamma_{n_{k_{l}}}K
separates the point
e_{l}
from all but finitely many
e_{n}
in
\overline{E}
.

Since
d_{\widetilde{X}}(c_{l},c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
, then
d_{\widetilde{X}}(\alpha(c_{l}),c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
and
d_{\widetilde{X}}(\omega(c_{l}),c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
. Moreover for any
n>n_{k_{l}}+R
we also have
d_{\widetilde{X}}(c_{n},c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
, then in particular
d_{\widetilde{X}}(\alpha(c_{n}),c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
and
d_{\widetilde{X}}(\omega(c_{n}),c_{n_{k_{l}}})>R
. In particular, any point of form
\delta e_{0}
where
\delta
belongs to the union of cosets
\alpha(c_{l})\cup\omega(c_{l})
is separated from any point of form
\delta^{\prime}e_{0}
where
\delta^{\prime}
belongs to the union of cosets
\alpha(c_{n})\cup\omega(c_{n})
. By remark
2.1.5

\varphi(c_{l})\in c_{l}\subseteq\alpha(c_{l})\cup\omega(c_{l})
and
\varphi(c_{n})\in c_{n}\subseteq\alpha(c_{n})\cup\omega(c_{n})
, then
\gamma_{n_{k_{l}}}K
separates
e_{l}
from all but finitely many
e_{n}
. By Lemma
3.3.16
the sequence
e_{n}
converges.
∎

Moreover it turns out that the limit of a sequence associated to the branch does only depend on the branch itself. This observation is formulated in a lemma below.

Lemma 4.1.5

The limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
as in the previous lemma does not depend on the initial choice of the function
\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
be any functions as in Definition
4.1.1
, and let
D
be a constant from Fact
4.1.2
. Then we can take a set
K_{n}=\varphi(c_{n})B_{S}(1,D)e_{0}
. By this definition and Fact
4.1.2
we have that
\varphi(c_{n})e_{0},\varphi^{\prime}(c_{n})e_{0}\in K_{n}
. The sets
K_{n}
are all
\Gamma
-translates of a finite (and thus compact) set
K_{0}=B_{S}(1,D)e_{0}
. Since the family of all
\Gamma
-translates of
K_{0}
is null by the definition of an
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure, the limits of the sequences
\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
and
\varphi^{\prime}(c_{n})e_{0}
have to be equal.
∎

The two previous lemmas motivate our definition of a branch point.

Definition 4.1.6

We say that a point
z\in Z
is an

if it can be expressed as a limit

z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})\cdot e_{0}

for some branch
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
in
\widetilde{X}
.

Note that in the definition above we can require without loss of generality that all involved branches start at a fixed base vertex of the Bass-Serre tree. This does not affect the set of branch points since for any vertex
\gamma G_{v}
and any branch
c
in
\widetilde{X}
there exists a branch
c^{\prime}
starting at
\gamma G_{v}
which is cofinal with
c
(i.e. coincides with
c

except at finitely many initial edges).

It turns out that for any two different branches starting at the same vertex of
\widetilde{X}
their associated points in
Z
have to be different.

Lemma 4.1.7

Suppose that
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots),c^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},\ldots)
are two different branches in a Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
starting at the same base vertex
\gamma_{0}G_{v_{0}}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}\neq\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n}^{\prime})e_{0}.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
c_{m}\neq c_{m}^{\prime}
. Then for any
n,n^{\prime}>m
the final vertices
\omega(c_{n})
and
\omega(c_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime})
of the corresponding oriented edges
lie in distinct connected components of the graph obtained from
\widetilde{X}

by deleting the interior of the edge
|c_{m}|
.
Moreover, by the construction of the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
,
we know that if
c=\gamma G_{y}
,
\omega(c)=\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
and
\alpha(c)=\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{v^{\prime\prime}}
,
then
\gamma G_{y}\subseteq\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}\cup\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{v^{\prime\prime}}
(compare Remark
2.1.5
), and hence
\varphi(c)\in\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}\cup\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{v^{\prime\prime}}
.
Combining the observations from the last two sentences, and applying
Lemma
3.4.18
, we get that there exists a compact set
K\subseteq E
which separates all but finitely many elements from the sequence
\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
from all but finitely many elements from the sequence
\varphi(c_{n}^{\prime})e_{0}
. Therefore, by Corollary
3.3.14
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}\neq\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n}^{\prime})e_{0}.

∎

We now turn to describing the
vertex points
of
Z
.

Definition 4.1.8

Let
G_{v}
be a vertex group of
\mathcal{G}
. We say that a point
z\in Z
is a
G_{v}
-vertex point
if it can be expressed as a limit

z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}\cdot e_{0},

where
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
is a sequence of group elements from some coset
\gamma G_{v}
. We also abbreviate and say that
z
is a
vertex point
if it is a
G_{v}
-vertex point for some vertex
v\in V_{Y}
.

Our aim now is to show that
Z
splits as the disjoint union of the sets of its branch points and its vertex points.
This "classification" of the points of
Z
will be justified by next three observations.

Lemma 4.1.9

The sets of all vertex points in
Z
and all branch points in
Z
are disjoint.

Proof.

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
be an branch in
\widetilde{X}
and let
\gamma_{n}
be a sequence of elements in a coset
\gamma G_{v}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}=z

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}e_{0}=z^{\prime},

we will show that
z\neq z^{\prime}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\alpha(c_{n})
is the closest vertex to
\gamma G_{v}
in
\widetilde{X}
and let
\widetilde{X}_{0}
denote the connected component of
\widetilde{X}
with the interior of
c_{n}
removed containing
\omega(c_{n})
. Then, since
\widetilde{X}
is a tree, for all
m>n
vertices
\alpha(c_{m})
and
\omega(c_{m})
are contained in
\widetilde{X}_{0}
. Note that by Remark
2.1.5
this consequently means that
\varphi(c_{m})\in\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. If
c_{n}=\gamma^{\prime}G_{y}
, then by Lemma
3.4.18
we know that there exist a compact set
K\subseteq E
such that
\gamma^{\prime}K
is separating all but finitely many points of form
\varphi(c_{m})e_{0}
from all but finitely many points
\gamma_{n}e_{0}
. In particular by using Corollary
3.3.14
we conclude that

z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}\neq\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}e_{0}=z^{\prime},

which ends the proof.
∎

To complete our classification of the points of
Z
we will need the following auxiliary technical fact.

Fact 4.1.10

Let
z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}e_{0}
be a point in
Z
. If there exists a positive constant
r
such that
d_{S}(\gamma G_{v},\gamma_{n})\leqslant r
for all
n
, then
z
is a
G_{v}
-vertex point.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq E
be a compact set such that
\Gamma K=E
and
e_{0}\in K
.
Let
\overline{B}_{d_{S}}(1,r)\subseteq\Gamma
be the ball around the unit
1\in\Gamma
,
with respect to the word metric
d_{S}
.
Put
K^{\prime}=\overline{B}_{d_{S}}(1,r)K
, and note that it is a compact set containing
K

(and thus also containing
e_{0}
).
Furthermore, for each
n
pick
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\in\gamma G_{v}
such that
d_{S}(\gamma_{n},\gamma_{n}^{\prime})\leqslant r
.
Then for each
n
we have
\gamma_{n}e_{0}\in\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\overline{B}_{d_{S}}(1,r)e_{0}\subseteq\gamma_{n}^{\prime}K^{\prime}
. It follows that for any
n
both
\gamma_{n}e_{0}\in\gamma_{n}^{\prime}K^{\prime}
and

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}e_{0}\in\gamma_{n}^{\prime}K^{\prime}
.
Since the diameters of
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}K^{\prime}
converge to 0, we get that

\lim\gamma_{n}^{\prime}e_{0}=z
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1.11

Each point
z\in Z
is a branch point or a
G_{v}
-vertex point for some vertex group
G_{v}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
z
is any point in
Z
. Let
e_{0}\in K
be any fixed base point, then

can be expressed as a limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}e_{0}
. Let now

be a compact set and

a constant as in the assertion of Lemma

.

Fix any

and define a function
\psi_{y}:\Gamma\to O_{\widetilde{X}}
as
\psi_{y}(\gamma)=\gamma G_{y}
. Consider the minimal subtree
\Upsilon
of
\widetilde{X}
containing all of the edges
\psi_{y}(\gamma_{n})
for
n\geqslant 1
. Since the sequence
\gamma_{n}
has infinitely many distinct values and the function
\psi_{y}
is finite-to-one, we know that
\Upsilon
contains infinitely many edges and therefore it is infinite. By Kőnig’s lemma, either there is a vertex of infinite degree in
\Upsilon

\Upsilon
contains a branch.

Suppose first that
\gamma G_{v}
is a vertex of
\Upsilon
with infinite degree. Then there is a sequence
\gamma g_{n}G_{y_{n}}
of pairwise distinct edges in

such that
\omega(\gamma g_{n}G_{y_{n}})=\gamma G_{v}

Without loss of generality, by referring to compactness of

, we can assume that the sequence

is chosen in such a way that
\gamma g_{n}e_{0}
is convergent, and by properness of the
\Gamma
-action on

, this sequence converges then to some point

.
By the definition of a final vertex of an edge in a Bass-Serre tree, we know that for each

we have either
g_{n}\in G_{v}
or
g_{n}=g_{n}^{\prime}s_{y_{n}}^{-1}
for

. Either way this means that

d_{S}(\gamma G_{v},\gamma g_{n})\leqslant 1.

[formula]

Therefore, by Fact
4.1.10
, the point

is a
G_{v}
-vertex point.
We will show that

is also a
G_{v}
-vertex point in this case.

Let
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
be such a subsequence of
\gamma_{n}
that
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}G_{y}

as the interior of
\gamma g_{n}G_{y_{n}}
.
Consider the subcase

which for infinitely many

we have

\gamma_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\in N_{R_{0}}(\gamma g_{n_{k}}G_{y_{n_{k}}})
. Then, by the fact that

, we get that

d_{S}(\gamma_{n}^{\prime},\gamma g_{n})\leqslant R_{0}+\diam(G_{y_{n}})\leqslant R_{0}+\max[\diam(G_{y}):y\in O_{Y}]

In view of the latter, forming an increasing sequence

and applying again Fact

we get that

is a

-vertex point.

Now, if we are not in the subcase considered above,

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\notin N_{R_{0}}(\gamma g_{n}G_{y_{n}})
.
In this subcase we rely on the observation that the interior of the edge

separates in

at least one vertex, say

of the edge
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}G_{y}

\gamma_{m}^{\prime}G_{y}
where

By construction of the Bass-Serre tree, we know that

and that

It follows then from Lemma

that the set

from all but finitely many of the points

for

(here we use the fact that

This means however that the sequences

from the statement of Lemma 3.16)
and

which again proves that

is a
G_{v}
-vertex point.

Suppose now, that there is a branch

in

\Upsilon
, and let

. By Lemma

converges then to some point

be as in the assertion of Lemma

,

of

(1) the edge

4.2
Some topological properties of
\mathbf{Z}

In this subsection, apart from keeping the standing assumptions for all of Section
4
, we assume additionally that graphs of groups under consideration are non-elementary (as in Definition
2.1.7
). We aim to prove two lemmas regarding the topology of

(Lemmas

Lemma 4.2.13

If
v\in V_{Y}
is a vertex such that the group
G_{v}
is infinite, then there exists an edge
y\in O_{Y}
with
\alpha(y)=v
such that for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, removing the interior of the edge
\gamma G_{y}
splits the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
into two trees of infinite diameter.

Proof.

By Lemma 4.1.6 (1) in
[
13
]
, there exist an edge
y
with
\alpha(y)=v
any lift of which separates
\widetilde{X}
into two subtrees, each containing lifts of all vertices of
Y
. We will show that each of those subtrees has infinite diameter. For that, let us fix a lift
\gamma G_{y}
of

. Suppose on the contrary that one of those trees, say
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, has finite diameter, and let
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v}
be a lift of

contained in
\widetilde{X}_{0}
for which
d_{\widetilde{X}}(\gamma G_{y},\gamma^{\prime}G_{v})
is maximal. Then, since

is infinite and

is finite, there exist infinitely many
\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma
such that
\alpha(\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y})=\gamma^{\prime}G_{v}
. In particular we can take
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
as above
such that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y}
is not contained in the geodesic between
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v}
and
\alpha(\gamma G_{y})
.
Let
\widetilde{X}^{\prime\prime}_{1}
be the connected component of

\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{int}(\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y})
not containing
the edge
\gamma G_{y}
.
Then
\widetilde{X}^{\prime\prime}_{1}
is contained in
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, and any vertex of
\widetilde{X}^{\prime\prime}_{1}
lies at distance at least

from

. Moreover, by referring again to Lemma 4.1.6 (1) from
[

]
,
\widetilde{X}^{\prime\prime}_{1}
contains at least one lift of

, and thus we have found a lift of

in

that lies further from

than

∎

Lemma 4.2.14

The set of all branch points is dense in
Z
.

Proof.

By the classification of points in
Z
given in Corollary
4.1.12
, it is enough show a sequence of branch points converging to any vertex point. Let
z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma g_{n}e_{0}
(where
g_{n}\in G_{v}
) be a vertex point. In that case group
G_{v}
has to be infinite, let

be an edge such that
\omega(y)=v
and any lift of

splits
\widetilde{X}
into two trees with infinite diameters (existence of such

is assured by the previous lemma). Now, if
y\in A\setminus O_{T}
we define
g_{n}^{\prime}=g_{n}s_{y}
, and otherwise define

. Note that then the final vertex of each edge of form
\gamma g_{n}^{\prime}G_{y}
is
\gamma G_{v}
. Since the sequence
g_{n}
has infinitely many distinct values, the same holds for
g_{n}^{\prime}
. Combining this with the fact that each coset of

is finite, we can without loss of generality (by passing to a subsequence) assume that
\gamma g_{n}^{\prime}G_{y}\neq\gamma g_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime}G_{y}
for

. For each

, let
\widetilde{X}^{(n)}
be the component of
\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{int}(\gamma g_{n}^{\prime}G_{y})

, let

and let

be the corresponding branch point. Note that, since

.

Lemma 4.2.15

Let
\gamma G_{y}
be an edge in the Bass-Serre tree

be the two connected components of

as subsets of

.

Firstly let us define an auxiliary set

is closed in

is also open it suffices to prove that

are disjoint. Let

and

be any two points. Then we can express them as limits

4.3
Finite vertex groups

In this subsection we study the
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary of a fundamental group of graph of groups when all of the vertex and edge groups are finite.
We show that in this case the
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary
can be identified with the boundary
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}

of the corresponding Bass-Serre tree.
The corresponding concept of the boundary of a tree
is understood here in the standard sense, recalled inside the proof of the corresponding result, i.e. Lemma
4.3.16
.
As a consequence of this result, we deduce that any
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary of a virtually free group is homeomorphic to the Cantor space
\mathcal{C}
(see Corollary
4.3.17
).
Even though this last result seems to belong to folklore, we include its proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.3.16

Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},Y)
with all vertex and edge groups finite. Then any
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary of
\Gamma
is canonically homeomorphic to the boundary
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}
of the corresponding Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{0}G_{v_{0}}
be a fixed basepoint in a Bass-Serre tree and let
(\overline{E},Z)
be any
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure for
\Gamma
. Since each vertex group
G_{v}
is finite, then by Lemma
4.1.9
there aren’t any vertex points in
Z
. Therefore each point in
Z
is represented as a limit
\lim\varphi(c_{n})
, where
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
is a branch starting from
\gamma_{0}G_{v_{0}}
. On the other hand, each point in the boundary
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}
of the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
also corresponds to a branch starting at our fixed base point
\gamma_{0}G_{v_{0}}
. We will show that the map
\Phi\colon\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}\to Z
given by

c\mapsto\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})

is a homeomorphism. Since both spaces
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}
and
Z
are compact it is enough to show that
\Phi
is a bijective open map.

Since every point in
Z
is of the form
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})
for some branch
c_{n}
then we know that
\Phi
is a surjection. Moreover, by Lemma
4.1.7
, the function
\Phi
is also an injection. Finally to show that
\Phi
is an open map it is enough to check that any image of a base open set from
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}
is also open in
Z
. The base open sets in
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}
are sets of form

U_{\gamma G_{y}}=\left\{c\in\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}:\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\ c_{n}=\gamma G_{y}\right\}.

We will show that
\Phi(U_{\gamma G_{y}})=\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
, where
\widetilde{X}_{0}
is a connected component of
\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{\rm int}(\gamma G_{y})
not containing
\gamma_{0}G_{v_{0}}
. Of course all but finitely many edges of each of the branches
c\in U_{\gamma G_{y}}
lie in the subgraph
\widetilde{X}_{0}
, thus for each of those edges
c_{n}
we have (by referring to Remark
2.1.5
)

\varphi(c_{n})\in\alpha(c_{n})\cup\omega(c_{n})\subseteq\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}.

Therefore in particular

\Phi(c)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})\in\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}},

which proves that
\Phi(U_{\gamma G_{y}})\subseteq\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. Conversely, let
z\in\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
be any point. Any point in
Z
is a branch point, therefore we can define
z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})
and suppose on the contrary that
c\not\in U_{\gamma G_{y}}
. Then the branch
c
do not contain edge
\gamma G_{y}
, therefore
c
lies in
\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{X}_{0}
. From this we can conclude that
\varphi(c_{n})\in\alpha(c_{n})\cup\omega(c_{n})\in\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{X}_{0}
By Lemma
3.4.18
for any fixed base point
e_{0}\in E
there exists a compact set
K\subseteq E
such that
\gamma K
separates all but finitely many elements of form
\delta e_{0}
from all but finitely many elements of form
\delta^{\prime}e_{0}
, where
\delta\in\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
and
\delta^{\prime}\in\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. In particular, if
e_{n}
is any sequence of elements from the set
(\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}})e_{0}
that converges to a point of
Z
, then all but finitely many elements of
e_{n}
are separated in
E
from all but finitely many elements
\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
by the set
\gamma K
, and thus, by Corollary
3.3.14
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}\not\in\Lambda(\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}).

This contradiction proves that
\Phi(U_{\gamma G_{y}})\supseteq\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
, which consequently means that
\Phi(U_{\gamma G_{y}})=\Lambda\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. By Lemma
4.2.15
, this means that
\Phi(U_{\gamma G_{y}})
is open, which proves that
\Phi
is an open map.
∎

By referring to Lemma
2.1.8
(ii), we can further analyse the case of non-elementary graphs of groups with finite vertex and edge groups. In that case, there is a vertex
v
in a graph of groups such that any lift of
v
splits
\widetilde{X}
into at least
3
infinite components. Moreover, any such infinite component necessarily contains another lift of a vertex
v
. In particular, in that case our Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
is easily seen to be locally finite and perfect (where by the latter we mean that any branch in
\widetilde{X}
shares arbitrarily large initial parts with other branches), therefore its boundary is a Cantor set.

Corollary 4.3.17

Let
(\mathcal{G},Y)
be a non-elementary graph of groups with finite vertex and edge groups, and let
\Gamma
be its fundamental group. Then any
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary of
\Gamma
is homeomorphic to the Cantor space
\mathcal{C}
.

5
Proofs of the main results (Theorems A and B)

In this section we present proofs of Theorems A and B of the introduction.

Proof of Theorem A

Firstly, note that for the case where all of the vertex groups are finite, by Corollary
4.3.17
we know that
Z\cong\mathcal{C}\cong\damalgam(\emptyset)
. Therefore it is enough to consider the case where at least one of the vertex groups is infinite; in that case by Remark
2.3.14
we have

\damalgam_{v\in V_{Y}}\Lambda G_{v}\cong\damalgam_{v\in V_{Y}^{+}}\Lambda G_{v}

where
V_{Y}^{+}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V_{Y}:\Lambda G_{v}\neq\emptyset\right\}=\left\{v\in V_{Y}:G_{v}\text{ is infinite}\right\}
.

For each vertex
v\in V_{Y}^{+}
we define a countable family
\mathcal{W}_{v}=\left\{\Lambda\gamma G_{v}:\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}
. Now put
\mathcal{W}=\bigsqcup_{v\in V_{Y}^{+}}\mathcal{W}_{v}
. We will show that so described families
\mathcal{W}_{v}
and
\mathcal{W}
satisfy conditions (a1)-(a5) from the definition of the dense amalgam (Definition
2.3.13
).

(a1)

First, observe that any
W\in\mathcal{W}_{v}
is homeomorphic to
\Lambda G_{v}
. Indeed, if
W=\Lambda\gamma G_{v}
then we have

\Lambda G_{v}\cong\gamma\Lambda G_{v}=\Lambda\gamma G_{v},

where the homeomorphism above is a consequence of the fact that
\Gamma
acts on
Z
by homeomorphisms, while the equality above is a consequence of the fact that the translation by
\gamma
is continuous on the entire
\overline{E}
. It remains to show, that any two distinct
W,W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
are disjoint. Let
w\in W=\Lambda\gamma G_{v}
and
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}=\Lambda\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
and let
\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y}
be an edge on the path
in the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}

joining
\gamma G_{v}
and
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
. By Fact
3.4.19
, there exists a compact set
K
such that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}K
separates all but finitely many points from
(\gamma G_{v})e_{0}
from all but finitely many points from
(\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}})e_{0}
. Therefore, by Corollary
3.3.14
, we have
w\neq w^{\prime}
, which proves disjointness of
W
and
W^{\prime}
.

(a2)

To prove nullness of the family
\mathcal{W}
, it is obviously sufficient
to show nullness of
\mathcal{W}_{v}
for each
v\in V_{Y}^{+}
.
Fix
v\in V_{Y}^{+}
and suppose that the family

\mathcal{W}_{v}=\{\Lambda\gamma G_{v}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}

is not null. Then there is
D>0
and a sequence of pairwise distinct
cosets
\gamma_{n}G_{v}
such that
\diam(\Lambda\gamma_{n}G_{v})>D

for all
n
. By passing to a subsequence, we can then find two sequences
of elements
z_{n}
and
z_{n}^{\prime}
, with
z_{n},z_{n}^{\prime}\in\Lambda\gamma_{n}G_{v}

for all
n
, which converge to distinct points
z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}\in Z
, respectively.

Now, viewing the cosets
\gamma_{n}G_{v}
as vertices in the Bass-Serre
tree
\widetilde{X}
of
(\mathcal{G},Y)
, by applying Kőnig’s lemma and passing to a subsequence,
we can assume that either the vertices
\gamma_{n}G_{v}
lie in a monotonic
order on some branch in
\widetilde{X}
, or there is a vertex
v_{0}
of

\widetilde{X}
such that all
\gamma_{n}G_{v}
are distinct from
v_{0}

and the edges adjacent to
v_{0}
on the paths
[v_{0},\gamma_{n}G_{v}]

in
\widetilde{X}
are pairwise distinct. In either case,
for each
n
there is an edge
\varepsilon_{n}
of
\widetilde{X}

which separates
\gamma_{n}G_{v}
from all
\gamma_{k}G_{v}
with
k>n
,
and the edges
\varepsilon_{n}
are pairwise distinct.
For each
n
, fix
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}=\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}G_{y_{n}^{\prime\prime}}

(where
y_{n}^{\prime\prime}
is the appropriate edge of
Y
).
By finiteness of
Y
, we can assume by passing to a subsequence that
all
y_{n}^{\prime\prime}
coincide, and hence
the elements
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}
are pairwise distinct.

Claim.

There is a compact
K\subseteq E
such that for each
n
the
translate
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}K
separates
z_{n}
from all
z_{k}
with
k>n

(and at the same time separates
z_{n}^{\prime}
from all
z_{k}^{\prime}
with
k>n
).

To prove the claim, let
e_{0}
and
K
be as in Lemma

3.4.18
.
It follows then from
Fact
3.4.19
that for each
n
and any
k>n
,
the set
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}K

separates all but finitely many points from the set
(\gamma_{n}G_{v})e_{0}

from all but finitely many points from the set
(\gamma_{k}G_{v})e_{0}
.
Since
z_{n}
is the limit of a sequence of points from
(\gamma_{n}G_{v})e_{0}
,
and similarly
z_{k}
is the limit of a sequence of points from

(\gamma_{k}G_{v})e_{0}
, we deduce from Corollary

3.3.14
that
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}K

separates
z_{n}
from
z_{k}
, as required. The same argument shows
also that
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}K

separates
z_{n}^{\prime}
from
z_{k}^{\prime}
, hence the claim.

Without loss of generality, we assume now that the sequence

\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}
converges to a point
z\in Z
.
In view of Lemma

3.3.16
, convergence of
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}
to
z

together with the claim above imply that
\lim z_{n}=z
,
and similarly that
\lim z_{n}^{\prime}=z
.
The equality
z_{0}=z_{0}^{\prime}
resulting from these observations
contradicts an earlier
assumption that
z_{0}\neq z_{0}^{\prime}
.
This concludes the verification of condition (a2).

(a3)

Let
W\in\mathcal{W}
be any set from the family
\mathcal{W}
. Since
W
is defined as the limit set of a coset
\gamma G_{v}
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
and some
v\in V_{Y}
, therefore all of the points in
W
are
G_{v}
-vertex points. From Lemma
4.1.9
we know that none of the points from
W
is a branch point, and from Lemma
4.2.14
we know that there is a sequence of branch points convergent to any
w\in W
. Thus
W
is a boundary subset of
Z
.

(a4)

Firstly, for any branch point
z\in Z
, we will find a sequence of points from
\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}
convergent to
z
. Let
z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
(where
\varphi
is a function from Definition
4.1.1
) and for any
n
let
z_{n}
be a point from the limit set
\Lambda\varphi(c_{n})G_{v}
. By Fact
3.2.9
, we know that the distance between the vertex
\varphi(c_{n})G_{v}
and the edge
c_{n}
in
\widetilde{X}
is at most
\left|\left|O\right|_{Y}\right|
. In particular, the edge
c_{n+\left|\left|O\right|_{Y}\right|+1}
separates the vertex
\varphi(c_{n})G_{v}
from any vertex
\varphi(c_{m})G_{v}
, where
m\geqslant n+2\left|\left|O\right|_{Y}\right|+2
. From Fact
3.4.19
we know that there exist a compact set
K\subseteq E
such that
\varphi(c_{n+\left|\left|O\right|_{Y}\right|+1})K
separates all but finitely many points
\delta e_{0}
where
\delta\in\varphi(c_{n})G_{v}
from all but finitely many points
\delta^{\prime}e_{0}
where
\delta^{\prime}\in\varphi(c_{m})G_{v}
. Since both points
z_{n}
and
z_{m}
can be described as limits of points from
\varphi(c_{n})G_{v}e_{0}
and
\varphi(c_{m})G_{v}e_{0}
respectively, then by Corollary
3.3.14
the compact set
\varphi(c_{n+\left|\left|O\right|_{Y}\right|+1})K
also separates
z_{n}
from
z_{m}
in
\overline{E}
. It follows that the sequences
\varphi(c_{n})
and
z_{n}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
3.3.16
, so therefore
\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=z
.

From the above argument we know that each of the branch points can be obtained as a limit of points from
\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}
, therefore the set of all branch points is contained in the closure of
\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}
. Since the set of all branch points is dense in
Z

(see Lemma
4.2.14
), we conclude that

\text{cl}_{Z}\left(\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}\right)=\text{cl}_{Z}\left(\text{cl}_{Z}\left(\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}\right)\right)\supseteq\text{cl}_{Z}\left(\left\{\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}:c_{n}\text{ is a branch in }\widetilde{X}\right\}\right)=Z,

therefore
\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{v}
is dense in
Z
.

(a5)

Let
z,z^{\prime}\in Z
be two points that do not belong to the same subset
W\in\mathcal{W}
, and let
K\subseteq E
be the compact set from the assertion of Lemma
3.4.18
. We need to define a
\mathcal{W}
-saturated clopen set
H
which separates
z
from
z^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.1.11
, it suffices to consider
3
cases regarding whether those points are vertex points or branch points, and we will indicate the appropriate set
H
separately in each of those cases.

Suppose first that
z
is a
G_{v}
-point and
z^{\prime}
is a
G_{v^{\prime}}
-point. Let
\gamma G_{v}
and
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
be the cosets of
G_{v},G_{v^{\prime}}
such that
z\in\Lambda\gamma G_{v}
and
z^{\prime}\in\Lambda\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
. By the assumption that
z,z^{\prime}
do not belong to the same set
W\in\mathcal{W}
we know that
\gamma G_{v}\neq\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
. Thus, those cosets correspond to different vertices in the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}
, so let
\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}}
be an edge of
\widetilde{X}
lying on the unique geodesic path joining
\gamma G_{v}
and
\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
. By removing the interior of the edge
\gamma^{\prime\prime}G_{y^{\prime\prime}}
from
\widetilde{X}
we get two connected subgraphs
\widetilde{X}_{0},\widetilde{X}_{1}
(we put
\widetilde{X}_{0}
to be the one of those subgraphs containing
\gamma G_{v}
). Let now
M
be the union of all cosets
\delta G_{v}\in V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
(i.e. cosets representing the vertices of
\widetilde{X}_{0}
). We put
H=\Lambda M
, and we note that
\gamma G_{v}\subseteq H
, and thus
z\in H
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.4.18
the set
\gamma^{\prime\prime}K
separates all but finitely many points of form
\delta^{\prime}e_{0}
where
\delta^{\prime}\in\gamma^{\prime}G_{v^{\prime}}
from all but finitely many points of form
\delta e_{0}
where
\delta\in M
. Therefore, by Corollary
3.3.14
, we get that
z^{\prime}\not\in\Lambda M=H
.

Suppose now that one of the points from the pair
\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}
is a
G_{v}
-vertex point and the other is a branch point. Without loss of generality, assume that
z^{\prime}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
for some branch
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
, and that
z\in\Lambda\gamma G_{v}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\omega(c_{n})
and
\gamma G_{v}
are contained in different connected components of
\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{int}(c_{n})
. Let
\widetilde{X}_{0},\widetilde{X}_{1}
be those connected components (again, we assume that
\widetilde{X}_{0}
is the one component containing the vertex
\gamma G_{v}
) and let
M=\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. Like in the previous case we put
H=\Lambda M
. The same argument as in the previous case proves that
z\in H
; it is left to show that
z^{\prime}\not\in H
. By definition we know that all of the edges
c_{m}
where
m>n
belong to the subgraph
\widetilde{X}_{1}
. By Remark
2.1.5
we know that
\varphi(c_{m})\in\alpha(c_{m})\cup\omega(c_{m})
, where vertices
\alpha(c_{m}),\omega(c_{m})
are treated as their underlying cosets in
\Gamma
. Of course sequence
\varphi(c_{m})
has infinitely many distinct values. Applying Lemma
3.4.18
to the previous observations we conclude that the set
\varphi(c_{n})K
separates all but finitely many points
\varphi(c_{m})e_{0}
from all but finitely many points from the set
Me_{0}
. In particular, by Corollary
3.3.14
, we conclude that
z^{\prime}\not\in H
.

Suppose finally that
b=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)
and
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
are some branches in
\widetilde{X}
starting at the same base vertex, and let
z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(b_{n})e_{0}
,
z^{\prime}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(c_{n})e_{0}
be the corresponding branch points. Since
z\neq z^{\prime}
, we have
b\neq c
, so we can choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that the entire path
b
is contained in a different connected component of
\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{int}(c_{n})
than
\omega(c_{n})
. Let
\widetilde{X}_{0},\widetilde{X}_{1}
be the connected components of
\widetilde{X}\setminus\hbox{int}(c_{n})
(with
\widetilde{X}_{0}
being the component containing vertex
\alpha(c_{n})
), and let
M=\bigcup V_{\widetilde{X}_{0}}
. We put
H=\Lambda M
. Note that for every
m>n
an edge
b_{m}
is contained in
\widetilde{X}_{0}
and that by Remark
2.1.5

\varphi(b_{m})\in\alpha(b_{m})\cup\omega(b_{m})\subseteq M
. Therefore
z=\lim_{m\to\infty}\varphi(b_{m})e_{0}\in\Lambda M=H
. Moreover, the argument as in the previous case shows that

.

By Lemma
4.2.15
, in all of the above three cases the indicated set

is a
\mathcal{W}
-saturated clopen, and this concludes the verification of condition (a5).

This completes also the whole proof of Theorem A. ∎

We now pass to the proof of our second main result.

Proof of Theorem B

By Freudenthal-Hopf theorem,
\Gamma
is 0-ended, 1-ended, 2-ended or

\infty
-ended. If
\Gamma
is 0-ended, it is obviously finite, and then its
any
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundary
Z
is empty.
If
\Gamma
is 1-ended then
Z
is connected by
Lemma
2.2.12
. If
\Gamma
is 2-ended, it is virtually infinite cyclic
(see e.g. Thorem 3.1 in
[
14
]
), and hence it is the fundamental group
of a graph of groups of finite order (compare Theorem 3.5 in
[
14
]
).
The Bass-Serre tree of this
graph of groups
is then 2-ended as well (because
\Gamma

acts on it geometrically, and because the number of ends is a quasi-isometry invariant of proper geodesic spaces). It follows from Lemma
4.3.16

that
Z
is then a doubleton.
Finally, if
\Gamma
is
\infty
-ended, we consider its Dunwoody-Stallings
decomposition as the fundamental group of a graph of groups

\mathcal{G}

with finite edge groups and with vertex groups that are either finite
or finitely generated and 1-ended (existence of such a decomposition has been justified by the accessibility result of M. Dunwoody
[
6
]
).

We claim that this graph of groups is then non-elementary.
To see this, suppose a contrario that some sequence of successive elementary collapses applied to
\mathcal{G}
converts it into
a new graph of groups
(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
which has one of the three
elementary forms listed in Definition
2.1.7
.
Observe that the fundamental group of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
still coincides with
\Gamma
, and that the families of the vertex groups and the edge groups of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are contained in the corresponding
families for
\mathcal{G}
, so that the edge groups of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

are still finite, and each vertex group of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is either finite or 1-ended. Now, if the underlying graph
Y^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
coincides with a single vertex, the corresponding vertex group has to coincide with
\Gamma
, which is infinitely-ended, contradicting its finiteness or 1-endedness. If
Y^{\prime}
has a single edge (either loop or non-loop), the conditions from Definition
2.1.7
easily imply that the Bass-Serre tree
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
coincides then with an infinite line, and all vertex groups of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are finite. It means that
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
acts on a line, properly and cocompactly, and this implies that
\Gamma
has 2 ends,
contradicting its assumed
\infty
-endedness. It follows that
\mathcal{G}
is indeed non-elemntary.

By Theorem A,
Z
is then homeomorphic to
the dense amalgam of the limit sets in
Z
of the vertex groups
of
\mathcal{G}
(viewed as subgroups of
\Gamma
). If all the vertex
groups are finite, Theorem A assures that
Z
is a Cantor set,
and it is thus homeomorphic to the dense amalgam of a single space
being a singleton. In the remaining case,
Z
is homeomorphic
to the dense amalgam of the limit sets of the infinite vertex groups
of
\mathcal{G}
, which are all 1-ended. Since by
Lemma
2.2.12

all these limit sets are connected, we get that in any case
Z

is the dense amalgam of a family of connected spaces.

These arguments together yield parts 1-3 of Theorem B,
which completes the proof. ∎

Remark 5.1

Note that Theorem B still holds true, with the same proof, if we assume that
instead of being finitely presented, the group
\Gamma
is finitely generated and accessible (where the latter means it can be raelized as the fundamental group
of a graph of groups whose all edge groups are finite, and the vertex groups are
either finite or 1-ended).

6
Final remarks and questions

The framework of
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundaries is very general and includes various commonly known frameworks,
such as Gromov boundaries, CAT(0) boundaries, and systolic boundaries.
Therefore our main results instantly translate into each of the just mentioned frameworks.

In order to fully understand the boundaries of infinitely ended groups in terms of their 1-ended factors
(for terminal splittings over finite subgroups), one needs to understand the limit sets of these factors
in the corresponding frameworks. In case of Gromov hyperbolic groups, all the factors have to be quasi-convex,
and Gromov
hyperbolic themeselves, so their limit sets (in the Gromov boundary of the whole group)
have to be homeomorphic with their own Gromov boundaries (compare Theorem 0.3 (2) in
[
13
]
).
One could ask whether that kind of observation is also feasible in other discussed frameworks.
We state the corresponding questions as the the question below.

Question 6.1

Suppose that
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},Y)
is a fundamental group of a graph of group with finite edge groups, and that it admits an
E\mathcal{Z}
-structure
(\overline{E},Z)
(respectively: CAT(0) structure, systolic structure).
Do the vertex groups also admit
E\mathcal{Z}
-structures (CAT(0) structure, systolic structure)?
If so, can their
E\mathcal{Z}
-structures (CAT(0) structures, systolic structures) be chosen in such a way
that the corresponding boundary of each such vertex group
G_{v}
is homeomorphic to its limit set
\Lambda G_{v}\subseteq Z
?

In view of Theorem 0.3 in
[
13
]
,
positive answers to both of the questions above (for a given type of boundary) would give a complete description of boundaries
of an infinitely ended group in terms of the boundaries
of its 1-ended factors.
On the other hand, negative answers would lead to further questions concerning possible forms of limits sets of groups appearing as vertex groups in the situations as above.

As a next comment, we note that one could try to generalise our Theorem A to the case where the edge groups in the discussed graphs of groups are allowed to be infinite.
In such a case, the boundary of the whole group
\pi_{1}\mathcal{G}

still contains copies of the limit sets
\Lambda G_{v}
of the vertex subgroups, but this time these copies overlap along
the subsets corresponding to the limit sets of the edge groups.
The whole picture thus becomes much more complicated.
Some example provided by M. Bestvina (Example 3.1 in
[
1
]
) shows that a graph of groups with quite
well behaved vertex groups and edge groups (where the latter are infinite) may have several significantly distinct and seemingly unrelated forms
of
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundaries.
We suspect that any reasonable generalisation of Theorem A
requires imposing some further restrictions on the structure of the considered graphs of groups.

Question 6.2

How can one generalize Theorem A to any appropriate cases where the edge groups of the considered graphs of groups are not necessarily finite?

Passing to our final remark, observe that Theorem B and Corollary
4.3.17
show some examples of groups with unique
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundaries (finite groups, virtually infinite cyclic groups and virtually free groups). Some other examples
include the Poincare duality groups of dimensions
n\leqslant 3
,
whose
E\mathcal{Z}
-boundaries are homeomorphic to
S^{n-1}
(see Theorem 2.8 and Remark 2.9 in
[

]
