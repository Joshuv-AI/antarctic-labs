What Is the System Frequency?

 What Is the System Frequency?
 • Frequency is the rate at which AC systems oscillate (50/60 Hz).
 • Frequency is one of the two heartbeats of a power system:
 ◦ Frequency
 ◦ Voltage.
 • Frequency in an interconnection is the same (Southern African Power Pool,
 Eastern African Power Pool, etc.).
 • An interconnection can contain a number of control areas.

Secondary Frequency Control

 Secondary Frequency Control
 • The response required to restore tie-lines to scheduled set-point values and system frequency to within acceptable
 range
 • Typically, centrally controlled
 • Can be automatic (via automatic generation control, AGC) or manual via system operator instruction.

Secondary Frequency Control (AGC and ACE)

 Secondary Frequency Control (AGC and ACE)
 • Automatic generation control (AGC) is the automated
 secondary frequency response tool available to SOs
 Simplified illustration of AGC control using scheduled/actual frequency (green)
 and interchange schedule/actual (red) to establish ACE
 ◦ Hold frequency at/close to nominal (50/60 Hz)
 ◦ Maintain scheduled interchanges
 ◦ Maintain unit generation optimally (system-wide).
 • AGC operates on a control signal called the area control
 error (ACE) for a control area and is a linear
 combination of:
 ◦ Deviation of frequency
 ◦ Deviation of tie-line flows.
 • ACE is distributed to resources through:
 ◦ Participation factors (splitting ACE signal to
 generators on AGC)
 ◦ “Raise/lower” pulses (~4 seconds).

 Control Strategies: Tertiary

Tertiary Frequency Control

 Tertiary Frequency Control
 • Quantity
 ◦ Required to restore primary and secondary response and to restore interconnectors back to scheduled values
 following large mismatches
 ◦ Typically manual response via SO instruction (sometimes automatic via under-frequency starting)
 ◦ Either quick start-up or quick ramp-up
 ◦ Longer time and thus not necessarily required to be synchronized
 ◦ Worst single contingency on own interconnected system.
 • Future
 ◦ With larger units or additional interconnectors carrying larger amounts of power, these may replace Kapichira
 as the largest unit contingency.
 • Activation and activation time
 ◦ Start-up times of existing thermal units and hydro units
 ◦ Slow and thus communications with thermal plants specifically to ascertain accurate start-up and synchronize
 times as well as ramp-rates to provide tertiary response should be verified.
