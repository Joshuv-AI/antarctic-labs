Search for new physics in B → K ​ π ​ π ​ γ B\to K\pi\pi\gamma with Belle II data
Sahil Saha, on behalf of the Belle II collaboration

Abstract
The measurement of the time-dependent CP -asymmetry of B 0 → K S 0 ​ π + ​ π − ​ γ B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma decays is sensitive to contributions from physics beyond the Standard Model. To translate this measurement into a constraint on new physics, it is necessary to distinguish between decay modes that contribute to the final state via a kaonic resonance that is a CP eigenstate, and those that proceed through non- CP eigenstates. This requires an amplitude analysis of the B → K r ​ e ​ s ​ γ → K ​ π + ​ π − ​ γ B\to K_{res}\gamma\to K\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma decay, that is discussed in this article.



1
Introduction

Radiative
b
decays, such as
b\to s\gamma
transitions, proceed through flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNC) forbidden at tree level in the Standard Model (SM). The FCNC process can only occur at first order through the penguin diagram displayed in Fig.
1.1
. This process is suppressed in the SM due to the Glashow–Iliopoulos–Maiani (GIM) mechanism. The possible contribution of a new heavy particle in the electroweak
loop would cause the process to deviate from the SM prediction.

Figure 1.1:
Feynman diagram for the
b\to s\gamma
transition.

The measurement of the
CP
-asymmetry
[
3
]
of
B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\rho^{0}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})\gamma
as a function of time between the
B
and
\bar{B}
decays -
t
and is expressed as:

\mathcal{A}_{{CP}}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\bar{B}^{0}(t)\to f_{{CP}}\;\gamma)-\Gamma(B^{0}(t)\to f_{{CP}}\;\gamma)}{\Gamma(\bar{B}^{0}(t)\to f_{{CP}}\;\gamma)+\Gamma(B^{0}(t)\to f_{{CP}}\;\gamma)}=\mathcal{S}_{{CP}}\sin(\Delta mt)-\mathcal{C}_{{CP}}\cos(\Delta mt).

(1.1)

Here,
f_{CP}
is the
CP
-eigenstate
K_{S}^{0}\rho^{0}
,
\Gamma
is the decay rate and
\Delta m
is the mass difference of the neutral
B
meson mass eigenstates.
\mathcal{S}_{CP}
and
\mathcal{C}_{CP}
are the mixing-induced and direct
CP
asymmetries respectively.
The presence of the non-
CP
eigenstates going to the same final state dilutes the
CP
contributions to the asymmetry. Therefore, an effective
CP
asymmetry -
\mathcal{S}_{B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma}
is defined and it is related with
\mathcal{S}_{CP}
with a dilution factor
[
3
]

\mathcal{D}
:

\mathcal{S}_{B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma}=\mathcal{D}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{CP}.

(1.2)

The effective
CP
parameter
\mathcal{S}_{B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma}=-0.29\pm 0.11\pm 0.05
was measured using Belle and Belle II data
[
1
]
.

In order to constrain new physics without knowing explicitly its field content, we can express the
b\to s\gamma
process in terms of an effective field theory, where the effective Hamiltonian for the process would be
[
6
]
:

\mathcal{H}_{eff}=-\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{tb}V_{ts^{*}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{8}C_{i}Q_{i}+C^{\prime}_{i}Q^{\prime}_{i}\right).

(1.3)

Here,
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
C_{i}/C_{i}^{\prime}
are the short-distance Wilson
coefficients, and
Q_{i}/Q_{i}^{\prime}
are the local long-distance operators.
The operators
Q_{7}
and
Q_{7}^{\prime}
describe left and right-handed chiral
structures of
b\to s\gamma
. In the SM,
C_{7}^{\prime}
arises from external
quark mass insertions, giving
C_{7}^{\prime}/C_{7}\sim m_{s}/m_{b}\approx 0.02
.
Thus,
b\to s\gamma
produces a predominantly left-handed photon
(right-handed for
\bar{b}\to\bar{s}\gamma
). The
CP
-asymmetry parameter
\mathcal{S}_{CP}
can be written as a function of the Wilson coefficients
[
3
]
-

\mathcal{S}_{CP}=-\frac{2\text{Im}(C_{7}C_{7}^{\prime})}{|C_{7}|^{2}+|C_{7}^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(1.4)

The dilution factor
\mathcal{D}
is a function of the decay amplitude
A(p1,p2,p3)
.

\mathcal{D}=-\frac{\int\text{Re}\left(A(p1,p2,p3)\cdot A(p2,p1,p3)\right)dp}{\int|A(p1,p2,p3)|^{2}dp},

(1.5)

where,
A(p1,p2,p3)=A\left(B^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}(p_{3})\pi^{+}(p_{1})\pi^{-}(p_{2})\gamma\right)
.This dilution factor can be measured from a complete amplitude analysis of the
B^{0}\to K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decay. In practice, we perform the amplitude analysis of the charged isospin partner
B^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decay (the charge-conjugated decay of
B^{-}
is also included assuming no direct CP violation), as it is statistically favourable.

2
Decay model

In this section, the model describing the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decay is outlined. This decay proceeds through three intermediate steps. Firstly, the
B^{+}\to K_{res}^{+}\gamma
step. Each possible
K_{res}^{+}
can be of the spin parity
J^{P}=1^{\pm},2^{\pm}
and interfere among each other. Then the
K_{res}^{+}
decays into
\rho^{0}K^{+}
,
K^{*0}\pi^{+}
or
(K\pi)_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}
which finally decay to the
K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
state. The list of intermediate paths to the
K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
final state are listed in Table
2.2
.

To account for the possible quantum interferences between all decay modes, a coherent sum of the amplitudes of each individual decay mode is necessary. The final probability is then:

P_{total}=|\sum_{i}c_{i}A_{i}|^{2},

(2.1)

where
c_{i}
and
A_{i}
are respectively the complex weights and amplitudes of each decay path. Thus, the amplitude for each intermediate path can be expressed as a product of three consecutive decays:

A_{i}=(S_{B}\cdot B_{L_{B}})\cdot(T_{K_{res}}\cdot S_{K_{res}}\cdot B_{L_{K_{res}}})\cdot(T_{X}\cdot{S_{X}}\cdot B_{L_{X}}),

(2.2)

where
S
is the spin-dependent part, dictating the angular distribution of the decay,
T
denotes the lineshapes of the
K_{res}
and of
X
which can be
\rho^{0},K^{*0}\text{ or }(K\pi)_{S}^{0}
. It must be noted that in our model, the contribution of
(K\pi)_{S}^{0}
is included in the decay of
K_{1}(1270)^{+}\to K^{*}(1430)^{0}\pi^{+}
.
The spin-dependent part of the amplitudes is computed with the covariant tensor formalism for the decay of
K_{res}\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, and the
B
decay expression includes radiative corrections
[
4
]
.
To estimate the contributions of each possible intermediate path from a fit to Belle II data, we describe the amplitude in terms of five observables: three invariant masses
m_{K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}},m_{K^{+}\pi^{-}}
and
m_{K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
, and two angles
\cos\theta
and
\phi_{1}
. The kinematics of the system and the definition of the cartesian frame is shown in Fig.
2.2
. The angles are defined in the
K_{res}
-rest frame with the photon along the
z
-axis while the
y
-axis is oriented towards
\vec{p}_{\pi^{+}}\times\vec{p}_{\pi^{-}}
.
\cos\theta
is the angle between the normal
\hat{n}
to the
K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
plane and the photon. (
\phi_{1/2}
) are the angles of the pions with respect to
\hat{x}^{\prime}=\hat{n}\times\hat{y}
. To describe the amplitude, either
\phi_{1/2}
or their mean
\phi
can be used.

3
Preliminary developments of the amplitude analysis

The complex weights described in Section
2
will be obtained by fitting our decay model to
B^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
candidates reconstructed and selected from the Belle II dataset, with the same criteria as in the published measurement of the time-dependent CP asymmetry of
B^{0}\to K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decays
[
1
]
.
Belle II data is collected from
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions produced by SuperKEKB at a center-of-mass energy of
10.58
GeV in order to produce the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance decaying into
B^{+}B^{-}
pairs.

Background-subtracted distributions of the five fit variables are obtained using the sPlot method
[
5
]
. The discriminating variable between signal and background is
\Delta E=\frac{2p_{B}p_{0}-s}{2\sqrt{s}}
, where
p_{B}
is the momentum of the
B
meson candidate,
p_{0}=p_{e^{+}}+p_{e^{-}}
is the sum of the momenta of the beam particles and
s
is the beam-energy. As an illustration, Fig.
3.1
shows the simulated
m_{K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
background-subtracted distribution corresponding to
1.4
ab
-1
of Belle II data.

Figure 3.1:
Simulated
m_{K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
distribution after background subtraction with the sPlot method.

The standard Monte-Carlo generator EvtGen
[
2
]
does not account for interferences between the different
K_{res}
modes and lacks the proper angular structure of the decay. Thus, we use AmpGen to generate the
B^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decay and process it through through the global Belle II simulation and reconstruction software.

Fig.
3.2
illustrates the amplitude fit result. The fit function is the decay model discussed in section 2 taking into account all possible intermediate path in this example. The magnitudes and phases of each intermediate mode are left as free parameters. Pole masses and widths of each resonance are fixed to the PDG average values. The result of the fit is all the amplitudes and phases of each intermediate mode, that will allow to compute the total amplitude and further the dilution factor
\mathcal{D}
using Eq.
1.5
.

4
Conclusions and prospects

The amplitude analysis formalism of
B^{+}\to K_{res}^{+}\gamma\to K^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\gamma
decays in Belle II was developed for the first time to describe all possible intermediate states. The next step will be to test the fit robustness and define the unbiased fit strategy, before performing the analysis of the Belle II data registered so far.
