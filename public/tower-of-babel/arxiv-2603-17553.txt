On dynamical semigroup for damped driven


Abstract
The article addresses the damped driven Jaynes–Cummings for quantised one-mode Maxwell field coupled to a two-level molecule. We consider a broad class of damping and pumping which are polynomial in the creation and annihilation operators. Our main result is the construction of a contraction dynamical semigroup in the Hilbert space of Hermitian Hilbert–Schmidt operators in the case of a nonpositive dissipation operator and time-independent pumping. All trajectories of the semigroup are generalised solutions to the Jaynes–Cummings equations. As a key example, we prove nonpositivity for the basic dissipation operator of Quantum Optics.

Denote

X=F\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
, where
F

endowed with
an orthonormal basis
|n\rangle
,
n=0,1,\dots
,

a

a^{\dagger}
:

a|n\rangle=\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle,\qquad a^{\dagger}|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle,\qquad[a,a^{\dagger}]=1.

(1.1)

We will consider a damped-driven version of the Jaynes–Cummings equations
(QRM)

\dot{\rho}(t)={\mathbb A}\rho(t):=-i[H,\rho(t)]+\gamma D\rho(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(1.2)

where the density operator
\rho(t)
of the coupled field-molecule system
is a Hermitian operator in
X
.
The Hamiltonian
H
is the sum

H=H_{0}+pV,\qquad\mbox{where}\quad H_{0}:=\omega_{c}a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{a}\sigma_{3},\quad V=(a+a^{\dagger})\otimes\sigma_{1}+A^{e}.

(1.3)

Here
H_{0}
is the Hamiltonian of the free field and a molecule without interaction,

pV
is the interaction Hamiltonian,

\omega_{c}>0
is the cavity resonance frequency,

\omega_{a}>0
is the molecular
frequency, and

p\in{\mathbb R}
is proportional to the molecular dipole moment.
The pumping is represented by a selfadjoint operator

A^{e}
, and

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
are the Pauli matrices
acting on the factor
{\mathbb C}^{2}
in
F\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
, so
[a,\sigma_{k}]=[a^{\dagger},\sigma_{k}]=0
.
Finally,
in (
1.2
),

\gamma>0
and
D
is a dissipation operator.
We will consider the operator

D_{1}\rho=a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a,

(1.4)

used in
[

]

]
,

,

,

,

,

]
,
and also its suitable modifications.

Definition 1.1
.

{\rm HS}
is the Hilbert space of Hermitian
Hilbert–Schmidt operators with the inner product
[
23
]

\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}\rho_{2}].

(1.5)

Definition 1.2
.

i)
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle=|n\rangle\otimes s_{\pm}
form an orthonormal basis in
X
,

s_{\pm}\in{\mathbb C}^{2}
and
\sigma_{3}s_{\pm}=\pm s_{\pm}
.

ii)
X_{\infty}
is the space
of finite linear combinations of the vectors
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle
.

iii)
{\cal D}\subset{\rm HS}
is the subspace of finite rank Hermitian operators

\rho=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}\rho_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|n,s\rangle\otimes\langle n^{\prime},s^{\prime}|.

(1.6)

Our main goal is to prove the well-posedness for the QRM
in the Hilbert space
{\rm HS}
in the case of time-independent
pumping
A^{e}
. The main issue is that
the operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
are unbounded by (
1.1
), so
the generator
{\mathbb A}
in QRM is also unbounded.
Accordingly, the meaning of the QRM must be adjusted (see Definition
2.3
).

The Hamilton operator
H
is selfadjoint, so,
in the case
\gamma=0
, solutions are given by

\rho(t)=e^{-iHt}\rho(0)e^{iHt},\qquad t\geq 0.

(1.7)

In this case, the trace
\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\!\rho(t)
is conserved, and
\rho(t)\geq 0
if
\rho(0)\geq 0
.
However, for
\gamma>0
the formula for solutions is missing.
We assume that

\bullet
The pumping
A^{e}
and
the dissipation operator
D

are polynomials (
2.14
) in
a
and
a^{\dagger}
.

\bullet
The dissipation operator
D
and its adjoint
D^{\dagger}
are nonpositive on
the dense domain
{\cal D}
:

\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\langle\rho,D^{\dagger}\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\rho\in{\cal D}.

(1.8)

For example,

A^{e}=a^{\dagger}+a
,
A^{e}=a^{\dagger}a
, and
D=D_{1}
fulfill the first assumption.
As a key example, we prove in Theorem
2.2
that
the dissipation operator
D_{1}
satisfies (
1.8
).

Our main results are as follows.

I. The generator

{\mathbb A}

admits a closure
\overline{{\mathbb A}}
in
{\rm HS}
from the domain
{\cal D}
.
This closure is
a generator of a strongly continuous contraction semigroup
in
{\rm HS}
.

II. For all
\rho(0)\in{\rm HS}
,
the trajectories
\rho(t)=e^{\overline{{\mathbb A}}t}\rho(0)\in C(0,\infty;{\rm HS})
are generalised solutions
to the QRM
(Definition
2.3
).

Our strategy is as follows.
We rewrite
the QRM as

\dot{\rho}(t)={\mathbb A}\rho(t):=K\rho(t)+\gamma D\rho(t),\qquad{\rm where}\quad K\rho=-i[H,\rho],\quad\rho\in{\rm HS}.

(1.9)

We show that the operator

K
is antisymmetric on
{\cal D}
:

\langle K\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}=-\langle\rho_{1},K\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS},\qquad\rho_{1},\,\rho_{2}\in{\cal D},

(1.10)

and that its quadratic form vanishes:

\langle\rho,K\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}=0,\qquad\rho\in{\cal D}.

(1.11)

Then both operators
{\mathbb A}
and
{\mathbb A}^{\dagger}

are nonpositive on
{\cal D}
by (
1.8
). Hence,
both operators
{\mathbb A}
and
{\mathbb A}^{\dagger}
are dissipative, so
the existence of the semigroup follows from the Lumer–Phillips theorem
[
21
]
. The crucial
role in the proofs is played by
the
polynomial structure (
2.14
).

Remark 1.3
.

The zero quadratic form (
1.11
)
means that the vector field
K\rho
is orthogonal to
\rho

in the space
{\rm HS}
. Thus, the first term on the right hand side
of (
1.9
) can be interpreted as a rotations in
{\rm HS}
.
By (
1.8
),
the second vector field
\gamma D\rho
is the generator of
contractions
of
{\rm HS}
which correspond to quantum spontaneous emission.

Let us comment on previous results in the field.
In the case of bounded generators
{\mathbb A}
,
semigroups for equations of type (
1.2
) obviously exist.
In this case,
Lindblad
[
20
]
and Gorini, Kossakowski, and Sudarshan
[
16
]

found necessary and sufficient conditions on
{\mathbb A}
providing the positivity and trace preservation.

For unbounded generators, the existence of dynamical semigroup
for Quantum Dynamical Systems (QDS) is not well-developed,
[
4
, p.110]
.
In
[
11
]
, E.B. Davies considered quantum-mechanical Fokker–Planck
equations (QFP).
The existence of the corresponding positive
contraction semigroup is established
in the Banach space of self-adjoint trace-class operators.
The uniqueness and trace preservation have not been proved.
Sufficient conditions, providing the trace preservation
for QFP equations,
were
found in
[
9
]
. The detailed characterisation of a class of covariant
QDS with unbounded generators is presented in
[
17
]
.
The survey
can be found in
[
10
,
15
]
.

The theory
[
16
,
20
]

implies that for a class of dissipation operators,
(
1.2
) can be represented in the form of the QFP equation
if
{\mathbb A}
is
a bounded generator of a completely positive semigroup (see Theorem 4.2 of
[
10
, Chapter 9]
).
For unbounded generators, such a representation holds under
appropriate conditions
[
14
]
.

We construct
strongly continuous contraction
semigroups for equation (
1.2
) in the Hilbert space of the Hilbert–Schmidt
operators
which, in particular, contains all trace class operators.
This framework allows us to employ the well-developed theory of contraction semigroups
in the Hilbert space
by Lumer and Phillips and others.
As a result, we prove that the semigroups exist under simple and easily verifiable
conditions
on the pumping and dissipation operators.
However, the questions of positivity and trace preservation
remain open;
the needed development will be addressed elsewhere.

2
Notations and main results

A density operator
\rho\in{\rm HS}

is defined uniquely by its matrix entries

\rho_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\langle n,s|\rho|n^{\prime},s^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad n,n^{\prime}=0,1,\dots,\quad{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}.

(2.12)

The Hilbert–Schmidt norm, corresponding to the inner product (
1.5
), can be written as

\|\rho\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho^{2}]=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}|\rho_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|^{2}<\infty.

(2.13)

Note that
by (
1.1
),
the space

X_{\infty}
is invariant with respect to
a
and
a^{\dagger}
. Hence,
the products
of

\rho\in{\cal D}
with any polynomials of
a
and
a^{\dagger}

are well-defined as operators
in
X_{\infty}
.

Denote
M_{2}={\mathbb C}^{2}\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
the space of

2\times 2
-matrices.
We assume that the pumping and the dissipation operator
satisfy the following conditions.

H1.

A^{e}
and
D\rho
are
polynomials in the creation and annihilation operators:

A^{e}=P(a,a^{\dagger}),\qquad D\rho=\sum_{j}Q_{j}\rho R_{j},

(2.14)

where
P,Q_{k},R_{k}

are polynomials in
a
and
a^{\dagger}
with coefficients from
M_{2}
.

H2.

The operators
A^{e}

and
D\rho
with
\rho\in{\cal D}

are symmetric in
X_{\infty}
.

H3.

D
and
D^{\dagger}
are nonpositive in
{\cal D}

(that is, (
1.8
) holds).

Remark 2.1
.

i)
H1–H2
imply that

{\mathbb A}\rho\in{\cal D}
for

\rho\in{\cal D}
.

ii)
H3
holds if
Q_{j}=R_{j}^{\dagger}
for all
j
.

Our first result is as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

All conditions
H1–H3
hold for the
dissipation
operator
D=D_{1}
.

To formulate other results, we need to
give a meaning to the QRM. The issue is that the operator
{\mathbb A}
is not well-defined on
{\rm HS}
.
Note that
the equation admits the treatment via the matrix
entries (
2.12
) as the system

\dot{\rho}_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(t)=[{\mathbb A}\rho(t)]_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0,\qquad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm},

(2.15)

since the right hand side is well-defined for all
\rho(t)\in{\rm HS}
.
Indeed,
by

H1
and (
1.1
),
the generator
{\mathbb A}
is well-defined on the domain
{\cal D}
, and

[{\mathbb A}\rho]_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}|k-n|+|k^{\prime}-n^{\prime}|\leq N\\
r,r^{\prime}=s_{\pm}\end{array}}{\mathbb A}_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{k,r;k^{\prime},r^{\prime}}\rho_{k,r;k^{\prime},r^{\prime}},\quad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm},\quad\rho\in{\cal D},

(2.16)

where
N=\max(2,\deg P,\max_{j}[\deg Q_{j}+\deg R_{j}])
.
Finally, since
the summation in (
2.16
) is finite,
the matrix entries
[{\mathbb A}\rho]_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
admit a unique extension by continuity from

\rho\in{\cal D}

to all
\rho\in{\rm HS}
. Hence,
the operator
{\mathbb A}
admits a closed extension
\overline{{\mathbb A}}
from the domain
{\cal D}
to
{\cal D}({\mathbb A})\supset{\cal D}
.
The structure (
2.16
) means that the matrix of the generator in the basis
|n,s\rangle
is almost
diagonal.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
a trajectory
\rho(t)\in C(0,\infty;{\rm HS})
is a generalised solution to the QRM if it
satisfies the system
(
2.15
) in the sense of distributions; that is,

\rho_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(t)-\rho_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(0)\!=\!\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathbb A}(\tau)\rho(\tau)]_{n,s;\,n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}d\tau,\qquad t\geq 0,\quad\forall\,\,n,n^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}.

(2.17)

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let conditions
H1–H3
hold.
Then

i)
\overline{{\mathbb A}}
is a generator of a
strongly continuous contraction semigroup
U(t)=e^{\overline{{\mathbb A}}t}
in
{\rm HS}
,
and for

\rho(0)\in{\cal D}(\overline{{\mathbb A}})
, the trajectories
\rho(t)=U(t)\rho(0)

are solutions to the equation

\dot{\rho}(t)=\overline{{\mathbb A}}\rho(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(2.18)

ii) For all
\rho(0)\in{\rm HS}
, the trajectories
\rho(t)=U(t)\rho(0)\in C(0,\infty;{\rm HS})

are generalised solutions to the QRM.

3
Nonpositivity of the dissipation operator and of its adjoint

Here we prove Lemma
2.2
.
Conditions
H1
and
H2
obviously hold. It remains to check
(
1.8
).
First, let us calculate the adjoint operator

D_{1}^{\dagger}
: for
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in{\cal D}
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}(D_{1}\rho_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho_{1}(a\rho_{2}a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{2}a^{{\dagger}}a)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho_{1}a\rho_{2}a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(a^{\dagger}\rho_{1}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big((a^{\dagger}\rho_{1}a-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{1})\rho_{2}\Big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[(D_{1}^{\dagger}\rho_{1})\rho_{2}].

Hence, the adjoint operator
D_{1}^{\dagger}
differs from
D_{1}

by swapping

a
and
a^{\dagger}
:

D_{1}^{\dagger}\rho=a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{{\dagger}}a-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho,\qquad\rho\in{\cal D}.

(3.1)

Second, let us prove
the nonpositivity (
1.8
) for
D=D_{1}
. For
\rho\in{\cal D}
,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D_{1}\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho D_{1}\rho\big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho\big(a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a\big)\Big)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}-\rho a^{\dagger}a\rho\big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a\rho^{2}\big).

Now we use the fact that
\rho
is a finite rank Hermitian operator (
1.6
).
Then (
2.14
) implies that
the operators
\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}
and
a^{\dagger}a\rho^{2}

have only finite
number of nonzero entries (
2.12
), so their traces
are well-defined.
Moreover,

\rho
admits a finite spectral resolution in the orthonormal basis of its eigenvectors
e_{i}\in X_{\infty}
:

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\rho_{i}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}.

(3.3)

In this basis, the entries
\rho_{ij}=\rho_{i}\delta_{ij}
, and
the entries

a_{jk}=\langle e_{j},ae_{k}\rangle

and
a^{\dagger}_{kl}=\langle e_{k},a^{\dagger}e_{l}\rangle

of the operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
are well-defined.
Hence, (
3.2
) implies,
with summation in repeated indices,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D_{1}\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\delta_{ij}a_{jk}\rho_{k}\delta_{kl}a^{\dagger}_{li}-a^{\dagger}_{kl}a_{lj}\rho_{j}^{2}\delta_{jk}=\rho_{i}a_{ik}\rho_{k}a^{\dagger}_{ki}-a^{\dagger}_{kl}a_{lk}\rho_{k}^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{i}a_{ik}\rho_{k}a^{\dagger}_{ki}-a^{\dagger}_{ki}a_{ik}\rho_{k}^{2}=a_{ik}a^{\dagger}_{ki}(\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{ik}a^{\dagger}_{ki}(\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2})+a_{ki}a^{\dagger}_{ik}(\rho_{k}\rho_{i}-\rho_{i}^{2}))=-\frac{1}{2}|a_{ik}|^{2}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{k})^{2}\leq 0

since
a^{\dagger}_{ik}=\overline{a}_{ki}
. Hence, the nonpositivity is proved for
D_{1}
.
For
D_{1}^{\dagger}
the proof is the same with the swapping

a
and
a^{\dagger}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The proof of the nonpositivity
essentially depends on the symmetry of
\rho
.

4
Semigroup and generalised solutions

Lemma 4.1
.

Both operators
{\mathbb A}
and
{\mathbb A}^{\dagger}
are well-defined and nonpositive on the domain
{\cal D}
.

Proof.

i)
For
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in{\cal D}
, we have in the notation (
1.9
):

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{1},K\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}[H,\rho_{2}])=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}(H\rho_{2}-\rho_{2}H))=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}H\rho_{2}-\rho_{2}H\rho_{1}).

(4.1)

where all the terms are well-defined by
H1
.
Hence, (
1.11
) holds.
Therefore,
{\mathbb A}=K+\gamma D
is nonpositive by
H3
.
Similarly, we obtain (
1.10
):

\displaystyle\langle K\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits([H,\rho_{1}]^{\dagger}\rho_{2})=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits((H\rho_{1}-\rho_{1}H)\rho_{2})=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}\rho_{2}H-\rho_{1}H\rho_{2})

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}[H,\rho_{2}])=-\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{1}(K\rho_{2}))=-\langle\rho_{1},K\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}.

Hence
{\mathbb A}^{\dagger}|_{\cal D}=-K+\gamma D^{\dagger}
, so
{\mathbb A}^{\dagger}
is also
well-defined on
{\cal D}
and
nonpositive by
H3
.
∎
