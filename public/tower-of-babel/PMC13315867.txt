Cardiac characteristics of Chinese patients with Danon disease associated with LAMP2 p.Leu325fs variants
Authors: Kunlun Yin, Yuanming Li, Huazheng Zhao, Jing Xu, Hang Yang, Yanhai Meng, Yang Sun, Shuiyun Wang, Zhou Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study characterized the clinical and genetic features of Danon disease (DD), focusing on participants harboring LAMP2 frameshift variants at the 325th amino acid position (p.Leu325fs), and explored their cardiac implications. These frameshift variants result in loss of functional LAMP2 protein through premature termination, thereby impairing lysosomal function. Although the association between LAMP2 variants and DD is established, the cardiac phenotype specifically associated with p.Leu325fs variants remains incompletely characterized.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of nine patients with DD diagnosed at the Fuwai Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, Beijing, China. Comprehensive clinical data, including biochemical markers, electrocardiographic findings, and imaging studies (echocardiography and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging), were collected. Pathogenic LAMP2 variants were confirmed through exome sequencing or clinical genetic testing.ResultsAll participants carried pathogenic frameshift variants at p.Leu325fs, presenting with prominent cardiac manifestations including myocardial hypertrophy or dilation, impaired systolic function, and arrhythmias. Electrocardiographic abnormalities, notably intraventricular conduction block and ST-T segment changes, were observed in all participants. Elevated N-terminal prohormone of brain natriuretic peptide and lactate dehydrogenase levels were observed in all participants, consistent with advanced heart failure and myocardial injury.ConclusionsOur findings suggest that p.Leu325fs pathogenic variants in DD are associated with a predominantly cardiac phenotype, characterized by universal intraventricular conduction block, ST-T segment changes, progressive myocardial fibrosis, and impaired systolic function. These results support the clinical value of integrating LAMP2 genetic testing, cardiac MRI, and electrocardiographic monitoring into the early diagnosis, risk stratification, and management of patients with DD.Keywords: Danon disease, LAMP2, Leu325fs variants, Cardiac electrophysiology, Genetic testing

Introduction

Danon disease (DD, Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man: 300257) is a rare X-linked dominant lysosomal storage disorder first reported by Danon et al. in 1981 [1], [2]. DD exhibits diverse clinical manifestations, with core features including cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, and intellectual disability [1]. DD affects both males and females, however, the clinical course is typically more severe in males, who often present in adolescence with rapidly progressive cardiomyopathy and die in early adulthood without intervention. Females, by contrast, tend to have a later onset and more variable phenotype due to X-chromosome inactivation mosaicism, though cardiac involvement remains the predominant cause of morbidity and mortality in both sexes [3], [4], [5]. Cardiac manifestations commonly include left ventricular hypertrophy or dilatation, impaired cardiac function, and arrhythmias, which can progress to heart failure in severe cases. The typical cardiac pathological changes in DD include myocardial fibrosis, myocardial hypertrophy, and the formation of autophagic vacuoles in cardiomyocytes.

DD is caused by variants in the Lysosome-Associated Membrane glycoProtein 2 (LAMP2) locus (HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee: 6501), which encodes the LAMP2 protein [6]. Highly expressed in cardiomyocytes and skeletal muscle cells, LAMP2 protein is crucial for maintaining normal lysosomal functions such as material degradation and autophagy regulation [7]. LAMP2 variants induce lysosomal dysfunction, preventing timely clearance of abnormal substances and leading to their intracellular accumulation, which causes further multi-organ damage [8]. In particular, frameshift variants—such as those affecting the p.Leu325fs position—are predicted to result in loss of functional LAMP2 protein through premature termination and nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, representing a distinct pathogenic mechanism from missense variants that may preserve partial protein function.

LAMP2 variants are diverse, mostly concentrated in the coding region. Notably, frameshift variants at the 325th amino acid position (p.Leu325fs) have been recurrent in some patients with DD [9], [10], [11]. These frameshift variants are predicted to result in loss of functional LAMP2 protein, thereby exacerbating lysosomal dysfunction and contributing to multi-organ damage. Although numerous studies have confirmed the association between LAMP2 variants and DD, research on how specific sites (especially Leu325fs) shape the clinical phenotype of DD, particularly its cardiac manifestations, remains limited [12], [13].

Inter-individual variability in the clinical phenotype of DD poses challenges to early diagnosis and precise treatment. Although some LAMP2 variants have been associated with distinct clinical presentations, phenotypic differences between specific variant sites remain incompletely characterized [3], [14], [15]. A 2019 study by Brambatti et al. showed that only 45.2% (66/146) of reported cases of DD had clear ethnic information, with Asians accounting for 20.6% [4]. Pathogenic variant loci in reported Chinese cases/pedigrees of DD are scattered across multiple exons, primarily involving exons 3, 4, 8, and the splice sites of exons 5/6. Examples include the c.973delG frameshift variant (p.Leu325fs) in exon 8, the c.467 T > G nonsense variant (p.Leu156X) in exon 4, the c.396delA frameshift variant in exon 3, and the c.741 + 2 T > C and c.741 + 1G > A splice variants at the exon 5/6 junction [4].

In our prior genetic analysis of 42 confirmed patients with DD, nine carried consistent LAMP2 p.Leu325fs variants—a notable enrichment suggesting that this position may represent a mutational hotspot in the Chinese DD population. Thus, this study conducted an in-depth analysis of these nine patients to characterize the clinical phenotype of DD associated with p.Leu325fs variants, with a particular focus on cardiac manifestations, aiming to provide clinical evidence to support the early diagnosis and individualized management of DD patients carrying these variants.

Methods

Study design and participant inclusion criteria

This was a single-center retrospective case series, enrolling nine patients with DD diagnosed at the Fuwai Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences between January 2014 and July 2024. The diagnostic criteria employed were in accordance with the International Consensus on Differential Diagnosis and Management of Patients with Danon Disease: Journal of the American College of Cardiology State-of-the-Art Review [15]. All participants met the following criteria to be included in this study: (1) Presence of at least one feature of the clinical triad, including cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, and intellectual disability; (2) Confirmation of pathogenic LAMP2 frameshift variants at the p.Leu325fs position by genetic testing; (3) Exclusion of glycogen storage disease type II and other secondary cardiomyopathies. This study was designed as a descriptive case series to characterize the clinical and genetic features of DD patients carrying p.Leu325fs variants. No concurrent comparison group was included.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital, CAMS&PUMC (Institutional Review Board Number: 2022-BG-006; Ethical Approval Number: 2024–2551). All participants and their family members signed informed consent forms.

Collection of clinical data

Clinical data were extracted from medical records, including: (1) Demographic characteristics (sex, age at first visit/diagnosis); (2) Clinical symptoms (chief complaints, early symptoms); (3) Family history; (4) Imaging findings (echocardiography, electrocardiogram [ECG], cardiac magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]); (5) Biochemical markers (N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide [NT-proBNP]), myoglobin (Mb), creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate transaminase (AST); (6) Pathology reports; (7) Genetic testing data. The relevant medical history data of family members were also collected. Given the well-established sex-dependent differences in DD phenotype and prognosis, demographic and clinical characteristics were summarized separately for male and female participants. Skeletal muscle involvement was assessed both clinically (muscle weakness, exercise intolerance) and biochemically (serum CK levels), recognizing that subclinical muscle involvement may be reflected by CK elevation in the absence of overt symptoms.

Genetic testing and data analysis

Genetic data were obtained through two approaches: (1) For Participants 1–8 and their immediate family members, 4 mL peripheral blood samples anticoagulated with EDTA were collected. Genomic DNA was extracted using a Promega DNA Extraction Kit (Cat. No. A1620), and exome sequencing was performed in the probands; (2) For Participant 9, genetic testing results, including exome sequencing findings, were retrieved from prior clinical records and externally performed genetic reports. The reported variant was evaluated in conjunction with the participant's clinical phenotype. Bioinformatic analyses in the present study were restricted to sequencing data generated by the research team, as raw sequencing data from external testing were unavailable because of data access and privacy restrictions. All included participants harbored LAMP2 frameshift variants at the p.Leu325fs position (c.973dup, c.973del, or c.974del), identified using the major LAMP2 transcript RefSeq NM_002294.3 as reference.

Sanger sequencing validation and pathogenicity interpretation

Primers targeting the candidate variant sites in LAMP2 exon 8 (forward: 5′-ggcaggcagagagaagaaga-3′; reverse: 5′-tgacttcaatgaaagctacctgg-3′) were designed for Sanger sequencing validation. Pathogenicity of suspected LAMP2 variants was determined in accordance with the Standards and Guidelines for Genetic Variant Classification published by the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG/AMP). Variants were classified as ‘Pathogenic’ or ‘Likely pathogenic’ based on applicable evidence codes (PVS1, PS4, PM2_Supporting), as detailed in Table 1.

Clinical follow-up

The primary outcome was major adverse cardio-cerebrovascular events (MACCE), defined as the first occurrence of: (1) All-cause mortality (including sudden cardiac death); (2) Non-fatal myocardial infarction; (3) Ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke; (4) Coronary revascularization; (5) Heart failure hospitalization; (6) Malignant arrhythmia requiring hospitalization or device intervention. Events were confirmed via medical records and surgical reports and reviewed uniformly. Follow-up started at the date of first clinical contact at our institution and ended at the first MACCE, loss to follow-up, or December 2024. Due to the small sample size, the inclusion of related family members, the heterogeneous disease stage at enrollment, and the variable interval from symptom onset to diagnosis, formal survival analysis was not performed; instead, individual clinical trajectories and vital status are reported descriptively.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of nine participants with confirmed DD were included, all carrying LAMP2 p.Leu325fs variants. Their baseline characteristics are summarized and described below, with demographic data reported separately for male and female participants given the sex-dependent nature of DD (Table 1).

Among the four male participants, the age at first visit ranged from 7.5 to 23 years (mean 11.9 ± 6.9 years), and the age at diagnosis ranged from 10.5 to 23 years (mean 14.8 ± 5.6 years), with a diagnostic delay of 0 to 5.5 years (mean 2.9 ± 2.2 years). Among the five female participants, the age at first visit ranged from 10 to 26 years (mean 17.0 ± 6.5 years), and the age at diagnosis ranged from 10 to 32 years (mean 21.1 ± 9.5 years), with a diagnostic delay of 0 to 15 years (mean 4.1 ± 5.8 years). Overall, males tended to present and be diagnosed at a younger age than females, consistent with the more severe and earlier-onset disease course characteristic of hemizygous LAMP2 variant carriers.

Clinical symptoms were dominated by cardiac manifestations. Four participants (44.4%) presented with palpitations as the chief complaint at initial diagnosis, six (66.7%) with chest tightness, and three (33.3%) with decreased exercise tolerance. Two participants (22.2%) were classified as New York Heart Association functional class IV. Notably, all four male participants had elevated serum CK levels (Table 2), suggesting possible subclinical skeletal muscle involvement even in the absence of overt myopathic symptoms.

Compared with cardiac manifestations, extra-cardiac involvement was less uniformly present across the cohort. Overt skeletal myopathy was identified in only one participant (11.1%), presenting with decreased muscle strength and poor exercise endurance at first visit. However, elevated serum CK was detected in all four male participants (Table 2), suggesting subclinical muscle involvement may be underrepresented by clinical assessment alone. Intellectual disability was present in three participants (33.3%), manifesting as learning difficulties with academic performance below that of their peers. Visual impairment was noted in three participants (33.3%), including two with bilateral refractive error and one with bilateral strabismus. In summary, while cardiac manifestations dominated the clinical presentation, extra-cardiac features—particularly intellectual disability and visual impairment—were not uncommon in this cohort.

Regarding family history, four participants (44.4%) had a family history of heart disease. Among them, clear pedigree maps of pathogenic variant loci could be constructed for three participants (Participants 3, 4, and 9) (Fig. 1). The family of Participant 1 had a history of early-onset heart-related death: her father died of heart disease in his twenties (the exact etiology could not be confirmed due to incomplete medical records), and her grandmother also passed away in her forties (the cause of death was unconfirmed, but heart disease could not be ruled out based on family history characteristics). Participant 3 and Participant 4 were mother and son from the same pedigree, both diagnosed with DD. Participant 3 was diagnosed with dilated cardiomyopathy, and her son (Participant 4) with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Additionally, the mother of Participant 3 had experienced hypertrophic cardiomyopathy during her lifetime. For Participant 9, venous blood samples were collected from her father, aunt, younger brother, and daughter for exome sequencing and family verification showed that only her younger brother carried this hemizygous variant site. Her mother, who had hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and was deceased, could not be genotyped. However, since her father did not carry this variant site while her younger brother (with similar symptoms) did, co-segregation of this variant in the pedigree was still confirmed.

Biochemical findings

Laboratory test results revealed consistent abnormalities across multiple organ systems in all nine participants, encompassing markers of cardiac stress, myocardial and skeletal muscle injury and hepatic involvement (Table 2).

Cardiac stress and myocardial injury markers

NT-proBNP was markedly elevated above the upper limit of normal in all nine participants (100%), with values ranging from 430.9 to 21,670 pg/mL, reflecting universally elevated cardiac filling pressures consistent with advanced heart failure. LDH was similarly elevated in all participants, ranging from 344 to 1259 IU/L. High-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) was elevated in five of the six participants with available data, indicating ongoing myocardial injury. CK-MB was elevated in six participants (66.7%), with markedly high values in Participants 7 and 8 (15.52 and 14.25 ng/mL, respectively).

Skeletal muscle involvement markers

Total serum CK was elevated in all four male participants (Participants 2, 4, 5, and 6; values: 465, 838, 914, and 1065 IU/L, respectively), whereas CK remained within the normal range in all five female participants. This sex-specific pattern of CK elevation suggests subclinical skeletal muscle involvement in male carriers of p.Leu325fs variants, even in the absence of overt myopathic symptoms. Myoglobin (Mb) was elevated in five participants (55.6%), including all three male participants with the highest CK values (Participants 4, 5, and 6) and two female participants (Participants 7 and 8), further corroborating myocyte membrane injury.

Hepatic and other markers

AST was elevated in eight of nine participants (88.9%), and ALT was elevated in five participants (55.6%), consistent with secondary hepatic involvement or skeletal muscle-derived enzyme release. Uric acid was markedly elevated in two participants (22.2%), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein was mildly elevated in two participants (22.2%), suggesting low-grade systemic inflammation in a subset of cases.

Seven of nine participants had an enlarged left atrial anteroposterior diameter, with two showing markedly enlarged left atria (Participant 3: 52 mm; Participant 1: 48 mm). Six participants (66.7%) had varying degrees of increased interventricular septal thickness, including one with extreme hypertrophy (Participant 7: 44 mm). Five participants (55.6%) had a reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (EF <55%), ranging from 14% to 34%, reflecting substantial systolic dysfunction. Four participants had an increased left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, well above the upper limit of normal. EF values ranged widely across the entire cohort (14%–70%), corresponding to the spectrum from preserved systolic function in non-end-stage hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) to severe systolic impairment in participants with dilated or end-stage HCM phenotypes. Five participants (55.6%) had increased left ventricular posterior wall thickness (range 19–27 mm), consistent with hypertrophic remodeling. In contrast, four participants (Participants 1, 3, 4 and 9) had relatively thin posterior walls, corresponding to their dilated cardiomyopathy phenotype. Mitral and tricuspid regurgitation of varying degrees were present in most participants, with two showing severe mitral regurgitation (Participants 2 and 6).

Electrocardiographic findings

All nine participants (100%) demonstrated intraventricular conduction block (IVCD) and ST-T segment changes on ECG (Fig. 2), representing the most consistent electrophysiological abnormality in this cohort. Of these, four participants (44.4%) demonstrated bundle branch block as a specific form of intraventricular conduction delay. Additionally, five participants presented with Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome, three had atrial fibrillation, and three had atrial flutter. Five participants showed atrial or ventricular tachycardia, six exhibited atrial or ventricular premature contractions, and three demonstrated left ventricular high voltage on ECG. The universal presence of IVCD and ST-T changes across all participants, irrespective of their echocardiographic phenotype (HCM or DCM) or degree of systolic dysfunction, suggests that these ECG abnormalities may represent an early and consistent electrophysiological marker of LAMP2 p.Leu325fs-related myocardial injury.

The eight participants were classified into one with a DCM phenotype (Participant 1, female) and seven with HCM phenotypes, of whom one had progressed to end-stage HCM (Participant 3, female) and six had non-end-stage HCM.

No participant had a fully normal LV structure or myocardial integrity. Six of eight participants had left ventricular end-diastolic volume indices (EDVi) above the upper limit of normal. LGE-positive enhancement signals were detected in all eight participants, confirming the universal presence of myocardial fibrosis in this cohort, irrespective of phenotype.

The six non-end-stage HCM participants shared several structural features: preserved or supranormal LVEF (range 50%–74%), markedly elevated LVMi (range 105–248 g/m2), and substantially thickened IVS (range 17–40 mm). Cardiac output and cardiac index were generally maintained in this subgroup compared with the two participants with severely impaired systolic function.

Participant 1 (female, DCM phenotype) had a markedly reduced LVEF of 19%, with LVMi and IVS thickness within normal limits and substantially reduced cardiac output and cardiac index, consistent with advanced dilated cardiomyopathy. Participant 3 (female, end-stage HCM phenotype) had a severely reduced LVEF of 16%, with LVMi and IVS thickness that had regressed toward normal limits—characteristic of the burned-out phase of HCM—accompanied by severe LV volume overload. She had the highest LGE ratio in the entire cohort (55%), reflecting the most extensive myocardial fibrosis.

Among the non-end-stage HCM participants, notable inter-individual variability was observed in LGE extent (range 2.8%–19.3%), degree of myocardial hypertrophy, and LV systolic function, underscoring the phenotypic heterogeneity associated with p.Leu325fs variants even within a single variant subgroup.

Pathological biopsy findings

Six participants underwent pathologic biopsy. Participants 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 underwent endomyocardial biopsy, and Participant 9 underwent deltoid muscle biopsy. All biopsies demonstrated pathological changes consistent with DD.

Endomyocardial biopsy findings

Endomyocardial biopsy of the interventricular septum in Participant 2 demonstrated cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, vacuolar degeneration, disorganized cardiomyocyte arrangement, and mild interstitial fibrous tissue hyperplasia. A comprehensive immunohistochemical panel demonstrated absent LAMP2 protein expression (−), confirming complete loss of functional LAMP2 in this hemizygous male carrier. Alpha-galactosidase A staining was negative, effectively excluding Fabry disease. And plakoglobin, desmin, and dystrophin expression were all preserved, arguing against arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, desminopathy, and dystrophinopathy, respectively. Masson trichrome staining was positive, and both PAS and AB-PAS staining showed individual cell positivity. The combined histopathological and immunohistochemical findings were diagnostic of DD (Supplementary Fig. S1).

Endomyocardial biopsy results of Participant 2. A, LAMP2 protein staining. B, Masson staining. C, Periodic acid-Schiff staining. Scale bars, 100 μm

Participant 3 underwent cardiac transplantation. Pathological examination of the explanted recipient heart revealed extensive cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and vacuolar degeneration in the left ventricle and interventricular septum, multifocal cardiomyocyte disorganization, prominent interstitial fibrous tissue hyperplasia, and widespread multifocal fibrous scar formation. The right ventricular myocardium showed cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, vacuolar degeneration, and small focal fibrous scar formation. No atherosclerotic changes were identified in any coronary artery branch. The pathological diagnosis was non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with left ventricular dilation, representing the burned-out end-stage phenotype of DD-related cardiomyopathy.

Participant 4 showed significant cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, extensive vacuolar degeneration, interstitial fibrosis, and small focal fibrous scar formation, with only mild cardiomyocyte disorganization, on interventricular septal biopsy. Pathological findings were consistent with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and electron microscopy was recommended for further ultrastructural characterization but was not performed.

Participant 5 demonstrated predominantly autophagic vacuoles with increased glycogen deposition and lipid droplets on endomyocardial biopsy, a pattern highly characteristic of Danon disease and related vacuolar myopathies.

Participant 6 showed endocardial thickening, significant and extensive cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and vacuolar degeneration, disorganized cardiomyocyte arrangement, and small focal fibrous scar formation on interventricular septal biopsy. The pronounced vacuolar degeneration raised the possibility of a metabolic cardiomyopathy and electron microscopy was recommended for further assessment but was not performed.

Deltoid muscle biopsy findings

Deltoid muscle biopsy of Participant 9, performed at an external institution, demonstrated myofibrillar disarray. NADH enzyme staining revealed multiple small areas of reduced oxidative enzyme activity, initially reported as a multiminicore-like pattern suggestive of multiminicore myopathy. However, in the context of the confirmed LAMP2 c.974del (p.Leu325ArgfsTer21) frameshift variant, these myopathic changes are most consistent with vacuolar myopathy secondary to DD-related lysosomal dysfunction, rather than a distinct multiminicore myopathy. This case illustrates how DD-related skeletal muscle pathology may be misattributed to other myopathic conditions when evaluated without genetic context. Taken together with the initial misdiagnosis of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in the same participant, this case underscores the critical role of LAMP2 genetic testing in establishing a definitive diagnosis of DD, particularly in patients presenting with ambiguous histopathological findings across multiple organ systems.

Clinical outcomes and follow-up

All nine participants completed the follow-up. The follow-up duration ranged from 0 to 9.4 years, with a mean follow-up time of 3.43 years (1252 days) and a median of 1.97 years (719 days). Given the small sample size (n = 9), the inclusion of related family members (Participants 3 and 4, a mother-son pair), the heterogeneous disease stage at enrollment, and the variable interval from symptom onset to diagnosis across participants, formal time-to-event survival analysis was not performed. Clinical outcomes are reported descriptively.

Two participants (22.2%) died during the follow-up period, yielding an overall survival rate of 77.8% at the end of the study. Participant 9 (female) died early in the follow-up period without having received surgical intervention and her death was attributed to delayed access to appropriate cardiac intervention. Participant 6 (male) died suddenly 8 months after undergoing the Morrow procedure for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

At least one MACCE event was recorded in five of nine participants (55.6%), yielding a MACCE-free rate of 44.4% across the entire cohort. Seven participants (77.8%) underwent surgical intervention, including heart transplantation (n = 2), radiofrequency ablation (n = 3), ICD implantation (n = 1), and the Morrow procedure (n = 2). Of these, four (57.1%) experienced at least one MACCE event during follow-up (Fig. 4).

Among the two participants who underwent heart transplantation, both maintained stable survival status at the end of follow-up. However, one developed persistent skeletal muscle weakness as a post-transplantation complication, consistent with ongoing systemic involvement in DD. All three participants who received radiofrequency ablation or ICD implantation for arrhythmia management subsequently developed heart failure symptoms, suggesting progressive underlying cardiomyopathy despite arrhythmia control. Of the two participants who underwent the Morrow procedure, one maintained a stable clinical status, while the other (Participant 6) died suddenly 8 months postoperatively, as noted above. Among the two participants who did not receive any surgical intervention, one (Participant 9) died due to delayed cardiac intervention, while the other remained event-free and maintained a stable clinical status throughout the follow-up period.

These outcomes collectively highlight the severe and progressive clinical trajectory of p.Leu325fs-associated DD. The favourable survival of both heart transplant recipients supports timely referral for advanced cardiac therapies in patients with end-stage disease.

Discussion

By integrating genetic data, clinical phenotypes, cardiac MRI, ECG findings, and biochemical markers across nine participants, this study characterizes the clinical features associated with LAMP2 p.Leu325fs variants in DD and describes the potential value of multi-modal assessment in its diagnosis and management. The findings suggest that cardiac MRI, when combined with ECG and serological markers, may serve as a useful framework for bridging genetic variant identification to clinical phenotyping in DD.

From the perspective of gene structure, the LAMP2 gene consists of 9 exons and produces three isoforms through alternative splicing of exon 9—LAMP2A, LAMP2B, and LAMP2C [16]. The junction region of exons 8–9 is crucial for maintaining the structure and function of these isoforms [7]. In this study, LAMP2 c.973dup, c.973del, and c.974del are frameshift variants that introduce premature stop codons downstream of p.Leu325, predicting loss of functional protein across all three LAMP2 isoforms via nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. This is distinct from variants like c.467 T > G, which only disrupt a single isoform [11]. Whether the pan-isoform loss of LAMP2 in p.Leu325fs carriers contributes to a more severe or distinct clinical phenotype compared with other variant types requires direct comparative studies, which were beyond the scope of this case series.

All nine participants demonstrated prominent cardiac involvement, including myocardial hypertrophy or dilation, electrocardiographic abnormalities, and varying degrees of systolic dysfunction, with EF ranging from 14% to 70%. While cardiac manifestations were the dominant feature of the clinical presentation, extra-cardiac involvement was not absent: intellectual disability was present in 33.3% and visual impairment in 33.3% of participants, though overt skeletal myopathy was identified in only one participant (11.1%). Compared with the classical triad of DD, extra-cardiac features were less uniformly present in this cohort, which may partly reflect referral patterns at a tertiary cardiac center rather than a true absence of systemic involvement. Gandaeva et al. reported a 9-year-old girl with the LAMP2 c.973dup variant who presented with palpitations, exertional dyspnea, and visual abnormalities leading to early diagnosis [17]. The relatively lower rate of skeletal myopathy in our cohort may reflect the high energy demands of cardiomyocytes rendering them particularly susceptible to LAMP2 deficiency, though this remains speculative in the absence of functional data.

Cardiac MRI bridges the gap between genetic diagnosis and clinical phenotype by visualizing and quantifying the structural myocardial consequences of LAMP2 dysfunction [18]. Patients with DD often exhibit diffuse LGE involving the subendocardial and mid-myocardial layers of the anterior, lateral, and posterior walls, typically showing a feathery transmural pattern with a characteristic increasing extent from base to apex [19], [20]. In our study, cardiac MRI revealed universally abnormal LV structure and myocardial fibrosis across all eight participants who underwent imaging, irrespective of phenotypic subtype. LGE-positive signals were detected in all participants, confirming the presence of myocardial fibrosis as a consistent downstream consequence of LAMP2 p.Leu325fs variants. Phenotype-specific LGE distribution patterns were observed: the DCM participant showed diffuse LGE involving approximately 50% of the myocardium, while non-end-stage HCM participants exhibited focal LGE localized to ventricular insertion points or free walls. The extent of LGE appeared to parallel the degree of LVEF reduction across participants, consistent with the hypothesis that progressive myocardial fibrosis contributes to systolic dysfunction in DD—though formal correlation analysis was not performed given the small sample size. Notably, cardiac MRI also distinguished the pattern of LV involvement in this cohort from the typically heterogeneous remodeling of idiopathic HCM, potentially supporting its role in differential diagnosis.

In this cohort, all participants demonstrated intraventricular conduction block and ST-T segment changes on ECG, representing the most consistent electrophysiological finding across all nine cases. Although previous studies have reported variable arrhythmias in DD (including Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome and atrial fibrillation) [14]. The universal presence of IVCD and ST-T changes in this cohort—regardless of echocardiographic phenotype—suggests that these ECG features may be a consistent marker of p.Leu325fs-related myocardial injury, though their specificity relative to other LAMP2 variants cannot be established from this case series alone. This aligns with a 2015 case report of a LAMP2 c.973del carrier with extensive ST-T changes, but extends the observation to a larger single-variant cohort [9]. Cardiac MRI findings provide a structural basis of these abnormalities: intraventricular conduction block may reflect fibrosis-related damage to the conduction system, while ST-T changes are consistent with myocardial repolarization dysfunction secondary to fibrosis and myocyte injury. These ECG abnormalities may therefore serve as accessible early biomarkers in the clinical follow-up of patients with DD.

All participants had markedly elevated NT-proBNP and LDH levels, consistent with the findings reported by He et al. in LAMP2 c.973del carriers [21]. These elevations, combined with cardiac MRI and ECG findings, are consistent with a proposed pathological progression in which LAMP2 frameshift variants lead to lysosomal dysfunction, subsequent myocardial fibrosis and myocyte injury, and ultimately systolic impairment with elevated cardiac stress markers. The integration of ECG (electrophysiology), cardiac MRI (structure and fibrosis), and serology (cardiac stress and myocyte injury) into a multi-modal assessment framework may reduce the risk of misattributing individual abnormalities—such as LV hypertrophy—to other conditions such as idiopathic HCM.

In clinical practice, DD is frequently misdiagnosed as idiopathic HCM, as the two conditions share similar echocardiographic features of LV hypertrophy, particularly in adolescents and young adults [15], [22], [23]. This distinction is clinically critical, as DD and sarcomeric HCM require fundamentally different management strategies and family screening approaches. The combination of characteristic LGE patterns on cardiac MRI, universal ECG abnormalities (especially IVCD and ST-T changes), markedly elevated LDH, and LAMP2 genetic testing resolved this diagnostic ambiguity in all participants in this cohort—as illustrated by Participant 9, whose initial HCM diagnosis was revised to DD only after pathological and genetic evaluation. For risk stratification, the triad of intraventricular conduction block or ST-T changes, extensive LGE, and elevated NT-proBNP or LDH levels may identify participants at higher risk for disease progression and sudden cardiac death. The latest international consensus on DD, published by the American College of Cardiology, emphasizes that a clinical diagnosis must be confirmed by LAMP2 genetic testing, and that cardiac MRI adds irreplaceable value in phenotyping, fibrosis quantification, and treatment response assessment [15].

This study has several inherent limitations. First, the single-center retrospective design, small sample size (n = 9, all of Chinese ethnicity), and limited follow-up restrict generalizability to broader ethnic groups. Second, clinical assessments were performed at single time points, representing cross-sectional snapshots of a disease that evolves over years to decades—particularly in female carriers—and longitudinal data are needed to characterize phenotypic progression. Third, the absence of a concurrent comparison group with other LAMP2 variant types precludes any direct genotype-phenotype comparison; conclusions regarding the clinical features of p.Leu325fs variants are therefore purely descriptive. Fourth, this study lacks mechanistic or functional data—including RNA analysis, LAMP2 protein quantification, or cellular studies—limiting interpretation of the molecular consequences of these frameshift variants. Fifth, electron microscopy was not performed in all participants who underwent biopsy. Sixth, due to the small sample size, inclusion of related family members, and non-uniform disease stage at enrollment, formal survival analysis was not performed; clinical outcomes are reported descriptively. Future multi-center prospective studies are needed to validate the specificity of p.Leu325fs-associated ECG and cardiac MRI features, establish genotype-guided risk prediction models, and evaluate early interventions targeting lysosomal dysfunction in this patient population.

Conclusions

This study provides descriptive evidence suggesting that LAMP2 p.Leu325fs hotspot variants in DD are associated with a predominantly cardiac phenotype characterized by universal intraventricular conduction block and ST-T segment changes on ECG, progressive myocardial fibrosis on cardiac MRI, variable systolic dysfunction (LVEF range 14%–70%), and markedly elevated NT-proBNP and LDH levels in all participants. Six of nine participants (66.7%) first presented in childhood or adolescence, highlighting the importance of early recognition of this inherited cardiomyopathy in young patients. However, adult-onset presentation was also observed, and clinical vigilance should extend beyond the paediatric age group.

The combination of universal ECG abnormalities, characteristic cardiac MRI findings, and elevated biomarkers may serve as clinically accessible phenotypic markers to support diagnosis, risk stratification, and prognostic assessment in children, adolescents, and young adults with unexplained hypertrophic or dilated cardiomyopathy. Routine electrocardiography, as a cost-effective and widely available tool, warrants integration into the systematic follow-up of patients with suspected or confirmed DD. When combined with LAMP2 genetic testing, cardiac MRI, and serological markers, this multimodal approach may support accurate diagnosis, individualized risk assessment, and timely clinical decision-making—including evaluation for ICD implantation and cardiac transplantation in advanced cases.

Future multicentre prospective studies are needed to validate the specificity of these ECG and cardiac MRI features in LAMP2 c.973 and c.974 variant carriers, to establish genotype-guided risk prediction models, and to explore early therapeutic interventions targeting lysosomal dysfunction. Together, the findings of this case series contribute to the clinical characterization of p.Leu325fs-associated DD and may offer practical guidance for cardiologists managing patients with rare inherited cardiomyopathies across the age spectrum.
