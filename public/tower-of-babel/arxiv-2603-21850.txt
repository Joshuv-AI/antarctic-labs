A Moser-Type Construction for the Liouville Equation
A. Borzì, M. Caponigro, A. Vicari

Abstract
We present a novel extension of Moser’s volume form lemma to the kinetic Liouville equation. In particular, we show that two smooth, positive phase-space densities f f and g g can be connected in unit time by the Liouville equation if and only if a natural compatibility condition on velocity marginals is satisfied. Under this condition, an explicit family of force fields is constructed via a weighted elliptic problem in the velocity variable. Results of numerical experiments are presented to validate the theoretical framework.



1
Introduction

In the seminal work
[
10
]
, J. Moser shows that on a compact manifold without boundary, any pair of smooth and positive
volume forms of equal total mass can be connected by a diffeomorphism. Later developments of this result, in particular
[
7
]
, provide a constructive approach based on interpolating the densities and solving an elliptic
Neumann problem for a time-dependent vector field whose flow realizes the
desired transport.

More precisely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded connected domain with smooth, i.e.

C^{\infty}
, boundary. Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy

f>0
,
g>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
and be such that

\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx.

Then, Moser’s volume forms lemma
[
10
]
states the
existence of a diffeomorphism
\Gamma:\overline{\Omega}\to\overline{\Omega}
pointwise fixing the boundary, such that

\Gamma_{\#}(f\,dx)=g\,dx
, namely

f(x)=g(\Gamma(x))\det D\Gamma(x),\quad\mbox{ for every }x\in\Omega.

A possible strategy to construct such a diffeomorphism, following the approach of Dacorogna and Moser
[
7
]
(see also
[
11
, Box 4.3]
), is to connect the initial and final densities by a smooth path, for instance,the linear interpolation

\rho(\cdot,t)=(1-t)f+tg,\qquad t\in[0,1].

The goal is then to find a time-dependent velocity field
b(x,t)
generating this path as a solution of the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\rho(x,t)+\nabla\cdot\big(b(x,t)\rho(x,t)\big)=0,\qquad\rho(\cdot,0)=f.

(1)

Hence, denoting by
\Gamma_{t}
the flow associated with the vector field
b
,
the solution of the continuity equation (
1
) can be written as
\rho(\cdot,t)dx=(\Gamma_{t})_{\#}(f\,dx)
. In particular, the
1
-time flow
\Gamma_{1}
pushes forward the initial density onto the final one

(\Gamma_{1})_{\#}(f\,dx)=g\,dx,

providing the diffeomorphism solving the prescribed transport problem.

In the present work, we apply this construction to the case of phase-space densities
of Hamiltonian systems. In this setting, the continuity equation governing the evolution of densities takes the form of

\partial_{t}\rho(x,v,t)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho(x,v,t)+\nabla_{v}\cdot\big(a(x,v,t)\,\rho(x,v,t)\big)=0,

(2)

where
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
More precisely, the problem is formulated as follows.

Let
\Omega=X\times V
,
with
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
(
n
-dimensional torus) and
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
be strictly positive and
such that

\int_{\Omega}f(x,v)\,dx\,dv=\int_{\Omega}g(x,v)\,dx\,dv.

Consider the Liouville equation (
2
) with initial condition
\rho(0)=f
.

Find
a:\Omega\times(0,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that the associated solution
\rho(t)
satisfy
\rho(1)=g
.

Equation (
2
), called Liouville Equation by L. Boltzmann in his lectures
[
4
]
, plays a fundamental role in kinetic theory and in the
statistical description of non-interacting many-particle systems,
where
\rho
denotes the normalized material density function.
At the microscopic level, the particles’ dynamics is described by the
differential system

\begin{cases}\chi^{\prime}(t)&=\upsilon(t),\\
\upsilon^{\prime}(t)&=a(\chi(t),\upsilon(t),t),\end{cases}

(3)

where the spatial drift is fixed to be the velocity variable
\upsilon
, and
the force field, which is the
only degree of freedom, may be non-conservative. This kinetic formulation represents a significant
obstruction in the direct application of Moser’s approach.
Indeed, in our framework, the pair of densities
(f,g)
that can be
be connected in finite time by a trajectory of the Liouville Equation (
2
) must satisfy an additional constraint on the velocity marginals.

The problem has been studied from various perspectives in the context of the continuity equation and, more recently, within control theory
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the corresponding problem for the Liouville equation (
2
), defined on the phase space, is yet to be studied.
In this paper we present a solution of this problem with
a Moser-type construction adapted
to the Liouville equation. We present a compatibility condition (see (
6
) below) and we construct an
acceleration/force field
a
defined by an elliptic equation in the velocity variable,
finally proving that the Liouville dynamics with drift
(v,a)
connects
f
to
g

in unit time. In this work we bridge the classical Moser approach
with kinetic transport,
opening the possibility of geometric transport methods in kinetic PDEs
[
3
,
6
]
.

In the following section, we present our main result. In Section

3
, we present analytical and numerical validation
of our framework. A section of conclusion completes this work.

2
Main result

Throughout this section we consider
X=\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the flat
n
-torus, periodic in
x
, and
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded connected with
C^{\infty}
boundary
\partial V
and outward unit normal
n_{v}
. Let
\Omega=X\times V
.
We consider the Liouville equation

\partial_{t}\rho+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho+\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(a\,\rho)=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,1),\qquad\rho(0)=f,

(4)

with zero–flux boundary conditions in
v

(\rho\,a)\cdot n_{v}=0\quad\text{ on }X\times\partial V\times(0,1),

(5)

and periodic boundary condition in
x
.

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
f>0
,
g>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a field

a\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,1];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in (
4
) such that
\rho(1)=g
, is given by the following compatibility condition

\int_{V}f(x,v)\,dv=\int_{V}g(x+v,v)\,dv,\qquad x\in X.

(6)

More precisely, if (
6
) holds, then
a
is given by

a(x,v,t)=\nabla_{v}U_{t}(x-tv,v),

(7)

where
U_{t}
solves, for any
x,t
fixed, the following
weighted elliptic equation in
v
:

\nabla_{v}\!\cdot\!\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,\nabla_{v}U_{t}(x,v)\big)=f(x,v)-g(x+v,v),\qquad\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,U_{t}\,dv=0,

(8)

with

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)=(1-t)f(x,v)+t\,g(x+v,v).

The solution of (
4
) with (
7
) satisfies
\rho(0)=f
,
\rho(1)=g
.

Notice that the compatibility condition (
6
) implies the Moser’s condition for the phase space
\Omega=X\times V
,

\int_{\Omega}f=\int_{\Omega}g.

The proof splits into three steps: a comoving reduction, an elliptic Neumann problem in
v
, and a reconstruction step. In what follows we denote
\Phi_{t}(x,v)=(x+tv,v)
.

Step 1. Comoving reduction

Lemma 2.1

(Comoving transform)
.

For any smooth
\rho
and
a
, define

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v):=\rho(\Phi_{t}(x,v),t),\qquad\widetilde{a}_{t}(x,v):=a(\Phi_{t}(x,v),t).

Then
\rho
solves (
4
)–(
5
) if and only if
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}
solves

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}+\nabla_{v}\!\cdot\big(\widetilde{a}_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\big)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,1),\qquad(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,\widetilde{a}_{t})\cdot n_{v}=0\ \text{ on }X\times\partial V\times(0,1),

(9)

with
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}=f
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=g\circ\Phi_{1}
.

Proof.

By the chain rule,

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}=(\partial_{t}\rho)\circ\Phi_{t}+(D\rho)\circ\Phi_{t}\,[\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}]=(\partial_{t}\rho+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho)\circ\Phi_{t}.

Hence, (
4
) is equivalent to

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}=-(\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(a\,\rho))\circ\Phi_{t}=-\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(\widetilde{a}_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{t}),

which is (
9
). The boundary condition is preserved
since
\Phi_{t}
acts only on the spatial variable
x
, so the velocity
v
, and therefore
\partial V
, are unaffected.
Notice that, in the chain rule above, the differentiation
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)=\rho(x+tv,v,t)
is done with respect to
t
and not
v
, so the only additional term comes from the time dependence
of the
x
-argument, namely
v\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho
. Furthermore,
the map
\Phi_{t}
leaves the velocity variable
v
unchanged;
consequently,

(\nabla_{v}\cdot(a\rho))\circ\Phi_{t}=\nabla_{v}\cdot\big((a\circ\Phi_{t})(\rho\circ\Phi_{t})\big),

or, in other words,
\nabla_{v}\cdot
commutes with pullback along
\Phi_{t}.

∎

Step 2. Necessity of the compatibility condition

Lemma 2.2
.

If (
9
) admits a smooth solution
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}
with
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}=f
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=g\circ\Phi_{1}
, then (
6
) holds.

Proof.

Integrate (
9
) in
v
and use the zero-flux condition (
5
)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,dv=-\int_{V}\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(\widetilde{a}_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{t})\,dv=-\int_{\partial V}(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\widetilde{a}_{t})\cdot n_{v}\,dS_{v}=0.

Hence,
M(x):=\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,dv
is independent of
t
. Evaluating at
t=0
and
t=1
gives

\int_{V}f(x,v)\,dv=\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{1}(x,v)\,dv=\int_{V}g(x+v,v)\,dv,

as claimed.
∎

Step 3. Elliptic construction in the comoving frame

Now, we assume (
6
) and construct
\widetilde{a}_{t}
so that (
9
) holds with a prescribed interpolation
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}
.
We obtain a weighted Neumann problem.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in X
let

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v):=(1-t)\,f(x,v)+t\,g(x+v,v).

Consider the Neumann problem in
v
as follows

\nabla_{v}\!\cdot\!\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,\nabla_{v}U_{t}(x,v)\big)=f(x,v)-g(x+v,v)\quad\text{in }V,\qquad(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\nabla_{v}U_{t})\cdot n_{v}=0\ \text{on }\partial V.

(10)

Then, under the normalization

\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,U_{t}(x,v)\,dv=0,

(11)

there exists a unique
U_{t}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{V})
solving (
10
)–(
11
). Moreover,
U_{t}
depends smoothly on
(x,t)
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in X
. The right-hand side of (
10
) has zero mean by (
6
) since

\int_{V}\big(f(x,v)-g(x+v,v)\big)\,dv=\int_{V}f(x,v)\,dv-\int_{V}g(x+v,v)\,dv=0,

(12)

which plays the role of compatibility condition for the Neumann problem. Define the bilinear form on
H^{1}(V)
:

B_{t,x}(\phi,\psi):=\int_{V}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)\,\nabla_{v}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\psi\,dv,

and the linear functional

\ell_{x}(\psi):=\int_{V}\big(f(x,v)-g(x+v,v)\big)\,\psi\,dv.

Since
f,g>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
, there is a constant
c_{*}>0
with
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\geq c_{*}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Moreover
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,
hence
B_{t,x}
is continuous and coercive on
H^{1}(V)/\mathbb{R}
uniformly in
x
and
t
. The linear functional
\ell_{x}
annihilates constants by (
12
). The Lax–Milgram theorem (see, for instance,
[
8
, Section 6.2]
) ensures the existence of a unique
U_{t}(x,\cdot)\in H^{1}(V)/\mathbb{R}
solving
B_{t,x}(U_{t},\psi)=\ell_{x}(\psi)
for all
\psi\in H^{1}(V)
. The normalization (
11
) gives the uniqueness of the solution
U_{t}(x,\cdot)
in
H^{1}(V)
fixing the additive constant.

By standard elliptic regularity for divergence-form operators with smooth coefficients and homogeneous Neumann boundary condition
[
8
]
, we have
U_{t}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{V})
. Smooth dependence on the parameters
(x,t)
follows from classical results of regularity for parametric elliptic PDEs, see for instance
[
12
]
.
∎

Next, we prove that the density interpolation is the solution
of the continuity equation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
U_{t}
be given by Lemma
2.3
and set
\widetilde{a}_{t}:=\nabla_{v}U_{t}
. Then the pair
(\widetilde{\rho}_{t},\widetilde{a}_{t})
satisfies

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}+\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(\widetilde{a}_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{t})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,1),

(13)

with
(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\widetilde{a}_{t})\cdot n_{v}=0
on
\partial V
,

\widetilde{\rho}_{0}=f
, and
\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=g\circ\Phi_{1}
.

Proof.

By construction
\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}=g(x+v,v)-f(x,v)
. On the other hand, by (
10
), we obtain

\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,\widetilde{a}_{t})=\nabla_{v}\!\cdot\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,\nabla_{v}U_{t}\big)=f(x,v)-g(x+v,v)=-\partial_{t}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}.

The condition
(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\widetilde{a}_{t})\cdot n_{v}=0
on
\partial V
follows the Neumann condition in (
10
). The endpoint identities
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}=f
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=g\circ\Phi_{1}
follow by definition of the interpolation.
∎

Step 4. Reconstruction in the original variables

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The necessity of the compatibility condition (
6
) is given in Lemma
2.2
.

Now, assume (
6
). Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
provide
(\widetilde{\rho}_{t},\widetilde{a}_{t})
solving the comoving continuity equation (
13
) with the prescribed endpoints.
Let
a(x,v,t)
be the pullback along the free transport, namely

a(x,v,t):=\widetilde{a}_{t}\big((\Phi_{t})^{-1}(x,v)\big)=\nabla_{v}U_{t}(x-tv,v).

Let
\rho
be defined by the comoving relation

\rho(x,v,t)=\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x-tv,v)
.
The pair
(\rho,a)
solves (
4
) with
\rho(0)=f
and

\rho(x,v,1)=\widetilde{\rho}_{1}(x-v,v)=g\big((x-v)+v,v\big)=g(x,v).

The regularity of
a
follows from the smoothness of
(t,x)\mapsto U_{t}(x,\cdot)
in (
10
).
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Besides (
11
), other normalizations are possible, e.g. zero unweighted mean, pointwise anchoring, energy minimization, dynamic consistency. They all select the same acceleration
\widetilde{a}=\nabla_{v}U_{t}
(since constants disappear under
\nabla_{v}
) and thus do not affect
\rho
.

Remark 2.2
.

Theorem
2.1
holds true when
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The proof is exactly the same except for the proof of Lemma
2.3
. In this case one has to consider
exhaustion of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by compact sets
K_{1},\dots,K_{m},\dots
and consider the solution
U_{t}
of the elliptic problem (
7
)–(
8
) for
x\in K_{m}
. Then the potential
U_{t}
can be extended by on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times V
by uniqueness.

Remark 2.3
.

If
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the proof adapts by working in weighted spaces.
Assume
f,g>0
, smooth, with sufficient decay in
v
, and define

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}=(1-t)f(x,v)+t\,g(x+v,v)

bounded from above and from below by positive weights on compact
v
-sets. Solve the weighted elliptic problem

\nabla_{v}\!\cdot(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\nabla_{v}U_{t})=f(x,v)-g(x+v,v)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{v},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,U_{t}\,dv=0,

in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\widetilde{\rho}_{t}\,dv)
using the Lax–Milgram theorem; the right-hand side has zero
v
-mean by the compatibility condition (
6
). Decay at infinity replaces the boundary condition and makes all integrations by parts legitimate. The rest of the argument is identical.

3
Analytical and numerical validation

In this section, we discuss a few examples of application of our framework.
In particular, we consider a periodic setting where the force field
a(\cdot)

is obtained in closed form. These examples are also used to validate our method
numerically.

Example 3.1
.

Consider a simple Gaussian shift. We have
n=1
,
X={\mathbb{R}}
,
V={\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
f(x,v)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(x^{2}+v^{2})}
and

g(x,v)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}((x-v)^{2}+v^{2})}
.
The compatibility condition (
6
) holds, and
g\circ\Phi_{1}=f
, so
a\equiv 0

since free transport suffices.

Example 3.2
.

Consider now a nontrivial Gaussian transport. Let
n=1
,
X={\mathbb{R}}
, and
V={\mathbb{R}}
. Let

f(x,v)=\frac{1}{2\pi\,\sigma_{x}^{f}\sigma_{v}^{f}}\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x-\mu_{x}^{f})^{2}}{2(\sigma_{x}^{f})^{2}}-\frac{(v-\mu_{v}^{f})^{2}}{2(\sigma_{v}^{f})^{2}}\Big),

and consider the target density

g(x,v)=\frac{1}{2\pi\,\sigma_{x}^{g}\sigma_{v}^{g}}\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x-\mu_{x}^{g})^{2}}{2(\sigma_{x}^{g})^{2}}-\frac{(v-\mu_{v}^{g})^{2}}{2(\sigma_{v}^{g})^{2}}\Big),

with

\mu_{x}^{g}:=\mu_{x}^{f}+\mu_{v}^{g},\qquad(\sigma_{x}^{f})^{2}:=(\sigma_{x}^{g})^{2}+(\sigma_{v}^{g})^{2}.

Then, the compatibility condition (
6
) holds

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x,v)\,dv=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_{x}^{f}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-\mu_{x}^{f})^{2}}{2(\sigma_{x}^{f})^{2}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g(x+v,v)\,dv,

since the integral
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}g(x+v,v)\,dv
is a Gaussian in
x
with mean

\mu_{x}^{g}-\mu_{v}^{g}=\mu_{x}^{f}
and variance
(\sigma_{x}^{g})^{2}+(\sigma_{v}^{g})^{2}=(\sigma_{x}^{f})^{2}
.

We can solve the elliptic problem explicitly. In the comoving frame, set

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)=(1-t)\,f(x,v)+t\,g(x+v,v),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad R_{t}(x,v):=f(x,v)-g(x+v,v).

For
V={\mathbb{R}}
with decay at
|v|\to\infty
, the weighted Neumann problem

(\widetilde{\rho}_{t}U_{t}^{\prime})^{\prime}(x,v)=R_{t}(x,v),\ \int\widetilde{\rho}_{t}U_{t}\,dv=0

admits the explicit primitive

U_{t}^{\prime}(x,v)=\frac{F_{t}(x,v)}{\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)},\quad\mbox{ with }\quad F_{t}(x,v):=\int_{-\infty}^{v}R_{t}(x,s)\,ds,

(14)

because
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}R_{t}(x,s)\,ds=0
by compatibility, so the flux

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}U_{t}^{\prime}
vanishes at
\pm\infty
in
v
. Then

U_{t}(x,v)=\int_{0}^{v}\frac{F_{t}(x,s)}{\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,s)}\,ds-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,w)\Big(\int_{0}^{w}\frac{F_{t}(x,s)}{\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,s)}\,ds\Big)dw}{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,w)\,dw}.

Finally,

a(x,v,t)=\nabla_{v}U_{t}(x-tv,v)=\frac{F_{t}(x-tv,v)}{\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x-tv,v)}.

Example 3.3
.

Consider a periodic setting with
X=V=\mathbb{T}={\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\varepsilon,\eta
parameters such that
|\varepsilon|+|\eta|<1
, and define

f(x,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big(1+\varepsilon\cos x\big),\qquad g(x,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big(1+\varepsilon\cos(x-v)+\eta\cos(2v)\big).

Then, the compatibility condition (
6
) holds since

\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(x,v)\,dv=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(1+\varepsilon\cos x\big)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x+v,v)\,dv.

Notice that
g\neq f\circ(\Phi_{1})^{-1}
, hence
f
and
g
cannot be connected by free transport, i.e. by
a=0
.

With the interpolant
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)=(1-t)f(x,v)+t\,g(x+v,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big(1+\varepsilon\cos x+t\,\eta\cos(2v)\big)
,
the Neumann problem has the explicit solution

\partial_{v}U_{t}(x,v)=-\frac{\eta}{2}\,\frac{\sin(2v)}{1+\varepsilon\cos x+t\,\eta\cos(2v)}.

Hence, the acceleration field in original variables is obtained in closed form
as follows

a(x,v,t)=\partial_{v}U_{t}(x-tv,v)=-\frac{\eta}{2}\,\frac{\sin(2v)}{\,1+\varepsilon\cos(x-tv)+t\,\eta\cos(2v)\,}.

The corresponding exact density is given by

\rho(x,v,t)=\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x-tv,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Big(1+\varepsilon\cos(x-tv)+t\,\eta\cos(2v)\Big),

so that
\rho(0)=f
and
\rho(1)=g
. Positivity holds since, by hypothesis,
1-|\varepsilon|-|\eta|>0
.

3.1
Numerical validation

In order to numerically validate the proposed Moser-type construction for the Liouville equation,
we refer to the last example, and compare
\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x-tv,v)
, where

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}(x,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big(1+\varepsilon\cos x+t\,\eta\cos(2v)\big),

with the numerical solution to the Liouville problem

\partial_{t}\rho+v\,\partial_{x}\rho+\partial_{v}(a\rho)=0,\qquad\rho(0)=f.

Here
a
is the acceleration field already computed

a(x,v,t)=-\frac{\eta}{2}\,\frac{\sin(2v)}{\,1+\varepsilon\cos(x-tv)+t\,\eta\cos(2v)\,}.

We solve the Liouville problem numerically
by Strang splitting:

(i)

half-step in
v
for
\rho_{t}+\partial_{v}(a\rho)=0
;

(ii)

full step in
x
for
\rho_{t}+v\partial_{x}\rho=0
;

(iii)

half-step in
v
.

Each subproblem is advanced by a conservative finite-volume update with periodic boundary conditions and MUSCL reconstructions (minmod limiter). The time step satisfies the dual CFL constraint

\Delta t\leq\min\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{CFL}_{x}\,\Delta x}{\max|v|},\;\frac{\mathrm{CFL}_{v}\,\Delta v}{\max_{x,v,t}|a(x,v,t)|}\right),\quad\max|v|=\pi,\quad\max|a|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{2(1-|\varepsilon|-|\eta|)}.

See
[
9
]
for more details about numerical solution
of hyperbolic problems.

We report in Table
1
the results of
a simulation with
\varepsilon=0.3
,
\eta=0.5
,
N_{x}=N_{v}=256
, and
\mathrm{CFL}_{x}=\mathrm{CFL}_{v}=0.8
. The table shows near-perfect mass conservation and small
L^{1}
errors of the computed density against the analytic path at multiple times; the terminal misfit
\|\rho(\cdot,\cdot,1)-g\|_{L^{1}}
coincides with the
t=1
entry.

Conclusions

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first adaptation of Moser’s lemma to the kinetic Liouville equation.
The key novelty is the marginal compatibility condition (
