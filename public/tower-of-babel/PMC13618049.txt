Real-Time Simulation
of Low-Energy Quantum Scattering
with Quantum Circuits

Joshua M. Courtney, Phillip C. Stancil

Abstract

Simulating chemical dynamics such as atomic and molecular
scattering
is a natural target for quantum computation, where conventional (classical)
hardware faces exponential cost. We present a product-formula quantum
algorithm that models single-channel helium–helium scattering
by real-time evolution of a Gaussian wavepacket, requiring no precomputation
of eigenstates. Interaction with the Lennard-Jones potential and centrifugal
barrier is applied unitarily with closed-form circuits, with the evolution
processed directly with error-free quantum circuit compilation. Tracking
the wave function results in converged phase shifts, from which the
deflection function, scattering amplitude, and differential cross
section are calculated. Identical-boson integral cross sections agree
with time-independent Numerov and log-derivative benchmarks to 6.1%,
with a localized residual in s-wave scattering. The
construction realizes any potential of the form ∑

single 1/r term, scales efficiently to multichannel
dynamics, and provides a ground-truth operator for benchmarking approximate
quantum simulation.

Atomic and molecular scattering
underlies reaction rates, spectral intensities, and the comparison
of theory with experiment across chemistry, astrophysics, and nuclear
physics.
,
 Quantum scattering in systems with many
degrees of freedom suffers the curse of dimensionality: the state
space grows exponentially, making the computation of all eigenstates
prohibitive. Subspace methods and semiclassical approximations isolate
specific interactions,
−

 and conventional computing has advanced to high-dimensional
simulation, incorporating neural-network potentials, physics-informed
machine learning, and GPU-accelerated multiconfigurational methods.
−

 These methods, however, remain bounded by the computational complexity
of scattering. Quantum computation is
a natural route to many-body and multichannel interactions,
,
 though chemistry is known as a high-overhead application, demanding
many logical qubits and near-fault-tolerant gate fidelities.
−

 Successes include variational eigensolvers, phase estimation, eigenstate
preparation by rodeo algorithms, and small-scale nonadiabatic dynamics
on analog simulators.
−

 Recent algorithms extract scattering phase shifts by first obtaining
eigenstates on conventional hardware and then evolving in real time.
−

 While this importantly reduces measurement overhead, eigenstate
precomputation remains exponentially complex and leaves chemical scattering
open to future advances in quantum simulation.

Here we evolve
a generic Gaussian wavepacket in real time under
a Trotterized Hamiltonian with no eigenstate precomputation. The effective
potential is composed of a 12–6 Lennard-Jones (LJ) surface
plus a centrifugal term for partial-wave analysis, applied as a closed-form
analytical gate composition on a binary-encoded position register.
We evolve the elastic collision of two ground-state helium atoms on
an error-free statevector emulator built with the Qiskit SDK, extract
phase shifts by spatial Fourier transform with reference subtraction,
and validate against time-independent solutions of the radial Schrödinger
equation by the Numerov and log-derivative methods.

Quantum signal processing offers optimal query complexity
and maps
the operator to a polynomial approximation.
,
 While preferred for the single-channel state, its query count grows
with the Hamiltonian spectral norm, growing unfavorable for the strongly
coupled, multichannel dynamics typical of chemical scattering and
collision systems. Trotterization handles intersection seams invariantly,
with commutator norms decreasing toward zero as diabatic surfaces
cross. Moreover, a general potential
energy surface (PES) is nonsparse, requiring O(2
n
) terms to qubitize the walk operator and
favoring direct encoding on the position register. Thus, Trotterization
remains valuable in quantum simulation despite suboptimal error scaling.

An exact operator enables rigorous, hardware-agnostic
benchmarking
of approximate algorithms. We confirm in the SI that the Hilbert–Schmidt distance between this binary circuit
and an independently derived binary-reflected Gray-code (BRGC) construction
vanishes to numerical precision. Since
the BRGC-encoded PES is provably efficiently approximable and the
binary-to-Gray reflection is O(n), the binary encoding is efficiently approximable as well, though
a concrete binary-basis approximation remains for future work. A validated
ground truth permits variational compression, opening the work to
variational fast-forwarding (for amenable systems), quantum-assisted
compiling, and reinforcement-learning circuit synthesis, optimizing
against operator-level metrics and a distributed T-budget instead
of another approximation.

In summary, we have presented a real-time quantum-circuit
emulation
of an atomic scattering event with no precomputation of eigenstates.
Phase shifts extracted by spatial FFT with reference subtraction reproduce
the deflection function, scattering amplitude, and differential and
integral cross sections of He–He scattering in agreement with
independent Numerov and log-derivative calculations obtained on conventional
hardware, and the Hilbert–Schmidt distance to the BRGC construction
vanishes to numerical precision, giving operator-level validation
without approximation. Practical deployment is currently limited by
rotation-angle precision far below present gate fidelities and by
the long evolution times required for phase-shift extraction,
−

,
 but the closed form bounds these
costs and charts a path to efficient approximation. With the multichannel
extension, this framework moves toward quantum simulation of coupled
potential surfaces beyond the reach of conventional computational
methods.
