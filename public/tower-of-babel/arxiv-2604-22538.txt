Abstract
We study the optimal transport problem on globally hyperbolic spacetimes associated with Orlicz-type Lorentzian cost functions of the form u ∘ ℓ u\circ\ell , where u u is a suitable monotonically increasing and concave function, and ℓ \ell is the time separation. Our work encompasses and generalises the case u ⁡ ( x ) = u p ​ ( x ) = p − 1 ​ x p u(x)=u_{p}(x)=p^{-1}x^{p} for p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) , as well as the more recent p < 0 p<0 , which have been the only examples considered so far in the literature. A fundamental notion for our purposes is the property of u u -separation for a pair of measures, which generalises McCann’s p p -separation and for which we are able to obtain strong duality to the full Orlicz-type optimization problem. In our main results, we characterise timelike Ricci curvature lower bounds via the convexity of the relative entropy along geodesics arising from the Orlicz-type optimal transport with cost u ∘ ℓ u\circ\ell , which is a far-reaching generalization of McCann’s seminal work in the case u = u p u=u_{p} , p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) . Introduction

Orlicz spaces are a generalization of
L^{p}
-spaces, allowing for more general convex functions
u
(usually called
Young functions
in the literature) in the definition of the norm, with
u(x):=x^{p}
reproducing the well-known
L^{p}
-norm. We refer to the textbooks of Rao–Ren
[
27
,
28
]
for a detailed treatment of this classical topic in analysis.

Just like in the case of
L^{p}
-spaces, one can study Wasserstein spaces where the distance arises from an Orlicz-type optimal transport problem, generalising the usual
L^{p}
-based problem in classical Wasserstein geometry. The definition is as follows: Given a compact metric space
X
(for simplicity) and a suitable convex function
u:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, the Orlicz–Wasserstein distance
W_{u}:\mathcal{P}(X)^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is defined via the double infimization problem

W_{u}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X^{2}}u\bigg(\frac{d(x,y)}{\lambda}\bigg)\,d\pi\leq 1\right\},

see e.g. Kuwada
[
18
]
. For
u=u_{p}=|\cdot|^{p}
,
p\geq 1
, this definition reproduces the usual
p
-Wasserstein distance
W_{p}
. An incomplete list of works on Orlicz–Wasserstein spaces includes works of Kuwada on gradient estimates
[
17
,
18
]
, Sturm
[
31
]
on more general classes of functions
u
, Lisini
[
19
]
on absolutely continuous curves and their speed, as well as Kell
[
14
,
15
]
who studied the implication of entropic convexity along Orlicz–Wasserstein geodesics from Ricci curvature lower bounds in the spirit of Lott–Villani
[
20
]
and Sturm
[
29
,
30
]
. Let us remark that
[
14
, Ch. 7]
provides a comprehensive and reader-friendly introduction to Orlicz–Wasserstein spaces.

As far as Lorentzian geometry and signature are concerned, methods of optimal transport have seen a significant rise in popularity over the last decade (see for example the works of Eckstein–Miller
[
11
]
, Suhr
[

]
, Kell–Suhr

). The seminal works of McCann

have established the equivalent characterization of timelike Ricci curvature lower (and, in the case of
[
25
]
, also upper) bounds via convexity properties of the Boltzmann–Shannon entropy, extended later to the Rényi entropy by Braun
[
6
]
and to Finsler spacetimes by Braun–Ohta
[
5
]
. This has led to a fruitful study of non-smooth Lorentzian spaces satisfying timelike Ricci curvature bounds, where powerful theorems from classical smooth spacetime geometry have been established in extremely low regularity (see e.g. Cavalletti–Mondino
[
9
]
, Braun
[
6
,

]
, Braun–McCann
[
4
]
, Beran–Braun–Calisti–Gigli–McCann–Ohanyan–Rott–Sämann
[

). So far, the majority of the literature involving optimal transport in the Lorentzian setting has concerned itself with studies of the
p
-Lorentz-Wasserstein distance

\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}\ell^{p}\,d\pi\right)^{1/p}=u_{p}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int u_{p}\circ\ell\,d\pi\right),

where
M
is either a smooth or a nonsmooth spacetime,
\ell:M^{2}\to\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
is the (extended) time separation function, and
u_{p}(x):=x^{p}/p
for
p\in(0,1]
(the more recent works among those cited above have also considered
p<0
, starting with
[
2
]
). The reason for these choices of
u_{p}
has to do with the concavity properties of the time separation function, much like the choices
u_{p}(x)=|x|^{p}
for
p\geq 1
mirror the convexity properties of the positive definite distance in metric and Riemannian geometry. One may thus conclude that the natural range of
p
for Lorentzian
L^{p}
-theory is
p\in[-\infty,1]
, which has been formalised by Gigli
[
12
]
in the context of hyperbolic Banach spaces. One may wonder whether
p=0
behaves like
p=2
in the positive definite case, i.e. a natural “best” self-dual choice. However, as pointed out in
[
12
, Rem. 5.9]
, there are many equally valid self-dual concave functions
u
besides
u_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\log
.

These observations are motivational for an Orlicz-type approach to Lorentzian optimal transport theory, both to remove the need for an (arbitrary) choice of
p
and for a deeper understanding as to how far known results extend for general (suitable) concave functions
u
. The introduction and study of Orlicz-type optimal transportation problems on smooth globally hyperbolic spacetimes is one of our principal aims in the current work. Our main objective in this context is to extend McCann’s
[
22
]
characterization of timelike Ricci curvature lower bounds via entropic convexity to hold along
\ell_{u}
-geodesics of probability measures for a large class of
u
, which includes in particular all
u_{p}
,
p\in(-\infty,1)
. Let us give an outline of our program in the following, along with the main results.

Definition 1.1

(Admissible function)
.

A function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
which is
C^{2}
, satisfies
u^{\prime}>0
,
u^{\prime\prime}<0
, and
u^{\prime}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is surjective, is called
admissible
.

For many purposes (such as the reverse triangle inequality for
\ell_{u}
), fewer assumptions on
u
are necessary. However, in the technical arguments necessary to study the relationship between Ricci curvature lower bounds and entropic convexity, we found the set of assumptions we have given for an admissible function to be indispensable. Note that, in particular, all
u_{p}
for
p\in(-\infty,1)
,
u_{p}(x)=x^{p}/p
for
p\neq 0
and
u_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\log(x)
, are admissible. A crucial observation is that if
u
is admissible, so is its
concave conjugate

u^{*}(x):=\inf_{y>0}(xy-u(y)).

This allows us to associate to the dual pair
(u,u^{*})
a dual convex Lagrangian–Hamiltonian pair
L(\cdot,x;u):T_{x}M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
H(\cdot,x;u^{*}):T_{x}^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

L(v,x;u):=\begin{cases}-u(|v|_{g}),&v\text{ future,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad H(p,x;u^{*}):=\begin{cases}-u^{*}(|p|_{g}),&p\text{ past,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

Given
u
admissible and
\lambda>0
, we set
u_{\lambda}(x):=u(x/\lambda)
, which is also admissible. In the context of Lorentzian Orlicz theory, we need to allow our admissible functions
u
to be unbounded above and below in general (
u_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\log
provides a natural unbounded example), which creates some complications. For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, let
\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu):=\{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu):(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)_{-}\in L^{1}(\pi)\}\subseteq\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, where we tacitly set
u(-\infty):=-\infty
. The
u
-Lorentz–Orlicz–Wasserstein time separation

\ell_{u}:\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}\to\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
is now defined as follows:
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu):=-\infty
if
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\emptyset
, otherwise

\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu):=\sup\bigg\{\lambda>0:\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi\geq u(1)\bigg\}\cup\{0\}.

In the cases where
u
is bounded above or bounded below, the integration requirement can be ignored, i.e. the supremum can be taken over
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
. The normalization
u(1)
is chosen precisely so that the compatibility
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)=\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)
holds. Under reasonable conditions on
(\mu,\nu)
, we can always find an
optimal
coupling
\pi
, i.e. a coupling that satisfies (with
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
)

\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=\sup_{\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\tilde{\pi}\geq u(1),

see Proposition
2.19
, and this inequality is saturated in many interesting examples. The corresponding dual problem to the one defining
\ell_{u}
is given by

\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\Bigg\{\eta>0\ :\ \inf_{\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu)}\Big\{\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\varphi^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu\Big\}\leq u(1)\Bigg\},

where
\varphi^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}(x):=\sup_{y\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)}u_{\eta}(\ell(x,y))-\varphi(y).
In this generality, the problem of duality is difficult to handle, however we identify a condition we term
u
-separation
(see Definition
2.29
) from which we are able to deduce strong duality (see Theorem
2.30
). Our notion of
u
-separation agrees with McCann’s
p
-separation
[
22
, Def. 4.1]
in the case
u=u_{p}
,
p\in(0,1)
. In the case where
(\mu,\nu)
are
u
-separated and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)
, there exists a unique optimal coupling that is induced by a map of the form
F(x):=\exp_{x}DH(D\varphi(x),x;u_{\lambda}^{*})
, where
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, see Theorem
2.39
. In this case,
\mu_{s}:=(F_{s})_{\#}\mu
, with
F_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,DH(D\varphi(x),x;u_{\lambda}^{*})
, is the unique
u
-geodesic connecting
\mu
to
\nu
, and moreover
\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)
for all
s<1
(see Corollary
2.42
).

Having established these properties of the Orlicz-type transport in Section
2
, which are in analogy to the usual transport for
u_{p}
, we can proceed with the usual Jacobi field computations in order to relate entropic convexity with timelike Ricci curvature bounds (see Section
3
). Before continuing, let
V\in C^{2}(M)
, recall that the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}^{(N,V)}
with
N\in[n,\infty]
(where the case
N=n
means
V=0
by convention; and
N=\infty
is understood in the limit sense) is defined by

\mathrm{Ric}^{(N,V)}:=\mathrm{Ric}+\mathrm{Hess}_{g}(V)+\frac{dV\otimes dV}{N-n},

and the Boltzmann–Shannon entropy
E_{V}:\mathcal{P}(M)\to[-\infty,\infty]
is defined by

E_{V}(\mu):=\int\frac{d\mu}{d\mathfrak{m}}\log\frac{d\mu}{d\mathfrak{m}}\,d\mathfrak{m}

if this expression is well-defined in
[-\infty,\infty]
, and set to
-\infty
otherwise, where
\mathfrak{m}:=e^{-V}\mathrm{vol}_{g}
.
The result of our efforts in Subsection
3.1
and Subsection
3.3
is collected in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Equivalence of timelike Ricci bounds and entropic convexity along
u
-geodesics)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime,
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
admissible,
V\in C^{2}(M)
,
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in[n,\infty]
, and set
\mathfrak{m}:=e^{-V}\mathrm{vol}_{g}
.

(i)

If
\mathrm{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v,v)\geq K\,g(v,v)
for every timelike
v\in TM
, then for every
u
-separated and absolutely continuous pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)^{2}
, letting
\mu_{s}
be the unique
u
-geodesic from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
and
\pi
the unique optimal coupling,
e(s):=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
satisfies

e^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime})^{2}\geq K\|\ell\|_{L^{2}(\pi)}

in the distributional sense. If moreover
K\geq 0
, then in fact

e^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime})^{2}\geq K\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v,v)<K\,g(v,v)
for some
v\in T_{x}M
, then, supported in any arbitrarily small neighborhood of
x
, one may find a pair of measures
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)^{2}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{0})\times\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{1})\subseteq\{\ell>0\}
such that
e(s)=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
is
C^{2}
and satisfies

e^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime})^{2}<K\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}.

In Subsection
3.2
, we relax the assumption of
u
-separation to obtain a weak entropic convexity inequality for a larger class of measures, in analogy with McCann
[
22
, Sec. 7]
. The main difference between our setting and McCann’s is that we make several technical assumptions which are implied by McCann’s assumption of attainment and finiteness of the infimum in the Kantorovich duality problem, cf. Remark
3.15
, in the case
u=u_{p}
,
p\in(0,1)
. The precise result we obtain is the following:

Theorem 1.3

(Weak convexity from timelike lower Ricci curvature bounds for more general measures)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Fix
V\in C^{2}(M)
bounded,
N\in[n,\infty]
, and
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
admissible, by convention
V=0
if
N=n
. If
\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v,v)\geq Kg(v,v)\geq 0
holds in every timelike direction
v\in TM
, then for the set
Q\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{ac}(M)^{2}
of measures
(\mu,\nu)
such that

(i)

\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
,

(ii)

there exist lower semicontinuous functions
a,b:M\to\mathbb{R}
with
a\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
b\in L^{1}(\nu)
such that
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\leq a\oplus b
on
\mathrm{supp}(\mu\times\nu)
, and

(iii)

there exists an optimal coupling
\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
with
\ell>0
holding
\pi
-a.e. which satisfies
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=u(1)
,

weak entropic convexity holds in the following sense: For each
(\mu,\nu)\in Q
there exists a
u
-geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{0\leq s\leq 1}
with
\mu_{0}=\mu
,
\mu_{1}=\nu
, such that the relative entropy
e(s):=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
satisfies

e^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime})^{2}\geq K\,\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)^{2}

in the distributional sense.

2.1
The Lorentz distance and admissible functions

We start by mentioning some well-known properties of the Lorentz distance, cf. McCann
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Singularities of the Lorentz distance)
.

We say a pair
(x,y)\in M^{2}
is
not singular
is
\ell(x,y)>0
and
x
and
y
both lie in the relative interior of some affinely parametrised proper-time maximising geodesic segment. In any other case, we say
(x,y)
are
singular
, and we write
(x,y)\in\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Midpoint continuity away from cut locus,
[
22
, Lem. 2.4]
)
.

Let
(x,y)\in M^{2}\setminus\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
(which is an open subset of
M^{2}
), and fix
s\in[0,1]
. Then there is a unique point
z=z_{s}(x,y)\in M
such that

\ell(x,z)=s\,\ell(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(z,y)=(1-s)\,\ell(x,y).

Moreover,
z
depends smoothly on
(s,x,y)\in[0,1]\times(M^{2}\setminus\operatorname{sing}(\ell))
.

Lemma 2.3

(Midpoint sets inherit compactness,
[
22
, Lem. 2.5]
)
.

Given
S\subset M^{2}
and
s\in[0,1]
, let

Z_{s}(S):=\bigcup_{(x,y)\in S}Z_{s}(x,y),

where

Z_{s}(x,y):=\Big\{z\in M:\ \ell(x,z)=s\,\ell(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(z,y)=(1-s)\,\ell(x,y)\Big\}

if
\ell(x,y)\geq 0
, and
Z_{s}(x,y)=\emptyset
otherwise. If
S
is precompact, then
Z_{s}(S):=\cup_{s\in[0,1]}Z_{s}(S)
is precompact. If, in addition,
S
is compact, then
Z(S)
and
Z_{s}(S)
are compact.

Lemma 2.4

(Selecting midpoints on the timelike cut locus,
[
22
, Lem. 2.8]
)
.

In the conditions of Lemma
2.2
, the maps
z_{s}
can be measurably extended to
\{\ell>0\}
by
\bar{z}_{s}
, satisfying that if
(x,y)\in\{\ell>0\}
, then
s\in[0,1]\mapsto\bar{z}_{s}(x,y)
is a proper-time maximising geodesic segment joining
x
to
y
.

For the next result, recall that the set of subgradients of a function

at

, denoted

is the set of

such that

and

u(\exp^{\tilde{g}}_{x}v)\geq u(x)+p[v]+o(|v|_{\tilde{g}})

for small enough

. This definition is easily seen to be independent of the background Riemannian metric

. The reversed inequality is characteristic of supergradients, denoted

.

(Smoothness of time separation,

The time separation

(b)

,

(c)

smooth precisely on the complement of the closed set

(d)

I.

Moreover, if

We now introduce the class of functions for which we will study the Orlicz-type Lorentzian optimal transport problem.

Definition 2.7

(Admissible function)
.

A function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
that is
C^{2}
, satisfies
u^{\prime}>0
,
u^{\prime\prime}<0
, and
u^{\prime}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is surjective, will be called
admissible
.

Note that an admissible function has well defined values
u(0)
and
u(\infty)
(possibly
-\infty
and
+\infty
, respectively). The convention
u(-\infty):=-\infty
will be used in some places.

Lemma 2.8

(Concave conjugate)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible. Then its
concave conjugate

u^{*}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

u^{*}(x):=\inf_{y>0}(xy-u(y))

is also admissible and satisfies
(u^{*})^{\prime}=(u^{\prime})^{-1}
, i.e.,
(u^{*})^{\prime}\circ u^{\prime}=u^{\prime}\circ(u^{*})^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}
on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover,
(u^{*})^{*}=u
.

Proof.

Clearly, the definition of admissibility gives that
u^{\prime}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism. For fixed
x>0
, the function
(0,\infty)\ni y\mapsto xy-u(y)
is strictly convex and
C^{2}
, its unique minimum is given by
y=(u^{\prime})^{-1}(x)
. Thus,

u^{*}(x)=x\,(u^{\prime})^{-1}(x)-u((u^{\prime})^{-1}(x))

is clearly
C^{1}
. An elementary calculation, using the above formula, yields
(u^{*})^{\prime}=(u^{\prime})^{-1}
, showing that
u^{*}
is
C^{2}
. The remaining properties of admissible functions also easily follow from this relationship between the first derivatives. Finally, to see that
(u^{*})^{*}=u
, observe that
(u^{*})^{*}\geq u
, and, for given
y>0
and
x:=u^{\prime}(y)
, we have

u(y)=xy-u^{*}(x)\geq(u^{*})^{*}(y),

from which we can conclude
u=(u^{*})^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.9

(On the definition of
u
)
.

(i)

Without the assumption of surjectivity of
u^{\prime}
, the concave conjugate
u^{*}
need not be admissible: For example, consider
u(x)=-e^{-x}
for
x>0
. Then
u^{\prime}(x)=e^{-x}>0
,
u^{\prime\prime}(x)=u(x)<0
, and
u^{\prime}
is bounded above by
1
. Thus, if
x>1
, then
y\mapsto xy-u(y)
is strictly increasing, so that

u^{*}(x)=\inf_{y>0}(xy-u(y))=u(0)=1.

In particular,
u^{*}
is neither strictly increasing nor strictly concave. For an example with
u^{\prime}
bounded below, consider for given
c>0
the function
u(x)=cx+\log(x)
which satisfies
u^{\prime}(x)=c+x^{-1}\geq c>0
and
u^{\prime\prime}(x)=-x^{-2}<0
. Then for any
0<x<c
,
y\mapsto xy-u(y)
is strictly decreasing, so that

u^{*}(x)=\inf_{y>0}(xy-u(y))=\lim_{y\to\infty}-(c-x)y-\log(y)=-\infty.

Thus, in this case,
u^{*}
is not even finite-valued.

(ii)

For many “synthetic-geometric” purposes, such as the triangle inequality of the time separation
\ell_{u}
on
\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
(see Proposition
2.22
below), it suffices to assume
u^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
and even
u^{\prime}\geq 0
, one may even weaken the differentiability assumption on
u
or drop it entirely (
u
, under any of these reduced assumptions, may reasonably be called
weakly admissible
). However, since we will be interested in the connection of entropic convexity and timelike Ricci curvature lower bounds, (strictly) convex Hamiltonian–Lagrangian duality (see Proposition
2.11
below) will be an essential technical tool, which on the other hand crucially relies on strict concavity of
u
and the fact that
u^{*}
is admissible.

Example 2.10

(Admissible functions)
.

The main examples of admissible functions considered so far in the literature are

\displaystyle u_{p}(x):=\frac{x^{p}}{p},\quad 0\neq p<1,

\displaystyle u_{0}(x):=\frac{1}{2}+\log(x),

for
p\in(0,1)
cf.
[
22
,
25
,
9
,
6
,
4
]
, for the more recent
p<0
see
[
2
,
7
,
12
]
.
They satisfy
u_{p}^{*}=u_{q}
, where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
(
q=0
if
p=0
). It is sometimes useful to consider the following shifted versions:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{p}(x):=\frac{x^{p}-1}{p},\quad 0\neq p<1,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{0}(x):=\log(x).

In this case, the joint assignment
(-\infty,1)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
(p,x)\mapsto\bar{u}_{p}(x)
is smooth.

The following is an analogue of the Lagrangian–Hamiltonian duality obtained by McCann in the case
u=u_{p}
for
p\in(0,1)
, cf.
[
22
, Lem. 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.11

(General convex Lagrangian–Hamiltonian duality)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible, denote by
u^{*}
its admissible concave conjugate. Fix a point
x\in M
. Define the
u
-Lagrangian
L(\cdot,x;u):T_{x}M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
via

L(v,x;u):=\begin{cases}-u(|v|_{g}),&v\text{ future causal,}\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Also, define the corresponding
u^{*}
-Hamiltonian
H(\cdot,x;u):T_{x}^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
via

H(p,x;u^{*}):=\begin{cases}-u^{*}(|p|_{g^{*}}),&p\text{ past causal},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
L(\cdot,x;u)
and
H(\cdot,x;u^{*})
are convex on
T_{x}M
resp.
T^{*}_{x}M
,
C^{2}
and strictly convex in the interior of the future lightcone in
T_{x}M
resp. the interior of the past lightcone in
T_{x}^{*}M
. Moreover,
H
is the Legendre–Fenchel convex conjugate of
L
(and vice versa) in the sense that

\displaystyle H(p,x;u^{*})

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in T_{x}M}(p(v)-L(v,x;u)),

\displaystyle L(v,x;u)

\displaystyle=\sup_{p\in T_{x}^{*}M}(p(v)-H(p,x;u^{*})).

Consequently,
DH=(DL)^{-1}
is a linear isomorphism from the past causal cone in
T_{x}^{*}M
into the future causal cone in
T_{x}M
.

Proof.

This follows from the same arguments used by McCann in the case of
u=u_{p}
and
u^{*}=u_{q}

[
22
, Lem. 3.1]
, using the fact that
(u^{*})^{\prime}=(u^{\prime})^{-1}
holds generally (cf. Lemma
2.8
) in conjunction with the following exact forms of
DL,DH,D^{2}L,D^{2}H
at future timelike vectors
v\in T_{x}M
resp. past timelike covectors
p\in T^{*}_{x}M
:

\displaystyle DL(v,x;u)=-u^{\prime}(|v|_{g})\frac{v_{*}}{|v|_{g}},\quad DH(p,x;u^{*})=-(u^{*})^{\prime}(|p|_{g})\frac{p_{*}}{|p|_{g}},

\displaystyle D^{2}L(v,x;u)=-\frac{u^{\prime}(|v|_{g})}{|v|_{g}}g+(u^{\prime}(|v|_{g})-|v|_{g}u^{\prime\prime}(|v|_{g}))\frac{v_{*}\otimes v_{*}}{|v|_{g}^{3}},

\displaystyle D^{2}H(p,x;u^{*})=-\frac{(u^{*})^{\prime}(|p|_{g})}{|p|_{g}}g+((u^{*})^{\prime}(|p|_{g})-|p|_{g}(u^{*})^{\prime\prime}(|p|_{g}))\frac{p_{*}\otimes p_{*}}{|p|_{g}^{3}}.

Here,
v_{*}=g(v,\cdot)\in T_{x}^{*}M
,
p_{*}=g(p,\cdot)\in T_{x}M
, and we use
g
to denote both the scalar product on
T_{x}M
and on
T_{x}^{*}M
.
∎

Corollary 2.12

(Twist and non-degeneracy)
.

For an admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,

(i)

u\circ\ell:M^{2}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
inherits properties (a)-(d)(I) of Theorem
2.5
from
\ell
;

(ii)

If there exists a supergradient
w\in T_{x}^{*}M
of
u\circ\ell(\cdot,y)
at the point
x
and
\ell(x,y)>0
, then
v=DH(w,x;u^{*})
is the unique vector such that
y=\exp_{x}(v)
;

(iii)

If
(x,y)\in\{\ell>0\}\cap\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
, then

\sup_{0<|w|_{\tilde{g}}<1}\frac{u\circ\ell\left(\exp^{\tilde{g}}_{x}w,y\right)+u\circ\ell\left(\exp^{\tilde{g}}_{x}(-w),y\right)-2u\circ\ell(x,y)}{2|w|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}}=+\infty;

(2.1)

(iv)

If
(x,y)\notin\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
, then
\det\frac{\partial^{2}(u\circ\ell)}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{i}}\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i)

The properties (a), (b) and (c) are immediate given that
u
is
C^{2}
and extendible to a continuous function
u:[0,\infty)\to[-\infty,+\infty)
.

To prove the inheritance of local semiconvexity from
\ell
, we work with the following characterisation of the property: a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
has semiconvexity constant
C
near
\bar{x}
if there exists
p\in\partial_{\cdot}f(\bar{x})
such that

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+p(x-\bar{x})-\frac{1}{2}C|x-\bar{x}|^{2}+O(|x-\bar{x}|^{2})

holds for all
x
in a small enough neighbourhood of
\bar{x}
. The idea now is to evaluate
u
with the elements of this inequality, which will be preserved since
u^{\prime}>0
. To obtain a similar expression for
u\circ f
, we use the Taylor expansion of
u
around a point:

u(\bar{y}+y)=u(\bar{y})+u^{\prime}(\bar{y})\,(y-\bar{y})+\frac{1}{2}u^{\prime\prime}(\bar{y})\,|y-\bar{y}|^{2}.

Bringing everything together, we conclude that
u\circ f
has at
\bar{x}
semiconvexity constant
Cu^{\prime}(f(\bar{x}))-2u^{\prime\prime}(f(\bar{x}))|p|^{2}
. Applying this argument in Riemannian normal coordinates gives the local semiconvexity of the function
u\circ\ell(\cdot,y)
at each point
\bar{x}
with
\ell(\bar{x},y)>0
.

From this, the fact that
u\circ\ell
is locally Lipschitz follows immediately, since locally bounded convex functions are locally Lipschitz.

(ii)

Suppose
u\circ\ell(\cdot,y)
admits
w\in T^{*}_{x}M
as a supergradient. Using the one-sided chain rule
[
21
, Lem. 5]
, one can obtain that
\ell(\cdot,y)
admits

\frac{1}{u^{\prime}\left(u^{-1}(u(\ell(x,y)))\right)}w=\frac{1}{u^{\prime}\left(\ell(x,y)\right)}w

as a supergradient at
x\in M
. Since the
\ell(\cdot,y)
is always subdifferentiable on
I^{-}(y)
by Theorem
2.5
, we conclude that it is thus differentiable at
x
, whence
w
is unique. Using the chain rule and noting that
\ell(x,y)=|\exp^{-1}_{x}y|_{g}
, we obtain

w=d(u\circ\ell(\cdot,y))(x)=u^{\prime}(|\exp^{-1}_{x}y|)\,\frac{-\exp_{x}^{-1}y}{|\exp_{x}^{-1}y|_{g}}=DL(\exp_{x}^{-1}y;u).

(2.2)

An application of
DH(\cdot,x;u^{*})
to this equation gives the claim.

(iii)

If (
2.1
) does not hold, this would imply that the function
f(\cdot):=u\circ\ell(\cdot,y)
has semiconcavity constant
C>0
at some
\bar{x}
with
(\bar{x},y)\in\{\ell>0\}\cap\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
. This would now imply that
u^{-1}\circ f
has semiconcavity constant
\tilde{C}
at
\bar{x}
, contradicting
[
22
, Thm. 3.5]
. Therefore (
2.1
) must hold.

(iv)

This is simply a consequence of convexity of
L
on the future timelike cone, upon differentiating (
2.2
) with respect to
y
.

∎

2.2
The
\ell_{u}
-optimal transport problem for admissible functions
u

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible. Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, we write
\Pi(\mu,\nu):=\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(M^{2}):(pr_{1})_{\#}\pi=\mu,\,(pr_{2})_{\#}\pi=\nu\}
for the set of couplings between
\mu
and
\nu
,
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu):\pi(M^{2}_{\leq})=1\}
for the set of causal couplings, as well as

\Pi_{\leq}^{u}(\mu,\nu):=\{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu):(u\circ\ell)_{-}\in L^{1}(\pi)\}\subseteq\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)

for the set of
u
-compatible causal couplings
between
\mu
and
\nu
. While the integrability condition
(u\circ\ell)_{-}\in L^{1}(\pi)
already forces
\pi(M^{2}_{\leq})=1
, we leave the subscript “
\leq
” in the notation for clarity. Note that if
u
is bounded below, the integrability condition is trivially satisfied. Also, note that for any
\lambda>0
also
u_{\lambda}(x):=u(x/\lambda)
is admissible.

Definition 2.14

(
u
-Lorentz–Orlicz–Wasserstein time separation)
.

We define the
u
-Lorentz–Orlicz–Wasserstein time separation

\ell_{u}:\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}\to[0,\infty)\cup\{-\infty\}
as follows: set
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu):=-\infty
if
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\emptyset
, otherwise

\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu):=\sup\bigg\{\lambda>0:\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u(\lambda^{-1}\ell(x,y))\,d\pi(x,y)\geq u(1)\bigg\}\cup\{0\}.

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, a coupling
\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
is said to be
optimal
if

\int_{M^{2}}u\left(\frac{\ell(x,y)}{\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)}\right)\,d\pi(x,y)\geq u(1).

(2.3)

Remark 2.15

(On
\ell_{u}
)
.

Observe that the set of eligible
\lambda>0
in the definition of
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
is the following: It is the set of those
\lambda>0
for which
\Pi_{\leq}^{u_{\lambda}}(\mu,\nu)\neq\emptyset
, and the given integral inequality holds. If there is no eligible
\lambda>0
of this type while
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)\neq\emptyset
, then
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=0
by definition. Also note that since
u(\ell(x,y)/\lambda)\leq u(\ell(x,y)/\lambda^{\prime})
whenever
\ell(x,y)\geq 0
and
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
, it follows that whenever
(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)_{-}\in L^{1}(\pi)
for some
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, then also
(u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell)_{-}\in L^{1}(\pi)
. We conclude that
\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)\subseteq\Pi^{u_{\lambda^{\prime}}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
for all
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
whenever
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
.

Lemma 2.16

(Simplifications for the sets
\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}
)
.

Let
u
be admissible.

(i)

If
u
is bounded below, then for every
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
and every
\lambda>0
,

\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu).

(ii)

If
u
is bounded above, then for every
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, if
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)>-\infty
, then

\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\bigg\{\lambda>0:\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u(\lambda^{-1}\ell(x,y))\,d\pi(x,y)\geq u(1)\bigg\}\cup\{0\}.

(iii)

If
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
are such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
is compact and contained in
\{\ell>0\}
, then for every
\lambda>0

\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

The claims in (i) and (iii) are elementary to check. For (ii), note that if
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)\setminus\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, by the boundedness of
u
from above we have that

\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=-\infty<u(1),

so taking the supremum over
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
does not change
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
.
∎

The following is easily checked.

Lemma 2.17

(Constant shifts)
.

For any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
and admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, also
u+c
is admissible and

\ell_{u+c}=\ell_{u}.

Example 2.18

(
\ell_{u}=\ell_{p}
for
u=u_{p}
)
.

In the Lorentzian optimal transport literature (see e.g.
[
22
,
25
,
9
,
6
]
), the most common function
u
which is considered is
u=u_{p}
for
p\in(0,1)
(more recently, also
p<0
, see e.g.
[
2
,
7
]
). For
0\neq p<1
, classically, the
p
-Lorentz-Wasserstein time separation
\ell_{p}:\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}\to\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
is defined as
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu):=-\infty
if
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\emptyset
, otherwise

\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu):=u_{p}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\circ\ell\,d\pi\right),

see
[
2
, Def. 2.12]
, where
u_{p}
is understood to be extended to
u_{p}(0)
and
u_{p}(\infty)
(and, accordingly, also
u_{p}^{-1}
is extended). We wish to show that
\ell_{u_{p}}=\ell_{p}
for
0\neq p<1
.

(i)

First, suppose
p\in(0,1)
. In this case
u_{p}
is bounded below, so that
\Pi^{(u_{p})_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
for every
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
. For simplicity, let
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)<+\infty
, the case
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)=+\infty
can easily be handled by taking limits. We suppose first that
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)>0
. Then for every
0<\lambda<\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)
, we have that (using the
p
-homogeneity of
u_{p}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\left(\frac{\ell(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\,d\pi(x,y)\geq u_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}(\ell(x,y))\,d\pi(x,y)\geq u_{p}(\lambda).

Inverting
u_{p}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lambda\leq u_{p}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\circ\ell\,d\pi\right)=\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu).

Taking a supremum over
\lambda<\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)
, we thus conclude that
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)\leq\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)
. In particular, also
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)>0
since we assumed
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)>0
. To prove the reverse inequality, observe that
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)
is itself among the eligible
\lambda
in the definition of
\ell_{u_{p}}
. Indeed, for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\left(\frac{\ell(x,y)}{\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)}\right)\,d\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{p}}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}(\ell(x,y))\,d\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\frac{1}{u_{p}(\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu))}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}(\ell(x,y))\,d\pi(x,y).

Taking the supremum over
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, the right hand side becomes
1/p=u(1)
, as claimed. Hence,
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)
and thus equality must hold.

Now, suppose that
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)=0
. Then also
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)=0
, since otherwise the calculation above shows that
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)>0
is among the eligible
\lambda
in the definition of
\ell_{u_{p}}
, from which
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)>0
would follow.

(ii)

Now we consider the case
p<0
. Note that due to
u_{p}(\lambda^{-1}\ell(x,y))=\lambda^{-p}\,u_{p}(\ell(x,y))
for every
\lambda>0
, we have that
\Pi_{\leq}^{(u_{p})_{\lambda}}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi^{u_{p}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
for every
\lambda>0
. Moreover, for
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)\setminus\Pi^{u_{p}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, given that
u_{p}<0
, we have that
\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\circ\ell\,d\pi=-\infty
, so that these couplings may be omitted in the definition of
u_{p}
:

\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)=u_{p}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{u_{p}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{p}\circ\ell\,d\pi\right).

Having made this observation, the compatibility
\ell_{u_{p}}(\mu,\nu)=\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)
is checked precisely as in the case
p\in(0,1)
in (i).

(iii)

Let us remark on the case
p=0
. Here,
u_{0}=\log
, and
\log(\ell(x,y)/\lambda)=\log(\ell(x,y))-\log(\lambda)
for every
\lambda>0
, so that for given
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
we have

\int_{M^{2}}\log(\ell(x,y)/\lambda)\,d\pi=\int_{M^{2}}\log(\ell(x,y))\,d\pi-\log(\lambda).

It follows that
\Pi^{(u_{0})_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi^{u_{0}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
for every
\lambda>0
and
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
. While a definition of
\ell_{0}
(analogous to
\ell_{p}
for
0\neq p<1
) has not been given in the literature so far, it is reasonable to define it as
\ell_{0}(\mu,\nu):=-\infty
if
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\emptyset
, and otherwise

\ell_{0}(\mu,\nu):=u_{0}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{u_{0}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{0}\circ\ell\,d\pi\right)=\exp\left(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{\log}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}\log\circ\ell\,d\pi\right).

As in the cases
0\neq p<1
, one readily checks that
\ell_{0}=\ell_{u_{0}}
. In fact, the normalization
u(1)
in the definition of
\ell_{u}
was chosen precisely in order for these compatibilities to be true (and is fairly arbitrary otherwise).

(iv)

The case
p=1
can also be covered by
\ell_{u}
if one weakens the definition of admissible function to only require
u^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
instead of
<0
. However, since we our primary interest in this paper is to characterise timelike Ricci curvature bounds via entropic convexity, strict concavity of
u
will be essential, so we require it throughout for simplicity.

The following gives a basic result on the existence of optimal couplings and their properties.

Proposition 2.19

(Existence of optimal couplings)
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose there are lower semicontinuous functions
a,b:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
a\in L^{1}(\mu),\,b\in L^{1}(\nu)
, and for some
\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
, we have
u_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\ell(x,y))\leq a\oplus b(x,y):=a(x)+b(y)
on
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
. Then for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
and any
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\geq\lambda^{\prime}
,
(u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell)_{+}\in L^{1}(\pi)
. In particular, in this case
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
can be written as

\lambda=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{\lambda^{\prime\prime}\geq\lambda^{\prime}:\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi\geq u(1)\right\}

(2.4)

and there exists an optimal coupling
\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
. Moreover, for any optimal coupling
\pi
such that
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
is bounded below on
\mathrm{supp}(\pi)
(this is for example the case if
u
is bounded below or
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
), equality holds in the sense that

\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=\sup_{\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\tilde{\pi}=u(1).

Finally, if
u(0)=-\infty
, then any optimal coupling
\pi
is concentrated on
\{\ell>0\}
.

Proof.

The claim about the integrability of
(u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell)
is trivial, since
u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell\leq u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell
whenever
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. From this, it is easily seen that one can rewrite
\ell_{u}
in the form (
2.4
), since any
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)\setminus\Pi^{u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
integrates
u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell
to
-\infty
and thus contributes nothing to the supremum.

It remains to show that there exists an optimal coupling. To this end, pick
\lambda_{k}\uparrow\lambda
, and couplings
\pi_{k}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{M^{2}}u_{\lambda_{k}}\circ\ell\,d\pi_{k}\geq u(1)-\frac{1}{k}.

Since
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
is compact, up to subsequences,
(\pi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
narrowly converges to some coupling
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
. Fix now
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda)
. Then, since
u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell-a\oplus b
is upper semicontinuous and bounded above (by
0
), using the inverse monotonicity of
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\mapsto u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell
, we have (using the narrow convergence
\pi_{k}\to\pi
)

\displaystyle\int u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell-a\oplus b\,d\pi

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int u_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\circ\ell-a\oplus b\,d\pi_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int u_{\lambda_{k}}\circ\ell-a\oplus b\,d\pi_{k}

\displaystyle\geq u(1)-\int_{M}a\,d\mu-\int_{M}b\,d\nu.

Adding
\int a\,d\mu+\int b\,d\nu
to both sides and using the monotone convergence theorem for the limit
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\uparrow\lambda
, we are able to conclude

\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi\geq u(1).

Thus,
\pi
is optimal. To show that equality must hold under the additional assumption that
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
is bounded below on
\mathrm{supp}(\pi)
, observe that for every
\delta>0
,

\int u_{\lambda+\delta}\circ\ell\,d\pi<u(1).

The family
(u_{\lambda+\delta}\circ\ell)_{1\geq\delta>0}
is uniformly bounded below on
\mathrm{supp}(\pi)
, thus taking the limit
\delta\downarrow 0
and an application of the monotone convergence theorem shows that
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi\leq u(1)
, hence equality must hold. Finally, observe that if
\pi
is optimal and
u(0)=-\infty
, then necessarily
\pi(\{\ell>0\})=1
since otherwise
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=-\infty=u(0)<u(1)
, contradicting optimality.
∎

Proposition 2.22

(Reverse triangle inequality)
.

Let

If either term on the right hand side of (

Assume first that

Set

. Fix

and let

\pi_{12}
and
\pi_{23}
to obtain a probability measure

\displaystyle u\left(\frac{\ell(x,z)}{\tau}\right)\geq u\left(\frac{\lambda}{\tau}\frac{\ell(x,y)}{\lambda}+\frac{\eta}{\tau}\frac{\ell(y,z)}{\eta}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda}{\tau}\,u\left(\frac{\ell(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)+\frac{\eta}{\tau}\,u\left(\frac{\ell(y,z)}{\eta}\right).

(2.7)

Taking the supremum over couplings in

\ell_{u}(\mu_{1},\mu_{3})\geq\tau
, and since

and
\eta
can be chosen arbitrarily close to

and
\ell_{u}(\mu_{2},\mu_{3})

If, for example,
\ell_{u}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=0

\eta>0
such that
\ell_{u}(\mu_{2},\mu_{3})>\eta

\lambda=0
and
\tau:=\eta
. Then, in (

\displaystyle u\left(\frac{\ell(x,z)}{\tau}\right)\geq u\left(\frac{\ell(y,z)}{\eta}\right),

where we have used the reverse triangle inequality for

and the monotonicity of

. In the same way as above, integrating this inequality with respect to
\pi_{123}

If both

and

Finally, if

Then, arguing as above,

Definition 2.24

(
u\circ\ell
- cyclical monotonicity)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function. A subset
\Gamma\subseteq M^{2}_{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}u(\ell(x_{i},y_{i}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}u(\ell(x_{i+1},y_{i})),

-optimal and

\partial_{(u\circ\ell)}\varphi\subset(U\times V)\cap M^{2}_{\leq}
is defined by

\partial_{(u\circ\ell)}\varphi:=\big\{(x,y)\in(U\times V)\cap M^{2}_{\leq}\ :\ \varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(y)+\varphi(x)=u\circ\ell(x,y)\big\}.

We will often require these notions for
u_{\lambda}
, where
u
is admissible and
\lambda>0
, and will speak of
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-convex functions
, the
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-transform
and the
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-subdifferential
.

Lemma 2.27
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function, and fix a timelike proper-time maximising segment
s\in[0,1]\mapsto x_{s}\in M
. For each
s\in(0,1)
and
x\in M
, we have

u(\ell(x,x_{1}))\geq s\,u_{s}\left(\ell(x,x_{s})\right)+(1-s)\,u\left(\ell(x_{0},x_{1})\right),

(2.9)

with equality if and only if
x=x_{0}
.

Proof.

Given
x,x_{s},x_{1}\in M
, we use the reverse triangle inequality of
\ell
to obtain:

\ell(x,x_{1})\geq s\,\frac{\ell(x,x_{s})}{s}+(1-s)\,\frac{\ell(x_{s},x_{1})}{1-s},

with equality only if
x_{s}
lies on the maximising segment joining
x
to
x_{1}
. Note that the segment from
x_{s}
to
x_{1}
is indeed, since
x_{s}
being internal to the maximising segment joining
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
forces
(x_{s},x_{1})\notin\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, the geodesic joining
x_{s}
and
x_{1}
is unique. Now, monotonicity and strict concavity of
u
yield (
2.9
) (using that
\ell(x_{s},x_{1})=(1-s)\,\ell(x_{0},x_{1})
), with equality forcing
\frac{\ell(x,x_{s})}{s}=\frac{\ell(x_{s},x_{1})}{1-s}
. The equation is uniquely solved on the geodesic in question by
x=x_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.28

(Star-shapedness of
u\circ\ell
-convex functions)
.

Let
U,V\subset M
be compact, and let
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}
be a
u\circ\ell
-convex function relatively to
(U,V)
. Then, for
t\in(0,1]
,
t^{-1}\varphi
is a
u_{t}\circ\ell
-convex function relatively to
(U,Z_{t}(U,V))
.

Proof.

For
t=1
, there is nothing to prove. For the rest, we follow the strategy of
[
10
, Lem. 5.1]
and
[
15
, Lem. A.9]
.

Fix
t\in(0,1)
and
y\in V
. For
x\mapsto u(\ell(x,y))
, we claim that the following representation holds for
m\in U
:

t^{-1}\,u(\ell(m,y))=\sup_{z\in Z_{t}(U,y)}\bigg\{u_{t}\left(\ell(m,z)\right)+\sup_{\{x\in U\,:\,z\in Z_{t}(x,y)\}}\Big\{t^{-1}\,(1-t)\,u\left(\ell(x,y)\right)\Big\}\bigg\}.

(2.10)

Indeed, from Lemma
2.27
, “
\geq
” holds for any
z\in Z_{t}(U,y)
. Moreover, if
\ell(m,y)\geq 0
, choosing
x=m
saturates (
2.9
). If
\ell(m,y)=-\infty
, by convention
u(\ell(m,y))=-\infty
, and the set on the right hand side is empty, and therefore this is
-\infty
. Thus, the representation holds.

Note that (
2.10
) implies, in particular, that the function
m\in U\mapsto t^{-1}\,u(\ell(m,y))
is the
u_{t}\circ\ell
-transform of the function

\psi(z)=-\sup_{\{x\in U\,:\,z\in Z_{t}(x,y)\}}\Big\{t^{-1}\,(1-t)\,u\left(\ell(x,y)\right)\Big\},

and therefore it is
u_{t}\circ\ell
-convex relative to
(U,Z_{t}(U,y))
, and we conclude that each
m\in U\mapsto t^{-1}\,u(\ell(m,y))
is
u_{t}\circ\ell
-convex relative to
(U,Z_{t}(U,V))
. The latter is true because, if we choose
z\in Z_{t}(U,V)
such that
z\notin Z_{t}(U,y)
, the second term on the right hand side of (
2.10
) is
-\infty
, and therefore can be neglected when computing the supremum over all
z\in Z_{t}(U,V)
.

Now, let
\varphi
be an arbitrary
u\circ\ell
-convex function. It holds that
\varphi=\left(\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}\right)^{(u\circ\ell)}
, hence

t^{-1}\,\varphi(x)=\sup_{y\in V}t^{-1}\,u(\ell(x,y))-t^{-1}\,\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(y).

But every function of the form

t^{-1}\,u(\ell(x,y))-t^{-1}\,\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(y)

is
u_{t}\circ\ell
-convex relative to
(U,Z_{t}(U,V))
. Indeed, this follows by rewriting
t^{-1}\,u(\ell(x,y))
using the claim (
2.10
). Therefore, since
\varphi(U)\subset\mathbb{R}
, the supremum of these
u\circ\ell
-convex functions relative to
(U,Z_{t}(U,V))
is also
u_{t}\circ\ell
-convex relative to
(U,Z_{t}(U,V))
, which yields the result.
∎

Let us now introduce the optimization problem which is dual to that of determining
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
:

\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\Bigg\{\eta>0\ :\ \inf_{\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu):\varphi^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\in L^{1}(\nu)}\Big\{\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\varphi^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu\Big\}\leq u(1)\Bigg\}.

(2.11)

Here,
\varphi^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}(y):=\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)}u_{\eta}(\ell(x,y))-\varphi(x)
,
y\in M
. For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
c:=\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, a function
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mu)
is a
Kantorovich potential
if
\varphi^{(u_{c}\circ\ell)}\in L^{1}(\nu)
and

\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\varphi^{(u_{c}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu\leq u(1).

(2.12)

While the Orlicz-type dual problem of determining
\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)
(and its relation to
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
) is difficult to handle in general, we identify the following condition which helps us do that. It is analogous to and generalises McCann’s notion of
p
-separation in the case
u=u_{p}
,
p\in(0,1)
, cf.
[
22
, Def. 4.1]
. Note that by convention, our Kantorovich potential(s) arise by
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-transform, where
\lambda=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
, so they are related to the usual Kantorovich potentials (e.g.
[
22
,
25
,
9
]
, etc.) by a factor of
\lambda^{p}
if
u=u_{p}
.

Definition 2.29

(
u
-separated)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible. We say that a pair
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
is
u
-separated
if there exists
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
,
\varphi:\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\psi:\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
lower semicontinuous, as well as
\lambda>0
, such that

(i)

u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\leq\varphi\oplus\psi
on
\mathrm{supp}(\mu\times\nu)
,

(ii)

\mathrm{supp}(\pi)\subseteq S:=\{(x,y)\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu\times\nu):\varphi(x)+\psi(y)=u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,y))\}\subseteq\{\ell>0\}
,

(iii)

\int_{M}\varphi\,d\mu+\int_{M}\psi\,d\nu=u(1)
.

Theorem 2.30

(Duality by
u
-separation)
.

Let
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
be
u
-separated for some admissible
u
. If
\lambda>0
is as in the definition of
u
-separation, then

(i)

\lambda=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
and
\pi
is optimal;

(ii)

(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi,\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)})
and
\varphi=\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
on
X\times Y:=\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
;

(iii)

the set
S
is compact and
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-cyclically monotone;

(iv)

\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\lambda
, and
\varphi
is a Kantorovich potential;

(v)

the extensions
\varphi:=\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
and
\psi:=\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
are semiconvex Lipschitz functions on neighbourhoods of
X
and
Y
, respectively, with Lipschitz and semiconvexity constants given by those of
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
on
S
.

Proof.

First, given that
\mu,\nu
are compactly supported, we may consider all causal couplings in the definition of
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
without changing its value.

(i)

We first show that
\lambda\leq\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
: Indeed,

\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=\int(\varphi\oplus\psi)d\pi=\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu=u(1).

To see that
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\lambda
, suppose that
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
, then for any
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
, using inverse monotonicity of
\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell
we obtain

\int u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}\leq\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}\leq\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu=u(1).

Now suppose that for some
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
,

\sup_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}=u(1).

Just like in the proof of Proposition
2.19
, we can use the compactness of
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
to find a causal coupling
\pi^{\prime}
of
(\mu,\nu)
realising this supremum, i.e.,

\int u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}=u(1).

By monotonicity of
u
, it is easily seen that
\pi^{\prime}
cannot be concentrated on
\{\ell=0\}
, thus there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
m_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{\prime}(\{\ell\geq\varepsilon\})>0
. Using strict inverse monotonicity of
\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto u_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\ell(x,y))
whenever
\ell(x,y)>0
, as well as continuity, since the set
\{\ell\geq\varepsilon\}\cap\mathrm{supp}(\pi^{\prime})
is compact, there is some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

u_{\lambda}\circ\ell-u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\geq C_{\varepsilon}>0

on
\{\ell\geq\varepsilon\}\cap\mathrm{supp}(\pi^{\prime})
. But then

\displaystyle u(1)

\displaystyle=\int u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}=\int_{\{\ell\geq\varepsilon\}}u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}+\int_{\{\ell<\varepsilon\}}u_{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{\varepsilon}m_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\{\ell\geq\varepsilon\}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}+\int_{\{\ell<\varepsilon\}}u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-C_{\varepsilon}m_{\varepsilon}+\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{\varepsilon}m_{\varepsilon}+\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu<u(1),

a contradiction. We conclude that
\lambda=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
and that
\pi
is optimal.

(ii)

Consider the
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-conjugate of
\psi
in
M
,

\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}(x)=\sup_{y\in Y}u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,y))-\psi(y).

(2.13)

The result will follow if we establish that
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}=\varphi
in
X
. To this aim, notice that, since
\nu
is compactly supported and both
-\psi
and
u\circ\ell
are upper semicontinuous, the supremum in the previous equation is attained.
Fix
\bar{x}\in X
. The inequality
\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}(\bar{x})
follows from Definition
2.29
(i). On the other hand, using Definition
2.29
(ii) and compactness of
X
and
Y
, and since
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
, there exists
\bar{y}\in Y
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S
. Hence, this
\bar{y}
must maximise (
2.13
), and
\varphi
and
\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}
agree on
X
. Since
\varphi
is only defined on
X
, it can be extended to
M
by imposing that it agrees with
\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}
outside
X
, which yields the first part of the proof.
The second part of the proof requires to show
\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}=\psi
in
Y
, which can be done analogously.

(iii)

Compactness of
S
is clear, as it is a closed set contained in the compact set
X\times Y
. To see
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-cyclical monotonicity of
S
, choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
. For
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{k},y_{k})\in S
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{\lambda}(\ell(x_{i},y_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(x_{i})+\psi(y_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(x_{i})+\psi(y_{\sigma(i)})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{\lambda}(\ell(x_{i},y_{\sigma(i)})),

where the last inequality is a consequence of the
u
-separation. This gives the result.

(iv)

First, since
\psi=\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
on
Y=\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
and

\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu=u(1),

it follows that
\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)\leq\lambda
. Now suppose that
\eta<\lambda
, and take any
\tilde{\varphi}\in L^{1}(\mu)
such that
\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\in L^{1}(\nu)
. Since
\tilde{\varphi}(x)+\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}(y)\geq u_{\eta}(\ell(x,y))
, for any
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
we have that

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\varphi}\,d\mu+\int\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu\geq\int u_{\eta}\circ\ell\,d\pi^{\prime}.

Given that
\eta<\lambda
, taking a supremum over
\pi^{\prime}
shows that

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\varphi}\,d\mu+\int\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu\geq u(1).

Take now
\pi^{\prime}=\pi
, the optimal plan for
\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\lambda
. Again using strict inverse monotonicity of
\eta\mapsto u_{\eta}\circ\ell
, given that
\pi
has support compactly contained in
\{\ell>0\}
, there is some
C>0
such that
u_{\eta}\circ\ell\geq u_{\lambda}\circ\ell+C
on
\mathrm{supp}(\pi)
. Thus, for any
\tilde{\varphi}\in L^{1}(\mu)
,

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\varphi}\,d\mu+\int\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int\tilde{\varphi}\oplus\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\eta}\circ\ell)}\,d\pi\geq\int u_{\eta}\circ\ell\,d\pi

\displaystyle\geq C+\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=C+u(1).

Thus
\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)\geq\lambda
, so altogether
\tilde{C}_{u}(\mu,\nu)=\lambda
and we conclude that
\varphi
is a Kantorovich potential.

(v)

By (iii),
S
is a compact set. Therefore, its Riemannian distance (using the auxiliary metric
\tilde{g}
) from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
is positive. Let this distance be denoted by
3R:=d_{\tilde{g}\oplus\tilde{g}}(\{\ell\leq 0\},S)>0
. Therefore,
S_{2R}\subset\{\ell>0\}
, where
S_{2R}
denotes the closed tubular neighbourhood of
S
with radius
2R
. In particular, since by Corollary
2.12
(i),
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
is locally Lipschitz and semiconvex in
\{\ell>0\}
, the same properties hold in
S_{2R}
. We will use these to obtain sufficiently small (tubular) neighbourhoods of
X
and
Y
where the claim holds.
Let
\bar{x}\in X
. By definition of
S
, there exists
\bar{y}\in Y
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S
. Then, for any
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x)

\displaystyle\geq u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,\bar{y}))-\psi(\bar{y})

(2.14)

\displaystyle\geq u_{\lambda}(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y}))-||u_{\lambda}\circ\ell||_{C^{0,1}(B_{R}(\bar{x}))}d_{\tilde{g}}(x,\bar{x})-\psi(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\geq\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(\bar{x})-||u_{\lambda}\circ\ell||_{C^{0,1}(S_{2R})}d_{\tilde{g}}(x,\bar{x}),

where in the second line we are using the local Lipschitzness of
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
. Exchanging above the roles of
x
and
\bar{x}
, it follows that
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
is Lipschitz continuous in
X
, and the Lipschitz constant is bounded by
||u_{\lambda}\circ\ell||_{C^{0,1}(S_{2R})}
. Analogously, one would obtain the same bound for
\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
on
Y
.
Note how the argument derived in (
2.14
) cannot be used if
x\notin X
, since it is no longer possible to exchange the role of
x
and
\bar{x}
, as there is no duality
a priori
for
x
. However, one can deduce lower semicontinuity of
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
, since the bound in (
2.14
) still holds, and taking the limit
x\to\bar{x}
,

\lim_{x\to\bar{x}}\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}(x)\geq\psi^{(u\circ\ell)}(x).

(2.15)

In order to conclude Lipschitzness of
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
and
\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
in neighbourhoods of
X
and
Y
, respectively, let
r>0
be sufficiently small so that
X_{r}
inherits compactness from
X
, and
Y_{r}
inherits compactness from
Y
(this can be done since our manifold is Hausdorff and second countable). Moreover,
r>0
can be chosen so that
S_{(r,0)}:=\{(x,y)\in X_{r}\times Y:\ \psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x)+\psi(y)=u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,y))\}
is contained in
S_{R}
(respectively
S_{(0,r)}
). Indeed, assume for contradiction that there exists a sequence
(x_{k},y_{k})\in S_{(\frac{1}{k},0)}\setminus S_{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since this is in particular contained in
S_{(\frac{1}{k},0)}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, this is contained in the compact set
X_{\frac{1}{k}}\times Y
, so there exists a converging subsequence (without loss of generality, let us not rename it) to
(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})
. Note that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by construction it holds that

\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x_{k})+\psi(y_{k})=u(\ell(x_{k},y_{k})).

Now, by assumption,
\psi
is lower semicontinuous. By (
2.15
),
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
is also lower semicontinuous. Finally, by Corollary
2.12
(i),
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
is upper semicontinuous. Bringing these together, and taking the limit above, we obtain

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(\ell(x_{\infty},y_{\infty}))\leq\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x_{\infty})+\psi(y_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x_{k})+\psi(y_{k})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{\lambda}(\ell(x_{k},y_{k}))\leq u_{\lambda}(\ell(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})),

which forces everything above to be an equality. This implies that
(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\in S
, but in particular, this would give that for sufficiently big
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
(x_{k},y_{k})\in S_{R}
, which is a contradiction.
To finally conclude Lipschitzness, let
\bar{x}\in X_{r}
and
x\in X_{r}\cap B_{R}(\bar{x})
. As above, there exists
\bar{y}\in Y
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S_{(r,0)}\subset S_{R}
, hence

\displaystyle\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(x)

\displaystyle\geq u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,\bar{y}))-\psi(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\geq u_{\lambda}(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y}))-||u_{\lambda}\circ\ell||_{C^{0,1}(B_{R}(\bar{x}))}d_{\tilde{g}}(x,\bar{x})-\psi(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\geq\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(\bar{x})-||u_{\lambda}\circ\ell||_{C^{0,1}(S_{2R})}d_{\tilde{g}}(x,\bar{x}),

and in this case one can interchange the roles of
x
and
\bar{x}
, yielding a Lipschitz bound for
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
on
X_{r}
. Analogously, one derives the same bound for
\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
on
Y_{r}
.
Finally, let us show the claimed semiconvexity. Denote by
C_{2R}
the semiconvexity constant of
u\circ\ell
in
S_{2R}
given by Corollary
2.12
(i). Choose
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S_{(r,0)}\subset S_{R}
and
w\in T_{\bar{x}}M
such that
|w|_{\tilde{g}}<R
. Then

\displaystyle\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(\exp_{\bar{x}}^{\tilde{g}}w)

\displaystyle+\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(\exp_{\bar{x}}^{\tilde{g}}-w)-2\,\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\geq u_{\lambda}(\ell(\exp_{\bar{x}}^{\tilde{g}}w,\bar{y}))+u_{\lambda}(\ell(\exp_{\bar{x}}^{\tilde{g}}-w,\bar{y}))-2\,u_{\lambda}(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

\displaystyle\geq C_{2R}\,|w|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}.

This yields semiconvexity of
\psi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
in
X_{r}
. Analogously,
\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}
is semiconvex in
Y_{r}
, which concludes the proof.

∎

Remark 2.31

(Simple situation where
u
-separation holds)
.

Let
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\subseteq\{\ell>0\}
. By Proposition
2.19
and Remark
2.20
,
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, and there exists an optimal coupling
\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
such that

u(1)=\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=\sup_{\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\tilde{\pi}.

Given that
u\circ\ell
is continuous and finite-valued on the compact set
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
, standard Kantorovich duality results (cf. Villani
[
33
]
) produce a pair
(\varphi,\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)})
, where
\varphi
is
u_{\lambda}\circ\ell
-convex, such that

\displaystyle u(1)

\displaystyle=\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=\sup_{\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\tilde{\pi}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{\varphi}\in L^{1}(\mu)}\int\tilde{\varphi}\,d\mu+\int\tilde{\varphi}^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)}.

Thus, the data
(\lambda,\pi,\varphi,\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)})
is easily seen to verify
u
-separation for
(\mu,\nu)
.

2.4
Geodesics of probability measures

Definition 2.32

(
u
-geodesic)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function. We say that a map
s\in[0,1]\mapsto\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
is a
u
-geodesic
if

\ell_{u}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})=(t-s)\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in(0,\infty)

for each
0\leq s<t\leq 1
. It will be denoted throughout as
(\mu_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
. If

Proposition 2.33

(Interpolants inherit compact support)
.

Let

be an admissible function and let

be a

-geodesic. If

and
\mu_{1}
are compactly supported, then

, and the latter are compact for every

Proof.

For the
u
-geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
, write

. By assumption,
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are compact. Hence, by Lemma
2.3
,

is compact, and so is

for any

. Fix

, from the definition of

-geodesic we get
\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{s})+\ell_{u}(\mu_{s},\mu_{1})=\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
. Proposition
2.23
(i) asserts that in this case

Theorem 2.34

(Existence of
u
-geodesics)
.

Let
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
such that
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose there exists an optimal
\pi\in\Pi^{u_{\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
which is concentrated on
\{\ell>0\}
. Then
\mu_{s}:=(\bar{z}_{s})_{\#}\pi
is a
u
-geodesic from
\mu
to
\nu
, and for each
s<t
,
(\bar{z}_{s}\times\bar{z}_{t})_{\#}\pi
is optimal for
(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})
. This optimal plan saturates the inequality in (
2.3
) if and only if
\pi
does. Moreover, if
\mu,\nu
are compactly supported, the optimal coupling
\pi
is unique and satisfies
\pi(\mathrm{sing}(\ell))=0
, then the
u
-geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
is unique.

Proof.

Fix
s,t\in[0,1]
,
s<t
. Since, by assumption, for
\pi
-a.e.
(x,y)
,
s\mapsto\bar{z}_{s}(x,y)
is a timelike geodesic parametrised proportional to arclength (so that
\ell(\bar{z}_{s}(x,y),\bar{z}_{t}(x,y))=(t-s)\,\ell(x,y)
for
\pi
-a.e.
(x,y)
), it follows that
\pi_{s,t}:=(\bar{z}_{s}\times\bar{z}_{t})_{\#}\pi\in\Pi^{u_{(t-s)\lambda}}_{\leq}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})
, as well as

\displaystyle\int u_{(t-s)\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi_{s,t}=\int u_{(t-s)\lambda}\circ\ell(\bar{z}_{s}(x,y),\bar{z}_{t}(x,y))\,d\pi=\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell(x,y)\,d\pi\geq u(1),

and clearly this is an equality for
\pi_{s,t}
if and only if it is for
\pi
. Thus,
\ell_{u}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})\geq(t-s)\,\lambda=(t-s)\,\ell(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.
This implies that
(\mu_{s})
saturates the reverse triangle inequality for
\ell_{u}
and is thus a
u
-geodesic. Moreover, the above calculation shows that
\pi_{s,t}
is optimal for
(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})
. The additional claim follows as in
[
22
, Thm. 2.11]
, using that, in this case, each

).
∎

Proposition 2.35

(Lagrangian trajectories do not cross)
.

Fix an admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in M^{2}
, and fix
s\in(0,1)
. If
Z_{s}(x,y)\cap Z_{s}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, and

u(\ell(x,y^{\prime}))+u(\ell(x^{\prime},y))\leq u(\ell(x,y))+u(\ell(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))

(2.16)

holds, then
(x,y)=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
u
is admissible, it is strictly increasing and strictly concave. Together with the reverse triangle inequality satisfied by
\ell
, we have for
m\in Z_{s}(x,y)\cap Z_{s}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle u\circ\ell(x,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq u\left(s\,\frac{\ell(x,m)}{s}+(1-s)\,\frac{\ell(m,y^{\prime})}{1-s}\right)

\displaystyle\geq s\,u\left(\frac{\ell(x,m)}{s}\right)+(1-s)\,u\left(\frac{\ell(m,y^{\prime})}{1-s}\right)

\displaystyle=s\,u\circ\ell(x,y)+(1-s)\,u\circ\ell(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

By strict monotonicity, the first inequality is strict unless
m
lies on a maximising timelike geodesic from
x
to
y^{\prime}
, while the second is strict unless
\ell(x,m)/s=\ell(m,y^{\prime})/(1-s)
and thus
\ell(x,y)=\ell(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, by strict concavity of
u
. The analogous inequality for
u\circ\ell(x^{\prime},y)
is strict unless
m
lies on a maximising timelike geodesic from
x^{\prime}
to
y
. Summing these two estimates, any strictness in the inequalities would contradict our assumption, thus equalities hold throughout. But the affine parametrization, combined with the previous observations on
x,x^{\prime},m,y,y^{\prime}
, forces
x=x^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}
.
∎

The above result will often be used for the admissible function
u_{\lambda}
, with
u
a fixed admissible function and
\lambda>0
.

Proposition 2.36

((Lipschitz) continuity of inverse maps)
.

Let
u
be admissible,
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
be
u
-separated and
s\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a Lipschitz continuous map
W:Z_{s}(S)\to\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{0})\times\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{1})
(where

is the set given in Definition
2.29
of
u
-separation) with the property that, whenever
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is a
u
-geodesic from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
, then
W_{\#}\mu_{s}
is optimal for
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and the restriction of
\bar{z}_{s}\circ W
to
\bar{z}_{s}(S)
is the identity map.

We sketch the argument, which is analogous to McCann
[
22
, Cor. 5.2]
and refer there for more details.

The duality obtained from the
u
-separation of
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
(cf. Theorem
2.30
) provides semiconvex optimisers
(\varphi,\varphi^{(u_{\lambda}\circ\ell)})
, where
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in(0,\infty)

, and since
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
, any optimal coupling
\pi
for
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is concentrated on

(here

and is

implies that setting

whenever

and

\bar{z}_{s}
on
\bar{z}_{s}(S)

Now, given
\mu_{s}
on a

-geodesic from
\mu_{0}
to

(ii), which gives

for

Proposition 2.37

(Star-shapedness of

Let

-separation for

and

Lemma 2.38

(Maps and their Jacobian derivatives)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function, and fix
X,Y\subset M
compact. Let
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a semiconvex and Lipschitz function such that
\varphi\geq\left(\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}\right)^{(u\circ\ell)}
holds in a neighbourhood of
X
.

(i)

If
\varphi\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}-u\circ\ell\geq 0
vanishes at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
, i.e. if
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\partial_{(u\circ\ell)}\varphi
, then
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{Dom}D\varphi
implies
\bar{y}=F_{1}(\bar{x})
, where
F_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,v(D\varphi(x),x;u)
, with

v(D\varphi(x),x;u)=-\frac{(u^{\prime})^{-1}(|\nabla\varphi|(x))}{|\nabla\varphi|(x)}\,\nabla\varphi(x)=DH(D\varphi(x),x;u^{*}).

(2.17)

Moreover,
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{Dom}\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi
implies
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
. Analogously,
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{Dom}\,D\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}
gives
\bar{x}=\exp_{\bar{y}}-DH(-D\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(\bar{y}),\bar{y};u^{*})
.

(ii)

For
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
-a.e.
x\in X
, the approximate derivative
\tilde{D}F_{s}(x):T_{x}M\to T_{F_{s}(M)}M
exists, depends smoothly on
s
, and
\tilde{D}F_{s}(x)\,w
gives a Jacobi field along the geodesic
s\in[0,1]\mapsto F_{s}(x)
for each
w\in T_{x}M
.

(iii)

Furthermore,

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\bigg|_{s=0}\tilde{D}F_{s}=\tilde{D}\frac{\partial F_{s}}{\partial s}\bigg|_{s=0}=(Dv\circ D\varphi)\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi=(D^{2}H\circ D\varphi)\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi

(2.18)

holds true
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
-a.e. on
X
. Here, the derivatives are computed with respect to the Lorentzian connection.

Proof.

(i)

Observe that, by definition of
\left(\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}\right)^{(u\circ\ell)}
, the inequality
\left(\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}\right)^{(u\circ\ell)}\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}-u\circ\ell\geq 0
holds on
M\times Y
. Thus
\varphi\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}-u\circ\ell\geq 0
on
U\times Y
, where
U
is the hypothesised neighbourhood of
X
on which
\varphi
is Lipschitz and semiconvex.

If the latter inequality is saturated at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
, and
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{Dom}D\varphi
, it follows that
u\circ\ell(\cdot,\bar{y})
is superdifferentiable at
\bar{x}
with supergradient
D\varphi(\bar{x})
. Indeed, for
v\in T_{\bar{x}}M
in a small neighbourhood around 0:

\displaystyle u\left(\ell(\exp_{\bar{x}}v,\bar{y})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(\exp_{\bar{x}}v,\bar{y})

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\varphi\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+D\varphi(\bar{x})v+O(|v|_{\tilde{g}})

\displaystyle=u\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)+D\varphi(\bar{x})v+O(|v|_{\tilde{g}}),

since
\left(\varphi\oplus\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}-u\circ\ell\right)(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0
. Therefore, use now Corollary
2.12
(ii) to conclude the first part of the statement.

Moreover, if
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{Dom}\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi
, the Taylor expansion of
\varphi
around
\bar{x}
gives a quadratic bound for
u\circ\ell(x,\bar{y})-\varphi^{(u\circ\ell)}(\bar{y})
at
\bar{x}
. According to Corollary
2.12
(iii), this implies
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
.

On the other hand, since
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
, in particular,
\bar{y}
is not in the timelike cut locus of
\bar{x}
. Therefore, we can apply the first variation formula to the following:

\displaystyle u\left(\ell(\exp_{\bar{x}}v,\bar{y})\right)-u\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{u^{\prime}\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)}{\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\,g_{\bar{x}}(v,v(D\varphi(x),x;u))+O(|v|_{\tilde{g}}).

Combining this with (
2.19
),

-\frac{u^{\prime}\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)}{\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\,g_{\bar{x}}(v,v(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u))\leq D\varphi(\bar{x})v,

and this readily implies that
\nabla\varphi=-\frac{u^{\prime}\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)}{\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\,v(D\varphi(x),x;u)
. In particular,

v(D\varphi(x),x;u)=-\frac{\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})}{u^{\prime}\left(\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})\right)}\,\nabla\varphi.

Finally, noting that
|v(D\varphi(x),x;u)|_{g}=\ell(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, the result follows.

(ii)

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Semiconvexity of
\varphi
implies that outside of a set of volume
\varepsilon
in
U\supset X
,
D\varphi
agrees with a continuously differentiable covector field
V_{\varepsilon}
on
M
. Moreover, its approximate second derivative agrees with
DV_{\varepsilon}
outside of this small set.

Consider, for each
x\in X
, the vector
v(V_{\varepsilon}(x),x;u)=DH(V_{\varepsilon}(x),x;u^{*})
. If we define the maps
F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,v(V_{\varepsilon}(x),x;u)
, these are
C^{1}
in
x\in M
and smooth in
s\in[0,1]
, and its mixed partial derivatives are continuous and equal:
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}DF_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)=D\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)
, where
D
is the derivative with respect to
x
.

The idea is now to check that this approximate derivative will define Jacobi fields whenever it acts on a vector of the tangent bundle. The way of doing it will be by defining a geodesic variation, and applying
[
26
, Lem. 8.3]
. Fix
(x,w)\in TM
, and let
r\in[-1,1]\mapsto x(r)
be a
C^{1}
curve such that
x(0)=x
and
\dot{x}(0)=w
. Then
r\in[-1,1]\mapsto F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x(r))
is a
C^{1}
-geodesic variation, since
s\in[0,1]\mapsto F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x(r))
is a geodesic segment for each
r\in[-1,1]
. Thus
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=0}F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x(r))=DF_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x(0))\,w
is a Jacobi field.

Since the approximate derivative
\tilde{D}F_{s}(x)
agrees with
DF_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)
outside of a set of volume
\varepsilon
, and this was arbitrary, we have that
\tilde{D}F_{s}(x(0))\,w
depends smoothly on
s
and is a Jacobi field for
x(0)\in U
in a subset of full volume.

(iii)

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider, as in the last point, the vector field
V_{\varepsilon}
and the associated map
F_{s}^{\varepsilon}
. Differentiating the vector field
\frac{\partial F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=0}=v(V_{\varepsilon}(x),x;u)
using the Lorentzian connection gives

D_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\bigg|_{s=0}F_{s}^{\varepsilon}(x)^{i}=(Dv\circ V_{\varepsilon})\,DV_{\varepsilon}.

We may again interchange the order of the
x
and
s
derivatives. Since these derivatives of
F^{\varepsilon}
and
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
coincide with those of
F
and
\varphi
outside of a set of volume
\varepsilon
, the result follows.

, where

is admissible and

.

Proposition 3.1

(Jacobian along
u
-geodesics)
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function. Let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(M)^{2}
be
u
-separated such that
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)
, let
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in(0,\infty)
and
\varphi
be the Kantorovich potential. Set
X\times Y=\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{0}\times\mu_{1})
, and let
F_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,DH(D\varphi(x),x;u^{*}_{\lambda})
be as obtained in Theorem
2.39
.

Then, for
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
-a.e.
x\in X
, the approximate derivative
A_{s}(x):=\tilde{D}F_{s}(x):T_{x}M\to T_{F_{s}(x)}M
exists. Moreover, it is invertible, depends smoothly on
s\in[0,1]
, and
\phi(s):=-\log|\det A_{s}(x)|
satisfies

\phi^{\prime}(s)=-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x),

(3.1)

\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)=\operatorname{Ric}_{F_{s}(x)}\left(F_{s}^{\prime}(x),F_{s}^{\prime}(x)\right)+\operatorname{Tr}\left(B_{s}^{2}(x)\right),

(3.2)

\operatorname{Tr}\left(B_{s}^{2}(x)\right)\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x)\right)^{2},

(3.3)

where
B_{s}(x):=A_{s}^{\prime}(x)\,A_{s}(x)^{-1}
and
′
denotes
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
.

Proof.

The first claim follows as a consequence of Lemma
2.38
(ii), which asserts that, in the same hypotheses as now, the approximate derivative
\tilde{D}F_{s}(x)=A_{s}(x)
exists for
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
-a.e.
x\in X
. Furthermore, this result also gives that
s\in[0,1]\mapsto A_{s}(x)\,w
is a smooth Jacobi field for each
w\in T_{x}M
, which will be useful to prove one of the identities for the derivatives in what follows.

The invertibility of
A_{s}
may be established as in the Riemannian case of
[
10
]
, using the (strict) convexity of
H
. We omit the details.

Set
\phi(s):=-\log|\det A_{s}(x)|
. A standard matrix identity gives the first claimed equality (
3.1
):

\phi^{\prime}(s)=(-\log|\det A_{s}(x)|)^{\prime}=-\operatorname{Tr}(A_{s}(x)^{\prime}A_{s}(x)^{-1})=-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x).

For the second equality (
3.2
), start by differentiating the previous one:

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=(-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x))^{\prime}=-\operatorname{Tr}(B_{s}(x)^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Tr}\left(A_{s}(x)^{\prime\prime}A_{s}(x)^{-1}\right)-\operatorname{Tr}\left(A_{s}(x)^{\prime}(-A_{s}(x)^{-1}A_{s}(x)^{\prime}A_{s}(x)^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Tr}\left(A_{s}(x)^{\prime\prime}A_{s}(x)^{-1}\right)+\operatorname{Tr}(B_{s}(x)^{2}).

Now, to rewrite the first term, we use the property from Lemma
2.38
(ii) that establishes that
s\in[0,1]\mapsto A_{s}(x)\,w\in T_{F_{s}(x)}M
is a smooth Jacobi field for each
w\in T_{x}M
, and hence it satisfies the Jacobi equation, i.e. in coordinates,

0=\nabla_{F^{\prime}}(\nabla_{F^{\prime}}A^{i}_{\bar{j}})+R_{jkl}\ ^{i}F^{\prime j}F^{\prime l}A^{k}_{\bar{j}}.

where
\bar{j}
denotes a fixed index, over which there is no summation. Multiplying this identity by
(A^{-1})^{\bar{j}}_{i}
gives the desired result to arrive at (
3.2
):

0=\left(\nabla_{F^{\prime}}(\nabla_{F^{\prime}}A^{i}_{\bar{j}})+R_{jkl}\ ^{i}F^{\prime j}F^{\prime l}A^{k}_{\bar{j}}\right)(A^{-1})^{\bar{j}}_{i}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(A_{s}(x)^{\prime\prime}A_{s}(x)^{-1}\right)+\operatorname{Ric}_{F_{s}(x)}(F_{s}^{\prime}(x),F_{s}^{\prime}(x)).

To obtain (
3.3
), we will use the standard inequality for a symmetric
n\times n
matrix
C
:
\operatorname{Tr}C^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}(\operatorname{Tr}C)^{2}
. Therefore, we only need to show that
B_{s}(x)
is symmetric for all
x\in X
and
s\in[0,1]
. Denoting by
B_{s}^{*}(x)
the adjoint, we have that

B_{s}^{*}(x)-B_{s}(x)=(A_{s}^{*}(x))^{-1}\left(A_{s}^{*}(x)^{\prime}\,A_{s}(x)-A_{s}^{*}(x)\,A_{s}(x)^{\prime}\right)\,A_{s}(x)^{-1},

and

\left(A_{s}^{*}(x)^{\prime}\,A_{s}(x)-A_{s}^{*}(x)\,A_{s}(x)^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=A_{s}^{*}(x)^{\prime\prime}\,A_{s}(x)-A_{s}^{*}(x)\,A_{s}(x)^{\prime\prime}.

(3.4)

On the other hand, the Jacobi equation we wrote above gives

A_{s}(x)^{\prime\prime}=-R(A_{s}(x),F_{s}^{\prime}(x))\,F_{s}^{\prime}(x)=-\mathcal{R}(s,x)\,A_{s}(x),

(3.5)

where
\mathcal{R}(s,x):T_{F_{s}(x)}M\to T_{F_{s}(x)}M
is given by
\mathcal{R}(s,x)(v):=R(v,F_{s}^{\prime}(x))F_{s}^{\prime}(x)
. The latter is symmetric, by simple symmetry properties of the Riemann tensor. Plugging now (
3.5
) into (
3.4
), we obtain that
\left(A_{s}^{*}(x)^{\prime}\,A_{s}(x)-A_{s}^{*}(x)\,A_{s}(x)^{\prime}\right)
is constant in
s
, since for
s=0
,
A_{0}(x)=\operatorname{id}_{T_{x}M}
, and
A_{0}^{\prime}(x)=(D^{2}H\circ D\varphi)\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi
. The latter is in principle not symmetric, but can be made symmetric for the purposes of establishing (
3.3
) by multiplying from the right and from the left with

as in McCann’s proof of
[
22
, Prop. 6.1]
. The result follows.
∎

\displaystyle-\infty

Definition 3.7

(
(K,N,u)
-convexity)
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(0,\infty]
.

(i)

A function
e:[0,1]\to[-\infty,\infty]
is said to be
(K,N)
-convex
if
e
is upper semicontinuous,
\operatorname{Dom}e:=\{s\in[0,1]:\ e(s)<\infty\}
is connected, and either
e^{-1}(-\infty)
contains the interior
I
of
\operatorname{Dom}e
or is empty, and in the latter case:
e
is semiconvex throughout
I
and satisfies

e^{\prime\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime}(s))^{2}\geq K

in the distributional sense on
I
, where
(1/\infty)(e^{\prime})^{2}:=0
.

(ii)

Given a globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M^{n},g)
and an admissible function
u
, a functional
E:\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is said to be
weakly
(K,N,u)
-convex for
Q\subset\mathcal{P}(M)^{2}
if for each
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in Q
there is a
u
-geodesic in
\mathcal{P}(M)
joining
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
(in particular,
\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in(0,\infty)
) on which
E(\mu_{s})
is
(K\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2},N)
-convex.
E
is said to be
(K,N,u)
-convex for
Q
if, in addition,
E(\mu_{s})
is
(K\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2},N)
-convex for all
u
-geodesics
s\in[0,1]\mapsto\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
with endpoints in
Q
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
, the following inequality holds:
(a-b)^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\,a^{2}+(1+\varepsilon)\,b^{2}
.

Proof.

The inequality claimed can be expanded to obtain that it is equivalent to
\varepsilon^{-1}\,a^{2}+\varepsilon\,b^{2}+2ab\geq 0
. If
a
or
b
are equal to 0, then the inequality is trivial, so assume from now on that they are both not 0.

Consider the function
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
f(x)=x^{-1}a^{2}+xb^{2}+2ab
. The function
f
is clearly smooth, hence we can write its derivative
f^{\prime}(x)=b^{2}-a^{2}x^{-2}
. We obtain that
f^{\prime}(x)=0
if and only if
x=|\frac{a}{b}|
. Furthermore,
f^{\prime\prime}(|\frac{a}{b}|)>0
, which makes the point
|\frac{a}{b}|
a minimum. Hence, since
f(|\frac{a}{b}|)=2|a||b|+2ab\geq 0
, the result follows.
∎

We are now in position to prove our main result of this subsection, namely the fact that timelike lower Ricci curvature bounds imply convexity of the relative entropy
E_{V}
along
u
-geodesics between
u
-separated measures.

Theorem 3.9

(Entropic convexity from timelike lower Ricci curvature bounds)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Fix
V\in C^{2}(M)
and
N\in[n,\infty]
(where
N=n
by convention means
V=0
and
\mathrm{Ric}^{(n,0)}:=\mathrm{Ric}
). For any admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, denote by
Q_{u}\subset\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(M)^{2}
the set of pairs of absolutely continuous probability measures which are
u
-separated. If
K\geq 0
and
\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v,v)\geq K|v|^{2}_{g}\geq 0
holds in every timelike direction
(x,v)\in TM
, then for any
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in Q_{u}
, the relative entropy
s\mapsto E_{V}(\mu_{s})
along the unique
u
-geodesic from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
is
(K,N,u)
-convex. For a general
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
e(s):=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
satisfies the distributional inequality

e^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{N}(e^{\prime})^{2}\geq K\,\|\ell\|_{L^{2}(\pi)},

where
\pi
is the unique optimal coupling of
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.

Proof.

We assume
n<N<\infty
, the edge cases can be argued via limits. Fix an admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, and let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in Q_{u}
. Corollary
2.42
gives the unique
u
-geodesic from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
, given by
(\mu_{s}:=(F_{s})_{\#}\mu_{0})_{s\in[0,1]}
, which satisfies that
\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(M)
for all
s\in[0,1)
.

Consider the entropy along the
u
-geodesic, and denote it by
e(s):=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
. By (
3.7
),
e(s)>-\infty
.

Assume first that
e(0)
and
e(1)
are both finite. Then, we can use Theorem
3.6
to establish bounds on
\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(s)^{2}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\int_{M}[DV_{F_{s}(x)}F_{s}^{\prime}(x)-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x)]\,d\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{M}[DV_{F_{s}(x)}F_{s}^{\prime}(x)-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x)]^{2}d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon^{-1}}{N}|DV_{F_{s}(x)}F_{s}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(B_{s}^{2}(x))\right)d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{N-n}|DV_{F_{s}(x)}F_{s}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+\operatorname{Tr}(B_{s}^{2}(x)\right)d\mu_{0}(x),

where in the first inequality we have used Jensen’s inequality, for the second inequality we have used (
3.3
) and Lemma
3.8
, and for the last equality we have chosen
\varepsilon=\frac{N-n}{n}>0
. Using this bound on
\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(s)^{2}
together with the expression of
e^{\prime\prime}(s)
given by (
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\prime\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(F_{s}^{\prime}(x),F_{s}^{\prime}(x))d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\geq K\int_{M}\ell(x,F_{1}(x))^{2}d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=K\,\left(\int_{M}\ell(x,y)d\pi(x,y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle(\text{only if }K\geq 0)\quad

\displaystyle\geq K\,u_{\lambda}^{-1}\left(\int_{M}u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,y))d\pi(x,y)\right)^{2}=K\,u_{\lambda}^{-1}(u(1))^{2}

\displaystyle=K\,\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2},

where in the inequality in the second line we have used the hypothesised bound on the Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor, for the inequality in the third line we have used Jensen’s inequality and the form of the optimal coupling
\pi=(\operatorname{id}\times F_{1})_{\#}\mu_{0}
given by Theorem
2.39
, and for the inequality in the fourth line we have used again Jensen’s inequality together with Proposition
2.19
.
In the case that either
e(0)
or
e(1)
are not finite, we can apply the argument above on any subinterval of
[0,1]
with finite entropy at its endpoints, to reach the conclusion.
∎

3.2
Relaxation of
u
-separation

In this subsection, we show that entropic convexity extends to
u
-geodesics whose endpoints are not necessarily
u
-separated. We follow McCann’s approach
[
22
, Sec. 7]
, but have to deal with the additional challenge that, a priori, restrictions of optimal couplings may no longer be optimal due to the nonlinearity of the optimal transport problem defining
\ell_{u}
.

Theorem 3.10

(Maps characterising interpolants without duality)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Fix
V\in C^{2}(M)
and an admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}(M)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
be such that
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose there are lower semicontinuous functions
a,b:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
a\in L^{1}(\mu),\,b\in L^{1}(\nu)
, and we have
u_{\lambda}(\ell(x,y))\leq a\oplus b(x,y):=a(x)+b(y)
on
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
. Then the following hold:

(i)

There is at most one
\ell_{u}
-optimal coupling
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}^{u_{\lambda}}(\mu,\nu)
such that
\ell>0
holds
\pi
-a.e. and
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=u(1)
.

(ii)

If such a coupling exists, then
\pi=(\operatorname{id}\times F)_{\#}\mu
for some map
F:\operatorname{supp}\mu\to\operatorname{supp}\nu
, and
\pi(\operatorname{sing}(\ell))=0
.

(iii)

In the hypotheses of (ii),
\pi
decomposes into countably many nonnegative, mutually singular measures, i.e.
\pi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\pi^{i}
, such that
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{supp}\pi^{i}
is
u_{\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)}\circ\ell
-cyclically monotone and the marginals
(\hat{\mu}^{i},\hat{\nu}^{i})
of
\hat{\pi}^{i}:=\pi^{i}/\pi^{i}(M^{2})
satisfy that
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{\mu}^{i}\times\hat{\nu}^{i})
is compact and disjoint from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
F
agrees
\mu^{i}
-a.e. with the unique
\ell_{u}
-optimal map
F^{i}
pushing
\hat{\mu}^{i}
forward to
\hat{\nu}^{i}
from Theorem
2.39
. Moreover,
\operatorname{graph}(F^{i})\subset\operatorname{supp}\pi^{i}
.

(iv)

The
u
-geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
defined by
\mu_{s}:=(\bar{z}_{s})_{\#}\pi
satisfies
\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}(M)
for
s\in[0,1)
.

(v)

The measures
\mu^{i}_{s}:=(z_{s})_{\#}\pi^{i}
decompose
\mu_{s}
into mutually singular pieces for
s\in[0,1)
.

(vi)

The sum
\pi=\sum_{i}\pi^{i}
is finite if and only if
\operatorname{supp}\pi
is compact and disjoint from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to find a suitable covering of the support of
\mu\times\nu
. In particular, we will need that the closure of each element of the covering is compact. By restricting the product measure to each of these suitable elements of the covering, we will be able to construct a decomposition of the optimal coupling
\pi
, provided it exists, and apply the results developed in Section
2
regarding the existence and uniqueness of optimal couplings for compactly supported measures. These will be glued together to obtain the final desired decomposition for
\pi
and the corresponding
u
-geodesic.

Assume first that (i) and (ii) hold, and suppose that there exists a unique
\ell_{u}
-optimal coupling
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}^{u_{\lambda}}(\mu,\nu)
which satisfies
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=u(1)
and for which
\ell>0
holds
\pi
-a.e.. By Theorem
2.5
,
\{\ell>0\}
is open, and since
M
is a manifold, in particular it is locally compact and Hausdorff, therefore
\{\ell>0\}\cap\operatorname{supp}\pi
can be covered by open rectangles
U\times W
whose compact closures are contained in
\{\ell>0\}
. Furthermore, since
M
is second countable, only countably many of these rectangles are necessary. Write the covering collection as
\{U_{i}\times W_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
With this, the decomposition of
\pi
is built inductively as follows: for
i=0
, fix
\pi^{0}=0
, and for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi^{i}
is defined as the restriction of
\pi-\pi^{i-1}
to
U_{i}\times W_{i}
. Therefore,
\pi
can be written as
\pi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\pi^{i}
, and it is clear by construction that the summands are mutually singular, and each
\pi^{i}
is nonnegative and vanishes outside the
i
-th rectangle, which makes them compactly supported. Moreover, by construction,
\pi
is supported on
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{supp}\pi^{i}
, and since
\pi
is assumed to be
\ell_{u}
-optimal and to satisfy
\int u_{\lambda}\circ\ell\,d\pi=u(1)

-cyclically monotone, with
\lambda:=\ell_{u}(\mu,\nu)
, by Proposition
2.25

It is immediate from the construction that the case in which
\operatorname{supp}\pi
is compact and contained in
\{\ell>0\}
gives a finite decomposition, proving the “if” implication of (vi). Now, consider the finite sum
\sum_{k=1}^{i}\pi^{k}
. The support of this measure is compact, as it is the finite union of compact sets, and since every one of these compact sets is disjoint from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
, a closed set, then so is the union. This establishes the “only if” implication of (vi).

Normalise
\hat{\pi}^{i}=\frac{1}{\pi^{i}(M^{2})}\pi^{i}
whenever
\pi^{i}
is non-vanishing.
Consider now, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the marginals of
\pi^{i}
and
\hat{\pi}^{i}
, denoted by
(\mu^{i},\nu^{i})
and
(\hat{\mu}^{i},\hat{\nu}^{i})

\pi^{i}
is by construction compactly supported, so are the two pairs of marginals, and the supports

are by construction disjoint from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
. Note that
\hat{\mu}^{i}
is the normalisation of
\mu^{i}
, and same with
\hat{\nu}^{i}
and
\nu^{i}
. This establishes the first claim in (iii).

To prove the second claim in (iii), notice that since
\hat{\pi}^{i}
is a probability measure, so are its compactly supported marginals
(\hat{\mu}^{i},\hat{\nu}^{i})
. By the disjointness of
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{\mu}^{i}\times\hat{\nu}^{i})
from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
obtained in the previous paragraph, this pair is
u
-separated (cf. Remark
2.31
), and we can apply Theorem
2.39
. This result gives the existence of a unique
u
-optimal coupling
\tilde{\pi}^{i}
(which does not necessarily agree
a priori
with
\hat{\pi}^{i}
because of the non-linearity in the definition of the Lorentz-Orlicz-Wasserstein time separation on the couplings) of the form
(\operatorname{id}\times\tilde{F}^{i}_{1})_{\#}\hat{\mu}^{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}:=\ell_{u}(\hat{\mu}^{i},\hat{\nu}^{i})
,
\tilde{F}^{i}_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,DH(D\tilde{\varphi}^{i},x;u_{\lambda_{i}}^{*})
and
\tilde{\varphi}^{i}
is the Kantorovich potential between
(\hat{\mu}^{i},\hat{\nu}^{i})
given by

-separation. From this, one obtains a coupling
\bar{\pi}^{i}\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu^{i},\nu^{i})
given by
(\operatorname{id}\times\bar{F}^{i}_{1})_{\#}\hat{\mu}^{i}
, with
\bar{F}^{i}_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}s\,DH(D\bar{\varphi}^{i},x;u_{\lambda_{i}}^{*})
, where
\bar{\varphi}^{i}
has absorbed the normalisation constant (using linearity of

). Another consequence of Theorem
2.39
is that
\bar{\pi}^{i}(\operatorname{sing}(\ell))=0
.

Note that the functions
\bar{F}^{i}_{1}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy that
\operatorname{Dom}\bar{F}^{i}_{1}\subset\operatorname{supp}\mu^{i}
, which is compact, since
\operatorname{supp}\pi^{i}
is too. Therefore, each
\bar{F}_{1}^{i}
can be extended to
\operatorname{supp}\mu^{i}
in such a way that
\operatorname{graph}(\bar{F}^{i}_{1})\subset\operatorname{supp}\pi^{i}
. This gives the last claim in (iii).

To conclude the proof of (iii), we need to show that the map
F
given by (ii) agrees
\mu^{i}
-a.e. with the map
\bar{F}_{1}^{i}\equiv F^{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, which we have just obtained above. We will do it in the following way: we define
F_{s}(x):=\bar{z}_{s}(x,F(x))
, and show that this must agree with
F^{i}_{s}

\mu^{i}
-a.e., for all
s\in[0,1]
. Note that
F_{s}
is well defined and depends smoothly on

We conclude our efforts concerning the relationship between timelike Ricci bounds and entropic convexity in this subsection, showing that the convexity of the relative entropy along

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function, and fix a compact set

with
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in its interior. Let

satisfying the first- and second-order conditions

\displaystyle D\varphi(\bar{x})=D_{x}(u\circ\ell)(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\quad\text{and}\quad D^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})>D^{2}_{xx}(u\circ\ell)(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

Then there exists a

-convex function
\tilde{\varphi}
on

which agrees with

in some neighbourhood of

.

Proof.

The result follows using the same arguments as McCann in
[

, Lem. 8.3]
, observing that
b:=u\circ\ell
has the same properties as
u_{p}\circ\ell
,
p\in(0,1)
, by Corollary
2.12
(iv).
∎

Theorem 3.17

(Entropic convexity implies a timelike Ricci bound)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Fix
V\in C^{2}(M)
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[n,\infty]
. Fix
V=0
if
N=n
. If
\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v,v)\geq K|v|^{2}_{g}
fails at some timelike vector
(x,v)\in TM
, then the relative entropy
E_{V}(\mu)
fails to be weakly
(K,N,u)
-convex for any admissible function
u:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, the
u
-geodesic
(\mu_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
along which
e(s):=E_{V}(\mu_{s})
is not
(\ell_{u}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}K,N)
-convex may be constructed so that
e\in C^{2}([0,1])
, and
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{0}\times\mu_{1})
is disjoint from
\{\ell\leq 0\}
, but contained in an arbitrarily small neighbourhood of
(x,x)
.

Proof.

Assume there exist a point
\bar{x}\in M
and a timelike tangent vector at
\bar{x}
,
\hat{v}\in T_{\bar{x}}M
with
|\hat{v}|_{g}=1
, for which
\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(\hat{v},\hat{v})<K
. The argument follows similarly as in
[
22
, Thm. 8.5]
, with suitable adaptations corresponding to our transport maps.

The idea is to construct a
u
-geodesic which transports some conveniently chosen initial measure
\mu_{0}
in the direction of
\hat{v}
. By building the geodesic appropriately, which will be possible by fixing first the Kantorovich potentials, we will be able to see that the associated entropy functional fails to be convex. Since we will also argue that this
u
-geodesic that we will build is unique, we can conclude that the relative entropy is not even weakly convex.

For
r>0
, consider the Riemannian ball of radius
r
centred around
\bar{x}
,

B_{r}(\bar{x})
, and take
\mu_{0}^{(r)}
to be the uniform distribution on this ball with respect to
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
. We have that
\mu_{0}^{(r)}\to\delta_{\bar{x}}
against test functions as
r\to 0
. Moreover, fix
t\in(0,1]
sufficiently small so that
t
does not exceed the injectivity radius of the exponential map. Define
y_{t}:=\exp_{\bar{x}}t\,\hat{v}
. By construction,
y_{t}
lies in the future of
\bar{x}
away from the cut locus, so there is a compact neighbourhood
X\times Y
of
(\bar{x},y_{t})
disjoint from
\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
.

Let us build a suitable optimal transport problem starting from
\mu_{0}^{(r)}
making use of the timelike vector
v_{t}:=t\,\hat{v}
. This will be possible by prescribing a conveniently chosen Kantorovich potential
\varphi=\left(\varphi^{(u_{t}\circ\ell)}\right)^{(u_{t}\circ\ell)}
defined on
B_{r}(\bar{x})
. Assuming that we have such a
\varphi
, consider
F_{s}(x):=\exp_{x}v\,(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t})
as described in Lemma
2.38
, where
v(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t})\equiv DH(D\varphi,x;(u_{t})^{*})
. This map describes the unique
u
-geodesic from
\mu_{0}^{(r)}
to
\mu_{1}^{(r)}:=F_{1\#}\mu_{0}^{(r)}
as follows. Note that for
r>0
sufficiently small,
B_{r}(\bar{x})\times F_{1}(B_{r}(\bar{x}))\subset X\times Y
, and therefore disjoint from
\operatorname{sing}(\ell)
. Using Remark
2.31
and Theorem
2.39
, we obtain that
\mu_{s}^{(r)}:=F_{s\#}\mu_{0}^{(r)}\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}(M)
defines the unique
u
-geodesic on
s\in[0,1]
connecting its endpoints.

Given that we expect to obtain the failure of the entropic convexity from the failure of the bound on the Ricci curvature, we need to study the first and second derivatives of the entropy functional. Since the contruction is focused around
\bar{x}
and
\hat{v}
, it is reasonable to worry only about this convexity at
s=0
, as well as at the limit
r\to 0
. Hence, by Theorem
3.6
,

\displaystyle e^{\prime}(0;r)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}[DV_{F_{s}(x)}F_{s}^{\prime}(x)-\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}(x)]\Big|_{s=0}d\mu_{0}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}[DV_{F_{0}(x)}F_{0}^{\prime}(x)-\operatorname{Tr}B_{0}(x)]\,d\mu_{0}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}[DV_{x}\,v(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t})-\operatorname{Tr}(D_{x}\,v(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t})\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi(x))]\,d\mu_{0}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle\to DV(\bar{x})\,v(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u_{t})-\operatorname{Tr}\left(Dv(D\varphi(\bar{x}))\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})\right)\quad\text{as}\ r\to 0,

and

\displaystyle e^{\prime\prime}(0:r)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}[\operatorname{Tr}B_{s}^{2}(x)+(\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}V)_{F_{s}(x)}(F_{s}^{\prime}(x),F_{s}^{\prime}(x))]\Big|_{s=0}d\mu_{0}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}\left((Dv(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t})\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi(x))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+(\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}V)_{x}(v(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t}),v(D\varphi(x),x;u_{t}))\Big]\,d\mu_{0}^{(r)}(x)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Tr}\left((Dv(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u_{t})\,\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x}))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+(\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}V)_{\bar{x}}(v(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u_{t}),v(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u_{t}))\qquad\text{as}\ r\to 0.

Using Lemma
3.16
, prescribe

\begin{split}v\left(D\varphi(\bar{x}),\bar{x};u_{t}\right)=v_{t},\qquad\text{and}\\
\tilde{D}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})=-\frac{1}{N-n}\left(DV(\bar{x})v_{t}\right)(Dv(D\varphi(\bar{x})))^{-1}.\end{split}

With these choices,

\displaystyle e^{\prime}(0;r)

\displaystyle\to DV(\bar{x})v_{t}-\operatorname{Tr}\left(-\frac{1}{N-n}DV(\bar{x})v_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=DV(\bar{x})v_{t}+n\frac{1}{N-n}DV(\bar{x})v_{t},

and

\displaystyle e^{\prime\prime}(0;r)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(-\frac{1}{N-n}DV(\bar{x})v_{t}\right)^{2}\right)+(\operatorname{Ric}+D^{2}V)_{\bar{x}}(v_{t},v_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(N-n)^{2}}(DV(\bar{x})v_{t})^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}(v_{t},v_{t})+D^{2}V(\bar{x})(v_{t},v_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(N-n)^{2}}(DV(\bar{x})v_{t})^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v_{t},v_{t})+\frac{1}{N-n}(DV(\bar{x})v_{t})^{2}.

Finally,

\lim_{r\to 0}e^{\prime\prime}(0;r)-\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(0;r)^{2}=\operatorname{Ric}^{(N,V)}(v_{t},v_{t})<K|v_{t}|^{2}_{g}=K\lim_{r\to 0}\ell_{u}\left(\mu_{0}^{(r)},\mu_{1}^{(r)}\right)^{2},

which gives that there exists
r>0
sufficiently small for which
e^{\prime\prime}(0;r)-\frac{1}{N}e^{\prime}(0;r)^{2}<K\ell_{u}\left(\mu_{0}^{(r)},\mu_{1}^{(r)}\right)^{2}
, contradicting the
\left(K\ell_{u}\left(\mu_{0}^{(r)},\mu_{1}^{(r)}\right)^{2},N\right)
-convexity of
e(s;r)
on
[0,1)
, which yields the proof.
∎

4
Outlook

Our work opens up the possibility of investigations in several different directions. First, one could study the connection between timelike Ricci curvature
upper
bounds as considered by Mondino–Suhr
[
25
]
in the general Orlicz setting introduced here, as well as broader ranges of synthetic dimensions
N
and the convexity of Rényi entropies in the Lorentz–Finsler context (cf. Braun–Ohta
[
5
]
). Moreover, natural questions of stability of concepts we studied in this work arise, such as optimal couplings, Kantorovich potentials, or the
u
-separation condition, under suitable perturbations of the function
u
.

Second, just like the results of McCann
[
22
]
and Mondino–Suhr
[
25
]
, our work motivates a study of nonsmooth Lorentzian spaces satisfying synthetic Ricci curvature bounds below, which we may term
\mathrm{TCD}_{u}
(timelike curvature-dimension) or
\mathrm{TMCP}_{u}
(timelike measure contraction property) spaces. One may ask whether these conditions are stable, not just under suitable convergence of Lorentzian spaces, but also with respect to certain convergences
u_{n}\to u
of admissible functions. Results which consider the stability under perturbations of
u
could help generalise the characterization of timelike Ricci lower bounds via entropic convexity, as presented in this work, to hold for a more general class of functions
u
, such as the interesting edge case
u(x)=u_{1}(x)=x
. Further topics worth looking into include a study of the degenerate elliptic
u
-d’Alembertian and relevant comparison results in analogy with
[
2
,
7
]
, as well as hyperbolic Orlicz spaces
L^{u}
which should fit into Gigli’s
[
12
]
setting of hyperbolic Banach spaces.

Appendix A
Global hyperbolicity and order-theoretic completeness

In this appendix, we clarify the relationship between certain notions of order-theoretic completeness recently studied for the chronological
[
8
]
and causal
[
2
,
12
]
relations, and global hyperbolicity. Assuming closedness of the causal relation, these turn out to be equivalent notions for (finite-dimensional) smooth spacetimes, mirroring the well-known equivalence between completeness and properness for a Riemannian manifold as a consequence of the Hopf–Rinow theorem. Recall that
\tilde{g}
is a background complete Riemannian metric on
M
.

Definition A.1

(Causal forward/backward completeness)
.

A spacetime
(M,g)
is called
causally forward complete
if every sequence
(x_{n})\subseteq M
with
x_{n}\leq x_{n+1}\leq z
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and some
z\in M
, is convergent in
M
. Similarly, we call
(M,g)

causally backward complete
if it is forward complete when considered with the opposite time orientation. Analogously, we say
(M,g)
is
chronologically forward complete
if every sequence
x_{n}\ll x_{n+1}\ll z
converges, and
chronologically backward complete
if its time-reversal is chronologically forward complete.

Lemma A.2

(Chronological completeness = causal completeness)
.

A spacetime
(M,g)
is chronologically forward (resp. backward) complete if and only if it is causally forward (resp. backward) complete.

Proof.

We only show the claim for the forward completeness notions. It is trivial that every causally forward complete spacetime is chronologically forward complete. For the converse direction, suppose
(M,g)
is chronologically forward complete and let
p_{n}\leq p_{n+1}\leq z
be a causally bounded ordered sequence. We claim that we can construct a sequence
(x_{n})
in such a way that
x_{n}\ll x_{n+1}\leq z
,
x_{n}\ll p_{n}
,
d_{\tilde{g}}(x_{n},p_{n})<n^{-1}
. Once we have done this, picking any
\tilde{z}\in I^{+}(z)
shows the necessary chronological boundedness of
x_{n}
, from which convergence of
(x_{n})
and thus of
(p_{n})
follows, establishing causal forward completeness.

To construct
x_{n}
, we proceed by induction. Pick
x_{1}\in I^{-}(p_{1})
such that
d_{\tilde{g}}(x_{1},p_{1})<1
. Suppose
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
have been constructed, then by assumption
x_{n}\ll p_{n}\leq p_{n+1}\leq z
. By push-up,
x_{n}\ll p_{n+1}
, so we may pick
x_{n+1}\in I^{+}(x_{n})\cap I^{-}(p_{n+1})
in such a way that
d_{\tilde{g}}(x_{n+1},p_{n+1})<(n+1)^{-1}
. Thus we have constructed the desired sequence
(x_{n})
.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Complete
\Rightarrow
causal)
.

Let
(M,g)
be either causally forward or causally backward complete. Then it is causal, i.e. there are no closed causal curves.

Proof.

We argue via causal forward completeness (the argument using causal backward completeness is analogous). Arguing by contradiction, suppose
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is a nontrivial closed causal curve, so that
\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)
. So there exists
t_{0}\in(a,b)
such that
\gamma(t_{0})\neq\gamma(a)
. Define a sequence
(x_{n})
via

x_{2n}:=\gamma(a),\quad x_{2n+1}:=\gamma(t_{0}).

Then
x_{n}\leq x_{n+1}\leq\gamma(b)
, and since
\gamma(a)\neq\gamma(t_{0})
,
(x_{n})
is not convergent, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem A.4

(Equivalence of order-theoretic completeness and global hyperbolicity)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth spacetime such that the causal relation is closed in
M^{2}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

(ii)

(M,g)
is chronologically forward complete.

(iii)

(M,g)
is chronologically backward complete.

(iv)

(M,g)
is causally forward complete.

(v)

(M,g)
is causally backward complete.

Proof.

We have shown
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
and
(iii)\Leftrightarrow(v)
in Lemma
A.2
. We show now that
(i)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
, the equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(v)
is analogous.

(i)\Rightarrow(iv):
Suppose
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic, and let
p_{n}\leq p_{n+1}\leq y
. Letting
x:=p_{1}
, we have that
p_{n}\in J(x,y)
for every
n
. By compactness of the latter, every subsequence of
(p_{n})
has a further subsequence that converges to some point in
J(x,y)
. All of these sublimits have to agree: Indeed, if
p_{n_{k}}
and
p_{m_{l}}
are two subsequences of
(p_{n})
with limits
p,q
, then for fixed
k
, for large enough
l
we have that
p_{n_{k}}\leq p_{m_{l}}
, so taking
l\to\infty
gives
p_{n_{k}}\leq q
, hence taking
k\to\infty
we get
p\leq q
, which yields
p=q
by symmetry. From this, it is easy to conclude that in fact the sequence
(p_{n})
itself has to converge, showing
(iv)
.

(iv)\Rightarrow(i):
By Lemma
A.3
,
(M,g)
is causal, and by assumption the causal relation is closed, so it suffices to show that causal diamonds
J(x,y)
are bounded for every
x,y\in M
. Indeed, suppose not and take
p_{n}\in J(x,y)
such that
d_{\tilde{g}}(x,p_{n})>n
. Connect
x
to
p_{n}
by
\tilde{g}
-arclength parametrised causal curves
\gamma_{n}:[0,b_{n}]\to M
. We may apply the limit curve theorem (see e.g. Minguzzi
[
23
, Thm. 3.1]
) to conclude the subsequential existence of an inextendible causal curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
starting at
x
. Since
\gamma
is the locally
\tilde{g}
-uniform limit of the
\gamma_{n}
, all of which lie in
J(x,y)
, by closedness of the latter also
\gamma([0,\infty))\subseteq J(x,y)
. Defining
z_{n}:=\gamma(n)
gives a sequence
z_{n}\leq z_{n+1}\leq y
which does not converge, contradicting
(iv)
.
∎

It would be interesting to examine whether the closedness assumption on the causal order can be removed from the above result, and if not, to construct an example demostrating this.

Remark A.5

(On the generality of the equivalence)
.

The only tool we used to prove the equivalence of chronological/causal forward/backward completeness and global hyperbolicity in this section was the limit curve theorem for inextendible causal curves, as well as approximability by points in their timelike futures/pasts in conjunction with the openness of the timelike relation. In particular, Theorem
A.4
holds verbatim for Lorentz-Finsler spacetimes. Moreover, in the generality of Lorentzian pre-length spaces as studied by Kunzinger-Sämann
[
16
]
, its proof yields the equivalence of compactness of causal diamonds with chronological/causal forward/backward completeness provided the Lorentzian pre-length space is causally closed, localizable, and its background positive definite metric is proper (cf.
[
16
, Thm. 3.14]
). It is unclear in this generality whether causality and compactness of causal diamonds are enough to recover global hyperbolicity in the sense of
[
16
, Def. 2.35(v)]
.
