AdaBoost Does Not Always Cycle: A Computer-Assisted Counterexample
Erik Y. Wang

Abstract
We give a computer-assisted counterexample to the open question, posed by Rudin, Schapire, and Daubechies in COLT 2012, of whether exhaustive AdaBoost always converges to a finite cycle. The construction is based on a block-product gadget whose two factors share an exact period-2 orbit for their 5-step branch maps, but whose linearized return maps have dominant eigenvalues with an irrational logarithmic ratio. This irrationality forces the burst-winner sequence to have an irrational asymptotic frequency, precluding eventual periodicity. All assertions are certified by exact rational arithmetic. This work was developed in collaboration with GPT-5.4 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6.

Introduction

The AdaBoost algorithm
[
3
]
defines a discrete dynamical system on the probability simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{m}

[
7
]
, where a sequence of distributions
D_{t}
is updated based on an
m\times N
matrix
M\in\{-1,+1\}^{m\times N}
representing the correlations between
N
weak hypotheses and
m
training examples. Under the exhaustive selection of weak hypotheses, the next distribution is determined by the mapping
D_{t+1}(i)=D_{t}(i)/(1+r_{t}M_{ij_{t}})
, where
j_{t}=\text{argmax}_{j}(D_{t}^{\top}M)_{j}
and
r_{t}=(D_{t}^{\top}M)_{j_{t}}
. While it is proven that
D_{t}
converges to a unique fixed point when the weak learning assumption (
r_{t}\geq c>0
) is violated, the behavior remains mathematically elusive when the assumption holds. The iterative map is highly discontinuous due to the
argmax
operation, leaving the long-term asymptotic behavior—specifically whether the sequence
\{D_{t}\}
always enters a cycle of length
l
such that
D_{kl+b}\to\bar{D}_{b}
as
k\to\infty
—an open question
[
7
,
8
]
.

The problem under consideration is the following: prove or disprove that for every
\{-1,+1\}
-valued matrix
M
, the sequence of distributions
D_{t}
computed by the AdaBoost
[
8
]
mapping necessarily converges to a cycle. In this paper, we disprove the conjecture by constructing an explicit counterexample.

Proof outline

We construct a
20\times 8
sign matrix
M_{0}=L_{A}\boxplus L_{B}
as a block product of two small gadgets whose dynamics decouple on an invariant product manifold (Section
3
). Both gadgets share an exact period-2 orbit for the 5-step branch maps (Section
6
). The linearized return maps at this orbit have dominant eigenvalues
\lambda
and
\kappa
whose ratio
\log\lambda/\log\kappa
is irrational, as shown by a norm argument in algebraic number theory (Section
7
). Rigorous interval arithmetic certifies explicit invariant sectors around the orbit, together with contraction rates and burst-locking margins (Sections
8
–
10
). Within these sectors, the irrational eigenvalue ratio forces the burst-winner sequence to have an irrational asymptotic frequency, which precludes eventual periodicity and hence finite-cycle convergence (Sections
11
–
13
). A row-duplication argument converts an explicit rational starting point into a uniform-start counterexample on a larger matrix
\widetilde{M}
with weak-learning margin
1/5
(Section
14
).

Main result

We work with exhaustive AdaBoost on a fixed sign matrix
M\in\{-1,+1\}^{m\times N}
, with smallest-index tie-breaking.
Defining the
edge

\mu_{t}(j)

of hypothesis
j
at time
t
by

\mu_{t}(j)=(D_{t}^{\top}M)_{j},\qquad j_{t}=\min\operatorname*{argmax}_{1\leq j\leq N}\mu_{t}(j),\qquad r_{t}=\mu_{t}(j_{t}),

the update is

D_{t+1}(i)=\frac{D_{t}(i)}{1+r_{t}M_{ij_{t}}}.

We call the distribution
D_{t}\in\Delta^{m-1}
the
state
of the system at time
t
; the map
D_{t}\mapsto D_{t+1}
defined above is a single AdaBoost
update step
.
Let
\Delta^{m-1}
be the
m-1
-dimensional simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The
weak-learning margin
of
M
is

\gamma(M)=\min_{D\in\Delta^{m-1}}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}(D^{\top}M)_{j}.

We show the following result:

Theorem 1
.

There exists a finite
\{-1,+1\}
-valued matrix
\widetilde{M}
with weak-learning margin

\gamma(\widetilde{M})=\frac{1}{5}>0

such that standard exhaustive AdaBoost on
\widetilde{M}
, started from the exact uniform distribution and using smallest-index tie-breaking, does
not
converge to any finite cycle.

Consequently, the COLT question “does exhaustive AdaBoost always converge to a finite cycle?” has a
negative
answer.

Corollary 2
.

There exists a finite
\{-1,+1\}
-valued matrix
\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
whose column set is closed under negation (i.e., for every column
h
of
\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
,
-h
also appears as a column of
\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
), with weak-learning margin

\gamma(\widetilde{M}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{5}>0,

such that standard exhaustive AdaBoost on
\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
, started from the exact uniform distribution and using smallest-index tie-breaking, does
not
converge to any finite cycle. In particular, the COLT question in its original hypothesis-class-closed-under-negation formulation also has a negative answer.

The proof of the Corollary appears at the end of Section
14
.

The remainder of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
, which occupies Sections
3
–
14

(Reduction to row marginals)
.

\displaystyle(W^{\top}M_{0})_{A_{j}}

\displaystyle=(a^{\top}L_{A})_{j}\qquad(1\leq j\leq 4),

\displaystyle(W^{\top}M_{0})_{B_{j}}

\displaystyle=(b^{\top}L_{B})_{j}\qquad(1\leq j\leq 4).

Lemma 4

(Exact invariance of the product manifold)
.

Let

\mathcal{P}=\{a\otimes b:\ a\in\Delta^{3},\ b\in\Delta^{4}\}.

Margin

We show using a single linear witness that the weak-learning margin of the block-product matrix

is exactly

. Let
\beta=(2,1,1,1)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 4}
. Every row

of
L_{A}
satisfies

, and every row
r
of
L_{B}
satisfies

.
For any gadget
G\in\{A,B\}
and any distribution
u
on the rows of
L_{G}
, write
\mu_{j}=(u^{\top}L_{G})_{j}
for the edge of column

Then

2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}+\mu_{4}=u^{\top}L_{G}\,\beta\geq 1,

so
\max_{j}\mu_{j}\geq 1/5
. Hence

\gamma(L_{A}),\gamma(L_{B})\geq\frac{1}{5}.

Equality for
L_{A}
is attained at

u_{*}=(1/5,2/5,1/5,1/5),\qquad u_{*}^{\top}L_{A}=(1/5,1/5,1/5,1/5).

It follows that
\gamma(L_{A})=1/5
. Since the first four rows of
L_{B}
coincide with
L_{A}
, the same
argument gives
\gamma(L_{B})\leq 1/5
, and together with the lower bound above,

\gamma(L_{A})=\gamma(L_{B})=\frac{1}{5}.

By Lemma
3
, for any distribution
W
on the rows of
M_{0}
with marginals
(a,b)
,

\max_{1\leq j\leq 8}(W^{\top}M_{0})_{j}=\max\Bigl(\max_{1\leq j\leq 4}(a^{\top}L_{A})_{j},\ \max_{1\leq j\leq 4}(b^{\top}L_{B})_{j}\Bigr).

Conversely, every pair of marginals
(a,b)
is realized by the product distribution
a\otimes b
.
Hence

\gamma(M_{0})=\min_{W}\max_{j}(W^{\top}M_{0})_{j}=\min_{a,b}\max\Bigl(\max_{j}(a^{\top}L_{A})_{j},\ \max_{j}(b^{\top}L_{B})_{j}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{5}.

Lemma 5

(Regularity of the branch maps)
.

The denominators
Q_{A},R_{A}
are nonzero on
\Sigma_{A}^{\circ}:=\{(p,d):\tfrac{1}{2}<p<1,\ 0<d<\tfrac{1}{2}\}
, and
Q_{B},R_{B}
are nonzero on
\Sigma_{B}^{\circ}=\{(p,d,e):\tfrac{1}{2}<p<1,\ d>0,\ e>0,\ d+e<\tfrac{1}{2}\}
.
Moreover, the branch maps
F_{i}^{A},F_{i}^{B}
and all their derivatives are smooth on these domains.

Proof.

Observe that every column of
L_{A}
contains both
+1
and
-1
entries.

Strict positivity implies
|\mu|<1
.
If
a\in\Delta^{m-1}
is strictly positive and
h\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}
has both signs, then

\mu=a^{\top}h=\sum_{h_{i}=+1}a_{i}-\sum_{h_{i}=-1}a_{i}.

Both partial sums are strictly positive, so
-1<\mu<1
. Hence every denominator
1+\mu h_{i}\in\{1+\mu,\,1-\mu\}
is strictly positive, and the updated vector

Review of interval arithmetic

We briefly discuss the framework. In
interval arithmetic

[
6
]
, every real quantity is enclosed by a closed interval
[\underline{x},\overline{x}]\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\underline{x}\leq\overline{x}
, and each elementary operation
\circ\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}
is replaced by an interval extension satisfying the
inclusion property
: whenever
x\in[\underline{x},\overline{x}]
and
y\in[\underline{y},\overline{y}]
,

x\circ y\;\in\;[\underline{x},\overline{x}]\circ[\underline{y},\overline{y}].

For example,
[\underline{x},\overline{x}]+[\underline{y},\overline{y}]=[\underline{x}+\underline{y},\,\overline{x}+\overline{y}]
and
[\underline{x},\overline{x}]\times[\underline{y},\overline{y}]=[\min S,\,\max S]
where
S=\{\underline{x}\,\underline{y},\,\underline{x}\,\overline{y},\,\overline{x}\,\underline{y},\,\overline{x}\,\overline{y}\}
; division requires
0\notin[\underline{y},\overline{y}]
. By induction on the number of operations, evaluating any rational function
f
via interval arithmetic on an axis-aligned box
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
—provided no intermediate divisor interval contains zero—yields an interval
I
satisfying
f(x)\in I
for every
x\in B
; that is,
I\supseteq\{f(x):x\in B\}
.

In our certificate, all interval endpoints are exact rational numbers,
so every enclosure is justified.
Also, Lemma
5
guarantees that all denominators arising from the branch maps are nonzero on the relevant domains, ensuring that no division by an interval containing zero occurs. This is the mechanism by which the branch maps, their Jacobians, and their Hessians are enclosed on the phase boxes defined below.

We emphasize that we always use closed intervals

Local branch words and whole-burst locking

Within the invariant sectors, we must verify two properties: that the column-selection sequence within each gadget matches the expected half-word (
H_{0}
or
H_{1}
), and that once a gadget wins the first round of a 5-step burst, it wins all five rounds. Both are established by certified gap bounds.

The certificate (Items 11–12 of Appendix

) checks that at each step of each half-word on the full phase boxes, the corresponding column has the largest edge among the four columns of the active gadget. The minimum margin is

g_{\mathrm{br}}^{\mathrm{box}}>0.0250983508506417014.

It also certifies that at steps
2,3,4,5
of each half-word the selected edge exceeds
\mu_{*}=2p_{*}-1
by at least

\Delta_{\mathrm{box}}>0.0758691457701749199.

The largest possible first-edge surplus on any phase box is only

\delta_{\mathrm{first}}^{\max}<1.05687287632489371\times 10^{-7}.

Consequently, at steps
2,3,4,5
of any burst the active gadget’s selected edge exceeds the best edge of the frozen gadget by at least
\Delta_{\mathrm{box}}-\delta_{\mathrm{first}}^{\max}
(which we denote as the

):

\Delta_{\mathrm{box}}-\delta_{\mathrm{first}}^{\max}>0.0758690400828872874.

These bounds have three immediate consequences. First, the column-selection sequence within the A-gadget is exactly
H_{0}
on
S_{0}^{A}
and exactly
H_{1}
on
S_{1}^{A}
; likewise for the B-gadget on
S_{0}^{B}
and
S_{1}^{B}
. Second, and more importantly, once a gadget wins the first round of a burst, it stays globally ahead for the remaining four rounds of that burst. To see this, observe that during an A-burst the B-state does not move at all, so the best B-correlation throughout the burst is bounded by its first-round best edge; the same holds with A and B reversed. Since the strict margin

Lemma 8

(Burst-ratio dynamics and run-length bounds)
.

For all sufficiently large
n
, one has

. Moreover:

(i)

every A-run has length exactly
1
;

(ii)

every B-run has length at most
2
.

Consequently, every block of three consecutive bursts contains at least one A-burst and at least one B-burst, and

x_{n}+s_{n}\to 0
.

Proof.

Entry into the compact interval.
After a B-burst at step
n
(so
r_{n}<1
), we have
r_{n+1}\leq r_{n}/m_{B}<1/m_{B}
. After an A-burst at step
n
(so
r_{n}\geq 1
), we have
r_{n+1}\geq m_{A}r_{n}\geq m_{A}
. Once the trajectory has experienced at least one A-burst and one B-burst,
r_{n}
enters and remains in
[m_{A},1/m_{B}]
.
Both burst types must eventually occur: if only A-bursts occurred, then
r_{n}\leq M_{A}^{n}r_{0}\to 0
(since
M_{A}<1
), eventually forcing
r_{n}<1
and hence a B-burst; symmetrically, if only B-bursts occurred, then
r_{n}\geq r_{0}/M_{B}^{n}\to\infty
(since
M_{B}<1
), eventually forcing
r_{n}\geq 1
and hence an A-burst.

A-runs have length 1.
Suppose A wins at step
n
, so
r_{n}\geq 1
, and that
r_{n}\leq 1/m_{B}
(since we are in the compact interval). Then,

r_{n+1}\leq M_{A}r_{n}\leq\frac{M_{A}}{m_{B}}<1,

where the last inequality is the certified bound
M_{A}/m_{B}<1
. Hence B wins at step
n+1
, and every A-run has length exactly
1
.

B-runs have length at most 2.
Suppose B wins at step
n
, so
r_{n}<1
, and that
r_{n}\geq m_{A}
. After one B-burst,

r_{n+1}\geq\frac{r_{n}}{M_{B}}\geq\frac{m_{A}}{M_{B}}.

If
r_{n+1}\geq 1
, the B-run ends after one step. If
r_{n+1}<1
, then B wins again, and

where the last inequality is the certified bound

. Hence A wins at step

, and every B-run has length at most

.

Exponential convergence.
By Steps 2 and 3, every block of three consecutive bursts contains at least one A-burst and at least one B-burst, so

∎

We now establish that the principal coordinates along each gadget’s dominant eigendirection contract at rates asymptotically governed by

\displaystyle x_{m+1}^{A}

\displaystyle=c_{A}x_{m}^{A}\bigl(1+\varepsilon_{m}^{A}\bigr),

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{m}^{A}|

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{A}\tau_{m}^{A}+\Delta_{A}x_{m}^{A},

\displaystyle x_{m+1}^{B}

\displaystyle=c_{B}x_{m}^{B}\bigl(1+\varepsilon_{m}^{B}\bigr),

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{m}^{B}|

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{B}\tau_{m}^{B}+\Delta_{B}x_{m}^{B},

with certified constants

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}

\displaystyle\leq 0.003273115711042833,

\displaystyle\Delta_{A}

\displaystyle\leq 5.232171784223787,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{B}

\displaystyle\leq 0.31803397447249865,

\displaystyle\Delta_{B}

\displaystyle\leq 8.463704731868965,

\displaystyle\leq 0.00000340392000564842791

\displaystyle\Gamma_{B}\tau+\Delta_{B}x

\displaystyle\leq 0.000318245567090795350

In particular, the relative errors are everywhere

Since

and

, both local

-sequences are summable. Iterating the transverse recurrence

Since

throughout, the bound
|\log(1+u)|\leq 2|u|
applies, giving

\displaystyle\log x_{m}^{A}

\displaystyle=\log x_{0}^{A}+m\log c_{A}+O(1),

\displaystyle\log x_{m}^{B}

\displaystyle=\log x_{0}^{B}+m\log c_{B}+O(1).

Let

denote the number of A-bursts among bursts
0,\dots,n-1
, and let
N_{B}(n)=n-N_{A}(n)
.
At burst boundary
n
, the A-state has been updated exactly
N_{A}(n)
times and the B-state exactly
N_{B}(n)
times,
so

\displaystyle\log x_{n}

\displaystyle=\log x_{0}+N_{A}(n)\log c_{A}+O(1),

\displaystyle\log s_{n}

\displaystyle=\log s_{0}+N_{B}(n)\log c_{B}+O(1).

stays in the

N_{A}(n)\log c_{A}-N_{B}(n)\log c_{B}=O(1).

Since

\frac{N_{A}(n)}{n}\to\frac{-\log c_{B}}{-\log c_{A}-\log c_{B}}=\frac{-\log\kappa}{-\log\lambda-\log\kappa}.

Because

Discussion of collaboration with AI

One of the primary goals of this research was to push the limits of what can be achieved with large language models (LLMs) for novel mathematical research. While many recent efforts have targeted open problems in mathematics, there have been fewer notable results in other areas of the mathematical sciences, such as theoretical computer science. We searched for problems on SolveAll
[
2
]
—a curated collection of open research problems spanning machine learning—and selected this problem.

The bulk of the research took roughly two weeks of effort. We began by posing the problem to various frontier language models as a single-shot task. Using the problem statement from SolveAll (shown below), we prompted GPT-5.4 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6, and Gemini Deep Think to solve the problem.

Problem statement (SolveAll)

Let
S=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a fixed binary-labeled dataset with
y_{i}\in\{-1,+1\}
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a weak-hypothesis class. Consider the discrete AdaBoost update of Freund & Schapire (1997), in the exhaustive weak-learner regime used in Rudin et al. (2012), with weight vectors
w_{t}\in\Delta^{m-1}
(the probability simplex). At iteration
t
, choose

h_{t}\in\arg\max_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{t,i}\,y_{i}\,h(x_{i}),

define weighted error

\varepsilon_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{t,i}\,\mathbf{1}\{h_{t}(x_{i})\neq y_{i}\},

and update with

\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1-\varepsilon_{t}}{\varepsilon_{t}},\qquad w_{t+1,i}=\frac{w_{t,i}\exp(-\alpha_{t}y_{i}h_{t}(x_{i}))}{Z_{t}},

where
Z_{t}
normalizes to
\sum_{i}w_{t+1,i}=1
.

Equivalently, with finite hypothesis set
\mathcal{H}=\{\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{N}\}
and matrix
M\in\{-1,+1\}^{m\times N}
defined by
M_{ij}=y_{i}\tilde{h}_{j}(x_{i})
, step
t
selects

j_{t}\in\arg\max_{j\in[N]}(w_{t}^{\top}M)_{j},\qquad h_{t}=\tilde{h}_{j_{t}}.

As specified in Rudin et al. (2012), if this argmax is not unique, ties are broken in a fixed deterministic way (for concreteness: pick the smallest index
j
). The generic no-tie condition means the argmax is unique at every iterate, i.e.

(w_{t}^{\top}M)_{j}\neq(w_{t}^{\top}M)_{j^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all }j\neq j^{\prime}\text{ and all }t,

equivalently,
w_{t}
never lands on a tie boundary between weak-hypothesis regions of the simplex.

This induces a discrete dynamical system
T\colon\Delta^{m-1}\to\Delta^{m-1}
by
w_{t+1}=T(w_{t})
.

Unsolved problem target:

Does every trajectory converge to a cycle under natural genericity conditions?

It quickly became apparent that the most sophisticated arguments were produced by GPT-5.4 Pro. Opus 4.6 regularly refused to engage because it recognized the problem as open, and Deep Think consistently returned solutions with numerous errors and logical flaws pointed out by both Opus 4.6 and other copies of itself. We therefore focused on generating solution attempts with GPT-5.4 Pro.

Single-shot prompts often ended in partial reductions or failed attempts. However, instead of using the API, we used the web interface to interact with GPT, which more natively supported multi-turn conversations. This allowed us to push the model to try again even if it refused to solve the problem in its first attempt, often by following up with the prompt, “Good progress. However, you must keep pushing to resolve Rudin, Schapire, and Daubechies’s problem. The only goal is a correct and complete resolution to the problem.”

After many failed attempts—roughly 10 of them—we began to compile previous results from the model into a condensed summary with Claude Opus 4.6, asking it to analyze the various attempts and suggest the most promising directions forward. We then conducted more rounds of summarizing previous attempts and generating new directions to pursue with Claude’s recommendations centered on testing additional reductions or simplified cases, none of which proved fruitful when passed back to GPT. However, at around the fifth summarization attempt, Claude produced the following review and list of potential directions.

Summary and proposed directions (Claude Opus 4.6)

Prior work from our research team

Critical correction: the right margin condition.

One collaborator identified that much of our earlier work used the wrong margin quantity. The quantity
\rho(M)=\max_{d\in\Delta^{m-1}}\min_{j\in[N]}(M^{\top}d)_{j}
(which asks for a distribution over

not

\gamma(M)=\min_{w\in\Delta^{m-1}}\max_{j\in[N]}(w^{\top}M)_{j}=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta^{N-1}}\min_{i\in[m]}(M\lambda)_{i}.

This is the quantity guaranteeing AdaBoost’s chosen edge is uniformly positive. One can make
\rho>0
for any matrix by appending an all-positive row, so “
\rho>0
” does not isolate the hard case. Any proof strategy must use
\gamma(M)>0
.

Useful: clarification that “eventual periodicity” is the wrong target.

An explicit counterexample to

Consequence:
The right conjecture is

Useful: exact reformulation as coordinate descent.

After aggregating examples by their sign pattern

w_{t}(r)=\frac{q_{r}e^{-\lambda_{t}\cdot r}}{\sum_{s}q_{s}e^{-\lambda_{t}\cdot s}},\qquad\lambda_{t+1}=\lambda_{t}+\operatorname{artanh}(\mu_{j_{t}}(\lambda_{t}))\,e_{j_{t}},

This is

Useful: toric invariant structure.

Let

Useful: periodic orbits carry canonical equal-weight vectors.

Correct but low strategic value:
N=3

Under the genuine weak-learning margin

N=3

N\geq 4

Not useful toward a resolution:
N=4

The team exhaustively classified all 13 feasible active families at

This is mathematically correct but

Not useful:

Not useful: repeated reformulations.

Across the prior work, the dynamics was restated as: KL projection / Gauss–Southwell, weighted-homogeneous quotient maps, greedy birational systems on antichain hypergraphs, toric-leaf dynamics, multiaffine polynomial replacement. What is needed: a top-down argument for all

The prior work has been almost entirely bottom-up case analysis. The problem almost certainly requires a structural argument that works at all

Direction 1: Exploit the coordinate-descent / potential structure.

AdaBoost is greedy coordinate descent on

\Delta\lambda_{j}=\operatorname{artanh}(\mu_{j}(\lambda))

Key properties to exploit:

•

•

The gradient

•

•

The key question

This connects to the literature on cyclic and greedy coordinate descent. Relevant results: Beck and Tetruashvili (2013) on convergence rates, Nutini et al. (2015) on greedy coordinate descent, and the general theory of nonlinear Gauss–Seidel methods. The specific structure here (exact minimization, real-analyticity, finite partition of the domain into greedy-selection regions) may admit a proof via the Łojasiewicz inequality or related real-analytic geometry.

Direction 2: Real-analytic dynamics and the branch partition.

The simplex is partitioned into finitely many open regions
\Omega_{j}=\{w:(w^{\top}M)_{j}>(w^{\top}M)_{k}\;\forall k\neq j\}
, and on each region

is eventually periodic.

After quotienting by the toric invariants, orbits live on a compact real-analytic manifold of dimension at most

A possible approach: show that the
\omega
-limit set of any orbit is a finite union of periodic orbits. For piecewise real-analytic maps, this might follow from showing that the orbit can only visit finitely many distinct branch sequences (using that each branch map is a contraction in some metric, or that the potential

provides a strict Lyapunov function for the branch dynamics).

Direction 3: Contraction of composite return maps.

In the

is a strict contraction (
\|DH\|_{\infty}\leq 0.65
). Is this an accident of low dimension, or is there a general mechanism?

For any eventually periodic branch word

, the return map is
R_{\sigma}=T_{j_{p-1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{j_{0}}
. Each

maps the selected coordinate’s error to exactly

and rescales everything else. The conjecture that all such return maps are eventually contracting (in some norm, on the relevant toric leaf) would immediately imply convergence to a unique periodic orbit for each branch word, and then finiteness of realizable branch words would give eventual periodicity.

Direction 4: The
\mu_{j}=0

After selecting hypothesis

\mu_{j}(\lambda^{+})=0

j
has zero edge. This is a strong constraint: it means the orbit is always being projected onto a coordinate hyperplane of the edge space. The itinerary question becomes: starting from a point where
\mu_{j}=0

This “reset” structure—each step annihilates one coordinate of the gradient—is very specific and may admit a combinatorial/topological argument about the sequence of active constraints.

Structural reduction of the cycling problem (GPT-5.4 Pro)

The Rudin–Daubechies–Schapire papers already show that AdaBoost admits genuine low-dimensional cyclic dynamics, and the COLT open problem asks for a full characterization of whether the weight distributions converge to a cycle in general.

I do not have a certified full resolution of the general problem. What I can certify is a sharp structural reduction. It isolates the real obstruction and shows exactly what a complete proof or disproof now has to control.

1. The compact analytic-descent route cannot work.

Let

F(\lambda)=\log\sum_{r}q_{r}e^{-\lambda\cdot r},\qquad\mu_{t}=\max_{j}\mu_{j}(\lambda_{t}),\qquad\beta_{t}=\operatorname{artanh}(\mu_{t}).

Under the correct weak-learning margin assumption
\gamma(M)>0
, every selected edge satisfies

\mu_{t}\geq\gamma>0,

hence

\beta_{t}\geq\beta_{*}:=\operatorname{artanh}(\gamma)>0.

Because AdaBoost performs the exact coordinate minimizer,

F(\lambda_{t+1})-F(\lambda_{t})=\log Z_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1-\mu_{t}^{2})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1-\gamma^{2})<0.

Therefore

F(\lambda_{t})\leq F(\lambda_{0})+t\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1-\gamma^{2})\to-\infty

linearly, and also

\|\lambda_{t}\|_{1}\geq t\,\beta_{*}.

So the orbit does
not
remain in a compact sublevel set of
F
. Any proof strategy based on ordinary real-analytic coordinate-descent convergence to a stationary point is therefore mis-aimed. The asymptotic object is necessarily a drift to infinity with a finite-dimensional transverse dynamics riding on top of it.

That is the first rigorous conclusion: the problem lives at infinity, not in a compact analytic basin.

2. AdaBoost admits an exact asynchronous product decomposition.

This is the clean top-down structure.

Suppose the sign-pattern space splits as a product

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{A}\times\mathcal{R}_{B}

and the base masses factor as

q(r_{A},r_{B})=q_{A}(r_{A})\,q_{B}(r_{B}).

Write
\lambda=(\lambda^{A},\lambda^{B})
. Then

F(\lambda^{A},\lambda^{B})=\log\sum_{r_{A},r_{B}}q_{A}(r_{A})\,q_{B}(r_{B})\,e^{-\lambda^{A}\cdot r_{A}-\lambda^{B}\cdot r_{B}}=F_{A}(\lambda^{A})+F_{B}(\lambda^{B}).

Consequently

w_{\lambda}(r_{A},r_{B})=w_{\lambda^{A}}^{A}(r_{A})\,w_{\lambda^{B}}^{B}(r_{B}),

and every edge in block
A
depends only on
\lambda^{A}
, every edge in block
B
only on
\lambda^{B}
.

Hence the full AdaBoost dynamics on the product system is exactly:

•

compute the current best local edge in subsystem
A
,

•

compute the current best local edge in subsystem
B
,

•

update only the subsystem whose local best edge is larger.

So global AdaBoost is closed under
asynchronous competition of local AdaBoost subsystems
.

This is the key reduction. Any universal theorem must survive such products. Any counterexample can be built by finding two local subsystems whose competition produces a nonperiodic symbolic schedule.

3. The correct local invariant near a periodic orbit.

Now fix a local subsystem with a period-
p
orbit

x_{0}^{*},\,x_{1}^{*},\,\dots,\,x_{p-1}^{*},

selected coordinates
j_{0},\dots,j_{p-1}
, step sizes
\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{p-1}
, and define

\Gamma:=\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\beta_{t}e_{j_{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

For any active sign pattern
r
on the cycle support, the exact
p
-step update gives

x_{t+p}(r)=x_{t}(r)\frac{e^{-r\cdot\Gamma}}{\prod_{\tau=0}^{p-1}Z_{\tau}}.

Since
x_{t+p}(r)=x_{t}(r)
on the cycle, every active
r
satisfies

r\cdot\Gamma=c,\qquad c:=-\sum_{\tau=0}^{p-1}\log Z_{\tau}.

This recovers the equal-weight relation in the strongest useful form: the whole cycle is encoded by the single vector
\Gamma
, and the active support lies on the affine hyperplane
r\cdot\Gamma=c
.

Now append one extra sign pattern
s
with tiny mass
u
. Along the affine slice

x_{t}(u)=(1-u)\,x_{t}^{*}+u\,\delta_{s},

the current edge of coordinate
j
is exactly

\mu_{t,j}(u)=(1-u)\,\mu_{t,j}^{*}+u\,s_{j}=\mu_{t,j}^{*}+(s_{j}-\mu_{t,j}^{*})\,u.

In particular, for the selected coordinate
j_{t}
,

\mu_{t}(u)=\mu_{t}^{*}+(s_{j_{t}}-\mu_{t}^{*})\,u.

After one full period, the extra mass is multiplied by

u^{\prime}=e^{c-s\cdot\Gamma}\,u+O(u^{2}).

So every extra sign pattern produces a scalar transverse mode whose:

•

instantaneous score correction is
s_{j_{t}}-\mu_{t}^{*}
,

•

full-period contraction exponent is
c-s\cdot\Gamma
.

If the visible cycle is hyperbolically attracting on its toric leaf, this scalar mode persists on an analytic invariant graph.

This is the exact local normal-form data. It is the right replacement for generic “coordinate descent on an analytic function.”

4. The exact normal form that would disprove phasewise cycling.

Here is the decisive reduction.

Assume we have two factorized local gadgets
A,B
, each near a phase-synchronized local cycle, with scalar mode coordinates
u,v>0
, and suppose on the chosen section their current best edges are

E_{A}=\bar{\mu}+a_{A}u,\qquad E_{B}=\bar{\mu}+a_{B}v,\qquad a_{A},a_{B}>0,

while selecting
A
multiplies
u
by
\kappa_{A}\in(0,1)
and leaves
v
unchanged, and selecting
B
multiplies
v
by
\kappa_{B}\in(0,1)
and leaves
u
unchanged.

Set

x=\log\frac{a_{A}u}{a_{B}v},\qquad\alpha=-\log\kappa_{A},\qquad\beta=-\log\kappa_{B}.

Choose the canonical representative
x\in[-\beta,\alpha)
. Then the branch rule is:

x\mapsto\begin{cases}x-\alpha,&x\in[0,\alpha),\\[2.84526pt]
x+\beta,&x\in[-\beta,0).\end{cases}

This map is exactly conjugate to rigid rotation by
\beta
on a circle of length
\alpha+\beta
.

Therefore:

•

if
\beta/(\alpha+\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}
, the symbolic itinerary is periodic;

•

if
\beta/(\alpha+\beta)\notin\mathbb{Q}
, the symbolic itinerary is Sturmian and
not eventually periodic
.

So a complete disproof of universal phasewise cycling reduces to finding one genuine AdaBoost realization of this scalar two-mode normal form with irrational rotation number.

That is a rigorous theorem, not a heuristic.

5. Why the simplest counterexample mechanism does not work.

There is also a rigorous obstruction.

Suppose a scalar mode comes from appending a passive sign pattern
s
that is correct on
every
hypothesis actually visited on the visible cycle, i.e.

s_{j}=+1\quad\text{for all }j\text{ with }\Gamma_{j}>0.

Then

s\cdot\Gamma=\sum_{\Gamma_{j}>0}\Gamma_{j},

so its contraction factor is

\kappa_{s}=e^{c-s\cdot\Gamma}=e^{c-\sum_{\Gamma_{j}>0}\Gamma_{j}},

which is independent of
s
.

Thus two “always-correct passive rows” can never supply two distinct exponents
\kappa_{A}\neq\kappa_{B}
. The naive plan

•

take a constant-edge local cycle,

•

append two harmless always-correct rows,

•

form a product,

•

get irrational rotation,

cannot succeed.

This is why the obvious symmetric examples do not immediately generate a counterexample. Any disproof has to use either sign-changing passive modes or intrinsic stable modes of the visible cycle itself.

6. What this reduces the original problem to.

The problem is no longer “analyze all global orbits directly.” It reduces to a local spectral classification problem at infinity.

A full
negative
solution would follow from one explicit hyperbolic local cycle with two scalar stable modes whose competition realizes the rotation normal form above.

A full
positive
solution would have to prove that no such mode pair can ever occur in any factorized product of hyperbolic local cycles.

So the correct top-down statement is:

The AdaBoost cycling problem is equivalent to ruling in or ruling out irrational circle-rotation codings generated by stable transverse modes of local cycles in asynchronous product systems.

That is the strongest rigorous reduction I can certify from the material supplied plus the structural calculations above.

The unresolved step is explicit realization or universal exclusion of that rotation normal form inside the actual AdaBoost class. The next mathematically sharp target is to classify constant-edge hyperbolic cycles and their stable scalar modes; that is the narrow bottleneck now.

, rational points are dense within it. We can therefore choose a rational starting point:

Write the 20 coordinates of

where

. Duplicate rows receive identical multiplicative updates and remain equal forever. If

is exactly the orbit on

started from

Hence, standard exhaustive AdaBoost on the finite matrix
\widetilde{M}
, from a uniform initialization, does not converge to a cycle.
\blacksquare

This initial proposal contained various imprecisions and other shortcomings, but was developed enough that we could push it toward a tighter argument. From this point, the work consisted primarily of prompting both Claude and GPT to critique GPT’s solutions, then feeding these critiques to GPT in fresh conversations for revision. To avoid misleading GPT with spurious objections, we ran self-verification loops with both Claude and GPT in fresh conversations, iterating until it no longer produced false positives (correct aspects of the solution incorrectly flagged as errors). At the expository level, Claude was primarily used for resolving minor issues and adding technical detail to the text.

We observed that once GPT had identified the approach of constructing a counterexample from a set of sign-matrices that provably did not converge, it became far more autonomous and quickly arrived at the final structure of the solution presented here. This suggests that the model is currently capable of attacking a problem from many angles provided they lead to the same conclusion, but struggles to pivot between approaches with substantively different outcomes—in this case, a disproof via counterexample versus a proof of the conjecture. We also note that the model easily produced the code for the certificate. This is perhaps unsurprising given the coding ability of recent models, but we were nonetheless struck by how nearly it “one-shotted” the certificate.

B.1
Role of the human

While the models drove the majority of the reasoning process, the human authors guided the overall strategy using carefully crafted prompts and by prioritizing certain directions. In particular, since the model could recognize the open status of the problem, it often refused to solve the problem–therefore, adding certain directions was crucial to making progress on the problem. Additionally, it took roughly two weeks and slightly less than 100 prompts with GPT-5.4 Pro to produce the final solution. Since each GPT-5.4 Pro (extended thinking) query could take up to two hours to complete, the selection of which directions to pursue and which to abandon was important from a resources perspective. One such example is the explicit instruction to GPT to pursue the disproof approach, given its numerous failures in attempting a proof.

We reviewed the final solution written by the models along with the certificate code. This component is still critical to the research process, as both Claude and GPT frequently missed gaps, errors, and ambiguities in the solution. While we found that multiple fresh conversations with the models and carefully crafted prompts (using language such as “be very careful in your review, as ambiguity or imprecision in the solution are unacceptable”) generally led to the highest-quality reviews from the models, there were still several areas in the solution that required additional elaboration and clarification that were only identified by human experts. That said, the speed and sophistication with which the top-performing models reasoned through and documented their findings suggest that their capabilities may already be comparable to those of human experts in the field.
