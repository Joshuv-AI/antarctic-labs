A stabilizer interpretation of the (extended) linearized double shuffle Lie algebra
Annika Burmester, Khalef Yaddaden

Abstract
. The linearized double shuffle Lie algebra introduced by Brown reflects the depth-graded structure of multiple zeta values. In a previous paper, the first author introduced an extension of this Lie algebra that accommodates multiple q-zeta values and multiple Eisenstein series. Inspired by the stabilizer interpretation of the double shuffle Lie algebra given by Enriquez and Furusho, we provide in this paper a stabilizer interpretation of both Lie algebras and show that the stabilizers preserve the extension from the first linearized Lie algebra to the second one.

Introduction

Multiple zeta values.
Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of
multiple zeta values
, so
\mathcal{Z}
is spanned by the elements

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}},\qquad k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1.

Here,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}
is the
weight
and
d
is the
depth
.

A key feature of the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
is the two different product expressions. First, let
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
be the non-commutative free algebra generated by the alphabet
X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. The
shuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
is given by
1\shuffle w=w\shuffle 1=w
and

\displaystyle av\shuffle bw=a(v\shuffle bw)+b(av\shuffle w),\qquad a,b\in X,\ v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle.

Let
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
, which is spanned by
1
and all words starting in
x_{0}
and ending in
x_{1}
.

Proposition I
.

There is a surjective algebra morphism

(\mathfrak{H}^{0},\shuffle)\to\mathcal{Z},\quad x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1}\mapsto\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),

which has a unique extension to
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
such that
x_{0}\mapsto 0
and
x_{1}\mapsto 0
.

Note that the shuffle product is homogeneous for the weight and the depth.
Next, consider the non-commutative free algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
generated by the alphabet
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\}
. The
stuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
is defined by
1\ast w=w\ast 1=w
and

\displaystyle y_{i}v\ast y_{j}w=y_{i}(v\ast y_{j}w)+y_{j}(y_{i}v\ast w)+y_{i+j}(v\ast w),\qquad i,j\geq 1,\ v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle.

Let
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle^{0}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
spanned by all words, which do not start in
y_{1}
.

Proposition II
.

There is a surjective algebra morphism

(\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle^{0},\ast)\to\mathcal{Z},\quad y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{d}}\mapsto\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),

which has a unique extension to
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
such that
y_{1}\mapsto 0
.

Note that the stuffle product is homogeneous for the weight and only filtered for the depth.
One of the central open problems in the field is the following well-known conjecture proposed by Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier.

Conjecture III
.

All algebraic relations in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
are obtained from the comparison of the extended maps
(\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle,\shuffle)\to\mathcal{Z}
and
(\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle,\ast)\to\mathcal{Z}
via the regularization map from
[
IKZ06
, Theorem 1]
.

In particular, by Conjecture
III
the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
would be graded by weight. Moreover, the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
is equipped with an increasing depth filtration

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d}\mathcal{Z}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\mid r\leq d),

so

\mathbb{Q}=\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{0}\mathcal{Z}\subset\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{1}\mathcal{Z}\subset\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{2}\mathcal{Z}\subset\cdots.

We are interested in the associated depth-graded algebra

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\bigoplus_{d\geq 1}\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d}\mathcal{Z}/\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d-1}\mathcal{Z}.

Denote by
\zeta_{D}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
the equivalence class of
\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
in
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}
.

The shuffle product is weight homogeneous, hence by Proposition
I
we get a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle,\shuffle)\to\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z},\quad x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1}\mapsto\zeta_{D}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

The stuffle product is only filtered for the depth, and the associated depth-graded product is the shuffle product on
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
, which we denote by
\shuffle_{Y}
. The map given in Proposition
II
induces a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle,\shuffle_{Y})\to\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z},\quad y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{d}}\mapsto\zeta_{D}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

Similar to Conjecture
III
, we expect the following.

Conjecture IV
.

All algebraic relations in the algebra
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}
are obtained by the comparison of the maps
(\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle,\shuffle)\to\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}
and
(\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle,\shuffle_{Y})\to\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}
.

This conjecture motivates the definition of the
linearized double shuffle Lie algebra

\mathfrak{ls}
first given in
[
Bro21
]
, see Definition
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

Conjecture V
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}/(\zeta_{D}(2))\simeq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{ls})^{\vee}.

In particular, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{ls}
is expected to be the graded dual of the indecomposables of
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}/(\zeta_{D}(2))
.
Note that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{ls}
is also studied from the perspective of its bigraded dual Lie coalgebra.
In fact, Maassarani proved in
[
Maa22
]
that this Lie coalgebra is isomorphic to the dihedral Lie coalgebra, studied by Goncharov in
[
Gon98
,
Gon01
]
in the context of multiple polylogarithms and mixed Tate motives.

In Section
1
, we prove that
\mathfrak{ls}
is essentially the stabilizer of the bialgebra coproduct
\Delta_{Y}
, dual to the shuffle product
\shuffle_{Y}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
for the action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
of primitive elements for the bialgebra coproduct
\Delta_{X}
, dual to the shuffle product
\shuffle
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
. This approach is highly inspired from the work in
[
EF18
]
. More precisely, we have the following main result:

Theorem VI

(Theorem
1.16
)
.

We have an isomorphism of bigraded spaces

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y})=\mathfrak{ls}\oplus\mathbb{Q}x_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Q}x_{1}.

The explicit definition of the stabilizer
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y})
is given in Definition
1.6
. We want to highlight the fact that the construction of the stabilizer induces a natural Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y})
. In particular, Theorem
1.16
implies that the space
\mathfrak{ls}
is a Lie algebra, cf Theorem
1.3
. This provides an alternative proof of a result originally stated in
[
Bro21
, Theorem 5.5]
.

Multiple q-zeta values.
For any parameter
0<q<1
, let
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of multiple
q
-zeta values, which may be defined in several ways. A straightforward one is

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left\{\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{q^{n_{1}s_{1}}}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{q^{n_{r}s_{r}}}{(1-q^{n_{r}})^{s_{r}}}\ \middle|\ s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ s_{r}\geq 1\right\}.

For further details on the equivalent definitions and more background of the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, we refer to
[
BK20
]
,
[
Bur23
]
. In the following, we focus on a specific spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
called the

balanced multiple q-zeta values

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
, where
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
s_{r}\geq 1
. The explicit construction is given in
[
Bur24
]
, here we just explain their main properties. For
\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
, the
weight
is
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}+\#\{s_{i}=0\}
and the
depth
is
r-\#\{s_{i}=0\}
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
be the non-commutative free algebra generated by the alphabet
B=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
. The
balanced quasi-shuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
is defined by
1\ast_{b}w=w\ast_{b}1=w
and

\displaystyle b_{i}v\ast_{b}b_{j}w=b_{i}(v\ast_{b}b_{j}w)+b_{j}(b_{i}v\ast_{b}w)+\delta_{ij>0}b_{i+j}(v\ast_{b}w),\quad i,j\geq 0,\ v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle.

Consider the subspace
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
spanned by all words which do not end in
b_{0}
. The subspace
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
freely generated by
(b_{0}^{m}b_{k})_{m\geq 0,k\geq 1}
.

We define the algebra antiautomorphism
\tau:\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
by

\displaystyle\tau(b_{0}^{m}b_{k})=b_{0}^{k-1}b_{m+1}

for all
m\geq 0
,
k\geq 1
. The map
\tau
is an involution.

Proposition VII
.

There is a surjective,
\tau
-invariant algebra morphism

(\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0},\ast_{b})\to\mathcal{Z}_{q},\quad b_{s_{1}}\cdots b_{s_{r}}\mapsto\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}),

which has a unique extension to
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
such that
b_{0}\mapsto 0
.

Note that the balanced quasi-shuffle product is homogeneous for the weight and only filtered for the depth. The map
\tau
is homogeneous for the weight and depth.

As an analogue of Conjecture
III
, we have the following.

Conjecture VIII
.

All algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
are induced by the balanced quasi-shuffle product and the
\tau
-invariance of balanced multiple q-zeta values.

In particular, Conjecture
VIII
would imply that the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is graded by weight. Moreover, the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
has an increasing depth filtration

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d}\mathcal{Z}_{q}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mid r-\#\{s_{i}=0\}\leq d),

and the associated depth-graded algebra is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\bigoplus_{d\geq 1}\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d}\mathcal{Z}_{q}/\operatorname{Fil}_{D}^{d-1}\mathcal{Z}_{q}.

We denote by
\zeta_{q,D}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
the equivalence class of
\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
in
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q}
.

The map
\tau
is weight and depth homogeneous, hence by Proposition
VII
we have
\tau
-invariance in
\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, which is given by the following explicit identity

\displaystyle\zeta_{q,D}(\{0\}^{m_{1}},k_{1},\ldots,\{0\}^{m_{d}},k_{d})=\zeta_{q,D}(\{0\}^{k_{d}-1},m_{d}+1,\ldots,\{0\}^{k_{1}-1},m_{1}+1),

for
k_{j}\geq 1,\ m_{j}\geq 0
. The balanced quasi-shuffle product is filtered for the depth, and the associated depth-graded product is the shuffle product on
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
, which we denote by
\shuffle_{B}
. So the map in Proposition
VII
induces a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle,\shuffle_{B})\to\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q},\quad b_{s_{1}}\cdots b_{s_{r}}\mapsto\zeta_{q,D}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}).

These two properties motivate the definition of the
linearized balanced Lie algebra

\mathfrak{lq}
introduced in
[
Bur25
]
, see Definition
2.5
and Theorem
2.6
. By construction, there is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{lq})^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{D}\mathcal{Z}_{q}/\big(\zeta_{q,D}(2),\zeta_{q,D}(4),\zeta_{q,D}(6)\big).

Note that in contrast to Conjecture
V
for multiple zeta values, we know that this map is not injective.

In Section
2
, we prove that
\mathfrak{lq}
is essentially the stabilizer of the involution
\tau
, for the action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
of primitive elements for the bialgebra coproduct
\Delta_{B}
, dual to the shuffle product
\shuffle_{B}
. The main result is as follows.

Theorem IX

(Theorem
2.16
)
.

We have an isomorphism of bigraded spaces

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)=\mathfrak{lq}\oplus\mathbb{Q}b_{0}.

For the explicit definition of the stabilizer
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
we refer to Definition
2.10
. As before, the stabilizer
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
is naturally equipped with a Lie algebra structure. Therefore, by Theorem
2.16
we obtain an alternative proof for
\mathfrak{lq}
being a Lie algebra, cf Theorem
2.6
. This was originally proven in
[
Bur25
]
.

In Section
3
we relate the two stabilizers
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y})
and

1.16

The injective Lie algebra morphism

\displaystyle\theta^{(10)}:\big(\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y}),\{-,-\}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau),\{-,-\}_{A}\big).

1.
The linearized double shuffle Lie algebra

1.1.
Algebraic setup

Consider the non-commutative free
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
generated by
X:=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. It is equipped with a bialgebra structure with coproduct
\Delta_{X}:\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
, which is the algebra morphism given by

(1.1)

\Delta_{X}(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes x_{i},\quad\text{ for }i\in\{0,1\}.

Next, consider the non-commutative free
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
generated by
Y:=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\}
. It is equipped with a bialgebra structure with coproduct
\Delta_{Y}:\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
, which is the algebra morphism given by

(1.2)

\Delta_{Y}(y_{n})=y_{n}\otimes 1+1\otimes y_{n},\quad\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra morphism

i_{Y}:\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle,\quad y_{n}\mapsto x_{0}^{n-1}x_{1}

is injective. Thanks to that, we will - by abuse of notation - often identify
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle
with its image
i_{Y}(\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle)
in
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
.
The direct sum decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}
-linear spaces

\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle=\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle\oplus\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle x_{0}

induces a surjective
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map

\pi_{Y}:\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle,\quad x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1}x_{0}^{r}\mapsto\begin{cases}y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{d}},&\quad r=0,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear isomorphism

(1.3)

\mathbb{Q}\langle Y\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle/\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle x_{0}.

Let
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
be the free
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra generated by the alphabet
X
. The
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
is isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
is identified with the Lie algebra of primitive elements in
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
for the coproduct
\Delta_{X}
from (
1.1
). Namely,

\mathfrak{Lie}(X)=\{\psi\in\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle\mid\Delta_{X}(\psi)=\psi\otimes 1+1\otimes\psi\}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
is graded by weight, that is, it is equipped with a grading for which
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are of degree
1
and we have the decomposition

\mathfrak{Lie}(X)=\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathfrak{Lie}(X)[m].

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
is also bigraded by weight and depth, that is, it is equipped with a bigrading for which
x_{0}
is of bidegree
(1,0)
and
x_{1}
is of bidegree
(1,1)
and we have the decomposition

(1.4)

\mathfrak{Lie}(X)=\bigoplus_{m\geq 1,\ n\leq m}\mathfrak{Lie}(X)[m,n].

For
\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
, let
d_{\psi}
be the derivation of
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
given by

d_{\psi}(x_{0})=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{\psi}(x_{1})=[x_{1},\psi].

The triple
(\mathfrak{Lie}(X),[-,-],d)
is a post-Lie algebra, so by Proposition-Definition
A.4
we get another Lie algebra bracket on
\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
given by

(1.5)

\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}=d_{\psi_{1}}(\psi_{2})-d_{\psi_{2}}(\psi_{1})+[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}].

The Lie bracket (
1.5
) is usually referred to as the
Ihara bracket
.

Lemma 1.1
.

The elements
x_{0},x_{1}
are central in the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{Lie}(X),\{-,-\})
.

Proof.

For any
\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
, we compute

\displaystyle\{\psi,x_{1}\}=d_{\psi}(x_{1})-d_{x_{1}}(\psi)+[\psi,x_{1}]=[x_{1},\psi]+[\psi,x_{1}]=0.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\{\psi,x_{0}\}=d_{\psi}(x_{0})-d_{x_{0}}(\psi)+[\psi,x_{0}]=-d_{x_{0}}(\psi)+[\psi,x_{0}].

Hence, we want to show that

-d_{x_{0}}(\psi)+[\psi,x_{0}]=0.

As
-d_{x_{0}}+[-,x_{0}]
is a derivation on
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
, it suffices to check this equality for
\psi\in X
. We have

\displaystyle-d_{x_{0}}(x_{1})+[x_{1},x_{0}]

\displaystyle=-[x_{1},x_{0}]+[x_{1},x_{0}]=0,

\displaystyle-d_{x_{0}}(x_{0})+[x_{0},x_{0}]

\displaystyle=0.\qed

Definition 1.2
.

The space
\mathfrak{ls}
consists of all
\psi\in\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
such that

(i)

(\psi\mid x_{i})=0
for
i=0,1
,

(ii)

\Delta_{X}(\psi)=\psi\otimes 1+1\otimes\psi
,

(iii)

\Delta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi))=\pi_{Y}(\psi)\otimes 1+1\otimes\pi_{Y}(\psi)
,

(iv)

(\psi\mid x_{0}^{n-1}x_{1})=0
for
n\geq 2
even.

In
[
Bro21
, Theorem 5.5]
, the following is stated without a detailed proof.

Theorem 1.3
.

The pair
(\mathfrak{ls},\{-,-\})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra.

1.2.
Stabilizer interpretation of the linearized double shuffle Lie algebra

In this section, our aim is to prove Theorem
1.3
by showing that
\mathfrak{ls}
is essentially the stabilizer of the coproduct
\Delta_{Y}
with respect to an action of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{Lie}(X),\{-,-\})
.

For
\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(X)
, let
s_{\psi}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
given by

s_{\psi}:=\ell_{\psi}+d_{\psi},

where
\ell_{\psi}
is the endomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle
given by left multiplication by
\psi
.

Lemma 1.4

(
[
Rac02

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}

Lemma 1.17
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{m-1}{k}\left(y_{m-k}\otimes y_{k+2}+y_{k+2}\otimes y_{m-k}-y_{m-k+1}\otimes y_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1}\right)

Proof.

First, one checks through direct computation that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{m-1}{k}\left(y_{m-k}\otimes y_{k+2}+y_{k+2}\otimes y_{m-k}-y_{m-k+1}\otimes y_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}(y_{m-k+1}\otimes y_{k+1}+y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{m-k}y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left((-1)^{m-k}+(-1)^{k}\right)\binom{m}{k}y_{k+1}\otimes y_{m-k+1}

If

is odd, then for any

2.

2.1.
Algebraic setup

Let

be an alphabet and
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
be the non-commutative free
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by

is the algebra morphism defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{B}(b_{i})=b_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes b_{i},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Let
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle

Therefore, we have a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}\simeq\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle/\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle b_{0}.

Note that
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
is also a subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
.

Let
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
be the free
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra generated by the alphabet

. The algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle
is isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)

is identified with the Lie algebra of primitive elements in

for the coproduct
\Delta_{B}
from (

\mathfrak{Lie}(B)=\{\psi\in\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle\mid\Delta_{B}(\psi)=\psi\otimes 1+1\otimes\psi\}.

The Lie algebra

has weight

and for each

i

where
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)[m]
denotes the homogeneous subspace of weight
m
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
is also bigraded for the weight and depth, where the letter
b_{0}
is of bidegree
(1,0)
and for
i\geq 1
the letter
b_{i}
is of bidegree
(i,1)

\mathfrak{Lie}(B)=\bigoplus_{m\geq,\ n\leq m}\mathfrak{Lie}(B)[m,n],

where
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)[m,n]
denotes the homogeneous subspace of bidegree
(m,n)
.

For a word

\partial_{\psi}^{R,0}
,
\partial_{\psi}^{L,0}
as in (
2.7
) we get

\displaystyle\big(\tau\circ\sigma_{\psi}^{0}\circ\tau\circ\pi_{0}\big)(v)

\displaystyle=\big(\tau\circ\pi_{0}\big)(\psi\tau(v))+\big(\tau\circ\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{R,0}\big)(\tau(v))-\big(\tau\circ\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{L,0}\big)(\tau(v))

\displaystyle=\tau\big(\psi\tau(v)\big)+\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{R,0}(v)+\sec(\pi_{0}(\psi))v-\tau\big(\sec(\pi_{0}(\psi))\tau(v)\big)-\partial_{\rho(\pi_{0}(\psi))}^{L,0}(v),

where the second equality follows from Proposition
2.14
. Applying again Proposition
2.12
and using the
\rho
-invariance of
\pi_{0}(\psi)
(cf Proposition
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big(\tau\circ\sigma_{\psi}^{0}\circ\tau\circ\pi_{0}\big)(v)

\displaystyle=\tau\big(\psi\tau(v)\big)+\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{R,0}(v)+\psi v-\tau\big(\psi\tau(v)\big)-\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{L,0}(v)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\pi_{0}(\psi)}^{0}(v)+\ell_{\psi}(v)

\displaystyle=\big(\pi_{0}\circ\partial_{\psi}\big)(v)+\ell_{\psi}(v)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{0}\circ\sigma_{\psi}\big)(v).\qed

The next result is nearly the converse statement of Proposition

Lemma 2.15
.

We have the following equality of subspaces of
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)\subset\{\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(B)\mid\tau(\pi_{0}(\psi))=\pi_{0}(\psi)\}.

Proof.

For
\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
, we compute

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\psi}^{0}\circ\tau(1)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\psi}^{0}(1)=\pi_{0}\circ\sigma_{\psi}(1)=\pi_{0}\circ\ell_{\psi}(1)+\pi_{0}\circ\partial_{\psi}(1)=\pi_{0}(\psi),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\tau\circ\sigma_{\psi}^{0}(1)

\displaystyle=\tau\circ\pi_{0}\circ\ell_{\psi}(1)+\tau\circ\pi_{0}\circ\partial_{\psi}(1)=\tau(\pi_{0}(\psi)).

For
\psi\in\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
, (

) and (

) agree by definition and hence we get the desired inclusion.
∎

Theorem 2.16
.

We have the following equality of bigraded subspaces of
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)=\mathfrak{lq}\oplus\mathbb{Q}b_{0}.

Proof.

The space
\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
is bigraded by weight and depth, where
b_{0}
is of bidegree
(1,0)
and
b_{i}
is of bidegree
(i,1)
for
i\geq 1
. We consider several cases depending on the bidegree
(m,n)
.

Case 1

(m,n)=(1,0)
. We show that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[1,0]=\mathfrak{lq}[1,0]\oplus\mathbb{Q}b_{0}.

By definition of
\mathfrak{lq}
, we have
\mathfrak{lq}[1,0]=0
. On the other hand, we have
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[1,0]\subset\mathfrak{Lie}(B)[1,0]=\mathbb{Q}b_{0}
. We compute
\sigma_{b_{0}}(w)=\ell_{b_{0}}w+\partial_{b_{0}}(w)=b_{0}w+[w,b_{0}]=wb_{0}
. Thus, the projection of
\sigma_{b_{0}}(w)
to
\mathbb{Q}\langle B\rangle^{0}
is always zero,

\sigma_{b_{0}}^{0}\equiv 0.

In particular,
\sigma_{b_{0}}^{0}
commutes with
\tau
and thus
b_{0}\in\mathfrak{Lie}(B)
is contained in
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
. We obtain equality (
2.10
).

Case 2

m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
. We have that

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[m,n]=\mathfrak{lq}[m,n].

This follows from Proposition
2.11
and Lemma
2.15
together with the observation that
\mathfrak{lq}
and
\{\psi\in\mathfrak{Lie}(B)\mid\tau(\pi_{0}(\psi))=\pi_{0}(\psi)\}
coincide in bidegree
(m,n)
.

Case 3

m\geq 2
is odd and
n=1
. By the same argumentation as in Case 2, we get

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[m,1]=\mathfrak{lq}[m,1].

Case 4

m\geq 2
is even and
n=1
. We show that

\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[m,1]=\mathfrak{lq}[m,1].

By definition of
\mathfrak{lq}
, we have
\mathfrak{lq}[m,1]=0
. On the other hand, by construction we have
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[m,1]\subset\mathfrak{Lie}(B)[m,1]=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}\mathbb{Q}\cdot\operatorname{ad}_{b_{0}}^{m-k}(b_{k})
. Let
\psi=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}\operatorname{ad}_{b_{0}}^{m-k}(b_{k})
for some
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and assume that
\psi\in\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
. We show that this implies
\psi=0
. Then, we have
\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)[m,1]=0
and hence the claimed equality.

We compute

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\psi}^{0}\circ\tau(b_{1})=\sigma_{\psi}^{0}(b_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}\pi_{0}\Big(\operatorname{ad}_{b_{0}}^{m-k}(b_{k})b_{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}(-1)^{k+l}\binom{k-1}{l-1}[b_{1+k-l},\operatorname{ad}_{b_{0}}^{m-k}(b_{l})]\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}\Bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{m-k}(-1)^{m-k-n}\binom{m-k}{n}b_{0}^{n}b_{k}b_{0}^{m-k-n}b_{1}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}(-1)^{k+l}\binom{k-1}{l-1}b_{1+k-l}b_{0}^{m-k}b_{l}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sum_{n=0}^{m-k}(-1)^{m+l-n}\binom{k-1}{l-1}\binom{m-k}{n}b_{0}^{n}b_{l}b_{0}^{m-k-n}b_{1+k-l}\Bigg),

and thus

(2.12)

\displaystyle\tau\circ\sigma^{0}_{\psi}(b_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}\Bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{m-k}(-1)^{m-k-n}\binom{m-k}{n}b_{m-k-n+1}b_{0}^{k-1}b_{n+1}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\sum_{l=1}^{k}(-1)^{k+l}\binom{k-1}{l-1}b_{0}^{l-1}b_{m-k+1}b_{0}^{k-l}b_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sum_{n=0}^{m-k}(-1)^{m+l-n}\binom{k-1}{l-1}\binom{m-k}{n}b_{0}^{k-l}b_{m-k-n+1}b_{0}^{l-1}b_{n+1}\Bigg).

As we assumed
\psi\in\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(B)}(\tau)
, the two expressions (
2.11

must agree in both expressions. In (
2.11

(2.13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}\Big((-1)^{t_{2}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}-(-1)^{t_{2}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}\Big)=0,

and in (
2.12

\displaystyle\lambda_{t_{1}+t_{2}+1}\Big((-1)^{t_{2}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}-(-1)^{m-t_{2}-1}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-(-1)^{m-1}\Big)\lambda_{t_{1}+t_{2}+1}(-1)^{t_{2}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{t_{1}+t_{2}+1}(-1)^{t_{2}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{2}}{t_{1}}

The last equality follows from

being even. As (
2.13
) and (
Case 4
) must coincide, we deduce that
\lambda_{t_{1}+t_{2}+1}=0
. As

were arbitrarily chosen, we get

\lambda_{k}=0\quad\text{ for }k=2,\ldots,m,

and hence
\psi=\lambda_{1}\operatorname{ad}_{b_{0}}^{m-1}(b_{1})

As the bigraded spaces

and

∎

Thanks to

\theta:(\mathfrak{ls},\{-,-\})\hookrightarrow(\mathfrak{lq},\{-,-\}_{A}),\quad\psi\mapsto\theta_{X}(\psi)+\theta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi)).

Motivated by the inclusions
\mathfrak{ls}\subset\mathfrak{stab}_{\mathfrak{Lie}(X)}(\Delta_{Y})

-Lie algebra morphism

By Lemma

, the space

\{-,-\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
\theta
from (
3.1
) extends to an injective
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra morphism

\displaystyle\theta^{(1)}:\big(\mathfrak{ls}\oplus\mathbb{Q}x_{1},\{-,-\}\big)

\displaystyle\to\big(\mathfrak{lq},\{-,-\}_{A}\big),

\displaystyle\psi\oplus\lambda x_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\theta(\psi)+\lambda b_{1}.

Proof.

Since
\theta:(\mathfrak{ls},\{-,-\})\to(\mathfrak{lq},\{-,-\}_{A})
is a Lie algebra morphism, and since

for any

1.1
; to prove that

\{\theta(\psi),b_{1}\}_{A}=0,\quad\forall\psi\in\mathfrak{ls}.

First, observe that by Remark
2.2
the Lie bracket
\{-,-\}_{A}
equals the Ihara bracket on

Fix some element
\psi\in\mathfrak{ls}

that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\{\theta_{X}(\psi),b_{1}\}_{A}=0.

Since

\displaystyle\{\theta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi)),b_{1}\}_{A}=\{\theta(\psi),b_{1}\}_{A}\in\mathfrak{lq}.

Observe that since

we have

for

(3.4)

\text{each word in }\theta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi))\in\mathbb{Q}\langle b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\rangle\text{ contains at least one letter }b_{i}\text{ with }i>1.

\text{ each word in }\{\theta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi)),b_{1}\}_{A}\text{ contains at least one letter }b_{i}\text{ with }i>1.

\{\theta(\psi),b_{1}\}_{A}=\{\theta_{Y}(\pi_{Y}(\psi)),b_{1}\}_{A}=0.

Appendix A
Review of Lie algebra actions

Definition A.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra and
V
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
acts on the space
V
by endomorphisms if there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra morphism

\mathrm{end}:(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V),\quad\psi\mapsto\mathrm{end}_{\psi}

where the space
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V)
of endomorphisms of
V
is equipped with the bracket

[u,v]:=u\circ v-v\circ u,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V).

Proposition-Definition A.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra acting on a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V
by endomorphisms
\mathrm{end}:(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V)
.
Let
v\in V
. The space

\mathfrak{stab}(v):=\{\psi\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\mathrm{end}_{\psi}(v)=0\}

is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
called the
stabilizer Lie algebra
of the element
v
.

Proof.

For
\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{stab}(v)
, one has

\mathrm{end}_{\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}(v)=\mathrm{end}_{\psi}\circ\mathrm{end}_{\phi}(v)-\mathrm{end}_{\phi}\circ\mathrm{end}_{\psi}(v)=\mathrm{end}_{\psi}(0)-\mathrm{end}_{\phi}(0)=0,

thus proving that
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\in\mathfrak{stab}(v)
.
∎

For a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
A
, denote by
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{Q}{\text{-}}\mathsf{alg}}(A)
the space of derivations of algebras, that is, linear endomorphisms
\delta
of
A
satisfying

\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+a\delta(b),\quad\forall a,b\in A.

One immediately checks that
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{Q}{\text{-}}\mathsf{alg}}(A)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra for the bracket

[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]:=\delta_{1}\circ\delta_{2}-\delta_{2}\circ\delta_{1}.

Definition A.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
be
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra and
A
be
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
acts on the algebra
A
by derivations if there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra morphism

\mathrm{der}:(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)\to\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{Q}{\text{-}}\mathsf{alg}}(A),\quad\psi\mapsto\mathrm{der}_{\psi}.

Proposition-Definition A.4

(
[
EFLMK15
, Proposition 2.2.]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra and
\triangleright:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a bilinear map. Set

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{\triangleright}:=\langle\psi,\phi\rangle+\psi\triangleright\phi-\phi\triangleright\psi,\qquad\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{g}.

If for all
\psi,\phi,\lambda\in\mathfrak{g}

(i)

\psi\ \triangleright
is a derivation on
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
,

(ii)

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{\triangleright}\triangleright\lambda=(\psi\triangleright\phi-\phi\triangleright\psi)\triangleright\lambda
,

then
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle,\triangleright)
is called a
post-Lie algebra
. In this case, the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle_{\triangleright})
forms a Lie algebra.

For a more comprehensive treatment of post‑Lie algebras, the reader may refer to §3 of
[
BCK24
]
.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle,\triangleright)
be a post-Lie algebra. Denote by
U(\mathfrak{g})
the universal enveloping
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle)
.
Then

(a)

The
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle_{\triangleright})
acts on the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
by derivations

\mathrm{der}:(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle_{\triangleright})\to\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{Q}{\text{-}}\mathsf{alg}}(U(\mathfrak{g})),\quad\mathrm{der}_{\psi}:=\psi\triangleright-,

(b)

The
\mathbb{Q}
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle_{\triangleright})
acts on the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear space
U(\mathfrak{g})
by endomorphisms

\mathrm{end}:(\mathfrak{g},\langle-,-\rangle_{\triangleright})\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(U(\mathfrak{g})),\quad\mathrm{end}_{\psi}:=\ell_{\psi}+\mathrm{der}_{\psi},

for any
\psi\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\ell_{\psi}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
is the left multiplication in the algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
by the element
\psi
.

Proof.

(a)

For any
\psi\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have
\mathrm{der}_{\psi}\in\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{Q}{\text{-}}\mathsf{alg}}
thanks to condition
(i)
in Proposition-Definition
A.4
; and for
\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{g}
, the identity

\mathrm{der}_{\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}=[\mathrm{der}_{\psi},\mathrm{der}_{\phi}]

follows from condition
(ii)
.

(b)

Let
\psi,\phi\in\mathfrak{g}
and
w\in U(\mathfrak{g})
. We have

\displaystyle[\mathrm{end}_{\psi},\mathrm{end}_{\phi}](w)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{end}_{\psi}\circ\mathrm{end}_{\phi}(w)-\mathrm{end}_{\phi}\circ\mathrm{end}_{\psi}(w)

\displaystyle=(\ell_{\psi}+\mathrm{der}_{\psi})\circ(\ell_{\phi}+\mathrm{der}_{\phi})(w)-(\ell_{\phi}+\mathrm{der}_{\phi})\circ(\ell_{\psi}+\mathrm{der}_{\psi})(w)

\displaystyle=\psi\phi w+\psi\mathrm{der}_{\phi}(w)+\mathrm{der}_{\psi}(\phi w)+\mathrm{der}_{\psi}\circ\mathrm{der}_{\phi}(w)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\phi\psi w-\phi\mathrm{der}_{\psi}(w)-\mathrm{der}_{\phi}(\psi w)-\mathrm{der}_{\phi}\circ\mathrm{der}_{\psi}(w)
