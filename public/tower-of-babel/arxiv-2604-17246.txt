Polarized light Raman scattering by an atom near an ultrathin periodically aligned carbon nanotube film
SK Firoz Islam, Michael Dean Pugh, Igor V. Bondarev

Abstract
We present a systematic theoretical study of the Raman scattering effect for a two-level atomic system in near proximity of an ultrathin dielectric film with an embedded parallel array of periodically aligned single-wall semiconducting carbon nanotubes. More generally, our model provides a unified description of the quantum near-field medium-assisted enhancement effects for in-plane anisotropic metasurfaces, of which ultrathin periodically aligned carbon nanotube films are the representative example. Particular attention is given to incoming photon parameters of the external light radiation such as polarization and incidence plane orientation relative to the main anisotropy axis (nanotube alignment axis). By explicitly deriving the Raman scattering cross-section, we establish that for the two-level atomic system in the near-field zone of the carbon nanotube metasurface the effect can be enhanced by a factor of up to 10 4 10^{4} , not only for p p -polarized but for s s -polarized light as well.

V
Conclusions

To summarize, we develop a quantum EM theory for anisotropic SERS phenomena by using a fully-quantized, medium-assisted QED approach for an atom modelled by a TLS in near proximity to an ultrathin closely packed SWCN film. This work extends and further develops the SERS theory previously reported by one of us for TLS near a single-wall carbon nanotube
bondarev2015plasmon
. Our model provides a unified description of the quantum near-field medium-assisted enhancement effects for in-plane anisotropic metasurfaces, of which ultrathin periodically aligned SWCN films are the representative example, and so it gives particular attention to incoming photon parameters of the external light radiation such as photon polarization and incidence plane orientation relative to the main anisotropy axis (CN alignment axis). Our theory applies to atomic type species such as atoms, ions, molecules, or semiconductor quantum dots that are
physisorbed
on the
achiral
SWCN metasurface. The effects of SWCN chirality are not included and will be addressed separately elsewhere. The analytical expression we obtain for the Raman cross-section covers both weak and strong TLS–MS coupling, and shows dramatic SERS enhancement in the strong coupling regime. We expect our work to help establish new advanced design concepts for future generation CN based nanophotonics platforms for single molecule/atom/ion detection and optical manipulation in general, to benefit from the extraordinary stability, flexibility and precise tunability of the electromagnetic properties of carbon nanotubes by means of their diameter and chirality variation.

Appendix A:
Averaging of transition dipoles

In general, transition dipole moments
d_{\alpha}=\langle u|\widehat{d}_{\alpha}|l\rangle=\langle l|\widehat{d}_{\alpha}|u\rangle^{\ast}
can be written in the operator form as follows

(A.1)

\widehat{d}_{\alpha}=d_{\alpha}\widehat{\sigma}^{\dagger}+d^{\ast}_{\alpha}\widehat{\sigma},

where
\alpha
is a coordinate index and
\widehat{\sigma}^{\dagger}
,
\widehat{\sigma}
are the Pauli operators. Together with the completeness relation

\displaystyle\widehat{I}

\displaystyle=|l\rangle\langle l|+|u\rangle\langle u|,

we may then expand and simplify pairs of dipoles as follows

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\mu}=d^{\ast}_{\mu}d_{\lambda}=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}|u\rangle\langle u|\widehat{d}_{\lambda}|l\rangle=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}(\widehat{I}-|l\rangle\langle l|)\widehat{d}_{\lambda}|l\rangle\hskip 54.06006pt

\displaystyle=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}\widehat{d}_{\lambda}|l\rangle-\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}|l\rangle\langle l|(d_{\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}^{\dagger}+d^{\ast}_{\lambda}\widehat{\sigma})|l\rangle=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}\widehat{d}_{\lambda}|l\rangle\,.

Repeated use of this technique brings the product in Eq. (
15
) to

(A.2)

d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\mu}d_{\nu}d^{\ast}_{\eta}=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}\widehat{d}_{\lambda}\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\eta}\widehat{d}_{\nu}|l\rangle\,.

We then substitute

(A.3)

\widehat{d}_{\alpha}=e\widehat{r}_{\alpha}=e\sqrt{\!\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_{A}}}\big(\widehat{a}_{\alpha}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\big)\,,

where

\widehat{a}_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{A}}{2\hbar}}\,\widehat{r}_{\alpha}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\widehat{p}_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega_{A}}}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{A}}{2\hbar}}\,\widehat{r}_{\alpha}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\widehat{p}_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega_{A}}}

are canonical ladder operators formed of electron’s canonical position and momentum (see, e.g., Ref.
AbersQM
). This effectively transfers the TLS problem from the two-level atomic space to a Fock space with ground state
|l\rangle\!=\!|\{0\}\rangle
representing the zero-excitation state of a quantum atomic oscillator, so that now one can use the commutation
relations

\displaystyle[\widehat{a}_{\alpha},\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=\widehat{a}_{\alpha}\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}-\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}\widehat{a}_{\alpha}=\mathsf{\delta}_{\alpha\beta}

to eventually obtain

(A.4)

\langle\{0\}|(\widehat{a}_{\mu}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mu})(\widehat{a}_{\lambda}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\lambda})(\widehat{a}_{\eta}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\eta})(\widehat{a}_{\nu}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{\nu})|\{0\}\rangle=\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\lambda}\mathsf{\delta}_{\eta\nu}\,,

yielding

d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\mu}d_{\nu}d^{\ast}_{\eta}=\biggl(\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2m\omega_{A}}\biggl)^{2}\Big(\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\lambda}\mathsf{\delta}_{\eta\nu}\Big)\,.

Note also that

(A.5)

d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\mu}=\langle l|\widehat{d}^{\,\dagger}_{\mu}\widehat{d}_{\lambda}|l\rangle=\frac{e^{2}\hbar}{2m\omega_{A}}\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\lambda}

by the above technique. It then follows directly that

(A.6)

|d|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\lambda}=\frac{3e^{2}\hbar}{2m\omega_{A}}\,,

so that Eq. (
A.4
) takes the form

(A.7)

d_{\lambda}d^{\ast}_{\mu}d_{\nu}d^{\ast}_{\eta}=\frac{|d|^{4}}{3^{2}}\Big(\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\lambda\eta}\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\nu}+\mathsf{\delta}_{\mu\lambda}\mathsf{\delta}_{\eta\nu}\Big)\,.

With this expression, one obtains

(A.8)

which by introducing the traceless symmetric tensor
