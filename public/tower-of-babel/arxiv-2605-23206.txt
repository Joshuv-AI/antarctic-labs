Yukawa-Screened Bose-Star Condensation
Jiajun Chen

Abstract
We study Bose-star formation in a Yukawa-Schrödinger-Poisson (YSP) system. A finite interaction range suppresses the infrared kinetic relaxation responsible for Bose-star condensation, modifying both the equilibrium Bose-star structure and the condensation timescale. We derive a screened kinetic condensation formula in which the ordinary gravitational Coulomb logarithm is replaced by a finite Yukawa transport logarithm. Static YSP solutions show that Yukawa screening broadens the Bose-star density profile relative to the ordinary Newtonian soliton. Fully dynamical pseudospectral simulations with homogeneous and isotropic initial conditions demonstrate that Yukawa screening systematically delays Bose-star condensation, in good agreement with the screened kinetic prediction after fitting a single overall normalization parameter.



I
Introduction

The nature of dark matter (DM) remains one of the most important unresolved problems in modern cosmology. Observations indicate that DM contributes about
27\%
of the total energy density of the Universe
Aghanim et al. 2020
, and many particle candidates have been proposed. Among them, light bosonic dark matter, including QCD axions and ultralight axionlike particles, has attracted significant attention
Peccei 2008
;
Weinberg 1978
;
Preskill et al. 1983
;
Kim 1979
;
Shifman et al. 1980
;
Dine and Fischler 1983
;
Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Hu et al. 2000
;
Marsh 2016
;
Hui et al. 2017
;
Hlozek et al. 2015
;
Hlozek et al. 2018
;
Luzio et al. 2020
;
Sikivie 2008
. In the large occupation-number limit, these bosons can be described by a classical complex scalar field in natural units with
\hbar=c=1
. Due to the interplay between gravity, gradient pressure, and possible self-interactions, the bosonic field can undergo gravitational Bose condensation and form compact solitonic objects known as boson stars or solitons
Kaup 1968
;
Ruffini and Bonazzola 1969
;
Seidel and Suen 1991
;
Seidel and Suen 1994
;
Chavanis 2011
;
Chavanis and Delfini 2011
;
Amin and Mocz 2019
;
Eby et al. 2016
;
Chavanis 2018
.

The formation and evolution of boson stars through kinetic relaxation has been studied extensively over the past decades
Sikivie and Yang 2009
;
Guth et al. 2015
. In particular, Refs.
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Kirkpatrick et al. 2020
demonstrated that self-gravitating bosons governed by the Schrödinger-Poisson equations can dynamically condense and form Bose stars in the kinetic regime, and derived the corresponding condensation timescale. Subsequent numerical studies investigated the relaxation process in systems with gravity and self-interactions, tested analytic condensation-time models, and followed the formation and growth of boson stars inside dark matter halos
Schwabe et al. 2016
;
Veltmaat et al. 2018
;
Eggemeier and Niemeyer 2019
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Bar et al. 2018
. These developments also motivated extensions beyond the minimal scalar scenario, including vector dark matter, polarization dynamics in vector halos, nonminimal gravitational couplings, and the nonlinear dynamics of self-interacting solitons
Chen et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Chen and Zhang 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2026
;
Zhang 2025
.

In this work, we study how finite-range interactions modify the infrared kinetic relaxation responsible for Bose-star condensation. A Yukawa interaction naturally arises when the mediator field acquires a finite mass or when dark matter possesses an additional finite-range interaction. Similar Yukawa parameterizations frequently appear in phenomenological studies of screened or finite-range gravitational interactions. In the present work, the Yukawa interaction is treated phenomenologically as an effective finite-range modification of the long-range gravitational interaction. In the Schrödinger-Newton context, Yukawa screening has previously been introduced to study how finite interaction ranges modify collapse and large-scale density evolution
Mendonça 2021
. However, the effect of screening on kinetic Bose-star condensation and condensation timescales has not yet been systematically investigated.

The finite interaction range influences the system in two closely related ways. First, it modifies the equilibrium Bose-star profile because the outer region of the bound object no longer experiences a purely Newtonian long-range attraction. Second, it changes the kinetic relaxation process responsible for condensation by regulating small-angle gravitational scattering and replacing the usual Coulomb logarithm with a finite Yukawa transport logarithm. We therefore study both the static YSP soliton solutions and the screened kinetic condensation process within a unified framework.

Using pseudospectral simulations in a periodic box with homogeneous and isotropic initial conditions, we numerically investigate the formation of Bose stars in the YSP system. We compare the measured condensation times with the screened kinetic prediction and determine the overall normalization through numerical fitting.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we derive the YSP equations and introduce the dimensionless form used in the simulations. In Sec.
III
, we study the static YSP ground-state solutions. In Sec.
IV
, we derive the screened kinetic condensation timescale by replacing the gravitational Coulomb logarithm with the Yukawa transport logarithm. In Sec.
V
, we describe the numerical methods, initial conditions, and numerical simulation setup, including projected density maps, radial profiles, maximum-density evolution, and comparisons between theoretical predictions and simulations. Finally, Sec.
VI
summarizes our conclusions and discusses possible future extensions.

II
YSP System

In this work, we consider a bosonic field (
\psi
) interacting through a Yukawa-screened attractive potential. The dynamics of the system can be derived from the mean-field action

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\int dt\,d^{3}x\left[\frac{i}{2}\left(\psi^{*}\dot{\psi}-\psi\dot{\psi}^{*}\right)-\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{2m}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-m\Phi\left(|\psi|^{2}-n\right)-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left((\nabla\Phi)^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}\Phi^{2}\right)\right],

(1)

where
m
is the boson mass,
G
is Newton’s gravitational constant,
\Phi
is the Yukawa-screened gravitational potential, and
n=\langle|\psi|^{2}\rangle
is the mean number density. The subtraction of the homogeneous background density is the standard finite-volume prescription adopted in periodic Schrödinger-Poisson simulations
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Mendonça 2021
.

Varying the action with respect to
\psi^{*}
and
\Phi
, we obtain the YSP equations

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m}\nabla^{2}\psi+m\Phi\psi,

(2)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}-\mu_{Y}^{2})\Phi

\displaystyle=4\pi Gm\left(|\psi|^{2}-n\right),

(3)

where
\mu_{Y}
is the Yukawa screening mass. In the limit
\mu_{Y}\rightarrow 0
, Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) reduce to the ordinary Schrödinger-Poisson equations.

To simplify the equations, we introduce the dimensionless quantities

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\widetilde{x}/(mv_{0}),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\widetilde{t}/(mv_{0}^{2}),

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Phi}v_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\psi}v_{0}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{m}{4\pi G}},

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}mv_{0},

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\widetilde{n}\frac{mv_{0}^{4}}{4\pi G},

(4)

where
v_{0}
is a reference velocity characterizing the initial state. Using these definitions, the dimensionless YSP equations become

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{\psi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}+\widetilde{\Phi}\widetilde{\psi},

(5)

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}-\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}^{2}\right)\widetilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}-\widetilde{n}.

(6)

The dimensionless particle number satisfies

\widetilde{N}=\int d^{3}\widetilde{x}\,|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}=4\pi\widetilde{M}.

(7)

III
Static YSP Bose Stars

We first consider static solutions of the time-independent Yukawa-SP equations using the ansatz

\widetilde{\psi}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\widetilde{t})=e^{-i\widetilde{\omega}\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{\psi}_{s}(\widetilde{r}),

(8)

where
\widetilde{r}=|\widetilde{\bm{x}}|
. For localized static solutions, the homogeneous background subtraction is omitted. The static YSP equations are then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}\widetilde{\psi}_{s}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}_{s}+\widetilde{\Phi}_{s}\widetilde{\psi}_{s},

(9)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}^{2}-\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}^{2})\widetilde{\Phi}_{s}

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\psi}_{s}|^{2}.

(10)

We solve Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) by imaginary-time relaxation at fixed total mass. Increasing
\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}
shortens the range of the attractive potential. In Fig.
1
, the profiles are normalized to the same central density. The screened solitonic solutions become broader, since a shorter-range attraction requires a less compact configuration at fixed mass. This effect is illustrated by comparing the ordinary Schrödinger-Poisson profile with the screened YSP profiles.

Figure 1:

Static YSP Bose-star profiles obtained by imaginary-time relaxation for different Yukawa masses
\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}
.
The profiles are normalized to the same central density to illustrate how Yukawa screening modifies the soliton structure.
The case
\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}=0
corresponds to the ordinary Schrödinger-Poisson soliton.

IV
Screened Kinetic Condensation

The condensation time of Bose stars in the kinetic regime is expected to be proportional to the relaxation time of the bosonic system
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
. For ordinary gravitational interactions, the condensation time takes the form

\tau_{\rm gr}=\frac{b\sqrt{2}}{12\pi^{3}}\frac{mv^{6}}{G^{2}n^{2}\ln(mvR)},

(11)

where
v
is the characteristic velocity dispersion,
n
is the average number density,
R
is the size of the virialized region, and
b
is an
\mathcal{O}(1)
coefficient depending on the initial distribution. For the Yukawa-screened interaction, the transport cross section is modified by the finite interaction range. Using the screened Rutherford scattering kernel with infrared cutoff

q_{\rm min}=R^{-1}
and ultraviolet cutoff
q_{\rm max}=mv
,
following the standard kinetic treatment of gravitational condensation
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
,
we obtain the screened transport logarithm

\Lambda_{Y}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\frac{m^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}{R^{-2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}+\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{m^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}-\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{R^{-2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}\right].

(12)

The corresponding screened condensation time becomes

\tau_{Y}=\frac{b\sqrt{2}}{12\pi^{3}}\frac{mv^{6}}{G^{2}n^{2}\Lambda_{Y}}.

(13)

Equation (
13
) reduces to the ordinary gravitational result in the limit
\mu_{Y}\rightarrow 0
. As the Yukawa screening mass increases, the long-range part of the interaction becomes suppressed, reducing the transport logarithm
\Lambda_{Y}
and increasing the condensation time relative to the Newtonian case. Physically, Yukawa screening suppresses infrared momentum transfer and therefore weakens the kinetic relaxation responsible for Bose-star condensation.

V
Numerical Simulations

To solve Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
), we employ a fourth-order pseudospectral method similar to previous Schrödinger-Poisson simulations of wave dark matter
Widrow and Kaiser 1993
;
Schive et al. 2014
;
Mocz et al. 2017
;
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
.
The screened gravitational potential is computed in Fourier space at each time step.

For the initial conditions, we consider a Dirac-delta momentum-shell distribution,

|\psi_{\bm{p}}|^{2}=N\delta(|\bm{p}|-mv_{0})

following the setup of Ref.
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2023
. The simulations are carried out in a periodic box with dimensionless size
\widetilde{L}
, where the total number of nonrelativistic bosons is given by
N\equiv nL^{3}
. The initial field configuration in position space,
\psi(\vec{x},0)
, is obtained by performing an inverse Fourier transform of
|\psi_{\bm{p}}|e^{iS}
with randomly distributed phases
S
. This construction produces an initially homogeneous and isotropic random-wave configuration. To investigate the effect of Yukawa screening, we vary the dimensionless screening parameter
\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}
within the range
[0,1]
.

We consider simulation box sizes in the range
30\leq\widetilde{L}\leq 50
, total mass parameters
15\leq\widetilde{M}\leq 100
, and Yukawa screening parameters
0\leq\widetilde{\mu}_{Y}\leq 1.0
. These parameters are chosen such that Bose-star formation occurs within the accessible simulation time interval while allowing comparisons between different screening strengths and system sizes.

To verify that the condensed object is indeed a Bose star, we also compare the density profile measured from simulations with the corresponding static YSP ground-state solution. The static profile is obtained numerically using imaginary-time relaxation.

VI
Conclusions

We have studied Bose-star formation in a YSP system using kinetic theory and fully dynamical pseudospectral simulations. A finite interaction range modifies both the equilibrium structure of the condensed object and the relaxation process responsible for Bose-star formation.

For the static solutions, we find that Yukawa screening broadens the Bose-star density profile relative to the ordinary Schrödinger-Poisson soliton by suppressing the long-range part of the attractive interaction. For the kinetic condensation process, we derive a screened condensation-time formula in which the ordinary gravitational Coulomb logarithm is replaced by a finite Yukawa transport logarithm. The resulting condensation time is

\tau_{Y}=\frac{b\sqrt{2}}{12\pi^{3}}\frac{mv^{6}}{G^{2}n^{2}\Lambda_{Y}}.

(15)

Fully dynamical simulations show that increasing the Yukawa screening mass systematically delays Bose-star condensation. After fitting a single overall normalization coefficient, we obtain
b_{\rm fit}\approx 0.54
, and find good agreement between the measured condensation times and the screened kinetic prediction.

Our results suggest that finite-range interactions can significantly modify both the formation timescale and internal structure of self-gravitating bosonic condensates. Beyond the condensation stage, Yukawa screening may also affect the subsequent nonlinear evolution of the compact object, including accretion, oscillations, mergers, and collapse
Eby et al. 2016
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Chen and Zhang 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2026
. It will therefore be interesting to extend the present framework to cosmological simulations and to vector dark matter systems, where polarization effects may further alter the condensation dynamics and soliton structure
Chen et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2025
.

Appendix A
Derivation of the Yukawa Condensation Time

For two nonrelativistic particles of mass
m
, the Newtonian potential is replaced by the Yukawa-screened potential

V_{Y}(r)=-\frac{Gm^{2}}{r}e^{-\mu_{Y}r}.

(16)

Its Fourier transform is

\widetilde{V}_{Y}(q)=-\frac{4\pi Gm^{2}}{q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}.

(17)

The scattering is dominated by small deflections. In this limit one may either use the Born amplitude or, equivalently, the screened Rutherford form. The momentum transfer is

q=2mv\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\simeq mv\theta,

(18)

where
v
is the relative velocity and
\theta
is the scattering angle. The Yukawa mass cuts off the Rutherford singularity at angles of order

\theta_{Y}=\frac{\mu_{Y}}{mv}.

(19)

Using the same small-angle normalization convention as in the gravitational kinetic treatment, we obtain

\frac{d\sigma_{Y}}{d\Omega}=\frac{4G^{2}m^{2}}{v^{4}}\frac{1}{\left(\theta^{2}+\theta_{Y}^{2}\right)^{2}},\qquad\theta_{Y}\equiv\frac{\mu_{Y}}{mv},

(20)

which reduces to the usual gravitational Rutherford expression for
\mu_{Y}\rightarrow 0
.

The total cross section is not the relevant quantity for relaxation, because very small deflections barely change the particle momentum. The relaxation rate is controlled by the transport cross section,

\sigma_{\rm tr,Y}=\int d\Omega\,(1-\cos\theta)\frac{d\sigma_{Y}}{d\Omega}.

(21)

Using
1-\cos\theta\simeq\theta^{2}/2
,
d\Omega\simeq 2\pi\theta\,d\theta
, and
q=mv\theta
, Eq. (
21
) becomes

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm tr,Y}

\displaystyle=\int 2\pi\theta\,d\theta\,\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\frac{4G^{2}m^{2}}{v^{4}}\frac{1}{(\theta^{2}+\theta_{Y}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi G^{2}m^{2}}{v^{4}}\int_{\theta_{\rm min}}^{\theta_{\rm max}}\frac{\theta^{3}\,d\theta}{(\theta^{2}+\theta_{Y}^{2})^{2}}.

(22)

Changing variables from angle to momentum transfer,
q=mv\theta
, gives
d\theta=dq/(mv)
and

\frac{\theta^{3}\,d\theta}{(\theta^{2}+\theta_{Y}^{2})^{2}}=\frac{q^{3}\,dq}{(q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2})^{2}}.

(23)

Up to the conventional normalization absorbed in the coefficient
b
, this is the same screened transport logarithm that replaces the Coulomb logarithm. With the normalization of Refs.
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
we write

\sigma_{\rm tr,Y}=\frac{8\pi G^{2}m^{2}}{v^{4}}\Lambda_{Y},

(24)

where

\Lambda_{Y}=\int_{q_{\rm min}}^{q_{\rm max}}\frac{q^{3}\,dq}{(q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2})^{2}}.

(25)

The lower cutoff is fixed by the finite size of the system,

q_{\rm min}=R^{-1},

(26)

because modes with wavelengths larger than the system do not contribute to kinetic relaxation. The upper cutoff is fixed by the typical momentum transfer in the gas,

q_{\rm max}=mv.

(27)

To evaluate Eq. (
25
), set
u=q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}
. Then
du=2q\,dq
and
q^{2}=u-\mu_{Y}^{2}
, so

\displaystyle\int\frac{q^{3}\,dq}{(q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{u-\mu_{Y}^{2}}{u^{2}}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln u+\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{u}\right].

(28)

Evaluating between
q_{\rm min}
and
q_{\rm max}
gives

\displaystyle\Lambda_{Y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln(q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2})+\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{q^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}\right]_{q_{\rm min}}^{q_{\rm max}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\frac{m^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}{R^{-2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}+\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{m^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}-\frac{\mu_{Y}^{2}}{R^{-2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}}\right],

(29)

which is Eq. (
12
). In the unscreened limit
\mu_{Y}\rightarrow 0
this reduces to

\Lambda_{Y}\rightarrow\ln(mvR).

(30)

The screened kinetic condensation time is proportional to the relaxation time of a highly occupied bosonic gas. A classical wave mode with occupation number
f\gg 1
has a Bose-enhanced scattering rate: the ordinary two-body rate
\sigma_{\rm tr}vn
is multiplied by the typical final-state occupation. Following Refs.
Levkov et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2022
, the condensation time can therefore be written as

\tau=\frac{4b\sqrt{2}}{\sigma_{\rm tr}vnf},

(31)

where
b=O(1)
depends on the initial momentum distribution and on the precise operational definition of condensation. The factor
4\sqrt{2}
follows the convention used in the kinetic simulations of gravitational condensation. For an isotropic distribution with characteristic momentum width
mv
, the characteristic phase-space occupation is

f=\frac{6\pi^{2}n}{(mv)^{3}}

(32)

where
n
is the number density. The particles occupy a momentum-space volume of order
(mv)^{3}
in the standard phase-space measure. Substituting Eqs. (
24
) and (
32
) into Eq. (
31
), we find

\displaystyle\tau_{Y}

\displaystyle=\frac{4b\sqrt{2}}{\left(8\pi G^{2}m^{2}\Lambda_{Y}/v^{4}\right)vn\left[6\pi^{2}n/(mv)^{3}\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{b\sqrt{2}}{12\pi^{3}}\frac{mv^{6}}{G^{2}n^{2}\Lambda_{Y}}.

(33)

This reproduces Eq. (
13
). The same expression may also be written as

\tau_{Y}=\tau_{\rm gr}\frac{\ln(mvR)}{\Lambda_{Y}},

(34)

where
\tau_{\rm gr}
is the unscreened gravitational condensation time.
