Shirokov and Shapiro Effects in the Hartle-Thorne Spacetime
Anuar Idrissov, Kuantay Boshkayev, Serzhan Momynov, Hernando Quevedo, Daniya Utepova, Ainur Urazalina, Bagila Baitimbetova

Abstract
We investigate the influence of rotation and quadrupole deformations of astrophysical compact objects on the Shirokov and Shapiro effects within the Hartle–Thorne spacetime, which describes the exterior gravitational field of slowly rotating, slightly deformed celestial objects. Using geodesic deviation equations, we analyze the oscillatory motion of neighboring test particle trajectories and show how the combined impact of angular momentum J J and quadrupole moment Q Q affects the Shirokov effect. The results are compared with our previous analysis for the Lense–Thirring and Zipoy–Voorhees metrics, revealing consistent trends in the coupling between radial and azimuthal oscillations. For the Shapiro time delay, we examine two limiting configurations: (i) the Lense–Thirring frame–dragging case with J 2 = 0 J^{2}=0 , Q = 0 Q=0 and J ≠ 0 J\neq 0 , where the effect persists for both positive and negative values of the angular momentum; and (ii) the static quadrupolar case with J = 0 J=0 and Q ≠ 0 Q\neq 0 , where more oblate sources produce a stronger gravitational time delay with increasing distance. We also study these effects in the Hartle–Thorne spacetime without employing the weak-field approximation, performing a full numerical analysis. In particular, we examine the mimicking effects produced by the quadrupole deformation and the angular momentum of the compact object. These results illustrate how the deformation and rotation of compact objects influence the relativistic observables in the surrounding spacetime.

A useful method for analyzing the motion of particles in curved spacetime is the

Another key relativistic observable is the

In the present work, we extend these analyses to include the Hartle–Thorne spacetime. Using the geodesic deviation formalism, we derive the governing equations for the Shirokov effect and examine how the combined influence of rotation and quadrupole deformation affects the oscillation frequencies and phase differences of test particles. We also compute the Shapiro time delay for light propagation in this spacetime and study how the angular momentum

Motivated by these considerations, we model two compact test bodies on circular orbits around a neutron star and compute the resulting strong-field Shapiro time delay. The inner object is treated as a rigid fragment rather than a planet, while the outer object may represent a more massive clump or remnant. These setups provide new insights into how rotation and deformation jointly shape relativistic effects in the vicinity of neutron stars and other compact bodies, and may contribute to the interpretation of high-precision observational data from pulsars, X-ray binaries, and gravitational lensing systems

III
Shirokov effect

In his early analysis of relativistic orbital dynamics, Shirokov
67
introduced a simplified physical model to illustrate the motion of a test particle in curved spacetime. He considered a hollow spherical satellite whose center of mass follows a circular geodesic in the field of the Earth, using the Schwarzschild metric. A small test particle, placed at the center of this sphere, is then given a tiny initial displacement and velocity relative to the satellite’s frame. The subsequent motion of the particle represents small oscillations about the reference geodesic. When radial or vertical perturbations are introduced, the oscillations occur with slightly different characteristic periods

\Delta T=T_{\theta}-T_{r}\ (\text{or }T_{\phi})\approx-\frac{3M}{r}T_{0},

(15)

where
T_{0}
is the corresponding Newtonian period. The appearance of this small frequency difference, absent in Newtonian gravity, directly reflects spacetime curvature and provides a subtle relativistic correction that, in principle, can be measured with modern experimental precision
75

Here, we generalize Shirokov’s approach to the strong-field regime near a rotating and deformed compact object described by the Hartle–Thorne metric. Instead of a terrestrial satellite, we consider a test particle in a slightly perturbed circular orbit around a slowly rotating neutron star. In this framework, the motion of the particle exhibits small oscillations around the reference geodesic as a result of the combined influence of spacetime curvature, frame dragging, and the quadrupole deformation of the central mass. The resulting differences between the azimuthal, radial, and vertical oscillation periods define the relativistic extension of the Shirokov effect in the Hartle–Thorne spacetime
71
. Since the quadrupole moment encodes the internal mass distribution and the equation of state of the central object
46
, its effect on the epicyclic frequencies in the Hartle–Thorne spacetime provides a potential observational probe of neutron-star internal structure via timing signatures such as quasi-periodic oscillations

IV
Shapiro time delay

The time delay of electromagnetic signals propagating near a massive object – known as the
Shapiro delay
– constitutes the fourth classical test of general relativity
65
. In his seminal work, Shapiro analyzed radar signals propagating through the Schwarzschild spacetime of the Sun. In the present work, we extend this framework to the strong-field environment of a neutron star, whose exterior spacetime is described by the Hartle-Thorne metric, thereby capturing the combined influence of mass, rotation, and quadrupole deformation on the light-travel time.

Restricting the analysis to the equatorial plane (
\theta=\pi/2
), we consider null geodesics representing photon trajectories. Using the Euler-Lagrange formalism, we obtain the conserved quantities associated with the spacetime symmetries – the photon energy
E
, angular momentum
L
, and the four-velocity components
\dot{t}
and
\dot{\phi}
– which, together with the null condition
g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=0
, allow us to compute the coordinate of propagation time between emission and reception points. Integration along the photon path then yields the total round-trip Shapiro delay with respect to flat spacetime.

For massless particles one can write the normalization of the four velocity in terms of metric tensor components of (
II
)

\displaystyle g_{tt}\,\dot{t}^{2}+g_{rr}\,\dot{r}^{2}+g_{\theta\theta}\,\dot{\theta}^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}+2g_{t\phi}\,\dot{t}\dot{\phi}=0.

(47)

The metric coefficients are independent of the
t
and
\phi
coordinates,
leading to the existence of the conserved specific energy at infinity,
E
,
and the conserved
z
-component of the specific angular momentum at infinity,
L_{z}
.
The
t
- and
\phi
-components of the 4-velocity of a test-particle can thus be written as
26
;
5

\displaystyle\dot{t}=\frac{Eg_{\phi\phi}+L_{z}g_{t\phi}}{g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}},\qquad\dot{\phi}=-\frac{Eg_{t\phi}+L_{z}g_{tt}}{g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}}.

(48)

Then,

\dot{t}=t_{0}\left[1+jW_{1}(r)+j^{2}W_{2}(r)+qW_{3}(r)\right],

(49)

where

,

V
Conclusions

In this work, we studied the combined influence of rotation and quadrupole deformation on two key observable relativistic effects -the Shirokov and Shapiro effects- within the Hartle-Thorne spacetime. This metric, representing the exterior gravitational field of a slowly rotating, axially symmetric neutron star, provides a consistent framework to analyze how rotation and deviation from spherical symmetry of compact objects affect measurable gravitational phenomena.

Using the geodesic deviation formalism, we derived the equations governing the relative motion of neighboring test-particle trajectories and examined how the presence of both the angular momentum
J
and the quadrupole moment
Q
modifies the frequency and amplitude of the resulting oscillations. Our analysis shows that the Shirokov effect, which captures the oscillatory deviation between nearby trajectories, exhibits a clear dependence on the sign and magnitude of both
J
and
Q
. In particular, we find that the radial and azimuthal oscillations become stronger when both effects are present, leading to an enhanced precession and modulation of the motion compared to the pure Lense–Thirring or purely static quadrupolar cases.

Moreover, we derived the time delay in analytical form for the Hartle-Thorne metric and then analyzed the Shapiro effect under two limiting conditions. In the Lense–Thirring configuration (
Q=0
,
J^{2}=0
,
J\neq 0
), we confirmed the persistence of frame dragging for both positive and negative values of the angular momentum, consistent with our previous findings in the Lense–Thirring spacetime. In the static quadrupolar configuration (
J=0
,
J^{2}=0
,
Q\neq 0
), corresponding to the absence of rotation, we observed that
more oblate sources (with large positive values of
Q
) produce a stronger gravitational time delay with increasing distance from the source. This behavior highlights the role of the quadrupole deformation in shaping light propagation and timing effects in the vicinity of neutron stars.

Analysis of both the Shirokov effect and the Shapiro time delay reveals a strong degeneracy between angular momentum
j
and quadrupole moment
q
in the Hartle–Thorne metric. In the low-spin regime, different
(q,j)
combinations yield nearly indistinguishable observational signatures, giving rise to a clear mimicking effect in which a slowly rotating neutron star can reproduce the timing or dynamical behavior of a compact object with a different multipole structure. Thus, each observable constrains only a combined effect of angular momentum and quadrupole, and cannot separate their individual contributions. Breaking this degeneracy requires combining several independent measurements.

Finally, by comparing our results with previous analyses for the Lense–Thirring and Zipoy–Voorhees metrics, we demonstrated consistent qualitative trends in how rotation and deformation interact to affect relativistic observables. The interplay between the angular momentum and quadrupole moment modifies both the frame–dragging and time–delay signatures in a coherent way, indicating that even moderate deviations from spherical symmetry can have measurable consequences in precision astrophysical observations.

We have also investigated these effects in the Hartle–Thorne spacetime without invoking the weak-field approximation, performing a full numerical analysis. In particular, we examined the mimicking effects associated with the quadrupole deformation and the angular momentum of the compact object. Our results demonstrate that the deformation and rotation of compact objects play an important role in shaping relativistic observables in the surrounding spacetime. Moreover, from the magnitude of the mimicking effects obtained in our analysis, we find that the Shirokov effect provides a stronger contribution than the Shapiro effect.

Overall, our findings show that rotation and deformation must be treated jointly when interpreting timing signatures and relativistic precession around neutron stars and other compact objects. A natural extension of this work is to include higher-order spin and quadrupolar contributions, or to examine observational consequences for pulsar timing and gravitational lensing in realistic neutron-star configurations. Another logical direction is to investigate relativistic tidal interactions and the gravitational-capture cross section for small bodies orbiting neutron stars. A similar analysis can be performed for black holes in alternative modified and extended theories of gravity, including Kalb-Ramond, Bumblebee, Scalar-Tensor-Vector, noncommutative,
F(R)
,
F(Q)
etc.
