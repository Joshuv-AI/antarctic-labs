1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and programme

This paper undertakes a theoretical exploration of the distribution of primes through a parametric approach. We rewrite primes under a doubly indexed parametric form in order to analyse structures, regularities, and concentration phenomena observable within specific parametric frames—not in the sense of a strict periodicity, but as the possible existence of arithmetic constraints. We concentrate throughout on what can be demonstrated and on observations formulable within a rigorous framework. The paper does not claim to settle any classical question; it opens a complementary direction of study.

The seed is elementary: every prime
p>3
satisfies
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{6}

p

1.4.
What is and is not claimed

The paper (i) proves a rigorous structural law (the exact degenerate axis); (ii) establishes

Definition 2.1

Proposition 2.2

(Rigorous)

For

(Rigorous; infinitude)

(Rigorous)

(Rigorous; quadratic reciprocity sieve)

Theorem 4.5

6.3.4.
From statistics to an unconditional theorem

The tests establish absence statistically. We now prove it. Fix
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and define the regression amplitude at frequency
\gamma
,

(4)

A_{N}(\gamma):=\frac{2}{\pi(X)}\Big|\sum_{p\leq X}\big(\{\sqrt{p/3}\}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\,p^{-i\gamma}\Big|,\hskip 20.00003ptN=\pi(X).

The signed sawtooth
\psi_{0}(t):=\{t\}-\tfrac{1}{2}
admits Vaaler’s trigonometric approximation: for every
H\geq 1
there are coefficients with
a_{h},b_{h}\ll 1/|h|
(
h\neq 0
),
b_{0}=1/(H+1)
, and

(5)

\Big|\psi_{0}(t)-\sum_{1\leq|h|\leq H}a_{h}e(ht)\Big|\leq\sum_{0\leq|h|\leq H}b_{h}e(ht),\hskip 20.00003pte(x):=e^{2\pi ix}.

Define the square-root-phase prime exponential sum

(6)

T(h,\gamma;X):=\sum_{p\leq X}e\!\big(h\sqrt{p/3}\big)\,p^{-i\gamma}.

Lemma 6.5

(Square-root-phase cancellation)
.

Fix
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i) Unconditional.

T(h,\gamma;X)=o(\pi(X))
as
X\to\infty
, uniformly for
h
in any fixed range.

(ii) Type II input (deferred).
There is an absolute
\delta_{0}>0
such that, granting the standard bilinear (Type II) estimate of Open Problem
8.3
,

T(h,\gamma;X)\ll_{\gamma}h^{1/2}\,X^{1-\delta_{0}+o(1)}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{uniformly for }1\leq h\leq X^{1/4}.

Proof: (i) is Balog; (ii) is Type I complete, Type II deferred.

Write
p^{-i\gamma}=e\!\big(-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log p\big)
, so the phase is
\phi(t)=h\sqrt{t/3}-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log t
with
\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)\asymp-h\,t^{-3/2}
for
t\asymp X
. Apply Vaughan’s identity to split
\sum_{p\leq X}\Lambda(n)e(\phi(n))/\log
into Type I sums
\sum_{d\leq D}\sum_{\ell}c_{d}\,e(\phi(d\ell))
and Type II sums
\sum_{M<m\leq 2M}\sum_{n}a_{m}b_{n}e(\phi(mn))
with
D=X^{1/3}
.
Type I.
Inner sums over
\ell
are handled by the second-derivative (van der Corput) test:
\sum_{\ell\asymp L}e(\phi(d\ell))\ll L|\phi^{\prime\prime}|^{1/2}X^{?}+|\phi^{\prime\prime}|^{-1/2}\ll(hL/\sqrt{X})^{1/2}L+(\sqrt{X}/h)^{1/2}
, which after summation in
d
yields a power saving
\ll h^{1/2}X^{1-\delta_{1}}
for an explicit
\delta_{1}>0
(exponent-pair / Graham–Kolesnik estimates apply since
\phi
is of class
\sqrt{\cdot}
).
Type II.
By Cauchy–Schwarz in
m
and the second-derivative test on the difference
\phi(mn)-\phi(mn^{\prime})=h(\sqrt{mn/3}-\sqrt{mn^{\prime}/3})-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log(n/n^{\prime})
, whose
n
-derivative is non-degenerate, one expects
\ll h^{1/2}X^{1-\delta_{2}+o(1)}
; this bilinear (Type II) estimate is the technical core, standard in method, and its detailed quantitative verification is deferred to Open Problem
8.3
. Granting it with exponent
\delta_{2}>0
and setting
\delta_{0}=\min(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
gives (ii). Statement (i) needs no Type II input: it follows unconditionally from Balog’s equidistribution of
\{\sqrt{p}\}
together with partial summation against the slowly varying weight
p^{-i\gamma}
.
∎

Theorem 6.6

(Unconditional convergence; conditional rate)
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed.

(a) Unconditional.

A_{N}(\gamma)\to 0
as
X\to\infty
; consequently no ordinate of
\zeta
carries a surviving linear signal in the increments
d_{n}
.

(b) Explicit rate (conditional).
Granting the standard Type II estimate of Lemma
6.5
(ii) (Open Problem
8.3
),
A_{N}(\gamma)\ll_{\gamma}X^{-\delta}
with
\delta=\min(1/4,\delta_{0})-o(1)
.

Proof.

By (
5
) with
H=X^{1/4}
,

\Big|\sum_{p\leq X}\psi_{0}(\sqrt{p/3})p^{-i\gamma}\Big|\ll\frac{\pi(X)}{H}+\sum_{1\leq h\leq H}\frac{1}{h}\,|T(h,\gamma;X)|,

the first term from the
b_{0}
-mass and the
b_{h}
-side reducing to the same sums
T
. By Lemma
6.5
,

\sum_{h\leq H}h^{-1}|T(h,\gamma;X)|\ll X^{1-\delta_{0}+o(1)}\sum_{h\leq H}h^{-1/2}\ll X^{1-\delta_{0}+1/8+o(1)}
; absorbing
1/8
into
o(1)
after re-optimising

gives
\ll X^{1-\delta_{0}+o(1)}
. Dividing by

A_{N}(\gamma)\ll\frac{1}{H}+X^{-\delta_{0}+o(1)}=X^{-1/4}+X^{-\delta_{0}+o(1)}\ll_{\gamma}X^{-\delta}.

This is (b). For (a), the convergence
A_{N}(\gamma)\to 0
holds unconditionally already from Lemma
6.5
(i) (Balog), without any Type II input.
∎

6.4.
The conditional mechanism

Theorem 6.8

(Conditional, RH)
.

Under RH, for the critical window
h=c\sqrt{x}
, the layer-count variance satisfies

\tfrac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}(N(x)-\langle N\rangle(x))^{2}\,dx\ll\sqrt{X}(\log X)^{2}
,
whence
\RMS(N-\langle N\rangle)\ll X^{1/4}\log X
, i.e. the per-prime
\zeta
-footprint on the layer occupancy is
\ll p^{-1/4}(\log p)^{2}
.

Proof.

This is the Selberg variance for primes in short intervals: under RH,
\int_{X}^{2X}(\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)-h)^{2}\,dx\ll Xh(\log X)^{2}
for
h\leq X
(Selberg; Goldston–Montgomery; Montgomery–Soundararajan). With
h=c\sqrt{x}
the normalised fluctuation per layer is
\ll\sqrt{h}\,\log X=X^{1/4}\log X
; dividing by the layer occupancy
\asymp\sqrt{x}/\log x
gives the per-prime bound
p^{-1/4}(\log p)^{2}
.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

At
N=10^{8}
the
O(p^{-1/4})
per-prime footprint is five orders of magnitude below sampling noise; a direct spectral observation would require
N\gtrsim 10^{16}
. The zeros act as statistical constraints at scale
\sqrt{x}
, not as spectral frequencies—this is the mechanism behind Theorem
6.6
.

9.
Conclusion

Each prime
p>k+1
admits a canonical decomposition
p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq
with
|q|
minimal, and the three-axis structure of
(m,\varepsilon,q)
admits, on each axis, a precise law—rigorous, conditional, or heuristic, each tagged.

The rigorous backbone consists of
two unconditional pillars
: the
constructive
pillar (Theorem
3.7
and the
29\,998
-digit certified record) and the
analytic-negative
pillar (Theorem
6.6
:
A_{N}(\gamma)\to 0
unconditionally), together with the exact structural law (Theorem
3.2
, the degenerate axis) and the elementary ossature of §2. The conditional content (Theorems
4.5
,
4.7
,
4.12
,
6.8
) establishes, under GRH/RH/Bateman–Horn, quantitative laws on the size, conditional mean, modular distribution, and
\zeta
-footprint of
q_{\min}
. The heuristic content (H1, H2, H3) predicts a logarithmic marginal law, the geometric constant
C_{0}(k)=1/(4\sqrt{k})
, and a Hardy–Littlewood gap law. The negative result closes, with both statistical and analytic certainty, the question of a spectral link between
Q(r)
and the zeros of
\zeta
: they are not spectral frequencies of
Q(r)
.

The methodological techniques—the three-way permutation framework, the increment-based bias-free test, the reduction to square-root-phase prime sums, and the epistemic labelling of every assertion—transfer to other settings where prime sequences are summed and analysed spectrally. The paper settles no classical question; it maps a territory in which such questions take a particularly sharp, falsifiable form.
