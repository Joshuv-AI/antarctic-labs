Immunogenicity and safety of AS03-adjuvanted A/Astrakhan/3212/2020 (H5N8)-like influenza vaccine in adults: Phase 1/2, observer-blinded, randomized trial
Authors: Andrew Hastie, Vinay Gupta, Patricia Lese, Jaynier Moya, Sonia Schoonbroodt, Serge Beudjekian, Frank Struyf
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTInfluenza pandemics arise from novel influenza A viruses. Recent emergence of a new clade (2.3.4.4.b) of the highly pathogenic H5N1 in animals and humans highlighted its pandemic potential. We evaluated the immunogenicity and safety of GSK’s AS03-adjuvanted H5N8 vaccine in adults. In this phase 1/2, observer-blinded, age-stratified, randomized trial, healthy US adults (age, ≥18 y) received two intramuscular doses of hemagglutinin antigen (3.75 or 7.50 μg) with AS03A or AS03B, administered 21 d apart. Immunogenicity – seroprotection rates (SPRs), seropositivity, geometric mean titers (GMTs), geometric mean fold rise (GMFR), and seroconversion rates (SCRs) – was evaluated on day 43 using hemagglutination inhibition (HI) and microneutralization (MN) assays. Safety was monitored throughout the study. Of 520 enrolled participants, 518 were vaccinated. On day 43, the US Food and Drug Administration’s (FDA) Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research criteria for influenza vaccines were met. HI SPRs, seropositivity rates, SCRs, GMTs, and GMFR appeared to be higher in the AS03A vs AS03B group. Immune responses were generally higher in younger (aged 18–64 y) vs older (aged ≥65 y) adults. Immune responses were also detected in MN assays, with a correlation between HI and MN responses on day 43 across age groups and vaccine formulations. Safety was acceptable, with no increase in adverse events post-dose 2. Reactogenicity appeared more common in younger adults. The antigen-sparing potential of AS03 was demonstrated, with an acceptable safety profile. The benefit/risk profile was favorable for all formulations tested, including 3.75 µg AS03A (licensed in the US).ClinicalTrials.gov registration: NCT05975840.

Introduction

Influenza pandemics occur when novel influenza A viruses emerge, leading to global epidemics due to a widespread lack of immunity.1 In the last two decades, several novel influenza A viruses, including subtypes H5N1, H3N2v, H1N1v, H9N2, A(H1N1)pdm09, H7N9, and H5N8, have emerged and infected humans.2,3 The emergence of the novel reassortant influenza A (H5N8) virus in Russia in December 2020 heightened concerns regarding avian influenza surveillance, highlighting its potential threat to both animal and human health.4 Historically, human infections with H5N8 have been rare; however, in 2020, seven human cases were reported in Russia following exposure to infected birds.4 Recently, the US experienced a surge in avian influenza cases among poultry and dairy cattle, leading to numerous human infections.5 This situation raises concerns about the potential of these viruses to trigger pandemics.

The first human case of highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) A(H5) virus in the US was reported in April 2022.6 By 2024, all 50 states had reported HPAI H5N1 cases in commercial and backyard poultry flocks, and 14 states had reported cases in livestock.7 In the US, 70 human cases of avian influenza A(H5) virus infection were reported between April 2024 and March 19, 2025.8 While most cases in the US presented with mild symptoms, one case in Canada (in November 2024) progressed to a critical condition,9 and there has been one death in Louisiana, US10; genetic analysis of the virus revealed mutations that may enhance its transmission potential to humans.9,11 The hemagglutinin (HA) sequence of the currently circulating HPAI H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b virus closely resembles that of the earlier isolated H5N8 (A/Astrakhan/3212/2020-like) clade 2.3.4.4b virus detected in Russia.4 Antigenic analyses indicate that the H5N8 influenza virus shares antigenic properties with recent A(H5) viruses from wild birds and poultry across the US, Europe, Africa, and Asia.4,12 The emergence of H5N1 cases – i.e., HPAI (H5) cases – raises concerns about its pandemic potential, with the possibility of rapid global spread and significant morbidity and mortality.

Influenza vaccine development continues to rely on the creation of strain-specific vaccines, and a major cornerstone of pandemic preparedness has been a preemptive assessment of novel circulating animal and human viruses for the clinical development of novel monovalent influenza A vaccines. Most of the novel A/H5 and A/H7 avian influenza vaccines have demonstrated poor immunogenicity in humans13,14 and require the addition of adjuvants such as MF59 and AS03 to improve immunogenicity.15 Immunization with AS03-adjuvanted vaccines against influenza caused by the potential pandemic subtypes H5N1, H1N1, H7N1, H7N9, and H9N2 has generally demonstrated satisfactory immunogenic potency, allowing reduced antigen doses in adults.15–21

Given the emergence of new influenza variants and the need to match vaccines with these variants, there is a critical need for the timely development of an A(H5) influenza virus vaccine, including A(H5N8). As part of the US Department of Health and Human Services’ influenza pandemic preparedness efforts, this phase 1/2 clinical study assessed the immunogenicity and safety of different formulations of GSK’s AS03-adjuvanted H5N8 influenza virus vaccine in adults aged 18–64 y and those aged ≥65 y.

Methods

Participants, vaccines, and study design

This phase 1/2, observer-blinded, age-stratified, randomized, multicenter trial included healthy US males and nonpregnant females aged ≥18 y (eligibility criteria are detailed in Supplementary Text 1). The study was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable laws and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study protocol was reviewed and approved by an independent US ethics committee (ADVARRA). GSK Vaccines (Quebec, Canada) manufactured the inactivated, split-virion vaccine, which was developed using reverse genetics–derived reassortant seed virus from A/Astrakhan/3212/2020 (H5N8), provided by the World Health Organization Collaborating Centres and Reference Laboratories. This vaccine was supplemented with AS03 as an oil-in-water emulsion containing 5.93 mg (AS03B) or 11.86 mg (AS03A) of DL-alpha-tocopherol. Participants were randomized into one of the following four groups in an allocation ratio of 1:1:1:1 as follows: (1) 3.75 µg HA/AS03B, (2) 3.75 µg HA/AS03A, (3) 7.50 µg HA/AS03B, or (4) 7.50 µg HA/AS03A. Each group was stratified by age (18–64 y and ≥65 y), creating eight subgroups of equal size for each dose group. Each group received two intramuscular doses of the vaccine, administered 21 d apart. HA antigen doses were selected based on the results of previous studies of GSK’s egg-based pandemic influenza vaccines.15–21

Study objectives and endpoints

The primary immunogenicity objective was to evaluate whether any of the four H5N8 vaccine formulations elicited an HI immune response to the vaccine-homologous virus that met or exceeded the US FDA CBER’s immunogenicity criteria22 at the day 43 visit. The secondary objectives were to assess the vaccine-homologous H5N8 HI profile in all study participants and the vaccine homologous (H5N8) (microneutralization [MN]) titers in 50% of the participants. The safety objectives were to assess the safety and reactogenicity of the vaccine formulations within 7 d after each vaccination, unsolicited adverse events (AEs) within 21 d after each vaccination, and serious adverse events (SAEs) and potential immune-mediated diseases (pIMDs) up to 6 months post-dose 2. The following derived parameters were estimated for HI titers: (1) geometric mean titers (GMTs) on days 1, 22, and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; (2) geometric mean fold rise (GMFR) on days 22 and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; (3) percentage of participants meeting the seroprotection criteria (defined as a titer ≥1:40) on d 1, 22, and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; (4) seropositivity on days 1, 22, and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; and (5) percentage of participants meeting the HI seroconversion criteria on days 22 and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2. The following derived parameters were estimated for MN titers: (1) GMTs on days 1, 22, and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; (2) seropositivity rate on days 1, 22, and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2; and (3) MN vaccine response rates (VRRs) on days 22 and 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2. The study procedures, including methods for immunogenicity assessments (HI and MN), and definitions of the endpoints assessed are detailed in Supplementary Text 2. Participants recorded solicited and unsolicited events on diary cards after each vaccination. Safety assessment procedures are described in Supplementary Text 3. All laboratory abnormalities were reviewed by the study medical team for clinical relevance and relationship to study treatment.

Statistical analyses

The primary immunogenicity objective was considered met if the CBER criteria were fulfilled simultaneously for the two age groups, i.e., if the lower limit (LL) of the 95% CI of the seroprotection rate (SPR) was ≥70% for participants aged 18–64 y and ≥60% for those aged ≥65 y for the adjuvanted vaccine formulations in the hierarchical order: (1) 7.50 µg HA/AS03A, (2) 7.50 µg HA/AS03B, (3) 3.75 µg HA/AS03A, and (4) 3.75 µg HA/AS03B. A nominal type I error of 2.5% was used for each dose group. Immunogenicity analyses were conducted in the per-protocol set. The Clopper-Pearson exact method, based on binomial distribution, was employed to calculate two-sided 95% CIs for SPRs, seroconversion rates (SCRs), seropositivity rates, and VRRs at various time points throughout the study. GMTs were calculated as the anti-log of the unadjusted mean of the log-transformed titer values, and GMFR was calculated as the anti-log of the unadjusted mean of the log-transformed fold rise in the titer values. Geometric SD was calculated as the anti-log of the SD of the log-transformed data. The CIs associated with GMT and GMFR were derived from an analysis of covariance model (ANCOVA) model, including the treatment group as a fixed effect and the log-transformed titer pre-dose 1 (day 1) as a covariate. A post-hoc analysis of the correlation between HI and MN GMTs was performed using the Pearson correlation coefficient. Other statistical details are provided in Supplementary Text 4.

Results

Participant disposition and characteristics

A total of 520 participants were enrolled in the study, of whom 518 were vaccinated: 258 in the 18–64 y and 260 in the ≥65 y age groups (Figure S1). In the 18–64 y and ≥65 y age groups, the mean age of participants was 42.4 y and 71.5 y, respectively; 127 (49.2%) and 144 (55.4%) participants, respectively, were female; 152 (58.9%) and 203 (78.1%) participants, respectively, were White; 193 (74.8%) and 194 (74.6%) participants, respectively, identified as not Hispanic or Latino; and 59 (22.9%) and 60 (23.1%) participants, respectively, reported prior exposure to poultry at the start of the study. Immunogenicity

HI SPR and seropositivity rate

In both age groups, both SPR and seropositivity rates increased from day 1 to day 22, peaking on day 43 (Figure 1, Table S1). SPRs and seropositivity rates were generally higher in the 18–64 y vs ≥65 y age group. On day 43, HI SPRs ranged from 97.8% to 100% and from 91.5% to 95.3% in the 18–64 and ≥65 y age groups, respectively, exceeding the CBER criteria (i.e., LL of 95% CI ≥70% for 18–64 y and ≥60% for ≥65 y). In general, on day 43 and at 6 months post-dose 2, SPRs were higher with AS03A vs AS03B in both age groups. A post hoc analysis of immunogenicity by poultry exposure status on day 43 showed no difference in the immune response between participants with and without poultry exposure, although the study was not powered for a statistical comparison between these groups (Table S2).

HI GMTs and GMFR

In both age groups, the GMTs for the vaccine-homologous H5N8 HI titer showed an increase from day 1 to day 43 across all treatment groups, indicating an immune response to vaccination. By 6 months post-dose 2, the GMTs decreased across all treatment and age groups but remained above prevaccination levels in both age groups. Furthermore, in both age groups, the GMTs for vaccine-homologous H5N8 HI titers on day 43 were higher with AS03A vs AS03B (18–64 y: 3.75 μg: 175.2 vs 117.5; 7.50 μg: 176.6 vs 121.8 and ≥65 y: 3.75 μg: 110.2 vs 80.6; 7.50 μg: 129.3 vs 84.0; Table 2). The GMFR values were higher in the 18–64 y vs ≥65 y age group across all time points and dose groups and were generally higher with AS03A (3.75/7.50 μg HA/AS03A) vs AS03B (3.75/7.50 μg HA/AS03B) in both age groups. Correlation analyses between HI and MN results

The overall correlation between HI and MN titers was strong in an analysis including data across all age groups and visits, with correlation coefficients ranging from 0.71 to 0.79 (p < .0001; Table S4). The highest correlation was observed on day 43, with coefficients ranging from 0.76 to 0.88 across all age groups. The correlation was generally higher in the 18–64 y vs ≥65 y age group (Figures S2 and S3).

Safety analysis

A total of 17 SAEs were reported over the study duration (up to 6 months after the last dose) in a total of 12 participants (18–64 y: 4; ≥65 y: 8), with one event each of hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, and completed suicide and schizophrenia in the 18–64 y age group and 1 event each of unstable angina, coronary artery disease, pyelonephritis, septic shock, spinal osteoarthritis, brain neoplasm, and acute respiratory failure; two events of myocardial infarction; and three events of atrial fibrillation in the ≥65 y of age group. A review of all pre- and post-vaccination biochemistry and hematology results found no pattern suggestive of a vaccine-related effect. As reporting of abnormal laboratory results was largely at the discretion of the investigator, not all abnormal results were reported as AEs. Two fatal SAEs (completed suicide in the 18–64 y and myocardial infarction in the ≥65 y age groups) were reported, neither of which was considered by the investigator to be related to vaccination. Two study pauses occurred due to SAEs: the first due to an SAE of worsening hyperkalemia, and the second due to an SAE of myocardial infarction 2 weeks after the restart. The study was restarted after a review of the SAEs by an unblinded internal review committee, which concluded that the SAEs causing the study pauses were not related to the study vaccine. No pIMDs were reported at any time during the study period. The incidence of unsolicited AEs was similar across all dose and age groups, with the most common unsolicited AE being hyperkalemia. In the 18–64 y age group, hyperkalemia was reported in four participants (two in the 3.75 μg HA/AS03B and two in the 7.50 μg HA/AS03B groups), and in the ≥65 y age group, it was reported in five participants (two in the 3.75 μg HA/AS03B, one in the 3.75 μg HA/AS03A, one in the 7.50 μg HA/AS03A, and one in the 7.50 μg HA/AS03B groups). Discussion

Since there is no sustained transmission of H5N8 viruses among humans, the efficacy of AS03-adjuvanted H5N8 vaccines against influenza caused by these viruses currently cannot be directly evaluated. However, immune responses against the influenza virus HA proteins of the virus strains contained in the vaccines are recognized as important mediators of immunity against influenza disease based on the seasonal influenza experience and, when measured by a validated HI test, may be considered a surrogate marker of activity that is reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. The findings from this phase 1/2, observer-blinded, randomized trial confirmed that the primary immunogenicity objective was met across all formulations and both age groups using HI, with a good correlation noted between HI and MN responses on day 43 across vaccine formulations and both age groups. Although MN and HI GMTs are difficult to compare directly, the magnitude of the MN immune response was generally observed to be lower than that observed with the HI assay, which may reflect a lower sensitivity of the MN assay. Both assays demonstrated an immune response on day 22, peaking on day 43 before declining at 6 months post-dose 2, but generally remained above baseline levels. This pattern is similar to that seen with AS03-adjuvanted A/Indonesia/5/05 H5N1 vaccine.23,24

In clinical trials, the AS03-adjuvanted pandemic influenza vaccines have been shown to be more immunogenic than nonadjuvanted vaccines.15–27 The demonstration of immunogenicity with a lower dose of H5N8, combined with enhanced immunogenicity using the AS03A adjuvant offers the potential for antigen sparing, given the inherent difficulties in supply and demand for pandemic vaccines once a pandemic is declared.15,16,28 Of note, the AS03A-adjuvanted H5N8 vaccine appeared to elicit higher HI immune responses in younger adults vs older adults. The observed age effects on the response could be due to age-related immunosenescence.

In recent studies, a non-GSK H5N8 monovalent inactivated split influenza A/H5N8 virus vaccine (A/gyrfalcon/Washington/41088-6/2014; clade 2.3.4.4c) adjuvanted with AS03A demonstrated that two doses of the vaccine were required to achieve adequate immune responses, and immunogenicity was enhanced by the addition of AS03A.29,30 Furthermore, cross-reactivity to clade 2.3.4.4b strains isolated from recent human cases was noted, with the highest cross-reactivity to the human strain from Russia (A/Astrakhan/3212/2020).29 A/H5N1 virus vaccines adjuvanted with AS03 or MF59 have been shown to improve the cross-reactivity of antibodies to different clades of H5 viruses.31,32 The HA sequence of the currently circulating HPAI H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b virus is similar to that of the H5N8 A/Astrakhan/3212/2020 virus used in the present study.4,33 Preclinical studies in ferrets have demonstrated that antisera produced against the H5N8 A/Astrakhan/3212/2020 clade 2.3.4.4b virus are cross-reactive with the H5N1 A/Texas/37/2024 virus isolated from a Texas dairy farm worker.33 Although cross-reactivity was not assessed in the present study, the study vaccine has a high degree of similarity with circulating H5 antigens.

Safety and reactogenicity data suggest that the H5N8 AS03-adjuvanted vaccine has an acceptable safety profile in both age groups. Reactogenicity events were more commonly reported in younger adults than in older adults, consistent with findings from other AS03-adjuvanted pandemic influenza vaccines.34

The evaluation of immunogenicity in this study was confined to HI and MN assays without incorporating additional assessments such as cellular immunity, which could have offered deeper insights. It is worth noting that regulatory standards for these broader assessments have yet to be established. Furthermore, no evaluation of cross-reactivity with other H5 strains was conducted, which would have been helpful in understanding the breadth of the vaccine-induced immune response against highly pathogenic H5 strains. These characteristics may represent the limitations of this study.

Two significant issues must be acknowledged that may impact the interpretation of the results. First, more participants than anticipated had been vaccinated despite not meeting the eligibility criteria due to abnormal biochemistry or hematology results. Despite this, a safety evaluation of all pre- and post-vaccination biochemistry and hematology results found no pattern suggestive of a vaccine-related effect. Second, two study pauses due to SAEs in quick succession caused many participants to fall outside the protocol windows for dose 2 vaccination and/or primary objective immunogenicity blood sample collection. These impacted the number of protocol deviations, and the size of the per-protocol set, potentially affecting the robustness of the study findings, and should be considered when interpreting the results. As it took longer to recruit healthy older adults, this group was more impacted by protocol deviations regarding visit scheduling as a result of the study pauses. A sensitivity analysis of immunogenicity in the exposed population found similar results to the PPS.

Conclusions

This study met its primary objectives across all formulations and age groups, confirming the antigen-sparing potential of the AS03 adjuvant with an acceptable safety profile. These results are consistent with the established data for the registered AS03-adjuvanted H5N1 vaccine. The results demonstrated a favorable benefit/risk profile for all formulations tested, including 3.75 µg/AS03A, which is the currently licensed formulation of H5N1 Arepanrix.
