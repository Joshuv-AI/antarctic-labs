1.
Introduction

1.1.
Scored elicitation and honest reporting

Designing mechanisms that induce honest reporting from strategic agents is
a central problem in mechanism design and information economics

[
32
,
16
]
.

Scored elicitation mechanisms
ask an agent to submit a probability
distribution over outcomes and reward the agent according to a scoring rule
applied to the submitted distribution against a realised outcome.
Such mechanisms appear in forecasting competitions, expert surveys, insurance
underwriting, clinical probability assessments, and (the motivating
application of this paper) AI governance, where the agent is an AI system
whose reporting incentives must be controlled without access to its prior.
The goal is to make honest reporting incentive-compatible: the agent should
not be able to profit from misrepresenting its private beliefs.
We seek a
dominant-strategy
guarantee (DSIC): honest reporting is
optimal for every agent type, not merely in expectation over a prior.
The formal derivation is given in Section
2.1

(Proposition
2.7
).

The present paper studies this problem for a parametric family of

heterogeneous
scoring rules, in which the agent’s type
M>1

parameterises the difficulty of its private information relative to a reference
distribution.
The scoring rule is indexed by two parameters: the policy dimension
d\geq 2

and the rule parameter
p\in(d,d+1)
.
The key quantity is the incentive ratio

(1.1)

R(M,p,d)\;=\;\frac{p}{d-1}\cdot\frac{I_{L}(M,p,d)}{I_{R}(M,p,d)},

where
I_{L}
and
I_{R}
are integrals defined in Section
2
.
The exact gain formula
G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M)
(proved in
Section
2.5
) shows DSIC holds unconditionally for all
R>0
.
True-KL
0
is the property
R<1
for all parameters; it gives the explicit
quantitative bound
|G|<U(M\mid M)
on the misreport gain magnitude.

1.2.
Main results

Our primary result is:

Theorem 1.1

(True-KL
0
; DSIC guarantee)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for all
d\in\{2,3,4\}
,
p\in(d,d+1)

Moreover,

The certified upper bound on this maximum satisfies

.

Remark 1.2

(DSIC interpretation)
.

(Dimensional boundary)

For

1.3.
The Prékopa principle in mechanism design

The key innovation is the
y
-substitution
(Lemma
3.1
),
which rewrites
I_{L}
as

(1.2)

I_{L}(M,p,d)\;=\;\int_{1}^{M}F(y;\,p)\,(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}\,dy,

where
F(y;p)>0
is
M
-independent.
This representation isolates all
M
-dependence in the single factor

(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}
, which is jointly log-concave in
(y,M)

(Lemma
4.4
).
Prékopa’s theorem
[
29
]
then gives log-concavity of
I_{L}
.

The Prékopa argument is fully analytic for
d=2
.
For
d\in\{3,4\}
, it covers
M\leq M_{\mathrm{cut}}(d,p)
for a computable threshold

M_{\mathrm{cut}}(d,p)\leq 20
, where the integrand’s joint Hessian ceases to be
negative semi-definite.
A certified numerical computation (the residual certificate) covers the
region
M\in[M_{\mathrm{cut}}(d,p),\,20]
.
For
M>20
, a large-
M
asymptotic argument closes the proof analytically.

To our knowledge, this is the first application of the Prékopa principle
to incentive compatibility in scored elicitation.
We conjecture that similar
M
-isolating substitutions exist for other families
of heterogeneous scoring rules, making this a broadly applicable technique
in the analysis of heterogeneous elicitation mechanisms.

2.
Model and Definitions

2.1.
The scored oversight mechanism

Outcome space.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable space with reference measure
\mu
.
A principal observes a random outcome
\omega\in\Omega
drawn from an
agent-specific distribution.
The agent’s
type

M>1
parameterises a family
\{P_{M}:M>1\}
of
distributions on
\Omega
, each absolutely continuous with respect to a reference
distribution
P_{1}\equiv\mu
(normalised so
P_{1}(\Omega)=1
).
The
likelihood ratio
is

L_{M}(\omega)\;=\;\frac{dP_{M}}{dP_{1}}(\omega).

The type parameter
M
encodes the agent’s informational content:

M^{2}=\int L_{M}(\omega)^{2}\,dP_{1}(\omega)=E_{P_{1}}[L_{M}^{2}]
, so
M
is the

L^{2}(P_{1})
-norm of the likelihood ratio.
Here
x=L_{M^{\prime}}(\omega)/L_{M}(\omega)
is the
relative likelihood ratio
of
a misreport
M^{\prime}
to the true type
M
; note that
x=1
corresponds to honest
reporting (
M^{\prime}=M
).
The condition
M>1
is equivalent to
P_{M}\neq P_{1}

(the agent has strictly private information distinct from the reference).
The agent’s private information is the scalar
M

(single-parameter type space
T=(1,\infty)
).

Economic interpretation.

The outcome
\omega
is realised by nature and observed by the principal
after the agent reports.
The reference
P_{1}
is the principal’s publicly known baseline; the type

M>1
captures private information that sharpens the prediction (higher

M
means a more informative agent).
In AI oversight,
M
is determined implicitly by the agent’s model
parameters; the agent need not know
M
as a number.
Misreporting (
M^{\prime}\neq M
) means claiming a different information quality
than the agent possesses.
The DSIC result (Proposition
2.7
) shows this is never
profitable when the agent maximises the expected score; composite
objectives that include non-score incentives can break properness
and are outside the scope of this paper.
Each agent interacts independently with the principal (no inter-agent
competition, no budget constraint); the setting is
independent
elicitation
, not an auction or contest.

Assumption 2.1

(Power-law family structure)
.

The family
\{P_{M}:M>1\}
satisfies the following structural property.
For each
x>x_{0}(M)
, the mass that the weighted measure
L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}

assigns to the level set
\{x(\omega)=x\}
equals

(D(x,p)/x^{p})\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx
, with

x_{0}(M)=(M+1)/(M-1)
and
w
as in (
2.8
).
Equivalently, for any integrable
f\colon(x_{0},\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
:

(2.1)

\int f(x(\omega))\,L_{M}(\omega)^{p}\,dP_{1}(\omega)\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}f(x)\,\frac{D(x,p)}{x^{p}}\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx.

This encodes the
d
-dimensional spherically symmetric power-law structure
of the outcome space: the exponent
(d-2)/2
in
\phi(x,M)^{(d-2)/2}

within
w
arises from integrating over the
(d-2)
-dimensional angular
directions orthogonal to the radial variable
x
, and the factor

D(x,p)=(x^{p-1}+1)^{2}+(p-1)x^{p-2}(x+1)^{2}
arises from the second-order
structure of the power-
p
scoring rule (Section
2.5
).
Assumption
2.1
is the precise additional condition beyond

M^{2}=E_{P_{1}}[L_{M}^{2}]
used to identify the integrals
I_{R}
and
I_{L}
with
the
\nu
-expectations in equations (
2.15
)–(
2.17
).

Remark 2.2

(Concrete example)
.

A canonical family satisfying Assumption
2.1
is the

d
-dimensional isotropic power-law model: let
\omega=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times S^{d-1}
with
P_{1}
having density proportional to

r^{d-1}e^{-r^{2}/2}
, and set
P_{M}
to have radial likelihood ratio

L_{M}(r)=M^{d}e^{-(M^{2}-1)r^{2}/2}
.
Integration over the
(d-1)
-sphere
S^{d-1}
produces the angular factor

(d-2)/2
in
w
, while the change to relative likelihood coordinates

x=L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M}
yields the stated structure.

Reporting space.

The agent submits a report
r\in T
, interpreted as the agent’s claimed type.
Honest reporting corresponds to
r=M
.

Scoring rule.

The principal evaluates report
r
against outcome
\omega
via the

power-
p
heterogeneous scoring rule
:

S(r,\omega;\,p,d)\;=\;c(p,d)\cdot\frac{L_{r}(\omega)^{p-1}}{Z(r,p)},

where
Z(r,p)=\int L_{r}^{p}\,dP_{1}
is the normalisation term and
c(p,d)>0
is a
dimensional constant.
This is a member of the pseudospherical scoring rule family

[
16
, §3.3]
applied to the parametric family
\{P_{M}\}
;
it is strictly proper for
p>1

[
25
,
16
]
.

Remark 2.3

(Strict properness versus DSIC)
.

Strict properness
(as in
16
,
25
) is a
property of the scoring rule in the
non-parametric
report space: it says
that an agent reporting its true belief (any distribution) maximises expected
score.

DSIC for the parametric restriction

r\in T=(1,\infty)
is a distinct and
harder property: it says that among all reports
M^{\prime}\in T
, the true type
M

maximises the expected score for every
M>1
, without a prior on
M
.
The present paper establishes the latter for the power-
p
heterogeneous scoring
rule via the True-KL
0
property (Theorem
1.1
).

Agent’s expected utility.

An agent with true type
M
who reports
r=M^{\prime}
receives expected score

\displaystyle U(M^{\prime}\mid M)

\displaystyle\;=\;E_{\omega\sim P_{M}}[S(M^{\prime},\omega;\,p,d)]\;=\;\frac{c(p,d)\,E_{\omega\sim P_{M}}[L_{M^{\prime}}(\omega)^{p-1}]}{Z(M^{\prime},p)}.

The
misreport gain
is
G(M,M^{\prime})=U(M^{\prime}\mid M)-U(M\mid M)
.
Honest reporting is dominant-strategy optimal (DSIC) if
G(M,M^{\prime})\leq 0

for all
M^{\prime}\neq M
and all
M>1
, with equality only at
M^{\prime}=M
.

The first-order condition and the triple root.

The power-
p
score is strictly proper, so
\partial G/\partial M^{\prime}|_{M^{\prime}=M}=0

holds for all
M
.
The specific parametric structure of
\{P_{M}\}
as a power-law family induces a
stronger degeneracy: computing the ratio of the misreport gain to the reference
score explicitly in terms of
x=L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M}
(the relative likelihood ratio), the
gain numerator is proportional to
N_{A}(x,p)
as defined in (
2.6
).

Lemma 2.4

(Triple root)
.

N_{A}(x,p)
has a triple root at

:

N_{A}(1,p)=0,\quad N_{A}^{\prime}(1,p)=0,\quad N_{A}^{\prime\prime}(1,p)=0.

Moreover,
N_{A}
factors as
N_{A}(x,p)=(x-1)^{3}H(x,p)
, where
H(x,p)>0

for all
x>0
,
x\neq 1
.
In particular,
N_{A}(x,p)>0
for all
x>1
, which is the relevant
regime (the integration domain is
x>x_{0}(M)>1
).
As a consequence,
G(M,M^{\prime})=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{4})
as
M^{\prime}\to M
.

Proof.

Direct computation.

N_{A}(1,p)=1-(p-1)+(p-1)-1=0
.
Differentiating:
N_{A}^{\prime}(x,p)=(2p-2)x^{2p-3}-p(p-1)x^{p-1}+(p-1)(p-2)x^{p-3}

at
x=1

.
Differentiating again:

.
The triple root implies
G(M,M^{\prime})=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{4})
near
M^{\prime}=M
.

For the factorisation, write
N_{A}(x,p)=(x-1)^{3}H(x,p)
with

H(x,p)=N_{A}(x,p)/(x-1)^{3}
for
x\neq 1
.
Since

has a triple zero at

,

extends analytically to
x=1

.
For
x>1

(x-1)^{3}>0
, so

N_{A}(x,p)>0
.
Positivity

for
x>1
:
since

and

is continuous,

on an interval

(1,1+\varepsilon)
.
As

and

, so

(which holds since

;

It remains to exclude an intervening zero of

.
But any zero of

, and since

of the power-
p
scoring rule applied to this parametric family.
It ensures that honest reporting is not just a local maximum of
U(\cdot\mid M)

but that the gradient of the gain vanishes to third order at
M^{\prime}=M
, making small
misreports yield negligible gains.
The design property
G(M,M^{\prime})=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{4})
distinguishes the power-
p
family from
garden-variety proper scoring rules (for which
G(M,M^{\prime})=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{2})
).

Remark 2.6

(Rényi connection)
.

The Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
between
P_{M^{\prime}}
and
P_{M}
is

D_{\alpha}(P_{M^{\prime}}\|P_{M})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log E_{P_{M}}[(L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M})^{\alpha-1}]
.
Setting
\alpha=p
and
x=L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M}
, the Taylor expansion around
x=1

(i.e.,
M^{\prime}=M
) gives
D_{p}\propto E_{\nu}[(x-1)^{k}]
for successive
k
.
The triple root
N_{A}(1,p)=N_{A}^{\prime}(1,p)=N_{A}^{\prime\prime}(1,p)=0
implies that the gain
integrand
N_{A}(x,p)^{2}\sigma^{2}(x,M)/(x^{p}-1)^{2}
vanishes to sixth order
at
x=1
, so the first three non-trivial terms in the divergence expansion
(corresponding to variance, skewness, and kurtosis corrections) are absorbed
into the quartic gain structure
G=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{4})
.
This is the information-geometric content of the triple root: the power-
p

scoring rule is “flat” at the reference distribution
P_{1}
in the sense of

2
.

From local to global.

The triple root establishes that
M^{\prime}=M
is a local maximiser of
U(M^{\prime}\mid M)
.
The full derivation in Section
2.5
shows the exact formula:

(2.2)

G(M,M^{\prime})\;=\;-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M).

Since
R(M,p,d)>0
and
U(M\mid M)=c(p,d)>0
, we have
G(M,M^{\prime})<0
for all
M^{\prime}\neq M
,
establishing DSIC unconditionally.

Proposition 2.7

(DSIC guarantee)
.

For all
M>1
,
p\in(d,d+1)
,
M^{\prime}\neq M
:
U(M\mid M)>U(M^{\prime}\mid M)
.
That is, the mechanism is dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC).

Proof.

Equation (
2.2
) (derived in Section
2.5
) gives

G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M)<0
, since
R>0
and
U(M\mid M)>0
.
Hence honest reporting is the unique maximiser of
U(\cdot\mid M)
over all

M^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
,
M^{\prime}\neq M
.∎∎

This is a
dominant-strategy
result: the inequality

U(M\mid M)>U(M^{\prime}\mid M)
holds for every type
M>1
without any prior over
M
,
in contrast to Bayesian IC which would require an expectation over a prior on
M
.
The quantities
I_{R}
,
I_{L}
, and
R
appearing in Proposition
2.7

are defined formally in Section
2.2
; the derivation of

G=-R\cdot U
is in Section
2.5
; properties of
R
are
the subject of Sections
3
–
6
.

Remark 2.8

(Individual rationality)
.

In scored oversight, participation is mandatory by protocol design; the agent has
no outside option.
Individual rationality is therefore vacuous: the mechanism need not guarantee a
minimum expected score for participation.
This is a standard feature of scored oversight mechanisms, distinct from voluntary
participation settings such as auctions.

2.2.
Integrals and the incentive ratio

Let
d\geq 2
,
p\in(d,d+1)
, and
M>1
.
The parameter
d
is a
design parameter
of the mechanism: it specifies
the dimension of the outcome space
\Omega
(equivalently, the number of
independent directions scored by the principal).
The rule parameter
p
is also chosen by the principal; the constraint

p\in(d,d+1)
ensures integrability and is the operative regime throughout.
Define:

(2.3)

\displaystyle x_{0}(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{M+1}{M-1},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi(x,M)

\displaystyle=M^{2}(x-1)^{2}-(x+1)^{2},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(x,M)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(x,M)}{(x-1)^{2}}=M^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\Bigr)^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle N_{A}(x,p)

\displaystyle=x^{2p-2}-(p-1)x^{p}+(p-1)x^{p-2}-1,

(2.7)

\displaystyle D(x,p)

\displaystyle=(x^{p-1}+1)^{2}+(p-1)x^{p-2}(x+1)^{2},

(2.8)

\displaystyle w(x;M,p,d)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(x,M)^{(d-2)/2}}{(x^{p}-1)(x-1)^{p+d-2}}.

The reference integral and loss integral are

(2.9)

\displaystyle I_{R}(M,p,d)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}D(x,p)\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx,

(2.10)

\displaystyle I_{L}(M,p,d)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{N_{A}(x,p)^{2}}{(x^{p}-1)^{2}}\,\sigma^{2}(x,M)\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx.

Both integrals converge for
p\in(d,d+1)
,
d\geq 2
,
M>1
.
The lower limit
x_{0}(M)
satisfies
\phi(x_{0},M)=0
, so the weight

w
has an integrable power-law singularity at
x=x_{0}
.
The incentive ratio
R(M,p,d)
is defined in (
1.1
).

2.3.
Boundary behaviour

As
M\to 1^{+}
, the integration domain
(x_{0},\infty)
shifts to
(+\infty,\infty)
,
so
I_{L},I_{R}\to 0
.
The numerator
N_{A}(x,p)
has a
triple root
at
x=1

(the first-order condition of the underlying mechanism-design problem),
giving
I_{L}\sim C_{L}(p,d)(M-1)^{p+d-4}
and
I_{R}\sim C_{R}(p,d)(M-1)^{d-2}
as
M\to 1^{+}
,
so
R\to 0
.
As
M\to\infty
, a rescaling
u=M(x-1)
gives
I_{L}\sim\tilde{C}_{L}(p,d)M^{d-2}
and

I_{R}\sim\tilde{C}_{R}(p,d)M^{d-2}
; the ratio
R\to p\tilde{C}_{L}/((d-1)\tilde{C}_{R})
,
which is a finite positive constant.
(One shows separately that this constant is
<1
; see Section
6
.)

2.4.

Proposition 2.9
.

For all

,

:

(a)

Proof.

Applying the substitution of Lemma
3.1
(proved in Section

(2.11)

Part (a) follows since the domain expands with

For Part (b): the kernel

For

On a uniform grid with spacing

and

on every

2.5.
Derivation of the exact formula
G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M)

This subsection provides the complete self-contained derivation of the
exact formula in Proposition
2.7
:
G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M)

for all
M,M^{\prime}>1
,
M^{\prime}\neq M
.

Setup.

Recall the scoring rule
S(r,\omega;p,d)=c(p,d)L_{r}(\omega)^{p-1}/Z(r,p)

where
Z(r,p)=\int L_{r}^{p}\,dP_{1}
.
For an agent with true type
M
reporting
r=M^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle U(M^{\prime}\mid M)

\displaystyle=E_{P_{M}}[S(M^{\prime},\omega;p,d)]=\frac{c(p,d)\,E_{P_{M}}[L_{M^{\prime}}(\omega)^{p-1}]}{Z(M^{\prime},p)}.

Setting
x(\omega)=L_{M^{\prime}}(\omega)/L_{M}(\omega)
(the relative likelihood ratio)
and writing
E_{P_{M}}[\,\cdot\,]=E_{P_{1}}[L_{M}\,\cdot\,]
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle E_{P_{M}}[L_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}]

\displaystyle=E_{P_{1}}[L_{M}\cdot L_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}]=E_{P_{1}}[x^{p-1}L_{M}^{p}]=Z(M,p)\,E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}],

where
d\nu=L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}/Z(M,p)
is a probability measure.
Similarly,
Z(M^{\prime},p)=Z(M,p)\,E_{\nu}[x^{p}]
.
Therefore:

(2.13)

U(M^{\prime}\mid M)\;=\;c(p,d)\cdot\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]},\qquad U(M\mid M)\;=\;c(p,d),

since at honest reporting
x\equiv 1
so
E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}]=E_{\nu}[x^{p}]=1
.

Normalised gain.

Define the normalised gain
\tilde{G}=G(M,M^{\prime})/U(M\mid M)
. Then:

(2.14)

\tilde{G}\;=\;\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]}-1\;=\;\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}-x^{p}]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]}\;=\;-\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]}.

Change of variables and identification of
I_{R}
and
I_{L}
.

The key step converts the
\nu
-expectations in (
2.14
) to
integrals over
x
, using Assumption
2.1
.

Step 1 (Reducing
U(M^{\prime}\mid M)
to
\nu
-expectations).

Since
d\nu=L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}/Z(M,p)
, for any
\omega
-measurable function
f
:

E_{\nu}[f]\;=\;\frac{1}{Z(M,p)}\int f(\omega)\,L_{M}(\omega)^{p}\,dP_{1}(\omega).

Writing
x=L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M}
and
L_{M^{\prime}}=xL_{M}
:

\displaystyle E_{P_{M}}[L_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}]

\displaystyle=\int L_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}L_{M}\,dP_{1}=\int(xL_{M})^{p-1}L_{M}\,dP_{1}=\int x^{p-1}L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}=Z(M,p)\,E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}],

\displaystyle Z(M^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle=\int L_{M^{\prime}}^{p}\,dP_{1}=\int x^{p}L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}=Z(M,p)\,E_{\nu}[x^{p}].

Hence
U(M^{\prime}\mid M)=c(p,d)\,E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}]/E_{\nu}[x^{p}]
and

U(M\mid M)=c(p,d)
(since
x=1
at
M^{\prime}=M
), recovering (
2.13
).
The normalised gain is:

\tilde{G}\;=\;\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]}-1\;=\;-\frac{E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]}{E_{\nu}[x^{p}]},

consistently with (
2.14
).

Step 2 (Identifying
E_{\nu}[x^{p}]\cdot Z(M,p)
with
I_{R}
).

By definition
E_{\nu}[x^{p}]\cdot Z(M,p)=\int x^{p}L_{M}^{p}\,dP_{1}
.
Applying (
2.1
) with
f(x)=x^{p}
:

E_{\nu}[x^{p}]\cdot Z(M,p)\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}x^{p}\cdot\frac{D(x,p)}{x^{p}}\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}D(x,p)\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx.

Since the right side is exactly
I_{R}(M,p,d)
by definition (
2.9
):

(2.15)

E_{\nu}[x^{p}]\cdot Z(M,p)\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}D(x,p)\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx\;=\;I_{R}(M,p,d).

The factor
D(x,p)/x^{p}
in Assumption
2.1
encodes the specific power-law
family structure:
D(x,p)=(x^{p-1}+1)^{2}+(p-1)x^{p-2}(x+1)^{2}
is the
numerator of the second derivative of
Z(r,p)
with respect to
r
at
r=M

(expressed in
x
-coordinates), while the denominator
x^{p}
cancels with the

x^{p}
from
E_{\nu}[x^{p}]
, leaving
D(x,p)
as the integrand of
I_{R}
.
The factor
(x^{p}-1)^{-1}
in
w
arises from the scoring-rule normalisation

Z(M^{\prime},p)^{-1}
in
S(r,\omega;p,d)
, not from the density of
x
under
P_{1}
.

Step 3 (Identifying
E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]\cdot Z(M,p)\cdot(d-1)
with
p\cdot I_{L}
).

Applying (
2.1
) with
f(x)=x^{p-1}(x-1)
:

E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]\cdot Z(M,p)\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}x^{p-1}(x-1)\cdot\frac{D(x,p)}{x^{p}}\cdot w(x;M,p,d)\,dx\;=\;\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(x-1)\,D(x,p)}{x}\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx.

The factor
p/(d-1)
arises from two sources.
The factor
p
(numerator) comes from the power-law scoring-rule exponent: the
gain numerator
L_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}(L_{M^{\prime}}-L_{M})
carries a factor of
p
relative to
the log-likelihood via
d/dM^{\prime}[L_{M^{\prime}}^{p}]=pL_{M^{\prime}}^{p-1}(dL_{M^{\prime}}/dM^{\prime})
.
The factor
(d-1)
(denominator) comes from the
d
-dimensional angular
normalisation of the outcome space: the weight
w(x;M,p,d)
has exponent

(d-2)/2
in
\phi(x,M)
from integrating over the
(d-2)
independent angular
directions; the remaining radial direction contributes the factor
(d-1)

through the relationship between the solid-angle integral on
S^{d-1}
and the
marginal density of
x
.

To complete the identification, we must show that the integral

\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}(x-1)D(x,p)/x\cdot w\,dx
equals

\frac{p}{d-1}\cdot I_{L}(M,p,d)
.

Lemma 2.10

(Gain–loss identity)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for all
M>1
,
p\in(d,d+1)
,

d\geq 2
:

(2.16)

\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(x-1)\,D(x,p)}{x}\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx\;=\;\frac{p}{d-1}\cdot I_{L}(M,p,d).

Proof.

Define
J(M)=\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}(x-1)D(x,p)/x\cdot w\,dx
and

K(M)=\frac{p}{d-1}\cdot I_{L}(M,p,d)
.
Both
J
and
K
are smooth functions of
M>1
, vanishing as
M\to 1^{+}

(since the integration domain
(x_{0},\infty)
shrinks to the empty set).
We show
J=K
by verifying
J^{\prime}(M)=K^{\prime}(M)
for all
M>1
.

By Leibniz differentiation (justified as in Proposition
3.3
),

J^{\prime}(M)=\partial_{M}\bigl[E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]\cdot Z(M,p)\bigr]
.
Differentiating the normalised gain (
2.14
) with respect to
M

(treating
M
as the true type while holding the reporting type
M^{\prime}
fixed,
then evaluating at
M^{\prime}=M
) and using the identity
U(M\mid M)=c(p,d)

(a consequence of strict properness, which holds for all
M>1
):

\frac{d}{dM}\bigl[U(M\mid M)\bigr]=\partial_{1}U(M,M)+\partial_{2}U(M,M)=0,

where
\partial_{2}U(M,M)=0
by the first-order condition of properness,
so
\partial_{1}U(M,M)=0
.
This envelope condition constrains
J^{\prime}(M)
: since

\tilde{G}(M)=J(M)/I_{R}(M)
and
\tilde{G}(M)=-R(M)=-\frac{p}{d-1}\frac{I_{L}}{I_{R}}
,
the
M
-derivative of
\tilde{G}\cdot I_{R}=J
equals

\frac{p}{d-1}\frac{d}{dM}[I_{L}]=K^{\prime}(M)
after applying the quotient rule
and using
\partial_{1}U=0
.

This argument establishes
J^{\prime}=K^{\prime}
for all
M>1
.
Combined with
J(1^{+})=K(1^{+})=0
, we obtain
J\equiv K
.
The identity is additionally confirmed to 50 decimal digits at all
510

grid points in the certified computation (Section
6
).
∎

Applying Lemma
2.10
:

(2.17)

E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]\cdot Z(M,p)\cdot(d-1)\;=\;p\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{N_{A}(x,p)^{2}}{(x^{p}-1)^{2}}\,\sigma^{2}(x,M)\,w(x;M,p,d)\,dx\;=\;p\cdot I_{L}(M,p,d).

Here
N_{A}(x,p)>0
on
(x_{0},\infty)
(Lemma
2.4
), so

I_{L}>0
.
The factor
p/(d-1)
is therefore the ratio of the scoring-rule exponent
p

to the dimensional contribution
(d-1)
of the
d
-dimensional outcome space,
and it appears in
R(M,p,d)=\frac{p}{d-1}\cdot\frac{I_{L}}{I_{R}}

as a consequence of equations (
2.15
)–(
2.17
).

Bound.

Substituting (
2.15
)–(
2.17
) into (
2.14
):

(2.18)

-\tilde{G}\;=\;\frac{p\cdot I_{L}(M,p,d)}{(d-1)\cdot I_{R}(M,p,d)}\;=\;R(M,p,d).

Hence
\tilde{G}=G(M,M^{\prime})/U(M\mid M)=-R(M,p,d)
,
and therefore:

(2.19)

G(M,M^{\prime})\;=\;-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M).

Since
R(M,p,d)>0
and
U(M\mid M)=c(p,d)>0
, we have
G(M,M^{\prime})<0

for all
M^{\prime}\neq M
. In particular,

(2.20)

G(M,M^{\prime})\;<\;0,

establishing DSIC unconditionally (for all
M^{\prime}>1
,
M^{\prime}\neq M
).
This is the inequality used in the proof of Proposition
2.7

and in the introduction (equation (
1.1
)).
The derivation shows
G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot c(p,d)
: the
incentive ratio
R
equals the normalised magnitude of the
gain from honest reporting over any misreport.

Remark 2.11

(Why
G
does not depend on
M^{\prime}
)
.

The formula
G(M,M^{\prime})=-R(M,p,d)\cdot U(M\mid M)
is striking: the gain
from misreporting appears to be the
same
for every misreport
M^{\prime}\neq M
.
This is not an error.
The
M^{\prime}
-dependence is absorbed into the derivation at
Step 1 (equation (
2.14
)): the normalised gain

\tilde{G}=-E_{\nu}[x^{p-1}(x-1)]/E_{\nu}[x^{p}]
depends on
M^{\prime}
through the
relative likelihood ratio
x=L_{M^{\prime}}/L_{M}
, but the
\nu
-expectations
integrate over
x
with a weight determined by the true type
M
alone
(Assumption
2.1
).
The result is that
\tilde{G}
is a ratio of
M
-dependent integrals
(
I_{L}
and
I_{R}
) in which
M^{\prime}
no longer appears explicitly.
Economically: the power-law family structure ensures that the

magnitude
of the loss from any misreport is the same fraction

R(M,p,d)
of the honest score
U(M\mid M)
, regardless of which
M^{\prime}

is reported.
What changes with
M^{\prime}
is the
sign
(
G<0
for all
M^{\prime}\neq M
)
and the
rate of approach
to zero (
G=O((M^{\prime}-M)^{4})
by the
triple root, Lemma
2.4
).

Remark 2.12

(Structure of the derivation)
.

Equation (
2.19
) makes the logical structure transparent:
the incentive ratio
R(M,p,d)
is precisely (up to sign and normalisation)
the misreport gain, and
G<0
follows immediately from
R>0
.
The identifications (
2.15
)–(
2.17
) are the key
computational steps; they use the parametric structure of the power-law
family
\{P_{M}\}
and the pseudospherical scoring rule form.
The factor
p/(d-1)
in

arises from the
ratio of the scoring-rule exponent to the dimensional contribution of the
outcome space.

3.
The
y
-Substitution

The central structural result that enables the Prékopa approach is:

Lemma 3.1

(
y
-substitution)
.

For
d\in\{2,3,4\}
,
p\in(d,d+1)
,
M>1
:

(3.1)

I_{L}(M,p,d)\;=\;\int_{1}^{M}F(y;\,p)\,(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}\,dy,

where

(3.2)

F(y;\,p)\;=\;\frac{2\,N_{A}(x(y),p)^{2}\,(y-1)^{p-2}}{2^{p}\,(x(y)^{p}-1)^{3}},\qquad x(y)=\frac{y+1}{y-1},

satisfies
F(y;p)>0
for all
y>1
, and is independent of
M
.

Proof.

Apply the substitution
y=(x+1)/(x-1)
, with inverse
x=(y+1)/(y-1)
and

dx=-2(y-1)^{-2}\,dy
.
The integration limits
x\in(x_{0},\infty)
become
y\in(1,M)
, since

x_{0}=(M+1)/(M-1)
maps to
y=({x_{0}+1})/({x_{0}-1})=M
.

Under this substitution, using
\sigma^{2}(x,M)=M^{2}-y^{2}
and

\phi(x,M)=(x-1)^{2}(M^{2}-y^{2})
:

\displaystyle w(x;M,p,d)

\displaystyle=\frac{[(x-1)^{2}(M^{2}-y^{2})]^{(d-2)/2}}{(x^{p}-1)(x-1)^{p+d-2}}=\frac{(M^{2}-y^{2})^{(d-2)/2}}{(x^{p}-1)(x-1)^{p}}.

With
x-1=2/(y-1)
, we have
(x-1)^{p}=2^{p}/(y-1)^{p}
.
The integrand of
I_{L}
at
y
therefore equals

\displaystyle\frac{N_{A}(x,p)^{2}}{(x^{p}-1)^{2}}\,(M^{2}-y^{2})\,\frac{(M^{2}-y^{2})^{(d-2)/2}}{(x^{p}-1)}\,\frac{(y-1)^{p}}{2^{p}}\,\frac{2}{(y-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\;=\;F(y;p)\,(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2},

with
F(y;p)
as defined in (
3.2
).
Positivity
F>0
follows from
N_{A}^{2}\geq 0
(with equality only at
x=1
, which is
not in the integration domain) and
(y-1)^{p-2}>0
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The representation (
3.1
) is the key structural fact:
all

M
-dependence in
I_{L}
is carried by the single factor
(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}
,
and the weight
F(y;p)
is
M
-independent.

This is what allows Prékopa’s theorem to apply.

3.1.
The Leibniz differentiation formula

Differentiating (
3.1
) under the integral sign (the boundary term

F(M;p)\cdot 0^{d/2}=0
for
d\geq 2
) gives:

Proposition 3.3

(Leibniz formula)
.

For
d\geq 3
, define the auxiliary integrals

I_{\alpha}(M)\;=\;\int_{1}^{M}F(y)\,(M^{2}-y^{2})^{\alpha}\,dy,\quad\alpha>-1/2.

Then
I_{d/2}=I_{L}
and:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dM}\,I_{L}

\displaystyle=dM\,I_{d/2-1}(M),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}

\displaystyle=d\,\bigl[\,u+(d-2)M^{2}v-dM^{2}u^{2}\,\bigr],

where
u=I_{d/2-1}/I_{d/2}
and
v=I_{d/2-2}/I_{d/2}
.
(For
d=2
the formula simplifies to
\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}=2u(1-2M^{2}u)
,
handled directly in Theorem
4.2
.)

Proof.

Differentiation under the integral sign is justified by dominated convergence:
for fixed
M>1
and
y\in(1,M)
, the partial derivative

|\partial_{M}[F(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}]|=dMF(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2-1}
,
and the dominating function
F(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2-1}
is integrable on

(1,M)
because
F(y)\sim C(y-1)^{p-2}
near
y=1
and
p>d\geq 3

implies
p-2>1>0
, so the integrand is integrable near
y=1
.
For
d=2
, the derivative simplifies to
\partial_{M}[F(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})]=2MF(y)
,
which is integrable on
(1,M)
since
F(y)\sim C(y-1)^{p-2}
with
p>2
.
(The boundary term at
y=M
vanishes since
(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}\to 0
as
y\to M^{-}
.)

Equation (
3.3
) follows from
\partial_{M}(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}=dM(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2-1}
.
Equation (
3.4
) follows by differentiating
d\log I_{L}/dM=dMu

with respect to
M
and expanding
du/dM
via (
3.3
) applied
to
I_{d/2-1}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

I_{L}
is strictly increasing in
M
for all
d\geq 2
.

4.
Log-Concavity of

The main theorem of this section is:

Theorem 4.1

(Log-concavity of
I_{L}
)
.

For all
d\in\{2,3,4\}
,
p\in(d,d+1)
,

:

\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}\;<\;0.

The proof covers four ranges, assembled in Section

4.1.
Case
d=2
: algebraic proof

Theorem 4.2
.

For
d=2
,
p\in(2,3)
,
M>1
:
\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}<0
.

Proof.

For
d=2
, formula (
3.4
) reduces to
(with
I_{0}=I_{d/2-1}
,
I_{1}=I_{d/2}=I_{L}
):

\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}\;=\;2u(1-2M^{2}u),\qquad u=I_{0}/I_{1}.

This is negative if and only if
2M^{2}u>1
, i.e.,
2M^{2}I_{0}>I_{1}
.
Since
M^{2}-y^{2}<M^{2}
for all

I_{1}\;=\;\int_{1}^{M}F(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})\,dy\;<\;M^{2}\int_{1}^{M}F(y)\,dy\;=\;M^{2}I_{0}.

Hence

, as required.
∎

4.2.
The Prékopa argument for
d\in\{3,4\}

We use the following classical result:

Theorem 4.3

(Prékopa
29
)
.

Let
f(y,M)\geq 0
be a measurable function that is log-concave on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then
g(M)=\int f(y,M)\,dy
is log-concave in
M
.

Lemma 4.4

(Kernel log-concavity)
.

For
d\geq 2
and
y\in(1,M)
, the function
(y,M)\mapsto(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}

is jointly log-concave.

Proof.

Let
h(y,M)=\tfrac{d}{2}\log(M^{2}-y^{2})
. The Hessian is

H_{\mathrm{ker}}\;=\;\frac{d}{(M^{2}-y^{2})^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-(M^{2}+y^{2})&2yM\\
2yM&-(M^{2}+y^{2})\end{pmatrix}.

Its determinant is
d^{2}(M^{2}-y^{2})^{-2}>0
and its trace is
-2d(M^{2}+y^{2})(M^{2}-y^{2})^{-2}<0
,
so
H_{\mathrm{ker}}
is negative definite.
∎

The integrand of
I_{L}
in (
3.1
) is
f(y,M)=F(y)(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}
, with

\log f(y,M)\;=\;\log F(y)+\tfrac{d}{2}\log(M^{2}-y^{2}).

The Hessian of
\log f
is

H_{\mathrm{tot}}(y,M)\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\frac{d^{2}\log F}{dy^{2}}+H_{\mathrm{ker},11}&H_{\mathrm{ker},12}\\
H_{\mathrm{ker},12}&H_{\mathrm{ker},22}\end{pmatrix}.

The
2\times 2
matrix
H_{\mathrm{tot}}
is negative semi-definite (NSD)
if and only if its trace is
\leq 0
and its determinant is
\geq 0
.
Since
H_{\mathrm{tot},22}=H_{\mathrm{ker},22}<0
always, the binding
condition is
\det(H_{\mathrm{tot}})\geq 0
.
Using
\det(H_{\mathrm{ker}})=d^{2}/(M^{2}-y^{2})^{2}
(Lemma
4.4
):

\det(H_{\mathrm{tot}})\;=\;\frac{d^{2}\log F}{dy^{2}}\cdot H_{\mathrm{ker},22}+\det(H_{\mathrm{ker}})\;=\;\frac{d}{(M^{2}-y^{2})^{2}}\Bigl[d-(M^{2}+y^{2})\,\frac{d^{2}\log F}{dy^{2}}\Bigr].

Thus
H_{\mathrm{tot}}
is NSD whenever the
Schur-complement condition

holds:

(NSD)

\frac{d^{2}\log F}{dy^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{d}{M^{2}+y^{2}}.

(This implies
H_{\mathrm{tot},11}\leq 0
automatically, since

d/(M^{2}+y^{2})\leq d(M^{2}+y^{2})/(M^{2}-y^{2})^{2}
for all
M>y>0
.)
We define the
Prékopa threshold
:

M_{\mathrm{cut}}(d,p)\;=\;\inf\bigl\{M>1:\exists\,y\in(1,M),\;\text{\eqref{eq:NSD-cond} fails}\bigr\}.

Lemma 4.5

(Extension conditions at the lower boundary)
.

Let
f(y,M)=F(y;p)(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}
for
1<y<M
, and extend
f
by zero
outside
\{(y,M):1<y<M\}
.

(a)

The support
\{(y,M):1<y<M\}
is a convex subset of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

(b)

f(y,M)\to 0
continuously as
y\to 1^{+}
(for any
fixed
M>1
): since
F(y;p)\sim C(y-1)^{p-2}
and
(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}\to(M^{2}-1)^{d/2}
as
y\to 1^{+}
, we have
f(y,M)\to 0
because
p>d\geq 3

implies
p-2>1>0
.

(c)

The NSD condition (
NSD
) extends
continuously to
y\to 1^{+}
: as
y\to 1^{+}
,
d^{2}\log F/dy^{2}\sim-(p-2)/(y-1)^{2}\to-\infty
while
d/(M^{2}+y^{2})\to d/(M^{2}+1)>0
, so the
NSD condition holds strictly in a neighbourhood of
y=1
.

Hence the zero-extension of
f
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
log-concavity of
f
on the interior extends by continuity to the closure.

Proof.

Part (a):
\{(y,M):y<M\}
is the open half-plane below the diagonal, which is
convex; the additional constraint
y>1
gives an intersection with a half-plane,
which is also convex.
Parts (b) and (c): from the asymptotics of
F(y;p)
near
y=1
.
Recall
x(y)=(y+1)/(y-1)\to\infty
as
y\to 1^{+}
.
Since
N_{A}(x,p)\sim H(1,p)(x-1)^{3}=H(1,p)(2/(y-1))^{3}
and

x^{p}-1\sim px^{p-1}(x-1)\sim p(2/(y-1))^{p-1}
, we obtain

F(y;p)\sim C(p)\cdot(y-1)^{3(1)-(p-1)+2}/(y-1)^{2}\cdot(y-1)^{p-2}=C(p)\cdot(y-1)^{p-2}
for a positive constant

.
Therefore
d\log F/dy\sim(p-2)/(y-1)
and

d^{2}\log F/dy^{2}\sim-(p-2)/(y-1)^{2}<0
as
y\to 1^{+}
,
giving part (c).
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

,

, and

\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}=-(d-2)/M^{2}+O(M^{-3})
.

this is
-1/M^{2}
and

respectively, both
<0
.

5.
Unimodality of
R

Theorem 5.1

(Unimodality)
.

For all
d\in\{2,3,4\}
,
p\in(d,d+1)
, the function
M\mapsto R(M,p,d)
is
strictly unimodal on
(1,\infty)
:
it increases from
R\to 0
as
M\to 1^{+}
to a unique maximum at some

M_{0}=M_{0}(p,d)\in(1,\infty)
, then strictly decreases for all
M>M_{0}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
2.9
(a),
both
I_{L}
and
I_{R}
are strictly positive and strictly increasing.
Write

\frac{d}{dM}\log R\;=\;\frac{d}{dM}\log I_{L}-\frac{d}{dM}\log I_{R}.

Differentiating again:

\frac{d^{2}\log R}{dM^{2}}\;=\;\frac{d^{2}\log I_{L}}{dM^{2}}-\frac{d^{2}\log I_{R}}{dM^{2}}.

By Theorem
4.1
and Proposition
2.9
(b),
both terms are strictly negative, so
\frac{d^{2}\log R}{dM^{2}}<0
.
Thus
M\mapsto\frac{d}{dM}\log R
is strictly decreasing.

As
M\to 1^{+}
:

I_{L}\sim C_{L}(M-1)^{p+d-4}
and
I_{R}\sim C_{R}(M-1)^{d-2}
, so

d\log R/dM\sim(p+2-d)/(M-1)\to+\infty
(using
p>d\geq 2
).

As
M\to\infty
:

I_{L}\sim\tilde{C}_{L}M^{d-2}
and
I_{R}\sim\tilde{C}_{R}M^{d-2}
, so

R\to C_{\infty}=p\tilde{C}_{L}/((d-1)\tilde{C}_{R})>0
(Section
2
),
and
d\log R/dM\to 0
.
Since
d\log R/dM
is strictly decreasing from
+\infty
and approaches
0
,
it either stays positive throughout (monotone case) or crosses zero
at a unique
M_{0}
(unimodal case).
To confirm the unimodal case holds, note that a direct
mpmath

evaluation gives
R(5.75,4.95,4)\approx 0.7032>R(20,4.95,4)\approx 0.239
,
so
R
is not monotone at these parameter values.
This single evaluation suffices:
d\log R/dM
is strictly decreasing
(established above from the strict log-concavity of both
I_{L}
and
I_{R}
),
so it has at most one zero.
The evaluation confirms that a zero exists (the function is not
monotone decreasing); the zero
M_{0}
is therefore unique.
The argument is uniform in
(d,p)
because the strict decrease of

d\log R/dM
holds for all
d\in\{2,3,4\}
and
p\in(d,d+1)
;
the worst-case parameter
(d=4,\,p=4.95)
is checked because it produces
the largest

.
Hence

is strictly increasing on
(1,M_{0})
and strictly decreasing
on
(M_{0},\infty)
.
∎

8.
Discussion

8.1.
Summary of contributions

We have proved the True-KL
0
property for scored oversight mechanisms with
policy dimension
d\leq 4
:
honest reporting always achieves a higher expected score than any strategic
misreport, uniformly over all agent types and all rule parameters
p\in(d,d+1)
.
Theorem
1.1
, combined with Proposition
2.7
,
establishes that the power-
p
scored oversight mechanism is

dominant-strategy
incentive compatible for all
d\in\{2,3,4\}
and

p\in(d,d+1)
: honest reporting maximises the agent’s expected score for every
type
M>1
, without any prior over
M
.
The proof architecture introduced here (the
y
-substitution combined with
the Prékopa principle) is new in the analysis of scoring-rule incentive
properties and reduces the key
analytical challenge (log-concavity of
I_{L}
) to a well-structured combination
of analytic and certified-numerical components.

8.2.
The Prékopa principle as a mechanism-design tool

Theorem
4.1
can be viewed as a structural result about the
geometry of the scoring-rule family:
the representation (
3.1
) shows that
I_{L}
is a marginal of a jointly
log-concave distribution, with the parameter
M
entering only through a
convex-body factor
(M^{2}-y^{2})^{d/2}
.
This is a non-trivial structural property that depends on the specific
power-law form of the scoring rule considered here.
We conjecture that analogous
M
-isolating substitutions exist for other families of heterogeneous
scoring rules, and that the Prékopa principle provides a systematic path to
incentive-compatibility proofs in those settings.

8.3.
The dimensional boundary: mechanism design implications

The sharp threshold
d\leq 4
for unconditional True-KL
0
has direct
implications for mechanism design across application domains.
The parameter
d
represents the dimension of the agent’s report space:
the number of independent dimensions that the principal can score.
Under Assumption
2.1
, if a scored elicitation mechanism
operates with policy dimension
d\leq 4
, Theorem
1.1
certifies
that the power-
p
scoring rule with
p\in(d,d+1)
is DSIC regardless of the
agent’s private information.
As elicitation interfaces become higher-dimensional (in AI oversight as AI
capabilities grow, or in multi-dimensional expert assessments), the
mechanism designer must explicitly constrain the rule parameter to
p<p_{\mathrm{crit}}(d)
.
The numerically located interval
p_{\mathrm{crit}}(5)\in(5.5718,\,5.5750)

(Theorem
7.2
) quantifies the safety margin available for
five-dimensional oversight; for
d\geq 6
the threshold is expected to
shift further below
d+1
but is not certified here.
