Explicit Two-Sided Eigenvalue Bounds for Schrödinger Operators with Singular Potentials via Finite Element Method
Xuefeng Liu

Abstract
We present, to the best of our knowledge, the first numerical algorithm for explicit, computable two-sided eigenvalue bounds for Schrödinger operators H = − Δ + V H=-\Delta+V on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} ( N = 2 , 3 N=2,3 ) in the presence of both an unbounded potential and an unbounded domain . “Explicit” here means that all constants and ingredients are derived in closed form from the mesh, the potential, and a small set of explicit inequalities (Payne–Weinberger, Hardy, and explicit bounded-domain Sobolev embeddings); the conversion to fully verified (IEEE-754-safe, interval-arithmetic) enclosures is a separate verification step and is left for future work. In particular, singular attractive potentials of Coulomb type, V ( x ) = − Z / | x | V(x)=-Z/|x| , which model the hydrogen atom and the H 2 + \mathrm{H}_{2}^{+} molecular ion, are covered by the theory. The method combines domain truncation to a bounded domain D ( R ) ⊃ { | x | ≤ R } D(R)\supset\{|x|\leq R\} with an extension of Liu’s Composite Enriched Crouzeix–Raviart (CECR) finite element method to sign-indefinite potentials. Upper bounds come from the standard conforming Galerkin method; lower bounds come from the CECR construction, whose gap to the exact eigenvalue closes as the mesh is refined. Numerical experiments on the 2D single- and two-centred Coulomb potentials and on the 3D hydrogen atom and H 2 + \mathrm{H}_{2}^{+} molecular ion illustrate the algorithm and confirm the predicted convergence.

Introduction

Computing explicit two-sided bounds on eigenvalues
of the Schrödinger operator

H=-\Delta+V,\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\;N=2,3,

(1)

arises in quantum chemistry, mathematical physics, and the numerical
analysis of partial differential equations. Upper bounds on the
k
-th
eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
are readily obtained by the Rayleigh–Ritz
(Galerkin) method: any conforming trial space
V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

yields
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k,h}^{\mathrm{CG}}
. Obtaining a

explicit lower bound
requires non-conforming or specialised
methods, since the variational principle provides only upper bounds.
This paper develops such lower bounds for the physically important
class of Coulomb-type potentials.

introduced the projection-based eigenvalue bound as follows:

\frac{\lambda_{k,h}}{1+\lambda_{k,h}C_{h}^{2}}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k},

(2)

is a finite element approximation to eigenvalue and

C_{h}=O(h^{\alpha})
is an explicit error constant.
Related work on explicit eigenvalue bounds was initiated
by the early work of Kikuchi and Liu
[
12
,
18
]

[

,
14

[

]

[

[

]
(Steklov eigenvalues).
To the author’s knowledge, no existing finite element framework provides explicit and convergent lower bounds for eigenvalues associated with such unbounded singular potentials. This constitutes the central difficulty addressed in the present work, which is overcome by employing the Composite Enriched Crouzeix–Raviart (CECR) finite element method recently proposed in the author’s book
[

The coercivity gap

The existing CECR application assumes
V\geq 0
:
this ensures coercivity of the bilinear form

a(u,u)=\|\nabla u\|^{2}+(Vu,u)\geq\|\nabla u\|^{2}
,
a step used in the proof of (
2
).
Physically important potentials—Coulomb (
V=-Z/r
),
two-centered Coulomb (
V=-Z_{1}/|x-a_{1}|-Z_{2}/|x-a_{2}|
)
as in the
\mathrm{H}_{2}^{+}
molecular ion—are purely attractive (
V<0
).
For these,
a(u,u)
may be negative, and (
2
)
breaks down. A natural first attempt replaces
V
by its piecewise
constant element average
c_{h}
and shifts by

\gamma_{h}=\|c_{h}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, but
\gamma_{h}
diverges as
the mesh is refined for Coulomb singularities and the bound fails to converge
on uniform or radially graded meshes.
This paper develops a
pair-space
framework and introduces
an
elementwise reaction constant

\Gamma_{h}:=\max_{K}c_{h}^{-}|_{K}\cdot h_{K}^{\,2}/\pi^{2}

that replaces the divergent
\gamma_{h}
by a vanishing quantity

\Gamma_{h}\to 0
under refinement. The resulting CECR lower bound
(
Theorem

2
) is fully computable; the
CECR gap closes at rate
O(h^{2})
in the mesh-family parameter on
graded meshes in both 2D and 3D.
With the patch radius chosen according to the direct estimates in

Appendix

B
, the final certified bound
L_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}

inherits the same
O(h^{2})
rate.
This provides the first explicit convergent eigenvalue lower bound
for Coulomb-type potentials via non-conforming finite elements.

Domain truncation

For operators on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, a standard strategy truncates to a bounded domain

D(R)\supset\{|x|\leq R\}
with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.
Dirichlet truncation gives upper bounds
[
27
]

Neumann truncation gives lower bounds directly under an explicit
confinement condition on the exterior potential minimum

\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R)):=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)}V>\lambda_{k}

without requiring the CECR machinery.
The truncation error decays exponentially in
R
for potentials
with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions
[
1
]
.
We adopt both boundary conditions and compare.

Problem Setting and Domain Truncation

2.1
The Schrödinger Eigenvalue Problem

Let
N\in\{2,3\}
and let
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function
with
V=V^{+}-V^{-}
where
V^{\pm}:=\max(\pm V,0)\geq 0
.
We consider the eigenvalue problem

(-\Delta+V)\,u=\lambda\,u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(3)

in the sense of quadratic forms: find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and

u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+V\,uv\,dx=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\,dx\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(4)

Assumption 1

(Standing assumptions on
V
)
.

Throughout the paper we assume:

(i)

V^{+}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(ii)

V^{-}\in L^{p_{0}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
p_{0}>N/2
,
with
V^{-}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
(no growth at infinity);

(iii)

The operator
H=-\Delta+V
is self-adjoint and bounded below on

\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the form-sum sense; this is guaranteed by the
KLMN/Kato–Rellich framework whenever
V^{-}
is locally

L^{p_{0}}
with
p_{0}>N/2

and
is form-bounded with
relative bound
<1
, see

2
and
[
27
, Theorem X.17]
.

Remark 1

(Why local rather than global integrability)
.

The Coulomb tail
V^{-}(x)\sim Z/|x|
is
not
in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

for any single
p_{0}>N/2
: integrability at the origin requires

p_{0}<N
, while integrability at infinity requires
p_{0}>N
, two
incompatible conditions. We therefore impose only local

L^{p_{0}}
-integrability on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, supplemented by decay at infinity;
this suffices for self-adjointness and discrete spectrum below

\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(H)
for Coulomb-type
V
via the form-bound
of
2
(Hardy in
N=3
,
Gagliardo–Nirenberg in
N=2
).
Global

L^{p_{0}}
-integrability
is invoked only after truncation, where it reduces to

V\in L^{p_{0}}(D(R))
on the bounded domain
D(R)
.

The Coulomb-type potentials treated in this paper satisfy

1
: the single-centre potential
V^{-}=Z/|x-a|
belongs to

L^{p_{0}}(D(R))
for every
p_{0}<N
(since

\int_{D(R)}|x-a|^{-p_{0}}\,dx<\infty
when
p_{0}<N
), so in particular
for any
p_{0}\in(N/2,\,N)
; the two-centre potential

V^{-}=Z_{1}/|x-a_{1}|+Z_{2}/|x-a_{2}|
inherits the same local integrability.
Concretely, we take
p_{0}=4/3
when
N=2
and
p_{0}=3/2+\delta

(any small
\delta>0
) when
N=3
. After truncation to
D(R)
,

V\in L^{p_{0}}(D(R))
holds and is what enters the perturbation
analysis of
Section

4
.

Under
1
, the operator
H
has a discrete spectrum

\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
below

\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(H)
; see, e.g.,
[
25
]
.
We study the problem of computing explicit two-sided bounds on

\lambda_{k}
for
k=1,2,\ldots
.

2.2
Truncation to a Bounded Domain

Since
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is unbounded, we truncate to a bounded domain

D(R)\supset\{|x|\leq R\}
and impose boundary conditions on

\partial D(R)
. We choose it large enough that the computed discrete eigenvalues
yield a valid
lower bound for the ground state

\lambda_{1}
, i.e. so that (i) the confinement condition

\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))>\lambda_{1}
of
Lemma

1

holds (see
Remark

2
), and (ii) the
truncation error
\delta_{1}(R)
is negligible relative to the
discretisation error. Higher eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
,
k\geq 2
,
are reported but may not be covered by the Neumann-truncation
lower-bound machinery at the chosen
R
.
The domains used in §
6
are listed in

Table

; they contain the relevant nuclei
and are large enough for the ground-state confinement check.

Dirichlet Truncation

Let
\lambda_{k}^{R,D}
denote the
k
-th eigenvalue of

-\Delta+V
on
D(R)
with Dirichlet boundary condition

u=0
on
\partial D(R)
. By the min-max principle and domain
monotonicity (restricting to
H^{1}_{0}(D(R))\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
):

\lambda_{k}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}^{R,D}.

(5)

Thus
\lambda_{k}^{R,D}
is a computable
upper bound
for
\lambda_{k}
.

Neumann Truncation

Let
\lambda_{k}^{R,N}
denote the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta+V

on
D(R)
with Neumann boundary condition

\partial u/\partial\bm{n}=0
on
\partial D(R)
.
Define the
exterior potential minimum

\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\;:=\;\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)}\,V(x).

(6)

For the Coulomb-type potentials considered in this paper, all
singularities
a_{i}
lie inside
D(R)
, so
V
is bounded below
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))>-\infty
.
Explicitly:
\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))=-Z/R
for the single
Coulomb potential on a ball of radius
R
, while for a general
box domain the value in (
6
) is obtained by minimizing
the explicit Coulomb expression over the boundary faces.

The Neumann lower bound below is a multidimensional Coulomb
specialisation of the classical Dirichlet–Neumann bracketing
principle for self-adjoint operators (see
[
27
, Sec. XIII.15]
),
in which the criterion takes the explicit

exterior-potential minimum
form (
7
). An
analogous Neumann-truncation lower bound under explicit checkable
conditions has been used in the one-dimensional Sturm–Liouville
literature for turning-point-type problems. The following lemma gives
the corresponding lower eigenvalue bound for the present setting.

Lemma 1

(Neumann truncation lower bound)
.

Assume that
H=-\Delta+V
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
has at least
k
discrete
eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}
below

\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(H)
, with orthonormal eigenfunctions

\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k}
. If the confinement condition

\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\;>\;\lambda_{k}

(7)

holds, then

\lambda_{k}^{R,N}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}.

(8)

Proof.

Let
U_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k}\}
and define the
restriction map
T\colon U_{k}\to H^{1}(D(R))
,
Tu=u|_{D(R)}
.

Injectivity.
If
u\in U_{k}
satisfies
u|_{D(R)}=0
,
then
u
is supported on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)
, and

\lambda_{k}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\;\geq\;a(u,u)\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)}\!\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}\bigr)\,dx\;\geq\;\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},

which forces
u=0
because
\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))>\lambda_{k}
.
Hence
W:=T(U_{k})
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
H^{1}(D(R))
,
admissible in the Neumann min-max formula.

Rayleigh quotient bound.
For
u\in U_{k}
and

v:=u|_{D(R)}\in W
, splitting

\mathbb{R}^{N}=D(R)\sqcup(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R))
gives

\displaystyle a_{D(R)}(v,v)

\displaystyle=a(u,u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(R)}\!\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}\bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq a(u,u)-\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\bigl(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-\|v\|_{L^{2}(D(R))}^{2}\bigr).

Writing
u=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\psi_{j}
and using orthonormality,

a(u,u)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}|c_{j}|^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\;.

Since
\lambda_{k}-\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))<0
and

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\geq\|v\|_{L^{2}(D(R))}^{2}
,

a_{D(R)}(v,v)\leq\bigl(\lambda_{k}-\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\bigr)\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(D(R))\|v\|_{L^{2}(D(R))}^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D(R))}^{2}.

Every Rayleigh quotient on
W
is at most
\lambda_{k}
; the
min-max principle yields
\lambda_{k}^{R,N}\leq\lambda_{k}
.
∎

Remark 3

(Why we do not use boundary flux signs)
.

For a single eigenfunction
\psi_{j}
, Green’s identity on
D(R)

gives

a_{D(R)}(\psi_{j},\psi_{j})=\lambda_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D(R))}^{2}+\int_{\partial D(R)}\psi_{j}\,\partial_{\bm{n}}\psi_{j}\,ds.

Thus a nonpositive boundary flux would imply that the Neumann
Rayleigh quotient of
\psi_{j}|_{D(R)}
is at most
\lambda_{j}
.
This observation can be useful in special situations, for example
for explicitly radial ground states.
However, it is not a robust proof of (
8
).
Agmon estimates
[
1
]
give exponential decay, but they
do not by themselves imply a pointwise sign or monotonicity
condition for
\psi_{j}
on large spheres; excited states may have
nodal sets reaching infinity. Moreover, for the
k
-th min–max
argument one needs a Rayleigh quotient bound on every linear
combination in
\mathrm{span}\{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k}\}
,
equivalently a sign condition on the corresponding boundary flux
quadratic form, not merely on the individual eigenfunctions.

Lemma

1
avoids these unverifiable boundary sign
assumptions and replaces them with the explicit, computable
criterion (
7
).

Remark 4
.

The two truncated problems satisfy

\lambda_{k}^{R,N}\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}^{R,D}

(under the confinement condition (
7
)),
providing a bracket whose width
\lambda_{k}^{R,D}-\lambda_{k}^{R,N}

decreases as
R\to\infty
.

2.3
Truncation Error

The gap
\delta_{k}(R):=\lambda_{k}^{R,D}-\lambda_{k}\geq 0

measures the error introduced by replacing
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
D(R)
.
A detailed derivation of explicit upper bounds for
\delta_{k}(R)

is deferred to future work; here we collect the qualitative
picture sufficient to guide the choice of
R
in practice.

Exponential decay of eigenfunctions

By Agmon estimates
[
1
]
, eigenfunctions of
H=-\Delta+V

decay exponentially outside any compact set, provided
V
grows or
stays bounded. More precisely:

•

Confining potential
(
V(x)\to+\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
):
the
k
-th eigenfunction satisfies

u_{k}(x)=O(e^{-\alpha_{k}|x|})
for some
\alpha_{k}>0
depending
on
\lambda_{k}
and the growth rate of
V
.

•

Attractive Coulomb
(
V=-Z/|x|
,
\lambda_{k}<0
):
eigenfunctions decay with rate
\alpha_{k}=\sqrt{|\lambda_{k}|}

(known exactly for the hydrogen atom
[
27
]
).

•

Two-centered Coulomb
(
V=-Z_{1}/|x-a_{1}|-Z_{2}/|x-a_{2}|
):
the same Agmon theory applies with
\alpha_{k}=\sqrt{|\lambda_{k}|}
,
since
V(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
and
\lambda_{k}<0
.

Consequence for
\delta_{k}(R)

The exponential decay of
u_{k}
implies that
\delta_{k}(R)
is
exponentially small in
R
: schematically,

\delta_{k}(R)\sim e^{-2\alpha_{k}R}
as
R\to\infty
.
In practice,
R
is chosen large enough that
\delta_{k}(R)
is
negligible compared to the CECR approximation gap
U_{k}-L_{k}
.

Remark 5
.

An explicit, computable upper bound for
\delta_{k}(R)
—taking into
account the constants in the Agmon estimate—is an important
complement to the CECR framework developed here, and is left
as a topic for a companion paper.

2.4
Two-Sided Truncation Sandwich

The Dirichlet and Neumann truncated eigenvalues together provide a
two-sided sandwich for the full-space eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
.

Upper bound via Dirichlet truncation.

By (
5
),
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}^{R,D}
.
A conforming
P_{1}
Galerkin discretisation of the Dirichlet problem

(-\Delta+V)\,u=\lambda\,u\;\text{ on }D(R),\quad u\in H^{1}_{0}(D(R)),

(10)

with bilinear forms

a(u,v):=(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{D(R)}+(Vu,v)_{D(R)}

and
b(u,v):=(u,v)_{D(R)}
,
yields Ritz values
\lambda_{k,h}^{\mathrm{CG}}\geq\lambda_{k}^{R,D}\geq\lambda_{k}
:

\lambda_{k}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}^{R,D}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k,h}^{\mathrm{CG}}.

(11)

Lower bound via Neumann truncation and CECR.

By (
8
),
\lambda_{k}^{R,N}\leq\lambda_{k}
.
The CECR framework of §§
3
–
4

delivers a computable lower bound
L_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}^{R,N}

(via the piecewise-constant approximation
c_{h}\approx V
and
the perturbation lemma of
Section

4
);
combining these gives
L_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}
.

Schematic two-sided bound.

\underbrace{L_{k}}_{\text{CECR lower bound}}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}^{R,N}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}\;\leq\;\lambda_{k}^{R,D}\;\leq\;\underbrace{\lambda_{k,h}^{\mathrm{CG}}}_{\text{Galerkin upper bound}}.

(12)

The truncation gap
\lambda_{k}^{R,D}-\lambda_{k}^{R,N}
is
exponentially small in
R
for large
R

(see §
2.3
) and is treated as
negligible in the numerical experiments of §
6
.

Remark 6

(Boundary conditions in the numerics)
.

The CECR eigenproblem (§§
3
–
4
)
uses the
Neumann
variational space
H^{1}(\Omega)

(natural boundary condition for the nonconforming ECR element;
no face-DOFs on
\partial\Omega
are eliminated), giving lower
bounds for
\lambda_{k}^{R,N}
and hence for
\lambda_{k}
.
The conforming
P_{1}
eigenproblem uses

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(
Dirichlet
BC), giving upper bounds for

\lambda_{k}^{R,D}
and hence, after noting
\lambda_{k}^{R,D}\geq\lambda_{k}
,
effective upper bounds for
\lambda_{k}
up to the exponentially small
truncation gap.
All CECR tables in §
6
report Neumann-BC computations;
all conforming Galerkin tables report Dirichlet-BC computations.

3
CECR FEM for Piecewise Constant Potentials

Throughout this section
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded polyhedral
domain with a shape-regular triangulation
\mathcal{T}^{h}

(triangles for
N=2
, tetrahedra for
N=3
).
We consider the model eigenvalue problem

a_{h}(u,v)=\mu\,b(u,v)\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(13)

where
c_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{h})
is a piecewise constant
function and

a_{h}(u,v)=(\nabla u,\nabla v)+(c_{h}\,u,v),\qquad b(u,v)=(u,v).

(14)

The eigenvalues of (
13
) are denoted

\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots

3.1
The CECR Space

ECR space

The Enriched Crouzeix–Raviart (ECR) finite element space on

\mathcal{T}^{h}
is

V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}:=\left\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)\oplus\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{K}\}\;\forall K,\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{F}\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket\,\,ds=0\;\forall\text{ interior face }F\end{array}\right\},

(15)

where
\phi_{K}
is the cell bubble on
K
and
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
is the
jump across an interior face.
The degrees of freedom are the face averages and the cell averages.

CECR bilinear form

For piecewise constant
c_{h}
, the CECR discretization of
(
13
) reads: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
such that

(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}+(c_{h}\,\Pi_{0,h}u_{h},\,\Pi_{0,h}v_{h})=\mu_{k,h}\,(u_{h},v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}},

(16)

where
\Pi_{0,h}
is the
L^{2}
-projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{h})

(element average), and
(\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot)_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}

is the broken gradient inner product.

ECR interpolant

The ECR interpolation operator
I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}

is defined by matching face and element moments:

\int_{F}I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v\,\,ds=\int_{F}v\,\,ds\;\forall\text{ face }F,\qquad\int_{K}I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v\,\,dx=\int_{K}v\,\,dx\;\forall K.

(17)

In particular,
v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v
has zero mean on every element
K
.

CECR interpolation constant

For each element
K
, the (dimensionless) ECR interpolation constant

C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)
is the smallest constant such that

\|v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\;\leq\;C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\,h_{K}\,\|\nabla(v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(K).

(18)

Since
v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v
has zero element mean, (
18
)
is a Poincaré-type estimate for the ECR interpolation residual.
The global CECR constant is

C_{h}\;:=\;\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\,h_{K}.

(19)

Proposition 1

(CECR constant via Payne–Weinberger)
.

For any simplex
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N=2,3
),

C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\pi}.

(20)

For
N=2
the sharper bound
C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\leq 0.1490
holds

[
32
]
.
In particular,

C_{h}=\max_{K}C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)h_{K}\leq h_{\max}/\pi
for every
shape-regular mesh.

Proof.

Fix
v\in H^{1}(K)
and set
w:=v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v
.
By the moment-matching definition (
17
),

\int_{K}w\,\,dx=0
, so
w
has zero mean on
K
.
The Payne–Weinberger inequality
[
23
,
3
]

for a convex domain of diameter
h_{K}
gives

\|w\|_{L^{2}(K)}\;\leq\;\frac{h_{K}}{\pi}\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(K)},

i.e.
\|v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\,h_{K}\,\|\nabla(v-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}
.
Taking the supremum over
v
yields
C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\leq 1/\pi
.
∎

Remark 7

(Sharper constants)
.

The Payne–Weinberger bound
C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\leq 1/\pi\approx 0.3183

holds for all zero-mean
H^{1}(K)
functions and is therefore
shape-universal but not sharp for the ECR residual.
For
N=2
, the tighter bound
C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\leq 0.1490
,
proved rigorously in
[
32
]
for the ECR element,
improves the explicit gap by a factor
0.1490\pi\approx 0.47
.
For
N=3
we use the universal bound
C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\leq 1/\pi
.
Applying the Crouzeix–Raviart interpolation estimate also gives a
rigorous but rough bound
[
16
]
. A corresponding rigorous and
sharp bound for the 3D ECR element is not available in the literature
and is left for future work.
In the numerical experiments (
Section

6
) we use

C_{h}^{\mathrm{PW}}=\max_{K}h_{K}/\pi
for all dimensions.

3.2
Pair-Space Lift and the Abstract CECR Explicit-Bound Theorem

Why a pair space?

The CECR bilinear form
a_{h}
in (
16
)
applies the potential
c_{h}
to the cell average

\Pi_{0,h}u_{h}
, not to
u_{h}
itself. This decoupling of the
gradient component (
\nabla u_{h}
) from the reaction component
(
\Pi_{0,h}u_{h}
) is naturally captured by embedding each function

u_{h}
into a pair
(u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h})
in a product space: the
first slot carries the gradient energy, the second the reaction
energy. In this pair space, the CECR projection

\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
becomes
\widehat{a}
-orthogonal, which is the
engine behind all subsequent lower-bound theorems.

The sign-definite bound (
30
) of

Theorem

1
and the convergent elementwise bound
(
46
) of
Theorem

2
below are
both instances of a single abstract theorem. We record the common
machinery here, following the pair-space construction of

[
17
, §4.1.3]
and the max-min argument of

[
17
, §3.1]
; the presentation is self-contained.

Pair space

On the Cartesian product
L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
define

\widehat{a}\bigl((u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\bigr):=(\nabla u_{1},\nabla v_{1})_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}+(c_{h}u_{2},v_{2}),\quad\widehat{b}\bigl((u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\bigr):=(u_{1},v_{1}).

(21)

Let

\widehat{V}:=\{(u,u):u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\},\qquad\widehat{V}_{h}:=\{(u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h}):u_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}\},

(22)

and
\widehat{V}(h):=\widehat{V}+\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Lemma 2

(Diagonal isometry)
.

For every
u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\widehat{u}:=(u,u)
,

\widehat{v}:=(v,v)
,

\widehat{a}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})\;=\;a_{h}(u,v),\qquad\widehat{b}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})\;=\;(u,v).

(23)

The linear map
u\mapsto(u,u)
is therefore an isometric bijection

H^{1}(\Omega)\to\widehat{V}
in the
(\widehat{a},\widehat{b})
-inner products
(when
c_{h}\geq 0
,
\widehat{a}
defines a norm; for sign-changing
c_{h}

it is a quadratic form used only in the isometry sense),
and the pair-space eigenvalue problem

\widehat{a}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})\;=\;\widehat{\mu}\,\widehat{b}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})\qquad\forall\,\widehat{v}\in\widehat{V}

(24)

has spectrum
\{\widehat{\mu}_{k}\}=\{\mu_{k}\}
, where
\{\mu_{k}\}
are
the eigenvalues of the original problem (
13
).

Proof.

For
u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}=(\nabla u,\nabla v)
because
u,v
carry no jumps across interior
faces, so
\widehat{a}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})=(\nabla u,\nabla v)+(c_{h}u,v)=a_{h}(u,v)
. The
\widehat{b}
-identity
is immediate. Bijectivity gives the eigenvalue correspondence.
∎

Pair-space interpolant

Define

\widehat{\Pi}_{h}:\widehat{V}(h)\to\widehat{V}_{h},\qquad\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(u_{1},u_{2}):=\bigl(I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1},\,\Pi_{0,h}u_{2}\bigr),

(25)

where
I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
is the canonical ECR
interpolant defined in (
17
) below (in the proof of

Theorem

1
). Because
\Pi_{0,h}I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u=\Pi_{0,h}u
, the image of a diagonal pair
(u,u)\in\widehat{V}
is

(I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u,\Pi_{0,h}I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u)\in\widehat{V}_{h}
, so

\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
does map
\widehat{V}\to\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Lemma 3

(
\widehat{a}
-orthogonality)
.

For every
\widehat{u}\in\widehat{V}(h)
and every

\widehat{v}_{h}=(v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}v_{h})\in\widehat{V}_{h}
,

\widehat{a}(\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u},\widehat{v}_{h})\;=\;0.

(26)

Consequently, whenever
\widehat{a}
is positive definite on

\widehat{V}_{h}
, the map
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
is the
\widehat{a}
-orthogonal
projection
\widehat{V}(h)\to\widehat{V}_{h}
, and the Pythagorean identity

\|\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}=\|\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}+\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}

(27)

holds for every
\widehat{u}\in\widehat{V}(h)
.

Proof.

Expanding
\widehat{a}
and writing
\widehat{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
,

\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}=(u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1},\,u_{2}-\Pi_{0,h}u_{2})
, so

\widehat{a}(\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u},\widehat{v}_{h})=(\nabla(u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1}),\nabla v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}+(c_{h}(u_{2}-\Pi_{0,h}u_{2}),\Pi_{0,h}v_{h}).

The first term vanishes by the ECR broken-gradient orthogonality,
derived as follows.
On each element
K
,
\text{div}\;(\nabla v_{h})|_{K}
is constant (since

v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathcal{T}^{h})
locally), so
integration by parts gives

(\nabla(u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1}),\nabla v_{h})_{K}=\int_{\partial K}(u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1})\underbrace{(\nabla v_{h}|_{K}\cdot\bm{n}_{K})}_{\text{const.\ on each face}}\,\mathrm{d}S.

By the definition of
I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
in (
17
), the
face average of
u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1}
vanishes on every face

F\subset\partial K
, and since
\nabla v_{h}|_{K}\cdot\bm{n}_{K}
is
constant on
F
, the integral over
F
is zero.
Summing over all faces of
K
gives

(\nabla(u_{1}-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u_{1}),\nabla v_{h})_{K}=0

for every
K
and every
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
.
For the
second,
c_{h}\Pi_{0,h}v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{h})
is
element-constant, and
\int_{K}(u_{2}-\Pi_{0,h}u_{2})\,\,dx=0
on every
K
,
so the
L^{2}
-pairing vanishes cell by cell. The Pythagorean identity
follows from
\widehat{a}
-orthogonality in the standard way.
∎

Remark 8
.

The assumption
c_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{h})
is essential: a
piecewise-affine
c_{h}
would break the cell-wise cancellation in the
second term above, invalidating the Pythagorean
identity (
27
) and the CECR lower-bound argument.

Admissibility and positivity on pair space

The CECR admissibility hypothesis “
a_{h}
is positive definite on

V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
” transfers to the pair space as follows. For

(u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h})\in\widehat{V}_{h}
,

\widehat{a}((u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h}),(u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h}))=\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}^{\,2}+(c_{h}\Pi_{0,h}u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}u_{h})=a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})
.
Hence
\widehat{a}
is positive definite on
\widehat{V}_{h}
if and only
if
a_{h}
is positive definite on
V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
. Similarly, by

Lemma

2
,
\widehat{a}
is positive definite on

\widehat{V}
if and only if
a_{h}
is positive definite on

H^{1}(\Omega)
. The sign of
c_{h}
never enters.

Lemma 4

(Pair-space CECR residual estimate)
.

Let
c_{h}\geq 0
be piecewise constant. Then for every

\widehat{u}=(u,u)\in\widehat{V}
,

\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{b}}\;\leq\;C_{h}\,\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}},

(28)

where
C_{h}:=\max_{K}C^{\mathrm{ECR}}(K)\,h_{K}
is the global CECR
interpolation constant from (
18
).

Proof.

From (
25
),
\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}=(u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u,\,u-\Pi_{0,h}u)
. Reading off the norms
from (
21
):

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{b}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle=\|u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{h}(u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u)\|^{2}\!+\!\bigl(c_{h}(u-\Pi_{0,h}u),\,u-\Pi_{0,h}u\bigr)\!\geq\!\|\nabla_{h}(u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u)\|^{2},

where the inequality uses
c_{h}\geq 0
. Applying the local CECR
interpolation estimate (
18
) on each
K

and summing,

\|u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u\|^{2}\;=\;\sum_{K}\|u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\,2}\;\leq\;C_{h}^{\,2}\,\|\nabla_{h}(u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u)\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{h}^{\,2}\,\|\widehat{u}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{u}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2},

(29)

which is (
28
).
∎

Theorem 1

(CECR explicit lower bound, Liu
[
16
]
)
.

Let
c_{h}>0
be piecewise constant. Then
\mu_{k,h}>0
for
all
k
, and

\mu_{k}\;\geq\;\frac{\mu_{k,h}}{1+C_{h}^{\,2}\,\mu_{k,h}},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.

(30)

Proof.

Since
c_{h}>0
, the form

a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})=\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}^{\,2}+(c_{h}\Pi_{0,h}v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}v_{h})\geq 0
, with equality implies
v_{h}=0
;
hence
a_{h}
is positive definite on both
H^{1}(\Omega)
and

V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
, so
\mu_{k,h}>0
.
The remaining argument uses only two ingredients:
(i)
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
is an
\widehat{a}
-orthogonal projection
from
\widehat{V}
onto
\widehat{V}_{h}
(so that the Pythagorean
identity (
27
) holds), and (ii) the
residual estimate of
Lemma

4
. Neither
the specific structure of
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
(defined in
(
25
)) nor that of
V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
is used
anywhere else in the derivation. Consequently, once

Lemma

4
is in hand, the argument is
the same, step for step, as the general projection-based
eigenvalue-bound framework of
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
and

[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
. For self-containedness,
we reproduce the proof in
Appendix

A
.
∎

3.3
Sign-Changing Piecewise Constant Potential: Classical Shift Remark

When
c_{h}
takes negative values,
a_{h}
need not be positive definite
and
Theorem

1
does not directly apply. A classical
remedy is to shift by the
L^{\infty}
norm of the negative part:

\gamma_{h}:=\|c_{h}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\max_{K:c_{h}|_{K}<0}|c_{h}|_{K}|.

(31)

Because the CECR mass matrix is unchanged by a constant shift of

c_{h}
, applying
Theorem

1
to

-\Delta+(c_{h}+\gamma_{h})
and un-shifting gives

\mu_{k}\;\geq\;\frac{\mu_{k,h}+\gamma_{h}}{1+(\mu_{k,h}+\gamma_{h})\,C_{h}^{2}}-\gamma_{h}\;=:\;L_{k}^{\mu,\gamma_{h}}.

(32)

Remark 9

(Limitation of the classical
\gamma_{h}
-shift)
.

The estimate (
32
) is sharp when
\gamma_{h}

is mesh-bounded, e.g. for
c_{h}^{-}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
uniformly in

h
. For Coulomb-type singularities, however, the element average
yields
\gamma_{h}\sim h_{K(a)}^{-1}
on
every
shape-regular mesh
(the singular element contributes

|K|^{-1}\int_{K}|x-a|^{-1}\,\,dx\sim h_{K}^{-1}
), so the additive
term
-\gamma_{h}
in (
32
) diverges as
the local diameter
h_{K(a)}
at the singularity tends to zero.
Convergence of
L_{k}^{\mu,\gamma_{h}}
then requires the
combined product
\gamma_{h}\varepsilon_{h}
(controlled by the
perturbation to the continuous potential, see §
4
)
to vanish, a condition that typically fails on uniform meshes and
forces aggressive mesh grading. This motivates the
elementwise
CECR bound developed in
§
3.4
below, which avoids
\gamma_{h}
entirely;
we keep (
32
) only for comparison purposes.

3.4
Convergent CECR Lower Bound via Elementwise Estimates

We now establish a
computable
CECR lower bound for
sign-changing
c_{h}
that bypasses the form-norm constant

\widetilde{C}_{h}
entirely. The key insight is that the negative
reaction error can be bounded elementwise by exploiting the
zero-cell-mean property of
w_{2}=u-\Pi_{0,h}u
via the
Payne–Weinberger inequality on each element. Combined with the
standard form-boundedness of
c_{h}^{-}
for coercivity, this yields
a bound with an explicit, mesh-dependent correction factor
A_{h}\to 0

under refinement, so that the gap closes.

Shifted pair-space forms

For a fixed shift
\sigma>0
, define the
shifted CECR bilinear
form
on
V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
by

a_{h}^{\sigma}(u_{h},v_{h}):=(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}+\bigl((c_{h}{+}\sigma)\,\Pi_{0,h}u_{h},\,\Pi_{0,h}v_{h}\bigr),\qquad u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}.

(33)

The corresponding shifted pair-space form is

\widehat{a}^{\sigma}\bigl((u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\bigr):=(\nabla u_{1},\nabla v_{1})_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}+((c_{h}{+}\sigma)\,u_{2},v_{2}).

(34)

All machinery from §
3.2
—diagonal isometry,

\widehat{a}^{\sigma}
-orthogonality of
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
, Pythagorean
identity—carries over with
c_{h}
replaced by
c_{h}+\sigma
.

Assumption 2

(Form-bounded negative part)
.

The negative part of the potential is form-bounded both at the
continuous level and at the discrete (cell-average) level:

(i)

There exist constants
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
such that

\int_{\Omega}V^{-}(x)\,|v(x)|^{2}\,\,dx\;\leq\;\alpha\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\,2}+\beta\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\,2}\qquad\forall\,v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(35)

(ii)

There exist
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
C_{\epsilon}>0
, independent of

h
, such that

(c_{h}^{-}\,u,u)\;\leq\;\epsilon\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\,\|u\|^{2}\qquad\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(36)

Item (i) underwrites self-adjointness of
H=-\Delta+V
via KLMN and the
form comparison of §
4.3
; item (ii) is the
hypothesis used by the CECR coercivity argument below. We retain both
as separate hypotheses because the inequality

(c_{h}^{-}\,u,u)\leq(V^{-}\,u,u)
holds Jensen-by-Jensen only on
piecewise-constant
u
, so transferring (i) to (ii) for general

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is not direct.

Remark 10

(Validity of
2
for Coulomb-type potentials)
.

For the Coulomb negative part
V^{-}=Z/|x-a|
on
\Omega=D(R)
(single
centre; finite sums of centres extend by adding the right-hand sides):

Continuous bound (i).

The required conclusion on the bounded Lipschitz domain

\Omega=D(R)
is the infinitesimal form bound

\int_{\Omega}\frac{Z|u|^{2}}{|x-a|}\,dx\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall\,\epsilon>0,\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(37)

with
C_{\epsilon}
independent of the mesh. This is the bounded-domain
version of (
35
); it is important that the
L^{2}

term is present. The classical Hardy inequality in
N=3
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|w|^{2}}{|x-a|^{2}}\,dx\leq 4\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

is a full-space (or zero-trace) statement and should not be read as a
gradient-only estimate for general Neumann-domain functions, since
constants would then be counterexamples.

One way to obtain (
37
) in
N=3
is to
extend
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
to
Eu\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, apply the
full-space Hardy inequality to
Eu
, use

|x-a|^{-1}\leq\delta|x-a|^{-2}+(4\delta)^{-1}
, and absorb the
extension constants into
C_{\epsilon}
. In
N=2
, one may instead
choose
p\in(1,2)
, use
|x-a|^{-1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, Hölder’s
inequality, and the bounded-domain Sobolev/Gagliardo–Nirenberg
embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2p/(p-1)}(\Omega)
, followed
by Young’s inequality. This again yields (
37
).

For later use it is convenient to record a direct local proof that gives
the same type of estimate in both
N=2
and
N=3
. After translating the
centre to the origin, a weighted Hardy estimate on a ball gives, for
every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B(\rho))
and
N=2,3
,

\int_{B(\rho)}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\;\leq\;C_{N}\rho\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B(\rho))}^{2},

(38)

where
C_{N}
is dimension dependent. In
N=3
this follows
immediately from the classical Hardy inequality for

|x|^{-2}
. In
N=2
the classical
|x|^{-2}
Hardy inequality
is not available, but (
38
) still holds for the
weaker Coulomb weight
|x|^{-1}
: writing

u(r,\theta)=-\int_{r}^{\rho}\partial_{s}u(s,\theta)\,ds
and applying
Cauchy–Schwarz with the radial weight gives a logarithmic factor
whose integral in
r
is finite and of order
\rho
.

Localising an arbitrary
u\in H^{1}(D(R))
with a radial cutoff

\chi_{\rho}
satisfying
\chi_{\rho}\equiv 1
on
B(\rho/2)
,

\chi_{\rho}\equiv 0
outside
B(\rho)
, and
|\nabla\chi_{\rho}|\leq 2/\rho
,
splitting the integral on
B(\rho/2)
versus
D(R)\setminus B(\rho/2)
,
applying (
38
) to
v=\chi_{\rho}u
on the inner piece, and
bounding
1/|x|\leq 2/\rho
on the outer piece, yields

\int_{D(R)}\frac{Z\,u^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\;\leq\;CZ\rho\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\frac{CZ}{\rho}\,\|u\|^{2}\qquad\forall\,\rho>0,\;u\in H^{1}(D(R)).

(39)

Here and below
C
denotes a dimension- and cutoff-dependent constant
independent of
h
. Choosing
\rho\simeq\epsilon/Z
in
(
39
) recovers (
37
) with,
for example,
C_{\epsilon}=CZ^{2}/\epsilon
. For a multi-centre potential,
the corresponding constants are summed over the centres.

Discrete cell-average bound (ii).

The preceding Hardy–cutoff argument proves the continuous form
bound for
V^{-}
. It does not, by itself, prove
(
36
) for arbitrary cell averages
c_{h}^{-}
and arbitrary
meshes. For the graded, shape-regular meshes used here, however,
one obtains the discrete estimate by comparing the cell averages
locally with the Coulomb weight. On elements in the singular patch,
shape regularity and the assumption that each nucleus is a mesh
vertex give
c_{h}^{-}|_{K}=O(h_{K}^{-1})
and

K\subset B(a,Ch_{K})
; hence

c_{h}^{-}|_{K}\int_{K}u^{2}\;\leq\;C\int_{B(a,Ch_{K})}\frac{u^{2}}{|x-a|}\,dx.

On elements away from the nuclei, the distance-grading condition

h_{K}\lesssim{\rm dist}(K,\{a_{i}\})
implies

c_{h}^{-}|_{K}\leq C\inf_{x\in K^{\ast}}V^{-}(x)
on a uniformly bounded
patch
K^{\ast}
. Summing over the mesh and using the finite overlap
of these patches reduces
(c_{h}^{-}u,u)
to the continuous Coulomb
form, up to a mesh-independent multiplicative constant. Combining
this reduction with (
37
) gives (
36
)
with an
h
-independent, although generally nonsharp, constant.
In the computations we instead use the sharper Rayleigh-quotient
certification described in
Remark

11
and

Appendix

D
.

It is worthy pointing out that the Hardy–cutoff route only certifies the
existence
of an
admissible pair
(\epsilon,C_{\epsilon})
; the resulting

C_{\epsilon}=O(Z^{2}/\epsilon)
is generally far from sharp. A computable
and substantially tighter value is obtained from a Rayleigh-quotient
principle; see
Remark

11
and
Appendix

D
.

Remark 11

(Rigorous and sharp estimation of
C_{\epsilon}
)
.

Let
\eta
be the minimal value of the following Rayleigh quotient for a fixed

\epsilon
:

\eta=\inf_{0\neq u\in H^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}c_{h}^{-}u^{2}\,dx}{\|u\|^{2}}.

(40)

The trial function
u=1
shows that
\eta<0
. By the definition of

\eta
, one has

\int_{\Omega}c_{h}^{-}u^{2}\,dx\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\eta\|u\|^{2}.

Thus the optimal constant is
C_{\epsilon}=-\eta
. The minimization problem
(
40
) has essentially the same structure as the Schrödinger
eigenvalue problem and can therefore be solved rigorously, by the sign-changing
CECR lower-bound theorem, to provide a sharper value of
C_{\epsilon}
than the
one obtained from general inequalities. The resulting computable lower bound
for
\eta
is given in
Appendix

D
.

Lemma 5

(Coercivity of the shifted pair-space form)
.

Under
2
, for every
\sigma>C_{\epsilon}
and every

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\|(u,u)\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}^{\,2}\;\geq\;(1-\epsilon)\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}+(\sigma-C_{\epsilon})\,\|u\|^{2}.

(41)

In particular,

\|\nabla u\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\,\|(u,u)\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}^{\,2}.

(42)

Proof.

Expand and separate the shift:

\displaystyle\|(u,u)\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u\|^{2}+(c_{h}\,u,u)+\sigma\|u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla u\|^{2}-(c_{h}^{-}\,u,u)+\sigma\|u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\|\nabla u\|^{2}+(\sigma-C_{\epsilon})\|u\|^{2}.

∎

Definition 1

(Elementwise reaction constant)
.

Define the
elementwise reaction constant

\Gamma_{h}\;:=\;\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\frac{c_{h}^{-}\big|_{K}\;\cdot\;h_{K}^{\,2}}{\pi^{2}},

(43)

where
c_{h}^{-}|_{K}=\max(-c_{h}|_{K},0)
is the negative part of
c_{h}
on
K
.

Lemma 6

(Elementwise reaction bound for zero-cell-mean functions)
.

For every
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
w_{2}:=u-\Pi_{0,h}u
,

(c_{h}^{-}\,w_{2},w_{2})\;\leq\;\Gamma_{h}\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}.

(44)

Proof.

Since
c_{h}^{-}
is constant on each
K
and

\int_{K}w_{2}\,dx=0
, the Payne–Weinberger inequality gives

\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\,2}\leq(h_{K}/\pi)^{2}\|\nabla w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\,2}=(h_{K}/\pi)^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\,2}

(
\nabla w_{2}=\nabla u
on
K
). Summing:

(c_{h}^{-}\,w_{2},w_{2})=\sum_{K}c_{h}^{-}\big|_{K}\|w_{2}\|_{K}^{\,2}\leq\sum_{K}\frac{c_{h}^{-}|_{K}\,h_{K}^{\,2}}{\pi^{2}}\,\|\nabla u\|_{K}^{\,2}\leq\Gamma_{h}\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}.

∎

Remark 12

(Behaviour of
\Gamma_{h}
for Coulomb potentials)
.

For
V=-1/|x|
on a shape-regular mesh with mesh
function
h_{K}
, the singular element
K_{0}\ni 0
contributes

c_{h}^{-}|_{K_{0}}\approx C_{0}/h_{K_{0}}
and

\gamma_{K_{0}}=c_{h}^{-}|_{K_{0}}\,h_{K_{0}}^{\,2}/\pi^{2}\approx C_{0}\,h_{K_{0}}/\pi^{2}
,
which vanishes with the local mesh size. On a uniform mesh
(
h_{K}=h
for all
K
), elements at distance
r\geq h
from the
origin contribute

\gamma_{K}\leq h^{2}/(r\pi^{2})
, with maximum
\gamma\sim h/\pi^{2}
attained
near the singularity, so that
\Gamma_{h}=O(h)
. On a
graded mesh with
h_{K}\propto h\,|x_{K}|^{\theta}
, where
h
is the
mesh-family parameter, the far-field dominates:

\gamma_{K}\sim h_{\max}^{2}|x_{K}|^{2\theta-1}/\pi^{2}

at distance
|x_{K}|
from the singularity, and

\Gamma_{h}=O(h_{\max}^{\,2}/R)
for
\theta>1/2
, where
R

is the domain radius. In either case,
\Gamma_{h}\to 0
as the mesh
is refined,
in sharp contrast to
\gamma_{h}=\|c_{h}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\to\infty
.

Theorem 2

(Convergent CECR lower bound for sign-changing
c_{h}
)
.

Let
c_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}^{h})
be piecewise constant
(sign unrestricted).
Suppose
2
holds with parameters
\epsilon\in(0,1)

and
C_{\epsilon}>0
, and let
\sigma>C_{\epsilon}
be such that

a_{h}^{\sigma}
(see definition in (
33
)) is positive definite on both
H^{1}(\Omega)
and

V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
. Define

A_{h}\;:=\;\frac{\Gamma_{h}}{1-\epsilon}.

(45)

Let
\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}>0
be the
k
-th eigenvalue of the shifted
CECR eigenproblem
a_{h}^{\sigma}(u_{h},v_{h})=\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}(u_{h},v_{h})

on
V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}
(i.e. the
k
-th CECR Ritz value for potential

c_{h}+\sigma
; see §
4.2
), and let

C_{h}
be the CECR interpolation constant from
(
19
). Then

\mu_{k}\;\geq\;\frac{\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}}{(1+A_{h})\bigl(1+C_{h}^{\,2}\,\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}\bigr)}\;-\;\sigma\;=:\;L_{k}^{\mu,\sigma}.

(46)

If a computable upper bound
\widetilde{C}_{h}\geq C_{h}
is available, then
the same lower bound remains valid with
C_{h}
replaced by

\widetilde{C}_{h}
. In particular, one may take the universal
Payne–Weinberger surrogate

\widetilde{C}_{h}=C_{h}^{\mathrm{PW}}:=\max_{K}h_{K}/\pi

from
Proposition

1
. Moreover,

\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}\leq\mu_{k,h}+\sigma
, with equality under
refinement (the difference is
O(\sigma h^{2}\mu_{k,h})
in the
mesh-family parameter); see also Remark
17
.

Proof.

The argument parallels that of
Theorem

1
, but
with a modified pair-space error estimate that keeps track of the
sign-indefinite reaction term.

Step 1: error estimate.

For
(u,u)\in\widehat{V}
, write
w_{1}=u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u
,

w_{2}=u-\Pi_{0,h}u
, and note
\int_{K}w_{1}\,dx=0
on every
K

(by the element-moment matching in (
17
)).
Expanding
\widehat{a}^{\sigma}
as a quadratic form on the error pair
(note:
\widehat{a}^{\sigma}
is not necessarily positive on off-diagonal
pairs
(w_{1},w_{2})
with
w_{1}\neq w_{2}
; we use it here purely as a
bilinear form, not a norm):

\|\nabla w_{1}\|_{\mathcal{T}^{h}}^{\,2}=\widehat{a}^{\sigma}((w_{1},w_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2}))-(c_{h}^{\sigma}\,w_{2},w_{2}).

(47)

Since
\sigma>0
, the negative part satisfies

(c_{h}+\sigma)^{-}\leq c_{h}^{-}
pointwise, so

-(c_{h}^{\sigma}\,w_{2},w_{2})\;\leq\;(c_{h}^{-}\,w_{2},w_{2})\;\leq\;\Gamma_{h}\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}

(48)

by
Lemma

6
, and

\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq(1{-}\epsilon)^{-1}\widehat{a}^{\sigma}((u,u),(u,u))

by
Lemma

5
. Denoting

\alpha:=\widehat{a}^{\sigma}(\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(u,u),\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(u,u))
and

\beta:=\widehat{a}^{\sigma}((w_{1},w_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2}))

(with
\alpha+\beta=\widehat{a}^{\sigma}((u,u),(u,u))
by the
Pythagorean identity (
27
),
which holds since
(u,u)=\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(u,u)+(w_{1},w_{2})
is

\widehat{a}^{\sigma}
-orthogonal by
Lemma

3
), we obtain

\|\nabla w_{1}\|^{2}\;\leq\;\beta+A_{h}(\alpha+\beta)\;=\;(1{+}A_{h})\,\beta+A_{h}\,\alpha.

(49)

Applying the CECR interpolation estimate
(
19
) to

w_{1}=u-I_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}u
,

\|w_{1}\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{h}^{\,2}\,\|\nabla w_{1}\|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{h}^{\,2}\bigl[(1{+}A_{h})\,\beta+A_{h}\,\alpha\bigr].

(50)

Step 2: max-min argument.

Let
E_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{\widehat{u}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}_{k}\}

(
\widehat{a}^{\sigma}
-orthonormal eigenvectors on
\widehat{V}
).
Write
\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi}):=\widehat{b}(\widehat{\phi},\widehat{\phi})/\widehat{a}^{\sigma}(\widehat{\phi},\widehat{\phi})

for the reciprocal Rayleigh quotient, so that

\min_{E_{k}}\widetilde{R}=1/\mu_{k}^{\sigma}

(cf. the max-min identity (
67
) adapted to
a^{\sigma}
).

Case 1.
If
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{\phi}=0
for some

\widehat{\phi}\in E_{k}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\alpha=0
and

\|(u,u)\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}^{\,2}=\beta
. From
(
50
),

\|w_{1}\|^{2}\leq C_{h}^{\,2}(1{+}A_{h})\beta
, so

\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})\leq C_{h}^{\,2}(1{+}A_{h})
.
Since
\min_{E_{k}}\widetilde{R}=1/\mu_{k}^{\sigma}
, we get

\mu_{k}^{\sigma}\geq 1/(C_{h}^{\,2}(1{+}A_{h}))\geq\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}/((1{+}A_{h})(1+C_{h}^{\,2}\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}))

(the last step is elementary), which is
(
46
) after un-shifting.

Case 2.
Otherwise
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
is injective on
E_{k}
,
and
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})\subset\widehat{V}_{h}
is
k
-dimensional.
Using
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})
as a trial subspace in the max-min for

1/\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}
, choose

\widehat{\phi}\in E_{k}
so that

\widehat{v}_{h}:=\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{\phi}
satisfies

\widetilde{R}(\widehat{v}_{h})=\min_{\widehat{z}_{h}\in\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})\setminus\{0\}}\widetilde{R}(\widehat{z}_{h})\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}}.

By homogeneity we scale
\widehat{\phi}
so that

\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}=1
. Then

\alpha+\beta=1
and triangle inequality gives

\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{b}}\;\leq\;\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}+\|\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}.

First term: by the choice of
\widehat{v}_{h}
,

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}\leq(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma})^{-1/2}\sqrt{\alpha}
.
Second term:
\|\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}^{\,2}=\|w_{1}\|^{2}\leq C_{h}^{\,2}[(1{+}A_{h})\beta+A_{h}\alpha]=C_{h}^{\,2}[A_{h}+\beta]
, where the simplification uses

(1{+}A_{h})\beta+A_{h}\alpha=\beta+A_{h}(\alpha{+}\beta)=\beta+A_{h}

together with
\alpha+\beta=1
(the normalisation

\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{a}^{\sigma}}=1
).
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{b}}

\displaystyle\leq(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma})^{-1/2}\sqrt{\alpha}+C_{h}\sqrt{A_{h}+\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma})^{-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}}\;\sqrt{\alpha+A_{h}+\beta}\;=\;\sqrt{(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma})^{-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}}\;\sqrt{1+A_{h}}.

Since
\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})=\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{b}}^{\,2}\leq(1{+}A_{h})((\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma})^{-1}+C_{h}^{\,2})

and
\min_{E_{k}}\widetilde{R}=1/\mu_{k}^{\sigma}\leq\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})
,
inverting gives
(
46
).
∎

Remark 17

(Relation to the full

-mass shift)

\mu_{k}

Let

under refinement

Define the coercivity constant of the shifted averaged form,

\kappa_{\sigma}\;:=\;\min\bigl(1-\epsilon,\;\sigma-C_{\epsilon}\bigr)\;>\;0,

(54)

where
(\epsilon,C_{\epsilon})
are the form-bound parameters of

2
(ii)—the discrete bound on

\sigma>C_{\epsilon}

The

|((V-c_{h})u,u)|\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{h}\,\kappa_{\sigma}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega).

For the Coulomb potentials considered here, this perturbation form

\sup_{\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=1}|((V-c_{h})u,u)|\;\longrightarrow\;0\qquad(h\to 0).

Thus the replacement of

Lemma 7

(Shifted perturbation bound)
.

Under
1
and
2

\varepsilon_{h}\leq 1
(as in
(

\sigma\geq C_{\epsilon}+c_{0}

(56)

No

control of
c_{h}^{-}
(no

) appears on either side.

Proof.

Introduce the shifted continuous form

and define, in one line, the shifted form obtained by replacing

V
with its cellwise averaged lower approximation

:

a_{h}^{\infty}(u,v):=a(u,v)-((V-c_{h})u,v)+\sigma(u,v),\qquad u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(57)

Equivalently,

a_{h}^{\infty}(u,v)=a^{\sigma}(u,v)-((V-c_{h})u,v)=(\nabla u,\nabla v)+(c_{h}u,v)+\sigma(u,v),

(58)

so

so

(59)

with

By the defining property (

Using (

Application of the min-max principle to the shifted eigenvalues

Assumption 3

For

Remark 19

The present framework reduces to

5.1
Problem Formulation

Let

with
|a_{1}-a_{2}|=R_{0}>0
(internuclear
distance) and
Z_{1},Z_{2}>0
(nuclear charges). The two-centered

(63)

In 3D, this models the Born–Oppenheimer Hamiltonian of a diatomic
molecule with fixed nuclei at

the most studied case is

(

The potential (

, and

as

5.2

5.4

For the purely attractive potential (

),

as

and all discrete eigenvalues satisfy

with all constants tracked is left for a companion paper.

6.1
Implementation

Pipeline

For each mesh level, the same pipeline is
executed:

(i)

The piecewise-constant element average
c_{h}|_{K}=|K|^{-1}\!\int_{K}V

is computed by a closed-form per-element polar quadrature for the
2D Coulomb and a Duffy-transform tetrahedral quadrature for the
3D Coulomb, so that
c_{h}|_{K}
is accurate to machine precision
for elements containing a singularity.

(ii)

The conforming
P_{1}
Galerkin matrices for
-\Delta+V
on the
same mesh are assembled using the same high-accuracy quadrature for
the Coulomb entries (with a positivising shift
\sigma_{\mathrm{CG}}
used
only as an eigensolver convenience).

(iii)

The shifted CECR Ritz values
\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}
of

-\Delta+(c_{h}+\sigma)
are obtained by shift-invert Arnoldi
(
eigs
); the reported column
\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}{-}\sigma

satisfies
\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}{-}\sigma\leq\mu_{k,h}
with gap

O(\sigma h^{2})
in the nominal mesh parameter.
The a-posteriori admissibility check
\mu_{1,h}^{\sigma}>0
is read
directly from the smallest Ritz value of the shifted CECR solve;
no additional eigenvalue computation is required.

(iv)

The conforming Galerkin eigenvalues
\lambda_{k,h}^{\mathrm{CG}}

are obtained in the same way from the
P_{1}
system.

Form-bound constants

Two types of estimates for
C_{\epsilon}
are used. The first is a
purely analytic estimate obtained from explicit Hardy, Hölder, and
Sobolev-type inequalities; the concrete constants used here are
recorded in
Appendix

C
. The second is the sharper
certified computation of the optimal form constant described in

Appendix

D
. We keep both in the numerical section: the
analytic constants give a fully explicit baseline, while the optimized
constants show how strongly the final enclosure depends on a sharp
choice of
C_{\epsilon}
and hence on the admissible shift

\sigma>C_{\epsilon}
.

Mesh families

Meshes are generated by distance-graded Delaunay triangulation (2D)
or tetrahedralisation (3D), with all nuclei
a_{i}
enforced as exact
mesh vertices. The 2D computations use the linear grading stated in

Sections

6.2
and
6.3
. The reported
3D computations use a nucleus patch of radius
O(h^{2})
together with
distance-based refinement away from the singularities; the certified
quantities in the tables are evaluated directly from the resulting
meshes, rather than inferred from an asymptotic grading formula.

7
Conclusion

We have developed a pair-space framework for the CECR FEM method on
sign-changing and unbounded potentials, introduced the elementwise
reaction constant
\Gamma_{h}
and a convergent CECR lower-bound
theorem, and combined these with a perturbation lemma into a fully
explicit two-sided eigenvalue bound for Coulomb-type potentials.
The main contributions are:

(i)

A
pair-space CECR framework

(
Section

3
) that lifts the kinetic/reaction split into a
two-component bilinear form with a self-contained max-min proof
of the CECR lower bound for non-negative piecewise-constant
c_{h}

(
Theorem

1
), recovering Liu’s positive-
c_{h}

lower bound
[
16
]
.

(ii)

A
convergent CECR lower bound
via the elementwise
reaction constant
\Gamma_{h}=\max_{K}c_{h}^{-}|_{K}\cdot h_{K}^{\,2}/\pi^{2}

(
Theorem

2
). The key insight is that

\Gamma_{h}\to 0
as the mesh is refined (while

\gamma_{h}\to\infty
), so the gap

(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}{-}\sigma)-L_{k}^{\mu,\sigma}
closes at rate

O(h^{2})
in the mesh-family parameter on graded meshes. This bypasses the
uncomputable form-norm constant

\widetilde{C}_{h}
entirely.

(iii)

A
fixed-shift two-sided theorem

Theorem

3

that combines the CECR lower bound with a perturbation lemma
(
Lemma

7
) for the
V\leftrightarrow c_{h}

substitution, yielding a fully computable enclosure

L_{k}\leq\lambda_{k}\leq U_{k}
without
L^{\infty}
-control of
c_{h}^{-}
.
The perturbation ratio
\varepsilon_{h}
is controlled by the
mesh-local estimates (
71
) and
(
75
); under
3
these reduce to the
graded-rate bound (
60
), namely

O(h^{2}/\kappa_{\sigma})
for both
N=3
and, with the patch radius
chosen as in
Remark

14
,
N=2
.

Open questions and future directions

•

Finer 3D meshes and tighter enclosures.

The 3D explicit gaps
(\mu_{k,h}^{\sigma}{-}\sigma)-L_{k}^{\mu,\sigma}
remain
substantial at the reported mesh sizes because the elementwise
reaction constant
\Gamma_{h}
decreases more slowly on coarse
tetrahedral meshes. Finer graded meshes or enriched trial
spaces would tighten both the CECR lower bound and the
conforming upper bound, narrowing the two-sided enclosure.

•

Interval-arithmetic verification.

The bounds reported in this paper are evaluated in
double-precision floating-point arithmetic and are therefore
subject to rounding and matrix-computation error (rounding
errors
O(10^{-15})
are small versus the reported gaps

O(10^{-2})
, but not accounted for in a verified sense).
Elevating the explicit bounds of this paper to

verified
(IEEE 754-safe) enclosures requires interval
arithmetic (e.g., INTLAB
[
28
]
) applied to all
matrix assembly and eigensolve steps, and is left for future
work.

•

Sharpened ground-state lower bound via the
Lehmann–Goerisch method.

The CECR bounds developed here are
projection-based
,
and their accuracy is ultimately limited by the coarse mesh
size entering
C_{h}^{\mathrm{PW}}
and
\Gamma_{h}
. Once an
explicit (even crude) lower bound

\rho\leq\lambda_{m+1}
is available—for instance,

\rho\leq\lambda_{2}
when
m=1
—classical
eigenvalue-enclosure techniques can, in a second stage,
combine
\rho
with high-quality approximate eigenfunctions
(e.g., from
hp
-FEM) to produce substantially sharper lower
bounds on
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
: Kato’s bound

[
11
]
, Temple’s bound
[
30
]
, the
intermediate-problem method of Weinstein and Stenger

[
31
]
, and the Lehmann–Maehly–Goerisch
method
[
15
,
9
,
5
]
.
As discussed in
[
17
, Ch. 5]
, the
Lehmann–Goerisch bounds are essentially insensitive to the
quality of
\rho
and recover the optimal convergence rate
of the approximate eigenfunction, thereby bypassing the
sub-optimal rate inherent in projection-based lower bounds.
The explicit CECR bounds of the present paper furnish
precisely such a
\rho
for Coulomb-type potentials, enabling
this two-stage strategy; its implementation is pursued in
forthcoming work.

•

Magnetic Hamiltonians and multi-electron systems.

Extension of the CECR framework to
(-i\nabla+A_{h})^{2}+V

(magnetic Hamiltonians) and to multi-electron operators
with antisymmetry constraints are natural next directions.

.

It is convenient to work with the reciprocal Rayleigh quotient

\widetilde{R}(\widehat{v}):=\frac{\widehat{b}(\widehat{v},\widehat{v})}{\widehat{a}(\widehat{v},\widehat{v})},\qquad\widehat{v}\in\widehat{V}(h)\setminus\{0\},

for which the max-min principle on
\widehat{V}
and on
\widehat{V}_{h}

reads

\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\subset\widehat{V}\\
\dim H=k\end{subarray}}\min_{\widehat{v}\in H}\widetilde{R}(\widehat{v}),\qquad\frac{1}{\mu_{k,h}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\subset\widehat{V}_{h}\\
\dim H=k\end{subarray}}\min_{\widehat{v}_{h}\in H}\widetilde{R}(\widehat{v}_{h}).

(67)

Let
\{\widehat{u}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and

\{\widehat{u}_{i,h}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be
\widehat{a}
-orthonormal
eigenfunctions of (
24
) on
\widehat{V}
and
\widehat{V}_{h}

respectively, and set
E_{k}:=\operatorname{span}\{\widehat{u}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
.

Case 1.
Suppose there exists

\widehat{\phi}\in E_{k}\setminus\{0\}
with

\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{\phi}=0
. Then

\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{\phi}=\widehat{\phi}
and
(
28
) gives

\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{b}}\leq C_{h}\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{a}}
, whence

\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})\leq C_{h}^{\,2}
. Since

\min_{E_{k}}\widetilde{R}=1/\mu_{k}
by (
67
)
(taking
H=E_{k}
attains the max), we conclude

1/\mu_{k}\leq\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})\leq C_{h}^{\,2}
, i.e.

\mu_{k}\geq 1/C_{h}^{\,2}\geq\mu_{k,h}/(1+C_{h}^{\,2}\mu_{k,h})

(the last inequality is elementary), which is (
30
).

Case 2.
Otherwise
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}
is injective on
E_{k}
, so

\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})\subset\widehat{V}_{h}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace.
Using
\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})
as a trial subspace in the max-min for

1/\mu_{k,h}
in (
67
),

\frac{1}{\mu_{k,h}}\;\geq\;\min_{\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\Pi}_{h}(E_{k})\setminus\{0\}}\widetilde{R}(\widehat{v}_{h}).

Let
\widehat{\phi}\in E_{k}
be a minimiser, so that with

\widehat{v}_{h}:=\widehat{\Pi}_{h}\widehat{\phi}
,

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}\;\leq\;\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1/2}\,\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{a}}.

(68)

Combining the triangle inequality in the
\widehat{b}
-norm,
estimate (
68
), the error estimate
(
28
), and Cauchy–Schwarz in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

with weights
(\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1/2},C_{h})
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{b}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}+\|\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{b}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1/2}\,\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{a}}+C_{h}\,\|\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{a}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}}\;\sqrt{\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}+\|\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{a}}^{\,2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}}\;\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\widehat{a}},

where the last equality uses the Pythagorean identity
(
27
). Hence

\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})\leq\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}
, and since

\widehat{\phi}\in E_{k}
the max-min principle gives

1/\mu_{k}=\min_{E_{k}}\widetilde{R}\leq\widetilde{R}(\widehat{\phi})\leq\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}
. Inverting,

\mu_{k}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{\mu_{k,h}^{\,-1}+C_{h}^{\,2}}\;=\;\frac{\mu_{k,h}}{1+C_{h}^{\,2}\,\mu_{k,h}}.

∎

Appendix B
Graded-mesh estimate for
\varepsilon_{h}

We record here the graded-mesh perturbation estimates used in the main text.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a simplicial mesh of
Q
, let
h
be the mesh parameter
used in the grading law, and let
c_{h}
denote the elementwise-average
projection of
V
:

c_{h}|_{K}=V_{K}:=|K|^{-1}\int_{K}V(x)\,dx.

Throughout this appendix,
\omega_{0}
denotes the singular patch, that is, the
union of the elements touching the singular point(s).

Key identity.

Since
\int_{K}(V-V_{K})\,dx=0
on every cell
K
,

\displaystyle((V-c_{h})u,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K}\int_{K}(V-V_{K})u^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{K}\int_{K}(V-V_{K})\bigl(u^{2}-(u^{2})_{K}\bigr)\,dx,

(69)

where
(u^{2})_{K}:=|K|^{-1}\int_{K}u^{2}\,dx
.

In this appendix we estimate an admissible mesh-dependent perturbation
constant
d_{h}
defined by

|((V-c_{h})u,u)|\leq d_{h}\|u\|_{H^{1}(Q)}^{2}\qquad\forall\,u\in H^{1}(Q).

(70)

Theorem 4

(Direct patch/off-patch estimate,
N=3
)
.

Let
N=3
, let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the auxiliary domain, and let

V(x)=|x|^{-1}
. Then the constant
d_{h}
in (
70
) satisfies

d_{h}\leq\|V-c_{h}\|_{L^{3/2}(\omega_{0})}\,C_{6}^{2}\;+\;\max_{\{K:\,0\notin\overline{K}\}}\frac{h_{K}}{2}\,\|V-V_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}.

(71)

Here
C_{6}
is the embedding constant of

H^{1}(Q)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(Q)
.

Proof.

By (
69
),

|((V-c_{h})u,u)|\leq\sum_{K\subset\omega_{0}}\int_{K}|V-V_{K}|\,u^{2}\,dx\;+\;\sum_{K\not\subset\omega_{0}}\left|\int_{K}(V-V_{K})\bigl(u^{2}-(u^{2})_{K}\bigr)\,dx\right|.

For the singular patch, Hölder’s inequality gives

\sum_{K\subset\omega_{0}}\int_{K}|V-V_{K}|\,u^{2}\,dx\leq\|V-c_{h}\|_{L^{3/2}(\omega_{0})}\|u\|_{L^{6}(\omega_{0})}^{2}\leq\|V-c_{h}\|_{L^{3/2}(\omega_{0})}C_{6}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(Q)}^{2}.

For an off-patch cell
K
, since
K
is convex,

\|u^{2}-(u^{2})_{K}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq\frac{h_{K}}{2}\|\nabla(u^{2})\|_{L^{1}(K)}=h_{K}\int_{K}|u||\nabla u|\,dx.

Hence

\|u^{2}-(u^{2})_{K}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq h_{K}\|u\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq\frac{h_{K}}{2}\bigl(\|u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigr).

Therefore

\left|\int_{K}(V-V_{K})\bigl(u^{2}-(u^{2})_{K}\bigr)\,dx\right|\leq\frac{h_{K}}{2}\|V-V_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\bigl(\|u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigr).

Summing over
K
with
0\notin\overline{K}
yields (
71
).
∎

Remark 23

Remark 24

(A directly computable
L^{6}
bound on rectangular boxes)
.

For a box
Q_{a,b,c}:=[-a,a]\times[-b,b]\times[-c,c]
, a sharper three-dimensional
embedding constant is obtained by combining a pointwise
fundamental-theorem-of-calculus estimate with interpolation.

For smooth
u
on
Q_{a,b,c}
, fix
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in Q_{a,b,c}
. Applying the
fundamental theorem of calculus successively in the three coordinate
directions and averaging over the corresponding interval gives

\displaystyle|u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|\leq{}

\displaystyle(8abc)^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{a,b,c})}+\sqrt{\frac{a}{2bc}}\|u_{x_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{a,b,c})}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{b}{2ac}}\|u_{x_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{a,b,c})}+\sqrt{\frac{c}{2ab}}\|u_{x_{3}}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{a,b,c})}.

Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{a,b,c})}^{2}\leq\frac{1+4(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})}{8abc}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(Q_{a,b,c})}^{2}.

Now interpolate between
L^{\infty}
and
L^{2}
:

\|u\|_{L^{6}(Q_{a,b,c})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{a,b,c})}^{2/3}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{a,b,c})}^{1/3},

so that

\|u\|_{L^{6}(Q_{a,b,c})}\leq\left(\frac{1+4(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})}{8abc}\right)^{1/3}\|u\|_{H^{1}(Q_{a,b,c})}.

This yields

C_{6}(Q_{a,b,c})\leq\left(\frac{1+4(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})}{8abc}\right)^{1/3}.

(82)

The derivation uses
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
only for smooth functions. Since

C^{\infty}(\overline{Q}_{a,b,c})
is dense in
H^{1}(Q_{a,b,c})
and the constant
in (
82
) depends only on the box, the same inequality extends
to every
u\in H^{1}(Q_{a,b,c})
by approximation.

For the two-center three-dimensional computation,

Q_{2}=[-8,8]\times[-6,6]^{2},

and therefore

C_{6}(Q_{2})\leq\left(\frac{545}{2304}\right)^{1/3}\approx 0.6184.

Remark 25

(Embedding constant for a ball)
.

For balls, however, one can obtain a fully explicit estimate by a
radial line-segment extension. Let

B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|<R\}.

We use the sharp Sobolev constant on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\|w\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq K_{3}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\qquad K_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3\pi}}\left(\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{1/3}\approx 0.4273,

(83)

see Talenti
[
29
]
.
For smooth
u
define an extension
Eu
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

Eu(x)=u(x),\quad|x|<R,

Eu(r\theta)=\left(\frac{2R-r}{R}\right)^{2}u((2R-r)\theta),\quad R<r<2R,\quad\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

and
Eu(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2R
. This extension is continuous across

\partial B_{R}
and vanishes at
|x|=2R
. Writing
s=2R-r
in the annulus,
we now estimate its gradient explicitly.

In polar coordinates,

|\nabla f|^{2}=|\partial_{r}f|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f|^{2}.

On the annulus
R<r<2R
, set

w(r,\theta):=Eu(r\theta)=\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{2}u(s\theta),\qquad s=2R-r.

The radial derivative is

\partial_{r}w=-\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{2}u_{r}(s\theta)-\frac{2s}{R^{2}}u(s\theta),

where
u_{r}
denotes the radial derivative of
u
. Hence, for any

\delta>0
,

|\partial_{r}w|^{2}\leq(1+\delta^{-1})\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{4}|u_{r}(s\theta)|^{2}+(1+\delta)\frac{4s^{2}}{R^{4}}|u(s\theta)|^{2}.

After multiplying by the annular Jacobian
r^{2}\,dr\,d\theta
and changing
variables from
r
to
s
, we use

r=2R-s,\qquad 0<s<R,\qquad\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{4}r^{2}\leq s^{2},\qquad\frac{4s^{2}r^{2}}{R^{4}}\leq\frac{16}{R^{2}}s^{2}.

Therefore

\int_{R<|x|<2R}|\partial_{r}Eu|^{2}\,dx\leq(1+\delta^{-1})\int_{B_{R}}|u_{r}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{16(1+\delta)}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx.

(84)

For the tangential part,

\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}w=\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(s\theta).

Thus

r^{-2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}w|^{2}r^{2}\,dr\,d\theta=\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{4}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(s\theta)|^{2}\,dr\,d\theta.

Changing variables again and using
s^{2}/R^{4}\leq s^{-2}
for
0<s<R
gives

\int_{R<|x|<2R}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Eu|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R}}s^{-2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u|^{2}\,dx.

(85)

Adding (
84
) and
(
85
), and then adding the interior
contribution over
B_{R}
, gives

\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\left(2+\delta^{-1}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+\frac{16(1+\delta)}{R^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}.

(86)

Applying the sharp Sobolev inequality (
83
) to
Eu

gives the direct ball embedding

C_{6}(B_{R})\leq K_{3}\max\left\{2+\delta^{-1},\frac{16(1+\delta)}{R^{2}}\right\}^{1/2},\qquad\delta>0.

(87)

Equating the two terms gives the optimal choice

2+\delta_{R}^{-1}=\frac{16(1+\delta_{R})}{R^{2}},

and hence the following rigorous values:

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hline\cr R&\delta_{R}&C_{6}(B_{R})\text{ from }\eqref{eq:C6-ball-polar-delta}\\
\hline\cr 5&2.703&0.658\\
6&4.055&0.642\\
8&7.531&0.624\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Appendix C
Explicit form-bound constants

We record here explicit form-bound constants for the Coulomb potentials used in
the numerical experiments. The form-bound constant
C_{\epsilon}
is defined as
the smallest constant such that

\int_{Q}\frac{u(x)^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\qquad(u\in H^{1}(Q)).

In the two-center cases, the potential is

V(x)=\frac{1}{|x-a_{1}|}+\frac{1}{|x-a_{2}|}.

C.1
Direct spectral estimate in two dimensions

For the square

Q=[-5,5]^{2},

the quantity of direct interest is

C_{\epsilon}^{\sharp}(Q):=\sup_{u\in H^{1}(Q)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{Q}|x|^{-1}u(x)^{2}\,dx-\epsilon\int_{Q}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{Q}u(x)^{2}\,dx}.

Then

\int_{Q}\frac{u(x)^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\leq\epsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}^{\sharp}(Q)\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}.

For
\epsilon=0.5
, the direct finite-dimensional spectral approximation gives

C_{0.5}^{\sharp}([-5,5]^{2})\approx 0.215.

This value is numerically sharp, though not a verified enclosure.

Rigorous one-center square bound.

For the same square, splitting
Q
into an inner disk
B_{\rho}
and the outer
part
Q\setminus B_{\rho}
yields

\int_{Q}\frac{u(x)^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\leq 0.5\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{(3\pi)^{3/4}}{10}\rho^{1/2}+(3\pi)^{3/2}\rho\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}.

Optimizing in
\rho
gives the rigorous explicit bound

C_{0.5}([-5,5]^{2})\leq 10.99.

Rigorous two-center rectangle bound.

Let

Q=[-7,7]\times[-5,5],\qquad a_{1}=(-2,0),\qquad a_{2}=(2,0).

Splitting
Q
into the two half-rectangles
\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
and
\{x_{1}\geq 0\}
,
the far-center contribution is bounded by
1/2
, and the same disk-splitting
argument gives

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|x-a_{1}|}+\frac{1}{|x-a_{2}|}\right)u(x)^{2}\,dx\leq 0.5\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+11.54\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}.

Hence

C_{0.5}^{(2)}([-7,7]\times[-5,5])\leq 11.54.

Appendix D
Rigorous lower bound for the sharp form constant

This appendix gives the computable formula promised in

Remark

11
for the minimization problem
(
40
). Fix the coefficient
\epsilon\in(0,1)
in that
quotient and write
c_{h}^{-}
for the negative part of the cell-average
potential.
The optimal form-bound constant for this fixed
\epsilon
is

C_{\epsilon}^{\rm opt}=-\eta,\qquad\eta=\inf_{0\neq u\in H^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{2}-(c_{h}^{-}u,u)}{\|u\|^{2}}.

Since the trial function
u=1
gives
\eta\leq-(c_{h}^{-},1)/(1,1)<0
whenever

c_{h}^{-}\not\equiv 0
, a rigorous lower bound for
\eta
immediately yields a
rigorous upper bound for
C_{\epsilon}^{\rm opt}
.

Scaled Schrödinger problem

Divide the Rayleigh quotient by
\epsilon
and introduce the sign-changing
piecewise constant potential

q_{h}:=-\frac{c_{h}^{-}}{\epsilon}.

Let
\nu_{1}
be the first eigenvalue of

-\Delta+q_{h}\quad\text{on }H^{1}(\Omega)

with the natural Neumann boundary condition. Then

\eta=\epsilon\,\nu_{1}.

(90)

Thus it is enough to compute a certified lower bound for
\nu_{1}
.

Preliminary coercive shift

Choose a preliminary, possibly
non-sharp
, form bound for
c_{h}^{-}
:

(c_{h}^{-}u,u)\leq\alpha\|\nabla u\|^{2}+C_{\alpha}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(91)

where
0<\alpha<\epsilon
. For instance,
\alpha
and
C_{\alpha}
may
be taken from the Hardy–cutoff/local-domination estimate discussed in

Remark

10
. Then the negative part of
q_{h}
satisfies

(q_{h}^{-}u,u)=\epsilon^{-1}(c_{h}^{-}u,u)\leq\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\epsilon}\|u\|^{2}.

Set

\varepsilon_{*}:=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon},\qquad C_{*}:=\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\epsilon},

and choose a shift
\sigma_{*}>C_{*}
. This makes the shifted form for

-\Delta+q_{h}+\sigma_{*}
coercive.

Discrete shifted problem

Let
\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}
be the first CECR Ritz value of the shifted problem

(\nabla_{h}u_{h},\nabla_{h}v_{h})+((q_{h}+\sigma_{*})\Pi_{0,h}u_{h},\Pi_{0,h}v_{h})=\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}(u_{h},v_{h}),\qquad u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{ECR}}.

(92)

Equivalently, this is the generalized matrix eigenvalue problem

\left(K_{\mathrm{ECR}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}C^{-}_{0,h}+\sigma_{*}M_{0,h}\right)U=\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}M_{\mathrm{ECR}}U,

(93)

where
K_{\mathrm{ECR}}
and
M_{\mathrm{ECR}}
are the CECR stiffness and mass matrices for

(\nabla_{h}\cdot,\nabla_{h}\cdot)
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
, while

M_{0,h}
and
C^{-}_{0,h}
are the projected mass and reaction matrices for

(\Pi_{0,h}\cdot,\Pi_{0,h}\cdot)
and

(c_{h}^{-}\Pi_{0,h}\cdot,\Pi_{0,h}\cdot)
.

Define the elementwise correction factor

\Gamma_{h}^{*}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\frac{h_{K}^{2}\|q_{h}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}}{\pi^{2}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}\frac{h_{K}^{2}\|c_{h}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}}{\pi^{2}},\qquad A_{h}^{*}:=\frac{\Gamma_{h}^{*}}{1-\varepsilon_{*}}.

(94)

Let
\widetilde{C}_{h}\geq C_{h}
be any certified upper bound for the CECR
interpolation constant, for example

\widetilde{C}_{h}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}}h_{K}/\pi.

Certified lower bound for
\eta

Applying
Theorem

2
to the scaled potential
q_{h}
gives

\nu_{1}\geq\underline{\nu}_{1,h}:=\frac{\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}}{(1+A_{h}^{*})\bigl(1+\widetilde{C}_{h}^{\,2}\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}\bigr)}-\sigma_{*}.

(95)

Combining (
90
) and (
95
), the desired
computable lower bound for the minimizer in (
40
) is

\eta\geq\underline{\eta}_{h}:=\epsilon\left[\frac{\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}}{(1+A_{h}^{*})\bigl(1+\widetilde{C}_{h}^{\,2}\nu_{1,h}^{\sigma_{*}}\bigr)}-\sigma_{*}\right].

(96)

Consequently,

C_{\epsilon}^{\rm opt}=-\eta\leq-\underline{\eta}_{h},

(97)

provided
\underline{\eta}_{h}<0
; otherwise the sharper statement is simply that

C_{\epsilon}^{\rm opt}=0
. In computation one therefore uses

C_{\epsilon,h}^{\rm cert}:=\max\{0,-\underline{\eta}_{h}\}

as a rigorous, mesh-computable replacement for the crude constant obtained from
Hardy or Sobolev inequalities.
