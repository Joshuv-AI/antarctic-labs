N the years which have passed since the first publication 
of this book, many important and lasting changes have 
taken place in the conditions and attitudes of mankind. 
The policies of economic materialism which have 
gradually come more and more to dominate the phys- 
ical existence of the race are failing of their own 
inherent weakness. Under the force of these changes 
the human mind turns from the passing triumphs of 
the hour to the more sublime concerns of philosophy 
and mysticism.

~ ie A 
oe

Circumstances beyond mortal control are forcing man 
to a course of reason. The old wisdom is returning to 
the world. Wisdom alone can bestow the security and happiness which all men seek. 
The gods and heroes of the Golden Age, long exiled from the sphere of mortal purposes, 
are being recalled by the common need and re-established upon their ancient thrones. 
The Mystery Religions of antiquity, which flourish only among a matured and chastened 
people, will be refounded in modern society before the close of the present century.

When I published this book in 1928 it appealed to a comparatively limited group of 
students who were sufficiently grounded in the philosophy of life not to be deceived by 
the success manias of the hour. The number of the seriously minded people has greatly 
increased with the mounting proportions of adversity. Undeceived as to the infallibility 
of the boasted knowledge of the worldly wise, thousands of progressive thinkers in every 
land are seeking nobler and more adequate codes of thought and action. |It is my complete conviction that certain outstanding philosophers, seers, and prophets 
of antiquity perceived with clarity the very truths and facts so desperately necessary for 
our present security. For that reason I made the attempt to gather some of the ‘fragments 
of the great wisdom of the past into an Encyclopedic Outline, that it might be readily 
available for modern utilization. Such a digestive treatment seemed to be indicated in 
the trend toward and the general approval of condensed methods of presentation. My 
aim was to condense into one volume of appropriate format the substance of ancient 
thought and speculation concerning the metaphysical truths underlying material existence.

The Encyclopedic Outline of Masonic, Hermetic, Oabbalistic and Rosicrucian Symbolical 
Philosophy has now passed through successive editions and is to be found in most of the 
great libraries of the world. Yet it seems that a great many who are desirous of having the 
book are unable to possess it or to obtain satisfactory access to a copy. It was therefore to 
meet a definite need that the present edition was decided upon.

To produce an inexpensive edition of this book was by no means an easy task owing 
to the size of the work and the numerous illustrations. That we are able to offer you this 
New Edition at an exceedingly low price is due to the generosity and vision of a friend 
who wishes to remain unknown. This friend personally defrayed the entire expense of 
preparing the photo-lithographic plates from which this reduced facsimile is printed.

The present book is identical with the original except that the color plates are repro- 
duced in black and white and the size of the page is reduced about one-third.

In sending forth this new edition it is my sincere hope that it will bring a fuller under- 
standing of the ancient Wisdom Teaching to the truth seekers of this generation.

PRINCE RAGOCZY OF TRANSYLVANIA—COMTE DE ST.-GERMAIN.

The great initiate who termed himself the Comte de St.-Germain must not be confused with the French general of the samé name, for the “‘ Wonderman,"’ as M. de St.-Germain was 
often called, was not a scion of the French family. The theory long -held that he was a Portuguese Jew has now been discarded as untenable. The most reasonable conclusion regard- 
ing his birth is that he was the'legitimate son of Franz-Leopold, Prince Ragoczy of Transylvania; in fact the Comte de St.-Germain appeared in Leipzig in 1777 as Prince Ragoczy. 
He also admitted to Prince Karl of Hesse that he was the son of Prince Ragoczy and that he wasreared and educated by the last Duc de Medici. 
The contradictory nature of the data regarding the Comte de St.-Germain is strikingly evidenced by several chronological inconsistencies. It is generally supposed that this mysterious 
adept was born in 1710, but the Countess v: Gergy declared that she had seen him during that year in Venice and that he appeared to be between forty-five and fifty years of age at that 
time. While the church register at Eckernforde contains a record of his death in 1784, it is known that he was seen upon several occasions subsequent to that date, having attended-a 
Masonic conference in 1785 nnd having been.recognizéd in Venice in 1788. The last historical mention of the Comte de St.-Germain was in 1822, at which time he was presumably on 
the eveo embarking for India. ~

of sense, is true. (4) An opinion contradicted, or not attested by the

the ancient Greek school of philosophy. The ninth century saw the 
rise of the new school of Scholasticism, which sought to reconcile 
philosophy with theology. Representative of the main divisions of 
the Scholastic school were the Eclecticism of John of Salisbury, the 
Mysticism of Bernard of Clairvaux and St. Bonaventura, the Ra- 
tionalism of Peter Abelard, and the pantheistic Mysticism of Meister 
Eckhart. Among the Arabian Aristotelians were Avicenna and Aver- 
roes. The zenith of Scholasticism was reached with the advent of 
Albertus Magnus and his illustrious disciple, St. Thomas Aquinas. 
Thomism (the philosophy of St. Thomas Aquinas, sometimes re- 
ferred to as the Christian Aristotle) sought to reconcile the various 
factions of the Scholastic school. Thomism was basically Aristote-
