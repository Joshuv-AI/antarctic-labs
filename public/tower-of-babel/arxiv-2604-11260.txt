Stochastically perturbed model of cell electropermeabilization
T. Gebäck, O. Misiats, I. Motschan-Armen, I. Pettersson

Abstract
Reversible electropermeabilization, commonly referred to as electroporation, is a transient increase in cell membrane permeability induced by short, high-voltage electric pulses. We present a stochastically perturbed version of a phenomenological electroporation model introduced in the deterministic setting by [ 11 ] . The deterministic model couples the electrostatic equations for the electric potential in the extra- and intracellular domains with a nonlinear evolution law for the transmembrane potential jump, itself coupled to an ordinary differential equation describing the porosity degree of the membrane. To account for various random effects, such as temperature fluctuations or uncerntainty in the applied electric field, we add noise on the cell membrane. We establish the existence and uniqueness of a variational solution to the resulting coupled SPDE-ODE system governing the membrane potential and the degree of porosity, where the stochastic perturbation is multiplicative and degenerate, acting only on the SPDE component of the coupled SPDE–ODE system. Any mixing in the ODE variables is therefore induced indirectly through the nonlinear coupling in the drift. The main technical challenge arises from the nonlinearities, which are neither Lipschitz continuous nor monotone. The result is proved by means of Galerkin method, following the methodology by Liu and Röckner [ 18 ] for treating equations under generalized monotonicity and coercivity conditions. Finally, we present numerical simulations of the solution and its time averages for both additive and multiplicative noise, that provide a numerical indication for existence of invariant measure.



Keywords:

Stochastically perturbed model of electropermeabilization, electroporation, electrical stimulation of biological cells, coupled nonlinear systems of SPDE-ODE, degenerate noise.

Introduction

Electroporation, or more generally electropermeabilization, designates a temporary increase in membrane permeability due to the applied short high-voltage pulses. In recent years, the interest in electropermeabilization has been constantly increasing, and it has become a widely used technique in food science

On the cell level, the electropermeabilization phenomenon can be modelled by a coupled system of electrostatics equations for the electric potential in the intracellular and extracellular regions, satisfying the conditions of continuity of flux and nonlinear dynamic interface conditions on the membrane. One of continuum models proposed by Neu and Krassowska
[

[

suggested a phenomenological electroporation model, where the membrane permeabilization is described

, instead
of adding an electroporation current based on the pore creation as Neu, Krassowska, et
al. did in
[
20
]
. In
[

, the model is formulated for a single cell and consists of electrostatics equations for the electric potential in a bulk domain that are coupled with a nonlinear ordinary differential equation describing the proportion of pores in the membrane. The resulting nonlinear coupled system can be rewritten as an abstract parabolic equation on the cell membrane with a nonlocal operator being a composition of Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps.

In the present work, in order to account for various random effects, such as temperature fluctuations or uncertainty in the applied electric field, we add noise to the cell membrane in the electroporation model presented in
[
11
]
. The resulting system is a coupled system of nonlinear stochastic partial differential equations, which includes one nonlinear evolution equation for the electric potential

3.2
), show that it is also a mild solution, and finally provide numerical simulations of the solution of (
9

This provides an evidence of existence of invariant measure. The next step is to investigate analytically the existence and uniqueness of invariant measure. Our main contribution is the proof of well-posedness for a coupled nonlinear SPDE-ODE system where the stochastic forcing is multiplicative and degenerate, acting only on the SPDE component of the coupled SPDE–ODE system. Any mixing in the ODE variables is therefore induced indirectly through the nonlinear coupling in the drift. The nonlinearity coupling the two equations is in the form

We follow the variational approach by Pardoux introduced in

Deterministic electroporation model on the cell scale

On the cell level, we adopt the model of the electropermeabilization phenomenon presented in

Let

The discontinuous electric potential satisfies the electrostatics equations in the intra- and extracellular medium, and solves the following coupled time-dependent transmission problem:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -{\rm div}(\sigma\nabla u)&=0&\quad&\mbox{in}\,\,(0,T]\times G_{i}\cup G_{e},\\

(2)

where

(3)

In this way, the voltage

Uniqueness

Let
U_{1}=(v_{1},X_{1}),U_{2}=(v_{2},X_{2})
be two solutions to (
10
) with initial values
U_{1}(0)
and
U_{2}(0)
, respectively.

The difference

satisfies

\displaystyle U(t)=U(0)+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{A}(U_{1})-\mathbb{A}(U_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}(B(U_{1})-B(U_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

The Itô formula yields

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|U(0)\|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle(\mathbb{A}(U_{1})-\mathbb{A}(U_{2}))\,,\,(U_{1}-U_{2})\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|b(v_{1})-b(v_{2})\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\Big((U_{1}-U_{2})\,,\,(B(U_{1})-B(U_{2}))\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)_{H}.

Estimating directly the RHS and then applying the Grönwall inequality does not allow to conclude that
U=0
. This is because of the presence of the random factor
(1+\|v_{2}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2})
in the generalized monotonicity of
\mathbb{A}
(see (ii), Lemma
3.3
). This makes it not possible to apply the Grönwall inequality to estimate
\mathbb{E}\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{H}^{2}
. Note that this problem is purely due to the stochasticity and does not appear in the deterministic case, since one does not need to take the expectation before applying the Grönwall inequality. We will overcome this difficulty by combining the Itô formula with the product rule.
Denote

\displaystyle\rho(t)=K(1+\|v_{2}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2})+\ddot{O},

(42)

where the first term comes from the generalized monotonicity condition (ii) in Lemma
3.3
(taken into account that
|X|\leq 1
), and the second one from the Lipschitz condition (
13
).
By the product rule,

\displaystyle e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)\mathrm{d}s}\|U(t)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|U(0)\|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho(r)\mathrm{d}r}\langle(\mathbb{A}(U_{1})-\mathbb{A}(U_{2}))\,,\,(U_{1}-U_{2})\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho(r)\mathrm{d}r}\|b([u_{1}])-b([u_{2}])\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho(r)\mathrm{d}r}\|U(t)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho(r)\mathrm{d}r}\Big((U_{1}-U_{2})\,,\,(B(U_{1})-B(U_{2}))\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)_{H}.

By the generalized monotonicity of
\mathbb{A}
and the Lipschitz continuity of
b(\cdot)
, the sum of the second, third, and the fourth terms in the RHS of the last identity is non-positive, which yields

\displaystyle e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)\mathrm{d}s}\|U(t)\|_{H}^{2}\leq\|U(0)\|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho(r)\mathrm{d}r}\Big((U_{1}-U_{2})\,,\,(B(U_{1})-B(U_{2}))\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)_{H}.

Taking the expectation on both sides and taking

, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)\mathrm{d}s}\|U(t)\|_{H}^{2}\Big]=0.

Since
\int_{0}^{T}\rho(s)\mathrm{d}s<\infty
a.s., we conclude that for all

,
U(t)=0
a.s., and so the solution is pathwise unique.
The proof of Theorem
3.2
is now complete.

(44)

for any

,

. Here

.
Let us consider operator
\mathcal{L}:D(\mathcal{L})\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
, an unbounded symmetric operator with dense domain
D(\mathcal{L})=\{v\in L^{2}(\Gamma):\,\,\mathcal{L}v\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\}
. It is monotone
(\mathcal{L}v,v)\geq 0
,
v\in D(\mathcal{L})
. Let us show that
\mathcal{L}
is
m
-accretive. Namely, let us prove that for every
\lambda>0
,
{\rm Id}+\lambda\mathcal{L}
is bijective from
D(\mathcal{L})
onto
L^{2}(\Gamma)
with
\|({\rm Id}+\lambda\mathcal{L})^{-1}\|_{L(L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq 1
. Clearly,
{\rm Id}+\lambda\mathcal{L}
is injective since
\mathcal{L}
is non-negative. Now, let us fix
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and find
v\in D(\mathcal{L})
such that
({\rm Id}+\lambda\mathcal{L})v=f
. The weak formulation reads

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{G_{i}\cup G_{e}}\sigma\nabla u^{(v)}\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}v\,[\phi]\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\Gamma}f\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}S,\quad\phi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma),

where

, there exists a solution

. Further,
\mathcal{L}v=\frac{1}{\lambda}(f-v)\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, that implies

. Since for

, we have also

. Thus, by the Hille-Yosida theorem (Theorem 7.8 in
[

]
)
\mathcal{L}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on

which we denote

.

Due to the Lipschitz continuity assumption
(B1)
and the estimates obtained in Theorem
3.2
, the variational solution in Theorem
3.2
is also mild (see Proposition G.0.5 in
[

]
).
