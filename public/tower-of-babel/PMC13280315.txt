Ramucirumab induces apoptosis in Caco-2 cells via mitochondrial impairment and caspase activation
Authors: Amal M Al-Dogmi, Khalid Mashay Al-Anazi, Wajhul Qamar, Mohammad Abul Farah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRamucirumab, a monoclonal antibody that targets vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR-2), is primarily known for its ability to inhibit angiogenesis in a variety of cancer types. Its direct cytotoxic mechanisms in colorectal cancer cells, especially those related to mitochondrial dysfunction and reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated apoptosis, are inadequately elucidated. This work aims to examine the involvement of mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and caspase activation in mediating ramucirumab induced cytotoxicity in Caco-2 cells. MTT (3-(4, 5-dimethylthiazolyl-2)-2, 5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) tests were used to measure cell viability and cytotoxicity, respectively by exposing Caco-2 cells to ramucirumab for 24 h. Flow cytometry was used to analyze apoptosis and cell cycle distribution. Hoechst 33342, rhodamine 123 and carboxy-H2DCFDA staining was used to examine nuclear condensation, mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) and intracellular ROS, respectively. Additionally, the expression of the apoptotic markers caspase-3 and caspase-7, and levels of VEGF was quantified. Ramucirumab enhanced the release of LDH, and significantly (p < 0.05) decreased cell viability with increasing concentration and IC50 value was estimated as 780 µg/ ml. A significant (p < 0.05) increase in the apoptotic cell population and cell cycle arrest primarily at the S-phase was observed. Nuclear condensation and fragmentation were observed and mitochondrial membrane potential was found to be decreased, indicating mitochondrial dysfunction. Additionally, oxidative stress (ROS) and VEGF was elevated and cleaved caspase-3 and caspase-7 were significantly upregulated, indicating caspase-mediated apoptosis. In conclusion, by causing cell cycle arrest and inducing apoptosis via mitochondrial malfunction and caspase pathway activation, ramucirumab demonstrates anti-cancer action against Caco-2 cells. These findings provide new perspectives on ramucirumab's wider anti-tumor potential beyond angiogenesis inhibition.Keywords: ramucirumab, Caco-2 cells, anti-angiogenesis, apoptosis, caspase activation

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is still a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide and it is the third most common type of cancer and the second leading cause of cancer death in 2020. Due to resistance to conventional treatments, the development of novel treatment strategies are urgently desired (Ferlay et al. 2020; Sung et al. 2021). A significant contemporary strategy for treating colorectal cancer involves the development of drugs that target the angiogenesis pathway to inhibit the proliferation and spread of cancer cells, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) (Muro et al. 2020; Hansen, et al. 2021). Compensatory resistance mechanisms can restrict the efficacy of anti-angiogenic treatments that target VEGF signaling, such as bevacizumab, despite improved results (Cremolini et al. 2020). Ramucirumab, a monoclonal antibody against VEGF receptor-2 (VEGFR-2), is approved for advanced gastric and non-small cell lung cancers and many other types of cancer (Goel et al. 2016; Debeuckelaere, et al. 2019; Refolo et al. 2020). Despite lacking approval for colorectal cancer, numerous preclinical and experimental research have investigated its potential anti-tumor properties in colorectal cancer models, especially on angiogenesis inhibition and tumor development (Tabernero et al. 2015). However, its direct effects on colorectal cancer cells remain unclear. In contrast to bevacizumab's neutralization of VEGF ligands, ramucirumab's suppression of VEGFR-2 may disrupt both angiogenic and tumor-intrinsic survival pathways, offering a double therapeutic advantage (Spratlin et al. 2010).

Normal cells have a balance between proangiogenic and anti-angiogenic factors; if this balance is disrupted for any reason, cancer develops (Colombo and Porretto 2023; Jain 2005). A recent study found that VEGFR-2 is expressed on both endothelial and epithelial cancer cells, where it initiates pro-survival signaling cascades such as PI3K/AKT and ERK (Wei et al. 2019). Inhibition of these pathways may result in apoptosis by generating mitochondrial dysfunction, which is characterized by cytochrome c release, caspase activation, and loss of membrane potential (ΔΨm) (Green and Kroemer 2004). Furthermore, the accumulation of ROS, a known consequence of disrupted growth factor signaling that can lead to DNA damage and apoptotic cell death, may exacerbate oxidative stress (Trachootham et al. 2009). Although ramucirumab's anti-angiogenic properties are well established, its capacity to directly cause mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis and ROS-dependent cytotoxicity in colorectal cancer cells has not been fully investigated. Recent data indicates that mitochondrial integrity and oxidative stress are pivotal in modulating cancer cell survival and apoptosis in colorectal cancer (Wu et al. 2024). Therefore, the present work was aimed to examine whether ramucirumab promotes apoptosis in Caco-2 cells via mitochondrial dysfunction, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and caspase-dependent mechanisms, revealing new anti-tumor mechanisms beyond angiogenesis inhibition.

The direct anticancer effects of ramucirumab were evaluated in this study using Caco-2 cells, a reliable model for colorectal cancer. By combining viability (MTT), cytotoxicity (LDH), and apoptosis assays (flow cytometry, caspase-3/7 activation), we demonstrated that ramucirumab suppresses proliferation and induces apoptotic cell death. As shown by Hoechst 33324 staining, we mechanistically link these effects to nuclear fragmentation, ROS overproduction, and mitochondrial depolarization. Our findings indicate that ramucirumab may have supplementary cellular effects beyond angiogenesis suppression in an in vitro colorectal cancer cell model; nevertheless, these results are confined to a single cell line and necessitate further validation in clinical samples and in vivo systems.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The colon carcinoma cells Caco-2, obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA). The cells were cultured in a cell culture minimum essential medium (MEM)/DMEM supplemented with 1% penicillin/streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37o C with a supply of 5% CO2. Cells grown as adherent monolayers and exponentially growing cells harvested by trypsinization. The cell viability was assessed using trypan blue staining and quantified using a Bio Rad TC20 automated cell counter. Cells were cultured in 96-well plates, 6-well plates, or flasks based on experimental protocols. The proliferating cells were rinsed with PBS and subjected to trypsinization. The harvested cells were centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C, thereafter diluted with medium to a density of 1 × 105 cells/ml and utilized in the tests.

Cell viability assessment using MTT assay

The MTT assay was utilized to investigate the cytotoxicity of ramucirumab against Caco-2 cells as described by Mosmann, (1983) using a commercial assay kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Caco-2 cells were cultured in 96-well plates (70–80% confluence) and subjected to two-fold successive dilutions (62–2000 µg/mL) of ramucirumab (obtained from Illy-Lilly Co. Indianapolis, USA), for 24 h, alongside an untreated control. Then, the MTT solution (5 mg/mL) was added to each well and further incubated for 3- 4 h. The resultant formazan crystals were solubilized in DMSO, and absorbance was quantified at 550 nm utilizing a microplate reader (BioTek®, Winooski, VA, USA). The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was ascertained using concentration–response analysis of cell viability data derived from the MTT experiment. Absorbance measurements were recorded for each concentration of ramucirumab, and the mean values from independent experiments were computed and shown as percentage cell viability in relation to untreated control cells. A concentration–response curve was created by graphing percentage cell viability versus the drug concentration. The IC50 value was determined as the concentration that results in a 50% decrease in cell viability relative to the control group.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity

The LDH leakage out of Caco-2 cells was assessed as described previously (Decker and Lohmann-Matthes 1988), using a commercial kit (Abcam®, Cambridge, UK). Cells were grown at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well in 96-well plates and exposed to specified concentrations (62–2000 µg/mL) of ramucirumab for 24 h. After centrifuging the 96-well plate, 100 µl of supernatant was transferred into fresh 96- well plate, and then the LDH reaction mixture was added to each well in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines. Absorbance was quantified at 450 nm, and LDH release (%) was determined in relation to the control.

Detection of cytomorphological changes

The Caco-2 cells were seeded at a confluence of 70% in a 6- well plate and incubated for overnight. Then the cells were treated with the selected concentrations (125, 250 and 500 µg/ml) of ramucirumab for 24 h at 37 °C. The cells were examined under an inverted microscope (Olympus IX51, Tokyo, Japan) fitted with digital camera, the cytomorphological alterations were compared with those cells in control group.

Measurement of VEGF in cell culture medium

Highly sensitive Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay kit (Molequle-On, New Zealand) utilized for in vitro quantitative assessment of human VEGF in the culture media of ramucirumab treated Caco-2 and control cells (Rodriguez et al. 1998). All reagents and working standards are prepared in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. We calculated the quantity of microwell strips necessary for testing based on the desired sample count and the number of wells needed for blanks and standards. 100 μl of the standard is introduced into the specified well, followed by the addition of samples to each well corresponding to their respective numbers, and then incubated for 90 min at 37 °C with agitation. Each well is aspirated and washed, and the process is repeated three times for a total of four washes. By filling each well with wash solution (350 μl) using a multichannel pipette and then completely removing the liquid at each step. After the last wash, any remaining wash solution is removed by aspiration or filtration and the plate is inverted and wiped with a clean paper towel. Finally, 100 μl of biotin-conjugated anti-VEGF antibody working solution added to each well, and incubated for 60 min at 37 °C with shaking. This step was repeated for washing and 100 μl of Streptavidin-HRP working solution was added to each well, and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Finally, after washing step, 100 μl of substrate solution was added to each well and incubate for 15 min at 37 °C in the dark. 50 μl of Stop solution was added to each well. The plate was gently tapped to ensure complete mixing. The absorbance was read at wavelength of 450 nm using a Synergy microplate reader (BioTek®, Winooski, VA, USA) and then, the VEGF percentages were calculated.

Assessment of intracellular Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)

The intracellular ROS generated in Caco-2 cells were assayed as previously reported (Wang and Joseph 1999), utilizing the cell-permeable fluorescent probe 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (H₂DCFDA), by using a commercial ROS Detection Kit (Molecular Probes, USA). Cells were cultured in 96-well black-bottom plates and subjected to specified concentrations (125, 250, and 500 µg/mL) of ramucirumab for 24 h. Cells were subsequently incubated with 25 µM carboxy-H₂DCFDA for 20 min at 37 °C in the absence of light. Following PBS washing, fluorescence intensity was measured utilizing a microplate reader (excitation: 485 nm; emission: 528 nm). Intracellular ROS levels were quantified via endpoint fluorescence analysis subsequent to carboxy-H2DCFDA labeling. Fluorescence intensity levels were standardized against untreated control cells designated as 100%, and utilized for comparative evaluation of oxidative stress among experimental groups.

Activity assay of caspase 3 and caspase 7

Caspase-3/7 activities in the Caco-2 cells were measured following the earlier reported method by Adamova et al. (2014), with Caspase-Glo 3/7 Assay Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were cultured in 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells/well) and exposed to the specified concentrations of ramucirumab for 24 h. Subsequent to treatment, caspase reagent was introduced and incubated at ambient temperature for one hour. Luminescence was quantified with a microplate reader and reported relative to the control group.

Detection of apoptosis using annexin V-FITC flow cytometry

To investigate early apoptosis, late apoptosis and necrosis induced by ramucirumab by flow cytometry, a commercial kit (FITC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit, BD Pharmingen™, USA) was used following the protocol provided (Rieger et al. 2011). The Caco-2 cells were seeded and then treated with the various concentrations of ramucirumab including untreated control, as mentioned above for 24 h. The cells were incubated with annexin V- FITC/PI for 20 min and incubated in the dark. After that, the cells were analyzed on a flow cytometer (Cytomics FC500, Beckman Coulter, USA), with a total of 10,000 events were recorded for each sample, and the data were analyzed using CXP software (version 2.3). Flow cytometric analysis was conducted via a sequential gating technique. Cellular debris was initially eliminated using forward scatter (FSC) and side scatter (SSC) criteria. Doublets were eliminated by FSC-A versus FSC-H gating to ensure the analysis of single-cell populations. The gated population was subsequently examined for Annexin V–FITC and PI fluorescence. The dot plot analysis was categorized into four quadrants according to fluorescence signals: Annexin V −/PI − denotes viable cells, Annexin V +/PI − indicates early apoptotic cells, Annexin V +/PI + signifies late apoptotic cells, and Annexin V −/PI + represents primary necrotic cells.

Cell cycle distribution analysis by flow cytometry

The cell cycle analysis was performed as described previously (Darzynkiewicz et al. 2017), using a commercial assay kit (BioVision, USA) and executed according to the instructions provided by the manufacturing company. Caco-2 cells were cultured on 6-well plates and exposed to ramucirumab at concentrations of 125, 250, and 500 µg/mL for 24 h. Then, the cells were trypsinized and fixed with chilled ethanol (70%) drop by drop with vortexing and kept for 1 h at 4 °C. After that the cells were stained with a working mixture of propidium iodide (PI) for 30 min in the dark condition. Finally, the cells were analyzed by flow cytometer (Cytomics FC500, Beckman Coulter, USA) with a total of 10.000 events observed for each sample.

Analysis of nuclear morphology by Hoechst 33324 staining

The nuclear morphological alterations in Caco-2 cells that underwent apoptosis were probed by Hoechst 33342 staining method (Crowley et al. 2016). The cells were plated on coverslip-loaded on a six-well plate overnight. Then, the cells were treated with the different concentrations of ramucirumab (125, 250 and 500 µg/ml) for 24 h. After fixing with formaldehyde, the cells were labeled with Hoechst 33342 stain for 10 min. Subsequently, the cells were washed with PBS, and then the nuclear morphology alterations were examined under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus BX41, Tokyo, Japan), equipped with a digital camera and suitable filter, at an excitation wavelength of 346 nm and emission wavelength of 497 nm. Nuclei were specifically assessed for chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and the development of apoptotic bodies. Nuclei that display homogeneous and diffused light blue color represent viable cells, while nuclei that display bright blue color nucleus with condensed and fragmented nuclei represent apoptosis.

For the quantitative analysis of nuclear staining, the Caco-2 cells were grown in black-bottomed 96 well plate and then assayed as mentioned above. The fluorescence intensity signal was measured using the microplate reader (BioTek®, Winooski, VA, USA) at 346 nm for excitation and 497 nm for emission and compared with untreated cells which was set as 100%.

Measurement of mitochondrial membrane potential

The mitochondrial membrane potential in Caco-2 cells was measured (Scaduto and Grotyohann 1999), using Rhodamine 123 probe (Molecular Probes, OR, USA). The cells were seeded on cover slip- loaded 6 well plate and then exposed the selected concentrations of ramucirumab (125, 250 and 500 µg/ml) and incubated for 24 h. The harvested cells were stained with a working solution of Rhodamine 123 for 10 min in the dark. After that, the cells were washed with 1X PBS and then observed under fluorescence microscope (Olympus BX41, Tokyo, Japan) fitted with suitable filter and digital camera. Correspondingly, in the quantitative estimation the cells were grown into black bottomed 96 well plate and then processed as aforementioned. The quantification of the fluorescence intensity was measured in a plate reader (BioTek®, Winooski, VA, USA) at excitation wavelength of 507 nm and emission wavelength of 529 nm, and compared to the control which was assumed to be 100%.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted independently in triplicate. Each experiment consisted of three to six replicates. IC50 values were derived from dose–response curves via nonlinear regression analysis of normalized cell viability data acquired from the MTT experiment. Nonlinear regression fitting was conducted using GraphPad Prism software (version 10.4.2) utilizing the dose–response inhibition model with varied slope values. Quantitative data were evaluated utilizing one-way ANOVA, followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparison post hoc test (GraphPad Prism software, 10.4.2). Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, with significance determined at p < 0.05.

Results

Cell viability by MTT assay

Caco-2 cells were exposed to six different concentrations of ramucirumab ranging from 62–2000 µg/ml (two-fold dilutions) for 24 h. The results of cell viability evaluation using MTT assay were shown in Fig. 1. A general trend of decreasing cell viability with increasing concentrations of ramucirumab was observed (14–68%), but slight variability at intermediate concentrations was noted. The proliferation of Caco-2 cells was reduced to 14% in the lowest concentration of 62 µg/ml while in the highest concentration of 2000 µg/ml, the viability was decreased to 68%. The IC50 value was estimated as 780 µg/ml under the present experimental conditions.

Cytotoxicity of ramucirumab evaluated by MTT assay on Caco-2 cells treated in different concentrations as indicated for 24 h. Data were presented as mean ± SD from three independent experiments. Significant at *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.0001, compared with controls (n = 6)

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) leakage assay

The induction of membrane damage in Caco-2 cells leading to release of LDH in the culture medium was evaluated by LDH leakage assay. Caco2 cells were treated with twofold dilutions of six different concentrations of ramucirumab (62–2000 µg/ml) for 24 h. The results of LDH assay were presented in the Fig. 2, in which highest concentrations (1000 and 2000 µg/ml) showed a significant (p < 0.05) release of LDH in a concentration dependent manner, while the lower concentrations were insignificant. The highest percentage of LDH release of 64% were recorded in the highest concentration used (2000 µg/ml) when compared to untreated controls. However, in the lowest concentrations (62 µg/ml) the increase in LDH release was only 5% which was found to be not significant.

Cytotoxicity in Caco-2 cells measured by LDH release assay after 24 h post treatments with different concentrations of ramucirumab. Data were presented as mean ± SD from three independent experiments. Significant at *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.001, compared with untreated control (n = 6)

Analysis of cytomorphological changes

The cytomorphological alterations Caco-2 cells were observed under inverted microscope and representative images were presented in Fig. 3. The cells were exposed to three sub-lethal concentrations of ramucirumab (125, 250 and 500 µg/ml) for 24 h. The untreated control cells (Fig. 3A) appeared to have a normal cell morphology firmly attached to the surface. In contrast, treatment with ramucirumab revealed explicit cytomorphological changes (Fig. 3B, C, and D). These alterations mainly included cell shrinkage, changes in the cell architecture, cell detachment appeared as rounded cells, and a decline in cell density. In contrast, in 250 and 500 µg/ml concentrations of ramucirumab, VEGF was increased to 310 and 365 pg/ml, respectively. These findings suggest that Caco-2 cells may secrete more VEGF when VEGFR-2 is blocked by ramucirumab.

Discussion

VEGFR-2 is the target of the monoclonal antibody ramucirumab, which is mainly used in cancer treatment to prevent angiogenesis. Ramucirumab interacts with high affinity and specificity to the extracellular ligand-binding epitope of VEGFR-2. This obstructs the binding of VEGF-A to VEGFR-2, resulting in the suppression of VEGF-A signaling, which consequently decreases tumor angiogenesis and proliferation. Ramucirumab possesses the potential to block several activities triggered by VEGF activation of VEGFR-2 (Tabernero et al. 2015; Verdaguer et al. 2016; Goel et al. 2016). However, its direct impacts on colorectal cancer cells, such as Caco-2, are still being studied. More recently, combination treatment options utilizing ramucirumab have been examined using diverse cancer models. Ramucirumab coupled with gold nanoparticles exhibited higher cellular recognition, uptake, and accumulation, leading to increased cytotoxicity in gastric cancer cells (Fan et al. 2021). The combination of sorafenib and ramucirumab exhibited synergistic anticancer effects in hepatocellular carcinoma (HepG2 cells), characterized by enhanced apoptosis, reduced proliferation, and suppression of angiogenesis (Taha et al. 2022). Furthermore, the concomitant administration of ramucirumab and 5-azacytidine (5-Aza) has been documented to augment therapeutic efficacy in hepatocellular carcinoma cells (HuH-7) (Almasoud et al. 2024).

Using a variety of assays, such as MTT, LDH, cell cycle and apoptosis analysis, caspase activation, ROS detection, mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), and Hoechst nuclear staining, this study assesses the effect of ramucirumab on Caco-2 cells. Cell viability and proliferation are reflected in the MTT assay, which quantifies cellular metabolic activity. In Caco-2 cells treated with ramucirumab, a significant fall in absorbance (p < 0.05) indicates a loss in cell viability and an anti-proliferative effect. Similar findings from earlier research on VEGFR-2 inhibitors showed that inhibiting VEGFR-2 signaling decreased the development of cancer cells (Spratlin et al. 2010). The concentration-dependent response observed aligns with findings that higher ramucirumab concentrations enhance growth inhibition in tumor models (Zhu et al. 2013).

By monitoring the release of lactate dehydrogenase following membrane injury, the LDH assay assesses cytotoxicity. Ramucirumab-induced cytotoxicity is indicated by elevated LDH levels in treated cells. Cell death may be the outcome of disturbed survival signaling pathways downstream of VEGFR-2 (Fuchs et al. 2014). The idea that ramucirumab not only prevents proliferation but also encourages cell death is supported by the association between decreased cell viability observed in MTT assay and increased LDH release. Ramucirumab treated Caco-2 cells showed enhanced apoptosis and cell cycle arrest, according to flow cytometry analysis. A significant (p < 0.05) accumulation in the G0/G1 or G2/M phase indicates cell cycle blocking, potentially as a result of VEGFR-2-mediated pro-survival signal suppression (Wilke et al. 2014). Additionally, cell cycle analysis showed an increased sub-G1 population and Annexin V/PI staining confirm apoptosis induction. These findings are consistent with studies showing that VEGFR-2 inhibition triggers apoptotic pathways in cancer cells (Youssoufian et al. 2007).

The elevation of VEGF levels noted after ramucirumab treatment may indicate a compensatory feedback mechanism triggered by the suppression of VEGFR-2 signaling. Inhibiting receptor-mediated signaling can interfere with downstream angiogenic pathways and cellular equilibrium, resulting in the increase of VEGF expression as a biological effort to re-establish angiogenic signaling. Nonetheless, despite increased VEGF levels, angiogenic signaling is suppressed due to receptor inhibition at VEGFR-2, obstructing downstream activation of endothelial proliferation and vessel formation. Comparable elevations in VEGF have been seen in anti-angiogenic therapy contexts and do not signify augmented angiogenesis; instead, they indicate receptor-level pathway blockage (Jayson et al. 2016).

Caspase-3 and −7 are executioner caspases required for apoptosis. The induction of apoptosis by either intrinsic or extrinsic routes is confirmed by increased activity of these caspases in treated cells. By interfering with death receptor signaling or mitochondrial integrity, ramucirumab may trigger caspase cascades (Elmore 2007). This is consistent with findings in other treatments that target VEGFR-2, where caspase-dependent apoptosis is a major mode of action. In cancer, reactive oxygen species contribute to both survival and death. Increased ROS levels in ramucirumab-treated cells show oxidative stress as a contributing factor to apoptosis. Excessive ROS can damage biological components, including DNA and mitochondria, leading to cell death (Trachootham et al. 2009).

The elevation of intracellular ROS during Ramucirumab treatment is likely attributable to mitochondrial malfunction and redox imbalance caused by the suppression of VEGFR-2 signaling. Under normal condition, the activation of VEGFR-2 stimulates pro-survival pathways including PI3K/Akt, which aid in preserving cellular redox equilibrium. Inhibiting this route can compromise antioxidant defenses and increase ROS generation (Karar & Maity 2011; Trachootham et al. 2009). Mitochondria are a principal intracellular generator of ROS, primarily due to electron leakage from the electron transport chain during oxidative phosphorylation. Impairment of mitochondrial function can exacerbate ROS production, establishing a positive feedback loop between oxidative stress and mitochondrial injury. Increased levels of ROS can lead to a decline in mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), promoting the release of pro-apoptotic proteins such cytochrome c and the subsequent activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway (Tait & Green 2010).

The findings in this study indicate that the elevated levels of ROS, alongside mitochondrial depolarization and caspase-3/7 activation, corroborate the theory that ramucirumab triggers death in Caco-2 cells via a ROS-mediated mitochondrial route. The current study does not explicitly differentiate between mitochondrial and non-mitochondrial sources of reactive oxygen species (e.g., NADPH oxidases); however, the significant correlation between ROS production and mitochondrial dysfunction implies that mitochondria are a primary contributor. Subsequent research employing ROS scavengers or mitochondrial-specific probes will be necessary to ascertain the exact origin of ROS. Moreover, the precise sequence and hierarchy of these signaling events cannot be conclusively determined from the existing data. The lack of pathway-specific inhibitors hinders the differentiation between upstream and downstream processes. Future research utilizing targeted inhibitors or genetic methodologies will be essential to clarify the specific signaling cascade responsible for these effects. It is important to acknowledge that DCFDA-based endpoint ROS tests have many methodological constraints, such as vulnerability to photo-oxidation, auto-oxidation, dye leakage, and possible fluorescence interference during washing processes. Consequently, the current findings ought to be regarded as a relative signal of intracellular oxidative stress rather than an absolute quantitative assessment of ROS generation.

ROS produced after ramucirumab exposure to Caco-2 cells may originate from various intracellular sources. Mitochondria are acknowledged as primary sources of cellular ROS, yet other enzymatic systems such as NADPH oxidases, xanthine oxidase, cytochrome P450 enzymes, inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), cyclooxygenases, and endoplasmic reticulum stress pathways can also play substantial roles in oxidative stress during pathological conditions (Liou and Storz 2010; Forrester et al. 2018). The current investigation indicated mitochondrial participation by observed mitochondrial membrane depolarization, elevated ROS production, and the activation of caspase-dependent apoptosis. The precise origin of ROS was not explicitly examined, so the role of non-mitochondrial oxidative processes remains unexcluded. Future evaluation utilizing ROS scavengers, pathway-specific inhibitors, and organelle-targeted probes will be necessary to clarify the exact source of ROS generated by ramucirumab in colorectal cancer cells.

Intrinsic apoptosis is characterized by mitochondrial malfunction, which is indicated by a loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm). Ramucirumab treated cells displayed reduced ΔΨm, suggesting mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening and cytochrome c release, activating caspase-9 and downstream effectors (Green and Kroemer 2004). This method is consistent with the observed caspase-3/7 activation and emphasizes the significance of mitochondrial apoptosis in ramucirumab’s cytotoxicity. Hoechst staining demonstrated nuclear condensation and fragmentation in treated cells, hallmark markers of apoptosis. This morphological alteration is consistent with mitochondrial malfunction and caspase activation, indicating that ramucirumab causes apoptotic cell death as opposed to necrosis (Krysko et al. 2008). The existence of apoptotic bodies further validates the flow cytometry and caspase activity data.

The current data indicate that Ramucirumab induces apoptosis in Caco-2 cells by reducing mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), oxidative stress, and activating executioner caspases. These events activate the intrinsic apoptotic pathway (Elmore 2007; Tait and Green 2010). However, complete mechanistic validation requires the identification of upstream regulatory proteins including Bax and Bcl-2, cytochrome c release, and caspase-9 activation, which were not assessed in this work. Our findings indicate a molecular sequence wherein ramucirumab mediated VEGFR-2 suppression generates oxidative stress, induce mitochondrial malfunction, and activates the caspase dependent intrinsic apoptotic pathway in Caco-2 cells. This study examined downstream apoptotic mechanisms in Caco-2 cells with ramucirumab, a targeted VEGFR-2 inhibitor. However, the study lacks mechanistic controls such non-specific IgG treatment, VEGFR-2 expression validation, and comparison with other VEGFR-2-targeting drugs. The current work provides robust association evidence for this pathway, however, additional research with pathway-specific inhibitors is necessary to verify direct effects.

Conclusions

In this study, the anti-tumor efficacy of ramucirumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting VEGFR-2, was investigated in Caco-2 colorectal cancer cells utilizing various functional assays. Our results consistently show that ramucirumab treatment decreases cell viability, causes cytotoxicity, stops the advancement of the cell cycle, and initiates apoptosis, all of which suggest that it may be a useful therapeutic agent against colorectal cancer. The possible mechanisms involve are inhibition of cell proliferation via VEGFR-2 inhibition, induction of oxidative stress and mitochondrial malfunction and activation of caspase-dependent apoptosis. Although these data indicate potential direct effects beyond the inhibition of angiogenesis, they are presently confined to a singular in vitro model. Additional research employing various colorectal cancer cell lines and in vivo models is necessary to confirm the wider applicability and translational significance of these findings.
