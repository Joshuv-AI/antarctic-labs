MUBs from bent functions
William M. Kantor

Abstract
. This note contains a simple construction of complete sets of MUBs, using bent functions to write the new basis vectors as explicit linear combinations of the standard basis.



1.
Introduction

This note contains a short, elementary construction of complete sets of mutually unbiased bases (MUBs)
in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N},

where
N=p^{n}
for
a prime
p
. This sequel to
[
Ka
]
uses an idea presented there
without the connection to bent functions being noticed.
Preferring brevity we refer to

[
Wo
,
Ka
]
for background and some references concerning complete sets of MUBs.

Our point of view is different from the groups/geometries approaches to MUBs

[
WoF
,
Wo
,
CCKS
,
Ka
]
. However, this has not led to the construction of any new complete sets of MUBs.

2.
Odd characteristic

Using row vectors, let
V=\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}^{n}
with
p
an odd prime, equipped with the usual dot product;
and equip
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N},

N=p^{n}
, with the usual hermitian
inner product
and orthonormal basis
\{e_{a}\mid a\in V\}
.
Orthonormal bases

\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}

are
mutually unbiased bases
(MUBs) if

|(u,v)|=1/\sqrt{N}
for all
u\in\mathfrak{M},v\in\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
.
A
complete set of
MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N}
is a set of
N+1
pairwise mutually unbiased orthonormal bases.

Let
\beth
be a
mubent set of functions

V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}
:
a set of
|V|
functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}
such that the difference of any two is bent.
A defining property of a
bent function

{B\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}}

[
Me
, p. 75]

is that, if
0\neq u\in V
, then
v\mapsto B(v+u)-B(v)
takes every value in
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}

exactly
|V|/p
times.

If
a\in V,

B\in\beth
, let

e_{a,B}:=\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}{\zeta}^{a\cdot v+B(v)}e_{v}
, where
{\zeta}\in\hbox{\Bbb C}
is a primitive
p
th root of 1.
This definition comes from coding theory, finite symplectic geometry
and finite affine planes
[
CCKS
, (5.3)]

(compare
[
WoF
, (14)]
).

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}_{\infty}:=\{e_{a}\mid a\in V\}
and

{\mathfrak{M}}_{B}:=\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a,B}\mid a\in V\}
for
B\in\beth
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\,\beth
is a mubent set of functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}
then

\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\infty}\}\cup\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{B}\mid B\in\beth\}
is a complete set of MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N}.

Proof.
For
a,a^{\prime}\in V
and
B,B^{\prime}\in\beth
let

\alpha:=(e_{a,B},e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})=\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}{\zeta}^{a\cdot v+B(v)-a^{\prime}\cdot v-B^{\prime}(v)}=\sum_{v\in V}{\zeta}^{d\cdot v+\Delta(v)}

with
d:=a-a^{\prime}
and
\Delta:=\!B-B^{\prime}
. Here

{(e_{a,B},e_{a^{\prime},B})=(|V|/p)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}{\zeta}^{j}=0}
when
{a\neq a^{\prime}}
,
and
(e_{a,B},e_{a,B})\!=\!|V|=\!N
.

Let
\Delta\neq 0
, so
\Delta
is bent by hypothesis. Use
u=v^{\prime}-v
in the following calculation:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\vskip 2.0pt|(e_{a,B},e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})|^{2}=\,\alpha\bar{\alpha}&=&\hskip-10.0pt\vskip 2.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in V}{\zeta}^{d\cdot v^{\prime}+\Delta(v^{\prime})-d\cdot v-\Delta(v)}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V}{\zeta}^{d\cdot u}\sum_{v\in V}{\zeta}^{\Delta(v+u)-\Delta(v)}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}{\zeta}^{d\cdot u}{\sum_{v\in V}{\zeta}^{0}}+\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{0\neq u\in V}{\zeta}^{d\cdot u}(|V|/p)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}{\zeta}^{j}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt|V|+0\,=\,N.\end{array}

Then

|(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a,B},\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})|^{2}=\frac{1}{{N^{2}}}N=\frac{1}{{N}},

as required for MUBs.
□

Examples 2.2
.

The simplest examples use
V=\hbox{\Bbb F}_{p^{n}},
the trace map

Tr\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}
, and
\beth:=\{x\mapsto Tr(ax^{2})\mid a\in V\}
.

The preceding calculation slightly generalizes one in
[
Ka
, p. 032204-5]
that used
quadratic functions
vMv^{T}/2
for symmetric
n\times n
matrices
M
.
If
\beth
consists of such quadratic functions then the mubent set condition states that the set of
these matrices
M
is a spread set.
(A
spread set
of
n\times n
matrices over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}

is a set of
p^{n}
such matrices for which all
differences between pairs of these matrices are nonsingular, and such sets produce affine planes

[
De
, p. 220]
.)
Many mubent sets
are closed under addition, but some are not
[
Ka
, Example 3.5(e)]
.

The preceding construction fails when
p=2
because there is then no suitable set
\beth
(the next section avoids this problem by modifying the meaning of “mubent”).
A set of bent functions
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
such that the difference of any two is bent has size at most
2^{n-1}
. (Such a set produces a set of MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb R}^{2^{n}}

(using
{\zeta}=-1
in the above construction), which
has size at most
2^{n-1}+1

[
CCKS
, (3.9)]
.)

3.
Characteristic
2

For 2-power dimensions our construction
of complete sets of MUBs is similar to the preceding one, using both
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
and
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
instead of just
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}

(as in
[
WoF
]
).

We use
V=\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}
,
N=2^{n}
,
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N}
and
\{e_{a}\mid a\in V\}
as before .

Let
\beth
be a
mubent set of functions

V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
:
a set of
|V|
functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
such that the difference of any two is a bent function

V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.
This time our definition of a bent function

B\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
(compare
[
Me
, p. 403]
) is that, if
0\neq u\in V
, then

n(u,0)\!=\!n(u,2)
and
n(u,1)\!=\!n(u,3)
,
where
n(u,k)\!:=\!|\{v\in V\!\mid\!B(v+u)-B(v)\!=\!k\}|
for
k\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.

If
a\in V,

B\in\beth
, let

e_{a,B}:=\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{a\cdot v}i^{B(v)}e_{v}
(compare
[
WoF
, (36)]
).

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}_{\infty}:=\{e_{a}\mid a\in V\}
and

{\mathfrak{M}}_{B}:=\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a,B}\mid a\in V\}
for
B\in\beth
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\beth
is a mubent set of functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
then

\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\infty}\}\cup\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{B}\mid{B\in\beth}\}
is a complete set of MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N}.

Proof.
For
a,a^{\prime}\in V
and
B,B^{\prime}\in\beth
let

\alpha:=(e_{a,B},e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})=\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{a\cdot v-a^{\prime}\cdot v}i^{B(v)-B^{\prime}(v)}=\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{d\cdot v}i^{\Delta(v)}

with
d:=a-a^{\prime}
and
\Delta:=B-B^{\prime}
. Here

(e_{aB},e_{a^{\prime}B})=0
when
a\neq a^{\prime}
and

(e_{aB},e_{aB})=N
.

Let
\Delta\neq 0
. Use
u=v^{\prime}-v
in the following calculation:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\vskip 2.0pt|(e_{a,B},e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})|^{2}\,=\,\alpha\bar{\alpha}&=&\hskip-10.0pt\vskip 2.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in V}(-1)^{d\cdot v^{\prime}-d\cdot v}i^{\Delta(v^{\prime})-\Delta(v)}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V}(-1)^{d\cdot u}\sum_{v\in V}i^{\Delta(v+u)-\Delta(v)}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt\displaystyle\sum_{u=0}(-1)^{d\cdot u}{\sum_{v\in V}i^{0}}+\!\!\sum_{0\neq u\in V}(-1)^{d\cdot u}\sum_{k=0}^{3}n(u,k)i^{k}\\
&=&\hskip-6.0pt|V|+0\,=\,N\end{array}

since
\Delta
is bent
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
, so

n(u,0)i^{0}+n(u,2)i^{2}=0
and
n(u,1)i^{1}+n(u,3)i^{3}=0
for
u\neq 0
.
Then

|(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a,B},\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e_{a^{\prime},B^{\prime}})|^{2}=\frac{1}{{N^{2}}}N=\frac{1}{N},

as required for MUBs.
□

The remainder of this section presents known constructions of complete sets of MUBs using Theorem
3.1
together with quadratic bent functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
and spread sets
[
CCKS
,
BBRV
]
.

If
v\in V=\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}
let
\hat{v}\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}^{n}
have the same coordinates as
v
but with 0 and 1 viewed in
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.
Similarly, any matrix
R
over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
produces a matrix
\hat{R}
over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Any symmetric
n\times n
matrix
M
over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
such that
M
(mod 2)
is nonsingular
produces a quadratic bent function
B_{M}\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
via
B_{M}(v):=\hat{v}M\hat{v}^{T}.

Proof.
Let
B:=B_{M}
. Claim:
B(u+v)\!=\!B(u)+B(v)+2\hat{u}M\hat{v}^{T}
for all

{u,v\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}}
(compare
[
CCKS
, (4.2)]
,
[
Me
, p. 403]
).

If
a,b\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
then, trivially,

\widehat{a+b}=\hat{a}+\hat{b}+2\hat{a}\hat{b}
.
If
u=(a_{i}),v=(b_{i})\in V
let
\hat{u}*\hat{v}:=(\hat{a}_{i}\hat{b}_{i})\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}^{n}
. Then

\alpha:=\widehat{u+v}M\widehat{u+v}^{T}
is

({\hat{u}+\hat{v}+{2\hat{u}*\hat{v}}})M(\hat{u}+\hat{v}+2\hat{u}*\hat{v})^{T}=(\hat{u}+\hat{v})M(\hat{u}+\hat{v})^{T}+(\hat{u}+\hat{v})M(2\hat{u}*\hat{v})^{T}+{(2\hat{u}*\hat{v})M(\hat{u}+\hat{v})^{T}}.

Since
M
is symmetric,

\hat{u}M\hat{v}^{T}=\hat{v}M\hat{u}^{T}
and

(\hat{u}+\hat{v})M(2\hat{u}*\hat{v})^{T}=(2\hat{u}*\hat{v})M(\hat{u}+\hat{v})^{T}.
Then

\alpha=(\hat{u}M\hat{u}^{T}+\hat{v}M\hat{v}^{T}+2\hat{u}M\hat{v}^{T})+0
, proving our claim.

Given
0\neq u\in V
and
k\in\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
we need the number of
v\in V
such that
B(v+u)-B(v)=k
;
that is, such that

2\hat{u}M\hat{v}^{T}=k-B(u).
There are

n(u,k)=n(u,k+2)=0
solutions if
k-B(u)
is 1 or 3, so assume that

k-B(u)=2s
for
s=0
or 1.
Let
M^{\prime}:=M
(mod 2).
If
v\in V
then
2\hat{u}M\hat{v}^{T}=2s
if and only if
uM^{\prime}v^{T}=s
.
Since
M^{\prime}
is nonsingular,
uM^{\prime}\neq 0
and

v\mapsto(uM^{\prime})v^{T}
takes every value in
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
exactly
|V|/2
times.
Then
n(u,k)=n(u,k+2)=|V|/2
, and
B\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
is bent.
□

Proposition 3.3
.

Every spread set
\Sigma
of symmetric
n\times n
matrices over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
produces a mubent set

\{B_{\hat{R}},0\mid R\in\Sigma\backslash\{0\}\}

of quadratic functions
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
and a complete set of MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{2^{n}}\!
.

Proof.
Let
B_{\bullet}\in\Sigma
and replace
\Sigma
by the spread set
\{B-B_{\bullet}\mid B\in\Sigma\}.

Now
0\in\Sigma
, and
\Sigma\backslash\{0\}
consists of
nonsingular symmetric matrices.
If
R
and
S
are distinct members of
\Sigma\backslash\{0\}
then

\hat{R}-\hat{S}
is a symmetric matrix that is nonsingular (mod 2) since
\Sigma
is a spread set.
By the preceding lemma,
B_{\hat{R}-\hat{S}}
is a bent function
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}^{n}\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.
Since
B_{\hat{R}}-B_{\hat{S}}=B_{\hat{R}-\hat{S}}
by definition,

\{B_{\hat{R}},0\mid R\in\Sigma\backslash\{0\}\}
is a mubent set
of quadratic functions, and Theorem
3.1
applies.
□

Examples 3.4
.

View
V
as
\hbox{\Bbb F}_{2^{n}}
, with trace map
Tr\!:\!V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
and nondegenerate symmetric
bilinear form
Tr(xy)
for
x,y\in V
. Choose an orthonormal basis for
V
, so
Tr(xy)=x\cdot y
for
x,y\in V
.
Then a self-adjoint
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{2}
-linear transformation
R
on
V

can be viewed as a symmetric matrix, producing a matrix
\hat{R}
over
\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
.

The set
\{cI\mid c\in V\}
of self-adjoint transformations of
V
is a spread set.
Proposition
3.3
produces an associated
mubent set
\beth:=\{B_{\widehat{cI\hskip 0.5pt}},0\mid c\in V\backslash\{0\}\}
and a complete set of MUBs:

\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{\infty}\}\cup\{{\mathfrak{M}}_{B}\mid{B\in\beth}\}
.

4.
Remarks

Examples
2.2
and
3.4
are the most familiar complete sets of MUBs
[
WoF
]
. Others are in
[
Ka
, Ex. 2.5, 2.7, 3.5, 3.7]
.

For
p>2
all known mubent sets of functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{p}
consist of quadratic functions, except for
sets obtained using
[
CM
]
(see
[
Ka
, Ex. 3.7]
).

All known mubent sets of functions
V\to\hbox{\Bbb Z}_{4}
consist of quadratic functions.

It is not clear how to use
Theorems
2.1
or
3.1

to deal with the unitary equivalence of complete sets of MUBs in
\hbox{\Bbb C}^{N}
.
When
\beth
consists of quadratic functions the
groups and geometries
approach in
[
CCKS
,
Ka
]
uses Heisenberg groups to
turn unitary equivalence into a question about isomorphisms of associated finite affine planes.
There are many known pairwise inequivalent complete sets of MUBs for any

p
and suitable
N
.
When
p>2
the number of known complete sets is
O(N)
, whereas when
p=2
that number
is not bounded above by any polynomial in
N
.
