Densiﬁcation of composite cathodes in all-solid-state Li-ion batteries
via sheet metal hydroforming pressing

Densiﬁcation of composite cathodes in all-solid-state batteries (ASSBs) was performed via sheet-metal-type
hydroforming pressing. The modiﬁed system enables pre-heated, “quasi” isostatic pressing under ﬂuid-free
conditions by applying the pressure thorough a rubber diaphragm. The structures of the composite electrodes
before and after densiﬁcation were examined using synchrotron X-ray computed tomography and compared
with those obtained using other conventional pressing methods. After pressing the composite cathodes at
140 MPa and room temperature, the normalized void ratio, estimated from the ratio of the number of voxels for
carbon/voids to the number of voxels for the active materials, decreased by 50 %. This reduction is equivalent to
that obtained by cold isostatic pressing under the same conditions. For uniaxial pressing, the disappearance of
large spotty-like voids was observed; however, their contribution to densiﬁcation was limited. An increase in the
pressing temperature eﬀectively promoted the densiﬁcation of the composite cathodes. The void ratio was
reduced by a factor of eight when pressing was performed at 150 °C. Owing to its ﬂuid-free nature, the proposed
method could be applied to ASSB manufacturing processes requiring severe dew point control.
Keywords: All solid-state battery, Composite cathode, Hydroforming pressing
[Received May 29, 2026; Accepted July 24, 2026]

1. Introduction
All-solid-state batteries (ASSBs) have been recognized
as high-performance Li-ion batteries (LIBs) suitable for
next-generation electric vehicles (EVs)1–3) as they are expected to provide higher power, a longer driving range,
and higher levels of safety than conventional liquid-type
LIBs.4–11) Over the past ten years, many kinds of structural
analysis were applied to characterize the morphologies of
electrodes in ASSBs and correlated with their performance.12–17) The detailed studies also pointed out inﬂuences
of important factors such as LiNbO3 coating onto the
active matrials18) and existence of voids and interphase in
the composite electrodes.19)
Although numerous eﬀorts have been made to develop
ASSBs with practical performance, a standard manufacturing process has not yet been established. To date, several manufacturing methods have been proposed, including layer transfer20) and the dry coating using extrusion,
ﬁbrillation, and spraying, to form composite cathodes, SE
sheets, and anodes with and without sheet stacking.21–24)
Isostatic pressing is widely adopted for manufacturing
ASSBs.20,25–27) Among them, warm isostatic pressing, that

has been applied to the ﬁnal densiﬁcation process of
ASSBs and has proven to signiﬁcantly improve the electrochemical performance of ASSBs. In addition, the manufacturing of ASSBs is composed of many types of critical
processes, some of which are densiﬁcation processes
performed in the low–middle pressure ranges under dry
room conditions, where the dew point is severely controlled. To exploit the superior nature of isostatic pressing,
hydroforming pressing28–31) was applied to ASSB manufacturing in this study. The hydroforming pressing, which
can generate quasi-isostatic pressure, is known as a forming process enabling creation of complicated shapes by the
utilization of ﬂuid pressure instead of traditional (one
directional) mechanical forces. In this study, a feasibility
study on the densiﬁcation of composite cathodes in ASSBs
was conducted using a sheet-metal-type hydroforming
press having ﬂuid cell presses.28,32) Unlike conventional
wet hydroforming process where water is pressed against
the sheet metal, the present system provides quasi-isostatic
pressing under ﬂuid-free conditions by applying the
pressure thorough a rubber diaphragm. To examine the
eﬀectiveness of this system, the structures of the composite
electrodes before and after densiﬁcation were examined
using X-ray computed tomography (CT) with synchrotron
radiation at SPring-8 (BL16B2, Sunbeam).

2. Experimental procedures
Composite cathodes consisting of LiNbO3 coated
Li(Ni1/3Mn1/3Co1/3)O2 (AM) and argyrodite-type
Li6PS5Cl (SE) were prepared. They were then mixed with
acetylene black (AB) as a conductive agent. The composition of the cathode was 74:23:3 wt %. After adding the
binder and solvent, the slurry was coated on an Al foil and
dried. Finally, the composite cathodes were placed in laminated bags and sealed using a vacuum sealer. The overall
thickness of the as-fabricated composite cathode was
approximately 170 ¯m.
Densiﬁcation was performed using a modiﬁed sheetmetal-type hydroforming press system (Flexform· made
by Quintus Technologies AB) that allows temperatures up
to 270 °C under a pressure of 140 MPa. The generated
pressure was applied through a rubber diaphragm that
separates the pressing zone from the region ﬁlled with the
high-pressure ﬂuid, enabling quasi-isostatic pressing under
ﬂuid-free conditions. The process duration varied between
1 and 30 min. Some experiments were conducted with
process temperatures of 100 and 150 °C. The cathodes
were preheated by a steel tool using induction heating
prior to densiﬁcation.
The structures of the composite cathodes before and
after densiﬁcation were examined by X-ray CT using synchrotron radiation at SPring-8 (SUNBEAM: BL16B2).
The samples for CT measurements were encapsulated in
a glass capillary and purged in an Ar atmosphere. The
capillary diameters were 0.8 mm. The incident X-ray beam
was monochromatic and irradiated the samples at 30 keV.
The sample rotation angle during the measurement was
180°, and the exposure time was set to 4 s. CT data were
detected using an Xsight Micron LC instrument (Rigaku
Innovative Technologies Europe) every 0.2°. The eﬀective
pixel size of the experimental setup was 0.65 ¯m.
Based on the CT values measured separately from the
AM, SE, and voids/carbon, each voxel was classiﬁed into
three categories to analyze the structure of the composite
cathodes. Figure 1 shows the reconstructed CT images
(upper panel) and the corresponding categorized composition images (lower panel) of the composite cathodes
before densiﬁcation. The green area in the categorized

image corresponds to the AMs, and the portion of the
green area is small, indicating that the structure of the asfabricated composite cathodes was coarse. A relatively
large spot-like black area, which corresponds to voids and
carbon-related materials, is clearly observed in the SEs
region (red area). Voids and carbon-related materials cannot be distinguished because of their highly transparent
nature to X-rays. The ratio of the number of voxels for
AM, SE, and void to those for all elements were calculated
and referred to as VAM, VSE, and Vvoids. The normalized
ratio for SE and voids to AM were also deﬁned as VSE/
VAM and Vvoids/VAM and used for discussion.
Some of the composite cathodes were characterized
using cross-sectional secondary electron microscopy with
energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDS). Crosssections were prepared by cryo-focused ion beam milling
to avoid damaging the samples during preparation.

3. Results and discussion
3.1

Figure 2 shows the structures of the composite cathodes densiﬁed at various process temperatures. The reconstructed CT images (upper) and the corresponding categorized composition images (lower) are displayed. All
cathodes were treated by hydroforming pressing under 140
MPa for 10 min. The AM area (green) gradually increased
with temperature. After pressing at room temperature, a
comparison with Fig. 1 shows that the voids/carbon
region (black) was still observable. With increasing process temperature, the reduction in the voids/carbon region
was remarkable.
Figure 3 summarizes the values of Vvoids/VAM calculated for the cathodes at various process temperatures. The
normalized void ratio was reduced by 50 % compared to
the as-prepared case after pressing at room temperature.
When pressed at higher temperatures, they were signiﬁcantly reduced to around 0.1, which was one fourth of that
for room temperature. Argyrodite-type SEs is known to
start softening at approximately 100 °C.33) Evidently, the
quasi-isostatic pressing adopted in this study is eﬀective
for the densiﬁcation of composite cathodes in ASSBs.
Notably, the Vvoids for the elevated cases were almost twice
those of the cathodes treated with WIP at 600 MPa, as
reported in a previous study.26) This diﬀerence originates
from the pressure applied to the cathodes.

3.3

Comparison of hydroforming press with
other processing methods

As described in the Introduction section, the hydroforming press system generates quasi-isostatic pressure as
the ﬂuid cell press conforms to the shape of the target
material on the solid die. Hence, the structures of the densiﬁed composite cathodes were compared to those of the
conventional pressing methods under the same conditions.
Figure 6(a) shows the cross-sectional SEM-EDS images of the as-fabricated cathodes (before pressing), where
AMs, SEs, and voids/carbons are clearly observed. The
AM particles were in isolated positions, as evidenced by
the EDS mapping of Mn, and the distribution of the SEs
marked by Cl mapping was spotty. Figure 6(b) shows the
cross-sectional SEM-EDS images of the cathodes densiﬁed by hydroforming pressing for 1 min at room temperature. The distances between adjacent AM particles decreased after hydroforming pressing. The distribution of
the SEs was similar; however, the coalescence of the SE
was slightly enhanced after densiﬁcation. As shown in
Fig. 6(c), these features were similar in the cathode treated
by conventional cold isostatic pressing (CIP) although
there were morphological diﬀerences in the cross-sections
of the AMs originating from the nature of the AMs
(diﬀerent production batches).
According to the CT analysis, Vvoids for both cathodes
were around 0.4 after pressing. Furthermore, Vvoids for the
cathode with 150 °C treatment reduced to one fourth of the
values at room temperature. This indicates that the present
quasi-isostatic pressing is comparable to CIP and could be
superior if the process temperature is increased to more
than 100 °C.

discussed by comparing the study results with the structure
of composite cathodes densiﬁed by conventional uniaxial

A distinct diﬀerence was observed in the changes in the
normalized SE ratio [Fig. 7(b)]; the VSE/VAM for the uniaxial pressing showed a reasonable gradual decrease with
duration, while hydroforming case had an abrupt drop of
VSE/VAM to approximately 0.8 after 1 min of pressing. On
the contrary, as shown in Fig. 7(c), Vvoids/VAM for the
uniaxial pressing rapidly decreased to approximately 0.1
after 1 min of pressing, and remained low. The reduction in
Vvoids/VAM for the hydroforming press was limited. The
contrasting changes between VSE/VAM and Vvoids/VAM
suggest the disappearance of the large spotty-like voids
before pressing, as shown in Figs. 8(a) and 8(b).
Considering that the AM ratio for the uniaxial case
remained low, the structural change did not originate from
the movement of the AM particles throughout the composite cathodes. The thicknesses of the composite cathodes
treated by uniaxial pressing for 1 and 10 min were approximately 138 and 125 ¯m, respectively. These values are
much larger than the expected thickness of the composite
cathodes (³95 ¯m).26) This indicates that the SEs contained a considerable number of voids that were smaller
than the present voxel resolution. It is reasonable to
assume that the pressure generated by uniaxial pressing
was applied dominantly to the topmost layers of the
cathode elements such as apexes of the AM particles near
the surfaces. This results in the formation of a local network of nearby AM particles. Once the AM particles near
the surface contacted each other and formed denser nearsurface layers, the pressure did not propagate into the
interior of the composite cathodes. Furthermore, the
applied pressure decreased with an increase in the number
of contact points. In contrast, in the (quasi) isostatic
pressing case, both the AM and SE particles moved along
with the pressure to form denser structures because pressure was applied identically to all the surfaces of the
composite cathodes. The thickness of the composite
cathodes was approximately 114 ¯m when they were
treated by hydroforming for 10 min. The voids in the SEs
could promote the densiﬁcation of the composite cathodes
and induce further movement of the AM and SE particles.
The enhanced movement of AM particles should originate

from their isostatic nature during pressing, which eﬀectively enhances densiﬁcation. In the case of the uniaxial
pressing, on the contrary, the densiﬁcation could proceed
dominantly by the uniaxial movement of the AM particles
as they ﬁll the adjacent spotty voids in the composites.
Notably, the variation in the composite cathodes is
strongly inﬂuenced by the nature of the mechanical properties, such as the hardness and size of the AM particles,
and their relation to the SE.

4. Conclusion
A feasibility study was conducted on the densiﬁcation
of composite cathodes for ASSBs via quasi-isostatic
pressing under ﬂuid-free conditions. Increasing the pressing temperature signiﬁcantly increased the density of the
cathodes. After pressing, the normalized void ratio, estimated from the ratio of the number of voxels for carbons/
voids to the number of voxels for the AM particles, was
reduced by a factor of approximately eight when pressing
was performed at 150 °C. The duration had less of an
impact when the hydroforming press was applied for 2 min
or longer. These results suggest that the proposed method
can be applied to ASSB manufacturing processes conducted in a dry-air atmosphere.
