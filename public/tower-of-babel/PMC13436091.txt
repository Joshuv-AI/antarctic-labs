A Platform-Independent Binary Gene-Pair Signature Derived from CRPC-Enriched Single-Cell Transcriptomics for Predicting Recurrence-Free Survival in Prostate Cancer
Authors: Yu He, Boyang Li, Zhaojie Tan, Kuanyao Jiang, Wenyue Ju, Weidong Yu, Meng Zhang, Chaozhao Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrence-free survival (RFS) following radical prostatectomy is a pivotal measure of therapeutic success in prostate cancer (PCa), yet conventional clinicopathological tools offer limited discriminative accuracy. We sought to construct a platform-independent prognostic signature to predict RFS by capturing early molecular traces of advanced disease potential.MethodsSingle-cell RNA sequencing data were analyzed to identify malignant epithelial subclusters and evaluate their compositional changes during the transition to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC). We benchmarked 12 machine learning algorithms and 104 algorithmic combinations to develop a robust binary gene-pair signature in TCGA-PRAD cohort (n = 493) and validated in five external cohorts (n = 694). Downstream analyses included functional enrichment, immune and mutational profiling, drug sensitivity prediction and virtual knockouts.ResultsA 36-gene-pair signature was established, showing robust performance in predicting RFS across five external validation cohorts, with an average C-index of 0.725. Distinct signatures in signaling and metabolic processes were identified between the two risk groups through enrichment analysis. High-risk patients exhibited an immune-inflamed microenvironment with elevated TP53 mutation frequency and greater tumor mutational burden, and shared significant transcriptional similarities with responders to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. These immunotherapy-related findings are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation. Virtual knockout identified CKS2 as a risk-associated candidate gene linked to an androgen-responsive network, suggesting CKS2’s potential role in the molecular reprogramming associated with PCa progression.ConclusionThe 36-gene-pair binary signature provides robust RFS risk stratification. High-risk individuals exhibit transcriptional similarity to reported anti-PD-1 therapy responders, and CKS2 emerges as a prognostic hub warranting validation.Keywords: recurrence-free survival, binary gene-pair signature, single-cell RNA sequencing, machine learning, prostate cancer, CKS2

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) ranks as the second most frequently diagnosed malignancy and the fifth leading cause of cancer death among men, accounting for an estimated 1.47 million new cases and roughly 397,000 deaths in 2022.1 Epidemiological modeling projects that annual global incidence will nearly double to approximately 2.9 million cases by 2040, driven by population aging and expanding PSA-based screening in low- and middle-income countries.2 Radical prostatectomy (RP) continues to be the gold standard for patients seeking curative outcomes in cases of localized PCa; still, approximately 20–40% of patients develop biochemical recurrence within five years after RP, resulting in reduced RFS.3 A key subset of patients with shorter RFS will eventually evolve into lethal castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC), where PSA or radiographic progression occurs despite castrate serum testosterone (<50 ng/dL).4 These epidemiological and clinical realities collectively underscore the urgent need for robust, early-stage prognostic tools which are able to accurately detect individuals with elevated likelihood of recurrence as well as CRPC progression, thereby enabling risk-adapted surveillance and timely therapeutic intervention.

Current postoperative risk stratification relies predominantly on clinicopathological variables, which, while prognostically informative in aggregate, exhibit inherently limited individual discriminative capacity. Clinical guidelines explicitly acknowledge that these standard variables alone are insufficient for precise risk stratification in localized PCa, and PSA and Gleason score individually demonstrate low specificity for predicting recurrence.5,6 To address this gap, numerous transcriptomic prognostic signatures have been developed, including commercially validated classifiers such as Decipher and Prolaris.7,8 However, systematic testing of gene signatures across large independent cohorts has uncovered strong cross-cohort variability in prediction accuracy and these signatures depend on dedicated commercial platforms and proprietary scoring algorithms, restricting accessibility and increasing cost. A fundamental technical barrier is platform dependency: conventional continuous gene expression scores are susceptible to batch effects, normalization artifacts, and systematic differences between RNA sequencing and microarray platforms, collectively restricting cross-platform clinical transferability.9 Binary gene-pair approaches elegantly circumvent this limitation by encoding the comparative expression ordering from gene pairs inside each individual sample. This rank-based strategy has demonstrated robust and reproducible prognostic performance across multiple cancer types, including bladder cancer,10 and colorectal cancer;11 however, its systematic application to post-prostatectomy RFS prediction, particularly within a framework anchored in CRPC-enriched transcriptional biology, has not been investigated.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has revolutionized our perspective on the intricate landscape of intratumoral heterogeneity within both PCa and CRPC. Landmark studies have confirmed that CRPC-like malignant epithelial subpopulations pre-exist within primary PCa specimens and actively promote resistance to androgen deprivation therapy through cell-autonomous transcriptional reprogramming.12 Critically, integrating scRNA-seq-derived, cell-type-specific gene programs with transcriptomic data from large clinical cohorts enables the construction of biologically grounded prognostic signatures that capture clinically relevant molecular heterogeneity-an approach that bridges single-cell resolution with population-level prognostic power.13

In this work, we established and validated a platform-independent signature in 1187 PCa patients based on binary gene pairs derived from CRPC-enriched single-cell transcriptomics for predicting RFS after radical prostatectomy. A nomogram was subsequently constructed to refine individualized RFS risk estimation. Enrichment analysis, immune profiling, somatic mutation analysis, drug sensitivity prediction and virtual knockouts constituted the core of our downstream computational pipeline. Collectively, this study facilitates more tailored therapeutic decision-making, thereby paving the way for enhanced personalized care and superior patient survival.

Materials and Methods

Data Collection and Patient Cohorts

We retrieved scRNA-seq transcriptomics covering benign prostate, primary PCa, and CRPC from Cheng et al (NCBI SRA: PRJNA699369).12 We sourced bulk transcriptomic from six PCa cohorts. TCGA-PRAD and DKFZ were retrieved from cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/). We retrieved GSE21034, GSE70770, and GSE94767 from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo). An in-house cohort from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (AHMU, n=70) served as an external validation cohort. The primary evaluation metric for all study groups was RFS, measured as the latency from surgical intervention to the first evidence of recurrence. In this study, recurrence was primarily defined as biochemical recurrence, characterized by two successive postoperative PSA levels surpassing 0.2 ng/mL.3 For patients who remained event-free, RFS was calculated up to the date of the last follow-up (censored). Collected clinicopathological variables included age at surgery, pathologic T stage, Gleason score, and preoperative PSA (when available). Missing clinical data were handled as follows: in the GSE70770 cohort, age was missing for 90 of 201 patients and was imputed using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE, m=5); in the remaining five cohorts, patients with missing values for any clinical variable used in the corresponding analysis were excluded by complete-case deletion. Subsequently, scRNA-seq data were processed to identify malignant epithelial cells and CRPC-enriched genes (see Supplementary Methods for details).

Construction and Selection of Gene Pairs

We first screened CRPC-enriched candidate genes within TCGA-PRAD dataset employing univariate Cox regression (P < 0.05) to select significant links to RFS. All pairwise combinations of these survival-associated genes were then generated. For each gene pair (Gene A, Gene B) in a sample, we defined a binary indicator: 1 if A’s expression > B’s, otherwise 0. Pairs with the proportion of 0s or 1s between 20% and 80% in TCGA-PRAD were retained. To isolate candidates linked to RFS outcomes, we implemented univariate Cox regression on the aforementioned pairs. After Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction, pairs with adjusted P < 0.05 became the final model inputs. All feature-selection procedures were restricted to the TCGA-PRAD training cohort to avoid information leakage from the validation cohorts.

Machine Learning-Based Prognostic Model Construction

We benchmarked 12 machine learning algorithms across 104 combinatorial configurations in the TCGA-PRAD cohort to build a prognostic signature. The algorithms included Ridge, elastic network (Enet), LASSO, CoxBoost, random survival forest (RSF), gradient boosting machine (GBM), stepwise Cox (StepCox), supervised principal components (SuperPC), partial least squares cox (plsRcox), survival support vector machine (survival-SVM), DeepSurv, and XGBoost. Several of these (CoxBoost, LASSO, RSF, StepCox) can perform feature selection.

Regularized regression models (LASSO, Ridge, Enet) used 10-fold cross-validation with lambda selected at minimum deviance; Enet additionally evaluated nine alpha values (0.1–0.9). StepCox was applied in three directions (both, backward, forward) via AIC (Akaike Information Criterion) -based selection. CoxBoost penalty and optimal boosting steps were both determined by 10-fold cross-validation. RSF hyperparameters were tuned by grid search using the Out-of-bag (OOB) method. GBM and XGBoost optimal iterations were selected by cross-validation with early stopping. SuperPC and plsRcox selected their respective complexity parameters by 10-fold cross-validation. Survival-SVM used a linear kernel. DeepSurv was implemented as a two-hidden-layer network (32–16 nodes) with dropout (rate = 0.2), trained for up to 200 epochs with early stopping (patience = 30) on an internal 80/20 train/validation split. All random processes were seeded at 1.

The five external validation cohorts were used solely for final evaluation and were not used iteratively to guide algorithm selection, thereby avoiding selection bias or information leakage from the validation data. To evaluate model efficacy, the concordance index (C-index) was initially derived from TCGA-PRAD cohort and then averaged across five external validation sets. The optimal configuration maximized the average validation C-index while keeping training performance comparable, thus reducing overfitting. Each patient’s risk score was computed as the linear combination of regression coefficients weighted by binary expression indicators (0 or 1) for each gene pair included in the model; the complete list of 36 gene pairs and their Ridge regression coefficients is provided in Supplementary Table 1. Patients across all study populations were categorized into distinct risk groups based on a cutoff value determined by the median score of the training set. The prognostic accuracy of our model was validated using time-dependent ROC analysis, while survival disparities across cohorts were depicted via Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curves.

Nomogram Construction and Clinical Utility Assessment

Using the rms R package,14 we built a multivariable nomogram in our training cohort that integrated the gene-pair risk score with T stage and Gleason score. This nomogram estimated RFS probability at 12, 36, and 60 months after radical prostatectomy. The consistency between anticipated RFS probability and observed outcomes was verified through calibration plots at each time point, with 1000 bootstrap iterations to correct optimism. Decision curve analysis (DCA),15 then evaluated clinical net benefit across threshold probabilities. C-index comparisons quantified the nomogram’s incremental discriminative value over individual predictors.

Differential Expression and Functional Enrichment Analyses

We applied DESeq2 to compare differential expression between different risk groups in TCGA-PRAD cohort. Gene expression data were obtained from UCSC Xena platform (https://xena.ucsc.edu/) in the form of STAR-aligned read counts, which were stored as log2 (counts + 1) values and back-transformed to raw counts prior to analysis. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) required |log2 fold change| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis was executed through the clusterProfiler R package,16 also at adjusted P < 0.05. We further performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) against hallmark gene set from Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB),17 considering those with adjusted P < 0.05 as significantly enriched.

Tumor Immune Microenvironment

We systematically profiled the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) composition in TCGA-PRAD bulk transcriptomic data using multiple complementary algorithms. Cross-algorithm validation of immune cell infiltration was achieved via CIBERSORT,18 EPIC, MCPcounter, xCell, and quanTIseq. Meanwhile, ssGSEA assessed relative abundance of 28 immune cell types.19 Stromal score, immune score, ESTIMATE score, and tumor purity were computed via the ESTIMATE algorithm.20 Utilizing the Spearman correlation framework, we examined how the risk score aligned with the expression profiles of immune checkpoints.

Somatic Mutation and Tumor Mutational Burden Analysis

Somatic mutation profiles were downloaded via the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The mutational landscape of the top ten genes was visualized through an oncoprint plot, generated via the maftools package and stratified by risk categories.21 Tumor mutational burden (TMB) scores were calculated via maftools package and subsequently log10-transformed for visualization. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test compared TMB values between different risk status.

Drug Sensitivity Prediction

Using the subclass mapping (SubMap) approach, we estimated the susceptibility to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) within both risk groups. A published melanoma cohort (47 patients treated with anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4) served as the reference. SubMap analysis ran on GenePattern (https://www.genepattern.org) with 200 marker genes and default settings.22 A Bonferroni-corrected p < 0.05 was set as the criterion for statistical significance. To assess drug sensitivity, we applied the pRRophetic R package.23 This method uses ridge regression trained on the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) pharmacogenomic data to predict the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of candidate drugs from tumor expression profiles. Drugs showing a significant disparity in IC50 predictions across risk groups (P < 0.05) were deemed potential risk-stratified therapeutic candidates.

Weighted Gene Co-Expression Network Analysis (WGCNA)

We performed WGCNA on the TCGA-PRAD cohort to identify modules associated with recurrence status and risk group.24 The pickSoftThreshold function determined a soft-thresholding power based on a scale-free topology criterion (R2 ≥ 0.85). Signed networks were built via blockwiseModules, with a minimum module size of 30 genes and a merge cut threshold of 0.25. Module eigengenes (MEs) were used to construct a clustering tree for calculating their dissimilarity. Pearson correlation analysis then assessed relationships between MEs, recurrence status, and risk group. Finally, we took the shared genes from the most risk-associated module and those in binary gene-pair signature for further study. Pan-cancer expression analysis was conducted to assess the clinical relevance of selected genes (see Supplementary Methods).

Virtual Knockout of CKS2 in CRPC Epithelial Cells

To simulate the effects of CKS2 deficiency, a computational virtual knockout analysis was executed through the scTenifoldKnk framework.25 From a batch-corrected single-cell transcriptomic dataset, we isolated CRPC epithelial cells and selected the top 5000 HVGs (including CKS2) for network construction. Using tensor decomposition and manifold alignment (500 cells per iteration), scTenifoldKnk built a gene regulatory network (GRN), then compared wild-type and knockout GRNs to simulate transcriptional perturbation following CKS2 removal. Genes with significantly altered regulation after CKS2 knockout (P < 0.05) were deemed potential downstream targets. Functional characterization of these perturbed genes, including GO and KEGG pathway enrichment, was performed via the clusterProfiler R package.16

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.1). Pearson correlation coefficients were used to assess relationships between continuous variables. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test, and continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or t-test, as appropriate. Unless noted, multiple testing correction employed FDR method. All tests were two-tailed, with P < 0.05 defined as significant.

Results

Single-Cell Transcriptomic Profiling and Identification of a CRPC-Enriched Malignant Epithelial Gene Signature

To delineate the cellular architecture of the prostate tumor microenvironment and uncover transcriptional programs underlying castration resistance, scRNA-seq profiles from benign prostate, primary PCa, and CRPC were integrated (Supplementary Figure 1A and B), resolving 18 transcriptionally distinct cell clusters (Figure 1A). Marker-based annotation assigned these clusters to five major lineages (Figure 1B).

As prostate cancer originates predominantly from the epithelial compartment, epithelial cells were re-clustered into eight subpopulations (MaligC0-MaligC7). InferCNV was used to identify malignant epithelial cells from CRPC and primary PCa specimens (Supplementary Figure 1C). Disease state-stratified UMAP visualization revealed pronounced compositional shifts across conditions (Figure 1C). Ro/e analysis further quantified subcluster enrichment by disease state (Figure 1D): MaligC3 and MaligC5 were the most stably and selectively expanded subpopulations in the castration-resistant setting, nominating them as candidate sources of CRPC-driving transcriptional programs.

Differential expression analysis was then performed at both the disease-state level and the epithelial subcluster level (Figure 1E), retaining only upregulated genes. We then crossed the 500 most highly upregulated genes in the CRPC separately with the top 500 upregulated genes from MaligC3 and MaligC5, which produced 234 and 88 common genes, respectively (Figure 1F and G). The MaligC3-derived 234-gene set paradoxically yielded lower AUCell scores in epithelial cells than in non-epithelial populations (Figure 1H), rendering it unsuitable as an epithelium-specific prognostic resource. The MaligC5-derived 88-gene set showed markedly higher scores in epithelial cells relative to all other cell types (Figure 1I), confirming its epithelial specificity. This 88-gene set was accordingly selected as the candidate gene pool for subsequent binary gene-pair prognostic model construction.

Development and Multi-Cohort Validation of a 36-Gene-Pair Binary Signature for RFS Prediction

Systematic benchmarking of 12 machine-learning algorithms across 104 combinatorial configurations identified Ridge regression as the optimal modeling approach, achieving a C-index of 0.759 in TCGA-PRAD and a top average C-index of 0.725 in five external cohorts (Figure 2A). The final signature comprised 36 gene pairs, each weighted by its Ridge regression coefficient (Figure 2B and Supplementary Table 1). High-risk patients consistently exhibited higher recurrence rates across all six cohorts (Supplementary Figure 2). Furthermore, K-M plots revealed a significant trend toward reduced RFS within the high-risk subgroup across all cohorts (Figure 2C–H), irrespective of geographic origin or sequencing platform.

For the TCGA-PRAD cohort, the time-dependent predictive power was evidenced by AUCs of 0.787, 0.788, and 0.781 at the 1-, 3-, and 5-year intervals. This prognostic reliability persisted during external validation, where the AUC ranges reached 0.644–0.905, 0.715–0.814, and 0.685–0.764, respectively (Figure 2I). The risk score was identified as the only independent prognostic variable that remained consistent across all datasets (Supplementary Figure 3A). C-index comparisons between this risk score and other clinical indicators further corroborated model performance (Supplementary Figure 3B–G). Taken together, these findings establish the binary gene-pair risk score as a robust and generalizable predictor of RFS across diverse clinical settings.

Clinicopathological Correlates of the Risk Score and Construction of a Nomogram for Individualized Postoperative RFS Prediction

We investigated how the risk score correlated with two primary clinicopathological variables. Elevated risk scores were closely linked to more progressive pathological stages (Figure 3A) and poorly differentiated histological grades (Figure 3D). Stratification analysis confirmed that high-risk group exhibited significantly shorter RFS in all four subcategories-T2 (Figure 3B), T3-T4 (Figure 3C), Gleason ≤ 7 (Figure 3E), and Gleason > 7 (Figure 3F). We then constructed a predictive nomogram incorporating the risk score, pathological T stage, and Gleason score within TCGA-PRAD cohort, enabling individualized estimation of RFS probability at 12, 36, and 60 months post-surgery (Figure 3G).

Multiple complementary metrics were used to evaluate the nomogram’s performance. In terms of time-dependent AUC, the nomogram consistently demonstrated refined predictive capability compared to individual variables and a subtle advancement over the risk score alone (Figure 3H). Calibration analysis for 1-, 3-, and 5-year RFS prediction revealed close concordance between nomogram-estimated and observed event probabilities (Figure 3I). DCA demonstrated that the nomogram yielded superior net clinical utility in most scenarios; however, exceptions were noted at specific decision threshold ranges, where the risk score alone showed superior performance (Figure 3J). C-index comparisons across all six cohorts confirmed that the nomogram achieved higher discriminative accuracy compared to other variables (Figure 3K); however, the improvement over the risk score alone reached statistical significance only in GSE21034 and DKFZ. This pattern indicates that the binary gene-pair model has already captured the predominant prognostic signal, and that while the incorporation of clinical variables can further enhance predictive performance, the magnitude of improvement remains modest.

Functional Enrichment Analysis Reveals the Biological Underpinnings of Risk Stratification

To illuminate the underlying biology that distinguished high- from low-risk tumors, differential expression analysis in TCGA-PRAD identified 414 upregulated along with 202 downregulated genes within the high-risk group (Figure 4A). GO and KEGG enrichment profiles were subsequently generated to characterize the biological attributes of these DEGs. The predominant terms were those linked to muscle cell development, muscle organ development, and muscle system process among biological processes, as well as sarcomere, myofibril and contractile fiber among cellular components (Figure 4B). Among molecular functions, enriched terms included structural constituent of muscle, antigen binding, and neurotransmitter receptor activity, while KEGG analysis highlighted cytoskeleton in muscle cells, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction and motor proteins as the most significantly represented pathways (Figure 4B). Overall, these results highlight extensive stromal, structural and signaling-related alterations in high-risk tumors.

GSEA demonstrated a strong enrichment of proliferative drivers (E2F targets, G2M checkpoint), immune and inflammatory programs (interferon alpha/gamma response, allograft rejection, inflammatory response), and epithelial-mesenchymal transition pathways within the high-risk group. Conversely, androgen response was negatively enriched (Figure 4C). Applying GSVA to immunologic gene signature sets and performing differential scoring between risk groups further identified a distinct immune microenvironment landscape (Supplementary Figure 4A and B). Together, these findings establish that risk stratification by our gene-pair model is accompanied by pervasive remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment, setting the stage for subsequent analyses of immune infiltration and immunotherapy response.

Immune Microenvironmental Characterization, Genomic Landscape, and Therapeutic Implications of the Risk Score

The immune signatures identified in the preceding transcriptomic analysis prompted a systematic profiling of the cancer immune microenvironment at this cellular resolution. Applying six complementary algorithms between the risk groups, the predicted abundances of several immune cell types differed markedly, including cytotoxic lymphocytes, CD8+ T cells, regulatory T cells, M1 and M2 macrophages (Figure 5A and B). ImmuneScore and ESTIMATEScore were notably elevated in the high-risk group, while a corresponding decline in TumorPurity was observed (Figure 5C).

Subsequently, we investigated the correlation of the risk score with 69 immunological checkpoint-related genes, which were curated from a published reference set and present in our dataset.26 Thirty-eight of these genes showed statistically significant correlations, with the risk score predominantly positively associated with key checkpoint and co-stimulatory molecules (Figure 5D). Extending the analysis to the genomic landscape, somatic mutation profiling uncovered divergent mutational architectures across the two groups. The low-risk group was predominantly marked by SPOP (13%), TTN (8%), and TP53 (5%) alterations (Figure 5E), whereas the high-risk group exhibited a markedly elevated TP53 mutation incidence (17%), alongside TTN (12%) and SPOP (9%) (Figure 5F). The high-risk group exhibited a consistently elevated TMB (Figure 5G). These features suggest that high-risk tumors possess a molecular background that may be amenable to immunotherapeutic intervention.

To test this hypothesis, the SubMap algorithm was employed, revealing that the high-risk group shared significant transcriptional similarities with anti-PD-1 responders (Bonferroni-corrected p = 0.024; Figure 5H). Drug sensitivity profiling via the GDSC2 database identified 101 agents with significantly different IC50 values between risk groups. Notably, only 26 agents displayed lower IC50 levels in the high-risk group. The four most significantly differentiated agents all displayed markedly elevated IC50 in the high-risk group (Figure 5I). This widespread resistance to multiple therapeutic agents in GDSC2 underscores the potential of immunotherapy as a critical alternative strategy for high-risk patients; however, these findings are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation.

WGCNA-Based Identification of CKS2 as a Risk-Associated Candidate Gene

To identify the core molecular determinant of the gene-pair risk signature, we applied WGCNA to the TCGA-PRAD transcriptomic data. With the soft-thresholding power set to 5, we resolved 13 biologically distinct co-expression modules (Supplementary Figure 5A–C). The MEblack module showed the most positive correlation with risk group and recurrence status (Figure 6A). Cross-referencing the MEblack module gene set against the 23 genes constituting the model identified a single gene at the intersection-CKS2 (Figure 6B). Compared with normal prostate tissue, CKS2 was markedly overexpressed in tumor specimens (Supplementary Figure 5D). An increase in expression levels of CKS2 was observed as the tumor advanced from T1-2 to T3-4 stages (Figure 6C). A similar gradient was observed across ISUP grade groups, with ISUP 5 tumors harboring significantly greater CKS2 levels than both ISUP 1–3 and ISUP 4 (Figure 6D). To contextualize these findings within the broader oncological landscape, pan-cancer profiling across 33 TCGA tumor types revealed significant tumor-versus-normal expression differences in 30 cancer types, with 28 exhibiting upregulation and 2 exhibiting downregulation in tumor tissue (Figure 6E).

CKS2 expression positively correlated with the risk score in the TCGA-PRAD cohort (Supplementary Figure 5E), with elevated levels being indicative of significantly poorer RFS (Supplementary Figure 5F). Moreover, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUCs were 0.627, 0.642, and 0.608 (Supplementary Figure 5G). To further validate CKS2’s prognostic relevance, we assessed its association with RFS in our external validation cohorts (Supplementary Figure 5H–L). CKS2 reached significance in GSE70770 and DKFZ, with consistent but non-significant trends in GSE21034 and GSE94767, and no association in AHMU. Collectively, these findings identify CKS2 as a potentially prognostic gene for prostate cancer; however, its standalone prognostic performance was weaker and less consistent than that of the full 36-gene-pair signature.

Virtual Knockout of CKS2 Identifies a KLK-Centered Androgen-Responsive Co-Regulatory Network in CRPC Epithelial Cells

Building on the prognostic significance of CKS2 established above, we sought to elucidate its downstream regulatory landscape in the castration-resistant setting. An in silico virtual knockout (KO) of CKS2 was performed in CRPC epithelial cells using the scTenifoldKnk framework. The resulting network revealed 13 downstream targets directly connected to CKS2 (Supplementary Figure 5M). The scatter plot demonstrated that these genes were the most markedly perturbed following CKS2 knockdown (Figure 6F).

The functional identity of these co-regulated targets offered a potential mechanistic hypothesis. GO enrichment of the significantly perturbed genes (P < 0.05) converged on serine-type endopeptidase and serine hydrolase activity at the molecular functions, alongside filopodium and actin-based cell projection among cellular components, and extracellular matrix disassembly, muscle contraction, and integrin-mediated cell-cell adhesion among biological processes (Figure 6G). KEGG pathway analysis reinforced this picture, with the four most enriched pathways being Prostate cancer, Vascular smooth muscle contraction, Transcriptional misregulation in cancer, and Hormone signaling (Figure 6H). Hallmark gene set analysis further identified the androgen response (AR) pathway as a major perturbed axis following the virtual knockout of CKS2 (Figure 6I). Together, these computational results suggest that CKS2 may be associated with oncogenic transcriptional programs in CRPC. However, these findings are hypothesis-generating, and the role of CKS2 in CRPC progression requires further experimental validation.

Discussion

Recurrence following radical prostatectomy serves as a decisive milestone in the natural history of prostate cancer, heralding the potential transition toward castration-resistant disease and ultimately metastatic lethality.3,27 Despite the widespread adoption of established clinicopathological parameters for postoperative risk stratification, these variables capture only a fraction of the underlying molecular heterogeneity that governs individual patient outcomes.6 The present study addresses this unmet need by developing and validating a binary gene-pair prognostic signature for RFS. By systematically integrating CRPC-enriched single-cell transcriptomics with a multi-algorithm machine-learning benchmarking framework, we provide a robust tool that bridges the gap between high-resolution cellular insights and precision clinical prognosis.

The robustness of this signature is rooted in its biological grounding within scRNA-seq-defined cellular states. By targeting CRPC-enriched subpopulations, our strategy leverages the paradigm that CRPC-like clones pre-exist within primary tumors and undergo positive selection during androgen deprivation.12 Given the profound transcriptional heterogeneity and lineage plasticity inherent in the malignant epithelium,28 these CRPC-specific markers serve as potent molecular surrogates, capturing the aggressive essence of localized disease long before clinical recurrence manifests. However, it should be acknowledged that the derivation of CRPC-specific markers from the single dataset, which may not capture the full spectrum of prior treatment histories, is a limitation, and independent scRNA-seq cohorts spanning diverse treatment backgrounds will be valuable to confirm the generalizability of the candidate gene pool. To translate these transcriptomic insights into a robust clinical tool, we evaluated 104 machine-learning algorithm combinations across six independent cohorts, ultimately identifying Ridge regression as the optimal framework for our gene-pair strategy. To enhance clinical utility, this risk score was integrated with T stage and Gleason score to develop a comprehensive nomogram. In practice, the risk score alone may be sufficient for initial risk stratification, particularly in settings where clinicopathological variables are incomplete or unavailable. The nomogram provides additional value by incorporating pathological stage and Gleason score to enable individualized probability estimation and support shared decision-making. We therefore present the nomogram as an optional refinement rather than a mandatory component.

When evaluated against current clinical standards, our signature may offer potential advantages over the commercially validated Decipher and Prolaris classifiers. Decipher and Prolaris rely on continuous gene-expression scores generated on dedicated commercial platforms, which may limit accessibility and can be influenced by batch effects and cross-platform variability.7,8 In contrast, our rank-based binary gene-pair approach is inherently platform-independent and less sensitive to normalization-related artifacts. Despite substantial heterogeneity across our datasets from different platforms and clinical settings, the model maintained stable predictive performance across cohorts, indicating potential robustness of the gene-pair–based framework. Nevertheless, residual unmeasured cohort heterogeneity, including differences in follow-up protocols and adjuvant treatment exposure, remains a limitation of retrospective multi-cohort analyses. Moreover, while our signature is biologically grounded in CRPC-enriched single-cell transcriptomic programs, further prospective comparisons within the same clinical cohort are required to formally assess its performance relative to existing commercial classifiers. Therefore, we position our model as complementary to, rather than superior to, Decipher and Prolaris.

Regarding real-world clinical implementation, the binary gene-pair approach is well suited for translation into a limited-panel molecular assay. Since the risk score is based on the relative expression ordering of 36 gene pairs within each sample, it could be measured using a focused Reverse Transcription Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) without the need for whole-transcriptome sequencing, thereby reducing cost and technical complexity. In clinical practice, the risk score could be integrated into post-radical-prostatectomy surveillance to guide risk-adapted follow-up intensity. The optimal decision thresholds, assay format, and clinical workflow would need to be defined and validated prospectively before adoption, and integration with existing guideline-based risk stratification should be evaluated in a prospective trial.

Biological characterization of the risk stratification showed that the high-risk group is characterized by a coherent and multidimensional molecular state. The predominant enrichment of E2F targets hallmark gene sets within this high-risk group aligns with the well-established role for E2F-driven transcription in prostate cancer aggressiveness and progression.29–31 E2F1 and E2F2 have been shown to promote tumor progression and sensitize cells to replication stress,32 whereas E2F8 has recently been identified as a druggable transcriptional activator in lethal CRPC.33 Conversely, the negative enrichment of androgen response in high-risk patients points to a transcriptional reprogramming away from the differentiated, hormone-responsive phenotype characteristic of low-risk disease. This pattern mirrors the molecular shift observed during the development from androgen-responsive to castration-resistant disease.

Across multiple algorithms, the high-risk group’s immune microenvironment displays greater infiltration of cytotoxic lymphocyte, CD8+ T cell, and regulatory T cell, broad upregulation of immune checkpoint genes, and elevated TMB. These findings agree with the nascent understanding that a subset of PCa contains an immunologically active microenvironment.34 PCa has historically been recognized as immunologically cold, exhibiting limited immune infiltration and poor responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade.35,36 However, accumulating evidence indicates that a subset of PCa-particularly those with higher TMB, TP53 mutations, or microsatellite instability-may harbor a more immunologically active microenvironment amenable to immunotherapy.34,37 The drug sensitivity analysis based on the pRRophetic algorithm indicated that the high-risk group exhibited relative resistance to multiple therapeutic agents in the GDSC2 database. However, it should be acknowledged that these predictions are derived from cancer cell-line pharmacogenomic data and may not fully capture the complexity of the in vivo tumor microenvironment in prostate cancer. Our SubMap analysis revealed that the transcriptional profiles of high-risk individuals highly mirrored those of anti-PD-1 responders. Nevertheless, this putative clinical advantage is preliminary and requires further substantiation within dedicated prostate cancer immunotherapy cohorts.

WGCNA identified CKS2 as a risk-associated candidate gene, providing a focused molecular feature within the broader transcriptomic risk signature. As a crucial regulatory subunit, CKS2 may contribute to the cell cycle forward through the augmentation of CDK1/2-mediated signaling. The pan-cancer analysis confirming CKS2 overexpression in 28 of 30 significantly altered TCGA tumor types between tumor and normal tissues is consistent with a recent meta-analysis suggesting that elevated CKS2 expression is a broadly conserved prognostic marker across multiple cancer types.38 The in silico virtual knockout of CKS2 in CRPC epithelial cells suggested an association with downstream transcriptional changes enriched for androgen response–related pathways. The convergent perturbation of KLK2, KLK3, KLK4, and KLK11-all established AR target genes regulated by androgen-responsive enhancers at the Kallikrein locus-together with the dominant enrichment of the androgen response pathway, indicate a potential computational link between CKS2-related expression patterns and AR signaling activity in CRPC epithelial cells.39,40 KLK2 and KLK4 hold notable translational promise: KLK2 has recently been recognized as a new therapeutic target in metastatic CRPC, with first-in-class bispecific T-cell engagers and targeted radiotherapy now under clinical evaluation;41,42 studies have shown that KLK4 integrates both AR and mTOR signaling pathways in PCa cells.43,44 However, the perturbation of these Kallikrein targets upon CKS2 virtual knockout should be interpreted cautiously, as they are derived from in silico analyses and do not establish direct regulatory relationships. Further experimental validation will be required to clarify the underlying mechanisms. In addition, potential confounding factors inherent to retrospective transcriptomic analyses, including tumor purity, stromal admixture, and unmeasured clinical covariates, may also contribute to the observed associations.45,46

Despite the encouraging prospects of our PCa signature, certain limitations warrant consideration. First, the virtual knockout results of CKS2 are computational and hypothesis-generating. The potential role of CKS2 in CRPC should be further validated through functional experiments, such as knockdown or overexpression studies in vitro and in vivo, to confirm its biological relevance. Second, all cohorts in this study are retrospective, and the true clinical utility of the signature cannot be established without prospective validation. Validation in larger, multicenter prospective cohorts will be necessary. Third, the AHMU in-house validation cohort comprised 70 patients, providing limited statistical power. Despite a consistent direction of effect across cohorts, the small sample size limits its standalone validation strength, and larger independent cohorts are warranted. Fourth, SubMap-derived immunotherapy predictions require prospective validation in dedicated prostate cancer cohorts to confirm their clinical utility.

Conclusions

We developed a robust binary gene-pair signature through an integrated computational framework, demonstrating high predictive accuracy in forecasting RFS after radical prostatectomy. The high-risk group exhibited an immune-inflamed microenvironment with elevated checkpoint expression, higher genomic instability, and transcriptional similarity to reported anti-PD-1 therapy responders, although these findings are exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Furthermore, CKS2 was identified as a candidate risk-associated gene computationally linked to androgen-responsive transcriptional programs. Collectively, this gene-pair signature may help improve risk stratification and refine personalized follow-up for patients post-prostatectomy. However, prospective validation and experimental studies are required before clinical translation. The biological role of CKS2 in PCa progression warrants further mechanistic investigation.
